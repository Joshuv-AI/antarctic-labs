A correction adaptive two-grid finite element method for nonselfadjoint or indefinite elliptic problems Journal: Elsevier
Fei Li, Qingguo Hong, Ming Tang, Liuqiang Zhong

Abstract
We propose, analyze, and numerically validate a correction adaptive two-grid finite element method (CATGFEM) for nonselfadjoint or indefinite elliptic problems. In contrast to the adaptive two-grid finite element method (ATGFEM) of Li and Zhang [SIAM J. Sci. Comput., 43 (2021), pp. A908–A928], which is restricted to symmetric positive-definite problems, the proposed method introduces an additional correction step that solves a small-scale discrete residual problem on the coarse mesh. This step entails negligible additional computational cost and allows us to show that the L 2 L^{2} -norm error of the corrected discrete solution is a higher-order of the energy-norm error of the discrete solution. Using this result, we prove a contraction property for a suitable sum of quasi-errors on two successive adaptive meshes and establish convergence of the method. Numerical experiments illustrate the improved effectiveness and robustness of our method in comparison with ATGFEM.



1
Introduction

Nonselfadjoint or indefinite elliptic problems constitute a fundamental class of model problems in computational science and engineering. Representative examples include convection–diffusion–reaction equations
[
8
,
27
,
1
]
and the Helmholtz equation
[
10
,
13
,
9
]
. In many applications, the solutions of such problems may exhibit singular behavior caused by nontrivial domain geometries or nonsmooth coefficients and source terms
[
12
,
17
]
. The adaptive finite element method (AFEM)
[
23
,
24
,
2
]
is one of the most effective approaches for treating elliptic problems with solution singularities. AFEM constructs computable a posteriori error estimators based on the discrete solution and known problem data, identifies elements with large local error contributions associated with singular behavior, and locally refines these elements to generate improved meshes. Repeating this adaptive loop balances discretization errors between singular and nonsingular regions, thereby yielding quasi-optimal meshes and accurate numerical solutions.

In many existing analyses of AFEM for nonselfadjoint or indefinite elliptic problems, see, for example,
[
18
,
3
,
11
,
16
]
, the availability of the exact discrete solution is assumed, although its computation is in general difficult
[
25
]
. It is well known that symmetric positive-definite (SPD) problems are typically easier to solve than nonselfadjoint or indefinite problems, and two-grid methods provide a classical strategy for transforming the latter into the former
[
26
]
.
Li and Zhang
[
14
]
incorporated the two-grid idea into adaptivity and proposed the adaptive two-grid finite element method (ATGFEM). In this approach, the

(k-1)
th and
k
th adaptive meshes are regarded as the coarse and fine grids, respectively, in contrast to the method proposed in
[
4
]
, which additionally introduces a uniform coarse grid at each adaptive level. Using the discrete solution on the
(k-1)
th mesh, ATGFEM constructs and solves only an SPD problem on the

k
th mesh.

It should be noted that the convergence result for ATGFEM in
[
14
]
holds up to certain
L^{2}
-norm errors of the discrete solutions, which are empirically treated as higher-order quantities of the corresponding energy-norm errors, under the assumption that the initial mesh is sufficiently fine. However, this key observation is not theoretically justified in
[
14
]
, and therefore the convergence analysis is not fully rigorous. Moreover, our numerical investigations indicate that the performance of ATGFEM may deteriorate in certain regimes, such as problems with small negative reaction coefficients or when large marking parameters are used.

For nonselfadjoint or indefinite elliptic problems, the main differences and contributions of the present work, in comparison with
[
14
]
, are summarized as follows:

1.

We introduce an additional correction step by solving a discrete residual problem on the initial mesh, whose solution is used to correct the discrete solution obtained on the previous adaptive mesh. Consequently, starting from the second adaptive step, the proposed correction adaptive two-grid finite element method (CATGFEM, Algorithm
3.5
) behaves as an iterative two-grid scheme, with the fixed initial mesh serving as the coarse grid and the adaptively refined mesh as the fine grid.

2.

We rigorously prove that a suitable sum of quasi-errors on two successive adaptive levels is contractive (Theorem
4.1
), which yields convergence of the CATGFEM (Corollary
4.1
). The contraction proof relies crucially on a new auxiliary result showing that the
L^{2}
-norm error of the corrected discrete solution is a higher-order-order of the energy-norm error of the discrete solution (Lemma
4.1
).

3.

Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical results and demonstrate that the proposed CATGFEM exhibits improved effectiveness and greater robustness with respect to the marking parameter when compared with ATGFEM.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces preliminaries, and Section
3
presents the CATGFEM. Section
4
establishes the key
L^{2}
-error estimate and develops the contraction and convergence analysis. Numerical experiments illustrating the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method are reported in Section
5
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some notations, the nonselfadjoint or indefinite elliptic model problem, and the finite element discretization.

2.1
Notation

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\;(d=2,\,3)
be a simply connected bounded open polygonal or polyhedral domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. For any set
S\subset\Omega
and any
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
,
we denote by
L^{p}(S)
the standard Lebesgue space equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|v\|_{0,p,S}:=\left(\int_{S}|v|^{p}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\;p\in(1,\infty),\quad\text{or}\quad\|v\|_{0,\infty,S}:=\esssup\limits_{\boldsymbol{x}\in S}|v(\boldsymbol{x})|.

Specially, we write
\|v\|_{0,2,S}
to be
\|v\|_{0,S}
for any
v\in L^{2}(S)
. We also denote the
L^{2}(S)
inner product by

(v,w)_{S}:=\int_{S}vw\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\quad\forall\;v,w\in L^{2}(S).

For any integer
m>0
, we denote by
W^{m,p}(S)
the standard Sobolev space equipped with the norm

\|v\|_{m,p,S}:=\left(\sum_{|j|\leqslant m}\|D^{j}v\|^{p}_{0,p,S}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\;p\in(1,\infty),\quad\text{or}\quad\|v\|_{m,\infty,S}:=\max_{|j|\leqslant m}\|D^{j}v\|_{0,\infty,S},

where
D^{j}v
is the
j
-th weak partial derivative.
In particular, we write
H^{m}(S)
instead of
W^{m,2}(S)
and write
\|v\|_{m,S}
instead of
\|v\|_{m,2,S}
, respectively. Moreover, note that if
S=\Omega
, the subscript
S
will be dropped in the norm and the inner product.

The space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
consists of all functions
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with zero traces, and
H^{-1}(\Omega)
denotes the dual space of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
with the norm

\|g\|_{-1}:=\sup_{v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\langle g,v\rangle}{\|v\|_{1}},\quad\forall\;g\in H^{-1}(\Omega),

(2.1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the dual pairing between
H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

The inequality
a\lesssim b
abbreviates
a\leqslant Cb
with some generic constant
C>0
independent of the notations
a
and
b
, the mesh size, and the adaptivity iteration counter mentioned later. Furthermore,
a\simeq b
denotes
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
. We also use
C=C(a,b)
to indicate that the constant
C
depends only on the notations
a
and
b
.

2.2
Model problem

Given the source term
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, we consider the following nonselfadjoint or indefinite elliptic boundary-value problem

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}u:=-\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla u)+\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla u+\gamma u=~

\displaystyle f,

\displaystyle\text{in}~\Omega,

(2.2)

\displaystyle u=~

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\text{on}~\partial\Omega,

(2.3)

where the matrix function (also called the diffusion coefficient)
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
is SPD with eigenvalues
0<\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}<\infty
such that

\mu_{0}|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}\leqslant\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{\xi}^{T}\leqslant\mu_{1}|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2},\quad\forall\;\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega,

(2.4)

the vector function (also called the convection coefficient)
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}
and the scalar function (also called the reaction coefficient)
\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

The weak formulation of (
2.2
)–(
2.3
) reads: Find
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\hat{a}(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle,\quad\forall\;v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(2.5)

where the bilinear form
\hat{a}(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined by

\hat{a}(v,w):=(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla v,\nabla w)+(\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla v,w)+(\gamma v,w),\quad\forall\;v,w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.6)

We suppose that the operator
\hat{\mathcal{L}}:\;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)
is an isomorphism, hence the weak formulation (
2.5
) admits a unique solution
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

We define the energy norm as

\|v\|_{a}:=\sqrt{a(v,v)},\quad\text{where}\quad a(v,w):=(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla v,\nabla w),\quad\forall\;v,w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.7)

According to the ellipticity of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
in (
2.4
) and the Poincaré inequality, we know that the energy norm
\|\cdot\|_{a}
is equivalent to the native norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e., for all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, there holds

\|v\|_{a}\simeq\|v\|_{1}.

(2.8)

By the assumptions on the model data
\boldsymbol{\alpha},\,\boldsymbol{\beta}
and
\gamma
, and the norm equivalence (
2.8
), we obtain the boundedness of the bilinear form
\hat{a}(\cdot,\cdot)
:

\hat{a}(v,w)\lesssim\|v\|_{a}\|w\|_{a},\quad\forall\;v,w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.9)

Since
\hat{\mathcal{L}}:\;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)
is an isomorphism, its dual operator
\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{*}:\;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)
is also an isomorphism, and there both hold the stability estimates (see, e.g.,
[
26
]
)

\|v\|_{1}\lesssim\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}v\|_{-1},\quad\|v\|_{1}\lesssim\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{*}v\|_{-1},\quad\forall\;v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.10)

By the stability estimates (
2.10
), the
\|\cdot\|_{-1}
norm definition (
2.1
) and the norm equivalence (
2.8
), we obtain

\|v\|_{a}\lesssim\sup_{w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\hat{a}(v,w)}{\|w\|_{a}},\quad\|v\|_{a}\lesssim\sup_{w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\hat{a}(w,v)}{\|w\|_{a}},\quad\forall\;v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.11)

To discretize the weak formulation (
2.5
), we consider a conforming and shape-regular simplex partition
\mathcal{T}=\{T\}
of the domain
\Omega
, where the element
T\in\mathcal{T}
is a triangle if
d=2
, or a tetrahedron if
d=3
. Associated with the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, we consider the continuous piecewise finite element space

\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{T}}:=\{v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathcal{C}(\Omega):\;v_{\mathcal{T}}|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(T),\;\text{for any element }T\in\mathcal{T}\},

(2.12)

where
\mathcal{C}(\Omega)
is the space of continuous functions on
\Omega
and
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(T)
consists of all polynomials of total degree less than or equal to
\ell
on the element
T
.
The finite element discretization of (
2.5
) reads: Find
\hat{u}_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{T}}
such that

\hat{a}(\hat{u}_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{T}})=\langle f,v_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle,\quad\forall\;v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{T}}.

(2.13)

The existence and uniqueness of the finite element solution of (
2.13
) can be proved under the condition that the mesh size
h_{\mathcal{T}}:=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h_{T}
of the mesh
\mathcal{T}
is sufficiently small
[
20
,
19
]
, where
h_{T}:=\mathrm{diam}(T)
is the diameter of any element
T\in\mathcal{T}
. Moreover, note that combining the assumption that the mesh size
h_{\mathcal{T}}
is sufficiently small with the norm equivalence (
2.8
) yields that

\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{a}\lesssim\sup_{w_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{T}}}\frac{\hat{a}(v_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}})}{\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{a}},\quad\|v_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{a}\lesssim\sup_{w_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{T}}}\frac{\hat{a}(w_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{T}})}{\|w_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{a}},\quad\forall\;v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{T}},

(2.14)

one can refer to
[
26
, Lemma 2.2]
.

3
Adaptive algorithm

The AFEM is based on the loop of the following procedures

\textbf{SOLVE}~\rightarrow~\textbf{ESTIMATE}~\rightarrow~\textbf{MARK}~\rightarrow~\textbf{REFINE}.

(3.1)

We below describe each procedure of loop (
3.1
) of the proposed CATGFEM (Algorithm
3.5
). Particularly in Section
3.1
, we also detailedly present Procedure
SOLVE
of the standard AFEM (SAFEM,
[
18
,
11
]
) and the ATGFEM
[
14
]
, respectively, to emphasize the main idea of our CATGFEM.

3.1
Procedure
SOLVE

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
be a mesh family generated by Procedure
REFINE
; see Section
3.4
in detail. We write the finite element space
\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
to
\mathbb{V}_{k}
for simplicity.

The SAFEM is to solve the discretization (
2.13
) on each mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k}
: Find
\hat{u}_{k}\in\mathbb{V}_{k}
such that

\hat{a}(\hat{u}_{k},v_{k})=\langle f,v_{k}\rangle,\quad\forall\;v_{k}\in\mathbb{V}_{k}.

(3.2)

Note that the resulting algebraic system is in general nonselfadjoint or indefinite, and solving such a problem is difficult
[
25
]
. Furthermore, numerical computation of (
3.2
) requires that the initial mesh size
h_{0}
is sufficiently small. Thus, these lead to that the computational cost for such a nonselfadjoint or indefinite problem grows expensive as the adaptivity iteration counter
k
increases.

It is well-known that solving SPD problems is often much easier than solving nonselfadjoint or indefinite problems, since there are many fast solvers, e.g., multigrid methods
[
15
]
, for the resulting algebraic system of the SPD problem. Therefore, the ATGFEM, by applying the two-grid method
[
26
]
to adaptivity, i.e., see the
(k-1)
th and the
k
th meshes as the coarse and fine grids, transforms the nonselfadjoint or indefinite problem into an SPD (approximate) problem to solve. The corresponding solving procedure is stated as follows:

1.

On the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, solve the nonselfadjoint or indefinite discretization (
3.2
) to obtain the finite element solution
\tilde{u}_{0}:=\hat{u}_{0}
.

2.

On the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k},\;k\geqslant 1
, solve the following SPD discretization: Find
\tilde{u}_{k}\in\mathbb{V}_{k}
such that

a(\tilde{u}_{k},v_{k})=\langle f,v_{k}\rangle-N(\tilde{u}_{k-1},v_{k}),\quad\forall\;v_{k}\in\mathbb{V}_{k},

(3.3)

where the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined in (
2.7
) and the second term in the left-hand side above is called the lower-order term, which is defined by

N(v,w):=(\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla v,w)+(\gamma v,w),\quad\forall\;v,w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(3.4)

Remark 3.1
.

Note that the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
in the adaptive mesh family
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
is the coarsest, hence the nonselfadjoint or indefinite discretization (
3.2
) on
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is a relatively small-scale problem in overall adaptivity.

However, we find that the ATGFEM behaves not well in some numerical examples; see Section
5
in details. Moreover, the convergence result of the ATGFEM is not rigorous since the analysis implicitly requires the higher-order
L^{2}
-norm error term which is only empirical but not theoretically justified.

In order to improve the ATGFEM, the proposed CATGFEM incorporates an additional correction step before solving the SPD problem (
3.3
), i.e., first solve a small-scale discrete residual problem on the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
(see Remark
3.1
), and then use the solution of the residual problem to correct the discrete solution on the previous mesh. The corresponding procedure is described as follows:

1.

On the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, solve the nonselfadjoint or indefinite discretization (
3.2
) to obtain the finite element solution
u_{0}=\hat{u}_{0}
.

2.

On the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k}\;(k\geqslant 1)
, perform the following two subprocedures:

(a)

Solve a nonselfadjoint or indefinite residual equation on the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, i.e., find
e_{0,k-1}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}
such that

\hat{a}(e_{0,k-1},v_{0})=\langle f,v_{0}\rangle-\hat{a}(u_{k-1},v_{0}),\quad\forall\;v_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}.

(3.5)

(b)

Solve an SPD discretization on
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, i.e., find
u_{k}\in\mathbb{V}_{k}
such that

a(u_{k},v_{k})=(f,v_{k})-N(u_{k-1}+e_{0,k-1},v_{k}),\quad\forall\;v_{k}\in\mathbb{V}_{k}.

(3.6)

Note that (
3.5
) is relatively small-scale; consequently, solving (
3.5
)–(
3.6
) makes the treatment of nonselfadjoint or indefinite problems almost as easy as solving SPD problems.

Remark 3.2
.

For the case of
k=1
, the right-hand side of (
3.5
) is zero due to
u_{0}=\hat{u}_{0}
being the solution of (
3.2
). This implies that
e_{0,0}=0
, which means that (
3.5
) does not work, and (
3.6
) will be transformed into

a(u_{1},v_{1})=\langle f,v_{1}\rangle-N(u_{0},v_{1}),\quad\forall\;v_{1}\in\mathbb{V}_{1},

(3.7)

which is indeed (
3.3
) for
k=1
of the ATGFEM due to
u_{0}=\tilde{u}_{0}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The discretization (
3.5
) is equivalent to the following form

\hat{a}(u_{k-1}^{*},v_{0})=\langle f,v_{0}\rangle,\quad\forall\;v_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0},

(3.8)

with
u_{k-1}^{*}:=u_{k-1}+e_{0,k-1}
. We note that
u_{k-1}^{*}\in\mathbb{V}_{k-1}
due to
e_{0,k-1}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}\subset\mathbb{V}_{k-1}
.

Remark 3.4
.

We summarize the similarities and differences of Procedure
SOLVE
of ATGFEM and our CATGFEM:

1.

On the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, ATGFEM and our CATGFEM both solve the nonselfadjoint or indefinite discretization (
3.2
).

2.

On the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, our CATGFEM also behaves like ATGFEM (see Remark
3.2
).

3.

The difference is that, starting from the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k},\;k\geqslant 2
, the SPD problem is constructed by the discrete solution on the previous mesh for the ATGFEM, while by a corrected one for our CATGFEM, where the corrected solution is updated via the solution of a small-scale residual problem (
3.5
).

3.2
Procedure
ESTIMATE

For the ATGFEM in
[
14
]
, starting from the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k}\;(k\geqslant 1)
, the discrete solutions
\tilde{u}_{k}
and
\tilde{u}_{k-1}
are used for the SPD operator
\mathcal{L}
and the lower-order term operator
\mathcal{N}
, respectively, in the elementwise residual of the error estimator, where

\mathcal{L}v:=-\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla v),\quad\mathcal{N}v:=\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla v+\gamma v.

We will adapt this idea for our CATGFEM setting.

With the discrete solution
u_{k}
and
u_{k-1}^{*}:=u_{k-1}+e_{0,k-1}
for
k\geqslant 1
at hand, define the residual

R_{T}(u_{k})=\begin{cases}f|_{T}-\hat{\mathcal{L}}u_{0}|_{T},&k=0,\\
f|_{T}-\mathcal{L}u_{k}|_{T}-\mathcal{N}u_{k-1}^{*}|_{T},&k\geqslant 1,\end{cases}

(3.9)

for any element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, and define the jump

J_{E}(u_{k}):=[\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}]|_{E}:=\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla u_{k}|_{T_{1}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla u_{k}|_{T_{2}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{2},

(3.10)

for any side
E\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}
denotes the set of all interior sides being an edge (
d=2
) or a face (
d=3
) in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, and
the elements
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
share
E
with the unit outward normal vectors
\boldsymbol{n}_{1},\boldsymbol{n}_{2}
.

We define the following local a posteriori error estimator:

\eta_{k}(u_{k},T):=\Big(h_{T}^{2}\|R_{T}(u_{k})\|_{0,T}^{2}+h_{T}\sum_{E\subset\partial T\cap\Omega}\|J_{E}(u_{k})\|_{0,E}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.11)

and for any element patch
S\subset\mathcal{T}_{k}
, define

\eta_{k}(u_{k},S):=\Big(\sum_{T\in S}\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k},T)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.12)

3.3
Procedure
MARK

This procedure aims to select which elements will be refined in the current mesh in term of the local contribution of the error estimator. We consider the Dörfler marking strategy
[
7
]
. Given a marking parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
, Procedure
MARK
outputs a set of marked elements
\mathcal{M}_{k}\subset\mathcal{T}_{k}
with minimal cardinality such that

\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k},\mathcal{M}_{k})\geqslant\theta\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k},\mathcal{T}_{k}).

(3.13)

3.4
Procedure
REFINE

Starting from a conforming and shape-regular initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, a conforming mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{T}_{k}
by the local mesh refinement. To generate
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}
. we first bisect all marked elements, i.e., divide each element in
\mathcal{M}_{k}\subset\mathcal{T}_{k}
into two equal volume parts, to get a new mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}^{\prime}
. In general, hanging nodes exist in
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}^{\prime}
, so we have to refine some elements in
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}^{\prime}
to generate a conforming mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}
.

We assume that the mesh family
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
generated by the above procedure is uniformly shape-regular. The possible choice of the local mesh refinement is the newest vertex bisection method
[
22
]
. Furthermore, note that meshes
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is nested in the sense that the finite element spaces
\mathbb{V}_{k}\subset\mathbb{V}_{k+1}
.

It is worth to note that the ATGFEM and our CATGFEM both adopt the Dörfler marking strategy and the bisection method in Procedures

; see Remark
3.4
. Note that, for Procedure
ESTIMATE
, the construction of the error estimator of the CATGFEM follows the idea of the ATGFEM.

4
Convergence analysis

In this section, we first prove a key auxiliary result called the
L^{2}
-lifting property of the error of the corrected discrete solution, which will be frequently used in the analysis below. We are then devoted to verifying three components of contraction analysis: quasi-orthogonality, reliability and estimator reduction. Finally, we prove a weaker version of contraction of the quasi-error compared with the classical version, and derive the convergence of the proposed CATGFEM (Algorithm
3.5
).

4.1
L^{2}
-lifting property

The following lemma indicates that, under the condition that the initial mesh size
h_{0}
is sufficiently small, the
L^{2}
-norm error of the corrected discrete solution
u_{k-1}^{*}=u_{k-1}+e_{0,k-1}
is a higher-order quantity of the energy-norm error of the discrete solution
u_{k-1}
. It is precisely this lemma that enables us to establish a series of stronger theoretical results, in particular more rigorous contraction and convergence properties for the CATGFEM, in comparison with the ATGFEM in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
L^{2}
-lifting property)
.

Let
u
,
e_{0,k-1}
and
u_{k}
be the solutions of (
2.5
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), and keep
u_{k-1}^{*}=e_{0,k-1}+u_{k-1}
in mind. Then, there holds

\|u-u_{k-1}^{*}\|_{0}\leqslant\kappa(h_{0})\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}

(4.1)

with a positive constant
\kappa(h_{0})=\kappa(h_{0},\Omega,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)
satisfying

\lim_{h_{0}\rightarrow 0}\kappa(h_{0})=0.

(4.2)

Remark 4.1
.

Note that Lemma
4.1
mathematically gives the fact that the
L^{2}
-norm error
\|u-u_{k}^{*}\|_{0}
is a higher-order quantity of the energy-norm error
\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}
provided that the initial mesh size
h_{0}
is sufficiently small, compared with
[
14
]
, where the
L^{2}
-norm error
\|u-\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{0}
may be only empirically seen as a higher-order quantity of the energy-norm errror
\|u-\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{a}
when
h_{0}
is sufficiently small, here
\tilde{u}_{k}
is the solution of (
3.3
).

For the purpose of the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we need the following projection operator
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}:\;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{0}
defined by

\hat{a}(v,w_{0})=\hat{a}(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}v,w_{0}),\quad\forall\;v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\;w_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}.

(4.3)

The projection operator
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
is well-defined if the initial mesh size
h_{0}
is sufficiently small according to (
2.10
) and (
2.14
); see
[
26
, Lemma 2.3]
. Moreover,
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
is bounded with respect to the energy-norm
\|\cdot\|_{a}
:

\|\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}v\|_{a}\lesssim\|v\|_{a}.

(4.4)

In fact, by (
2.14
), (
4.3
) and (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}v\|_{a}\lesssim~

\displaystyle\sup_{w_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}}\dfrac{\hat{a}(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}v,w_{0})}{\|w_{0}\|_{a}}=\sup_{w_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}}\dfrac{\hat{a}(v,w_{0})}{\|w_{0}\|_{a}}\lesssim\|v\|_{a}.

(4.5)

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Substracting (
3.8
) from (
2.5
) yields

\hat{a}(u-u_{k-1}^{*},v_{0})=\langle f,v_{0}\rangle-\hat{a}(e_{0,k-1}+u_{k-1},v_{0})=0,\quad\forall\;v_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}.

(4.6)

By (
4.6
), (
2.9
) and the norm equivalence (
2.8
), as the proof of the Aubin-Nitsche lemma
[
6
, Theorem 3.2.4]
, we have

\displaystyle\|u-u_{k-1}^{*}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u-u_{k-1}^{*}\|_{a}\sup_{g\in L^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{1}{\|g\|_{0}}\inf_{\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}}\|\phi_{g}-\phi_{0}\|_{a}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\|u-u_{k-1}^{*}\|_{a}\sup_{g\in L^{2}(\Omega),\|g\|_{0}=1}\inf_{\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}}\|\phi_{g}-\phi_{0}\|_{a},

(4.8)

where for given
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\phi_{g}
is the unique solution of the following problem: Find
\phi_{g}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{*}\phi_{g},v)=(\hat{\mathcal{L}}v,\phi_{g})=\hat{a}(v,\phi_{g})=(g,v),\quad\forall\;v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(4.9)

and hence
\phi_{g}=(\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{*})^{-1}g
.
Moreover, from
[
6
, Eqn. (2.4.5)]
, as the initial mesh size
h_{0}
tends to zero, there exists a family of subspaces of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\lim_{h_{0}\rightarrow 0}\sup_{g\in L^{2}(\Omega),\|g\|_{0}=1}\inf_{\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}}\|(\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{*})^{-1}g-\phi_{0}\|_{a}=0.

For the result (
4.1
), it remains to prove that

\|u-u_{k-1}^{*}\|_{a}\lesssim\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}.

Collecting (
4.3
), (
2.5
) and (
3.8
) yields

\hat{a}(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}u,v_{0})=\hat{a}(u_{k-1}^{*},v_{0})=\hat{a}(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(u_{k-1}+e_{0,k-1}),v_{0}),\quad\forall\;v_{0}\in\mathbb{V}_{0},

which implies that

\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}u=\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(u_{k-1}+e_{0,k-1}).

Noting thtat
e_{0,k-1}\in\mathbb{V}_{0}
, we have
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}u=\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}u_{k-1}+e_{0,k-1}
, which implies

e_{0,k-1}=\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(u-u_{k-1}).

(4.10)

Combining this identity (
4.10
) with (
2.11
), (
2.9
) and (
4.4
) and keeping
u_{k-1}^{*}=u_{k-1}+e_{0,k-1}
in mind lead to that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{k-1}^{*}\|_{a}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\hat{a}(u-u_{k-1}^{*},v)}{\|v\|_{a}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\hat{a}(u-u_{k-1}-\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(u-u_{k-1}),v)}{\|v\|_{a}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a},

∎

Remark 4.2
.

If the solution
\phi_{g}
of (
4.9
) satisfies the extra regularity property
\phi_{g}\in H^{1+s}(\Omega)\;(0<s\leqslant 1)
, then, applying the approximation property of
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}

[
26
, Lemma 2.4]
to (
4.7
) and using the regularity estimate yield that

\|u-u_{k-1}^{*}\|_{0}\lesssim h_{0}^{s}\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}.

However, the extra regularity property is generally not satisfied in the presence of singularities, so we consider the lowest regularity property
\phi_{g}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
throughout this paper.

4.2
Quasi-orthogonality

The following lemma establishes quasi-orthogonality for the energy-norm error without any remainder term, owing to Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 4.2

(Quasi-orthogonality)
.

Let
u
,
e_{0,k-1}
and
u_{k}
be the solutions of (
2.5
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), and keep
u_{k-1}^{*}=e_{0,k-1}+u_{k-1}
in mind.
Then, there holds

\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}\leqslant\dfrac{1}{1-c_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})}\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}-\|u_{k-1}-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}.

(4.11)

with a constant
c_{\mathrm{qo}}=c_{\mathrm{qo}}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)>0
and the initial mesh size
h_{0}
is sufficiently small such that
c_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})<1
,

Proof.

We show that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle=a(u-u_{k-1}+u_{k-1}-u_{k},u-u_{k-1}+u_{k-1}-u_{k})

\displaystyle=\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+\|u_{k}-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+2a(u-u_{k-1},u_{k-1}-u_{k})

\displaystyle=\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}-\|u_{k}-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+2a(u-u_{k},u_{k-1}-u_{k}).

(4.12)

For the third term in the right-hand side of (
4.12
), using (
3.8
), the following estimate of the lower-order term
\mathcal{N}
is defined in (
3.4
)

N(v,w)\lesssim\|v\|_{0}\|w\|_{1},\quad\forall\;v,w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(4.13)

Then the result (
4.1
) from Lemma
4.1
and the norm equivalence (
2.8
) leads to

\displaystyle 2a(u-u_{k},u_{k-1}-u_{k})

\displaystyle=-2N(u-u_{k-1}^{*},u_{k-1}-u_{k})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u-u_{k-1}^{*}\|_{0}\|u_{k-1}-u_{k}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa(h_{0})\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}\|u_{k-1}-u_{k}\|_{a}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})\Big(\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}\|u_{k-1}-u_{k}\|_{a}+\|u_{k-1}-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}\Big)

(4.14)

with a positive constant
\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}=\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)
.
Applying the Young inequality with any
\delta>0
to the first term in the right-hand side of (
4.14
) and substituting the arising result into (
4.12
) yield that

\Big(1-\delta\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})\Big)\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}\leqslant\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}-\Big(1-\delta^{-1}\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})-2\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})\Big)\|u_{k-1}-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}.

Setting

1-\delta\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})=1-\delta^{-1}\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})-2\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})\quad\text{implies}\quad\delta=\sqrt{2}+1.

Let the initial mesh size
h_{0}
be sufficiently small such that
(\sqrt{2}+1)\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})<1
. We conclude the proof.
∎

4.3
Reliability

We first give the following approximation properties of the quasi-interpolation operator; see, e.g.,
[
24
, Lemma 1.3]
), which is a necessary tool to prove the reliability of the error estimator. A typical example of the quasi-interpolation operator is the Scott-Zhang interpolant
[
21
]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a quasi-interpolation operator
\Pi_{k}:\,H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{k}
such that for any
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
, and any
E\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}
, there hold

\displaystyle\left\|v-\Pi_{k}v\right\|_{0,T}\lesssim h_{T}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{0,\hat{\omega}_{T}},

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left\|v-\Pi_{k}v\right\|_{0,E}\lesssim h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{0,\hat{\omega}_{E}},

(4.16)

where the hidden constants only depend on the uniform shape regularity of the mesh family
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
, and the sets
\hat{\omega}_{T}
and
\hat{\omega}_{E}
denote the unions of elements sharing at least a vertex with
T
and
E
, respectively.

The following lemma indicates that the global error estimator is the a posteriori upper bound of the energy-norm error, up to some theoretical higher-order quantity.
To save notations, we write
\eta_{k}(u_{k})
instead of
\eta_{k}(u_{k},\mathcal{T}_{k})
below.

Lemma 4.4

(Reliability)
.

Let
u
and
u_{k}
be the solutions of (
2.5
) and (
3.6
). Then, for
k\geqslant 1
, there holds

\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}\leqslant C_{\mathrm{rel}}\eta_{k}(u_{k})+\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{rel}}\kappa(h_{0})\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}

(4.17)

with positive constants
C_{\mathrm{rel}}=C_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\alpha})
dependent of the uniform shape regularity of the mesh family
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
and
\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{rel}}=\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)
.

Specially for
k=0
, the second term in the right-hand side of (
4.17
) will vanish.

Remark 4.3
.

In
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
, the reliability of the error estimator reads in our setting as follows:

\|u-\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{a}\lesssim\tilde{\eta}_{k}(\tilde{u}_{k})+\|u-\tilde{u}_{k-1}\|_{0},

(4.18)

where
\tilde{\eta}_{k}(\tilde{u}_{k})
is defined in
[
14
, (3.9)]
. Notice that the reference
[
14
]
thinks that (
4.18
) holds up to an empirical higher-order quantity
\|u-\tilde{u}_{k-1}\|_{0}
compared with the corresponding energy-norm error
\|u-\tilde{u}_{k-1}\|_{a}
. Therefore, our reliablity (
4.17
) given by Lemme
4.4
is a better result than (
4.18
) due to the theoretical higher-order remainder term.

Proof.

Note that if
k=0
, we readily obtain
\|u-u_{0}\|_{a}\leqslant C_{\mathrm{rel}}\eta_{0}(u_{0})
according to
[
11
, Eqn. (2.7)]
and the norm equivalence (
2.8
). It remains to prove (
4.17
) for
k\geqslant 1
.

For any
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
v_{k}\in\mathbb{V}_{k}
, using (
2.5
) and (
3.6
), we show that

\displaystyle a(u-u_{k},v)=~

\displaystyle a(u-u_{k},v-v_{k})+a(u-u_{k},v_{k})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\langle f,v-v_{k}\rangle-N(u,v-v_{k})-a(u_{k},v-v_{k})-N(u-u_{k-1}^{*},v_{k})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\langle f,v-v_{k}\rangle-a(u_{k},v-v_{k})-N(u_{k-1}^{*},v-v_{k})-N(u-u_{k-1}^{*},v).

(4.19)

Applying integration by parts, the Hölder inequality, the estimate (
4.13
), and the result (
4.1
) of Lemma
4.1
to (
4.19
) yields

\displaystyle a(u-u_{k},v)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}(f-\mathcal{L}u_{k}-\mathcal{N}u_{k-1}^{*},v-v_{k})_{T}-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}}\int_{E}[\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}](v-v_{k})\mathrm{d}s-N(u-u_{k-1}^{*},v)

\displaystyle\leqslant~

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\|R_{T}(u_{k})\|_{0,T}\|v-v_{k}\|_{0,T}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}}\|J_{E}(u_{k})\|_{0,E}\|v-v_{k}\|_{0,E}+C(\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)\kappa(h_{0})\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}\|v\|_{1}.

(4.20)

Taking
v_{k}=\Pi_{k}v
, where
\Pi_{k}
is the quasi-interpolation operator in Lemma
4.3
, and using the corresponding approximation properties (
4.15
)–(
4.16
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the uniform shape regularity of the mesh family
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
and the norm equivalence (
2.8
) lead to that

\displaystyle a(u-u_{k},v)\leqslant~

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\|R_{T}(u_{k})\|_{0,T}\|v-\Pi_{k}v\|_{0,T}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}}\|J_{E}(u_{k})\|_{0,E}\|v-\Pi_{k}v\|_{0,E}+C(\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)\kappa(h_{0})\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}\|v\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim~

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}h_{T}\|R_{T}(u_{k})\|_{0,T}\|\nabla v\|_{0,\hat{\omega}_{T}}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}}h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|J_{E}(u_{k})\|_{0,E}\|\nabla v\|_{0,\hat{\omega}_{E}}\Big)+\kappa(h_{0})\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}\|v\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim~

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}h_{T}^{2}\|R_{T}(u_{k})\|_{0,T}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}}h_{T}\|J_{E}(u_{k})\|_{0,E}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{a}+\kappa(h_{0})\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}\|v\|_{a},

(4.21)

where the uniform shape regularity implies that

\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}\|\nabla v\|_{0,\hat{\omega}_{T}}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{I}}\|\nabla v\|_{0,\hat{\omega}_{E}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|v\|_{1}.

Taking
v=u-u_{k}
in (
4.21
) yields (
4.17
).
∎

4.4
Estimator reduction

The following lemma indicates that the error estimator is contractive up to some perturbations.

Lemma 4.5

(Estimator reduction)
.

Let
u
and
u_{k}
be the solutions of (
2.5
) and (
3.6
). Then, for
k\geqslant 2
, there holds

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k})\leqslant~

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{est}}\eta_{k-1}^{2}(u_{k-1})+C_{\mathrm{est}}\|u_{k}-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}\left(\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+\|u-u_{k-2}\|_{a}^{2}\right)

(4.22)

with
\rho_{\mathrm{est}}=\rho_{\mathrm{est}}(d,\theta)\in(0,1)
, and the positive constants
C_{\mathrm{est}}=C_{\mathrm{est}}(d,\Omega,\boldsymbol{\alpha})
and
C_{\mathrm{rem}}=C_{\mathrm{rem}}(\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)
both also dependent of the uniform shape regularity of the mesh family
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
.

Particularly for
k=1
, the term
C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}(\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+\|u-u_{k-2}\|_{a}^{2})
of (
4.22
) will vanish.

Proof.

For the elementwise error estimator (
3.11
), the following inequality

(b_{1}^{2}+c_{1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}-(b_{2}^{2}+c_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant(b_{1}-b_{2})+(c_{1}-c_{2}),\quad\forall\;b_{1},b_{2},c_{1},c_{2}\geqslant 0,

implies that

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(u_{k},T)-\eta_{k}(u_{k-1},T)\leqslant~

\displaystyle h_{T}\|R_{T}(u_{k})-R_{T}(u_{k-1})\|_{0,T}+\sum_{E\subset\partial T\cap\Omega}h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|J_{E}(u_{k})-J_{E}(u_{k-1})\|_{0,E}.

(4.23)

By the definition (
3.9
) of
R_{T}(\cdot)
and the inverse estimate, the first term in the right-hand side of (
4.23
) is bounded by

\displaystyle h_{T}\|R_{T}(u_{k})-R_{T}(u_{k-1})\|_{0,T}\leqslant~

\displaystyle h_{T}\Big(\|(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha})\cdot\nabla(u_{k}-u_{k-1})\|_{0,T}+\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}:\nabla^{2}(u_{k}-u_{k-1})\|_{0,T}

\displaystyle+\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla(u_{k-1}^{*}-u_{k-2}^{*})\|_{0,T}+\|\gamma(u_{k-1}^{*}-u_{k-2}^{*})\|_{0,T}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim~

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{k-1})\|_{0,T}+\|u_{k-1}^{*}-u_{k-2}^{*}\|_{0,T}

(4.24)

for
k\geqslant 2
, where
\nabla^{2}(\cdot)
denotes the Hessian matrix. Note that, for
k=1
, the last term in the right-hand side of (
4.24
) will vanish due to
u_{0}^{*}=u_{0}+e_{0,0}=u_{0}
from Remark
3.2
.

For the estimation of the second term in the right-hand side of (
4.23
), we note that for any side
E\in\partial T\cap\Omega
, there exists an element
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
such that
T^{\prime}\cap T=E
. By the definition (
3.10
) of the jump, the trace theorem and the inverse estimate, we show that

\displaystyle h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|J_{E}(u_{k})-J_{E}(u_{k-1})\|_{0,E}\leqslant~

\displaystyle h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla(u_{k}-u_{k-1})|_{\partial T\cap E}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{T}\|_{0,E}+\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\nabla(u_{k}-u_{k-1})|_{\partial T^{\prime}\cap E}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{T^{\prime}}\|_{0,E})

\displaystyle\lesssim~

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{k-1})\|_{0,T\cup T^{\prime}}.

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\partial T\cap\Omega}h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|J_{E}(u_{k})-J_{E}(u_{k-1})\|_{0,E}\lesssim~

\displaystyle(d+1)\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{k-1})\|_{0,\omega_{T}},

(4.25)

where
\omega_{T}
denotes the set of elements sharing the edge
E\in\partial T\cap\Omega
with the element
T
.
Substituting (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) into (
4.23
) yields that

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(u_{k},T)\leqslant~

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(u_{k-1},T)+C_{1}\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{k-1})\|_{0,\omega_{T}}+C_{2}\|u_{k-1}^{*}-u_{k-2}^{*}\|_{0,T}

for
k\geqslant 2
with positive constants
C_{1}=C_{1}(d,\Omega,\boldsymbol{\alpha})
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(\boldsymbol{\beta},\gamma)
both also dependent of the uniform shape regularity of the mesh family
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
, where the last term in the above right-hand side will vanish for
k=1
.

Applying the Young inequality with
\varepsilon>0
to the square of the above inequality, summing the arising result over all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
and using the norm equivalence (
2.8
) as well as (
4.1
) of Lemma
4.1
lead to that

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k})\leqslant~

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k-1})+2(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\Big((d+2)C_{1}^{2}\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{k-1})\|_{0}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\|u_{k-1}^{*}-u_{k-2}^{*}\|_{0}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant~

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k-1})+2(1+\varepsilon^{-1})(d+2)C_{1}^{2}C(\Omega,\boldsymbol{\alpha})\|u_{k}-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle+4(1+\varepsilon^{-1})C_{2}^{2}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}(\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+\|u-u_{k-2}\|_{a}^{2})

(4.26)

for
k\geqslant 2
, where we use the fact that the sum of norms on
\omega_{T}
covers each element at least
d+2
times. While the term
4(1+\varepsilon^{-1})C_{2}^{2}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}(\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+\|u-u_{k-2}\|_{a}^{2})
in (
4.26
) will vanish for
k=1
.

Furthermore, it is easy to obtain that

\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k-1})\leqslant\rho_{\mathrm{red}}\eta_{k-1}^{2}(u_{k-1}),

(4.27)

with the constant
\rho_{\mathrm{red}}:=1-2^{-1/d}\theta
. Subsituting (
4.27
) into (
4.26
) and taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small such that

\rho_{\mathrm{est}}:=(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{\mathrm{red}}\in(0,1)
conclude the proof.
∎

4.5
Contraction and convergence

Now, by Lemma
4.2
, Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
, we are in a position to prove the following contraction result of the quasi-error, which shows that some sum of quasi-errors on the successively two levels is contractive.

Theorem 4.1

(Contraction)
.

Let
u
be the solution of the weak formulation (
2.5
), and
\{u_{k},\eta_{k}(u_{k})\}_{k\geqslant 0}
be the sequence of the discrete solution
and the error estimator obtained by Algorithm
3.5
. Then, there exist constants
\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}=\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}(\rho_{\mathrm{est}},C_{\mathrm{est}},C_{\mathrm{rem}},C_{\mathrm{rel}},\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{rel}},c_{\mathrm{qo}},\kappa(h_{0}))\in(0,1)
and
\tau>0
having the same dependence as
\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}
such that

E_{k,\sigma}^{2}+\tau\kappa(h_{0})^{2}E_{k-1,\sigma}^{2}\leqslant\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{2}(E_{k-1,\sigma}^{2}+\tau\kappa(h_{0})^{2}E_{k-2,\sigma}^{2}),

(4.28)

where
E_{k,\sigma}^{2}=\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}+\sigma\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k})
with the constant
\sigma=C_{\mathrm{est}}^{-1}
. In particular, there holds

E_{1,\sigma}^{2}\leqslant\rho_{0}^{2}E_{0,\sigma}^{2},

(4.29)

with the constant
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(\rho_{\mathrm{est}},C_{\mathrm{est}},C_{\mathrm{rel}},c_{\mathrm{qo}},\kappa(h_{0}))
.

Proof.

For convenience, we write
\eta_{k}(u_{k})
to
\eta_{k}
. Adding the quasi-orthogonality (
4.11
) and the
\sigma=C_{\mathrm{est}}^{-1}
multiple of the estimator reduction (
4.22
) leads to that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}+\sigma\eta_{k}^{2}\leqslant~

\displaystyle\Big(\dfrac{1}{1-c_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})}+\sigma C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}\Big)\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+\sigma\rho_{\mathrm{est}}\eta_{k-1}^{2}+\sigma C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}\|u-u_{k-2}\|_{a}^{2}.

(4.30)

Introducing a free parameter
\nu>0
and invoking the reliability (
4.17
) yield that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}+\sigma\eta_{k}^{2}\leqslant~

\displaystyle\Big(\dfrac{1}{1-c_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})}+\dfrac{C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}-\nu}{C_{\mathrm{est}}}\Big)\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2}+\sigma(\rho_{\mathrm{est}}+2C_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}\nu)\eta_{k-1}^{2}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{C_{\mathrm{rem}}+2\nu\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}}{C_{\mathrm{est}}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}\|u-u_{k-2}\|_{a}^{2}.

(4.31)

Noting that
\rho_{\mathrm{est}}\in(0,1)
, we can choose
\nu>0
sufficiently small such that

\rho_{\mathrm{est}}+2C_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}\nu\in(0,1).

Also, we can make
\kappa(h_{0})
sufficiently small (i.e., the initial mesh size
h_{0}
is sufficiently small) such that
C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}<\nu
and

\dfrac{1}{1-c_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})}+\dfrac{C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}-\nu}{C_{\mathrm{est}}}\in(0,1).

Therefore, it holds

E_{k,\sigma}^{2}\leqslant\rho^{2}E_{k-1,\sigma}^{2}+C_{\mathrm{ctr}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}\|u-u_{k-2}\|_{a}^{2},

(4.32)

where
E_{k,\sigma}^{2}:=\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}+\sigma\eta_{k}^{2}
, and the constants

\displaystyle\rho^{2}

\displaystyle:=\max\{\dfrac{1}{1-c_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})}+\dfrac{C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}-\nu}{C_{\mathrm{est}}},\rho_{\mathrm{est}}+2C_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}\nu\}\in(0,1),

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{ctr}}

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{C_{\mathrm{rem}}+2\nu\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}}{C_{\mathrm{est}}}.

In particular, for the above deduction for
k=1
, the last term in the right-hand side of (
4.32
) will vanish due to Lemma
4.5
for
k=1
and the constant
\rho^{2}
will become

\rho_{0}^{2}:=\max\left\{\dfrac{1}{1-c_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})}-C_{\mathrm{est}}^{-1}\nu,~\rho_{\mathrm{est}}+2C_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}\nu\right\}\in(0,1).

In order to obtain the following contraction

E_{k,\sigma}^{2}+\tau\kappa(h_{0})^{2}E_{k-1,\sigma}^{2}\leqslant\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{2}\left(E_{k-1,\sigma}^{2}+\tau\kappa(h_{0})^{2}E_{k-2,\sigma}^{2}\right),

where the constants
\tau>0
and
\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}\in(0,1)
will be determined, we know that

\rho^{2}=\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{2}-\tau\kappa(h_{0})^{2},\quad\text{and}\quad C_{\mathrm{ctr}}=\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{2}\tau,

which implies that

\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{2}=\dfrac{\rho^{2}+\sqrt{\rho^{4}+4C_{\mathrm{ctr}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}}}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\tau=\dfrac{2C_{\mathrm{ctr}}}{\rho^{2}+\sqrt{\rho^{4}+4C_{\mathrm{ctr}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}}}.

The sufficiently small
\kappa(h_{0})
can guarantee
\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}\in(0,1)
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Although the contraction (
4.28
) of the CATGFEM is a weaker result compared with the classical contraction of the SAFEM that is the quasi-error is constractive with respect to the adaptivity iteration counter
k
, it is a necessary relaxation since note that the CATGFEM is based on the efficient inexact numerical solver while the SAFEM is based on the inefficient exact solver.

If the initial mesh size
h_{0}
is sufficiently small such that

\kappa(h_{0})\|u-u_{k-2}\|_{a}^{2}\leqslant\|u-u_{k-1}\|_{a}^{2},\quad\forall\;k\geqslant 2,

(4.33)

holds, then (
4.32
) will become the classical contraction

E_{k,\sigma}^{2}\leqslant\rho_{1}^{2}E_{k-1,\sigma}^{2},

(4.34)

with the constant

\rho_{1}^{2}:=\max\left\{\dfrac{1}{1-c_{\mathrm{qo}}\kappa(h_{0})}+\dfrac{C_{\mathrm{rem}}\kappa(h_{0})^{2}-\nu}{C_{\mathrm{est}}}+C_{\mathrm{ctr}}\kappa(h_{0}),~\rho_{\mathrm{est}}+2C_{\mathrm{rel}}^{2}\nu\right\}\in(0,1).

Theorem
4.1
immediately derives the convergence of our proposed CATGFEM (Algorithm
3.5
).

Corollary 4.1

(Convergence)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
4.1
, then there holds

\|u-u_{k}\|_{a}^{2}+\sigma\eta_{k}^{2}(u_{k})\leqslant\rho_{\mathrm{conv}}^{2k}E_{0,\sigma}^{2}\rightarrow 0\;(k\rightarrow\infty),

with the constant
\rho_{\mathrm{conv}}^{2k}=\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{2k-2}\left(\rho_{0}^{2}+\tau\kappa(h_{0})^{2}\right)
, where the sufficiently small initial mesh size
h_{0}
can make the constant
\kappa(h_{0})
sufficiently small such that

\rho_{\mathrm{conv}}=\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}\sqrt[2k]{\frac{\rho_{0}^{2}+\tau\kappa(h_{0})^{2}}{\rho_{\mathrm{ctr}}^{2}}}\in(0,1).

Remark 4.5
.

In
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
, the convergence of the ATGFEM reads in our settings: there exist constants
\tilde{\sigma}>0
and
0<\tilde{\rho}<1
such that

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{a}^{2}+\tilde{\sigma}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{2}(\hat{u}_{k})\leqslant~

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{2k}\left(\|u-\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{a}^{2}+\tilde{\sigma}\tilde{\eta}_{k}^{2}(\tilde{u}_{k})\right)+C\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\|u-\tilde{u}_{k-1-i}\|_{0}^{2}\tilde{\rho}^{2i}+C\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\|u-\tilde{u}_{k-2-i}\|_{0}^{2}\tilde{\rho}^{2i},

(4.35)

where the two sums in the right-hand side of (
4.35
) are only empirically regarded as higher-order quantities, which is not theoretically justified. Therefore, our convergence result (
4.34
) is better and more rigorous than (
4.35
) due to no remainder terms.
This is essentially attributed to the key
L^{2}
-lifting property from Lemma
4.1
, which allows us to rigorously prove the contraction result (
4.28
) also without remainder terms.
