Nontrivial automorphisms of 𝒫 ⁡ ( ω ) / Fin \mathcal{P}(\omega)/\mathrm{Fin} in Cohen models Thanks: The first author is supported in part by NSF grant DMS-2154229
Will Brian, Alan Dow

Abstract
. We show that if κ < ℵ ω \kappa<\aleph_{\omega} Cohen reals are added to a model of 𝖢𝖧 \mathsf{CH} , then there are nontrivial automorphisms of 𝒫 ⁡ ( ω ) / Fin \mathcal{P}(\omega)/\mathrm{Fin} in the extension. Under some further hypotheses on the ground model, namely the existence of long enough sage Davies trees (which follows from 𝖲𝖢𝖧 \mathsf{SCH} plus □ λ \square_{\lambda} for every λ \lambda with cf ⁡ ( λ ) = ω \mathrm{cf}(\lambda)=\omega ), we prove the same result for cardinals κ ≥ ℵ ω \kappa\geq\aleph_{\omega} as well. This extends a result a Shelah and Steprāns, who proved the result for κ = ℵ 2 \kappa=\aleph_{2} .

1.
Introduction

Given a cardinal
\kappa\geq\aleph_{2}
, the “
\kappa
-Cohen model” means any model obtained by forcing over a model of
\mathsf{CH}
with the poset
\mathrm{Fn}(\kappa,2)
of finite partial functions
\kappa\to 2
, the usual poset for adding
\kappa
mutually generic Cohen reals.
In this paper we look at the question of whether the
\kappa
-Cohen model contains nontrivial automorphisms of the Boolean algebra
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
.
We prove that the answer is yes if
\kappa<\aleph_{\omega}
, and the answer is yes for all
\kappa
under some additional “
L
-like” hypotheses on the ground model.
Furthermore, if
\kappa
is regular then not only do these models contain nontrivial automorphisms, but they contain
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
of them, the maximum possible number.

Main Theorem
.

Assume
\mathsf{CH}
, let
\kappa\geq\aleph_{2}
, and let
\mathbb{P}=\mathrm{Fn}(\kappa,2)
.

(1)

If
\kappa<\aleph_{\omega}
, then every automorphism of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V
extends to
2^{\kappa}
automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V^{\mathbb{P}}
.

(2)

More generally, if
\kappa
is a regular cardinal and there is a sage Davies tree of length
\kappa
, then every automorphism of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V
extends to
2^{\kappa}
automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V^{\mathbb{P}}
.

(3)

If
\kappa
is a singular cardinal and there is a sage Davies tree of length
\nu=\kappa^{\omega}
, then there are nontrivial automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V^{\mathbb{P}}
.

A Davies tree is a sequence
\left\langle M_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
of elementary submodels of some large fragment
H_{\theta}
of the set-theoretic universe such that the
M_{\alpha}
enjoy certain coherence and covering properties. (These sequences are called “trees” because they are constructed by cleverly enumerating the leaves of a tree of elementary submodels of
H_{\theta}
.) These structures provide a unified framework for carrying out a wide variety of constructions in infinite combinatorics. They were introduced by R. O. Davies in
[
3
]
, and an excellent survey of their many uses can be found in
[
22
]
.
A version of Davies trees using countably closed models, called sage Davies trees, is introduced in
[
22
]
.
Their construction uses the singular cardinals hypothesis
\mathsf{SCH}
below
\kappa
(the length of the sequence), plus
\square_{\lambda}
for limit cardinals
\lambda<\kappa
of countable cofinality.
As these hypotheses are vacuously true for
\kappa<\aleph_{\omega}
, sage Davies trees of length
\kappa<\aleph_{\omega}
can be proved to exist from
\mathsf{ZFC}
alone.
In particular, part
(1)
of our main theorem is a direct consequence of
(2)
; we have stated it separately simply to emphasize that
(1)
is a theorem of
\mathsf{ZFC}
, while the more general
(2)
requires an additional hypothesis when
\kappa\geq\aleph_{\omega}
.
As shown in
[
22
]
, it is consistent relative to a supercompact cardinal that there are no sage Davies trees of length
\geq\aleph_{\omega}
(a large cardinal hypothesis is necessary because the failure of either
\mathsf{SCH}
or of
\square_{\lambda}
has large cardinal strength).
We do not know whether the general conclusion of
(2)
is a theorem of
\mathsf{ZFC}
.

The
\kappa=\aleph_{2}
case of our main theorem was proved by
Shelah and Steprāns in
[
20
]
.
(Their theorem states only that the
\aleph_{2}
-Cohen model contains nontrivial automorphisms, but their argument can be modified to obtain
2^{\aleph_{2}}
automorphisms extending any given ground model automorphism.)
However, the Shelah-Steprāns proof does not work for
\kappa\geq\aleph_{3}
.

Roughly, the reason is that
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
has a special structure in the
\aleph_{2}
-Cohen model, a kind of “near-saturation” that enables some
\mathsf{CH}
-like constructions, or modifications of them, to go through in the
\aleph_{2}
-Cohen model (see
[
21
]
or
[
6
]
).
(Recall that
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
is a countably saturated Boolean algebra, as shown in
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
.
Under
\mathsf{CH}
, where
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
can be written as an increasing union of countable subalgebras, this enables us to recursively construct many automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
.)
The Shelah-Steprāns result in
[
20
]
predates
[
21
]
and
[
6
]
, but in hindsight, their argument utilizes aspects of this special structure in the
\aleph_{2}
-Cohen model.
And these nice features of the
\aleph_{2}
-Cohen model that drive the Shelah-Steprāns proof are simply lacking in the
\kappa
-Cohen model for larger
\kappa
.

In a bit more detail, the Shelah-Steprāns argument utilizes the fact that if
M\models\mathsf{ZFC}
, and
g
is a Cohen-generic real over
M
, then every real
x\in M[g]
cuts
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap M
in an
(\omega,\omega)
-gap.
This means that there are countable sets
L,U\subseteq\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap M
such that
x
is above every member of
L
and below every member of
U
, and meets every member of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap M
that is not below some
\ell\in L
or above some
u\in U
.
This fact is important when trying to build an automorphism of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
. To extend a partial automorphism
\Phi
with domain
D
to some
x\notin D
, we need to find a
y\in\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
such that if
x
cuts
D
in the gap
(L,U)
, then
y
cuts
\Phi[D]
in the gap
(\Phi[L],\Phi[U])
.
The natural forcing poset to fill an
(\omega,\omega)
-gap in
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
is countable, hence forcing equivalent to the Cohen poset
\mathrm{Fn}(\omega,2)
.
Therefore, if we have access to a real
h
Cohen-generic over
\Phi
, and
D
, then a generic for the poset that fills
(\Phi[L],\Phi[U])
can be defined from
h
.

Now let us consider the
\aleph_{2}
-Cohen model
V[G]
.
Because
\mathrm{Fn}(\omega_{2},2)
is equivalent to a length-
\omega_{2}
finite support iteration of
\mathrm{Fn}(\omega_{1},2)
, we can write
V[G]
as an increasing union
V[G]=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{2}}V[G_{\alpha}]
, where each
V[G_{\alpha}]
is a model of
\mathsf{CH}
and contains
\aleph_{1}
“fresh” Cohen-generic reals.
To build an automorphism
\Phi
in
V[G]
, we begin with some automorphism
\Phi_{0}
of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V
and recursively extend it to each
V[G_{\alpha}]
. At successor steps, to extend
\Phi
from
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap V[G_{\alpha}]
to
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap V[G_{\alpha+1}]
, we do the following:

\circ

Using the fact that
V[G_{\alpha+1}]\models\mathsf{CH}
, enumerate
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap V[G_{\alpha+1}]
in order type
\omega_{1}
, say
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap V[G_{\alpha+1}]=\{b_{\xi}:\,\xi<\omega_{1}\}
.

\circ

Stepping through the
b_{\xi}
one at a time, at each step we add one or two new reals to the domain of
\Phi
, and close the domain under Boolean operations. So at step
\xi
, the domain of
\Phi
is countably generated over
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap V[G_{\alpha}]
. From this and the comments above, it follows that the new real
b_{\xi}
cuts the domain of
\Phi
in an
(\omega,\omega)
-gap
(L,U)
.

\circ

Because
V[G_{\alpha+1}]
contains
\aleph_{1}
“fresh” Cohen reals over
V[G_{\alpha}]
, some of these are still untouched and can be used to fill the
(\omega,\omega)
-gap
(\Phi[L],\Phi[U])
and find an image for
b_{\xi}
. After adding
b_{\xi}
to the domain of
\Phi
, it can be added to the image of
\Phi
in a similar fashion.

If
\kappa\geq\aleph_{3}
then this argument breaks down. In this case
V[G]
is no longer a union of intermediate models
V[G_{\alpha}]
satisfying
\mathsf{CH}
.
Yet in the preceding argument, it was essential that when extending
\Phi
from
V[G_{\alpha}]
to
V[G_{\alpha+1}]
that the new reals be enumerated in type
\omega_{1}
.
Without this, we cannot keep the domain of
\Phi
countably generated over
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap V[G_{\alpha}]
, and there is no reason to think a new real
b_{\xi}
should cut the domain of
\Phi
in an
(\omega,\omega)
-gap.

In order to extend this argument to higher Cohen models, we need a sequence
\left\langle B_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
of subalgebras of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
such that

\circ

each
B_{\alpha+1}
is
\omega_{1}
-generated over
B_{\alpha}
(so the generators can be dealt with in order type
\omega_{1}
), and

\circ

each
B_{\alpha+1}
is able to generically fill all the
(\omega,\omega)
-gaps that arise in the process of extending our automorphism.

The main insight of this paper is that such a sequence of subalgebras is provided by forcing over a sage Davies tree.
Given a sage Davies tree
\left\langle M_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
in the ground model, we set
B_{\alpha}=\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}[G]
.
It is not too hard to show that each
B_{\alpha+1}
is
\omega_{1}
-generated over
B_{\alpha}
and contains
\aleph_{1}
“fresh” Cohen-generic reals over
B_{\alpha}
.
The difficulty lies in making sure that
M_{\alpha+1}[G]
is able to utilize these Cohen reals to fill all the
(\omega,\omega)
-gaps that arise while trying to extend
\Phi
from
B_{\alpha}
to
B_{\alpha+1}
.
For this to happen, we must ensure these gaps, and their images under
\Phi
, are members of
M_{\alpha+1}[G]
, since then
M_{\alpha+1}[G]
can compute sufficiently generic filters on the required gap-filling posets.

Section 2 describes what becomes of a sage Davies tree after a
\kappa
-Cohen forcing. While the resulting sequence of models is no longer a sage Davies tree, it remembers enough of its original structure to provide a useful combinatorial tool in the extension.
In Section 3 we show how to use this tool to extend the Shelah-Steprāns argument from
[
20
]
and obtain many automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
.
Using remnants of sage Davies trees in Cohen models was first explored in
[
2
]
, though in a rather different way. Also, let us point out that Theorem
2.1
(2) is reminiscent of
[
10
,
9
]
, where it is shown that
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
has the weak Freese-Nation property in
\kappa
-Cohen models under essentially the same hypotheses as in our main theorem.

2.
Sage Davies trees after Cohen forcing

In what follows,
H_{\theta}
denotes the set of all sets hereditarily smaller than some big enough cardinal
\theta
. (Taking
\theta=(2^{\kappa})^{+}
suffices in all that follows.) Given two sets
M
and
N
, we write
M\prec N
to mean that
(M,\in)
is an elementary submodel of
(N,\in)
. A set
M
is
countably closed
if
M^{\omega}\subseteq M
. If
M
satisfies (enough of)
\mathsf{ZFC}
, this is equivalent to the property
[M]^{\omega}\subseteq M
.

Let
\kappa
be a cardinal with
\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)>\omega
. A
sage Davies tree for
\kappa
over a set
P
(a parameter) is a sequence
\left\langle M_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
of elementary submodels of
H_{\theta}
, for some “big enough” regular cardinal
\theta
, such that

(1)

Each
M_{\alpha}
is countably closed, and
|M_{\alpha}|=\aleph_{1}
, and
P\in M_{\alpha}
.

(2)

[\kappa]^{\omega}\subseteq\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}M_{\alpha}
.

(3)

For each
\alpha<\kappa
, there is a countable collection
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}
of countably closed elementary submodels of
H_{\theta}
, each containing
P
, such that

\textstyle\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\,=\,\bigcup\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}.

(4)

For each
\alpha<\kappa
,
\left\langle M_{\xi}\colon\xi<\alpha\right\rangle\in M_{\alpha}
.

(5)

\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}M_{\alpha}
is a countably closed elementary submodel of
H_{\theta}
.

(6)

Furthermore, if
\kappa
is a regular cardinal then

\textstyle\left\{\delta\in\kappa\,\colon\delta=\kappa\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\delta}M_{\xi}\right\}

is a closed and unbounded subset of
\kappa
.

The definition of sage Davies trees in
[
22
]
lists only properties
(1)-(5)
. We have taken the liberty of adding
(6)
into the definition because it follows easily from the construction of the sage Davies trees in
[
22
, Section 14]
.
Also, though we have stated the full definition from
[
22
]
in
(1)-(5)
, property
(5)
is not used anywhere in what follows.

Let
\mathrm{Fn}(A,2)
denote the usual Cohen forcing consisting of all the finite partial functions
A\to 2
, ordered by extension.
Let
\mathrm{supp}(q)
denote the domain of a condition
q\in\mathrm{Fn}(A,2)
, and if
\dot{x}
is an
\mathrm{Fn}(A,2)
-name then let
\mathrm{supp}(\dot{x})=\bigcup\left\{\mathrm{supp}(q)\colon q\in\mathrm{Fn}(A,2)\cap\mathrm{TC}(\dot{x})\right\}
(where
\mathrm{TC}(\dot{x})
denotes the transitive closure of
\dot{x}
).

Given a forcing poset
\mathbb{P}
, recall that a
nice
\mathbb{P}
-name
for a subset of
\omega
is a subset
\dot{x}
of
\check{\omega}\times\mathbb{P}
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left\{p\colon(\check{n},p)\in\dot{x}\right\}
is an antichain in
\mathbb{P}
.
For convenience, we will write
n
for
\check{n}
, and consider a nice name to be a subset of
\omega\times\mathbb{P}
.
The evaluation of a nice name
\dot{x}
with respect to a filter
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is denoted

\mathrm{val}_{G}(\dot{x})=\left\{n\in\omega\colon(n,p)\in\dot{x}\text{ for some }p\in G\right\}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\kappa
be an infinite cardinal with
\kappa^{\omega}=\kappa
,
and suppose
\left\langle M_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
is a sage Davies tree for
\kappa
. If
G
is
\mathrm{Fn}(\kappa,2)
-generic over
V
, then the sequence
\left\langle M_{\alpha}[G]\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
in
V[G]
satisfies:

(1)

\left\lvert\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\right\rvert=\aleph_{1}
for each
\alpha<\kappa\vphantom{f^{f}}
.
Consequently, there is a function
C:\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\to\mathcal{P}(\omega)

such that for all
\alpha<\kappa
,
C\in M_{\alpha}[G]
and
C
maps
\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\cap M_{\alpha}
to a set of
\aleph_{1}
reals mutually Cohen generic over
V\big[G\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\big]
.

(2)

For each
\vphantom{f^{f^{f}}}\alpha<\kappa
and
r\in\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap M_{\alpha}[G]\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}[G]
, there are countable sets
L^{r},U^{r}\subseteq\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}[G]
such that for every
x\in\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}[G]
,

\circ

\vphantom{f^{f}}x\leq r\ \,\Leftrightarrow\ \,x\leq\ell\text{ for some }\ell\in L^{r}
,

\circ

r\leq x\ \,\Leftrightarrow\,\ u\leq x\text{ for some }u\in U^{r}
.

Furthermore, there are some such sets
\vphantom{f^{f^{f}}}L^{r},U^{r}\in M_{\alpha}[G]
.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha<\kappa
.
To prove the first claim, we begin by working in
V
.
As
|M_{\xi}|=\aleph_{1}
for each
\xi<\alpha
, we have
\left\lvert\kappa\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\right\rvert\leq|\alpha|\cdot\aleph_{1}<\kappa
, which implies
\left\lvert\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\right\rvert=\kappa
.
By part
(4)
of our definition of sage Davies trees,
\left\langle M_{\xi}\colon\xi<\alpha\right\rangle\in M_{\alpha}
, and (because
\kappa\in M_{\alpha}
and
M_{\alpha}
is closed under basic set-theoretic operations)
\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\in M_{\alpha}
.
By elementarity,
M_{\alpha}\models\left\lvert\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\right\rvert=\kappa
, and in particular there is a bijection
f\in M_{\alpha}
from
\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}
to
\kappa
. Restricting
f
to
M_{\alpha}
gives a bijection from
\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}
to
\kappa\cap M_{\alpha}
, and
|\kappa\cap M_{\alpha}|=\aleph_{1}
. (In fact, standard arguments show that
\omega_{1}\subseteq M
whenever
M
is a countably closed model of enough of
\mathsf{ZFC}
.)
Thus
\left\lvert\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\right\rvert=\aleph_{1}
.

Because
M_{\alpha}
is closed under basic set-theoretic operations, an ordinal
\lambda\in M_{\alpha}
if and only if
\lambda+n\in M_{\alpha}
for all
n\in\omega
. Thus for
\lambda\in\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)
,

\textstyle\lambda\in M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad[\lambda,\lambda+\omega)\subseteq M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}.

Still working in the ground model, for each
\lambda\in\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)
let
c_{\lambda}
denote the natural isomorphism
\mathrm{Fn}\big([\lambda,\lambda+\omega),2\big)\to\mathrm{Fn}(\omega,2)
.

In
V[G]
, each
c_{\lambda}
maps
G\cap\mathrm{Fn}\big([\lambda,\lambda+\omega),2\big)
to a generic-over-
V
subset of
\mathrm{Fn}(\omega,2)
, whose union is a Cohen-generic subset of
\omega
, say
g_{\lambda}
. Define
C(\lambda)=g_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)
. This definition of
C
has only
G
as a parameter, so
C\in M_{\alpha}[G]
for all
\alpha<\kappa
, and it is clear that
C
maps
\mathrm{Lim}(\kappa)\cap M_{\alpha}
to a set of
\aleph_{1}
reals mutually Cohen generic over
V\big[G\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\big]
.

Before proving the second claim we must introduce a few definitions. For convenience, let
\mathbb{P}=\mathrm{Fn}(\kappa,2)
, and given
S\subseteq\kappa
, let
\mathbb{P}_{S}=\mathrm{Fn}(S,2)
.
Let
G_{S}=G\cap\mathbb{P}_{S}
.
Note that if
\kappa\supseteq T\supseteq S
then
\mathbb{P}_{T}=\mathbb{P}_{S}*\mathbb{P}_{T\setminus S}
,
G_{T\setminus S}
is
\mathbb{P}_{T\setminus S}
-generic over
V[G_{S}]
, and
V[G_{T}]=V[G_{S}][G_{T\setminus S}]
.
If
\dot{x}
is a nice
\mathbb{P}
-name for a subset of
\omega
and
S\subseteq\kappa
, define the
partial evaluation
of
\dot{x}
with respect ot
G_{S}
to be

\mathrm{pval}_{S}(\dot{x})\,=\,\left\{\big(n,p\!\restriction\!(\kappa\setminus S)\big)\colon(n,p)\in\dot{x}\text{ and }p\!\restriction\!S\in G_{S}\right\}.

For example, if
\dot{x}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{S}
-name, then
p\!\restriction\!(\kappa\setminus S)=\emptyset
whenever
(n,p)\in\dot{x}
, in which case

\mathrm{pval}_{S}(\dot{x})\,=\,\left\{(n,\emptyset)\colon(n,p)\in\dot{x}\text{ and }p\in G_{S}\right\},

which is just the canonical name
\check{y}
for
y=\mathrm{val}_{G_{S}}(\dot{x})=\mathrm{val}_{G}(\dot{x})
.
At the other extreme, if
\dot{x}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa\setminus S}
-name then
p\!\restriction\!(\kappa\setminus S)=p
and
p\!\restriction\!S=\emptyset\in G
whenever
(n,p)\in\dot{x}
, so that
\mathrm{pval}(\dot{x})=\dot{x}
.
Observe that for any nice
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{x}
and any
S\subseteq\kappa
,
\mathrm{pval}_{S}(\dot{x})
is a nice
\mathbb{P}_{\kappa\setminus S}
-name for a subset of
\omega
, and
\emptyset\Vdash\mathrm{val}_{G}(\dot{x})=\mathrm{val}_{G_{\kappa\setminus S}}\big(\mathrm{pval}_{G_{S}}(\dot{x})\big)
.

We now proceed to the proof of the second claim.
Fix
\alpha<\kappa
.
By part
(3)
of the definition of a sage Davies tree, there is a countable set
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}
of countably closed elementary submodels of
H_{\theta}
with
\bigcup\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}
.

Claim
.

Let
r\in\mathcal{P}(\omega)\cap M_{\alpha}[G]\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}[G]
, let
\dot{r}\in M_{\alpha}
be a nice name for
r
, and let
N\in\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}
.
For each
p\in\mathbb{P}
, there is a nice name
\dot{\ell}\in N
for the set

\ell^{r}_{p,N}\,=\,\textstyle\bigcup\left\{\mathrm{val}_{G}(\dot{x})\colon\dot{x}\text{ is a nice $\mathbb{P}$-name in $N$ and }p\Vdash\dot{x}\subseteq\dot{r}\right\}.

Consequently,
\ell^{r}_{p,N}\in N[G]
.

Proof of claim:

Fix
p\in\mathbb{P}
.
Working in
V
, let
X
denote the set of all nice names
\dot{x}
for subsets of
\omega
such that
\dot{x}\in N
and
p\Vdash\dot{x}\subseteq\dot{r}
.
Let

\dot{\ell}_{0}\,=\,\textstyle\bigcup X\,=\,\left\{(n,q)\colon\text{there is some }\dot{x}\in X\text{ with }(n,q)\in\dot{x}\right\}.

If
\dot{x}\in X
then
\dot{x}\in N

3.
Building the automorphisms

An
almost permutation
on
\omega
is a bijection between cofinite subsets of
\omega
.
Every permutation of
\omega
is an almost permutation, but not every almost permutation is a permutation or even the restriction of one (e.g. consider the successor function
n\mapsto n+1
).
Every almost permutation
f
of
\omega
induces an automorphism
\alpha_{f}
of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
, defined by the formula

\alpha_{f}\big([A]_{\mathrm{Fin}}\big)\,=\,\big[f[A]\big]_{\mathrm{Fin}}

for every
A\subseteq\omega
.
An automorphism of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
that is induced in this way by an almost permutation of
\omega
is called
trivial
.
Note that as there are only
\mathfrak{c}
functions
\omega\to\omega
, there are only
\mathfrak{c}
trivial automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
. Consequently, if there are more than
\mathfrak{c}
automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
, then some (most) are nontrivial.

Walter Rudin proved in
[
15
]
that
\mathsf{CH}
implies there are
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
.
Years later, Shelah proved in
[
17
, Chapter 4]
that it is consistent with
\mathsf{ZFC}
that all automorphisms are trivial. Shelah’s result was extended and refined in
[
19
]
,
[
23
]
, and
[
4
]
, revealing ultimately that the forcing axiom
\mathsf{OCA}
(also known as
\mathsf{OGA}
or
\mathsf{OCA}_{T}
) implies all automorphisms are trivial.
Further models in which all automorphisms are trivial include models with
\mathfrak{c}>\aleph_{2}
in
[
5
]
or with
\mathfrak{d}=\aleph_{1}
in
[
7
]
. (Recall that
\mathsf{OCA}
implies
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_{2}
).

The following theorem covers parts
(1)
and
(2)
of the main theorem stated in the introduction.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
V\models\mathsf{CH}
, let
\kappa\geq\aleph_{2}
be a regular cardinal, and let
G
be a
\mathrm{Fn}(\kappa,2)
-generic filter over
V
.
Assume that for any
P
there is a sage Davies tree of length
\kappa
over
P
.
If
\phi
is an automorphism of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V
, then in
V[G]
there are
2^{\kappa}
automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
extending
\phi
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be an automorphism of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V
, and (still in
V
) fix a well ordering
R
of
H_{\kappa}
.
Let
F
be a set of
2^{\kappa}
functions
\kappa\to 2
with the property that if
h,h^{\prime}\in F
and
h\neq h^{\prime}
, then
\left\{\alpha\in\kappa\colon h(\alpha)\neq h^{\prime}(\alpha)\right\}
is stationary.
(To see that there is such a set of functions, first recall that there is a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
\kappa
into
\kappa

F
to be the set of all functions
h:\kappa\to 2
such that

.)

To prove the theorem, we construct for every
h\in F
an automorphism
\Phi_{h}
of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V[G]
such that
\Phi_{h}\!\restriction\!V=\phi
, and furthermore, we do this in such a way that
h\neq h^{\prime}
implies
\Phi_{h}\neq\Phi_{h^{\prime}}
.
Fix
h\in F
, and let
\left\langle M_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
be a sage Davies tree over
P=(R,h,\phi)
.

Let
C
denote the function described in Theorem
2.1
(1)
.
For each
\alpha<\kappa
, let
\delta_{\alpha}=\min\big(\kappa\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}\big)
.
Note that
\delta_{\alpha}
is a limit ordinal, and because
\delta_{\alpha}
and
\delta_{\alpha}+\omega
are definable from each other,
\delta+\omega\in\kappa\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}
as well.
Define
c^{\alpha}_{0}=C(\delta_{\alpha})
and
c^{\alpha}_{1}=C(\delta_{\alpha}+\omega)
for all
\alpha<\kappa
.

Define
B_{0}=\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap V
, and if
0<\alpha<\kappa
then define

B_{\alpha}\,=\,\textstyle\big\langle\hskip-3.41432pt\big\langle\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}[G]\,\big\rangle\hskip-3.41432pt\big\rangle,

the subalgebra of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
generated by
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}[G]
.
The fact that
M_{0}
is countably closed and
M_{0}\preceq H_{\theta}
implies
\mathcal{P}(\omega)\cap V\subseteq M_{0}
.
Hence
B_{0}\subseteq B_{1}
, and it is clear that if
\alpha<\beta<\kappa
then
B_{\alpha}\subseteq B_{\beta}
.
Thus
\left\langle B_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
is an increasing sequence of subalgebras of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
, and (it follows easily from the definition that)
B_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}B_{\xi}
for limit
\alpha
.

We claim furthermore that
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}B_{\alpha}
.
To see this, it suffices to show, working in
V
, that every nice
\mathbb{P}
-name for a subset of
\omega
is a member of
\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}M_{\alpha}
. If
\dot{x}
is such a name, then
\mathrm{supp}(\dot{x})
is a countable subset of
\kappa
.
By part
(2)
of the definition of a sage Davies tree, there is some
\alpha<\kappa
with
\mathrm{supp}(\dot{x})\in M_{\alpha}
.
Using the fact that
M_{\alpha}
is countably closed and
\omega\subseteq M_{\alpha}
, it follows that
\dot{x}\in M_{\alpha}
.

Suppressing the subscript for now, let us construct from
h
an automorphism
\Phi
of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V[G]
.
We aim to build
\Phi
by transfinite recursion, working in
V[G]
.
The recursion is organized into
\kappa
main stages.
At stage
\alpha
of the recursion we begin with an automorphism
\Phi_{\alpha}
of
B_{\alpha}
, and we wish to extend it to an automorphism
\Phi_{\alpha+1}
of
B_{\alpha+1}
while also encoding the value of
h(\alpha)
into
\Phi_{\alpha+1}
.
Through the recursion we maintain the following inductive hypothesis at stage
\alpha
:

(*)

the sequence of automorphisms
\left\langle\Phi_{\xi}\colon\xi\leq\alpha\right\rangle
is uniformly definable from
P
and
\left\langle M_{\xi}[G]\colon\xi<\alpha\right\rangle
. In particular
\Phi_{\alpha}\in M_{\alpha}
.

For the base case
\alpha=0
we begin with the automorphism
\Phi_{0}=\phi
of
B_{0}
.
Note that our inductive hypothesis is trivially satisfied at
\alpha=0
.
At limit stages we simply take
\Phi_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}\Phi_{\xi}
.
This is an automorphism of
B_{\alpha}
because
B_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}B_{\xi}
for limit
\alpha
. And this preserves our inductive hypothesis:
\left\langle\Phi_{\xi}\colon\xi\leq\alpha\right\rangle
is defined as
\left\langle\Phi_{\xi}\colon\xi<\alpha\right\rangle\!\,^{\frown}\langle\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}\Phi_{\xi}\rangle
, and this defining formula is uniform across all limit ordinals.

We must now describe how
\Phi_{\alpha+1}
is obtained from
\Phi_{\alpha}
. This single stage of the main recursion is itself accomplished by a transfinite recursion of length
\omega_{1}
.
Observe that
M_{\alpha}\in M_{\alpha+1}
(it is definable as the last element of
\left\langle M_{\xi}\colon\xi\leq\alpha\right\rangle\in M_{\alpha+1}
), which means that
M_{\alpha+1}
contains a canonical well ordering, given by

, of the nice names for subsets of
\omega
in
M_{\alpha}
.
Evaluating these names, and deleting repeats and members of
B_{\alpha}
(which is also definable in
M_{\alpha+1}
), this means
M_{\alpha+1}
contains a canonical enumeration
\left\langle b_{\xi}\colon\xi<\omega_{1}\right\rangle
of the members of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap M_{\alpha}[G]\setminus B_{\alpha}
.
Making some simple modifications to this well order if needed, we may (and do) assume

b_{0}=c^{\alpha}_{0}
.
The goal of our stage
\alpha
, length-
\omega_{1}
sub-recursion is to construct

(1)

two increasing sequences
\left\langle D_{\alpha}^{\beta}\colon\beta<\omega_{1}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle E_{\alpha}^{\beta}\colon\beta<\omega_{1}\right\rangle
of subalgebras of
B_{\alpha+1}
, such that
D_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\bigcup_{\xi<\beta}D_{\alpha}^{\xi}
and
E_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\bigcup_{\xi<\beta}E_{\alpha}^{\xi}
for limit
\beta
, and
\bigcup_{\beta<\omega_{1}}D_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\bigcup_{\beta<\omega_{1}}E_{\alpha}^{\beta}=B_{\alpha+1}
;

(2)

a sequence of injective homomorphisms
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}:D_{\alpha}^{\beta}\to E_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, for
\beta<\omega_{1}
, such that
\Phi_{\alpha}^{0}=\Phi_{\alpha}
and if
\beta<\beta^{\prime}
then
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta^{\prime}}\!\restriction\!D_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.

Furthermore, at each step of this sub-recursion on
\beta
we maintain the following inductive hypotheses:

(1)

D_{\alpha}^{\beta},E_{\alpha}^{\beta}\supseteq\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle B_{\alpha}\cup\left\{b_{\xi}\colon\xi<\beta\right\}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
.

(2)

There is a countable
X^{\beta}\subseteq M_{\alpha}[G]
such that
D_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle B_{\alpha}\cup X^{\beta}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
.

(3)

Furthermore, the sequence
\left\langle X^{\xi}\colon\xi\leq\beta\right\rangle
is uniformly definable from parameters in

, hence it is a member of
M_{\alpha}[G]
.

(4)

The sequence
\left\langle\Phi_{\alpha}^{\xi}\colon\xi\leq\beta\right\rangle

, hence a member of
M_{\alpha}[G]
.

For the base step, take
D_{\alpha}^{0}=E_{\alpha}^{0}=B_{\alpha}
and
\Phi_{\alpha}^{0}=\Phi_{\alpha}
.
This trivially satisfies hypotheses
(1)-(3)
. It satisfies
(4)

\Phi_{\alpha}\in M_{\alpha}[G]
by our larger inductive hypothesis
(*)

The limit steps are also easy: because
D_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\bigcup_{\xi<\beta}D_{\alpha}^{\xi}
and
E_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\bigcup_{\xi<\beta}E_{\alpha}^{\xi}
for limit
\beta
, we simply take
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\bigcup_{\xi<\beta}\Phi_{\alpha}^{\xi}
.
This preserves all four of the inductive hypotheses.

To obtain
\Phi^{1}_{\alpha}
, we do one of two things, depending on the value of
h(\alpha)
.
If
h(\alpha)=0
then we map
b_{0}=c^{\alpha}_{0}
to itself.
Because
c^{\alpha}_{0}
is Cohen-generic over

, it is free over
B_{\alpha}
. Consequently, the map
\Phi^{0}_{\alpha}\cup\{(c^{\alpha}_{0},c^{\alpha}_{0})\}
naturally extends to an automorphism
\Phi^{1}_{\alpha}
of
\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle B_{\alpha}\cup\{c^{\alpha}_{0}\}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
, and we set

.
If
h(\alpha)=1
, then we map
c^{\alpha}_{0}
to
c^{\alpha}_{1}
and
c^{\alpha}_{1}
to
c^{\alpha}_{0}
.
Because
c^{\alpha}_{0}
and
c^{\alpha}_{1}
are mutually Cohen-generic over

, each

is free over
\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle B_{\alpha}\cup\{c^{\alpha}_{1-i}\}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
.
Consequently, the map
\Phi^{0}_{\alpha}\cup\{(c^{\alpha}_{0},c^{\alpha}_{1}),(c^{\alpha}_{1},c^{\alpha}_{0})\}
naturally extends to an automorphism
\Phi^{1}_{\alpha}
of
\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle B_{\alpha}\cup\{c^{\alpha}_{0},c^{\alpha}_{1}\}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
, and we set
D^{1}_{\alpha}=E^{1}_{\alpha}=\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle B_{\alpha}\cup\{c^{\alpha}_{0},c^{\alpha}_{1}\}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
.
This clearly preserves the inductive hypotheses (bearing in mind, for inductive hypothesis
(4)

M_{\alpha}[G]
is closed under the Boolean operations of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
).

For the general successor step, fix
\beta
with
0<\beta<\omega_{1}
, and let
D_{\alpha}^{\beta}
,
E_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, and
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}
be given satisfying
(1)-(4)
.
We obtain
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta+1}
in two steps, first extending our algebras and mappings so that

gets into the domain of
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta+1}
, and extending again to put

into the image.
Specifically, we first extend
D_{\alpha}^{\beta},E_{\alpha}^{\beta},\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}
to some
\bar{D}_{\alpha}^{\beta},\bar{E}_{\alpha}^{\beta},\bar{\Phi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
with

,
and then we extend
\bar{D}_{\alpha}^{\beta},\bar{E}_{\alpha}^{\beta},\bar{\Phi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
to

with

.

If
b_{\beta}\in D_{\alpha}^{\beta}
already, then there is nothing to do in the first step: simply define
\bar{D}_{\alpha}^{\beta}=D_{\alpha}^{\beta}
,
\bar{E}_{\alpha}^{\beta}=E_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, and
\bar{\Phi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.

Suppose instead that
b_{\beta}\notin D_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.
Applying Theorem
2.1
, fix countable sets
L,U\subseteq\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}\cap\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}[G]

of the theorem with

Let
X^{\beta}
denote the set described in inductive hypotheses
(2)
and
(3)
,
and define

\displaystyle\tilde{L}

\displaystyle\,=\,\left\{\ell\in\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle L\cup X^{\beta}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle\colon\ell<b_{\beta}\right\},

\displaystyle\tilde{U}

\displaystyle\,=\,\left\{\ell\in\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle U\cup X^{\beta}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle\colon b_{\beta}<u\right\}.

These are countable subsets of

and, using the properties of

, if

then

\circ

x\leq b_{\beta}\ \,\Leftrightarrow\ \,x\leq\ell\text{ for some }\ell\in\tilde{L}

b_{\beta}\leq x\ \,\Leftrightarrow\,\ u\leq x\text{ for some }u\in\tilde{U}

In other words,
(\tilde{L},\tilde{U})
is an

.
It follows that

\big(\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{L}],\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{U}]\big)

If we are to extend

Because

By inductive hypothesis

\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{L}],\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{U}]\in M_{\alpha}[G]

Because

is countable and
D_{\alpha}^{\beta}=\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle B_{\alpha}\cup X^{\beta}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
, and because the members of the (uncountable) set

are all Cohen-generic over
B_{\alpha}
, all but countably many members of
C_{\alpha}
are Cohen-generic over
D_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.
Fix some such

.

The standard forcing poset used to split an
(\omega,\omega)
-gap in
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
is countable, hence forcing equivalent to the Cohen forcing
\mathrm{Fn}(\omega,2)
.
Because
c
is Cohen-generic over
E_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, and because
M_{\alpha}[G]
satisfies (enough of)
\mathsf{ZFC}
, this means that, working within
M_{\alpha}[G]
, it is possible to define a generic splitting
s_{\beta}\in M_{\alpha}[G]
of the
(\omega,\omega)
-gap

\big(\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{L}],\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{U}]\big)
in
E_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.
Furthermore, we may (and do) make the choice of
s_{\beta}
canonical by taking the
R
-least
c\in C_{\alpha}
(i.e., the one with the
R
-least name) generic over
D^{\beta}_{\alpha}
, the
R
-least dense embedding between
\mathrm{Fn}(\omega,2)
and
\big(\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{L}],\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{U}]\big)
, etc.
Because
s_{\beta}
splits the gap
\big(\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{L}],\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}[\tilde{U}]\big)
, the function
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}\cup\{(b_{\beta},s_{\beta})\}
extends to an isomorphism from
\bar{D}^{\beta}_{\alpha}=\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle D_{\alpha}^{\beta}\cup\{b_{\beta}\}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
to
\bar{E}^{\beta}_{\alpha}=\langle\hskip-2.56073pt\langle E_{\alpha}^{\beta}\cup\{s_{\beta}\}\rangle\hskip-2.56073pt\rangle
.
Let
\bar{\Phi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
denote this isomorphism.

The construction of
D_{\alpha}^{\beta+1}
,
E_{\alpha}^{\beta+1}
, and
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta+1}
from
\bar{D}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
,
\bar{E}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, and
\bar{\Phi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
works in exactly the same way, only using
\bar{E}_{\alpha}^{\beta}
in the place of
\bar{D}_{\alpha}^{b}
,
(\bar{\Phi}_{\alpha}^{\beta})^{-1}
in the place of
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, etc.

These choices clearly preserve inductive hypotheses
(1)
and
(2)
.
Because all the choices in this construction were made canonically via
R
, the preceding construction constitutes a definition of
D_{\alpha}^{\beta+1}
,
E_{\alpha}^{\beta+1}
, and
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta+1}
from
D_{\alpha}^{\beta}
,
E_{\alpha}^{\beta}
, and
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.
Moreover this definition is uniform, as it works the same way for all
\beta
.
It follows that
(3)
and
(4)
are preserved.

This concludes our length-
\omega_{1}
sub-recursion. Inductive hypothesis
(1)
makes it clear that
\bigcup_{\beta<\omega_{1}}D^{\beta}_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta<\omega_{1}}E^{\beta}_{\alpha}=B_{\alpha+1}
, and the fact that
\Phi^{\beta}_{\alpha}=\Phi^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\alpha}\!\restriction\!D_{\alpha}^{\beta}
whenever

shows that
\Phi_{\alpha+1}=\bigcup_{\beta<\omega_{1}}\Phi^{\beta}_{\alpha}
is an automorphism of
B_{\alpha+1}
.
Finally, the entire sub-recursion was defined from
M_{\alpha}[G]
,
C
, and
R
(or
P
).
Applying the Recursion Theorem in
M_{\alpha+1}[G]
, and using inductive hypothesis
(4)
, this means that our entire length-
\omega_{1}
sub-recursion, and its result
\Phi_{\alpha+1}
, are definable from
\left\langle M_{\xi}[G]\colon\xi\leq\alpha\right\rangle
and
R
in
M_{\alpha+1}[G]
. Furthermore, this definition is uniform across various
\alpha
(i.e., our description of the inductive step does not depend on
\alpha

(*)
is preserved at successor steps of the main recursion.

This completes the recursion, the result of which is an automorphism
\Phi=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}\Phi_{\alpha}
of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}B_{\alpha}
in
V[G]
that extends
\phi=\Phi_{0}
.
It remains to show that there are
2^{\kappa}
automorphisms like this.

Recall that our notation has been slightly dishonest: the construction above depends on a function
h\in F
, although we redacted
h
from
\Phi
for the sake of tidiness.
Restoring our subscript notation from the beginning of the proof, for every
h\in F
the preceding construction gives an automorphism
\Phi_{h}
of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in
V[G]
extending
\phi
.
To finish the proof, we must show that if
h,h^{\prime}\in F
and
h\neq h^{\prime}
, then
\Phi_{h}\neq\Phi_{h}^{\prime}
.

Given our construction, one might be tempted to think that
h
can be recovered from
\Phi_{h}
: if
\Phi_{h}(c^{\alpha}_{0})=c^{\alpha}_{0}
then
h(\alpha)=0
, and if
\Phi_{h}(c^{\alpha}_{0})\neq c^{\alpha}_{0}
then
h(\alpha)=1
.
However, the value of
c^{\alpha}_{0}
is determined note only by
C
, which does not depend on
h
, but also
M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}
, which does depend on
h
.
Recall that
h
was taken as part of the parameter
P
for the sage Davies tree
\left\langle M_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
used to obtain
\Phi_{h}
, which means that the identity of the
c^{\alpha}_{0}
’s depends on
h
.
Thus the proposed definition of
h
from
\Phi_{h}
is circular.

To get around this, we use part
(6)
of our definition of a sage Davies tree.
Let
\left\langle M_{\alpha}^{h}\colon\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
denote the sage Davies tree used to obtain
\Phi_{h}
, and
let
\big\langle M_{\alpha}^{h^{\prime}}:\,\alpha<\kappa\big\rangle
denote the sage Davies tree used to obtain
\Phi_{h^{\prime}}
.
Similarly, let

(c^{\alpha}_{0})^{h}\!=C\big(\!\min(\kappa\cap M_{\alpha}^{h}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}^{h})\big)
and

(c^{\alpha}_{0})^{h^{\prime}}\!=C\big(\!\min(\kappa\cap M_{\alpha}^{h^{\prime}}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}^{h^{\prime}})\big)
for all
\alpha<\kappa
.
Applying part
(6)
of our definition of sage Davies trees,

D\,=\,\left\{\delta\in\kappa\colon\delta=\kappa\cap\textstyle\bigcup_{\xi<\delta}M_{\xi}^{h}=\kappa\cap\textstyle\bigcup_{\xi<\delta}M_{\xi}^{h^{\prime}}\right\}

is closed and unbounded in
\kappa
.
If
\delta\in D
, then
(c^{\delta}_{0})^{h}=(c^{\delta}_{0})^{h^{\prime}}=C(\delta)
.

By our choice of
F
, the set
S=\left\{\alpha<\kappa\colon h(\alpha)\neq h^{\prime}(\alpha)\right\}
is stationary. In particular, there is some
\delta\in D
with
h(\delta)\neq h^{\prime}(\delta)
.
By the previous paragraph,
(c^{\delta}_{0})^{h}=(c^{\delta}_{0})^{h^{\prime}}=C(\delta)
.
But since
h(\delta)\neq h^{\prime}(\delta)
, one of the functions
\Phi_{h}
and
\Phi_{h^{\prime}}
has
C(\delta)
as a fixed point, and the other does not. Hence
\Phi_{h}\neq\Phi_{h^{\prime}}
.
∎

Proof of part
(3)
of the main theorem.

We now describe how to modify the preceding argument in order to accommodate singular
\kappa
.

First consider the case
\kappa^{\omega}=\kappa
. In this case Theorem
2.1
applies, and the proof of the previous theorem goes through unmodified except for the last 2 paragraphs, where we use part
(6)
of the definition of a sage Davies tree (the part of the definition that assumes
\kappa
is regular) in order to prove the
\Phi_{h}
are all distinct. When
\kappa
is singular we do not aim to prove all the
\Phi_{h}
are distinct, but only that at least one of them is a nontrivial automorphism.

Consider the automorphism
\Phi_{h}
constructed from the constant function
h(\alpha)=1
.
We claim that this function is a nontrivial automorphism of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
.
To see this, fix an almost bijection
f:\omega\to\omega
.
In the ground model
V
, there is a nice name
\dot{f}
for
f
with countable support
S
.
Setting
G_{S}=G\cap\mathrm{Fin}(S,2)
, we have
f\in V[G_{S}]
.
The reals
\left\{c^{\alpha}_{\xi}\colon\alpha<\kappa,\xi<\omega_{1}\right\}
are mutually Cohen-generic over
V
, and it follows that for all but countably many values of
\alpha
, the reals
c_{\alpha}^{0},c_{\alpha}^{1}
are mutually Cohen-generic over
V[G_{S}]
.
In particular, this implies that
f[c^{0}_{\alpha}]\neq^{*}c^{1}_{\alpha}
. Because
\Phi_{h}(c^{0}_{\alpha})=c^{1}_{\alpha}
, this means that
\Phi_{h}
is not induced by
f
.
As
f
was an arbitrary almost bijection of
\omega
, this shows
\Phi_{h}
is nontrivial.

Next consider the case
\kappa^{\omega}>\kappa
. In this case we must use a sage Davies tree of length
\nu=\kappa^{\omega}
rather than length
\kappa
.
Also in this case, the proof of Theorem
2.1
(1) does not work, although Theorem
2.1
(2) still goes through.

To work around not having access to Theorem
2.1
(1), we use a generalized almost disjoint family in
[\kappa]^{\omega}
. First, note that the set
\kappa^{<\omega}
of all finite sequences in
\kappa
is a set of size
\kappa
. Associating to each infinite sequence
s\in\kappa^{\omega}
its set of restrictions
A_{s}=\left\{s\!\restriction\!n\colon n\in\omega\right\}
, we see that there is a size-
\kappa^{\omega}
family
\left\{A_{s}\colon s\in\kappa^{\omega}\right\}
of pairwise almost disjoint countable subsets of
\kappa^{<\omega}
.

Fix a bijection
\psi
between
\nu=\kappa^{\omega}
and some size-
\kappa^{\omega}
family of pairwise almost disjoint countable subsets of
\kappa
.
Now modify the proof of Theorem
3.1
by including
\psi
in the parameter for our sage Davies tree: that is, take
P=(R,h,\phi,\psi)
instead of just
P=(R,h,\phi)
.

Using the fact that
\nu>\kappa
, we can show (using an argument essentially identical to the first paragraph of the proof of Theorem
2.1
) that
\left\lvert\nu\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\right\rvert=\aleph_{1}
for each
\alpha<\nu
. Because
\psi\in M_{\alpha}
, the
\aleph_{1}
-many new ordinals

give us access to
\aleph_{1}
-many new countable subsets of
\kappa
, namely the sets
\psi(\zeta)
where
\zeta\in\nu\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}
.

Now suppose
S\in M_{\xi}
for some
\xi<\alpha
and
S\in[\kappa]^{\omega}
.
Because
\psi
maps to an almost disjoint family,
\mathrm{Hit}(S)=\left\{\zeta<\nu\colon\psi(\zeta)\cap S\text{ is infinite}\right\}
is a countable subset of
\nu
definable from
S
.
Consequently,
\mathrm{Hit}(S)\subseteq M_{\xi}
.
Furthermore, if
\zeta\in\nu\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}
, then
\psi(\zeta)\cap S
is finite for all

In particular,
C_{\alpha}=\left\{\psi(\zeta)\colon\zeta\in\nu\cap M_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\xi<\alpha}M_{\xi}\right\}
is a size-
\aleph_{1}

\kappa
in
M_{\alpha}
, each one of which is almost disjoint from every such set in

These sets in

take the role of the sets

in the proof of Theorem

As with the

\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
.
∎

We conclude with some further observations and open questions.
The most pressing question raised by our main theorem is:

Question 3.2
.

Can parts
(2)
and
(3)
of the main theorem be proved from
\mathsf{ZFC}
? And can the number of automorphisms in
(3)
be raised to
2^{\kappa}
?

It is worth pointing out that if it is consistent that all automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
are trivial in the
\kappa
-Cohen model for some
\kappa\geq\aleph_{\omega}
, then proving this will require a large cardinal hypothesis.

As was mentioned already, the hypothesis of the theorem, that there is a sage Davies tree of length
\kappa
, holds provided that
\mathsf{SCH}
and
\square_{\lambda}
hold at all
\lambda\leq\kappa
with
\mathrm{cf}(\lambda)=\kappa
.
The failure of
\square_{\lambda}
at a singular
\lambda
while
\mathsf{SCH}
holds is quite high:
[
16
, Corollary 5]
shows that it gives rise to an inner model with infinitely many Woodin cardinals. (And even without
\mathsf{SCH}
, one still gets an inner model with a Woodin cardinal.)
The failure of
\mathsf{SCH}
has much lower consistency strength: by work of Gitik in
[
11
,
12
]
, the failure of
\mathsf{SCH}
is equiconsistent with the existence of a measurable cardinal
\mu
with Mitchell order
\mu^{++}
. This, then, is the bottleneck for constructing sage Davies trees: if there is a model without long sage Davies trees, then it is consistent to have (at least) measurable cardinals of large Mitchell order.

If we assume
\mathsf{GCH}
in the ground model rather than merely
\mathsf{CH}
, then
\mathsf{SCH}
holds automatically, so a lack of sage Davies trees would entail the failure of
\square_{\lambda}
for singular
\lambda
.
Furthermore, it is proved in
[
1
]
that under
\mathsf{GCH}
, the “Very Weak Square” of Foreman and Magidor from
[
8
]
suffices to construct sage Davies trees.

Question 3.3
.

Is it consistent, relative to some large cardinal hypothesis, that all automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
are trivial in the
\kappa
-Cohen model for some
\kappa\geq\aleph_{\omega}
?
Is it consistent (relative to large cardinals) that there is a regular
\kappa>\aleph_{\omega}
such that not all ground model automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
extend to
2^{\kappa}
automorphisms in the
\kappa
-Cohen model?

Finally, let us point out that while we have a decent understanding of automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
after adding Cohen reals, it is open what happens after adding random reals.

Question 3.4
.

Are there nontrivial automorphisms of
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{P}(\omega)}}{{\mathrm{Fin}}}
in the random real model? What about the
\kappa
-random real model for
\kappa\geq\aleph_{3}
?
