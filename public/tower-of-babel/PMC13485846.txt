Efficacy of prophylactic ursodeoxycholic acid in preventing gallstone formation after metabolic bariatric surgery: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials protocol
Authors: Abdulaziz Alrubaiaan, Farah Altunaib, Sarah A. AlMutairi, Noor AlFadhly, Ahmed F. AlKandari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and objectives:Rapid weight loss following metabolic bariatric surgery (MBS) is associated with an increased risk of developing de novo gallstone formation. The aim of this systematic review and meta-analysis is to evaluate the efficacy of prophylactic ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) in preventing gallstone formation after MBS and to provide evidence-based recommendations.Methods:This systematic review will be conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols. We will conduct a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials evaluating prophylactic UDCA after MBS. The following databases will be searched: PubMed/MEDLINE, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Embase via Ovid, ClinicalTrials.gov, and the World Health Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform. Reference lists of the included trials and reviews will be manually screened for additional trials. Adults without preexisting gallstones will be enrolled to evaluate prophylactic UDCA versus placebo or no treatment. Two reviewers will conduct study selection, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment (Cochrane RoB 2.0). A third reviewer will resolve disagreements. Pooled risk ratios will be calculated using a random-effects model. Heterogeneity will be assessed using the I2 statistic, and results will be presented as forest plots with subgroup analyses and UDCA dose. Exploratory meta-regression analyses will assess the potential influence of baseline body mass index (BMI) and patient age on treatment effects.Result:This review will synthesize the available randomized controlled trial evidence regarding prophylactic UDCA after metabolic bariatric surgery. Planned analyses include the evaluation of gallstone prevention efficacy, subgroup analyses according to bariatric procedure type and UDCA regimen, and an exploratory assessment of patient-related factors such as baseline BMI and age.Conclusion:This protocol outlines the methodology for an updated systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating prophylactic UDCA after MBS. The planned review aims to provide a comprehensive synthesis of the current evidence to help inform postoperative prophylactic strategies and future clinical guidelines.

Introduction

Obesity is an unprecedented global public health concern. By 2022–2024, more than one billion adults were classified as obese. Furthermore, approximately 43% of adults were deemed overweight, contributing to a rapid increase in demand for metabolic bariatric surgery (MBS) worldwide[1,2]. Contemporary registry cycles report over half a million procedures captured annually, with sleeve gastrectomy (SG) being the predominant primary operation (≈60%–65% in recent IFSO data) and Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB) being the leading revision procedure[3,4].

Rapid and significant postoperative weight loss leads to biliary cholesterol supersaturation and gallbladder hypomotility. This process is causally associated with de novo gallstone formation following metabolic bariatric procedures. Observational cohorts and controlled trials report a wide range of gallstone incidence depending on the operation, follow-up, and baseline gallstone prevalence; however, pooled and registry estimates commonly place first-year de novo gallstone rates between ~10% and >50%, with many contemporary series clustering near 30%–40% in high-risk populations[5,6]. These gallstones translate into significant clinical issues: symptomatic cholelithiasis, readmission, and cholecystectomy, contributing to morbidity and resource use during the first two postoperative years[7,8].

Several previous meta-analyses have evaluated the prophylactic use of ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) after MBS and have generally demonstrated reductions in postoperative gallstone formation. Nevertheless, important uncertainties remain unresolved because earlier reviews included heterogeneous bariatric procedures, variable UDCA dosing regimens, inconsistent treatment durations, and differing definitions of gallstone-related outcomes, limiting the generalizability of their findings[8,9].

In addition, the large multicenter UPGRADE trial, which evaluated UDCA 900 mg daily for 6 months after MBS, reported no significant reduction in symptomatic gallstone disease at 24 months in the overall cohort, although subgroup analyses suggested potential benefits among patients without baseline gallstones and those undergoing RYGB. Furthermore, the trial primarily evaluated symptomatic gallstone disease rather than overall radiologic gallstone formation, limiting direct comparability with earlier randomized studies. Collectively, these inconsistencies continue to generate uncertainty regarding the optimal prophylactic strategy after MBS[10].

Therefore, this updated systematic review and meta-analysis aim to synthesize the most recent randomized evidence regarding prophylactic UDCA after MBS, evaluate its efficacy in preventing gallstone formation and symptomatic biliary disease, assess procedure-specific outcomes, explore the impact of dose and treatment duration, and perform exploratory meta-regression analyses according to baseline body mass index (BMI) and age to better define patient-specific effects and optimize postoperative prophylactic strategies.

Methods

The protocol follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P 2015) statement and the methodological standards outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions (version 6.3). The review will be reported using PRISMA. The review protocol has been registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews database (PROSPERO) before data extraction (registration number: CRD420251004594)[11–13].

Information sources and search strategy

A comprehensive search will be conducted across the following electronic databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), and Embase (Ovid interface). To minimize publication bias and identify ongoing or unpublished studies, additional searches will be performed in ClinicalTrials.gov and the World Health Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (WHO ICTRP). Reference lists of included studies and relevant systematic reviews will also be manually screened for additional eligible trials. No language or publication date restrictions will be applied during the search process. When potentially eligible non-English studies are identified, they will be assessed for inclusion if sufficient extractable data are available.

The preliminary PubMed search strategy will include MeSH terms and free-text keywords as follows: (“ursodeoxycholic acid” OR “ursodiol” OR “UDCA”) AND (“bariatric surgery” OR “sleeve gastrectomy” OR “gastric bypass” OR “Roux-en-Y”) AND (“gallstone” OR “cholelithiasis” OR “biliary sludge”) AND (“randomized controlled trial” OR “RCT” OR “placebo”). The PRESS 2022 checklist will be used to peer-review the search strategy before execution.

Study selection

All search results will be imported into Covidence systematic review software (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia)[14]. Duplicates will be automatically removed. Titles and abstracts will be independently screened against the predefined eligibility criteria by two reviewers. Full-text articles deemed potentially relevant will then be retrieved and assessed for inclusion. Any discrepancies between the two reviewers will be resolved through discussion or by consulting a third reviewer. The overall selection process will be documented using a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram, including the number of studies identified, screened, excluded, and included, with reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage.

Data synthesis

A quantitative synthesis (meta-analysis) will be conducted when at least two studies report similar outcomes. For dichotomous outcomes, relative risks with 95% confidence intervals will be calculated using a random-effects model (DerSimonian–Laird method) to account for heterogeneity among studies. For studies with multiple eligible intervention arms sharing a common comparator group, unit-of-analysis issues will be addressed according to Cochrane Handbook recommendations. When clinically appropriate, intervention groups will be combined into a single comparison group. Alternatively, when separate comparisons are required, the shared comparator group will be divided proportionally to avoid double-counting participants within the meta-analysis. Statistical heterogeneity will be quantified using the I2 statistic, categorized as low (<40%), moderate (40%–60%), or substantial (>60%). Cochran’s Q test (P < 0.10) will also be used to assess heterogeneity. Predefined subgroup analyses may be performed, when sufficient data are available, according to bariatric procedure type (SG vs. RYGB vs OAGB), UDCA dose, duration of prophylaxis, and study risk of bias. These analyses will be considered exploratory and interpreted cautiously because of the anticipated variability in study characteristics and the potentially limited number of eligible randomized trials within each subgroup. If an adequate number of studies (generally ≥10) are available, meta-regression analyses may be conducted to explore potential associations between treatment effect and variables such as UDCA dose, baseline BMI, or patient age. Meta-regression findings will be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

Discussion

Gallstone formation is a frequent and clinically significant complication after MBS, especially during periods of rapid postoperative weight loss, with reported incidences ranging between 30% and 40% within the first postoperative year[16]. Several RCTs indicate that UDCA may substantially reduce the risk of developing gallstones and related biliary complications after SG and RYGB; nevertheless, evidence regarding dosage regimens, treatment duration, and surgical subtypes remains uncertain[17,18].

The pathophysiology is multifactorial and includes biliary cholesterol supersaturation, impaired gallbladder motility, altered bile composition, and bile stasis[19]. Rapid weight loss and metabolic changes following sleeve gastrectomy and Roux-en-Y gastric bypass may further contribute to postoperative gallstone formation[20]. UDCA may reduce gallstone formation by decreasing biliary cholesterol saturation and improving bile composition, supporting its potential role as a prophylactic therapy after metabolic bariatric procedures[21].

Although several RCTs and previous meta-analyses have suggested a protective role for prophylactic UDCA, important uncertainties remain regarding the optimal dosage, treatment duration, and procedure-specific efficacy after MBS[22]. Variability in patient selection, outcome definitions, and treatment regimens across studies may contribute to heterogeneity in reported outcomes[23,24]. In addition, differences in postoperative physiology between sleeve gastrectomy and Roux-en-Y gastric bypass may influence gallstone formation risk and the preventive efficacy of UDCA[20]. The planned subgroup and exploratory analyses may help clarify whether specific surgical procedures or UDCA regimens are associated with greater preventive efficacy and tolerability.

Most postoperative gallstones remain asymptomatic; however, a proportion of patients may develop symptomatic biliary disease requiring hospital readmission, endoscopic intervention, or cholecystectomy[25]. Therefore, identifying effective prophylactic strategies after MBS remains clinically important. Clarifying the preventive efficacy of UDCA may help guide postoperative management strategies and potentially reduce the need for secondary biliary procedures. In addition, uncertainty persists regarding the role of prophylactic cholecystectomy in asymptomatic patients undergoing MBS, and improved evidence regarding postoperative gallstone prevention may further inform clinical decision-making[26].

The dose and duration of UDCA therapy also remain important clinical considerations. Variability in dosing regimens and treatment duration across randomized trials may contribute to differences in preventive efficacy, patient adherence, and tolerability[18,24]. The planned analyses may help clarify whether specific UDCA regimens are associated with improved postoperative outcomes and whether shorter or prolonged treatment courses provide additional clinical benefit.

This systematic review and meta-analysis will provide an up-to-date synthesis of all available randomized evidence assessing the efficacy and safety of prophylactic UDCA after MBS. We aim to clarify the magnitude and consistency of UDCA’s protective effect against postoperative gallstone formation, symptomatic gallstone disease, and the need for cholecystectomy by pooling data across studies. Subgroup analyses will further determine whether the benefits differ between surgical types (SG vs. RYGB), dosage ranges, and treatment durations. In addition, exploratory meta-regression analyses may help clarify whether patient-related factors, such as baseline BMI and age, influence postoperative gallstone formation or modify the preventive effect of UDCA after MBS. Understanding the potential impact of these variables may contribute to more individualized postoperative prophylactic strategies and help identify patient populations that could derive the greatest benefit from UDCA therapy.

The planned review may help establish evidence-based recommendations for the use of prophylactic UDCA in bariatric practice. When a consistent protective effect is confirmed, short-term UDCA prophylaxis, especially during the rapid weight-loss phase, could be a simple, cost-effective, and well-tolerated strategy for preventing postoperative gallstone disease. This approach would have significant clinical and economic implications, potentially reducing the requirement for secondary cholecystectomy and improving long-term postoperative outcomes.

Conclusion

This protocol outlines a systematic review and meta-analysis designed to assess UDCA’s effect in preventing gallstone formation after MBS. The planned review will also evaluate whether the effectiveness of UDCA differs according to bariatric procedure type, treatment regimen, and patient-related factors such as BMI and age. The findings of this review may help inform evidence-based postoperative prophylactic strategies and future clinical guidelines.
