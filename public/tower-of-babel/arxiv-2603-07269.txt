Motivic Chern Classes of Open Projected Richardson Varieties and of Affine Schubert Cells
Changjian Su, Rui Xiong, Changlong Zhong

Abstract
. The open projected Richardson varieties are images of the open Richardson varieties of the complete flag variety under the canonical projection to the partial flag variety. Our main result compares the Segre motivic Chern (SMC) classes of the open projected Richardson varieties with those of the affine Schubert cells by pushing or pulling these classes to the affine Grassmannian. The main method is the recursive relation determined by the Demazure–Lusztig operators. As another application of this recursive relation, we relate the localization of the SMC classes to the twisted Kazhdan–Lusztig R-polynomials. In the case of Grassmannians, the open projected Richardson varieties are known as the open positroid varieties. We give a combinatorial formula for the SMC classes of these varieties.

Introduction

Let
G

are called open (resp. closed) projected Richardson varieties.
When

[

,
KLS13
]
.
Richardson varieties are originally introduced in
[
KL80
]
, where the number of points of open Richardson varieties over finite fields is shown to be given by the Kazhdan–Lusztig R-polynomials associated to the Weyl group
W
of

. Such varieties are latter studied by Richardson
[
Ric92
]
and Deodhar
[
Deo85
]
. Many geometric properties of projected Richardson varieties are studied by Knutson, Lam and Speyer
[
KLS14
]
.
Projected Richardson varieties play a crucial role in recent advancements in totally positivity; see
[
GKL22
,
BH24
]
and the references within.
See
[
Spe23
]
for an excellent survey of this subject, especially from the point of view of Schubert calculus.

Among many studies of (projected) Richardson varieties and their related geometry and combinatorial structures, the following property (along with its special cases and more general cases) plays a central role. Let

be a dominant coweight whose stabilizer

determines a parabolic subgroup

. Let

be the translation element in the extended affine Weyl group. Let

be the set of minimal length representatives of

. In
[

]
, it is shown that there exists an order-preserving embedding

where

is the index set of projected Richardson varieties, ordered by closure inclusion.
This combinatorial property was lifted geometrically to Bruhat atlas and Kazhdan–Lusztig atlas
[

]

[

,
BH21
]
for an introduction.

Following
[

]

Let

be the

One can then consider various cohomology and K-theory classes determined by open/closed projected Richardson varieties in

The proof is distinct from that in
[

Instead of computing the restriction formulas, recursive formulas are established on both sides, which uniquely determine the classes on the finite and affine sides. One then shows that the two recursive formulas are identical by using the bijection

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the definition of the Kazhdan-Lusztig R-polynomials and some properties of the motivic Chern classes of Schubert cells in the flag variety.

2.1.
Kazhdan-Lusztig R-polynomials

In this subsection, we use
W
to denote a Coxeter group with length function
\ell(\cdot)
and Bruhat order
\leq
, for example, a finite Weyl group or an affine Weyl group. Let
H(W)
be the Hecke algebra over the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
. It has basis
\{T_{w}\mid w\in W\}
, with multiplication defined by
(T_{s}+1)(T_{s}-q)=0
for any simple reflection
s
, and
T_{w}T_{v}=T_{wv}
if
\ell(wv)=\ell(w)+\ell(v)
. Define an involution
h\mapsto\bar{h}
on
H(w)
by

\overline{\sum a_{w}T_{w}}=\sum\bar{a}_{w}T_{w^{-1}}^{-1},

where
\bar{a}_{w}
is the involution on
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
defined by
\bar{q}=q^{-1}
.
For any
u,w\in W
, the Kazhdan-Lusztig R-polynomial
R_{u,w}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
is defined by the formula (see
[
KL79
]
)

(1)

T_{w^{-1}}^{-1}=\sum_{u\leq w}\overline{R_{u,w}(q)}q^{-\ell(u)}T_{u},

and we set
R_{u,w}(q)=0
if
u\nleq w
. From the definition, we can get the following recursive formulae:

(2)

R_{u,w}=\begin{cases}R_{su,sw}&\textit{ if }su<u\textit{ and }sw<w;\\
(q-1)R_{su,w}+qR_{su,sw}&\textit{ if }su>u\textit{ and }sw<w.\end{cases}

If
W
is finite and
w_{0}
is the longest element,

(3)

R_{w^{-1}w_{0},u^{-1}w_{0}}(q)=R_{u,w}(q)

for all
u,w\in W
.

3.
Recursion for the SMC classes in the finite case

In this section, we study the Segre motivic Chern class for the open projected Richardson variety in the partial flag variety.

3.1.
MC class of open Richardson variety

Recall that for any
u\leq w
, the open Richardson variety in the complete flag variety is
\mathring{R}_{u,w}:=X(w)^{\circ}\cap Y(u)^{\circ}\subset G/B
. We let
\mathring{R}_{u,w}=\varnothing
if
u\nleq w
.

Definition 3.1
.

We define the MC class of the open Richardson variety
\mathring{R}_{u,w}
to be

\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w}):=\MC_{y}(X(w)^{\circ})\otimes\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})\in K_{T}(G/B)[[y]].

Remark 3.2
.

By an unpublished result of Schürmann, which is the K-theoretic analogue of
[
Sch17
]
, the right hand side of the above equation equals the MC classes of
\mathring{R}_{u,w}
inside the full flag variety
G/B
. Hence, the notation is unambiguous.

Recall the DL operators
\mathcal{T}_{i}^{L},\mathcal{T}_{i}^{L,\vee}
from
Eq.

4
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\varphi,\psi\in K_{T}^{*}(G/B)[[y]]
, we have

(1+y)s_{i}^{L}(\varphi\cdot\psi)+(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\varphi\cdot\mathcal{T}_{i}^{L,\vee}(\psi))=(1+y)\varphi\cdot\psi+(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot(\mathcal{T}_{i}^{L}(\varphi)\cdot\psi).

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{i}^{L}
and
\mathcal{T}_{i}^{L,\vee}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{i}^{L}(\varphi)\cdot\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{1+ye^{-\alpha_{i}}}{1-e^{-\alpha_{i}}}\psi\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\varphi)-\frac{1+y}{1-e^{-\alpha_{i}}}\varphi\cdot\psi,

\displaystyle\varphi\cdot\mathcal{T}_{i}^{L,\vee}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+ye^{\alpha_{i}}}{1-e^{\alpha_{i}}}\varphi\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\psi)-\frac{1+y}{1-e^{\alpha_{i}}}\varphi\cdot\psi.

The lemma follows from comparison of the two identities.
∎

Applying the Lemma to
\varphi=\MC_{y}(X(w)^{\circ})
and
\psi=\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})
, and using
2.4
, we get

Proposition 3.4
.

For any
u,w\in W
and simple root
\alpha_{i}
, we have

(1)

If
s_{i}w>w
and
s_{i}u>u
,

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w}))-(1+y)\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{s_{i}u,w}))+(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,s_{i}w}).

(2)

If
s_{i}w>w
and
s_{i}u<u
,

\displaystyle(1+y)s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w}))-(1+y)\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,s_{i}w})-(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{s_{i}u,w})).

(3)

If
s_{i}w<w
and
s_{i}u>u
,

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}(s_{i}^{L}-\textrm{id})(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{s_{i}u,w}))-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,s_{i}w}).

(4)

If
s_{i}w<w
and
s_{i}u<u
,

\displaystyle(1+y)(s_{i}^{L}-e^{-\alpha_{i}})(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{s_{i}u,w}))-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,s_{i}w}).

3.2.
MC class of the open projected Richardson variety

Now let
P
be a parabolic subgroup containing the Borel subgroup
B
, and let
\pi:G/B\rightarrow G/P
be the natural projection. Let
W_{P}\subset W
be the Weyl group determined by
P
, and let
W^{P}
be the set of minimal length representatives in the cosets
W/W_{P}
.

Definition 3.5
.

For any
u\in W
,
w\in W^{P}
,

(1)

the open projected Richardson variety is defined by
\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}=\pi(\mathring{R}_{u,w})
;

(2)

the motivic Chern class of
\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}
is defined by

\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}):=\pi_{*}\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w}).

Remark 3.6
.

By the functoriality of
\MC_{y}
and
3.2
, the class
\pi_{*}\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w})
coincides with the usual motivic Chern class of
\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}
if we assume Schürmann’s K-theoretic intersection formula. Moreover,
\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}=\varnothing
if
u\nleq w
.

Recall the Demazure–Lusztig operators
\mathcal{T}_{j}^{R},\mathcal{T}_{j}^{R,\vee}
from
Eq.

6
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any simple reflection
s_{j}\in W_{P}
, and
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in K_{T}(G/B)[y]
, we have

\pi_{*}(\mathcal{T}^{R}_{j}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{G})=\pi_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{R,\vee}_{j}(\mathcal{G})).

Proof.

Recall
\pi_{j}
is the natural projection from
G/B
to
G/P_{j}
. Since
s_{j}\in W_{P}
,
P_{j}\subset P
. Let
f:G/P_{j}\rightarrow G/P
be the natural projection. Then
\pi=f\circ\pi_{j}
. Therefore, by the projection formula, we get

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(\pi_{j}^{*}\pi_{j*}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{G})=

\displaystyle f_{*}\pi_{j*}(\pi_{j}^{*}\pi_{j*}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{*}(\pi_{j*}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\pi_{j*}(\mathcal{G}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{*}\pi_{j*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\pi_{j}^{*}\pi_{j*}(\mathcal{G}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\pi_{j}^{*}\pi_{j*}(\mathcal{G})).

Then the lemma follows from this and the definitions of
\mathcal{T}^{R}_{j}
and
\mathcal{T}_{j}^{R,\vee}
.
∎

Using this lemma, we get the following formula.

Corollary 3.8
.

For any
u\in W
,
w\in W^{P}
,
s_{j}\in W_{P}
, we have

\pi_{*}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,ws_{j}}))=\begin{cases}\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{us_{j},w})&\textit{ if }us_{j}<u;\\
-(1+y)\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w})-y\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{us_{j},w})&\textit{ if }us_{j}>u.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
w\in W^{P}
and
s_{j}\in W_{P}
,
ws_{j}>w
. By the definition of MC classes of open Richardson and open projected Richardson varieties,
3.7
, and
2.4
(4), we get

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,ws_{j}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\bigg(\MC_{y}(X(ws_{j})^{\circ})\otimes\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\bigg(\mathcal{T}_{j}^{R}(\MC_{y}(X(w)^{\circ}))\otimes\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\bigg(\MC_{y}(X(w)^{\circ})\otimes\mathcal{T}_{j}^{R,\vee}(\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ}))\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\pi_{*}\bigg(\MC_{y}(X(w)^{\circ})\otimes\SMC_{y}(Y(us_{j})^{\circ})\bigg)&\textit{ if }us_{j}<u;\\
-(1+y)\pi_{*}\bigg(\MC_{y}(X(w)^{\circ})\otimes\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})\bigg)-y\pi_{*}\bigg(\MC_{y}(X(w)^{\circ})\otimes\SMC_{y}(Y(us_{j})^{\circ})\bigg)&\textit{ if }us_{j}>u\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\pi_{*}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{us_{j},w}))&\textit{ if }us_{j}<u;\\
-(1+y)\pi_{*}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{u,w}))-y\pi_{*}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{R}_{us_{j},w}))&\textit{ if }us_{j}>u\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{us_{j},w})&\textit{ if }us_{j}<u;\\
-(1+y)\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w})-y\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{us_{j},w})&\textit{ if }us_{j}>u.\end{cases}

∎

We also need the following result of Deodhar.

Lemma 3.9
.

[
Deo87
, Lemma 2.1]

For any simple root
\alpha_{i}
and
w\in W^{P}
, exactly one of the following cases hold:

(1)

s_{i}w<w
, in which case
s_{i}w\in W^{P}
;

(2)

s_{i}w>w
and
s_{i}w\in W^{P}
;

(3)

s_{i}w>w
and
s_{i}w=ws_{j}
for some simple reflection
s_{j}\in W_{P}
.

Since
G
acts on
G/P
, we can also define the action of the Weyl group
W
on
K_{T}(G/P)[y]
, denoted by
w^{L}
. Since the projection
\pi
commutes with the
G
-action,
w^{L}
commutes with the pushforward
\pi_{*}
. Applying
\pi_{*}
to the equations in
3.4
, and using
3.9
and
3.8
, we get the following recursion formuale for the MC classes of the open projected Richardson varieties.

.

For any

,

If
s_{i}w>w
,
s_{i}w=ws_{j}

s_{j}\in W

s_{i}u>u

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}))-(1+y)\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w})

\displaystyle y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{s_{i}u,w}))+(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{us_{j},w}).

(3)

If
s_{i}w>w
,
s_{i}w=ws_{j}
for some simple reflection
s_{j}\in W
,
s_{i}u>u
, and
us_{j}>u

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}(s_{i}^{L}-\textrm{id})(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{s_{i}u,w}))-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{us_{j},w}).

(4)

If
s_{i}w>w
,
s_{i}w\in W^{P}
, and
s_{i}u<u
,

\displaystyle(1+y)s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}))-(1+y)\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,s_{i}w})-(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{s_{i}u,w})).

(5)

If
s_{i}w>w
,
s_{i}w=ws_{j}
for some simple reflection
s_{j}\in W_{P}
,
s_{i}u<u
, and
us_{j}<u
,

\displaystyle(1+y)s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}))-(1+y)\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{us_{j},w})-(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{s_{i}u,w}))

(6)

If
s_{i}w>w
,
s_{i}w=ws_{j}

\displaystyle(1+y)s_{i}^{L}(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}))-(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w})

and

,

\displaystyle(1+y)(s_{i}^{L}-e^{-\alpha_{i}})(\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}))

\displaystyle=

Now, let us rewrite the recursion in terms of the extended affine Weyl group
W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}:=W\ltimes X_{*}(T)
, where
X_{*}(T)
is the cocharacter lattice.
In the remaining part of this section, let
\lambda
be a dominant cocharacter whose stabilizer is
W_{P}
. For any
u,w\in W
, let

f^{\lambda}_{u,w}:=ut_{\lambda}w^{-1}\in W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}.

Let us denote
\mathcal{B}^{+}=\{f^{\lambda}_{u,w}\mid u\in W,w\in W^{P}\}
. By
[
HL15
]
, there is a bijection

W\times W^{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}^{+},\quad(u,w)\mapsto f^{\lambda}_{u,w}.

Moreover, for any
w\in W^{P}
,
t_{\lambda}w^{-1}
is a minimal length representative in the right coset
W\cdot t_{\lambda}w^{-1}\subset W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}
. In particular, for any
(u,w)\in W\times W^{P}
,

(13)

\ell(ut_{\lambda}w^{-1})=\ell(u)+\ell(t_{\lambda})-\ell(w).

Using these, it is easy to get

Lemma 3.11
.

Fix
u\in W,w\in W^{P}
, and let
f=ut_{\lambda}w^{-1}
. For any simple root
\alpha_{i}
, we have

(1)

s_{i}f<f\Leftrightarrow s_{i}u<u
.

(2)

s_{i}f>f\Leftrightarrow s_{i}u>u
.

(3)

fs_{i}<f\Leftrightarrow w<s_{i}w\in W^{P}
, or
s_{i}w=ws_{j}
for some simple reflection
s_{j}\in W_{P}
and
us_{j}<u
.

(4)

fs_{i}>f\Leftrightarrow s_{i}w<w
, or
s_{i}w=ws_{j}
for some simple reflection
s_{j}\in W_{P}
and
us_{j}>u
.

Moreover, if
s_{i}w=ws_{j}
for some simple reflection
s_{j}\in W_{P}
, then
us_{j}t_{\lambda}w^{-1}=ut_{\lambda}(s_{i}w)^{-1}=fs_{i}
.

Definition 3.12
.

For
u\in W,w\in W^{P}
, denote by
\mathring{\Pi}_{f^{\lambda}_{u,w}}=\mathring{\Pi}_{u,w}\subset G/P

the open projected Richardson variety. Define the SMC class of
\mathring{\Pi}_{f^{\lambda}_{u,w}}
by

\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f^{\lambda}_{u,w}}):=\frac{\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f^{\lambda}_{u,w}})}{\lambda_{y}(T^{*}(G/P))}\in K_{T}(G/P)[[y]].

Since the class
\lambda_{y}(T^{*}(G/P))
is
G
-equivariant, it is invariant under the action
w^{L}
for any
w\in W
. Therefore, using
3.11
, we can reformulate
3.10
in the following way.

Theorem 3.13
.

Assume
f=f^{\lambda}_{u,w}
for some
u\in W,w\in W^{P}
. For any simple root
\alpha_{i}
, we have

(1)

If
s_{i}f<f
and
fs_{i}<f
, then

\displaystyle(1+y)\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f}))-(1+y)\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{fs_{i}})-(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{s_{i}f})).

(2)

If
s_{i}f<f
and
fs_{i}>f
, then

\displaystyle(1+y)\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f}))-(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{s_{i}f}))-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{fs_{i}}).

(3)

If
s_{i}f>f
and
fs_{i}<f
, then

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f}))-(1+y)\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{s_{i}f}))+(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{fs_{i}}).

(4)

If
s_{i}f>f
and
fs_{i}>f
, then

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f}))-(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\cdot(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{s_{i}f}))-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{fs_{i}}).

Proof.

Let us only prove the first one, as the others can be checked similarly. By

, the case

and

4.
SMC classes in the affine flag variety

In this section, we study the motivic Chern classes in the affine flag variety.

4.1.
Affine flag variety

Let
X_{*}(T)
be the cocharacter lattice of
T
, and let

W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}:=W\ltimes X_{*}(T)

be the extended affine Weyl group.
For a cocharacter
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
, we denote by
t_{\lambda}
the corresponding element in
W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}
. Note that in
W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}
, we have

wt_{\lambda}w^{-1}=t_{w\lambda},\qquad w\in W,\lambda\in X_{*}(T).

Denote the coroot lattice as
Q^{\vee}\subseteq X_{*}(T)
.
It is known that the subgroup
W_{\operatorname{aff}}:=W\ltimes Q^{\vee}
, called the affine Weyl group, is the Coxeter group of the corresponding affine Dynkin diagram with generators

s_{i}\in W\,(i\in I),\quad\text{ and \quad}s_{0}=t_{\theta^{\vee}}s_{\theta},

where
\theta
is the highest root.
Let
R
be the set of roots, with positive (resp. negative) roots denoted by
R^{+}
(resp.,
R^{-}
). For any
\alpha\in R
, we use
\alpha>0
to denote that
\alpha\in R^{+}
.
The length function on
W_{\operatorname{aff}}
can be extended to
W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}
:

\ell(wt_{\lambda})=\sum_{\alpha>0,w\alpha>0}\big|\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\big|+\sum_{\alpha>0,w\alpha<0}\big|\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle+1\big|.

Let
\Omega\subset W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}
be the set of length 0 elements. Then
W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}=W_{\operatorname{aff}}\rtimes\Omega
, and we can also extend the Bruhat order on
W_{\operatorname{aff}}
to
W_{\operatorname{ext}}
by
w\gamma\leq w^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
if and only if
w\leq w^{\prime}
and
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
, where
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{\operatorname{aff}}
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Omega
. With this length function and Bruhat order, all the constructions in
Section

2.1
can be extended to
W_{\operatorname{ext}}
.

Let
\mathbb{C}[[z]]
(resp.
\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!):=\mathbb{C}[[z]][z^{-1}]
) be the formal power series ring (resp. formal Laurent series ring), and let
G[[z]]
(resp.
G(\!(z)\!)
) be the
\mathbb{C}[[z]]
-points (resp.
\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)
-points) of
G
. There is an evaluation at
z=0
map from
G[[z]]
to
G
, and let
\mathcal{I}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}^{-}
) be the inverse image of the Borel subgroup
B
(resp., the opposite Borel subgroup
B^{-}
). Then the
affine flag variety
is

\Fl_{G}=G(\!(z)\!)/\mathcal{I},

whose
T
-fixed points

(\Fl_{G})^{T}
can be identified with
W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}
as follows. Any cocharacter
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
defines a morphism
\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)^{*}\rightarrow T(\!(z)\!)\subset G(\!(z)\!)
, we use
z^{\lambda}\in G(\!(z)\!)
to denote the image of
z
. For any
w\in W
, let
\dot{w}
denote a lift of it in
G[[z]]
. Then for each
wt_{\lambda}\in W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}
, the corresponding fixed point in
\Fl_{G}
is
\dot{w}z^{-\lambda}\mathcal{I}\in\Fl_{G}
, which will be just denoted by
wt_{\lambda}
throughout the paper. Let
\mathring{\Sigma}_{wt_{\lambda}}:=\mathcal{I}\dot{w}z^{-\lambda}\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}
be the
affine Schubert cell
of dimension
\ell(wt_{\lambda})
. The affine flag variety has a cell decomposition

\Fl_{G}=\bigsqcup_{wt_{\lambda}\in W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}}\mathring{\Sigma}_{wt_{\lambda}}.

For each
n\geq 0
, let

(14)

X_{n}:=\bigsqcup_{wt_{\lambda}\in W_{{\operatorname{ext}}},\,\ell(wt_{\lambda})\leq n}\mathring{\Sigma}_{wt_{\lambda}}.

Then
\Fl_{G}
is the increasing limit of
X_{n}
, which is called an ind-variety. Let
\mathring{\Sigma}^{wt_{\lambda}}:=\mathcal{I}^{-}\dot{w}z^{-\lambda}\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}
be the opposite affine Schubert cell.

There is a loop rotation action of
\mathbb{C}^{*}_{\rot}:=\mathbb{C}^{*}
on
\Fl_{G}
by scaling the parameter
z
. Let
\delta
be the degree one character of this action, which is also the imaginary root for the corresponding Kac–Moody Lie algebra
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then the positive (real) affine roots are

(15)

R^{+}_{{\operatorname{aff}}}=\{\alpha+k\delta\mid\alpha\in R^{+},k\geq 0\text{ or }\alpha\in R^{-},k\geq 1\},

and the negative (real) affine roots are

R^{-}_{{\operatorname{aff}}}=\{\alpha+k\delta\mid\alpha\in R^{+},k\leq-1\text{ or }\alpha\in R^{-},k\leq 0\}.

The extended affine Weyl group
W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}
acts on the lattice

Theorem 4.5

For any simple root
\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{i}^{R,\vee}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f}))

-(1+y)\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})-y\cdot\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{fs_{i}})&\textit{ if }fs_{i}>f.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{L,\vee}_{i}(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f}))

5.
Comparison between the finite and affine case

In this section, we prove our main result of this paper by relating the Segre motivic Chern classes of the open projected Richardson varieties in the finite partial flag variety
G/P
and the Segre motivic Chern class of the Schubert cells in the affine flag variety
\Fl_{G}
.

5.1.
Affine Grassmannian

To build a bridge between
G/P
and
\Fl_{G}
, we need to use the affine Grassmannian.
Let
\Gr_{G}:=G(\!(z)\!)/G[[z]]
be the
affine Grassmannian
. There is a natural projection map
\Fl_{G}\rightarrow\Gr_{G}
whose fibers are isomorphic to
G/B
. The
T
-fixed points
(\Gr_{G})^{T}
are in bijection with the coset
W_{{\operatorname{ext}}}/W\simeq X_{*}(T)
. For any
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
, we let
t_{\lambda}W
represent the fixed point
z^{-\lambda}G[[z]]/G[[z]]
.
For any
\mu\in X_{*}(T)
and
\gamma\in K_{T}(\Gr_{G})
, let
\gamma|_{t_{\mu}W}
be the localization at the fixed point
t_{\mu}W
in
\Gr_{G}
.

Let
K\subset G
be the maximal compact subgroup and
T_{\mathbb{R}}:=K\cap T
the compact torus. Then we have

r\colon\Gr_{G}\simeq\Omega K\rightarrow LK\rightarrow LK/T_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\Fl_{G},

where
LK
(resp.,
\Omega K
) is the space of polynomial maps
S^{1}\to K
(resp.
(S^{1},1)\to(K,1)
). Hence, there is a pullback map, which is called the wrong way map,

r^{*}:K_{T}(\Fl_{G})\rightarrow K_{T}(\Gr_{G}).

In terms of localization, this is defined as follows:

(25)

r^{*}(\gamma)|_{t_{\mu}W}=\gamma|_{t_{\mu}},

where
\mu\in X_{*}(T)
, see
[
LSS10
]
. Since
\Gr_{G}
can be realized as a partial flag variety for the affine Kac-Moody group
[
Kum02
]
, we have the operators
w^{L}
on
K_{T}(\Gr_{G})
for any
w\in W_{\operatorname{ext}}
, and it satisfies the following formula:

w^{L}(\gamma)|_{t_{\mu}W}=w(\gamma|_{w^{-1}t_{\mu}W})=w(\gamma|_{t_{w^{-1}\mu}W}),

where
\gamma\in K_{T}(\Gr_{G})
and
\mu\in X_{*}(T)
. It is easy to check that
r^{*}
does not commute with the operators
w^{L}
. But we have the following relation.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\gamma\in K_{T}(\Fl_{G})
,
\mu\in X_{*}(T)
, and any simple root
\alpha_{i}
with
i\in I_{\operatorname{aff}}
,

s_{i}(\gamma|_{t_{s_{i}\mu}})=\bigg(s_{i}^{L}(r^{*}\gamma)\bigg)|_{t_{\mu}W}.

With
Eqs.

25
and
5.1
,
4.7
yields the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

For any simple root
\alpha_{i}\in I_{\operatorname{aff}}
,
\mu\in X_{*}(T)
, and
f\in W_{\operatorname{ext}}
,

(1)

if
s_{i}f>f
and
fs_{i}>f
,

\displaystyle-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}\bigg(r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{s_{i}f})\bigg)+(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\cdot s_{i}^{L}\bigg(r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\cdot r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{fs_{i}}).

(2)

if
s_{i}f>f
and
fs_{i}<f
,

\displaystyle-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}\bigg(r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{s_{i}f})\bigg)+(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\cdot s_{i}^{L}\bigg(r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+y)\cdot r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})+(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{fs_{i}}).

(3)

if
s_{i}f<f
and

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}\bigg(r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{s_{i}f})\bigg)+(1+y)\cdot s_{i}^{L}\bigg(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-\alpha_{i}}\cdot r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})-y(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{fs_{i}}).

(4)

if

and

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\alpha_{i}})\cdot s_{i}^{L}\bigg(r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{s_{i}f})\bigg)+(1+y)\cdot s_{i}^{L}\bigg(r^{*}\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})\bigg)

5.2.
Main result

For any dominant cocharacter
\mu

\Gr_{\mu}:=G[[z]]z^{-\mu}G[[z]]/G[[z]]\subset\Gr_{G}
be the spherical Schubert cell. Then

\Gr_{G}=\bigsqcup_{\mu\in X_{*}(T)^{+}}\Gr_{\mu},

where
X_{*}(T)^{+}
denotes the dominant cocharacters.
Let
\ev:G[[z]]\rightarrow G
be the evaluation at

Now let us fix a dominant cocharacter
\lambda

\{\alpha_{i}\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle=0\}

P
be the corresponding parabolic subgroup containing the Borel subgroup

\ev\bigg(G[[z]]\cap z^{-\lambda}G[[z]]z^{\lambda}\bigg)=P\subset G.

Hence,

\Gr_{\lambda}\simeq G[[z]]/(G[[z]]\cap z^{-\lambda}G[[z]]z^{\lambda})

maps to

via the map
\ev

is an affine bundle over
G/P
,
and denote the closed embedding

by regarding

as a subgroup of

of constant loops, see
[
Zhu17
]

[
FGSX25
]
for a proof.

Lemma 5.3
.

The
T\times\mathbb{C}^{*}_{\rot}
weights of the tangent space

\{-\alpha+k\delta\mid 0\leq k<\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\}.

Consider the following diagram,

\textstyle{G/P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{\lambda}}
\textstyle{\Gr_{\lambda}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\Gr_{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{r}
\textstyle{\Fl_{G},}

where
i_{\lambda}
and
j_{\lambda}

q^{*}:=j_{\lambda}^{*}\circ r^{*}:K_{T}(\Fl_{G})\rightarrow K_{T}(\Gr_{\lambda}).

Recall from
3.12
that for

and

, we have the Segre motivic Chern class of the open projected Richardson variety
\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f}),f=f^{\lambda}_{u,w}=ut_{\lambda}w^{-1}\in W_{\operatorname{ext}}
. It is related to the SMC class
\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})
in

Theorem 5.4
.

With the above notations and let
\mathcal{N}
be the normal bundle of

inside

, then

i_{\lambda*}\bigg(\frac{\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f})}{\lambda_{y}(\mathcal{N}^{*})}\bigg)=q^{*}\bigg(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})\bigg)\in K_{T}(\Gr_{\lambda})[[y]].

Remark 5.5
.

The spherical Schubert cell
\Gr_{\lambda}
has a
G
action by left multiplication. Hence,
K_{T}(\Gr_{\lambda})
has a
W
action as before. Since the map

is

commutes with the action

. Besides, the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}
is invariant under the

q^{*}\bigg(\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})\bigg)
satisfies the same recursive formulae as
\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Sigma}^{f})
in
5.2
.

Remark 5.6
.

Since the left hand side vanishes if
u\nleq w
, we get same vanishing result for the right hand side, which can also be obtained directly from localization.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
\ell(w),w\in W^{P}
. Assume

. Then the left hand side is

if

. On the other, all the torus fixed points in
\Gr_{\lambda}
are of the form

for some

. If

then
ut_{\lambda}\leq t_{v\lambda}
, which implies

because of
Eq.

Localization of SMC classes and Twisted R-polynomials

In this section, we study the relation between the localization of the SMC classes
\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})
and the twisted R-polynomials from
[

]
,
which is a generalization of the Kazhdan–Lusztig R-polynomials in

6.1.
Subwords

Let
u,w\in W
. A
w
-word is a sequence
\mathbf{w}=(s_{i_{1}},\ldots,s_{i_{\ell}})
of simple reflections such that
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
.
The word
\mathbf{w}
is said to be
reduced
if
\ell=\ell(w)
.
We define

\beta_{\mathbf{w},k}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k-1}}\alpha_{i_{k}},\qquad k=1,2\ldots,\ell.

If
\mathbf{w}
is reduced,

\{\beta_{\mathbf{w},1},\ldots,\beta_{\mathbf{w},k}\}=\{\alpha>0:w^{-1}\alpha<0\}.

We denote
\beta_{k}=\beta_{\mathbf{w},k}
for simplicity. A
u
-subword of
\mathbf{w}
is a sequence

\mathbf{u}=(\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{\ell})
such that
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in\{s_{i_{k}},e\}
for
k=1,\ldots,\ell
and

u=\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdots\mathbf{u}_{\ell}
.
We set the partial product

\mathbf{u}_{(k)}=\mathbf{u}_{1}\cdots\mathbf{u}_{k}
, and introduce subsets
J_{\mathbf{u}}^{\pm},E_{\mathbf{u}}^{\pm}\subset[\ell]:=\{1,2,\cdots,\ell\}
by the following table:

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\hline\cr\vphantom{\dfrac{1}{2}}\text{Cases }&\mathbf{u}_{k}=s_{i_{k}}&\mathbf{u}_{k}=e\\
\hline\cr\vphantom{\dfrac{1}{2}}\mathbf{u}_{(k-1)}s_{i_{k}}<\mathbf{u}_{(k-1)}&k\in J_{\mathbf{u}}^{-}&k\in E_{\mathbf{u}}^{-}\\
\hline\cr\vphantom{\dfrac{1}{2}}\mathbf{u}_{(k-1)}s_{i_{k}}>\mathbf{u}_{(k-1)}&k\in J_{\mathbf{u}}^{+}&k\in E_{\mathbf{u}}^{+}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\qquad\begin{aligned} J_{\mathbf{u}}&=J_{\mathbf{u}}^{+}\sqcup J_{\mathbf{u}}^{-},\\
E_{\mathbf{u}}&=E_{\mathbf{u}}^{+}\sqcup E_{\mathbf{u}}^{-},\\
[\ell]&=J_{\mathbf{u}}\sqcup E_{\mathbf{u}}.\end{aligned}

Then
\mathbf{u}
is called
reduced
if
\mathbf{u}_{(k-1)}\leq\mathbf{u}_{(k)}
for all
k
, i.e.

J_{\mathbf{u}}^{-}=\varnothing
. Similarly,

\mathbf{u}
is called
distinguished
if
\mathbf{u}_{(k)}\leq\mathbf{u}_{(k-1)}s_{i_{k}}
for all
k
, i.e.

E_{\mathbf{u}}^{-}=\varnothing
.

6.2.
Subword recursion

Let
\mathbf{w}
be a
w
-word and
\mathbf{w}\sqcup s
be a
ws
-word with
s
a simple reflection. For any
u
, there are two types of
u
-subwords in
\mathbf{w}s
:

•

Type I:
\mathbf{x}\sqcup\makebox[9.60004pt]{\makebox[0.0pt]{$s$}\makebox[0.0pt]{$\diagdown$}}
with
\mathbf{x}
a
u
-subword of
\mathbf{w}
;

•

Type II:
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\sqcup s
with
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
a
us
-subword of
\mathbf{w}
.

Moreover, Type I subwords are in bijection to
u
-subwords
\mathbf{x}
of
\mathbf{w}
, and Type II subwords are in bijection to
us
-subwords
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{w}
.
By definition, if we compare the
J^{\pm}
-subsets and
E^{\pm}
-subsets of
\mathbf{x}\sqcup\makebox[9.60004pt]{\makebox[0.0pt]{$s$}\makebox[0.0pt]{$\diagdown$}}
and
\mathbf{x}
(or
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\sqcup s
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
, respectively), they will coincide, except for the following cases

(28)

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}&\text{Type I}&\text{Type II}\\
\hline\cr us<u&E^{-}_{\mathbf{x}\sqcup\makebox[9.60004pt]{\makebox[0.0pt]{$s$}\makebox[0.0pt]{$\diagdown$}}}=E^{-}_{\mathbf{x}}\sqcup\{s\}&J^{+}_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\sqcup s}=J^{+}_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}\sqcup\{s\}\\
us>u&E^{+}_{\mathbf{x}\sqcup\makebox[9.60004pt]{\makebox[0.0pt]{$s$}\makebox[0.0pt]{$\diagdown$}}}=E^{+}_{\mathbf{x}}\sqcup\{s\}&J^{-}_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\sqcup s}=J^{-}_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}\sqcup\{s\}\end{array}.

Here we abuse the notation and refer the last term in the word
\mathbf{w}\sqcup s
as
s
.

Assume there is a collection of elements
\mathcal{S}_{u,w},v,w\in W
with
\mathcal{S}_{u,\textrm{id}}=\delta_{u,\textrm{id}}
and subject to the recursive relation (
s=s_{\alpha}
):

(29)

\mathcal{S}_{u,ws}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{p}_{11}(w\alpha)\mathcal{S}_{u,w}+\mathrm{p}_{12}(w\alpha)\mathcal{S}_{us,w},&us<u;\\
\mathrm{p}_{21}(w\alpha)\mathcal{S}_{u,w}+\mathrm{p}_{22}(w\alpha)\mathcal{S}_{us,w},&us>u.\end{cases}

Here
p_{ij}(\alpha)
for any root
\alpha
are certain elements (and could be constants that do not depend on
\alpha
). We refer to (
29
) as ‘subword recursion’ condition. Examples of
\mathcal{S}_{u,w}
include various cohomology and K-theory classes associated to Schubert cells/varieties ((
32
) and (
35
)), and also the
R
-polynomials (
33
).

It turns out that the subword recursion condition can be transformed into a sub-word formula. For each
w
-word
\mathbf{w}
, with
\beta_{k}=\beta_{\mathbf{w},k}
, we have

(30)

\mathcal{S}_{u,w}=\sum_{u\text{-subwords }\mathbf{u}}~~\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}\begin{cases}\mathrm{p}_{11}(\beta_{k}),&k\in E^{-}_{\mathbf{u}};\\
\mathrm{p}_{12}(\beta_{k}),&k\in J^{+}_{\mathbf{u}};\\
\mathrm{p}_{21}(\beta_{k}),&k\in E^{+}_{\mathbf{u}};\\
\mathrm{p}_{22}(\beta_{k}),&k\in J^{-}_{\mathbf{u}}.\end{cases}

Indeed, it suffices to show that the RHS satisfies the recursive relation as (
29
). This can be proved by using (
28
).

We recall two famous combinatorial formulas that can be derived from subword recursions. The classical AJS-Billey (Anderson, Jantzen, Soergel) formula
[
AJS94
,
Bil99
]
states the localization of the fundamental class of the opposite Schubert variety
[Y(u)]\in H_{T}^{*}(G/B)
at the torus fixed point
\dot{w}B\in G/B
:

(31)

[Y(u)]\big|_{w}=\sum_{\mathbf{u}}\prod_{k\in J_{\mathbf{u}}}\beta_{\mathbf{w},k},

with the sum over reduced
u
-subwords of
\mathbf{w}
(so
J^{-}_{\mathbf{u}}=\varnothing
).
Indeed, in this case it is well-known that if
ws_{\alpha}>w
, then

(32)

[Y(u)]_{ws_{\alpha}}=\begin{cases}[Y(u)]|_{w}+w(\alpha)[Y(us_{\alpha})]|_{w},&us_{\alpha}<u;\\
[Y(u)]|_{w},&us_{\alpha}>u.\end{cases}

One can then derive (
31
) by using (
30
).

Corresponding formulas for the Segre–MacPherson classes (cohomological stable basis), K-theoretic Schubert classes, SMC classes, and elliptic Schubert classes can be found in
[
Gra02
,
Su17
,
SZZ20
,
LXZ25
]
.

The second one is for
R
-polynomials (see Section
2.1
). Assuming
ws>w
, one can rewrite the recursive formula in Section
2.1
as

(33)

R_{u,ws}=\begin{cases}R_{us,w},&us<u;\\
(q-1)R_{u,w}+qR_{us,w},&us>u.\end{cases}

Therefore,
if
\mathbf{w}
be a reduced word of
w
, then it follows from (
30
) that

(34)

R_{u,w}(q)=\sum_{\mathbf{u}}q^{|J_{\mathbf{u}}^{-}|}(q-1)^{|E_{\mathbf{u}}|},

with the sum over distinguished
u
-subwords of
\mathbf{w}
(so
E^{-}_{\mathbf{u}}=\varnothing
).

We derive a subword formula for the localization of
\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})
, which is slightly more explicit than
[
SZZ20
]
.
In type
A
, a diagrammatic way of presenting it can be found in
[
KZJ26
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

For any
w
-word
\mathbf{w}
(not necessarily reduced), we have

\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})|_{w}=\sum_{\text{$u$-subwords $\mathbf{u}$}}(-y)^{|J_{\mathbf{u}}^{-}|}\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}\begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{\beta_{k}}-1}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y},&k\in J_{\mathbf{u}},\\[8.61108pt]
\dfrac{(1+y)e^{\beta_{k}}}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y},&k\in E_{\mathbf{u}}^{-},\\[8.61108pt]
\dfrac{1+y}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y},&k\in E_{\mathbf{u}}^{+},\end{cases}.

Proof.

First of all, if
w=\textrm{id}
,
\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})|_{w}=0
if
u\neq\textrm{id}
. If
u=\textrm{id}
, then
\SMC_{y}(Y(\textrm{id})^{\circ})|_{\textrm{id}}=\sum_{v}\SMC_{y}(Y(v)^{\circ})|_{\textrm{id}}=1
. Moreover, the right hand side also equals
\delta_{u,\textrm{id}}
if
w=\textrm{id}
. Thus, both sides are equal if
w=\textrm{id}
.
By the definition of
T^{R,\vee}_{i}
in (
6
), we have

\gamma|_{ws_{i}}=-\frac{1-e^{w\alpha_{i}}}{y+e^{w\alpha_{i}}}(T_{i}^{R,\vee}\gamma)|_{w}+\frac{(1+y)e^{w\alpha_{i}}}{y+e^{w\alpha_{i}}}\gamma|_{w}

for any class
\gamma
.
By (
9
), we get

(35)

\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})|_{ws_{i}}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{w\alpha_{i}}-1}{e^{w\alpha_{i}}+y}\SMC(Y(us_{i})^{\circ})|_{w}+\dfrac{(1+y)e^{w\alpha_{i}}}{e^{w\alpha_{i}}+y}\SMC(Y(u)^{\circ})|_{w},&us_{i}<u,\\[8.61108pt]
-y\dfrac{e^{w\alpha_{i}}-1}{e^{w\alpha_{i}}+y}\SMC(Y(us_{i})^{\circ})|_{w}+\dfrac{1+y}{e^{w\alpha_{i}}+y}\SMC(Y(u)^{\circ})|_{w},&us_{i}>u.\end{cases}

Now the formula follows immediately from (
30
).
∎

It turns our that this formula specializes to the AJS-Billey formula.
By
[
AMSS24a
, Theorem 5.1]
, the
y=0
specialization of
\SMC_{y}(Y(u)^{\circ})
is

\SMC_{0}(Y(u)^{\circ})=\MC_{0}(Y(u)^{\circ})=[\mathcal{O}_{Y(u)}(-\partial Y(u))]=\sum_{w\geq u}(-1)^{\ell(w)-\ell(u)}[\mathcal{O}_{Y(w)}],

the ideal sheaf of the boundary of the Schubert variety
Y(u)
. Via the equivariant Chern character map
\ch
, we see that
[Y(u)]
is the lowest degree term of
\ch(\SMC_{0}(Y(u)^{\circ}))
. Hence, if we expand
e^{\beta}=1+\beta+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2!}+\cdots
for any root
\beta
, the lowest degree term in
\SMC_{0}(Y(u)^{\circ})|_{w}
is precisely
[Y(u)]|_{w}
. Since
y=0
, only the
u
-subwords with
J_{\mathbf{u}}^{-}=\varnothing
, i.e. reduced subwords, will contribute to
\SMC_{0}(Y(u)^{\circ})|_{w}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to 0}\dfrac{e^{\beta_{k}}-1}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y}

\displaystyle=1-e^{-\beta_{k}}=\beta_{k}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to 0}\dfrac{(1+y)e^{\beta_{k}}}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y}

\displaystyle=1,\qquad\lim_{y\to 0}\dfrac{1+y}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y}=e^{-\beta_{k}}=1+\cdots

It is immediate to see that (
35
) converges to (
32
), so the formula in Proposition
6.2
converges to the AJS-Billey formula.

Now let us explain how this formula relates to the formula for
R
-polynomial (
34
). This limit process has already appeared in
[
BN19
, Theorem 1.2]
from a different perspective.

Proposition 6.3
.

For
u,w\in W
, we have

\lim_{e^{\alpha_{i}}\to 0}\prod_{\alpha>0}\frac{1+y{e^{w\alpha}}}{1-e^{w\alpha}}\SMC(Y(u)^{\circ})|_{w}=R_{u,w}(-y),

where
\lim\limits_{e^{\alpha_{i}}\to 0}
means we let
e^{\alpha_{i}}
approach
0
for any simple root.
When
\mathbf{w}
is reduced, each summand in Proposition
6.2
converges to the corresponding summand in (
34
).

Proof.

Under the assumption that
\mathbf{w}
is a reduced word,
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}
are all positive roots.
The formula follows from the following computation

\displaystyle\lim_{e^{\alpha_{i}}\to 0}\prod_{\alpha>0}\frac{1+y{e^{w\alpha}}}{1-e^{w\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha>0}\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }w\alpha>0\\
(-y),&\text{ if }w\alpha<0\end{cases}=(-y)^{\ell(w)}.

\displaystyle\lim_{e^{\alpha_{i}}\to 0}\frac{e^{\beta_{k}}-1}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y}

\displaystyle=(-y)^{-1},~\lim_{e^{\alpha_{i}}\to 0}\dfrac{(1+y)e^{\beta_{k}}}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y}=0,~\lim_{e^{\alpha_{i}}\to 0}\dfrac{(1+y)}{e^{\beta_{k}}+y}=(-y)^{-1}((-y)-1).\qed

6.3.

Let

There is also a combinatorial formula for it. We define subsets

Proof.

Example 7.2
.

Let
k=1
, and consider

X:=\operatorname{Gr}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.
Let
L_{A}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{A})
be the coordinate plane for a subset
A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
L_{A}^{\circ}\subset L_{A}
be such that

L_{A}=\bigsqcup_{B\subset A}L_{B}^{\circ}
.

There is a bijection between
\mathcal{B}
subsets of
\{1,...,n\}
given by
f\mapsto A_{f}:=\{1\leq i\leq n:f(i)\neq i\}
, and we have

\mathring{\Pi}_{f}=L^{\circ}_{A_{f}}
. Denote
A=A_{f}
and
L=L_{A}
, and we compute
\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f})
geometrically.

The Euler exact sequence over
L

0\longrightarrow T_{L}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{A}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}(-1)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}\longrightarrow 0

gives

\lambda_{y}(T_{L}^{*})=\frac{\lambda_{y}(\mathbb{C}^{A}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}(-1))}{\lambda_{y}(\mathcal{O}_{L})}=\frac{1}{1+y}\prod_{j\in A}\left(1+y\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right).

The structure sheaf of
L=L_{A}
can be represented by Koszul complex

\bigotimes_{j\notin A}[\mathcal{O}(-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x_{j}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{X}]=\prod_{j\notin A}\left(1-\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right).

Thus,

\MC_{y}(L_{A})=\frac{1}{1+y}\cdot\prod_{j\in A}\left(1+y\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right)\cdot\prod_{j\notin A}\left(1-\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right)=\frac{1}{1+y}\cdot\prod_{j\in A}\left(1+\frac{(1+y)\frac{x}{t_{j}}}{1-\frac{x}{t_{j}}}\right)\cdot\prod_{j}\left(1-\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right),

and

\MC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f})=\MC_{y}(L_{A}^{\circ})=\frac{1}{1+y}\cdot\prod_{j\in A}\left((1+y)\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right)\cdot\prod_{j\notin A}\left(1-\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right).

As a result,

\SMC_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f})=\frac{\operatorname{MC}_{y}(\mathring{\Pi}_{f})}{\lambda_{y}(T_{X}^{*})}=\prod_{j\in A}\frac{(1+y)\frac{x}{t_{j}}}{1+y\frac{x}{t_{j}}}\cdot\prod_{j\notin A}\frac{1-\frac{x}{t_{j}}}{1+y\frac{x}{t_{j}}}=\prod_{j\in A}\frac{(1+y)x}{t_{j}+yx}\cdot\prod_{j\notin A}\frac{t_{j}-x}{t_{j}+yx}.
