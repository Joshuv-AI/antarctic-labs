The Memory-Enhanced Gaussian Noise (MEGN) Model for Fiber-Optic Channels
Kaiquan Wu, Gabriele Liga, Marco Secondini, Stella Civelli, Hussam Batshon, Greg Raybon, Xi Chen, Alex Alvarado

Abstract
The enhanced Gaussian noise (EGN) model is widely used for estimating the nonlinear interference (NLI) power accumulated in coherent fiber-optic transmission systems. Given a fixed fiber link, under the assumption that transmitted symbols are independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.), the EGN model establishes that the NLI power depends on time-invariant signal statistics, i.e., the second-, fourth-, and sixth-order moments of the symbols, which are determined by the modulation format and its probability distribution. However, recent advances in coded modulation have sought to mitigate NLI by introducing controlled temporal correlations among transmitted symbols, thereby violating the i.i.d. assumption underlying the EGN model. Among these correlations, symbol energy correlations are believed to exert the most significant influence on NLI. This work presents a rigorous mathematical derivation of a memory extension of the EGN model that explicitly accounts for symbol energy correlations, referred to as the MEGN model. The proposed MEGN model is validated through both numerical simulations and transmission experiments. Normalized average NLI power estimations with less than 5 % 5\% errors across a wide range of symbol rates and transmission distances are reported. The model also provides a theoretical framework for analyzing and optimizing optical transmission systems employing temporally correlated modulation schemes.



I
Introduction

The fiber channel degrades the transmitted signal through signal-dependent nonlinear interference (NLI). The generation of NLI is a complex process arising from the joint effects of fiber impairments and the transmitted signal. An NLI power model that characterizes the relationship between signal statistics and the resulting NLI power is therefore valuable for various applications. For example, an accurate NLI power model provides important insights for the design of NLI-tolerant coded modulation (CM) schemes and enables offline system configuration optimization with respect to parameters such as the symbol rate.

An NLI power spectral density (PSD) model typically follows a common structure that can be expressed as

\displaystyle\text{NLI PSD}=\sum\text{Signal Statistics}\times\text{Channel Functions}.

(1)

This structure highlights that NLI arises from the interplay between the statistical properties of the transmitted symbols (signal statistics) and the physical characteristics of the fiber channel (channel functions).

Fig.
1
(a) summarizes the symbol-level signal statistics captured by state-of-the-art NLI PSD models. The first pioneering model is the Gaussian noise (GN) model
[
2
]
, revealing that the NLI power simply depends on the average symbol energy (a.k.a. the second-order moment) that is equivalent to the signal power. More precisely, the GN model describes that the NLI power is proportional to the cube of the signal power. However, as its name suggests, the GN model oversimplifies the signal waveform by treating it as a Gaussian process. In reality, coherent systems employ CM to transmit quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) symbols, and the modulated pulse train generally violates the Gaussian-process assumption. As a consequence, the GN model fails to capture the dependence of NLI on the underlying modulation format.

The enhanced Gaussian noise (EGN) model
[
3
,
4
]
is an alternative to the GN model that has been very successful in various applications. The EGN model is derived under the assumption of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) symbols. This assumption typically holds when CM schemes (
i
) employ a random interleaver or (
ii
) use sufficiently long blocklengths such that the generated symbols asymptotically mimic an i.i.d. process. The EGN model shows that accounting for the fourth- and sixth-order moments of the transmitted symbols, determined by the 2D modulation format, improves the accuracy of NLI power prediction compared to the GN model
[
3
,
4
]
, as illustrated in Fig.
1
(a). The
Kurtosis
of the transmitted symbol, a metric incorporating the second- and fourth-order moments, has been widely used to improve the design of CM to reduce NLI power. Particularly in the context of constellation shaping
[
5
,
6
]
, the EGN model can be used to improve the geometry or the probability mass function (PMF) of the QAM symbols for better NLI tolerance.

For advanced CM schemes that generate nonstationary and correlated symbols, the EGN model becomes inadequate. Relaxing the i.i.d. symbol assumption across the four optical field dimensions and the time dimension has therefore become an important research direction. Several refined extensions of the EGN model have been proposed. For example, the 4D-EGN model
[
7
]
considers various temporal correlations of symbols across the polarizations, and shows that temporal symbol-energy correlations play a dominant role in the resulting NLI power (see Fig.
1
(a)). These symbol statistics partially explains the effectiveness of heuristically designed high-dimensional modulation formats for NLI mitigation
[
8
]
.

Due to chromatic dispersion and fiber nonlinearity, the optical fiber can be modeled as a channel with memory, in which symbols interact in a nonlinear manner. Ideally, the channel functions in (
1
) should account for such a memory, while the signal statistics should account for the correlations among the interacting symbols. In fact, it has been shown that by varying the strength and length of the temporal symbol-energy correlations, it is possible to control the generated NLI strength
[
9
]
. Some advanced CM schemes, such as temporal probabilistic shaping
[
10
]
, short-blocklength probabilistic amplitude shaping (PAS)
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
and PAS with sequence selection
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
have recently emerged to exploit temporal correlations of symbols (energy-wise or phase-wise) for the purpose of NLI reduction. In particular, PAS, although originally designed for additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channels, can mitigate NLI simply by decreasing the shaping blocklength, in which case the transmitted symbols possess strong temporal symbol-energy correlations. However, only limited progress has been made toward incorporating such correlations in the NLI power models, aside from a few attempts using
heuristic models

[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
. The additional nonlinear shaping gains brought by the temporal symbol-energy correlations, currently lack rigorous support from an underlying theoretical model. Without an effective channel model, these techniques must rely on computationally intensive split-step Fourier method (SSFM) simulations
[
10
,
14
]
or heuristic NLI metrics
[
15
,
19
,
22
,
21
]
to reflect the NLI power induced by the correlated symbols, which limits interpretability and generalization.

In this paper, to the best of our knowledge, we present the first EGN-based
analytical model
that incorporates the temporal symbol-energy correlations of the transmitted symbols. We call this model the memory-EGN (MEGN) model. The proposed model can be viewed as an extension of the aforementioned GN-type NLI power models, as it accounts for additional temporal symbol-energy correlations such as between the second- and fourth-order moments, as illustrated in Fig.
1
(a). Specifically, as illustrated in Fig.
1
(b), the MEGN model accounts for three types of symbol-energy interactions spanning the time and polarization domains:
(
i
) self-polarization temporal interactions (SPT),
(
ii
) cross-polarization temporal interactions (XPT), and
(
iii
) purely cross-polarization interactions (XP).

In this work, we restrict our analysis on NLI to
single-channel interference
(SCI). This model is evaluated for PAS schemes with constant-composition distribution matcher (CCDM) shaped amplitudes
[
11
,
23
]
and independent and uniformly distributed (i.u.d.) signs. The transmitted symbol energies are therefore (
i
) temporally correlated and (
ii
) exhibiting nonstationary higher-order statistics. We also examine different signaling scenarios where such correlations may extend to the I/Q and polarizations through the amplitude-to-symbol mapping strategies proposed in
[
24
]
. Moreover, analytical expressions for the energy correlations of the considered signaling schemes are derived and presented. Our results show that the proposed model exhibits excellent accuracy, achieving an estimation error for the normalized average NLI power below
5\%
for various symbol rates and distances.

II
The Memory-EGN (MEGN) Model

This section presents the MEGN model. Considering the NLI PSD characterized by the EGN model, denoted as
G_{\text{EGN}}(f)
, the proposed MEGN model accounts for the PSD deviation from
G_{\text{EGN}}(f)
caused by the temporal correlations of symbol energies, denoted as
G_{C}(f)
. Therefore, the corrected PSD by MEGN is

\displaystyle G_{\text{MEGN}}(f)=G_{\text{EGN}}(f)+G_{C}(f).

(2)

As shown in Fig.
1

G_{C}(f)
in (
2

\displaystyle G_{C}(f)=G_{\text{SPT}}(f)+G_{\text{XPT}}(f)+G_{\text{XP}}(f),

(3)

where the term

[
7
]
. The main contribution of the present model is hence to discover the terms
G_{\text{SPT}}(f)
and
G_{\text{XPT}}(f)
, manifesting themselves as the
memory
effects of the fiber channel. Following the structure in (
1
), these terms are expressed as the product of symbol-energy correlations and the channel functions.

and

II-B

Symbol-Energy Correlation Statistics

From now on, we treat the sequence of symbol energies
[\ldots,|a^{\mathrm{x}}_{i}|^{2},|a^{\mathrm{y}}_{i}|^{2},|a^{\mathrm{x}}_{i+1}|^{2},|a^{\mathrm{y}}_{i+1}|^{2},\ldots]
as a random process. The following assumptions on their statistics are made:

1.

first-order stationarity;

2.

symmetric with respect to both polarizations;

3.

wide-sense cyclostationarity with
finite
fundamental period equal to the symbol correlation length
M_{s}
.

Assumption 1) indicates that the symbol statistics at individual times are time invariant
[
13
]
. Assumption 2) further implies identical statistics for
\mathrm{x}
and
\mathrm{y}

\displaystyle P_{\text{ch}}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\{|a|^{2}\},\quad\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{4}\right]=\mathbb{E}\{|a|^{4}\}.

(11)

where
\mathrm{p}\in\{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\}
and

denotes the average symbol energy per polarization. The energy correlations of interest are

\displaystyle R_{1}^{\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}(i,i^{\prime})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}}|^{2}\right],

\displaystyle R_{2}^{\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}(i,i^{\prime})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a_{p\!,i}|^{2}|a_{p^{\prime}\!,i^{\prime}}|^{4}\right],

\displaystyle K_{1}^{\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}(i,i^{\prime})\triangleq R_{1}^{\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}(i,i^{\prime})-\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{2},

(13a)

III
Case Study: Energy Correlation of Symbols with Constant Composition Amplitudes

The symbol-energy correlations (or covariances) required in (
26
) and (
30
) are generic quantities that can be computed numerically from the transmitted data, either by brute-force evaluation or more efficiently using progressive binning with logarithmic density
[
28
,
29
]
, or by an FFT-based approach via the Wiener–Khinchin theorem
[
30
]
. Alternatively, analytical expressions might be derived for specific shaping algorithms with known structural properties.

CCDM is one of the most widely used distribution-matching algorithms for PAS. Due to the block-wise energy constraint imposed by CCDM, the symbol energies exhibit an inherent negative correlation
[
13
]
, yielding negative covariance in (
14
).

In this section, we present the analytical expressions of QAM symbols generated by
ideal
CCDM, meaning that all possible amplitude blocks with the same constant composition are used as CCDM codewords with equal probability. First, we present analytical expressions for the correlations between amplitudes within the same CCDM shaping block in Sec.
III-A
. These correlated amplitudes are then mapped onto the I/Q of the
\mathrm{x}
and
\mathrm{y}
polarizations to form blocks of correlated QAM symbols, where the resulting correlation structure depends on the chosen mapping strategy. In this work, only the 2-D, 2-D, and 4-D mapping strategies are considered
[
24
]
. Next, we show analytical expressions for the correlations between QAM symbols within a shaped block in Sec.
III-B
. Finally, inter-block effect is considered, and analytical expressions for the energy covariance functions in (
26
) and (
30
) are presented in Sec.
III-C
. In particular,
\bar{K}_{\text{S}1}(\tau)
in (
26a
) was derived in our previous work
[
13
, Appendix A]
, and for brevity the derivations of other energy correlations are omitted here.

III-A

Intra-Block Amplitude Correlation

For amplitude
u
in the alphabet
\mathcal{U}
, consider the generation of constant composition codeword as drawing amplitudes in series from
N
amplitudes without replacement. The number of each possible amplitude is a constant number denoted by
N_{u}
in every sequence, and thus
N=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}N_{u}
. Although the composition is pre-determined, the ordering of amplitudes is uncertain. We can easily obtain its
k
-th order moments

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[u^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}u^{k}\frac{N_{u}}{N}.

(37)

The amplitudes within each codeword are inherently correlated due to the constant composition. For the relevant energy-level statistics when
k
is a multiple integer of 2, we consider the energy-correlations given in the following theorems.

Theorem 4
.

Consider a constant-composition amplitude block
(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{N})
with amplitudes drawn from the alphabet
\mathcal{U}
. For any distinct amplitude-position indices
i,i^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with
i\neq i^{\prime}
, the correlation of the second-order moments is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[u_{i}^{2}u_{i^{\prime}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{N\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[u^{4}\right]}{N-1},

(38)

\displaystyle<\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]^{2}.

(39)

Furthermore, the correlation between the second- and fourth-order moments is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{2}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[u_{i}^{2}u_{i^{\prime}}^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{N\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[u^{4}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[u^{6}\right]}{N-1},

(40)

\displaystyle<\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[u^{4}\right].

(41)

Theorem 5
.

Consider a constant-composition amplitude block
(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{N})
with amplitudes drawn from the alphabet
\mathcal{U}
. For any distinct amplitude position indices
\forall i,i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime}\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with
i\neq i^{\prime},i\neq i^{\prime\prime},i^{\prime}\neq i^{\prime\prime}
, the correlation among a triplet of the second-order moments is

\displaystyle\rho_{3}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[u_{i}^{2}u_{i^{\prime}}^{2}u_{i^{\prime\prime}}^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle\;\frac{N^{2}}{(N-1)(N-2)}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]^{3}

\displaystyle-\frac{3N}{(N-1)(N-2)}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[u^{4}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{(N-1)(N-2)}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{6}\right],

(42)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\;\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]^{3}.

(43)

It is readily verified from Theorems
4
and
5
that these intra-block amplitude-energy correlations vanish as
N\to\infty
.

Without loss of generality, in the following analysis, the shaping blocklength
N
is assumed to be an integer multiple of four. Hence, for
H
-D mapping, the time-domain QAM symbol vectors
(a^{\mathrm{x}}_{i},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{i})
are correlated over each block of length

\displaystyle M_{s}=N/H,

(44)

which therefore defines the correlation length. Using Theorems
4
and
5
, the temporal symbol-energy correlations within a block are expressed as follows.

Theorem 6
.

Consider a block of QAM symbols
(a^{\mathrm{x}}_{1},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{1},a^{\mathrm{x}}_{2},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{2},\ldots,a^{\mathrm{x}}_{M_{s}},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{M_{s}})
, generated by mapping a constant-composition amplitude block
(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{N})
, with amplitudes drawn from the alphabet
\mathcal{U}
, using
H
-D mapping. Then, for any distinct time indices
i,i^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\ldots,M_{s}\}
and any
\mathrm{p},\mathrm{p}^{\prime}\in\{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\}
, the second-order moment correlation in the SPT case with
\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{S1}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i^{\prime}}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}2\rho_{1}+2\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]^{2},&\text{if}$H=1$,\\
4\rho_{1},&\text{if}$H=2$\text{or}$4$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

The XPT case with
\mathrm{p}\neq\mathrm{p}^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{X1}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}4\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]^{2},&\text{if}$H=1$\text{or}$2$,\\
4\rho_{1},&\text{if}$H=4$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

Furthermore, for the correlation between the second- and fourth-order moments, the SPT case with
\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{S2}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i^{\prime}}|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}2\rho_{2}+2\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[u^{4}\right]+4\rho_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right],&\text{if}$H=1$,\\
4\rho_{2}+4\rho_{3},&\text{if}$H=2$\text{or}$4$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

the XPT case with
\mathrm{p}\neq\mathrm{p}^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{X2}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}}|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}4\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[u^{4}\right]+4\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]^{3},&\text{if}$H=1$,\\
4\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[u^{4}\right]+4\rho_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right],&\text{if}$H=2$,\\
4\rho_{2}+4\rho_{3},&\text{if}$H=4$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

Theorem 7
.

Consider a block of QAM symbols
(a^{\mathrm{x}}_{1},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{1},a^{\mathrm{x}}_{2},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{2},\ldots,a^{\mathrm{x}}_{M_{s}},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{M_{s}})
, generated by mapping a constant-composition amplitude block
(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{N})
, with amplitudes drawn from the alphabet
\mathcal{U}
, using
H
-D mapping. Then, for any distinct time indices
\forall i,i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime}\in\{1,2,\ldots,M_{s}\}
satisfying
i\neq i^{\prime}
,
i\neq i^{\prime\prime}
, and
i^{\prime}\neq i^{\prime\prime}
, and
\forall\mathrm{p},\mathrm{p}^{\prime},\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}\in\{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\}
, the correlation among a triplet of second-order moments is characterized as follows. In the SPT case with
\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}
, it is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{S3}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i^{\prime}}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i^{\prime\prime}}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}2\rho_{3}+6\rho_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right],&\text{if}$H=1$,\\
8\rho_{3},&\text{if}$H=2$\text{or}$4$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

In the XPT case with
\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime},\mathrm{p}^{\prime}\neq\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}
, it is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{X3}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}}_{i^{\prime\prime}}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}4\rho_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]+4\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right]^{3},&\text{if}$H=1$,\\
8\rho_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}\right],&\text{if}$H=2$,\\
8\rho_{3},&\text{if}$H=4$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

III-C

Inter-Block Symbol-Energy Correlation

The MEGN model requires the time-averaged covariances as defined in (
14
), which accounts for the inter-block effects that the two QAM symbols may be independent when positioned at different shaped blocks. Based intra-block correlations in Theorems
6
and
7
, the resulting time-averaged symbol-energy correlations are given as follows.

Theorem 8
.

Consider a sequence of QAM symbols
(a^{\mathrm{x}}_{1},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{1},a^{\mathrm{x}}_{2},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{2},\ldots)
, obtained by mapping constant-composition amplitude blocks of blocklength
N
, with amplitudes drawn from the alphabet
\mathcal{U}
, using
H
-D mapping. For
N
being an integer multiple of four, the correlation length is
M_{s}=N/H
. Then, for any time delay
\tau>0
and any
\mathrm{p},\mathrm{p}^{\prime}\in\{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\}
, the time-averaged second-order moment correlation in the SPT case with
\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\text{S}1}(\tau)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{M_{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{M_{s}}\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i+\tau}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}\frac{\tau\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{2}+(M_{s}-\tau)\rho_{S1}}{M_{s}},&\text{if}$ \tau< M_s$,\\
\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{2},&\text{if}$ \tau\geq M_s$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

Furthermore, for the correlation between the second- and fourth-order moments, it is given by

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\text{S}2}(\tau)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{M_{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{M_{s}}\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i+\tau}|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}\frac{\tau\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{4}\right]+(M_{s}-\tau)\rho_{S2}}{M_{s}},&\text{if}$\tau< M_s$,\\
\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{4}\right],&\text{if}$ \tau\geq M_s$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

In the XPT case with
\mathrm{p}\neq\mathrm{p}^{\prime}
,
\bar{R}_{\text{X}1}(\tau)
and
\bar{R}_{\text{X}2}(\tau)
can be obtained in the same manner as in (
8
) and (
8
) by replacing
\rho_{S1}
,
\rho_{S2}
with
\rho_{X1}
,
\rho_{X2}
.

Theorem 9
.

Consider a sequence of QAM symbols
(a^{\mathrm{x}}_{1},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{1},a^{\mathrm{x}}_{2},a^{\mathrm{y}}_{2},\ldots)
, obtained by mapping constant-composition amplitude blocks of blocklength
N
, with amplitudes drawn from the alphabet
\mathcal{U}
, using
H
-D mapping. For
N
being an integer multiple of four, the correlation length is
M_{s}=N/H
. Then, for time delays
\tau^{\prime}>\tau>0
, and
\forall\mathrm{p},\mathrm{p}^{\prime},\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}\in\{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\}
, the correlations among a triplet of second-order moments is characterized as follows. In the SPT case with
\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}
, it is given in (
9
).

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\text{S}3}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{M_{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{M_{s}}\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i+\tau}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i+\tau^{\prime}}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}\frac{\tau^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]\rho_{S1}+(M_{s}-\tau^{\prime})\rho_{S3}}{M_{s}},&\text{if\>}$\tau^\prime< M_s$,\\
\frac{\tau\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{3}+(M_{s}-\tau)\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]\rho_{S1}}{M_{s}},&\text{if\>}$\tau^\prime\geq M_s$,$\tau< M_s$\text{\>and\>}$\tau^\prime-\tau\geq M_s$,\\
\frac{(\tau^{\prime}-M_{s})\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{3}+(2M_{s}-\tau^{\prime})\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]\rho_{S1}}{M_{s}},&\text{if\>}$ \tau^\prime\geq M_s $,$\tau< M_s$\text{\>and\>}$\tau^\prime-\tau< M_s$,\\
\frac{(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{3}+(M_{s}-\tau^{\prime}+\tau)\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]\rho_{S1}}{M_{s}},&\text{if\>}$\tau\geq M_s$\text{\>and\>}$\tau^\prime-\tau< M_s$,\\
\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{3},&\text{otherwise.}\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

In the XPT case with
\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathrm{p}^{\prime}\neq\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}
, the result for 4D mapping is given as in (
9
) by replacing
\rho_{S1}
,
\rho_{S3}
with
\rho_{X1}
,
\rho_{X3}
. For 1-D and 2-D mapping, since polarizations are independent of each other, we have

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\text{X}3}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{M_{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{M_{s}}\mathbb{E}\left[|a^{\mathrm{p}}_{i}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}_{i+\tau}|^{2}|a^{\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}}_{i+\tau^{\prime}}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][c]{l^{\prime}s}\frac{\tau\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{3}+(M_{s}-\tau)\rho_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]}{M_{s}},&\text{if}$ \tau< M_s$\\
\mathbb{E}\left[|a|^{2}\right]^{3},&\text{if}$ \tau\geq M_s$.\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}\right.

Finally, the symbol-energy covariances in (

IV-C

MEGN Model Breakdown and Approximation

In this section, we evaluate the NLI contributions of the SPT, XP, and XPT symbol-energy interactions, together with the effect of the memory length in the MEGN model. Based on these results, we validate the approximations proposed for the MEGN model in Sec.
II-F
.

The overall contributions from the different interaction types are summarized in Fig.
7
.
We consider the representative case of shaping blocklength
N=100
with 4-D mapping.
First, both XP and XPT contributions increase almost linearly with distance, with XPT contributing slightly more than XP.
This trend highlights the importance of temporal cross-polarization interactions when designing high-dimensional modulation formats.
Second, the SPT contribution is initially lower than XP and XPT in the first few spans, but it grows more rapidly with distance and eventually surpasses both.
This indicates that, for reducing NLI through high-dimensional modulation formats, the dominant interaction to target (intra-pol. vs. inter-pol.) depends strongly on the intended transmission distance. Finally, the contribution from SPT/XPT interactions involving three symbols is negligible, which is more than two orders of magnitude smaller than the other terms. This observation justifies the approximation in (
2

Fig.

shows the prediction accuracy, quantified by the NLI power-coefficient error

VI
Conclusions

We have presented a
novel analytical model
for predicting NLI power in optical systems that utilize symbol correlation across the four dimensions of the optical field. Our key finding demonstrates that the proposed model achieves high accuracy, which was rigorously validated against simulations and experiment. Crucially, the model’s performance excels where established methods like the EGN model fail to converge, particularly in the short-blocklength regime, which is a critical area for practical PAS operation range.

While this work focused on PAS, the fundamental principles allow for a straightforward extension to other advanced high-dimensional modulation. The use of this model is significant for several key applications:
(i)
capacity analysis: Our model quantitatively reveals how correlation across optical field dimensions can be strategically exploited to suppress NLI, opening a new avenue for refining capacity limits and optimizing spectral efficiency in fiber-optic channels.
(ii)
the DSP for NLI mitigation: The model serves as a powerful tool for DSP optimization, for example, to improve the sequences selection in PAS or guide the design of multi-dimensional modulation formats.
(iii)
system optimization: The model enables the selection of optimal signaling and channel parameters, such as identifying the best combination of shaping blocklength, mapping strategy, and symbol rate for a given system configuration.

Finally, the current framework focuses on SCI and can be directly applied to evaluate SCI in WDM transmission scenarios. Moreover, the present MEGN framework can be naturally extended to model cross-channel interference (XCI) and multi-channel interference (MCI). Since XCI and MCI are the dominant impairments in practical WDM systems, extending the proposed short-blocklength-compatible model to account for these effects constitutes a natural and impactful direction for future work.

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}^{\prime}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}R_{3}^{\mathrm{p}\mathrm{p}^{\prime}\mathrm{p}^{\prime\prime}}(i,i+\tau,i+\tau^{\prime}).

(59c)
