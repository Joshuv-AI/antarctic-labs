commuting probability of skew left braces
Susanta Mondal, Manoj K. Yadav

Abstract
. We introduce a concept of the commuting probability of a skew left brace analogous to group theory. We establish upper and lower bounds for the commuting probability and prove that, for finite non-trivial skew left braces, it is always at most 3 4 \frac{3}{4} . Interestingly, there is no skew left brace with commuting probability in the open interval ( 5 / 8 , 1 ) (5/8,1) , except 3 4 \frac{3}{4} , for which we construct an explicit example. A characterization of skew left braces having commuting probability 3 4 \frac{3}{4} or 5 8 \frac{5}{8} is presented. We further show that the finite skew left braces with commuting probability larger than 65 128 \frac{65}{128} are necessarily nilpotent. We prove that the commuting probability remains invariant under isoclinism of skew braces. We introduce a concept of a compact Hausdorff topological skew left brace B B , where we prove that the set of all elements of B B having finite centraliser index in B B is a Borel subgroup. For such infinite non-trivial skew left braces too 3 4 \frac{3}{4} is the upper bound for the commuting probability, and 3 4 \frac{3}{4} is the only rational number which occurs as commuting probability in the open interval ( 5 / 8 , 1 ) (5/8,1) .

1.
Introduction

Commuting probability of groups has long been studied in the literature, starting from its genesis in
[
13
]
by Erdos and Turan, and its inception in
[
18
]
by Gustafson for groups. This article aims at introducing an analogous concept for skew left braces and obtaining several fundamental results. A set
B
equipped with two group structures
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
is said to be a skew left brace if
a\circ(b+c)=(a\circ b)-a+(a\circ c)
for all
a,b,c\in B
, where
-a
is the inverse of
a
in
(B,+)
. It turns out that the group identities of both the groups
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
coincide, which we denote by
1
. If
(B,+)
is an abelian group, the skew left braces is called a left brace.
The concept of left braces and skew left braces were, respectively, introduced by Rump
[
30
]
and Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
17
]
in connection with the study of set-theoretical solutions of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation, suggested by Drienfeld
[
12
]
.

For a finite skew left brace
(B,+,\circ)
, we define commuting probability, denoted by
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
, by the quantity

\operatorname{Pb}(B):=\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}|\{(x,y)\in B\times B\mid x+y-x-y=x\circ y\circ x^{-1}\circ y^{-1}=-x+(x\circ y)-y=1\}|,

where
x^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
x
in
(B,\circ)
. For infinite skew left brace, we first introduce the concept of compact Hausdorff topological skew left brace, and then the concept of a measure on the closed subsets of the skew left brace. To be more precise, we say that a skew left brace
B
topological if there exists a common topology of subsets of
B
under which both the groups
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are topological groups. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on the group
(B,\circ)
and

Z:=\{(x,y)\in B\times B\mid x+y-x-y=x\circ y\circ x^{-1}\circ y^{-1}=-x+(x\circ y)-y=1\}.

Then the commuting probability of a compact Hausdorff topological skew left brace
B
, again denoted by
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
, is defined by

\operatorname{Pb}(B):=(\mu\times\mu)(Z).

See Section 5 for more details.

The article is constituted of four sections after the present one. The second section is on preliminaries and some key observations. It is observed that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
, the brace centraliser of
x\in B
is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
. Also the set
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
of all elements of
B
whose centralisers have finite index in
(B,\circ)
is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
. Commuting probability for finite skew braces is introduced in Section 3, where several bounds on it are obtained. We prove that if
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
is different from
\frac{3}{4}
and
1
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\in(0,\frac{5}{8}]
. Further,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{3}{4}
if and only if
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is of order
2
, where
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
denotes the annihilator of the skew brace
B
. It is proved that if
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{5}{8}
, then both the additive and the multiplicative groups of quotient skew brace
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. It is also proved that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(A)
for any sub-skew brace
A
of a finite skew brace
B
. Connections between commuting probability and nilpotency of skew left braces is investigated in Section 4. It is proved that a finite skew brace
B
with
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\geq\frac{65}{128}
is nilpotent. In the same section, we also show that commuting probability is invariant under isoclinism of skew left braces. In the concluding section, Section 5, we introduce the concept of commuting probability of compact Hausdorff topological skew left braces, where, among several interesting results, we prove that
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
is a Borel set, and
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
is open in
B
if and only if
\operatorname{Pb}(B)>0
. It is also established that if
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
is different from
\frac{3}{4}
and
1
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\in[0,\frac{5}{8}]
.

We conclude this section by setting some notations. As mentioned above, the common additive and multiplicative identity of a skew left brace is denoted by
1
. For a skew left brace
(B,+,\circ)
and
a,b\in B
, the commutator
[a,b]^{+}=a+b-a-b
in
(B,+)
is denoted by
\gamma^{+}(a,b)
and the commutator
[a,b]^{\circ}=a\circ b\circ a^{-1}\circ b^{-1}
in
(B,\circ)
is denoted by
\gamma^{\circ}(a,b)
. Left normed higher commutators in
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are defined inductively. The center of a group
G
is denoted by
\operatorname{Z}(G)
, and for any element
g\in G
, the centraliser of
g
in
G
is denoted by
\operatorname{C}_{G}(g)
.

2.
Preliminaries and some key results

A non-empty set
B
, equipped with two group structures
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
, is said to be a
skew left brace
if for
a,b,c\in B
the following compatibility condition (skew left distributivity) holds:

a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c,

where
-a
is the inverse of
a
in
(B,+)
. A
skew right brace
can be defined analogously, that is, demanding skew right distributivity.
A skew left brace
(B,+,\circ)
is said to be a
two-sided skew brace
if
(B,+,\circ)
is also a skew right brace. Note that if
(B,+,\circ)
is a skew left brace with
(B,\circ)
abelian, then
(B,+,\circ)
is a two-sided skew brace.

The groups
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are, respectively, called
additive group
and
multiplicative group
of the skew left brace
(B,+,\circ)
. For simplicity of notation we denote a skew left brace
(B,+,\circ)
by
B
. If we want to emphasise the role of group operations, then we use the original notation. We will normally drop the use of word ‘left’ from skew left brace, and just say skew brace, unless it is necessary to specify the property.

Every group
(B,+)
can viewed as a skew brace by defining
\circ=+
. Such a skew brace is called
trivial
. A skew brace which is not a trivial skew brace is called a
non-trivial skew brace
.
Easiest among non-trivial skew braces are skew braces
(B,+,+_{op})
with
(B,+)
any non-abelian group and
(B,+_{op})
the opposite group of
(B,+)
, that is,
a+_{op}b=b+a
for all
a,b\in B
.

Let
X
be a given property of groups. A skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
is said to be
X
-type
if its additive group
(B,+)
satisfies the property
X
. If
X
is the abelian property of groups, then a skew left brace
(B,+,\circ)
is said to be
abelian-type
. An abelian-type skew brace is called a
brace
in the literature. For simplicity of notation, ‘an abelian-type trivial skew brace’ will be termed as ‘a trivial brace’.
Let
\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)
denote the automorphism group of the group
(B,+)
. For a skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
and its element
a
, define a map
\lambda_{a}:(B,+)\to(B,+)
by setting

\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+(a\circ b)

for all
b\in B
. It is now well known that
\lambda_{a}
is an automorphism of
(B,+)
and the map
\lambda:(B,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)
;
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
is a group homomorphism.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace. A subset
H
of
B
is said to be a
sub-skew brace
of the skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
if
(H,+,\circ)
is also a skew brace. For a given subset
S
of
B
, the sub-skew brace generated by
S
is defined to be the smallest sub-skew brace of
B
containing
S
. A sub-skew brace
(I,+,\circ)
of
(B,+,\circ)
is said to be a left ideal of
(B,+,\circ)
if
\lambda_{a}(I)\subseteq I
for all
a\in B
. A left ideal
(I,+,\circ)
of
(B,+,\circ)
is said to be an ideal of
(B,+,\circ)
if
(I,+)
and
(I,\circ)
are normal subgroups of
(B,+)
and
(B,,\circ)
respectively. An ideal generated by a given subset
S
of
B
is defined to be the smallest ideal of
B
containing
S
. It is easy to see that the kernel of the homomorphism
\lambda
is given by

\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)=\{a\in B\mid a+b=a\circ b\mbox{ for all }b\in B\}.

For two skew braces
A
and
B
, a map
f:A\to B
is said to be a
brace homomorphism
if
f
is a group homomorphism from the additive group of
A
to the additive group of
B
and also from the multiplicative group
A
to the multiplicative group of
B
. For any brace homomorphism
f:A\to B
, it turns out that
\operatorname{Ker}(f)
is an ideal of
A
. A bijective brace homomorphism is called a
brace isomorphism
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace and
a,b\in B
, define

a\ast b:=\lambda_{a}(b)-b=-a+(a\circ b)-b.

The
socle
of
B
, denoted by
\operatorname{Soc}(B)
, is defined by

\operatorname{Soc}(B):=\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Z}(B,+).

The
annihilator
of
B
, denoted by
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, is defined by

\operatorname{Ann}(B):=\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)\cap Z(B,+)\cap\operatorname{Z}(B,\circ)=\operatorname{Soc}(B)\cap\operatorname{Z}(B,\circ).

It is not very difficult to see that
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is an ideal of
B
. It follows from
[
17
, Lemma 2.5]
or
[
2
, Proposition 2.9]
that
\operatorname{Soc}(B)
is also ideal of
B
. We now define higher annihilators of
B
. For
n\geq 2
, define
\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)
, the
n
th annihilator of
B
, by

\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)/\operatorname{Ann}_{n-1}(B)=\operatorname{Ann}\big(B/\operatorname{Ann}_{n-1}(B)\big).

This is possible because
\operatorname{Ann}_{n-1}(B)
is an ideal of
B

[
24
, Definition 2.2]
. It turns out that

\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)=\{a\in B\mid a*b,b*a,[a,b]^{+}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{n-1}(B)\mbox{ for all }b\in B\}.

This is an ascending series of ideals of
B
, which is called the
annihilator series
. We shall view this series analogous to the upper central series of a group.

Let
B:=(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace and
\gamma_{n}(B,+)
and
\gamma_{n}(B,\circ)
, respectively, denote the
n
th terms of the lower central series of
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
. For the left ideals
I
and
J
of a skew brace
B
, define

I*J:=\langle x*y\mid x\in I,y\in J\rangle^{+},

the subgroup generated by the set
\{x*y\mid x\in I,y\in J\}
in
(B,+)
.
Set
B^{1}=B
and, recursively, define

B^{n}:=B*B^{n-1}=\left<a*b\mid a\in B,b\in B^{n-1}\right>^{+}.

It is well known that
B^{2}
is an ideal of
B
. But
B^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, in general is not an ideal of
B
. However,
B^{n}
is a left ideal of
B
. Further, set
B^{(1)}=B
and, recursively, define

B^{(n)}:=B^{(n-1)}*B=\left<b*a\mid a\in B,b\in B^{(n-1)}\right>^{+}.

It is proved in
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
that
B^{(n)}
is an ideal of
B
.

We consider the lower central series of a skew brace defined in
[
6
]
.
Set
\Gamma_{1}(B):=B
and define

\Gamma_{n}(B):=\left<B*\Gamma_{n-1}(B),\Gamma_{n-1}(B)*B,[B,\Gamma_{n-1}(B)]^{+}\right>^{+},

where
[B,\Gamma_{n-1}(B)]^{+}
denotes the subgroup of
(B,+)
generated by the set
\{[a,u]^{+}\mid a\in B,u\in\Gamma_{n-1}(B)\}
.
The authors of
[
6
]
start with
0
index, but we start with
1
.
\Gamma_{n}(B)
is an ideal of
B
and
\Gamma_{n+1}(B)\leq\Gamma_{n}(B)
for all integers
n\geq 1
. We shall view this series of ideals of a skew left brace analogous to the lower central series of a group.

A skew brace
B
is said to be
nilpotent
if there exists an integer
n
such that
\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)=B
. The least such integer is called the
nilpotency class
of
B
. The authors of
[
6
]
call such skew braces centrally nilpotent and prove that
\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)=B
if and only if
\Gamma_{n+1}(B)=1
(
[
6
, Theorem 2.8]
).

We define the
brace centralizer
of an element
x\in B
to be the subset

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{b\in A\mid x*b=b*x=[x,b]^{+}=1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{b\in B\mid x*b=[x,b]^{\circ}=[x,b]^{+}=1\}.

The
brace centralizer
of a subset
S
of a skew brace
B:=(B,+,\circ)
, denoted as
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(S)
, is defined to be

\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(S):=\cap_{x\in S}\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x).

Note that if
x\in\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, then
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)=B
. Also note that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(B)=\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. It follows from
[
32
, Proposition 2.19]
that if
x\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
for all
x\in B
, then
B
is a two-sided skew brace. The converse need not be true in general (see Example
3.6
below).

Following
[
11
]
, for a skew brace
B:=(B,+,\circ)
and an element
x\in B
, we define

\operatorname{Fix}^{l}_{B}(x)=\{b\in B\mid\lambda_{b}(x)=x\}\mbox{ and }\operatorname{Fix}^{r}_{B}(x)=\{b\in B\mid\lambda_{x}(b)=b\}.

We now define the left and the right centralizers, respectively, of an element
x\in B
as follows:

\operatorname{Cb}^{l}_{B}(x):=\operatorname{Fix}^{l}_{B}(x)\cap\operatorname{C}_{(B,\circ)}(x)\mbox{ and }\operatorname{Cb}^{r}_{B}(x):=\operatorname{Fix}^{r}_{B}(x)\cap\operatorname{C}_{(B,+)}(x).

It is easy to see that

\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)=\operatorname{Cb}^{l}_{B}(x)\cap\operatorname{Cb}^{r}_{B}(x).

A straightforward computation shows that
\operatorname{Fix}^{r}_{B}(x)\cap\operatorname{C}_{(B,\circ)}(x)
is a sub-skew brace of
B
. Note that both
\operatorname{C}_{(B,\circ)}(x)
and
\operatorname{Fix}^{l}_{B}(x)
are subgroups of
(B,\circ)
. Thus we get the following key result
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace. If
x\notin\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, then
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is a proper subgroup of
(B,\circ)
.

As remarked in
[
11
, page 175]
,
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is a sub-skew brace of a two sided skew brace
B
. But in general
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
may not be a subgroup
(B,+)
for each
x\in B
. A GAP search tells that skew braces [8, 18], [12, 15] and several for order 16, including [16, 62-63], [16, 73-76] admit elements
x
such that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is not a subgroup
(B,+)
. The case [8,18] is worked out in
[
11
, Example 3.6]
. This skew brace
B
is such that its additive group is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
and its multiplicative group is
D_{8}
, the dihedral group of order 8. It follows that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(0,3)=\{(0,0),(1,1)\}
, which is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
, but not of
(B,+)
as
(1,1)+(1,1)=(0,2)\not\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(0,3)
. This example also shows that the element
(0,3)
does not lie in its brace centralizer. This too reveals that
-(1,1)=(-1,-1)=(1,3)\not\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(0,3)
. At this point skew brace theory differs from group theory.

A skew left brace
(B,+,\circ)
is said to be
symmetric
if
(B,\circ,+)
is also a skew left brace. This notion was introduced in
[
10
]
, and was further studied in
[
5
,
8
]
. Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace. Note that the map
(B,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)
,
b\mapsto\lambda_{b}
is also a map from
(B,+)
to
\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)
. We say that the skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
is
\lambda
-homomorphic
if this map is also a group homomorphism from
(B,+)
to
\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)
. This concept was introduced in
[
7
]
, and was further explored in
[
4
]
. We now observe

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be either a symmetric skew brace or a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace. Then
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is a sub-skew brace of
B
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
be two arbitrary elements. Since
\operatorname{Fix}^{r}_{B}(x)
and
\operatorname{C}_{(B,+)}(x)
are subgroups of
(B,+)
for all
x\in B
, it suffices to prove that
\operatorname{Fix}^{l}_{B}(x)
is a subgroup of
(B,+)
, that is,
\lambda_{a+b}(x)=x
and
\lambda_{(-a)}(x)=x
. For a symmetric skew brace, by the given hypothesis and
[
5
, Proposition 3.6.]
, we get

\lambda_{a+b}(x)=\lambda_{b\circ a}(x)=\lambda_{b}(\lambda_{a}(x))=x

and

x=\lambda_{b-b}(x)=\lambda_{(-b)\circ b}(x)=\lambda_{(-b)}(\lambda_{b}(x))=\lambda_{(-b)}(x).

Also, for a
\lambda
-homomorphic skew brace, by its definition, we get

\lambda_{a+b}(x)=\lambda_{a\circ b}(x)=\lambda_{a}(\lambda_{b}(x))=x

and

x=\lambda_{-b+b}(x)=\lambda_{(-b)\circ b}(x)=\lambda_{-b}(\lambda_{b}(x))=\lambda_{-b}(x).

The proof is complete.
∎

We say that
x
has
finite centraliser index in
B
if
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)]
, the index of
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
in
(B,\circ)
is finite. Let
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
denote the set of all elements of
B
having finite centraliser index in
B
. The skew brace
B
is said to be
FCI
if
B=\operatorname{FCI}(B)
. Note that if
[B:\operatorname{Ann}(B)]
is finite, then
B
is FCI. So if
B
is not FCI, then
[B:\operatorname{Ann}(B)]
is not finite. The skew braces with property (S) were introduced in
[
11
, Definition 3.7]
. An element
x\in B
is said to be an
s
-element
if both the indices
[(B,+):\operatorname{Cb}^{r}_{B}(x)]
and
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}^{l}_{B}(x)]
are finite.
A skew brace
B
is said to have
property (S)
if each
x\in B
is an
s
-element. We remark that each
s
-element of
B
lies in
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
. However, the two concepts, namely skew brace with property (S) and FCI brace, may not be the same in general. We further remark that, if either
\operatorname{Cb}^{r}_{B}(x)
or
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is a left ideal of
B
for all
x\in B
, then both theses concepts coincide.

We now prove

Proposition 2.3
.

For a skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
,
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
. Moreover, if
B
is two sided, symmetric or
\lambda
-homomorphic, then
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
is a sub-skew brace of
B
.

Proof.

For
a,x_{1},x_{2}\in B
such that
a\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{1})\cap\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{2})
, notice that
x_{1},x_{2}\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(a)
. Thus, by Proposition
2.1
,
x_{1}\circ x_{2}\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(a)
, which gives
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{1})\cap\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{2})\subset\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{1}\circ x_{2})
. Now, assuming
x_{1},x_{2}\in\operatorname{FCI}(B)
, we see that
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{1}\circ x_{2})]\leqslant[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{1})][(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{2})]<\infty
, and therefore
x_{1}\circ x_{2}\in\operatorname{FCI}(B)
. Similarly, it is easy to see that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)=\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x^{-1})
for all
x\in\operatorname{FCI}(B)
. Other assertions can be proved by a routine calculation. The proof is complete.
∎

For a two-sided brace we can prove the following result, which is analogous to
[
11
, Proposition 3.10]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a two-sided brace. Then
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
is an ideal of
B
.

Proof.

Set
F:=\operatorname{FCI}(B)
and
c\in F
. Since
B
is a two-sided brace, each inner automorphism of
(B,\circ)
is a brace automorphism, and therefore,
x\circ c\circ x^{-1}\in F
for all
x\in B
. Thus
F
is normal in both
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
. As
\lambda_{b}(c)=b\ast c+c
for all
b\in B
and
F
is a subgroup of
(B,+)
, it suffices to prove
b\ast c\in F
. As
B
is a two sided brace, ‘
\ast
’ is associative on
B
. Thus it follows that the sets
\{x\ast(b\ast c)\mid x\in B\}
and
\{(b\ast c)\ast x\mid x\in B\}
are finite. Note that
x\circ(b\ast c)\circ x^{-1}=(x\circ b\circ x^{-1})\ast(x\circ c\circ x^{-1})
for all
x\in B
. Since
x\circ c\circ x^{-1}\in F
,
\{x\circ(b\ast c)\circ x^{-1}\mid x\in B\}
is finite. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
commuting probability of finite skew braces

We start this section by defining commuting probability of a finite skew brace. For a finite skew brace
B:=(B,+,\circ)
, we define the
commuting probability
of
B
, denoted by
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
, to be

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\big(|\{(a,b)\mid a*b=b*a=\gamma^{+}(a,b)=1\}|\big).

It is clear that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
for all trivial braces
B
. For any other brace
B
,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
lies in the open interval
(0,1)
.
Note that, analogous to group theory, we can derive
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
in terms of brace centralizers as follows:

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{\sum_{x\in B}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|}{|B|^{2}},

which can be further expanded to

(3.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\Big(\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Ann}(B)}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|+\sum_{x\in B-Ann(B)}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|}{|B|}+\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\sum_{x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|.

One baby observation is that if
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\neq 1
, then
B
must admit a non-trivial proper sub-skew brace, which follows from the fact that a skew brace having no non-trivial proper sub-skew brace is always a trivial brace of prime order (
[
3
, Theorem A]
). The converse, of course, is not true as
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
for all trivial braces. As a direct consequence of the definition, one can easily see that

\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\min\{\operatorname{Pr}(B,+),\operatorname{Pr}(B,\circ)\},

where
\operatorname{Pr}(G)
denotes the commuting probability of a given group
G
.

We remark that several proofs in this section can also be obtained as special cases of proofs from Section 5. We have chosen to present these here for completeness. As we observed above,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
lies in the interval
(0,1]
. But we can say better on these bounds.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any finite skew brace
B
with
|B|>1
, and proper sub-skew brace
H
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

\frac{2}{|B|}\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(B)
.

(2)

\operatorname{Pb}(B)<2\frac{|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|}{|B|}
for some
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
.

(
3)
\frac{\operatorname{Pb}(H)}{{[B:H]}^{2}}<\operatorname{Pb}(B)
.

Moreover, if
H
is a trivial brace, then

\frac{1}{{[B:H]}^{2}}<\operatorname{Pb}(B)
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
, we see that the pairs
(b,1)
and
(1,b)
,
b\in B
, contribute to the numerator of
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
. Since these count
2|B|
, assertion (1) holds.

For assertion (2), we choose an element
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
such that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is of maximal order. Then by (
3.1
) we directly get

|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant|B||Ann(B)|+|B||\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|-|Ann(B)||\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|,

which further implies

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|}{|B|}+\frac{|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|}{|B|}-\frac{|\operatorname{Ann}(B)||\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|}{|B|^{2}}

\displaystyle<\frac{2|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|}{|B|}.

Assertion (3) follows from

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=~\frac{\sum_{x\in B}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|}{|B|^{2}}>~\frac{\sum_{x\in H}|\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)|}{|B|^{2}}=~\frac{|H|^{2}\sum_{x\in H}|\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)|}{|B|^{2}|H|^{2}}=~\frac{\operatorname{Pb}(H)}{{[B:H]}^{2}}.

If
H
is a trivial brace, then
\operatorname{Pb}(H)=1
. Final assertion now follows from the preceding bound, and the proof is complete.
∎

For the rest of this article, for a given finite skew brace
B
, let
d
denote the integer
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|
, the index of
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
in
B
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
B
be a finite skew brace. Then
\frac{2d-1}{d^{2}}\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{d+1}{2d}
.

Proof.

We begin by noting that for all
x\in B
,
\operatorname{Ann}(B)\subseteq\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
. Now using (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=|\operatorname{Ann}(B)||B|+\sum_{x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|

\displaystyle\geqslant|\operatorname{Ann}(B)||B|+|B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)||\operatorname{Ann}(B)|

\displaystyle=2|\operatorname{Ann}(B)||B|-|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|^{2}.

\displaystyle=2|B|\frac{|B|}{d}-\frac{|B|^{2}}{d^{2}}.

\displaystyle=|B|^{2}\frac{2d-1}{d^{2}}.

Hence
\frac{2d-1}{d^{2}}\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(B)
.

Now we prove the upper bound. By Proposition
2.1
we have
|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|\leqslant\frac{|B|}{2}
for all
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. Thus, from the definition of the commuting probability, we get

\displaystyle|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant|\operatorname{Ann}(B)||B|+|(B-\operatorname{Ann}(B))|\frac{|B|}{2}.

\displaystyle=|B|\frac{|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|}{2}+\frac{|B|^{2}}{2}.

\displaystyle=|B|\frac{|B|}{2d}+\frac{|B|^{2}}{2}.

\displaystyle=|B|^{2}\frac{d+1}{2d},

which implies that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{d+1}{2d}
. The proof is complete.
∎

A natural question which arises here is that whether these bounds are really attained for some skew braces. We now show that indeed these are attained. The lower bound is attained when
d=p
, a prime integer.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
B
be a finite skew brace and
p
be the smallest prime which divides
|B|
. Then the following holds true:

(1)

If
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=p
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
.

(2)

\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{p+d-1}{pd}\leqslant\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
. Furthermore, if
\operatorname{Ann}(B)\subsetneq\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(x)
for all
x\in B
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\geqslant\frac{pd+d-p}{d^{2}}
.

(3)

If
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is of non-prime power order and
q
is the second smallest prime divisor of
|B|
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{1}{p}+\frac{|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|(p-1)-1}{p|B|}+\frac{1}{s|B|}
, where
s
either
q
or
p^{2}
.

(4)

In particular, if
|B|=p^{n}
, then either
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
or
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{p^{2}+p-1}{p^{3}}
.

(5)

If
|B|=p^{n}
,
|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=1
and
(B,\circ)
is not an elementary abelian group, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{1}{p}+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{p^{n+2}}
.

Proof.

Let
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=p
. Note that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)=\operatorname{Ann}(B)
for all
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. Then a straightforward computation, using definition (
3.1
), gives

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
, which proves (1). For (2), let
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=d
. Then, by (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant|\operatorname{Ann}(B)||B|+|(B-\operatorname{Ann}(B))|\frac{|B|}{p}

\displaystyle=|\operatorname{Ann}(B)||B|(1-\frac{1}{p})+\frac{|B|^{2}}{p}

\displaystyle=|B|\frac{|B|}{d}\frac{(p-1)}{p}+\frac{|B|^{2}}{p},

which implies that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{p+d-1}{pd}
. Since
p\leqslant d
, we get
\frac{p+d-1}{pd}\leqslant\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
.

Now assume that
\operatorname{Ann}(B)\subsetneq\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(x)
for all
x\in B
.
Since
\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(x)
is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
(by Proposition
2.1
), it follows that
|\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(x)/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|\geqslant p
. So for any
x\in B
, we get

|\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(x)|\geqslant\frac{p|B|}{d}.

A straightforward computation, using the preceding inequality in (
3.1
) gives

\operatorname{Pb}(B)\geqslant\frac{pd+d-p}{d^{2}}
. This completes the proof of (2).

Note that any subgroup
H
of
(B,\circ)
of index
p
is normal in
(B,\circ)
, and therefore,
H
contains all Sylow
q^{\prime}
-subgroups of
(B,\circ)
, where
q^{\prime}
is a prime divisor of
|B|
different from
p
. For (3), if
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)]=p
for all
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, then Sylow
q^{\prime}
-subgroups of
(B,\circ)
are contained in
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, and therefore
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is of
p
power order, which contradicts the given hypothesis. Hence, there exists at least one element
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
such that
|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|\leq\frac{|B|}{s}
, where
s=q
if
p^{2}>q
, otherwise
s=p^{2}
. Now the assertion (3) directly follows from the definition of the commuting probability of a skew brace.

Next assume that
|B|=p^{n}
. If
B=\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, the obviously
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
, and if
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=p
, then by (1) we get
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
. So assume that
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=p^{r}\geqslant p^{2}
. Then by the first assertion of (2) we get

\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{p+d-1}{pd}=\frac{p+p^{r}-1}{p^{r+1}}\leqslant\frac{p^{2}+p-1}{p^{3}},

which proves (4).

We now prove (5). Note that if
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is a maximal subgroup of
(B,\circ)
for all
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, then the Frattini subgroup of
(B,\circ)
is contained in
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. Since
(B,\circ)
is not elementary abelian, its Frattini subgroup is non-trivial, which contradicts the hypothesis that
|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=1
. Hence, there exists an element
y\in B
such that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)
is not a maximal subgroup of
(B,\circ)
. We now get

|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant|B|+(|B|-2)\frac{|B|}{p}+\frac{|B|}{p^{2}}.

The proof is now complete by taking
|B|=p^{n}
.
∎

We remarks that assertion (1) of the preceding result holds true for any prime divisor of
|B|
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a finite skew brace such that
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are nilpotent groups.
As we know that a finite nilpotent group can be written as a direct product of its sylow
p
-subgroups,
(B,+)
can be written as

(B,+)\cong\Pi_{p_{i}\in\pi(B)}P_{i},

where
P_{i}
is a Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroup of
(B,+)
.
It follows from
[
9
, Corollary 4.3]
that each
P_{i}
is an ideal of
B
, and the above isomorphism is indeed a brace isomorphism. Since
\operatorname{Pb}(H\times K)=\operatorname{Pb}(H)\operatorname{Pb}(K)
for any two skew braces
H
and
K
,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{Pb}(P_{i})
, where each Sylow
p_{i}
-subgroup
P_{i}
is such that
\operatorname{Ann}(P_{i})\subsetneq P_{i}
. Now by the preceding result we can compute an upper bound for
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
for a skew brace with nilpotent
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
(as groups). In particular, this construction can be applied to nilpotent skew braces.

We now explore a sort of converse of one of the last results.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
B
be a skew brace such that
B\neq\operatorname{Ann}(B)
and
p
be a prime with
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
. Then
p||B|
. Moreover, if
p
is the smallest prime dividing
|B|
, then
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
, the given hypothesis gives the equation

|B|^{2}\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}=\sum_{x\in B}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|.

This is possible only when
p
divides
|B|
.

Now assume that
p
is the smallest prime dividing
|B|
. By (
3.1
) we get

|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant|\operatorname{Ann}(B)||B|+|(B-\operatorname{Ann}(B))|\frac{|B|}{p}.

This gives

|B|(2p-1)\leqslant|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|p(p-1)+|B|p,

which implies that

|B|(p-1)\leqslant|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|p(p-1).

Hence
|B|/|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=p
, as
B\neq\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. Since there exists only one skew brace of order
p
, we must have
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Next we give an example of a skew brace where the upper bound of Proposition
3.3
is attained.

Example 3.6
.

Let
B=\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, consider ‘
+
’ as natural ‘
+
’ and ‘
\circ
’ is defined by

x\circ y=x+y+2xy~\mbox{ for all }x,y\in B,

where
xy
is the natural multiplication.
It is easy to see that
(B,+,\circ)
is a skew brace. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{4}=\{0,1,2,3\}
. Now, notice that

\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(0)=\mathbb{Z}_{4}
,
\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(1)=\{0,2\}
,
\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(2)=\mathbb{Z}_{4}
,
\operatorname{Cb}_{(B)}(3)=\{0,2\}
.
So
d=2
, and therefore

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{4+2+4+2}{4^{2}}=\frac{3}{4}=\frac{d+1}{2d}

as
|B|=4

We now compute the commuting probability of a cyclic brace whose multiplicative group is commutative. Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a finite cyclic brace whose multiplicative group is commutative. Then, by
[
31
, Theorem 1]
,
B=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
with ‘
+
’ as natural ‘
+
’ and ‘
\circ
’ is defined by

x\circ y=x+y+dxy~\mbox{ for all }x,y\in B,

where

p|d|n

.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
B=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, concider ‘
+
’ as natural ‘
+
’ and ‘
\circ
’ is defined by

x\circ y=x+y+dxy~\mbox{ for all }x,y\in B,

where
xy
is the natural multiplication such that
p|d|n
for each prime divisor
p
of
n
. Then

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{{x\in(B,+)}\atop{\overline{dx}=\bar{t}}}gcd(t,n).

Proof.

Let
\bar{x}\in B
, then
\gamma^{+}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=1
and
\gamma^{\circ}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=1
for all
\bar{y}\in B
. Now
\bar{y}\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
if and only if
\bar{x}\ast\bar{y}=1
if and only if
d\bar{x}\bar{y}=1
. Set
\bar{t}=\overline{dx}
. Then
\bar{y}\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
if and only if the order of
\bar{t}
in
(\mathbb{Z}_{n},+)
divides
y
. Now

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}{(x)}|

\displaystyle=|\{\bar{y}\in B\mid y=m|\bar{t}|\text{ for some }m\in\mathbb{Z}\}|

\displaystyle=|\{0\leqslant m|\bar{t}|\leqslant n-1\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\displaystyle=|\{0\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n-1}{|\bar{t}|}\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}\}|

\displaystyle=|\{0\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n}{|\bar{t}|}-1\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}\}|

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{|\bar{t}|}=gcd(t,n).

Hence, by the definition of the commuting probability, we get

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{{x\in(B,+)}\atop{\overline{dx}=\bar{t}}}gcd(t,n),

which completes the proof.
∎

Now we obtain exact upper bounds for
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
for all finite skew braces
B
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a finite skew brace. Then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{3}{4}
or
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{5}{8}
.

Proof.

Let
B
be any finite skew brace. Let
f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a function defined by
f(x)=\frac{x+1}{2x}
. Then the derivative of
f
is

f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{-1}{x^{2}}~(\leqslant 0).

Thus
f
is a monotone decreasing function. If
d=1
, that is,
B
is a trivial skew brace of abelian-type, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
. If
d=2,3
, then, by Proposition
3.4
, we get
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{3}{4}
and
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{5}{9}
, respectively. For all
d\geqslant 4
, we apply Proposition
3.3
. Note that for
d=4
, we obtain
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{5}{8}
. Since
f
is a monotone decreasing function and
\frac{5}{9}\leqslant\frac{5}{8}
, it follows that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{5}{8}
for all

, which completes the proof.
∎

We now characterise finite skew braces
B
such that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{5}{8}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For a finite skew brace
B
,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{5}{8}
if and only if
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=4
and
|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|=\frac{|B|}{2}
for all
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{5}{8}
, then, using Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
(1), we get
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=4
, which is possible only if
|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|=\frac{|B|}{2}
for all

. On the other hand if
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=4
and
|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|=\frac{|B|}{2}
for all

, then it follows, by Proposition

.
∎

A host of examples satisfying the hypothesis of the preceding result can be obtained using GAP. One such skew brace is

is the quaternion group of order 8. We can say more in the following result.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
B
be a finite skew brace with
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=5/8
. Then both the additive and the multiplicative groups of quotient skew brace
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By the preceding lemma, we know that
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=4
and
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)]=2
for all
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. If possible, suppose that the additive group
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is cyclic, say, generated by
y+\operatorname{Ann}(B)
for some

. Then
(B,+)
is an abelian group. If
y\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)
, then it turns out that
B=\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)
, which is not possible. Note that
3y\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)
if and only if

, as

. Thus,

, not possible again. Hence,
(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B),+)

∎

We now prove the following interesting results, which we’ll use later and whose group theory analogues follow from
[
15
]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace of finite order and
N
be an ideal of
B
. Then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(N)\operatorname{Pb}(B/N)
. In particular,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(B/N)
and
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(N)
.

Proof.

We work with group
(B,\circ)
and the quotient group
(B,\circ)/(N,\circ)
. For the simplicity of notation we suppress the use of ‘
\circ
’ and write
x\circ N=xN
for a left coset of
N
in
(B,\circ)
. For the product of two subgroups
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
of
(B,\circ)
we simply write
N_{1}N_{2}
.

We first prove that
({\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)N)/N}\subset\operatorname{Cb}_{B/N}(xN)
for all
x\in B
. Let
y\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
. Since
N
is an ideal of
B
, we know that

x+N=x\circ N
for all
x\in B
. Thus by an easy computation we get

\displaystyle(yN)*(xN)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(y*x)N=N,

\displaystyle(xN)*(yN)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x*y)N=N

and

[xN,yN]^{\circ}=[x,y]^{\circ}N=N.

Hence
({\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)N)/N}\subset\operatorname{Cb}_{B/N}(xN)
.

Note that
N\cap\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)=\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(x)
for all
x\in B
. Thus, as
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
and
\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(x)
are subgroups of
(B,\circ)
, we get

(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)N)/N\cong\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)/(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)\cap N)=\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)/\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(x).

Now, using the definition of
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
, we have

\displaystyle|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in B}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|=\sum_{bN\in{B/N}}~\sum_{x\in bN}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{bN\in{B/N}}~\sum_{x\in bN}|{(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)N})/N||\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{bN\in{B/N}}~\sum_{x\in bN}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B/N}(xN)||\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(x)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{bN\in{B/N}}\Big(|\operatorname{Cb}_{B/N}(bN)|\sum_{x\in bN}|\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(x)|\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{bN\in{B/N}}\Big(|\operatorname{Cb}_{B/N}(bN)|\sum_{y\in N}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)\cap(bN)|\Big).

Let
z\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)\cap(bN)
. Then
zN=bN
as
z\in bN
. Now, since
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)
is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
and
z\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)
, we get

\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)\cap(zN)=(z\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y))\cap(zN)=z(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)\cap N)=z\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(y),

which gives
|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)\cap(bN)|=|\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(y)|
. By clubbing in these values in the preceding inequality, we get

\displaystyle|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{bN\in B/N}\Big(|\operatorname{Cb}_{B/N}(bN)|\sum_{y\in N}|\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(y)|\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{bN\in B/N}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B/N}(bN)|\Big)\Big(\sum_{y\in N}|\operatorname{Cb}_{N}(y)|\Big),

which, by the definition of the commuting probability, can be written as

|B|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\big(|B/N|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B/N)\big)\big(|N|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(N)\big).

Hence
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(N)\operatorname{Pb}(B/N)
and the proof is complete.
∎

We conclude this section by generalising one of the assertions of the preceding result to sub-skew braces.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
H
be a sub-skew brace of a finite skew brace
B
. Then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(H)
.

Proof.

For any
x\in B
, note that
\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
are subgroups of
(B,\circ)
. Let
x\in B
such
[\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x):\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)]=k
for some integer
k
, and
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be a set of left coset representatives of
\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)
in
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
as subgroup in
(B,\circ)
. It follows that
b_{l}\circ H\neq b_{m}\circ H
for
l,m\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
with
l\neq m
. For, if
b_{l}\circ H=b_{m}\circ H
, then
{b_{l}}^{-1}\circ b_{m}\in H\cap\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)=\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)
, a contradiction. Hence

[(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x),\circ),(\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x):\circ)]\leqslant[(B,\circ):(H,\circ)]
, which gives

|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|=[\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x):\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)]|\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)|\leqslant[(B,\circ):(H,\circ)]|\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)|.

Now

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in B}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant[(B,\circ):(H,\circ)]\sum_{x\in B}|\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(x)|

\displaystyle=[(B,\circ):(H,\circ)]\sum_{y\in H}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant{[(B,\circ):(H,\circ)]}^{2}\sum_{y\in H}|\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(y)|.

Using this, we finally get

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{\sum_{x\in B}|\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)|}{|B|^{2}}\leqslant\frac{{[(B,\circ):(H,\circ)]}^{2}\sum_{y\in H}|\operatorname{Cb}_{H}(y)|}{|B|^{2}}=\operatorname{Pb}(H),

which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Nilpotency, Isoclinism and Commuting Probability

We first study the impact of the commuting probability on the nilpotency of skew braces. As we have seen above, if
\operatorname{Pb}(B)>\frac{3}{4}
, then
B
is a trivial brace. We here show that a skew brace
B
is nilpotent if
\operatorname{Pb}(B)>\frac{65}{128}
. We start with a very basic observation on a skew brace
B
with
|\Gamma_{2}(B)|=2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace such that
|\Gamma_{2}(B)|=2
. Then
B
is a nilpotent skew brace of nilpotency class
2
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{2}(B)=\{1,b\}
. Then the set
\{a*b,b*a,a+b-a-b\mid a,\in B\}
generates
\Gamma_{3}(B)
. An easy computation shows that all elements of the generating set of
\Gamma_{3}(B)
must be the identity element, proving that
\Gamma_{3}(B)=\{1\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a two-sided skew brace of order
p^{n}
. Then
B
is a nilpotent skew brace.

Proof.

It follows from
[
26
, Proposition 3.22]
that
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is a non-trivial ideal of
B
. Hence, it easily follows by an inductive argument that
B
is a nilpotent skew brace.
∎

As we observed above, every skew brace whose multiplicative group is abelian is two-sided. Thus as an immediate consequence of the preceding result we get

Corollary 4.3
.

Any skew brace of order
p^{2}
is nilpotent.

There do exist skew braces of prime power order which are not nilpotent. One such example of order
p^{3}
for
p=2
is
(8,18)
in GAP library (see
[
16
,
34
]
). There are total 5 skew braces of order 8, namely (8, 2), (8, 3), (8, 18), (8, 19), (8, 47), which are not two-sided. It turns out that
Pb(B)\leq\frac{1}{2}
for all skew braces
B
mentioned in the preceding sentence. For more knowledge on non-nilpotent braces of prime power order see
[
27
,
28
]
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace of order
p^{2}
. Then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
or
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.3
,
B
is a nilpotent skew brace. So
\operatorname{Ann}(B)\neq\{1\}
, which implies that
|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|
is
p
or
p^{2}
. If
|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=p^{2}
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
. If
|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=p
, then by Proposition
3.4
,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
.
∎

We remark that a finite skew brace
B
is nilpotent if and only of
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is nilpotent.
If
B
is a finite skew brace which is not nilpotent, then there exists a positive integer
m
such that
\operatorname{Ann}(B/\operatorname{Ann}_{m}(B))=1
. By Theorem
3.11
we know that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\operatorname{Pb}(B/\operatorname{Ann}_{m}(B))
.

We are now ready to prove

Theorem 4.5
.

If B is a finite skew brace such that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)>\frac{65}{128}
, then
B
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Pb}(B)>\frac{65}{128}
, it follows from Proposition
3.3
that
d\in\{1,2,\ldots,63\}
. A GAP search for non-nilpotent skew braces of orders lying in the set
\{1,\ldots,63\}-\{32,48\}
shows that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leq\frac{1}{2}
. As remarked above, a finite skew brace
B
is nilpotent if and only of
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is nilpotent. So assume that
B
is a non-nilpotent skew brace such that
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is of order 32 or 48. Let
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=32
. Then
(B,\circ)
can not be abelian as, otherwise,
B
will be a two sided skew brace, and therefore nilpotent. If
|\operatorname{Ann}(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B))|=1
, then, by Proposition
3.4
(5),
\operatorname{Pb}(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B))\leqslant\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{128}=\frac{65}{128}
. If
|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|>1
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leq\operatorname{Pb}(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B))\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
. Now take
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=48
. If
|\operatorname{Ann}(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B))|=1
, then, by Proposition
3.4
(3),
\operatorname{Pb}(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B))\leqslant\frac{73}{144}<\frac{65}{128}
. If
|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|>1
, then, again,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leq\operatorname{Pb}(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B))\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
. The proof is complete.
∎

We now turn attention to isoclinism of skew braces and prove that the commuting probability of skew braces is invariant under isoclinism.
We begin by defining the concept of isoclinism of skew braces introduced in
[
26
]
(The concept of isoclinism of groups was introduced by P. Hall
[
19
]
). For a skew brace
B
, it is easy to check that the following maps are well defined:

\displaystyle\phi^{B}_{+}

\displaystyle:(B/{\operatorname{Ann}B})^{2}\to\Gamma_{2}(B),\hskip 42.67912pt(\bar{a},\bar{b})\mapsto[a,b]^{+},

\displaystyle\phi^{B}_{\ast}

\displaystyle:(B/{\operatorname{Ann}B})^{2}\to\Gamma_{2}(B),\hskip 42.67912pt(\bar{a},\bar{b})\mapsto a\ast b,

where
\bar{a},\bar{b}\in B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
.
We say that two skew braces
A
and
B
are
isoclinic
if there exist brace isomorphisms
\xi:A/{\operatorname{Ann}A}\to B/{\operatorname{Ann}B}
and
\theta:\Gamma_{2}(A)\to\Gamma_{2}(B)
such that the diagram

(4.6)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{2}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A/{\operatorname{Ann}A})^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{2}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{2}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B/{\operatorname{Ann}B})^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{2}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{+}^{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{\ast}^{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi\times\xi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{+}^{B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{\ast}^{B}}

commutes. The pair
(\xi,\theta)
is called a
skew brace isoclinism
. It is not difficult to see that isoclinism is an equivalence relation in the category of all skew left braces. A skew brace
B
is said to be a
stem skew brace
is
\operatorname{Ann}(B)\subseteq\Gamma_{2}(B)
. That each isoclinism class (or family) admits a stem skew brace is proved in
[
26
, Theorem 2.18]
.

We now prove that commuting probability is invariant under isoclinism of finite skew braces. An analogous result for finite groups was proved in
[
25
]
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
A
and
B
be two isoclinic skew braces of finite order. Then

\operatorname{Pb}(A)=\operatorname{Pb}(B)

and

|A^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{Ann}(A)|=|B^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{Ann}(B)|
.

Proof.

Let
(\xi,\theta)
be an isoclinism between
A
and
B
. Since
A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)\cong B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)
, for the first assertion we only need to prove that
|A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(A)=|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B).

First we expand the right hand side.

\displaystyle|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|^{2}}|\{(x,y)\in B\mid[x,y]^{+}=x\ast y=y\ast x=1\}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Ann}(B)|^{2}}|\{(x,y)\in B\mid\phi_{+}^{B}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\phi_{\ast}^{B}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\phi_{\ast}^{B}(\bar{y},\bar{x})=1\}|

\displaystyle=|\{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)\mid\phi_{+}^{B}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\phi_{\ast}^{B}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\phi_{\ast}^{B}(\bar{y},\bar{x})=1\}|.

Similarly,

|A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(A)=|\{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)\mid\phi_{+}^{A}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\phi_{\ast}^{A}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\phi_{\ast}^{A}(\bar{y},\bar{x})=1\}|.

Now using the fact that
\theta
is an isomorphism and the diagram (
4.6
) commutes, we get

\displaystyle|A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(A)

\displaystyle=|\{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in A/\operatorname{Ann}(A):\theta\phi_{+}^{A}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\theta\phi_{\ast}^{A}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\theta\phi_{\ast}^{A}(\bar{y},\bar{x})=1\}|,

\displaystyle=|\{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in A/\operatorname{Ann}(A):\phi_{+}^{B}(\xi,\xi)(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\phi_{\ast}^{B}(\xi,\xi)(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\phi_{\ast}^{B}(\xi,\xi)(\bar{y},\bar{x})=1\}|,

\displaystyle=|\{(\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{y}^{\prime})\in B/\operatorname{Ann}(B):\phi_{+}^{B}(\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{y}^{\prime})=\phi_{\ast}^{B}(\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{y}^{\prime})=\phi_{\ast}^{B}(\bar{y}^{\prime},\bar{x}^{\prime})=1\}|,

where
\xi(\bar{y})=\bar{y}^{\prime},\xi(\bar{x})=\bar{x}^{\prime}
.
Since
\xi
is an isomorphism, from the preceding identities, we get

|A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(A)=|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|^{2}\operatorname{Pb}(B),

which, using the fact that
|A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)|=|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|
, proves
\operatorname{Pb}(A)=\operatorname{Pb}(B)
.

Note that
\Gamma_{2}(A/\operatorname{Ann}(A))=\Gamma_{2}(A)\operatorname{Ann}(A)/\operatorname{Ann}(A)
. Now by the third isomorphism theorem of skew braces
[
2
]
, we get

\Gamma_{2}(A/\operatorname{Ann}(A))=\Gamma_{2}(A)/\big(\Gamma_{2}(A)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(A)\big).

Similarly
\Gamma_{2}(B/\operatorname{Ann}(B))\cong\Gamma_{2}(B)/\big(\Gamma_{2}(B)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(B)\big)
.
The second assertion now follows using the given hypotheses.
∎

A stem skew brace in a given isoclinism family of skew braces is a skew brace
B
such that
\operatorname{Ann}(B)\subseteq\Gamma_{2}(B)
. We now derive some easy consequences of the preceding theorem.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace of finite order such that
\Gamma_{2}(B)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(B)=1
. Then there exist a finite skew brace
A
such that
\operatorname{Pb}(A)=\operatorname{Pb}(B)
and
\operatorname{Ann}(A)=1
and
\Gamma_{2}(A)
isomorphic to
\Gamma_{2}(B)
.

Proof.

By
[
26
, Theorem 2.18]
, there exists a stem skew brace
A
such that
A
is isoclinic to
B
. This
A
is the required skew brace.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
A
and
B
be two isoclinic skew braces of finite order such that B is non-trivial and simple. Then
A
is isomorphic to
B\times C
, where
C
is a skew brace with
\operatorname{Pb}(C)=1
.

Proof.

Since
B
is non-trivial and simple, we have
B=\Gamma_{2}(B)
and
\operatorname{Ann}(B)=1
. Thus
\Gamma_{2}(A)\cong B
and

\operatorname{Ann}(\Gamma_{2}(A))\cong\operatorname{Ann}(\Gamma_{2}(B))=\operatorname{Ann}(B)=1.

Note that
\Gamma_{2}(A)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(A)\subset\operatorname{Ann}(\Gamma_{2}(A))=1
and
A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)\cong B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)\cong B=\Gamma_{2}(A)
. Thus, we get an exact sequence

1\to\operatorname{Ann}(A)\to A\to\Gamma_{2}(A)\to 1,

which, comparing the orders, gives
A\cong\Gamma_{2}(A)\times\operatorname{Ann}(A)\cong B\times\operatorname{Ann}(A)
. Thus,
\operatorname{Ann}(A)
is the required skew brace
C
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

We conclude this section with two easy facts whose proofs follow directly from the definition. By
[
26
, Theorem 2.18]
we know that each isoclinism class of skew braces admits a stem skew brace. Let a given isoclinism class of skew braces contain a finite skew brace. Then every stem skew brace in that class is finite. It is proved in
[
26
, Proposition 2.17]
that any two stem skew braces in an isoclinism class have the same order. We remark that any two skew braces having equal finite orders in an isoclinism class of skew braces are simultaneously stem skew braces.

5.
COMMUTING PROBABILITY OF INFINITE SKEW BRACES

The definition of the commuting probability defined for finite skew braces above does not bear any meaning for infinite skew braces. The concept of the commuting probability of an infinite group was also introduced by Gustafson
[
18
]
in
1973
, where he proved that the commuting probability of a non-abelian compact Hausdorff topological group is always bounded above by
\frac{5}{8}
. Further, it was proved in
[
14
]
that the commuting probability of a non-abelian compact Hausdorff topological group is
\frac{5}{8}
if and only if
G/Z(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. For a similar study of the commuting probability of infinite groups and its advancements, the reader is referred to
[
1
,
33
]
. It will be interesting to obtains brace analog of this theory. In this section we define this concept for infinite skew braces and obtain a skew brace analogue of the above bound. We now prepare for the definition of the commuting probability of infinite skew braces.

We start by recalling the definition of a topological group. A group
G
is said to be a
topological group
if
G
is a topological space such that the maps
G\times G\rightarrow G
,
(x,y)\mapsto xy
and
G\rightarrow G
,
x\mapsto x^{-1}
are continuous. We now define a topological skew brace. A skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
is called a
topological skew brace
if both the groups
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are topological groups admitting a common topology. Thus, all four maps
\tau^{+}_{1}:(B,+)\times(B,+)\to(B,+)
,
\tau^{\circ}_{1}:(B,\circ)\times(B,\circ)\to(B,\circ)
,
\tau_{2}^{+}:(B,+)\to(B,+)
and
\tau_{2}^{\circ}:(B,\circ)\to(B,\circ)
, respectively, given by
\tau^{+}_{1}(a,b)=a+b
,
\tau^{\circ}_{1}(a,b)=a\circ b
,
\tau^{+}_{2}(a)=-a
and
\tau^{\circ}_{2}(a)=a^{-1}
are continuous. Also note that
\operatorname{Id}_{B}:B\to B
, the identity map, is also continuous. We can construct topological spaces
B^{n}:=B\times\cdots\times B
(n copies) by considering the product topology on
B^{n}
. The concepts of compact and Hausdorff topological skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
are defined by considering these properties with respect to a given common topology for
(B,+)
and (
B,\circ)
. We now make some key observations in the form of lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a topological skew brace. Then the map
\tau:B\times B\to B
, given by
\tau(a,b)=a*b
, is continuous.

Proof.

Note that the composition of two continuous maps is again continuous. So the map
f_{1}:B\times B\to B\times B\times B
, defined by
f(a,b)=(\tau_{2}^{+}(a),\tau_{1}^{\circ}(a,b),\tau_{2}^{+}(b))
is continuous. Also the map
f_{2}:B\times B\times B\to B
, defined by
f_{2}(a,b,c)=\tau_{1}^{+}\big(\tau_{1}^{+}(a,b),c\big)
, is continuous. Now,
\tau
, which is nothing but
f_{2}
composed with
f_{1}
, is continuous.
∎

The following is well known.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a topological skew brace. Then the commutator maps
\gamma^{+}:(B,+)\times(B,+)\to(B,+)
and
\gamma^{\circ}:(B,\circ)\times(B,\circ)\to(B,\circ)
are continuous.

Our last key observation is

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a Hausdorff topological skew brace. Then
Z:=\{(a,b)\mid\tau(a,b)=\gamma^{\circ}(a,b)=\gamma^{+}(a,b)=1\}
is a closed subset of
B\times B
, where
B\times B
is a topological space with the product topology. Moreover, the sets
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
and
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
are also closed subsets of
B
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows by noting that
Z=\tau(1)\cap\gamma^{\circ}(1)\cap\gamma^{+}(1)
. The second assertion also holds by considering appropriate continuous maps.
∎

Let
B:=(B,+,\circ)
be a compact Hausdorff topological skew brace. Then
(B,+)
is a compact Hausdorff topological group, and therefore, there is a unique probability Haar measure space
((B,+),\mathcal{M},\mu)
. Consider the product measure
\mu\times\mu
on the product space
B\times B
, which turns out to be a probability measure. Since
Z
is closed in
B\times B
,
(\mu\times\mu)(Z)
is well defined. The
commuting probability
of the skew brace
B
, denoted by
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
, is defined to be

\operatorname{Pb}(B):=(\mu\times\mu)(Z).

We remark that if
B
is a finite skew brace, then
B
is a compact skew brace with discrete topology,
and so the Haar measure of
B
is the counting measure. Therefore,

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=(\mu\times\mu)(Z)=\frac{|Z|}{|B|^{2}},

which agrees with the definition of the commuting probability of a finite skew brace given in (
3.1
).

Remark 5.4
.

Let
B:=(B,+,\circ)
be a compact Hausdorff topological skew brace, and
((B,+),\mathcal{M},\mu)
be the probability Haar measure space. We claim that
((B,\circ),\mathcal{M},\mu)
is a probability Haar measure space. Since
{\lambda_{x}}
is a homeomorphism from
(B,+)
onto itself, it follows that

{\lambda_{x}}
maps Borel sets to Borel sets, that is,
\mathcal{M}
is mapped onto itself. Note that for any
D\in\mathcal{M}
and
x\in B
, we have
x\circ D=x+\lambda_{x}(D)
. So
x\circ D\in\mathcal{M}
. Define a function
\mu_{\lambda_{x}}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
by
\mu_{\lambda_{x}}(D)=\mu({\lambda_{x}}(D))
. Since
\lambda_{x}
is a group homomorphism on
(B,+)
, it turns out that
\mu_{\lambda_{x}}
is also a probability Haar measure on
(B,+)
. Hence, by the uniqueness of the probability Haar measure,
\mu_{\lambda_{x}}=\mu
. Now
\mu(x\circ D)=\mu(x+\lambda_{x}(D))=\mu(\lambda_{x}(D))=\mu(D)
. So
\mu
is also a probability Haar measure on
(B,\circ)
. Hence we can simply say that the skew brace
B
admits the unique probability Haar measure
\mu
.

For simplicity of notation, a ‘compact Hausdorff topological skew brace’ will be termed just as a ‘compact skew brace’. We now prove

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a compact skew brace. Then

\operatorname{Pb}(B)\;=\;\int_{B}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)
,
where

\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\;=\;\int_{B}\chi_{Z}(x,y)\,d\mu(y)
.

Proof.

By the definition of the commuting probability, we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=(\mu\times\mu)(Z)=\int_{B\times B}\chi_{Z}\,d(\mu\times\mu),

which by the Fubini-Tonelli’s Theorem
[
20
, Section 36]
gives

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\int_{B}\chi_{Z}(x,y)\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)=\int_{B}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x),

which, using the given value of
\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))
, completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume that
H
is a Borel subgroup of
(B,\circ)
of a compact skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
. If
[B:H]\geqslant n
, then
\mu(H)\leqslant\frac{1}{n}
, where
[B:H]
denotes the index of
(H,\circ)
in
(B,\circ)
. Moreover, if
[B:H]=n
, then
\mu(H)=\frac{1}{n}
. Specifically, if
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
is properly contained in
B
, then
\mu(Ann(B))\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
. Further, if the index of
H
in
(B,\circ)
is not finite, then
\mu(H)=0
.

Proof.

As
H
is a Borel subset of
B
,
H
is a measurable set, and so
\mu(H)
is well defined.
Since
[B:H]\geqslant n
, there exist at least
n
disjoint cosets

x_{1}\circ H,\;x_{2}\circ H,\ldots,x_{n}\circ H,

of
H
in
(B,\circ)
, which are all measurable sets.
Thus, we have

1=\mu(B)\;\geqslant\;\mu\!\left(\sqcup_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\circ H\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu(x_{i}\circ H)=n\mu(H)

If
[B:H]=n
, then
(B,\circ)=\sqcup_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\circ H
, and therefore
\mu(H)=\frac{1}{n}
.

Finally assume that the index of
H
in
B
is not finite. Contrarily assume that
\mu(H)=\epsilon>0
. Then there exists a natural number
m
such that
m\epsilon>1
. Now choose any
m
left cosets
\{x_{1}\circ H,\;x_{2}\circ H,\ldots,x_{m}\circ H\}
of
H
in
(B,\circ)
. Therefore

1=\mu(B)\;\geqslant\;\mu\!\left(\sqcup_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\circ H\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(x_{i}\circ H)=m\mu(H)=m\epsilon>1,

which is not possible. The proof is complete.
∎

We would like to remark that if
H
is a Borel subgroup of
(B,+)
, then the assertions of the preceding result hold true. Moreover, if
H
is a sub-skew brace of
B
, which is a Borel set, then
[(B,+):H]
and
[(B,\circ):H]
are simultaneously finite or infinite; if finite, then
[(B,+):H]=[(B,\circ):H]
.

Let
B:=(B,+,\circ)
be a compact skew brace such that
B\neq\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. Suppose that no element
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
has finite centraliser index
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)]
in
B
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=(\mu\times\mu)(Z)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{lem: int and cbd})

\displaystyle=\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)+\int_{B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\int_{B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=0~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{lem:closed}).

Thus we get

Lemma 5.7
.

For a compact skew brace
B
with
B\neq\operatorname{Ann}(B)
and
\operatorname{Ann}(B)=\operatorname{FCI}(B)
,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=0
.

Now we prove that
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
is a Borel set.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a Hausdorff topological skew brace. Then
\operatorname{FCI}(B)
is a Borel set.

Proof.

Let
F_{n}:=\{y\in B\mid[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)]\leqslant n\}
. Then
\operatorname{FCI}(B)=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}
. Let
x
be a limit point of
F_{n}
. Then there is a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}
in
F_{n}
which converge to
x
. Since
x_{\lambda}\in F_{n}
, we see that
B=\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}(b_{i}\circ\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{\lambda}))
for some
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
and
b_{i}\in B
. Let
b\in B
be an arbitrary element. Then
b\in b_{i}\circ\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{\lambda})
, for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
. So
b_{i}^{-1}\circ b\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x_{\lambda})
. Define three functions
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}:B\rightarrow B
by
f_{1}(y)=(b_{i}^{-1}\circ b)\circ y\circ(b_{i}^{-1}\circ b)^{-1}
and
f_{2}(y)=(b_{i}^{-1}\circ b)\ast y
and
f_{3}(y)=y\ast(b_{i}^{-1}\circ b)
for all
y\in B
. Then
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
are continuous functions. Hence
f_{j}(x_{\lambda})
converges to
f_{j}(x)
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant 3
, and therefore
b_{i}^{-1}\circ b\in\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
. This implies that
b\in b_{i}\circ\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
. Consequently,
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)]\leqslant k
, which gives
x\in F_{n}
proving
F_{n}
is closed in
B
. The proof is complete.
∎

We now prove a brace analogue of
[
23
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a compact skew brace. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{Pb}(B)>0
.

(2)

\operatorname{FCI}(B)
is open in
B
.

(3)

[(B,\circ):\operatorname{FCI}(B)]<\infty
.

Proof.

Set
F:=\operatorname{FCI}(B)
. Since the index of
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is infinite in
(B,\circ)
for all
x\in B-F
, it follows that

\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\int_{B}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)=\int_{F}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)\leqslant\int_{F}\,d\mu(x)=\mu(F).

So by (1), we get
\mu(F)>0
. Then it follows from
[
21
, Corollary 20.17]
that
F\circ F^{-1}
contains a nonempty open set. Since
F\circ F^{-1}=F
, by
[
21
, Theorem 5.5]
,
F
is open in
B
.

Now we assume (2). Since
F
is nonempty and open in
B
, we have
\mu(F)>0
. Then, by Lemma
5.6
,
[(B,\circ):F]<\infty
.
This proves (3). If (3) holds, then, by Lemma
5.6
,
\mu(F)>0
. If
\int_{F}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)=0
, then, by
[
29
, Theorem 1.39]
,
\mu\operatorname{Cb}_{B}=0
almost everywhere on
F
. But we know that
\mu\operatorname{Cb}_{B}
is nowhere zero on
F
. Hence,
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\int_{F}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)>0
, which proves (1), and the proof is complete.
∎

Now we present an example of a compact non-trivial skew brace
B
with
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=0
. Let
(G,+)
be a non-abelian connected compact Lie group. Let
B:=(G,+,+_{op})
. Note that if
[a,x]^{+}=1
, then
a*x=1
. It now follows that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)=\operatorname{C}_{(B,+)}(x)=\operatorname{C}_{(B,\circ)}(x)
for all
x\in B
. Thus we get
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\operatorname{Pb}(G,+,+)
is equal to the commuting probability of the group
(G,+)
. Let
\Phi(G,+)
be the subset of all pairs
(x,y)\in(G,+)\times(G,+)
such that
x
and
y
generate a non-abelian free subgroup of
(G,+)
. Then it follows from
[
22
, Proposition 6.86. and Exercise E 6.18.]
that Haar measure of the complement
C
of
\Phi(G,+)
in
(G,+)
is zero. Since
Z
is contained in
C
, we have
(\mu\times\mu)(Z)=0
, where
\mu
is the probability Haar measure. This proves that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=0
.

We now get a lower bound on
\operatorname{Pb}(B)
for a compact skew brace
B
.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a compact skew brace such that
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=d
. Then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\geqslant\frac{2d-1}{d^{2}}
. Moreover, if
d=p
, a prime integer, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}
.

Proof.

By a direct computation, we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{lem: int and cbd})

\displaystyle=\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)+\int_{B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)+\int_{B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\tfrac{1}{d}\mu(B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)))~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{lem:closed})

\displaystyle=\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\tfrac{1}{d}\big(\mu(B)-\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))\big)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d-1}{d}\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\tfrac{1}{d}\mu(B)

\displaystyle=\frac{d-1}{d^{2}}+\frac{1}{d}~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{lem:closed})

\displaystyle=\frac{2d-1}{d^{2}}.

Now assume that
d=p
. So, by Proposition
2.1
,
\operatorname{Ann}(B)=\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
for all
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. Let
\{1,y_{1},\ldots,y_{p-1}\}
be a set of coset representatives of
\operatorname{Ann}(B)
in
B
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B))

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)~~(\text{ by Lemma }\ref{lem: int and cbd}.)

\displaystyle=\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\big(\int_{y_{i}+\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\big(\tfrac{1}{p}\mu(y_{i}+\operatorname{Ann}(B))\big)

\displaystyle=\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\tfrac{p-1}{p}\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{p-1}{p^{2}}=\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

We now compute some upper bounds.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a compact skew brace. Then the following hold true:

(1)

For all
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
,
\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
.

(2)

\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{3}{4}
.

(3)

If
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=d
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{d+1}{2d}
.

(4)

In particular, if
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=d\geq 3
, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{5}{8}
.

(5)

If
B
is not FCI, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

For
x\in B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)
we know that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)\subsetneq B
. It now follows from Proposition
2.1
that
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is a proper subgroup of
(B,\circ)
. Since
\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)
is closed, it admits a value under the Haar measure
\mu
. Hence, by Lemma
5.6
, we get

\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

For the second assertion, we compute

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=(\mu\times\mu)(Z)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{lem: int and cbd})

\displaystyle=\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)+\int_{B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)))~~(\text{by (1)})

\displaystyle=\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\mu(B)-\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))\big)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{3}{4},~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{lem:closed}).

Putting
\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))=\frac{1}{d}
, in the preceding computation, we readily get assertion (3). Assertion (4) holds on the lines of the proof of Theorem
3.8
, using Proposition
5.10
for
d=3
, and the last assertion holds using the fact that
\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))=0
(Lemma
5.6
) in the above inequalities. The proof is now complete.
∎

Recall that
F_{n}:=\{y\in B\mid[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(y)]\leqslant n\}
is a Borel set for a compact skew brace
B
. We consider a specific situation when
B
a compact skew brace and
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=4
. Then
F_{2}\subseteq F_{4}
are Borel sets. Define
E_{4}:=F_{4}-F_{2}
, which is the collections all elements
x
of
B
such that
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)]=4\}
. Note that
E_{4}=F_{4}\cap(B-F_{2})
. Since both
F_{4}
and
B-F_{2}
are Borel sets,
E_{4}
is Borel too, and therefore Haar measure
\mu
is defined on
E_{4}
. We can now say little more on the number
\frac{5}{8}
as a commuting probability.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
B
be an infinite compact skew brace. Then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{5}{8}
if and only if
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=4
and
\mu(E_{4})=0
.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{5}{8}
. Then, by Proposition
5.10
and Theorem
5.11
, we obtain
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=4
. Thus
[(B,\circ):\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x)]
is either 2 or 4. Now

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pb}(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\operatorname{Ann}(B)}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)+\int_{B-\operatorname{Ann}(B)-E_{4}}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)+\int_{E_{4}}\mu(\operatorname{Cb}_{B}(x))\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B)+\frac{1}{2}\big(\mu(B)-\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B)-\mu(E_{4})\big)+\frac{1}{4}\mu(E_{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big(\mu(\operatorname{Ann}(B))-\frac{1}{4}\mu(E_{4})\big)=\frac{5}{8}-\frac{1}{4}\mu(E_{4})).

This gives
\mu(E_{4})=0
. The converse follows from the preceding equations using the given hypotheses.
∎

We finally get the skew brace analogue of the main result of
[
18
]
.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a compact skew brace. Then

\operatorname{Pb}(B)>\frac{5}{8}
if and only if
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|\leqslant 2
. Moreover,
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=2
if and only if
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{3}{4}
.

Proof.

If
B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)=1
, that is,
B
is a trivial brace, then
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
. So assume that
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=2
. Then it follows from Proposition
5.10
that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{3}{4}
. Conversely, assume that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)>\frac{5}{8}
. If
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=1
, then
B=\operatorname{Ann}(B)
. Assume that
B
is not a trivial brace. Then
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|\geqslant 2
. If
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=2
, then, by Proposition
5.10
we get
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{3}{4}
. So we assume that
|B/\operatorname{Ann}(B)|=d\geqslant 3
. It now follows from Theorem
5.11
(4) that
\operatorname{Pb}(B)\leqslant\frac{5}{8}
, which proves the first assertion. The second one follows by using Theorem
5.11
(3).
∎

We conclude with an explicit example of an infinite skew brace
B
with
\operatorname{Pb}(B)=\frac{3}{4}
. We first observe that
for two compact skew braces
(B_{1},+,\circ)
and
(B_{2},+,\circ)
, it follows that
\operatorname{Pb}(B_{1}\times B_{2})=\operatorname{Pb}(B_{1})\operatorname{Pb}(B_{2})
.
Let
B_{1}=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},+,+)
be the trivial brace, where
+
is the natural addition on real numbers mod
\mathbb{Z}
, and
B_{2}
be the skew brace which we defined in Example
3.6
. Then
\operatorname{Pb}(B_{1})=1
,
\operatorname{Pb}(B_{2})=3/4
, and therefore
\operatorname{Pb}(B_{1}\times B_{2})=3/4
.
