A finitary criterion for selfless tracial C*-algebras
Ali Jabbari

Abstract
. We study the class of selfless C*-probability spaces introduced by Robert. It is known that a selfless tracial algebra has strict comparison and a unique trace. We prove that for separable tracial C*-algebras, selflessness is equivalent to approximate selflessness , a finitary condition: for every finite set F F , every N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 and ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 there exists a unitary u u with | τ ⁡ ( u k ) | < ε |\tau(u^{k})|<\varepsilon ( 1 ≤ | k | ≤ N 1\leq|k|\leq N ) and | τ ⁡ ( w ) | < ε |\tau(w)|<\varepsilon for all alternating words w w of length ≤ N \leq N built from centered elements of F F and powers u n u^{n} ( | n | ≤ N |n|\leq N ). The equivalence is established using a diagonalisation argument in the tracial ultrapower. As an application we give a concise proof that countable groups with a topologically‑free extreme boundary are C*-selfless. We also discuss the relation to nuclearity and 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -stability.

Introduction

The notion of a
selfless
C*-probability space was introduced in
[
10
]
. A unital C*-algebra
A\neq\mathbb{C}
equipped with a state
\rho
having faithful GNS representation is called selfless if the first-factor embedding
(A,\rho)\hookrightarrow(A,\rho)*(A,\rho)
is existential in the sense of continuous model theory. Intuitively, a selfless algebra is one that, after passing to a suitable ultrapower, contains a free Haar unitary independent from the diagonal copy of
A
; this property lies strictly between being existentially closed and having a central sequence of matrix units, blending aspects of model theory and free probability. Selfless C*-probability spaces enjoy strong structural properties such as simplicity, possessing a unique trace (if tracial), satisfying strict comparison, and possessing the uniform Dixmier property
[
10
]
.

Authors in
[
1
]
have obtained deep structural results for reduced free product C*-algebras. In particular,
[
1
]
proves that Voiculescu’s free semicircular algebras

(

) are selfless, and more generally establishes selflessness for a wide class of free products under mild rapid‑decay hypotheses. Those results do not follow from the approximate selflessness characterisation given here; rather they illustrate the power of constructive techniques (filtrations, Khintchine inequalities, asymptotic orthogonality) that are complementary to our model‑theoretic approach. We therefore refer the reader to
[
1
]
for a comprehensive treatment of selflessness in the context of free products.

Recently, Hayes et al.
[
5

introduced the notion of
selfless inclusions
of C*-probability spaces and used it to establish selflessness for new families of C*-algebras, including the reduced free unitary compact quantum groups

(

More recently, Gao et al.
[

have proved selflessness for reduced amalgamated free products and HNN extensions of C*-algebras, using a version of Ozawa’s PHP property in the C*-algebraic setting. Their results further expand the class of selfless algebras by addressing amalgamation and graph products, and provide another route to selflessness that is independent of the approximate selflessness characterisation used here.

It was proved in
[

(see also the comprehensive geometric study
[

[
8

Existential embeddings are global statements that are often difficult to verify directly. A natural goal is to find a finitary, quantitative reformulation that can be tested on generators and relations. This paper provides such a reformulation for separable algebras. We introduce the notion of
approximately selfless
(Definition
2
) which only requires, for every finite set and every tolerance, the existence of a unitary satisfying explicit moment conditions. This yields a finitary reformulation of an existential embedding property. Our main result (Theorem
2
) states that for separable C*-probability spaces with a faithful trace, selflessness is equivalent to approximate selflessness.

Value of the finitary criterion.

Approximate selflessness provides a finitary, local test for an inherently infinitary (model-theoretic) property. Instead of constructing an existential embedding into an infinite free product, one only needs to produce unitaries that satisfy explicit moment conditions up to arbitrary precision. This makes selflessness accessible to concrete computations and provides a bridge to free probability and asymptotic freeness.

The equivalence is obtained by a diagonalisation argument in a tracial ultrapower. The approximate conditions allow us to construct, via a cofinal sequence, a single unitary in an ultrapower that is exactly free from the diagonal copy of
A
. This yields an existential embedding, and conversely selflessness transfers back through the ultrapower to give the required approximate unitaries.

We also discuss the interaction with nuclearity and
\mathcal{Z}
-stability. While selfless nuclear algebras automatically have strict comparison and a unique trace, it remains an open question whether they are necessarily
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. We emphasise that the known structural consequences (Theorem
1
) are due to Robert
[
10
]
; no claim of
\mathcal{Z}
-stability is made here.

Conceptual position of selflessness.

Selflessness is a global property: it requires the first-factor embedding into the free product to be existential. This is weaker than being existentially closed (which would require existential embeddings into any extension), and it is distinct from being
strongly self-absorbing
(which involves tensor products and central sequences rather than free products). In the tracial setting it is also unrelated to nuclearity:
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
is selfless and non‑nuclear, while many nuclear algebras fail to be selfless. It implies strict comparison and a unique trace, but it does not obviously entail finite nuclear dimension or
\mathcal{Z}
‑stability. The property also appears independent of exactness and of the completely bounded approximation property, though a precise delineation remains a topic for future study. The finitary criterion we provide — approximate selflessness — is new and can be seen as a C*-algebraic analogue of mixing properties for von Neumann algebras, but without requiring a distinguished subalgebra. Unlike McDuff-type properties (which involve a central sequence of matrix algebras), approximate selflessness involves only a single unitary that is asymptotically free from the algebra. This is close in spirit to Voiculescu’s asymptotic freeness and to central sequence techniques, but with the crucial twist that the freeness is required only in an ultrapower, not in the algebra itself.

In this paper we work exclusively with
tracial
C*-probability spaces, i.e. the state is a faithful trace; we denote it by
\tau
. The general selflessness notion of

10
]
allows arbitrary states, but our results require a trace to make the free product with

2.
Background on existential embeddings and selflessness

We work in the continuous first‑order language for tracial C*-algebras: the trace is a
1
-Lipschitz unary predicate, unitaries are defined by the relation
xx^{*}=x^{*}x=1
, and existential formulas are built from norm inequalities using finite suprema. An embedding
\theta:(A,\tau)\to(B,\tau)
is called

if for every quantifier-free formula
\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and every tuple
\bar{a}
in
A
, one has

\inf_{\bar{y}}\Phi(\bar{a},\bar{y})^{(A,\tau)}=\inf_{\bar{y}}\Phi(\theta(\bar{a}),\bar{y})^{(B,\tau)},

where the infimum ranges over the unit ball of the respective algebra. Equivalently (see
[
10
]
, diagram (1.2)), there exists an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
and an embedding
\sigma:(B,\tau)\to(A^{\mathcal{U}},\tau^{\mathcal{U}})
such that
\sigma\circ\theta
is the diagonal embedding of
A
into
A^{\mathcal{U}}
.

For our purposes it is crucial that selflessness can be tested with a single free Haar unitary. Indeed, by
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
a C*-probability space
(A,\tau)
is selfless if and only if the first-factor embedding
\theta:(A,\tau)\hookrightarrow(A,\tau)*(C(\mathbb{T}),\lambda)
is existential, where
\lambda
is the trace induced by the normalized Lebesgue measure on the circle and
*
denotes the reduced free product. The state
\tau*\lambda
is faithful because
\tau
is a faithful trace and
\lambda
is faithful on
C(\mathbb{T})

[
2
]
.

Theorem 1
.

[
10
, Theorem 3.1]

If
(A,\tau)
is selfless and
\tau
is a faithful trace, then

(i)

A
is simple,

(ii)

\tau
is the unique tracial state on
A
,

(iii)

A
has the uniform Dixmier property,

(iv)

A
has
strict comparison of positive elements by
\tau
: for all positive

,

d_{\tau}(a)<d_{\tau}(b)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a\precsim b,

where

and

(v)

These properties hold unconditionally for any tracial selfless C*-probability space with a faithful trace; no additional assumptions such as nuclearity or exactness are required.
Thus selflessness is a robust source of regularity for tracial C*-algebras.

3.
Approximately selfless algebras and the finitary criterion

We now give a quantitative characterisation of selflessness for separable algebras. All freeness statements below are understood with respect to the trace and the reduced free product construction. The approximate conditions are designed so that, via ultrapowers, they yield exact freeness in the sense of free probability: a Haar unitary that is independent from the original algebra with respect to the ultrapower trace.

Definition 1
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
. A unitary
u\in A
is called an
(N,\varepsilon)
-approximate Haar unitary
if

|\tau(u^{k})|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ with }1\leq|k|\leq N.

Note that
u^{-k}=(u^{*})^{k}
is covered by the same condition because
\tau(u^{-k})=\overline{\tau(u^{k})}
.
Given a finite set
F\subset A
, we denote by
\widetilde{F}
its centering, i.e.,

\widetilde{F}=\{x-\tau(x)1:x\in F\}\cup\{x^{*}-\overline{\tau(x)}1:x\in F\}.

(1)

The inclusion of the adjoints guarantees that
\widetilde{F}
is self‑adjoint. All elements of
\widetilde{F}
are thus centered, i.e., have trace zero. In the sequel we shall work exclusively with centered elements, and whenever we later talk about an element of
F
inside an alternating word, we actually mean the matching centered version from
\widetilde{F}
.

Definition 2
.

A C*-probability space
(A,\tau)
with
\tau
a faithful trace is
approximately selfless
if for every finite set
F\subset A
, every integer
N\geq 1
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a unitary
u\in A
satisfying the following two conditions:

(i)

(N,\varepsilon)
-approximate Haar:
|\tau(u^{k})|<\varepsilon
for all
1\leq|k|\leq N
.

(ii)

For every alternating word built from factors that alternate between elements of
\widetilde{F}
and powers
u^{n}
(
1\leq|n|\leq N
), with total number of factors at most
N
, we have
|\tau(w)|<\varepsilon
. Such a word is necessarily of one of the four patterns

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle=y_{1}u^{n_{1}}y_{2}u^{n_{2}}\cdots y_{k}u^{n_{k}},

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=u^{n_{1}}y_{1}u^{n_{2}}y_{2}\cdots u^{n_{k}}y_{k},

\displaystyle w_{3}

\displaystyle=y_{1}u^{n_{1}}y_{2}u^{n_{2}}\cdots y_{k-1}u^{n_{k-1}}y_{k},

\displaystyle w_{4}

\displaystyle=u^{n_{1}}y_{1}u^{n_{2}}y_{2}\cdots u^{n_{k-1}}y_{k-1}u^{n_{k}},

where each
y_{i}\in\widetilde{F}
,
1\leq|n_{i}|\leq N
, and no two consecutive factors are both powers of
u
or both from
\widetilde{F}
(the word strictly alternates between centered elements and nontrivial powers of
u
, with negative exponents interpreted as the corresponding powers of
u^{*}
). The total number of factors (counting each
y_{i}
and each
u^{n_{i}}
) is at most
N
; this implicitly bounds
k
and the exponents.

The conditions involve only finitely many inequalities for given
F,N,\varepsilon
; therefore the definition is purely quantitative and avoids any model‑theoretic machinery.
Note that for a fixed finite set
F
and a fixed integer
N
, there are only finitely many alternating words of the above form, because
\widetilde{F}
is finite and the length and exponents are bounded. Hence the condition in (ii) is a finite collection of trace bounds.

Why are alternating words sufficient? In the reduced free product
A*C(\mathbb{T})
, any *-polynomial in
A
and the Haar unitary
u
can be written, after centering the elements of
A
, as a linear combination of alternating words of the above form plus terms involving only
A
or only
u
(see, e.g.,
[
13
]
). Since the trace annihilates all higher moments of a Haar unitary separately, vanishing of all alternating moments implies that the subalgebras
A
and
C(\mathbb{T})
are free with respect to the free product trace. Hence controlling these alternating words, even in a truncated form, captures the full freeness condition.

Because the definition requires the condition for every finite subset of the C*-algebra, it is useful to know that it suffices to verify it on a dense generating set. The following perturbation result makes this precise.

Lemma 1
.

Let

Proof.

Let

For each

A standard estimate using the fact that

|\tau(w)-\tau(w^{\prime})|\leq\sum_{i}\|z_{i}-\tilde{y}_{x_{i}}\|\ \!\!\prod_{j\neq i}\max\{\|z_{j}\|,\|\tilde{y}_{x_{j}}\|\}.

The same bound holds for the variants starting or ending with

The following result serves as a fundamental bridge in the argument. It demonstrates that the family of approximate unitaries arising from the definition can be systematically organized into a single sequence whose ultralimit, taken in the tracial ultrapower, is free from the algebra. The central observation is that alternating words of arbitrary but finite length already generate all mixed moments required to establish freeness with respect to the trace; the preceding remark on alternating words explains why this reduction is sufficient. Moreover, since the dense *-subalgebra is countable, the collection of such alternating words is itself countable. Consequently, one may construct a diagonal sequence
(u_{m})
with the property that each alternating word is eventually captured by the approximating sequence.

\lim_{m\to\mathcal{U}}\tau(w(u_{m}))=0
, so by definition of the ultrapower trace,
\tau^{\mathcal{U}}(w(v))=\lim_{m\to\mathcal{U}}\tau(w(u_{m}))=0
for every alternating word
w
. The vanishing of all mixed alternating moments characterizes free independence of a Haar unitary from
A
with respect to the trace; see
[
13
, Definition 2.5.1 and Proposition 1.5.5]
for more details. Hence
v
is free from the diagonal copy of
A
.
∎

The next result makes the connection between ultrapower freeness and selflessness completely rigorous. The key point is that free independence of tracial C*-algebras with faithful traces forces their generated C*-algebra to be the reduced free product with the free product trace; this is a standard fact in free probability, see
[
13
, Definitions 1.4.1, 1.5.4 and Proposition 1.5.5]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
(A,\tau)
be a tracial C*-probability space and let
\mathcal{U}
be an ultrafilter. If there exists a unitary
v\in A^{\mathcal{U}}
such that

(i)

\tau^{\mathcal{U}}(v^{k})=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,

(ii)

the subalgebras generated by the diagonal copy of
A
and by
v
are free with respect to
\tau^{\mathcal{U}}
,

then the first-factor embedding
\theta:A\hookrightarrow A*_{\mathrm{red}}C(\mathbb{T})
is existential, and consequently
(A,\tau)
is selfless.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be the normalized Lebesgue trace on
C(\mathbb{T})
and
u
the identity function.
By
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
, the free product state
\tau*\lambda
on the reduced free product
A*_{\mathrm{red}}C(\mathbb{T})
is faithful; hence its restriction to the algebraic free product
A\odot C(\mathbb{T})
is a faithful trace. Since
A
and
v
are free in
(A^{\mathcal{U}},\tau^{\mathcal{U}})
and both have faithful traces, the C*-algebra they generate is naturally isomorphic to the reduced free product
A*_{\mathrm{red}}C^{*}(\{v\})\cong A*_{\mathrm{red}}C(\mathbb{T})
with the free product trace; the isomorphism sends
v
to
u
and acts as the identity on
A
. Indeed, vanishing of all mixed alternating moments is precisely the condition that the pair
(A,\{v\})
is free with respect to the trace; by the universal property of the reduced free product for tracial C*-algebras, see
[
13
, Sections 1.4 and 1.5]
, the canonical map from
A*_{\mathrm{red}}C(\mathbb{T})
to
A^{\mathcal{U}}
is injective and identifies the two algebras. In particular, the
*
-homomorphism
\sigma:\sum a_{i}\otimes f_{i}\mapsto\sum a_{i}f_{i}(v)
extends to an injective
*
-homomorphism
\widetilde{\sigma}:A*_{\mathrm{red}}C(\mathbb{T})\to A^{\mathcal{U}}
.

By construction,
\widetilde{\sigma}\circ\theta
is exactly the diagonal embedding of
A
into
A^{\mathcal{U}}
. According to the ultrapower characterisation of existential embeddings recalled in Section 2, this is the defining condition for
\theta
to be existential. Finally,
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
states that
(A,\tau)
is selfless if and only if such an existential embedding exists.
∎

The following result is the main result of the paper. It shows that the infinitary, model‑theoretic property of selflessness can be recast as a finitely testable moment condition, making it computationally accessible. The proof relies on the tracial ultrapower and the separability of
A
, which allows a diagonalisation over a countable dense subset.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(A,\tau)
be a separable C*-probability space with
\tau
a faithful trace. Then
(A,\tau)
is selfless if and only if it is approximately selfless.

Proof.

Assume first that
(A,\tau)
is selfless. By
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
, the first-factor embedding
\theta:A\to A*_{\mathrm{red}}C(\mathbb{T})
is existential. Hence there exist an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
and an embedding
\sigma:A*_{\mathrm{red}}C(\mathbb{T})\to A^{\mathcal{U}}
such that
\sigma\circ\theta
is the diagonal embedding of
A
. Let
u\in C(\mathbb{T})
be the canonical Haar unitary and set
v=\sigma(u)\in A^{\mathcal{U}}
. Since every element of
A^{\mathcal{U}}
can be represented by a bounded sequence, we write
v=[(u_{n})]
for a sequence of unitaries
u_{n}\in A
(the lift is possible because the quotient map is surjective and unitaries form a closed subset).

Fix an arbitrary finite set
F\subset A
, an integer
N\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
. In
A^{\mathcal{U}}
, the unitary
v
satisfies
|\tau^{\mathcal{U}}(v^{k})|=0<\varepsilon
for all
1\leq|k|\leq N
, and
\tau^{\mathcal{U}}(w(v))=0
for every alternating word
w
of the form in Definition
2
built from centered elements of
\widetilde{F}
and powers of
v
. The collection of these finitely many strict inequalities constitutes a finite set of first-order conditions in the language of tracial C*-algebras. By Łoś’s theorem for tracial ultrapowers (see
[
3
, Theorem 2.3.1]
),
these conditions must already hold of
u_{n}
for
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
n
.
In particular, there exists an index
n
such that
u_{n}
satisfies them in
A
.
Thus
(A,\tau)
is approximately selfless.

Conversely, assume that
(A,\tau)
is approximately selfless. Lemma
2
supplies an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
and a unitary
v\in A^{\mathcal{U}}
satisfying the hypotheses of Proposition
1
. That proposition then directly yields that
(A,\tau)
is selfless, completing the equivalence.
∎

4.
Groups with topologically‑free extreme boundaries are C*-selfless

In this section we apply the approximate selflessness criterion to give a concise proof of the main theorem of
[
9
]
(see also the comprehensive geometric treatment in
[
8
]
). The geometric construction of axial sequences relies on the deep results of
[
8
]
, but the verification of the approximate condition is brief.

We recall the necessary definitions from
[
9
]
(and
[
8
]
). Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group and let
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
be an action on a compact Hausdorff space
X
. The action is called an
extreme boundary
if it is minimal and
extremely proximal
: for every non‑empty open sets
U,V\subset X
there exists
g\in\Gamma
such that
g(X\setminus U)\subset V
. The action is
topologically free
if the set of points with trivial stabiliser is dense in
X
. A sequence
(z_{n})_{n}
in
\Gamma
is called
axial
if there exists a topologically‑free extreme boundary
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
with distinct points
z_{\pm}\in X
such that:

(i)

for every neighbourhood
U_{\pm}
of
z_{\pm}
, one has
z_{n}(X\setminus U_{-})\subset U_{+}
eventually, i.e., for all sufficiently large
n
,

(ii)

the
\Gamma
-action on
\{z_{\pm}\}
is free, i.e.,
g\{z_{\pm}\}\cap\{z_{\pm}\}\neq\emptyset
implies
g=1
.

It is shown in
[
9
]
and
[
8
]
that a second‑countable extreme boundary with
|X|>2
admits an axial sequence if and only if it is topologically free.

Let
\mathrm{C}_{\lambda}^{*}(\Gamma)
be the reduced group C*-algebra with its canonical tracial state
\tau
given by
\tau(g)=\delta_{g,1}
. We view each group element as a unitary in
\mathrm{C}_{\lambda}^{*}(\Gamma)
.

4.1.
Verification of the approximate condition on group elements

The following lemma translates the dynamics of the axial sequence into the estimates required by Definition
2
for the set
S=\Gamma
; it thus provides explicit unitaries witnessing approximate selflessness directly from the axial sequence. The proof is a complete verification, leaving no further work to be done.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group with a topologically‑free extreme boundary and let
(z_{n})_{n}
be an axial sequence. Then for every finite set
F_{0}\subset\Gamma
, every
N\geq 1
and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n
such that the unitary
u=z_{n}
satisfies the conditions of Definition
2
for the set
F_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
F_{0}\subset\Gamma
be finite, let
N\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
. Because
\tau(g)=\delta_{g,1}
, the centered set
\widetilde{F}_{0}
is simply
F_{0}\setminus\{1\}
(the identity becomes
0
, and can be ignored in alternating products). An alternating word of the four types with factors from
\widetilde{F}_{0}
and powers of
z_{n}
is therefore a group word of the form

w=g_{1}z_{n}^{k_{1}}g_{2}z_{n}^{k_{2}}\cdots g_{m}z_{n}^{k_{m}}

(or a variant starting/ending with a power of
z_{n}
), where each
g_{i}\in F_{0}\setminus\{1\}
and
1\leq|k_{i}|\leq N
. We have
\tau(w)=0
exactly when the corresponding group element is not the identity.

Consider the free product
\Gamma*\langle z\rangle
and the homomorphism
\phi_{n}:\Gamma*\langle z\rangle\to\Gamma
that is the identity on
\Gamma
and sends
z
to
z_{n}
. For any reduced word
\widetilde{w}
in
\Gamma*\langle z\rangle
containing at least one factor
z^{\pm 1}
, topological freeness and the ping‑pong behaviour of the axial sequence imply that
\phi_{n}(\widetilde{w})\neq 1
for all sufficiently large
n
(
[
9
, Lemma 6]
; see also
[
8
]
). Since there are only finitely many such reduced words of length bounded by a function of
N
and
|F_{0}|
, we can find an index
n
such that
\phi_{n}(\widetilde{w})\neq 1
for all of them simultaneously and, moreover, no power
z_{n}^{k}
(
1\leq|k|\leq N
) equals the identity (otherwise the action would have a finite‑order fixed point, contradicting topological freeness). For this
n
we have
\tau(z_{n}^{k})=0
for all
1\leq|k|\leq N
and
\tau(w)=0
for every alternating word
w
as above. Hence
u=z_{n}
satisfies the requirements of Definition
2
(in fact with the exact value zero).∎

We now investigate the C*-selflessness of the reduced group C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}_{\lambda}^{*}(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Gamma
be an infinite countable discrete group that admits a topologically‑free extreme boundary action. Then its reduced group C*-algebra
\mathrm{C}_{\lambda}^{*}(\Gamma)
is selfless (with respect to the canonical trace).

This result provides a vast source of selfless algebras coming from geometric group theory, demonstrating that the finitary criterion is effective in practice.

Proof.

The reduced group C*-algebra
A=\mathrm{C}_{\lambda}^{*}(\Gamma)
is separable and its canonical trace
\tau
is faithful. The set
S=\Gamma
(viewed as unitaries in
A
) generates a dense *-algebra (the group algebra). By Lemma
3
, the hypothesis of Lemma
1
is satisfied for
S
. Therefore
(A,\tau)
is approximately selfless. Theorem
2
now implies that
(A,\tau)
is selfless.
∎

Remark 1
.

The proof above uses only the dynamics of the axial sequence; the tree‑graded space construction of
[
9
]
is completely avoided. The same method applies to all classes of groups for which an axial (or powers‑type) sequence is available, notably acylindrically hyperbolic groups with trivial finite radical
[
9
, Proposition 15]
and Zariski‑dense subgroups of
\mathrm{PSL}(d,\mathbb{R})

[
9
, Proposition 16]
. For a much wider class of groups and deeper geometric results (including visual boundaries of CAT(0) spaces, Coxeter groups, and cube complexes) we refer the reader to
[
8
]
.

5.
Examples and non-examples

We now collect examples and non-examples to illustrate the concept.

Example 2
.

The reduced group C*-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
is selfless; see
[
10
, Corollary 2.3]
. The same argument shows that
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
for
n\geq 2
is selfless. As demonstrated in Example
1
, approximate selflessness can be verified directly using the free group generators, giving an alternative proof.

Example 3
.

Let
A=M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
with its unique tracial state. Then
A
is not selfless. A selfless C*-algebra must contain a Haar unitary in its ultrapower (Lemma
2
), hence it is infinite‑dimensional.
M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
is finite‑dimensional, so it cannot be selfless. (One may also note that the first‑factor embedding
M_{k}\hookrightarrow M_{k}*C(\mathbb{T})
is not existential, but the dimension argument is simpler.)

Example 4
.

Let
A=C(X)
with a faithful tracial state
\tau
, where
X
is a compact metric space with a probability measure of full support. Then
A
is not selfless because it is not simple (unless
X
is a singleton, but then
A\cong\mathbb{C}
is excluded).

Example 5
.

It is not known whether the Jiang–Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
is selfless. If it is, then Question
1
would be answered positively. Conversely, a counterexample to Question
1
would be a nuclear selfless algebra not isomorphic to
\mathcal{Z}\otimes A
for any
A
; no such algebra is currently known.

6.
Relation to nuclearity and
\mathcal{Z}
-stability

We emphasise that we do not prove
\mathcal{Z}
-stability here. The only known consequences of selflessness for tracial algebras are those listed in Theorem
1
(strict comparison, unique trace, uniform Dixmier property, stable rank one). Whether nuclear selfless algebras are
\mathcal{Z}
-stable is an open problem.

It is a common misconception that
\mathcal{Z}
-stability implies nuclearity. In fact, if
B
is any non-nuclear C*-algebra, then
A=B\otimes\mathcal{Z}
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable and non-nuclear, since tensoring a non-nuclear algebra with a unital nuclear algebra yields a non-nuclear algebra. Thus nuclearity is not necessary for
\mathcal{Z}
-stability. Consequently, the absence of nuclearity (as in the free group C*-algebras) does not provide a counterexample.

The reduced group C*-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
is selfless and non-nuclear. Whether it is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable is a deep open problem; it does not serve as a test case for nuclear selfless algebras.

While selflessness implies strict comparison and uniqueness of trace, these are also necessary conditions for
\mathcal{Z}
-stability in the nuclear case. By the Toms–Winter theorem, a nuclear, simple, separable, unital C*-algebra with strict comparison and finite nuclear dimension is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
[
12
,
15
]
. However, strict comparison alone is not sufficient; counterexamples exist with infinite nuclear dimension (e.g., the Razak–Jacelon algebra
[
6
]
).

Selflessness provides additional structure: the existence, in every ultrapower, of a unitary that is free from the diagonal copy of
A
. This is reminiscent of the uniform property Gamma of Kirchberg, but stronger. It is plausible that for nuclear algebras, selflessness forces finite nuclear dimension or the existence of a strongly self-absorbing tensor factor.

Thus the natural question remains:

Question 1
.

Let
(A,\tau)
be a separable nuclear selfless C*-probability space with
\tau
a faithful trace. Does it follow that
A
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable (i.e.,
A\cong A\otimes\mathcal{Z}
)?

A positive answer would make selflessness a new sufficient condition for
\mathcal{Z}
-stability, independent of finite nuclear dimension. A negative answer would require a nuclear, selfless algebra with strict comparison and unique trace but infinite nuclear dimension – an object not known to exist.

7.
Concluding remarks

(1)

Theorem
2
provides a concrete, finitary criterion for selflessness for separable algebras. The four alternating word patterns ensure that the state on the free product vanishes on all mixed terms.

(2)

Selfless tracial algebras automatically satisfy strict comparison and have a unique trace. In the nuclear case, these are necessary conditions for
\mathcal{Z}
-stability, but they are not known to be sufficient. Question
1
remains a natural open problem.

(3)

The example
C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
shows that selflessness does not force nuclearity, but does not settle the
\mathcal{Z}
-stability question.

Appendix A
Comparison with mixing and weak mixing notions

For completeness we briefly position selflessness relative to better-known concepts in von Neumann algebra theory.

(i)

Mixing MASA:
A subalgebra
B\subset M
(usually a MASA) is mixing if for all
a,b\in M\ominus B
one has
\tau(u_{n}au_{n}^{*}b)\to 0
along a net of unitaries from
B
, see
[
11
]
for more details. This requires a distinguished subalgebra and is a property of an inclusion.

(ii)

Weak mixing:
Similar but with Cesàro convergence, we refer to
[
11
]
.

(iii)

Selflessness:
A property of a single C*-algebra (with a faithful trace) that does not refer to any subalgebra. It is equivalent to the existence of a Haar unitary that is free from the algebra in the ultrapower, i.e., asymptotic freeness. Selflessness is closer in spirit to the notion of
strongly self-absorbing
but is distinct, see
[
14
]
. For example
\mathcal{Z}
is strongly self-absorbing but it is not known whether it is selfless.

Thus selflessness is not directly comparable to mixing or weak mixing; it is a different kind of regularity condition.
