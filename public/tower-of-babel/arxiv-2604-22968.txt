Spin-current model of electric polarization with the tensor gyromagnetic ratio
Mariya Iv. Trukhanova 1,2, Pavel A. Andreev 1

Abstract
The spin-current model of electric polarization of spin origin is developed for a magnetic structure with anisotropic tensor gyromagnetic ratio (g-factor). Three mechanisms of the magnetoelectric effect are proposed, caused by the symmetric Heisenberg exchange interaction, the Dzyaloshinsky-Moriya interaction, and the spin-spin interaction related to the odd anisotropy of the symmetric exchange interaction of magnetic ions via nonmagnetic ion. The dependence of electric polarization on the spin density and tensor g-factor in the generalized spin-current model is derived. New solutions for macroscopic electric polarization, that arise in the cycloidal and helicoidal spin orders and are caused by the non-diagonal components of the gyromagnetic ratio, are predicted. The extended of the spin-current model to including a tensor g-factor can be important for the magnetic ferroelectrics with heavy ions which take part in the formation of magnetoelectric effect.

II
Spin-current model for the tensor g-factor

II.1
The formulation of the problem

We use the method of many-particle quantum hydrodynamics (MPQH) to describe dynamic non-equilibrium processes in environments with a large number of interacting particles and allows one to construct a closed system of dynamical equations for the main physical observables, such as the particle number density, spin density, electric polarization, momentum density. The main idea of this method is the representation of quantum mechanics as the evolution of material fields whose dynamics follow the evolution of the many-particle wave function
Andreev 2019
;
Andreev and Kuz’menkov 2015
;
Andreev and Trukhanova 2023
.

In this paper we use the MPQH method for the developing the spin-current model. As a physical system we consider clusters consisting of ions of two different sublattices: positively charged magnetic transition metal ions with an arbitrary spin order
\bm{S}_{i}
,
\bm{S}_{j}
and a negatively charged diamagnetic oxygen ion
O^{2-}
located at the middle point between the bond of the magnetic ions, which can shift from this point. Each such cluster will be considered here as a point-like quasi-particle. In the first stage, it is necessary to specify the interactions between particles. We take into account that electric dipole moments
\bm{d}_{i}
are induced in clusters and can be affected by an electric field. We also include spin-orbit coupling and the effect of exchange interactions on the dynamical properties of the system. The evolution of such a system is described by the microscopic many-particle Schrödinger–Pauli equation
i\hbar\partial_{t}\psi(R,t)=\hat{H}\psi(R,t)
with the Hamiltonian of interactions

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-\sum_{i}^{N}\biggl(\hat{d}_{i}^{\alpha}\cdot E_{i}^{\alpha}+\gamma^{\alpha\nu}_{i}\hat{S}_{i}^{\nu}B^{\alpha}_{i}+\frac{\gamma_{i}^{\alpha\nu}}{m_{i}c}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}^{\nu}_{i}E^{\beta}_{i}\hat{p}_{i}^{\gamma}\biggr)\qquad

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\neq i}^{N}\biggl(U_{ij}(r_{ij})\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\cdot\hat{S}_{j}^{\alpha}+D^{\alpha}_{ij}(r_{ij})\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat{S}^{\beta}_{i}\hat{S}^{\gamma}_{j}\biggr),\qquad

(1)

where
N
is the total number of magnetic ions (particles) in the system,
\psi(R,t)
is the many-particle wave function, which depends on the coordinates of all particles
R=\{r_{1},...,r_{N}\}
and time
t
,
\hat{\bm{d}}_{i}
is the operator of the electric dipole moment vector,
\hat{\bm{p}}_{i}=-i\hbar\hat{\bm{\nabla}}
is the momentum operator of the
i
-th ion, and
\hbar
,
m_{i}
and
c
are the reduced Planck constant, the mass of the ions and the speed of light in vacuum, respectively.
\bm{E}_{i}
and
\bm{B}_{i}
are the strengths of the electric and magnetic fields acting on the
i
-th ion,
\hat{\bm{S}}_{i}
is the dimensional spin operator,
\gamma_{i}^{\alpha\nu}
is the gyromagnetic tensor, and for the electron
\gamma_{e}^{\alpha\nu}=-\frac{|e|\hbar}{4m_{e}c}g^{\alpha\nu}_{e}
. The first, second, and third terms represent the energy of electric dipoles in an electric field, the Zeeman energy, and the spin-orbit coupling, respectively. The last two terms characterize the different types of exchange interactions: the symmetric exchange interaction (or the Coulomb exchange interaction of the Heisenberg Hamiltonian), where
U_{ij}(r_{ij})=U(r_{i}-r_{j})
is the scalar coefficient (exchange integral), and the antisymmetric exchange interaction, where
\bm{D}_{ij}(r_{ij})=\bm{D}(r_{i}-r_{j})
is the vector coefficient of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction.

II.2
The anisotropic g-factor of heavy ions

It is well known that the magnetic moment is related to the mechanical momet through the gyromagnetic ratio.
Let us discuss the introduction of the gyromagnetic ratio tensor in more detail. In light atoms, the electrostatic interaction of electrons in the atomic shell structure significantly exceeds the spin-orbit interaction, and the so-called
LS
-bond is formed. In this case, on specific time scales, the effective magnetic moment and the total mechanical moment
\bm{J}_{i}
of the
i
-th ion is approximately co-directional
\bm{\mu}_{i}=g_{i}\mu_{B}\bm{J}_{i}
when averaged over time. The Zeeman energy in Hamiltonian is determined by the expression
H_{zeeman}=-\bm{\mu}_{i}\cdot\bm{B}_{i}=-g_{i}\mu_{B}\bm{J}_{i}\cdot\bm{B}_{i}
, where
g_{i}
and
\mu_{B}
are the Lande factor for ion and Bohr magneton, respectively.
When the nuclear charge number in atoms exceeds 27, the energy of the residual electrostatic interaction becomes less than the energy of the spin-orbit interaction, and the LS-bond begins to break, leading to a tensor nature of the gyromagnetic ratio. The breaking of the LS-bond primarily occurs in rare-earth ions, but we can also apply this approach to the transition metal ions. For most heavy transition metal and rare-earth ions in multiferroics, the magnetic moment of the ion no longer lies on a straight line with the mechanical moment of the electron shell. In this case we can represent the magnetic moment vector of the
i
-th ion as
\mu^{\alpha}_{i}=\mu_{B}g^{\alpha\beta}_{i}S^{\beta}_{i}

Scaramucci et al. 2012
, where
g^{\alpha\beta}_{i}
is the gyromagnetic
3\times 3
tensor at the
i
-th site

g^{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}g^{xx}&g^{xy}&g^{xz}\\
g^{yx}&g^{yy}&g^{yz}\\
g^{zx}&g^{zy}&g^{zz}\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

Taking into consideration the tensor nature of the gyromagnetic ratio is important when we describe the properties of multiferroics such as the rare-earth manganites with perovskite structure
Schwan et al. 2011
. In the context of the the multiferroic with the ferroelectrisity of spin origin TbMnO
3

Solovyev 2025
, the g-factor tensor describes the anisotropic response of the magnetic moments of ions Tb
3+
and Mn
3+
to an external magnetic field due to the orthorhombic structure with space group P
bnm
of this material. It has been shown
Soumya et al. 2023
that TbMnO
3
has a perovskite structure, in which the ions of
Tb^{3+}
and
Mn^{3+}
are located inside the oxygen octahedra.
Manganese ions are surrounded by an octahedron consisting of six oxygen ions MnO
6
, and its electronic configuration
d^{4}
has a half-filled low-lying threefold level
t_{2g}
and a higher-lying twofold level
e_{g}
with one electron in a crystallographic field of oxygen ions. In a crystallographic field, the octahedron is lengthened, leading to the removal of the degeneracy of the d-levels and a decrease in the electron energy (Jahn-Teller effect
Dubrovskiy et al. 2017
). Such distortion of MnO
6
octahedra and spin-orbit interaction lead to anisotropy of the g-factor tensor of manganese ions, which can be especially important in describing the magnetoelectric effect. The symmetry of the Mn-O-Mn bonds is low and all these bonds are noncentrosymmetric, which can lead to additional contributions to electric polarization, besides the predictions of the original spin-current model.

Thereby, when we describe a magnetoelectric phenomena, we need to take into consideration that the LS-bond does not work and the magnetic moments of transition and rare-earth metal ions are not collinear to the mechanical moments. This feature of magnetoelectric materials should be taken into account in the development of the spin-current model. We use the tensor nature of the gyromagnetic ratio (g-factor) in the construction of the many-particle interaction Hamiltonian (
1
).

III
The justification of the spin-current model

III.1
The momentum balance equation

In this paper we aim to find the relation between the vector of the macroscopic polarization of the medium
P^{\alpha}
and the tensor of the effective spin current density
J_{s}^{\mu\nu}
for the tensorial form of hyromagnetic ratio, thus generalizing and describing the spin-current model.
Within the many-particle quantum hydrodynamics method, the spin-current model can be derived from the equation of momentum balance, and the balance of forces in this equation. It was shown in Ref.
Andreev and Trukhanova 2024b
that the balance of forces in a crystal lattice leads to a dependence of the equilibrium polarization on the spin current density or on the spin-current model for the scalar isotropic g-factor. To generalize this result to the case of a tensor g-factor, a momentum balance equation should also be derived.
For this purpose, we introduce the microscopic definition of momentum density as the quantum-mechanical average of the momentum operator

\displaystyle p^{\alpha}(\bm{r},t)=\int\frac{1}{2m_{i}}\biggl(\psi^{\dagger}(R,t)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})\times\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\times\hat{p}^{\alpha}_{i}\psi(R,t)+h.c.\biggr)dR,

(3)

where
h.c.
is used to denote the Hermitian conjugate term. The momentum density (
III.1
) in the vicinity of a point
\bm{r}
in the physical space is determined as the quantum-mechanical average of the momentum density operator. We can further represent the microscopic density of all physical variables in the more compact form

\xi(\bm{r},t)=\int dR\sum_{i}^{N}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})\psi^{\dagger}(R,t)\hat{\xi}_{i}\psi(R,t).

(4)

In the next step, we have to take the derivative of the function (
III.1
) with respect to time and use the many-particle Pauli-Schrödinger equation with Hamiltonian (
1
). This derivative acts on the wave function
\psi(R,t)
in the integral so that we can replace the partial time derivatives of the wave functions with the action of the Hamiltonian operator

\partial_{t}p^{\alpha}=\biggl\langle\frac{i}{2\hbar m_{i}}\biggl((\hat{H}\psi)^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{i}^{\alpha}\psi-\psi^{\dagger}\hat{p}^{\alpha}_{i}(\hat{H}\psi)+h.c.\biggr)\biggr\rangle.

(5)

After the set of calculations, following the method of many-particle quantum hydrodynamics, we obtain the corresponding momentum balance equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p^{\alpha}=g_{ou}\bm{S}\cdot(\partial^{\alpha}\bm{S})+\frac{1}{m}\bm{P}\cdot(\partial^{\alpha}\bm{E})\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma^{\mu\nu}}{mc}(\partial^{\alpha}E^{\beta})\varepsilon^{\beta\delta\mu}J^{\nu\delta}_{s}+\frac{\gamma^{\mu\nu}}{m}S^{\nu}(\partial^{\alpha}B^{\mu})+F^{\alpha}_{DM}.

(6)

Let us discuss each term on the right side of the momentum balance equation (
III.1
). The first force field density on the right side of this equation is caused by the symmetric exchange interaction modeled by the Heisenberg Hamiltonian and does not follow from the relativistic effect of spin-orbital coupling, where
g_{ou}=\int U(\xi)d\bm{\xi}
is the constant of the exchange interaction, which arises as an integral characteristic of the interaction of pairs of neighboring magnetic ions. The third term follows from the spin-orbit coupling and is proportional to the product of the spatial derivative of an electric field on the spin current density tensor
J_{s}^{\nu\delta}
. The second force field density on the right side characterizes the influence of a spatially non-uniform electric field on the electric dipole moments. The fourth force field density follows from the Zeeman energy of magnetic ions in a magnetic field. The coefficient of proportionality is determined by the gyromagnetic tensor
\gamma^{\mu\nu}
. And the last term represents the force density of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction. The microscopic definition of the spin current tensor in Eq. (
III.1
) can be derived in the form

J_{s}^{\alpha\beta}=\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2m_{i}}\biggl(\psi^{\dagger}(R,t)\hat{S}^{\alpha}_{i}\hat{p}^{\beta}_{i}\psi(R,t)+h.c.\biggr)\biggr\rangle.

(7)

In crystals, there are no particle flows, and the ions are localized in the nodes of the crystal lattice and oscillate around these nodes. However, we are interested in the description of the stationary state
\partial_{t}\bm{p}=0
, since it is important to obtain the relation between the spin current and the polarization. As we can see from Eq. (
III.1
), this state is realized in the presence of spatially inhomogeneous fields that can be created by external sources, as well as electric dipoles and charges inside the crystal structure. As we can see, the balance of the third and second force densities gives us polarization, which is defined by the effective spin current tensor

P^{\alpha}(\bm{r},t)=\frac{\gamma^{\mu\nu}}{c}\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\delta}J^{\nu\delta}_{s}(\bm{r},t).

(8)

This dependence defines the relation between macroscopic electric polarization and spin currents densities as a result of spin-orbit coupling in an internal electric field of the crystal lattice. As will be shown below, effective spin currents are a consequence of different types of interactions between lattice ions. The expression for electric polarization (
8
) generalizes the spin-current model and can describe the orientation of the induced electric polarization for various incommensurate magnetic states with tensor g-factor.

III.2
Derivation of the structure of spin currents

After we have obtained the dependence of the electric polarization on the spin current density tensor (
8
), it is necessary to reconstruct the structure of the macroscopic electric polarization depends on the spin density, and interrelate this dependence with the type of interparticle interactions that cause it. For this purpose, we need to derive the spin density balance equation and define the spin current structures. We introduce the spin density vector in the vicinity of a point in the physical space as the quantum average of the spin density operator
\sum_{i}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})\hat{s}^{\alpha}_{i}

\displaystyle S^{\alpha}(\bm{r},t)=\int dR\sum_{i}^{N}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})\psi^{\dagger}(R,t)\hat{s}^{\alpha}_{i}\psi(R,t).

(9)

The time derivative acts on the wave function
\psi(R,t)
, while the time derivative of the wave function is replaced with the Hamiltonian (
1
) in accordance with the Pauli equation
\partial_{t}S^{\alpha}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\int\sum_{i}^{N}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})\psi^{\dagger}(R,t)[\hat{H},\hat{s}_{i}^{\alpha}]\psi(R,t)dR.
After a series of calculations, the final equation of spin density evolution is obtained in the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S^{\alpha}+\partial_{\mu}J_{s}^{\alpha\mu}=\frac{2\gamma^{\mu\beta}}{\hbar}\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}S^{\beta}B^{\nu}\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}g_{u}S^{\mu}\triangle S^{\nu}+T^{\alpha}_{DM},

(10)

where the density of the moment of the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya force depends on the spin density and can be derived in the form

T^{\alpha}_{DM}=g_{\beta}\delta^{\nu}\biggl(\varepsilon^{\mu\lambda\nu}S^{\mu}\partial^{\lambda}S^{\alpha}-\varepsilon^{\alpha\lambda\nu}S^{\beta}\partial^{\lambda}S^{\beta}\biggr),

(11)

here we introduce the exchange interaction coefficient
g_{\beta}=\frac{1}{3}\int\xi^{2}\beta(\xi)d\bm{\xi}
.
The physical mechanism of such an interaction is as follows: the exchange interaction between magnetic ions through the non-magnetic oxygen ion between them contributes to the spin-orbit coupling. If the spins of magnetic ions are non-collinear, then distortion of the lattice with a shift of oxygen ions perpendicular to the bond of magnetic ions is advantageous for gaining energy. The rotational symmetry of this bond is broken and the Dzyaloshinskii vector becomes non-zero
D^{\alpha}_{ij}
. For multiferroics such as perovskite oxides of rare-earth manganites, magnetic transition metal ions interact indirectly via the oxygen ion between them as a result of the overlap of the electron clouds of the oxygen p-orbitals and the d-orbitals of the magnetic ion. This mechanism leads to the following structure of the Dzyaloshinskii vector coefficient

D^{\alpha}_{ij}(r_{ij})=\beta(r_{ij})\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}r^{\mu}_{ij}\delta^{\nu},

(12)

where the proportionality coefficient
\beta
depends on the relative distance between the magnetic ions (or clusters)
\bm{r}_{ij}
and decreases rapidly with increasing distance in such a way that the interaction occurs only with the neighboring ions. At the same time we assume that the oxygen ion in each cluster of three ions is displaced by the same distance in one direction
\bm{\delta}_{i}=\bm{\delta}
.

We can express the torque for the exchange interactions in terms of the spatial derivative of the spin current density tensor by representing the spin density balance equation in the form
\partial_{t}S^{\alpha}+\partial_{\mu}J^{\alpha\mu}_{s}=0.
By doing this for the antisymmetric exchange interaction of Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya we extract the divergence of the spin current density tensor
J^{\alpha\mu}_{DM}
from the expression for the torque

T^{\alpha}_{DM}=-\partial_{\mu}J^{\alpha\mu}_{DM}+g_{\beta}\delta^{\nu}\varepsilon^{\mu\lambda\nu}S^{\mu}\partial^{\lambda}S^{\alpha},

(13)

where the tensor of the spin current density has the form

J^{\alpha\mu}_{DM}=\frac{g_{\beta}}{2}\delta^{\nu}\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}\bm{S}^{2}.

(14)

As we can see from the expression (
14
), this spin current takes the maximum value for collinear spins and appears even in the case of a uniform distribution of spin density, and being proportional to its square. Moreover, the spin current density is proportional to the shift
\delta^{\nu}
of the non-magnetic oxygen ion from the middle point between two magnetic ions in each cluster.

The first term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
III.2
) represents the density of the torque that acts from the magnetic field, and in essence, this torque is part of the standard Landau-Lifshitz equation. The second torque density follows from the influence of the symmetric exchange interactions, which are modeled by the Heisenberg Hamiltonian, where
q_{u}=\frac{1}{6}\int\xi^{2}U(\xi)d\bm{\xi}
is the second constant of the exchange interaction, which is the ”second moment” of the exchange integral, while the constant
g_{ou}
can be called the ”zero moment” of the exchange integral. Thus, the second term on the right-hand side of (
III.2
) can be represented as the divergence of the spin current tensor

\displaystyle g_{u}\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}S^{\mu}\triangle S^{\nu}=\partial_{\delta}\biggl(g_{u}\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}S^{\mu}\partial^{\delta}S^{\nu}\biggr),\qquad

\displaystyle J^{\alpha\delta}_{ex}=-g_{u}\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}S^{\mu}\partial^{\delta}S^{\nu}.

(15)

We have described the spin-current model and obtained the general expression for the macroscopic electric polarization that is caused by different types of interaction between the magnetic ions.

VI
The electric polarization caused by the Keffer-like form of the symmetric Heisenberg exchange interaction

VI.1
The isotropic case

For symmetric exchange interaction and antisymmetric Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction, the spin-current model has been applied to describe the induced polarization in ferromagnetic spin structures. The symmetric Heisenberg exchange we considered does not require displacement of the diamagnetic oxygen ion, while the antisymmetric Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya exchange interaction is realized solely through displacement of the nonmagnetic oxygen ion relative to the bond between two magnetic ions in the cluster. The obtained results can also be generalized to antiferromagnetic spin order. In this section, we consider the symmetric indirect exchange interaction, which occurs through an intermediate diamagnetic oxygen ion. VII
Conclusion

In this paper a mechanism of the magnetoelectric effect, based on the effective spin currents, caused by different types of interactions, has been theoretically developed. We have described and justified the spin-current model of Katsura, Nagaosa, and Balatsky (KNB) when taking into account the tensor gyromagnetic ratio (or g-factor), using the basic principles of the many-particle quantum hydrodynamics method. Our description is based on the idea of a cluster model with two magnetic positively charged ions with the diamagnetic negatively charged oxygen atom
O^{2-}
between them, where each cluster is considered as a point-like quasi-particle in the many-particle description of quantum hydrodynamics. However, in contrast to approach of KNB, the oxygen ions can shift from the middle point of the magnetic ions bond. Using the results of the investigated model, we have considered main mechanisms of ferroelectricity of spin origin. The first one, is the symmetric exchange Heisenberg interaction. In the spin current, caused by this type of interaction, the displacement of oxygen ions is not explicitly taken into account for the occurrence of polarization, although it should not be zero for the antisymmetric Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction which leads to the oxygen ion shift and the formation of the canted spin structure. We demonstrate that the symmetric exchange coupling leads to the effective spin current, and this spin current produces the electric polarization in cycloidal and screw (helicoidal) spin systems. But, unlike the isotropic case (
16
), where the cycloidal spin order and the scalar g-factor give only one projection of electric polarization (
18
), the non-diagonal component of g-factor leads to the additional out-of-plane projection of electric polarization (
IV.1.2
), (
IV.1.2
). For the screw spin order, we have obtained non-zero components of electric polarization (
IV.2.2
), (
IV.2.2
), also caused by non- diagonal elements of gyromagnetic ratio, that is atypical for the isotropic case.

In the second mechanism electric polarization caused by the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction is determined by the displacement vector of the oxygen ion from the middle point of the bond and the square of the spin density. This means that this polarization can occur even in systems with collinear spins, in contrast to the spin-current model of KNB, where only a non-collinear structure is considered and electric polarization arises from the deformation of electronic density. Solutions have been also derived for the cycloidal and helicoidal spin orders (
30
) - (
32
). We have demonstrated results of the model for the ferromagnetic state, but its can be extended to the antiferromagnetic orders.

In addition, we have considered the new mechanism of electric polarization formation caused by the novel form of the spin-spin interaction related to the odd anisotropy of the symmetric exchange interaction. This is the indirect exchange that is carried out through the oxygen ion, that shifts from the middle point. We have derived the electric polarization for the isotropic scalar g-factor (
37
) and generalized the result on the case of tensor gyromagnetic ratio (
44
