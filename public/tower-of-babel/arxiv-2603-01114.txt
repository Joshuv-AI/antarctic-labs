1.
Introduction

For the necessary definitions and notations, see Section
2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\omega
and
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. A sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in A}
in a topological space
X
is said to be
\mathcal{I}
-convergent, if there is
x\in X
such that
\{n\in A:x_{n}\notin U\}\in\mathcal{I}
for every open neighborhood
U
of
x
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\omega
. A topological space
X
has the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
property if for every sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}
in
X
there is
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
(x_{n})_{n\in A}
is
\mathcal{I}
-convergent. In this case we briefly write
X\in\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
(however, formally
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
is a class of topological spaces, not a set).

Obviously,
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})
is the class of sequentially compact spaces (
\mathrm{Fin}
denotes the ideal consisting of all finite subsets of
\omega
). For this reason, the classes
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
can be viewed as generalizations of sequential compactness. The aim of this paper is to study inclusions between the classes
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
.

The case of
[0,1]
is of particular interest, since the famous Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem states that the space
[0,1]
is sequentially compact, i.e., that
[0,1]\in\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})
. Deep studies of ideals satisfying
[0,1]\in\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
have been performed by Filipów, Mrożek, Recław and Szuca in
[
6
]
, where it is shown, among others, that
[0,1]\in\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
for every
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
ideal
\mathcal{I}
. However, there are
\mathbf{F_{\sigma\delta}}
ideals
\mathcal{I}
with
[0,1]\notin\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
(see
[
15
]
).

The classes
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
have been studied previously. Below we present a short overview of the known results. Bernstein showed in
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
that every compact space has the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
property for every maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
. In particular, for maximal ideals
\mathcal{I}
it is possible to find a space with the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
property that is not sequentially compact. If
\mathcal{I}
is an
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
ideal then the class
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
includes all compact metric spaces, all compact linearly ordered topological spaces and every limit ordinal of uncountable cofinality considered with the order topology (
[
11
, Corollary 5.7]
; see also
[
12
, theorem 1.1]
).

There is also another way of generalizing sequential compactness with the aid of ideals on
\omega
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\omega
. A topological space
X
has the
\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{I})
property if for every sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of elements of
X
there is
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
(x_{n})_{n\in A}
is convergent (in the usual sense).

In contrast to
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
, each
\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{I})
is a subclass of sequentially compact spaces. Studies of such spaces have been initiated by Kojman in
[
20
]
, where he considered the class
\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{W})
given by the van der Waerden ideal
\mathcal{W}
consisting all subsets of
\omega
that do not contain arbitrary long finite arithmetic progressions. Later Kojman and Shelah in
[
19
]
performed under CH the first construction of a Mrówka space with the
\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{W})
property. This construction inspired a long list of papers devoted to studies of the classes
\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{I})
– see for instance
[
13
,
7
,
12
,
14
,
17
,
22
,
24
]
.

It is known that in general
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}
implies
\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{I})
, where
\leq_{K}
denotes the Katětov order on ideals (see
[
24
, Corollary 10.2]
; for the definition of
\leq_{K}
see Section
2
). Recently, the article
[
24
]
established a deeper connection of the classes
\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{I})
with the Katětov order. Namely, under CH the following holds for all
\mathbf{G_{\delta\sigma\delta}}
ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
:

\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}\ \iff\ \mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{I}).

The space constructed in the proof (in order to show the implication
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}\ \implies\ \mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{J})\not\subseteq\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{I})
) is based on ideas developed by Kojman and Shelah in
[
19
]
.

Inspired by the above result, in this paper we introduce a new order on ideals, denoted
\leq_{BW}
, show that in general
\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}
implies
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
(see Theorem
3.3
), while in the realm of
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
the following holds under CH:

\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}\ \iff\ \mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})

(see Corollary
5.3
). It is worth mentioning that the space constructed in our proof (showing that
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}\ \implies\ \mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})\not\subseteq\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
) is completely different from the ones based on Kojman’s and Shelah’s proof from
[
19
]
.

Additionally, we examine basic properties of
\leq_{BW}
(cf. Section
3
), compare it with the Katětov order (Proposition
3.2
(d) and Remark
4.6
) and study the relation
\leq_{BW}
among some well-known ideals (Section
4
). In particular, we answer two open questions posed by Filipów and Tryba in
[
12
, Questions 2 and 3]
by proving that
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{W})
is a subclass of sequentially compact spaces, while under CH there is a sequentially compact space without the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{W})
property (see Corollaries
4.2
(a) and
5.4
(a)).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basics about ideals

A family
\mathcal{I}
of subsets of a set
M
is called an ideal on
M
if it is closed under taking subsets and finite unions of its elements. Additionally, we assume that ideals are proper (
\neq\mathcal{P}(M)
) and that all finite subsets of
M
belong to
\mathcal{I}
. Note that
M=\bigcup\mathcal{I}
. In this paper we only consider ideals on countable sets.

For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
we denote by
\mathcal{I}^{+}=\{A\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{I}:A\notin\mathcal{I}\}
the family of all
\mathcal{I}
-positive sets. If
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, then the restriction of
\mathcal{I}
to
A
is given by
\mathcal{I}|A=\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{P}(A)
. It is an ideal on
A
.

An ideal on a set
M
is called Borel (
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
, etc.) if it is a Borel (
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
, etc., respectively) subset of
\mathcal{P}(M)
with the topology induced from
2^{M}
by identifying subsets of
M
with their characteristic functions (
2^{M}=\{0,1\}^{M}
is considered with the product topology, where each space
2=\{0,1\}
carries the discrete topology). The simplest example of an ideal is
\mathrm{Fin}
, which is equal to the family of all finite subsets of
\omega
. This ideal is
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
. By a result of Sierpiński, there are no
\mathbf{G_{\delta}}
ideals.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be ideals. We say that:

•

\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are isomorphic (written
\mathcal{I}\cong\mathcal{J}
) if there is a bijection
f:\bigcup\mathcal{J}\to\bigcup\mathcal{I}
such that:

f^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal{J}\iff B\in\mathcal{I}

for all
B\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{I}
;

•

\mathcal{I}
is below
\mathcal{J}
in the Katětov order (written
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}
) if there is an arbitrary function
f:\bigcup\mathcal{J}\to\bigcup\mathcal{I}
such that
f[A]\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
for all
A\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{I}
is Katětov below
\mathcal{J}
if there is a function
f:\bigcup\mathcal{J}\to\bigcup\mathcal{I}
such that
f^{-1}[A]\in\mathcal{J}
for all
A\in\mathcal{I}
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is homogeneous if
\mathcal{I}|A\cong\mathcal{I}
for all
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.

All considered in this paper notions concerning ideals are invariant over isomorphisms. Therefore one can think about all ideals on countable sets as ideals on
\omega
by identifying the domain set of the ideal with
\omega
via a fixed bijection.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is tall if for every infinite
A\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{I}
there is an infinite
B\subseteq A
with
B\in\mathcal{I}
. It is not difficult to see that
\mathcal{I}
is tall if and only if
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{K}\mathrm{Fin}
. We say that an ideal
\mathcal{I}
is nowhere tall if
\mathcal{I}|A
is not tall, for every
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is maximal if there is no other ideal
\mathcal{J}
on
\bigcup\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{J}\supsetneq\mathcal{I}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{I}
is maximal if for each
A\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{I}
either
A\in\mathcal{I}
or
\bigcup\mathcal{I}\setminus A\in\mathcal{I}
. We say that an ideal
\mathcal{I}
is nowhere maximal if
\mathcal{I}|A
is not maximal, for every
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. It is known that maximal ideals do not have the Baire property and are non-measurable (see
[
28
]
). In particular, every Borel ideal is nowhere maximal (since restrictions of Borel ideals remain Borel).

2.2.
Examples of ideals

In the sequel, we will prove results about the following ideals:

•

The summable ideal is given by

\mathcal{I}_{1/n}=\left\{A\subseteq\omega:\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n+1}<\infty\right\}.

More generally, if
f:\omega\to[0,\infty)
is such that
\sum_{n\in\omega}f(n)=\infty
, then

\mathcal{I}_{f}=\left\{A\subseteq\omega:\sum_{n\in A}f(n)<\infty\right\}

is called a summable ideal. All such ideals are
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
.

•

The ideal
\mathrm{NWD}
consisting of all
A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
that are nowhere dense (in the topology inherited from
\mathbb{R}
) is
\mathbf{F_{\sigma\delta}}
.

•

The van der Waerden’s ideal is defined by

\mathcal{W}=\{A\subseteq\omega:\exists_{n\in\omega}\textrm{ }A\textrm{ does not contain a finite arithmetic progression of length n}\}.

It is an ideal by the van der Waerden’s Theorem (see
[
29
]
).
\mathcal{W}
is
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
.

Each of the above ideals is tall.

For
A\subseteq M\times N
and
m\in M
, write
A_{(m)}=\{n\in N:(m,n)\in A\}
, i.e.,
A_{(m)}
is
the vertical section of
A
at the point
m
. For two ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
on
M
and
N
, respectively, define:

•

\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J}=\{(A\times\{0\})\cup(B\times\{1\}):A\in\mathcal{I}\text{ and }B\in\mathcal{J}\}
. This is a proper ideal on
(M\times\{0\})\cup(N\times\{1\})
even if only one of
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
is proper. Hence, we allow
\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{P}(N)
and
\mathcal{P}(M)\oplus\mathcal{J}
.

•

\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{J}=\left\{A\subseteq M\times N:\{m\in M:A_{(m)}\notin\mathcal{J}\}\in\mathcal{I}\right\}
. This is a proper ideal even if one of
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
is equal to
\{\emptyset\}
. Hence, we allow
\mathcal{I}\otimes\{\emptyset\}
and
\{\emptyset\}\otimes\mathcal{J}
.

A particular case of the latter is
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}=\mathrm{Fin}\otimes\mathrm{Fin}
, which is an
\mathbf{F_{\sigma\delta\sigma}}
ideal on
\omega^{2}
.

A map
\mu:\mathcal{P}(\omega)\to[0,\infty]
is a submeasure on
\omega
provided that
\mu(\emptyset)=0
,
\mu(\{n\})<\infty
for all
n\in\omega
and:

\forall_{A,B\subseteq\omega}\ \mu(A)\leq\mu(A\cup B)\leq\mu(A)+\mu(B)\text{.}

It is lower semicontinuous if additionally:

\forall_{A\subseteq\omega}\ \mu(A)=\lim_{n\in\omega}\mu(A\cap n)\text{.}

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
is called a generalized density ideal if there is a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of lower semicontinuous submeasures on
\omega
and a sequence of pairwise disjoint finite sets
(I_{n})_{n\in\omega}
such that:

\mathcal{I}=\left\{A\subseteq\omega:\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{n}(A\cap I_{n})=0\right\}\text{.}

All such ideals are
\mathbf{F_{\sigma\delta}}
. The best known member of this class is the ideal of sets of asymptotic density zero:

\mathcal{I}_{d}=\left\{A\subseteq\omega:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap n|}{n}=0\right\}\text{.}

Generalized density ideals were introduced by Farah in
[
4
, Section 2.10]
(see also
[
5
]
) and later used in different contexts (see for instance
[
2
,
6
,
25
,
26
]
).

2.3.
P-like properties of ideals

For any set
M
and two its subsets
A,B\subseteq M
write
A\subseteq^{\star}B
if
A\setminus B
is finite. We say that an ideal
\mathcal{I}
is:

•

P^{+}
if for every
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of elements of
\mathcal{I}^{+}
there is
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
A\subseteq^{\star}A_{n}
for all
n
,

•

\widehat{P}^{+}
if for every
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of elements of
\mathcal{I}^{+}
there is
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
A\setminus A_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for all
n
.

Note that sometimes
\widehat{P}^{+}
ideals are called
P^{+}(\mathcal{I})
ideals (see
[
8
]
). By
[
18
, Lemma 1.2]
), every
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
ideal is
P^{+}
. It is clear that
P^{+}
implies
\widehat{P}^{+}
.

Following
[
6
]
, we say that an ideal
\mathcal{I}
has the:

•

\mathrm{hFinBW}
property, if
[0,1]\in\mathrm{FinBW}(\mathcal{I}|A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
;

•

\mathrm{hBW}
property, if
[0,1]\in\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I}|A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.

Obviously, the
\mathrm{hFinBW}
property implies the
\mathrm{hBW}
property. If
\mathcal{I}
is a
\widehat{P}^{+}
ideal, then
\mathcal{I}
has the
\mathrm{hBW}
property (by
[
6
, Proposition 3.5]
; see also
[
21
, Proposition 2.13]
for an example of a non-
\widehat{P}^{+}
ideal with the
\mathrm{hBW}
property). It is also known that all
P^{+}
ideals (so also all
\bf{F_{\sigma}}
ideals) have the
\mathrm{hFinBW}
property (by
[
10
, Proposition 2.8]
, see also
[
6
, Proposition 3.4]
). We will need the following characterization (see
[
6
, Proposition 3.3]
and
[
10
, Proposition 2.9]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
has the hBW property if and only if for every set
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
and every family
\{A_{s}:s\in 2^{<\omega}\}
of subsets of
A
such that:

(1)

A_{\emptyset}=A
;

(2)

A_{s}=A_{s^{\frown}(0)}\cup A_{s^{\frown}(1)}
for all
s\in 2^{<\omega}
;

(3)

A_{s^{\frown}(0)}\cap A_{s^{\frown}(1)}=\emptyset
for all
s\in 2^{<\omega}
;

there exist
x\in 2^{\omega}
and
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
B\setminus A_{x\restriction n}\in\mathcal{I}
for each
n\in\omega
.

3.
A new preorder for ideals

In this section we introduce the relation
\leq_{BW}
, study its basic properties and compare it with the Katětov order.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be ideals. We write
\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}
if there is
f:\bigcup\mathcal{J}\to\bigcup\mathcal{I}
such that for every
A\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
there is
B\subseteq f[A]
,
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that given any
C
with
C\cap B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
we have
A\cap f^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
.

Next result summarizes some basic properties of
\leq_{BW}
. In Remark
4.6
we will see that the implication from Proposition
3.2
(d) cannot be reversed.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
,
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{K}
be ideals.

(a)

The relation
\leq_{BW}
is reflexive and transitive.

(b)

\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
.

(c)

\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{J}
.

(d)

If
\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}
, then
\mathcal{I}\leq_{K}\mathcal{J}
.

(e)

There is a
\leq_{BW}
-antichain of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
consisting of summable ideals.

(f)

The relation
\leq_{BW}
is invariant over isomorphisms of ideals, i.e. if
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{J}
, then

\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}\ \iff\ \mathcal{I}^{\prime}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}.

(g)

If
\mathcal{K}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{K}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}
then
\mathcal{K}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J}
.

Proof.

(a): Since reflexivity is obvious, we will only prove that
\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}
is transitive. Suppose that
f
witnesses
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal{J}
and
g
witnesses
\mathcal{J}\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal{K}
. We will show that
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}K
. Let us define function
h:\bigcup\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\bigcup\mathcal{I}
as
h(x)=f(g(x))
for any
x\in\bigcup\mathcal{K}
. Fix any
A\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
. By assumption we know that there is
B^{*}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
,
B^{*}\subseteq g[A]
such that given any
C
with
C\cap B^{*}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
we have
A\cap g^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
. Since
f
witnesses
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal{J}
, there is
B\subseteq f[B^{*}]
,
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that given any
C
with
C\cap B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
we have
B^{*}\cap f^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
. Then
B\subseteq f[g[A]]=h[A]
. Fix any
C
with
C\cap B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Then we have
B^{*}\cap f^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and that implies
A\cap h^{-1}[C]=A\cap g^{-1}[f^{-1}[C]]\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
.

(b): Without loss of generality we may assume that both
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are ideals on
\omega
.
Let
f:\omega\to\omega\times\{0,1\}
be such that
f(i)=(i,0)
for all
i\in\omega
. We will show that
f
is a function that witnesses
\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J}\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal{I}
. Fix any
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Then
f[A]=A\times\{0\}\in(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J})^{+}
. Let
B=f[A]
. Fix any
C
with
C\cap B\in(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J})^{+}
. Then
\mathcal{I}^{+}\ni\{i\in\omega:(i,0)\in B\cap C\}=A\cap\{i\in\omega:(i,0)\in C\}=A\cap f^{-1}[C]
.

(c): Without loss of generality we may assume that both
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are ideals on
\omega
.
Let
f:\omega^{2}\to\omega
be given by
f(i,j)=i
for all
(i,j)\in\omega^{2}
. We will show that
f
witnesses
\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{J}
. Fix any
A\in(\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{J})^{+}
. Then
B=\{i\in\omega:A_{(i)}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}\}
is
\mathcal{I}
-positive and contained in
f[A]
. Let
C\subseteq\omega
be such that
C\cap B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Then
A\cap f^{-1}[C]\supseteq\bigcup_{i\in C\cap B}(\{i\}\times A_{(i)})\in(\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{J})^{+}
(as
A_{(i)}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
for all
i\in C\cap B
).

(d): Straightforward.

(e): By
[
16
, Theorem 1]
(see also
[
27
, Corollary 3.6]
) there is a family
\{\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\}
consisting of summable ideals such that
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\not\leq_{K}\mathcal{I}_{\beta}
for all
\alpha,\beta<\mathfrak{c}
,
\alpha\neq\beta
. Thus, it suffices to apply item (d).

(f): Straightforward.

(g): Suppose that
f:\bigcup\mathcal{I}\to\bigcup\mathcal{K}
witnesses
\mathcal{K}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
and
g:\bigcup\mathcal{J}\to\bigcup\mathcal{K}
witnesses
\mathcal{K}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}
. Define the function
h:(\bigcup\mathcal{I}\times\{0\})\cup(\bigcup\mathcal{J}\times\{1\})\to\bigcup\mathcal{K}
by:

h(n,i)=\begin{cases}f(n),&\text{if }i=0,\cr g(n),&\text{if }i=1,\end{cases}

for all
(n,i)\in(\bigcup\mathcal{I}\times\{0\})\cup(\bigcup\mathcal{J}\times\{1\})
. Let
A\in(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J})^{+}
. Then either
\{n\in\bigcup\mathcal{I}:(n,0)\in A\}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
or
\{n\in\bigcup\mathcal{J}:(n,1)\in A\}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
. Without loss of generality we will assume that
A^{\prime}=\{n\in\bigcup\mathcal{I}:(n,0)\in A\}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Then there is
\mathcal{K}^{+}\ni B\subseteq f[A^{\prime}]\subseteq h[A]
such that for every
C
with
C\cap B\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
we have
A^{\prime}\cap f^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, which gives us
A\cap h^{-1}[C]\supseteq\{0\}\times(A^{\prime}\cap f^{-1}[C])\in(\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J})^{+}
. Hence,
h
is a witness for
\mathcal{K}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}\oplus\mathcal{J}
.
∎

Next result establishes a connection of
\leq_{BW}
with the classes
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
, which are of our main interest. As we will see in Example
4.4
, the below implication cannot be reversed in general. However, we will be able to reverse it under additional assumptions (see Theorem
5.1
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be ideals. If
\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}
, then
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that both
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are ideals on
\omega
. Suppose that a space
X
has the property
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})
. We will show that
X
has the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
property. Fix any sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\subseteq X
. By assumption of the theorem we know that there exists a function
f:\omega\rightarrow\omega
witnessing
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal{J}
. Then
(x_{f(n)})_{n\in\omega}
is also a sequence in
X
. Since
X\in\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})
, there is
A\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
x\in X
with
(x_{f(n)})_{n\in A}\overset{\mathcal{J}}{\longrightarrow}x
. Now, let
f[A]\supseteq B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
be the set from the definition of
\mathcal{I}\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal{J}
. To show that
(x_{n})_{n\in B}\overset{\mathcal{I}}{\longrightarrow}x
we need to fix an open neighborhood
U
of
x
. Define
C=\{n\in B:x_{n}\notin U\}
. Then

A\cap f^{-1}[C]=A\cap\{n\in f^{-1}[B]:x_{f(n)}\notin U\}\subseteq\{n\in A:x_{f(n)}\notin U\}\in\mathcal{J}.

By the choice of
B
, we have
\{n\in B:x_{n}\notin U\}=C\cap B\in\mathcal{I}
. Hence,
(x_{n})_{n\in B}\overset{\mathcal{I}}{\longrightarrow}x
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal.

(a)

\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathrm{Fin}
if and only if
\mathcal{I}
is not tall. In particular, if
\mathcal{I}
is not tall, then
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
contains all sequentially compact spaces.

(b)

If
\mathcal{I}
is nowhere tall, then
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathrm{Fin}
are
\leq_{BW}
-equivalent. In particular, if
\mathcal{I}
is nowhere tall, then
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
is the class of all sequentially compact spaces.

Proof.

(a): The implication
\impliedby
follows from Proposition
3.2
(b), since any non-tall ideal is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Fin}\oplus\mathcal{J}
for some ideal
\mathcal{J}
. On the other hand, the implication
\implies
follows from Proposition
3.2
(d), since
\mathcal{I}\not\leq_{K}\mathrm{Fin}
for tall ideals
\mathcal{I}
. The second part follows from Theorem
3.3
and the fact that
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})
is the class of all sequentially compact spaces.

(b): Let
\mathcal{I}
be nowhere tall. Then
\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathrm{Fin}
by item (a). We will show that
\mathrm{Fin}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
. The second part will then follow from Theorem
3.3
and the fact that
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})
is the class of all sequentially compact spaces.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal on
\omega
. Let
f:\omega\to\omega
be the identity function. Fix
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Since
\mathcal{I}
is nowhere tall, we can find an infinite
B\subseteq A
such that
\mathcal{I}|B=\mathrm{Fin}|B
. Then
B=f[B]\subseteq f[A]
and
B\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
. Fix
C
such that
C\cap B\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
and observe that then
A\cap f^{-1}[C]=A\cap C\supseteq B\cap C\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.
∎

In Remark
4.8
we discuss possible strengthenings of Proposition
3.4
(b).

4.
The new preorder among some well-known ideals

In this section we examine
\leq_{BW}
among some of the ideals introduced in Section
2
. Firstly, we give examples of ideals that are
\leq_{BW}
-above
\mathrm{Fin}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

(a)

\mathrm{Fin}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{W}
.

(b)

\mathrm{Fin}\leq_{BW}\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
.

(c)

\mathrm{Fin}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
, for every generalized density ideal
\mathcal{I}
. In particular,
\mathrm{Fin}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}_{d}
.

Proof.

(a): By
[
23
, Example 2.6]
,
\mathcal{W}
is homogeneous. Therefore, it is enough to show that there exists
E\in\mathcal{W}^{+}
such that
\mathrm{Fin}\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal{W}|E
(see Proposition
3.2
(f)). Let
E_{n}=\{10^{n},10^{n}+1,\ldots,10^{n}+n\}
for all
n\in\omega
and
E=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}E_{n}\in\mathcal{W}^{+}
. Now, let the function
f:E\rightarrow\omega
be such that:

f(i)=n\ \iff\ i\in E_{n}

for all
i\in E
. Fix
A\in(\mathcal{W}|E)^{+}
and for all
n\in\omega
take arithmetic progression
A_{n}
of length
n
such that
A_{n}\subseteq A
.

For all
n>2
take
k_{n}
such that
A_{n}\subseteq E_{k_{n}}
. This is possible. Indeed, suppose that there is
n\in\omega
for which
A_{n}\nsubseteq E_{i}
for all
i\in\omega
. Let
(a_{j})_{j<n}
be the increasing enumeration of
A_{n}
and
r>0
be the common difference of
A_{n}
. We can find the smallest
k
for which there are
l_{1},l_{2}\in\omega
such that
l_{1}<l_{2}
,
a_{k}\in E_{l_{1}}
and
a_{k+1}\in E_{l_{2}}
. Then
r\geq 10^{l_{2}}-10^{l_{1}}-l_{1}\geq 10^{l_{1}+1}-10^{l_{1}}-l_{1}\geq 10^{l_{1}}+l_{1}
. Hence,
k=1
. There are two possible cases:

•

If
a_{k+2}=a_{3}\in E_{l_{2}}
, we have
a_{3}-a_{2}\leq 10^{l_{2}}+l_{2}-10^{l_{2}}=l_{2}
and
a_{2}-a_{1}\geq 10^{l_{2}}-10^{l_{1}}-l_{1}>10^{l_{2}-1}\geq l_{2}
. A contradiction with the fact that
A_{n}
is an arithmetic progression.

•

Now we have to consider the case in which
a_{3}\notin E_{l_{2}}
. Then
a_{3}-a_{2}\geq 10^{l_{2}+1}-10^{l_{2}}-l_{2}>10^{l_{2}}+l_{2}
and
a_{2}-a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq 10^{l_{2}}+l_{2}
. Just like in the previous case, it leads us to a contradiction with the fact that
A_{n}
is an arithmetic progression.

Therefore, for every
n>2
it is possible to find
k_{n}
such that
A_{n}\subseteq E_{k_{n}}
.

Define
B=\{k_{i}:i>2\}
. Clearly, such
B
is infinite and contained in
f[A]
. Take any
C\subseteq\omega
with
C\cap B\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
. Then
f^{-1}[C]\supseteq\bigcup\{E_{k_{n}}:k_{n}\in C\cap B\}
and hence
f^{-1}[C]\cap A\supseteq\bigcup\{A_{n}:k_{n}\in C\cap B\}\in(\mathcal{W}|E)^{+}
.

(b): Follows from Proposition
3.2
(c).

(c): If
\mathcal{I}
is a generalized density ideal, there is a sequence of lower semicontinuous submeasures
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\omega}
and a sequence of pairwise disjoint finite sets
(I_{n})_{n\in\omega}
such that
\mathcal{I}=\{A\subseteq\omega:\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{n}(A\cap I_{n})=0\}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}I_{n}=\omega
(by adding, if necessary, to the sequences
(I_{n})_{n\in\omega}
and
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\omega}
new elements
I^{\prime}_{b}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{b}
, for
b\in\omega\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\omega}I_{n}
, such that
I^{\prime}_{b}=\{b\}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{b}(A)=0
for all
A\subseteq\omega
). Let
f:\omega\rightarrow\omega
be given by

f(k)=n\ \iff\ k\in I_{n}.

We will prove that such function
f
witnesses
\mathrm{Fin}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
. Fix any
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{n}(A\cap I_{n})\neq 0
. Hence, there exist
k\in\omega
and an increasing sequence of natural numbers
(n_{i})_{i\in\omega}
satisfying
\mu_{n_{i}}(A\cap I_{n_{i}})>\frac{1}{k}
for all
i\in\omega
. Now, let
B=\{n_{i}:i\in\omega\}
. Clearly,
\mathrm{Fin}^{+}\ni B\subseteq f[A]
. Fix any
C\subseteq\omega
that satisfies
A\cap f^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{I}
. Consequently, there can be only finitely many
i\in\omega
for which
n_{i}\in C
. Therefore,
C\cap B
is finite.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

(a)

Every
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{W})
space is sequentially compact.

(b)

Every
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2})
space is sequentially compact.

(c)

If
\mathcal{I}
is a generalized density ideal, then every
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
space is sequentially compact. In particular, every
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I}_{d})
space is sequentially compact.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
3.3
, Proposition
4.1
and the fact that
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})
is the class of all sequentially compact spaces.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Filipów and Tryba in
[
12
, Question 3]
asked whether there is a
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{W})
space which is not sequentially compact. Corollary
4.2
(a) gives a negative answer.

Next example shows that the implication from Theorem
3.3
(i.e.,
\mathcal{I}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{J}\ \implies\ \mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
) cannot be reversed in general.

Example 4.4
.

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{BW}\mathrm{Fin}
, but
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})=\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2})
.

Proof.

\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{BW}\mathrm{Fin}
follows from Proposition
3.2
(d) and the fact that
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}\not\leq_{K}\mathrm{Fin}
(as
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
is a tall ideal). The inclusion
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})\supseteq\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2})
is proved in Corollary
4.2
(b). Thus, we only need to show that
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})\subseteq\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin}^{2})
.

Let
X
be a sequentially compact space and fix any sequence
f:\omega^{2}\to X
. Then for each
n\in\omega
we can find
M_{n}\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
and
x_{n}\in X
such that
(f(n,i))_{i\in M_{n}}
converges to
x_{n}
. Moreover, there are
M\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
and
x\in X
such that
(x_{n})_{n\in M}
converges to
x
. Define
A=\bigcup_{n\in M}\{n\}\times M_{n}
. Then
A\in(\mathrm{Fin}^{2})^{+}
. We will show that
(f(i,j))_{(i,j)\in A}
is
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}
-convergent to
x
.

Let
U
be an open neighborhood of
x
. Then there is
K\in\mathrm{Fin}
such that
x_{n}\in U
for all
n\in M\setminus K
, i.e.,
U
is an open neighborhood of each
x_{n}
with
n\in M\setminus K
. Thus, for each
n\in M\setminus K
there is
F_{n}\in\mathrm{Fin}
such that
f(n,i)\in U
for all
i\in M_{n}\setminus F_{n}
. Observe that

B=(K\times\omega)\cup\bigcup_{n\in M\setminus K}(\{n\}\times F_{n})\in\mathrm{Fin}^{2}

and
f(n,i)\in U
for all
(n.i)\in A\setminus B
.
∎

Now we give examples of ideals that are not
\leq_{BW}
-above
\mathrm{Fin}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

(a)

\mathrm{Fin}\not\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}_{1/n}
.

(b)

\mathrm{Fin}\not\leq_{BW}\mathcal{ED}_{-}
, where
\mathcal{ED}_{-}=\{A\subseteq\omega^{2}:\exists_{k\in\omega}\ \forall_{n\in\omega}\ |A_{(n)}|\leq k\}
.

(c)

\mathrm{Fin}\not\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
for every maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

(d)

\mathrm{Fin}\not\leq_{BW}\mathrm{NWD}
.

Proof.

(a): Take any function
f:\omega\rightarrow\omega
and denote
S_{n}=f^{-1}[\{n\}]
for all
n\in\omega
. In case there is
n\in\omega
with
S_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{1/n}^{+}
, the set
S_{n}
is the one we need (as there is no
\mathrm{Fin}^{+}\ni B\subseteq\{n\}=f[S_{n}]
). Let us now consider the case when all
S_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{1/n}
. Define numbers
p_{n}=\sum_{i\in S_{n}}\frac{1}{i+1}<\infty
for all
n\in\omega
. We will consider two cases.

Case 1:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}p_{n}=0
. Let
A=\omega
. Fix any
\mathrm{Fin}^{+}\ni B\subseteq f[A]
. Now, inductively for each
n\in\omega
take
c_{n}\in B\setminus\{c_{i}:i<n\}
such that
p_{c_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}
. Define
C=\{c_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. For that
C
it holds that
C\cap B=C\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
and
A\cap f^{-1}[C]=f^{-1}[C]=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{c_{n}}\in\mathcal{I}_{1/n}
.

Case 2:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}p_{n}\neq 0
. Then we can find a set
Z\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
and
p>0
such that
p_{z}>p
for all
z\in Z
. Let
(z_{n})_{n\in\omega}
be the increasing enumeration of
Z
. Take
k\in\omega
such that
\frac{1}{k+1}<p
. Observe that it is possible to find a set
A\subseteq\bigcup_{n\geq k}S_{z_{n}}
such that

r_{n}=\sum_{i\in S_{z_{n}}\cap A}\frac{1}{i+1}\geq\frac{1}{n+1}

for all
n\geq k
and moreover
\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}=0
. Clearly, such
A
belongs to
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{1/n}
. Now, fix
\mathrm{Fin}^{+}\ni B\subseteq f[A]\subseteq Z
. Similarly as in the previous case, inductively for each
n\in\omega
take
c_{n}\in B\setminus\{c_{i}:i<n\}
such that

\sum_{i\in S_{c_{n}}\cap A}\frac{1}{i+1}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\text{.}

Define
C=\{c_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. Then
C\cap B=C\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
and
f^{-1}[C]\cap A=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_{c_{n}}\cap A\in\mathcal{I}_{1/n}
.

(b): Fix any
f:\omega^{2}\to\omega
. If
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\notin\mathcal{ED}_{-}
for some
n\in\omega
, then put
A=f^{-1}[\{n\}]
and observe that there is no
B\subseteq f[A]=\{n\}
with
B\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
, so there is nothing to check. Thus, we can assume from now on that
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\in\mathcal{ED}_{-}
for all
n\in\omega
.

Inductively pick
k^{n}_{i}\in\omega
for all
n\in\omega
and
i\leq n
such that
f(n,k^{n}_{i})\neq f(m,k^{m}_{j})
whenever
(n,i)\neq(m,j)
. Observe that this is possible. Indeed, given
n\in\omega
and
i\leq n
, the set

E^{n}_{i}=\{f(m,k^{m}_{j}):m<n,j\leq m\}\cup\{f(n,k^{n}_{j}):j<i\}

is finite, so
f^{-1}[E^{n}_{i}]\in\mathcal{ED}_{-}
, which gives that
(\{n\}\times\omega)\setminus f^{-1}[E^{n}_{i}]\neq\emptyset
.

Define
A=\{(n,k^{n}_{i}):n\in\omega,i\leq n\}
. Then
A\in(\mathcal{ED}_{-})^{+}
as
|A_{(n)}|=n+1
for all
n\in\omega
. Fix any infinite
B\subseteq f[A]
. Since the sets
\{f(n,k^{n}_{i}):i\leq n\}
are finite, we can find an infinite
C\subseteq B
such that
|C\cap\{f(n,k^{n}_{i}):i\leq n\}|\leq 1
for all
n\in\omega
. Then
B\cap C=C\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
, but
A\cap f^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{ED}_{-}
, as
|(A\cap f^{-1}[C])_{(n)}|\leq 1
for all
n\in\omega
.

(c): Let
\mathcal{I}
be a maximal ideal and assume towards contradiction that
\mathrm{Fin}\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
. Then
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})\subseteq\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})
(by Theorem
3.3
). However,
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
contains all compact spaces (by
[
1
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.3]
; see also
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
) and there are compact spaces which are not sequentially compact (i.e., are not
\mathrm{BW}(\mathrm{Fin})
), a contradiction.

(d): Let
f:\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]\to\omega
be arbitrary. We need to find
A\in\mathrm{NWD}^{+}
such that for all
B\subseteq f[A]
,
B\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
there is an infinite
C\subseteq B
such that
A\cap f^{-1}[C]\in\mathrm{NWD}
.

If
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\in\mathrm{NWD}^{+}
for some
n\in\omega
, it suffices to put
A=f^{-1}[\{n\}]
. Thus, we can assume that
f^{-1}[\{n\}]\in\mathrm{NWD}
for all
n\in\omega
.

Let
\{U_{n}:n\in\omega\}
be an enumeration of all non-empty open sub-intervals of
[0,1]
with rational endpoints. Inductively find a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}
such that
x_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap U_{n}
and
f(x_{n})\neq f(x_{m})
for all
n,m\in\omega
,
n\neq m
. This is possible as given any
n\in\omega
, the set
f^{-1}[\{f(x_{m}):m<n\}]

is nowhere dense. Put
A=\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. Then
A\in\mathrm{NWD}^{+}
as it is dense in
[0,1]
.

Fix any
B\subseteq f[A]
,
B\in\mathrm{Fin}^{+}
. Then there is an infinite
D\subseteq\{n\in\omega:f(x_{n})\in B\}
such that
(x_{n})_{n\in D}
is convergent. Put
C=\{f(x_{n}):n\in D\}
. Then
C\subseteq B
,
C
is infinite and
A\cap f^{-1}[C]=\{x_{n}:n\in D\}\in\mathrm{NWD}
.
∎

5.
Main result

In this section, we prove our main result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume CH. Let
\mathcal{J}
be a nowhere maximal
P^{+}
ideal and
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal with the property
\mathrm{hBW}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{J}\not\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
,

(b)

there is a
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
space which is not
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})
,

(c)

there is a Hausdorff non-compact
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
space which is not
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})
.

Proof.

(c)
\implies
(b): Obvious.

(b)
\implies
(a): Follows from Theorem
3.3
.

(a)
\implies
(c): For every
n\in\omega
define
x_{n}\in 2^{\omega}
and
h_{n}:2^{\omega}\to 2
by:

x_{n}(i)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i\neq n,\cr 1,&\text{if }i=n,\end{cases}

and
h_{n}(x)=x(n)
for all
x\in 2^{\omega}
.

Let
\{f_{\alpha}:\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
be such an enumeration of the family
\{f\in\omega^{\omega}_{1}:\forall_{\beta<\omega_{1}}\ f^{-1}[\{\beta\}]\in\mathcal{I}\}
that
f_{\alpha}[\omega]\subseteq\alpha
for all
\alpha
. Enumerate also
\mathcal{J}^{+}=\{A_{\alpha}:\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
and denote
\nabla=\{\alpha\in[\omega,\omega_{1}):f_{\alpha}^{-1}[[\omega,\omega_{1})]]\in\mathcal{I}\}
.

Using transfinite induction, we will construct
\{x_{\alpha}\in 2^{\omega}:\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
,
\{E_{\alpha}\subseteq\omega:\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
,
\{Z_{\alpha}\subseteq\omega:\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
and
\{h_{\alpha}\in 2^{2^{\omega}}:\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
satisfying the following properties for every
\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}
:

(P1)

E_{\alpha}\subseteq A_{\alpha}
,
E_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
,
A_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
,

(P2)

x_{\alpha}(i)=1\ \iff\ i\in E_{\alpha}
,

(P3)

(h_{f_{\beta}(n)}(x_{\alpha}))_{n\in Z_{\beta}}
is
\mathcal{I}
-convergent to
0=h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})
, for all
\omega\leq\beta\leq\alpha
,

(P4)

x_{\alpha}\neq x_{\beta}
for all
\beta<\alpha
,

(P5)

Z_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
and:

–

if
\alpha\notin\nabla
then
f_{\alpha}[Z_{\alpha}]\subseteq[\omega,\alpha)
,

–

if
\alpha\in\nabla
then
f_{\alpha}[Z_{\alpha}]\subseteq\omega
and for every
B\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
,
B\subseteq f_{\alpha}[Z_{\alpha}]
there is
C
such that
C\cap B\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
Z_{\alpha}\cap f_{\alpha}^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{I}
,

(P6)

(h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{\beta}))_{n\in Z_{\alpha}}
is
\mathcal{I}
-convergent to
h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})
, for all
\beta<\alpha
,

(P7)

h_{\alpha}\restriction 2^{\omega}\setminus\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}=0
and
h_{\alpha}\restriction\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}=0
.

Before presenting the construction, we show how to finish the proof using the above properties.

Define
X=\{h_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
and
\tilde{X}=\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
. For each
h\in X
let

\mathcal{B}_{h}=\left\{\left\{g\in X:\forall_{y\in G}\ g(y)=h(y)\right\}:G\in[\tilde{X}]^{<\omega}\right\}.

It is easy to check that
\{\mathcal{B}_{h}:h\in X\}
is a neighborhood system, so it generates a topology on
X
(
[
3
, Proposition 1.2.3]
). Consider
X
as a topological space with this topology. We need to show that:

(i)

X\notin\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})
,

(ii)

X\in\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
,

(iii)

X
is Hausdorff,

(iv)

X
is not compact.

(i): We will show that
X
does not have the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})
property. Consider the sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\omega}
. Suppose to the contrary that there are
A\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
\beta<\omega_{1}
such that
(h_{n})_{n\in A}\overset{\mathcal{J}}{\longrightarrow}h_{\beta}
. Then there is
\alpha
for which
A=A_{\alpha}
. The set
V=\{g\in X:g(x_{\alpha})=h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})\}
is an open neighborhood of
h_{\beta}
. We have two possible cases:

Case 1:

h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})=0
. Then
\{n\in A:h_{n}\notin V\}=\{n\in A_{\alpha}:h_{n}(x_{\alpha})\neq h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})\}=\{n\in A_{\alpha}:h_{n}(x_{\alpha})\neq 0\}=A_{\alpha}\cap E_{\alpha}=E_{\alpha}
, by (P1) and (P2).

Case 2:

h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})=1
. Then
\{n\in A:h_{n}\notin V\}=\{n\in A_{\alpha}:h_{n}(x_{\alpha})\neq h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})\}=\{n\in A_{\alpha}:h_{n}(x_{\alpha})\neq 1\}=A_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\alpha}
, by (P2).

It follows from (P1) that both
E_{\alpha}
and
A_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\alpha}
are
\mathcal{J}
-positive, which contradicts the assumption that
(h_{n})_{n\in A}\overset{\mathcal{J}}{\longrightarrow}h_{\beta}
.

(ii): Now we prove that
X
is
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
. Fix any sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
in
X
. Let
f\in\omega_{1}^{\omega}
be a function such that
g_{n}=h_{f(n)}
for all
n\in\omega
. If there is
\beta<\omega_{1}
for which
f^{-1}[\{\beta\}]\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, then
(g_{n})_{n\in f^{-1}[\{\beta\}]}
is
\mathcal{I}
-convergent to
h_{\beta}
and we are done. Otherwise, there is
\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}
such that
f=f_{\alpha}
. We will prove that
(g_{n})_{n\in Z_{\alpha}}\overset{\mathcal{I}}{\longrightarrow}h_{\alpha}
(as
Z_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
by (P5), this will finish the proof of
X\in\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
). For this purpose, let us fix an open neighborhood
V
of
h_{\alpha}
. Then

V=\{h\in X:\forall_{i\leq m}h(x_{\beta_{i}})=h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta_{i}})\}

for some
m\in\omega
and
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{m}<\omega_{1}
. Hence, we have

\begin{split}\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:g_{n}\notin V\}&=\left\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:\exists_{i\leq m}g_{n}(x_{\beta_{i}})\neq h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta_{i}})\right\}\\
&=\bigcup_{i\leq m}\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:g_{n}(x_{\beta_{i}})\neq h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta_{i}})\}\textrm{.}\end{split}

Thus, it is enough to show that for all
\beta<\omega_{1}
we have

\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:g_{n}(x_{\beta})\neq h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})\}\in\mathcal{I}\textrm{.}

Fix
\beta<\omega_{1}
and observe that
(h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{\beta}))_{n\in Z_{\alpha}}\overset{\mathcal{I}}{\longrightarrow}h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})
. Indeed, if
\beta<\alpha
, then it follows from (P6) and if
\beta\geq\alpha
, then this is (P3). Therefore, since
U=\{h\in X:h(x_{\beta})=h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})\}
is an open neighborhood of
h_{\alpha}
, we get

\begin{split}\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:g_{n}\notin U\}&=\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:g_{n}(x_{\beta})\neq h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})\}\\
&=\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{\beta})\neq h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})\}\in\mathcal{I}\textrm{.}\end{split}

(iii): We show that
X
is Hausdorff. Fix
h_{\alpha},h_{\beta}\in X
such that
h_{\alpha}\neq h_{\beta}
. We will consider 3 cases.

Case 1:

\alpha,\beta\geq\omega
. Since
h_{\alpha}\neq h_{\beta}
and
h_{\alpha}\restriction 2^{\omega}\setminus\tilde{X}=h_{\beta}\restriction 2^{\omega}\setminus\tilde{X}=0
(by (P7)), there is
\gamma<\omega_{1}
such that
h_{\alpha}(x_{\gamma})\neq h_{\beta}(x_{\gamma})
. Then
V_{\alpha}=\{h\in X:h_{\alpha}(x_{\gamma})=h(x_{\gamma})\}
and
V_{\beta}=\{h\in X:h_{\beta}(x_{\gamma})=h(x_{\gamma})\}
are neighborhoods of
h_{\alpha}
and
h_{\beta}
, respectively, such that
V_{\alpha}\cap V_{\beta}=\emptyset
.

Case 2:

\alpha,\beta<\omega
. Then
h_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})=1
and
h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})=0
, so
V_{\alpha}=\{h\in X:h_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})=h(x_{\alpha})\}
and
V_{\beta}=\{h\in X:h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})=h(x_{\alpha})\}
are the required disjoint neighborhoods of
h_{\alpha}
and
h_{\beta}
, respectively.

Case 3:

\beta<\omega\leq\alpha
. In this case
h_{\beta}(x_{\beta})=1
and
h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})=0
(by (P7)). Similarly to the previous cases,
V_{\alpha}=\{h\in X:h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta})=h(x_{\beta})\}
and
V_{\beta}=\{h\in X:h_{\beta}(x_{\beta})=h(x_{\beta})\}
are the neighborhoods we need.

(iv): Before showing that
X
is not compact, we need to construct (by induction) an injective map
g:\omega_{1}\to\omega_{1}\setminus\omega
such that
E_{g(\beta)}\supseteq^{\star}E_{g(\alpha)}
whenever
\beta<\alpha<\omega_{1}
. Start with
g(0)=\omega
. If
n\in\omega
and
g(n)
is already defined, find
\omega\leq g(n+1)<\omega_{1}
such that
E_{g(n)}=A_{g(n+1)}
(this is possible as
E_{g(n)}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
by (P1)) and note that
E_{g(n+1)}\subseteq A_{g(n+1)}=E_{g(n)}
(by (P1)) and
g(n+1)\neq g(i)
for
i\leq n
(as
g(n+1)=g(i)
would imply
A_{g(i)}=A_{g(n+1)}=E_{g(n)}\subseteq^{\star}E_{g(i)}
, which contradicts
A_{g(i)}\setminus E_{g(i)}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
). If
\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}
and all
g(\beta)
for
\beta<\alpha
are constructed, enumerate
\alpha=\{\beta_{i}:i\in\omega\}
and define
B_{n}=\bigcap_{i\leq n}E_{g(\beta_{i})}
for all
n\in\omega
. Then
(B_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is a
\subseteq
-decreasing sequence of elements of
\mathcal{J}^{+}
(as given
n\in\omega
we have
E_{\max\{\beta_{i}:\ i\leq n\}}\subseteq^{\star}E_{\beta_{i}}
for all
i\leq n
, which gives us
\mathcal{J}^{+}\ni E_{\max\{\beta_{i}:\ i\leq n\}}\subseteq^{\star}B_{n}
by (P1)). Using the fact that
\mathcal{J}
is
P^{+}
, find
g(\alpha)\in[\omega,\omega_{1})
such that
A_{g(\alpha)}\setminus B_{n}
is finite for all
n\in\omega
. Then for each
i\in\omega
we have:

E_{g(\alpha)}\setminus E_{g(\beta_{i})}\subseteq A_{g(\alpha)}\setminus B_{i}\in\mathrm{Fin},

(by (P1)) and
g(\alpha)\neq g(\beta_{i})
for all
i\in\omega
(as
g(\alpha)=g(\beta_{i})
would imply
A_{g(\beta_{i})}\setminus E_{g(\beta_{i})}=A_{g(\alpha)}\setminus E_{g(\beta_{i})}\subseteq A_{g(\alpha)}\setminus B_{i}\in\mathrm{Fin}
contradicting (P1)). Thus,
g(\alpha)
is as needed.

We are ready to define an open cover of
X
without a finite subcover. Let
U_{\alpha}=\{h\in X:h(x_{g(\alpha)})=0\}
for all
\alpha<\omega_{1}
and
V_{n}=\{h\in X:\ h(x_{n})=1\}
for all
n\in\omega
. Then
X=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}U_{\alpha}\cup\bigcup_{n\in\omega}V_{n}
. Indeed, it is clear that
h_{n}\in V_{n}
for all
n\in\omega
and if
h=h_{\beta}
for some
\omega\leq\beta<\omega_{1}
then there is
\alpha<\omega_{1}
with
g(\alpha)>\beta
(by injectivity of
g
), so
h\in U_{\alpha}
by (P7).

Fix now
\alpha_{0}<\ldots<\alpha_{k}<\omega_{1}
and
n_{0}<\ldots<n_{m}<\omega
. We want to find some
h\in X\setminus(\bigcup_{i\leq k}U_{\alpha_{i}}\cup\bigcup_{j\leq m}V_{n_{j}})
. Since
\mathcal{J}^{+}\ni E_{g(\alpha_{k})}\subseteq^{\star}\bigcap_{i\leq k}E_{g(\alpha_{i})}
, we have
\bigcap_{i\leq k}E_{g(\alpha_{i})}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
, so there is some
p\in\bigcap_{i\leq k}E_{g(\alpha_{i})}\setminus\{n_{j}:\ j\leq m\}
. Then
h_{p}\in X
,
h_{p}\notin\bigcup_{j\leq m}V_{n_{j}}
and
h_{p}\notin U_{\alpha_{i}}
for all
i\leq k
(as
h_{p}(x_{g(\alpha_{i})})=1
by (P2) and
p\in E_{g(\alpha_{i})}
).

Now we return to the construction of
x_{\alpha}\in 2^{\omega}
,
E_{\alpha}\subseteq\omega
,
Z_{\alpha}
and
h_{\alpha}\in 2^{2^{\omega}}
for
\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}
.

Suppose that
\omega\leq\alpha<\omega_{1}
and
x_{\beta},E_{\beta},Z_{\beta}
as well as
h_{\beta}
are defined for all
\omega\leq\beta<\alpha
in such a way that (P1)-(P7) hold for them. We will divide the construction into two parts. At first we will construct
Z_{\alpha}
and
h_{\alpha}
. Next, we will move on to
x_{\alpha}
and
E_{\alpha}
.

First part of the construction

Before constructing
Z_{\alpha}
, we need to define auxiliary sets
\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. We will do this separately for
\alpha\in\nabla
and for
\alpha\notin\nabla
.

Case 1:
If
\alpha\notin\nabla
, then put
\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}^{-1}[[\omega,\omega_{1})]\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Note that actually in this case we have
\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}^{-1}[[\omega,\alpha)]
as
f_{\alpha}[\omega]\subseteq\alpha
.

Case 2:
If
\alpha\in\nabla
, then let
f\in\omega^{\omega}
be defined by

f(n)=\begin{cases}f_{\alpha}(n),&\text{if }f_{\alpha}(n)<\omega,\\
0,&\text{if }f_{\alpha}(n)\geq\omega\textrm{,}\end{cases}

for all
n\in\omega
. From the fact that
\mathcal{J}\nleq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal{I}
it follows that there exists
\widetilde{Z}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that for any given
B\subseteq f[\widetilde{Z}_{\alpha}]
with
B\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
there is
C
for which
C\cap B\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{\alpha}\cap f^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{I}
. We know that
f^{-1}[\{0\}]=f_{\alpha}^{-1}[\{0\}]\cup f_{\alpha}^{-1}[[\omega,\omega_{1})]\in\mathcal{I}
. Therefore
\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}=\widetilde{Z}_{\alpha}\setminus f^{-1}[[\omega,\omega_{1})]\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Moreover, it holds that
f_{\alpha}[\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}]\subseteq\omega
and for any given
B\subseteq f_{\alpha}[\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}]
with
B\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
there is
C
for which
C\cap B\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}\cap f_{\alpha}^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{I}
(as
f\upharpoonright\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}\upharpoonright\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}
).

Now, we will define
Z_{\alpha}
. Fix any enumeration
\alpha=\{\beta_{i}:i\in\omega\}
. Let
B_{\emptyset}=\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}
and for each
(s_{0},\ldots,s_{k})=s\in 2^{<\omega}
define:

B_{s}=\left\{n\in\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}:\forall_{i\leq k}\ h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{\beta_{i}})=s_{i}\right\}\textrm{.}

Observe that
\{B_{s}:s\in 2^{<\omega}\}
is a family of subsets of
\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}
such that:

(1)

B_{\emptyset}=\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}
;

(2)

B_{s}=B_{s^{\frown}(0)}\cup B_{s^{\frown}(1)}
for all
s\in 2^{<\omega}
;

(3)

B_{s^{\frown}(0)}\cap B_{s^{\frown}(1)}=\emptyset
for all
s\in 2^{<\omega}
.

It follows from Theorem
2.1
that there are
y\in 2^{\omega}
and
Z_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
Z_{\alpha}\setminus B_{y\upharpoonright n}\in\mathcal{I}
for all
n\in\omega
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
Z_{\alpha}\subseteq\widehat{Z}_{\alpha}
. Hence, (P5) holds for such
Z_{\alpha}
. Now, let
h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta_{i}})=y_{i}
for all
i\in\omega
and
h_{\alpha}(x)=0
for any
x\in 2^{\omega}\setminus\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
. It remains to show that (P6) and (P7) hold for such
Z_{\alpha}
and
h_{\alpha}
.

We start with (P6). Fix
\beta<\alpha
. There is
i\in\omega
such that
\beta=\beta_{i}
. Then

\begin{split}\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{\beta_{i}})\neq h_{\alpha}(x_{\beta_{i}})\}&=\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{\beta_{i}})\neq y_{i}\}\\
&\subseteq\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:\exists_{j\leq i}\ h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{\beta_{j}})\neq y_{j}\}\\
&=Z_{\alpha}\setminus B_{y\upharpoonright i+1}\in\mathcal{I}.\end{split}

Therefore (P6) holds for
Z_{\alpha}
and
h_{\alpha}
.

As
h_{\alpha}\upharpoonright 2^{\omega}\setminus\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}=0
, in order to get (P7) it remains to show that
h_{\alpha}\upharpoonright\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}=0
. Fix
k\in\omega
. We need to consider two cases.

Case 1:

\alpha\notin\nabla
. Then
f[Z_{\alpha}]\subseteq[\omega,\omega_{1})
by (P5). Hence, for any
n\in Z_{\alpha}
there is
\beta
such that
f_{\alpha}(n)=\beta\in[\omega,\alpha)
and therefore
h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{k})=h_{\beta}(x_{k})=0
(by (P7) applied to
\beta
). Since by (P6) we know that
(h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{k}))_{n\in Z_{\alpha}}\overset{\mathcal{I}}{\longrightarrow}h_{\alpha}(x_{k})
, we get
h_{\alpha}(x_{k})=0
.

Case 2:

\alpha\in\nabla
. Then
f_{\alpha}(n)<\omega
for all
n\in Z_{\alpha}
(by (P5)). Therefore,
h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{k})=1
only if
f_{\alpha}(n)=k
. Hence,
\{n\in Z_{\alpha}:h_{f_{\alpha}(n)}(x_{k})=1\}\subseteq f_{\alpha}^{-1}[\{k\}]\in\mathcal{I}
. From
(P6)
it follows that
h_{\alpha}(x_{k})=0
.

Second part of the construction

Using the fact that
\mathcal{J}
is nowhere maximal, find
E_{\alpha}^{0}\subseteq A_{\alpha}
such that
E_{\alpha}^{0}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
A_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\alpha}^{0}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
. Let
\nabla\cap(\alpha+1)=\{\delta_{i}:i\in\omega\}
. Before we will define
E_{\alpha}
, we need to construct (by induction) a sequence
(E_{\alpha}^{i})_{i>0}
such that the following conditions hold for all
i>0
:

(a)

E_{\alpha}^{i}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
;

(b)

E_{\alpha}^{i+1}\subseteq E_{\alpha}^{i}\subseteq E_{\alpha}^{0}\subseteq A_{\alpha}
;

(c)

Z_{\delta_{i}}\cap f^{-1}_{\delta_{i}}[E_{\alpha}^{i+1}]\in\mathcal{I}
.

Suppose that
i>0
and
E_{\alpha}^{i}
is already defined. We need to consider two cases.

Case 1:
If
E_{\alpha}^{i}\cap f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}]\in\mathcal{J}
, then put
E_{\alpha}^{i+1}=E_{\alpha}^{i}\setminus f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}]=E_{\alpha}^{i}\setminus(E_{\alpha}^{i}\cap f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}])\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and observe that
Z_{\delta_{i}}\cap f^{-1}_{\delta_{i}}[E_{\alpha}^{i+1}]=\emptyset\in\mathcal{I}
.

Case 2:
If
E_{\alpha}^{i}\cap f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}]\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
, then
\delta_{i}\in\nabla
(otherwise
E_{\alpha}^{i}\cap f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}]\subseteq\omega\cap[\omega,\delta_{i})=\emptyset
by (P5)). Applying condition (P5) to
\delta_{i}\in\nabla
and the set
\mathcal{J}^{+}\ni B=E_{\alpha}^{i}\cap f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}]\subseteq f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}]
, we get
C
such that
E_{\alpha}^{i+1}=C\cap B=C\cap(E_{\alpha}^{i}\cap f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}])\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
Z_{\delta_{i}}\cap f_{\delta_{i}}^{-1}[E_{\alpha}^{i+1}]\subseteq Z_{\delta_{i}}\cap f_{\delta_{i}}^{-1}[C]\in\mathcal{I}
.

This finishes the construction of the sequence
(E_{\alpha}^{i})_{i\in\omega}
. Now, applying the fact that
\mathcal{J}
is
P^{+}
to this sequence, we obtain a set
\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
such that
\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\alpha}^{i}
is finite for all
i\in\omega
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\subseteq E_{\alpha}^{0}
.

Observe that there are
2^{\omega}
many
\mathcal{J}
-positive subsets of
\tilde{E}_{\alpha}
(more precisely, using the fact that
\mathcal{J}
is nowhere maximal, find
E\subseteq\tilde{E}_{\alpha}
such that both
E
and
\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\setminus E
are
\mathcal{J}
-positive and observe that
E\cup E^{\prime}\in(\mathcal{J}|\tilde{E}_{\alpha})^{+}
for every
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\setminus E)
). Therefore, we can find some
E_{\alpha}\in(\mathcal{J}|\tilde{E}_{\alpha})^{+}\setminus\{E_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
. Then
E_{\alpha}\subseteq\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\subseteq E^{0}_{\alpha}\subseteq A_{\alpha}
,
E_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
A_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\alpha}\supseteq A_{\alpha}\setminus E^{0}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
, so condition (P1) is satisfied. Let
x_{\alpha}
be as in (P2). It remains to check that (P3) and (P4) hold.

At first we show (P3). Let
\omega\leq\beta\leq\alpha
. We know that
h_{\beta}(x_{\alpha})=0
(by (P7) applied to
\beta
). We need to consider two cases.

Case 1:

\beta\in\nabla
. Let
i\in\omega
be such that
\beta=\delta_{i}
and denote
F=\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\alpha}^{i+1}
(which is a finite set, by the definition of
\tilde{E}_{\alpha}
). Recall that
f_{\delta_{i}}[Z_{\delta_{i}}]\subseteq\omega
(by (P5)). Thus, using (P2) and (c), we have:

\begin{split}\{n\in Z_{\delta_{i}}:h_{f_{\delta_{i}}(n)}(x_{\alpha})=1\}&=\left\{n\in Z_{\delta_{i}}:f_{\delta_{i}}(n)\in E_{\alpha}\right\}=Z_{\delta_{i}}\cap f^{-1}_{\delta_{i}}[E_{\alpha}]\\
&\subseteq Z_{\delta_{i}}\cap f^{-1}_{\delta_{i}}[\tilde{E}_{\alpha}]\subseteq Z_{\delta_{i}}\cap\left(f^{-1}_{\delta_{i}}[E^{i+1}_{\alpha}]\cup f^{-1}_{\delta_{i}}[F]\right)\\
&\subseteq\left(Z_{\delta_{i}}\cap f^{-1}_{\delta_{i}}[E^{i+1}_{\alpha}]\right)\cup f^{-1}_{\delta_{i}}[F]\in\mathcal{I}.\end{split}

Case 2:

\beta\notin\nabla
. Then by (P5) we know that
f_{\beta}[Z_{\beta}]\subseteq[\omega,\beta)\subseteq[\omega,\alpha)
, so
h_{f_{\beta}(n)}(x_{\alpha})=0
for every
n\in Z_{\beta}
(by (P7) applied to
f_{\beta}(n)
). It follows that
(h_{f_{\beta}(n)}(x_{\alpha}))_{n\in Z_{\beta}}
is
\mathcal{I}
-convergent to
0
.

Now, it remains to show that (P4) holds as well. Fix
\beta<\alpha
. Then
E_{\alpha}\neq E_{\beta}
, therefore we can either find
i
such that
i\in E_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\beta}
or find
i\in E_{\beta}\setminus E_{\alpha}
. In the former case,
x_{\alpha}(i)=1\neq 0=x_{\beta}(i)
. In the latter case,
x_{\alpha}(i)=0\neq 1=x_{\beta}(i)
.
∎

Remark 5.2

Since
\mathrm{Fin}^{2}

\widehat{P}^{+}

8
, Proposition 2.6(2)]

4.4
shows that in Theorem
5.1
we cannot replace
P^{+}

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume CH. Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
ideals. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{J}\not\leq_{BW}\mathcal{I}
,

(b)

there is a
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
space which is not
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})
,

(c)

there is a Hausdorff non-compact
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I})
space which is not
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{J})
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
5.1
, since every
\mathbf{F_{\sigma}}
ideal is nowhere maximal,
P^{+}
and has the
\mathrm{hBW}
property (cf. Section
2
).
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Assume CH. Then there exists:

(a)

a Hausdorff sequentially compact (but not compact) space without the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{W})
property,

(b)

a Hausdorff sequentially compact (but not compact) space without the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{1}{n}})
property,

(c)

a Hausdorff
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{1}{n}})
space which is not compact and not sequentially compact,

(d)

a Hausdorff non-compact
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{W})
space without the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{1}{n}})
property,

(e)

a Hausdorff non-compact
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{1}{n}})
space without the
\mathrm{BW}(\mathcal{W})
property.

Proof.

The first two items follow from Corollary
5.3
, since Proposition
3.4
(a) implies that
\mathcal{W}\not\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathrm{Fin}
and
\mathcal{I}_{1/n}\not\leq_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathrm{Fin}
(as
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{I}_{1/n}
are tall). The last three items follow from Corollary
5.3
together with Propositions
4.5
(a) and
4.9
.
∎
