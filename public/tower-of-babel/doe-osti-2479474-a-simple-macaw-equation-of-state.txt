Executive Summary
Here, we construct a simple version of the thermodynamically complete MACAW [1, 2] equation
of state (EOS). The EOS is constructed in a Helmholtz free energy formulation with a Murnaghan
cold curve [3] and a thermal component given by a simplified version of the MACAW specific
heat model [4]. Details of the cold curve are given in Section 2, while the thermal portion of
the model is presented in Section 3. Section 4 demonstrates some of the properties of the EOS,
including the specific internal energy, thermodynamic stability and isentropic sound speed needed
for hydrodynamic computations. Lastly, in Section 5, an example model set of parameters are given
for copper.

1 Introduction
The Helmholtz free energy, F , is given by [5]:

                                               F = E − T S,                                       (1)
where E is the specific internal energy, T is the absolute temperature and S is the specific en-
tropy. The independent thermodynamic parameters for the Helmholtz free energy are the specific
volume, V and temperature. We construct a Helmholtz free energy by presuming there exists a cold
contribution Fc (V ) at T = 0, and a thermal portion, Fth (V, T ):

                                   F (V, T ) = Fc (V ) + Fth (V, T ).                             (2)

5 Example Calibration to Copper
There are strategies for EOS calibration mentioned in the MACAW references [1, 2]. The terms
A and B are related to the isothermal bulk modulus and its derivative with respect to pressure
at T = 0, while V0 specifies the specific volume at T = 0 and P = 0. Clearly most experimental
data is obtained at room temperature, near 300 K. Therefore, we need to also have the thermal
parameters, CV∞ , T0 and Γc to compare with experimental data taken at room temperature. Table
1 contains parameters that are representative for copper.
 These parameters were constructed to yield a realistic specific heat, matching the Dulong-Petit
limit at high temperatures and a reasonable match to a Debye model at finite temperatures. The
A, B, Γc and V0 parameters were constructed to give a realistic density at ambient conditions
(ρ = 8.93 g/cm3 at P = 0 and T ≈ 295.9 K) and to match Hugoniot data. See Figure 1 for
comparison of experimental Hugoniot with the simple MACAW model.
 Note that the real copper Grüneisen parameter at ambient conditions is near Γ = 1.96 [9] and it
likely decreases with increases to density and temperature. As the present model is rather simplistic
