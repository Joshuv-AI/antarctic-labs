Abstract
We present a computational method for reconstructing a vector field on a convex polytope 𝒫 ⊂ ℝ d \mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} of arbitrary dimension from discrete samples. We specifically address the scenario where the vector field is subject to a no-penetration (slip) boundary condition, requiring it to be tangent to the boundary ∂ 𝒫 \partial\mathcal{P} . Given a degree bound k k , our algorithm computes a polynomial vector field of degree at most k k that fits the observed data in the least-squares sense while exactly satisfying the tangency constraints. Central to our approach is an explicit characterization of the module of polynomial vector fields tangent to ∂ 𝒫 \partial\mathcal{P} , derived using algebraic concepts from the theory of hyperplane arrangements.



Keywords:

vector field reconstruction, polynomial interpolation, no-penetration boundary condition, hyperplane arrangement

Introduction

Model fitting aims to identify a function within a parameterized family that accurately reproduces the observed data. A classical example is curve fitting, where a finite set of points is interpolated or approximated using splines or polynomials. More generally, given a collection of input–output pairs
\{(X_{i},Y_{i})\}
and a model family
\{F_{\theta}\}
indexed by parameters
\theta
, the objective is to determine the optimal
\theta
such that
F_{\theta}(X_{i})
approximates
Y_{i}
with minimal error.

In this work, we consider a specific instance of this problem in which the observations are vector-valued and confined within a convex polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the desired model is a polynomial vector field subject to two constraints:

(i)

it minimizes the approximation error relative to the data; and

(ii)

it
exactly
satisfies the no-penetration (slip) boundary condition along the boundary
\partial\mathcal{P}
.

This setting arises naturally in the modeling of fluid or particle dynamics within rigid containers, where velocity measurements may be sparse. In such contexts, strictly enforcing tangency is critical for maintaining physical consistency, particularly for conservation laws such as mass preservation.

We propose an algorithm that, for a prescribed degree
k\in\mathbb{N}
, constructs a polynomial vector field of degree at most
k
that minimizes the squared error relative to the observations while exactly satisfying the no-penetration boundary condition. A key insight of our approach is that the no-penetration boundary condition corresponds precisely to the defining property of the
logarithmic derivation module
associated with a hyperplane arrangement–specifically, the arrangement formed by the affine hyperplanes supporting the facets of
\mathcal{P}
. This identification allows us to reformulate the fitting problem in purely algebraic terms, facilitating an efficient computational solution.

Specifically, our method proceeds as follows:

1.

Represent the polytope
\mathcal{P}
as the intersection of finitely many half-spaces.

2.

Homogenize the defining affine hyperplanes to obtain a central arrangement
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

3.

Establish an explicit bijection between polynomial vector fields of degree
\leq k
that are tangent to
\partial\mathcal{P}
(no-penetration boundary condition) and homogeneous vector fields of degree
k
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
that are tangent to every hyperplane of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
and have vanishing component in the direction of the homogenizing coordinate.

4.

Identify the latter space with the syzygy module of the Jacobian ideal of the defining polynomial of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
.

5.

Employ Gröbner basis techniques (specifically Schreyer’s algorithm) to compute a basis for this module, reducing the problem to linear least-squares fitting.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first algorithm guaranteeing exact satisfaction of the tangency boundary condition for polynomial vector field interpolation on general convex polyhedral domains.
From a theoretical perspective, our framework bridges seemingly disparate fields by applying algebro-geometric techniques to a classical approximation problem, demonstrating the utility of derivation modules in applied settings.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

Exact constraint satisfaction
: Unlike penalty-based or soft-constraint methods, our approach ensures the tangential boundary condition is met exactly for all polynomial degrees.

•

Arbitrary dimension
: Our framework is applicable to convex polytopes in any dimension
d
, not restricted to 2D or 3D cases.

•

Integration with least-squares fitting
: By explicitly computing a basis for the logarithmic derivation module, we reduce the geometric tangency problem to a standard linear least-squares optimization over the basis coefficients. This structure naturally accommodates additional linear constraints, such as divergence-free or curl-free conditions.
In particular, when combined with a divergence-free condition, the volume is conserved exactly (
7.2
).

Related Work

The reconstruction and interpolation of vector fields from sparse data lie at the intersection of numerical analysis, dynamical systems, and approximation theory. This section reviews relevant prior work and clarifies how our algebraic approach differs from existing methods.

2.1
Vector Field Reconstruction and Interpolation

Reconstructing vector fields from discrete observations has a long history, originating in practical applications such as meteorology. Early applications, such as those by Schaefer and Doswell
[
16
]
, focused on interpolating wind fields from station data.
For scattered data interpolation, basis expansion serves as a foundational tool. Mussa-Ivaldi
[
11
]
formalized basis expansions for vector fields as a generalization of scalar interpolation. Subsequent works have refined these techniques, notably through the use of radial basis functions (RBFs)
[
19
,
3
]
and local polynomial approximations
[
17
,
10
]
.
While effective for general approximation, these approaches typically do not guarantee constraints along boundaries.

Recent advances in machine learning have introduced deep learning approaches for flow reconstruction
[
9
]
. While these methods can capture complex nonlinear dynamics, they generally require substantial training data. Moreover, unlike the algebraic method proposed in this work, neural network approaches, including Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), typically enforce boundary conditions and conservation laws via soft constraints (penalty terms in the loss function). Consequently, they do not guarantee exact compliance with the no-penetration condition.

Distinct from spatial interpolation, “global vector field reconstruction” in dynamical systems aims to recover governing differential equations from temporal trajectory data. Gouesbet and Letellier
[
8
]
established methods for fitting polynomial models from time series observations, grounded in Takens’ embedding theorem.

2.2
Structure-Preserving Approximation and Boundary Conditions

A significant body of literature addresses the preservation of differential properties, such as divergence-free (incompressible) or curl-free fields
[
12
,
6
]
. These methods primarily focus on differential constraints in the interior of the domain rather than tangency along the boundary.
Boundary conditions are typically imposed weakly via penalty terms, which do not guarantee exact tangency at all points on the boundary.

In the context of computer graphics and discrete differential geometry, the design of tangent vector fields on surface meshes is a mature field
[
22
,
7
,
2
]
. These methods deal with vector fields tangent to curved surfaces embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In contrast, our work addresses vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that are tangent to the boundary of a polyhedral domain.

A distinct but related area is the study of unit vector fields on convex polyhedra, motivated by liquid crystal modeling. Robbins and Zyskin have classified the homotopy types of such fields
[
14
]
. While these works share the geometric domain (polyhedra) and the tangency condition, their focus is on the topological classification of unit-length fields rather than the approximation of arbitrary data with polynomial fields.

2.3
Hyperplane Arrangements and Logarithmic Derivations

The theoretical foundation of our method lies in the theory of hyperplane arrangements
[
13
]
, initiated by Saito
[
15
]
. Saito introduced the module of logarithmic derivations,
D(\mathcal{A})
, consisting of polynomial vector fields tangent to a set of hyperplanes
\mathcal{A}
. When this module is free, the arrangement is called a
free arrangement
, a concept extensively studied by Terao
[
20
]
.
To the best of our knowledge, the connection between Saito’s theory of logarithmic derivations and the practical, data-driven problem of vector field reconstruction has not been previously exploited.

3
Polynomial Interpolation of Vector Fields

In this section, we establish the necessary geometric notation and formally state the interpolation problem.

Definition 3.1
.

A
convex polyhedral space
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 1
) is a (possibly unbounded) set defined as the intersection of a finite number of closed half-spaces. Let
\{\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a set of normal vectors and
\{\ell_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\}_{i=1}^{m}
a set of scalar offsets. We define:

\mathcal{P}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\langle\alpha_{i},x\rangle+\ell_{i}\leq 0\},

where
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
denotes the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The hyperplane
H_{i}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\langle\alpha_{i},x\rangle+\ell_{i}=0\}
is called a supporting hyperplane of
\mathcal{P}
, and
F_{i}=H_{i}\cap\partial\mathcal{P}
is called a facet (if it is
(d-1)
-dimensional). We denote by
h_{i}
the affine linear function

h_{i}(x)=\langle\alpha_{i},x\rangle+\ell_{i}=\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}x_{q}+\ell_{i},

which defines the hyperplane
H_{i}
.

We assume the representation is minimal; that is, no supporting hyperplane is redundant. Specifically,

\mathcal{P}\subsetneq\bigcap_{i\neq j}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\langle\alpha_{i},x\rangle+\ell_{i}\leq 0\}

for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
In the case
m=0
, we set
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By abuse of notation, we may identify
\mathcal{P}
with its defining set of hyperplanes
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, assuming the orientations of the half-spaces are fixed.

We now define the spaces of polynomial vector fields associated with
\mathcal{P}
.

Definition 3.2
.

A homogeneous polynomial vector field of degree
k
on
\mathcal{P}
is a map
\xi:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
represented by a tuple of homogeneous polynomials
(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{d})
, where each
f_{q}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
has
\deg(f_{q})=k
for
1\leq q\leq d
. Denote by
{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}_{k}
the space of such vector fields. Similarly, denote by
{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
the space of vector fields where each
f_{q}
is a polynomial of degree at most
k
.

The subspace of
{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}_{k}
(respectively,
{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
) consisting of vector fields tangent to the boundary of
\mathcal{P}
is defined by

{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{k}=\left\{\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}_{k}\mid\langle\xi(x),\alpha_{i}\rangle=0\text{ for all }x\in F_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq m\right\}.

(1)

In other words, the direction of
\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{k}
at any point on a facet
F_{i}
is orthogonal to the normal vector
\alpha_{i}
. The space
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
is defined analogously for non-homogeneous vector fields.

The space
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
of non-homogeneous polynomial tangential fields
and the space
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{hom}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{k}
of homogeneous polynomial tangential fields are both
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
-modules under componentwise multiplication of vector fields by polynomials.

The following example illustrates the distinction between homogeneous and non-homogeneous polynomial tangential fields.

Example 3.3
.

Consider the case
d=2
. Let
\alpha_{1}=(1,0)
,
\alpha_{2}=(0,1)
, and
\alpha_{3}=(-1,-1)
, with
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=0
and
\ell_{3}=1
, so that
h_{1}=x_{1}
,
h_{2}=x_{2}
, and
h_{3}=-x_{1}-x_{2}-1
. Let
h_{4}=-h_{3}
. Define

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{x\mid h_{1}(x)\leq 0,\ h_{2}(x)\leq 0,\ h_{3}(x)\leq 0\},\quad\mathcal{P}_{2}=\{x\mid h_{1}(x)\leq 0,\ h_{2}(x)\leq 0,\ h_{4}(x)\leq 0\}.

Then
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is bounded, and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
is unbounded. Both are convex polyhedral spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, as illustrated in
Figure

1
.

For either
\mathcal{P}_{1}
or
\mathcal{P}_{2}
, the vector field
\xi=(x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}^{2}+x_{2})
is a non-homogeneous tangential field. We verify the tangency condition facet by facet:

- On
F_{1}
(defined by
x_{1}=0
),
\xi(0,x_{2})=(0,x_{2}^{2}+x_{2})
, and
\alpha_{1}=(1,0)
, so
\langle\xi,\alpha_{1}\rangle=0
.

- On
F_{2}
(defined by
x_{2}=0
),
\xi(x_{1},0)=(0,0)
, and
\alpha_{2}=(0,1)
, so
\langle\xi,\alpha_{2}\rangle=0
.

- On
F_{3}
(defined by
-x_{1}-x_{2}-1=0
, or
x_{1}=-1-x_{2}
), the normals are
\alpha_{3}=(-1,-1)
and
\alpha_{4}=(1,1)
, and

\displaystyle\xi(-1-x_{2},x_{2})=((-1-x_{2})x_{2},x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}),

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\alpha_{3}\rangle=-\langle\xi,\alpha_{4}\rangle=-\left((-1-x_{2})x_{2}+(x_{2}^{2}+x_{2})\right)=0.

Thus,
\xi
is tangent to all three facets.

Note that the degree-one component of
\xi
, namely
(0,x_{2})
, is not tangential to
F_{3}
, since
\langle(0,x_{2}),(1,1)\rangle=x_{2}\neq 0
in general. This demonstrates that
\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{j}\subsetneq{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
in general, making a degree-wise analysis of
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
more intricate.

Figure 1:

Examples of convex polyhedral spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a tangential vector field defined on them.

A vector field
\xi=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{d})
acts on a polynomial
h\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
via the Lie derivative:

\xi[h]=\langle\nabla h,\xi\rangle=\sum_{q=1}^{d}f_{q}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{q}}.

(2)

For brevity, we denote
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{q}}
by
\partial_{q}
.

We now formulate our main goal. Suppose we are given a set of
observations

\left\{(x_{s},u_{s})\in\mathcal{P}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid s\in\mathcal{O}\right\},

indexed by a finite set
\mathcal{O}
.

Problem 3.4
.

Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, find
\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
that minimizes the sum of squared errors:

\mathcal{E}(\xi,\mathcal{O})=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{O}}\left|\xi(x_{s})-u_{s}\right|^{2}.

(3)

Problem
3.4
seeks a polynomial model that optimally fits the data (in the least-squares sense) while
strictly
enforcing the no-penetration boundary condition on
\partial\mathcal{P}
. The degree
k
serves as a hyperparameter controlling the trade-off between model expressiveness and complexity.

4
Identity Theorem for Polynomial Vector Fields

It is straightforward to verify that any polynomial vector field defined on
\mathcal{P}
can be uniquely extended to a polynomial vector field on all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so that
{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}_{k}={\mathrm{Poly}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}_{k}
. In this section, we establish an analogous result for tangential vector fields.
We begin by recalling a classical fact: a polynomial function is uniquely determined by its values on a sufficiently large finite set.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\geq 1
, and let
f,g\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
be polynomials of degree at most
k
. Suppose
X=C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where each
C_{q}\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfies
|C_{q}|>k
. Then
f\equiv g
if and only if
f(x)=g(x)
for all
x\in X
. Consequently, two vector fields
\xi,\gamma\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}_{\leq k}
coincide on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if
\xi(x)=\gamma(x)
for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

By considering
f-g
in place of
f
, it suffices to show that a polynomial
f
of degree at most
k
vanishes identically if
f(x)=0
for all
x\in X
.

We proceed by induction on
d
. The base case
d=1
follows from the classical fact that a univariate polynomial of degree at most
k
with more than
k
zeros must be identically zero. Assume the result holds for
d-1
. For a fixed
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1})\in C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{d-1}
, consider the univariate polynomial

p(t)=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1},t).

Since
p
vanishes on
C_{d}
, which contains more than
k\geq\deg p
points, we conclude
p\equiv 0
. Thus, all coefficients of powers of
x_{d}
in
f
vanish as polynomials in
x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1}
. By the induction hypothesis,
f\equiv 0
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a hyperplane with non-zero normal vector
\alpha
. A polynomial vector field
\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}_{\leq k}
is tangent to
H
if and only if
\langle\xi(x),\alpha\rangle=0
on some non-empty open subset of
H
.

Consequently, every
\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
admits a unique extension to a polynomial vector field in
{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}_{\leq k}
that is tangent to all supporting hyperplanes of the polyhedral domain
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Apply
Lemma

4.1
to the polynomial
\langle\xi(x),\alpha\rangle
.
∎

By
Corollary

4.2
, the space
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
can be identified with the solution space of a linear system. However, in the next section, we introduce a more sophisticated approach for computing
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
.

5
Space of Tangent Fields

3.4
reduces to a standard least-squares problem, provided a basis for
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
is available. Our key idea is to recast the boundary condition as a module membership problem in a polynomial ring, and to identify
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
with the syzygy module of a Jacobian ideal, which can be effectively computed using Gröbner basis techniques.

We begin with a simple observation that translates
the tangency condition into an algebraic one.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
be a degree-one polynomial defining the hyperplane
H=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid h(x)=0\}
.
A polynomial vector field
\xi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is tangent to
H
if and only if
\xi[h]\in(h)
, where
(h)
is the principal ideal generated by
h
in the ring
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
.

Proof.

Write

h(x)=\langle\alpha,x\rangle+\ell

so that
\nabla h=\alpha
.
By
Equation

1
and
Equation

2
,

\xi
is tangent to

H
if and only if

0=\langle\alpha,\xi(x)\rangle=\langle\nabla h,\xi(x)\rangle=\xi[h](x)\quad\forall\,x\in H.

Hence

\xi\text{ tangent to }H\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\xi[h]\big|_{V(h)}\equiv 0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\xi[h]\in I\bigl(V(h)\bigr),

where we write
H
as the variety
V(h)
defined by
h
, and
I\bigl(V(h)\bigr)
is the vanishing ideal of
the variety
V(h)
.
Since
h
is a nonzero degree-one polynomial, the ideal
(h)
is
prime. Therefore, the assertion follows by

I\bigl(V(h)\bigr)=\sqrt{(h)}=(h)
.
∎

To use algebraic machinery, it is more convenient to work with linear subspaces than with affine subspaces
{H_{i}}
.
For this, we embed
\mathcal{P}
into one dimension higher space
by appending one more variable
x_{0}
.

Definition 5.2
.

For a convex polyhedral space
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
its
cone

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}

is the (unbounded) convex polyhedral space in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

defined by the following supporting hyperplanes

\widehat{H}_{i}=\left\{\widehat{x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\mid\ell_{i}x_{0}+\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}x_{q}=0\right\}.

We denote by
\widehat{h}_{i}
the corresponding linear form.
See
fig.

2
.

Figure 2:

The cone
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

corresponding to a trapezoid
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
First,
\mathcal{P}
is embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
on the plane
x_{0}=1
. By joining the origin with each facet of
\mathcal{P}
, we obtain hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
that share the origin as the apex.
An element of
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}
can be extended to a field tangent to
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
simply by scaling by the “height”
x_{0}
.
The field thus obtained is parallel to the plane
x_{0}=0
.
Conversely, any homogeneous tangential field to
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
can be made parallel to
x_{0}=0
by subtracting an
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
-multiple of
\xi_{E}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})

By restricting the parallel field to
x_{0}=1

{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}
.

All the facets of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
meet at the origin,
and
the original convex polyhedral space
\mathcal{P}
is embedded in
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
at the slice
x_{0}=1
, hence the name “cone”.
We will see that there is also a correspondence between
certain tangential polynomial vector fields of
\mathcal{P}

and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
.
A small but important trick is to add a special
plane
\widehat{H}_{0}=\{x_{0}=0\}
.
Let
{\overline{\mathrm{Poly}}}_{\partial}({\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})_{k}

be the subspace of
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})}_{k}

consisting of those fields parallel to
\widehat{H}_{0}
.
That is, the first coordinate of an element
of
{\overline{\mathrm{Poly}}}_{\partial}({\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})_{k}
is the zero polynomial.

Lemma 5.3
.

We have an isomorphism of vector spaces

{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}\cong{\overline{\mathrm{Poly}}}_{\partial}({\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})_{k}.

Proof.

The degree-
k
homogenization of

f_{q}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
with
\deg{f_{q}}\leq k

is given by

\widehat{f}_{q}=x_{0}^{k}f_{q}(x_{1}/x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}/x_{0})

with
\deg{\widehat{f}_{q}}=k
.
Define a map
\Psi
from
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
to
{\overline{\mathrm{Poly}}}_{\partial}({\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})_{k}
by

(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{d})\mapsto(0,\widehat{f}_{1},\widehat{f}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{f}_{d}).

We show that the image of
\Psi
is contained in
{\overline{\mathrm{Poly}}}_{\partial}({\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})_{k}
.
Since
(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{d})
is tangent to every
H_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq m
, we have

\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}f_{q}(x)=0,\text{ for any }x\in H_{i}.

By
lemma

5.1
, we may assume that
\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}f_{q}=h_{i}g
for some
g\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}\widehat{f}_{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}(x_{0}^{k}f_{q}(x_{1}/x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}/x_{0}))

\displaystyle=x_{0}^{k}\left(\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}f_{q}(x_{1}/x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}/x_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=x_{0}^{k}h_{i}(x_{1}/x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}/x_{0})g(x_{1}/x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}/x_{0})

\displaystyle=\widehat{h}_{i}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{d})x_{0}^{k-1}g(x_{1}/x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}/x_{0}).

Since
\deg g\leq k-1
, we deduce that
x_{0}^{k-1}g(x_{1}/x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}/x_{0})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k-1
.
This implies that
\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}\widehat{f}_{q}\in(\widehat{h}_{i})
and we have
(0,\widehat{f}_{1},\widehat{f}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{f}_{d})\in{\overline{\mathrm{Poly}}}_{\partial}({\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})_{k}
.

Conversely,
define a map
\Phi
from
{\overline{\mathrm{Poly}}}_{\partial}({\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})_{k}
to
{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
by

(0,\widehat{f}_{1},\widehat{f}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{f}_{d})\mapsto(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{d}),

where
\widehat{f}_{q}
is homogenous polynomial in
\mathbb{R}[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
with
\deg{\widehat{f}_{q}}=k
, and

f_{q}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
with
\deg{f_{q}}\leq k

are obtained by evaluating
\widehat{f}_{q}
at
x_{0}=1
.
Observe that

(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{d})\in{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
.
Note that
(0,\widehat{f}_{1},\widehat{f}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{f}_{d})
is tangent to every
\widehat{H}_{i}\in\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
Since the normal vector of
\widehat{H}_{i}
is
\widehat{\alpha}_{i}=(\ell_{i},(\alpha_{i})_{1},\ldots,(\alpha_{i})_{d})
,
it follows that

\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}\widehat{f}_{q}(\widehat{x})=0,\text{ for any }\widehat{x}\in\widehat{H}_{i}.

By restricting at
x_{0}=1
, we have

\sum_{q=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i})_{q}f_{q}(x)=0,\text{ for any }x\in H_{i}

and
(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{d})
is tangent to every
H_{i}
.
Since both
\Phi\circ\Psi
and
\Psi\circ\Phi
are the identities, the assertion follows.
∎

Definition 5.4

The simultaneous tangency to multiple hyperplanes

Proposition 5.5

(
[

, Chap. 4]
)
.

We have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(\widehat{\mathcal{P}})

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{i=0}^{m}\left\{\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathbb{R}^{d+1}})}\mid\xi[\widehat{h}_{i}]\in(\widehat{h}_{i})\right\}.

Proof.

First, we see for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{D}}(\widehat{\mathcal{P}})
,
it holds that
\xi[\widehat{h}_{i}]\in(\widehat{h}_{i})
for any

As
\xi
is a derivation,

\xi[Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}]=\xi\left[\widehat{h}_{i}\cdot\frac{Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}}{\widehat{h}_{i}}\right]=\xi[\widehat{h}_{i}]\,\frac{Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}}{\widehat{h}_{i}}+\xi\!\Bigl[\frac{Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}}{\widehat{h}_{i}}\Bigr]\,\widehat{h}_{i}.

Since
\xi[Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}]\in(Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})\subseteq(\widehat{h}_{i})
and obviously

\xi[\widehat{h}_{i}]\,\frac{Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}}{\widehat{h}_{i}}\in(\widehat{h}_{i})

As

is prime and

\xi[\widehat{h}_{i}]\in(\widehat{h}_{i})

Conversely, we show

for any

We have

\displaystyle\xi[Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}]

\displaystyle=\xi[\widehat{h}_{0}\cdots\widehat{h}_{m-1}\,\widehat{h}_{m}]

\displaystyle=\xi[\widehat{h}_{0}\cdots\widehat{h}_{m-1}]\,\widehat{h}_{m}+\xi[\widehat{h}_{m}]\,\widehat{h}_{0}\cdots\widehat{h}_{m-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\xi[\widehat{h}_{0}\cdots\widehat{h}_{m-2}]\,\widehat{h}_{m-1}\widehat{h}_{m}+\xi[\widehat{h}_{m-1}]\,\widehat{h}_{0}\cdots\widehat{h}_{m-2}\widehat{h}_{m}\right)+\xi[\widehat{h}_{m}]\,\widehat{h}_{0}\cdots\widehat{h}_{m-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{\xi[\widehat{h}_{i}]}{\widehat{h}_{i}}Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}}\in(Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{P}}})

as required.
∎

Lemma 5.7

When
\bigcap_{i}\widehat{H}_{i}=\{0\}

the set

,
and

Proof.

By

By the definition of the action of a vector field

The following proposition ensures that

terminates; more strongly, for
\epsilon=0
(exact interpolation), a solution exists with a sufficiently high polynomial degree.

Proposition 6.2

(Exact interpolation)
.

Let

\{(x_{s},u_{s})\mid x_{s}\in\mathcal{P},\ u_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}_{s\in\mathcal{O}}

be a finite set of observations, where the points
x_{s}
are distinct and lie in a polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and each associated vector
u_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assume that whenever
x_{s}
lies on a boundary facet
F_{j}
of
\mathcal{P}
, the vector
u_{s}
is tangent to
F_{j}
.
Then there exists a polynomial vector field
\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
for some
k
such that
\xi(x_{s})=u_{s}
for all
s\in\mathcal{O}
.
Consequently,
6.1
admits a solution for any
\epsilon\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
be the facets of
\mathcal{P}
with affine linear defining functions
h_{j}
and unit normals
\alpha_{j}
.
For each observation index
s\in\mathcal{O}
, set

J_{s}:=\{\,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}\mid x_{s}\notin F_{j}\,\},\qquad W_{s}(x):=\prod_{j\in J_{s}}h_{j}(x).

We have
W_{s}(x_{s})\neq 0
and

W_{s}
vanishes on every facet that does not contain
x_{s}
.

Let

L_{s}(x)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{O}\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}\frac{\|x-x_{t}\|^{2}}{\|x_{s}-x_{t}\|^{2}},

so that
L_{s}(x_{t})=\delta_{st}
for all
s,t\in\mathcal{O}
.
Define the polynomial vector field

\xi(x):=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{O}}L_{s}(x)\,\frac{W_{s}(x)}{W_{s}(x_{s})}\,u_{s}.

For any
t\in\mathcal{O}
,

\xi(x_{t})=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{O}}L_{s}(x_{t})\,\frac{W_{s}(x_{t})}{W_{s}(x_{s})}\,u_{s}=\frac{W_{t}(x_{t})}{W_{t}(x_{t})}\,u_{t}=u_{t}.

Thus it interpolates all prescribed data
(x_{s},u_{s})
.

Fix
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and take
x\in F_{j}
.
Split the defining sum for
\xi
into indices with
j\in J_{s}
and those with
j\notin J_{s}
.
If
j\in J_{s}
, then
h_{j}
is a factor of
W_{s}
, hence
W_{s}(x)=0
on
F_{j}
and that term vanishes on
F_{j}
.
If
j\notin J_{s}
, the hypothesis gives
\langle u_{s},\alpha_{j}\rangle=0
.
Therefore for all
x\in F_{j}
,

\big\langle L_{s}(x)\,\tfrac{W_{s}(x)}{W_{s}(x_{s})}\,u_{s},\alpha_{j}\big\rangle=L_{s}(x)\,\tfrac{W_{s}(x)}{W_{s}(x_{s})}\,\langle u_{s},\alpha_{j}\rangle=0.

Summing over
s
yields
\langle\xi(x),\alpha_{j}\rangle=0
on
F_{j}
, and as
j
was arbitrary,
\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}
.
A crude bound is

\deg\xi\;\leq\;2(|\mathcal{O}|-1)\;+\;\max_{s\in\mathcal{O}}\big|J_{s}\big|\;\leq\;2(|\mathcal{O}|-1)+m.

∎

The optimization framework in
3.4
can be naturally extended to search for vector fields within specific subspaces of
{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}
. This extension is particularly relevant for physical applications.
We define the following three subspaces of polynomial vector fields:

First, the space of
divergence-free
(or solenoidal) polynomial vector fields is defined as:

{\mathrm{DivFree}({\mathcal{P}})}=\left\{\xi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{d})\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}\;\middle|\;\nabla\cdot\xi=\sum_{q=1}^{d}\frac{\partial f_{q}}{\partial x_{q}}=0\right\}.

Second, the space of
rotation-free
(irrotational or curl-free) polynomial vector fields is defined as:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{RotFree}({\mathcal{P}})}

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{d})\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}\;\middle|\;\frac{\partial f_{q}}{\partial x_{p}}=\frac{\partial f_{p}}{\partial x_{q}}\quad\forall\,1\leq p,q\leq d\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}\;\middle|\;\exists\,\Phi\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}\text{ such that }\xi=\nabla\Phi\right\}.

Note that the second equality holds because the convex polytope
\mathcal{P}
is simply connected (by the Poincaré Lemma), ensuring that every closed 1-form is exact.

Third, the space of
harmonic
polynomial vector fields is defined as:

{\mathrm{Harm}({\mathcal{P}})}=\left\{\xi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{d})\in{\mathrm{Poly}({\mathcal{P}})}\;\middle|\;\Delta\xi=(\Delta f_{1},\dots,\Delta f_{d})=\mathbf{0}\right\},

where
\Delta=\sum_{q=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{q}^{2}}
denotes the scalar Laplacian acting component-wise.

We focus on the intersections of these subspaces with the space of tangential vector fields. Specifically, we define:

{\mathrm{DivFree_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}={\mathrm{DivFree}({\mathcal{P}})}\cap{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k},

{\mathrm{RotFree_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}={\mathrm{RotFree}({\mathcal{P}})}\cap{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k},

{\mathrm{Harm_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}={\mathrm{Harm}({\mathcal{P}})}\cap{\mathrm{Poly_{\partial}}({\mathcal{P}})}_{\leq k}.
