Executive Summary
The Launch Alaska Transportation and Energy Accelerator (LATEA), funded through the U.S. Department
of Energy Office of Technology Commercialization’s Energy Program for Innovation Clusters (EPIC),
advanced deployment of innovative and efficient transportation and energy technology in Alaska from
October 2021 through June 2025. The project was designed to leverage Launch Alaska’s accelerator
model to identify, recruit, and support transportation technology companies with novel solutions to
market needs while building the stakeholder networks, demonstration opportunities, and institutional
capacity necessary to accelerate commercialization in one of the most challenging operating
environments in the United States.

Alaska presents a uniquely valuable proving ground for transportation innovation. Extreme cold, limited
transportation infrastructure, geographically dispersed communities, reliance on aviation and marine
transportation, and energy costs among the highest in the nation create challenges that are not
adequately represented in lower-48 deployment environments. Technologies capable of succeeding in
Alaska frequently demonstrate applicability to rural America, Arctic regions, military installations,
disaster response environments, and other infrastructure-constrained locations.

Through accelerator programming, proof-of-concept development, stakeholder engagement,
educational events, and strategic partnerships, LATEA exceeded nearly all performance objectives
established under the cooperative agreement. The project recruited 42 transportation-focused startup
companies, accepted 23 companies into accelerator cohorts, facilitated seven proof-of-concept projects,
accelerated 25 transportation projects representing approximately $605.9 million in project value,

secured more than $666,000 in follow-on funding and contracts, and contributed directly to the
formation of the Alaska Clean Cities and Communities Coalition (AK4C).

Beyond quantitative outcomes, the project established Alaska as a nationally recognized testbed for
transportation innovation, demonstrated electric vehicle operation to the Arctic Ocean, advanced
hybrid-electric aviation, facilitated hydrogen transportation planning, supported statewide
transportation decarbonization planning, and created long-term organizational capabilities that will
continue beyond the conclusion of the EPIC award.

Key results include:

 1. Acceleration of 25 transportation projects in the Alaska market with projects totaling $605.9M
 in value
 2. First-ever Arctic Ocean EV drive (2022 Arctic Road Rally)
 3. Production and premiere of Electrified Alaska documentary
 4. Proof-of-concepts in aviation (Ampaire Electric EEL hybrid-electric aircraft and Remora air
 logistics app), energy resilience and EV charging (Jule EVSE), and transportation and energy
 system planning (Constellation)
 5. Formation and designation of the Alaska Clean Cities & Communities Coalition (AK4C)

Project Activities
Alaska’s transportation sector faces conditions that differ substantially from those in most U.S. states.
Communities are often separated by hundreds of miles, many are inaccessible by road, and aviation,
marine transportation, and seasonal logistics networks play critical roles in daily life and economic
activity. Fuel costs are elevated by transportation expenses, and infrastructure deployment frequently
encounters challenges associated with permafrost, extreme weather, and remote operations.

At the same time, Alaska possesses significant opportunities for transportation innovation. The state
serves as a strategic gateway between North America, Asia, and Europe, hosts globally significant
aviation and maritime infrastructure, and contains communities eager to reduce energy costs and
improve transportation resilience. These characteristics make Alaska an ideal location for demonstrating
advanced transportation technologies and business models.

LATEA was developed to bridge the gap between emerging transportation technologies and real-world
deployment opportunities. Through Launch Alaska’s Tech Deployment Track accelerator, participating
companies receive structured support that includes customer discovery, stakeholder engagement,
strategic planning, project development, and introductions to prospective partners, customers, and
investors.

LATEA operated four program tracks:

 1. Accelerator (Tech Deployment Track) – recruited 42 transportation startups, accepted 23,
 exceeded goals.
 2. EV Demonstrations – Arctic Road Rally → EV fleet demos → Electrified Alaska film.

 3. Education/Outreach – convened Clean Transportation Leadership Roundtables, Hydrogen
 Charrette, engagement with DOT&PF, AEA, Tribal entities and Port of Alaska.
 4. Sustainability – contracts and new grants (Alaska Municipal League (AML) Charging and Fueling
 Infrastructure (CFI) and Low-Emissions Ferry; Alaska Department of Transportation and Public
 Facilities Carbon Reduction Strategy; Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Port of Alaska
 Climate and Air quality Planning (PortCAP); DOE-VTO Rising TIDES).

Award modifications extended performance to June 2025, allowing full completion of EV demo film and
coalition formation.

Lessons Learned
Throughout the EPIC award period, Launch Alaska encountered challenges typical of pioneering clean
transportation in extreme environments. Supply chain delays for electric trucks and snowmobiles
presented recurring obstacles, with promised deliveries from manufacturers like Chevy and Polaris
postponed or canceled. These setbacks underscored the importance of developing contingency plans
and cultivating diverse partnerships. Launch Alaska mitigated the risks by engaging other manufacturers
such as Rivian, Ford, and Peterbilt, ensuring demonstrations could proceed and momentum was
maintained.

Regulatory and compliance hurdles also revealed critical lessons. The deployment of Jule’s battery-
supported EVSE units at municipal sites highlighted the need for early engagement with local fire
inspectors and permitting authorities. Misalignments in setback requirements and fire suppression
systems required costly adjustments that could have been avoided through earlier involvement of
subject matter experts. The experience reinforced the value of involving regulators from the outset of
project design to reduce risk, streamline approvals, and safeguard limited resources.

The volatility of federal funding during the 2024 administrative transition further emphasized the
necessity of diversified revenue streams. Launch Alaska successfully leveraged EPIC with commercial
contracts. These parallel funding sources not only sustained program continuity but also demonstrated

that clean transportation initiatives could remain resilient even amidst shifting federal priorities. The
lesson learned was that long-term sustainability hinges on blending government support with private-
sector, nonprofit, and community-based partnerships.

Another key insight was the power of storytelling and public demonstration to accelerate technology
acceptance. The Arctic Road Rally and Electrified Alaska documentary captured widespread attention,
building public trust in EV viability under Alaska’s harsh conditions. These events were not simply
marketing efforts but essential tools in overcoming skepticism and driving cultural change. Launch
Alaska learned that high-visibility, narrative-driven demonstrations can be as impactful as technical
pilots in fostering stakeholder buy-in and building momentum for adoption.

Success Stories
Tech Deployment Track
The Tech Deployment Track served as the centerpiece of the LATEA program. Throughout the project
period, Launch Alaska conducted extensive outreach to transportation technology companies
throughout the United States and internationally. Annual recruitment campaigns targeted startups
working in aviation, maritime transportation, alternative fuels, electric mobility, logistics, autonomous
systems, and related transportation sectors.

Recruitment efforts exceeded project goals. More than 42 transportation-focused companies applied to
participate in the accelerator, compared to an original goal of 40 companies. Twenty-three companies
ultimately participated in accelerator cohorts, exceeding the project target of 18 companies.

Representative transportation companies supported through the program included:

 • ZeroAvia (hydrogen-electric aviation)

 • Ampaire (hybrid-electric aviation)

 • ElectricFish (battery-supported EV charging)

 • Avari Aerospace (cargo unmanned aircraft)

 • Talyn Air (advanced air mobility)

 • General Galactic (synthetic fuels)

 • GreenTaxi (aircraft taxiing systems)

 • Photon Marine (electric marine propulsion)

 • Jule (battery-supported EV charging)

 • Remora (aviation logistics software)

The program’s customer discovery process generated hundreds of deployment opportunities and
introductions. Transportation-focused cohort companies received 213 advisor-generated leads during
the 2022 cycle, 241 leads during the 2023 cycle, and 313 leads during the 2024 cycle. These
introductions connected startups with utilities, ports, airlines, tribal organizations, municipalities, state
agencies, and private industry partners.

Demonstration Projects and Proof-of-Concepts
Arctic Road Rally
One of the most visible accomplishments of the project was the Arctic Road Rally. Conducted in August
2022, the event successfully demonstrated that electric vehicles could travel from Fairbanks to the
Arctic Ocean and return using a temporary charging network developed specifically for the
demonstration. Ten electric vehicles representing Tesla, Rivian, Ford, and General Motors completed the
rugged journey from Fairbanks to Oliktok Point, supported by temporary charging infrastructure. The
event established the first documented drive of electric vehicles to the Arctic Ocean in North America.

The Rally required extensive collaboration among utilities, industry partners, state agencies, and
volunteers. Temporary charging infrastructure was installed along the Dalton Highway, and event
organizers worked closely with ConocoPhillips, DOT&PF, Alyeska Tire, Golden Valley Electric Association,
and numerous other partners.

The event generated widespread media coverage and public interest, reaching more than 1.3 million
people through traditional and social media channels.

EV Fleet Demonstrations
Following the withdrawal of anticipated charging infrastructure support for a larger 2023 Arctic Road
Rally, Launch Alaska successfully pivoted to fleet-focused EV demonstrations. Launch Alaska produced
the Electrified Alaska documentary, a capstone success of the EV demonstration program. Released in
2025, the film chronicled how Alaskans—from state fleets to utilities and Tribal organizations—are
adopting EVs under some of the harshest conditions in North America. Premiered at the Alaska
Sustainable Energy Conference and distributed online, the film attracted hundreds of viewers within its
first weeks and continues to serve as an educational tool nationally. The storytelling approach bridged
technical data with real-world experiences, offering a compelling narrative that resonates with
policymakers, fleets, and the general public.

Ampaire Hybrid-Electric Aviation Demonstration
In August 2023, Launch Alaska partnered with Ampaire and ARPA-E to host the Alaska showcase of the
Electric EEL hybrid-electric aircraft. The aircraft completed a first-of-its-kind flight from Southern
California to Fairbanks, Alaska, demonstrating the feasibility of hybrid-electric aviation technologies for
regional transportation applications. The event drew participation from federal and state agencies,
utilities, and local stakeholders, showing how small aircraft could serve as a testbed for scaling new
technologies. The successful flight positioned Alaska as a potential leader in clean aviation, aligning with
global decarbonization trends in regional air travel.

Jule Battery-Supported EV Charging
Launch Alaska supported deployment of Jule battery-supported EV charging systems in Alaska. Initial
installations demonstrated the potential value of energy-storage-assisted charging infrastructure in
locations with limited electrical capacity. While regulatory and code-compliance challenges affected the
first installation with the Municipality of Anchorage, subsequent projects advanced at commercial
hospitality locations and generated valuable lessons for future deployments.

Remora Logistics Platform
Launch Alaska supported Remora’s aviation logistics platform, which improves utilization of available
aircraft capacity for freight and passenger transportation. The platform successfully launched during the
project period and demonstrated the value of digital solutions for improving transportation efficiency in
remote regions.

Stakeholder Education and Ecosystem Development
LATEA recognized that technology deployment requires informed stakeholders and supportive
institutions. To address this need, Launch Alaska established recurring transportation education and
stakeholder engagement activities.

Clean Transportation Leadership Roundtable
The 2022 and 2023 Clean Transportation Leadership Roundtables brought together utilities, fleet
operators, government agencies, academia, technology companies, and transportation providers to
discuss opportunities and barriers associated with transportation decarbonization. Attendance
expanded over time, with participants representing a broad cross-section of Alaska’s transportation
ecosystem.

Hydrogen Charrette
In 2023 Launch Alaska organized a Hydrogen Charrette focused on developing practical hydrogen
demonstration projects in the Cook Inlet region. Participants included infrastructure owners, utilities,
national laboratories, state agencies, technology companies, and policymakers. The workshop
accelerated project development and established collaborative relationships that continue beyond the
grant period.

The charrette forged collaboration around the Port of Alaska hydrogen pilot concepts. Working with the
U.S. Army CERL, Cummins Accelera, Chugach Electric, and the Municipality of Anchorage, Launch Alaska
advanced planning for hydrogen fuel-cell yard tractors and local hydrogen production. While logistical
challenges delayed demonstrations, the effort positioned Alaska as a testbed for hydrogen applications
in maritime and port operations. These early pilots laid the groundwork for broader hydrogen
infrastructure development, supported by the DOE’s national roadmap.

Program Sustainability and Legacy
Another notable success was the development and designation of the Alaska Clean Cities &
Communities Coalition (AK4C). With DOE support, Launch Alaska convened a diverse advisory
committee including state agencies, utilities, nonprofits, and national labs to establish the coalition.
AK4C now serves as a durable platform to continue clean transportation initiatives beyond the EPIC
grant. Its formation represents a major institutional win, ensuring that the networks, expertise, and
momentum cultivated under EPIC will persist with federal and local support.

Finally, the cumulative impact of EPIC funding—25 transportation projects with an aggregate value of
$605.9 million—stands as the overarching success story. Transportation projects made up 80% of
Launch Alaska’s portfolio value during the grant, far exceeding goals. From rural EVSE planning to
sustainable aviation fuel studies, these projects demonstrate how DOE’s investment catalyzed a clean
transportation ecosystem in Alaska. Together, they highlight how Alaska’s unique geography and

challenges, once seen as barriers, have become drivers of innovation and proof-points for technologies
that can be scaled nationally and globally.
