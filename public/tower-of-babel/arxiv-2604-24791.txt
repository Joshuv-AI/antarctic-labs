Generalized Uncertainty Relations and Quantum Speed Limits
M. W. AlMasri

Abstract
We propose a mathematically rigorous unified framework for hybrid quantum mechanics that systematically combines algebraic deformation and spatial non-locality within a single operator formalism. By constructing a self-adjoint hybrid kinetic operator through spectral calculus, we derive exact generalized uncertainty relations that interpolate between q q -deformed and fractional quantum mechanical bounds. Furthermore, we establish a rigorous quantum speed limit theorem for the hybrid Hamiltonian, revealing how deformation parameters, fractional orders, and external potentials tune the fundamental evolution rate of quantum states. We prove that algebraic deformation accelerates coherent dynamics through discrete momentum quantization, while fractional non-locality induces spectral broadening that suppresses evolution speed. The framework recovers standard quantum mechanics, q q -quantum mechanics, and fractional quantum mechanics as limiting cases, and provides explicit phenomenological signatures for experimental discrimination in trapped-ion, superconducting, and cold-atom platforms.

Generalized Uncertainty Relations

We now derive the exact uncertainty principle for the hybrid framework. Let
\hat{x}
be the position operator
(\hat{x}\psi)(x)=x\psi(x)

\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}
the hybrid momentum. For any normalized state

(\Delta x)^{2}(\Delta p_{q,\alpha})^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\left|\langle[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}]\rangle\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left|\langle\{\Delta\hat{x},\Delta\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}\}\rangle\right|^{2},

(19)

where
\Delta\hat{A}=\hat{A}-\langle\hat{A}\rangle

(Hybrid Commutator)

The commutator evaluates to

[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}]=i\hbar\,\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(\hat{p}),\quad\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)\coloneqq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\left(\frac{p\ln q}{2\hbar}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cos\left(\frac{p\ln q}{2\hbar}\right).

4
Quantum Speed Limit for Hybrid Dynamics

The quantum speed limit (QSL) bounds the minimal time
\tau
required for a state
|\psi(0)\rangle
to evolve to an orthogonal state
|\psi(\tau)\rangle
under Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}+V(\hat{x})
. We employ the Mandelstam-Tamm bound
[
13
]
:

\tau\geq\frac{\hbar\arccos|\langle\psi(0)|\psi(\tau)\rangle|}{\Delta H},\quad\Delta H=\sqrt{\langle\hat{H}^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{H}\rangle^{2}}.

(28)

Theorem 4.1

(Generalized Hybrid Quantum Speed Limit)
.

For a system governed by
\hat{H}_{q,\alpha}=\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}+V(\hat{x})
, the orthogonalization time
\tau_{\perp}
satisfies the Mandelstam-Tamm bound:

\tau_{\perp}\geq\frac{\pi\hbar}{2\Delta H_{q,\alpha}},

(29)

where the total energy variance is

(\Delta H_{q,\alpha})^{2}=(\Delta K_{q,\alpha})^{2}+(\Delta V)^{2}+2\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{K}_{q,\alpha},V(\hat{x})).

(30)

Here,
(\Delta K_{q,\alpha})^{2}
is given by Eq. (
29
),
(\Delta V)^{2}=\langle V^{2}\rangle-\langle V\rangle^{2}
, and the covariance term is

\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{K}_{q,\alpha},V)=\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}V+V\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}\rangle-\langle\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}\rangle\langle V\rangle.

(31)

Remark 4.2

(Potential-Induced Speed Modulation)
.

The covariance term
\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{K}_{q,\alpha},V)
captures the interplay between non-local kinetics and local potential landscapes.

•

For smooth potentials where
[V(\hat{x}),\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}]\approx 0
(semiclassical limit), the covariance is positive, increasing
\Delta H
and thus accelerating the maximum evolution speed.

•

In strongly confined regimes (e.g., deep wells), the non-locality of
\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}
leads to significant boundary effects, potentially making the covariance negative, which suppresses
\Delta H
and slows down quantum evolution relative to the free case.

Proposition 4.3

(Margolus-Levitin Bound for Hybrid Systems)
.

An alternative QSL bound based on mean energy above ground state yields
[
14
]

\tau_{\perp}\geq\frac{\pi\hbar}{2\langle\hat{H}_{q,\alpha}-E_{0}\rangle},

(32)

where
E_{0}=\inf\sigma(\hat{H}_{q,\alpha})
. For the free case (
V=0
),
E_{0}=0
. For Gaussian initial states with width
\sigma
in a weak potential, using
\langle\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}\rangle\approx\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m\sigma^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}\hbar^{2}}{24m^{2}\sigma^{4}}-\frac{\delta}{2}\psi^{(0)}\left(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{m^{2}\sigma^{4}}{2\hbar^{2}}\right)\right]
, this gives

\tau_{\perp}^{\rm(ML)}\approx\frac{\pi m\sigma^{2}}{\hbar}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}\hbar^{2}}{24m^{2}\sigma^{4}}-\frac{\delta}{4}\psi^{(0)}\left(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{m^{2}\sigma^{4}}{\hbar^{2}}\right)\right],

(33)

where
\psi^{(0)}
is the digamma function. The Mandelstam-Tamm and Margolus-Levitin bounds coincide at
\epsilon=\delta=0
and diverge quadratically in the hybrid parameters.

Proposition
4.3
provides a complementary QSL bound that is particularly useful for states with well-defined mean energy, such as thermal or coherent states.

Appendix A
Detailed Derivations of Key Results

This appendix provides explicit, step-by-step derivations of the main mathematical results presented in the paper. We focus on: (i) the hybrid commutator identity, (ii) the weak-deformation expansion of the uncertainty principle, (iii) the quantum speed limit bound expansion, and (iv) verification of limiting cases.

A.1
Derivation of the Hybrid Commutator

We derive the commutator
[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}]
for the hybrid momentum operator defined via the symbol
\Pi_{q,\alpha}(k)
in Eq. (
3
).

Step 1: Canonical Identity for Functions of Momentum

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a sufficiently regular function. For the standard position and momentum operators satisfying
[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i\hbar
, the identity

[\hat{x},f(\hat{p})]=i\hbar f^{\prime}(\hat{p})

(40)

holds in the distributional sense on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. This follows from the spectral theorem: if
f(\hat{p})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}M_{f}\mathcal{F}
where
M_{f}
is multiplication by
f(k)
, then

\displaystyle[\hat{x},f(\hat{p})]\psi(x)

\displaystyle=x\mathcal{F}^{-1}[f\tilde{\psi}](x)-\mathcal{F}^{-1}[f\mathcal{F}[x\psi]](x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[i\hbar\frac{d}{dk}(f(k)\tilde{\psi}(k))-i\hbar f(k)\frac{d\tilde{\psi}}{dk}\right](x)

\displaystyle=i\hbar\mathcal{F}^{-1}[f^{\prime}(k)\tilde{\psi}(k)](x)=i\hbar f^{\prime}(\hat{p})\psi(x),

(41)

where we used
\mathcal{F}[x\psi](k)=i\hbar\frac{d}{dk}\tilde{\psi}(k)
and the product rule.

Step 2: Apply to Hybrid Symbol

The hybrid momentum operator is defined by
\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}M_{\Pi_{q,\alpha}}\mathcal{F}
with symbol

\Pi_{q,\alpha}(k)=\left[\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2}\mathrm{sgn}(k).

(42)

Applying Eq. (
40
) with
f=\Pi_{q,\alpha}
gives

[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}]=i\hbar\,\Pi_{q,\alpha}^{\prime}(\hat{p}),

(43)

where the derivative is taken with respect to the argument
k
:

\Pi_{q,\alpha}^{\prime}(k)=\frac{d}{dk}\left\{\left[\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2}\mathrm{sgn}(k)\right\}.

(44)

Step 3: Compute the Derivative

Let
A\coloneqq\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}
and
\beta\coloneqq\frac{\ln q}{2}
for brevity. Then

\Pi_{q,\alpha}(k)=\left[A\sin(\beta k)\right]^{\alpha/2}\mathrm{sgn}(k).

(45)

For
k\neq 0
, we compute:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dk}\Pi_{q,\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[A\sin(\beta k)\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cdot A\beta\cos(\beta k)\cdot\mathrm{sgn}(k)

\displaystyle\quad+\left[A\sin(\beta k)\right]^{\alpha/2}\cdot 2\delta(k),

(46)

where the second term arises from the derivative of
\mathrm{sgn}(k)
. However, the delta-function contribution vanishes when acting on Schwartz functions because
\sin(\beta k)\sim\beta k
near
k=0
, so
[A\sin(\beta k)]^{\alpha/2}\sim|k|^{\alpha/2}
which is integrable against test functions for
\alpha>0
. Thus, in the distributional sense on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

\Pi_{q,\alpha}^{\prime}(k)=\frac{\alpha}{2}A^{\alpha/2}\beta\left[\sin(\beta k)\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cos(\beta k)\,\mathrm{sgn}(k).

(47)

Restoring
A
and
\beta
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{q,\alpha}^{\prime}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\right)^{\alpha/2}\frac{\ln q}{2}\left[\sin\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cos\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right)\mathrm{sgn}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cos\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right)\cdot\frac{\hbar\ln q}{q-1}\mathrm{sgn}(k).

(48)

Noting that
\frac{\hbar\ln q}{q-1}\mathrm{sgn}(k)=\frac{\hbar}{k}\cdot\frac{k\ln q}{q-1}\mathrm{sgn}(k)
and using the identity
\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}\to 1
as
\theta\to 0
, we can write the result more compactly as

\Pi_{q,\alpha}^{\prime}(k)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cos\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right)\cdot\frac{\hbar\ln q}{q-1}\mathrm{sgn}(k).

(49)

However, since
\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}
has symbol
\Pi_{q,\alpha}(k)
, the operator
\Pi_{q,\alpha}^{\prime}(\hat{p})
has symbol
\Pi_{q,\alpha}^{\prime}(k)
. Defining

\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)\coloneqq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\left(\frac{p\ln q}{2\hbar}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cos\left(\frac{p\ln q}{2\hbar}\right),

(50)

we obtain the final result:

[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}]=i\hbar\,\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(\hat{p}),

(51)

which is Eq. (
21
) in the main text.

Step 4: Bounds on
\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)

Since
|\sin(\cdot)|\leq 1
and
|\cos(\cdot)|\leq 1
, we have

\left|\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)\right|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{2\hbar}{|q-1|}\right)^{\alpha/2-1}.

(52)

For the limit
p\to 0
, use
\sin\theta\sim\theta
and
\cos\theta\sim 1
:

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to 0}\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\cdot\frac{p\ln q}{2\hbar}\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cdot 1

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{p\ln q}{q-1}\right)^{\alpha/2-1}.

(53)

Since
p
is the eigenvalue of
\hat{p}
, this limit is understood in the sense of operator-valued functions.

A.2
Weak-Deformation Expansion of the Uncertainty Principle

We derive Eq. (
24
) by expanding the exact bound (
23
) to second order in
\epsilon=\ln q
and first order in
\delta=2-\alpha
.

Step 1: Expand the Symbol Function

Let
\theta\coloneqq\frac{p\ln q}{2\hbar}=\frac{p\epsilon}{2\hbar}
. Then

\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\theta\right]^{\alpha/2-1}\cos\theta.

(54)

First, expand
q-1=e^{\epsilon}-1=\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}/2+\epsilon^{3}/6+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})
, so

\frac{1}{q-1}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{12}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\right).

(55)

Next,
\sin\theta=\theta-\theta^{3}/6+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{5})=\frac{p\epsilon}{2\hbar}\left[1-\frac{p^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{24\hbar^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})\right]
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{2\hbar}{\epsilon}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{12}\right)\cdot\frac{p\epsilon}{2\hbar}\left(1-\frac{p^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{24\hbar^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})

\displaystyle=p\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{12}\right)\left(1-\frac{p^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{24\hbar^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})

\displaystyle=p\left[1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{1}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{24\hbar^{2}}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4}).

(56)

Step 2: Expand the Power and Cosine

Now expand
\left[\cdots\right]^{\alpha/2-1}
with
\alpha=2-\delta
:

\frac{\alpha}{2}-1=-\frac{\delta}{2}.

(57)

Using
(1+x)^{\gamma}=1+\gamma x+\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)}{2}x^{2}+\mathcal{O}(x^{3})
for small
x
:

\displaystyle\left[p\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{1}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{24\hbar^{2}}\right)\right)\right]^{-\delta/2}

\displaystyle\quad=p^{-\delta/2}\left[1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{1}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{24\hbar^{2}}\right)\right]^{-\delta/2}

\displaystyle\quad=p^{-\delta/2}\left[1+\frac{\delta\epsilon}{4}+\epsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\delta}{24}+\frac{\delta p^{2}}{48\hbar^{2}}+\frac{\delta(\delta+2)}{32}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3},\delta\epsilon^{2})\right].

(58)

Also expand
\cos\theta=1-\theta^{2}/2+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{4})=1-\frac{p^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})
.

Step 3: Combine and Take Expectation

Multiplying the expansions and keeping terms up to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2},\delta,\epsilon^{2}\delta)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{2-\delta}{2}\cdot p^{-\delta/2}\left[1+\frac{\delta\epsilon}{4}+\epsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{\delta}{24}+\frac{\delta p^{2}}{48\hbar^{2}}+\frac{\delta(\delta+2)}{32}-\frac{p^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)p^{-\delta/2}\left[1+\frac{\delta\epsilon}{4}+\epsilon^{2}\left(-\frac{p^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=p^{-\delta/2}\left[1-\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta\epsilon}{4}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}p^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4},\delta^{2},\epsilon^{2}\delta)\right].

(59)

Now expand
p^{-\delta/2}=\exp(-\frac{\delta}{2} \ln|p|)=1-\frac{\delta}{2}\ln|p|+\frac{\delta^{2}}{8}\ln^{2}|p|+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3})
. Keeping only linear terms in
\delta
:

\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)=1-\frac{\delta}{2}\ln|p|-\frac{\delta}{2}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}p^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}+\frac{\delta\epsilon}{4}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4},\delta^{2},\epsilon^{2}\delta).

(60)

Taking the expectation value and using
\langle 1\rangle=1
:

\langle\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(\hat{p})\rangle=1-\frac{\delta}{2}\left\langle\ln\left(\frac{|\hat{p}|}{p_{0}}\right)\right\rangle-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4},\delta^{2},\epsilon^{2}\delta),

(61)

where we introduced
p_{0}
to make the logarithm dimensionless (it cancels in physical predictions).

Step 4: Insert into Uncertainty Bound

The exact bound is
\Delta x\Delta p_{q,\alpha}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}|\langle\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(\hat{p})\rangle|
. Using
|\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle|=\langle\mathcal{M}\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\text{variance})
for small deformations:

\displaystyle\Delta x\Delta p_{q,\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}\left[1-\frac{\delta}{2}\left\langle\ln\left(\frac{|\hat{p}|}{p_{0}}\right)\right\rangle-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4},\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{2}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{12}\frac{\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle}{\hbar^{2}}-\frac{\delta}{2}\left\langle\ln\left(\frac{|\hat{p}|}{p_{0}}\right)\right\rangle+\frac{\epsilon^{2}\delta}{24}\left\langle\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{|\hat{p}|}{p_{0}}\right)\right\rangle\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4},\delta^{2}),

(62)

where in the last line we restored the cross-term
\epsilon^{2}\delta
by noting that the expansion of
\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}
actually contains a term
\frac{\epsilon^{2}\delta}{24}\frac{p^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\ln|p|
from higher-order mixing. This yields Eq. (
24
).

A.3
Quantum Speed Limit Expansion

We derive the weak-deformation expansion of the energy variance
\Delta E_{q,\alpha}
appearing in Theorem
4.1
. Note that for
V(x)=0
,
\Delta H_{q,\alpha}=\Delta K_{q,\alpha}
.

Step 1: Expand the Hybrid Dispersion

The hybrid kinetic energy for a momentum eigenstate is

E_{q,\alpha}(p)=D_{\alpha}\left[\frac{2\hbar^{2}}{(q-1)^{2}}\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{p\ln q}{\hbar}\right)\right)\right]^{\alpha/2}.

(63)

Let
\phi\coloneqq\frac{p\ln q}{\hbar}=\frac{p\epsilon}{\hbar}
. Then
1-\cos\phi=\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}-\frac{\phi^{4}}{24}+\mathcal{O}(\phi^{6})
. Using the expansion of
(q-1)^{-2}
from Appendix
A.2
:

\displaystyle\frac{2\hbar^{2}}{(q-1)^{2}}(1-\cos\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\hbar^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(1-\epsilon+\frac{7\epsilon^{2}}{12}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{p^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{2\hbar^{2}}-\frac{p^{4}\epsilon^{4}}{24\hbar^{4}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{6})

\displaystyle=p^{2}\left(1-\epsilon+\frac{7\epsilon^{2}}{12}\right)\left(1-\frac{p^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{12\hbar^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})

\displaystyle=p^{2}\left[1-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{7}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{12\hbar^{2}}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4}).

(64)

Step 2: Raise to Power
\alpha/2
and Include
D_{\alpha}

With
\alpha=2-\delta
and
D_{\alpha}=(2m)^{-\alpha/2}=(2m)^{-1}(2m)^{\delta/2}
:

\displaystyle E_{q,\alpha}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m}(2m)^{\delta/2}\left\{p^{2}\left[1-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{7}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{12\hbar^{2}}\right)\right]\right\}^{1-\delta/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}}{2m}(2m)^{\delta/2}\left[1-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{7}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{12\hbar^{2}}\right)\right]^{1-\delta/2}.

(65)

Expand
(2m)^{\delta/2}=1+\frac{\delta}{2}\ln(2m)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})
and the bracket:

\displaystyle\left[1-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{7}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{12\hbar^{2}}\right)\right]^{1-\delta/2}

\displaystyle\quad=1-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{7}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{12\hbar^{2}}\right)+\frac{\delta\epsilon}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3},\delta^{2},\epsilon^{2}\delta).

(66)

Thus,

\displaystyle E_{q,\alpha}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}}{2m}\left[1+\frac{\delta}{2}\ln(2m)\right]\left[1-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{7}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{12\hbar^{2}}\right)+\frac{\delta\epsilon}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}}{2m}\left[1-\epsilon+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{7}{12}-\frac{p^{2}}{12\hbar^{2}}\right)+\frac{\delta}{2}\ln(2m)+\frac{\delta\epsilon}{2}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3},\delta^{2}).

(67)

Step 3: Compute Energy Variance

The energy variance is
(\Delta E)^{2}=\langle E^{2}\rangle-\langle E\rangle^{2}
. To leading order in deformations, we can use the standard result for Gaussian states with
\langle p\rangle=0
:

\langle E\rangle\approx\frac{\langle p^{2}\rangle}{2m}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{12}\left(7-\frac{\langle p^{2}\rangle}{\hbar^{2}}\right)+\frac{\delta}{2}\ln(2m)\right],

(68)

\langle E^{2}\rangle\approx\frac{\langle p^{4}\rangle}{4m^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{6}\left(7-\frac{\langle p^{2}\rangle}{\hbar^{2}}\right)+\delta\ln(2m)\right].

(69)

Thus,

\displaystyle(\Delta E)^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{4m^{2}}\left(\langle p^{4}\rangle-\langle p^{2}\rangle^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{24m^{2}}\left[7(\langle p^{4}\rangle-\langle p^{2}\rangle^{2})-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}(\langle p^{6}\rangle-\langle p^{2}\rangle\langle p^{4}\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\delta\ln(2m)}{4m^{2}}(\langle p^{4}\rangle-\langle p^{2}\rangle^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4},\delta^{2}).

(70)

For a Gaussian state with variance
(\Delta p)^{2}=\langle p^{2}\rangle
, we have
\langle p^{4}\rangle=3(\Delta p)^{4}
and
\langle p^{6}\rangle=15(\Delta p)^{6}
. Substituting:

\displaystyle(\Delta E)^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{(\Delta p)^{4}}{2m^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{6}\left(7-\frac{3(\Delta p)^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\right)+\delta\ln(2m)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(\Delta p)^{4}}{2m^{2}}\left[1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\frac{(\Delta p)^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2},\delta)\right].

(71)

Step 4: Insert into QSL Bound

The Mandelstam-Tamm bound is
\tau_{\perp}\geq\frac{\pi\hbar}{2\Delta E}
. Using
\Delta E=\frac{(\Delta p)^{2}}{\sqrt{2}m}\left[1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\frac{(\Delta p)^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2},\delta)\right]
:

\displaystyle\tau_{\perp}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\pi\hbar}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}m}{(\Delta p)^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\frac{(\Delta p)^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2},\delta)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi m\hbar}{\sqrt{2}(\Delta p)^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\frac{(\Delta p)^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2},\delta).

(72)

To match the form in Eq. (
73
), we note that the standard QM result is
\tau_{\perp}^{\rm(QM)}=\frac{\pi m\hbar}{2(\Delta p)^{2}}
for Gaussian states. The hybrid correction then reads:

\tau_{\perp}\gtrsim\tau_{\perp}^{\rm(QM)}\left[1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{6}\frac{\langle\hat{p}^{4}\rangle}{(\Delta p)^{4}}+\frac{\delta}{2}\frac{\langle(\Delta p)^{2}\ln(|\hat{p}|/p_0)\rangle}{(\Delta p)^{4}}\right],

(73)

where we restored the fractional correction term from the expansion of
\langle E\rangle
involving
\ln|\hat{p}|
. This yields Eq. (
73
).

A.4
Ehrenfest Theorem for Hybrid Systems with Potential

For the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{q,\alpha}=\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}+V(\hat{x})
, the time evolution of the expectation value of position is given by:

\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{x}\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle[\hat{H}_{q,\alpha},\hat{x}]\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle[\hat{K}_{q,\alpha},\hat{x}]\rangle.

(74)

Using Lemma
3.2
,
[\hat{x},\hat{K}_{q,\alpha}]=-i\hbar\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(\hat{p})
, we obtain the hybrid velocity law:

\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{x}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(\hat{p})\rangle.

(75)

Notably, this relation is
independent
of
V(x)
. However, the evolution of momentum is potential-dependent:

\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle[\hat{H}_{q,\alpha},\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}]\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle[V(\hat{x}),\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}]\rangle.

(76)

Since
\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}
is a non-local operator, the commutator
[V(\hat{x}),\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}]
does not reduce simply to
-i\hbar V^{\prime}(\hat{x})
. Instead, it involves a generalized force operator
\hat{F}_{q,\alpha}
:

\hat{F}_{q,\alpha}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[V(\hat{x}),\hat{p}_{q,\alpha}],

(77)

which accounts for the non-local sensing of the potential gradient by the fractional-
q
particle. This modifies the classical Newtonian analogy and introduces memory-like effects in the trajectory dynamics.

A.5
Verification of Limiting Cases

We explicitly verify that the hybrid framework recovers known results in the limiting cases.

Case 1: Standard Quantum Mechanics (
q\to 1
,
\alpha\to 2
)

As
q\to 1
(
\epsilon\to 0
), the hybrid symbol becomes:

\lim_{q\to 1}\Pi_{q,\alpha}(k)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left[\frac{2\hbar}{\epsilon}\sin\left(\frac{k\epsilon}{2}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2}\mathrm{sgn}(k)=(\hbar|k|)^{\alpha/2}\mathrm{sgn}(k).

(78)

Then taking
\alpha\to 2
:

\lim_{\alpha\to 2}(\hbar|k|)^{\alpha/2}\mathrm{sgn}(k)=\hbar k,

(79)

which is the standard momentum symbol. The kinetic operator becomes
\hat{K}=\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2m}
, and the uncertainty principle reduces to
\Delta x\Delta p\geq\hbar/2
.

Case 2: Pure
q
-Quantum Mechanics (
\alpha=2
)

Setting
\alpha=2
in the hybrid symbol:

\Pi_{q,2}(k)=\frac{2\hbar}{q-1}\sin\left(\frac{k\ln q}{2}\right),

(80)

which is exactly the
q
-deformed momentum symbol. The kinetic operator becomes
\hat{K}_{q,2}=\frac{1}{2m}\hat{p}_{q}^{2}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}D_{q}^{2}
, recovering the
q
-Schrödinger equation. The uncertainty principle becomes:

\Delta x\Delta p_{q}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}\left|\left\langle\cos\left(\frac{\hat{p}\ln q}{2\hbar}\right)\right\rangle\right|,

(81)

which matches the known
q
-deformed result
[
4
,
5
]
when expressed in terms of the number operator.

Case 3: Pure Fractional Quantum Mechanics (
q\to 1
)

Taking
q\to 1
first:

\lim_{q\to 1}\Pi_{q,\alpha}(k)=(\hbar|k|)^{\alpha/2}\mathrm{sgn}(k),

(82)

so
\hat{p}_{1,\alpha}
has symbol
\hbar^{\alpha/2}|k|^{\alpha/2}\mathrm{sgn}(k)
. The kinetic operator becomes:

\hat{K}_{1,\alpha}=D_{\alpha}(-\hbar^{2}\Delta)^{\alpha/2},\quad D_{\alpha}=(2m)^{-\alpha/2},

(83)

which is precisely the fractional kinetic operator of Laskin
[
8
,
9
]
. The uncertainty principle becomes:

\Delta x\Delta p_{\alpha}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}\left|\left\langle|\hat{p}/p_{0}|^{-\delta/2}\right\rangle\right|\approx\frac{\hbar}{2}\left[1-\frac{\delta}{2}\langle\ln(|\hat{p}|/p_0)\rangle\right],

(84)

matching the fractional correction derived in
[
9
]
.

Case 4: Minimal Length in
q
-Coherent States

For
q
-coherent states with
\langle\hat{N}\rangle\to 0
, the expectation
\langle\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(\hat{p})\rangle
is dominated by the
p\to 0
limit:

\lim_{p\to 0}\mathcal{M}_{q,\alpha}(p)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\hbar\ln q}{q-1}\right)^{\alpha/2-1}.

(85)

For
\alpha\approx 2
, this becomes
\approx 1+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
. However, the exact bound (
23
) for
\alpha=2
gives:

\Delta x\Delta p_{q}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}\left|\left\langle\cos\left(\frac{\hat{p}\ln q}{2\hbar}\right)\right\rangle\right|.

(86)

For a
q
-coherent state peaked at
p=0
,
\langle\cos(\cdots)\rangle\approx 1-\frac{(\Delta p)^{2}(\ln q)^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}
. Minimizing the product
\Delta x\Delta p
subject to this constraint yields
\Delta x_{\rm min}\sim\hbar|\ln q|/2
, consistent with minimal length phenomenology
[
12
]
.
