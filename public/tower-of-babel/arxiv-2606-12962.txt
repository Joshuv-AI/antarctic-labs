The reverse mathematics of the Ordered Variable Word theorem
Lu Liu, Ludovic Patey

Abstract
In this article, we study the reverse mathematics of variable word theorems used in the proof of the Dual Ramsey theorem. We prove that the Ordered Variable Word theorem does not imply Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and that every computable instance admits a solution of low 2 degree. This is used to prove the Carlson-Simpson Lemma and the Open Dual Ramsey theorem over 𝖠𝖢𝖠 0 \mathsf{ACA}_{0} , thereby answering some 40-years old open questions. These results have consequences in the reverse mathematics of structural Ramsey theory.

Introduction

Celebrated Ramsey’s theorem
[
43
]
(
\mathsf{RT}^{n}
) states the existence, for every
k
-coloring of
[\mathbb{N}]^{n}
,
of an infinite set
H\subseteq\mathbb{N}
on which
[H]^{n}
is monochromatic, for
k,n\geq 1
. Here, we write
[X]^{n}
for the collection of all
n
-subsets of
X
. In 1984, Carlson and Simpson
[
5
]
proved a dual version of Ramsey’s theorem, in which
k
-colorings of
\mathbb{N}
are colored instead of
[\mathbb{N}]^{n}
.
Given
\alpha\leq\omega
, let
(\mathbb{N})^{\alpha}
denotes the class of partitions of
\mathbb{N}
into exactly
\alpha
non-empty parts, and given
p\in(\mathbb{N})^{\omega}
, let
(p)^{\alpha}
denotes the class of coarsenings of
p
into exactly
\alpha
many parts, that is, given
q\in(p)^{\alpha}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{N}
, if
p(x)=p(y)
, then
q(x)=q(y)
.

Theorem 1.1

(Dual Ramsey Theorem
[
5
]
)
.

For all finite
k,\ell\geq 1
, for every Borel
\ell
-coloring of
(\mathbb{N})^{k}
,
there exists some
p\in(\mathbb{N})^{\omega}
such that
(p)^{k}
is monochromatic.

We write
\mathsf{DRT}^{k}
and
\mathsf{ODRT}^{k}
for the Dual Ramsey theorem restricted to Borel and open colorings of
(\mathbb{N})^{k}
, respectively. The core combinatorial content of the Open Dual Ramsey theorem is known as the
Carlson-Simpson Lemma
and can be formulated in terms of variable word theorems.

A finite or infinite
word
over a finite alphabet
A
is a map
w:\alpha\to A
for some
\alpha\leq\omega
.
We write
|w|
for its
length

\alpha
. Given
\beta\leq\omega
, a
\beta
-variable word over
A
is a word
w
over the alphabet
A\sqcup\{x_{i}:i<\beta\}
where each
x_{i}
appears at least once, and the first occurrence of
x_{i}
appears before the first occurrence of
x_{i+1}
. We write
A^{\alpha,\beta}
and
A^{<\alpha,\beta}
for the sets of all
\beta
-variable word of length
\alpha
, and smaller than
\alpha
, respectively. Given
\gamma\leq\beta\leq\omega
, a
\beta
-variable word
w
over
A
and a
\gamma
-variable word
u
of length
|u|\leq\beta
, we write
w[u]
for the
\gamma
-variable word over
A
where each occurrence of
x_{j}
is replaced with
u(j)
, and cut before the first occurrence of
x_{|u|}
. If
|u|=\beta
, then
|w[u]|=|w|
.

Lemma 1.2

(Carlson-Simpson Lemma
[
5
]
)
.

Fix a finite alphabet
A
and some
n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
\ell
-coloring of
A^{<\omega,n}
,
there exists an
\omega
-variable word
W
over
A
such that
\{W[u]:u\in A^{<\omega,n}\}
is monochromatic.

We write
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
for the Carlson-Simpson Lemma restricted to
\ell
-colorings of
A^{<\omega,n}
for any finite alphabet
A
and any
\ell\geq 1
. Since then, the Carlson-Simpson Lemma found other applications in proving the existence of finite big Ramsey degree in structural Ramsey theory
[
29
]
, such as reproving a result by Dobrinen
[
12
]
for the triangle-free Henson graph.
The Carlson-Simpson Lemma admits an inductive proof along
n
which is similar to the proof of Ramsey’s theorem. For the base case, Carlson and Simpson
[
5
, Theorem 6.3]
proved a stronger statement in terms of
ordered
variable words. Given
\beta\leq\omega
, an ordered
\beta
-variable word over
A
is a word
w
over the alphabet
A\sqcup\{x_{i}:i<\beta\}
where each
x_{i}
appears at least once, and the
last
occurrence of
x_{i}
appears before the first occurrence of
x_{i+1}
. Note that contrary to the unordered version, each variable kind appears only finitely often in an ordered
\omega
-variable word.

Theorem 1.3

(Ordered Variable Word theorem
[
5
]
)
.

Fix a finite alphabet
A
and some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
\ell
-coloring of
A^{<\omega,0}
,
there exists an ordered
\omega
-variable word
W
over
A
such that
\{W[u]:u\in A^{<\omega,0}\}
is monochromatic.

We write
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
for the Ordered Variable Word theorem restricted to
\ell
-colorings of
A^{<\omega,0}
for any finite alphabet
A
and any
\ell\geq 1
. There exists actually an ordered counterpart of the Carlson-Simpson Lemma proven by Carlson
[
6
]
but for which no elementary proof is known.

We are interested in the meta-mathematics of the Dual Ramsey theorem and its core lemmas from the viewpoint of computability theory and reverse mathematics.

1.1
Reverse mathematics

Reverse mathematics is a foundational program started by Harvey Friedman, whose goal is to find optimal axioms to prove ordinary theorems. It uses the framework of subsystems of second-order arithmetic, with a base theory,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, capturing
computable mathematics
. More precisely,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
consists of the axioms of a discrete ordered commutative semiring, together with the
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-induction scheme and the
\Delta^{0}_{1}
-comprehension scheme. See Simpson
[
50
]
or Dzhafarov and Mummert
[
17
]
for a reference in reverse mathematics. Overall this article, we shall be interested in the following subsystems, which are listed in increasing axiomatic strength:

•

\mathsf{WKL}_{0}
, standing for
Weak König’s lemma
, is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
augmented with the statement “Every infinite binary tree admits an infinite path”. It can be thought of as capturing compactness arguments.

•

\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
, standing for
Arithmetic Comprehension Axiom
, is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
together with the comprehension scheme for arithmetic formulas (where set parameters are allowed). It is a
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative extension of Peano arithmetic. From a computability-theoretic viewpoint, it can be equivalently defined as
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
augmented with the statement “
\forall X\exists Y(Y=X^{\prime})
”, where
X^{\prime}=\{e:\Phi^{X}_{e}(e)\downarrow\}
is the Turing jump of
X
.

•

\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{\prime}
is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
augmented with the statement
\forall n\forall X\exists Y(Y=X^{(n)})
,
where
X^{(n)}
is the
n
th iterate of the Turing jump, defined by
X^{(0)}=X
and
X^{(n+1)}=(X^{(n)})^{\prime}
.
By Yokoyama
[
54
]
(see also
[
39
]
),
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{\prime}
is equivalent to the
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-reflection principle for
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
, which is the statement “every
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-formula provable in
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
is true”.

•

\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{+}
is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
augmented with the statement
\forall X\exists Y(Y=X^{(\omega)})
, where
X^{(\omega)}=\bigoplus_{n}X^{(n)}
is the
\omega
-jump of
X
. Given the Cantor pairing function
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}
, the operator
\bigoplus_{n}X_{n}=\{\langle a,b\rangle:a\in X_{n}\rangle\}
is the effective union of the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. The system
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{+}
was popularized as being the weakest system known to prove Hindman’s theorem.

•

\Pi^{1}_{1}\mathsf{-CA}_{0}
is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
augmented with the comprehension scheme for
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-formulas. As such, it is an impredicative system of extremely high axiomatic strength compared to the systems above.

We shall mostly study
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problems
, which are
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-sentences of the form

\mathsf{P}=\forall X[\Phi(X)\to\exists Y\Psi(X,Y)]

where
\Phi
and
\Psi
are arithmetic formulas. A
\mathsf{P}
-instance
is any set
X
such that
\Phi(X)
holds,
and a
\mathsf{P}
-solution to
X
is a set
Y
such that
\Psi(X,Y)
holds. For example, an
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
-instance
is a coloring
f:A^{<\omega,0}\to\ell
for a finite alphabet
A
and some
\ell\geq 1
. An
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
-solution
to
f
is an ordered
\omega
-variable word
W
such that
\{W[u]:u\in A^{<\omega,0}\}
is monochromatic.

In their article, Carlson and Simpson
[
5
]
asked about the proof-theoretic and computability-theoretic strength of the Dual Ramsey theorem and its combinatorial lemmas. In an unpublished paper, Slaman
[
51
]
noticed that
\mathsf{DRT}^{k}_{\ell}
holds over
\Pi^{1}_{1}\mathsf{-CA}_{0}
. Miller and Solomon
[
37
]
proved that
\mathsf{ODRT}^{k+1}
implies
\mathsf{RT}^{k}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, hence that
\mathsf{ODRT}^{4}
implies
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. They also proved that
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
is not provable over
\mathsf{WKL}_{0}
, even for binary alphabets and 2-colorings. Erhard
[
20
]
proved that
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
is not provable by Ramsey’s theorem for pairs restricted to stable colorings over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. Dzhafarov, Flood, Solomon and Westrick
[
18
]
studied extensively the Dual Ramsey theorem and its variable word lemmas.
They showed in particular that the full statement of the Dual Ramsey theorem is sensitive to representation, in terms of Borel or Baire codes. Liu, Monin and Patey
[
35
]
proved
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
for binary alphabets and constructed a computable 2-coloring of
\{0,1\}^{<\omega,0}
with no
\Delta^{0}_{2}

\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
-solution. More recently, Anglès d’Auriac, Mignoty, Liu and Patey
[
1
]
proved two versions of
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
: a piecewise syndetic and a density version, the latter being based on Dodos, Kanellopoulos and Tyros
[
14
]
. They deduced that the Carlson-Simpson Lemma holds over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{+}
.

1.2
Main contributions

In this article, we give a new proof of
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
in the style of Towsner
[
53
]
, and derive from its combinatorics a new parametric notion of effective forcing for building solutions to
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
with a good first-jump control. Thanks to this notion of forcing, we prove the following theorem:

Main Theorem 1.4
.

\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
implies neither
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
nor
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
.

Moreover, such a separation is witnessed by an
\omega
-model
, that is, a structure
\mathcal{M}=(M,S,0,1,+,\cdots,<)
in which the first-order part consists of the standard integers
\omega
, equipped with the natural operations and order. This answers partially
[
38
, Question 11]
and
[
37
, Problem 2]
and answers negatively
[
37
, Problem 1]
and
[
35
, Question 4.9]
.

Second, we prove that every computable instance of
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
admits a solution of low
2
degree
by constructing a
\emptyset^{\prime}
-computable 1-generic filter for this notion of forcing. Formalizing the construction over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
, we use this as the base case of an inductive proof
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
. We therefore obtain an exact characterization of the strength of the Carlson-Simpson Lemma and the Open Dual Ramsey Theorem.

Main Theorem 1.5
.

For every
n\in\omega
,
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
and
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{ODRT}^{n+2}
.
Moreover, a reversal holds for every
n\geq 2
.

This therefore gives a partial answer to the original question of Carlson and Simpson
[
5
]

and a complete answer to
[
37
, Problem 3]
. Based on the work of
Hubička
[
29
]
,
formalized in reverse mathematics by Anglès d’Auriac et al.
[
1
]
,
1.5
shows over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
that the triangle-free Henson graph has finite big Ramsey degree. Note that this fact was recently proven over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
by Cholak, Dobrinen, and Towsner
[
9
]
for every Henson graph, using a Milliken-style proof for coding trees, yielding exact bounds for finite big Ramsey degree contrary to the approach of
Hubička
[
29
]
.

Last, we complete the picture by showing that
\mathsf{CSL}^{1}
and
\mathsf{ODRT}^{3}
are logically weak, with the following theorem:

Main Theorem 1.6
.

Neither
\mathsf{CSL}^{1}
, nor
\mathsf{ODRT}^{3}
implies
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
.

1.6
is by far the most complicated part of the article, involving three non-trivial steps: we first prove that an infinite variant of the Hales-Jewett theorem admits so-called strong preservation of hyperimmunity, then use it to prove that the Ordered Variable Word theorem admits the same property, and last, we prove that a cohesive version of
\mathsf{CSL}^{1}
admits preservation of hyperimmunity.

1.6
shows that the statement of the indivisibility of the triangle-free Henson graph does not imply
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
, answering a question of Gill
[
22
]
and Cholak, Dobrinen and McCoy
[
8
, Question 48]
.

2
A simple proof of
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}

Anglès d’Auriac et al.
[
1
]
gave proofs of the piecewise syndetic and the density versions of the zero-dimensional Ordered Variable Word theorem, respectively, yielding two proofs of
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
. Both proofs were based on finitary versions of the respective statements, one of which was proven by Dodos et al
[

. In this section, we give an arguably more natural proof of

, using the Hales-Jewett theorem below as a bootstrap. The combinatorics will be then reused in later sections to show that
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}

[

(Hales-Jewett,
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
)

There exists a primitive recursive function

such that for every

For simplicity, given a finite alphabet

of size

for

.

3
Weakly low basis theorem for
\mathsf{OVW}

The levels of the arithmetic hierarchy are not closed under relativization, in the sense that for
n\geq 2
, the relation“
X
is
\Delta^{0}_{n}(Y)
” is not transitive. It is therefore useful to work with notions of lowness.
A set
X
is of
low
n
degree
over
Y
if
(X\oplus Y)^{(n)}\leq_{T}Y^{(n)}
. In particular, the relation “
X
is of low
n
degree over
Y
” is transitive. When
n=1
, we simply say low for low
1
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
admits a
low
n
basis
if for every set
Z
and every
Z
-computable
\mathsf{P}
-instance
X
,
there is a
\mathsf{P}
-solution
Y
to
X
such that
(Y\oplus Z)^{(n)}\leq Z^{(n)}
.

For instance, Jockusch and Soare
[
30
]
proved that
\mathsf{WKL}
admits a low basis. Some
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problems are “close” to admit a low basis, in that any PA degree over
\emptyset^{\prime}
can decide the jump of a solution. We then say that it admits a
weakly low basis
.

Definition 3.2
.

A
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
admits a
weakly low
n
basis
if for every set
Z
, every set
P
of PA degree over
Z^{(n)}
and every
Z
-computable
\mathsf{P}
-instance
X
,
there is a
\mathsf{P}
-solution
Y
to
X
such that
(Y\oplus Z)^{(n)}\leq P
.

For any
Z
, by the low basis theorem
[
30
]
relative to
Z^{(n)}
, there is a set
P
of PA degree over
Z^{(n)}
such that
P^{\prime}\leq_{T}Z^{(n+1)}
. It follows that if
(Y\oplus Z)^{(n)}\leq P
, then
(Y\oplus Z)^{(n+1)}\leq_{T}P^{\prime}\leq_{T}Z^{(n+1)}
, so if
\mathsf{P}
admits a weakly low
n
basis, then it admits a low
n+1
basis.
The most famous theorem admitting a weakly low basis
[
7
]
, but no low basis
[
32
]
, is Ramsey’s theorem for pairs.

Liu, Monin and Patey
[
35
, Corollary 4.5]
constructed a computable instance of
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
with no
\Delta^{0}_{2}
solutions, so
a fortiori
,
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
does not admit a low basis.
The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 3.3
.

\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
admits a weakly low basis.

As mentioned, if a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem admits a weakly low basis, then its admits a low
2
basis.

Corollary 3.4
.

\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
admits a low
2
basis theorem.

Proof.

Immediate by
Theorem

3.3
and by the low basis theorem
[
30
]
.
∎

\mathsf{ACA}_{0}

)

3.1
Notion of forcing

We now use the combinatorics of

Definition 3.8

\mathbb{P}
-branch

(v,W)

\omega

One can think of a

\displaystyle[v,W]=\big\{G\in\mathsf{OSub}(v\cdot W):\

\displaystyle v\preceq G\big\}

Note that, although an

3.2
Effective forcing

It is common in effective forcing to work with a restricted set of
\mathbb{P}
-conditions with computability-theoretic constraints. In our situation, we shall restrict ourselves to
\mathbb{P}
-preconditions
(I,W)
such that
W
belongs to a non-empty class of sets
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the following closure properties:

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is downward-closed under Turing reducibility:
\forall X\in\mathcal{C}\forall Y\leq_{T}X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
;

(2)

\mathcal{C}
is closed under PA degrees:
\forall X\in\mathcal{C}\exists Y\in\mathcal{C},Y
is of PA degree over
X
.

Any such class is called a
Scott class
. If one furthermore requires that
\mathcal{C}
is closed under the effective join, then we obtain the well-known notion of Scott ideal.
Any basis theorem for
\Pi^{0}_{1}
classes induces a Scott class. For instance, by the computably dominated and the low basis theorems
[
30
]
, the classes of all sets of computably dominated degree or of all low degree form Scott classes, respectively. None of the above examples form Scott ideals.

Definition 3.13
.

Given a Scott class
\mathcal{C}
, a
\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{C}}
-(pre)condition is a
\mathbb{P}
-(pre)condition
(I,W)
such that
W\in\mathcal{C}
.

To effectivize the construction of a filter, we shall be particularly interested in the class
\mathsf{LOW}
of all sets of low degree. Any
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{LOW}}
-precondition can be represented as a finite object as follows:

Definition 3.14
.

A
lowness index
of a set
X
of low degree is an integer
e
such that
\Phi_{e}^{\emptyset^{\prime}}=X^{\prime}
.
A
code
for a
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{LOW}}
-precondition
(I,W)
is a pair
\langle I,e\rangle
where
e
is a lowness index of
W
.

Note that
\langle I,e\rangle
is a finitary object, hence can be coded by an integer using any reasonable Gödel numbering. We shall prove the core lemmas of the notion of
\mathbb{P}
-forcing in the general case, but one should keep in mind that they will all hold even when restricting
\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{C}}
for any fixed Scott class. Furthermore, when proving a density lemma, we will always specify the amount of computational power needed to transform a code of a
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{LOW}}
-precondition into a code of the desired
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{LOW}}
-extension.

3.3
First-jump control

Given a notion of forcing, there exists a general forcing relation defined as follows:
p

forces
a property
\varphi(G)
if
\varphi(G_{\mathcal{F}})
holds for every sufficiently generic filter
\mathcal{F}
containing
p
, where
G_{\mathcal{F}}
is the set induced by the filter
\mathcal{F}
. Although this forcing relation also admits a simple inductive and syntactical definition, the complexity of the forcing relation often remains too high with respect to the complexity of the forced formula.

For our purpose, it will be sufficient to force
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
and
\Pi^{0}_{1}
formulas. At this level of the arithmetic hierarchy, there is often a stronger forcing relation which does not only hold for any sufficiently generic filter, but for
every
filter containing
p
. We now give a formal definition of a forcing relation for
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
and
\Pi^{0}_{1}
-formulas.

By Kleene’s normal form theorem, every
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-formula
\varphi(G)
can be written of the form
\exists x\psi(G\upharpoonright_{x})
, where
\psi(\sigma)
is a
monotonous

\Delta^{0}_{0}
-formula, that is, if
\psi(\sigma)
holds and
\sigma\preceq\tau
, then
\psi(\tau)
also holds. We will always assume that a
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-formula is in normal form.

Definition 3.15

(Forcing relation)
.

Let
(v,W)
be a
\mathbb{P}
-branch and
\varphi(G):=\exists n\psi(G\upharpoonright n)
be a
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-formula. Let

(1)

(v,W)\Vdash\varphi(G)

iff
\psi(v\upharpoonright n)
holds for some
n
;

(2)

(v,W)\Vdash\neg\varphi(G)
iff
for every
\hat{v}\in[v,W]
, every

,
\neg\psi(\hat{v}\upharpoonright n)
holds.

We also say
(v,W)

forces

\varphi(G)
for
(v,W)\Vdash\varphi(G)
.
A
\mathbb{P}
-precondition
(I,W)

decides
a
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-formula
\varphi(G)
iff
for every
v\in I
, either
(v,W)\Vdash\varphi(G)
or
(v,W)\Vdash\neg\varphi(G)
.

Suppose
(v,W)\Vdash\varphi(G)
for some
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
or
\Pi^{0}_{1}
-formula
\varphi(G)
, then
\varphi(G)
holds for every

.
It is also clear that if
(v,W)\Vdash\varphi(G)
, then for every
(\hat{v},\hat{W})\leq(v,W)

(\hat{v},\hat{W})\Vdash\varphi(G)
. Indeed,
[\hat{v},\hat{W}]\subseteq[v,W]
.

A
\mathbb{P}
-filter
\mathcal{F}
is

if for every
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-formula
\varphi(G)
, there is a condition

deciding
\varphi(G)
. It is not clear at first sight that every sufficiently generic

-filter is 1-generic. We shall however see that this is the case. For this, we define the so-called

for
\Sigma^{0}_{1}

In general, a forcing question is a relation
p\operatorname{{?}{\vdash}}\varphi(G)
between a condition
p
and a formula
\varphi(G)
, such that if
p\operatorname{{?}{\vdash}}\varphi(G)
, then there is an extension

, and if

forcing

. The computability-theoretic features of a generic set are closely related to the existence of a forcing question with appropriate definability and combinatorial properties
[
40
]
. In our case, since we work with a tree-like notion of forcing, we need to adapt the definition.

Recall that

Definition 3.16

-precondition and

4
Preservation of hyperimmunities

The halting set being
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-definable, every
\omega
-model of
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
contains
\emptyset^{\prime}
.
It follows that to prove that a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
does not imply
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, it suffices
to show the existence of an
\omega
-model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{P}
which does not contain
\emptyset^{\prime}
.
Actually, most statements
\mathsf{P}
such that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{P}\nvdash\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
are shown to satisfy a stronger property, called
cone avoidance
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
admits
cone avoidance
if for every set
Z
, every set
C\not\leq_{T}Z
and every
Z
-computable
\mathsf{P}
-instance
X
, there is a
\mathsf{P}
-solution
Y
such that
C\not\leq_{T}Y\oplus Z
.

If a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
admits cone avoidance, then for every non-computable set
C
, there is an
\omega
-model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{P}
which does not contain
C
. This technique was used, for instance, by Seetapun and Slaman
[
47
]
to prove that Ramsey’s theorem for pairs is strictly weaker than
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
.

Later, the author
[
42
]
introduced the notion of
preservation of hyperimmunities
, as a refinement of cone avoidance to prove separations from other statements such as
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
, where
\mathsf{RT}^{n}_{k}
is the restriction of Ramsey’s theorem for
k
-colorings of the
n
-tuples. A function
h:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is
Z
-hyperimmune
if it is not dominated by any
Z
-computable function, where
g

dominates

h
means that
\forall xg(x)\geq h(x)
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
k\leq\omega
.
A
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
admits
preservation of
k
hyperimmunities
if for every set
Z
, every sequence
\langle h_{i}:i<k\rangle
of
Z
-hyperimmune functions and every
Z
-computable
\mathsf{P}
-instance
X
, there is a
\mathsf{P}
-solution
Y
such that for every
i<k
,
h_{i}
is
Y\oplus Z
-hyperimmune.

Both notions are related: Downey et al
[
15
]
proved that cone avoidance and preservation of 1 hyperimmunity coincide. On the other hand, for every
k
, there is a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem which admits preservation of
k
, but not
k+1
hyperimmunities. In particular,
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
admits preservation of 1 hyperimmunity, but the following classical argument shows that the bound is optimal:

Lemma 4.3

(
[
32
]
)
.

\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
does not admit preservation of 2 hyperimmunities.

Proof.

The
principal function

p_{X}
of an infinite set
X=\{x_{0}<x_{1}\dots\}
is the map
n\mapsto x_{n}
.
By
[
31
, Theorem 5.2]
, there exists a
\Delta^{0}_{2}

A
such that
p_{A}
and
p_{\overline{A}}
are both hyperimmune. By Shoenfield’s limit lemma
[
49
, Theorem 2]
, there exists a computable coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
such that for every

,
\lim_{y}f(x,y)
exists and equals
A(x)
.
Seeing

as a computable instance of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
, every infinite
f
-homogeneous set
H
is either a subset of

or of

, hence
p_{H}
dominates either
p_{A}
or
p_{\overline{A}}
.
∎

Preservation of
k
hyperimmunities is a particular case of a general notion of preservation of a weakness property.
One can show that if a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
preserves some weakness property, but another

-problem

(
[

]
)

Suppose that
\mathsf{P}
admits preservation of

hyperimmunities, but

does not.

\omega
-model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{P}

\mathsf{Q}

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{Q}
does not admit preservation of
k

of

such that for every
\mathsf{Q}

-hyperimmune. Since

5
Strong weakness of
\exists^{\infty}
-
\mathsf{IHJ}

Preservation of 1 hyperimmunity shows the inability, for a
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
, to encode in its computable instances sufficiently fast-growing functions that dominate some fixed hyperimmune function. There are some
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problems that are so weak that even arbitrary instances have this inability. This yields the notion of strong preservation of 1 hyperimmunity.

Definition 5.1
.

Let

A
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem
\mathsf{P}
admits

\langle h_{i}:i<k\rangle

-hyperimmune functions and every

\mathsf{P}
-instance

\mathsf{P}
-solution
Y
such that for every

,
h_{i}
is
Y\oplus Z
-hyperimmune.

The equivalence from Downey et al
[
15
]
between preservation of 1 hyperimmunity and cone avoidance extends to their strong counterparts. The most famous example of
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem admitting strong cone avoidance is Ramsey’s theorem for singletons (
\mathsf{RT}^{1}
), also known as the infinite pigeonhole principle (see Dzhafarov and Jockusch
[

]
). For many hierarchies of partition theorems

, there exists a deep link between strong preservation of properties for
\mathsf{P}^{n}
and preservation of properties for
\mathsf{P}^{n+1}
. This will be exemplified in
Section

8
to show that
\mathsf{CSL}^{1}
admits preservation of 1 hyperimmunity, and therefore does not imply
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
.

It is natural to ask whether
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
admits strong preservation of 1 hyperimmunity. While it is the case, the proof happens to be surprisingly intricate, and will be decomposed into two important steps. In this section, we will prove that an infinite version of the Hales-Jewett theorem admits strong preservation of
\omega
hyperimmunities. Then, in
Section

6
, this fact will be used to prove that
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
admits strong preservation of 1 hyperimmunity.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of the following theorem:

\omega

The study of this seemingly artificial statement is justified by its use in the proof of strong preservation of 1 hyperimmunity of

6

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem:

\omega
-variable word

This weaker notion of monochromaticity yields the following statement, that we shall refer to as

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\mathsf{LOVW}^{0}

\Pi^{1}_{2}

Before proving

Proof of

As usual, we prove it in its unrelativized form.

Through this section, fix a coloring

We shall construct

7

\mathsf{ACA}_{0}

Carlson and Simpson
[
5
]
used the Ordered Variable Word theorem as a pigeonhole principle
to prove inductively what is now known as the Carlson-Simpson Lemma.
As for Ramsey’s theorem, the proof consists in reducing the dimension
n+1
to the dimension
n
through the notion of
pre-homogeneous
space, which is a sub-space over which the coloring does not depend on the last dimension. In the case of the Carlson-Simpson Lemma, one can obtain a pre-homogeneous unordered
\omega
-variable word by sequentially applying the Ordered Variable Word. The previous best known upper bound for
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
being any PA over
\emptyset^{\prime}
, a pre-homogeneous
\omega
-variable word was computed using
\emptyset^{(\omega)}
and the proof was formalizable over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}^{+}
. In our case, thanks to the low
2
basis theorem for
\mathsf{OVW}^{0}
, pre-homogeneous
\omega
-variable words can be obtained over
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
.

The following proof is in the spirit of the original one
[
5
]
and uses the same definitions as
[
1
, Section 5]
. Only the meta-analysis based on
Theorem

3.5
differs.

Lemma 7.2

(
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
)
.

Fix a set

, a finite alphabet

\ell\geq 1

Let

\omega
-variable word,

-computable coloring and

\omega
-variable word
\hat{W}\leq_{|u|+1}W

for every

(2)

\hat{W}
is of low

Z
, that is,
(\hat{W}\oplus Z)^{\prime\prime}\leq_{T}Z^{\prime\prime}

Proof.

Let

Let

are part of the alphabet

for
\ell

By

, there is an (ordered)
\omega
-variable word

over

such that the set

For simplicity, we can assume that the variable set of

Let

.
∎

Lemma 7.3

(
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}

(1)

(2)

7.3
Open and Combinatorial Dual Ramsey theorem

Given
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}(k)
for the restriction of
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
to alphabets of size
k
.
Dzhafarov, Flood, Solomon and Westrick
[
18
]
introduced a the
Combinatorial Dual Ramsey theorem
(
\mathsf{CDRT}^{n}
) representing the combinatorial core of the Open Dual Ramsey theorem (
\mathsf{ODRT}^{n}
). Formulated in terms of unordered variable words theorems,
\mathsf{CDRT}^{n}
corresponds to the statement
\mathsf{CSL}^{n-1}(0)
. They proved that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\forall n(\mathsf{ODRT}^{n}\leftrightarrow\mathsf{CDRT}^{n})
. Anglès d’Auriac, Mignoty, Liu and Patey
[
1
]
proved that the statements
\forall n\mathsf{CDRT}^{n}
and
\forall n\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
are equivalent over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. We give a slight improvement:

Lemma 7.5

(
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
)
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}(1)
implies both
\mathsf{CDRT}^{n+2}
and
\mathsf{ODRT}^{n+2}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Dzhafarov, Flood, Solomon and Westrick
[
18
, Theorem 3.9]
,
\mathsf{CDRT}^{n+2}
and
\mathsf{ODRT}^{n+2}
are equivalent over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
. Furthermore,
\mathsf{CDRT}^{n+2}
is
\mathsf{CSL}^{n+1}(0)
, so it suffices to prove
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{CSL}^{n}(1)\to\mathsf{CSL}^{n+1}(0)
.

Let
f:\emptyset^{<\omega,n+1}\to\ell
be an instance of
\mathsf{CSL}^{n+1}(0)
.
It induces an instance
g:\{0\}^{<\omega,n}\to\ell
of
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}(1)
defined by
g(u)=f(x_{0}\cdot u[x_{i}\mapsto x_{i+1},0\mapsto x_{0}])
, where
u[x_{i}\mapsto x_{i+1},0\mapsto x_{0}]
means that all the occurrences of
x_{i}
are replaced with
x_{i+1}
and all the occurrences of the unique alphabet symbol
0
are replaced with
x_{0}
. By
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
, there is an unordered
\omega
-variable word
U
over
\{0\}
such that
\mathsf{USub}^{n,\star}_{\{0\}}(U)
is
g
-monochromatic for some color
c<\ell
. Let
W=x_{0}\cdot U[x_{i}\mapsto x_{i+1},0\mapsto x_{0}]
. Then
W
is an
\omega
-variable word over
\emptyset
such that
\mathsf{USub}^{n+1,\star}_{\emptyset}(W)
is
f
-monochromatic for color
c
. Indeed, given
v\in\mathsf{USub}^{n+1,\star}_{\emptyset}(W)
, since the alphabet is empty,
v=x_{0}\cdot v^{\prime}
for some
v^{\prime}\in\{x_{0}\}^{<\omega,n}

where the variable set is
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots\}
. Then, letting

.
∎

We can now prove our main theorem:

.

For every

8.1
Level Carlson-Simpson lemma

As in
Section

6
, instead of directly proving that
\mathsf{CSL}^{1}
admits preservation of 1 hyperimmunity, we shall actually consider a level version of the Carlson-Simpson lemma and prove that it admits preservation of
k
hyperimmunities simultaneously. Note that one could have proven
Theorem

8.1
without this level version, but it actually gives more information about the relation between the combinatorial features of
\mathsf{CSL}^{1}
and its computability-theoretic strength.

Definition 8.3
.

The
projection

\pi(w)
of an unordered
n
-variable-word
w
over an alphabet
A
, is the set
\{t_{0}<\cdots t_{n}\}
such that for
i<n
,
t_{i}
is the position of the first occurrence of the variable
x_{i}
in
w
, and
t_{n}=|w|
. Given a coloring
f:A^{<\omega,n}\to\ell
, an unordered
\omega
-variable word
W
is
level-homogeneous
for
f
if for every
u_{0},u_{1}\in\mathsf{USub}^{n,\star}_{A}(W)
such that
\pi(u_{0})=\pi(u_{1})
, we have
f(u_{0})=f(u_{1})
.

This notion of level-homogeneity is a natural generalization of
Definition

6.2
for unordered variable words. It induces the following Level Carlson-Simpson lemma:

Definition 8.4
.

\mathsf{LCSL}^{n}
is the
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problem whose instances are colorings
f:A^{<\omega,n}\to\ell
for a finite alphabet
A
and some
\ell\geq 1
. A
\mathsf{LCSL}^{n}
-solution
to
f
is an unordered
\omega
-variable word
W
which is level-homogeneous for
f
.

It is useful to think of
\mathsf{LCSL}^{n}
as a “Ramsey-free” version of
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
, in the sense that
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
is equivalent to
\mathsf{RT}^{n+1}\wedge\mathsf{LCSL}^{n}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, and we shall see that
\mathsf{LCSL}^{1}
does not imply
\mathsf{RT}^{2}
, even for 2-colorings. In what follows, given an unordered
\omega
-variable word
W
over
A
, we write
\pi(W)
for the set of all
t_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t_{i}
is the position of the first occurrence of
x_{i}
in
W
.

Lemma 8.5
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\forall n(\mathsf{CSL}^{n}\leftrightarrow\mathsf{LCSL}^{n}\wedge\mathsf{RT}^{n+1})
.

Proof.

Fix
n
. Suppose first
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
holds. Then
\mathsf{LCSL}^{n}
clearly also holds as it is a weakening of
\mathsf{CSL}^{n}
. Moreover, by
Lemma

7.5

\mathsf{ODRT}^{n+2}
holds, hence by Miller and Solomon
[
37
, Theorem 2.3]
,
\mathsf{RT}^{n+1}
holds.
Suppose now that
\mathsf{LCSL}^{n}
and
\mathsf{RT}^{n+1}
both hold. Let
f:A^{<\omega,n}\to\ell
be a coloring, for some alphabet
A
and some
\ell\geq 1
.
By
\mathsf{LCSL}^{n}
, there is an infinite
\omega
-variable word
W
such that for every
u_{0},u_{1}\in\mathsf{USub}^{n,\star}_{A}(W)
, if
\pi(u_{0})=\pi(u_{1})
, then
f(u_{0})=f(u_{1})
. Let
X=\pi(W)
and
g:[X]^{n+1}\to\ell
be defined by
g(F)=f(u)
, for any
u\in\mathsf{USub}^{n,\star}_{A}(W)
such that
\pi(u)=F
. Such a coloring is well-defined by choice of
W
. By
\mathsf{RT}^{n+1}
, there is an infinite
g
-homogeneous subset
Y\subseteq X
, for some color
c<\ell
. Let
\hat{W}
be an unordered
\omega
-variable word in
\mathsf{USub}^{\omega}_{A}(W)
such that
\pi(\hat{W})=Y
. Note that
\hat{W}
can be obtained from
W
by replacing any variable
x_{n}
for
n\in Y-X
with any fixed constant in
A
. Then, for any
u\in\mathsf{USub}^{n,\star}_{A}(\hat{W})
,
\pi(u)\in[Y]^{n+1}
, so
f(u)=g(\pi(u))=c
.
∎

We will prove the following theorem:

Theorem 8.6

\mathsf{LCSL}^{1}
admits preservation of
k
hyperimmunities for every
k\in\mathbb{N}

Before proving

8.6

8.1
:

Proof of
Theorem

By
Theorem

8.6

\mathsf{LCSL}^{1}
admits preservation of 1 hyperimmunity.
By Patey

8.2

In order to prove

Recall that given

Definition 8.7
.

Fix
A
and
\ell\geq 1
. Let
W
be an unordered
\omega
-variable word over
A
.
A coloring

if for every

and a color

Note that any stable coloring

Such a coloring is called the

of

.
Let
\mathsf{CCSL}^{1}
, standing for
Cohesive
\mathsf{CSL}^{1}
, be the statement “For every finite alphabet
A
, every
\ell\geq 1

9
Applications and conclusion

Besides the proof of the Dual Ramsey theorem, the main application of infinite variable word theorems comes from Structural Ramsey Theory, which studies partition theorems of mathematical structures. Given two structures
\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G}
, we write
{\mathbf{G}\choose\mathbf{F}}
for the set of all embeddings from
\mathbf{F}
to
\mathbf{G}
.
We write
\mathbf{F}\cong\mathbf{G}
iff
\mathbf{F}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{G}
.
Given integers
k,\ell\in\omega
, structures
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{C}
we write
\mathbf{C}\longrightarrow(\mathbf{A})^{\mathbf{B}}_{k,\ell}
for the following statement:

Definition 9.1
.

\mathbf{C}\longrightarrow(\mathbf{A})^{\mathbf{B}}_{<\omega,\ell}
: For any
k\geq 1
and any coloring
\chi:{\mathbf{A}\choose\mathbf{B}}\to k
, there is an embedding
g\in{\mathbf{A}\choose\mathbf{C}}
such that
{g(\mathbf{C})\choose\mathbf{B}}
uses at most
\ell
many colors.

For instance, Ramsey’s theorem can be reformulated as

(\mathbb{N},<)\to(\mathbb{N},<)^{\mathbf{n}}_{<\omega,1}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathbf{n}
is the ordered set
(\{0,\dots,n-1\},<)
.

Definition 9.2
.

An infinite structure
\mathbf{A}
has the
Ramsey property
if for every finite substructure
\mathbf{B}
, the relation
\mathbf{A}\to(\mathbf{A})^{\mathbf{B}}_{<\omega,1}
holds.

All the infinite structures
\mathbf{A}
that we shall consider in this section have a unique sub-structure
\mathbf{1}
of size 1, in which case any coloring of
{\mathbf{A}\choose\mathbf{1}}
corresponds to a coloring of the elements of
\mathbf{A}
. The Ramsey property of
\mathbf{A}
restricted to
\mathbf{1}
is called
indivisibility
of
\mathbf{A}
.

Definition 9.3
.

An infinite structure
\mathbf{A}
is
indivisible
if
\mathbf{A}\to(\mathbf{A})^{\mathbf{1}}_{<\omega,1}
holds, where
\mathbf{1}
is its unique sub-structure of size 1.

Many infinite structures do not admit the Ramsey property, but still enjoy a weaker partition theorem:

Definition 9.4
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be an infinite structure.
A finite sub-structure
\mathbf{B}
has
big Ramsey degree

\ell\in\mathbb{N}
if
\ell
is the least number such that the relation
\mathbf{A}\to(\mathbf{A})^{\mathbf{B}}_{<\omega,\ell}
holds, if it exists. We say that
\mathbf{A}
has
finite big Ramsey degree
if every finite sub-structure admits a big Ramsey degree.

Structural Ramsey theory gives a particular focus on Fraissé limits, such as the dense linear order with no endpoints
(\mathbb{Q},<)
, the Rado (or random) graph
\mathcal{R}
, and in our case, the universal
k
-clique-free Henson graph
\mathbb{H}_{k}
.
Each of the previous examples are known to admit a finite big Ramsey degree.
Such partition theorems are usually proven by coding the mathematical structures into trees, and applying a tree partition theorem. For instance, Devlin
[
10
]
and Sauer
[
45
]
used Milliken’s tree partition theorem to prove that
(\mathbb{Q},<)
and
\mathcal{R}
admit finite Big Ramsey degree, respectively. The exact big Ramsey degrees for the Rado graph were then characterized by Laflamme, Sauer and Vuksanovic
[
34
]

9.1

The big Ramsey degree of the Henson graphs were a longstanding open question.
In 1986, Komjáth and Rödl
