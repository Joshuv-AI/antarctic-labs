Exact analytical edge states in the extended Su–Schrieffer–Heeger model
P. A. Grizzi, A. A. Aligia, P. Roura-Bas

Abstract
We investigate the topology of the different phases of the extended Su–Schrieffer–Heeger (eSSH) model, which includes hopping processes between translationally inequivalent atoms beyond nearest neighbors. Exact analytical expressions for the edge states of a semi-infinite eSSH chain are derived, with wave functions that decay exponentially from the boundary with a unit-cell decay factor z z . From the winding number of the bulk Hamiltonian under periodic boundary conditions, we determine the topological phase diagram and establish the bulk–boundary correspondence: changes in the winding number coincide with bulk gap closings and with the condition | z | = 1 |z|=1 for the edge-state solutions. For finite chains, we further obtain analytical, approximate expressions for the low-energy edge states, which are shown to be highly accurate.

II
Extended Su–Schrieffer–Heeger (eSSH) model and its bulk winding number
\delta

One-dimensional two-band chiral models with winding number greater than one can be constructed by introducing long-range hopping processes beyond the simplest nearest-neighbor one. The simplest example is a generalization of the SSH model that includes hopping to the
j
-neighbor between atoms that are not equivalent under translational symmetry, with amplitudes
u_{j}
with
j\in\{1,\dots,M\}
, in addition to the intracell coupling
u_{0}
. The hopping amplitudes are assumed to be real, ensuring that the system preserves time-reversal symmetry, which, as discussed below, is crucial for determining the topological class of the model.

Note that with the choice of unit cell (AB instead of BA in Fig.
1
), the hoppings
u_{j}
with
j\geq 2
connect the second site of the unit cell only with the first site of a unit cell at the right,
while the first site is connected only with the second site of a unit cell at the left.
This ordering is inverted with the alternative choice of unit cell.

Following Ref.
li
and the choice of unit cell shown in Fig.
1
, the Bloch Hamiltonian matrix takes the form:

\displaystyle{H}(k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&Q(k)\\
\overline{Q(k)}&0\end{array}\right),

where
Q(k)=d^{1}(k)-id^{2}(k)=d(k)\mbox{exp}(-i\phi(k))
,
d(k)=|Q(k)|
and vanishes only at phase transitions, and
\overline{Q(k)}
is the complex conjugate of
Q(k)
. The real functions
d^{1}(k)
and
d^{2}(k)
are given by:

\begin{array}[]{l}d^{1}(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{M}u_{j}\mbox{cos}(jk)\\[8.0pt]
d^{2}(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{M}u_{j}\mbox{sin}(jk).\end{array}

(4)

The model possesses both time-reversal and particle–hole symmetries, with the corresponding symmetry operators squaring to the identity. It also exhibits the composite chiral symmetry (see Sec. III of Ref.
malakar
and Ref.
avron
). As a consequence, within the ten-fold way classification, the model belongs to class BDI of topological insulators, characterized by an integer
\mathbb{Z}
topological invariant
ten-fold-way
.

It is worth mentioning that, while the ten-fold way classification formally relies on the assumption of a sufficiently large number of bands, the winding number remains the appropriate topological invariant even in the case of chiral two-band models. Therefore, the homotopy classes of Bloch Hamiltonians in Eq. (
II
) can be labeled by their winding number. For further details, see the work of Avron and Turner in Ref.

Furthermore, the model exhibits an additional discrete symmetry: inversion through the center of each bond.

The topological invariant—the winding number—is given by:

\delta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dot{\phi}(k)dk,

(5)

where

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}(k)=\frac{\partial\phi(k)}{\partial k}=\frac{1}{(d(k))^{2}}\sum_{ij}\epsilon_{ij}d^{i}(k)\frac{\partial d^{j}(k)}{\partial k}.

(6)

The curve in the plane described by the point

is required for the model to be insulating. The value of

avron

For the deformations considered in this work, these correspond to smooth variations of the hopping parameters

entering Eq. (
4

The Hamiltonian is given by:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{0}|B_{j}\rangle\langle A_{j}|+u_{1}|B_{j}\rangle\langle A_{j+1}|+u_{2}|B_{j}\rangle\langle A_{j+2}|

(7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mbox{H.c.},

By a convenient change of sign of the states, we

Associated with this Hamiltonian, we define the projector operators onto the bulk,
P_{b}
, and the edge,
P_{e}
, as follows

\begin{array}[]{l}P_{b}=\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}P_{j}\\[8.0pt]
P_{e}=P_{1}+P_{2},\end{array}

(8)

with
P_{j}=|A_{j}\rangle\langle A_{j}|+|B_{j}\rangle\langle B_{j}|
. By definition
P_{b}+P_{e}=1

The origin of this separation can be understood as follows. Deep in the bulk, all hopping processes (both to the left and to the right) are allowed, whereas this is no longer the case near the boundary. The projector onto the bulk
P_{b}
excludes sites close to the boundary that would otherwise involve hoppings outside the system. This projection yields a locally translationally invariant Hamiltonian, allowing one, as we show below, to solve the projected
Schrödinger equation in terms of states analogous to Bloch waves, but with a complex wave vector
[see Eqs. (
11

The main idea is to split the Schrödinger equation,

H|\psi\rangle=E|\psi\rangle

\begin{array}[]{l}P_{b}H|\psi\rangle=EP_{b}|\psi\rangle\\[8.0pt]
P_{e}H|\psi\rangle=EP_{e}|\psi\rangle.\end{array}

(9)

In particular,

\displaystyle P_{e}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{0}\big(|A_{1}\rangle\langle B_{1}|+|B_{1}\rangle\langle A_{1}|+|A_{2}\rangle\langle B_{2}|+|B_{2}\rangle\langle A_{2}|\big)+

\displaystyle u_{1}\big(|B_{1}\rangle\langle A_{2}|+|A_{2}\rangle\langle B_{1}|+|B_{2}\rangle\langle A_{3}|\big)+

\displaystyle u_{2}\big(|B_{1}\rangle\langle A_{3}|+|B_{2}\rangle\langle A_{4}|\big)

\displaystyle P_{b}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H-P_{e}H.

(10)

We aim to solve the bulk eigenvalue equation of the Eq. (
9

\begin{array}[]{l}|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|A_{j}\rangle\\[8.0pt]
|\psi_{B}(z)\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|B_{j}\rangle.\end{array}

(11)

Note that

\displaystyle H|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}H|A_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|B_{j}\rangle+u_{1}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|B_{j-1}\rangle+

\displaystyle u_{2}\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|B_{j-2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(u_{0}+zu_{1}+z^{2}u_{2}\big)|\psi_{B}(z)\rangle.

(12)

However,

\displaystyle H|\psi_{B}(z)\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}H|B_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|A_{j}\rangle+u_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|A_{j+1}\rangle+

\displaystyle u_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|A_{j+2}\rangle

\displaystyle\big(u_{0}+\frac{u_{1}}{z}+\frac{u_{2}}{z^{2}}\big)|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle-

(13)

\displaystyle\big(\frac{u_{1}}{z}+\frac{u_{2}}{z^{2}}\big)|A_{1}\rangle-\frac{u_{2}}{z}|A_{2}\rangle.

By applying

\displaystyle P_{b}H|\psi_{B}(z)\rangle=

\displaystyle\big(u_{0}+\frac{u_{1}}{z}+\frac{u_{2}}{z^{2}}\big)P_{b}|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle-

\displaystyle\big(\frac{u_{1}}{z}+\frac{u_{2}}{z^{2}}\big)P_{b}|A_{1}\rangle-\frac{u_{2}}{z}P_{b}|A_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\big(u_{0}+\frac{u_{1}}{z}+\frac{u_{2}}{z^{2}}\big)P_{b}|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle.

V
Summary and discussion

We have employed analytical methods to study the extended Su–Schrieffer–Heeger model, which includes hopping to the
j
-neighbour between atoms that are not equivalent under translational symmetry, with real amplitudes
u_{j}
with
j\in\{0,\dots,M\}
.

We show that the resulting number of edge states is consistent with the bulk–boundary correspondence of topological phases. The off-diagonal structure of the Bloch Hamiltonian under periodic boundary conditions [Eq. (
II
)] allows one to compute the winding number,
which takes integer values between 0 and
M
.

To obtain analytical expressions for the edge states of a chain with open boundary conditions, we have restricted our study to the case
M=2
.
Nevertheless, for small values of
M
,
the procedure based on the method of Alase
et al.

alase-1
;
alase-2
;
aligia-2
can be straightforwardly extended by solving a polynomial equation of degree
M
.
This approach entails a significantly lower computational cost than standard numerical methods, particularly for long chains.

We have derived exact analytical expressions for the edge states of a chain
with open boundary conditions. Moreover, for finite chains we derive approximate analytical expressions for the low-energy edge states and their energies, which show excellent agreement with the numerical results of Ref.
li
. We also demonstrate that the approximation is highly accurate.

In Ref.
li2
, the authors tune the experimental setup along a one-parameter trajectory that connects a bulk sector with winding number two (a topologically nontrivial phase) to one with winding number zero (a trivial phase). We derive the corresponding edge states and analyze their structure along this path. Our results reveal nontrivial contributions from the different lattice sites to the spatial weight distribution of these states.

Our findings clarify the fundamental nature of the end states emerging in the extended Su–Schrieffer–Heeger model.

Appendix A
Orthonormalized edge states for winding number 2

In this appendix, we obtain a pair of orthonormalized edge states corresponding to a winding number
\delta=2
.
In particular, we show that these states are still present along the line
1-4\tilde{u}_{0}\tilde{u}_{2}=0
in Eq. (
26
).

In general,
z
is a complex number,
z=|z|e^{i\phi}
.
If
z
, with
|z|<1
, is a root of
Q_{1}(z)
of Eq. (
24
), where
\tilde{u}_{2}
is sufficiently large and positive, then
\overline{z}=|z|e^{-i\phi}
is also a root.
Therefore, we have the following two normalized edge states:

\displaystyle|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z^{j-1}|A_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}e^{i(j-1)\phi}|A_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\overline{z}^{j-1}|A_{j}\rangle

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}e^{-i(j-1)\phi}|A_{j}\rangle.

The norm is computed using the complex geometric series
\sum_{j=p}^{\infty}z^{j}=\frac{z^{p}}{1-z}
, valid for
|z|<1
.
The overlap of these states is the complex number

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi)}{1-2|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi)+|z|^{4}}

(38)

\displaystyle+i~\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(|z|^{2}\sin(2\phi)}{1-2|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi)+|z|^{4}},

with modulus

\displaystyle|\langle\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle|^{2}|=\frac{1-2|z|^{2}+|z|^{4}}{1-2|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi)+|z|^{4}}.

(39)

Note that over the curve
1-4\tilde{u}_{0}\tilde{u}_{2}=0
,
z=\overline{z}
and therefore
|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle=|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle
suggesting the presence of only one edge state. However, as we will see in what follows, there are two well defined edge states along that curve.
Now we introduce the sum and difference of
|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle
and
|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle
:

\displaystyle|\psi_{+}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle+|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}\cos((j-1)\phi)|A_{j}\rangle,

\displaystyle|\psi_{-}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle-|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle}{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}\sin((j-1)\phi)|A_{j}\rangle.

Their overlap is given by

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{-}(z)|\psi_{+}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2|z|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})\sin(2\phi)}{1-2|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi)+|z|^{4}}.

(41)

The leading term in the series expansion is of order
O(|z|^{2})
. Regarding their inner products, they are:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{+}(z)|\psi_{+}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2-2\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi))}{1-2|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi)+|z|^{4}}.

With this, we define the following normalized difference state:

\displaystyle|\widehat{\psi_{-}}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\psi_{-}(z)\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\psi_{-}(z)|\psi_{-}(z)\rangle}}

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{-}(z)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}\sin(j\phi)|A_{j+1}\rangle,

with

\displaystyle c^{2}_{-}(z)=\frac{2(1-|z|^{2})(1-2|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi)+|z|^{4})}{(1+|z|^{2})(1-\cos(2\phi))}.

(43)

Note that

\frac{\sin(j\phi)}{\sqrt{1-\cos(2\phi)}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{sgn}(\phi)\,\frac{j}{\sqrt{2}},

so in the limit
\phi\to 0
we may choose

\displaystyle|\widehat{\psi_{-}}(z)\rangle\big|_{\phi=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{1+|z|^{2}}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j|z|^{j-1}|A_{j+1}\rangle.

(44)

Similarly, the normalized sum state is:

\displaystyle|\widehat{\psi_{+}}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{+}(z)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}\cos((j-1)\phi)|A_{j}\rangle,

(45)

where

\displaystyle c_{+}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2(1-|z|^{2})}{1+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi))}{1-2|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi)+|z|^{4}}}}.

(46)

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\widehat{\psi_{+}}(z)\rangle\big|_{\phi=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}|A_{j}\rangle.

(47)

Note that, however, we can choose an orthogonal state to
|\widehat{\psi_{-}}(z)\rangle
by using the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization procedure:

\displaystyle|\psi^{\prime}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\widehat{\psi_{+}}(z)\rangle-\langle\widehat{\psi_{-}}(z)|\widehat{\psi_{+}}(z)\rangle|\widehat{\psi_{-}}(z)\rangle,

(48)

where

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\prime}(z)|\psi^{\prime}(z)\rangle=1-\lvert\langle\widehat{\psi_{-}}(z)|\widehat{\psi_{+}}(z)\rangle\rvert^{2}.

(49)

Explicitly:

\displaystyle|\widehat{\psi^{\prime}}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{+}(z)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}\cos((j-1)\phi)|A_{j}\rangle

(50)

\displaystyle-a(z)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z|^{j-1}\sin(j\phi)|A_{j+1}\rangle,

with

\displaystyle a(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{+}(z)c^{2}_{-}(z)\frac{2(1-|z|^{2})|z|^{2}\sin(2\phi))}{1+|z|^{4}-2|z|^{2}\cos(2\phi))}.

(51)

In particular, over the line
u_{1}^{2}=4u_{0}u_{2}
(
\phi=0
),
a(z)=0
and we obtain:

\displaystyle|\widehat{\psi^{\prime}}(z)\rangle\big|_{\phi=0}=|\widehat{\psi_{+}}(z)\rangle\big|_{\phi=0}.

(52)

Therefore, along that curve there are two orthonormalized edge states,
|\widehat{\psi_{+}}(z)\rangle\big|_{\phi=0}
and
|\widehat{\psi_{-}}(z)\rangle\big|_{\phi=0}
if
|z|<1
or none otherwise.

Appendix B
Edge states for a finite chain

For a chain of
N
unit cells, the Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N-2}\Big(u_{0}|B_{j}\rangle\langle A_{j}|+u_{1}|B_{j}\rangle\langle A_{j+1}|+u_{2}|B_{j}\rangle\langle A_{j+2}|\Big)

(53)

\displaystyle+u_{0}\Big(|B_{N-1}\rangle\langle A_{N-1}|+|B_{N}\rangle\langle A_{N}|\Big)

\displaystyle+u_{1}|B_{N-1}\rangle\langle A_{N}|+\mathrm{H.c.}

For simplicity we explain the formalism for the case with winding number 2 and
two complex conjugate states. Extension to other cases is straightforward.
The normalized states analogous to those in Eq. (
A
) are

\begin{array}[]{l}|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle=\sqrt{\dfrac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2N}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}z^{\,j-1}|A_{j}\rangle,\\[8.0pt]
|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle=\sqrt{\dfrac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2N}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{z}^{\,j-1}|A_{j}\rangle.\end{array}

(54)

Similar states, whose maximum weight is localized at the right end of the chain (a
B
site), are given by

\begin{array}[]{l}|\psi_{B}(z)\rangle=\sqrt{\dfrac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2N}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}z^{\,j-1}|B_{N-j+1}\rangle,\\[8.0pt]
|\overline{\psi}_{B}(z)\rangle=\sqrt{\dfrac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2N}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{z}^{\,j-1}|B_{N-j+1}\rangle.\end{array}

(55)

Note that if
|z|<1
, then

\sqrt{\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2N}}}\simeq\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}

for sufficiently large
N
.

Acting with the Hamiltonian on
|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle
, the contribution from the sum

\sum_{j=1}^{N-2}
in Eq. (
53
) produces a term proportional to
Q_{1}(z)
and therefore vanishes.
The remaining terms yield

\displaystyle H_{N}|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\Big(z^{N-1}u_{0}|B_{N}\rangle-z^{N}u_{2}|B_{N-1}\rangle\Big).

Similarly, for
|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle
, since
Q_{1}(\overline{z})=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{N}|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}\Big(\overline{z}^{N-1}u_{0}|B_{N}\rangle-\overline{z}^{N}u_{2}|B_{N-1}\rangle\Big).

As a result, the matrix elements are

\begin{array}[]{l}\langle\psi_{B}(z)|H_{N}|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle=\alpha_{1},\\[6.0pt]
\langle\psi_{B}(z)|H_{N}|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle=\overline{\alpha}_{2},\\[6.0pt]
\langle\overline{\psi}_{B}(z)|H_{N}|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle=\alpha_{2},\\[6.0pt]
\langle\overline{\psi}_{B}(z)|H_{N}|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle=\overline{\alpha}_{1},\end{array}

(56)

with
\alpha_{1}=z^{N-1}(1-|z|^{2})\big(u_{0}-|z|^{2}u_{2}\big)
, and
\alpha_{2}=z^{N-1}(1-|z|^{2})\big(u_{0}-z^{2}u_{2}\big)
.
The set

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{low}}=\big\{|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle,|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle,|\psi_{B}(z)\rangle,|\overline{\psi}_{B}(z)\rangle\big\}

defines a Hilbert subspace that is sufficient to describe, approximately yet accurately, the low-energy states localized at the edges. Following an argument similar to that of Ref.
aligia-2
, one can show that the effect of the remaining states in the full Hilbert space on the lowest-energy levels is of order
|z|^{2N}/\Delta_{g}

The matrix elements of the restriction of
H_{N}
to this subspace form the following matrix:

\displaystyle\left[H_{N}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{low}}}\right]=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&0&\overline{\alpha}_{1}&\overline{\alpha}_{2}\\
0&0&\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha_{1}&\overline{\alpha}_{2}&0&0\\
\alpha_{2}&\overline{\alpha}_{1}&0&0\end{array}\right).

Our goal is to solve the Schrödinger equation within this subspace,

H_{N}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{low}}}|\psi\rangle=E|\psi\rangle,

where

|\psi\rangle=a_{1}|\psi_{A}(z)\rangle+a_{2}|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle+b_{1}|\psi_{B}(z)\rangle+b_{2}|\overline{\psi}_{B}(z)\rangle.

Since the basis is non-orthogonal, the Schrödinger equation leads to the generalized eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\left[H_{N}\big|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{low}}}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
b_{1}\\
b_{2}\end{array}\right)=ES\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
b_{1}\\
b_{2}\end{array}\right),

where the overlap matrix is

\displaystyle S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&s&0&0\\
\overline{s}&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&s\\
0&0&\overline{s}&1\end{array}\right),

with

s=\langle\psi_{A}(z)|\overline{\psi}_{A}(z)\rangle=\langle\psi_{B}(z)|\overline{\psi}_{B}(z)\rangle
.

Equation (
B
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Xb\\
X^{\dagger}a\end{array}\right)=E\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Ya\\
Yb\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\overline{\alpha}_{1}&\overline{\alpha}_{2}\\
\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{1}\end{array}\right),\qquad Y=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&s\\
\overline{s}&1\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{array}\right),\qquad b=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b_{1}\\
b_{2}\end{array}\right).

Since
Y
is nonsingular, it follows that

\displaystyle Y^{-1}X^{\dagger}a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Eb,

\displaystyle Y^{-1}Xb

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ea.

(91)

Because
X=X^{\dagger}
, both vectors
a
and
b
satisfy the same equation,

h^{2}c=E^{2}c,

(92)

where
c=(c_{1},c_{2})^{T}
and
h=Y^{-1}X
.
Nontrivial solutions require
\det(h^{2}-E^{2})=0
, which determines the allowed values of
E^{2}
.

We now focus on the case where
z
is a complex solution of
Q_{1}(z)=0
.
In this situation,
\alpha_{1}=0
, since
u_{0}-|z|^{2}u_{2}=0
, independently of
u_{1}
.
Thus,

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\overline{\alpha}_{2}\\
\alpha_{2}&0\end{array}\right),\qquad Y^{-1}=\frac{1}{1-|s|^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-s\\
-\overline{s}&1\end{array}\right).

This yields

\displaystyle E^{2}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha_{2}|^{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_{2}^{2}s^{2})}{(1-|s|^{2})^{2}}

(93)

\displaystyle\pm\frac{\sqrt{\big(|\alpha_{2}|^{2}+\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_{2}^{2}s^{2})\big)^{2}+(1-|s|^{2})^{2}|\alpha_{2}|^{4}}}{(1-|s|^{2})^{2}}.

Note that since
\alpha_{2}
is of order
z^{N-1}

|E|
scale as
|z|^{(N-1)}

\sim|z|^{2N}/\Delta_{g}
decays much faster. Consequently,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{low}}
constitutes a good approximation
for the low-energy sector for a sufficiently long chain.
