Transmission electron microscopy of cultured cells: Optimized protocols for ultrastructural studies and post-embedding immunogold labeling
Authors: Anna Richert, Robert Lenartowski, Jolanta Nowak, Piotr Wasąg, Marta Lenartowska
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNanometer-scale localization of specific proteins within cells is essential for understanding their roles in diverse physiological and pathological cellular processes. Such high-resolution localization of protein epitopes can be achieved by combining transmission electron microscopy (TEM) with immunogold labeling. Several immuno-TEM approaches are available, including pre-embedding and post-embedding techniques, each offering distinct advantages and limitations when applied to cultured cells. One of the major challenges is preparation of cell culture samples embedded in resin in a manner that enables reliable ultrathin sectioning and efficient collection of sections. Here, we describe an optimized post-embedding immunogold labeling procedure for the localization of one or more proteins in both adherent and non-adherent cells cultured on plastic coverslips. This approach has been successfully applied in our previous studies and proved suitable for routine ultrastructural immunolabeling of cultured cells. Although the protocol is designed to achieve an optimal balance between the preservation of cellular ultrastructure and antigenicity, optimal results may still depend on the specific characteristics of the proteins under investigation and their localization within particular cellular compartments. Therefore, we also present a complementary procedure for the ultrastructural analysis of cultured cells that can support and extend immuno-TEM investigations.

Introduction

Immunogold transmission electron microscopy (immuno-TEM) combines the localization of specific proteins using antibodies with detailed visualization of cellular ultrastructure. This approach is therefore essential for understanding how proteins function in diverse cellular processes, including gene expression, cell signaling, motility, and intracellular trafficking. Notably, immuno-TEM remains one of the best morphological techniques providing sufficient spatial resolution to allow precise localization of proteins within nuclear and cytoplasmic compartments, as well as to evaluate their association with the nucleus, cellular organelles, or cytoskeletal structures. Several immuno-TEM strategies have been developed, including post-embedding and pre-embedding approaches, each offering specific advantages and limitations. In post-embedding protocols, samples are typically fixed with a relatively weak fixative (usually 4% formaldehyde, as recommended for immunocytochemistry), embedded in a low-viscosity resin, and sectioned into ultrathin sections [1, 2]. Immunolabeling is then performed on the sections using conventional gold-conjugated antibodies with particle sizes typically ranging from 5 to 30 nm. In contrast, pre-embedding protocols involve initial fixation with formaldehyde followed by membrane permeabilization (usually with Triton X-100) and immunostaining using ultrasmall gold conjugates (approximately 0.8–1.4 nm), which are subsequently visualized by silver or gold enhancement [2, 3]. The samples are then post-fixed with a strong cross-linking fixative (typically 1%–2% glutaraldehyde and 1%–2% osmium tetroxide), embedded in epoxy resin, and sectioned into ultrathin sections. While the post-embedding approaches generally provide stronger and more accessible immunolabeling, pre-embedding methods are superior in preserving cellular ultrastructure. Both immuno-TEM strategies theoretically allow the simultaneous detection of multiple antigens in cells by using primary antibodies raised in different species, which can then be visualized with species-specific secondary antibodies. In practice, however, the post-embedding immunogold method enables more precise co-localization of two distinct protein epitopes by using gold particles of different sizes (e.g. 10 and 20 nm) to label species-specific secondary antibodies (from different animals) that bind to corresponding primary antibodies. In the pre-embedding approach, double labeling is achieved by sequential incubation with species-specific ultrasmall gold conjugates. First, samples are incubated with the first ultrasmall gold conjugate, followed by silver or gold enhancement, and then with a second ultrasmall gold conjugate, followed by additional silver or gold enhancement [4]. This procedure is expected to generate two distinct populations of particles: smaller particles that have undergone a single round of enhancement and larger particles that have undergone two rounds of enhancement. In practice, however, the signal amplification process used in both single- and double-labeling pre-embedding immunogold procedures frequently produces particles of intermediate size [4, 5]. The presence of these heterogeneous gold-silver particles complicates reliable discrimination of labeling signals and makes accurate co-localization of multiple antigens within cells difficult. Nevertheless, the pre-embedding immunogold labeling approach remains widely used in TEM studies of cultured cell lines [6, 7]. However, one of the major technical challenges in both pre- and post-embedding approaches is the preparation of resin-embedded cell culture samples that consistently yield high-quality ultrathin sections and allow their efficient collection for subsequent immunocytochemical analyses.

To overcome these limitations, we developed a post-embedding immunogold labeling protocol optimized for studies of cultured cells [8, 9]. This approach enabled efficient immunolabeling of non-adherent PC12 neurosecretory cells in vitro, allowing precise determination of the subcellular localization of protein epitopes within the cytoplasm and nucleus, as well as within distinct cytoplasmic subdomains, including the endoplasmic reticulum, secretory vesicles, and clathrin-coated pits, and nuclear subdomains such as the nuclear envelope, interchromatin granules, and nucleolus. Here, we describe a detailed protocol for single- and double-immunogold labeling applicable to both adherent and non-adherent cells cultured in vitro (Fig. 1), complemented by a procedure for ultrastructural analysis of investigated cells that can support and extend immuno-TEM studies (Fig. 2). In our experience, the principal technical challenge is obtaining high-quality ultrathin sections of cultured cells suitable for TEM analysis. To address this challenge, we provide a detailed graphical workflow that guides the user through the entire sample-preparation process, from establishing a cell monolayer on plastic coverslips, through fixation, resin embedding, and block trimming, to ultrathin sectioning (Fig. 3). By enabling the reliable preparation of high-quality ultrathin sections from cultured cells, this protocol facilitates routine ultrastructural and immunogold analyses in diverse experimental models. The methodology described here is applicable to numerous cell lines and allows the localization of cellular components at a resolution that exceeds the limits of confocal microscopy.
