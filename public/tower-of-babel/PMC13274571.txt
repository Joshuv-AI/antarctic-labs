High‐risk genomic features predict extramedullary progression in multiple myeloma with paraskeletal plasmacytomas
Authors: Martin Stork, Eva Ondrouskova, Johana Mayerova, Andrea Mareckova, Jakub Pawel Porc, Tomas Reigl, Klara Mensikova, Nela Sendlerova, Ivanna Boichuk, Jakub Cieslar, Michaela Bilcikova, Zdenek Adam, Marta Krejci, Viera Sandecka, Zdenka Knechtova, Zuzana Jelinkova, Milan Krticka, Vladimir Nekuda, Tomas Rohan, Viera Hrabcakova, Lenka Janska Vojtkova, Marek Borsky, Lenka Radova, Marie Jarosova, Ludek Pour, Jana Kotaskova
Published: 2026

Multiple myeloma (MM) may present with extraosseous manifestations, including paraskeletal (PS) plasmacytomas extending from osteolytic bone lesions and true extramedullary disease (EMD) arising independently of the bone microenvironment, which represent clinically and biologically distinct entities. While EMD is consistently associated with poor prognosis, PS plasmacytomas are observed in approximately 30% of newly diagnosed MM (NDMM) patients and are generally associated with outcomes comparable to those without extramedullary involvement. 1 , 2 However, growing evidence suggests that a subset of PS lesions may carry an increased risk of progression to secondary EMD, a condition linked to treatment resistance and dismal outcomes. 3

In the era of genomic risk stratification as recommended by the International Myeloma Society/International Myeloma Working Group (IMS/IMWG), 4 it remains unclear whether genomic features can identify PS patients at risk of subsequent extramedullary progression.

To address this, we analyzed patients with newly diagnosed and relapsed MM treated at University Hospital Brno between 2015 and 2025. A total of 888 NDMM patients with defined extramedullary status based on advanced imaging were included. Genomic and cytogenetic analyses were performed on CD138+ bone marrow plasma cells (BMPCs) and native plasmacytoma tissue samples (PS or EMD). Genomic profiling was performed using in‐house targeted next‐generation sequencing (NGS) 5 and fluorescence in situ hybridization to assess recurrent genetic alterations. Detailed methodology is provided in the Supporting Information S1: Supplementary Methods. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee, and all patients provided informed consent.

Within this cohort, patients were stratified as follows: 646 without extraosseous manifestation, 226 with PS plasmacytomas, and 16 with primary EMD. Clinical characteristics and outcomes were consistent with previous reports (Supporting Information S2: Supplementary Materials). 3 Among patients without baseline EMD (n = 872), the presence of PS plasmacytomas at diagnosis was associated with an increased risk of subsequent EMD development. Secondary EMD developed in 8.0% (18/226) of patients with PS lesions compared to 3.7% (24/646) of patients without baseline PS involvement (odds ratio [OR] 2.24; 95% CI 1.19–4.22; P = 0.017). Furthermore, EMD emerged earlier in patients with baseline PS (median 22.3 vs. 38.0 months, P = 0.017), supporting the hypothesis that a subset of PS lesions is associated with increased risk of extramedullary progression.

To explore the biological basis of this observation, we analyzed genomic features in BMPCs from 167 NDMM patients without baseline EMD lesions. Clinical characteristics of this cohort are also mentioned in Supporting Information S2: Supplementary Materials. Chromosome 1 alterations were strongly associated with subsequent EMD development. Del(1p32) was enriched among patients who later developed EMD (OR 4.25, 95% CI 1.29–14.00; EMD: 5/15 vs. non‐EMD: 16/152), with an even stronger association observed for broad deletions affecting the 1p arm detected by NGS‐based copy number alteration (CNA) profiling (OR 7.20, 95% CI 2.14–24.3; EMD: 11/15 vs. non‐EMD: 42/152). The combined presence of del(1p32) and gain(1q21) was highly predictive of EMD spread (OR 8.85, 95% CI 2.17–36.09; EMD: 4/15 vs. non‐EMD: 6/152). Alterations affecting TENT5C (1p12) were also associated with increased risk (OR 3.39, 95% CI 1.10–9.94; EMD: 6/15 vs. non‐EMD: 25/152). Monoallelic TP53 alterations were not significantly associated with EMD development, whereas biallelic TP53 inactivation showed a stronger effect size with borderline statistical significance (OR 3.97; 95% CI 0.95–16.65; EMD: 3/15 vs. non‐EMD: 9/152). While common MAPK pathway activating mutations were not predictive, rare biallelic MAPK activating alterations were strongly enriched among patients who developed EMD (OR 13.45, 95% CI 2.96–61.2; EMD: 4/15 vs. non‐EMD: 4/152). Interestingly, elevated β2‐microglobulin in the absence of renal impairment at NDMM, used as a surrogate of proliferative disease, was not significantly associated with subsequent EMD development (Table 1).

To assess whether these genomic patterns are also reflected in tissue compartments, we analyzed plasmacytoma samples from 44 patients, including 25 PS lesions (14 at NDMM and 11 at disease progression) and 19 EMD lesions obtained in the relapsed/refractory MM (RRMM) setting.

Genomic profiling of PS plasmacytomas in NDMM revealed marked spatial heterogeneity between plasmacytoma tissue and paired BMPCs. In a subset of patients with aggressive disease and subsequent EMD spread, tissue samples harbored additional high‐risk alterations, including biallelic inactivation of tumor suppressor genes (TP53, RB1, and TENT5C) and MAPK pathway alterations, not detected in BMPCs. Detailed clinical courses and paired genomic profiles are provided in Supporting Information S2: Supplementary Materials.

Plasmacytoma tissue from RRMM patients showed a clear shift toward higher genomic complexity compared to that in NDMM (Table 2). High‐risk features were most pronounced in EMD lesions, including co‐occurrence of gain(1q21) and del(1p32) (21.1%), biallelic TP53 inactivation (21.1%), and biallelic MAPK pathway activation mutations (15.8%), together with frequent deletions affecting the 1p arm detected by NGS‐based CNA profiling (57.9%). Interestingly, in RRMM patients, PS plasmacytomas displayed largely overlapping genomic features with EMD lesions, with only minor differences between groups. TENT5C alterations were significantly enriched in EMD compared to PS lesions (47.4% vs. 9.1%; P = 0.049) (Supporting Information S2: Supplementary Materials).

In summary, PS plasmacytomas should not be considered simply as extensions of bone disease, given their distinct spatial genomic and immunological characteristics. 6 , 7 They represent a heterogeneous group, with certain cases defined by high‐risk genomic profiles and increased risk of subsequent EMD spread.

Targeted genomic profiling of BMPCs identified chromosome 1 abnormalities as dominant genomic determinants of this behavior. Deletions involving TENT5C and CDKN2C together with gain(1q21) showed the strongest association with subsequent EMD development, consistent with current high‐risk biology frameworks. 8 , 9 , 10 These results are also consistent with recent studies linking chromosome 1 alterations and MAPK pathway activation to extramedullary evolution. 11 , 12 Similarly, biallelic TP53 inactivation was associated with aggressive disease evolution, supporting a role for “second‐hit” biology in extramedullary spread. 13 Notably, elevated β2‐microglobulin (in the absence of renal impairment) was not significantly associated with subsequent EMD development, suggesting that these associations are not fully explained by general markers of disease burden.

Genomic profiling of PS plasmacytoma tissue in NDMM revealed marked spatial heterogeneity compared to paired BMPCs. Despite the limited number of samples, biallelic inactivation of tumor suppressor genes, including TP53, RB1, and TENT5C, was observed predominantly in tissue from aggressive cases. This pattern supports the presence of lesion‐confined “second‐hit” events not captured by standard BMPC profiling. 6 As expected, tissues of EMD plasmacytomas were significantly enriched for IMS/IMWG established high‐risk abnormalities, including chromosome 1 alterations and TP53 mono‐ or biallelic hits. 4 , 9 , 10 , 11 Interestingly, although EMD lesions carried the highest overall burden of adverse genomic events, differences between PS and EMD tissues in RRMM patients were not statistically significant, likely reflecting overlapping aggressive biology and generally dismal prognosis. 14

A notable finding was the enrichment of biallelic MAPK pathway activating mutations in BMPCs of patients who developed EMD, with similar findings in EMD or PS plasmacytoma tissue in RRMM patients. While monoallelic MAPK mutations are common, 13 biallelic activation—often mediated by copy‐neutral loss of heterozygosity 15 —is rarely described and may represent an underrecognized mechanism of EMD evolution, warranting further study.

These findings have potential clinical implications. The discrepancy between BMPC‐ and tissue‐based profiling suggests that biopsy of plasmacytoma lesions, when feasible, may refine risk stratification beyond standard bone marrow assessment. In this context, emerging non‐invasive approaches such as circulating cell‐free tumor DNA (cfDNA) may further complement tissue‐based profiling, as higher cfDNA levels have been associated with disease spread and adverse prognosis, potentially enabling a more comprehensive assessment of spatio‐temporal tumor heterogeneity. 16 Identification of high‐risk genomic patterns associated with increased risk of extramedullary progression may support closer imaging monitoring and inform therapeutic decision‐making, including consideration of emerging dual T‐cell–redirecting strategies in selected high‐risk patients. 17

Our study is limited by its retrospective design and the relatively small number of tissue samples. Nevertheless, our data demonstrate that routinely applied genomic approaches can capture clinically relevant signals associated with the risk of EMD development.

In conclusion, the combination of del(1p32) and gain(1q21) identifies a high‐risk subset of PS patients within current IMS/IMWG frameworks. Biallelic genomic events, including TP53 inactivation and MAPK pathway activating mutations, represent important features of aggressive disease evolution across bone marrow and extramedullary tissue compartments.
