Large Order Enumerative Geometry, Black Holes and Black Rings
Sergei Alexandrov, Albrecht Klemm, Boris Pioline

Abstract
Exploiting newly available data on Gopakumar-Vafa invariants at high genus for one-parameter hypergeometric Calabi-Yau threefolds, we study numerically the growth of the 5D indices, stable pair (PT) invariants and rank one Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants at large charges. For the 5D index Ω 5 ​ D ​ ( d , m ) \Omega_{5D}(d,m) , below a critical value of the angular momentum m m , we find perfect agreement with the Bekenstein-Hawking-Wald entropy of rotating 5D BMPV black holes, including the subleading correction from 4-derivative interactions. When m m exceeds the critical value, the 5D index is instead dominated by black rings with the smallest possible dipole charge. The stable pair invariant PT ⁡ ( d , m ) \operatorname{PT}(d,m) , which is determined by 5D indices, has a similar black ring/hole transition at negative m m (now interpreted as the D0-brane charge) but surprisingly exhibits two other phase transitions at positive m m : first, to a plateau and then to a polynomial growth ∼ m 2 ​ d − 1 \sim m^{2d-1} . In each phase, we derive an approximate expression for the invariant. Finally, the rank one DT invariant DT ⁡ ( d , m ) \operatorname{DT}(d,m) is similar to PT ⁡ ( d , m ) \operatorname{PT}(d,m) at negative m m , and then transitions to a phase dominated by D0-branes, with entropy of order m 2 / 3 m^{2/3} . Along the way, we determine the fixed genus, large degree behavior of GV invariants (including the overall g g -dependent constant), extend it to an approximate formula valid also for large g g , point out the unreasonable effectiveness of a simple PT/MSW relation, and study the growth of topological free energies at fixed degree, confirming a conjecture of Mariño.

Introduction

Due to its relevance for understanding instanton effects
and black hole micro-states in string theory, the enumerative geometry of Calabi-Yau (CY)
manifolds has been
a favorite arena for physicists and mathematicians.
Historically, the first enumerative
invariants to appear in the string theory literature were the Gromov-Witten (GW) invariants, counting holomorphic
maps from the string worldsheet at fixed genus with image in a given curve class (see e.g.
[
1
]

for a review of various approaches to curve counting). In particular, GW invariants
determine worldsheet instanton corrections to the low energy effective action in type II strings compactified
on a CY
threefold
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
The Gopakumar-Vafa (GV) invariants
were introduced in
[
5
,
6
]
in order to elucidate the integral structure
underlying GW invariants, and conjectured to count micro-states of supersymmetric spinning black holes
in 5 dimensions
[
7
]
. They are related by the GV/DT/PT correspondence
[
8
]

to Donaldson-Thomas (DT)
[
9
]
and Pandharipande-Thomas (PT) invariants

[
10
,
1
]
, which count micro-states of certain four-dimensional
black holes with one unit of D6-brane charge. For more general charge vector, BPS micro-states
are counted by generalized Donaldson-Thomas invariants
[
11
,
12
]
,
which are, at least in principle, computable from GV invariants by wall-crossing
[
13
,
14
]
.
The Bekenstein-Hawking-Wald (BHW) entropy of the corresponding black hole solution predicts the
growth of these enumerative invariants at large charges,
but it is hard to test these predictions in practice, due to the difficulty of computing these invariants in the first place.

Indeed, for CY manifolds of generic
SU(3)
holonomy, the only known way of computing
GV invariants (and hence all other invariants) is to obtain them via mirror symmetry at genus zero
[
2
]
and genus one
[
15
]
, and then integrate
the holomorphic anomaly equations
[
3
]

to determine them at higher genus – a method called
direct integration

[
16
,
17
]

(see also
[
18
,
19
]
). Unfortunately, the number of integration constants
(called holomorphic ambiguities) increases linearly with the genus, and the current knowledge of the analytic
structure of the topological string partition function, and of Castelnuovo-type bounds on the maximal genus
for given degree, only allow to fix these ambiguities up to a relatively low genus.
For example, using this method, the maximal genus attainable in principle for the quintic threefold
X_{5}
is
53

(slightly
higher than the value 51 quoted in
[
16
]
, but much higher than the genus
that was reachable in practice with computer resources available at the time).
Using wall-crossing identities between GV invariants, PT invariants and
rank 0 DT invariants

Using GV invariants available at the time for one-parameter CY threefolds of hypergeometric type

Using numerical techniques to accelerate convergence (namely Richardson transforms), the

In the case of spinning black holes, the evidence was weaker and limited to an angular momentum of order

The study of the asymptotics of rank 1 DT invariants

[
30

Our goal in this note is to update and extend the analysis of
[
29
]

in light of the new available enumerative data.
First, we revisit the asymptotics of GV invariants at fixed genus and large degree.
An asymptotic formula in this regime has been derived for the quintic
in
[
2
]
at genus 0, based on the singular behavior of the Yukawa coupling at the conifold point, and extended
in
[
3
,
31
]
to arbitrary genus.
While our analysis confirms the general structure of the formula,
the precise value of the overall coefficient differs from the one quoted
for genus 0 in
[
2
]
, and is confirmed by numerical analysis using higher depth logarithmic Richardson transform.

We then compare the 5D index defined in
[
7
]

with the Bekenstein-Hawking-Wald prediction for the entropy of 5D black holes. Using the new available data on GV invariants, we obtain more precise estimates for the leading and subleading terms
in the BH entropy for static BMPV black holes, providing a very strong confirmation of the BHW prediction.
Unfortunately, our results for the coefficient of the logarithmic correction are inconclusive, and potentially in tension with the prediction of
[
32
,
33
,
34
]
.
For spinning black holes, we find a remarkable agreement with
the entropy including the higher curvature correction that was computed in
[
35
]
, correcting earlier predictions
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
in the
literature.

At large spin, we confirm the existence of a kink in the curve interpolating the values of the index
\Omega_{5D}(d,m)
at fixed degree,
which was first observed in
[
39
]
and
interpreted as a transition into a phase governed by black rings.
We trace back this feature to the existence of a similar kink in the distribution of GV invariants, and determine its position as
d
becomes large (rectifying the conjecture in
[
39
]
). We find strong evidence that only black rings with unit dipole charge contribute to the index, although we are not able to justify why.

Furthermore, observing that the dependence of GV invariants at fixed degree
is well approximated by a Gaussian function, we propose an approximate
formula for these invariants, which is supposed to work at sufficiently large degree,
but does not require a fixed genus. The coefficients of this approximation
are derived by match with the asymptotic formula at fixed genus and
the entropy of 5D static black holes tested in the previous steps.

We then analyze the growth of DT and PT invariants, counting
4D BPS states with unit D6 or anti-D6-brane charge, respectively.
Their profile at fixed degree exhibits the same phase transition at negative D0-brane charge
that was observed for the index, but also shows new unexpected features.
First, for D0-brane charge between the Castelnuovo bound and the transition point, PT invariants appear to be accurately captured by a simple linear relation (
4.16
) to MSW invariants.
This relation is a very special case of the PT/MSW correspondence established in
[
21
]
, and is known to hold true
only under restrictive conditions which are not satisfied in this extended range.
Second, the profile of PT invariants turns out to
exhibit two additional phase transitions at positive D0-brane charge
giving rise to a ‘plateau’ and a ‘ramp’ (unrelated, as far as we know, to the ramp and plateau in quantum many-body physics).
In contrast, the profile of DT invariants exhibits only one additional phase dominated by D0-branes.
The macroscopic interpretation of the new phases remains however unclear.
For all phases, using the GV/DT/PT correspondence, we derive simple approximate expressions of PT and DT invariants in terms of GV invariants,
which sometimes involve only a small subset of the latter.
An interesting (and mysterious) feature is that all phase transitions for the various enumerative invariants (GV, PT, DT,
\Omega_{5D}
)
turn out to be correlated with a change in the behavior of their sign.

In §

In §

In §

In Appendix

describe several numerical techniques for accelerating the convergence of series, generalizing the standard Richardson transform. In Appendix

2
Phenomenology of GW and GV invariants

In this section, we recall how Gromov-Witten and Gopakumar-Vafa invariants
can be computed using mirror symmetry and holomorphic anomaly equations.
Using the behavior of the topological amplitudes at the conifold point,
we then determine the growth of GW and GV invariants at fixed genus, large degree. Finally, we make some numerical observations on the profile of GV invariants at fixed degree (and genus less than the Castelnuovo bound).

•

3.1
5D index from GV invariants

The GV invariants are related to counting of five-dimensional BPS states in M-theory
compactified on
X
as follows
[
7
]
. Since the little group for a massive particle
in 4+1 dimensions is
SO(4)=(SU(2)\times SU(2))/\mathds{Z}_{2}

characterized by its (quantized) electric charge
d

and by a pair of angular momenta
(j_{L},j_{R})

J_{\psi}=j_{L}^{z}+j_{R}^{z}

(x^{1},x^{2})
and
(x^{3},x^{4})

Following
[
6
]

N^{j_{L},j_{R}}_{d}

be the number of BPS states whose highest weight in the multiplet
carries momentum
(j_{L},j_{R})
. The Gopakumar-Vafa invariants
are defined by tracing over
j_{R}
with an insertion of
(-1)^{2j_{R}}
, and expanding the character of the
SU(2)_{L}

action in powers of the character of the representation
[1/2]\oplus[0]\oplus[0]
of
SU(2)_{L}

\sum_{j_{L},j_{R}}(-1)^{2j_{L}+2j_{R}}(2j_{R}+1)N^{j_{L},j_{R}}_{d}\chi_{j_{L}}(y)=\sum_{g=0}^{g_{\rm max}(d)}\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g)}\,(2-y-1/y)^{g},

(3.1)

where
\chi_{j}(y)=y^{-2j}+y^{-2j+2}+\dots+y^{2j}
is the character of a spin
j
representation of
SU(2)
.
Adding in the descendants of the highest weight state in the BPS multiplet, which fill in the representation

[1/2]\oplus[0]\oplus[0]
of
SU(2)_{L}
,
one defines the 5D index
\Omega_{5D}(d,m)
through the
relation
[

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}(-1)^{2(j_{L}+j_{R})}y^{2j_{L}^{z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g=0}^{g_{\rm max}(d)}\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g)}\,(2-y-1/y)^{g+1}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g=0}^{g_{\rm max}(d)}\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g)}\,(-1)^{g+1}\left(\sqrt{y}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}\right)^{2g+2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-(g_{\rm max}(d)+1)}^{g_{\rm max}(d)+1}\Omega_{5D}(d,m)(-y)^{m}.

The index counts 5D BPS states with electric charge
d

and angular momentum
j_{L}^{z}=m/2

,

with

As explained in
[
32
]
, the number of states with
fixed total angular momentum

\begin{split}\tilde{\Omega}_{5D}(d,m)=&\,\Omega_{5D}(d,m)-\Omega_{5D}(d,m+2)\\

In practice, we shall find that

so the difference between the two ensembles is negligible, except when discussing certain subleading corrections.

3.4
Approximate formula for GV invariants

As was noticed in §
2.4
, at fixed degree
d
the GV invariants
\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g)}
are well
approximated by a Gaussian function of the genus
g
. In this section, we build on this observation to propose
a formula that approximates GV invariants for all (sufficiently large)
degrees and genera smaller than
g_{\rm kink}(d)
. To fix the parameters of the Gaussian,
we shall use the previous results, namely, the asymptotic formula at fixed genus
(
2.22
) and the 5D index for static black holes (
3.28
).

As a starting point, let us consider the following ansatz

\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g)}\approx\mathcal{P}(d,g)\left(\xi d\log d\right)^{2g-2}e^{C-A(g-B)^{2}},

(3.34)

where the parameters
A
,
B
and
C
are functions of
d
,

\xi
is a constant and
\mathcal{P}(d,g)
is a polynomially bounded function of
(d,g)
.
Note that, up to the factor
\mathcal{P}(d,g)
, the dependence on
g
is Gaussian,
while the power of
d\log d
already appears in the fixed genus asymptotic formula (
2.22
).
We want to fix the unknown parameters by imposing the two conditions, (
2.22
) and (
3.28
).

To impose the first condition, we assume for the moment that
A
vanishes at large
d
.
Then, to match the asymptotics (
2.22
),
one has to set

B=0,\qquad C=2\pi\mathcal{V}d,\qquad\mathcal{P}_{0}(d,g)=\frac{a_{g}}{d}\,\xi^{2-2g},

(3.35)

where
\mathcal{P}_{0}(d,g)
denotes the asymptotics of
\mathcal{P}(d,g)
at large
d
and fixed
g
.
Due to (
2.45
) and (
2.47
), in order to cancel the exponential dependence on the genus
we should take

\xi=\frac{\mathcal{V}}{4\pi^{2}},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{0}(d,g)=\frac{1}{d}\,\frac{|(2g-1)B_{2g}|}{\Gamma(2g+1)}(2\pi)^{2g-2}\approx\frac{g}{\pi^{2}d}\,,

(3.36)

where the last estimate is already accurate at small
g
.

To impose the second condition, we analyze the 5D index at zero angular momentum,

\Omega_{5D}(d,0)=\sum_{g=0}^{g_{\rm max}(d)}\frac{(2g+2)!}{((g+1)!)^{2}}\,\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g)}.

(3.37)

First, using Stirling’s formula
\Gamma(x+1)\approx\sqrt{2\pi x}(x/e)^{x}
,
one finds that the binomial coefficient behaves at large
g
as

\frac{(2g+2)!}{((g+1)!)^{2}}\approx\frac{2^{2g+2}}{\sqrt{\pi g}}.

(3.38)

Next, we approximate the sum in (
3.37
) by an integral.
As a result, after substitution of
(
3.34
) with (
3.35
) and the approximation (
3.38
), the formula for the index takes the form

\Omega_{5D}(d,0)\approx 16\int_{g=0}^{g_{\rm max}(d)}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{\sqrt{\pi g}}\,\mathcal{P}(d,g)\,e^{2\pi\mathcal{V}d-Ag^{2}+(2g-2)\log(2\xi d\log d)}.

(3.39)

The approximations are justified because the integral is expected to be dominated by
a saddle point
g_{\rm sp}(d)
which scales as a positive power of
d
and thus is supposed
to be large.
26
26

26

In fact, the approximation (
3.34
) is supposed
to be true only for
g<g_{\rm kink}
. Thus, writing (
3.39
),
we also assumed that the saddle point is before the kink and the integration
in the interval
(g_{\rm kink},g_{\rm max})
does not contribute to the asymptotics.

27
27

27

One
could worry that the boundary contribution
coming from the Castelnuovo bound grows faster than the saddle point contribution because
at
g=g_{\rm max}(d)
the binomial coefficient is of order
2^{d^{2}/\kappa}
,
whereas we argued previously that
\log|\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g)}|
was bounded by
d^{3/2}
.
However, between the kink and the Castelnuovo bound the sign of GV invariants fluctuates
and it appears that it leads to cancellations suppressing the growth.

Indeed, the saddle point is found to be at

g_{\rm sp}=\frac{\mathcal{A}^{-1}}{\log(2\xi d\log d)}\,,

(3.40)

and the integral evaluates to

\Omega_{5D}(d,0)\approx\frac{4\,\mathcal{P}(d,g_{\rm sp})\,e^{2\pi\mathcal{V}d+\mathcal{A}^{-1}}}{(\xi d\log d)^{2}(\log(2\xi d\log d))^{1/2}}\,,

(3.41)

where we introduced

\mathcal{A}=\frac{A}{(\log(2\xi d\log d))^{2}}\,.

(3.42)

This condition allows to fix the coefficient
\mathcal{A}
in terms of the 5D BPS index,
namely,
28
28

28

Since the last term depends on
g_{\rm sp}
, which in turn depends on
\mathcal{A}
,
it looks like (

) is an equation rather than an expression.
However, in the given approximation where
d
is sufficiently large
one can replace
\mathcal{A}^{-1}

\mathcal{A}^{-1}\approx\log\Omega_{5D}(d,0)-2\pi\mathcal{V}d+2\log(\xi d\log d)+{1\over 2}\,\log(\log(2\xi d\log d))-\log(4\mathcal{P}(d,g_{\rm sp})).

(3.43)

Substituting (
3.28
), we arrive at the following expression,
which is supposed to hold at sufficiently large
d

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\approx&\,b_{0}d^{3/2}\sqrt{1+\frac{3c_{2}}{8d}}-2\pi\mathcal{V}d+(\alpha+2)\log d+2\log\log d\\
&\,+{1\over 2}\log\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}}{2\pi^{2}}\,d\log d\right)+\beta-\log\left(\frac{64\pi^{4}}{\mathcal{V}^{2}}\,\mathcal{P}(d,g_{\rm sp})\right).\end{split}

(3.44)

This implies that at large
d
one has

A\sim\frac{(\log(d\log d))^{2}}{b_{0}d^{3/2}}\,,\qquad g_{\rm sp}\sim\frac{b_{0}d^{3/2}}{\log(d\log d)}\,,

(3.45)

which confirms our approximations used above.

Combining the above results and substituting the value of
\xi
from (
3.36
),
we conclude that GV invariants can be approximated
for
d
sufficiently large by
29
29

29

This result certainly does not hold for very small

.
For the formula (
3.46
) to make sense,
one should at least ensure that
d
is sufficiently large to have

\frac{\mathcal{V}}{4\pi^{2}}\,d\log d>1
and
\mathcal{A}>0
.

\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g)}\approx\mathcal{P}(d,g)\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}}{4\pi^{2}}\,d\log d\right)^{2g-2}e^{2\pi\mathcal{V}d-\left(\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}}{2\pi^{2}}\,d\log d\right)\right)^{2}\mathcal{A}g^{2}},

(3.46)

where
\mathcal{A}
is given by (
3.44
) and the function
\mathcal{P}(d,g)
is restricted to have asymptotics (

).
Note that, upon replacing

\mathcal{P}_{0}(d,g)
,
the approximate formula differs from the asymptotics at fixed genus (

)
only by the Gaussian factor

This shows that for that asymptotics to be valid, one should have

Unfortunately, our approach does not allow to fix the form of the function

Replacing it by its large

3.4.1
Implications

The result (
3.46
) has several interesting implications
which we now discuss.

Maxima at fixed degree

The first, obvious implication of
the approximate formula (
3.46
) is that
the position of the maximum at fixed degree
d
and the value
at the maximum are given by

\displaystyle g_{\rm top}(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\log(\xi d\log d)}{(\log(2\xi d\log d))^{2}}\,\mathcal{A}^{-1}\sim\frac{b_{0}d^{3/2}}{\log(d\log d)}\,,

(3.47)

\displaystyle\operatorname{GV}_{d}^{(g_{\rm top}(d))}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{P}(d,g_{\rm top}(d))}{(\xi d\log d)^{2}}\,e^{2\pi\mathcal{V}d+\frac{(\log(\xi d\log d))^{2}}{(\log(2\xi d\log d))^{2}}\,\mathcal{A}^{-1}}\sim e^{b_{0}d^{3/2}}.

(3.48)

In particular, at large degree the leading asymptotics of
g_{\rm top}(d)

is exactly the same as that of the saddle point
g_{\rm sp}
(
3.45
).

Relation between two ensembles

Let us consider the index (
3.4
) counting states with
both the total angular momentum
j_{L}
and its projection
j_{L}^{z}
fixed in the regime where
m\sim d^{3/2-\gamma}
.
At large
d
, upon approximating the sum by an integral and evaluating it by saddle point as in (
3.39
), one easily gets

\log|\tilde{\Omega}_{5D}(d,m)|\approx\log|\Omega_{5D}(d,m)|+\left(\frac{3}{2}-\gamma\right)\log(d)-\log g_{\rm sp}.

(3.49)

Since according to (
3.45
)
g_{\rm sp}\sim d^{3/2}
,
one obtains

\log|\tilde{\Omega}_{5D}(d,m)|\approx\log|\Omega_{5D}(d,m)|-\gamma\log d,

(3.50)

which reproduces (
3.15
)
and thus provides a microscopic derivation of this relation.

Growth of topological free energies

In Appendix
C
, we use the approximate formula (
3.46
)
to estimate the sum over genera
F_{d}(\lambda)
(
C.2
) appearing
in the Gopakumar-Vafa representation of
the topological free energy (
2.15
), in the limit where the
degree is large.
In particular, we confirm and make precise the expectation of
[
87
]

that at sufficiently large degree,
F_{d}(\lambda)
grows quadratically in
d
(assuming that the topological
string coupling
\lambda
has a non-zero imaginary part).
We also point out the existence of a transient regime where the sum is
dominated by a saddle point similar to (
3.40
) and behaves differently.

4
Growth of PT invariants

In this section, we study the asymptotics of the Pandharipande-Thomas invariants
\operatorname{PT}(d,m)
,
which physically count bound states of a single anti D6-brane with
d
D2-branes and
m
D0-branes
in a suitable large volume, large
B
-field limit (see e.g.
[
1
]

for a review of their mathematical definition in terms of stable pairs).
Since PT invariants vanish when
m
is sufficiently large and negative,
the generating series

Z_{PT}({\rm y},\mbox{q})=1+\sum_{d>0,m}\operatorname{PT}(d,m)\,{\rm y}^{d}\,\mbox{q}^{m}

(4.1)

is a well-defined Laurent series in
q
, and Taylor series in
{\rm y}
(a fugacity conjugate to the degree,
not to be confused with the fugacity
y
conjugate to angular momentum).
Moreover, at each order in
{\rm y}
, the coefficient

\sum_{m}\operatorname{PT}(d,m)\mbox{q}^{m}
is known to be a rational function of
q
, invariant under
\mbox{q}\mapsto 1/\mbox{q}
.

PT invariants are related to GV invariants by the
GV/PT correspondence
[
8
]

Z_{PT}({\rm y},\mbox{q})=Z_{PT}^{(0)}({\rm y},\mbox{q})\,Z_{PT}^{(1)}({\rm y},\mbox{q}),

(4.2)

where

\begin{split}Z^{(0)}_{PT}({\rm y},\mbox{q})=&\,\prod_{d=1}^{\infty}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1-(-\mbox{q})^{k}{\rm y}^{d}\right)^{k\operatorname{GV}^{(0)}_{d}},\\
Z^{(1)}_{PT}({\rm y},\mbox{q})=&\prod_{d=1}^{\infty}\prod_{g=1}^{g_{\rm max}(d)}\prod_{\ell=0}^{2g-2}\left(1-(-\mbox{q})^{g-\ell-1}{\rm y}^{d}\right)^{(-1)^{g+\ell}{\scriptsize\begin{pmatrix}2g-2\\
\ell\end{pmatrix}}\operatorname{GV}^{(g)}_{d}}\,.\end{split}

(4.3)

In particular, the Castelnuovo bound for GV invariants (
2.16
) is equivalent to the following lower bound for PT invariants

m\geq m_{\rm min}(d)=-\left\lfloor\frac{d^{2}}{2\kappa}+\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor.

(4.4)

Following
[
30
]
, we shall refer to the factors
Z_{PT}^{(0)}
and
Z_{PT}^{(1)}
,
coming from genus 0 and higher genus contributions, respectively, as ”halo” and ”core” states.
For various purposes it is advantageous to rewrite the GV/PT relation as
[
1
, §
4\tfrac{1}{2}
]

Z_{PT}({\rm y},\mbox{q})={\rm PE}\left[\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\sum_{g=0}^{g_{\rm max}(d)}(-1)^{g+1}\operatorname{GV}^{(g)}_{d}\left(1-\mbox{q}\right)^{2g-2}\mbox{q}^{(1-g)}{\rm y}^{d}\right]_{\mbox{q}\to-\mbox{q}},

(4.5)

where

{\rm PE}[f]({\rm y},\mbox{q})=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\,f({\rm y}^{k},\mbox{q}^{k})\right)

(4.6)

is the plethystic exponential.

In particular, the representation (
4.5
) allows to establish a relation to the 5D index
\Omega_{5D}(d,m)
.
Indeed, using (
3.3
), one finds

Z_{PT}({\rm y},\mbox{q})={\rm PE}\left[\frac{1}{(\mbox{q}+\mbox{q}^{-1}-2)^{2}}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|m|\leq|m_{\rm min}(d)|}\Omega_{5D}(d,m)\,(-\mbox{q})^{m}{\rm y}^{d}\right]_{\mbox{q}\to-\mbox{q}},

(4.7)

which can be interpreted as the partition function of a gas of 5D BPS black holes
(and black rings) in a Taub-NUT geometry
[
88
]
.
We note that a similar factor

(\mbox{q}+\mbox{q}^{-1}-2)^{-2}=(\sqrt{\mbox{q}}-1/\sqrt{\mbox{q}})^{-4}

appears in the ratio

\frac{Z_{4D}^{\rm hair}}{Z_{5D}^{\rm hair}}=\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\rho}}{\eta^{24}(\rho)(e^{\mathrm{i}\pi v}-e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi v})^{4}}

(4.8)

of the indices of the hair degrees of freedom living outside the horizon of a 4D BPS black hole
at the center of Taub-NUT and a 5D BMPV black hole, respectively, computed in
[
89
]

in a setup with twice as much supersymmetry (namely, type IIB compactified on
K3\times T^{2}
).
In the present case, the BPS black holes cannot be lifted to black strings in 6 dimensions,
so the
\rho
dependent factor does not arise,
but they preserve the same number of supercharges, presumably leading
to the same term
(e^{\mathrm{i}\pi v}-e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi v})^{4}
in the denominator.
The plethystic exponential accounts for all possible ways of splitting the electric charge
and angular momentum in an arbitrary number of 5D BPS states placed in the Taub-NUT geometry.

\Omega_{5D}(d,m)\approx\operatorname{PT}(d,m+2),\qquad m<0.

(4.13)

This explains the identical form of the curves representing the 5D index and PT invariants before the plateau.
This relation can also be easily understood from the identity (
4.7
).
Indeed, it is enough, as above, to drop the plethystic exponential and in addition to pick up only the first term
in the expansion of the prefactor, i.e.
\mbox{q}^{-2}
. It is this factor that produces the shift in (
4.13
).

It is clear that the dominant contribution to
\operatorname{PT}(d,m)
for
m
after the kink but before the plateau
comes from single centered black holes with D6-D4-D2-D0 charge given by
\left(-1,0,-d+\frac{c_{2}}{24},m\right)

where the shift in the D2-charge is due to its non-trivial quantization.
On the other hand, before the kink the dominant contribution is generated by multi-centered black holes

[
90
,
91
,
92
]
.
Indeed, the relation (
4.13
) identifies this regime with the phase of the 5D index with large angular momentum
which is dominated by black rings and the latter descend by the 4D/5D relation to multi-centered black holes
[
80
]
.
Moreover, due to observations in §
3.3.4
, one can expect that the relevant black holes are bound states
of an anti-D6-brane with unit D4-flux and D4-D2-D0 black hole with charge (
3.10
) specialized to
r=1
.

4.2
The unreasonable effectiveness of the PT/MSW relation

In
[
21
, §4.2]
, a relation between PT invariants and
MSW invariants counting D4-D2-D0 bound states was established,
by studying the chamber structure of the DT-invariants for an anti-D6-brane.
Under a restrictive set of conditions on charges,
which typically hold only close to the Castelnuovo bound (
4.4
),
it was shown that

\operatorname{PT}(d,m)=\sum_{d^{\prime},m^{\prime}}(-1)^{\chi(d^{\prime},m^{\prime})}\chi(d^{\prime},m^{\prime})\operatorname{PT}(d^{\prime},m^{\prime})\,\Omega_{1,d-d^{\prime}}(\hat{q}^{\prime}_{0}),

(4.14)

where
\Omega_{r,d}(\hat{q}_{0})
is the rank
r
MSW invariant with charges

d
and
\hat{q}_{0}
defined as in §
3.2.2
,

\begin{split}\chi(d^{\prime},m^{\prime})=m-m^{\prime}+d+d^{\prime}-\chi_{D},\qquad&\chi_{r\mathcal{D}}=\frac{\kappa r^{3}}{6}+\frac{c_{2}r}{12}\,,\\
\hat{q}^{\prime}_{0}=m^{\prime}-m-\frac{(d^{\prime}-d)^{2}}{2\kappa}-\frac{d+d^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{\chi(\mathcal{D})}{24}\,,\qquad&\chi(r\mathcal{D})=\kappa r^{3}+c_{2}r,\end{split}

(4.15)

and the sum over
(d^{\prime},m^{\prime})
runs over a finite set satisfying certain inequalities,
which ensure in particular that
0\leq d^{\prime}<d
.
This provides a recursive way to compute PT invariants, assuming the
rank 1 MSW invariants are known. Under even more restrictive conditions
(the so called optimal case,
[
21
, (4.26)]
),
one can show that the only term contributing on the r.h.s. of (
4.14
) is
(d^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(0,0)
.
Since
\operatorname{PT}(0,0)=1
, the expression for
\operatorname{PT}(d,m)
reduces to

\operatorname{PT}_{1}(d,m):=(-1)^{m+d-\chi_{\mathcal{D}}}(m+d-\chi_{\mathcal{D}})\,\Omega_{1,d}(\hat{q}_{0}).

(4.16)

While this expression is exact very close to the Castelnuovo bound,
it becomes only an approximation when the conditions for its validity are violated
and more terms in (
4.14
) start contributing.
Moreover, if one further relaxes the conditions for (
4.14
), PT invariants get contributions from higher rank MSW invariants.
In particular, an exact formula including rank 2 contributions and
the corresponding conditions on charges have been derived in
[
26
]
.
Nonetheless, it turns out that the approximation (
4.16
) works amazingly well all the way
from the Castelnuovo bound up to the kink!
In Fig.
20
, we plot the ratio
\frac{\operatorname{PT}_{1}(d,m)}{\operatorname{PT}(d,m)}

(without taking the logarithm!) as a function of
m
.
Remarkably, the ratio is indistinguishable from unity over the full range
before the kink despite the fact that the stringent conditions ensuring that

\operatorname{PT}(d,m)=\operatorname{PT}_{1}(d,m)
are never obeyed in this range.
If this property can be quantified, it might be useful in fixing holomorphic ambiguities
in the direct integration method, at least at a heuristic level.

4.3
Plateau

The plateau phase of PT invariants is the only regime where

Since the contribution discussed in the previous subsection starts rapidly decreasing at positive

4.4
After the plateau

As was already noticed in Fig.

In contrast to PT invariants, DT invariants do not exhibit any plateau.

•

Another difference is that in both regions where DT invariants take positive and negative values

•

At negative

than DT invariants.

6
Discussion

In this paper we analyzed enumerative invariants of one-parameter hypergeometric CY threefolds
using both analytical and numerical methods.
Here we summarize our main results and observations.

1.

We derived the asymptotic form (
2.46
) of GW and GV invariants at large degree and fixed genus,
improving on earlier results by fixing the overall coefficient
a_{g}
.
This formula follows from the singular behavior of the free energy
\mathcal{F}^{(g)}
near the conifold point,
and was extensively checked to high genus.

2.

The values of all invariants such as GV, PT, DT and
\Omega_{5D}
, at fixed but large degree
d
can be interpolated by piece-wise smooth curves
and exhibit several phase transitions.

3.

One phase transition is common to all invariants. For the 5D index, it is interpreted as a transition between phases
where the leading contribution to the index is due to either black holes or black rings, as anticipated in
[
39
]
.
For the 4D indices (namely, PT and DT invariants), it is a transition between single-centered black holes and two-centered bound states.
We found that the kink takes place at the value (
3.32
),
correcting the value conjectured in
[
39
]
.

4.

Perhaps unexpectedly, PT invariants exhibit two additional transitions at positive values of the D0-brane charge
m
.
Using the GV/PT relation (
4.5
), we obtained approximate expressions for PT invariants in terms of GV invariants
in all regimes, which can be found in (
4.10
), (
4.19
) and (
4.23
). DT invariants
appear to exhibit only one additional transition, with asymptotics at large D0-brane charge given in (
5.4
),
dominated by D6-D0 bound states.

5.

We also observed that in the phase dominated by two-centered black holes, PT invariants
are accurately captured by MSW invariants with unit D4-brane charge or, more precisely, by the combination (
4.16
).
While this relation is exact very close to the Castelnuovo bound, it is an unexpected surprise that it continues to hold with high accuracy
throughout this phase.

6.

We noticed a general relation between
\Omega_{5D}
and PT invariants (
4.7
), which is a direct consequence
of the MNOP formula and can be seen as a manifestation of the 4D/5D lift
[
97
]
.
Furthermore, in the regime where PT invariants are dominated by black holes (before the plateau),
this relation is approximated by the equality (
4.13
) identifying the two indices up to a shift in the D0-brane charge.

7.

We have checked the behavior of the 5D index against supergravity predictions for the entropy of five-dimensional
black holes and black rings. In the static case, we have found a perfect agreement with the classical
entropy and the first higher derivative correction.
In the rotating case, our results strongly support the validity of the prediction (
3.27
) from
[
35
]
, over earlier computations in the literature.
Unfortunately, our results for the coefficient of the logarithmic correction are inconclusive and suggest,
if anything, that this coefficient may vanish, rather than taking the value predicted by
[
32
]
.

8.

In the black ring regime, the 5D index is in perfect
agreement with the quantum corrected black ring entropy (
3.13
)
with dipole charge
r
set to 1. By the 4D/5D lift this entropy
is the same as the entropy of D4-D2-D0 black hole with unit D4-brane charge
and therefore the agreement appears to be a consequence of the approximate relation (
3.29
).
On the other hand, we find evidence that black rings with
r>1
should not contribute to the index.

9.

Returning to GV invariants, we proposed an approximate formula (
3.46
),
which extends the fixed genus asymptotics (
2.46
) to arbitrary genus up to
g_{\rm kink}(d)
.
It is based on the observation that GV invariants at fixed degree are well approximated by a Gaussian.
The main parameter
\mathcal{A}
in the formula is fixed in terms of
the asymptotics
of the 5D index at zero angular momentum (
3.43
).

10.

Finally, assuming that this approximate formula for GV invariants holds, we proved a conjecture by Mariño
about the asymptotics of topological free energies at fixed but large degree (and complex string coupling),
and pointed out an intriguing transient behavior at intermediate degree.

These results also raise various issues which we discuss next.

a.

An important problem is to resolve the tension between
the supergravity prediction for the coefficient of the logarithmic correction to the entropy
of static BMPV black holes and our numerical results. One possibility is that this tension
disappears upon going to higher degree, which requires
computing GV invariants to higher genus.
If the deviations persist, one would have to reconsider the supergravity analysis,
perhaps taking into account specificities of theories with eight supercharges.

b.

Another important but subtle issue is the relation between the 5D index, computed in asymptotically flat spacetime
and in a specific ensemble, and the actual microcanonical degrees
of freedom localized near the horizon, which can be meaningfully compared with the black hole entropy. To our knowledge, this issue has only been studied
so far in string vacua with higher supersymmetry
[
89
,
98
]

and it deserves further attention.

c.

Alternatively, one may try to compute the 5D index macroscopically
by identifying the complex saddle points which dominate the gravitational path integral, along the lines of

[
99
,
34
,
100
,
71
]
.
In this respect, it is interesting to note that the authors of
[
71
]
have found that black rings generally dominate over black holes in the canonical ensemble. It would be interesting to understand how this is consistent with the microcanonical picture which emerges from our work.

d.

Our results strongly indicate that black rings with dipole charge
r>1
do not contribute to the 5D index,
but what prevents them to contribute remains unclear to us.
It becomes even more puzzling taking into account the relation (
4.13
) to PT invariants, which in turn
can be expressed through MSW invariants via wall-crossing.
The latter have exactly the same entropy (
3.13
) as black rings and MSW invariants with D4-brane charge
r>1

are known to contribute to PT invariants in certain
regimes
[
26
]
.

e.

The existence of new phases where (the logarithm of) PT and DT invariants behave differently from
the black hole entropy came as a surprise. It also raised the natural question: what is
the macroscopic interpretation of these phases?
A hint to the answer can be gleaned from the approximate expressions for the invariants,
which we found from the MNOP formula,
and, in particular, which GV invariants they involve.
For example, it is striking that, due to (
4.23
), at large D0-brane charge the asymptotics of PT invariants
is determined by a single GV invariant
\operatorname{GV}^{(0)}_{1}
!

f.

There are several features of the curves interpolating invariants at fixed degree that call for an explanation.
For example, why are GV invariants at fixed degree
well approximated by a Gaussian?
What is the mathematical origin of the slope discontinuity
at
g=g_{\rm kink}(d)
? What is origin of the kink for PT invariants and DT invariants?

g.

An open problem is to fix the prefactor
\mathcal{P}(d,g)

in the approximate formula (
3.46
) for GV invariants. While one obtains satisfactory results by
replacing it by its large degree limit
\mathcal{P}_{0}(d,g)
(
3.36
), one may hope
to extend the regime of validity of the formula by including
further corrections.
Another interesting related problem is to find a similar approximation for GV invariants at
g>g_{\rm kink}(d)
.

h.

Despite our attempts, we have not been able to determine the growth of DT invariants
\operatorname{DT}(\lambda^{2}d,\lambda^{3}m)
at large
\lambda
, a question which was identified
in
[
30
]
as key for the validity of the OSV conjecture
[
96
]
,
and studied in
[
29
]
inconclusively. It remains an important question
for future studies.

i.

In this work we restricted ourselves to one-parameter hypergeometric models.
Clearly, it would be interesting to extend this analysis to the one-parameter CY threefolds
in the database
[
41
]
, for which the knowledge of higher genus GV invariants is unfortunately scarce.
In particular, it would be interesting to extend the fixed genus asymptotic formula (
2.46
)
and the approximation (
3.46
)
to examples with hyperconifold singularities.

j.

Eventually, one may hope that the qualitative features identified in our study,
and in particular the unreasonable effectiveness of the simplest PT/MSW relation noted in §
4.2
,
could help in fixing the holomorphic ambiguities in the direct integration method, and allow us to
push it to higher genus than currently accessible.

k.

Finally, it is interesting to extend our analysis to the case of local non-compact CY threefolds, where 5D BPS states can be counted at fixed angular momenta
(j_{L},j_{R})
, without tracing over
j_{R}
.
In
[
70
]
we shall establish an analogue of the asymptotic
formula (
2.46
) for refined GV invariants at fixed degree, and analyze the
profile of the 5D index and refined DT/PT invariants.

Appendix B
Richardson transform and
E
-algorithm

In this appendix, we recall some useful techniques for accelerating the convergence of
slowly convergent series
\{s_{d}\}
, following
[
29
,
106
]
.
In practical applications,
s_{d}
often arises as the partial sum
s_{d}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_{k}

of some series
\sum_{k\geq 1}r_{k}
, but this need not be the case.
For us
d
will typically be the degree of GV invariants or the black hole charge,
and
s_{d}
is known only up to some maximal number
d_{\rm max}
.
The challenge is then to guess
\lim_{d\to\infty}s_{d}
, assuming it exists, from the
knowledge of a finite number of terms.

The simplest technique, known as Richardson transform, assumes that the series

s_{d}
converges to a finite value
s_{\infty}
, such that for
d\to\infty
the error is suppressed by
inverse powers of
d
,

s_{d}=s_{\infty}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{a_{k}}{d^{k}}\,.

(B.1)

For any
N\geq 1
, the depth
N
Richardson transform is a new series

\left(R_{N}[s]\right)_{d}:=\sum_{k=0}^{N}(-1)^{k+N}\frac{(d+k)^{N}}{k!(N-k)!}\,s_{d+k},

(B.2)

which converges to the same value
s_{\infty}
, but such that the error is now suppressed by
1/d^{N+1}
.
Indeed, it is easy to check that the linear combination in (
B.2
) annihilates all terms
with
k\leq N
in (
B.1
). Note that
R_{0}[s]=s
, and that the composition
R_{N_{1}}[R_{N_{2}}[s]]

achieves the same acceleration as
R_{N_{1}+N_{2}}[s]
(i.e. both eliminate all terms with
k\leq N_{1}+N_{2}
),
although the two prescriptions yield different remainders.

Similarly, one may consider a series
s_{d}
, which converges to a finite value
s_{\infty}
,
but with an error suppressed by inverse powers of
\log d
,

s_{d}=s_{\infty}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{b_{k}}{(\log d)^{k}}\,.

(B.3)

The analogue of (
B.2
) is the depth
N
logarithmic Richardson transform given by

\left(L_{N}[s]\right)_{d}=\sum_{m=0}^{N}\frac{\log(d+m)^{N}}{\prod_{l\neq m}\log\left(\frac{d+m}{d+l}\right)}\,s_{d+m}.

(B.4)

One may check that it
converges to the same value
s_{\infty}
, but such that the error is now suppressed by
1/(\log d)^{N+1}
.

More generally, consider a series
s_{d}
,
which converges to a finite value
s_{\infty}
, with an error given by

s_{d}=s_{\infty}+\sum_{k\geq 1}f_{k}(d),

(B.5)

where
f_{k}(d)
are functions of
d
, ordered such that
f_{k+1}(d)/f_{k}(d)\to 0
as
d\to\infty
.
For any
N
, the
E
-algorithm
[
107
,
108
]
,
(see
[
106
, §2.2.7]
) provides a linear combination of
s_{d+k}
with
0\leq k\leq N

which annihilates all
f_{k}(d)
with
1\leq k\leq N
, such that the error is suppressed by
f_{N+1}(d)
.
While the linear combination can be found in principle by solving a linear problem,
it is more efficient to construct it through the recursion

\begin{split}\left(E_{N}[s]\right)_{d}=&\,\left(E_{N-1}[s]\right)_{d}-\frac{\left(E_{N-1}[s]\right)_{d+1}-\left(E_{N-1}[s]\right)_{d}}{f_{N-1,k}(d+1)-f_{N-1,N}(d)}\,f_{N-1,N}(d),\\
f_{N,k}(d)=&\,f_{N-1,k}(d)-\frac{f_{N-1,k}(d+1)-f_{N-1,k}(d)}{f_{N-1,N}(d+1)-f_{N-1,N}(d)}\,f_{N-1,N}(d),\qquad k\geq N+1,\end{split}

(B.6)

initialized with

\left(E_{0}[s]\right)_{d}=s_{d},\qquad f_{0,k}(d)=f_{k}(d).

(B.7)

This implements a sequence of projections, such that
f_{N,k}(d)=0
for
k\leq N

and
\left(E_{N}[s]\right)_{d}
is the desired linear combination.
