Data-driven approximation of regions of attraction via an LP-based selection of PWA Lyapunov functions
Oumayma Khattabi, Matteo Tacchi-Bénard, Martin Gulan, Sorin Olaru

Abstract
This paper presents a method to approximate regions of attraction of unknown nonlinear dynamical systems from data. Assuming point-wise evaluations of the vector field and known Lipschitz bounds, a polyhedral uncertainty set of admissible dynamics is constructed. This uncertainty description enables the synthesis of a continuous PWA ( PWA ) Lyapunov candidate via a LP ( LP ), enforcing a robust decrease condition for all admissible vector fields. The approach allows certification of a RoA ( RoA ) consistent with the available data. Numerical examples illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in extracting certified RoA s from sparse data. Keywords : Data-driven, PWA Lyapunov functions, Region of attraction, Lipschitz continuity, Uncertainty modeling.

Introduction

In control theory, stability analysis is the theoretical foundation for system analysis and the construction of stability certificates. It is typically carried out using a mathematical model of the system dynamics, which is often unavailable or difficult to obtain for nonlinear systems. This limitation can be mitigated through data-driven methods, which have seen a significant increase in use as data and computational power have become widely available. Data-driven stability analysis is carried out through different approaches, among which the construction of Lyapunov functions
[
18
]
or the verification of barrier certificates
[
21
]
are common. These rely on a range of computational tools, including neural networks and convex optimization frameworks, but certifying stability properties such as regions of attraction from data remains challenging.

Neural network-based methods have attracted increasing attention due to their learning and approximation capabilities. For instance, in
[
15
]
, a Lyapunov candidate is learned jointly with the dynamics to certify stability, whereas in
[
25
]
it is learned jointly with the controller to ensure convergence. In
[
7
]
, the approximation of a Lyapunov candidate is used to counteract the curse of dimensionality typically encountered in neural network-based approaches. Neural networks can also be used to learn invariant sets via learned Lyapunov functions for general reference-tracking control laws
[
14
]
. Despite their flexibility, these approaches often lack explicit robustness guarantees and rely on sampled data, which can limit their ability to provide reliable certification.

Other approaches rely on convex optimization and polynomial methods. Sum-of-Squares programming is used in
[
19
]
to compute polynomial representations of the system and certify stability through dissipativity constraints, and in
[
20
]
to develop super-stabilizing controllers to counter measurement corruption and noise. Polynomial optimization is also used in
[
2
]
, where, coupled with Koopman operators, dynamical system properties are certified, and in
[
26
]
, where a data-driven safe controller is developed with barrier constraints to guarantee stability and safety. Such methods rely on function approximations and structural assumptions, which may limit their applicability in fully data-driven settings.

Some early model-based works related to this paper include
[
3
,
4
]
, which proposed systematic procedures for constructing a Lyapunov function for nonlinear systems, and
[
22
]
, which introduced computer-aided methods for the automatic generation of Lyapunov candidates. Other works, such as
[

9
,
17

[

This paper uses an uncertainty set derived from data to induce a differential inclusion framework and construct a
PWA
Lyapunov candidate. Related approaches using differential inclusions include
[
11
]
, which employs a set-valued map of the model for stability certification, and
[

While approaches based on differential inclusions and polyhedral uncertainty bounds, as well as approaches constructing Lyapunov functions from data sets, have been explored in the literature, the proposed approach combines explicit uncertainty sets derived from data with
PWA

Preliminaries and problem formulation

Throughout this paper, we consider the dynamical system

\dot{x}=f(x),

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is an unknown vector field.

We seek to approximate the region of attraction (

\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

2.1
Assumptions

The following assumptions are adopted to formulate the problem and develop the proposed approach:

Assumption 1
.

The system admits a unique locally asymptotically stable equilibrium in
\mathcal{X}
, which, without loss of generality, is taken to be the origin.

Assumption 2
.

The function
f
is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{X}
, and a componentwise upper bound
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
on its Lipschitz constant is known. Specifically, Lipschitz continuity is interpreted with respect to the
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
-norm:

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq M||x-y||_{\infty},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Assumption 3
.

f(.)
is accessible for point-wise evaluation, either through simulation or measurements. This implies the availability of a data set
\mathcal{D}_{N_{d}}=\{(x_{i},f_{i})\}_{i\in[N_{d}]}
, where
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
,
f_{i}=f(x_{i})
and
N_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Assumption 4
.

An arbitrarily small polyhedral set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{X}
containing the equilibrium is available, and the behavior of the system in this set is safe.

2.2
Problem formulation

This work aims to certify the system’s stability in a region around the equilibrium using data. The proposed method consists of constructing a
PWA
Lyapunov function and sub-sequently extracting a certified
RoA
from its level set.

The approximation of the
RoA
is carried out in three main steps. The first step consists of identifying the uncertainty set,
i.e.
, the set of all admissible pairs
(x,f(x))
over
\mathcal{X}
consistent with the available data and the Lipschitz bound. The second step consists of partitioning the state space into a tessellation and, using the uncertainty set, constructing a
PWA
Lyapunov candidate. The third step is to extract a valid
RoA
with stability certificates. The remainder of the paper details these three steps in the following sections.

Lemma 1

Lemma 3

For any

4
PWA Lyapunov candidate

This section presents the method used to derive a Lyapunov candidate function for the uncertainty set consistent with a data set
\mathcal{D}_{N_{d}}
. When defining a Lyapunov candidate, the parametrization must satisfy at least two basic principles:

•

be a universal approximator of a generic nonlinear con-tinuous Lyapunov function;

•

have manageable complexity in the number of degrees of freedom for the approximation.

While several alternatives exist, we focus here on
PWA
functions:

Definition 1
.

Given a polyhedral partition of
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a function
g:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a
PWA
function if

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=\bigcup_{j\in[N_{c}]}Y_{j},\ \ \mathrm{int}(Y_{j})\cap\mathrm{int}(Y_{j^{\prime}})=\varnothing,\,\forall j\neq j^{\prime},

\displaystyle g(x)=a_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}x+b_{j},\ \ \forall x\in Y_{j},

where
a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
N_{c}\in\mathbb{N}
.

These functions are continuous and inherit the polyhedral geometry of the sets
Y_{j}
, while satisfying the two desiderata stated above. Moreover,
PWA
functions involve a partition whose structure is closely related to the polyhedral partition induced by the differential inclusion describing the uncertainty in Section
3
.

4.1
State-space tessellation as a design parameter

A
PWA
function, according to Definition
1
, relies on a partition and associated coefficients
(a_{j},b_{j})
. In this work, we restrict attention to polyhedral tessellations of the state space, which provide a structured class of partitions suitable for our construction.
For Lyapunov-based certification, we treat the tessellation as a design degree of freedom and enforce Lyapunov-related properties by optimizing the coefficients
(a_{j},b_{j})
.

Accordingly, we propose to construct the tessellation by selecting, in an initial phase of the procedure, its vertices
\{v_{u}\}_{u\in[N_{v}]}
. The rationale for this choice is the availability of points obtained as projections of the vertices of
\mathrm{graph}(\mathbf{F})
onto the state space.
This choice serves two purposes. First, it adapts the tessellation complexity to the complexity of the dynamics using information encoded in the uncertainty set. Second, it ensures consistency between the state-space partition and the uncertainty set, which can be exploited in the certification procedure.

The tessellation is constructed from geometric features of
\mathrm{graph}(\mathbf{F})
. In particular, the vertices
\{v_{u}\}_{u\in[N_{v}]}
are obtained by projecting the vertices of
\mathrm{graph}(\mathbf{F})
onto the state space. This ensures that
\{v_{u}\}_{u\in[N_{v}]}
capture the extreme points of the uncertainty set. Ideally, the resulting tessellation forms a subpartition of the partition induced by the projection of
\mathrm{graph}(\mathbf{F})

•

•

\nabla V(x)^{\mathsf{T}}f(x)=a_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}f(x)<0,\,\forall j\in[N_{c}],\,\forall x\in\mathrm{int}(Y_{j})\setminus\{0\}.

(18)

For

of

\mathcal{L}^{V}_{\alpha}\triangleq\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid V(x)\leq\alpha\}.

.

If there exists

satisfying (

Proof.

By Proposition
1
, admissible dynamics satisfy

. Since

satisfies (

) for all admissible

Proposition 2
.

Let
V(\cdot)
satisfy (
16
)–(
18
) for all admissible
f(\cdot)\in\mathbf{F}(\cdot)
, and let

be a sublevel set for some

, with

\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{X}
is such that
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{L}^{V}_{\alpha}

\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{L}^{V}_{\alpha}
defines a certified

consistent with the data.

Proof.

By Proposition
1
, admissible dynamics satisfy
\dot{x}\in\mathbf{F}(x)
, and

is a Lyapunov function for this inclusion on
\mathcal{X}
. By Lemma

Proposition 3
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}
. If

\nabla V^{\mathsf{T}}z<0\quad\forall z\in\mathcal{F}_{v}(x)

(

*)

is verified, then (

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{F}(x)
is a convex polyhedral set (a hyperbox), any

can be expressed as a convex combination of its vertices

. The mapping

is linear, and thus its maximum over
\mathbf{F}(x)
is attained at the vertices, i.e.

\max_{z\in\mathbf{F}(x)}\nabla V^{\mathsf{T}}z=\max_{z\in\mathcal{F}_{v}(x)}\nabla V^{\mathsf{T}}z.

If
\nabla V^{\mathsf{T}}z<0
for all

\nabla V^{\mathsf{T}}z<0
for all

The preceding results show that stability can be certified directly from data by constructing a Lyapunov function
V(.)
that decreases along all admissible dynamics consistent with the available information. In particular, Proposition

shows that the connected component of the sublevel set
\mathcal{L}^{V}_{\alpha}
containing the equilibrium defines a certified
RoA

To make this condition practically verifiable, the key idea behind Proposition

is to replace the unknown vector field
f(\cdot)
in (

\mathbf{F}(x)
, these vertices fully characterize the admissible values of the vector field, so the decrease condition can be enforced through a finite number of inequalities.

PWA
Lyapunov candidate and an associated region of attraction. The resulting formulation admits an implementable algorithm proposed in this section.

The second issue arises from the equilibrium point itself and the points in its neighborhood, for the same reason as the infeasibility of constraint (

Alternative PWA Lyapunov constructions

The approach proposed in the previous subsection explicitly describes the uncertainty bounds before constructing the Lyapunov function, thereby decoupling the computational burden into two separate stages—uncertainty characterization and LP-based certification. As an alternative, in

Conclusion

This paper proposes a data-driven framework for constructing PWA Lyapunov functions and approximating the region of attraction for nonlinear systems with unknown dynamics. The approach relies on the explicit construction of a polyhedral uncertainty set from data, which induces a differential inclusion describing all admissible dynamics. This representation enables a tractable LP formulation for enforcing Lyapunov conditions over the uncertainty set.

A key feature of the proposed method is the separation of uncertainty characterization from Lyapunov certification, thereby improving interpretability and enabling iterative refinement via data enrichment. The resulting PWA Lyapunov candidates provide certified regions of attraction, even when the available data is sparse. The comparison with an alternative SOCP formulation highlights the trade-off between computational complexity and structural transparency.

Future work includes improving scalability and refining the state-space tessellation to derive sufficient conditions for its construction.
