Comment on comparator design in regional versus general anesthesia for ambulatory breast-conserving surgery
Authors: Ming-Hui Hung, Shu-Yueh Cheng
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with interest the randomized trial by Ling et al. reporting higher early Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) scores with regional anesthesia and dexmedetomidine sedation than with general anesthesia for ambulatory breast-conserving surgery [1]. We wish to raise one point relevant to interpretation of these findings.

The general anesthesia comparator included both induction-dose sufentanil and intraoperative remifentanil. Although the authors acknowledge that opioid-sparing anesthesia reduces early adverse effects, their comparator was not designed accordingly. This is important because remifentanil may worsen early recovery after discontinuation through opioid-related rebound pain and nausea [2]. The item-level QoR-15 data support this concern. At 2 hours, the largest between-group differences were seen in functional items, including personal hygiene and readiness for home or work, rather than pain items, while the sleep item favored general anesthesia. Postoperative nausea and vomiting were also more frequent in the general anesthesia group. By 24 hours, total QoR-15 scores had narrowed to the minimum clinically important difference [3]. This pattern may partly reflect remifentanil-related early adverse effects rather than anesthetic technique alone. Future trials using an opioid-minimizing general anesthesia comparator may better define the independent benefit of regional anesthesia in this setting.
