Pro- p p Iwahori-Hecke modules in semisimple rank one and singularity categories
Nicolas Dupré

Abstract
. Let 𝔉 {\mathfrak{F}} be a non-archimedean local field of residue characteristic p p and G G be one of the groups GL 2 ​ ( 𝔉 ) {\mathrm{GL}}_{2}({\mathfrak{F}}) , SL 2 ​ ( 𝔉 ) {\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathfrak{F}}) or PGL 2 ​ ( 𝔉 ) {\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}({\mathfrak{F}}) . Let ℋ G {\mathcal{H}}_{G} denote the pro- p p Iwahori-Hecke algebra of G G over 𝔽 ¯ p \overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p} . We study the homotopy category Ho ⁡ ( ℋ G ) {\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G}) of Hovey’s Gorenstein projective model structure on the category of ℋ G {\mathcal{H}}_{G} -modules and relate it to the singularity category Sing ⁡ ( X q , 𝐆 ) {\operatorname{Sing}}(X_{q,{\mathbf{G}}}) of an explicit scheme. When G = GL 2 ​ ( 𝔉 ) G={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}({\mathfrak{F}}) , this scheme was first introduced by Dotto-Emerton-Gee [ 8 ] . We obtain in that case an equivalence Ho ⁡ ( ℋ GL 2 ) ≃ Sing ⁡ ( X q , GL 2 ) {\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}})\simeq{\operatorname{Sing}}(X_{q,{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}) and recover from this Große-Klönne’s mod- p p Langlands correspondence for Hecke modules [ 16 ] , building on work of Pépin-Schmidt [ 37 ] . We furthermore describe Ho ⁡ ( ℋ G ) {\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G}) completely explicitly when G = SL 2 ​ ( 𝔉 ) G={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathfrak{F}}) or PGL 2 ​ ( 𝔉 ) {\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}({\mathfrak{F}}) , and make additional computations in the GL 2 {\mathrm{GL}}_{2} case.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime and
{\mathfrak{F}}
be a non-archimedean local field with residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of characteristic
p
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a split reductive group over the field
{\mathfrak{F}}
and let
G={\mathbf{G}}({\mathfrak{F}})
. Let
I
be a pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of
G
and let
{\mathcal{H}}_{G}=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}[I\backslash G/I]
be the corresponding
pro-
p
Iwahori-Hecke algebra
of
G
, i.e. the convolution algebra of
I
-biinvariant compactly supported functions on
G
. Write
{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
for the category of left
{\mathcal{H}}_{G}
-modules.

The Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{G}
plays an important role in the mod-
p
Langlands program. For instance, by work of Große-Klönne
[
16
]
it is known when
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
and
{\mathfrak{F}}
is an extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
that there is a functorial bijection
M\mapsto V(M)
between the isomorphism classes of
n
-dimensional simple supersingular
{\mathcal{H}}_{G}
-modules and the isomorphism classes of irreducible, continuous representations
{\operatorname{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathfrak{F}}}/{\mathfrak{F}})\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
. Furthermore, when
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
there is a scheme with connected components given by chains of projective lines, introduced by Dotto-Emerton-Gee
[
8
]
and Pépin-Schmidt
[
37
]
, whose
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-rational points are in canonical bijection with the isomorphism classes of semisimple 2-dimensional Galois representations. Pépin-Schmidt show that this scheme is a certain quotient of the centre
Z({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}})
of the Hecke algebra and use this relationship to reconstruct geometrically Große-Klönne’s bijection in that case. A more general link between
Z({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}})
and Galois representations was also obtained in the recent preprint
[
38
]
.

The algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{G}
is in general known to be a Gorenstein ring
[
31
]
and it follows from work of Hovey
[
17
]
that
{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
has a so-called Gorenstein projective model structure. In earlier work
[
10
]
we investigated the associated homotopy category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
, which is the localisation of
{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
obtained after trivialising all modules of finite projective dimension. In particular, we showed that distinct simple supersingular
{\mathcal{H}}_{G}
-modules remain generically non-isomorphic in
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
. The goal of the present work is to describe the category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
in more details when
{\mathbf{G}}
has semisimple rank one, and to relate it to Galois representations via the aforementioned constructions.

From now on, we assume that
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{GL_{2}}
,
\mathrm{SL_{2}}
or
\mathrm{PGL_{2}}
. The first part of our paper aims to explicitly describe the category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
, which we recall is by construction triangulated. Our first result achieves this when
{\mathbf{G}}
is semisimple.

Theorem A

(
3.29
)
.

There is an equivalence

{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})\simeq{\mathcal{C}}\times\prod_{\text{$\gamma$ regular}}\left({\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}\times{\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}\right),

of triangulated categories, where
{\mathcal{C}}=0
if
p=2
and
{\mathcal{C}}={\operatorname{Mod}}(k)_{\operatorname{triv}}\times{\operatorname{Mod}}(k)_{\operatorname{triv}}
if
p>2
. Assuming
p>2
, there is also an equivalence

{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}})\simeq\prod_{\text{$\gamma$ regular}}{\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}

of triangulated categories.

The notation
{\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}
and
{\operatorname{Mod}}(k)_{\operatorname{triv}}
in the above statement refers to certain rather simple triangulated structures on these categories, cf.
3.24
. The products in the statement of the above theorem are indexed by the so-called regular Weyl group orbits in the character group of the finite torus
\mathbf{T}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. The reason for the extra factor
{\mathcal{C}}
when
p>2
and
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
is that, in that case, the algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
has an additional component corresponding to the ‘sign’ character
\sigma
of
\mathbf{T}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
which sends a generator to
-1
. The proof of
A
uses Bruhat-Tits theory and the canonical resolutions of Ollivier-Schneider
[
31
]
to reduce the question to describing the homotopy category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}})
of the finite Hecke algebra associated to the standard maximal compact subgroup of
G
, cf.
3.3
. The latter homotopy category can then be computed directly, by describing the module categories rather explicitly (cf.
3.22
and
3.25
). We note that this reduction argument to
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}})
has an analogue on the side of smooth
G
-representations, cf.
3.5
.

We then aim to study
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
in terms of singularity categories. We recall that the singularity category of a Noetherian separated scheme
X
, as defined by Krause
[
26
]
, is the triangulated category of chain complexes of injective quasi coherent sheaves on
X
up to chain homotopy. These categories also feature in the geometric Langlands program under the more general definition
{\operatorname{Ind}}({\operatorname{Coh}}(X))/{\operatorname{QCoh}}(X)
, see
4.2
and the references therein. Our main result relates
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{G})
to the singularity category
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X_{q,{\mathbf{G}}})
of a projective scheme. When
{\mathbf{G}}\neq{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
, the scheme
X_{q,{\mathbf{G}}}
is constructed very explicitly as a quotient of
{\operatorname{Spec}}(Z({\mathcal{H}}_{G}))
and parametrises suitable Galois representations. In particular,
X_{q,{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}
is the aforementioned scheme of Dotto-Emerton-Gee and Pépin-Schmidt. The case
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
is slightly different because
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
has in a suitable sense more singularities than its centre. We will elaborate further on this below. We construct
X_{q,{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
instead as a quotient of
{\operatorname{Spec}}({\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}})
, for a certain explicit central subalgebra
{\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}}
of the affine Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2},{\operatorname{aff}}}
which contains
Z({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
.

Theorem B

(
4.9
,
4.11
&
4.14
)
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be as above and assume that
p>2
. There is a triangulated functor
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathbf{G}},*}:{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathbf{G}}})\to{\operatorname{Sing}}(X_{q,{\mathbf{G}}})
, which is an equivalence when
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
or
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
. Furthermore, there is a well-defined induced map

\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{simple supersingular}\\
\text{${\mathcal{H}}_{G}$-modules of}\\
\text{infinite projective dimension}\end{subarray}\right\}_{/\cong}\longrightarrow\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{singular $\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}$-points}\\
\text{of $X_{q,{\mathbf{G}}}$}\end{subarray}\right\}

given by
M\mapsto{\operatorname{Supp}}(\mathscr{L}_{{\mathbf{G}},*}(M))
. This map satisfies the following:

(i)

it is a bijection if
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
or
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
and agrees with Große-Klönne’s map, in other words
{\operatorname{Supp}}(\mathscr{L}_{{\mathbf{G}},*}(M))=V(M)
, when
{\mathfrak{F}}
is an extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
; and

(ii)

it is surjective if
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
, with fibers equal to the
L
-packets of simple supersingular modules as defined in
[
24
]
.

Part (i) above makes sense because of the canonical bijection between
X_{q,{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
and the set of isomorphism classes of semsimiple 2-dimensional Galois representations from
[
37
]
and because
X_{q,{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}}
is by definition a closed subscheme of
X_{q,{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}
. The notion of support in the statement was defined by Krause in
[
26
]
and can be characterised in terms of injective dimensions in the affine case, see
4.6
. By construction, it is here always a subset of the singular locus of
X_{q,{\mathbf{G}}}
. We also point out that the restriction to simple supersingulars of infinite projective dimension is rather mild by a result of Koziol
[
25
]
, see
Section

2.2
for the details.

The functor
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathbf{G}},*}
is constructed by utilising a certain
spherical
Hecke module, which was introduced in
[
36
]
for the generic pro-
p
Iwahori-Hecke algebra of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
. We define analogues of this construction for
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
and
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
which fit our purposes. The proof of
B
then mainly consists in showing that pushing forward along the quotient morphism
{\operatorname{Spec}}(Z({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}))\to X_{q,{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}
from
[
37
]
induces an equivalence of singularity categories. We also give an explicit description of
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2},*}
via
A
, cf.
3.30
. Heuristically, the above says that our functor
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathbf{G}},*}
lifts the known mod-
p
Langlands correspondences for Hecke modules to a nice functor between singularity categories. In particular, the fact that
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2},*}
is not an equivalence may be viewed as analogous to the mod-
p
Langlands correspondence not being a bijection in that case.

We also note that Ardakov-Schneider constructed a scheme
\tilde{X}_{q,{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
in
[
2
]
, given by a disjoint union of two chains of projective lines, using the action of
Z({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
on the Hecke-Ext algebra. Our scheme
X_{q,{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
is obtained by gluing one more
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
at the end of one of their two chains, and that end point corresponds exactly to the aforementioned
\sigma
-component of
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
. This component has a non-zero contribution to
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
as seen in
A
, but its centre is a polynomial algebra and thus has no singularities. Our result says that, morally, that end point of
\tilde{X}_{q,{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
should be a singularity in order to fully capture
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
. We note that an interpretation of the
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
-rational points of
\tilde{X}_{q,{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
in terms of Galois representations is expected to appear in
[
42
]
, and that the sheaves in
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X_{q,{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
are supported on
\tilde{X}_{q,{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
.

At the end of our paper, we do some additional computations in the case
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
. Namely, we show that
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}})
is equivalent to the derived category of an explicit DGA by singling out a generator, cf.
5.2
and
5.4
. This generator is closely related to the aforementioned spherical module. Finally, we show that the endomorphism rings of the simple supersingular modules in
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}})
are isomorphic to the regular component of the finite Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}
, cf.
5.5
.

Conventions and notation

We fix a prime
p
and a nonarchimedean local field
{\mathfrak{F}}
of residue characteristic
p
. We write
{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{F}}}
for the valuation ring of
{\mathfrak{F}}
with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{F}}}
, uniformiser
\varpi
, and residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Throughout this paper we fix
k\coloneqq\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
and an embedding
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\subseteq k
. For any group
H
, the trivial
H
-representation on
k
will be denoted by
\mathbf{1}
.

If we denote an adjunction by
F:{\mathcal{C}}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{D}}:U
then
F
is always assumed to be left adjoint to
U
. The unit (resp. the counit) of an adjunction will always be denoted by
\eta
(resp.
\varepsilon
). We also say that
F

preserves
(resp.
reflects
) a property (P) if
F*
(resp.
*
) has property (P) whenever
*
(resp.
F*
) does. All rings considered will be assumed to be unital and associative. For such a ring
R
we will denote by
{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
the category of left
R
-modules. When working with chain complexes of
R
-modules, our convention is that differentials have degree
+1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Assumptions and notation for
{\mathbf{G}}

We let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a split reductive group over
{\mathfrak{F}}
and set
G={\mathbf{G}}({\mathfrak{F}})
. We fix a maximal split
{\mathfrak{F}}
-torus
\mathbf{T}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
. The torus
T=\mathbf{T}({\mathfrak{F}})
of
G
has a maximal compact subgroup
T^{0}
, and we let
T^{1}
be its maximal pro-
p
subgroup. The quotient
T^{0}/T^{1}
is then a finite group which we denote by
T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.

We let
\mathscr{X}
denote the semisimple Bruhat-Tits building. We fix a chamber
C
in the standard apartment of
\mathscr{X}
and a hyperspecial vertex
x_{0}
in
\overline{C}
. Given a face
F
in
\mathscr{X}
, we let
P_{F}
denote the corresponding parahoric subgroup of
G
and
P_{F}^{\dagger}
be the stabiliser subgroup of
F
in
G
. We now let
I
be the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup corresponding to our choice of central chamber, i.e. the pro-
p
radical of
P_{C}
.

Note that
T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is a normal subgroup of
\widetilde{W}\coloneqq N_{G}(T)/T^{1}
with quotient
W=N_{G}(T)/T^{0}
. The choice of
C
determines a set of positive roots and hence a basis in the root system of
({\mathbf{G}},\mathbf{T})
. This in turn gives length function
\ell
on the finite Weyl group
N_{G}(T)/T
, which extends to both
W
and
\widetilde{W}
. There is an equality
W=\Omega\rtimes W_{\operatorname{aff}}
, where
\Omega=\{w\in W\,:\,\ell(w)=0\}
and
W_{\operatorname{aff}}
denotes the affine Weyl group generated by the set of simple affine reflections fixing the walls of
C
. The group
\Omega
is finitely generated abelian and we write
\Omega_{\text{tor}}
for its (finite) torsion subgroup. We will also denote by
\widetilde{\Omega}
the pre-image of
\Omega
in
\widetilde{W}
.

We will be primarily be interested in the case where
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{SL_{2}}
,
\mathrm{PGL_{2}}
or
\mathrm{GL_{2}}
. In that case, we will always choose
\mathbf{T}
to be the standard torus of diagonal matrices, and moreover
C
and the central vertex
x_{0}
are fixed so that
I=\begin{pmatrix}1+{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{F}}}&{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{F}}}\\
{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{F}}}&1+{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathfrak{F}}}\end{pmatrix}
and
P_{x_{0}}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{F}}})
,
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}({\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{F}}})
or
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}({\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{F}}})
as is usual. When
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{PGL_{2}}
or
\mathrm{GL_{2}}
, we write
\omega
for (the image of) the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}0&\varpi\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
in
G
. By abuse of notation, we will also denote by
\omega
the image of that matrix in
W
and
\widetilde{W}
. It then holds that
\Omega=\langle\omega\rangle
, so that
\Omega\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
for
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
and
\Omega\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
for
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
. In the case
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
we have
\Omega=1
. We let
x_{1}=\omega\cdot x_{0}
and note that
C
is the chamber/edge joining
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
. We also write
\alpha
for the unique simple root in the root system, and
s_{0}\coloneqq s_{\alpha}
and
s_{1}
for the two simple affine reflections. The group
\Omega
acts on the affine Weyl group by conjugation and we note that
\omega s_{0}\omega^{-1}=s_{1}
and that
\omega^{2}
acts trivially.

2.2.
Hecke algebras and supersingular modules

The pro-
p
Iwahori Hecke algebra is the convolution algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{G}=k[I\backslash G/I]
of
I
-biinvariant compactly supported functions on
G
. We will sometimes omit the subscript
G
when dealing with general
G
or when the group is clear from context. The set
I\backslash G/I
is in bijection with
\widetilde{W}
and there is an associated Iwahori-Matsumoto basis
\{T_{w}\mid w\in\widetilde{W}\}
of
{\mathcal{H}}
. For each face
F
of
\mathscr{X}
, we have corresponding convolution algebras
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}=k[I\backslash P_{F}^{\dagger}/I]
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}=k[I\backslash P_{F}/I]
, and there are canonical inclusions
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
.

Specialising to our rank one situation, recall that the affine Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
of
G
is the
k
-algebra generated by
T_{t}
(
t\in T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
),
T_{s_{0}}
and
T_{s_{1}}
, satisfying the braid relations and the quadratic relations

T_{s_{i}}^{2}=T_{s_{i}}\cdot\left(|\mu_{\alpha}|\sum_{t\in\alpha^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times})}T_{t}\right)

for
i=0,1
, where
\alpha^{\vee}
denotes the unique positive coroot of
{\mathbf{G}}
and
\mu_{\alpha}\cong\ker(\alpha^{\vee}|_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}})
has order one for
{\mathbf{G}}\neq{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
or two otherwise, cf.
[
33
, §2.2, eqs. (27)-(28), & §2.1.6, eq. (13)]
. From the quadratic relations, any character
\chi
of
{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
automatically satisfies
\chi(T_{s_{i}})\in\{0,-1\}
for
i=0,1
. We say that
\chi
is a
supersingular character
in either one of the following two situations:

(i)

the restriction
\chi|_{\alpha^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times})}
is non-trivial, in which case we automatically have
\chi(T_{s_{i}})=0
for
i=0,1
by the quadratic relations; or

(ii)

the restriction
\chi|_{\alpha^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times})}
is trivial and
\chi(T_{s_{0}})\neq\chi(T_{s_{1}})
.

By Koziol’s result
[
25
, Theorem 7.7]
, we have that a supersingular character
\chi
has finite projective dimension if and only if it is given as in (ii) above.

We then have
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}={\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2},{\operatorname{aff}}}
. A supersingular simple
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
-module will then just mean a supersingular character as above. In the other two cases,
{\mathcal{H}}
is generated over
{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
by
T_{\omega}^{\pm 1}
. This satisfies the relations
T_{\omega}T_{s_{0}}T_{\omega}^{-1}=T_{s_{1}}
and
T_{\omega}T_{t}T_{\omega}^{-1}=T_{s_{0}\cdot t}
for all
t\in T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. In particular,
T_{\omega}
normalises
{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
. We also note that
\omega^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\varpi&0\\
0&\varpi\end{pmatrix}
, and consequently we have that
T_{\omega}^{2}=1
, resp.
T_{\omega}^{2}
is central, in
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}}
, resp. in
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}
. A simple
supersingular

{\mathcal{H}}
-module is in these two cases then a module
M
defined as follows. We have

M=ke_{0}\oplus ke_{1}

with

h\cdot e_{0}=\chi(h)e_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad h\cdot e_{1}=\chi^{\omega}(h)e_{1}\coloneqq\chi(T_{\omega^{-1}}hT_{\omega})e_{1}

for all
h\in{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
, where
\chi
is a supersingular character of
{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
as above. Furthermore,
T_{\omega}e_{0}=e_{1}
and
T_{\omega}e_{1}=\lambda e_{0}
for some fixed scalar
\lambda\in k^{\times}
. When
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{PGL_{2}}
, we necessarily have
\lambda=1
.

Remark 2.1
.

Concretely, the relationships between the Hecke algebras of all three groups is as follows. We have that
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
is a subalgebra of
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2},{\operatorname{aff}}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2},{\operatorname{aff}}}=k[T_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})]\otimes_{k[T_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})]}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}
as
(k[T_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})],{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
-bimodules. Meanwhile, we have

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}}={\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}/(T_{\omega}^{2}-1,T_{t}-1:t\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\cdot{\operatorname{id}}).

so that
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}}
is a quotient of
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}
.

Additionally to the above, we will consider the subalgebras of
{\mathcal{H}}
corresponding to the vertices
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
and the chamber
C
. Namely,
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}
(
i=0,1
) is the subalgebra generated by
T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
and
T_{s_{i}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
is the group algebra
k[T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})]
, viewed as a subalgebra of
{\mathcal{H}}
. Note that, being semisimple,
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
has global dimension 0.

If
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
, we then have
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}^{\dagger}={\mathcal{H}}_{C}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}={\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}
(
i=0,1
). When
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
or
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
, we instead have that
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}^{\dagger}=k[\widetilde{\Omega}]
is the subalgebra of
{\mathcal{H}}
generated by
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
and
T_{\omega}
, and
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}
(
i=0,1
) is the subalgebra of
{\mathcal{H}}
generated by
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}
and
T_{\omega}^{2}
. For
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}^{\dagger}
is the group algebra
k[T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}]
while for
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
it is a skew-Laurent polynomial algebra over
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
, and hence necessarily of global dimension 1 in the latter case. Finally, if
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
then
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}={\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}
(
i=0,1
), while for
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
we have that
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}^{\dagger}
is isomorphic to the Laurent polynomial ring
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{i}}[Z^{\pm 1}]
.

2.3.
Gorenstein rings and homotopy categories

We recall that a ring
R
is called
Gorenstein
if it is left and right Noetherian and has finite selfinjective dimension both as a left and as a right module. In this case, the left and right selfinjective dimensions of
R
have equal value
n
, say, and an
R
-module has finite projective dimension if and only if it has finite injective dimension, in which case both of these dimensions are bounded above by
n
, cf.
[
12
, 9.1.8 & 9.1.10]
. We will call such an
R
-module
trivial
. The relevance of this notion to us is that the
k
-algebras
{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}
are all Gorenstein, cf.
[
31
, Theorem 0.1 & Proposition 5.5]
, and furthermore the algebras
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}

(F\subseteq\overline{C})
are even self-injective, cf.
[
9
, Example 4.1(ii)]
.

2.3.1.
Hovey’s model structures on
{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)

We recall that if
R
is a Gorenstein ring then a module
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
is called
Gorenstein projective
, resp.
Gorenstein injective
, if there is an isomorphism
M\cong Z_{0}(E_{\bullet})
, where
E_{\bullet}
is an acyclic chain complex of
R
-modules which is termwise projective, respectively termwise injective. Such a chain complex will then be called a
complete resolution
for
M
. Furthermore, we recall that a Gorenstein projective module, resp. Gorenstein injective module, is trivial if and only if it is projective, resp. injective, cf.
[
17
, Corollary 8.5]
. We also recall as a special case that when
R
is self-injective then the classes of injective and projective modules coincide, and hence every
R
-module has a complete resolution by combining an injective and a projective resolution. Thus every module is both Gorenstein projective and Gorenstein injective in that case.

Example 2.2
.

We provide an example of a non-trivial Gorenstein projective module for the ring
A=k[X_{1},X_{2}]/(X_{1}X_{2})
, as this will play a role for us later. Note that this ring is Gorenstein by
[
4
, 0AWX & 0DWA]
but is not self-injective. We consider the module
M\coloneqq A/X_{1}A\cong X_{2}A
. This occurs as the zero-th cycles of the complete resolution

\cdots\xrightarrow{X_{1}\cdot}A\xrightarrow{X_{2}\cdot}A\xrightarrow{X_{1}\cdot}A\xrightarrow{X_{2}\cdot}\cdots

and thus is Gorenstein projective. Truncating this complex gives a projective resolution for
M
, and using this one computes that

{\operatorname{Ext}}^{j}_{A}(M,M)=\begin{cases}M&\text{if $j=0$}\\
M/X_{2}M\cong k&\text{if $j\geq 2$ is even}\\
0&\text{if $j$ is odd}\end{cases}.

In particular,
M
has infinite projective dimension. A completely analogous computation holds for the module
A/X_{2}A
, which is thus also Gorenstein projective of infinite projective dimension.

Using these notions, Hovey constructed the so-called Gorenstein projective and Goreinstein injective model structures on
{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
, cf.
[
17
, Theorem 8.6]
. We do not recall here the formalism of (abelian) model categories, we refer the reader instead to our previous works
[
9
, §1]
and
[
10
, §2.4]
as well as the references therein. In this paper we will exclusively work with the aforementioned model structures on
{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
, which are defined as follows. In the Gorenstein projective model structure, the fibrations are the epimorphisms and the cofibrations are the monomorphisms with Gorenstein projective cokernel. Dually, the Gorenstein injective model structure has fibrations the epimorphisms with Gorenstein injective kernel and cofibrations the monomorphisms. In both cases, a fibration, resp. cofibration, is a weak equivalence if and only if its kernel, resp. cokernel, is trivial. Furthermore, the factorisation axioms and
[
17
, Lemma 5.8]
imply that the weak equivalences in both of these model structures are the same, namely they are given by the smallest class of morphisms closed under composition and containing all epimorphisms with trivial kernels and all monomorphisms with trivial cokernel. Consequently, the two model structures have the same homotopy category, which we will denote by
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
(see also
[
10
, Remark 2.12]
).

If
M,N\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
are two (left)
R
-modules then we will set
[M,N]_{R}\coloneqq{\operatorname{Hom}}_{{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)}(M,N)
. By
[
17
, Proposition 9.1]
, whenever
M
is Gorenstein projective we have

(2.1)

[M,N]_{R}={\operatorname{Hom}}_{R}(M,N)/\sim

where the homotopy relation
\sim
trivialises those maps which factor through a projective
R
-module. We next recall that
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
is a triangulated category with arbitrary direct sums, where direct sums are computed by taking the image in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
of direct sums in
{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
, cf.
[
15
, Theorem 6.34 & Corollary 8.9(i)]
. The triangulated structure is given as follows:

•

For
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
, its shift
\Sigma(M)
is given as the cokernel of any injection into a module of finite projective dimension. This is well-defined up to isomorphism in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
.

•

Given a short exact sequence

0\to M_{1}\to M_{2}\to M_{3}\to 0

in
{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
, there is a canonical way of constructing a morphism
M_{3}\to\Sigma M_{1}
in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
so that the resulting triangle
M_{1}\to M_{2}\to M_{3}\to\Sigma M_{1}
is distinguished (see e.g.
[
10
, eq. (2.11)]
, applied to the Gorenstein injevctive model structure). Furthermore, any distinguished triangle in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
is up to shift isomorphic to one of this form.

Finally, by
[
17
, Theorem 9.3]
we have for a Gorenstein projective module
M
, any
N\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
, and any
i\geq 1
that

(2.2)

[\Sigma^{-i}M,N]_{R}\cong[M,\Sigma^{i}N]_{R}\cong{\operatorname{Ext}}_{R}^{i}(M,N).

2.3.2.
Tensor-hom adjunctions between homotopy categories

Let
F:{\mathcal{C}}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{D}}:U
be an adjunction between the underlying categories of two model categories. We recall that this adjunction is called
Quillen
when
F
preserves cofibrations and trivial cofiabrations (or equivalently, if
U
preserves fibrations and trivial fibrations). In that case the functor
F
, resp.
U
, is called
left Quillen
, resp.
right Quillen
. We will then denote by
\mathbf{L}F:{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{C}})\rightleftarrows{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{D}}):\mathbf{R}U
the induced total derived adjunction between the homotopy categories.

Suppose now that
R
and
S
are two Gorenstein rings, and let
X
be an
(S,R)
-bimodule. Then we write
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{X}
for the adjoint functors
X\otimes_{R}(-)
and
{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(X,-)
respectively. We will need criteria to determine when this adjunction is Quillen.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
X
is projective as a left
S
-module and flat as a right
R
-module. Then the adjunction

{\mathcal{L}}_{X}:{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)\rightleftarrows{\operatorname{Mod}}(S):{\mathcal{R}}_{X}

is Quillen for the Gorenstein projective model structures. Moreover,
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{X}
preserve weak equivalences so that there are natural isomorphisms
\mathbf{L}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}(M)\cong{\mathcal{L}}_{X}(M)
and
\mathbf{R}{\mathcal{R}}_{X}(N)\cong{\mathcal{R}}_{X}(N)
in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
and
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
respectively, for all
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
and all
N\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(S)
, i.e. the derived adjunction

\mathbf{L}{\mathcal{L}}_{X}:{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)\rightleftarrows{\operatorname{Ho}}(S):\mathbf{R}{\mathcal{R}}_{X}

is computed without the need for any replacements.

Proof.

By our assumptions on
X
, the functor
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
is exact and preserves projectives. Therefore,
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
preserves both Gorenstein projectives and modules of finite projective dimension. It follows that
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
preserves both monomorphisms with Gorenstein projective cokernels (i.e. cofibrations) and monomorphisms with projective cokernel (i.e. trivial cofibrations) and so is left Quillen. By what we’ve shown, we therefore have that
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
is exact and preserves trivial objects. Furthermore, we also have that
{\mathcal{R}}_{X}
is exact and that it preserves trivial objects since those are trivially fibrant and
{\mathcal{R}}_{X}
is right Quillen. By applying
[
10
, Lemma 2.8]
, we now immediately get that
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{X}
preserve all weak equivalences and that the derived adjunction is of the claimed form.
∎

In order to test whether a Quillen adjunction like in the above can be a Quillen equivalence, the following result will be useful.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
F:{\mathcal{C}}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{D}}:G
is a Quillen adjunction between model categories. If
F
reflects and preserves weak equivalences, then the adjunction is a Quillen equivalence if and only if the counit
\varepsilon_{M}:FG(M)\to M
is a weak equivalence for all fibrant objects
M
in
{\mathcal{D}}
. Dually, if
G
reflects and preserves weak equivalences, then the adjunction is a Quillen equivalence if and only if the unit
\eta_{N}:N\to GF(N)
is a weak equivalence for all cofibrant objects
N
in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

This is just
[
9
, Lemma 1.6]
and its dual, whose statement assumes that the model structure on one side is right or left transferred from the other side, but whose proof applies verbatim with our assumptions.
∎

We will later need to consider Laurent polynomial rings over certain Gorenstein algebras. Such algebras are themselves automatically Gorenstein, but since we did not find an explicit reference we record it here.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
R
is a Gorenstein ring. Then:

(i)

the polynomial ring
R[Z]
is Gorenstein; and

(ii)

any Ore localisation
\mathrm{S}^{-1}R
at a central multiplicative set
\mathrm{S}
is Gorenstein.

In particular, the Laurent polynomial ring
R[Z^{\pm 1}]
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

It is clear in both cases that the rings are left and right Noetherian, noting for (ii) that
\mathrm{S}^{-1}R
is both a left and a right Ore localisation since
\mathrm{S}
is central. The fact that
R[Z]
has finite self-injective dimension follows immediately from
[
11
, Corollary 2.2]
. For the localisation, the finite self-injective dimension property is equivalent to
\mathrm{S}^{-1}R
having finite injective dimension as a (left and right)
R
-module. But this is immediate from the fact that
\mathrm{S}^{-1}R
is flat over
R
(cf.
[
17
, Theorem 8.2]
).
∎

In the Laurent polynomial case, the next couple of results will be useful to us later on when trying to establish Quillen equivalences.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
S=R[Z^{\pm 1}]
is a Laurent polynomial ring over
R
and that
X=S
. Then the functors
{\mathcal{R}}_{S}={\operatorname{Res}}^{S}_{R}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{S}
each preserve and reflect weak equivalences.

Proof.

By
2.3
the two functors preserve weak equivalences. For reflection, it suffices to show that they reflect modules of finite projective dimension by
[
10
, Lemma 2.8(ii)]
. For this, let
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
and
N\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(S)
and assume that both
{\mathcal{L}}_{S}(M)
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{S}(N)
have finite projective dimension. We have that
M
is an
R
-linear direct summand of
{\mathcal{L}}_{S}(M)=M[Z^{\pm 1}]
, which gives the statement for
{\mathcal{L}}_{S}
. For
{\mathcal{R}}_{S}
, we apply
[
29
, Proposition 7.5.2]
to get that
{\operatorname{pd}}_{S}(N)\leq{\operatorname{pd}}_{R}(N)+1<\infty
as claimed.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that
X
is an
(S,R)
-bimodule which is projective as a left
S
-module and flat as a right
R
-module. Furthermore, consider the Laurent polynomial rings
R^{\prime}=R[Z^{\pm 1}]
and
S^{\prime}=S[Z^{\pm 1}]
. Let
X^{\prime}\coloneqq X[Z^{\pm 1}]
and assume that
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
is a left Quillen equivalence. Then
{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{\prime}}:{\operatorname{Mod}}(R^{\prime})\to{\operatorname{Mod}}(S^{\prime})
is also a left Quillen equivalence.

Proof.

First, we note that
X^{\prime}\cong X\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
as an
(S,R^{\prime})
-bimodule and
X^{\prime}\cong S^{\prime}\otimes_{S}X
as an
(S^{\prime},R)
-bimodule. Consequently,
X^{\prime}
is flat as a right
R^{\prime}
-module (cf.
[
4
, 00HI]
) and projective as a left
S^{\prime}
-module. Hence by
2.3
it follows that
{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{\prime}}
is left Quillen. Next, by the above we have

{\operatorname{Res}}^{S^{\prime}}_{S}({\mathcal{L}}_{X^{\prime}}(M))={\operatorname{Res}}^{S^{\prime}}_{S}(X^{\prime}\otimes_{R^{\prime}}M)\cong X\otimes_{R}M

for all
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R^{\prime})
. In other words, the diagram of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Mod}}(R^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Mod}}(S^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Mod}}(S)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}}^{R^{\prime}}_{R}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}}^{S^{\prime}}_{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{L}}_{X}}

commutes up to natural isomorphism. Note that our Quillen equivalence assumption and
2.3
imply that
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
both preserves and reflects trivial objects and thus weak equivalences by

. But the two restriction functors also both preserve and reflect weak equivalences by

, hence so does
{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{\prime}}
from the commutativity of the diagram.

therefore applies, so that we must check that the counit
\varepsilon_{M}:{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{\prime}}{\mathcal{R}}_{X^{\prime}}(N)\to N
is a weak equivalence for all

.

For this, note that by the tensor-hom adjunction there is a natural isomorphism
{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(X,{\operatorname{Res}}_{S}^{S^{\prime}}(-))\cong{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S^{\prime}}(X^{\prime},-)
. Viewing both sides as functors valued in

, this says that there is a natural isomorphism

, and hence that we have a commuting square as above for the right adjoints as well. It follows that

for all

. By chasing down these identifications we obtain that the restriction to

is the counit of the adjunction
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}\vdash{\mathcal{R}}_{X}
. But by assumption and

. Since

reflects weak equivalences by

, this implies that

is a weak equivalence as well and we are done.
∎

2.3.3.
Decomposition of
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
by a central idempotent

Suppose that we are given a non-trivial central idempotent
e\in R
. This situation will be relevant for when considering Hecke algebras, cf. (
3.5
). Then the decomposition
R=eR\times(1-e)R
of rings induces a decomposition
{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)={\operatorname{Mod}}(eR)\times{\operatorname{Mod}}((1-e)R)
. Since
{\operatorname{Hom}}
sets and thus
{\operatorname{Ext}}
groups decompose similarly into a product, we deduce that
eR
and
(1-e)R
are both Gorenstein algebras (of self-injective dimension bounded above by that of
R
) and that we have a decomposition

(2.3)

{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)={\operatorname{Ho}}(eR)\times{\operatorname{Ho}}((1-e)R).

We will say that an
R
-module
factors through

eR
if it is annihilated by
1-e
. If
M,N\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
factor through
eR
, we see from (
2.3
) that
[M,N]_{R}=[M,N]_{eR}
.

3.
The category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
in rank one

In this section, we prove that
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
is equivalent to the homotopy category of some explicit commutative Gorenstein rings. We also describe these categories completely explicitly when
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
or
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
.

3.1.
Homotopy categories in terms of facets in the Bruhat-Tits building

We begin with a general discussion that applies for
{\mathbf{G}}
an arbitrary split connected reductive group. As this will play a role in the sequel, we first recall that, given an adjunction
F:{\mathcal{C}}\rightleftarrows{\mathcal{D}}:U
between the underlying categories of two model categories, then we say that the model structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
is the
left transfer
along
F
of the model structure on
{\mathcal{D}}
when a morphism
f
in
{\mathcal{C}}
is a cofibration, resp. weak equivalence, if and only if
Ff
is a cofibration, resp. weak equivalence, in
{\mathcal{D}}
. Dually, we say that the model structure on
{\mathcal{D}}
is the
right transfer
along
U
of the model structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
when a morphism
f
in
{\mathcal{D}}
is a fibration, resp. weak equivalence, if and only if
Uf
is a fibration, resp. weak equivalence, in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

For each
0\leq i\leq d=r_{\text{ss}}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{(i)}
denote a set of representatives for the
G
-orbits of the
i
-dimensional faces contained in
\overline{C}
in
\mathscr{X}
. Recall that the Ollivier-Schneider resolution of a Hecke module
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}})
is of the form

(3.1)

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{(d)}}{\mathcal{H}}(\epsilon_{F})\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger}_{F}}M\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{(0)}}{\mathcal{H}}(\epsilon_{F})\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\dagger}_{F}}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\longrightarrow 0,

where
\varepsilon
is the map induced by
h\otimes m\mapsto h\cdot m
, cf.
[
31
, Theorem 3.12]
.

In what follows, we will view
{\mathcal{H}}
as an
({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger})
-bimodule for each
F\subseteq\overline{C}
and will write
{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
for the tensor functor
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}}(-):{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger})\to{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}})
in order to distinguish between faces. Using that
{\mathcal{H}}
is free over
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}
for any
F\subseteq\overline{C}
, cf.
[
31
, Proposition 4.21]
, we see from
2.3
that
{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
is left Quillen and that
{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
and
{\operatorname{Res}}^{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}}
each both preserve and reflect weak equivalences. In fact, something stronger holds as we now see.

Lemma 3.1
.

We let
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq d-1}\mathcal{F}_{(i)}
and
\mathcal{F}_{C}=\mathcal{F}\cup\{C\}
. The Gorenstein projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}})
is the right transfer along the ‘diagonal functor’

(3.2)

\Delta_{C}\coloneqq\left({\operatorname{Res}}^{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}}\right)_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{C}}\colon{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}})\to\prod_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{C}}{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger})

of the product Gorenstein projective model structures on the right hand side (i.e. with all classes of morphisms defined termwise). Furthermore, the left adjoint functor

L\colon\left(M_{F}\right)_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{C}}\longmapsto\bigoplus_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{C}}{\mathcal{H}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}}M_{F}

both preserves and reflects weak equivalences. Assuming that
p\nmid|\Omega_{\text{tor}}|
, theses statements hold as well for the functor
\Delta\coloneqq\left({\operatorname{Res}}^{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}}\right)_{F\in\mathcal{F}}
and its left adjoint.

Proof.

The proof that the model structure is right transferred through
\Delta
was done in
[
10
, Lemma 5.6(iii)]
for the affine Hecke algebra and the finite Hecke algebras
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, and it applies verbatim here (one only needs to appeal to
[
25
, Lemma 4.2]
in order to get reflection of trivial objects).

For the statement on the left adjoint, note that
L\left(M_{F}\right)_{F}=\bigoplus_{F}{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}(M_{F})
by definition. Since weak equivalences in the product category
\prod_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{C}}{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger})
are defined termwise, and as each
{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
preserves weak equivalences by our discussion above, it follows that
L
preserves weak equivalences. Conversely, assume that
L(f_{F})=\bigoplus_{F}{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}(f_{F})
is a weak equivalence. Since weak equivalences are preserved under retracts and since each
{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
reflects weak equivalences by the above discussion, it follows that each
{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}(f_{F})
is a weak equivalence and hence so is
f_{F}
. Thus
L
reflects weak equivalences as claimed.

Finally, we can omit
\mathcal{F}_{(d)}=\{C\}
in the definition of
\Delta
when
p\nmid|\Omega_{\text{tor}}|
because the algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}^{\dagger}=k[\widetilde{\Omega}]
is an iterated skew Laurent polynomial algebra over the group algebra
k[\widetilde{\Omega}_{\text{tor}}]
(cf. the proof of
[
25
, Lemma 3.4]
). When
p\nmid|\Omega_{\text{tor}}|
, the latter is semisimple and hence
{\mathcal{H}}_{C}^{\dagger}
has then finite global dimension (cf.
[
29
, Theorem 7.5.3(ii)]
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

While we won’t need this, the functor
{\mathcal{L}}^{F}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
in fact reflects cofibrant objects, i.e. Gorenstein projective modules. Indeed, this holds because the map
M\to{\mathcal{H}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger}}M
,
m\mapsto 1\otimes m
, is a split monomorphism for any

by

, Proposition 4.21(ii)]
. Thus one deduces that the Gorenstein projective model structure on

is the left transfer through

of the Gorenstein projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}})
. Similarly, the product model structure on
\prod_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{C}}{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}}_{F}^{\dagger})
is the left transfer through
L
of the Gorenstein projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Mod}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

We now apply the above result to our rank 1 setting. In the case that
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
or
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
, we note that
\Omega_{\text{tor}}=1
and the condition on

above is empty, and when

we have

so that we are only excluding

.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose that
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{SL_{2}}
,
\mathrm{PGL_{2}}
or
\mathrm{GL_{2}}
, and assume that

when

. Then the functor

from

is a Quillen equivalence.

Proof.

By
3.1
and
2.4
, it suffices to show that the counit of the adjunction

is a weak equivalence. But this counit is precisely the map
\varepsilon
from the Ollivier-Schneider resolution (

), which is now a short exact sequence, and is therefore a weak equivalence by
[

, Lemma 5.8]
since our condition on

ensures that

has finite global dimension.
∎

Explicitly,
3.3
tells us that
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\operatorname{Res}}^{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}^{\dagger}}):{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})\to{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}^{\dagger})
is an equivalence when
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{PGL_{2}}
or
\mathrm{GL_{2}}
(and for

Remark 3.4

The analogue of

3.2.
The block decomposition of

In order to describe

Following the terminology of Vignéras in

defines both a left and right Quillen equivalence

Proof.

From the above discussion and by

is both a left and a right Quillen equivalence when

∎

3.2.2.
The case
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}

We can now use the above to investigate

in the case

. We first pick a character

of

induces a surjection

. Since

for all

, we deduce that

e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}}\cong e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}/(e_{\gamma}T_{\omega}^{2}-1)\cong e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}}\otimes_{Z(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}})}Z(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}})/(e_{\gamma}T_{\omega}^{2}-1)\,.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
and
\gamma\in T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})^{\vee}/W_{0}
.

(i)

If
\gamma=\{\xi\}

By applying the functor

3.8
and using

Remark 3.13
.

Suppose that we have

3.2.3.
The case

This case is different as the regular components of

-vector space decomposition

(3.12)

\{e_{i}(T_{0}T_{1})^{m},e_{i}(T_{1}T_{0})^{m},e_{i}T_{0}(T_{1}T_{0})^{m},e_{i}T_{1}(T_{0}T_{1})^{m}\mid m\geq 0\}

As a final piece of notation, we write

-span of

Proposition 3.14
.

There is an injective
k
-algebra homomorphism

0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\rho(T_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}0&X_{1}\\
X_{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\rho(T_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}0&X_{2}\\
X_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

One checks immediately that the relations between the generators of

are preserved under the map

. Furthermore, we have for any

that

\displaystyle\rho((T_{0}T_{1})^{m})=\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}^{2m}&0\\
0&X_{2}^{2m}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\quad\rho((T_{1}T_{0})^{m})=\begin{pmatrix}X_{2}^{2m}&0\\

\displaystyle\rho(T_{0}(T_{1}T_{0})^{m})=\begin{pmatrix}0&X_{1}^{2m+1}\\

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\rho(T_{1}(T_{0}T_{1})^{m})=\begin{pmatrix}0&X_{2}^{2m+1}\\

3.12

∎

To now link the above to

The affine spherical module

{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}}):{\operatorname{Ho}}({\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}})\rightleftarrows{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}):{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{R}}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}})\,.

Lemma 3.20

Let
{\mathbf{G}}\in\{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2},{\mathrm{GL}}_{2},{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}\}

we have an equivalence

{\operatorname{Ho}}(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}})\simeq{\operatorname{Ho}}(A)\,.

Proof.

As we will see in the next subection, the algebra
e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}
does not depend on
{\mathbf{G}}
nor on the specific choice of regular
\gamma
, cf. the relations (
3.15
) below. We therefore might as well assume that
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
. We then obtain the claimed equivalence by combining
3.12
(i),
3.9
and
3.11
(ii).
∎

From
3.3
and
3.20
,
{\operatorname{Ho}}(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
is equivalent to
{\operatorname{Ho}}(A)\times{\operatorname{Ho}}(A)
while
{\operatorname{Ho}}(Z(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}))
is equivalent to
{\operatorname{Ho}}(A)
by
3.15
. Furthermore, by
[
32
, Remark 3.5]
the centre

is a polynomial algebra and hence has homotopy category equal to zero, while
{\operatorname{Ho}}(e_{\sigma}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})\neq 0
(cf.
3.28
). Therefore, the centre does not ‘see’ the full homotopy category anymore. Nevertheless, we have that the regular part of
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}})
is again a product of copies of
{\operatorname{Ho}}(A)
, similarly as for
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
. In the next subsection, we will compute the non-regular component for

as well as describe

explicitly.

3.3.
The homotopy category of
{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}

In this section, we give an explicit description of the category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}})
for any of our three choices of
{\mathbf{G}}
and prove
A
.

We first fix a regular orbit
\gamma
and a representative
\xi
of it. Then
e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}
identifies with the
k
-algebra
R
generated by two primitive idempotents
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
and an element
T
, satisfying the relations

(3.15)

e_{1}+e_{2}=1,\quad e_{i}e_{j}=\delta_{ij}e_{i},\quad e_{i}T=Te_{3-i},\quad\text{and}\quad T^{2}=0

for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
, by identifying
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
and
T
with
e_{\xi}
,
e_{\xi^{s_{0}}}
and
e_{\gamma}T_{s_{0}}
respectively, cf. the proof of
[
25
, Lemma 7.5]
. The ring
R
has dimension 4 over
k
with basis
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
,
Te_{1}
and
Te_{2}
.

Next, there are exactly two
k
-valued characters
\chi_{1}
,
\chi_{2}
of
R
defined by

(3.16)

\chi_{i}(T)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{i}(e_{j})=\delta_{ij}

for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
. Furthermore, we have a decomposition
R=Re_{1}\oplus Re_{2}
of left
R
-modules, and for
i=1,2
we have a non-split short exact sequence

(3.17)

0\to\chi_{3-i}\to Re_{i}\to\chi_{i}\to 0.

The modules
\chi_{i}
and
Re_{i}
(
i=1,2
) are indecomposable. In fact, they are the only ones.

Lemma 3.21
.

Suppose that
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
is a non-zero indecomposable module. Then
M
is isomorphic to one of
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
,
Re_{1}
or
Re_{2}
.

Proof.

First, assume that there exists
m\in M
such that
Tm\neq 0
. Then there is some
i=1,2
such that
Te_{i}m\neq 0
, and in fact
Te_{i}m\notin k\cdot e_{i}m
since
T^{2}=0
. Thus the
R
-linear map
\varphi:Re_{i}\to M
,
re_{i}\mapsto re_{i}m
, is injective. Since
R
is self-injective, the classes of projective and injective modules coincide so that
Re_{i}
is injective. It follows that
\varphi
splits and thus must be an isomorphism by indecomposability of
M
.

We are therefore only left with the case where
TM=0
. In that case,
M=e_{1}M\oplus e_{2}M
is an
R
-linear decomposition and
M=e_{i}M
for some
i=1,2
by indecomposability. Hence
M
is a direct sum of copies of
\chi_{i}
and so must be isomorphic to
\chi_{i}
, again by indecomposability.
∎

Proposition 3.22
.

For any
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
, there is an isomorphism

M\cong\chi_{1}^{\oplus\mathcal{I}_{1}}\oplus\chi_{2}^{\oplus\mathcal{I}_{2}}\oplus Re_{1}^{\oplus\mathcal{J}_{1}}\oplus Re_{2}^{\oplus\mathcal{J}_{2}}

for some indexing sets
\mathcal{I}_{i}
and
\mathcal{J}_{i}
(
i=1,2
).

Proof.

By
3.21
, the result holds for any finite dimensional
R
-module. Let
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{K}}
denote the collection of all indecomposable projective submodules of
M
(i.e. isomorphic to one of
Re_{1}
and
Re_{2}
). We then let

X=\left\{\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{K}\;\middle|\;\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}M_{j}\text{ is direct}\right\},

ordered by inclusion. By Zorn’s lemma, there is a maximal element
\mathcal{J}\in X
. Let
N=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}M_{j}
, which is by definition a projective submodule of
M
of the form
Re_{1}^{\oplus\mathcal{J}_{1}}\oplus Re_{2}^{\oplus\mathcal{J}_{2}}
.

As
R
is self-injective, the module
N
is injective and thus there is a submodule
N^{\prime}
such that
M=N\oplus N^{\prime}
. Further, by maximality of
\mathcal{J}
there is no
i\in\mathcal{K}
such that
M_{i}\subseteq N^{\prime}
and hence every indecomposable submodule of
N^{\prime}
is isomorphic to either
\chi_{1}
or
\chi_{2}
by
3.21
. Since
N^{\prime}
is the sum of its finite dimensional submodules, it therefore follows that
N^{\prime}
is semisimple and thus is isomorphic to a module of the form
\chi_{1}^{\oplus\mathcal{I}_{1}}\oplus\chi_{2}^{\oplus\mathcal{I}_{2}}
as required.
∎

Remark 3.23
.

The above argument shows more generally that if
\mathscr{R}
denotes any finite dimensional, self-injective
k
-algebra with the property that any indecomposable
\mathscr{R}
-module is either projective or simple, then any
\mathscr{R}
-module is a direct sum of indecomposables. Note that this decomposition is in fact unique up to auto-bijection of the indexing set for the decomposition by the Azumaya-Krull-Remak-Schmidt theorem, cf.
[
13
, Theorem 2.12]
.

Using the above, we can give a very elementary description of
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
as a triangulated category. Below, we will call a triangle in a triangulated category
trivial
if it is isomorphic to a shift of a triangle of the form
X\xrightarrow{{\operatorname{id}}}X\to 0\to\Sigma X
. We note that finite direct sums of trivial triangles are always distinguished, cf.
[
30
, Remark 1.2.2]
. We will need two distinct but elementary triangulated structures on semisimple abelian categories, which we define now.

Definition 3.24
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a semisimple abelian category.

(i)

We will call the
trivial triangulated structure on
\mathscr{A}
the one whose shift functor is the identity and with distinguished triangles the finite direct sums of the trivial triangles. We will write
\mathscr{A}_{\operatorname{triv}}
for
\mathscr{A}
equipped with this triangulated structure.

(ii)

Suppose
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}_{1}\times\mathscr{A}_{2}
is a product of two semisimple abelian categories. We define
\Sigma:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
to be the autoequivalence given by
(X,Y)\mapsto(Y,X)
. Moreover, we define a distinguished triangle in
\mathscr{A}
to be a sequence

X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{h}\Sigma X

which is exact at
Y
and
Z
and which satisfies
{\operatorname{Im}}(h)=\Sigma{\operatorname{Ker}}(f)
. We will write
\mathscr{A}_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}
for
\mathscr{A}
equipped with this triangulated structure.

We point out that any distinguished triangle as in
\mathscr{A}_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}
is also a finite direct sum of trivial triangles by semisimplicity of
\mathscr{A}
. It was shown in
[
14
, Section 8.1]
that
\mathscr{A}_{\operatorname{triv}}
is a well-defined triangulated structure on
\mathscr{A}
, and a completely analogous argument shows that
\mathscr{A}_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}
is also well-defined. In what follows, we will apply the construction of (ii) to
{\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})
, identified with the product category
{\operatorname{Mod}}(k)\times{\operatorname{Mod}}(k)
. It was pointed out to us by Martin Kalck that the triangulated structure this induces on the compact objects may also be defined as the orbit category
D^{b}({\operatorname{Mod}}_{\mathrm{f.g.}}(k))/[2]
in the sense of
[
22
]
.

Corollary 3.25
.

The functor
{\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}\to{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
defined by

(V_{1},V_{2})\mapsto(\chi_{1}\otimes_{k}V_{1})\oplus(\chi_{2}\otimes_{k}V_{2})

is a triangle equivalence.

Proof.

By
3.22
, the objects of
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
are all of the form
\chi_{1}^{\oplus\mathcal{I}_{1}}\oplus\chi_{2}^{\oplus\mathcal{I}_{2}}
up to isomorphism. Fix an arbitrary
j=1,2
. Using (
3.17
), we obtain a projective resolution

\cdots\xrightarrow{\cdot T}Re_{j}\xrightarrow{\cdot T}Re_{3-j}\xrightarrow{\cdot T}Re_{j}\to\chi_{j}\to 0

from which we compute

{\operatorname{Ext}}_{R}^{i}(\chi_{j},\chi_{j})=\begin{cases}k&\text{if $i$ is even}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,,

cf. also
[
25
, Example 7.2]
. This implies in particular that
\chi_{j}\neq 0
in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
. Moreover, the sequence (
3.17
) shows that the shift functor on
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
swaps
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
. By (
2.2
) we may now deduce that

[\chi_{j},\chi_{j}]_{R}=k\quad\text{and}\quad[\chi_{j},\chi_{3-j}]_{R}=0\,.

From these observations, we obtain that the functor
(V_{1},V_{2})\mapsto(\chi_{1}\otimes_{k}V_{1})\oplus(\chi_{2}\otimes_{k}V_{2})
is essentially surjective and fully faithful, i.e. an equivalence. By the above we also see that this functor commutes with the shifts. Since the finite direct sums of trivial triangles are always distinguished, our equivalence is therefore exact and we are done.
∎

By
3.20
, we now immediately deduce from the above that
{\operatorname{Ho}}(A)\simeq{\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})
. We can make such an equivalence explicit as follows. Recall from
2.2
that the

and

Corollary 3.26
.

The functor
{\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})_{2,{\operatorname{triv}}}\to{\operatorname{Ho}}(A)
defined by

(V_{1},V_{2})\longmapsto({\mathcal{M}}_{1}\otimes_{k}V_{1})\oplus({\mathcal{M}}_{2}\otimes_{k}V_{2})

is a triangle equivalence.

From
3.20
and
3.25
, we deduce that there is a triangle equivalence

4.
The singularity category of semisimple Galois representations

In this section, we give a Galois theoretic interpretation of the category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
. More precisely, we relate the homotopy categories
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
in semisimple rank one to the singularity categories of certain schemes parametrising the Galois representations occuring on the other side of the mod-
p
Langlands program.

4.1.
Singularity categories

We first recall some elementary notions related to singularity categories. Following Krause
[
26
]
, our definition of singularity categories is as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a separated Noetherian scheme over
k
. The
singularity category
of
X
is defined to be
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)\coloneqq\mathbf{K}_{\operatorname{ac}}({\operatorname{Inj}}(X))
, the category of acyclic complexes of injective quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
up to chain homotopy.

When
X={\operatorname{Spec}}(S)
is affine we will often also write
{\operatorname{Sing}}(S)
for
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
.

Remark 4.2
.

(i)

The singularity category of a Gorenstein ring
S
was first investigated by Buchweitz
[
7
]
, who defined it to be the Verdier quotient
D^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(S))/\mathrm{Perf(S)}
of the bounded derived category of finitely generated
S
-modules by the thick subcategory of perfect complexes. This definition was taken up and later studied systematically for a large family of schemes by Orlov
[
34
]
. In general, one has that
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
as we defined it is compactly generated and its compact objects are equivalent to the idempotent completion of
D^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))/\mathrm{Perf(X)}
in a natural way, cf.
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
. In particular, one deduces that
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)=0
if and only if
X
is regular.

(ii)

Singularity categories also feature in the geometric Langlands program, see for example
[
3
, Appendix H]
. In that context, these categories are defined as the quotient
{\operatorname{Ind}}{\operatorname{Coh}}(X)/{\operatorname{QCoh}}(X)
of the ind-completion of the (derived infinity) category of coherent sheaves on
X
by the quasi-coherent sheaves, and
X
itself is allowed to be a more general stack. The quotient category
{\operatorname{Ind}}{\operatorname{Coh}}(X)/{\operatorname{QCoh}}(X)
is known to be compactly generated by
{\operatorname{Coh}}(X)/\mathrm{Perf}(X)
in relatively high generality, cf. Remark H.1.5 of
loc. cit
., and hence agrees with our definition for separated Noetherian schemes by the above. Finally, we note that there is a 6-functor formalism on
{\operatorname{Ind}}{\operatorname{Coh}}(X)
, see
[
39
, §8.7]
, and we expect that the pushforward/pullback functors between singularity categories which we use later in this section can be recovered from that far more general construction. In practice however, we will only work with affine schemes or chains of projective lines in this paper, so that
4.1
and the constructions we introduce later will suffice for our purposes.

Let
S
be a commutative Noetherian ring. We recall that Becker showed that
{\operatorname{Sing}}(S)
can be constructed as the homotopy category of the so-called
singular injective
model structure on on the category
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)
of unbounded chain complexes of
S
-modules, cf.
[
6
, Proposition 2.2.1]
. When
S
is Gorenstein, the functor
\iota_{0}:{\operatorname{Mod}}(S)\to{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)
sending an
S
-module to the corresponding chain complex concentrated in degree
0
is a left Quillen equivalence for the Gorenstein injective model structure on
{\operatorname{Mod}}(S)
and the singular injective model structure on
{\operatorname{Ch}}(S)
, cf. the dual of Proposition 3.1.3 in
loc. cit
. There is also a projective analogue of this singular model structure with
\mathbf{K}_{\operatorname{ac}}({\operatorname{Proj}}(S))
as its homotopy category, and by
loc. cit
. the functor
\iota_{0}
is then a right Quillen equivalence for the Gorenstein projective model structure. Recalling that the Gorenstein projective and injective model structures have naturally the same homotopy categories (cf.
[
10
, Remark 2.12]
), we therefore obtain the following.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
S
be a commutative Gorenstein ring. Then
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
is canonically equivalent to
{\operatorname{Sing}}(S)
.

Identifying
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
with
\mathrm{GInj}(S)/{\operatorname{Inj}}(S)
, the functor giving the aforementioned equivalence maps the homotopy class of an acyclic complex of injectives
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}
to the homotopy class of its zero-th cycles
Z_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}))
.

Remark 4.4
.

(i)

Using
4.3
, we can reinterpret
3.26
as saying that the singularity category of two affine lines meeting at the origin is equivalent to
{\operatorname{Mod}}(k^{2})
. This result is well-known to the experts, see e.g.
[
40
, Propositions 5.4.13-14]
or
[
19
, Example 3.4]
, however the arguments there are very different and typically rely on techniques from the representation theory of quivers. Our work therefore gives a completely new proof of the computation of this singularity category using Hecke algebras. Meanwhile,
3.28
is a special case of the computation of the singularity categories of the infinitesimal thickenings of a point, which was done in
[
34
, §3.3]
.

(ii)

We are now switching from a projective language (Gorenstein projective modules and acyclic complexes of projectives) to an injective language (Gorenstein injective modules and acyclic complexes of injectives). We point out that the passage between those is natural, since Gorenstein rings have a dualising complex and tensoring with it provides an equivalence between the homotopy categories of complexes of projectives and of injectives, cf.
[
18
, Theorem I]
.

Next, we recall that given a morphism
f:X\to Y
of separated Noetherian schemes the pushforward functor
f_{*}
induces a functor
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f_{*}):{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)\to{\operatorname{Sing}}(Y)
, cf.
[
26
, Theorem 1.5]
. It is in fact shown in the proof of
loc. cit
. that

applied termwise sends acyclic complexes of injectives to acyclic complexes, and that
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f_{*})
has a right adjoint
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f^{!})
. We will be primarily interested in the case where
f
is flat, in which case
f^{*}
is exact and
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f_{*})
is therefore computed by applying
f_{*}
termwise to a complex. In that case, the exactness of

also implies the existence of an induced functor
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f^{*}):{\operatorname{Sing}}(Y)\to{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
that is left adjoint to
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f_{*})
, cf. Theorem 6.6 & Remark 6.7 in
loc. cit
. When
X={\operatorname{Spec}}(A)
and
Y={\operatorname{Spec}}(B)
are affine Gorenstein schemes, the adjoint pair of functors
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f^{*})\vdash{\operatorname{Sing}}(f_{*})
then identifies under
4.3
with the adjoint pair
\mathbf{L}{\mathcal{L}}_{A}\vdash\mathbf{R}{\mathcal{R}}_{A}
from
2.3
.

In the special case of an open embedding
j:U\hookrightarrow X
, the isomorphism
j^{*}\circ j_{*}\cong{\operatorname{id}}
implies that
{\operatorname{Sing}}(j_{*})
is fully faithful. Under a natural condition on the singularities, one even gets an equivalence as was originally shown by Orlov (cf.
[
34
, Proposition 1.14]
).

Proposition 4.5

(
[
26
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
X
be a separated Noetherian scheme of finite Krull dimension and suppose that
j:U\to X
is a Zariski open embedding such that the open subscheme
U
contains all the singular points of
X
. Then
{\operatorname{Sing}}(j_{*})
is an equivalence.

Finally, we recall the notion of support in the singularity category
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
. Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}
is a chain complexes of injective quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
. We define its
support
to be

{\operatorname{Supp}}({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\operatorname{Supp}}({\mathcal{M}}_{n})\,.

This definition is not invariant under chain homotopy and thus does not naively descend to a notion of support on
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
. However, by
[
26
, Appendix B]
, each homotopy class of complexes of injective quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
is represented by a unique (up to chain isomorphism) so-called
homotopically minimal
complex. Here, the homotopically minimal complexes are the complexes
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}
of injectives satisfying the property that any homotopy equivalence
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}
is an isomorphism. We thus define the support of a chain homotopy class in
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
to be the support of a homotopically minimal complex representing that class. We now describe how this notion translates under the equivalence of
4.3
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
R
be a commutative Gorenstein ring and
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(R)
. Write
\underline{M}
for the image of
M
in
{\operatorname{Sing}}(R)
under
4.3
. Then

(4.1)

{\operatorname{Supp}}(\underline{M})=\left\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}(R)\mid\mathrm{injdim}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\infty\right\}\,.

In particular,
{\operatorname{Supp}}(\underline{M})\subseteq{\operatorname{Supp}}(M)
and we have equality when
M\cong Z_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})
for some homotopically minimal acyclic complex of injectives
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}
.

Proof.

We first recall that an acyclic complex of injectives
\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}
is homotopically minimal if and only if the inclusion
Z_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{\bullet})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{i}
of the
i
-th cycles is an injective envelope for each
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, cf.
[
26
, Lemma B.1]
. Assuming that holds, we first claim that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}=0
if and only if
M_{i}\coloneqq Z_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{\bullet})
is injective for some (or, equivalently, every)
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Indeed, if
M_{i}
is injective then it must equal its injective envelope
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
. By exactness this forces
M_{i+1}=0
and thus the injective envelope
{\mathcal{M}}_{i+1}
is zero as well. Exactness now forces
M_{i+2}=0
and iterating one obtains that
{\mathcal{M}}_{j}=0
for all
j>i
. Meanwhile, since
M_{i+1}=0
it follows that each
M_{j}
with
j<i
has finite injective dimension. Being Gorenstein injective, each such
M_{j}
must therefore be injective and hence
M_{j}={\mathcal{M}}_{j}
. It follows that every differential in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}
is zero and thus
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}=0
by exactness, as required. In particular, we have show that
M_{i}=0
if and only if
M_{i}
is injective (or, equivalently, has finite injective dimension) for any
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The final statement will therefore follow from (
4.1
).

To show (
4.1
), we fix
Q_{f}(M)
a Gorenstein injective replacement of
M
, i.e. such that there is an injection
\iota:M\to Q_{f}(M)
with
\mathrm{injdim}_{R}({\operatorname{coker}}(\iota))<\infty
, and a homotopically minimal complex
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}
representing
\underline{M}
. We then have
M\cong Z_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})
in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
, which by
[
17
, Proposition 9.2]
implies that there are injective
R
-modules
E
and
E^{\prime}
such that
Q_{f}(M)\oplus E\cong Z_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})\oplus E^{\prime}
. Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}(R)
and recall that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein (cf.
2.5
). We then have that
\mathrm{injdim}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
if and only if
Q_{f}(M)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is injective, if and only if
Z_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})
is injective. Since
(\mathcal{M}_{\bullet})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a homotopically minimal acyclic complex of injective
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-modules by
[
5
, Corollary 1.3]
, these are equivalent to
(\mathcal{M}_{\bullet})_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
by the above and we are done.
∎

Now let
j:U\to X
be once again an open embedding and let
Z=X\setminus U
. One then always has a functorial triangle

(4.2)

{\mathcal{M}}_{Z,\bullet}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}\to(j_{*}\circ j^{*}){\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{+1}

where
{\operatorname{Supp}}({\mathcal{M}}_{Z,\bullet})\subseteq Z
. In fact, let
{\operatorname{Sing}}_{Z}(X)\subseteq{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
denote the localising subcategory consisting of those (homotopically minimal) complexes whose support is contained in

. One has alternatively that
{\operatorname{Sing}}_{Z}(X)={\operatorname{Ker}}({\operatorname{Sing}}(j^{*}))
, cf.
[
26
, Lemma 6.8]
. The triangulated categories
{\operatorname{Sing}}(U)
,
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
and
{\operatorname{Sing}}_{Z}(X)
in fact fit together in a recollement as explained in Proposition 6.9 and its following paragraph in
loc. cit
. Using the above, we now prove that the singularity category behaves well under nice Zariski covers.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
X
be a separated Noetherian scheme. Suppose that
(U_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is an open cover such that
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
has no singular points for each
i\neq j
. If
f:\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\twoheadrightarrow X
is the corresponding covering morphism, then

{\operatorname{Sing}}(f_{*}):\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\operatorname{Sing}}(U_{i})\to{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)

is an equivalence.

Proof.

By taking
U=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}U_{i}
and
V=U_{n}
and arguing by induction, we may assume that
n=2
so that
U=U_{1}
and
V=U_{2}
. We also let
Z=X\setminus U
and
Z^{\prime}=X\setminus V
, and write
j:U\hookrightarrow X
and
j^{\prime}:V\hookrightarrow X
for the inclusions. By assumption
U\cap V
has no singularities, hence
{\operatorname{Sing}}(U\cap V)=0
and we deduce from (
4.2
) applied to
U\cap V\subseteq U
that
{\operatorname{Sing}}(U)={\operatorname{Sing}}_{Z^{\prime}}(U)
. It follows that
{\operatorname{Sing}}(j_{*})
has essential image contained in
{\operatorname{Sing}}_{Z^{\prime}}(X)
, and we claim that this is actually an equality. Indeed, let
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}\in{\operatorname{Sing}}_{Z^{\prime}}(X)
and note that also
(j_{*}\circ j^{*})({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})\in{\operatorname{Sing}}_{Z^{\prime}}(X)
. The distinguished triangle (
4.2
) now implies that
{\mathcal{M}}_{Z,\bullet}
is supported on
Z^{\prime}
as well. But since
U
and
V
cover
X
, we have
Z\cap Z^{\prime}=\emptyset
and so
{\mathcal{M}}_{Z,\bullet}=0
, which implies by (
4.2
) that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}
lies in the essential image of
j_{*}
as required.

The above implies that the essential images of
{\operatorname{Sing}}(j_{*})
and
{\operatorname{Sing}}(j^{\prime}_{*})
are equal to
{\operatorname{Ker}}({\operatorname{Sing}}(j^{\prime*}))
and
{\operatorname{Ker}}({\operatorname{Sing}}(j^{*}))
, respectively, cf.
[
26
, Lemma 6.8]
. Hence it follows by adjointness that
{\operatorname{Sing}}(j_{*})
and
{\operatorname{Sing}}(j^{\prime}_{*})
have orthogonal essential images in
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
, in the sense there are no non-zero morphisms betweem them, and thus
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f_{*})={\operatorname{Sing}}(j_{*})\oplus{\operatorname{Sing}}(j^{\prime}_{*})
is fully faithful. It remains to show that it is essentially surjective. For this, we let
{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}\in{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
be arbitrary and apply the unit of the adjunction
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f^{*})\vdash{\operatorname{Sing}}(f_{*})
to (
4.2
) to get a morphism of triangles

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{M}}_{Z,\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{*}\circ j^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(j^{\prime}_{*}\circ j^{\prime*})({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(j^{\prime}_{*}\circ j^{\prime*})({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})\oplus(j_{*}\circ j^{*})({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(j_{*}\circ j^{*})({\mathcal{M}}_{\bullet})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces+1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces+1}

where the outer vertical arrows are isomorphisms by the above. Thus so is the middle vertical arrow by
[
4
, 014A]
and we are done.
∎

We first prove a preparatory lemma. Recall our notation

Lemma 4.10

5.
DGAs and endomorphism rings

For the rest of this paper, we assume that
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
. We do not expect the homotopy category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
to admit such a simple description as in
A
because the singular locus of

is now 1-dimensional. In this section, we attempt to give some description of this category, by exhibiting an explicit generator for
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
which we use to describe this category as the derived category of an explicit DGA. We also compute the endomorphism rings in
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
of the simple supersingular modules.

5.1.
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
as the derived category of a DGA

It follows from very general considerations, cf.
[
15
, Theorem 8.6]
, that the homotopy category of any Gorenstein ring is a so-called
algebraic
triangulated category, i.e. triangle equivalent to the stable category of a Frobenius exact category, and it is also known to be compactly generated, cf.
[
17
, Theorem 9.4]
. It follows from a classical theorem of Keller that any such triangulated category is triangle equivalent to the derived category of a DG-category, cf.
[
20
, Theorem 4.3]
. More generally, when
X
is a Noetherian separated scheme then
{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
is compactly generated and algebraic, cf.
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
27
, Lemma 7.5]
, and thus is also equivalent to the derived category of a DG-category. A special case of this result of Keller is the following.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
21
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
k
-linear Frobenius exact category with arbitrary direct sums and let
{\mathcal{T}}
be its stable category. Assume that
{\mathcal{T}}
has a single compact generator
X
. Let
P_{\bullet}
be an acyclic, termwise projective chain complex over
{\mathcal{C}}
with
Z_{0}(P_{\bullet})\cong X
, and set
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}\coloneqq\underline{{\operatorname{End}}}(P_{\bullet})
to be the corresponding endomorphism DGA. Then there is an equivalence
{\mathcal{T}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}D(\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}})
of triangulated categories which sends
X
to
\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

The above equivalence is constructed more or less explicitly. Indeed, if
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
denotes the category of chain complexes of injective-projective objects of
{\mathcal{C}}
up to chain homotopy, then the proof of
loc. cit
. shows that
\underline{{\operatorname{Hom}}}(P_{\bullet},-)
defines an equivalence
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}\simeq D(\widetilde{A})
. The theorem follows because taking zero-th cycles induces and equivalence between
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{T}}
.

We now explain how to make the above DGA explicit in the case of
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
, or equivalently for the singularity category of the scheme of semisimple 2-dimensional Galois representations. By
3.3
,
3.6
and
3.7
, it suffices to describe
{\operatorname{Ho}}(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}^{\dagger})
as the derived category of a DGA for
\gamma\in T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})^{\vee}/W_{0}
regular. We keep our notation from
Section

3.3
and identify
e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}
with the ring
R
, so that
S\coloneqq R[Z^{\pm 1}]\cong e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}^{\dagger}
. We recall that
R
has two characters
\chi_{1}
,
\chi_{2}
(cf. (
3.16
)). This induces two Gorenstein projective
S
-modules
\chi_{1}[Z^{\pm 1}]\coloneqq S\otimes_{R}\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}[Z^{\pm 1}]\coloneqq S\otimes_{R}\chi_{2}
by tensoring.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
M\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(S)
satisfies

\left[\chi_{1}[Z^{\pm 1}],M\right]_{S}=0=\left[\chi_{2}[Z^{\pm 1}],M\right]_{S}\,.

Then

has image 0 in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
, i.e. has finite projective dimension. In particular, the module
\chi_{1}[Z^{\pm 1}]\oplus\chi_{2}[Z^{\pm 1}]
is a compact generator for the triangulated category
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
.

Proof.

Since
\chi_{1}[Z^{\pm 1}]\oplus\chi_{2}[Z^{\pm 1}]
is finitely generated Gorenstein projective, it must be compact in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
by (
2.1
). Next, we know from
3.25
that
\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2}
is a generator of
{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)
. Furthermore, we know from
2.6
that the two functors in the adjunction

{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{L}}_{S}):{\operatorname{Ho}}(R)\rightleftarrows{\operatorname{Ho}}(S):{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{R}}_{S})

are conservative, i.e. reflect isomorphisms. Since a left adjoint of a conservative functor always preserves a generator, the result follows as
\chi_{1}[Z^{\pm 1}]\oplus\chi_{2}[Z^{\pm 1}]={\mathcal{L}}_{S}(\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2})
.
∎

Using the above, we can give a genuine
{\mathcal{H}}
-module which generates

. In what follows, we write

, where we identify

with

via

, and call the direct sum

the
Gorenstein projective spherical
{\mathcal{H}}
-module
. To see that this is really a Gorenstein projective module, we note that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}^{\operatorname{GP}}
is the image of the Gorenstein projective module
\chi_{1}[Z^{\pm 1}]\oplus\chi_{2}[Z^{\pm 1}]\in{\operatorname{Mod}}(S)
under the left Quillen equivalence

{\operatorname{Mod}}(S)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_{B^{2}}}{\operatorname{Mod}}(B)\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\operatorname{Mod}}(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}})

for each

, where the first arrow is the functor obtained from
3.27
after tensoring with
S
. This observation and
5.2
immediately yield:

Corollary 5.3
.

The category
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
is generated by
{\mathcal{M}}^{{\operatorname{GP}}}
.

Going back to investigating a regular component
{\operatorname{Ho}}(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}^{\dagger})
, we see by applying

to (

) that our generator

Consequently, we have by
5.1
that
{\operatorname{Ho}}(e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}^{\dagger})\simeq D({\mathcal{E}})
, where
{\mathcal{E}}\coloneqq\underline{{\operatorname{End}}}(P_{\bullet})
. In order to describe this DGA explicitly, we first note that the complex

splits into a direct sum
P_{1,\bullet}\oplus P_{2,\bullet}
, where

P_{1,\bullet}=\cdots\xrightarrow{\cdot T}Se_{2}\xrightarrow{\cdot T}Se_{1}\xrightarrow{\cdot T}Se_{2}\xrightarrow{\cdot T}\cdots

with
P_{1,0}=Se_{1}
and
P_{2,\bullet}=P_{1,\bullet}[1]
. Writing
{\mathcal{E}}_{ij}\coloneqq\underline{{\operatorname{Hom}}}(P_{i,\bullet},P_{j,\bullet})
, we thus have

{\mathcal{E}}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathcal{E}}_{11}&{\mathcal{E}}_{12}\\
{\mathcal{E}}_{21}&{\mathcal{E}}_{22}\end{pmatrix}\,.

By the periodicity of
P_{i,\bullet}
, we actually have
{\mathcal{E}}_{11}={\mathcal{E}}_{22}={\mathcal{E}}_{12}[1]={\mathcal{E}}_{21}[1]
as complexes. In order to describe these, we consider the maps
Se_{i}\xrightarrow{\cdot T}Se_{3-i}
for
i=1,2
and by slight abuse of notation denote them both by
\tau
. We then have
{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(Se_{i},Se_{i})=k[Z^{\pm 1}]\cdot{\operatorname{id}}_{Se_{i}}
and
{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(Se_{i},Se_{3-i})=k[Z^{\pm 1}]\cdot\tau
for

. We may thus describe
{\mathcal{E}}_{11}
as follows. We consider

E_{0}\coloneqq\prod_{{\mathbb{Z}}}k[Z^{\pm 1}]\quad\text{and}\quad E_{1}\coloneqq\prod_{{\mathbb{Z}}}k[Z^{\pm 1}]\cdot\tau\,,

viewed as subspaces of the algebra
\prod_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(k[\tau]/(\tau^{2})\right)[Z^{\pm 1}]
. By the definition of the internal Hom of chain complexes and using the above description of the morphisms between the terms of
P_{1,\bullet}
, we see that we have

{\mathcal{E}}_{11}\cong\underline{E}\coloneqq\cdots\to E_{1}\to E_{0}\to E_{1}\to E_{0}\to\cdots\,,

with even degree terms equal to
E_{0}
, and differential given by

(5.1)

d_{n}:(x_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mapsto((x_{l}-(-1)^{n}x_{l+1})\tau)_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,.

In particular, we note that
d_{n}=0
when
n
is odd. We gather some facts about the DGA
{\mathcal{E}}
below.

Proposition 5.4
.

We have

(5.2)

{\mathcal{E}}\cong\begin{pmatrix}\underline{E}&\underline{E}[1]\\
\underline{E}[1]&\underline{E}\end{pmatrix}

with
\underline{E}
as described above. Furthermore, we have:

(i)

there is a
k
-algebra isomorphism

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\cong\prod_{{\mathbb{Z}}}e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}^{\dagger}\,\text{; and}

(ii)

if
H_{\bullet}
denotes the cohomology algebra of
{\mathcal{E}}
and
\mathscr{R}\coloneqq k[X^{\pm 1},Z^{\pm 1}]
, then

H_{\bullet}\cong\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{R}_{e}&\mathscr{R}_{o}\\
\mathscr{R}_{o}&\mathscr{R}_{e}\end{pmatrix}\subseteq M_{2}(\mathscr{R}),

where
\mathscr{R}_{e}
, resp.
\mathscr{R}_{o}
, is the
k[Z^{\pm 1}]
-submodule of
\mathscr{R}
generated by the even powers of
X
, resp. the odd powers of
X
, and where
Z^{\pm 1}
has degree 0 and
X^{n}
has degree
n
(in any matrix position).

Proof.

We just need to show (i) and (ii). For (i), we have

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\cong\begin{pmatrix}E_{0}&E_{1}\\
E_{1}&E_{0}\end{pmatrix}\cong\prod_{\mathbb{Z}}\begin{pmatrix}k[Z]^{\pm{1}}&k[Z]^{\pm{1}}\cdot\tau\\
k[Z]^{\pm{1}}\cdot\tau&k[Z]^{\pm{1}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

The isomorphism is then obtained by sending in each factor
e_{\gamma}T_{\omega^{2}}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}Z&0\\
0&Z\end{pmatrix}
,
e_{1}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
,
e_{2}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
and
e_{\gamma}T_{s_{0}}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&\tau\\
\tau&0\end{pmatrix}
. For (ii), we deduce from (
5.1
) that in

one has

{\operatorname{Ker}}(d_{n})=\begin{cases}E_{1}&\text{if $n$ is odd}\\
k[Z^{\pm 1}]\cdot(\ldots,1,1,1,\ldots)&\text{if $n$ is even}\end{cases}

and

{\operatorname{Im}}(d_{n})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $n$ is odd}\\
E_{1}&\text{if $n$ is even}\end{cases}\,.

Therefore the cohomology of

is zero in odd terms and isomorphic to
k[Z^{\pm 1}]
in the even terms. We write
\iota_{n}
for the cohomology class of
(\ldots,1,1,1,\ldots)
in
H_{n}(\underline{E})
if
n
is even, resp. in
H_{n}(\underline{E}[1])
if
n
is odd. From (
5.2
) one deduces that

H_{\bullet}=\left(\bigoplus_{\text{$n$ even}}\begin{pmatrix}k[Z^{\pm 1}]\cdot\iota_{n}&0\\
0&k[Z^{\pm 1}]\cdot\iota_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{\text{$n$ odd}}\begin{pmatrix}0&k[Z^{\pm 1}]\cdot\iota_{n}\\

is of the claimed form, by identifying

with

∎

5.2.
Endomorphism rings of supersingular modules

We conclude by computing the endomorphisms in
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})
of the simple supersingular modules of infinite projective dimension. We fix a regular orbit
\gamma
and consider the rings
R
and
S
as in the previous section. For
i=1,2
and each
\lambda\in k^{\times}
, we will write
\chi_{i,\lambda}\coloneqq\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]/(Z-\lambda)
, i.e.
\chi_{i,\lambda}
is the one-dimensional
S
-module whose restriction to
R
is
\chi_{i}
and on which
Z
acts by multiplication by
\lambda
. Recall from
Section

2.2
the definition of the simple supersingular
{\mathcal{H}}
-modules. There is a unique such module factoring through
e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}
and such that
T_{\omega}^{2}
acts as
\lambda\cdot{\operatorname{id}}
, which we denote by
M_{\gamma,\lambda}
. It is associated to a supersingular character
\chi
of
{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}
such that
{\operatorname{Res}}^{{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}}_{T({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(\chi)\in\gamma
, and in particular we have that
{\operatorname{Res}}^{e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}}_{e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}^{\dagger}}(M_{\gamma,\lambda})\cong\chi_{1,\lambda}\oplus\chi_{2,\lambda}
. Our aim is to show the following.

Theorem 5.5
.

There is a
k
-algebra isomorphism
{\operatorname{End}}_{{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})}(M_{\gamma,\lambda})\cong e_{\gamma}{\mathcal{H}}_{x_{0}}
.

By the equivalence from
3.3
we have

{\operatorname{End}}_{{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathcal{H}})}(M_{\gamma,\lambda})\cong{\operatorname{End}}_{{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)}(\chi_{1,\lambda}\oplus\chi_{2,\lambda})\cong\begin{pmatrix}[\chi_{1,\lambda},\chi_{1,\lambda}]_{S}&[\chi_{1,\lambda},\chi_{2,\lambda}]_{S}\\
[\chi_{2,\lambda},\chi_{1,\lambda}]_{S}&[\chi_{2,\lambda},\chi_{2,\lambda}]_{S}\end{pmatrix}\,,

where we recall our notation
[-,-]_{S}\coloneqq{\operatorname{Hom}}_{{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)}(-,-)
. We first need to calculate each entry above. Fix
i\in\{1,2\}
. By definition, we have a short exact sequence

(5.3)

0\to\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]\xrightarrow{(Z-\lambda)\cdot}\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]\xrightarrow{p_{i}}\chi_{i,\lambda}\to 0

and it will be useful to first compute self-extensions of
\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]
. For this, we note that by tensoring (
3.17
) with
S
we get a non-split extension

(5.4)

0\to\chi_{3-i}[Z^{\pm 1}]\to Se_{i}\to\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]\to 0\,.

By arguing exactly as in the proof of
3.25
, we obtain

(5.5)

{\operatorname{Ext}}_{S}^{j}\left(\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]\right)\cong\begin{cases}k[Z^{\pm 1}]&\text{if $j$ is even}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,.

Remark 5.6
.

The projectivity of
Se_{i}
implies by (
5.4
) that
\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]\cong\Sigma\chi_{3-i}[Z^{\pm 1}]
in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
, and thus the above computation is equivalent to

\left[\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{j}[Z^{\pm 1}]\right]_{S}=\begin{cases}k[Z^{\pm 1}]&\text{if $i=j$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,.

In particular, the generator
\chi_{1}[Z^{\pm 1}]\oplus\chi_{2}[Z^{\pm 1}]
has endomorphism ring
k[Z^{\pm 1}]^{2}
in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
.

Using the above, we may now prove the following.

Lemma 5.7
.

We have

\dim_{k}\left[\chi_{i,\lambda},\chi_{j,\lambda}\right]_{S}=1

for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2
and any
\lambda\in k^{\times}
.

Proof.

We keep having
i\in\{1,2\}
fixed. First note that
\chi_{i,\lambda}\cong\Sigma\chi_{3-i,\lambda}
in
{\operatorname{Ho}}(S)
. Indeed, the short exact sequence (
5.3
) induces a distinguished triangle and we have a commuting diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{3-i}[Z^{\pm 1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{3-i}[Z^{\pm 1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{3-i,\lambda}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma\chi_{i,\lambda}}
.
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Z-\lambda)\cdot}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces+1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Z-\lambda)\cdot}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces+1}

By the axioms of triangulated categories and the triangulated 5-lemma (cf.
[

[\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{3-i,\lambda}]_{S}\cong{\operatorname{Ext}}_{S}^{1}(\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{i,\lambda})\quad\text{and}\quad[\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{i,\lambda}]_{S}\cong{\operatorname{Ext}}_{S}^{2}(\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{i,\lambda})

for

. But we can compute this. Indeed, by considering the long exact sequence on

from (

) and by (

0\to{\operatorname{Ext}}^{1}_{S}(\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{i,\lambda})\to k[Z^{\pm 1}]\xrightarrow{(Z-\lambda)\cdot}k[Z^{\pm 1}]\to{\operatorname{Ext}}^{2}_{S}(\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{i,\lambda})\to 0.

and
[\chi_{i}[Z^{\pm 1}],\chi_{3-i,\lambda}]_{S}=0
. From this we can now compute our Hom spaces by viewing (

and applying the cohomological functor

. Indeed, the associated long exact sequence now gives

Proof of

.

We keep the notation from the proof of
