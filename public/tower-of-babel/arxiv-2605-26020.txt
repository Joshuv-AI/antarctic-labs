Boundary CM points and class groups of small exponent
David Aiken, Erick Ross, Dmitriy Shvydkoy, Hui Xue

Abstract
. Let ℱ \mathcal{F} denote the fundamental domain for SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) on the upper half plane ℋ \mathcal{H} . William Duke showed that as fundamental discriminants D → − ∞ D\to-\infty , the sets CM D \textnormal{CM}_{D} (CM points of discriminant D D ) are equidistributed in ℱ \mathcal{F} . In this paper, we investigate the behavior of CM points on the boundary of ℱ \mathcal{F} . We prove that such CM points are equidistributed on the boundary, and also give a complete characterization of when every CM D \textnormal{CM}_{D} point lies on the boundary. Along the way, we also (conditionally) give a complete classification of negative discriminants with class group of small exponent.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}\coloneq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\text{Im }z>0\}
denote the complex upper half plane, and

\mathcal{F}\coloneq\left\{z=x+iy\in\mathcal{H}:|z|\geq 1,-\frac{1}{2}\leq x\leq 0\right\}\cup\left\{z=x+iy\in\mathcal{H}:|z|>1,0<x<\frac{1}{2}\right\}

For negative discriminants

denote the set of CM points in

of discriminant

is also an action on these
\textnormal{CM}_{D}

Hereafter, when we refer to “discriminants” in this paper, we are only referring to negative discriminants.

In

D
; as

In this paper, we investigate the behavior of CM points on the boundary of
\mathcal{F}
. We study this question for all discriminants

, not just the fundamental ones.

First, we show that the CM points on the boundary of

.

Let
\textnormal{CM}^{\textnormal{LB}}_{|D|\leq\Delta}
denote the CM points on the left boundary of
\mathcal{F}
with absolute discriminant
|D|\leq\Delta
, and
\textnormal{CM}^{\textnormal{LA}}_{|D|\leq\Delta}
denote the CM points on the lower arc of
\mathcal{F}
with absolute discriminant

(1)

(2)

(1)

This equidistribution theorem differs from Duke’s result in that we are considering

The two metrics

\mathcal{F}
and to the lower arc of

2.
Background on CM Theory

In this section, we review the basics of CM theory. The details are all standard, and can be found in Cox
[
2
]
.

Recall that a CM point
\tau
is the solution in
\mathcal{H}
of a quadratic equation with integer coefficients
az^{2}+bz+c=0
. Without loss of generality, we impose here the conditions
a\geq 1
and
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
.
This CM point
\tau
is uniquely determined by the triple
[a,b,c]
,
so we will often refer to a CM point
\tau
by its corresponding triple
[a,b,c]
. Here,
D\coloneq b^{2}-4ac
is called the
discriminant
of
\tau=[a,b,c]
, and we let
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
denote the set of CM points with discriminant
D
. Note that the discriminant
D
here is necessarily negative and congruent to
0
or
1
modulo
4
.

We call two CM points equivalent if they are in the same orbit under the standard action of
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{H}
:
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p&q\\
r&s\end{smallmatrix}\right):\tau\mapsto\frac{p\tau+q}{r\tau+s}
.
It turns out that mapping a CM point
\tau
to the ideal
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\tau\mathbb{Z}
induces a bijection between
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivalence classes of
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
and the class group
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
. Under this bijection,
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\textnormal{CM}_{D}
inherits the group structure of
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
.
Here,
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
denotes the class group of the imaginary quadratic order
\mathcal{O}_{D}
of discriminant
D
.

Now, a discriminant
D_{0}
is called
fundamental
if it is the discriminant of the quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{0}})
. In this case,
\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}
would be the full ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{0}})
, and so
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}})=\text{Cl}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{0}}))
.
It turns out that every discriminant
D
can be factored uniquely as
D=D_{0}f^{2}
, where
D_{0}
is a fundamental discriminant, and
f
is called the conductor. Under this factorization, for any
\tau
such that
\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\tau\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\mathcal{O}_{D}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus f\tau\mathbb{Z}
.

Finally, we mention two facts about the group structure of
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\textnormal{CM}_{D}
. First, the group inverse of the
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
point
[a,b,c]
is
[a,-b,c]
. Second, the
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
group identity in
\mathcal{F}
is given by

\tau=\begin{cases}[1,0,-\frac{D}{4}]&\text{if}\quad D\equiv 0\pmod{4}\\
[1,1,-\frac{D-1}{4}]&\text{if}\quad D\equiv 1\pmod{4}.\\
\end{cases}

(2.1)

Observe that when
D\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, this point lies on the imaginary axis
\{iy\ :\ y\geq 1\}
, and when
D\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, it lies on the left boundary
\{-\frac{1}{2}+iy\ :\ y\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\}
of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof of Theorem

We first give a lemma characterizing the CM points that lie on the boundary of
\mathcal{F}
(or on the imaginary axis).

Lemma 4.1
.

The
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
points in
\mathcal{F}
lying on the boundary of
\mathcal{F}
or on the imaginary axis are precisely those of order dividing
2
.
Moreover,
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
includes a point on the imaginary axis only when
D\equiv 0\pmod{4}
.

Proof.

Recall that the inverse of
[a,b,c]
is
[a,-b,c]
, and observe that this operation corresponds to reflecting points in
\mathcal{H}
across the imaginary axis. This means that a
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
point of order dividing
2
must necessarily be
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivalent to its reflection. Hence such
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
points in
\mathcal{F}
are precisely those that lie on the boundary of
\mathcal{F}
or on the imaginary axis.

Moreover, note that every
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
point on the imaginary axis is of the form
[a,0,c]
. In this case, the discriminant
D=-4ac
must be congruent to
0
modulo
4
.
∎

This lemma allows us to classify all discriminants
D
satisfying the property that every
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
point in
\mathcal{F}
lies on the boundary of
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, we prove Theorem
1.2
here, assuming Theorem
1.3
. Then Theorem
1.3
will be proven in the last two sections of the paper.

Theorem
1.2
.

Consider negative discriminants
D
. Then every
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
point in
\mathcal{F}
lies on the boundary of
\mathcal{F}
if and only if
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
has exponent dividing
2
and
D
is odd or
-4
.

Conditionally on the non-existence of Siegel zeros, such discriminants
D
are precisely those given in the following table. Unconditionally, this table could possibly also include the discriminants arising from one additional fundamental discriminant.

Proof.

The first half of the theorem for even discriminants follows from (

). In this case, the
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
identity
[1,0,-\frac{D}{4}]=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{2}
lies on the boundary of
\mathcal{F}
only for
D=-4
. And since
|\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{-4})|=1
,
\textnormal{CM}_{-4}
consists of just this point.

The first half of the theorem for odd discriminants follows from Lemma
4.1
. In this case, a
\textnormal{CM}_{D}
point in
\mathcal{F}
lies on the boundary of
\mathcal{F}
if and only if it is of order dividing
2
. This immediately yields the desired result.

Finally, the second half of the theorem follows from Theorem

. Here, the desired table of discriminants comes from the classification of class groups of exponent
1
and

.
∎

5.
A Divisibility Property for Conductors

In these last two sections, we work towards a proof of Theorem
1.3
. This section uses ideas from Fan and Pollack
[
5
]
to show that conductors with class groups of a given exponent must satisfy a certain divisibility property (Lemma
5.1
).
It turns out that for any given exponent
E
, there are only finitely many conductors
f
that satisfy this divisibility property.
Hence in the next section, we develop an algorithm to compute the complete list of conductors
f
associated to a fundamental discriminant
D_{0}
such that
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}f^{2}})
has exponent
E
. This algorithm will then be used to construct Tables
A.1
-
A.8
.

We now work towards a proof of Lemma
5.1
.
Fix a fundamental discriminant
D_{0}
, and let
D=D_{0}f^{2}
be a discriminant with conductor
f
. The following exposition follows Fan and Pollack
[
5
]
.
Define the
principal part
of the class group as follows:

\text{PrinCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})\coloneq(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}/f\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}})^{\times}/\langle\text{images of units of }\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}\text{ and images of integers coprime to }f\rangle.

In particular,
\text{PrinCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
can be identified with a subgroup of
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
. Next, we define the
pre-class group
by

\text{PreCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})\coloneq(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}/f\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}})^{\times}/\langle\text{images of integers coprime to }f\rangle.

It follows from the definitions that

\text{PreCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})/U_{f}\cong\text{PrinCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})

(5.1)

where
U_{f}
is the image of
\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}
in
\text{PreCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
. So in particular,
|U_{f}|
divides
|\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|
. Note that for imaginary quadratic fields, we have the following formula for
|\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|
:

|\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|=\begin{cases}6&D_{0}=-3\\
4&D_{0}=-4\\
2&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Now, define the function

L(f)\coloneq\textnormal{lcm}\left\{p^{k}\left(1-\left(\frac{D_{0}}{p}\right)\frac{1}{p}\right)\ \ \colon\ \ p^{k}||f\right\}.

We now show the following lemma that bounds
L(f)
in terms of
\textnormal{Exp}\,\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
.
This is the divisibility property referenced previously that all conductors
f
with class group of a given exponent must satisfy.

Lemma 5.1
.

Fix a negative discriminant
D=D_{0}f^{2}
. Then
L(f)
divides
12\cdot|\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|\cdot\textnormal{Exp}\,\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
.

Proof.

First, by
[
5
, Proposition 2.3]
, we have

L(f)\ \bigg|\ 12\cdot\textnormal{Exp}\,\text{PreCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D}).

Next, as a consequence of (
5.1
), we have

\textnormal{Exp}\,\text{PreCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})\ \bigg|\ |U_{f}|\cdot\textnormal{Exp}\,\text{PrinCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})\ \bigg|\ |\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|\cdot\textnormal{Exp}\,\text{PrinCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D}).

Finally, since
\text{PrinCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
is a subgroup of
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
,

\textnormal{Exp}\,\text{PrinCl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})\ \bigg|\ \textnormal{Exp}\,\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D}).

Combining these three statements,

L(f)\ \bigg|\ 12\cdot|\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|\cdot\textnormal{Exp}\,\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D}),

as desired.
∎

Finally, for any fixed exponent
E
, we take note of a method to determine which
f
satisfy the divisibility property
L(f)\mid(12\cdot|\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|\cdot E)
from Lemma
5.1
. This problem reduces to the question of determining which
f
can take on a given value of
L(f)
. And this question is answered by the following lemma, essentially amounting to a way to invert the function
f\mapsto L(f)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\theta(m)\coloneq\prod_{L(p^{k})|m}p^{k}
. Then

f
divides
\theta(L(f))
.

This lemma follows immediately from the fact that
L(p^{k})\mid L(f)
for each
p^{k}\parallel f
.

6.
An algorithm to compute discriminants

Fix
1\leq E\leq 8
. Then employing Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
, one can use the following algorithm to compute the (conditionally) complete list of discriminants
D
such that
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
has exponent
E
.

•

First, compute the fundamental discriminants
D_{0}
with class group of exponent dividing
E
. (It suffices to check these
D_{0}
, since
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}f^{2}})
surjects onto
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}})
for all conductors
f
.)

\circ

To know how high to compute, one can use
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, which (conditionally) gives the largest fundamental discriminant
D_{0}
with class group of exponent dividing
E
.

•

Then for each fundamental discriminant
D_{0}
, compute the complete list of
f
candidates: those satisfying the divisibility property
L(f)\mid(12\cdot|\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|\cdot E)
from Lemma
5.1
.

\circ

By Lemma
5.2
,
f\mid\theta(L(f))
. So to determine the complete list of
f
candidates, one only needs to check the divisors of
\theta(m)
for
m\mid(12\cdot|\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}}^{\times}|\cdot E)
.

•

For each
f
candidate, check if the exponent of
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}f^{2}})
is
E
.

\circ

To check very large
f
candidates, it is most efficient to first verify that the exponent of
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}f^{\prime 2}})
divides
E
for small divisors
f^{\prime}\mid f
(since for
f^{\prime}\mid f
,
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}f^{2}})
surjects onto
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}f^{\prime 2}})
by
[
2
, Corollary 7.17]
). This optimization quickly excludes the vast majority of
f
candidates.

For exponents
1\leq E\leq 8
, we used this algorithm to compute Tables
A.1
-
A.8
(see
[
9
]
for the code). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Observe that to compute fundamental discriminants
D_{0}
with
\textnormal{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{D_{0}})
of exponent dividing
E
, the above algorithm uses the classifications from
[
4
, Theorem 1]
. The completeness of each of these classifications depends on the conditions listed in Theorem
1.3
(see
[
4
, Theorem 2]
). And in fact, this is only reason we need such conditions; the rest of the algorithm is based on the unconditional results about conductors developed in this paper.
