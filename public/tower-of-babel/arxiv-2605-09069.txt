Approximation of Degenerate Hyperbolic Equations with Interior Degeneracy and Applications to Controllability This work was carried out with the support of the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant nos. 12131008 and U23B2033, and National Key R&D Program of China under grant no. 2024YFA1013101.
Dong-Hui Yang, Bao-Zhu Guo

Abstract
In this paper, we establish the existence of solutions for a particular class of degenerate hyperbolic equations. Following this, we approximate these degenerate equations by employing a sequence of uniformly hyperbolic equations. Notably, this specific approximation result has remained unexplored in the existing body of literature. Ultimately, we utilize this approximation framework to derive controllability results for the original degenerate hyperbolic equations, marking what could potentially be the inaugural investigation into higher-dimensional degenerate hyperbolic equations. Keywords: Degenerate partial differential equations, shape design. AMS subject classifications (2010): 35J70, 35K65, 49Q10, 93B05.

From both physical and mathematical perspectives, it is more intuitive to consider controls that operate either on a segment of the degenerate region or on a non-degenerate portion of the domain.

In order to derive the observability inequality for equation (

2.1
Solution spaces

We introduce the definition

H^{1}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla u)w\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\},

where
\nabla u=(\partial_{x_{1}}u,\cdots,\partial_{x_{N}}u)
. The inner product and the norm on
H^{1}(\Omega;w)

are defined, respectively, as

(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}uv\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v)w\mathrm{d}x,\mbox{ and }\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(u,u)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We define

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)=\text{the closure of }\mathcal{D}(\Omega)\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega;w),

where
\mathcal{D}(\Omega)=C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is the space of test functions.
We denote by
H^{-1}(\Omega;w)
the dual space of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
with pivot space
L^{2}(\Omega)
, which is a subspace of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
.

Now, we define

H^{2}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;w)\colon\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A}u)^{2}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\},

where
\mathcal{A}
is given by (
1.3
).
The inner product and the norm on
H^{2}(\Omega;w)
are given, respectively, by

(u,v)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}=(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A}u)(\mathcal{A}v)\mathrm{d}x,\mbox{ and }\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}=(u,u)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is well-known the spaces

(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}),\ (H^{1}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}),\mbox{ and }(H^{2}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)})

are Hilbert spaces (
[

17
]

Lemma 2.1

Let

and
\alpha\in(0,2)

(N-2+\alpha)^{2}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq 4\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla u)\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

The first part of this lemma can be deduced from
[
27

or
[

. A similar proof can be found in the proof of Lemma

\square

Remark 2.1

From Lemma
2.1
and given that

, we can derive

\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq M^{2-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{4M^{2-\alpha}}{(N-2+\alpha)^{2}}\int_{\Omega}w|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\text{ with }M:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|x|+1,

This is the Poincaré inequality.
In particular, the norm

\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla u)w\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(2.2)

is an equivalent norm in

. From now on, we use (

) to define the norm of

.

is just the

Lemma 2.2

For each

2.2
Spectrum

Now, we consider the following degenerate elliptic equation

\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}u=g,&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.3)

where
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.1

We call
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
is a weak solution of (
2.3
) with respect to
g
, provided

\int_{\Omega}w\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}gv\mathrm{d}x

for all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.

Lemma 2.4

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
. The equation (
2.3
) has a unique solution
u\in D(\mathcal{A})
with respect to
g
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
\alpha
, such that

\int_{\Omega}w\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}g^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Denote

B[u,v]=\int_{\Omega}w\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x,\mbox{ for all }u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w).

Then
B[u,u]=\int_{\Omega}w\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\mathrm{d}x
and

|B[u,v]|\leq\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}.

Note that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)\subseteq H^{-1}(\Omega;w)
by

\int_{\Omega}fv\mathrm{d}x\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}

according to Remark
2.1
. From the Lax-Milgram theorem, we get there exists a unique
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
such that
B[u,v]=(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Finally, from
\mathcal{A}u=g
we get
u\in H^{2}(\Omega;w)
. This complete the proof of this lemma.

\square

Notation 2.1

Based on Remark
2.1

This means that for each

\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}\Phi_{n}=\lambda_{n}\Phi_{n},&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\

(2.5)

Furthermore, from (

\lambda_{1}=\inf_{0\neq u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}w\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x}\geq\frac{(N-2+\alpha)^{2}}{4M^{2-\alpha}}.

(2.6)

For subsequent discussions, we let
\Phi_{n}(x)\in D(\mathcal{A})
denote the

n
-th eigenfunction of
\mathcal{A}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
where
n\in\mathbb{N}

The set
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
forms an orthonormal basis for

, and, in addition, constitutes an orthogonal subset of

.

Lemma 2.5

Let
u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\Phi_{i}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
with
u_{i}=(u,\Phi_{i})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,
\nabla u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\nabla\Phi_{i}
and
\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, Moreover,

u\in H^{2}(\Omega;w)\Leftrightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}<\infty,

and

\mathcal{A}u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i}\mbox{ and }\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

From (
2.4
), we obtain

(\Phi_{k},\Phi_{l})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\Phi_{k}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{l})w\mathrm{d}x=\delta_{kl}\lambda_{k}\mbox{ for }k,l\in\mathbb{N},

(2.7)

where
\delta_{kl}
denotes the Kronecker delta function, which is defined as
\delta_{kl}=1
when
k=l
and
\delta_{kl}=0
when
k\neq l
.
We now proceed to show that
\{\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
forms an orthonormal basis for
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.
From (
2.7
), it is readily apparent that
\{\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal subset of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
. To establish that it is indeed a basis, we employ a proof by contradiction. Assume that there exists a nonzero function
0\neq u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
such that

\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\Phi_{k}\cdot\nabla u)w\mathrm{d}x=0\mbox{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
forms an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
, for any
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, we can express
u
as

u=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}\Phi_{k},\mbox{ where }d_{k}=(u,\Phi_{k})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.8)

Then, from (
2.5
), we have

0=(u,\Phi_{k})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\Phi_{k}\cdot\nabla u)w\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{k}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}u\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{k}d_{k},

which implies that
d_{k}=0
. This leads to the conclusion that
u=0
, which is a contradiction.

From the above discussion, as
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, we have

u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{i}\left(\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{k}\right),\mbox{ and }\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{i}^{2},

where

e_{i}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left[\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{i}\right]\right)w\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Phi_{i})w\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\Omega}u\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x=\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{i}.

Hence,
\nabla u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\nabla\Phi_{i}
. Moreover,
\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}u_{i}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Let
u\in H^{2}(\Omega;w)
. Define
\varphi_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\Phi_{i}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\cap H^{2}(\Omega;w)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, from

\begin{split}(\mathcal{A}u,\mathcal{A}\varphi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A}u)\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}w\nabla u\cdot\nabla\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\int_{\Omega}u\Phi_{i}\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\end{split}

and
\|\mathcal{A}\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}
, by the Cauchy inequality, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}<\infty
.

Let
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}<\infty
. For each
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

\begin{split}(\mathcal{A}u,\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi)w\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\Phi_{i}\cdot\nabla\varphi)w\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{i}\varphi\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Note that

\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}<\infty\mbox{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

that is,
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Hence,

(\mathcal{A}u,\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i},\varphi\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This implies that
\mathcal{A}u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i}
and
\|\mathcal{A}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We complete the proof of this lemma.

\square

2.3
Existence of the weak solution

This section focuses on establishing the existence of weak solutions for equation (
1.1
).

Definition 2.2

A function

\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\mbox{ with }\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}(Q),\ \partial_{tt}\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))

is said to be a weak solution of (
1.1
) with respect to
(\varphi^{0},\varphi^{1},f)
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i) For every
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
and almost every
t\in[0,T]
, the following equation holds:

\langle\partial_{tt}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\int_{\Omega}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\psi\mathrm{d}x;

(2.9)

(ii) The initial conditions are satisfied as
\varphi(0)=\varphi^{0},\partial_{t}\varphi(0)=\varphi^{1}
.

The following lemma is just
[
3
, Lemma 2.3, p. 61)]
.

Lemma 2.6

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a
C^{m}
-Riemannian manifold with compact boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}
. Then,
there exists a
C^{m-1}
-vector field
\boldsymbol{n}
such that

\boldsymbol{n}(x)=\nu(x),\ x\in\partial\mathcal{M},\mbox{ and }|\boldsymbol{n}(x)|\leq 1,\ x\in\mathcal{M},

where
\nu
denotes the unit outward normal vector to
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

We are now in a position to demonstrate the existence of a weak solution to equation (
1.1
).

Theorem 2.1

Given
\varphi^{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w),\varphi^{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{2}(Q)
, there exists a unique weak solution

\varphi
characterized by

\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap H^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))

(2.10)

for the equation (
1.1
). Moreover,

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\in L^{2}(\partial Q).

(2.11)

Additionally, the following estimate holds:

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|\varphi(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|\partial_{t}\varphi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)+\|\partial_{tt}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))}+\left\|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(\partial Q)}\\
&\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\right),\end{split}

(2.12)

where the constant
C>0
is only dependent on
\alpha,T
and
M
.

Furthermore, if
f\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\varphi^{0}\in D(\mathcal{A}),\varphi^{1}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, then

\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;D(\mathcal{A}))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))\cap H^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

(2.13)

Additionally, the following estimate is valid:

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|\varphi(t)\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}+\|\varphi(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0}))}+\|\partial_{t}\varphi(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right)+\left\|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\right\|_{H^{1}(0,T;\partial\Omega)}\\
&\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right),\end{split}

(2.14)

where
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})=\{x\in\Omega\colon\dist(x,\partial\Omega)<2R_{0}\}
, and the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,R_{0},T
and
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0})
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem proceeds through the following nine steps.

Step 1: Galerkin Method.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

y^{m}(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{m}d_{n}^{m}(t)\Phi_{n}(x).

(2.15)

Consider the following ordinary differential equations

(d_{n}^{m})^{\prime\prime}+\lambda_{n}d_{n}^{m}=f_{n}^{m},\ n=1,\cdots,m

(2.16)

with initial conditions

d_{n}^{m}(0)=(\varphi^{0},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad(d_{n}^{m})^{\prime}(0)=(\varphi^{1},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\ n=1,\cdots,m,

(2.17)

where

f^{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{m}f_{n}^{m}\Phi_{n},\quad f_{n}^{m}=(f,\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mbox{ for }n=1,\cdots,m.

(2.18)

By the standard theory for ordinary differential equations, there exists a unique function
d^{m}(t)=(d_{1}^{m}(t),\cdots,d_{m}^{m}(t))
, satisfying (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) for
0\leq t\leq T
. It is clear that (
2.16
) is equivalent to

\left(\partial_{tt}y^{m},\Phi_{n}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(y^{m},\Phi_{n})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(f^{m},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\ n=1,\cdots,m

(2.19)

by (
2.7
) and (
2.15
).

Step 2: Energy Estimate.

Multiply equation (
2.16
) by
(d_{n}^{m})^{\prime}(t)
and sum over
n=1,\cdots,m
, we arrive at

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{tt}y^{m})(\partial_{t}y^{m})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}w\nabla y^{m}\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}y^{m}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f^{m}\partial_{t}y^{m}\,dx.\end{split}

(2.20)

It is worth noting that

\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{tt}y^{m})(\partial_{t}y^{m})\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{t}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\quad\int_{\Omega}w\nabla y^{m}\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}y^{m}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}.

Combining these results with (
2.20
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\partial_{t}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\right)\leq\|f^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Consequently, by employing Gronwall’s inequality
[
8
, Appendix B.2 (j), p. 708]
along with (
2.17
), (
2.18
), and Lemma
2.5
, we have for almost every
t\in[0,T]

that

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}&\leq e^{t}\left(\|\partial_{t}y^{m}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|y^{m}(0)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}+\|f^{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\right)\\
&\leq e^{T}\left(\|\varphi^{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\varphi^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(2.21)

Furthermore, for any
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
satisfying
\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq 1
,
we can decompose
v
as
v=v^{1}+v^{2}
, where
v^{1}\in{\rm{span}}\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
and
(v^{2},\Phi_{k})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
for
k=1,\cdots,m
. Based on (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), we get

\begin{split}\langle\partial_{tt}y^{m},v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}&=(\partial_{tt}y^{m},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(\partial_{tt}y^{m},v^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&=(f^{m},v^{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(y^{m},v^{1})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}.\end{split}

Thus, by invoking Remark
2.1
and the fact that

\|v^{1}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\leq 1
, we have for almost every
t\in[0,T]
that

\|\partial_{tt}y^{m}(t)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w)}\leq C\|f^{m}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y^{m}(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)},

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
and
M
. As a result, according to (
2.21
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{tt}y^{m}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\,dt\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\right),

(2.22)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,T
, and
M
.

Step 3: Approximation or weak convergence.

Based on (
2.21
) and (
2.22
), we can extract a subsequence from

\{y^{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
, which we shall continue to denote by the same symbol, along with a function

y\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))

satisfying the following weak convergence properties:

\begin{split}y^{m}&\rightharpoonup y\;\text{ weak}^{*}\hbox{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\\
\partial_{t}y^{m}&\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}y\;\text{ weak}^{*}\hbox{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\\
\partial_{tt}y^{m}&\rightharpoonup\partial_{tt}y\quad\text{weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w)).\end{split}

(2.23)

Furthermore, leveraging (
2.21
), we derive the estimate

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|y(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|\partial_{t}y(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)+\|\partial_{tt}y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))}\\
&\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\right),\end{split}

(2.24)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,T
, and
M
. Additionally, we observe that

y\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\hbox{ and }\partial_{t}y\in C([0,T];H^{-1}(\Omega;w)).

(2.25)

Step 4: The function
y
is a weak solution of (
1.1
).

We proceed to demonstrate that

y

fulfills Definition
2.2
(i).

Fix an arbitrary integer

k\in\mathbb{N}
, and select a function
\psi\in C^{2}([0,T];H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))

that takes the form

\psi(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\psi_{n}(t)\Phi_{n}.

(2.26)

By choosing

m\geq k
, multiplying (
2.16
) by
\psi_{n}(t)
for
n=1,\cdots,k
,
and then integrating with respect to
t\in[0,T]
, we arrive at

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\langle\partial_{tt}y^{m},\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+(y^{m},\psi)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}(f^{m},\psi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt.

(2.27)

Based on (
2.23
), taking the limit as

m\to\infty
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\langle\partial_{tt}y,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+(y,\psi)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}(f,\psi)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt.\end{split}

(2.28)

It is important to note that the set of functions

\psi(t)

of the form (
2.26
) is dense in
L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
. Consequently, (
2.28
) holds for all

\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
. Combining this with (
2.28
), we derive

\langle\partial_{tt}y,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+(y,v)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
and almost every
t\in[0,T]
. This establishes the validity of Definition
2.2
(ii).

Next, we proceed to verify that
y(0)=\varphi^{0}
and
\partial_{t}y(0)=\varphi^{1}
.

For any
h\in C^{2}([0,T];H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
satisfying
h(T)=\partial_{t}h(T)=0
, from (
2.28
) and (
2.25
), we have

\begin{split}&\iint_{Q}y\partial_{tt}h\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}w\nabla y\cdot\nabla h\,dx\,dt\\
&=\iint_{Q}fh\,dx\,dt-(y(0),\partial_{t}h(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\partial_{t}y(0),h(0)\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}.\end{split}

(2.29)

Similarly, from (
2.27
) we obtain

\begin{split}&\iint_{Q}y^{k}\partial_{tt}h\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}w\nabla y^{k}\cdot\nabla h\,dx\,dt\\
&=\iint_{Q}f^{k}h\,dx\,dt-(y^{k}(0),\partial_{t}h(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\partial_{t}y^{k}(0),h(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\end{split}

which, when combined with (
2.17
), (
2.18
), and (
2.23
), leads to

\begin{split}&\iint_{Q}y\partial_{tt}h\,dx\,dt+\iint_{Q}w\nabla y\cdot\nabla h\,dx\,dt\\
&=\iint_{Q}fh\,dx\,dt-(\varphi^{0},\partial_{t}h(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\varphi^{1},h(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

This, together with (
2.29
), implies

y(0)=\varphi^{0},\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}y(0)=\varphi^{1}.

This completes the proof of Definition
2.2
(ii).

Step 5: Uniqueness.

Assume that
\varphi^{0}=\varphi^{1}=f=0
. Let

y

denote the solution of (
1.1
) corresponding to the parameter values

(\varphi^{0},\varphi^{1},f)=(0,0,0)
. For a fixed
s\in[0,T]
, define the function

h(t)=\begin{cases}\int_{t}^{s}y(\tau)\,d\tau,&t\in[0,s],\\
0,&t\in[s,T].\end{cases}

It follows that
h(t)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, and the following equality holds:

\int_{0}^{s}\langle\partial_{tt}y,h\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\,dt+\int_{0}^{s}w\nabla y\cdot\nabla h\,dt=0.

Observe that
\partial_{t}y(0)=h(s)=0
.
By integrating by parts with respect to

t

and utilizing the fact that
\partial_{t}h=-y
for
0\leq t\leq s
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{s}(\partial_{t}y,y)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt-\int_{0}^{s}(\partial_{t}h,h)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\,dt=0.

This can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{d}{dt}\|y(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,dt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{d}{dt}\|h\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\,dt=0.

Given that
y(0)=0
and
h(s)=0
, we deduce that

\|y(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|h(0)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}=\|y(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|h(s)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}=0.

Consequently,
y(s)=0
. Since
s\in[0,T]
is arbitrary, it follows that
y=0
. This establishes the uniqueness of the solution.

Step 6: Improved regularity.

We further assume that
\partial_{t}f\in L^{2}(Q)
, that is,
f\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
. Let
\overline{y}^{m}=\partial_{t}y^{m}
.
From (
2.15
), (
2.16
) and (
2.19
), we obtain

(\partial_{tt}\overline{y}^{m},\Phi_{k})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\overline{y}^{m},\Phi_{k})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(\partial_{t}f^{m},\Phi_{k})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mbox{ for }0\leq t\leq T\mbox{ and }k=1,\cdots,m.

By multiplying with
(d_{k}^{m})^{\prime\prime}(t)
, and summing over
k=1,\cdots,m
, we have

(\partial_{tt}\overline{y}^{m},\partial_{t}\overline{y}^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\overline{y}^{m},\partial_{t}\overline{y}^{m})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(\partial_{t}f^{m},\partial_{t}\overline{y}^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Following the same reasoning as in (
2.21
), we derive

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|\partial_{t}\overline{y}^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\overline{y}^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right)\\
&\leq\|\overline{y}^{m}(0)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|\partial_{t}\overline{y}^{m}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{t}f^{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}.\end{split}

(2.30)

Note that

\partial_{t}\overline{y}^{m}(0)=(\partial_{tt}y^{m})(0)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(d_{k}^{m})^{\prime\prime}(0)\Phi_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}f_{k}^{m}(0)\Phi_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}d_{k}^{m}(0)\Phi_{k};

and

\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}f_{k}^{m}(0)\Phi_{k}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\|f(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\right)

by
[
8
, Theorem 2 (iii) in Chapter 5.9.2, p. 302]
and
f\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
; and

\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}d_{k}^{m}(0)\Phi_{k}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}^{2}(\varphi^{0},\Phi_{k})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{A}\varphi^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

by (
2.17
) and Lemma
2.5
. We obtain

\|\partial_{t}\overline{y}^{m}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right),

(2.31)

where the constants
C>0
depending only on
T
. Now, we have

\begin{split}\|\overline{y}^{m}(0)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}&=\|\partial_{t}y^{m}(0)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\\
&=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{m}(\varphi^{1},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n},\sum_{n=1}^{m}(\varphi^{1},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Phi_{n}\right)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\\
&\leq\sum_{n=1}^{m}(\varphi^{1},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lambda_{n}\leq\|\varphi^{1}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2},\end{split}

(2.32)

by (
2.15
), (
2.17
), and Lemma
2.5
. Combining (
2.30
), (
2.31
), and (
2.32
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|\partial_{t}\overline{y}^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\overline{y}^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\|\mathcal{A}\varphi^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right)\end{split}

(2.33)

for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, where the constants
C>0
depends only on
T
.

Now, multiplying (
2.19
) by
\lambda_{k}d_{k}^{m}
, summing over
k=1,\cdots,m
, since
\mathcal{A}y^{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{m}y_{n}^{m}\lambda_{n}\Phi_{n}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, we get

\|\mathcal{A}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(y^{m},\mathcal{A}y^{m})_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(f^{m}-\partial_{tt}y^{m},\mathcal{A}y^{m})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(2.34)

Choose
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{N}),0\leq\zeta\leq 1
such that

\begin{split}\zeta=1\mbox{ on }\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0}),\mbox{ and }\zeta=0\mbox{ on }\Omega-\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0}),\end{split}

and

|\nabla\zeta|\leq CR_{0}^{-1},\mbox{ and }\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\zeta}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right|\leq CR_{0}^{-2},\mbox{ for all }i,j=1,\cdots,N,

where the constants
C>0
are absolute. Then,
\xi=\zeta y^{m}
is the solution of the following equation

\mathcal{A}\xi=g\mbox{ in }\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0}),\quad\xi=0\mbox{ in }\partial\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0}),

(2.35)

where

g=-\zeta\partial_{tt}y^{m}+\zeta f^{m}-2w\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla y^{m}-y^{m}\Div(w\nabla\zeta).

It is evident that (
2.35
) is a uniformly elliptic equation with
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
By the classical elliptic regularity theory
[
8
, Theorem 1, Chapter 6.3.1, p.327 and Theorem 4, Chapter 6.3.2, p. 334]
, we have

\|\xi\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0}))}\leq C\left(\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,R_{0}
and
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0})
.
Applying this to (
2.34
), we obtain

\|y^{m}\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0}))}+\|\mathcal{A}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|f^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{tt}y^{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|y^{m}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\right),

(2.36)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,R_{0},T
and
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0})
.

Recalling
\overline{y}^{m}=\partial_{t}y^{m}
, from (
2.21
) and (
2.33
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|y^{m}(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0}))}+\|y^{m}(t)\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}+\|\partial_{t}y^{m}(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|\partial_{tt}y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right).\end{split}

Letting
m\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\operatorname*{ess\ \!sup}_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|\varphi(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0}))}+\|\varphi(t)\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}+\|\partial_{t}\varphi(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|\partial_{tt}\varphi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right),\end{split}

(2.37)

where the constant
C>0
depends only
\alpha,R_{0},T
and
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0})
.

Step 7: Hidden regularity.

By invoking a density argument, it is sufficient to establish (
2.11
) for the cases where

\varphi^{0}\in D(\mathcal{A}),\varphi^{1}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w),f\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
. According to Lemma
2.6
, there exists a smooth vector field

\boldsymbol{n}
defined on
\Omega
satisfying

\boldsymbol{n}(x)=\nu(x)\mbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega,\mbox{ and }\supp\boldsymbol{n}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})},

as well as

|\boldsymbol{n}(x)|\leq 1\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\mbox{ and }|\Div\boldsymbol{n}(x)|\leq CR_{0}^{-1}\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega,

where the positive constant
C
depends solely on
\boldsymbol{n}
(and thus, by Lemma
2.6
, only on

\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0}
.

If the above conditions are not met, we can multiply

\boldsymbol{n}

by a cut-off function
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
such that
\zeta=1
on
\overline{\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;R_{0})}
,

\zeta=0
on
\Omega\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})}
, and
|\nabla\zeta|\leq CR_{0}^{-1}
. Consequently,

\zeta\boldsymbol{n}
will be the desired function.

From
Step 6
, we know that
\partial_{tt}\varphi,\mathcal{A}\varphi\in L^{2}(Q)
. By multiplying both sides of (
1.1
) by

\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi
and integrating over
Q
(the integrations are indeed on
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})\times(0,T)
since
\boldsymbol{n}=0
on
(\Omega\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})})\times(0,T)
), we obtain

\begin{split}I&\equiv\iint_{Q}f(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\iint_{Q}(\partial_{tt}\varphi)(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\iint_{Q}\Div(w\nabla\varphi)(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\equiv I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

(2.38)

Employing integration by parts and considering that

\supp\boldsymbol{n}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})}
, we have

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}\varphi)(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathrm{d}x\bigg|_{t=0}^{t=T}-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla|\partial_{t}\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}\varphi)(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathrm{d}x\bigg|_{t=0}^{t=T}-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\partial Q}|\partial_{t}\varphi|^{2}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q}|\partial_{t}\varphi|^{2}\Div\boldsymbol{n}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

Given that
\partial_{t}\varphi=0
on
\partial Q
,
\supp\boldsymbol{n}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})
and (
2.24
), we derive

\begin{split}|I_{1}|&\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\right),\end{split}

(2.39)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,R_{0},T
and
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})
.

Further integration by parts, considering
\varphi=0
on
\partial Q
and
\supp\boldsymbol{n}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;2R_{0})
, yields

\begin{split}I_{2}&=-\iint_{\partial Q}w\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\partial Q}w\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\left(\nabla\varphi\cdot[(D\boldsymbol{n})\nabla\varphi]\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 12.80373pt-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q}(\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla w)|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{Q}w|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\Div\boldsymbol{n}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Combining this result with (
2.38
) and (
2.39
) and
I=I_{1}+I_{2}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\iint_{\partial Q}\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}S\mathrm{d}t&\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\right)^{2},\end{split}

(2.40)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,R_{0},T
and
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0})
.

Step 8: We prove the forth term in (
2.14
).

Since
z=\partial_{t}\varphi
serves as the weak solution to the following system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}z+\mathcal{A}z=\partial_{t}f,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
z=0,&\mbox{on }\partial Q,\\
z(0)=\varphi^{1},\partial_{t}z(0)=f(0)-\mathcal{A}\varphi^{0},&\mbox{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

as derived in
Step 7
, we obtain

\left\|\frac{\partial z}{\partial\nu}\right\|_{L^{2}(\partial Q)}\leq C\left(\|\varphi^{0}\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}+\|\varphi^{1}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right),

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,R_{0},T
and
\mathcal{O}(\partial\Omega;3R_{0})
.

By combining this result with the fourth term of (
2.12
) and utilizing the identity

\partial_{t}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial(\partial_{t}\varphi)}{\partial\nu}
, we arrive at the fourth term of (
2.14
).

Step 9: We end the proof of this theorem.

From
Steps 1-5
, we establish the first three terms in (
2.12
). The fourth term of (
2.12
) is derived from
Step 7
. Additionally, (
2.14
) is obtained by combining the results from
Step 6
and
Step 8
. This completes the proof of the theorem.

\square

The subsequent lemma provides an equivalent criterion for the existence of solutions to equation (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.7

The function

\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\mbox{ satisfying }\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}(Q),\ \partial_{tt}\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))

constitutes the weak solution of (
1.1
) with respect to
(\varphi^{0},\varphi^{1},f)
if and only if

\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)),\mbox{ with }\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}(Q)

fulfills the following condition:

\begin{split}&\iint_{Q}\varphi(t)\partial_{tt}\psi(t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{1}\psi(0)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{0}\partial_{t}\psi(0)\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(2.41)

for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q})
such that
\supp\psi(t)\subseteq\Omega
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\psi(T)=\partial_{t}\psi(T)=0
.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q})
with
\supp\psi(t)\subseteq\Omega
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\psi(T)=\partial_{t}\psi(T)=0
. According to Definition
2.2
(i), we have

\begin{split}\langle\partial_{tt}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\int_{\Omega}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(2.42)

for almost every
t\in[0,T]
, and

\varphi\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)),\text{ and }\partial_{t}\varphi\in C([0,T];H^{-1}(\Omega;w)).

(2.43)

By integrating over the interval
[0,T]
based on (
2.42
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{tt}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Applying integration by parts with respect to
t\in[0,T]
and utilizing (
2.43
), we derive

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\varphi\partial_{tt}\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-(\varphi(0),\partial_{t}\psi(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle\partial_{t}\varphi(0),\psi(0)\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\\
&=\iint_{Q}f\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{1}\psi(0)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{0}\partial_{t}\psi(0)\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

This establishes (
2.41
).

We proceed to establish the sufficiency.

Indeed, for each
\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
, we have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))}&=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))}\leq 1}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{A}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))}\leq 1}\iint_{Q}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))}.\end{split}

This implies that
\mathcal{A}\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))
. Now, for each
\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
, since

\begin{split}\iint_{Q}\langle f,\xi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t&=\iint_{Q}f\xi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))}\end{split}

by Remark
2.1
, we conclude that
f\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w))
.

Next, taking
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q)
, from (
2.41
), we obtain

\begin{split}\iint_{Q}\varphi\partial_{tt}\xi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t&=\iint_{Q}f\xi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\iint_{Q}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\xi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\langle f,\xi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{A}\varphi,\xi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

and thus

\partial_{tt}\varphi=f-\mathcal{A}\varphi

in the sense of distribution. Therefore,

\partial_{tt}\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega;w)).

Fix
t\in(0,T)
and
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4}\min\{t,T-t\})
. For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
, choose
\zeta\in C^{\infty}(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})

with
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
such that
\zeta=1
on
(t-\delta,t+\delta)
and
\supp\zeta\subseteq(t-\delta-\varepsilon,t+\delta+\varepsilon)
. Taking
\psi=\zeta v
, then
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q)
, and from (
2.41
), we get (in distribution sense)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\zeta\langle\partial_{tt}\varphi,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{Q}\zeta w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{Q}\zeta fv\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Hence, letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

\int_{t-\delta}^{t+\delta}\langle\partial_{tt}\varphi,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{\Omega\times(t-\delta,t+\delta)}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{\Omega\times(t-\delta,t+\delta)}fv\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Therefore, letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
, for each
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and almost every
t\in(0,T)
, we get

\langle\partial_{tt}\varphi,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\int_{\Omega}w\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}fv\mathrm{d}x

according to the Lebesgue point theorem. This implies (
2.9
) by the density of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.

Finally, denote
\varphi(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(t)\Phi_{n}(x)
. Then, for each
\zeta\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\zeta(T)=0,\partial_{t}\zeta(T)=0
, and for each
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, take
\psi(x,t)=\zeta(t)v(x)
. From (
2.41
) we have

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}(\varphi(t),v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\partial_{tt}\zeta\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}(\varphi(t),v)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}\zeta\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}(f,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\zeta\mathrm{d}t+\zeta(0)(\varphi^{1},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(\partial_{t}\zeta(0))(\varphi^{0},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Letting
v\rightarrow\Phi_{n}\ (n\in\mathbb{N})
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, noting that
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{n}(t)\partial_{tt}\zeta\mathrm{d}t+\lambda_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{n}(t)\zeta\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}f_{n}(t)\zeta\mathrm{d}t+\zeta(0)\varphi_{n}^{0}-(\partial_{t}\zeta(0))\varphi_{n}^{1},\end{split}

where
f_{n}(t)=(f(t),\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\varphi_{n}^{1}=(\varphi^{1},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\varphi_{n}^{0}=(\varphi^{0},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
It is easily verified that
\varphi_{n}
is a weak solution of the following one-dimensional system

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}\varphi_{n}(t)+\lambda_{n}\varphi_{n}(t)=f_{n}(t),&t\in[0,T],\\
\varphi_{n}(0)=\varphi_{n}^{0},\partial_{t}\varphi_{n}(0)=\varphi_{n}^{1}.\end{cases}

(2.44)

By the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we know that the sequence

\{\varphi^{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{m}\varphi_{n}(t)\Phi_{n}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}

converges weakly to the solution
\widetilde{\varphi}
of (
1.1
) with respect to the data
(\varphi^{0},\varphi^{1},f)
. Note that the sequence
\{\varphi^{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly to
\varphi(x,t)
in
L^{2}(Q)
. Hence
\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi
. This implies that
\varphi(0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(0)\Phi_{n}=\varphi^{0},\partial_{t}\varphi(0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[\partial_{t}\varphi_{n}(0)]\Phi_{n}=\varphi^{1}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

Lemma 3.1

There exists a

\psi_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=|x|\quad\text{for }|x|\geq\varepsilon,\quad|x|\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{on }[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon],

\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R},\quad|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\leq C\text{ on }\mathbb{R},\quad|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon},\text{ and }|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\text{ on }\mathbb{R},

Proof.

Consider the polynomial function

Lemma 3.2

Let

(N+\alpha-2)^{2}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-2}z^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq 4\int_{\Omega}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}|\nabla z|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Remark 3.2

Theorem
1.1
is far from being a trivial consequence. Its proof relies essentially on Theorems

and

, whose estimates are uniform with respect to the parameter

.

A major difficulty in the proof of Theorem

lies in the fact that the weighted Sobolev spaces (i.e., the solution spaces) depend on

with

\Omega
.

(4.8)

We directly confirm that
(\Omega,g_{\varepsilon})
constitutes a
C^{2}
Riemannian manifold. Its Christoffel symbol
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{N}g_{\varepsilon}^{kl}(\frac{\partial g_{li}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial x^{j}}+\frac{\partial g_{lj}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial x^{i}}-\frac{\partial g_{ij}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial x^{l}})\ (i,j,k=1,\cdots,N)
is

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x^{j}}\delta_{ik}+\frac{\partial\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x^{i}}\delta_{jk}-\frac{\partial\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x^{k}}\delta_{ij}\right)

(4.9)

according to (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).

The gradient of a function
u
on
(\Omega,g_{\varepsilon})
is expressed as

\nabla_{g_{\varepsilon}}u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{\varepsilon}^{ij}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}.

(4.10)

Then, from (
4.7
), we obtain

\langle\nabla_{g_{\varepsilon}}u,\nabla_{g_{\varepsilon}}v\rangle_{g_{\varepsilon}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}w_{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x^{j}}\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\right\rangle_{g_{\varepsilon}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x^{i}}=(\nabla_{0}u\cdot\nabla_{0}v)w_{\varepsilon}.

(4.11)

The divergence of a vector field
X=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
on
(\Omega,g_{\varepsilon})
is given by

\begin{split}\Div_{g_{\varepsilon}}X=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}X^{i}\Gamma_{ik}^{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}-\frac{\alpha N}{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}\frac{\partial\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x^{i}}.\end{split}

Hence

\mathcal{A}u=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\left(w_{\varepsilon}(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=-\Div_{0}(\nabla_{g_{\varepsilon}}u).

(4.12)

Denote the Levi-Civita connection in metric
g_{\varepsilon}
by

, that is,

D_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}\mbox{ for all }i,j=1,\cdots,N.

(4.13)

In other words, for each vector field

on Riemannian manifold
(\Omega,g_{\varepsilon})
, we have

DH(X,Y)=\langle D_{X}H,Y\rangle_{g_{\varepsilon}}\mbox{ for all }X,Y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{N}.

(4.14)

Lemma 4.1

(4.15)

according to (

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we first approximate the degenerate hyperbolic equation by uniformly hyperbolic equations and establish the controllability (i.e., the observability inequality) for the degenerate hyperbolic equation with a degenerate interior point. To the best of our knowledge, these results do not appear in the existing literature. These findings imply that the degenerate hyperbolic equation can be controlled at the boundary.

As is well known, a natural approach to address the controllability of degenerate hyperbolic equations is to approximate them by uniformly hyperbolic equations. If the degenerate points are interior points, we approximate the coefficients in the partial differential operator
\mathcal{A}
using appropriate functions. If the degenerate points lie on the boundary, the shape design method must be employed. However, we note that the behavior of wave propagation near the degenerate points remains an
open problem
. This is a purely mathematical issue.

We emphasize that the cut-off method plays a fundamental role in the analysis of degenerate partial differential equations. The so-called cut-off method involves isolating problematic regions (the “bad” points or regions) and focusing on the well-behaved ones (the “good” points or regions), allowing us to derive useful properties on the latter. In the proof of Theorem
2.1
, this method is essential for obtaining the improved regularity estimates (
2.35
) and (
2.36
), as well as for handling the normal vector
\boldsymbol{n}
in the hidden regularity estimate. Furthermore, even in the construction of solutions to (
4.39
), the Dirichlet map relies on the cut-off method (see
[
33
, Lemmas 2.10 and 2.11]
). In degenerate partial differential equations, the “bad” regions typically correspond to points where regularity cannot be improved. At such points, certain quantities may fail to be well defined or controllable—for instance, the normal derivative
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}
may not exist on the degenerate part of the boundary. Consequently, one cannot directly obtain enhanced regularity or boundary estimates there. This difficulty motivates us to isolate these problematic regions and instead perform the analysis on the “good” regions, where stronger regularity and controllability properties can be established.
