Low-profile conformal triple-band textile antenna using conductive fabric and PDMS for wearable electronics
Authors: Musa Hussain, Wahaj Abbas Awan, Syed Muzahir Abbas, Yong Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper presents a low-profile, lightweight, and flexible antenna to work over three bands used by wearable electronic devices. Wearable applications demand the flexibility of the antenna, and this is achieved by employing a nickel-copper-coated ripstop conductive fabric of the radiative part (patch and ground) and a silicon-based polymer, polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), for the substrate material. The geometry of the antenna is a triangular patch with two stubs and two slots added to the antenna to enhance the impedance and matching bandwidth. The suggested antenna offers three frequency bands at 3.5, 5.15, and 6.6 GHz, which enable 5G-enabled wearables, Wi-Fi devices, and short-range sensing applications, respectively. The proposed work is validated by studying performance parameters such as S-parameter, gain, and radiation pattern. To make the proposed antenna suitable for wearable devices, conformal analysis, SAR analysis, and gain on the body are studied. The conformal analysis shows a good agreement with the flat antenna analyzed in terms of S11, gain, efficiency, and radiation pattern. The design is also retrieved on a human body phantom to investigate both SAR and gain using the Sim4Life EM simulation tool. Moreover, the antenna’s performance is compared with recently published articles. The findings provided by the suggested antenna, together with comprehensive comparisons to existing literature, demonstrate that the antenna exhibits robust performance and is well-suited for incorporation into wearable electronic devices.

Introduction

Recently, body area networks (BAN) have experienced rapid growth in research and academia due to their applications in wearable technology. It has applications in diverse fields like sports, healthcare, education, firefighting, and other emergency services. In the case of healthcare services and wearable electronic gadgets like smartwatches, smartphones, smart glasses, and health care bands are used to monitor blood pressure, pulse rate, human body temperature, glucose level, heart rate, and calories burnt1,2. This information is shared with concerned hospitals and nearby emergency centers to provide better assistance. Similarly, point-to-point communication is adopted to make connections between users and nearby police or emergency services centers. Due to these multiple and important applications, the demand for these electronic devices has increased3. Besides the significant requirement of these devices, the advancement is also observed due to their multiple applications; especially for wearable applications, compact, lightweight, low-cost, flexible, and efficient devices are required4,5.

The antenna is regarded as one of the most valuable elements of communication and electronic devices. In the case of the above wireless electronic devices, the antennas that are necessary are small, flexible, low-profile, and geometrically simplified structured antennas6. One of these devices is the patch antenna, which is an important device owing to its simple construction and low profile and is commonly applied in textile applications7. The patch antenna is a form of an antenna that is also referred to as a ‘textile antenna’, and it is made by using conductive material in the form of a cloth and is used in wearable gadgets8. The lightweight, compact and simple geometry, outward invisibility and flexibility make these antennas easily implantable on the human body or can be fitted into wearable gadgets9.

One of the key characteristics of the presented and designed antenna to be used in the wearable applications is also its operational band10. The lower frequency bands, e.g., the industrial, scientific and medical band (ISM band) and wireless local area network band (WLAN band), were popular targets in the past. The devices running on 5G technologies, or in the future, 6G technologies, in recent years also have some more spectrums aimed at fast, reliable, high data rate, and efficient combinations11,12. In this development, the 5G sub-6 GHz band (n77, n78, and n79), the 5G Wi-Fi band, and Wi-Fi 6e or 7e are used in 6G communication systems13,14.

In addition to the operation of bands, wearable-targeted antennas are designed with the help of different flexible, bendable, and stretchable materials15. Wearable antennas are made using flexible materials including felt, chitosan, PDMS, polyester, Kapton, and denim. These are materials that are used depending on applications and availability16. Wearable antennas are also not confined to PET and copper, as other types of material such as silver-based ink, Au-Ni, graphene, carbon nanotubes (CNT), and other conductive fabric materials are used in wearable antennas17. They are flexible materials, which are used as substrate materials and conductive ones and provide a reliable operation for both on- & off-body applications18.

The literature provides several designs of wearable device antennas, where a wide variety of materials applicable to the body are used. Certain of these works19,20 do not contain conformal and SAR analysis, which is highly crucial in a wearable application. The wearable and textile antennas21 are most important in terms of compactness, though the antennas with high size22,23 or complicated geometry are also introduced24. The use of a multiple-band antenna renders it suitable for heterogeneous applications in smart IoT devices25; they must possess wide impedance, multiple bands and high or moderate gain26. The antenna deficit found in the literature is of low or negative gain6, narrow band27, or one band working28.

Keeping in mind the requirements and limitations in existing antenna designs, in this study a compact, lightweight, and simplified structured antenna is presented which offers three frequency bands with a moderate value of gain. Below are contributions and features of the proposed antenna:

Proposed antenna geometry

Antenna structural overview

The geometry and architecture of the suggested antenna are provided in Fig. 1. The structure of the proposed antenna contains the microstrip feedline with the triangular patch along with two stubs and slots. The proposed antenna structure is obtained to achieve three broadbands to make it operational for several purposes in wireless communication. The antenna proposed is of the total size of SW × SL = 40 mm × 35 mm. The proposed antenna uses a flexible, lightweight and bendable substrate material, polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), with a relative permittivity of 2.77, a thickness of 3 mm and a loss tangent of 0.0429. The nickel-copper-coated ripstop conductive fabric used in the ground plane and radiative part of the antenna has a conductivity of 5.4 × 104 S/m and a thickness of 0.08 mm.

Figure 1c depicts the equivalent circuit model (ECM) of the proposed triband antenna. The ECM has been developed via a combination of series and parallel RLC components to depict the electromagnetic characteristics of various sections of the antenna structure.

In this circuit model, the inductors (L1–L3) signify the effective current pathways via the radiating stubs and conductive strips. The capacitors (C1–C3) represent the coupling effects caused by slots, gaps, and fringing electric fields between neighbouring metallic parts. The resistors (R1–R3) represent radiation and ohmic losses in the antenna. The circuit component values are retrieved and optimised to closely align with the full-wave simulated antenna response. Designing methodology

The design steps and the effect of design steps on the S-parameter of the antenna are shown in Fig. 2. As seen in the figure, achieving the triple band occurs at the end of four design steps. The rectangular patch antenna in the first phase is designed using equations in30. The rectangular patch is cut off along both sides at the end of the feedline in the second step to form the triangular patch. The current path is altered to allow several resonances as the discussion assumes a triangular pattern instead of a rectangular one. The design step leads to dual frequency, 4.7 GHz, and 7.5 GHz. Two stubs are loaded in the third step at the two corners of the triangular patch, as shown in the figure. The loading of a stub also yields an extra band at 3.6 GHz because it adds the extra reactive paths, forming the third resonance and enhancing resonance but also enhancing the impedance matching of other frequency bands. Two rectangular slots are added in the last design stage, extending the length of the feedline and assisting in improving bandwidth and minor frequency changes. This last step results in the designed structure of the antenna, working at 3.5, 5.15, and 6.6 GHz. Figure 2 describes the design steps and their effects on the reflection coefficient (S11).

Results and discussions

Hardware prototype fabrication and measurement

The hardware model of the triband antenna offered is shown in Fig. 3. As depicted in the figure, the antenna construction has three components: ground plane, substrate, and radiator, where the substrate is constructed of PDMS; the ground plane and radiator are produced by conductive fabric. The fabrication process consists of minimal stages, starting with the production of PDMS material that will be used in the proposed antenna, as described in31. The PDMS is cast by putting the liquid PDMS into a rectangular-shaped mold of the same size as the proposed antenna. The cured PDMS is then put in the oven at 40 °C for approximately 4 h. Then the conductive part and ground plane can be formed by manually cutting the used conductive cloth. The conductive glue is applied to the top layer (radiative part) and bottom layer (ground plane) stuck together by the PDMS layer. Finally, the SMA connector is mounted with conductive glue. The antenna shape will be shown in the figure with the bending checked.

Off body analysis

This section investigates the results and performance measures, i.e. the radiation pattern, gain, and reflection coefficient (S11) of the proposed antenna in a planar form. Figure 4a describes the S-parameter plots of the developed antenna based on simulation, hardware test, and equivalent circuit simulation model. In the mentioned figure, it is evident that the antenna has 3 bands, namely, 3.5, 5.15, and 6.6 GHz, respectively. The bandwidth of these bands is 3.42, 4.67, and 6.19 for the 1st, 2nd and 3rd bands, respectively. The plot indicates the comparison of simulated, circuit-generated, and measured results. This difference between the simulated and experimental results can be explained by the measuring apparatus and the signal attenuation in the connecting wire. Figure 4b presents the gain of the proposed antenna in the areas of operation, in both simulated and measured forms. As shown in the illustration, the proposed antenna has peak gains of 2.65 dBi, 5.3 dBi and 6.05 dBi at 1st, 2nd and 3rd bands of frequencies, respectively. The envisaged and measured results are almost identical, and that is the reason why the proposed antenna layout is ideal for future miniature, multi-purpose electronic devices.

The radiation patterns of the fabricated antenna have been measured and calculated at three resonant frequencies at 3.5 GHz, 5.15 GHz and 6.6 GHz are shown in Fig. 5. The figure shows that the designed antenna has a broadside and consistent radiation pattern on the E and H planes over the three operating frequencies. The radiation distribution pattern displayed indicates the high gain and directivity of the proposed triband antenna. The radiation pattern of the prototype is largely similar to the expected radiation pattern. The proposed antenna would also be a remarkable example in future electronic applications.

The surface current distribution of the proposed antenna is given in Fig. 6 at 3.5 GHz, 5.15 GHz, and 6.6 GHz. The figure expresses the antenna structural components contribute at each frequency. A triangle radiating patch, two L-shaped arms that offer different current flow, and a central feed line make up the antenna. At 3.5 GHz, the current is widely dispersed throughout the whole structure of the antenna, including the feedline, edges of the triangular patch, and two L-shaped arms, which demonstrate the fundamental resonance with the longest effective path. At 5.15 GHz, the L-shaped arms carry less current which indicates a higher-order mode with a shorter route, while the current concentrates more in the slots between feedline and patch as well in feedline region. The last resonance band, 6.6 GHz is mostly contained near the feedline and edge of the left side L-shaped arm stub, which shows a higher-order resonance with a significantly shorter effective path. Overall, the active current region and the effective current path decrease with increasing frequency, which prove that the different parts of the antenna structure are responsible for generating each operating band.

Bending analysis

The bending analysis verifies the conformal usage of the proposed design of the antenna. To this end, the proposed antenna structure is a bent cylindrical one, with radii of 30, 50 & 70 mm. It bents the antenna on both X & Y axis, and the analysis is done in both simulation setups, and hardware modal is also examined to validate the results calculated. The recommended antenna is curved along the X-axis as seen in Fig. 7a and b, which shows the simulated compared to the observed reflection coefficient (S11). The figure accompanying this reveals that the performance of the antenna varies with a decrease in the radius of the cylinder, especially at the higher band. The closer the bending radius, the farther the antenna gets away from an optimum planar shape (i.e., the curvature is less steep). The reason behind this change in the upper band is the change in the antenna segments, which influences mutual coupling between the antenna segments, resulting in the cause of a small mismatch in the impedance and distortion in the upper band and effective electrical length.

(a–b) Simulated and experimental reflection coefficient (S11) of proposed antenna X-axis bending (c–d) simulated and experimental reflection coefficient (S11) of proposed design of antenna Y-axis bending.

Figures 7c–d illustrate the simulated and experimental reflection coefficient (S11) when placed under bending along the Y-axis. The findings indicate that the antenna maintains stable performance when flexed along the Y-axis at various radii (30, 50 & 70 mm). The major change is observed when placing the antenna on a 30 mm cylinder, as the antenna deviates more than its original planar configuration. The differences are observed between measured and simulated outcomes, which are due to the lossy connections, old cable and personal errors. The overall performance of the antenna is considered stable, as it still offers three bands and is resonant over the required band region. Obvious changes are observed while bending the antenna towards the X-axis. These changes in outcomes are obtained as the SMA connector and feedline experience more strain, imperfect contact and parasitic coupling changing over the X-axis bending of the antenna. The mechanical stress and deformation in the substrate may also be the reason behind these changes.

In Fig. 8, the gain of the antenna proposed is also examined as well when the antenna is curled around a cylinder in the X & Y axis with a radius of 30, 50 & 70 mm. The largest change is made when the antenna is placed and curved on the cylinder of 30 mm. When the antenna is curved, its electrical lengths change. The proposed antenna has a gain of over 2 dBi at 1st band with or without the design being curved perpendicularly to the X or Y axis at any radius. The antenna gains are greater than 4.5 dBi at the 2-band and greater than 5.0 dBi at the 3-band in all circumstances. The resultant increment aligns well with the predicted results. This gain number of the proposed antenna in its bent form is good enough to make the work a potential candidate for use in the future as a high-gain electronic device.

The radiation properties of the antenna were investigated at three operating frequencies of 3.5, 5.15 and 6.6 GHz. In Fig. 9, it is possible to see that the E & H-plane radiation patterns do not vary significantly once they are placed on the cylindrical surfaces with bending radii of 30, 50 & 70 mm, regardless of the degree to which the X-axis is bent. The main lobe direction, beamwidth, and cross-polarisation values are very similar to those of the unbent (flat) antenna. This demonstrates that the antenna does not change its constant directional characteristics and radiation efficiency when it is manipulated physically.

The antenna exhibits an insignificant change in radiation patterns, even at the lowest bending radius of 30 mm. On body analysis

In wearable applications, this triband antenna is built on flexible materials with the help of PDMS and conductive fabric. Designing the proposed antenna with such materials adds one more benefit of being lightweight to the wearable devices, as it is more appropriate and demanded. Such attributes of flexibility and being lightweight enabled the recommended triple-band antenna to work in wearables. There is one more critical parameter that is needed to ensure that the proposed design can be used in wearable implementation, which is SAR analysis and on-body gain. To this end, the proposed antenna will be mounted on a model of a human body (on the chest and the wrist), as shown in Fig. 11. The Sim4Life EM tool is used for analyzing Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) and antenna gain on the human body, showing similarities. During the simulation setup, the author tried their best to keep the minimum distance they could for better analysis. Finally, the gap between the antenna and the body was adjusted to 1 mm.

Fig. 11Proposed triple band antenna placements on human body model (a) chest and (b) wrist.

Proposed triple band antenna placements on human body model (a) chest and (b) wrist.

SAR is a critical metric for wearable applications since it measures the quantity of electromagnetic radiation absorbed by the human body. It is also examined to reduce detrimental radiation exposure to the human body. Figure 12 defines the SAR analysis of the presented triband antenna. The illustration shows the antenna placed on the chest and wrist of a human model, considering 1 g & 10 g tissue volumes for SAR analysis. The suggested triband antenna exhibits SAR analysis values of 1.14 W/kg, 0.4 W/kg, and 0.17 W/kg at 1st, 2nd and 3rd bands, respectively, when positioned on 1 g of tissue quantity. The antenna positioned on the chest with 10 g of tissue yields SAR values of 0.46 W/kg at 1st band, 0.14 W/kg at 2nd band & 0.0 bands. kg at 3rd band. Likewise, when the suggested antenna is positioned on the human body model’s wrist, the SAR analysis is conducted on 1 g & 10 g tissues. The figure indicates that when the antenna is positioned on the wrist, the SAR values are 1.36 W/kg, 0.7 W/kg, and 0.59 W/kg at 1st, 2nd and 3rd band respectively, measured across 1 g of tissue. The utilization of 10 g of tissue yields SAR values of 0.52 W/kg at 1st band, 0.30 W/kg at 2 bands, 2nd band, and 0.21 W/kg at 3rd band. The values derived from the proposed antenna fall within the permitted limits set by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) and the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE). The FCC and IEEE establish the standard specific absorption rates (SAR) of 1.6 W/kg & 2 W/kg for 1 g & 10 g, respectively32. The suggested antenna has SAR values far below appropriate thresholds, making the design appropriate for wearable applications.

The suggested antenna exhibits gain values of 1.52, 5.26, and 4.96 dBi at 1st, 2nd and 3rd band respectively, when positioned at the wrist of the body model. Comparison with literature

Size, bandwidth, gain and SAR outcomes of the recommended triple-band antenna are evaluated as compared with recently published literature work. The materials used for the substrate and radiative part are also compared. The comparative data is gathered and provided in Table 2. It was noted from the provided data that this antenna gives a compact size and offers high gain and low SAR values. Although20,25,26,29,33 have smaller antennas compared to the proposed work, these works either offer one band or are limited to applications (no SAR or conformal analysis). The work presented in20 operates at a single band, and there are no SAR and conformal analyses. On the other hand25,26, operate at a single band and also25 offer a very low gain of -0.35 dBi. The work presented in29 operates at triple band, but there is no SAR analysis, and the operational bandwidth is narrow as compared to the proposed work. The other listed work either operates with a single band19,21–24 or does not analyse the SAR of the antenna6–28. The work presented in34,35 offers triband capabilities and provides flexibility. In particular35, achieves high gain due to the placement of the metamaterial, which provides flexibility; especially35, offers high gain due to the placement of the metamaterial layer. The setbacks of these works are either narrow band or large-sized. -size. This contrast with literature depicts the advantages of the antenna and verifies that this triple-band antenna is compact with multiband operation, high gain and low SAR value, which makes it suitable for future wearable electronic devices.

Conclusion

A compact, low-profile, lightweight and flexible antenna operating over a triple band is proposed in this paper for wearable purposes. The antenna presented in this paper has a simplified shape, which is a triangular-shaped patch along with two stubs and slots. The stubs and slots are loaded to the triangular patch antenna to obtain multiple frequency bands along with the improvement in the impedance matching. For wearable applications, the flexible electronics devices are needed, which require a flexible antenna, and for this purpose, the flexible substrate material PDMS and conductive fabric for the radiating patch and ground plane are utilized. The proposed triband antenna operates at frequencies of 3.5, 6.6, and 5.15 GHz, exhibiting gains of 2.65, 6.05, and 5.3 dBi, respectively. The bending study of the proposed antenna is conducted alongside SAR analysis and on-body gain to validate the antenna for wearable applications. In the bending analysis, the proposed antenna is curved along the X & Y-axis on cylinders having radii of 30, 50, & 70 mm. The performance of the suggested triband antenna is steady, demonstrating a strong correlation between measured, simulated, and bending data for S11, gain, and radiation pattern. Furthermore, the SAR analysis and body gain of the proposed antenna are examined by positioning the antenna on a human body phantom (chest and wrist). The performance metrics of the proposed triband antenna are compared with current publications. The findings, analyses, and comparisons with existing literature render the suggested triband antenna appropriate for forthcoming wearable electronic devices used in IoT applications.
