When identical particles cease to be indistinguishable: violation of statistics in quantum spacetime
Nicola Bortolotti, Catalina Curceanu, Antonino Marciano, Kristian Piscicchia

Abstract
Quantum gravity may modify the fundamental symmetries that govern identical particles. In particular, noncommutative spacetime frameworks predict deformations of Bose and Fermi statistics. Here we develop a relativistic quantum field theory based on the most general oscillator algebra compatible with θ \theta -deformed Poincaré symmetry. This construction generalizes twisted statistics to a class of quon-like deformations allowing non-involutive particle exchange. We show that the resulting theory is consistent at both the free and interacting levels and derive its implications for atomic systems. Purely twisted statistics predicts Pauli-forbidden atomic transitions at rates incompatible with experiments. By contrast, a class of quon deformations suppresses such processes by powers of the noncommutativity scale, but only if superselection rules between permutation-symmetry sectors are violated. This implies an effective breakdown of particle indistinguishability and provides theoretical motivation for high-precision experimental tests of the Pauli exclusion principle.



keywords
Quantum gravity phenomenology, Pauli exclusion principle, noncommutative spacetimes.

The indistinguishability of identical particles is one of the most profound principles of quantum physics. It underlies Bose-Einstein and Fermi-Dirac statistics and gives rise to phenomena ranging from atomic structure to superconductivity. More generally, quantum states transform under irreducible representations of the symmetric group, not necessarily restricted to the one-dimensional totally symmetric or antisymmetric ones, with superselection rules (SSRs) forbidding transitions between sectors of different permutation symmetry
Messiah and
Greenberg 1964
.

The Pauli exclusion principle (PEP) emerges naturally in local quantum field theory (QFT) as a consequence of the spin-statistics theorem. The connection between spin and statistics is rooted in the structure of the net of local algebras
Haag and Kastler 1964
and follows directly from locality (i.e. local commutativity of observables) and Poincaré covariance
Guido and Longo 1995
. However, these assumptions may be modified in quantum gravity (QG), where spacetime itself may acquire a non-classical structure.

Consistent quantum theories can nevertheless be constructed in which violations of locality and statistics are confined below arbitrarily small scales. This possibility is exemplified by the quon model
Greenberg 1991
, which interpolates between Bose, Fermi, infinite and anyonic statistics
Meljanac and
Perica 1994
via a deformation of canonical commutation relations. The model preserves indistinguishability, SSRs, cluster decomposition, and CPT symmetry. However, the loss of locality obscures the status of relativistic invariance, in particular of the
S
-matrix, restricting the framework for many years to the non-relativistic regime. A weakened notion of locality for infinite statistics fields
Greenberg 1990
was subsequently shown to ensure Lorentz invariance of physical scattering amplitudes
Cao et al. 2009
;
Cao et al. 2010
. Contrary to earlier concerns
Chow and Greenberg 2001
, the resulting framework admits consistent interactions involving infinite statistics particles, thereby extending the viability of the quon model to the relativistic domain.

The existence of nonlocal QFTs with modified statistics is particularly significant in QG, where sharp notions of causality fail, potentially challenging the conventional spin-statistics connection. Frameworks such as the quon model may therefore capture aspects of the low-energy limit of QG.

On the other hand, it is widely expected that spacetime acquires a noncommutative structure at high energies, preventing arbitrarily precise localization of events
Doplicher
et al. 1994
. A concrete realization arises for open strings ending on D-branes in a background
B
-field

x^{\mu}\star x^{\nu}-x^{\nu}\star x^{\mu}=i\theta^{\mu\nu}

\theta^{\mu\nu}=c^{\mu\nu}/\Lambda_{\theta}^{2}

;
Moyal 1949

Early field-theoretic constructions on Moyal space

faced both experimental and theoretical problems, since they explicitly broke Lorentz covariance and renormalizability was obstructed by UV-IR mixing effects in non-planar diagrams
Minwalla
et al. 2000

This enables “closed-system” experimental configurations, in contrast to the traditional “open-system” approach of Ramberg and Snow

Our framework provides a relativistic generalization of the quon model and, for the first time, a physical realization linking it to QG scenarios. It establishes a consistent setting for the quantitative investigation of noncommutative QG models and provides theoretical support for closed-system experimental searches for PEP violation.

Results

Twisted quon statistics

Unlike the twist element
f_{\theta}
, the function
\eta(p,q)
cannot be determined solely from symmetry considerations. Nevertheless, it is constrained by a number of physical consistency requirements.

First, the algebra (
2
) induces a positive-definite inner product on multiparticle Hilbert spaces only if
\eta(p,q)
is real and bounded

-1\leq\eta(p,q)\leq 1,

(3)

implying a quon-like deformation
Greenberg 1991
;
Meljanac and
Perica 1994
. Indeed, Lorentz-invariant combinations of the four-momenta
p^{\mu}
and
q^{\mu}
can only be symmetric, so
\eta(p,q)
must be symmetric under particle exchange. Any symmetric phase would render the norms of physical states complex (see Methods, Eq. (
32
)), requiring
\eta
to be real. It follows that the exchange function

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)\coloneqq\eta(p,q)f_{\theta}^{-2}(p,q),

(4)

is Hermitian,
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}(p,q)=\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(q,p)
, and the bound (
3
) then follows from the theorem proved by Bożejko et al. in
Bożejko et al. 2017
.

Second, at energies much lower than the noncommutativity scale
\Lambda_{\theta}
, which is assumed to be of order the Planck scale, experiments constrain the quon deformation for all known particles to be close to the standard bosonic or fermionic values
\eta=\pm 1
, which must be recovered in the commutative limit
\Lambda_{\theta}\to\infty
. Assuming analyticity in the deformation, we can expand
\eta
in powers of the dimensionless ratio
\sigma(p,q)/\Lambda_{\theta}
, where
\sigma(p,q)
is a scalar function with dimensions of energy. Up to first order

\eta(p,q)\sim\pm\left[1-\left(\frac{\sigma(p,q)}{\Lambda_{\theta}}\right)^{\kappa}\right],\quad\kappa>0.

(5)

Different values of
\kappa
have distinct implications for the low-energy behavior of the theory. A quon deformation, for example, induces non-vanishing field commutators at space-like separations of order
\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\theta}^{-\kappa})
. In contrast, the contribution of the twist to this violation scales as
\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\theta}^{-2})

Balachandran
et al. 2007
.

Crucially, when
|\eta|<1
, no algebraic relation exists between
a(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{q})
and
a(\tilde{q})a(\tilde{p})
, or between
a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q})
and
a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})
. While this may appear to hinder the construction of a consistent formalism, no such relations are required to define the Fock space or to compute matrix elements of physical observables. However, this absence has profound implications: two monomials of equal degree in the creation operators that differ only by a permutation of distinct labels must be treated as independent. In the following, we will discuss how this feature affects the construction of multiparticle states.

For
n\geq 2

n

\mathscr{H}^{n}=\mathscr{H}^{\otimes n}

\mathscr{H}

\ket{\psi}\in\mathscr{H}^{n}

S_{n}

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{\gamma}c_{\gamma}\ket{\psi_{\gamma}}.

(6)

In general, this decomposition is not unique. However, the presence of
\theta
-deformed Poincaré symmetry naturally selects a twisted action of
S_{n}
on
\mathscr{H}^{n}
, in which the projection operators
\mathbb{P}_{\gamma_{\theta}}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}}
are constructed by deforming permutations with the twist element
f_{\theta}

\gamma_{\theta}
to distinguish them from the untwisted ones.

The associated symmetrized states obey twisted statistics, encompassing the twisted bosons and fermions introduced in
Balachandran
et al. 2006
as well as higher-dimensional mixed representations, and realizing the generalized statistics studied in
Liguori and Mintchev 1995
. For example, the amplitudes of twisted symmetric and antisymmetric two-particle states satisfy

\displaystyle\psi_{s_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=f^{2}_{\theta}(q,p)\psi_{s_{\theta}}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p}),

(7a)

\displaystyle\psi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=-f^{2}_{\theta}(q,p)\psi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p}).

(7b)

Each space
\mathscr{H}^{n}
inherits from the
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
algebra a deformed inner product. In the two-particle sector, it takes the explicit form

\braket{\psi|\phi}=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\left[\phi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(q,p)\phi(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\right],

(8)

where
\int_{\tilde{p}}=\int d\boldsymbol{p}\sum_{\rho}

However, unlike in quon or parastatistics frameworks, untwisted symmetry sectors are not superselected, therefore transitions between states of different untwisted symmetry are in general allowed. The breakdown of these conventional SSRs originates entirely from the twist element
f_{\theta}(p,q)
—specifically from its antisymmetric component

When twisted symmetry is imposed, orthogonality between distinct symmetry sectors is restored, and the full Hilbert space decomposes as a direct sum of the subspaces
\mathscr{H}^{n}_{\gamma_{\theta}}=\mathbb{P}_{\gamma_{\theta}}\mathscr{H}^{n}

6

(10)

where
\ket{\psi^{\text{n}}_{\gamma_{\theta}}}=\ket{\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}}/\|\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}\|

|c_{\gamma_{\theta}}^{\text{n}}|^{2}=\frac{\|\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}\|^{2}}{\sum_{\gamma_{\theta}^{\prime}}\|\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}^{\prime}}\|^{2}},\qquad\sum_{\gamma_{\theta}}|c_{\gamma_{\theta}}^{\text{n}}|^{2}=1.

(11)

This structure has important physical implications. Although the amplitudes

Eq. (

with twisted permutation symmetry, weighted by probabilities

Quantum field theory

The existence of quantization schemes extending beyond Bose and Fermi statistics is well established
Green 1953
. Expanding a given field operator in terms of particle and antiparticle creation and annihilation operators (in the interacting case, within the interaction picture), the equation of motion
i\partial_{t}\Psi(x)=[\Psi(x),H]
implies

\begin{split}&[a(\tilde{p}),H]=E_{p}a(\tilde{p}),\qquad[a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p}),H]=-E_{p}a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p}),\\
&[b(\tilde{p}),H]=E_{p}b(\tilde{p}),\qquad\,[b^{\dagger}(\tilde{p}),H]=-E_{p}b^{\dagger}(\tilde{p}),\end{split}

(12)

where
E_{p}=\sqrt{m^{2}+p^{2}}
and
m
are the energy and mass of the particles and antiparticles created by
\Psi
. A central consequence of Eq. (
12
) is the additivity of energy, so that each particle contributes equally and linearly to total energy.

Imposing a quadratic Hamiltonian in the creation and annihilation operators yields parastatistics of finite order
Green 1953
, including Bose and Fermi statistics as special cases. In the present construction, however, the mode expansion of the Hamiltonian must be derived consistently from the
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
algebra itself. Specifically, one begins by expressing the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
12
) in terms of the
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
field and subsequently expanding it in creation and annihilation operators. For purely twisted fields (
|\eta|=1
), the standard bilinear form of the Hamiltonian is preserved,
H=\int_{\tilde{p}}E_{p}[a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{p})+b^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})b(\tilde{p})]
. By contrast, quon deformations (
|\eta|<1
) require a modification of the Hamiltonian operator due to the failure of commutativity between the creation and annihilation operators among themselves. The resulting operator is of infinite degree, admitting an infinite expansion in the creation and annihilation operators. Up to two particle states, the number operator takes the form

N(\tilde{p})=a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{p})+\int_{\tilde{q}}\frac{[a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q}),a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}}\left[a(\tilde{p}),a(\tilde{q})\right]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}}}{1-\eta^{2}(p,q)}+...,

(13)

where the higher-orders contribute only to matrix elements involving three or more particles and the
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
-mutator is defined as
[a(\tilde{p}),a(\tilde{q})]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}}\coloneqq a(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{q})-\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)a(\tilde{q})a(\tilde{p})
. Then
H=\int_{\tilde{p}}E_{p}[N(\tilde{p})+N^{c}(\tilde{p})]
, where an analogous expression holds for antiparticle. Eq. (
13

Meljanac and
Perica 1994
;
Stanciu 1992

12

Propagators retain their standard form. This holds even nonperturbatively, since the derivation of the Källén-Lehmann spectral representation relies only on one-particle states and the algebraic sector of the Poincaré group.

Finally, all correlation functions are invariant under the

We next incorporate interactions. For the purpose of deriving PEP-violating transition amplitudes, which is the primary phenomenological objective of this work, the specific noncommutative extension of gauge symmetries is not essential. Corrections to standard amplitudes arise from both deformations of statistics and interactions, as well as their interplay. However, because PEP-forbidden processes necessarily involve modified statistics, interaction corrections do not contribute at lowest order. Nevertheless, few aspects must be addressed when a quon deformation is included.

Restricting to QED, both photon and electron creation and annihilation operators obey
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
-deformed algebras, characterized respectively by quon deformations
\eta_{\gamma}
and
\eta_{e}
, as well as mixed photon-electron exchange relations with deformation
\eta_{\gamma e}
. The free Hamiltonians
H_{\gamma}
and
H_{e}
satisfy the consistency conditions (
12

\Psi_{I}

H_{I}^{\theta}(\tau)=e^{i(H_{\gamma}+H_{e})\tau}\,H_{int}^{\theta}\,e^{-i(H_{\gamma}+H_{e})\tau}.

(15)

For
|\eta|<1

does not commute with

15

As a result, even at lowest order in the electromagnetic coupling, the interacting theory contains infinitely many terms. This is an unavoidable consequence of implementing quon deformations. As demonstrated in
Cao et al. 2009

S

Atomic bound states

QFT provides the appropriate relativistic framework to describe bound states in terms of Green functions, which encode the propagation of multiparticle systems even when they do not asymptotically approach free states. The Dirac (or Breit) equation generalizes to the Bethe-Salpeter (BS) equation
Salpeter and Bethe 1951
;
Salpeter 1952
;
Nakanishi 1969
, which incorporates the full relativistic interaction among the constituents.

For simplicity, we focus on the helium atom, the simplest system sensitive to violations of fermionic statistics. In the commutative theory, the relativistic “wave function” is described by the BS amplitude

\phi_{BS}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{n};P)=\bra{\Omega}T\{\Psi_{e}(x_{1})\Psi_{e}(x_{2})\Psi_{n}(x_{n})\}\ket{\phi;P},

(16)

where
\ket{\phi;P}
denotes the bound state with total four-momentum
P^{\mu}
, other quantum numbers are not explicitly written. Here
\Psi_{e}
and
\Psi_{n}
are the Heisenberg electron and nucleus fields, and
\ket{\Omega}
is the vacuum. By inserting a complete set of states into the associated six-point Green function
G_{0}^{(6)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{n};x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{n}^{\prime})
and isolating the bound state contribution, one finds

G_{0}(\{p_{i}\},\{p_{i}^{\prime}\})=\frac{i\phi_{BS}(\{p_{i}\})\phi_{BS}^{\dagger}(\{p_{i}^{\prime}\})}{2E_{P}[P^{0}-E_{P}+i\epsilon]},

(17)

We now present the results for amplitudes and rates of PEP-violating transitions under the assumption that SSRs hold. In this case, physical states belong to superselected sectors with twisted permutation symmetry. Therefore, we focus on processes of the type

For helium-like atoms, the transition matrix element reads

Unlike standard quon model, which forbids PEP-violating transitions in the antisymmetric sector, such transitions may occur in all twisted sectors. Therefore, PEP-violating transitions remain unsuppressed even with a quon deformation.

Pauli violation for distinguishable particles

We now consider the alternative scenario in which atomic states lack a definite permutation symmetry. In this case the two-particle amplitudes
\psi(p,q)=\psi_{s}(p,q)+\psi_{a}(p,q)
and
\psi(q,p)=\psi_{s}(p,q)-\psi_{a}(p,q)
generally correspond to two independent states,
\ket{\psi^{+}}
and
\ket{\psi^{-}}
. Transitions are not confined to fixed symmetry sectors, implying the absence of SSRs. The corresponding density operators
\rho^{\pm}=\ket{\psi^{\pm}}\bra{\psi^{\pm}}
are no longer equivalent, and interference between different sectors contributes to measurement outcomes through expectation values
\text{Tr}(\rho^{\pm}O)
.

PEP-forbidden transitions in helium-like atoms are then described by

\ket{\psi^{\pm}}\to\ket{\psi^{\prime}_{s}}\ket{\boldsymbol{k}\lambda},

(26)

where the final electronic state has vanishing antisymmetric component. Both the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of the initial states
\ket{\psi^{\pm}}
contribute to the transition amplitude, which decomposes as
H_{if}^{\pm}=H_{if}^{s}\pm H_{if}^{a}
, with

\begin{split}H_{if}^{\gamma}=\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}}\int d\boldsymbol{p}d\boldsymbol{q}D_{\theta}^{\gamma}\psi_{s}^{\prime\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{*}_{\lambda}\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q}),\end{split}

(27)

where

\begin{split}&D_{\theta}^{s}=\frac{[1+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)][1+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}(p-k,q)]+1-\eta^{2}(p-k,q)}{\|{\psi^{\prime}_{s}}\|\|{\psi}\|},\\
&D_{\theta}^{a}=\frac{[1-\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)][1+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}(p-k,q)]-1+\eta^{2}(p-k,q)}{\|{\psi^{\prime}_{s}}\|\|{\psi}\|}.\end{split}

(28)

The explicit form of the quon deformation
\eta

\eta
can be expanded as in Eq. (

p^{\mu}
and

reduce to either

or
|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}|
, allowing the function

to be parametrized as

\sigma_{\text{nr}}(p,q)=m^{1-a}|\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{q}|^{a},\quad a\in\mathbb{R}.

(29)

Any multiplicative coefficient can be absorbed into a redefinition of the noncommutativity scale and the coefficients
c^{\mu\nu}

The PEP-violating transition rates then read

\frac{d\Gamma_{\text{PV}}^{\pm}}{d\Omega}=\delta^{\pm}_{\kappa}\left(\frac{E}{\Lambda_{\theta}}\right)\frac{d\Gamma_{0}}{d\Omega},

(30)

where

For radiative capture processes involving a free electron, the photon angular distribution exhibits a characteristic dependence on the background-field components
c^{ij}

For quon deformations with

\delta^{\pm}_{\kappa}\sim\Lambda_{\theta}^{-\kappa}

Discussion

Noncommutative spacetimes possess quantum symmetries that profoundly shape the structure of multiparticle states. In this context, ordinary Bose and Fermi statistics are incompatible with deformed relativistic symmetries. To preserve covariance, they must be replaced by deformed statistics satisfying
\tau_{\theta}\Delta_{\theta}=\Delta_{\theta}\tau_{\theta}
, which ensures consistency between the transposition map
\tau_{\theta}
and the twisted coproduct
\Delta_{\theta}
of the Poincaré Hopf algebra. Crucially, this condition fixes statistics only up to a Lorentz-invariant multiplicative function of momenta,
\eta
. To our knowledge, this freedom has not previously been explored. However, it allows statistical structures beyond the commonly studied twisted Bose and Fermi cases, with significant theoretical and phenomenological implications.

Covariant approaches based solely on twisted statistics fail to improve the UV behavior of QFT
Oeckl 2000
;
Balachandran
et al. 2007
;
Bogdanović
et al. 2024
;
Ćirić et al. 2023
;
Ćirić et al. 2024
, while noncommutative gauge theories with ordinary Bose and Fermi statistics are non-renormalizable. By contrast, strict braidings—where a double particle exchange does not restore the original configuration—can regularize QFT
Oeckl 2001
. Including quon deformations in the oscillator algebra therefore offers a promising framework for UV-complete theories on Moyal space and motivates exploration of their physical consequences.

In particular, we have investigated the implications for the PEP in atomic systems. Our analysis shows that any
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
-deformation modifies the permutation symmetry of atomic electrons and allows the existence of PEP-forbidden atomic states. For the case of pure twisted statistics, PEP-violating transitions occur at rates comparable to allowed transitions, far exceeding current experimental bounds.

By contrast, a nontrivial quon deformation may be compatible with experimental data, but only at the price of violating SSRs. This entails a breakdown of indistinguishability for identical particles which manifests at higher orders in the noncommutative deformation, whereas at leading order the theory preserves ordinary statistics. A similar conclusion has been found in the Lie-algebraic noncommutative spacetime
\kappa
-Minkowski, both in the time-like
Arzano and
Kowalski-Glikman 2023
and light-like
Fabiano and Mercati 2024
cases. However, these scenarios are generally expected to induce excessively large violations of the PEP, in strong disagreement with experimental constraints
Addazi et al. 2018
;
Piscicchia
et al. 2023b
. For a restricted class of quon deformations with low-energy expansion characterized by exponents
0<\kappa<4
, PEP-violating transitions become suppressed by the noncommutativity scale.

These results provide a theoretical framework linking noncommutative quantum gravity models with high-precision atomic tests of the Pauli exclusion principle, such as those performed by the VIP and VIP-2 experiments
Piscicchia
et al. 2022
;
Piscicchia
et al. 2023a
;
Piscicchia
et al. 2023b
;
Porcelli et al. 2025
;
Porcelli et al. 2024
;
Manti et al. 2024
.

Derivation of the rates with SSRs

Using the complete basis of free electron states, atomic states can be written as

\ket{\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}}=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\tilde{p}_{1},...,\tilde{p}_{n}}\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}(\tilde{p}_{1},...,\tilde{p}_{n})\ket{\tilde{p}_{1},...,\tilde{p}_{n}},

(31)

where the amplitudes
\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}(\tilde{p}_{1},...,\tilde{p}_{n})
are the projection of the
4^{n}
-component spinors
(\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}})_{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}}(\boldsymbol{p}_{1},...,\boldsymbol{p}_{n})
onto free Dirac spinors
u_{\alpha}
. For example, in the presence of a quon deformation, a two-electron atom admits both twisted symmetric and antisymmetric states, with norms

\begin{split}&\left\lVert\psi_{s_{\theta}}\right\rVert^{2}=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\left(1+\eta(p,q)\right)|\psi_{s_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})|^{2},\\
&\left\lVert\psi_{a_{\theta}}\right\rVert^{2}=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\left(1-\eta(p,q)\right)|\psi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})|^{2},\end{split}

(32)

which reduce to a purely twisted antisymmetric atom for
\eta\to-1
.

Assuming
\eta=-1
, the transition matrix element is
H_{if}=\bra{\psi^{\prime}_{a_{\theta}},\boldsymbol{k}\lambda}H_{I}^{\theta}(0)\ket{\psi_{a_{\theta}}}
, where
H_{I}^{\theta}(t)
is the time-dependent perturbation inducing the transition. For helium-like atoms

H_{if}=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q},\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{q}^{\prime}}\int d\boldsymbol{x}\,\frac{\psi^{\prime*}_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{q}^{\prime})\braket{\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{q}^{\prime},\tilde{k}|\mathcal{H}_{I}^{\theta}|\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})}{4\left\lVert\psi_{a_{\theta}}^{\prime}\right\rVert\left\lVert\psi_{a_{\theta}}\right\rVert},

(33)

where the twisted amplitudes are constructed from the full Hilbert space amplitudes
\psi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
as

\displaystyle\psi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=\frac{1}{2}\left[\psi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})-f_{\theta}^{-2}(p,q)\psi(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\right] ,

(34)

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=\frac{1}{2}\psi_{s}^{\prime}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})(1-f^{-2}_{\theta}(p,q)),

(35)

with
\psi_{s}^{\prime}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
symmetric, as it describes two electrons in the same state.

In Coulomb gauge (
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\boldsymbol{A}=0
)

\begin{split}\int d\boldsymbol{x}\braket{\tilde{k},\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{q}^{\prime}|\mathcal{H}_{I}^{\theta}(\boldsymbol{x})|\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}=e\int d\mu\sum_{\text{pol}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{l}^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{l})\\
\times\frac{F_{\theta}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{t}}}\bar{u}(\tilde{l}^{\prime})\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{*}_{\tilde{k}}u(\tilde{l})\braket{\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{q}^{\prime},\tilde{k}|\alpha^{\dagger}(\tilde{k}^{\prime})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{l}^{\prime})a(\tilde{l})|\tilde{p},\tilde{q}},\end{split}

(36)

where
d\mu=(d\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}/\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{k^{\prime}}})(d\boldsymbol{l}/\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{l}})(d\boldsymbol{l}^{\prime}/\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{l^{\prime}}})
, the sum runs over electron spins and photon polarizations and
\alpha^{\dagger}
is the photon creation operator. Here,
F_{\theta}
accounts for noncommutative corrections to the QED interaction, eventually arising from the covariant derivative as well as from permutations of the creation and annihilation operators. Eq. (
36
) can be worked out using the twisted oscillator algebra

\begin{split}&a(\tilde{p})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q})-f_{\theta}^{-2}(p,q)a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q})a(\tilde{p})=\delta(\tilde{p},\tilde{q}),\\
&a(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{q})-f_{\theta}^{2}(p,q)a(\tilde{q})a(\tilde{p})=0,\\
&a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q})-f_{\theta}^{2}(p,q)a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})=0.\end{split}

(37)

Inserting the resulting expression into Eq. (
33
) yields

H_{if}=\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}}\int_{p,q}\frac{F_{\theta}(k,p,q)\psi^{\prime\dagger}_{a_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{*}_{\lambda}\psi_{a_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})}{\left\lVert\psi_{a_{\theta}}^{\prime}\right\rVert\left\lVert\psi_{a_{\theta}}\right\rVert},

(38)

with
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{*}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{*}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathbb{I}
. When both initial and final states have commutative limit given by standard atomic states
\psi_{a}^{(0)}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})
and
\psi_{a}^{\prime(0)}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})
, the matrix element (
38

24

The norm of the PEP-violating state is computed from
\braket{\psi_{a_{\theta}}^{\prime}|\psi_{a_{\theta}}^{\prime}}
using the expansion (
31
) and the algebra (
37
)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\psi_{a_{\theta}}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}|\psi^{\prime}_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})|^{2}\\
&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}|\psi^{\prime}_{s}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})|^{2}|1-f_{\theta}^{-2}(p,q)|^{2}\\
&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}|\psi^{\prime}_{s}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})|^{2}\left[\sin^{2}(p\wedge_{\theta}q)+2\sin^{4}\left(\frac{p\wedge_{\theta}q}{2}\right)\right]\\

(39)

which scales linearly with the deformation parameter:
\left\lVert\psi^{\prime}_{a_{\theta}}\right\rVert\sim\mathcal{O}(\theta)

Derivation of the rate without SSRs

Let
\psi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})=\psi(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)\psi(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{p})
denote the (time-independent) electronic component of a solution to the atomic Salpeter equation, satisfying
H\psi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}}=E\psi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}}
. In abstract notation, this corresponds to
\ket{\psi}=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\ket{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}
with
H\ket{\psi}=E\ket{\psi}
, as follows from
\bra{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}H\ket{\psi}=E\braket{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}|\psi}
and
\psi_{\alpha\beta}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})=\sum_{sr}u_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p}s)u_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{q}r)\psi(\boldsymbol{p}s,\boldsymbol{q}r)
.

The matrix elements
H_{if}=\bra{\psi^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\lambda}H_{I}^{\theta}(0)\ket{\psi^{\pm}}
are thus evaluated using initial amplitudes
\psi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
and
\psi(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})

\psi^{\prime}_{s}(\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{q}^{\prime})
. The interaction Hamiltonian
H_{I}^{\theta}=\exp[i(H_{\gamma}+H_{e})]H_{int}^{\theta}\exp[-i(H_{\gamma}+H_{e})]
is of infinite degree. In undeformed QED, the leading contribution to atomic transitions is built from the transition operator
N(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{q})
, which annihilates an electron in state
\tilde{q}
and creates one in state
\tilde{p}
. In the present framework, this structure generalizes to an infinite-degree operator defined by
[N(\tilde{p},\tilde{q}),a^{\dagger}(\tilde{r})]=\delta(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})

a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{p})
with
a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{q})
in the number operator
Greenberg 1992
. Indeed, since
H_{int}^{\theta}
is linear in the photon creation operator and we consider processes with a single photon in the final state, the free photon Hamiltonian
H_{\gamma}
does not contribute to
H_{I}^{\theta}
. Likewise, only the electron part of
H_{e}
contributes, as the states involve no positrons. Consequently, the interaction reduces to an infinite series involving only electron operators. Moreover, while the explicit form of
H_{int}^{\theta}
depends on the noncommutative extension of QED, to leading order PEP-forbidden amplitudes are controlled entirely by the modified statistics, with deformations of the covariant derivative contributing only at higher order. Accordingly, the matrix elements
\bra{\psi^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\lambda}H_{I}^{\theta}(0)\ket{\psi^{\pm}}
can be computed from the interaction Hamiltonian density

\mathcal{H}_{I}^{\theta}=\int d\mu\sum_{\text{pol}}e^{-i(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{p})\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\bar{u}(\tilde{p}^{\prime})\gamma^{\mu}\varepsilon_{\mu}u(\tilde{p})\alpha^{\dagger}(\tilde{k})N(\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{p}),

(40)

where
d\mu=(d\boldsymbol{k}/\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{k}})(d\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}/\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{p^{\prime}}})(d\boldsymbol{p}/\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{p}})
and the sum runs over electron spins and photon polarizations. The transition operator between two-electron states takes the form

N(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{q})+\int_{\tilde{r}}\frac{[a^{\dagger}(\tilde{r}),a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}}\left[a(\tilde{q}),a(\tilde{r})\right]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}}}{1-\eta^{2}(p,r)}.

(41)

Although the commutative limit is not manifest in this expression, all matrix elements reduce to their standard values as
\theta\to 0
. The evaluation of
\braket{\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{q}^{\prime},\tilde{k}|\mathcal{H}_{I}^{\theta}|\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}
follows directly from the
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
algebra. Using the permutation symmetry of the initial and final amplitudes then leads to Eq. (
28

Covariance on the Moyal space

Here, we recall the implementation of covariance on Moyal space, which will provide the starting point for our analysis of deformed statistics in the subsequent Sections. The existence of the twist element

The isometry group of the Moyal space is the quantum group
Majid 1995
defined by the so-called
\theta
-Poincaré algebra
Chaichian et al. 2004
. This preserves the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{p}
while allowing a nontrivial coproduct structure
\Delta_{\theta}:U(\mathfrak{p})\mapsto U(\mathfrak{p})\otimes U(\mathfrak{p})
which deforms the universal enveloping of the Poincaré algebra
U(\mathfrak{p})

[x^{\mu},x^{\nu}]=i\theta^{\mu\nu}
invariant in all frames. This can be done by introducing the twist element

\mathcal{F}_{\theta}=\exp\left(-\frac{i}{2}\theta^{\mu\nu}P_{\mu}\otimes P_{\nu}\right)\in U(\mathfrak{p})\otimes U(\mathfrak{p}),

(42)

which deforms the primitive coproduct
\Delta

\Delta_{\theta}(Y)=\mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{-1}\Delta(Y)\mathcal{F}_{\theta}.

(43)

Now, let
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{1,d})
be the algebra of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{1,d}

m(\Phi\otimes\Psi)(x)=\Phi(x)\Psi(x)

\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{1,d})
. Later will specialize to 1+3 dimensions. The action of the twisted Poincaré group is not an automorphism of
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{1,d})
. One is then forced to deform the multiplication map in order to satisfy the compatibility condition

m_{\theta}(\Delta_{\theta}(\Lambda)\rhd\Phi\otimes\Psi)=\Lambda\rhd m_{\theta}(\Phi\otimes\Psi),

(44)

where
\Lambda
is a suitable representation of a Lorentz transformation. Eq. (
44
) means that applying a boost to
\Phi\otimes\Psi
and then multiplying the boosted fields is the same as applying the boost to the fields after having multiplied them. It is easy to see that the following noncommutative multiplication map

m_{\theta}(\Phi\otimes\Psi)(x)\coloneqq\Phi(x)\star\Psi(x)=\Phi(x)\exp\left(-\frac{i}{2}\theta^{\mu\nu}\overleftarrow{\partial_{\mu}}\,\overrightarrow{\partial}_{\nu}\right)\Psi(x),

(45)

called the

-product

44

Note that the operation of deforming
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{1,d})
into the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{1,d})
equipped with the noncommutative multiplication map (
45

\star
-product to the coordinate functions yields the canonical commutation relations

x^{\mu}\star x^{\nu}-x^{\nu}\star x^{\mu}=i\theta^{\mu\nu}.

(46)

This is analogous to the Groenewold-Moyal phase space formulation of QM
Groenewold 1946
;
Moyal 1949
where, unlike the approach to phase space quantization
\{x,p\}=1\to[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i\hbar
, phase space retains a smooth structure and the algebra of the observable is deformed into a noncommutative algebra equipped by a
\star
-product. The two formulations are equivalent and connected by the Wigner-Weyl transform.

Let us consider the action of twisted Lorentz transformations on tensor products of real scalar free fields (the argument extends to arbitrary tensor fields). This action is immediately given by the coproduct (
43
)

\begin{split}\Delta_{\theta}(\Lambda)\,\Phi\otimes\Psi&=\int d\mu(p)d\mu(q)\,\Phi(p)\Psi(q)\,\Delta_{\theta}(\Lambda)e_{p}\otimes e_{q}\\
&=\int d\mu(p)d\mu(q)\,\Phi(p)\Psi(q)\,f_{\theta}^{-1}(\Lambda p,\Lambda q)f_{\theta}(p,q)e_{\Lambda p}\otimes e_{\Lambda q}\\
&=\int d\mu(p)d\mu(q)\,\Phi(\Lambda^{-1}p)\Psi(\Lambda^{-1}q)\,f_{\theta}(\Lambda^{-1}p,\Lambda^{-1}q)f^{-1}_{\theta}(p,q)e_{p}\otimes e_{q},\end{split}

(47)

where
d\mu(p)
is a Lorentz-invariant measure,

e_{p}(x)=e^{-ip\cdot x}\in\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{1,d}),

(48)

and
f_{\theta}
is defined by

f_{\theta}(p,q)=\exp\left(-\frac{i}{2}p\wedge_{\theta}q\right),\quad p\wedge_{\theta}q\coloneqq p_{\mu}\theta^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}.

(49)

Therefore, we can deduce the action of twisted Lorentz transformations on the Fourier amplitudes to be

\Phi(p)\Psi(q)\xrightarrow{\Lambda}f_{\theta}(\Lambda^{-1}p,\Lambda^{-1}q)f^{-1}_{\theta}(p,q)\Phi(\Lambda^{-1}p)\Psi(\Lambda^{-1}q).

(50)

We are now ready to proceed to quantize the fields. They admit the usual expansion in terms of positive and negative frequency modes

\Phi(x)=\Phi^{+}(x)+\Phi^{-}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int\frac{d^{d}p}{\sqrt{2E_{p}}}\left(c(\boldsymbol{p})e^{-ip\cdot x}+c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})e^{ip\cdot x}\right),

(51)

where
c(\boldsymbol{p})
and
c(\boldsymbol{p})^{\dagger}
are quantum operators. As for the classical Fourier amplitudes, these operators transform as

c(\boldsymbol{p})c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})\xrightarrow{\Lambda}f_{\theta}(\Lambda^{-1}p,\Lambda^{-1}q)f_{\theta}^{-1}(p,q)c(\Lambda^{-1}\boldsymbol{p})c^{\dagger}(\Lambda^{-1}\boldsymbol{q}),

(52)

etc., so the canonical commutation relations (CCRs) cannot be compatible with twisted Lorentz transformations. It turns out that there exists a generalization of the CCRs in terms of a complex-valued exchange factor
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)

[c(\boldsymbol{p}),c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}}\coloneqq c(\boldsymbol{p})c(\boldsymbol{q})^{\dagger}-\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)c(\boldsymbol{q})^{\dagger}c(\boldsymbol{p})=\delta^{(d)}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{q}),

(53)

which preserves covariance under the twisted Poincaré group. This is given by those
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
which make commutate the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c(\boldsymbol{p})c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{\theta}(\Lambda)\,c(\boldsymbol{p})c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\theta}\Delta_{\theta}(\Lambda)\,c(\boldsymbol{p})c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\theta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda}

(54)

where
\tau_{\theta}
is the transposition or flip operator
\tau_{\theta}c(\boldsymbol{p})c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})=\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})c(\boldsymbol{p})
. This leads to the condition

\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(\Lambda^{-1}p,\Lambda^{-1}q)}{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)}=\frac{f_{\theta}^{-2}(\Lambda^{-1}p,\Lambda^{-1}q)}{f_{\theta}^{-2}(p,q)},

(55)

whose solution is

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)=\eta(p,q)f_{\theta}^{-2}(p,q),

(56)

where

is a Lorentz invariant function.

The same argument can also be applied to the commutators
[c(\boldsymbol{p}),c(\boldsymbol{q})]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{\prime}}
and
[c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p}),c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{\prime\prime}}
, leading to the following exchange functions

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{\theta}(p,q)=\eta^{\prime}(p,q)f_{\theta}^{2}(p,q),\quad\mathcal{Q}^{\prime\prime}_{\theta}(p,q)=\eta^{\prime\prime}(p,q)f_{\theta}^{2}(p,q),

(57)

where
\eta^{\prime}
and
\eta^{\prime\prime}
are Lorentz invariant functions of the momenta, in principle different from

and from each other. However, for

\bra{0}c(\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime})c(\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})c^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})\ket{0}
using only the mixed relation (

\delta^{(d)}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\delta^{(d)}(\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime})+\eta(p,q)f_{\theta}^{2}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{p})\delta^{(d)}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime})\delta^{(d)}(\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}),

(58)

whereas permuting the particle order with

and

\eta=\eta^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime\prime}=\pm 1
. To derive Eq. (

c(\boldsymbol{p})\ket{0}=0
.

To conclude, for particles with a generic spin the twisted quon algebra writes

a(\tilde{p})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q})-\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)a^{\dagger}(\tilde{q})a(\tilde{p})=\delta(\tilde{p},\tilde{q}),

(59)

where
\tilde{p}=(\boldsymbol{p},\rho)
denotes momentum and spin projection, and

Multiparticle state spaces

The algebra (

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}=\int_{\tilde{p}_{1},...,\tilde{p}_{n}}\psi(\tilde{p}_{1},...,\tilde{p}_{n})a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p}_{1})\cdot\cdot\cdot a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p}_{n})\ket{0},

(60a)

(60b)

where
\int_{\tilde{p}}=\int d^{3}p\sum_{\rho}

\braket{\psi|\phi}
using Eq. (
59

a(\tilde{p})\ket{0}=0
for all momenta and spins. We introduce the mapping
\mathcal{T}_{\theta}:\pi_{i}\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i}
from the adjacent permutations
\pi_{i}\in S_{n}
to the operators
\{\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i}:i=1,...,n-1\}
acting on
\mathscr{H}^{n}
as

(61)

The mapping
\mathcal{T}_{\theta}

S_{n}

\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i}
are self-adjoint and satisfy the braid relations

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i}\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{j}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{j}\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i},\quad|i-j|\geq 2,

(62a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i}\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i+1}\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i+1}\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i}\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i+1}.

(62b)

They are not, however, involutions

(\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{i})^{2}=\eta^{2}(p_{i},p_{i+1})\neq 1,

(63)

unless
\eta=\pm 1

T_{\theta}
on
\mathscr{H}^{n}
defined by

T_{\theta}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}\mathcal{T}_{\theta}(\pi).

(64)

Then, the modified inner product on

is given by the matrix elements of

(65)

where

It is convenient to focus on two-particle states. In this case, the inner product reduces to

\braket{\psi|\phi}=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\left[\phi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(q,p)\phi(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\right].

\displaystyle\psi_{s}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=\psi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})+\psi(\tilde{q},\tilde{p}),

(67a)

\displaystyle\psi_{a}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=\psi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})-\psi(\tilde{q},\tilde{p}),

(67b)

are no longer orthogonal. Their inner product is given by

\begin{split}\braket{\psi_{s}|\phi_{a}}&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\left[\phi_{a}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(q,p)\phi_{a}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\right]\\
&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\left[1-\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(q,p)\right]\\
&=-\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(q,p).\end{split}

(68)

Using the explicit expression
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=\eta f_{\theta}^{-2}
and the symmetry properties of the amplitudes
\psi_{s,a}

\begin{split}-\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)&=-\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\eta(p,q)[\cos(p\wedge_{\theta}q)-i\sin(p\wedge_{\theta}q)]\\
&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\phi_{a}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\eta(p,q)[\cos(p\wedge_{\theta}q)-i\sin(p\wedge_{\theta}q)];\end{split}

(69)

then exploiting the symmetry properties of
\eta
,
\cos(p\wedge_{\theta}q)
,
\sin(p\wedge_{\theta}q)
and of the integration measure, one obtains

\begin{split}-\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p,q)&=\int_{\tilde{q},\tilde{p}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\eta(q,p)[\cos(q\wedge_{\theta}p)-i\sin(q\wedge_{\theta}p)]\\
&=\int_{\tilde{q},\tilde{p}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\eta(p,q)[\cos(p\wedge_{\theta}q)+i\sin(p\wedge_{\theta}q)]\\
&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\eta(p,q)[\cos(p\wedge_{\theta}q)+i\sin(p\wedge_{\theta}q)].\end{split}

(70)

The term proportional to
\cos(p\wedge_{\theta}q)
cancels since it is an odd function of
\tilde{p}
and
\tilde{q}
, whereas the other does not vanish due to
\sin(p\wedge_{\theta}q)

\braket{\psi_{s}|\phi_{a}}=i\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\phi_{a}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\sin(p\wedge_{\theta}q).

(71)

This result shows that transitions between symmetric and antisymmetric states are allowed

\braket{\psi_{s}|O|\phi_{a}}\neq 0,

(72)

therefore the subspaces
\mathscr{H}_{s}^{2}
and
\mathscr{H}_{a}^{2}
are no longer superselected on the Moyal space.

The proper irreducible decomposition of the states in the full Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}^{2}
involves twisted symmetric and antisymmetric components

\displaystyle\psi_{s_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\coloneq\psi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})+f_{\theta}^{-2}(p,q)\psi(\tilde{q},\tilde{p}),

(73a)

\displaystyle\psi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\coloneq\psi(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})-f_{\theta}^{-2}(p,q)\psi(\tilde{q},\tilde{p}),

(73b)

which satisfy

\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})=\pm f_{\theta}^{2}(p,q)\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p}),

(74)

where the

(

\displaystyle\left\lVert\psi_{s_{\theta}}\right\rVert^{2}=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\left(1+\eta(p,q)\right)|\psi_{s_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})|^{2},

(75a)

(75b)

\begin{split}\braket{\psi_{s_{\theta}}|\phi_{a_{\theta}}}&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s_{\theta}}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\left[\phi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(q,p)\phi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\right]\\

(76)

where in the second line we used Eq. (
74

\begin{split}\braket{\psi_{s_{\theta}}|\phi_{a_{\theta}}}&=\int_{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}\psi_{s_{\theta}}^{*}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})\left[\phi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(q,p)\phi_{a_{\theta}}(\tilde{q},\tilde{p})\right]\\

&=-\braket{\psi_{s_{\theta}}|\phi_{a_{\theta}}},\end{split}

(77)

which implies that the inner product vanishes. The full Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}^{2}
is then the direct sum of the twisted bosonic and fermionic subspaces obtained from the projection operators

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{s_{\theta}}=\frac{1}{2!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\pi),

(78a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{a_{\theta}}=\frac{1}{2!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{2}}\text{sgn}(\pi)\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\pi),

(78b)

where

are permutations on
\mathscr{H}^{2}
constructed by twisting the flip operator as
\tau\to\mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{-2}\tau

as usual using the Young symmetrizer and the twisted permutations

\mathbb{P}_{\gamma_{\theta}}\propto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi_{r}\in R_{\gamma}\\

(79)

where

and

denote the subgroups of

that preserve, respectively, each row and each column of
\gamma

This result suggests that superselection rules may persist in a generalized form, applying to states with twisted symmetry. In this case, physical observables correspond to Hermitian operators which commute with the twisted permutations
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
. Each eigenvector of
O
thus belongs to an irreducible subspace

(80)

Remarkably, this implies that interference between states belonging to different twisted sectors is unobservable. To see this, we derive the density operator associated to the normalized vector

(83)

(84)

We conclude this section by showing why the restriction

(85a)

(85b)

These expressions are inevitably complex unless

(86)

Analogously,

(91)

where

acts on

, and we defined the

-mutators as

(93)

In the commutative limit

,
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=1
and the integrals in Eq. (

) vanish via standard canonical anti-commutation relations, yielding

. For
\Lambda_{\theta}
finite, the
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
-mutators

still vanish if the operators satisfy strict twisted statistics (
\eta=-1
); in this case Eq. (
87
) holds with the bilinear Hamiltonian
H{|_{\eta=1}}=\int_{\tilde{p}}E_{p}[a^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})a(\tilde{p})+b^{\dagger}(\tilde{p})b(\tilde{p})]
. For quon-like deformations
|\eta|<1

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
-mutators
[\Psi^{\pm}(x),\Psi^{\pm}(y)]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}}
do not vanish. To investigate this, we move to momentum space and assume the validity of relations (
88
). This leads to the following relations

\begin{split}h_{D}(\boldsymbol{p})u_{s}(\boldsymbol{p})a_{s}(\boldsymbol{p})=&E_{p}u_{s}(\boldsymbol{p})a_{s}(\boldsymbol{p})\\
&+\sum_{r,t}\int d^{3}qa_{r}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})\left[a_{t}(\boldsymbol{q}),a_{s}(\boldsymbol{p})\right]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}}\left(\frac{1}{2E_{q}}u_{r}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q})h_{D}(\boldsymbol{q})u_{t}(\boldsymbol{q})\right)u_{s}(\boldsymbol{p}),\end{split}

(94)

and

\begin{split}h_{D}(-\boldsymbol{p})v_{s}(\boldsymbol{p})b_{s}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})=&-\mathcal{\bar{Q}}_{\theta}^{-1}E_{p}v_{s}(\boldsymbol{p})b_{s}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\\

(95)

and an analogous equation for antiparticles. The terms in square brackets

(1-\eta^{2})^{-1}
in the second line arises from a geometric series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}[\eta(p,q)]^{k}
, which converges due to the constraint
|\eta(p,q)|<1
for all values of the momenta. Inserting the results into Eq. (

H=\int d^{3}pE_{p}\left[N(\boldsymbol{p})+N^{c}(\boldsymbol{p})\right],

(97)

where now

and

are particle and antiparticle number operators of infinite rank. At the end, the explicit expression for the particle number operator, up to annihilation of all one- and two-particles, is the following

N(\boldsymbol{p})=\sum_{s}a_{s}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})a_{s}(\boldsymbol{p})+\sum_{s,r}\int d^{3}q\frac{1}{1-\eta^{2}(p,q)}\left[a_{r}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{q}),a_{s}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\right]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}}\left[a_{s}(\boldsymbol{p}),a_{r}(\boldsymbol{q})\right]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}^{*}}+...,

(98)

with a corresponding expression for
N^{c}(\boldsymbol{p})
.

3.2

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}

[\Phi^{+}(x),\Phi^{-\dagger}(y)]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}}=[\Phi^{+\dagger}(x),\Phi^{-}(y)]_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}}=\Delta^{+}(x-y),

(104)

\Delta^{+}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{2E_{p}}e^{-ip\cdot x}.

(105)

\begin{split}\Delta(x-y)&=\braket{0|T\{\Phi(x)\Phi^{\dagger}(x^{\prime})\}|0}\\

where

4
Relativistic wave equations for atoms

QFT provides the appropriate relativistic framework to describe bound states, which appear as poles of Green functions. These functions describe the propagation of multiparticles systems and generalize the Dirac (or Breit) equation through the Bethe-Salpeter (BS) equation
Salpeter and Bethe 1951
;
Salpeter 1952
;
Nakanishi 1969
, which encodes the full relativistic interaction among the particles forming the bound state. We employ this approach to analyze
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
-deformed atomic systems, focusing on the helium atom as the simplest configuration exhibiting violations of ordinary fermionic statistics.

Let us begin with the commutative framework. The relativistic definition of a wave function is given by the BS amplitude, which for the helium-like atom reads

\phi_{BS}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{n};P)=\bra{\Omega}T\{\Psi_{e}(x_{1})\Psi_{e}(x_{2})\Psi_{n}(x_{n})\}\ket{\phi;P},

(107)

where
\ket{\phi;P}
denotes the bound state with total four-momentum
P^{\mu}
(other quantum numbers are omitted). Here,
\Psi_{e,n}
are the Heisenberg fields of the electron and nucleus, respectively, and
\ket{\Omega}
is the vacuum state of the interacting theory. The corresponding six-point Green function is

G_{0}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{n},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{n}^{\prime})=\braket{\Omega|T\{\Psi_{e}(x_{1})\Psi_{e}(x_{2})\Psi_{n}(x_{n})\Psi_{e}^{\dagger}(x_{1}^{\prime})\Psi_{e}^{\dagger}(x_{2}^{\prime})\Psi_{n}^{\dagger}(x_{n}^{\prime})\}|\Omega}.

(108)

The BS amplitude is antisymmetric under exchange of the electrons and the Green function shares this antisymmetry in both its initial and final electron coordinates. By inserting a complete set of states into Eq. (
108
) through

\begin{split}\mathbb{I}&=\sum_{n}\ket{n}\bra{n}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{bound}\\
\text{states}\end{subarray}}\int\frac{d^{4}Q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\delta(P^{2}-M_{B}^{2})\theta(P_{0})\ket{\phi;Q}\bra{\phi;Q}+\text{“1-particle”}+\cdot\cdot\cdot,\end{split}

(109)

and isolating the specific contribution of our bound state, one obtains

\begin{split}G_{0}(\{x_{i}\},\{x^{\prime}_{i}\})=\int\frac{d^{3}Q}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{Q}}\Big[\phi_{BS}(\{x_{i}\};Q)\phi_{BS}^{\dagger}(\{x_{1}^{\prime}\};Q)\\
\times\theta(\min(x_{1}^{0},x_{2}^{0},x_{n}^{0})-\min(x_{1}^{\prime 0},x_{2}^{\prime 0},x_{n}^{\prime 0}))\Big],\end{split}

(110)

where
\{x_{i}\}=x_{1},x_{2},x_{n}
, etc. In momentum space, the Green function has a pole at
P^{0}=E_{P}
, and in the limit
P^{0}\to E_{P}
it becomes

G_{0}(\{p_{i}\},\{p_{i}^{\prime}\})=\frac{i\phi_{BS}(\{p_{i}\})\phi_{BS}^{\dagger}(\{p_{i}^{\prime}\})}{2E_{P}[P^{0}-E_{P}+i\epsilon]}.

(111)

In the interaction picture, the Green function can be written as

G_{0}(\{x_{i}\},\{x_{i}^{\prime}\})=\frac{\bra{0}T\{\Psi_{I}^{e}(x_{1})\Psi_{I}^{e}(x_{2})\Psi_{I}^{n}(x_{n})\Psi_{I}^{e\dagger}(x_{1}^{\prime})\Psi_{I}^{e\dagger}(x_{2}^{\prime})\Psi_{I}^{e\dagger}(x_{n}^{\prime})e^{i\int d^{4}x\mathcal{H}_{I}}\}\ket{0}}{\bra{0}e^{i\int d^{4}x\mathcal{H}_{I}}\ket{0}},

(112)

where
\mathcal{H}_{I}
is the interaction Hamiltonian density. Expanding
G
perturbatively yields a series of connected Feynman diagrams. The symmetry properties of the Green function under electron exchange are entirely determined by those of the free fields and are unaffected by the specific form of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
. The Green function describing the atom propagation can thus be decomposed as

\displaystyle G_{0}(\{p_{i}\};\{p^{\prime}_{i}\})=S_{e}(p_{1},p_{2})S_{n}(p_{n})\mathcal{I}_{0}S_{n}(p_{n}^{\prime})S_{e}(p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime}),

(113)

\displaystyle S_{e}(p_{1},p_{2})\coloneqq S_{e}(p_{1})S_{e}(p_{2})-S_{e}(p_{2})S_{e}(p_{1}),

(114)

where
S_{e,n}
denote the dressed propagators for the electrons and the nucleus, and
\mathcal{I}_{0}
is the amputated correlation function.

To proceed, let us express the amplitude in terms of an unsymmetrized amplitude
\hat{\phi}_{BS}
obeying infinite statistics

\phi_{BS}(p,q)=\hat{\phi}_{BS}(p,q)-\hat{\phi}_{BS}(q,p).

(115)

Using Eqs. (
111
) and (
113
), one finds

\frac{i\hat{\phi}_{BS}(\{p_{i}\})\hat{\phi}_{BS}^{\dagger}(\{p_{i}^{\prime}\})}{2E_{P}[P^{0}-E_{P}+i\epsilon]}=S_{e}(p)S_{e}(q)S_{n}(p_{n})\mathcal{I}_{0}S_{e}(p^{\prime})S_{e}(q^{\prime})S_{n}(p_{n}^{\prime}).

(116)

We stress that so far the explicit form of the interaction Hamiltonian has not been specified.

By reorganizing diagrams, all two-body and three-body irreducible interactions can be isolated into a kernel
\mathcal{K}
, as shown in Figure (
4
), leading to

\begin{split}\frac{i\hat{\phi}_{BS}(\{p_{i}\})\hat{\phi}_{BS}^{\dagger}(\{p_{i}^{\prime}\})}{2E_{P}[P^{0}-E_{P}+i\epsilon]}=S_{e}(p_{1})S_{e}(p_{2})S_{n}(p_{n})\int\prod_{i=1}^{3}d^{4}k_{i}\,\mathcal{K}(\{p_{i}\};\{k_{i}\})\\
\times\left[S_{e}(k_{1},k_{2})S_{n}(k_{n})\mathcal{I}_{0}S_{n}(p_{n}^{\prime})S_{e}(p_{1}^{\prime})S_{e}(p_{2}^{\prime})\right].\end{split}

(117)

Substituting (
116
) into the square brackets of Eq. (
117
) and antisymmetrizing with respect to
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
yields the homogeneous BS equation

S^{-1}_{e}(p_{1})S^{-1}_{e}(p_{2})S^{-1}_{n}(p_{n})\phi_{BS}(\{p_{i}\})=\int\prod_{i=1}^{3}d^{4}k_{i}\,\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\{p_{i}\};\{k_{i}\})\phi_{BS}(\{k_{i}\}).

(118)

This is the exact covariant equation for a helium-like system.

Note that the amplitudes
\phi_{BS}
and
\hat{\phi}_{BS}
depend on the four-momenta
p_{i}^{\mu}=(E_{i},\boldsymbol{p}_{i})
, which satisfy
\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\mu}=P^{\mu}
. Owing to time-translation invariance, the amplitudes can be expressed in terms of relative energies (or, equivalently, relative times
x_{i}^{0}-x_{j}^{0}
in configuration space). This feature represents a key difference from the Schrödinger or Dirac equations, which involve only a single time variable. To eliminate the dependence on relative energies, Eq. (
118
) can be reduced to a three-dimensional equation—the Salpeter equation
Salpeter 1952
. The multi-time amplitude
\phi_{BS}
can be reconstructed from a single-time wave function
\psi(t,\{\boldsymbol{p}_{i}\})
by propagating it to all possible relative time orderings. As shown in Ref.
Feldman and
Fulton 1982
, this is achieved by applying one- and two-particle propagators to
\psi
as follows

\phi_{BS}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[(S_{1}+S_{2})G_{12}+(S_{2}+S_{3})G_{23}+(S_{1}+S_{3})G_{13}\right]\psi,

(119)

where
G_{ij}
denotes the two-body Green function propagating particles
i
and
j

Noncommutative setting

Now we turn to the noncommutative generalization. In the general case where a quon deformation
\eta
is present, a straightforward extension of the above derivation encounters difficulties. As illustrated in Figure
5
, the external legs involve the exchange factor
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
whenever electron permutations occur. Consequently, the expansion of the Green function modifies Eq. (
113

S_{e}^{(\mathcal{Q}_{\theta})}(p_{1},p_{2})\coloneqq S_{e}(p_{1})S_{e}(p_{2})+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(p_{1},p_{2})S_{e}(p_{2})S_{e}(p_{1}),

(121)

which has no definite symmetry.

On the other hand, inserting a complete set of states into (
108
), and proceeding as in Eq. (
110
), yields BS amplitudes that inherit the symmetry of the bound state under consideration. Indeed, physical states may retain a (twisted) symmetry even including a quon deformation; the key novelty is that all irreducible representation of the symmetric group contribute (for helium, the twisted-symmetric and twisted-antisymmetric representations). Hence, the BS amplitude naturally reflects the symmetry of the physical state annihilated by the fields. As an example, consider a twisted-symmetric state of two free scalar particles. The equal-time BS amplitude is

\begin{split}\phi_{BS}^{free}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},t)&=\braket{0|\Phi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\Phi(\boldsymbol{y},t)|\phi_{s_{\theta}}}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{\sqrt{2E_{k}}}\frac{d^{3}l}{\sqrt{2E_{l}}}e^{i(E_{k}+E_{l})t}e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}e^{-i\boldsymbol{l}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}}\left[\phi_{s_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l})+\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}(l,k)\phi_{s_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{k})\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{\sqrt{2E_{k}}}\frac{d^{3}l}{\sqrt{2E_{l}}}e^{i(E_{k}+E_{l})t}e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}e^{-i\boldsymbol{l}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}}\phi_{s_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l})\left[1+\eta(l,k)\right].\end{split}

(122)

In Fourier space this becomes

\phi_{BS}^{free}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q},t)\propto e^{i(E_{p}+E_{q})t}\phi_{s_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})\left[1+\eta(p,q)\right]=f_{\theta}^{2}(q,p)\phi_{BS}^{free}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{p},t).

(123)

Therefore, each amplitude can be labeled according to the symmetry type
\gamma_{\theta}
of its associated state

\phi_{BS}^{\gamma_{\theta}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{n};P)=\braket{\Omega|T\{\Psi_{e}(x_{1})\Psi_{e}(x_{2})\Psi_{n}(x_{n})\}|\phi;\gamma_{\theta},P}.

(124)

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{\gamma_{\theta}}(\{p_{i}\};\{p_{i}^{\prime}\})=\frac{1}{4}\Big[&G_{\theta}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{n};p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime},p_{n}^{\prime})+s_{\gamma_{\theta}}f^{2}_{\theta}(p_{2},p_{1})G_{\theta}(p_{2},p_{1},p_{n};p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime},p_{n}^{\prime})\\

&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times S_{e}^{(\gamma_{\theta})}(p_{1},p_{2})S_{n}(p_{n})\mathcal{I}_{\theta}S_{n}(p_{n}^{\prime})S_{e}^{(\gamma_{\theta})}(p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime}),\end{split}

\hat{\phi}_{BS}
and derive an equation analogous to (

\mathcal{I}_{\theta}
, we wish to construct an interaction such that the amplitude enters with the appropriate symmetry
\gamma_{\theta}
. To verify whether this is the case, one would need explicit knowledge of the interaction Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{I}^{\theta}
, which, in the presence of quon deformations, is not known.

This observation leaves two distinct possibilities: either the electron-electron interaction part of the kernel preserves statistics, or it does not. In the first case, after reducing the BS amplitude to a single-time amplitude, one obtains the Salpeter equation

\left[\sum_{i=1}^{3}h_{D}^{(i)}+\sum_{i>j}V_{\theta,ij}^{+}-E\right]\psi_{\gamma_{\theta}}=0,

(131)

where again

are the electron-electron and electron-nucleus interactions, now modified by noncommutative corrections. Physical states then define a subset of the irreducible subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma_{\theta}}^{2}
. In the second case, where the electron-electron interaction breaks permutation symmetry, a closed equation can be obtained only for the non-symmetrized amplitude. The corresponding single-time amplitude then satisfies

\left[\sum_{i=1}^{3}h_{D}^{(i)}+\sum_{i>j}V_{\theta,ij}^{\prime+}-E\right]\psi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}}=0,

(132)

where

\Lambda_{\theta}\to\infty
. Consequently, to lowest order in
\Lambda_{\theta}^{-1}
, the eigenfunctions coincide with the ordinary ones. These equations, as well as their
n
-electron generalization, allow to compute noncommutative corrections to atomic spectra. However, for the purposes of our analysis, the explicit expressions of Eqs. (
131
) and (
132
) are not required.

(H_{if})_{0}
corresponds to the PEP-allowed transition
\beta\to\alpha
. Forgetting the contribution of
H_{if}^{a}
, the PEP-forbidden rates are then given by

\frac{d\Gamma_{\text{PV}}^{s}}{d\Omega}=\left(\frac{m}{\Lambda_{\theta}}\right)^{2}\frac{d\Gamma_{0}}{d\Omega},

(161)

where
\frac{d\Gamma_{0}}{d\Omega}
is the rate for the associated PEP-allowed transition.

5.2.2
Case
\sigma_{\text{nr}}=|\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{q}|

Using the expressions given in Eqs. (
156
),(
157
), (
158
) and (
159
), the component
H_{if}^{s}
of the matrix elements (
150
) can be written as

H_{if}^{s}=\frac{2mE_{0}}{\mathcal{N}_{2}\Lambda_{\theta}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}m}\left[X^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}+X_{\alpha\alpha}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha\alpha}\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{i*}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}^{i}(1-2\Omega_{\alpha\alpha}^{ii})\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}\right].

(162)

For
\alpha
corresponding to an s state,
\Omega_{\alpha\alpha}^{xx}=\Omega_{\alpha\alpha}^{yy}=\Omega_{\alpha\alpha}^{zz}=\frac{1}{3}
and hence
\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{i*}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}^{i}(1-2\Omega_{\alpha\alpha}^{ii})=\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\alpha\beta}
. Substituting into Eq. (
162
) gives

H_{if}^{s}=\frac{2mE_{0}(X^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{3}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha\alpha}^{(2)})}{\mathcal{N}_{2}\Lambda_{\theta}}(H_{if})_{0}.

(163)

In particular,
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha\alpha}^{(2)}=1
when
\alpha
corresponds to the 1s level. The norm of the PEP-violating state is

\mathcal{N}_{2}=\left[2\int_{p}|\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})|^{2}p^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{4mE_{0}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{(2)}_{\alpha\alpha}}.

(164)

We define the coefficients

\mathcal{X}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\left(X^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{3}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha\alpha}^{(2)}\right)^{2}}{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha\alpha}^{(2)}},

(165)

so that the resulting rates become

\frac{d\Gamma_{\text{PV}}^{s}}{d\Omega}=\left(\frac{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha\beta}\,mE_{0}}{\Lambda_{\theta}^{2}}\right)\frac{d\Gamma_{0}}{d\Omega}.

(166)

5.2.3
Case
\sigma_{\text{nr}}=\sqrt{m|\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{q}|}

The matrix elements
H_{if}^{s}
take the form

\begin{split}H_{if}^{s}&=\frac{m}{\mathcal{N}_{2}\Lambda_{\theta}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}m}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\int_{p,q}\boldsymbol{p}\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})\phi_{[\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\alpha]}(\boldsymbol{q})\\
&=\frac{m}{\mathcal{N}_{2}\Lambda_{\theta}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}m}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\int_{p,q}(\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{q})\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\left|\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})\right|^{2}.\end{split}

(167)

Using the identity

\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{q}\left(p^{2}+q^{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}\right)^{3/2}=(\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{p})\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}},

(168)

one can see that Eq. (
167
) can be recast into the form

\begin{split}\delta H_{if}^{s}=\frac{m}{\mathcal{N}_{2}\Lambda_{\theta}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}m}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\int_{p,q}\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\left|\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})\right|^{2}\Bigg[2\boldsymbol{p}\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}}\\
+\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{q}\left(p^{2}+q^{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}\right)^{3/2}\Bigg].\end{split}

(169)

We consider transitions to the
\alpha=
1s level, since spherical symmetry simplifies the computation. The second integral vanishes due to the orthogonality of the angular components
\Omega_{\alpha}
and
\Omega_{\beta}
of the wave functions (Eq. (
146
)): performing integration in
\boldsymbol{p}
yields

\frac{1}{3}\int dqq^{2}R_{\alpha}(q)R_{\beta}(q)\frac{\partial K(q)}{\partial q}\boldsymbol{e}_{q}\int d\varphi d\cos\theta\Omega^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\theta,\varphi)\Omega_{\beta}(\theta,\varphi)=0

(170)

where
K
depends only on
q=|\boldsymbol{q}|
and
\boldsymbol{e}_{q}
denotes the radial unit vector.

Defining the coefficients

Y_{1s,\beta}=\frac{\int dpp^{3}R_{1s}(p)R_{\beta}(p)I(p)}{\mathcal{R}_{1s,\beta}}=\begin{cases}1.16\quad\beta=2p\\
1.27\quad\beta=3p\end{cases}

(171)

where the function
I(p,q)
is given by

\begin{split}I(p)&=\int_{q}\left|\phi_{1s}(q)\right|^{2}\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}-2pq\cos\theta}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int dqq^{2}[R_{1s}(q)]^{2}\frac{(p+q)^{3}-|p-q|^{3}}{3pq},\end{split}

(172)

the remaining integral yields

\int_{p,q}\,\boldsymbol{p}\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\left|\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})\right|^{2}=Y_{1s,\beta}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{1s,\beta}\mathcal{R}_{1s,\beta}.

(173)

We thus find that Eq. (
167
) is proportional to the ordinary transition matrix element.

The norm of the final state is

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}_{2}&=\left(m\sqrt{2mE_{0}}\int_{p,q}R_{1s}^{2}(p)R_{1s}^{2}(q)\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\left(\frac{35}{9\pi}m\sqrt{2mE_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(174)

Finally, we obtain the following expression for the PEP-violating rates

\frac{d\Gamma_{\text{PV}}^{s}}{d\Omega}=\frac{4\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha\beta}\sqrt{2m^{3}E_{0}}}{\Lambda_{\theta}^{2}}\frac{d\Gamma_{0}}{d\Omega},

(175)

where
\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha\beta}
combines
Y_{\alpha\beta}
with the numerical coefficient of the normalization:

\mathcal{Y}_{1s,\beta}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{9\pi}{35}}Y_{1s,\beta}\right)^{2}=\begin{cases}1.234\quad\beta=2p\\
1.345\quad\beta=3p\end{cases}

(176)

5.2.4
Contribution of
H_{if}^{a}

We now focus on the contribution of the initial state’s antisymmetric amplitude, which contributes to the matrix element as

H_{if}^{a}=\frac{i}{\mathcal{N}_{2}\Lambda_{\theta}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}m}\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{i*}c^{jk}\int_{p}p^{k}\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\int_{q}q^{i}q^{j}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q}).

(177)

For the 1s level, the second integral evaluates to

\int_{q}q^{i}q^{j}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{1s}(\boldsymbol{q})\phi_{1s}(\boldsymbol{q})=\frac{2mE_{0}}{3}\delta^{ij},

(178)

which, when substituted into Eq. (
177
), gives

H_{if}^{a}=\frac{i2mE_{0}}{3\mathcal{N}_{2}\Lambda_{\theta}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}m}\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{i*}c^{ij}\int_{p}p^{j}\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p}),

(179)

where summation over the indices
i,j
is implied. The integral does not vanish in general and, modulo constant factors, this expression differs from the undeformed matrix element
(H_{if})_{0}
only by the presence of the background-field components
c^{ij}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
instead of
\delta^{ij}
. Therefore, we can estimate
H_{if}^{a}
as follows

H_{if}^{a}\sim\frac{i2mE_{0}}{3\mathcal{N}_{2}\Lambda_{\theta}}(H_{if})_{0}.

(180)

For frameworks in which time-space noncommutativity preserves unitarity and yields well-defined QFT, the components
c^{0i}
should also contribute. However, in the dipole approximation these contributions are proportional to

\begin{split}&\varepsilon^{i*}_{\lambda}c^{0j}\left[\int_{p}p^{i}\phi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\int_{q}q^{j}\phi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})-\int_{p}p^{i}\phi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\int_{q}q^{j}\phi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\right],\\
&\varepsilon^{i*}_{\lambda}c^{j0}\left[\int_{p}p^{i}p^{j}\phi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\int_{q}\phi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})-\int_{p}p^{i}p^{j}\phi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\int_{q}\phi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{p})\right],\end{split}

(181)

which vanish upon angular integration for transitions
\beta\to\alpha
between states of opposite parity, such as 2p
\to
1s. Consequently, in atoms with sufficiently low
Z
, the dominant contribution is given by (
179
).

5.3
Remaining scenarios:
0<\kappa<2
and
2<\kappa<4

For
0<\kappa<2
, the leading contribution arises from the symmetric component
\psi_{s}
of the initial state. The corresponding matrix element is

H_{if}^{\pm}=\frac{2}{\mathcal{N}_{\kappa}\Lambda_{\theta}^{\kappa/2}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\int_{p,q}\psi^{\prime(0)\dagger}_{s}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{(1)}\psi_{s}^{(0)}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})\sigma^{\kappa}(p-k,q).

(182)

In contrast, for
2<\kappa<4
, the antisymmetric component
\psi_{a}
provides the leading contribution and the matrix element becomes

H_{if}^{\pm}=\pm\frac{2i}{\mathcal{N}_{\kappa}\Lambda_{\theta}^{2-\kappa/2}}\frac{e}{\sqrt{2\omega_{k}}}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\int_{p,q}\psi^{\prime(0)\dagger}_{s}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{(1)}\psi_{a}^{(0)}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})\,(p-k)\wedge_{c}q.

(183)

In both cases, the PEP-violating transition rates can be cast in the form

\frac{d\Gamma_{\text{PV}}^{\pm}}{d\Omega}=\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\Lambda_{\theta}}\right)^{h(\kappa)}\frac{d\Gamma_{0}}{d\Omega},

(184)

where the exponent
h(\kappa)
is defined as

h(\kappa)=\begin{cases}\kappa&\kappa\in(0,2)\\
4-\kappa&\kappa\in(2,4)\end{cases}

(185)

and ranges between 0 and 2. For
\kappa=1
and
\kappa=3
, the rates are suppressed by factors of order
\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\theta}^{-1})
. The expression of the function
\mathcal{E}
depends on the value of
\kappa
as well as either
\sigma_{\text{nr}}(p,q)
or
c^{\mu\nu}
. For
\kappa\in(0,2)
it is given by
\mathcal{E}\sim m^{1-a/2}E_{0}^{a/2}
, while for
\kappa\in(2,4)
it is
\mathcal{E}\sim\sqrt{2mE_{0}}
, up to order-one multiplicative coefficients.

5.4
Directionality in PEP-violating transitions

We now consider a radiative capture process in which an ionized helium atom captures a free electron with momentum
\boldsymbol{p}_{0}
into a PEP-forbidden state. We focus in particular on the contribution

H_{if}^{a}\sim\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\int_{p,q}\psi^{\prime(0)\dagger}_{s}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{(1)}\psi_{a}^{(0)}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})(p-k)^{i}c^{ij}q^{j},

(186)

which is present in all matrix elements and for all values of
\kappa
. The antisymmetric component of the initial state reads

\psi_{a}^{(0)}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})=\frac{1}{2}[\chi(\boldsymbol{p})\varphi^{(0)}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})-\varphi^{(0)}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\chi(\boldsymbol{q})],

(187)

where
\alpha
labels the state of the bound electron. For simplicity, we take the incoming electron to be a plane wave
\chi(\boldsymbol{p})=u(\boldsymbol{p})\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{0})
. In the nonrelativistic limit

H_{if}^{a}\sim\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\int_{p,q}\boldsymbol{p}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k})\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})\left[\xi_{s}\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{0})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})-\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\xi_{s}\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{p}_{0})\right](p-k)\wedge_{c}q.

(188)

The first term vanishes upon integration over
\boldsymbol{q}
, since

\int d\boldsymbol{q}|\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})|^{2}q^{i}\propto\int d\theta d\varphi\sin\theta|\Omega_{\alpha}(\hat{\boldsymbol{q}})|^{2}\hat{q}^{i}=0.

(189)

The matrix element therefore reduces to

\begin{split}H_{if}^{a}&\sim\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\int_{p,q}\boldsymbol{p}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\phi^{*}_{\alpha|s}(\boldsymbol{q})\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{p}_{0})(p-k)\wedge_{c}q\\
&=\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{i*}c^{jk}\int_{p}p^{i}(p^{j}-k^{j})\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p})\int_{q}q^{k}\phi^{*}_{\alpha|s}(\boldsymbol{q})\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{p}_{0})\\
&=\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{i*}c^{jk}p_{0}^{k}[\Phi^{ij}(\boldsymbol{k})-\Xi^{i}(\boldsymbol{k})k^{j}]\phi^{*}_{\alpha|s}(\boldsymbol{p}_{0}),\end{split}

(190)

where
\phi^{*}_{\alpha|s}(\boldsymbol{q})=\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{q})\xi_{s}
, and we have defined

\begin{split}&\Phi^{ij}(\boldsymbol{k})\coloneqq\int_{p}p^{i}p^{j}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p}),\\
&\Xi^{i}(\boldsymbol{k})\coloneqq\int_{p}p^{i}\,\phi^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k})\phi_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{p}).\end{split}

(191)

Introducing the constant vector
\boldsymbol{c}=(c^{23},c^{31},c^{12})
, the expression can be rewritten as

H_{if}^{a}\sim\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{i*}c^{jk}p_{0}^{k}\Phi^{ij}(\boldsymbol{k})\phi^{*}_{\alpha|s}(\boldsymbol{p}_{0})-[\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Xi}(\boldsymbol{k})][\boldsymbol{c}\cdot(\boldsymbol{k}\times\boldsymbol{p}_{0})]\phi^{*}_{\alpha|s}(\boldsymbol{p}_{0}).

(192)

In experiments involving a large ensemble of atoms,
\Phi^{ij}
and
\Xi^{i}
average to quantities that depend only on the photon energy. The residual angular dependence is therefore governed by

\boldsymbol{c}\cdot(\boldsymbol{k}\times\boldsymbol{p}_{0}),

(193)

which couples the direction of the emitted photon
\hat{\boldsymbol{k}}
to the background coefficients. This induces a characteristic anisotropy in PEP-violating transition rates.
