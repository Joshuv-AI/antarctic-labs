Equidistribution in shrinking sets for arithmetic spherical harmonics
Maximiliano Sanchez Garza

Abstract
. We study a variant of the equidistribution of mass conjecture on the sphere posed by Böcherer, Sarnak, and Schulze-Pillot: quantum unique ergodicity in shrinking sets. Conditionally on the generalized Lindelöf hypothesis, we show that quantum unique ergodicity holds on every shrinking spherical cap whose radius is considerably larger than the Planck scale, and that it holds on almost every shrinking spherical cap whose radius is larger than the Planck scale. Additionally, conditionally on GLH, we provide explicit upper bounds for the 1 1 -Wasserstein distance and the spherical cap discrepancy between the involved measures.

Introduction

Let

We say that the basis

converges in distribution to the probability measure

; that is,
\{\phi_{n}\}
is quantum ergodic if there exists a subsequence
\{\phi_{m_{n}}\}
of
\{\phi_{n}\}
of density
1
such that for every continuity set
1
1

1

A subset

The previous notion of QUE is known as
quantum unique ergodicity in configuration space
. There is a stronger version of QUE, called
quantum unique ergodicity in phase space
, where one instead asks for the associated microlocal lifts of these measures to the unit cotangent bundle
S^{*}M
to equidistribute with respect to the Liouville measure. Here, we will only concern ourselves with QUE in configuration space; for more on QUE in phase space, see
[
44
]
.

A foundational result in this context is due to Shnirel’man, Colin de Verdière, and Zelditch (see
[
47
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
59
]
,
[
48
]
), which states that if the geodesic flow in
S^{*}M
is ergodic, then

orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(M)
is quantum ergodic. However, Rudnick and Sarnak in
[
42
]
conjectured that, in the case that
M
is a compact Riemannian manifold of negative curvature, any orthonormal basis should be quantum unique ergodic. In the case of compact hyperbolic surfaces arising from quaternion algebras and the orthonormal basis consisting of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator and all the Hecke operators, this conjecture was proven by Watson in
[
55
, §4.2]
assuming the generalized Lindelöf hypothesis (GLH) and by Lindenstrauss
[
34
, Thm. 1.4]
unconditionally.

Even though this discussion is in the context of compact Riemannian manifolds, similar conjectures can be made in case that the manifold is noncompact but has finite volume. In particular, for
M=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
, one has to consider a continuous spectrum and a discrete spectrum of the Laplacian operator. It is known that the continuous spectrum satisfies a version of quantum unique ergodicity by work of Jakobson in
[
32
]
and of Luo and Sarnak in
[
37
]
. For the discrete spectrum, the basis of Hecke–Maass cusp forms is known to be quantum unique ergodic conditionally to GLH via work of Watson in
[
55
, §4.2]
. Later, Lindenstrauss in
[
34
]
almost showed the result unconditionally, but could not eliminate the possibility of escape of mass at the cusp at infinity. This possibility was eliminated by Soundararajan in
[
49
]
, so that the basis of Hecke–Maass cusp forms was shown unconditionally to be quantum unique ergodic. This version of QUE is often referred to as
arithmetic quantum unique ergodicity
.

There are three remarks in order. The first one, as Soundararajan mentions in
[
49
, pp. 1530–1531]
, is that his proof of arithmetic QUE works not only for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, but for
any
congruence subgroup. Hence, arithmetic QUE is known for any noncompact arithmetic quotient of
\mathbb{H}
. The second remark is that, since it is believed that all the Laplacian eigenvalues in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
are simple, the additional requirement on the Laplacian eigenfunctions to be eigenfunctions of all the Hecke operators would be a vacuous condition and we would obtain QUE automatically in this case. The third remark is that both Lindenstrauss’ and Soundararajan’s results are only qualitative, i.e. they do not quantify the rate of convergence of (
1
). However, the proof of Watson in
[
55
]
shows that the optimal rates of convergence follow from GLH. For a more extensive discussion on QUE and its arithmetic version, see
[
44
]
.

1.1.
Quantum ergodicity on the sphere

Our main focus for the rest of this paper is the
2

M=S^{2}
. We give it the round metric and associated measure, which gives it the structure of a Riemannian manifold. Let
\Delta

[

[

First, since the geodesic flow of the sphere is not ergodic, the aforementioned results do not apply. In fact, quantum ergodicity (and hence QUE) is not expected for all bases of

[
10

[

showed that almost every orthonormal basis of

composed of Laplacian eigenfunctions is quantum ergodic

Although the

Using this inequality in the case of the

\frac{\mathrm{vol}(B_{R-\rho})}{12\mathrm{vol}(B_{R})}K_{R-\rho}*K_{\rho}(z,w)\leq K_{R}(z,w)\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}(B_{R+\rho})}{12\mathrm{vol}(B_{R})}K_{R+\rho}*K_{\rho}(z,w).

Proof.

The convolution
k_{R}*k_{\rho}
is nonnegative since both
k_{R}
and
k_{\rho}
are. Since
k_{\rho}(t,w)\neq 0
only when
t\in B_{\rho}(w)
, we have

k_{R}*k_{\rho}(z,w)=\int_{S^{2}}k_{R}(z,t)k_{\rho}(t,w)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(t)\leq\int_{B_{\rho}(w)}\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{R})}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{\rho})}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(t)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{R})}.

We now prove the first identity in the result. If
d(z,w)>R+\rho
, then the inequalities
d(z,t)\leq R
and
d(t,w)\leq\rho
cannot be true simultaneously, and so
k_{R}(z,t)k_{\rho}(t,w)=0
for all
t\in S^{2}
. Now, assume that
d(z,w)\leq R-\rho
. If
d(t,w)>\rho
, then
k_{R}(z,t)k_{\rho}(t,w)=0
, so assume otherwise. It follows that

d(z,t)\leq d(z,w)+d(w,t)\leq(R-\rho)+\rho=R,

so that
k_{R}(z,t)k_{\rho}(t,w)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{R})}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{\rho})}
for all
t\in B_{\rho}(w)
. From here the first identity follows.

The inequalities (
19
) follow from the previous observations. As for the inequalities (
20
), they follow from (
19
) and from the computation

(21)

\displaystyle K_{R}*K_{\rho}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\gamma_{1}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\sum_{\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\int_{S^{2}}k_{R}(\gamma_{1}z,t)k_{\rho}(\gamma_{2}t,w)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\gamma_{1}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\sum_{\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}}k_{R}*k_{\rho}(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}z,w)

\displaystyle=6\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}}k_{R}*k_{\rho}(\gamma z,w),

where in the last line we used that, for a fixed
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, the equation
\gamma=\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}
has
|\mathcal{O}^{\times}|=24
pairs of solutions
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
in
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
.
∎

Additionally, we have the following spectral expansion of the convolutions
K_{R}*K_{\rho}
.

Lemma 2.17
.

We have the spectral expansion

K_{R}*K_{\rho}(z,w)=\frac{12}{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2})}+12\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}}h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})h_{\rho}(\ell_{\phi})\phi(z)\overline{\phi(w)},

which converges absolutely and uniformly.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2})
and
K_{R}*K_{\rho}
is defined on
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
, we just need to compute the coefficient corresponding to
f_{0}=1/\sqrt{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2})}
and to
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
. Since
\phi\in\mathcal{B}
is
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
-invariant, by (
21
) and Lemma
2.13
we have that

\displaystyle\langle\phi,K_{R}*K_{\rho}(\cdot,w)\rangle

\displaystyle=6\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}}k_{R}*k_{\rho}(\gamma z,w)\phi(\gamma z)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)=12\int_{S^{2}}k_{R}*k_{\rho}(z,w)\phi(z)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)

\displaystyle=12\int_{S^{2}}\int_{S^{2}}k_{R}(z,t)k_{\rho}(t,w)\phi(z)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(t)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)

\displaystyle=12h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})\int_{S^{2}}k_{\rho}(t,w)\phi(t)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(t)=12h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})h_{\rho}(\ell_{\phi})\phi(w).

Similarly, one can show that
\langle f_{0},K_{R}*K_{\rho}(\cdot,w)\rangle=12
, and so we obtain the desired spectral expansion. The spectral expansion converges absolutely and uniformly since the convolution
K_{R}*K_{\rho}
is continuous in both variables
z
and
w
.
∎

2.5.
1-Wasserstein distance and the Berry–Esseen inequality

To be able to bound the
1
-Wasserstein distance between the relevant measures in our problem, we will need the Berry–Esseen inequality, which gives upper bounds for the
1
-Wasserstein distance in terms of the Fourier transforms of the involved measures.

This inequality was originally proved for the real line by Esseen in
[
15
, Ch. II, Thm. 2b]
, but the main ideas to prove it had already appeared in the earlier paper
[
2
]
by Berry. The analogous inequality for the
n
-sphere was later shown in
[
19
, Thm. 1]
by Grabner and Tichy. In the last few years, such an inequality was proved for the
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{n}
by Bobkov and Ledoux in
[
3
, Prop. 2]
and independently by Borda in
[
6
, Prop. 3]
. Recently, it was shown in a greater generality for compact Lie groups, compact homogeneous spaces, and compact Riemannian manifolds in
[
5
]
by Borda and Cuenin, and then extended to finite-volume (not necessarily compact) hyperbolic surfaces in
[
28
, Thms. 1.5, 1.10]
by Humphries. The version we require for our purposes is the following, which is a consequence of
[
5
, Thm. 7]
and
[
19
, Thm. 1]
.

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
\sigma^{\prime}
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}
. Then, for any integer
T\geq 1
,

\mathscr{W}_{1}\left(\sigma^{\prime},\frac{1}{\sigma(\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2})}\sigma\right)\ll\frac{1}{T}+\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\mathcal{B}\\
\ell_{\phi}\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\phi}(\ell_{\phi}+1)}\left|\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}}\phi(x)\>\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}\left(\left\{w\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}:\left|\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}(w)}|\psi(z)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)-\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2})}\right|>c\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c^{2}}\mathrm{Var}(\psi;R)\ll_{\varepsilon,\delta}\frac{\ell_{\psi}^{-(1-\delta)+\varepsilon}}{\left(\ell_{\psi}^{-(1-\delta)/2+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}}=\ell_{\psi}^{-\varepsilon},

which implies the result. ∎

Then Theorem
1.5
follows from Proposition
3.1
. To prove Proposition
3.1
, we first give a spectral expansion of
\mathrm{Var}(\psi,R)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\psi
be an
L^{2}
-normalized spherical harmonic of Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda_{\psi}^{2}=\ell_{\psi}(\ell_{\psi}+1)
for some integer
\ell_{\psi}>0
. Then, for
R>0
,

\mathrm{Var}(\psi;R)=\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}}|h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})|^{2}\left|\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi\rangle\right|^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.15
, we have

\displaystyle\langle|\psi|^{2},K_{R}(\cdot,w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle|\psi|^{2},f_{0}\rangle\langle f_{0},K_{R}(\cdot,w)\rangle+\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}}\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi\rangle\langle\phi,K_{R}(\cdot,w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2})}+\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}}h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})\phi(w)\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi\rangle.

This equation implies, again by Lemma
2.15
, that

\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{R}(w)}|\psi(z)|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)-\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2})}=\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}}h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})\phi(w)\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi\rangle.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2})
, squaring and integrating over
w
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\psi;R)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}}\left|\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}}h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})\phi(w)\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi\rangle\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}}\sum_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}}h_{R}(\ell_{\phi_{1}})\phi_{1}(w)\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi_{1}\rangle\overline{h_{R}(\ell_{\phi_{2}})\phi_{2}(w)\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi_{2}\rangle}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}}h_{R}(\ell_{\phi_{1}})\overline{h_{R}(\ell_{\phi_{2}})}\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi_{1}\rangle\overline{\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi_{2}\rangle}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}\backslash S^{2}}\phi_{1}(w)\overline{\phi_{2}(w)}\>\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi\in\mathcal{B}}|h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})|^{2}\left|\langle|\psi|^{2},\phi\rangle\right|^{2}.\qed

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

By Corollary
2.9
, Lemma
2.10
, Theorem
2.11
, GLH, and Proposition
3.2
, we have that

\mathrm{Var}(\psi;R)\ll_{\varepsilon}\ell_{\psi}^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\mathcal{B}\\
\ell_{\phi}\leq 2\ell_{\psi}\end{subarray}}\frac{|h_{R}(\ell_{\phi})|^{2}}{\ell_{\phi}(1+2\ell_{\psi}-\ell_{\phi})^{1/2}}.

We now separate the double sum into three ranges:
\ell_{\phi}\leq R^{-1}
,
R^{-1}\leq\ell_{\phi}\leq\ell_{\psi}
, and
\ell_{\psi}\leq\ell_{\phi}\leq 2\ell_{\psi}
. By Lemma
2.18
, we obtain

(34)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\psi;R)

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\ell_{\psi}^{-\frac{1}{2}+3\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\mathcal{B}\\
\ell_{\phi}\leq R^{-1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\phi}(1+2\ell_{\psi}-\ell_{\phi})^{1/2}}+\ell_{\psi}^{3\delta-\frac{1}{2}+3\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\mathcal{B}\\
R^{-1}\leq\ell_{\phi}\leq\ell_{\psi}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\phi}^{4}(1+2\ell_{\psi}-\ell_{\phi})^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\ell_{\psi}^{3\delta-\frac{1}{2}+3\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\mathcal{B}\\
\ell_{\psi}\leq\ell_{\phi}\leq 2\ell_{\psi}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\phi}^{4}(1+2\ell_{\psi}-\ell_{\phi})^{1/2}}.

For the first sum on the right-hand side of (
34
), we have that
1+2\ell_{\psi}-\ell_{\psi}\asymp\ell_{\psi}
since
\ell_{\phi}\leq R^{-1}\ll\ell_{\psi}
. Hence, by the Weyl law, we obtain

(35)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\mathcal{B}\\
\ell_{\phi}\leq R^{-1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\phi}(1+2\ell_{\psi}-\ell_{\phi})^{1/2}}\ll\ell_{\psi}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\ell\leq R^{-1}}1\ll R^{-1}\ell_{\psi}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ll\ell_{\psi}^{\delta-\frac{1}{2}}.

For the second and third sums on the right-hand side of (
34
), we follow the same idea as with the last two ranges in the proof of Theorem
1.3
: subdivide into dyadic ranges and use the Weyl law. Using that
0<\delta<1
, we obtain that both sums are
\ll_{\varepsilon,\delta}\ell_{\psi}^{-2\delta-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
. The result follows from combining these bounds together with (
34
) and (
35
).
∎
