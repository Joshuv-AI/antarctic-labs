intervention can close the bias gap, because no thresholding choice on
the shared axis can.

4
Closed-Form Predictor

The first-order effect of a rank-1 representation-space intervention on a
linear-head classifier is

\Delta\mathrm{logit}_{t}=-\varepsilon\cdot\langle\mathbf{cls}_{t},\mathbf{d}\rangle\cdot(\mathbf{w}_{h,t}\cdot\mathbf{d})

per text, exact for linear decoder heads
(
R^{2}=1.000
on 72 decoder measurements spanning four parameter scales
125M–8B) and Taylor-accurate to
\varepsilon\leq 0.7
on encoders, with
direction-asymmetric scope on RoBERTa-base and DeBERTa-v3.
Here
\mathbf{cls}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{h}
is the per-text
[CLS]
embedding
at the intervention layer,
\mathbf{d}
a unit direction, and

\mathbf{w}_{h,t}=\partial\mathrm{logit}_{\mathrm{diff}}/\partial\mathbf{cls}

the per-text head Jacobian obtained by autograd in a single backward pass.
For decoder LMs with linear score heads the expression is mathematically
exact; the 72 measurements span 3 decoder LM detectors
(GPT-Neo-125M, Pythia-160M, GPT-2-large) plus LLaMA-3-8B at a single
configuration, across 3 seeds, 2 axes and 4
\varepsilon
values.
On encoders,
\varepsilon\leq 0.7
is first-order Taylor-accurate, with
direction-asymmetric scope: symmetric on ELECTRA, direction-conditional on
RoBERTa-base and DeBERTa-v3 (Figure
2
).
A random-head universality control passes 4/4 tasks
(AI-detection, HANS, AAVE, Bios); zeroing the head output projection yields a
degenerate constant-prediction outcome by construction, refuting a
linear-probe-tautology objection.

7
Discussion

Returning to the
33.5\%
NYT-formal FPR from §1: that number is a
calibration of the pretrained typicality axis, not a learned
classification of AI text every load-bearing detector phenomenon in
this paper follows from the single fact that fine-tuning amplifies what
pretraining already encodes. The shared axis is read three ways (§2,
§3, §5) and parameterised in closed form (§4); the four findings
reduce to one statement and the central claim’s most predictive
consequence is the asymmetry of §5 against §4. Because the variant
zoo is calibration-equivalent on the axis, no thresholding choice on
that axis can move the bias only the closed-form rank-1 intervention
of §4 can, and it does so on the canonical detector by lifting
deployment TPR from
0.000
to
0.904
at
\mathrm{FPR}=1\%
. What we
describe in this paper is not a debiasing method that happens to work;
it is the only category of intervention we have evidence for on the
shared axis, because every variant in the evaluated zoo (canonical CE,
CC-loss, dealign-f2c, LoRA, full-FT) recalibrates rather than
reconstructs.
The practitioner consequence is direct: AI-text detectors currently
deployed against fluent formal-register populations are calibrating a
pretrained typicality axis these populations sit on the AI side of, and
variant-shopping across the standard recipe zoo will not move them —
the matched-TPR equivalence of §5 closes that route off-the-shelf.
What can move them is the axis-manipulating intervention parameterised
by the §4 closed-form predictor, which lifts deployment TPR from

0.000
to
0.904
on the canonical detector and transfers to three
third-party deployed detectors without retraining.

The same per-text Jacobian that parameterises fairness intervention also
parameterises attack cost: in preliminary results deferred to future
work, predictor-selected
\varepsilon
-to-flip correlates with measured
per-text adversarial robustness at Spearman
0.97
.
Three open questions follow from this duality.
First, the central claim is population/AUROC-level: per-text mechanisms
within the shared axis differ across architectures (Limitations), so the
shared axis at population level does not imply per-text mechanism
universality.
Second, the architectural typology that organises four scattered RoBERTa
anomalies under one prediction (Limitations) remains an observation at
four backbones; whether it is causally driven by pretraining objective,
model size, or pretraining data is open.
Third, the mechanism magnitude we report is HC3-anchored: the recipe
generalises cross-data while the magnitude does not (Limitations).
The same closed-form predictor that flags which texts are easy to debias
should, by symmetry, flag which texts are easy to attack; we leave the
attack-cost reading for future work.

Limitations

Our central claim is population/AUROC-level; within the shared typicality
axis, three architectures route per-text variance through three different
surface features.
On the NYT-vs-HC3 anchor, raw projection inheritance of fine-tuned
discrimination is universal at the pool level, but the per-text

3/3

0/3

density (Cohen’s

average sentence length (partial Pearson

),
and DeBERTa-v3 to a near-degenerate geometric subspace
(effective rank
4.72
vs
6.55/6.15
on ELECTRA / RoBERTa-base).
Cross-architecture Spearman correlation per-text is only
