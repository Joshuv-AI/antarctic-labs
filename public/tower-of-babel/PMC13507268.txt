Design of compact multi stub resonator based flexible monopole antenna for 5G wearable applications in N77 and N78 bands
Authors: Gaurav Kumar Soni, Dinesh Yadav, Ashok Kumar, Swati Varun Yadav, Manish Varun Yadav
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this article, a simply structured small-size multi stub resonator based flexible monopole wearable antenna is proposed for N77 (3.3–4.2 GHz) and N78 (3.3–3.8 GHz) bands of 5G communication that was design on the RT/Duroid 5880 substrate with a thickness of 20 mil (0.508 mm). The presented antenna configuration comprised of a monopole antenna and a vertically aligned symmetrically loaded multi stub resonator which excite resonant mode at 3.525 GHz for N77 and N78 bands of 5G applications. At the outset, it is fabricated, tested and its performance experimentally verified. It has the measured impedance bandwidth of about 1.3 GHz (3.15–4.45 GHz) and peak gain of 2.37 dBi at 3.475 GHz. Afterward, the presented monopole antenna is investigated by utilizing multi-layer tissue model for specific absorption rate (SAR) evaluation. At 3.5 GHz, the 1 g/10 g SAR values are 0.112/0.073 W/Kg, respectively, which is substantially under typical values. Lastly, the measured performance of the presented monopole wearable antenna is assessed at numerous on-body parts (i.e., wrist, chest, and leg) and diverse bending scenarios for flexibility and conformability analysis. With the small antenna footprint of 30 mm × 16 mm × 0.508 mm (0.35λ0 × 0.187λ0 × 0.0059λ0 at 3.5 GHz), wide bandwidth, low SAR values, flexible and conformal in nature makes it is suitable for modern 5G wearable applications.

Introduction

With the rapid growth of wireless body area networks (WBAN) and increasing attractiveness of wearable electronics or technologies, there is a resilient attention on emergent wearable antennas for the application in the diverse range of wearable 5G supported devices1,2. In 5G communication, a wearable antenna plays a key role for communication near human body with certain features including enabling high data rate, low-latency, efficient data transmission and wide-range connectivity among 5G networks and devices. The different wearable electronics devices which operate at sub-6 GHz bands of 5G technology are smart watches, fitness trackers, medical care units, Wi-Fi, smart clothing, and Internet of Things (IOT)3–6. The sub-6 GHz band (2–6 GHz) is crucial for 5G technology, providing a balance trade-off between coverage and performance. Key 5G frequency bands include N77 (3.3–4.2 GHz), N78 (3.3–3.8 GHz), and N79 (4.4–5.0 GHz), which help ensure reliable connectivity7–9. To support 5G and wearable technology, flexible antennas is an alternative solution over traditional rigid antennas due to numerous features namely lightweight, compact size, bendable, adaptable, foldable and conformal features which can fit around the human body or uneven surfaces or integration into clothing/garments, while still providing strong wireless communication without losing their performance10–12. Numerous types of wearable antennas based on flexible materials are explored by researchers such as conductive fabrics and stretchable substrates, ensuring good performance, durability and comfort through bent or stretched conditions. Among the essential 5G bands, N77 and N78 frequency bands play a major role in balancing coverage, capacity, and network efficiency, particularly in densely populated urban areas13–16.

In the literature, numerous categories of wearable antennas have been demonstrated by researchers. A deformed rectangular patch antenna with sawtooth partial ground and reflector on air gap PDMS substrate was reported for 3.5 GHz band11. This configuration was improved stretchability and bandwidth up to 20% and 13.65%, respectively. A rectangular microstrip textile antenna with a size of 40.35 mm×23.57 mm was reported for single (2.5 GHz) band and a gain of 1.73 dBi and SAR 1.2 W/Kg17. A defected ground structure deformed microstrip antenna with a size of 0.54λ0 × 0.44λ0 was reported18. The − 10 dB bandwidth of this antenna was very narrow for 2.45 GHz band, and the SAR was 0.249 W/Kg (1 g) and 0.137 W/Kg (10 g). A mobile terminal loop antenna was reported for low SAR19. The SAR is reduced about 26.1% and exhibit − 10 dB bandwidth of 320 MHz. A dual-polarized magneto-electric dipole antenna was reported for N77/N78 bands20. To stabilize the far-field radiations, fishtail-shaped corners of the horizontal patch were utilized. A metamaterial inspired tulip flower-shaped antenna was reported with a size of 0.35λ0 × 0.35λ0 and dual-band operation21. The dual-band response was achieved by using slotted frequency selective surface (FSS) and reflector techniques. A self-grounding slit antenna with an area of 44 mm×40 mm was discussed in22, which operated in the 2.38–2.81 GHz and 4.28–5.10 GHz bands. However, the antenna reported11–19 were low-profile and operated on the single frequency but suffer from a narrow bandwidth, large antenna footprint, low peak gains, and high SAR. Correspondingly, the antenna reported in20–22 were not low-profile and as they had complex multi-layer structures due to utilizing additional elements such as magneto-electric dipole, metamaterial structures, FSS and reflector surfaces. So, the design of wearable antenna having simple geometry, low-profile, wide bandwidth, low SAR values, and exhibit capability of flexibility, bendability, and conformability is a key research motivation to cover N77 and N78 bands of 5G communication.

In this work, a small size multi stub resonator based flexible monopole wearable antenna is demonstrated for the N77 and N78 frequency bands of 5G communication. The presented antenna has the operating frequency of 3.5 GHz, − 10 dB bandwidth of 1.3 GHz (3.15–4.45 GHz) and a peak gain of 2.37 dBi. This presented antenna is revealed its low-profile capability due to its small footprint of 0.35λ0 × 0.187λ0 × 0.0059λ0 at 3.5 GHz. Based on the numerical model, the presented antenna is fabricated and tested. For flexibility, conformability and wearability analysis, the presented antenna is explored a widespread investigation on a SAR, different bending conditions and its interaction with different on-body positions namely antenna placement on the wrist, chest, and leg accompanied by their results. The presented antenna has a low SAR level of 0.112/0.073 W/Kg for 1 g/10 g tissues, respectively, which underneath the regulated limit. The antenna is positioned in locality of the human body which demonstrating its good suitability for operation when bent at both the X-axis and Y-axis.

The entire research work is planned as follows: Sect. 2 outlines the presented multi stub resonator based wearable monopole antenna design, parametric analysis, placement of antenna on multilayer tissue model and SAR analysis. The antenna fabrication, measured and simulated results, bending analysis and on-body performance are investigated in Sect. 3. Finally, the comprehensive outcomes of the presented multi stub resonator based flexible monopole wearable antenna are provided in Sect. 4.

Antenna design and analysis

Presented multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna design

The schematic of the proposed multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna accompanied by zoomed view of labeled stubs resonator element and design axis is presented in Fig. 1. It comprised of a microstrip feed having width Wf, monopole antenna configuration and vertically aligned symmetrically loaded multi stub resonator. For 50Ω impedance matching network, a gap g is formed among the multi stub resonator and partial ground plane. The length of the feedline (Lf) is sum of the length of partial ground plane (Lg) and gap g. It is designed on the low-loss semi-flexible RT/Duroid 5880 substrate of dielectric constant (εr) = 2.2, loss tangent (tanδ) = 0.0009, and a thickness (say h) = 20 mil (0.508 mm). For stub resonator elements and partial ground plane of monopole antenna configuration, copper is utilized as conducting material with thickness of 0.035 mm. The presented antenna configuration has overall footprint of L×W×h = 30 mm×16 mm×0.508 mm (which corresponds to 0.35λ0 × 0.187λ0 × 0.0059λ0, where λ0 is the free space wavelength at 3.5 GHz). The antenna configuration is optimized using the CST Microwave Studio software. The presented multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna configuration is analyzed in four steps. The different progressive steps are considered as shown in the Fig. 2 (a) to (e) and their |S11| in Fig. 3. All the progressive step antennas (Antenna#1 to Antenna#4) are designed on the low-loss RT/Duroid 5880 substrate and analyzed through CST Microwave Studio simulation tool. In step 1 (Antenna#1), a 50Ω microstrip-line fed microstrip antenna loaded with multi stub resonator is depicted in Fig. 2(a) and Fig. 2(b) top and bottom view respectively. It can be seen that poor impedance matching occurred at 3.07 GHz with |S11| value of – 6.11 dB. A partial ground plane of Lg×W is utilized in step 2 (Antenna#2) instead of full ground plane in step 1 which leads to an improved impedance matching with |S11| value of – 29.21 dB, wider bandwidth and produce adequate resonance. Such antenna configuration is considered as multi stub resonator based monopole antenna. Antenna#2 exhibits the resonances at 3.625 GHz and 4.22 GHz with wide bandwidth 3.28–3.98 GHz and narrow bandwidth 4.1–4.43 GHz, respectively, but the 4.22 GHz band has poor impedance matching with |S11| value of – 12.14 dB. To enhance the impedance matching and bandwidth, an additional stub of W5×L5 is embedded in step 2 to get the configuration of step 3 (Antenna#3). Antennas#3 exhibit the resonance and impedance bandwidth of 3.625 GHz and 3.23–4.19 GHz, respectively. In step 4, to create the intended resonance at about 3.5 GHz and better impedance matching, the multi stub resonator length is augmented by forming deformation in multi stub resonator of step 3 using additional pair stubs of W6×L6 and W7×L7 symmetrically along y-axis as depicted in Fig. 2 (e). Such antenna configuration will alter the surface current on the deformed multi stub resonator which increases the current path to shift the 3.625 GHz resonance to lower side at about 3.525 GHz and |S11| value of − 43.39 dB. It exhibits the wide bandwidth of 887 MHz (3.183–4.07 GHz) and covers 5G N77 and N78 frequency bands, validating its suitability for 5G wearable applications. Therefore, the presented Antenna#4 configuration is utilized as our final design which consists of a multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna and a 50Ω microstrip feedline to cover 5G N77 and N78 bands.

Parametric study of presented multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna

To realize the impact of major geometrical parameters of the proposed multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna on |S11|, a parametric study is carried out. Dominated parameters such as multi stub resonator lower-arm length (L2) and upper-arm length (L4) of the antenna is presented here.

The impact of variation in multi stub resonator lower-arm (along y-axis) length L2 on |S11| is presented in Fig. 7. When the lower-arm stub length L2 increased from 5.6 mm to 7.6 mm with 0.5 mm step size, it can be perceived that the resonant frequency is shifted towards lower frequency side from 3.675 (|S11| = − 42.12 dB) to 3.4 GHz (|S11| = − 37.53 dB), respectively. In addition, the lower-band edge frequency, upper-band edge frequency and impedance bandwidth (IBW) is shifted downward from 3.3 to 4.28 GHz to 3.08–3.88 GHz. Based on the study, the optimal value of L2 = 6.6 mm is selected to cover the whole 5G N77 and N78 bands. Analysis of presented multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna with multi-layer tissue model

In order to investigate the presented monopole antenna for wearable on-body communication, a multi-layer (skin-fat-muscle-bone) tissue model with antenna is utilized as depicted in Fig. 9(a). The dielectric properties of each layer of the tissue model at 3.5 GHz are summarized in Table 4. The utilized size of multi-layer tissue model in simulation is 40 mm×40 mm. The simulated |S11| of the presented wearable monopole antenna regardless of multi-layer tissue model is depicted in Fig. 9(b). When the presented antenna with multi-layer tissue model is utilized then it provides |S11| value of − 20.9 dB at 3.625 GHz whereas |S11| value of − 43.39 dB at 3.525 GHz while presented antenna without multi-layer tissue model. It can be realized that the resonance frequency detuning from 3.525 GHz to 3.625 GHz and impedance mismatch is perceived due to loading of multi-layer tissue model in conjunction of presented wearable monopole antenna, which shows how multi-layer tissue model affects the antenna characteristics.

Figure 9(c) shows that far-field at 3.625 GHz in XZ-plane (phi = 00) and YZ-plane (phi = 900) of the presented monopole wearable antenna with multi-layer tissue model. In XZ-plane (phi = 00), it exhibit omnidirectional far-filed pattern whereas in YZ-plane (phi = 900) nearly bi-directional radiation features. The simulated far-field gain is 2.76 dBi at 3.625 GHz as shown in Fig. 9(d). From the examinations, it is apparent that the radiation features are enriched due to loading of multi-layer tissue layers.

Moreover, the SAR (W/kg) is a significant parameter to examine the performance of the monopole wearable antenna while utilizing the antenna near human body. It offers data about the amount of power absorbed by biological tissues when an antenna positioned on the human body. According to ICNIRP and FCC safety standards, the SAR limit for 1 g and 10 g biological tissues should be less than 1.6 W/kg and 2 W/kg, respectively. According to Fig. 10(a) and (b), the presented wearable monopole antenna with a multi-layer tissue model has SAR value of 0.112 W/kg for 1 g and 0.073 W/kg for 10 g tissues, which are well below the safety limits. This confirms that presented wearable monopole antenna is safe and suitable for wearable 5G applications. Fabrication and measurement

The presented multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna is fabricated on the Roger RT/Duroid 5880 substrate with 0.508 mm thickness and its fabricated prototype with a 50Ω SMA connector mounted to microstrip feed is depicted in Fig. 11(a). After the fabrication, |S11| of the prototype antenna is measured using a calibrated 2-port N5247A PNA. A worthy agreement among measured and simulated |S11| is found as depicted in Fig. 11(b). The measured |S11| value of the antenna is – 43.483 dB at 3.475 GHz (resonant frequency) whereas the simulated |S11| value is – 43.39 dB at 3.525 GHz, demonstrating excellent impedance matching. The presented antenna exhibits the measured bandwidth of 1.3 GHz (3.15–4.45 GHz) whereas simulated bandwidth of 0.882 GHz (3.183–4.065 GHz). The probable discrepancy in |S11| result (measured and simulated) is due to lossy boundary conditions, soldering joint among dual copper and the availability of 0.2 mm fabrication tolerance facility.

After investigation of |S11| and radiation patterns of the presented multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna, the bending effect is very important to check the flexibility, conformability and wearability performance. With the aim of enrich comfort, it is worthwhile for wearable antenna to adjust the shape of the human body. For the flexibility and conformability, the presented antenna bends in two principal planes, namely, X-axis and Y-axis, employing diverse cylindrical structures which are made by utilizing Tough Polylactic Acid (PLA) and foam materials with their diverse diameters. To evaluate the novelty of the presented multi stub resonator based monopole wearable antenna, a widespread study was conducted in contrast to the previously reported wearable antennas23–30 in connection with antenna electrical footprint, antenna types, substrate material, resonant frequency, |S11| value, peak antenna gain, and SAR (1 g and 10 g tissues) as outlined in Table 7. In24, DGS based ring slot antenna with gain of 6.27 dBi was reported but it suffer with a very narrow bandwidth, large electrical footprint of 0.58λ0 × 0.58λ0 × 0.00595λ0 and high SAR values. The hexagonal shaped microstrip antenna had the wide bandwidth but it suffers with a large electrical footprint of 0.467λ0 × 0.35λ0 × 0.00315λ0 and high SAR values. The antenna reported in28–30 have high gains but they suffers from the narrow bandwidths, large antenna electrical footprint and complex structures. In comparison to widespread study in Table 7, the presented multi stub resonator based flexible monopole wearable antenna reveals exceptional performance in respect of smallest antenna footprint of 0.35λ0 × 0.187λ0 × 0.0059λ0, low-profile, wide bandwidth of about 1.3 GHz except26, flexibility due to small thickness 0.0059λ0, minimum |S11| values of − 43 dB, moderate gain of 2.37 dBi at 3.475 GHz except28–30, and remarkably very low SAR value of 0.112 W/Kg (1 g)/0.073 W/Kg (10 g). With these excellent features, the presented multi stub resonator based flexible monopole wearable antenna is greatly favorable for wearable devices especially N77 and N78 bands of 5G communication.

Conclusion

This work presented a small size multi stub resonator based flexible monopole wearable antenna for the N77 and N78 bands for 5G applications. Initially, the antenna performances are discussed in terms of |S11|, radiation pattern and gain. It exhibits measured bandwidth of 1.3 GHz (3.15–4.45 GHz) and peak gain of 2.37 dBi at 3.475 GHz resonant frequency. The simulated radiation efficiency of the presented antenna is 98.99%, and the total efficiency is 98.94%. The realized gain of the antenna is 2.37 dBi. Afterward, it is investigated for SAR calculation, bending conditions, and on-body conditions (antenna placement on the wrist, chest, and leg with wearable clothes). The calculated SAR using multilayer tissue model is 0.112 W/Kg and 0.073 W/Kg for 1 g and 10 g tissues, respectively, which well within SAR safe limits. For flexibility, conformability and wearability, the presented antenna is demonstrated for bending in two principal planes, namely, X-axis and Y-axis, employing diverse cylindrical structures with their diverse diameters. The resonant frequency detuning and varied impedance bandwidth is perceived due to two principal bending but stable |S11| performance which still cover entire N77 and N78 bands. Correspondingly, the presented antenna reveals consistent |S11| across all placements during on-body measurements. The presented antenna has a small electrical footprint of 0.35λ0 × 0.187λ0 × 0.0059λ0, wide bandwidth, flexible and conformal in nature, stable |S11| performance during bending conditions and on-body circumstances which makes it appropriate for 5G wearable applications namely N77 and N78 frequency bands.
