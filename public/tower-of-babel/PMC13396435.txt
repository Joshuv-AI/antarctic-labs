Optimized IoT clustering and assignment in semi-synchronous federated learning
Authors: Hadi Farajvand, Nahideh Derakhshanfard, Abbas Mirzaei, Ali Ghaffari, Ali Asghar Pour Haji Kazem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study focuses on the important task of optimizing device clustering and assigning them to edge servers, while also implementing data redistribution in hierarchical semi-synchronous federated learning within the realm of advancing edge computing. Our research goal is to increase the performance and scalability of federated learning systems by improving resource allocation and data processing efficiency, which will in turn enhance edge computing frameworks. The current literature does not have thorough methods that can effectively combine model accuracy with optimal device clustering algorithms in hierarchical semi-synchronous federated learning, leading to below-par performance and inefficient use of resources. This difference highlights the need for creative measures that enhance not only model training accuracy but also the grouping of devices as opposed to current methods. The study utilizes a Graph Neural Network (GNN) to group IoT devices according to their hardware features and local datasets, then applies the K-means algorithm to create efficient device clusters. After that, Hybrid Data Redistribution is used to equalize local datasets in each cluster, and Proximal Policy resource allocation optimization algorithm is implemented to allocate devices to edge servers according to bandwidth usage, and energy consumption based on real-time updates, ultimately enabling hierarchical semi-synchronous federated learning to improve model training. The results show a 15% increase in clustering metrics compared to current algorithms, showcasing how our method improves device assignment and data redistribution in hierarchical semi-synchronous federated learning, addressing issues in model accuracy and resource optimization.

Introduction

THE Federated Learning (FL) is now seen as a cru-cial model for allowing cooperative machine learning among decentralized Internet of Things (IoT) devices while also protecting data privacy1,2. This decentralized method reduces the hazards linked to centralized data gath-ering, making it very appropriate for various IoT settings limited in resources3,4. The importance of FL lies in its capacity to improve model accuracy and scalability through effective device heterogeneity management and resource al-location optimization5,6. Although FL has its benefits, there are various obstacles that hinder its common use in IoT systems. Non-identical and non-independent data distributions among devices can cause lower model performance and convergence problems7,8. Moreover, energy usage and delay are still significant issues, particularly in extensive hierarchical FL arrangements1,6. Previous research has suggested different approaches, like enhanced scheduling algorithms and resource optimiza-tion frameworks, but there are still gaps in achieving the ideal balance between efficiency and accuracy2,9. Driven by these obstacles, this research seeks to enhance FL techniques by tackling the inherent constraints of existing methods. Expanding upon existing hierarchical and clustered FL frameworks1,2, we present a new method for device scheduling and resource allocation that improves scalability and lowers energy usage while maintaining model accu-racy. Moreover, we incorporate sophisticated reinforcement learning methods to dynamically adjust to changing network conditions and data distributions5,10. By addressing the deficiencies highlighted in previous studies, this research aids in the advancement of stronger and more effective FL systems designed specifically for diverse IoT settings.

Federated Learning (FL), Edge Computing, and the Inter-net of Things (IoT) are the essential components of modern decentralized intelligence systems. FL allows collaborative training of models on multiple devices without centralizing data, improving data privacy and decreasing communication overhead10. Edge Computing supports Federated Learning11,12 by handling data nearer to its origin, reducing delay and enabling immediate decision-making crucial for IoT applica-tions13. The IoT ecosystem depends on these technologies to provide scalable and efficient solutions14,15. It is described as a large network of diverse devices7,16.

FL works based on a decentralized model training ap-proach, where individual devices train local models that are then centrally aggregated to create a global model10,17. Over time, FL has developed to tackle issues like data diversity and communication effectiveness. Hierarchical FL architectures represent a notable progress by adding intermediate aggrega-tion layers to improve scalability and lessen the workload on central servers1,6. These structured systems help stream-line device scheduling and resource management, which are essential for the success of extensive IoT implementations. Edge Computing has a critical role in handling and ana-lyzing data produced by IoT devices. Edge servers manage data clustering and device allocation to maximize resource usage and reduce latency2,8. Current techniques for device clustering frequently depend on algorithms such as K-means and reinforcement learning to categorize devices according to their similarity and resource availability9,18. Nevertheless, these strategies may face challenges in handling scalability and adaptability in changing network environments19. Redistributing data is vital to evenly distributing compu-tational work and ensuring fair use of resources within the network. Present approaches in federated and edge comput-ing environments prioritize enhancing data flow efficiency and reducing redundancy3,20. Even with these attempts, there are still notable deficiencies in combining clustering, device allocation, and data redistribution into a unified sys-tem that can adjust to different network circumstances5,21. Various obstacles continue to exist in the implementation of Federated Learning in Internet of Things (IoT) systems22,23. Combining clustering, device allocation, and data redistribu-tion separately can result in less than optimal performance and inefficiencies5,24. Moreover, expanding hierarchical FL systems to support a growing number of devices without compromising efficiency is still a significant challenge1,6. The management of resources and stability of FL mod-els are made more complex by dynamic and unpredictable network conditions25,26.

Disagreements in the industry frequently involve the com-promises between synchronization techniques and the equi-librium of data privacy and model performance. Discussions persist on whether synchronous or semi-synchronous training techniques provide the optimal balance between speed of convergence and utilization of resources27. Furthermore, strict adherence to data privacy measures may impede the ef-fectiveness and precision of FL models, leading to continued debates on the best methods for preserving privacy28. There is a requirement for thorough research in unexplored territories that considers optimal clustering, device alloca-tion, and data redistribution in hierarchical semi-synchronous FL frameworks. Furthermore, a lack of studies implementing these combined techniques in actual IoT situations hinders the usefulness and expansiveness of current solutions29,30.

Although hierarchical federated learning (FL) frameworks have improved scalability and efficiency in IoT settings, cur-rent methods frequently view device clustering, assignment, and data redistribution as separate steps5. This division results in less than optimal performance, especially in net-works that are dynamic and heterogeneous, experiencing fluctuations in resource availability and data distributions9,31,32. Imbalanced computational loads and inefficient resource utilization cause higher latency and lower model accuracy. The importance of this issue is found in how it affects the implementation of Federated Learning in IoT systems on a large scale. Poor integration of clustering and assignment mechanisms limits the scalability and responsiveness of FL models due to ineffective adaptation to changing network conditions8,33. Additionally, lacking effective methods for re-distributing data can lead to uneven workloads on devices, in-tensifying energy usage and decreasing system performance overall1. If not dealt with, these restrictions could hinder the ef-fective use of FL in important IoT fields like smart cities and industrial automation, where quick and precise data processing is crucial13. The absence of a unified structure that can adjust to changes in the network hinders the possible advantages of FL, such as improved privacy and decreased communication costs10,34.

The main goal of this research is to create a unified frame-work that enhances device grouping, arrangement, and allo-cation in a hierarchical semi-synchronous federated learning (FL) system for diverse IoT settings35. Our goal is to improve efficiency and resource usage by using GNN-Kmeans to cluster devices and assign those clusters to edge servers1,2.

Another goal is to apply advanced reinforcement learn-ing methods, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), for the dynamic scheduling and assigning of devices. This method gives priority to devices according to data diversity tags and resource availability, adjusting to varying network conditions for enhancing model convergence and accuracy5,36,37.

Furthermore, our goal is to integrate analysis of di-verse data and labeling into the decision-making process for scheduling, to prioritize devices that provide valuable and varied data in the FL process8,27. Strategies like FedProx and Gradual Mixing will be applied to manage variations in devices and improve convergence, alleviating problems associated with non-IID data distributions29,38.

Our hypothesis is that combining optimized device group-ing with GNN-Kmeans, dynamic scheduling and assignment using PPO, and stabilization methods such as FedProx and Gradual Mixing will result in a notable decrease in latency and enhancement in model accuracy within hierarchical semi-synchronous39 FL systems. This integration tackles the scalability, efficiency, and adaptability hurdles in diverse IoT environments. The study aims to improve the effectiveness and expandability of FL in IoT, addressing previous research gaps and advancing decentralized intelligence systems1,5.

Methodology

This study employs a simulation-based experimental ap-proach to develop and evaluate a comprehensive framework aimed at optimizing device clustering, scheduling, and as-signment within a hierarchical semi-synchronous federated learning system tailored for heterogeneous IoT environ-ments.

Hierarchical semi-synchronous federated learning framework

The proposed framework is structured in a multi-tiered hi-erarchy comprising IoT devices, edge servers, and a central cloud server. Edge servers manage clusters of devices, co-ordinating local training and aggregation. The central server periodically synchronizes with the edge servers to update the global model. A semi-synchronous mechanism is im-plemented, allowing devices and edge servers to conduct training and updates at their own pace while maintaining sufficient synchronization to ensure model convergence.

Clustering and device assignment strategy

To effectively manage the diversity and dynamics of IoT de-vices, a Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based K-means clus-tering algorithm is utilized. Devices are represented as nodes within a graph, with edges capturing relationships and simi-larities based on features such as computational capabilities, network latency, data distribution, and energy availability. The GNN processes this graph to generate embeddings for each device, encapsulating complex relationships and feature interactions. These embeddings are subsequently clustered using the K-means algorithm, grouping devices with sim-ilar characteristics and relationships.Clusters are allocated to edge servers by considering factors such as geographical proximity and resource availability, thereby promoting ef-ficient communication and computation within clusters and optimizing resource utilization across the network.

Data redistribution and labeling based on diversity

To enhance model generalization and address non-IID data distributions, the local data on each device is analyzed for diversity. Diversity metrics, including label distribution entropy and statistical distance measures, are employed to evaluate how well each device’s data represents the overall distribution. Based on these metrics, devices are categorized to inform scheduling decisions.Data redistribution strate-gies are implemented to balance computational loads and improve data diversity within clusters. Techniques such as intra-cluster data sharing, synthetic data generation, and data augmentation are utilized to enhance the diversity of data available for local training.

Dynamic device scheduling and reassignment

A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learn-ing agent is employed as a scheduling agent to dynamically select devices for participation in each training round. The agent takes into account data diversity labels, resource avail-ability, device reliability, and network conditions to make optimal scheduling decisions. This dynamic scheduling pri-oritizes devices that contribute valuable and diverse data, thereby improving model convergence and accuracy.The sys-tem supports dynamic device reassignment, allowing devices to be reallocated to different clusters or edge servers in response to changes in device states, network conditions, or data distributions. This adaptability enhances the system’s resilience and efficiency in the face of environmental fluctuations.

Related work

Federated Learning (FL) has attracted tremendous attention as a decentralized machine learning paradigm, particularly suitable for resource-constrained and privacy-sensitive envi-ronments such as the Internet of Things (IoT). Many works have been devoted to various aspects of FL in an effort to improve its efficiency, scalability, security, and adaptability in heterogeneous IoT networks. This section performs an in-depth analysis of the existing body of literature by categoriz-ing the developments into various principal domains: Device Selection and Clustering, Energy Efficiency and Resource Management, Security and Privacy, Split and Hierarchical Learning, and Integration with Emerging Technologies.

Device selection and clustering in federated learning

Optimal selection and grouping of devices play a crucial role in enhancing Federated Learning (FL) efficiency in diverse Internet of Things (IoT) networks. Zhang and colleagues3 introduced a spectrum allocation system that saves energy while also selecting robust devices to tackle the issues with non-identically distributed data, resulting in quicker conver-gence and improved training speed. In the same way, Xabier Sáez-de-Cámara and colleagues4 presented a clustered FL structure to detect anomalies in massive and diverse IoT networks, improving model stability and decreasing privacy concerns by using device grouping. Fei Dou and colleagues5 expanded FL by incorporating few-shot learning for indoor positioning on devices, support-ing different types of devices while safeguarding privacy. Yiran Shi and colleagues8 created the Quality-aware De-vice Selection (QDS) algorithm to achieve a balance between training efficiency and accuracy in federated edge learning (FEEL), resulting in enhanced training accuracy and de-creased delays. Furthermore, Fangtong Zhou and colleagues21 specifically studied the improvement of scalability in non-IID FL scenarios by selecting coresets and reducing dimensionality for efficient data distribution estimation. These research studies emphasize the significance of care-fully choosing devices and grouping them strategically to reduce data differences and improve FL effectiveness. Nev-ertheless, challenges like expensive computations and depen-dence on precise device profiling continue to be widespread4,8,21.

Energy efficiency and resource management

Energy efficiency is an important issue when deploying FL on IoT networks. Rami Hamdi et al.6 focused on energy-efficient hierarchical FL in heterogeneous networks through wireless energy transfer (WET) to lower energy usage and enhance training efficiency. In a similar manner, Zhuoer Hu and colleagues8 presented the Clustered-FedProx method for detecting anomalies in Industrial IoT (IIoT), improving energy efficiency and ensuring stable training.

Wei Yang and colleagues9 created the cFL method, which uses cosine similarity and edge-based clustering to decrease communication overhead by 60% and enhance convergence time by 10.3%. Furthermore, Zheng Lin and colleagues40 introduced AdaptSFL, an adaptive split fed-erated learning framework that enhances model aggregation and splitting techniques, greatly decreasing training time in edge networks with limited resources. Split learning and hierarchical learning

Split Learning (SL) is an alternative to FL by splitting models across devices and edge servers for better utilization of re-sources. Zheng Lin et al.41 proposed the EPSL framework for parallel split learning, which lowers training latency and communication overhead. Wen Wu et al.37,42 derived the CPSL (Cluster-based Parallel SL) framework, which further reduces training latency in a non-IID environment. Further, Seychi Lin et al.43 suggested SplitLoRA, com-bining SL with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for the ef-ficient fine-tuning of large language models, showing a re-duced convergence time and resource requirement. However, these techniques usually involve a high amount of compu-tation and poor generalization in many real heterogeneous settings41–43.

Integration with emerging technologies

FL integration with other emerging technologies, such as blockchain and deep reinforcement learning (DRL), has shown promise in improving FL capabilities. Romano Fan-tacci and Benedetta Picano44 integrated the Device-to-Device (D2D) communication mechanism and incentive mechanism into FL frameworks to improve the convergence time and energy efficiency in 6G networks. Besides, Wei et al.36 used digital twin and DRL to optimize FL in IIoT, which achieved better training efficiency and lower convergence time.

Zheng Lin et al.45 introduced blockchain into hierar-chical FL for IoT to enhance robustness and convergence efficiency but suffered from high computational overhead. Aristeidis Karras et al.46 studied Federated NAS (Fedo-rAS) for dealing with system heterogeneity and obtained substantial performance improvement but was burdened by the scalability issue.

These integrations enhance FL’s functionality and effi-ciency but require careful consideration of implementation complexity and scalability36,44–46.

Gaps and weaknesses in current research

System model

The architecture to enhance federated learning for IoT set-tings involves sophisticated device clustering and assignment techniques, data reconfiguration, and semi-synchronous fed-erated learning. The aim is to mitigate heterogeneity, re-source limitation, and communication overhead in large IoT networks with emphasis on scalability and reliability. The basic idea involves grouping Internet of Things (IoT) devices based on their computational capacities, energy availability, and network connections. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), such as Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and Graph Attention Networks (GATs), infer high-dimensional embed-dings that represent the features of devices and their topolog-ical correlations. The embeddings are then fed to a clustering algorithm like KMeans or Agglomerative Clustering, which groups similar devices, hence simplifying resource allocation and reducing complexity. Clusters are typically assigned to edge servers, resulting in a list of suitable edge servers for devices with similar demands. This improves device deployment and load balancing. Dynamic mapping to edge servers also reduces communication overhead and optimizes resource utilization using a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent learned in a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework. The PPO agent learns the optimal policy of mapping device-to-edge-server through a reward func-tion that negatively weights communication overhead and resource wastage but positively weights load balancing and bandwidth utilization efficiency. The back-off mechanism redistributes devices to less congested servers when an edge server is congested. Heterogeneity and sparsity of data in IoT devices are significant challenges for federated learning. In this paper, we explore the use of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for data augmentation. A GAN trained on actual aggregated data generates synthetic samples for devices with small datasets, enhancing diversity and quan-tity. Better local model training results in a stronger global federated model. Semi-synchronization in federated learning balances efficiency and reliability. FedProx is used by all the edge servers to reduce heterogeneity impacts of data when there is localized model training. The edge servers’ locally trained models are sent asynchronously to the global server, which aggregates updates sporadically with Federated Averaging (FedAvg). This reduces idle time in addition to speeding up model convergence. In all, the approach miti-gates key federated learning challenges in IoT settings by reducing communication overhead through device cluster-ing, ensuring optimum resource allocation through dynamic device assignment using DRL-based PPO agents, mitigating data imbalance through data redistribution using GAN-based algorithms, and achieving a trade-off between asynchronous updates and global aggregation at regular intervals. The method has four main steps: device clustering, device assignment, data redistribution, and semi-synchronous federated learning.

Device clustering

Optimization of resource allocation and communication pro-cesses in federated learning within Internet of Things (IoT) settings is of utmost importance. To tackle these issues, a device clustering methodology based on graph-based feature extraction coupled with advanced clustering algorithms is introduced. The core hypothesis of this research work posits that graph-based feature extraction methodologies combined with either KMeans or Agglomerative Clustering are capa-ble of providing groupings of IoT devices that accurately represent intrinsic characteristics in both computation and network communication. This grouping further optimizes resource allocation and efficiency in communication within the context of a federated learning setting Fig. 1.

Semi-synchronous federated learning

This paper optimizes the federated learning framework in IoT environments using a semi synchronous paradigm. This approach balances training efficiency and model consistency by allowing edge servers to update models asynchronously and aggregate the global model periodically. The central hypothesis is that this paradigm accelerates convergence and improves overall model performance.

Let k be the number of edge servers and E= {e1,e2,…,ek} denote the set of these edge servers. The global model at aggregation step t is defined as Mglobal(t), whereas the model at edge server e after the local training at step t is denoted by Me(t). The weight or aggregation rate assigned to every edge server in the process of global aggregation is defined by αe. To address data scarcity in edge devices, synthetic samples are generated and incorporated into the local training process. In the revised approach, instead of assigning random labels, a more informed labeling strategy is adopted. Specifically, labels are assigned based on the local data distribution or inferred using the current local model through pseudo-labeling. To ensure reliability, only high-confidence predictions are selected for training, thereby reducing noise and preventing performance degradation.

Proposed method enhancements

Conclusion

The main challenges discussed in using federated learning in IoT, such as heterogeneity of devices, sparsity of data, scarcity of resources, and communication overhead, are ad-dressed in this article. An end-to-end system framework for integrating device clustering, data redistribution, dynamic device assignment, and semi-synchronous federated learning is offered to enable efficient and effective federated learning in large-scale heterogeneous IoT systems.

The primary benefit of this methodology lies in its struc-tured division into well-defined components. Firstly, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) receive high-dimensional embed-dings of Internet of Things (IoT) devices to facilitate ef-fective load balancing and resource allocation. Secondly, a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)-inspired Proximal Pol-icy Optimization (PPO) agent provides optimal device-edge resource assignments for reducing communication overhead. Finally, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) enhance data augmentation for alleviating issues with class imbalance and data sparsity in IoT environments, resulting in improved training of local models.

Semi-synchronous federated learning balances the flexi-bility of asynchronous updates and the stability of periodic global aggregations, trading off convergence rate for robust-ness. It maximizes scalability and efficiency, enabling fed-erated learning for large-scale heterogeneous IoT networks. It is particularly valuable for safety-critical applications in industrial IoT, smart cities, and healthcare, where timeliness and reliability matter.

There are some limitations still. Though this framework supports considerations such as heterogeneity, load balanc-ing, and data augmentation, it needs further optimization for high dynamism or high-churning network environments. Energy consumption along with privacy-preserving solutions need to be studied further in a focused manner. Adaptive clus-tering techniques, state-of-the-art privacy-enhancing mecha-nisms, and device failure or data integrity handling need to be investigated by future studies.
