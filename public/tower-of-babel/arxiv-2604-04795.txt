Sample Complexity for Markov Decision Processes and Stochastic Optimal Control with Static Risk Measures
Cristian Chávez, Yan Li

Abstract
We present an elementary state augmentation method for a class of static risk measure applied to the total cost for both Markov decision processes and stochastic optimal control, such that dynamic programming equations can be derived on the augmented space. Through this we discuss the sample complexities of these two problems for both finite-horizon and infinite-horizon settings. We demonstrate the application of the proposed approach through studying distributionally robust functional generated by ϕ \phi -divergences including conditional value-at-risk.

Introduction

We consider in this manuscript the following risk-averse sequential decision-making problem

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t})\right)

(1.1)

and its corresponding infinite-horizon counterpart

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})\right).

(1.2)

Here the risk functional
\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}
is defined through

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}(X)=\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[f_{\theta}(X)\right],

(1.3)

for some parameterized nonlinear function
f_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with parameter space
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Depending on how the distribution of
S_{t+1}
is modeled given
X_{t}
and
A_{t}
,
we will consider problem (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)
in the context of Markov decision process (MDP) and stochastic optimal control (SOC).
Unless stated otherwise, we consider the set of randomized history-dependent policy class
\Pi
, i.e.,
\pi\in\Pi:H_{t}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{A}_{t}}
, where
\Delta_{\mathcal{A}_{t}}
denotes the probability distributions supported over the action space,
and
H_{t}
denotes the history up to stage
t
.
The above expectation is then taken w.r.t the probability law of data process
\left\{(S_{t},A_{t})\right\}_{t\geq 0}
where
S_{0}=s
,
A_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|H_{t})
under policy
\pi
.
We make the following assumption on the nonlinear function
f_{\theta}
and the parameter space
\Theta
throughout the rest of the manuscript.

Assumption 1
.

We assume that
f_{\theta}
is
(L_{\Theta},L_{C})
-Lipschitz, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\lvert f_{\theta}(z)-f_{\theta}(z^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq L_{C}\left\lvert z-z^{\prime}\right\rvert,~~\left\lvert f_{\theta}(z)-f_{\theta^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert\leq L_{\Theta}\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right\rVert,~\forall z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta.

In addition,
\Theta
is bounded in
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
with radius
R_{\Theta}
.

Below we discuss some examples of risk measures that admits the form of (
1.3
).

Example 1.1

(Conditional value-at-risk)
.

Let
X
be a random variable on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. Its conditional value-at-risk with risk level
\alpha\in(0,1)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{CVaR}_{\alpha}(X)=\inf_{\theta}\left\{\theta+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[X-\theta\right]_{+}\right\}.

In this case we have

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(z)=\theta+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}[z-\theta]_{+},

and
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
If in addition that
\left\lvert X\right\rvert\leq b
almost surely, one can take
\Theta=[-b,b]
.

The conditional value-at-risk can be viewed as a special case of the distributionally robust functional generated by
\phi
-divergence based ambiguity sets
[
28
]
.

Example 1.2

(
\phi
-divergence distributionally robust functional)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}
be a convex lower semicontinuous function with
\phi(0)=1
and
\phi(x)=+\infty
for
x<0
.
Let
X
be a random variable on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
.
For
\tau\geq 0
, consider the set of density function

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}=\left\{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}:\int_{\Omega}\phi(\zeta(w))dP(w)\leq\tau\right\},

where
\mathfrak{D}
denotes the set of probability density function w.r.t
P
.
The distributionally robust functional corresponding to
\phi
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X)=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}}\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\zeta(\omega)dP(\omega),~\mathrm{s.t.}~\zeta\in\mathfrak{M}.

(1.4)

From
[
28
]
, (
1.4
) admits the following dual problem

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X)=\inf_{\lambda>0,\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\lambda\tau+\mu+\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(\lambda\phi)^{*}(X-\mu)\right]\right\},

(1.5)

where
\lambda,\mu
correspond to dual variables associated with constraints in (
1.4
),
and
\phi^{*}
denotes the conjugate of
\phi
.
In this case, one can take
\theta=(\lambda,\mu)
, and

\displaystyle f_{(\lambda,\mu)}(z)=\lambda\tau+\mu+(\lambda\phi)^{*}(z-\mu).

Clearly, taking
\Theta=\left\{(\lambda,\mu):\lambda>0,\mu\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
does not satisfy Assumption
1
.
On the other hand, since Slater condition holds for (
1.4
), (
1.5
) is solvable with finite optimal dual variables
(\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})
.
3
3

3

It should be noted that such an approach only establish the finiteness of the dual variables. Establishing a concrete bound on the dual variable often depends on the concrete form of
\phi
and the underlying reference probability
P
.

Subsequently in (
1.5
), it suffices to take
\Theta
as a bounded set around the optimal dual variables.

It could be worth mentioning that the risk functional
\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}
defined in (
1.3
) is law-invariant, and consequently does not include Wasserstein distance-based distributionally robust functional
[
17
,
11
,
6
]
.

Risk measure of the form (
1.1
) (resp. (
1.2
)) is referred to as the static risk measure in the context of sequential decision making, as the decision maker considers the risk applied to the total cost accumulated acrosss all stages.
In contrast to the risk-neutral setting, for the static risk measure a central challenge is the lack of dynamic programming (DP) equations except for some special cases
[
10
]
.
To address this challenge, DP equations defined with the assistance of augmented state variables have been introduced for risk-averse MDPs
[
2
,
8
,
12
,
3
]
.
In particular, for conditional value-at-risk (Example
1.1
),
[
2
]
proposes DP equations based on its variational representation and subsequently define the augmented state variable as the accumulated total cost,
and
[
8
]
proposes DP equations based on the dual representations and defining the augmented state variable as the risk level.
4
4

4

It has been recently pointed out in
[
12
]
that the DP equations in
[
8
]
only hold for policy evaluation.

The approach in
[
2
]
has since been generalized to general nonlinear utility functions
[
3
,
35
]
, optimized certainty equivalent
[
31
]
, and a class of spectral risk measures
[
1
]
.
With DP equations in place, there has been an active line of research in developing the computational methods for risk-averse MDPs
[
8
,
9
,
7
]
, and in determining the sample complexities of finding an approximate optimal control policy when the underlying model is unknown and has to be learned from the data
[
30
,
35
,
31
,
19
]
.

It should be also noted that a related line of research considers nested risk measure, where risk is iteratively constructed across the history of the decision process, and hence DP equations can be naturally derived.
We refer to
[
24
,
16
]
for relevant discussions on MDPs and to
[
26
]
for SOCs.
The nested risk measure for MDPs is also closely related to robust MDPs with
(\mathrm{s,a})
-rectangular ambiguity sets
[
13
,
20
]
through dual representation of coherent risk measures
[
25
]
.
Correspondingly, computational methods and sample complexities have been studied for a variety of ambiguity sets
[
21
,
33
,
34
,
32
,
15
]
.

In this manuscript, we propose an elementary construction of DP equations for (
1.1
), based on augmenting the state variable by the accumulated total cost. The proposed DP equations can be applied to both the MDP and SOC models without essential changes.
Different from
[
2
]
, the underlying idea behind our construction is that the nonlinearity of
f_{\theta}
in (
1.3
) can be handled through simple telescoping operations, which produces dense cost functions.
Through this one can, in a straightforward manner, define a MDP (resp. SOC) over the augmented state space with a modified cost function.
A similar cost function has also been recently studied in the context of conditional value-at-risk
[
18
]
for infinite-horizon MDPs.
Through the proposed DP equations, we investigate the sample complexities of (
1.1
) for both the MDP and SOC models.
We obtain a sample complexity of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\left\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert T^{4}/\epsilon^{2})
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{5}/\epsilon^{2})
) for finding an
\epsilon
-optimal policy for the MDP (resp. SOC) model,
where
T
denote the number of horizons.
Extensions to infinite-horizon setting (
1.2
) for both models are discussed with a similar approach.
The simplicity of the proposed approach also offer some improvement on the sample complexities over existing development on risk-averse MDPs, with improved dependence on
T
compared to
[
35
]
when specialized to nonlinear utility functions, and on
(\left\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\right\rvert,\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert)
compared to
[
1
]
when specialized for a class of spectral risk measures.
The approach we take in this manuscript suggests that the risk-averse MDP (resp. SOC) with the considered class of static risk measures may be not substantially more challenging than that of the risk-neutral case in terms of its sample complexity, and the obtained
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\left\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert T^{4}/\epsilon^{2})
can be potentially improved to
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\left\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert T^{3}/\epsilon^{2})
with the optimal methods developed for risk-neutral MDPs
[
29
]
.

2
Risk-averse Markov Decision Processes

In this section, we will proceed to discuss risk-averse MDP for both finite-horizon and infinite-horizon settings.
For any MDP instance
\mathcal{M}
, we use
\Pi(\mathcal{M})
to indicate the set of randomized history-dependent policies,
and
\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\mathcal{M})
for the set of randomized Markovian policies.

2.1
Finite-horizon Risk-averse Markov Decision Processes

Consider a finite-horizon MDP

\mathcal{M}=(\left\{{\mathcal{S}}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T},\left\{\mathcal{A}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T},\left\{\mathbb{P}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1},\left\{c_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T})
.
Here
{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
denotes the finite state space at stage
t
,
\mathcal{A}_{t}
denotes its corresponding finite action space,

c_{t}:{\mathcal{S}}_{t}\times\mathcal{A}_{t}\to[0,1]

denotes the cost function, and

\mathbb{P}_{t}:{\mathcal{S}}_{t}\times\mathcal{A}_{t}\to\Delta_{{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}}

denotes the transition kernel that maps a given state action pair onto the set of probability distributions
\Delta_{{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}}
supported over
{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}
.
For notational convenience, we will occasionally write
\mathbb{P}
in short for
\left\{\mathbb{P}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
.
We seek to find the optimal policy of the following risk,

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t})\right)

(2.1)

where
the data process
\left\{(S_{t},A_{t})\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
is generated by
S_{0}=s
,
A_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|H_{t})
,
S_{t+1}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|S_{t},A_{t})
,
where
H_{t}=(S_{0},A_{0},\ldots,S_{t})
denotes the history up to stage
t
.
For ease of presentation, we define the value function

\displaystyle V^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s)=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[f_{\theta}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t})\right)|S_{0}=s\right].

(2.2)

That is,

\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)=\min_{\theta\in\Theta}V_{0}^{\pi,\theta}(s)
.
It is then clear that (
1.1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}V_{0}^{\pi,\theta}(s).

(2.3)

A potential challenge in solving (
2.1
) is the nonlinearity of
f_{\theta}
in (
2.2
), which breaks the dynamic decomposition of (
2.2
) that underlies the dynamic equations associated with risk-neutral MDPs.
Our first observation in this section is the following simple reformulation that allows us to define the dynamic equations over an augmented state space.

Definition 2.1

(Finite-horizon augmented MDP)
.

For any
\theta\in\Theta
, the augmented MDP
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}
is defined as follows.
The state space is
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}_{t}={\mathcal{S}}_{t}\times\mathcal{X}_{t}
, where
\mathcal{X}_{t}=\mathbb{R}
, and the action space is
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{t}=\mathcal{A}_{t}
.
The cost function and the transition kernel are defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s_{t},x_{t},a_{t})

\displaystyle=f_{\theta}({x_{t}+c_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})})-f_{\theta}(x_{t}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{t}(s_{t+1},x_{t+1}|s_{t},x_{t},a_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{t}(s_{t+1}|s_{t},a_{t})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x_{t+1}=x_{t}+c_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})\right\}}.

(2.4)

From (
2.1
), it is not difficult to see that the set of history-dependent, randomized policies for
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}
is also
\Pi(\mathcal{M})
.
Then for any policy
\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})
and any
\theta\in\Theta
, we define the value function as

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s,x)=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(S_{t},X_{t},A_{t})|S_{0}=s,X_{0}=x\right].

(2.5)

With Definition
2.1
in place, we make the following observation.

Proposition 2.1
.

We have for any
\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})
,

\displaystyle V^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s)=\widetilde{V}^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s,0)+f_{\theta}(0),~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

Proof.

It is immediate to verify that
for any policy
\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{{\pi},\theta}_{0}(s,0)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{{\pi}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(S_{t},X_{t},A_{t})|S_{0}=s,X_{0}=0\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{{\pi}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}f_{\theta}({X_{t}+c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t})})-f_{\theta}(X_{t})|S_{0}=s,X_{0}=0\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\mathbb{E}^{{\pi}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}\left[f_{\theta}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t})\right)|S_{0}=s\right]-f_{\theta}(0)=V^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s)-f_{\theta}(0),

where
(a)
follows from the definition of
\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{t}
in (
2.1
).
The proof is then completed.
∎

In view of Proposition
2.1
, one can immediately obtain the following dynamic programming equations for the inner minimization of (
2.3
) through the augmented MDP
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Define the optimal cost-to-go function in the augmented MDP
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(s_{t},x_{t})=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{i=t}^{T}\widetilde{c_{i}}^{\theta}(S_{i},X_{i},A_{i})|S_{t}=s_{t},X_{t}=x_{t}\right].

Then we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{\theta}_{t}(s_{t},x_{t})=\min_{a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s_{t},x_{t},a_{t})+\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{s_{t+1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}}\mathbb{P}_{t}(s_{t+1}|s_{t},a_{t})\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}(s_{t+1},x_{t}+c_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})),~\forall t=0,\ldots T;s_{t}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}.

(2.6)

In addition, the policy
{\pi^{*}_{\theta}}
defined as

\displaystyle\pi^{*}_{\theta}(s_{t})\in\mathop{\mathrm{Argmin}}_{a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left\{\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s_{t},x_{t},a_{t})+\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{s_{t+1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}}\mathbb{P}_{t}(s_{t+1}|s_{t},a_{t})\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}(s_{t+1},x_{t}+c_{t}(s_{t},a_{t}))\right\}

(2.7)

is an optimal policy of
\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}V^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s)
.

Proof.

The first part of the claim (
2.6
) is a direct consequence of dynamic equations applied to the augmented MDP
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}
.
The rest of the claim follows from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Given Proposition
2.2
, we can obtain the dynamic equations for (
2.1
) as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\theta^{*}
be an optimal solution of (
2.3
).
Consider
\left\{\widetilde{V}^{\theta^{*}}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
defined as in (
2.6
)
and
\pi^{*}_{\theta^{*}}
defined in (
2.7
).
Then we have

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)=\widetilde{V}^{\theta^{*}}_{0}(s,0)+f_{\theta^{*}}(0),

and
\pi^{*}_{\theta^{*}}
is an optimal policy.

Remark 2.1
.

Despite considering the set of randomized history-dependent policies in (
2.1
), it might be worth noting that there exist an optimal policy
\pi^{*}
for (
2.1
) that is history-dependent only through the accumulated cost
x_{t}
, and non-randomized.

In what follows, we establish the dynamic programming equations
of
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)
for any
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta})
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
\theta\in\Theta
and
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta})
, define

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s_{t},x_{t})=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{i=t}^{T}\widetilde{c_{i}}^{\theta}(S_{i},X_{i},A_{i})|S_{t}=s_{t},X_{t}=x_{t}\right].

Then we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{\pi,\theta}_{t}(s_{t},x_{t})=\mathbb{E}_{a_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{t},x_{t})}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s_{t},x_{t},a_{t})+\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{s_{t+1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}}\mathbb{P}_{t}(s_{t+1}|s_{t},a_{t})\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s_{t+1},x_{t}+c_{t}(s_{t},a_{t}))\right],

(2.8)

for
t=0,\ldots T
and any
s_{t}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}.

In addition,
denote
\theta_{\pi}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Argmin}}_{\theta\in\Theta}\widetilde{V}^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s)
.
We have for any
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\widetilde{M}_{\theta})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)=\widetilde{V}^{\pi,\theta_{\pi}}_{0}(s,0)+f_{\theta^{\pi}}(0).

Proof.

The first part of the claim (
2.8
) follows from standard dynamic programming principle applied to the augmented MDP.
The rest of the claim follows from the definition of
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
,
\theta_{\pi}
and Proposition
2.1
.
∎

2.2
Sample Complexity of Risk-averse Policy Evaluation and Optimization

We will now turn our attention to (
2.1
) when the underlying transition kernels
\left\{\mathbb{P}_{t}\right\}
are unknown to us.
Instead, we assume sample access to
{\mathbb{P}_{t}}
such that for any
(s_{t},a_{t})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}\times\mathcal{A}_{t}
, one can generate
n
i.i.d. samples following the distribution
\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s_{t},a_{t})
.
Let
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{t}
be the empirical kernel constructed from such samples,
and let us write
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
in short for
\{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{t}\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
.

2.2.1
Risk-averse Policy Evaluation

Denote

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{n}
as the corresponding risk defined by
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
,
and
\widehat{V}^{\pi,\theta}_{t,n}
the corresponding value function in the augmented MDP
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}
.
We are interested in the number of samples needed for
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{n}
to be an accurate estimation of
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
.
In particular, we will focus on
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta})
that is
L^{\pi}
-Lipschitz w.r.t the augmented state variable, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x)-\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x^{\prime})\|_{1}\leq L^{\pi}|x-x^{\prime}|,\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.9)

We begin by first establishing the Lipschitz continuity of the value function
\widetilde{V}^{\pi,\theta}_{t}
defined in (
2.8
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
t\in[T]
and
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
, the value functions
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,\cdot)

and
\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,\cdot)
are Lipschitz with modulus

L^{\pi,t}=\left(2+\tfrac{T-t+2}{2}L^{\pi}\right)(T-t+1)L_{C}.

Proof.

We proceed by backward induction. For
t=T
, we have

\displaystyle|\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)-\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\pi,\theta}(s,y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(\cdot|s,x)}[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)]-\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(\cdot|s,y)}[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)]|

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle{|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(\cdot|s,x)}[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)]-\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(\cdot|s,y)}[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)]|}+{|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(\cdot|s,x)}[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)]|}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\|\pi_{T}(\cdot|s,x)-\pi_{T}(\cdot|s,y)\|_{1}\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{T}}|\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)|+2L_{C}|x-y|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2+L^{\pi})L_{C}|x-y|,

where
(a)
follows from Hölder’s inequality and Assumption
1
. For
0\leq t\leq T-1
, we have

\displaystyle|\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)+\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right]\right.

\displaystyle~~~~-\left.\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,y)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)+\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},y+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)+\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},y+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right]\right.

\displaystyle\underbrace{~~~~-\left.\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,y)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)+\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},y+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right]\right|}_{(A)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)+\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))-\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},y+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right]\right|}_{(B)}.

From Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle(A)

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi(\cdot|s,x)-\pi(\cdot|s,y)\|_{1}\cdot\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left|\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)+\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},y+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq(T-t+1)L^{\pi}L_{C}|x-y|.

In addition, given Assumption
1
and the inductive hypothesis, we obtain

\displaystyle(B)

\displaystyle\leq 2L_{C}|x-y|+\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x),s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))-\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},y+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2L_{C}|x-y|+L^{\pi,t+1}|x-y|.

The proof is then concluded by the above observations and the inductive hypothesis.
∎

With Lemma
2.1
in place, we are ready to establish the sample complexity for evaluating the risk
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)
defined in (
2.1
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose
\pi
satisfies (
3.9
).
For any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, take

\displaystyle n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{T^{4}L_{C}^{2}d}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{T^{6}L_{C}L_{\Theta}L^{\pi}|{\mathcal{S}}||\mathcal{A}|R_{\Theta}}{\delta\epsilon^{2}}\right),

where
\left\lvert{\mathcal{S}}\right\rvert=\max\left\{\left\lvert{\mathcal{S}}_{t}\right\rvert\right\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
and

\left\lvert\mathcal{A}\right\rvert=\max\left\{\left\lvert\mathcal{A}_{t}\right\rvert\right\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
.
Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{\pi}(s)-\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\right\rvert\leq\epsilon,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}.

Proof.

We first introduce some notations. Denote for all
t\in[T-1],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},a\in\mathcal{A}_{t},x\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right],

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{t}^{n}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right],

\displaystyle B^{\pi}=\sup\left\{\left|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)\right|:t\in[T-1],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},a\in\mathcal{A}_{t},x\in[0,t],\theta\in\Theta\right\}.

For any
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta}>0
, consider
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
an
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}
-net for the interval
[0,T]
and
\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}
an
\varepsilon_{\Theta}
-net for
\Theta
.
Let us denote

B^{\pi}(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta})=\sup\left\{\left|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)\right|:t\in[T-1],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},a\in\mathcal{A}_{t},x\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}\cap[0,t],\theta\in\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}\right\}.

We start by showing that for any
t\in[T],0\leq x\leq t,s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
and
\theta\in\Theta
,

|\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)-\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)|\leq(T-t)B^{\pi}.

(2.10)

We proceed by backward induction. For
t=T
and
0\leq x\leq T
, we have

|\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)-\widehat{V}_{T,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)|=|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)\right]|=0,\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{T}.

Suppose (
2.10
) holds for
t+1,...,T
. Then for
0\leq x\leq t
and any
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)-\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right]-\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{t}^{n}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widehat{V}_{t+1,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left\{\left|\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right]-\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{t}^{n}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right|\right.

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~+\left.\left|\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))-\widehat{V}_{t+1,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right]\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left\{\left|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)\right|+\max_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}}\left|\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))-\widehat{V}_{t+1,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a))\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(T-t)B^{\pi},

where the last inequality follows from the inductive hypothesis. Given (
2.10
), it suffices to control
B^{\pi}
. For any
0\leq x\leq T
(resp.
\theta\in\Theta
), let
x_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
(resp.
\theta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}
) be the closest point in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}
). For any
t\in[T]
,
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
and
a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2L^{\pi,t+1}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}+\left|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x_{\varepsilon},a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x_{\varepsilon},a)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2L^{\pi,t+1}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}+2L_{\Theta}\varepsilon_{\Theta}+\left|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta_{\varepsilon}}(s,x_{\varepsilon},a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta_{\varepsilon}}(s,x_{\varepsilon},a)\right|,

where we first use Lemma
2.1
and then Assumption
1
. Since
L^{\pi,0}\geq L^{\pi,t}
for any
t\geq 0
, we obtain

B^{\pi}\leq 2L^{\pi,0}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}+2L_{\Theta}\varepsilon_{\Theta}+B^{\pi}(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta}).

(2.11)

With above observations in place,
for any
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
, let
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}=\epsilon/8TL^{\pi,0},\varepsilon_{\Theta}=\epsilon/8TL_{\Theta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left|\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)-\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{\pi}(s)\right|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\left|\widetilde{V}_{0}^{\pi,\theta}(s,0)-\widehat{V}_{0,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,0)\right|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}(B^{\pi}(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta})>\epsilon/2T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\cup_{t\in[T-1]}\cup_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}}\cup_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\cup_{x\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}\cap[0,t]}\cup_{\theta\in\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}}\left\{|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)|>\epsilon/2T\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t\in[T-1]}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{x\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}\cap[0,t]}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{\theta\in\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}}\mathrm{Pr}(|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)|>\epsilon/2T)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}

\displaystyle 2T|{\mathcal{S}}||\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}||\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}|\exp\left(-\tfrac{2(\epsilon/2T)^{2}n}{(2TL_{C})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}

\displaystyle 2T|{\mathcal{S}}||\mathcal{A}|\tfrac{T}{\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}}\left(\tfrac{3R_{\Theta}}{\varepsilon_{\Theta}}\right)^{d}\exp\left(-\tfrac{\epsilon^{2}n}{8T^{4}L_{C}^{2}}\right),

where
(a)
follows from (
2.10
) and (
2.11
),
(b)
uses the union bound,
(c)
applies the Hoeffding’s inequality and
(d)
bounds the cardinality of the nets. Consequently, to ensure
\mathrm{Pr}(|\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)-\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{\pi}(s)|>\epsilon)\leq\delta
, it suffices to take

n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{T^{4}L_{C}^{2}d}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{T^{2}|{\mathcal{S}}||\mathcal{A}|R_{\Theta}}{\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}\varepsilon_{\Theta}\delta}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{T^{4}L_{C}^{2}d}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{T^{4}L^{\pi,0}L_{\Theta}|{\mathcal{S}}||\mathcal{A}|R_{\Theta}}{\epsilon^{2}\delta}\right).

The proof is concluded by invoking Lemma
2.1
, which implies
L^{\pi,0}=\mathcal{O}(T^{2}L^{\pi}L_{C})
.
∎

2.2.2
Risk-averse Policy Optimization

With a similar argument as in risk-averse policy evaluation, we now proceed to establish the sample complexity for policy optimization (
2.1
).
We first establish the Lipshitz continuity of the optimal value function
\widetilde{V}^{\theta}_{t}
(defined in (
2.6
)) and its empirical estimate

\widehat{V}^{\theta}_{t,n}

w.r.t the augmented state variable.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
t\in[T]
and
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
, the value functions
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(s,\cdot)
and
\widehat{V}^{\theta}_{t,n}(s,\cdot)
are Lipschitz with modulus

L^{t}=2(T-t+1)L_{C}.

Proof.

As in Lemma
2.1
, we proceed by backward induction. For
t=T
and any
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\theta}(s,x)-\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\theta}(s,y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{T}}\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{T}}\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)\right|\leq 2L_{C}|x-y|,

where the last inequality follows from Assumption
1
. For
0\leq t\leq T-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(s,x)-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(s,y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,y,a)+\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a)-\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},y+c_{t}(s,a)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2L_{C}|x-y|+2(T-t)|x-y|,

where the last inequality applies the inductive hypothesis.
∎

We are now ready to establish the sample complexity for the policy optimization problem (
2.1
).
Let us denote
\mathcal{R}^{*}(s)=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{n}(s)

and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{*}_{n}(s)=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

For any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, take

n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{T^{4}L_{C}^{2}d}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{T^{5}L_{C}L_{\Theta}|{\mathcal{S}}||\mathcal{A}|R_{\Theta}}{\delta\epsilon^{2}}\right).

Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\left|\mathcal{R}^{*}(s)-\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{*}_{n}(s)\right|\leq\epsilon,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}.

Proof.

We begin by noting that from (
2.3
) and Proposition
2.1
, to control
\left\lvert\mathcal{R}^{*}-\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{*}_{n}\right\rvert
, it suffices
to bound the difference between

\widetilde{V}^{\theta}_{t}
and
\widehat{V}^{\theta}_{t,n}
.
For any
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta}>0
,
let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
be the
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}
-net for
\mathcal{X}=[0,T]
, and

\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}
be the
\varepsilon_{\Theta}
-net for
\Theta
.
We define for all
t\in[T-1],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},a\in\mathcal{A}_{t},x\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{\theta}(s,x,a)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a)\right],

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\theta}(s,x,a)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}^{n}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x+c_{t}(s,a)\right],

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left|\Phi_{t}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t}^{\theta}(s,x,a)\right|:t\in[T-1],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},a\in\mathcal{A}_{t},x\in[0,t],\theta\in\Theta\right\},

\displaystyle B(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta})

Similarly to (

) and (

), it can be readily shown that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(s,x)-\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\theta}(s,x)|

\displaystyle\leq(T-t)B,~\forall t\in[T],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},x\in[0,t],

\displaystyle\leq 2L^{0}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}+2L_{\Theta}\varepsilon_{\Theta}+B(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta}).

The rest of the argument then follows essentially the same lines as that of Theorem

, but invoking Lemma
2.2

.
∎

Before we conclude our discussion in this section, we illustrate the application of our development to conditional value-at-risk.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
denote the conditional value-at-risk, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)=\mathrm{CVaR}_{\alpha}(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t}))=\inf_{\theta\in[0,T]}\left\{\theta+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}^{\pi}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t}))-\theta\right]_{+}\right\}.

, with

and

.
∎

2.3
Infinite-horizon Risk-averse Markov Decision Processes

We proceed to show that our prior discussions of finite-horizon MDPs extend naturally to the infinite-horizon setting.
To begin, let
\mathcal{M}=({\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{A},c,\mathbb{P},\gamma)
denote an infinite-horizon MDP.
Here
{\mathcal{S}}
denotes the state space,
\mathcal{A}
denotes the action space,
c:{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
denotes the cost function,
\mathbb{P}
denotes transition kernel, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
denotes the discount factor.
As before, we are interested in

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})\right).

(2.12)

where the data process
\left\{(S_{t},A_{t})\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is generated by
S_{0}=s
,
A_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|S_{t})
and
S_{t+1}\sim\mathbb{P}(\cdot|S_{t},A_{t})
, and
the risk functional
R_{\Theta,f}
is defined through as in (
1.3
).
Define value function

\displaystyle V^{\pi,\theta}(s)=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[f_{\theta}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})\right)|S_{0}=s\right].

(2.13)

Then

\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)=\min_{\theta\in\Theta}V^{\pi,\theta}(s)
,
and consequently (
2.12
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}V^{\pi,\theta}(s).

(2.14)

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a finite-horizon MDP
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is a
T
-stage approximation of
\mathcal{M}
if its per-stage state, action spaces and transition kernel are given by
{\mathcal{S}}_{t}={\mathcal{S}}
,
\mathcal{A}_{t}=\mathcal{A}
,
\mathbb{P}_{t}=\mathbb{P}
,
and its cost at stage
t
is given by
c_{t}=\gamma^{t}c
.

Throughout the rest of Section
2.3
, we make the following additional structural assumption on
f_{\theta}
.
We will discuss later how Assumption
2
can be satisfied when we work with the distributionally robust functional generated by
\phi
-divergences (Example
1.2
).

Assumption 2
.

For any
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(\alpha x)=\alpha f_{\theta/\alpha}(x),~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.15)

In addition, and there exists
L_{f}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\left\lvert f_{\theta}(x)-f_{\theta}(x^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq L_{f}\left\lvert x-x^{\prime}\right\rvert,~\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.16)

We are now ready to establish the dynamic equations of
V^{\pi,\theta}
defined in (
2.13
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose Assumption
2
holds.
Then for any
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\mathcal{M})
, we have

\displaystyle V^{\pi,\theta}(s)=\overline{V}^{\pi}(s,0,\theta),~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}},~\forall\theta\in\Theta,

where
\overline{V}^{\pi}
is the unique fixed point of operator

{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}:\mathrm{C}_{b}({\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{C}_{b}({\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}\left[V\right](s,x,\theta)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)f_{\theta}(x+c(s,a))-f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~~~+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[V\left(S_{1},\tfrac{X_{1}}{\gamma},\tfrac{\theta}{\gamma}\right)|S_{0}=s,A_{0}\sim\pi(\cdot|S_{0}),X_{1}=x+c(S_{0},A_{0})\right].

(2.17)

Proof.

For any
T>0
, consider the
T
-stage approximation
\mathcal{M}_{T}
of
\mathcal{M}
, and its corresponding augmented MDP
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T,\theta}
.
Let
V^{\pi,T}(s,x,\theta)
denote the corresponding value function in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T,\theta}
, defined as the right hand side of (
2.2
).
For notational simplicity, we use
\mathbb{E}\left[\cdot\right]
below to denote the expectation w.r.t the process
\left\{(S_{t},X_{t},A_{t})\right\}
generated by
\pi
in augmented MDP
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T,\theta}
.
We have

\displaystyle V^{\pi,T+1}(s,x,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)f_{\theta}(x+c(s,a))-f_{\theta}(x)+\mathbb{E}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{T+1}f_{\theta}(X_{t}+\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t}))-f_{\theta}(X_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)f_{\theta}(x+c(s,a))-f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~~~+\mathbb{E}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{T+1}f_{\theta}\left(\gamma\left(\tfrac{X_{t}}{\gamma}+\gamma^{t-1}c(S_{t},A_{t})\right)\right)-f_{\theta}\left(\gamma\cdot\tfrac{X_{t}}{\gamma}\right)|S_{0}=s,X_{0}=x\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)f_{\theta}(x+c(s,a))-f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~~~+\mathbb{E}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}f_{\theta}\left(\gamma\left(\tfrac{X_{t+1}}{\gamma}+\gamma^{t}c(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})\right)\right)-f_{\theta}\left(\gamma\cdot\tfrac{X_{t+1}}{\gamma}\right)|S_{0}=s,X_{0}=x\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)f_{\theta}(x+c(s,a))-f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~~~+\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}f_{\theta/\gamma}\left({\tfrac{X_{t+1}}{\gamma}+\gamma^{t}c(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})}\right)-f_{\theta/\gamma}\left(\tfrac{X_{t+1}}{\gamma}\right)|S_{0}=s,X_{0}=x\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)f_{\theta}(x+c(s,a))-f_{\theta}(x)+\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[V^{\pi,T}\left(S_{1},\tfrac{X_{1}}{\gamma},\tfrac{\theta}{\gamma}\right)|S_{0}=s,X_{0}=x\right],

(2.18)

where
(a)
follows from Assumption
2
,
and
(b)
follows from the definition of

2.4
Sample Complexity for Risk-averse Policy Evaluation and Optimization

Similar to Section
2.2
, we now proceed to establish the number of samples needed for estimating the risk
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
for a given policy
\pi
when the underlying transition kernel
\mathbb{P}
is unknown, and similarly for estimating optimal risk.
To this end, note that given Assumption
1
, one can truncate the infinite-horizon problem by its finite-horizon counterpart with sufficiently large horizon

, denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{T}
,
and consider estimating the risk for
\mathcal{M}_{T}
.
Formally, we have the following sample complexity for estimating the risk of a given policy

.

Theorem 2.7
.

For any
T>0
, let
\mathcal{M}_{T}
be the finite-horizon approximation (Definition
2.2
) of
\mathcal{M}
.
For any policy
\pi
, let
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}_{T}
denote its risk defined as in (

In addition, let

corresponding to the empirical kernel
\mathbb{P}_{n}
in
\mathcal{M}_{T}
.
For any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
,
take
T=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\log(\tfrac{L_{C}}{(1-\gamma)\epsilon})
and

\displaystyle n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{L_{C}^{2}d\log^{4}(L_{C}/(1-\gamma)\epsilon)}{(1-\gamma)^{4}\epsilon^{2}}\log\left(\tfrac{L_{C}L_{\Theta}L^{\pi}|{\mathcal{S}}||\mathcal{A}|R_{\Theta}\log^{6}(L_{C}/(1-\gamma)\epsilon)}{(1-\gamma)^{6}\epsilon^{2}\delta}\right)\right)

Then with probability at least

, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{T,n}(s)-\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\right\rvert\leq\epsilon,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

Proof.

Let
V^{\pi,\theta}_{T}
denote the value function of policy
\pi
within
\mathcal{M}_{T}
defined as in (
2.2
).
We have

\displaystyle\left\lvert V^{\pi,\theta}_{T}(s)-V^{\pi,\theta}(s)\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[f_{\theta}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})\right)|S_{0}=s\right]-\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[f_{\theta}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})\right)|S_{0}=s\right]\right\rvert\leq\tfrac{L_{C}\gamma^{T}}{1-\gamma},

where the last inequality follows from Assumption
1
.
Consequently we obtain

\left\lvert\mathcal{R}^{\pi}_{T}(s)-\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\right\rvert\leq\tfrac{L_{C}\gamma^{T}}{1-\gamma}
.
We can then conclude the proof by first taking

, followed by invoking Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Similarly, we can obtain the sample complexity for estimating the optimal risk.

Theorem 2.8

Under the same setup as in Theorem

.

and

, take

The proof follows from similar lines as in Theorem
2.7

2.4

.

.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
denote the conditional value-at-risk.

and

3
Risk-averse Stochastic Optimal Control

In this section, we turn our attention to risk-averse SOC problems and show that the approach presented in Section
2
can be naturally extended to SOC for both the finite-horizon and infinite-horizon settings.
Similar to MDPs, for any SOC instance
\mathcal{M}
, we use
\Pi(\mathcal{M})
to indicate the set of randomized history-dependent policies,
and
\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\mathcal{M})
for the set of randomized Markovian policies.

3.1
Finite-horizon Risk-averse Stochastic Optimal Control

Our discussion for the SOC problem adopt similar notations as in MDPs.
In particular,
let
{\mathcal{S}}_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{S}}}
denote the compact state space of stage
t
, and let
\mathcal{A}_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{A}}}
denote the compact control (action) space of stage
t
,
we seek to find the optimal policy of

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t})\right).

(3.1)

As before, we assume cost function
c_{t}
satisfies
0\leq c_{t}\leq 1

and is continuous.
In comparison to MDPs, here
\left\{(S_{t},A_{t})\right\}_{t=0}^{T}
is generated by
S_{0}=s
,
A_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|H_{t})
, and
S_{t+1}=F_{t}(S_{t},A_{t},\xi_{t})
.
Here

F_{t}:{\mathcal{S}}_{t}\times\mathcal{A}_{t}\times\Xi_{t}\to{\mathcal{S}}_{t+1}
denotes a given continuous deterministic mapping termed as the transition function, and
\xi_{t}\in\Xi_{t}
denotes the random transition noise/data following distribution
P_{t}
, and

H_{t}=\xi_{[t-1]}
denotes the history up to stage
t
.
We assume that
\left\{\xi_{t}\right\}
are independent with each other, and
\xi_{t}^{\prime}s
distribution
P_{t}
does not depend on the state and action.
For notational convenience, we will occasionally write
P
in short for
\left\{P_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
.
For any
\theta\in\Theta
, we define the value function of policy
\pi
as

\displaystyle V^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s)=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{P}\left[f_{\theta}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}c_{t}(S_{t},A_{t})\right)|S_{0}=s\right].

(3.2)

That is,

\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)=\min_{\theta\in\Theta}V^{\pi,\theta}(s)
.
It is then clear that (
3.1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}V_{0}^{\pi,\theta}(s).

(3.3)

Similar to Section
2.1
, we introduce the following SOC problem with the augmented state.

Definition 3.1

\theta\in\Theta

The cost function and the transition function are defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s_{t},x_{t},a_{t})

\displaystyle(s_{t+1},x_{t+1})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{F}_{t}(s_{t},x_{t},a_{t},\xi_{t}),

where

\displaystyle s_{t+1}=F(s_{t},a_{t},\xi_{t}),~x_{t+1}=x_{t}+c_{t}(s_{t},a_{t}).

\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{\pi,\theta}_{0}(s,x)=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{P}\left[\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T}\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(S_{t},X_{t},A_{t})|S_{0}=s,X_{0}=x\right].

(3.5)

We then make the following observations regarding the augmented SOC and its connection to the the original SOC problem (

3.2.1
Risk-averse Policy Evaluation

Denote

To develop the sample complexity result, we make the following assumption on the underlying SOC problem.

Assumption 3

(i) The state space

\displaystyle\left|c_{t}(s,a)-c_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\right|\leq L\|(s,a)-(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\|,

\displaystyle\left\|F_{t}(s,a,\xi)-F_{t}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime},\xi)\right\|\leq L\|(s,a)-(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\|,

for all
s,s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,
\xi\in\Xi
and
t\in[T]
.

As similar to MDPs, we will focus on
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta})
that is
L^{\pi}
-Lipschitz w.r.t the augmented state variable, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\pi(\cdot|s,x)-\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime},x^{\prime})\|_{1}\leq L^{\pi}\left\lVert(s,x)-(s^{\prime},x^{\prime})\right\rVert,\forall s,s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.9)

We first establish the Lipschitz continuity of value functions w.r.t both the original and the augmented state variables.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
t\in[T]
and
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
, the value functions
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,\cdot)
and
\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,\cdot)
are Lipschitz continuous with modulus

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{X}}^{\pi,t}=\left(2+\tfrac{T-t+2}{2}L^{\pi}\right)(T-t+1)L_{C}.

(3.10)

In addition, for any
t\in[T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(\cdot,x)
and
\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(\cdot,x)
are Lipschitz continuous with modulus

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,T}

\displaystyle=L^{\pi}L_{C}+LL_{C},

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,t}

\displaystyle=(T-t+1)L^{\pi}L_{C}+(L_{C}+L_{\mathcal{X}}^{\pi,t+1}+L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,t+1})L.

(3.11)

Proof.

The proof of (
3.10
) follows from similar lines as in Lemma
2.1
.
We proceed to establish (
3.11
) by backward reduction.
For
t=T
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)-\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(s^{\prime},x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(s^{\prime},x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)\right]\right|}+{\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{T}(s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)\right]\right|}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\|\pi_{T}(\cdot|s,x)-\pi_{T}(\cdot|s^{\prime},x)\|_{1}\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{T}}\left|\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)\right|+LL_{C}\|s-s^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L^{\pi}L_{C}\|s-s^{\prime}\|+LL_{C}\|s-s^{\prime}\|,

where
(a)
uses Hölder’s inequality, Assumption
1
and
3
. For
0\leq t\leq T-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s,x,a,\xi)\right)\right]\right]-\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s^{\prime},x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s^{\prime},x,a,\xi)\right)\right]\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s^{\prime},x,a,\xi)\right)\right]\right]-\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s^{\prime},x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s^{\prime},x,a,\xi)\right)\right]\right]\right|}_{(A)}

\displaystyle~+\underbrace{\left|\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{t}(\cdot|s,x)}\left[\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s,x,a,\xi)\right)-\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s^{\prime},x,a,\xi\right)\right]\right]\right|}_{(B)}.

From Hölder’s inequality and (
3.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle(A)

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi(\cdot|s,x)-\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime},x)\|_{1}\cdot\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left|\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s^{\prime},x,a,\xi)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq(T-t+1)L^{\pi}L_{C}\|s-s^{\prime}\|.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle(B)

\displaystyle\leq LL_{C}\|s-s^{\prime}\|+\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s,x,a,\xi)\right)-\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s^{\prime},x,a,\xi)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle=LL_{C}\|s-s^{\prime}\|+\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(F_{t}(s,a,\xi),x+c_{t}(s,a)\right)-\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(F_{t}(s^{\prime},a,\xi),x+c_{t}(s^{\prime},a)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq LL_{C}\|s-s^{\prime}\|+(L_{\mathcal{X}}^{\pi,t+1}+L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,t+1})L\|s-s^{\prime}\|.

(3.12)

Combining the above observations completes the proof.
∎

With Lemma
3.1
in place, we are now ready to establish the sample complexity for estimating risk
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, take

n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{T^{5}L_{C}^{2}d_{\mathcal{S}}d_{\mathcal{A}}d}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{TL_{C}L_{\Theta}LL^{\pi}RR_{\Theta}}{\epsilon\delta}\right).

The with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

|\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)-\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{\pi}(s)|\leq\epsilon,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{S}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta}>0
,
let
\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{t}}
be the
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{S}}
-net for
{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
,
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}_{t}}
be the
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}
-net for
\mathcal{A}_{t}
,
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
be the
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}
-net for
\mathcal{X}=[0,T]
, and

\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}
be the
\varepsilon_{\Theta}
-net for
\Theta
.
In addition,
for all
t\in[T-1],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},a\in\mathcal{A}_{t},x\in\mathbb{R}
,
define

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s,x,a,\xi)\right)\right],

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\widehat{P}_{t}^{n}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\pi,\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s,x,a,\xi)\right)\right],

\displaystyle B^{\pi}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a,\xi)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a,\xi)\right|:t\in[T-1],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},a\in\mathcal{A}_{t},x\in[0,t],\theta\in\Theta\right\},

\displaystyle B^{\pi}(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{S}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}},\varepsilon_{\Theta})

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a,\xi)-\widehat{\Phi}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a,\xi)\right|:t\in[T-1],s\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{t}},a\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}_{t}},x\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}\cap[0,t],\theta\in\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}\right\}.

With a similar argument to (
2.10
), it can be readily shown that

\left|\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)-\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x)\right|\leq(T-t)B^{\pi},~\forall t\in[T],s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t},x\in[0,t].

In addition, similar to (
3.12
), for any
t\in[T-1]
,
s,s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
,
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\displaystyle|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)-\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)|

\displaystyle\leq(L_{\mathcal{X}}^{\pi,t+1}+L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,t+1})L\|s-s^{\prime}\|\leq L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,t}\|s-s^{\prime}\|,

\displaystyle|\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a)-\Phi_{t}^{\pi,\theta}(s,x,a^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq(L_{\mathcal{X}}^{\pi,t+1}+L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,t+1})L\|a-a^{\prime}\|\leq L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,t}\|a-a^{\prime}\|.

With the above observations in place, similar to (
2.11
), we obtain

B^{\pi}\leq 2L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,0}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{S}}+2L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,0}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}+2L_{\mathcal{X}}^{\pi,0}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{X}}+2L_{\Theta}\varepsilon_{\Theta}+B^{\pi}(\varepsilon_{\mathcal{S}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{S}},\varepsilon_{\Theta}).

Then following similar lines as in Theorem
2.3
,
it suffices to take

\displaystyle n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{T^{4}L_{C}^{2}d_{\mathcal{S}}d_{\mathcal{A}}d}{\epsilon^{2}\delta}\log\tfrac{T^{6}(L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,0})^{2}L_{\mathcal{X}}^{\pi,0}L_{\Theta}RR_{\Theta}}{\epsilon^{4}\delta}\right)

for
|\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)-\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{\pi}(s)|\leq\epsilon
with probability
1-\delta
.
The proof is completed by invoking Lemma
3.1
, which implies
L_{\mathcal{X}}^{\pi,0}=\mathcal{O}(T^{2}L^{\pi}L_{C})
and
L_{\mathcal{S}}^{\pi,0}=\mathcal{O}(T^{2}L^{\pi}L_{C}L^{T+1})
.
∎

3.2.2
Risk-averse Policy Optimization

We now proceed to establish the sample complexity for policy optimization (
3.1
).
To begin, we first establish the Lipschitz continuity of the optimal value function
\widetilde{V}^{\theta}_{t}
(defined in (
3.6
)) and its empirical estimate

\widehat{V}^{\theta}_{t,n}

w.r.t the original and the augmented state variables.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
t\in[T]
and
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
, the value functions
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(s,\cdot)
and
\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\theta}(s,\cdot)
are Lipschitz continuous with modulus

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{X}}^{t}=2(T-t+1)L_{C}.

(3.13)

In addition,
for any
t\in[T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(\cdot,x)
and
\widehat{V}_{t,n}^{\theta}(\cdot,x)
are Lipschitz continuous with modulus

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}

\displaystyle=LL_{C},

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{S}}^{t}

\displaystyle=(L_{C}+L_{\mathcal{X}}^{t+1}+L_{\mathcal{S}}^{t+1})L.

(3.14)

Proof.

The proof of (
3.13
) follows similar lines as in Lemma
2.2
.
We proceed to establish (
3.14
) by backward induction.
For
t=T
, we have

|\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\theta}(s,x)-\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x)|=\left|\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{T}}\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\inf_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{T}}\widetilde{c_{T}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)\right|\leq L_{C}L\|s-s^{\prime}\|,

where the last inequality follows from Assumption
1
and
3
.
Suppose the claim holds for at
t+1,\ldots,T
, then

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(s,x)-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left|\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s,x,a)-\widetilde{c_{t}}^{\theta}(s^{\prime},x,a)+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s,x,a,\xi)\right)-\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s^{\prime},x,a,\xi)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(a)}}{\leq}

\displaystyle LL_{C}\|s-s^{\prime}\|+\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P_{t}}\left[\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s,x,a,\xi)\right)-\widetilde{V}_{t+1}^{\theta}\left(\widetilde{F}_{t}(s^{\prime},x,a,\xi)\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(b)}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left(L_{C}+L_{\mathcal{X}}^{t+1}+L_{\mathcal{S}}^{t+1}\right)L\|s-s^{\prime}\|,

where (a) follows from Assumption
1
and
3
, while (b) follows the same argument as (
3.12
).
∎

With Lemma
3.2
in place, we are now ready to establish the sample complexity of estimating the optimal risk.
Let us denote
\mathcal{R}^{*}(s)=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{n}(s)

and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{*}_{n}(s)=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, take

n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{T^{5}L_{C}^{2}d_{\mathcal{S}}d_{\mathcal{A}}d}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{TL_{C}L_{\Theta}LRR_{\Theta}}{\epsilon\delta}\right).

Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}^{*}(s)-\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}^{*}(s)|\leq\epsilon,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}.

Proof.

The argument follows from the same lines as that of Theorem
3.3
, with Lemma
3.1
replaced by Lemma
3.2
.
∎

As an application, we establish the sample complexity associated with conditional value-at-risk.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
denote the conditional value-at-risk. For any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, with

\displaystyle n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{T^{5}d_{{\mathcal{S}}}d_{\mathcal{A}}}{\alpha^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{TLR}{\delta\alpha\epsilon}\right),

we have with probability
1-\delta
that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{R}^{*}(s)-\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{*}_{n}(s)\right|\leq\epsilon,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}.

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
3.3
and the fact that for conditional value-at-risk, we have
L_{C}=L_{\Theta}={1}/{\alpha}
and
\Theta=[0,T]
.
∎

3.3
Infinite-horizon Risk-averse Stochastic Optimal Control

We now extend our discussion to the infinite-horizon SOC problems.
The approach we take in this section will be largely similar to that of Section
2.3
.
Let
\mathcal{M}=({\mathcal{S}},\mathcal{A},c,P,F,\gamma)
denote an infinite-horizon SOC instance.
Here
{\mathcal{S}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the compact state space,
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
denotes the compact control (action) space,
c:{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
denotes the cost function,
\gamma\in[0,1)
denotes the discount factor,
P
denotes distribution of the transition noise/data supported over
\Xi
, and
F:{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}\times\Xi
denotes the transition function.
We are interested in

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\coloneqq\mathcal{R}_{\Theta,f}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})\right).

(3.15)

where
\left\{(S_{t},A_{t})\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is generated by
S_{0}=s
,
A_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot|S_{t})
and
S_{t+1}=F(S_{t},A_{t},\xi_{t})
.
We assume
\left\{\xi_{t}\right\}
are independent of each other, and
\xi_{t}\sim P
for
t\geq 0
.
The risk functional
R_{\Theta,f}
is defined through as in (
1.3
).
As before, let us define value function

\displaystyle V^{\pi,\theta}(s)=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[f_{\theta}\left(\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c(S_{t},A_{t})\right)|S_{0}=s\right].

(3.16)

Then

\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)=\min_{\theta\in\Theta}V^{\pi,\theta}(s)

and consequently (
3.15
) is also equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}V^{\pi,\theta}(s).

(3.17)

We have the following dynamic programming equations of
V^{\pi,\theta}
defined above.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose Assumption
2
holds.
Then for any
\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{MR}}(\mathcal{M})
, we have

\displaystyle V^{\pi,\theta}(s)=\overline{V}^{\pi}(s,0,\theta),~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}},~\forall\theta\in\Theta,

where
\overline{V}^{\pi}
is the unique fixed point of operator

{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}:\mathrm{C}_{b}({\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{C}_{b}({\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}\left[V\right](s,x,\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}f_{\theta}(x+c(s,a))-f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~~~+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[V\left(S_{1},\tfrac{X_{1}}{\gamma},\tfrac{\theta}{\gamma}\right)|S_{0}=s,A_{0}\sim\pi(\cdot|S_{0}),X_{1}=x+c(S_{0},A_{0})\right].

Similar to Theorem
2.6
, one can establish the dynamic equations of (
3.15
) under Assumption
2
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose Assumption
2
holds.
Define
V^{\theta}(s)=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}V^{\pi,\theta}(s)
.
We have

\displaystyle V^{\theta}(s)=\overline{V}(s,0,\theta),~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}},~\forall\theta\in\Theta,

where
\overline{V}
is the unique fixed point of operator

{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}:\mathrm{C}_{b}({\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{C}_{b}({\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}\left[V\right](s,x,\theta)

\displaystyle=\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}}f_{\theta}(x+c(s,a))-f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~~~+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[V\left(S_{1},\tfrac{X_{1}}{\gamma},\tfrac{\theta}{\gamma}\right)|S_{0}=s,A_{0}=a,X_{1}=x+c(S_{0},A_{0})\right].

Accordingly, we have
\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)=V^{\theta^{*}}(s)
, where
\theta^{*}
is the optimal solution of (
2.14
).

The proof of Theorem
3.5
and
3.6
follows from similar lines as in Theorem
2.5
and
2.6
and hence omitted here.
Note that both of them can be specialized directly to the conditional value-at-risk.
For general
\phi
-divergences where Assumption
3.6
may not hold, one can adopt a similar approach as discussed in Section
2.3
.

3.4
Sample Complexity of Risk-averse Policy Evaluation and Optimization

We now proceed to establish the number of samples needed for estimating the risk
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
for a given policy
\pi
when the underlying distribution
P
of transition noise is unknown, and similarly for estimating optimal risk.
Similar to Section
2.4
, we define the following finite-horizon truncation of the infinite horizon SOC problem.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a finite-horizon SOC
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is a
T
-stage approximation of
\mathcal{M}
if its per-stage state, action spaces and the distribution of transition noise are given by
{\mathcal{S}}_{t}={\mathcal{S}}
,
\mathcal{A}_{t}=\mathcal{A}
,
P_{t}=P
,
and its cost at stage
t
is given by
c_{t}=\gamma^{t}c
.

We first establish the sample complexity result for estimating the risk
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
defined in (
3.15
).

Theorem 3.7
.

For any
T>0
, let
\mathcal{M}_{T}
be the finite-horizon approximation (Definition
3.2
) of
\mathcal{M}
.
For any policy
\pi
, let
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}_{T}
denote its risk defined as in (
3.1
).
In addition, let
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{T,n}
be the risk of policy
\pi
corresponding to the empirical distribution
P_{n}
in
\mathcal{M}_{T}
.
For any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
,
take
T=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\log(\tfrac{L_{C}}{(1-\gamma)\epsilon})
and

\displaystyle n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{L_{C}^{2}d_{\mathcal{S}}d_{\mathcal{A}}d_{\Theta}\log^{5}(L_{C}/(1-\gamma)\epsilon)}{(1-\gamma)^{5}\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{L_{C}L_{\Theta}LL^{\pi}RR_{\Theta}\log(L_{C}/(1-\gamma)\epsilon)}{(1-\gamma)\epsilon\delta}\right).

Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{T,n}(s)-\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\right\rvert\leq\epsilon,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

Similarly, we can obtain the sample complexity for estimating the optimal risk.

Theorem 3.8
.

Under the same setup as in Theorem
3.7
.
For any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, take

\displaystyle n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{L_{C}^{2}d_{\mathcal{S}}d_{\mathcal{A}}d_{\Theta}\log^{5}(L_{C}/(1-\gamma)\epsilon)}{(1-\gamma)^{5}\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{L_{C}L_{\Theta}LRR_{\Theta}\log(L_{C}/(1-\gamma)\epsilon)}{(1-\gamma)\epsilon\delta}\right).

Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{T,n}(s)-\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\right\rvert\leq\epsilon.

The proof for Theorem
3.7
and
3.8
follows from similar argument as their counterparts for MDPs (Theorem
2.7
and
2.8
).
Before we conclude our discussion in this section, we establish the sample complexity associated with conditional value-at-risk.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{\pi}
denote the conditional value-at-risk.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, with

\displaystyle T=\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\log(\tfrac{1}{(1-\gamma)\alpha\epsilon}),~n=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{d_{\mathcal{S}}d_{\mathcal{A}}d_{\Theta}\log^{5}(1/(1-\gamma)\alpha\epsilon)}{(1-\gamma)^{5}\alpha^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\log\tfrac{LR\log(1/\alpha(1-\gamma)\epsilon)}{(1-\gamma)\alpha\epsilon\delta}\right),

we have with probability
1-\delta
that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\pi}_{T,n}(s)-\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{M})}\mathcal{R}^{\pi}(s)\right\rvert\leq\epsilon,~\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

4
Concluding Remarks

To conclude our discussions in this manuscript, it is worth noting that the dynamic equations for risk-averse MDP (SOC) with static risk measures are by no means unique
[
3
,
18
,
2
]
.
A potential consequence of this is that in defining the notion of time-consistency
[
27
]
, the corresponding tail-problems for which the policy is optimal with respect to depends on the dynamic equations one consider.
In addition, while we mainly focus on dynamic programming equations and sample complexities in this manuscript, it could be also rewarding to develop efficient solution methods for risk-averse MDP and SOC problems with static risk measures.
In particular, for risk-averse SOC with convex cost and affine transitions, it remains interesting to explore the possibility of developing cutting plane-based methods that is widely used in the risk-neutral setting

,

,

]
.

Appendix A
Supplementary Proofs

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a measurable space, and
X:\Omega\to[0,B]
be
\mathcal{F}
-measurable where
B>0
.
Consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X)=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}}\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\zeta(\omega)dP(\omega),~\mathrm{s.t.}~\zeta\in\mathfrak{D},~\int_{\Omega}\phi(\zeta(w))dP(w)\leq\tau.

(A.1)

where
\mathfrak{D}
denotes the set of probability density function w.r.t
P
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
L(\epsilon)>1
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X)\geq\mathcal{R}_{L(\epsilon)}(X)\geq\mathcal{R}(X)-\epsilon,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{L}(X)=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}}\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\zeta(\omega)dP(\omega),~\mathrm{s.t.}~\zeta\in\mathfrak{D},~\int_{\Omega}\phi(\zeta(w))dP(w)\leq\tau,\esssup\zeta\leq L.

(A.2)

If in addition, that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\phi(x)/x=\infty
,
then
L(\epsilon)
can be chosen independent of
X
and
P
.

Proof.

We proceed to first show that for any
\zeta
feasible to (
A.1
), there exists
\widetilde{\zeta}
feasible to (
A.2
) such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[\widetilde{\zeta}X]\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta X\right]-B\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta-L\right]_{+}.

(A.3)

Consider

\overline{\zeta}
defined as
\overline{\zeta}(\omega)=\min\left\{\zeta(\omega),L\right\}
.
Denote

\delta=\mathbb{E}_{P}{[\zeta-L]_{+}},

then we have

\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\overline{\zeta}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta-[\zeta-L]_{+}\right]=1-\delta
.
Note that one can assume without loss of generality that
\delta>0
, otherwise (
A.3
) holds with
\widetilde{\zeta}=\zeta
.
We now proceed to construct
\widetilde{\zeta}\in\mathfrak{D}
that is feasible to (
A.2
).

Consider
s
defined as
s(\omega)=[1-\overline{\zeta}(\omega)]_{+}
.
It is clear that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[s]=\mathbb{E}_{P}{[1-\overline{\zeta}]_{+}}\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}{[1-{\zeta}]_{+}}=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[1-\zeta+\left[\zeta-1\right]_{+}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta-1\right]_{+}\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta-L\right]_{+}\geq\delta.

Now consider
\widetilde{\delta}=\overline{\zeta}+\delta/\mathbb{E}_{P}[s]\cdot s
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[\widetilde{\delta}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\overline{\delta}]+\delta=1,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\overline{\zeta}(\omega)+\delta/\mathbb{E}_{P}[s]\cdot s(\omega)\leq\overline{\zeta}(\omega)+s(\omega)=\overline{\zeta}(\omega)+[1-\overline{\zeta}(\omega)]_{+}\leq\max\left\{L,1\right\}\leq L.

(A.5)

In addition,
since
\phi(\cdot)
attains minimum at
1
,

\phi(\cdot)
is non-increasing over
[0,1]
and non-decreasing over
[1,\infty)
.
Hence for
\omega
such that
\zeta(\omega)\geq 1
, we have

s(\omega)=0
and
\overline{\zeta}(\omega)\geq 1
, and hence

\phi(\widetilde{\zeta}(\omega))=\phi(\overline{\zeta}(\omega))\leq\phi({\zeta}(\omega))
.
On the other hand, for
\omega
such that
\zeta(\omega)<1
, we have

\overline{\zeta}(\omega)=\zeta(\omega)<1
and
\widetilde{\delta}(\omega)<1
, and hence

\phi(\widetilde{\zeta}(\omega))\leq\phi(\overline{\zeta}(\omega))=\phi({\zeta}(\omega))
.
In either of the cases, we have

\phi(\widetilde{\zeta}(\omega))\leq\phi(\zeta(\omega))

and consequently

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\widetilde{\zeta}(\omega)dP(\omega)\leq\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\zeta(\omega)dP(\omega)\leq\tau.

(A.6)

Combining (
A.4
), (
A.5
) and (
A.6
), we conclude that
\widetilde{\delta}
is feasible to (
A.2
).
In addition,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[\widetilde{\zeta}X]=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(\overline{\zeta}+\delta/\mathbb{E}_{P}[s]\cdot s)X\right]\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\overline{\zeta}X\right]=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta X-[\zeta-L]_{+}X\right]\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta X\right]-B\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta-L\right]_{+},

from which we obtain (
A.3
).

In view of (
A.3
), for any
\epsilon>0
, let
\zeta_{\epsilon}
be the
(\epsilon/2)
-optimal solution of (
A.1
), there exists
\widetilde{\zeta}_{\epsilon}
feasible to (
A.2
) such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{L}(X)\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}[\widetilde{\zeta}_{\epsilon}X]\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta_{\epsilon}X\right]-B\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta_{\epsilon}-L\right]_{+}\geq\mathcal{R}(X)-\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}-B\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta_{\epsilon}-L\right]_{+}.

(A.7)

Since
\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta\right]=1
and
\zeta\geq 0
, from dominated convergence theorem we have
\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta-L\right]_{+}=0
, and consequently there exists
L=L(P,X,B,\epsilon)

such that

\mathcal{R}_{L}(X)\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}[\widetilde{\zeta}X]\geq\mathcal{R}(X)-{\epsilon}.

If in addition that
we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\phi(x)/x=\infty
,
define
g(L)=\inf_{x\geq L}\phi(x)/x
.
Note that
g(\cdot)
is well defined for
L
large enough,
and for any
\zeta
feasible to (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\zeta-L\right]_{+}=\int_{\zeta\geq L}(\zeta-L)dP(\omega)\leq\int_{\zeta\geq L}\zeta dP(\omega)=\int_{\zeta\geq L}\tfrac{\phi(\zeta(\omega))}{\phi(\zeta(\omega))/\zeta(\omega)}dP(\omega)\leq\tfrac{\tau}{g(L)}.

Combining the above observation with (
A.7
) concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}
be a convex lower semicontinuous function with
\phi(0)=1
and
\phi(x)=+\infty
for
x<0
.
Then

\displaystyle f_{\lambda,\mu}(x):=\lambda\tau+\mu+{(\lambda\phi_{L})^{*}(x-\mu)}

is Lipschitz continuous for any
\lambda,\mu
,
where

\phi_{L}(\cdot)=\phi(\cdot)+\delta_{[0,L]}(\cdot)
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
g(x)={(\lambda\phi_{L})^{*}(x-\mu)}={\sup_{z\in\mathrm{dom}(\phi_{L})}z(x-\mu)-\lambda\phi_{L}(z)}
is Lipschitz continuous.
For any
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert g(x)-g(x^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert x-x^{\prime}\right\rvert L,

from which we conclude the proof.
∎

We are now ready for the proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

In view of Lemma
A.1
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
L(\epsilon)>1
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X)\geq\mathcal{R}_{L(\epsilon)}(X)\geq\mathcal{R}(X)-\epsilon.

If in addition, that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\phi(x)/x=\infty
,
then
L(\epsilon)
can be chosen independent of
X
and
P
.
Note that for any
L>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{L}(X)=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}}\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\zeta(\omega)dP(\omega),~\mathrm{s.t.}~\zeta\in\mathfrak{D},~\int_{\Omega}\phi_{L}(\zeta(w))dP(w)\leq\tau.

The dual to the above problem is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{L}(X)=\inf_{\lambda>0,\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{{\lambda\tau+\mu+\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[(\lambda\phi_{L})^{*}(X-\mu)\right]}\right\}=\inf_{\lambda>0,\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[f_{(\lambda,\mu)}(X)\right].

(A.8)

The proof is then completed by invoking Lemma

, and
the observation that

\displaystyle f_{(\lambda,\mu)}(\alpha x)=\lambda\tau+\mu+(\lambda\phi_{L})^{*}(\alpha x-\mu)

\displaystyle=\lambda\tau+\mu+(\lambda\phi_{L})^{*}\left(\alpha\left(x-\tfrac{\mu}{\alpha}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\lambda\tau+\mu+\alpha(\tfrac{\lambda}{\alpha}\phi_{L})^{*}\left({x-\tfrac{\mu}{\alpha}}\right)=\alpha f_{({\lambda}/{\alpha},{\mu}/{\alpha})}(x),
