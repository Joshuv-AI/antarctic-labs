A note on the parameter ℓ \ell in Buchbinder–Feldman’s deterministic submodular matroid algorithm
Shisheng Li

Abstract
Buchbinder and Feldman [ 1 ] recently gave a deterministic ( 1 − 1 / e − ε ) (1-1/e-\varepsilon) -approximation for maximizing a non-negative monotone submodular function subject to a matroid constraint, with query complexity O ~ ε ​ ( n ​ r ) \widetilde{O}_{\varepsilon}(nr) . Their algorithm uses an integer parameter ℓ \ell , which Buchbinder and Feldman fix to ℓ = 1 + ⌈ 1 / ε ⌉ \ell=1+\lceil 1/\varepsilon\rceil (their Corollary 3.6, with the choice anticipated in the discussion following their Proposition 1.1) via a loose bound on ( 1 + 1 / ℓ ) − ℓ (1+1/\ell)^{-\ell} . We point out two purely elementary refinements. First, the classical Pólya–Szegő inequality ( 1 + 1 / ℓ ) − ℓ ≤ e − 1 ​ ( 1 + 1 / ( 2 ​ ℓ ) ) (1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\leq e^{-1}(1+1/(2\ell)) replaces the loose step in their proof and permits ℓ = ⌈ 1 / ( 2 ​ e ​ ε ) ⌉ \ell=\lceil 1/(2e\varepsilon)\rceil , shrinking the hidden constant in O ~ ε ​ ( n ​ r ) \widetilde{O}_{\varepsilon}(nr) by a factor ≈ 2 0.816 / ε \approx 2^{0.816/\varepsilon} . Second, an alternating-series tail bound for log ⁡ ( 1 + t ) \log(1+t) yields the asymptotically sharp inequality ( 1 + 1 / ℓ ) − ℓ ≤ 1 e ​ exp ⁡ ( 1 2 ​ ℓ − 1 3 ​ ℓ 2 + 1 4 ​ ℓ 3 ) , (1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\,\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2\ell}-\frac{1}{3\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\ell^{3}}\right), which matches the true expansion of ( 1 + 1 / ℓ ) − ℓ (1+1/\ell)^{-\ell} through order ℓ − 3 \ell^{-3} and translates into ℓ ⋆ = 1 / ( 2 ​ e ​ ε ) − 5 / 12 + O ⁡ ( ε ) \ell_{\star}=1/(2e\varepsilon)-5/12+O(\varepsilon) . The asymptotic class O ~ ε ​ ( n ​ r ) \widetilde{O}_{\varepsilon}(nr) of the query complexity is unchanged in either case; only the implicit constant in ε \varepsilon is improved. All inequalities in this note are formalized and machine-checked in Lean 4 against Mathlib.

Introduction

Background.

Maximizing a non-negative monotone submodular function

f\colon 2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
subject to a matroid

\mathcal{M}=(N,\mathcal{I})
of rank
r
is a foundational
constrained optimization problem with applications in approximation
algorithms, machine learning, and combinatorial optimization. The
celebrated continuous greedy algorithm of Călinescu, Chekuri,
Pál, and Vondrák
[
2
]
achieves a
(1-1/e)
-approximation
in expectation, matching the information-theoretic threshold of
Nemhauser and Wolsey
[
3
]
. Whether a
deterministic

(1-1/e-\varepsilon)
-approximation exists in polynomial time was
open for over a decade.

The Buchbinder–Feldman result.

Buchbinder and Feldman
[
1
]
resolved this question, presenting
the first deterministic
(1-1/e-\varepsilon)
-approximation with
query complexity
\widetilde{O}_{\varepsilon}(nr)
.

The body
of
[

]
(e.g. their Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 3.6) phrases
the guarantee as
1-1/e-O(\varepsilon)
, since their fast Algorithm 2
incurs a separate discretization slack of
2\varepsilon\cdot f(\mathrm{OPT})

in
[
1
, Cor. 3.6]
; the
(1-1/e-\varepsilon)
form quoted here
matches their abstract and is recovered by relabelling

\varepsilon
. We make the dependence on the

(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}
-component explicit in §
2
.
Their Algorithm 2 is a
non-oblivious local search parameterised by a positive integer

\ell
; its analysis (their Proposition 1.1) gives the guarantee

f(S_{[\ell]})\;\geq\;\bigl(1-(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\bigr)\cdot f(\mathrm{OPT})\;+\;(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\cdot f(\emptyset).

(1)

Translating (
1
) into a
(1-1/e-\varepsilon)

guarantee requires
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\leq e^{-1}+\varepsilon
.
Buchbinder and Feldman bound

(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e(1-1/\ell)}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{2}{\ell}\right)\quad(\ell\geq 2),

(2)

and
set

\ell_{\mathrm{BF}}=1+\lceil 1/\varepsilon\rceil
,
for which the inequality
\ell_{\mathrm{BF}}\geq 1+1/\varepsilon>2/(e\varepsilon)
(using

) gives

2/(e\ell_{\mathrm{BF}})<\varepsilon
, and (
2
)
then yields
(1+1/\ell_{\mathrm{BF}})^{-\ell_{\mathrm{BF}}}\leq e^{-1}+\varepsilon
. They do not pursue the minimal
\ell
that
suffices.

Per-iteration cost.

Each evaluation of the auxiliary function
g^{\prime}
used by
Algorithm 2 reduces to
2^{\ell}
value-oracle queries on
f
(one per
subset of a size-
\ell
set); the dependence of the total query
complexity on
\ell
is therefore of the form

2^{\ell}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(n,r,1/\varepsilon)
. Reducing

\ell
by an additive constant thus reduces the hidden constant
in
\widetilde{O}_{\varepsilon}(nr)
by a multiplicative factor.

This note.

We point out that the step (
2
) can be tightened by
purely elementary means, with no change to Algorithm 2 or to its
correctness analysis. Two tightenings are presented.

1.

Pólya–Szegő bound (§
3
).
The classical
inequality
e<(1+1/n)^{n+1/2}

[
5
,
4
]
gives

(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\ell}\right),

which is a factor of
4
tighter than (
2
) and permits

\ell=\lceil 1/(2e\varepsilon)\rceil
. The factor
2e
saved in

\ell
contracts the hidden constant in the query complexity by

2^{(1-1/(2e))/\varepsilon}\approx 2^{0.816/\varepsilon}
.

2.

Asymptotically sharp bound (§
4
).
An
alternating-series tail bound for
\log(1+t)
yields

(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\,\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2\ell}-\frac{1}{3\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\ell^{3}}\right),

(3)

matching the true asymptotic expansion of
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}

through order
\ell^{-3}
. This translates to

\ell_{\star}(\varepsilon)=1/(2e\varepsilon)-5/12+O(\varepsilon)

in the real-valued sense; after rounding up to an integer, the saving
over the Pólya–Szegő value is at most one unit in
\ell
and
is occasionally zero (cf. §
5
).
The value of (
3
) is asymptotic rather than practical. The elementary line of analysis can be
extended to higher orders, but the returns diminish rapidly; matching
through
\ell^{-3}
already places
\ell_{\star}(\varepsilon)
within
an additive
O(\varepsilon)
of its asymptotic value.

Scope and what this note is not.

The contribution of this note is purely analytical: we replace one
elementary inequality used in the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 1.1]

with a sharper one. Algorithm 2 of
[
1
]
, its correctness
analysis, and its asymptotic class
\widetilde{O}_{\varepsilon}(nr)
are
unchanged. The improvements are best read as a refinement of the
implicit constant in
\varepsilon
rather than as a separate
algorithmic result. The BF’24 algorithm is, as the authors emphasise,
theoretical and not intended as a practical implementation; finding a
practical deterministic
(1-1/e-\varepsilon)
-approximation under a
matroid constraint remains an interesting open problem. The
elementary line of analysis pursued here therefore has primarily
expository value.

f\colon 2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
on a finite ground set

|N|=n
, and a matroid
\mathcal{M}=(N,\mathcal{I})
of rank

.
We assume access to

via a value oracle that, on input

in unit time.
Buchbinder and Feldman
[

establish the
guarantee (
1
) above for the output

S_{[\ell]}\in\mathcal{I}
(a base of
\mathcal{M}
) of their

[

),
parameterised by a positive integer
\ell
. Ignoring the non-negative
term

, the corresponding approximation ratio is

\rho(\ell)\;=\;1-(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell},

[
1
]
, the polynomial-time version)

, to guarantee

(up to the

3
The Pólya–Szegő bound

We replace (
2
) by a sharper, elementary bound.

Lemma 1
.

For every integer
\ell\geq 1
,

(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\ell}\right).

(4)

Remark
.

Inequality (
4
) is a direct consequence of the
classical bound
e<(1+1/n)^{n+1/2}
– see
Khattri
[
5
]
for three short proofs; the inequality
also appears in the problem book of Pólya and
Szegő
[
4
, Part I, Ch. 4, §2, Problem 168]
, where
the monotonicity of
(1+1/x)^{x+p}
for
p\geq 1/2
together with
the limit
\lim(1+1/x)^{x+1/2}=e
gives
e\leq(1+1/n)^{n+1/2}

for every integer
n\geq 1
. For related sharper approximations of

(1+1/n)^{n}
in the Batir–Cancan parameterisation see
Hu–Mortici
[
6
]
. We give a short self-contained
derivation of (
4
) below; the Hermite–Hadamard step
mirrors Khattri’s second proof
[
5
, §3]
, applied to
a different convex/concave pairing.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Taking logarithms, (
4
) is equivalent to

1-\ell\log(1+1/\ell)\;\leq\;\log(1+1/(2\ell)).

(5)

From
\log(1+x)=\int_{0}^{x}(1+s)^{-1}\,ds
with the substitution

u=\ell s
,

\ell\log(1+1/\ell)\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ell}{\ell+u}\,du\;=\;1-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{u}{\ell+u}\,du,

so the left-hand side of (
5
) equals

\int_{0}^{1}u/(\ell+u)\,du
. The integrand
g(u)=u/(\ell+u)
is concave
on
[0,1]
, since
g^{\prime\prime}(u)=-2\ell/(\ell+u)^{3}<0
. By the upper half
of the Hermite–Hadamard inequality (for concave
g
on
[a,b]
,

\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}g\leq g((a+b)/2)
),

\int_{0}^{1}g(u)\,du\;\leq\;g\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\;=\;\frac{1}{2\ell+1}.

(6)

For the right-hand side of (
5
), set
s=1/(2\ell)
.
The elementary bound
\log(1+s)\geq s/(1+s)
for
s\geq 0
(whose
difference has non-negative derivative
s/(1+s)^{2}
and vanishes at

s=0
) gives

\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{2\ell}\right)\;\geq\;\frac{1/(2\ell)}{1+1/(2\ell)}\;=\;\frac{1}{2\ell+1}.

(7)

Combining (
6
) and (
7
)
yields (
5
), and hence (
4
).
∎

Lemma
1
tightens the factor
2/\ell

in (
2
) by a factor of
4
, and we obtain a
correspondingly smaller
\ell
:

Proposition 2
.

Let
f\colon 2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be non-negative monotone
submodular,
\mathcal{M}
a matroid, and
\varepsilon>0
. Setting

\ell\;=\;\left\lceil\frac{1}{2e\varepsilon}\right\rceil

in
[
1
, Proposition 1.1]
suffices to guarantee

f(S_{[\ell]})\;\geq\;\left(1-\frac{1}{e}-\varepsilon\right)\cdot f(\mathrm{OPT}).

Proof.

By Lemma
1
,

(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\ell}\right)\;=\;\frac{1}{e}+\frac{1}{2e\ell}.

The choice
\ell=\lceil 1/(2e\varepsilon)\rceil
gives

1/(2e\ell)\leq\varepsilon
, so
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\leq 1/e+\varepsilon
. The conclusion follows from (
1
) and

f(\emptyset)\geq 0
.
∎

4
An asymptotically sharp bound

The Pólya–Szegő bound (
4
) captures the leading

1/(2\ell)
term of the expansion

(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\;=\;\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\ell}-\frac{5}{24\ell^{2}}+\frac{5}{48\ell^{3}}-O(\ell^{-4})\right),

but its
1/(2\ell)
correction differs from the true second-order
behaviour. We give a one-line tightening that captures the expansion
through order
\ell^{-3}
.

Lemma 3

(Alternating tail of
\log(1+t)
)
.

For every
t\geq 0
,

\log(1+t)\;\geq\;t-\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\frac{t^{3}}{3}-\frac{t^{4}}{4}.

(8)

Proof.

Let
h(t)=\log(1+t)-(t-t^{2}/2+t^{3}/3-t^{4}/4)
. Then
h(0)=0

and

h^{\prime}(t)\;=\;\frac{1}{1+t}-(1-t+t^{2}-t^{3})\;=\;\frac{1-(1+t)(1-t+t^{2}-t^{3})}{1+t}\;=\;\frac{t^{4}}{1+t},

which is non-negative for
t>-1
, in particular for
t\geq 0
.
Thus
h
is non-decreasing on
[0,\infty)
, and
h(t)\geq h(0)=0
.
∎

Theorem 4
.

For every integer
\ell\geq 1
,

(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{e}\,\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2\ell}-\frac{1}{3\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\ell^{3}}\right).

(9)

Moreover the right-hand side, expanded as an asymptotic series in

1/\ell
, agrees with
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}/e^{-1}
through order

\ell^{-3}
:

\frac{1}{e}\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2\ell}-\frac{1}{3\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\ell^{3}}\right)\;=\;\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\ell}-\frac{5}{24\ell^{2}}+\frac{5}{48\ell^{3}}+O(\ell^{-4})\right).

Proof.

Apply Lemma
3
with
t=1/\ell
:

\log(1+1/\ell)\;\geq\;\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{2\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{3\ell^{3}}-\frac{1}{4\ell^{4}}.

Multiplying by
\ell
,

\ell\,\log(1+1/\ell)\;\geq\;1-\frac{1}{2\ell}+\frac{1}{3\ell^{2}}-\frac{1}{4\ell^{3}}.

Exponentiating gives

(1+1/\ell)^{\ell}\;\geq\;e\cdot\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2\ell}+\frac{1}{3\ell^{2}}-\frac{1}{4\ell^{3}}\right),

and (
9
) follows on taking reciprocals.

For the asymptotic claim, write

\eta:=1/(2\ell)-1/(3\ell^{2})+1/(4\ell^{3})
. Expanding
\exp(\eta)=1+\eta+\eta^{2}/2+\eta^{3}/6+O(\eta^{4})
and collecting powers of

1/\ell
gives

1+\frac{1}{2\ell}+(\frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{3})\ell^{-2}+\cdots=1+1/(2\ell)-5/(24\ell^{2})+5/(48\ell^{3})+O(\ell^{-4})
,
which matches the known expansion of
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}/e^{-1}
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
f\colon 2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be non-negative monotone
submodular,
\mathcal{M}
a matroid, and
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
\ell_{\star}(\varepsilon)
denote the smallest integer
\ell\geq 1

such that
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\leq e^{-1}+\varepsilon
(well-defined
since
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}\to e^{-1}
as
\ell\to\infty
, and equal
to
1
for
\varepsilon\geq 1/2-e^{-1}\approx 0.132
); equivalently, the
smallest
\ell
that suffices in
[
1
, Proposition 1.1]
to give

f(S_{[\ell]})\;\geq\;\left(1-\frac{1}{e}-\varepsilon\right)\cdot f(\mathrm{OPT}).

Then
\ell_{\star}(\varepsilon)
satisfies the asymptotic estimate

\ell_{\star}(\varepsilon)\;=\;\frac{1}{2e\varepsilon}-\frac{5}{12}+O(\varepsilon)\qquad(\varepsilon\to 0^{+}).

Proof.

Treat
\phi(\ell):=(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}
as a smooth positive
decreasing function of real
\ell\geq 1
, with the standard expansion

\phi(\ell)\;=\;\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\ell}-\frac{5}{24\ell^{2}}+O(\ell^{-3})\right).

(10)

A direct computation shows
\phi^{\prime}(\ell)<0
on
\ell\geq 1
, so

\phi
is a strict bijection from
[1,\infty)
onto
(e^{-1},1/2]
;
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2-e^{-1}]
there is therefore a
unique real
\tilde{\ell}(\varepsilon)\geq 1
with

\phi(\tilde{\ell})=e^{-1}+\varepsilon
, and

\ell_{\star}(\varepsilon)=\lceil\tilde{\ell}(\varepsilon)\rceil

differs from
\tilde{\ell}(\varepsilon)
by at most
1
.

Substitute
\ell=1/(2e\varepsilon)+c
with
c
to be determined.
Then
1/\ell=2e\varepsilon-4e^{2}c\,\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3})
, hence

\frac{1}{2\ell}\;=\;e\varepsilon-2e^{2}c\,\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}),\qquad\frac{5}{24\ell^{2}}\;=\;\frac{5e^{2}}{6}\,\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Inserting into (
10
) and using

\phi(\tilde{\ell})-1/e=\varepsilon
,

\varepsilon\;=\;\frac{1}{e}\left(e\varepsilon-2e^{2}c\,\varepsilon^{2}-\frac{5e^{2}}{6}\,\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3})\right)\;=\;\varepsilon-2ec\,\varepsilon^{2}-\frac{5e}{6}\,\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Matching terms of order
\varepsilon^{2}
forces

2c+5/6=0
, i.e.
c=-5/12
. Therefore

\tilde{\ell}(\varepsilon)=1/(2e\varepsilon)-5/12+O(\varepsilon)
,
and the result follows on rounding up.

A constructive certificate for any specific
\ell
is provided by
Theorem
4
: it suffices to verify

\exp\bigl(1/(2\ell)-1/(3\ell^{2})+1/(4\ell^{3})\bigr)\leq 1+e\varepsilon
,
which can be checked in closed form for any candidate
\ell
.
∎

Remark
.

Compared to Proposition
2
’s

\lceil 1/(2e\varepsilon)\rceil
, the sharp value

\ell_{\star}(\varepsilon)
saves an additive
5/12+O(\varepsilon)
in

\ell
, hence a factor of
2^{5/12}\approx 1.33
in the per-iteration

2^{\ell}
factor. This is at most a factor of
2
in
practice: the integer-rounding boundary may occasionally swallow the
saving, leaving the same
\ell
as Proposition
2
.
The value of Theorem
4
is therefore primarily
asymptotic: it certifies that the elementary line of analysis
captures the true second-order behaviour of
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}
.
Pushing Lemma
3
further to

\log(1+t)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{2k}(-1)^{j+1}\,t^{j}/j
for any
k\geq 2

gives correspondingly higher-order bounds on
(1+1/\ell)^{-\ell}
, but
the returns diminish rapidly: matching through
\ell^{-3}
already
places
\ell_{\star}(\varepsilon)
within an additive
O(\varepsilon)

of its asymptotic value, and integer rounding renders any further
refinement effectively invisible at the resolutions of interest.
