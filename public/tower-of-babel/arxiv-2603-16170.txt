Jianjun Jin

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the norms of multiplication operators acting between weighted Bergman spaces. First, we provide a proof for a norm estimate claim previously announced in our recent paper [ 10 ] . Second, we establish a sharp norm estimate for certain special multiplication operators between weighted Bergman spaces, a result that appears to be new in the literature.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\Delta=\{z:|z|<1\}
denote the unit disk in the
complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. For an open domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}
, we use
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
to denote the class of all analytic functions in
\Omega
. For
-1<\alpha<\infty
, we define the weighted Bergman space
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}={\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}(\Delta)
as

{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}(\Delta)=\{\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta):\|\phi\|_{\alpha}^{2}:=(\alpha+1)\iint_{\Delta}|\phi(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\frac{dxdy}{\pi}<\infty\}.

Let
g\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta)
. The
multiplication operator

M_{g}
, induced by
g
, is defined by

M_{g}(\phi)(z):=g(z)\phi(z),\,\,\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta).

For
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the growth space
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}=\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(\Delta)
as

\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(\Delta)=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta):\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}}:=\sup_{z\in\Delta}|\phi(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}<\infty\right\},

which is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}}
defined above.
It is well known that

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\beta>\alpha>-1
.
The multiplication operator
M_{g}
is bounded from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf A}_{\beta}^{2}
if and only if
g
belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
with
\gamma=(\beta-\alpha)/2.

Remark 1.2
.

See
[
12
]
for a proof of this result.

For
\beta>\alpha>-1
, when
M_{g}
is bounded from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf A}_{\beta}^{2}
, we say
g
is a
multiplier
from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf A}_{\beta}^{2}
. The class of all multipliers, denoted by
\mathbf{M}_{\alpha,\beta}
, is a Banach space with the following multiplier norm

\|g\|_{{\bf{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in{\bf A}^{2}_{\alpha}\\
\phi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|g\phi\|_{\beta}}{\|\phi\|_{\alpha}}.

In the paper
[
11
]
, Shimorin studied a special multiplication operator from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf A}_{\alpha+4}^{2}
, which is induced by the Schwarzian derivative of a univalent function. To recall the results of
[
11
]
, we first introduce some notations and definitions. We let
\mathcal{S}
be the class of all univalent functions
f
in
\Delta
with
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
.

For a locally univalent function
f
in an open domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{C}
, the
Schwarzian derivative

S(f)
of
f
is defined by

S(f)(z)=\bigg[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg]^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]^{2},\,z\in\Omega.

Here,
N(f)(z):=f^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z)
is called the Pre-Schwarzian derivative of
f
. The following multiplication operator has been considered by Shimorin in
[
11
]
.

M_{S(f)}(\phi)(z):=S(f)(z)\phi(z),\,\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta).

It is well known that

|S(f)|(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\leq 6,

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}
, and hence
S(f)
belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{2}
, see
[
3
]
. Then
S(f)
is a multiplier from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf A}_{\alpha+4}^{2}
for any
f\in\mathcal{S}
.
Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}
, the
integral means spectrum

\beta_{f}(t)
is defined by

(1.1)

\beta_{f}(t):=\limsup_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{\log\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{t}d\theta}{|\log(1-r)|}.

The
universal integral means spectrum

B(t)
is defined by

B(t)=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{S}}\beta_{f}(t).

It was proved in
[
11
]
that

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha>-1
. Then

\|S(f)\|_{{\bf{M}}_{\alpha,\alpha+4}}\leq 36\frac{\alpha+3}{\alpha+1},

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{S}
.

By using this result, Shimorin obtained better bounds for the universal integral means spectrum
B(t)
for
t=-1
and
t=-2
.
B(-2)=1
is known as the famous Brennan conjecture, see
[
5
]
. Later, following this line of thought, Hedenmalm and Shimorin established in
[
7
]
the current best upper bound estimate about
B(-2)
and other universal integral means spectra. For more results about the Brennan conjecture and universal integral means spectrum, see
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
.

For simplicity, in the sequel, for a univalent function
f\in\mathcal{S}
, we will write

\mathbb{M}_{f}(\alpha):=\|S(f)\|_{{\bf M}_{\alpha,\alpha+4}}.

For a multiplier
g
from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf A}_{\beta}^{2}
with
\beta>\alpha>-1
, we will write

\mathbb{M}_{g}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathbb{M}_{g(z)}(\alpha,\beta):=\|g\|_{{\bf M}_{\alpha,\beta}}.

There is a close connection between the norm of the multiplication operator
{M}_{S(f)}
and the Brennan conjecture.
It has been shown in
[
11
]
that

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\alpha>-1
. We have

\mathbb{M}_{\kappa}^{2}(\alpha)=\frac{36(\alpha+3)(\alpha+5)}{(\alpha+2)(\alpha+4)},

Here,
\kappa
is the well-known Koebe function, which is defined by

\kappa(z):=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}},z\in\Delta.

If some univalent function
f\in\mathcal{S}
satisfies that

\mathbb{M}_{f}^{2}(\alpha)\leq\mathbb{M}_{\kappa}^{2}(\alpha)=\frac{36(\alpha+3)(\alpha+5)}{(\alpha+2)(\alpha+4)},

for any
\alpha>0
. Then
\beta_{f}(-2)\leq 1
, that is to say, the Brennan conjecture is true for the function
f
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{R}
be the class of all univalent rational functions
f
with
f\in\mathcal{S}
. In the recent paper
[
10
]
, we have proved that
if
R\in\mathcal{U}_{R}
has at least one critical point on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
, i.e., there is at least one point
z_{0}
on
\mathbb{T}
such that
R^{\prime}(z_{0})=0
, then
\beta_{R}(-2)=1
.
On the other hand, by checking carefully the arguments in the proof of Proposition
8
in
[
11
, Page 1632]
, we see that, if there is a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathbb{M}_{f}(\alpha)+\mathbb{M}_{f}(\alpha+2)\leq\mathbb{M}_{\kappa}(\alpha)+\mathbb{M}_{\kappa}(\alpha+2),

for
\alpha\in(0,\varepsilon)
, then
\beta_{f}({-2})\leq 1
. Moreover, for
R\in\mathcal{U}_{R}
that have at least one critical point on
\mathbb{T}
, if there are two small constants
\epsilon>0,\eta>0
such that
\mathbb{M}_{R}(\alpha)<\mathbb{M}_{\kappa}(\alpha)-\eta

for
\alpha\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\cup(2-\epsilon,2+\epsilon)
, then we will obtain that
\beta_{R}(-2)<1
, which is a contradiction. This leads us to conjecture the following result.
The first aim of this paper is to give a proof for the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

For each
\alpha>-1
, we have
\mathbb{M}_{R}(\alpha)\geq\mathbb{M}_{\kappa}(\alpha)
for all
R\in\mathcal{U}_{R}
that have at least one critical point on
\mathbb{T}.

In view of Theorem
1.1
, two natural questions are the following

Question 1.6
.

For a multiplier
g
with
\|g\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{(\beta-\alpha)/2}}=1
, what is its exact multiplier norm?

Question 1.7
.

What is the exact value of

\mathbb{M}(\alpha,\beta):=\sup_{\|g\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{(\beta-\alpha)/2}}=1}\|g\|_{{\bf M}_{\alpha,\beta}}?

We will partially answer these two questions. We shall prove that

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
\beta-\alpha>0
. Let
g_{0}(z)=(1-z^{2})^{-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}},z\in\Delta
. Then we have

\mathbb{M}_{g_{0}}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{2^{\beta-\alpha}}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+2)}{\Gamma(\alpha+2)}\Big[\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha/2)}{\Gamma(1+\beta/2)}\Big]^{2}.

Here
\Gamma
is the usual Gamma function, see
[
1
]
.

Remark 1.9
.

Note that

S(\kappa)(z)=-\frac{6}{(1-z^{2})^{2}}.

Then the first part of Proposition
1.4
follows by taking
\beta=\alpha+4
in Theorem
1.8
. To the best of our knowledge, this result is novel.

From Theorem
1.8
, we obtain that

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
\beta>\alpha>-1
. Then

\mathbb{M}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)\geq\frac{1}{2^{\beta-\alpha}}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\Big[\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha/2)}{\Gamma(1+\beta/2)}\Big]^{2}.

1.5
and
1.8
in Section 3 and 4, respectively. In Section 5, we present several remarks.

2.
Some Auxiliary Results

In this section, we present some auxiliary results which are needed in our proof of main theorems of this paper. First, we need the following integral representation of weighted Bergman spaces. Let
\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{w=u+iv:v>0\}
be the upper half-plane and
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,\infty)
be the set of positive real numbers. For
\alpha>-1
, we define the weighted Bergman space on the upper half-plane
\bf{A}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
as

{\bf{A}}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})=\left\{h\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C}_{+}):\|h\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|h(u+iv)|^{2}v^{\alpha}\frac{dudv}{\pi}<\infty\right\}.

The following lemma will be useful.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
. Then
h
belongs to
{\bf{A}}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if there is a function
\tilde{h}\in L_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

(2.1)

h(w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{h}(t)e^{iwt}\,dt,\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

and we have the following norm identity

\|h\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{2^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{h}(t)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t^{\alpha+1}}.

Here
L_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
denotes the weighted Lebesgue space consisting of all measurable functions
\tilde{h}
with norm

\|\tilde{h}\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{h}(t)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t^{\alpha+1}}.

Remark 2.2
.

{\bf{A}}_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is isometrically isomorphic to the space
L_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
under the Fourier-Laplace type transform (
2.1
). See
[
4
]
for a proof of this lemma.

We will need the following estimates.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha>-1
. If
2\lambda>\alpha+2
, then

(2.2)

\|{(1-rz^{2})^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+2)\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{2^{\alpha+1}[\Gamma(\lambda)]^{2}}\cdot\frac{1+{o}(1)}{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}},\,{\textup{as}}\,\,r\rightarrow 1^{-}.

and

(2.3)

\|{(1-rz)^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+2)\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{[\Gamma(\lambda)]^{2}}\cdot\frac{1+{o}(1)}{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}},\,{\textup{as}}\,\,r\rightarrow 1^{-}.

If
0<2\lambda<\alpha+2
, then there is a constant
M>0
such that for all
r\in(0,1)
,

(2.4)

\|{(1-rz)^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}\leq M.

Here and in the sequel,
{o}(1)
denotes a quantity depending on
r
such that
{o}(1)\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
, and which may vary from line to line.

Proof.

First, for
\gamma>0
, we have

(2.5)

\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\gamma}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+\gamma)}{n!\Gamma(\gamma)}z^{n},z\in\Delta.

Also, for any
\phi=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}^{2}(\Delta)
, we have

(2.6)

\|\phi\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n!\Gamma(\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+2)}|a_{n}|^{2}.

Let
r\in(0,1)
and
2\lambda>\alpha+2
. Then, from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we obtain that

(2.7)

\|{(1-rz^{2})^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n)!\Gamma(\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(2n+\alpha+2)}\Big|\frac{\Gamma(n+\lambda)}{n!\Gamma(\lambda)}\Big|^{2}r^{2n},

and

(2.8)

\|{(1-rz)^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n!\Gamma(\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+2)}\Big|\frac{\Gamma(n+\lambda)}{n!\Gamma(\lambda)}\Big|^{2}r^{2n}.

By Stirling’s formula, we have

\frac{\Gamma(n+\lambda)}{n!}=(n+1)^{\lambda-1}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)],\,\,{\textup{as}}\,\,n\rightarrow\infty.

Here and later, we use
\widetilde{o}(1)
to denote a sequence that tends to
0
, i.e.,
o(1)\rightarrow 0,\,{\textup{as}}\,\,n\rightarrow\infty
, which may be different in different places. Then it follows from (
2.7
) that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|{(1-rz^{2})^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+2)}{[2(n+1)]^{\alpha+1}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]\cdot\frac{(n+1)^{2\lambda-2}}{[\Gamma(\lambda)]^{2}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\alpha+1}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+2)}{[\Gamma(\lambda)]^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(n+1)^{2\lambda-\alpha-3}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}.

Similarly, from (
2.8
), we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|{(1-rz)^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+2)}{[\Gamma(\lambda)]^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(n+1)^{2\lambda-\alpha-3}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}.

When
a<-1
, we have the well-known fact that there is a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{a}r^{2n}\leq C,

for all
r\in(0,1)
. Consequently, when
0<2\lambda<\alpha+2
so that
2\lambda-\alpha-3<-1
, we see that (
2.4
) holds.

On the other hand, when
2\lambda>\alpha+2
, we obtain from Stirling’s formula and
2.5
that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(n+1)^{2\lambda-\alpha-3}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}\cdot[\widetilde{o}(1)]\cdot r^{2n}.

Let
\widetilde{o}(1):=b_{n}
. Note that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
there exist two constants
C_{0}>0,N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|b_{n}|\leq C_{0}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
|b_{n}|\leq\varepsilon/2
for all
n\geq N_{0}
in the last term of (
2.11
).
It follows from again (
2.5
) that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}\cdot[\widetilde{o}(1)]\cdot r^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{0}}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}r^{2n}+\varepsilon/2\sum_{n={{n_{0}}+1}}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}r^{2n}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{0}}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}+\frac{\varepsilon/2}{{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}}:=C_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon/2}{{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}}

Meanwhile,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}\cdot[\widetilde{o}(1)]\cdot r^{2n}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=0}^{N_{0}}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}b_{n}r^{2n}-\varepsilon/2\sum_{n={{n_{0}}+1}}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}r^{2n}

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{n=0}^{N_{0}}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}|b_{n}|-\frac{\varepsilon/2}{{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}}:=-C_{2}-\frac{\varepsilon/2}{{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}}

Combining (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we obtain that

\displaystyle[{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}]\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(n+1)^{2\lambda-\alpha-3}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}\leq 1+\varepsilon/2+C_{1}[{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}],

and

\displaystyle[{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}]\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(n+1)^{2\lambda-\alpha-3}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}\geq 1-\varepsilon/2-C_{2}[{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}].

Since
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are independent of

, then we can find a constant

such that

C_{1}{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}<\varepsilon/2,\,\,\text{and}\,\,C_{2}{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}<\varepsilon/2,

for all

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon\leq[{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}}]\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(n+1)^{2\lambda-\alpha-3}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}\leq 1+\varepsilon,

for all

(2.14)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(n+1)^{2\lambda-\alpha-3}}[1+\widetilde{o}(1)]r^{2n}=\frac{1+{o}(1)}{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}},\,{\textup{as}}\,\,r\rightarrow 1^{-}.

Then, in view of (
2.14
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) follow from (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), respectively. The lemma is proved.

The following key lemma will also be needed.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha>-1,\beta-\alpha>0
. If
g
is a multiplier from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf A}_{\beta}^{2}
, then for any

\mathbb{M}_{g(az)}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\mathbb{M}_{g}(\alpha,\beta).

Remark 2.5
.

We provide a detailed proof of Lemma
2.4
, which also clarifies some imprecise statements in
[
9
, page 2310, lines 17-20]
.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Let
\gamma=(\beta-\alpha)/2>0
.We first show that if
g
is a multiplier from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf A}_{\beta}^{2}
, then the map
{\dagger}:a\mapsto g(az)
is analytic from the unit disk into
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
.
Since
g\in\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
, then for a fixed
a\in\Delta
and any
z\in\Delta
, we have
1-|z|^{2}\leq 1-|az|^{2}
so that

|g(az)|(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}=|g(az)|(1-|az|^{2})^{\gamma}\Big[\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|az|^{2}}\Big]^{\gamma}\leq\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}}.

This yields that
g(az)\in\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
and the mapping
{\dagger}
is well-defined.

We will show that
{\dagger}
is complex differentiable at an arbitrary point
a_{0}\in\Delta
. Choose
r_{0}>0
such that
|a_{0}|+r_{0}<1
. Then for any complex
\tau
with
|\tau|<r_{0}
, we have
a_{0}+\tau\in\Delta
, and for any
z\in\Delta
,
t\in[0,1]
,

|(a_{0}+t\tau)z|\leq|a_{0}||z|+t|\tau||z|\leq|a_{0}|+|\tau|<|a_{0}|+r_{0}<1.

For a fixed
z\in\Delta
, when
|\tau|<r_{0}
, we consider the function

\Psi_{z,\tau}(t)=g((a_{0}+t\tau)z)=g(a_{0}z+t\tau z),\,\,t\in[0,1].

Since
g
is analytic,
\psi_{z,h}
is continuously differentiable on
[0,1]
, and

\Psi_{z,\tau}^{\prime}(t)=\tau z\,g^{\prime}(a_{0}z+t\tau z).

Then, by the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

\Psi_{z,\tau}(1)-\psi_{z,\tau}(0)=\int_{0}^{1}\Psi_{z,\tau}^{\prime}(t)\,dt.

That is

g((a_{0}+\tau)z)-g(a_{0}z)=\tau z\int_{0}^{1}g^{\prime}(a_{0}z+t\tau z)\,dt.

It follows that for
|\tau|<r_{0}
,

(2.15)

\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}(z):=\frac{g((a_{0}+\tau)z)-g(a_{0}z)}{\tau}=z\int_{0}^{1}g^{\prime}(a_{0}z+t\tau z)\,dt.

We define
\widetilde{g}(z)=zg^{\prime}(a_{0}z)
. Note that
g^{\prime}
is continuous on the closed disk
\overline{\Delta}_{r_{1}}
centered at the origin with radius
r_{1}=\frac{|a_{0}|+r_{0}+1}{2}<1
. Then it is easy to see that

\widetilde{g}\in\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
.

From (
2.15
), for
|\tau|<1
, we have

(2.16)

\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}(z)-\widetilde{g}(z)=z\int_{0}^{1}\bigl[g^{\prime}(a_{0}z+t\tau z)-g^{\prime}(a_{0}z)\bigr]\,dt.

Since the derivative
g^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous on the closed disk
\overline{\Delta}_{r_{1}}
. For any given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
r_{2}>0
such that for all
w_{1},w_{2}\in\overline{\Delta}_{r_{1}}
with
|w_{1}-w_{2}|<r_{2}
, we have
|g^{\prime}(w_{1})-g^{\prime}(w_{2})|<\varepsilon
.

Choose
\delta=\min\{r_{0},r_{2}\}
. Note that for any
\tau
with
|\tau|<\delta
and for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
t\in[0,1]
, the points
a_{0}z
and
a_{0}z+t\tau z
lie in
\overline{\Delta}_{r_{1}}
because

|a_{0}z|\leq|a_{0}|<\frac{|a_{0}|+1}{2}<r_{1},

|a_{0}z+t\tau z|\leq|a_{0}|+|\tau|\leq|a_{0}|+r_{0}<r_{1}.

Furthermore,

|(a_{0}z+t\tau z)-a_{0}z|=|t\tau z|\leq|\tau|<r_{2}.

Thus the condition for uniform continuity is satisfied and it holds that for
|\tau|<\delta
,

|g^{\prime}(a_{0}z+t\tau z)-g^{\prime}(a_{0}z)|<\varepsilon,

for any
t\in[0,1]
, and
z\in\Delta
. It follows from (
2.16
) that for
|\tau|<\delta
,

|\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}(z)-\widetilde{g}(z)|\leq|z|\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon\,dt=\varepsilon|z|.

Consequently, we obtain that

\|\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}-\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}}=\sup_{|z|<1}|\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}(z)-\widetilde{g}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}\leq\varepsilon\sup_{|z|<1}|z|(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}\leq\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we conclude that

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\|\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}-\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}}=0.

This means that the mapping
{\dagger}:a\mapsto g(az)
is analytic from the unit disk to
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
.

Now, for a fixed
\phi\in\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}^{2}
, by using the above notations and arguments, we see that
\widetilde{g}\phi
belongs to
\mathbf{A}_{\beta}^{2}
and

(2.17)

\|\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}\phi-\widetilde{g}\phi\|_{\beta}\leq\|\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}-\widetilde{g}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{(\beta-\alpha)/2}}\|\phi\|_{\alpha},

when
\tau
is close enough to
0
. It follows that

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\|\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}\phi-\widetilde{g}\phi\|_{\beta}=0.

This proves that

Sub-Lemma 2.6
.

For a fixed
\phi\in\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}^{2}
, the mapping
{\ddagger}:a\mapsto g(az)\phi(z)
is analytic from the unit disk to
\mathbf{A}_{\beta}^{2}
.

Next we shall prove that

Sub-Lemma 2.7
.

For a fixed
\phi\in\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}^{2}
,

\lim_{\Delta\ni a\to e^{i\theta}}\|g(az)\phi(z)-g(e^{i\theta}z)\phi(z)\|_{\beta}=0,

for any
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
.

We write
g_{a}(z)=g(az)
. As noted above, we obtain that
\|g_{a}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\beta-\alpha}}\leq\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\beta-\alpha}}
so that both
g_{a}\phi
and
g\phi
belong to
\mathbf{A}_{\beta}^{2}
. Then from

\|g(az)\phi(z)-g(e^{i\theta}z)\phi(z)\|_{\beta}^{2}\leq 2\|g_{a}(z)\phi(z)\|_{\beta}^{2}+2\|g(e^{i\theta}z)\phi(z)\|_{\beta}^{2}\leq 4\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{(\beta-\alpha)/2}}^{2}\|\phi\|_{\alpha}^{2},

we see that, for any given
\varepsilon>0
, there is a constant
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

{\bf Int_{1}}:=\frac{\beta+1}{\pi}\int_{|z|>r_{0}}|g(az)\phi(z)-g(e^{i\theta}z)\phi(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta}dxdy<\varepsilon.

We proceed to consider the following integral

(2.18)

{\bf Int_{2}}:=\frac{\beta+1}{\pi}\int_{|z|\leq r_{0}}|g(az)\phi(z)-g(e^{i\theta}z)\phi(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta}dxdy.

Note that, on the closed disk
\{|z|\leq r_{0}\}
,
g(az)
converges uniformly to
g(e^{i\theta}z)
as
\Delta\ni a\to e^{i\theta}
, i.e.,

\lim_{\Delta\ni a\to e^{i\theta}}\sup_{|z|\leq r_{0}}|g(az)-g(e^{i\theta}z)|=0.

Hence for given
\varepsilon>0
, there is a constant
\delta>0
such that

\sup_{|z|\leq r_{0}}|g(az)-g(e^{i\theta}z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{(\beta-\alpha)/2}<\sqrt{\varepsilon},

for all
a\in\Delta
with
|a-e^{i\theta}|<\delta
. Consequently, when
a\in\Delta
with
|a-e^{i\theta}|<\delta
, we have

(2.19)

{\bf Int_{2}}\leq{\varepsilon}\frac{\beta+1}{\pi}\int_{|z|\leq r_{0}}|\phi(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dxdy\leq{\varepsilon}\frac{\beta+1}{(\alpha+1)\pi}\|\phi\|_{\alpha}^{2}.

From (
2
) and (
2.19
), we conclude that

\lim_{\Delta\ni a\to e^{i\theta}}\|g(az)\phi(z)-g(e^{i\theta}z)\phi(z)\|_{\beta}^{2}=0,

for any
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
. This proves Sub-Lemma
2.7

Finally, for any
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
, it is easy to check that
\|\phi(e^{i\theta}z)\|_{\alpha}=\|\phi(z)\|_{\alpha}
for any
\phi\in\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}^{2}

\mathbb{M}_{g(e^{i\theta}z)}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathbb{M}_{g}(\alpha,\beta),

for any
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]

2.6
and

\phi\in\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}^{2}

{\ddagger}:a\mapsto g(az)\phi(z),

is analytic from the unit disk

to

and continuous on the unit circle. Then

is subharmonic in

, see
[

. It follows from the maximum modulus principle that

\mathbb{M}_{g_{a}}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\mathbb{M}_{g}(\alpha,\beta),

for any

2.4
.

We also need the following weighted Hardy inequality. For completeness, we include a proof here for this result.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
p>1,r>0
,
a<p-1
. Let
f
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx<\infty
. Then we have

(2.20)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}\Bigl|\frac{f_{r}(x)}{x^{r}}\Bigr|^{p}dx\leq\Bigl[\frac{\Gamma\bigl(1-\frac{a+1}{p}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(r+1-\frac{a+1}{p}\bigr)}\Bigr]^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx,

where

f_{r}(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)}\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)^{r-1}f(t)\,dt.

Proof.

For fixed
x>0
, let
t=xu
with
u\in(0,1)
. Then

\frac{f_{r}(x)}{x^{r}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{r-1}f(xu)\,du.

Let
K(u)=\frac{(1-u)^{r-1}}{\Gamma(r)}
. By the Minkowski integral inequality,

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}\left|\frac{f_{r}(x)}{x^{r}}\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\leq\int_{0}^{1}K(u)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}|f(xu)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}du.

For fixed
u>0
, let
y=xu
so that

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}|f(xu)|^{p}dx=u^{-a-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{a}|f(y)|^{p}dy.

Consequently,

(2.21)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a}\left|\frac{f_{r}(x)}{x^{r}}\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{a}|f(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{1/p}\int_{0}^{1}K(u)u^{-(a+1)/p}du.

On the other hand, by the definition of Gamma function, we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}K(u)u^{-(a+1)/p}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{r-1}u^{-(a+1)/p}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{a+1}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(r+1-\frac{a+1}{p}\right)}.

Then the inequality (
2.20
) follows from (
2.21
) and (
2.22
). The lemma is proved.

) is true.

Final remarks

Remark 5.1
.

For
\beta>\alpha>-1
, take

g_{1}(z)=2^{(\alpha-\beta)/2}(1-z)^{-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}},\,z\in\Delta.

We can check that
g_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{(\beta-\alpha)/2}
and
\|g_{1}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{(\beta-\alpha)/2}}=1.
We will show that

(5.1)

{\mathbb{M}}_{g_{1}}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathbb{M}_{g_{0}}(\alpha,\beta).

First note that
\mathbb{M}_{g_{1}}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\mathbb{M}_{g_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
because for any
z\in\Delta,

|g_{1}(z)|/|g_{0}(z)|\leq\frac{|(1+z)^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}|}{2^{(\beta-\alpha)/2}}\leq 1.

Next we will show that

(5.2)

\mathbb{M}_{g_{1}}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)\geq\mathbb{M}_{g_{0}}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{2^{\beta-\alpha}}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+2)}{\Gamma(\alpha+2)}\Big[\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha/2)}{\Gamma(1+\beta/2)}\Big]^{2}.

From Lemma
2.3
, for
r\in(0,1)
,
2\lambda>\alpha+2
, we have

\|{(1-rz)^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+2)\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{[\Gamma(\lambda)]^{2}}\cdot\frac{1+{o}(1)}{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}},\,{\textup{as}}\,\,r\rightarrow 1^{-},

and

\|{(1-rz)^{-\lambda-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\|_{\beta}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(\beta+2)\Gamma(2\lambda-\alpha-2)}{[\Gamma(\lambda+\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2})]^{2}}\cdot\frac{1+{o}(1)}{(1-r^{2})^{2\lambda-\alpha-2}},\,{\textup{as}}\,\,r\rightarrow 1^{-}.

It follows from Lemma
2.4
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{g_{1}}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{g_{1}(rz)}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)\geq\sup\limits_{r\in(0,1)}\frac{\|2^{(\alpha-\beta)/2}{(1-rz)^{-\lambda-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\|_{\beta}^{2}}{\|{(1-rz)^{-\lambda}}\|_{\alpha}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\beta-\alpha}}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+2)}{\Gamma(\alpha+2)}\frac{[\Gamma(\lambda)]^{2}}{[\Gamma(\lambda+\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2})]^{2}}.

Then, (
5.2
) follows by letting
\lambda\to(\frac{\alpha}{2}+1)^{+}
. This proves (
5.1
).

Remark 5.2
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta)
. The multiplication operators are related to the following Volterra-type integral operators
I_{g}
and
J_{g}
, which are defined by

I_{g}(\phi)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\phi(\zeta)g^{\prime}(\zeta)\,d\zeta,\,\,\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta),

and

J_{g}(\phi)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\phi^{\prime}(\zeta)g(\zeta)\,d\zeta,\,\,\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta).

It is easy to see that

I_{g}(\phi)(z)+J_{g}(\phi)(z)=M_{g}(\phi)(z)-g(0)\phi(0).

For
\alpha>-1
, we recall that the Dirichlet-type space
{\bf{D}}_{\alpha}^{2}={\bf{D}}_{\alpha}^{2}(\Delta)
is defined by

{\bf D}_{\alpha}^{2}(\Delta)=\{\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta):\|\phi\|_{{\bf D}_{\alpha}}^{2}:=|\phi(0)|^{2}+(\alpha+1)\iint_{\Delta}|\phi^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\frac{dxdy}{\pi}<\infty\}.

Then we see that
\phi\in{\bf D}_{\alpha}^{2}
if and only if
\phi^{\prime}\in{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
.
From this, we can restate Theorem
1.1
equivalently as

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\beta>\alpha>-1
.
The operator
I_{g}
is bounded from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf D}_{\beta}^{2}
if and only if
g
belongs to the Bloch-type space
\mathfrak{B}_{\gamma}
with
\gamma=(\beta-\alpha)/2.

Here, for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, the Bloch-type space
\mathfrak{B}_{\gamma}=\mathfrak{B}_{\gamma}(\Delta)
is defined by

\mathfrak{B}_{\gamma}(\Delta)=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta):\|\phi\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{\gamma}}:=\sup_{z\in\Delta}|\phi^{\prime}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}<\infty\right\}.

Note that
g\in\mathfrak{B}_{\gamma}
if and only if
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
. When
I_{g}
is bounded from
{\bf A}_{\alpha}^{2}
to
{\bf D}_{\beta}^{2}
, the norm of the operator
I_{g}
about the symbol
g
, denoted by
\|g\|_{I}
, is defined by

\|g\|_{I}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in{\bf A}^{2}_{\alpha}\\
\phi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|I_{g}(\phi)\|_{{\bf D}_{\beta}}}{\|\phi\|_{\alpha}}.

Theorem
1.1
also can equivalently be present as

\beta>\alpha>-1

5.1
