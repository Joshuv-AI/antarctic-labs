On the center of distances of finite ultrametric spaces
Oleksiy Dovgoshey, Olga Rovenska

Abstract
The center of distances of a metric space ( X , d ) (X,d) is the set C ⁡ ( X ) C(X) of all t ∈ ℝ + t\in\mathbb{R}^{+} for which the equation d ⁡ ( x , p ) = t d(x,p)=t has a solution for each p ∈ X p\in X . We prove the inequality | C ⁡ ( X ) | ≤ 1 + ⌊ log 2 ⁡ n ⌋ |C(X)|\leq 1+\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor for all finite ultrametric spaces ( X , d ) (X,d) which have exactly n n points. It is also shown that for every integer n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 there exists a finite ultrametric space ( Y , ρ ) (Y,\rho) such that | Y | = n |Y|=n and | C ⁡ ( Y ) | = 1 + log 2 ⁡ ⌊ n ⌋ . |C(Y)|=1+\log_{2}\lfloor n\rfloor.

Introduction

The concept of the center of distances was introduced by Wojciech Bielaś, Szymon Plewik and Marta Walczyńska in paper
4
as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and let
D(X)
be the distance set of
(X,d)
,

D(X):=\{d(x,y):x,y\in X\}.

The center of distances of
(X,d)
is a subset
C(X)
of the distance set
D(X)

defined as

C(X):=\{t\in D(X):\forall p\in X\ \exists x\in X\ d(p,x)=t\}.

(1)

This concept was used in
4
for a generalization of John von Neumann Theorem on permutations of two sequences with the same set of cluster points
23
. The centers of
distances of ultrametric
spaces generated by labeled trees are found in
14
.
Some interesting properties of the center of distances are proved in
2
;
3
;
1
;
17
;
19
, but the authors are not aware of any results describing the properties of these centers in the case of arbitrary ultrametric spaces.

In the present paper we investigate the center of distances of finite ultrametric spaces.
The main purpose of the paper is to provide a solution to the following problem.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer number. Find the smallest integer
M(n)
satisfying the inequality

|C(X)|\leq M(n)

for each finite ultrametric spaces
(X,d)
which have at most
n
points.

The paper is organized as follows. The next section contains some definitions and facts from the theory of metric spaces and graph theory. Theorem
3.10
of Section
3
gives a solution of Problem
1.2
and it is the main result of the paper. In the final Section
4
we formulate three conjectures connected with the center of distances of finite ultrametric spaces.

Definition 2.1

A metric space

Definition 2.2

Let

(3)

Definition 2.10

Definition 2.11

.
If the number
n
can be written as

n=2^{l},

where
l
is an integer number, then the equality

\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor=l

(96)

holds and, consequently (
54
) turns to the inequality

|C(X)|\leq 1+l.

(97)

The last inequality follows from (
60
) and the definition of the function
M:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, this definition implies also the existence of an ultrametric space
(Y,\rho)
that satisfies (
55
) and the equality
|Y|=n
.

Let us consider the case when the number
n
cannot be written in the form
2^{l}
with integer
l
. In this case we can find
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that

2^{l}<n\leq 2^{l+1}-1.

(98)

The last double inequality implies equality (
96
), that, as was noted above, gives us (
54
) in form (
97
).

To complete the proof it suffices to note that equalities (
60
), (
61
) imply the equality

M(n)=l+1

for each
n
satisfying (
98
), since
M:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is increasing. Thus, by the definition of
M:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, we can find an ultrametric space
(Y,\rho)
such that (
55
) holds whenever
n
satisfies (
98
).

The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

In paper
14
, the authors conjectured that Theorem
3.10
holds for all finite ultrametric spaces (see Conjecture 3 of
14
).

The next result can be considered as a dual form of Theorem
3.10
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer number and let
(X,d)
be a finite ultrametric space such that

|C(X)|=m,

where
C(X)
is the center of distances of the space
(X,d)
. Then the set
X
contains at least
2^{m-1}
points,

|X|\geq 2^{m-1}.

Example 3.13
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer number and let
X
be the set of all finite binary words
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, where
x_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then the cardinal number of
X
is
2^{n}
,

|X|=2^{n}.

Let us define the distance between words
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
as

d(x,y):=2^{-m}

if
m
is the first place at which the words
x
and
y
are different, and write

d(x,y):=0

if
x=y
.
Then
(X,d)
is an ultrametric space with the center of distances

C(X)=\{0\}\cup\left\{\tfrac{1}{2},\ldots,\tfrac{1}{2^{n}}\right\}.

Thus the equalities

|C(X)|=1+n=1+\log_{2}|X|=1+\lfloor\log_{2}|X|\rfloor

hold.

Remark 3.14
.

The above example and results of paper
22
show that the centers of distances of finite ultrametric spaces are also connected with topologies on the set of finite words.

Concluding Remarks and Expected Results

Below we will use the concept of weakly similar ultrametric spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,d)
and
(Y,\rho)
be ultrametric spaces. A mapping
\Phi:X\to Y
is a weak similarity of
(X,d)
and
(Y,\rho)
if
\Phi
is bijective and there is a strictly increasing function
f:D(Y)\to D(X)
such that the equality

d(x,y)=f\bigl(\rho(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))\bigr)

holds for all
x,y\in X
.

If
\Phi:X\to Y
is a weak similarity, then we say that
(X,d)
and
(Y,\rho)
are
weakly similar
.

Remark 1
.

The concept of weak similarity was introduced in
10
for general semimetric spaces.

Conjecture 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(X,d)
and
(Y,\rho)
be ultrametric spaces such that

|X|=|Y|=2^{n}

(99)

and

|C(X)|=n+1.

(100)

Then the equality

|C(Y)|=|C(X)|

holds if and only if
(X,d)
and
(Y,\rho)
are weakly similar.

It is easy to see that isometric ultrametric spaces have one and the same center of distances. The following conjecture claims, in particular, that the converse statement is also valid under some restrictions on the spaces and their centers of distances.

Conjecture 4.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(X,d)
and
(Y,\rho)
be ultrametric spaces satisfying equalities (
99
) and (
100
). Then the equality

C(X)=C(Y)

holds if and only if
(X,d)
and
(Y,\rho)
are isometric.

The next conjecture was partially motivated by results of Mateusz Kula on the center of distances of Bernstein subsets of
\mathbb{R}

18
.

Conjecture 4.4
.

Let
A
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

0\in A.

Then there exists a finite ultrametric space
(X,d)
that satisfies the equalities

D(X)=C(X)=A,

where
D(X)
and
C(X)
are the distance set of
(X,d)
and, respectively, the center of distances of
(X,d)
.
