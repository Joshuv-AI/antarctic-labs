Psychometric evaluation of the Mastocytosis Symptom Severity Daily Diary (MS2D2) in adults with nonadvanced systemic mastocytosis (NonAdvSM)
Authors: James C Marcus, Cem Akin, Jenna Zhang, Rachael Easton, Michelle Lim-Watson, Frank Siebenhaar, Chad Gwaltney, Ralph Turner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study evaluated the measurement properties and ability to detect clinically meaningful change of the Mastocytosis Symptom Severity Daily Diary (MS2D2), a patient-reported outcome instrument designed to measure symptom severity in adults with nonadvanced systemic mastocytosis (NonAdvSM).MethodsPsychometric evaluation of the MS2D2 used blinded data from 230 adults with NonAdvSM enrolled in the Summit Phase 2 study (NCT05186753). The study comprised two parts: Part 1 (n = 54) for dose-finding and initial domain structure development, and Part 2 (n = 179) for efficacy evaluation, validation and longitudinal assessment. Analyses included item-level statistics, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), internal consistency (Cronbach’s α), test–retest reliability (ICC), convergent and known-groups validity, responsiveness, and anchor-based estimation of meaningful change thresholds.ResultsStructural validity supported a four-domain, 11-item Total Symptom Score (TSS) framework. Internal consistency and test–retest reliability were excellent (α = 0.79–0.95; ICC = 0.95–0.98). Convergent validity was supported through strong correlations with disease-specific symptom and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) measures (r = 0.85 and r = 0.59). Known-groups validation revealed domain scores differing by 2–5 points between anchor severity strata and TSS by 2.8 points (all p < 0.05). Scores were sensitive to change, correlating (r = 0.39–0.74) with changes in patient status. TSS within-patient meaningful improvement thresholds were 1.6–2.2 points (0–10 scale) and 17.2–24.7 points (0–110 sum), with domains showing similar ranges. Percent change thresholds indicated meaningful improvement at approximately 30–40% reduction for both TSS and domain scores.ConclusionsMS2D2 is a reliable, valid, and responsive instrument for assessing symptom severity in NonAdvSM, with evidence supporting its use in clinical trials to evaluate treatment benefit.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s11136-026-04291-z.Keywords: Non-advanced systemic mastocytosis, Patient-reported outcome, Instrument development, Psychometric evaluation, Symptoms

Introduction

Systemic mastocytosis (SM) is a rare, heterogeneous disease marked by the buildup of abnormal mast cells within the bone marrow and other extracutaneous tissues, most commonly driven by a somatic mutation in the KIT gene, which encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase involved in mast cell growth and survival [1, 2]. SM can be divided into two main categories: advanced SM and non-advanced SM (NonAdvSM). NonAdvSM includes indolent systemic mastocytosis (ISM), bone marrow mastocytosis (BMM), and smoldering systemic mastocytosis (SSM), each of which manifests with a spectrum of symptoms [3–5]. Most SM cases fall under the NonAdvSM classification, with ISM representing the predominant subtype [6, 7].

NonAdvSM is associated with a chronic, fluctuating, and multisystem symptom profile driven by excessive release of mast cell mediators, resulting in a wide range of disabling and potentially serious manifestations. Among these, recurrent anaphylaxis, affecting over 25% of patients, is the most severe and life-threatening [8, 9]. The pervasive, unpredictable nature of symptoms—including skin, gastrointestinal, pain, fatigue, and cognitive issues—leads to substantial limitations in daily functioning and contributes to a pronounced decline in health-related quality of life (HRQoL) [10, 11].

NonAdvSM is primarily managed through symptomatic treatments—such as antihistamines, leukotriene receptor antagonists, mast cell stabilizers, and proton pump inhibitors—which do not target the underlying clonal mast cell proliferation. This limitation underscores a critical gap in treatment and highlights the need for disease-modifying therapies in NonAdvSM. To inform development of novel targeted therapies, patient‑reported outcome (PRO) measures are crucial for assessing treatment efficacy in clinical trials in NonAdvSM. PRO measures intended to support regulatory submissions must meet strict standards for content validity, structural validity, reliability, construct validity, responsiveness, and interpretability [12–14]. However, there is currently a lack of publicly available PRO instruments for NonAdvSM that satisfy these regulatory requirements.

The Mastocytosis Symptom Severity Daily Diary (MS2D2) is a 17-item, PRO measure designed for use in clinical trials to capture the severity and frequency of symptoms experienced by adult patients with NonAdvSM over a 24-h recall period. Mindful of the limitations in current assessments, development of the MS2D2 followed a robust qualitative research strategy based on regulatory guidance, including a comprehensive concept of interest targeted literature review, interviews with leading SM clinicians, concept elicitation with patients, and cognitive interviews to ensure patient relevance and clarity [12].

The primary endpoint in the Summit study was change from baseline to Week 24 in the MS2D2 Total Symptom Score (TSS; 0–110 scale). Summit was a multi-part, randomized, placebo‑controlled, Phase 2 study, designed to assess the safety and efficacy of bezuclastinib (CGT9486)—a highly selective type I tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) that targets mutations in the KIT receptor—in adult participants with NonAdvSM. The Summit study’s multi-part design enabled both the development and rigorous validation of the MS2D2 instrument. Part 1 (dose-finding) provided opportunity to refine the instrument’s structure and scoring prior to its incorporation into Part 2 of the trial (efficacy assessment). The MS2D2 domain structure and scoring were subsequently confirmed using Part 2 data. All psychometric analyses for both study parts were conducted by an independent team blinded to treatment assignment at all times. This psychometric study aimed to evaluate the measurement properties of the MS2D2 using data from 230 participants enrolled in the Summit study.

Structural validity analyses were conducted separately in Part 1 and Part 2; Part 1 baseline data were used to evaluate and refine the scoring structure and baseline Part 2 data were used to test the retained structure in an independent sample. Cross-sectional reliability and construct-validity analyses used pooled baseline data from Part 1 and Part 2, whereas longitudinal responsiveness and meaningful-change analyses used Part 2 data through Week 24, corresponding to the primary endpoint.

Methods

Study design and population

The Summit study (NCT05186753) is a multi-part, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 clinical study comparing the safety and efficacy of bezuclastinib (CGT9486) plus best supportive care (BSC) with placebo plus BSC in adult participants (aged ≥ 18 years) with NonAdvSM (ISM, BMM and SSM) whose symptoms remained inadequately controlled despite a stable regimen of at least 2 anti-mediator therapies used as BSC. The protocol specified the anti-mediator therapy classes (e.g. histamine receptor type 1 antagonists) which were acceptable BSC medications. All participants had moderate to severe disease, based on a Mastocytosis Activity Score (MAS) ≥ 26. MS2D2 TSS change from baseline to Week 24 was the primary endpoint. Key secondary endpoints included: Proportion of subjects who had ≥ 50% reduction from baseline to Week 24 in serum tryptase, peripheral blood D816V VAF (or undetectable for subjects with detectable mutation at baseline), MS2D2 TSS, bone marrow mast cells (or clearance of aggregates for subjects with aggregates at baseline), and proportion of subjects with ≥ 30% reduction from baseline at Week 24 in MS2D2 TSS.

The study was conducted in three parts. In Part 1 of the study, 54 participants were randomly assigned (1:1:1) to 1 of 2 dose levels of bezuclastinib plus BSC, or to matched placebo plus BSC for 12 weeks. Based on the Part 1 safety, pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic and efficacy results, bezuclastinib 100 mg once daily was selected for evaluation in Part 2. In Part 2, 179 participants were randomized 2:1 to the selected dose of bezuclastinib plus BSC or to matched placebo plus BSC for 24 weeks. Patients who completed Part 1 or Part 2 could enter Part 3, an open label extension in which all participants receive bezuclastinib plus BSC for up to 5 years. Parts 1 and 2 of the study are complete.

Study assessments

MS2D2 instrument and scoring

The MS2D2 is a disease-specific daily diary designed to measure symptom severity in patients with NonAdvSM. Comprising 17 items, each with a recall period of “the past 24 h”, the MS2D2 measures symptom severity for 15 items using a 0–10 numeric rating scale (NRS), with higher numbers indicating greater severity. The remaining 2 items elicit frequency of diarrhea episodes and incidence of mast cell reactions (y/n).

Comparator and anchor measures

Several established instruments were administered to support validity and interpretation.

MAS

Designed for patients with mastocytosis, the MAS tracks symptom severity over a 24-h period through 9 items covering skin, gastrointestinal, and other domains, each rated on a 5-point scale [13]. The total score is calculated by summing all items, and scores are normalized to a 0–100 range, where higher results indicate more severe symptoms.

MC-QoL

The Mastocytosis Quality of Life Questionnaire (MC-QoL) measures HRQoL impairment in patients with mastocytosis over the past two weeks using 27 items in four domains—symptoms, social functioning, emotions, and skin [14]. Each item is rated on a 5-point scale for intensity or frequency, with scores converted to a 0–100 scale; higher scores indicate greater impairment.

SF-12V2

The Short-Form 12-Item Survey (SF-12v2) is a generic measure of health status consisting of 12 items with a 4-week recall period that represent 8 health domains. It is primarily scored to yield two norm-based (mean = 50, SD = 10), summary scores: the Physical Composite Summary (PCS) and Mental Composite Summary (MCS) with higher scores indicating better health status.

EQ-5D-5L

The EQ-5D-5L uses a 5-item descriptive system to evaluate key domains: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each rated on five severity levels [15]. Response patterns to the five items are converted to a utility index using a country-specific value set. While generally ranging from 0 to 1.00, the possible range depends on the country-specific tariff and may fall outside those bounds. Higher values indicate better HRQoL. Additionally, the visual analogue scale (VAS) captures patients’ overall health perceptions from 0 (“worst imaginable”) to 100 (“best imaginable”).

PGIS

The Patient Global Impression of Severity (PGIS) used in the SUMMIT trial was a study-specific 6-item variant rather than the canonical single item global PGIS. It assessed patients’ perceived severity of symptoms over the past 7 days for six concepts matching the initial domains of the MS2D2: (1) overall symptoms, (2) dermatological symptoms, (3) gastrointestinal symptoms, (4) pain, (5) fatigue, and (6) neurocognitive symptoms. All items used a 5-point verbal rating scale (VRS) with 5 response categories assessing severity from “None” to “Very severe,” and the neurocognitive item assessing difficulty from “Not difficult at all” to “Extremely difficult”. All PGIS items were scored separately and were not combined into a total score.

PGIC

The Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC) used in the SUMMIT trial was a study-specific 6-item variant rather than the canonical single-item global PGIC. It assessed perceived change since starting treatment for six concepts: overall symptoms, dermatological symptoms, gastrointestinal symptoms, pain, fatigue, and neurocognitive symptoms. Responses on a 7-point bidirectional VRS ranged from “Very much worse” to “Very much better”, and all items were scored separately rather than combined.

Psychometric analyses

Measurement properties of the MS2D2 were assessed using data from Part 1 (n = 51) and Part 2 (n = 179) of the Summit study, which had separate samples of the same population, allowing for both independent validation and pooled analysis.

Structural validity analyses were conducted separately in Part 1 and Part 2. Baseline data from Part 1 were used to evaluate candidate item/domain configurations and select the retained scoring structure. Part 2 data were then used to test the retained structure in an independent sample.

The cross-sectional analyses (reliability and construct validity) used pooled baseline data from Summit Part 1 and Part 2. Pooling data across parts increased sample size and statistical power, enhancing the robustness of the evaluations which is particularly important in the context of a rare disease population. Before pooling cross-part measurement comparability of the scoring structure was formally evaluated via multigroup confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) and configural, metric, and scalar invariance were supported.

Longitudinal measurement properties, including sensitivity to change and meaningful change thresholds were evaluated using Part 2 data because Part 2 included the Week 24 blinded assessment corresponding to the pivotal trial’s primary endpoint, whereas the blinded treatment period in Part 1 ended at Week 12.

All psychometric analyses were conducted using blinded data by an independent team which was not involved in the clinical trial efficacy analysis.

MS2D2 structural validity

Item-level statistics

Item-level descriptive analyses were conducted to evaluate the response distribution of MS2D2 items at baseline. Given the 0–10 numeric response scale, item responses were treated as continuous variables and summarized using means, medians, and interquartile ranges to assess central tendency and variability. Categorical response frequencies were also examined to describe use of the response scale and to identify potential floor or ceiling patterns.

Item-to-item correlations

Inter-item correlations were analyzed using baseline day data to assess the associations among MS2D2 items and determine their correspondence with the instrument’s proposed domain structure while avoiding inflation that would arise from combining repeated daily observations across the full 14-day baseline window. Pearson correlation coefficients were computed for all item pairs and interpreted descriptively; Spearman coefficients were examined as a nonparametric check. As a sensitivity analysis, the inter-item correlation analyses were repeated at D-8 to assess whether the observed item-clustering pattern was robust to the choice of baseline-day timepoint.

Within an intended domain, correlations ranging from 0.40 to 0.80 were interpreted as evidence of conceptual consistency; coefficients below 0.20 indicated potential misalignment, while those exceeding 0.80 suggested possible item redundancy [16]. High correlations among items hypothesized to belong to different domains signaled a need to reconsider domain composition. These correlations were interpreted as part of the overall structural evaluation, alongside item-level distributions and CFA results, rather than as standalone inferential tests.

Confirmatory factor analysis

Structural validity analyses began from an a priori hypothesized domain structure derived from the MS2D2 conceptual framework and prior qualitative development work. The goal of this process was not to derive domains de novo, but to evaluate whether the hypothesized structure was supported by the data and to identify a retained scoring model for confirmation in Part 2. Using the item response distributions, the observed pattern of inter-item correlations, and stakeholder input, a series of CFA models were estimated on Part 1 baseline day data (N = 51) using a robust maximum likelihood estimator. The 0–10 NRS MS2D2 symptoms were treated as approximately continuous because they provide 11 response options and were analyzed in a small sample, consistent with prior simulation work [17]. Analyses were conducted in Mplus 8.8. Global fit was assessed using chi-square, Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA), Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR), Comparative Fit Index (CFI), and Tucker-Lewis Index (TLI) criteria, and local fit was evaluated using residual correlations and modification indices. Item retention considered loading strength, absence of problematic cross-loadings, and clinical relevance. A second-order CFA was specified to test whether the resulting domain-level structure loaded on a higher order symptom construct. The CFA models were repeated using Part 2 baseline day data (N = 179) to evaluate whether the same overall configuration was supported in the larger independent sample.

MS2D2 reliability

Internal consistency

Internal consistency reliability for each 14-day MS2D2 domain score and the TSS was assessed at baseline using pooled baseline data from Parts 1 and 2 and summarized using Cronbach’s alpha (α) and reported with 95% confidence intervals, with values ≥ 0.70 considered acceptable [18]. In this context, alpha was used as a cross-sectional index of item-set homogeneity at a single timepoint, not as evidence of score stability over time. Alpha-if-item-deleted values were examined to identify items potentially detrimental to scale homogeneity. In addition, overlap-corrected item-total correlations were also computed, with coefficients ≥ 0.50 indicating meaningful contribution to the domain and values ≥ 0.80 suggesting potential redundancy.

Test–retest reliability

Test–retest reliability of the MS2D2 was assessed via an agreement-based null random effects intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC [2,1]/ICC [A,1]; [19, 20]) between 14-day screening and baseline scores, restricted to participants with no PGIS change between those two times. ICC values > 0.90 indicated excellent reliability; values between 0.70 and 0.90 indicated good reliability; and values < 0.70 indicated low reliability [21].

As a sensitivity analysis of the chosen diary scoring period, agreement-based ICC between 14-day and 7-day MS2D2 scores was examined at baseline (and at other time periods). To compute a 7-day score, a minimum of 4 daily entries was required.

MS2D2 construct validity

Convergent validity

Convergent validity was evaluated at baseline by examining associations between MS2D2 scores and conceptually related measures. Pearson correlations were used for associations between MS2D2 scores and other multi-item scale scores treated as approximately continuous. Polyserial correlations were used for associations involving ordinal global ratings, including the domain-specific and overall PGIS measures. Correlations between 0.40 and 0.80 were considered indicative of adequate convergence.

Known-groups validity

Known-groups validity of the MS2D2 was assessed by comparing its domain scores across PGIS severity levels (None to Very Severe) at baseline. Each domain was mapped to its corresponding PGIS item. Differences in scores across severity levels were evaluated using ANOVA, with post-hoc comparisons of ordered differences conducted via Helmert contrast.

MS2D2 sensitivity to change

Sensitivity to change from baseline to Week 24 for the MS2D2 was evaluated in Part 2 using PGIS and PGIC anchors. Change score distributions were summarized by anchor level as raw and percent changes, with standardized differences calculated to convey the magnitude of change relative to variability. Polyserial correlations were evaluated between MS2D2 change scores and anchors. Responsiveness was tested using linear mixed models to evaluate whether change on the MS2D2 could be distinguished by change across ordinal anchor levels, accounting for time, baseline, and covariates.1

MS2D2 meaningful change thresholds

Thresholds for within-patient meaningful improvement were estimated using an anchor-based approach with PGIS and PGIC at Week 24. Mean and median change at each anchor level was summarized, and linear mixed models tested whether the change associated with each unit of anchor improvement differed from no change group. Empirical cumulative distribution functions (eCDFs) were examined to identify the MS2D2 change value that maximized vertical separation between the one-level improvement and no-change curves, while also requiring that no more than 30% of the no change group exceed the candidate threshold).2 Baseline distribution data [MDC = 1.96*sqrt(2)*SD*sqrt(1-ICC)] was used to confirm the instrument’s precision for detecting proposed changes.

Results

Study population and COA completion

A total of 230 patients were included in the analyses, with 51 in Part 1 and 179 in Part 2. The average disease severity, based on the MAS score, was 41.5 (13.9) The mean age of patients was 51.5 years (SD = 12.8) and 33.9% of patients were male and 66.1% were female.

Across pooled data, patients showed consistently high adherence to the MS2D2 daily diary, with a mean of 12–13 of the 14 days completed at each timepoint, and at least 96.5% of participants completing 7 or more days for each timepoint.

Inter-item correlations

Part 1 baseline Pearson correlations (Table 1) partially supported the conceptual framework while also indicating that some items were not well aligned with their initially hypothesized domains. Dermatological items were cohesive (r = 0.58–0.74). The mast‑cell reaction item, initially intended for the dermatologic domain, correlated poorly with those items (rmax = 0.27) and with all others, supporting its exclusion. Nausea correlated more strongly with pain (nausea–abdominal pain r = 0.76) than with diarrhea (nausea–diarrhea r = 0.35), suggesting combining Gastrointestinal and Pain domains. Within neurocognitive, correlations between the items assessing concentration (#11), remembering (#12) and brain fog (#14) were strong (r = 0.78–0.86), but headache aligned with pain -specific items (e.g., headache–abdominal pain r = 0.55) and dizziness showed diffuse links, suggesting removal. Feeling of tiredness, originally grouped with neurocognitive, showed broad cross-domain correlations (r = 0.30–0.50) and the highest baseline severity, supporting its retention as a standalone Fatigue domain. Part 2 and pooled inter-item correlations are consistent with the findings and are included in (Supplemental Table 6 and Table 7). The observed correlation pattern was also robust to the choice of baseline-day timepoint: Sensitivity analyses using D-8 yielded a matrix closely similar to that at D-1, with the same substantive item-clustering pattern.

Confirmatory factor analysis

Using Part 1 data, factor models were tested beginning from the a priori conceptual framework, with item response distributions, inter‑item correlations and measures of global and local fit used to evaluate whether items should be retained, removed, reassigned, or combined in the final scoring model. CFA favored a four‑domain structure with 11 items—Dermatological (4 items), Gastrointestinal/Pain (4 items), Neurocognitive (2 items), and Fatigue (1)—with all domains loading on a higher order TSS (Supplemental Fig. 3). This solution had excellent global fit: RMSEA = 0.024 [90% CI: 0–0.10], SRMR = 0.07, CFI = 0.99, TLI = 0.99. Part 2 re‑estimation showed acceptable fit for the same configuration in the larger independent sample (RMSEA = 0.084 [0.062–0.107], SRMR = 0.06, CFI = 0.94, TLI = 0.93), supporting generalizability of the measurement model. Part 2 standardized loadings ranged from λ = 0.62–0.97 at the domain levels and second-order loading on the TSS ranged from λ = 0.44–0.83 (Fig. 2). Most residual correlations were ≤|0.10| (largest exception: Itching-Tiredness rdiff = 0.21).

To evaluate whether pooling across Parts 1 and 2 was appropriate for cross-sectional analyses, we conducted a formal multi-group CFA across study parts. MS2D2 structure and scoring

Based on item-level analyses and CFA modeling conducted in Parts 1 and 2, the MS2D2 was refined to a final structure comprising 11 items grouped into four domains:

Dermatological: Itching; Flushing; Skin redness; Covered with spots.

Gastrointestinal/Pain: Nausea; Abdominal pain; Bone pain; Headache.

Each item is rated on a 0–10 NRS, tailored to each symptom (e.g., 0 = “No Itching,” 10 = “Severe Itching”). Daily domain scores were calculated as the mean of items within each domain.3 A daily TSS derived from the 11 domain-specific items, is reported both as a 0–10 average to allow comparison across domains and a 0–110 sum (average × 11) to maintain interpretability across mastocytosis research programs.

For each assessment period, MS2D2 scores (TSS and domain scores) were calculated using the last 14 days of the screening (i.e., days − 14 to − 1) or planned 28-day treatment cycle (i.e., days 15–28 of the cycle), based on the average of non-missing daily domain/TSS scores and provided at least 7 daily entries were available. As a sensitivity analysis, scores were calculated analogously using the last 7 days of the same assessment period, provided that at least 4 daily entries were available.

MS2D2 measurement properties

Score-level distributions

Pooled baseline MS2D2 domain scores showed a wide range of symptom burden, with mean values spanning from 4.0 to 7.0 on the 0–10 scale. Fatigue was the most severe domain (M = 7.0; SD = 2.0), followed by Dermatological (M = 5.2; SD = 2.3) and Neurocognitive (M = 5.2 SD = 2.6), while Gastrointestinal/Pain was the least endorsed (M = 4.0; SD = 2.1); the TSS averaged 4.9 (SD = 1.8). These distributions indicate a symptomatic population, with substantial variability across participants (Supplemental Table 3).

MS2D2 reliability

Internal consistency

Latent. Model-based reliability estimates of Part 2 data indicated high overall reliability for the TSS (TSS; ω = 0.92), although omega hierarchical (ωH) was lower (0.68), suggesting that a more modest proportion of total-score variance was attributable specifically to the general symptom burden factor after accounting for multidimensional structure. The domain scores showed acceptable to strong reliability (ω = 0.87 for Dermatological, 0.77 for Gastrointestinal/Pain, and 0.96 for Neurocognitive), with reliable variance in Dermatological primarily reflecting domain-specific variance and reliable variance in Gastrointestinal/Pain and Neurocognitive reflecting both general and residual domain-specific contributions. Fatigue was modeled as an observed indicator rather than as a latent domain; therefore, internal consistency was not estimated for a separate fatigue subscale, although the fatigue item showed substantial explained variance in relation to TSS (R2 = 0.69).

Observed. MS2D2 scales demonstrated strong cross-sectional internal consistency, with Cronbach’s alpha values ranging from 0.79 to 0.95 using pooled data at baseline (Table 2), and no scale showed improved reliability with item deletion (Supplemental Table 5). Most items had high overlap-corrected item-total correlations (r > 0.50), supporting their contribution to the TSS (Supplemental Table 6).

Test–retest reliability

Test–retest reliability between screening and baseline among PGIS-stable participants indicated excellent agreement (ICC > 0.9) for all MS2D2 scores (Table 3).

MS2D2 construct validity

Convergent validity

MS2D2 demonstrated strong convergent validity at baseline for pooled data with high Pearson correlations to conceptually related measures: TSS with MAS Total (r = 0.85), Dermatological with MAS Skin (r = 0.88), Gastrointestinal/Pain with MAS GI (r = 0.64), and MAS Other with related MS2D2 domains (r = 0.70–0.76). MC-QoL correlations were moderate to strong, highest for MC-QoL Skin with MS2D2 Dermatological (r = 0.84) and moderate between MC-QoL’s Total and Symptom scales and MS2D2’s TSS and for other domains (r = 0.49–0.67); MC-QoL’s Social and Emotional scales showed lower correlations with the MS2D2 (r ≤ 0.45 and ≤ 0.30). Generic HRQoL measures showed modest associations (SF-12v2: r = 0.11–0.49 across MS2D2; EQ-5D VAS: rmax = 0.50 with MS2D2 GI/Pain). PGIS domain ratings were highly correlated (polyserial) with MS2D2 counterparts (r = 0.77–0.79), as was overall PGIS with TSS (r = 0.56) (Supplemental Table 8).

Known-groups validity

MS2D2 scores differentiated clearly across PGIS severity strata at baseline (Supplemental Table 9). Omnibus ANOVA tests were significant for all domains and the TSS (p < 0.05), and scores increased monotonically with higher PGIS severity. Differences between None/Mild and ≥ Moderate were substantial, averaging 2–5 points on the 0–10 scale (Dermatological − 4.07, Gastrointestinal/Pain − 2.58, Neurocognitive − 4.66, Fatigue − 4.25, TSS − 2.84; all p < 0.05). Contrasts between Moderate and Severe/Very severe remained significant, with mean differences around 2 points (range 1.96–2.97). At the upper end, most domains distinguished Severe from Very severe (e.g., Neurocognitive Δ≈1.97, TSS Δ≈1.12), while Gastrointestinal/Pain showed the expected trend but did not reach significance (p = 0.057). These results demonstrate that MS2D2 scores effectively discriminate among clinically distinct severity groups, supporting its sensitivity to differences in symptom burden at baseline.

MS2D2 sensitivity to change

At Week 24, MS2D2 change scores from baseline for Part 2 showed clear, graded improvement across PGIS and PGIC anchors (Table 4). For TSS, mean (SD) raw change was − 1.4 (1.3), − 2.2 (1.4), and − 3.0 (1.5) for PGIS-defined no change, 1-unit, and 2-unit improvement, respectively; corresponding percent changes from baseline were – 25.1%, – 43.3%, and – 57.4%. PGIC-based TSS changes followed a similar pattern (– 1.3 to – 2.7; – 25.1% to – 57.4%). Domain-level scores equally demonstrated ordinal gradients, with Fatigue showing the largest absolute changes.

Mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) analyses confirmed statistical separation across anchor-defined levels. For TSS, least square mean differences were – 0.49 (p < 0.001) for PGIS no change vs 1-unit improvement, and – 0.87 (p < 0.001) for no change vs 1–2 units. PGIC comparisons yielded – 0.51 (p < 0.001) and – 0.76 (p < 0.001), respectively. Fatigue consistently showed the largest domain-level differences.

These findings support the MS2D2’s responsiveness to clinically meaningful change in NonAdvSM and, as shown later, the observed changes exceeded the relevant distribution-based error benchmarks.

MS2D2 within-patient meaningful change thresholds

Absolute change

TSS. Absolute-change descriptive summaries and eCDFs consistently indicated a CDF-based threshold of 2.0 points (0–10 scale), which maximized anchor group separation while keeping “No change” rates low. At this cutoff, 58% of the PGIS 1-point improvement group and 64% of the PGIC “Much or very much better” group exceeded the threshold, compared to 18% and 22% of “No change” groups, respectively (40–42 point gaps). These findings converged on 1.6–2.2 points (≈17–25 on the 0–110 sum score), exceeding distribution-based criteria (0.5 × SD ≈1.3, MDC ≈1.2) and approximating 0.8 × SD (~ 2.0), supporting 2.0 as a within-patient meaningful improvement which the MS2D2 has the measurement precision to detect.

Domains. Across Dermatological, Gastrointestinal/Pain, Neurocognitive, and Fatigue domains, CDF-based estimates generally fell between 1.5 and 2.0 points, with improved groups exceeding stable by ~ 30–40 percentage points. For example, in Fatigue, PGIS change suggested –1.75 (53% vs. 22%), and PGIC suggested 2.0 (49% vs. 16%). Domain-level thresholds also exceeded 0.5 SD and MDC and approached 0.8 SD, reinforcing that the CDF-based thresholds represent true improvement.

Percent change

TSS. Percent-change eCDFs and descriptive summaries provided complementary evidence. Analyzing CDF curve separation yielded thresholds of ~ 40% for PGIS change and ~ 30% for PGIC, chosen because they maximized separation while keeping the “No change” group below 30%. At these points, PGIS showed ~ 58% of the 1-point improvement group exceeded the value vs. ~ 23% of “No change” (35 percentage point separation), and PGIC showed ~ 53% of “A little better” vs. ~ 26% of “No change” (27 percentage point separation). Descriptive means and medians were consistent with these thresholds: PGIS ≈40% reduction; PGIC ≈30%.

Domains. Domain-level percent-change thresholds mirrored TSS, clustering around 30–40% reduction. For example, Fatigue showed PGIS change at –33% (39% vs. 9%) and PGIC at 30% (54% vs. 10%). Other domains demonstrated similar ranges and separation magnitudes, confirming that meaningful improvement spans roughly 30–40% reduction depending on anchor.

Discussion

This study presents a comprehensive psychometric evaluation of the MS2D2 in adults with NonAdvSM using data from 230 participants across Parts 1 and 2 of the Summit study. The multi-part study design supported structural refinement in Part 1 and confirmation of the retained scoring model in the larger independent Part 2 sample, while pooled baseline analyses improved precision for cross-sectional psychometric evaluations in this rare disease setting. The MS2D2 is a patient-reported outcome instrument developed to assess symptom severity in adults with NonAdvSM. The MS2D2 TSS comprises 11 items grouped into four domains—Dermatological, Gastrointestinal/Pain, Neurocognitive, and Fatigue—each rated on a 0–10 numeric scale. Psychometric validation was essential to confirm the instrument’s reliability, validity, and sensitivity to change, ensuring it can accurately capture symptom burden and detect meaningful treatment effects. These properties are critical for supporting the use of scores derived from the MS2D2, particularly the TSS, as clinical trial endpoints and for meeting regulatory standards for patient-focused drug development.

The analytic approach taken for these analyses was designed to meet current standards for clinical outcome assessments and regulatory expectations. A key strength of this work was the deliberate analytic sequencing of psychometric evaluations, which ensured that each step in the analysis was logically ordered and methodologically sound. In addition, all psychometric analyses were conducted under blinded conditions by an independent research team, without access to treatment assignment or efficacy data, ensuring independence between measurement validation and treatment effect evaluation.

In addition to the psychometric validation, the MS2D2 was developed using a patient-centered qualitative process, including concept elicitation and cognitive debriefing interviews. Detailed methods and findings from that qualitative development work will be reported separately, which provides the primary evidence supporting item relevance, clarity, and content coverage.

The MS2D2’s domain structure was established through CFA on data from Part 1 of the Phase 2 Summit study (n = 51), building on a conceptual framework, inter-item correlations, response distributions, and refinement based on fit statistics and residual correlations. This process enabled the identification of 4 empirically supported domains (Dermatological Symptoms, Gastrointestinal and Pain Symptoms, Neurocognitive Symptoms and Fatigue) and a higher-order TSS.

An advantage of the multi-part trial design used in the MS2D2 validation was the use of CFA in an iterative process during Part 1, with independent confirmation in Part 2. A formal multi-group CFA as a supplementary sensitivity analysis also supported scalar invariance across Part 1 and Part 2. This analytic approach provided robust evidence that the instrument’s domain structure is both conceptually and empirically supported. By demonstrating that items cluster into distinct, patient-data driven domains, CFA enhances confidence in the interpretability and relevance of both domain and total scores. This advancement enables a more precise and nuanced understanding of symptom patterns in NonAdvSM, supporting the differentiation of symptom domains that matter most to patients. As a result, the MS2D2 offers a clearer window into the patient experience, ensuring that changes in scores reflect meaningful changes in health status. The rigorous structural validation achieved through CFA not only meets contemporary regulatory standards but also represents a methodological improvement over legacy instruments, ultimately facilitating more patient-centered and interpretable clinical trial endpoints.

The study also introduced improved methodologies for identifying within-patient thresholds for meaningful change, compared to existing publicly accessible PRO instruments for NonAdvSM. These methods included a combination of descriptive statistics, CDF analysis, and MMRM, all stratified by anchor level. These methods enabled a more nuanced understanding of symptom improvement, which was the primary focus due to the rarity of symptom worsening in the study population.

However, limitations should be considered. The Gastrointestinal/Pain domain, while empirically supported, combines nausea and pain symptoms, which may not fully reflect clinical convention. This grouping may reflect how patients experience these symptoms together, though separating them for clinical reporting could be considered. However, GI and Pain were evaluated as potential separate constructs, but the available psychometric evidence did not support retaining them as distinct MS2D2 domains. Inter-item and item-total results showed overlap rather than clear separation, CFA results supported a single GI/Pain factor, and the same general pattern was observed in Part 2-only analyses. Further separate GI and Pain subdomain sensitivity outputs were generated including Part 2-only meaningful-change displays, (outputs not reported in this manuscript), and these did not support separate domains.

Additionally, the fatigue domain is represented by a single item. While feeling of tiredness was highly endorsed at baseline and is clinically important in this population, since the score is based on a single item, its internal consistency and latent-domain could not be assessed in the same way as the multi-item domains. Accordingly, Fatigue should be interpreted as a clinically important observed symptom score within the broader MS2D2 framework, and complementary instruments could be considered to expand the assessment of fatigue-related symptoms. Further, evaluation of the MS2D2 in other mastocytosis populations and real-world clinical settings may further inform its utility.

Some mean improvement was observed in the anchor-defined no-change groups. This pattern may reflect a combination of factors, including imperfect anchor classification, regression to the mean, or heterogeneity within the stable group. However, this did not obscure the interpretation of the meaningful-change analyses because the no-change and improvement groups remained clearly separated in the observed change distributions. In particular, the box-plot and eCDF displays showed separation between stable and improved groups, and the proposed thresholds were interpreted in light of that separation rather than under the assumption that the no-change group would exhibit zero mean change.

Conclusions

The MS2D2 demonstrated strong measurement properties, including structural validity, internal consistency, test–retest reliability, and construct validity. The instrument’s ability to discriminate among severity strata and detect meaningful within-patient change supports the suitability of MS2D2-derived scores, particularly the TSS, for use as trial endpoints in NonAdvSM trials. These properties align with FDA guidance for patient-focused drug development and address key gaps identified in prior measures.

In summary, the MS2D2 is a psychometrically supported and clinically relevant instrument for capturing symptom severity in patients with NonAdvSM. Its development and validation reflect careful analytic sequencing, empirical rigor, and alignment with regulatory standards. As such, the MS2D2 provides a strong foundation for its use in clinical research, contributing significantly to the advancement of patient-focused outcomes and improving the quality of measurement in mastocytosis studies.

Linear mixed models used random effects for subject and time. PGIS change (and in a separate model, PGIC) served as an indicator of change in clinical status, to determine whether, for a given domain, change scores differed by ordinal change levels after controlling for other factors.

The model was conditioned on the following fixed effects: Time, PGIS ordinal change (or PGIC), Time x PGIS change (or PGIC), mean-centered baseline MS2D2 score, mean-centered baseline MS2D2 x Time, and any randomization covariates (e.g., baseline serum tryptase centered at 11.4 ng/mL). Separate models were fit with either PGIS change or PGIC. Given that few subjects in the study population were expected to worsen in either treatment or control arms, anchors were constrained to their no change and improvement categories.

As a restricted cubic spline did not indicate potential non-linearity in scores over time (Harrell 2015), time was treated as continuous.

In the Part 1 only iteration of the analysis, data were included in the model through Week 12 and the baseline MS2D2 score on the predictor side was mean-centered on the Part 1 sample.

In the Part 2 only iteration of the above model, data were included through Week 24 and baseline MS2D2 score on the predictor side was mean-centered on the Part 2 sample.

In the pooled Part 1 and Part 2 analysis, data were included through Week 24, with a time-varying indicator variable denoting when subjects enter the open-label extension phase. Part 2 subjects, who remained blinded to treatment up to Week 24, always had an indicator value of 0; Part 1 subjects, who were blinded to treatment up to Week 12, had an indicator value of 0 through Week 12 and an indicator value of 1 at Weeks 16, 20, and 24. The Time by extension phase indicator interaction was also included in the pooled model. For timepoint-specific estimates generated from the model, Week 24 was emphasized (though all weeks contributed to the estimate). For eCDF-based threshold selection, candidate values were chosen to maximize separation between the improved and no-change distributions while requiring that no more than 30% of the no-change group exceed the threshold. This value was not arbitrary. Rather, it was used as a practical approximation to Cohen’s medium-effect benchmark (d = 0.5), which corresponds to approximately 33.0% nonoverlap between two normal distributions and implies that about 30.9% of the reference group would exceed a threshold set at the median of the improved group. The ≤ 30% constraint was therefore intended to ensure that selected thresholds reflected a meaningful degree of discrimination, rather than trivial overlap, between improved and non-improved patients.

The eDiary was designed to prevent missing responses within an assessment. In determining daily scores, no missing data rule was required since MS2D2 item responses were either all present or all missing on a given day. Nevertheless, forward-looking rules regarding missing data were specified: Each domain was scored, on a given day, via summing the (non-missing) item responses of the items assigned to the domain and then dividing by the number of (non-missing) items within the domain, placing all scales on a common 0–10 metric. Had partial responses to the eDiary been permitted, at least half the items within a domain would have had to have been present to receive a daily score for that domain (at least half the total items would have had to have been presented to receive a TSS score).
