A finite-board reduction for the Erdős Matching Conjecture and the 4-uniform case via exact certificates
Jianfeng Hou, Caiyun Hu, Xizhi Liu

Abstract
We prove the 4-uniform Erdős Matching Conjecture for every matching number s ≥ 6961 s\geq 6961 . The proof has two parts. First, building on ideas from Frankl–Rödl–Ruciński [ 14 ] , we formulate a general finite-board criterion for the r r -uniform conjecture. The criterion has two assumptions: the ( r − 1 ) (r-1) -uniform cover-side bound for links with matching number at most t t holds at every m ≥ n r ​ ( t ) m\geq n_{r}(t) , and a finite optimization problem for mixed-size trace configurations on an ( r 2 + r − 1 ) (r^{2}+r-1) -vertex board. Together with the corresponding lower-uniformity input, this finite-board optimization implies the Erdős Matching Conjecture with explicit large-matching thresholds. Second, we verify the finite-board assumption for r = 4 r=4 . The local board has 19 vertices, and the required inequality is decomposed into three weighted local inequalities: a leading wide layer, a 15-board layer, and an 11-board layer. The verification is reduced to exact finite optimization and certificate-validation problems: Ferrers down-set enumerations for pair and triple traces, rational Farkas-dual certificates for the top-star branch, integer branch-and-bound up-set hitting and pattern searches for the no-top-star branch, and residual-cut dual certificates for the 15-board and 11-board layers.



1
Introduction and statement

The Erdős Matching Conjecture is one of the central extremal problems for
uniform hypergraphs. It asks for the largest possible size of an
r
-graph when
a matching of prescribed size is forbidden. The conjecture was proposed by
Erdős
[
3
]
; in the special case of matching number one it
recovers the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem
[
5
]
. It is also a basic
hypergraph Turán problem, with a matching as the excluded configuration, but
the expected extremal answer has two competing models rather than a single
Turán-type construction.

For an
r
-uniform hypergraph
F
, let
\nu(F)
be its matching number. Define

m_{r}(n,s)\coloneqq\max\left\{|F|:F\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r},\ \nu(F)\leq s\right\}.

The Erdős Matching Conjecture predicts that, for
n\geq r(s+1)
,

m_{r}(n,s)=M_{r}(n,s)\coloneqq\max\{a_{r}(s),b_{r}(n,s)\},

(1)

where

a_{r}(s)\coloneqq\binom{rs+r-1}{r},\qquad b_{r}(n,s)\coloneqq\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-s}{r}.

The two examples are the complete
r
-graph on
rs+r-1
vertices and the family of all
r
-sets meeting
a fixed
s
-set. These examples represent the two regimes of the problem: a
dense clique-type construction near the minimum admissible number of vertices,
and a cover-type construction when the ambient vertex set is large.

Historically, the graph case
r=2
follows from the theorem of Erdős and Gallai

[
4
]
. Early large-
n
results for general uniformity go back
to Bollobás, Daykin, and Erdős
[
2
]
, and were subsequently
strengthened in several directions, including work of Huang, Loh, and Sudakov

[
16
]
, Frankl, Łuczak, and Mieczkowska
[
13
]
, and Frankl

[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. The two extremal regimes have
also been studied separately: stability and cover-side large-
n
results appear
in work of Frankl and Kupavskii
[
10
,
11
]
,
while the almost-perfect, clique-side range was advanced by Frankl

[
9
]
and Kolupaev and Kupavskii

[
18
]
. A 2026 preprint of Frankl, Lu, Ma, and Wu

[
12
]
proves stability results toward the 4-uniform case and
establishes the conjecture in the range
n\geq 5s
with
n
sufficiently large.
The present paper gives an independent certificate-based proof in an explicit
4-uniform large-
s
range: all admissible
n\geq 4s+4
once
s\geq 6961
, whereas
Frankl–Lu–Ma–Wu treat the large-
n
range
n\geq 5s
with
n
sufficiently
large.
The case
r=3
is now known in full by Frankl
[
8
]
.
Łuczak and Mieczkowska
[
19
]
proved the all-
n
result for
sufficiently large
s
, and Frankl, Rödl, and Ruciński
[
14
]
gave a
short proof in a large-
s
range. Related matching and degree versions have
also played an important role in the development of the area, for example in

[
1
,
15
,
17
]
. As in much of extremal set theory,
shifting is a key structural tool; we use it in the standard compressed-family
form described, for instance, in Frankl’s survey
[
6
]
.

For fixed
r
, define the transition value

n_{r}(s)\coloneqq\min\left\{n\geq r(s+1):\binom{rs+r-1}{r}\leq\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-s}{r}\right\}.

(2)

Thus, within the admissible range
n\geq r(s+1)
, the clique term is strictly
larger for
n<n_{r}(s)
, while the cover term is at least as large for

n\geq n_{r}(s)
.

The main theorem of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Main theorem)
.

For every integer
s\geq 6961
and every
n\geq 4s+4
, every
4
-graph

H\subseteq\binom{[n]}{4}
with
\nu(H)\leq s
satisfies

|H|\leq M_{4}(n,s)
, where

M_{4}(n,s)\coloneqq\max\left\{\binom{4s+3}{4},\binom{n}{4}-\binom{n-s}{4}\right\}
.

We did not try to optimize the lower bound on
s
in Theorem
1.1
.
The numerical constant
6961
is explicit and is the value delivered by the
present finite verification: the local 19-board finite-board input is verified
uniformly for all
s\geq 3481
at the two critical parameter pairs

n\in\{n_{4}(s)-1,n_{4}(s)\}
, and the non-ONE deletion chain can reduce the matching
parameter by almost a factor of two. Thus the global theorem follows for

s\geq 2\cdot 3481-1=6961
.

The proof has two layers. The first layer, Section
2
,
proves a general finite-board criterion for the
r
-uniform conjecture. After
shifting, saturated maximalization, the critical-value reduction, and the
non-ONE deletion chain, the global problem is reduced to two assumptions:

(i)

the
(r-1)
-uniform EMC gives the cover-side bound

\binom{m}{r-1}-\binom{m-t}{r-1}
for every
m
-vertex link with matching number
at most
t
, for all
m\geq n_{r}(t)
; and

(ii)

a finite optimization problem for mixed-size trace configurations on an

(r^{2}+r-1)
-vertex board, arising from the two critical parameter pairs

n\in\{n_{r}(s)-1,n_{r}(s)\}
.

For
r=4
, the parameter comparison in Proposition
3.2

shows that this link range is already beyond the 3-uniform transition threshold,
so the known 3-uniform theorem supplies the required lower-uniformity input.
The finite-board input is proved on a 19-vertex local board.

The second layer, Sections
3
–
8
,
verifies these inputs for
r=4
. The 19-board inequality is decomposed into
three weighted local inequalities: a leading wide layer, a 15-board layer, and
an 11-board layer. The leading layer is further split into a top-star branch
and a no-top-star branch. The top-star branch is certified by exact rational
Farkas certificates; the no-top-star branch is certified by exhaustive finite
searches over up-sets; and the lower layers are certified by residual-cut dual
certificates.

All computations are presented in a certificate style. A program may be used to
discover a certificate, but the proof cites only deterministic verification of
the certificate. The Farkas certificates are checked after clearing
denominators, in the usual linear-programming duality sense
[
20
]
.
The no-top-star branch is reduced to finite branch-and-bound searches over
principal up-closures, followed by exact arithmetic and rectangle-extension
checks. The 15-board and 11-board layers use nonnegative integer combinations
of local matching-obstruction rows that dominate the relevant objective
coefficient-wise. The ancillary scripts are described in
Section
9
; the proof-critical success markers and the
mathematical assertion certified by each script are stated there. All
calculations and certificates are available at

EMC_certificate.zip
.

2
A finite-board criterion for general
r

This section is written for a fixed integer
r\geq 2
. It proves the global reduction in a form that is
suited to certificate-assisted applications. The reduction uses a selected matching rule. For every
stable maximal property-ONE
r
-graph, the rule chooses one
s
-matching avoiding vertex
1
and
contained in
[rs+r-1]
. The rule may impose extra deterministic tie-breaking conditions. For
r=4

we shall choose the matching so that the two residual vertices outside
1
are lexicographically minimal;
this is the source of the seed traces in the 19-board proof.

2.1
Critical values and the two inputs

Let
\rho_{r}
denote the unique real root greater than
r
of

\rho_{r}^{r}-(\rho_{r}-1)^{r}=r^{r}.

(3)

Lemma 2.1

(Critical value and gap)
.

For fixed
r\geq 2
,
n_{r}(s)=\rho_{r}s+o(s)
. Moreover, for all sufficiently large
s
,

n_{r}(s)-n_{r}(s-1)\geq 2
.

Proof.

Put

\Delta_{s}(n)\coloneqq\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-s}{r}-\binom{rs+r-1}{r}.

Then
n_{r}(s)
is the first admissible integer
n
for which
\Delta_{s}(n)\geq 0
, and
\Delta_{s}(n)
is
strictly increasing in
n
because

\Delta_{s}(n+1)-\Delta_{s}(n)=\binom{n}{r-1}-\binom{n-s}{r-1}>0
.
For fixed
x>1
and
n=xs+O(1)
,

\Delta_{s}(n)=\frac{s^{r}}{r!}\left(x^{r}-(x-1)^{r}-r^{r}\right)+O(s^{r-1}).

The function
x^{r}-(x-1)^{r}-r^{r}
is strictly increasing on
(1,\infty)
, is negative at
x=r
, and tends to
infinity. This gives the unique root
\rho_{r}>r
and the asymptotic formula.

Let
N=n_{r}(s)
. Since
N=\rho_{r}s+o(s)
and
\rho_{r}>r
, the integer
N-2
is admissible for the
parameter
s-1
when
s
is large. A direct subtraction gives

\displaystyle\Delta_{s-1}(N-2)-\Delta_{s}(N)

\displaystyle=-\binom{N-1}{r-1}-\binom{N-2}{r-1}+\binom{N-s-1}{r-1}+\sum_{i=-1}^{r-2}\binom{rs+i}{r-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{r}+(\rho_{r}-1)^{r-1}-2\rho_{r}^{r-1}}{(r-1)!}s^{r-1}+o(s^{r-1}).

Using
r^{r}=\rho_{r}^{r}-(\rho_{r}-1)^{r}
, the numerator equals

(\rho_{r}-2)\bigl(\rho_{r}^{r-1}-(\rho_{r}-1)^{r-1}\bigr)>0
.
Hence
\Delta_{s-1}(N-2)>0
for large
s
, and therefore
n_{r}(s-1)\leq N-2
.
∎

Assumption 2.2

(Link-bound input)
.

There is an integer
s_{\rm link}(r)
such that, for every
t\geq s_{\rm link}(r)
and every

m\geq n_{r}(t)
, every
(r-1)
-uniform hypergraph
J
on
m
vertices with
\nu(J)\leq t
satisfies

|J|\leq\binom{m}{r-1}-\binom{m-t}{r-1}.

(4)

Known input 2.3

(Frankl’s JCTA large-
n
theorem)
.

We use Frankl’s 2013 JCTA theorem: for
r
-graphs, the Erdős Matching Conjecture holds whenever

n\geq(2s+1)r-s=(2r-1)s+r.

(5)

Equivalently, if
H
is an
r
-graph on
n
vertices with
\nu(H)\leq s
and (
5
)
holds, then
|H|\leq\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n-s}{r}
.

2.2
Stable families, property ONE, and selected matchings

For two
r
-sets
A=\{a_{1}<\cdots<a_{r}\}
and
B=\{b_{1}<\cdots<b_{r}\}
, write
A\preceq B
if

a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
i
. An
r
-graph
F\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
is
stable
if

B\in F
and
A\preceq B
imply
A\in F
.

Definition 2.4

(Property ONE)
.

A stable
r
-graph
F
has property ONE if it has a maximum matching avoiding vertex
1
.
More generally, for an ordered
r
-graph, property ONE with respect to its first
vertex means that some maximum matching avoids that first vertex.

Lemma 2.5

(Compressing a matching away from a forbidden set)
.

Let
F
be a stable
r
-graph on
[n]
. Let
R_{0}\subseteq[n]
, and let
I^{\prime}
be an initial segment of the
ordered set
[n]\setminus R_{0}
with
|I^{\prime}|\geq rt
. If
F
contains a
t
-matching avoiding
R_{0}
, then

F
contains a
t
-matching avoiding
R_{0}
and contained in
I^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Among all
t
-matchings avoiding
R_{0}
, choose one whose ordered union is lexicographically minimal.
If it uses a vertex
x\notin I^{\prime}
, then, since
|I^{\prime}|\geq rt
, some
y\in I^{\prime}
is unused. As
I^{\prime}
is an initial
segment of
[n]\setminus R_{0}
, we have
y<x
. Let
E
be the matching edge containing
x
. Stability
gives
(E\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}\in F
. Replacing
E
by this edge gives a lexicographically smaller
matching avoiding
R_{0}
, a contradiction.
∎

Definition 2.6

(Selected compressed matching)
.

Let
F\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
be stable, have
\nu(F)=s
, and have property ONE. Put

K\coloneqq[rs+r-1]
. A
selected compressed matching
for
F
is an
s
-matching

E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}\in F

avoiding vertex
1
and contained in
K
. Such a matching exists by Lemma
2.5
,
applied with
R_{0}=\{1\}
and
I^{\prime}=K\setminus\{1\}
, which is an initial segment
of
[n]\setminus\{1\}
, since
|K\setminus\{1\}|=rs+r-2\geq rs
.

A
selection rule

\mathfrak{S}
assigns one such matching to every stable property-ONE family to
which the reduction is applied.

Definition 2.7

(Maximality)
.

An
r
-graph
F
with
\nu(F)=s
is maximal with respect to the constraint
\nu\leq s
if every missing

r
-set
Q\notin F
satisfies
\nu(F\cup\{Q\})=s+1
.

Lemma 2.8

(Stable maximalization preserving a stable core)
.

Let
F\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
be stable and satisfy
\nu(F)\leq s
. Suppose that
Q\notin F
and

\nu(F\cup\{Q\})\leq s
. Then there is a stable family
F^{\prime}\supset F
with
|F^{\prime}|=|F|+1
and

\nu(F^{\prime})\leq s
. If, in addition,
\nu(F)=s
and
F
has property ONE, then
F^{\prime}
has property ONE.

Proof.

Start from
F\cup\{Q\}
and apply the usual shifts until a stable family is reached. We use the
standard
(i,j)
-shift convention: an edge is replaced by its shifted image only when that image is
not already present; otherwise the original edge is retained. Shifts preserve
cardinality and do not increase matching number. No old edge of the stable family
F
is deleted:
whenever an old edge is eligible for a shift, its shifted image already lies in
F
by stability. Thus the
final family contains
F
, has one additional edge, and has matching number at most
s
. Any matching
witnessing property ONE in
F
remains present. Under the additional hypotheses
\nu(F)=s
and
property ONE, this old
s
-matching avoiding vertex
1
is still maximum in
F^{\prime}
, since
\nu(F^{\prime})\leq s
.
∎

Corollary 2.9

(Saturated stable ONE representatives)
.

If
F
has maximum size among stable
r
-graphs on
[n]
with matching number
s
and property ONE,
then
F
is maximal with respect to
\nu\leq s
.

Corollary 2.10

(Stable saturated counterexamples)
.

If EMC fails for fixed
(n,s)
with
n\geq r(s+1)
, then there is a counterexample

F\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
which is stable, has
\nu(F)=s
, and is maximal with respect to
\nu\leq s
.

Proof.

Choose a largest counterexample and shift it. Shifting preserves the edge count and does not increase
matching number. If the shifted family had matching number at most
s-1
, then it would not be the
complete
r
-graph because
n\geq r(s+1)
; adding a missing edge would increase the matching number by
at most one and produce a larger counterexample. Hence the matching number is exactly
s
. The same
argument shows maximality.
∎

2.3
The trace and the local board

Let
F\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
be stable and maximal, with
\nu(F)=s
, property ONE, and

n\geq N\coloneqq rs+r-1
. Fix the selected compressed matching

E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}
from Definition
2.6
. Put

K\coloneqq[N]
and
q\coloneqq n-N
, and define the trace family

\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\{E\cap K:E\in F\}
. Let

R_{F}\coloneqq K\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}E_{i}
.
Then
|R_{F}|=r-1
and
1\in R_{F}
.

Lemma 2.11

(No traces of size zero or one)
.

Every member of
\mathcal{G}
has size at least two.

Proof.

Let
E\in F
. If
E\cap K=\varnothing
, then
E
is disjoint from the selected
s
-matching, a
contradiction. Suppose
E\cap K=\{x\}
. If
x\in R_{F}
, then
E
is again disjoint from the selected
matching. If
x\in E_{i}
for some
i
, then
x>1
, and stability gives
(E\setminus\{x\})\cup\{1\}\in F
;
this edge is disjoint from the selected matching, again a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.12

(The trace has matching number
s
)
.

The mixed-size trace family
\mathcal{G}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{G})=s
.

Proof.

The selected edges
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}
lie in
\mathcal{G}
, so
\nu(\mathcal{G})\geq s
. Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
contains

s+1
pairwise disjoint traces. Among all choices of
s+1
pairwise disjoint traces

H_{1},\ldots,H_{s+1}\in\mathcal{G}
, and all choices of corresponding edges
A_{i}\in F
with

A_{i}\cap K=H_{i}
, choose one maximizing
|A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{s+1}|
.
If the
A_{i}
are pairwise disjoint, they form an
(s+1)
-matching in
F
, impossible. Hence two of
them meet in an outside vertex
u\notin K
.

There is a vertex
v\in K\setminus(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{s+1})
. Indeed, if the traces used all
N

vertices of
K
, then the total number of outside positions among the
A_{i}
would be

r(s+1)-N=r(s+1)-(rs+r-1)=1
,
which cannot create an outside intersection. Replace
u
in one edge containing it by this unused
vertex
v
. Since
v<u
, the new edge is coordinatewise no larger than the old one, and hence belongs
to
F
by stability. Replace the corresponding trace
H_{i}
by the new exact trace

H_{i}\cup\{v\}
; because
v
was unused in all chosen edges, the
s+1
selected traces remain pairwise
disjoint traces of
\mathcal{G}
. Moreover
u
is still contained in another chosen edge,
so the union of the chosen
s+1
edges strictly increases. This contradicts the maximality of the
choice.
∎

Lemma 2.13

(Completion closure)
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{G}
and
|H|=j<r
. Then every
r
-set
Q
with
H\subseteq Q\subseteq[n]
belongs to
F
.
In particular, every
r
-set
T\subseteq K
containing
H
lies in
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Since
H\in\mathcal{G}
, there is an edge
E\in F
with exact trace
E\cap K=H
. Write

E=H\cup X_{0}
, where
X_{0}\subseteq[n]\setminus K
and
|X_{0}|=r-j
. Suppose that

Q=H\cup X
is a missing
r
-set. By maximality,
F\cup\{Q\}
contains an
(s+1)
-matching using

Q
, so
F
contains
s
disjoint edges disjoint from
Q
.

Let
S=(K\setminus H)\cup X_{0}
. Its size is
|S|=(N-j)+(r-j)=rs+2r-1-2j
.
After deleting the vertices of the
s
disjoint edges, at least
2r-1-2j\geq r-j
vertices of
S
remain.
Choose a set
Y
of
r-j
remaining vertices. Then
H\cup Y\preceq H\cup X_{0}
. Indeed,

Y\subseteq(K\setminus H)\cup X_{0}
and
|Y|=|X_{0}|
; the elements of
Y\cap X_{0}
form a sublist of

X_{0}
, while every element of
Y\setminus X_{0}
lies in
K
and is smaller than every outside vertex.
Thus the sorted list of
Y
is coordinatewise no larger than the sorted list of
X_{0}
. Stability gives

H\cup Y\in F
, and this edge is disjoint from the
s
edges above, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 2.14

(Exact edge-count identity)
.

With
q=n-(rs+r-1)
,

|F|=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{G}}\binom{q}{r-|H|}
.

Proof.

Every edge of
F
has a unique exact trace
H\in\mathcal{G}
. Conversely, Lemma
2.13

says that every outside completion of a trace
H
is present.
∎

For
H\in\mathcal{G}
, define the spread

z(H)\coloneqq|\{i\in[s]:H\cap E_{i}\neq\varnothing\}|
.
For
\tau\in\binom{[s]}{r}
, put

V_{\tau}\coloneqq R_{F}\cup\bigcup_{i\in\tau}E_{i}
and

\mathcal{G}_{\tau}\coloneqq\{H\in\mathcal{G}:H\subseteq V_{\tau}\}
. The local board has size

|V_{\tau}|=(r-1)+r^{2}
.
Define

w_{s,q}(H)\coloneqq\frac{\binom{q}{r-|H|}}{\binom{s-z(H)}{r-z(H)}}.

(6)

The denominator is the number of
r
-subsets
\tau\subseteq[s]
for which
H\subseteq V_{\tau}
.

Proposition 2.15

(Weighted double count)
.

One has

|F|=\sum_{\tau\in\binom{[s]}{r}}\sum_{H\in\mathcal{G}_{\tau}}w_{s,q}(H)
.

Proof.

A fixed trace
H
is contained in exactly
\binom{s-z(H)}{r-z(H)}
local boards. Its total contribution
is therefore
\binom{q}{r-|H|}
. Sum over
H\in\mathcal{G}
and apply Corollary
2.14
.
∎

Let
B_{r}\coloneqq R\sqcup C_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_{r}
, where
|R|=r-1
and
|C_{i}|=r
.
For

Definition 2.16

(Actual selected local configurations)
.

Fix a selection rule
\mathfrak{S}

, with respect to

, if it arises from some stable maximal

-graph
F\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
with
\nu(F)=s
,

, and with the selected

\mathfrak{S}
, as follows. For some
\tau\in\binom{[s]}{r}
, there is a bijection

V_{\tau}\to B_{r}
sending
R_{F}
to

and sending the matching edges
E_{i}
,

, to the columns

C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}

\mathcal{G}_{\tau}
. In particular, the

We denote the class of such configurations by
\mathcal{A}^{\rm act}_{r,s,q}(\mathfrak{S})

For

For a fixed pair support

Lemma 5.1

(Pair Ferrers)

For every pair support

of size at

Proof.

The Ferrers property follows from the same coordinatewise stability observation: replacing a pair value

The last condition means that a low value

or

the corresponding seed from Lemma

and the two full columns outside the support,

4.2

The remaining assertion is a finite enumeration. A Ferrers down-set in

is represented by a
nonincreasing height vector

The checker

(Triple Ferrers certificate)

9
Certificate bundle and exact verification architecture

This section records the proof-critical finite statements verified by the ancillary certificate bundle.
The certificate files are data, and the checkers are deterministic verifiers that regenerate finite
universes or check integer and rational certificate arithmetic.
The guiding principle is that a numerical optimizer is not accepted as a proof object. The proof object is
one of the following exact finite certificates:

(a)

an exhaustive enumeration of a finite down-set universe;

(b)

an exact branch-and-bound proof that no up-set satisfying specified hitting constraints exists below
a target size;

(c)

a Farkas certificate, namely a nonnegative rational combination of valid linear inequalities;

(d)

a residual-cut dual certificate, namely a nonnegative integer combination of local matching
obstruction rows.

The scripts either regenerate the relevant finite universe from definitions or verify the listed certificate
arithmetic. All proof-critical arithmetic is integer or rational arithmetic after clearing denominators.

No-top-star soundness

Theorem 9.1

(No-top-star encoding and checker soundness)
.

Every genuine no-top-star 19-board in the layer-1 branch gives support-indexed data

(M,(P_{J}),(T_{I}))
satisfying the finite
conditions
(N1)–(N5)
of Theorem
9.2
. On the verification side, the
proof-critical no-top-star checker verifies Theorem
9.2
for the finite universe
specified by these conditions. In particular, every local residual configuration consisting of a present
trace
H
, a seed trace disjoint from
H
, and three pairwise disjoint residual wide quads disjoint from
both is one of the enumerated seed obstructions.

Proof.

Let
M\subseteq[4]^{4}
be the missing wide-quad set of a genuine board. For each pair support

J\in\binom{[4]}{2}
and triple support
I\in\binom{[4]}{3}
, let
P_{J}\subseteq[4]^{2}
and

T_{I}\subseteq[4]^{3}
be the present pair and triple value sets on those supports. Stability of the
ambient shifted family makes the present wide quads a
down-set, so
M
is an up-set. This is (N1). In the no-top-star branch after
Lemma
5.3
, the present upper cube
C=\{1,2,3\}^{4}\setminus M
has no
upper 3-matching. This is (N2). The branch assumption that the missing upper cube contains no full
top-star is exactly (N3), since for an up-set a full top-star in direction
i
is contained in
M
precisely
when its minimal point lies in
M
.

Again by stability, the pair and triple trace sets on each fixed support are Ferrers down-sets. The
forbidden pair obstructions and bad triple matchings are exactly the local five-trace obstructions from
Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
; hence they cannot occur in a genuine board.
This is (N4). Finally, if
H
is a supported trace in some
P_{J}
or
T_{I}
,
Lemma
2.13
says that every wide quad extending
H
is present, so no such quad lies
in
M
. For a residual seed matching of
H
, the seed is
present by Lemma
4.1
; if all three residual wide quads were also present, then
H
, the
seed, and those three quads would be five pairwise disjoint traces on the board, contradicting
Lemma
4.2
. Therefore
M
hits every residual seed matching. This is (N5).

The completeness of the residual seed obstructions is finite and explicit. Fix
H
and a seed disjoint
from
H
. In coordinate
j
, let
A_{j}\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}
be the values already used by
H
and by the
seed. A residual triple of wide quads is disjoint from
H
and from the seed exactly when, in every
coordinate
j
, its three values are three distinct elements of
\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus A_{j}
. Thus every
such residual configuration is obtained by choosing these three available values in each coordinate and
pairing them across the four coordinates by permutations. This is precisely the residual matching
enumeration used by the checker, and each enumerated matching contributes the hitting condition that
at least one of its three wide quads lies in
M
.
∎

Theorem 9.2

(Finite no-top-star theorem)
.

Let
M\subseteq[4]^{4}
. For each pair support
J\in\binom{[4]}{2}
let

P_{J}\subseteq[4]^{2}
, and for each triple support
I\in\binom{[4]}{3}
let

T_{I}\subseteq[4]^{3}
. Write
P_{2}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{J}P_{J}
and

T_{3}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{I}T_{I}
, where elements are remembered with their supports. Suppose that:

(N1)

M
is an up-set in
[4]^{4}
.

(N2)

If
U=\{1,2,3\}^{4}
and
C=U\setminus M
, then
C
has no three pairwise disjoint upper quads.

(N3)

M\cap U
contains no full top-star; equivalently, the four points

(3,1,1,1)
,
(1,3,1,1)
,
(1,1,3,1)
, and
(1,1,1,3)
all lie outside
M
.

(N4)

On every pair support
J
,
P_{J}
is a Ferrers down-set containing no forbidden disjoint pair
obstruction; on every triple support
I
,
T_{I}
is a Ferrers down-set containing no bad 3-matching.

(N5)

For every supported trace
H\in P_{2}\cup T_{3}
, closure holds: every wide quad
Q\supseteq H
lies outside
M
.
Also, every residual seed matching for
H
is hit by
M
.

Then
300|T_{3}|+144|P_{2}|\leq 625|M|
.

Proof.

The certified proof uses exact finite consequences of (N1)–(N5).

(C1) The upper-blocker enumeration verifies that
|M|\geq 33
. Equivalently, the largest present upper
cube down-set satisfying the no-upper-matching and no-top-star assumptions has size 48.

(C2) For a supported trace value
H
, let
\mu(H)
be the minimum
|M^{\prime}|
over up-sets satisfying (N1)–(N3),
closure for
H
, and all residual seed hitting constraints for
H
. The exact hitting solver verifies all
10 pair orbits and 20 triple orbits of
\mu(H)
. Combining the necessary thresholds
\mu(H)\leq m

with the legal pair and triple Ferrers down-set restrictions gives, for
33\leq m=|M|\leq 63
, the
per-support maxima

\begin{array}[]{c|cccccccc}m&33&34\text{--}35&36\text{--}41&42\text{--}47&48\text{--}49&50\text{--}55&56\text{--}59&60\text{--}63\\
\hline\cr p(m)&0&0&0&0&0&0&0&1\\

This gives a valid upper bound because the single-trace minima are used only as necessary conditions:
allowing traces that are individually feasible but not jointly feasible can only enlarge the admissible
trace family.
Since there are six pair supports and four triple supports,
|P_{2}|\leq 6p(m)
and
|T_{3}|\leq 4t(m)
. This
proves the inequality for
33\leq m\leq 63
.

(C3) In the range
64\leq|M|\leq 66

|T_{3}|\leq 4\cdot 31
and
|P_{2}|\leq 6\cdot 3
, which is enough. If an extremal support is present, the

,
proves a rectangle forcing statement: an extremal pair square or triple slab forces one of the rectangles

The rectangle-extension checker then enumerates all 72 up-set extensions of the six rectangles of total

.

Finally, if

, the universal Ferrers bounds give

300|T_{3}|+144|P_{2}|\leq 300\cdot 128+144\cdot 24<625\cdot 67\leq 625|M|.

The theorem follows.
∎
