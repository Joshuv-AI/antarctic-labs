I
Introduction

Exactly solvable relativistic bound-state models remain essential for isolating how relativistic dispersion and deformation scenarios reshape spectra, eigenfunctions, and branch structure. In the scalar sector, the
Klein–Gordon oscillator
(KGO) is a canonical benchmark, generated by a non-minimal coupling that produces an oscillator-like structure for the squared energies
BruceMinning1993
. Its simplicity makes it particularly useful for comparative studies of relativistic deformations (including DSR) at the level of spectral reconstructions.

A natural extension replaces the linear oscillator coupling by a general function
f(x)
, yielding the
Generalized Klein–Gordon Oscillator
(G-KGO). In
(1+1)
dimensions this construction leads to a factorized Schrödinger-like operator
H_{-}=\mathcal{A}^{\#}\mathcal{A}
whose solvability can be imported from supersymmetric quantum mechanics (SUSY QM), in particular via
shape invariance

Gendenshtein1983
;
Cooper1995
. Allowing
f(x)
to be complex turns the G-KGO into a controlled arena for non-Hermitian quantum mechanics: real spatial spectra can persist when the Hamiltonian is
\eta
-pseudo-Hermitian and/or
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric, provided a suitable metric defines the physical inner product
Bender1998
;
Bender2007RPP
;
BBJ2002PRL
;
Mostafazadeh2002
;
Mostafazadeh2003
;
Mostafazadeh2010
;
ElGanainy2018NatPhys
. A key mechanism is the translation-type metric
\eta=e^{-\theta p}
, which implements an imaginary coordinate shift
x\to x+i\theta
and can map a complex potential to its complex conjugate
Ahmed2001
.

On a different front, Doubly Special Relativity (DSR) introduces, in addition to
c
, a second observer-independent scale
k
(often associated with Planckian physics) while retaining a relativity principle through deformed Lorentz transformations in momentum space
AmelinoCamelia2002a
;
MagueijoSmolin2002
;
KowalskiGlikman2005
;
AmelinoCamelia2010
;
AmelinoCamelia2002Open
. Phenomenological consistency requirements (e.g. locality-related constraints for certain modified-dispersion scenarios) motivate treating admissibility bounds as physically meaningful rather than purely formal
Hossenfelder2010PRL
;
Hossenfelder2013LRR
. In the present work we exploit a clean separation of layers: the spatial problem yields
\{\epsilon_{n}\}
, and DSR modifies only the
energy reconstruction map

\epsilon_{n}\mapsto E_{n,\pm}
.

Why a complexified Morse interaction?

We choose a
complexified Morse interaction
for three complementary reasons.

(1) Physical motivation and intrinsic finiteness.

The Morse potential is a classic model for anharmonic molecular binding, improving on the harmonic approximation by incorporating dissociation and producing a
finite
number of bound states
Morse1929
. This intrinsic finiteness makes Morse an ideal testbed to disentangle two truncation mechanisms: the
potential-induced
finite ladder and a
DSR-induced
admissibility cutoff (present in the AC prescription).

(2) Exact solvability via shape invariance.

Morse is shape-invariant in SUSY QM, enabling closed-form spectra and wavefunctions in terms of confluent hypergeometric/associated Laguerre functions
Gendenshtein1983
;
Cooper1995
. This solvability persists in the G-KGO factorization, yielding analytic
\epsilon_{n}
and an explicit eigenbasis.

(3) A standard non-Hermitian benchmark with real spectra.

Complexifications of Morse are among the best studied exactly solvable non-Hermitian models with real spectra. In particular, complex Morse potentials provide textbook illustrations of pseudo-Hermiticity under imaginary coordinate shifts
Ahmed2001
;
BagchiQuesne2002EtaOrth
and admit
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric continuations with real, discrete spectra
Znojil1999
;
Dorey2001
;
BagchiQuesne2002PLA
. This allows us to keep the spatial spectrum real while the interaction remains genuinely complex, and to isolate how DSR reshapes the final relativistic energies.

Main objective.

We provide a unified, analytically controlled framework in which: (i) the G-KGO is reviewed as an exactly solvable (and, when appropriate, pseudo-Hermitian/
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric) relativistic scalar system; (ii) MS and AC DSR deformations are implemented at the level of the energy reconstruction map; and (iii) the resulting spectral signatures are exhibited explicitly for a solvable pseudo-Hermitian complexified Morse interaction, including explicit wavefunctions, MS/AC closed forms, and the combined truncation rules.

II
Review: the
(1+1)
Generalized Klein–Gordon Oscillator

II.1
Non-minimal coupling, factorization, and Schrödinger-like form

We adopt units with
c=1
and keep
\hbar
explicit. Consider the stationary Klein–Gordon equation

\left(p^{2}+m^{2}\right)\phi=E^{2}\phi,\qquad p=-i\hbar\frac{d}{dx}.

(1)

The generalized Klein–Gordon oscillator is generated by the non-minimal substitution

p\ \longrightarrow\ \Pi\equiv p-if(x),

(2)

where
f(x)
is a (generally complex) interaction function. A natural scalar realization replaces
p^{2}
by
\Pi^{\#}\Pi
:

\left(\Pi^{\#}\Pi+m^{2}\right)\phi=E^{2}\phi,

(3)

where
\#
denotes the appropriate adjoint (standard adjoint if Hermitian; metric-adjoint if pseudo-Hermitian).

Define

\mathcal{A}=p-if(x),\qquad\mathcal{A}^{\#}=p+if^{\#}(x),

(4)

so that
\Pi=\mathcal{A}
and
\Pi^{\#}=\mathcal{A}^{\#}
. Then (
3
) becomes the Schrödinger-like spectral problem

\mathcal{A}^{\#}\mathcal{A}\,\phi=\left[-\hbar^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V_{-}(x)\right]\phi=\epsilon\,\phi,

(5)

with

V_{-}(x)=f^{2}(x)-\hbar f^{\prime}(x),

(6)

and (in the undeformed relativistic case)

\epsilon=E^{2}-m^{2}.

(7)

Partner potential.

The reverse ordering yields
V_{+}(x)=f^{2}(x)+\hbar f^{\prime}(x)
. The two potentials are SUSY partners, but the scalar G-KGO spatial problem (
5
) selects one member (here
V_{-}
) as the physical operator.

II.2
SUSY viewpoint and shape invariance

Define the partner Hamiltonians

H_{-}=\mathcal{A}^{\#}\mathcal{A},\qquad H_{+}=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^{\#},

(8)

so that
H_{\mp}=-\hbar^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V_{\mp}(x)
. The intertwining relations

\mathcal{A}H_{-}=H_{+}\mathcal{A},\qquad\mathcal{A}^{\#}H_{+}=H_{-}\mathcal{A}^{\#}

(9)

imply (almost) isospectrality up to the SUSY ground-state issue. If shape invariance holds,

V_{+}(x;a_{0})=V_{-}(x;a_{1})+R(a_{0}),\qquad a_{1}=f(a_{0}),

(10)

then

\epsilon_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}R(a_{j}),\qquad a_{j+1}=f(a_{j}),

(11)

supplemented by normalizability
Gendenshtein1983
;
Cooper1995
.

II.3
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry and pseudo-Hermiticity

For complex
f(x)
,
H_{-}
is generally non-Hermitian in the standard inner product. Two frameworks explain why spectra may remain real.

(i)
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry.

A sufficient condition for Schrödinger-type operators is
V_{-}(x)=V_{-}^{*}(-x)

Bender1998
.

(ii) Pseudo-Hermiticity.

H_{-}
is
\eta
-pseudo-Hermitian if

H_{-}^{\dagger}=\eta\,H_{-}\,\eta^{-1},

(12)

for some invertible Hermitian
\eta
. Then the physical inner product is

\langle\phi|\psi\rangle_{\eta}=\langle\phi|\eta|\psi\rangle,

(13)

and (under standard assumptions) the spectrum is real
Mostafazadeh2002
;
Mostafazadeh2003
;
Mostafazadeh2010
. A particularly useful class is the translation metric

\eta=e^{-\theta p},\qquad\eta\,x\,\eta^{-1}=x+i\theta,

(14)

which implements an imaginary shift of the coordinate and can enforce
V_{-}(x+i\theta)=V_{-}^{*}(x)

Ahmed2001
;
BagchiQuesne2002EtaOrth
.

II.4
Exact solution for a complexified Morse interaction in one dimension

We now
solve explicitly
the one-dimensional complexified Morse case in the G-KGO spatial problem (
5
). Consider

f(x)=D-q\,e^{-\alpha x},\qquad q\equiv A+iB,\qquad A,B,D,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

(15)

Introduce the convenient parameters

a\equiv\hbar\alpha,\qquad\lambda\equiv\frac{D}{a},\qquad q=|q|e^{i\varphi}.

(16)

From (
6
), the partner potential is

\displaystyle V_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=f^{2}(x)-\hbar f^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=D^{2}+q^{2}e^{-2\alpha x}-q(2D+a)e^{-\alpha x}.

(17)

Hence the spatial equation (
5
) becomes

\left[-\hbar^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+D^{2}+q^{2}e^{-2\alpha x}-q(2D+a)e^{-\alpha x}\right]\phi(x)=\epsilon\,\phi(x).

(18)

Pseudo-Hermiticity by an imaginary shift.

Choose the shift parameter

\theta=\frac{2\varphi}{\alpha}=\frac{2}{\alpha}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{B}{A}\right),\qquad\eta=e^{-\theta p}.

(19)

Then
e^{-\alpha(x+i\theta)}=e^{-\alpha x}e^{-i2\varphi}
implies

q\,e^{-\alpha(x+i\theta)}=q^{*}e^{-\alpha x},\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad f(x+i\theta)=f^{*}(x),

(20)

and consequently
V_{-}(x+i\theta)=V_{-}^{*}(x)
, realizing
\eta
-pseudo-Hermiticity for
H_{-}=-\hbar^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}+V_{-}(x)

Ahmed2001
;
Mostafazadeh2010
;
BagchiQuesne2002EtaOrth
. Therefore, the discrete eigenvalues
\epsilon_{n}
are real (under standard diagonalizability assumptions), even when
B\neq 0
.

Reduction to the standard Morse form.

Subtract the asymptotic constant and define
\tilde{\epsilon}\equiv\epsilon-D^{2}
:

\left[-\hbar^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+q^{2}e^{-2\alpha x}-q(2D+a)e^{-\alpha x}\right]\phi(x)=\tilde{\epsilon}\,\phi(x),\qquad\tilde{\epsilon}<0\ \text{for bound states}.

(21)

Introduce the Morse variable

z\equiv\frac{2q}{a}\,e^{-\alpha x},\qquad\frac{dz}{dx}=-\alpha z,

(22)

and use the standard ansatz

\phi(z)=z^{s}\,e^{-z/2}\,y(z),\qquad s>0.

(23)

After substitution into (
21
), one obtains the associated Laguerre (confluent hypergeometric) equation

z\,y^{\prime\prime}(z)+\bigl(2s+1-z\bigr)y^{\prime}(z)+n\,y(z)=0,

(24)

provided the quantization condition

s=\lambda-n,\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots

(25)

is satisfied. Normalizability at
x\to+\infty
(i.e.
z\to 0
) requires
s>0
, yielding the finite ladder

n<\lambda=\frac{D}{\hbar\alpha}.

(26)

Eigenfunctions.

With
s=\lambda-n
, the polynomial solutions of (
24
) are
y(z)=L_{n}^{(2s)}(z)
, hence

\phi_{n}(x)=\mathcal{N}_{n}\,z^{\,\lambda-n}\,e^{-z/2}\,L_{n}^{(2\lambda-2n)}(z),\qquad z=\frac{2q}{a}e^{-\alpha x},

(27)

where
\mathcal{N}_{n}
is fixed by the standard inner product in the Hermitian case (
B=0
) or by the
\eta
-inner product

\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle_{\eta}=\langle\cdot|\eta|\cdot\rangle
in the pseudo-Hermitian case
Mostafazadeh2010
.

Eigenvalues.

The quantization (
25
) yields the standard Morse energy shift

\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}=-(D-na)^{2},

(28)

and therefore the G-KGO separation constants are

\epsilon_{n}=D^{2}-(D-na)^{2}=2D\,na-n^{2}a^{2}=a^{2}\bigl(2\lambda n-n^{2}\bigr),\qquad n<\lambda.

(29)

These
\epsilon_{n}
are real and independent of the complex phase of
q
(the complexification affects eigenfunctions/metric rather than the discrete ladder), in agreement with standard complex Morse analyses
Znojil1999
;
Ahmed2001
;
BagchiQuesne2002PLA
.

III
DSR theory: kinematics and deformed dispersion

III.1
Conceptual overview

DSR introduces an observer-independent energy scale
k
while retaining a relativity principle through a deformation of Lorentz symmetry in momentum space
AmelinoCamelia2002a
;
MagueijoSmolin2002
;
KowalskiGlikman2005
;
AmelinoCamelia2010
;
AmelinoCamelia2002Open
. In bound-state applications one often adopts leading-order realizations that preserve analytic tractability.

III.2
Two prescriptions: MS and AC

In the undeformed G-KGO,
\epsilon_{n}=E_{n}^{2}-m^{2}
. In our DSR implementations, the spatial spectrum remains
\epsilon_{n}
but the energy relation is deformed.

MS deformation.

E^{2}-m^{2}\left(1-\frac{E}{k}\right)^{2}=\epsilon_{n},

(30)

which is not even in
E
and breaks exact
E\leftrightarrow-E
symmetry.

AC deformation.

\frac{E^{2}-m^{2}}{\left(1+\frac{E}{2k}\right)^{2}}=\epsilon_{n},

(31)

which becomes singular as
\epsilon_{n}\to 4k^{2}
, motivating

\epsilon_{n}<4k^{2}.

(32)

IV
G-KGO energies in DSR: closed forms and the massless limit

IV.1
MS energies

From (
30
) one obtains

\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)E^{2}+\frac{2m^{2}}{k}E-(m^{2}+\epsilon_{n})=0,

(33)

hence

E_{n,\pm}^{\text{(MS)}}=\frac{-\frac{2m^{2}}{k}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{k}\right)^{2}+4\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)(m^{2}+\epsilon_{n})}}{2\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)}.

(34)

IV.2
AC energies and admissibility

From (
31
) one finds

\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{4k^{2}}\right)E^{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{k}E-(m^{2}+\epsilon_{n})=0,

(35)

hence

E_{n,\pm}^{\text{(AC)}}=\frac{\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{k}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{k}\right)^{2}+4\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{4k^{2}}\right)(m^{2}+\epsilon_{n})}}{2\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{4k^{2}}\right)}.

(36)

The admissibility condition is (
32
).

IV.3
Massless limit (
m=0
)

MS model.

Setting
m=0
in (
30
) gives

E_{n,\pm}^{\text{(MS)},\,m=0}=\pm\sqrt{\epsilon_{n}}.

(37)

AC model.

Setting
m=0
in (
31
) yields, with
s_{n}=\sqrt{\epsilon_{n}}
,

E_{n,+}^{\text{(AC)},\,m=0}=\frac{2k\,s_{n}}{2k-s_{n}},\qquad E_{n,-}^{\text{(AC)},\,m=0}=-\frac{2k\,s_{n}}{2k+s_{n}},\qquad s_{n}<2k.

(38)

V
Example: a pseudo-Hermitian complexified Morse interaction

V.1
Interaction choice, complexification, and pseudo-Hermiticity mechanism

We adopt the complexified Morse-type interaction

f(x)=D-(A+iB)e^{-\alpha x},\qquad A,B,D,\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

(39)

and define

q\equiv A+iB=|q|e^{i\varphi},\qquad a\equiv\hbar\alpha,\qquad\lambda\equiv\frac{D}{a}.

(40)

The dependence on the
phase
of
q
can be absorbed by an imaginary shift of
x
, which is the pseudo-Hermiticity mechanism emphasized in
Ahmed2001
;
Mostafazadeh2003
;
Mostafazadeh2010
;
BagchiQuesne2002EtaOrth
. Choose

\theta=\frac{2\varphi}{\alpha}=\frac{2}{\alpha}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{B}{A}\right),\qquad\eta=e^{-\theta p},

(41)

so that
e^{-\alpha(x+i\theta)}=e^{-\alpha x}e^{-i2\varphi}
and therefore

q\,e^{-\alpha(x+i\theta)}=q^{*}\,e^{-\alpha x}.

(42)

Hence

f(x+i\theta)=D-q\,e^{-\alpha(x+i\theta)}=D-q^{*}e^{-\alpha x}=f^{*}(x),

(43)

and the induced potential satisfies
V_{-}(x+i\theta)=V_{-}^{*}(x)
, realizing
\eta
-pseudo-Hermiticity for
H_{-}=-\hbar^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}+V_{-}(x)

Ahmed2001
;
Mostafazadeh2003
;
Mostafazadeh2010
;
BagchiQuesne2002EtaOrth
.

V.2
Partner potential and reduction to a standard Morse problem

With (
39
) the potential (
6
) becomes

\displaystyle V_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=f^{2}(x)-\hbar f^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=D^{2}+q^{2}e^{-2\alpha x}-q(2D+a)e^{-\alpha x}.

(44)

The eigenvalue equation (
5
) is

\left[-\hbar^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+D^{2}+q^{2}e^{-2\alpha x}-q(2D+a)e^{-\alpha x}\right]\phi=\epsilon\,\phi.

(45)

It is convenient to subtract the asymptotic constant
D^{2}
and define

\tilde{\epsilon}\equiv\epsilon-D^{2},\qquad\left[-\hbar^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+q^{2}e^{-2\alpha x}-q(2D+a)e^{-\alpha x}\right]\phi=\tilde{\epsilon}\,\phi.

(46)

Bound states correspond to
\tilde{\epsilon}<0
and the quantization is finite. The complex Morse structure is a standard solvable non-Hermitian benchmark
Znojil1999
;
Dorey2001
;
BagchiQuesne2002PLA
.

V.3
Spatial spectrum: exact quantization and finite bound-state count

Introduce the Morse variable

z\equiv\frac{2q}{a}\,e^{-\alpha x},\qquad\frac{dz}{dx}=-\alpha z.

(47)

The standard Morse reduction yields the quantization condition

\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}=-(D-na)^{2},\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots

(48)

and therefore

\epsilon_{n}=D^{2}-(D-na)^{2}=2D\,na-n^{2}a^{2}=a^{2}\bigl(2\lambda n-n^{2}\bigr).

(49)

Normalizability yields a
finite
ladder:

n<\lambda=\frac{D}{\hbar\alpha}.

(50)

This intrinsic finiteness is the Morse hallmark
Morse1929
and will compete with the AC admissibility cutoff.

Monotonicity.

For
0\leq n<\lambda
,

\epsilon_{n+1}-\epsilon_{n}=a^{2}\bigl(2\lambda-(2n+1)\bigr)>0,

(51)

so
\epsilon_{n}
increases monotonically with
n
across the bound ladder.

V.4
Morse wavefunctions: Laguerre form and inner-product issues

A convenient Laguerre representation of the bound states is

\phi_{n}(x)=\mathcal{N}_{n}\,z^{\,\lambda-n}\,e^{-z/2}\,L_{n}^{(2\lambda-2n)}(z),\qquad z=\frac{2q}{a}e^{-\alpha x},

(52)

where
L_{n}^{(\nu)}
are associated Laguerre polynomials. In the Hermitian Morse case (
B=0
and
q>0
), normalization may be taken in the standard inner product. In the pseudo-Hermitian complex case (
B\neq 0
), physical orthogonality and normalization are defined with the
\eta
-inner product

\langle\phi_{m}|\phi_{n}\rangle_{\eta}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{m}^{*}(x)\,(\eta\phi_{n})(x)\,dx,\qquad\eta=e^{-\theta p},

(53)

which is the natural setting when
H_{-}
is
\eta
-pseudo-Hermitian
Mostafazadeh2010
;
BagchiQuesne2002EtaOrth
.

V.5
Undeformed Klein–Gordon energies for the Morse spectrum

In the undeformed relativistic case, (
7
) gives

E_{n,\pm}^{(0)}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+\epsilon_{n}}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+a^{2}(2\lambda n-n^{2})}.

(54)

V.6
MS energies for the Morse spectrum: explicit branches and constraints

Substituting (
49
) into (
34
) yields

E_{n,\pm}^{\text{(MS)}}=\frac{-\frac{2m^{2}}{k}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{k}\right)^{2}+4\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\left[m^{2}+a^{2}(2\lambda n-n^{2})\right]}}{2\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)}.

(55)

Because MS is not even in
E
, the positive and negative branches are not symmetric at finite
k
. The Morse bound-state count remains (
50
); in addition one typically assumes
k>m
so that
1-m^{2}/k^{2}>0
.

V.7
AC energies for the Morse spectrum: criticality and DSR truncation

Substituting (
49
) into (
36
) yields

E_{n,\pm}^{\text{(AC)}}=\frac{\frac{a^{2}(2\lambda n-n^{2})}{k}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{a^{2}(2\lambda n-n^{2})}{k}\right)^{2}+4\left(1-\frac{a^{2}(2\lambda n-n^{2})}{4k^{2}}\right)\left[m^{2}+a^{2}(2\lambda n-n^{2})\right]}}{2\left(1-\frac{a^{2}(2\lambda n-n^{2})}{4k^{2}}\right)}.

(56)

The AC admissibility condition (
32
) becomes

a^{2}(2\lambda n-n^{2})<4k^{2}.

(57)

Since
\epsilon_{n}
increases with
n
for
n<\lambda
, the AC constraint truncates the
highest
Morse levels when it is active. Solving
\epsilon_{n}=4k^{2}
gives the root in
[0,\lambda]

n_{\star}=\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-\left(\frac{2k}{a}\right)^{2}}=\frac{D-\sqrt{D^{2}-4k^{2}}}{\hbar\alpha},\qquad(D>2k),

(58)

so the admissible bound states satisfy

n=0,1,2,\dots,\qquad n<\min\!\left(\lambda,\;n_{\star}\right).

(59)

If
D\leq 2k
then
\epsilon_{n}\leq D^{2}\leq 4k^{2}
for all Morse bound states, and AC does not further truncate the ladder.

V.8
Massless limit for Morse: MS collapse vs AC persistence

For
m=0
, MS collapses to the undeformed map:

E_{n,\pm}^{\text{(MS)},\,m=0}=\pm\sqrt{\epsilon_{n}}=\pm a\,\sqrt{2\lambda n-n^{2}}.

(60)

In contrast, AC remains deformed. With
s_{n}\equiv\sqrt{\epsilon_{n}}=a\sqrt{2\lambda n-n^{2}}
,

E_{n,+}^{\text{(AC)},\,m=0}=\frac{2k\,s_{n}}{2k-s_{n}},\qquad E_{n,-}^{\text{(AC)},\,m=0}=-\frac{2k\,s_{n}}{2k+s_{n}},\qquad s_{n}<2k,

(61)

and
s_{n}<2k
is exactly (
57
).

VI
Discussion

The complexified Morse interaction provides a transparent analytic benchmark for the G-KGO in DSR because (i) the full spatial problem is solvable in closed form by shape invariance
Gendenshtein1983
;
Cooper1995
, (ii) the discrete spatial spectrum
\epsilon_{n}
is real and intrinsically finite
Morse1929
, and (iii) non-Hermiticity is controlled by a constructive pseudo-Hermitian mechanism based on an imaginary shift of the coordinate
Ahmed2001
;
Mostafazadeh2003
;
Mostafazadeh2010
;
BagchiQuesne2002EtaOrth
. In this setting the complexification parameters
(A,B)
primarily reshape eigenfunctions and the metric, while leaving the discrete ladder (
49
) invariant; this separates non-Hermitian structure (inner product and eigenbasis) from kinematical deformation (energy reconstruction).

DSR then acts purely algebraically. In the MS prescription the deformation yields branch-asymmetric spectra through the non-even dependence on
E
in (
30
), while the Morse finiteness (
50
) remains the universal truncation mechanism. In contrast, the AC prescription introduces a criticality at
\epsilon_{n}\to 4k^{2}
, producing the additional admissibility inequality (
57
). Because the Morse ladder is monotone in
n
, this AC condition truncates the
highest
bound levels whenever
D>2k
, leading to the combined cutoff summarized by (
59
). The massless limit highlights the qualitative difference: MS collapses to the undeformed mapping
E^{2}=\epsilon_{n}
, whereas AC retains a nonlinear map and the same admissibility threshold.

From a phenomenological perspective, DSR-inspired modified dispersion relations are also constrained by locality and related consistency considerations, which motivates treating admissibility bounds (such as
\epsilon_{n}<4k^{2}
in AC) as physically meaningful rather than purely formal
Hossenfelder2010PRL
;
Hossenfelder2013LRR
;
AmelinoCamelia2002Open
. More broadly, complexified solvable families such as Morse remain valuable because they permit controlled non-Hermitian effects without sacrificing analytic tractability
Bender2007RPP
;
ElGanainy2018NatPhys
.

Conclusion and outlook

We reviewed the
(1+1)
generalized Klein–Gordon oscillator as a factorized (SUSY-like) relativistic scalar system and discussed
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry/pseudo-Hermiticity as mechanisms ensuring spectral reality for complex interactions. For a complexified Morse interaction we derived the spatial spectrum and wavefunctions, exhibited an explicit pseudo-Hermitian imaginary-shift metric, and obtained MS/AC DSR-deformed energies in closed form. AC enforces an additional admissibility cutoff
\epsilon_{n}<4k^{2}
which can further truncate the intrinsically finite Morse ladder when
D>2k
, while MS does not introduce an analogous universal truncation in the present realization. Extensions include higher-dimensional G-KGOs, external fields, and thermodynamic analyses based on the deformed spectra.
