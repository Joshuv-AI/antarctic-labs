Abstract
For an integer t ≥ 3 t\geq 3 , let ℒ ⁡ ( t ) \mathcal{L}(t) denote the linear equation x 1 + x 2 + ⋯ + x t − 1 = x t , x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{t-1}=x_{t}, where all variables are positive integers. For integers k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 and t 0 , t 1 , … , t k − 1 ≥ 3 t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1}\geq 3 , the generalized Schur number S ⁡ ( k , t 0 , t 1 , … , t k − 1 ) S(k;t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1}) is the least positive integer N N such that every k k -coloring of [ 1 , N ] [1,N] , for some i ∈ { 0 , 1 , … , k − 1 } i\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\} , a solution to ℒ ⁡ ( t i ) \mathcal{L}(t_{i}) with all variables monochromatic in color i i . In 2015, Ahmed and Schaal proposed a conjecture: S ⁡ ( 3 , 3 , t , u ) > 3 ​ t ​ u − t ​ u − u − 1 S(3;3,t,u)>3tu-tu-u-1 for 3 = t < u 3=t<u and 3 < t ≤ u 3<t\leq u . In this paper, we confirm this conjecture. At the same paper, they also conjecture that S ⁡ ( 3 , s , t , u ) = s ​ t ​ u − t ​ u − u − 1 S(3;s,t,u)=stu-tu-u-1 for 4 ≤ s ≤ t ≤ u 4\leq s\leq t\leq u . Motivated by the second conjecture, we give a recursive lower bound of S ⁡ ( r , k 0 , k 1 , … , k r − 1 ) S(r;k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}) and upper bounds for S ⁡ ( r , k 0 , k 1 , … , k r − 1 ) S(r;k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}) and S ⁡ ( r , k 0 , … , k r − 2 , u ) S(r;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-2},u) for all sufficiently large u u . Let

Throughout, variables such as

A function

A recursive lower bound

Ahmed and Schaal in
[
2
]
obtained a general lower bound, as shown below.

Theorem 3
.

[
2
]

For all
r\geq 1
and for all
k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}
where
3\leq k_{0}\leq k_{1}\leq\dots\leq k_{r-1}
,

S(r;k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1})\geq\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}k_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i}^{r-1}k_{j}-1.

In the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 2.11]
, the following conclusion is established.
Let
r\geq 2
, let
3\leq k_{0}\leq k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r-1}
.

S(r;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-1})\geq k_{r-1}\,S(r-1;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-2})-1

(1)

Theorem 4
.

Let
r\geq 3
and let
3\leq k_{0}\leq k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r-1}
. Then for every integer
m
with

2\leq m\leq r-1
,

S(r;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-1})\geq\Bigl(\prod_{j=m}^{r-1}k_{j}\Bigr)\,S(m;k_{0},\dots,k_{m-1})-\sum_{i=m}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-1}k_{j}.

Here an empty product is interpreted as
1
.

Proof.

We induct on
r-m
. If
r-m=1
, then the formula is exactly (
1
).
Assume now that
r-m\geq 2
and that the statement holds with
r
replaced by
r-1
.
By (
1
),

S(r;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-1})\geq k_{r-1}\,S(r-1;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-2})-1.

Applying the induction hypothesis to the factor
S(r-1;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-2})
gives

S(r-1;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-2})\geq\Bigl(\prod_{j=m}^{r-2}k_{j}\Bigr)S(m;k_{0},\dots,k_{m-1})-\sum_{i=m}^{r-2}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-2}k_{j}.

Substituting this into the previous inequality yields

\displaystyle S(r;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-1})

\displaystyle\geq k_{r-1}\left[\Bigl(\prod_{j=m}^{r-2}k_{j}\Bigr)S(m;k_{0},\dots,k_{m-1})-\sum_{i=m}^{r-2}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-2}k_{j}\right]-1

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\prod_{j=m}^{r-1}k_{j}\Bigr)S(m;k_{0},\dots,k_{m-1})-\sum_{i=m}^{r-2}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-1}k_{j}-1

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\prod_{j=m}^{r-1}k_{j}\Bigr)S(m;k_{0},\dots,k_{m-1})-\sum_{i=m}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-1}k_{j},

as required.
∎

Corollary 5
.

For every
r\geq 3
and every
3\leq k_{0}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r-1}
,

S(r;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-1})\geq\Bigl(\prod_{j=2}^{r-1}k_{j}\Bigr)S(2;k_{0},k_{1})-\sum_{i=2}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-1}k_{j}.

In particular, if
4\leq s\leq t\leq k_{2}\leq\cdots\leq k_{r-1}
, then

S(r;s,t,k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1})\geq\Bigl(\prod_{j=2}^{r-1}k_{j}\Bigr)(st-t-1)-\sum_{i=2}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-1}k_{j}.

Proof.

Upon setting
m=2
in Theorem
4
, the second displayed equation is a consequence of
[
21
]
, namely

S(2;s,t)=st-t-1
for
4\leq s\leq t.

∎

Using Theorem
4
, we are able to give an alternative proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We proceed by induction on
r
. For
r=1
, it is readily seen that
S(1;k_{0})=k_{0}-1
. Suppose the statement holds for
r-1
, we now establish the result for
r
. For
2\leq m\leq r-1
,
by the induction hypothesis, we have

S(m;k_{0},\dots,k_{m-1})\geq\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}k_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\prod_{j=i}^{m-1}k_{j}-1,

By Theorem
4
, we have

\displaystyle S(r;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-1})

\displaystyle\geq\left(\prod_{j=m}^{r-1}k_{j}\right)\left(\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}k_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\prod_{j=i}^{m-1}k_{j}-1\right)-\sum_{i=m}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-1}k_{j}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}k_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\prod_{j=i}^{r-1}k_{j}-\prod_{j=m}^{r-1}k_{j}-\sum_{i=m}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-1}k_{j}.

Note that

\prod_{j=m}^{r-1}k_{j}+\sum_{i=m}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{r-1}k_{j}=\sum_{i=m}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i}^{r-1}k_{j}+1,

since the empty product appearing in the final term is equal to
1
.
Consequently, the aforementioned inequality reduces to

S(r;k_{0},\dots,k_{r-1})\geq\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}k_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\prod_{j=i}^{r-1}k_{j}-1,

which is precisely the stated bound.
∎
