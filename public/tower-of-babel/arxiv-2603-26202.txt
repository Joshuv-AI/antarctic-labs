Non-bulging Fatou components for transcendental skew-products
Tom Potthink, Jasmin Raissy

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the bulging of escaping or oscillating Fatou components on invariant fibers for general skew-products, with a focus on the dependence on the perturbation. We show that any orbitally unbounded component is non-bulging for an appropriate choice of perturbation, whereas sufficiently well-behaved perturbations can render it bulging when the fiber is attracting. Our results highlight that bulging is influenced by more than just the dynamics on the fiber and in the one-dimensional coordinate, contrasting sharply with established results for non-escaping Fatou components.

1.
Introduction

A central goal of complex dynamics is the study of the Fatou and Julia sets, which correspond, respectively, to the sets of stable and unstable behavior of the iterates of a holomorphic function.

While the dynamics of entire functions in one complex variable is by now well understood, even simple extensions to higher dimensions, such as skew-products, exhibit fundamentally new phenomena that are not yet fully explored.

In dimension one, the types of components that constitute the Fatou set of an entire function are nearly completely classified. The basic classification was first established by Fatou and later further developed and completely proven by several authors (Julia, Leau, Siegel, Herman…). Furthermore, more recently, progress has been made to better understand wandering domains (cf.
[
BEF+22
]
and
[
FR24
]
). In higher dimensions, however, no similarly complete understanding of Fatou components exists. A natural way to bridge one- and two-dimensional dynamics is to consider maps with a partially decoupled structure, where one coordinate evolves independently.

In this paper, we study two-dimensional Fatou components of
skew-products
, i.e., maps
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
of the form

F(z,w)=(f(z,w),g(w)).

Skew-products serve as a natural stepping stone from one-dimensional to two-dimensional dynamics, since they often allow the reduction of two-dimensional behavior to one-dimensional analysis. Of particular interest is the behavior near an invariant fiber
\{w=c\}
, as on the fiber itself, the skew-product reduces to a one-dimensional function. In fact, the interplay between the dynamics on invariant fibers and the full two-dimensional system raises subtle questions about stability and the structure of Fatou components. This setting has been explored, for example, in
[
Lil04
]
for polynomial skew-products and
attracting
fibers, that is, fibers
\{w=c\}
where
c
is an
attracting
fixed point of
g
(i.e.,
g(c)=c
and
|g^{\prime}(c)|<1
).

Without loss of generality, we assume throughout that
c=0
and we write the skew-product
F
as

F(z,w)=(f(z)+r(z,w),g(w))

where the holomorphic function
f
gives the one-dimensional action of
F
on the invariant fiber and
r\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic map such that
r(z,0)=0
. We refer to
r
as the
perturbation
or
error term
, since it measures the deviation from the one-dimensional dynamics on the fiber.

Since our work concerns the Fatou set in higher dimensions, it is necessary to discuss which definition of normality is used. For polynomial skew-products, the choice of normality is clear; however, this is not the case in the transcendental setting. Building on the discussion in
[
ABFP19
]
, several viable extensions of the one-dimensional notion exist, depending on which compactification for
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is used. In this paper, we consider three options: the one-point compactification
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{2}}
, the projective space
\mathbf{P}_{2}{\mathbb{C}}
, and the product space
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. We show in
\Autoref
SECTION:2 that for our results the choice of definition is immaterial.

Geometrically, when investigating the dynamics near an invariant fiber it is natural to study whether a stable one-dimensional region persists when embedded in the full two-dimensional system. More precisely, one may ask: when does a one-dimensional Fatou component extend to a two-dimensional one? In particular, we seek to understand whether all one-dimensional Fatou components of
f
on the invariant fiber
bulge
, that is, extend to two-dimensional Fatou components of
F
. Bulging creates a direct link between the dynamics on the fiber and the full skew-product near the fiber, potentially reducing the analysis to the well-understood one-dimensional case. This setting and related phenomena have been studied extensively; see, e.g.,
[
Lil04
]
,
[
PV16
]
,
[
PS17
]
,
[
Ji19
]
, and
[
JS25
]
(see also
[
Rai16
]
for an overview). In particular, Lilov showed in
[
Lil04
]
that periodic one-dimensional Fatou components with bounded orbits on an
attracting
invariant fiber always bulge.

In this paper, we focus on
escaping
or
oscillating
Fatou components on the fiber, that is, components for which at least a subsequence of iterates tends uniformly to infinity. This includes
escaping
and
oscillating wandering domains
,
Baker domains
, and
basins of attraction at infinity
, (these last basins can arise if the skew-product restricts to a polynomial on the fiber). In these cases, we show that the bulging of a component depends sensitively on the perturbation, reflecting the fact that the perturbation may evolve as the orbit escapes to infinity.

Our main result shows the existence of perturbations preventing stable behavior around points with unbounded orbits. The main idea is to construct a perturbation that forces nearby orbits to exhibit incompatible behaviors, some escaping to infinity while others remain bounded, thereby destroying normality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be nonconstant entire functions with
g(0)=0
. Furthermore, assume that there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
with
f^{n_{k}}(z_{0})\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
for some subsequence of iterates. Then there exists
h
entire such that the point
(z_{0},0)
belongs to the Julia set of the skew-product
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
defined as

(1)

F(z,w)=(f(z)+wh(z),g(w)).

Observe that, for normality with respect to the compactifications
\mathbf{P}_{2}{\mathbb{C}}
or
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{2}}
, but not
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{2}
, the fact that
0
is in the Julia set of
g
does not automatically imply that
(z_{0},0)
is in the Julia set of
F
. In particular, our result remains nontrivial even when
0
is in the Julia set of
g
.

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following result about the existence of non-bulging Fatou components.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f
and
g
be nonconstant entire functions with
0
a fixed point of
g
belonging to the Fatou set of
g
, and
U
a Fatou component of
f
. Furthermore assume that there exists
z_{0}\in U
with
f^{n_{k}}(z_{0})\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
for some subsequence of iterates.
Then there exists
h
entire such that the Fatou component
U
does not bulge for the skew-product
F
as defined in (
1
).

This statement holds for escaping wandering domains, oscillating wandering domains, Baker domains, and basins of attraction at infinity on either an attracting fiber or an invariant fiber in a Siegel disk.
Escaping and oscillating domains arise only in the transcendental setting, thus our focus here is mainly on transcendental skew-products. The only exception is when the function reduces to a polynomial on the fiber, which always has a basin at infinity. In this case, the construction still applies and the resulting skew-product is in general non-polynomial. However, the basin at infinity does not need to bulge for polynomial perturbations, as shown in
\Autoref
EXAMPLE:basininfinity. This provides the only case where a polynomial skew-product has a non-bulging Fatou component on an attracting fiber, showing that basins at infinity for polynomial skew-products can be non-bulging.

Our constructions constitute the first known example of non-bulging wandering domains and the second example of non-bulging Fatou components on attracting fibers in general, we achieved a similar construction for Baker domains in
[
BPR25
]
, in collaboration with Benini.

On the other hand, we also show that for attracting invariant fibers, wandering domains can bulge in non-trivial settings and we give a sufficient condition for bulging for a particular class of functions with wandering domains. These results are described in
\Autoref
THEOREM:BULGINGORDER and
\Autoref
COROLLARY:GROWTH.

These results highlight a key difference with periodic Fatou components with bounded orbits: for orbitally unbounded components, the behavior depends sensitively on the interaction between the base dynamics and the perturbation.

2.
Preliminaries

First, we recall the following definitions and statements from one-dimensional dynamics.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be an entire function.
The
Fatou set
of
f
is defined as the set of normality of the iterates
\{f^{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
The components of the Fatou set are called
Fatou components
of
f
. Given
U\subset\mathbb{C}
a Fatou component of the entire function
f
,
f^{n}(U)
is contained in some Fatou component
U_{n}
of
f
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and we call
U

(1)

periodic
if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
U=U_{n}
, and
invariant
if this holds for
n=1
,

(2)

preperiodic
if
U_{n}=U_{m}
for some
n\neq m
, and

(3)

wandering
if
U_{n}\neq U_{m}
for all
n\neq m
.

Fatou Classification Theorem gives a complete classification for (pre-)periodic Fatou components. In particular, if
U
is an invariant Fatou component of
f
, then either:

(i)

U
is the
immediate attractive basin
of some
z_{0}\in U
. In particular, it holds that
\lvert f^{\prime}(z_{0})\rvert<1
, or

(ii)

U
is the
parabolic domain
of some
z_{0}\in\partial U
, or

(iii)

U
is a
rotation domain
, that is, either a
Siegel disk
around an elliptic fixed point
z_{0}\in U
or an
Herman ring
, or

(iv)

U
is a
Baker domain
, that is,
f^{n}(z)\to\infty
for all
z\in U
as
n\to\infty
, but
f(\infty)
is not well-defined.

Recall that only transcendental entire functions can have Baker domains.

The Non Wandering Domain Theorem due to Sullivan
[
Sul85
]

states that for rational functions of degree
d\geq 2
every Fatou component is eventually periodic. Therefore, for rational functions of degree
d\geq 2
, we have a complete description of the dynamics in the Fatou set. On the other hand, prior to Sullivan’s result, the first examples of an entire function with a wandering domains were found by Herman and published in
[
Bak84
]
.
The well-understood one-dimensional dynamics of such functions, similar to
f(z)=z+2\pi i+e^{z}
, involve a wandering domain
U_{0}
around an attracting fixed point
z_{0}
of an associated function as described in the following lemma, that we shall use in
\Autoref
SECTION:3 to find a class of skew-products with bulging wandering domains.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
Bak84
, Section 5]
)
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
p
a
T
-periodic entire function, and
f(z)=z+p(z)+T
.
If the entire function
z+p(z)
has an attracting fixed point
z_{0}
with corresponding basin of attraction
U_{0}
, then the sets
U_{n}:=U_{0}+nT
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, are pairwise disjoint Fatou components of
f
satisfying
f(U_{n})\subset U_{n+1}
. In particular, they form a sequence of wandering domains for
f
.

We now discuss normality in the higher-dimensional setting. While in one dimension, it is natural to consider the compactification of
\mathbb{C}
given by the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, the one point compactification of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is not a complex manifold, and there are several natural compactifications of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
that are complex manifolds (see
[
Mor73
]
and
[
Bre73
]
for example).
Several definitions of normality have been used in higher-dimensional dynamics.
Here, we consider definitions based on either the one-point compactification
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{n}}:=\mathbb{C}^{n}\cup\{\infty\}
, as first presented in
[
Wu67
]
, the projective space
\mathbf{P}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
, as in
[
ABFP19
]
, or
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{n}=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\times\dots\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
The last is of interest since it extends the dynamics of direct products in an intuitive manner.

Definition 2.2

(
[
Wu67
, Definition 1.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
A family
\mathcal{F}\subset\operatorname{Hol}(X,\mathbb{C}^{n})
of holomorphic maps is called
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
-normal
if every sequence in
\mathcal{F}
is either uniformly divergent to infinity on compact subsets or has a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets to a limit in
\operatorname{Hol}(X,\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Definition 2.3

(
[
ABFP19
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
A family
\mathcal{F}\subset\operatorname{Hol}(X,\mathbb{C}^{n})
of holomorphic maps is called
\mathbf{P}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
-normal
if every sequence in
\mathcal{F}
has a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets to a limit in
\operatorname{Hol}(X,\mathbf{P}_{n}{\mathbb{C}})
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
A family
\mathcal{F}\subset\operatorname{Hol}(X,\mathbb{C}^{n})
of holomorphic maps is called
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{n}
-normal
if every sequence in
\mathcal{F}
has a subsequence converging uniformly on compact subsets to a limit in
\operatorname{Hol}(X,(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{n})
.

Using the definitions above, the Fatou set of a holomorphic function in higher dimensions is defined as the set where the iterates form a normal family.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a holomorphic function for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
-,
\mathbf{P}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
-, and
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{n}
-
Fatou set
of
F
as the respective sets of normality of the iterates
(F^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The corresponding Julia sets are defined as the complements.

The Fatou sets in higher dimensions share some properties with their one-dimensional counterpart, though not all.
While the Fatou set remains backward invariant, a property which we will use later, it is no longer forward invariant.
For
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, the lack of forward invariance was discussed in
[
FS98
, Proposition 2.4]
.
The proof of backward invariance follows the same argument as in the one-dimensional case.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any holomorphic map
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the Fatou set is backward invariant:

F^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(F))\subset\mathcal{F}(F).

While the three notions of normality are not equivalent, they satisfy an inclusion relation: normality with respect to
\mathbf{P}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
or
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{n}
implies normality with respect to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
.
This is described in the following lemma; the
\mathbf{P}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
case was proven in
[
ABFP19
, Lemma 2.4]
and the
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{n}
case follows analogously.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
ABFP19
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a connected complex manifold and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\operatorname{Hol}(X,\mathbb{C}^{n})
is
\mathbf{P}_{n}{\mathbb{C}}
-normal or
(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{n}
-normal, then it is already
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
-normal.
As a consequence, for each of these three compactifications, any limit function either lies in
\operatorname{Hol}(X,\mathbb{C}^{n})
or the corresponding sequence diverges locally uniformly to infinity.

Our main result
\Autoref
THEOREM:NONBULGING establishes nonbulging for
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
-normality, which by the above lemma suffices for all three notions of normality.
Hence, the choice of normality is immaterial for the rest of this paper.
When the type of Fatou set is not explicitly specified in a statement, it applies to all three definitions.
In particular, we use
\mathcal{F}(F)
and
\mathcal{J}(F)
to denote the Fatou and Julia sets of
F
regardless of which notion of normality is used.

Lastly, the following definition provides the precise meaning of bulging.

Definition 2.8
.

Given
f,g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
and
h\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
holomorphic functions with
0
a fixed point in the Fatou set of
g
, consider the skew-product
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
of the form
F(z,w)=(f(z)+wh(z,w),g(w))
.
We call a Fatou component
U\subset\mathbb{C}
of
f

bulging
with respect to a given compactification (i.e., a notion of normality) if
U\times\{0\}
lies in the corresponding Fatou set of
F
.

3.
Non-bulging Fatou Components

In this section, we construct perturbations that prevent bulging. The strategy is to carefully design an entire function using approximation techniques so that nearby orbits behave in fundamentally different ways. The main tool is an approximation argument based on the following two well-known results, namely a variant of Runge’s Theorem and Weierstraß’s Theorem for uniformly convergent sequences.

Lemma 3.1

(Runge,
[
EL87
, p. 460]
; see also
[
BEF+22
, Lemma 5.1]
)
.

Let
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
such that

(1)

the sets
\mathbb{C}\setminus E_{n}
are connected for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

the sets are pairwise disjoint, i.e.,
E_{n}\cap E_{m}=\emptyset
for all
n\neq m
, and

(3)

\min\lvert E_{n}\rvert:=\min\{\lvert z\rvert\mid z\in E_{n}\}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Furthermore, suppose that
\psi
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
E=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}
, and let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers. Then there exists an entire function
h
such that

\lvert h(z)-\psi(z)\rvert<\varepsilon_{n}

for all
z\in E_{n}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Weierstraß,
[
Ahl79
, p. 176]
)
.

Let
G_{1}\subset G_{2}\subset\dots
be an increasing sequence of domains in
\mathbb{C}
and
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of holomorphic functions
f_{n}\colon G_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
. Let
G:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}G_{n}
and assume that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly on any compact subset of
G
to a limit function

. Then

is holomorphic on
G
.

We now prove
\Autoref
THEOREM:NONBULGING. The key step in the proof is to construct a perturbation

that forces orbits near to the wandering domain in the invariant fiber to behave in incompatible ways, as one orbit grows large while another remains bounded, violating normality.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that a priori, the entire function
h
may be transcendental, and it is reasonable to ask whether for polynomial skew-products, the basin of attraction at infinity always bulges.
However, the following example shows that the basin at infinity does not need to bulge for polynomial perturbations.

Example 3.6
.

The basin at infinity of the polynomial skew-product
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}

F(z,w)=(z^{2}-wz^{3},\lambda w),

is non-bulging for any real number
0<\lambda<1
.
Indeed, let
x_{0}>0
be such that
\lambda x_{0}>1
and
\lambda^{2}x_{0}>1
.
In particular, since
x_{0}>1
, the point lies in the basin at infinity of
z\mapsto z^{2}
.
Since
F
preserves
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it suffices to show that there are points arbitrarily close to
(x_{0},0)
whose orbits are bounded and hence cannot diverge to infinity. Therefore, the basin at infinity cannot bulge.

We first show that for every
\delta>0
there exists
0<y_{0}<\delta
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{n_{0}}>0
and
x_{n_{0}+1}\leq 0
, where
(x_{n_{0}},y_{n_{0}}):=F^{n_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})
.

Let
\delta>0
.
By our choice of
x_{0}
, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\lambda^{2}x_{0})^{2^{n}}=\infty
and so there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
with

(6)

\frac{1}{(\lambda^{2}x_{0})^{2^{n-1}}}<1-\lambda

for all
n\geq N
. Fix
n_{0}\geq N
large enough so that

y_{0}:=\frac{1}{(\lambda x_{0})^{2^{n_{0}}}\lambda^{n_{0}-1}}<\delta.

Using (
6
) and the definition of
y_{0}
, we deduce

x_{0}^{2^{n_{0}-1}}\lambda^{n_{0}-1}y_{0}=\frac{1}{(\lambda^{2}x_{0})^{2^{n_{0}-1}}}<1-\lambda<1=\frac{y_{0}}{y_{0}}=y_{0}(\lambda x_{0})^{2^{n_{0}}}\lambda^{n_{0}-1}.

Let
k\leq n_{0}-1
. Observe that
y_{k}=\lambda^{k}y_{0}
and furthermore, if
x_{0},\dots,x_{k}>0
, then
x_{k}\leq x_{0}^{2^{k}}
. If one of
x_{0},\dots,x_{n_{0}-1}
is already nonpositive, then we are done. Thus, we may assume that all
x_{0},\dots,x_{n_{0}-1}
are positive.
For
k\leq n_{0}-1
, we compute

x_{k}y_{k}\leq x_{0}^{2^{k}}\lambda^{k}y_{0}\leq x_{0}^{2^{n_{0}-1}}\lambda^{n_{0}-1}y_{0}<1-\lambda

where we used that
x_{0}\lambda>1
. Thus, we have
1-x_{k}y_{k}>\lambda
, and therefore

x_{k+1}=x_{k}^{2}(1-x_{k}y_{k})\geq x_{k}^{2}\lambda.

Induction over
k
yields

x_{k+1}\geq x_{0}^{2^{k+1}}\lambda^{2^{k+1}-1}

for all
k\leq n_{0}-1
. For
k=n_{0}-1
, we obtain

x_{n_{0}}y_{n_{0}}\geq x_{0}^{2^{n_{0}}}\lambda^{2^{n_{0}}-1}\lambda^{n_{0}}y_{0}=y_{0}(\lambda x_{0})^{2^{n_{0}}}\lambda^{n_{0}-1}=1

which implies
1-x_{n_{0}}y_{n_{0}}\leq 0
and thus

x_{n_{0}+1}=x_{n_{0}}^{2}(1-x_{n_{0}}y_{n_{0}})\leq 0.

Now that we have proven the existence of such

, the Intermediate Value Theorem implies the existence of
0\leq\widetilde{y}_{0}\leq y_{0}
such that
\pi_{z}F^{n}(x_{0},\widetilde{y}_{0})=0
.
Since this holds for arbitrarily small

and since the basin of attraction at

is bulging (as shown in
[

]
and
[

]
), the orbits of

get caught in the bulged basin of attraction at
0
for

F^{N}(z,0)=(f^{N}(z),0)=(b^{N}(z)+NT,0)\in D\left(NT,\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\times\{0\}\subset\mathcal{F}(F)

where we used (
10
) in the last step.
Using the backward invariance of the Fatou set, established in
\Autoref
LEMMA:BackwardInvariance, we deduce that
(z,0)
belongs to the Fatou set of
F
, which completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

First, observe that the convergence of the series in (
8
) yields an integer
L\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{k=L}^{\infty}\rho_{k}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\rho_{k},\\
\lvert\zeta-kT\rvert\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\lvert h(\zeta,\xi)\rvert<\frac{\delta}{2}.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
l\geq L
.
The uniform convergence of
w_{k}
to
0
in a small neighborhood of
0
follows directly from the fact that
0
is a attracting fixed point of
g
.
By our assumption that
\rho_{k}
tends to
0
as
k\to\infty
, this holds in particular on
D(0,\rho_{l})
.

Inductively, we prove for all
(z,w)\in D\left(lT,\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\times D(0,\rho_{l})
and all
k\in\mathbb{N}
that

(11)

\left\lvert z_{k}-(l+k)T\right\rvert<\sum_{j=l}^{l+k-1}\rho_{j}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\rho_{j},\\
\lvert\zeta-jT\rvert\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\lvert h(\zeta,\xi)\rvert+\frac{\delta}{2}

where
z_{k}=\pi_{z}F^{k}(z,w)
.
Here, empty sums are interpreted as
0
.
This already implies that
z_{k}
diverges uniformly to
\infty
on
D\left(lT,\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\times D(0,\rho_{l})
as
k\to\infty
, since

\begin{split}\sum_{j=l}^{l+k-1}\rho_{j}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\rho_{j},\\
\lvert\zeta-jT\rvert\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\lvert h(\zeta,\xi)\rvert+\frac{\delta}{2}&\leq\sum_{j=L}^{\infty}\rho_{j}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\rho_{j},\\
\lvert\zeta-jT\rvert\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\lvert h(\zeta,\xi)\rvert+\frac{\delta}{2}<\delta\end{split}

and
(l+k)T
diverges to
\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

Let
z\in D\left(lT,\frac{\delta}{2}\right)
and
w\in D(0,\rho_{l})
.
The base case
k=0
of the induction is evident.
Assume that (
11
) holds for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular, it follows that
z_{k}\in D((l+k)T,\delta)
.
Furthermore, by our hypotheses on the sequence
(\rho_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have
\lvert w_{k}\rvert=\lvert g^{k}(w)\rvert\leq\rho_{l+k}
.
As a consequence, we may apply (
9
) and obtain

\begin{split}|z_{k+1}-(l+k+1)T|&=\lvert f(z_{k})+w_{k}h(z_{k},w_{k})-(l+k+1)T\rvert\\
&\leq\lvert f(z_{k})-(l+k+1)T\rvert+\lvert w_{k}h(z_{k},w_{k})\rvert\\
&\leq\lvert z_{k}-(l+k)T\rvert+\lvert w_{k}\rvert\lvert h(z_{k},w_{k})\rvert\\
&\leq\lvert z_{k}-(l+k)T\rvert+\rho_{l+k}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\rho_{l+k},\\
\lvert\zeta-(l+k)T\rvert\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\lvert h(\zeta,\xi)\rvert\\
&<\sum_{j=l}^{l+k}\rho_{j}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\rho_{j},\\
\lvert\zeta-jT\rvert\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\lvert h(\zeta,\xi)\rvert+\frac{\delta}{2}\,.\end{split}

This completes the induction and therefore the proof of the lemma.
∎

A more readily verifiable, though not equivalent, condition for (
8
) can be given using the order of an entire function in one variable. We need the following definition.

Definition 4.3

(cf.
[
Ron89
, Sections 1.1 and 1.2]
)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be entire and nonconstant. The
maximum modulus
of
f
at radii
r_{1},r_{2}>0
is defined by

M(r_{1},r_{2};f):=\max_{\lvert z\rvert\leq r_{1},\lvert w\rvert\leq r_{2}}\lvert f(z,w)\rvert.

The
order of
f
is defined as

\rho(f):=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log M(r,r;f)}{\log r}\geq 0.

The
order of
f
in the
z
variable
is defined as

\rho_{1}(f):=\limsup_{r_{1}\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}\log M(r_{1},r_{2};f)}{\log r}\geq 0

for some
r_{2}>0
.

Remark 4.4
.

As discussed in
[
Ron89
, Section 1.2]
, the definition of
\rho_{1}(f)
is independent of the choice of
r_{2}>0
.

The following corollary gives a more practical criterion in terms of the growth of the perturbation.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a skew-product as in (
7
).
The wandering domain
U_{0}
of
f
at
z_{0}
bulges to a Fatou component of
F
if

(1)

0
is a geometrically attracting fixed point of
g
and
\rho_{1}(h)<1
, or

(2)

0
is a superattracting fixed point of
g
and
\rho_{1}(h)<\infty
.

Proof.

This corollary can be proven by itself using the same approach as
\Autoref
THEOREM:BULGINGORDER, but it also suffices to verify that the hypotheses of
\Autoref
THEOREM:BULGINGORDER hold.
As in the previous proof, we may assume that
z_{0}=0
. Let
\delta>0
.

Before looking at the two cases separately, note that
k\lvert T\rvert+\delta<C(k+1)
with
C:=\lvert T\rvert+\delta
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, we have

(12)

(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta)^{\beta}<C^{\beta}(k+1)^{\beta}

for every
\beta>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

We start with the case
\rho_{1}(h)<1
.
Since
0
is an attracting fixed point of
g
, there exist
0<\alpha<1
and
\delta_{g}>0
such that
\lvert g(w)\rvert\leq\alpha\lvert w\rvert
for all
w\in\overline{D(0,\delta_{g})}
.
Observe, that this already implies that
w_{n}=g^{n}(w)
converges to
0
uniformly on
D(0,\delta_{g})
as
n\to\infty
.
In particular, choosing
\rho_{k}:=\alpha^{k}\delta_{g}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain
\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho_{k}=0
and
g(\overline{D(0,\rho_{k})})\subset\overline{D(0,\rho_{k+1})}
.

Since
\rho_{1}(h)<1
, we can choose
\tau>0
such that
\beta:=\rho_{1}(h)+\tau<1
.
By definition, there exists
L>0
such that for all
\sigma\geq L
we have
\log^{+}\log M(\sigma,\delta_{g};h)\leq\beta\log\sigma
and so,
M(\sigma,\delta_{g};h)\leq e^{\sigma^{\beta}}
.
Since
0<\beta<1
and
\log\alpha<0
, there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that for all
k\geq K
, we have
\log\alpha+C^{\beta}\frac{(k+1)^{\beta}}{k}<0

which implies that

q:=e^{\log\alpha+C^{\beta}\frac{(K+1)^{\beta}}{K}}<1.

Up to further increasing
K
, we may assume that
k\lvert T\rvert+\delta\geq L
for all
k\geq K
.
Together with (
12
), this gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\alpha^{k}e^{(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}e^{k\log\alpha+(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}e^{k\log\alpha+C^{\beta}(k+1)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}e^{k\left(\log\alpha+C^{\beta}\frac{(k+1)^{\beta}}{k}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}q^{k}<\infty.

Hence we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{k}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert w\rvert\leq\rho_{k},\\
\lvert z-kT\rvert\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\lvert h(z,w)\rvert&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{g}\alpha^{k}M(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta,\delta_{g};h)\\
&\leq\delta_{g}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha^{k}M(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta,\delta_{g};h)+\delta_{g}\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\alpha^{k}e^{(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta)^{\beta}}<\infty\end{split}

completing the proof when
\rho_{1}(h)<1
.

We now turn to the second case, that is, we assume that
0
is super-attracting for
g
and
\rho_{1}(h)<\infty
.
Hence, we have
g^{\prime}(0)=\dots=g^{(d-1)}(0)=0
and
g^{(d)}(0)\neq 0
for some
d\geq 2
.
As a result, there exists
\widetilde{g}
entire such that
g(w)=w^{d}\widetilde{g}(w)
for all
w\in\mathbb{C}
.
For
\delta_{g}>0
, define

C_{g}:=\max_{\lvert w\rvert\leq\delta_{g}}\lvert\widetilde{g}(w)\rvert>0.

Up to shrinking
\delta_{g}
, we may assume that
\delta_{g}^{d}C_{g}\leq\delta_{g}
and
\lvert g(w)\rvert\leq\lvert w\rvert
for all
w\in\overline{D(0,\delta_{g})}
.
This ensures that for all
w\in D(0,\delta_{g})
, we have

(13)

\lvert w_{k}\rvert=\lvert g^{k}(w)\rvert\leq C_{g}^{1+d+\dots+d^{k-1}}\lvert w\rvert^{d^{k}}=C_{g}^{\frac{1-d^{k}}{1-d}}\lvert w\rvert^{d^{k}}.

Set

D:=\frac{1}{1-d}\log C_{g}.

As before, but this time for
\tau=1
and
\beta:=\rho_{1}(h)+1
, there exists
L>0
such that for all
\sigma\geq L
, we have
\log^{+}\log M(\sigma,\delta_{g};h)\leq\beta\log\sigma
, and so
M(\sigma,\delta_{g};h)\leq e^{\sigma^{\beta}}
. Given any
0<q<1
, we can choose
K\in\mathbb{N}
large enough and
t>0
small enough such that for all
k\geq K
, we have
\log t-D+C^{\beta}\frac{(k+1)^{\beta}}{d^{k}}<\log q<0
and hence,

e^{\log t-D+C^{\beta}\frac{(k+1)^{\beta}}{d^{k}}}<q<1

for all
k\geq K
.
Up to further increasing
K
, we may assume that
k\lvert T\rvert+\delta\geq L
for all
k\geq K
.
Using (
12
), we obtain

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}e^{d^{k}(\log t-D)}e^{(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}e^{d^{k}(\log t-D)+(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}e^{d^{k}(\log t-D)+C^{\beta}(k+1)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}e^{d^{k}\left(\log t-D+C^{\beta}\frac{(k+1)^{\beta}}{d^{k}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}q^{d^{k}}<\infty.

Up to shrinking
t
, we may assume that
t<\delta_{g}
. Setting
\rho_{0}:=t
and
\rho_{k}:=C_{g}\rho_{k-1}^{d}
, we have
g(\overline{D(0,\rho_{k})})\subset\overline{D(0,\rho_{k+1})}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By induction, it further follows that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\rho_{k}=C_{g}^{\frac{1-d^{k}}{1-d}}t^{d^{k}}=e^{d^{k}(\log t-D)+D}.

In particular, we obtain
\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho_{k}
= 0.
Using the explicit formula for
\rho_{k}
together with (
12
) and (
14
), and arguing similarly to the first case, we obtain that

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{k}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert w\rvert\leq\rho_{k},\\
\lvert z-kT\rvert\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\lvert h(z,w)\rvert&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{k}M(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta,\delta_{g};h)\\
&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\rho_{k}M(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta,\delta_{g};h)+\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}C_{g}^{\frac{1-d^{k}}{1-d}}t^{d^{k}}e^{(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta)^{\beta}}\\
&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\rho_{k}M(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta,\delta_{g};h)+C_{g}^{\frac{1}{1-d}}\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}e^{d^{k}(\log t-D)}e^{(k\lvert T\rvert+\delta)^{\beta}}<\infty,\end{split}

completing the proof for the second case.
∎
