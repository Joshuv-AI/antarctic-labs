Mini-Batch Stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann Algorithm for Nonexpansive Fixed Point Problems
Hideaki Iiduka

Abstract
The Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm is a well-known method for finding fixed points of a nonexpansive mapping with strong theoretical guarantees. However, there are practical large-scale problems to which this algorithm cannot be applied. Here, to resolve the issue caused by the computational difficulty of the mapping, we define a computable mini-batch stochastic mapping, which is a unbiased estimator of the nonexpansive mapping, and implement it in the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm. We show that the algorithm with increasing batch sizes converges almost surely to a fixed point of the nonexpansive mapping. We also perform a convergence rate analysis on the algorithm.

Introduction

1.1
Nonexpansive fixed point problem

Fixed point problems
goebel1
;
goebel2
;
b-c
;
takahashi
constitute a fundamental class of mathematical problems with wide-ranging applications. In particular, the
nonexpansive fixed point problem
is generally one of finding the fixed points of a nonexpansive mapping, and it includes significant problems, such as convex feasibility problems, monotone variational inequality problems, and convex minimization problems.

Many iterative algorithms for solving nonexpansive fixed point problems have been developed (see, e.g.,
berinde
for fixed point approximation algorithms); one especially useful method is the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm
kra
;
mann
. Here, let
T\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be nonexpansive (see Assumption
2.1
(A1) for the definition of nonexpansivity). When the current point is the
k
-th approximate point
\bm{x}_{k}
, the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm updates its iterates in the direction:
\bm{d}_{k}=T(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k}
with a step size
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\bm{d}_{k}=\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}(T(\bm{x}_{k})-\bm{x}_{k})=(1-\alpha_{k})\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}T(\bm{x}_{k}).

(1)

In theory, convergence of the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm (
1
) to a fixed point of
T
is guaranteed if
\alpha_{k}
satisfies
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})=+\infty

(
b-c
, Theorem 5.15)
,
(
GROETSCH1972369
, Corollaries 1-3)
. Moreover, the algorithm satisfies that
\|\bm{x}_{K}-T(\bm{x}_{K})\|=O(1/\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})})

(
Cominetti:2014aa
, Theorem 1)
. Hence, by using a constant step size
\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1)
, the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm converges with an
O(1/\sqrt{K})
convergence rate more quickly than when using a diminishing step size (see also Section
3.2.1
).

Meanwhile, in practice, the computation of the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm (
1
) strongly depends on the computability of
T
. For example, let us consider a minimization problem that is to find a global minimizer of the sum of many convex functions, denoted by
f\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
. Algorithm (
1
) with

\displaystyle T(\bm{x})\coloneqq\bm{x}-\beta\nabla f(\bm{x})=\bm{x}-\frac{\beta}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\underbrace{\left(\bm{x}-\beta\nabla f_{i}(\bm{x})\right)}_{T_{i}(\bm{x})}

(2)

converges to a global minimizer of
f
under sufficient conditions of
\beta
for the nonexpansivity of
T
(see Example
1
(iii)). However, since practical convex minimization problems have large
n
and
d
, the computation of
T
is expensive. Accordingly, it is not necessarily true that the Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm (
1
) can be applied to practical problems.

1.2
Stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithms

Here, we suppose that
T
consists of a finite number of
T_{1},\cdots,T_{n}
(see also
T
in the above subsection). When
T
cannot be used at each iteration, the following stochastic nonexpansive mapping can be defined for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle T_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\begin{cases}T(\bm{x})+\bm{\xi}&\text{ (noisy observation)}\\
\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{b}T_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{x})&\text{ (mini-batch estimation)},\end{cases}

where, in the “noisy observation” case, the noise
\bm{\xi}
of
T(\bm{x})
is a random variable such that the expectation of
\bm{\xi}
is
0
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}[\bm{\xi}]=\bm{0}
, in the “mini-batch estimation” case,
b

(\leq n)
is the batch size (the number of samples),
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{b})^{\top}
comprises
b
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) variables, and
T_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{x})
is a unbiased estimator of
T
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}[T_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{x})]=T(\bm{x})
. Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})\right]=\begin{cases}T(\bm{x})+\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}}[\bm{\xi}]=T(\bm{x})&\text{(noisy observation)}\\
\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{i}}\left[\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{b}T_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{x})\right]=T(\bm{x})&\text{(mini-batch estimation)},\end{cases}

we expect that upon replacing
T
with
T_{\bm{\xi}}
, algorithm (
1
) will converge to a fixed point of
T
. In fact,
(
doi:10.1137/22M1515550
, Theorem 2.5)
and
(
doi:10.1137/140971233
, Corollary 2.7)
showed that the stochastic Krasnoselskiĭ-Mann algorithm in the noisy observation case defined for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
by

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=(1-\alpha_{k})\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})=(1-\alpha_{k})\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}(T(\bm{x}_{k})+\bm{\xi}_{k})

converges almost surely to a fixed point of
T
under certain conditions.

Mini-batch estimation is often used in practice. For example, let us consider empirical risk minimization in machine learning. Let
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a parameter of a deep neural network,
S=\{(\bm{d}_{1},\bm{y}_{1}),\ldots,(\bm{d}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})\}
be the training set, where each data point
\bm{d}_{i}
is associated with a label
\bm{y}_{i}
, and let
f_{i}(\cdot)\coloneqq f(\cdot;(\bm{d}_{i},\bm{y}_{i}))\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be the loss function corresponding to the
i
-th labeled training data
(\bm{d}_{i},\bm{y}_{i})
. Empirical risk minimization minimizes the empirical risk defined for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
f(\bm{x})\coloneqq(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\bm{x})
. In general, the number
n
of training data and the number of dimensions
d
of the deep neural network are very large, and we cannot use
\nabla f(\bm{x})=(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\bm{x})
directly to minimize
f
(see also (
2
)). Here, instead of
\nabla f(\bm{x})
, we can use the mini-batch stochastic gradient of
f
defined by

\displaystyle\nabla f_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{b}\nabla f_{\xi_{i}}(\bm{x}).

Many optimizers using the mini-batch stochastic gradient have been presented to minimize
f
. For example, mini-batch stochastic gradient descent (SGD) defined by

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=\bm{x}_{k}-\eta\nabla f_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})=\bm{x}_{k}-\frac{\eta}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{b}\nabla f_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k})=\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{b}\underbrace{\left(\bm{x}_{k}-\eta\nabla f_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k})\right)}_{T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k})}

can minimize
f
under certain conditions
(
garrigos2024handbookconvergencetheoremsstochastic
, Section 5)
, where
\eta>0
and
\xi_{k,i}
is a random variable generated by the
i
-th sampling in the
k
-th iteration. The motivation behind this work is thus to show that the stochastic Krasnoselskiĭ-Mann algorithm converges in the mini-batch estimation case.

1.3
Mini-batch stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm

This paper considers algorithm (
1
) in the setting that replaces
T
with the mini-batch stochastic mapping
T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\coloneqq(1/b_{k})\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}T_{\xi_{k,i}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=(1-\alpha_{k})\bm{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})=(1-\alpha_{k})\bm{x}_{k}+\frac{\alpha_{k}}{b_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{b_{k}}T_{\xi_{k,i}}(\bm{x}_{k}),

(3)

where

is the batch size at the
k
-th iteration. We show that, under certain conditions (see Assumption
2.1
and its Example

), algorithm (

) with
(\alpha_{k})
and

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})=+\infty\text{ and }\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}<+\infty

converges almost surely to a fixed point of

(Theorem

). The condition on
b_{k}
in (

) implies that increasing batch sizes are essential for guaranteeing almost-sure convergence of the algorithm (
3
). In sampling with replacement, even if the batch size
b_{k}
exceeds
n
,
T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\neq T
holds in general. Hence, to examine the convergence of mini-batch algorithms under sampling with replacement, we can use
b_{k}\to+\infty

(k\to+\infty)
. Intuitively, when the batch size (the number of samples)

is large enough,

is approximately

. Hence, for large enough

, algorithm (
3
) is approximately algorithm (
1
). As a result, the convergence of algorithm (
1
) implies the convergence of the algorithm (
3
). Below, we theoretically prove almost-sure convergence of algorithm (
3
) through Proposition
1
and Lemmas
1
and
2
(Subsection
3.1
).

We also show that algorithm (
3
) with (
4
) has the following convergence rate (Theorem
3.2
):

\displaystyle\min_{t\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|\right]

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})}}\right),

which, together with
\alpha_{k}=\alpha\in(0,1)
, implies that
\min_{t\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|]=O(1/\sqrt{K})
, where
[0:K-1]\coloneqq\{0,1,\cdots,K-1\}
for
K\in\mathbb{N}
. Condition (
4
) implies that algorithm (
3
) with a constant batch size

would not be applicable to nonexpansive fixed point problems. To emphasize our claim, we reconsider the empirical risk minimization in the above subsection. Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 in
umeda2025increasing
show that mini-batch SGD with a constant step size
\alpha_{k}=\alpha
and a constant batch size

satisfies

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f(\bm{x}_{k})\|\right]=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{K}+\frac{1}{b}}\right)\to\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\right)\neq 0\quad(K\to+\infty),

while mini-batch SGD with a constant step size
\alpha_{k}=\alpha
and an increasing batch size

(e.g.,

doubly increases every epoch) satisfies

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f(\bm{x}_{k})\|\right]=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\right)\to 0\quad(K\to+\infty),

where the notation

is used in distinction from the Landau symbol

and
C=\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{b})
implies there exists
M>0
such that

Nevertheless, when using an increasing batch size

, there exists

such that, for all

. This implies that, for all

would be difficult in practice. However, the previous numerical results in

showed that increasing the batch size a finite number of times (e.g., for training ResNet-34 on ImageNet with
n=1,281,167

b_{0}=2^{5},b_{1}=2^{6},b_{2}=2^{7}
,

, and

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\leq\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|+\frac{2\sigma}{\sqrt{b_{k}}},

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}<+\infty\Rightarrow\exists\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|\text{ a.s.}.

Proof

The triangle inequality implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|(1-\alpha_{k})(\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1}))+\alpha_{k}(T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T(\bm{x}_{k+1}))\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+\alpha_{k}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}_{\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}]}}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T(\bm{x}_{k})-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}_{X_{k}},

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\right)^{2}&\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|^{2}\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\end{split}

(19)

comes from Jensen’s inequality and Proposition
1
(i). From the nonexpansivity (
15
) of
T
and the definition of
\bm{x}_{k+1}
(Step 4 of Algorithm
1
),

\displaystyle X_{k}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k+1}\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]=\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right].

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}_{Y_{k}}+\alpha_{k}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k+1}\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right].

Moreover, the triangle inequality implies that

\displaystyle Y_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k+1}\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right].

Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k+1}\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}+\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{k+1}\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+\alpha_{k}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right],

which implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}_{Z_{k}}.

The triangle inequality and (
19
) thus ensure that

\displaystyle Z_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|T(\bm{x}_{k})-T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle=\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|+\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}.

Therefore, from Proposition
1
(ii),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-T(\bm{x}_{k+1})\|\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|+2\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}\left[T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k})\big|\bm{\xi}_{[k-1]}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|+\frac{2\sigma}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}.

The super martingale convergence theorem
(
bert
, Proposition 8.2.10)
with
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}1/\sqrt{b_{k}}<+\infty
ensures the existence of the limit of
\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|
with probability
1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemmas

and

lead to the following.

Theorem 3.1

Under Assumption
2.1
, the sequence
(\bm{x}_{k})
generated by Algorithm
1
with
(\alpha_{k})
and
(b_{k})
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})=+\infty\text{ and }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{k}}}<+\infty

(20)

converges almost surely to a fixed point of
T
.

Proof

Let
\bm{x}^{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
. From
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}/b_{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}1/\sqrt{b_{k}}<+\infty
, Lemma
1
(the almost-sure existence of
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|
) provides the almost-sure boundedness of
(\bm{x}_{k})
. Hence, there exists a subsequence
(\bm{x}_{k_{i}})
of
(\bm{x}_{k})
such that
(\bm{x}_{k_{i}})
converges almost surely. Meanwhile, Lemmas
1
and
2
imply that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|=0\text{ a.s.}.

Accordingly, by letting
\bar{\bm{x}}
be a convergent random variable of
(\bm{x}_{k_{i}})
, the continuity of
T
ensures that
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
a.s.. In addition, Lemma
1
leads to the almost-sure existence of
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bar{\bm{x}}\|
. We also have another subsequence
(\bm{x}_{k_{j}})
of
(\bm{x}_{k})
such that
(\bm{x}_{k_{j}})
converges almost surely to a random variable
\hat{\bm{x}}
. A similar argument to the one above for obtaining
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
and the almost-sure existence of
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bar{\bm{x}}\|
implies that
\hat{\bm{x}}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
and the almost-sure existence of
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bar{\bm{x}}\|=\lim_{i\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k_{i}}-\bar{\bm{x}}\|=0\text{ a.s.},

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\|\bm{x}_{k_{j}}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|=0\text{ a.s.}.

Since the triangle inequality ensures that
\|\bar{\bm{x}}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|\leq\|\bar{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}_{k}\|+\|\bm{x}_{k}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|
, we have that
\bar{\bm{x}}=\hat{\bm{x}}
a.s.. That is,
(\bm{x}_{k})
converges almost surely to
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
, which completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\text{[Diminishing step size] }\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{(k+1)^{a}}\in(0,1],

(23)

where

, which satisfies

, from

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\geq\begin{cases}\frac{(K+1)^{1-a}-1}{1-a}-\frac{K^{1-2a}}{1-2a}&\text{ }\left(a\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\\

\frac{(K+1)^{1-a}-1}{1-a}-\frac{2a}{2a-1}&\text{ }\left(a\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)\right)\\

3.2
Convergence rate

Lemma
1
is used here to prove the following theorem on the rate of convergence of Algorithm
1
.

Theorem 3.2

Under Assumption
2.1
and (
20
), the sequence
(\bm{x}_{k})
generated by Algorithm
1
satisfies that, for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|\right]

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})}}\sqrt{\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{b_{k}}}.

Proof

Let
\bm{x}^{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}(T)
. Lemma
1
ensures that, for all
k\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{\sigma^{2}\alpha_{k}}{b_{k}}-\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|^{2}\right].

Let
K\in\mathbb{N}
. Summing the above inequality from
k=0
to
k=K-1
leads to the finding that

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|^{2}\right]\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]\right\}+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{b_{k}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{K}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}\right]+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{b_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{b_{k}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[0:K-1]}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})}\left(\|\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{x}^{\star}\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{b_{k}}\right).

The assertion of Theorem
3.2
follows from Jensen’s inequality, which ensures that
(\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|])^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{x}_{k}-T(\bm{x}_{k})\|^{2}]
.

4
Conclusion and Future Work

This paper considered a stochastic fixed point problem for nonexpansive mappings and presented a convergence analysis of the mini-batch stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm for solving it. The analysis showed that the algorithm using an increasing batch size converges almost surely to a fixed point of the expectation of stochastic nonexpansive mappings. This paper also presented a convergence rate analysis demonstrating that the use of a constant step size leads to faster convergence of the algorithm compared with the use of diminishing step sizes.

The Halpern algorithm is a useful fixed point algorithm for nonexpansive mappings. Hence, in the future, we should verify whether this algorithm, defined by

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=\alpha_{k}\bm{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})T_{\bm{\xi}_{k}}(\bm{x}_{k}),

can be applied to the stochastic fixed point problem. While this paper assumed the boundedness of the variance of a stochastic mapping (Assumption
2.1
(A2)), the previously reported results in
pmlr-v97-simsekli19a
;
pmlr-v139-garg21b
;
pmlr-v238-battash24a
;
ahn2024linear
showed that stochastic noise in practical machine learning may exhibit heavy-tailed behavior, violating the bounded-variance assumption. Hence, we should also verify whether the mini-batch stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann algorithm converges under heavy-tailed noise.
