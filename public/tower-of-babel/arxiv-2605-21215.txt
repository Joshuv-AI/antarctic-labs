Revisiting 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} and 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} through Interval Structures Thanks: The first would like to thank the grant No. PCT160, Dirección de Tecnología e Investigación and Oficina de Internacionalización, Institución Universitaria Pascual Bravo
Miguel A. Cardona, Adam Marton

Abstract
. We investigate a family of relational systems arising from interval partitions of ω \omega , inspired by Vojtáš’s characterization of the bounding and dominating numbers. By varying the underlying asymptotic quantifiers and interval constraints, we obtain several natural interval-type generalizations. We show that the universal variants are remarkably robust: in all the discrete, colored, restricted, bounded, and measure-theoretic settings considered here, the associated bounding and dominating numbers coincide with the classical invariants 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} and 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} . In contrast, the existential variants systematically reverse these invariants, yielding bounding number 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} and dominating number 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} .

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Cardinal characteristics of the continuum measure the complexity of combinatorial and structural properties of the real line and related spaces. Classical examples include the bounding and dominating numbers,
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}
, which play a central role in Cichoń’s diagram and in the interaction between set theory, topology, and measure and category.

In his seminal work
[
Voj98
]
, Vojtáš provided a precise characterization of the classical bounding and dominating numbers in terms of partitions of
\omega
into finite intervals induced by strictly increasing functions. More concretely, if
\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
denotes the set of strictly increasing functions from
\omega
to
\omega
, then the bounding and dominating numbers admit the following equivalent formulations:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}

\displaystyle=\min\bigl\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subseteq\omega^{\uparrow\omega},\;\forall X\in[\omega]^{\omega}\;\exists f\in\mathcal{F}\ \neg(\forall^{\infty}n\;\bigl|\,[\,f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq 1)\bigr\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}

\displaystyle=\min\bigl\{|\mathcal{S}|:\mathcal{S}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega},\;\forall f\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}\;\exists X\in\mathcal{S}\ \forall^{\infty}n\;\bigl|\,[\,f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq 1\bigr\}.

This viewpoint not only clarifies the combinatorial nature of these invariants but also opens the door to systematic generalizations by modifying the underlying relations.

The framework of relational systems has since proven to be a powerful and flexible tool for studying cardinal characteristics (see e.g.,
[
Voj93
,
Voj94
,
Bla10
]
. A
relational system

\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,\sqsubset\rangle
consists of two non-empty sets
X
and
Y
, together with a relation
\sqsubset
.

(1)

A set
F\subseteq X
is
\mathbf{R}
-bounded
if
\exists\,y\in Y\ \forall\,x\in F\ x\sqsubset y
.

(2)

A set
E\subseteq Y
is
\mathbf{R}
-dominating
if
\forall\,x\in X\ \exists\,y\in E\ x\sqsubset y
.

We associate two cardinal characteristics with the relational system
\mathbf{R}
:

\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}):=\min\{|F|\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muF\subseteq X\text{ is }\mathbf{R}\text{-unbounded}\}
the
bounding number of
\mathbf{R}
, and

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}):=\min\{|D|\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muD\subseteq Y\text{ is }\mathbf{R}\text{-dominating}\}
the
dominating number of
\mathbf{R}
.

The interval characterization introduced by Vojtáš fits naturally into the general framework of relational systems described above. From this perspective, the bounding and dominating numbers arise from a simple asymptotic relation measuring how infinite subsets of
\omega
distribute inside the interval partitions generated by strictly increasing functions. More importantly, this viewpoint suggests that the specific relation considered by Vojtáš is not isolated, but rather part of a much broader interval paradigm.

The main objective of this paper is to show that this phenomenon is remarkably robust: after varying the underlying asymptotic condition in several natural ways, combinatorial, geometric, colored, bounded, and measure-theoretic, the resulting relational systems continue to recover the classical invariants
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}
, often exhibiting unexpected duality phenomena between their universal and existential forms.

We introduce a hierarchy of interval relations parameterized by an integer
k\geq 1
. For
\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\}
, let
f\sqsubset_{\iota}^{k}X
express that the intervals
[f(n),f(n+1))
contain at most
k
points of
X
either eventually (
\iota=\forall
) or infinitely often (
\iota=\exists
). This yields the relational systems
\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{k}=\langle\omega^{\uparrow\omega},[\omega]^{\omega},\sqsubset_{\iota}^{k}\rangle.
Our first result reveals a sharp dichotomy between the universal and existential regimes.

Theorem A

(
2.4
,
2.8
–
2.10
)
.

For every
k\geq 1
,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{k})=\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{k})=\mathfrak{d}
, whereas
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{k})=\mathfrak{d}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{k})=\mathfrak{b}.

Thus, merely replacing “eventually” by “infinitely often” completely reverses the associated cardinal invariants. In particular, the interval paradigm naturally encodes both sides of the classical
(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{d})
-duality.

We show that this phenomenon persists under several substantial modifications of the underlying combinatorics. First, instead of counting points of a set inside an interval, we count the number of blocks of an infinite partition intersected by the interval. This leads to the colored systems
\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{\mathsf{col},k}=\langle\omega^{\uparrow\omega},\text{Part}(\omega),\sqsubset_{\iota}^{\mathsf{col},k}\rangle
for
\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\}

where
\text{Part}(\omega)
denotes the family of infinite partitions of
\omega
into finite sets.

Theorem B

(
2.16
–
2.19
)
.

For every
k\geq 2
,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathsf{col},k})=\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathsf{col},k})=\mathfrak{d}
, and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{\mathsf{col},k})=\mathfrak{d}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{\mathsf{col},k})=\mathfrak{b}
.

Next, we impose geometric restrictions on the interval decomposition itself. Let

\mathbf{D}_{k}=\{f\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}:|[f(n),f(n+1))|>k\text{ for all }n\},

and consider

\mathbf{L}_{\iota}^{k}=\langle\mathbf{D}_{k},[\omega]^{*},\sqsubset_{\iota}^{k}\rangle,

where
[\omega]^{*}
is the family of infinite co-infinite subsets of
\omega
.

Theorem C

(
2.25
–
2.29
)
.

For every
k>0
,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{L}_{\forall}^{k})=\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{L}_{\forall}^{k})=\mathfrak{d}
while
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{L}_{\exists}^{k})=\mathfrak{d}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{L}_{\exists}^{k})=\mathfrak{b}
for any
k<\omega
.

The interval method also admits a measure-theoretic counterpart. Replacing finite counting by Lebesgue measure, we define relational systems
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}^{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon>0

by requiring the intersections

[f(n),f(n+1))\cap Y

to have measure at most
\varepsilon
eventually or infinitely often. Despite their analytic appearance, these systems are Tukey equivalent to the original combinatorial relations.

Theorem D

(
2.36
–
2.38
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
k>0
,
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}^{\varepsilon}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{k}
for
\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\}
. Consequently,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{M}_{\forall}^{\varepsilon})=\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{M}_{\forall}^{\varepsilon})=\mathfrak{d}
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{M}_{\exists}^{\varepsilon})=\mathfrak{d}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{M}_{\exists}^{\varepsilon})=\mathfrak{b}
.

We also investigate interval relations with variable asymptotic tolerances.
Instead of requiring a fixed bound
k
, we allow the admissible number of points to increase with the interval index itself. Let

\omega^{>\mathrm{id}}=\{f\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}:f(n+1)-f(n)>n\},

and for
\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\}
define

\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\iota}=\langle\omega^{>\mathrm{id}},[\omega]^{\omega},\sqsubset_{\mathrm{id}}\rangle,

where

f\sqsubset^{\mathrm{id}}_{\iota}X\;\iff\;\iota^{\infty}n\ \bigl|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq n.

At first sight, allowing the bounds to diverge suggests a substantially weaker asymptotic restriction. Surprisingly, the associated cardinal characteristics remain unchanged.

Theorem E

(
2.40
-
2.42
)
.

\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\forall})=\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\forall})=\mathfrak{d}
while
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\exists})=\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\exists})=\mathfrak{d}

Finally, we study bounded interval relations in which the parameter
k
is no longer fixed in advance, but may depend on the witnessing pair itself. Let

\omega^{\omega}_{\uparrow}=\{f\in\omega^{\omega}:f(n+1)-f(n)\to\infty\},

and define

\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{\mathrm{bd}}=\langle\omega^{\omega}_{\uparrow},[\omega]^{\omega},\sqsubset_{\iota}^{\mathrm{bd}}\rangle,\qquad\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\},

where

f\sqsubset_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}}X\;\iff\;\exists k\in\omega\ \forall^{\infty}n\ \bigl|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq k,

and

f\sqsubset_{\exists}^{\mathrm{bd}}X\;\iff\;\exists k\in\omega\ \exists^{\infty}n\ \bigl|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq k.

These bounded variants show that the universal/existential dichotomy persists even when the threshold is allowed to vary dynamically.

Theorem F

(
2.47
–
2.49
)
.

In
\mathsf{ZFC}
,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{\mathrm{bd}})=\mathfrak{d}
,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{\mathrm{bd}})=\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}})=\mathfrak{d}
,
\mathfrak{v}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}})=\mathfrak{b}
.

Together, these results show that the classical pair
(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{d})
is encoded in a remarkably broad spectrum of interval phenomena. The persistence of the same invariants across fixed, variable, colored, geometric, bounded, and measure-theoretic settings suggests that Vojtáš-type interval systems form a robust universality class governing asymptotic sparsity on
\omega
.

Methodologically, the proofs throughout the paper rely heavily on Tukey connections between relational systems.
This approach allows us to dualize cardinal inequalities (for a general overview
on this framework, see, e.g.,
[
Voj93
,
Bla10
]
).

Let
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,{\sqsubset}\rangle
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}=\langle X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},{\sqsubset^{\prime}}\rangle
be two relational systems. We say that

(\Psi_{-},\Psi_{+})\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}^{\prime}

is a
Tukey connection from
\mathbf{R}
into
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
if

\Psi_{-}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muX\to X^{\prime}
and
\Psi_{+}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muY^{\prime}\to Y
are functions such that

\forall x\in X\;\forall y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}\allowbreak\ (\Psi_{-}(x)\sqsubset^{\prime}y^{\prime}\Rightarrow x\sqsubset\Psi_{+}(y^{\prime})).

The
Tukey order
between relational systems is defined by

\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
iff there is a Tukey connection from
\mathbf{R}
into
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
.
Tukey equivalence
is defined by
\mathbf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
iff
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
.

The dual of
\mathbf{R}
is defined by
\mathbf{R}^{\perp}:=\langle Y,X,{\sqsubset^{\perp}}\rangle
where
y\sqsubset^{\perp}x
iff
x\not\sqsubset y
. Note that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\perp})=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\perp})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
.

Example 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{D}=\langle\omega^{\uparrow\omega},\omega^{\uparrow\omega},\leq^{*}\rangle
where
x\leq^{*}y
means
\forall^{\infty}n\;x(n)\leq y(n)
. Then
\mathfrak{b}:=\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{D})
and
\mathfrak{d}:=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{D})
.

2.
ZFC-results

In this section, we establish all the main results stated in
Section 1
. Throughout, we analyze the interval relational systems introduced above and compute their associated bounding and dominating numbers within
\mathsf{ZFC}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
.

(a)

For
f\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
and
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
, let

f\;\sqsubset_{\forall}^{k}\;X\;\iff\;\forall^{\infty}n\;\bigl|\,[\,f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq k,

f\;\sqsubset_{\exists}^{k}\;X\;\iff\;\exists^{\infty}n\;\bigl|\,[\,f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq k.

(b)

Define the relational system
\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{k}=\langle\omega^{\uparrow\omega},[\omega]^{\omega},\sqsubset_{\iota}^{k}\rangle
for
\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\}
.

We begin by distinguishing the case
k=0
for
\sqsubset_{\forall}^{k}
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is easy to see that any infinite set
X
has a nonempty intersection with infinitely many intervals of any interval partition, therefore,
f\not\sqsubset^{0}_{\forall}X
for every
f\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
,
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
and consequently
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{0})=1
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{0})
is not well-defined or
\infty
.

We now record the basic monotonicity properties of these relational systems.

Fact 2.3
.

Let
f\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
and
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
.

(1)

If
k<\ell
then
\mathbf{R}^{\ell}_{\iota}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{k}_{\iota}
for
\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\}
.

(2)

For each
k\in\omega
, we have
\mathbf{R}^{k}_{\exists}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{k}_{\forall}
.

As recalled in
Section 1
, Vojtáš
[
Voj98
]
showed that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{1})=\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{1})=\mathfrak{d}
, so by
2.3
we have
\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{k})
and
\mathfrak{d}\geq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{k})
. Therefore, in order to establish the converse, it suffices to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
k\geq 2
, we have
\mathbf{D}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{k}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{b}\geq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{k})
and
\mathfrak{d}\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{k})
.

Proof.

Let
\Psi_{-}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\omega^{\uparrow\omega}\to\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
be defined as the identity map. To define
\Psi_{+}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu[\omega]^{\omega}\to\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
, first define
h_{X}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\omega\to\omega
for every
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
as follows. Let
X=\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\}
be the increasing enumeration of
X
, i.e.,
x_{0}<x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots
.

Define
h_{X}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\omega\to\omega
by:

h_{X}(n)=x_{(k+1)n}.

Put
\Psi_{+}(X)=h_{X}
. Now, let
f\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
,
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
, and suppose that
f\sqsubset_{\forall}^{k}X
. This means there exists
N\in\omega
such that for all
n\geq N
,

\left|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\right|\leq k.

For each
n\in\omega
, let

a_{n}=|\{i\in\omega\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\,x_{i}<f(n)\}|.

By the definition of
\langle a_{n}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\,n\in\omega\rangle
, for every
n\geq 1
we have

x_{a_{n}-1}<f(n)\leq x_{a_{n}}.

For
n\geq N
,
f\sqsubset_{\forall}^{k}X
implies that the number of elements of
X
in
{[f(n),f(n+1))}
is at most
k
. Thus,

a_{n+1}\leq a_{n}+k.

Solving this recurrence for
n\geq N
,

a_{n}\leq a_{N}+k(n-N)\leq c+kn,

where
c=a_{N}-kN
is a constant. Therefore, for all
n\geq N
,

a_{n}\leq c+kn.

Since
f(n)\leq x_{a_{n}}
and the sequence
\langle x_{i}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\,i\in\omega\rangle
is increasing, for
n\geq N
we have

f(n)\leq x_{a_{n}}\leq x_{c+kn}.

Now, consider
h_{X}(n)=x_{(k+1)n}
. For sufficiently large
n
(specifically, when
n>c
),

(k+1)n>c+kn,

so

x_{(k+1)n}>x_{c+kn}.

Thus, for
n>\max\{c,N\}
,

f(n)\leq x_{c+kn}<x_{(k+1)n}=h_{X}(n),

which implies
f(n)\leq h_{X}(n)
.

Therefore,
f(n)\leq h_{X}(n)
for all but finitely many
n
, i.e.,
f\leq^{*}h_{X}
.
∎

The existential variant behaves dually. More precisely, the next two lemmas identify the cardinal characteristics associated with
\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{1}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{1}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}^{\perp}
. In particuar,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{1})\leq\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{1})\geq\mathfrak{d}
.

Proof.

Define the maps:

\Phi_{-}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\omega^{\uparrow\omega}\to\omega^{\uparrow\omega},\quad\Phi_{-}(f)=f,

\Phi_{+}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\omega^{\uparrow\omega}\to[\omega]^{\omega},\quad\Phi_{+}(b)=b[\omega]:=\{b(n):n\in\omega\}.

We verify the Tukey condition: for any
f,b\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
,

\Phi_{-}(f)(\leq^{*})^{\perp}b\;\Rightarrow\;f\sqsubset_{\exists}^{1}\Phi_{+}(b).

Assume
b\not\leq^{*}\Phi_{-}(f)
, i.e.,
b\not\leq^{*}f
. This means
\exists^{\infty}n\;\,b(n)>f(n)
. We prove that
f\sqsubset_{\exists}^{1}b[\omega]
. Suppose, for contradiction, that
\neg(f\sqsubset_{\exists}^{1}b[\omega])
, i.e.,
\exists N\,\forall n\geq N\,|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap b[\omega]|\geq 2
. For each
n\geq N
, let

s(n)=\min\{j:b(j)\in[f(n),f(n+1))\}.

Because each interval
[f(n),f(n+1))
contains at least two points of
b[\omega]
, we have
s(n+1)\geq s(n)+2
, so
s(n)\geq s(N)+2(n-N)
. Consequently, there exists
n_{0}
such that
s(n)>n
for all
n>n_{0}
. For such
n
, since
n<s(n)
, the minimality of
s(n)
implies
b(j)<f(n)
for all
j<s(n)
; in particular,
b(n)<f(n)
. This yields
b(n)<f(n)
for all sufficiently large
n
, i.e.,
b\leq^{*}f
, which contradicts the assumption
b\not\leq^{*}f
. Hence,
f\sqsubset_{\exists}^{1}b[\omega]
.
Thus,
\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{1}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}^{\perp}
.
∎

To obtain the reverse inequalities, we relate
\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{1}
to the interval inclusion relation introduced by Blass
[
Bla10
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Define the following relation on
\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
:

f\sqsubseteq g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall^{\infty}n\in\omega\;\exists m\in\omega\;[f(m),f(m+1))\subseteq[g(n),g(n+1)).

Let
\mathds{I}=\langle\omega^{\uparrow\omega},\omega^{\uparrow\omega},\sqsubseteq\rangle
. Blass
[
Bla10
]
proved that
\mathfrak{b}(\mathds{I})=\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathds{I})=\mathfrak{d}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For every
k\geq 1
,
\mathds{I}^{\perp}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{k}
.
Consequently,
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{k})\leq\mathfrak{d}
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{k})\geq\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

Define maps

\Psi_{-}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\omega^{\uparrow\omega}\to\omega^{\uparrow\omega}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{+}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu[\omega]^{\omega}\to\omega^{\uparrow\omega}

such that for every
g\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
and
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
:

\Psi_{-}(g)\sqsubset_{\exists}^{k}X\;\Rightarrow\;g\sqsubseteq^{\perp}\Psi_{+}(X).

By the definition of the dual relation
\sqsubseteq^{\perp}
, the condition
g\sqsubseteq^{\perp}\Psi_{+}(X)
is equivalent to
\Psi_{+}(X)\not\sqsubseteq g
. Thus, we need to satisfy the contrapositive:

\Psi_{+}(X)\sqsubseteq g\;\Rightarrow\;\neg(\Psi_{-}(g)\sqsubset_{\exists}^{k}X).

Let
\Psi_{-}(g)=g
(the identity function). For
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
, let
e_{X}
be the increasing enumeration of
X
. Define
\Psi_{+}(X)=f_{X}\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
by

f_{X}(n)=e_{X}(n\cdot(k+1)).

Suppose
\Psi_{+}(X)\sqsubseteq g
, which means
f_{X}\sqsubseteq g
. By the definition of
\sqsubseteq
we have

\forall^{\infty}n\;\exists m\;[f_{X}(m),f_{X}(m+1))\subseteq[g(n),g(n+1)).

Consider such an interval
[f_{X}(m),f_{X}(m+1))
. By the definition of
f_{X}
we have

[f_{X}(m),f_{X}(m+1))=[e_{X}(m(k+1)),e_{X}((m+1)(k+1))).

This interval contains the elements
\{e_{X}(m(k+1)),e_{X}(m(k+1)+1),\dots,e_{X}(m(k+1)+k)\}
. There are exactly
k+1
elements of
X
in this interval.

Since
[f_{X}(m),f_{X}(m+1))\subseteq[g(n),g(n+1))
, it follows that

|[g(n),g(n+1))\cap X|\geq|[f_{X}(m),f_{X}(m+1))\cap X|=k+1>k,

thus, we have
|[g(n),g(n+1))\cap X|>k
for all but finitely many
n
.

By the definition of
\sqsubset_{\exists}^{k}
, the condition
g\sqsubset_{\exists}^{k}X
requires infinitely many
n
such that
|[g(n),g(n+1))\cap X|\leq k
. However, we just showed that for almost all
n
, the intersection has size
>k
.
Therefore, we have
\neg(g\sqsubset_{\exists}^{k}X)
, i.e.,
\neg(\Psi_{-}(g)\sqsubset_{\exists}^{k}X)
.
∎

Combining
2.3
(1)
,
2.5
, and

, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.8
.

For any
k\geq 1

\mathbf{M}^{\varepsilon}_{\iota}=\langle\omega^{\uparrow\omega},\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),\vartriangleleft^{\varepsilon}_{\iota}\rangle
for
\iota\in\{\forall.\exists\}
.

We next show that the measure-theoretic systems introduced above are, from the Tukey viewpoint, equivalent to the discrete interval systems studied previously.

Lemma 2.31
.

For any
k>0
, we have
\mathbf{M}^{k}_{\forall}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{k}_{\forall}
.

Proof.

\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}
: Define
\Psi_{-}=\mathrm{id}_{\omega^{\uparrow\omega}}
and
\Psi_{+}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu[\omega]^{\omega}\to\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
by
\Psi_{+}(X)=\bigcup_{j\in X}[j,j+1)
.
Assume
f\sqsubset^{k}_{\forall}X
and let
N\in\omega
be such that for all
n\geq N
,
|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X|\leq k
. Fix
n\geq N
. Then

[f(n),f(n+1))\cap\Psi_{+}(X)=\bigcup_{j\in[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X}[j,j+1).

Since
|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X|\leq k
, we have

\mu([f(n),f(n+1))\cap\Psi_{+}(X))\leq k\cdot 1=k.

\succeq_{\mathrm{T}}
: Define
\Psi_{-}=\mathrm{id}_{\omega^{\uparrow\omega}}
and
\Psi_{+}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})\to[\omega]^{\omega}
inductively as follows: for any
Y\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
put
y_{0}=0
and for
n\geq 1
,

y_{j}:=\min\{m\in\omega\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\,\mu([y_{j-1},m)\cap Y)\geq 2\}.

Then set
\Psi_{+}(Y)=\{y_{j}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\,j\in\omega\}
.

Assume
f\vartriangleleft^{k}_{\forall}Y
and let
N\in\omega
be such that for all
n\geq N
we have
\mu([f(n),f(n+1))\cap Y)\leq k
. Fix
n\geq N
. Assume, for sake of contradiction, that
|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap\Psi_{+}(Y)|\geq k+1
, i.e., there are at least
k+1
points
y_{j_{0}}<\dotso<y_{j_{k}}
of
\Psi_{+}(Y)
inside
[f(n),f(n+1))
. By the definition of
\Psi_{+}
, we have
\mu(Y\cap[y_{j_{0}},y_{j_{1}}))\geq 2
,
\mu(Y\cap[y_{j_{1}},y_{j_{2}}))\geq 2
, etc. Since each of these intervals is a subset of
[f(n),f(n+1))
and the intervals are disjoint, we have
\mu(Y\cap[f(n),f(n+1)))\geq k\cdot 2>k
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proceeding as in
2.31
, we arrive at the following reduction

Lemma 2.32
.

\mathbf{M}^{k}_{\exists}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{k}_{\exists}
.

The next lemma shows that the specific value of the parameter
\varepsilon
is irrelevant up to Tukey equivalence.

Lemma 2.33
.

For any

\varepsilon,\delta>0
,
\mathbf{M}^{\varepsilon}_{\forall}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{M}^{\delta}_{\forall}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
\varepsilon<\delta
.
\succeq_{\mathrm{T}}
is trivial, so we need to prove only the
\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}
direction. Let
B\in\omega
be such that
\varepsilon\cdot B>\delta
. To define
\Psi_{-}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\omega^{\uparrow\omega}\to\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
, set
\Psi_{-}(f)(n)=B\cdot f(n)
for every
n
.
To define
\Psi_{+}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})\to\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, first define a contraction
c\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by
c(x)=\frac{x}{B}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Now put
\Psi_{+}(Y)=c[Y]
.

Assume
B\cdot f\vartriangleleft^{\delta}_{\forall}Y
, i.e., there is
N\in\omega
such that for every
n\geq N

\mu([B\cdot f(n),B\cdot f(n+1))\cap Y)\leq\delta.

Fix
n\geq N
and denote

A_{f,Y}=[B\cdot f(n),B\cdot f(n+1))\cap Y.

Since the function
c
is just a linear contraction, we can use the scaling property of Lebesgue measure (i.e.,
\mu(\alpha X)=|\alpha|\mu(X)
for nonzero
\alpha
):

\mu(c[A_{f,Y}])=\frac{1}{B}\mu(A_{f,Y})\leq\frac{\delta}{B}<\varepsilon.

Notice that by the injectivity of
c
we have

c[[B\cdot f(n),B\cdot f(n+1))\cap Y]=[f(n),f(n+1))\cap c[Y],

so

\mu([f(n),f(n+1))\cap c[Y])\leq\varepsilon
. Since this holds for any
n\geq N
, we get
f\vartriangleleft^{\varepsilon}_{\forall}c[Y]=\Psi_{+}(Y)
.
∎

The following reduction follows by the same argument as in
2.33
.

Lemma 2.34
.

For any

\varepsilon,\delta>0
,

\mathbf{M}^{\varepsilon}_{\exists}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{M}^{\delta}_{\exists}
.

Corollary 2.35

For any

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{M}^{\varepsilon}_{\forall})=\mathfrak{d}
.

Combining the
\exists

Corollary 2.37

For any

and
k>0
we have

\mathbf{M}^{\varepsilon}_{\exists}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{k}_{\exists}
.

Corollary 2.38
.

\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{M}^{\varepsilon}_{\exists})=\mathfrak{b}

and

As an immediate consequence of the previous corollaries,

follows.

Finally, we consider a variant in which the admissible number of intersections increases with the interval index.

Definition 2.39
.

Let
\omega^{>\mathrm{id}}=\{f\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\,f(n+1)-f(n)>n\}
.

(a)

For any
f\in\omega^{>\mathrm{id}}
and
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
let

f\sqsubset^{\mathrm{id}}_{\forall}X\;\iff\;\forall^{\infty}n\;\bigl|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq n,

f\sqsubset^{\mathrm{id}}_{\exists}X\;\iff\;\exists^{\infty}n\;\bigl|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq n.

(b)

Define the relational system
\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\iota}=\langle\omega^{>\mathrm{id}},[\omega]^{\omega},\sqsubset^{\mathrm{id}}_{\iota}\rangle
for
\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\}
.

The next lemma relates this variable-threshold relation to the systems previously considered and to the classical dominating relation.

Lemma 2.40
.

\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\forall}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{k}_{\forall}
and
\mathbf{D}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\forall}
.

Proof.

The first Tukey connection is immediate, since both maps are identities. Now define
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{id}}=\langle\omega^{>\mathrm{id}},\omega^{>\mathrm{id}},\leq^{*}\rangle
. It is easy to see that
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{id}}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}
, so we can work with
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{id}}
instead of
\mathbf{D}
. Define
\Psi_{-}=\mathrm{id}_{\omega^{>\mathrm{id}}}
and
\Psi_{+}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu[\omega]^{\omega}\to\omega^{>\mathrm{id}}
by
\Psi_{+}(X)=h
, where
h\in\omega^{>\mathrm{id}}
is any function such that
\bar{x}:=\langle x_{n^{2}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\,n\in\omega\rangle\leq^{*}h
, i.e.,
\forall^{\infty}n\;x_{n^{2}}\leq h(n)
. Assume
N\in\omega
is such that
\forall n\geq N\;|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X|\leq n
and denote
C=|[0,f(N))\cap X|
. Note that for any
n\geq N
, there are at most
C+(n-N)(n-1)
points of
X
inside
[0,f(n))
. Fix

. Since
C+(n^{\prime}-N)(n^{\prime}-1)\leq C+(n^{\prime})^{2}-n^{\prime},
the largest possible point from
X
inside
[0,f(n^{\prime}))
is
x_{C+(n^{\prime})^{2}-n^{\prime}}
. Since
x_{C+(n^{\prime})^{2}-n^{\prime}}<x_{(n^{\prime})^{2}}

f(n^{\prime})\leq x_{(n^{\prime})^{2}}=\bar{x}(n^{\prime})

f\leq^{*}\bar{x}\leq^{*}h=\Psi_{+}(X)
.
∎

.

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}})=\mathfrak{d}
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}})=\mathfrak{b}
.

. The same argument yields

and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{g})=\mathfrak{d}
.

Lemma 2.42
.

\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\exists}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{0}_{\exists}
.

Proof.

\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\exists}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{0}_{\exists}
is clear, so we show
\mathbf{R}^{0}_{\exists}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\exists}
. For any

define
h

, for each

and put
\Psi_{-}(f)=h
. Set
\Psi_{+}=\mathrm{id}_{[\omega]^{\omega}}
.

Assume
|[h(n),h(n+1))\cap X|\leq n

n\in\omega
and fix such an
n
.
Since
f
is increasing, we have
h(n)<f(h(n))<f(h(n)+n+1)=h(n+1)
. Note that there are at least
n+1
intervals constituted by
f

, namely

[f(h(n)),f(h(n)+1)),[f(h(n)+1),f(h(n)+2)),\dotso,[f(h(n)+n)),f(h(n)+n+1)),

thus, at least one of these intervals has an empty intersection with

.
∎

Corollary 2.43
.

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\exists})=\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{id}}_{\exists})=\mathfrak{d}
.

Similarly, for the relation

\sqsubset_{\exists}^{g}

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{g})=\mathfrak{d}

Definition 2.44
.

Let
\omega^{\omega}_{\uparrow}=\{f\in\omega^{\omega}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\allowbreak\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\,f(n+1)-f(n)\to\infty\}

(1)

For

f\;\sqsubset_{\exists}^{\mathrm{bd}}\;X\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists k\in\omega\;\exists^{\infty}n\;\bigl|\,[\,f(n),f(n+1))\cap X\bigr|\leq k.

(2)

Define the relational system
\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{\mathrm{bd}}=\langle\omega^{\omega}_{\uparrow},[\omega]^{\omega},\sqsubset_{\iota}^{\mathrm{bd}}\rangle
for

The bounded versions are naturally below the fixed-threshold systems in the Tukey order.

Fact 2.45
.

\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{\mathrm{bd}}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{k}
for
\iota\in\{\forall,\exists\}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{\mathrm{bd}})\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{k})

\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{k})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\iota}^{\mathrm{bd}})

We next show that, in the existential case, the bounded relation is already strong enough to recover the behavior of
\mathbf{R}_{\exists}^{k}

Lemma 2.46
.

\mathbf{R}^{k}_{\exists}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\exists}
for
k\geq 0
, in particular
\mathfrak{b}\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\exists})
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\exists})\leq\mathfrak{d}

Proof.

Define
\Psi_{-}(f)(n)=f(n^{2})
for every

. Observe that each interval

[\Psi_{-}(f)(n),\Psi_{-}(f)(n+1))

contains
(n+1)^{2}-n^{2}=2n+1
many intervals
[f(j),f(j+1))
. Let
\Psi_{+}=\mathrm{id}_{[\omega]^{\omega}}
.

Assume
\exists k\;\exists^{\infty}n\;|[f(n^{2}),f(n^{2}+2n+1))\cap X|\leq k
. Let
N\in\omega
be such that

. Then for every
n\geq N
, the interval
[f(n^{2}),f(n^{2}+2n+1))
contains
\geq 2N+1>k
intervals
[f(j),f(j+1))
, so at least one of them must have empty intersection with

, i.e.,
f\sqsubset^{0}_{\exists}X
and consequently

.
∎

Combining
2.45
,
2.46
, and

Corollary 2.47
.

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\exists})=\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\exists})=\mathfrak{d}
.

Lemma 2.48
.

\mathbf{D}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\forall}
, in particular

\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}})\leq\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}})
.

Proof.

We define the maps
\Phi_{-}
and
\Phi_{+}
as follows. For any
g\in\omega^{\uparrow\omega}
, let
f=\Phi_{-}(g)
be defined recursively:

•

f(0)=g(0)
,

•

f(n+1)=g(f(n))+n+1
.

Since
g
is strictly increasing,
g(f(n))\geq f(n)+1
. Thus,
f(n+1)-f(n)=g(f(n))-f(n)+n+1\geq n+2
. As
n\to\infty
,
(f(n+1)-f(n))\to\infty
, so
f\in\omega^{\omega}_{\uparrow}
. Additionally,
f(n+1)>f(n)
, so
f
is strictly increasing and
f(n)\geq n
for all
n
.

For any
X\in[\omega]^{\omega}
, let
X=\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots\}
be its increasing enumeration. We define
h=\Phi_{+}(X)
by

h(n)=x_{n^{2}}.

We must show that if
\Phi_{-}(g)\sqsubset_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}}X
, then
g\leq^{*}\Phi_{+}(X)
.

Assume
f\sqsubset_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}}X
. By the definition, there exist
k\in\omega
and
N\in\omega
such that for all
i\geq N
,

|[f(i),f(i+1))\cap X|\leq k.

For each
n\in\omega
, let
m_{n}
be the index such that
x_{m_{n}}
is the smallest element of
X
with
x_{m_{n}}\geq f(n)
. For any
n>N
, the elements of
X
below
f(n)
consist of those below
f(N)
and those in the intervals
[f(i),f(i+1))
for
i=N,\dots,n-1
. Therefore

m_{n}\leq|X\cap[0,f(N))|+k\cdot(n-N).

This shows that
m_{n}
is bounded by a linear function of
n
, say
m_{n}\leq A\cdot n+B
.

By the definition of
f
, we have
f(n+1)>g(f(n))
. Since
|[f(n),f(n+1))\cap X|\leq k
, the subinterval
[f(n),g(f(n)))
contains at most
k
points of
X
.

The point
x_{m_{n}}
is the first point of
X
such that
x_{m_{n}}\geq f(n)
. Among the set of
k+1
consecutive points
\{x_{m_{n}},x_{m_{n}+1},\dots,x_{m_{n}+k}\}
, at least one must fall outside the interval
[f(n),g(f(n)))
. Therefore, the largest of these points must satisfy

x_{m_{n}+k}\geq g(f(n)).

Since
f(n)\geq n
and
g
is strictly increasing, we have

g(n)\leq g(f(n))\leq x_{m_{n}+k}.

Since
m_{n}
grows linearly (
m_{n}\leq An+B
), there exists an
N_{0}
such that for all
n\geq N_{0}
,
n^{2}\geq m_{n}+k
. Thus,

g(n)\leq x_{n^{2}}=h(n).

This implies
g\leq^{*}h
, completing the Tukey reduction
\mathbf{D}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}}

∎

Combining
2.45
together with
A
and
2.48
, we obtain the following result

Corollary 2.49
.

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}})=\mathfrak{d}
and
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}_{\forall}^{\mathrm{bd}})
.

This completes

.

3.
Open questions

Motivated by
2.30
, it is natural to consider stronger asymptotic forms of interval avoidance and to ask whether they still recover the classical bounding and dominating numbers. In this direction, we introduce the following variants of the relation
\vartriangleleft^{\varepsilon}_{\forall}
.

(1)

Let

\vec{\varepsilon}=\langle\varepsilon_{n}:n<\omega\rangle
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=0
. We define

f\vartriangleleft^{\vec{\varepsilon}}_{\forall}Y\;\iff\;\forall^{\infty}n\in\omega\left(\mu([f(n),f(n+1))\cap Y)\leq\varepsilon_{n}\right).

(2)

We say that

f
strongly captures

Y
if the total intersection across all intervals is finite

f\vartriangleleft^{sum}Y\;\iff\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu([f(n),f(n+1))\cap Y)<\infty.

These relations form a natural hierarchy of increasing strength

f\vartriangleleft^{sum}Y\implies f\vartriangleleft^{\vec{\varepsilon}}_{\forall}Y\implies f\vartriangleleft^{\varepsilon}_{\forall}Y.

Consequently, the associated cardinal invariants satisfy
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{M}^{\varepsilon}_{\forall})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{M}^{sum})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{M}^{sum})\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{M}^{\varepsilon}_{\forall})
.

This naturally leads to the following problem, which asks whether the summable strengthening still yields the classical characteristics.

Question 3.1
.

Is
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{M}^{sum})\leq\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{M}^{sum})
provable in
\mathsf{ZFC}
?
