Explicit Betti Numbers for Skeletons of Chordal Clique Complexes and Their Alexander Duals
Mohammed Rafiq Namiq

Abstract
We study the homological properties of Δ 𝐫 ​ ( n 1 , … , n e ) \Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e}) , a simplicial complex formed by sequentially gluing complete graphs along ( r i − 1 ) (r_{i}-1) -simplices. This construction generates precisely the chordal clique complexes, whose Stanley-Reisner ideals admit 2-linear resolutions. By computing the f f -vector and evaluating the Hilbert series, we establish explicit graded Betti numbers for all k k -skeletons. We show that the regularity of these skeletons is k + 1 k+1 and the projective dimension stabilizes at N 𝐫 − r min − 1 N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1 for k ≥ r min k\geq r_{\min} , providing a complete classification of when the complex is Cohen-Macaulay, sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, or initially Cohen-Macaulay. We also obtain explicit formulas for the ring multiplicity and reduced Euler characteristic. Applying Alexander duality, we derive the f f -vector, rational h h -polynomial, and exact graded Betti numbers of the dual and its skeletons. Furthermore, analyzing these dual skeletons yields a family of complexes that resolve recent open bounds on regularity. Finally, equating the topological and rational evaluations of the Hilbert series produces a new family of combinatorial binomial identities.

1
Introduction

The study of the topological properties of simplicial complexes and the algebraic invariants of their associated rings is a central topic in combinatorial commutative algebra. A primary tool for this translation is the Stanley-Reisner ring
\mathbb{K}[\Sigma]
over a field
\mathbb{K}
, defined as the quotient
R/I_{\Sigma}
, where
I_{\Sigma}
is generated by squarefree monomials corresponding to the minimal non-faces of the simplicial complex
\Sigma
.

A well-studied class of simplicial complexes consists of those whose Stanley-Reisner rings admit 2-linear resolutions. By a foundational result of Fröberg
[
9
]
, the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a simplicial complex admits a 2-linear resolution if and only if the complex is the independence complex of a co-chordal graph. Equivalently, via graph complementation, these correspond to the clique complexes of chordal graphs. In the literature, these complexes are also widely studied under the names generalized forests
[
9
]
, quasi-forests
[
16
]
, and fat forests
[
3
]
. The topological significance of these structures traces back to Dirac
[
7
]
, who proved that a graph is chordal if and only if it is the 1-skeleton of a fat forest. Furthermore, the minimal free resolutions of edge ideals with 2-linear resolutions were algebraically characterized by Chen
[
5
]
.

A recent focus in the literature has been the extraction of exact Betti numbers for specific subclasses of these complexes. Because the Stanley-Reisner ideals of co-chordal graphs admit 2-linear resolutions, their graded Betti numbers are uniquely determined by their Hilbert series, and consequently, by the
f
-vectors of their associated independence complexes
[
9
]
. Early foundational work in extracting explicit combinatorial formulas for these Betti numbers includes the study of complete multipartite graphs by Jacques
[
13
]
, as well as Ferrers graphs by Corso and Nagel
[
6
]
. Building upon these families, Fröberg
[
10
]
recently demonstrated that the Betti numbers for these specific subclasses of fat forests can be elegantly extracted by evaluating their rational Hilbert series. Another perspective of interest is computing the Betti numbers of skeletons, a method algebraically established for general simplicial complexes in
[
15
]
. Subsequently, Fröberg
[
11
]
investigated the skeletons of a highly restricted subclass of fat forests, extracting their exact Betti numbers directly through evaluations of their
f
-vectors and rational Hilbert series. In this paper, we provide an explicit combinatorial construction that generalizes this approach to the entire class of chordal clique complexes, their Alexander duals, and all of their intermediate skeletons.

Central to our approach is the introduction of the construction
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
, representing a simplicial complex formed by
e
simplices
S_{i}
of dimension
n_{i}-1
, sequentially glued such that the intersection
(S_{1}\cup\dots\cup S_{i})\cap S_{i+1}
is an
(r_{i}-1)
-simplex for all
i=1,\dots,e-1
. By extending combinatorial gluing techniques, we compute the
f
-vector and evaluate the rational Hilbert series to determine the exact graded Betti numbers not only for the full complex, but for all intermediate
k
-skeletons.

We further prove that the regularity of these intermediate
k
-skeletons is
k+1
, dropping to
1
for the full complex. Moreover, we establish that the projective dimension is governed by a piecewise function stabilizing at
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1
for all
k\geq r_{\min}
. Consequently, we provide an application to the theory of initially Cohen-Macaulay modules, classifying the homological nature of the full complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
. Specifically, we prove it is initially Cohen-Macaulay when
r_{\min}=\min\{n_{i}\}-1

[
14
]
, and strictly Cohen-Macaulay when
r_{\min}=\max\{n_{i}\}-1

[
9
]
. Finally, we demonstrate that the complex is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay when
r_{i}=n_{i+1}-1
for all
i=1,\dots,e-1
, thereby recovering known bounds for the clique complexes of
(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
-tree graphs
[
1
]
.

Next, we systematically classify the algebraic invariants of the Alexander dual of our complex. While the Betti numbers of the Alexander dual have been previously established for uniform
(d,r)
-forests
[
10
]
, we extend this by computing the
f
-vector, the rational
h
-polynomial, and the exact graded Betti numbers for the duals of arbitrarily glued complexes. By analyzing the deep
k
-skeletons of these duals, we establish a homological bound, yielding an infinite family of geometric solutions to recent open bounds on regularity.

Finally, the topology of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
encodes rich combinatorial relations. Equating the
f
-vector expansion with the rational Hilbert series yields a significant binomial identity with broad enumerative consequences. Specializations in
N_{\mathbf{r}}
recover classical formulas, including alternating Chu–Vandermonde variants.

2
Preliminaries

Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}
be a finite vertex set and
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{K}
.

2.1
Simplicial Complexes, Skeletons, and Duality

An
abstract simplicial complex

\Sigma
on
X
is a collection of subsets of
X
closed under inclusion such that
\{x_{i}\}\in\Sigma
for all
x_{i}\in X
. The elements of
\Sigma
are
faces
. The
dimension
of a face
F\in\Sigma
is
\dim F=|F|-1
, and the
dimension
of
\Sigma
, denoted
\dim\Sigma
, is the maximum dimension among all its faces. A
facet
is a maximal face. A simplicial complex is
pure
if all its facets share the same dimension.

A pure complex
\Sigma
is
strongly connected
if for any two facets
F
and
F^{\prime}
, there exists a sequence of facets
F=F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{t}=F^{\prime}
such that
|F_{i}\cap F_{i+1}|=\dim\Sigma
for all
0\leq i<t
. A subset
N\subseteq X
is a
non-face
of
\Sigma
if
N\notin\Sigma
. The
k
-skeleton
of
\Sigma
, denoted
\Sigma_{(k)}
, is the subcomplex comprising all faces in
\Sigma
of dimension at most
k
.

The
Alexander dual
of
\Sigma
, denoted
\Sigma^{\vee}
, is defined on the same vertex set as
\Sigma^{\vee}=\{X\setminus F\mid F\notin\Sigma\}
. The
clique complex
of a graph
G
is the simplicial complex whose faces are the mutually adjacent vertex sets of
G
. The
independence complex
of
G
is the complex whose faces are the independent sets of vertices. A graph is
chordal
if every cycle of length greater than three has a chord. A graph is
co-chordal
if its complement graph is chordal.

The
f
-vector
of a
(d-1)
-dimensional complex
\Sigma
is
f(\Sigma)=(f_{-1},f_{0},\dots,f_{d-1})
, where
f_{i}
denotes the number of
i
-dimensional faces, and
f_{-1}=1
.

2.2
Stanley-Reisner Rings and Homological Invariants

The
Stanley-Reisner ideal

I_{\Sigma}
is the squarefree monomial ideal in
R
generated by the minimal non-faces of
\Sigma
. The
Stanley-Reisner ring
of
\Sigma
is the quotient ring
\mathbb{K}[\Sigma]=R/I_{\Sigma}
. A
minimal graded free resolution
of
\mathbb{K}[\Sigma]
as an
R
-module takes the form:

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j}R(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j}R(-j)^{\beta_{1,j}}\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}[\Sigma]\longrightarrow 0.

The invariants
\beta_{i,j}(\mathbb{K}[\Sigma])
are the
graded Betti numbers
. We define the
projective dimension
,
\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[\Sigma])=\max\{i\mid\beta_{i,j}\neq 0\}
, and the (
Castelnuovo-Mumford
)
regularity
,
\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[\Sigma])=\max\{j-i\mid\beta_{i,j}\neq 0\}
.

A monomial ideal
I
admits a
linear resolution
if all its minimal generators share a uniform degree
d
, and
\beta_{i,i+j}(I)=0
for all
j\neq d
. An ideal admits a
degree resolution
if its regularity equals the maximum degree of its minimal generators
[
15
]
.

2.3
The Hilbert Series, Multiplicity, and Euler characteristic

The
Hilbert series
of
\mathbb{K}[\Sigma]
admits two rational formulations:

H(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{f_{i-1}t^{i}}{(1-t)^{i}}=\frac{\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}\beta_{i,j}t^{j}}{(1-t)^{N}}.

When expressed over the uniform denominator
(1-t)^{N}
, the numerator is denoted
P_{\mathbb{K}[\Sigma]}(t)
. By factoring out
(1-t)^{N-d}
, where
d=\dim\mathbb{K}[\Sigma]
, we extract the
h
-polynomial
, yielding
H(t)=h(t)/(1-t)^{d}
. The
multiplicity
of the ring is defined as
e(\mathbb{K}[\Sigma])=h(1)=f_{d-1}
. The
reduced Euler characteristic
of
\Sigma
is defined as
\tilde{\chi}(\Sigma)=\sum_{i=-1}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}f_{i}=(-1)^{d-1}h_{d}
.

2.4
Cohen-Macaulay and Initially Cohen-Macaulay Properties

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module. The
dimension
of
M
, denoted
\dim M
, is the supremum of the coheights of its associated primes, whereas its
depth
, denoted
\operatorname{depth}M
, is the length of a maximal
M
-regular sequence. A module
M
is
Cohen-Macaulay
if
M=0
or if
\operatorname{depth}M=\dim M
. By the Eagon-Reiner theorem
[
8
]
,
I_{\Sigma}
admits a linear resolution if and only if
\mathbb{K}[\Sigma^{\vee}]
is Cohen-Macaulay. The Auslander-Buchsbaum formula
[
2
]
implies
\operatorname{depth}(\mathbb{K}[\Sigma])=N-\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[\Sigma])
.

The
initially Cohen-Macaulay
property generalizes this invariant
[
14
]
. The
initial dimension
of a module
M
, denoted
\operatorname{indim}M
, is the infimum of the coheights of its associated primes. A finitely generated module
M
is
initially Cohen-Macaulay
if
M=0
or if
\operatorname{depth}M=\operatorname{indim}M
. The generalized Eagon-Reiner theorem
[
14
]
establishes that
I_{\Sigma}
admits a degree resolution if and only if
\mathbb{K}[\Sigma^{\vee}]
is initially Cohen-Macaulay. A graded module
M
is
sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
if there exists a finite filtration
0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\dots\subset M_{t}=M
of graded submodules such that each quotient
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is Cohen-Macaulay and their Krull dimensions are strictly increasing.

3
The Glued Complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}

For a sequence of non-negative integers
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{e-1})
, let
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
be a simplicial complex formed by
e
simplices
S_{1},\dots,S_{e}
, where each simplex
S_{i}
contains exactly
n_{i}
vertices, such that the sequential intersection
(S_{1}\cup\dots\cup S_{i})\cap S_{i+1}
is an
(r_{i}-1)
-simplex for all
i=1,\dots,e-1
. For brevity, we denote
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
simply as
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
when the simplex sizes are clear from context. The total number of vertices is

N_{\mathbf{r}}=\sum_{i=1}^{e}n_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{e-1}r_{j}.

(1)

By construction, the 1-skeletons of the simplicial complexes
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
are chordal graphs. Consequently, their minimal non-faces, which generate the Stanley-Reisner ideals, form co-chordal graphs. By Fröberg’s theorem
[
9
]
, this establishes that these complexes are exactly those whose Stanley-Reisner rings admit a 2-linear resolution. Therefore, the construction
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
provides a precise combinatorial model for the class of simplicial complexes known in the literature as generalized forests
[
9
]
, quasiforests
[
16
]
, and fat forests
[
3
,
10
]
.

Since the sequential intersections evaluate to
(r_{j}-1)
-simplices, the
f
-vector of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
is obtained by summing the face counts of the maximal simplices and subtracting the intersections. For all
t\geq 0
,

f_{t-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\binom{n_{i}}{t}-\sum_{j=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{j}}{t}.

(2)

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
-1\leq k<\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
, the Krull dimension of the Stanley-Reisner ring of the
k
-skeleton is
\dim\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}]=k+1
. Its algebraic multiplicity is given by

e(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\binom{n_{i}}{k+1}-\sum_{j=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{j}}{k+1}.

Proof.

Since
k<\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
, the maximum dimension of any face in the skeleton is
k
, establishing the Krull dimension as
k+1
. For the Stanley-Reisner ring of any simplicial complex, the algebraic multiplicity is exactly the number of faces of maximum dimension (see, for instance,
[
4
, Section 5.1]
). Evaluating Equation (
2
) at
t=k+1
precisely enumerates these
k
-dimensional faces.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be the co-chordal graph whose independence complex is
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
. The independence polynomial
I(G,x)=\sum_{t\geq 0}i_{t}x^{t}
is given by

I(G,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{e}(1+x)^{n_{i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{e-1}(1+x)^{r_{j}}.

Proof.

By definition, the independent sets of size
t
in
G
correspond exactly to the faces of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
containing
t
vertices, meaning
i_{t}=f_{t-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})
. Substituting Equation (
2
) and applying the binomial identity
\sum_{t\geq 0}\binom{n}{t}x^{t}=(1+x)^{n}
to each term yields the polynomial form.
∎

The rational Hilbert series of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})]
can be computed via standard face counting using Equation (
2
), or by applying
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, yielding

H(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{n_{i}}}-\sum_{j=1}^{e-1}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r_{j}}}.

(3)

Remark 3.3
.

Recently, Fröberg
[
11
]
studied the homological invariants of the
k
-skeletons for a class of complexes with 2-linear resolutions. His construction corresponds precisely to our special case
r_{j}=1
(gluing at a single vertex) for all
j
, where the 1-skeletons of these complexes are exactly the Wollastonite graphs
W(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
.

4
Homological Invariants of the Skeletons

To determine the graded Betti numbers of the
k
-skeletons, we first establish a combinatorial identity.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any non-negative integers
n,s
, and
A
,

\sum_{t=0}^{s}(-1)^{t}\binom{A}{t}\binom{n-t}{s-t}=\binom{n-A}{s}.

Proof.

We extract the coefficient of
y^{s}
from the generating function. Recall
[y^{s-t}](1+y)^{n-t}=\binom{n-t}{s-t}
. The summation is

\sum_{t\geq 0}(-1)^{t}\binom{A}{t}[y^{s-t}](1+y)^{n-t}=[y^{s}](1+y)^{n}\sum_{t\geq 0}\binom{A}{t}\left(\frac{-y}{1+y}\right)^{t}=[y^{s}](1+y)^{n}\left(1-\frac{y}{1+y}\right)^{A}.

Simplifying the base gives
[y^{s}](1+y)^{n-A}=\binom{n-A}{s}
.
∎

We recall a general result established in
[
15
]
for the Betti numbers of the
k
-skeleton.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
n
vertices and
\Delta^{k}
its
k
-skeleton for
-1\leq k\leq\dim\Delta
. If
I_{\Delta}
admits a
t
-linear resolution, then for
s=i+j
, the graded Betti numbers of the skeleton are determined as follows. If
j<t
, all graded Betti numbers vanish except at the shifted row
j=k+1
, yielding

\beta_{i,s}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta^{k}])=\sum_{r=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{j-r}\binom{n-r}{s-r}f_{r-1}(\Delta).

Otherwise, if
j\geq t
, the graded Betti numbers are given by

\beta_{i,s}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta^{k}])=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{s}(-1)^{j-r}\binom{n-r}{s-r}f_{r-1}(\Delta)&\text{if }j\leq k,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{r=k+2}^{s}(-1)^{j-r+1}\binom{n-r}{s-r}f_{r-1}(\Delta)&\text{if }j=k+1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We apply this general computation to our explicit construction.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}=\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
be the constructed complex on
N_{\mathbf{r}}
vertices, and let
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}
be its
k
-skeleton for
-1\leq k\leq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
. The graded Betti numbers
\beta_{i,i+j}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])
are given by

\begin{cases}-\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}{i+1}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}{i+1}&\text{if }j=1\text{ and }k\geq 1,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{t=k+2}^{i+j}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{t}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{t}\right)(-1)^{k-t}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-t}{k+i+1-t}&\text{if }j=k+1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}}
admits a 2-linear resolution, Theorem
4.2
applies with
t=2
. For
j\leq k
, the graded Betti numbers vanish unless
j=1
. Substituting Equation (
2
) into Theorem
4.2
at
j=1
yields

\beta_{i,i+1}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=\sum_{t=0}^{i+1}(-1)^{1-t}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-t}{i+1-t}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{t}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{t}\right).

Factoring out
(-1)^{1-t}=-(-1)^{t}
and applying Lemma
4.1
to the components
\sum_{r\geq 0}(-1)^{r}\binom{n_{m}}{r}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r}{i+1-r}=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}{i+1}
and
\sum_{r\geq 0}(-1)^{r}\binom{r_{m}}{r}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r}{i+1-r}=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}{i+1}
yields the first condition. For
j=k+1
, substituting Equation (
2
) yields

\beta_{i,i+k+1}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=\sum_{t=k+2}^{i+k+1}(-1)^{k-t+2}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-t}{i+k+1-t}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{t}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{t}\right).

The sign simplifies to
(-1)^{k-t}
, establishing the second condition.
∎

Let
r_{\min}=\min_{1\leq i\leq e-1}\{r_{i}\}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}=\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
. The algebraic invariants of its
k
-skeletons are given by

1.

Regularity:

\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=\begin{cases}k+1&\text{for all }-1\leq k<\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}},\\
1&\text{for all }k\geq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}.\end{cases}

2.

Projective Dimension:

\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=\begin{cases}N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1&\text{for all }-1\leq k\leq r_{\min},\\
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1&\text{for all }k\geq r_{\min}.\end{cases}

In particular, the full complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
is initially Cohen-Macaulay if
r_{\min}=\min\{n_{i}\}-1
, Cohen-Macaulay if
r_{\min}=\max\{n_{i}\}-1
, and sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if
r_{i}=n_{i+1}-1
for all
i=1,\dots,e-1
.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Corollary 3.7]
,
I_{(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}}
admits a degree resolution, establishing
\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=k+1
for
k<\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
. For
k\geq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
admits a 2-linear resolution
[
9
]
, implying
\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=1
. The
k
-skeletons correspond to strongly connected chordal clique complexes for
k\leq r_{\min}
, making them Cohen-Macaulay
[
14
, Corollary 5.5]
. The projective dimension equates to the height,
N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1
. For
k\geq r_{\min}
, the shifted row has maximal homological shift
N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1
, while the linear row ends at
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1
. Hence the linear row determines the projective dimension, and
\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1
for all
k\geq r_{\min}
. For
k>r_{\min}
, the linear row terminates at
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1
, dominating the homological shift. If
r_{\min}=\min\{n_{i}\}-1
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
is initially Cohen-Macaulay
[
14
, Definition 3.13]
. If
r_{\min}=\max\{n_{i}\}-1
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
is pure and Cohen-Macaulay. If
r_{i}=n_{i+1}-1
, the complex is an
(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
-tree graph, proven sequentially Cohen-Macaulay in
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Because the
k
-skeletons correspond to strongly connected chordal clique complexes for all
k\leq r_{\min}
, they are vertex decomposable, implying these intermediate skeletons are shellable.

Corollary 4.6
.

The depth of the Stanley-Reisner rings of the
k
-skeletons is
\operatorname{depth}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=k+1
for
-1\leq k\leq r_{\min}
, and
r_{\min}+1
for all
k\geq r_{\min}
.

∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
c_{0}=|\{m\mid r_{m}=0\}|
denote the number of disjoint graph components. For
k<\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
,
\operatorname{deg}h_{\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}]}(t)=k+1
, except when
r_{\min}=0
,
k
is odd, and
\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}-1}{k+1}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}-1}{k+1}=c_{0}
, in which case the degree is less than
k+1
.

Proof.

Let
d=k+1
. The degree of the
h
-polynomial corresponds to the maximum internal degree
q
of the numerator polynomial
P(t)
. Thus,
\operatorname{deg}h_{\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}]}(t)=k+1
if and only if
[t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}]P(t)\neq 0
. The maximum internal degree
q=N_{\mathbf{r}}
on the shifted row occurs at
p=N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1
, generating a positive coefficient. The linear row terminates at
p=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1
. If
r_{\min}\geq 1
, this does not generate
t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}
, implying
\operatorname{deg}h_{\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}]}(t)=k+1
. If
r_{\min}=0
, the linear row extends to
p=N_{\mathbf{r}}-1
, yielding the algebraic sum
[t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}]P(t)=(-1)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}-1}{k+1}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}-1}{k+1}+(-1)^{k}c_{0}\right)
. This evaluates to zero precisely under the stated conditions.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

For the Cohen-Macaulay skeletons where
-1\leq k\leq r_{\min}
,
a(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}])=0
, except when
r_{\min}=0
and the cancellation condition of Proposition
4.7
is met, in which case the
a
-invariant is negative.

∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
c_{0}=|\{m\mid r_{m}=0\}|
. The reduced Euler characteristic
\tilde{\chi}((\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)})
is given by

\tilde{\chi}((\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{t-1}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{t}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{t}\right)&\text{for }-1\leq k<\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}},\\
c_{0}&\text{for }k\geq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}\text{ and }r_{\min}=0,\\
0&\text{for }k\geq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}\text{ and }r_{\min}\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

The reduced Euler characteristic satisfies
\tilde{\chi}=(-1)^{d-1}h_{d}
, where

is the Krull dimension. If
-1\leq k<\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
,

. Since
\operatorname{deg}h_{\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}]}(t)\leq d
,

evaluates to the alternating sum of the

-vector up to dimension

, yielding the first case. If

, the skeleton coincides with
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
, with

. From
\operatorname{pdim}=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1
and
\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}])=1
, the invariants equality gives
\operatorname{deg}h_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}]}(t)=d-r_{\min}

. If

\operatorname{deg}h_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}]}(t)<d

.
∎

5
The Alexander Dual of Glued Complexes

In this section, we determine the homological and combinatorial invariants of the full Alexander dual of a glued chordal clique complex, beginning with its face enumeration and the rational form of its
h
-polynomial.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
be the Alexander dual of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})
. The
f
-vector components, for
-1\leq i\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
, are defined by

f_{i}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{i+1}-\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-1}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-1}.

The
h
-polynomial of the Stanley-Reisner ring of the Cohen-Macaulay dual is given by

h_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]}(t)=\frac{1-\sum_{m=1}^{e}t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}}{(1-t)^{2}}.

Proof.

The Alexander dual is
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}=\{F\subseteq X\mid X\setminus F\notin\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}\}
. To enumerate the
i
-dimensional faces of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
, we consider subsets of
X
of size
i+1
. A subset
F
with
|F|=i+1
is a face of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
if and only if its complement
X\setminus F
, which has cardinality
N_{\mathbf{r}}-(i+1)
, is a non-face of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
. Therefore, the number of faces
f_{i}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})
is exactly the total number of
(i+1)
-subsets minus the number of faces of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
of dimension
N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-2
. This yields the relation

f_{i}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{i+1}-f_{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-2}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}).

Substituting Equation (
2
) evaluated at
t=N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-1
provides the face enumeration. The numerator of the dual expands as
P_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}f_{i-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})t^{i}(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}
. Substituting the dual face relation yields
P_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]}(t)=1-P_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}]}(1-t)
. From (
3
), we write
H(t)
with denominator
(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}
, obtaining the numerator as

P_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]}(t)=1-\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}\right).

Because
I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}}
admits a 2-linear resolution, the dual ring is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d^{\vee}=N_{\mathbf{r}}-2
. Factoring
(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}=(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-2}(1-t)^{2}
, the
h
-polynomial is the numerator of the remaining fraction over
(1-t)^{2}
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

The multiplicity of the Stanley-Reisner ring of the Alexander dual is given by

e(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}(N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m})(N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}-1)-\sum_{m=1}^{e}(N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m})(N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}-1)\right).

Proof.

The multiplicity is defined as
h(1)
. Since
t=1
is a root of multiplicity
2
for both the numerator and denominator,
h(1)=P^{\prime\prime}(1)/2
. Evaluating the second derivative of the polynomial
P(t)
from Theorem
5.1
at
t=1
produces the formula.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The entries of the
h
-vector,
h(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{N_{\mathbf{r}}-2})
, are given by

h_{k}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=(k+1)-\sum_{m=1}^{e}\max(0,k-N_{\mathbf{r}}+n_{m}+1)+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\max(0,k-N_{\mathbf{r}}+r_{m}+1).

Proof.

Expanding
(1-t)^{-2}=\sum_{j\geq 0}(j+1)t^{j}
, the component
h_{k}
is the coefficient of
t^{k}
in the product
P(t)(1-t)^{-2}
. Extracting this coefficient shifts the index of the power series by the respective monomial degrees
N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}
and
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}
. This term implies the non-negativity condition via the maximum function.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The minimal free resolution of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
has length exactly 2, and its graded Betti numbers
\beta_{i,j}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])
are given by

\beta_{i,j}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i=0\text{ and }j=0,\\
\big|\{m\in\{1,\dots,e\}\mid N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}=j\}\big|&\text{if }i=1,\\
\big|\{m\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}\mid N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}=j\}\big|&\text{if }i=2,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By the Eagon-Reiner theorem
[
8
]
, the 2-linear resolution of
I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}}
implies
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
is Cohen-Macaulay with Krull dimension
N_{\mathbf{r}}-2
. Hence,
\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=2
. Therefore, the minimal free resolution of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
has length
2
, consisting only of generators and first syzygies. The numerator of the rational Hilbert series determines the graded Betti numbers via
P_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]}(t)=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}\beta_{i,j}t^{j}
. Because the projective dimension is exactly 2, this expands precisely as

P_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]}(t)=1-\sum_{j}\beta_{1,j}t^{j}+\sum_{j}\beta_{2,j}t^{j}.

Comparing coefficients with the polynomial
P(t)=1-\sum_{m=1}^{e}t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}t^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}
from Theorem
5.1
determines the Betti numbers. The negative terms correspond to
i=1
, giving
\beta_{1,N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}=1
for
1\leq m\leq e
, while the positive terms correspond to
i=2
, giving
\beta_{2,N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}=1
for
1\leq m\leq e-1
. Collecting equal shifts yields the stated multiplicities. Summing the instances of identical homological shifts yields the stated cardinalities.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

By the running intersection property of chordal graphs, for each
m\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
, the sequential intersection
(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}S_{i})\cap S_{m+1}
must be fully contained in some previous clique
S_{p(m+1)}
, where
1\leq p(m+1)\leq m
. The minimal free resolution of the Stanley-Reisner ideal of the Alexander dual
I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}}
is given by

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{m=1}^{e-1}R(-N_{\mathbf{r}}+r_{m})\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}\bigoplus_{m=1}^{e}R(-N_{\mathbf{r}}+n_{m})\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}}\longrightarrow 0

where the maps
\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{2}
are defined as follows:

1.

The
1\times e
matrix
\partial_{1}
has entries
g_{m}=\prod_{x\in X\setminus S_{m}}x
, for
m=1,\dots,e
.

2.

The
e\times(e-1)
matrix
\partial_{2}
has exactly two non-zero entries in each column
m\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
, located at rows
p(m+1)
and
m+1
:

(\partial_{2})_{p(m+1),m}=\prod_{x\in S_{p(m+1)}\setminus S_{m+1}}x\quad\text{and}\quad(\partial_{2})_{m+1,m}=-\prod_{x\in S_{m+1}\setminus S_{p(m+1)}}x.

Proof.

By Alexander duality, the minimal non-faces of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
are the complements of the facets
S_{1},\dots,S_{e}
of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
. Hence,
I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}}
is minimally generated by

g_{m}=\prod_{x\in X\setminus S_{m}}x,\qquad m=1,\dots,e,

of degree
N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}
. This establishes the first map
\partial_{1}
.

By Theorem
5.4
,
\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=2
with exactly
e-1
minimal relations occurring at shifts
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}
. Let
R_{m}=(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}S_{i})\cap S_{m+1}
, with
|R_{m}|=r_{m}
. By the running intersection property of chordal graphs, we can choose an index
p(m+1)\leq m
such that
S_{p(m+1)}\cap S_{m+1}=R_{m}
. For each
m
, the fundamental relation between
g_{p(m+1)}
and
g_{m+1}
occurs in the multidegree

\operatorname{lcm}(g_{p(m+1)},g_{m+1})=\prod_{x\in X\setminus R_{m}}x,

of degree
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}
, matching the expected shifts. Algebraically, the relation is given by

\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(g_{p(m+1)},g_{m+1})}{g_{p(m+1)}}e_{p(m+1)}-\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(g_{p(m+1)},g_{m+1})}{g_{m+1}}e_{m+1}=0.

Evaluating the monomial quotients, we obtain

\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(g_{p(m+1)},g_{m+1})}{g_{p(m+1)}}=\prod_{x\in S_{p(m+1)}\setminus S_{m+1}}x\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(g_{p(m+1)},g_{m+1})}{g_{m+1}}=\prod_{x\in S_{m+1}\setminus S_{p(m+1)}}x.

These form the entries of the
m
-th column of
\partial_{2}
. Row
p(m+1)
contains the positive product and row
m+1
contains the negative product, with all other entries zero. These
e-1
independent relations form a complete minimal basis for the module of relations, and since
\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=2
, the sequence is exact.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

The regularity of the Stanley-Reisner ring of the Alexander dual is
\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-2
.

Proof.

The regularity of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
is given by
\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=\max\{j-i\mid\beta_{i,j}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])\neq 0\}
. By Theorem
5.4
, the nonzero Betti numbers occur for
i=0,1,2
with shifts
j=N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}
(for
i=1
) and
j=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}
(for
i=2
). Hence

j-i=N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}-1\quad\text{or}\quad N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}-2.

Since
r_{m}\leq n_{m}-1
, we have
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}-2\geq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}-1
. Thus the maximum occurs at
i=2
, giving

\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-2.

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

The
a
-invariant of the Stanley-Reisner ring of the Alexander dual is exactly the negative of the minimum gluing dimension:
a(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=-r_{\min}
.

Proof.

Since
I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}}
admits a 2-linear resolution,
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
N_{\mathbf{r}}-2
. For a Cohen-Macaulay graded ring, the
a
-invariant satisfies
a(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])-\dim\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
. Substituting the regularity established in Corollary
5.6
yields
(N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-2)-(N_{\mathbf{r}}-2)=-r_{\min}
.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

If exactly
c_{0}
of the intersection parameters
r_{m}
are zero, then
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
has the homology of a wedge of
c_{0}
spheres of dimension
N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
. If
r_{m}\geq 1
for all
m
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
is acyclic.

Proof.

By Hochster’s formula
[
12
]
, the dimension of the top homology group
\widetilde{H}_{N_{\mathbf{r}}-3}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee};\mathbb{K})
equals the extremal graded Betti number
\beta_{2,N_{\mathbf{r}}}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])
. Theorem
5.4
establishes that this Betti number precisely counts the number of intersection parameters satisfying
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}=N_{\mathbf{r}}
, which occurs if and only if
r_{m}=0
. Because
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
is Cohen-Macaulay, its reduced homology vanishes in all lower dimensions. Thus, the complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of exactly
c_{0}
spheres. If
r_{m}\geq 1
for all
m
, then
c_{0}=0
and the top homology also vanishes, making the complex acyclic.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

The Cohen-Macaulay type of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
is exactly
e-1
. The ring
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
is Gorenstein if and only if
e=2
, in which case it is a complete intersection.

Proof.

The Cohen-Macaulay type of a module equals its last non-zero total Betti number. Since
\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=2
, the type is
\sum_{j}\beta_{2,j}
. By Theorem
5.4
, there is exactly one minimal relation for each of the
e-1
gluing intersections, yielding a type of
e-1
. The ring is Gorenstein if and only if its type is 1, which implies
e=2
. Any Gorenstein ideal of codimension 2 is necessarily a complete intersection.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

The ideal
I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}}
admits a pure resolution if and only if
n_{1}=\dots=n_{e}=n
and
r_{1}=\dots=r_{e-1}=r
. The resolution is
(N_{\mathbf{r}}-n)
-linear if and only if
r=n-1
.

Proof.

A free resolution is pure if the graded Betti numbers
\beta_{i,j}
are supported in a single degree
j
for each homological step
i
. By Theorem
5.4
, this holds if and only if the generator shifts
N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}
are uniform, and the relation shifts
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}
are uniform, yielding
n_{m}=n
and
r_{m}=r
. Furthermore, the resolution is linear if the degree increases by
1
at each step. This requires the relation degree to equal the generator degree plus
1
, meaning
(N_{\mathbf{r}}-r)-(N_{\mathbf{r}}-n)=1
, which reduces precisely to
r=n-1
.
∎

6
Homological Invariants of Dual Skeletons

Having established the algebraic structure of the full Alexander dual
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
, we turn our attention to its
k
-skeletons. Applying
[
15
, Theorem 3.9]
, we now obtain explicit expressions for the graded Betti numbers of the
k
-skeletons of the Alexander dual.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}
denote the
k
-skeleton of the Alexander dual complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
, where
-1\leq k<N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
. The graded Betti numbers of the Stanley-Reisner ring
\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}]
are completely determined as follows:

1.

For homological shifts
j<k+1
, the Betti numbers strictly coincide with those of the original dual complex:

\beta_{i,i+j}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])=\beta_{i,i+j}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]).

2.

For homological shifts
j>k+1
, the Betti numbers vanish:

\beta_{i,i+j}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])=0.

3.

For the maximal non-vanishing shift
j=k+1
and homological degree
i\geq 1
, the Betti numbers are explicitly given by

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+k+1}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])

\displaystyle=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{i+k+1}\binom{i+k}{k+1}-\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-k-1}\binom{n_{m}-N_{\mathbf{r}}+i+k}{i-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-k-1}\binom{r_{m}-N_{\mathbf{r}}+i+k}{i-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta_{i,1}\big|\{m\mid N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}=k+2\}\big|,

where
\delta_{i,1}
denotes the Kronecker delta.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Theorem 3.9]
, the graded Betti numbers of the
k
-skeleton coincide with those of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]
for shifts
j<k+1
and vanish for
j>k+1
, establishing the first two conditions. For the shifted row
j=k+1
, set
s=i+k+1
. Then:

\beta_{i,i+k+1}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])=(-1)^{i}\sum_{r=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{s-r}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r}{s-r}f_{r-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})-(-1)^{i}\sum_{i^{\prime}>i}(-1)^{i^{\prime}}\beta_{i^{\prime},s}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]).

Since
\operatorname{pdim}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=2
, the second sum is non-zero only when
i=1
and
i^{\prime}=2
. In this case, the sum evaluates to

-(-1)^{1}(-1)^{2}\beta_{2,k+2}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}])=\beta_{2,k+2}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}]).

By Theorem
5.4
,
\beta_{2,k+2}=|\{m\mid N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}=k+2\}|
. Hence, for any
i\geq 1
, this secondary sum reduces to the Kronecker delta term:

\delta_{i,1}\big|\{m\mid N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}=k+2\}\big|.

For the primary sum, substitute
f_{r-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{r}-f_{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})
to obtain two contributions. The positive term is

\Sigma_{1}=\sum_{r=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{k+1-r}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r}{s-r}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{r}=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{s}\sum_{r=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{k+1-r}\binom{s}{r}.

Applying the alternating binomial identity yields

\Sigma_{1}=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{s}\binom{s-1}{k+1}=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{i+k+1}\binom{i+k}{k+1}.

The negative term involves the original faces

\Sigma_{2}=-\sum_{r=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{k+1-r}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r}{s-r}f_{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}).

Substituting the
f
-vector from Equation (
2
) and applying Lemma
4.1
gives

\Sigma_{2}=-\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-k-1}\binom{n_{m}-N_{\mathbf{r}}+i+k}{i-1}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i-k-1}\binom{r_{m}-N_{\mathbf{r}}+i+k}{i-1}.

Combining
\Sigma_{1}
,
\Sigma_{2}
, and the Kronecker delta term completes the expression.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

For
-1\leq k<N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
, the
k
-skeleton
\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}]
is Cohen-Macaulay with projective dimension
N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1
, and its Cohen-Macaulay type is given by

\operatorname{type}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-1}{k+1}-\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}-1}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-2}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}-1}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-2}.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Corollary 3.17 ]
,
\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}]
is Cohen-Macaulay for
k\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-2
, with projective dimension
i=N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1
. Substituting this

into Theorem
6.1
, the assumption

implies

, so
\delta_{i,1}=0
. The lower arguments in the correction binomials become
0
, yielding the result.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

For
-1\leq k<N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
, the regularity of the Stanley-Reisner ideal of the
k
-skeleton is
\operatorname{reg}(I_{(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}})=k+2
.

Proof.

By definition,
\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])=\max\{j\mid\beta_{i,i+j}\neq 0\}
. Theorem
6.1
shows
\beta_{i,i+j}=0
for all
j>k+1
, while the shifted row

contains nonzero entries. Hence the regularity of the ring is
k+1
, making the ideal regularity
k+2
.
∎

By Corollary
6.3
, we have
\operatorname{reg}(I_{\Delta^{(k)}})=k+2
. Thus, bounding
\operatorname{reg}(I_{\Delta})\leq\operatorname{deg}(I_{\Delta^{(k)}})=k+2
reduces to determining for which complexes the regularity decreases under skeletal truncation. The explicit homological formulas above provide a family of complexes that resolve the open problem in
[
15

.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
be a glued chordal clique complex on
N_{\mathbf{r}}
vertices with minimal intersection size

. Then for all skeleton parameters in the range
N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-3\leq k<N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
, its Alexander dual
\Delta=\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
satisfies the regularity bound

\operatorname{reg}(I_{\Delta})\leq\operatorname{deg}(I_{\Delta^{(k)}})=k+2.

In particular, the bound is satisfied with equality at
k=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-3
.

Proof.

By Alexander duality,
\dim\Delta=N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
. By Corollary
5.6
,
\operatorname{reg}(\mathbb{K}[\Delta])=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-2
, yielding
\operatorname{reg}(I_{\Delta})=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1
. For any proper skeleton where
k<N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
, Corollary
6.3
establishes that
\operatorname{deg}(I_{\Delta^{(k)}})=k+2
. Applying the lower limit of the given range,
k\geq N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-3
, immediately yields the inequality

\operatorname{deg}(I_{\Delta^{(k)}})=k+2\geq(N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-3)+2=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-1=\operatorname{reg}(I_{\Delta}).

Evaluating exactly at
k=N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{\min}-3
reduces the inequality to an exact equality.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

The inequality
\operatorname{reg}(I_{\Delta})\leq\operatorname{deg}(I_{\Delta^{(k)}})
shows that the minimal free resolution of
\Delta
is completely determined by that of its
k
-skeleton, with the
h
-vector of
\Delta
needed only in the boundary equality case
[
15
, Corollaries 3.12 and 3.13]
. Although illustrated here for Alexander duals of glued chordal clique complexes, this reconstruction holds to any Cohen–Macaulay complex with
\operatorname{deg}h_{\mathbb{K}[\Delta]}(t)-1\leq k<\dim\Delta
.

Proposition 6.6

(Multiplicity and Euler Characteristic)
.

Let
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
be the Alexander dual of a glued chordal clique complex with
\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}=N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
. For any skeleton parameter
-1\leq k\leq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
, the
k
-skeleton
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}
satisfies

(i)

\displaystyle e(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])=f_{k}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{k+1}-\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-1}.

(ii)

\tilde{\chi}((\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{k}\left[\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-1}{k+1}-\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}-1}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-2}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}-1}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-2}\right]&\text{if }k<N_{\mathbf{r}}-3,\\[10.00002pt]
0&\text{if }k=N_{\mathbf{r}}-3.\end{cases}

Proof.

(i) Each maximal face of
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}
has dimension
k
. The multiplicity of its Stanley-Reisner ring equals the number of such facets, which is exactly
f_{k}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})
. Substituting
j=k+1
into the
f
-vector derivation in Theorem
5.1
yields the stated expression.

(ii) For
k<N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
, the
k
-skeleton is Cohen-Macaulay, so
\widetilde{H}_{i}((\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)};\mathbb{K})=0
for
i<k
. Thus,
\tilde{\chi}((\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)})=(-1)^{k}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{k}((\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)};\mathbb{K})
. By Hochster’s formula
[
12
]
, this dimension is exactly the Cohen-Macaulay type of the ring, yielding the binomial expression by Corollary
6.2
. For
k=N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
, the reduced Euler characteristic is given by
\tilde{\chi}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=(-1)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-3}h_{N_{\mathbf{r}}-2}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})
. Applying Corollary
5.3
and evaluating at the index
N_{\mathbf{r}}-2
yields

h_{N_{\mathbf{r}}-2}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=(N_{\mathbf{r}}-1)-\sum_{m=1}^{e}(n_{m}-1)+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}(r_{m}-1)=N_{\mathbf{r}}-\sum_{m=1}^{e}n_{m}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}r_{m}.

By Equation (
1
), we know that
\sum_{m=1}^{e}n_{m}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}r_{m}=N_{\mathbf{r}}
. Substituting this into our expression yields
h_{N_{\mathbf{r}}-2}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=N_{\mathbf{r}}-N_{\mathbf{r}}=0
, which implies
\tilde{\chi}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})=0
. This verifies the piecewise definition in (ii).
∎

The vanishing of the correction terms determines a truncation bound below which the shifted row Betti numbers are unaffected by the gluing structure.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\mathbf{\Delta}_{N_{\mathbf{r}}}
denote the full simplex on
N_{\mathbf{r}}
vertices, and let
n_{\max}=\max\{n_{m}\}
be the maximum clique order of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
. If
-1\leq k\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}-2
, then for all
i\geq 1

\beta_{i,i+k+1}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])=\beta_{i,i+k+1}(\mathbb{K}[(\mathbf{\Delta}_{N_{\mathbf{r}}})_{(k)}])=\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}}{i+k+1}\binom{i+k}{k+1}.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.1
,
\beta_{i,i+k+1}(\mathbb{K}[(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}])
equals the corresponding Betti number of the
k
-skeleton of the full simplex plus correction terms. If
k\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}-2
, then
A-N_{\mathbf{r}}+i+k<i-1
for all
A\leq n_{\max}
and
i\geq 1
, so the second binomial vanishes. Hence all correction terms disappear. Moreover,
k+2\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}<N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}
, so the delta term is zero.
∎

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
be a glued clique complex on
N_{\mathbf{r}}
vertices and let
n_{\max}=\max\{n_{m}\}
. If
k\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}-2
, then
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}=(\mathbf{\Delta}_{N_{\mathbf{r}}})_{(k)}
. Consequently,
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of
k
-dimensional spheres, and

\widetilde{H}_{i}((\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)};\mathbb{K})=0\quad\text{for all }i\neq k.

Proof.

The minimal nonfaces of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
are the complements of the maximal cliques of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
. Hence the smallest minimal nonface has cardinality
N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}
. Therefore every subset of vertices of size
<N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}
is a face of
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
. The
k
-skeleton consists of all faces of size at most
k+1
. If
k\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}-2
, then
k+1\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}-1
, so all such subsets occur in
\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}
, and hence the
k
-skeleton coincides with
(\mathbf{\Delta}_{N_{\mathbf{r}}})_{(k)}
. Since the
k
-skeleton of a simplex is
(k-1)
-connected with nontrivial homology only in dimension
k
, the result follows.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

For
k<N_{\mathbf{r}}-3
, the complex
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of
k
-spheres. The number of spheres is

\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-1}{k+1}-\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}-1}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-2}+\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}-1}{N_{\mathbf{r}}-k-2}.

In particular, if
k\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}-2
, then

(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-1}{k+1}}S^{k}
.

Proof.

For a simplicial complex, the number of spheres in a wedge decomposition equals
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee}}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee};\mathbb{K})
. Applying Hochster’s formula
[
12
]
to
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}^{\vee})_{(k)}
and using the face enumeration from Theorem
5.1
yields the stated expression. Under the assumption
k\leq N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{\max}-2
, the correction terms vanish, yielding the explicit wedge sum.
∎

Remark 6.10
.

For
-1\leq k\leq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}
, an immediate and powerful consequence of these explicit formulas is that the homological invariants of
(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})_{(k)}
and its Alexander dual depend entirely on the underlying multisets of clique sizes
\mathcal{N}=\{n_{1},\dots,n_{e}\}
and intersection sizes
\mathcal{R}=\{r_{1},\dots,r_{e-1}\}
. Because these algebraic invariants are completely independent of the topological gluing order, non-isomorphic chordal graphs constructed from the same multisets possess identical
f
-vectors,
h
-polynomials, and graded Betti tables.

8
Combinatorial Identities via Hilbert Series Evaluations

We derive a sequence of combinatorial identities for binomial coefficients by evaluating the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{e})]
using its rational function form and its topological
f
-vector expansion. We begin with the most general description of the complex and then systematically specialize these geometric parameters, notably recovering classical convolutions such as the Chu-Vandermonde identity.

Theorem 8.1
.

For integers
n_{m}\geq r_{m}\geq 1
,
e\geq 1
, and
j\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}{j}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{i}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{i}\right)\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}{j-i}.

Proof.

We compute the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{K}[\Delta_{\mathbf{r}}]
in two ways and compare the numerators over the common denominator
(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}
. By Equation (
3
), the rational Hilbert series is given by

H(t)=\sum_{m=1}^{e}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{n_{m}}}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r_{m}}}.

Expressing this over the common denominator
(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}
yields the numerator

P_{1}(t)=\sum_{m=1}^{e}(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}.

Using
(1-t)^{A}=\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\binom{A}{j}t^{j}
, the coefficient of
t^{j}
is

[t^{j}]P_{1}(t)=\sum_{m=1}^{e}(-1)^{j}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}{j}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}{j}.

On the other hand, the Hilbert series evaluated topologically is

H(t)=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}f_{i-1}(\Delta_{\mathbf{r}})t^{i}(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}}{(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}}.

Substituting the
f
-vector from Equation (
2
) yields the numerator

P_{2}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{N_{\mathbf{r}}}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{i}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{i}\right)t^{i}(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}.

Expanding
(1-t)^{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}
and collecting the terms where the total power of
t
is
j
gives

[t^{j}]P_{2}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{i}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{i}\right)(-1)^{j-i}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}{j-i}.

Since
P_{1}(t)=P_{2}(t)
, equating
[t^{j}]P_{1}(t)=[t^{j}]P_{2}(t)
yields

\sum_{m=1}^{e}(-1)^{j}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}{j}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-i}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{i}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{i}\right)\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}{j-i}.

Multiplying both sides by
(-1)^{j}
and simplifying the exponent
(-1)^{2j-i}=(-1)^{-i}=(-1)^{i}
establishes the desired combinatorial identity.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

For integers
n\geq r_{m}\geq 1
,
e\geq 1
, and
j\geq 1
, we have

e\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n}{j}=\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r_{m}}{j}+\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\left(e\binom{n}{i}-\sum_{m=1}^{e-1}\binom{r_{m}}{i}\right)\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}{j-i}.

Proof.

Substituting
n_{m}=n
for all
m\in\{1,\dots,e\}
in Theorem
8.1
reduces the corresponding sums to
e
identical terms, yielding the simplified identity upon rearrangement.
∎

Corollary 8.3
.

For integers
n_{m}\geq r\geq 1
,
e\geq 1
, and
j\geq 1
, we have

(e-1)\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r}{j}=\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n_{m}}{j}-\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{e}\binom{n_{m}}{i}-(e-1)\binom{r}{i}\right)\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}{j-i}.

Proof.

Set
r_{m}=r
for all
m\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
in Theorem
8.1
. Extracting the common binomial term on the left-hand side gives the identity.
∎

Assuming uniform graph and gluing sizes eliminates the left-hand summation in Theorem
8.1
.

Corollary 8.4
.

For integers
n\geq r\geq 1
,
e\geq 1
, and
j\geq 1
, we have

e\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-n}{j}-(e-1)\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-r}{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\left(e\binom{n}{i}-(e-1)\binom{r}{i}\right)\binom{N_{\mathbf{r}}-i}{j-i}.

Proof.

Set
n_{m}=n
and
r_{m}=r
in Theorem
8.1
. The sums over
m
reduce to scalar multiples, leaving pure binomial terms on the left.
∎

Corollary 8.5
.

For integers
n\geq r\geq 1
,
e\geq 1
, and
j\geq 1
, we have

(e-1)\binom{n-r}{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i+1}\left(e\binom{n}{i}-(e-1)\binom{r}{i}\right)\binom{n-i}{j-i}.

Proof.

Corollary
8.4
is a polynomial identity in
N_{\mathbf{r}}
. Substituting
N_{\mathbf{r}}=n
gives

e\binom{0}{j}-(e-1)\binom{n-r}{j}=-(e-1)\binom{n-r}{j}.

Equating with the right-hand side at
N_{\mathbf{r}}=n
and multiplying by
-1
yields the reduced identity.
∎

Corollary 8.6
.

For integers
n\geq r\geq 1
and
j\geq 1
, we have

\binom{r-n}{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{r-i}{j-i}.

Proof.

Evaluating Corollary
8.4
at
N_{\mathbf{r}}=r
simplifies the left-hand side to
e\binom{r-n}{j}
. On the right-hand side, the
(e-1)
-term vanishes since
\binom{r}{i}\binom{r-i}{j-i}=\binom{r}{j}\binom{j}{i}
and
\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{i}\binom{j}{i}=0
. Dividing by
e
yields the desired identity.
∎

Remark 8.7
.

The relation in Corollary
8.6
is a known alternating variant of the Chu-Vandermonde convolution, which can be transformed into the standard form via upper negation.

Finally, evaluating the reduced identity from Corollary
8.5
for a single complete graph directly recovers another standard binomial identity.

Corollary 8.8
.

For integers
n\geq 1
and
