Problems in additive number theory, VII: The structure of additive h h -bases for n n
Melvyn B. Nathanson

Abstract
. In additive number theory, a finite set A A of integers is an h h -basis for n n if every integer in { 0 , 1 , 2 , … , n } \{0,1,2,\ldots,n\} can be represented as the sum of exactly h h not necessarily distinct elements of A A . This paper introduces a new class of problems for these and related additive bases. The problems are designed, in part, to be susceptible to solution by AI.

Intervals contained in sumsets

Let
\mathbf{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
be the set of positive integers,
\mathbf{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}

the set of nonnegative integers, and
\mathbf{Z}
the set of all integers.
An
interval of integers
is a set of the form

[a,b]=\{j\in\mathbf{Z}:a\leq j\leq b\}

with
a,b\in\mathbf{Z}
and
a<b
. Thus, every interval contains at least two integers.
The
length
of the interval
[a,b]
is
\ell=b-a
.
An interval of length
\ell
contains
\ell+1
integers.
An integer in a subset
A
of
\mathbf{Z}
is
isolated
if it is not a member of an interval
contained in
A
.

The cardinality of the set
A
is denoted
|A|
.
For every nonempty set
X
of integers, we define

\mathcal{A}_{X}(k)=\{A\subseteq X:|A|=k\}.

We are interested in infinite sets
X
and, especially, in the sets
\mathbf{N}_{0}
and
\mathbf{Z}
.

The
h
-fold sumset of a set
A
of integers is the set
hA
of all sums of
h

not necessarily distinct elements of
A
:

hA=\{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}:a_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i\in[1,h]\}.

For
n\in\mathbf{N}
, the set
A
of integers is an
additive
h
-basis for
n

or a
basis of order
h
for
n

if the sumset
hA
contains the interval
[0,n]
.
Bases of order
h
for
n
are also called

segment bases
and, in German,
Abschnittsbasen
.
For every nonempty finite set
A
of integers, let
\ell_{h}(A)
be the largest integer
n

such that
A
is a basis of order
h
for
n
,
that is, the largest integer
n
such that
[0,n]\subseteq hA
.
If
A\subseteq\mathbf{N}_{0}
and
0\notin A
, then
\ell_{h}(A)
is undefined.
If
A\subseteq\mathbf{N}_{0}
and
0\in A
but
1\notin A
, then
\ell_{h}(A)=0
.

Let
X
be a nonempty set of integers. For all positive integers
h
and
k
, let

L_{X,h}(k)=\{\ell_{h}(A):A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}(k)\}.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be an infinite set of integers. For all positive integers
h
and
k
,

L_{X,h}(k)\subseteq L_{X,h}(k+1).

If
\mathbf{N}_{0}\subseteq X
, then

L_{X,h}(k)\subsetneq L_{X,h}(k+1).

Proof.

If
n\in L_{X,h}(k)
, then
X
contains a subset
A
such that
|A|=k
and

\ell_{h}(A)=n
, that is,
[0,n]\subseteq hA
but
n+1\notin hA
.
Let
v=\max\{|a|:a\in A\}
. Then
hA\subseteq[-hv,hv]
and so
n\leq hv
.
Because
X
is an infinite set, there exists
a^{\prime}\in X
such that
a^{\prime}\notin A
and

|a^{\prime}|\geq(h-1)v+n+2
. The set
A^{\prime}=A\cup\{a^{\prime}\}
is a subset of
X
such that
|A^{\prime}|=k+1

and
[0,n]\subseteq hA\subseteq hA^{\prime}
. If
x\in hA^{\prime}\setminus hA
, then

x=b+ra^{\prime}
for some
r\in[1,h]
and
b^{\prime}\in(h-r)A
. Therefore,

|x|\geq r|a^{\prime}|-|b|\geq(h-1)v+n+2-(h-1)v=n+2

and so
n+1\notin hA^{\prime}
and
\ell_{h}(A^{\prime})=n\in L_{X,h}(k+1)
.
Therefore,
L_{X,h}(k)\subsetneq L_{X,h}(k+1)
.

Suppose that
X
contains
\mathbf{N}_{0}
. Let
n=\max L_{X,h}(k)
.
There exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}\in A
such that

a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{h}
and
n=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h}
.
Moreover,
n+1\notin hA
implies
a_{h}+1\notin A
, but
n\geq 0
implies

a_{h}\geq 0
and so
a_{h}+1\in\mathbf{N}_{0}\subseteq X
.
Therefore,
A^{\prime}=A\cup\{a_{h}+1\}\subseteq X
and satisfies
|A^{\prime}|=k+1
and

[0,n+1]\subseteq hA^{\prime}
. It follows that
\ell_{h}(A^{\prime})\geq n+1
and
so
\ell_{h}(A^{\prime})\in L_{X,h}(k+1)\setminus L_{X,h}(k)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2
.

The sequences
(n_{h}(k))_{k=1}^{\infty}
,
(m_{h}(k))_{k=1}^{\infty}
,

(n^{\sharp}_{h}(k))_{k=1}^{\infty}
, and
(m^{\sharp}_{h}(k))_{k=1}^{\infty}

are strictly increasing.

Proof.

Choose
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{Z}}(k)
with
\ell^{\sharp}_{h}(A)=m^{\sharp}_{h}(k)=n
and let
[c,c+n]\subseteq hA
.
There exist integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{h-1},a_{h}\in A
with
a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{h-1}\leq a_{h}

such that

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h-1}+a_{h}=c+n.

The maximality of

implies

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{h-1}+(a_{h}+1)=c+n+1\notin hA

and so
a_{h}+1\notin A
.
It follows that the set

satisfies
|A^{\prime}|=k+1
amd

and so

[c,c+n+1]=[c,c+n]\cup\{c+n+1\}\subseteq hA\cup\{c+n+1\}\subseteq hA^{\prime}.

Therefore,
m^{\sharp}_{h}(k+1)\geq\ell^{\sharp}_{h}(A^{\prime})\geq n+1>m^{\sharp}_{h}(k)
.
The same argument with

4.
A generalization of Sidon sets

The study of additive
h
-bases for
n
has consisted mostly of
(still unsuccessful) attempts to compute
or to estimate asymptotically the counting function
n_{h}(k)
,
often by constructing explicit families of such sets.
Also investigated is the number of subsets of
[0,n]
that are
h
-bases for
n

(for calculations with
h=2
, see OEIS A066062) and the size, denoted
k_{h}(n)
,
of the smallest subset of
[0,n]

that is an
h
-basis for
n
(for calculations of
k_{2}(n)
, see OEIS A066063). The other
counting functions constructed in Section
3
are largely unstudied.

Little is known about the possible arithmetical structures of interval
h
-bases for
n
.
In Section
5
we construct a new class of interval
2-bases. The proof uses a generalization of the concept of Sidon set.

A
Sidon set
is a finite or infinite set
A=\{a_{i}:i\in I\}
of positive integers
such that, for all
{r_{1}},{r_{2}},{s_{1}},{s_{2}}\in I
, we have

a_{r_{1}}+a_{r_{2}}=a_{s_{1}}+a_{s_{2}}

if and only if

\{{r_{1}},{r_{2}}\}=\{{s_{1}},{s_{2}}\}.

Equivalently, if

(1)

\{{r_{1}},{r_{2}}\}\neq\{{s_{1}},{s_{2}}\}

then

\left|(a_{r_{1}}+a_{r_{2}})-(a_{s_{1}}+a_{s_{2}})\right|\geq 1.

Let
\Delta>0
. The set
A
has a
\Delta
-separated sumset if (
1
) impllies

\left|(a_{r_{1}}+a_{r_{2}})-(a_{s_{1}}+a_{s_{2}})\right|\geq\Delta.

More generally, a
B_{h}
-set
is a set
A=\{a_{i}:i\in I\}
of integers such that,
for all
r_{1},\ldots,r_{h},s_{1},\ldots,s_{h}\in I
, conditions

(2)

r_{1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{h}\qqand s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{h}

and

(3)

r_{i}\neq s_{i}\qquad\text{for some $i\in[1,h]$}

imply

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{h}a_{r_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{h}a_{s_{i}}\right|\geq 1.

The set
A
has a
\Delta
-separated
h
-fold sumset
if
conditions (
2
) and (
3
) imply

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{h}a_{r_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{h}a_{s_{i}}\right|\geq\Delta.

Thus, a
B_{h}
-set is a set with a
1
-separated
h
-fold sumset.
If the set
A
has a
\Delta
-separated
h
-fold sumset
, then

A
has a
\Delta
-separated
h^{\prime}
-fold sumset
for all
h^{\prime}\in[1,h]
.

Let

. The set
A
is

\Delta
-separated of level

(1)

\Delta
-separated

-fold sumset for all

-separated of level

is

Theorem 4

\displaystyle B_{0}

\displaystyle=\{c+j+a_{j}-a_{i}:i,j\in[0,n]\}

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\{2c+i+j+a_{i}+a_{j}:i,j\in[0,n]\}

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\{-a_{i}-a_{j}:i,j\in[0,n]\}.

.

(1)

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle=\left|r_{1}+r_{2}-s_{1}-s_{2}+a_{r_{1}}+a_{r_{2}}-a_{s_{1}}-a_{s_{2}}\right|

Here is a simple observation about translating intervals in sumsets.

Theorem 5
.

Let
A
be a set of integers.

(a)

If
[c,c+n]\subseteq hA
, then there exist
r\in[0,h-1]

and
c_{0}\in\mathbf{Z}
such that
[r,r+n]\subseteq h(A-c_{0})
.

(b)

For all integers
h\geq 2
and
k\geq 2
,

m^{\sharp}_{h}(k)=\max\left\{n\in\mathbf{N}:[r,r+n]\subseteq hA\text{ for some }A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{Z}}(k)\text{ and }r\in[0,h-1]\right\}.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbf{Z}
with
[c,c+n]\subseteq hA
for some
c\in\mathbf{Z}
.
By the division algorithm, we have

c=hc_{0}+r

for some
c_{0}\in\mathbf{Z}
and
r\in[0,h-1]
.
The translate
A^{\prime}=A-c_{0}
satisfies
A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{Z}
and
|A^{\prime}|=|A|
. We have

\displaystyle hA^{\prime}

\displaystyle=h(A-c_{0})=hA-hc_{0}

\displaystyle\supseteq[c,c+n]-hc_{0}=[c-hc_{0},c-hc_{0}+n]

\displaystyle=[r,r+n]

and so

\displaystyle m^{\sharp}_{h}(k)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{n\in\mathbf{N}:[c,c+n]\subseteq hA\text{ for some }A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{Z}}(k)\text{ and }c\in\mathbf{Z}\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{n\in\mathbf{N}:[r,r+n]\subseteq hA\text{ for some }A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{Z}}(k)\text{ and }r\in[0,h-1]\right\}.

This completes the proof.

Elementary inequalities

The relations
\mathbf{N}_{0}\subseteq\mathbf{Z}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{N}_{0}}(k)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{Z}}(k)

imply
n^{\sharp}_{h}(k)\leq m^{\sharp}_{h}(k)
.
The following lemma removes the inequality.

Theorem 6
.

For all positive integers
h
and
k
,

n^{\sharp}_{h}(k)=m^{\sharp}_{h}(k).

Proof.

If
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{Z}}(k)
and
A\notin\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{N}_{0}}(k)
, then
\min(A)=-a_{0}<0
and

\min(hA)=-ha_{0}
.
Let
\ell^{\sharp}_{h}(A)=n
. There exists
c\in\mathbf{Z}
with
[c,c+n]\subseteq hA
.
Then
c\geq-ha_{0}
and
c+ha_{0}\geq 0
.
The translate
A^{\prime}=A+a_{0}
satisfies
\min(A^{\prime})=0
and
|A^{\prime}|=|A|=k
,
and so
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{N}_{0}}(k)
.
For
c^{\prime}=ha_{0}+c
, we have

\displaystyle hA^{\prime}

\displaystyle=h(A+a_{0})=hA+ha_{0}

\displaystyle\supseteq[c,c+n]+ha_{0}

\displaystyle=[ha_{0}+c,ha_{0}+c+n]

\displaystyle=[c^{\prime},c^{\prime}+n]

and so
\ell^{\sharp}_{h}(A^{\prime})\geq n=\ell^{\sharp}_{h}(A)
.
If there is a set
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{Z}}(k)
with
A\notin\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{N}_{0}}(k)

and
\ell^{\sharp}_{h}(A)=m^{\sharp}_{h}(k)
, then we obtain a set

A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{N}_{0}}(k)
with

n^{\sharp}_{h}(k)\geq\ell^{\sharp}_{h}(A^{\prime})\geq\ell^{\sharp}_{h}(A)=m^{\sharp}_{h}(k)

and so
n^{\sharp}_{h}(A)=m^{\sharp}_{h}(k)
.
This completes the proof.
