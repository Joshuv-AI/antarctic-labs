Spectral Properties of Zero-Divisor Graphs of Truncated Polynomial Rings
Bilal Ahmad Rather

Abstract
Let R \displaystyle R be a commutative ring with identity and let Z ∗ ​ ( R ) \displaystyle Z^{\ast}(R) denote the set of nonzero zero-divisors of R \displaystyle R . The zero-divisor graph Γ ⁡ ( R ) \displaystyle\varGamma(R) is the simple graph with vertex set V ⁡ ( Γ ⁡ ( R ) ) = Z ∗ ​ ( R ) \displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=Z^{\ast}(R) , where two distinct vertices x , y ∈ Z ∗ ​ ( R ) \displaystyle x,y\in Z^{\ast}(R) are adjacent if and only if x ​ y = 0 \displaystyle xy=0 in R \displaystyle R . In this paper we investigate the zero-divisor graph of the truncated polynomial ring R = ℤ p ​ [ x ] / ⟨ x c ⟩ , \displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{c}\rangle, for c ∈ ℕ . \displaystyle c\in\mathbb{N}. We determine the spectrum of the A α \displaystyle A_{\alpha} -matrix associated with Γ ⁡ ( R ) \displaystyle\varGamma(R) , and, as special cases, explicitly obtain both the adjacency spectrum and the signless Laplacian spectrum of Γ ⁡ ( R ) \displaystyle\varGamma(R) . Furthermore, we prove that the Laplacian eigenvalues, as well as the distance eigenvalues, of these graphs are all integers.

Introduction

A (finite, simple) graph
\displaystyle G=(V(G),E(G))
consists of a finite vertex set
\displaystyle V(G)
and an edge set

\displaystyle E(G)\subseteq\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in V(G),u\neq v\}
. The
order
and
size
of
\displaystyle G
are

\displaystyle n=|V(G)|
and
\displaystyle m=|E(G)|
, respectively. For vertices
\displaystyle u,v\in V(G)
, we write
\displaystyle u\sim v,
if

\displaystyle\{u,v\}\in E(G)
. The
open neighbourhood
of a vertex
\displaystyle v
is
\displaystyle N_{G}(v)=\{u\in V(G):u\sim v\},

and the
degree
of
\displaystyle v
is
\displaystyle d_{G}(v)=|N_{G}(v)|
(or simply
\displaystyle d(v)
if the graph is clear).
An
independent set
in
\displaystyle G
is a subset of vertices no two of which are adjacent. A

clique
is a vertex set
\displaystyle X\subseteq V(G)
such that the induced subgraph
\displaystyle G[X]
is
complete. A vertex is universal if it is adjacent to all other vertices of
\displaystyle G
. The complete graph on
\displaystyle n
vertices is denoted by
\displaystyle K_{n}
. For other notations, see
[
8
]
.

The
adjacency matrix
of
\displaystyle G
is the
\displaystyle n\times n
matrix
\displaystyle A(G)=(a_{ij}),
where

a_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }v_{i}\sim v_{j},\\[2.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The matrix
\displaystyle A(G)
is real symmetric, hence all its eigenvalues are real. We list them in
non-increasing order as
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}(G).

The multiset
\displaystyle\{\lambda_{1}(G),\dots,\lambda_{n}(G)\}
is called the
adjacency spectrum
(or spectra) of
\displaystyle G
.
The
characteristic polynomial
of a real matrix
\displaystyle M
is
\displaystyle\Phi(M,\lambda)=\det(\lambda I-M),

where
\displaystyle I
is the identity matrix. The
energy
of a graph
\displaystyle G
, introduced by Gutman
in the context of theoretical chemistry, is defined by

\mathcal{E}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}(G)|.

The energy of
\displaystyle G
has been extensively studied, see

[
10
,
9
,
15
,
18
]
and the references therein. Let
\displaystyle D(G)
be the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees, that is,
\displaystyle D(G)=\operatorname{diag}(d(v_{1}),\dots,d(v_{n})).
The
Laplacian matrix
of
\displaystyle G
is defined as
\displaystyle L(G)=D(G)-A(G),
while the
signless Laplacian
is defined by
\displaystyle Q(G)=D(G)+A(G).

Both
\displaystyle L(G)
and
\displaystyle Q(G)
are real symmetric and positive semidefinite (definite) matrices. Their spectra
encode a number of combinatorial properties of the underlying graph, see

[
8
,
7
]
.

Motivated by unifying the adjacency and signless Laplacian theories, Nikiforov
[
19
]
, introduced the one–parameter family of matrices

A_{\alpha}(G)=\alpha D(G)+(1-\alpha)A(G),\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq 1.

For some special values of
\displaystyle\alpha
,
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(G)
matrix reduces to classical matrices, like

A_{0}(G)=A(G),\qquad A_{1}(G)=D(G),\qquad 2A_{1/2}(G)=D(G)+A(G)=Q(G).

Moreover, for any distinct
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]
one has

\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\bigl(A_{\alpha}(G)-A_{\beta}(G)\bigr)=D(G)-A(G)=L(G).

So, the adjacency, the Laplacian and the Laplacian matrices can be recovered from the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
–family. Nikiforov
[
19
]
, provided basic properties of
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
matrix, like positive definiteness, inequalities for the spectral radius (the largest eigenvalue), and spectra of various graph families. The
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
spectra of power graphs, energy and other properties are given in
[
24
,
22
,
25
]
. Nikiforov et. al.
[
20
]
presented
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
spectra of trees, Alhevaz
[
1
]
obtained the inequalities for the spectral radius of the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
matrix, and Liu, Xue and Shu
[
16
]
found results on
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
spectral radius and related invariants.

The
distance matrix
of a connected graph
\displaystyle G
is the
\displaystyle n\times n
matrix
\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{dist}}(G)

whose
\displaystyle(i,j)
–entry is the graph distance
\displaystyle d_{G}(v_{i},v_{j})
. Let

\operatorname{Tr}(G)=\operatorname{diag}(t(v_{1}),\dots,t(v_{n})),\qquad t(v_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{G}(v_{i},v_{j}),

be the diagonal matrix of vertex transmissions. Following Aouchiche and Hansen

[
4
]
, the
distance Laplacian
of
\displaystyle G
is defined by
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(G)=\operatorname{Tr}(G)-D_{\mathrm{dist}}(G).

The spectral properties of
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(G)
form an analogue of Laplacian spectral theory
for distance–based matrices and have attracted considerable attention in recent years

[
4
,
3
]
. A detailed survey can be seen in
[
5
,
26
]
.

A real square matrix
\displaystyle M
is called
integral
if all its eigenvalues are integers. A graph

\displaystyle G
is said to be
integral
(with respect to a given graph matrix) if the eigenvalues of
that matrix are integers. Integral spectra arise naturally in several contexts: they appear in
the study of combinatorial designs and strongly regular graphs, and they are related to algebraic
graph theory and number–theoretic questions, see, for example,

[
8
]
. Harary and Schwenk first studied the problem of the integral graphs
[
11
]
. Klotz and Sander
[
14
]
presented properties of integral graphs. Some circulant integral graphs can be seen in
[
28
]
. Integral Laplacian or
distance Laplacian spectra are particularly attractive, since they combine structural graph
information with arithmetic constraints and often lead to strong classification results. If a graph has an eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda
with multiplicity
\displaystyle k
, then we represent it by
\displaystyle\lambda^{[k]}
. In the present work, we are interested in graphs for which various matrices are integral, and we determine their spectra explicitly for a family of
zero-divisor graphs.

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle x,y

\displaystyle xy=0

[

, and has since been studied in a variety of settings, see, for instance,

[

,
17
,
12
,
21
,

,

Beyond adjacency spectra, Laplacian and signless Laplacian spectra, as well as other spectral

2
Spectra of zero-divisor graph of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime and let
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer. We consider the quotient ring
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{c}\rangle.
The ring
\displaystyle R
is a finite commutative local ring with maximal ideal
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}=\langle x\rangle
and residue field
\displaystyle R/\mathfrak{m}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. As a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-vector space,
\displaystyle R
has basis
\displaystyle\{1,x,\dots,x^{c-1}\}
, and hence
\displaystyle|R|=p^{c}
. Moreover,
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}
is nilpotent with
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{c}=\langle x^{c}\rangle=0
. So,
\displaystyle R
is an Artinian principal ideal ring of length
\displaystyle c
which is not reduced. The following result gives the structural properties of the zero-divisor graph of ring
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime and let
\displaystyle a=2b
, where
\displaystyle b\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{2b}),
and
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
be its zero-divisor graph. Then the following hold.

1.

The order of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle|V(\varGamma(R))|=|Z(R)\setminus\{0\}|=p^{2b-1}-1
, and its vertex set can be written as
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=L\cup U,
where
\displaystyle L=\bigcup_{k=1}^{b-1}V_{k},~U=\bigcup_{k=b}^{2b-1}V_{k},
and
\displaystyle|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i},
for
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,2b-1.

2.

In
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
,
\displaystyle C=\bigcup_{k=b}^{2b-1}V_{k}
is a clique of size
\displaystyle p^{b}-1
, and
\displaystyle L=\bigcup_{k=1}^{b-1}V_{k}
is an independent set of size
\displaystyle p^{2b-1}-p^{b}
.

3.

For
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, its neighbors are exactly the vertices in
\displaystyle\bigcup_{j:i+j\geq 2b}V_{j}
, and if
\displaystyle i\geq b
, then
\displaystyle v
is also adjacent to all other vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
.

4.

The diameter of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle 2
, and its girth is
\displaystyle 3.

Proof.

With the description of
\displaystyle R
as an
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}
–vector space, the characterization of
units by the constant term, and the identification of nonzero zero-divisors with the
nonzero elements of
\displaystyle(x)
are standard for
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{2b})
. The
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}
–vector space with multiplication modulo
\displaystyle x^{2b}
is

R=\Bigl\{\sum_{k=0}^{2b-1}a_{k}x^{k}:a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\Bigr\}.

So, all terms of degree greater or equal to
\displaystyle 2b
vanish. An element
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2b-1}a_{k}x^{k}
is a unit if and only if
\displaystyle a_{0}\neq 0
.
Since
\displaystyle R
is finite, every nonunit is a zero-divisor, denoted by
\displaystyle Z(R)
. Thus, the set of nonzero zero-divisors is

Z(R)\setminus\{0\}=\Bigl\{\sum_{k=1}^{2b-1}a_{k}x^{k}:(a_{1},\dots,a_{2b-1})\neq(0,\dots,0)\Bigr\},

which are precisely the the nonzero elements of the ideal
\displaystyle(x)
, and hence we have

|V(\varGamma(R))|=|Z(R)\setminus\{0\}|=p^{2b-1}-1.

For (1), every nonzero element
\displaystyle f
of
\displaystyle(x)
has a smallest degree
\displaystyle k
with nonzero coefficient,
denoted by
\displaystyle\operatorname{mindeg}(f)
. For a nonzero zero-divisor
\displaystyle f\in Z(R)
, define the minimal degree

\operatorname{mindeg}(f)=\min\bigl\{k\in\{1,\dots,2b-1\}:\text{the coefficient of $\displaystyle x^{k}$ in $\displaystyle f$ is nonzero}\bigr\}.

Fixing
\displaystyle a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
and allowing the
higher coefficients to vary freely gives cardinalities of the sets
\displaystyle V_{i}
. This partitions the vertex set
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=Z(R)\setminus\{0\}
into

V(\varGamma(R))=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\dots\cup V_{b-1}\cup V_{b}\cup V_{b+1}\cup\dots\cup V_{2b-1},

where

V_{i}=\left\{\sum_{k=i}^{2b-1}a_{k}x^{k}:a_{i}\neq 0\right\},\qquad 1\leq i\leq 2b-1.

For each
\displaystyle i
, the coefficient
\displaystyle a_{i}
can be any nonzero element of
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
and the coefficients
\displaystyle a_{i+1},\dots,a_{2b-1}
are arbitrary. Hence, we obtain
\displaystyle|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i},
for
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,2b-1,

and

\sum_{i=1}^{2b-1}|V_{i}|=(p-1)\sum_{t=0}^{2b-2}p^{t}=p^{2b-1}-1=|V(\varGamma(R))|.

We decompose
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))
into subsets
\displaystyle L=\bigcup_{k=1}^{b-1}V_{k},
and
\displaystyle U=\bigcup_{k=b}^{2b-1}V_{k},
so that
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=L\cup U.

For (2), let
\displaystyle f,g\in Z(R)\setminus\{0\}
with
\displaystyle\operatorname{mindeg}(f)=i
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{mindeg}(g)=j
, where
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq 2b-1
. Consider

f=a_{i}x^{i}+\text{(higher degree terms)},~\text{and}~g=b_{j}x^{j}+\text{(higher degree terms)},

where
\displaystyle a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.
In product
\displaystyle fg
, the smallest degree term is
\displaystyle a_{i}b_{j}x^{i+j}

\displaystyle x^{i+j}
with coefficient
\displaystyle a_{i}b_{j}
, as all other products of coefficients have strictly larger degree. Thus
\displaystyle fg=0
if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b.
Indeed, if
\displaystyle i+j\leq 2b-1
, then the
\displaystyle x^{i+j}
term is nonzero in
\displaystyle R
. If
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b
, then every term of
\displaystyle fg
has degree
\displaystyle\geq 2b
and vanishes modulo
\displaystyle(x^{2b})
. Consequently, adjacency in
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is completely determined by the minimal degrees, that is,
\displaystyle f\in V_{i},g\in V_{j},
with
\displaystyle f\neq g
, then
\displaystyle f
is adjacent to
\displaystyle g
if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b.
Thus, it follows that, for each
\displaystyle i
, two distinct vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
are adjacent if and only if
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b
, that is,
\displaystyle i\geq b
. Thus,
\displaystyle V_{i}
is an independent set, if
\displaystyle i<b,
and a clique, if
\displaystyle i\geq b.
In particular,
\displaystyle L=\bigcup_{k=1}^{b-1}V_{k}
is a disjoint union of independent subsets. For
\displaystyle i\neq j
, all edges between
\displaystyle V_{i}
and
\displaystyle V_{j}
either exist or do not exist simultaneously. In particular, each vertex of
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to
\displaystyle V_{j}
, if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b.
We note that if
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq b-1
, then
\displaystyle i+j\leq 2b-2<2b
, so there are no edges at all inside
\displaystyle L
. On the other hand, for all
\displaystyle i,j\geq b
, we have
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b
. So, every pair of vertices in
\displaystyle C=\bigcup_{k=b}^{2b-1}V_{k}=V_{b}\cup U
is adjacent. Thus
\displaystyle C
induces a clique, while
\displaystyle L
is an independent set.

For (3), with
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, its neighbors are exactly the vertices in
\displaystyle\bigcup_{j:i+j\geq 2b}V_{j}
, that is, each vertex of
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to every
\displaystyle V_{j}
, if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b.
Also, for
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, if
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b
, that is,
\displaystyle i\geq b
, then
\displaystyle v
is also adjacent to all other vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
.
Using

\sum_{j=m}^{2b-1}|V_{j}|=p^{2b-m}-1,\quad\text{for }1\leq m\leq 2b-1,

and with
\displaystyle m=2b-i
, we get the total number of vertices in subsets
\displaystyle V_{2b-i},\dots,V_{2b-1}
as
\displaystyle\sum_{j=2b-i}^{2b-1}|V_{j}|=p^{i}-1.

This includes
\displaystyle V_{i}
itself, provided
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b
, or
\displaystyle i\geq b
. Thus, for
\displaystyle v\in V_{i},
we have

\deg(v)=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&\text{if }i<b,\\
p^{i}-2,&\text{if }i\geq b.\end{cases}

In particular, for
\displaystyle i=2b-1
, it is clear that
\displaystyle i\geq b
, so each vertex in
\displaystyle V_{2b-1}
is adjacent to every
\displaystyle V_{i}
in
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
, and degree of
\displaystyle v\in V_{2b-1}
is
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p^{2b-1}-2.
Thus
\displaystyle V_{2b-1}
consists of
\displaystyle p-1
universal vertices. For the middle subset with
\displaystyle i=b
, each vertex in
\displaystyle V_{b}
has degree
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p^{b}-2,
for
\displaystyle v\in V_{b},
and is adjacent to all vertices in
\displaystyle C
except itself.

As each
\displaystyle V_{i}
induces a clique if
\displaystyle i\geq b
and each vertex of
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex of
\displaystyle V_{i}
, since
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b
for
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq j\leq 2b-1.
Thus, the union of
\displaystyle V_{i}

\displaystyle C=\bigcup_{k=b}^{2b-1}V_{k}
, and its size is

|C|=\sum_{k=b}^{2b-1}|V_{k}|=\sum_{k=b}^{2b-1}(p-1)p^{2b-1-k}=p^{2b-b}-1=p^{b}-1.

We claim that the clique number of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle\omega(\varGamma(R))=p^{b}-1
. Let
\displaystyle K
be any clique in
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
, and write

i_{0}=\min\{\operatorname{mindeg}(v):v\in K\}.

Then
\displaystyle K\subseteq\bigcup_{j\geq i_{0}}V_{j}
, and
\displaystyle K
contains some vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i_{0}}
.
If
\displaystyle w\in V_{j}
lies in
\displaystyle K
, adjacency with a vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i_{0}}
forces
\displaystyle i_{0}+j\geq 2b
, that is,
\displaystyle j\geq 2b-i_{0}
. Thus every vertex of
\displaystyle K
lies in
\displaystyle\bigcup_{j=m}^{2b-1}V_{j},
with
\displaystyle m=\max\{i_{0},2b-i_{0}\}.
Hence, it follows that

|K|\leq\sum_{j=m}^{2b-1}|V_{j}|=p^{2b-m}-1.

It is clear that
\displaystyle m\geq b
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq i_{0}\leq 2b-1
, with equality
\displaystyle m=b
, only
when
\displaystyle i_{0}=b
. Thus,
\displaystyle|K|\leq p^{2b-b}-1=p^{b}-1,
and since
\displaystyle C
has size
\displaystyle p^{b}-1
, and we obtain
\displaystyle\omega(\varGamma(R))=p^{b}-1.
In particular, when
\displaystyle b=1
(that is,
\displaystyle a=2
),
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)\cong K_{p-1}
and
\displaystyle\omega(\varGamma(R))=p-1
. Furthermore,
\displaystyle L=\bigcup_{k=1}^{b-1}V_{k}
is an independent set, so
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=L\cup C
. For each
\displaystyle i<b
, the subset
\displaystyle V_{i}
is an independent set of size
\displaystyle|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i}.
In particular,
\displaystyle V_{1}
is an independent set of size
\displaystyle|V_{1}|=(p-1)p^{2b-2}.

\displaystyle C

The following result gives the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
spectra of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime and
\displaystyle a=2b
with
\displaystyle b\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
be the zero-divisor graph of
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{2b})
. Then the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
–spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is as follows: For each
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,2b-1
, there is an eigenvalue

\lambda_{i}=\begin{cases}\alpha\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr),&\text{if }1\leq i\leq b-1,\\[3.0pt]
\alpha\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr)-1,&\text{if }b\leq i\leq 2b-1,\end{cases}

with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i}-1
. The remaining
\displaystyle 2b-1
eigenvalues are the eigenvalues

\displaystyle\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{2b-1}
of the
\displaystyle(2b-1)\times(2b-1)
matrix

\displaystyle B=(B_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2b-1}
, where

B=\alpha D^{\ast}+(1-\alpha)Q,\quad\text{with}\quad D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{2b-1})

and
\displaystyle Q=(Q_{ij})
the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle A(\varGamma(R))

with respect to the partition
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{2b-1}V_{i}
, given by

Q_{ij}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i=j,\ 1\leq i\leq b-1\ (2i<2b),\\[3.0pt]
n_{i}-1,&\text{if }i=j,\ b\leq i\leq 2b-1\ (2i\geq 2b),\\[3.0pt]
n_{j},&\text{if }i\neq j,\ i+j\geq 2b,\\[3.0pt]
0,&\text{if }i\neq j,\ i+j<2b.\end{cases}

Equivalently,

B_{ij}=\begin{cases}\alpha d_{i},&\text{if }i=j,\ 1\leq i\leq b-1,\\[3.0pt]
\alpha d_{i}+(1-\alpha)(n_{i}-1),&\text{if }i=j,\ b\leq i\leq 2b-1,\\[3.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{j},&\text{if }i\neq j,\ i+j\geq 2b,\\[3.0pt]
0,&\text{if }i\neq j,\ i+j<2b,\end{cases}

where for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 2b-1
, the degree of any
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
is

d_{i}=\deg(v)=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&\text{if }1\leq i\leq b-1\ (2i<2b),\\[3.0pt]
p^{i}-2,&\text{if }b\leq i\leq 2b-1\ (2i\geq 2b).\end{cases}

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, the vertex set of the zero-divisor graph
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is decomposed into partition of subsets
\displaystyle V_{i}
with
\displaystyle n_{i}=|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i},
and
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2b-1}n_{i}=p^{2b-1}-1.
By the adjacency rule
\displaystyle f\in V_{i},g\in V_{j},
with
\displaystyle f\neq g
and
\displaystyle f
is adjacent to
\displaystyle g
if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b.
Thus, for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b-1
, then
\displaystyle 2i<2b
, so
\displaystyle V_{i}
is an independent set, and no edges inside
\displaystyle V_{i}
. A vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{j}
with
\displaystyle j\geq 2b-i
. The degree of
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
is
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p^{i}-1
when
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b-1
. If
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq 2b-1
, then
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b
, so
\displaystyle V_{i}
is a clique, and
each vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to the other
\displaystyle n_{i}-1
vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}

and to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{j}
for
\displaystyle j\geq 2b-i
,
\displaystyle j\neq i
. Thus, for
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, the degree of vertex
\displaystyle v
is
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p^{i}-2
, when
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq 2b-1
. Thus, the degree of every vertex is

d_{i}=\deg(v)=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&\text{if }1\leq i\leq b-1\ (2i<2b),\\[3.0pt]
p^{i}-2,&\text{if }b\leq i\leq 2b-1\ (2i\geq 2b).\end{cases}

Now, for each
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,2b-1
, define

W_{i}=\Bigl\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{V(\varGamma(R))}:\mathrm{supp}(x)\subseteq V_{i},\ \sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=0\Bigr\},

where for
\displaystyle x=(x_{v})_{v\in V(\varGamma(R))}\in\mathbb{R}^{V(\varGamma(R))},
we have
\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{v\in V(\varGamma(R)):x_{v}\neq 0\}.
Then
\displaystyle\dim W_{i}=|V_{i}|-1=n_{i}-1
. We show that each
\displaystyle W_{i}
is invariant under
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
and compute the eigenvalue. Let
\displaystyle x\in W_{i}
, so
\displaystyle x
is supported in
\displaystyle V_{i}
and
\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=0
. For
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b-1
, and
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, there are no neighbors of
\displaystyle v
in
\displaystyle V_{i}
, and
\displaystyle x
vanishes on all
other subsets
\displaystyle V_{j}
, so it implies that
\displaystyle(Ax)_{v}=0
. For
\displaystyle v\in V_{k}
with
\displaystyle k\neq i
, if
\displaystyle i+k<2b
, then
\displaystyle v
has no neighbors in
\displaystyle V_{i}
, and hence
\displaystyle(Ax)_{v}=0
. If
\displaystyle i+k\geq 2b
, then
\displaystyle v
is adjacent to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
, hence by the defining condition of
\displaystyle W_{i}
, we have

(Ax)_{v}=\sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}=0.

Thus
\displaystyle Ax=0
. On the other hand,
\displaystyle Dx=d_{i}x=(p^{i}-1)x
on
\displaystyle V_{i}
, and therefore

A_{\alpha}x=\alpha Dx+(1-\alpha)Ax=\alpha(p^{i}-1)x.

So every nonzero vector in
\displaystyle W_{i}
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue

\lambda_{i}=\alpha(p^{i}-1),\quad 1\leq i\leq b-1.

For
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq 2b-1
, with
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, the neighbors of
\displaystyle v
in the support of
\displaystyle x
comprise the other vertices of
\displaystyle V_{i}
. Thus, we have

(Ax)_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in V_{i}\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}x_{u}=\sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}-x_{v}=-x_{v}.

For
\displaystyle v\in V_{k}
with
\displaystyle k\neq i
, if
\displaystyle i+k<2b
, then
\displaystyle v
lacks neighbors in
\displaystyle V_{i}
, so it gives
\displaystyle(Ax)_{v}=0
.
If
\displaystyle i+k\geq 2b

\displaystyle v
is adjacent to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
, and it gives

(Ax)_{v}=\sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}=0.

Hence
\displaystyle Ax=-x
on its support, and
\displaystyle Dx=d_{i}x=(p^{i}-2)x
on
\displaystyle V_{i}

A_{\alpha}x=\alpha Dx+(1-\alpha)Ax=\alpha(p^{i}-2)x+(1-\alpha)(-x)=\bigl(\alpha(p^{i}-1)-1\bigr)x.

So, each nonzero vector in
\displaystyle W_{i}
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\alpha(p^{i}-1)-1,
for
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq 2b-1.
So,
\displaystyle W_{i}
is
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
–invariant and contributes an eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}
with multiplicity
\displaystyle\dim W_{i}=n_{i}-1
. Summing over
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,2b-1
, the overall dimension of
\displaystyle W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2b-1}W_{i}
is

\sum_{i=1}^{2b-1}(n_{i}-1)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2b-1}n_{i}\right)-(2b-1)=\bigl(p^{2b-1}-1\bigr)-(2b-1)=p^{2b-1}-2b.

Now, take in consideration vectors that are constant on each subset
\displaystyle V_{i}
as

U_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{V(\varGamma(R))}:x\text{ is constant on each }V_{i}\}.

Then
\displaystyle\dim U_{0}=2b-1
, as
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{V(\varGamma(R))}=W\oplus U_{0},
and

\dim W+\dim U_{0}=(p^{2b-1}-2b)+(2b-1)=p^{2b-1}-1=|V(\varGamma(R))|.

Clearly, the partition
\displaystyle\{V_{i}\}
is equitable as every vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
has the same number
of neighbors in each
\displaystyle V_{j}
. Thus
\displaystyle U_{0}
is invariant under
\displaystyle A
, and hence under
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
. Let
\displaystyle x\in U_{0}
and write
\displaystyle x_{i}
for the common value of
\displaystyle x
on
\displaystyle V_{i}

\displaystyle x

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{2b-1})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2b-1}.

For
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}

(Ax)_{v}=\sum_{j=1}^{2b-1}Q_{ij}x_{j},

where
\displaystyle Q_{ij}
is the number of neighbors in
\displaystyle V_{j}
of a given vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}

By the adjacency rule
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b
in
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)

\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b-1

\displaystyle 2i<2b

\displaystyle V_{i}

\displaystyle Q_{ii}=0.

For
\displaystyle j\neq i
, a vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to all vertices of
\displaystyle V_{j}
if
\displaystyle j\geq 2b-i
, and to none otherwise. Thus, we have

Q_{ij}=\begin{cases}n_{j},&j\neq i,\ j\geq 2b-i,\\[2.0pt]
0,&j\neq i,\ j<2b-i.\end{cases}

If
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq 2b-1
, then
\displaystyle V_{i}
is a clique, so each vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent
to the other
\displaystyle n_{i}-1
vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
and to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{j}
for
\displaystyle j\geq 2b-i
,

\displaystyle j\neq i
. Hence, we have

Q_{ii}=n_{i}-1,\qquad Q_{ij}=\begin{cases}n_{j},&j\neq i,\ j\geq 2b-i,\\[2.0pt]
0,&j\neq i,\ j<2b-i.\end{cases}

Now, on
\displaystyle U_{0}
it is evident that
\displaystyle Ax
is equivalent to
\displaystyle Q\mathbf{x},
and
\displaystyle Dx
is equivalent to
\displaystyle D^{\ast}\mathbf{x},
where
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{2b-1})
.
Therefore, we obtain
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}x=\alpha Dx+(1-\alpha)Ax
is equivalent to
\displaystyle\bigl(\alpha D^{\ast}+(1-\alpha)Q\bigr)\mathbf{x}=B\mathbf{x}.

Thus, the restriction of
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
to
\displaystyle U_{0}
is represented by
\displaystyle B

in the basis
\displaystyle\{\mathbf{1}_{V_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{V_{2b-1}}\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{V_{i}}
is the indicator of
\displaystyle V_{i}

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{V_{i}}
is a vector which is
\displaystyle 1
for all coordinate
\displaystyle x_{i}
of eigenvector
\displaystyle X
on
\displaystyle V_{i},
and
\displaystyle 0
on other sets
\displaystyle V_{j}

\displaystyle\dim U_{0}=2b-1

(of size

\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\[2.0pt]

The bottom–left block
\displaystyle B^{(21)}
(of size
\displaystyle b\times(b-1)
) is

B^{(21)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&(1-\alpha)n_{b-1}\\[2.0pt]
0&0&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{b-2}&(1-\alpha)n_{b-1}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\[2.0pt]
0&(1-\alpha)n_{2}&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{b-2}&(1-\alpha)n_{b-1}\\[2.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{1}&(1-\alpha)n_{2}&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{b-2}&(1-\alpha)n_{b-1}\end{pmatrix},

Finally, the bottom–right block
\displaystyle B^{(22)}
(of size
\displaystyle b\times b
) is

B^{(22)}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha d_{b}+(1-\alpha)(n_{b}-1)&(1-\alpha)n_{b+1}&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{2b-1}\\[2.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{b}&\alpha d_{b+1}+(1-\alpha)(n_{b+1}-1)&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{2b-1}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[2.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{b}&(1-\alpha)n_{b+1}&\cdots&\alpha d_{2b-1}+(1-\alpha)(n_{2b-1}-1)\end{pmatrix},

where, for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 2b-1
,

d_{i}=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&1\leq i\leq b-1,\\[3.0pt]
p^{i}-2,&b\leq i\leq 2b-1,\end{cases}\qquad n_{i}=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i}.

The following are the immediate consequences of Theorem
2.2
, and gives the adjacency and the singless Laplacian spectra of
\displaystyle\varGamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle).

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime and
\displaystyle a=2b
with
\displaystyle b\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\displaystyle G=\varGamma(R)
be the zero-divisor graph of
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{2b}).
From
2.2
, let
\displaystyle V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{2b-1}V_{i}
be the partition of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
with

\displaystyle n_{i}=|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i}
, and

d_{i}=\deg(v)=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&1\leq i\leq b-1,\\[3.0pt]
p^{i}-2,&b\leq i\leq 2b-1,\end{cases}\quad v\in V_{i}.

Let
\displaystyle Q^{\ast}
be the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle A(G)
with respect to this partition, as in Theorem
2.2
, and let
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{2b-1}).

Then, we have the following.

1.

The adjacency spectrum of
\displaystyle G
is

\{0\}^{\left[\sum_{i=1}^{b-1}(n_{i}-1)\right]}\cup\{-1\}^{\left[\sum_{i=b}^{2b-1}(n_{i}-1)\right]}\cup\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{2b-1}\},

where
\displaystyle\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{2b-1}
are the eigenvalues of the quotient matrix
\displaystyle Q^{\ast}
.

2.

The signless Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle G
, where
\displaystyle Q(G)=D(G)+A(G)
, is

\bigcup_{i=1}^{b-1}\{(p^{i}-1)^{[n_{i}-1]}\}\cup\bigcup_{i=b}^{2b-1}\{(p^{i}-3)^{[n_{i}-1]}\}\cup\{\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{2b-1}\},

where
\displaystyle\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{2b-1}
are the eigenvalues of the
\displaystyle(2b-1)\times(2b-1)
matrix
\displaystyle D^{\ast}+Q^{\ast}.

Proof.

(1) The adjacency matrix is
\displaystyle A(G)=A_{0}(G)
. With
\displaystyle\alpha=0
in Theorem
2.2
, we have for

\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b-1,\lambda_{i}(0)=0,
with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1,
and for
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq 2b-1
,
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(0)=-1,
with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1.
Theorem
2.2
, further states that the remaining
\displaystyle 2b-1
eigenvalues of
\displaystyle A_{0}(G)
are precisely the eigenvalues of

B(0)=0\cdot D^{\ast}+(1-0)Q^{\ast}=Q^{\ast},

which is the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle A(G)
with respect to the partition
\displaystyle\{V_{i}\}
.

(2) The signless Laplacian of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is

Q(G)=D(G)+A(G)=2A_{1/2}(G).

If
\displaystyle\mu
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle A_{1/2}(G)
of multiplicity
\displaystyle m
, then
\displaystyle 2\mu
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle Q(G)
with the same multiplicity
\displaystyle m
. From Theorem
2.2
, for
\displaystyle\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2}
and
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b-1,
we obtain

\lambda_{i}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr),\quad\text{ with multiplicity }n_{i}-1,

and for
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq 2b-1
,

\lambda_{i}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr)-1=\frac{p^{i}-3}{2},\quad\text{with multiplicity }n_{i}-1.

Thus, the corresponding eigenvalues of
\displaystyle Q(G)=2A_{1/2}(G)
are

p^{i}-1,\quad 1\leq i\leq b-1,\quad\text{and}\quad p^{i}-3,\quad b\leq i\leq 2b-1,

with multiplicities
\displaystyle n_{i}-1
. For the remaining
\displaystyle 2b-1
eigenvalues, we see that

B\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{2}D^{\ast}+\frac{1}{2}Q^{\ast}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(D^{\ast}+Q^{\ast}\bigr).

Hence the remaining
\displaystyle 2b-1
eigenvalues of
\displaystyle Q(G)
are exactly twice the eigenvalues of

\displaystyle B\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)
, that is, they are the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle D^{\ast}+Q^{\ast}
.

The following consequence of Theorem
2.2
show that the Laplacian matrix of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)\cong\varGamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle)
has only integer eigenvalues.

Corollary 2.5
.

The Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is integral, and its spectrum is

\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq 2b-1\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}\bigl\{(p^{i}-1)^{[n_{i}]}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{(p^{b}-1)^{[n_{b}-1]}\bigr\}.

Proof.

Since, for any
\displaystyle\alpha\neq\beta
,

\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\bigl(A_{\alpha}(G)-A_{\beta}(G)\bigr)=D(G)-A(G)=L(G).

So, by Theorem
2.2
, we get

\alpha\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr)-\beta\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr)=(\alpha-\beta)(p^{i}-1).

Thus, for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b-1,
it follows that
\displaystyle p^{i}-1
is the Laplacian eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
with
\displaystyle n_{i}-1=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i}-1
. Similarly, for
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq 2b-1,
we see that

\alpha\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr)-1-\beta\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr)+1=(\alpha-\beta)(p^{i}-1)

is the eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i}-1
. By Theorem
2.2
, the quotient of
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(\varGamma(R))

with respect to the partition
\displaystyle\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2b-1}
is

B(\alpha)=\alpha D^{\ast}+(1-\alpha)Q^{\ast},

where
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{2b-1})
and
\displaystyle Q^{\ast}
is the quotient of
\displaystyle A(G)
. Hence for any
\displaystyle\alpha\neq\beta
,

\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\bigl(B(\alpha)-B(\beta)\bigr)=D^{\ast}-Q^{\ast}=:\overline{L}.

The
\displaystyle(2b-1)\times(2b-1)
matrix
\displaystyle\overline{L}
is therefore the quotient of the Laplacian
\displaystyle L(G)
with respect to
the partition
\displaystyle\{V_{i}\}
. With the description of
\displaystyle Q^{\ast}
and
\displaystyle d_{i}
from Theorem
2.2
, we have

\overline{L}_{ij}=\begin{cases}d_{i},&i=j,\ 1\leq i\leq b-1,\\
d_{i}-(n_{i}-1)=p^{i}-1-n_{i},&i=j,\ b\leq i\leq 2b-1,\\
-n_{j},&i\neq j,\ i+j\geq 2b,\\
0,&i\neq j,\ i+j<2b.\end{cases}

In explicit matrix form,
\displaystyle\overline{L}
is the
\displaystyle(2b-1)\times(2b-1)
matrix

\overline{L}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0&0&-n_{2b-1}\\[3.0pt]
0&d_{2}&0&\cdots&0&-n_{2b-2}&-n_{2b-1}\\[3.0pt]
0&0&d_{3}&\cdots&-n_{2b-3}&-n_{2b-2}&-n_{2b-1}\\[3.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\[3.0pt]
0&0&-n_{3}&\cdots&p^{2b-3}-1-n_{2b-3}&-n_{2b-2}&-n_{2b-1}\\[3.0pt]
0&-n_{2}&-n_{3}&\cdots&-n_{2b-3}&p^{2b-2}-1-n_{2b-2}&-n_{2b-1}\\[3.0pt]
-n_{1}&-n_{2}&-n_{3}&\cdots&-n_{2b-3}&-n_{2b-2}&p^{2b-1}-1-n_{2b-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Now, we show that see that
\displaystyle\{0\}
along with
\displaystyle p^{i}-1
are the eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 2b-1
except
\displaystyle i=b.

We let
\displaystyle N=2b-1
and recall that
\displaystyle n_{j}=(p-1)p^{N-j}
. So, with
\displaystyle 1\leq a\leq b\leq N
, w ehave

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=a}^{b}n_{j}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(p-1)\sum_{j=a}^{b}p^{N-j}=(p-1)\sum_{t=N-b}^{N-a}p^{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(p-1)\frac{p^{N-a+1}-p^{N-b}}{p-1}=p^{N-a+1}-p^{N-b}.

(1)

In particular, for
\displaystyle r\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{j=N-r+1}^{N}n_{j}=p^{r}-1.

(2)

Due to positive semidefinite property of the Laplacian of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
,
\displaystyle 0
is the simple eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
. Next, for eigenvalues
\displaystyle p^{k}-1
with
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq b-1
, we fix an integer
\displaystyle k
,
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq b-1
and let
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=p^{k}-1.
Now, consider a vector
\displaystyle v^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

v^{(k)}_{j}=\begin{cases}0,&1\leq j<k,\\
-\varGamma_{k},&j=k,\\
1,&k<j\leq N-k,\\
0,&N-k<j\leq N,\end{cases}

(3)

where
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
is a scaler, we are to be chosen so that eigenequation is satisfied. For the computation of
\displaystyle(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}
, we consider the following cases. For
\displaystyle 1\leq i<k
, we have
\displaystyle v_{i}^{(k)}=0
. If
\displaystyle j
is such that
\displaystyle v_{j}^{(k)}\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle k\leq j\leq N-k
, so it gives

i+j\leq(k-1)+(N-k)=N-1<N+1.

Hence
\displaystyle i+j\geq N+1
never holds, so all off-diagonal entries in row
\displaystyle i
of
\displaystyle\overline{L}

multiply zeros of
\displaystyle v^{(k)}
. Thus, we have

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=d_{i}v^{(k)}_{i}=0=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}_{i}.

For
\displaystyle i=k
, we have
\displaystyle v^{(k)}_{k}=-\varGamma_{k}
. For
\displaystyle j
in the support of
\displaystyle v^{(k)}
with

\displaystyle j\neq k
, we have
\displaystyle k<j\leq N-k
, so it gives

k+j\leq k+(N-k)=N<N+1.

Thus
\displaystyle k+j\geq N+1
never holds, and there are no nonzero off-diagonal
contributions in row
\displaystyle k
. So,
\displaystyle(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{k}=d_{k}v^{(k)}_{k}.

Since
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq b-1
, we have
\displaystyle d_{k}=p^{k}-1=\lambda_{k}
, and we obtain

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{k}=(p^{k}-1)v^{(k)}_{k}=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}_{k},

which is independent of
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
. If
\displaystyle k<i\leq N-k
, then in this range
\displaystyle v^{(k)}_{i}=1
. The only indices
\displaystyle j
with
\displaystyle v_{j}^{(k)}\neq 0
are
\displaystyle j=k
and
\displaystyle k<j\leq N-k
. However, the column
\displaystyle k
does not appear in the off-diagonal part of row
\displaystyle i
. Indeed, for
\displaystyle j=k
, we obtain

i+k\leq(N-k)+k=N<N+1.

So,
\displaystyle i+k\geq N+1
fails and it gives
\displaystyle(\overline{L})_{ik}=0
. Thus
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
plays
no role in this range. The nonzero off-diagonal terms come from those

\displaystyle j
with
\displaystyle k<j\leq N-k,
with
\displaystyle i+j\geq N+1.

The inequality
\displaystyle i+j\geq N+1
is
\displaystyle j\geq N+1-i
, so the relevant
\displaystyle j
are

j\in\bigl[\max(k+1,N+1-i),N-k\bigr].

Since
\displaystyle i\leq N-k
, we get
\displaystyle N+1-i\geq N+1-(N-k)=k+1,
so the lower bound is
\displaystyle j\geq N+1-i
. In summary, the contributing
indices are

j=N+1-i,N+2-i,\dots,N-k.

These all satisfy
\displaystyle j>k
, hence
\displaystyle v^{(k)}_{j}=1
. If
\displaystyle i<b
then
\displaystyle N+1-i>i
, so
\displaystyle j=i
is not in this interval and the
sum includes no diagonal index. If
\displaystyle i\geq b
then
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b=N+1
, so

\displaystyle N+1-i\leq i
, and
\displaystyle j=i

is
in the interval. In this case we
must omit
\displaystyle j=i
from the off-diagonal sum. Both these cases can be treated
uniformly by writing

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=d_{i}-\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{N-k}n_{j}+\varepsilon_{i}n_{i},

where
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}=0
if
\displaystyle i<b
and
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}=1
if
\displaystyle i\geq b
. By (
1
) (with
\displaystyle a=N+1-i
,
\displaystyle b=N-k
),

\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{N-k}n_{j}=p^{N-(N+1-i)+1}-p^{N-(N-k)}=p^{i}-p^{k}.

If
\displaystyle k<i\leq b-1
, then
\displaystyle d_{i}=p^{i}-1
and
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}=0
.
Thus, we have

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=(p^{i}-1)-(p^{i}-p^{k})=p^{k}-1=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}_{i}.

If
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq N-k
, then
\displaystyle d_{i}=p^{i}-1-n_{i}
and

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}=1
, so we obtain

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=(p^{i}-1-n_{i})-(p^{i}-p^{k})+n_{i}=p^{k}-1=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}_{i}.

Thus the eigenvalue equation holds for all
\displaystyle k<i\leq N-k
for every
choice of
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
. With
\displaystyle N-k<i\leq N
, here
\displaystyle v^{(k)}_{i}=0
, so we need
\displaystyle(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=0
.
Now
\displaystyle v^{(k)}_{j}\neq 0
only for
\displaystyle k\leq j\leq N-k
. Since

\displaystyle i\geq N-k+1
, for any such
\displaystyle j
we have

i+j\geq(N-k+1)+k=N+1.

So, all of these
\displaystyle j
appear in the off-diagonal sum. Therefore, we have

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=-\sum_{j=k}^{N-k}n_{j}v^{(k)}_{j}=-\Bigl(n_{k}(-\varGamma_{k})+\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-k}n_{j}\cdot 1\Bigr)=\varGamma_{k}n_{k}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-k}n_{j}.

This expression is independent of
\displaystyle i
in this range. Thus all the
bottom rows impose the same scalar condition
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}n_{k}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-k}n_{j}.

In order to solve
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
explicitly , we use (
1
),

\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-k}n_{j}=\sum_{j=k}^{N-k}n_{j}-n_{k}=\bigl(p^{N-k+1}-p^{N-(N-k)}\bigr)-n_{k}=(p^{N-k+1}-p^{k})-n_{k}.

But
\displaystyle n_{k}=(p-1)p^{N-k}
, so
\displaystyle p^{N-k+1}-p^{k}-n_{k}=p^{N-k}-p^{k}.

Hence, with these facts, we obtain
\displaystyle\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-k}n_{j}=p^{N-k}-p^{k}.
Also,
\displaystyle n_{k}=(p-1)p^{N-k}
, so we have

\varGamma_{k}=\frac{\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-k}n_{j}}{n_{k}}=\frac{p^{N-k}-p^{k}}{(p-1)p^{N-k}}=\frac{1-p^{2k-N}}{p-1}=\frac{1-p^{2k-2b+1}}{p-1}.

With the above value of
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
, all bottom rows give
\displaystyle(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=0=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}_{i}
. Therefore, we have

\overline{L}v^{(k)}=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)},\qquad\lambda_{k}=p^{k}-1,\quad 1\leq k\leq b-1.

Now, for the eigenvalues
\displaystyle p^{k}-1
of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
for
\displaystyle b+1\leq k\leq N
, we fix an integer
\displaystyle k
with
\displaystyle b+1\leq k\leq N
and set again
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=p^{k}-1
. For
\displaystyle m=N+1-k=2b-k,
with
\displaystyle 1\leq m\leq b-1<k
, we define
\displaystyle w^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

w^{(k)}_{j}=\begin{cases}0,&1\leq j<m,\\
-\delta_{k},&m\leq j\leq k-1,\\
1,&j=k,\\
0,&k<j\leq N,\end{cases}

(4)

where
\displaystyle\delta_{k}
is the scalar, which we are required to find that
\displaystyle w^{(k)}
is eigenvector of some eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
. Again we compute
\displaystyle(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}
by different ranges over
\displaystyle i
. For
\displaystyle 1\leq i<m
, we have
\displaystyle w^{(k)}_{i}=0
. For any
\displaystyle j
with
\displaystyle w^{(k)}_{j}\neq 0
, we have
\displaystyle m\leq j\leq k
, and thus

i+j\leq(m-1)+k=(N+1-k-1)+k=N.

Hence
\displaystyle i+j\geq N+1
never holds, so the off-diagonal sum of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
is empty, and we obtain

(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}=d_{i}w^{(k)}_{i}=0=\lambda_{k}w^{(k)}_{i}.

For
\displaystyle i=k
, we have
\displaystyle w^{(k)}_{k}=1
. The only nonzero
\displaystyle w^{(k)}_{j}
with
\displaystyle j\neq k
occur
for
\displaystyle m\leq j\leq k-1
, and for all such
\displaystyle j
, we have

k+j\geq k+m=k+(N+1-k)=N+1,

and they all contribute to the off-diagonal sum. Thus, we obtain

(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{k}=d_{k}\cdot 1-\sum_{j=m}^{k-1}n_{j}(-\delta_{k})=d_{k}+\delta_{k}\sum_{j=m}^{k-1}n_{j}.

As
\displaystyle k\geq b+1
, so
\displaystyle k\geq b
and hence
\displaystyle d_{k}=p^{k}-1-n_{k}
. In order for
\displaystyle(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{k}=\lambda_{k}w^{(k)}_{k}=p^{k}-1
to hold, so we must have

p^{k}-1-n_{k}+\delta_{k}\sum_{j=m}^{k-1}n_{j}=p^{k}-1,

which is equivalent to
\displaystyle\delta_{k}\sum_{j=m}^{k-1}n_{j}=n_{k}.

Using (
1
) with
\displaystyle a=m
,
\displaystyle b=k-1
, we obtain

\sum_{j=m}^{k-1}n_{j}=p^{N-m+1}-p^{N-(k-1)}=p^{k}-p^{N-k+1}.

Thus

\delta_{k}=\frac{n_{k}}{p^{k}-p^{N-k+1}}=\frac{(p-1)p^{N-k}}{p^{k}-p^{N-k+1}}=\frac{p-1}{p^{2k-N}-p}=\frac{p-1}{p\bigl(p^{2(k-b)}-1\bigr)}.

With the choice of
\displaystyle\delta_{k}
, for
\displaystyle m\leq i\leq k-1
, we have
\displaystyle w^{(k)}_{i}=-\delta_{k}
. The nonzero
\displaystyle w^{(k)}_{j}
occur for
\displaystyle j\in[m,k-1]
(where
\displaystyle w_{j}=-\delta_{k}
) and
\displaystyle j=k
(where
\displaystyle w_{k}=1
). With
\displaystyle m\leq i\leq k-1
, we have
\displaystyle i+k\geq m+k=N+1,
so
\displaystyle j=k
always appears in the off-diagonal sum. Among
\displaystyle m\leq j\leq k-1
, the condition
\displaystyle i+j\geq N+1
is
\displaystyle j\geq N+1-i
. Since

N+1-i\geq N+1-(k-1)=N-k+2=m+1,

we have
\displaystyle N+1-i\geq m
and the contributing
\displaystyle j
satisfy
\displaystyle j\in[N+1-i,k-1].
We must again check whether
\displaystyle i
lies in this interval. If
\displaystyle m\leq i<b
, then
\displaystyle N+1-i>i
, and it shows that
\displaystyle i
is not in
\displaystyle[N+1-i,k-1]
. In this case

(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}=d_{i}(-\delta_{k})-\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{k-1}n_{j}(-\delta_{k})-n_{k}\cdot 1=-\delta_{k}d_{i}+\delta_{k}\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{k-1}n_{j}-n_{k}.

Using (
1
) with
\displaystyle a=N+1-i
,
\displaystyle b=k-1
, we have

\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{k-1}n_{j}=p^{N-(N+1-i)+1}-p^{N-(k-1)}=p^{i}-p^{N-k+1}.

Since
\displaystyle i<b
,
\displaystyle d_{i}=p^{i}-1
, so

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\delta_{k}(p^{i}-1)+\delta_{k}(p^{i}-p^{N-k+1})-n_{k}=-\delta_{k}(p^{N-k+1}-1)-n_{k}.

On the other hand, we have

\lambda_{k}w^{(k)}_{i}=(p^{k}-1)(-\delta_{k})=-\delta_{k}(p^{k}-1).

Thus the eigenvalue equation is equivalent to

-\delta_{k}(p^{N-k+1}-1)-n_{k}=-\delta_{k}(p^{k}-1)

or

\delta_{k}\bigl(p^{k}-p^{N-k+1}\bigr)=n_{k}.

But this is precisely how
\displaystyle\delta_{k}
was chosen above. Hence

\displaystyle(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}=\lambda_{k}w^{(k)}_{i}
for
\displaystyle m\leq i<b
. Now, for
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq k-1
, we get
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b=N+1
. So,
\displaystyle N+1-i\leq i
and the interval

\displaystyle[N+1-i,k-1]
contains
\displaystyle i
. Thus, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq j\leq k-1\\
i+j\geq N+1\end{subarray}}n_{j}(-\delta_{k})=-\delta_{k}\left(\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{k-1}n_{j}-n_{i}\right),

and

(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}=d_{i}(-\delta_{k})-\Bigl(-\delta_{k}\bigl(\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{k-1}n_{j}-n_{i}\bigr)\Bigr)-n_{k}=-\delta_{k}d_{i}+\delta_{k}\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{k-1}n_{j}-\delta_{k}n_{i}-n_{k}.

Again with
\displaystyle\sum_{j=N+1-i}^{k-1}n_{j}=p^{i}-p^{N-k+1}
and

\displaystyle d_{i}=p^{i}-1-n_{i}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\delta_{k}(p^{i}-1-n_{i})+\delta_{k}(p^{i}-p^{N-k+1})-\delta_{k}n_{i}-n_{k}=\delta_{k}-\delta_{k}p^{N-k+1}-n_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\delta_{k}(p^{N-k+1}-1)-n_{k}.

As in the previous case this equals
\displaystyle-\delta_{k}(p^{k}-1)
exactly when

\displaystyle\delta_{k}(p^{k}-p^{N-k+1})=n_{k}
, that is, for our
\displaystyle\delta_{k}
, and hence for
\displaystyle b\leq i\leq k-1
, we obtain

(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}=\lambda_{k}w^{(k)}_{i}.

For the last range
\displaystyle i>k
, we have
\displaystyle w^{(k)}_{i}=0
. For such
\displaystyle i
and with any
\displaystyle j\in[m,k]
, we have

i+j\geq(k+1)+m=(k+1)+(N+1-k)=N+2>N+1.

So, all indices in the support
\displaystyle\{m,\dots,k\}
contribute to the off-diagonal sum. Thus, with the defining relation for
\displaystyle\delta_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=m}^{k-1}n_{j}(-\delta_{k})-n_{k}\cdot 1

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\delta_{k}\sum_{j=m}^{k-1}n_{j}-n_{k}=\delta_{k}(p^{k}-p^{N-k+1})-n_{k}=0.

Since
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}w^{(k)}_{i}=0

as well, the eigenvalue equation holds. Thus, it shows that for
\displaystyle b+1\leq k\leq N
,

\overline{L}w^{(k)}=(p^{k}-1)w^{(k)}.

The numbers
\displaystyle p^{k}-1
are strictly increasing in
\displaystyle k
, so the

\displaystyle 2b-2
values
\displaystyle\{p^{k}-1:1\leq k\leq N,\ k\neq b\}
are all distinct, and
together with
\displaystyle 0
they give
\displaystyle 2b-1=N
distinct eigenvalues. Thus, for every
\displaystyle i
with
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 2b-1
and
\displaystyle i\neq b
,

\displaystyle p^{i}-1
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
.

3
Spectra of zero-divisor graph of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b+1}\rangle

In this section, we consider the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
spectra of the zero-divisor graph of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b+1}\rangle
. First, we understand the combinatorial structure of
\displaystyle\varGamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b+1}\rangle).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime and let
\displaystyle b\geq 1
be an integer, and let
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
be the zero-divisor graph of
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b+1}\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{2b+1})
. Then the following hold

1.

The vertex set
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))
can be decomposed into subsets
\displaystyle V_{i}=\left\{\sum_{k=i}^{2b}a_{k}x^{k}:a_{i}\neq 0\right\},1\leq i\leq 2b
with
\displaystyle|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-i},i=1,\dots,2b,
and each vertex of
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex of
\displaystyle V_{j}
if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b+1.

2.

The induced subgraph of
\displaystyle V_{i}
is
\displaystyle\overline{K}_{|V_{i}|}
if
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b
and it is
\displaystyle K_{|V_{i}|}
if
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b.

3.

The clique number of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle\omega(\varGamma(R))=p^{b}
, the independence number is
\displaystyle p^{2b}-p^{b}
, and the domination number is
\displaystyle\varGamma(\varGamma(R))=1
.

4.

The diameter of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle 2
and its girth is
\displaystyle 3.

Proof.

We use the description of
\displaystyle R
as an
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}
–vector space, and the characterize the
units by the constant term in the quotient rings
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{2b+1})
. Since
\displaystyle R
is finite, every nonunit is a zero-divisor, so the nonzero zero-divisors are precisely the nonzero elements of the ideal
\displaystyle(x)
. Thus, as an
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}
–vector space, and with multiplication modulo
\displaystyle x^{2b+1}
, we have

R=\Bigl\{\sum_{k=0}^{2b}a_{k}x^{k}:a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\Bigr\}.

So, it follows that all terms of degree greater than or equal to
\displaystyle 2b+1
vanish. An element
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2b}a_{k}x^{k}
of
\displaystyle R
is a unit if and only if
\displaystyle a_{0}\neq 0
. Thus, the set of nonzero zero-divisors of
\displaystyle R
is

Z(R)\setminus\{0\}=\Bigl\{\sum_{k=1}^{2b}a_{k}x^{k}:(a_{1},\dots,a_{2b})\neq(0,\dots,0)\Bigr\},

which are precisely the nonzero elements of the ideal
\displaystyle(x)
. So, it follows that
\displaystyle|V(\varGamma(R))|=|Z(R)\setminus\{0\}|=p^{2b}-1.
For a nonzero zero-divisor
\displaystyle f\in Z(R)
, the minimal degree is defined as

\operatorname{mindeg}(f)=\min\bigl\{k\in\{1,\dots,2b\}:\text{the coefficient of $\displaystyle x^{k}$ in $\displaystyle f$ is nonzero}\bigr\}.

This partitions the vertex set
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=Z(R)\setminus\{0\}
into

V(\varGamma(R))=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\dots V_{b-1}\cup V_{b}\cup V_{b+1}\cup\dots\cup V_{2b},

where

V_{i}=\left\{\sum_{k=i}^{2b}a_{k}x^{k}:a_{i}\neq 0\right\},\qquad 1\leq i\leq 2b.

For each
\displaystyle i
, the coefficient
\displaystyle a_{i}
can be any nonzero element of
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
and the coefficients
\displaystyle a_{i+1},\dots,a_{2b}
are arbitrary. Hence
\displaystyle|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-i},
for
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,2b,
and we have

\sum_{i=1}^{2b}|V_{i}|=(p-1)\sum_{t=0}^{2b-1}p^{t}=p^{2b}-1=|V(\varGamma(R))|.

Let
\displaystyle f,g\in Z(R)\setminus\{0\}
with
\displaystyle\operatorname{mindeg}(f)=i
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{mindeg}(g)=j
, where
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq 2b
. Write

f=a_{i}x^{i}+\text{(higher degree terms)},\qquad g=b_{j}x^{j}+\text{(higher degree terms)},

with
\displaystyle a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. In
\displaystyle fg
, the term of minimal degree is
\displaystyle x^{i+j}
, whose coefficient is
\displaystyle a_{i}b_{j}
,
since all other products of coefficients have strictly larger degree.
Thus, it implies that
\displaystyle fg=0
if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b+1.
Clearly, if
\displaystyle i+j\leq 2b
, then the
\displaystyle x^{i+j}
term is nonzero in
\displaystyle R
, and if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b+1
, then every term of
\displaystyle fg
has degree
\displaystyle\geq 2b+1
and vanishes modulo
\displaystyle(x^{2b+1})
. Consequently, the adjacency between two vertices in
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is completely determined by the minimal degrees, that is,
\displaystyle f\in V_{i},\ g\in V_{j},\ f\neq g
then
\displaystyle f\sim g
if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b+1.
In particular, for the induced subgraphs on the sets
\displaystyle V_{i}
, two distinct vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
are adjacent if and only if
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b+1
. Since
\displaystyle 2i
is an integer, this is equivalent to
\displaystyle i\geq b+1
. Thus
\displaystyle V_{i}
is an independent set if
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b
, and
\displaystyle V_{i}
is a clique if
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b.
For
\displaystyle i\neq j
, either all vertices of
\displaystyle V_{i}
are adjacent to every vertex of
\displaystyle V_{j}
, or there exist no edges between them. In particular, no vertex of
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to any
\displaystyle V_{j},
if
\displaystyle i+j\leq 2b
, and each vertex of
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex of
\displaystyle V_{j}
if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b+1.

A particularly important subgraph is given by the subset
\displaystyle C=\bigcup_{k=b+1}^{2b}V_{k}.

So, for all
\displaystyle i,j\geq b+1
, it gives us
\displaystyle i+j\geq(b+1)+(b+1)=2b+2>2b+1.
Thus, every pair of vertices in
\displaystyle C
is adjacent, and
\displaystyle C
induces a clique of size

|C|=\sum_{k=b+1}^{2b}|V_{k}|=\sum_{k=b+1}^{2b}(p-1)p^{2b-k}=(p-1)\sum_{t=0}^{b-1}p^{t}=p^{b}-1.

Moreover, every vertex in
\displaystyle V_{b}
is adjacent to every vertex in
\displaystyle C
, since
\displaystyle b+k\geq b+(b+1)=2b+1
for all
\displaystyle k\geq b+1.
Thus, any set of the form
\displaystyle\{v\}\cup C
with
\displaystyle v\in V_{b}
is a clique of size
\displaystyle|C|+1=(p^{b}-1)+1=p^{b}.
To identify the clique number, from above we note that
\displaystyle\omega(\varGamma(R))\geq p^{b}
. On the other hand, let
\displaystyle S
be any clique and let
\displaystyle i_{0}=\min\{\operatorname{mindeg}(f):f\in S\}
. If
\displaystyle i_{0}\geq b+1
, then
\displaystyle S\subseteq C
, so
\displaystyle|S|\leq|C|=p^{b}-1
.
If
\displaystyle i_{0}\leq b
, then for any
\displaystyle g\in S
with
\displaystyle\operatorname{mindeg}(g)=j
, we must have

\displaystyle i_{0}+j\geq 2b+1
, so
\displaystyle j\geq 2b+1-i_{0}\geq b+1
, and hence
\displaystyle S\subseteq\{f\}\cup C
for some

\displaystyle f\in V_{i_{0}}
. Thus
\displaystyle|S|\leq 1+|C|=p^{b}
, and it follows that
\displaystyle\omega(\varGamma(R))=p^{b}
.
For
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b
, each
\displaystyle V_{i}
is independent,and if
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq b
then
\displaystyle i+j\leq b+b=2b<2b+1,

so there are no edges between
\displaystyle V_{i}
and
\displaystyle V_{j}
either. Thus,
\displaystyle S_{0}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{b}V_{i}
is an independent set with cardinality

|S_{0}|=\sum_{i=1}^{b}|V_{i}|=\sum_{i=1}^{b}(p-1)p^{2b-i}=(p-1)\sum_{t=b}^{2b-1}p^{t}=(p-1)\frac{p^{2b}-p^{b}}{p-1}=p^{2b}-p^{b}.

So, it follows that
\displaystyle\alpha(\varGamma(R))\geq p^{2b}-p^{b}.
We now show that no independent set can be larger than
\displaystyle S_{0}
. Let
\displaystyle S
be an arbitrary independent set in
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
. If
\displaystyle S\cap\bigcup_{i=b+1}^{2b}V_{i}=\varnothing
, then
\displaystyle S\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{b}V_{i}=S_{0}
, so
\displaystyle|S|\leq|S_{0}|
. If
\displaystyle S
contains a vertex from some
\displaystyle V_{j}
with
\displaystyle j\geq b+1
. Since
\displaystyle V_{j}
is a clique for
\displaystyle j\geq b+1
,
\displaystyle S
can contain at most one vertex from each such
\displaystyle V_{j}
, and in particular, at most one from the entire set
\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=b+1}^{2b}V_{i}
. Let
\displaystyle j_{0}=\max\{j:S\cap V_{j}\neq\varnothing\},
with
\displaystyle j_{0}\geq b+1.
Then
\displaystyle S
contains exactly one vertex from
\displaystyle V_{j_{0}}
, and no vertices from
\displaystyle V_{j}
with
\displaystyle j>j_{0}
. Now, if
\displaystyle v\in V_{j_{0}}
, then for a vertex
\displaystyle u\in V_{i}
with
\displaystyle i+j_{0}\geq 2b+1
we have
\displaystyle u\sim v
, so
\displaystyle u
cannot belong to
\displaystyle S
. Thus,
\displaystyle S
can contain vertices from
\displaystyle V_{i}
only when
\displaystyle i+j_{0}\leq 2b
, if
\displaystyle i\leq 2b-j_{0}.
Consequently,
\displaystyle S\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{2b-j_{0}}V_{i}\right)\cup(S\cap V_{j_{0}}),

and hence
\displaystyle|S|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2b-j_{0}}|V_{i}|+1.

Comparing it with
\displaystyle|S_{0}|
, we have

|S_{0}|-|S|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{b}|V_{i}|-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2b-j_{0}}|V_{i}|+1\right)=\sum_{i=2b-j_{0}+1}^{b}|V_{i}|-1.

Since
\displaystyle j_{0}\geq b+1
, we have
\displaystyle 2b-j_{0}+1\leq b
, so the index set

\displaystyle\{2b-j_{0}+1,\dots,b\}
is nonempty. Moreover, for each
\displaystyle i
,
\displaystyle|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-i}\geq p-1\geq 1,
so it gives
\displaystyle\sum_{i=2b-j_{0}+1}^{b}|V_{i}|\geq 1,
and hence
\displaystyle|S_{0}|-|S|\geq 0
, or
\displaystyle|S|\leq|S_{0}|
. Combining the two cases, every independent set
\displaystyle S
satisfies
\displaystyle|S|\leq|S_{0}|
, and we already have an independent set
\displaystyle S_{0}
of size
\displaystyle p^{2b}-p^{b}
. Therefore, we obtain
\displaystyle\alpha(\varGamma(R))=|S_{0}|=p^{2b}-p^{b}.

As for
\displaystyle i\geq b+1
the induced subgraph on
\displaystyle V_{i}
is a clique, and for
\displaystyle i=2b,\deg(v)=p^{2b}-2,
for
\displaystyle v\in V_{2b},
while
\displaystyle|V(\varGamma(R))|=p^{2b}-1
. Thus, each vertex
\displaystyle v\in V_{2b}
is adjacent to every
other vertex of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
, that is, every vertex in
\displaystyle V_{2b}
is a universal vertex.

For
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, its neighbors are exactly the vertices in
\displaystyle\bigcup_{j:i+j\geq 2b+1}V_{j}
,
with the additional edges inside
\displaystyle V_{i}
, if
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b+1
or
\displaystyle i\geq b+1
, then in this case
\displaystyle v
is also adjacent
to all other vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
. With
\displaystyle\sum_{j=m}^{2b}|V_{j}|=p^{2b-m+1}-1
, for
\displaystyle 1\leq m\leq 2b,
and taking
\displaystyle m=2b+1-i
, we get the total number of vertices in subsets
\displaystyle V_{2b+1-i},\dots,V_{2b}
as
\displaystyle\sum_{j=2b+1-i}^{2b}|V_{j}|=p^{i}-1.

This includes
\displaystyle V_{i}
itself exactly when
\displaystyle 2i\geq 2b+1
. Therefore, the degree of
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
is
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p^{i}-1
if
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b
, and
\displaystyle\deg(v)p^{i}-2,
if
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b
. In particular, for
\displaystyle i=2b
, we have
\displaystyle 2(2b)\geq 2b+1
, so each vertex in

\displaystyle V_{2b}
has degree
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p^{2b}-2,v\in V_{2b},
and is adjacent to every other vertex of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
.
Thus
\displaystyle V_{2b}
consists of
\displaystyle p-1
universal vertices. As
\displaystyle V_{2b}
is nonempty with
\displaystyle p-1
vertices, and every vertex is adjacent

\displaystyle V_{2b}

\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is connected for all
\displaystyle b\geq 1
.
Moreover, if
\displaystyle b=1
, so
\displaystyle a=3
, then there are two subsets
\displaystyle V_{1}
an independent set,
\displaystyle V_{2}
a clique, and every vertex in
\displaystyle V_{1}
is adjacent to every vertex in
\displaystyle V_{2}
. Thus, the diameter of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle 2
. If
\displaystyle b\geq 2

\displaystyle a\geq 5

\displaystyle V_{1}
, but any two vertices are at distance at most
\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle V_{2b}
. Hence, for all
\displaystyle b\geq 1
, the diameter of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle 2
. For
\displaystyle b\geq 2
, the sets
\displaystyle V_{2b-1}
and
\displaystyle V_{2b}

\displaystyle 2(2b-1)\geq 2b+1,\quad(2b-1)+(2b)\geq 2b+1,

\displaystyle V_{2b-1}

\displaystyle V_{2b-1}

\displaystyle V_{2b}
. Since
\displaystyle|V_{2b-1}|\geq 2

\displaystyle|V_{2b}|\geq 1
, this yields triangles, so for all primes
\displaystyle p

\displaystyle b\geq 2
, the girth of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle 3
. Here, we ignore the case
\displaystyle a=3
and
\displaystyle p=2
, as
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)

We will illustrate Theorem
3.1
for
\displaystyle b=2
, so that
\displaystyle a=5.

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime and let
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{5}\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{5}).
Let
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
be its zero–divisor graph. Then the vertices of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
are partitioned as
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}\cup V_{4},

where

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=\{a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{3}x^{3}+a_{4}x^{4}:a_{1}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle|V_{1}|

\displaystyle=p^{4}-p^{3},

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=\{a_{2}x^{2}+a_{3}x^{3}+a_{4}x^{4}:a_{2}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle|V_{2}|

\displaystyle=p^{3}-p^{2},

\displaystyle V_{3}

\displaystyle=\{a_{3}x^{3}+a_{4}x^{4}:a_{3}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle|V_{3}|

\displaystyle=p^{2}-p,

\displaystyle V_{4}

\displaystyle=\{a_{4}x^{4}:a_{4}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle|V_{4}|

\displaystyle=p-1.

Then, the order of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is
\displaystyle p^{4}-1.
The degree of a vertex
\displaystyle v\in V(\varGamma(R))
is
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p-1,p^{2}-1,p^{3}-2,
and
\displaystyle p^{4}-2
for
\displaystyle v\in V_{1},,v\in V_{2},v\in V_{3},
and
\displaystyle v\in V_{4}
, respectively. The set
\displaystyle V_{3}\cup V_{4}
induces a clique of size
\displaystyle|V_{3}|+|V_{4}|=p^{2}-1
, and for any
vertex
\displaystyle v\in V_{2}
, the set
\displaystyle\{v\}\cup V_{3}\cup V_{4}

\displaystyle p^{2}
. The sets
\displaystyle V_{1}
and
\displaystyle V_{2}
are independent, with no edges joining
\displaystyle V_{1}
to
\displaystyle V_{2}
. Therefore,
\displaystyle V_{1}\cup V_{2}
constitutes an independent set of size
\displaystyle\alpha\bigl(\varGamma(R)\bigr)=|V_{1}|+|V_{2}|=p^{4}-p
.

For
\displaystyle p=2,
every element of
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]/(x^{5})
can be written uniquely as
\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{3}x^{3}+a_{4}x^{4},
where
\displaystyle a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}=\{0,1\},
with multiplication modulo
\displaystyle x^{5}
. The units are those with
\displaystyle a_{0}\neq 0
, so the nonzero zero-divisors are exactly the nonzero
multiples of
\displaystyle x
:

Z(R)\setminus\{0\}=\{a_{1}x+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{3}x^{3}+a_{4}x^{4}\neq 0\},

and
\displaystyle|V(\varGamma(R))|=2^{4}-1=15.
For a nonzero zero-divisor
\displaystyle f
, define

\operatorname{mindeg}(f)=\min\{k\in\{1,2,3,4\}:\text{the coefficient of }x^{k}\text{ in }f\text{ is nonzero}\}.

This partitions
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))
into four subsets
\displaystyle V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4}
, where
\displaystyle V_{1}
is set of elements of minimal degree
\displaystyle 1
(coefficient of
\displaystyle x
is nonzero). Here
\displaystyle a_{1}=1
, and
\displaystyle a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in\{0,1\}
arbitrary, so
\displaystyle|V_{1}|=2^{3}=8
, and

\displaystyle V_{1}=\{

\displaystyle x,x+x^{2},x+x^{3},x+x^{4},x+x^{2}+x^{3},x+x^{2}+x^{4},

\displaystyle x+x^{3}+x^{4},x+x^{2}+x^{3}+x^{4}\}.

Similarly,
\displaystyle V_{2}
consists of elements with a minimal degree of
\displaystyle 2
, as the coefficient of
\displaystyle x
is
\displaystyle 0
and the coefficient of
\displaystyle x^{2}
is non-zero. Let
\displaystyle a_{1}=0
,
\displaystyle a_{2}=1
, and
\displaystyle a_{3},a_{4}\in\{0,1\}
.
Therefore, the size of
\displaystyle V_{2}
is
\displaystyle 2^{2}=4
, and
\displaystyle V_{2}
is defined as
\displaystyle\{x^{2},x^{2}+x^{3},x^{2}+x^{4},x^{2}+x^{3}+x^{4}\}
.
Moreover,
\displaystyle V_{3}
includes all elements that have a minimum nonzero degree of
\displaystyle 3
.
The coefficients of
\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle x^{2}
must equal zero, but the coefficient of
\displaystyle x^{3}
must equal
\displaystyle 1
. The residual coefficient
\displaystyle a_{4}
can assume a value of either
\displaystyle 0
or
\displaystyle 1
. Therefore,
\displaystyle V_{3}=\{x^{3},x^{3}+x^{4}\}
. Moreover,
\displaystyle V_{4}
includes all elements with a minimum degree of
\displaystyle 4
. The coefficients of
\displaystyle x
,
\displaystyle x^{2}
, and
\displaystyle x^{3}
are all zero, while the coefficient of
\displaystyle x^{4}
is not zero.
Here
\displaystyle a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=0
,
\displaystyle a_{4}=1
, so
\displaystyle|V_{4}|=1
, and
\displaystyle V_{4}=\{x^{4}\}.
Thus,
\displaystyle f,g
are two nonzero zero-divisors with

\operatorname{mindeg}(f)=i,\quad\operatorname{mindeg}(g)=j,\quad i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\},

then
\displaystyle fg=0
if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 5.

So vertices
\displaystyle f,g
are adjacent in
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
if and only if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 5
. From this, it follows that:
\displaystyle V_{1}
is an independent set of order
\displaystyle 8
, each vertex is adjacent to
\displaystyle V_{4}=\{x^{4}\}
,
\displaystyle V_{2}
is an independent set of
\displaystyle 4
vertices, each joined to both vertices of
\displaystyle V_{3}

and to
\displaystyle x^{4}
,
\displaystyle V_{3}\cup V_{4}=\{x^{3},\ x^{3}+x^{4},\ x^{4}\}
induces a
\displaystyle K_{3}
. Also, there are no edges between
\displaystyle V_{1}
and
\displaystyle V_{2}
, and no edges between
\displaystyle V_{1}
and
\displaystyle V_{3}
. The unique vertex
\displaystyle x^{4}\in V_{4}
is adjacent to
every
other vertex of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
,
so it is a universal vertex. The graph
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is shown in Figure
2
.

.

The following result gives the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
–spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma\bigl(\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{2b+1})\bigr)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime and
\displaystyle b\geq 1
a positive integer, and let
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
be the zero–divisor graph of
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b+1}\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(x^{2b+1}).
Then the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
–spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
consist of the eigenvalue
\displaystyle\alpha\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr),~1\leq i\leq b
, and the eigenvalue
\displaystyle\alpha\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr)-1,~b+1\leq i\leq 2b
with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1=(p-1)p^{2b-i}-1.
The remaining
\displaystyle 2b
eigenvalues are the eigenvalues of the following
\displaystyle 2b\times 2b
matrix
\displaystyle B=(B_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2b}
,
where
\displaystyle B=\alpha D^{\ast}+(1-\alpha)Q,
with
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{2b})

and
\displaystyle Q=(Q_{ij})
the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle A(\varGamma(R))
with respect to the partition

\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{2b}V_{i}
, given by

Q_{ij}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i=j,\ 1\leq i\leq b,\\[3.0pt]
n_{i}-1,&\text{if }i=j,\ b+1\leq i\leq 2b,\\[3.0pt]
n_{j},&\text{if }i\neq j,\ i+j\geq 2b+1,\\[3.0pt]
0,&\text{if }i\neq j,\ i+j\leq 2b.\end{cases}

Equivalently,

B_{ij}=\begin{cases}\alpha d_{i},&\text{if }i=j,\ 1\leq i\leq b,\\[3.0pt]
\alpha d_{i}+(1-\alpha)(n_{i}-1),&\text{if }i=j,\ b+1\leq i\leq 2b,\\[3.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{j},&\text{if }i\neq j,\ i+j\geq 2b+1,\\[3.0pt]
0,&\text{if }i\neq j,\ i+j\leq 2b,\end{cases}

where for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 2b
,

d_{i}=\deg(v)=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&\text{if }1\leq i\leq b,\\[3.0pt]
p^{i}-2,&\text{if }b+1\leq i\leq 2b,\end{cases}\qquad v\in V_{i}.

Proof.

For
\displaystyle a=2b+1
, and by Theorem
3.1
, the vertex set of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is partitioned into
\displaystyle V_{i}
(
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 2b
) with order
\displaystyle n_{i}=|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-i}.

By the adjacency rule, for
\displaystyle f\in V_{i},\ g\in V_{j},
with
\displaystyle f\neq g
, a vertex
\displaystyle f
is adjacent to
\displaystyle g
if
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b+1.
For
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b
, there are no edges inside
\displaystyle V_{i}
, and a vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{j}
with
\displaystyle j\geq 2b+1-i
. With
\displaystyle\sum_{j=2b+1-i}^{2b}|V_{j}|=p^{i}-1,
we get
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p^{i}-1
for
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
. For
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b
,
\displaystyle V_{i}
induces a clique, and a vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to all other vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
and to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{j}
for
\displaystyle j\geq 2b+1-i
,
\displaystyle j\neq i
. The same sum gives
\displaystyle p^{i}-1
neighbors in
\displaystyle V_{2b+1-i}\cup\cdots\cup V_{2b}
, which now includes
\displaystyle V_{i}
, so we subtract
\displaystyle 1
for the vertex itself and obtain
\displaystyle\deg(v)=p^{i}-2
for
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
. For each
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,2b
, define

W_{i}=\Bigl\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{V(\varGamma(R))}:\mathrm{supp}(x)\subseteq V_{i},\ \sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=0\Bigr\},

where for
\displaystyle x=(x_{v})_{v\in V(\varGamma(R))}\in\mathbb{R}^{V(\varGamma(R))},
we have
\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{v\in V(\varGamma(R)):x_{v}\neq 0\}.
Thus, it is clear that
\displaystyle\dim W_{i}=|V_{i}|-1=n_{i}-1
. We show that
\displaystyle W_{i}
is invariant under
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
and compute the eigenvalue. If
\displaystyle x\in W_{i}
, then
\displaystyle x
is supported on
\displaystyle V_{i}
and
\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=0
. Consider the action of the adjacency matrix
\displaystyle A
on independent sets
\displaystyle V_{i}
, for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b.
For
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, there are no neighbors of
\displaystyle v
in
\displaystyle V_{i}
, and
\displaystyle x
vanishes on all other levels,
so
\displaystyle(Ax)_{v}=0
.
For
\displaystyle v\in V_{k}
with
\displaystyle k\neq i
, if
\displaystyle i+k\leq 2b
, there are no neighbors of
\displaystyle v
in
\displaystyle V_{i}
. If
\displaystyle i+k\geq 2b+1
, then
\displaystyle v
is adjacent to all vertices of
\displaystyle V_{i}
, and on defining condition of
\displaystyle W_{i}
, we obtain
\displaystyle(Ax)_{v}=\sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}=0.
Thus,
\displaystyle Ax=0
. Similarly,
\displaystyle Dx=d_{i}x=(p^{i}-1)x
on
\displaystyle V_{i}
. Consequently, we have

A_{\alpha}x=\alpha Dx+(1-\alpha)=\alpha(p^{i}-1)x.

Thus, every nonzero vector in
\displaystyle W_{i}
is an eigenvector with corresponding eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\alpha(p^{i}-1),
for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b.
Consider the action of the matrix
\displaystyle A
on clique
\displaystyle V_{i}
, for
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b.
For
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, the neighbors of
\displaystyle v
in the support of
\displaystyle x
are the other vertices of
\displaystyle V_{i}
, so we have

(Ax)_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in V_{i}\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}x_{u}=\sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}-x_{v}=-x_{v}.

For
\displaystyle v\in V_{k}
with
\displaystyle k\neq i
, either
\displaystyle i+k\leq 2b
, in this case
\displaystyle v
has no neighbors in
\displaystyle V_{i}
,
or
\displaystyle i+k\geq 2b+1
, and in this case
\displaystyle v
is adjacent to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
. So, it gives us
\displaystyle(Ax)_{v}=\sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}=0.
Thus,
\displaystyle Ax=-x
on its support, and
\displaystyle Dx=d_{i}x=(p^{i}-2)x
on
\displaystyle V_{i}
. So, we obtain

A_{\alpha}x=\alpha Dx+(1-\alpha)Ax=\alpha(p^{i}-2)x+(1-\alpha)(-x)=\bigl(\alpha(p^{i}-1)-1\bigr)x.

So, every nonzero vector in
\displaystyle W_{i}
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\alpha(p^{i}-1)-1,
for
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b.

In either case
\displaystyle W_{i}
is
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
–invariant and contributes an eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}
with
multiplicity
\displaystyle\dim W_{i}=n_{i}-1
. Summing over
\displaystyle i=1,\dots,2b
, the total dimension of
\displaystyle W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2b}W_{i}
is

\sum_{i=1}^{2b}(n_{i}-1)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2b}n_{i}\right)-2b=(p^{2b}-1)-2b=p^{2b}-(2b+1).

Thus,
\displaystyle 2b

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
eigenvalues are yet to be determined. Now, consider vectors that are constant on each subset
\displaystyle V_{i}
.
Consider
\displaystyle U_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{V(\varGamma(R))}:x\text{ is constant on each }V_{i}\},
so that
\displaystyle\dim U_{0}=2b
, and
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{V(\varGamma(R))}=W\oplus U_{0},
since

\dim W+\dim U_{0}=(p^{2b}-(2b+1))+2b=p^{2b}-1=|V(\varGamma(R))|.

As, every vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
has the same number of neighbors in each
\displaystyle V_{j}
, so the partition
\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{2b}V_{i}
is equitable, it implies that
\displaystyle U_{0}
is invariant under
\displaystyle A
, and hence under
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
. Let
\displaystyle x\in U_{0}
and write
\displaystyle x_{i}
for the common value of
\displaystyle x
on
\displaystyle V_{i}
. Then
\displaystyle x
is encoded by the column vector
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{2b})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2b}.
For
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, we have
\displaystyle(Ax)_{v}=\sum_{j=1}^{2b}Q_{ij}x_{j},
where
\displaystyle Q_{ij}
is the number of neighbors in
\displaystyle V_{j}
of a given vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
. From the adjacency rule
\displaystyle i+j\geq 2b+1
and the structure for
\displaystyle a=2b+1
, we have the following facts: If
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b
,
\displaystyle V_{i}
is independent, so
\displaystyle Q_{ii}=0
, and
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex in
\displaystyle V_{j}
for
\displaystyle j\geq 2b+1-i
, hence
\displaystyle Q_{ii}=0,
and

Q_{ij}=\begin{cases}n_{j},&j\neq i,\ j\geq 2b+1-i,\\
0,&j\leq 2b-i.\end{cases}

For
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b
,
\displaystyle V_{i}
constitutes a clique, implying that each vertex
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
is adjacent to the remaining
\displaystyle n_{i}-1
vertices within
\displaystyle V_{i}
and to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{j}
for
\displaystyle j\geq 2b+1-i
, where
\displaystyle j\neq i
. So,
\displaystyle Q_{ii}=n_{i}-1,
and

Q_{ij}=\begin{cases}n_{j},&j\neq i,\ j\geq 2b+1-i,\\
0,&j\leq 2b-i.\end{cases}

Thus, on
\displaystyle U_{0}
,
\displaystyle Ax
gives rise to
\displaystyle Q\mathbf{x}
, and
\displaystyle Dx
is equivalent to
\displaystyle D^{\ast}\mathbf{x},

where
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{2b})
.
By applying the definition
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}=\alpha D+(1-\alpha)A
, it follows that the operation on the subspace can be simplified to
\displaystyle\bigl(\alpha D^{\ast}+(1-\alpha)Q\bigr)\mathbf{x}=B\mathbf{x}
. Consequently, the matrix
\displaystyle B
serves as the representation for the restriction of
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
to the subspace
\displaystyle U_{0}
when expressed in the basis of indicator vectors
\displaystyle\{\mathbf{1}_{V_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{V_{2b}}\}
. In this context,
\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{V_{i}}
denotes the characteristic vector for each respective set
\displaystyle V_{i}
. Given that
\displaystyle\dim U_{0}=2b
, the eigenvalues of this restricted operator correspond precisely to the simple eigenvalues
\displaystyle\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{2b}\}
of the
\displaystyle 2b\times 2b
matrix
\displaystyle B
.
The matrices
\displaystyle D^{\ast},Q
and
\displaystyle B
are as in the statement. In matrix form, we write
\displaystyle B=(B_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2b}
in
\displaystyle 2\times 2
block form as
\displaystyle B=\begin{pmatrix}B^{(11)}&B^{(12)}\\[4.0pt]
B^{(21)}&B^{(22)}\end{pmatrix},
where each block is of size
\displaystyle b\times b
. More precisely,
\displaystyle B^{(11)}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha d_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\alpha d_{2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\alpha d_{b}\end{pmatrix}
, and the top–right block has the form

B^{(12)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&(1-\alpha)n_{2b}\\[2.0pt]
0&0&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{2b-1}&(1-\alpha)n_{2b}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\[2.0pt]
0&(1-\alpha)n_{b+2}&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{2b-1}&(1-\alpha)n_{2b}\\[2.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{b+1}&(1-\alpha)n_{b+2}&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{2b-1}&(1-\alpha)n_{2b}\end{pmatrix}.

The bottom–left block is

B^{(21)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&(1-\alpha)n_{b}\\[2.0pt]
0&0&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{b-1}&(1-\alpha)n_{b}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\[2.0pt]
0&(1-\alpha)n_{2}&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{b-1}&(1-\alpha)n_{b}\\[2.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{1}&(1-\alpha)n_{2}&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{b-1}&(1-\alpha)n_{b}\end{pmatrix}.

Finally, the bottom–right block is

B^{(22)}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha d_{b+1}+(1-\alpha)(n_{b+1}-1)&(1-\alpha)n_{b+2}&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{2b}\\[2.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{b+1}&\alpha d_{b+2}+(1-\alpha)(n_{b+2}-1)&\cdots&(1-\alpha)n_{2b}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[2.0pt]
(1-\alpha)n_{b+1}&(1-\alpha)n_{b+2}&\cdots&\alpha d_{2b}+(1-\alpha)(n_{2b}-1)\end{pmatrix},

where for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 2b
, we have

d_{i}=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&1\leq i\leq b,\\[3.0pt]
p^{i}-2,&b+1\leq i\leq 2b,\end{cases}\qquad n_{i}=(p-1)p^{2b-i}.

We will illustrate Theorem
3.2
by the following example, with
\displaystyle b=2
and
\displaystyle p=2
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
the zero–divisor graph of
\displaystyle R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]/\langle x^{5}\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]/(x^{5})
.
Then
\displaystyle V(\varGamma(R))=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3}\cup V_{4},
where
\displaystyle|V_{1}|=2^{3}=8,|V_{2}|=4,|V_{3}|=2,|V_{4}|=1,
and
\displaystyle|V(\varGamma(R))|=2^{4}-1=15
. By Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
(with
\displaystyle p=2
,
\displaystyle b=2
),
the degrees of vertices in each
\displaystyle V_{i}
are:
\displaystyle d_{1}=1,d_{2}=3,d_{3}=6,
and
\displaystyle d_{4}=14.
Thus, for
\displaystyle p=2
,
\displaystyle b=2
, we have the following eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\alpha(2^{1}-1)=\alpha,

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{mult}(\lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=|V_{1}|-1=8-1=7,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha(2^{2}-1)=3\alpha,

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{mult}(\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=|V_{2}|-1=4-1=3,

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=\alpha(2^{3}-1)-1=7\alpha-1,

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{mult}(\lambda_{3})

\displaystyle=|V_{3}|-1=2-1=1,

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}

\displaystyle=\alpha(2^{4}-1)-1=15\alpha-1,

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{mult}(\lambda_{4})

\displaystyle=|V_{4}|-1=1-1=0,

where
\displaystyle\operatorname{mult}(\lambda_{i})
is the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}.
Hence
\displaystyle\lambda_{4}
does not actually appear in the spectrum as its multiplicity is zero. The remaining
\displaystyle 2b=4
eigenvalues arise from the restriction of
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
to the
subspace
\displaystyle U_{0}
, which has dimension
\displaystyle\dim U_{0}=4
. As in Theorem
3.2
, this
restriction is represented (in the basis
\displaystyle\{\mathbf{1}_{V_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{V_{4}}\}
)
by the
\displaystyle 4\times 4
matrix

B=\alpha D^{\ast}+(1-\alpha)Q,

where
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})
and
\displaystyle Q=(Q_{ij})
is the
quotient matrix of the adjacency matrix with respect to the partition

\displaystyle V_{1},\dots,V_{4}
. Also,
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(1,3,6,14)
, the matrices
\displaystyle Q
and
\displaystyle B
are given below:

Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&1\\
0&0&2&1\\
0&4&1&1\\
8&4&2&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0&0&1-\alpha\\[3.0pt]
0&3\alpha&2(1-\alpha)&1-\alpha\\[3.0pt]
0&4(1-\alpha)&5\alpha+1&1-\alpha\\[3.0pt]
8(1-\alpha)&4(1-\alpha)&2(1-\alpha)&14\alpha\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\displaystyle\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\theta_{4}
be the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle B
. Then the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
–spectrum of

\displaystyle\varGamma\bigl(\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]/(x^{5})\bigr)
is

\bigl\{\underbrace{\alpha,\dots,\alpha}_{7},\underbrace{3\alpha,\dots,3\alpha}_{3},7\alpha-1,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\theta_{4}\bigr\}.

From Theorem
3.2
, we have the following consequence.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime and
\displaystyle b\geq 1
a positive integer, and the notation of
Theorem
3.2
,
\displaystyle n_{i}=(p-1)p^{2b-i}
and
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{2b})
,
and let
\displaystyle Q
be the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle A(\varGamma(R))
with respect to
the partition
\displaystyle\{V_{1},\dots,V_{2b}\}
.
Then the following hold.

1.

The adjacency spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is given by

\bigcup_{i=1}^{b}\{0^{[n_{i}-1]}\}\cup\bigcup_{i=b+1}^{2b}\{(-1)^{[n_{i}-1]}\}\cup\sigma(Q),

where
\displaystyle\sigma(Q)
is the spectrum of
\displaystyle Q.

2.

The signless Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
if given by

\bigcup_{i=1}^{b}\{(p^{i}-1)^{[n_{i}-1]}\}\cup\bigcup_{i=b+1}^{2b}\{(p^{i}-3)^{[n_{i}-1]}\}\cup\sigma(D^{\ast}+Q),

where
\displaystyle\sigma(D^{\ast}+Q)
is the spectrum of
\displaystyle D^{\ast}+Q=2B\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
.

Proof.
Let
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(\alpha)
be the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
. Then by Theorem
3.2
, we have the following cases.

(1) The adjacency matrix of
\displaystyle G
is
\displaystyle A(G)=A_{0}(G)
. So, with
\displaystyle\alpha=0
in Theorem
3.2
, we obtain

\lambda_{i}(0)=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq b,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda_{i}(0)=-1,\quad b+1\leq i\leq 2b,

each with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1
. Theorem
3.2
further shows that the other
\displaystyle 2b
eigenvalues of
\displaystyle A_{0}(G)=A(G)
coincide with the eigenvalues of

B(0)=0\cdot D^{\ast}+(1-0)Q^{\ast}=Q^{\ast},

that is, the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle A(G)
corresponding to the partition
\displaystyle\{V_{i}\}
.

(2) The signless Laplacian of
\displaystyle G
is defined by

Q(G)=D(G)+A(G)=2A_{1/2}(G).

Hence, if
\displaystyle\mu
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle A_{1/2}(G)
, then
\displaystyle 2\mu
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle Q(G)
with equal multiplicities.
For for
\displaystyle\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2}
. Theorem
3.2
implies that

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr),
for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b,
and

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(p^{i}-1\bigr)-1=\tfrac{p^{i}-3}{2},
for
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b,
each with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1
.
Hence, the corresponding eigenvalues of
\displaystyle Q(G)=2A_{1/2}(G)
are

p^{i}-1,\quad 1\leq i\leq b,\quad\text{and}\quad p^{i}-3,\quad b+1\leq i\leq 2b,

each with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1
. The remaining
\displaystyle 2b
eigenvalues of
\displaystyle A_{1/2}(G)
are the eigenvalues of the following matrix

B\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{2}D^{\ast}+\frac{1}{2}Q^{\ast}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(D^{\ast}+Q^{\ast}\bigr).

Multiplying by
\displaystyle 2
, the remaining
\displaystyle 2b
eigenvalues of
\displaystyle Q(\varGamma(R))
are exactly the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle D^{\ast}+Q^{\ast}
. This proves part (2).

The following consequence of Theorem
3.2
shows that the Laplacian eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
are integers.

Corollary 3.5
.

The Laplacian matrix
\displaystyle L(\varGamma(R))
is integral, and its spectrum is

\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq 2b\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}\{(p^{i}-1)^{[n_{i}]}\}\cup\{(p^{b}-1)^{[n_{b}-1]}\},

where
\displaystyle n_{i}=|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-i}
.

Proof.

We continue with the notation of Theorem
3.2
. Recall that
\displaystyle V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{2b}V_{i},n_{i}=|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-i},
and for
\displaystyle v\in V_{i}
, we have

d_{i}=\deg(v)=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&1\leq i\leq b,\\
p^{i}-2,&b+1\leq i\leq 2b.\end{cases}

It is known that

\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\bigl(A_{\alpha}(G)-A_{\beta}(G)\bigr)=D(G)-A(G)=L(G).

For
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq b
, we get the Laplacian eigenvalues

\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\Big(\alpha(p^{i}-1)-(\beta(p^{i}-1))\Big)=p^{i}-1,

with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1.
Similarly, for
\displaystyle b+1\leq i\leq 2b
, we have the Laplacian eigenvalues

\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\Big(\alpha(p^{i}-1)-1-(\beta(p^{i}-1))-1\Big)=p^{i}-1,

each with multiplicity
\displaystyle n_{i}-1.
Let
\displaystyle D^{\ast}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{2b})
be the diagonal matrix, and let
\displaystyle Q^{\ast}=(Q_{ij})
be the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle A(G)
with respect to
\displaystyle\{V_{i}\}
as in Theorem
3.2
. Then the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(G)
is

B(\alpha)=\alpha D^{\ast}+(1-\alpha)Q^{\ast}.

For
\displaystyle\alpha=1
and
\displaystyle\alpha=0,
we have
\displaystyle B(1)=D^{\ast},
and
\displaystyle B(0)=Q^{\ast},
so, for any
\displaystyle\alpha\neq\beta
, where

\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}\bigl(B(\alpha)-B(\beta)\bigr)=D^{\ast}-Q^{\ast},

where
\displaystyle D^{\ast}-Q^{\ast}
is the quotient matrix of
\displaystyle L(G)
with respect to the partition
\displaystyle\{V_{i}\}
. Denote this
\displaystyle 2b\times 2b
matrix by
\displaystyle\overline{L}=D^{\ast}-Q^{\ast},
with

\overline{L}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0&0&-n_{2b}\\[3.0pt]
0&d_{2}&0&\cdots&0&-n_{2b-1}&-n_{2b}\\[3.0pt]
0&0&d_{3}&\cdots&-n_{2b-2}&-n_{2b-1}&-n_{2b}\\[3.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\[3.0pt]
0&0&-n_{3}&\cdots&p^{2b-2}-1-n_{2b-2}&-n_{2b-1}&-n_{2b}\\[3.0pt]
0&-n_{2}&-n_{3}&\cdots&-n_{2b-2}&p^{2b-1}-1-n_{2b-1}&-n_{2b}\\[3.0pt]
-n_{1}&-n_{2}&-n_{3}&\cdots&-n_{2b-2}&-n_{2b-1}&p^{2b}-1-n_{2b}\end{pmatrix},

where, for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 2b
, we have

d_{i}=\begin{cases}p^{i}-1,&1\leq i\leq b,\\[3.0pt]
p^{i}-2,&b+1\leq i\leq 2b,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad n_{i}=(p-1)p^{2b-i}.

Next, we calculate the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
. It is known that
\displaystyle 0
is the eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\overline{L},
due to positive semidefinite property of the Laplacian matrix of a connected
\displaystyle\varGamma(R).

For
\displaystyle r\geq 1
, we recall the tail identity,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=2b-r+1}^{2b}n_{j}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2b-r+1}^{2b}(p-1)p^{2b-j}=(p-1)\sum_{t=0}^{r-1}p^{t}=p^{r}-1.

(5)

For the eigenvalues
\displaystyle p^{k}-1
of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
, for
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq b-1
, we fix
\displaystyle k
with
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq b-1
, and set

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=p^{k}-1.
Now, consider a vector
\displaystyle v^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2b}
defined as

v^{(k)}_{j}=\begin{cases}0,&1\leq j<k,\\
-\varGamma_{k},&j=k,\\
1,&k<j\leq 2b-k,\\
0,&2b-k<j\leq 2b,\end{cases}

(6)

for some scalar
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
to be chosen. We claim that we can choose
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
so that
\displaystyle\overline{L}v^{(k)}=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}.

For rows of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
, if
\displaystyle i<k
, then
\displaystyle v^{(k)}_{i}=0
, and all nonzero off–diagonal entries in row
\displaystyle i
are in columns
\displaystyle j\in\{2b-i+1,\dots,2b\},
which are strictly greater than
\displaystyle 2b-k
because
\displaystyle i<k
. Thus
\displaystyle v^{(k)}_{j}=0
for all such columns, and hence

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=d_{i}v^{(k)}_{i}-\sum_{j}n_{j}v^{(k)}_{j}=0=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}_{i}.

For the
\displaystyle k
-th row of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
, the off–diagonal
\displaystyle-n_{j}
’s again exists only in columns
\displaystyle 2b-k+1,\dots,2b
, where
\displaystyle v^{(k)}_{j}=0
, so we obtain

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{k}=d_{k}v^{(k)}_{k}=(p^{k}-1)v^{(k)}_{k}=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}_{k}.

Thus the eigenvalue equation holds for
\displaystyle i=k
independently of
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
.
For the middle rows
\displaystyle k<i\leq 2b-k
, the only nonzero entries of
\displaystyle v^{(k)}
visible to row
\displaystyle i
are some of the
\displaystyle 1
’s (from the block
\displaystyle k<j\leq 2b-k
) and possibly the entry
\displaystyle-\varGamma_{k}
at position
\displaystyle j=k
. Using again the geometric-series identity (
5
) to evaluate the sums of
\displaystyle n_{j}
against the block of
\displaystyle 1
’s, we find that the contribution of the
\displaystyle 1
-block always sums to exactly
\displaystyle p^{i}-1
, matching the diagonal part. The terms involving
\displaystyle-\varGamma_{k}
cancel between the diagonal and off–diagonal contributions. Thus, we have

(\overline{L}v^{(k)})_{i}=(p^{i}-1)v^{(k)}_{i}=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}_{i},

for all
\displaystyle k<i\leq 2b-k
, again without imposing any restriction on
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
.

For bottom rows
\displaystyle i>2b-k
, the only rows that constrain
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
are the bottom ones with
\displaystyle i>2b-k
.
A direct calculation, utilizing the explicit representation of these rows and the pattern (
6
), demonstrates that all such rows provide the same scalar condition.

\varGamma_{k}p^{2b+1-k}-\varGamma_{k}p^{2b-k}-p^{2b-k}+p^{k}=0.

Upon analyzing the earlier equation for
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
, we derive

\varGamma_{k}=\frac{p^{2b-k}-p^{k}}{p^{2b-k}(p-1)}=\frac{1-p^{2k-2b}}{p-1}.

(7)

With this choice, every row of
\displaystyle\overline{L}v^{(k)}=\lambda_{k}v^{(k)}
holds, so
\displaystyle p^{k}-1
is an eigenvalue for
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq b-1
.

Now, for the eigenvalues
\displaystyle p^{k}-1
of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
, with
\displaystyle b+1\leq k\leq 2b
, we fix
\displaystyle k
with
\displaystyle b+1\leq k\leq 2b
, and set
\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=p^{k}-1
. With
\displaystyle m=2b+1-k
, consider

w^{(k)}_{j}=\begin{cases}0,&1\leq j<m,\\
-\varGamma_{k},&m\leq j\leq k-1,\\
1,&j=k,\\
0,&k<j\leq 2b,\end{cases}

(8)

for another scalar
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
to be chosen. We are required to show that
\displaystyle\overline{L}w^{(k)}=\lambda_{k}w^{(k)}.
The rows of
\displaystyle\overline{L}
, whose nonzero entries lie entirely in the zero part of
\displaystyle w^{(k)}
trivially satisfy
\displaystyle 0=0
. For rows that see the constant block of
\displaystyle-\varGamma_{k}
and the single
\displaystyle 1
at position
\displaystyle k
, a direct computation using geometric–series sums (
5
) of the
\displaystyle n_{j}
gives that the eigenvalue equation reduces to the scalar condition
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}p^{2(k-b)}-\varGamma_{k}p-p+1=0.
Its evaluation gives

\varGamma_{k}=\frac{p-1}{p^{2(k-b)}-p}=\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{1}{1+p+\cdots+p^{2(k-b)-2}}.

(9)

With the above choice of
\displaystyle\varGamma_{k}
, each row satisfies
\displaystyle(\overline{L}w^{(k)})_{i}=\lambda_{k}w^{(k)}_{i},
and
hence if follows that

\overline{L}w^{(k)}=(p^{k}-1)w^{(k)},\qquad b+1\leq k\leq 2b.

The numbers
\displaystyle p^{k}-1
are strictly increasing in
\displaystyle k
, so all these eigenvalues are distinct, and together with
\displaystyle 0
they give
\displaystyle 2b
eigenvalues for the
\displaystyle 2b\times 2b
matrix
\displaystyle\overline{L}
.

The following result due to Aouchiche and Hensen related the Laplacian eigenvalues with the distance Laplacian eigenvalues of a connected graph with diameter
\displaystyle 2.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be a connected graph on
\displaystyle n
vertices with diameter
\displaystyle D\leq 2
. Let
\displaystyle\lambda^{L}_{1}\geq\lambda^{L}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{L}_{n-1}>\lambda^{L}_{n}=0
be the Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle G
. Then the distance Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle G
is
\displaystyle 2n-\lambda^{L}_{n-1}\geq 2n-\lambda^{L}_{n-2}\geq\cdots\geq 2n-\lambda^{L}_{1}>\partial^{L}_{n}=0
. Moreover, for every
\displaystyle i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
the eigenspaces corresponding to
\displaystyle\lambda^{L}_{i}
and to
\displaystyle 2n-\lambda^{L}_{n-i}
are the same.

From Corollary
2.5
, the Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b}\rangle)
is

\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq 2b-1\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}\bigl\{(p^{i}-1)^{[n_{i}]}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{(p^{b}-1)^{[n_{b}-1]}\bigr\},

where
\displaystyle n_{i}=|V_{i}|=(p-1)p^{2b-1-i},1\leq i\leq 2b-1,
and the total number of vertices is
\displaystyle n=|V(\varGamma(R))|=p^{2b-1}-1.
By Lemma
4.1
, with Laplacian spectrum
\displaystyle\sigma(L(\varGamma(R))),
the distance Laplacian spectrum is

\{2n-\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(L(\varGamma(R)))\setminus\{0\}\}\cup\{0\},

with the same multiplicities as the corresponding Laplacian eigenvalues (and one zero eigenvalue). Since
\displaystyle 2n=2(p^{2b-1}-1)=2p^{2b-1}-2
, we obtain the distance Laplacian spectrum
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
as

\{0\}\cup\{2n-0\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq 2b-1\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}\bigl\{(2n-(p^{i}-1))^{[n_{i}]}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{(2n-(p^{b}-1))^{[n_{b}-1]}\bigr\}.

With
\displaystyle 2n=2p^{2b-1}-2
, the explicit form is

\{0\}\cup\{2p^{2b-1}-2\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq 2b-1\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}\bigl\{(2p^{2b-1}-p^{i}-1)^{[n_{i}]}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{(2p^{2b-1}-p^{b}-1)^{[n_{b}-1]}\bigr\}.

Now, for
\displaystyle\varGamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{2b+1}\rangle)
, order is
\displaystyle n=p^{2b}-1,
and
\displaystyle 2n=2(p^{2b}-1)=2p^{2b}-2.
By the Corollary
3.5
, the Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
is

\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq 2b\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}\{(p^{i}-1)^{[n_{i}]}\}\cup\{(p^{b}-1)^{[n_{b}-1]}\}.

With Lemma
4.1
,
\displaystyle\mu_{i}=2n-\lambda_{i},
the distance Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R))
is

\{2n-0\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq 2b\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}\bigl\{(2n-(p^{i}-1))^{[n_{i}]}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{(2n-(p^{b}-1))^{[n_{b}-1]}\bigr\}.

By substituting
\displaystyle 2n=2p^{2b}-2
, we derive the exact distance Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle\varGamma(R)
as

\{2p^{2b}-2\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq 2b\\
i\neq b\end{subarray}}\bigl\{(2p^{2b}-p^{i}-1)^{[n_{i}]}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{(2p^{2b}-p^{b}-1)^{[n_{b}-1]}\bigr\}.

Thus, we have the following result related to the distance Laplacian integral eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\varGamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{c}\rangle)
.

Corollary 4.2
.

The zero-divisor graph
\displaystyle\varGamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{b}\rangle)
is distance Laplacian integral.

Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated the zero-divisor graph
\displaystyle\varGamma\!\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{c}\rangle\right)
associated with the commutative ring
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{c}\rangle
, where
\displaystyle p
is a prime and
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{N}
. We explicitly determined the spectrum of the
\displaystyle A_{\alpha}
-matrix of these graphs in Theorems
2.2
and
3.2
, and as important special cases obtained the adjacency and signless Laplacian spectra. Furthermore, we proved that the Laplacian matriix of
\displaystyle\varGamma\!\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{c}\rangle\right)
have integral eigenvalues (Corollaries
3.5
and
3.5
), thereby showing that these zero-divisor graphs are Laplacian integral. As a consequence for graph of diameter
\displaystyle 2
, Corollary
4.2
shows that
\displaystyle\varGamma\!\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{c}\rangle\right)
is distance Laplacian integral. These results highlight a strong interplay between the algebraic structure of the underlying ring and the spectral properties of its zero-divisor graph, and provide a foundation for further spectral analysis of zero-divisor graphs arising from other families of commutative rings.
