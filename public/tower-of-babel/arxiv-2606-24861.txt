First-Order Recoverability Collapse in Self-Referential Information Decoders
Pieter van Rooyen

Abstract
We analyze adaptive systems that couple inference to irreversible action under sustained nonequilibrium informational driving. Treating information processing as a thermodynamic load, we model such systems as finite-capacity decoders whose irreversible commitments eliminate counterfactual future options. We characterize recoverable operation using a feasibility margin and a regime-level stability diagnostic, which together determine when irreversible action remains admissible. We show that, under sustained overload—when induced informational flux exceeds effective integrative capacity—loss of recoverability and divergence of the stability diagnostic arise as structural consequences of capacity saturation, independent of optimization objectives, control policies, or physical substrate. Increased capacity alone does not restore recoverability: absent explicit certification or gating, higher throughput accelerates non-recoverable loss — with contemporary high-throughput artificial-intelligence systems a concrete application. Making this feedback explicit — each uncertified commitment spawning, on average, α \alpha new candidates — converts the continuous feasibility transition into a first-order one: lucid and collapsed states coexist inside a cusp-organized bistable region with closed-form spinodals, collapse pre-empts the continuous divergence at finite stability ratio, recovery is hysteretic, and for α ≥ 1 \alpha\geq 1 load reduction alone cannot restore recoverable operation. Cascade sizes are bounded by the grounded fraction of input: a genealogy × \times congestion factorization sets a cutoff that grows as grounding shrinks, scale-free cascades surviving only in the self-referential corner, with the expected mean-field τ = 3 / 2 \tau=3/2 exponent recovered away from the boundary and each cascade carrying a Landauer-priced burst of synthetic entropy; event-driven simulations confirm the cutoff law and the phase structure. This supplies the statistical mechanics of the “metastable failures” documented in large-scale distributed systems. The analysis is constraint-based and substrate-agnostic, establishing recoverable dissipation as a necessary physical criterion for decoder stability in sustained high-flux informational regimes.

Several established frameworks address adaptation under informational and

I.1
High-Flux Regimes and the Emergence of Effective Irreversibility

In this regime the decoder is forced to act on partially validated internal
macrostates — not because uncertainty is unusually high, but because
deferral is no longer admissible — and many such actions (allocations,
commitments, irreversible state updates) alter the future state space itself.
This effective irreversibility does not depend on whether the internal
computation is logically reversible in principle. Even if inference were
implemented via reversible dynamics, the coupling of internal macrostates to
external commitments renders the closed-loop system non-invertible. Once an
output conditions an irreversible physical update—such as a transaction,
allocation, or policy decision—the counterfactual trajectories not taken are
eliminated from the future state space.

In high-flux regimes, errors therefore propagate not primarily as incorrect
beliefs, but as path-dependent contractions of admissible future trajectories.
Subsequent evidence may improve posterior estimates, but it cannot restore option
space eliminated by earlier commitments. Recoverability thus becomes a property
of
trajectories
, rather than instantaneous states, as formalized in
Section
III
.

Physical Framework

load and capacity

II.1
The Ambiguity Gradient as a Statistical Property of Large State Spaces

We now formalize the thermodynamic concepts introduced qualitatively above.
When gradients are weak, systems remain near equilibrium and respond linearly

de Groot and Mazur 1984
;
Callen 1985
; beyond linear response, matter reorganizes
into
dissipative structures
— convection cells, chemical
oscillations, fluid vortices — that enhance transport and stabilize entropy
production under continued driving, at the cost of structural complexity and
ongoing dissipation
Prigogine and Stengers 1984
;
Nicolis and Prigogine 1977
. England extended this
perspective to adaptive matter: driven systems preferentially evolve toward
configurations that dissipate imposed work more effectively, a thermodynamic
account of adaptation without teleology
England 2013
.

In what follows, we apply this nonequilibrium framework explicitly to
informational processes. An imposed informational gradient induces flux
through systems that encode, transform, and act upon information; when that
flux exceeds the capacity of existing integrative mechanisms, structural
reorganization becomes thermodynamically admissible — not as an optimization
objective, but as a response to sustained driving.

II.2
Information as a Thermodynamic Load

Information processing is physically instantiated and therefore subject to
thermodynamic constraints. The storage, transformation, and erasure of
information correspond to controlled state transitions of material degrees of
freedom and necessarily incur energetic and entropic costs whenever they are
logically irreversible. This connection is formalized by Landauer’s principle
and its extensions, which bound the minimum heat dissipation associated with
irreversible information processing

Landauer 1961
;
Bennett 1982
. More generally, information-bearing degrees of
freedom participate in entropy production whenever they are coarse-grained,
irreversibly updated, or committed to action, independent of the semantic content
of the information involved
Shannon 1948
;
Cover and Thomas 2006
.

We model this burden as an
informational load
: a rate at which uncertainty
is induced into a system by measurements, signals, coordination demands, or
internal update pressures that must be integrated to maintain coherent
operation. Following Shannon’s formulation of information as uncertainty
reduction
Shannon 1948
, let
R_{\mathrm{self}}(t)
denote the induced
informational flux, measured as the rate at which new informational microstates
must be processed, interpreted, or acted upon. Sustained informational flux
implies sustained entropy production, because acquisition, buffering,
coarse-graining, and eventual erasure of information are physically irreversible
operations that dissipate free energy into the environment

Shannon 1948
;
Landauer 1961
;
Bennett 1982
.

Dissipation bridge (operational).

The induced informational flux
R_{\mathrm{self}}(t)
admits a direct physical
interpretation through the thermodynamic cost of logical irreversibility. For any
admissible operational proxy of
R_{\mathrm{self}}(t)
, there exists a
corresponding lower bound on entropy production arising from irreversible
informational updates. Let
\dot{N}_{\mathrm{irr}}(t)
denote the rate of logically
irreversible bit erasures implied by these updates over the decision horizon.
The associated heat dissipation then satisfies

\dot{Q}(t)\;\geq\;k_{B}T\ln 2\;\dot{N}_{\mathrm{irr}}(t),

(1)

independent of architecture, algorithm, or substrate. Differences between
biological and artificial systems enter only through the mapping between
informational updates and
\dot{N}_{\mathrm{irr}}(t)
—for example, synaptic reset
events in neural systems versus bit erasures in silicon—without altering the
existence of this bound.

Physical instantiation of artificial computation.

Within this framework, artificial intelligence is not treated as abstract
“software” layered atop a physical substrate: any physically realizable
computation corresponds to state transitions of matter and energy and is
constrained by finite capacity, dissipation, and irreversibility

Lloyd 2000
;
Lloyd 2002
. In contemporary artificial systems, logically
irreversible updates are accompanied by Joule heating, leakage, and
device-level entropy production; the software/hardware distinction is
descriptive rather than physical, and erasure, commitment, buffering, and
coarse-graining are physical events subject to thermodynamic constraints.

Within this physical interpretation, the regime commonly labeled
“hallucination” in generative architectures has a specific thermodynamic
signature: logically irreversible updates whose Landauer cost is paid but
whose resulting macrostate retains no recoverable mapping to the driving
environmental state. The associated entropy production is
synthetic

— internally generated rather than anchored by certified interaction with
the environment
Bennett 1982
;
Cover and Thomas 2006
;
Jaynes 1957
. This is formalized
as a regime condition in Definition
4
and
operationalized through the stability ratio
SR(t)
in
Section
III.3
.

Any physical system tasked with integrating informational flux possesses a
finite
integrative capacity

C_{\mathrm{self}}(t)
, determined by its
substrate, architecture, temporal constraints, and validation mechanisms.
To formalize this balance, we define the
feasibility
margin

\mathcal{M}(t)=C_{\mathrm{self}}(t)-R_{\mathrm{self}}(t),

(2)

which measures the instantaneous slack between induced informational load and
integrative capacity. When
\mathcal{M}(t)\geq 0
, uncertainty can in principle be
managed through delayed commitment, validation, or revision. When

\mathcal{M}(t)
is persistently driven toward zero or negative values, linear
adaptation fails and new dissipative regimes become admissible, mirroring the
behavior of nonequilibrium physical systems driven beyond their linear response
range
Prigogine and Stengers 1984
;
de Groot and Mazur 1984
.

Feasibility and Recoverability: A Substrate-Independent Constraint

We model a
decoder
as any physical or informational
process
that integrates
uncertain observations into internal state updates and downstream commitments,
some of which are irreversible

;
Cover and Thomas 2006
. All such systems
operate under finite rate and capacity constraints imposed by substrate,
architecture, and the latency of validation prior to action.

Remark

(Scope: the single-decoder case)
.

We treat throughout a
single
decoder that coarse-grains its observed
microstates into one macrostate and takes a hard decision — the canonical
single-stream case, in the sense that the single-link Gaussian (and, for the
arrival statistics, Poisson) channel is the canonical starting point in
information theory. The realistic generalization is a
joint
decoder
that, rather than committing per stream, estimates the several latent states
— some estimable within a coherence bandwidth, some independent (diversity),
some mutually correlated — and resolves the hard decision by joint,
mutual-information detection that combines them. (This is MIMO-like in spirit,
but the gain here is joint estimation and combining, not transmitter
channel-state feedback.) Cross-stream agreement then supplies
internal

certification that a single stream cannot (the error-correcting role noted
in Sec.
III.4
). This work is deliberately the
single-stream baseline those joint extensions build on
(Sec.
VI
).

As established in Section
II.2
, sustained informational flux entails
irreversible coarse-graining and therefore nonzero entropy production.
R_{\mathrm{self}}(t)

thus represents not merely informational load, but the rate at which the decoder is
driven away from equilibrium by its environment.

Let
C_{\mathrm{self}}(t)
denote the decoder’s
effective integrative capacity

over the relevant decision horizon. Capacity here is not limited to raw computational
throughput; it includes the ability to validate interpretations, certify internal
macrostates prior to action, buffer uncertainty, and preserve coherence under irreversible
updates
de Groot and Mazur 1984
;
Callen 1985
.

When the feasibility margin [Eq. (
2

\mathcal{M}(t)\geq 0
, the decoder can in principle certify internal
macrostates and defer or revise irreversible commitments; as

III.1
Minimal model: delayed certification in a finite-state decoder

We now present a minimal, falsifiable model in which all assumptions are
explicit. Over a finite environmental microstate space
X
, the decoder
observes
y_{k}=h(x_{k})

single-decoder baseline analysed here deliberately does not assume it; in its
absence, recoverability must be secured downstream by certifying

before
commitment, which is why feasibility and local invertibility, not a code rate,

distinguish which equivalence class in

prior to action,

\tau_{\mathrm{cert}}

(Recoverability Breakdown Under Sustained High-Flux)

Consider a decoder that couples inference to irreversible downstream commitments

Assume:

(A1)

Irreversible commitments eliminate counterfactual future options

(A2)

(A3)

III.3
Entropy Production and the Stability Ratio

Physical meaning of internal entropy production.

Internal entropy production
\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(t)
is defined here through
logical irreversibility rather than microscopic disorder.
Each uncertified internal commitment corresponds to a logically irreversible
state update that erases counterfactual distinctions among environmental
microstates, independent of the physical substrate on which the update is
implemented.

By Landauer’s principle, any such erasure dissipates at least

k_{B}T\ln 2
of heat per lost bit—equivalently, produces at least

k_{B}\ln 2
of entropy—providing a substrate-independent lower bound on
internal entropy production
Landauer 1961
;
Bennett 1982

(no identification with microscopic thermodynamic entropy beyond this bound is
implied; see Appendix
A
). Accordingly,

\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(t)\;\geq\;k_{B}\ln 2\;\dot{N}_{\mathrm{uncert}}(t),

(5)

where
\dot{N}_{\mathrm{uncert}}(t)
denotes the rate of uncertified internal
commitments over the decision horizon.

Definition of the stability ratio.

Within the Recoverable Self-Coding (RSC) framework, the
stability
ratio
is defined primarily as a population (count) ratio — the order
parameter of the feasibility transition:

SR(t)\;\triangleq\;\frac{\dot{N}_{\mathrm{uncert}}(t)}{\dot{N}_{\mathrm{cert}}(t)},

(6)

the rate of irreversible commitments taken without completed certification
relative to those taken with it. Its thermodynamic content is the one-way
Landauer bound of Eq. (
5
):
every uncertified commitment erases an unresolved alternative at a strictly
positive entropy cost.

Here
\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(t)
and
\dot{S}_{\mathrm{anc}}(t)
denote the

class-resolved
entropy productions of the commitment stream —
synthetic (produced by uncertified commitment) and anchored (produced by
certified, externally validated commitment). Both are non-negative and their
sum is the stream’s total entropy production; they are
not
the
production/exchange pair of the Prigogine balance, whose exchange term is a
signed flux. The entropy-production ratio

SR_{\mathrm{EP}}\triangleq\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}/\dot{S}_{\mathrm{anc}}
tracks the order parameter
(
6
) with a bounded
O(1)
prefactor in the explicit driven
model of Appendix
D
, and coincides with it exactly when
the per-event entropy budgets of the two classes are equal; the prefactor
renormalizes the threshold
SR_{c}
, never the divergence exponent.

An order-of-magnitude remark pre-empts misreading. The floor
k_{B}\ln 2
per
commitment (
\sim\!3\times 10^{-21}\,
J at room temperature) is many orders of
magnitude below the actual dissipation of any physical decoder —
contemporary accelerators dissipate
\gtrsim\!10^{10}\,k_{B}T
per generated
token. Nothing in what follows depends on the numerical value of the floor:
its role is structural. Strict positivity and one-way-ness lock the
direction of the synthetic/anchored decomposition; the divergences and
discontinuities are carried by count ratios, with per-event costs entering
only as bounded prefactors. The floor is the substrate-independent component
of the cost; the remainder is substrate overhead.

Where the dissipation comes from.

One subtlety must be settled, because the M/M/1 backlog of
Proposition
1
is, in its stationary state, a reversible
(detailed-balanced) birth–death chain
Kelly 1979
: its housekeeping entropy
production is identically zero. The divergence of
SR
is therefore
not

the steady-state dissipation of the queue, and no per-event cost diverges
anywhere: the queue sets only the
counting statistics
of the two
commitment classes, while the finite per-event entropy is carried by the
certification event itself — a
driven
two-level realization of a
single certification produces strictly positive but bounded entropy
production (Appendix
C
). The divergence of

SR_{\mathrm{EP}}=\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}/\dot{S}_{\mathrm{anc}}
is thus a

population
effect: finite costs multiplied by a diverging count ratio.
In the explicit construction of Appendix
D
, where
certification
is
the spin’s first passage (so the exponential service
of the queue is derived from the spin dynamics rather than postulated beside
it), both class-resolved productions emerge from one driven population and

SR_{\mathrm{EP}}
inherits the exponent of the count ratio with a provably
bounded prefactor. What the thermodynamic layer adds beyond the (reversible)
queueing statistics is therefore specific, not decorative: it fixes the

sign
— the one-way-ness of the synthetic/anchored decomposition,
which the detailed-balanced count process alone does not orient — and it
predicts a
discontinuity
in
SR_{\mathrm{EP}}
at the fold
(Appendix
D
). The Landauer floor enters only as a bounded
prefactor and is numerically negligible; its role is to lock direction and
supply the entropic order parameter, not to set a scale.

Both
\mathcal{M}(t)\geq 0
and
SR(t)\leq SR_{c}
are treated as
constraints

defining admissible action regimes, not optimization targets; the threshold

SR_{c}=O(1)
is a calibrated free parameter of the framework, not a universal
constant.
SR(t)
is a coarse-grained
diagnostic
of operating regime
— not a microscopic observable, a conserved quantity, or an objective.
Where a normalized margin is needed,

1-\mathrm{CR}=(C_{\mathrm{self}}-R_{\mathrm{self}})/C_{\mathrm{self}}
; the
rate-valued
\mathcal{M}
of Eq. (
2
) is retained
throughout. As with
R_{\mathrm{self}}
and
C_{\mathrm{self}}
, the
class-resolved productions are defined up to equivalence classes of
admissible operational proxies preserving ordering and divergence over the
decision horizon.

Proposition 2

(Necessity under sustained overload)
.

Under the conditions of Structural Claim
1
, sustained
overload saturates certification capacity, implying that a nonzero fraction of
internally generated state updates must be acted upon without completed local
invertibility certification. Divergence of the stability ratio
SR(t)
is
therefore not a design choice, control objective, or contingent diagnostic
outcome, but a structural consequence of capacity saturation.

When the induced informational flux satisfies

R_{\mathrm{self}}(t)\geq C_{\mathrm{self}}(t)
over a nonzero decision horizon,
the fraction of irreversible commitments taken on uncertified internal states is
necessarily bounded away from zero. By Assumption (A3), each such uncertified
transition is logically irreversible and contributes strictly positive internal
entropy production. Consequently
SR(t)
obeys the closed-form
divergence of Proposition
1
,
SR\to(\mathcal{M}\Delta t)^{-1}

as the feasibility margin collapses
\mathcal{M}(t)\to 0
— and with it the
entropy-production ratio
SR_{\mathrm{EP}}
, up to its bounded prefactor.

Synthetic entropy production
\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}(t)
therefore grows at
least proportionally with sustained informational flux, while anchored
production
\dot{S}_{\mathrm{anc}}(t)
remains capacity-limited. The stability
ratio thus increases independently of any optimization
principle, control policy, or preference over dissipation pathways. In this
sense, elevated or divergent
SR(t)
follows as a corollary of
Structural Claim
1
(proof implicit in
Appendix
B
): once feasibility margins collapse,
loss of recoverability is necessarily accompanied by dominance of synthetic
entropy.

Operational interpretation.

As
R_{\mathrm{self}}(t)\to C_{\mathrm{self}}(t)
, validation latency forces
an increasing fraction of updates to occur without completed certification,
elevating
SR(t)
. A decoder may continue to generate internally consistent
inferences even as
SR(t)
rises; what changes is the admissibility of
irreversible action.

Feasibility determines whether a decoder can operate at all; the next question is
whether its operation remains structurally recoverable once irreversible updates
are permitted.

III.4
Local Invertibility

Definition 3

(Local invertibility)
.

Let
x_{t}
denote an environmental microstate,
y_{t}
an observation,
z_{t}
an internal macrostate, and
a_{t}
an irreversible action. A measurement–action regime is locally invertible over a horizon
\Delta t
if sufficiently small perturbations in
x_{t}
induce distinguishable distributions over
z_{t:t+\Delta t}
prior to irreversible commitment, and if corrective actions remain admissible before
a_{t}
eliminates future options.

Local invertibility is the weakest sufficient condition
ruling out irreversible many-to-one collapse prior to action; it is necessary
only for decoders without external reset or rollback, and alternative
mechanisms (robust redundancy, reversible action sets, error-correcting
commitments) are admissible provided they equivalently preserve
counterfactual structure. The following lemma provides a falsifiable
sufficient condition for its preservation over a finite horizon.

Lemma 1

(Sufficient condition for local invertibility)
.

Let
x_{t}
denote the environmental microstate,
y_{t}
the observation,
z_{t}
the internal macrostate,
and
a_{t}
an irreversible commitment taken at time
t_{a}
.
Let
\tau_{\mathrm{cert}}(t)
denote the characteristic latency required to certify (validate/ground)
a candidate macrostate
z_{t}
against the environment, and let
\tau_{\mathrm{upd}}(t)
denote the
characteristic time between successive internally generated candidate updates that could trigger
commitment.

Assume that, over a decision horizon
\Delta t
, there exists a measurable certification gate

q(t)\in[0,1]
such that irreversible commitment is suppressed whenever certification is not complete.
If there exist constants
\zeta_{\star}>1
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that, for all
t
in the operating
interval,

\zeta(t)\;\triangleq\;\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{cert}}(t)}{\tau_{\mathrm{upd}}(t)}\;\leq\;\zeta_{\star}\quad\text{and}\quad q(t)\;\geq\;1-\delta,

(7)

then the measurement–action regime is locally invertible over
\Delta t
in the operational sense
of Definition
3
: with probability at least
1-\delta
, irreversible
commitments occur only after a certification cycle has completed, so small perturbations in
x_{t}

that are resolvable by the certifier remain distinguishable in
z_{t:t+\Delta t}
prior to
commitment. Under the minimal-model proxies
R_{\mathrm{self}}\sim\tau_{\mathrm{upd}}^{-1}
and

C_{\mathrm{self}}\sim\tau_{\mathrm{cert}}^{-1}
, the timescale ratio coincides with the capacity
ratio,
\zeta=\tau_{\mathrm{cert}}/\tau_{\mathrm{upd}}=R_{\mathrm{self}}/C_{\mathrm{self}}=\mathrm{CR}
,
so the gating threshold
\zeta\gtrsim 1
and the feasibility boundary
\mathrm{CR}\to 1
mark the
same saturation. This coincidence is a feature of the minimal model, not a
general identity: the invertibility timescale ratio
\zeta
and the capacity ratio

\mathrm{CR}
are in general
independent
control axes—a decoder far below
saturation (
\mathrm{CR}
small,
SR
low) can still lose invertibility when the
environmental cause drifts faster than certification tracks it—so that
SR
, the
observable of the capacity axis, leaves the invertibility axis unresolved. The
two-axis instrument separating them (capacity read via
SR
, invertibility via

\zeta
) is developed in the open-loop companion
van Rooyen 2026c
.

Falsifiability (measurable test).
Condition (
7
) is
falsified empirically if the observed fraction of irreversible commitments made without completed
certification exceeds
\delta
on any sustained interval, or if
\zeta(t)
is observed to persistently
exceed
\zeta_{\star}
while commitments continue to be executed.

Local invertibility concerns the
geometry
of the measurement–action
mapping rather than the accuracy of any particular estimate.
When local invertibility holds, inference errors remain
epistemic
:
misinterpretations can be revised through additional evidence, delayed
commitment, or compensatory action. When invertibility fails, however, the mapping
from microstates to macrostates becomes effectively many-to-one. Structural
history is erased prior to validation, and subsequent information cannot restore
option space already eliminated by irreversible commitment.

Feasibility is not accuracy.

Accuracy concerns correctness of belief; feasibility concerns admissibility
of action — a decoder may produce accurate posteriors while operating
infeasibly, just as a controller may minimize cost while driving a system
unstable outside its stability margins
Callen 1985
;
de Groot and Mazur 1984
.

III.5
Statement of the Recoverability Constraint

Recoverability Constraint.

For any adaptive decoder that couples inference to irreversible downstream
commitments, stable operation requires simultaneous satisfaction of:
(i) a non-negative feasibility margin,
\mathcal{M}(t)\geq 0
, and
(ii) local invertibility of the measurement–action regime.

If either condition is violated, irreversible actions become inadmissible.
Actions taken in such regimes induce path-dependent loss of future option space
that cannot be reversed by subsequent information, capacity increases, or
posterior revision.

III.6
Irreversibility and Feasibility Boundaries

The joint behavior of the feasibility margin
\mathcal{M}(t)
and the stability
ratio
SR(t)
defines a boundary between recoverable and non-recoverable operating
regimes. We denote the primary feasibility boundary by

\Gamma=\{\,\mathcal{M}(t)=0\,\},

(8)

which marks the point at which induced informational flux exhausts effective
integrative capacity.

Crossing
\Gamma
is not a gradual degradation but a qualitative regime
transition. Actions taken beyond this boundary collapse distinctions that would
otherwise support correction or revision, inducing irreversible contraction of
future option space.

The regime geometry is that of a plane parameterized by
\mathcal{M}
and

SR
: the vertical boundary
\Gamma
separates feasible from overloaded
operation, and the horizontal threshold
SR_{c}
marks loss of action
admissibility through dominance of synthetic entropy. The recoverability
constraint is substrate-independent; what differs across substrates is not
the logic of the constraint, but the location of operating regimes within
the
(\mathcal{M},SR)
plane.

In physical systems, such regime shifts manifest as the emergence of new
dissipative structures. Section
IV
first closes the loop
that the present analysis leaves open — the feedback of uncertified
commitment onto the induced load — and Sec.
V
then
applies the framework to artificial decoders.

IV
Closed-Loop Decoders: First-Order Recoverability Collapse

The preceding analysis treats the induced flux
R_{\mathrm{self}}
as an
external drive. The framework’s own mechanism, however, implies feedback:
commitments taken on uncertified macrostates generate
further
load —
rework, error-conditioned downstream actions, unvalidated outputs re-entering
the input stream. Systems engineering documents the resulting failure class
at scale as
metastable failures
: self-sustaining congestive collapses
(retry storms, death spirals) that persist after the triggering load is
removed and resolve only under a large corrective action

Bronson et al. 2021
. That literature borrows the word
“metastable” from physics explicitly while providing no order parameter,
phase diagram, exponents, or thermodynamics; first-order congestion
transitions with hysteresis are known in packet-network traffic

Hu et al. 2007
, and analogous error cascades have recently been reported
in multi-agent language-model pipelines
Jamshidi et al. 2026
.
The mathematical literature on retrial queues

Artalejo and Gómez-Corral 2008
;
Phung-Duc 2017
develops stability conditions
for feedback through an orbit with constant or queue-length-dependent retrial
rates; the feedback kernel here differs — offspring are fired by

deadline violation
, so the feedback probability is itself
congestion-dependent — and it is this coupling that produces the fold. This section supplies the missing
statistical mechanics: making the feedback explicit converts the continuous
feasibility transition of Sec.
III
into a

first-order
transition with hysteresis, branching cascades, and a
thermodynamic discontinuity. Derivations and simulation methods are
collected in Appendix
D
.

IV.3
Beyond mean-field: enhancement, hysteresis, and the

Three simulation results delimit the mean-field theory. First, with zero-lag

IV.4
Thermodynamic signature and the price of gating

The first-order character is visible thermodynamically: sweeping
\ell_{0}

across the wedge, the synthetic fraction

\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}/(\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}+\dot{S}_{\mathrm{anc}})
jumps
from
\lesssim 0.1
to
\gtrsim 0.95
— the order-parameter discontinuity
rendered in entropy production — and each cascade carries a
Landauer-priced burst
\Sigma_{\mathrm{syn}}\geq u\,k_{B}\ln 2
(
u
uncertified
members) inheriting the
s^{-3/2}
tail: synthetic entropy is produced in
scale-free bursts near the self-referential corner. On the collapsed branch

\dot{S}_{\mathrm{syn}}\geq k_{B}\ln 2\,\lambda_{0}/(1-\alpha)

\alpha\geq 1
. Finally, a certification gate
q\in[0,1]
acts on the
feedback as
\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}=(1-q)\alpha
, so exiting the bistable
wedge requires
q>1-1/[\alpha(1+\theta)]
; the gate is itself an information
engine whose operation is bounded by the second law with feedback

Sagawa and Ueda 2008

thermodynamic price — and the certifier, being a finite-capacity decoder,
inherits its own feasibility constraint (cf. the No-Ideal-Decoder remark
of Sec.
V.5
).

Recursive-training degradation of generative models — model collapse
under self-consumption
Shumailov et al. 2024
;
Alemohammad et al. 2024
—
is the

sibling of this mechanism: generational
distribution drift, distinct from the inference-time queue saturation
analyzed here in order parameter, timescale, and remedy.

V
Illustrative Application: Variational Consequences in Artificial Decoders

Scope of application.

The analysis in this section is illustrative and interpretive; it introduces no
new formal results and does not extend the feasibility or recoverability claims
beyond those established in Section
III
.

Artificial intelligence provides a concrete, contemporary example of a
decoder operating deep within the high-flux regime analyzed above. Throughout
this section,
intelligence
denotes representational and inferential
capacity (mapping observations to macrostates, compressing, and manipulating
them), while
agency
denotes the admissible coupling of those
macrostates to irreversible commitments under the feasibility and
invertibility constraints.

Observation 1

(Representational optimality does not imply recoverability)
.

Let
X
denote environment microstate,
Y
denote observations, and
Z
denote an internal macrostate produced by a decoder (e.g.,
Z=f_{\theta}(Y)
). Suppose
Z
is optimized for predictive or inferential performance under a loss
L(\theta)
(e.g., negative log-likelihood, cross-entropy) so that
\theta^{\star}=\arg\min_{\theta}\mathbb{E}[L(\theta)]
. Then, in general, optimality of
Z
for
L
does
not
guarantee local invertibility of the measurement–action regime nor recoverability under irreversible commitment.

Sketch.
Loss-optimal representations are generically
many-to-one
compressions of
Y
(and hence of
X
): distinct microstates (or observation histories) that are equivalent for prediction under
L
are mapped to the same
Z
. This is precisely the condition under which local invertibility fails: the mapping needed to reconstruct or certify distinctions relevant for later correction is destroyed by compression. Therefore, even when inference accuracy improves (lower
L
), irreversible actions conditioned on

V.4
Design Implications: Gating, Certification, and the Role of LLMs

The structural instability of ungated LLMs has direct architectural

V.5
Admissible Action and Irreversible Loss

The analysis developed in this work identifies agency not with intelligence
or capacity alone, but with the ability to manage irreversible loss under
constraint: a system may be arbitrarily intelligent in inference while
remaining unstable as an agent if irreversible commitments occur outside
regimes of feasibility and local invertibility. Unlike classical
control-theoretic stability or reachability, which assume a fixed state
space, the RSC criterion tracks irreversible contraction of the option space
itself under action — a failure mode not captured by controllability or
Lyapunov stability alone.

Synthetic entropy as wasted counterfactual structure.

Within this interpretation,

This framing clarifies why increasing throughput alone does not stabilize agency. Higher

VI
Conclusion: Recoverable Dissipation as the Stability Criterion

This work has framed high-flux information processing — of which the
contemporary digital setting is one interpretation — as a particular
instance of a more general physical regime: sustained nonequilibrium driving
by an imposed informational (ambiguity) gradient. The central result is a
substrate-independent constraint on all high-flux decoders: for any adaptive
system — biological, artificial, or otherwise — that couples inference to
irreversible action, recoverable operation requires both (i) a non-negative
feasibility margin between induced informational flux and integrative
capacity, and (ii) local invertibility of the measurement–action regime.
When either is violated, irreversible actions induce path-dependent
contraction of future option space: inference may continue, but irreversible
action becomes inadmissible, with loss of invertibility captured
operationally by sustained elevation of the stability diagnostic
SR(t)
.

Within this framework, contemporary artificial intelligence systems appear not
as exceptional agents, but as one realization of a broader class of dissipative
structures that become admissible when existing decoders can no longer sustain
recoverable operation under high informational flux. Increased capacity is
stabilizing only conditionally: it can restore feasibility margins, but it does not
relax the requirement for local invertibility. Ungated throughput therefore
accelerates synthetic entropy production and hastens entry into non-recoverable
regimes, even as inference accuracy improves.

When the feedback of uncertified output onto load is made explicit, this
acceleration acquires a sharp phase structure: recoverability collapse is
first order, with coexisting lucid and collapsed states, hysteresis whose
recovery branch is set by grounding alone (
\ell_{0}=1-\alpha
), an
irreversibility threshold at
\alpha=1
beyond which only gating or reset
— not load reduction — restores operation, and Landauer-priced cascades
whose scale is bounded by the grounded fraction of input. The continuous
divergence of the stability ratio, the centerpiece of the open-loop
analysis
van Rooyen 2026c
;
van
Rooyen 2026d
, survives as the

\alpha\to 0
boundary of this phase diagram —
and is dynamically pre-empted by discontinuous collapse whenever feedback
exceeds the cusp value. These results give quantitative, falsifiable
content — exponents, spinodals, early-warning scalings — to a failure
class that operational practice has long recognized

Bronson et al. 2021
.

Practical consequence.

The stability ratio
SR(t)
— with its fluctuation
precursors (rising lag-one autocorrelation and variance near the fold) — is a
runtime gauge of how close a high-throughput decision system is to collapse;
consistent with Proposition
3

(1-q)\alpha<1/(1+\theta)

than built into the fast generator. Where feedback is already supercritical

\alpha\geq 1

cut the feedback, not merely throttle the load.

The broader implication for physics is that informational dissipation admits a
regime constraint analogous to those familiar from transport and stability in
driven matter: just as energy flux through material media is limited by
transport coefficients and instability boundaries, informational flux through
finite decoders is limited by recoverability. And as reliable communication is
bounded by channel capacity, recoverable
action
is bounded by this
margin — the feasibility boundary playing for irreversible commitment the
role the Shannon limit plays for reliable decoding
(Prop.
1
), with the added ingredient that commitment
cannot be deferred until certification completes. The appropriate criterion for
evaluating high-throughput adaptive systems is therefore not maximal
capacity, performance, or entropy production, but whether irreversible
dissipation remains recoverable: stability in high-flux informational regimes
is defined by preservation of option space, not by throughput alone.

As the first in a series, this work is scoped to the single decoder
(Remark
Remark
). The principal extension is a
joint

decoder that estimates the several latent states — estimable, diversity, and
correlated streams alike — and resolves commitment by joint,

high-capacity joint representation is forced through a hard-decision
bottleneck. Whether this correspondence sharpens into a genuine capacity
theorem for recoverable action — a mutual-information characterization of
certification capacity, with achievability and converse — is the question the
joint formulation poses, and one we do not settle here.

x_{f}
solving

\alpha(1+\theta x_{f})e^{-(1-x_{f})\theta}=1
, giving

\ell_{0}^{c}=F(x_{f})=\theta x_{f}^{2}/(1+\theta x_{f})

[Eq. (
10
)]; between
\ell_{0}=1-\alpha
and
\ell_{0}^{c}
the
stable lucid state coexists with an unstable threshold and the collapsed
state. The spinodals merge (
x_{f}\to 1
) at the cusp
[Eq. (
11
)]. On the collapsed branch the backlog grows
without bound, every commitment times out (
P_{\mathrm{u}}=1
), and the
fluid balance gives
\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=\lambda_{0}/(1-\alpha)
,
self-sustaining iff
\ell_{0}\geq 1-\alpha
; for
\alpha\geq 1
no finite balance
exists (Prop.
4
). The use of the stationary sojourn tail
inside the slow load dynamics is an adiabatic approximation, controlled at
any fold with
x_{f}<1
since the queue’s relaxation time diverges only as

x\to 1
.

D.2
Branching identities

Each commitment is uncertified with probability
P_{\mathrm{u}}
and then
spawns mean
\alpha
offspring, so the genealogical ratio is

b=\alpha P_{\mathrm{u}}(x^{\ast})
. From Eq. (
9
),

\alpha x^{\ast}P_{\mathrm{u}}=x^{\ast}-\ell_{0}
, hence
b=1-\ell_{0}/x^{\ast}
and

\langle s\rangle=1/(1-b)=x^{\ast}/\ell_{0}
(cascade size = load
amplification; a parameter-free internal check of the simulation, satisfied
to
\sim
1%). Subcritical Galton–Watson (Otter) statistics give

P(s)\sim s^{-3/2}e^{-s/s_{c}}
,
s_{c}\simeq 2/(1-b)^{2}
. At the fold,

F^{\prime}=0
reads
b_{\mathrm{fold}}(1+\theta x_{f})=1
: the instability is the
product of genealogical branching and the congestion coupling

\partial P_{\mathrm{u}}/\partial\lambda
, so
b_{\mathrm{fold}}<1
strictly.

D.3
Simulation methods

Event-driven, seeded simulation of the closed loop: exogenous Poisson
arrivals at rate
\lambda_{0}
(aggregate
N\lambda_{0}
for the fleet);
N

FCFS servers (default
N=1
) with
\mathrm{Exp}(\mu)
service; a job whose
sojourn exceeds
\Delta t
fires uncertified
at its deadline
and
spawns
\mathrm{Poisson}(\alpha)
offspring (routed uniformly across
servers in the fleet), with an optional exponential spawn latency

\tau_{\mathrm{lag}}
; jobs remain enqueued until served either way, so
certification capacity is consumed even by already-fired commitments,
matching Eq. (
9
). Genealogy tags track cascades to closure.
Validation: at
\alpha=0
the exact M/M/1 sojourn statistics are reproduced
(
P_{\mathrm{u}}=0.606
vs
e^{-1/2}=0.607
at
\ell_{0}=0.5
,
\theta=1
); the
identity
\langle s\rangle=x/\ell_{0}
holds to
\sim
1% at every measured
point;
\tau_{\mathrm{lag}}\gg
the queue correlation time recovers the
mean-field fixed point (
x=0.2825
vs
x^{\ast}=0.2828
at

\alpha=0.6,\ \ell_{0}=0.2
). The mean-field solvers and the event-driven
engine, with the seeded commands that regenerate the figures of this paper,
are openly available
van
Rooyen 2026e
.

The per-event entropies use a minimal exact wiring in which certification

is
a Glauber two-level system’s first passage to its field-aligned
state: a two-state chain with an absorbing target has exactly exponential
absorption time, so
\mu=k_{+}=\tfrac{1}{2\tau_{0}}[1+\tanh(\beta h)]
and the
M/M/1 service law is
derived
from the spin dynamics. A certified
event dissipates the finite, load-independent relaxation entropy of the
aligning flip (
\sigma_{\mathrm{anc}}=2\beta h\,k_{B}
in this wiring); an
uncertified event erases its unresolved bit at cost

\sigma_{\mathrm{syn}}\geq k_{B}\ln 2
. Hence

SR_{\mathrm{EP}}=(\langle\sigma_{\mathrm{syn}}\rangle/\langle\sigma_{\mathrm{anc}}\rangle)\,SR
with a prefactor bounded above and
below by load-independent constants: the entropy-production ratio inherits
the order parameter’s divergence exponent, and equals it exactly when the
two per-event budgets coincide.
