t-tone colorings of outerplanar and Halin graphs
Hadeel Al Bazzal, Olivier Togni

Abstract
A t t -tone k k -coloring of a graph G G assigns a set of t t distinct colors from { 1 , … , k } \{1,\dots,k\} to each vertex so that vertices at distance d d share fewer than d d common colors. The t t -tone chromatic number of G G is the minimum k k such that G G has a t t -tone k k -coloring. This paper investigates the t t -tone coloring of two specific subclasses of planar graphs: subcubic outerplanar graphs and Halin graphs. We provide a complete characterization of the 2 2 -tone chromatic number for subcubic outerplanar graphs and establish a sharp upper bound for their 3 3 -tone chromatic number. We then turn to Halin graphs and prove that every cubic Halin graph of order n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 is 2 2 -tone 7 7 -colorable. Moreover, we derive an upper bound on the 2 2 -tone chromatic number for Halin graphs with arbitrary maximum degree.

t
-tone coloring,
t
-tone chromatic number, subcubic graph, outerplanar graph, Halin graph.

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are assumed to be simple and finite. For a graph

, we let

denote its vertex set. The minimum degree and maximum degree of

A graph

The primary focus of this paper is the

For a given

Theorem 1.1

Let

\tau_{2}(G)=\begin{cases}5,&\text{if }G\text{ contains no }C_{3},C_{4},C_{7},\\

Next, we establish a sharp upper bound for the

Cranston and LaFayette

\tau_{2}(G)\leq\left\lceil\sqrt{2\Delta(G)+4.25}+5.5\right\rceil.

Later, Bickle

\tau_{2}(G)\leq\max\left\{7,\;\left\lceil\frac{5+\sqrt{1+8(\Delta(G)+3)}}{2}\right\rceil\right\}

Given an outerplane graph

contains either exactly one vertex of degree three or exactly two consecutive vertices of degree three.

Lemma 2.1

(

For the cycle

\tau_{2}(C_{n})=\begin{cases}6,&\text{if }n\in\{3,4,7\},\\

A subcubic outerplanar graph

contains
2
. Since
x_{1}
has at most one internal second neighbor,
y_{2}
, whose label may contain
1
or
2
, one label from
\{13,14\}
can be assigned to
x_{1}
. Without loss of generality, assign
13
to
x_{1}
; then label
x_{\ell}
by
24
. Thus,
f(y_{2})=25
, relabel
x_{1}
by
45
, and label
x_{\ell}
by
23
.
\Box

This result confirms part (2) of Conjecture
1.1
for cubic Halin graphs of order
n\geq 6
. However, no cubic Halin graph requiring seven colors is currently known, suggesting that the above bound may not be tight. This observation motivates the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Every cubic Halin graph of order
n\geq 6
is
2
-tone
6
-colorable.

Since cubic Halin graphs are
(K_{4}-e)
-free, the above conjecture, if confirmed, would confirm part (3) of Conjecture
1.1
for this class of graphs.

3.2
Halin Graphs

For planar graphs, Cranston and LaFayette
[
10
]
proved that for any planar graph
G
, the
2
-tone chromatic number satisfies

\tau_{2}(G)\leq\left\lfloor\sqrt{4\Delta(G)+50.25}+31.1\right\rfloor\leq\left\lfloor\sqrt{4\Delta(G)}+36.5\right\rfloor.

Moreover, for sufficiently large
\Delta(G)
, they established the stronger bound

\tau_{2}(G)\leq\max\left\{41,\left\lfloor\sqrt{4\Delta(G)+50.25}+11.5\right\rfloor\right\}.

They further conjectured
[
10
]
that there exists a constant
C
such that
\tau_{2}(G)\leq\sqrt{3\Delta(G)}+C
for every planar graph
G
.

We start with a lemma for the wheel graph
W_{d}
, which is a basic special case of Halin graphs.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
3
]
)
For the wheel graph
W_{d}=C_{d}+K_{1}
, the 2-tone chromatic number is:

\tau_{2}(W_{d})=\begin{cases}7,&\text{if }d\in\{5,6,8,9\},\\[5.69054pt]
8,&\text{if }d\in\{3,4,7,10,\dots,15\},\\[5.69054pt]
\left\lceil\dfrac{5+\sqrt{1+8d}}{2}\right\rceil,&\text{if }d\geq 11.\end{cases}

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
H=T\cup C
be a Halin graph. Then

\tau_{2}(H)\leq\max\left\{10,\;\left\lceil\frac{13+\sqrt{8\Delta(H)-15}}{2}\right\rceil\right\}.

Proof.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that the theorem is false.
Let
H=T\cup C
be a counterexample with the minimum number of vertices,
where
C=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{\ell}x_{1}
is a cycle. Set
k=\max\left\{10,\;\left\lceil\frac{13+\sqrt{8\Delta(H)-15}}{2}\right\rceil\right\}
.

By Lemma
3.2
,
H
is not a wheel graph; hence, the tree
T
contains at least two internal vertices. Root
T
at an arbitrary internal vertex, and let
x
be an internal vertex at maximum distance from the root in
T
. By this choice,
x
has exactly one internal neighbor (its parent, say
p
) and all its remaining neighbors are leaves of
T
that appear consecutively on
C
. Denote, without loss of generality, these leaves by
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}
. Since
T
has no vertices of degree two, we have
m\geq 2
.

Claim 3.2.1
.

m\geq 3
.

Proof.

Suppose, to the contrary, that
m=2
. Define a smaller graph
H^{\prime}=(H-\{x_{1},x_{2}\})+\{x_{\ell}x,\,xx_{3}\}
.
H^{\prime}
is again a Halin graph: the vertex
x
becomes a leaf on the new cycle
C^{\prime}
, and its degree in the new tree
T^{\prime}=T-\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
remains three. By the minimality of
H
,
H^{\prime}
admits a valid
2
-tone
k
-coloring
f
.

We now extend
f
to
H
. First, consider
x_{1}
. Its neighbors in
H
are
\{x,x_{\ell},x_{2}\}
; at this stage,
x_{2}
is uncolored. Thus, the number of available labels for
x_{1}
satisfying the adjacency condition is at least
\binom{k-4}{2}
. Moreover,
x_{1}
has at most four vertices at distance two, and since
k\geq 10
, we have
\binom{k-4}{2}>4
. Hence, at least one label exists for
x_{1}
.

Next, consider
x_{2}
. Its neighbors in
H
are
\{x,x_{1},x_{3}\}
, all of which are now colored and together use at least six colors. Thus, the number of labels available for
x_{2}
satisfying the adjacency condition is at least
\binom{k-6}{2}
. The vertex
x_{2}
has at most four second neighbors, and since
k\geq 10
, we have
\binom{k-6}{2}>4
. Therefore, a label can be assigned to
x_{2}
.

This yields a valid
2
-tone
k
-coloring of
H
, contradicting the assumption that
H
is a minimal counterexample.
\Box

Thus, the neighbors of
x
in
T
are
\{p,x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}\}
with
m\geq 3
. In this case,
d_{H}(x)=m+1\geq 4
. Construct a smaller graph
H^{\prime}=(H-\{x_{2}\})+\{x_{1}x_{3}\}
. Since
m\geq 3
,
x
remains adjacent to at least two leaves (
x_{1}
and
x_{3}
) and to its parent
p
in
H^{\prime}
, and hence
d_{H^{\prime}}(x)\geq 3
. Thus,
H^{\prime}
is a Halin graph with
|V(H^{\prime})|<|V(H)|
. By the minimality of
H
,
H^{\prime}
admits a valid
2
-tone
k^{\prime}
-coloring,
where

k^{\prime}=\max\left\{10,\;\left\lceil\frac{13+\sqrt{8\Delta(H^{\prime})-15}}{2}\right\rceil\right\}.

Since
\Delta(H^{\prime})\leq\Delta(H)
, we have
k^{\prime}\leq k
. Hence,
H^{\prime}
admits a
2
-tone
k
-coloring
f
.

We now extend
f
to
x_{2}
in
H
. The vertices at distance two from
x_{2}
are:

•

from
x
: the parent
p
and the leaves
\{x_{4},\dots,x_{m}\}
;

•

from
x_{1}
: the neighbor of
x_{1}
on
C
other than
x_{2}
, namely
x_{\ell}
;

•

from
x_{3}
: the neighbor of
x_{3}
on
C
other than
x_{2}
, namely
x_{4}
.

Thus,
|N_{H}^{2}(x_{2})|=|\{p,x_{4},x_{5},\dots,x_{m}\}\cup\{x_{\ell}\}|\leq(d_{H}(x)-3)+1\leq\Delta(H)-2
. Since
x_{2}
has three colored neighbors, at most
6
colors are forbidden by adjacency, leaving at least
\binom{k-6}{2}
possible labels for
x_{2}
. By the definition of
k
and since
\Delta(H)\geq 3
, we have
\binom{k-6}{2}>\Delta(H)-2
, ensuring the existence of a label for
x_{2}
. This again yields a
2
-tone
k
-coloring of
H
, a contradiction.
\Box

While Theorem
3.2
provides a general upper bound on
\tau_{2}(H)
in terms of
\Delta(H)
, Theorem
3.1
yields a strictly stronger result in the cubic case.

Asymptotically, the upper bound for general planar graphs grows as
\sqrt{4\Delta}
. In contrast, our bound for Halin graphs scales as
\frac{\sqrt{8\Delta}}{2}=\sqrt{2\Delta}
. Moreover, our bound is strictly stronger than the conjectured bound
\sqrt{3\Delta}
proposed by Cranston and LaFayette.
