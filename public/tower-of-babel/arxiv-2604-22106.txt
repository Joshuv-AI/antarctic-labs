Stable 2–systole bounds in positive scalar curvature
Douglas Stryker

Abstract
. We prove that the stable 2–systole is uniformly bounded on the space of Riemannian metrics with scalar curvature at least one for closed spin 2–essential manifolds, which includes S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} , S 2 × T n S^{2}\times T^{n} , and ℂ ​ ℙ 2 ​ n + 1 \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2n+1} .

We recall the relevant definitions. Let

1.1.1.
Products of 2–spheres

1.2.
Strategy and discussion

In
[
13
]
, Gromov introduced the notion of cowaist (called
\mathrm{K}
–area in
[
13
]
) and showed that the twisted spinor techniques of
[
11
]
give upper bounds for the cowaist when the scalar curvature is positive. Roughly speaking, the cowaist is the inverse of the smallest possible curvature over all sufficiently topologically nontrivial Hermitian vector bundles. In
[
13
]
and, as far as the author is aware, almost all subsequent related works (see the non–exhaustive list
[
6
,
23
,
3
,
38
,
5
,
34
,
36
]
), the cowaist bound is used in situations where the cowaist of the manifold (or the cowaist of a suitable submanifold) is shown or assumed to be infinite. Typically, arguments showing that the cowaist is infinite follow coarse estimates for the curvature of Hermitian vector bundles, usually given by pulling back a model Hermitian bundle on a model manifold by a map with controlled Lipschitz constant. In these cases, precise quantitative control of the curvature of any given bundle is not required.

A notable exception is the theorem of Llarull
[
25
]
, where the precise value of the curvature of the spinor bundle on the round sphere is used to obtain a sharp result. This approach was generalized by
[
9
,
37
]
to manifolds with nonnegative curvature operator and nonzero Euler characteristic. However, there seems to be no general setting where a well–chosen finite dimensional Hermitian bundle with precisely controlled curvature on an arbitrary metric with positive scalar curvature has been used to deduce geometric conclusions.

In this paper, we take on this task by studying the curvature of Hermitian
line
bundles, which can be computed precisely using Chern–Weil theory. In particular, we can control the curvature of a well–chosen line bundle by a certain invariant of the integer lattice in the Banach space
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
with the
comass
norm. Using the theory of lattices in Banach spaces, we can then control the stable 2–systole, which is the shortest length vector in the dual lattice in
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
with the
stable
norm.

We emphasize that our analysis is relatively elementary and quite classical, and yet it still provides control on the stable 2–systole by the cowaist. An important question left open by this paper is how much better one can do by looking at higher rank Hermitian vector bundles. This question seems daunting, however, as we are not aware of any general higher rank situation where the full curvature can be prescribed or controlled.

The main disadvantages of the spin techniques developed here, as compared to the geometric measure theory methods of
[
4
,
40
,
31
]
, are

(1)

the manifold must be spin,

(2)

they apply to the weaker
stable
2–systole invariant, and

(3)

they do not give the expected sharp bound (except in dimension 2).

However, these techniques have a few important advantages, including

(1)

they apply in all dimensions, and

(2)

they apply to a large general class of manifolds.

Norms on homology and cohomology

We recall the relevant norms on homology and cohomology, as established by

2.1.
Norms on 2–forms

For any 2–form
\omega
on
M
, we define

\|\omega\|_{\infty}\coloneq\sup\{|\omega_{p}(X,Y)|:p\in M,\ X,Y\in T_{p}M,\ |X\wedge Y|=1\}.

For
\Xi\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
, we define the
comass norm
of
\Xi
to be

\|\Xi\|^{*}\coloneq\inf\{\|\omega\|_{\infty}:\omega\in\Xi\}.

By
[
8
]
,
(H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M),\|\cdot\|^{*})
is a Banach space.

For any Hermitian vector space
V
and any endomorphism
A:V\to V
, we define the operator norm

|A|_{\mathrm{op}}\coloneq\sup\{|A(v)|:v\in V,\ |v|=1\}.

For any Hermitian vector bundle
E\to M
and any
\mathrm{End}(E)
–valued 2–form
\mathcal{R}
, we define

\|\mathcal{R}\|_{\infty}\coloneq\sup\{|\omega_{p}(X,Y)|_{\mathrm{op}}:p\in M,\ X,Y\in T_{p}M,\ |X\wedge Y|=1\}.

2.2.
Norms on 2–cycles

For any integer homology class
\sigma\in H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, the
mass norm
of
\sigma
is

\mathbb{M}(\sigma)\coloneq\inf\{\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma):\Sigma\ \text{integer\ cycle\ in\ }\sigma\}.

In other words,
\mathbb{M}(\sigma)
is the area of any homological area minimizer in the class
\sigma
.

For any real homology class
\sigma\in H_{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
, the
norm
of
\sigma
is

\|\sigma\|\coloneq\inf\{\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma):\Sigma\ \text{real\ cycle\ in\ }\sigma\}.

By
[
8
]
,
(H_{2}(M,\mathbb{R}),\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach space.

For
\sigma\in H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
the
stable norm
of
\sigma
is

\|\sigma\|\coloneq\|\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}\|,

where
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}
is the image of
\sigma
in
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Let
\sigma\in H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. By
[
8
]
, we have

\|\sigma\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{M}(k\sigma).

In other words, there is a sequence of integers
\{k_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and area minimizing integer cycles
\{\Sigma_{j}\in k_{j}\sigma\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
so that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{j})}{k_{j}}=\|\sigma\|.

It is for this reason that the norm is referred to as the stable norm, since it is the mass norm up to stabilizing by taking integer multiples.

2.3.
Lattices and duality

Let
\Lambda_{2}(M)
denote the image of
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
in
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Note that
\Lambda_{2}(M)
is a lattice in
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Let
\Lambda^{2}(M)
denote the image of
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
. Note that
\Lambda^{2}(M)
is a lattice in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)

By
[

(H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M),\|\cdot\|^{*})

are dual Banach spaces, and
\Lambda^{2}(M)
and
\Lambda_{2}(M)
are dual lattices.

For any lattice
\Lambda
in a Banach space
(V,|\cdot|)
, let

\lambda_{k}(\Lambda)\coloneq\inf\left\{\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}}|v_{j}|:v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\ \text{are\ linearly\ independent\ in\ }\Lambda\right\}.

As in
[
10

, we define
\Gamma_{b}
to be the supremum of
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)\lambda_{b}(\Lambda^{*})
over all lattices in any
b

\Lambda^{*}
is the dual lattice to
\Lambda
. It is clear that
\Gamma_{1}=1
. By an argument of David Speyer (see
[
10

),
\Gamma_{2}=\frac{3}{2}
. By
[
1
]
(see
[
10

), we have
\Gamma_{b}=O(b\log b)
.

2.4.
Stable 2–systole

The
2–systole
is

\mathrm{sys}_{2}(g)\coloneq\inf\{\mathrm{Area}(S):S\ \text{is\ a\ non-contractible\ immersed\ 2--sphere\ in\ }M\}.

The
homological 2–systole
is

\mathrm{hom}\text{-}\mathrm{sys}_{2}(g)\coloneq\inf\{\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma):\Sigma\ \text{is\ a\ homologically\ non-zero\ integer\ 2--cycle\ in\ }M\}.

In other words, the homological 2–systole is the area of the least area nontrivial area minimizing surface in
(M,g)
.

The
stable 2–systole
is

\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)\coloneq\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{2}(M)).

In other words, the stable 2–systole is the stable norm of the smallest nonzero element in the lattice
\Lambda_{2}(M)
. It represents the area of an area minimizer up to stabilization by taking integer multiples.

For
(S^{2})^{m}\times T^{n}
, we make a new specialized definition. For
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, let
\sigma_{j}
be the homology class of points times the 2–sphere in the
j
–th factor:

\{\mathrm{pt}\}\times\cdots\times S^{2}\times\cdots\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}\subset(S^{2})^{m}\times T^{n}.

We define the
stable spherical 2–systole
to be

\mathrm{stsys}_{2}^{\mathrm{sph}}(g)\coloneq\inf\{\|\sigma\|:\sigma\in\mathrm{Span}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})\cap\Lambda_{2}(M)\setminus\{0\}\}.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold. Let
E\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle with metric connection, and let
R^{E}
be the curvature 2–form. We say
E
is
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
–admissible
if

\int_{M}\hat{\mathbf{A}}(M)\cdot\mathrm{ch}(E)\neq 0.

The
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
–cowaist
of
M
is

\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(g)\coloneq\left(\inf\left\{\|R^{E}\|_{\infty}:E\ \text{is}\ \hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{--admissible}\right\}\right)^{-1}

The
line bundle
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
–cowaist
of
M
is

\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(g)\coloneq\left(\inf\left\{\|R^{L}\|_{\infty}:L\ \text{is\ an}\ \hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{--admissible\ line\ bundle}\right\}\right)^{-1}

Note that by definition we have
\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(g)\leq\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(g)
.

The following inequality is essentially proved in the course of the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 5
\frac{1}{4}
]
(see also
[
38
, §1]
).

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian spin manifold of dimension
2n
. Then

\frac{4n(2n-1)}{\min_{M}\mathrm{scal}(g)}\geq\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(g).

Proof.

We need only compute the constant depending on dimension in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 5
\frac{1}{4}
]
.

By inspecting the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 5
\frac{1}{4}
]
, it suffices to show that

(3.1)

|\mathcal{R}^{L}(\psi)|\leq n(2n-1)\|R^{L}\|_{\infty}|\psi|,

for any Hermitian line bundle with metric connection
L\to M
, where
\mathcal{R}^{L}
is defined in
[
21
, Chapter II, (8.22)]
, and
R^{L}
is the curvature 2–form of the connection on
L
.

Fix
x\in M
. Let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
be an orthonormal basis for the complex spinor bundle
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\to M
at
x
, let
\xi
be a nonzero unit length element in the fiber of
L
at
x
, and let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n}
be an orthonormal basis for
T_{x}M
. Then

\psi(x)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}s_{i}\right)\otimes\xi,

which is a simple element of
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes L
with

|\psi(x)|^{2}=\sum_{i}a_{i}^{2}.

Then we compute

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}^{L}(\psi)|(x)

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p,q}e_{p}\cdot e_{q}\cdot\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}s_{i}\right)\otimes R^{L}_{e_{p},e_{q}}\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p<q}\left|\sum_{i}a_{i}s_{i}\right|\|R^{L}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=n(2n-1)\sqrt{\sum_{i}a_{i}^{2}}\|R^{L}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=n(2n-1)\|R^{L}\|_{\infty}|\psi|(x),

as desired.
∎

4.
Curvature of complex line bundles

The following result, which plays a central role in our analysis, is a well–known consequence of Chern–Weil theory. We refer the reader to

, §1.8]
for a proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold of dimension at least 2. Let
\omega
be a closed 2–form on

so that

is an integer cohomology class. Then there is a Hermitian line bundle

with a metric connection satisfying

R^{L_{\omega}}=-2\pi i\omega\ \ \text{and}\ \ c_{1}(L_{\omega})=[\omega].

5.
Digression: Cowaist of the 2–sphere

We make a brief digression to provide sharp analysis in the case of the 2–sphere.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(S^{2},g)
be a Riemannian 2–sphere. Then

\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(g)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g).

Proof.

Let
\Xi
be the positive generator of
H^{2}(S^{2},\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\omega
be the area 2–form of the metric
g
. Note that
\|\omega\|_{\infty}=1
. We have
[\omega]=A\Xi
for some
A>0
. Note that

\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g)=\int_{S^{2}}\omega=A\int_{S^{2}}\Xi=A.

Let
L
be the Hermitian line bundle with metric connection associated to
\frac{\omega}{\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g)}\in\Xi
from Lemma
4.1
. Then we have

c_{1}(L)=\Xi,

which implies that
L
is
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
–admissible, since
\hat{\mathbf{A}}(S^{2})=1
. Moreover, we have

(5.1)

\|R^{L}\|_{\infty}=\frac{2\pi\|\omega\|_{\infty}}{\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g)}=\frac{2\pi}{\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g)},

so the conclusion follows.
∎

By the above arguments, we can give a spinor proof of the following classical consequence of the Gauss–Bonnet formula.

Theorem 5.2
.

If
(S^{2},g)
has
K_{g}\geq 1
, then
\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g)\leq 4\pi
, with equality if and only if
(S^{2},g)
is isometric to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
L
be the line bundle as in the proof of Proposition
5.1
. By the Atiyah–Singer index theorem (see
[
21
, Chapter III, Theorem 13.10]
), since
L
is
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
–admissible, the Dirac operator
\mathbf{D}_{L}
on the twisted spinor bundle
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes L
constructed in
[
21
, Chapter II, Proposition 5.10]
admits a nonzero section
\psi
of
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes L
in the kernel of
\mathbf{D}_{L}
. By the twisted Lichnerowicz formula (see
[
21
, Chapter II, Theorem 8.17]
), (
3.1
), and (
5.1
), we have

0=\int_{S^{2}}|\mathbf{D}_{L}\psi|^{2}\geq\int_{S^{2}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}K_{g}|\psi|^{2}-\frac{2\pi}{\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g)}|\psi|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4\pi}{\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g)}\right)|\psi|^{2}.

Since
\psi
is not identically zero, the inequality follows. Moreover, if
\mathrm{Area}(S^{2},g)=4\pi
, then
|\nabla\psi|\equiv 0
, which implies that
\nabla|\psi|\equiv 0
, meaning that
\psi
has constant nonzero length. Then we must have
K_{g}\equiv 1
. The conclusion now follows from the classification of constant curvature metrics on simply connected spaces (see for instance
[
29
, Corollary 5.6.14]
).
∎

6.
Cowaist lower bounds

By making judicious choices of Hermitian bundles with prescribed curvature using Lemma
4.1
, we prove lower bounds for the cowaist in terms of the stable 2–systole for a large class of manifolds.

6.1.
K–cowaist of 2–essential manifolds

As in
[
10
]
, we say a closed manifold of dimension
2m
is
2–essential
if a nonzero element in
H^{2m}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
can be written as the product of classes in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
. We prove a lower bound for the K–cowaist of 2–essential manifolds.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M
be a
2m
–dimensional closed 2–essential manifold with second Betti number
b_{2}(M)=b
. Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
. Then

\mathrm{K}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(g)\geq\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)}{2\pi\Gamma_{b}}.

Proof.

Let
\Xi_{1},\ldots,\Xi_{b}
be a linearly independent spanning set for
\Lambda^{2}(M)
with

\|\Xi_{j}\|^{*}\leq\lambda_{b}(\Lambda^{2}(M)).

As in
[
10
, Proof of Theorem 3.3]
(without loss of generality by reordering), there are positive integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
with

\sum_{j=1}^{l}k_{j}=m

so that

\Xi_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\Xi_{l}^{k_{l}}\neq 0\in H^{2m}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M).

Fix
\varepsilon>0
, and let
\omega_{j}\in\Xi_{j}
so that

\|\omega_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\Xi_{j}\|^{*}+\varepsilon/(2\pi).

Let
L_{j}\to M
be the Hermitian line bundle with metric connection associated to
\omega_{j}
from Lemma
4.1
. Then the bundle

E=L_{1}^{\oplus k_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{l}^{\oplus k_{l}}

satisfies

c_{m}(E)=\Xi_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\Xi_{l}^{k_{l}}\neq 0,

so
E
is
\mathrm{K}
–admissible. Moreover, we have by
[
13
, §4, Page 18]

\displaystyle\|R^{E}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}}\|R^{L_{j}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}}2\pi\|\omega_{j}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}}2\pi\|\Xi_{j}\|^{*}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\lambda_{b}(\Lambda^{2}(M))+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, by
[
10
, §2]
, we have

\mathrm{K}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(g)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda_{b}(\Lambda^{2}(M))}\geq\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)}{2\pi\Gamma_{b}},

as desired.
∎

The above technique also applies if we take a product with an enlargeable manifold. As in
[
11
]
, we say that a closed manifold
N
of dimension
2k
is
enlargeable
if for any Riemannian metric
g
on
N
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a finite covering space that admits a nonzero degree map to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2k}
with Lipschitz constant at most
\varepsilon
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
M
be a
2m
–dimensional closed 2–essential manifold. Let
N
be a
2k
–dimensional closed enlargeable manifold. Let
b=b_{2}(M\times N)
be the second Betti number of
M\times N
, and let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M\times N
. Then

\mathrm{K}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(g)\geq\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)}{2\pi\Gamma_{b}}.

Proof.

Let
\zeta_{N}
be the pullback of a generator
\bar{\zeta}_{N}\in H^{2k}(N,\mathbb{Z})
by the projection map

\pi_{N}:M\times N\to N.

Fix a Riemannian metric
g_{N}
on
N
. Let

C_{N}\coloneq\mathrm{Lip}(\pi_{N}:(M\times N,g)\to(N,g_{N}))<\infty.

Since
N
is enlargeable, by
[
11
]
, there is a Hermitian vector bundle with metric connection
E_{N}^{0}\to N
with
c_{k}(E_{N}^{0})=a_{1}\bar{\zeta}_{N}
for some
a_{1}\neq 0
, and

\|R^{E_{N}^{0}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{C_{N}^{2}}.

If we let
E_{N}\to M\times N
be the pullback of
E_{N}^{0}
by
\pi_{N}
, then
c_{k}(E_{N})=a_{1}\zeta_{N}
for
a_{1}\neq 0
,
c_{l}(E_{n})=0
for
l>k
, and

\|R^{E_{N}}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon.

Let
\zeta_{M}
be the pullback of a generator
\bar{\zeta}_{M}\in H^{2m}(M,\mathbb{Z})
by the projection map

\pi_{M}:M\times N\to M.

Let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m}\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
so that
\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{m}=a_{2}\bar{\zeta}_{M}
for some
a_{2}\neq 0
, which exists because
M
is 2–essential.
Let
\Xi_{1},\ldots,\Xi_{b}
be a linearly independent spanning set for
\Lambda^{2}(M\times N)
with

\|\Xi_{j}\|^{*}\leq\lambda_{b}(\Lambda^{2}(M\times N)).

Writing each
\pi_{M}^{*}(\eta_{i})
in the basis
\{\Xi_{j}\}
and taking the product, we find that there are positive integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{l}
summing to
m
so that

\Xi_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\Xi_{l}^{k_{l}}=a_{3}\zeta_{M}+\beta

for some
a_{3}\neq 0
and some
\beta\in H^{2m}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M\times N)
so that
\zeta_{N}\cdot\beta=0
. Let
\omega_{j}\in\Xi_{j}
so that

\|\omega_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\Xi_{j}\|^{*}+\varepsilon/(2\pi).

Let
L_{j}\to M\times N
be the Hermitian line bundle with metric connection associated to
\omega_{j}
from Lemma
4.1
. Then the bundle

E_{M}=L_{1}^{\oplus k_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{l}^{\oplus k_{l}}

satisfies

c_{m}(E_{M})=\Xi_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\Xi_{l}^{k_{l}}=a_{3}\zeta_{M}+\beta,

and
c_{l}(E_{M})=0
for all
l>n
. Moreover, by
[
13
, §4, Page 18]
, we have

\displaystyle\|R^{E_{M}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}}\|R^{L_{j}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}}2\pi\|\omega_{j}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}}2\pi\|\Xi_{j}\|^{*}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\lambda_{b}(\Lambda^{2}(M\times N))+\varepsilon.

We set
E=E_{N}\oplus E_{M}
. By the formula for the Chern classes under direct sum, we have

c_{m+k}(E)=a_{1}a_{3}\zeta_{M}\cdot\zeta_{N}\neq 0\in H^{2(m+k)}(M\times N),

so
E
is
\mathrm{K}
–admissible. Moreover, by
[
13
, §4, Page 18]
, we have

\displaystyle\|R^{E}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\|R^{E_{M}}\|_{\infty},\ \|R^{E_{N}}\|_{\infty}\right\}\leq 2\pi\lambda_{b}(\Lambda^{2}(M\times N))+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, by
[
10
, §2]
, we have

\mathrm{K}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(g)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda_{b}(\Lambda^{2}(M\times N))}\geq\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)}{2\pi\Gamma_{b}},

as desired.
∎

6.2.
Line bundle A-hat–cowaist of products of 2–spheres

In the case of a product of 2–spheres, we can find a lower bound for the line bundle
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
–cowaist.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
M=(S^{2})^{n}
be the product of
n
copies of
S^{2}
. Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on

\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(g)\geq\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)}{2\pi V_{n}\Gamma_{n}},

where

V_{n}\coloneq\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Let
\Xi_{1},\ldots,\Xi_{n}
be a linearly independent spanning set for
\Lambda^{2}(M)
with

\|\Xi_{j}\|^{*}\leq\lambda_{n}(\Lambda^{2}(M)).

Let
\eta_{j}
be the pullback of the generator of

by the projection onto the
j
–th 2–sphere. By Proposition
8.1
, there are coefficients
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}

\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j}|\leq V_{n}

so that

a_{1}\Xi_{i}+\ldots+a_{n}\Xi_{n}=b_{1}\eta_{1}+\ldots+b_{n}\eta_{n}

for
b_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Hence, the top degree term of

(a_{1}\Xi_{i}+\ldots+a_{n}\Xi_{n})^{n}

is nonzero.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
, and let
\omega\in a_{1}\Xi_{i}+\ldots+a_{n}\Xi_{n}
satisfy

\|\omega\|_{\infty}\leq\|a_{1}\Xi_{i}+\ldots+a_{n}\Xi_{n}\|^{*}+\varepsilon/(2\pi).

Let
L\to M
be the Hermitian line bundle with metric connection associated to
\omega
from Lemma
4.1
. Since
\hat{\mathbf{A}}(M)=1
, we have

\int_{M}\hat{\mathbf{A}}(M)\cdot\mathrm{ch}(L)\neq 0,

so
L
is
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
–admissible. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\|R^{L}\|_{\infty}=2\pi\|\omega\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\|a_{1}\Xi_{i}+\ldots+a_{n}\Xi_{n}\|^{*}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j}|\|\Xi_{j}\|^{*}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi V_{n}\lambda_{n}(\Lambda^{2}(M))+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitary, by
[
10

, we have

\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(g)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi V_{n}\lambda_{n}(\Lambda^{2}(M))}\geq\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)}{2\pi V_{n}\Gamma_{n}},

∎

6.3.
Line bundle A-hat–cowaist of 3–dimensional complex projective space

Towards Theorem
1.8
, we compute a lower bound for the line bundle
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
–cowaist of

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Then

\hat{\mathrm{A}}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}^{\mathrm{line}}(g)\geq\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)}{4\pi}.

Proof.

Let
\Xi\in\Lambda^{2}(M)
be a generator of
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. Since

, we have

\|\Xi\|^{*}=\frac{1}{\|\sigma\|}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(g)},

is a generator of

.

7.2.
Non–quantitative bound for the volume of Kähler metrics on complex projective spaces

We make a digression to prove the curious Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
\omega
be the Kähler form corresponding to the Kähler metric
g
. Then
[\omega]=A\Xi
, where
\Xi
is the generator of
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{Z})
. Inspecting the proof of Lemma

\mathrm{K}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(g)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\|\omega/A\|_{\infty}}=\frac{|A|}{2\pi},

where the last equality follows from the Wirtinger formula. Moreover, the Wirtinger formula implies

\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n},g)=\frac{1}{n!}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}}\omega^{n}\right|=\frac{|A|^{n}}{n!}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}}\Xi^{n}\right|=\frac{|A|^{n}}{n!}.

Hence, we have

\mathrm{K}\text{-}\mathrm{cw}(g)\geq\frac{(n!\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n},g))^{1/n}}{2\pi}.

The theorem follows by Theorem
3.2
and
[
21
, Chapter II, Example 2.4]
.
∎

Finally, we use the quantitative bounds from Theorem

implies

8.
Appendix: Linear algebra fact

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a linearly independent set in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. There are coefficients
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
with

\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rceil

so that

a_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}v_{n}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
b_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, the conclusion is trivial.

Suppose the conclusion holds for
n-1
, and consider the case of
n
. By linear independence, we have (without loss of generality) that the projection onto the first
n-1
coordinates of
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}
is a linearly independent set in
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. Hence, there are
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
so that

\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|a_{j}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil

and

v\coloneq a_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+a_{n-1}v_{n-1}

is nonzero in the first
n-1
coordinates. If the
n
–th coordinate of
v
is nonzero, then we are done. Otherwise, choose
l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
so that the
n
–th coordinate of
v_{l}
is nonzero. Note that the
j
–th coordinate of
v+bv_{n}
is zero for at most one choice of
b
. Then there is some

c\in\left\{-\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil\right\}

so that

v^{\prime}\coloneq a_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+a_{n-1}v_{n-1}+cv_{l}=a_{1}^{\prime}v_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}^{\prime}v_{n}

is nonzero in every coordinate. Note that
v^{\prime}
has the desired form with

\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j}^{\prime}|\leq|c|+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|a_{j}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil+\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil.

One easily checks by cases depending on the parity of
n
that the right hand side above equals the desired bound.
∎
