Sinusoidal injury and portal hypertension after hepatectomy for colorectal liver metastases: predictors, risk stratification, and clinical outcomes
Authors: Xavier Hernández-Yagüe, Santiago López-Ben, Ana Aula-Olivar, Walter Carbajal-Ochoa, Joan Martínez-Sancho, Maria Teresa Albiol-Quer, Gemma Osca-Gelis, Maria Rosa Ortíz, Laia Falgueras-Verdaguer, Maria Buxó, Cristina Meléndez-Muñoz, Joan Figueras i Felip
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS) and early portal hypertension (ePHT) represent significant hepatic vascular complications following preoperative chemotherapy and liver resection for colorectal liver metastases (CRLM). This study aimed to identify predictors of SOS and ePHT and to develop a risk model for preoperative stratification.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted in 108 patients undergoing hepatectomy for CRLM after ≥ 4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with anti-EGFR or anti-VEGF therapy. SOS severity was graded histologically, and ePHT was defined by thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly, varices, or ascites within 3 months postoperatively. Logistic regression models identified predictors of moderate-to-severe SOS (G2-3) and ePHT. Predictive performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis.ResultsModerate-to-severe SOS occurred in 24.07% of patients and ePHT in 21.3%. Independent predictors of SOS were anti-VEGF therapy (protective, OR = 0.214; 95% CI 0.075–0.610; p = 0.004) and preoperative portal vein embolization (PVE) or ligation (risk factor, OR = 2.819; 95% CI 1.019–7.796; p = 0.046). The SOS predictive model showed good discrimination (AUC = 0.75). For ePHT, major hepatectomy (OR = 7.78; 95% CI 2.32–25.99; p < 0.001) and the calculated probability to SOS G2-3 (OR = 40.82; 95% CI 2.60–640.53; p = 0.008) were independent predictors (AUC = 0.80; specificity = 84.3%). Neither SOS nor ePHT significantly affected 90-day mortality (8.3%) or overall survival (p > 0.05).ConclusionsMajor hepatectomy and chemotherapy-induced sinusoidal injury are key determinants of ePHT after CRLM resection. Anti-VEGF therapy exerts a protective endothelial effect, while PVE increases risk. Despite their incidence, neither SOS nor ePHT compromised survival, suggesting reversibility with optimized perioperative management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00384-026-05157-z.Keywords: Sinusoidal obstruction syndrome, Early portal hypertension, Hepatectomy, Colorectal cancer

Introduction

Liver resection remains the only potentially curative treatment for colorectal cancer liver metastases (CRLM) and is commonly preceded by neoadjuvant chemotherapy to improve resectability [1–3]. While the short-term toxicities of systemic therapy are well established, its long-term hepatic consequences remain incompletely characterized. Chemotherapy-induced hepatotoxicity is clinically relevant, as it may affect perioperative risk, postoperative outcomes, and long-term liver function [4].

Sinusoidal injury represents a key histopathological pattern associated with systemic therapy. It is graded from 0 (absent) to 3 (severe, full-lobular involvement) and may progress from sinusoidal dilatation and hepatocellular atrophy to perisinusoidal fibrosis and nodular regenerative hyperplasia [5]. Moderate-to-severe injury (grade 2–3) has not been associated with increased postoperative mortality (OR 1.21; 95% CI 0.23–6.35; p = 0.82) [6]. However, oxaliplatin significantly increases the risk of sinusoidal injury, particularly after ≥ 6 cycles (RR 3.20; 95% CI 1.01–10.13; p = 0.048) [7], whereas irinotecan does not appear to increase this risk [4]. The addition of bevacizumab reduces the incidence of sinusoidal injury (HR 0.50; 95% CI 0.37–0.67; p < 0.00001), although it does not significantly decrease postoperative hepatic failure (HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.34–1.07; p = 0.08) [8]. Long-term outcomes may also be impacted, as moderate-to-severe sinusoidal injury has been associated with reduced 5-year survival (HR 2.52; 95% CI 1.25–5.50; p = 0.010) [9].

Portal hypertension is an uncommon complication following hepatic resection for colorectal liver metastases in patients without underlying liver disease. It is primarily related to the extent of resection; however, sinusoidal obstruction syndrome, particularly after oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy, has also been implicated, as described in multiple case reports [10, 11].

These findings underscore the need for structured long-term surveillance in patients undergoing hepatic resection for CRLM, particularly those exposed to perioperative chemotherapy. Preventive and monitoring strategies, including regular hepatic imaging and multidisciplinary follow-up, are essential to enable early detection and management of complications and to improve long-term outcomes.

This study evaluates medium- and long-term hepatic complications following chemotherapy and liver surgery for CRLM and discusses their implications for patient follow-up.

Materials and methods

Hypothesis and study objectives

The primary objective was to identify factors associated with the development of hepatic sinusoidal injury (sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS)) and the occurrence of early clinical manifestations of portal hypertension (ePHT) (defined as those appearing within the first three months after hepatic surgery) in patients undergoing liver surgery for colorectal cancer liver metastases (CRLM) following preoperative systemic therapy. The secondary objective was to evaluate the impact of SOS and ePHT on overall survival (OS), providing the rationale for targeted long-term surveillance in this population. Additionally, a risk calculator for predicting SOS or early portal hypertension will be developed in Microsoft Excel format to enable clinical use and facilitate external validation by other research groups. This investigation represents a secondary analysis derived from a previously published study originally powered to assess pathological tumor response, specifically, the degree of chemotherapy-induced fibrosis, in patients treated with epidermal growth factor receptor (anti-EGFR) versus vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) inhibitor-based regimens [12]. This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines. The completed STROBE checklist is provided as Supplementary Material (Online Resource 1).

Study design and population

This retrospective, registry-based study included patients who underwent hepatic resection for CRLM after at least four cycles of preoperative chemotherapy combined with targeted therapy, anti-EGFR or anti-VEGF, at the Hospital Universitari de Girona Dr. Josep Trueta. Patient identification was performed using data from the hepatic surgery registry of Dr. Josep Trueta Hospital (Girona) between 2005 and 2023. Data were collected in an anonymized database. All quantitative and qualitative variables were measured according to simple and internationally accepted criteria. RAS and BRAF mutational status was determined using the automated Idylla™ molecular diagnostic platform. The severity of sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS) was defined according to the Rubbia-Brandt classification [5]. Early portal hypertension (ePHT) was defined as the presence of any of the following within 3 months after liver resection: platelet count < 100 × 10⁹/L, radiological or clinical evidence of esophagogastric varices, splenomegaly > 12 cm (maximum diameter on computed tomography (CT)), or radiological/clinical ascites.

Potential clinical or biological determinants of SOS or ePHT were selected based on previously reported literature or considered clinically relevant due to biological plausibility. Control for potential confounding or effect-modifying variables was performed using collinearity testing within multivariable logistic regression models. Efforts to minimize potential sources of bias included the use of predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, standardized data collection procedures, and adjustment for relevant confounders through multivariable analysis. Some multicategorical variables were dichotomized (e.g., clinical risk score and pre-existing liver disease) according to previously published literature and clinically rational groupings to preserve statistical power given the limited sample size. Quantitative variables were not categorized.

The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee (code: 2019.130) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (Online Resource 2). All surviving patients gave written informed consent for participation in the study (Online Resource 3).

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) histologically confirmed colorectal adenocarcinoma, (2) liver metastases deemed resectable or potentially resectable by a multidisciplinary tumor board (MDT), (3) receipt of ≥ 4 cycles of neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus anti-EGFR or anti-VEGF therapy, (4) R0 liver resection (complete macroscopic and microscopic clearance), regardless of concurrent resection of extrahepatic disease. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) preoperative platelet count < 100 × 10⁹/L, (2) preoperative splenomegaly > 12 cm on CT scan, and (3) radiologic or clinical evidence of ascites or esophagogastric varices before surgery.

Based on these criteria, 108 patients were included in the final analysis. For the primary endpoint, all patients underwent thoracoabdominal computed tomography and complete blood count at 3 months post-hepatectomy.

For special definitions or clinical criteria, preoperative hepatopathy was graded as follows: 0 = none (no history of liver disease), 1 = mild (abnormal liver enzymes, negative viral serology, no imaging evidence of chronic disease), 2 = moderate (abnormal enzymes ± viral positivity, imaging or clinical evidence of chronic disease without cirrhosis), and 3 = severe (liver cirrhosis of any etiology, Child–Pugh A). For primary tumor location (right vs left), right-sided colon cancer was defined as tumors extending from the cecum to the splenic flexure, while left-sided colon cancer included tumors from the splenic flexure to the anopectineal line. Rectal cancer was grouped with left-sided tumors based on their shared embryological origin from the hindgut, in contrast to right-sided tumors, which arise from the midgut. This distinction reflects relevant differences in molecular features and tumor biology, supporting the biological rationale for combining rectal and left-sided colon cancers, in line with established classifications in colorectal cancer research. Clinical risk score (CRS) was calculated according to Fong et al. criteria and categorized as low (0–2) or intermediate/high (3–5) [13]. Lymph node ratio (LNR) was defined as the ratio of positive regional lymph nodes to the total number retrieved in the colectomy or rectal resection specimen. Liver failure was defined according to the International Study Group of Liver Surgery (ISGLS) and categorized as No or Yes (grades A, B, or C) [14].

Statistical analysis

Sample size

The original study design was powered to detect a difference in the proportion of patients achieving ≥ 40% tumor fibrosis between EGFR inhibitor (45.7%) and VEGF inhibitor-based (24.2%) regimens. Based on this endpoint, 52 patients per group (total N = 104) were required for adequate power [12]. The current analysis, focusing on SOS and ePHT, was exploratory and hypothesis-generating.

Statistical methods

Categorical variables were summarized as absolute frequencies and percentages, and compared using the Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR), depending on data distribution.

Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Parametric data were analyzed using Student’s t test (two groups) or ANOVA (multiple groups); nonparametric data were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U or Kruskal–Wallis test.

Binary logistic regression models were constructed to identify factors associated with moderate-to-severe SOS (grade 2–3) or ePHT. Variables with p ≤ 0.10 in univariate analysis and those deemed clinically relevant were entered into multivariate models. Predictive performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calculation of the area under the curve (AUC). Given the predicted limited number of events relative to candidate predictors in the regression models and the potential risk of model overfitting, particularly in the ePHT model, a sensitivity analysis was performed using Firth’s penalized logistic regression to assess the robustness of the estimates [15].

Using the risk and protective coefficients of the variables included in the multivariate model for grade 2–3 sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS), the individual probability of developing SOS G2-3 was calculated for each patient. This derived variable, termed Calculated Probability to SOS G2-3, was subsequently incorporated as a preoperative informative variable into the early portal hypertension (ePHT) risk prediction model.

To prevent multicollinearity and model overfitting, the covariates used in the derivation of the Calculated Probability to SOS G2-3 were excluded from the multivariate logistic regression model evaluating independent predictors of early portal hypertension (ePHT).

Missing data were assessed for all variables. When present, multiple imputations by chained equations were performed to reduce potential bias, assuming data were missing at random. Estimates were pooled according to Rubin’s rules.

Overall survival was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier estimates and compared using the log-rank test. A two-tailed p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) and R statistical software. Patients lost to follow-up were treated as censored observations in the survival analysis. Overall survival (OS) was calculated from the date of liver resection to death or last known follow-up. For patients whose vital status could not be determined due to loss to follow-up, survival time was censored at the date of their last documented clinical contact. Censoring was assumed to be non-informative.

Results

Descriptives

Clinical and treatment-related characteristics by sinusoidal obstruction syndrome severity (grades 0–1 vs. 2–3)

Between September 2005 and January 2023, 437 hepatic resections were performed; 59 non-CRLM cases, 257 without ≥ 4 chemotherapy cycles plus anti-EGFR/anti-VEGF, and 13 treated with liver ablation were excluded, leaving 108 patients. Patients were equally distributed between treatment groups: 54 received chemotherapy plus anti-EGFR and 54 plus anti-VEGF (Fig. 1). Median age was 63 years [IQR 56–70] and body mass index (BMI) was 25.08 kg/m2 [IQR 22.9–27.0], with no differences between groups. No differences were observed in tumor location, metastasis distribution or timing (synchronous versus metachronous), preoperative hepatopathy, lymph node ratio, or type and cumulative dose of chemotherapy (Table 1).

Clinical and systemic treatment related characteristics

aSinusoidal obstruction syndrome

Anti-VEGF use was less frequent in high-grade sinusoidal obstruction (23.1% vs. 58.5%, p = 0.001), while the number of antibody cycles was similar (p = 0.406). According to the CRS-Fong score, 61.7% were high risk (score 3–5) and 38.3% low risk (score 0–2), with no differences between groups (p = 0.395) (Table 1).

RAS/BRAF mutations were present in 39.4%, with a non-significant trend toward lower prevalence in high-grade sinusoidal obstruction (26.9% vs. 43.6%, p = 0.100) (Table 1). Among 108 patients, 38 had KRAS, 2 NRAS, and 1 BRAF mutations; 4 were undetermined. Treatment intensity did not differ between wild-type and mutated groups (cycles 9.1 [IQR 5–11] vs. 8.07 [IQR 4–11], p = 0.333; oxaliplatin dose 726.48 mg [IQR 340.00–850.00] vs. 640.31 mg [IQR 358.06–717.19], p = 0.436). Monoclonal antibody type differed significantly (p < 0.001): anti-EGFR in 79.36% of wild-type vs. anti-VEGF in 90.24% of mutated patients (supplementary Table 1).

Postoperative outcomes did not differ significantly by SOS status. Clinical, pathological, and surgery characteristics by early portal hypertension (no vs yes)

Among perioperative factors, preoperative portal vein embolization or ligation was significantly more frequent in patients who developed ePHT (47.8% vs. 21.2%, p = 0.022). Hepatic inflow occlusion time (clamping time) showed a non-significant trend toward longer duration in the ePHT group (median 54.5 vs. 35.0 min, p = 0.054).

With respect to targeted therapy, patients who developed ePHT less frequently received anti-VEGF inhibitors (34.8% vs. 54.1%), though these differences did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.158). Reintervention rates, pathological tumor response, and other postoperative parameters were likewise similar across groups.

Histopathological assessment revealed a significantly higher proportion of grade 2–3 sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS) among patients with ePHT (p < 0.001). Based on its strong statistical association and potential preoperative predictive value, a derived variable, Calculated Probability to SOS G2-3, was generated from the multivariate SOS prediction model. This variable also differed significantly between ePHT groups (ePHT = YES: median risk = 0.30 [0.14–0.49] vs ePHT = NO: median risk = 0.21 [0.08–0.26], p = 0.018) and was included in the logistic regression model for early portal hypertension prediction.

Predictive model for sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS)

Logistic regression identified factors associated with moderate-to-severe SOS (grade 2–3). In univariate analysis (Table 4), no associations were found for sex, age, or chemotherapy backbone (all p > 0.05). Chemotherapy cycles showed a trend (OR = 1.08; 95% CI 1.00–1.16; p = 0.056), and cumulative oxaliplatin dose was significant (p = 0.043). Anti-VEGF therapy was protective (OR = 0.22; 95% CI 0.07–0.58; p = 0.002), while portal vein embolization/ligation showed a trend toward increased risk (OR = 2.58; 95% CI 0.99–6.69; p = 0.052). No associations were observed for tumor response (Rubbia-Brandt or Poulsides criteria) [17, 18], hepatopathy, BMI, necrosis, or CRS (all p > 0.05).

Predictive model for early clinical portal hypertension (ePHT)

Logistic regression identified factors associated with early clinical portal hypertension (ePHT). In univariate analysis (Table 5), sex (p = 0.418) and age (p = 0.605) were not associated, nor were chemotherapy backbone (p = 0.202), cumulative chemotherapy dose (p = 0.382), or monoclonal antibody type (p = 0.108). Portal vein embolization/ligation was more frequent in ePHT (OR = 0.30; 95% CI 0.11–0.80; p = 0.016), and major hepatectomy showed a strong association (OR = 7.52; 95% CI 2.53–28.6; p < 0.001). Clamping time showed a non-significant trend (p = 0.225). Histopathological variables were not associated with ePHT (p > 0.05). The variable calculated probability to SOS G2-3 was strongly associated (OR = 44.3; 95% CI 3.44–570.33; p = 0.004).

In multivariate analysis (Table 5), independent predictors associated with the occurrence of clinical ePHT were major hepatectomy (OR = 7.78; 95% CI 2.33–25.99; p < 0.001) and calculated probability to SOS G2-3 (OR = 40.82; 95% CI 2.60–640.53; p = 0.008), with no other significant variables.

In the Firth penalized logistic regression model, major hepatectomy remained independently associated with increased ePHT risk (OR = 6.87; 95% CI 2.35–24.32; p < 0.001), as did higher predicted SOS probability (OR = 34.9; 95% CI 2.58–560.2; p = 0.007). The overall model was significant (likelihood ratio test p < 0.001).

Model performance showed sensitivity 25% (4/12), specificity 84.3% (81/96), and AUC 0.796 (95% CI 0.690–0.902; SE 0.053) (Fig. 2b).

Overall survival

Two patients were lost to follow-up after relocating, and therefore their survival status and information on disease progression could not be ascertained. In the survival analyses, overall survival was censored at the date of their last recorded follow-up, so they contributed data only up to that time point. The median follow-up was 41.6 months.

The Kaplan-Meier analysis of overall survival showed no significant differences between patients with mild or no sinusoidal obstruction (SOS grade 0–1) and those with moderate to severe sinusoidal obstruction (SOS grade 2–3). The median overall survival was 29 months (95% CI, 23.31–34.68) for the SOS 0–1 group and 28 months (95% CI, 5.37–50.62) for the SOS 2–3 group (log-rank p = 0.59) (Fig. 3a).

Similarly, overall survival did not differ significantly according to the presence of clinical features of early portal hypertension. The median overall survival was 27 months (95% CI, 23.08–30.91) among patients without clinical ePHT, compared with 46 months (95% CI, 27.36–64.63) among those who developed clinical signs of ePHT (log-rank p = 0.37) (Fig. 3-b).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, we analyzed clinical, pathological, and therapeutic factors associated with early clinical portal hypertension (ePHT) after hepatic resection for colorectal liver metastases (CRLM), as well as its relationship with chemotherapy-induced sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS). Although portal hypertension is an uncommon complication, our findings suggest that it may result from the combined effect of extensive liver resection and pre-existing sinusoidal injury related to neoadjuvant chemotherapy.

Regarding SOS, multivariate analysis identified two independent factors: anti-VEGF therapy, which was associated with a reduced risk, and preoperative portal vein embolization (PVE) or ligation, which was associated with an increased risk. Although oxaliplatin has been classically associated with sinusoidal injury, it was not independently associated with SOS in the multivariate model. However, the number of chemotherapy cycles showed a trend (OR = 1.085; 95% CI 0.998–1.180; p = 0.055), and cumulative oxaliplatin dose was significant in univariate analysis (p = 0.043), suggesting a dose-dependent effect. These findings are consistent with the multifactorial nature of chemotherapy-related liver injury [19] and prior evidence linking oxaliplatin-induced endothelial dysfunction and sinusoidal resistance to portal hypertension [20]. The observed association with anti-VEGF therapy (OR = 0.214; 95% CI 0.075–0.610; p = 0.004) may be partly explained by its endothelial-stabilizing properties, although further studies are needed to clarify the underlying biological mechanisms [21]. Similarly, Firth penalized regression confirmed these findings.

No statistically significant association was observed between SOS grade and the extent of liver resection (minor vs major: 53.7% vs 50.0% and 46.3% vs 50.0%, respectively; p = 0.459), suggesting that SOS may reflect a diffuse vascular injury rather than to tumor burden, although this finding should be interpreted cautiously. Post-hepatectomy liver failure appeared more frequent in low-grade SOS; however, stratified analysis showed that this was driven by major hepatectomy (supplementary Table 2). Liver failure occurred in 28.9% vs 4.5% (major vs minor) in SOS G0-1 and in 15.4% vs 0% (major vs minor) in SOS G2-3, highlighting resection extent as the main determinant.

Surgical technique, including clamping strategy, was not associated with ePHT. Among 97 patients requiring clamping, Pringle maneuver was used in 70.19% (n = 73), selective clamping in 18.26% (n = 19), and no clamping in 11.53% (n = 12), with similar ischemia times (56.19 min [20.5–82] vs 56.16 [28–86] vs 0 min; p = 0.204) respectively (Table 2). No association with ePHT was observed (Pringle: OR = 1.63; 95% CI 0.32–8.17; p = 0.549; selective: OR = 0.93; 95% CI 0.13–6.62; p = 0.948), suggesting that early portal hypertension is more closely related to parenchymal loss and the future liver remnant [22] (Table 5).

For ePHT, multivariate analysis identified two independent predictors: major hepatectomy (OR = 7.78; 95% CI 2.33–25.99; p < 0.001) and predicted probability of SOS G2-3 (OR = 40.82; 95% CI 2.60–640.53; p = 0.008). These results were confirmed by Firth regression (major hepatectomy: OR = 6.87; 95% CI 2.35–24.32; p < 0.001; SOS probability: OR = 34.9; 95% CI 2.58–560.2; p = 0.007; likelihood ratio p < 0.001), suggesting a pathophysiological link between sinusoidal injury and increased portal pressure. The predictive model showed good discrimination (AUC = 0.796; 95% CI 0.690–0.902; SE 0.053), high specificity (84.3%), but low sensitivity (25%, 4/12), likely reflecting the low event rate (21%). Thus, the model may be more informative for identifying low-risk patients.

Clinically, patients with a higher predicted risk of ePHT might potentially benefit from strategies such as limiting chemotherapy cycles, prioritizing anti-VEGF therapy, avoiding PVE, or reducing the extent of resection when feasible, although these approaches require prospective validation. Conversely, patients classified as low risk may not necessarily require intensive surveillance. A risk calculator based on both models was developed (Online Resource 4), but its clinical utility should be interpreted with caution pending external validation.

The 90-day mortality rate was 8.3%, comparable to the 8.4% reported by Mayo et al. [23] in 1903 patients, suggesting the external validity of our cohort. Importantly, neither SOS nor ePHT was independently associated with postoperative morbidity, 90-day mortality, or overall survival, consistent with previous reports [26].

Survival analysis showed no significant differences: median overall survival was 29 vs 28 months for SOS G0-1 vs G2-3 (p = 0.59) and 27 vs 46 months for no ePHT vs ePHT (p = 0.37). The apparently longer survival in ePHT patients is likely due to residual confounding, as survival could be influenced by other oncological factors not included in the model. Moreover, ePHT was defined within 3 months and not assessed longitudinally, limiting its prognostic value.

PVE was associated with both SOS and ePHT in univariate analyses, suggesting that while it promotes hypertrophy, it may exacerbate portal pressure and sinusoidal stress in chemotherapy-exposed livers. Its indication should therefore be carefully weighed.

This study has limitations, including its retrospective single-center design, small number of ePHT events, and lack of direct hemodynamic measurements. The long inclusion period (2005–2023) could introduce heterogeneity in treatments and surgical techniques. Additionally, adjuvant or consecutive therapies and recurrence treatments may confound survival outcomes. Despite these limitations, the consistent association between SOS and ePHT could favor a biologically plausible relationship.

Finally, ePHT was defined using surrogate clinical markers (thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly, ascites, varices), which are recognized indicators of clinically significant portal hypertension [24–26], although the absence of hepatic venous pressure gradient measurement remains a limitation.

Conclusions

In summary, anti-VEGF therapy was associated with a reduced risk of sinusoidal injury, whereas preoperative PVE appeared to be associated with an increased risk among patients undergoing liver resection for CRLM. Major hepatectomy and moderate-to-severe sinusoidal obstruction syndrome were identified as factors associated with the development of early clinical portal hypertension in patients treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Importantly, the occurrence of ePHT was not independently associated with impaired long-term survival in this cohort, suggesting that its clinical impact may be limited with appropriate perioperative management, although this observation should be interpreted with caution given the retrospective design. Prospective studies incorporating direct portal pressure measurements are warranted to validate these findings and further refine predictive models for postoperative ePHT.
