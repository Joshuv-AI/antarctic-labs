1.
Introduction

For graphs
F,H
and an integer
r\geq 2
, write
F\longrightarrow_{\mathrm{ind}}(H)_{r}
if every
r
-colouring of the edges of
F
contains a monochromatic
induced copy of
H
. The induced Ramsey number
R_{\mathrm{ind}}(H;r)
is the
least integer
N
for which there is an
N
-vertex graph
F
with

F\to_{\mathrm{ind}}(H)_{r}
. A recent theorem of Aragão, Campos, Dahia, Filipe and
Marciano gives the exponential bound

R_{\mathrm{ind}}(H;r)\leq r^{Crh}

for every graph
H
on
h
vertices
[
1
]
. Their proof is random in
nature: at the scale
M=r^{O_{H}(r)}
, a typical graph
G(M,1/2)
is
induced-Ramsey for
H
with
r
colours. More importantly for us, their
argument proves a stronger weighted statement. In every colouring of such a
typical dense graph, some colour contains induced copies of
H
supporting a
measure with small weighted overlaps. More precisely, these copies form a hypergraph admitting a non-zero measure

\nu
for which the weighted sum of squared codegrees

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V\\
|S|\geq 2\end{subarray}}d_{\nu}(S)^{2}\rho^{-|S|}

is small compared with
e(\nu)^{2}
, where
d_{\nu}(S)
denotes the

\nu
-mass of copies containing
S
, and
e(\nu)
is the total mass of

\nu
. This is the Janson condition of Aragão, Campos, Dahia, Filipe and
Marciano; we recall the precise form in
Section

2
.

The purpose of this note is to show that this extra distributional information
has a consequence in the sparse setting. The idea of the proof is as follows. The bare
assertion that
G(M,1/2)
is induced-Ramsey would only allow one to use

M
-vertex subgraphs in which no edge has been lost. The weighted statement
is stronger: its pair-overlap consequence implies resilience under the deletion
of many edges. Once this resilience is averaged over the
M
-vertex induced
subgraphs of a larger random graph
G(N,1/2)
, it gives a supersaturation
statement for tuples of edge sets. Hypergraph containers then compress the
possible colour classes, and a random thinning of
G(N,1/2)
hits the
complement of every bad tuple.

Throughout the paper
H
is fixed, has no isolated vertices, and has

e(H)\geq 2
. Put
h=v(H)
,
q=e(H)
, and define

m_{2}(H)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq H\\
v(J)\geq 3\end{subarray}}\frac{e(J)-1}{v(J)-2}.

This is the usual maximum 2-density in sparse Ramsey theory. All
high-probability statements in this paper mean that the relevant probability
tends to one as
N\to\infty
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Sparse random induced Ramsey theorem)
.

Let
H
be fixed, with no isolated vertices and with
e(H)\geq 2
. For every

\eta>0
, there is a constant
C>0
such that the following holds.
For every
r\geq 2
, if
N\geq r^{Cr}
and
N^{-1/m_{2}(H)+\eta}\leq p\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
then
G(N,p)\longrightarrow_{\mathrm{ind}}(H)_{r}
with high probability.

The upper bound
p\leq 1/2
is part of the formulation, not a technical
artefact. It reflects the non-monotonicity discussed above: for non-complete

H
, the conclusion cannot be extended to all larger values of
p
.
Equivalently, the theorem says that at a slightly enlarged version of the
vertex scale supplied by
[
1
]
, the dense edge exponent
2
may be
replaced by the sparse exponent
2-1/m_{2}(H)
, up to an arbitrary loss.

As a direct consequence we obtain the following induced size-Ramsey bound. Let

\widehat{r}_{\mathrm{ind}}(H;r)
denote the minimum number of edges in a graph

G
satisfying
G\to_{\mathrm{ind}}(H)_{r}
.

Corollary 1.2

(Size-Ramsey consequence)
.

For every
\eta>0
,

\widehat{r}_{\mathrm{ind}}(H;r)\leq r^{Cr(2-1/m_{2}(H)+\eta)}.

Equivalently, for
N=r^{Cr}
one can find an
N
-vertex graph

G
with
G\to_{\mathrm{ind}}(H)_{r}
and

e(G)\leq N^{2-1/m_{2}(H)+\eta}.

2.
The weighted dense random input

We begin by recalling the precise form of the weighted input needed from

[
1
]
. The notation below is equivalent to the notation used there, but
is specialized to the present application.

Let
T
be a graph and let
G\subseteq T
. We write

\mathcal{I}_{H}(G,T)
for the
h
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set
V(T)
whose
hyperedges are the sets
U\in\binom{V(T)}{h}
such that
T[U]=G[U]
is
isomorphic to
H
. Thus the hyperedges are induced copies of
H
in

T
whose graph-edges all lie in
G
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hypergraph on vertex set
V
. If
\nu
is a
non-negative measure on
E(\mathcal{G})
, write

e(\nu)=\sum_{Q\in E(\mathcal{G})}\nu(Q),

and, for
S\subseteq V
,

d_{\nu}(S)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in E(\mathcal{G})\\
S\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}\nu(Q).

For
0<\rho\leq 1
, set

\Lambda_{\rho}(\nu)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V\\
|S|\geq 2\end{subarray}}d_{\nu}(S)^{2}\rho^{-|S|}.

We say that
\mathcal{G}
is
(\rho,R)
-Janson if there is a non-zero
non-negative measure
\nu
on
E(\mathcal{G})
such that

\Lambda_{\rho}(\nu)<\frac{e(\nu)^{2}}{R}.

This condition is homogeneous in
\nu
, so
\nu
may be normalized to have

e(\nu)=1
.

Theorem 2.1

(Weighted random-host input, extracted from
[
1
]
)
.

Let
H
be fixed, with
h=v(H)
, and let
r\geq 2
. Put
\rho=2^{-25h^{2}}r^{-4}
.
There are constants
A_{H},b_{H},B_{H}>0
such that, whenever
M\geq r^{A_{H}r}
, the graph

T\sim G(M,1/2)
has the following property with probability at least

1-\exp\{-b_{H}r^{-B_{H}r}M^{2}\}.

For every colouring
c:E(T)\to[r]
, there is a colour
a\in[r]
such that,
writing
T_{a}
for the colour-
a
subgraph of
T
, the hypergraph
\mathcal{I}_{H}(T_{a},T)
is
(\rho,\rho M)
-Janson. In particular, after increasing
A_{H}
if
necessary, the probability is at least
0.99
.

Extraction from
[
1
]
.

We spell out the reduction because the statement used here is stronger than
the headline induced Ramsey bound. In the notation of
[
1
]
, Definition
2.1 defines, for a graph
F
and graphs
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
, the hypergraph

\mathcal{J}_{F,G^{\prime},G}
with vertex set
V(G)
, whose hyperedges are the sets

L\subseteq V(G)
such that
F\cong G^{\prime}[L]=G[L]
. Thus, if
G^{\prime}
is a
colour class in an edge-colouring of
G
, this is precisely our hypergraph of
monochromatic copies of
F
which are induced in the underlying graph
G
.
Their Definition 2.2 says that a hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
is
(p,R)
-Janson
if there is a non-zero measure
\nu:E(\mathcal{G})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with

\Lambda_{p}(\nu)<\frac{e(\nu)^{2}}{R},

where
\Lambda_{p}
is the quantity displayed above. Observation 2.4 in

[
1
]
allows this measure to be normalized to have any prescribed
positive total mass.

In Section 2.2 of
[
1
]
the authors fix
C=300
,
\delta=r^{-50}
, and
p=p(r,k)=2^{-25k^{2}}r^{-4}
.
Definition 2.7 defines the bad event
\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{H})
, where

\mathbf{H}=(H_{i})_{i\in[r]}
, as the event that there exists an edge-colouring
of
G
for which, for every colour
i
, the hypergraph

\mathcal{J}_{H_{i},G_{i},G}
is not
(p,pv(G))
-Janson. When

H_{1}=\cdots=H_{r}=H
, the complement of
\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{H})
is exactly
the assertion that every
r
-colouring has a colour
a
for which

\mathcal{I}_{H}(T_{a},T)
is
(\rho,\rho M)
-Janson, with
\rho=p(r,h)
.

Lemma 2.8 of
[
1
]
, together with Claim 2.9 and the union-bound
calculation following it, gives for
G\sim G(M,1/2)
a failure probability of the form

\mathbb{P}\bigl(G\in\mathcal{B}(H;r)\bigr)\leq\exp\{-b_{H}r^{-B_{H}r}M^{2}\}

provided
M\geq r^{Ahr}
for a sufficiently large absolute constant
A
,
where
b_{H},B_{H}>0
depend only on
H
. Writing
A_{H}=Ah
gives the
displayed statement.
∎

The following elementary consequence is the only part of the Janson condition
used later.

Remark 2.2

(Pair-overlap consequence)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
h
-uniform hypergraph on vertex set
V
, and suppose
that
\mathcal{G}
is
(\rho,\rho M)
-Janson. Then there is a probability
measure
\nu
on
E(\mathcal{G})
such that, for

d_{\nu}(xy)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in E(\mathcal{G})\\
\{x,y\}\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}\nu(Q),\qquad xy\in\binom{V}{2},

one has

\sum_{xy\in\binom{V}{2}}d_{\nu}(xy)^{2}<\frac{\rho}{M}.

Proof.

Choose
\nu
with
e(\nu)=1
and

\Lambda_{\rho}(\nu)<\frac{1}{\rho M}.

The terms with
|S|=2
are among the non-negative terms in

\Lambda_{\rho}(\nu)
. Hence

\rho^{-2}\sum_{xy\in\binom{V}{2}}d_{\nu}(xy)^{2}\leq\Lambda_{\rho}(\nu)<\frac{1}{\rho M}.

Multiplying by
\rho^{2}
gives the claim.
∎

3.
Resilience and supersaturation

The first step in the transference argument is that the pair-overlap estimate
makes the dense graph robust under edge deletion.

Lemma 3.1

(Resilience under edge deletion)
.

Let
T
be an
M
-vertex graph satisfying the conclusion of
Theorem
2.1
, and let
L\subseteq E(T)
. If

|L|\leq\frac{M}{4\rho},

then every
r
-colouring of
E(T)\setminus L
contains a monochromatic
induced copy of
H
.

Proof.

Extend the colouring of
E(T)\setminus L
arbitrarily to a colouring of all
of
E(T)
. By Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, there is
a colour
a
and a probability measure
\nu
on colour-
a
induced copies
of
H
in
T
such that

\sum_{xy\in\binom{V(T)}{2}}d_{\nu}(xy)^{2}<\frac{\rho}{M}.

The total
\nu
-mass of copies using at least one edge of
L
is at most

\sum_{xy\in L}d_{\nu}(xy).

By Cauchy’s inequality,

\sum_{xy\in L}d_{\nu}(xy)\leq|L|^{1/2}\left(\sum_{xy\in L}d_{\nu}(xy)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(|L|\frac{\rho}{M}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Thus positive
\nu
-mass remains on colour-
a
induced copies avoiding

L
. Such a copy is induced in
T
, and all its graph-edges lie in

E(T)\setminus L
, so it is a monochromatic induced copy in the deleted
graph.
∎

We now average this statement over
M
-vertex subsets of a larger dense
random graph.

Lemma 3.2

(Supersaturation in the dense random host)
.

Let
M\geq r^{A_{H}r}
, let
N\geq M
, and let
F\sim G(N,1/2)
. With
probability at least
1-\exp\{-cN^{2}/M^{4}\}
,
where
c>0
is absolute, the following holds. For every

C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\subseteq E(F)
, if

|E(F)\setminus(C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{r})|\leq c_{H}\frac{N^{2}}{\rho M},

then some
C_{a}
contains at least

c_{H}\frac{N^{h}}{rM^{h}}

labelled induced copies of
H
in
F
. Here
c_{H}>0
depends only on

H
.

Proof.

Call a set
S\in\binom{V(F)}{M}
good if
F[S]
satisfies the conclusion of
Theorem
2.1
. For each fixed
S
, the graph
F[S]
has
distribution
G(M,1/2)
, so

\mathbb{E}|\{S:S\text{ is good}\}|\geq.99\binom{N}{M}.

Changing one edge of
F
affects at most
\binom{N-2}{M-2}
of the
indicators
1_{\{S\text{ good}\}}
. The bounded-differences inequality
therefore gives

\mathbb{P}\left[|\{S:S\text{ is good}\}|<.9\binom{N}{M}\right]\leq\exp\{-cN^{2}/M^{4}\}

for an absolute constant
c>0
. Condition on the complementary event.

Let
L=E(F)\setminus(C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{r})
and assume

|L|\leq c_{H}\frac{N^{2}}{\rho M}.

Call
S\in\binom{V(F)}{M}
light if

|L\cap E(F[S])|\leq\frac{M}{4\rho}.

Averaging over
S
gives

\mathbb{E}_{S}|L\cap E(F[S])|=|L|\frac{\binom{N-2}{M-2}}{\binom{N}{M}}\leq 2|L|\frac{M^{2}}{N^{2}}\leq 2c_{H}\frac{M}{\rho}.

If
c_{H}
is chosen small enough, Markov’s inequality
implies that at least
.9\binom{N}{M}
sets are light. Hence at least

.8\binom{N}{M}
sets are both good and light.

Fix such an
S
. Colour every edge of
F[S]\setminus L
by one colour

a
for which it lies in
C_{a}
. Since
S
is good and light,
Lemma
3.1
gives a monochromatic induced copy of
H
in

F[S]\setminus L
. If its colour is
a
, then all graph-edges of the copy
lie in
C_{a}
.

Choose one labelled copy obtained in this way for every good light set
S
.
A fixed labelled induced copy of
H
is contained in at most

\binom{N-h}{M-h}
choices of
S
. If
X_{a}
is the number of labelled
induced copies of
H
in
F
whose graph-edges all lie in
C_{a}
, then

\sum_{a=1}^{r}X_{a}\geq\frac{.8\binom{N}{M}}{\binom{N-h}{M-h}}\geq c_{H}\frac{N^{h}}{M^{h}}.

Thus some
a
satisfies
X_{a}\geq c_{H}N^{h}/(rM^{h})
, as required.
∎

We shall use the following contrapositive form.

Corollary 3.3

(Tuple supersaturation)
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemma
3.2
, with probability at least

1-\exp\{-cN^{2}/M^{4}\}
, the graph
F\sim G(N,1/2)
has the following
property. If
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\subseteq E(F)
and each
C_{a}
contains fewer than

c_{H}N^{h}/(rM^{h})
labelled induced copies of
H
, then

|E(F)\setminus(C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{r})|\geq c_{H}\frac{N^{2}}{\rho M}.

4.
Containers for induced-copy-free edge sets

Let
F
be a graph on
N
vertices. Define a
q
-uniform hypergraph

\mathcal{H}_{F}
as follows. The vertex set of
\mathcal{H}_{F}
is
E(F)
. The
hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}_{F}
are the edge sets of induced copies of
H
in

F
. An edge set
I\subseteq E(F)
is independent in
\mathcal{H}_{F}
precisely
when
I
contains no induced copy of
H
whose non-edges are also non-edges
of
F
.

We use the following standard form of the hypergraph container theorem.

Theorem 4.1

(Hypergraph containers)
.

For every integer
q\geq 2
and every
A\geq 1
, there are constants

C=C(q,A)>0
and
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(q,A)>0
with the following property. Let

\mathcal{G}
be a
q
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices with average degree

d
. Suppose that
0<\tau<\tau_{0}
and

\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{G})\leq Ad\tau^{j-1}\qquad\text{for every }2\leq j\leq q.

Then, for every
0<\alpha<1
, there is a family

\mathcal{C}\subseteq 2^{V(\mathcal{G})}
such that:

(1)

every independent set of
\mathcal{G}
is contained in some
C\in\mathcal{C}
;

(2)

every
C\in\mathcal{C}
satisfies
e(\mathcal{G}[C])\leq\alpha e(\mathcal{G})
;

(3)

\log|\mathcal{C}|\leq Cn\tau\log(1/\tau)\log(1/\alpha)
.

Lemma 4.2

(Codegrees in a dense random host)
.

With high probability,
F\sim G(N,1/2)
satisfies:

(1)

|E(F)|=\Theta(N^{2})
;

(2)

e(\mathcal{H}_{F})=\Theta_{H}(N^{h})
;

(3)

the average degree of
\mathcal{H}_{F}
is
d=\Theta_{H}(N^{h-2})
;

(4)

if
\tau=N^{-1/m_{2}(H)}
, then, there exists a constant
A_{H}
,

\Delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H}_{F})\leq A_{H}d\tau^{\ell-1}
for every
2\leq\ell\leq q
.

Proof.

The first two assertions follow from the usual concentration estimates for the
number of edges and for the number of induced copies of a fixed graph in

G(N,1/2)
. They imply the third, since

d=\frac{qe(\mathcal{H}_{F})}{|E(F)|}=\Theta_{H}(N^{h-2}).

For the codegree estimate, fix a set
\sigma\subseteq E(F)
of
\ell
host
edges. If
\sigma
is contained in no induced copy of
H
, then its
codegree is zero. Otherwise, in every induced copy containing
\sigma
, the
edges of
\sigma
correspond to an
\ell
-edge subgraph
J\subseteq H
.
For a fixed such
J
, once the images of the vertices incident with these

\ell
edges are fixed, the remaining vertices of the copy can be chosen in
at most
O_{H}(N^{h-v(J)})
ways. Hence

\Delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H}_{F})\leq C_{H}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq H\\
e(J)=\ell\end{subarray}}N^{h-v(J)}.

Since
\ell\geq 2
, every contributing
J
has
v(J)\geq 3
. By the
definition of
m_{2}(H)
,
\ell-1\leq m_{2}(H)(v(J)-2)
,
and therefore
N^{h-v(J)}\leq N^{h-2-(\ell-1)/m_{2}(H)}
. Together with
d=\Theta_{H}(N^{h-2})
, this gives the required bound.
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Containers in the dense random host)
.

With high probability,
F\sim G(N,1/2)
has the following property. For each

0<\alpha<1
, there is a family
\mathcal{C}\subseteq 2^{E(F)}
such that:

(1)

every independent set in
\mathcal{H}_{F}
is contained in some
C\in\mathcal{C}
;

(2)

every
C\in\mathcal{C}
contains at most
\alpha e(\mathcal{H}_{F})
hyperedges of

\mathcal{H}_{F}
;

(3)

\log|\mathcal{C}|\leq C_{H}N^{2-1/m_{2}(H)}\log N\log(1/\alpha).

Moreover, since
H
has no isolated vertices, each hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}_{F}

corresponds to at most
h!
labelled induced copies of
H
.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
4.1
to
\mathcal{H}_{F}
, using Lemma
4.2
and

\tau=N^{-1/m_{2}(H)}
. Since
|E(F)|=\Theta(N^{2})
,

|E(F)|\tau\log(1/\tau)\leq C_{H}N^{2-1/m_{2}(H)}\log N.

This gives the stated bound on
\log|\mathcal{C}|
. Finally, if
H
has no
isolated vertices, then the edge set of a copy determines its vertex set, and
there are at most
h!
labelled embeddings on that vertex set.
∎

5.
Random thinning

We now combine the supersaturation and container statements.

Theorem 5.1

(Random thinning)
.

Let
H
be fixed with no isolated vertices and
e(H)\geq 2
. Put
\rho=2^{-25v(H)^{2}}r^{-4}
,
and let
M=\lceil r^{A_{H}r}\rceil
, where
A_{H}
is as in
Theorem
2.1
. Let
F\sim G(N,1/2)
, and let
W\subseteq E(F)
be
obtained by retaining every edge of
F
independently with probability

\theta
. There is a constant
K_{H}>0
such that, if

\theta\geq K_{H}\rho MrN^{-1/m_{2}(H)}\log N\log(rM),

then
(V(F),W)\longrightarrow_{\mathrm{ind}}(H)_{r}
with probability tending to one whenever
N^{2}/M^{4}\to\infty
.

Proof.

Condition on the conclusions of Corollary
3.3
and
Proposition
4.3
. The probability of their failure is

o(1)
under the assumption
N^{2}/M^{4}\to\infty
.

Choose
\alpha
small enough that every container contains fewer than

c_{H}N^{h}/(rM^{h})
labelled induced copies of
H
. By
Proposition
4.3
, and using
e(\mathcal{H}_{F})=\Theta_{H}(N^{h})
, it is enough to
take
\alpha=c^{\prime}_{H}(rM^{h})^{-1}
for a sufficiently small constant
c^{\prime}_{H}>0
. Then

\log|\mathcal{C}|\leq C_{H}N^{2-1/m_{2}(H)}\log N\log(rM).

Suppose that
(V(F),W)
has an
r
-colouring with no monochromatic induced
copy of
H
. Let
I_{a}\subseteq W
be the set of edges of colour
a
.
Each
I_{a}
is independent in
\mathcal{H}_{F}
: if an induced copy of
H
in

F
had all its graph-edges in
I_{a}
, then the same vertex set would span a
monochromatic induced copy of
H
in
(V(F),W)
, since the non-edges of the
copy are already absent from
F
. Hence
I_{a}\subseteq C_{a}
for some

C_{a}\in\mathcal{C}
, and therefore
W\subseteq C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{r}
.

By Corollary
3.3
, every such tuple satisfies

|E(F)\setminus(C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{r})|\geq c_{H}\frac{N^{2}}{\rho M}.

For a fixed tuple
(C_{1},\ldots,C_{r})
, the probability that the random
thinning selects no edge from this complement is at most

\exp\left\{-c_{H}\frac{\theta N^{2}}{\rho M}\right\}.

Taking a union bound over
\mathcal{C}^{r}
, the probability that a bad colouring
exists is at most

\exp\left\{C_{H}rN^{2-1/m_{2}(H)}\log N\log(rM)-c_{H}\frac{\theta N^{2}}{\rho M}\right\}.

The assumed lower bound on
\theta
makes the second term dominate the first,
after increasing
K_{H}
. This proves the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
H
and
\eta>0
. Let
A_{H}
and
K_{H}
be as in
Theorem
5.1
, and put
M=\lceil r^{A_{H}r}\rceil
and

\rho=2^{-25v(H)^{2}}r^{-4}
. Since a
\theta
-thinning of
G(N,1/2)
has
distribution
G(N,\theta/2)
, we set
\theta=2p
. The upper bound

p\leq 1/2
ensures
\theta\leq 1
.

By Theorem
5.1
, it is enough that

p\geq K_{H}\rho MrN^{-1/m_{2}(H)}\log N\log(rM).

Using
p\geq N^{-1/m_{2}(H)+\eta}
, it suffices that

N^{\eta}\geq K_{H}\rho Mr\log N\log(rM).

Since
M=r^{O_{H}(r)}
and
\rho r=2^{-25v(H)^{2}}r^{-3}
, this inequality
holds for all
N\geq r^{C_{H,\eta}r}
if
C_{H,\eta}
is chosen sufficiently
large. Increasing
C_{H,\eta}
if necessary also ensures
N^{2}/M^{4}\to\infty
in the range under consideration, so the error term in
Theorem
5.1
tends to zero. Bounded values of
r
are absorbed into
the same constant.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Choose
N=r^{C_{H,\eta}r}
with
C_{H,\eta}
sufficiently large and take

p=N^{-1/m_{2}(H)+\eta/2}.

By Theorem
1.1
, with high probability

G(N,p)\to_{\mathrm{ind}}(H)_{r}
. Chernoff’s inequality gives, with high probability,

e(G(N,p))\leq 2p\binom{N}{2}\leq N^{2-1/m_{2}(H)+\eta}.

Thus there exists an
N
-vertex induced-Ramsey graph with at most

N^{2-1/m_{2}(H)+\eta}
edges. Rewriting this bound in terms of
r
gives
the displayed estimate, after increasing
C_{H,\eta}
once more if needed.
∎

6.
Concluding remarks

The weighted input is used only through the pair-overlap estimate in
Lemma
2.2
. If one used merely that a small dense graph is
induced-Ramsey, then the averaging argument would require
M
-vertex sets
containing no deleted edge. This gives a complement lower bound of order

N^{2}/M^{2}
for bad tuples of containers. The pair-overlap estimate instead
allows each good
M
-vertex subgraph to survive the deletion of

\Theta(M/\rho)
edges. Consequently the complement lower bound becomes

N^{2}/(\rho M)
, and the thinning threshold changes from

M^{2}rN^{-1/m_{2}(H)}\log N\log(rM)
to
\rho MrN^{-1/m_{2}(H)}\log N\log(rM)
.

The theorem is therefore best viewed as a transference statement from the
weighted dense random theorem of
[
1
]
, rather than as a monotone
threshold theorem. The restriction
p\leq 1/2
is not a technical artefact: for
non-complete
H
, adding edges can destroy induced copies of
H
.
