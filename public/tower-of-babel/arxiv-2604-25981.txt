Combinatorial sums derived from properties of Legendre polynomials
Michel Bataille, Robert Frontczak

Abstract
. From an identity connecting a combinatorial sum and Legendre polynomials, we derive closed forms for a number of combinatorial sums. Some of them are obtained via results about the integrals of functions associated with Legendre polynomials. Key words : Sum, (central) binomial coefficient, Legendre polynomial, Gamma function. MSC 2020 : 05A10, 11B65, 11B83.



1.
Introduction

As an introduction and for the convenience of the reader, we start by briefly reviewing some basic properties of the Legendre polynomials.
This important class of polynomials is named after A.-M. Legendre, who discovered them around 1780.

First, recall that for a complex number
x
these polynomials
P_{n}(x),\ (n=0,1,\ldots)
are defined by

P_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n!2^{n}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left((x^{2}-1)^{n}\right),

which is called Rodrigues’ formula for
P_{n}(x)
. Applying Leibniz’s formula to the product
(x+1)^{n}(x-1)^{n}
, we get their equivalent form

P_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}(x+1)^{n-k}(x-1)^{k}.

(1.1)

Thus,
P_{0}(x)=1,\ P_{1}(x)=x,\ P_{2}(x)=\frac{3x^{2}-1}{2},\ldots
.

The following important formula (generating series) is well-known:

\frac{1}{(z^{2}-2xz+1)^{1/2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(x)z^{n}.

(1.2)

We immediately deduce that for all
n\geq 0
we have
P_{2n+1}(0)=0
and
P_{2n}(0)=\binom{-1/2}{2n}=(-1)^{n}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}
as well as
P_{n}(1)=1
. Note also that
P_{n}(-x)=(-1)^{n}P_{n}(x)
.

By differentiation with respect to
z
followed by a multiplication by
1-2xz+z^{2}
, we derive the recursion

(n+1)P_{n+1}(x)-(2n+1)xP_{n}(x)+nP_{n-1}(x)=0\quad(n\geq 1).

(1.3)

We also recall the orthogonality property of the Legendre polynomials, that is,

\int_{-1}^{1}P_{n}(x)P_{m}(x)\,dx=0\quad\text{for}\,\,n\neq m.

In particular with
m=0
, we obtain that

\int_{-1}^{1}P_{n}(x)\,dx=0\quad\text{for}\,\,n\geq 1.

Although classical, Legendre polynomials are still the subject of mathematical research. Papers covering different aspects of the topic
include Klemm and Larsen
[
9
]
, Wan and Zudilin
[
12
]
, Diekemaa and Koornwinder
[
4
]
, Guo
[
7
]
,
Chu and Campbell
[
3
]
, and Aloui
[
1
]
, to mention a few.
The On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (OEIS)
[
10
]
contains some number sequences associated with Legendre polynomials:
A001801, A008316, A110129 or A330203.

In this article, we first use a coefficient extraction formula to link a class of combinatorial sums to Legendre polynomials.
From this connection we deduce a range of combinatorial sums via integrals of certain functions associated with Legendre polynomials.

2.
A first result

Let
c_{n},n\geq 0,
be a real or complex sequence of numbers and let
F(z)
be its ordinary generating function, i.e.,

F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\,z^{k}.

Let
[z^{n}]F(z)
denote the coefficient in
F(z)
belonging to
z^{n}
. We state the following coefficient extraction identity
as a lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
n\geq 1
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2n}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}c_{k}=[z^{n}]\frac{1-z}{1+z}F\left(\frac{z}{(1+z)^{2}}\right).

(2.1)

Proof.

From

\frac{1-z}{1+z}F\left(\frac{z}{(1+z)^{2}}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}z^{k}}{(1+z)^{2k+1}}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}z^{k+1}}{(1+z)^{2k+1}}

and

\frac{1}{(1+z)^{2k+1}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\binom{2k+m}{2k}z^{m},

we deduce that

\displaystyle[z^{n}]\frac{1-z}{1+z}F\left(\frac{z}{(1+z)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}(-1)^{n-k}\left(\binom{2k+n-k}{2k}+\binom{2k+n-k-1}{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{(n+k-1)!}{(2k)!(n-k-1)!}\cdot\frac{2n}{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2n}{n+k}\binom{n+k}{2k}c_{k}.

∎

Now, our first main result follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for all
n\geq 1
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{2^{-2k}}{n+k}x^{k}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}\left(P_{n}\left(-\frac{x+2}{2}\right)-P_{n-1}\left(-\frac{x+2}{2}\right)\right),

(2.2)

where
P_{n}(x)
is the
n
th Legendre polynomial.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
2.1
. Let
c_{k}=\binom{-1/2}{k}x^{k}=\binom{2k}{k}2^{-2k}(-1)^{k}x^{k}.
Then

F(z)=\frac{1}{(1+xz)^{1/2}},

and

\frac{1-z}{1+z}F\left(\frac{z}{(1+z)^{2}}\right)=\frac{1-z}{(z^{2}+(2+x)z+1)^{1/2}}.

From (
1.2
), we obtain

\frac{1}{(z^{2}+(2+x)z+1)^{1/2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{k}\left(-\frac{x+2}{2}\right)\,z^{k},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

We note that

\binom{n+k}{k}\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n+k}{n}\binom{n}{n-k}=\binom{n+k}{n-k}\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}.

Corollary 2.4
.

For all
n\geq 1

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{2^{-2k}}{n+k}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}(S_{n}-S_{n-1}),

(2.3)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{2^{-2k}}{n+k}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}(Q_{n}-Q_{n-1}),

(2.4)

where

S_{n}=\left(-\frac{5}{4}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}5^{-k}

and

Q_{n}=\left(-\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}(-1)^{k}3^{-k}.

Proof.

From (
1.1
),
S_{n}
and
Q_{n}
are the values for
P_{n}(-3/2)
and
P_{n}(-1/2)
, respectively.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

For
n\geq 1

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n+k}=0.

(2.5)

Proof.

Use Theorem
2.2
and the fact that
P_{n}(1)=1
for all
n\geq 0
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

For
n\geq 1

\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\binom{2n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{-k}}{2n+k}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{4n}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n},

(2.6)

and
n\geq 0

\sum_{k=0}^{2n+1}\binom{2n+1+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{-k}}{2n+1+k}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2(2n+1)}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}.

(2.7)

Proof.

Use the values

P_{2n+1}(0)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad P_{2n}(0)=(-1)^{n}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}.

∎

3.
Some combinatorial identities derived from integration

Proposition 3.1
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(k+1)}=\begin{cases}1/2,&n=1\\
0,&n\geq 2.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Proof.

Under the transformation
x\mapsto-4x
, the main identity in Theorem
2.2
becomes

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n+k}x^{k}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}\left(P_{n}(2x-1)-P_{n-1}(2x-1)\right).

(3.2)

The result follows by integrating both sides from 0 to 1 and noting that for
n\geq 1

\int_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}P_{n}(x)dx=0.

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)}\frac{k}{k+1}=\begin{cases}-1/2,&n=1\\
0,&n\geq 2.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Proof.

Combine (
3.1
) with (
2.5
).
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu
be a complex number with
\Re(\mu)>0
. Then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(2k+2\mu)}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{4n}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\mu)}{\Gamma(\mu+n+1)\Gamma(\mu-n)}-\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\mu)}{\Gamma(\mu+n)\Gamma(\mu+1-n)}\right),

(3.4)

where
\Gamma(z)
denotes the famous Gamma function defined by

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{z-1}dt\qquad(\Re(z)>0).

Proof.

We shall use the formula

\int_{0}^{1}x^{2\mu-1}P_{n}(2x^{2}-1)dx=\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\mu)}{2\Gamma(\mu+n+1)\Gamma(\mu-n)},\quad\Re(\mu)>0

directly extracted from Gradshteyn and Ryzhik
[
6
, Equation (7.233)]
or easily proved by induction using (
1.3
).
Hence, working with (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(2k+2\mu)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)}\int_{0}^{1}x^{2k+2\mu-1}dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}\left(\int_{0}^{1}x^{2\mu-1}P_{n}(2x^{2}-1)dx-\int_{0}^{1}x^{2\mu-1}P_{n-1}(2x^{2}-1)dx\right),

and the proof is finished.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For
n\geq 1
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(2k+1)}=\frac{2}{(2n-1)(2n+1)},

(3.5)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(2k+3)}=-\frac{2}{(2n-3)(2n-1)(2n+1)(2n+3)},

(3.6)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)^{2}}=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{2}\binom{2n}{n}},

(3.7)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(n+1+k)}=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}}.

(3.8)

Proof.

These identities are special cases of Theorem
3.3
for
\mu=1/2
,
\mu=3/2
,
\mu=n
, and
\mu=n+1
, respectively.
For instance, using the classical properties of the Gamma function (see
[
11
]
)

\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi},\quad\Gamma(1+z)=z\Gamma(z),\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)},

we calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(2k+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\Gamma(1/2+n)\Gamma(1/2-n)}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}\left(\frac{1}{2n+1}+\frac{1}{2n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}\left(\frac{1}{2n+1}+\frac{1}{2n-1}\right).

The case
\mu=3/2
is similar, noting that
\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt{\pi}/2
. Setting
\mu=n
in Theorem
3.3
produces

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{2}(n)}{\Gamma(2n+1)\Gamma(0)}-\frac{\Gamma^{2}(n)}{\Gamma(2n)\Gamma(1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2n}\frac{(n-1)!^{2}}{(2n-1)!}.

It is worth remarking that in the last step we used the fact that since
\Gamma(z)
has simple poles at
z=0,-1,-2,\ldots
,
its reciprocal
1/\Gamma(0)
is a simple zero and the first term vanishes. Finally, with
\mu=n+1
in Theorem
3.3
we get
(
3.8
) after some simple manipulations.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

For
n\geq 1
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{(n+k)}\frac{k}{2k+1}=\frac{1}{(2n-1)(2n+1)},

(3.9)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)}\frac{k+1}{2k+3}=\frac{1}{(2n-3)(2n-1)(2n+1)(2n+3)}.

(3.10)

Proof.

Combine the first two identities in Corollary
3.4
with identity (
2.5
).
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For
n\geq 1
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)^{2}(n+1+k)}=\frac{(-1)^{n}(n^{2}-2n-1)}{n^{2}(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}}.

(3.11)

Proof.

Combine the last two identities in Corollary
3.4
.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
m\geq 0
be an integer such that
m<n-1
. Then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(m+k+1)^{2}}=\frac{(-1)^{m}(m!)^{2}(n-m-2)!}{(n+m+1)!}.

(3.12)

Proof.

Work with (
3.2
) again, multiply both sides by
x^{m}\ln(1/x)
and integrate from 0 to 1. This yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)}\int_{0}^{1}x^{k+m}\ln(1/x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2n}\left(\int_{0}^{1}x^{m}\ln(1/x)(P_{n}(2x-1)-P_{n-1}(2x-1))dx\right).

The left-hand side is quickly calculated using the well-known

\int_{0}^{1}x^{p}\ln(1/x)dx=\frac{1}{(p+1)^{2}}

for any nonegative integer
p
.

To evaluate the integrals on the right, we make use of Gautschi’s formula
[
5
]
which states that for
n>m

\int_{0}^{1}x^{m}\ln(1/x)P_{n}(2x-1)dx=(-1)^{n-m}(m!)^{2}\frac{(n-m-1)!}{(n+m+1)!}.

∎

Corollary 3.8
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(k+1)^{2}}=\begin{cases}3/4,&n=1\\
\frac{1}{(n-1)n(n+1)},&n\geq 2,\end{cases}

(3.13)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{(n+k)(k+2)^{2}}=\begin{cases}-5/36,&n=1\\
1/288,&n=2\\
\frac{1}{(n-2)(n-1)n(n+1)(n+2)},&n\geq 3.\end{cases}

(3.14)

Proof.

Set
m=0
and
m=1
in Theorem
3.7
, respectively.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

For
n\geq 2
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(n+k)(n-1+k)^{2}}=(-1)^{n}\frac{2}{(n-1)^{2}n\binom{2n}{n}}.

(3.15)

Proof.

Set
m=n-2
in Theorem
3.7
and use

\frac{((n-2)!)^{2}}{(2n-1)!}=\frac{2}{(n-1)^{2}n\binom{2n}{n}}.

∎

We conclude with the following theorem dealing with the square of central binomial coefficients.

Theorem 3.10
.

For
n\geq 1
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\binom{2n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}^{2}2^{-2k}(2k+1)\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2n+k)(k+1)^{2}}=\frac{2^{-4n}}{n^{3}}\binom{2(n-1)}{n-1}^{2}

(3.16)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{2n+1}\binom{2n+1+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}^{2}2^{-2k}(2k+1)\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2n+1+k)(k+1)^{2}}=\frac{2^{-(4n+3)}}{(2n+1)(n+1)^{2}}\binom{2n}{n}^{2}.

(3.17)

Proof.

The two results are readily checked when
n=1
, so we suppose that
n\geq 2
.

Working with (
3.2
) again we see that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n+k}2^{-k}(1+x)^{k}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}\left(P_{n}(x)-P_{n-1}(x)\right),

which after multiplying both sides with
\arcsin(x)
and integrating from
-1
to 1 becomes

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n+k}2^{-k}\int_{-1}^{1}(1+x)^{k}\arcsin(x)\,dx=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n}\left(I_{n}-I_{n-1}\right),

with

I_{n}=\int_{-1}^{1}P_{n}(x)\arcsin(x)\,dx.

We begin by evaluating the integral on the left-hand side. As
\arcsin(x)
is an odd function it is readily seen that

\int_{-1}^{1}x^{k}\arcsin(x)\,dx=\begin{cases}0,&k\,\,\text{even}\\
&\\
2\int_{0}^{1}x^{k}\arcsin(x)\,dx,&k\,\,\text{odd}\end{cases}

and hence

\int_{-1}^{1}(1+x)^{k}\arcsin(x)\,dx=2\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}\binom{k}{2j-1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{2j-1}\arcsin(x)\,dx.

The next step is to use the integral

\int_{0}^{1}x^{2n-1}\arcsin(x)\,dx=\frac{\pi}{4n}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\binom{2n}{n}\right),

which follows from

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{2n-1}\arcsin(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\sin u)^{2n-1}u\cos u\,du

\displaystyle=\left[u\cdot\frac{(\sin u)^{2n}}{2n}\right]_{0}^{\pi/2}-\frac{1}{2n}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\sin u)^{2n}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{1}{2n}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\binom{2n}{n}.

This yields

\int_{-1}^{1}(1+x)^{k}\arcsin(x)\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}\binom{k}{2j-1}\frac{1}{j}\left(1-2^{-2j}\binom{2j}{j}\right).

The two sums involved can be evaluated in closed form. First, by integration from
0
to
1
, the identity

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}\binom{k}{2j-1}x^{2j-1}=\frac{1}{2}((1+x)^{k}-(1-x)^{k})

provides

\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}\binom{k}{2j-1}\frac{1}{j}=\frac{2^{k+1}-2}{k+1}

Second, the known relation (see Bataille
[
2
]
)

\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor{m/2}\rfloor}\binom{m}{j}\binom{m-j}{j}2^{m-2j}=\binom{2m}{m}\quad(m\geq 0),

leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}\binom{k}{2j-1}\frac{1}{j}2^{-2j}\binom{2j}{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}\binom{k+1}{2j}\binom{2j}{j}2^{-2j}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor}\binom{k+1}{j}\binom{k+1-j}{j}2^{-2j}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{k+1}\left(2^{-(k+1)}\binom{2k+2}{k+1}-1\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{k+1}+\frac{2^{-k+1}}{(k+1)^{2}}(2k+1)\binom{2k}{k}.

Finally

\int_{-1}^{1}(1+x)^{k}\arcsin(x)\,dx=\pi\left(\frac{2^{k}}{k+1}-\frac{2^{-k}}{(k+1)^{2}}(2k+1)\binom{2k}{k}\right).

(3.18)

Using (
3.18
) the sum on the left hand side becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n+k}2^{-k}\int_{-1}^{1}(1+x)^{k}\arcsin(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\pi\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}^{2}2^{-2k}(2k+1)\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{(n+k)(k+1)^{2}},

where we have also applied (
3.1
). The two results in Theorem
3.10
now follow from the integral

I_{n}=\int_{-1}^{1}P_{n}(x)\arcsin(x)\,dx=\begin{cases}0,&n\,\,\text{even}\\
&\\
\pi\left(\frac{(n-2)!!}{2^{(n+1)/2}\big(\frac{n+1}{2}\big)!}\right)^{2},&n\,\,\text{odd};\end{cases}

which is Eq. 7.249 (1) in Gradshteyn and Ryzhik
[
6
]
.
∎
