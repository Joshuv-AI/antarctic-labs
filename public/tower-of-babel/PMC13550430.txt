Influences of different particle characteristics on particle movement in a forced vortex flow
Authors: Sina Gouran, S. Suresh Kumar Raju, Saad Alshahrani, S. E. Ghasemi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present paper analyzes the particle movement in a rotational fluid flow domain to increase the efficiency of the system that is used to diminish damages coming from hazardous substances due to coal and petroleum ignition in the environmental issues. The nonlinear differential equations for the fine particle are solved utilizing the Optimal Homotopy Analysis Method. The capability of the proposed method to solve non-linear differential equations of particle transportation is observed to be reasonable. Eradicating the grid generation difficulties is the most significant advantageous feature of the proposed method. The particle location profile follows an increasing trend by the drag-to-inertia ratio and particle primary radius reduction. In addition, the particle location increases during the time in all various conditions. The particle’s peripheral speed also decreases by boosting the particle’s primary radius, but increases by raising the drag to inertia ratio.

Introduction

Particle tracking in an operating flow field has some well-known applications like the rotatory filtration process for plasma separation from blood or phase separation using rotational vortices in the petroleum industry. One obvious example of particle motion is drying mineral materials such as brown coal in the energy production process. When a fluid works in rotational devices like diesel engines, compressors, and generators used in oil and gas companies, vorticities are originated. There is a crucial demand for a better perception of the problems containing complex flow fields or variable particle properties.

Chein and Chung1 used the Lagrangian method to predict particle motion in a turbulent flow. Martin and Meiburg2 declare that the higher strain areas contain more particle concentration. The significant importance of gravity when the density of the particle was higher than its surrounding flow was reported3, too. The particle trajectory in a 2D free vortex phenomenon was done by Lecuona et al.4. Verkhoglyadova and le Roux5 declared an acceptable agreement on the radial velocity of the particle between numerical and analytical findings. Different particle diameters in an incompressible flow were assessed by Uchiyama and Fukasethe6. Mukundakrishnan et al.7 declared the axial fluctuations, and different spiraling and orbital motions for the particles due to various Reynolds numbers. The impact of particle density on its motion was expressed by Dehghani et al.8. They stated that in a rotational flow domain, particles tended to move in the peripheral direction due to the drag force. When a particle moves in a fluid, a velocity-dependent drag force is exerted on it. Therefore, these forces play a crucial role for instance in the calculation of raindrop velocity or powder technology in chemical engineering9,10. The particle characteristics such as its position and its velocity components were evaluated by the differential transformation method11. The role of particle motion on the energy transfer was examined in12. Sun et al.13 analyzed the settling process of particles used in hydraulic transport applications. Wang et al.14 utilized a particle tracer to evaluate particle’s spatial location and control separation processes. It was a substantial task to increase the efficiency of the solar panel by preventing the dust accumulation15. A reduction in the vortex ring strength in the presence of the solid particles was determined by Uchiyama et al.16. Issakhov et al.17 expressed that the particle distribution was highly dependent on the circular behavior of the vortex. How the structure of the vortex changes the particle motion in a two-phase flow was examined by Gao et al.18. The movement of the three various particles with different densities in vortex flow was experimentally studied in19. The difference in the particle’s inertia and their initial velocity led to a remarkable variation in their trajectories20. The radial velocity of the particle also increased by raising the particle diameter and a non-circular central motion was observed. Zahtila et al.21 analyzed the effects of the drag and lift force on the particle distribution in a turbulent flow field. The direction of particle movement and the effect of solar radiation on it were expressed by Miki22. Due to the swirling motion of the two-phase operating flow, the influences of the interphase forces on the vortex structure were analyzed by Chen et al.23. To split the solid materials with various sizes from their base flow, a novel vortex generator was designed24, and significant enhancement in the settling performance was obtained due to the enlargement of the contact surface area. The role of nanoparticles on the flow behavior in a porous medium was numerically studied in25. Hybrid-dispersed nanoparticles were added to the base flow to improve the thermal performance of a fluid flowing through a wedge-type surface26. A three dimensional revolving flow was modeled to investigate the effects of nanoparticles’ concentration27. The literature review indicates that much more research has been done on particle trajectory simulation. However, the main research gap common between previous works11,28 is to investigate the effects of different particle characteristics such as particle radius and initial velocity on the particle trajectory. In addition, there is still a need to investigate particle motion in the free and forced vortex situation by a faster and simpler method rather than existing literature. The significant novelty of this work is applying a well-known analytical method called OHAM to solve non-linear phenomena. This method uses the least squares approach to lessen the errors in finding unknown coefficients based on what is explained in the analytical solution section. The important feature of the current method is eradicating the grid generation difficulties in the numerical method. Finally, variation of the parameters in the governing equation leads this paper to a complete perception of particle movement in the forced vortex situation.

The different numerical29,30 and analytical31,32, and semi-analytical33,34 methods are used to acquire acceptable results for non-linear differential equations. Moreover, the grid generation barrier seems to be an obvious difficulty in analyzing the natural phenomenon problem by the numerical procedures. Therefore, using analytical methods that are independent of grid points is a reasonable way to investigate these non-linear phenomena. The use of unknown convergence-control parameters in the Optimal Homotopy Analysis Method improves the accuracy and efficiency of these kinds of methods. Conclusion

This work aimed to understand the particle motion characteristics in a liquid domain and the interaction between the small solid particles and surrounding rotatory flow. In the presence of the forced vortex situation, the Optimal Homotopy Analysis Method was used to estimate the particle trajectory. The prediction of the particle trajectory was reported in good agreement in comparison with the previous work. To understand the particle behavior under various operating conditions, the primary rotational speed and primary particle speed were varied. The radial part of particle speed diminished but the tangential part of particle speed was enhanced by raising the primary speed of the particle. Moreover, raising the primary initial speed of the domain led to the radial speed enhancement and tangential speed reduction. Investigating the particle motion in a porous medium is suggested as a further research in order to widen the practical application of the present problem.
