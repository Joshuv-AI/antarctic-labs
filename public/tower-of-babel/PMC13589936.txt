Xiangshao granules regulate the dopaminergic synapse pathway and improve menopausal anxiety in peri- and postmenopausal Chinese women: A multi-center, double-blind, randomized controlled trial
Authors: Ruiyi Tang, Zhuolin Xie, Yubo Fan, Jing Zhang, Ling Lu, Yali Zhuang, Cunju Guo, Kuanyong Shu, Shize Li, Ping Zhang, Kun Liu, Yuanyuan Zhang, Jianghai Ji, Qingling Ren, Qingfang Meng, Yi Lin, Xianghua Huang, Huimin Li, Ying Liu, Qijian Deng, Hongyan Chen, Mingming Su, Xinlei Zhang, Yufei Wang, Yaoyao Liang, Peng Zhang, Rong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAnxiety is common among peri- and postmenopausal women, and many conventional treatments cause side effects. This multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial was conducted from December 2021 to June 2023 at 18 centers in China, enrolling 360 women aged 40‒65 years with menopausal anxiety. Participants were randomly assigned to receive Xiangshao granules (high-dose group, n = 180) or a placebo (very-low-dose group, n = 180) for 8 weeks. The primary outcome was the reductive ratio in self-rating anxiety scale (SAS) scores after 8 weeks, while secondary outcomes included the self-rating depression scale (SDS), Kupperman Index, and gastrointestinal symptom score (GIS). After eight weeks, the mean SAS reductive ratio was greater in the high-dose group than in the very-low-dose group (37.7% vs. 28.9%, P = 0.0052), and scores declined more in the high-dose than in the very-low-dose group, with a higher clinical improvement rate (69.8% vs. 56.9%, P = 0.0151). Although no significant between-group differences were observed for SDS, Kupperman Index, or GIS, all showed improvements from baseline. Adverse event rates were comparable. Transcriptomic analysis revealed modifications in the dopaminergic synapse pathway following Xiangshao treatment, while network pharmacology identified FOS, ATF2, and CREB1 as key dopaminergic pathway-related targets. Metabolomic data corroborated these findings. Collectively, Xiangshao granules safely and effectively alleviate menopausal anxiety, potentially by modulating dopaminergic signaling.Key words: Anxiety symptoms, Traditional Chinese medicine, Xiangshao granules, Menopause, Menopausal symptoms, Dopaminergic synapse pathway, Omics, Network pharmacology analysis

Introduction

The menopausal period is characterized by specific clinical symptoms, endocrinological alterations, and biological markers. It is generally defined as extending until one year after the final menstrual period, which typically occurs between the ages of 45 and 55. This phase represents a transitional stage from reproductive maturity to senescence. During this period, women may experience a variety of distressing symptoms, particularly emotional disturbances such as anxiety, depression, irritability, and, in some cases, paranoia. These symptoms may be attributed to the progressive decline in estrogen levels, as well as psychosocial stressors associated with the loss of fertility and shifting familial roles1. Psychological studies have consistently shown that, beginning at menarche, females exhibit greater vulnerability than males to developing depressive and anxiety disorders2. This sex-based disparity remains stable across the lifespan, persisting into old age and beyond the reproductive years3. As one of the most prevalent psychiatric disorders in the general population3,4, anxiety occurs more frequently in postmenopausal and perimenopausal women than in premenopausal women5, 6, 7. Failure to recognize and address menopausal anxiety may lead to significant adverse consequences, negatively affecting the physical and psychological well-being of affected women and their families. Therefore, timely medical intervention is recommended for the management of anxiety disorders during the menopausal transition.

Currently, conventional therapies primarily focus on psychological counseling and oral pharmacological agents. However, these medications are frequently associated with adverse effects, such as dizziness, somnolence, and constipation, and may lead to long-term dependence, which can ultimately compromise patient adherence to treatment8. In addition, not all patients respond favorably to the pharmacological options available8. While menopausal hormone therapy is effective in alleviating depressive symptoms, it does not appear to benefit anxiety symptoms9. Therefore, there is an urgent need for novel therapeutic agents with improved efficacy and a more favorable side effect profile.

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) can alleviate menopausal symptoms and improve overall quality of life while maintaining a favorable safety profile10. Xiangshao granules, a purely herbal formulation, include Baishao, Xiangfu, Chuanlianzi, Chaihu, Chuanxiong, Zhike, Banxia, Doukou, Muxiang, and Gancao. This formulation has demonstrated therapeutic efficacy and a good safety record in the treatment of perimenopausal symptoms, particularly emotional disturbances, as shown in both animal studies8 and randomized controlled trials11. Mechanistic studies of the therapeutic effects of Xiangshao granules for managing emotional symptoms during perimenopause have primarily focused on depressive symptoms. Potential mechanisms include the regulation of serum neurotransmitter levels12 and the enhancement of hippocampal brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)8, which is known to exert antidepressant effects13. Saikosaponin A has been identified as a key active component in these mechanisms14. However, the specific mechanisms by which Xiangshao granules alleviate anxiety symptoms during perimenopause remain to be fully elucidated.

Transcriptomics and metabolomics were employed in the present study to investigate the therapeutic mechanisms of Xiangshao granules. The term “omics” refers to a comprehensive approach for analyzing the molecular profiles of cells and tissues, encompassing disciplines such as genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, epigenomics, and epitranscriptomics15. Transcriptomics focuses on the transcriptome, including gene structure, expression, and regulation16, while metabolomics, situated downstream of genomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics17, aims to characterize metabolites in biological systems in response to pathophysiological changes or genetic modifications18. In addition, network pharmacology was also conducted in the study. Network pharmacology is a methodological approach that integrates pharmacology and pharmacodynamics through network construction and topology analysis, grounded in omics- and systems biology-based methods19. In recent years, it has emerged as a useful tool for systematically examining the complex network relationships between bioactive components and potential mechanisms of traditional Chinese medicine formulas20.

This study aims to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy and safety of Xiangshao granules for relieving perimenopausal anxiety symptoms through a multicenter clinical trial conducted across multiple regions in China. Additionally, using omics-based methods, the study seeks to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms via transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses, together with network pharmacology, thereby addressing a current knowledge gap in the mechanistic understanding of traditional Chinese medicine for perimenopausal emotional disorders.

Methods

Study design and setting

The present study was a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, multicenter trial with an 8-week follow-up conducted at 18 clinical centers in China between December 2021 and June 2023. The study aimed to recruit 360 healthy perimenopausal or postmenopausal women with anxiety symptoms. These participants were randomly allocated to either the Xiangshao group or the placebo group, and their assessments were conducted at three time points: baseline, after 4 weeks, and after 8 weeks. A medical review, which involved the administration of a standardized questionnaire, was conducted at baseline and during each subsequent visit. Additionally, to further investigate the mechanism of the drug’s effects on anxiety symptoms, blood samples were collected from some participants at enrollment and after 8 weeks of treatment for omics analysis. Ethics approval for human participants was granted by the Human Research Ethics Committees of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (HS-2958D) on 30 July 2021. The trial was registered with the ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05003336) on 10 August 2021. All participants provided written informed consent before proceeding with the trial. The animal experiment in this study was conducted in accordance with ethical treatment guidelines for animals and was approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the Institute of Zoology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. All animals used in this study were approved by the Ethics Review Committee of the Institute of Zoology of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (XHDW-2024-190-1).

Participants

The study included women aged 40 to 65 years with an intact uterus and at least one ovary. Participants were required to be either in the menopausal transition stage, defined by a consistent difference of at least 7 days in menstrual cycle length or amenorrhea lasting more than 60 days, or in the postmenopausal stage, characterized by at least 12 consecutive months of amenorrhea. These classifications were based on the 2011 Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop +10 (STRAW+10) criteria21. Eligible participants also were required to exhibit mild to moderate anxiety symptoms, indicated by a self-rating anxiety scale (SAS) score between 50 and 69, and to report noticeable menopausal symptoms, defined by a modified Kupperman score of 16 or higher.

Women were excluded from the study if they had a history of using menopausal hormone therapy (MHT), psychotropic medications, or other treatments for anxiety disorders, or if they had used healthcare products within the past four weeks. Exclusion also applied to those with a diagnosis of anxiety or other psychiatric disorders unrelated to menopause, a self-rating depression scale (SDS) score of 70 or higher at screening, or a medical history of severe systemic diseases such as hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, unstable coronary heart disease, severe hypertension, pheochromocytoma, severe liver or kidney disease, or malignant gynecological or breast tumors. Women with known allergies or sensitivities to the investigational product, or a history of alcohol or drug abuse, were also excluded. Additional exclusions included participation in another interventional drug clinical trial within the previous 3 months, pregnancy or lactation, plans to become pregnant during the study, unwillingness to use reliable contraception for the duration of the study, and any condition deemed by the investigators to render a participant unsuitable.

Randomization and masking

The participants were allocated to either the Xiangshao group or the placebo group using a randomization table. Upon assignment, each randomization number was permanently linked to a specific participant, ensuring it could not be reassigned within the study. The randomization schedules were held by an impartial statistician who was not involved in the study’s implementation, and these schedules were generated using computer software. The randomization sequence was generated using SAS software (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA). The randomized coding table is designated as the “blind code”. Following the completion of the blinding procedure, the blind code is duplicated, sealed, and stored.

Before the start of the study, the blinded medications were supplied, with the Xiangshao granule simulant designed to have an identical appearance, taste, and packaging as the Xiangshao granules. Any packaged medication found to be damaged was required to be discarded. Participants and investigators were blinded to group assignment throughout the study.

Intervention

In the Xiangshao group, female participants consumed one 4-g bag of Xiangshao granules (manufactured by Sichuan Hairong Pharmaceutical Co., China) brewed in warm water three times daily, 30 min after a meal, for 8 weeks. Conversely, female participants in the placebo group consumed a Xiangshao granule simulant following the same regimen. When Xiangshao granules were dissolved in hot water, they released a strong, distinctive volatile odor that could not be replicated with conventional excipients or a single flavoring agent, and because alternative approaches (food-grade flavorings or plant-derived volatile oils) did not pass blinded sensory testing, we added a small amount of the original formula’s volatile oil inclusion compound to the placebo to ensure that participants and investigators could not distinguish it by smell. On this basis, the placebo was prepared with 150 g of Xiangshao granule extract clear cream per 1000 g, corresponding to about 9.3% of the active preparation. The remaining components were mainly corn dextrin, soluble starch, edible pigment, and flavoring agents. The simulant and the Xiangshao granules were packaged and labeled in an identical manner.

Given that the placebo group contained a minimal amount of Xiangshao granule components, the comparison between the Xiangshao group and the placebo group can be interpreted as a comparison between the high-dose group and the very-low-dose group, which are hereafter referred to as the high-dose and very-low-dose groups.

Clinical study measurements

A self-administered questionnaire composed of 5 parts was developed, including the general conditions questionnaire, the SAS, SDS, Kupperman index, and gastrointestinal symptom score (GIS).

The symptoms of anxiety were assessed using the SAS questionnaire22, which consists of 20 items. Each item was assigned a score ranging from 1 to 4, with 1 indicating none or seldom, 2 sometimes, 3 often, and 4 always. The questionnaire contains 15 negative statements scored in sequential order (1‒4) and 5 positive statements scored in reverse order (4‒1). The total raw SAS score was calculated by summing the scores of each item. The standard total SAS score was obtained by rounding down the total score multiplied by 1.25. If the standard total SAS score was below 50 points, the individual was classified as not experiencing anxiety23. The severity of anxiety symptoms was categorized as mild when the standard total score ranged from 50 to 59 points, moderate when it fell between 60 and 69 points, and severe when it reached 70 points or higher.

The SDS is also a standardized scale used to evaluate the symptoms of depression24, which includes 20 items. These items assess both psychological and physiological symptoms and are rated by respondents based on how each applied to them over the past week, using a 4-point scale (1–4)25. An index score of 50 or higher on the 20-item scale indicates the presence of depression25. Higher scores reflect greater symptom severity.

The Chinese version of the modified Kupperman Menopausal Index (KMI), which has been validated and widely used in China, was used to quantify the severity and intensity of menopausal symptoms. The items in the KMI are rated on a 4-point Likert scale ranging from 0 (absence of symptoms) to 3 (severe symptoms). Hot flashes and sweating with the highest total intensity are each multiplied by 4, nervousness and sleep disorders are multiplied by 2, and the remaining symptoms are not weighted. The total score is calculated as the sum of all items after applying the weighting factors.

The GIS is a reliable tool for assessing the severity of symptoms of functional gastrointestinal diseases and has demonstrated good reliability and validity26. It includes 10 items and the intensity of each item is rated on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from “asymptomatic” to “very severe”, with a total score of 40. The higher the total score, the more severe the gastrointestinal symptoms.

Clinical study outcomes

The primary outcomes was the reductive ratio in SAS scores after 8 weeks of treatment compared with baseline scores. The SAS reductive ratio (%) = (SAS score at baseline‒SAS score after 8 weeks of treatment)/(SAS score at baseline‒25) × 100. Categorical variables based on the degrees of the SAS reductive ratio were used to analyze clinical improvement, where it was deemed that <30% reduction indicated that the treatment had been ineffective, reductions of 30%–50% indicated remission, reductions of >50%–80% indicated that the treatment had been moderately effective, and reductions of >80% indicated that the treatment had been highly effective. In addition, SAS scores <50 at the 8-week timepoint were deemed to indicate that the symptoms had been cured, consistent with the SAS definition that scores <50 indicate no anxiety.

The secondary outcomes analyzed were the SAS score reductive ratio after 4 weeks of treatment; SDS, Kupperman index, GIS, and their score reductive ratios after 4 and 8 weeks of treatment. Sociodemographic data were acquired via a questionnaire. Age, menopausal status, blood pressure, and levels of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and estradiol (E2) were recorded.

For safety assessment, vital signs, routine blood and urine tests, liver and renal function tests, electrocardiography, pelvic sonography, breast sonography, and physical examinations were performed at baseline and at the 8-week visit. All adverse events were recorded during the visits, including laboratory test abnormalities. The rates of adverse events in the high-dose group were compared with those in the very-low-dose group.

Sequencing of transcriptomic and metabolomic profiles

Omics sample preparation

For the omics component, biospecimen collection was conducted at eight centers with the necessary capacity for sampling, processing, and storage. At these eight centers, we used convenience sampling together with competitive enrollment.

Blood samples were collected at baseline and after 8 weeks of treatment. These samples were promptly stored at a temperature of −80 °C and transported in a standardized cold chain. The purpose of this process was to facilitate transcriptomic and metabolomics analysis.

RNA sequencing and quantification

RNA integrity was assessed using the RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit on the Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). Total RNA served as input for library preparation with the TruSeq PE Cluster Kit v3 (Illumina), following the manufacturer’s guidelines. Library quality was evaluated, and 150 bp paired-end reads were generated on the Illumina NovaSeq platform.

Raw data were processed using Fastp software27, followed by removal of adapter sequences, poly-N regions, and low-quality reads. Clean paired-end reads were then aligned to the GRCh38/hg38 reference genome using Hisat2 v2.0.527. Gene-level read counts were obtained using FeatureCounts v1.5.0-p328.

Serum sample preparation and metabolite quantification

Blood serum samples were diluted with LC‒MS grade water to achieve a final methanol concentration of 53%, then centrifuged at 15,000×g, 4 °C, for 20 min. The supernatant was subsequently injected into the LC‒MS/MS system for analysis29,30. Metabolite quantification was performed using an ultra-performance liquid chromatography‒mass spectrometry (UPLC‒MS) system30, 31, 32. Raw data were processed with Compound Discoverer 3.1 (Thermo Fisher) to align peaks, perform peak picking, and quantify metabolites. Peak intensities were normalized to total spectral intensity, and metabolites were identified by matching the peaks with mzCloud, mzVault, and MassList databases to obtain both qualitative and relative quantitative results.

Identification of differentially expressed genes and metabolites

Differential expression analysis was conducted using the DESeq2 R package (version 1.46.0)33 for each pair of conditions/groups, with results visualized using the ggplot2 (version 3.5.1) and pheatmap (version 1.0.12) packages in R.

Genes with a P-value <0.05 and a fold change (FC) threshold > 1.1 or < 0.909 were considered differentially expressed. The metabolites were annotated using the KEGG database (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html), HMDB database (https://hmdb.ca/metabolites), and LIPID MAPS database (http://www.lipidmaps.org/). Principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) were performed using MetaX34. Metabolites with a variable importance in projection (VIP) score >1, P-value <0.05, and FC > 2 or < 0.5 were considered differentially expressed.

Enrichment analysis

GO and KEGG pathway analysis35 were performed using the clusterProfiler R package (version 4.14.3). A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant, indicating that the associated pathways were enriched with differential genes or metabolites.

Network pharmacology construction

Pharmacological targets for the active compounds of each key ingredient in Xiangshao granules (Baishao, Xiangfu, Chuanlianzi, Chaihu, Chuanxiong, Zhike, Banxia, Doukou, Muxiang, and Gancao) were retrieved from the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology Database and Analysis Platform (TCMSP)36. A pharmacological network was constructed by intersecting drug-related targets with differentially expressed genes and compound components. The network was visualized using Cytoscape 3.7.237.

Mouse brain tissue collection and RNA sequencing

Animals and sample collection

Twelve 8-week-old SPF-grade female C57BL/6J mice were housed in the institutional animal facility under standard conditions (temperature 20‒25 °C, relative humidity 50 ± 5%). After a 7-day acclimation period, the mice were subjected to model induction. Bilateral ovariectomy (OVX) was performed under isoflurane anesthesia. Starting on postoperative Day 4, vaginal exfoliative cytology was examined once daily for five consecutive days. The absence of estrous phase changes was considered indicative of successful OVX modeling.

Following OVX, the mice were randomly assigned to two groups: (1) placebo group and (2) Xiangshao granule group (1.6 g/kg/day). Beginning on postoperative Day 7, chronic restraint stress (CRS) was applied to establish an anxiety-like behavior model. Drug or placebo was administered by oral gavage 1 h before CRS each day.

For CRS, mice were placed into ventilated 50 mL centrifuge tubes with the caps secured to prevent escape while ensuring adequate airflow. Starting on postoperative Day 7, OVX mice were restrained for 6 h per day (09:00‒15:00) for 28 consecutive days. No food or water was provided during the restraint period. The tubes were cleaned after each session. After 4 weeks of CRS, anxiety-related behavioral tests were conducted, including the Open Field Test (OFT) and the Elevated Plus Maze (EPM).

After the CRS procedure and repeated behavioral assessments were concluded, following the final behavioral assessment, all mice were anesthetized and the thoracic cavity was opened to expose the heart. Approximately 50 mL of physiological saline was perfused through the left ventricle until the effluent became clear. The hippocampus was then dissected, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for 20 min, and subsequently transferred to a −80 °C freezer for storage prior to RNA sequencing.

RNA sequencing library construction and sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using a magnetic bead-based RNA extraction kit (Magigene, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The extraction was performed in 2 independent batches, with each batch comprising 3 mice from the Xiangshao granule group and 3 from the placebo group. RNA purity and concentration were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). RNA integrity was evaluated with the RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit on the Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). A total of 12 RNA sequencing libraries were constructed using Oligo (dT) enrichment and random primers and were subsequently sequenced on an Illumina platform (Annoroad Gene Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) to generate 150 bp paired-end reads (PE150).

Gene expression analysis and enrichment analysis

Unqualified reads were removed, including adapter-contaminated reads, reads containing more than 5% ambiguous bases, and low-quality reads (Qphred ≤20 for more than 50% of the read length). Q30 and GC content were calculated for the clean data, and all subsequent analyses were performed using these high-quality clean reads. The reference genome and gene model annotation files were downloaded directly from Ensembl. The index of the reference genome was constructed using HISAT2 v2.0.5, and paired-end clean reads were compared to the reference genome using HISAT2 v2.0.5. The fragments per kilobase of exon model per million mapped fragments (FPKM) of each gene were calculated based on gene length and the number of reads mapped to each gene.

The count data of gene expression were log2-transformed, and batch effects were removed using the ComBat method implemented in the sva R package. For the target genes Fos, Atf2, and Creb1, expression differences were tested using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The further differential expression analysis was subsequently performed using DESeq2. Significant genes were filtered based on a FC > 1.1 or < 0.909 and a P-value <0.05, and were analyzed for functional enrichment using GO and KEGG pathways analysis. The details of enrichment analysis were performed using the same procedures as described above for the peripheral blood samples from human subjects.

Statistical analysis

Sample size calculations conducted prior to the clinical study indicated that enrolling 137 women per group would provide 80% power to detect a between-group difference using a two-sided alpha of 0.025. This calculation was based on prior data suggesting anxiety symptom improvement rates of 61% in the Xiangshao group and 44% in the placebo group11. Improvement rates were derived from the reductive ratio of anxiety scale scores, with participants exhibiting a reductive ratio greater than 50% or scores below the diagnostic cutoff classified as “improved”. To account for a potential dropout rate of up to 20%, 180 participants were enrolled in each group. However, because the previous study only provided improvement rates rather than direct data on the primary outcome used in the present study, our initial sample size estimation was based on those rates rather than the reductive ratio of anxiety scores. After study completion, we conducted a post hoc power analysis using the actual results and final sample size. A two-sample t-test with unequal variances yielded a statistical power of 84.69%, exceeding the conventional 80% threshold for adequate power38, thereby confirming that the current sample size was sufficient.

Continuous variables with normal distributions were presented as means ± standard deviations, while categorical variables were reported as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons of continuous variables were conducted using analysis of variance (ANOVA). Categorical data were compared using Pearson’s chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, depending on the sample size. For non-normally distributed data, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS software (version 9.4), with a two-sided P value <0.05 considered statistically significant.

Changes in clinical symptoms

Primary outcomes

For the primary outcome, the mean SAS reductive ratio in the high-dose group was 37.7 ± 24.74%, which was significantly higher than that in the very-low-dose group (28.9 ± 27.87%; P = 0.0052). Also, compared with baseline, mean SAS scores were lower in both groups 8 weeks after the initiation of treatment (44.7 ± 7.94 in the high-dose group, 47.2 ± 8.79 in the very-low-dose group; both P < 0.0001) (Fig. 2A), while the SAS score was significantly lower in the high-dose group than in the very-low-dose group after 8 weeks (P = 0.0086).

(B) Clinical improvements of SAS at baseline and after 4 weeks treatment in both groups. (C) Clinical improvements of SAS at baseline and after 8 weeks treatment in both groups. After 8 weeks of treatment, 69.8% of women in the high-dose group were classified as ‘cured’, while only 54% of participants were cured after 4 weeks. Meanwhile, the proportion classified as cured was 56.9% and 54% at the 8- and 4- week time points in the very-low-dose group. The differences in clinical improvement between the two groups were statistically significant after 8 weeks (P = 0.0151; Fig. 2C).

Secondary outcomes

Regarding the secondary outcomes, the mean SAS reductive ratio at four weeks was 28.8 ± 27.39% in the high-dose group and 26.9 ± 25.71% in the very-low-dose group, with no statistically significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.6616). However, mean SAS scores showed a significant reduction in both groups four weeks after the initiation of treatment when compared with baseline (47.7 ± 8.78 in the high-dose group and 47.8 ± 8.27 in the very-low-dose group; both P < 0.0001). The differences in cure rates between the two groups after four weeks of treatment were also assessed, and no statistically significant differences were observed (P = 0.3190).

Mean SDS scores at week 4 were 50.8 ± 10.64 in the high-dose group and 50.9 ± 10.44 in the very-low-dose group, while at week 8 the scores were 48.5 ± 11.83 and 49.4 ± 11.90, respectively. Compared with baseline, SDS scores significantly decreased in both groups at both time points (P < 0.0001; Fig. 3A). However, no significant differences between the groups were observed in mean SDS scores at any time point or in the reductive ratios (P > 0.05).

Adverse events

Participants who took the medication at least once were included in the safety analysis, as summarized in Table 2, with 176 in the high-dose group and 169 in the very-low-dose group. In the high-dose group, 99 adverse events were reported by 52 participants, corresponding to an incidence of 29.5% (52/176). Thirteen adverse reactions occurred in 10 participants, with an incidence of 5.7% (10/176). In the very-low-dose group, 81 adverse events were reported by 52 participants (30.8%, 52/169), and 16 adverse reactions were reported by 11 participants (6.5%, 11/169).

Transcriptomic analysis: enrichment of differential genes in the pathways including dopaminergic synapse

Transcriptome profiling was performed on the samples. Differentially expressed genes were identified based on FC and P-value, with thresholds set at P-value <0.05 and a FC > 1.1 or < 0.909. The results showed a significantly higher number of differentially expressed genes in the high-dose group than in the very-low-dose group (Fig. 4A and B). After treatment with Xiangshao granules, the high-dose group exhibited 526 upregulated genes and 366 downregulated genes, while the very-low-dose group showed 189 upregulated genes and 400 downregulated genes, with 57 genes in common (Fig. 4C). GO analysis was performed for differentially expressed genes before and after medication in the high-dose group, after excluding those shared with the very-low-dose group. The enriched biological processes included those related to neuronal activity and stress responses, such as intracellular calcium ion homeostasis39, cellular response to chemical stress40, positive regulation of secretion, and learning or memory (Fig. 4D). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was then applied to ascertain the primary biological functions of these differentially expressed genes. Using a hypergeometric test, pathways significantly enriched in these genes were identified relative to the entire identified gene background. The enrichment analysis of KEGG pathways for differentially expressed genes is presented in Fig. 4E, highlighting the dopaminergic synapse pathway as the only pathway associated with emotional regulation.

Network pharmacology analysis: Revealing the target genes FOS, ATF2, CREB1, and the target pathway——dopaminergic synaptic pathway

The network pharmacology analysis of Xiangshao granules was conducted using a network pharmacology database. Initially, the target genes associated with the primary constituents of Xiangshao granules (including Baishao, Xiangfu, Chuanlianzi, Chaihu, Chuanxiong, Zhike, Banxia, Doukou, Muxiang, and Gancao) were screened from the database. Subsequently, a comparison was made between the gene alterations before and after treatment with Xiangshao granules in the high-dose group. A total of 566 genes, corresponding to the active small molecules of the primary constituents, were identified based on the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology (TCMSP) database. Upon intersecting these genes with the 892 differential genes in the high-dose group, a subset of 20 genes was found to overlap (Fig. 5A). The expression changes of these 20 differential genes in the high-dose group before and after treatment with Xiangshao granules are depicted in Fig. 5B. Following this, we conducted pathway enrichment analysis of the aforementioned 20 coincident genes using the KEGG database and identified the top 15 significantly enriched pathways, which were then visualized in a bubble chart (Fig. 5C). Notably, one of these pathways was the dopaminergic synapse pathway, consistent with the transcriptomic analysis, as well as related to emotional regulation. To delineate the biological processes jointly supported by multi-omics evidence, we integrated pathway enrichment results from the transcriptomic analysis with those obtained through network pharmacology. The top ten enriched pathways identified from transcriptomic profiling were combined with the top ten pathways derived from the 20 shared genes obtained through network pharmacology, yielding a total of 13 non-redundant pathways. For each pathway, genes originating from the transcriptomic dataset and those mapped from the network pharmacology gene set were compared, providing a consolidated view of convergent molecular mechanisms (Fig. 5D).

Metabolomic analysis: Enrichment of differential metabolites in the dopaminergic synapse

A comparative metabolomic analysis was conducted on samples from the high-dose and very-low-dose groups before and after treatment to further validate the previously explored results. Differential metabolites were identified using VIP (Variable Importance in the Projection) from the PLS-DA model, FC, and P-value calculated by t-test. Criteria for differential metabolites included VIP score >1, P-value <0.05, and FC > 2 or < 0.5. Results indicated a higher number of differential metabolites in the high-dose group after medication than in the very-low-dose group (Fig. 6A‒F). The comparison of intersection metabolites between the high-dose and very-low-dose groups before and after treatment is presented in Fig. 6G. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was employed to ascertain the primary biological functions associated with differential metabolites. The enrichment outcomes were derived from KEGG pathways, and hypergeometric testing techniques were utilized to discern the pathways that exhibit enrichment in differential metabolites relative to the entire identified metabolite background. A KEGG pathway that met the threshold of P < 0.05 was considered significantly enriched in differential metabolites. By conducting KEGG pathway enrichment analysis, we identified two primary pathways that exhibited statistical significance: dopaminergic synapse (P < 0.05) and vitamin digestion and absorption (P < 0.05). The corresponding differential metabolites associated with these pathways are homovanillic acid (HVA) (implicated in dopamine synaptic/tyrosine metabolism) and menadione (also known as vitamin K, involved in vitamin metabolism and absorption) (Fig. 6H).

The dopaminergic synaptic pathway exhibited notable differences before and after medication, as evidenced by both transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses. Additionally, network pharmacology analysis indicated that the dopaminergic pathway was among the pathways associated with the target genes linked to the primary components of Xiangshao granules. In the very-low-dose group, no transcriptional metabolic co-enriched pathway was observed. In conclusion, the findings from metabolomic, transcriptomic, and network pharmacological analyses in the high-dose group indicate that target genes within the dopaminergic pathway and associated metabolites underwent alterations in peripheral blood following drug administration.

Animal experiment validation: Target genes related to the dopaminergic pathway showed broadly consistent expression changes in the hippocampus of mice

Compared with the placebo group, mice in the Xiangshao group showed reduced anxiety-like behavior. To validate the target genes identified from the omics analysis of human participants, we performed transcriptome sequencing of hippocampal tissue from mice. We found that hippocampal expression of Fos (P = 0.026) and Creb1 (P = 0.041) was significantly higher in the Xiangshao group than in the placebo group. Although Atf2 expression did not differ significantly between groups, these results were broadly consistent with our previous findings (Supporting Information Fig. S1A).

KEGG pathway analysis of differentially expressed genes between the two groups revealed enrichment in the “dopaminergic synapse” pathway, corroborating the enrichment analysis results from human data (Fig. S1B). Similarly, GO enrichment analysis further supported this pattern, with differentially expressed genes significantly enriched in biological processes related to anxiety-related emotional regulation, such as circadian rhythm41, cellular response to nutrient levels42, response to starvation43, and adenylate cyclase-modulating G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway, which is highly associated with dopaminergic signaling44 (Fig. S1C). Taken together, the enrichment analysis results from the mouse hippocampus largely align with those from human peripheral blood.

Discussion

As societies age and the number of older women increases, greater attention is needed to support physical and mental health of perimenopausal women. In addition to physical discomforts such as hot flushes and sweating, this population is also prone to psychological issues, including anxiety and depression. Randomized controlled trials have suggested that certain Chinese medicines may help reduce anxiety symptoms in peri- and postmenopausal women45, 46, 47, particularly in cases where the use of MHT is contraindicated. However, these studies often involved small sample sizes, lacked double-blind designs, or included non-pharmacological interventions such as psychotherapy, thereby weakening the strength of the evidence regarding the efficacy of Chinese medicine for menopause-related anxiety. A recent study reported that Xiangshao granules may have a beneficial effect on emotional symptoms in menopausal women11. Based on this, the present study further evaluated the therapeutic effect of Xiangshao granules on anxiety symptoms in menopausal women through a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, multicenter trial, and found that the high-dose Xiangshao granules group demonstrated significantly greater efficacy than the very-low-dose group. To explore the underlying mechanisms, transcriptomic and network pharmacology analyses were conducted on samples collected before and after treatment, revealing that the therapeutic effect of Xiangshao granules may be mediated through modulation of the dopaminergic synapse pathway, which plays a key role in emotional regulation. This finding was further supported by metabolomic analysis.

Anxiety is a common symptom experienced by women during the menopausal transition. Among anxiety disorders, two major subtypes are recognized: panic disorder and generalized anxiety disorder (GAD). GAD is characterized by persistent anxiety without a specific object or content, often accompanied by symptoms of autonomic nervous system activation, muscle tension, and motor restlessness48. This type of anxiety is particularly prevalent among perimenopausal women49. A cohort study in Chinese women reported that both anxiety and depression were more common during and after menopause compared with the premenopausal period7. Similarly, research by Tangen et al.50 found a significant positive correlation between anxiety scores and the onset of perimenopause, followed by a decline in anxiety levels during the postmenopausal stage. Other studies have also linked the loss of ovarian function with an increased risk of various neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative conditions, including anxiety and depressive disorders51, 52, 53. In addition, several risk factors contributing to anxiety during menopause have been identified. For example, one study showed that postmenopausal women with lower levels of physical activity were more likely to experience moderate to severe trait anxiety symptoms54. However, it is imperative to employ a validated anxiety scale when investigating the association between symptoms of anxiety and menopausal status, so as to avoid conflating emotional distress with physiological discomforts such as menopause-induced hot flushes and sweating55. In our study, we used the SAS scale to assess anxiety levels.

Xiangshao granules are composed of a blend of Baishao, Xiangfu, Chuanlianzi, Chaihu, Chuanxiong, Zhike, Banxia, Doukou, Muxiang, and Gancao. The present study confirmed the efficacy and safety of Xiangshao granules in alleviating anxiety symptoms in menopausal Chinese women. However, the underlying mechanism of action had remained unclear prior to this investigation.

Existing studies on the antidepressant mechanisms of traditional Chinese medicine have highlighted the role of the brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) signaling pathway, which is known to exert antidepressant effects. It has been speculated that Xiangshao granules may affect BDNF levels both through direct enhancement8 and indirect modulation via the upregulation of saikosaponin A14,56. This process may subsequently enhance the expression of high-affinity tropomyosin receptor kinase B13, while also reducing levels of serum corticotropin-releasing hormone, corticosterone, and corticotropin8,12, thereby contributing to the relief of depressive symptoms. Moreover, saikosaponin A has been shown to restore neuroendocrine, neuroinflammatory, and neurotrophic functions in the hippocampus of perimenopausal women57.

However, as most existing research has focused primarily on depression-related symptoms, further investigation is needed to clarify the mechanisms by which Xiangshao granules alleviate anxiety. Currently, omics technologies are widely applied to investigate disease mechanisms and drug actions in modern biomedical research. However, their use in the study of traditional Chinese medicine remains limited. In this context, our study employed omics approaches to explore the potential mechanisms through which Xiangshao granules may exert anxiolytic effects.

Our study, through transcriptomics, network pharmacology analysis, and metabolomics validation, found that Xiangshao granules primarily alleviate anxiety by influencing the dopaminergic synaptic pathway. The specific action of this drug is mediated through three target genes—FOS, CREB1, and ATF2—as well as the metabolite HVA. We found that FOS was upregulated after treatment with Xiangshao granules in peripheral blood samples. FOS plays a critical role in regulating neuronal activity and has been closely linked to mood disorders such as anxiety and depression58. Dysfunction of FOS-mediated transcriptional networks in the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) under stress disrupts calcium and cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) signaling, leading to social interaction deficits and other stress-related behaviors, thereby highlighting FOS as a potential therapeutic target for stress-related psychiatric disorders59.

CREB1 and ATF2 are members of the ATF/CREB transcription factor family, and were both upregulated following treatment with Xiangshao granules. CREB1 is an important transcription factor that is widely expressed in neuroendocrine tissues59 and has been shown to play a crucial role in multiple antidepressant and anxiolytic mechanisms. Regulated by cAMP, CREB1 modulates anxiety and depression-like behaviors through the BDNF/tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB)/CREB pathway60. When BDNF binds to TrkB, CREB1 is activated, thereby influencing the biological functions of BDNF, including enhancing synaptic connections, promoting the synthesis and release of neurotransmitters, and participating in neuronal differentiation, proliferation, maturation, and nutrition61,62, which makes CREB1 a key target for the study of mood disorders such as anxiety and depression. ATF2 is widely expressed in the brain63. Although current research has mainly focused on its role in depression, the frequent co-occurrence and shared pathogenic mechanisms of anxiety and depression make it reasonable to consider these conditions together64. ATF2 has been linked to the expression of genes responsive to certain antidepressant treatments65,66. Antidepressant treatment reduces phosphorylated ATF2 (pATF2) levels in the frontal cortex, whereas chronic stress increases pATF2 expression and decreases total (non-phosphorylated) ATF2 protein67. Another study has also reported that overexpression of ATF2 in the nucleus accumbens enhances emotional reactivity and produces an antidepressant-like effect68, which may help explain the ATF2 upregulation and reduced anxiety symptoms observed in our study following Xiangshao granule administration.

In our study, treatment with Xiangshao granules increased HVA levels and alleviated patient anxiety, findings that are consistent with previous reports. HVA is one of the main metabolites of dopamine and has attracted increasing attention in anxiety disorder research in recent years. Multiple studies have reported that individuals with a history of suicide attempts exhibit reduced levels of both dopamine and its primary metabolite, HVA67,69. Further investigations have shown a negative correlation between cerebrospinal fluid HVA levels and anxiety severity, particularly among female subjects70.

Through the analysis of peripheral blood target genes and metabolic alterations, we found that all targets showing significant changes after Xiangshao granule treatment are well documented to be associated with anxiety, with changes consistently aligning with symptom improvement. Our omics analysis revealed that these targets are related to dopaminergic synapses, and the altered metabolite represents a major metabolite of dopamine. These findings suggest that the anxiolytic effects of Xiangshao granules in menopausal women may involve peripheral blood signals potentially linked to dopaminergic pathways. Regarding adverse effects, we observed a borderline significant increase in newly diagnosed lipid-related disorders in the high-dose group compared with the very-low-dose group, although the overall number of cases was relatively small. This finding suggests that a potential adverse impact of Xiangshao granules on lipid metabolism cannot be completely ruled out. It highlights the importance of monitoring lipid profiles during treatment and underscores the need for future studies specifically designed to further investigate the underlying mechanisms of this effect.

This study possesses several notable strengths. Foremost, its primary advantage lies in the integration of transcriptomics, network pharmacology, and metabolomics to explore the mechanisms underlying the efficacy of Xiangshao granules in treating perimenopausal anxiety—specifically through the modulation of dopamine metabolism to help alleviate anxiety symptoms. Given the limited application of omics technologies in traditional Chinese medicine research, the therapeutic mechanism of Xiangshao granules had not been previously elucidated, thereby addressing a key gap in the field. In addition, the incorporation of network pharmacology not only reinforces the reliability of our findings but also helps identify potential target genes for future therapeutic development. From a clinical perspective, this study employed standardized rating scales to assess anxiety symptoms, thereby avoiding reliance on subjective evaluations. Furthermore, although racial differences in menopausal symptoms are well recognized, current literature lacks sufficient research focusing on Asian women and on the use of traditional Chinese medicine to manage menopause-related emotional symptoms. Our study helps to bridge this gap and provides valuable insights into this underexplored area.

This study also has several limitations. First, the investigation focused exclusively on women who experienced natural menopause without the use of hormone therapy. Previous studies have indicated that women undergoing surgical menopause or receiving hormone replacement therapy may exhibit distinct psychological symptoms during the menopausal transition71,72. Therefore, the findings regarding the efficacy of Xiangshao granules may not be generalizable to these populations. Second, the assessment of anxiety symptoms was based solely on self-reported questionnaires rather than clinical diagnoses. Although self-reported symptoms are strongly associated with clinically defined anxiety and depression, they are not interchangeable. Caution is therefore warranted when extrapolating these findings to formally diagnosed psychiatric conditions. Third, although omics analyses revealed potential pathways, particularly involving dopamine metabolism, through which Xiangshao granules may exert anxiolytic effects, and although changes in our target genes were confirmed in the mouse hippocampus, these experiments were conducted with a relatively small sample size. Studies with larger animal cohorts and more extensive mechanistic experiments are needed to further validate these findings. Future studies are planned to further explore and confirm these findings. In addition, the omics subgroup was drawn from the overall cohort. Although baseline characteristics did not differ significantly from those not included, potential sampling error cannot be excluded, and the subgroup may not fully represent the overall population. Lastly, in this study, a small amount of the active extract was incorporated into the placebo to achieve similarity in odor. This reflects a well-recognized methodological difficulty in RCTs involving TCM, where the distinctive smell and taste of the preparation constrain the development of an entirely inert placebo73. Odor matching is particularly difficult because the characteristic aroma of TCM is closely linked to its chemical constituents and is often highly specific. Consequently, producing a placebo that has no pharmacological activity while remaining indistinguishable from the investigational product is challenging73. We fully acknowledge, however, that the placebo used in this trial may have exerted mild therapeutic effects. The absence of a completely inert placebo might have attenuated the observed between-group differences, potentially leading to an underestimation of the true treatment effect of Xiangshao granules. The development of more optimal placebo controls in the future will be crucial for better evaluating the genuine efficacy of TCM interventions.

Conclusions

In conclusion, Xiangshao granules were effective and well tolerated in reducing anxiety symptoms in menopausal women. Multi-omics analysis further suggested that their therapeutic effect may involve modulation of the dopaminergic synapse pathway. These findings contribute novel insights into the treatment of menopausal anxiety and offer a scientific basis for future clinical and mechanistic studies.
