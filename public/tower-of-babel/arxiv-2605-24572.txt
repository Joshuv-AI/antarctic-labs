Orbifold Bergman Kernels

Abstract
. Let ( 𝒳 , ω ) (\mathcal{X},\omega) be a compact n n -dimensional Kähler orbifold, the stabilizer groups of which are abelian and have rank at most two. Let ℰ \mathcal{E} be an orbi-ample vector bundle of rank 2 2 over 𝒳 \mathcal{X} and let H H be a Hermitian metric on ℰ \mathcal{E} such that the curvature form of det H \det H is − 2 ​ π ​ − 1 ​ ω -2\pi\sqrt{-1}\omega . 1.
Introduction

Let
(\mathcal{X},\omega)
be a compact
n
-dimensional Kähler orbifold with cyclic singularities and let
\mathcal{L}
be an orbifold line bundle over
\mathcal{X}
equipped with a Hermitian metric
h
such that the curvature form of
h
is
-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\omega
. The Hermitian metric
h
and the Kähler form
\omega
induce an
L^{2}
-inner product on the space of global sections
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k})
. The Bergman function is the smooth function

B_{k}(x)=\sum_{\alpha}\|s_{\alpha}(x)\|_{h^{k}(x)}^{2},

where
\{s_{\alpha}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k})
.

In the case when
(\mathcal{X},\omega)
is a compact Kähler manifold, the works of Tian
[
20
]
, Zelditch
[
23
]
, Catlin
[
4
]
, Ruan
[
17
]
, Berman-Berndtsson-Sjöstrand
[
2
]
, Dai-Liu-Ma
[
5
]
, and Liu-Lu
[
9
]
show that the Bergman function admits an asymptotic expansion as
k
tends to infinity. Moreover, the first nontrivial coefficient in this asymptotic expansion equals half the scalar curvature of
\omega
, making the Bergman function a central tool in the study of Kähler metrics of constant scalar curvature (
[
7
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
21
,
22
,
24
]
). If
(\mathcal{X},\omega)
is a Kähler orbifold, then the expansion of
B_{k}
over the orbifold locus is not smooth (e.g.
[
5
]
), but in
[
15
]
Ross-Thomas show that, on Kähler orbifolds with cyclic singularities, certain
weighted sums
of the Bergman functions associated to different powers of
\mathcal{L}
admit a global smooth expansion under the following mild condition.

Definition 1.1
.

(
[
8
]
,
[
15
]
)
Let
\mathcal{E}
be an orbifold vector bundle over
\mathcal{X}
. Then,
\mathcal{E}
is said to be
locally ample
if for all points
x\in X
the representation of the orbifold isotropy group
G_{x}
on
\mathcal{E}_{x}
is faithful. We say
\mathcal{E}
is
orbi-ample
if it is locally ample and the orbifold line bundle
\mathcal{L}=\text{det}(\mathcal{E})^{\text{ord}(X)}
descends to an ample line bundle on the underlying space of
\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
15
]
, Theorem 1.7)
Let
(\mathcal{X},\omega)
be a compact
n
-dimensional Kähler orbifold with cyclic singularities and let
\mathcal{L}
be an orbi-ample line bundle over
\mathcal{X}
. Let
h
be a Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{L}
such that the curvature form of
h
is
-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\omega
. Fix nonnegative integers
N,p\geq 0
and suppose that
\{c_{i}\}
are finitely many positive constants chosen so that

\frac{1}{\ord(\mathcal{X})}\sum_{i}i^{\ell}c_{i}=\sum_{i\equiv u\text{ \rm mod}\ord(\mathcal{X})}i^{\ell}c_{i}

for all
u
and
\ell=0,\dotsc,N+p
. Then, the function

B_{k}^{\orb}:=\sum_{i}c_{i}B_{k+i}

admits a global
C^{p}
-expansion of order
N
.
That is, there exist smooth functions
b_{0},\dotsc,b_{N}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that

B_{k}^{\orb}=b_{0}k^{n}+b_{1}k^{n-1}+\dotsb+b_{N}k^{n-N}+O(k^{n-N-1}),

where the
O(k^{n-N-1})
term is to be taken in the
C^{p}
-norm. Furthermore, the
b_{j}
are universal polynomials in the constants
c_{i}
and the derivatives of the Kähler metric
\omega
. In particular

b_{0}=\sum_{i}c_{i}\hskip 10.0pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.0ptb_{1}=\sum_{i}c_{i}\left(ni+\frac{1}{2}\Scal_{\omega}\right),

where
\Scal_{\omega}
is the scalar curvature of
\omega
.

For
k
sufficiently large,

\iota_{k}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}\left(\bigoplus_{i}H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k+i})^{*}\right)

is an embedding of
\mathcal{X}
into a weighted projective space and
\iota_{k}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\cong\mathcal{L}
. Analogous to the case when
\mathcal{X}
is a manifold, the asymptotic expansion of the orbifold Bergman functions has applications to the study of asymptotics of Fubini-Study metrics obtained by the embeddings
\iota_{k}
. For instance,
1.2
is used in
[
16
]
to show that if
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is a polarized orbifold with cyclic stabilizer groups and
\mathcal{X}
admits an orbifold Kähler metric in
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
with constant scalar curvature, then
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is
K
-semistable.

Now, let
(\mathcal{X},\omega)
be a Kähler orbifold with stabilizer groups that are abelian and have rank at most two. In general it is not possible for the stabilizer groups to act faithfully on the fibers of an orbifold line bundle. So, we instead consider Bergman functions associated to symmetric powers
\Sym^{i}\mathcal{E}
of an orbifold vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
twisted by powers of the determinant bundle
\det(\mathcal{E})
(this is the analytic analogy of the construction in
[
8
]
).

Now the Bergman functions are endomorphisms of
\Sym^{i}\mathcal{E}
, where the powers
i
may vary, and so they cannot directly be summed. To form a suitable weighted sum we first take a “trace” that is induced by the natural multiplication map that we describe now.

Let
V
be a complex vector space and

\mathfrak{a}_{i,k}:\End(V)\to\End(\Sym^{i}V)

denote the Lie algebra homomorphism associated to the multiplication map

\Sym^{i}\otimes\,\,{\det}^{\otimes k}:\GL(V)\to\GL(\Sym^{i}V\otimes\det(V)^{\otimes k})\cong\GL(\Sym^{i}V).

We define
\tau_{i,k}:\End(\Sym^{i}V)\to\End(V)
to be the dual to
\mathfrak{a}_{i,k}
under the natural pairing, so by definition

(1.3)

\tr(\tau_{i,k}(A)B)=\tr(A\mathfrak{a}_{i,k}(B)).

Now let
\mathcal{E}
be an orbi-ample vector bundle over
\mathcal{X}
of rank
2
. Suppose that
H
is a Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{E}
such that
-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\omega
is the curvature form of
h:=\det(H)
, and set
\mathcal{L}=\det(\mathcal{E})
. We will denote by

B_{i,k+j}\in\mathcal{A}^{0}(\End(\Sym^{i}\mathcal{E}))

the Bergman function for
(\Sym^{i}\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{k+j},\Sym^{i}H\otimes h^{k+j})
. Then,
\tau_{i,k+j}(B_{i,k+j})
is an element of
\mathcal{A}^{0}(\End(\mathcal{E}))
for any
i
,
j
, and
k
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
f
be an analytic function in a neighborhood
U
of
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
f(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}f_{r}(z)
denote the power series expansion of
f
at the point
w
where
f_{r}
is the
r
-th graded piece of the expansion. The function
f
is said to have
total order
r
at the point
w
if
f_{r}
is the lowest degree term that does not vanish identically.

Theorem 1.5
.

Fix nonnegative integers
N,p\geq 0
. Let
\zeta_{\ord(\mathcal{X})}
be a primitive
\ord(\mathcal{X})
-th root of unity. Let
\{c_{ij}\}_{i,j\geq 0}
be a set of nonnegative constants such that the polynomial

\sum_{i,j\geq 0}c_{ij}\left(\sum_{\mu\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2},|\mu|=i}{T_{1}}^{\mu_{1}}{T_{2}}^{\mu_{2}}\right){T_{3}}^{j}

has total order at least
5N+5p+5
at
(T_{1},T_{2},T_{3})=({\zeta_{\ord(\mathcal{X})}}^{m_{1}},{\zeta_{\ord(\mathcal{X})}}^{m_{2}},{\zeta_{\ord(\mathcal{X})}}^{m_{1}+m_{2}})
for all
m_{1}
,
m_{2}

\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
({\zeta_{\ord(\mathcal{X})}}^{m_{1}},{\zeta_{\ord(\mathcal{X})}}^{m_{2}})
does not equal
(1,1)
.

For all nonnegative integers
k\geq 0
, define
B_{k}^{\orb}\in\mathcal{A}^{0}(\End(\mathcal{E}))
by

(1.6)

B_{k}^{\orb}(x)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}c_{ij}\tau_{i,k+j}\left(B_{i,k+j}(x)\right).

Then
B_{k}^{\orb}
admits an asymptotic expansion of order
N
in the
C^{p}
-norm. That is, there exist smooth sections
b_{0}
,
\dotsc
,
b_{N}
in
\mathcal{A}^{0}(\End(\mathcal{E}))
such that

B_{k}^{\orb}=b_{0}k^{n+1}+\dotsb+b_{N}k^{n-N+1}+O(k^{n-N})

with respect to the
C^{p}
-norm. Moreover the
b_{i}
are universal quantities in terms of
\omega
and its derivatives; in particular

b_{0}(x)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}c_{ij}(i+1)\Id_{E}

and

b_{1}(x)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}c_{ij}\left(nj+\frac{(i+1)(2i+1)}{2}\Scal_{\omega}(x)\right)\Id_{E}.

There are many possible choices of
c_{ij}
that satisfy the hypothesis of the theorem. One of these is made by setting

c_{ij}=a_{i+j}a_{j}-a_{i+j+1}a_{j-1}

where the
a_{i}
are defined by

\sum_{i}a_{i}t^{i}=(1+t+\cdots t^{d})^{p}.

These
c_{ij}
are non-negative as the
a_{i}
are log concave.

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we will adapt the arguments in
[
15
]

of

for

. In
Section

2
, we recall some basic facts about orbifolds. In
Section

3
, we will define local reproducing kernels as in
[

]
and
[

]
, and show that the local reproducing kernels approximate the global Bergman kernels. In
Section

4
, we analyze the coefficients

of the Bergman functions

and show that they must take on a particular form. We will prove
1.5
in
Section

5

Appendix

B

that satisfy the hypotheses of

(1+z+\cdots+z^{d})^{p}(1+w+\cdots+w^{d})^{p}

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space. An
orbifold chart
of dimension
n\geq 0
on
X
is a triple
(U,G,\varphi)
where
U
is a connected open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
G
is a finite subgroup of diffeomorphisms of
U
, and
\varphi:U\to X
is an open map that induces a homeomorphism
U/G\to\varphi(U)
.

Let
(U,G,\varphi)
and
(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
be orbifold charts. An
embedding

\lambda:(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\to(U,G,\varphi)
between orbifold charts is a smooth embedding
\lambda:U^{\prime}\to U
such that
\varphi\circ\lambda=\varphi^{\prime}
.

We say that an orbifold chart
(U,G,\varphi)
is a
holomorphic chart
of complex dimension
n
if
U
is a subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
G
is contained in the group of biholomorphisms of
U
. Similarly, we say that an embedding
\lambda:(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\to(U,G,\varphi)
between orbifold charts is a
holomorphic embedding
between orbifold charts if
\lambda:U^{\prime}\to U
is holomorphic.

We recall some notation regarding group actions on a manifold. Let
M
be a manifold and denote the group of diffeomorphisms of
M
by
\Diff(M)
. Let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\Diff(M)
. The
isotropy group
of a subset
S\subseteq M
is

G_{S}=\{g\in G:g\cdot S=S\}.

If
S=\{x\}
only has one element, then we will write
G_{x}
for
G_{S}
. A subset
S\subseteq M
is called
G
-stable
if it is connected and, for any
g\in G
, either
g\cdot S=S
or
g\cdot S\cap S=\emptyset
. The following proposition lists basic properties of embeddings between orbifold charts. The proof of the proposition can be found in
[
13
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(1)

For any embedding
\lambda:(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\to(U,G,\varphi)
between orbifold charts on
X
, the image
\lambda(U^{\prime})
is a
G
-stable open subset of
U
, and there is a unique isomorphism
\overline{\lambda}:G^{\prime}\to G_{\lambda(U^{\prime})}
for which
\lambda(g^{\prime}\cdot\widetilde{x})=\overline{\lambda}(g^{\prime})\cdot\lambda(\widetilde{x})
.

(2)

The composition of embeddings between orbifold charts is an embedding between orbifold charts.

(3)

For any orbifold chart
(U,G,\varphi)
, any diffeomorphism
g\in G
is an embedding of
(U,G,\varphi)
into itself and
\overline{g}(h)=ghg^{-1}
.

(4)

If
\lambda,\mu:(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\to(U,G,\varphi)
are two embeddings between the same orbifold charts, there exists a unique
g\in G
with
\lambda=g\circ\mu
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that two (holomorphic) orbifold charts
(U,G,\varphi)
and
(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
of (complex) dimension
n
on
X
are
compatible
if, for any
x\in\varphi(U)\cap\psi(U^{\prime})
, there exist an (holomorphic) orbifold chart
(W,K,\theta)
on
X
with
x\in\theta(W)
and (holomorphic) embeddings between orbifold charts
\lambda:(W,K,\theta)\to(U,G,\varphi)
and
\mu:(W,K,\theta)\to(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
.

An
(complex) orbifold atlas
of dimension
n
on a topological space
X
is a collection
\mathcal{U}=\{(U_{i},G_{i},\varphi_{i})\}_{i\in I}
of pairwise compatible (holomorphic) orbifold charts of (complex) dimension
n
on
X
such that
\bigcup_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}(U_{i})=X
. Two (complex) orbifold atlases of
X
are equivalent if their union is an (complex) orbifold atlas.

An
(complex) orbifold
of dimension
n
is a pair
\mathcal{X}=(X,\mathcal{U})
, where
X
is a second-countable Hausdorff topological space and
\mathcal{U}
is a maximal (complex) orbifold atlas of (complex) dimension
n
on
X
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X,\mathcal{U})
be an orbifold and let
x\in X
. Choose an orbifold chart
(U,G,\varphi)\in\mathcal{U}
and
\widetilde{x}\in U
such that
\varphi(\widetilde{x})=x
. Then, the
isotropy group of
x
is
G_{x}:=G_{\widetilde{x}}
. By
2.2
, the isotropy group of
x
does not depend on our choice of
(U,G,\varphi)
and
\widetilde{x}\in U
.

Definition 2.5
.

A smooth real
orbifold vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
r
over an orbifold
\mathcal{X}=(X,\mathcal{U})
consists of a smooth real vector bundle
E_{U}
of rank
r
over
U
for each orbifold chart
(U,G,\varphi)\in\mathcal{U}
together with an anti-homomorphism
\rho_{U}
from
G
to the group of equivalences of
E_{U}
onto itself satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

If
\xi
lies in the fiber
{E_{U}}_{\mid x}
over
x\in U
, then, for each
g\in G
,
\rho_{U}(g)(\xi)
lies in the fiber over
g^{-1}\cdot x
.

(2)

If
\lambda:(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\to(U,G,\varphi)
is an embedding between orbifold charts on
X
, then there is a bundle map
\lambda^{*}:{E_{U}}_{\mid\lambda(U^{\prime})}\to E_{U^{\prime}}
such that, for any
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
,

\rho_{U^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})\circ\lambda^{*}=\lambda^{*}\circ\rho_{U}\left(\overline{\lambda}(g^{\prime})\right).

(3)

If
\lambda_{ji}:(U_{i},G_{i},\varphi_{i})\to(U_{j},G_{j},\varphi_{j})
and
\lambda_{kj}:(U_{j},G_{j},\varphi_{j})\to(U_{k},G_{k},\varphi_{k})
are embeddings between orbifold charts on
X
, then
(\lambda_{kj}\circ{\lambda_{ji}})^{*}=\lambda_{ji}^{*}\circ\lambda_{kj}^{*}
.

Smooth complex orbifold vector bundles are defined in an analogous way. We say that a smooth complex orbifold vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over a complex orbifold
\mathcal{X}=(X,\mathcal{U})
is

(U,G,\varphi)\in\mathcal{U}
,

is a holomorphic vector bundle and, for any embedding
\lambda:(U^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\to(U,G,\varphi)
,
\lambda^{*}:{E_{U}}_{\mid\lambda(U^{\prime})}\to E_{U^{\prime}}

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an orbifold vector bundle over an orbifold
\mathcal{X}=(X,\mathcal{U})
. Then, a

\sigma

\mathcal{E}

\sigma_{U}

Lemma B.2
.

Let

denote the Schur polynomial associated to the partition

S_{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}(z,w)=z^{\lambda_{1}}w^{\lambda_{2}}+\dotsb+z^{\lambda_{2}}w^{\lambda_{1}}.

We write

By the Jacobi-Trudy identity,

So, we see that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{z^{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}}S_{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}(z,w)\\

&=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda_{2}}T^{i}+\dotsb+T^{\lambda_{1}+i}\right)-\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\lambda_{2}-1}T^{j}+\dotsb+T^{\lambda_{1}+1+j}\right)\\
&=T^{\lambda_{2}}+\dotsb+T^{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}-\sum_{j=0}^{\lambda_{2}-1}T^{\lambda_{1}+1+j}\\

Proposition B.5

are defined by

\sum_{i}a_{i}t^{i}=(1+t+\cdots t^{d})^{p}.

Remark B.6
.

The
c_{ij}
are non-negative since the
a_{i}
are log-concave.

Proof.

Since
f(T)=1+T+\dotsb+T^{d}
is log-concave with no internal zeros,
B.4
implies that

(1+z+\dotsb+z^{d})(1+w+\dotsb+w^{d})

is Schur positive. As products of Schur positive polynomials are Schur positive,
q(z,w)
is Schur positive.

Next, we compute the coefficients of
q(z,w)
. We observe that

\begin{split}(1+z+\dotsb+z^{d})^{p}&=\frac{(1-z^{d+1})^{p}}{(1-z)^{p}}\\
&=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{k}\binom{p}{k}z^{(d+1)k}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+p-1}{p-1}z^{k}\right)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{dp}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor k/(d+1)\rfloor}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{p}{\ell}\binom{k+p-(d+1)\ell-1}{p-1}\right)z^{k}.\end{split}

Set

a_{k}=\begin{cases}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor k/(d+1)\rfloor}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{p}{\ell}\binom{k+p-(d+1)\ell-1}{p-1}&\text{if $0\leq k\leq dp$ and}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then,

\begin{split}(1+z+\dotsb+z^{d})^{p}(1+w+\dotsb+w^{d})^{p}&=\left(\sum_{k}a_{k}z^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{k}a_{k}y^{k}\right)\\
&=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j=r}a_{i}a_{j}z^{i}y^{j}.\end{split}

For any
r\geq 0
, we can use
B.2
to write

\sum_{i+j=r}a_{i}a_{j}z^{i}y^{j}=\sum_{i=\lceil r/2\rceil}^{r}(a_{i}a_{r-i}-a_{i+1}a_{r-i-1})S_{(i,r-i)}.

As a result,

\begin{split}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j=r}a_{i}a_{j}z^{i}y^{j}&=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=\lceil r/2\rceil}^{r}(a_{i}a_{r-i}-a_{i+1}a_{r-i-1})S_{(i,r-i)}\\
&=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}(a_{i+j}a_{j}-a_{i+j+1}a_{j-1})S_{(i+j,j)}.\end{split}

Substituting
a_{k}=0
for all
k<0
and
k>dp
, we see that

\begin{split}q(z,w)=&\sum_{i=0}^{dp-1}a_{i}a_{0}S_{(i,0)}\\
&+\sum_{i+j=dp}a_{dp}a_{j}S_{(dp,j)}\\
&+\sum_{i+j<dp,j>0}(a_{i+j}a_{j}-a_{i+j+1}a_{j-1})S_{(i+j,j)}.\end{split}

∎
