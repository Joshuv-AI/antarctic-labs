On planar functions over 𝔽 q 3 \mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
João Paulo Guardieiro, Adler Marques, Luciane Quoos, Guilherme Tizziotti

Abstract
. Let 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} denote the finite field of order q q . For q q odd, we investigate the planarity over 𝔽 q 3 \mathbb{F}_{q^{3}} of the family f E , A , B , C , D ​ ( X ) := E ​ X 2 + A ​ X q + 1 + B ​ X q 2 + 1 + C ​ X 2 ​ q + D ​ X 2 ​ q 2 ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] . f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X):=EX^{2}+AX^{q+1}+BX^{q^{2}+1}+CX^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]. Using results from the theory of q-polynomials, we establish conditions under which these polynomials are planar functions. In particular, we provide characterizations for the planarity property and present new families of planar trinomials, quadrinomials, and pentanomials.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
be the multiplicative group of non-zero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is finite, every function
f
from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to itself corresponds to a unique polynomial
f(X)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of degree at most
q-1
. A polynomial
f(X)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is called a
permutation polynomial
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if the associated polynomial function
f:c\mapsto f(c)
from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a bijection. Permutation polynomials over finite fields constitute a classical area of research, principally motivated by their diverse applications in cryptography and coding theory.

In
[
9
]
, Dembowski and Ostrom introduced the notion of planar functions in the following way. Let
q
be odd, a function
f:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is called
a planar function
if for each
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\ast}
, the polynomial defined by

F_{\epsilon}(X)=f(X+\epsilon)-f(X)

is a permutation polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. That is, the equations
f(X+\epsilon)-f(X)=\beta
have exactly one solution for all
\epsilon,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, with
\epsilon\neq 0
. Note that, if
q
is even, then
F_{\epsilon}(0)=F_{\epsilon}(\epsilon)
for every
\epsilon\neq 0
. Therefore, functions satisfying the previous condition exist only for
q
odd. In the same paper, Dembowski and Ostrom also observed that such functions have close connections with projective planes. Zhou
[
21
]
defined a natural analogue of planar functions for even characteristic in the following way: if
q
is even, a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is planar on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if

f(X+\epsilon)+f(X)+\epsilon X

is a permutation polynomial on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for each
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
. As shown by Zhou
[
21
]
and Schmidt and Zhou
[
18
]
, such planar functions have similar properties and applications as their odd characteristic counterparts.

Planar functions in odd characteristic achieve optimal differential properties, making them useful for constructing cryptographic protocols, particularly for designing S-boxes in block ciphers, see
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, for error-correcting codes see
[
10
]
and
[
20
]
, and applications in signal sets
[
11
]
. In
[
22
]
, Zieve studied finite projective planes involving planar functions. In particular, he determined all planar functions on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of the form
a\mapsto a^{t}
, under the assumption that
q\geq(t-1)^{4}
. As a consequence, the results presented by Zieve solved two conjectures of Hernando, McGuire and Monteserrat
[
12
]
and also yield a new proof of a conjecture of Segre and Bartocci
[
19
]
about monomial hyperovals in finite Desarguesian projective planes. The existence and non-existence of planar mappings have been extensively studied in the literature, using various methods, see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
15
]
. Constructing planar polynomials, or characterizing the conditions under which they arise, is a problem in finite field theory that has drawn the attention of many researchers in recent years. Bartoli and Timpanella
[
2
]
investigated planar polynomials of the type

f_{A,B}(X)=AX^{2^{2m}+1}+BX^{2^{m}+1},\text{ where }A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{3m}}^{\ast}.

In
[
1
]
, Bartoli and Bonini construct planar polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
of the type

f(X)=X(X^{q^{2}}+AX^{q}+X),\text{ where }A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

In
[
7
]
, Chen and Mesnager, presented a class of planar functions of the form

Tr(aX^{q+1})+\ell{X^{2}}

on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, in particular, they construct several typical kinds of planar functions on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, characterize them on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
, and proved that such planar functions do not exist given certain conditions when the degree of the extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is higher. Chan and Xiong
[
6
]
characterized planar quadrinomials of the form

f_{\underline{c}}(X)=c_{0}X^{qQ+q}+c_{1}X^{qQ+1}+c_{2}X^{Q+q}+c_{3}X^{Q+1}

over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, where
Q
is a power of the characteristic of the field, for any
\underline{c}=(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{4}
in terms of linear equivalence. For a survey with results on planar functions see e.g.
[
17
]
.

For
q
odd, in this work we investigate the planarity over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
of the following family of pentanomials

f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X):=EX^{2}+AX^{q+1}+BX^{q^{2}+1}+CX^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X].

In particular, we obtain the following new families of planar functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
for
q
odd:

(1)

EX^{2}+CX^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}
, with
C,D,E\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying
C^{3}+D^{3}+E^{3}-3CDE=1/2
. (see Theorem
3.1
).

(2)

-X^{2}+2X^{q^{2}+1}+X^{2q}-X^{2q^{2}}
(see Theorem
3.2
).

(3)

EX^{2}+2(E-D)X^{q+1}+2DX^{q^{2}+1}+(E-D)X^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}
, with
D,E\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying
E(3D^{2}-3DE+E^{2})\neq 0
(see Theorem
3.3
).

(4)

X^{2}-X^{q+1}-X^{q^{2}+1}+X^{2q}+X^{2q^{2}}
(see Theorem
3.4
).

(5)

X^{2}+X^{q+1}+X^{q^{2}+1}+X^{2q}+X^{2q^{2}}
(see Theorem
3.4
).

We organize this work in the following way. Section 1 is devoted to presenting some notations and results related to the concept of the
q
-polynomial over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In Section 2, we present the main results of the paper, which establish conditions for the planarity of pentanomials as described above. Finally, Section 3 provides illustrative examples of planar polynomials.

1.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some results involving the concept of
q
-polynomials, which will be used throughout this paper.

A polynomial of the form
\displaystyle L(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}X^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]
is called a
q
-polynomial (or a
q
-linearized polynomial) over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. A
q
-polynomial
L(X)
induces a linear transformation
L:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, defined by
a\mapsto L(a)
, where
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is considered as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space. The following theorem provides a simple, yet powerful, criterion for a

q
-polynomial to induce a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
16
, Th. 7.9]

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space. Then the
q
-polynomial

\displaystyle L(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}X^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]

is a permutation polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
if and only if
L(X)
only has the
0
root in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

This criterion based on the roots of the
q
-polynomial
\displaystyle L(X)
is not always the most convenient tool for explicit computations. An equivalent condition can be given in terms of the Dickson matrix associated with the polynomial. This matrix, known as the
q
-circulant matrix, represents the action of the polynomial on a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The following lemma translates the permutation property of
\displaystyle L(X)
into a nonvanishing determinant condition on the Dickson matrix, a result that will be particularly useful in the next section.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
16
, p. 362]

Let
q
be a prime power,
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
an extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and consider

L(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}x^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[x].

Let
D_{L}
be the associated Dickson matrix, defined as

D_{L}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}&\cdots&c_{n-1}\\
c_{n-1}^{q}&c_{0}^{q}&c_{1}^{q}&\cdots&c_{n-2}^{q}\\
c_{n-2}^{q^{2}}&c_{n-1}^{q^{2}}&c_{0}^{q^{2}}&\cdots&c_{n-3}^{q^{2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
c_{1}^{q^{n-1}}&c_{2}^{q^{n-1}}&c_{3}^{q^{n-1}}&\cdots&c_{0}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\end{array}\right).

Then
L(x)
is a permutation polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
if and only if

\det(D_{L})\neq 0
.

Throughout this work, to simplify the notation, for a polynomial
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
we define for each
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
the polynomial

F_{\epsilon}(X):=f(X+\epsilon)-f(X)-f(\epsilon).

In the case the polynomial
F_{\epsilon}(X)
is a
q
-polynomial, the determinant of the Dickson matrix associated with
F_{\epsilon}(X)
will be denoted by
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}
.

2.
Planar functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}

In this section, we investigate the planarity of the following family over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}[X]
. Let
q
be a power of an odd prime, and
consider

(1)

f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X)=EX^{2}+AX^{q+1}+BX^{q^{2}+1}+CX^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X].

A characterization of when such a pentanomial is planar over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
is established in the next result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
A,B,C,D,E\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, not all zero. The polynomial
f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}[X]
given in (
1
) is planar if and only if

\displaystyle(-2A^{2}D+2ABE-2B^{2}C)(\epsilon^{3}+\epsilon^{3q}+\epsilon^{3q^{2}})+

\displaystyle(2A^{2}C-2ABD-4ACD+4AE^{2}+2B^{2}E-4BCE+4BD^{2})(\epsilon^{2+q}+\epsilon^{1+2q^{2}}+\epsilon^{2q+q^{2}})+

\displaystyle(2A^{2}E-2ABC+4AC^{2}-4ADE+2B^{2}D-4BCD+4BE^{2})(\epsilon^{2+q^{2}}+\epsilon^{1+2q}+\epsilon^{q+2q^{2}})+

\displaystyle(2A^{3}+2B^{3}+8C^{3}-24CDE+8D^{3}+8E^{3})\epsilon^{1+q+q^{2}}\neq 0

for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
.

Proof.

By the definition of planar function, we have that the polynomial
f_{E,A,B,C,D}
is planar if and only if, for all

\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
, the associated polynomial

F_{\epsilon}(X):=f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X+\epsilon)-f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X)-f_{E,A,B,C,D}(\epsilon)

is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
. We have

\displaystyle F_{\epsilon}(X)

\displaystyle=E(X+\epsilon)^{2}+A(X+\epsilon)^{q+1}+B(X+\epsilon)^{q^{2}+1}+C(X+\epsilon)^{2q}+D(X+\epsilon)^{2q^{2}}

\displaystyle-(EX^{2}+AX^{q+1}+BX^{q^{2}+1}+CX^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}})

\displaystyle-(E\epsilon^{2}+A\epsilon^{q+1}+B\epsilon^{q^{2}+1}+C\epsilon^{2q}+D\epsilon^{2q^{2}})

\displaystyle=(2E\epsilon+A\epsilon^{q}+B\epsilon^{q^{2}})X+(A\epsilon+2C\epsilon^{q})X^{q}+(B\epsilon+2D\epsilon^{q^{2}})X^{q^{2}}.

Since
F_{\epsilon}(X)
is a
q
-polynomial, by Lemma
1.2
,
F_{\epsilon}(X)
is a permutation polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
if and only if its associated Dickson matrix is nonsingular for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. Now, the determinant
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}
of the Dickson matrix of
F_{\epsilon}(X)
is given by

\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}=\det\begin{pmatrix}2E\epsilon+A\epsilon^{q}+B\epsilon^{q^{2}}&A\epsilon+2C\epsilon^{q}&B\epsilon+2D\epsilon^{q^{2}}\\
B\epsilon^{q}+2D\epsilon&2E\epsilon^{q}+A\epsilon^{q^{2}}+B\epsilon&A\epsilon^{q}+2C\epsilon^{q^{2}}\\
A\epsilon^{q^{2}}+2C\epsilon&B\epsilon^{q^{2}}+2D\epsilon^{q}&2E\epsilon^{q^{2}}+A\epsilon+B\epsilon^{q}\\
\end{pmatrix},

which simplifies exactly to the expression in the statement. So, the result follows from Lemma
1.2
.
∎

This characterization reduces the planarity condition to a polynomial not having zeros in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
. Although explicit, this condition is somewhat complex and not easily verified directly. In the following, we present some results to enable explicit constructions of planar pentanomials.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q
be an odd prime power. For
a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the polynomial

L(X)=aX^{q^{2}}+bX^{q}+cX

has no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
if and only if
a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}-3abc\neq 0
.

Proof.

Since
L(X)
is a
q
-polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
, by Lemma
1.2
, it is a permutation polynomial if and only if the determinant of its respective associated Dickson matrix is non-zero.
The associated Dickson matrix of
L(X)
is

A_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&c\\
c&a&b\\
b&c&a\end{pmatrix},

which has determinant
\det(A_{L})=a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}-3abc
.
Since
L(0)=0
and
L
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
, we conclude that it has no root in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
.
∎

In the next results
we examine two families of
3\times 3
matrices, where the entries are functions of an element
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. For each of these families of matrices, we are going to show that the determinant is never zero, regardless of the value chosen for
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. These technical results will play a key role in verifying the nonvanishing condition that appears in the planarity characterization given in Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
q
be odd. For
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
consider the matrix

A_{\epsilon}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}}&(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}&(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q^{2}}\\
\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}&(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}&(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}\\
\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}}&(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q}&(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}\end{array}\right).

Then,
\det(A_{\epsilon})\neq 0
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

The determinant of the matrix
A_{\epsilon}
is given by

\displaystyle\det(A_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}+q+1}+(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q^{2}+q+1}+(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}+q+1}

\displaystyle-(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q^{2}}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})

\displaystyle-(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}

\displaystyle-(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}.

To obtain an expression for
\det(A_{\epsilon})
, we do the calculations separately, using the facts that
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
and that
q
is odd.
Define

P:=(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}+q+1}+(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q^{2}+q+1}+(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}+q+1},

and

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q^{2}}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})+(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}

\displaystyle+(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}.

Then
\det(A_{\epsilon})=P-N
.

At first we compute
P
,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}+\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})(2\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}}+\epsilon)(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})+(\epsilon^{q^{2}}+\epsilon)(\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})

\displaystyle+(\epsilon^{q^{2}}+\epsilon^{q})(\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon)(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})

\displaystyle=2\epsilon^{3q^{2}}+9\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}+9\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}+9\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}+20\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}+9\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}+2\epsilon^{3q}+9\epsilon^{2q+1}

\displaystyle+9\epsilon^{q+2}+2\epsilon^{3}.

Now, we compute
N
,

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q^{2}}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})

\displaystyle+(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})^{q}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}

\displaystyle+(2\epsilon+\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q})(\epsilon+\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{3q^{2}}+2\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}+3\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}+4\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}+3\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}+2\epsilon^{q+2}+\epsilon^{3}

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{3q^{2}}+3\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}+2\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}+3\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}+4\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}+\epsilon^{3q}+2\epsilon^{2q+1}

\displaystyle+2\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}+4\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}+2\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}+\epsilon^{3q}+3\epsilon^{2q+1}+3\epsilon^{q+2}+\epsilon^{3}

\displaystyle=2(\epsilon^{3q^{2}}+\epsilon^{3q}+\epsilon^{3})+5(\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}+\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}+\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}+\epsilon^{2q+1}+\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}+\epsilon^{q+2})+12\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}.

Then, we have that

\det(A_{\epsilon})=4\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}+4\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}+4\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}+8\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}+4\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}+4\epsilon^{2q+1}+4\epsilon^{q+2}.

So, we get

\det(A_{\epsilon})=4(\epsilon^{q^{2}}+\epsilon^{q})(\epsilon^{q^{2}}+\epsilon)(\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon).

By Lemma
2.2
, for
p(X)=X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}[X]
, we have
\epsilon^{q^{2}}+\epsilon^{q}\neq 0
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
, thus we also have
\epsilon^{q^{2}}+\epsilon\neq 0
and
\epsilon^{q}+\epsilon\neq 0
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
. Hence,
\det(A_{\epsilon})\neq 0
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
q
be a power of an odd prime. For
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
consider the matrix

B_{\epsilon}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}}&(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)^{q}&(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}}\\
2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon&(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}&(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}}\\
2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon&(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)^{q}&(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}\end{array}\right).

Then,
\det(B_{\epsilon})\neq 0
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

The determinant of the matrix
B_{\epsilon}
is given by

\begin{array}[]{rl}\det(B_{\epsilon})&=(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}+q+1}+(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}+q+1}+(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}+q+1}\\
&-(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}}(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)\\
&-(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)^{q}(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}}\\
&-(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)^{q}.\\
\end{array}

To obtain an expression for
\det(B_{\epsilon})
, we do the calculations separately, using the facts that
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
and that
q
is odd.

\bullet
The first line of the determinant reduces to:

\begin{array}[]{l}(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}+q+1}+(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}+q+1}+(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}+q+1}\\
\mbox{ }=(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon-\epsilon^{q})(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})\\
\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\mbox{ }+(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q^{2}})(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon^{q})(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)+(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q})(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)\\
\mbox{ }=2\epsilon^{3q^{2}}-5\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}-5\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}-5\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}+26\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}-5\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}+2\epsilon^{3q}\\
\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\mbox{ }\mbox{ }-5\epsilon^{2q+1}-5\epsilon^{q+2}+2\epsilon^{3}.\end{array}

\bullet
Now we simplify, one by one, the second, third and fourth line of the determinant:

\diamond

(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q}(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}}(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)=2\epsilon^{3q^{2}}-4\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}-3\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}+10\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}-3\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}-4\epsilon^{q+2}+2\epsilon^{3}
.

\diamond

(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)^{q}(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)^{q^{2}}=2\epsilon^{3q^{2}}-3\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}-3\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}-4\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}+10\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}+2\epsilon^{3q}-4\epsilon^{2q+1}
.

\diamond

(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon^{q^{2}})^{q^{2}}(2\epsilon^{q}-\epsilon)(2\epsilon^{q^{2}}-\epsilon)^{q}=-4\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}+10\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}-4\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}+2\epsilon^{3q}-3\epsilon^{2q+1}-3\epsilon^{q+2}+2\epsilon^{3}
.

And we get that the sum of the three last lines is given by:

-(4\epsilon^{3q^{2}}-7\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}-7\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}-7\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}+30\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}-7\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}+4\epsilon^{3q}-7\epsilon^{2q+1}-7\epsilon^{q+2}+4\epsilon^{3}).

Finally, we conclude that

\det(B_{\epsilon})=-2\epsilon^{3q^{2}}+2\epsilon^{2q^{2}+q}+2\epsilon^{2q^{2}+1}+2\epsilon^{q^{2}+2q}-4\epsilon^{q^{2}+q+1}+2\epsilon^{q^{2}+2}-2\epsilon^{3q}+2\epsilon^{2q+1}+2\epsilon^{q+2}-2\epsilon^{3}.

Then, for

\begin{array}[]{rl}p(X):=&-2X^{3q^{2}}+2X^{2q^{2}+q}+2X^{2q^{2}+1}+2X^{q^{2}+2q}-4X^{q^{2}+q+1}+2X^{q^{2}+2}-2X^{3q}\\
&+2X^{2q+1}+2X^{q+2}-2X^{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}[X].\end{array}

we have
\det(B_{\epsilon})=p(\epsilon)
. So,
\det(B_{\epsilon})\neq 0
for every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
if and only if
p(X)
has no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
.

Note that,
p(X)
factors exactly as

p(X)=2(X^{q^{2}}-X^{q}+X)(X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}-X)(-X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}+X).

By Lemma
2.2
, the polynomial
L(X)=X^{q^{2}}-X^{q}+X
has no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
.
The other two factors of
p(X)
in the product are simply the
q
-th and
q^{2}
-th powers of
L(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
, so they also have no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. We conclude that
p(X)
has no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
.

∎

According to Proposition
2.1
, the planarity of
f_{E,A,B,C,D}
reduces to the condition that the determinant
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}
is nonzero for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. A direct treatment of this determinant in terms of arbitrary coefficients is, however, algebraically non-trivial. However, motivated by the organized factorizations observed in Propositions
2.3
and
2.4
, we can seek specific algebraic conditions on the coefficients
A,B,C,D
, and
E
that forces
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}
to be factored into a product of simpler
q
-polynomials. If we can guarantee that none of these factors have roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
, then the determinant
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}
will remain nonzero, automatically satisfying the condition of planarity.

The following theorem establishes a general framework for this approach. It provides a specific system of equations over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which, when satisfied, guarantees that the determinant
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}
factors exactly into three linear terms with no non-trivial roots.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let

P_{i}(X)=a_{i}X^{q^{2}}+b_{i}X^{q}+c_{i}X,\text{ for }i=1,2,3

be polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
with no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. Suppose that

(1)

a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}=c_{1}c_{2}c_{3},

(2)

a_{1}a_{2}b_{3}+a_{1}b_{2}a_{3}+b_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=b_{1}b_{2}c_{3}+b_{1}c_{2}b_{3}+c_{1}b_{2}b_{3}=a_{1}c_{2}c_{3}+c_{1}c_{2}a_{3}+c_{1}a_{2}c_{3},
and

(3)

a_{1}a_{2}c_{3}+a_{1}c_{2}a_{3}+c_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=b_{1}c_{2}c_{3}+c_{1}c_{2}b_{3}+c_{1}b_{2}c_{3}=a_{1}b_{2}b_{3}+b_{1}a_{2}b_{3}+b_{1}b_{2}a_{3}.

Define

\alpha=a_{1}a_{2}a_{3},\quad\beta=a_{1}a_{2}b_{3}+a_{1}b_{2}a_{3}+b_{1}a_{2}a_{3},\quad\gamma=a_{1}a_{2}c_{3}+a_{1}c_{2}a_{3}+c_{1}a_{2}a_{3}

and

\delta=a_{1}b_{2}c_{3}+a_{1}c_{2}b_{3}+b_{1}a_{2}c_{3}+b_{1}c_{2}a_{3}+c_{1}a_{2}b_{3}+c_{1}b_{2}a_{3}.

If the system of equations

(2)

\begin{cases}-A^{2}D+ABE-B^{2}C=2\alpha\\
A^{2}C-ABD-2ACD+2AE^{2}+B^{2}E-2BCE+2BD^{2}=2\beta\\
A^{2}E-ABC+2AC^{2}-2ADE+B^{2}D-2BCD+2BE^{2}=2\gamma\\
A^{3}+B^{3}+4C^{3}-12CDE+4D^{3}+4E^{3}=2\delta\end{cases}

has a solution
(E,A,B,C,D)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{5}
, then the polynomial

f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X)=EX^{2}+AX^{q+1}+BX^{q^{2}+1}+CX^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}

is planar.

Proof.

By the system of equations, we can rewrite the determinant
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}
given in Proposition
2.1
as

(3)

\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}=\alpha(\epsilon^{3}+\epsilon^{3q}+\epsilon^{3q^{2}})+\beta(\epsilon^{2+q}+\epsilon^{1+2q^{2}}+\epsilon^{2q+q^{2}})+\gamma(\epsilon^{2+q^{2}}+\epsilon^{1+2q}+\epsilon^{q+2q^{2}})+\delta\epsilon^{1+q+q^{2}}.

By the definitions of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
and
\delta
, this expression expands and factors exactly as the product of the three
q
-polynomials evaluated at
\epsilon

P_{1}(\epsilon)P_{2}(\epsilon)P_{3}(\epsilon)=(a_{1}\epsilon^{q^{2}}+b_{1}\epsilon^{q}+c_{1}\epsilon)(a_{2}\epsilon^{q^{2}}+b_{2}\epsilon^{q}+c_{2}\epsilon)(a_{3}\epsilon^{q^{2}}+b_{3}\epsilon^{q}+c_{3}\epsilon).

Since by hypothesis none of these
q
-polynomials have roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
, it follows that
P_{i}(\epsilon)\neq 0
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
and
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. Therefore,
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}\neq 0
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
, and by Proposition
2.1
, it follows that
f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X)
is planar.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
aX^{q^{2}}+bX^{q}+cX\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial with no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. Define

\alpha=abc,\quad\beta=a^{2}b+ac^{2}+b^{2}c,\quad\gamma=a^{2}c+ab^{2}+bc^{2},\quad\delta=a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}+3abc.

If the system of equations

\begin{cases}-A^{2}D+ABE-B^{2}C=2\alpha\\
A^{2}C-ABD-2ACD+2AE^{2}+B^{2}E-2BCE+2BD^{2}=2\beta\\
A^{2}E-ABC+2AC^{2}-2ADE+B^{2}D-2BCD+2BE^{2}=2\gamma\\
A^{3}+B^{3}+4C^{3}-12CDE+4D^{3}+4E^{3}=2\delta\end{cases}

has a solution
(A,B,C,D,E)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{5}
, then the polynomial

f_{E,A,B,C,D}(X)=EX^{2}+AX^{q+1}+BX^{q^{2}+1}+CX^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}

is planar.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the previous theorem, by considering the polynomials

\displaystyle P_{1}(X)=aX^{q^{2}}+bX^{q}+cX,

\displaystyle P_{2}(X)=bX^{q^{2}}+cX^{q}+aX,

\displaystyle P_{3}(X)=cX^{q^{2}}+aX^{q}+bX,

and noticing that for
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
we have
P_{2}(\epsilon)=P_{1}(\epsilon)^{q}
and
P_{3}(\epsilon)=P_{1}(\epsilon)^{q^{2}}
.
∎

3.
Explicit Families of Planar Polynomials

In this section, we apply the results given in the previous section to construct explicit families of planar polynomials. By choosing suitable
q
-polynomials that have no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
, we obtain the parameters
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
introduced in Theorem
2.5
. Solving the resulting system of equations yields several distinct families of planar functions, which are presented below.

In the next theorem we provide a family of planar trinomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
. This theorem comes from the most simple choice of
q
-polynomials having no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
, namely
X^{q^{2}}
,
X^{q}
and
X
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
q
be odd and consider the polynomial

f(X)=EX^{2}+CX^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X].

If
C^{3}+D^{3}+E^{3}-3CDE=2^{-1}
, then
f(X)
is planar over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
.

Proof.

We notice that for
A=B=0
we have
f_{E,0,0,C,D}=f(X)
.
We apply Theorem
2.5
choosing the tuples

(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})=(1,0,0),\,(a_{2},b_{2},c_{2})=(0,1,0),\text{ and }(a_{3},b_{3},c_{3})=(0,0,1).

From Lemma
2.2
the corresponding polynomials
a_{i}X^{q^{2}}+b_{i}X^{q}+c_{i}X
, for
i=1,2,3
, have no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. This also yields
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=0
and
\delta=1
in Theorem
2.5
.

Substituting these conditions into the system (
2
), the problem reduces to verifying whether the coefficients of
f(X)
satisfy the following equation

(4)

4(C^{3}+D^{3}+E^{3}-3CDE)=2,

Since
q
is an odd prime power, the equation (
4
) reduces to

C^{3}+D^{3}+E^{3}-3CDE=2^{-1}.

Thus, under these conditions,
(0,0,C,D,E)
is a valid solution to the system, and by Theorem
2.5
,
f(X)
is planar over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
.
∎

Now we present explicit examples of planar quadrinomials and pentanomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
q
be an odd prime power. Then, the quadrinomial

f(X)=-X^{2}+2X^{q^{2}+1}+X^{2q}-X^{2q^{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]

is planar over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
2.5
. Consider the tuples

(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})=(1,-1,1),\quad(a_{2},b_{2},c_{2})=(1,1,-1),\quad(a_{3},b_{3},c_{3})=(-1,1,1).

For each
i
, we have

a_{i}^{3}+b_{i}^{3}+c_{i}^{3}-3a_{i}b_{i}c_{i}=4\neq 0,

hence, by Lemma
2.2
, the corresponding polynomials
a_{i}X^{q^{2}}+b_{i}X^{q}+c_{i}X
for
i=1,2,3
have no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. A direct computation gives

\alpha=-1,\quad\beta=1,\quad\gamma=1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\delta=-2.

in Theorem
2.5
. Choosing
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
satisfying
B^{3}=4
, and the 5-tuple given by

(A,B,C,D,E)=\left(0,\;B,\;\frac{2}{B^{2}},\;-\frac{B}{2},\;-\frac{B}{2}\right).

it can be verified that it satisfies the system (
2
). In fact, since
B^{3}=4
,
substituting into the system (
2
), we obtain

\begin{cases}&-B^{2}C=-2=2\alpha,\\
&B^{2}E-2BCE+2BD^{2}=2=2\beta,\\
&B^{2}D-2BCD+2BE^{2}=2=2\gamma,\\
&B^{3}+4C^{3}-12CDE+4D^{3}+4E^{3}=-4=2\delta.\end{cases}

Therefore, by Theorem
2.5
, the polynomial

f_{-\frac{B}{2},\;0,\;B,\;\frac{2}{B^{2}},\;-\frac{B}{2}}(X)=-\frac{B}{2}X^{2}+BX^{q^{2}+1}+\frac{2}{B^{2}}X^{2q}-\frac{B}{2}X^{2q^{2}}

is planar over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
. So, also
f(X)=B^{2}/2f_{-\frac{B}{2},\;0,\;B,\;\frac{2}{B^{2}},\;-\frac{B}{2}}(X)
is planar.
∎

We now present a family of planar pentanomials with two parameters.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
q
be an odd prime power, and let
D,E
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Define
\omega=3D^{2}-3DE+E^{2}
and consider the polynomial

f(X)=EX^{2}+2(E-D)X^{q+1}+2DX^{q^{2}+1}+(E-D)X^{2q}+DX^{2q^{2}}.

Then,
f(X)
is planar over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
if and only if
E\omega\neq 0
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.2
, the polynomials

4X^{q^{2}}+4X^{q},\quad EX^{q^{2}}+EX,\quad\omega X^{q}+\omega X

have no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{*}
. These polynomials satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem
2.5
, and we obtain

\alpha=0,\quad\beta=\gamma=4E\omega,\quad\delta=8E\omega.

A straightforward analysis shows that the coefficients of
f_{E,2(E-D),2D,E-D,D}(X)
satisfy the corresponding system of equations, and so
f_{E,2(E-D),2D,E-D,D}(X)
is planar over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
.

∎

In the last theorem we provide two new pentanomials planar functions.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
q
be a power of an odd prime. Then the polynomials

(1)

f_{1,-1,-1,1,1}(X)=X^{2}-X^{q+1}-X^{q^{2}+1}+X^{2q}+X^{2q^{2}}
and

(2)

f_{1,1,1,1/2,1/2}(X)=X^{2}+X^{q+1}+X^{q^{2}+1}+\frac{1}{2}X^{2q}+\frac{1}{2}X^{2q^{2}}

are planar over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
2.1
. For the function
f_{1,-1,-1,1,1}(X)
and

\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
we have

\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}=B_{\epsilon}\neq 0
following the notation and results of Proposition
2.4
. Similarly, for the polynomial
f_{1,1,1,1/2,1/2}(X)
and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}^{\ast}
, we have
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}=\det(A_{\epsilon})\neq 0
, following the notation and results of Proposition
2.3
.
∎
