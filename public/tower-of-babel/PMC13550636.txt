Secure adaptive federated learning for scalable anomaly detection in industrial IoT networks
Authors: Mohammed Naif Alatawi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rapid expansion of Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) infrastructures has increased the demand for robust, scalable, and privacy-preserving intrusion and anomaly detection mechanisms. Traditional centralized detection systems face critical limitations, including high privacy risks, communication bottlenecks, and vulnerability to adversarial tampering. To address these challenges, we propose SAFNet-IoT, a Secure Adaptive Federated Network that integrates Blockchain-Based Authentication (BBA) with an Adaptive Autoencoder–LSTM (AAEL) anomaly detection module for decentralized IIoT environments. The BBA mechanism ensures secure model update validation and integrity preservation through smart contracts, effectively mitigating malicious model manipulation. Meanwhile, the AAEL module dynamically adjusts its hyperparameters based on real-time feedback to enhance detection accuracy under heterogeneous and non-stationary IIoT conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that SAFNet-IoT achieves 94.7% anomaly detection accuracy, surpassing conventional federated and deep learning baselines such as FedAVG-LSTM (93.1%) and deep autoencoders (91.2%). The framework additionally reduces communication overhead by 30%, achieves a bandwidth reduction ratio of 0.67, and maintains an average local training time of 1.4 s with an end-to-end system latency of 4.6 s, enabling deployment on resource-constrained IIoT nodes. The blockchain layer provides 98.5% authentication success and 91.2% malicious update detection, outperforming traditional secure aggregation schemes. These results highlight SAFNet-IoT as an effective and resilient intrusion and anomaly detection solution for next-generation IIoT environments. Future work will focus on optimizing smart contract execution costs and extending SAFNet-IoT toward multi-modal data fusion and large-scale, real-time anomaly detection.

Introduction

The growing adoption of the Internet of Things (IoT) in the industry, automation, predictive maintenance and real time monitoring has been improved to a great extent. Today, there are more than 75 billion IoT devices globally deployed with industrial IoT (IIoT) making up a major share1. However, these benefits come with great vulnerability of IoT systems to security breaches, data tampering and operational failures. Current anomaly detection algorithms are centralized and have high risk of privacy, and high limit in scalability. Moreover, the number of cyberattacks on IIoT networks has increased, while the cost of an industrial control system (ICS) attack is $4.35 million on average in 20232. Therefore, it points out the need for such a decentralized, secure, and adaptive anomaly detection framework with the ability to reduce the risk while maintaining efficiency.

Federated Learning (FL) is a decentralized technique to achieve collaborative learning without transmitting raw data. Despite that, adversarial attacks, where compromised nodes inject manipulated updates, degrade overall model performance exist in the existing FL models3,4.

Also, learning convergence is hindered by non-IID data distributions among heterogeneous IoT nodes. The major challenge is the high communication overhead in FL for the frequent model exchanges, which does not scale in industrial environments5.

We propose in this paper SAFNet-IoT (Secure Adaptive Federated Network for IoT), as it consists in combining Blockchain Based Authentication (BBA) for secure model aggregation and an Adaptive Autoencoder LSTM (AAEL) anomaly detection for performing better. Smart contracts are used in BBA to ensure integrity of the federated learning updates against adversarial attacks6,7. At the same time, AAEL dynamically adjusts hyperparameters according to real time feedback that IoT nodes provide to improve the accuracy of anomaly detection in nonIID environments. The experiment results indicate that SAFNet-IoT can achieve 94.7% anomaly detection accuracy that is 4.4% higher than the conventional FL models with reduced communication overhead of 30%. The results of these findings indicate the need for incorporating blockchain security along with adaptive learning to improve industrial IoT anomaly detection.

To cope with these emerging challenges, this research proposed SAFNet-IoT, which is a secure adaptive federated network combined with the blockchain based authentication and adaptive anomaly detection mechanism. The major contribution was a bridge of the research gap identified by8 using robust security features and adaptive learning within a single adaptation, which is intended to increase overall system resilience and efficiency. This research offered an important contribution to the field in which it could enhance anomaly detection accuracy by an order of magnitude, reduce communication overhead, and shield industrial operations from the ever-evolving cyber threats.

The IoE attack surface in industrial settings was spent in the millions of IoT devices becoming exponentially larger with fast proliferation of devices, which was increasing the risk of sophisticated cyber threats and operational anomalies. By itself, privacy was already a problem with conventional centralized anomaly detection models9,10 while these models would fail with single point failures. Despite this, federated learning also provided a decentralized way, but it had been unreasonably in its current implementations due to data heterogeneity, high communication overhead, and adversarial vulnerabilities. This problem was of critical importance because any industrial IoT system security breach or undetected anomaly would have resulted in severe financial losses, safety hazards, and operational disruptions. This research afforded justification due to the need for the development of secure, reliable, and scalable anomaly detection frameworks to protect sensitive industrial infrastructures.

Literature review

Federated learning for industrial IoT

Federated learning (FL) had been extensively explored in Industrial IoT (IIoT) to enable privacy-preserving collaborative learning across distributed devices. Zhang et al.11 proposed a multi-agent FL framework that optimized communication efficiency, improving model convergence by 28%, but struggled with dynamic device participation. Nguyen et al.12 demonstrated that hierarchical FL reduced industrial network latency by 35%, yet incurred high computational costs. Boobalan et al.13 reviewed FL-based IIoT models and found that differential privacy mechanisms enhanced security but introduced accuracy trade-offs. Yang et al.14 employed deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for FL model selection, reducing energy consumption by 22%, though its dependency on predefined reward functions limited adaptability. Lu et al.15 integrated blockchain with FL to enhance security and reported a 92% improvement in model integrity, yet additional blockchain transactions led to latency overhead.

Several studies focused on anomaly detection and security enhancements in FL-based IIoT. Zhang et al.16 implemented a blockchain-enabled FL system for device failure detection, improving anomaly detection accuracy by 16%, though computational overhead remained a challenge. Rashid et al.17 developed an FL-based intrusion detection system, achieving 96.3% accuracy but lacking adaptability to evolving threats. Farahani and Monsefi18 introduced a collaborative IIoT framework that enhanced device cooperation but increased communication overhead. Li et al.19 proposed a client selection mechanism to address data heterogeneity in FL, reducing performance variability by 40%, yet requiring high computational resources. Liu et al.20 explored FL for deep anomaly detection in time-series IIoT data, reducing communication overhead by 50%, though sensitivity to adversarial attacks remained a limitation.

Blockchain-based authentication and secure model aggregation

Blockchain-based authentication has been widely adopted to enhance security and privacy in federated learning (FL) systems, particularly in industrial IoT environments. Kalapaaking et al.21 proposed a blockchain-integrated FL framework with secure aggregation in a trusted execution environment, reducing model tampering risks by 95%. However, the computational complexity of blockchain operations increased latency in large-scale deployments. Singh et al.22 combined blockchain with homomorphic encryption to achieve privacy-preserving data aggregation in smart grids, demonstrating a 40% improvement in confidentiality. Despite its security benefits, the overhead encryption led to higher energy consumption. Li et al.,23 introduced a blockchain-based data aggregation and group authentication scheme for electronic medical records, ensuring secure access control with 98% authentication accuracy. However, their approach required significant storage capacity due to the growing size of blockchain ledgers. Qureshi et al.24 developed a blockchain-based authentication model for intelligent transportation systems, reducing identity fraud incidents by 78%. Nevertheless, real-time authentication delays remained a limitation for latency-sensitive applications.

Several studies focused on improving secure model aggregation in blockchain-enabled federated learning frameworks. Mahmood et al.25 proposed a privacy-preserving data aggregation scheme for smart grids, achieving a 65% reduction in data leakage risks, but suffering from scalability issues. Ranathunga et al.26 implemented a blockchain-based decentralized model aggregation approach for cross-silo federated learning in Industry 4.0, increasing model robustness against adversarial attacks by 30%. However, communication overhead remained a concern due to frequent blockchain transactions.

Several studies have explored recent Byzantine-robust federated learning techniques that aim to secure model aggregation without relying on blockchain-based authentication. Krum, Trimmed-Mean, and coordinate-wise median are popular aggregation methods that are designed to tolerate malicious updates by identifying and excluding outlier updates from the aggregation process. For example, Krum relies on selecting the update closest to the majority of others, while Trimmed-Mean discards a predefined percentage of extreme values before aggregation. These methods, however, have limitations in terms of communication overhead and may still be vulnerable to coordinated attacks from multiple malicious nodes.

Recent Byzantine-robust methods have also proposed novel defenses such as Median of Means and Krum-based aggregation with robust clustering that aim to enhance the resilience of federated models against manipulated data. These methods, while improving robustness, still struggle with scalability when applied to large federations, as the computational complexity of their aggregation increases significantly with the number of nodes.

In contrast, the blockchain-based authentication in SAFNet-IoT provides a more robust solution by verifying the legitimacy of model updates before aggregation, ensuring that only trusted updates contribute to the global model. This approach enhances security by directly addressing adversarial threats such as poisoning attacks, which are not fully mitigated by traditional aggregation methods.

Fan and Zhang27 utilized a consortium blockchain for secure data aggregation in smart grids, demonstrating improved regulatory compliance, but facing challenges in integrating multiple heterogeneous devices. Shu et al.28 introduced a certificate less aggregate signature scheme for blockchain-based medical cyber-physical systems, enhancing authentication efficiency by 25%. Nevertheless, certificate revocation complexities limited its practical adoption. Esposito et al.29 explored blockchain-based authentication in smart cities, achieving a 90% improvement in identity verification accuracy, yet requiring high computational resources for large-scale implementations. Lin et al.30 developed a secure data aggregation strategy using a sixth-generation network-in-box approach, improving industrial IoT security, though the real-time processing capability needed further optimization. While previous works such as An Explainable and Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning Model for Threat Detection in Cyber-Physical-Social Systems31 primarily emphasize explainability and privacy preservation, their federated learning settings remain largely static and do not explicitly address adaptive learning under evolving data distributions. In contrast, the proposed SAFNet-IoT framework incorporates adaptive anomaly modeling that dynamically responds to non-IID and time-varying IIoT data patterns. Similarly, A Robust Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning Model Against Model Poisoning Attacks32 focuses on defending against model poisoning through robust aggregation mechanisms, but assumes relatively stable data distributions and does not consider continuous adaptation to concept drift. The SAFNet-IoT framework extends beyond these isolated defenses by jointly integrating adaptive learning mechanisms, blockchain-based authentication for secure aggregation, and communication-efficient federated learning. This unified design enables sustained anomaly detection performance and security resilience in dynamic and adversarial industrial IoT environments, positioning SAFNet-IoT as a comprehensive solution for next-generation federated learning in IIoT deployments.

Research gap

Despite extensive research on federated learning and blockchain-based secure aggregation in Industrial IoT, existing approaches had been limited by high computational overhead, increased latency, and vulnerability to adversarial attacks. Additionally, a great many studies have been focused on single components such as secure model aggregation or anomaly detection without exploiting adaptive learning mechanisms that are able to deal with the heterogeneity and the dynamics of industrial environments. The result highlighted the need for a holistic solution that blends blockchain-based authentication with adaptive federated learning to achieve a highly lifted amount of anomaly detection in IIoT.

Materials and methods

The SAFNet-IoT framework is developed to mitigate the security vulnerabilities and optimize anomaly detection in the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) environment. It goes into the details of the dataset used for training and evaluation, preprocessing techniques, the proposed federated learning framework, and the communication efficiency strategies and evaluation metrics. The methodology guarantees safety, adaptivity and the decentralization of the learning process in large scale IIoT networks and guarantees high efficiency.

Long-term robustness evaluation

To assess the long-term robustness of the SAFNet-IoT framework, we also performed experiments that evaluate model performance over time. The goal was to observe how well the model adapts to drifting data patterns and whether its performance degrades as the IIoT system runs continuously.

For this evaluation, we ran experiments using the TON_IoT and Edge-IIoTset datasets for 30 consecutive training and testing rounds. The model performance degradation over time was monitored by tracking key metrics such as accuracy, F1-score, and AUC-ROC across each round.

The reason for picking this dataset out was its industrial context. It derives the data from real-world IIoT deployments; thus, it’s representative of real-world deployments where federated learning can be used. In addition, the model includes both device based and network-based anomalies, such that the model can generalize well across different attack vectors and failure patterns. The dataset contains a large amount of time series data that allows one to train deep learning models like Autoencoder-LSTM networks. As the proposed federated learning framework is set in a distributed environment, the heterogeneity of the dataset is suited to the issues related to training and aggregation of a distributed model.

However, this dataset does have some disadvantages when being used. Bias in model predictions may occur due to the class imbalance (normal instances are much more numerous than anomalous ones). To tackle this problem, oversampling techniques like the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) or cost sensitive learning strategies are attempted. Furthermore, the dataset has a high dimensionality (over eighty features) which makes the computational burden high and needs feature selection techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Autoencoders to reduce complexity and retain essential information. Temporal dependencies also pose another challenge because many industrial anomalies take place in sequence of events rather than isolated incidents. In fact, this characteristic demands sequential models, such as LSTM, to effectively model long-term dependencies in IoT data streams.

The dataset used in the evaluation of the SAFNet-IoT framework is chosen to be realistic and diverse, which serves as a realistic and diverse testbed of IIoT anomalies. Both cybersecurity attacks and operational failures are included to make the model robust to multiple anomaly types. In addition, the dataset’s non-IID structure is like the typical federated learning challenges in industrial settings, which makes it a good match for this research.

Data preprocessing

The dataset is preprocessed through a structured pipeline to handle missing values, normalize features and optimize the data representation for anomaly detection so as to ensure effective training of the SAFNet-IoT framework. Since the dataset is of time series sensor readings along with network traffic data, each preprocessing step is carefully chosen to optimize model performance at the same time as computational efficiency.

Handling missing values using linear interpolation

Missing are quite frequent in industrial IoT data due to sensor failures, communication losses, and data corruption. Rather than discarding incomplete records, leaving a large amount of data lost, an interpolation technique of the linear type is used to fill in missing values based on neighboring data points. Given a time-series feature \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x_t}$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x_t}$$\end{document} is missing, the interpolated value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\hat {x}_t}$$\end{document} is computed as:1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\hat {x}_t}={x_{t - 1}}+\frac{{\left( {{x_{t+1}} - {x_{t - 1}}} \right)}}{2}~$$\end{document}

where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x_{t - 1}}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x_{t+1}}$$\end{document} are the closest available values before and after the missing point. This method ensures smooth data continuity while preserving the temporal integrity of IoT sensor readings.

Problem formulation: secure and efficient federated learning in industrial IoT

Federated Learning (FL) in Industrial IoT (IIoT) environment is to train decentralized models across multiple IoT devices and aggregate local models periodically to obtain a global model. Nevertheless, in IIoT, FL encounters high communication overhead, adversarial vulnerability, and inefficient model update caused by the heterogeneity of device data. The problem is to design a secure and efficient FL framework, which leverages blockchain authentication to achieve good communication efficiency and model convergence.

Analysis and communication overhead

Scaling federated learning in IIoT environments introduces challenges, particularly communication overhead and model aggregation efficiency. These challenges become more pronounced as the number of devices increases. The communication cost for each round of federated learning can be described as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O\left( n \right)$$\end{document}, where nnn is the number of participating devices. Each device transmits its local model updates, and these updates are aggregated to form the global model, which grows linearly with the number of devices.

The latency for federated learning also grows linearly with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O\left( n \right)$$\end{document}, due to the increasing number of devices sending updates and the additional time required to verify and aggregate these updates through the blockchain. This leads to higher transaction volume and network congestion as the size of the federation increases.

Proposed framework: SAFNet-IoT

This work proposes the SAFNet IOT framework that offers anomaly detection advantage in Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) environments via federated learning, blockchain based authentication and a adaptive Autoencoder-LSTM Model. The main objective is to construct a secure, adaptive, communication efficient anomaly detection mechanism to secure IIoT networks from cyber security attacks and from operational failures. In this section we present an overview of the proposed framework, its system architecture, mathematical formulation, communication efficiency optimizations and the algorithmic implementation of SAFNet-IoT.

Overview

The SAFNet-IoT framework uses FL for collaborative model training of anomaly detection across multiple distributed IIoT nodes without exposing raw data. So, unlike traditional centralized learning approaches, FL also allows each IoT device to train its own model locally and to update the model but only share model updates. A blockchain-based authentication is added to mitigate adversarial attacks, allowing only authorized model updates to be aggregated in the global model. Furthermore, an Autoencoder-LSTM based anomaly detection module is made adaptive with respect to real time feedback from IIoT devices in such a way that it dynamically changes its learning parameters to increase accuracy in non-IID environments. Computational complexity justification:

To address the concern regarding the added computational complexity of the adaptive mechanism, we compared the FLOPs, runtime, and memory usage of the baseline FL-LSTM and the proposed Adaptive AAEL on a resource-constrained IIoT device profile (Raspberry Pi 4, 4GB RAM, quad-core 1.5 GHz). The adaptive mechanism introduces two additional lightweight operations: (1) drift-score calculation and (2) hyperparameter adjustment per round. The drift-score operation adds 0.42 million additional FLOPs and less than 6.5 MB of temporary memory allocation, which is negligible relative to the total model footprint. Empirical profiling shows that AAEL increases per-round computation time from 2.84 s (baseline FL-LSTM) to 2.94 s, resulting in only + 0.1 s overhead. Memory consumption increased by 3.8%, remaining well within IIoT device constraints. Despite this minor overhead, AAEL reduces total convergence rounds from 50 to 40, resulting in an overall 12% reduction in total training time. Thus, the added adaptive complexity is minimal and is fully compensated by faster convergence and higher predictive performance.

Sensitivity analysis of drift threshold

To evaluate the robustness of the adaptive anomaly detection mechanism, a sensitivity analysis was conducted on the drift threshold used in the KL-divergence trigger. The default value of 0.15 was selected based on initial empirical validation, but its effectiveness may vary across different IIoT environments or with varying noise levels in the data. In this section, we analyze how different values of the drift threshold affect the overall performance, focusing on key metrics such as anomaly detection accuracy, F1-score, and AUC-ROC across multiple IIoT datasets.

Justification for threshold selection:

In our ablation study, we tested several drift threshold values, including 0.10, 0.15, and 0.20, using different IIoT datasets. The results showed that the performance of the model remained consistent across these thresholds, with the 0.15 threshold providing the best balance between model accuracy and robustness to changes in drift. Specifically, the 0.15 threshold yielded the highest F1-score and AUC-ROC while maintaining strong accuracy.

We also conducted a grid search over several threshold values (from 0.05 to 0.25), and the 0.15 threshold consistently outperformed others in terms of accuracy and robustness. This provides empirical evidence for its selection, confirming that 0.15 is not an arbitrary value but a result of extensive testing.

The sensitivity analysis results for three tested thresholds (0.10, 0.15, and 0.20) are presented in the table below, showing the performance metrics (Accuracy, F1-score, AUC-ROC) for each threshold. The analysis demonstrates that while the model performs optimally at a threshold of 0.15, slight variations in this parameter (e.g., values between 0.10 and 0.20) do not drastically affect performance, with minimal variation observed in the Accuracy, F1-score, and AUC-ROC metrics. This indicates that the framework is robust to moderate changes in drift detection sensitivity, and the choice of drift threshold within this range does not significantly compromise detection performance.

Blockchain security validation

Under the IIoT (Industrial IoT) environments, malicious actors can inject adversarial updates that degrade the models; this type of integrity challenge is highly critical to protect federated learning updates. The SAFNet-IoT framework combines a blockchain based authentication algorithm to validate the updates of the model before aggregation to prevent data manipulation, model poisoning and adversarial attacks. Through smart contract authenticated, each participating IoT node would send the locally trained model update to a blockchain ledger for verification prior to the inclusion of those updates to the global model aggregation process.

SAFNet-IoT is evaluated in terms of security performance by using authentication success rate (ASR) and malicious update detection (MUD) rate. ASR is the percentage of submitted updates successfully verified by the blockchain, and only legitimate model contributions are included. The MUD rate is used to evaluate how well the framework detects and rejects malicious updates.

Blockchain storage overhead and Ledger growth analysis

Integrating Blockchain-Based Authentication (BBA) into SAFNet-IoT introduces additional storage and computational requirements that must be carefully evaluated, particularly for memory-constrained IIoT devices. Each global federated round generates a compressed model update that is stored on the blockchain. After quantization and sparsification, the average update size is 180 KB. With 20 IIoT clients participating in 40 global rounds, the total ledger size reaches approximately 144 MB (180 KB × 20 × 40). While this size is manageable for cloud or edge servers acting as full nodes, it exceeds the storage capabilities of typical IIoT devices, which often possess only 16–64 MB of available storage. To address this limitation, SAFNet-IoT adopts a light-node architecture where only block headers—roughly 80 bytes per block—are stored locally. For 40 rounds, this results in merely 3.2 KB of storage, making it entirely feasible for PLCs, microcontrollers, and embedded IoT devices.

Beyond storage, the computational and energy overhead of BBA must also be assessed. Authentication of each model update incurs an average latency of 18.4 ms, while committing a transaction to the ledger requires an additional 4.1 ms. Lightweight cryptographic operations such as SHA-256 hashing take approximately 2.6 ms on embedded ARM Cortex-M4 or ESP32 devices, and the overall energy consumption per update remains low at around 0.42 mJ. The memory footprint for running the blockchain light client stack is approximately 6.2 MB of RAM, which comfortably fits within the operational limits of modern IIoT gateways. These measurements show that the BBA overhead is minimal and does not hinder the real-time performance demands of industrial environments.

Scaling and blockchain overhead analysis

As the number of federated learning participants grows, blockchain storage and performance overhead will naturally scale. The ledger storage for full nodes grows linearly with the number of rounds and participants. For larger federations, we expect the storage requirements for full nodes to increase accordingly. However, light nodes (IIoT devices) continue to benefit from minimal storage requirements—storing only block headers, reducing storage needs by over 95%. This scalable architecture ensures that as the system grows, the storage demands on IIoT devices remain manageable.

Moreover, the transaction latency and network congestion will also increase as the federation size grows. The on-chain commit latency of 4.1 ms may increase in larger federations, especially if a Proof of Work (PoW) mechanism were used. However, to mitigate this, SAFNet-IoT employs a Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT)-based consensus mechanism. PBFT enables faster consensus and reduces transaction latency, making it ideal for federated learning in large-scale IIoT deployments.

In addition, light-client feasibility has been tested on constrained hardware such as Raspberry Pi and ESP32 devices, which are commonly used in IIoT environments. These devices can act as light clients in the blockchain network, significantly reducing the storage and computational overhead. The Raspberry Pi 4B and ESP32 devices were able to handle blockchain authentication with only a slight increase in latency (approximately 20 ms per transaction for Raspberry Pi and 25 ms for ESP32). This proves that even low-power devices can participate in the blockchain-based federated learning process without significant performance degradation.

Consensus mechanism

The PBFT consensus mechanism is employed to ensure fast transaction validation and fault tolerance in the system. PBFT provides a highly scalable solution for federated learning, as it reduces the amount of computational power needed compared to Proof of Work (PoW), which would be infeasible in IIoT environments due to its high energy consumption. The Proof of Stake (PoS) mechanism is integrated to optimize transaction processing, further reducing energy requirements and ensuring that the system can scale to handle federations of hundreds or thousands of devices.

Light-client feasibility, Merkle verification, and pruning strategy

In the proposed system, IIoT nodes operate as light clients to minimize local storage. Light clients do not maintain the full blockchain; instead, they store only block headers containing the Merkle root. During verification, the client requests a Merkle proof from a full node. This proof includes the minimal set of sibling hashes needed to trace the model-update transaction back to the Merkle root, enabling the node to validate authenticity without downloading the complete block data. This reduces storage requirements by over 95%. To further manage ledger growth, pruning is applied on full nodes, where historical block bodies older than a defined retention window are removed and only compressed state summaries are kept. Checkpointing is used every fixed number of rounds, where a compact snapshot of the global model state, its hash, and essential metadata are stored as a stable reference point. Light clients sync only the latest checkpoint and its subsequent headers, avoiding continuous chain replay. This combined use of Merkle proofs, header-only synchronization, pruning, and periodic checkpointing ensures low memory usage, fast verification, and long-term sustainability of the blockchain layer in IIoT environments.

Real-time performance and cost–benefit analysis of BBA

Blockchain-Based Authentication (BBA) introduces computational and memory overhead that must remain feasible for IIoT devices. To evaluate this, we measured real-time latency, RAM usage, energy consumption, and ledger growth during federated training.

On average, BBA authentication requires 18.4 ms per model update, while writing the update to the ledger adds 4.1 ms. Lightweight cryptographic operations such as SHA-256 hashing require 2.6 ms on IIoT-class ARM Cortex-M4/ESP32 devices. The memory footprint for running SAFNet-IoT’s BBA light client is 6.2 MB, which fits comfortably within the 16–64 MB memory range of modern industrial IIoT nodes. Energy consumption per authenticated update remains low at 0.42 mJ, confirming suitability for battery-powered and embedded devices.

To understand long-term impact, we quantified blockchain ledger growth: with 20 clients participating in 40 global rounds, the ledger increases to 144 MB. This storage is handled exclusively by edge/cloud full nodes. IIoT devices operate as light nodes that only store block headers—roughly 3.2 KB—ensuring that the storage overhead is negligible. The combination of low latency, minimal energy use, and tiny storage footprint demonstrates that BBA provides strong update integrity without degrading the real-time performance requirements of industrial automation systems.

Overall, the cost–benefit evaluation confirms that while BBA introduces small computational delays, the security benefits—tamper-proof model update validation, poisoned-update filtering, and Byzantine resistance—far outweigh the overhead. This makes BBA practical and advantageous for securing federated IIoT environments.

Extended security evaluation against adversarial threats

To address the broader threat landscape highlighted in recent IIoT security literature and in the reviewer feedback, this subsection evaluates SAFNet-IoT under three critical adversarial scenarios: data poisoning attacks, model inversion attacks, and Byzantine faults. These attacks were simulated using standard adversarial settings to demonstrate the robustness of the proposed framework.

Data poisoning attack:

A 20% poisoning rate was applied by injecting mislabeled and manipulated samples into selected client datasets. Under this scenario, a standard FedAvg-based LSTM model experienced a significant performance drop, with accuracy declining from 93.1% to 80.3%. In contrast, SAFNet-IoT maintained an accuracy of 90.6%, representing a 4.1% drop compared to its clean performance. This resilience is achieved through blockchain-based gradient validation, anomaly scoring of client updates, and the adaptive AAEL mechanism which restricts abnormal parameter shifts.

Model inversion attack:

The gradient-matching inversion technique was used to reconstruct feature patterns from federated updates. The baseline federated model produced reconstructed feature maps with an average structural similarity index (SSIM) of 0.41, indicating partial leakage of input structure. SAFNet-IoT reduced this significantly to 0.19 SSIM, making reconstruction highly unreliable. The improvement results from hashing-based update authentication and the reduced exposure of raw gradients due to light-node architecture and adaptive quantization.

Byzantine attack:

A sign-flipping Byzantine strategy was introduced on 30% of participating clients to corrupt aggregated gradients. The baseline FedAvg model dropped to 61.3% accuracy under this attack, failing to recover normal update direction. SAFNet-IoT maintained 87.4% accuracy thanks to blockchain-based verification, detection of abnormal gradient magnitudes, and rejection of inconsistent or malicious updates before aggregation. These mechanisms collectively prevent a small subset of compromised clients from destabilizing global training.

Overall, these evaluations confirm that SAFNet-IoT offers strong protection against key adversarial threats commonly faced in federated industrial environments. By combining BBA with adaptive update validation and anomaly-aware learning, the framework significantly improves robustness compared to traditional federated learning approaches.

Practical integration of blockchain-based authentication with federated aggregation:

In the proposed framework, Blockchain-Based Authentication (BBA) acts as a verification layer that executes before each federated aggregation round. When a local client completes its model update, the update hash and metadata are submitted to a smart contract as a signed transaction. The smart contract verifies the node identity using stored public keys and timestamps the update. Full nodes then perform consensus to record the update on the blockchain. Once the block is confirmed, a Merkle proof is produced to show that the update is included in the ledger. The aggregator fetches only the hashed update references from the blockchain and validates each incoming model delta by comparing the submitted hash with the authenticated hash stored in the blockchain transaction. Discussion and comparative analysis

The results demonstrate that SAFNet-IoT offers a balanced trade-off between security, accuracy, and efficiency, making it a viable solution for anomaly detection in IIoT networks. Specifically, if the models based on baseline federated learning are compared, SAFNet-IoT can enhance anomaly detection accuracy 4.4% by saving communication overhead 30%, which indicates that integrating with blockchain authentication and adaptive anomaly detection can well enhance overall system performance. Additionally, model’s hyper parameters can be dynamically changed according to real time feedback to provide more accurate detection mainly in environments of non IID and heterogeneous IIoT.

Model updates are authenticated using the blockchain such that only verified updates are aggregated to defend against adversarial poisoning and unauthorized modifications. The ASR obtained is 98.5%, and MUD rate of 91.2%, which is much higher than any existing federated learning security models. Furthermore, SAFNet-IoT achieves 40 training rounds to stabilize, 25 rounds faster than that of the conventional FedAVG based models.

However, blockchain authentication has a greater resulting computational overhead, and this computation may adversely impact on the real time performance characteristics of latency sensitive applications. By including smart contract-based verification it improves the security but with additional processing time and storage requirement. Future work in the area can concentrate on optimizing the smart contract execution and reducing blockchain transaction cost, use of lightweight cryptographic techniques, or off chain validation mechanism to improve the scalability further.

Scalability in large-scale federations

While the SAFNet-IoT framework was tested with a federation of 20 IoT devices, we recognize that real-world IIoT environments may involve significantly larger federations with hundreds or even thousands of participating nodes. Scaling the framework to larger federations introduces additional challenges, primarily in terms of communication overhead and model aggregation efficiency. As the number of participating devices increases, communication overhead tends to grow linearly, leading to increased network congestion. In the case of hundreds of devices, the system would likely experience a substantial increase in latency, particularly due to the need for more frequent blockchain transactions and the complexity of aggregating updates from a larger number of devices.

The blockchain authentication layer, though effective at smaller scales, may introduce delays with increased transaction volume and network congestion in larger federations. To mitigate this, we propose optimizing the smart contract execution process and exploring off-chain verification techniques to minimize latency. Additionally, techniques such as model compression, partial model updates, and hierarchical aggregation can help reduce the burden on both communication and computational resources as the scale increases.

Further work will focus on evaluating the framework’s performance with larger-scale federations, both analytically and experimentally, to provide more comprehensive insights into its scalability. Expected findings from such an analysis include the identification of optimal thresholds for communication overhead and model update efficiency as well as strategies to handle network congestion and transactional latency in federations of hundreds or thousands of devices.

Ablation study

To systematically evaluate the contribution of each component in the SAFNet-IoT framework, we conducted a comprehensive ablation study. This analysis isolates the impact of three core components: (1) the Adaptive Autoencoder–LSTM (AAEL) mechanism, (2) Blockchain-Based Authentication (BBA), and (3) Federated Learning (FL). Each component was selectively disabled while keeping other components intact, and the resulting performance was measured against the full SAFNet-IoT framework. The experiments were conducted on the TON_IoT dataset using 20 federated clients over 40 global training rounds.

Component-wise analysis

Impact of AAEL: Removing the Adaptive Autoencoder–LSTM mechanism results in a 2.6% accuracy drop (from 94.7% to 92.1%) and a reduction in F1-score from 0.92 to 0.89. The AAEL module contributes to improved detection performance through its drift detection capability using KL-divergence and dynamic hyperparameter adjustment (learning rate, LSTM window size, and dropout rate). Without adaptive tuning, the model struggles to maintain optimal performance under non-IID data distributions and evolving anomaly patterns typical in IIoT environments.

Impact of BBA: Disabling Blockchain-Based Authentication causes the largest performance degradation, with accuracy dropping by 3.4% (from 94.7% to 91.3%) under simulated Byzantine attacks (20% malicious clients). The F1-score decreases significantly from 0.92 to 0.86, and AUC-ROC drops from 0.96 to 0.91. This demonstrates the critical role of BBA in filtering poisoned model updates before aggregation, thereby preserving global model integrity against adversarial threats.

Impact of FL: Replacing federated learning with centralized training results in a 3.2% accuracy reduction (from 94.7% to 91.5%). While centralized learning converges faster (20 rounds vs. 40 rounds), it increases communication overhead by 45% due to raw data transmission to the central server. Federated learning preserves data privacy, reduces bandwidth consumption by 30%, and achieves superior model generalization by training on diverse local data distributions across IIoT nodes.

Summary of ablation findings

The ablation study confirms that each component of SAFNet-IoT provides a distinct and measurable contribution to overall system performance. Blockchain-Based Authentication (BBA) contributes the most significant improvement (+ 3.4%), followed by Federated Learning (+ 3.2%) and the Adaptive AAEL mechanism (+ 2.6%). The synergistic integration of all three components enables SAFNet-IoT to achieve its optimal performance of 94.7% accuracy, 0.92 F1-score, and 0.96 AUC-ROC, demonstrating that the full framework is essential for robust, secure, and adaptive anomaly detection in Industrial IoT environments (Tables 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21 and 22).

Conclusion

By integrating the federated learning, blockchain authentication, and an Autoencoder-LSTM model with an adaptive Autoencoder-LSTM model and an adaptive Autoencoder-LSTM model, this study shows that SAFNet-IoT is a secure and efficient framework for anomaly detection in Industrial IoT (IIoT). The proposed system has 4.4% better anomaly detection accuracy than traditional models and 98.5% authentication success rate and 91.2% malicious update detection rate in model integrity. Selecting and aggregating the quantized updates greatly reduces communication overhead by 30% with a bandwidth reduction ratio (BRR) of 0.67, which shows the effectiveness of using quantized model updates and reduced aggregation. In addition, SAFNet-IoT converges in 40 rounds, that is, 25 rounds faster than conventional models, achieving better training efficiency. Although the blockchain authentication is better secured, it runs the risk of affecting the real time performance via computational overhead. However, PCA-based feature selection and lightweight cryptographic techniques help maintain efficiency, with an average local training time of 1.4 s, making it suitable for edge devices. Compared to previous studies, SAFNet-IoT achieves superior accuracy, security, and communication efficiency, making it a robust and scalable solution for IIoT anomaly detection. Future research should focus on optimizing blockchain execution, minimizing communication costs, and extending support for multi-modal industrial data to further enhance system performance.
