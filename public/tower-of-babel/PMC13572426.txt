A multimodal deep learning framework for precise silicosis detection on radiographic images

ABSTRACT
AbstractPneumoconiosis, a common occupational lung illness, arises from inhaling dust, with silicosis specifically caused by fine crystalline silica dust, leading to lung scarring and inflammation. The diagnosis of silicosis relies on routine monitoring, which includes physical examinations, medical history reviews, and imaging. Chest radiography is a common form of medical screening due to its affordability, efficiency, and suitability for routine use. Recent success in deep learning (DL) for medical image classification has demonstrated that DL algorithms can identify silicosis with high precision by classifying CT images. DL models, specifically convolutional neural networks, have become an effective approach due to their ability to analyse medical images. Therefore, this study presents a novel Multimodal Deep Learning Framework for Early Identification of Silicosis Diagnosis (MDLF-EISD) using radiological images, focused on enabling timely clinical intervention and improving patient outcomes. The proposed framework applies feature fusion (EfficientNet-B3, a capsule network, and ConvNext V2) for integrating complementary radiographic representation, enhancing the ability of the model in capturing disease-specific patterns over different levels of silicosis. Moreover, a convolutional bidirectional attention model is utilised to classify silicosis into corresponding categories effectively. An extensive simulation studies were carried out to evaluate the enhanced performance of the MDLF-EISD method under Silicodata. The comparative analysis of the MDLF-EISD method illustrated a superior accuracy value of 98.73% over other models. These results demonstrate that the feature fusion improves discriminative capabilities for silicosis-related radiographic findings. The proposed system has the ability to support as a computer-aided screening tool for early silicosis diagnosis, particularly in resource-limited clinical and occupational health settings where access to expert radiologists is limited.Keywords: Precision diagnostics, Radiological image analysis, Silicosis diagnosis, Feature fusion, Artificial intelligence, Deep learningSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

Silicosis is described as a debilitating and chronic pneumoconiosis, etiologically categorised as a wide range of interstitial lung disease (ILD). Its pathogenesis is connected to a chronic inhalation of silica dust, which leads to a gradual pulmonary fibrosis, irreparable respiratory failure, and demise1. Traditionally related to higher-risk environments such as quarrying and mining, silicosis has been established as a significant challenge to people’s health. However, in the last few years, silicosis has undergone a worldwide resurgence, which marks a changeover in the primary source of revelation2. As the primary pulmonary indication, simple silicosis (SS) is represented by the diffusion of tiny, distinct fibrotic nodules in parenchymal tissues3. This way can lead rapidly to the elevation to progressive massive fibrosis (PMF), described by the permanent coalescence of fibrotic lesions into huge conglomerate groups, therefore influencing severe and permanent damage to lung functions4. The latency period is short in ES-related silicosis when compared with traditional silicosis. The primary procedures for analysing silicosis are clinical and occupational history, chest X-rays (CXRs), higher-resolution computed tomography (HRCT), and pulmonary function tests (PFTs)5.

In spite of current techniques accessible in medical applications, the early and precise recognition of silicosis is one of the significant challenges6. Intra- and inter-observer variation in the analysis of CXRs, the complication in understanding radiological results like tiny opacities, in the earlier disorder stages, restricted access to clinical image tests, higher cost-effectiveness, and the HRCT radiation vulnerability make it necessary to determine novel cost-effective tools that support analysis7. Moreover, a few recent studies demonstrate that assured biomarkers extracted from routine blood tests, like angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), fibrinogen, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), and several cellular ratios, are associated with the chronic inflammatory condition and might help in differentiating patients with SS from healthy persons3. These biomarkers may serve as an extra element to an analysis performed through imaging methods, even though the possible role of these biomarkers is currently not recognised8.

The fast evolution of artificial intelligence (AI) has led to wide applications of deep learning (DL) methods for the analysis of medical images. The methods mentioned above use a multi-layer network to assist the automatic learning of implied relations within the data9. The resultant features are frequently different and significant, especially in the application of silicosis images. DL can offer unsupervised or semi-supervised independent learning of a target image for a classification task10. Also, it can analyse images with similar features and emulate the autonomous learning and analysis abilities of human beings, therefore decreasing the individuality of feature extraction11. Currently, no appropriate reports on the valuation of pneumoconiosis image analysis depend on the DL computer-aided diagnostic (CAD) system technique. Deep residual networks (DRNs) and Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are standard DL methods utilised for the classification of images. Such techniques give the benefits of generalisability, practicability, and simplicity12. Early-stage silicosis often presents with subtle opacities that overlap with tuberculosis and STB, leading to diagnostic ambiguity in CXR. Existing CNN-based CAD systems struggle to retain spatial hierarchies and global contextual features needed to distinguish these conditions. Therefore, integrating EfficientNet (local features), CapsNet (pose-aware spatial relations), and ConvNeXt V2 (global response normalisation) provides a more biologically meaningful representation. Thus, the multimodal DL model improves precision diagnostics by enabling finer discrimination between silicosis, TB, STB, and normal CXRs, overcoming common clinical interpretive challenges.

To accurately identify and classify silicosis, a kind of pulmonary fibrosis, a lung condition triggered by breathing in tiny bits of silica, this paper proposes a novel Multimodal Deep Learning Framework for Early Identification of Silicosis Diagnosis (MDLF-EISD) by utilising radiological images. The proposed model aims to achieve early and precise identification and classification of silicosis, facilitating appropriate clinical intervention and increasing patient outcomes. This study follows a multi-faceted approach encompassing a refined image pre-processing pipeline through resizing, normalisation, gamma correction, and noise removal, multimodal feature fusion, and classification. The proposed MDLF-EISD framework is evaluated on benchmark datasets using several performance metrics. This study hypothesises that combining heterogeneous feature extractors with a bidirectional attention classifier can improve early-stage silicosis detection in chest radiographs. Present studies on the diagnosis of silicosis employing radiological images mainly based on chest X-ray (CXR) data and frequently concentrates on limited classification challenges, like distinctive silicosis from normal cases. But, in real medical settings, silicosis should be distinguished from other pulmonary circumstances like silico-tuberculosis and tuberculosis that display similar radiographic features. Assessment metrics

Several performance measures are utilised to validate the model’s performance. In this study, accuracy, precision, recall, F1-Score, and AUC are employed.

Accuracy assesses how close a measurement or prediction is to the true, which indicates its exactness or correctness.

Precision is the percentage of true positive results among all positive classifications. It measures how many of the instances identified as positive were really correct.

Recall is a measure that calculates the ratio of actual positive samples that are accurately predicted by a model.

The F1-Score is the harmonic mean of a system’s precision as well as recall. It is utilised for balancing the importance of both FP and FN, which gives equal weight to precision and recall.

The AUC score determines the system’s capability to differentiate between negative and positive classes across every possible classification threshold.

The experimental analysis of the MDLF-EISD methodology is examined under Silicodata. The model is simulated using Python 3.6.5 on a PC with an i5-8600k, 250GB SSD, GeForce 1050Ti 4GB, 16GB RAM, and 1 TB HDD. The parameter setting is given as follows: learning rate: 0.01, dropout: 0.5, batch size: 5, epoch count: 300, and activation: ReLU. For experimental validation, 80:20 and 70:30 of training/testing dataset is used. Table 3 display the parameter setting of the proposed model. Moreover, these hyperparameters and model choices were selected to achieve a balance between accuracy, efficiency, and generalization. EfficientNet-B3 and ConvNeXt V2 are used as complementary feature extractors because they capture strong visual representations while remaining computationally efficient. Capsule Networks are included to better understand spatial relationships in the data, while attention-based fusion helps combine features from different models in a meaningful way. The convolutional bidirectional attention module further improves performance by capturing both local patterns and long-range dependencies. A moderately sized classification head with ReLU activation and Softmax ensures stable and accurate final predictions. Training is controlled using AdamW with a small learning rate and weight decay to improve generalization, while dropout and early stopping help prevent overfitting and ensure the model does not train beyond its optimal point.

The experimental outcomes illustrate the efficiency of the presented MDLF-EISD architecture for silicosis disease classification by chest X-ray images. The incorporation of EfficientNet-B3, Capsule Network, and ConvNextV2 allows the separation of rich and complementary feature representations on multiple views, thus enhancing the overall discriminative potential of the methodology. The feature fusion techniques additionally consolidate these representations into a specified feature space, improving inter-class separability. The stable performance recognized over various evaluation measures and various train-test split ratios, which illustrates the strongness of the presented architecture. However, the comparative analysis with existing methods and the ablation study validate the contribution of every individual component, confirming, which integrated influence of multi-backbone feature extraction, feature fusion, and attention-guided classification importantly improves silicosis recognition performance.

Conclusion

In this manuscript, a novel MDLF-EISD model has been designed to attain early and precise detection and classification of silicosis, thus facilitating timely clinical intervention and enhancing patient outcomes. To achieve that, the proposed MDLF-EISD model primarily pre-processed the input images through numerous steps: image resizing, pixel intensity normalisation, gamma correction, and denoising, which prepared the raw image data for analysis. For feature representation, a multimodal feature fusion approach is integrated, combining three DL architectures, namely EfficientNet-B3, CapsNet, and ConvNext V2, which effectively extract informative features essential for the classification process. Lastly, the convolutional bidirectional attention technique is employed for accurate classification of silicosis into corresponding categories. A comprehensive simulation investigation is conducted to guarantee the improved performance of the MDLF-EISD methodology. The comparative analysis of results inferred that the proposed MDLF-EISD model performed well compared to existing techniques across multiple evaluation measures. Thus, the proposed MDLF-EISD model is a promising solution for early and accurate identification and classification of silicosis using radiological images. The proposed system can be integrated into occupational health screening centres, enabling precise and consistent triage of CXR images, reducing dependence on radiologist interpretation variability. Traditional radiographic assessment of silicosis is limited by inter-observer variability. This model reduces classification uncertainty by utilising multimodal imaging features, improving consistency and precision.

Even though the proposed MDLF-EISD model achieves a strong performance for silicosis classification from chest X-ray images, it has some limitations, this study is only focused on the single imaging modality (CXR) and depending upon on publicly available datasets, which may not fully denote the diversity of real-world clinical conditions. In addition, the methodology has not been extensively evaluated over multi-center datasets or various imaging devices, which might be influence its generalizability in broader clinical conditions.

Future work should focus on integrating multimodal imaging, expanding geographic and demographic diversity, and conducting prospective clinical trials to validate model performance in real-world hospital environments. By integrating heterogeneous feature representations, the system reduces diagnostic ambiguity and provides more reliable precision diagnostics for high-risk occupational groups. This aligns with the goals of precision medicine by enabling targeted follow-up, early intervention, and individualised risk stratification. Deep features act like radiomic descriptors, and multimodal fusion captures both textural and spatial patterns, thus enhancing personalised precision in imaging-based classification.
