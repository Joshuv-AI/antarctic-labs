Controllable Synthesis of PdSe2 Noncontinuous Nanomaterial Thin-Film Electrodes for Photoelectrochemical Application
Authors: Zhengtian Shi, Xianze Zhao, Wenjing Jian, Dongmei Liu, Shengnan Guo, Xuan Zhang, Zhihong Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PdSe2 nanomaterials, characterized by their layered structure, high specific surface area, and tunable bandgap, exhibit remarkable advantages in optoelectronic devices, energy storage, and other fields. This paper focuses on the CVD synthesis of PdSe2 nanomaterials through four distinct selenation methods. The feasibility of product preparation is verified by SEM (Product morphology) and EDS (confirming the Pd/Se atomic ratio). Besides, the Raman scattering spectra are further utilized to confirm that the product is PdSe2 through definite characteristic peaks at 162 cm–1 (Eg modes) and 259 cm–1 (Ag modes). PdSe2, as one of the candidates for photoelectrode materials, has demonstrated excellent photoelectrochemical properties. The results of this paper indicate that, under visible light irradiation (λ = 400–800 nm), the photocurrent density exhibits a nonlinear correlation with light intensity (10–50 mW/cm2), reaching 0.008 mA/cm2 at optimal conditions, and it has good photocurrent stability.

Introduction

In recent years, new multifunctional nanomaterials have emerged as transformative materials in multiple fields because of their unique physical and chemical properties. The most notable advantages of these materials are reflected in three aspects: First, the preparation method is controllable and can be mass-produced for industry; second, the fabrication processes are simple and pollution-free. More importantly, this material possesses the characteristic of a multidimensional function and can be applied in fields such as optoelectronics, new energy, and so on. On the basis of the above advantages, this material has demonstrated breakthrough applications in multiple key fields. For example, the photodevice, − with its high sensitivity and rapid response characteristics, has significantly improved the detection accuracy; in the field of photocatalysis, − these materials exhibit high catalytic activity and have played a significant role in environmental governance. In the field of new energy, , the new materials possess outstanding energy conversion and storage capabilities. Notably, it is particularly worth noting that, in forensic science, − these latent fingerprint imaging techniques play a significant role in the identification of case evidence.

Transition-metal dichalcogenide compounds and their doped composite structures possess unique layered geometric structures and physical-chemical properties. − Notably, Hao et al. demonstrated that two-dimensional semiconductor materials possess unique crystal structures and electronic structures. By precise regulation of their band structure, the optical and electronic properties of two-dimensional materials can be efficiently altered. Based on the above facts, they exhibit remarkable photoelectric effects, excellent sensitivity, and structural stability, making them promising candidate materials in the field of fingerprint detection. Accurate analysis of evidence is of vital importance in modern forensic science, so it is particularly important to find cost-effective and nonpolluting multifunctional materials. At present, traditional criminal investigation methods such as powder spraying (brushing), iodine fumigation, silver nitrate, ninhydrin, 502 fumigation, etc., often fail to reveal the latent fingerprints on the crime scene and the criminal evidence. Additionally, these traditional methods have significant negative impacts on the health of inspectors and the environment, and the cost is also relatively high. Therefore, it is particularly important to construct efficient, sensitive, and background fluorescence latent fingerprint display materials. In recent years, the magnetic brush display method , has been increasingly applied in on-site investigation. Different from the traditional brushing method, this method uses a magnetic field to attract magnetic powder and form a powder peak, then uses the powder spike for fingerprint brushing. The technique offers dual advantages of powder recovery capability and reduced environmental contamination. As for the magnetic imaging powder required by the magnetic brush display method, TMD materials, due to their excellent properties such as photoelectricity, magnetism, etc., have become one of the most valuable materials for research, and they are also suitable for the latent fingerprints. −

TMD nanomaterials are connected by weak van der Waals force and do not rely on direct covalent bond, which brings high application freedom. Palladium diselenide (PdSe2), as the VIII TMD material, with a pentagonal crystal structure, − has attracted wide attention from material physicists because of its special interlayer structure that results in an ideal photoelectric effect. PdSe2 has a unique pentagonal structure: Pd atoms coordinate with 4 in-plane Se atoms, and adjacent layers are independent via Se–Se covalent bonds. This unique van der Waals heterostructure can compete with traditional transition-metal halides in the field of new optoelectronic materials. Recently, the research on broadband metal oxide semiconductors (such as zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) and new structures (such as exfoliated graphene, functional nanomaterials) has received widespread attention, particularly in the construction of heterojunction structures. Lin et al. , demonstrate that some doped heterogeneous TMD materials possess self-powered effects and exhibit stability and durability. Furthermore, Shautsova et al. concluded that PdSe2 films can be fabricated by controlled laser irradiation. This technique induces localized phase transformation to metallic PdSe2–x . Compared to the pristine product, it shows 20-fold enhancement in both stability and conductivity. These research advancements indicate that PdSe2 is expected to become a key focus in the fields of next-generation optoelectronics and forensic sciences.

Criminal investigation techniques are gradually evolving toward high precision, intelligence, wide coverage, and depth analysis. With the continuous improvement of detection methods, fingerprint examination techniques have undergone significant diversification, making fingerprints a “key evidence” in criminal investigations and courtroom evidence. Just as in the research of Podder et al., in criminal case investigations, latent fingerprint identification is commonly employed. The key lies in the selection of multifunctional nanocomposite powders. These advanced materials offer the dual advantages of excellent loading capacity and high specific surface area, which can effectively solve the problems in fingerprint detection and analysis. In addition, during the process of nanopowder manifestation, there are certain interactions among the particles. For instance, they can effectively be combined with fluorescent probes, thereby enhancing the fingerprint manifestation rate. Karsharma et al. studied the MoS2 suspension, noting that it has strong adsorption, clear lines, stable display, and good applicability and can effectively reveal hidden handprints. Concurrently, Yadav et al. revealed the distinct advantages of quantum dot nanomaterials, including exceptional reproducibility and cost-effective production. These materials, characterized by tunable surface chemistry and biocompatibility, enable the development of fingerprint detection systems with high sensitivity and minimal background interference. Because of their remarkable physicochemical properties, TMD nanomaterials have become important available materials for synthesizing quantum dots. The new trace imaging powder is mainly composed of TMD and can be used as a bioluminescent probe. It reveals fresh mixed fingerprints on various surfaces (permeable/nonpermeable, smooth/rough) under specific light wavelengths. Furthermore, it can resist background interference; thus, it is an ideal fingerprint visualization material and has high research significance for improving the accuracy of trace detection.

The research on the preparation of TMD materials has a history of several decades, but the defects of high production difficulty, insufficient purity of the products, and high production cost have not been solved. In previous studies, due to the limitations of synthesis methods and costs, PdSe2 has not been industrially mass-produced. PdSe2 is mainly used in the laboratory stage. Marinova et al. studied a self-melting method for preparing PdSe2 single crystals, that is, using molten Se to react with Pd powder under high pressure. However, this method includes excessive energy consumption and operational hazards. Ohtani et al. showed a planetary ball-milling method to synthesize TMDs. The working principle is grinding elemental precursors (Pd powder, S, Se, and Te) in air at room temperature. The method presents significant practical limitations: rely on agate grinding vessels and high-precision instrumentation. Unfortunately, the preparation route not only increases the material costs, but also renders the process unsuitable for industrial-scale production. Lasek et al. studied the generating process of TMD materials, analyzed their monolayer growing on MBE, and made further study of their fundamental properties. This research revealed some unique monolayer properties of the materials and discussed the growth methods and vdW epitaxy. Based on the above facts, the paper proposes a simplified and cost-effective method for preparing high-quality PdSe2 materials.

This study focuses on how to prepare large-area PdSe2 films simply and reliably. Among them, chemical vapor deposition technology is a relatively mature method. Previous research by Fan et al. demonstrated a one-step selenation method for fabricating vertically stacked PdSe2/PtSe2 heterostructures and systematically analyzing the composition of different heterostructures. Zhou et al. proposed the controllable preparation of wafer-level (2-in.) 2D PdS2x Se2(1–x) nanofilms (NFs) on various substrates through the predeposited Pd nanofilm-assisted. Tai et al. proposed an innovative low-temperature selenification method for synthesizing 2D materials via the chemical vapor deposition method. This way could reduce energy consumption notably. In the previous studies, many aspects regarding the preparation principles and the chemical vapor deposition method. In this work, we have systematically refined and optimized the CVD methodology for PdSe2 nanofilm preparation. We designed four experiments to develop several CVD synthesis routes, characterized using EDS and Raman spectroscopy. , These analytical techniques confirmed the successful formation of the final material. There are also other methods for preparation, such as ultraviolet irradiation, etc. In this experiment, we not only adopted the existing chemical vapor deposition (CVD) technology, but also optimized the preparation process to enhance the repeatability and scalability.

Result and Discussion

Physical and chemical solvothermal methods are important preparation methods used in daily research. In previous research, chemical vapor deposition is the most common and simple method. The following are four types of PdSe2 prepared by the chemical vapor deposition method, as shown in Figure .

PdSe2 product preparation flowchart. (a) Chemical vapor deposition reaction flowchart; (b, c) FTO PdSe2 on base 2 growth and formation process of thin film.

First method: (1) PdCl2 solution preparation: dissolve PdCl2 powder in deionized water with stirring and ultrasonication to form a homogeneous dark solution. (2) Substrate coating: Apply the solution onto precleaned FTO glass (plasma-treated or solvent-cleaned) using a rubber dropper, dry at 60 °C for 5 min on a heating plate. (3) High-temperature deposition: Place Se powder (270 °C, 30 min) and PdCl2-coated FTO (400 °C, 30 min) in designated zones (∼16 cm separation), purge the tube furnace with Ar gas (5–10 min) prior to heating, and maintain an Ar atmosphere throughout the reaction. (4) Cool to room temperature, terminate the Ar flow, and take out the sample.

Second method: (1) The method is the same as above for preparing the PdCl2 solution. (2) The method is the same as above; the PdCl2 solution is spin-coated on the FTO conductive substrate. (3) The FTO conductive substrate coated with the PdCl2 solution is heated and dried. (4) Weigh a certain amount of SeO2 and place it in zone 2 (350 °C, 30 min). The FTO substrate of the PdCl2 solution is placed at zone 1 (400 °C, 30 min), there is no need to place the substrate in the deposition area. (5) Take out the sample.

Third method: (1) The method is the same as above for preparing a PdCl2 solution. (2) The method is the same as above; the PdCl2 solution is spin-coated on the FTO conductive substrate. (3) The method is the same as above for heating and drying the FTO conductive substrate coated with PdCl2 solution. (4) Weigh a certain amount of SeO2, dissolve it in an alcohol solution (ultrasonic treatment), use a dropper to take the SeO2-ethanol solution on FTO, heat and dry it, and place it in b zone 2 (400 °C, 30 min). (5) After the temperature drops to room temperature, take out the sample.

Fourth method: (1) Prepare a palladium film by magnetron sputtering to deposit a thin layer of palladium on the FTO conductive substrate. (2) Weigh a certain amount of selenium powder and place it in area 1 (at 270 °C for 30 min), and place the Pd film on the FTO conductive substrate in area 2 (at 400 °C for 30 min). (3) Turn on the heating device to room temperature, then remove the sample.

Raman spectroscopy technology was systematically employed to detect the characteristic peaks and purity of synthesized PdSe2 films (Figure ). Due to the short scanning time of the samples, the smoothness of the spectral curve is limited, and the contours of the characteristic peaks are relatively rough. In the spectrum, the Eg and Ag characteristic peaks correspond to the vibration modes of palladium (Pd) and selenium (Se) in the product PdSe2, respectively. By analyzing the relationship between peak position and peak intensity, PdSe2’s generation can be preliminarily determined. This result is basically consistent with the theoretical chemical calculation result of PdSe2. Therefore, we can confirm that the main phase of the product is PdSe2. However, the element proportions vary in products from different preparation methods, possibly due to defects during the film preparation process. For instance, factors such as spin coating speed, solution concentration, substrate treatment method, and annealing conditions may affect the composition distribution and microstructure of the product. Figure (a–d) are the optical photos of the products prepared by four different methods. The Raman spectra of the four preparation methods did not differ (the Eg and Ag peaks were almost consistent), indicating the products were the same substance (Figure e).

(a–d) Optical micrographs of PdSe2 products prepared by four different methods; (e) corresponding Raman spectra of these as-fabricated samples.

In this experiment, a PdCl2 solution was used as the precursor, and the structural characteristics of the reaction products under different conditions were explored by adjusting its concentration. As shown in Figure a, when analyzing the films of PdCl2 solutions of different concentrations, Raman analysis revealed that peak positions and heights remained stable despite increasing concentration, which indicates a minimal impact on phase composition within a certain range. However, in the step with a concentration of 0.05 mg/mL, the spectral results showed abnormalities and the characteristic peaks exhibited significant deviations. These discrepancies are likely due to inconsistent operations during the rotation coating process or environmental factors impacting subsequent annealing. The observed spectral anomalies may indicate incomplete product formation or unintended structural modifications at this specific concentration threshold. In addition, in the sample with a concentration of 0.1 mg/mL, the heights of the two main characteristic peaks were almost the same, which shows significant differences from the peak intensity distribution under other concentration conditions. It might be caused by improper selection of the detection point, incomplete local reaction of the film or residual precursors, impurity contamination, and other reasons. Therefore, the experiment needs to be optimized in the following aspects: (1) precisely control the coating parameters to ensure the uniformity of the film deposition, (2) strictly control the annealing conditions to ensure complete reaction conversion, and (3) control the reaction time and temperature. Implementing the above measures is of great significance for achieving the quality of products under different concentration gradients.

Figure b presents the Raman spectroscopy results of the product films under different laser intensities. , This indicates that the positions and shapes of the characteristic peaks of the products are independent of the laser intensities. Among them, when the light intensity is set to 50%, the spectral signal is the clearest, the intensity of the characteristic peak is moderate, and the background interference is low, so the detection effect is the best. However, no obvious characteristic peak appears when the light intensity is increased to 100% because of the high laser power; the local temperature of the thin film rises sharply, and the material structure may be destroyed or directly broken down, so the effective Raman scattering signal can not be obtained. The experimental results show that choosing an appropriate laser intensity can effectively protect the samples. Among them, an intensity of 50% is the optimal value for this test.

Figure b presents the photocurrent response test results of the PdSe2 film under different light intensities. The intermittent lighting experiment (5 s interval) revealed a pronounced photoresponse in PdSe2 films, demonstrating a strong positive correlation between photocurrent intensity and incident light intensity. As the light intensity increases, the current signal generated by the film is significantly enhanced. Conversely, when the light intensity decreases, the current response also weakens accordingly. This linear response characteristic is satisfactory. It indicates the photosensitivity of the PdSe2 films. As the light intensity increases, this difference also shows an upward trend, indicating the higher photoelectric conversion efficiency of the film under strong light irradiation. Besides, the excitation and transport of carriers are more effective. In this period, regardless of how the lighting conditions changed, the current signal remained basically stable under the same light intensity, without any obvious attenuation or fluctuation. The relatively high dark current observed in this study may be attributed to the following aspects: (1) The intrinsic electronic structure and conductivity of PdSe2 may lead to a certain background current under dark conditions; (2) The surface defects and imperfect crystallinity of the as-prepared samples could induce charge carrier recombination and leakage current; (3) The interface contact between the active material and the substrate may also contribute to the relatively high dark current. Conclusion

The study shows that all experimental precursors can effectively produce PdSe2 films with acceptable purity and strong photoelectric response. As the experiment proves, chemical vapor deposition (CVD) is an ideal approach for the large-scale, continuous, and high-precision synthesis of PdSe2 films. However, there are still many uncontrollable biases in the experiment. The results of various microscopic analysis techniques can prove the effectiveness of the 4 methods listed in the article. In the tested light intensity range (10–50 mW cm–2), the response current increases exponentially with light intensity, and the products show ideal optical properties such as high sensitivity and stability. In the realm of contemporary fingerprint optical detection, a diverse array of methods has emerged, with the technology steadily maturing. A series of progresses has led to significantly enhanced display effects and increasingly convenient instrumentation. However, there are still some aspects that need to be improved and broken through in the future; developing new imaging materials is one of the fields with great research prospects. If this material can be mass-produced and put into application, it will be a major leap in the image technology of public security trace examination. Waiting for this technology to be applied in police investigations, these intelligent systems could revolutionize forensic technology, greatly improving the efficiency and accuracy of criminal probes.
