Public Executive Summary
The HERO coating was developed to protect refractory alloys for application in the harsh hot
section of the turbine engine environment addressing ARPA-E ULTIMATE Project Topic 2: Coating
Development. The effort was both innovative in its utilization of high entropy rare earth oxides
as well as transformational in our approach: holistic design of a single layer
thermal/environmental barrier coating (T/EBC) for refractory alloys, with an excellent coefficient
of thermal expansion (CTE) match to the alloy substrate, chemical compatibility with the
underlying alloy, low oxidant permeability, stability in combustion environments, low thermal
conductivity, thermal shock resistance, and resistance to degradation by siliceous debris (calcium
magnesium alumino-silicates-CMAS). The high entropy rare earth oxide approach enables two
critical coating properties: tailoring CTE match to the substrate and, most significantly,
substantially reduced thermal conductivity. The summation of these proposed coating
capabilities goes well beyond the technical requirements specified in the ULTIMATE program
objectives, and additionally is essential for a successful first stage turbine blade application.
 Our approach was to identify HERO compositions that simultaneously satisfied the multiple
(and competing) coating needs that go well beyond ARPA-E objectives. The synergistic
integration between density functional theory (DFT), machine learning (ML) and
synthesis/characterization allowed us to explore the compositional space of multicomponent
HEROs and identify coating candidates for targeted experimental synthesis and CALPHAD
modeling while avoiding trial-and-error methods. ML, CALPHAD and DFT-guided composition
optimization significantly accelerated the development process and increased the success rate
of overcoming the critical development challenge of coating oxidant permeability. A suite of
coating validation testing including high-temperature air exposures, high-velocity steam and
CMAS stability, thermal conductivity, and thermal expansion matching, was used in parallel to
identify promising compositions and validate coating manufacturing techniques, also
accelerating coating development. Two manufacturing routes were explored in parallel to
optimize properties already mentioned. Air plasma spray with novel HERO feedstock and slurry
processing were both utilized for optimization of coating performance and cost. Our team of two
experienced computational scientists, two experts in coating properties, and two specialists in
manufacturing ensured that design and manufacture of HERO coatings enabled a successful
solution for protecting refractory alloys for use in first stage turbine blade applications. Our
partnership with the Commonwealth Center for Advanced Manufacturing and advice from Rolls-
Royce North America both provide pathways for technology to market transition.
 The project focused on Y2O3-Er2O3-Yb2O3 base compositions with additions of Nd2O3,
La2O3, Ho2O3 and/or CeO2 both above and below the solubility limits in the base three component
mixture. Increasing the fraction of Y2O3 in the coating benefits economic feasibility as it is the
least expensive rare earth oxide. The thermal expansion coefficient of the base three component
composition showed a good match to Nb-base alloy C-103. Nd2O3 and Ho2O3 additions raised
the thermal expansion by about 0.5 ppm/K showing some tunability to match other higher
thermal expansion refractory alloys. The thermal conductivity of Y2O3 was measured as 10 W/(m
K) and can be reduced as low as 1 W/(m K) via additions of additional RE elements (as oxides) of
different size, mass, equilibrium crystal structure. Once the oxides exceed their solubility limit,
further reductions in thermal conductivity are observed. All rare earth oxides (RE 2O3) are

extremely stable in high temperature water vapor found in combustion products. Nd 2O3
additions improved CMAS resistance due to rapid nucleation of a protective Nd-stabilized Ca
apatite phase. Sintering C-103 buttons in a thick three component HERO powder bed was
successful in limiting catastrophic oxidation for exposures at 1500C in air 100h. Dense, adherent
coatings were developed for both atmospheric plasma spray and slurry-based coating methods.
Single layer coatings also benefit economics of the system by reducing complexity of the material
system. Coating cracking at sharp radii remained problematic. An oxygen diffusion barrier will
be needed to eliminate catastrophic oxidation in the event of top-coat HERO cracks at sharp
features. The single layer coating design methodology demonstrated that all desired properties
excluding oxygen protection at sharp radii cracks were met. The resulting technology is of benefit
for higher temperature, more efficient turbine engines for power generation and aero
propulsion.

Accomplishments and Objectives

This award allowed the University of Virginia to demonstrate a number of key objectives toward
development of high temperature coatings for refractory metal alloys. The focus of the project
was on developing a single layer environmental/thermal barrier coating suitable for use of
refractory alloys in hot-section turbine engine environments with significantly higher upper use
temperatures and improved efficiencies relative to state-of-the-art nickel base superalloys and
yttria stabilized zirconia thermal barrier coatings.

Project Activities
 The project focused on Y2O3-Er2O3-Yb2O3 base compositions with additions of Nd2O3,
La2O3, Ho2O3 and/or CeO2 both above and below the solubility limits in the base three component
mixture. Increasing the fraction of Y2O3 in the coating benefits economic feasibility as it is the
least expensive rare earth oxide. The thermal expansion coefficient of the base three component
composition showed a good match to Nb-base alloy C-103. Nd2O3 and Ho2O3 additions raised
the thermal expansion by about 0.5 ppm/K showing some tunability to match other higher
thermal expansion refractory alloys. The thermal conductivity of Y2O3 was measured as 10 W/(m
K) and was reduced to values as low as 2 W/(m K) via additions of additional RE elements (as
oxides) of different size, mass, equilibrium crystal structure. Once the oxides exceed their
solubility limit, further reductions in thermal conductivity to about 1 W/(m K) are observed with
second phase formation. All rare earth oxides (RE2O3) are extremely stable in high temperature
water vapor found in combustion products, showing non-measurable recession after 100h
exposure to water vapor at 250 m/s and 1500C. Nd2O3 additions improved CMAS resistance due
to rapid nucleation of a protective Nd-stabilized Ca apatite phase. Sintering C-103 buttons in a
thick three component HERO powder bed was successful in limiting catastrophic oxidation for
exposures at 1500C in air for 100h. No interface reaction between the alloy and RE2O3 was
observed however, internal oxidation to form HfO2 was observed. Dense, adherent coatings
were developed for both atmospheric plasma spray and slurry-based coating methods. Single
layer coatings also benefit economics of the system by reducing complexity of the material
system. Coating cracking at sharp radii remained problematic. An oxygen diffusion barrier will
be needed to eliminate catastrophic oxidation in the event of HERO top-coat cracks at sharp
features. The single layer coating design methodology demonstrated that all desired properties
excluding oxygen protection at sharp radii cracks were met. The resulting technology is of benefit
for higher temperature, more efficient turbine engines for power generation and aero
