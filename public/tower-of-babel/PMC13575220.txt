Bearing characteristics of modified suction caissons under long-term horizontal cyclic loading
Authors: Xingzheng Zhou, Lunbo Luo, Weihuan Zhuang, Quanhong Chen, Qiming Song, Dayong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Model tests are carried out to study the bearing characteristics of modified suction caissons (MSC) under long-term horizontal cyclic loading. The influences of the cyclic loading frequency, combination of two kinds of vertical loading and cyclic loading on the MSC horizontal displacement, cumulative rotation angle, pore water pressure inside the caisson and stiffness are obtained. The experimental results show that the horizontal displacement of MSC increases with the increase of loading frequency. Under different loading frequencies, the MSC shows an upward trend in the vertical direction, and under the same loading cycle, the upward displacement of MSC decreases with the increase of loading frequency. The maximum water pressure both inside the internal compartment and the external structure accumulates with the increase of the loading cycles. As the vertical load on the lid increases, the horizontal displacement of the MSC decreases, its overall stiffness improves, the rotation angle of the caisson is reduced, and the recovery of this rotation angle is enhanced. When the number of cycles is greater than 2000, the rotation angle of MSC increases rapidly. Under the condition of multistage cyclic loading, the stiffness of MSC gradually decreases with the increase in the number of cycles.

Introduction

The world is facing the crisis of the depletion of traditional fossil energy, such as coal and oil. Countries around the world are gradually transitioning to a multi-energy structure, forming an energy pattern that is dominated by fossil fuels, supplemented by renewable energy and coexisting with new energy. Developing offshore wind energy attracts extensive attention around the world due to the advantages of abundant wind power, no visual and noise pollution, and no occupation of farmland. China is rich in offshore wind energy. In December 2024, the cumulative installed capacity of offshore wind in China reached 37.7 GW. Developing offshore wind energy is one of the important ways to achieve the “carbon peaking and carbon neutrality goals”.

Offshore wind power constitutes a critical component in the global transition to renewable energy, in which the service of offshore wind turbine foundation is subjected to horizontal cyclic loads such as wind and waves for a long time, which will lead to the accumulation of pore water pressure in the soil and the decrease of soil strength1, and then the rotation angle of the caisson increases and the bearing capacity of the foundation decreases2. Therefore, the cyclic bearing capacity and cumulative deformation characteristics of offshore wind power foundations should be clarified to ensure the safe operation of offshore wind turbines. A suction caisson is usually made of steel, open-ended at the bottom and closed at the top. The suction caisson has the advantages of fast installation, cost saving, and reuse3. Suction caissons have been widely used as foundations in mooring floating offshore oil and gas platforms4. Recently, suction caissons have been extended to fixed structures such as offshore wind towers and converter stations in China.

In order to improve the horizontal bearing capacity of suction caisson, Li et al.5,6 put forward a new form of suction caisson ---modified suction caisson (MSC) as shown in Fig. 1, and verified the reliability and practicability of MSC through a series of model tests7–10. The skirted suction caisson is an innovative offshore foundation derived from the conventional suction caisson concept. It features a structural enhancement—a short outer skirt attached to the main caisson. Compared to traditional suction caissons with equivalent steel usage, the skirted design significantly reduces penetration resistance while increasing horizontal bearing capacity.

Domestic and foreign scholars have carried out a series of studies on the horizontal cyclic bearing characteristics of suction foundation11–14, but there are few related studies.

Cox et al.15 found that the stiffness of a suction foundation increases logarithmically with the number of loading cycles in the case of complete drainage. Nielsen16 et al. carried out cyclic loading tests on suction foundations under different loading frequencies in saturated dense sand, and found that drainage conditions will have an important impact on the test results. The higher the loading frequency, the higher the excess pore water pressure in the soil, so that the foundation has a greater cumulative rotation angle. Zhu17 studied the bearing capacity characteristics, cumulative deformation law, and stiffness variation law of suction foundation under long-term cyclic loading in dry sand by conducting laboratory model tests. In this test, a sinusoidal load with a frequency of 0.107 Hz was applied to the horizontal load, and the number of cycles per working condition was 10,000. The test results show that the cyclic cumulative deformation of the suction foundation is the largest when the cyclic load waveform is asymmetric bidirectional loading. It is also found that the cyclic stiffness of the foundation changes little with the increase of the number of cycles, and the formula for predicting the cumulative deformation of the suction foundation in the whole life cycle is proposed. Chen et al.18 found that the anti-pulling capacity of suction foundation under cyclic loading is 72% ~ 86% of that under monotonic non-drainage loading. On the other hand, the amplitude of cyclic loading and the number of cycles have a significant effect on the bearing capacity of the foundation19. Hung et al.20 carried out a series of 1 g model tests, and the number of load cycles applied was up to 104 times. It was found that the cumulative vertical displacement of the foundation increased with the increase of load cycles and load amplitude. Shen21 studied the variation characteristics of pore pressure around the suction foundation under cyclic loading by finite element numerical simulation. The results show that the accumulation of pore pressure in the shallow soil outside the suction foundation under cyclic uplift load is significant, which reduces the effective stress of the soil and weakens the friction resistance between the inner side and soil, which may cause the transformation of the failure mode of the suction foundation. The amplitude and period of cyclic load will affect the size and distribution of pore pressure response. The accumulation of pore pressure in the shallow soil outside the suction foundation under cyclic uplift load is significant.

The offshore wind turbine is subjected to cyclic loads such as waves for a long time, resulting in cyclic cumulative deformation and stiffness changes of the foundation, thus making the natural frequencies of the foundation and superstructure change. The change of natural frequencies of foundation and superstructure will change the dynamic characteristics of offshore wind turbines under the combined action of wind, wave, current, and impeller rotation. Therefore, attention should be paid to the study of bearing and deformation characteristics of suction foundation under long-term cyclic load. Based on the self-developed wind power foundation loading system, the model test was carried out to study the cumulative deformation (displacement of loading point, cumulative rotation angle, vertical displacement of foundation), stiffness variation characteristics, and internal pore pressure variation law of MSC under long-term wave cyclic load. The test results have important reference significance for the design of offshore wind power foundations.

Soil preparation and cyclic loading system

Natural marine sand collected from the Golden Beach of Qingdao of East China, was used in the experiments. The physical properties of the sand, which were obtained from standard tests, are listed in Table 2. And its relative density is 91%.

According to the particle size distribution curve shown in Fig. 2, the fine sand used is poorly graded with diameters of all grains less than 0.37 mm and greater than 0.075 mm. It may be regarded as relatively uniform, so that the testing conditions can be consistent. Steel model box length, width, and height of 1.0, 1.0, and 0.8 m. Our previous model test and numerical simulation results for the horizontal bearing capacity of the MSC have confirmed that the size of the model box can eliminate the influence of the boundary effect on the test process5.

The preparation methods of the saturated sand foundation in the model box are as follows: Firstly, a 10 cm thick gravel cushion is laid at the bottom of the model box. A layer of geotextile is laid on the gravel cushion as an inverted filter to prevent fine sand particles from being washed away by water during drainage. After the geotextile is laid, water is injected into the model box at a height of 50 cm. Then evenly spread the test sand into the model box. Before sand sowing, a 2 mm sieve was first passed to filter out impurities such as shells. Sand sowing by the rain method. During the sand sowing process, water is continuously injected into the model box to ensure that the water level in the model box is higher than the sand surface. When the height of the sand in the model box is 60 cm, the sand stops, and then the drainage valve at the bottom of the model box is opened. At this time, the downward seepage occurs in the sand. and the effective stress in the soil began to increase, which accelerates the consolidation of the sand in the model box. When the water level drops to 2 cm from the top of the model box, close the drain valve at the bottom of the model box. Water is injected into the model box again, and the above steps are repeated twice; saturated sand foundation preparation is completed.

Relative density is an important parameter to determine the strength and deformation characteristics of sand. Before each test, the soil in the range of 1.5 times the height of the main bucket below the sand surface is loosened as a whole to ensure that the relative density of the sand in each test model box is equal. At the same time, the water injection valve at the bottom of the model box is opened, which makes the soil loose. When the water level in the model box is higher than 20 cm on the sand surface, stop injecting water into the model box. Then open the drainage valve at the bottom of the model box to reduce the water level in the model box and accelerate the consolidation of the soil. During the drainage process, the sand surface of the model box is flattened. When the water level drops to 2 cm above the sand surface, the drainage valve is closed.

The permeability coefficient of sand is large, so the consolidation time of sand in the model box is short. In order to explore the optimal consolidation time of test sand, the horizontal monotonic loading test of MSC under different consolidation times was carried out22. The test results show that the optimal consolidation time of sand is 4 h, which is set as the standard of consolidation time of test foundation.

Test procedure

The MSC is first penetrated into the sand to a certain depth under the action of self-weight. During the process of self-weight penetration, the level bubble at the lid of the MSC is observed at all times to ensure that the MSC is level and that the drainage holes in the lid of the MSC remain open during the self-weight penetration process.

After the self-weight penetration is completed, the pressure sensor and the vacuum pump are respectively connected with the holes on the lid of the MSC, and a closed space is formed in the internal compartment. Then open the vacuum pump, extract the internal air of MSC, and MSC continues to penetrate under the action of suction. During the suction-assisted installation, the pumping rate of the vacuum pump is 1.25 L/min. The vacuum system completed the installation by pumping out the water and air encased in the suction caisson. When the MSC penetration to a certain depth, the air inside the MSC is completely extracted, and the PVC pipe connecting the MSC and the vacuum pump is filled with water. Since then, the penetration has been achieved by extracting the internal water body of the MSC. When sand particles appear in the PVC water pipe, the internal soil plug is completely in contact with the lower edge of the lid of the MSC. At this time, the suction penetration of MSC terminated, and MSC reached the maximum penetration depth. After the penetration is completed, the horizontal monotonic bearing characteristics of MSC are studied to determine its ultimate bearing capacity, and the bending moment Mult of the foundation in the limit state is obtained. In the horizontal cyclic loading test of MSC, the drain hole on the lid of the internal compartment and the external structure t remain closed at all times.

It should be noted that the bearing characteristics of skirted suction caissons obtained through model tests can provide a theoretical basis for the design and application of prototype suction caissons.

Results and discussions

Displacement variation law of MSC

Figure 5 shows the variation law of the loading point of the MSC and the vertical displacement of the front and back of the MSC lid with the number of cycles under different loading frequencies. The horizontal displacement of the MSC and the vertical displacement of the caisson lid varied significantly under different loading frequencies. The horizontal displacement of MSC increases with the increase in the cyclic loading frequency. And the sinking tendency of the front side of the caisson lid under different loading frequencies. The back side of the caisson lid shows an upward pull-up trend with the increase of the number of cycles under different loading frequencies, and the uplift displacement increases with the increase of the number of cycles. Figure 5d shows the average vertical displacements of the front and back of the caisson lid under different cyclic loading frequencies. Under different loading frequencies, MSC shows an upward trend in the vertical direction, and under the same number of cycles, the uplift displacement of MSC decreases with the increase in the loading frequency of the cyclic load. It can be seen that the change of loading frequency will not affect the movement trend of MSC in the horizontal and vertical directions, but only affect the movement displacement value of it in the horizontal and vertical directions.

Figure 6 shows the variation law of horizontal displacement of MSC and vertical displacement at the front and rear sides of the caisson lid with the number of cycles under two vertical loads. The vertical load on MSC can effectively control the deformation of it under cyclic loading. At the end of the test, the displacement of the loading point of the MSC with vertical load is 71% less than that without vertical load. This remarkable reduction in lateral movement primarily stems from the enhanced soil-foundation interaction. The superimposed vertical load increases the confining pressure around and beneath the MSC skirts, thereby strengthening the sand through an increase in effective stress. This strengthened soil provides greater resistance against the development of a passive failure wedge in front of the caisson and reduces the tendency for soil yielding beneath the lid, which are the primary mechanisms for permanent lateral displacement.

Figure 6b–d show the vertical displacements of the front and rear sides of MSC under different vertical loads, and the variation rules of their average values with the number of cycles, respectively. The front side of the MSC lid will settle with the increase of loading cycle under the action of self-weight; When V/Vult=0.20, the front side of the MSC lid shows an upward trend with the increase of loading cycle. The change of the front side of the MSC lid in different vertical movement trends is most obvious in the first 1000 cycles. It can be seen from Fig. 6c that under different vertical loads, the rear side of MSC shows an upward trend. Under the same number of cycles, the uplift displacement of the rear side of the MSC decreases with the increase of vertical load.

Figure 6d shows the variation law of the average vertical displacement of the front and rear sides of the MSC lid with the number of cycles under different vertical loads. In the vertical direction, MSC shows an upward trend with the increase of the number of cycles. And at the same number of cycles, the uplift displacement difference of MSC under different vertical loads is small. It can be seen that different vertical loads have a significant effect on the movement trend of MSC along the horizontal direction, but have little effect on the movement trend along the vertical direction.

At the end of the test, the settlement on the front side of the MSC lid increased with the value of ξb; the uplift on the rear side of the MSC lid decreased with the value of ξb.

Figure 7d shows the variation law of the average vertical displacement of the front and rear sides of the lid of MSC under multistage loadings. Under multistage loadings, MSC settlement occurs in the vertical direction with the increase of the number of cycles. When the cyclic loading mode is unidirectional loading, the final settlement of MSC is the largest. When MSC is subjected to bi-directional cyclic loading (ξb = -0.5 and-0.7), MSC settlement reaches the maximum value when the number of cycles reaches 3000 times, and then the displacement in the vertical direction decreases. When MSC is subjected to unidirectional cyclic loading, MSC continues to increase in the vertical direction with the increase of the number of cycles under each level of cyclic loading, and the maximum settlement of MSC occurs at the last level of load. Under the action of bi-directional cyclic loading, the settlement of MSC increases with the increase in the number of cycles. The maximum settlement value is reached when the ξc value increases to 0.8, after which the settlement remains stable.

Figure 11 shows the law of dimensionless cumulative rotation angle of MSC with the number of cycles under multistage cyclic loading. The dimensionless accumulated deformation of MSC firstly increases with the number of cycles, and when the number of cycles reaches 2000, the dimensionless accumulated deformation of MSC reaches its maximum value at this time. Since then, the dimensionless cumulative rotation angle of the foundation decreases rapidly with the increase in the number of cycles. In the initial low-amplitude loading stages, the soil surrounding and inside the MSC experiences primarily elastic. The excess pore water pressure generated is low and dissipates quickly, allowing the soil skeleton to re-stabilize. This leads to very limited net cumulative rotation. The fitted minimum represents the point where the accumulation of permanent plastic deformation begins to outweigh the recoverable elastic deformation, marking the onset of significant, irreversible degradation. Shortly after this point, as ξb increases, the cyclic loads are large enough to cause progressive particle rearrangement, accelerated pore pressure accumulation, and a consequent rapid reduction in soil effective stress and shear strength, leading to the sharp increase in cumulative rotation.

While the proposed models (Eqs. 4–6) effectively characterize the cumulative deformation of MSC in saturated sand under long-term horizontal cyclic loading, their applicability is subject to the specific conditions of this study. The predictive accuracy of these models for foundations embedded in different soil types, such as soft clay where time-dependent consolidation and pore pressure dissipation differ significantly, or under multi-directional loading conditions that may alter the soil failure mechanism, remains unverified and should be a focus of future research. Conclusions

The horizontal displacement of MSC increases with the increase of cyclic loading frequency, and the displacement of the loading point of MSC decreases by 71% when the lid has a vertical load compared with that without a vertical load.
