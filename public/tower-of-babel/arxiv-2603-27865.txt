On the Dirichlet-Neumann operator for nearly spherical domains
Pietro Baldi, Vesa Julin, Domenico Angelo La Manna

Abstract




Abstract.

We consider the Dirichlet-Neumann operator for a nearly spherical domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
and prove sharp analytic and tame estimates in Sobolev class.
The novelty of this paper concerns technical improvements,
the most important of which are the independence of the analyticity radius on the high norms
and the regularity loss of one in the elevation function.
These properties are expectable but nontrivial to prove.
The result is obtained by introducing local charts and a convenient class
of non-isotropic Sobolev spaces of high, possibly fractional tangential regularity
and integer, limited regularity in the normal direction.

1
Introduction and main results

The Dirichlet-Neumann operator is a nonlocal operator
appearing in many free boundary problems coming from physical models,
especially in fluid dynamics, in shape optimization and inverse problems.
In this paper we consider the Dirichlet-Neumann operator for nearly spherical domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
and we improve some recent results about its analytical dependence on the shape of the domain
and tame (i.e., linear in the highest order norms) estimates in Sobolev class.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open, bounded set, and assume that its boundary
\partial\Omega

is the graph of a function
h
on the unit sphere,

\partial\Omega=\{x(1+h(x)):x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\},\quad\ {\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|x|=1\},

(1.1)

with
1+h>0
. We call
h:{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
the height, or elevation, function.
We also define

\gamma:{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\to\partial\Omega,\quad\ \gamma(x)=x(1+h(x)),

(1.2)

which is a bijection of the unit sphere onto the surface
\partial\Omega
.
Given a function
\psi:{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we consider the Laplace problem
with Dirichlet datum
\psi(x)
at the point
\gamma(x)
on the boundary, that is,

\Delta\Phi=0\ \ \text{in }\Omega,\quad\ \Phi=\psi\circ\gamma^{-1}\ \ \text{on }\partial\Omega,\quad\ \Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(1.3)

Under suitable assumptions on
h
and
\psi
, the solution
\Phi
is unique.
We define
G(h)\psi:{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as the normal derivative of
\Phi

at the point
\gamma(x)
of the boundary
\partial\Omega
, i.e.,

G(h)\psi(x)=\langle(\nabla\Phi)(\gamma(x)),\nu_{\Omega}(\gamma(x))\rangle,\quad x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1},

(1.4)

where
\nu_{\Omega}(p)
is the unit normal vector to the boundary
\partial\Omega

at the point
p\in\partial\Omega
pointing outwards,
and
\langle\,,\,\rangle
is the usual scalar product of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The map
(h,\psi)\to G(h)\psi
is called the Dirichlet-Neumann operator.
It is linear in
\psi
, and it depends nonlinearly on
h
.

The analytic properties of the Dirichlet-Neumann and related operators
have been studied in the classical works
[
9
,
10
]
,
and then in the water wave literature,
especially for nearly flat unbounded domains (models for the ocean),
often including a normalizing factor in front of the right-hand side in (
1.4
):
see the relevant works
[
2
,
3
,
12
,
13
,
21
]
,
the recent papers
[
1
,
6
,
14
]
,
the articles
[
16
,
24
]
for nonzero vorticity,
and the many other references within.
For non-flat geometries, the literature is less abundant;
see the recent works
[
19
]
for the cone,

[
18
,
30
]
for the cylinder,

[
20
]
for the disc,
and
[
5
,
7
,
25
,
26
]
for the sphere.

The present paper deals with nearly spherical domains.
The main result is Theorem
1.1
, which is
set in the usual
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces
H^{s}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})

of functions defined on the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
We recall that the
H^{s}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
norm of a function
can be defined by localization, rectification and extension,
using a partition of unity of the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,
or by means of its orthogonal decomposition into spherical harmonics of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
These two definitions give equivalent norms
(
[
22
]
, Remark 7.6 in Section 7.3).
For the general theory of Sobolev spaces on open bounded domains and on smooth manifolds
we refer, e.g., to Lions-Magenes
[
22
]
,
Taylor
[
27
]
, Triebel
[
29
]
.
We denote, in short,

\|f\|_{s}:=\|f\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}.

(1.5)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s_{0},s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, with
s\geq 1/2
,
s_{0}>(n-1)/2
.
There exist
\delta,C_{0},C_{s}>0
such that,
for
h
in the set

U:=\{h\in H^{s_{0}+1}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}):\|h\|_{s_{0}+1}<\delta\}\cap H^{s+1}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}),

for
\psi\in H^{s+1}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
,
the function
G(h)\psi
satisfies

\displaystyle\|G(h)\psi\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|\psi\|_{s+1}+(\chi_{s>s_{0}})C_{s}(1+\|h\|_{s+1})\|\psi\|_{s_{0}+1},

(1.6)

where

(\chi_{s>s_{0}})=1
if
s>s_{0}
,

(\chi_{s>s_{0}})=0
if
s\leq s_{0}
.
The constants
\delta,C_{0}
are independent of
s
,
the constant
C_{s}
is increasing in
s
,
and all
\delta,C_{0},C_{s}
are independent of
h,\psi
.
Moreover, the map
h\to G(h)
is analytic from
U

(with the norm
\|\ \|_{s+1}+\|\ \|_{s_{0}+1}
)
to the space of bounded linear operators

\mathcal{L}(H^{s+1}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}),H^{s}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}))
.

In (
1.6
), the norms
\|h\|_{s+1}
and
\|\psi\|_{s+1}
appear linearly,
even if
G(h)\psi
depends nonlinearly on
h
:
for this reason inequality (
1.6
) is said to be a
tame
estimate.
We also prove (Theorem
1.2
) similar multilinear estimates
for the first and second derivative of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator with respect to the elevation function
h
.
Analogous multilinear estimates can also be proved for higher order derivatives.

Theorem 1.2
.

In the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
,
the first and second derivative of
G
with respect to
h

in direction
\eta
,
\eta_{1}
,
\eta_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\|G^{\prime}(h)[\eta]\psi\|_{s}\leq C_{0}\|\eta\|_{s_{0}+1}\|\psi\|_{s+1}+(\chi_{s>s_{0}})C_{s}(\|\eta\|_{s+1}+\|h\|_{s+1}\|\eta\|_{s_{0}+1})\|\psi\|_{s_{0}+1}

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle\|G^{\prime\prime}(h)[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}]\psi\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|\eta_{1}\|_{s_{0}+1}\|\eta_{2}\|_{s_{0}+1}\|\psi\|_{s+1}+(\chi_{s>s_{0}})C_{s}(\|\eta_{1}\|_{s+1}\|\eta_{2}\|_{s_{0}+1}

\displaystyle\quad\ +\|\eta_{1}\|_{s_{0}+1}\|\eta_{2}\|_{s+1}+\|h\|_{s+1}\|\eta_{1}\|_{s_{0}+1}\|\eta_{2}\|_{s_{0}+1})\|\psi\|_{s_{0}+1},

(1.8)

where
C_{s}
is increasing in
s

and
C_{0}
is independent of
s
.

Theorem
1.1
improves a similar result in
[
5
]
, where it is proved that

\|G(h)\psi\|_{H^{k-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})}\leq C(\|\psi\|_{H^{k+\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})}+\|h\|_{H^{k+1}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})}\|\psi\|_{H^{\frac{5}{2}}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})})

(1.9)

for all
h\in H^{k+1}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
with
\|h\|_{H^{3}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})}<\delta
,
all
\psi\in H^{k+\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
,
where
k\geq 2
is an integer, and
\delta,C
depend on
k

(see Theorem 5.12 of
[
5
]
).
Thus, Theorem
1.1
improves the result in
[
5
]

under these technical aspects:

•

the “loss of regularity” of the elevation function
h
,
that is, the difference between the Sobolev regularity of
h
in the right-hand side
and that of
G(h)\psi
in the left-hand side,
is 1 in (
1.6
)
and
3/2
in (
1.9
);

•

the constant
\delta
, which is the radius of the ball where analyticity and tame estimates hold,
is independent of the high regularity parameter
s
in Theorem
1.1
,
while
\delta
depends on
k
in
[

]
;

•

the regularity parameter

in (

)
is a real number, while

in (

) is an integer;

•

the constant

in front of

in (

) is independent of

,
while the constant
C
in (

) depends on

;

•

on the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}

is any real

and it is

in
[

]
;

•

Theorem

.

Strategy of the proof
.
The first part of our proof is rather classical:
we consider a change of variable
\tilde{\gamma}:B_{1}\to\Omega

that transforms (
1.3
) into

\mathrm{div}\,(P\nabla u)=0\ \ \text{in }B_{1},\quad\ u=\psi\ \ \text{on }{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1},\quad\ u\in H^{1}(B_{1}),

(1.10)

where
B_{1}
is the open unit ball of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

u=\Phi\circ\tilde{\gamma}
is the new unknown,
and
P
is a
n\times n
matrix depending on
\tilde{\gamma}
.
Note that (
1.10
) is a problem in a
fixed
(i.e., independent of
h
) domain.
As is observed in
[
5
]
, the Dirichlet-Neumann operator (
1.4
) can be re-written
in terms of
h
and
u
, see formula (
4.9
).
Then we prove that the solution
u
of (
1.10
) depends analytically on
h
,
by writing it as a power series
u=\sum u_{m}
of
h
and its derivatives,
see (
4.11
), (
4.12
), and estimating each term
u_{m}
of the series.
Such estimates are simple to obtain in low norm, see Lemma
4.3

and Proposition
4.4
.

The nontrivial part of the proof comes with the estimates in higher norms.
These estimates are not too difficult to obtain
if the goal is to prove estimate (
1.9
),
and, in fact, the proof of (
1.9
) in
[
5
]

is not long and it largely relies on classical results.
Here, however, we aim to prove Theorem
1.1
,
with all the technical improvements listed above,
and some new ingredient is required.

The fact that the loss of regularity for the elevation function
h
is 1, instead of
3/2
,
is obtained by using a diffeomorphism
\tilde{\gamma}

which, close to the boundary of
\Omega
,
is a right inverse of the trace operator.
This can be done in many ways; we find it convenient
to use the harmonic extension of
h
to the unit ball,
and to define
\tilde{\gamma}
through it, see (
4.1
).
As an alternative, one can use localization, rectification,
and an explicit construction with the Fourier transform, one for each local chart,
similarly as it is done (globally, i.e., without local charts)
in the flat case, for example, in
[
21
,
6
]
.

To prove that the radius
\delta
in Theorem
1.1

is independent of
s
is more demanding.
Estimate (
1.6
) is deduced from high regularity boundary estimates
for the solution
u
of problem (
1.10
),
which is the sum of the power series
\sum u_{m}
.
To estimate each term
u_{m}
of the series, a key ingredient is
a tame estimate with asymmetric constants for the Sobolev norm
of the product of two functions,
that is, an estimate of the form

\|fg\|_{s}\leq C_{s_{0}}\|f\|_{s}\|g\|_{s_{0}}+C_{s}\|f\|_{s_{0}}\|g\|_{s},

(1.11)

where
C_{s_{0}}
depends on
s_{0}
and it is independent of
s
,
while
C_{s}
also depends on
s
.
This inequality makes it possible to estimate
the high norm of any power series, say
\|\sum f^{m}\|_{s}
,
only assuming a condition of the form
\|f\|_{s_{0}}<\delta

on the low norm of
f
, with radius
\delta
independent of
s
.
Estimate (
1.11
) is well-known and commonly used
in the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})
,
where (
1.11
) is not difficult to prove
by using Fourier transform or Fourier series.
The same proof for the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
on the sphere,
instead, are hard to adapt to the analogous of Fourier series,
namely the series expansion of functions in spherical harmonics,
and this is one of the reasons for which we use
Sobolev norms defined by localization and rectification,
see (
2.18
), instead of spectral norms.
With the norms (
2.18
) of
H^{s}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
,
estimate (
1.11
) becomes simple to prove,
see (
2.50
).

The validity of estimate (
1.11
)
becomes a more serious issue for functions defined in the open ball
B_{1}
,
where Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(B_{1})
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
are defined as the spaces of the restrictions to
B_{1}

of the functions in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, or by intrinsic representation for integer
s

and then by interpolation (see Taylor’s
[
28
]

and Triebel’s
[
29
]
books).
The difficulty in proving (
1.11
) in
B_{1}

is related to the use of extension operators,
say
\mathtt{E}
, usually constructed by taking coefficients,
obtained by inverting a Van der Monde matrix,
that depend on (the integer part of) the high Sobolev index
s
.
This gives bounds in low and high Sobolev norms of the type

\|\mathtt{E}f\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}(B_{1})},\quad\ \|\mathtt{E}f\|_{H^{s_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}(B_{1})},

where the constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
both depend on
s
.
The fact that also
C^{\prime}
(in an estimate for the low
s_{0}
norms)
depends on
s
makes the validity of the asymmetric product estimate (
1.11
)
for Sobolev functions in the ball
B_{1}
uncertain, or, at least, highly nontrivial to prove.

We overcome this delicate issue by using localizations and introducing

non-isotropic
seminorms where the high, and possibly fractional, Sobolev regularity
is only taken in the tangential directions,
while in the normal direction only integer, limited
H^{1}
or
H^{2}
regularity is considered,
see (
3.6
).
This idea is very efficient, simple and more or less classical,
as it can be found in reference books like Triebel
[
29
, section 4.2.1]

and Taylor
[
27
, section 5.11]
,
and also, in the water wave literature, in Lannes
[
21
]
,
Coutand-Shkoller
[
11
]
, and other authors.
The use of non-isotropic seminorms for functions in
B_{1}

allows one to avoid estimating high order derivatives in the normal direction,
which is not necessary for analyzing the Dirichlet-Neumann operator
and is what ultimately causes the dependence of the constants by
s
.

.

Function spaces

In this section we collect some properties for both standard and non-isotropic versions
of Sobolev spaces of functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
.
To define these spaces, we follow the approach of Triebel’s book
[
29
]
.
The results of this subsection are all more or less classical,
but it is nontrivial to find a complete proof for all of them in literature.
The proofs are collected in the Appendix.

Sobolev spaces on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
On
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we consider the usual Sobolev spaces

H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):\|u\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty\},\quad\ \|u\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}\,d\xi,

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the space of tempered distributions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
and
\hat{u}
is the Fourier transform of
u
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We also consider the following non-isotropic version of those spaces,
where the last real variable
x_{n}
plays a distinguished role.
For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, denote
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
, so that
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
.
For
s,r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define

\displaystyle H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):\|u\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{r}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}\,d\xi=\|\Lambda^{\prime}_{r}u\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2},

(2.2)

where
\Lambda^{\prime}_{r}
is the Fourier multiplier of symbol
(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})^{\frac{r}{2}}
.
These spaces will be mainly used for
s\in\{0,1,2\}
and
r\geq 0
real.
They are used by Triebel, see
[
29
]
, Definition 4.2.1, page 218;
see also Lannes
[
21
]
, Definition 2.11.

Sobolev spaces on open domains
.
On open sets
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, following
[
29
]
,
Definition 1 in Section 3.2.2, we define

\displaystyle H^{s}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\Omega):\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=\inf\,\{\|v\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:v\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\ v|_{\Omega}=u\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle H^{s,r}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\Omega):\|u\|_{H^{s,r}(\Omega)}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s,r}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=\inf\,\{\|v\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:v\in H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\ v|_{\Omega}=u\},

with the convention that the infimum of the empty set is

.
In other words,

is the set of the restrictions to

,
and similarly for

.
We mainly deal with the case when

(2.5)

.

Local charts on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
For
\Gamma={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, the construction above can be more explicit
and well adapted to the spherical geometry.
Let
B_{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
be the open unit ball and unit sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
For
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, let

K_{0}=B_{1-\delta}(0),\quad K_{j}=B_{2\delta}(p_{j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,N,

be open balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
where the points
p_{j}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
are appropriately positioned to obtain the covering property

\overline{B}_{1}\subset\cup_{j=0}^{N}K_{j}
.
Fix a resolution of unity
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
,
where
\psi_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
C^{\infty}
function with
\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{j})\subset K_{j}
,
and
\sum_{j=0}^{N}\psi_{j}(x)=1
for all
x\in\overline{B}_{1}
.
Thus,

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(x)=1\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ 1-\delta\leq|x|\leq 1.

(2.7)

Let
\mathtt{R}_{j}
be a
n\times n
rotation matrix mapping the center
p_{j}
of the ball
K_{j}

into the “South Pole”
p_{0}:=(0,...,0,-1)
of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
Let
\mathtt{f}_{j}(x):=\mathtt{f}(\mathtt{R}_{j}x)
be the composition of the rotation
\mathtt{R}_{j}

with the function

\mathtt{f}:\mathbb{H}_{0}\to\mathbb{H}_{1},\quad\ \mathtt{f}(x):=\Big(-\frac{x^{\prime}}{x_{n}},1-|x|\Big),

(2.8)

where
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
, and
\mathbb{H}_{0}
,
\mathbb{H}_{1}

are the open half spaces

\mathbb{H}_{0}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:x_{n}<0\}
,

\mathbb{H}_{1}:=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:y_{n}<1\}
.
The function
\mathtt{f}
is a well defined, smooth bijection of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
onto
\mathbb{H}_{1}
.
Its inverse is the function

\mathtt{g}:\mathbb{H}_{1}\to\mathbb{H}_{0},\quad\ \mathtt{g}(y):=(1-y_{n})(1+|y^{\prime}|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(y^{\prime},-1),

(2.9)

because, if
\mathtt{f}(x)=y
, then
|x|=1-y_{n}
,
x^{\prime}=-x_{n}y^{\prime}
,
whence
(1-y_{n})^{2}=|x|^{2}=|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}=x_{n}^{2}(1+|y^{\prime}|^{2})
;
for
x_{n}<0
, one obtains
x=\mathtt{g}(y)
.
The map
\mathtt{g}
is a well defined, smooth bijection of
\mathbb{H}_{1}
onto
\mathbb{H}_{0}
.
Moreover
\mathtt{f}(\mathbb{H}_{0}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{0}

and
\mathtt{g}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{0})=\mathbb{H}_{0}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, where
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{0}
is the hyperplane

\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:y_{n}=0\}
.
Let
\mathtt{g}_{j}(y):=\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}\mathtt{g}(y)
.
Then

\mathtt{f}_{j}(\mathtt{g}_{j}(y))=y\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{H}_{1},\qquad\ \mathtt{g}_{j}(\mathtt{f}_{j}(x))=x\quad\forall x\in\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}\mathbb{H}_{0},

(2.10)

where
\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}\mathbb{H}_{0}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\mathtt{R}_{j}x\in\mathbb{H}_{0}\}=\{\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}z:z\in\mathbb{H}_{0}\}
,
and

\mathtt{f}_{j}((\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}\mathbb{H}_{0})\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{0},\quad\ \mathtt{g}_{j}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{0})=(\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}\mathbb{H}_{0})\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}.

(2.11)

For
\delta<\frac{1}{2}
, the ball
B_{2\delta}(p_{0})
is contained in the half space
\mathbb{H}_{0}
,
therefore
\mathtt{f}_{j}
is well defined in the ball
K_{j}
,
with bijective image

\mathtt{f}_{j}(K_{j})=\mathtt{f}(B_{2\delta}(p_{0}))=:A_{0}.

One has
\mathtt{f}_{j}(p_{j})=\mathtt{f}(p_{0})=0
and

\mathtt{f}_{j}(K_{j}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})=\mathtt{f}(B_{2\delta}(p_{0})\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})=A_{0}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{0}=D\times\{0\},

(2.12)

where

D:=\{y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}:(y^{\prime},0)\in A_{0}\}=\{y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}:|y^{\prime}|<r_{\delta}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}

(2.13)

is the ball of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
of radius
r_{\delta}=2\delta(1-\delta^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-2\delta^{2})^{-1}
.
Also,

\mathtt{g}_{j}(A_{0})=K_{j},\quad\ \mathtt{g}_{j}(D\times\{0\})=K_{j}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}.

(2.14)

Moreover, we define

E_{j}:=\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{j})\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\subset K_{j}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1},\quad F_{j}:=\{y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\mathtt{g}_{j}(y^{\prime},0)\in\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{j})\}\subset D,

(2.15)

and we note that

\mathtt{f}_{j}(E_{j})=F_{j}\times\{0\},\quad\ \mathtt{g}_{j}(F_{j}\times\{0\})=E_{j}.

(2.16)

Sobolev spaces on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
Given a function
u:{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, its Sobolev norm (
2.6
)
is defined by localization and rectification.

Localization
: one has
u=\sum_{j=1}^{N}u\psi_{j}
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,
and, for each
j
, the product
u\psi_{j}
vanishes in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\setminus\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{j})
;
recall that
\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{j})
is a compact subset of the open ball
K_{j}
.

Rectification
: by (
2.11
),

\mathtt{g}_{j}(\cdot,0)
maps
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
into
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
Hence both
u(\mathtt{g}_{j}(\cdot,0))
and
\psi_{j}(\mathtt{g}_{j}(\cdot,0))
are defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
, and

\psi_{j}(\mathtt{g}_{j}(y^{\prime},0))=0\quad\forall y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\setminus F_{j},

(2.17)

where
F_{j}
is the compact set defined in (
2.15
).
Then the Sobolev norm is defined by (
2.6
), that is,

\|u\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|(u\psi_{j})\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}.

(2.18)

We note that, unlike for a more general manifold,
in (
2.18
) the extension step is not required,
because
(u\psi_{j})\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}(\cdot,0)
is already defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
,
with compact support contained in
F_{j}
.
This is due to the geometry of
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
(invariance by rotations)
and to our choice of local charts
(
\mathtt{f}
gives the stereographical projection of the half sphere

{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{H}_{0}
onto the hyperplane
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{0}={\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times\{0\}
).

Transition maps
.
If two balls
K_{j},K_{\ell}
have nonempty intersection,
then the transition map
\mathtt{f}_{\ell}\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}

can be explicitly calculated using (
2.8
), (
2.9
)
and the basic property
|\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}x|=|x|
of rotation matrices.
One finds that

\mathtt{f}_{\ell}\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}(y)=(\mathtt{T}_{\ell j}(y^{\prime}),y_{n}),\quad\ \mathtt{T}_{\ell j}(y^{\prime})=-\frac{[\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(y^{\prime},-1)]^{\prime}}{[\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(y^{\prime},-1)]_{n}}

(2.19)

for all
y\in\mathtt{f}_{j}(K_{j}\cap K_{\ell})
.
Formula (
2.19
) shows the remarkable property that
the transition map
\mathtt{f}_{\ell}\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}
does not move the last variable
y_{n}
,
and it acts on
y^{\prime}
as a function independent of
y_{n}
.

Extension of local charts
.
To estimate the
H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
norms of products and compositions of functions,
it is convenient to extend
\mathtt{f}
,
\mathtt{g}
and
\mathtt{f}_{k}\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}

outside their original domains.
The first order Taylor expansion of
\mathtt{f}(x)
around
p_{0}
is
x-p_{0}
;
therefore we define

\tilde{\mathtt{f}}(x):=x-p_{0}+\varphi_{\delta}(x-p_{0})(\mathtt{f}(x)-x+p_{0})\quad\ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(2.20)

where
\varphi_{\delta}(x-p_{0}):=\varphi(\frac{|x-p_{0}|}{2\delta})

for some cut-off function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\varphi(t)=1
for
t\leq 1
,

\varphi(t)=0
for
t\geq 2
, and
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
.
Taking the half radius
\delta
of the balls
K_{j}
sufficiently small,
the function
\tilde{\mathtt{f}}
is a diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

because
|D\tilde{\mathtt{f}}(x)-I|\leq 1/2
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Also,
\tilde{\mathtt{f}}=\mathtt{f}
in
B_{2\delta}(p_{0})
.
We define
\tilde{\mathtt{g}}
as the inverse of
\tilde{\mathtt{f}}
in (
2.20
).
We also define
\tilde{\mathtt{f}}_{j}:=\tilde{\mathtt{f}}\circ\mathtt{R}_{j}

and
\tilde{\mathtt{g}}_{j}
as its inverse function.

Extension of transition maps
.
Concerning the transition map, of course

\tilde{\mathtt{f}}_{\ell}\circ\tilde{\mathtt{g}}_{j}

is an extension of (
2.19
);
however, it is convenient to introduce another, more explicit extension,
preserving the property of not moving
y_{n}
and acting on
y^{\prime}
independently on
y_{n}
.
The affine function
y^{\prime}\mapsto\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(y^{\prime},-1)=\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(0,-1)+\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(y^{\prime},0)

is defined for all
y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
, and its value at
y^{\prime}=0
is

\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(0,-1)=\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}p_{0}=\mathtt{R}_{\ell}p_{j}=\mathtt{R}_{\ell}p_{\ell}+\mathtt{R}_{\ell}(p_{j}-p_{\ell})=p_{0}+\mathtt{R}_{\ell}(p_{j}-p_{\ell}),

(2.21)

with
|\mathtt{R}_{\ell}(p_{j}-p_{\ell})|=|p_{j}-p_{\ell}|<4\delta

because
p_{j},p_{\ell}
are the centers of the two intersecting balls
K_{j},K_{\ell}
of radius
2\delta
.
We write the rotation matrix
\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}
as

\big(\!\begin{smallmatrix}\mathtt{A}&\mathtt{b}\\
\mathtt{c}&\mathtt{d}\end{smallmatrix}\!\big)
,
where
\mathtt{A}
is an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix,

\mathtt{b}
is a column with
n-1
components,

\mathtt{c}
is a row with
n-1
components,
and
\mathtt{d}
is a scalar.
Thus

\displaystyle\mathtt{A}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=[\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(y^{\prime},0)]^{\prime},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathtt{b}

\displaystyle=[\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(0,1)]^{\prime}=-[\mathtt{R}_{\ell}(p_{j}-p_{\ell})]^{\prime},

\displaystyle\mathtt{c}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=[\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(y^{\prime},0)]_{n},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathtt{d}

\displaystyle=[\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(0,1)]_{n}=1-[\mathtt{R}_{\ell}(p_{j}-p_{\ell})]_{n}

(2.22)

for all
y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
, where we have used (
2.21
)
and the fact that
p_{0}^{\prime}=0,(p_{0})_{n}=-1
.
Hence
\mathtt{T}_{\ell j}(y^{\prime})
in
(
2.19
) becomes

\mathtt{T}_{\ell j}(y^{\prime})=\frac{-\mathtt{b}+\mathtt{A}y^{\prime}}{\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{c}y^{\prime}}.

(2.23)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\delta<1/8
. Then the scalar
\mathtt{d}
and
the matrix
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c}
are invertible,
one has

|\mathtt{d}-1|<4\delta,\quad\ |\mathtt{c}|=|\mathtt{b}|<4\delta,\quad\ |(\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c})^{-1}y^{\prime}|\leq\lambda|y^{\prime}|\quad\forall y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},\quad\ \lambda:=\mathtt{d}^{-1},

(2.24)

and, for all
|y^{\prime}|\leq 1
, one has the converging Taylor expansion

\frac{-\mathtt{b}+\mathtt{A}y^{\prime}}{\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{c}y^{\prime}}=-\lambda\mathtt{b}+\lambda^{2}(\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c})y^{\prime}(1+\mathtt{S}(y^{\prime})),\quad\ \mathtt{S}(y^{\prime}):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(\lambda\mathtt{c}y^{\prime})^{m}.

(2.25)

Proof.

By (
2.22
), the vector
(\mathtt{b},\mathtt{d}-1)

is the opposite of
\mathtt{R}_{\ell}(p_{j}-p_{\ell})
,
and its norm is
|(\mathtt{b},\mathtt{d}-1)|

=|\mathtt{R}_{\ell}(p_{j}-p_{\ell})|

=|p_{j}-p_{\ell}|

<4\delta
.
This gives the bound for
|\mathtt{b}|,|\mathtt{d}-1|
in (
2.24
).
Since
\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}
is a rotation matrix,
all its columns and rows have unitary norm.
In particular, taking the
n
th row and
n
th column, one has

|\mathtt{b}|^{2}+\mathtt{d}^{2}=1
and

|\mathtt{c}|^{2}+\mathtt{d}^{2}=1
,
whence
|\mathtt{c}|=|\mathtt{b}|
.

To study the matrix
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c}
,
we observe that

\begin{pmatrix}\mathtt{A}&\mathtt{b}\\
\mathtt{c}&\mathtt{d}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathtt{d}I^{\prime}&0\\
-\mathtt{c}&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c}&\mathtt{b}\\
0&\mathtt{d}\end{pmatrix}\!,

(2.26)

where
I^{\prime}
is the
(n-1)\times(n-1)
identity matrix.
The first matrix in (
2.26
) is the rotation
\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}
,
whose determinant is 1, while the determinant of the second matrix is
\mathtt{d}^{n-1}
.
Hence the determinant of the third matrix in (
2.26
) is
\mathtt{d}^{n-1}
, which is nonzero,
and this is also the product of
\mathtt{d}
with the determinant
of
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c}
.
This proves that the matrix
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c}
is invertible.

Now let
z^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
.
We consider the product of (
2.26
) with the vector
(z^{\prime},0)
,
and we calculate its norm.
The matrix in the RHS of (
2.26
)
times the vector
(z^{\prime},0)
gives
((\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c})z^{\prime},0)
,
whose norm is
|(\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c})z^{\prime}|
.
The matrix product in the LHS of (
2.26
)
times the same vector
(z^{\prime},0)
gives

\mathtt{R}_{\ell}\mathtt{R}_{j}^{-1}(\mathtt{d}z^{\prime},-\mathtt{c}z^{\prime})
,
whose norm is equal to the norm of
(\mathtt{d}z^{\prime},-\mathtt{c}z^{\prime})
,
which is
\geq\mathtt{d}|z^{\prime}|
.
This proves that
|(\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c})z^{\prime}|\geq\mathtt{d}|z^{\prime}|

for all
z^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
.
Since
\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c}
is invertible,
taking
z^{\prime}=(\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c})^{-1}y^{\prime}
gives
the third inequality of (
2.24
).

Formula (
2.25
) is obtained by writing
(\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{c}y^{\prime})^{-1}=\lambda(1-\lambda\mathtt{c}y^{\prime})^{-1}
as a power series, which converges for
\lambda|\mathtt{c}y^{\prime}|<1
.
One has
\lambda\leq(1-4\delta)^{-1}<2
and
|\mathtt{c}|<4\delta
,
whence
\lambda|cy^{\prime}|<8\delta<1
for all
|y^{\prime}|\leq 1
.
∎

We define

\tilde{\mathtt{T}}_{\ell j}(y^{\prime}):=-\lambda\mathtt{b}+\lambda^{2}(\mathtt{A}\mathtt{d}-\mathtt{b}\mathtt{c})y^{\prime}\big(1+\varphi_{\delta}(y^{\prime})S(y^{\prime})\big),\quad\ \varphi_{\delta}(y^{\prime}):=\varphi\Big(\frac{|y^{\prime}|}{4\delta}\Big),

(2.27)

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
is a smooth cut-off function such that

\varphi(t)=1
for
t\leq 1
,

\varphi(t)=0
for
t\geq 2
,
and
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
.
Hence
\tilde{\mathtt{T}}_{\ell j}=\mathtt{T}_{\ell j}
where
\varphi_{\delta}=1
,
i.e., in the ball
|y^{\prime}|\leq 4\delta
.
Since
\delta<1/4
, the set
A_{0}=\mathtt{f}(B_{2\delta}(p_{0}))

is contained in the ball
|y|<4\delta
, and

(\tilde{\mathtt{T}}_{\ell j}(y^{\prime}),y_{n})=(\mathtt{T}_{\ell j}(y^{\prime}),y_{n})=\mathtt{f}_{\ell}(\mathtt{g}_{j}(y))\quad\ \forall y=(y^{\prime},y_{n})\in A_{0}.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a universal constant
\delta_{0}\in(0,1/8)
such that,
for
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, if
K_{j}\cap K_{\ell}\neq\emptyset
, then
the map
\tilde{\mathtt{T}}_{\ell j}
in (

)
is a

diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}

that coincides with
\mathtt{T}_{\ell j}
in (

)
in the ball

,
and coincides in

with the affine map

Proof.

Let us prove that
\tilde{\mathtt{T}}_{\ell j}
is a bijection of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
.
Given

, one has

.
The

Properties of Sobolev spaces on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
.
The next lemma collects some useful properties of the Sobolev spaces, both standard and non-isotropic,
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

On
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
one has the following properties.

(i)

Density
.
For
s,r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is dense in
H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
and the set
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}):=\{\varphi|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}:\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\}

of the restrictions to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
of the Schwartz functions of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is dense in
H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
.

(ii)

Embedding
.
Let
s,r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, with
s>1/2
,
s+r>n/2
.
Then
H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and

H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
, with

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C_{s,r}\|v\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},\quad\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}\leq C_{s,r}\|u\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})},

(2.29)

for all
v\in H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
all
u\in H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
,
where the constant
C_{s,r}
depends on
s,r+s
.
Moreover, if
v\in H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then
v
is uniformly continuous in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

and
v(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
.
If
u\in H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
, then
u
is uniformly continuous in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
,
and
u(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
.

(iii)

Partial derivatives
.
The partial derivatives (in the sense of distributions) satisfy

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{k}}u\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{H^{s,r+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{s+1,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})},\quad k=1,\ldots,n-1,

(2.30)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{n}}u\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{H^{s+1,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})},

(2.31)

for all
u\in H^{s+1,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
, for all
s,r\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(iv)

Trace
.
Let
s,r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, with
s>1/2
.
On
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the trace operator

T:H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to H^{s+r-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}),\quad u\mapsto Tu=u(\cdot,0)

(2.32)

is a well defined, bounded, linear operator, with

\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{s+r-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}\leq C_{s}\|u\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

(2.33)

for all
u\in H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

(first defined on

We decompose the annulus
1-(\delta/2)<|x|<1-(\delta/4)

into a sequence of annuli
\rho_{k+1}<|x|<\rho_{k}
,
where
\rho_{0}=1-(\delta/4)
,
the sequence
(\rho_{k})
is decreasing and it converges to
1-(\delta/2)
,
and for each annulus we introduce a smooth, radial cut-off function
\zeta_{k}

in the following way.
Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
with

\varphi=0
on
(-\infty,0]
,

\varphi=1
on
[1,\infty)
,
and
0\leq\varphi\leq 1

We define

\rho_{k}:=1-\frac{\delta}{4}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{1}{2^{\ell}},\quad\zeta_{k}(x):=\varphi\Big(\frac{|x|-\rho_{k+1}}{\rho_{k}-\rho_{k+1}}\Big)\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ \ k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

(3.1)

Thus
\zeta_{k}(x)=0
for
|x|\leq\rho_{k+1}
,

\zeta_{k}(x)=1
for
|x|\geq\rho_{k}
, and

|\zeta_{k}(x)|\leq 1,\quad|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\zeta_{k}(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{k|\alpha|}\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ \ k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ \ \alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n},

(3.2)

where the constant
C_{\alpha}
depends on the length
|\alpha|
of the multi-index
\alpha

(and also on
\varphi
and
\delta
, which we consider as fixed).
We note that
\zeta_{k+1}=1
where
\zeta_{k}\neq 0
, i.e.,

\zeta_{k+1}=1\text{ on }\mathrm{supp}(\zeta_{k}),\quad\ \forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

(3.3)

We also define

\rho_{k}^{*}:=1-\frac{\delta}{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{1}{2^{\ell}},\quad\zeta_{k}^{*}(x):=\varphi\Big(\frac{|x|-\rho^{*}_{k+1}}{\rho^{*}_{k}-\rho^{*}_{k+1}}\Big)\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ \ k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

(3.4)

Note that
\rho_{k}^{*},\zeta_{k}^{*}
are defined in the same way as
\rho_{k},\zeta_{k}
,
except that
\delta/4
in (
3.1
)
is replaced by
\delta/2
in (
3.4
),
so that the sequence of annuli
\rho^{*}_{k+1}<|x|<\rho^{*}_{k}

covers the annulus

Thus,

\zeta^{*}_{k^{\prime}}=1\text{ on }\mathrm{supp}(\zeta_{k}),\quad\ \forall k,k^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

(3.5)

For any function
u
, we denote

\displaystyle|u|_{X^{s,r}_{k}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|(u\zeta_{k}\psi_{j})\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})},\quad|u|_{X^{s,r}_{*,k}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|(u\zeta^{*}_{k}\psi_{j})\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}\|_{H^{s,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})},

where

Lemma 4.1

For

\leq C_{r_{0}}\delta_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}

For every

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|(u\psi_{j})\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|(u\psi_{j})\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{s_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})}=C_{s_{0}}\|u\|_{H^{s_{0}}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})},

because
K_{j}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}=\{\mathtt{g}_{j}(y^{\prime},0):(y^{\prime},0)\in A_{0}\}

and
\psi_{j}=0
outside
K_{j}
,

\psi_{j}\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}=0
outside
A_{0}
.
Moreover
(u\psi_{j})\circ\mathtt{g}_{j}(\cdot,0)
is continuous for all
j
,
whence we deduce that
u(x)\to u(x_{0})
as
x\to x_{0}
.
∎
