Tight Entropic Uncertainty Relations
Alberto Riccardi, Lorenzo Maccone 1

Abstract
Entropic uncertainty relations H ⁡ ( A ) + H ⁡ ( B ) ⩾ γ H(A)+H(B)\geqslant\gamma give a nonzero lower bound γ \gamma to the sum of the Shannon entropies H H of the outcome probabilities of incompatible observables A A and B B . They are better than the variance-based uncertainty relations because they only depend on the Born statistics of the outcomes and not on the outcomes themselves, and because bounds γ \gamma typically are state independent. Here we provide a state-independent lower bound γ s \gamma_{s} that is better than the textbook Maassen-Uffink bound and, in the limit of the parameter s → 2 s\to 2 , becomes asymptotically tight for all A , B A,B . The bound can be extended to Renyi entropies.

I
Proof of the bound

The bound (
1
) is an application
of the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem
dunford

(incidentally, also the Maassen-Uffink bound
muffink
is an
application of the Riesz–Thorin theorem): Consider a linear operator

T
that is bounded both according to the
p_{0}\to q_{0}
and the

p_{1}\to q_{1}
norms:
\|{T}\|_{p_{i}\to q_{i}}\leqslant M_{i}
,
i=0,1
. Then
the following upper “interpolating” bound to the

p_{\theta}\to q_{\theta}
norm holds

\displaystyle\|{T}\|_{p_{\theta}\to q_{\theta}}\leqslant(M_{0})^{1-\theta}(M_{1})^{\theta},\qquad\mbox{ with }

(3)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\tfrac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\tfrac{\theta}{p_{1}},\ \tfrac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\tfrac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\tfrac{\theta}{q_{1}},

for all
\theta\in[0,1]
.

Consider the operator
U_{ji}=\langle b_{j}|a_{i}\rangle
, which is unitary because
it transforms the basis
\{|b_{j}\rangle\}
of eigenstates of
B
into the
basis
\{|a_{i}\rangle\}
of eigenstates of
A
. Its
\ell^{2}
-norm is

\|U\|_{2}=1
, because
\|a_{i}\|_{2}=\|b_{i}\|_{2}=1
due to the Born rule
probability normalization, with
a_{i}\equiv\langle a_{i}|\psi\rangle
,

b_{j}\equiv\langle b_{j}|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i}U_{ji}a_{i}
[namely,
|x_{i}|^{2}=p(x_{i})
,
x=a,b
]. Use the Riesz–Thorin inequality for

q_{1}=p_{1}=2
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\|b\|_{q_{\theta}}}{\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}}=\frac{\|Ua\|_{q_{\theta}}}{\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}}\leqslant\sup_{b}\frac{\|Ua\|_{q_{\theta}}}{\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}}=\|U\|_{p_{\theta}\to q_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|U\|^{1-\theta}_{p_{0}\to q_{0}}\|U\|^{\theta}_{p_{1}\to q_{1}}=\|U\|^{1-\theta}_{p_{0}\to q_{0}}\;,

(4)

which, taking the
\log
, becomes

\displaystyle\log_{2}\|b\|_{q_{\theta}}-\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}\leqslant(1-\theta)\log_{2}\|U\|_{p_{0}\to q_{0}}\;.

(5)

Choose
p_{0}=s
and
q_{0}=s^{\prime}
and divide both members by

1-\theta>0
. Take the limit
\theta\to 1^{-}
using the definition of
the derivative: since
\log_{2}\|b\|_{q_{1}}=\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{1}}=0
, we
find

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 1^{-}}-\tfrac{\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}}{1-\theta}=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}\Big|_{\theta=1}=-\tfrac{2-s}{2s}H(A),\ \;

(6)

and similarly for
\log_{2}\|b\|_{q_{\theta}}
whose derivative gives

-\tfrac{2-s}{2s}H(B)
. This statement and the last equality in
(
6
) are proven in App.
C
. The bound (
1
)
is finally found by dividing both members of (
5
) by
-\tfrac{2-s}{2s}<0
,
which flips its inequality.
\square

II
Bound tightness

We now prove that, in the limit
s\to 2
, the bound (
1
) is tight, namely that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 2^{-}}\frac{-2s}{2-s}\log_{2}\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}=\inf_{|\psi\rangle}[H(A)+H(B)]\;.

(7)

Writing explicitly
\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}=\sup_{\psi}\|U\psi\|_{s^{\prime}}/\|\psi\|_{s}
in the left-hand-term and considering that the
\log
is a growing function, while the term
-2s/(2-s)<0
, the sup becomes an inf:

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 2^{-}}\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}\log_{2}\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}=\inf_{\psi}\lim_{s\to 2^{-}}\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}\log_{2}\tfrac{\|U\psi\|_{s^{\prime}}}{\|\psi\|_{s}}\;.

(8)

With a change of variables
x\equiv 2-s>0
, and remembering that
s^{\prime}=s/(s-1)
, the right-hand-side becomes

\displaystyle\inf_{\psi}\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\tfrac{-2(1-x)}{x}\log_{2}{\textstyle\sum_{i}|\sum_{j}}U_{ij}\psi_{j}|^{\tfrac{2-x}{1-x}}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{2}{x}\log_{2}{\textstyle\sum_{i}}|\psi_{i}|^{2-x}\;.

(9)

Using the trick of App.
C
, these two limits can be turned into
derivatives. Indeed, the second term is equal to

\displaystyle 2\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\log_{2}\sum_{i}|\psi_{i}|^{2-x}\Big|_{x=0}=-\sum_{i}|\psi_{i}|^{2}\log_{2}|\psi_{i}|^{2}\;,

(10)

which is equal to
H(A)
by choosing
\psi_{i}=\langle a_{i}|\psi\rangle
. Similarly,
the first term in (
9
) is equal to

-\sum_{i}|\sum_{j}U_{ij}\psi_{j}|^{2}\log_{2}|\sum_{j}U_{ij}\psi_{j}|^{2}
, which is
equal to
H(B)
for
\psi_{j}=\langle a_{j}|\psi\rangle
, since
b_{i}=\sum_{j}U_{ij}a_{j}
. This proves (
7
), since the
minimization over
|\psi\rangle
is equivalent to the minimization over

\psi_{j}
.
\square

III
Comparison with Maassen-Uffink

We now show that the bound (
1
) is stronger than the
Maassen-Uffink one (the latter is tight if the observables
A,B
have
mutually unbiased eigenbases). Take
c=\max_{ji}|U_{ji}|
, then for all
a_{i}
and all
j

\displaystyle|{\textstyle\sum_{i}}U_{ji}a_{i}|\leqslant{\textstyle\sum_{i}}|U_{ji}|\,|a_{i}|\leqslant c{\textstyle\sum_{i}}|a_{i}|=c\|a\|_{1}\;,

(11)

which (maximizing over
j
) implies
\|U\|_{1\to\infty}\leqslant c
. Moreover, unitarity implies
\|U\|_{2\to 2}=1
. So we can employ
Riesz–Thorin (
3
) with
p_{0}=1
,
q_{0}=\infty
,
p_{1}=q_{1}=2
, which
gives
\|U\|_{p_{\theta}\to q_{\theta}}\leqslant c^{1-\theta}
. Choose

s=p_{\theta}
and
s^{\prime}=s/(s-1)=q_{\theta}
. Taking the log of both
members, the Riesz–Thorin inequality becomes

\displaystyle\log_{2}\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}\leqslant\tfrac{2-s}{s}\log_{2}c\;,

(12)

since
1-\theta=\tfrac{2-s}{s}
. Multiplying both members of (
12
)
by
\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}<0
we find that our inequality is stronger than
Maassen-Uffink’s:

\displaystyle H(A)+H(B)\geqslant\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}\log_{2}\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}\geqslant-2\log_{2}c\;.

(13)

IV
Renyi entropies

We now prove bound (
2
). Again, use the Riesz–Thorin bound
with
p_{0}=s
,
q_{0}=s^{\prime}
,
p_{1}=q_{1}=2
and choose
\theta=0
in
(
5
), namely

\log_{2}\|b\|_{s^{\prime}}-\log_{2}\|a\|_{s}\leqslant\log\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}
. This is
equivalent to (
2
), because for
s^{\prime}=s/(s-1)
the definition
of the Renyi entropy implies

\displaystyle-\log_{2}\|a\|_{s}=

\displaystyle-\tfrac{2-s}{2s}H_{s/2}(A)\;,

\displaystyle\log_{2}\|b\|_{s^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle-\tfrac{2-s}{2s}H_{{s^{\prime}}/2}(B)\;.

(14)

The tightness of the bounds (
2
) follows from the definition
of
\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}\log_{2}\sup_{\psi_{i}}\tfrac{\|U\psi\|_{s^{\prime}}}{\|\psi\|_{s}}=\inf_{\psi_{i}}\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}\log_{2}\tfrac{\|U\psi\|_{s^{\prime}}}{\|\psi\|_{s}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{|\psi\rangle}[H_{s/2}(A)+H_{s^{\prime}/2}(B)]\;,

(15)

where we used (
14
) in the last equality and the fact that
minimizing over
\psi_{i}
is equivalent to minimizing over
|\psi\rangle
as
discussed above.
\square

In particular, this implies that the Maassen-Uffink lower bound

-2\log_{2}c
of (
13
) is a tight lower bound for the Renyi
entropy uncertainty relations with
s=1
and
s^{\prime}=\infty
, or with

s=\infty,s^{\prime}=1
. Indeed,
\|U\|_{1\to\infty}=c
, since (
11
)
implies
\|U\|_{1\to\infty}\leqslant c
, and one can also prove the
“
\geqslant
” by considering a vector
e
that is composed by all
zeros except for a one in correspondence to the index
i
for which
the
\max_{ji}|U_{ji}|=c
is realized, so that

\|Ue\|_{\infty}\geqslant c
, while
\|e\|_{1}=1
.

V
Conclusions

In conclusion, we have provided the lower bound (
1
) to the
sum of entropies of the quantum statistics of the outcomes of two
arbitrary observables
A
and
B
. We have shown that it is tight in
general, in contrast to all known bounds

muffink
;
deutsch
;
colespiani
;
karol
, and that the right-hand-side
of the inequality (
1
) can be numerically evaluated using
NPIM. Finally, we have shown that the bound can also be extended to
Renyi entropies as Eq. (
2
), which are all tight. We have
discussed pure states here, but the bounds apply also to mixed states,
since mixed states trivially have larger entropies.

Why the bound? The inequality (
2
), which comprises the best
among the ones of (
1
), is tight, so there must be a deep
reason why its lower bound is what it is. Indeed, we have shown that
it comes from the Riesz-Thorin inequality with all its free parameters
fixed:
p_{0}=s
and
q_{0}=s^{\prime}
are determined by the choice of the Renyi
entropies we are considering, while
p_{1}=2
is determined by the fact
that the Born rule uses
square
moduli of the amplitudes and not
a different power
sorkin
which is an, experimentally determined

urbasi
, postulate of quantum mechanics. Instead,
q_{1}=2

because we are considering observables
A,B
, which are associated to
eigenbases (another postulate of quantum mechanics), so a unitary
U

is required to move from one basis to the next. Finally, the fact that
\tfrac{1}{s}+\tfrac{1}{s^{\prime}}=1

is only a request for elegance: we want a bound that only involves
sums of entropies, if we settle for sums of entropies weighted by
appropriate coefficients, one can drop this last request and
immediately obtain an amended version of (
2
).

Appendix A
NONLINEAR POWER ITERATION METHOD

In this appendix we review the nonlinear power iteration method, also
referred to in the literature as “power method for
\ell^{p}
norms”
or “nonlinear power method”
boyd
;
hing
. It was used to
calculate the plots in Figs.
1
and
2
. The
algorithm’s goal is to find the maximum value of
\|Ux\|_{s^{\prime}}
subject
to the constraint
\|x\|_{s}=1
, namely, the operator norm

\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}
, with
s\in(1,2)
and
s^{\prime}=s/(s-1)>2
.

NPIM Algorithm:

1.

Generate a random complex vector (seed)
u^{(k)}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
with
k=0
.

2.

Normalize it to create the vector
v^{(k)}=u^{(k)}/\|u^{(k)}\|_{p}
.

3.

Apply the operator
U
:
w^{(k)}_{j}=\sum_{i}U_{ji}v^{(k)}_{i}
.

4.

Calculate the norm for the
k
-th iteration as
\|w^{(k)}\|_{s^{\prime}}
.

5.

Rescale it as
x^{(k)}_{j}=w^{(k)}_{j}\>|w^{(k)}_{j}|^{s^{\prime}-2}
, where
s^{\prime}-2>0
, since
s^{\prime}>2
.

6.

Apply the adjoint to go back:
y_{i}=\sum_{j}U^{*}_{ji}x_{j}
.

7.

Rescale it again as
z^{(k)}_{j}=y^{(k)}_{j}\>|y^{(k)}_{j}|^{s^{\prime}-2}
.

8.

Iterate from step
2
, choosing
v^{(k+1)}=z^{(k)}
,
and stop when the iteration converges:

\left|\,\|w^{(k+1)}\|_{s^{\prime}}-\|w^{(k)}\|_{s^{\prime}}\right|<\epsilon
, with
\epsilon
some
(small) accuracy parameter.

9.

Repeat the whole procedure
N_{seeds}
times from step

1
and select the largest
L
of the obtained norms

\|w^{(k)}\|_{s^{\prime}}
. This is needed because the algorithm converges
to a
local
maximum and one needs to repeat it multiple times
with
N_{seeds}
random seeds to find the global maximum.

The final quantity
L
is the estimated norm
\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}
. A good
check to see whether
N_{seeds}
is sufficiently large is to calculate
the norm for different
s
and check whether there are large
fluctuations for adjacent values of
s
.

Appendix B
Qubits

In this appendix we give a partially closed formula of the bound for
qubits, Eq. (
28
) below, which was studied in

zozor
, and give an analysis for this case.

Without loss of generality, by appropriately choosing the
z
-axis of the Bloch sphere to coincide with the eigenstates of the operator
B
and the
x
axis to cancel all the phases in the eigenstates of
A
, we can write the unitary transition matrix
U_{ji}=\langle b_{j}|a_{i}\rangle
as a real rotation matrix

\displaystyle U=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi&\sin\varphi\\
-\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi\end{pmatrix},\qquad\varphi\in\bigl(0,\tfrac{\pi}{4}\bigr].\;,

(16)

where
\cos\varphi\geqslant 0
,
\sin\varphi\geqslant 0
. We must
maximize the norm of
U
when evaluated over an arbitrary 2-
d

vector
e
with
\|e\|_{s}=1
, namely (apart from irrelevant phases):

\displaystyle e=\begin{pmatrix}\cos^{2/s}(\alpha)\\
e^{i\gamma}\sin^{2/s}(\alpha)\end{pmatrix}\;.

(17)

Taking
y_{j}=\sum_{i}U_{ji}e_{i}
, we find

\displaystyle|y_{1}|^{2}=\cos^{2}(\varphi)\cos^{4/s}(\alpha)+\sin^{2}(\varphi)\sin^{4/s}(\alpha)

\displaystyle+2\cos(\varphi)\sin(\varphi)\cos^{2/s}(\alpha)\sin^{2/s}(\alpha)\cos(\gamma),

\displaystyle|y_{2}|^{2}=\sin^{2}(\varphi)\cos^{4/s}(\alpha)+\cos^{2}(\varphi)\sin^{4/s}(\alpha)

\displaystyle-2\cos(\varphi)\sin(\varphi)\cos^{2/s}(\alpha)\sin^{2/s}(\alpha)\cos(\gamma)\;,

(18)

which we can write more compactly as
|y_{1}|^{2}=M_{1}-I\cos(\gamma)
,

|y_{2}|^{2}=M_{2}+I\cos(\gamma)
. The
s^{\prime}
-norm of
y
is then

\displaystyle(\|y\|_{s^{\prime}})^{s^{\prime}}=\left(M_{1}-I\cos(\gamma)\right)^{s^{\prime}/2}+\left(M_{2}+I\cos(\gamma)\right)^{s^{\prime}/2}\;,

(19)

where
s^{\prime}\geq 2
so the above norm is a convex function with respect
to
\cos(\gamma)
, so the maximum of (
19
) must lie on the
boundary, namely for
\cos\gamma=\pm 1
. In turn, this implies that

\gamma=0,\pm\pi
and we can limit ourselves to real vectors in
(
17
), namely

\displaystyle x=\binom{\cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha}\ \Rightarrow\ Ux=\binom{\cos(\varphi-\alpha)}{\sin(\alpha-\varphi)}\;.

(20)

Then, from the definition of
\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}
:

\displaystyle\|U\|_{s\to s^{\prime}}=\max_{\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]}\frac{(|\cos(\varphi-\alpha)|^{s^{\prime}}+|\sin(\varphi-\alpha)|^{s^{\prime}})^{1/s^{\prime}}}{(\cos^{s}\alpha+\sin^{s}\alpha)^{1/s}}\;,

(21)

and the bound (
1
) becomes

\displaystyle H(A)+H(B)\geqslant\min_{\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]}B_{s}(\alpha),

\displaystyle\mbox{ with }B_{s}(\alpha)\equiv\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}\log_{2}\|U\|_{s\to s/(s-1)}.\;

(22)

As a function of
s
, the best of these bounds is obtained for

s\to 2
. To calculate this limit, it is better to reparametrize using

u_{\alpha}\equiv\cos^{2}\alpha
and

w_{\alpha}\equiv\cos^{2}(\alpha-\varphi)
, so that

\displaystyle B_{s}(\alpha)=\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}[

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{s^{\prime}}\log_{2}(w_{\alpha}^{s^{\prime}/2}+(1-w_{\alpha})^{s^{\prime}/2})

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{s}\log_{2}(u_{\alpha}^{s/2}+(1-u_{\alpha})^{s/2})]\;,

(23)

where the function
f_{r}(p)\equiv\tfrac{1}{r}\log_{2}(p^{r/2}+(1-p)^{r/2})

appears twice, with
p\in[0,1]
. To calculate the
s\to 2
limit of
(
23
), we take the Taylor expansion of
f_{r}(p)
around

,
where
f_{2}(p)=0
and

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial r}f_{r}(p)\Big|_{r=2}=\tfrac{1}{r^{2}}\log_{2}(p^{r/2}+(1-p)^{r/2})+

\displaystyle\tfrac{p^{r/2}\log_{2}p+(1-p)^{r/2}\log_{2}(1-p)}{2r(p^{r/2}+(1-p)^{r/2})}\Big|_{r=2}=-\tfrac{1}{4}h(p)\;,

(24)

where

is the
binary entropy. So,

f_{2\pm\epsilon}(p)\simeq f_{2}(p)\mp\tfrac{\epsilon}{4}h(p)+O(\epsilon^{2})
. Then choose
s=2-\epsilon
, so that

s^{\prime}=\tfrac{s}{s-1}=\tfrac{2-\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\tfrac{1+\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\simeq 2+\epsilon+O(\epsilon^{2})
. Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 2^{-}}B_{s}(\alpha)=h(\cos^{2}\alpha)+h(\cos^{2}(\alpha-\varphi))\equiv F(\alpha)\;,

(25)

because
\tfrac{-2s}{2-s}\simeq-\tfrac{4}{\epsilon}
for

\epsilon\simeq 0
. Finally, (
25
) must be minimized over

\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]
, see (
22
). To find the minimum, consider

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}F=

\displaystyle-2\log_{2}|\tan\alpha|\sin(2\alpha)

\displaystyle-2\log_{2}|\tan(\alpha-\varphi)|\sin(2(\alpha-\varphi))\;,

(26)

where we used
\tfrac{\partial}{\partial p}h(p)=\log_{2}\tfrac{1-p}{p}
. It
is null for
\alpha=\varphi/2
. This value of
\alpha
is not always a minimum, as can
be seen by looking at the second derivative, calculated in

\varphi/2
:

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}F\big|_{\alpha=\varphi/2}=-\tfrac{8}{\ln 2}[1+\cos\varphi\ln\tan(\varphi/2)]\;.

(27)

The second derivative can be obtained by defining

g(\alpha)\equiv\log_{2}|\tan\alpha|\sin(2\alpha)
so that

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}F(\alpha)=-2g(\alpha)-2g(\alpha-\varphi)
,
and then deriving
g
. It is strictly negative for all
\varphi

smaller than a critical angle
\varphi_{c}\simeq 0.586
radians. Thus,
for
\alpha=\varphi/2
,
F(\alpha)=2h(\cos^{2}\tfrac{\varphi}{2})
is the
minimum sum of entropies for
\varphi\leqslant\varphi_{c}
. Instead, for

\varphi>\varphi_{c}
there is no closed form of the bound, which must
be calculated numerically. In essence, the bound is

\displaystyle H(A)+H(B)\geqslant\min[2h(\cos^{2}\tfrac{\varphi}{2}),\min_{\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]}F(\alpha)]\;,

(28)

with
h
the binary entropy, where the minimum is attained at the
first term for
\varphi\leqslant\varphi_{c}\simeq 0.586
, and at the
second otherwise. Eq. (
28
) is validated in
Fig.
3
with a Montecarlo simulation that, for each

\varphi
, calculates the minimum sum-of-entropy by minimizing it over
a large set of randomly generated qubit states.

Appendix C
Proof of Eq. (
6
)

In this section we prove the last equality of Eq. (
6
),

-\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}\Big|_{\theta=1}=-\frac{2-s}{2s}H(A)
. We
also show that

+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log_{2}\|b\|_{q_{\theta}}\Big|_{\theta=1}=-\frac{2-s}{2s}H(B)
.

Since
p_{1}=2
, then
\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{1}}=0
, so, using the definition of the derivative,

\displaystyle-\lim_{\theta\to 1^{-}}\frac{\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}}{1-\theta}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}\Big|_{\theta=1}\;.

(29)

Moreover, since
1/p_{\theta}=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, with
p_{0}=s
and
p_{1}=2
, then
\tfrac{\partial p_{\theta}}{\partial\theta}|_{\theta=1}=2\tfrac{2-s}{s}
, so

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}\Big|_{\theta=1}=\frac{\partial p_{\theta}}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial\log_{2}\|a\|_{p_{\theta}}}{\partial p_{\theta}}\Big|_{\theta=1}

\displaystyle=2\frac{2-s}{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\frac{1}{p}\log_{2}\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{p}\Big|_{p=2}

\displaystyle=2\frac{2-s}{2s}\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}\log_{2}|a_{i}|=-\frac{2-s}{2s}H(A)\;.

(30)

A similar procedure applies to the term with
\|b\|_{q_{\theta}}
, the
only difference being that
q_{0}=s^{\prime}=s/(s-1)
. So, using the same
procedure of Eq. (
30
), swapping
p_{\theta}
with
q_{\theta}
, we
find

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log_{2}\|b\|_{q_{\theta}}\Big|_{\theta=1}=-\tfrac{2-s}{2s}H(B)
.

[The choice
p_{0}=s
and
q_{0}=s^{\prime}=s/(s-1)
is necessary in order to
obtain the same coefficients with opposite signs in front of
H(A)

and
H(B)
.]
