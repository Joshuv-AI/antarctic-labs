A partial wave analysis is performed simultaneously in three decay channels T c ​ c ¯ ​ ( 4020 ) − → D ∗ 0 ​ D ∗ ⁣ − T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}\to{D}^{*0}D^{*-} , π − ​ J / ψ \pi^{-}J/\psi , and π − ​ h c \pi^{-}h_{c} , based on data samples taken at s = 4.395 \sqrt{s}=4.395 and 4.416 ​ GeV 4.416\,\mathrm{GeV} with an integrated luminosity of 1598.9 ​ pb − 1 1598.9\,\mathrm{pb}^{-1} collected with the BESIII detector operating on the BEPCII collider. For the first time, the spin-parity of the T c ​ c ¯ ​ ( 4020 ) − T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-} is determined to be J P = 1 + J^{P}=1^{+} with a significance 11.7 ​ σ 11.7\sigma . Pole positions are extracted on the Riemann sheets with three branch points in the complex energy plane. Furthermore, the relative branching fractions are obtained as ℬ [ T c ​ c ¯ ( 4020 ) − → π − J / ψ ] / ℬ [ T c ​ c ¯ ( 4020 ) − → D ∗ 0 D ∗ ⁣ − ] = ( 3.6 ± 0.6 ± 1.6 ) × 10 − 3 \mathcal{B}[T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}\to\pi^{-}J/\psi]/\mathcal{B}[T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}\to{D}^{*0}D^{*-}]=(3.6\pm 0.6\pm 1.6)\times 10^{-3} and ℬ [ T c ​ c ¯ ( 4020 ) − → π − h c ] / ℬ [ T c ​ c ¯ ( 4020 ) − → D ∗ 0 D ∗ ⁣ − ] = ( 8.9 ± 1.3 ± 2.3 ) × 10 − 2 \mathcal{B}[T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}\to\pi^{-}h_{c}]/\mathcal{B}[T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}\to{D}^{*0}D^{*-}]=(8.9\pm 1.3\pm 2.3)\times 10^{-2} , where the first uncertainties are statistical, and the second are systematic.



I. INTRODUCTION

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) allows for the existence of exotic states beyond typical quark-antiquark mesons and three-quark baryons.
Numerous experiments searched for and explored various exotic states to enhance the understanding of QCD, particularly of the color confinement mechanism.
In recent years, charged charmonium-like states and their neutral isospin partners, known as
T_{c\bar{c}}
(also
Z_{c}
) and
T_{c\bar{c}\bar{s}}
(also
Z_{cs}
) states, have been observed with different processes in a number of experiments
52
;
38
;
53
;
42
. These states are of great interest as candidates for exotic tetraquarks.

The isotriplet charmonium-like states,
T_{c\bar{c}1}(3900)
and
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)
(also
T_{c\bar{c}}(4025)
) are investigated in various processes. The
T_{c\bar{c}1}(3900)
is observed in the processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi\pi J/\psi
,
\pi\bar{D}D^{*}
and
\pi D\bar{D}^{*}
by BESIII
4
;
6
;
9
;
12
;
11
, Belle
50
, and with CLEO-c data
62
. In particular, its spin-parity is identified to be
J^{P}=1^{+}
by BESIII
18
;
14
.
For the heavier
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)
, it is observed by BESIII
5
;
7
;
8
;
10
with the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi\pi h_{c}
and
\pi D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
processes.
These
T_{c\bar{c}}
states, composed of at least four quarks of
c\bar{c}q\bar{q}
(
q
=
u
,
d
), have been interpreted through various theoretical frameworks, such as tetraquark configurations
58
;
51
;
27

40
;
34
;
35
;
39

36
;
57
and kinematic effect
30
;
61
;
47
;
49
;
56
but their underlying structures remain unclear.
To identify the nature of these states, a multi-channel joint analysis with different processes is a necessity.

The relative branching fractions (BFs) for different decay channels of the
T_{c\bar{c}}
provide crucial information for distinguishing between models of its internal structure. For example, a charmonium-core scenario would favor its decays into hidden-charm final states (
e.g.
,
\pi J/\psi
and
\pi h_{c}
), suppressing the open-charm decays. Conversely, a significantly larger BF for open-charm decays (
e.g.
,
D\bar{D}^{*}
and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
) would support a hadronic molecular interpretation.

The mass, width, and spin-parity are fundamental properties of particles, which are highly correlated with their intrinsic nature. The
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)
resonance parameters known so far are obtained through one-dimensional fits of the
\pi h_{c}
or
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
invariant mass spectra.
However, the discrepancy of measured widths between the two decay channels, as illustrated in Fig.
1
, indicates that the line-shape model used in the one-dimensional mass fit is deficient.
The spin-parity of
T_{c\bar{c}}
state is crucial to figure out its nature and can only be determined experimentally. However, unlike
T_{c\bar{c}1}(3900)
, the spin-parity of
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)
has not been identified in experiment yet.

68\,\mathrm{ps}

. Besides, any remaining NR component is added to the fit if its significance exceeds 5
\sigma
. The iteration process ends when all retained components satisfy the above criteria. The final baseline solution includes
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{\pm}\pi^{\mp}
and
T_{c\bar{c}1}(3900)^{\pm}\pi^{\mp}
resonances, the non-resonance
\mathrm{NR}^{(0^{-},\,1^{-},\,2^{-})}D^{*0}
,
(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})_{\mathrm{S-wave}}J/\psi
,
f_{2}(1270)J/\psi
, and
(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})_{\mathrm{S-wave}}h_{c}
.
To verify the robustness of the solution, the significance of each component excluded from the baseline is retested, and none is found to meet the significance criteria. These components will be considered for the assessment of systematic uncertainties related to the fitting model.

In the simultaneous fit performed at the two energy points

\sqrt{s}=4.395
and
4.416\,\mathrm{GeV}
, the coupling parameters that dominate the production of the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
state are constrained separately for each point. In contrast, the common parameters governing its decay are shared between them.

B. Spin and parity

In addition to the
1^{+}
assignment for the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
state, which is favored by the nominal fit, other spin-parity hypotheses (
1^{-}
,
2^{+}
, and
2^{-}
) require further testing. Note that the
0^{+}
assignment is forbidden by spin-parity conservation in
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*}\to T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}\pi^{+}
, and
0^{-}
is prohibited by
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}\to\pi^{-}h_{c}
. The amplitudes are reconstructed by replacing the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}(1^{+})
component with the
1^{-}
,
2^{+}
, or
2^{-}
assumption in the PWA fit.
The fit with the
1^{+}
hypothesis yields the minimum NLL value, which indicates the best spin-parity assignment for the resonance
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
. The fit quality deteriorates when considering alternative
J^{P}
hypotheses.

Furthermore, to test the spin-parity of the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
as
1^{+}
against other
J^{P}
assignments (
1^{-}
,
2^{+}
, and
2^{-}
), two hypotheses are examined
66
: the null hypothesis
H_{0}
and the alternative hypothesis
H_{1}
.
Under
H_{0}
, data are assumed to be consistent with a single
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
component with one of
J^{P}
assignment:
1^{-}
,
2^{+}
, or
2^{-}
, while the
H_{1}
posits that data can be better described with an additional
1^{+}

T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
component besides the
H_{0}
components.
Following the fit, the significance of the
1^{+}
assignment relative to other hypotheses is assessed by examining
\Delta(-2\ln\mathcal{L})
as a function of
\Delta(\mathrm{ndof})
to estimate the
p
-value and its corresponding significance level.
The detailed differences are summarized in the Table
10
in the methodology section
E
.
Comparing the hypotheses
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
, we obtain confidence levels of
18.0\sigma
,
18.7\sigma
, and
15.8\sigma
to exclude the hypotheses with only
J^{P}=1^{-}
,
2^{+}
, and
2^{-}
, respectively.

To consider the statistical fluctuation effect in estimating the
J^{P}
significance, an additional test is conducted using a pseudo-experiment based on MC simulations, in which variations according to systematic uncertainties are taken into account.
A large set of toy MC samples is generated according to the fitted amplitude model, then subjected to detector response and the same event selection criteria as applied to real data.
In each toy MC sample, the yields of signals and backgrounds are set to those observed in data, with fluctuations according to the Poisson distributions. Based on the NLL values of the PWA fits under different
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}

J^{P}
assumptions, the likelihood ratio variable
t\equiv\Delta(-2\ln\mathcal{L})=-2\ln\mathcal{L}^{J^{P}}-(-2\ln\mathcal{L}^{1^{+}})
is used to evaluate the discrimination level.
The
t
distributions for various
J^{P}
hypotheses are illustrated in Fig.
4
. In each plot, the toy MC samples in the right peak are generated with the amplitude model under the
J^{P}=1^{+}
hypothesis, while the remaining samples are generated assuming
J^{P}=1^{-}
,
2^{+}
, or
2^{-}
. It is evident that all
t
values from nominal fit to data favor the toy samples generated under the
J^{P}=1^{+}
hypothesis.
The
t
distributions can be well-fitted with Gaussian functions with parameters
(\overline{t},\,\sigma_{t})
.
The deviations between data and the toy samples assuming
J^{P}=1^{-}
,
2^{+}
, and
2^{-}
are calculated as
(t_{\mathrm{data}}-\overline{t})/\sigma_{t}
, yielding confidence levels of rejection power of
14.1\sigma
,
13.3\sigma
, and
11.7\sigma
, respectively. Therefore, the spin-parity of
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
is established to be
1^{+}
.

V. SYSTEMATIC UNCERTAINTIES

The sources of systematic uncertainties in this analysis can be divided into two categories: the three-body cross sections, which affect only the FFs, and those from the PWA, which affect both the FFs and the pole positions.

For the systematic uncertainty from three-body cross sections, those associated with cross section measurement include tracking, particle identification (PID), photon detection, ISR factor, integrated luminosity, cited BFs, MC model, and fitting. These relevant systematic uncertainties of three processes are quoted directly from Refs.
16
;
20
;
19
, as 7.3%, 5.8%, 16.3% at
\sqrt{s}=4.395\,\mathrm{GeV}
and 7.0%, 5.8%, 16.3% at
\sqrt{s}=4.416\,\mathrm{GeV}
.

The systematic uncertainties associated with the pole position of the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
and the FFs arise predominantly from the model dependencies of the
\pi\pi
S-wave amplitude, the background estimation, the fit model,
T_{c\bar{c}1}(3900)^{\pm}
propagator, other fixed parameters, the fit strategy and the mass resolution. For each
viable term, the difference between the nominal and the alternative fit results is taken as the systematic uncertainty.
The uncertainty of
\pi\pi
S-wave amplitude is estimated by replacing these amplitudes with
\pi\pi
scattering amplitudes, which have been used in the soft
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
mass spectrum analyses by CLEO
24
and BESIII
17
. The uncertainty from the background estimation is determined by altering the sideband regions and refitting.
The modified
J/\psi
mass sideband regions in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
are
(3.020,\,3.050)
or
(3.150,\,3.180)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
, and the modified
h_{c}
mass sideband regions in
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
are
(3.470,\,3.490)
or
(3.560,\,3.580)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
. For the
D^{*0}D^{*-}\pi^{+}
channel, the background-free analysis is performed under nominal conditions, and the few background events survived in the hadron MC sample are adopted to check the systematic uncertainty. The uncertainty from the fit model is studied by adding the NR component with highest significance out of the baseline (
\mathrm{NR}(D^{*-}\pi^{+})^{2^{+}}D^{*0}
,
\mathrm{NR}(\pi^{-}J/\psi)^{2^{-}}\pi^{+}
,
\mathrm{NR}(\pi^{-}h_{c})^{2^{+}}\pi^{+}
) to check their influence.
The contribution of
D_{1}(2420)
at
\sqrt{s}=4.395\,\mathrm{GeV}
is also investigated. The uncertainty from fit model variations is quantified by combining two independent sources in quadrature.
T_{c\bar{c}}(3900)
is described by a Flatte-type BW in baseline, which is varied to a constant width BW
23
as the alternative. The uncertainty of the introduced propagator parameters is estimated by changing the fixed parameters within one standard deviation individually and summing up the contributions in quadrature.
Potential fitting biases in the PWA fit are extensively tested with toy MC studies, and the differences between the generation truths and the fitted outcomes are taken as the systematic uncertainties from the fit strategy. The detector resolution is not considered in the nominal fit, and the resulting uncertainty on the pole width of the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
is evaluated by comparing the input width in the simulation and the reconstructed one, considering the detector responses.

J^{P}
assignments are all above
14.1\sigma
. Detailed numbers are summarized in Table

A multi-channel joint analysis of the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
state is performed for the first time using data samples collected with the BESIII detector at the BEPCII collider. The data were taken at center-of-mass energies of
\sqrt{s}=4.395
and
4.416\,\mathrm{GeV}
, with a total integrated luminosity of
1598.9\,\mathrm{pb}^{-1}
. The analysis is based on the processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to D^{*0}D^{*-}\pi^{+}
,
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
, and
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}
.
The spin-parity of the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
is determined for the first time to be
1^{+}
with the significance larger than
11.7\sigma
. The pole mass and width of the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
are determined to be
m_{\mathrm{pole1}}=(4022.44\pm 1.55\pm 1.26)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{pole1}}=(38.54\pm 2.94\pm 2.95)\,\mathrm{MeV}
and
m_{\mathrm{pole2}}=(4023.01\pm 1.35\pm 0.98)\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{pole2}}=(35.02\pm 2.20\pm 2.68)\,\mathrm{MeV}
.
Based on the fit, the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
Born cross sections times the corresponding BFs are obtained and summarized in Table
5
.

T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
decaying into
D^{*0}D^{*-}
,
\pi^{-}J/\psi

\pi^{-}h_{c}
are derived for the first time as

T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
exhibits a significantly stronger coupling to
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
process than to hidden-charm channels.

With the determination of
J^{P}=1^{+}
, the notation of the
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
can be updated to
T_{c\bar{c}1}(4020)^{-}
following the PDG naming scheme.
The alignment of its spin-parity with that of the
T_{c\bar{c}1}(3900)

T_{c\bar{c}1}
being
D^{*}\bar{D}^{(*)}
molecules’ structure rather than a charmonium core.

Eight poles are investigated and searched on the eight-sheet Riemann surface due to three branch points, as summarized in Table
9
.
Several of them (Poles 2, 5-8) reside on non-adjacent Riemann sheets relative to the physical region
63
, whose contributions are suppressed but non-negligible.
Additional experimental data are necessary to further constrain the above estimation, and more theoretical research is required to quantify the relative contributions of the distinct poles.

VII. METHODOLOGY

A. Event selections

The charged tracks and photon candidates are identified based on the criteria outlined in Ref.
22
. Candidate events for the three signal processes are selected as follows.

1.
e^{+}e^{-}\to D^{*0}D^{*-}\pi^{+}

Two decay channels of
D^{*0}
in the signal processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to D^{*0}D^{*-}\pi^{+}
are considered:
D^{*0}\to D^{0}\pi^{0}
or
D^{0}\gamma
, while
D^{*-}\to\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{-}
.
We utilize a partial reconstruction method to reconstruct those decays, in which
\pi^{-}
originated from
D^{*-}
decays are not reconstructed because of their low momentum and low detection efficiency.

Both
D^{0}
and
\bar{D}^{0}
are reconstructed via three dominant channels:
D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}
,
K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
, and
K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
.
The reconstructed invariant masses are required to fall within the range of
(1.850,\,1.880)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
. The charged kaons and pions from the
D^{0}
(
\bar{D}^{0}
) decays are identified by comparing PID likelihoods, which are formed by combining measurements of the energy deposited in the MDC (d
E
/d
x
) and the flight time in the TOF based on the kaon and pion hypotheses,
\mathcal{L}(K)>\mathcal{L}(\pi)
and
\mathcal{L}(\pi)>\mathcal{L}(K)
, respectively.
Then, one
\pi^{0}
or
\gamma
is detected and associated with the reconstructed
D^{0}
.
The
\pi^{0}
from the
D^{*0}\to\pi^{0}D^{0}
decay is reconstructed by two photons with an invariant mass within the interval
(0.120,\,0.145)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
.
In addition, the charged
\pi^{+}
originating from the primary production vertex, labelled as bachelor
\pi^{+}
, are identified with the PID requirement by looping over all charged tracks.
The reconstructed
D^{0}\pi^{+}
invariant mass is required to be
MQ(D^{0}\pi^{+})>2.03\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
to reject the
\pi^{+}
from
D^{*+}
decay.
Here and in the following, a resolution decorrelation procedure is applied to improve the resolution of the reconstructed invariant masses:
for the invariant mass reconstructed by particles
A
and
B
, one can decorrelate the instrumental resolution effect of the intermediate state
B
by adopting the variables
MQ(AB)=M(AB)-M(B)+m(B)
and
RQ(AB)=RM(AB)+M(B)-m(B)
, where
M
,
RM
, and
m
are the invariant mass, the recoil invariant mass, and known mass, respectively.

B
represents the intermediate states (
D^{0}
,
\bar{D}^{0}
, and
\pi^{0}
).

At

or
MQ(\gamma D^{0})\in(1.995,\,2.015)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
.
The signal candidates are identified by requiring the recoil invariant mass
RQ(D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{+})
falling within
(0.120,\,0.160)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}

Events with the recoil invariant mass

(1.990,\,2.030)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
or

(1.990,\,2.040)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
are kept for further analysis.

For the events that meet the aforementioned criteria, a kinematic fit is performed to constrain the reconstructed
D^{0}
,
D^{*0}
, and
\bar{D}^{0}
to their respective known masses.
Additionally, the recoil mass of
D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{+}
and
D^{*0}\pi^{+}
are constrained to the known mass of
\pi^{-}
and
D^{*-}

52
, respectively.
For the
D^{*0}\to D^{0}\pi^{0}
decay, an additional mass constraint is applied to the reconstructed invariant mass of
\pi^{0}
.
If multiple candidates survive in a single event, the one with the minimum
\chi^{2}
value from the kinematic fit is retained.

After applying all the selection criteria outlined above, the background contribution is expected to be negligible, as illustrated by the
RQ(D^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+})
distribution depicted in the left panel of Fig.
5
. Therefore, we perform a nearly background-free analysis in the case of the
e^{+}e^{-}\to D^{*0}D^{*-}\pi^{+}
process, and the influence of the backgrounds is treated as a systematic uncertainty.

2.
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi

With the same event selection criteria in Ref.
20
, the
J/\psi
candidates are reconstructed by
l^{+}l^{-}(l=e,\mu)
pairs.
The number of selected charged tracks in each event must be equal to four for the signal process
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}l^{+}l^{-}
.
Charged tracks with momentum in the MDC less than
1.06\,\mathrm{GeV}/c
are assumed to be pions, while the others are considered as leptons.
The leptons with deposited energy in the EMC,
E_{\mathrm{EMC}}<0.35\,\mathrm{GeV}
are classified as muons, while those with
E_{\mathrm{EMC}}>1.1\,\mathrm{GeV}
are treated as electrons.

To remove the backgrounds from radiative Bhabha and radiative dimuon events with
e^{+}e^{-}
from
\gamma
-conversion misidentified as
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
candidates, the cosine of the angle between
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{-}
in the laboratory system
\cos\theta_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}
is required to be less than
0.98
.
The four-momentum (4C) kinematic fit is performed to constrain the momentum of
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}l^{+}l^{-}
to the initial
e^{+}e^{-}
system with a fit quality requirement
\chi^{2}_{\rm 4C}<40
to improve the momentum resolution and reduce the background.
Moreover, for the
J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-}
process, the constraint of the cosine of the angle between pion and electron
\cos\theta_{\pi^{\pm}e^{\mp}}<0.98
is applied to reject background from
\gamma
-conversion.
To improve the purity of
J/\psi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays, we require that the number of hit layers in the muon chamber must be larger than 5 for at least one muon candidate.

The events that remain within the mass window of
M(l^{+}l^{-})\in(3.090,\,3.105)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
are classified as signal candidates. The background events in the signal region are estimated using the sideband range of the
J/\psi
invariant mass spectrum, defined as
M(l^{+}l^{-})\in(3.030,\,3.060)\cup(3.140,\,3.170)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
, as shown in the middle panel of Fig.
5
.
Finally, the four-momentum updated from kinematic 4C with additional mass constraint of
J/\psi
is adopted to further the PWA fit.

3.
e^{+}e^{-}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}h_{c}

The event selection criteria for this channel are consistent with Ref.
19
.
For the

process, where

decays into
\gamma\eta_{c}

\eta_{c}

,

2(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})

2(K^{+}K^{-})

\pi^{+}\pi^{-}K^{+}K^{-}

h_{c}

For the decay channels with an intermediate state

is the
K_{S}^{0}
nominal mass

The

The

and
\eta

The same 4C method used in the

process is applied to all final particles.
In cases where one event can be selected by different channels with the same number of charged tracks, the best candidate is chosen based on a combination of chi squares from the 4C kinematic fit, the PID of each charged track, and the mass-constrainted fit of
\pi^{0}/\eta
, represented by
\chi^{2}=\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{4C}}+\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{PID}}+\chi^{2}_{\pi^{0}/\eta}

\chi^{2}
value is retained for further analysis.

(2.934,\,3.034)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
, and the four-momentum kinematic fit must have
\chi^{2}_{\rm 4C}<35

For final states including
\pi^{0}/\eta
candidates, we require

(2.939,\,3.029)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}

\chi^{2}_{\rm 4C}<20
.

\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
from
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilations and
\gamma
from
h_{c}
decays in an event, the candidate with the minimum
|M(\eta_{c})-m_{\eta_{c}}|
is kept in each event, where
m_{\eta_{c}}
is the
\eta_{c}
nominal mass
52
.

The signal events are chosen with
RM(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
falling within the
h_{c}
signal mass window
(3.515,\,3.535)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}
. The background events are estimated using the
h_{c}
mass sidebands, with
RM(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
in
(3.475,\,3.495)
or
(3.555,\,3.575)\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^{2}

5

Finally, the four-momentum updated from kinematic 4C with additional mass constraint of
h_{c}
is adopted to further the PWA fit.

4. Alignment rotation

Under the
SU(2)
group, the rotation and boost operations can be expressed as

\displaystyle R_{z}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}&0\\
0&e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}\end{pmatrix},\,R_{y}(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}&-\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\sin\frac{\theta}{2}&\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle B_{z}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-\frac{\omega}{2}}&0\\
0&e^{\frac{\omega}{2}}\end{pmatrix},

where
\omega=\tanh^{-1}{\frac{|\bf{p}|}{E}}
, with
\bf{p}
and
{E}
the boosted momentum and energy, respectively.
\theta
and
\phi
are the helicity angles of the decay. The alignment rotation can be expressed by a 2-D matrix. For instance, in the first (reference) subprocess
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*}\to T_{c\bar{c}}^{-}\pi^{+}
,

,

\gamma
is

\displaystyle L_{T_{c\bar{c}}^{-}}=

\displaystyle R_{y}(\theta^{e^{+}e^{-}}_{T_{c\bar{c}}^{-}})R_{z}(\phi^{e^{+}e^{-}}_{T_{c\bar{c}}^{-}})B_{z}(\omega^{e^{+}e^{-}}_{T_{c\bar{c}}^{-}})R_{y}(\theta^{T_{c\bar{c}}^{-}}_{h_{c}})R_{z}(\phi^{T_{c\bar{c}}^{-}}_{h_{c}})

\displaystyle\cdot B_{z}(\omega^{T_{c\bar{c}}^{-}}_{h_{c}})R_{y}(\theta^{h_{c}}_{\gamma})R_{z}(\phi^{h_{c}}_{\gamma})B_{z}(\omega^{h_{c}}_{\gamma}).

(26)

Similar in second subprocess

\displaystyle R_{y}(\theta^{e^{+}e^{-}}_{T_{c\bar{c}}^{+}})R_{z}(\phi^{e^{+}e^{-}}_{T_{c\bar{c}}^{+}})B_{z}(\omega^{e^{+}e^{-}}_{T_{c\bar{c}}^{+}})R_{y}(\theta^{T_{c\bar{c}}^{+}}_{h_{c}})R_{z}(\phi^{T_{c\bar{c}}^{+}}_{h_{c}})

C. Likelihood Function in PWA Fit

The complex coupling constants
g_{LS}
, the resonance mass of
T_{c\bar{c}}(4020)^{-}
and the value of
\overline{\sum}|H_{X^{\prime}}|^{2}
are determined by an unbinned maximum-likelihood fit to data.
The likelihood function of each channel at one energy point is constructed according to
46
:

-\ln\mathcal{L}=-\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{data}}}\ln p^{(i)}_{\mathrm{data}}-\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{bkg}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{bkg}}}\ln p^{(i)}_{\mathrm{bkg}}-\mu\right),

(29)

where
\alpha=\frac{N_{\mathrm{data}}-\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{bkg}}N_{\mathrm{bkg}}}{N_{\mathrm{data}}+\mathcal{W}^{2}_{\mathrm{bkg}}N_{\mathrm{bkg}}}
is a normalization factor to account for the background level
46
and the numbers are quoted from Table
1
.,
p^{(i)}_{\mathrm{data}(\mathrm{bkg})}=\epsilon(x_{i})|A(x_{i})|^{2}
is the likelihood function for
i
-th data (background) event, while
\mu=\int\epsilon(x_{i})|A(x_{i})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\Phi
represents the likelihood value from integral MC sample.
The function
\epsilon(x_{i})
is the detection efficiency, which depends on the four-momentum
x_{i}
of final particles.

\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{bkg}}
is the weight factor for each background event,
and is given by the ratio of the lengths of the signal and sideband ranges.
The modulus squared
|A|^{2}
for the three channels is defined as

|A|^{2}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{\lambda_{\gamma^{*}},\,\lambda_{\pi^{0}(\gamma)}}\left|A_{D}(\lambda_{\gamma^{*}},\,\lambda_{\pi^{0}(\gamma)})\right|^{2},\\
&\sum_{\lambda_{\gamma^{*}},\,\lambda_{l^{+}},\,\lambda_{l^{-}}}\left|A_{J}(\lambda_{\gamma^{*}},\,\lambda_{l^{+}},\,\lambda_{l^{-}})\right|^{2},\\
&\sum_{\lambda_{\gamma^{*}},\,\lambda_{\gamma}}\left|A_{H}(\lambda_{\gamma^{*}},\,\lambda_{\gamma})\right|^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

To perform a simultaneous fit to data corresponding to the three signal processes at the two energy points, the likelihood
\ln\mathcal{L}
is independently calculated for each data set and then aggregated to form the objective function across the six data sets as

\displaystyle S=-\ln\mathcal{L}=-\sum_{n=1}^{6}\ln\mathcal{L}_{n},

(30)

which is minimized with
minuit

44
.
The leading-order rates of these three-body decay processes are incorporated to further constrain the coupling parameters used in extracting
\Gamma(m)
. In the PWA fit, the integral of each MC sample is fixed to the leading-order rates for these three processes.
