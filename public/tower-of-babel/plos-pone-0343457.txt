An efficient brain tumor classification system with accurate recognition and complementary downsampling blocks

Jincan Zhang, Chuanqi Cai, XingHua Tan, Wenna Chen, Xin Zhao, Ganqin Du, Hongwei Jiang

ABSTRACT
Due to the substantial impact of brain tumors on human health, precise identification of tumor types is vital for patient prognosis and guiding treatment decisions. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) technology, owing to its non-invasive nature and high resolution, is indispensable in accurately classifying brain tumor types. However, manual interpretation of MRI images is not only time-consuming and labor-intensive but also susceptible to subjective bias. Hence, many automatic brain tumor diagnosis systems utilizing deep learning techniques have been developed. This paper introduces an advanced modularized Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) that incorporates ConvDAtt block, Complementary Information (CI) downsampling block, and Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) block. Firstly, the ConvDAtt block combines convolution, depth-separable convolution and Coordinated Attention (CA) mechanism. The module alleviates the gradient vanishing problem in deep neural network training, enhancing the training stability and performance of the model. In the ConvDAtt block, the CA mechanism can not only capture information across channels, but also sense direction and position sensitive information, improving the accuracy of the model’s identification of objects of interest. Secondly, the CI block is a downsampling block with convolution and pooling functions. In the CI block, there is a complementary relationship between the pooling layer and the convolution layer, which is mainly reflected in two aspects, feature extraction and dimensionality reduction, enlargement of receptive field. Moreover, the complementary relationship can solve the problem of information loss in the process of downsampling, while also significantly reducing computational cost. Thirdly, the SPP block extracts features from different scales of the input data and integrates these features, thus enhancing the performance of the network, and enhancing the robustness of the model. The combination of ConvDAtt block, CI block and SPP block can effectively extract the information features and discriminant features of brain tumor MRI images, which has the advantages of parameter reduction, stable training and clear structure.

1. Introduction Brain tumors are primarily categorized into two distinct types, primary brain tumors and brain metastases. Surgical intervention, particularly complete resection, is often regarded as the optimal approach for managing this condition [ 1 ]. Prior to treatment, a thorough evaluation of the tumor is imperative to ensure the effectiveness of the therapeutic intervention. MRI, a widely utilized imaging technique for elucidating the shape, size, and location of brain tumors, holds a crucial role in this assessment [ 2 ]. However, the analysis of MRI images requires professional expertise, rendering it a potentially error-prone process. Therefore, research on automatic diagnosis of brain tumors is of great significance to patients, doctors, technicians and hospitals [ 3 ]. Traditional Machine Learning (ML) algorithms have long been prevalent in the medical field and widely employed [ 4 ]. For example, Twinkle Bansal et al. [ 5 ] have combined the Grayscale Covariance Matrix (GLAM) technique with shape features to comprehensively and accurately extract mixed tumor region characteristics and a hybrid classification strategy incorporating Random Forest (RF), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Decision Tree (DT) classifiers has been employed to enhance classification accuracy and stability. In [ 6 ], the Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) and Gray Level Run Length Matrix (GLRLM) were utilized to extract image features. Then, a Probabilistic Neural Network (PNN) classifier was utilized to categorize MRI brain images. In [ 7 ], multiple supervised machine learning were compared in depth to assess their actual effectiveness for internal effectiveness and accuracy in predicting clinical events. In [ 8 ], ML algorithms have been applied to predict the development of cancer symptoms and have successfully identified predictors of these symptoms. In [ 9 ], the categorization of brain tumors was successfully accomplished through the integration of various techniques, Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Feedforward Backpropagation Artificial Neural Network (FP-ANN), and the KNN classifier. These algorithms heavily rely on hand-crafted features, resulting in time-consuming processes prone to errors [ 10 ]. Convolutional Neural Networks(CNNs) have effectively addressed this issue by autonomously learning invariant features, showcasing superior performance and gaining wide application [ 11 ]. Recently, CNNs have yielded outstanding outcomes in brain tumor classification. In [ 12 ], a CNN is used to classify primary brain tumors. To screen out the best-performing models, a series of experiments were designed and conducted. Finally, a CNN architecture with an overall performance of 96% was successfully built. Roy et al. [ 13 ] conducted in-depth comparative analysis of simple artificial neural networks including basic CNNs and extended CNNs. The experimental results show that extended CNN exhibits excellent performance in classification tasks, with an accuracy rate of 97%. G. Vidya Sagar et al. [ 14 ] introduced a residual learning technique tailored for brain tumor classification, involving three core steps, preprocessing, feature extraction, and classification. In [ 15 ], a technique for training CNN was introduced, employing a class-weighted focal loss approach. In addition, in order to further improve the effectiveness of the classifier, this study utilized the integrated features of multiple CNN models. In [ 16 ], the proposed framework adopted transfer learning, wherein deep features were extracted from MRI images using a pre-trained deep convolutional neural network. Subsequently, these features undergone evaluation with multiple machine learning classifiers, and the top three best-performing classifiers were selected and integrated into a deep feature set to enhance feature representation and classification accuracy. In [ 17 ], a stacked modeling strategy was proposed, which significantly improved the final accuracy of the model and effectively overcame the challenge of insufficient labeled training data. Three CNN architecture models namely VGG19, Inception V3, and ResNet101 have been adequately trained on medical image datasets and were used in this study. Khan et al. [ 18 ] proposed a model that adds three additional layers to the MobileNet backbone and integrates residual learning, while replacing the ReLU activation function with Swish to improve information retention and feature representation. This model achieved an accuracy of 96.94% on the Figshare multi-class classification dataset. In order to further improve the accuracy of the model, many novel models have appeared in recent years. For example, in [ 19 ], DenseNet201, ResNet152V2, and InceptionResNetV2 were utilized as pre-trained models, forming a stacked ensemble model to enhance generalization ability. In [ 20 ], six optimization algorithms were combined with two classifiers, softmax and Support Vector Machine (SVM). The best performance was observed when the softmax classifier was combined with the Nesterov Accelerated Adaptive Moment Estimation (Nadam) optimizer. Muhammad Irfan et al. [ 21 ] used multiple pre-trained models (ResNet50, VGG16, VGG19 and U-Net) to classify brain tumors or no tumors. Among the pre-trained models compared, the U-Net model showed excellent performance with an accuracy of 95%. Bala Venkateswarlu Isunuri et al. [ 22 ] proposed an Ensemble Coupled Convolutional Network (ECCN) model designed specifically for classifying three distinct types of brain tumors. This proposed model was evaluated on the REMBRANDT dataset, demonstrating an accuracy rate of 96.95%. Gomez-Guzman et al. [ 23 ] conducted an evaluation of seven deep convolutional neural network (CNN) models in the context of brain tumor classification, with one model being generic and the remaining six being pre-trained. Amongst all the models compared, InceptionV3 emerged as the superior model, achieving an impressive average accuracy of 97.12%. Trisheela et al. [ 24 ] utilized VGGNet for feature extraction, employed Invasive Weed Optimization (IWO) to select the most relevant features, and utilized an enhanced DarkNet-53 deep learning (DL) model to classify brain tumors. The model achieved an accuracy of 95% on a dataset consisting of 3,264 MRI scan images. Tariq et al. [ 25 ] achieved an accuracy rate of 96% on the brain tumor MRI dataset by combining EfficientNetV2 for efficient feature extraction and Vision Transformer (ViT) for capturing global contextual information, and applying geometric mean ensemble learning to integrate the predictions from both models. Clinically, the precise identification of brain tumor types directly informs treatment planning, surgical strategy, and prognostic evaluation. However, manual MRI interpretation is labor-intensive and subject to inter-observer variability, which poses significant challenges in time-sensitive clinical environments. Therefore, developing an automated and reliable classification system holds great potential to address these challenges in clinical practice. In this paper, we propose a modular deep convolutional neural network specifically designed for high-accuracy, multi-class brain tumor classification. The network incorporates three key components: the ConvDAtt block, which combines convolution, depth-separable convolution, coordinate attention (CA), and residual connections to enhance feature extraction and training stability [ 26 ], the Complementary Information (CI) downsampling block, which exploits the complementary strengths of convolution and pooling to preserve rich feature information during downsampling, and the Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) block, which captures multi-scale spatial information and aggregates it into a fixed-length feature vector [ 27 ]. This modular design not only achieves state-of-the-art classification accuracy but also significantly reduces computational overhead, thereby serving as a promising methodological framework that could potentially inform future clinical decision-support systems, pending rigorous prospective validation in real-world settings. The effectiveness of the proposed method is extensively validated on two public benchmark datasets, consistently demonstrating substantial advantages over existing approaches. It introduces an efficient modular brain tumor classification network [ 28 ] that improves classification accuracy through the synergistic interaction of multiple modules, showing strong potential as a reliable auxiliary system for computer-aided diagnosis. The contribution of this research includes the following aspects: A novel modular convolutional neural network is proposed for brain tumor image classification from MRI. The ConvDAtt block, integrating convolution, depth-separable convolution, CA mechanism, and residual connectivity, is proposed to enhance network performance and training efficiency. The introduction of CA mechanism [ 29 ] helps the network to extract key features from the original data, thus improving the performance of the model. The CI downsampling block leverages the complementary strengths of convolution and pooling to preserve rich spatial information during downsampling, while also substantially improving computational efficiency. The SPP block is employed to capture multi-scale spatial information from input feature maps, which further strengthens the model’s robustness against image quality degradation. The proposed method demonstrates superior effectiveness and accuracy compared to prior art methods, as evidenced by experimental results using two public datasets. Preprocessing The Chen dataset comprises grayscale images uniformly sized at 512 × 512 pixels. To ensure compatibility and computational efficiency, preprocessing was conducted [ 32 ]. Initially, all images were resized uniformly to 224 × 224 pixels, aligning them with the input requirements of the deep learning model. Subsequently, considering the model’s expectation of receiving three-channel RGB input, the original grayscale images were transformed into three-channel RGB format by duplicating the grayscale values across the RGB channels. For the Kaggle dataset, inconsistencies exist in image size and color space, with some being grayscale and others RGB. To ensure data consistency and model training stability, similar preprocessing steps were applied. Specifically, all images were uniformly resized to 224 × 224 pixels, and grayscale images were converted to the three-channel RGB format. This standardization of image size and color space across both datasets facilitates subsequent deep learning model training. 2.3. Classification system The proposed brain tumor classification network, designed with a modular approach, efficiently captures informative and discriminative features from brain tumor MRI images. The network is specifically constructed to preserve significant spatial features in brain MRI images, ensuring the integrity and accuracy of essential information. Considering the importance of key features and capturing multi-scale spatial information in brain tumor MRI image classification, the CA mechanism and spatial pyramid pool are utilized to further improve classification accuracy. The integration enables the network to better discern and differentiate between different types of brain tumors. ConvDAtt block. The ConvDAtt module is illustrated in Fig 4 . The input data first passes through Conv1, followed by depth-separable convolution, the CA attention mechanism, Conv2, and finally a residual connection. 10.1371/journal.pone.0343457.g004 Fig 4 ConvDAtt block. Initially the Conv1 layer is used. This convolutional layer is designed to extract effective features from the input data. Subsequently, a depth-separable convolution is used to reduce the number of parameters and improve the computational efficiency of the model. Subsequently, the Coordinate Attention (CA) mechanism is employed. Because traditional attention mechanisms (e.g., SENet, ECANet) rely on global pooling, it is commonly used in channel attention to encode spatial information. However, global pooling compresses spatial information into a single channel descriptor, making it difficult to retain positional cues, which are essential for capturing spatial structures in visual tasks. In contrast, the CA mechanism enhances spatial feature recognition by embedding precise positional information into channel attention. The structure of the CA module is illustrated in Fig 5 . 10.1371/journal.pone.0343457.g005 Fig 5 Coordinate attention block. To enable attention blocks to more effectively capture long-range spatial dependencies with accurate positional information, the CA mechanism splits the computational process of channel attention into two independent directional components using a 1D feature encoding operation. Compared with traditional attention methods such as Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) and Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), which either ignore positional information or only capture local spatial dependencies, the CA mechanism provides a more efficient and comprehensive approach to capture inter-channel relationships and remote dependencies with precise positional information, improving spatial feature discrimination and thus helping the model to better classify brain tumor images. Subsequently, Conv2 is employed to reduce the computational complexity of the model. Finally residual connections were used to ensure that network information was not lost. 2.3.2. CI block. The structure of the CI block is shown in Fig 6 , which is designed to efficiently downsample the input data. Downsampling plays a crucial role in neural network architectures [ 33 ] to reduce the number of parameters and improve the generalization of the model. However, during the downsampling process, the model discards a portion of the data, which may lead to the loss of critical information. Therefore, convolution and pooling are proposed to complement each other to solve this problem. 10.1371/journal.pone.0343457.g006 Fig 6 CI block. The CI block is characterized by the complementary use of convolution and pooling. The convolutional layer captures detailed information such as edges in images through the sliding operation of convolution kernels on the input data. Conversely, the pooling layer diminishes the dimensionality of the feature map generated by the convolutional layer through max pooling, which effectively shrinks the feature map and mitigates data redundancy. This complementarity enables the network to fully extract useful information from images while also reducing computational complexity and storage requirements. Additionally, the complementarity is evident in the expansion of receptive fields. Due to the limited size of convolution kernels, the input region covered by a single convolutional layer (i.e., the receptive field) is also limited. However, the pooling layer enlarges the receptive fields of subsequent convolutional layers through downsampling, enabling the network to capture more global information. 2.4. Given the cumbersome and time-consuming nature of manual examination of brain tumors, the imperative need arises for the development of automated detection methods to aid physicians. A method for efficient detection of multiple types of brain tumors is proposed in this paper. Initially, the ConvDAtt block, featuring depthwise separable convolution, attention mechanisms, and residual connectivity, is utilized. The use of depthwise separable convolution reduces the number of parameters and improves the feature extraction capability of the proposed model. The introduced CA mechanism dynamically adjusts weight allocation based on input data characteristics, enabling the model to prioritize key features while disregarding irrelevant information. Secondly, the CI block is introduced, which is a downsampling module that combines convolution and pooling. In the CI block, the complementary nature of convolution and pooling helps to extract feature information and enables the CNN to make decisions using both local and global information while also significantly reducing the computational burden (decreasing FLOPs from 97.14G to 2.85G). Thirdly, the SPP block is introduced to capture multi-scale spatial features and enhance feature representation, which enhances model robustness. And the method’s effectiveness was verified through thorough ablation experiments. Experimental results show that the proposed method achieves 98.69% accuracy on Chen dataset and 97.25% accuracy on Kaggle dataset. These results demonstrate the competitive performance of the proposed method on both Chen and Kaggle datasets, further validating its effectiveness. With the increasing availability and diversity of medical image data, the generalization ability and robustness of deep learning models can be further improved. Therefore, future studies will focus on expanding datasets with larger-scale and more diverse samples. Moreover, external multi-center clinical validation will be explored to further evaluate the generalization capability of the proposed method. Future research directions also include multimodal image fusion, explainable artificial intelligence, and further exploration of practical deployment strategies.
