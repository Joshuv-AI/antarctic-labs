Letter regarding “The cardiovascular impact of chronic venous disease: A systematic review and meta-analysis”
Authors: Ercan Keleş, Alper Özbakkaloğlu
Published: 2026

We read with interest the recent systematic review and meta-analysis by Del Río-Solá et al1 evaluating the association between chronic venous disease (CVD) and cardiovascular (CV) risk. The topic is clinically relevant; however, several methodological issues warrant clarification.

The principal concern is the interpretation of the pooled effect estimate. The authors reported a pooled odds ratio (OR) of 0.92 for CV events among patients with CVD and concluded that CVD was associated with increased CV risk.1 Furthermore, the article reports a log OR of 0.916, which corresponds to an OR of approximately 2.50 rather than 0.92. An additional inconsistency arises from the reported 95% confidence interval of 0.14 to 1.69, which includes the null value of 1.0 despite a reported P value of .021. These findings make it difficult to determine the actual magnitude and direction of the reported association and warrant further clarification regarding the calculation and presentation of the pooled estimate.

Another important issue is the substantial heterogeneity observed across studies (I2 = 98%).1 Although the use of a random-effects model is appropriate, heterogeneity of this magnitude considerably limits the interpretability of pooled estimates. The included studies differed substantially in design, patient characteristics, outcome definitions, and adjustment strategies, raising concerns regarding the comparability of the combined data sets.2

Finally, the absence of a formal risk-of-bias assessment further complicates the interpretation of the pooled findings, particularly given marked between-study heterogeneity.3

Despite these concerns, the study makes an important contribution to the discussion regarding the systemic implications of CVD. Clarification of these statistical inconsistencies would strengthen confidence in the reported conclusions.
