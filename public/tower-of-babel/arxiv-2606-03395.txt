Self-interacting neutrinos in cosmological perturbation theory - integrating the collision kernel
Thomas Tram

Abstract
Cosmological constraints on self-interacting neutrinos require a Boltzmann hierarchy in which the collision term is projected onto momentum-averaged multipoles. We revisit the collision kernel for neutrino-neutrino scattering mediated by a light scalar and derive an exact analytic expression for the multipole integral that determines the coefficients α ℓ \alpha_{\ell} . The key idea is to express the integration kernel as angular derivatives of the Yukawa-potential e − P / 2 P \frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{P} , move the derivatives onto Legendre polynomials, and reduce the remaining momentum integrals to a single base family obeying a first-order recurrence. This gives an exact rational-plus- π 2 \pi^{2} representation for every multipole, together with a compact implementation based on exact rational arithmetic. We provide the recurrence relations, a closed form for the base integral, and an asymptotically constrained approximation suitable for Boltzmann codes such as class . Our numerical implementation is publicly available in the Jupyter notebook IntegralComputation.ipynb .

Within the standard model of particle physics neutrinos are strictly massless particles. However, it has long been known from both astrophysical and terrestrial neutrino oscillation observations that neutrinos do indeed possess non-zero masses (see e.g.

The fact that neutrino masses are tiny relative to the masses of other standard model fermions is very likely related to the fact that neutrinos are chargeless, allowing for a Majorana mass term and the possibility of tiny neutrino masses via the See-Saw mechanism (see e.g.

In any case, non-zero neutrino masses clearly point to neutrinos possessing interactions with new particle degrees of freedom beyond the standard model, usually referred to as non-standard or secret neutrino interactions (see
[

In cosmology, it has long been known that non-standard neutrino interactions can dramatically alter observables such as the cosmic microwave background anisotropy and properties of the cosmological large scale structure. This is possible because such interactions can prevent free-streaming of neutrinos provided that they are sufficiently strong

The cosmological impact of neutrino non-standard interactions in general depends strongly on specifics of the interaction, and many possible scenarios have been investigated

2.1
The Boltzmann Equation

Our starting point is the relativistic Boltzmann equation for the phase-space distribution

The collision term encapsulates all the relevant scattering processes for the particle species in focus.
As such, utilising the notation of ref.

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)\left(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\,\mathrm{d}x^{j}\right).

We will express any momentum in terms of the comoving momentum

Likewise, we split the phase-space distribution function of a species

\displaystyle f_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q},\tau)=\bar{f}_{i}(q,\tau)\left[1+\Psi_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q},\tau)\right].

(2.3)

Momentum averaging of the hierarchy

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}[\Psi_{\ell}](q)=\frac{\mathrm{N}T_{\nu,0}^{5}G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{a^{4}(2\pi)^{3}}\left[4\int\frac{q^{\prime}\bar{f}(q^{\prime})}{q\bar{f}(q)}\left[K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})-S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\right]\Psi_{\ell}(q^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}-\frac{80}{3}q\Psi_{\ell}(q)\right]\,,

(3.2)

and we defined
S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})
to match

\displaystyle S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv q^{2}q^{\prime 2}e^{-q}\times\begin{cases}\frac{10}{9}&\ell=0,\\
-\frac{5}{9}&\ell=1,\\
\frac{1}{9}&\ell=2,\\
0&\ell>2.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Our goal from here on is to rewrite this in terms of the momentum-averaged moments
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, such that we can match it with equation (2.7) of ref.
[
23
]
, thereby deriving a formula for the model-dependent
\alpha_{\ell}
coefficients used in numerical implementations of this cosmological model in
class

[
26
]
.

To do so, we will utilise eq. (46) of ref.
[
24
]
, but with the
\ell
th
moment expansion instead of the full perturbation.
The first step is to multiply
eq.

3.1
with
q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q
and integrate this over
q\geq 0
.
Since the background distribution is time-independent under the assumption of Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics, we may move the conformal time derivative operator outside the integral.
Then, we divide by the same weight
q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q
and integrate that over
q\geq 0
.
Ignoring the collision term
\mathcal{C}[\Psi_{\ell}](q)
for now, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\dot{\Psi}_{\ell}(q)\,\mathrm{d}q}{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\frac{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\left[\ell\Psi_{\ell-1}(q)-(\ell+1)\Psi_{\ell+1}(q)\right]\,\mathrm{d}q}{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\frac{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\Psi_{\ell}(q)\,\mathrm{d}q}{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell\frac{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\Psi_{\ell-1}(q)\,\mathrm{d}q}{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q}-(\ell+1)\frac{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\Psi_{\ell+1}(q)\,\mathrm{d}q}{\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q}\right]

(3.5)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}-(\ell+1)\mathcal{F}_{\ell+1}\right].

(3.6)

Including the collision term is then as simple as adding the momentum-averaged expression of it to
eq.

3.6

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}-(\ell+1)\mathcal{F}_{\ell+1}\right]+\frac{\int q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q\,q\bar{f}(q)\mathcal{C}[\Psi_{\ell}](q)}{\int q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q\,q\bar{f}(q)}.

(3.7)

The weight integral in the denominator is trivial, since
\bar{f}(q)
takes the Maxwell-Boltzmann form of
eq.

2.17
(see eg. equation (3.351.3) in
[
27
]
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q\,q\bar{f}(q)=\mathrm{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}q\,q^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-q}=6\mathrm{N}.

(3.8)

Recall now equation (2.7) of ref.
[
23
]
:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}-(\ell+1)\mathcal{F}_{\ell+1}\right]+\alpha_{\ell}\dot{\kappa}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\ell},

(3.9)

where

is the rate of change of the neutrino opacity given by
eq.

2.12
.
Clearly, this must match with
eq.

3.7
, or in other words,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell}\dot{\kappa}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{6\mathrm{N}}\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\mathcal{C}[\Psi_{\ell}](q)\,\mathrm{d}q.

(3.10)

3.2
Separable ansatz and the coefficient
\alpha_{\ell}

To proceed, we will enforce the “separable ansatz”, which implies that all the momentum dependence factors into the background distribution
\bar{f}(q)
, while the momentum-averaged moments
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
solely encapsulates the amplitudes.
The separable ansatz is well described in
[
13
]
and allows us to write the
\ell
th
moment expansion of the Boltzmann hierarchy as

\displaystyle\Psi_{\ell}(q)\sim-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\bar{f}(q)}{\mathrm{d}\ln q}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}.

(3.11)

As
\mathrm{d}\ln q=\mathrm{d}q/q
, the logarithmic derivative evaluates to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\bar{f}(q)}{\mathrm{d}\ln q}=q\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\bar{f}(q)}{\mathrm{d}q}=q\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}q}(\ln\mathrm{N}-q)=-q,

(3.12)

and hence

\displaystyle\Psi_{\ell}(q)\sim\frac{1}{4}q\mathcal{F}_{\ell}.

(3.13)

Inserting this in
eq.

3.2
, we get an expression for the collision operator under the separable ansatz,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}[\Psi_{\ell}](q)=\frac{\mathrm{N}T_{\nu,0}^{5}G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{a^{4}(2\pi)^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\left(\int\frac{q^{\prime 2}\bar{f}(q^{\prime})}{q\bar{f}(q)}\left[K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})-S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\right]\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}-\frac{20}{3}q^{2}\right).

(3.14)

Now we insert
in
eq.

3.10
to arrive at an explicit formula for the
\alpha_{\ell}
coefficients:

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6\mathrm{N}}\frac{-1}{aG_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}T_{\nu}^{5}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\frac{\mathrm{N}T_{\nu,0}^{5}G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{a^{4}(2\pi)^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\left(\int\frac{q^{\prime 2}\bar{f}(q^{\prime})}{q\bar{f}(q)}\left[K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})-S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\right]\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}-\frac{20}{3}q^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}q\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6(2\pi)^{3}}\int\left(\frac{20}{3}q^{5}\bar{f}(q)-\int q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\bar{f}(q^{\prime})\left[K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})-S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\right]\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}q\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{N}}{6(2\pi)^{3}}\int\left(\frac{20}{3}q^{5}\mathrm{e}^{-q}-\int q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\mathrm{e}^{-q^{\prime}}K(q,q^{\prime},\mu)P_{\ell}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}+\int q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\mathrm{e}^{-q^{\prime}}S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}q\,,

(3.15)

where the first term is evaluated via, once again, equation (3.351.3) in
[
27

, and
I_{\ell}
encapsulates everything that still involves integrals.
Similar to the first term, the

They are equal to

\displaystyle s_{0}

\displaystyle=640\,,\qquad s_{1}=-320\,,\qquad s_{2}=64\,,\qquad s_{\ell>2}=0.

(3.16)

4
Analytic reduction of the kernel

The remaining task is the evaluation of the dimensionless integral
I_{\ell}
in
eq.

3.23
. The key insight is that the full integral can be reduced to a finite sum of simpler momentum integrals whose recurrence relations are stable and easy to implement.

The strategy has three ingredients. First, the complicated integration kernel is written as
\mu
-derivatives of the base-kernel
\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{P}
which turns out to be analytically tractable. Second, integration by parts moves these derivatives onto Legendre polynomials, producing three finite angular mixing operators. Third, the momentum integrals generated by these operators are reduced to one base family,
\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}
, by recurrence relations.

4.1
The base momentum integrals

We use the symmetry under
q\leftrightarrow q^{\prime}
to work on the triangular domain
0<q^{\prime}<q<\infty
and multiply by two. On this domain the addition theorem for modified spherical Bessel functions,
DLMF eq. (10.60.3)
, gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{P}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)i_{k}\!\left(\frac{q^{\prime}}{2}\right)k_{k}\!\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)P_{k}(\mu),

(4.1)

where
i_{k}
and
k_{k}
are modified spherical Bessel functions. In this paper we use
i_{k}\equiv i_{k}^{(1)}
, i.e.
i_{k}
is always of the first kind. After the rescaling
q\mapsto 2q
and
q^{\prime}\mapsto 2q^{\prime}
, the momentum objects needed below are

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{m,n}_{k}\equiv\frac{2^{m+n+3}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}q\int_{0}^{q}\!\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\,q^{m}q^{\prime n}\mathrm{e}^{-q-q^{\prime}}i_{k}(q^{\prime})k_{k}(q).

(4.2)

The factor
2^{m+n+3}/\pi
is included so that the final formula below contains only rational angular weights.
The leading member,
\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}
, has the closed form given in appendix
A
. For computation it is more convenient to use its recurrence relation. The seed values are

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{0}

\displaystyle=2484,\qquad\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{1}=2060,\qquad\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{2}=1620,\qquad\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{3}=1260,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{4}

\displaystyle=-4641676+470400\pi^{2},

(4.3)

while, for
k\geq 5
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}=-\frac{(k+4)^{2}}{(k-4)^{2}}\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k-1}+\frac{80640}{(k-4)^{2}}.

(4.4)

Since the recurrence coefficients are rational, the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}=r_{k}+s_{k}\pi^{2},\qquad r_{k},s_{k}\in\mathbb{Q},

(4.5)

is preserved exactly.
The reduction of the full kernel also produces
\mathcal{I}^{4,2}_{k}+\mathcal{I}^{2,4}_{k}
and
\mathcal{I}^{3,3}_{k}
. These do not need an independent treatment, since they are related to
\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{4,2}_{k}+\mathcal{I}^{2,4}_{k}

\displaystyle=a_{k}\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}+b_{k},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{3,3}_{k}

\displaystyle=c_{k}\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}+d_{k},

(4.7)

where

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&k<4,\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{24}{(k-3)(k-2)(k+3)(k+4)},&k\geq 4,\end{cases}

(4.8)

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}168,&k=0,\\
120,&k=1,\\
84,&k=2,\\
60,&k=3,\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1440(k^{2}+k-33)}{(k-3)(k-2)(k+3)(k+4)},&k\geq 4,\end{cases}

(4.9)

\displaystyle c_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&k<4,\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{16}{(k-3)^{2}(k+4)^{2}},&k\geq 4,\end{cases}

(4.10)

\displaystyle d_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}76,&k=0,\\
56,&k=1,\\
40,&k=2,\\
23716-2400\pi^{2},&k=3,\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{720(k^{2}+k-40)}{(k^{2}+k-12)^{2}},&k\geq 4.\end{cases}

(4.11)

The derivation of (
4.4
), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
) is summarised in appendix
B
. For
k<4
the
b_{k}
and
d_{k}
entries were found from direct evaluations of the corresponding integrals. This is necessary since the general recurrences are not well-defined for small
k
.

4.2
Removing the high inverse powers of
P

The apparent obstacle in
eq.

3.23
is the factor
P^{-5}
. This can be eliminated before doing the angular integral. From the law of cosines we find,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=-\frac{qq^{\prime}}{P}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}P},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{2}}=q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\left(\frac{1}{P}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}P}\right)^{2}.

(4.12)

Acting on the two simple functions
\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}/P
and
P\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{P}\right)

\displaystyle=4q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{16P^{5}}\left(P^{2}+6P+12\right),

(4.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{2}}\left(P\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}\right)

\displaystyle=4q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{16P^{5}}P^{2}\left(3P^{2}-2P-4\right)-q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{2P}.

(4.14)

Using these identities in the kernel and replacing
P\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}=P^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}/P
gives the exact decomposition

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{Q_{-}/2}K(q,q^{\prime},\mu)

\displaystyle=2(1-\mu)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{2}}\left(\left[q^{2}+q^{\prime 2}+qq^{\prime}(1-\mu)\right]\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{P}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+q^{2}q^{\prime 2}(1-\mu)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}}{2P}.

(4.15)

Thus every remaining angular integral involves only the base kernel
\mathrm{e}^{-P/2}/P
.

It is convenient to define the angular function
u_{\ell}
,

\displaystyle u_{\ell}(\mu)\equiv(1-\mu)^{2}P_{\ell}(\mu).

(4.16)

Integrating the first term of
eq.

4.15
by parts twice moves the
\mu
-derivatives onto
u_{\ell}
.
The endpoint at
\mu=1
vanishes because both
u_{\ell}
and
u_{\ell}^{\prime}
vanish there.
At
\mu=-1
, where
P=q+q^{\prime}
, the remaining boundary term can be evaluated analytically and is simply

\displaystyle B_{\ell}=-48(-1)^{\ell}\left[7+2\ell(\ell+1)\right]\,.

(4.17)

After the triangular-domain factor of two is included, the physical bulk angular operators multiplying
\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}
,
\mathcal{I}^{4,2}_{k}+\mathcal{I}^{2,4}_{k}
, and
\mathcal{I}^{3,3}_{k}
are

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}=u_{\ell}(\mu),\qquad\mathcal{I}^{4,2}_{k}+\mathcal{I}^{2,4}_{k}=4u_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\mu),\qquad\mathcal{I}^{3,3}_{k}=4(1-\mu)u_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\mu).

(4.18)

The bulk terms are controlled by three angular operators,

\displaystyle O^{(1)}_{\ell}(\mu)

\displaystyle=u_{\ell}(\mu),

(4.19)

\displaystyle O^{(2)}_{\ell}(\mu)

\displaystyle=4u_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\mu),

(4.20)

\displaystyle O^{(3)}_{\ell}(\mu)

\displaystyle=4(1-\mu)u_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(\mu).

(4.21)

We define the weights

\displaystyle O^{(i)}_{\ell}(\mu)=\sum_{k\geq 0}W^{(i)}_{\ell k}P_{k}(\mu),\qquad W^{(i)}_{\ell k}=\frac{2k+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}O^{(i)}_{\ell}(\mu)P_{k}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(4.22)

The explicit forms are

\displaystyle W^{(1)}_{\ell k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k+1}{2}\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)(2\ell+5)},&k=\ell+2,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{-4(\ell+1)}{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)},&k=\ell+1,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{4(3\ell^{2}+3\ell-2)}{(2\ell-1)(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)},&k=\ell,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{-4\ell}{(2\ell-1)(2\ell+1)},&k=\ell-1,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2\ell(\ell-1)}{(2\ell-3)(2\ell-1)(2\ell+1)},&k=\ell-2,\\[6.0pt]
0,&\mathrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

(4.23)

\displaystyle W^{(2)}_{\ell k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k+1}{2}\begin{cases}0,&k>\ell,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{8(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{2\ell+1},&k=\ell,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle 8(-1)^{\ell+k}\left[\ell(\ell+1)-k(k+1)+2\right],&k<\ell,\end{cases}

(4.24)

\displaystyle W^{(3)}_{\ell k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k+1}{2}\begin{cases}0,&k>\ell+1,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{-8(\ell+1)^{2}(\ell+2)}{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)},&k=\ell+1,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{8(\ell+1)(3\ell+2)}{2\ell+1},&k=\ell,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{-8(17\ell^{3}+7\ell^{2}-4\ell-2)}{4\ell^{2}-1},&k=\ell-1,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle 16(-1)^{\ell+k}\left[\ell(\ell+1)-k(k+1)+1\right],&k<\ell-1.\end{cases}

(4.25)

The lower limit in (

6
Discussion and conclusion

Using the relaxation time approximation to the full collision term in the Boltzmann equation for massless (or very light) neutrinos interacting via the exchange of a very massive scalar or pseudo-scalar, we have managed to analytically integrate the collision term to provide an exact analytic expression for the
\alpha_{\ell}
coefficients defined in
[
23
]
.

The reduction above replaces an unstable direct integration of the collision kernel by the finite algebraic expression in
eq.

4.27
. The important point is that the angular and momentum problems are separated. The angular dependence is carried by the three weight families
W^{(1)}_{\ell k}
,
W^{(2)}_{\ell k}
, and
W^{(3)}_{\ell k}
, while the momentum dependence is reduced to
\mathcal{I}^{4,4}_{k}
and rational corrections. This is the notation used in the implementation.

The practical implementation details, including the exact rational evaluation, the Chudnovsky conversion for
\pi^{2}
, and the asymptotically constrained fit, are collected in appendix
D
. It is possible that our analytical solutions could be useful outside of cosmology (or even Physics), although we did not find any clear candidate except from maybe ref.
[
28
]
.

Using the exact integration only leads to modest changes to the numerical coefficients used in codes such as
class
or
camb
, which in the end translate to only minute changes to actual neutrino observables such as
\delta_{\nu}
,
\theta_{\nu}
, and
\sigma_{\nu}
. A similar set of
\alpha_{\ell}
-coefficients has also been derived for inverse neutrino decays
[
29
]
. In this case, the
\alpha_{\ell}
-coefficients are much larger due to the small angle involved in the inverse scattering process, and it would be interesting to see to what extent a fully analytical treatment could be carried out in this model.

Appendix B
Derivation of the recurrence relations

This appendix outlines the derivation of the recurrence relations used in
section

4
. First define the unnormalised triangular integrals

\displaystyle J^{m,n}_{\ell}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}q\int_{0}^{q}\!\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\,q^{m}q^{\prime n}\mathrm{e}^{-q-q^{\prime}}k_{\ell}(q)i_{\ell}(q^{\prime}),

The modified spherical Bessel functions obey the ladder relations

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}q\,q^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-q}\left[-k_{\ell}^{\prime}(q)+\frac{\ell}{q}k_{\ell}(q)\right]A^{(4)}_{\ell+1}(q).
