Identification to Subclasses of Chordal Graphs The research leading to these results has been supported by the Research Council of Norway via the BWCA Grant 314528 and the Extreme-Algorithms Grant 355137 and by the Leverhulme Trust Grant RPG-2024-182.
Petr A. Golovach, Laure Morelle, Daniël Paulusma

Abstract
An identification of two vertices u u and v v in a graph replaces them with a new vertex whose neighborhood is the union of the neighborhoods of u u and v v . We study the ℋ {\cal H} -Identification problem, which is to decide whether a given graph G G can be transformed (“identified”) to a graph in ℋ {\cal H} by applying at most k k vertex identifications. We determine the classical and parameterized complexity of this problem for various subclasses ℋ {\cal H} of chordal graphs, obtaining an almost complete picture for two parameters: k k and n − k n-k . We also consider the Identification problem, which is to test for two given graphs G G and H H if G G can be identified to H H . We determine the parameterized complexity of this problem when H H is a graph from one of our testbed classes, taking the number of simplicial vertices of H H as the parameter.

Methods.

We emphasize that our results are not isolated. In many cases, we use an algorithm developed for one result as a subroutine in another, allowing us to extend our techniques. For example, we first develop an
FPT
algorithm for
\cal H
-Identification
, parameterized by

{\cal H}
is the class of complete graphs. We then use this algorithm as a black box to obtain

algorithms for the cases where
{\cal H}
is the class of cluster graphs and the class of split graphs. Moreover, it is not possible to adapt known techniques from other graph modification problems due to the nature of the identification operation. This is illustrated in Figure
1

Lemma 1

Let

.
So,
(G,p)
is a
yes
-instance of
Independent Set
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Since a star with
n
vertices is unique and has
n-1
leaves when
n\geq 3
,
Theorem 1
immediately implies the following algorithmic lower bound where the target is given as a part of the input.

Corollary 2
.

Identification
is
W[1]
-hard on instances
(G,F)
where
F
is a forest, when parameterized by the number of leaves of
F
.

We also observe that, while
Identification
can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(nm)
time on instances
(G,P)
where
P
is a path by
Proposition 1
, the problem becomes hard when the target graph is a linear forest.

Theorem 2
.

Identification
is
W[1]
-hard on instances
(G,L)
where
L
is a linear forest, when parameterized by the number of connected components of
L
.

Proof.

We reduce from the
Bin Packing
problem. In this problem, the input contains a set
of
n
items of positive integer sizes
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
and positive integers
k
and
B
, and the task is to decide whether there is a partition
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}
of
[n]
such that
for every
i\in[k]
,
\sum_{j\in I_{i}}s_{i}\leq B
.
Bin Packing
parameterized by
k
is known to be
W[1]
-hard when item’s sizes are encoded in unary
[
19
]
. Furthermore, the result holds when
\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}=kB
, that is, the capacity of each bin should be fully used. We consider such an instance of
Bin Packing
and construct the graphs
G
and
L
:

•

G
is the disjoint union of
n
paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
of length
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
, respectively,

•

L
is the disjoint union of
k
paths
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
of length
B
.

We claim that
G
can be identified to
L
if and only if the considered instance of
Bin Packing
is a
yes
-instance.

Suppose that there is a partition
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}
of
[n]
such that
for every
i\in[k]
,
\sum_{j\in I_{i}}s_{i}=B
. For each
i\in[k]
, we consider the paths
P_{j}
with
j\in I_{i}
and consequently identify their endpoints to obtain a single path. Note that this is a path of length
B
. Thus,
L
is an identification of
G
. For the opposite direction, assume that
G
can be identified to
L
. For each
i\in[k]
, let
I_{i}
be the set of indices of the connected components of
G
whose vertices are used to obtain
Q_{i}
. Notice that by
Observation 1
, the vertices of the same connected component cannot be used in distinct
Q_{i}
and
Q_{j}
. Then, because the total length of paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
is the same as the total length of
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
, we obtain that the total length of the paths
P_{j}
with
j\in I_{i}
is exactly
B
for each
i\in[k]
. Thus, for every
i\in[k]
,
\sum_{j\in I_{k}}s_{j}=B
, and the partition
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}
is a solution to the instance of
Bin Packing
. This concludes the proof.
∎

3.2
Kernelization for Identification to Acyclic Graphs

In this section, we investigate the parameterized complexity of
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
parameterized by
k
for the cases when
\mathcal{H}
is the class of stars, trees, or forests.
We show that in all these cases,
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
admits a polynomial kernel when parameterized by
k
.

For stars, we observe that the problem is equivalent to
Vertex Cover
.
We remind that the task of the
Vertex Cover
problem is to decide, given a graph
G
and a non-negative integer
k
, whether
G
has a vertex cover of size at most
k
.

Observation 2
.

Let
G
be a graph
with
p
isolated vertices, and let
k\geq 0
be an integer. Then
G
has a vertex cover of size at most
k
if and only if
G
admits an identification to a star by at most
p+k-1
identifications.

Proof.

The claim is trivial if
G
has no edges. Assume from now on that this is not the case.

Let
X
be a vertex cover of size at most
k
. As
G
has an edge,
X\neq\emptyset
.
We identify the vertices of
X
into a single vertex, and then identify this vertex with the
p
isolated vertices of the original graph. It is straightforward to see that the resulting graph is a star obtained by
|X|-1+p\leq p+k-1
identifications.

For the opposite direction, assume that a star
S
is an identification of
G
obtained by at most
p+k-1
identifications. Denote by
c
the center vertex of
S
. Let
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(S)\}
be an
S
-witness structure. By
Lemma 1
, we can assume that for every
v\in V(S)
distinct from
c
,
|W(v)|=1
. Then
|W(c)|\leq p+k
and the isolated vertices of
G
are in
W(c)
. Let
X\subseteq W(c)
be the set of non-isolated vertices of
W(c)
. We have that
|X|\leq k
, and because for each leaf
v
of
S
, its bag
W(v)
is a singleton,
X
is a vertex cover of
G
. This completes the proof.
∎

The equivalence between
Vertex Cover
and
Star-Identification
,
established in
Observation 2
, immediately implies that all
FPT
and kernelization results for
Vertex Cover
(see
[
6
,
11
]
) can be rewritten for
Star-Identification
parameterized by
k
. We leave this to the reader and consider more interesting cases of
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
when
\mathcal{H}
is either the class of forests or the class of trees. For this, we use the following lemma. To state it, denote by
{\sf Id}_{F}(G)
the minimum number of identifications needed to obtain a forest from
G
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
(A,B)
be a separation of
G
of order at most one.
Suppose that
F
is a forest such
F
can be obtained from
G
using
{\sf Id}_{F}(G)
identifications.
Then for any
F
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(F)\}
,
there are exactly
|A\cap B|
vertices
x\in V(F)
with
W(x)\cap A\neq\emptyset
and
W(x)\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Furthermore,
A\cap B\subseteq W(x)
if
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(F)\}
be an arbitrary
F
-witness structure.
Clearly, if
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
, that is,
A\cap B=\{w\}
for some
w\in V(G)
, then there is
x\in V(F)
such that
A\cap B\subseteq W(x)
. Therefore, it is sufficient to show that
there are at most
|A\cap B|
vertices
x\in V(F)
with
W(x)\cap A\neq\emptyset
and
W(x)\cap B\neq\emptyset
.

For the sake of contradiction, assume that there is
y\in V(F)
such that
W(y)
contains vertices of both
A
and
B
and the unique vertex of
A\cap B
is not in
W(y)
if
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Let
X_{1}=\{x\in V(F)\mid W(x)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}
and

X_{2}=\{x\in V(F)\mid W(x)\cap B\neq\emptyset\}
.
Consider
\mathcal{W}_{1}=\{W_{1}(x)=W(x)\cap A\mid x\in X_{1}\}
and
\mathcal{W}_{2}=\{W_{2}(x)=W(x)\cap B\mid x\in X_{2}\}
.
Note that, by the construction, the sets of
W_{i}(y)
for
i\in[2]
are disjoint. Furthermore, there are
|A\cap B|
sets in
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\mathcal{W}_{2}
containing common vertices, and if

A\cap B\neq\emptyset
then the unique vertex of
A\cap B
is this common vertex.
For
i\in[2]
, denote by
F_{i}
the graph with the vertex set
X_{i}
such that distinct
x_{1},x_{2}\in X_{i}
are adjacent if and only if
W_{i}(x_{1})
and
W_{i}(x_{2})
are adjacent in
G
. By the definition,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are subgraphs of
F
and, therefore, are forests.

Suppose that
A\cap B=\emptyset
. Then the disjoint union of copies of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
is an identification of
G
, since
G
has no edges with their endpoints in
A
and
B
.
Also, we have that
\mathcal{W}_{1}\cup\mathcal{W}_{2}
is the set of bags in the corresponding witness structure.
This is a forest obtained by

\sum_{x\in X_{1}}(|W_{1}(x)|-1)+\sum_{x\in X_{2}}(|W_{2}(x)|-1)
identifications. However,

\sum_{x\in X_{1}}(|W_{1}(x)|-1)+\sum_{x\in X_{2}}(|W_{2}(x)|-1)<\sum_{x\in V(F)}(|W(x)|-1)
because the bag
W(y)
contains vertices of both
A
and
B
. This contradicts the assumption that the forest
F
is an identification of
G
requiring the minimum number of identifications.

Assume that
A\cap B=\{w\}
for
w\in V(G)
. Let
x_{1}\in X_{1}
and
x_{2}\in X_{2}
be the vertices of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, respectively, such that
w\in W_{1}(x_{1})
and
w\in W_{2}(x_{2})
. Consider the forest
F^{\prime}
obtained from the disjoint copies of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
by identifying the copies of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. Because
G
has no edges with their endpoints in
A\setminus\{w\}
and
B\setminus\{w\}
,
F^{\prime}
is the identification of
G
with witness structure

(\mathcal{W}_{1}\setminus\{W_{1}(x_{1})\})\cup(\mathcal{W}_{2}\setminus\{W_{2}(x_{2})\}\cup\{W_{1}(x_{1})\cup W_{2}(x_{2})\}
.
The number of identifications is

\sum_{x\in X_{1}\setminus\{x_{1}\}}(|W_{1}(x)|-1)+\sum_{x\in X_{2}\setminus\{x_{2}\}}(|W_{2}(x)|-1)+|W_{1}(x_{1})|+|W_{2}(x_{2})|-1=\sum_{x\in X_{1}\setminus\{x_{1}\}}(|W_{1}(x)|-1)+\sum_{x\in X_{2}\setminus\{x_{2}\}}(|W_{2}(x)|-1)+|W(z)|-1
, where
z\in V(F)
is a vertex of
F
such that
w\in W(z)
. Because
y\neq z
and
W(y)\neq W(z)
contains vertices of both
A\setminus\{w\}
and
B\setminus\{w\}
,

\sum_{x\in X_{1}\setminus\{x_{1}\}}(|W_{1}(x)|-1)+\sum_{x\in X_{2}\setminus\{x_{2}\}}(|W_{2}(x)|-1)+|W(z)|-1<\sum_{x\in V(F)}(|W(x)|-1)
contradicting the choice of
F
.

In both cases, we got a contradiction. This proves the lemma.
∎

In particular,
Lemma 2
immediately implies the following two properties.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
(A,B)
be a separation of
G
of order one. Let also
k\geq 0

be an integer. Then the instances
(G,k)
and
(G^{\prime},k)
of
Forest-Identification
are equivalent, where
G^{\prime}
is the disjoint union of the copies of
G[A]
and
G[B]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G
be a graph with connected components
G_{1},\ldots,G_{s}
. Then

{\sf Id}_{F}(G)={\sf Id}_{F}(G_{1})+\dots+{\sf Id}_{F}(G_{s})
.

For
Forest-Identification
, we show that the problem admits a polynomial kernel when parameterized by
k
.

Theorem 3
.

Forest-Identification
admits a kernel with
\mathcal{O}(k^{3})
vertices
when parameterized by
k
.

Proof.

Let
(G,k)
be an instance of
Forest-Identification
.
We exhaustively apply the following reduction rules.

Reduction rule 1
.

Delete all bridges and isolated vertices in
G
.

Claim 1
.

The instances
(G,k)
and
(G^{\prime},k)
, where
G^{\prime}
is the graph obtained after applying
1
, are equivalent instances of
Forest-Identification
.

Proof of claim.

Since
G^{\prime}
is a subgraph of
G
and the class of forests is closed under taking subgraphs, if
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance, then
(G^{\prime},k)
is a
yes
-instance as well. For the opposite direction, assume that
(G^{\prime},k)
is a
yes
-instance.

Suppose first that
G^{\prime}
is constructed from
G
by deleting isolated vertices.
Then, because an edgeless graph is a forest, we have that

{\sf Id}_{F}(G)={\sf Id}_{F}(G^{\prime})
by
Lemma 4
. Then
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Forest-Identification
.

Assume now that
G^{\prime}
is constructed by deleting bridges, and denote by
G_{1},\ldots,G_{s}
the connected components of
G^{\prime}
. By
Lemma 4
,

{\sf Id}_{F}(G^{\prime})={\sf Id}_{F}(G_{1})+\dots+{\sf Id}_{F}(G_{s})
. For each
i\in[s]
, let
F_{i}
be a forest obtained from
G_{i}
by
{\sf Id}_{F}(G_{i})
identifications, and let
F
be the disjoint union of
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
. We have that
F
is an identification of
G
obtained by
{\sf Id}_{F}(G^{\prime})
identifications. Consider the corresponding
F
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(F)\}
.
For every bridge
uv
of
G
, there are
x_{u},x_{v}\in V(F)
in distinct connected components of
F
such that
u\in W(x_{u})
and
v\in W(x_{v})
. Let
F^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
F
by making
x_{u}
and
x_{v}
adjacent for all bridges
uv
. It is straightforward to see that
F^{\prime}
is a forest. Furthermore,
\mathcal{W}
is an
F^{\prime}
-witness structure of
G
. Thus,
{\sf Id}_{F}(G)\leq{\sf Id}_{F}(G^{\prime})
and, therefore,

(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Forest-Identification
.

Since the deletion of isolated vertices and bridges can be done consequently, the above arguments prove that
(G,k)
is a yes-instance. This completes the proof.
∎

If
G
becomes empty after applying
1
, then before the application of the rule
G
was a forest. Thus, in this case,
we return a trivial
yes
-instance of constant size and stop.

Reduction rule 2
.

For any two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, if
|N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)|>k+1
, then identify
u
with
v
and decrease
k
by one.

Claim 2
.

The instances
(G,k)
and
(G^{\prime},k-1)
, where
G^{\prime}
is the graph obtained after applying
2
, are equivalent instances of
Forest-Identification
.

Proof of claim.

Obviously, if
(G^{\prime},k-1)
is a
yes
-instance, then
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance.
Reciprocally, let
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(F)\}
be an
F
-witness structure of
G
for some forest
F
obtained from
G
by at most
k
identifications.
Let
x_{u},x_{v}\in V(F)
be the vertices such that
u\in W(x_{u})
and
v\in W(x_{v})
. We claim that

x_{u}=x_{v}
.
Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
x_{u}\neq x_{v}
.
Let
a
(resp.
b
) be the number of vertices of
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)
in
W(x_{u})
(resp.
W(x_{v})
).
Let
R
be the set of remaining vertices of
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)
, i.e., that are neither identified with
u
nor
v
.
Given that
|N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)|\geq k+2
,
|R|\geq k+2-a-b
.
If there are two distinct witness bags
W(r_{1})
and
W(r_{2})
containing vertices of
R
, then
r_{1},u,r_{2},v
is a
4
-cycle in
F
, a contradiction.
Therefore, there is a vertex
r\in V(H)
such that
R\subseteq W(R)
.
However, the total number of identifications in this case is at least

(|W(x_{u})|-1)+(|W(x_{v})|-1)+(|W(r)|-1)\geq a+b+|R|-1\geq k+1
.
Therefore, identifying to
F
requires at least
k+1
identifications; a contradiction.
Thus, if
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance, then
u
and
v
are identified. Since this identification can be done first, we have that
(G^{\prime},k-1)
is a
yes
-instance. This proves the claim.
∎

We immediately stop and return a trivial no-instance of
Forest-Identification
of constant size and stop if
k
becomes negative after applying
2
. From now on, we assume that this is not the case.

We claim that after exhaustively applying
1
and
2
, either the obtained graph has bounded size, or we have a
no
-instance.

Claim 3
.

If
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance to which
1
and
2
cannot be applied, then
|V(G)|\leq 2k+(k+1)\cdot\binom{2k}{2}
.

Proof of claim.

Let
(G,k)
be a
yes
-instance of
Forest-Identification
to which
1
and
2
cannot be applied, and let
F
be a forest obtained from
G
by performing at most
k
identifications.
Denote by
S
the set of vertices of
G
that are identified with some other vertices, that is, belong to non-trivial bags of the
F
-witness structure. Since the total number of identifications is at most
k
,
|S|\leq 2k
.
For any edge
e
in
G
, given that
e
is part of a cycle (because
e
is not a bridge), at least one of its endpoints has to be identified with some other vertex. This means that
S
is a vertex cover of
G
of size at most
2k
.
Because
G
has no isolated vertices and bridges by
1
, every vertex of
G
has degree at least
2
. In particular, every
v\in I
of the independent set
I=V(G)\setminus S
has at least two neighbors in
S
. Observe that because
2
is not applicable, for any distinct
u,v\in S
, there are at most
k+1
common neighbors of these vertices. Therefore,
|I|\leq(k+1)\cdot\binom{|S|}{2}
, and
|V(G)|=|S|+|I|\leq 2k+(k+1)\cdot\binom{2k}{2}
. This proves the claim.
∎

Using
Claim 3
, we return a trivial
no
-instance if the graph
G
obtained by
the exhaustive application of
1
and
2
has more than
2k+(k+1)\cdot\binom{2k}{2}
vertices. Otherwise, we return
(G,k)
.

The correctness of the kernelization algorithm follows from
Claim 1
,
Claim 2
, and
Claim 3
. Since bridges can be found in linear time by the standard depth-first search algorithm,
1
can be applied in linear time. Since

2
can be trivially applied in
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
time, the overall running time is polynomial. This proves that
Forest-Identification
admits a kernel with
\mathcal{O}(k^{3})
vertices.
∎

Using
Theorem 3
, we prove a similar result for
Tree-Identification
.

Corollary 3
.

Tree-Identification
admits a kernel with
\mathcal{O}(k^{3})
vertices when parameterized by
k
.

Proof.

We reduce the problem to
Forest-Identification
where
\mathcal{F}
is the class of forests using the following claim.

Claim 4
.

An instance
(G,k)
of
Tree-Identification
for a graph
G
with
s
connected components is equivalent to the instance
(G,k-s+1)
of
Forest-Identification
.

Proof of claim.

Let
G
be a graph with connected components
G_{1},\ldots,G_{s}
. The claim holds for
s=1
by
Observation 1
. Assume that
s\geq 2
.

Suppose that
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Tree-Identification
. Then there is a sequence of at most
k
identifications of the vertices of
G
resulting in a tree
T
.
Because
G
has
s
connected components, this sequence contains
s-1
identifications resulting in a connected graph
G^{\prime}
. Then
G^{\prime}
can be identified to a forest by at most
k-s+1
identifications. Denote by
S
the vertices of
G^{\prime}
obtained by identifying distinct vertices of
G
.
Notice that for each vertex
v\in S
, there is a separation
(A,B)
of
G^{\prime}
with
A\cap B=\{v\}
. Then by
Lemma 3
, if
(G^{\prime},k-s+1)
is a
yes
-instance of
Forest-Identification
, then
(G,k-s+1)
is a
yes
-instance.

For the opposite implication, assume that
(G,k-s+1)
is a
yes
-instance of
Forest-Identification
.
Denote by
G_{1},\ldots,G_{s}
the connected components of
G
.
By
Lemma 4
,
{\sf Id}_{F}(G)={\sf Id}_{F}(G_{1})+\dots+{\sf Id}_{F}(G_{s})
.
For
i\in[s]
, denote by
F_{i}
the forest obtained from
G_{i}
by
{\sf Id}_{F}(G_{i})
identifications. By
Observation 1
, each
F_{i}
is a tree.
Let
F
be the disjoint union of
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
. We have that
F
is an identification of
G
obtained by
{\sf Id}_{F}(G)\leq k-s+1
identification. Finally, we observe that we can obtain a tree from
F
by
s-1
identification. Thus,
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Tree-Identification
. This proves the claim.
∎

Let
(G,k)
be an instance of
Tree-Identification
. Denote by
G_{1},\ldots,G_{s}
the connected components of
G
. If
k+1<s
, we conclude that
(G,k)
is a
no
-instance by
Claim 4
. In this case, we return a trivial
no
-instance of
Tree-Identification
and stop. Otherwise, we apply the kernelization algorithm from
Theorem 3
. It returns an instance
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
of
Forest-Identification
, where
G^{\prime}
has
\mathcal{O}(k^{3})
vertices. By
Claim 4
,
(G,k)
and
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
are equivalent. If
G^{\prime}
is a connected graph, then because of
Observation 1
,
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
is an equivalent instance of
Tree-Identification
. However,
G^{\prime}
may be disconnected. In this case, consider the connected components
G_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,G_{t}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
. For each
i\in[t]
, let
v_{i}
be an arbitrary vertex of
G_{i}^{\prime}
. Denote by
G^{\prime\prime}
the graph obtained by identifying
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}
into a single vertex
v
. By
Lemma 3
, the instances
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
and
(G^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime})
of
Forest-Identification
are equivalent. Since
G^{\prime\prime}
is connected,
(G^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime})
is an equivalent instance of
Tree-Identification
.
Therefore, our kernelization algorithm returns
(G^{\prime\prime},k^{\prime})
. This completes the description of the kernelization algorithm and its correctness proof. Since we can find the connected components in linear time by the standard breadth-first search, the running time is polynomial. This proves the corollary.
∎

Theorem 3
and
Corollary 3
imply that
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
can be solved in
k^{\mathcal{O}(k)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time when
\mathcal{H}
is the class of forests or trees, as we can solve the problem by brute force on the reduced instances by guessing identifications.

Corollary 4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of forests or trees.
Then
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
can be solved in
k^{\mathcal{O}(k)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.

3.3
Identification to a Given Forest

By
Corollary 2
,
Identification
is
W[1]
-hard on instances
(G,F)
where
F
is a forest, when parameterized by the number of leaves of
F
. In this section, we complement this result by demonstrating an
XP
algorithm for this parameterization. First, we show the result when the input graph
G
is connected.
Note that any identification of a connected graph
G
is connected by
Observation 1
. Therefore, we can assume that the input forest
F
is a tree.

Lemma 5
.

Identification
is solvable in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{\ell-1}\cdot(n+m))
on instances
(G,T)
where
G
is a connected graph and
T

is a tree on
\ell
leaves.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected graph, and let
T
be a tree. We assume that
|V(T)|\leq|V(G)|=n
, as otherwise, we have a trivial
no
-instance. We also assume that
\ell\geq 2
because for the single-vertex
T
, the problem is trivial.
We select an arbitrary leaf
r
of
T
as a
root
vertex which defines the parent-child relation on
V(T)
. For
x\in V(T)
, we denote by
T_{x}
the subtree of
T
induced by the descendants of
x
(including
x
). Given a
T
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(T)\}
of
G
, we set
W_{x}=\bigcup_{y\in V(T_{x})}W(y)
for
x\in V(T)
.

Let
L
be the set of leaves of
T
in the rooted tree; note that
|L|=\ell-1
as the root is not a leaf of the rooted tree
T
.
Our algorithm constructs a special
T
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(T)\}
bottom-up starting from
x\in L
.
We say that a
T
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(T)\}
is
regular
if
(i)
|W(x)|=1
for
x\in L
and (ii) for every non-root vertex
y\in V(T)\setminus L
,

W(y)=N_{G}(\bigcup_{x\in C_{y}}W_{x})
where
C_{y}\subseteq V(G)
is the set of children of
y
in
T
.

Claim 5
.

If
G
can be identified to
T
, then
G
has a regular
T
-witness structure.

Proof of claim.

Since the vertices of
L
are pairwise non-adjacent, there is a
T
-witness structure where each leaf-bag is a singleton by
Lemma 1
. For each
T
-witness structure of this type, property (i) is fulfilled. Among all
T
-witness structures satisfying (i), we select a
T
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(T)\}
such that the sum of distances in
T
from
r
to the non-root vertices
y\in V(T)\setminus L
with
W(y)\neq N_{G}(\bigcup_{x\in C_{y}}W_{x})
is minimum. We claim that
\mathcal{W}
satisfies (ii).

To obtain a contradiction, assume that
y\in V(T)\setminus L
is a non-root vertex at maximum distance from
r
for which,
W(y)\neq N_{G}(\bigcup_{x\in C_{y}}W_{x})
. Since
y\neq r
,
y
has the unique parent
z
. Since
T
is a tree, we have that
N_{G}(\bigcup_{x\in C_{y}}W_{x})\subseteq W(y)
by the definition of a witness structure.
Also, each neighbor
v
of a vertex
u\in W(y)
, is in
N_{G}[W(y)]
, that is, either
v\in W(z)
or
v\in W(t)
for
t\in C\cup\{y\}
. Consider

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}=\{W^{\prime}(x)\mid x\in V(T)\}
where
W^{\prime}(y)=N_{G}(\bigcup_{x\in C_{y}}W_{x})
,
W^{\prime}(z)=W(z)\cup(W(y)\setminus W^{\prime}(y))
, and
W^{\prime}(x)=W(x)
for all
x\neq y,z
.
Since
G
is connected, for every
x\in C\cup\{z\}
, there are adjacent
v_{x}\in W^{\prime}(x)
and
v_{y}\in W(y)
. Then, because
N_{G}[W(y)\setminus W^{\prime}(y)]\subseteq W(y)\cup W(z)
, we have that
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is a
T
-witness structure. However,
W^{\prime}(y)=N_{G}(\bigcup_{x\in C_{y}}W_{x})
contradicting the choice of
\mathcal{W}
. Thus,
\mathcal{W}
satisfies (i) and (ii). This completes the proof.
∎

Based on
Claim 5
, our algorithm finds a regular
T
-witness structure (if it exists). For each
x\in L
, we guess a vertex
v_{x}
of
G
such that
W(x)=\{v_{x}\}
. As
|L|=\ell-1
, there are at most
n^{\ell-1}
possibilities to guess the vertices
v_{x}
. For each choice, we verify whether it can be extended to a regular
T
-witness structure.
If we find such a choice, we return the corresponding solution. Otherwise, we report a
no
-instance.

From now on, we assume that the bags
\{v_{x}\}
for
x\in L
are given. To extend the witness structure, we apply the standard bottom-up dynamic programming approach. For every non-root vertex
y\in V(T)\setminus L
such that for all children
x
of
y
in
T
(and, therefore, for their descendants), the bags are constructed, we define

W(y)=N_{G}(\bigcup_{x\in C_{y}}W_{x})
where
C_{y}\subseteq V(G)
is the set of children of
y
in
T
.
Finally, we set
W(r)=V(G)\setminus\bigcup_{x\in V(T)\setminus\{r\}}W(x)
. Since
T
has at most
n
vertices, the family
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(T\}
can be constructed in
\mathcal{O}(n+m)
time for a given set of leaf-bags. In the last step, we verify in
\mathcal{O}(n+m)
time whether
\mathcal{W}
is a
T
-witness structure using the definition.

This completes the description of the algorithm. Its correctness follows from
Claim 5
. Since initially we guess
n^{\ell-1}
vertices of
G
in the leaf-bags, the overall running time is
\mathcal{O}(n^{\ell-1}(n+m))
. This concludes the proof.
∎

In
Theorem 2
, we proved that
Identification
is
W[1]
-hard on instances
(G,L)
where
L
is a linear forest, when parameterized by the number of connected components of
L
. The proof was based on the close relation of our problem to
Bin Packing
. Using this relation further, we show that the problem is in
XP
when parameterized by the number of connected components of
L
. We use this result as a building block for the algorithm for
Identification
on instances
(G,F)
where
F
is a forest.

Lemma 6
.

Identification
can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(nm+n^{t+1})
time on instances
(G,L)
where
L
is a linear forest with
t
connected components.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph with
s
connected components
G_{1},\ldots,G_{s}
, and let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{t}
be the connected components of
L
. We assume that
|V(L)|\leq|V(G)|=n
,
|E(L)|\leq|E(G)|=m
, and
t\leq s
as, otherwise, we have a trivial
no
-instance of the problem. Also, if
L
is an edgeless graph and
t\leq s
, then
G
can be identified to
L

in a straightforward way.
From now on, assume that these cases do not apply.
For
i\in[s]
, let
d_{i}={\sf diam}(G_{i})
. Let also
\ell_{i}
be the length of
P_{i}
for
i\in[t]
.
We have the following property.

Claim 6
.

The graph
G
can be identified to
L
if and only if there is a partition
I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}
of
[s]
such that for every
i\in[t]
,
\sum_{j\in I_{i}}d_{j}\geq\ell_{i}
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose that
G
can be identified to
L
. Then by
Observation 1
, there is a partition
I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}
of
[s]
such that for every
i\in[t]
,
P_{i}
is an identification of the union of
G_{j}
for
j\in I_{i}
. Then by
Proposition 1
, for every
i\in[i]
,
\ell_{i}\geq\sum_{j\in I_{i}}{\sf diam}(G_{j})=\sum_{j\in I_{i}}d_{j}
. For the opposite implication, assume that
there is a partition
I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}
of
[s]
such that for every
i\in[t]
,
\sum_{j\in I_{i}}d_{j}\geq\ell_{i}
. Then by
Proposition 1
, for each
i\in[t]
, the subgraph of
G
consisting of the connected components
G_{j}
with

can be identified to
P_{i}
. This immediately implies that
G
can be identified to
L
. This completes the proof.
∎

Using
Claim 6
, we construct a dynamic programming algorithm inspired by the standard dynamic programming for
Bin Packing
for a bounded number of bins.
However, it is convenient to do the following preprocessing. Notice that if
\ell_{i}=0
for some
i\in[t]
, that is,
P_{i}
is a trivial path, then we can select
I_{i}=\{j\}
for
j\in[s]
, where
d_{j}=\min\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{s}\}
. Thus, we may greedily select
I_{i}
for trivial
P_{i}
.
By this procedure, we reduce it to the case when
\ell_{i}\geq 1
for
i\in[t]
.
From now on, we assume that
\ell_{i}\geq 1
for
i\in[t]
and
s<n
.

For
i\in[0,s]
and a
t
-tuple
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t})
, where
p_{i}\in[0,\ell_{i}]
for
i\in[t]
, we define the Boolean function
T(i,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}))
by setting

T(i,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}))={\sf true}
if there is a partition
I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}
of
[0,i]
, where some sets may be empty, such that for every
i\in[t]
,

\sum_{j\in I_{i}}d_{j}\geq p_{i}
, and we set
T(i,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}))={\sf false}
, otherwise.
Here, we assume that for
I_{i}=\emptyset
,
\sum_{j\in I_{i}}d_{j}=0
.
Observe that
T(s,(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{t}))={\sf true}
if and only if
there is a partition
I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}
of
[s]
such that for every
i\in[t]
,
\sum_{j\in I_{i}}d_{j}\geq\ell_{i}
; in particular, each
I_{i}\neq\emptyset
because
\ell_{i}\geq 1
.
Our dynamic programming algorithm computes the table of values of
T(s,(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{t}))
for all
i\in[0,s]
and all
t
-tuples
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t})
.

We consequently compute the tables for
i=0,\ldots,s
. Initially, for
i=0
, we set

T(0,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}))={\sf true}
for
p_{1}=\dots=p_{t}=0
, and
T(i,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}))={\sf false}
, otherwise. For
i\geq 1
, we use the following recurrence:

T(i,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}))=\bigvee_{j\in[t]}T(i-1,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{j-1},\max\{0,p_{j}-d_{i}\},p_{j+1},\ldots,p_{t})).

(1)

The correctness of computing

immediately follows from the definition of the function. The correctness of the recurrence (
1
) is proved by the standard arguments using the observation that for each
i\in[s]
,
i
should be in some
I_{j}
for
j\in[t]
. Since
\ell_{j}\leq n-1
for
j\in[t]
and
s<n
, the table of values of
T(s,(p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}))
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n\cdot n^{t})
time. Because the connected components of
G
can be found in linear time and the diameters of the components can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(nm)
time, the overall running time is
\mathcal{O}(nm+n^{t+1})
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

is in
XP
on instances

where

is a forest, when parameterized by the number of leaves of

Assume that

∎

Now we are ready to construct our

4
Identification to Cliques and Cluster Graphs

In this section, we consider
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
where
\mathcal{H}
is a class of complete or cluster graphs.
We refer to these problems as
Clique-Identification
and
Cluster-Identification
, respectively.

4.1
Algorithmic Lower Bounds

We begin with establishing the following algorithmic and kernelization lower bound.

Theorem 5
.

Clique-Identification
is
NP
-complete and does not admit a polynomial kernel when parameterized by
k
unless
co-NP

\subseteq

NP/poly
. Furthermore, these results hold for connected input graphs.

Proof.

We present a reduction from the
Set Cover
problem. The task of this problem is, given
a universe
\mathcal{U}
, a family
\mathcal{S}
of subsets of
\mathcal{U}
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, to decide whether
there a subfamily
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
of size at most
k
such that
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}S=\mathcal{U}
.

Set Cover
is well-known to be
NP
-complete
[
12
]
. Furthermore,

Set Cover
parameterized by the number
m
of sets has no polynomial kernel
[
9
,
6
]
unless

co-NP

\subseteq

NP/poly
.
Since our reduction is a polynomial parameter transformation, we simultaneously prove both claims of the theorem.

Figure 3:
The graph
G
constructed in the proof of
Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a universe with
n
elements,
\mathcal{S}
be a family of
m
subsets of
\mathcal{U}
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We construct a graph
G
on
2nm+2m-k
vertices as follows (cf.
Figure 3
for an illustration).

•

For each element
u\in\mathcal{U}
, create a set
V_{u}
containing
2m
vertices.

•

For each subset
S\in\mathcal{S}
, create a vertex
v_{S}
.

•

Make a clique out of
V_{\mathcal{U}}=\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}V_{u}
and out of
V_{\mathcal{S}}=\{v_{S}\mid S\in\mathcal{S}\}
.

•

For each
u\in\mathcal{U}
and each
S\in\mathcal{S}
, add an edge between
v_{S}
and each vertex of
V_{u}
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{S}
.

•

Construct an independent set
I
of size
m-k
and add an edge between each vertex of
I
and each vertex of
V_{u}
, for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
.

Notice that
G
is connected.
We claim that
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{S},k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Set Cover
if and only if
(G,m-1)
is a
yes
-instance of
Clique-Identification
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{S},k)
is a
yes
-instance of
Set Cover
, and let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
be a family of size
k
such that
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}S=\mathcal{U}
.
We identify the vertices in
G
corresponding to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
into a single vertex (this requires
k-1
identifications). Since
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a set cover, the obtained vertex is adjacent to every vertex of
V_{\mathcal{U}}
. Then we identify pairwise each vertex corresponding to a set in
\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
with a vertex of
I
(this requires
m-k
identifications).
The obtained graph is a clique.
So,
(G,m-1)
is a
yes
-instance of
Clique-Identification
.

Suppose now that
(G,m-1)
is a
yes
-instance of
Clique-Identification
.
Let
K_{p}
for
p\geq 2nm+m-k+1
be the clique obtained after at most
m-1
identifications, and let
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(K_{p})\}
be a
K_{p}
-witness structure of
G
.
In the next two claims, we show that there is a bag
W(x)\subseteq V_{\mathcal{S}}
in
\mathcal{W}
corresponding to a set cover of size at most
k
.

Claim 10
.

There exists
x\in V(K_{p})
such that
W(x)\subseteq V_{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof of claim.

For the sake of contradiction, assume that for each
v\in V_{\mathcal{S}}
, there is
u\in V(G)\setminus V_{\mathcal{S}}
such that
u
and
v
are in the same bag of
\mathcal{W}
. This means that each vertex
v\in V_{\mathcal{S}}
is identified with a vertex outside
V_{\mathcal{S}}
, and because
|V_{\mathcal{S}}|\geq m
, we need at least
m
identifications, contradicting that the number of identifications is at most
m-1
. This proves the claim.
∎

Claim 11
.

We have
|W(x)|\leq k
.

Proof of claim.

Notice that for each
v\in I
, there is a vertex
u\in V(G)\setminus I
such that
u
and
v
are in the same bag of
\mathcal{W}
. Otherwise, if there is
y\in V(K_{p})
such that

, the bags
W(x)
and
W(y)
are not adjacent, contradicting that we obtain a clique by the identifications. Thus, we need at least
|I|=m-k
identifications for the vertices of

. This implies that to identify the vertices in

into a single vertex, we can use at most

|W(x)|\leq k
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{S\in\mathcal{S}\mid v_{S}\in W(x)\}
.
Given that we do at most
m-1
identifications, for each
u\in\mathcal{U}
, there is at least one vertex
v_{u}
out of the

such that

.
Given that

is adjacent to each

, there is

such that

is adjacent to

in

. This implies that the element

is covered by

.
Thus,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
covers
\mathcal{U}
and

is a
yes
-instance of

. This completes the proof.
∎

Because the lower bounds from

hold for connected graphs, by making use of

, we obtain the same results for cluster graphs.

Corollary 5
.

Cluster-Identification
is
NP
-complete and does not admit a polynomial kernel when parameterized by

unless
co-NP

\subseteq

NP/poly
.

4.2
FPT Algorithms and Kernelization for Identification to Cliques and Cluster Graphs

In this section, we provide algorithmic results for
Clique-Identification
and
Cluster-Identification

parameterized by
k
and the dual parameterization by
n-k
.
In
Theorem 5
and
Corollary 5
, we proved that it is unlikely that
Clique-Identification
and
Cluster-Identification
have polynomial kernels when parameterized by
k
. However, the problems are
FPT
for this parameterization. We first demonstrate such an algorithm for

Clique-Identification
and then use it for
Cluster-Identification
.

We consider the auxiliary
S
-Constrained Clique-Identification
problem. This problem asks, given a graph
G
, a set of vertices
S\subseteq V(G)
such that

G-S
is a clique, and an integer
k
, whether
G
can be identified to a clique
K_{h}
for
h\geq n-k
with a
K_{h}
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(K_{h})\}
, where each bag contains at most one vertex of
S
. In other words, we ask whether we can obtain a clique by at most
k
identifications in such a way that the vertices of
S
are not identified with each other. This variant of
Clique-Identification
has a polynomial kernel when parameterized by the size of
S
and
k
.

Lemma 7
.

S
-Constrained Clique-Identification
has a kernel with
\mathcal{O}(|S|k)
vertices
and can be solved in
(|S|k)^{\mathcal{O}(k)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.

Proof.

Let
(G,S,k)
be an instance of
S
-Constrained Clique-Identification
.
If we have
|V(G)|\leq|S|(k+2)+k
, we return the input instance, as it has a bounded size. Assume that this is not the case and
|V(G)|\geq|S|(k+2)+k+1
.
The following claim will be useful for us.

Claim 12
.

Suppose that
v\in S
has at least
k+1
non-adjacent vertices in
V(G)\setminus S
.
Then in any
K_{h}
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(K_{h})\}
for
h\geq n-k
, there is
x\in V(K_{h})
such that
W(x)
contains
v
and a vertex of
V(G)\setminus S
.

Proof of claim.

The proof is by contradiction.
Suppose that there is a
K_{h}
-witness structure
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(K_{h})\}
such that the bag
W(x)
containing
v
does not include any vertex of
V(G)\setminus S
. Let
X\subseteq V(G)\setminus S
be the set of non-neighbors of
v
that are not in
S
. Since
vu\notin E(G)
for
u\in X
, we have that each bag
W(y)
containing a vertex of
X
should contain at least one vertex in
V(G)\setminus X
. However, this means that we need at least
|X|\geq k+1
identifications, contradicting that
h\geq n-k
. This proves the claim.
∎

For each
v\in S
, we construct the set
R_{v}
of at most
k+1
non-neighbors of
v
in
V(G)\setminus S
—if
v
has at most
k
such non-neighbors, we include all of them in
R_{v}
, and we include arbitrary
k+1
non-neighbors, otherwise. We say that the vertices of
R_{v}
are
marked
. After marking vertices for
v\in S
, we select an arbitrary set

U\in V(G)\setminus(S\cup(\bigcup_{v\in S}R_{v}))
of size
k+1
and mark these vertices as well; note that
U
exists because
|V(G)|\geq|S|(k+2)+k+1
. Now we delete all non-marked vertices of
V(G)\setminus S
. Denote by
G^{\prime}
the obtained graph. Note that

|V(G^{\prime})|\leq|S|+\sum_{v\in S}|R_{v}|+|U|\leq|S|(k+2)+k+1
. We show that the instances
(G,S,k)
and
(G^{\prime},S,k)
of
S
-Constrained Clique-Identification
are equivalent.

Claim 13
.

(G,S,k)
and
(G^{\prime},S,k)
are equivalent instances of
S
-Constrained Clique-Identification
.

Proof of claim.

Let
v
be a non-marked vertex of
G-S
. Assume that
G^{\prime}=G-v
. As non-marked vertices can be deleted one by one, it is sufficient to show that
(G,S,k)
and
(G^{\prime},S,k)
are equivalent.

Suppose that
(G,S,k)
is a yes-instance of
S
-Constrained Clique-Identification
, and let
\mathcal{W}=\{W(x)\mid x\in V(K_{h})\}
be a
K_{h}
-witness structure for
h\geq n-k
. Let
y\in V(K_{h})
be the vertex such that
v\in W(y)
. Since
|U|\geq k+1
, there is
z\in V(K_{h})
distinct from
y

such that
W(z)
contains a vertex
u\in U
but

.
Consider the family of sets
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}=\{W^{\prime}(x)\mid x\in V(K_{h})\setminus\{y\}\}
, where

W^{\prime}(z)=(W(y)\cup W(z))\setminus\{v\}
and
W^{\prime}(x)=W(x)
for
x\in V(K_{h})
distinct from
y
and
z
.
Observe that because
W(z)\cap S=\emptyset
, each bag
W^{\prime}(x)
contains at most one vertex of
S
.
We show that
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is a
(K_{h}-y)
-witness structure for
G^{\prime}
.
By the construction
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is a partition of the vertex set of
K_{h}-y
. Thus, we have to show that the sets
W^{\prime}(x)
are pairwise adjacent.
As
W^{\prime}(x)=W(x)
for
x\neq y,z
, we have that
W^{\prime}(x)
and
W^{\prime}(x^{\prime})
are adjacent if both
x
and

are distinct from

and
z
because
\mathcal{W}
is a
K_{h}
-witness structure.
Since

,

and

for

are adjacent.
This
proves that
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is a
(K_{h}-y)
-witness structure for
G^{\prime}
. Since
|V(G^{\prime})|=n-1
and

has at least

vertices, we obtain that

(G^{\prime},S,k)
is a
yes
-instance of
S
-Constrained Clique-Identification
.

For the opposite implication, assume that
(G^{\prime},S,k)
is a yes-instance of
S
-Constrained Clique-Identification
, and let
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}=\{W^{\prime}(x)\mid x\in V(K_{h-1})\}
be a corresponding

-witness structure for

. Assume that

for some

. We define

by setting

for

, and defining

. We claim that

is a

-witness structure for

.
Note that
\mathcal{W}
is a partition of

. Because

is a

-witness structure for

, the sets

for

are pairwise adjacent. If

for

contains a vertex of

, then

and

are adjacent because

is a clique. Assume that

does not contain a vertex of

. Then by

,

for

and

. Then

and

are adjacent. This proves that

is a

-witness structure for

. Therefore,

is a

-instance of

-Constrained Clique-Identification
. This proves the claim.
∎

Because
(G,S,k)
and
(G^{\prime},S,k)
are equivalent and

, our kernelization algorithm returns

.
Since marking of the vertices of

can be constructed in polynomial time. This proves that

has a polynomial kernel with
\mathcal{O}(|S|k)

To see that the problem can be solved in

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we prove the main result of the subsection.

Theorem 6
.

Clique-Identification
is solvable in time
k^{\mathcal{O}(k)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
.

Proof.

Let
(G,k)
be an instance of
Clique-Identification
.

Observe that if
(G,k)
is a
yes

2k

.

Assume that there is

∎

Corollary 6
.

Proof.

We reduce

Theorem 7

Let

be an instance of

Given that the class of cliques is closed under identifications,

We first greedily compute an arbitrary inclusion-maximal matching

is a

-instance.

Claim 15
.

If

is a

-instance of

.

Proof of claim.

We set
\ell=\binom{p}{2}
.
Let

be the edges of
K_{p}
and
\{u_{1}v_{1},\dots,u_{\ell}v_{\ell}\}
be the

first edges of

For each

We additionally add all remaining vertices of

and

-witness structure in

∎

Hence, if

-instance of

of constant size and stop.

From now on, we assume that this is not the case and

of the endpoints of the edges of

is a vertex cover of

|V(G^{\prime})|\leq|S|+|S|\binom{p}{2}\leq p^{4}

.
We claim that the instances
(G,k)
and
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
are equivalent.

Claim 16
.

(G,k)
and
(G^{\prime},k^{\prime})
are equivalent instances of
Clique-Identification
.

Notice that we have to show that

can be identified to

.

can be identified to

, we have that
n=\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
.
Recall that
(G,k)
is a

-instance if and only if

can be identified to

If

Notice that

if and only if there is a set

For

, the algorithm is almost the same. For an instance

of

, we apply the kernelization algorithm from

.
The problem is trivial if

We use the fact that

is a

-instance of

if and only if

Notice that

In

Given an

k^{\mathcal{O}(k)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time. Then the overall running time is
k^{\mathcal{O}(k)}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

5.3
Kernelization for Dual Parameterization

We conclude this section by proving that for the parameterization by
p=n-k
,
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
admits a trivial polynomial kernel, where
\mathcal{H}
is the class split, interval, or chordal graphs.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of split, interval, or chordal graphs (or more generally, any class containing the complete graphs, edgeless graphs, stars, and the disjoint unions of stars and isolated vertices).
Let
G
be a graph and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
p=n-k
.
If
n\geq p^{2}-2p-2
,
then
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance of
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
. Particularly,
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
admits a kernel with at most
p^{2}
vertices.

Proof.

Let
(G,k)
be an instance of
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
. We construct the following kernelization algorithm.
If
G
is an edgeless graph, then
G\in\mathcal{H}
and
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance. Assume that this is not the case.

If
G
has a matching of size at least
\binom{p}{2}
, then
G
is a
yes
-instance of
Clique-Identification
by the arguments from
Claim 15
. We remind that in this case, we can construct a
K_{p}
-witness structure by putting the endpoints of
\binom{p}{2}
edges of the matching in the bags to ensure that the bags are pairwise adjacent, and other vertices can be placed in the bags arbitrarily. Since
K_{p}\in\mathcal{H}
, we have that
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance of
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
.
Otherwise,
G
has a vertex cover
S
of size at most
2\cdot\binom{p}{2}-2
composed by the endpoints of a maximum matching. Since
G
has at least one edge,
S\neq\emptyset
.
If
|V(G-S)|\geq p-1
, then we can identify
S
into a single vertex. We thus obtain either a star of with at least
p
vertices or the disjoint union of a star and some isolated vertices with at least
p
vertices. This implies that
G
is a
yes
-instance of
\mathcal{H}
-Identification
.

If we concluded that
(G,k)
is a
yes
-instance, then the kernelization algorithm returns
the trivial
yes
-instance
(G=(\{v\},\emptyset),0)
, where
G
has a single vertex.
Otherwise, if
|S|\leq 2\binom{p}{2}-2
and
|V(G)\setminus S|\leq p-2
,

|V(G)|\leq 2\binom{p}{2}-2+p-2\leq p^{2}
. Then the algorithm returns the input instance
(G,k)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Combining the kernelization from
Theorem 11
with the brute force guessing at most
k\leq p^{2}
identifications, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 8
.

\mathcal{H}
-Identification
, where
\mathcal{H}
is the class of split, interval, or chordal graphs, can be solved in
p^{\mathcal{O}(p^{2})}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time when parameterized by
p=n-k
.

6
Future Work

We presented a comprehensive study of the complexity of
{\cal H}
-Identification
and
Identification
for ten well-known classes of chordal graphs, including the class of chordal graphs themselves. Our results are summarized in Tables
2
and
2
. By using a systematic approach, we were able to identify two open problems:
Is
\cal H
-Identification
, parameterized by
k
,
FPT
or
W[1]
-hard when
{\cal H}
is the class of interval graphs or the class of chordal graphs?

It is also possible to consider alternative parameters. For example, we may ask whether a given graph
G
can be modified into a graph from a specified class
{\cal H}
by involving at most
p
vertices in a sequence of identifications. This variant was studied in
[
24
]
, where a polynomial kernel was obtained when

{\cal H}
is the class of forests (through a reduction to
Vertex Cover
). Since
k
identifications involve
p\leq 2k
vertices,
this result yields an
FPT

2
-approximation algorithm for
\cal H
-Identification
.

Finally, we recall that for the minor relation, the set of permitted graph operations consists of vertex deletion, edge deletion and edge contraction. If we only allow vertex deletion and edge contraction, then we obtain the
induced minor
relation. In both relations we could replace edge contraction by vertex identification. In the first case, we obtain the
identification minor
relation. This containment relation was introduced in
[
24
]
. We name the second case as
induced identification minor
.

We note that the corresponding decision problems

H
-
Identification Minor
and
H
-
Induced Identification Minor
are in
XP
, when parameterized by
|V(H)|
. This can be seen as follows. For each edge
xy\in E(H)
, we guess an edge
uv\in E(G)
where we place
u
in
W(x)
and
v
in
W(y)
. We also guess a vertex
t
of
G
to be in a bag
W(z)
for each isolated vertex
z\in V(H)
.
We assign the set
R
of all non-guessed vertices to an arbitrary bag. For the variant
H
-
Identification Minor
, we delete all edges incident to
R
and between vertices of bags that must be non-adjacent.
For the variant
H
-
Induced Identification Minor
, we simply delete all vertices of
R
. We now identify each bag to a single vertex. If we do not obtain
H
(which may happen when edge deletions are not allowed), then we discard the branch and try again.

We leave a more extensive complexity study of

{\cal H}
-
Identification Minor
and
{\cal H}
-
Induced Identification Minor
for future work.
As a final remark, we note that the identification minor relation is well-quasi-ordered
[
24
]
, which implies that every identification-minor-closed graph class admits a finite obstruction set. In contrast, this does not hold for the induced identification minor relation: for example, the complements of cycles form an infinite antichain (see also
[
24
]
for another infinite antichain).
