External validation of SOFA 2 score: a retrospective Chinese cohort study
Authors: Rong Liufu, Maomao Cao, Xian-Qing Shi, Xiao-Yun Fu, Feng Shen, Yan Chen, Jin-Min Peng, Wen-Ying Han, Chun-Yao Wang, Li Weng, Xiang Zhou, Bin Du, China Critical Care Clinical Trials Group (CCCCTG), China National Critical Care Quality Control Center Group
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe revised Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) 2 score was developed to improve early identification of acute organ dysfunction, which restricted from incomplete data in resource-limited settings. To compare the performance of original (SOFA-1) and updated SOFA-2 scores in these settings.MethodsThe retrospective multicenter analysis included 118,542 adult patients admitted to intensive care units (ICUs) between 2014 and 2024. SOFA scores were calculated using clinical data from the first seven ICU days. The primary outcome was ICU mortality. Multiple approaches were applied to address missing data. We assessed the discriminative performance of total SOFA and organ-specific scores using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC).ResultsA total of 5,286 patients (4.45%) died in the ICU, and 6,173 patients (5.2%) died during hospitalization. Across all cohorts, higher SOFA-2 subscores (0–4) and total scores were associated with lower frequency and higher ICU mortality. SOFA-2 demonstrated significantly better discrimination for ICU mortality than SOFA-1 [AUROC of 80.9% (95% CI, 80.3%–81.5%) vs. 76.7% (95% CI, 76.0%–77.4%)]. Each 1-point increase in SOFA-2 was associated with increasing odds of ICU mortality (OR 1.33, 95% CI 1.33–1.35). Between-cohort variance was lower for SOFA-2 (0.07) than for SOFA-1 (0.23), indicating greater robustness across these cohorts. Longitudinal analyses showed widening divergence in SOFA-2 trajectories between survivors and non-survivors over the first 7 days.ConclusionIn this external validation, SOFA-2 demonstrated improved discrimination for ICU mortality and more consistent score distribution than SOFA-1. These findings support the broader applicability of SOFA-2 across diverse healthcare settings, including those with substantial resource constraints.

Introduction

Over the past decades, the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score has been essential for assessing organ dysfunction and predicting outcomes in critically ill patients [[1], [2], [3]]. It is widely used in clinical studies, where daily maximum, mean, and delta SOFA scores serve as key endpoints [[4], [5], [6], [7], [8], [9]]. However, the original 1996 SOFA system no longer meets modern ICU needs. Earlier and more convenient identification of organ failure is required, and contemporary organ support technologies are not captured in the original (SOFA-1) score. These limitations underscore the need for an updated scoring framework.

The newly revised SOFA-2 addresses several key updates [10,11]: (1) Inclusion of organ support therapeutic strategies: Incorporation of specific drugs (such as all other vasopressor and inotrope agents) and organ supportive devices [noninvasive respiratory support modalities, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) and cardiac mechanical support, replacement therapy (RRT)]; (2) Simplified the evaluation parameters: provision of alternatives including SpO2 while arterial blood gas analysis is not available; for the Glasgow Coma Score (GCS), motor-scale domain was used when the 3 domains remained impossible; (3) The modified point score cutoff for platelet, bilirubin level, PaO2/FiO2, and GCS. These modifications are intended to improve both clinical applicability and prognostic accuracy across diverse healthcare settings.

Although SOFA-2 was developed to improve applicability across diverse healthcare settings, its performance in healthcare systems with different ICU capacities and critical care delivery models remains uncertain. Compared with many high-income countries, ICU resources in some regions may be characterized by lower ICU bed availability, lower staffing ratios, variable access to advanced organ support therapies, and differences in data collection practices. These factors may directly influence the assessment of organ dysfunction and the implementation of updated SOFA-2 components, particularly those related to respiratory, cardiovascular, and renal support [10]. Consequently, external validation is essential in healthcare systems with heterogeneous resource availability and varying organ support practices.

This study involves multicenter cohorts, reflecting the regional diversity and uneven development across mainland China. The primary aim is to validate the updated SOFA-2 score in routine clinical practice.

Method

Study design and population

This was a multicenter, retrospective study involving 118,542 patients from three medical centers in China between January 1st, 2013, and December 31st, 2024. Clinical data were extracted from PUMCH (Peking Union Medical College Hospital cohort), Guiyang (Guiyang cohort), and Zunyi (Zunyi cohort). The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Ethics Committee at PUMCH (Approval NO.I-25PJ1730, 2025.08.04). The current study comprised adult patients (≥18 years) who were admitted to the ICU during their hospital stay. The exclusion criteria included: (1) missing data for completeness of SOFA domain; (2) missing outcome data (e.g., death status); (3) ICU readmission; (4) hospital stay exceeding 365 days; (5) prolonged mechanical ventilation (>5000 h); 6) <18 years.

Data extraction

Patient data were extracted from electronic health record and included: (1) demographic information, indigenous status, and lengths of ICU and hospital stay; (2) vital signs during hospitalization, including mean arterial pressure (MAP), and oxygen saturation; (3) laboratory tests results, including white blood cell count (WCC), platelet count, serum creatinine, total bilirubin, and atrial blood gas; (4) the SOFA-1 score, GCS, and neurological evaluation including sedation status; (5) administrations of vasopressor and other organ support therapies used for SOFA score calculation. Comorbidities were identified using discharge diagnoses coded in the Chinese version of International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10), and categorized according to the Charlson Comorbidity Index. Consistent with the previous study [10], baseline data were defined as the worst values recorded within the first 24 h of ICU admission.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was ICU mortality, defined as death prior to ICU discharge.

Missing data

To ensure accurate SOFA scoring, completeness of key parameters was required across the three cohorts. These parameters included Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), PaO2/FiO2, MAP and laboratory tests (total bilirubin, serum creatinine, and platelets count). Some flexibility in handling missing data was applied under the following conditions: (1) Missing GCS score was inferred using the best motor response. For sedated patients, the pre-sedation level of consciousness was used to estimate the GCS. (2) Missing PaO2/FiO2 values were substituted with SpO2/FiO2 ratios. A missing FiO2 was assumed to be 0.21 (room air). (3) MAP values were obtained via intra-arterial blood pressure measurements, or when unavailable, by cuff-based measurements.

Missing baseline laboratory data were imputed logically, using the earliest available ICU measurements. For longitudinal analyses, three strategies were employed to handle missing data: (1) considered the missing values as zero, assuming no organ dysfunction was observed; (2) carrying forward the last available observation; (3) performing multiple imputation using AMELIA R-package. These approaches were applied to both individual SOFA components and the total SOFA score. Main analyses were based on zero-imputation and complete-case data, while sensitivity analyses evaluated the alternative imputation methods.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were summarized as frequency (percentages), while continuous variables were presented as mean (standard deviation, SD) or median (interquartile range), depending on distribution. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for non-normally distributed continuous variables, while the t-test was applied for normally distributed data. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. To evaluate predictive performance, random-effects models were used to estimate the area under receiving operator curves (AUROC) for both total SOFA-1 and SOFA-2, following the methodology described in previous studies [10]. The association between every 1-point increase in the total SOFA score (SOFA-1 VS SOFA-2) and ICU mortality was analyzed using generalized mixed-effects models with random intercepts for each database.

Longitudinal analyses were performed to assess trends in total SOFA-2 scores from Day 1 to Day 7 across all three cohorts, applying different strategies for handling missing data. The mean, and maximum of total SOFA-2 score over this 7-day period and its association with ICU mortality were also analyzed. Additionally, SOFA-2 and SOFA-1 scores on Day 1 were compared across the six domains using a Sankey diagram. The difference and association between SOFA-1 and SOFA-2 scores, and stratified ICU mortality across difference categories was jointly characterized score agreement, distributional shifts, and their prognostic implications.

A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analysis were conducted using R software (version 4.2.1, R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Overall, 66,043 patients (55.7%) received invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV), with a mean duration of 77.3 ± 196.3 h (68.2 ± 161.3 h in PUMCH, 55.0 ± 205.4 h in Guiyang, and 110.9 ± 209.0 h in the Zunyi cohort). During ICU stay, 5,194 patients (4.4%) received continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), and 34,249 (28.9%) were administered vasopressors or inotropic agents via continuous IV infusion for at least 1 h. Dopamine was used as the sole vasoactive agent in 4,906 of 118,542 patients. Most veno-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VA-ECMO) cases occurred in the Pxx cohort (87 of 110), and veno-venous ECMO (VV-ECMO) was implanted to 81 of 120 patients. The mean ICU and in-hospital lengths of stay were 6 ± 10 days and 18 ± 21 days, respectively. In total, 5,286 patients (4.45%) died in the ICU, and 6,173 patients (5.2%) died during hospitalization.

SOFA domain and distribution

The distribution of SOFA scores for each organ system, along with corresponding ICU mortality across the three cohorts, is presented in eFig. S2. As SOFA-2 scores increased from 0 to 4 in each of the six domains, a consistent decrease in frequency and an increase in ICU mortality were observed across the three cohorts. In contrast, SOFA-1 scores in the kidney and cardiovascular domains did not display this expected gradient, showing similar frequency and mortality between scores of 2 and 3 (eFig. S2). Analysis comparing complete case data with imputed data (missing values replaced with zero) demonstrated similar patterns across all six domains (eFig. S2). Day 1 ICU data for the six SOFA-2 domains yielded comparable results across the three individual cohorts (eFigure S3–S6).

The total SOFA-2 and SOFA-1 score during the first 24 h revealed that increasing scores were associated with rising ICU mortality and decreasing frequency (eFigure S7). The progression was smoother in SOFA-2 version, and the distribution in the complete case analysis was consistent with that from the primary imputation method (eFigure S8–S10). Reclassification analyses indicated that 39.1% of patients have the same total SOFA scores, and SOFA-2 was greater in 24.4% and lower in 36.6% (eTable S2). There is no significant systematic deviation (the mean difference is close to zero) between the SOFA-1 and SOFA-2 scores in general or across each domain (Fig. 1A-C, eFig. S11–S16). Prediction of ICU mortality

Data completeness allowed for the total SOFA score calculation in 84,353 patients for SOFA-1 and 89,398 patients for SOFA-2 (Table 2). Discriminatory performance for ICU mortality was significantly better for SOFA-2 than SOFA-1 score, with AUROC of 80.9% (95% CI, 80.3 %–81.5 %) and 76.7% (95% CI, 76.0 %–77.4 %) (Figure 4). AUROC values were highest when using complete-case data to other imputation approaches (eTable 3). The improvement in AUROC with SOFA-2 were consistent across all three cohorts and the overall population, with most of the six SOFA domain scores demonstrating better discrimination in SOFA-2 (Fig. 3). The same pattern was observed in the mean and maximum SOFA score. Discussion

Key findings

This study externally validated the newly developed SOFA-2 criteria against the original SOFA-1, demonstrating superior prognostic accuracy for ICU mortality from a developing country. As an external validation, this study included a relatively completed 6 organ systems with a score ranging from 0 to 4 and highlighted the enhanced discriminatory capacity of the SOFA-2 criteria in resource-limited setting. SOFA scores were derived from electronic health records, which showed a strong correlation to mortality. In line with the baseline findings, longitudinal SOFA-2 scores from Day 1 to Day 7 demonstrated a sustained increase among non-survivors, reinforcing its predictive value. Multiple imputation approaches confirmed the robustness of the results despite missing data.

Flexibility of updated criteria in resource-heterogeneous setting

The systematically higher values observed for SOFA-2 can be attributed to its revised scoring framework, including the incorporation of additional organ-support interventions, more flexible SpO2-based respiratory criteria suitable for low-resource settings, and updated cutoff points. As illness severity increased, the divergence between SOFA-1 and SOFA-2 widened, indicating reduced agreement at higher levels of organ dysfunction. These larger discrepancies reflect the influence of the updated SOFA-2 components—particularly the reliance on S/F ratio when arterial blood gases are unavailable and the refined assessment of cardiovascular and renal support—which reclassified a subset of patients into substantially different severity strata compared with SOFA-1.

Importantly, patients who received higher scores under SOFA-2 than SOFA-1 exhibited disproportionately elevated ICU mortality, whereas those assigned lower scores by SOFA-2 had correspondingly reduced risk. This gradient suggests that SOFA-2 not only shifts the overall score distribution but also improves mortality discrimination by more accurately capturing physiologic derangements that SOFA-1 underestimated.

Moreover, the missing-data strategies proposed in the original SOFA-2 framework performed robustly across our multicenter cohorts, demonstrating its practical adaptability in resource-limited environments. In the respiratory domain, for example, the S/F ratio effectively substituted for PaO2-based metrics when arterial blood gas measurements were unavailable, enabling reliable identification of high-risk patients. These findings offer pragmatic validation that SOFA-2 enhances early recognition of clinical deterioration and supports timely clinical decision-making in resource-heterogeneous settings.

Improvements for earlier detection of organ dysfunction

Key updates in SOFA-2 addressed known limitations of SOFA-1, particularly in the kidney and hemostasis domains. In the kidney domain, SOFA-2 adopted the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcome (KDIGO) criteria, refining both the urine output and serum creatinine thresholds [12,13]. Unlike the SOFA-1 criterion, which required 24 -h urine output [3], SOFA-2 uses a 6-h time frame, allowing earlier detection of acute kidney injury, along with earlier intervention, which could improve patient outcomes [[14], [15], [16]]. The inclusion of CRRT - both initiated and indicated but not received - also better accommodates resource-limited settings. The mortality risk stratification of kidney domain of the three cohorts was suitable for not only the baseline (the first 24 h ICU admission), but also the longitudinal analyses from Day 2 to Day 7. However, assigning chronic RRT using a score of 4 may raise concerns about conflating chronic and acute organ dysfunction.

In the hemostasis domain, platelet less than 50 × 109/L was categorized into score 4, instead of less than 20 × 109/L in SOFA-1, enabling earlier identification of critically ill patients. This change led to improved stratification in frequency and mortality, with slightly higher AUROC in SOFA-2 version than SOFA-1 in the total cohort.

Integration of organ support and therapeutic indicators

Another major update of SOFA-2 included the incorporation of organ support therapies, such as ECMO, ventilation assistance, and CRRT, acknowledged their increasing role in ICU care [[17], [18], [19]]. Drugs administration for delirium and cardiac dysfunction was also considered in the new criteria.

Despite these enhancements, integrating organ support into SOFA scoring system contributed substantially to discrepancies between SOFA versions.

vasoactive drugs were used in 48.3% of patients in ASDI (Austria) cohort, but only 14.6% in ORCHESTRA (Brazil) and 16.4% in NICRF (Nepal), respectively. These cohort had comparable severity scores (SAPS-3, 49 [16] in ASDI vs. 46 [17] in ORCHESTRA vs. APACHE II, 13 [8]) and ICU mortality (9.7%, 8.4%, and 15.6%, respectively) [10]. The use of vasopressors and inotropes often depends on intensivists’ preference and local resource constraints, affecting both patients care and score assignment. According to SOFA-1, patients in use of dopamine ≤5 mcg/kg/min or any dose of dobutamine corresponded to score 2 in cardiovascular domain. But in many centers, only a few patients would attribute a score of 2 because of rare use of dopamine and the norepinephrine would be assessed for higher score [20]. Conversely, in resource-limited area, dopamine is still used as the primary, even only choice for many critically ill patients (4.13%). Therefore, more patients will be attributed a score of 2 points, which negatively influence regression models in research and evaluation of patients at the bedside [20]. The updated cardiovascular domain in SOFA-2 better accounts for such variability, improving the score’s interpretability and utility. Nevertheless, the incorporation of advanced organ support therapies may increase the influence of resource availability on score assignment, potentially exacerbating disparities and limiting comparability between ICUs with differing levels of technological capability.

The same pattern was showed in application of cardiovascular mechanical support, RRT, and IMV among three cohorts. Patients in Zxx cohort had higher Charlson score, longer duration of ventilator assistance, and higher mortality rate, yet showed a skewed score distribution with fewer patients scoring 0–3 and similar mortality for those scoring 14−21. The highlights geographic inequities in access to critical care resource, not only in different countries of Asia, but within a single country such as China [21].

Given that not all patients have the equal chance of receiving the specified organ support, we caution that updated SOFA criteria may under-detect critically ill patients in resource-constrained environments.

Limitations

There are some limitations in our study that must be addressed. First, the inclusion of only three cohorts in China inherently limits the study's national representativeness. Nevertheless, this cohort represented diverse healthcare settings, ranging from a national tertiary referral center in Beijing to hospitals in Guizhou province, a relatively resource-constrained region. Although China is classified as an upper-middle-income country, substantial regional variation in critical care resources exists, and overall ICU capacity remains lower than that of many high-income countries. As a result, the study cohort encompasses considerable heterogeneity in resource availability, ICU infrastructure, and clinical practice, which may enhance the relevance of our findings across settings with diverse critical care capabilities. Second, as we focused on ICU mortality to align with the original SOFA-2 study, we did not assess the relationship between SOFA scores and in-hospital or 28-day mortality. The relatively low ICU mortality observed in current study may partly reflect local end-to-life care practices, whereby some patients with a poor prognosis are discharged from hospital before death. Consequently, ICU mortality may underestimate the true short-term mortality burden in this population. Third, neurological evaluation relied on the GCS, but delirium treatment was assessed retrospectively using electronic records, potentially introducing bias. Fourth, left ventricular assist devices (LVADs) were used in only five patients (Pxx cohort), underscoring the disparity in organ support availability and its impact on scoring. Additionally, the study excluded data from pediatric and neonatal ICUs, limiting generalizability to younger populations. Nonetheless, our dataset did include emergency, respiratory, and cardiovascular ICUs, representing major critical care settings in China.

Conclusions

In this external validation study conducted in a developing country, the updated SOFA-2 criteria demonstrated better discrimination in ICU mortality and frequency distribution, compared to the original SOFA-1. These findings support the broader applicability of SOFA-2, though caution is warranted in LMICs due to disparities in organ support availability.
