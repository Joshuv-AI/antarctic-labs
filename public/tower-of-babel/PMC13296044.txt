Add-on tocilizumab versus placebo for resistant major depression in psychiatric outpatients with low-grade inflammation in a tertiary public hospital in Southern Brazil: randomised triple-blind clinical trial protocol
Authors: Pedro Henrico Grazziotin Portal, Graziella Nunes Peixoto, Marina Ribeiro de Matos, Luiz Carlos Nascimento da Silva, Giulio Bertollo Alexandrino, Paulo Henrique Gonçalves Dutra, Bruno Perosa Carniel, Neusa Sica da Rocha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionEmerging evidence supports a role for interleukin 6 (IL-6), a pro-inflammatory cytokine, in the pathogenesis of treatment-resistant major depressive disorder (TRD). However, interventional studies targeting IL-6 in this population remain scarce. Tocilizumab is a humanised monoclonal antibody that inhibits IL-6 signalling and is approved for the treatment of autoimmune conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis. The primary objective of this study is to examine whether IL-6 inhibition via tocilizumab can impact depressive symptoms, inflammation-related biomarkers and cognition in patients with TRD. A secondary objective is to compare the biological profiles of patients with TRD with elevated inflammation to those of healthy controls.Methods and analysisThis is a proof-of-concept, randomised, parallel-group, triple-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial. 22 adult outpatients diagnosed with TRD and evidence of low-grade inflammation (serum C reactive protein≥3 mg/L) will be randomised (1:1) to receive either one intravenous infusion of tocilizumab (8 mg/kg; maximum 800 mg) or normal saline, administered as an add-on to their ongoing treatment. Psychiatric, cognitive and biomarker assessments will be performed at baseline and at follow-up visits on days 7, 14 and 28 post-infusion. Additionally, 10 healthy controls with no psychiatric history will undergo the same baseline assessments for biomarker comparison.Ethics and disseminationThe study has been approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre (Project number: 2025-0245, CAAE: 88904825.7.0000.5327). Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, scientific meetings and, on request, lay summaries for participants.Trial registration numberNCT07052058.

Introduction

Scientific background and study rationale

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is one of the most prevalent psychiatric conditions in the general population,1 associated with a substantial disease burden and significant functional and financial impairments. According to the WHO, depressive disorders collectively rank as the second leading cause of years lived with disability (YLDs) worldwide.2 According to the World Mental Health (WMH), the average prevalence of depression in developing countries is 11.1%.3 Despite advances in pharmacotherapy and psychotherapy, a significant portion of patients does not achieve satisfactory remission with conventional treatments, as many individuals with major depression do not recover after initial treatment: a prospective observational study, for example, showed that among 3671 outpatients treated with citalopram, only 37% achieved remission.4

The concept of treatment-resistant depression (TRD) is widely disseminated in the literature and refers to the absence of response to at least two adequate courses of antidepressants during a single major depressive episode.5–7 Although most experts agree on this definition, there is no absolute consensus on whether failed treatments must involve distinct classes of antidepressants. In general, an adequate treatment course is defined as one in which the patient receives an effective dose of the medication for a sufficient duration, typically 4–8 weeks, depending on the study and clinical guideline.8 9 Furthermore, formal definitions of TRD usually do not include failures in non-pharmacological approaches, such as psychotherapy, electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) or transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), although these strategies are indicated for specific cases.8 This variation in diagnostic criteria can affect the estimated prevalence of TRD, influence clinical guidelines and generate inconsistencies in the therapeutic management of the condition.10 The degree of treatment response is classified based on symptom severity reduction: less than 25% improvement indicates non-response, 26% to 49% indicates partial response, while a reduction greater than 50% constitutes a clinical response. Remission is defined as the absence or minimal presence of symptoms.11 12

Evidence supporting this definition includes findings from the Sequenced Treatment Alternatives to Relieve Depression (STAR*D) study, one of the largest prospective trials on the topic, which evaluated 3671 patients with major depression who underwent up to 4 sequential pharmacological treatment attempts.4 13 14 Results showed that the remission rate was 37% after the first treatment, 31% after the second and dropped drastically to 14% and 13% in the third and fourth attempts, respectively. These findings support the hypothesis that treatment resistance becomes progressively harder to overcome, highlighting the need for more effective interventions for this patient group. Furthermore, TRD is associated with poorer psychosocial functioning, greater use of healthcare resources and increased all-cause mortality.1 15 Treatment costs and healthcare utilisation are significantly higher in patients with TRD compared with individuals with non-resistant depression or no depression,16 17 reinforcing the urgency for new therapeutic strategies that can offer effective and accessible alternatives for this population. Supporting this need, a multicentre, multinational observational study conducted in psychiatric reference centres revealed that Brazil had the highest prevalence of TRD among the countries evaluated, reaching 40.4%, a value higher than the Latin American average (29.1%) and the rates observed in Mexico (20.7%), Colombia (32.1%) and Argentina (33.2%).18 These data underscore the importance of developing new therapeutic options in the Brazilian context, especially for outpatients who often lack access to more complex resources such as ECT.

In recent years, interest has grown in the role of inflammation in the pathophysiology of depression. Consistent evidence shows that patients with depression have elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin-6 (IL-6), and acute-phase proteins, such as C reactive protein (CRP).19–21 These findings support the neuroimmune hypothesis of depression, which posits that inflammatory processes may contribute to the neurobiological dysfunction associated with the disorder.22 Meta-analyses indicate that IL-6 and CRP are persistently elevated in patients with TRD,23 24 suggesting a subgroup of individuals whose pathogenesis may be directly linked to inflammatory processes.25

The causal relationship between inflammation and depression is not yet fully understood. Longitudinal studies indicate that elevated IL-6 and CRP levels in childhood are associated with an increased risk of depression in adulthood, even after controlling for confounding factors such as body mass index (BMI) and maternal depression.26 27 This association suggests that inflammation precedes the development of depression in some individuals, rather than merely resulting from the disorder.28 Additionally, genetic analyses based on Mendelian randomisation indicate that variants in the IL-6 receptor gene, associated with lower inflammatory activity, confer protection against severe depression.29 30 The aetiology of depression was long supported by the serotonergic hypothesis; however, a recent systematic review found no association between reduced serotonin activity and/or concentrations and depression.31 Nevertheless, several meta-analyses have found an association between levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines and MDD32–34 and have shown that such cytokines can induce what is known as sickness behaviour—a marked behavioural change characterised predominantly by demotivation in severely ill patients.35 The most frequently observed cytokine alterations include increased IL-6 and Tumour Necrosis Factor alpha (TNF-α), reinforcing the role of inflammation in the pathophysiology of major depression.32 34

The use of anti-inflammatory agents as a potential therapeutic strategy for depression has been widely studied. A randomised clinical trial in patients with TRD, which excluded individuals with chronic physical illnesses, assessed infliximab, a TNF-α antagonist, and found that patients with elevated baseline CRP levels responded better to treatment.36 Similarly, meta-analyses indicate that drugs that block the action of pro-inflammatory cytokines can improve depressive symptoms, regardless of improvement in the underlying disease.37 38 This finding reinforces the need for clinical trials specifically designed to assess the efficacy of IL-6 blockade in the treatment of TRD. From this perspective, clinical trials aiming to modulate IL-6 should focus on patients with depression with signs of inflammation, as individuals who do not respond to antidepressants are more likely to present with low-grade inflammation.23 24 Although evidence indicates that prophylactic tocilizumab administration in a longitudinal cohort of allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation patients was associated with worse depressive symptoms (β = 5.74; 95% CI 0.75 to 10.73; p=0.03),39 the literature does not consistently support a detrimental psychiatric effect of IL-6 blockade in other cohorts. Patients with rheumatoid arthritis exhibited a favourable impact of tocilizumab on depression and anxiety severity.40 Furthermore, improvements in depressive symptoms were reported during tocilizumab therapy that appeared partly independent of changes in disease activity.41 Similarly, improvements in depressed mood and anhedonia were observed among patients with rheumatoid arthritis and multicentric Castleman disease treated with IL-6 antibodies,42 and a systematic review reported a lower risk of depression among patients with rheumatoid arthritis treated with tocilizumab (relative risk (RR) 0.68, 95% CI 0.20 to 2.31).43 Also, Mendelian randomisation studies44 45 suggest that IL-6 signalling shows heterogeneous associations with depressive phenotypes rather than a uniform harmful effect of IL-6 inhibition. Taken together, these data suggest that investigating IL-6 antagonism in carefully selected patients with TRD and low-grade inflammation represents a significant gap warranting further study. In this context, tocilizumab, a humanised monoclonal antibody that blocks IL-6 signalling, emerges as a potential candidate for modulating the inflammatory axis in TRD.

In addition to potential clinical advantages, the use of tocilizumab as an adjunct in the treatment of TRD may be a more financially and logistically viable option than ECT. ECT administration requires complex infrastructure, including a specialised centre with a procedure room, access to general anaesthesia, a psychiatrist trained in the procedure, an anaesthetist and a nursing team trained to provide anaesthetic support and post-procedure monitoring. On the other hand, intravenous infusion of tocilizumab can be performed in a lower-complexity hospital setting, such as a day hospital, under the supervision of a trained nurse, thereby significantly reducing operational costs and expanding access to treatment. This characteristic makes the use of tocilizumab a potentially more accessible and sustainable strategy within the healthcare system, especially for resource-limited centres.

Proposed study

We propose a randomised, triple-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial to evaluate the effects of tocilizumab in patients with TRD. The primary objective of the study is to determine whether IL-6 inhibition leads to significant improvements in depressive symptoms and inflammatory biomarkers in outpatients with TRD.

This study is based on evidence suggesting that low-grade inflammation may be a critical factor in resistance to antidepressant treatment.23 24 Therefore, we will include only patients with elevated CRP levels (≥3 mg/L), allowing us to test the hypothesis that IL-6 modulation may be particularly beneficial in individuals with low-grade systemic inflammation. This design will enable a better clinical and mechanistic understanding of this potentially distinct subgroup.

Due to the higher prevalence of TRD in Brazil (40.4%) compared with other Latin American settings (where the regional average is approximately 29.1%, with lower rates in countries such as Mexico (20.7%), Colombia (32.1%) and Argentina (33.2%)),18 there is an urgent need to develop accessible and scalable therapeutic strategies adapted to the Brazilian context, particularly in outpatient settings where access to advanced interventions such as ECT remains severely restricted. Although ECT is an established and effective intervention for severe TRD, it is not even included in the official procedure lists of the National Supplementary Health Agency (ANS), limiting its availability within Brazil’s public health system (SUS).

Given this scenario, the inclusion of outpatients with TRD who do not meet criteria for hospitalisation is particularly relevant, as this population represents a growing clinical demand. A pharmacological intervention such as tocilizumab may be a logistically feasible and potentially cost-effective alternative, especially in settings where complex ECT infrastructure is unavailable. Tocilizumab infusion can be administered in a lower-complexity hospital environment, such as a day hospital, under nursing supervision, reducing operational costs and expanding access.

In addition to changes in depressive symptoms, measured by the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS), other outcomes will be assessed, including the need for ECT following the intervention, changes in cognitive performance, quality of life and levels of IL-6, CRP and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF).

BDNF is a well-established mediator of antidepressant response and neuroplasticity, with substantial evidence supporting its role as a key transducer of antidepressant effects and cognitive improvement in MDD.46 Additionally, preclinical studies investigating IL-6 blockade provide biological plausibility for assessing BDNF in the context of tocilizumab treatment. In particular, experimental studies in juvenile collagen-induced arthritis rat models demonstrated that IL-6 inhibition with tocilizumab reduced anxiety-like behaviour, improved cognitive performance and reversed neuropathological alterations, findings consistent with modulation of neuroplasticity-related pathways, including BDNF signalling.47 48 These data suggest that IL-6 blockade may influence neurobiological mechanisms relevant to depression beyond inflammatory pathways alone. Therefore, measuring BDNF in this study may help elucidate potential neuroplastic mechanisms underlying IL-6 inhibition in TRD.

Study aims and hypotheses

The primary aim of this study is to conduct a randomised, triple-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial to evaluate whether a single intravenous infusion of tocilizumab, which inhibits IL-6 signalling, reduces depressive symptoms in patients with TRD. Our primary hypothesis is that tocilizumab will result in a greater reduction in depressive symptoms compared with placebo. The study will focus on individuals with low-grade systemic inflammation, defined as serum CRP levels≥3 mg/L, who are hypothesised to benefit most from IL-6 modulation. We also hypothesise that tocilizumab will decrease serum levels of IL-6 and CRP and increase BDNF levels, and that these findings will be associated with clinical global improvement. Additional anticipated effects include improved global clinical impression and reduced need for ECT following the intervention.

Secondary objectives include evaluating the intervention’s impact on cognitive functioning and assessing changes in quality of life. We hypothesise that participants receiving tocilizumab will demonstrate better cognitive performance and improved quality of life compared with those receiving a placebo. Furthermore, we expect that the rate of psychiatric hospitalisation within 6 months post-infusion will be lower in the tocilizumab group, indicating a potential sustained effect of inflammatory modulation on depressive outcomes.

Methods

This protocol has been prepared in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) 2025 statement.49 The planned start date was scheduled for 25 August 2025; however, recruitment was delayed due to the postponement of funding disbursement from the governmental grant supporting the study. Disbursement is expected in the early part of the second half of 2026; therefore, the planned recruitment start date is 1 July 2026, with the planned end of intervention on 1 July 2028.

Patient and public involvement

The development of the study protocol incorporated feedback from individuals with lived experience of mental illness, who contributed to the design of the participant information materials, consent forms, and data collection procedures. Their input helped ensure that the study documents are accessible and sensitive to participants’ experiences. A summary of the study results will be shared with interested participants on completion.

Study design and sample

This is a randomised, triple-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial conducted in a tertiary hospital. Participants will be randomised into two arms: an intervention group receiving a single intravenous infusion of tocilizumab and a control group receiving a placebo (0.9% saline) in an identical protocol. The sample will include 22 psychiatric outpatients with a diagnosis of TRD, defined as failure to respond to at least two adequate courses of antidepressant treatment. In addition, 10 healthy controls (HCs) with no history of psychiatric disorders will be recruited to provide an internal reference for baseline inflammatory biomarker measurements. These samples will be collected and processed using the same pre-analytical and analytical procedures as those used for the TRD group, enabling within-study comparability and minimising variability due to assay conditions. Given the known heterogeneity of cytokine levels across studies and populations,50 this approach improves the interpretability of biomarker findings within the study context. The HC group will be used exclusively for baseline comparisons and will not be included in treatment-effect analyses. For a detailed overview of the study design, see figure 1.

Intervention

Participants allocated to the experimental group will receive a single intravenous infusion of tocilizumab (8 mg/kg; maximum dose of 800 mg), diluted in 100 mL of 0.9% saline solution, administered over approximately 60 min, with gradual titration of the infusion rate based on patient tolerance. The placebo group will receive 0.9% saline following an identical protocol to ensure study blinding.

All participants will be under ongoing psychiatric care at study entry and receiving guideline-concordant treatment.51 Background psychotropic treatment will be maintained without protocol-driven changes during the primary outcome period, unless modification is required for clinical safety or urgent therapeutic need. Concomitant treatments will be carefully documented at baseline, including medication class, dose, duration and augmentation strategies, and major treatment changes during the acute follow-up period will not be permitted unless clinically necessary.

Tocilizumab is the first-in-class, humanised monoclonal antibody targeting the interleukin-6 receptor (IL-6R) approved for clinical use. It is commercially available and authorised by regulatory agencies such as Anvisa, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis and juvenile idiopathic arthritis. Tocilizumab inhibits both IL-6 classic and trans-signalling pathways, the latter being responsible for most of the pro-inflammatory actions of IL-6, making it one of the most effective options for complete IL-6 inhibition.52

Evidence from interferon studies in humans53 and rodent models54 indicates that peripheral immune activation can contribute to the development of depressive symptoms, as circulating IL-6 and other cytokines influence brain function through neural, humoral and cellular mechanisms.35 55–57 Therefore, tocilizumab, which acts predominantly in the periphery, may alleviate depressive symptoms by reducing systemic inflammatory activation. This hypothesis is supported by findings on infliximab, an anti-TNF-α monoclonal antibody that, despite limited blood–brain barrier penetration, has shown efficacy in modulating depressive symptoms.36 Similarly, peripherally acting anti-IL-6 monoclonal antibodies have been shown to reduce depression-like behaviour in murine models exposed to repeated stress.58 In rheumatoid arthritis, a single infusion of tocilizumab has been shown to improve clinical and laboratory parameters within 48 hours, with more pronounced results observed between 1 and 2 weeks.59 60

Eligibility criteria

We will recruit adult outpatient participants aged 18 years or older with a diagnosis of TRD, as defined by the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition (DSM-5) criteria and confirmed using the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview (MINI).61 Treatment resistance will be defined as the absence of a clinically meaningful response to at least two adequate antidepressant trials during the current depressive episode, as determined retrospectively through structured clinical assessment and review of available medical records.5–7 9 Adequate treatment trials will be defined as antidepressants administered at or above the minimum licensed dose for at least 4 weeks.9 Participants must also have a baseline HDRS score of 19 or higher at study entry. Participants must have elevated serum CRP levels≥3 mg/L as evidence of low-grade inflammation62 and the capacity to provide written informed consent.

Exclusion criteria will include any current or past diagnosis of bipolar disorder or personality disorder, history of substance use disorder within the past 6 months, presence of active infection or autoimmune disease, pregnancy or lactation, active liver disease or transaminase elevation>1.5× the upper limit of normal,63 64 history of significant cardiovascular disease, recent cancer or haematological disorders. Participants with a BMI greater than 40 kg/m² or a weight over 100 kg will also be excluded, as it would not be possible to administer the intended dose of 8 mg/kg without exceeding the maximum allowable dose of 800 mg. Individuals with known hypersensitivity to tocilizumab or its excipients will not be eligible.

HCs will be required to have no current or past psychiatric disorders, as assessed by the Self-report Questionnaire (SRQ-20), an instrument developed by the WHO to measure the level of suspicion of mental disorders.65 See table 1 for complete inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome of this study is treatment response, defined as a≥50% reduction in HDRS score from baseline to day 28.66 Secondary outcomes will include changes in serum levels of the biomarkers IL-6, CRP and BDNF from baseline to day 28, as indicators of biological response to the intervention. Global clinical improvement will be assessed using the Clinical Global Impression (CGI) scale,67 and overall psychiatric symptomatology will be evaluated as change from baseline in the Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale (BPRS) total score.68

Secondary outcomes will also include change in cognitive performance, assessed using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) total score from baseline to day 28,69 and change in quality of life, measured using the WHO Quality of Life Abbreviated Instrument (WHOQOL-BREF) domain scores and the European Health Interview Survey-Quality of Life (EUROHIS-QOL) 8-item total score from baseline to day 180.70 71 In addition, the proportion of participants with at least one psychiatric hospitalisation within 180 days following the intervention will be assessed as a clinically relevant outcome reflecting longer-term patient stability and healthcare utilisation, and the proportion of participants requiring initiation of ECT during follow-up will also be assessed as a clinically relevant outcome reflecting treatment escalation.

Sample size and statistical power

The sample size calculation was based on the study ‘Evidence for Anti-inflammatory Effects of Adalimumab in Treatment of Patients with Major Depressive Disorder’.72 Considering a 5% alpha error, 80% statistical power, a 1:1 case–control ratio, a true mean difference of 2.93, an expected SD of±2.0, a clinically significant margin of 5 and an anticipated dropout rate of 10%, the estimated sample size was 22 participants (11 in the intervention group and 11 in the control group). The calculation was performed using G*Power software.73

Randomisation and blinding

Participants will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to receive either tocilizumab or a placebo using REDCap software. The trial will follow a triple-blind design, ensuring that participants, investigators and outcome assessors remain unaware of treatment allocation. Randomisation codes will be generated within REDCap and securely communicated to the hospital’s central pharmacy. The pharmacy will be responsible for dispensing tocilizumab or 0.9% saline placebo according to the randomisation schedule. Infusion packs will be prepared in identical packaging to ensure that the study medication and placebo are visually indistinguishable. This approach will preserve allocation concealment and ensure that all parties involved, including clinical staff administering the infusion and those assessing outcomes, remain blinded throughout the study.

Statistical analysis

Analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle. Data normality will be assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. For analysis of the primary outcomes, repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) will be used to evaluate treatment outcome parameters (clinical and psychiatric scales) and biological parameters. Linear mixed-effects models will be applied to handle missing data. The effect size will be estimated using Cohen’s D test. Differences with p values<0.05 will be considered statistically significant, and all analyses will be conducted in SPSS Statistics for Windows.

Participant identification

Participants will be recruited at the clinical research centre of Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre (HCPA), primarily from outpatient psychiatry services in Porto Alegre. Potentially eligible patients will be identified through medical record review and clinical evaluation conducted by the research team. In addition, 10 HCs will be recruited through blood banks and social media advertisements. These HCs will have no current or past psychiatric diagnoses, as attested by the SRQ-20, and will be included solely to compare inflammatory biomarker profiles. All participants, including patients and HCs, will be included only after providing written informed consent, in accordance with ethical guidelines.

Eligibility assessment

Eligibility assessment will include an initial clinical screening to confirm the diagnosis of TRD based on DSM-5 criteria, using the MINI for diagnostic confirmation. Participants will attend an initial visit to assess clinical eligibility and review the inclusion and exclusion criteria, during which a blood sample will be collected to determine whether CRP levels≥3 mg/L indicate low-grade systemic inflammation. HC participants will be screened using the SRQ-20 to rule out the presence of mental disorders. Participants who meet all inclusion criteria and present no exclusion criteria will be invited to proceed to the intervention phase.

Baseline assessment

Eligible participants will undergo a baseline assessment comprising psychiatric rating scales and cognitive testing, as previously described, as well as blood sampling to measure IL-6 and CRP levels. This baseline visit will also include collecting clinical and laboratory data required to ensure the safety of the infusion. Participants who met the eligibility criteria during the screening stage will be invited to a second pre-infusion visit, during which additional safety procedures will be performed, including a chest X-ray for tuberculosis screening and blood tests to exclude pregnancy (for women of childbearing potential) and active infections such as hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV. Both participants randomised to the tocilizumab arm and those assigned to the placebo group will follow the same baseline protocol. Data collected during this phase will serve as a reference point for longitudinal comparisons. HC participants will also attend a baseline visit, which will include a single blood draw for IL-6 and CRP measurements; this will be their only study contact, and the data will be used to compare biomarkers with the patient group. For a detailed overview of assessments conducted at each stage of the study, see table 2.

Intervention

Participants will receive a single intravenous infusion of either tocilizumab or normal saline. The placebo group will receive an identical volume of 0.9% sodium chloride solution under the same administration conditions, ensuring the triple-blind design of the trial. Infusions will be conducted over 1 hour at a hospital facility, under the supervision of trained clinical staff. Participants will remain under continuous clinical observation throughout the infusion and for an additional hour following its completion.

Follow-up assessments

Follow-up assessments will take place at approximately 7, 14 and 28 days after the infusion and will include measures like those collected at baseline. Participants will undergo psychiatric evaluations using the HDRS, the CGI-S and the BPRS. Cognitive function will be reassessed using the MoCA, and quality of life will be evaluated using the WHOQOL-BREF and EUROHIS-QOL 8-item instruments. Blood samples will be collected at each visit to measure levels of inflammatory and neurotrophic biomarkers (IL-6, CRP and BDNF) and to perform additional safety blood tests, including complete blood count and liver function tests. A final follow-up assessment will be conducted approximately 6 months post-infusion, during which psychiatric and cognitive evaluations will be repeated, and a blood sample will be collected.

Risk management

Depression-related risks

All participants will be adults diagnosed with TRD who are followed at outpatient psychiatric services in Porto Alegre. If any participant shows signs of emotional distress or clinical decompensation at any stage of the study, the medical team will be immediately contacted to provide the most appropriate clinical approach, including referral for emergency care if necessary. This will not affect the participant’s continued participation or ethical withdrawal from the study, as appropriate. The protocol was designed not to interfere with standard clinical care, ensuring that previously established therapeutic measures will be maintained.

Procedure-related risks

Venepuncture

Blood collection is associated with mild discomfort and bruising, though serious side effects are rare. Efforts will be made to minimise risk and discomfort. Blood taking will be performed by a nurse, doctor or research team member trained in venepuncture.

Chest X-ray

Participants in the intervention group will undergo a chest X-ray for tuberculosis screening to ensure safety before tocilizumab administration. The radiation dose from the X-ray is low and equivalent to 3–10 days of natural background radiation exposure. Only adult, non-pregnant participants will be included.

CRP levels

CRP will be measured before and after the infusion to help analyse the inflammatory response. Having a serum CRP level above 3 mg/L is not necessarily concerning. In the USA, approximately 30% of the general population has serum CRP levels between 3.0 and 9.9 mg/L, and about 10–15% have levels above 10 mg/L. Elevated CRP may result from infection, obesity, smoking or sedentary behaviour; thus, awareness of CRP levels may encourage participants to adopt a healthier lifestyle. These findings will be disclosed to participants for clinical reference only and will not affect their inclusion in the study. However, any participant with a CRP level>20 mg/L without a clear clinical explanation will be excluded from the trial.

Risks of drug interactions

Tocilizumab may interfere with the expression of cytochrome P450 enzymes. Drugs metabolised by these enzymes—such as warfarin, phenytoin, benzodiazepines, cyclosporine and calcium channel blockers—may have altered efficacy. All concomitant medications will be assessed before inclusion, and patients and their physicians will be informed of the possibility of dose adjustments.

Risks of breach of confidentiality

All information collected from participants will be handled with the utmost confidentiality, using coded identification and password-protected databases accessible only to authorised staff. Despite these security measures, there is a minimal risk of breach of confidentiality inherent to any research involving personal and clinical data. This risk will be minimised through staff training on best practices in data privacy and research ethics, the use of anonymised forms and strict access control. The informed consent form will clearly outline these precautions and explain the ethical and confidential handling of participants’ data. At no point will personal data be disclosed in reports, publications or scientific presentations.

Risks to the research team

All procedures will follow current biosafety guidelines. If travel or contact outside the hospital is necessary, visits will preferably be conducted in pairs.

Safety considerations for infusion and monitoring of adverse reactions

Before infusion

Participants will be screened according to strict inclusion and exclusion criteria to ensure that only eligible individuals receive the intervention. Laboratory tests will be performed to screen for infections such as tuberculosis, hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV, as tocilizumab infusion—although unlikely to cause complications after a single dose—could exacerbate these conditions. Female participants of childbearing potential will undergo a pregnancy test, and those who test positive will be excluded from the study. All participants will be advised to use effective contraception for at least 6 weeks after the infusion, due to the immunomodulatory effects of the medication.

During infusion

Tocilizumab or placebo infusion will be administered in a hospital setting under continuous physician supervision. Vital signs will be monitored at regular intervals throughout the procedure, and any adverse reactions will be documented. In the event of a serious adverse event, the infusion will be immediately discontinued, and the participant will receive appropriate medical care. All adverse events will be systematically recorded and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAEs), and serious adverse events will be reported in accordance with institutional and regulatory requirements. Appropriate clinical management will be provided.

After infusion

Following the infusion, participants will remain under clinical observation for a minimum of 1 hour to monitor for any early adverse reactions. During this period, clinical parameters will be continuously assessed to ensure participant safety. Participants will be instructed to report any unusual symptoms immediately, such as fever, excessive fatigue or signs of an allergic reaction. If necessary, the study team may unblind the allocation to guide appropriate clinical decisions and safeguard the participant’s well-being. The expected elimination time for tocilizumab from the body is approximately 42 days after a single infusion.60

Ethics and dissemination

The study has been reviewed and approved by the Research Ethics Committee (Comitê de Ética em Pesquisa, CEP) of the HCPA (CAE: 88904825.7.0000.5327). All procedures will be conducted in accordance with Brazilian regulatory guidelines, including Resolutions No. 466/2012 and 510/2016 of the National Health Council, the Declaration of Helsinki and the Brazilian General Data Protection Law (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados—LGPD, Law No. 13.709/2018). Any amendments to the study protocol will be submitted for ethics approval before implementation, and updates will be communicated to the appropriate trial registry and regulatory authorities.

Consent

Informed consent will be obtained from all participants before any study-related procedures (see the participant consent form in the online supplemental material). Participants will be fully informed about the objectives, risks and benefits of the trial. Consent will also be obtained to access electronic medical records and, when needed, to contact healthcare teams to verify psychiatric treatment history. Participants may also choose to authorise the storage of biological samples for future research.

Study management

The research team will include specialists in psychiatry and rheumatology, who will oversee participant care and data quality control. Regular team meetings will be held to monitor participant safety and study progress. As the intervention involves a medication already approved for clinical use, an independent data monitoring committee is not required. However, a study management group has been established to enhance oversight, consisting of clinical and academic experts in psychiatry, immunology, neuroscience and rheumatology.

Data management and retention of samples

All participants will be assigned a unique study ID to ensure data anonymisation. Data will be securely stored in password-protected systems (REDCap), with access restricted to authorised members of the research team. Participants may request deletion of their data or withdrawal of their samples at any time. Biological samples collected may be stored for future research for up to 10 years, labelled only with coded identifiers to maintain confidentiality throughout storage and analysis. Participants may withdraw their consent for their samples to be stored for future research.

Dissemination plan

The findings of this study will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed scientific journals and presented at relevant academic conferences and professional meetings. All publications will follow the guidelines of the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE). Participants will be offered a lay summary of the study results on request.
