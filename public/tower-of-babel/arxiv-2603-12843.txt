The geometry of Stein’s method of moments: A canonical decomposition via score matching
Mitsuki Nagai, Keisuke Yano

Abstract
In this paper, we elucidate the geometry of Stein’s method of moments (SMoM). SMoM is a parameter estimation method based on the Stein operator, and yields a wide class of estimators that do not depend on the normalizing constant. We present a canonical decomposition of an SMoM estimator after centering the score matching estimator, which sheds light on the central role of the score matching within the SMoM framework. Using this decomposition, we construct an SMoM estimator that improves upon the score matching estimator in the asymptotic variance. We also discuss the connection between SMoM and the Wasserstein geometry. Specifically, using the Wasserstein score function, we provide a geometrical interpretation of the gap in the asymptotic variance between the score matching estimator and the maximum likelihood estimator. Furthermore, it is shown that the score matching estimator is asymptotically efficient if and only if the Fisher score functions span the same space as the Wasserstein score functions.



1
Introduction

We consider estimation of statistical models whose probability density
q_{\theta}
has the form

q_{\theta}(x)=\frac{1}{Z(\theta)}\tilde{q}_{\theta}(x),

where
\tilde{q}_{\theta}
is a non-normalized density, and
Z(\theta)\coloneqq\int\tilde{q}_{\theta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
.
Such models are known as non-normalized models or energy-based models, and appear in various fields
(
21
;
33
, cf.)
.
Although flexible, non-normalized models often involve a computationally intractable normalizing constant
Z(\theta)
,
and thus the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) is computationally infeasible for non-normalized models.

15
introduced
score matching as an estimation method for non-normalized models.
Score matching estimates the parameter by minimizing the distance between the score function
\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta}
and the true one
\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
.
This procedure does not depend on the normalizing constant or the true score function
\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
since the minimization problem can be rewritten in a form that avoids
\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
using integration by parts.

Recently,
8
proposed
yet another approach for non-normalized models,
Stein’s method of moments (SMoM).
This approach utilizes a Stein operator
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
, an operator satisfying the identity
\E_{\theta}[\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f]=0
for a class of test functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
In this paper, we allow test functions
f_{\theta}
that may depend on
\theta
.
Since the Stein operator is not unique, we need to choose an appropriate one, and employ the divergence-based Stein operator
(
26
)
:

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta}\coloneqq\frac{\div(q_{\theta}f_{\theta})}{q_{\theta}},\quad f_{\theta}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}.

Under the boundary condition
\lim_{\lVert x\rVert\to\infty}q_{\theta}(x)f_{\theta}(x)=0
, we have
\E_{\theta}[\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta}]=0
.
This Stein operator does not depend on the normalizing constant, implying that SMoM yields a wide class of normalizing-constant-free estimators.

In this paper, we provide a geometric framework for analyzing SMoM on the basis of score matching.
We derive the asymptotic linear representation of SMoM estimators after centering the score matching estimator (Theorem
1
).
The representation reveals that, within the framework of SMoM, the relevant orthogonal structure arises from the space of test functions,
rather than from the space of estimating functions
(
5
, e.g.,)
.
Using the difference of orthogonality between these spaces, we construct an SMoM estimator that improves upon the score matching estimator in the asymptotic variance (Theorem
2
),
and show the condition for the asymptotic efficiency of the score matching estimator (Theorem
3
).

One intriguing aspect of our construction is its connection to the Wasserstein geometry
(
28
;
29
;
22
;
1
;
3
;
27
;
36
, e.g.,)
. In fact, our construction of the SMoM estimator improving the score matching estimator
admits a geometric interpretation as the approach to the space spanned by the Wasserstein score functions
(
22
)
.
Also, using the Wasserstein score function, we show that the gap in the asymptotic variance between the score matching estimator and the MLE also has a geometrical interpretation.
Furthermore, we show that the score matching estimator is asymptotically efficient if and only if the Fisher score functions span the same space as the Wasserstein score functions.

1.1
Literature review and contributions

There is an extensive literature on the estimation for non-normalized models, beginning with Hyvärinen’s pioneering work
(
15
)
.
Regarding the denoising autoencoder as a non-normalized model,

40
proposed denoising score matching.
To improve computational efficiency on score matching,
32

The framework of scoring rules is extended to cover score matching
(
30
;
17
;
35
)
.

34
proposed density estimation using infinite dimensional exponential families with score matching.

14
introduced yet another estimation method called noise contrastive estimation.

13
provided a unified framework based on the Bregman divergence that encompasses the score matching and noise contrastive estimation.

25
derived model selection criteria for non-normalized models.
In settings with missing data,
38
combined imputation techniques with estimators for non-normalized models, and
12
utilized marginal score functions.

37
developed asymptotically efficient estimators that do not depend on the normalizing constant by employing density-ratio matching with a nonparametric density estimator.

Despite the broad literature, the statistical efficiency of the original score matching has not been well understood.
Using the isoperimetric constant,

19
analyzed
the condition under which the score matching estimator is comparable to the maximum likelihood estimator.
Our work provides a further characterization within the SMoM framework, and constructs an estimator that improves upon the score matching when the latter is not asymptotically efficient.

Non-normalized models also naturally arise on more general domains, such as truncated domains in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
(e.g., the non-negative orthant
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{p}
) or Riemannian manifolds (e.g., the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{p-1}
).
Score matching has been generalized to general domains by changing the metric
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
to a weighted metric
w\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle

(
16
;
23
, e.g.,)
, or replacing differential operators with their manifold counterparts
(

;

;
41
, e.g.,)
.
SMoM also has been generalized in the same way
(
10
;
11
, c.f.,)
.
In line with these generalizations, our results are extended to these settings;
see
Appendix C
.
For simplicity, we primarily focus on non-normalized models on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
in the main text.

Our analysis is motivated by the fact that
the score matching estimator corresponds to the SMoM estimator based on the test functions
f_{\theta,j}\coloneqq\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta}
; see Lemma
1
.
This lemma has been previously mentioned by
8
,
9
, and
20
; it stands as a clue to investigate the relationship between score matching and the Stein operator, which has delivered various estimators.

extended Lemma

Closely related to the present paper is

together with the generalized method of moments (GMM) framework is employed to construct an estimator that improves upon score matching
in exponential families. We emphasize that our geometric construction delivers a different perspective on score matching in general statistical models, and reveals an unexpected connection between score matching and the Wasserstein geometry.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we prepare notations and review the score matching and the SMoM.

The standard inner product and the induced norm on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
is denoted by
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
and
\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert
, respectively.
The gradient operator on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
is denoted by
\nabla_{x}
,
the divergence of a vector field
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is defined as
\div{f}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{p}\partial f_{k}/\partial x_{k}
,
and the Laplacian of a function
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as
\Delta_{x}g\coloneqq\div(\nabla_{x}g)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\partial^{2}{g}/\partial{x_{k}^{2}}
.
Let
\partial_{\theta_{j}}
be the partial differential operator
\partial/\partial\theta_{j}
.

Let the parameter space
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set, and let
q_{\theta}
be a probability density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
q_{\theta}>0
almost everywhere.
We assume that the model
\{q_{\theta}\mid\theta\in\Theta\}
is identifiable, that is,
q_{\theta}=q_{\theta^{\prime}}
if and only if
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
.
For an estimator
\hat{\theta}
that is asymptotically normal, i.e.
\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-\theta^{\star})\rightsquigarrow N(0,V)
, we define its asymptotic variance as
\AVar[\hat{\theta}]\coloneqq V
.
The Fisher information matrix
I(\theta^{\star})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is defined by
I(\theta^{\star})_{jk}\coloneqq\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})(\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})]
.
We assume that
q_{\theta}
and test functions
f_{\theta,j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
for
j=1,\dots,d
are smooth with respect to both
\theta
and
x
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. random variables from
q_{\theta^{\star}}
.
The score matching estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}
is given as a solution to the estimating equations:

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{\theta_{j}}H(X_{i},\theta)=0,\quad j=1,\dots,d,

where the Hyvärinen score
H
is defined by

H(x,\theta)\coloneqq\lVert\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta}(x)\rVert^{2}/2+\Delta_{x}\log q_{\theta}(x).

Under the boundary condition
\lim_{\lVert x\rVert\to\infty}q_{\theta^{\star}}(x)\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta}(x)=0
, using integration by parts, we have the relation

\E_{\theta^{\star}}[H(X,\theta)]=\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\lVert\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}-\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta}\rVert^{2}]/2+c,

where
X\sim q_{\theta^{\star}}
and
c
is a constant with respect to
\theta
.
Thus, minimizing
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[H(X,\theta)]
over
\Theta
is equivalent to minimizing
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\lVert\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}-\nabla_{x}\log q_{\theta}\rVert^{2}]
.

The SMoM estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
based on the test functions
f_{\theta,1},\dots,f_{\theta,d}
is given as a solution to the estimating equations:

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta,j}(X_{i})=0,\quad j=1,\dots,d.

The following lemma states that the score matching estimator is an SMoM estimator.

Lemma 1

(Section 2.1 of
9
; Lemma 3.2 of
20
)
.

The SMoM estimator based on the test functions
f_{\theta,j}\coloneqq\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta},\kern 5.0ptj=1,\dots,d
is the score matching estimator.

Proof.

Since
f_{\theta,j}=\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta}
is a solution of the equation
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta,j}(x)=\partial_{\theta_{j}}H(x,\theta)
,
the estimating equations of score matching coincide with that of SMoM based on the test functions.
∎

Remark 1

(Test functions and gradient vector fields)
.

By the Helmholtz decomposition
(
3
)
, a test function
f_{\theta}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
can be decomposed a gradient term and a divergence-free term; there exist
h_{\theta}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g_{\theta}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}g_{\theta}=0\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\theta}=\nabla h_{\theta}+g_{\theta}.

However, we do not restrict test functions to gradients because modeling vector fields directly is computationally efficient.

3
Main results

This section presents our main results.
To state the results rigorously, we impose regularity conditions for the model and test functions detailed in
Appendix A
.
Particularly, we assume the consistency and asymptotic linearity for SMoM estimators, and they are verified by the standard theory of
Z
-estimators
(
39
, e.g.,)
.
Under these conditions and Lemma
1
, the score matching estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}
and the SMoM estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
based on the test functions
f_{\theta,1},\dots,f_{\theta,d}
have the following asymptotic linear representation:

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}-\theta^{\star}

\displaystyle=-G^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{1}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{d}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}+o_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right),

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}-\theta^{\star}

\displaystyle=-G_{\mathrm{SMoM}}^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{\theta^{\star},1}(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{\theta^{\star},d}(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}+o_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right),

(2)

respectively,
where
G,G_{\mathrm{SMoM}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
are the matrices whose
(j,k)
-th entries are defined by

G_{jk}\coloneqq\E_{\theta^{\star}}\Big[\partial_{\theta_{k}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta})\Big|_{\theta=\theta^{\star}}\Big],\quad(G_{\mathrm{SMoM}})_{jk}\coloneqq\E_{\theta^{\star}}\Big[\partial_{\theta_{k}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta,j}\Big|_{\theta=\theta^{\star}}\Big],

respectively.

3.1
A canonical decomposition of SMoM estimators

We begin with presenting a canonical decompotision of SMoM estimators (Theorem
1
).
Before presenting the main theorem, we introduce two notions of orthogonality:
W
-orthogonality and
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonality. The acronym
W
refers to Wasserstein; see Section
4
for details.

Definition 1
.

A test function
f
is called
W
-orthogonal to a test function
g
if we have

\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle f,g\rangle]=0.

A test function
f
is called
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonal to a test function
g
if we have

\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f)(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}g)]=0.

The following theorem
provides the asymptotic linear representation of the SMoM estimator after centering the score matching estimator.

Theorem 1

(Canonical decomposition of SMoM estimator)
.

Under the regularity conditions, there exist
u_{j}^{\star}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}\,(j=1,\dots,d)
such that the asymptotic linear representation of
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
can be decomposed as follows:

\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}-\theta^{\star}=\Big(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}-\theta^{\star}\Big)-G^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{1}^{\star}(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{d}^{\star}(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}+o_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right),

where each
u_{j}^{\star}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is
W
-orthogonal to the test function corresponding to score matching:

\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle u_{j}^{\star},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle]=0,\quad k=1,\dots,d.

Through this canonical decomposition, the asymptotic linear representation of SMoM estimators is characterized only by its
W
-orthogonal term
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{k}^{\star}
.
The score matching estimator is the SMoM estimator without
W
-orthogonal terms.

Remark 2

(
W
-orthogonality does not imply
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonality)
.

Importantly, the Stein operator does not preserve orthogonality; that is,
even if
u_{k}^{\star}
is
W
-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
,
the
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonal condition
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{k}^{\star}]=0
does not necessarily hold.
This give a clue to constructing an SMoM estimator that improves upon the score matching estimator;
by finding appropriate elements that are
W
-orthogonal to the test functions corresponding to the score matching,
we can reduce the asymptotic variance.
In Section
3.2
, we show how to construct such orthogonal elements.

To prove Theorem
1
,
we need the following lemma telling us that
G_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
is an inner product matrix, and in particular,
G
is a Gram matrix.

Lemma 2
.

Under the regularity conditions, for the test functions
f_{\theta,1},\dots,f_{\theta,d}
, we have

\E_{\theta^{\star}}\Big[\partial_{\theta_{k}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta,j}\Big|_{\theta=\theta^{\star}}\Big]=\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle f_{\theta^{\star},j},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle],\quad j,k=1,\dots,d.

In particular, we have
G_{jk}=\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle]
.

Proof.

Observe the relation

\partial_{\theta_{k}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta,j}=\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\partial_{\theta_{k}}f_{\theta,j})+\langle f_{\theta,j},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta}\rangle.

Together with
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\partial_{\theta_{k}}f_{\theta^{\star},j})]=0
under the regularity conditions,
taking the expectation of this yields
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\partial_{\theta_{k}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta,j}|_{\theta=\theta^{\star}}]=\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle f_{\theta^{\star},j},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle]
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark that this representation of
G
gives the Riemannian metric induced by the score matching
(
18
)
.

Now, we prove Theorem
1
by combining
the asymptotic linear representations (
1
)-(
2
) and Lemma
2
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Fix
f_{\theta,1},\ldots,f_{\theta,d}
arbitrarily.
We begin with the
W
-orthogonal decomposition:

f_{\theta^{\star},j}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{jk}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}+u_{k}^{\star}),

(3)

where
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a coefficient matrix and
u_{k}^{\star}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
are
W
-orthogonal elements such that
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle u_{k}^{\star},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle]=0
for
j=1,\dots,d
.
This decomposition, together with Lemma
2
, gives the relation
G_{\mathrm{SMoM}}=BG
.
Importantly, the coefficient matrix
B
must be invertible for
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
to admit the asymptotic linear representation.

We next substitute the relation
G_{\mathrm{SMoM}}=BG
to Equation (
2
).
Then,
we have

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}-\theta^{\star}

\displaystyle=-(BG)^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}B\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{1}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}+u_{1}^{\star})(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{d}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}+u_{d}^{\star})(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}+o_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right).

From the linearity of
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
,
the invertibility of
B
,
and Equation (
1
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}-\theta^{\star}

\displaystyle=-G^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{1}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{d}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}-G^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{1}^{\star}(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{d}^{\star}(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}+o_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\Big(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}-\theta^{\star}\Big)-G^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{1}^{\star}(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{d}^{\star}(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}+o_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

Example 1

(Exponential families)
.

Consider an exponential family whose density is defined by

q_{\theta}(x)=\frac{1}{Z(\theta)}\exp\Big(t(x)^{\top}\theta+b(x)\Big),\kern 5.0ptZ(\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\exp\Big(t(x)^{\top}\theta+b(x)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}{x},

(4)

where
t\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a sufficient statistics and
b\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is a base measure.

The SMoM estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
based on (parameter-independent) test functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
has the closed-form solution:

\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}=-G_{\mathrm{SMoM},n}^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\div f_{1}(X_{i})+\langle f_{1}(X_{i}),\nabla_{x}b(X_{i})\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\div f_{d}(X_{i})+\langle f_{d}(X_{i}),\nabla_{x}b(X_{i})\rangle\end{pmatrix},

where
G_{\mathrm{SMoM},n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the empirical inner product matrix whose
(j,k)
-th entry is defined by
(G_{\mathrm{SMoM},n})_{jk}\coloneqq n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle f_{j}(X_{i}),\nabla_{x}t_{k}(X_{i})\rangle
.
Observe that the relation

E_{\theta^{\star}}[\div{f_{j}}+\langle f_{j},\nabla_{x}b\rangle]=\E_{\theta^{\star}}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle f_{j},\nabla_{x}t_{k}\rangle\theta^{\star}_{k}\right]=-G_{\mathrm{SMoM}}\theta^{\star}.

Then, if
G_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
is invertible,
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
is consistent and has asymptotic normality under some conditions on integrability.
The linear independence of
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{d}
follows from Lemma
3
.

The score matching estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}
is the SMoM estimator based on the test functions
f_{j}=\nabla_{x}t_{j}
, and also has the closed form
(
16
)
:

\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}=-G_{n}^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{x}t_{1}(X_{i})+\langle\nabla_{x}t_{1}(X_{i}),\nabla_{x}b(X_{i})\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\Delta_{x}t_{d}(X_{i})+\langle\nabla_{x}t_{d}(X_{i}),\nabla_{x}b(X_{i})\rangle\end{pmatrix},

where
G_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the empirical inner product matrix whose
(j,k)
-th entry is defined by
(G_{n})_{jk}\coloneqq n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{x}t_{j}(X_{i}),\nabla_{x}t_{k}(X_{i})\rangle
.
Consider the test functions
f_{j}=\nabla_{x}t_{j}+u_{j}^{\star}
, where
u_{j}^{\star}
is
W
-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}t_{k}
for
k=1,\dots,d
.
The SMoM estimator based on this test functions can be decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}-\theta^{\star}

\displaystyle=-\big(G_{n}+(G_{\mathrm{SMoM},n}-G_{n})\big)^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}t_{1})(X_{i})+\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{1}^{\star}(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}t_{d})(X_{i})+\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{d}^{\star}(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\Big(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}-\theta^{\star}\Big)-G^{-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{1}^{\star}(X_{i})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{d}^{\star}(X_{i})\end{pmatrix}+o_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right),

which agrees with Theorem
1
.

3.2
Improving asymptotic variance of score matching estimator

We next construct an SMoM estimator improving upon the score matching estimator in the asymptotic variance.
Fix
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
and
\tilde{v}_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p},\kern 5.0pt\alpha=1,\dots,K
arbitrarily.
Using the test function
\tilde{v}_{\alpha}
, we define
v_{\theta_{0},\alpha}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
by the following orthogonalization procedure:

v_{\theta_{0},\alpha}\coloneqq\tilde{v}_{\alpha}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(F_{\theta_{0}}G_{\theta_{0}}^{-1}\right)_{\alpha j}\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta_{0}},

(5)

where
F_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
and
G_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
are inner product matrices whose
(\alpha,j)
-th entry and
(j,k)
-th entry are defined by

\displaystyle(F_{\theta_{0}})_{\alpha j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\E_{\theta_{0}}\left[\langle\tilde{v}_{\alpha},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta_{0}}\rangle\right],

\displaystyle(G_{\theta_{0}})_{jk}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\E_{\theta_{0}}\left[\langle\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta_{0}},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta_{0}}\rangle\right],

respectively.
Observe the relation

\E_{\theta_{0}}[\langle v_{\theta_{0},\alpha},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta_{0}}\rangle]=0,\quad j=1,\dots,d.

Denote by
\hat{\theta}[\theta_{0}]
the SMoM estimator based on the following test functions:

f_{\theta,j}\coloneqq\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{K}\left(S_{\theta_{0}}T_{\theta_{0}}^{-1}\right)_{j\alpha}v_{\theta_{0},\alpha},\quad j=1,\dots,d,

(6)

where
S_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}
and
T_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}
are inner product matrices whose
(j,\alpha)
-th entry and
(\alpha,\beta)
-th entry are defined by

\displaystyle(S_{\theta_{0}})_{j\alpha}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\E_{\theta_{0}}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\theta_{0}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta_{0}})\mathcal{A}_{\theta_{0}}v_{\theta_{0},\alpha}\right],

\displaystyle(T_{\theta_{0}})_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\E_{\theta_{0}}\left[\left(\mathcal{A}_{\theta_{0}}v_{\theta_{0},\alpha}\right)\left(\mathcal{A}_{\theta_{0}}v_{\theta_{0},\beta}\right)\right],

respectively.
For the matrices
F_{\theta_{0}},G_{\theta_{0}},S_{\theta_{0}},T_{\theta_{0}}
, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 1
.

F_{\theta_{0}},G_{\theta_{0}},S_{\theta_{0}},T_{\theta_{0}}
are continuous at
\theta_{0}=\theta^{\star}
.

The following theorem tells us that
\hat{\theta}[\theta^{\star}]
improves the asymptotic variance of
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}
.
Furthermore, such improvement remains even when
\hat{\theta}_{0}
is plugged in to
\theta^{\star}
.
The proof is given in
Appendix C
.

Theorem 2
.

Under the regularity conditions, we have

\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}\right]-\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}[\theta^{\star}]\right]=G^{-1}S_{\theta^{\star}}T_{\theta^{\star}}^{-1}S_{\theta^{\star}}^{\top}G^{-1}\succeq 0.

The equality
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}]=\AVar[\hat{\theta}[\theta^{\star}]]
holds if and only if
S_{\theta^{\star}}=O
with a zero matrix
O
.
Furthermore, under Assumption
1
,
for an estimator
\hat{\theta}_{0}
satisfying
\hat{\theta}_{0}-\theta^{\star}=O_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right)
, we have

\hat{\theta}[\hat{\theta}_{0}]-\theta^{\star}=\hat{\theta}[\theta^{\star}]-\theta^{\star}+o_{\mathrm{p}}\left(n^{-1/2}\right).

Figure
1
illustrates the improvement procedure.
Consider the SMoM estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
based on the following test functions:

f_{\theta,j}\coloneqq\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{K}C_{j\alpha}v_{\theta^{\star},\alpha},\quad j=1,\dots,d,

(7)

where
C\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}
is a coefficient matrix.
By Theorem
1
, the asymptotic variance
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}]
can be decomposed as follows:

\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}\right]=\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}\right]+G^{-1}C(T_{\theta^{\star}}+S_{\theta^{\star}}+S_{\theta^{\star}}^{\top})C^{\top}G^{-1},

and it is minimized at
C=-S_{\theta^{\star}}T_{\theta^{\star}}^{-1}
.
In Figure

, the score matching estimator, which is the SMoM estimator without
W
-orthogonal terms, is located at
c_{1}=c_{2}=0
.
When
K=1
, i.e.
c_{2}=0
,
since
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}]
is a quadratic function of
c_{1}
,
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}
can be moved in the descent direction of
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}]
starting from
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}
, resulting in
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}]
being minimized at
c_{1}=c_{1}^{\star}
.
When
K=2
,
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}]
is further improved by additional direction
v_{2}^{\star}
, and
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}]
is minimized at
(c_{1},c_{2})=c^{\star}
.

Let
U_{\theta_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

(j,k)

(U_{\theta_{0}})_{jk}\coloneqq\E_{\theta_{0}}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\theta_{0}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta_{0}})\mathcal{A}_{\theta_{0}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta_{0}})\right].

.
As the estimate of asymptotic relative efficiency
\AVar[\hat{\theta}[\hat{\theta}_{0}]]_{jj}/\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}]_{jj}

(8)

and this quantity can be calculated using the Monte Carlo approximation.

Remark 3

(The choice of
\hat{\theta}_{0},\tilde{v}_{\alpha}

)
.

A reasonable choice of

are the score matching estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}
and neural networks with random weight, which yields flexible and easily differentiable functions.
Also, theoretically, increasing

.
In Section

4
Connections between SMoM and the Wasserstein geometry

In this section, we relate SMoM to the Wasserstein geometry.
This unexpected connection presents a further characterization of the SMoM and the score matching.

We first introduce the Wasserstein score function,
a central concept in the Wasserstein geometry.

Definition 2

(The Wasserstein score function (
22
))
.

The Wasserstein score function
\Phi_{\theta,j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is the solution of the following partial differential equation:

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\left(\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta,j}\right)=-\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta},\quad j=1,\dots,d

with
\E_{\theta}[\Phi_{\theta,j}]=0
.

The Wasserstein score function induces a second-order approximation of the 2-Wasserstein distance, and plays a key role in the Wasserstein geometry
(
22
;
27
, e.g.,)
.
Also,
W
-orthogonality corresponds to the orthogonality with respect to the Wasserstein covariance.

Within the SMoM framework,
the maximum likelihood estimator is
the SMoM estimator based on the test functions
f_{\theta,j}=-\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta,j}
.
Remark that
since the Wasserstein score function often does not has a closed form and depends on the normalizing constant,
this SMoM estimator (MLE) is computationally infeasible.

Yet, regarding MLE as the SMoM from the (minus) Wasserstein score function offers the following characterization of the score matching estimator: the score matching estimator cannot be improved if and only if the Fisher score functions span the same space as the Wasserstein score functions.
In this situation, the score matching estimator coincides with the MLE.
The proof is given in
Appendix B
.

Theorem 3
.

Assume the regularity conditions.
Then, the following are equivalent:

1.

the score matching estimator is asymptotically efficient;

2.

if
u^{\star}
is
W
-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
for
j=1,\dots,d
, then it is also
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
for
j=1,\dots,d
;

3.

there exists a matrix
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
for which we have

\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Lambda_{jk}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},k},\quad j=1,\dots,d.

Let us provide two examples in which the score matching estimator is asymptotically efficient.

Example 2

(Normal distribution)
.

For the normal distribution
N(\mu,\Sigma)
, the Wasserstein score functions are given as follows
(
1
)
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mu_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle=x_{j}-\mu_{j},

\displaystyle j=1,\dots,p,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\Sigma_{jk}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\trace(S_{jk}\Sigma)+\frac{1}{2}(x-\mu)^{\top}S_{jk}(x-\mu),

\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq k\leq p.

Here,
S_{jk}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
is the symmetric matrix defined by the unique solution of the Sylvester equation

S_{jk}\Sigma+\Sigma S_{jk}=\begin{cases}E_{jj}&j=k\\
E_{jk}+E_{kj}&j\neq k\end{cases},

where
E_{jk}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
is the matrix whose
(j,k)
-th entry is 1 and all other entries are 0.
The Fisher score functions are givens as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu_{j}}\log q_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\Sigma^{-1})_{jk}(x_{k}-\mu_{k}),

\displaystyle\partial_{\Sigma_{jk}}\log q_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=-\trace S_{jk}+\frac{1}{2}(x-\mu)^{\top}(S_{jk}\Sigma^{-1}+\Sigma^{-1}S_{jk})(x-\mu).

By the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the Sylvester equation, there exist unique constants
c_{lm}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
S_{jk}\Sigma^{-1}+\Sigma^{-1}S_{jk}=\sum_{l\leq m}c_{lm}S_{lm}
. In addition, we have

\sum_{l\leq m}c_{lm}\trace(S_{lm}\Sigma)=\trace\big((S_{jk}\Sigma^{-1}+\Sigma^{-1}S_{jk})\Sigma\big)=2\trace S_{jk}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu_{j}}\log q_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\Sigma^{-1})_{jk}\Phi_{\mu_{k}},

\displaystyle\partial_{\Sigma_{jk}}\log q_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l\leq m}c_{lm}\Phi_{\Sigma_{lm}},

which, together with Theorem
3
, concludes that the score matching is asymptotically efficient; in this example, the score matching coincides with MLE and thus is efficient
(
15
)
.

Example 3

(Generalized gamma-type distribution)
.

Consider the model on
\mathbb{R}
whose density is defined by

q_{\theta}(x)=\frac{\theta^{\beta+1/2}}{\Gamma(\beta+1/2)}x^{2\beta}e^{-x^{2}\theta},

where
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
is a fixed shape parameter and
\theta>0
is a scale parameter.
Since the Fisher score function is given by
\partial_{\theta}\log q_{\theta}(x)=-x^{2}+(2\beta+1)/(2\theta)
, we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta}\log q_{\theta})=-4\theta\partial_{\theta}\log q_{\theta}.

Thus, the Wasserstein score function is given by
\Phi_{\theta}(x)=-x^{2}/(4\theta)+(2\beta+1)/(8\theta^{2})
,
which, together with Theorem
3
, concludes that the score matching is asymptotically efficient. This gives a counterexample to the latter part of Theorem 13.5 in
2
.

Remark 4

(Iterative subspace construction)
.

Our Theorem
2
shows that the score matching is improved upon by incorporating appropriate
W
-orthogonal elements.
If these
W
-orthogonal elements corresponds to the Wasserstein score functions, the resulting SMoM estimator attains the Fisher efficiency.
Even if we cannot identify such elements, the asymptotic variance approaches the efficiency bound as we incorporate more orthogonal elements.
As illustrated in Figure
1
, this procedure is interpreted as the iterative orthonormal subspace expansion of test functions to cover the Wasserstein score functions.

To prove Theorem
3
, we need the two lemmas.
The following lemma connects two inner products related to
W
- and
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonality.
The proof is given in
Appendix B
.

Lemma 3
.

Under the regularity conditions, for
f_{\theta}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we have

\E_{\theta^{\star}}\left[\langle f_{\theta^{\star}},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle\right]=\E_{\theta^{\star}}\left[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{\theta^{\star}})(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},j}))\right],\quad j=1,\dots,d.

In particular,
for
j=1,\dots,d
,
if the test function
u^{\star}
is
W
-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
,
then it is
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},j}
.

The following lemma shows that the gap between
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}]
and
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}]
is characterized by the
W
-orthogonal elements
u_{k}^{\star}
.
The proof is given in
Appendix B
.

Lemma 4
.

Under the regularity conditions, we have

\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}\right]-\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}\right]=G^{-1}\E_{\theta^{\star}}\left[\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{1}^{\star}\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{d}^{\star}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{1}^{\star}\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{d}^{\star}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\right]G^{-1},

where
u_{j}^{\star}
is the
W
-orthogonal element of
\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},j}
given by the
W
-orthogonal decomposition (
3
).

Lemma
4
tells us that if the
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonal terms
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{j}^{\star}
is dominant in
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},j})
, the asymptotic variance
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}]_{jj}
is much worse than
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}]_{jj}
.
Conversely, if
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{j}^{\star}
is negligible compared to
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})
, we have
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}]_{jj}\approx\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}]_{jj}
.

Example 4

(Generalized normal distribution)
.

Consider the special case of the generalized normal distribution whose density is defined by

q_{\theta}(x)=\frac{\beta\theta^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}}{\Gamma(1/(2\beta))}\exp\left(-\theta x^{2\beta}\right),

(9)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
is a fixed shape parameter, and
\theta>0
is the scale parameter.
If
\beta=1
, the distribution is
N(0,1/(2\theta))
.
Both of the score matching estimator and maximum likelihood estimator have the closed-form solutions:

\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}=\frac{n}{2\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2\beta}},\quad\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}=\frac{2\beta-1}{2\beta}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2\beta-2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{4\beta-2}}.

They coincide only in case
\beta=1
.
The SMoM estimator based on the test function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
also has the closed form:

\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SMoM}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}f(X_{i})}{2\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2\beta-1}f(X_{i})}.

The MLE corresponds to the case of
f(x)=x
, and the score matching estimator corresponds to the case of
f(x)=-2\beta x^{2\beta-1}
.
The Wasserstein score function
\Phi_{\theta}
has the explicit form:

\Phi_{\theta}(x)=-\frac{1}{4\beta\theta}x^{2}+\frac{\Gamma(3/(2\beta))}{4\beta\theta^{1+1/\beta}\Gamma(1/(2\beta))}.

Observe that
\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta}
is orthogonally decomposed as follows:

\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta}=-\frac{\Gamma(1/(2\beta))}{4\beta^{2}(2\beta-1)\theta^{1/\beta}\Gamma(1-1/(2\beta)}\left(-2\beta x^{2\beta-1}+u_{\theta}\right),

where
u_{\theta}
is defined by

u_{\theta}(x)\coloneqq-\frac{2\beta(2\beta-1)\theta^{-1+1/\beta}\Gamma(1-1/(2\beta))}{\Gamma(1/(2\beta))}x+2\beta x^{2\beta-1}

and satisfies
\E_{\theta}[\langle-2\beta X^{2\beta-1},u_{\theta}\rangle]=0
.
Combine it with Lemma
4
to get the asymptotic relative efficiency:

\frac{\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}\right]}{\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}\right]}=\frac{2\beta-1}{2(3\beta-2)}\frac{\Gamma(1-1/(2\beta))^{2}}{\Gamma(1+1/(2\beta))\Gamma(2-3/(2\beta))}\to\frac{1}{3}\quad(\beta\to\infty).

(10)

Figure
2
shows the behavior of (
10
) with respect to
\beta
.
It tells us that the gap in the asymptotic variance between the score matching estimator and the MLE widens as
\beta
increases.

5.2
Polynomially tilted pairwise interaction model

Consider the
(p-1)
-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{p-1}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\mid\lVert x\rVert=1\}
,
Let
\mathbb{S}^{p-1}_{+}
be its non-negative orthant, i.e.,
\mathbb{S}^{p-1}_{+}\coloneqq\mathbb{S}^{p-1}\cap[0,\infty)^{p}
.
The polynomially tilted pairwise interaction (PPI) model
(
31
)
has the following density:

q_{\theta}(x)=\frac{1}{Z(A,\mu,\beta)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p}x_{j}^{1+2\beta_{j}}\right)\exp\left(x^{2\top}Ax^{2}+\mu^{\top}x^{2}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{p-1}_{+},

where
\theta=(A,\mu)

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the geometry of SMoM estimators focusing on
W
-orthogonality and
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonality.

The canonical decomposition (Theorem
1
) gives the asymptotoic linear representation of SMoM estimators after centering the score matching estimator, and it implies that the asymptotic linear representation of SMoM estimators is characterized by its
W
-orthogonal term.
Using the fact that the
W
-orthogonality does not imply
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonality, we constructed a SMoM estimator which improves the asymptotic variance of the score matching estimator (Theorem
2
).
Through the numerical experiments, we comfirmed that the improvement is effective in finite sample settings under an appropriate choice of
K
.

The unexpected connection between the Wasserstein geometry and SMoM presents further characterization of the asymptotic efficiency of the score matching estimator.
We showed that the score matching estimator is asymptotically efficient if and only if the Fisher score functions span the same space as the Wasserstein score functions (Theorem
3
).
We also showed that the
W
-orthogonal term of the Wasserstein score function characterizes the gap of the asymptotic variance between the score matching estimator and the MLE (Lemma
4
).
The geometry of SMoM may provide further connection between the three geometries: the geometry of score matching, Fisher-Rao geometry, and Wasserstein geometry.

Proof of Lemma

By the Leibniz rule and the definition of the Wasserstein score function, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{\theta_{j}}\E_{\theta}[\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta}]|_{\theta=\theta^{\star}}

\displaystyle=\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta}|_{\theta=\theta^{\star}}]+\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{\theta^{\star}})(\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log{q}_{\theta^{\star}})]

\displaystyle=\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}f_{\theta}|_{\theta=\theta^{\star}}]-\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{\theta^{\star}})\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},j})],\quad j=1,\dots,d.

Combining this with the relation

\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle f_{\theta^{\star}},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle]=\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}f_{\theta^{\star}})(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},j})],

which completes the proof.

(11)

\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle u_{j}^{\star},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle]=0,\quad k=1,\dots,d.

\displaystyle\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}\right]-\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}\right]=G^{-1}TG^{-1},

(12)

(13)

For the proof of Theorem

, we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

For
h:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(h\nabla_{x}h)]=0
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}h)=0
if and only if the identity
\nabla_{x}h=0
holds with probability 1.

Proof.

If
\nabla_{x}h=0
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}h)=0
by the linearity of
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
.
Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}h)=0
. Observe the relation

\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(h\nabla_{x}h)=h\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}h)+\lVert\nabla_{x}h\rVert^{2}=\lVert\nabla_{x}h\rVert^{2}.

Taking the expectation of this yields

and thus implies

Proof of Theorem
3
.

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
:
Suppose the score matching estimator is asymptotically efficient:
\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}]=\AVar[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}]
.
By Lemma
4
, it implies
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{j}^{\star})^{2}]=0
, that is,
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{j}^{\star}=0
with probability 1, where
u_{j}^{\star}
is defined by (
11
).
Since there exists
h_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
two identities

u_{j}^{\star}=\nabla_{x}h_{j}
and
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[h_{j}\nabla_{x}h_{j}]=0
hold under the regularity conditions, we have
u_{j}^{\star}=0
with probability 1 by Lemma
5
.
Substituting
u_{j}^{\star}=0
to (
11
), we get

\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}(GF^{-1})_{jk}\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},k}.

Thus, there exists a constant
c_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}(GF^{-1})_{jk}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},k}+c_{j}.

Since
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\Phi_{\theta^{\star},j}]=0
and
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}]=0
, taking the expectation of this gives
c_{j}=0
,
which proves
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
:
Suppose there exists a matrix
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that

\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Lambda_{jk}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},k}.

Applying
h\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}h)
on both sides, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Lambda_{jk}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},k})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Lambda_{jk}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}},

which implies that the estimating equation of the score matching estimator coincides with the MLE. Thus,
the score matching estimator is asymptotically efficient, implying
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
.

(3)\Rightarrow(2)
:
Suppose there exists a matrix
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that

\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Lambda_{jk}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},k}.

Fix

that is

-orthogonal to

for

arbitrarily.

, we have

\displaystyle\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u^{\star})\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Lambda_{jk}\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u^{\star})\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},j})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Lambda_{jk}\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\langle u^{\star},\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}\rangle]

\displaystyle=0,

which shows

is
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
and completes

.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)

Suppose for any element

that is

-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}

\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}
-orthogonal to
\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}
for

.
Let
u_{j}^{\star}
be the

-orthogonal elements defined by (

).
By Lemma

, we have

\E_{\theta^{\star}}[(\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{j}^{\star})\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\Phi_{\theta^{\star},k})]=0,\quad k=1,\dots,d.

By Theorem
1
, we can decompose the asymptotic variance of the MLE:

\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}\right]=\AVar\left[\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{SM}}\right]+G^{-1}(T+S+S^{\top})G^{-1},

where
S\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
T\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
are the inner produce matrices whose
(j,k)
-entries are defined by

\displaystyle S_{jk}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}u_{j}^{\star}\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\star}}(\nabla_{x}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}})],

Appendix C.1
Preliminaries

We begin with preparing notations, review a generalization of score matching
(

)
, and introducing a Stein operator we employ in the Appendix.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an oriented and connected Riemannian manifold with corners, and let
\mathrm{d}x
be the volume form given by the Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
\partial\mathcal{M}
be the boundary of
\mathcal{M}
, and let
N
and
\mathrm{d}s
denote the unit outward normal vector field and volume form on
\partial\mathcal{M}
, respectively.
The Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{M}
and the induced norm on the tangent space
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
are denoted by
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
and
\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert
, respectively.

Let
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
be the space of smooth functions on
\mathcal{M}
, and let
\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
be the space of smooth vector fields on
\mathcal{M}
.
The gradient operator on
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}
, and the divergence of a vector field
f\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is denoted by
\divm{f}
.

Let the parameter space
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set, and let
q_{\theta}
be a probability density on
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
\mathrm{d}x
such that
q_{\theta}>0
almost everywhere.
We assume that the model
\{q_{\theta}\mid\theta\in\Theta\}
is identifiable; that is,
q_{\theta}=q_{\theta^{\prime}}
if and only if
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
.
We also assume that
q_{\theta}
and test functions
f_{\theta,j}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
for
j=1,\dots,d
are smooth with respect to both
\theta
and
x
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. random variables from
q_{\theta^{\star}}
.
Fix a weight function
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
that is strictly positive almost everywhere.
The case
w=1
and
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
corresponds to the main text.
The weighted score matching estimator
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{wSM}}
is given as a solution to the estimating equations:

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{\theta_{j}}H_{w}(X_{i},\theta)=0,\quad j=1,\dots,d,

where the weighted Hyvärinen score
H_{w}
is defined by
H_{w}(x,\theta)\coloneqq w(x)\lVert\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\log q_{\theta}(x)\rVert^{2}/2+\divm(w(x)\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\log q_{\theta}(x))
.
Under the condition
\int_{\mathcal{M}}\divm(wq_{\theta^{\star}}\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\log q_{\theta})\,\mathrm{d}x=0
, we have the relation

\E_{\theta^{\star}}[H_{w}(X,\theta)]=\E_{\theta^{\star}}[w\lVert\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}-\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\log q_{\theta}\rVert^{2}]/2+c,

where
X\sim q_{\theta^{\star}}
and
c
is a constant independent of
\theta
.
Thus, minimizing
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[H_{w}(X,\theta)]
over
\Theta
is equivalent to minimizing
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[w\lVert\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\log q_{\theta^{\star}}-\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\log q_{\theta}\rVert^{2}]
.

We employ the following weighted Stein operator:

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}^{w}f\coloneqq\frac{\divm(q_{\theta}wf)}{q_{\theta}},\quad f\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

For a test function
f
satisfying the condition
\int_{\mathcal{M}}\divm(q_{\theta^{\star}}wf)\,\mathrm{d}x=0
, the identity
\E_{\theta^{\star}}[\mathcal{A}^{w}_{\theta^{\star}}f]=0
holds.
The SMoM estimator based on the test functions
f_{\theta,1},\dots,f_{\theta,d}
is given as a solution to the estimating equations
n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{w}_{\theta}f_{\theta,j}(X_{i})=0
for
j=1,\dots,d
.

The following lemma states that the weighted score matching estimator is an SMoM estimator.

Lemma 6

(Lemma 3.2 of
20
)
.

The SMoM estimator based on the test functions
f_{\theta,j}\coloneqq\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta},\kern 5.0ptj=1,\dots,d
is the weighted score matching estimator.

Proof.

Since
f_{\theta,j}=\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\partial_{\theta_{j}}\log q_{\theta}
is a solution of the equation
\mathcal{A}^{w}_{\theta}f_{\theta,j}(x)=\partial_{\theta_{j}}H_{w}(x,\theta)
,
the estimating equations of weighted score matching coincide with that of SMoM based on the test functions.
∎

Remark 5

(The weight function
w
)
.

The weight function
w
assists in satisfying the boundary condition.
In
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the condition
\int_{\mathcal{M}}\divm(wq_{\theta^{\star}}f)\,\mathrm{d}x=0
is satisfied for
f\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
if we have
\lim_{\|x\|\to\infty}w(x)q_{\theta^{\star}}(x)f(x)=0
.
If
\mathcal{M}
is compact and
w|_{\partial\mathcal{M}}=0
, by Stokes’ theorem, we have

\int_{\mathcal{M}}\divm(wq_{\theta^{\star}}f)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}wq_{\theta^{\star}}\langle f,N\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s,

which automatically vanishes.
