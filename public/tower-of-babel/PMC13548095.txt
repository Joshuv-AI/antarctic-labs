Donor and stem cell source selection in hematopoietic cell transplantation in : the Asia-Pacific region: results from the APBMT Activity Survey 2022-2023
Authors: Minako Iida, Xiao Jun Huang, Meng Lv, Kaiyan Liu, Yachiyo Kuwatsuka, Yoon Seok Choi, Hee-Je Kim, Seong-Kyu Park, Sadhana Kannan, Bor-Sheng Ko, Chi-Cheng Li, Kim Wah Ho, Gin Gin Gan, Suradej Hongeng, Usanarat Anurathapan, Aloysius Ho, Michelle Poon, Tasneem Farzana, Shafaq Samad, Joycelyn Sim, Garret Leung Man Kit, Phu Chi Dung, Vo Thi Thanh Binh, Mafruha Akter, Lallindra Gooneratne, Marjorie Rose Bravo, Teresita Dumagay, Aye Aye Gyi, Damai Santosa, Desta Ardini, Bishesh Sharma Poudyal, Khishigjargal Batsukh, Mikhail Drokov, Takanori Teshima, Yoshiko Atsuta, Alok Srivastava, Navin Khattry, Registry Committee of the Asia-Pacific Blood and Marrow Transplantation Group (APBMT)
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground: Donor selection strategies for allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) have evolved with the increasing use of haploidentical transplantation. However, contemporary patterns of donor and stem cell source selection according to disease category in the Asia-Pacific (AP) region remain insufficiently characterized.Methods: Aggregated data from the APBMT Activity Survey 2022-2023 were analyzed to evaluate donor type and stem cell source distributions for allogeneic HCT. Donors were categorized as HLA-identical sibling, haploidentical (including other related donors), or unrelated donor. Stem cell sources were classified as bone marrow (BM), peripheral blood (PB), cord blood (CB), or mixed graft sources. Malignant and non-malignant diseases were compared, and disease-specific analyses were performed for acute myeloid leukemia, myelodysplastic syndrome/myeloproliferative neoplasms, severe aplastic anemia, and hemoglobinopathy. Sensitivity analyses excluding data from China were conducted.Results: Donor selection differed between malignant and non-malignant diseases, with haploidentical donors accounting for approximately 45%-60% of allogeneic HCTs, followed by unrelated donors (20%-30%) and HLA-identical sibling donors (15%-25%). Regarding stem cell source, PB was the predominant source across disease categories, accounting for approximately 60%-70% of allogeneic HCTs. BM accounted for approximately 5%-17% of transplants, with somewhat higher use in non-malignant diseases. CB accounted for approximately 10%-12% of transplants in malignant diseases and 3%-4% in non-malignant diseases. These patterns were consistent in disease-specific analyses; however, exclusion of China resulted in a relative decrease in haploidentical transplantation and an increase in unrelated donor use, particularly in malignant diseases, as well as a marked reduction in mixed graft sources with a corresponding increase in bone marrow use. Despite these shifts, the overall predominance of peripheral blood as the stem cell source and the disease-specific trends were maintained.Conclusions: Across AP region, donor and stem cell source selection for allogeneic HCT demonstrates modest but consistent disease-specific differences, reflecting adaptation of transplantation strategies to disease context in contemporary clinical practice.Keywords: Asia-Pacific region, hematopoietic cell transplantation, donor selection, stem cell sources

Introduction

Allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) is a potentially curative therapy for a wide range of malignant and non-malignant hematologic diseases1,2. Historically, HLA-identical sibling donors have been considered the optimal donor source; however, only a minority of patients have access to such donors. Over the past two decades, unrelated donor registries have expanded globally, enabling broader access to transplantation, particularly for patients with malignant diseases3,4. More recently, advances in graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) prophylaxis, most notably the introduction of post-transplant cyclophosphamide, have facilitated the rapid expansion of haploidentical transplantation, providing a near-universal donor option5,6.

As a result of these developments, donor availability is no longer the primary limitation to performing allogeneic HCT in many regions. Instead, donor selection has become increasingly nuanced and influenced by multiple factors, including disease indication, urgency of transplantation, patient age, institutional experience, and regional resources3,4,7. Several studies have reported comparable outcomes among HLA-identical sibling, haploidentical, and unrelated donor transplantation in selected settings, further supporting a more flexible approach to donor choice7-9.

The Asia-Pacific (AP) region encompasses countries/regions with diverse healthcare systems, transplant infrastructure, and donor registry capacities. The Asia-Pacific Blood and Marrow Transplantation Group (APBMT) Activity Survey has provided comprehensive information on transplantation activity in this region for more than two decades, primarily focusing on overall transplant numbers and temporal trends10-15. However, these reports have not specifically examined disease-specific donor and stem cell source selection or evaluated the impact of dominant contributing countries on regional patterns, and limited data are available regarding whether donor selection strategies differ according to disease category at a regional level. In addition, recent worldwide and national registry reports have provided important context for interpreting regional trends. The latest Worldwide Network for Blood and Marrow Transplantation (WBMT) survey confirmed continuous worldwide growth of transplantation activity, whereas contemporary reports from China documented rapid expansion of haploidentical transplantation and the predominance of peripheral blood as the graft source in that country16-18.

Understanding disease-specific donor selection patterns is clinically relevant, as malignant and non-malignant diseases differ substantially with respect to disease biology, urgency of transplantation, acceptable toxicity, and long-term outcome priorities. We therefore analyzed data from the APBMT Activity Survey 2022-2023 to examine donor selection according to disease category and to explore disease-specific donor patterns for representative malignant and non-malignant indications. We also performed sensitivity analyses excluding data from China to assess the robustness of observed patterns.

Materials and methods

Data collection

As in previous years, the APBMT Data Center in Japan conducted the annual Activity Survey and collected information regarding the number of transplants in 2022 and 2023. The survey collects aggregated numbers of HCT performed in participating countries/regions, and the data includes information on disease indication, donor type, and stem cell source. No patient-level identifiers such as age, sex, or detailed donor-matching information (e.g., degree of HLA mismatch for unrelated donors) were included in this analysis.

Disease classification

For this report, the diseases were grouped into malignant and non-malignant categories. Malignant diseases included acute myeloid leukemia (AML), acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), myelodysplastic syndrome/myeloproliferative neoplasms (MDS/MPN), plasma cell disorders (PCD), Hodgkin lymphoma (HL), non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) (including lymphoblastic lymphoma), and solid tumors. Non-malignant diseases included severe aplastic anemia (SAA), hemoglobinopathy, and hemophagocytic syndrome. The following disease categories were excluded from disease-class analyses because of heterogeneity or limited comparability across countries/regions: other leukemias, other bone marrow failure syndromes, non-malignant non-hematological diseases, and others.

Donor classification

For allogeneic HCT, donors were classified as HLA-identical sibling, haploidentical, or unrelated. Transplantations from other related donors were included in the haploidentical category. Twin donors were excluded from donor-type analyses because of small numbers.

Stem cell source classification

For allogeneic HCT, stem cell sources were classified as bone marrow (BM), peripheral blood (PB), cord blood (CB), or mixed graft sources (referred to as “mixture” in this survey) based on the reported activity survey categories. For source-based analyses, CB was limited to unrelated donor CB, and transplantations from twin donors were excluded.

Sensitivity analysis

In recent years, the number of HCTs in China has increased significantly, accounting for more than half of all transplants in this region. Therefore, to assess the impact of Chinese data, a sensitivity analysis was performed excluding all transplants reported from China. The distribution of donor types and stem cell sources was recalculated using the same definitions and compared with the main analysis.

Statistical analysis

This study was descriptive in nature. Donor-type distributions were summarized as counts and proportions. For figures, proportions were calculated using the sum of HLA-identical sibling, haploidentical, and unrelated donor transplantations as the denominator. No formal hypothesis testing was performed.

Ethical approval

Because we collected only the annual total of all HCT types, the Data Center and the registries/facilities did not obtain informed consent from each patient. The APBMT Activity Survey was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Aichi Medical University School of Medicine (2022-M025 / 2022-M026) and the APBMT Registry Committee.

Results

Overview of HCT in 2022-2023

In 2022 and 2023, 31,880 and 36,505 HCTs were performed across 19 countries/regions, respectively, and the total number of HCTs in this region has continued to increase. Among them, 18,218 (57.1%) and 21,724 (59.5%) were performed in China in 2022 and 2023, respectively, and China's share of transplants in this region has grown substantially over the past decade (Figure 1A). The trends in the number of transplants, separated into allogeneic and autologous, are shown in Supplementary Figure 1. Main indications for all HCT in 2023 were AML (9,087, 24.9%), PCD (6,543, 17.9%), malignant lymphoma (5,916, 16.2%), ALL (4,845, 13.3%), MDS/MPN (2,857, 7.8%), SAA (2,436, 6.7%), hemoglobinopathy (1,439, 3.9%), and so on (Figure 1B). The relatively high proportion of PCD reflects the large number of autologous HCT performed for this indication. Looking at trends in donor selection for allogeneic transplants over the past 10 years, the number of transplants from HLA-identical siblings has remained fairly constant at approximately 4,000-5,000 per year, whereas unrelated transplants hovered at slightly above 4,000 for several years before gradual rising to around 6,000 per year. However, the number of haploidentical transplants has increased substantially, by 4.7 times (from 2,686 in 2014 to 12,616 in 2023) (Figure 1C). Overall distribution of donor types according to disease category

To investigate whether donor selection differs by disease category, we compared the distribution of donor types between malignant and non-malignant diseases using aggregated data from 2022 to 2023. When all participating countries/regions were included, donor selection varied somewhat between malignant and non-malignant diseases.

In both malignant and non-malignant disease indications, haploidentical donors constituted the largest proportion of allogeneic HCTs. For the malignant diseases, it was followed by unrelated donors, whereas HLA-identical sibling donors accounted for a smaller proportion. Among non-malignant diseases, it also showed a pronounced predominance of haploidentical transplantation, with HLA-identical sibling donors representing the second most common donor source and unrelated donors being used less frequently (Figure 2A). The pattern of donor selection changed when China was excluded from the analysis. When China was excluded, the proportion of haploidentical donors decreased for both malignant and non-malignant diseases, and the use of unrelated donors increased compared with haploidentical donors, especially for malignant diseases (Figure 2B).

Disease-specific donor selection patterns

To further delineate disease-specific differences, donor type distributions were examined for representative malignant and non-malignant indications, including AML, MDS/MPN, SAA, and hemoglobinopathy. Among malignant diseases, both AML and MDS/MPN demonstrated a predominance of haploidentical transplantation, with unrelated donors constituting a substantial secondary donor source. HLA-identical sibling donors accounted for a smaller proportion in these indications. In contrast, non-malignant diseases exhibited a different pattern. In SAA and hemoglobinopathy, haploidentical donors still represented the majority of allogeneic HCTs, while HLA-identical sibling donors were more frequently used than unrelated donors. The contribution of unrelated donors was relatively limited in these non-malignant indications (Figure 2C). When excluding Chinese data (Figure 2D), nearly half of AML and MDS/MPN cases involved unrelated transplants. For SAA and hemoglobinopathies, a clear trend was observed where the proportion of HLA-identical sibling transplants was higher than that of haploidentical transplants (Figure 2D).

Taken together, these results demonstrate that donor selection strategies in this region are disease-specific, with malignant diseases relying on both haploidentical and unrelated donors, whereas non-malignant diseases predominantly utilize family-based donor sources.

Stem cell source selection according to disease category

Stem cell source distributions were broadly similar between malignant and non-malignant diseases in both 2022 and 2023. In the China-included analysis (Figure 2E), PB was the predominant stem cell source in both malignant and non-malignant diseases, accounting for 60%-70% of allogeneic HCTs across years. BM represented a smaller proportion (5%-17%), while CB accounted for 10%-12% in malignant diseases and 3%-4% in non-malignant diseases. Mixture sources constituted 14%-30% of transplantations. When China was excluded (Figure 2F), mixed sources became rare (< 2% across categories). PB remained the most frequently used source (66%-70%), whereas the proportion of BM increased to approximately 12%-13% in malignant diseases and 24%-36% in non-malignant diseases. CB accounted for approximately 19%-20% in malignant diseases and 3%-4% in non-malignant diseases. These patterns were consistent in disease-specific analyses for AML, MDS/MPN, SAA, and hemoglobinopathy (Figure 2G and 2H).

To explore the potential contribution of major countries/regions to the regional patterns, donor and stem cell source distributions were examined in the four countries/regions with the largest transplantation activity (Japan, South Korea, China, and India). Substantial variation exists between countries/regions; however, several common patterns are evident. Haploidentical transplantation predominates in China and India, whereas unrelated donor transplantation contributes more substantially in Japan and South Korea. As for the stem cell source, PB was the predominant source across South Korea and India. CB for malignant disease and BM for non-malignant disease contributed more substantially in Japan, and mixed sources are prominent in China. The relative use of BM, CB, and mixed graft sources differed between countries/regions (Supplementary Figures 2, 3, and 4).

Discussion

In this study, we analyzed contemporary donor and stem cell source selection patterns for allogeneic HCT across the AP region using APBMT Activity Survey data from 2022 and 2023. Unlike previous APBMT Activity Survey reports, which primarily described overall transplant activity and temporal trends, the present study provides a disease-specific evaluation of donor and stem cell source selection and examines the impact of dominant contributing countries through sensitivity analyses. Several important observations emerge from this analysis. First, haploidentical donors constitute the predominant donor type across disease categories, reflecting the widespread adoption of haploidentical transplantation throughout this region11-15. Second, donor selection differs modestly but consistently between malignant and non-malignant diseases, with malignant diseases showing a relatively higher use of unrelated donors and non-malignant diseases demonstrating greater reliance on family-based donors. Third, stem cell source selection shows broadly similar patterns across disease categories, with PB as the predominant source, while modest disease-specific differences are observed.

The predominance of haploidentical transplantation observed in this study is consistent with previous APBMT reports and reflects major advances in GVHD prophylaxis and supportive care that have enabled safe and effective use of haploidentical donors11-15. In malignant diseases, the somewhat higher proportion of unrelated donors may reflect continued reliance on established unrelated donor registries, particularly in countries/regions with well-developed registry infrastructures. In contrast, the greater use of family-based donors in non-malignant diseases may be influenced by considerations such as younger patient age, urgent transplant timing, and the desire to minimize long-term toxicity3,4,7.

With respect to stem cell source selection, PB accounted for approximately two-thirds of all allogeneic HCTs across disease categories, underscoring its role as the dominant graft source in contemporary practice4,19,20. Differences between malignant and non-malignant diseases were modest. Malignant diseases demonstrated a slightly higher proportion of CB transplantation, whereas non-malignant diseases showed somewhat greater use of BM, particularly in analyses excluding China, where BM exceeded 20% in several non-malignant indications. These findings are clinically plausible, as BM grafts are often preferred in non-malignant diseases to reduce the risk of chronic GVHD and preserve long-term quality of life8,19,20.

Notably, exclusion of China resulted in a marked reduction in the contribution of mixed graft sources and a corresponding increase in BM use, especially among non-malignant diseases. This suggests that reporting practices and/or clinical utilization of mixed graft sources in China substantially influence regional stem cell source distributions17,18. Importantly, however, the overall predominance of PB remained consistent regardless of the inclusion or exclusion of China, indicating that this feature represents a stable regional practice pattern.

China represents the largest contributor to transplantation activity in the Asia-Pacific region, followed by Japan, South Korea, and India15,17,18. The donor and stem cell source distributions observed in the China-excluded analysis closely resemble those seen in these three high-volume countries, suggesting that transplantation practice in this region is shaped by the combined influence of several major contributors rather than by a single dominant nation. Japan and South Korea have long-established unrelated donor registries and extensive experience with unrelated donor transplantation, which may contribute to the relatively higher proportion of unrelated donors observed in malignant diseases in the China-excluded dataset. In contrast, China and India have reported widespread adoption of haploidentical transplantation, reflecting the importance of family-based donor availability in regions where access to large unrelated donor registries may be limited11-18. These observations are broadly consistent with other international registry analyses. Global and European surveys have shown increasing use of alternative donors and continuing regional variation in graft-source selection, while Chinese registry studies have shown that haploidentical transplantation now accounts for the majority of allogeneic procedures and that peripheral blood has become the predominant stem cell source4,16-20. It should also be noted that haploidentical transplantation strategies may differ across countries/regions, including the use of anti-thymocyte globulin-based approaches in China and T cell depletion-based strategies in some countries, which may further contribute to regional heterogeneity in transplantation practices, although such methodological details were not captured in the present activity survey.

Country-specific analyses further illustrate how these high-volume countries shape the overall regional transplantation patterns. Donor selection differs considerably between Japan, South Korea, China, and India, and the aggregated APBMT patterns largely reflect the combined influence of these major contributors.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. This study is based on aggregated registry data, and detailed patient-level information, including conditioning intensity, GVHD prophylaxis, and clinical outcomes, was not available. In addition, patient-level variables such as age, sex, and detailed donor-matching information (e.g., degree of HLA mismatch for unrelated donors) were not available, limiting the ability to assess their association with donor selection. Definitions and reporting practices, particularly for mixed graft sources, may vary across countries. Information on access to international donor registries or the proportion of patients with such access was not available in the present dataset, which may also influence donor selection patterns across regions. Center-level differences in transplant indication thresholds and reporting practices may also contribute to regional heterogeneity in transplantation patterns, although such differences could not be assessed in this aggregated dataset. In addition, alternative analytical approaches, such as country-level stratification or weighted analyses based on transplant volume, may provide further insights into regional heterogeneity, but were not feasible in the present study due to the aggregated nature of the data. Also, although sensitivity analyses excluding China were performed, residual heterogeneity in data collection and reporting across participating countries may persist. Finally, this analysis focuses on transplantation activity rather than outcomes, and therefore does not address the comparative effectiveness of specific donor or stem cell source strategies.

In conclusion, contemporary allogeneic HCT practice in the AP region is characterized by widespread use of haploidentical donors and predominant use of PB as the stem cell source, with modest but consistent disease-specific differences. These findings highlight the adaptability of transplantation strategies to disease context and provide a contemporary regional benchmark for donor and stem cell source selection.
