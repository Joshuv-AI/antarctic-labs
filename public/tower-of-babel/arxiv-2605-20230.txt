Abstract
We formulate a Maxwell version of the codimension-three Riesz/Gaussian quadratic-form representation for perfectly conducting parallel plates. This paper is the Maxwell follow-up to the scalar codimension-three Riesz/Gaussian representation theorem of [ 7 ] : the same transverse Riesz reduction and prescribed-covariance quadratic-form mechanism are carried over here to the physical parallel-plate Maxwell operator. The construction is carried out in finite lateral volume Ω L , a = T L 2 × [ 0 , a ] \Omega_{L,a}=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a] , using the physical electric-field Hilbert space of divergence-free fields satisfying the perfect-conductor tangential condition n × E = 0 n\times E=0 , with the static normal zero mode removed. The Maxwell curl–curl operator is defined by its closed quadratic form, and an explicit Fourier-domain analysis proves the finite-volume spectral gap, compact resolvent, and heat-trace admissibility needed for the stochastic construction. For this reduced Maxwell operator ℒ Mx \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}} , the codimension-three Riesz integral gives the transversely reduced Riesz mediator g ​ ℒ Mx − 1 g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1} . A prescribed heat-regularized Gaussian source with covariance ( ℏ ​ c / g ) ​ ℒ Mx 3 / 2 ​ e − τ ​ ℒ Mx (\hbar c/g)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}} then has expected quadratic Green energy equal to the heat-regularized physical Maxwell trace. The finite-volume trace is shown to be spectrally equivalent to a scalar Dirichlet channel plus a scalar Neumann channel with its constant zero mode removed. The result is a representation theorem for the Maxwell parallel-plate trace under a prescribed covariance in the flat-plate geometry considered here.

1
Introduction, scope, and main theorem

The scalar Riesz/Gaussian construction represents a heat-regularized scalar
Casimir trace as the expectation of a quadratic form. Its operator-theoretic
core is simple: in codimension three, transverse reduction of the fractional
product operator
(L+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}
produces the Green multiplier

L^{-1}
, up to the constant
(6\pi^{2})^{-1}
. If a heat-regularized
Gaussian source is then assigned covariance proportional to

L^{3/2}e^{-\tau L}
, pairing this covariance with the induced Green
operator produces the trace multiplier
L^{1/2}e^{-\tau L}
.
The companion scalar preprint
[
7
]
established this
mechanism for positive scalar operators and, in the Dirichlet parallel-plate
benchmark, recovered the standard one-channel scalar interaction finite part.
The present paper asks whether the same representation survives for the true
Maxwell plate operator, where divergence constraints, boundary traces, zero
modes, and physical polarizations must all be handled explicitly.

The purpose of the present paper is to carry out the first Maxwell extension of
that representation in the one geometry where all boundary and polarization
issues can be handled explicitly: two perfectly conducting parallel plates. We
replace the scalar plate operator by the physical Maxwell curl–curl operator
on divergence-free electric fields satisfying the perfect-conductor tangential
boundary condition. The construction is performed first in finite lateral
volume,

\Omega_{L,a}=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a],\qquad A=L^{2},

with periodic lateral directions and conducting plates at
z=0
and

z=a
. The large-area limit is taken only after the heat-regularized trace
density has been formed. We use the term
transverse reduction
for the
codimension-three operator-valued integral; no additional geometric brane
dynamics is involved.

There are two reasons for this finite-volume formulation. First, the Maxwell
operator has a static normal zero mode, which must be removed before a reduced
inverse can be used. Second, after that removal the finite-volume operator has
a positive spectral gap, whereas the gap collapses as
L\to\infty
because of
the zero-vertical-mode branch. Thus the inverse-operator identities below are
finite-volume statements. The infinite-area result is an interaction
finite-part statement for traces per unit area.

1.1
Scope

The paper proves a representation theorem for the reduced physical Maxwell
operator in the parallel-plate geometry. It does not derive the electromagnetic
field from a microscopic quantum field theory, and it does not derive the
Gaussian covariance from a photon path integral, a matter loop, or a QED
effective action. The covariance is prescribed, just as in the scalar
representation theorem, and the result records the quadratic-form identity that
follows from that prescribed covariance.
The overlap with
[
7
]
is therefore the operator-theoretic
Riesz/Gaussian mechanism and the use of a prescribed heat-regularized
covariance. The extension proved here is the passage from that scalar
framework to the reduced physical Maxwell operator with perfect-conductor
boundary conditions. The plate-scale calibration and cube-extremality
discussion from the scalar preprint are not used in the present paper.

The ambient product operator used below is also a mathematical Riesz-type
construction. It is not asserted to be the ordinary Green operator of a local
six-dimensional Maxwell theory. Its role is to provide a codimension-three
spectral reduction whose multiplier after transverse reduction is the Maxwell Green
operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
on the reduced physical Hilbert space.

The paper also does not address non-planar conductors, spherical shells, cube
reference-cell calibration, loop corrections, running couplings, or any
fundamental-constant interpretation. The single physical benchmark evaluated
here is the standard parallel-plate interaction finite part for perfect
conductors
[
2
,
1
]
.

1.2
Strategy

The proof is organized around five gates.
The first four gates parallel the scalar construction of

[
7
]
at the level of spectral calculus and prescribed
covariance. The genuinely new gate is the fifth: the Maxwell trace must be
identified from the physical mode structure itself, not by any scalar doubling
shortcut.

First, the Maxwell operator is defined without using a vector potential. The
primary Hilbert-space variable is the electric field
E
. Perfect-conductor
boundary conditions are imposed as

n\times E=0\qquad\text{on the plates},

and the physical transverse condition is

\nabla\cdot E=0.

The form domain is

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}=\left\{E\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3}):\nabla\cdot E=0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega_{L,a})\right\}.

The unreduced Hilbert space is
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}=\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}}^{\,L^{2}}
. The
unique static normal zero mode is

h_{0}=(L^{2}a)^{-1/2}e_{z},

and the physical Hilbert space is

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\ominus\operatorname{span}\{h_{0}\}.

The reduced Maxwell operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is the positive self-adjoint operator
associated with

q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E,F]=\int_{\Omega_{L,a}}(\nabla\times E)\cdot\overline{(\nabla\times F)}\,\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad E,F\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}.

This form-first definition prevents ambiguity about the operator domain.

Second, the form domain is characterized by an explicit mixed sine/cosine
Fourier expansion. The tangential components use sine modes in the plate
coordinate and the normal component uses cosine modes. The divergence-free
condition becomes the coefficient constraint

\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}=0\qquad(n\geq 1),

and the reduced physical space is obtained by imposing
b_{0,0}=0
. This
coefficient-domain lemma ties the subsequent mode calculation to the actual
closed Maxwell form domain.

Third, with the reduced finite-volume operator in hand, the codimension-three
Riesz reduction is applied by spectral calculus:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}.

Thus, with
g=\kappa/(6\pi^{2})
, the transversely reduced Maxwell Riesz
mediator is
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}=g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
.
Fourth, a heat-regularized Gaussian source is prescribed on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
:

\Sigma_{\tau}=\left(\frac{\hbar c}{g}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/4}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}/2}\Xi.

Its covariance is

C_{\tau}=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}.

The heat-trace estimates proved from the finite-volume spectrum imply that

\Sigma_{\tau}
is an honest
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
-valued Gaussian random variable for every

\tau>0
. Consequently,

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Sigma_{\tau},g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}\right]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

Fifth, the physical Maxwell trace is evaluated. The TE/TM analysis is not
implemented by inserting a scalar factor of two. Instead one proves the
spectral equivalence

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\simeq L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime},

where
L_{\mathrm{D}}
is the scalar Dirichlet Laplacian and
L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
is the scalar
Neumann Laplacian with the constant zero mode removed. Thus

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}})

for Borel functions
f
for which these traces are finite. The remaining
zero-vertical Neumann branch is
retained in the regulated trace and removed only at the interaction
finite-part stage because it is independent of the plate separation
a
.

1.3
Main theorem

The following theorem is the result assembled by the technical sections.

Theorem 1.1

(Maxwell parallel-plate Riesz/Gaussian representation)
.

Let

\Omega_{L,a}=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a]

with periodic lateral directions and perfectly conducting plates at

z=0,a
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
be the reduced perfect-conductor Maxwell operator on

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, defined by the closed form

q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E,F]=\int_{\Omega_{L,a}}(\nabla\times E)\cdot\overline{(\nabla\times F)}\,\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad E,F\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}},

where
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
consists of divergence-free electric fields satisfying

n\times E=0
on the plates, after removing the static normal mode

h_{0}=(L^{2}a)^{-1/2}e_{z}
. Then, for finite
L
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is positive
self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, has compact resolvent, and satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\geq\ell_{L,a}I,\qquad\ell_{L,a}:=\min\left\{\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2},\left(\frac{\pi}{a}\right)^{2}\right\}.

Moreover,

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{p}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)<\infty\qquad(\tau>0,\ p\geq 0).

Define

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}:=\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2},\qquad g:=\frac{\kappa}{6\pi^{2}}.

Then

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}=g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}

as a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
for finite
L
.

Let
\Xi
be cylindrical white noise on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
. For
\tau>0
, set

\Sigma_{\tau}:=\left(\frac{\hbar c}{g}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/4}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}/2}\Xi.

Then
\Sigma_{\tau}
is an
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
-valued Gaussian random variable with
covariance

C_{\tau}=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}.

For

U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}:=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Sigma_{\tau},g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}},

one has the regulated identity

\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

In the large-area limit below,
U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}=U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}(L,a)
denotes the
finite-volume quadratic energy associated with
\Omega_{L,a}
.
Furthermore, the Maxwell trace decomposes as

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}})=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime})

for every Borel function
f
for which these traces are finite. Here

L_{\mathrm{D}}
is the scalar Dirichlet Laplacian,
L_{\mathrm{N}}
is the scalar Neumann
Laplacian, and
L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
is the restriction of
L_{\mathrm{N}}
to the orthogonal
complement of its constant zero mode. The prime on
\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}})
removes
only the constant Neumann zero mode. The remaining
n=0,\ m\neq 0
Neumann
branch is retained in the finite-volume trace and contributes an

a
-independent term to the large-area density. Consequently, under the standard
parallel-plate interaction finite-part prescription,

\boxed{\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}(L,a)]\right]=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{720a^{3}}.}

Proof roadmap.

Section
2
defines the physical electric-field Hilbert
space and the reduced Maxwell operator by a closed quadratic form.
Section
3
proves the mixed Fourier
coefficient-domain lemma, identifies the zero mode, proves the finite-volume
spectral gap, and proves the heat-trace estimates. Section
4

proves the Hilbert-space Riesz reduction, constructs the mollified

Section
5
proves the spectral equivalence

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\simeq L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
. Finally,
Section
6
defines the standard interaction
finite part, removes the
a
-independent zero-vertical branch, and evaluates

is Theorem
6.3
.
∎

Remark 1.2

(Finite-volume discipline)
.

The theorem should be read in the order stated. The identity

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}=g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
uses the finite-volume spectral gap. The large-area limit
is taken only after forming the heat-regularized trace density and applying the
standard interaction finite-part prescription. No bounded inverse

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
is asserted in the infinite-area limit.

Remark 1.3

(Prescribed covariance)
.

The covariance

C_{\tau}=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}

is part of the data of the representation. The paper proves the exact
quadratic-form identity that follows from this covariance. It does not derive
this covariance from microscopic quantum electrodynamics.

2
The reduced Maxwell operator for perfectly conducting plates

This section defines the Maxwell operator used in the representation theorem. The construction is deliberately finite-volume. The slab is

\Omega_{L,a}:=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a],\qquad A=L^{2},

with periodic lateral variables and two perfectly conducting plates at

z=0
and
z=a
. We work over complex vector fields; the real theory is the corresponding real subspace.

The primary field variable is the electric field
E
, not a vector potential. This avoids any need to choose a gauge in the main proof. Gauge-fixed vector-potential formulations may be compared with this construction later, but the operator below is defined directly on the physical transverse electric-field space.

2.1
Perfect-conductor boundary conditions

For a perfect electric conductor, the physical boundary conditions for the electromagnetic fields are

n\times E=0,\qquad n\cdot B=0,

on the conducting boundary. For a nonzero time-harmonic source-free mode with time dependence
e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}
, Maxwell’s equations give

B=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\omega}\nabla\times E

up to the conventional placement of the factor
c
, and the electric field satisfies

\nabla\times\nabla\times E=\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}E,\qquad\nabla\cdot E=0.

Thus the spectral parameter of the positive operator below is
\lambda=\omega^{2}/c^{2}
, and the zero-point energy contribution is
(\hbar c/2)\lambda^{1/2}
.

On the flat plates of
\Omega_{L,a}
, the outward unit normal is
n=\pm e_{z}
. Hence

n\times E=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad E_{x}=E_{y}=0\quad\text{on }z=0,a.

For smooth nonzero modes, this tangential electric condition also implies the normal magnetic condition on the flat plates. Indeed,

n\cdot(\nabla\times E)=\partial_{x}E_{y}-\partial_{y}E_{x}

on a plate. Since the tangential trace
(E_{x},E_{y})
vanishes there, its tangential derivatives vanish in the trace sense, and therefore
n\cdot B=0
. The form construction below takes
n\times E=0
as the essential boundary condition and imposes transversality by
\nabla\cdot E=0
.

Equivalently, if
E
is identified with the one-form

E_{x}\,dx+E_{y}\,dy+E_{z}\,dz,

then
n\times E=0
on the plates is the condition that the pullback of this
one-form to the boundary vanish. In differential-form terminology this is the
relative trace condition for one-forms, namely the form-domain part of the
relative boundary condition. We will not rely on that language in the proofs.

2.2
The PEC form domain

Let

H(\operatorname{curl})(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3}):=\left\{E\in L^{2}(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3}):\nabla\times E\in L^{2}(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3})\right\}

with derivatives understood in the sense of distributions and with graph norm

\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{H(\operatorname{curl})}^{2}:=\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\times E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Because the lateral variables live on the torus
T_{L}^{2}
, there are no lateral boundary faces. We use the following convention throughout the paper:

H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3})

denotes the closure in the
H(\operatorname{curl})
-graph norm of smooth lateral-periodic vector fields whose tangential components satisfy

E_{x}=E_{y}=0\quad\text{at }z=0,a.

Thus
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
encodes zero tangential electric trace on the two conducting plates.
This is the standard
H(\operatorname{curl})
framework for Maxwell boundary-value
problems with perfect-conductor trace conditions; see, for example,

[
6
]
.

Define the divergence-free perfect-conductor form domain by

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}:=\left\{E\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3}):\nabla\cdot E=0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega_{L,a})\right\}.

(2.1)

Here
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega_{L,a})
is understood with lateral periodicity. The divergence constraint is closed under
L^{2}
-convergence: if
E_{j}\to E
in
L^{2}
and
\nabla\cdot E_{j}=0
, then for every smooth periodic test function
\varphi
compactly supported in the open interval
(0,a)
in the plate variable,

\left\langle\nabla\cdot E,\varphi\right\rangle=-\int_{\Omega_{L,a}}E\cdot\nabla\overline{\varphi}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{j\to\infty}-\int_{\Omega_{L,a}}E_{j}\cdot\nabla\overline{\varphi}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Consequently
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
is a closed subspace of
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3})
in the
H(\operatorname{curl})
-graph norm.

The unreduced PEC Hilbert space is the
L^{2}
-closure of this form domain:

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}:=\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}}^{\,L^{2}(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3})}.

(2.2)

The boundary condition is imposed at the level of the form domain
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
, not as a pointwise condition on arbitrary elements of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
.

2.3
The static normal mode and the reduced physical space

The constant normal field

e_{z}=(0,0,1)

satisfies

\nabla\times e_{z}=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot e_{z}=0,\qquad n\times e_{z}=0\text{ on }z=0,a.

Hence it belongs to
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
and has zero Maxwell energy. Its normalized version is

h_{0}:=\left\lvert\Omega_{L,a}\right\rvert^{-1/2}e_{z}=(L^{2}a)^{-1/2}e_{z}.

(2.3)

This is the uniform static electric field normal to the plates. It represents a zero-frequency electrostatic sector, not a photon oscillator contributing to the Casimir trace.

We therefore remove this mode before defining the physical Maxwell operator. Let

P_{0}E:=\left\langle E,h_{0}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L,a})}h_{0}

(2.4)

be the orthogonal projection onto
\operatorname{span}\{h_{0}\}
. Define

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\ominus\operatorname{span}\{h_{0}\}=(I-P_{0})\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}

(2.5)

and

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}:=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}.

(2.6)

The space
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
. Indeed, if
F\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, choose
E_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
with
E_{j}\to F
in
L^{2}
. Since
h_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
, each

E_{j}-P_{0}E_{j}

belongs to
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, and

E_{j}-P_{0}E_{j}\longrightarrow F-P_{0}F=F

in
L^{2}
.

The next section proves, by an explicit mixed Fourier decomposition, that
\operatorname{span}\{h_{0}\}
is the entire kernel of the unreduced finite-volume PEC Maxwell form. This section only removes the known static normal mode and constructs the reduced operator. Positivity with a finite-volume spectral gap is not asserted until after the Fourier-domain analysis.

2.4
The closed Maxwell quadratic form

Define the unreduced perfect-conductor Maxwell form on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
by

q_{\mathrm{PEC}}[E,F]:=\int_{\Omega_{L,a}}(\nabla\times E)\cdot\overline{(\nabla\times F)}\,\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad E,F\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}.

(2.7)

The form is nonnegative. It is also closed: the norm

\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{q_{\mathrm{PEC}}}^{2}:=\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+q_{\mathrm{PEC}}[E,E]=\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\times E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

is exactly the
H(\operatorname{curl})
-graph norm on the closed subspace
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
is complete in this norm,
q_{\mathrm{PEC}}
is a closed densely defined nonnegative form on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
.

Restrict this form to the reduced physical space:

q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E,F]:=q_{\mathrm{PEC}}[E,F]=\int_{\Omega_{L,a}}(\nabla\times E)\cdot\overline{(\nabla\times F)}\,\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad E,F\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}.

(2.8)

The subspace
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
is closed in
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
for the graph norm. To see this, let
E_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
converge to
E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
in the graph norm. Then
E_{j}\to E
in
L^{2}
, and since
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
is closed in
L^{2}
, one has
E\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
. Thus
E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
. Hence
q_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is closed, nonnegative, and densely defined on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
.

By the representation theorem for closed semibounded quadratic forms

[
4
]
, there is a unique nonnegative self-adjoint operator

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}

on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
such that

q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E,F]=\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}E,F\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}\qquad\text{for all }F\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}

(2.9)

whenever
E\in\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})
. Equivalently,

\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=\left\{E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}:\exists G\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}\text{ such that }q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E,F]=\left\langle G,F\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}\text{ for all }F\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}\right\},

(2.10)

and then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}E:=G
.

For smooth fields in this operator domain, integration by parts gives the differential expression

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}E=\nabla\times\nabla\times E.

(2.11)

Since the form domain imposes
\nabla\cdot E=0
, this agrees on smooth fields with

\nabla\times\nabla\times E=-\Delta E.

The boundary condition and transversality are not imposed as additional
pointwise assumptions on
\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})
. They are encoded by the form domain

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
.

Definition 2.1

(Reduced PEC Maxwell operator)
.

The reduced perfect-conductor Maxwell operator on the finite slab
\Omega_{L,a}
is the positive self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
associated with the closed form
q_{\mathrm{Mx}}
in (
2.8
) on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
in (
2.5

Remark 2.2

(Why the electric-field formulation is used)
.

The construction does not introduce a vector potential and therefore does not require a gauge choice in the main proof. The physical constraints are imposed directly on the electric field: zero tangential electric trace at the plates, distributional transversality, and removal of the static normal zero mode. A vector-potential or covariant gauge-fixed formulation may be compared with this operator later, but it is not needed for the Riesz reduction or the stochastic trace identity.

Remark 2.3

(Next spectral step)
.

The mixed sine/cosine Fourier description of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
in the next section
identifies the zero mode and supplies the spectral gap, compact-resolvent, and
heat-trace properties used later in the paper.

3
Fourier description and heat-trace admissibility

The preceding section defined
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
by a closed quadratic form. This section ties that abstract form domain to an explicit Fourier model on

\Omega_{L,a}=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a].

The result is the finite-volume spectral input needed for the Riesz and stochastic constructions: after removal of the static normal mode,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
has a positive spectral gap, compact resolvent, and finite heat traces

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{p}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)<\infty\qquad(\tau>0,\ p\geq 0).

No large-area limit is taken in this section.

3.1
Mixed Fourier bases

Write
r=(x,y)\in T_{L}^{2}
, and set

k_{m}:=\frac{2\pi}{L}m,\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\qquad\nu_{n}:=\frac{\pi n}{a},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.1)

The normalized lateral Fourier modes are

\phi_{m}(r):=\frac{1}{L}e^{\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot r},\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

(3.2)

For the plate variable we use the sine basis

s_{n}(z):=\sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\sin(\nu_{n}z),\qquad n\geq 1,

(3.3)

and the cosine basis

c_{0}(z):=a^{-1/2},\qquad c_{n}(z):=\sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\cos(\nu_{n}z),\qquad n\geq 1.

(3.4)

Thus
\{\phi_{m}s_{n}:m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},n\geq 1\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(T_{L}^{2}\times(0,a))
, and so is
\{\phi_{m}c_{n}:m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},n\geq 0\}
.

The choice of sine modes for
E_{x},E_{y}
is adapted to the perfect-conductor tangential condition

E_{x}=E_{y}=0\quad\text{on }z=0,a,

whereas no Dirichlet condition is imposed on
E_{z}
. Hence an
L^{2}
vector field is written in the mixed form

E_{\parallel}(r,z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{m,n}\,\phi_{m}(r)s_{n}(z),\qquad a_{m,n}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

(3.5)

where
E_{\parallel}=(E_{x},E_{y})
, and

E_{z}(r,z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}b_{m,n}\,\phi_{m}(r)c_{n}(z),\qquad b_{m,n}\in\mathbb{C}.

(3.6)

Parseval gives

\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L,a})}^{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}.

(3.7)

At the level of
L^{2}
alone, the sine expansion should be viewed only as a
choice of orthonormal coordinates. The perfect-conductor trace condition is
not read pointwise from an arbitrary
L^{2}
series; it is recovered below
from the weighted coefficient domain and the graph-norm closure defining

H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
.

For later use define

\lambda_{m,n}:=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\qquad(n\geq 1).

(3.8)

When
n=0
, the corresponding normal-branch eigenvalue will be written simply as
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
.

3.2
The coefficient-domain lemma

Let
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
be the space of coefficient families

\left\{a_{m,n}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\ (m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},n\geq 1),\ b_{m,n}\in\mathbb{C}\ (m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},n\geq 0)\right\}

satisfying

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}(1+\lambda_{m,n})\left(\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(1+\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2})\left\lvert b_{m,0}\right\rvert^{2}<\infty

(3.9)

and the coefficient transversality equations

\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}=0,\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad n\geq 1.

(3.10)

Let

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{phys}}:=\left\{(a,b)\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}:b_{0,0}=0\right\}.

(3.11)

Lemma 3.1

(Fourier characterization of the PEC form domain)
.

The reconstruction map defined by (
3.5
)–(
3.6
) identifies
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
with the form domain

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}=\left\{E\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3}):\nabla\cdot E=0\right\}

defined in (
2.1
). Under this identification,

\left\lVert\nabla\times E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{m,n}\left(\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert b_{m,0}\right\rvert^{2}.

(3.12)

Consequently,
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{phys}}
identifies with

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}.

Proof.

We prove both inclusions. The distributional coefficient calculations and the
density argument are given in full in
Appendix
A
, and we record the main steps here to
keep the spectral calculation self-contained.

First suppose that a coefficient family lies in
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
. Its finite rectangular truncations reconstruct smooth lateral-periodic fields. Their tangential components are finite sums of sine modes and therefore vanish at
z=0,a
. Hence each truncation belongs to the smooth PEC class used to define
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
.

For a finite truncation, direct differentiation gives

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot E

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}\right)\phi_{m}(r)s_{n}(z),

(3.13)

so the constraint (
3.10
) makes the truncation divergence-free.
The curl coefficients are as follows. For
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle C^{x}_{m,n}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{i}k_{m,y}b_{m,n}-\nu_{n}a^{y}_{m,n},

\displaystyle C^{y}_{m,n}

\displaystyle:=\nu_{n}a^{x}_{m,n}-\mathrm{i}k_{m,x}b_{m,n},

(3.14)

\displaystyle C^{z}_{m,n}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{i}\left(k_{m,x}a^{y}_{m,n}-k_{m,y}a^{x}_{m,n}\right).

For the normal branch
n=0
,

C^{x}_{m,0}=\mathrm{i}k_{m,y}b_{m,0},\qquad C^{y}_{m,0}=-\mathrm{i}k_{m,x}b_{m,0},\qquad C^{z}_{m,0}=0.

(3.15)

A direct algebraic identity gives, for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert C^{x}_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert C^{y}_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert C^{z}_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt=\lambda_{m,n}\left(\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}\right).

Since the divergence coefficient vanishes, this identity and (
3.15
) imply (
3.12
) for finite truncations. The weighted summability condition (
3.9
) therefore makes the finite truncations Cauchy in the
H(\operatorname{curl})
-graph norm. Their graph-norm limit lies in
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
, the divergence constraint passes to the distributional limit, and the reconstructed field belongs to
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
. This proves

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}.

Conversely, let
E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
. Since
E\in L^{2}
, it has the mixed coefficient expansion (
3.5
)–(
3.6
), with Parseval identity (
3.7
). The distributional identity
\nabla\cdot E=0
implies the coefficient constraint (
3.10
), because the divergence has the expansion (
3.13
) in the sense of distributions. Equivalently, testing against the modes
\overline{\phi_{m}}s_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, yields exactly (
3.10
); no boundary term appears because
s_{n}(0)=s_{n}(a)=0
.

The weak curl
\nabla\times E
belongs to
L^{2}
. The coefficient formulas
(
3.2
) and (
3.15
) are understood
distributionally: they are obtained by differentiating finite partial sums,
testing against smooth periodic sine/cosine modes, and passing to the
distributional limit. Since the weak curl is an
L^{2}
field, uniqueness of
Fourier coefficients identifies its
L^{2}
-Fourier coefficients with the
displayed expressions. Applying Parseval to
\nabla\times E
and using
(
3.16
) with the divergence coefficient equal to
zero gives

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{m,n}\left(\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert b_{m,0}\right\rvert^{2}<\infty.

Together with (
3.7
), this is exactly (
3.9
). Hence the coefficient family of
E
lies in
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
, and the curl norm is given by (
3.12
). Thus

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\subset\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}.

The two inclusions prove the first assertion. Finally, the coefficient
b_{0,0}
is precisely the component of
E
in the direction of the normalized constant normal field
h_{0}=(L^{2}a)^{-1/2}e_{z}
. Therefore imposing
b_{0,0}=0
is exactly the condition
E\perp h_{0}
, and
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{phys}}
identifies with
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
.
∎

Remark 3.2

(Why this lemma is needed)
.

The Maxwell operator was defined by a closed form, not by first writing down separated eigenfunctions. Lemma
3.1
proves that the separated coefficient model is the actual form domain. Thus the spectral calculations below are calculations for the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
, not merely formal mode counting.

3.3
Kernel and reduced inverse in finite volume

The Fourier characterization identifies the zero-energy fields immediately. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
denote the nonnegative self-adjoint operator associated with the unreduced closed form
q_{\mathrm{PEC}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
.

Proposition 3.3

(Kernel of the unreduced PEC form)
.

The kernel of the unreduced PEC Maxwell form
q_{\mathrm{PEC}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
is

\operatorname{Ker}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PEC}}=\operatorname{span}\{h_{0}\}.

Equivalently, the only finite-volume divergence-free PEC field with zero curl is the uniform static normal field.

Proof.

If
E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
and
q_{\mathrm{PEC}}[E,E]=0
, then
\nabla\times E=0
. By (
3.12
), all coefficients with positive weights must vanish:

a_{m,n}=0,\quad b_{m,n}=0\quad(n\geq 1),\qquad b_{m,0}=0\quad(m\neq 0).

Only
b_{0,0}
may remain. The corresponding field is a constant multiple of
e_{z}
, or of the normalized field
h_{0}
. Conversely,
e_{z}
is divergence-free, satisfies the PEC tangential condition, and has zero curl. Hence the kernel is exactly
\operatorname{span}\{h_{0}\}
.
∎

After passing to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\ominus\operatorname{span}\{h_{0}\}
, the zero mode is removed. The first positive spectral scale is

\ell_{L,a}:=\min\left\{\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2},\left(\frac{\pi}{a}\right)^{2}\right\}.

(3.17)

The next proposition makes this precise.

Proposition 3.4

(Finite-volume spectral gap)
.

For finite
L<\infty
and
a>0
, the reduced Maxwell operator satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\geq\ell_{L,a}I\qquad\text{on }\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}.

(3.18)

Consequently
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
is a bounded positive operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, with

\left\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}\right\rVert\leq\ell_{L,a}^{-1}.

(3.19)

Proof.

For
E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, the coefficient
b_{0,0}
vanishes. In (
3.12
), every remaining coefficient is weighted either by
\lambda_{m,n}
with
n\geq 1
, or by
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
with
m\neq 0
. The smallest possible value of
\lambda_{m,n}
for
n\geq 1
is
(\pi/a)^{2}
, attained at
m=0,n=1
. The smallest value of
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
for
m\neq 0
is
(2\pi/L)^{2}
. Therefore

q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E,E]\geq\ell_{L,a}\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\qquad(E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}),

which is the quadratic-form inequality (
3.18
). The boundedness of the inverse follows from the spectral theorem.
∎

Remark 3.5

(Why finite volume is essential)
.

The bound (
3.18
) collapses as
L\to\infty
, because the normal branch with
n=0
has eigenvalues
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
. Therefore all uses of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
as a bounded operator are finite-volume statements. The large-area limit is taken later only after heat-regularized traces per unit area have been formed.

3.4
Diagonal form and spectrum

The coefficient model also diagonalizes the self-adjoint operator. For
n\geq 1
, define the constrained block

W_{m,n}:=\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a-\nu_{n}b=0\right\}.

(3.20)

For
n=0
and
m\neq 0
, define
W_{m,0}:=\mathbb{C}
, represented by the normal coefficient
b_{m,0}
. The removed block
(m,n)=(0,0)
is absent from the physical space.

For
n\geq 1
, the block
W_{m,n}
has dimension two. Indeed, it is the kernel of one nonzero linear functional on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, because
\nu_{n}>0
. The block
W_{m,0}
has dimension one for
m\neq 0
.

Under the unitary coefficient representation of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, the form
q_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is

q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E,E]=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{m,n}\left\lVert(a_{m,n},b_{m,n})\right\rVert_{\mathbb{C}^{3}}^{2}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert b_{m,0}\right\rvert^{2}.

(3.21)

It follows from the representation theorem for diagonal closed forms that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
acts as multiplication by
\lambda_{m,n}
on
W_{m,n}
,
n\geq 1
, and by
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
on
W_{m,0}
,
m\neq 0
.

Thus the reduced Maxwell spectrum is

\lambda_{m,n}=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2},\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad n\geq 1,

(3.22)

with multiplicity two, together with

\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2},\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\},

(3.23)

with multiplicity one. The missing
m=0,n=0
member of the second family is exactly the removed static normal mode.

Proposition 3.6

(Trace formula for diagonal spectral functions)
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
be Borel and suppose that the sums below converge absolutely. Then

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=2\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\right).

(3.24)

Proof.

The block decomposition above is an orthogonal Hilbert direct sum. The operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is scalar multiplication by
\lambda_{m,n}
on each two-dimensional block
W_{m,n}
,
n\geq 1
, and scalar multiplication by
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
on each one-dimensional block
W_{m,0}
,
m\neq 0
. Taking the trace of
f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})
block by block gives (
3.24
).
∎

Remark 3.7

(Not yet the TE/TM theorem)
.

Formula (
3.24
) is a diagonal spectral formula. The later TE/TM section will identify the same trace as a Dirichlet scalar trace plus a reduced Neumann scalar trace. That later interpretation is not needed for the present heat-trace admissibility result.

3.5
Compact resolvent and heat-trace admissibility

Proposition 3.8

(Compact resolvent)
.

For finite
L<\infty
and
a>0
, the reduced Maxwell operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
has compact resolvent.

Proof.

By (
3.22
)–(
3.23
), the spectrum consists of eigenvalues

\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\quad(m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},n\geq 1)

with finite multiplicity, and eigenvalues
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
with
m\neq 0
, also with finite multiplicity. For every
R>0
, only finitely many lattice pairs satisfy

\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\leq R,

and only finitely many
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
satisfy
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\leq R
. Hence the eigenvalues tend to infinity with finite multiplicity. Therefore
(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+I)^{-1}
is compact.
∎

Proposition 3.9

(Heat-trace admissibility)
.

For every
\tau>0
and every
p\geq 0
,

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{p}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)<\infty.

(3.25)

In particular, the traces needed in the Gaussian construction are finite:

\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}})<\infty,\qquad\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)<\infty,\qquad\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)<\infty.

(3.26)

Proof.

Using (
3.24
) with
f(\lambda)=\lambda^{p}e^{-\tau\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{p}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\right)^{p}e^{-\tau(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2})}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2p}e^{-\tau\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}}.

Let

c_{L,a}:=\min\left\{\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2},\left(\frac{\pi}{a}\right)^{2}\right\}>0.

Then

\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\geq c_{L,a}\left(\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2}+n^{2}\right)\qquad(n\geq 1),

and
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\geq(2\pi/L)^{2}\left\lvert m\right\rvert^{2}
for the normal branch. A polynomial factor times a Gaussian lattice factor is summable on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{N}
and on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Thus both sums in (
3.27
) converge absolutely, proving (
3.25
). The three special cases in (
3.26
) follow by taking
p=0,1/2,3/2
.
∎

Corollary 3.10

(Spectral hypotheses for the finite-volume Maxwell construction)
.

For each fixed finite
L<\infty
and
a>0
, the reduced Maxwell operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is positive self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, has compact resolvent, satisfies the gap estimate (
3.18
), and obeys the heat-trace bounds (
3.25
). Thus it satisfies the finite-volume spectral hypotheses needed for the Riesz reduction and the heat-regularized Maxwell Gaussian source.

Remark 3.11

(Use in the next section)
.

This section supplies the finite-volume spectral input for the Riesz reduction
and Gaussian source construction of the next section.

4
Riesz reduction and the Maxwell Gaussian source

Throughout this section the lateral size
L<\infty
and the plate spacing

a>0
are fixed. Thus
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
denotes the reduced perfect-conductor Maxwell
operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
constructed in Section
2
. By
Corollary
3.10
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is
positive self-adjoint, has compact resolvent, and has a positive finite-volume
spectral gap

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\geq\ell_{L,a}I,\qquad\ell_{L,a}:=\min\left\{\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2},\left(\frac{\pi}{a}\right)^{2}\right\}>0.

(4.1)

It also satisfies the heat-trace admissibility statement

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{p}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)<\infty\qquad(\tau>0,\ p\geq 0).

(4.2)

Consequently
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
is a bounded positive operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
for
fixed finite
L
. The large-area limit is not taken in this section.

The purpose of this section is to prove the regulated representation theorem
for this finite-volume Maxwell operator. The construction has two parts. The
first is deterministic: the codimension-three transverse Riesz reduction of

(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}
gives the reduced Maxwell Green operator

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
, up to the universal constant
(6\pi^{2})^{-1}
. The second is
stochastic: a prescribed heat-regularized Gaussian source with covariance
proportional to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}
turns the quadratic form with

g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
into the heat-regularized Maxwell trace.
This section is the point of closest formal overlap with

[
7
]
. At the level of spectral calculus, the
codimension-three Riesz reduction and the prescribed-covariance
quadratic-form identity are the same mechanism. What changes here is the
underlying operator: instead of an abstract scalar operator, we apply the
construction to the reduced perfect-conductor Maxwell operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
.

Remark 4.1

(Finite-volume nature of the inverse)
.

The finite-volume lower bound in (
4.1
) is used in
two places: to make the transverse Riesz integral an operator-norm integral and
to make
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
bounded in the stochastic quadratic form. The lower bound
collapses as
L\to\infty
, because of the
n=0
normal branch identified in
Section
3
. Hence all identities below are
finite-volume identities. Later, only after taking traces per unit area and
applying the parallel-plate interaction finite part, is the limit

L\to\infty
taken.

4.1
An abstract codimension-three Riesz reduction

We first record the Hilbert-space version of the transverse momentum integral.
It is independent of whether the operator acts on scalar functions, vector
fields, or sections of a vector bundle. Only the spectral theorem and the
positive lower bound are used.

Lemma 4.2

(Abstract transverse Riesz integral)
.

Let
A
be a positive self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
and suppose

A\geq\ell I\qquad\text{for some }\ell>0.

For
s>3/2
, define

T_{s}(A):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(A+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-s}.

(4.3)

Then the integral in (
4.3
) converges in operator norm
and

T_{s}(A)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{\Gamma(s-3/2)}{\Gamma(s)}A^{3/2-s}.

(4.4)

In particular,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(A+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}A^{-1}.

(4.5)

Proof.

The lower bound gives

\left\lVert(A+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-s}\right\rVert\leq(\ell+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-s}.

The right-hand side is integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
exactly when
s>3/2
.
Hence (
4.3
) is an operator-norm Bochner integral.

Let
E_{A}(\,\mathrm{d}\lambda)
be the spectral resolution of
A
. For

u,v\in\mathcal{H}
, the preceding norm bound justifies Fubini and gives

\displaystyle\left\langle u,T_{s}(A)v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\ell}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\lambda+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-s}\right]\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{u,v}(\lambda),

where
\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{u,v}(\lambda)=\left\langle u,E_{A}(\,\mathrm{d}\lambda)v\right\rangle
. The scalar
integral is computed by the Schwinger representation:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\lambda+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-t\lambda}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-t\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{3/2}\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1-3/2}e^{-t\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{\Gamma(s-3/2)}{\Gamma(s)}\lambda^{3/2-s}.

Substitution into the spectral integral proves
(
4.4
). For
s=5/2
,

\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{\Gamma(1)}{\Gamma(5/2)}=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}},

which gives (
4.5
).
∎

Applying Lemma
4.2
with
A=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
gives
the finite-volume Maxwell identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}.

(4.6)

This is the deterministic codimension-three reduction used below.

4.2
The ambient product operator and transverse restriction

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
denote the three auxiliary transverse coordinates. The ambient
Hilbert space is

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{y}),

(4.7)

and the ambient product operator is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}:=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\otimes I+I\otimes(-\Delta_{y}).

(4.8)

The Maxwell constraints and boundary conditions are already built into the
factor
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
; the transverse factor is only the Euclidean auxiliary factor
used for the Riesz reduction.

Proposition 4.3

(Ambient product operator)
.

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
in (
4.8
) is positive
self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}
. Under Fourier transform in the
transverse variable
y
, it is represented as the direct integral

\widehat{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{\oplus}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

(4.9)

Consequently,

\widehat{\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-5/2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{\oplus}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

(4.10)

Proof.

By Proposition
3.8
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
has an orthonormal
eigenbasis
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, with

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}u_{j}=\lambda_{j}u_{j},\qquad 0<\ell_{L,a}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots,\qquad\lambda_{j}\to\infty.

Thus

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{y}),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}(\lambda_{j}-\Delta_{y}).

Each operator
\lambda_{j}-\Delta_{y}
is positive self-adjoint. The direct-sum
operator is therefore positive self-adjoint. Fourier transform in
y
sends

-\Delta_{y}
to multiplication by
\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}
, which gives the direct-integral
representation (
4.9
). Formula
(
4.10
) follows from the spectral
theorem.
∎

Let
\kappa>0
be the normalization of the ambient Riesz mediator and set

g:=\frac{\kappa}{6\pi^{2}}.

(4.11)

Define the transversely reduced Maxwell Riesz mediator by the operator-norm
integral

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}:=\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}.

(4.12)

By (
4.6
),

\boxed{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}=g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}.}

(4.13)

This is the Maxwell analogue of the scalar codimension-three Riesz reduction,
with
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
replacing the corresponding scalar plate operator.

The same operator is obtained from
\kappa\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-5/2}
by a canonical
mollified transverse restriction. This avoids any illegal point evaluation in
the transverse
L^{2}
-factor.

Proposition 4.4

(Mollified transverse restriction)
.

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=1,

and set

\eta_{\varepsilon}(y):=\varepsilon^{-3}\eta(y/\varepsilon),\qquad\varepsilon>0.

For
J\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, define

R_{\varepsilon}J:=J\otimes\eta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}.

Then for all
J,K\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left\langle R_{\varepsilon}J,\kappa\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-5/2}R_{\varepsilon}K\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}}=\left\langle J,\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}K\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}=\left\langle J,g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}K\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}.

(4.14)

The limit is independent of the normalized mollifier
\eta
.

Proof.

Use the Fourier transform convention in the transverse variable for which
Plancherel has measure
(2\pi)^{-3}\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q
. Then

\widehat{\eta_{\varepsilon}}(q)=\widehat{\eta}(\varepsilon q)
, and
Proposition
4.3
gives

\displaystyle\left\langle R_{\varepsilon}J,\kappa\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-5/2}R_{\varepsilon}K\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\lvert\widehat{\eta}(\varepsilon q)\right\rvert^{2}\left\langle J,(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}K\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}.

Because
\widehat{\eta}
is bounded and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\geq\ell_{L,a}I
,

\left\lvert\left\langle J,(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}K\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert J\right\rVert\left\lVert K\right\rVert(\ell_{L,a}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2},

and the majorant is integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Also
\widehat{\eta}(0)=1
.
Dominated convergence therefore gives

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left\langle R_{\varepsilon}J,\kappa\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-5/2}R_{\varepsilon}K\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\langle J,(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}K\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle J,\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}K\right\rangle.

Equation (
4.13
) gives the final equality.
Since only
\widehat{\eta}(0)=1
enters the limiting value, the limit is
independent of the particular mollifier.
∎

Remark 4.5

(Meaning of the ambient mediator)
.

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-5/2}
is a fractional Riesz-type mediator on the product
space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{y})
. It is not the ordinary Green operator

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}
of a local higher-dimensional Maxwell theory. Its role here is
narrower: after transverse restriction, the exponent
5/2
produces the
ordinary reduced Maxwell Green operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
on the physical plate
Hilbert space.

4.3
The prescribed Maxwell Gaussian source

Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be an orthonormal eigenbasis of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
in

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}u_{j}=\lambda_{j}u_{j},\qquad\lambda_{j}>0.

Let
\Xi
denote cylindrical white noise on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, formally

\Xi=\sum_{j\geq 1}\xi_{j}u_{j}.

(4.15)

For the complex Hilbert-space convention, the coefficients may be taken as
centered circular complex Gaussians satisfying

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}\overline{\xi_{j}}]=\delta_{ij},\qquad\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}\xi_{j}]=0.

The real convention gives the same covariance formulas with complex conjugates
removed. The formal series (
4.15
) is not, in
general, an
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
-valued random variable; see

[
3
]
for the Hilbert-space Gaussian background.

For
\tau>0
, define

\boxed{\Sigma_{\tau}:=\left(\frac{\hbar c}{g}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/4}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}/2}\Xi.}

(4.16)

Equivalently,

\Sigma_{\tau}=\left(\frac{\hbar c}{g}\right)^{1/2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{3/4}e^{-\tau\lambda_{j}/2}\xi_{j}u_{j}.

(4.17)

Proposition 4.6

(Heat-regularized Maxwell source)
.

For every
\tau>0
, the series (
4.17
) converges in

L^{2}(\Omega_{\mathrm{prob}};\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}})
. Hence
\Sigma_{\tau}
is an

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
-valued centered Gaussian random variable. Its covariance operator is

C_{\tau}:=\mathbb{E}[\Sigma_{\tau}\otimes\Sigma_{\tau}^{*}]=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}.

(4.18)

In particular
C_{\tau}
is positive and trace class.

Proof.

By orthonormality and the Gaussian normalization,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

The trace is finite by (
4.2
), with

p=3/2
. This proves convergence in

L^{2}(\Omega_{\mathrm{prob}};\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}})
and therefore defines an

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
-valued centered Gaussian random variable.

The covariance operator is characterized by its matrix entries in the
orthonormal basis
\{u_{j}\}
. With the complex convention stated above,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\langle u_{i},\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle\overline{\left\langle u_{j},\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle}\right]=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\lambda_{j}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\lambda_{j}}\delta_{ij},

with the analogous formula in the real convention. Hence
C_{\tau}
is diagonal
in this basis with eigenvalues

(\hbar c/g)\lambda_{j}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\lambda_{j}}
, which is exactly the spectral
operator
(\hbar c/g)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}
. Its trace is the finite number
computed above.
∎

Remark 4.7

(Prescribed covariance)
.

The covariance (
4.18
) is prescribed. It is the Maxwell
analogue of the scalar covariance chosen so that pairing with the induced Green
operator converts the multiplier
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}
into
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}
. Thus the
present section proves the resulting representation theorem for this prescribed
covariance.

Remark 4.8

(Projection is spectral, not a flat-space Fourier projector)
.

The projection used here is the spectral projection encoded by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
and

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
in the bounded plate geometry. No separate flat-space tensor
projector
\delta_{ij}-q_{i}q_{j}/\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}
enters the construction. The
physical transversality and boundary conditions are encoded by the Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
and the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
. The
covariance (
4.18
) is therefore the spectral covariance
on the already reduced Maxwell Hilbert space.

4.4
The regulated quadratic-form trace identity

Define the regulated Maxwell quadratic energy by

\boxed{U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}:=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Sigma_{\tau},g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}.}

(4.19)

For finite
L
, the operator
g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
is bounded and positive. Hence

U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}
is well-defined almost surely and is nonnegative. Here the inner
product is the Hermitian inner product on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, so the positive operator

g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
defines a nonnegative quadratic form. Moreover,

\mathbb{E}\left\lvert U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}\right\rvert\leq\frac{g}{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}\right\rVert\,\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}^{2}<\infty.

Theorem 4.9

(Regulated Maxwell quadratic-form representation)
.

For every
\tau>0
,

\boxed{\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).}

(4.20)

The trace on the right is finite.

Proof.

Using the eigen-expansion (
4.17
),

U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}=\frac{g}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\left\lvert\left\langle u_{j},\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}.

For each
j
,

\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\left\langle u_{j},\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\lambda_{j}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\lambda_{j}}.

Since the terms are nonnegative, monotone convergence gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\frac{\hbar c}{g}\lambda_{j}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

The final trace is finite by (
4.2
), with

p=1/2
.
∎

Equivalently, the same calculation can be written as the Hilbert-space
covariance trace identity

\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}C_{\tau}\right)=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

(4.21)

Here
g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
is bounded in finite volume and
C_{\tau}
is trace class.

Remark 4.10

(Regulated identity, not yet the plate finite part)
.

Theorem
4.9
is an identity for every
fixed
\tau>0
and finite
L
. The trace evaluation requires the Maxwell
TE/TM spectral decomposition, and the electromagnetic plate coefficient
requires the standard parallel-plate interaction finite-part prescription.
Those steps are carried out in the next sections.

Remark 4.11

(Finite parts of positive regulated quantities)
.

For each fixed
\tau>0
,
U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}\geq 0
almost surely. A later
renormalized interaction finite part can nevertheless be negative, because it
is obtained after subtracting divergent local and separation-independent terms
from the regulated expectation. This is the usual situation in Casimir
finite-part calculations and is not a contradiction.

4.5
Summary of the finite-volume representation

Combining (
4.13
),
(
4.16
), and
Theorem
4.9
, we have proved the
finite-volume representation

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}=g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1},\qquad\Sigma_{\tau}=\left(\frac{\hbar c}{g}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/4}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}/2}\Xi,

(4.22)

with

C_{\tau}=\mathbb{E}[\Sigma_{\tau}\otimes\Sigma_{\tau}^{*}]=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}},

(4.23)

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Sigma_{\tau},\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}}\right]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

(4.24)

This is the Maxwell analogue of the scalar Riesz/Gaussian trace representation
at fixed finite lateral volume.

Remark 4.12

(Use in the next sections)
.

The regulated identity (
4.24
) is the starting
point for the next two sections, which identify the physical Maxwell trace and
evaluate its standard parallel-plate interaction finite part.

5
The TE/TM trace decomposition

The previous section proved the regulated Maxwell quadratic-form identity

\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)\qquad(\tau>0)

(5.1)

for the finite slab
\Omega_{L,a}=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a]
. It remains to identify
this physical Maxwell trace with scalar plate traces. This section proves the
finite-volume spectral equivalence

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\simeq L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime},

(5.2)

where
L_{\mathrm{D}}
is the scalar Dirichlet Laplacian in the plate direction and

L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
is the scalar Neumann Laplacian with its constant zero mode removed.
The prime is essential: it corresponds exactly to removing the static normal
Maxwell mode
h_{0}
.
This is the step with no scalar analogue in
[
7
]
. The
scalar paper reduces the plate benchmark to scalar traces; the present section
must prove that the physical Maxwell trace is exactly
L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
, with
the electromagnetic multiplicity coming from Maxwell mode structure rather than
from a hand-inserted factor of two.

The argument is finite-volume and spectral. We do not use a vector potential
or a gauge-fixed determinant. The physical degrees of freedom have already
been built into the electric-field Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, and the Fourier
coefficient lemma in Section
3
identifies
that form domain with the mixed sine/cosine coefficient space. The present
section reorganizes that coefficient-space diagonalization into the standard
TE/TM language and then into scalar Dirichlet and reduced Neumann traces.
The phrase TE/TM is used for the nonzero lateral-momentum blocks; at zero
lateral momentum the labels are noncanonical, and the invariant statement is
the spectral equivalence
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\simeq L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
.

5.1
Scalar comparison operators

Let
L_{\mathrm{D}}
denote the positive scalar Laplacian on
\Omega_{L,a}
with periodic
boundary conditions in the lateral variables and Dirichlet boundary conditions
at
z=0,a
. Thus the separated orthonormal eigenfunctions are

d_{m,n}(r,z):=\phi_{m}(r)s_{n}(z),\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad n\geq 1,

(5.3)

with eigenvalues

\lambda_{m,n}=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2},\qquad k_{m}=\frac{2\pi}{L}m,\quad\nu_{n}=\frac{\pi n}{a}.

(5.4)

Let
L_{\mathrm{N}}
denote the positive scalar Laplacian with periodic lateral
conditions and Neumann boundary conditions at
z=0,a
. Its separated
orthonormal eigenfunctions are

u_{m,n}(r,z):=\phi_{m}(r)c_{n}(z),\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad n\geq 0,

(5.5)

with the same eigenvalues
\lambda_{m,n}=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}
, now allowing

n=0
. The mode
u_{0,0}
is the scalar constant mode and has eigenvalue
zero. We write
L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
for the restriction of
L_{\mathrm{N}}
to

u_{0,0}^{\perp}
. Equivalently,

\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}}):=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\ n\geq 0\\
(m,n)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\right),

(5.6)

whenever the sum is absolutely convergent.

Thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\right),

(5.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\right).

(5.8)

Adding the two traces gives

\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}})=2\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\right).

(5.9)

This is the scalar expression to be matched with the Maxwell trace.

Remark 5.1

(Complex modes and real fields)
.

The paper works over the complex Hilbert space. Thus the Fourier modes

m
and
-m
are distinct basis vectors, as usual in complex spectral
calculus. A real-field formulation would impose the corresponding conjugation
condition on coefficients, but it gives the same spectral multiplicities and
trace formulas.

5.2
Physical Maxwell blocks

We recall the coefficient blocks from Section
3
. For

n\geq 1
, the Maxwell coefficients at fixed
(m,n)
are

(a_{m,n},b_{m,n})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{C}
subject to the divergence constraint

\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}=0.

(5.10)

The corresponding block is

W_{m,n}=\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a-\nu_{n}b=0\right\}.

(5.11)

Since
\nu_{n}>0
, this is a two-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. For

n=0
, the only Maxwell coefficient is the normal coefficient
b_{m,0}
.
The branch
(m,n)=(0,0)
is absent from
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, because it is the removed
static mode
h_{0}
. Thus for
m\neq 0
the
n=0
block is one-dimensional.

By Proposition
3.6
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
acts as scalar
multiplication by
\lambda_{m,n}
on each two-dimensional block
W_{m,n}
,

n\geq 1
, and by
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
on each one-dimensional
n=0
block,

m\neq 0
. The present section chooses explicit orthonormal bases for these
blocks. These bases are the finite-volume TE and TM modes.

5.3
TE and TM modes for nonzero lateral momentum

Assume first that
m\neq 0
, so that
k_{m}\neq 0
. Define the lateral unit
vectors

\widehat{k}:=\frac{k_{m}}{\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert},\qquad\widehat{k}^{\perp}:=\frac{1}{\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert}(-k_{m,2},k_{m,1}).

(5.12)

Here
k_{m}=(k_{m,1},k_{m,2})
. For
n\geq 1
, define the TE mode

E^{\mathrm{TE}}_{m,n}(r,z):=\phi_{m}(r)s_{n}(z)\,\widehat{k}^{\perp}.

(5.13)

It has coefficient vector

a_{m,n}=\widehat{k}^{\perp},\qquad b_{m,n}=0.

(5.14)

The divergence constraint holds because
k_{m}\cdot\widehat{k}^{\perp}=0
. The PEC
condition holds because the tangential components are proportional to
s_{n}
,
which vanishes at the plates.

Define the TM mode by

E^{\mathrm{TM}}_{m,n}(r,z):=\phi_{m}(r)\left[-\mathrm{i}\frac{\nu_{n}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{m,n}}}\,\widehat{k}\,s_{n}(z)+\frac{\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert}{\sqrt{\lambda_{m,n}}}\,e_{z}\,c_{n}(z)\right].

(5.15)

Its coefficient vector is

a_{m,n}=-\mathrm{i}\frac{\nu_{n}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{m,n}}}\,\widehat{k},\qquad b_{m,n}=\frac{\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert}{\sqrt{\lambda_{m,n}}}.

(5.16)

The divergence constraint is

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert\left(-\mathrm{i}\frac{\nu_{n}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{m,n}}}\right)-\nu_{n}\frac{\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert}{\sqrt{\lambda_{m,n}}}

\displaystyle=0.

Again the tangential components are proportional to
s_{n}
, so the PEC
condition is satisfied.

The two modes (
5.13
) and
(
5.15
) are orthonormal. The TE mode has norm one.
The TM mode has norm

\frac{\nu_{n}^{2}}{\lambda_{m,n}}+\frac{\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}}{\lambda_{m,n}}=1,

(5.17)

using
\lambda_{m,n}=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}
. Their inner product is zero because

\widehat{k}^{\perp}\cdot\widehat{k}=0
and the TE mode has no normal component. Hence

E^{\mathrm{TE}}_{m,n}
and
E^{\mathrm{TM}}_{m,n}
form an orthonormal basis
of the two-dimensional block
W_{m,n}
for
m\neq 0
,
n\geq 1
.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is scalar multiplication by
\lambda_{m,n}
on the entire block

W_{m,n}
, both modes are eigenvectors:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}E^{\mathrm{TE}}_{m,n}=\lambda_{m,n}E^{\mathrm{TE}}_{m,n},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}E^{\mathrm{TM}}_{m,n}=\lambda_{m,n}E^{\mathrm{TM}}_{m,n}.

(5.18)

5.4
The zero lateral-momentum blocks

When
m=0
and
n\geq 1
, there is no distinguished lateral direction

\widehat{k}
. The divergence constraint reduces to

-\nu_{n}b_{0,n}=0,

(5.19)

so
b_{0,n}=0
, while the two tangential coefficients are free. A convenient
orthonormal basis is

E^{x}_{0,n}(r,z):=\phi_{0}(r)s_{n}(z)e_{x},\qquad E^{y}_{0,n}(r,z):=\phi_{0}(r)s_{n}(z)e_{y}.

(5.20)

Both modes are divergence-free, satisfy the PEC condition, and have eigenvalue

\lambda_{0,n}=\nu_{n}^{2}.

(5.21)

The TE/TM labels are not canonical at zero lateral momentum, but the physical
multiplicity is still exactly two.

5.5
The normal zero-vertical branch

For
n=0
, there is no tangential sine mode. For each
m\neq 0
, define

E^{0}_{m}(r,z):=\phi_{m}(r)c_{0}(z)e_{z}.

(5.22)

This field is divergence-free because
\partial_{z}c_{0}=0
, and it satisfies

n\times E=0
because it is purely normal to the plates. Its eigenvalue is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}E^{0}_{m}=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}E^{0}_{m}.

(5.23)

The missing
m=0
member of this branch is
c_{0}e_{z}
, equivalently

h_{0}
, the removed static normal mode. Thus the branch contributes one
physical mode for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.

5.6
Completeness and unitary equivalence

The preceding modes form a complete orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
. Indeed,
for
m\neq 0
,
n\geq 1
, the pair

E^{\mathrm{TE}}_{m,n},E^{\mathrm{TM}}_{m,n}
is an orthonormal basis of the
block
W_{m,n}
. For
m=0
,
n\geq 1
, the pair

E^{x}_{0,n},E^{y}_{0,n}
is an orthonormal basis of the corresponding
block. For
n=0
, the vectors
E^{0}_{m}
,
m\neq 0
, span the one-dimensional
normal blocks. The Fourier coefficient characterization of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
is the
orthogonal Hilbert direct sum of precisely these blocks, with the static

(m,n)=(0,0)
normal block removed.

This gives an explicit unitary equivalence with
L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
. Let

\mathcal{H}_{D}:=L^{2}(\Omega_{L,a})
be the scalar Dirichlet Hilbert space and let

\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}:=u_{0,0}^{\perp}\subset L^{2}(\Omega_{L,a})
be the reduced Neumann
Hilbert space. Define a unitary map

\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{H}_{D}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{N}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}

(5.24)

first on the separated scalar eigenbasis by the following rules.

For
m\neq 0
,
n\geq 1
, set

\mathcal{U}(d_{m,n},0)=E^{\mathrm{TE}}_{m,n},\qquad\mathcal{U}(0,u_{m,n})=E^{\mathrm{TM}}_{m,n}.

(5.25)

For
m=0
,
n\geq 1
, set

\mathcal{U}(d_{0,n},0)=E^{x}_{0,n},\qquad\mathcal{U}(0,u_{0,n})=E^{y}_{0,n}.

(5.26)

For the reduced Neumann
n=0
branch, set

\mathcal{U}(0,u_{m,0})=E^{0}_{m},\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.

(5.27)

Because both sides are mapped between complete orthonormal eigenbases, this
extends uniquely to a unitary operator. Moreover, it intertwines the scalar
direct-sum operator and the Maxwell operator:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}(L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime})\quad\text{on the operator domain of }L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}.

(5.28)

Equivalently,

\boxed{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\simeq L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}.}

(5.29)

Remark 5.2

(What is and is not canonical)
.

For
m\neq 0
, the labels TE and TM are tied to the lateral direction
k_{m}
,
and the split in (
5.25
) is canonical up to
phase conventions. For
m=0
, there is no lateral direction, so the split
(
5.26
) is only a convenient choice of basis in
a two-dimensional degenerate eigenspace. The trace identity below is
independent of that choice.

5.7
Trace identity

The unitary equivalence immediately gives the trace identity. We state it in
a form adapted to the later regulated and finite-part calculations.

Theorem 5.3

(Maxwell trace as Dirichlet plus reduced Neumann)
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
be Borel. Suppose that the scalar sums in
(
5.7
) and (
5.8
)
converge absolutely. Then

\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}})=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}).}

(5.30)

Equivalently,

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=2\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}f\left(\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\right).

(5.31)

Proof.

By (
5.29
), functional calculus gives

f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=\mathcal{U}\bigl(f(L_{\mathrm{D}})\oplus f(L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime})\bigr)\mathcal{U}^{-1}

whenever these spectral operators are trace class. Taking traces gives
(
5.30
). Expanding the scalar traces
using (
5.7
) and
(
5.8
) gives
(
5.31
). The absolute-convergence
hypothesis justifies taking the traces as sums over the displayed eigenbases.
∎

For the heat-regularized functions used in this paper, the hypotheses of
Theorem
5.3
are satisfied by the
heat-trace admissibility proved in Proposition
3.9
. In particular, for every
\tau>0
,

f_{\tau}(\lambda):=\lambda^{1/2}e^{-\tau\lambda}

(5.32)

is trace-admissible, and

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(L_{\mathrm{D}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau L_{\mathrm{D}}}\right)+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}\left(L_{\mathrm{N}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau L_{\mathrm{N}}}\right).

(5.33)

Combining this with Theorem
4.9
gives

\boxed{\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\left[\operatorname{Tr}\left(L_{\mathrm{D}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau L_{\mathrm{D}}}\right)+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}\left(L_{\mathrm{N}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau L_{\mathrm{N}}}\right)\right].}

(5.34)

5.8
Explicit finite-volume sum

Using (
5.31
) with

f=f_{\tau}
, the regulated Maxwell trace can be written as

\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\left[2\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m,n}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\lambda_{m,n}}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert e^{-\tau\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}}\right].

(5.35)

The first double sum is the combined contribution of the two physical
polarizations for every nonzero vertical mode. The final sum is the reduced
Neumann
n=0
branch. It is a genuine finite-volume Maxwell branch, not an
error or an extra scalar channel. Its dependence on
a
is absent, and that
fact is used only later, when the standard parallel-plate interaction finite
part is taken.

Remark 5.4

(No scalar doubling shortcut)
.

The factor of two in the first term of
(
5.35
) has not been inserted by
hand. It comes from the two-dimensional divergence-free Maxwell block

W_{m,n}
for each
n\geq 1
, together with the PEC boundary condition. The
additional
n=0
branch and the removed static mode are both visible in the
operator spectrum. Thus (
5.30
) is a
Maxwell trace identity, not a scalar two-channel assumption.

Remark 5.5

(Use in the next section)
.

This decomposition rewrites the regulated stochastic Maxwell expectation as
scalar Dirichlet and reduced Neumann traces. The next section evaluates their
standard parallel-plate interaction finite parts.

6
Finite part and the electromagnetic plate coefficient

The preceding sections proved the finite-volume representation

\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)

(6.1)

and the finite-volume spectral identity

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}}).

(6.2)

The one-channel scalar Dirichlet finite part used below is the same standard
parallel-plate scalar input that appears in
[
7
]
. The
new point is how that scalar input enters the Maxwell problem: through the
exact decomposition
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}})
, together with
the
a
-independent reduced Neumann branch.
This section evaluates the standard parallel-plate interaction finite part of
that trace. The order of operations is fixed throughout:

\displaystyle L<\infty

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\quad\text{regulated identity}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\text{energy density at fixed }\tau

(6.3)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\quad L\to\infty\quad\longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}.

We do not take a large-area limit of the bounded inverse
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
. The
large-area limit is taken only after the heat-regularized trace density has
been formed.

6.1
The regulated finite-box Maxwell trace

Recall that

\lambda_{m,n}=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2},\qquad k_{m}=\frac{2\pi}{L}m,\qquad\nu_{n}=\frac{\pi n}{a}.

(6.4)

Define the one-channel scalar Dirichlet regulated energy in the finite box by

E_{D,\tau}(L,a):=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m,n}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\lambda_{m,n}}.

(6.5)

Define also the reduced Neumann zero-vertical branch

B_{\tau}(L):=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert e^{-\tau\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}}.

(6.6)

The explicit trace formula of Section
5
, namely
(
5.35
), is then

\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}(L,a)]=2E_{D,\tau}(L,a)+B_{\tau}(L).

(6.7)

Equivalently, if

E_{N,\tau}^{\prime}(L,a):=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}\left(L_{\mathrm{N}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau L_{\mathrm{N}}}\right),

(6.8)

then the reduced Neumann identity is

E_{N,\tau}^{\prime}(L,a)=E_{D,\tau}(L,a)+B_{\tau}(L),

(6.9)

and

\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}(L,a)]=E_{D,\tau}(L,a)+E_{N,\tau}^{\prime}(L,a).

(6.10)

The term
B_{\tau}(L)
is not discarded at the regulated finite-volume level.
It is a genuine Maxwell branch. Its special role is that it has no dependence
on the plate separation
a
.

6.2
The interaction finite part

The quantity computed below is the standard parallel-plate interaction energy
per unit area. This fixes the finite-part convention used in this section.

Definition 6.1

(Parallel-plate interaction finite part)
.

For a regulated large-area energy density
F_{\tau}(a)
, the notation

\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}F_{\tau}(a)

means the following operation: subtract the bulk vacuum terms, the separate
one-plate self-energy terms, and all terms independent of the separation

a
, and retain the finite separation-dependent part. After these standard
bulk and one-plate subtractions have been made, the remaining finite part
determines the finite plate-separation force

-\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}F_{\tau}(a).

Thus an
a
-independent regulated density has zero interaction finite part.

This convention is weaker than, and different from, assigning an absolute
renormalized vacuum energy. It fixes the physically relevant plate
interaction and leaves no freedom to keep an arbitrary constant independent of

a
.

The branch
B_{\tau}(L)
is exactly of this removable type. For fixed

\tau>0
, the Riemann-sum limit gives

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{B_{\tau}(L)}{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left\lvert k\right\rvert e^{-\tau\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}}

(6.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}k^{2}e^{-\tau k^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}k

(6.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{16\sqrt{\pi}}\,\tau^{-3/2}.

(6.13)

The right-hand side has no dependence on
a
. Therefore

\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{B_{\tau}(L)}{L^{2}}\right]=0.

(6.14)

Consequently,

\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}(L,a)]\right]=2\,\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{E_{D,\tau}(L,a)}{L^{2}}\right].

(6.15)

Thus the remaining calculation is the one-channel scalar Dirichlet plate
finite part.

6.3
The scalar Dirichlet finite part

For completeness, we recall the standard zeta extraction of the one-channel
Dirichlet interaction finite part. At fixed
\tau>0
, the large-area limit
of
L^{-2}E_{D,\tau}(L,a)
is

\mathcal{E}_{D,\tau}(a)=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}+\left(\frac{\pi n}{a}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}e^{-\tau\left(\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}+(\pi n/a)^{2}\right)}.

(6.16)

The corresponding spectral zeta density is initially defined for

\mathrm{Re}\,s>3/2
by

Z_{D}(s,a):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}+\left(\frac{\pi n}{a}\right)^{2}\right)^{-s}.

(6.17)

Equivalently, the energy zeta regularization may be written, for

\mathrm{Re}\,s>2
, as

\mathcal{E}_{D}(s,a):=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\,\mu^{2s}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}+\left(\frac{\pi n}{a}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2-s}.

(6.18)

Here
\mu
is an arbitrary reference scale inserted only to keep dimensions
fixed away from
s=0
. The standard dimensional integral gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}+M^{2}\right)^{1/2-s}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\Gamma(s-3/2)}{\Gamma(s-1/2)}M^{3-2s},

(6.19)

by analytic continuation from the convergent half-plane. Therefore

\mathcal{E}_{D}(s,a)=\frac{\hbar c}{8\pi}\mu^{2s}\frac{\Gamma(s-3/2)}{\Gamma(s-1/2)}\left(\frac{\pi}{a}\right)^{3-2s}\zeta(2s-3).

(6.20)

This meromorphic expression is regular at
s=0
. Since

\frac{\Gamma(-3/2)}{\Gamma(-1/2)}=-\frac{2}{3},\qquad\zeta(-3)=\frac{1}{120},

(6.21)

one obtains

\mathcal{E}_{D}(0,a)=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}}.

(6.22)

This is the standard one-channel scalar Dirichlet parallel-plate interaction
finite part:

\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}_{D,\tau}(a)=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}}.

(6.23)

Remark 6.2

(Heat regulator and zeta extraction)
.

The stochastic representation uses the heat cutoff

\lambda^{1/2}e^{-\tau\lambda}
, whereas
(
6.18
) uses analytic continuation. These
extract the same interaction finite part. Indeed, Mellin inversion gives,
for
\gamma
in the convergent half-plane,

\mathcal{E}_{D,\tau}(a)=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\gamma-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\gamma+\mathrm{i}\infty}\Gamma(z)\tau^{-z}Z_{D}(z-1/2,a)\,\,\mathrm{d}z.

(6.24)

Shifting the contour gives the short-time expansion. The poles away from

z=0
produce the local divergent terms. The residue at
z=0
from

\Gamma(z)
is exactly
(\hbar c/2)Z_{D}(-1/2,a)=\mathcal{E}_{D}(0,a)
, which is the finite separation-dependent term displayed
in (
6.22
). This is the sense in
which (
6.23
) is a heat finite part and
not a change of regulator.

6.4
The reduced Neumann finite part

The reduced Neumann channel differs from the Dirichlet channel only by the
zero-vertical branch
B_{\tau}(L)
. Combining
(
6.9
) with
(
6.14
) gives

\displaystyle\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{E_{N,\tau}^{\prime}(L,a)}{L^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{E_{D,\tau}(L,a)}{L^{2}}\right]

(6.25)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}}.

(6.26)

Thus the reduced scalar Neumann sector has the same interaction finite part as
the scalar Dirichlet sector. The equality is not a statement that the raw
regulated traces are identical; their difference is the real branch

B_{\tau}(L)
, which is independent of
a
.

6.5
The Maxwell plate result

We now combine the preceding identities. From
(
6.10
), the Maxwell interaction
finite part per unit area is the sum of the Dirichlet and reduced Neumann
interaction finite parts. Therefore,

\displaystyle\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}(L,a)]\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}}-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}}

(6.27)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{720a^{3}}.

(6.28)

Theorem 6.3

(Maxwell parallel-plate finite part)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
be the reduced Maxwell operator on

\Omega_{L,a}=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a],

with perfect-conductor boundary conditions: it is defined on divergence-free
electric fields with

on the plates, and the static normal zero
mode is removed. Let
\Sigma_{\tau}
be the

Section

,
all objects in the expectation below are the finite-volume objects associated

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}(L,a)

\boxed{\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Sigma_{\tau},g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle\right]\right]=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{720a^{3}}.}

(6.29)

Proof.

For finite

gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Sigma_{\tau},g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}\Sigma_{\tau}\right\rangle\right]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

) and

∎

Remark 6.4

(Dependence of the calculation)

The factor of two in (

7
Discussion and limitations

The preceding sections prove a Maxwell version of the scalar
codimension-three Riesz/Gaussian representation in the parallel-plate geometry.
The result is narrow but complete within that scope. Starting from the
physical perfect-conductor Maxwell operator on divergence-free electric fields,
we obtain a finite-volume Riesz-reduced Green operator, a heat-regularized
Gaussian source with prescribed covariance, a regulated quadratic-form trace
identity, an exact TE/TM trace decomposition, and finally the standard
parallel-plate interaction finite part

-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{720a^{3}}.

The final coefficient is the standard electromagnetic parallel-plate value.
The contribution of the theorem is the operator-theoretic representation that
produces this value from the reduced Maxwell Hilbert space for perfectly
conducting plates.
The factor of two in the final coefficient is not inserted by hand. It comes
from the spectral equivalence

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\simeq L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime},

proved in Theorem
5.3
. The reduced
Neumann zero-vertical branch is present in the finite-volume Maxwell trace and
is discarded only at the interaction finite-part stage because it is
independent of the plate separation.
Read together with
[
7
]
, the present paper separates the
program into two levels. The scalar preprint isolates the codimension-three
Riesz/Gaussian trace mechanism and records the scalar Dirichlet plate
benchmark. The present paper shows that the same mechanism extends to the
genuine Maxwell parallel-plate operator once the physical Hilbert space,
perfect-conductor boundary condition, static zero mode, and TE/TM decomposition
are treated directly.

7.1
What the theorem establishes

The first established point is the operator construction. The Maxwell operator
is not introduced by a formal differential expression alone. It is defined by
the closed form

q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E,F]=\int_{\Omega_{L,a}}(\nabla\times E)\cdot\overline{(\nabla\times F)}\,\,\mathrm{d}x

on the reduced physical form domain
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
. The boundary condition

n\times E=0
and the constraint
\nabla\cdot E=0
are built into this
form domain. The static normal field is removed before the inverse is used.
Thus the operator entering the Riesz identity is a positive self-adjoint
finite-volume Maxwell operator with a genuine spectral gap.

The second established point is spectral admissibility. The mixed sine/cosine
Fourier characterization of the form domain shows that the spectrum is
discrete in finite volume and that

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{p}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right)<\infty\qquad(\tau>0,\ p\geq 0).

This justifies the heat-regularized Gaussian source as an honest Hilbert-valued
random variable and makes the covariance trace-class.

The third established point is the codimension-three Riesz reduction on the
Maxwell Hilbert space. Since the proof uses only the spectral theorem for a
positive self-adjoint operator with a lower bound, it applies to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
just
as it applies to a scalar operator:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}+\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})^{-5/2}=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}.

With
g=\kappa/(6\pi^{2})
, this gives the finite-volume mediator

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Mx}}=g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
.

The fourth established point is the stochastic trace identity. For

\Sigma_{\tau}=\left(\frac{\hbar c}{g}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/4}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}/2}\Xi,

the covariance is

C_{\tau}=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}.

Pairing this covariance with the Green operator
g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
gives

g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}C_{\tau}=\hbar c\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}},

and therefore

\mathbb{E}[U_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Mx}}]=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

The fifth established point is the plate finite part. The Maxwell trace is
Dirichlet plus reduced Neumann. The reduced Neumann trace differs from the
Dirichlet trace by the zero-vertical branch

B_{\tau}(L)=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\sum_{m\neq 0}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert e^{-\tau\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}},

which carries no
a
-dependence. Under the standard parallel-plate
interaction finite-part prescription, this branch contributes zero. The two
remaining scalar interaction finite parts are equal, each giving

-\pi^{2}\hbar c/(1440a^{3})
, and their sum gives the electromagnetic coefficient.

7.2
Why the electric-field formulation was used

The main proof avoids vector-potential gauge issues by working directly with
physical electric fields. Vector-potential and covariant gauge-fixed
formulations may also be used, but the present choice of variables makes the
Hilbert-space statement precise. Nonzero Maxwell modes may be related to
temporal/Coulomb-gauge vector-potential modes, but that relationship is not
needed for the proof of the spectral trace identity.
Appendix
C
records the limited gauge-theoretic
interpretation and explicitly separates it from a full covariant determinant
calculation.

The important consequence is that no Faddeev–Popov cancellation is hidden in
the main argument. The trace is taken directly over the physical electric-field
Hilbert space after the static zero mode has been removed. If one chooses a
covariant gauge-fixed formulation instead, one must independently show that the
vector and ghost determinants reduce to the same physical trace. The present
paper does not use that determinant formulation; it works directly with the
physical electric-field trace.

7.3
What the theorem does not establish

The covariance is prescribed. The construction does not show that

C_{\tau}=\frac{\hbar c}{g}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}

is forced by microscopic electrodynamics. It shows that if this
heat-regularized covariance is assigned on the reduced Maxwell Hilbert space,
then the expected quadratic Green energy is exactly the heat-regularized
Maxwell trace. Deriving the covariance from a photon path integral, a
worldline representation, or an effective action with matter fields would be a
different project.

The ambient product operator is also not a local higher-dimensional Maxwell
operator. It is the product spectral operator

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}\otimes I+I\otimes(-\Delta_{y})

used to define a fractional Riesz-type mediator. The transverse restriction is
implemented through a transverse momentum integral, or equivalently through a
mollified transverse distributional limit. No point-evaluation map on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{y})
is used, and no ordinary six-dimensional Maxwell propagator is
claimed.

The result is also not a statement about arbitrary conductor geometries. The
proof uses the product slab
T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a]
, lateral Fourier modes, and
the explicit sine/cosine decomposition induced by flat plates. Non-planar
geometries require new spectral analysis. In particular, conducting spherical
shells, curved boundaries, and edge geometries are outside the theorem.

Finally, this paper does not use or justify any reference-cell calibration,
cube extremality argument, loop correction, running coupling, or
fundamental-constant identification. Those topics would require additional
normalizations or additional physics not present in the theorem proved here.
Those calibration questions are treated separately in

[
7
]
and are not part of the Maxwell theorem proved here.

7.4
Order of limits and finite parts

The order of operations is essential:

\displaystyle L<\infty

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\quad\text{regulated operator identity}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\text{trace density at fixed }\tau

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\quad L\to\infty\quad\longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}.

For finite
L
, the reduced operator has a bounded inverse. In the
large-area limit, the spectral gap collapses because the zero-vertical branch
has eigenvalues
\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}
with
k_{m}\to 0
. Thus it would be incorrect
to treat
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
as a bounded infinite-area operator. The theorem avoids
this by proving the stochastic identity in finite volume and taking the
large-area limit only at the level of the heat-regularized trace density. The
interaction finite part is then applied to that large-area density.

The finite part used in the final result is the standard interaction finite
part. It removes bulk terms, one-plate self-energies, and terms independent of
the separation
a
, retaining the finite separation-dependent interaction
energy. This convention is not optional bookkeeping: it is what permits the
real but
a
-independent branch
B_{\tau}(L)
to be discarded in the
interaction energy without discarding it from the finite-volume Maxwell trace.

7.5
Beyond parallel plates

Non-planar conductor geometries require separate spectral analysis. In such
geometries the physical Maxwell Hilbert space, boundary conditions, zero modes,
and TE/TM or vector-spherical spectral structure must be treated anew. The
present result should therefore be read as a parallel-plate theorem.

Thus the correct conclusion is deliberately modest. The parallel-plate Maxwell
operator passes the Riesz/Gaussian representation test, and the standard
interaction finite part is the electromagnetic plate coefficient. No broader
geometric universality or microscopic QED derivation follows from this result
alone.

7.6
Conclusion

The theorem proved in this paper can be summarized as follows. On the reduced
physical Maxwell Hilbert space for perfectly conducting parallel plates, the
codimension-three transverse reduction produces the Green operator

g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
. With the prescribed heat-regularized covariance

(\hbar c/g)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{3/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}
, the expected quadratic Green energy is
exactly

\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right).

The physical Maxwell trace is
L_{\mathrm{D}}\oplus L_{\mathrm{N}}^{\prime}
, and the standard
parallel-plate interaction finite part is

-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{720a^{3}}.

The relation to the companion scalar preprint
[
7
]
is
therefore straightforward: that paper established the scalar codimension-three
Riesz/Gaussian representation scheme and its one-channel Dirichlet
parallel-plate specialization, while the present paper extends the same scheme
to the reduced physical Maxwell operator and derives the electromagnetic plate
coefficient from the actual Maxwell spectrum.
This completes the Maxwell parallel-plate version of the codimension-three
Riesz/Gaussian trace representation.

Appendix A
Details of the Fourier coefficient-domain lemma

This appendix gives a detailed proof of the coefficient-domain statement used in
Section
3
. The point is to connect the
closed-form definition of the Maxwell operator in Section
2

with the separated Fourier calculation. No general Maxwell compactness theorem
is used here; the proof is specific to the finite periodic slab

\Omega_{L,a}=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a].

The convention from Section
2
is important:

H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3})
denotes the closure, in the

H(\operatorname{curl})
graph norm, of smooth lateral-periodic fields whose
tangential trace vanishes on the two plates. Thus the perfect-conductor
condition is imposed at the form-domain level.

A.1
Mixed Fourier expansions

Let

k_{m}:=\frac{2\pi}{L}m,\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\qquad\nu_{n}:=\frac{\pi n}{a},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

and define

\phi_{m}(r):=L^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot r},\qquad r=(x,y)\in T_{L}^{2}.

For the plate direction set

s_{n}(z):=\sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\sin(\nu_{n}z),\qquad n\geq 1,

and

c_{0}(z):=a^{-1/2},\qquad c_{n}(z):=\sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\cos(\nu_{n}z),\qquad n\geq 1.

The lateral modes
\{\phi_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
are an orthonormal basis of

L^{2}(T_{L}^{2})
, the sine modes
\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
are an orthonormal basis of

L^{2}(0,a)
, and the cosine modes
\{c_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
are another orthonormal
basis of
L^{2}(0,a)
.

Consequently every
L^{2}
vector field has the mixed expansion

\displaystyle E_{\parallel}(r,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{m,n}\,\phi_{m}(r)s_{n}(z),\qquad a_{m,n}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

(A.1)

\displaystyle E_{z}(r,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}b_{m,n}\,\phi_{m}(r)c_{n}(z),\qquad b_{m,n}\in\mathbb{C}.

(A.2)

Here
E_{\parallel}=(E_{x},E_{y})
. Parseval gives

\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{L,a})}^{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}.

(A.3)

The use of sine modes for the tangential components is compatible with the
condition
E_{x}=E_{y}=0
on
z=0,a
for finite trigonometric sums. For general

L^{2}
fields the boundary condition is not read pointwise from the series; it
is read through the graph-norm closure defining
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
.

For
n\geq 1
, put

\lambda_{m,n}:=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}+\nu_{n}^{2}.

(A.4)

A.2
The coefficient spaces

Define
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
to be the space of coefficient families

(a,b)
satisfying

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}(1+\lambda_{m,n})\left(\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(1+\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2})\left\lvert b_{m,0}\right\rvert^{2}<\infty

(A.5)

and, for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
n\geq 1
,

\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}=0.

(A.6)

The reduced physical coefficient space is

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{phys}}:=\left\{(a,b)\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}:b_{0,0}=0\right\}.

(A.7)

Lemma A.1

(Detailed Fourier characterization)
.

The reconstruction map (
A.1
)–(
A.2
)
identifies
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
with

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}=\left\{E\in H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})(\Omega_{L,a};\mathbb{C}^{3}):\nabla\cdot E=0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega_{L,a})\right\}.

For
E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
, the curl norm is

\left\lVert\nabla\times E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{m,n}\left(\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert b_{m,0}\right\rvert^{2}.

(A.8)

Moreover, imposing
b_{0,0}=0
identifies
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{phys}}
with

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}.

Proof.

We split the proof into four elementary steps.

Step 1: divergence coefficients.

For a finite trigonometric field of the form
(
A.1
)–(
A.2
), direct
differentiation gives

\nabla\cdot E=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}\right)\phi_{m}(r)s_{n}(z).

(A.9)

There is no contribution from
b_{m,0}
, since
c_{0}^{\prime}(z)=0
. Thus the
constraint (
A.6
) is exactly the Fourier form of

\nabla\cdot E=0
.

Step 2: curl coefficients and the finite-dimensional identity.

For
n\geq 1
, the curl coefficients are

\displaystyle C^{x}_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}k_{m,y}b_{m,n}-\nu_{n}a^{y}_{m,n},

\displaystyle C^{y}_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\nu_{n}a^{x}_{m,n}-\mathrm{i}k_{m,x}b_{m,n},

(A.10)

\displaystyle C^{z}_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\left(k_{m,x}a^{y}_{m,n}-k_{m,y}a^{x}_{m,n}\right).

For the normal branch
n=0
,

C^{x}_{m,0}=\mathrm{i}k_{m,y}b_{m,0},\qquad C^{y}_{m,0}=-\mathrm{i}k_{m,x}b_{m,0},\qquad C^{z}_{m,0}=0.

(A.11)

For
n\geq 1
, let

D_{m,n}:=\mathrm{i}k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}-\nu_{n}b_{m,n}.

A direct expansion gives the identity

\displaystyle\left\lvert C^{x}_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert C^{y}_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert C^{z}_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert D_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}

(A.12)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt=\lambda_{m,n}\left(\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}\right).

One way to check the cancellation is to observe that the terms involving

b_{m,n}\overline{a^{x}_{m,n}}
and
b_{m,n}\overline{a^{y}_{m,n}}
occur with
opposite signs in the curl part and in
\left\lvert D_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}
. The remaining
terms use the two-dimensional identity

\left\lvert k_{m}\cdot a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert k_{m,x}a^{y}_{m,n}-k_{m,y}a^{x}_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}=\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2},

valid for complex
a_{m,n}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and real
k_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Step 3: from coefficients to the form domain.

Let
(a,b)\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
. Let
E^{(N)}
be any increasing
sequence of finite rectangular truncations of
(
A.1
)–(
A.2
). Each truncation is
smooth, lateral-periodic, and has tangential components built from sine modes,
so

n\times E^{(N)}=0\qquad\text{on }z=0,a.

The coefficient constraint (
A.6
) is preserved under
truncation, hence
\nabla\cdot E^{(N)}=0
. By
(
A.12
) with
D_{m,n}=0
and by
(
A.11
), the summability condition
(
A.5
) implies that
E^{(N)}
is Cauchy in the

H(\operatorname{curl})
graph norm. Since
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
is the graph-norm
closure of smooth PEC fields, the limit belongs to
H_{0}(\operatorname{curl})
. The divergence
constraint passes to the limit distributionally. Therefore the reconstructed
field belongs to
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
. This proves

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}.

Step 4: from the form domain to coefficients.

Conversely, let
E\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
. Its mixed Fourier coefficients are defined by
(
A.1
)–(
A.2
). Since

\nabla\cdot E=0
in distributions, testing against

\overline{\phi_{m}}s_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, gives precisely
(
A.6
). No endpoint term arises in this test because

s_{n}(0)=s_{n}(a)=0
.

The weak curl
\nabla\times E
is in
L^{2}
. Its Fourier coefficients are the
distributional derivatives displayed in
(
A.2
)–(
A.11
). These
formulas are obtained by differentiating finite partial sums, testing against
the mixed sine/cosine modes, and passing to the distributional limit. Since

\nabla\times E\in L^{2}
, uniqueness of Fourier coefficients identifies the

L^{2}
-Fourier coefficients of the weak curl with the displayed expressions.
Parseval
therefore gives

\sum\left\lvert C\right\rvert^{2}<\infty.

Using (
A.12
) and the fact that

D_{m,n}=0
, we obtain

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{m,n}\left(\left\lvert a_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert b_{m,n}\right\rvert^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\lvert k_{m}\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert b_{m,0}\right\rvert^{2}<\infty.

Together with the
L^{2}
Parseval identity (
A.3
), this is
exactly (
A.5
). Hence the coefficient family of

E
lies in
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}
, and the curl norm is
(
A.8
). Thus

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{PEC}}\subset\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{PEC}}.

The two inclusions prove the identification.

Finally, the mode
b_{0,0}
reconstructs the vector field

b_{0,0}\phi_{0}(r)c_{0}(z)e_{z}=b_{0,0}(L^{2}a)^{-1/2}e_{z}=b_{0,0}h_{0}.

Thus the condition
b_{0,0}=0
is exactly orthogonality to the static normal
mode
h_{0}
. This proves the reduced statement.
∎

Appendix B
Heat cutoff, zeta extraction, and the plate finite part

The stochastic identity in the main text uses the heat cutoff

\lambda^{1/2}e^{-\tau\lambda},

whereas the scalar parallel-plate coefficient is often recalled by analytic
continuation of a spectral zeta function; see, for example,

[

5

-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}}

for one scalar Dirichlet channel. The reduced scalar Neumann channel has the
same interaction finite part; its difference from the Dirichlet channel is an

a
-independent zero-vertical branch.

B.1
The large-area scalar Dirichlet trace

Let the plate separation be
a>0

\mathcal{E}_{D,\tau}(a):=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}+\left(\frac{\pi n}{a}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}e^{-\tau\left(\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}+(\pi n/a)^{2}\right)}.

(B.1)

For
\Re w>3/2
, define the scalar Dirichlet spectral zeta density

Z_{D}(w,a):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}+\left(\frac{\pi n}{a}\right)^{2}\right)^{-w}.

(B.2)

The heat trace (
B.1

Proposition B.1

(Mellin representation of the heat cutoff)
.

Let
\gamma>2
. Then for every

\mathcal{E}_{D,\tau}(a)=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\,\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\gamma-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\gamma+\mathrm{i}\infty}\Gamma(z)\tau^{-z}Z_{D}\left(z-\frac{1}{2},a\right)\,\,\mathrm{d}z.

(B.3)

Proof.

For
\gamma>2
, the exponent

has real part greater than
3/2
,
so the defining sum and integral for

are absolutely
convergent on the vertical line. The scalar Mellin inversion formula

integral with the sum over
n
and the integral over

. This gives
(
B.3
).
∎

B.2
Analytic continuation of the Dirichlet zeta density

The two-dimensional momentum integral gives the meromorphic continuation

Proposition B.2

(Dirichlet zeta density)

The function

Equivalently,

Proof.

B.3
Heat asymptotics and the interaction finite part

We now extract the same value directly from the heat cutoff. Insert

\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}_{D,\tau}(a)=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}}.

(B.12)

Proof.

Starting from (
B.3

, with residue one. Therefore
the constant term is

\frac{\hbar c}{2}Z_{D}\left(-\frac{1}{2},a\right)=\mathcal{E}_{D}(0,a)=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}},

using (
B.10
).

The pole at
z=2
is the bulk vacuum divergence proportional to the volume
per unit area, namely to
a
. The pole at
z=3/2
is the one-plate surface
divergence and is independent of
a
. The standard interaction finite part
subtracts these local terms and retains the finite separation-dependent
constant. This proves (
B.12
).
∎

Remark B.4

(What is meant by heat–zeta compatibility)
.

Equation (
B.12
) is not a claim that arbitrary
finite counterterms are scheme-independent. It says that for the standard
parallel-plate interaction prescription, the finite term extracted from the
heat cutoff
\lambda^{1/2}e^{-\tau\lambda}
agrees with the analytic
continuation value
\mathcal{E}_{D}(0,a)
. This is the compatibility used in

B.4
Reduced Neumann branch

The scalar Neumann comparison operator with its constant zero mode removed has
vertical modes
n\geq 1
, matching the Dirichlet eigenvalues, plus the
zero-vertical branch
n=0
,
m\neq 0
. At the heat-regulated finite-area
level this is exactly the identity

E_{N,\tau}^{\prime}(L,a)=E_{D,\tau}(L,a)+B_{\tau}(L),

with
B_{\tau}(L)
defined in (
6.6
). The large-area
limit of this branch is

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{B_{\tau}(L)}{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left\lvert k\right\rvert e^{-\tau\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}}

(B.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{16\sqrt{\pi}}\tau^{-3/2}.

(B.14)

It has no dependence on the plate separation

. Hence

\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{B_{\tau}(L)}{L^{2}}\right]=0.

(B.15)

Therefore the reduced Neumann scalar channel has the same interaction finite
part as the Dirichlet scalar channel:

\operatorname{FP}_{\mathrm{int},\,\tau\to 0^{+}}\left[\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{E_{N,\tau}^{\prime}(L,a)}{L^{2}}\right]=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar c}{1440a^{3}}.

(B.16)

This is the only Neumann input needed for the Maxwell plate coefficient: the

Remark B.5

(No additional physical assumption)
.

The subtraction of (
B.13
) is not a new assumption about

Appendix C
Gauge-potential formulations and the physical electric-field trace

The main text deliberately avoids using a vector potential as the primary
Hilbert-space variable. Instead it defines the reduced Maxwell operator

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
directly on divergence-free electric fields satisfying the perfect
conductor tangential boundary condition, with the static normal zero mode
removed. This appendix explains how that choice relates to the more familiar
vector-potential language for the nonzero parallel-plate modes. Nothing in the
main proof depends on this appendix.

There are two reasons for keeping the main proof in the electric-field
formulation. First, the physical perfect-conductor boundary condition is
stated directly on the fields:

n\times E=0,\qquad n\cdot B=0\quad\text{on the plates}.

(C.1)

Second, the vector potential has a gauge redundancy. If one uses
A
as the
basic variable, one must either quotient by gauge transformations, impose a
gauge condition and handle residual gauge freedom, or work in a covariant
gauge-fixed formalism with the associated ghost determinant. The electric-field
construction in Sections
2
–
5

works directly with the reduced physical Hilbert space and therefore avoids
introducing gauge variables that later have to be removed.

C.1
Nonzero modes and temporal/Coulomb gauge

We use the time dependence
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}
. For a nonzero-frequency
source-free Maxwell mode in vacuum, Faraday’s law gives

B=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\omega}\,\nabla\times E,

(C.2)

and Ampere’s law gives

\nabla\times\nabla\times E=\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}E,\qquad\nabla\cdot E=0.

(C.3)

Thus the eigenvalue
\lambda
of the spatial operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
is related to
the physical angular frequency by

\omega=c\sqrt{\lambda}.

(C.4)

For the operator-theoretic parts of the paper, the factor
c
is kept outside
the spectral operator and appears in the trace as
\hbar c\sqrt{\lambda}/2
.

For nonzero modes one may choose temporal gauge,
\Phi=0
, so that

E=-\partial_{t}A=\mathrm{i}\omega A.

(C.5)

Since
\omega\neq 0
, this gives a one-to-one correspondence between electric
field modes and vector-potential modes:

A=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\omega}E.

(C.6)

If
\nabla\cdot E=0
, then
\nabla\cdot A=0
. Thus the corresponding
potential is in Coulomb gauge. Moreover, the perfect-conductor condition

n\times E=0
implies

n\times A=0\quad\text{on the plates}.

(C.7)

Conversely, a nonzero-frequency Coulomb-gauge vector-potential mode satisfying

n\times A=0
yields a physical electric mode by
E=\mathrm{i}\omega A
.

The magnetic boundary condition in (
C.1
)
is also compatible with this description. Indeed, on the flat plates

z=0,a
, if the tangential trace of
A
vanishes, then the normal component
of
\nabla\times A
is

n\cdot(\nabla\times A)=\partial_{x}A_{y}-\partial_{y}A_{x},

(C.8)

with the right-hand side understood in the tangential trace sense. Since the
tangential components of
A
vanish on the flat boundary, their tangential
derivatives vanish there in the trace sense for smooth modes, and then by
density for the form-domain modes. Hence

n\cdot B=n\cdot(\nabla\times A)=0.

(C.9)

This is the field-level reason why the boundary condition used in the main
text captures the perfect-conductor plate problem.

Proposition C.1

(Mode-level equivalence for nonzero finite-volume modes)
.

For the finite slab
\Omega_{L,a}=T_{L}^{2}\times[0,a]
, the nonzero eigenmodes of

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
on the reduced electric-field Hilbert space are in one-to-one
correspondence with nonzero-frequency Coulomb-gauge vector-potential modes
satisfying
n\times A=0
on the plates. Under this correspondence the
spatial eigenvalue is unchanged.

Proof.

Let
E\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}}
be an eigenmode of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
with eigenvalue

\lambda>0
. Set
\omega=c\sqrt{\lambda}
and define

A=(\mathrm{i}\omega)^{-1}E
. Then
\nabla\cdot A=0
, since

\nabla\cdot E=0
, and
n\times A=0
, since
n\times E=0
. Also

\nabla\times\nabla\times A=\lambda A,

(C.10)

because the equation is obtained from the corresponding eigenvalue equation
for
E
by multiplication by the nonzero scalar
(\mathrm{i}\omega)^{-1}
. Thus

A
is a Coulomb-gauge vector-potential mode with the same spatial
eigenvalue.

Conversely, let
A
be a Coulomb-gauge vector-potential mode satisfying

n\times A=0
and

\nabla\times\nabla\times A=\lambda A
with
\lambda>0
. With

\omega=c\sqrt{\lambda}
, define
E=\mathrm{i}\omega A
. Then

\nabla\cdot E=0
,
n\times E=0
, and

\nabla\times\nabla\times E=\lambda E
. Hence
E
is a nonzero eigenmode
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
in the physical electric-field space. These two constructions are
inverse to each other.
∎

Remark C.2

(The removed static mode)
.

The mode
E=h_{0}
, removed in (
2.5
), has eigenvalue zero and
represents a uniform static normal electric field. It is not a nonzero photon
oscillator and is not covered by the temporal-gauge correspondence above. This
is one reason the finite-volume construction first removes
h_{0}
and only
then forms
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{-1}
.

C.2
Relation to the TE/TM trace

The TE/TM decomposition in Section
5
is not a
gauge artifact. It is a decomposition of the reduced physical Maxwell
Hilbert space itself. For
m\neq 0
and
n\geq 1
, the two modes

E^{\mathrm{TE}}_{m,n}
and
E^{\mathrm{TM}}_{m,n}
form an orthonormal
basis of the two-dimensional divergence-free coefficient block

W_{m,n}
in (
5.11
). For
m=0
, the TE/TM labels are
not canonical, but the physical multiplicity is still two. For
n=0
, the
normal branch contributes one reduced Neumann-type family, with the constant
zero mode removed.

Thus the trace identity

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}f(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}})=\operatorname{Tr}f(L_{\mathrm{D}})+\operatorname{Tr}^{\prime}f(L_{\mathrm{N}})

(C.11)

from Theorem
5.3
is a statement about
physical Maxwell modes. The factor of two comes from the physical Maxwell
mode multiplicities after the unphysical degrees of freedom have been removed.

C.3
What a covariant gauge-fixed calculation would have to show

A covariant path-integral treatment would start from a gauge-fixed action for
a vector potential and would include ghost fields. With boundaries, the gauge
condition, the vector boundary conditions, and the ghost boundary conditions
must be chosen compatibly. That is a different formulation from the one used
in this paper.

For the present flat parallel-plate geometry, such a calculation would have to
recover the same reduced physical trace,

\frac{\hbar c}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{phys}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}}\right),

(C.12)

because after gauge and ghost cancellations only the physical TE and TM
oscillators remain. However, the main text does not need this determinant
identity and does not prove it in general. Instead, it constructs the physical
Hilbert space first and proves the trace identity directly from the Maxwell
mode decomposition.

This distinction is important. The statement proved in this paper is a
Riesz/Gaussian representation on the reduced physical Maxwell space, followed
by the standard parallel-plate interaction finite part. Questions of
covariant path-integral derivation and of other boundary geometries lie beyond
the scope of this appendix.

C.4
Differential-form terminology

In differential-form language, the electric field is a one-form and the
condition
n\times E=0
on a plate is the vanishing of the tangential
pullback of that one-form. This is the relative trace condition for
one-forms. The corresponding operator-domain boundary conditions are encoded
here through the closed curl–curl form rather than imposed separately. The
constraint
\nabla\cdot E=0
is the co-closedness condition.
Thus the main Hilbert space may equivalently be described as the reduced space
of co-closed relative one-forms, with the harmonic relative one-form
corresponding to the constant normal electric field removed.

This form-language description is useful for comparing with other treatments
of Maxwell theory with boundary. It should not obscure the concrete content
of the present proof: in the slab geometry the physical operator is the
positive self-adjoint curl-curl operator defined by the quadratic form

q_{\mathrm{Mx}}[E]=\left\lVert\nabla\times E\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
on

\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}
, and its finite-volume spectrum is the one computed explicitly in
Sections
3
and
5
.

Remark C.3

(No additional input to the main theorem)
.

The discussion in this appendix supplies interpretation only. The proof of

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Mx}}
