Second-order optimality conditions for optimization problems with generalized equation constraints
Matúš Benko, Helmut Gfrerer, Jane J. Ye, Jin Zhang, Jinchuan Zhou

Abstract




Abstract.
This paper provides second-order optimality conditions for optimization problems with generalized equation constraints (GEPs), a framework that encompasses several important and challenging models in mathematical programming, including mathematical programs with variational inequality constraints (MPVIs) and bilevel programs.
The obtained optimality conditions are novel even for these particular problem classes.
As an application, second-order optimality conditions for MPVIs are detailed.
The technical key lies in developing first- and second-order variational analysis of the highly intricate constraint system,
which is needed to capture the local curvature of the feasible set entering these optimality conditions.
Part of this task was already carried out in our companion paper
[
4
]
, and here we complete the study.
Comprehensive variational analysis results are derived, which are of independent interest.

Keywords.
Generalized equation constraints, second-order optimality conditions, variational analysis, mathematical programs with variational inequality constraints, bilevel programming.

AMS subject classifications.
49J53, 90C26, 90C33, 90C46.

1
Introduction

This paper completes our investigation initiated in
[
4
]
and develops second-order optimality conditions for the class of optimization problems of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mbox{(GEP)}\hskip 142.26378pt\min&\ \ f(x)\\
{\rm s.t.}&\ \ 0\in F(x)+b(x)^{T}N_{P}(g(x)),\end{split}

where
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{l}
is a convex polyhedral set,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l}
, and
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l\times m}
are twice continuously differentiable, and
N_{P}
denotes the normal cone of
P
.
In the special case where
n=m=l
,
g(x)=x
and
b(x)
is the identity matrix, the constraint reduces to the generalized equation studied by Robinson
[
27
]
.
In this spirit, we refer to this problem as an optimization problem with generalized equation constraints and employ the GEP moniker.
Still with
b(x)
equal to the identity matrix but with general
g
, one can model prominent disjunctive problems such as programs with complementarity or vanishing constraints.
Yet even more complex problems and structures can be targeted with nontrivial matrices
b(x)
, and we are particularly interested in these.
Such problems include, e.g., mathematical programs with second-order cone complementarity constraints (SOC-MPCC) (see, e.g., Outrata and Sun
[
24
]
and Ye and Zhou
[
30
]
)
or problems with variational inequality constraints and bilevel programs as detailed below.

The constraints of GEP involve both the normal cone mapping
N_{P}
and the coefficient matrix
b(x)
;
this requires us to simultaneously account for the nonlinear coupling effects introduced by
b(x)
and the geometric structure of the set-valued mapping
N_{P}
.
This makes the second-order theory for GEPs highly challenging and accounts for our decision to split the investigation into two papers.
As far as we know, a systematic study of second-order optimality conditions for such general problems is lacking.
Even for the particular cases mentioned above, the second-order theory remains relatively limited.

A notable advantage of the GEP model lies in providing a unified framework for describing a wide range of optimization problems.
For example, consider the following mathematical program with variational inequality constraints (MPVI):

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mbox{(MPVI)}\hskip 130.88268pt\min\limits_{x,y}&\ \ f(x,y)\\
{\rm s.t.}&\ \ \langle F(x,y),y^{\prime}-y\rangle\geq 0,\ \ \ \forall y^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x),\end{split}

where
\Gamma(x):=\{y\,|\,\psi(x,y)\in P\}
,
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{l}
is a convex polyhedral set,

f:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}
,
F:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
, and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l}
are twice continuously differentiable.
Alternative names for MPVIs include generalized bilevel programming problems or mathematical programs with equilibrium constraints (see e.g.,
[
15
,
18
,
19
,
29
,
31
]
).
Particularly interesting and important is the case where
\Gamma(x)
is convex-valued.
Then, the variational inequality constraint describes the normal cone inclusion
-F(x,y)\in N_{\Gamma(x)}(y)

and if the constraint system
\Psi(x,y)\in P
in variable
y
defining
\Gamma(x)
satisfies a constraint qualification, the MPVI becomes

\displaystyle\hskip 96.73936pt\min

\displaystyle\ \ f(x,y)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\ \ 0\in F(x,y)+N_{\Gamma(x)}(y)=F(x,y)+\nabla_{y}\psi(x,y)^{T}N_{P}(\psi(x,y)),

(1)

which is in the GEP form.
On the other hand, if
F(x,y):=\nabla_{y}\varphi(x,y)
with
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
being differentiable and pseudo-convex in
y
,
the MPVI is equivalent to the following bilevel program:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mbox{(BP)}\hskip 130.88268pt\min&\ \ f(x,y)\\
{\rm s.t.}&\ \ y\in{\rm arg\,min\,}_{y^{\prime}}\{\varphi(x,y^{\prime})\,|\,y^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x)\}.\end{split}

The reader is referred to the monographs
[
8
,
11
,
18
,
23
]
and the references therein for background and applications of MPVIs and BPs.

To derive second-order optimality conditions for the GEP,
we reformulate it in the standard form of a general constrained optimization problem:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mbox{(GP)}\hskip 139.4185pt\min&\ \ f(x)\ \ \ {\rm s.t.}\ \ \varphi(x)\in\Omega.\end{split}

To this end, we set

\varphi(x):=(x,-F(x))\ \text{ and }\ \Omega:=\{(x,b(x)^{T}\eta)\,|\,(g(x),\eta)\in{\rm gph}N_{P}\},

(2)

where
{\rm gph}N_{P}
stands for the graph of the normal cone mapping
N_{P}
.
Most results are available for second-order conditions for the GP when
\Omega
is convex; see the classical reference
[
7
]
.
For more recent works in the convex setting, we refer to
[
20
]
based on modern variational analysis,
as well as
[
1
]
where some of the fundamental results of
[
7
]
are extended to infinite-dimensional spaces
and even the smoothness requirements are relaxed.

In our case, however,
\Omega
is rarely convex.
Results most relevant to us were initiated in
[
16
]

and further developed in
[
4
,
5
,
25
]
.
Our companion paper
[
4
]
contains a deeper discussion on second-order optimality conditions for GP;
here, we limit our remarks to just mentioning that such conditions will, naturally, combine first- and second-order
derivatives of
f
and
\varphi
as well as the local curvature of
\Omega
, see Proposition
2.14
.
As in
[
4
]
, we will use three variational tools to capture the curvature of
\Omega
.

The above is true for GPs with an arbitrary set
\Omega
.
If
\Omega
is simple, its curvature can perhaps be easy to compute or even vanish altogether.
Our
\Omega
from (
2
), however, possesses very complicated structure.
Computing its curvature is actually precisely the key step and the major challenge of our work.
Ultimately, it will be expressed in terms of derivatives of
b
and
g
and the curvature of
{\rm gph}N_{P}
,
which is trivial due to its polyhedrality.

Instead of focusing specifically on
\Omega
from (
2
), we employ a slightly more general setting, targeting the structural essence of (
2
).
To this end, we consider

\Omega:=\{B(z)\,|\,z\in\Gamma\}\quad\mbox{ with }\quad\Gamma:=\{z\,|\,G(z)\in D\},

where
B:\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\hat{m}}
and
G:\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\hat{l}}
are twice continuously differentiable mappings, and
D
is a polyhedral set.
The particular choices
z:=(x,\eta)
,
B(z):=(x,b(x)^{T}\eta)
,
G(z):=(g(x),\eta)
, and
D:={\rm gph}N_{P}
indeed lead to (
2
).
The key is to connect the curvature of
\Omega
with the curvature of
D
and even variational geometries of the two sets more broadly.
As expected, this is done in two main steps via the intermediate set
\Gamma
.
The core part of
[
4
]
was dedicated to linking variational geometries of
\Gamma
and
D

and here we proceed with the second step of connecting
\Omega
with
\Gamma
.
These fundamental results are of independent interest beyond the purposes of this paper.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews necessary tools from variational analysis and relevant preliminary results, including the main results of
[
4
]
.
Section 3 provides a crucial and comprehensive geometric analysis of the set
\Omega=B(\Gamma)
.
Based on this, Section 4 tackles the structure of (
2
) and Section 5 establishes second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for the GEPs.
As an application, Section 6 details second-order optimality conditions for a specific MPVI.
Conclusions are drawn in Section 7.

2
Preliminaries and auxiliary results

In this section, we clarify the notation, recall background material from variational analysis, and present preliminary results.
The open unit ball is denoted by
\mathbb{B}
.
For a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote by
{\rm span}\,S
the linear subspace generated by
S
.
The indicator function
\delta_{S}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}:=[-\infty,+\infty]
of
S

is given as
\delta_{S}(z)=0
for
z\in S
and
\delta_{S}(z)=+\infty
if
z\notin S
.
Let
S^{\circ}
and
\sigma_{S}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
stand for the polar cone of
S
and the support function of
S
, respectively, i.e.,

S^{\circ}:=\{z^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\mid\,\langle z^{*},z\rangle\leq 0,\ \forall z\in S\}

and
\sigma_{S}(z^{*}):=\sup\{\langle z^{*},z\rangle\,\mid\,z\in S\}
for
z^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For an extended-valued function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-\infty,\infty]
, its effective domain is
{\rm dom}\varphi:=\{z\,|\,\varphi(z)<+\infty\}
.
For
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote by
{\{w\}}^{\perp}
the orthogonal complement of the linear subspace generated by
w
.
Let
o:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
stand for a mapping satisfying
o(t)/t\rightarrow 0
as
t\downarrow 0
.
The symbol
z^{\prime}\overset{S}{\to}z
indicates that
z^{\prime}\in S

and
z^{\prime}\rightarrow z
.
For a mapping
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a point
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by

\nabla\psi(z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
its Jacobian
and by
\nabla^{2}\psi(z)
its second derivative at
z
as defined by

w^{T}\nabla^{2}\psi(z):=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\nabla\psi(z+tw)-\nabla\psi(z)}{t}\quad\forall\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Hence

\nabla^{2}\psi(z)(w,w):=w^{T}\nabla^{2}\psi(z)w=(w^{T}\nabla^{2}\psi_{1}(z)w,\dots,w^{T}\nabla^{2}\psi_{d}(z)w)^{T}\quad\forall\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

2.1
Variational geometry

First we review the various concepts of tangent cones and normal cones.

Definition 2.1

(Tangent Cones,
[
21
,
28
]
)
.

Given a closed set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
z\in C
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the tangent/contingent cone to
C
at
z
is defined by

\displaystyle T_{C}(z):=\big\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\exists\ t_{k}\downarrow 0,\;u_{k}\to u\ \ {\rm with}\ \ z+t_{k}u_{k}\in C\big\}.

For
z\in C
and
u\in T_{C}(z)
,
the outer second-order tangent set to
C
at
z
in direction
u
is defined by

T_{C}^{2}(z;u):=\left\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\exists\ t_{k}\downarrow 0,w_{k}\rightarrow w\ {\rm such\ that}\ z+t_{k}u+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}w_{k}\in C\right\}.

Definition 2.2

(Normal Cones,
[
13
,
17
,
21
,
28
]
)
.

Given a closed set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
z\in C
and a direction
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the regular/Fréchet normal cone, the limiting/Mordukhovich normal cone to
C
at
z
and the limiting normal cone to
C
at
z
in direction
u
are given respectively by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{C}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\langle v,z^{\prime}-z\rangle\leq o\big(\|z^{\prime}-z\|\big),\ \forall z^{\prime}\in C\right\},

\displaystyle N_{C}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\exists z_{k}\overset{C}{\to}z,v_{k}\rightarrow v\ {\rm with}\ v_{k}\in\widehat{N}_{C}(z_{k})\right\},

\displaystyle N_{C}(z;u)

\displaystyle:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,u_{k}\rightarrow u,v_{k}\rightarrow v\ {\rm with}\ v_{k}\in\widehat{N}_{C}(z+t_{k}u_{k})\right\}.

Definition 2.3

(Directional Proximal Normal Cones,
[
4
]
)
.

Given a closed set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a point
z\in C
and a direction
u\in T_{C}(z)
,
we define the proximal prenormal cone to
C
at
z
in direction
u
as

\hat{\cal N}^{p}_{C}(z;u):=\{z^{*}\,|\,\exists\delta,\rho,\gamma>0:\langle z^{*},z^{\prime}-z\rangle\leq\gamma\|z^{\prime}-z\|^{2}\quad\forall z^{\prime}\in C\cap(z+V_{\delta,\rho}(u))\},

where

V_{\delta,\rho}(u):=\left\{u^{\prime}\in\delta\mathbb{B}\left|\big\|\|u\|u^{\prime}-\|u^{\prime}\|u\big\|\leq\rho\|u^{\prime}\|\|u\|\right.\right\}

is called a directional neighborhood of the direction
u
.
The proximal normal cone to
C
at
z
in direction
u
is given as

\widehat{N}^{p}_{C}(z;u):=\hat{\cal N}^{p}_{C}(z;u)\cap[u]^{\perp}.

In case when
u\not\in T_{C}(z)
we set
\hat{\cal N}^{p}_{C}(z;u):=\widehat{N}^{p}_{C}(z;u):=\emptyset
.

Definition 2.4

(Lower Generalized Support Function,
[
16
]
)
.

Given a nonempty closed set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the lower generalized support function to
C
as the mapping
\hat{\sigma}_{C}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
by

\hat{\sigma}_{C}(\lambda):=\liminf_{\tilde{\lambda}\to\lambda}\inf_{u}\{\tilde{\lambda}^{T}u\mid\tilde{\lambda}\in N_{C}(u)\}=\liminf_{\tilde{\lambda}\to\lambda}\inf_{u}\{\tilde{\lambda}^{T}u\mid\tilde{\lambda}\in\widehat{N}_{C}(u)\}.

If
C=\emptyset
, then we define
\hat{\sigma}_{C}(\lambda):=-\infty
for all
\lambda.

It was shown in
[
16
, Proposition 6]
that in general
\hat{\sigma}_{C}(\lambda)\leq\sigma_{C}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda
and the equality holds when
C
is convex.

Definition 2.5

(Second-order Subderivative,
[
22
,
28
]
)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
,
\varphi(\bar{z})
be finite and
\bar{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The second-order subderivative of
\varphi
at
\bar{z}
for
\bar{v}
and
u
is a function defined by

\displaystyle d^{2}\varphi(\bar{z};\bar{v})(u):=\liminf\limits_{{t\downarrow 0}\atop{u^{\prime}\to u}}\frac{\varphi(\bar{z}+tu^{\prime})-\varphi(\bar{z})-t\langle\bar{v},u^{\prime}\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}.

In this paper we are mainly interested in the second subderivative of the indicator function
\delta_{C}
. In this case we have

\displaystyle d^{2}\delta_{C}(\bar{z};u^{*})(u)=\liminf\limits_{{t\downarrow 0}\atop u^{\prime}\to u}\frac{\delta_{C}(\bar{z}+tu^{\prime})-\delta_{C}(\bar{z})-t\langle u^{*},u^{\prime}\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}=\liminf\limits_{{t\downarrow 0,u^{\prime}\to u}\atop{\bar{z}+tu^{\prime}\in C}}\frac{-2\langle u^{*},u^{\prime}\rangle}{t}.

(3)

Definition 2.6

(Directional Metric Subregularity,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
G(\bar{z})\in C
.
We say that the set-valued map

M(z):=G(z)-C
is metrically subregular (MS) at
(\bar{z},0)
in direction
\bar{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if there exist

\kappa,\delta,\rho>0
such that

{\rm dist}(z,M^{-1}(0))\leq\kappa\,{\rm dist}(G(z),C),\quad\forall z\in\bar{z}+V_{\delta,\rho}(\bar{w}).

In the case where
\bar{w}=0
, we simply say that
M
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{z},0)
or the metric subregularity constraint qualification (MSCQ) holds at
\bar{z}
.

Sufficient conditions for directional metric subregularity are given in, e.g.,
[
6
,
12
]
.

2.2
Polyhedral sets

Given an arbitrary set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we call a subspace
L
the
generalized linearity space
of
C
and denote it by
{\cal L}(C)
provided it is the largest subspace
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
C+L\subseteq C
. In the case where
C
is a closed and convex cone, we have
{\cal L}(C)=C\cap(-C)
. Further we denote by

C^{+}:={\rm span\;}(C-C)

the unique subspace parallel to the affine hull of
C
. In addition, if
C
is a cone, then

C^{+}={\rm span}\,C
; if
C
is a closed and convex cone, then
C^{+}=C-C
. For a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
[v]
denote the subspace generated by
v
, i.e.,
[v]:=\{tv\,|\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

A set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
convex polyhedral
if it is the intersection of finitely many halfspaces, whereas it is said to be
polyhedral
whenever it is the union of finitely many convex polyhedral sets. If
C
is convex polyhedral, then

T_{C}(\bar{z})\subseteq T_{C}(z)
for
z\in C
near
\bar{z}
. But this relation fails to hold when
C
is nonconvex polyhedral. For example,
let
C:=(\mathbb{R}_{-},0)\cup(0,\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\bar{z}=0
. Then
T_{C}(\bar{z})=C

and
T_{C}(z)=\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
for all
z=(0,a)\in C
with
a>0
. So
T_{C}(\bar{z})\nsubseteq T_{C}(z)
and
T_{C}(z)\nsubseteq T_{C}(\bar{z})
.
The following relations between
T_{C}(\bar{z})
and
T_{C}(z)
will be used in the subsequent analysis.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a polyhedral set and
\bar{z}\in C
. Then there exists an open neighborhood
V
of
0
such that

T_{C}(z)=T_{T_{C}(\bar{z})}(z-\bar{z}),\ \ \ T_{C}(z)\subseteq T_{C}(\bar{z})+[z-\bar{z}],\ \ \ T_{C}(z)^{+}\subseteq T_{C}(\bar{z})^{+},\ \ \ \forall\ z\in C\cap(\bar{z}+V).

Proof.

By definition
C=\displaystyle\cup_{i=1}^{s}C_{i}
where each
C_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
is convex polyhedral.
Then by
[
10
, Theorem 2E.3]
there is an open neighborhood
V
of
0
such that

(C_{i}-\bar{z})\cap V=T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})\cap V
for
i=1,\ldots,s
.
Taking the union on both sides of the above equations, since
T_{C}(\bar{z})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})
, we obtain

(C-\bar{z})\cap V=T_{C}(\bar{z})\cap V,

(4)

or equivalently

{C\cap(\bar{z}+V)=(\bar{z}+T_{C}(\bar{z}))\cap(\bar{z}+V)}.

(5)

Hence

T_{C}(z)=T_{\bar{z}+T_{C}(\bar{z})}(z)=T_{T_{C}(\bar{z})}(z-\bar{z}),\quad\forall z\in C\cap(\bar{z}+V).

For
z\in C\cap(\bar{z}+V)
, define the active index set as
I(z):=\{i\,|\,z\in C_{i}\}
. Clearly

I(z)\subseteq I(\bar{z})
by shrinking
V
if necessary. For
i\in I(z)
, since
C_{i}
is convex polyhedral, we have

\displaystyle T_{C_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle=T_{T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})}(z-\bar{z})=[N_{T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})}(z-\bar{z})]^{\circ}=\left[[T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})]^{\circ}\cap(z-\bar{z})^{\perp}\right]^{\circ}

\displaystyle=[T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})]+[(z-\bar{z})^{\perp}]^{\circ}=T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})+[z-\bar{z}].

Therefore

T_{C}(z)=\cup_{i\in I(z)}T_{C_{i}}(z)=\cup_{i\in I(z)}\left(T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})+[z-\bar{z}]\right)\subseteq\cup_{i\in I(\bar{z})}T_{C_{i}}(\bar{z})+[z-\bar{z}]=T_{C}(\bar{z})+[z-\bar{z}].

(6)

Moreover by (
5
) we have
z-\bar{z}\in T_{C}(\bar{z})

implying
[z-\bar{z}]\subseteq T_{C}(\bar{z})^{+}
. This together with (
6
) implies

\displaystyle T_{C}(z)-T_{C}(z)\subseteq T_{C}(\bar{z})-{T_{C}(\bar{z})}+[z-\bar{z}]\subseteq T_{C}(\bar{z})^{+},

and hence

T_{C}(z)^{+}\subseteq T_{C}(\bar{z})^{+},\ \quad\forall z\in C\cap(\bar{z}+V).

This completes the proof.
∎

.

(9)

), the first equality follows from (

) since

, and the last equality is given by

. The formula (

) is obtained by (

Recall that a face of a convex set
C
is a convex subset
C^{\prime}
of
C
such that every (closed) line segment in
C
with a relative interior point in
C^{\prime}
has both
endpoints in
C^{\prime}
. We denote the collection of all faces of
C
by
{\cal F}(C)
. A convex polyhedral set
P
has finitely many faces which itself are convex polyhedral sets and therefore closed. For a convex polyhedral cone
K
, the faces are exactly the sets of the form
K\cap[v^{*}]^{\perp}
for some
v^{*}\in K^{\circ}
, and there holds
{\cal L}({\cal F})={\cal L}(K)
for each
{\cal F}\in{\cal F}(K)
.

The following theorem is a consequence of
[
9
, Proof of Theorem 2]
and
[
14
, Theorem 2.12]
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{l}
be a convex polyhedral set and
(\bar{d},\bar{d}^{*})\in{\rm gph}N_{P}
. Then

\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{P}}(\bar{d},{\bar{d}^{\ast}})={\cal K}_{P}(\bar{d},{\bar{d}^{\ast}})^{\circ}\times{\cal K}_{P}(\bar{d},{\bar{d}^{\ast}})

and for every
(e,e^{*})\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{P}}(\bar{d},{\bar{d}^{\ast}})

the directional limiting normal cone
N_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{P}}((\bar{d},{\bar{d}^{\ast}});(e,e^{\ast}))
is the union of all product sets
K^{\circ}\times K
associated with cones
K
of the form
{\cal F}_{1}-{\cal F}_{2}
, where
{\cal F}_{1}
and
{\cal F}_{2}
are closed faces of the critical cone
{\cal K}_{P}(\bar{d},{\bar{d}^{\ast}})
satisfying
e\in{\cal F}_{2}\subseteq{\cal F}_{1}\subseteq[e^{\ast}]^{\perp}
.

Lemma 2.10

[

Consider a feasible point

(11)

Assumption 1

2.4
Second-order optimality conditions for GPs

In this section, we review second-order optimality conditions for the constrained problem GP.
Recall the following definition introduced by Penot
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.13

(Essential local minimizer of second order)
.

A point
\bar{x}
is said to be an essential local minimizer
of second order for problem

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mbox{(GP)}\hskip 139.4185pt\min&\ \ f(x)\ \ \ {\rm s.t.}\ \ \varphi(x)\in\Omega,\end{split}

if there exist
\beta>0
and
\delta>0
such that

\max\{f(x)-f(\bar{x}),\ {\rm dist}(\varphi(x),\Omega)\}\geq\beta\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2},\ \ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

For
\alpha\geq 0
, define the generalized Lagrangian function for GP as

L^{\alpha}(x,\lambda):=\alpha f(x)+\langle\lambda,\varphi(x)\rangle.

For simplicity, we omit
\alpha
when
\alpha=1
.
At a feasible point
\bar{x}
of GP, the critical cone is defined as

C(\bar{x}):=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})d\in T_{\Omega}(\varphi(\bar{x})),\ \ \nabla f(\bar{x})d\leq 0\}.

The following second-order optimality conditions for the general constrained optimization problem come
from
[
16
, Theorem 2, Corollary 5]
and
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Proposition 2.14

(Second-order optimality conditions for GP)
.

Let
\bar{x}
be a feasible point of problem GP. The following statements hold.

(i)

Let
\bar{x}
be a local optimal solution of GP. Suppose that the set-valued map
x\rightrightarrows\varphi(x)-\Omega
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},0)
in direction
d\in C(\bar{x})
with
T_{\Omega}^{2}(\varphi(\bar{x});\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})d)\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists a multiplier
\lambda\in\Lambda(\bar{x};d):=\{\lambda\,|\,\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0,\ \lambda\in{N}_{\Omega}(\varphi(\bar{x});\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})d)\}
such that

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\hat{\sigma}_{T^{2}_{\Omega}(\varphi(\bar{x});\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})d)}(\lambda)\geq 0.

(13)

Moreover, if the following directional nondegeneracy condition

\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda=0,\ \ \lambda\in\big(N_{\Omega}(\varphi(\bar{x});\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})d)\big)^{+}\Longrightarrow\lambda=0,

holds, then
\Lambda(\bar{x};d)=\{\lambda_{0}\}
and

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},{\lambda_{0}})(d,d)-\sigma_{T^{2}_{\Omega}(\varphi(\bar{x});\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})d)}({\lambda_{0}})\geq 0.

(iii)

Suppose that for every
d\in C(\bar{x})\backslash\{0\}
there is some
\alpha\geq 0
and
\lambda\in\widehat{N}_{\Omega}^{p}(\varphi(\bar{x});\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})d)
not all equal to zero,
satisfying

\nabla_{x}L^{\alpha}(\bar{x},\lambda)=0

and

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L^{\alpha}(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)+d^{2}\delta_{\Omega}(\varphi(\bar{x});\lambda)(\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})d)>0.

(14)

Then
\bar{x}
is an essential local minimizer of second order for GP.

3
Variational analysis of
\Omega

In this core section, we analyze the set
\Omega
having the special representation
motivated by the common structure of optimization problems from the introduction, namely

\Omega:=\{B(z)\,|\,z\in\Gamma\}\quad\mbox{ with }\quad\Gamma:=\{z\,|\,G(z)\in D\}.

Here
B:\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\hat{m}}
and
G:\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\hat{l}}
are twice continuously differentiable maps and
D
is a polyhedral set.
The ultimate goal is to bridge the variational geometry of
\Omega
and that of
\Gamma

with particular focus on second-order constructions.
Such a connection between the sets
\Gamma
and
D
was provided in
[
4
, Sections 4 and 5]
and reviewed
in the previous section.

One might wonder why not to proceed with an arbitrary set
\Gamma
here.
This is of course possible, but it will soon become clear how we actually
utilize the pre-image structure of
\Gamma
to articulate our key assumption
in a tractable and more explicit form.
Note, however, that the results are valid without any constraint qualification for the system
G(z)\in D
.

In the first part, we establish a technical foundation,
essentially securing that the mapping

\omega\rightrightarrows B^{-1}(\omega)\cap\Gamma

locally behaves like a single-valued Lipschitzian function.
The second part contains the formulas for the tangent and normal cones,
and the third part provides those for the curvature terms associated with
\Omega
.

3.1
The main assumption and technical foundation

For
z\in\Gamma
, we denote by
L_{z}
the linear subspace defined by

L_{z}:=\{w\,|\,\nabla G(z)w\in\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}\}.

Note that
T_{\Gamma}(z)\subseteq\{w\,|\,\nabla G(z)w\in T_{D}(G(z))\}
and consequently

\big(T_{\Gamma}(z)\big)^{+}\subseteq\big(\{w\,|\,\nabla G(z)w\in T_{D}(G(z))\}\big)^{+}\subseteq\{w\,|\,\nabla G(z)w\in\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}\}=L_{z}.

(15)

Throughout this section we assume that the following condition is fulfilled at the reference point
\bar{\omega}\in\Omega
.

Assumption 2
.

Let
\bar{\omega}\in\Omega
.
Suppose that there exists a point
\bar{z}\in B^{-1}(\bar{\omega})
lying in the set

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bar{\omega}}:=\liminf_{\omega\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}\bar{\omega}}\big(B^{-1}(\omega)\cap\Gamma\big)=\big\{\bar{z}\,|\,\forall\omega^{k}\overset{\Omega}{\to}\bar{\omega},\exists z^{k}\rightarrow\bar{z}\ \ {\rm with}\ \ z^{k}\in B^{-1}(\omega^{k})\cap\Gamma\big\}

(16)

such that the following condition holds:

\displaystyle\nabla B(\bar{z})w=0,\ w\in L_{\bar{z}}\ \Longrightarrow\ w=0.

(17)

This assumption has several important consequences.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\bar{\omega}\in\Omega
and
\bar{z}\in\Sigma_{\bar{\omega}}
. For every positive real
\gamma_{B}
satisfying

\min\{\|\nabla B(\bar{z})w\|\,\mid\,w\in L_{\bar{z}},\|w\|=1\}>\gamma_{B},

(18)

there exists a neighborhood
\widetilde{W}
of
\bar{z}
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla B(z)w\|\geq\gamma_{B}\|w\|,\ \ \ \forall z\in\Gamma\cap\widetilde{W},\ w\in L_{z},

(19)

\displaystyle\|B(z_{1})-B(z_{2})\|\geq\gamma_{B}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|,\ \ \ \forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma\cap\widetilde{W}.

(20)

Proof.

To the contrary, assume that (
19
) does not hold for any neighborhood
\widetilde{W}
of
\bar{z}
. Then we can find sequences
z_{k}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\Gamma}\bar{z}
and
w_{k}\in L_{z_{k}}
such that
\|\nabla B(z_{k})w_{k}\|<\gamma_{B}\|w_{k}\|
. Hence
w_{k}\not=0
and we can assume without loss of generality that
\|w_{k}\|=1
. By possibly passing to a subsequence we can assume that
w_{k}
converges to some
w
with
\|w\|=1
. Since
w_{k}\in L_{z_{k}}
, for
k
sufficiently large we have

\nabla G(z_{k})w_{k}\in(T_{D}(G(z_{k})))^{+}\subseteq(T_{D}(G(\bar{z})))^{+},

where the second step follows from Lemma
2.7
.
Passing to the limit and taking into account that
\|\nabla B(z_{k})w_{k}\|<\gamma_{B}\|w_{k}\|
yields
\nabla G(\bar{z})w\in\big(T_{D}(G(\bar{z}))\big)^{+}
, i.e.,
w\in L_{\bar{z}}
, but
\|\nabla B(\bar{z})w\|\leq\gamma_{B}
. This contradicts (
18
), showing (
19
).

Assume now that (
20
) does not hold. Then we can find sequences
z_{1}^{k}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\Gamma}\bar{z}
and
z_{2}^{k}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\Gamma}\bar{z}
such that

\|B(z_{1}^{k})-B(z_{2}^{k})\|<\gamma_{B}\|z_{1}^{k}-z_{2}^{k}\|,\ \ \forall\,k.

(21)

Particularly,
z_{1}^{k}\not=z_{2}^{k}
. By passing to a subsequence we may assume that the sequence
(z_{1}^{k}-z_{2}^{k})/\|z_{1}^{k}-z_{2}^{k}\|
converges to some
w
with
\|w\|=1
. Using (
4
), for all
k
sufficiently large we have
G(z_{i}^{k})-G(\bar{z})\in T_{D}(G(\bar{z}))
for
i=1,2
, implying
G(z_{1}^{k})-G(z_{2}^{k})\in\big(T_{D}(G(\bar{z}))\big)^{+}
. Since
\big(T_{D}(G(\bar{z}))\big)^{+}
is a subspace, we obtain

\nabla G(\bar{z})w=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{G(z_{1}^{k})-G(z_{2}^{k})}{\|z_{1}^{k}-z_{2}^{k}\|}\in\big(T_{D}(G(\bar{z}))\big)^{+}.

Hence
w\in L_{\bar{z}}
and
\|w\|=1
. Further, it follows from (
21
) that

\|\nabla B(\bar{z})w\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\|B(z_{1}^{k})-B(z_{2}^{k})\|}{\|z_{1}^{k}-z_{2}^{k}\|}\leq\gamma_{B},

contradicting (
18
). This proves (
20
).
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\bar{\omega}\in\Omega
and
\bar{z}\in\Sigma_{\bar{\omega}}
. For every positive real
\gamma_{B}
satisfying (
18
) and every open neighborhood
\widetilde{W}
of
\bar{z}
such that (
20
) holds, there are open neighborhoods
{\mathcal{O}}
of
\bar{\omega}
and
W
of
\bar{z}
satisfying
W\subseteq\widetilde{W}
and a bijective mapping
\hat{z}:\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}\to\Gamma\cap W
, such that
\hat{z}
is Lipschitzian on
\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}
with constant
1/\gamma_{B}
and

\big(B^{-1}(\omega)\cap\Gamma\big)\cap W=\{\hat{z}(\omega)\},\ \ \ \forall\,\omega\in\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}.

Proof.

By (
16
) there is an open neighborhood
{\mathcal{O}}
of
\bar{\omega}
such that
Z(\omega):=B^{-1}(\omega)\cap\Gamma\cap\widetilde{W}\not=\emptyset
for all
\omega\in\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}
. Further, for all
\omega\in\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}
the set
Z(\omega)
consists of exactly one element because for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z(\omega)
we have
\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|\leq 1/\gamma_{B}\|B(z_{1})-B(z_{2})\|=1/\gamma_{B}\|\omega-\omega\|=0
by (
20
). We denote this element by
\hat{z}(\omega)
. Then we have shown that
\hat{z}(\omega)
is an injective mapping on
\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}
.

For every
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}
we have
\hat{z}(\omega_{1}),\hat{z}(\omega_{2})\in\Gamma\cap\widetilde{W}
and therefore

\|\hat{z}(\omega_{1})-\hat{z}(\omega_{2})\|\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{B}}\|B(\hat{z}(\omega_{1}))-B(\hat{z}(\omega_{2}))\|=\frac{1}{\gamma_{B}}\|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\|

by (
20
). Thus we conclude that
\hat{z}
is Lipschitzian on
\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}
with constant
1/\gamma_{B}
. Now let
\widehat{\Gamma}:=\hat{z}(\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}})
. Then
\hat{z}:\Omega\cap{\mathcal{O}}\to\widehat{\Gamma}
is bijective and, since
\hat{z}^{-1}=B
,
\widehat{\Gamma}
is relatively open. By Assumption
2
,
\bar{z}\in B^{-1}(\bar{\omega})\cap\Gamma
and hence
\hat{z}(\bar{\omega})=\bar{z}
. Together with
\bar{z}\in\widehat{\Gamma}\subseteq\Gamma\cap\widetilde{W}
we can find an open neighborhood
W
of
\bar{z}
satisfying
W\subseteq\widetilde{W}
such that
\widehat{\Gamma}=\Gamma\cap W
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Assumption
2
and the statement of Proposition
3.2
resemble the strict derivative criterion for strong metric regularity given in
[

, Theorem 4D.1]
,
a generalization of the Kummer inverse function theorem
[

, Theorem 4D.6]
.
Without going into details, the local single-valuedness and Lipschitz continuity of

can be derived from

, Theorem 4D.1]
under Assumption
2

with (

) replaced by

\nabla B(\bar{z})w=0,\ w\in T^{P}_{\Gamma}(\bar{z})\ \Longrightarrow\ w=0

\nabla B(\bar{z})w=0,\ \nabla G(\bar{z})w\in T^{P}_{D}(G(\bar{z}))\ \Longrightarrow\ w=0.

Here,

denotes the paratingent cone, see
[

]
,
which is much larger than the tangent cone and often hard to compute.
The first condition can be applied to an arbitrary set

, but is hard to verify; the latter resembles ours, but

is still easier to compute.

Even more can be derived from Assumption

.
For every

\displaystyle:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla B(z)w\\

\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle:=\nabla B(z)^{T}\omega^{*}-L_{z}^{\perp}.

Clearly,
{\rm Range\,}\psi_{z}
and
{\rm Range\,}\theta_{z}
are linear subspaces. Let
(\eta,q)\in{\rm Range\,}\psi_{z}

\psi_{z}^{-1}(\eta,q):=\left\{w\,|\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla B(z)w\\
\nabla G(z)w\end{array}\right)\in\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\eta\\
q\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0_{\mathbb{R}^{\hat{m}}}\\
\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}\end{array}\right)\right\}.

(22)

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\bar{\omega}\in\Omega
and
\bar{z}\in\Sigma_{\bar{\omega}}

)

for all

.

the mapping

(23)

\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0_{\mathbb{R}^{\hat{m}}}\\
\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}\end{array}\right).

This means that

Thus

. Now assume that
\|\hat{w}_{z}\|
is not bounded on some compact subset
M
of
\Gamma\cap\widetilde{W}
. Then there are sequences
z_{k}\in M
and
(\eta_{k},q_{k})
with
\|(\eta_{k},q_{k})\|=1
such that
\|w_{k}\|\to\infty
with
w_{k}:=\hat{w}_{z_{k}}(\eta_{k},q_{k})
. By possibly passing to a subsequence we can assume that

converges to some
z\in M
and
w_{k}/\|w_{k}\|
converges to some
w
with
\|w\|=1
. By Lemma
2.7
we have
\big(T_{D}(G(z_{k}))\big)^{+}\subseteq\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}
for all
k
sufficiently large and therefore

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla B(z_{k})\frac{w_{k}}{\|w_{k}\|}\\
\nabla G(z_{k})\frac{w_{k}}{\|w_{k}\|}\end{array}\right)\in\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{\eta_{k}}{\|w_{k}\|}\\
\frac{q_{k}}{\|w_{k}\|}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0_{\mathbb{R}^{\hat{m}}}\\
\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}\end{array}\right).

Passing to the limit yields
\nabla B(z)w=0
and
\nabla G(z)w\in\big(T_{D}(G(z))\big)^{+}
, contradicting (
19
) because of
w\in L_{z}
and
\|w\|=1
. Thus,
\|\hat{w}_{z}\|
is bounded on
M
.

It remains to show (
23
). Since
N_{L_{z}^{\perp}}(0)=L_{z}
, we conclude from (
19
) that
\|\nabla B(z)w\|\geq\gamma_{B}\|w\|
for all
w\in N_{L^{\perp}}(0)
. Thus, by
[
28
, Example 9.44]
the mapping
\theta_{z}
is metrically regular near
(0,0)
and hence for every
\kappa>1/\gamma_{B}
there holds

{\rm dist}(\omega^{*},\theta_{z}^{-1}(z^{*}))\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(z^{*},\theta_{z}(\omega^{*}))

for all
(\omega^{*},z^{*})
sufficiently close to
(0,0)
. Due to the linear structure of
\theta_{z}
, we have
\theta_{z}^{-1}(\alpha z^{*})=\alpha\theta_{z}^{-1}(z^{*})
and
\theta_{z}(\alpha\omega^{*})=\alpha\theta_{z}(\omega^{*})

for all
\alpha>0
, yielding

\alpha{\rm dist}(\omega^{*},\theta_{z}^{-1}(z^{*}))={\rm dist}(\alpha\omega^{*},\theta_{z}^{-1}(\alpha z^{*}))\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \alpha{\rm dist}(z^{*},\theta_{z}(\omega^{*}))={\rm dist}(\alpha z^{*},\theta_{z}(\alpha\omega^{*})).

Consequently, the above inequality is valid for all
(\omega^{*},z^{*})
and since it holds for all
\kappa>1/\gamma_{B}
, it must hold with
\kappa=1/\gamma_{B}
as well and the proof is complete.
∎

is bounded. Assuming that

, then

Second-order optimality conditions for MPVIs

For illustration, consider the mathematical program with variational inequality constraints from the introduction

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mbox{(MPVI)}\hskip 130.88268pt\min\limits_{x,y}&\ \ f(x,y)\\
{\rm s.t.}&\ \ \langle F(x,y),y^{\prime}-y\rangle\geq 0,\ \ \ \forall y^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x)\end{split}

with twice continuously differentiable functions
f
,
F
, and
\psi
of appropriate dimensions and
where
\Gamma(x):=\{y\,|\,\psi(x,y)\in P\}
for the particular choice
P:=\mathbb{R}^{l}_{-}
.
For this special case, the expressions of
T_{{\rm gph}N_{P}}
,
N_{{\rm gph}N_{P}}
, and
\widehat{N}_{T_{{\rm gph}N_{P}}}

can be obtained by simply calculating the corresponding ones for
\mathbb{R}_{-}
. Note that
{\rm gph}N_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}=(\mathbb{R}_{-},0)\cup(0,\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then

T_{{\rm gph}N_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}}(a,b)=\begin{cases}(\mathbb{R},0)&a<0,b=0,\\
(\mathbb{R}_{-},0)\cup(0,\mathbb{R}_{+})&a=0,b=0,\\
(0,\mathbb{R})&a=0,b>0,\end{cases}

N_{{\rm gph}N_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}}((a,b);(c,d))=\begin{cases}(0,\mathbb{R})&a<0,b=0,c\in\mathbb{R},d=0,\\
(0,\mathbb{R})&a=0,b=0,c<0,d=0,\\
(0,\mathbb{R})\cup(\mathbb{R},0)\cup(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}_{-})&a=0,b=0,c=0,d=0,\\
(\mathbb{R},0)&a=0,b=0,c=0,d>0,\\
(\mathbb{R},0)&a=0,b>0,c=0,d\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

and

\widehat{N}_{T_{{\rm gph}N_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}}(a,b)}(c,d)=\begin{cases}(0,\mathbb{R})\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ &a<0,b=0,c\in\mathbb{R},d=0,\\
(0,\mathbb{R})&a=0,b=0,c<0,d=0,\\
(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}_{-})&a=0,b=0,c=0,d=0,\\
(\mathbb{R},0)&a=0,b=0,c=0,d>0,\\
(\mathbb{R},0)&a=0,b>0,c=0,d\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Theorem 6.1

(Second-order optimality conditions for MPVIs)
.

Let
\bar{z}:=(\bar{x},\bar{y})
be a feasible point of MPVI. Assume that
\psi(x,y)
is convex in variable
y
and
that the lower level program satisfies the nondegeneracy condition

\nabla_{y}\psi(\bar{z})^{T}d^{*}=0,\ d^{*}\in\big(N_{P}(\psi(\bar{z}))\big)^{+}\ \Rightarrow d^{*}=0.

(107)

Then there exists a unique lower level multiplier
\eta^{*}\in N_{P}(\psi(\bar{z}))
such that
0=F(\bar{z})+\nabla_{y}\psi(\bar{z})^{T}\eta^{*}
.
Let direction
d
satisfy

\nabla f(\bar{z})d\leq 0,\ \ 0=\nabla[F+\nabla_{y}\psi^{T}\eta^{*}](\bar{z})d+\nabla_{y}\psi(\bar{z})^{T}e^{*},

(108)

where
(\nabla\psi(\bar{z})d,e^{*})\in T_{{\rm gph}N_{P}}(\psi(\bar{z}),\eta^{*})
.

(i)

Let
\bar{z}
be a local optimal solution of MPVI. Suppose that

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla\big(F+\nabla_{y}\psi^{T}\eta^{*}\big)(\bar{z})^{T}\lambda-\nabla\psi(\bar{z})^{T}\tau^{*}=0,\\
(\tau^{*},\nabla_{y}\psi(\bar{z})\lambda)\in N_{{\rm gph}N_{P}}\left((\psi(\bar{z}),\eta^{*});(\nabla\psi(\bar{z})d,e^{*})\right)\end{array}\right\}\Longrightarrow\lambda=0.

\lambda,\tau^{*}
such that
the first-order condition

\nabla f(\bar{z})-\nabla\big(F+\nabla_{y}\psi^{T}\eta^{*}\big)(\bar{z})^{T}\lambda+\nabla\psi(\bar{z})^{T}\tau^{*}=0

and the second-order condition

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f(\bar{z})(d,d)-\langle\lambda,\nabla^{2}\big(F+\nabla_{y}\psi^{T}\eta^{*}\big)(\bar{z})(d,d)+2\big(\nabla(\nabla_{y}\psi)(\bar{z})d\big)^{T}e^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\tau^{*},\nabla^{2}\psi(\bar{z})(d,d)\rangle\geq 0,

where

(\tau^{*},\nabla_{y}\psi(\bar{z})\lambda)\in N_{{\rm gph}N_{P}}\left((\psi(\bar{z}),\eta^{*});(\nabla\psi(\bar{z})d,e^{*})\right).

(ii)

Suppose that for every nonzero
d
satisfying (
108

(\alpha,\lambda,\tau^{*})
not all equal to zero satisfying
\alpha\geq 0
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha\nabla f(\bar{z})-\nabla\big(F+\nabla_{y}\psi^{T}\eta^{*}\big)(\bar{z})^{T}\lambda+\nabla\psi(\bar{z})^{T}\tau^{*}=0,\\
(\tau^{*},\nabla_{y}\psi(\bar{z})\lambda)\in\widehat{N}_{T_{{\rm gph}N_{P}}(\psi(\bar{z}),\eta^{*})}(\nabla\psi(\bar{z})d,e^{*})\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\alpha\nabla^{2}f(\bar{z})(d,d)-\langle\lambda,\nabla^{2}\big(F+\nabla_{y}\psi^{T}\eta^{*}\big)(\bar{z})(d,d)+2\big(\nabla(\nabla_{y}\psi)(\bar{z})d\big)^{T}e^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\tau^{*},\nabla^{2}\psi(\bar{z})(d,d)\rangle>0,

is an essential local minimizer of second order for MPVI.

Proof.

Note that
\Gamma(x)

\displaystyle\nabla_{y}\psi(\bar{z})^{T}d^{*}=0,\ d^{*}\in\big(N_{P}(\psi(\bar{z}))\big)^{+}\ \Rightarrow d^{*}=0.

7
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we have derived second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for very general problem GEP.
Our approach was to reformulate the GEP as a standard constrained optimization problem GP,
in which the set
\Omega
is the image of the solution set of a disjunctive system under a smooth mapping.
We developed a comprehensive variational analysis of
\Omega
and calculated the required second-order objects describing the curvature of the set
\Omega
.
As an illustration, the proposed framework was used to establish second-order optimality conditions for mathematical programs with variational inequality constraints (MPVIs).
Many other important problem classes can be modeled as GEPs, and we aim to apply these results to bilevel programs and mathematical programs with second-order cone complementarity constraints in future work.
