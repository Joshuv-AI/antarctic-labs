Phase-Space Contractions of Carrollian Black-Hole Thermodynamics
Yingnan Xu, Shuangshuang Chu

Abstract
We study Carrollian limits of Schwarzschild-AdS black-hole thermodynamics using covariant phase space. Allowing the cosmological constant to vary, we derive the extended Iyer-Wald identity and identify the renormalized bulk term proportional to δ ​ Λ \delta\Lambda with the generator-normalized thermodynamic volume contribution V ξ ​ δ ​ P V_{\xi}\,\delta P . We show that the Carroll limit contracts the full thermodynamic phase space together with the metric. For fixed Newton constant, the Lorentzian generator ∂ t \partial_{t} collapses to a zero-norm direction as c → 0 c\to 0 , yielding a degenerate sector with vanishing Hamiltonian variation, temperature and volume. Introducing ξ λ = c − α ∂ t \xi_{\lambda}=c^{-\alpha}\partial_{t} and G = c γ ​ G C G=c^{\gamma}G_{C} , we find that the extended first law scales as c 1 − α − γ c^{1-\alpha-\gamma} , so finite phase-space contractions require α + γ = 1 \alpha+\gamma=1 . The endpoint ( α , γ ) = ( 1 , 0 ) (\alpha,\gamma)=(1,0) , obtained by τ = c ​ t \tau=ct , is the ordinary non-degenerate Lorentzian finite-clock normalization. Carrollian finite first laws lie on the segment α < 1 \alpha<1 , hence γ = 1 − α > 0 \gamma=1-\alpha>0 , and give T → 0 T\to 0 , S → ∞ S\to\infty , with finite T ​ δ ​ S T\,\delta S and V ξ ​ δ ​ P V_{\xi}\,\delta P . We test the scaling principle for fixed-charge and fixed-rotation AdS black holes, and extend it to arbitrary spacetime dimension within the Schwarzschild-AdS family.

2
Extended Iyer-Wald identity with variable cosmological constant

We first recall the covariant phase-space identity in a form appropriate for
varying the cosmological constant. The starting point is four-dimensional
Einstein gravity,

\mathbf{L}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\left(R-2\Lambda\right)\bm{\epsilon},\qquad\Lambda=-\frac{3}{\ell^{2}},

(1)

where
\bm{\epsilon}
is the spacetime volume form. Under a general
variation of the metric and of the parameter
\Lambda
, the Lagrangian form
varies as

\delta\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{E}_{g}^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}+d\bm{\Theta}(g,\delta g)+\mathbf{E}_{\Lambda}\,\delta\Lambda,

(2)

with

\mathbf{E}_{\Lambda}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{L}}{\partial\Lambda}=-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\bm{\epsilon}.

(3)

For a vector field
\xi
, the Wald–Iyer formalism associates a Noether
charge
(D-2)
-form
\mathbf{Q}_{\xi}
and the surface form

\bm{\chi}_{\xi}=\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\xi}-\xi\cdot\bm{\Theta}.

(4)

For fixed couplings,
\bm{\chi}_{\xi}
is closed on shell for
perturbations satisfying the linearized field equations. Once
\Lambda
is
varied, this closure property is obstructed by the explicit parameter
variation in (
2
). On shell, and for a Killing vector

\xi
, one finds

d\bm{\chi}_{\xi}=-\xi\cdot\mathbf{E}_{\Lambda}\,\delta\Lambda=\frac{\delta\Lambda}{8\pi G}\,\xi\cdot\bm{\epsilon}.

(5)

This equation is the key identity underlying the extended first law. It
shows that the cosmological-constant variation appears as a bulk obstruction
to the usual statement that the Iyer-Wald surface form is closed.

Let
\Sigma
be a spacelike slice bounded by the horizon bifurcation surface

B
and the sphere at infinity
S_{\infty}
. Integrating
(
5
) over
\Sigma
gives

\int_{S_{\infty}}\bm{\chi}_{\xi}-\int_{B}\bm{\chi}_{\xi}=\frac{\delta\Lambda}{8\pi G}\int_{\Sigma}^{\rm ren}\xi\cdot\bm{\epsilon}.

(6)

The first boundary term defines the Hamiltonian variation
\delta H_{\xi}
.
For a stationary black hole, the horizon term gives
T_{\xi}\delta S
, where

S
is the Wald entropy. Defining the pressure and the

\xi
-normalized thermodynamic volume by

P=-\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G},\qquad V_{\xi}=-\int_{\Sigma}^{\rm ren}\xi\cdot\bm{\epsilon},

(7)

we obtain

\delta H_{\xi}=T_{\xi}\,\delta S+V_{\xi}\,\delta P.

(8)

Thus the
V_{\xi}\,\delta P
term is the renormalized bulk contribution
generated by
\delta\Lambda
in the covariant phase-space identity. The
subscript emphasizes that the volume is normalized by the same generator that
defines the Hamiltonian. Since
V_{\xi}
is linear in
\xi
, a rescaling of
the time generator rescales the thermodynamic volume in the same way as the
Hamiltonian and temperature. This is the Iyer-Wald form of extended AdS
black-hole mechanics, in which the cosmological constant is interpreted as
pressure and the mass as enthalpy

47
;
27
;
28
;
31
.

3
Schwarzschild-AdS with explicit Carroll scaling

We now apply (
8
) to the four-dimensional
Schwarzschild-AdS family while keeping the Carroll contraction parameter
explicit. The extension to arbitrary spacetime dimension is given in
section
9
. The metric is

ds^{2}=-c^{2}f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2},\qquad f(r)=1-\frac{r_{0}}{r}+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}.

(9)

The horizon radius is determined by

f(r_{h})=0,\qquad r_{0}=r_{h}+\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{\ell^{2}}.

(10)

At finite
c
, the standard static Killing generator is

\xi_{t}=\partial_{t},

(11)

with norm

g(\xi_{t},\xi_{t})=-c^{2}f(r).

(12)

Thus
\xi_{t}
is timelike outside the horizon for finite
c
. Its norm
vanishes as
c\to 0
, so the original relativistic time translation becomes
a degenerate thermal generator in the Carroll limit.

The Hamiltonian associated with
\xi_{t}
is

H_{t}=\frac{c\,r_{0}}{2G}=\frac{c}{2G}\left(r_{h}+\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(13)

Here
H_{t}
denotes the Hamiltonian charge associated with the generator

\xi_{t}=\partial_{t}
. Its normalization tracks the normalization of the time
flow and should be distinguished from a generator-independent mass parameter.
The corresponding temperature, entropy, pressure and

\xi_{t}
-normalized thermodynamic volume are

\displaystyle T_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{4\pi}\left(\frac{1}{r_{h}}+\frac{3r_{h}}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(14)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi r_{h}^{2}}{G},

(15)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8\pi G\ell^{2}},

(16)

\displaystyle V_{t}

\displaystyle\equiv V_{\xi_{t}}=\frac{4\pi cr_{h}^{3}}{3}.

(17)

The expression for
V_{t}=V_{\xi_{t}}
follows directly from the bulk term in
(
7
). Indeed,

\xi_{t}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}=c\,r^{2}\sin\theta\,dr\wedge d\theta\wedge d\phi.

(18)

The bare integral from the horizon to a cutoff
R
is

\int_{r_{h}}^{R}c\,r^{2}dr\int d\Omega_{2}=\frac{4\pi c}{3}\left(R^{3}-r_{h}^{3}\right).

(19)

Subtracting the pure AdS contribution,
\frac{4\pi c}{3}R^{3}
, gives

\int_{\Sigma}^{\rm ren}\xi_{t}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}=-\frac{4\pi cr_{h}^{3}}{3},\qquad V_{t}=-\int_{\Sigma}^{\rm ren}\xi_{t}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}=\frac{4\pi cr_{h}^{3}}{3}.

(20)

The factor of
c
is the consequence of using the Hamiltonian generator

\xi_{t}=\partial_{t}
. Using the finite Lorentzian clock generator

\partial_{\tau}=c^{-1}\partial_{t}
gives

V_{\tau}=V_{t}/c=4\pi r_{h}^{3}/3
.

It follows that

\delta H_{t}=T_{t}\,\delta S+V_{t}\,\delta P.

(21)

Equivalently, using (
10
),

\delta H_{t}=\frac{c}{2G}\left(1+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h}-\frac{c\,r_{h}^{3}}{G\ell^{3}}\delta\ell,

(22)

while

T_{t}\delta S=\frac{c}{2G}\left(1+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h},\qquad V_{t}\delta P=-\frac{c\,r_{h}^{3}}{G\ell^{3}}\delta\ell.

(23)

Thus the extended first law follows directly from the covariant phase-space
identity.

4
The collapsed strict Carroll sector

The Carroll group arises as the ultra-relativistic contraction of the
Poincare group
34
;
44
. In gravitational
settings, Carrollian structures naturally appear at null boundaries and
black-hole horizons
13
. More recently, Carroll black
holes have been formulated as Carrollian geometries with thermal properties
and Carroll extremal surfaces
15
;
46
. We use this
setting to analyze the contraction of the thermodynamic phase space.

The strict fixed-
G
limit keeps the generator
\xi_{t}=\partial_{t}
and
sends
c\to 0
with
r_{h}
and
\ell
fixed. From
(
12
),

g(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})=-c^{2}f(r)\longrightarrow 0.

(24)

Thus
\partial_{t}
remains a formal stationary symmetry, while its
thermal normalization collapses. Correspondingly,

H_{t}\to 0,\qquad T_{t}\to 0,\qquad V_{t}\to 0,\qquad S=\frac{\pi r_{h}^{2}}{G}\quad\text{fixed}.

(25)

The first law (
21
) contracts to the degenerate identity

0=0.

(26)

We therefore identify this sector as the collapsed Carrollian image of the
relativistic thermal generator.

5
Clock normalization and the Lorentzian endpoint

The strict Carroll limit described above shows that the original Lorentzian
generator
\partial_{t}
collapses to a zero-norm direction. We can avoid
this collapse by renormalizing the time generator. We introduce a family of
normalized time translations

\xi_{\lambda}=\lambda(c)\partial_{t},\qquad\lambda(c)=c^{-\alpha}.

(27)

Because the Iyer-Wald Hamiltonian, the surface gravity and the
generator-normalized thermodynamic volume are linear in
\xi
, one has

H_{\lambda}=\lambda H_{t},\qquad T_{\lambda}=\lambda T_{t},\qquad V_{\lambda}=\lambda V_{t}.

(28)

At fixed
G
,

H_{\lambda},\;T_{\lambda},\;V_{\lambda}\sim\lambda c=c^{1-\alpha}.

(29)

Thus
\lambda c\to 0
gives a degenerate sector, while

\lambda c\to\infty
gives a divergent one. A finite nonzero sector at
fixed
G
requires

\lambda c\to{\rm const.}

(30)

The canonical representative is

\xi_{C}=c^{-1}\partial_{t}=\partial_{\tau},\qquad\tau=ct.

(31)

In terms of this coordinate, the metric becomes

ds^{2}=-f(r)d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2}.

(32)

The metric is non-degenerate and Lorentzian. The

(\alpha,\gamma)=(1,0)
endpoint is therefore the ordinary Lorentzian
Schwarzschild-AdS black hole written in the finite clock
\tau
. This
endpoint is finite because the clock normalization restores the Lorentzian
time direction.

The associated thermodynamic variables are finite:

\displaystyle H_{C}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2G}\left(r_{h}+\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(33)

\displaystyle T_{C}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{1}{r_{h}}+\frac{3r_{h}}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(34)

\displaystyle V_{C}

\displaystyle\equiv V_{\xi_{C}}=\frac{4\pi r_{h}^{3}}{3}.

(35)

They obey

\delta H_{C}=T_{C}\,\delta S+V_{C}\,\delta P,

(36)

and the Smarr relation

H_{C}=2T_{C}S-2PV_{C}.

(37)

These are precisely the finite Lorentzian Schwarzschild-AdS thermodynamic
relations expressed in terms of the finite clock
\tau
.

It is useful to regard the normalization itself as an additional
thermodynamic modulus. If
\lambda
is allowed to vary at finite
c
, then

H_{\lambda}=\lambda H_{t}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\delta H_{\lambda}=\lambda\delta H_{t}+H_{t}\delta\lambda.

(38)

Using (
21
), this becomes

\delta H_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}\,\delta S+V_{\lambda}\,\delta P+\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}\,\delta\lambda,\qquad\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}=H_{t}.

(39)

Thus the clock normalization labels an additional direction in the enlarged
thermodynamic phase space.

The same conclusion is visible from the boundary thermal circle. If the
finite time coordinate is

\tau=ct,

(40)

then the Euclidean period rescales as

\beta_{\tau}=c\,\beta_{t}.

(41)

The Hamiltonian conjugate to
\tau
is

H_{\tau}=\frac{H_{t}}{c}.

(42)

Consequently,

\beta_{t}H_{t}=\beta_{\tau}H_{\tau},\qquad e^{-\beta_{t}H_{t}}=e^{-\beta_{\tau}H_{\tau}}.

(43)

This identity explains why the endpoint is finite: it keeps the ordinary
Lorentzian thermal weight finite in the clock
\tau
.

6
Strong-gravity Carroll contraction

A finite Carrollian first law can be obtained before reaching the Lorentzian
finite-clock endpoint. The simplest choice keeps
\xi_{t}=\partial_{t}
fixed
and scales Newton’s constant as

G=cG_{C}.

(44)

In this sector
c
is the contraction parameter. We take thermodynamic
variations at fixed
c
and fixed
G_{C}
, acting on the black-hole
parameters such as
r_{h}
and
\ell
. We then take the Carroll limit

c\to 0
.

Then

H_{t}=\frac{c}{2G}\left(r_{h}+\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{\ell^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{2G_{C}}\left(r_{h}+\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{\ell^{2}}\right)

(45)

remains finite. The temperature vanishes,

T_{t}=\frac{c}{4\pi}\left(\frac{1}{r_{h}}+\frac{3r_{h}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\longrightarrow 0,

(46)

while the entropy diverges,

S=\frac{\pi r_{h}^{2}}{cG_{C}}\longrightarrow\infty.

(47)

Nevertheless the entropy term in the first law remains finite:

T_{t}\,\delta S=\frac{1}{2G_{C}}\left(1+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h}.

(48)

The pressure and volume scale oppositely,

P=\frac{3}{8\pi cG_{C}\ell^{2}}\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad V_{t}=\frac{4\pi cr_{h}^{3}}{3}\longrightarrow 0,

(49)

while their product has a finite variation:

V_{t}\,\delta P=-\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{G_{C}\ell^{3}}\delta\ell.

(50)

Consequently the extended first law has a finite nontrivial limit:

\delta H_{t}=T_{t}\,\delta S+V_{t}\,\delta P=\frac{1}{2G_{C}}\left(1+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h}-\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{G_{C}\ell^{3}}\delta\ell.

(51)

In this sector the metric remains Carrollian in the
c\to 0
limit. It is a
zero-temperature, infinite-entropy contraction in which the products

T\,\delta S
and
V\,\delta P
remain finite. This makes precise, within
the extended phase space, the incompressible thermodynamic behavior found in
strict Carroll black holes
46
.

7
General phase-space contractions

The Lorentzian finite-clock endpoint and the strong-gravity Carroll sector are
two distinguished points in a more general contraction space. We parametrize
the contraction by allowing both the normalization of the time generator and
Newton’s constant to scale with
c
,

\xi_{\lambda}=c^{-\alpha}\partial_{t},\qquad G=c^{\gamma}G_{C}.

(52)

We take the contraction after evaluating thermodynamic variations at fixed

c
, fixed
G_{C}
, and fixed exponents. In this minimal contraction the
geometric parameters
r_{h}
and
\ell

assumed to be
O(1)

c

r_{h}
or

\ell

We distinguish the scaling of the first law from the geometric nature of the
limit. The norm of the normalized generator scales as

g(\xi_{\lambda},\xi_{\lambda})=-c^{2(1-\alpha)}f(r).

(53)

Thus
\alpha<1
gives a Carrollian degeneration of the time direction,

\alpha=1
gives the non-degenerate Lorentzian endpoint, and
\alpha>1

gives an over-renormalized divergent clock normalization. The point

is the ordinary Lorentzian finite-clock
normalization
\partial_{\tau}=c^{-1}\partial_{t}

The finite-

thermodynamic quantities scale as

H_{\lambda}\sim c^{1-\alpha-\gamma},\qquad T_{\lambda}\sim c^{1-\alpha},\qquad S\sim c^{-\gamma},\qquad V_{\lambda}\sim c^{1-\alpha},\qquad P\sim c^{-\gamma}.

Here
V_{\lambda}

\xi_{\lambda}

H_{\lambda}

H_{\lambda}
and
T_{\lambda}
do.
The three terms in the extended first law therefore scale homogeneously,

\delta H_{\lambda}\sim T_{\lambda}\delta S\sim V_{\lambda}\delta P\sim c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}.

8
Charged and rotating tests of the scaling principle

We now test the phase-space scaling principle in examples with additional
work terms. We use the standard Lorentzian charged and rotating AdS first
laws and show that they contract homogeneously under the same scaling of the
time generator and Newton constant used above. We work in four dimensions
and use the same convention as in the Schwarzschild analysis:

\tau=ct,\qquad\xi_{\lambda}=c^{-\alpha}\partial_{t},\qquad G=c^{\gamma}G_{C}.

We take thermodynamic variations at fixed
c
and fixed
G_{C}
, and then
take the contraction
c\to 0

8.1
Reissner–Nordström-AdS

We first consider Einstein-Maxwell theory with Lagrangian normalization

L=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\left(R-2\Lambda-F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right)\epsilon,\qquad\Lambda=-\frac{3}{\ell^{2}}.

(62)

With the finite Lorentzian clock
\tau
, the four-dimensional
Reissner–Nordström-AdS solution is

ds^{2}=-f(r)d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2},\qquad f(r)=1-\frac{2m}{r}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}.

(63)

The horizon radius
r_{h}
obeys
f(r_{h})=0
, hence

m=\frac{r_{h}}{2}\left(1+\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(64)

We choose the gauge potential regular at the horizon,

A=q\left(\frac{1}{r_{h}}-\frac{1}{r}\right)d\tau,

(65)

so that the electrostatic potential conjugate to the finite-clock generator

\partial_{\tau}
is

\Phi_{\tau}=\frac{q}{r_{h}}.

(66)

In these conventions the finite-clock thermodynamic variables are

\displaystyle H_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{G}=\frac{r_{h}}{2G}\left(1+\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(67)

\displaystyle T_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{h}}\left(1-\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(68)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi r_{h}^{2}}{G},

(69)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8\pi G\ell^{2}},

(70)

\displaystyle V_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi r_{h}^{3}}{3},

(71)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{G}.

(72)

They obey the extended charged first law

\delta H_{\tau}=T_{\tau}\delta S+V_{\tau}\delta P+\Phi_{\tau}\delta Q.

(73)

Indeed, using (
67
),

\delta H_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2G}\left(1-\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h}+\frac{q}{Gr_{h}}\delta q-\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{G\ell^{3}}\delta\ell,

(74)

while

\displaystyle T_{\tau}\delta S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2G}\left(1-\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h},

(75)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau}\delta Q

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{Gr_{h}}\delta q,

(76)

\displaystyle V_{\tau}\delta P

\displaystyle=-\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{G\ell^{3}}\delta\ell.

(77)

Equations (
74
)–(
77
) give
(
73
) explicitly.

We now rewrite the solution in the Carroll scaling coordinate
t
, with

\tau=ct
. Since
d\tau=c\,dt
, the gauge field becomes

A=A_{t}(r)dt,\qquad A_{t}=cA_{\tau}.

(78)

Therefore the thermodynamic quantities conjugate to
\partial_{t}
are

H_{t}=cH_{\tau},\qquad T_{t}=cT_{\tau},\qquad V_{t}=cV_{\tau},\qquad\Phi_{t}=c\Phi_{\tau}.

(79)

For the normalized generator

\xi_{\lambda}=c^{-\alpha}\partial_{t},

(80)

one obtains

H_{\lambda}=c^{1-\alpha}H_{\tau},\qquad T_{\lambda}=c^{1-\alpha}T_{\tau},\qquad V_{\lambda}=c^{1-\alpha}V_{\tau},\qquad\Phi_{\lambda}=c^{1-\alpha}\Phi_{\tau}.

(81)

Using
G=c^{\gamma}G_{C}
, the explicit contracted variables are

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}\frac{r_{h}}{2G_{C}}\left(1+\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(82)

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{4\pi r_{h}}\left(1-\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(83)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=c^{-\gamma}\frac{\pi r_{h}^{2}}{G_{C}},

(84)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=c^{-\gamma}\frac{3}{8\pi G_{C}\ell^{2}},

(85)

\displaystyle V_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha}\frac{4\pi r_{h}^{3}}{3},

(86)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=c^{-\gamma}\frac{q}{G_{C}},

(87)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha}\frac{q}{r_{h}}.

(88)

Therefore

\displaystyle\delta H_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}\left[\frac{1}{2G_{C}}\left(1-\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h}+\frac{q}{G_{C}r_{h}}\delta q-\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{G_{C}\ell^{3}}\delta\ell\right],

(89)

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}\delta S

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}\frac{1}{2G_{C}}\left(1-\frac{q^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}+\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h},

(90)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}\delta Q

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}\frac{q}{G_{C}r_{h}}\delta q,

(91)

\displaystyle V_{\lambda}\delta P

\displaystyle=-c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{G_{C}\ell^{3}}\delta\ell.

(92)

Thus the charged first law becomes

\delta H_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}\delta S+V_{\lambda}\delta P+\Phi_{\lambda}\delta Q,

(93)

and every term carries the same overall factor,

\delta H_{\lambda}\sim T_{\lambda}\delta S\sim V_{\lambda}\delta P\sim\Phi_{\lambda}\delta Q\sim c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}.

(94)

For fixed geometric charge parameter
q
, the finite nonzero charged sector
therefore obeys the same condition as in the neutral case,

\alpha+\gamma=1.

(95)

Assigning an independent scaling to
q
gives additional charged
contraction sectors.

8.2
Kerr-AdS

We next consider the rotating AdS black hole. In the finite Lorentzian clock

\tau
, the four-dimensional Kerr-AdS metric can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta_{r}}{\rho^{2}}\left(d\tau-\frac{a\sin^{2}\theta}{\Xi}d\phi\right)^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}dr^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta_{\theta}}d\theta^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{\Delta_{\theta}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\left(a\,d\tau-\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Xi}d\phi\right)^{2},

(96)

where

\Delta_{r}=(r^{2}+a^{2})\left(1+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)-2mr,\qquad\Delta_{\theta}=1-\frac{a^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\cos^{2}\theta,

(97)

and

\rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\qquad\Xi=1-\frac{a^{2}}{\ell^{2}}.

(98)

The horizon radius
r_{h}
is the largest root of
\Delta_{r}(r_{h})=0
, so

m=\frac{(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})(1+r_{h}^{2}/\ell^{2})}{2r_{h}}.

(99)

The thermodynamic quantities in the non-rotating frame at infinity are

\displaystyle H_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{G\Xi^{2}},

(100)

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\frac{am}{G\Xi^{2}},

(101)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})}{G\Xi},

(102)

\displaystyle T_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{h}^{2}-a^{2}+\ell^{-2}\left(3r_{h}^{4}+a^{2}r_{h}^{2}\right)}{4\pi r_{h}(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})},

(103)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{a(1+r_{h}^{2}/\ell^{2})}{r_{h}^{2}+a^{2}},

(104)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8\pi G\ell^{2}},

(105)

\displaystyle V_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})}{3r_{h}\Xi^{2}}\left(2r_{h}^{2}+a^{2}-\frac{a^{2}r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(106)

These quantities obey the standard Kerr-AdS extended first law

\delta H_{\tau}=T_{\tau}\delta S+\Omega_{\tau}\delta J+V_{\tau}\delta P.

(107)

We now pass to the Carroll scaling coordinate
\tau=ct
. Since

d\tau=c\,dt
, the Kerr-AdS line element has the component scalings

g_{tt}=c^{2}g_{\tau\tau},\qquad g_{t\phi}=cg_{\tau\phi},\qquad g_{\phi\phi}=g_{\phi\phi}^{(\tau)}.

(108)

Equivalently,

g_{tt}\sim c^{2},\qquad g_{t\phi}\sim c,\qquad g_{\phi\phi}\sim O(1).

(109)

The horizon generator in the finite clock is

\chi_{\tau}=\partial_{\tau}+\Omega_{\tau}\partial_{\phi}.

(110)

Since
\partial_{t}=c\,\partial_{\tau}
, the corresponding generator with
leading term
\partial_{t}
is

\chi_{t}=\partial_{t}+\Omega_{t}\partial_{\phi}=c\left(\partial_{\tau}+\Omega_{\tau}\partial_{\phi}\right),\qquad\Omega_{t}=c\Omega_{\tau}.

(111)

For the normalized generator

\chi_{\lambda}=c^{-\alpha}\chi_{t}=c^{-\alpha}\partial_{t}+\Omega_{\lambda}\partial_{\phi},

(112)

the angular velocity scales as

\Omega_{\lambda}=c^{-\alpha}\Omega_{t}=c^{1-\alpha}\Omega_{\tau}.

(113)

Similarly, the normalized time generator satisfies

g(\xi_{\lambda},\xi_{\lambda})\sim c^{2(1-\alpha)},\qquad g(\xi_{\lambda},\partial_{\phi})\sim c^{1-\alpha}.

(114)

Thus
\alpha<1
gives a Carrollian degeneration of the time direction,
whereas
\alpha=1
gives the non-degenerate Lorentzian finite-clock
endpoint.

The thermodynamic quantities associated with the normalized time translation
are

H_{\lambda}=c^{1-\alpha}H_{\tau},\qquad T_{\lambda}=c^{1-\alpha}T_{\tau},\qquad\Omega_{\lambda}=c^{1-\alpha}\Omega_{\tau},\qquad V_{\lambda}=c^{1-\alpha}V_{\tau}.

(115)

With
G=c^{\gamma}G_{C}
, the explicit contracted variables are

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}\frac{m}{G_{C}\Xi^{2}},

(116)

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=c^{-\gamma}\frac{am}{G_{C}\Xi^{2}},

(117)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=c^{-\gamma}\frac{\pi(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})}{G_{C}\Xi},

(118)

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha}\frac{r_{h}^{2}-a^{2}+\ell^{-2}\left(3r_{h}^{4}+a^{2}r_{h}^{2}\right)}{4\pi r_{h}(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})},

(119)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha}\frac{a(1+r_{h}^{2}/\ell^{2})}{r_{h}^{2}+a^{2}},

(120)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=c^{-\gamma}\frac{3}{8\pi G_{C}\ell^{2}},

(121)

\displaystyle V_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=c^{1-\alpha}\frac{2\pi(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})}{3r_{h}\Xi^{2}}\left(2r_{h}^{2}+a^{2}-\frac{a^{2}r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(122)

Substituting these formulae into the Kerr-AdS first law gives

\delta H_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}\delta S+\Omega_{\lambda}\delta J+V_{\lambda}\delta P.

(123)

Each term carries the same overall scaling:

\delta H_{\lambda}\sim T_{\lambda}\delta S\sim\Omega_{\lambda}\delta J\sim V_{\lambda}\delta P\sim c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}.

(124)

Therefore the finite nonzero rotating sector with fixed geometric rotation
parameter
a
again satisfies

\alpha+\gamma=1.

(125)

The charged and rotating examples therefore support the same phase-space
scaling principle found in the Schwarzschild-AdS sector. With fixed
geometric parameters
q
and
a
, all work terms scale homogeneously with

c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}
. Independent scalings of
q
or
a
define
further contraction sectors, but they introduce additional exponents beyond
the minimal phase-space contraction studied here. We interpret the Kerr-AdS calculation as a Lorentzian rotating
phase-space test of the contraction principle. Indeed, in the Carrollian
sector
\alpha<1
, (
113
) gives

\Omega_{\lambda}\sim c^{1-\alpha}\to 0
, and
(
114
) shows that the mixed time-rotation
component also degenerates. Thus the rotational motion is washed out in the
Carroll limit, while the work term
\Omega_{\lambda}\delta J
can still have a
finite phase-space limit when
\alpha+\gamma=1
. This is consistent with the no-go theorem of
29
.

9
Extension to
D
dimensions

The preceding analysis extends straightforwardly to arbitrary spacetime
dimension. This shows that the contraction condition

\alpha+\gamma=1
follows from the homogeneous scaling of the extended
phase space.

We consider the
D
-dimensional Schwarzschild-AdS family

ds^{2}=-c^{2}f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega_{D-2}^{2},

(126)

with

f(r)=1-\frac{\mu}{r^{D-3}}+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}.

(127)

Here
d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}
is the metric on the unit
S^{D-2}
, whose volume
we denote by
\Omega_{D-2}
. The horizon condition
f(r_{h})=0
gives

\mu=r_{h}^{D-3}\left(1+\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(128)

For the finite-
c
generator
\xi_{t}=\partial_{t}
, the Iyer-Wald
Hamiltonian is

H_{t}=c\,\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{16\pi G}\,\mu=c\,\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{16\pi G}r_{h}^{D-3}\left(1+\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right).

(129)

The temperature, entropy, pressure and generator-normalized thermodynamic
volume are

\displaystyle T_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{4\pi}\left(\frac{D-3}{r_{h}}+\frac{D-1}{\ell^{2}}r_{h}\right),

(130)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{D-2}r_{h}^{D-2}}{4G},

(131)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}=\frac{(D-1)(D-2)}{16\pi G\ell^{2}},

(132)

\displaystyle V_{t}

\displaystyle=c\,\frac{\Omega_{D-2}r_{h}^{D-1}}{D-1}.

(133)

The expression for
V_{t}
again follows from the renormalized bulk term in
the extended Iyer-Wald identity. Indeed,

\xi_{t}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}=c\,r^{D-2}dr\wedge d\Omega_{D-2},

(134)

so that subtracting the pure AdS contribution gives

\int_{\Sigma}^{\rm ren}\xi_{t}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}=-c\,\frac{\Omega_{D-2}r_{h}^{D-1}}{D-1},\qquad V_{t}=-\int_{\Sigma}^{\rm ren}\xi_{t}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}.

(135)

One may verify explicitly that these quantities obey the extended first law

\delta H_{t}=T_{t}\,\delta S+V_{t}\,\delta P.

(136)

Using (
128
), the variation of the Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\delta H_{t}

\displaystyle=c\,\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{16\pi G}\left[\left((D-3)r_{h}^{D-4}+(D-1)\frac{r_{h}^{D-2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h}-\frac{2r_{h}^{D-1}}{\ell^{3}}\delta\ell\right].

(137)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle T_{t}\,\delta S

\displaystyle=c\,\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{16\pi G}\left((D-3)r_{h}^{D-4}+(D-1)\frac{r_{h}^{D-2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\delta r_{h},

(138)

\displaystyle V_{t}\,\delta P

\displaystyle=-c\,\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{8\pi G}\frac{r_{h}^{D-1}}{\ell^{3}}\delta\ell.

(139)

Equations (
137
)–(
139
) reproduce
(
136
). The corresponding Smarr relation is

(D-3)H_{t}=(D-2)T_{t}S-2PV_{t}.

(140)

This reduces to
H_{t}=2T_{t}S-2PV_{t}
in four dimensions.

We now repeat the contraction analysis. Let

\xi_{\lambda}=c^{-\alpha}\partial_{t},\qquad G=c^{\gamma}G_{C}.

(141)

From (
129
)–(
133
), the individual quantities scale as

H_{\lambda}\sim c^{1-\alpha-\gamma},\qquad T_{\lambda}\sim c^{1-\alpha},\qquad S\sim c^{-\gamma},\qquad V_{\lambda}\sim c^{1-\alpha},\qquad P\sim c^{-\gamma}.

(142)

Therefore

\delta H_{\lambda}\sim T_{\lambda}\delta S\sim V_{\lambda}\delta P\sim c^{1-\alpha-\gamma}.

(143)

Thus the condition for a finite nonzero first law is again

\alpha+\gamma=1.

(144)

As in four dimensions, the Carrollian segment is
\alpha<1
, and the finite
first-law condition then gives
\gamma=1-\alpha>0
. Therefore the
conclusion is dimension-independent: within the Schwarzschild-AdS family,
finite Carrollian thermodynamics gives
T\to 0
,
S\to\infty
, with finite
first-law products. The endpoint
(\alpha,\gamma)=(1,0)
remains
Lorentzian in any dimension, while
(\alpha,\gamma)=(0,1)
is the simplest
strong-gravity Carrollian representative.

10
Conclusion

We have shown that Carrollian black-hole thermodynamics is best understood as
a contraction of the full thermodynamic phase space. The extended
Iyer-Wald identity with variable cosmological constant gives the appropriate
bookkeeping: the bulk source in (
5
) produces the
generator-normalized volume in (
7
), and hence the extended
first law (
8
). This makes it possible to track, in
one framework, the scaling of the Hamiltonian charge, temperature, entropy,
pressure and thermodynamic volume.

The fixed-
G
Carroll limit with the original generator
\partial_{t}

collapses to the degenerate sector (
25
). By contrast,
the clock-renormalized generator

\xi_{C}=c^{-1}\partial_{t}=\partial_{\tau}
gives the finite Lorentzian endpoint
described by (
36
) and (
37
). The
Carrollian finite sectors are obtained only after correlating the time
normalization with the scaling of Newton’s constant. In the notation of
(
52
), the first law scales as
(
55
), so a finite nonzero contraction requires
(
56
). Imposing the Carrollian condition
(
57
) then gives the strong-gravity scaling
(
59
) and the thermodynamic behavior summarized in
(
60
) and (
61
). The
strong-gravity point
G=cG_{C}
is the simplest representative of this class.

The charged, rotating and higher-dimensional examples support the same
phase-space interpretation. With fixed geometric charge and rotation
parameters, the additional work terms
\Phi\,\delta Q
and

\Omega\,\delta J
carry the same homogeneous scaling as the neutral terms,
as in (
94
) and
(
124
). In the
D
-dimensional
Schwarzschild-AdS family, the coefficients and Smarr relation take their
standard dimension-dependent forms, but the finite-sector condition remains
(
144
). Thus the zero-temperature,
divergent-entropy behavior is not an isolated feature of a particular
solution; it is the thermodynamic signature of finite Carrollian
phase-space contractions.

Several extensions remain. Higher-derivative gravities are a natural next
step, since the Wald entropy, Noether charge and renormalized bulk volume may
scale differently from their Einstein-gravity counterparts. It would also be
useful to analyze charged and rotating contractions with independently scaled
charge or rotation parameters, and to derive the boundary Carrollian stress
tensor for the
\alpha<1
sectors. These directions would test whether the
finite first-law products identified here admit a microscopic interpretation
in terms of a Carrollian or BMS-invariant field theory.

Appendix A
Conventions and limiting procedure

Throughout the paper we use
c
as a dimensionless contraction parameter
that keeps track of the relative normalization between the Lorentzian clock

\tau
and the Carroll scaling coordinate
t
,

\tau=ct.

(145)

We write the formulas in gravitational units with
k_{B}=\hbar=1
. The
parameter
c
appearing in the metric is a bookkeeping parameter for the
Carroll contraction of the time direction, while all physical-unit factors
associated with
\hbar
are held fixed. With these conventions the
Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is

S=\frac{A}{4G}.

(146)

This convention distinguishes the present phase-space contraction from the
tantum-gravity triple scaling of
14
, where
c
,

G_{N}
, and
\hbar
are scaled simultaneously while
G_{N}c^{-4}
and

\hbar c
are kept fixed. We classify contractions of classical AdS
black-hole thermodynamics at fixed
\hbar=1
.

We allow Newton’s constant to scale with the contraction parameter as

G=c^{\gamma}G_{C},

(147)

where
G_{C}
is held fixed. For each fixed nonzero
c
, we take
thermodynamic variations at fixed
c
, fixed
G_{C}
, and fixed contraction
exponents. Thus
G=c^{\gamma}G_{C}
is fixed during a thermodynamic
variation. We evaluate the finite-
c
variation first and take the
contraction limit
c\to 0
afterwards.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, we keep the geometric black-hole
parameters at
O(1)
in the contraction. In the Schwarzschild-AdS sector
this means

r_{h}=O(1),\qquad\ell=O(1),\qquad\delta r_{h}=O(1),\qquad\delta\ell=O(1).

(148)

In charged or rotating examples we similarly keep the geometric charge and
rotation parameters at
O(1)
. The finite-sector condition

\alpha+\gamma=1
derived in the main text is therefore the condition for
this minimal phase-space contraction. Additional
c
-dependent scalings of

r_{h}
,
\ell
, the charge parameter, or the rotation parameter define
further contraction sectors with correspondingly modified scaling
constraints.

Finally, we use the thermodynamic volume conjugate to the chosen Hamiltonian
generator
\xi
. We denote it by
V_{\xi}
, or by
V_{t}
,
V_{C}
, and

V_{\lambda}
for the generators
\partial_{t}
,

c^{-1}\partial_{t}=\partial_{\tau}
, and
c^{-\alpha}\partial_{t}
,
respectively. This generator-normalized volume is linear in
\xi
, because
it is defined by the Iyer-Wald bulk term

V_{\xi}=-\int_{\Sigma}^{\rm ren}\xi\cdot\bm{\epsilon}.

(149)

Consequently
V_{t}\sim c
, whereas
V_{C}=V_{t}/c
is finite in the Lorentzian
clock. The
c
-scaling of
V_{\xi}
follows directly from the normalization
of the time translation whose Hamiltonian first law is being considered.
