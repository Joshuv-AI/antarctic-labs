FINAL TECHNICAL REPORT
 Project Title: Robust Dark Energy Constraints with the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Survey
 Federal Award Number: DE-SC0021165
 Principal Investigator: Lado Samushia
 Institution: Kansas State University
 Project Period: September 1, 2020 – August 31, 2025

 1. Executive Summary

 This project developed and applied advanced theoretical, computational, and data-analysis
 methodologies to extract robust and precise cosmological constraints from the Dark Energy
 Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI). The work focused on maximizing the scienti c return of DESI
 through optimized survey strategy, novel higher-order clustering statistics, improved modeling
 of small-scale structure, and rigorous mitigation of observational systematics.

 Over the award period, the project made substantial contributions to DESI science planning,
 produced new methods for bispectrum and three-point correlation function analyses,
 advanced constraints on primordial non-Gaussianity, and delivered widely used software tools.
 The project also played a major role in training graduate students and a postdoctoral
 researcher who contributed directly to DESI key projects. The results have signi cantly
 enhanced the cosmological reach of DESI and provide a strong foundation for future surveys
 such as DESI-II and Stage-V experiments.

 2. Project Goals and Scienti c Scope

 The overarching goal was to extract robust dark energy and cosmological constraints from
 DESI data. This objective was pursued through ve closely connected e orts:

 Optimization of DESI observational strategy and target selection through forecasting.

 Development of higher-order clustering statistics, including the galaxy bispectrum and three-
 point correlation functions.

 Exploitation of small-scale clustering to constrain cosmology and the galaxy–halo connection.

 Measurement of primordial non-Gaussianity from imaging and spectroscopic data.

 Identi cation, modeling, and mitigation of observational systematics to levels well below DESI
 statistical precision.

 3. Technical Accomplishments
 3.1 Survey Strategy and Forecasting

 The PI led DESI forecasting e orts during the early and intermediate phases of the survey.
 Forecasting tools developed within this project were used to evaluate target selection choices,
 observing strategies, and expected performance for BAO and RSD measurements. These
 forecasts informed o cial DESI decisions and later contributed to planning e orts for DESI-II.
 The forecasting infrastructure developed under this project remains in active use within the
 collaboration.

 3.3 Small-Scale Clustering and the Galaxy–Halo Connection

 The project developed new methods for extracting cosmological and galaxy–halo information
 from small-scale clustering while avoiding scales dominated by observational systematics.
 Innovations include:

 Fast computation of small-scale three-point correlation functions.

 Extension of tabulation-based methods (CorrTab) to higher-order statistics and multitracer
 analyses.

 Demonstration that small-scale three-point statistics signi cantly improve constraints on halo
 occupation distribution (HOD) parameters.

 Identi cation of clustering features that are relatively insensitive to galaxy-formation
 uncertainties, enabling cosmological inference from small scales.

 These methods were tested extensively on DESI mock catalogs and are directly relevant to
 ongoing DESI analyses.

 3.4 Primordial Non-Gaussianity

 The project produced some of the most detailed studies of primordial non-Gaussianity
 achievable with DESI imaging and spectroscopic data. Major achievements include:

 Development of machine-learning-based techniques to mitigate imaging systematics.

 Creation of realistic simulations incorporating non-Gaussian initial conditions and observational
 systematics.

 Measurement of constraints on local primordial non-Gaussianity from photometric DESI
 samples and cross-correlations with CMB lensing data.

 These methods are now considered standard within DESI large-scale structure analyses.

3.5 Systematics Mitigation and Validation Infrastructure

A substantial e ort was devoted to understanding and mitigating systematics, including:

Optimization of bright-star masking for DESI ELG samples.

Modeling and validation of imaging systematics and window-function e ects.

Development of pipelines for imprinting systematics into mock catalogs.

Extensive contributions to DESI mock challenges and covariance estimation e orts.

These activities were essential for ensuring the robustness of DESI cosmological
measurements.

4. Software and Data Products

The project produced multiple widely used, publicly available software packages, including:

GoFish: DESI forecasting software for BAO and RSD constraints.

Bispectrum: Fast computation of galaxy power spectra and bispectra.

3pt_survey: Small-scale three-point correlation function measurements.

ELGMask: Optimization of bright-star masks for DESI ELG samples.

AddSys: Injection of realistic imaging systematics into simulations.

Extensions of CorrTab to higher-order and multitracer statistics.

These tools have been adopted broadly within the DESI collaboration and beyond.

5. Publications

The project resulted in numerous peer-reviewed publications in leading journals, including
Physical Review D, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, and the Journal of
Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics. Topics include DESI forecasting, higher-order statistics,
bispectrum BAO modeling, primordial non-Gaussianity, and observational systematics. Several
additional works were submitted or accepted during the award period.

6. Training and Workforce Development

The project supported and trained multiple early-career scientists, including one postdoctoral
researcher and several Ph.D. students. Trainees gained expertise in cosmological theory,
simulations, high-performance computing, statistical inference, and machine learning. Many
held leadership roles within DESI working groups and key projects. Alumni of the project have
transitioned to successful careers in academia and industry.

7. Broader Impact and Legacy

This project substantially enhanced the scienti c output of DESI by enabling new analyses,
improving robustness against systematics, and expanding the use of higher-order statistics.

8. Summary

Over its full duration, this project delivered major methodological, scienti c, and infrastructural
