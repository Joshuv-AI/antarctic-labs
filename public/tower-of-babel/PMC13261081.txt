Inferior Vena Cava Filter Fracture Presenting as Deep Vein Thrombosis
Authors: Koki Shigemoto, Takuya Otsuki, Kosuke Ishizuka, Miharu Goto, Kenya Ie, Yoshiyuki Ohira, Chiaki Okuse
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTLong‐term indwelling inferior vena cava filters can cause device fracture and chronic caval occlusion, leading to deep vein thrombosis. Clinicians should consider filter‐related complications in patients with a history of inferior vena cava filter placement who present with lower extremity edema or thrombosis even many years after placement.Keywords: deep vein thrombosis, device‐related complications, filter fracture, inferior vena cava filter, venous thromboembolism

Case

A 65‐year‐old Japanese woman presented with a 1‐month history of bilateral lower leg pain and edema. She had a history of an inferior vena cava (IVC) filter placement 30 years ago for thrombosis secondary to compression by uterine fibroids. She was lost to follow‐up after the procedure. Her medications were brexpiprazole and escitalopram. Physical examination revealed tenderness and pitting edema of both lower legs. Laboratory tests showed a D‐dimer level of 10.5 μg/mL but no other abnormal findings. Venous ultrasonography revealed an acute thrombus with absent flow in the left popliteal vein and chronic thrombus in the right femoral vein; the right popliteal vein was not adequately visualized. Computed tomography (CT) demonstrated organized thrombus and calcification localized around the fractured IVC filter, with no evidence of acute thrombus in the common iliac veins (Figure 1). Prominent collateral circulation through the azygos and hemiazygos veins was also identified, consistent with chronic IVC occlusion complicated by acute DVT (Figure 2). Anticoagulant therapy with edoxaban (60 mg once daily) resulted in clinical improvement. IVC filter retrieval and other surgical interventions were considered but deferred because of the high operative risk.

Coronal computed tomography (CT) of the inferior vena cava (IVC) demonstrating the fractured struts of the IVC filter (arrow) and the associated organized thrombus.

Axial CT section demonstrating marked dilation of the azygous (Az) and hemiazygos (Hz) veins, serving as prominent collateral pathways to bypass the occluded IVC.

The inferior vena cava filter (IVCF) is an established adjunctive device to anticoagulation therapy for the prevention of deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary thromboembolism; however, long‐term implantation has been associated with various complications, including occlusion, fracture, and perforation [1, 2]. In recent years, the use of retrievable filters has increased substantially [3]. Nevertheless, owing to insufficient patient follow‐up and limited retrieval systems at medical institutions, the overall retrieval rate remains low, with one study reporting 15.3% at a median follow‐up of 1.2 years [2]. Although retrieval success rates are reported to reach 98% when attempted within 3 months of placement [1], a considerable proportion of filters remain in situ, effectively functioning as permanent implants [2]. In conclusion, meticulous longitudinal follow‐up and prompt retrieval at the earliest feasible time after IVCF placement are recommended to minimize long‐term complications.
