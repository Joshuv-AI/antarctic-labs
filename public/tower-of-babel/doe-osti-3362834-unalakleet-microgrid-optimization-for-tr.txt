1.0 Executive Summary

The Unalakleet Microgrid Optimization Project aimed to strengthen the reliability and efficiency of the isolated
electric power system that serves the Tribal community of Unalakleet, Alaska. The community relies entirely on
a local wind-diesel microgrid, consisting of four 475 kW diesel generators and six 100 kW wind turbines, to
provide electricity to approximately 745 residents and Tribal facilities. Because Unalakleet is not on a road
system and is located nearly 400 miles from the nearest major power grid, maintaining a resilient and efficient
local energy system is critical. The scope of this project included upgrading a portion of the transmission line
between the wind farm and the power plant to increase voltage and reduce line losses, along with modernizing
the Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) system to improve monitoring, control, and data
management of the power system. These upgrades were designed to increase wind energy utilization, reduce
diesel fuel consumption by tens of thousands of gallons annually, and improve overall grid stability. By
allowing more of the community’s electricity to be supplied by local renewable wind resources, the project was
designed to lower operating costs, reduce dependence on imported fuel, and strengthen the long-term
resilience of the power system. These improvements represent an important step toward the community’s long-
term energy vision of expanding renewable generation, incorporating energy storage, and eventually achieving
“diesels-off” operation, where essential Tribal loads are powered primarily by local renewable resources.

3.0 Project Objectives
Project objectives included a high voltage line upgrade along a portion of the transmission line between the
wind farm and the power plant, replacement of associated transformers, as well as improvements to the
Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) and associated data management systems in the Unalakleet
Valley Electric Cooperative (UVEC) power plant. The proposed upgrades were estimated to reduce annual fuel
consumption of the utility and community by reducing the amount of time two diesel generators were required
to operate simultaneously, eliminate foundational vulnerabilities in the transmission/distribution lines, improve
power grid stability, and increase the portion of essential Tribal loads powered by local, renewable energy
resources, namely 600 kW of wind capacity.

5.0 Conclusions and Recommendations
The project outcomes are described below in alignment with the key performance indicators (KPI) identified in
the metering and verification plan. The plan focuses on system reliability and quality, energy production and
availability of renewables, and diesel and cost savings. Substantial construction of the project was completed in
December 2024. Therefore, data from 2023 and 2024 (pre-retrofits) are compared to 2025 (post-retrofits) in the
following analysis. All system upgrades included in the original grant scope as described in the Project Objectives
section of this report were completed, including high voltage transmission/distribution system upgrades,
residential/commercial/utility transformer upgrades, and decommissioning/rerouting of vulnerable existing
power line. The results of this project are foundational to the Tribe's energy vision of a reliable, resilient power
system that maximizes locally available energy sources to stabilize the cost of energy.

Conclusions
The project achieved its primary objectives. Reliability improvements are clearly demonstrated: outages
occurred less frequently, were substantially shorter in duration, and an increasing share resolved within trivial
timeframes. The wind generation and reactive power results are similarly consistent: the upgrades eliminated
prior system constraints, enabling more turbines to remain online, wind penetration to regularly exceed 50%
during high-wind months, and the plant to maintain stability on a single genset under conditions that previously
required two or more. In cases where individual turbines underperformed, the limitation was attributable to the
turbines themselves rather than to grid acceptance.

Fuel use is the one outcome that requires additional time to interpret. Annual consumption increased 15%, but
the 16% reduction observed in December — the windiest month, and the period in which the upgrades should
yield the greatest benefit — suggests that load growth and wind variability are the likely drivers of the annual
increase rather than a shortfall in the retrofits. A normalized metric, such as fuel consumption per kWh served,
tracked over multiple years, will be necessary to resolve this question.

Taken together, the results validate the technical premise of the project. The next phase of value will depend
less on additional infrastructure investment than on sustained operational follow-through: maintaining turbine
availability and dispatching to maximize single-genset operation.

6.0 Lessons Learned
Schedule and logistics in remote Alaska
The project's most persistent challenge was schedule. Initial delays from COVID-19 and Typhoon Merbok pushed
the project well past its original timeline, and once construction resumed, late delivery of long-lead materials
caused the team to miss the planned barge window. In a community accessible only by air and sea, a missed
barge does not simply delay a shipment by days — it can defer work by months and force construction into the
winter season, when short daylight hours and sub-zero temperatures meaningfully reduce productivity. For
future remote-Alaska projects, schedule contingencies should be built around barge cycles rather than calendar
quarters, and procurement of long-lead items should begin as early in the project as funding mechanics allow.

Baseline data collection should precede construction
Individual wind turbine production tracking did not begin until late May 2024, which left only seven months of
pre-retrofit turbine-level data and required the post-retrofit analysis to be limited to a June–December
comparison window. This was a workable but meaningfully constrained basis for evaluating wind generation
outcomes. Future projects should treat baseline metering and data logging as a prerequisite to construction
rather than as part of the scope being constructed, ideally establishing at least a full year of pre-retrofit data
across all key performance indicators.

Data infrastructure is foundational, not auxiliary
The SCADA and historian upgrades delivered under this project were essential not only to operations but to the
verification of every other project outcome. The reactive power and concurrent-genset analyses presented in
this report would not have been possible without the modernized data systems. Hybrid microgrid projects
elsewhere should plan their controls and data investments early in the project lifecycle, since these systems
determine what can be measured, optimized, and ultimately demonstrated to funders.

Fuel savings require normalized, multi-year evaluation
Annual fuel consumption is sensitive to load growth and inter-annual wind variability, neither of which can be
controlled within a single project year. The 15% increase in 2025 annual fuel use, set against a 16% decrease in
December — the windiest month of the year — illustrates how raw annual totals can mislead in either direction.
Future M&V plans for hybrid microgrid projects should specify a normalized metric such as fuel consumption per
kWh served and commit to a multi-year evaluation window.

Realized value depends on operational follow-through
The upgrades removed prior grid-side constraints on wind integration and single-genset operation; with those
constraints removed, the binding limits on realized value have shifted to turbine availability and dispatch
practice. Where individual turbines underperformed in 2025, the cause was turbine-side maintenance rather
than grid acceptance. Sustaining the project's benefits will depend on a maintenance program that keeps all six
turbines available and on operator dispatch practices that consistently capture the single-genset opportunities
the upgraded system now permits.
