Central Message

Obstructed total anomalous pulmonary venous return with “nutmeg” lung may not contraindicate surgery; immediate relief of obstruction should prompt a different strategy than used for typical cases.

Congenital pulmonary lymphangiectasia (CPL) is a rare disorder characterized by dilation of the lung lymphatic channels during fetal development. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the fetus reveals a characteristic “nutmeg lung” pattern. Our patient was not deemed a surgical candidate because of this diagnosis at other programs. We hypothesized that pulmonary vein congestion from severely obstructed total anomalous pulmonary venous return (TAPVR) caused the appearance of a “nutmeg” lung (Figure 1), and the patient's clinical improvement after surgical repair is consistent with this hypothesis. In addition, patients with heterotaxy often present with complex cardiac anatomy, which historically led many to undergo single-ventricle palliation.1 Our ability to create an anatomical digital twin and practice repair strategies allows us to perform repairs in many of these patients. Case Presentation

A male infant born at 38 weeks (3 kg) was prenatally diagnosed with heterotaxy, right atrial isomerism, dextrocardia, obstructed infracardiac TAPVR, L-looped ventricles, double outlet right ventricle, malposed great arteries, large outlet ventricular septal defect (VSD), small apical muscular VSDs, large atrial septal defect, and discontinuous branch pulmonary arteries (PAs) supplied by bilateral patent ductus arteriosus. At 29 weeks of gestation, MRI of the fetus revealed a nutmeg appearance of the lung consistent with CPL. Given the coexistence of CPL and complex congenital heart disease (CHD), the infant was deemed inoperable by several institutions. At our institution, delivery occurred in one cardiac operating room (OR), with TAPVR repair, PA unifocalization, and modified Blalock-Thomas-Taussig shunt creation beginning in the neighboring OR within 60 minutes of birth. After a complex postoperative course, the patient was discharged 2 months later with oxygen saturation in the low 80s on room air.

Cardiac computed tomography images were imported into our virtual reality surgical simulation suite to trial approaches, baffling strategies, and conduit routing (Figure 2). Although echocardiography remained the primary diagnostic tool, cardiac computed tomography and virtual reality planning were additive for anatomical characterization and surgical simulation in this uniquely complex case. On the basis of the virtual planning software and discussion, the decision was made to proceed with biventricular repair. Surgery was performed when the patient was 7 months of age. After shunt ligation, the apical muscular VSDs were primarily closed. The outlet VSD was enlarged anterolaterally to shorten the baffle to the aorta, which was initiated through the tricuspid valve and completed via the right ventriculotomy. The atrial septal defect patch baffled the pulmonary venous confluence to the right-sided morphologic left atrium. A 12-mm pulmonary homograft was used for the right ventricle-PA conduit. Postoperative transesophageal echocardiogram showed no residual lesions and good biventricular function (Figure 3). The patient had sternal closure on postoperative day (POD) 5, was discharged from the intensive care unit on POD 37, and was discharged from the hospital on POD 51. Transthoracic echocardiogram at 19 months of age showed unobstructed right ventricle-PA conduit flow, well-functioning atrioventricular valves, and normal biventricular function. Discussion

We suggest that fetal “nutmeg” lung, when associated with obstructed TAPVR, reflects extreme obstruction causing engorged pulmonary veins and lymphatics and thus is not a surgical contraindication. We propose distinguishing this secondary lymphatic engorgement from true primary CPL: severe TAPVR obstruction elevates pulmonary venous pressure, which in turn causes back-pressure dilation of pulmonary lymphatics, producing a nutmeg appearance on MRI. This process is distinct from primary CPL and, critically, is reversible upon relief of venous obstruction, as evidenced by our patient's clinical improvement after TAPVR repair. Relief of obstruction should be performed expeditiously. In our institution, this is best achieved by birthing the child in one OR and operating in the neighboring OR.

Prenatal diagnosis of pulmonary lymphangiectasia was critical to the successful planning and surgical management. Delivery in the OR with the assistance of a multidisciplinary team to support this critically ill child allowed for efficient resuscitation and immediate TAPVR repair. We believe that the sooner we can relieve pulmonary venous obstruction, the better it is for lung function. Therefore, if this technique is used, one should move toward surgical repair quickly.

Patients with isomerism and complex CHD are often referred for single ventricular palliation. Advances in prenatal and postnatal imaging have increased early diagnosis of CHD and may improve identification of candidates for biventricular repair.2,3 Three-dimensional modeling and virtual surgical planning enabled stepwise surgical planning and visualization. Our program identifies many of these patients with heterotaxy prenatally or as neonates to be candidates for biventricular repair. After initial palliation, we prefer to do a biventricular repair when the patient is 6 to 12 months of age.

In the presence of severely obstructed TAPVR, the diagnosis of “nutmeg” lung should not be considered an absolute contraindication to surgery. This single case cannot definitively establish that surgical timing reversed established intrauterine lymphatic changes; however, the patient's clinical trajectory is consistent with secondary, pressure-driven lymphatic engorgement. Immediate relief of obstruction in these severe cases warrants consideration of an expedited surgical strategy, and prospective study is needed to confirm this mechanistic hypothesis.
