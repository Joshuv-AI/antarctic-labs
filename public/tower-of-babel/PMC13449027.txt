Plasma P‐tau217 for detecting amyloid clearance after donanemab in Alzheimer's disease
Authors: Emily C. Collins, Ming Lu, Rose Beck, James Hendrix, Michael E. Hodsdon, Oskar Hansson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractINTRODUCTIONThis analysis of the phase 3 TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial examined whether plasma tau protein phosphorylated at threonine 217 (p‐tau217) level can reliably monitor treatment‐related amyloid clearance (TRAC) after donanemab treatment in early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (AD).METHODSAmyloid positron emission tomography (PET) and plasma p‐tau217 levels were assessed at baseline and longitudinally during study treatment. The diagnostic performance of p‐tau217 in detecting post‐treatment TRAC (< 24.1 Centiloids [CL]) was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis.RESULTSPlasma p‐tau217 showed suboptimal performance in detecting TRAC by PET in donanemab‐treated participants (N = 830), with an area under the ROC curve of 0.61 at 52 weeks.DISCUSSIONDespite reductions in plasma p‐tau217 with donanemab treatment, this biomarker cannot currently be used to accurately detect TRAC by PET (< 24.1 CL) in individuals with early symptomatic AD.

INTRODUCTION

The cognitive decline that characterizes Alzheimer's disease (AD) is preceded by neuropathologic changes, including intracellular accumulation of neurofibrillary tangles composed of phosphorylated tau protein and extracellular deposition of amyloid beta (Aβ) plaques. 1 , 2 , 3 Early detection of these changes may allow for the initiation of disease‐modifying treatments that can slow AD progression. 4 , 5

Neurofibrillary tangles and amyloid plaques can be detected by positron emission tomography (PET), 6 but the cost and limited accessibility of this technology are barriers to its routine use outside the hospital setting. 7 Detection of blood‐based biomarkers by mass spectrometry or high‐sensitivity immunoassay is a less costly and time‐saving alternative compared to neuroimaging or cerebrospinal fluid biomarker measurement. 8 , 9 , 10 Tau protein phosphorylated at threonine 217 (p‐tau217) is a biomarker with high diagnostic accuracy in AD, including the early stages. 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 In addition to reflecting tau pathology, 17 plasma p‐tau217 levels have been shown to correlate strongly with Aβ accumulation and amyloid plaque levels detected by PET. 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 , 22 However, it is unclear whether the association between plasma p‐tau217 level and treatment‐related amyloid clearance (TRAC) 23 is maintained over the course of AD treatment 24 with amyloid‐targeting therapies in patients with early symptomatic AD.

Donanemab is one of two amyloid‐targeting therapies currently approved and in clinical use for the treatment of early symptomatic AD. 25 , 26 , 27 In a phase 2 trial, donanemab treatment reduced amyloid plaque levels, and TRAC was associated with a slower spread of tau pathology, as determined by PET, and slower clinical decline. 28 A post hoc cohort‐wide analysis showed that compared to placebo, plasma p‐tau217 levels decreased with donanemab treatment, which showed a weakly positive correlation with percent change in amyloid plaque level on PET imaging. 29 Similarly, in the pivotal phase 3 TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial, participants with early symptomatic AD treated with donanemab showed a statistically significant group‐level decrease in plasma p‐tau217, along with a reduction in amyloid burden and slower cognitive decline at 76 weeks. 30

This study evaluated whether plasma p‐tau217 accurately reflects attainment of the TRAC threshold (< 24.1 Centiloids [CL]) in early symptomatic AD after donanemab treatment. This question was addressed by examining the relationship between plasma p‐tau217 and amyloid plaque levels by PET using data from the TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial.

METHODS

Study design

The phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial evaluated the safety and efficacy of donanemab in 1736 participants with early symptomatic AD (mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia due to AD). The results of the primary study have been published elsewhere; 30 the current study analyzed additional data from the trial.

The original study was conducted according to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and local regulations. The study protocol was approved by the ethics review board at each study site. Written, informed consent was obtained from participants or their legal guardians and from study partners. The study is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04437511). 31

Study participants

Participants were 60 to 85 years of age with a diagnosis of early symptomatic AD (prodromal AD or AD with mild dementia), elevated amyloid and tau levels on PET imaging, and a Mini‐Mental State Examination (MMSE) score of 20 to 28. The characteristics of the TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial population have been previously reported. 30 Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive donanemab or placebo, administered intravenously every 4 weeks for up to 72 weeks; donanemab was initiated at 700 mg for the first three doses and increased to 1400 mg thereafter. If amyloid plaque levels at 24 and 52 weeks were < 11 CL on a single PET scan or at least 11 CL but < 25 CL on two consecutive scans, participants receiving donanemab were switched to placebo in a blinded procedure. 30

Positron emission tomography imaging

The effect of donanemab treatment on amyloid plaque levels was assessed by PET as previously described. 29 Briefly, florbetapir F18 and florbetaben F18 were used to detect amyloid plaques at baseline and at weeks 24, 52, and 76. A composite standardized uptake value ratio was calculated from six predetermined cortical regions, referenced against the whole cerebellum, and converted to CL units.

Plasma biomarker assays

Plasma samples were collected at baseline and at weeks 24, 52, and 76. Plasma p‐tau181 was measured using a custom immunoassay (Quanterix). 29 To evaluate the performance of plasma p‐tau217 in detecting TRAC by PET, p‐tau217 levels were measured using a mass spectrometry‐based C2N plasma P‐tau217 assay (C2N Diagnostics) and an Elecsys P‐tau217 immunoassay (Roche Diagnostics). Plasma glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) levels were measured using the Simoa Human Neurology 4‐Plex E Advantage Kit (Quanterix).

Statistical analysis

Plasma p‐tau217, p‐tau181, and GFAP values were normalized by log10 transformation. Associations between plasma biomarker and PET amyloid plaque levels were described and analyzed with scatterplots and quantified using the Spearman rank correlation coefficient.

The diagnostic performance of plasma p‐tau217 level in detecting TRAC (prespecified at < 24.1 CL 30 , 32 , 33 ) by amyloid PET was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. A univariate model of p‐tau217 was first evaluated, followed by a multivariate model that included p‐tau217, p‐tau181, GFAP, log10‐transformed neurofilament light chain (NfL), age, and apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 status, which have been previously explored and found to be associated with donanemab treatment response in the TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial.

All biomarker analyses were exploratory and unadjusted for multiplicity. Data were analyzed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute), with a two‐sided P value of 0.05 considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Participant characteristics

The evaluable efficacy set of TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 included 830 participants treated with donanemab. The mean (standard deviation) age of the participants was 72.9 (6.2) years, 57.1% were female, 69.8% were carriers of the APOE ε4 allele, 61.1% had acetylcholinesterase inhibitor or memantine use at baseline, and the mean (standard deviation) MMSE score was 22.5 (3.8; Table 1). Of the 830 participants, 761 had non‐missing values at week 24, 670 at week 52, and 614 at week 76.

Diagnostic performance of plasma p‐tau217 in detecting TRAC by PET

Plasma p‐tau217 levels varied among donanemab‐treated participants of the TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial (Figure S1 in supporting information). Despite this variability, TRAC (amyloid plaque level of < 24.1 CL on PET) was achieved by 66.1% of participants at 52 weeks and by 29.7% and 76.4% of participants at weeks 24 and 76, respectively, reflecting an increasing trend over the treatment period.

Given the clinical utility of p‐tau217 in detecting AD pathology at diagnosis in patients with AD, 34 the present study evaluated whether plasma p‐tau217 levels reflected achievement of the TRAC threshold (< 24.1 CL on PET) after donanemab treatment. At 52 weeks, p‐tau217 measured by mass spectrometry showed low diagnostic performance in detecting TRAC (area under the ROC curve [AUROC]: 0.61; Figure 1), with similarly low performance at 24 weeks (AUROC: 0.64) and 76 weeks (AUROC: 0.63; Figure S2 in supporting information). A comparable pattern was observed with the p‐tau217 immunoassay, with AUROC values of 0.67 at 24 weeks and 0.64 at 52 and 76 weeks (Figure S3 in supporting information).

Correlation between p‐tau217 level and amyloid burden

Correlation analyses of change from baseline in p‐tau217 level and amyloid CL on PET imaging were performed to clarify their relationship after donanemab treatment. At 52 weeks, there was a statistically significant positive but weak correlation between change in p‐tau217 level and change in PET amyloid plaque level (R = 0.34; P < 0.0001; Figure 2). Results were similar at 24 and 76 weeks (R = 0.26 and 0.22, respectively; both P < 0.0001; Figure S4 in supporting information).

Diagnostic performance of combined AD biomarkers in detecting TRAC by PET

An ROC analysis was performed to determine whether the multivariate model combining participant characteristics and plasma biomarkers improved the detection of TRAC compared to the univariate model of p‐tau217 alone. Age, APOE ε4 status, p‐tau217, p‐tau181, NfL, and GFAP were included in the model. At 76 weeks, no meaningful association with TRAC was observed (AUROC: 0.71; Figure S5 in supporting information).

DISCUSSION

In the TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial, 30 donanemab‐treated participants demonstrated slowing of cognitive and functional decline, and change in plasma p‐tau217 level from baseline in donanemab‐treated participants was correlated with change in amyloid plaque levels by PET at 24, 52, and 76 weeks. Nonetheless, change in p‐tau217 level showed suboptimal diagnostic performance at the individual level in detecting attainment of TRAC threshold (< 24.1 CL on PET). Incorporating additional AD biomarkers—plasma p‐tau181 and GFAP—and other AD‐related participant characteristics yielded no meaningful improvement in diagnostic performance compared to the use of p‐tau217 alone. These findings based on available data from the TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial suggest that plasma p‐tau217 cannot reliably be used to assess whether amyloid plaque levels reach and remain below the clearance threshold after treatment with donanemab.

As disease‐modifying therapies for AD become more widely used in clinical practice, there is a growing need for sensitive and accurate tools to routinely monitor treatment response and disease progression. Although PET imaging is the reference standard for visualizing and quantifying amyloid plaque pathology, 35 measuring blood‐based biomarkers is more practical, given the cost and accessibility challenges of PET. 9 , 10 The association between plasma p‐tau217 levels and PET amyloid positivity is well established, 36 , 37 and plasma p‐tau217 has comparable specificity and sensitivity to cerebrospinal fluid p‐tau217 for detecting amyloid plaques observed by PET in both cognitively impaired and unimpaired individuals. 38 , 39 In a separate analysis of donanemab‐treated TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 participants, a strong correlation (R2 = 0.86, 95% confidence interval: 0.73–0.97) was observed between post‐treatment amyloid value by PET, which was grouped into deciles, and change in p‐tau217 level at 76 weeks. 40 Similarly, the results of the current study demonstrated a clear group‐level relationship between p‐tau217 and PET amyloid plaque level after treatment; however, these treatment‐induced changes were only weakly correlated at the individual level. This may be attributable to the biological variability in p‐tau217 levels observed within and between individuals. 41 , 42 , 43

Because tau pathology is thought to arise via both Aβ‐dependent and Aβ‐independent mechanisms, 44 plasma p‐tau217 levels reflect not only pathologic amyloid plaque load but also the load of tau aggregates, including neurofibrillary tangles. 22 , 45 The results of the current study suggest that amyloid‐targeting therapy disrupts the association between amyloid deposition and hyperphosphorylated tau accumulation and that post‐treatment plasma p‐tau217 levels could depend to a greater extent on non‐amyloid factors such as cerebral neurofibrillary tangles or peripheral tau forms.

P‐tau217 demonstrated consistently low diagnostic performance in detecting TRAC by PET (< 24.1 CL). We also found that incorporating AD‐associated biomarkers p‐tau217, p‐tau181, and GFAP, along with NfL, age, and APOE ε4 status into a multivariate model for detecting PET amyloid plaque levels yielded a numerically higher AUROC at 76 weeks (0.71) than p‐tau217 alone. However, the value was still too low to be clinically useful for monitoring TRAC, given that an AUROC of > 0.80 is considered clinically valuable in most cases. 46 Given the current limitations of plasma p‐tau217, there is an urgent need for novel plaque‐specific plasma biomarkers to evaluate TRAC after treatment with amyloid‐targeting therapies.

Limitations

There were some limitations to this study. Only two plasma p‐tau217 assays 47 were evaluated, and it is possible that we would have obtained different results with other assays. However, both assays have excellent diagnostic performance 16 , 48 and yielded similar findings despite their technical differences (mass spectrometry vs. immunoassay). Finally, the data analyzed in this study were from a specific clinical trial cohort of individuals with early symptomatic AD enrolled in the TRAILBLAZER‐ALZ 2 trial, precluding the generalization of the conclusion to more diverse, real‐world populations with early symptomatic AD.

CONCLUSION

Plasma p‐tau217 is a highly accurate biomarker for detecting amyloid pathology in early symptomatic AD, and the levels decline with amyloid plaque lowering. However, the results of this study show that plasma p‐tau217 level cannot currently be used—either alone or combined with other available AD‐related biomarkers and participant characteristics—to monitor post‐donanemab TRAC at the individual level. Future research will investigate other plaque‐specific blood biomarkers for monitoring treatment effects on amyloid plaque levels in the brain.
