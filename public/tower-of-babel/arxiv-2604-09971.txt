Kauffman bracket skein module of the connected sum of two solid tori
Rhea Palak Bakshi, Thang T. Q. Lê, Józef H. Przytycki

Abstract
. We determine the structure of the Kauffman bracket skein module of the connected sum of two genus one handlebodies over the ring of Laurent polynomials ℤ ⁡ [ q ± 1 ] \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] , thereby proving a conjecture posed by the first and third authors. Our results lay the groundwork for computing the Kauffman bracket skein module of arbitrary connected sums over the ring ℤ ⁡ [ q ± 1 ] \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Kauffman bracket skein modules and algebras

The Kauffman bracket skein module is an invariant of

3
-manifolds that has played a central role in low-dimensional topology and knot theory. Skein modules were introduced independently by the third author
[
Prz1
]
and by Turaev
[
Tur1
,
Tur2
]
, and they provides a powerful algebraic framework for studying links embedded in

3
-manifolds and for connecting classical topology with quantum invariants. Given an oriented

3
-manifold

M
, possibly with non-empty boundary

\partial M
, the Kauffman bracket skein module,
\mathscr{S}(M)
is defined as the module over the Laurent polynomial ring
R={\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]}

generated by isotopy classes of unoriented framed links in
M
, modulo the Kauffman bracket skein relations
[
Kau
]
. Skein modules serve as a natural bridge between topology, geometry, and quantum algebra. They are closely related to algebraic geometry via
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
character varieties
[
Bul1
,
PS
]
, hyperbolic geometry via quantum Teichmüller spaces and quantum cluster algebras
[
BW
,
CF
,
Le2
,
Kas
,
Mul
]
, the AJ conjecture
[
FGL
,
Le1
]
, and the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev
3
-manifold invariants and Topological Quantum Field Theories
[
BHMV
]
, to name a few.

1.2.
Calculations of skein modules

The Kauffman bracket skein module (KBSM), over the ring
{\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]}
, has been fully calculated only for a few classes of manifolds. These include lens spaces
[
HP1
]
,
S^{1}\times S^{2}

[
HP2
]
, the Whitehead manifold
[
HP3
]
,
3
-manifolds obtained from integral surgery on the trefoil knot
[
Bul2
]
, the product of the pair of pants with
S^{1}

[
DM
]
, a family of prism manifolds
[
Mro1
]
,
\mathbb{R}P^{3}\ \#\ \mathbb{R}P^{3}

[
Mro2
]
, and the exteriors of two-bridge knots and links
[
Le1
,
LT
]
. In
[
Prz3
]
, the third author stated a theorem about the structure of the KBSM of the connected sum of two handlebodies of arbitrary genera, over the ring
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
. This “theorem” was disproved in
[
BP
]
for connected sums involving handlebodies that are not both solid tori. However, the first and third authors conjectured that the statement about the structure of the KBSM over
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
remains valid when both summands are solid tori.

In this paper, we refine this conjecture and completely determine the structure of the Kauffman bracket skein module of
H_{1}\ \#\ H_{1}
.
Note that this
3
-manifold is also the complement of the trivial link with two components.
For a positive integer
k
, let

\{k\}=q^{2k}-q^{-2k}
. Define
the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind
S_{n}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
recursively by

(1)

S_{0}(x)=1,\ S_{1}(x)=x,\ S_{n}(x)=xS_{n-1}(x)-S_{n-2}(x).

Let
\hat{R}=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2}]
, the ring of polynomials in two formal variables
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.
The bigger ring
R[x_{1},x_{2},y]
is an
\hat{R}
-module. Consider its
\hat{R}
-submodule

\mathcal{G}\subset\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},x_{2},y]
spanned by
\{G_{n},n\geq 1\}
, where

G_{n}=\{n+1\}S_{n}(y)+(-1)^{(n+1)}\{1\}S_{n}(x_{1})S_{n}(x_{2})\}.

Let
H_{1}
be the solid torus. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let the ground ring
R
be
{\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]}
. There is an explicit
R
-linear isomorphism

\phi:R[x_{1},x_{2},y]/\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathscr{S}(H_{1}\#H_{1}).

Roughly speaking,
\phi(x_{1}^{n_{1}}x_{2}^{n_{2}}y^{n})
is the union of
n_{1}
parallel copies of a core
\overline{x}_{1}
of the first copy of
H_{1}
,
n_{2}
parallel copies of a core
\overline{x}_{2}
of the second copy of
H_{2}
, and
n
parallel copies of a connected sum
\overline{y}
of
\overline{x}_{1}
and
\overline{x}_{2}
. The conjecture of the first and third author in
[
BP
]
does not describe the kernel
\mathcal{G}
explicitly, which we do. For details see Section
3
.

Our results will help in the study of the Kauffman bracket skein module of the connected sums of
3
-manifolds. The proof of our main result utilises a recently proved identity of Esebre and Gelca
[
EG
]
. Furthermore, the stated skein modules of connected sums of
3
-manifolds at roots of unity exhibit exotic and strange properties, which are counterintuitive, see for example
[
CL
]
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the Kauffman bracket skein module and its relative version.
In Section
3
, we prove our main theorem. We also exhibit some torsion elements in the KBSM of
H_{1}\ \#\ H_{1}
.

In this section, we discuss the definition of the Kauffman bracket skein module (KBSM) and algebra, along with their fundamental properties, including the behaviour of the KBSM under handle slides. Throughout the paper, the ground ring

is the ring
{\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]}
of Laurent polynomials in the formal variable

2.1.
Skein modules

Let
M
be an oriented 3-manifold, with possibly non-empty boundary. The Kauffman bracket skein module of
M
, denoted by
\mathscr{S}(M)
, is the
R
-module freely spanned by all isotopy classes of unoriented framed links in
M
, including the empty link, subject to the following relations:

(2)

\displaystyle-q\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{framedL0_unoriented.pdf}}}-q^{-1}\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{framedLinfty_unoriented.pdf}}}\ \text{and}

(3)

\displaystyle+q^{2}+q^{-2}.

Here the framed links in each expression are identical outside the
3
-balls pictured in the diagrams. For an unoriented framed link
L
in
M
, we call the
skein class
of
L
the element of
\mathscr{S}(M)
determined by
L
. When there is no confusion, we will identify a framed link in
M
with its skein class. An orientation preserving embedding
f:M\hookrightarrow M^{\prime}
of oriented
3
-manifolds induces an
R
-linear map
f_{*}:\mathscr{S}(M)\to\mathscr{S}(M^{\prime})
, sending the skein class of a link
L
to the skein class of
f(L)

[
Prz1
,
Prz2
]
.

For an oriented surface
F
with possibly non-empty boundary, let
\mathscr{S}(F)=\mathscr{S}(F\times[-1,1])
. The
R
-module
\mathscr{S}(F)
has an algebra structure, formally introduced by Turaev
[
Tur2
]
(see also
[
HK
,
Prz1
]
), where the product of two links is defined by placing the first link above the second in the direction given by the interval
[-1,1]
. The empty link is the identity. There is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
\tau:\mathscr{S}(F)\to\mathscr{S}(F)
, called the reflection, defined by

(4)

\tau(q)=q^{-1},\tau(x,t)=(x,-t)\ \text{for }\ (x,t)\in F\times[-1,1].

We have
\tau(xy)=\tau(y)\tau(x)
and
\tau^{2}=\mathrm{id}
. From the defining relations in the Kauffman bracket skein module, one can easily derive the following relation, known as the
framing relation
.

(5)

\displaystyle-q^{-3}\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{framedL1.pdf}}}=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{framedsingle_strand.pdf}}}=-q^{3}\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{framedL1inverse.pdf}}}
