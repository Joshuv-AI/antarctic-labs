Canonical Extensions of Quantale-Enriched Categories
Alexander Kurz, Apostolos Tzimoulis

Abstract
Drawing on well-known results from the theory of canonical extensions and the theory of categories enriched over a quantale, we define canonical extensions of quantale-enriched categories and establish their basic properties.



1
Introduction

Drawing on well-known results from the theory of canonical extensions and the theory of categories enriched over a quantale

(\Omega,\sqsubseteq,\bigsqcup,e,\cdot,)

we define canoncial extensions of quantale-enriched categories and establish their basic properties. Elements of
\Omega
can be understood as distances or weights or truth-values.

Canonical extensions arise as certain MacNeille completions. MacNeille completions, in turn, are given by the fixed points of the MacNeille-Birkhoff-Isbell-Lawvere adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{U}A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi\ \mapsto\ \varphi\blacktriangleright{\bf I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi\ \mapsto\ {\bf I}\blacktriangleleft\psi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bot}

between “weighted downsets”
\varphi
on
X
and “weighted upsets”
\psi
on
A
induced by a binary relation

{\bf I}:X\looparrowright A

which is an
\Omega
-valued relation

{\bf I}:X\times A\to\Omega

on the product of the enriched categories
X
and
A
. The lattice theoretic results on MacNeille completions and canonical extensions are recovered in the case where the quantale
\Omega
is the familiar set
2=\{0<1\}
of truth-values.

We develop our work in a language that stays as faithful as possible to
both
lattice theory (as in the work of Dunn, Gehrke and Palmigiano
[
23
]
on the canonical extensions of posets) and to enriched category theory (as in the work of Stubbe
[
45
]
on quantaloid enriched categories).
This does require some compromise.
For example, since we do not assume that the quantale
\Omega
is commutative, we need two implications (residuals), written as
\rhd
and
\lhd
.
Because of the associativity of the quantale multiplication
(a\cdot b)\cdot c=a\cdot(b\cdot c)
the residuals satisfy

(a\rhd b)\lhd c=a\rhd(b\lhd c)

We systematically extend quantale notation to a “distributor calculus”
[
45
]
, where this equation becomes

(\varphi\blacktriangleright{\bf I})\blacktriangleleft\psi=\varphi\blacktriangleright({\bf I}\blacktriangleleft\psi).

and is nothing but the MacNeille-Birkhoff-Isbell-Lawvere adjunction

-\blacktriangleright{\bf I}\ \dashv\ {\bf I}\blacktriangleleft-.

This “blacktriangle calculus” provides a useful bridge between quantale-enriched category theory on the one hand and lattice theory, algebraic logic and proof theory on the other hand.

The reason for paying attention to the language in which we formulate our results is not only to make our work accessible to category theorists and to lattice theorists: In our own ongoing work on applications to logic, discussed below, we need to have easy access to general category theoretic results and to an algebraic language in the style of lattice theory and logic.
This approach also sheds new light on known results in lattice theory.

In the remainder of this section, we discuss applications and related work.

Canonical Extensions

Canonical extensions of Boolean algebras were introduced by Jonsson and Tarski
[
33
,
32
]
in order to study relation algebras and, more generally, modal algebras (Boolean algebras with operators). In particular, they pioneered the use of canonical extensions to prove completeness of modal logics via representation theorems generalizing Stone duality for Boolean algebras. This line of research was extended by Gehrke and Jonsson
[
29

to distributive lattices and by Dunn, Gehrke and Palmigiano
[
23

to lattices and posets, that is, in our terms, to the case
\Omega=2

Substructural Logics, Algebraic Logic

On the one hand, quantales can be seen as particular residuated lattices, which constitute the algebraic semantics of
substructural logics
, see Galatos, Jipsen, Kowalski and Ono
[
26

logics. Our work will allow us to generalize duality-based results in modal logic to non-distributive substructural logics.

2.1
Quantales

We denote by
\mathsf{Sup}
the monoidal closed category of complete join semilattice (sup-lattices). A quantale is a monoid in the monoidal category of sup-lattices or also a one-object
\mathsf{Sup}
-enriched category. Explicitly, a quantale

(\Omega,\sqsubseteq,\bigsqcup,e,\cdot,)

is a complete join semilattice
(\Omega,\sqsubseteq,\bigsqcup)
and a monoid
(\Omega,e,\cdot)
in which multiplication distributes over joins. We write top as
\top
and bottom as
\bot
. Since
\Omega
is complete it also has meets
\bigsqcap
.
2
2

2

The order of operations is defined such that in
\bigsqcap_{x}(\alpha(x)\rhd\beta(x))
the parentheses can be omitted.
Since multiplication preserves joins in each argument, multiplication has a left-residual
\lhd
and a right-residual
\rhd
defined as

b\,\sqsubseteq\,a\rhd c\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a\cdot b\,\sqsubseteq\,c\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a\,\sqsubseteq\,c\lhd b

In a commutative quantale, we have
a\rhd c=c\lhd a
.

Example 2.1
.

1.

The
two-chain

2=\{0\sqsubseteq 1\}
is a commutative quantale in which multiplication and meet coincide. The residual is implication.

2.

The
Lawvere quantale

[0,\infty]
is a subset of the extended real numbers
[
36
]
. It is ordered by
\geq
with top

and has

a\lhd b=a\dotminus b
.

3.

The
similarity quantale

\Omega=\{0,1,2,\ldots\infty\}
is ordered like the extended natural numbers, has
\min(-,-)
as multiplication and
\top=\infty
as the neutral element. We interpret the elements of
\Omega
as a measure of similarity. The residual

is given by
{\sf if~}m\leq n{\sf~then~}\infty{\sf~else~}n
.

4.

The

is given with respect to a set

\Sigma^{\ast}
(the set of finite words over

[

where

2.2
Quantale Laws

The counits of the residuals are

\displaystyle r\cdot(r\rhd s)\sqsubseteq s

\displaystyle s\sqsupseteq(s\lhd r)\cdot r

For all
r,s,t\in\Omega
we have

\displaystyle r\rhd(s\lhd t)=(r\rhd s)\lhd t

\displaystyle(r\rhd s)\cdot(s\rhd t)\sqsubseteq r\rhd t

\displaystyle(t\lhd s)\cdot(s\lhd r)\sqsubseteq t\lhd r

\displaystyle e\sqsubseteq(r\rhd r)\quad\quad e\sqsubseteq(r\lhd r)

For all
r\in\Omega
there is an adjunction between partial orders

(-\rhd r)\ \dashv\ (r\lhd-):\Omega^{\partial}\to\Omega

due to
\Omega^{\partial}(a\rhd r,b)=\Omega(a,r\lhd b)
.

2.3
Duality

The notation

\Omega^{\partial}

refers to the poset obtained from
\Omega
by reversing the order while

\Omega^{o}

is obtained from
\Omega
by reversing the multiplication. Considering
\Omega
as a one-object order-enriched category,
\Omega^{o}
is the category obtained from
\Omega
by reversing 1-cells.

2.4
Quantale Spaces

We call a category enriched over a quantale
\Omega

, or,
\Omega
-space. Explicitely, an
\Omega
-space

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle\sqsubseteq X(x,x),

\displaystyle X(x,y)\cdot X(y,z)

\displaystyle\sqsubseteq X(x,z).

A morphism of quantale spaces is a functor of quantale-enriched categories. Explicitely, a function
f:X\to Y
is a morphism of quantale spaces, also known as a functor, if

X(x,x^{\prime})\sqsubseteq Y(fx,fx^{\prime}).

Definition 2.2
.

Every quantale space

has an underlying order

x\leq y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}e\sqsubseteq X(x,y).

Given

the order on functors is defined by

if
fx\leq gx
for all

. A space is called

Definition 2.3
.

\mathsf{Spa}(\Omega)
is the order-enriched category of quantale spaces and quantale space morphisms.

2.5
Truth-Values vs Distances

As can be seen from the examples, the elements of the quantale can be interpreted as
truth values
or as
distances
. For example,
[0,\infty]
with
0
as top and
\infty
as bottom is naturally interpreted as a quantale of distances. On the other hand, there is an isomorphism of quantales
[0,\infty]\to[0,1]
defined by
x\mapsto e^{-x}
mapping
0
to
1
and
\infty
to
0
. While both quantales give rise to isomorphic quantale enriched categories, the second quantale is more naturally interpreted as a quantale of truth values.

In this example, in the
truth-value interpretation
we have

X(a,b)\cdot X(b,c)\leq X(a,c)
while in the
distance interpretation
we have
X(a,b)+X(b,c)\geq X(a,c)
, also known as the triangle inequality of metric spaces. Similarly,
\bigsqcap
is conjunction in the truth-value interpretation and supremum in the distance interpretation.

In the following, for
\Omega
-spaces
X
, we often find it convenient to speak of
X(x,y)
as
the distance from
x
to
y
without making assumptions on
\Omega
or implying that we favour the distance interpretation over the truth-value interpretation.

2.6
Duality

Dualising the order of the multiplication of
\Omega
,
X
is an
\Omega^{o}
-space if it satisfies

X(y,z)\cdot X(x,y)\sqsubseteq X(x,z).

In addition, one can also dualise the order of
\Omega
. This leads to 4 different ways in which
\Omega
itself can be turned into a quantale space
A
:

1.

A(a,b)=a\rhd b

2.

A(a,b)=a\lhd b

3.

A(a,b)=b\rhd a

4.

A(a,b)=b\lhd a

In cases 1 and 4 the order defined by
a\leq b\ \Leftrightarrow\ e\sqsubseteq A(a,b)
agrees with the order of
\Omega
, in cases 2 and 3 it is the dual. In cases 1 and 2,
A
is an
\Omega
-space and, in cases 3 and 4,
A
is an
\Omega^{o}
-space. The
A
of case 1 is the
A^{o}
of case 3 and the
A
of case 2 is the
A^{o}
of case 4. If
\Omega
is commutative, case 1 equals case 4 and case 2 equals case 3.

Remark 2.5
.

X
is an
\Omega
-space iff
X^{o}
is an
\Omega^{o}
-space.

2.7
Weighted Relations

A
quantale-valued relation
(also known as bimodule, profunctor, distributor, or weakening relation)
R:X\looparrowright Y
between quantale spaces
X
and
Y
is a function
X\times Y\to\Omega
satisfying

X(x^{\prime},x)\cdot R(x,y)\sqsubseteq R(x^{\prime},y)\quad\quad\quad R(x,y)\cdot Y(y,y^{\prime})\sqsubseteq R(x,y^{\prime}).

We call such an
R
also a quantale relation or
weighted relation
or metric relation or
\Omega
-relation or just relation. If we want to name the quantale, we call it an
\Omega
-relation. Weighted relations
R:X\looparrowright Y
and
S:Y\looparrowright Z
are composed in diagrammatic order according to

(R\bullet S)(x,z)=\bigsqcup_{y\in Y}R(x,y)\cdot S(y,z).

Given an
\Omega
-space
X
, we use
X
also to denote the hom
X(-,-):X\looparrowright X
. Homs play the role of identity relations:

X\bullet R=R=R\bullet Y.

(1)

We may write
Rxy
or
xRy
for
R(x,y)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The operation
\bullet
is associative, that is,

R\bullet(S\bullet T)=(R\bullet S)\bullet T.

Proof.

This follows from the associativity of the quantale operation and that in a quantale the equations
(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}r_{i})\cdot s=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}(r_{i}\cdot s)
and
s\cdot(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}r_{i})=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}(s\cdot r_{i})
hold:

\displaystyle R\bullet(S\bullet T)(w,z)

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{x}R(w,x)\cdot(\bigsqcup_{y}S(x,y)\cdot T(y,z))

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{x}\bigsqcup_{y}R(w,x)\cdot(S(x,y)\cdot T(y,z))

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{x}\bigsqcup_{y}(R(w,x)\cdot S(x,y))\cdot T(y,z)

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{y}\bigsqcup_{x}(R(w,x)\cdot S(x,y))\cdot T(y,z)

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{y}(\bigsqcup_{x}R(w,x)\cdot S(x,y))\cdot T(y,z)

\displaystyle=(R\bullet S)\bullet T(w,z).

∎

.

\Omega
-relations form an order-enriched category
\mathsf{Rel}(\Omega)

for short, with composition and identities given as above and the order in

R\sqsubseteq S\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \forall x\in X.\forall y\in Y.xRy\sqsubseteq xSy.

5
Conclusion

We generalized the canonical extension construction from lattices and posets to quantale-enriched categories and developed the basic theory of these canonical extensions. On the category theoretic side this adds to a line of developement that started with Lawvere
[
36
]
and continued through, for example,
[
45
,
44
,
49
,
25
]
. On the logic side, our work is a stepping stone as part of the larger project of developing the logical foundations of categorization theory
[
20
,
18
]
. In particular, our result is the theoretical groundwork needed for the development of the duality theory of quantale-enriched categories with many-valued polarities and the introduction of logical systems with modal operations and consequence relations weighted over a quantale, and the development of their proof theory and relational semantics. The work in the present article will allow the systematic development of classes of such logical systems, and allow to develop tools from the theory of algorithmic canonicity in the context of quantale enriched categories.
