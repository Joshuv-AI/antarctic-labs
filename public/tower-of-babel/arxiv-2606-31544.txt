The classification of real quadratic fields which satisfy Hammarhjelm’s condition Thanks: This research was supported by the Israel Science Foundation (grant No. 2860/24).
Zeév Rudnick

Abstract
. A real quadratic field satisfies Hammarhjelm’s condition if its ring of integers has unique factorization, and the Minkowski lattice of its ring of integers contains no point in a certain rectangle determined by the fundamental unit. Such fields have recently appeared in the study of visible points in algebraic cut-and-project sets. We prove that there are exactly seven real quadratic fields satisfying Hammarhjelm’s condition, namely those with discriminant 8 , 5 , 13 , 29 , 53 , 173 , 293 . 8,\;5,\;13,\;29,\;53,\;173,\;293. The proof is based on showing that for such fields, the fundamental unit is small relative to the discriminant, together with genus theory and Biró’s classification of class number one fields in Yokoi’s family.

The known examples until now were

About the proof: The main point is to realize that Hammarhjelm’s condition implies that the regulator of the field is small relative to the discriminant, so the class number formula forces the class number to be large. This can be converted to a proof of finiteness of such fields using Siegel’s theorem, which is ineffective. To pin down the full list of extensions, we use a number of steps, with one of the cases reduced to a special family of fields where those with class number one are all known: Yokoi’s family, with squarefree discriminant of the form

Background on real quadratic fields

We may write
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{D})
with

\Delta_{K}=\begin{cases}4D,&D=2,3\bmod 4\\

The ring of integers of

2.1.
The fundamental unit

The following Lemma is well known, a proof is included for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
D>1
be squarefree with
D\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
Write the fundamental unit
\lambda>1
in the form

\lambda=m+n\omega,\qquad m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then

m\geq 0,\qquad n\geq 1.

Proof.

First note that
n\neq 0
. Indeed, if
n=0
, then
\lambda=m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and since

\lambda
is a unit, we would have
\lambda=\pm 1
, contradicting
\lambda>1
.

Suppose now that
n<0
. Writing

\lambda=\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)+\frac{n}{2}\sqrt{D},

we see that

\sigma(\lambda)=\left(m+\frac{n}{2}\right)-\frac{n}{2}\sqrt{D}=\left(m-\frac{|n|}{2}\right)+\frac{|n|}{2}\sqrt{D}.

Since
\sqrt{D}>1
, it follows that

\sigma(\lambda)>\lambda>1.

On the other hand, because
\lambda
is a unit,

N(\lambda)=\lambda\,\sigma(\lambda)=\pm 1,

and therefore

|\sigma(\lambda)|=\frac{1}{\lambda}<1,

a contradiction. Hence
n>0
, and since
n
is integral, we obtain

n\geq 1.

Next, because
\lambda>1
is a unit, we again have

|\sigma(\lambda)|<1.

Writing

\sigma(\lambda)=m+\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\sqrt{D},

the inequality
|\sigma(\lambda)|<1
implies

-1<m+\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\sqrt{D}<1.

From the left-hand inequality we obtain

m>\frac{n}{2}(\sqrt{D}-1)-1.

Since
D\geq 5
and
n\geq 1
, the right-hand side is strictly greater than
-1
. Thus

m>-1
. As
m
is an integer, it follows that

m\geq 0.

Therefore
\lambda=m+n\omega
with
m\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
.
∎

2.2.
Narrow vs wide equivalence

For a real quadratic field
K
, there is a distinction between narrow and wide equivalences of ideals, that is two fractional ideals are narrowly equivalent if there is an element
\alpha\in K
of positive norm
N(\alpha)>0
, so that
I=(\alpha)J
, and equivalent in the ordinary sense without the restriction that
\alpha
has positive norm. If there a unit of negative norm, then the two notions coincide, otherwise they are distinct.

Denote by
{\rm Cl}(K)
the ideal classes, and by
{\rm Cl}^{+}(K)
the narrow ideal classes. The narrow class number is
h_{K}^{+}=\#{\rm Cl}^{+}(K)
, and the wide class number is
h_{K}=\#{\rm Cl}(K)
. If there is a unit of negative norm, then
h_{K}^{+}=h_{K}
, and otherwise
h_{K}^{+}=2h_{K}
.

2.3.
Genus theory

We recall the restrictions imposed by genus theory on the prime factorization of the discriminant of a real quadratic field with class number one.

According to genus theory, the narrow class number
h_{K}^{+}
is divisible by
2^{r(K)-1}
,
where the integer
r(K)
is the number of distinct prime factors of the discriminant
\Delta_{K}
; recall
\Delta_{K}=4D
if
D=2,3\bmod 4
, and
\Delta_{K}=D
if
D=1\bmod 4
.

Hence a necessary condition for
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{D})
to have narrow class number one is one of the following two cases:

(1)

D=1\bmod 4
then
\Delta_{K}=D=p
is prime.

(2)

D=2\bmod 4
then the only option is
D=2
,
\Delta_{K}=8
.

If there is a unit of negative norm, then
h_{K}=h_{K}^{+}
and the cases above determine when the class number is one. However, if there are no units with negative norm, so that
h_{K}^{+}=2h_{K}
, then we will need to treat the case when
r(K)=2
. Of concern to us in this case will be when
D=1\bmod 4
, so that the discriminant
\Delta=D=pq
is a product of two distinct odd primes.

2.4.
The
4
-rank of the narrow class group

The
4
-rank of a finite abelian group

is the number of cyclic summands of

whose order is divisible by
4
. For a discriminant
\Delta
, denote by

the
4
-rank of the narrow ideal class group of
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{\Delta})
. So if
e_{4}(\Delta)>0
then

is divisible by

, and therefore
h_{K}>1
. Rédei and Reichardt
[
7
,
8
]
showed
that

e_{4}(\Delta)=r(K)-1-{\rm rank}\,M(\Delta)

where
M(\Delta)=(\varepsilon_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{r(K)}
is the

matrix defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, whose entries are given by

(-1)^{\varepsilon_{ij}}=\left(\frac{d_{i}}{d_{j}}\right),\quad i\neq j,

where
\Delta=\prod_{i=1}^{r(K)}d_{i}
is the decomposition of the discriminant into prime power discriminants, we set
\left(\frac{x}{d_{j}}\right)=\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)=(-1)^{(x-1)/4}

\varepsilon_{jj}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\\

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Delta=pq
with
p
,
q
distinct primes,
p\equiv q\equiv 1\bmod 4
and
q=p+4
.
Then the
4
-rank of the narrow class group is one, and hence the class number is at least
2
.

Proof.

In this case,
r=2
,
d_{1}=p
,
d_{2}=q
, and by quadratic reciprocity

\left(\frac{d_{1}}{d_{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)

and hence all entries in
M
are equal:
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&u\\
u&u\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Assume further that
q=p+4
. Then
\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{p+4}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{4}{p}\right)=+1
so that
(-1)^{\varepsilon_{12}}=1=(-1)^{\varepsilon_{21}}
hence
\varepsilon_{12}=0=\varepsilon_{21}
and the Rédei matrix is

M(pq)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

which has rank zero over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. Therefore the
4
-rank of the narrow class group is

e_{4}(pq)=r(K)-1-{\rm rank}\,M(pq)=2-1-0=1.

We deduce that
4\mid h_{K}^{+}
, and since
h_{K}
equals
h_{K}^{+}
or

, we cannot have
h_{K}=1
.
∎

3.
Condition H

Recall condition H:

f(O_{K})\cap\bigl((1,\lambda)\times(-1,1)\bigr)=\emptyset.

We explore some consequences of condition H.

We first show that condition H forces the fundamental unit
\lambda
to be small relative to the discriminant
\Delta_{K}
of the field:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{D})
be a real quadratic field, where
D>1
is squarefree, and let

\lambda>1
be the fundamental unit of
K
. Assume that
K
satisfies condition H.
Then:

(1)

If
D\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}
, then

\lambda<2\sqrt{D}=\sqrt{\Delta_{K}}.

(2)

If
D\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then

\lambda<\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{2}=\sqrt{\Delta_{K}}+\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

Suppose first that
D\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}
. Then

O_{K}={\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{D}].

Assume, for contradiction, that

\lambda\geq 2\sqrt{D}.

Let

\alpha=\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor+\sqrt{D}\in O_{K}.

Since
D>1
is squarefree,
\sqrt{D}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
, and hence

\sigma(\alpha)=\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor-\sqrt{D}=-\{\sqrt{D}\},

where
\{\sqrt{D}\}\in(0,1)
denotes the fractional part of
\sqrt{D}
. Thus

-1<\sigma(\alpha)<0.

Moreover,

1<\alpha=\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor+\sqrt{D}<2\sqrt{D}\leq\lambda

by our assumption.
Therefore

f(\alpha)\in(1,\lambda)\times(-1,1)

which contradicts condition H. Hence we must have

\lambda<2\sqrt{D}.

Suppose now that
D\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Then

O_{K}={\mathbb{Z}}[\omega],\qquad\omega=\frac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}.

We claim that condition H implies

\lambda<\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{2}.

Assume, for contradiction, that

\lambda\geq\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{2}.

Let

x=\frac{\sqrt{D}-1}{2},\qquad m=\left\lfloor x+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{D}}{2}\right\rfloor,

and define

\alpha=m+\omega.

Since

\omega^{\prime}=\sigma(\omega)=\frac{1-\sqrt{D}}{2}=-x,

we have

\sigma(\alpha)=m-x.

By construction,
m
is the nearest integer to
x
, and therefore

|\sigma(\alpha)|=|m-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}<1.

On the other hand,

m<x+\frac{1}{2},

so

\alpha=m+\omega<x+\frac{1}{2}+\omega=\frac{\sqrt{D}-1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}=\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{2}.

Hence

\alpha<\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda.

Since
D\geq 5
, we have
\omega>1
, and therefore

\alpha=m+\omega>1.

Thus

1<\alpha<\lambda\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\sigma(\alpha)|<1.

Consequently,

f(\alpha)\in(1,\lambda)\times(-1,1)

which contradicts condition H.
Therefore

\lambda<\sqrt{D}+\frac{1}{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Yokoi’s family

Yokoi’s family of real quadratic fields is
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{D})
with
D=x^{2}+4
squarefree.
We show that they all satisfy condition H.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
D=x^{2}+4>5
be squarefree, where
x=2m+1
is odd, and let

K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{D})
.
Then
K
satisfies condition H.

Proof.

The ring of integers here is
O_{K}={\mathbb{Z}}[\omega]
,
\omega=\frac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}
.
Since
D=x^{2}+4
, we have
x^{2}-D=-4
, and therefore

\lambda_{1}=\frac{x+\sqrt{D}}{2}=m+\omega

is a unit of norm
-1
, with
\lambda_{1}>1
. Hence it is a positive (odd) power of the fundamental unit
\lambda
, in particular
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda
.

Hence it suffices to show that there is no nonzero element

\alpha=a+b\omega\in O_{K}={\mathbb{Z}}[\omega]

such that

1<\alpha<\lambda_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\sigma(\alpha)|<1.

Let

\omega^{\prime}=\sigma(\omega)=\frac{1-\sqrt{D}}{2}

be the Galois conjugate. Then we require

|\sigma(\alpha)|=|a+b\omega^{\prime}|<1.

Since
\omega+\omega^{\prime}=1
, we may write

\alpha=(a+b\omega^{\prime})+b(\omega-\omega^{\prime})=\sigma(\alpha)+b\sqrt{D}.

Hence, since
|\sigma(\alpha)|<1
,

\alpha>b\sqrt{D}-1.

On the other hand,

\lambda_{1}=\frac{x+\sqrt{D}}{2}<\sqrt{D},

because
x<\sqrt{D}=\sqrt{x^{2}+4}
.
If
b\geq 2
, then

\alpha>b\sqrt{D}-1\geq 2\sqrt{D}-1>\lambda_{1},

a contradiction. Therefore
b\leq 1
.

Next suppose
b\leq 0
. Since
\omega^{\prime}<0
, the condition

|a+b\omega^{\prime}|<1
implies
a\leq 0
, because otherwise
a\geq 1
by integrality, and then
a+b\omega^{\prime}\geq 1
. Consequently,

\alpha=a+b\omega\leq 0,

contrary to
\alpha>1
. Hence
b=1
.

Thus every potential counterexample must be of the form

\alpha=a+\omega.

The condition
|\sigma(\alpha)|<1
becomes

|a+\omega^{\prime}|<1.

which is equivalent to

-\omega^{\prime}-1<a<-\omega^{\prime}+1.

Now

-\omega^{\prime}=\frac{\sqrt{D}-1}{2}.

Since
D=x^{2}+4
and
x=2m+1
, we have

m=\frac{x-1}{2}<\frac{\sqrt{D}-1}{2}<m+\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore

m-1<-\omega^{\prime}-1<m-\frac{1}{2},

and

m+1<-\omega^{\prime}+1<m+\frac{3}{2}.

Hence the interval

(-\omega^{\prime}-1,-\omega^{\prime}+1)

contains exactly the two integers
m
and
m+1
. It follows that

a=m\quad\text{or}\quad a=m+1.

On the other hand,

\alpha=a+\omega<\lambda_{1}=m+\omega

implies

a<m.

This contradicts
a\in\{m,m+1\}
.

Therefore there is no element
\alpha\in O_{K}
satisfying

1<\alpha<\lambda_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\sigma(\alpha)|<1.

Hence
K
satisfies condition H.
∎

is not the fundamental unit.
The fundamental unit is
\omega=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
, which has norm
-1
.

Hence we deduce that
N(\lambda)=-1
, and since
\lambda=m+\omega_{K}
, we obtain the equation

-1=N(\lambda)=\frac{(2m+1)^{2}-p}{4}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad p=(2m+1)^{2}+4

that is

D=p=(2m+1)^{2}+4.

Thus we are a subset of Yokoi’s family, and we showed in Corollary
4.2
that the discriminants in Yokoi’s family which satisfy Hammarhjelm’s condition are precisely those with

D=5,13,29,53,173,293.

These are all cases of prime discriminants where Hammarhjelm’s condition holds.

7.2.
D=pq

Next, assume that
\Delta=D=pq
with
p,q
distinct primes,
p=q\bmod 4
.

Case 1:
p=q=1\bmod 4
:
We showed (Lemma
2.2
) that the
4
-rank of the narrow class group is
1
, hence
4\mid h_{K}^{+}
and
h_{K}
is even, so cannot be one.

Case 2:
p=q=3\bmod 4
:
Here there is no unit of negative norm. By Lemma
7.1
,
the fundamental unit is of the form
\lambda=m+\omega=\frac{2m+1+\sqrt{D}}{2}
, and since it has norm
N(\lambda)=+1
, and we have

+1=N(\lambda)=\frac{(2m+1)^{2}-D}{4}\longleftrightarrow D=(2m+1)^{2}-4.

This is one of the family of “narrow Richaud-Degert” extensions, and we showed in Proposition
5.1
that these never satisfy condition H if
D>5
.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
