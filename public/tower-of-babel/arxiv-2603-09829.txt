Control and stabilization of cascade coupled systems: application to a 1-d heat and wave coupled system
Lucas Davron, Swann Marx, Pierre Lissy

Abstract
We study cascade coupled systems, for which our prototypical example is a 1-d heat equation coupled with a 1-d wave equation. The heat component is controlled through one boundary and the information is transmitted through another one to the wave component, while the wave component does not influence the heat component. Our aim is to understand the well-posedness, controllability and stabilizability properties for such a system. Establishing well-posedness is tedious using the classical energy method, which motivates us to take advantage of the cascade structure. Taking again advantage of this structure, we prove a simultaneous exact and approximate controllability result. Finally, we obtain polynomial stabilization by means of a closed-loop control defined through the solution to a Sylvester equation. These results are all discussed in an abstract LTI framework and most of our findings apply to more general situations.

Stabilization

We will consider three stability concepts for a

•

The semigroup

\exists\lambda,c>0,\quad\forall\mathbf{Z}^{0}\in\mathcal{X},\quad\forall t>0,\quad\|e^{t\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{Z}^{0}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq ce^{-\lambda t}\|\mathbf{Z}^{0}\|_{\mathcal{X}}.

•

\forall\mathbf{Z}^{0}\in D(\mathcal{A}),\quad\forall t>0,\quad\|e^{t\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{Z}^{0}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq m(t)\|\mathbf{Z}^{0}\|_{D(\mathcal{A})}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

\left\langle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{S}_{t-\sigma}-1)F\mathbb{F}(1\otimes\mathrm{v})(\sigma)d\sigma,\psi^{0}\right\rangle_{D(E^{*})^{\prime},D(E^{*})}=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathbb{F}(1\otimes\mathrm{v})(\sigma),F^{*}(\mathbb{S}_{t-\sigma}^{*}-1)\psi^{0}\rangle_{Y}d\sigma,

where
\mathbb{F}(1\otimes\mathrm{v})\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}([0,\infty);Y)
(which implies that
||\mathbb{F}(1\otimes\mathrm{v})||_{L^{2}([0,t);Y)}=O(\sqrt{t})
as

(which implies that

as

2.3
Riesz basis property

In this § we transfer certain types of Riesz basis properties from
A
and
E
to
\mathcal{A}
.

Assumption 2.8
.

•

A
(resp.
E
) is a closed densely defined unbounded operator on
Z
(resp.
W
).

•

The operators
C,F
are respectively bounded
D(A)\to Y
and
Y\to D(E^{*})^{\prime}
.

•

The operators
A
and
E
are both diagonalizable in Riesz bases, respectively denoted
(z_{j})
and
(w_{k})
.

•

The operators
A
and
E
do not share any eigenvalue.

We define a unbounded operator
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{X}:=Z\times W
exactly as in Theorem
2.4
. We denote
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...
(resp.
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},...
) the eigenvalues of
A
(resp.
E
), possibly counted with multiplicities, in such a way that
z_{j}
(resp.
w_{k}
) is associated to
\lambda_{j}
(resp.
\mu_{k}
). Under the above assumptions, it is easy to show that
\sigma_{p}(\mathcal{A})=\sigma_{p}(A)\cup\sigma_{p}(E)
, with eigenvectors given by

\mathbf{Z}_{k}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
w_{k}\end{array}\right),\quad\mathbf{Z}_{j}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}z_{j}\\
(\lambda_{j}-E)^{-1}FCz_{j}\end{array}\right).

The following is a sufficient condition for
\mathcal{A}
to be diagonalizable in a Riesz basis.

Proposition 2.9
.

The normalized eigenvectors of
\mathcal{A}
form a Riesz basis of
\mathcal{X}
whenever

\sum_{j}\|(\lambda_{j}-E)^{-1}FCz_{j}\|_{W}^{2}<\infty.

(28)

Proof.

We aim at showing that the family

\mathcal{F}:=\{\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_{j}:j=1,2,...\}\cup\{\mathbf{Z}_{k}:k=1,2,...\},\quad\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_{j}:=\frac{\mathbf{Z}_{j}}{\|\mathbf{Z}_{j}\|_{\mathcal{X}}}

is a Riesz basis of
\mathcal{X}
. Note that the hypothesis (
28
) implies that
(\lambda_{j}-E)^{-1}FCz_{j}
is bounded in
W
with respect to
j
, and therefore
(\mathbf{Z}_{j})_{j}
is almost normalized in
\mathcal{X}
. We can therefore equivalently consider the family

\mathcal{F}:=\{\mathbf{Z}_{j}:j=1,2,...\}\cup\{\mathbf{Z}_{k}:k=1,2,...\}.

By
[
19
, Chapter 6, Theorem 2.3]
, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{F}
is
\omega
-independent and that it is quadratically close to another Riesz basis
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{X}
. Our reference Riesz basis will be

\mathcal{G}:=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}z_{j}\\
0\end{array}\right):j=1,2,...\right\}\cup\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
w_{k}\end{array}\right):k=1,2,...\right\},

which is clearly a Riesz basis of
\mathcal{X}
. The fact that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are quadratically close is equivalent to the hypothesis (
28
), hence we are left with the
\omega
-independence of
\mathcal{F}
. Because the family
\mathcal{F}
is almost normalized, its
\omega
-independence can be rephrased as: for any sequence of coefficients
\alpha\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{F})
, if the series
\sum\alpha_{f}f
converges (for some summation order) to the zero vector, then, all the coefficients are zero. So let
(\alpha_{f})
be as such, write

\{\alpha_{f}\}_{f\in\mathcal{F}}=\{\beta_{j}\}_{j}\cup\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k},

and take the first component of the convergence

\sum\alpha_{f}f\xrightarrow{Z\times W}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\end{array}\right),

(29)

(where we do not specify the summation order for simplicity) to obtain that

\sum\beta_{j}z_{j}=0.

Taking advantage of the
\omega
-independence of
(z_{j})
in
Z
, we obtain:

\forall j=1,2,...,\quad\beta_{j}=0.

Considering the second component of (
29
), we obtain:

0=\sum\beta_{j}(\lambda_{j}-E)^{-1}FCz_{j}+\sum\gamma_{k}w_{k}=\sum\gamma_{k}w_{k}.

Because the
(w_{k})
are
\omega
-independent, we obtain:

\forall k=1,2,...,\quad\gamma_{k}=0,

which shows that
(\alpha_{f})_{f\in\mathcal{F}}
is identically zero. This concludes the proof.
∎

2.4
Applications to the heat-wave system

In this section we apply all the results of the previous section to the heat-wave system (

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

Following the transposition method (see
[
12
, Chapter 2]
and
[
41
, Section 10.2]
), the equations (
2
) are well-posed in the Hadamard sense on the state space
Z=L^{2}(0,1)
and the input space

. More precisely, for all
0<T<\infty
,
z^{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
and

, there is a unique solution

to (

). It satisfies the bound

\|z(\cdot)\|_{C([0,T];Z)}\leq C(T)\left\{\|z^{0}\|_{Z}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right\}.

Introduce the Neumann Laplacian as the unbounded operator
A
on
L^{2}(0,1)
with

A=\partial_{xx},\quad D(A)=\{z\in H^{2}(0,1):z_{x}(0)=z_{x}(1)=0\}.

It is known that
-A
is diagonalizable in a Hilbert basis, with simple eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<...
. We have

\lambda_{j}=((j-1)\pi)^{2},\quad e_{j}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&j=1,\\
\sqrt{2}\cos((j-1)\pi x),&j\geq 2,\end{array}\right.

where

is a normalized eigenvector of
-A
associated to
\lambda_{j}
. The solution

of (
2
) then writes

z(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\langle z^{0},e_{j}\rangle e_{j}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}(t-\sigma)}B^{*}e_{j}u(\sigma)e_{j}d\sigma,

where we have defined
B^{*}:=\delta_{1}
. One sees that
z(\cdot)
belongs to
C([0,T]\times[0,1])
, with norm controlled by that of
z^{0}
and
u
, hence the output

is well-defined. For
z^{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
and
u\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}[0,\infty)
we denote

the solution of (
2
). We put

\mathbb{T}_{t}z^{0}=z[z^{0},0](t,\cdot),\quad\Phi_{t}u=z[0,u](t,\cdot),\quad(\mathbb{L}z^{0})(t)=z[z^{0},0](t,0),\quad(\mathbb{F}u)(t)=z[0,u](t,0),

which obviously defines an abstract linear system, with output

. Its infinitesimal generator is

, its control operator is
B
and the observation operator is

. We verify that it is regular with

: for

and

we compute

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{F}(1\otimes\mathrm{v})(\sigma)d\sigma=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}z[0,u](\sigma,0)d\sigma,

and observe that the function
t\mapsto z[0,u](t,0)
is continuous and vanishes as

, hence the regularity and
D=0
.

A similar reasoning applies to the wave system (

is given by (

hence we put

\varphi^{e}
the extension. One easily sees that
\varphi^{e}\in C([0,T);L^{2}(0,\infty))
, and for all
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times(0,\infty))

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{e}(\zeta_{t}+\zeta_{xx})dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\zeta_{t}+\zeta_{xx})dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\zeta_{t}+\zeta_{xx})dxdt-\int_{0}^{T}\zeta(t,0)f(t)dt=0.

Thus,
\varphi^{e}
solves
(-\partial_{t}-\partial_{xx})\varphi^{e}=0
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times(0,\infty))
. Up to a modification on a null set, the modified function still denoted by
\varphi^{e}
, we have
\varphi^{e}\in C^{\infty}((0,T)\times(0,\infty))
and for all
0<t<T
the function
\varphi^{e}(t,\cdot)
is analytic. Since
\varphi^{e}(t,x)=0
for almost every
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(1,\infty)
, we deduce that
\varphi^{e}(t,x)=0
for almost all
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,\infty)
. Returning to (

for almost every

. Then, on the one hand, because the wave equation (

\|\varphi^{T}\|_{*}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}|\Gamma^{e}(\varphi^{T},0)(t)|^{2}dt=\int_{0}^{T}|\Gamma^{e}(\varphi^{T},\psi^{T})(t)|^{2}dt=0,

where the first equality is the definition of

\varphi^{T}=0

3.1
The Sylvester equation

In this §, we discuss the existence and properties of a solution of the Sylvester equation (
10

are generators of semigroups on a Banach space
\mathcal{B}
, and that

is a bounded operator on

, the equation for

admits the unique solution

x=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ta}ce^{tb}dt,

and the integral converges absolutely in
\mathcal{L}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
, see
[
8
, §9]
for a survey and
[

, Theorem 3]
for a sharp result. Returning to (

(-E)\Pi+\Pi A=-FC
and observe that the above-mentioned result should apply, at least formally, if
-E
generates a semigroup (or equivalently, if

generates a group).

Assumption 3.1
.

•

(A,B,C)
are the generating operators of a regular abstract linear system with

, on the state space
Z
, the input space
U
and the output space

.

•

(E,F)
are the generating operators of an abstract linear control system, on the state space

and the input space

.

•

The operator

is skew-adjoint.

We emphasize that

and

, which allows us to give meaning to the computations below.

It is convenient to introduce the following notion of weak solution.

Definition 3.2
.

A weak solution of (

) is a bounded operator
\Pi:Z\rightarrow W
such that for all

and

, there holds

\langle\Pi z,Ew\rangle_{W}+\langle\Pi Az,w\rangle_{W}=-\langle Cz,F^{*}w\rangle_{Y}.

Because

is skew-adjoint, the above is equivalent to: for all

, we have the equality

\Pi Az-E\Pi z=-FCz\mbox{ in }W_{-1}.

Our purpose is to exhibit and study a solution; we shall not deal with its uniqueness.
9
9

9

See
[
16
, §3.3, Proposition 1]
.
Observe that the operator
FC
is bounded
Z_{1}\rightarrow W_{-1}
, hence we cannot apply the previously cited result

. Denoting by
(\mathbb{S}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the semigroup generated by
E
, and

being skew-adjoint, we have that
(\mathbb{S}_{t}^{*})_{t\geq 0}
is the semigroup generated by

) is then equivalent to
E^{*}\Pi+\Pi A=-FC
, for which the

is

\Pi=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{S}_{t}^{*}FC\mathbb{T}_{t}dt,

(39)

where we recall that
(\mathbb{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the semigroup generated by

. The above integrand is a continuous function of

, taking values in the Banach space
\mathcal{L}_{c}(Z_{1};W_{-1})
. The following is a sufficient condition for the above integral to converge in a certain sense in

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume the Assumptions
3.1
hold. Assume moreover that there exists a weight
\mathfrak{w}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
and a constant

such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|C\mathbb{T}_{t}z\|_{Y}^{2}\mathfrak{w}(t)dt\leq c\|z\|_{Z}^{2},\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\|F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}w\|_{Y}^{2}\frac{dt}{\mathfrak{w}(t)}\leq c\|w\|_{W}^{2},

(40)

holds for all
z\in Z
and
w\in W
. Then, for all
z\in Z
and
w\in W
, the integral

\pi(z,w):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle C\mathbb{T}_{t}z,F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}w\rangle_{Y}dt

absolutely converges, and defines a continuous sesquilinear form
\pi:Z\times W\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. The associated bounded operator
\Pi:Z\rightarrow W
is a weak solution of (

).

Proof.

We first show that the integral defining
\pi
is convergent. Let

and
w\in W
, from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\langle C\mathbb{T}_{t}z,F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}w\rangle_{Y}|dt\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|C\mathbb{T}_{t}z\|_{Y}^{2}\mathfrak{w}(t)dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}w\|_{Y}^{2}\frac{dt}{\mathfrak{w}(t)}\right)^{1/2}\leq c\|z\|_{Z}\|w\|_{W}.

Thus, the integral defining
\pi
is absolutely convergent, defines a bounded sesquilinear form

, which defines a bounded linear operator

from the Riesz representation theorem.

We now verify that

is a weak solution of (

,

and compute

\displaystyle\langle\Pi z,Ew\rangle_{W}+\langle\Pi Az,w\rangle_{W}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle C\mathbb{T}_{t}z,F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}Ew\rangle_{Y}dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle C\mathbb{T}_{t}Az,F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}w\rangle_{Y}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\langle C\mathbb{T}_{t}z,F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}w\rangle_{Y}dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\langle C\mathbb{T}_{t}z,F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}w\rangle_{Y}-\langle Cz,F^{*}w\rangle_{Y}.

We conclude by noticing that the limit at the right-hand side vanishes, as the function whose limit is taken is

3.3
Asymptotic stability

In this subsection, we obtain general asymptotic stability results for the closed-loop system generated by
\mathfrak{A}
. It will be useful to consider the following observability notion.

Definition 3.6
.

Consider an observation pair
(G,O)
, where
G
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\mathbb{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on some Hilbert space
H
. The pair
(G,O)
is said to be approximately observable in infinite-time when the following holds

\forall h^{0}\in H,\quad O\mathbb{G}_{t}h^{0}\equiv 0\mbox{ on }(0,\infty)\Longrightarrow h^{0}=0.

We shall work under the following additional hypotheses.

Assumption 3.7
.

•

The operator
E
has compact resolvent.

•

The operator
A
is exponentially stable.

•

The pair
(E^{*},F^{*})
is approximately observable in infinite-time.

•

For all eigenvalue
i\mu
of
E
, the operator
\mathbf{H}(i\mu)
has dense range, where
\mathbf{H}
stands for the transfer function of
(A,B,C)
.

For the convenience of the reader we provide the definition of the transfer function of an abstract linear system
(\mathbb{T},\Phi,\mathbb{L},\mathbb{F})
. Given
u\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}([0,\infty);U)
we define the output
y(t):=(\mathbb{F}u)(t)\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}([0,\infty);Y)
. By standard semigroup techniques one can show that if
u\in L^{2}([0,\infty);U)
, then
y
is Laplace transformable, with abscissa of absolute convergence
\leq\omega_{0}(\mathbb{T})
. It can be further shown that there exists a holomorphic function
\mathbf{H}:\mathbb{C}_{\omega_{0}(\mathbb{T})}\to\mathcal{L}_{c}(U;Y)
such that

\hat{y}(s)=\mathbf{H}(s)\hat{u}(s),\quad\operatorname{Re}s>\max(\omega_{0}(\mathbb{T}),0),

where
\mathbb{C}_{\alpha}:=\{s\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}s>\alpha\}
. The above relation further makes
\mathbf{H}
unique, it is called the transfer function of
(\mathbb{T},\Phi,\mathbb{L},\mathbb{F})
.

Remark 3.8
.

The hypothesis that
\mathbf{H}(i\mu)
has dense range for all
i\mu\in\sigma_{p}(E)
is a non-resonance type condition. It already appeared in the context of the stabilization of cascade systems in
[
33
, Proposition 1]
and
[
35
, Assumption 2.2]
, in a different form.

We first recall a few standard facts. Firstly, the assumption
3.4
imposes that
\Pi B
is an admissible control operator for
E
.
E
being skew-adjoint, the operator
(\Pi B)^{*}
is also an admissible observation operator for
E
. Because
E
has compact resolvent, it is diagonalizable in a Hilbert basis, and its eigenvalues are of course purely imaginary. As shown in
[
41
, Proposition 6.9.1]
, the approximate observability in infinite-time of the pair
(E,(\Pi B)^{*})
is equivalent to the following condition: for every eigenvector
w
of

, we have
(\Pi B)^{*}w\neq 0
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Proposition
3.5
, together with Assumptions
3.7
, are all satisfied. Then, the pair
(E,(\Pi B)^{*})
is approximately observable in infinite-time.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exists an eigenvector
w
of
E
such that
(\Pi B)^{*}w=0
and put
i\mu
the eigenvalue associated to
w
. For all
u\in U
, we compute

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle u,(\Pi B)^{*}w\rangle_{U}

\displaystyle=\langle\Pi Bu,w\rangle_{W_{-1},W_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle C\mathbb{T}_{t}Bu,F^{*}\mathbb{S}_{t}w\rangle_{Y}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle C\mathbb{T}_{t}Bu,F^{*}e^{i\mu t}w\rangle_{Y}dt

\displaystyle=\left\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i\mu t}C\mathbb{T}_{t}Budt,F^{*}w\right\rangle_{Y}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{H}(i\mu)u,F^{*}w\rangle_{Y}.

In the above, the third equality is due to the fact that
\Pi
assumes the same representation formula on

(see the discussion after Assumption

, contradicting the approximate observability of

in infinite time.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Proposition

From the previously shown fact and standard approximation arguments, we may take

Note that the resolvents of

A_{\alpha}

which is enough to conclude. As established earlier

\|z\|_{Z}\lesssim\|f\|_{Z}+\|(is-A_{\alpha})^{-1}B(\Pi_{\alpha}B)^{*}p\|_{Z}.

Observe that

is bounded. We claim that

\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\|(is-A_{\alpha})^{-1}B\|_{U\to Z}<\infty.

To see this, we pass to the adjoint and observe that since
A_{\alpha}
is self-adjoint,

\|(is-A_{\alpha})^{-1}B\|_{U\to Z}=\|B^{*}(-is-A_{\alpha})^{-1}\|_{Z\to U}.

For
z\in Z
written as
z=\sum_{j\geq 1}z_{j}e_{j,\alpha}
, we have

(-is-A_{\alpha})^{-1}z=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{z_{j}}{-is-\lambda_{j,\alpha}}\,e_{j,\alpha}.

Hence

B^{*}(-is-A_{\alpha})^{-1}z=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{z_{j}\,B^{*}e_{j,\alpha}}{-is-\lambda_{j,\alpha}}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\|B^{*}(-is-A_{\alpha})^{-1}\|_{Z\to U}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|B^{*}e_{j,\alpha}|^{2}}{s^{2}+\lambda_{j,\alpha}^{2}}.

Since the sequence
(B^{*}e_{j,\alpha})_{j\geq 1}

\lambda_{j,\alpha}\asymp j^{2}
, we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|B^{*}e_{j,\alpha}|^{2}}{s^{2}+\lambda_{j,\alpha}^{2}}\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|B^{*}e_{j,\alpha}|^{2}}{\lambda_{j,\alpha}^{2}}<\infty,

uniformly for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

This proves the desired bound. From
[

, Theorem 2.4]
,
\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
is polynomially stable, at rate

(for small enough

For the last item we proceed as follows: we introduce the subset
