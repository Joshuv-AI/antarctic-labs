Peng Gao

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Let
r(n)
denote the number of representations of
n
as sums of two squares of integers, where different signs and different orders of the summands are counted as different. The well-known Gauss circle problem can be viewed as the evaluation of the sum
\sum_{n\leq x}r(n)
for real
x\geq 2
.

It is shown by Gauss that (see
[
5
, (1.70)]
) for
\theta=1/2
, we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}r(n)=\pi x+O(x^{\theta}).

The best estimate for the error term in (
1.1
) up to date is due to M. N. Huxley, who proved in
[
4
]
that the above expression holds with
\theta=131/208+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
. We remark here (see
[
5
, p. 215]
) that it is conjectured that the best possible error term in (
1.1
) should be
\theta=1/4+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

We denote
P(n)
the largest prime factor of any positive integer
n
. We say an integer
n\geq 1
is
y
-smooth or
y
-friable if
P(n)\leq y
.
In
[
1
]
, A. Goswami studied the Gauss circle problem over smooth integers. To be more precise, we define for
x\geq y\geq 2
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Psi_{G}(x,y):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}r(n).

Let
\rho(u)
, known as the Dickman function to be the unique continuous function on
[0,\infty)
satisfying the differential difference equation

\displaystyle u\rho^{\prime}(u)+\rho(u-1)=0,

subject to the initial condition
\rho(u)=1,0\leq u\leq 1
.

We further set
x=y^{u}
for any
x\geq y\geq 2
throughout the paper so that
u\geq 1
. Then it is shown in
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
that for any
\varepsilon>0
and
\exp((\log x)^{2/3+\varepsilon})\leq y\leq x
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that uniformly in
u
and
y
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Psi_{G}(x,y)=\pi\rho(u)x+O\Big(\frac{x\rho(u)\log^{2}(u+1)}{\log y}+xu^{2}\log u\log y\exp(-c\sqrt{\log y})\Big).

It is the aim of this paper to evaluate
\Psi_{G}(x,y)
asymptotically for a wider range of
u
. Our approach here is inspired by the treatments employed by
A. Hildebrand and G. Tenenbaum
[
2
]
to obtain an asymptotic formula for
\Psi(x,y)
, where
\Psi(x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}1
denotes the number of integers
\leq x
that are
y
-smooth. Their approach starts by observing that one may apply Rankin’s trick to see that for any
\sigma>0
,
x\geq y\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\Psi(x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{P(n)\leq y}\biggl(\frac{x}{n}\biggr)^{\sigma}=x^{\sigma}\zeta(\sigma,y),

where for complex
s=\sigma+it
with
\sigma,t\in\mathbb{R}
, we set

\displaystyle\zeta(s,y)=\prod_{p\leq y}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}.

Here and throughout the paper, we reserve the letter
p
for a prime number.

We write
\varphi(s,y)=\log\zeta(s,y)
and we denote
\varphi_{k}(s,y)
the
k
-th partial derivative of
\varphi(s,y)
with respect to
s
for all integers
k\geq 1
. For
x\geq y\geq 2
, let
\alpha=\alpha(x,y)
denote the unique solution of the equation
\varphi_{1}(\alpha,y)+\log x=0
, then it is shown in
[
2
]
that
x^{\sigma}\zeta(\sigma,y)
is minimized at this
\alpha
. The key idea in
[
2
]
is to evaluate
\Psi(x,y)
using Perron’s formula by choosing the integral involved (say with respect to the variable
s
) to be on the line
\Re(s)=\alpha
. This way, it was shown in
[
2
, Theorem 1]
that uniformly for
x\geq y\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\Psi(x,y)=\frac{x^{\alpha}\zeta(\alpha,y)}{\alpha\sqrt{2\pi\varphi_{2}(\alpha,y)}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{u}+\frac{\log y}{y})\Big).

Moreover, asymptotical behaviors of both
\alpha(x,y)
and
\varphi_{2}(\alpha,y)
were given in
[
2
, Theorem 2]
.

In our case, we denote for
s\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle H(s,G;y):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{r(n)/4}{n^{s}}.

Here we note that we consider
r(n)/4
instead of
r(n)
is because that the function
r(n)
is multiplicative (see the remark made below (
2.6
)). We set
\phi(s,G;y):=\log H(s,G;y)
, and we denote
\phi_{k}(s,G;y)
the
k
-th partial derivative of
\phi(s,G;y)
with respect to
s
for all integers
k\geq 1
.

We apply Rankin’s trick to see that for any
\sigma>0
,
x\geq y\geq 2
,

\Psi_{G}(x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}r(n)\leq\sum_{P(n)\leq y}r(n)\biggl(\frac{x}{n}\biggr)^{\sigma}=4x^{\sigma}H(\sigma,G;y).

It follows that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Psi_{G}(x,y)\leq 4\min_{\sigma>0}x^{\sigma}H(\sigma,G;y).

It is shown in Lemma
3.1
below that the infimum above is in fact a minimum, and is attained for a unique
\alpha_{G}=\alpha_{G}(x,y)>0
defined as
the solution of the equation

(1.5)

\displaystyle\log x+\phi_{1}(\alpha_{G},G;y)=0.

We shall henceforth call
\alpha_{G}
the saddle point.

Our main result evaluates
\Psi_{G}(x,y)
in terms of
H(\alpha_{G},G;y)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

With the notation as above. Let
x\geq y\geq 2
and set
x=y^{u}
. For any fixed
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, we have for
(\log\log y)^{2}\leq u\leq y^{1/(2+\varepsilon_{0})}/\log y
and
y
large enough,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Psi_{G}(x,y)=\frac{4x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)}{\alpha_{G}\sqrt{2\pi\phi_{2}(\alpha_{G},G;y)}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{u})\Big).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is similar to that of the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 1]
. We also apply Perron’s formula to evaluates
\Psi_{G}(x,y)
, and again with the integral involved to be on the line determined by the saddle point.

For
u>1
, we define
\xi=\xi(u)
to be the non-zero solution of the equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{\xi}=1+u\xi.\end{split}

We also set
\xi(1)=0
.

Our next result evaluates the main term on the right-hand side of (
1.6
) asymptotically in terms of simple functions of
x,y
.

Theorem 1.2
.

With the notation as above. Let
x\geq y\geq 2
and set
x=y^{u}
. For any fixed
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, we have uniformly for
(\log x)^{2+\varepsilon_{0}}<y<x
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{G}(x,y)=&\frac{\log(1+y/\log x)}{\log y}\Big(1+O\big(\frac{\log\log(1+y)}{\log y}\big)\Big),\\
\phi_{2}(x,G;y)=&(1+\frac{\log x}{y})\log x\cdot\log y\Big(1+O\big(\frac{1}{\log(1+u)}+\frac{1}{\log y}\big)\Big),\\
\frac{4x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)}{\alpha_{G}\sqrt{2\pi\phi_{2}(\alpha_{G},G;y)}}=&\pi x\Big(\frac{\xi^{\prime}(u)}{2\pi}\Big)^{1/2}\exp\Big(-\gamma_{0}-u\xi(u)+\int^{\xi(u)}_{0}\frac{e^{s}-1}{s}ds+O_{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{\log(1+u)}{\log y}+u\exp(-(\log y)^{3/5-\varepsilon})\big)\Big),\end{split}

where
\gamma_{0}=0.577\ldots
is the Euler constant.

Note that it is shown in
[
2
, Corollary 2]
that as
u\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho(u)=(1+O(\frac{1}{u}))\Big(\frac{\xi^{\prime}(u)}{2\pi}\Big)^{1/2}\exp\Big(-\gamma_{0}-u\xi(u)+\int^{\xi(u)}_{0}\frac{e^{s}-1}{s}ds\Big).\end{split}

Thus the result given in (
1.7
) is consistent with that given in (
1.3
). Moreover, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
and the above together implies an asymptotical formula for
\alpha_{G}(x,y)
that is similar to that given in (
1.3
) but valid for much larger values of
u
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is similar to that of
[
2
, Theorem 2]
upon using (
2.5
) below, so we omit it here. We only point out that in the proof, we may assume
y
is large enough so that by (
3.7
) below, the condition
u\leq y^{1/(2+\varepsilon_{0})}/\log y
given in Theorem
1.1
or the condition
(\log x)^{2+\varepsilon_{0}}<y
given in Theorem
1.2
implies that we have

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{G}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{1},\end{split}

where
\varepsilon_{1}>0
is a constant depending on
\varepsilon_{0}
only. This in turn implies that the contribution from sums over
p^{-2\alpha_{G}}
is bounded and therefore may be ignored.

2.
Preliminaries

We now collect some auxiliary results needed in our proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Sums over primes

Recall that we denote
\chi_{4}
the (only) primitive Dirichlet character modulo
4
, so that we have
\chi_{4}(n)=(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
if
(n,2)=1
,
\chi_{4}(n)=0
if
2|n
.
We include in this section a result on certain sums over primes.

Lemma 2.2
.

With the notation as above. Let
x\geq 2
and let
C>0
be a constant. We have, for some constants
\gamma_{G}
and
b_{i}>0,1\leq i\leq 4
, and for
0\leq\sigma\leq 1+C/\log x
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\log p=

\displaystyle x+O\Big(x\exp(-b_{1}\sqrt{\log x})\Big),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\chi_{4}(p)\log p=

\displaystyle O\Big(x\exp(-b_{2}\sqrt{\log x})\Big),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}}=

\displaystyle\int^{x}_{1}\frac{du}{u^{\sigma}}+O\Big(1+x^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{3}\sqrt{\log x})\Big),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\sigma}}=

\displaystyle O\Big(1+x^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{4}\sqrt{\log x})\Big),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq x}(1-\frac{1}{p})^{-1}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4}e^{\gamma_{0}}\log x+O(1).

Proof.

The expression in (
2.1
) follows from
[
6
, Theorem 6.9]
. To establish (
2.2
), we let
K=\mathbb{Q}(i)
be the Gaussian number field and consider the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{K}(s)
of
K
, which is defined for
\Re(s)>1
to be

\displaystyle\zeta_{K}(s)=\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}}\frac{1}{N(\mathcal{A})^{s}},

where
\mathcal{A}
runs over all non-zero integral ideals in
K
and
N(\mathcal{A})
is the norm of
\mathcal{A}
.

It is shown in
[
5
, p. 17]
that we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\zeta_{K}(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{4})=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{r(n)}{4n^{s}}.

In particular, this implies that the function
r(n)/4
is multiplicative.
Note also that it is established in
[
7
]
a Vinogradov–Korobov type zero-free region
\zeta_{K}(s)
. Namely, for
s=\sigma+it
with
\sigma,t\in\mathbb{R}
, then for suitable positive constants
c
and
t_{0}
,
\zeta_{K}(s)\neq 0
in the region

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sigma\geq 1-c(\log|t|)^{-2/3}(\log\log|t|)^{-1/3},\quad|t|\geq t_{0}.

In view of (
2.6
), the above now implies that both
\zeta(s)
and
L(s,\chi_{4})
are non-zero in the above region. In particular, there is no exceptional zero for
L(s,\chi_{4})
so that the estimation (
2.2
) follows from
[
6
, (11.24)]
.

We apply (
2.1
) and partial summation to see that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}}=

\displaystyle\int^{x}_{2}\frac{du}{u^{\sigma}}+\int^{x}_{2}\frac{dO\Big(x\exp(-b_{1}\sqrt{\log x})\Big)}{u^{\sigma}}.

It follows from the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 7.3]
that for some constant
b_{3}>0
, the last integration above is

(2.9)

\displaystyle\ll 1+x^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{3}\sqrt{\log x}).

Here we note that
[
6
, Lemma 7.3]
is stated for
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
. However, an inspection of the proof, especially that lead to
[
6
, (7.19)]
shows that (
2.9
) continues to hold for
0\leq\sigma\leq 1+C/\log x
.
Note moreover that we have
\int^{x}_{2}\frac{du}{u^{\sigma}}=\int^{x}_{1}\frac{du}{u^{\sigma}}+O(1)
. Substituting this and (
2.9
) into (
2.8
) now leads to the validity of (
2.3
). The estimation in (
2.4
) is similarly established upon using (
2.2
).

Lastly, to establish (
2.5
), we may assume that
x
is sufficiently large. We then note that by Mertens’ formula (see
[
6
, Theorem 2.7 (e)]
), we have for
x\geq 2
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq x}(1-\frac{1}{p})^{-1}=

\displaystyle e^{\gamma_{0}}\log x+O(1).

We recall that the residue of
\zeta_{K}(s)
at
s=1
equals
\pi/4
. In view of (
2.6
) and keep in mind that the residue of
\zeta(s)
at
s=1
equals
1
, we deduce that
L(1,\chi_{4})=\pi/4
. Now we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq x}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}\prod_{p>x}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}=L(1,\chi_{4}(p))\prod_{p>x}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}=\frac{\pi}{4}\prod_{p>x}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}.

Note that we have

\displaystyle\prod_{p>x}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}=\exp\Big(\sum_{p>x}-\log(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})\Big)=\exp\Big(\sum_{p>x}\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p}+O(\sum_{p>x}\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)=\exp\Big(\sum_{p>x}\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p}+O(\frac{1}{x})\Big).

We set
\sigma=1
in (
2.4
) to see that
\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p}=O(1)
. Applying this and partial summation, we see that

\sum_{p>x}\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p}=O(1/\log x)
. As
e^{z}=1+O(|z|)
for complex
|z|\leq 1
, we see from the above that when
x
is large enough, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{p>x}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}=\exp\Big(O(\frac{1}{\log x})\Big)=1+O(\frac{1}{\log x}).

Substituting the above into (
2.11
), we see that

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq x}(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p})^{-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4}\big(1+O(\frac{1}{\log x})\big).

This together with (
2.10
) now implies the validity of (
2.5
) and thus completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We denote
L_{p}
the local factor at the prime
p
in the Euler product of
L
for any
L
-function. In particular,
\zeta_{p}
denotes the local factor at the prime
p
in the Euler product of the Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)
. Recall the definition of
H(s,f;y)
from (
1.2
). In view of (
2.6
), we see that for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we have for
y\geq 2
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle H(s,G;y)=\prod_{p\leq y}\zeta_{p}(s)L_{p}(s,\chi_{4})=\prod_{p\leq y}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}(1-\chi_{4}(p)p^{-s})^{-1}.

Note that
H(s,G;y)
is meromorphic in
s
in the whole complex plane. It never vanishes, and all its poles are located on the line
\sigma=0
.

3.
Properties of the saddle point

We first show that the minimum in (
1.4
) is attained at a unique point
\alpha_{G}>0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

With the notation as above. For
x\geq y\geq 2
, the minimum at the right-hand side expression of (
1.4
) is attained at a
\alpha_{G}>0
which is the unique solution of the equation (
1.5
).

Proof.

We recall from (
1.4
) that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{G}(x,y)\leq 4\min_{\sigma>0}x^{\sigma}H(\sigma,G;y)=4\min_{\sigma>0}\exp\big(\sigma\log x+\phi(\sigma,G;y)\big).

We derive from the definition of
\phi_{2}(s,G;y)
and (
2.12
) that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{2}(s,G;y)=\sum_{p\leq y}\Big(\frac{p^{s}(\log p)^{2}}{(p^{s}-1)^{2}}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)p^{s}(\log p)^{2}}{(p^{s}-\chi_{4}(p))^{2}}\Big)=:\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{2p^{\sigma}(\log p)^{2}f(p^{\sigma},\chi_{4}(p))}{(p^{\sigma}-1)^{2}(p^{\sigma}-\chi_{4}(p))^{2}},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(z,\chi_{4}(p))=z^{2}-(1+\chi_{4}(p))z+1\geq(z-1)^{2}\geq 0,\quad z\geq 0.\end{split}

The above and (
3.2
) now implies that
\phi_{2}(\sigma,G;y)\geq 0
for
\sigma\geq 0
, so that the function
\sigma\mapsto\sigma\log x+\phi(\sigma,G;y)
is a convex function of
\sigma
, which in turn shows that there is a unique
\alpha_{G}>0
minimizing it. This
\alpha_{G}
certainly satisfies equation (
1.5
) and the solution of the equation (
1.5
) is unique in view of
\phi_{2}(\sigma,G;y)\geq 0
. The above discussions together with (
3.1
) now allows us to complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

Recall that we set
x=y^{u}
so that
u\geq 1
. Our next result establishes certain asymptotical properties of
\alpha_{G}(x,y)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

With the notation as above. For
y
sufficiently large, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\alpha_{G}(y^{u},y)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\log y},\quad 1\leq u\leq\frac{y}{8\log y},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{G}(y^{u},y)=

\displaystyle 1+O(\frac{1}{\log y}),\quad 1\leq u\leq 14,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\alpha_{G}(y^{u},y)\leq

\displaystyle 1-\frac{4}{\log y},\quad u\geq 14,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\alpha_{G}(y^{u},y)=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\xi(u)}{\log y}+O\biggl(\frac{1}{(\log y)^{2}}+\frac{u}{y}\biggr),\quad 1\leq u\leq\frac{y}{8\log y},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\alpha_{G}(y^{u},y)=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\log(u\log u)}{\log y}+O\biggl(\frac{\log\log u}{\log u\log y}+\frac{1}{(\log y)^{2}}+\frac{u}{y}\biggr),\quad 3\leq u\leq\frac{y}{8\log y}.

Proof.

We deduce from (
1.5
) and (
2.12
) that we have for any
x\geq y\geq 2
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log x=\sum_{p\leq y}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{\alpha_{G}(x,y)}-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\alpha_{G}(x,y)}-\chi_{4}(p)}\Big)=:\sum_{p\leq y}f_{1}(p^{\alpha_{G}(x,y)}(x,y),\chi_{4}(p))\log p,\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{1}(z,\chi_{4}(p))=\frac{1}{z-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{z-\chi_{4}(p)}=\frac{1}{z-1}-1+\frac{z}{z-\chi_{4}(p)},\quad z>1.\end{split}

It follows from the above that
f_{1}(z,\chi_{4}(p))
is an increasing function of
\chi_{4}(p)
for any fixed
z>1
. As
\chi_{4}(p)
only takes value
\pm 1
or
0
, we deduce that for
z>1
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{1}(z,\chi_{4}(p))\leq&\frac{1}{z-1}+\frac{1}{z-1}=\frac{2}{z-1},\\
f_{1}(z,\chi_{4}(p))\geq&\frac{1}{z-1}+\frac{1}{z+1}=\frac{2}{z^{2}-1}>0.\end{split}

Moreover, we notice that
\chi_{4}(p)=1
when
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
so that we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{1}(z,\chi_{4}(p))=&\frac{1}{z-1}+\frac{1}{z-1}=\frac{2}{z-1},\quad p\equiv 1\pmod{4}.\end{split}

We apply from the second estimation in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) in (
3.8
) to see that

(3.11)

\displaystyle u\log y=\sum_{p\leq y}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{\alpha_{G}}-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\alpha_{G}}-\chi_{4}(p)}\Big)\geq 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{\alpha_{G}}-1}\geq\frac{2}{y^{\alpha_{G}}-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}\end{subarray}}\log p.

We then use (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) to see that for
y
sufficiently large,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}\end{subarray}}\log p=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{(1+\chi_{4}(p))\log p}{2}\geq\frac{2y}{5}.

We derive from (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) that

\displaystyle u\log y\geq\frac{4y}{5(y^{\alpha_{G}}-1)}.

As
u\leq y/(8\log y)
, the above implies that

\displaystyle\alpha_{G}\geq\frac{\log(1+4y/(5u\log y))}{\log y}\geq\frac{\log(1+32/5)}{\log y}>\frac{2}{\log y}.

This establishes the estimation given in (
3.3
).

We now assume that
\sigma\geq\frac{2}{\log y}
and observe that uniformly for
|\delta|\leq 1/2
, we have
(1-\delta)^{-1}=1+O(\delta)
. By setting
\delta=p^{-\sigma},v=v(\sigma)=e^{1/\sigma}
, we see that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{v<p\leq y}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\sigma}-\chi_{4}(p)}\Big)=\sum_{v<p\leq y}\frac{(1+\chi_{4}(p))\log p}{p^{\sigma}}+O(\sum_{v<p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\sigma}}).\end{split}

We apply (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) to see that when
\sigma\leq 1+C/\log y
for any fixed constant
C>0
, then there exists some constant
b_{5}>0
,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{v<p\leq y}\frac{(1+\chi_{4}(p))\log p}{p^{\sigma}}=&\int^{y}_{v}\frac{dw}{w^{\sigma}}+O\Big(1+y^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log y}+v^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log v})\Big)\\
=&\int^{y}_{1}\frac{dw}{w^{\sigma}}-\int^{v}_{1}\frac{dw}{w^{\sigma}}+O\Big(1+y^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log y})+v^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log v})\Big)\\
=&\int^{y}_{1}\frac{dw}{w^{\sigma}}+O\Big(v+y^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log y})\Big).\end{split}

We next consider

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq v}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\sigma}-\chi_{4}(p)}\Big)=\sum_{p\leq v}f_{1}(p^{\sigma},\chi_{4}(p))\log p.

It follows from (

) that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq v}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\sigma}-\chi_{4}(p)}\Big)\ll\sum_{p\leq v}\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}-1}=\sum_{p\leq v}\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}(1-p^{-\sigma})}.

If
\sigma>1/3
, then the sum above is
\ll 1
. We may thus assume that
2/\log y\leq\sigma\leq 1/3
, in which case we note that it is shown in the proof of

, Lemma 7.5]
that
(1-p^{-\sigma})^{-1}\ll\log v/\log p
for
p<v
. We deduce from this and the above that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq v}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\sigma}-\chi_{4}(p)}\Big)\ll\sum_{p\leq v}\frac{\log v}{p^{\sigma}}\ll v\log v.

We deduce from this, (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that when
2/\log y\leq\sigma\leq 1+C/\log y
for any fixed constant
C>0
,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\phi_{1}(\sigma,G;y)=&\sum_{p\leq y}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{\sigma}-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\sigma}-\chi_{4}(p)}\Big)\\
=&\int^{y}_{1}\frac{dw}{w^{\sigma}}+O\Big(v\log v+y^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log y})\Big)+O(\sum_{v<p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\sigma}})\\
=&\int^{y}_{1}\frac{dw}{w^{\sigma}}+O\Big(v(\sigma)\log v(\sigma)+y^{1-\sigma}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log y})\Big)+O(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\sigma}}).\end{split}

Note that in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, it is shown that
\phi_{2}(\sigma,G;y)\geq 0
for
\sigma\geq 0
. This implies that
-\phi_{1}(\sigma,G;y)
is a decreasing function of
\sigma>0
. Thus, if
\alpha_{G}\geq 1+\frac{\delta_{1}}{\log y}
for some constant
\delta_{1}>0
, then we have by (
1.5
) and (
3.15
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}u\log y=&-\phi_{1}(\sigma_{G},G;y)\\
\leq&-\phi_{1}(1+\frac{\delta_{1}}{\log y},G;y)\\
\leq&\int^{y}_{1}\frac{dw}{w^{1+\frac{\delta_{1}}{\log y}}}+O\Big(v(1+\frac{\delta_{1}}{\log y})\log v(1+\frac{\delta_{1}}{\log y})+y^{-\frac{\delta_{1}}{\log y}}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log y})\Big)+O(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2(1+\frac{\delta_{1}}{\log y})}})\\
\leq&\frac{1-e^{-\delta_{1}}}{\delta_{1}}\log y+O(1),\end{split}

This leads to a contradiction for
1\leq u\leq 14
by taking
\delta_{1}
large enough. Thus, we must have
\alpha_{G}\leq 1+\frac{\delta_{1}}{\log y}
for some constant
\delta_{1}>0
. Similarly, we must have
\alpha_{G}\geq 1+\frac{\delta_{2}}{\log y}
for some constant
\delta_{2}>0
. This implies the validity of (
3.4
). An analogue argument also leads to the validity of (

It remains to establish (
3.6
). For this, we first note that it suffices to consider the case

and (
3.4

3.5

large enough. It follows from this that we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}1+x\leq e^{x},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{split}

we see that

. We thus derive that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}v(\alpha_{G})\log v(\alpha_{G})\ll\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}{(1-\alpha_{G})\log y}.\end{split}

We next deduce from (
3.8
) and the second estimation in (

(3.18)

\displaystyle u\log y=\sum_{p\leq y}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{\alpha_{G}}-1}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)\log p}{p^{\alpha_{G}}-\chi_{4}(p)}\Big)\geq 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}-1}\geq 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}}.

Note that we have

Thus when

, we deduce from the above, (

) and (

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}-1}{1-\alpha_{G}}\ll\int^{y}_{1}\frac{dw}{w^{\alpha_{G}}}+O\Big(v(\alpha_{G})\log v(\alpha_{G})+y^{1-\alpha_{G}}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log y})\Big)+O(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}})\ll u\log y.\end{split}

When

, we apply (

), (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}-1}{1-\alpha_{G}}\ll\int^{y}_{1}\frac{dw}{w^{\alpha_{G}}}+O\Big(v(\alpha_{G})\log v(\alpha_{G})+y^{1-\alpha_{G}}\exp(-b_{5}\sqrt{\log y})\Big)\ll u\log y+O(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}})\ll u\log y.\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}{1-\alpha_{G}}\ll\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}-1}{1-\alpha_{G}}\ll u\log y.\end{split}

The above implies that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}y^{-\alpha_{G}}\ll\frac{(1-\alpha_{G})u\log y}{y}.\end{split}

We next estimate
\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}}
by noting that when

, we have

(3.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}}\ll 1.\end{split}

While when

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}}\ll\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{1-2/\log y}}\ll\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p}\ll\log y\ll\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}{1-\alpha_{G}}\cdot\frac{1}{\log y}.\end{split}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}}\ll&\frac{y^{1-2\alpha_{G}}-1}{1-2\alpha_{G}}+O(1+y^{1-2\alpha_{G}}\exp(-b_{3}\sqrt{\log y}))\\
\ll&\frac{y^{1-2\alpha_{G}}}{1-2\alpha_{G}}+O(1+y^{1-2\alpha_{G}}\exp(-b_{3}\sqrt{\log y}))\\
\ll&\frac{y^{1-2\alpha_{G}}}{1-2\alpha_{G}}+1\\
\ll&\frac{y^{1-2\alpha_{G}}}{1-\alpha_{G}}+1\\
\ll&\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}{1-\alpha_{G}}\cdot\frac{u\log y}{y}+1,\end{split}

Putting together (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{2\alpha_{G}}}\ll&1+\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}{1-\alpha_{G}}\cdot\frac{u\log y}{y}+\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}{1-\alpha_{G}}\cdot\frac{1}{\log y}\\
\ll&\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}{1-\alpha_{G}}\cdot\Big(\frac{(1-\alpha_{G})}{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}+\frac{u\log y}{y}+\frac{1}{\log y}\Big)\\
\ll&\frac{y^{1-\alpha_{G}}}{1-\alpha_{G}}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\log y}+\frac{u\log y}{y}\Big),\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from (
3.16
).

We now set

to see from (
3.17
), (
3.19
) and (

) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{u}{u_{1}}-1=O\Big(\frac{1}{\log y}+\frac{u\log y}{y}\Big).\end{split}

The above implies that
u\asymp u_{1}
for
y
large enough. Moreover, it follows from the definition of

that we have
\xi(u_{1})=(1-\alpha_{G})\log y
. Note further that we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}1-\alpha_{G}-\frac{\xi(u)}{\log y}=\frac{\xi(u_{1})-\xi(u)}{\log y}\ll\frac{|u-u_{1}|}{u_{1}\log y}=O\Big(\frac{1}{(\log y)^{2}}+\frac{u}{y}\Big).\end{split}

This implies the validity of (
3.6
).

Lastly, the expression in (

2
, Lemma 1]
, which shows that for

\displaystyle\begin{split}\xi(u)=\log(u\log u)+O\Big(\frac{\log\log u}{\log u}\Big).\end{split}

This completes the proof of the proposition.

), one has for
y\geq 2,0<\beta,\varepsilon<1,t\in\mathbb{R},|t|\leq Y(\lambda),s=1-\beta+it
,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n\leq y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{s}}=&\frac{y^{\beta-it}}{\beta-it}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}(1+y^{\beta}\exp(-(\log y)^{\varepsilon/2}))\Big),\end{split}

where
\Lambda(n)
is the Von Mangoldt function defined to be
\Lambda(n)=\log p
if
n=p^{k}
for integers
k\geq 1
and
\Lambda(n)=0
otherwise.

Note that as shown in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we have the same zero-free region given in (
2.7
) holds for
L(s,\chi_{4})
. Thus, similar to
[
2
, Lemma 6]
, we see that under the same conditions as for the
\zeta(s)
case, we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n\leq y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}=&O\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}(1+y^{\beta}\exp(-(\log y)^{\varepsilon/2}))\Big).\end{split}

Using (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) allows us to see, in the same manner as the derivation of the Corollary to
[
2
, Lemma 6]
, that under the same condition as above, we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n\leq y}\frac{\Lambda(n)(1+\chi(n))}{n^{1-\beta}}(1-\cos(t\log n))=\frac{y^{\beta}}{\beta}(1-\beta\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}+t^{2}}})+O_{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}(1+y^{\beta}\exp(-(\log y)^{\varepsilon/2}))\Big),\end{split}

where
\eta=t\log y-\arctan(t/\beta)
.

Note moreover that by
[
2
, Lemma 5]
, we have uniformly for
x\geq y\geq 2
,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\log y}\sum_{\nu\geq 2}\sum_{p^{\nu}\leq y}\frac{\log p\chi(p^{\nu})}{p^{\nu\alpha_{G}}},\ \ \frac{1}{\log y}\sum_{\nu\geq 2}\sum_{p^{\nu}\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p^{\nu\alpha_{G}}}\ll 1+y^{1/2-\alpha_{G}}.\end{split}

Observe also that for
1/2+\varepsilon_{1}\leq\alpha_{G}\leq 1-4/\log y
and
y
large enough, we have
u\gg y^{1-\alpha_{G}}
by (
3.22
).
We now apply (
4.16
) with
\beta=1-\alpha_{G}
and (
4.17
) to proceed as in the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 8, (ii)]

to estimate the right-hand side expression in (
4.13
) to see that the estimation given in (
4.11
) is valid. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Our last result in this section provides an estimation for the difference of the
\Psi_{G}
function, which is an analogue to
[
2
, Lemma 9]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

With the notation as above. There exists a constant
d_{0}>0
such that uniformly for
x\geq y\geq 2,0<\varepsilon<1,1\leq z\leq Y(\lambda)
, we have

(4.18)

\displaystyle\Psi_{G}(x+x/z,y)-\Psi_{G}(x,y)\ll_{\varepsilon}x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)(\frac{1}{z}+e^{-d_{0}u}).

Proof.

One checks readily that the left-hand side expression above is

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}r(n)\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}(z\log(\frac{x}{n}))^{2}\Big).

We then proceed similar to the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 9]
upon using part (ii) of Lemma
4.3
and (
4.12
) to see that the estimation given in (
4.18
) is valid. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.5.
Completion of the proof

We may assume that
y
is large enough so that by (
1.8
) and (
3.7
) we have
1/2+\varepsilon_{1}\leq\alpha_{G}\leq 1-4/\log y
for some fixed constant
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. We set
T=Y(\frac{1}{4})
and apply Perron’s formula similar to that given in
[
6
, (4.5)]
to see that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}r(n)=&\frac{4}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha_{G}-iT}^{\alpha_{G}+iT}H(s,G;y)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}+O\Biggl(x^{\alpha_{G}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{r(n)}{n^{\alpha_{G}}}\min\biggl\{1,\frac{1}{T|\log(x/n)|}\biggl\}\Biggr)\\
=&\frac{4}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha_{G}-iT}^{\alpha_{G}+iT}H(s,G;y)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}+O\Biggl(\frac{x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)}{\sqrt{T}}+x^{\alpha_{G}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P(n)\leq y\\
|\log(x/n)|\leq T^{-1/2}\end{subarray}}\frac{r(n)}{n^{\alpha_{G}}}\Biggr).\end{split}

Note that we have for some constant
d_{1}>0
,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{\alpha_{G}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P(n)\leq y\\
|\log(x/n)|\leq T^{-1/2}\end{subarray}}\frac{r(n)}{n^{\alpha_{G}}}\ll\Psi_{G}(x+\frac{d_{1}x}{\sqrt{T}},y)-\Psi_{G}(x-\frac{d_{1}x}{\sqrt{T}},y)\ll_{\varepsilon}x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+e^{-d_{0}u}),\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from Lemma
4.4
and where
d_{0}>0
is the same constant given there.

We apply (
4.20
) in (
4.19
) to see that for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(4.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}r(n)=\frac{4}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha_{G}-iT}^{\alpha_{G}+iT}H(s,G;y)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}+O(x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+e^{-d_{0}u})).\end{split}

We now follow the treatment in the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 10]
to apply part (i) of Lemma
4.3
see that for some constant
b_{7}>0
,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\alpha_{G}+i/\log y}^{\alpha_{G}+iT}H(s,G;y)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}+\int_{\alpha_{G}-i/\log y}^{\alpha_{G}-iT}H(s,G;y)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}\ll x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)\exp(-b_{7}u(\log 2u)^{-2})\log T.\end{split}

We next note that similar to the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we have uniformly for
1\leq u\leq y/(8\log y),\alpha_{G}=\alpha_{G}(x,y)\geq 1/2+\varepsilon_{1}
that

(4.23)

\displaystyle\phi_{4}(\alpha_{G}+it,G;y)\ll u(\log y)^{4}.

We now proceed as in the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 11]
to see by (
4.23
), Lemma
4.2
that

(4.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha_{G}-i/\log y}^{\alpha_{G}+i/\log y}H(s,G;y)x^{s}\frac{ds}{s}=\frac{x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)}{\alpha_{G}\sqrt{2\pi\phi_{2}(\alpha_{G},G;y)}}(1+O(\frac{1}{u})).\end{split}

We gather (
4.21
), (
4.22
) and (
4.24
) to see that

(4.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
P(n)\leq y\end{subarray}}r(n)=&\frac{4x^{\alpha_{G}}H(\alpha_{G},G;y)}{\alpha_{G}\sqrt{2\pi\phi_{2}(\alpha_{G},G;y)}}\Big(1+O(E)\Big),\end{split}

where

(4.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E=&\frac{\alpha_{G}\sqrt{\phi_{2}(\alpha_{G},G;y)}}{\sqrt{T}}+\alpha_{G}\sqrt{\phi_{2}(\alpha_{G},G;y)}e^{-d_{0}u}+\alpha_{G}\sqrt{\phi_{2}(\alpha_{G},G;y)}\exp(-b_{7}u(\log 2u)^{-2})\log T+\frac{1}{u}.\end{split}

Note that we have
T=Y(\frac{1}{4})=\exp((\log y)^{5/4}),(\log\log 2y)^{2}\leq u\leq y/(8\log y),1/2+\varepsilon_{1}\leq\alpha_{G}\leq 1-4/\log y
. Also, we have by Lemma
4.2
that

\sqrt{\phi_{2}(\alpha_{G},G;y)}\ll u^{1/2}\log y
. We apply these estimations into (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) to see that the expression given in (
1.6
) is valid. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
