The force of attraction between nucleons due to vacuum fluctuation
Anupam Ghosh

Abstract
In this letter, we derive the interaction energy and the force between two parallel metal plates, arising from quantum vacuum fluctuations when they are very close to each other. We consider the vacuum to be composed of a meson field. In Quantum Hadrodynamics, mesons are the carriers of the nuclear force.



I
Introduction

According to the quantum field theory, space is not empty; it is filled with vacuum field fluctuations
9
;
10
. These fluctuations can lead to real macroscopic physical effects. One example is the Casimir-Polder force between two perfectly conducting plates. This force exerts a measurable pressure on the plates as a result of the vacuum fluctuations. The phenomenon was predicted by H. B. G. Casimir
3
;
2
in 1948 and later observed in experiments
8
. Over the years, various technological advancements have improved the precision of these measurements
4
;
5
;
7
;
6
.

The nucleus contains protons and neutrons, collectively known as nucleons. The nucleons are composed of quarks, and the quarks are bound together due to the strong interaction. Both the proton and the neutron have non-zero magnetic moments due to quark substructure
11
;
1
. Nucleons undergo quantum fluctuations due to the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle. A proton, for instance, can fluctuate into a neutron and a virtual pion,
p\longleftrightarrow n+\pi^{+}
. In this paper, we will derive the force between two perfectly conducting parallel plates separated by a very small distance due to vacuum fluctuations. Vacuum is considered to be composed of virtual meson fields- most notably the pion field. Unlike the electromagnetic Casimir effect, which involves massless photons, here the vacuum is composed of scalar pion fields. Consequently, the resulting interaction energy and the force between the plates depend on the pion’s Compton wavelength, the Planck constant, the speed of light, and exhibit a non-trivial dependence on the separation between the plates.

II
Force between metal plates due to vacuum fluctuations

Consider a perfectly conducting square plate of side
L
placed parallel to the
xy
plane in a cubic cavity of volume
L^{3}
with perfectly conducting walls. The plate is placed at a distance
a
from the
xy
face (
a\ll L
). We calculate the difference between the vacuum zero-point energy of this configuration and that of the configuration in which the plate is far away. Although the individual energy sums,
\sum\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega
, diverge, the difference between these two situations,

\delta E=\left(\sum\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega\right)_{I}-\left(\sum\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega\right)_{II}\,,

(1)

results in a finite and well-defined value, which is the physical interaction energy between the plate and the
xy
face. When the plate is close to the wall, the wave number
k_{z}
can only have discrete values, while they are far away,
k_{z}
contains all possible modes and can be approximated as a continuous variable. The cavity is defined by
0\leq x,y\leq L
, and
0\leq z\leq a
. The spatial part of the scalar field solution,
\phi(x,y,z)
is given by

\phi(x,y,z)\propto\sin\left(k_{x}x\right)\sin\left(k_{y}y\right)\sin\left(k_{z}z\right)\,,

(2)

satisfying the boundary condition
\phi=0
on all conducting walls. The components of the wave vector
\mathbf{k}
are given by

k_{x}=\frac{n_{x}\pi}{L},\quad k_{y}=\frac{n_{y}\pi}{L},\quad k_{z}=\frac{n_{z}\pi}{a}\,.

(3)

The magnitude of the wave vector
k
is expressed as

k=\sqrt{k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}}\,,

(4)

where
n_{x},n_{y},n_{z}\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}
. Unlike the electromagnetic case, the
n_{i}=0
modes do not exist as they lead to a null field solution.
For a massive relativistic pion field, the energy dispersion relation is

\omega=c\left[k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\right]^{1/2},\qquad n\geq 1,

(5)

where
m
denotes the pion mass. The momenta
k_{x}
and
k_{y}
may be considered continuous variables for large
L
. The zero-point energy is

\displaystyle\left(\sum\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega\right)_{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\hbar c\,\frac{L^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}}{a^{2}}+k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}+\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}}\;dk_{x}\,dk_{y}.

(6)

After we introduced polar coordinates in the
(k_{x},k_{y})
plane,

\left(\sum\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega\right)_{I}=\frac{1}{2}\hbar c\,\frac{L^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\,\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}}{a^{2}}+x^{2}+\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}}\;x\,dx\,.

(7)

For large
a
, the summation over
n
may be replaced with the integral. Therefore, the interaction energy is

\displaystyle\delta E

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar cL^{2}}{4\pi}\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}}{a^{2}}+x^{2}+\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}}\;x\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int x\,dx\,\sqrt{k_{z}^{2}+x^{2}+\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}}\;\left(\frac{a}{\pi}\,dk_{z}\right)\bigg].

(8)

Consider the variable

u=\frac{a^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\left(x^{2}+\frac{m^{2}c^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\right),

(9)

the interaction energy becomes

\displaystyle\delta E

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar cL^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{\pi^{3}}{2a^{3}}\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{u_{\min}}^{\infty}\sqrt{n^{2}+u}\,du

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\int_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{u_{\min}}^{\infty}\sqrt{n^{2}+u}\,\,du\,dn\bigg],

(10)

where

u_{\min}=\frac{a^{2}m^{2}c^{2}}{\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}.

(11)

We define

f(n)=\int_{u_{\min}}^{\infty}\sqrt{n^{2}+u}\,\,du\,.

(12)

The integral diverges. To obtain a finite result, we introduce an exponential regulator.

f_{\epsilon}(n)=\int_{u_{\min}}^{\infty}\sqrt{n^{2}+u}\,e^{-\epsilon(n^{2}+u)}\,du,\qquad\epsilon>0.

(13)

We take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
at the end of the calculation. The regularized energy functional
I(\epsilon)
becomes

I(\epsilon)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{\epsilon}(n)-\int_{1}^{\infty}f_{\epsilon}(n)\,dn~.

(14)

Extraction of the Finite Part.

Let
t=n^{2}+u
, which implies the limits shift from
[u_{\min},\infty]
to
[n^{2}+u_{\min},\infty]
. The integral becomes

f_{\epsilon}(n)=\int_{n^{2}+u_{\min}}^{\infty}t^{1/2}e^{-\epsilon t}\,dt\,.

(15)

To evaluate this, we use the definition of the
upper incomplete gamma function
,
\Gamma(s,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}z^{s-1}e^{-z}dz
. Setting
z=\epsilon t
, the integral becomes

f_{\epsilon}(n)=\epsilon^{-3/2}\int_{\epsilon(n^{2}+u_{\min})}^{\infty}z^{3/2-1}e^{-z}\,dz\,.

(16)

Thus, we have

f_{\epsilon}(n)=\epsilon^{-3/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2},\epsilon(n^{2}+u_{\min})\right)\,.

(17)

Using the series expansion
\Gamma(s,x)=\Gamma(s)-\frac{x^{s}}{s}+\mathcal{O}(x^{s+1})
, we expand
f_{\epsilon}(n)
for
\epsilon\to 0
,

f_{\epsilon}(n)\approx\frac{\Gamma(3/2)}{\epsilon^{3/2}}-\frac{2}{3}(n^{2}+u_{\min})^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\,.

(18)

The term
\Gamma(3/2)\epsilon^{-3/2}
is the ultraviolet (UV) divergence, which cancels in the difference when calculating
I(\epsilon)
. We retain the finite part of
f_{\epsilon}(n)
for
\epsilon\to 0
, which is

F(n)=-\frac{2}{3}(n^{2}+u_{\min})^{3/2}\,.

(19)

The sum in equation
14
starts at
n=1
. The
Euler–Maclaurin
formula gives the following expression,

I=\frac{1}{2}F(1)-\frac{1}{12}F^{\prime}(1)+\frac{1}{720}F^{\prime\prime\prime}(1)+\cdots

(20)

Differentiating
F(n)
with respect to
n
,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(n)

\displaystyle=-2n(n^{2}+u_{\min})^{1/2}\,,

(21)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(n)

\displaystyle=-2(n^{2}+u_{\min})^{1/2}-2n^{2}(n^{2}+u_{\min})^{-1/2}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime\prime}(n)

\displaystyle=-6n(n^{2}+u_{\min})^{-1/2}+2n^{3}(n^{2}+u_{\min})^{-3/2}\,.

(23)

Evaluating at
n=1
, we have

\displaystyle F(1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{3}(1+u_{\min})^{3/2}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(1)

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}\,,

(25)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime\prime}(1)

\displaystyle=-6(1+u_{\min})^{-1/2}+2(1+u_{\min})^{-3/2}\,.

(26)

Substituting into equation
20
, the integral becomes

I=-\frac{(1+u_{\min})^{3/2}}{3}+\frac{\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}{6}-\frac{1}{120\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}+\dots\,.

(27)

III
Final Result and Discussion

u_{\min}
can be written as the reduced pion’s Compton wavelength
\lambda_{c}=\frac{\hbar}{mc}
,

u_{\min}=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\lambda_{c}}\right)^{2}\,.

(28)

From equations
27
and
10
, we get the energy of the interaction,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta E}{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar c\,\pi^{2}}{8a^{3}}\bigg[\frac{(1+u_{\min})^{3/2}}{3}-\frac{\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}{6}+\frac{1}{120\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}+\dots\bigg]

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{\hbar c\,\pi^{2}}{960\,a^{3}}\frac{21+20\,u_{\min}(3+2\,u_{\min})}{\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}\,.

(29)

The negative interaction energy indicates the binding energy
.
The force per unit area between the metal plates with the vacuum in between can be calculated as follows,

\frac{F}{L^{2}}=-\frac{d}{da}\bigg(\frac{\delta E}{L^{2}}\bigg)\,.

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{F}{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar c\pi^{2}}{8a^{4}}\bigg[\frac{\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}{2}+\frac{u_{\min}}{6\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}+\frac{1}{40\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{u_{\min}}{(1+u_{\min})^{3/2}}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar c\pi^{2}}{960\,a^{4}}\bigg[\frac{63+80\,u_{\min}}{\sqrt{1+u_{\min}}}+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{u_{\min}}{(1+u_{\min})^{3/2}}\bigg)\bigg]\,.

(31)

The physical force between the plates is attractive.

IV
Conclusions

In this article, we derive the physical interaction energy and the force between two parallel, perfectly conducting plates with the vacuum in between. Here, the vacuum is considered to be composed of meson field. We find that the interaction energy is negative, which indicates the binding energy. The force between them is attractive. Both the energy and force increase rapidly as the distance between them decreases. The energy and force expressions depend on the Compton wavelength of the meson field, the Planck constant, the speed of light, and the distance between the plates. However, experimental confirmation of this effect could offer valuable insights.
