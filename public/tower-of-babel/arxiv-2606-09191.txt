Asymptotic Optimality of Thompson Sampling for Risk-Averse Bandits with Sub-Gaussian Rewards
Joel Q. L. Chang

Abstract
We prove that ρ ​ - ​ NPTS SG \rho\text{-}\mathrm{NPTS}_{\mathrm{SG}} , an anchor-free nonparametric Thompson Sampling algorithm for risk-averse bandits, achieves regret matching the instance-dependent lower bound to leading order in log ⁡ n \log n , establishing it as asymptotically optimal for any continuous risk functional ρ \rho (CVaR, mean-variance, Sharpe ratio, distortion risk measures, and more) on the class 𝒫 ( B , σ ) \mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)} of distributions with bounded density and sub-Gaussian tails, including Gaussian arms. Both this result and its bounded-support counterpart for ρ ​ - ​ NPTS \rho\text{-}\mathrm{NPTS} of 4 on 𝒫 ( B ) \mathcal{P}^{(B)} require only continuity of ρ \rho : strictly weaker than the dominance condition of prior parametric Thompson Sampling results, and strictly weaker than the Lipschitz condition of UCB-type algorithms, yielding the first instance-optimal guarantees for non-Lipschitz functionals such as the Sharpe ratio without parametric reward assumptions. The bounded-support case is developed first as a stepping stone sharing the same proof structure. The key technical contributions are a discretisation lemma (bounded support) and a truncated discretisation lemma (sub-Gaussian tails), each projecting the growing-alphabet Dirichlet posterior onto a fixed grid via the Dirichlet aggregation property, holding all polynomial prefactors at fixed degree independent of sample size and breaking the super-exponential barrier that blocked prior proofs.

Introduction

Sequential decision-making is rarely about expected reward alone.
In medical trials, a treatment that is reliably effective is preferred over one
that is spectacular but variable in online portfolio management, the Sharpe
ratio
\mathbb{E}[X]/\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(X)}
is the canonical measure of risk-adjusted return
in robotics and safety-critical control, catastrophic outcomes must be avoided
even when they are rare.
The multi-armed bandit (MAB) framework has been extended to these settings via
risk-averse objectives: mean-variance
(
14
;
9
)

(
2

)

(

)

4

\rho

They proved instance-optimal regret for a parametric companion algorithm

Sub-Gaussian Rewards: The Primary Result

We prove the primary result: instance-optimal regret for the class

\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}
of distributions on
\mathbb{R}
with bounded density and
sub-Gaussian tails, covering Gaussian and truncated-Gaussian arms, via a
truncated discretisation lemma.
The bounded-support results of Section

are the special case

\sigma=1/2

(see the Remark at the end of this section).

7.1
Distribution class and algorithm

Definition 3

(

)

Remark 5

(b)

N_{k}

9
Discussion

Implications.

Theorems
1
and
2
settle the theoretical status of

\rho\text{-}\mathrm{NPTS}_{\mathrm{SG}}
and
\rho\text{-}\mathrm{NPTS}
: they are not merely
empirically competitive heuristics but provably optimal algorithms.
For practitioners this is meaningful because both ask for less than

\rho\text{-}\mathrm{MTS}
: no pre-specified reward grid, no tuned alphabet size, just raw
observations fed into a Dirichlet.
The discretisation grids in our proofs are
analytical
devices, not
algorithmic ones the algorithms themselves are unchanged.
The anchor-free variant
\rho\text{-}\mathrm{NPTS}_{\mathrm{SG}}
additionally removes the

[0,1]
endpoint anchor, requiring only round-robin initialisation, and covers
the practically important class of Gaussian arms.

Several directions remain open.
Extensions to
contextual
risk-averse bandits are natural, but the
nonparametric posterior would couple across contexts, requiring new
concentration arguments.
Handling
heavy-tailed
rewards (outside
\mathcal{P}^{(B)}
) would require
replacing the bounded-density bin bound with a moment-based analogue.
Finally, the discretisation lemma itself may be of independent interest
wherever a growing-alphabet Dirichlet must be controlled at fixed resolution.

Computational complexity.

Both algorithms require
\mathcal{O}(N_{k})
work per round to draw a Dirichlet
sample and evaluate
\rho
, giving
\mathcal{O}(T^{2})
total cost.
This is unavoidable for any nonparametric posterior that maintains a full
empirical history, but can be reduced in practice: replacing the growing buffer
with a fixed-size reservoir of
R
samples (reservoir sampling) caps the cost at

\mathcal{O}(R)
per round at the price of approximating the posterior.
Proving optimality under reservoir sampling is an open question.

Conditions and complementarity with
\rho
-MTS.

The bounded-density assumption is used in exactly one place: the bin-probability
bound
\nu_{k}(I_{j})\leq B/M
(and its sub-Gaussian counterpart
\nu_{k}(I_{j})\leq Bh_{M}
).
It fails for distributions with point masses (Bernoulli, or any arm with a
non-zero probability atom), because a bin containing an atom may carry weight
exceeding
B/M
.

\rho\text{-}\mathrm{MTS}

4
handles purely discrete support under the
additional dominance condition, making the two algorithms complementary across
distribution classes.
The grid size
M
is a proof parameter, not algorithmic: it is determined by

\omega_{\rho}
and
\min_{k}\Delta_{k}^{\rho}
, and for
C^{1}
distortion functions
grows only logarithmically in
1/\Delta
.

10
Conclusion

We proved that
\rho\text{-}\mathrm{NPTS}_{\mathrm{SG}}
is asymptotically optimal for any
continuous risk functional
\rho
on the sub-Gaussian class
\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}

(Theorem
2
), and that its bounded-support counterpart
\rho\text{-}\mathrm{NPTS}

achieves the same on
\mathcal{P}^{(B)}
(Theorem
1
); both match the
instance-dependent lower bound exactly.
The key technical contributions are a discretisation lemma (bounded support)
and its truncated analogue (sub-Gaussian tails), each projecting the growing-alphabet
Dirichlet posterior onto a fixed grid and capping the polynomial prefactor at
degree
(M-1)/2
independent of sample size.

Natural directions for future work include extensions to contextual bandits
(where the nonparametric posterior couples across contexts), discrete reward
distributions (where the bin-probability argument breaks), and heavy-tailed
rewards beyond the sub-Gaussian regime.
The discretisation lemma itself may find use beyond bandits wherever a
growing-alphabet Dirichlet must be approximated at fixed resolution.

and pass to a subsequence
(still denoted

) with
\mathcal{G}_{\inf}^{\rho}(\nu,r^{\prime}_{n})\to L

(possible since
L
is a limit inferior).
For each

, pick

with

\mathrm{KL}(\nu,\eta_{n})\leq\mathcal{G}_{\inf}^{\rho}(\nu,r^{\prime}_{n})+\frac{1}{n}\leq\mathcal{G}_{\inf}^{\rho}(\nu,r)+\frac{1}{n},

\sup_{n}\mathrm{KL}(\nu,\eta_{n})<\infty

\mathcal{P}(C)
is compact in the

Hence there exists a subsequence

converging weakly to

\eta^{*}\in\mathcal{P}(C)

.
Since
\rho
is
D_{\infty}
-continuous and
D_{\mathrm{L}}\leq D_{\infty}
,
\rho
is also

D_{\mathrm{L}}
-continuous.
Therefore
\rho(\eta_{n_{k}})\to\rho(\eta^{*})
.
Since
\rho(\eta_{n_{k}})\leq r^{\prime}_{n_{k}}\to r
, we conclude
\rho(\eta^{*})\leq r
,
so
\eta^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{r}

Since

is lower semicontinuous
jointly in the weak topology, a standard consequence of the

\mathrm{KL}(\nu,\eta^{*})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{KL}(\nu,\eta_{n_{k}}).

Since
\mathcal{G}_{\inf}^{\rho}(\nu,r^{\prime}_{n})\to L
, every further subsequence also converges to

,
so
\liminf_{k}\mathcal{G}_{\inf}^{\rho}(\nu,r^{\prime}_{n_{k}})=L
.
Since
\eta^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{r}

Combining (UB) and (LB):

\lim_{r^{\prime}\searrow r}\mathcal{G}_{\inf}^{\rho}(\nu,r^{\prime})=\mathcal{G}_{\inf}^{\rho}(\nu,r)
.

Remark 8

(Local modulus)
.

Since
\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}
is not
D_{\infty}
-compact, the global uniform modulus
of
\rho
may be infinite.
We instead work with the local modulus

\omega_{\rho,k}(\delta):=\sup\bigl\{|\rho(\mu)-\rho(\eta)|:D_{\mathrm{L}}(\mu,\eta)\leq\delta,\ D_{\mathrm{L}}(\mu,\nu_{k})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr\},

defined using
D_{\mathrm{L}}
to match the LP-metric bound of Lemma
7
(ii).

We claim
\omega_{\rho,k}(\delta)\to 0
whenever
\rho
is
D_{\infty}
-continuous
on
\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}
.
The argument has two steps.
First, the density bound
f\leq B
gives a comparison between the two metrics: for
any
\mu,\eta\in\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}
, take a coupling
(X,Y)
with

\mathbb{P}(|X-Y|>\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon
where
\varepsilon=D_{\mathrm{L}}(\mu,\eta)
.
For every
x
,

\mathbb{P}(X\leq x,\,Y>x)\leq\mathbb{P}(x-\varepsilon<X\leq x)+\mathbb{P}(|X-Y|>\varepsilon)\leq B\varepsilon+\varepsilon,

and the symmetric bound gives
|F_{\mu}(x)-F_{\eta}(x)|\leq(B+1)\varepsilon
for all
x
, so

D_{\infty}(\mu,\eta)\leq(B+1)\,D_{\mathrm{L}}(\mu,\eta)\qquad\text{for all }\mu,\eta\in\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}.

(1)

Second, the local
D_{\mathrm{L}}
-neighbourhood
\{D_{\mathrm{L}}(\mu,\nu_{k})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\}\cap\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}

is therefore contained in
\{D_{\infty}(\mu,\nu_{k})\leq(B+1)/2\}\cap\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}
.
The CDFs of this latter set are uniformly bounded in
[0,1]
and equicontinuous
with Lipschitz constant
B
.
For tightness (required for Arzelà–Ascoli on
\mathbb{R}
): if
|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[X]|\to\infty

along some sequence in the set, then
\mu
and
\nu_{k}
become mutually singular, so

,
contradicting
D_{\mathrm{L}}(\mu,\nu_{k})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Hence the means
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[X]
are uniformly bounded over the neighbourhood, and with
sub-Gaussian tails the set is uniformly tight, so Arzelà–Ascoli applies and the
set is precompact in
D_{\infty}
.
On any precompact set a
D_{\infty}
-continuous function is uniformly continuous,
and by (
1
) uniform
D_{\infty}
-continuity implies uniform

D_{\mathrm{L}}
-continuity, so
\omega_{\rho,k}(\delta)\to 0
.

This covers all risk functionals in Section
4
.
In particular it applies to the Sharpe ratio, which has no finite global Lipschitz
constant but is
D_{\infty}
-continuous on
\mathcal{P}^{(B,\sigma)}
because the
density bound forces

bounded away from zero; see Remark
3
.
