Abstract
We develop a theoretical framework for describing superposed coherent states in graphene quantum systems using the concept of catability as a phase-sensitive metric functional measure. In this case, the formalism quantifies interference stability and coherence structure via phase-dependent contributions of quantum superposition states. Catability is defined as a functional measure sensitive to relative phase variations within coherent state combinations, serving as a diagnostic tool for quantum interference effects in graphene-based systems. Also, the formulation is extended using Lie algebra techniques, where the underlying symmetry structure of graphene quantum states is represented through operator algebras governing state transformations in quantum space. In this context, to describe nonlocal propagation and phase-resolved dynamics, a Green function approach is incorporated, enabling systematic treatment of quantum correlations in a spatially extended structures framework. A unified framework is constructed by combining Lie algebraic symmetry analysis with Green function propagation theory, yielding a consistent description of phase-sensitive catability in complex graphene quantum configurations within the framework approach. Results provide a structured route for testing coherence, interference stability, and quantum state control in low-dimensional quantum materials systems. Keywords : Graphene quantum systems; Coherent states; Catability; Lie algebra; Green functions; Quantum interference.

Catability as a Metric for Evaluating Superposed Coherent States

In this part, the analysis in

A measurable quantity termed catability is introduced, enabling the detection of cat-state signatures without full quantum state tomography while preserving operational accessibility and experimental feasibility. The motivation is associated with limitations of conventional characterization tools, such as fidelity, which require complete reconstruction of the quantum state and do not provide a transparent interpretation in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. The present formulation employs nonlinear squeezing concepts, extending standard quadrature squeezing criteria to general operators. Also, this extension permits a direct evaluation of non-Gaussian states through fluctuation-based observables accessible in measurement schemes

(1)

where
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
denote bosonic annihilation and creation operators, while
\hat{\Pi}
represents the parity operator defined as
The quadratic term quantifies the phase-space separation of coherent components, whereas the parity-dependent contribution encodes interference between the superposed branches. In this case, the parameter

From this construction, catability is defined as

\xi^{(\pm)}(\alpha)=\min_{\gamma}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{O}^{(\pm)}(\alpha,\gamma)\hat{\rho}]}{\min_{\hat{\rho}_{G}}\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{O}^{(\pm)}(\alpha,\gamma)\hat{\rho}_{G}]},

(2)

The phase dynamics of such superpositions are determined by magnetic flux, strain-induced gauge potentials, and electrostatic gating, producing phase-sensitive interference effects. Conventional formulations of catability that omit directional phase structure in mode space do not capture these contributions. To address this limitation, a phase-resolved catability operator is formulated, incorporating coherence orientation within Hilbert space. The Green function representation is used to express observables in terms of propagators, providing direct access to interference and decoherence processes.

G^{<}
:

\langle\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{m_{0}}\rangle=\int d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,\psi_{m_{0}}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{m_{0}}^{*}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\,G^{<}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r};t).

(53)

Two-particle correlations are obtained from the four-point Green function
G^{(2)}
:

\langle\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger 2}\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{2}\rangle=\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}\cdots d\mathbf{r}_{4}\,\psi_{m_{0}}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\psi_{m_{0}}(\mathbf{r}_{2})\psi_{m_{0}}^{*}(\mathbf{r}_{3})\psi_{m_{0}}^{*}(\mathbf{r}_{4})G^{(2)}.

(54)

The parity structure in the rotated representation is written as

\langle\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}\rangle=\sum_{n}P_{n}e^{in\phi}(-1)^{n},

(55)

with
P_{n}
denoting the occupation probability of number states.

The expectation value of the operator is given by

\displaystyle\langle\hat{Q}_{\phi}^{(\pm)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger 2}\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{2}\rangle-\alpha^{2}e^{2i\phi}\langle\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger 2}\rangle-\alpha^{*2}e^{-2i\phi}\langle\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{2}\rangle+|\alpha|^{4}

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\left(1\mp\langle\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}\rangle\right).

(56)

For a coherent state
|\alpha\rangle
, the Green function factorizes, yielding

\langle\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{2}\rangle=\alpha^{2},\qquad\langle\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger 2}\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{2}\rangle=2|\alpha|^{4}.

(57)

The parity expectation takes the closed form

\langle\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}\rangle=\exp\!\left[-2|\alpha|^{2}\sin^{2}\!\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)\right]\exp\!\left[i|\alpha|^{2}\sin\phi\right],

(58)

which shows explicit dependence of coherence on the phase parameter.

The phase is fixed through magnetic flux via
\phi=2\pi\Phi/\Phi_{0}
, with
\Phi_{0}=h/e
. The resulting catability functional is written as

\xi_{\phi}^{(\pm)}\sim\frac{\langle\hat{Q}_{\phi}^{(\pm)}\rangle}{|\alpha|^{4}},

(59)

which provides a normalized measure of phase-controlled superposition strength.

This formulation unifies orbital dynamics, valley coherence, and sublattice structure within a Green-function framework. Environmental contributions enter through self-energy terms in
G^{<}
and
G^{(2)}
, accounting for phonon scattering, disorder, and interaction effects. Experimental access is achieved through tunneling spectroscopy and current noise correlations, both of which probe the same Green functions, establishing a direct correspondence between theoretical catability measures and measurable response functions.

V
Phase-Sensitive Catability in Graphene Quantum Systems via Green’s Functions and Lie Algebra Structure

Graphene quantum rings form a relativistic condensed-matter configuration in which low-energy excitations obey an effective Dirac Hamiltonian. The ring confinement imposes quantization of orbital channels, whereas valley and sublattice degrees of freedom extend the internal Hilbert space into a multi-component algebraic structure. Within this framework, coherence phenomena are not restricted to superposed wave amplitudes but are reformulated in operator algebra language, where phase-sensitive catability emerges as a structural consequence of non-compact Lie representations coupled to Green’s-function evolution.

The analysis begins from an effective single-channel truncation where one dominant orbital mode
m_{0}
is selected. The fermionic operators
\hat{c}_{m_{0}}
and
\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger}
obey canonical anticommutation relations

\{\hat{c}_{m_{0}},\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger}\}=1,\qquad\{\hat{c}_{m_{0}},\hat{c}_{m_{0}}\}=0.

(60)

Even though the microscopic structure is fermionic, the emergent coherent sector is generated by quadratic combinations that close under commutation into a bosonic non-compact algebra. Introducing

\hat{J}_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger 2},\qquad\hat{J}_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{2},\qquad\hat{J}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{c}_{m_{0}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{m_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

(61)

the Lie algebra structure follows from direct operator manipulation.

The commutator
[\hat{J}_{0},\hat{J}_{+}]
is evaluated by explicit expansion of operator products,

[\hat{J}_{0},\hat{J}_{+}]=\frac{1}{4}\left[\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}+\frac{1}{2},\hat{c}^{\dagger 2}\right]=\frac{1}{4}\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}[\hat{c},\hat{c}^{\dagger 2}]+[\hat{c}^{\dagger},\hat{c}^{\dagger 2}]\hat{c}\right).

(62)

Using the canonical identity

[\hat{c},\hat{c}^{\dagger 2}]=\hat{c}\hat{c}^{\dagger 2}-\hat{c}^{\dagger 2}\hat{c}=2\hat{c}^{\dagger},

(63)

together with
[\hat{c}^{\dagger},\hat{c}^{\dagger 2}]=0
, one obtains

[\hat{J}_{0},\hat{J}_{+}]=\frac{1}{4}(2\hat{c}^{\dagger 2})=\hat{J}_{+}.

(64)

An analogous derivation yields

[\hat{J}_{0},\hat{J}_{-}]=-\hat{J}_{-},\qquad[\hat{J}_{+},\hat{J}_{-}]=2\hat{J}_{0}.

(65)

These relations define closure of the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
Lie algebra without central extension. The closure guarantees reduction of higher commutators to polynomial expressions in the generators, providing an invariant algebraic framework for the dynamics.

The Casimir operator is introduced as

\hat{C}=\hat{J}_{0}^{2}-\hat{J}_{0}-\hat{J}_{+}\hat{J}_{-},

(66)

which commutes with all generators. Verification proceeds through repeated use of the commutation relations,

[\hat{C},\hat{J}_{0}]=0,\qquad[\hat{C},\hat{J}_{\pm}]=0,

(67)

ensuring algebraic invariance. Acting on a lowest-weight state
|k,0\rangle
, one finds

\hat{C}|k,0\rangle=k(k-1)|k,0\rangle,

(68)

so that
C=k(k-1)
characterizes the representation class governing coherence stability.

Phase dependence is introduced through the unitary operator generated by
\hat{J}_{0}
,

\hat{U}(\phi)=e^{i\phi\hat{J}_{0}}.

(69)

The adjoint transformation of
\hat{J}_{\pm}
is computed via the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series,

\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\phi)\hat{J}_{\pm}\hat{U}(\phi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\phi)^{n}}{n!}\mathrm{ad}^{n}_{\hat{J}_{0}}(\hat{J}_{\pm}),

(70)

with
\mathrm{ad}_{\hat{J}_{0}}(\cdot)=[\hat{J}_{0},\cdot]
. Since

\mathrm{ad}_{\hat{J}_{0}}^{n}(\hat{J}_{\pm})=(\pm 1)^{n}\hat{J}_{\pm},

(71)

the resummation yields

\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\phi)\hat{J}_{\pm}\hat{U}(\phi)=e^{\pm i\phi}\hat{J}_{\pm}.

(72)

This exponential structure establishes
\phi
as a coordinate on the
\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
manifold, with magnetic flux entering through
\phi=2\pi\Phi/\Phi_{0}
.

The operator describing phase-sensitive catability is defined as a displaced quadratic form in the lowering sector,

\hat{O}_{\phi}=4\left(\hat{J}_{-}-\alpha^{2}e^{2i\phi}\right)^{\dagger}\left(\hat{J}_{-}-\alpha^{2}e^{2i\phi}\right)+\gamma(1-\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}).

(73)

Expansion gives

\hat{O}_{\phi}=4\hat{J}_{+}\hat{J}_{-}-4\alpha^{2}e^{2i\phi}\hat{J}_{+}-4\alpha^{*2}e^{-2i\phi}\hat{J}_{-}+4|\alpha|^{4}+\gamma(1-\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}).

(74)

Using the Casimir relation

\hat{J}_{+}\hat{J}_{-}=\hat{J}_{0}^{2}-\hat{J}_{0}-\hat{C},

(75)

one obtains

\hat{O}_{\phi}=4(\hat{J}_{0}^{2}-\hat{J}_{0}-\hat{C})-4\alpha^{2}e^{2i\phi}\hat{J}_{+}-4\alpha^{*2}e^{-2i\phi}\hat{J}_{-}+4|\alpha|^{4}+\gamma(1-\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}).

(76)

This decomposition separates the invariant Casimir sector from coherent ladder dynamics, yielding an exact algebraic partition of fluctuations.

The microscopic formulation is expressed through Green’s functions. The lesser Green’s function is defined as

G^{<}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime};t)=i\langle\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t)\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{r},t)\rangle.

(77)

Expanding the field operator,

\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{m}\psi_{m}(\mathbf{r})\hat{c}_{m},

(78)

and projecting onto
m_{0}
yields

\langle\hat{J}_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\psi_{m_{0}}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{m_{0}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})G^{<}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}).

(79)

For the pairing channel,

\langle\hat{J}_{-}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2}}\psi_{m_{0}}^{*}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\psi_{m_{0}}^{*}(\mathbf{r}_{2})G^{(2)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2}).

(80)

In the non-interacting limit, Wick decomposition gives

G^{(2)}(1,2)=G^{<}(1,1)G^{<}(2,2)+G^{<}(1,2)G^{<}(2,1).

(81)

For a coherent state
|\alpha\rangle
satisfying
\hat{c}_{m_{0}}|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle
, one obtains

\langle\hat{J}_{-}\rangle=\alpha^{2},\qquad\langle\hat{J}_{+}\hat{J}_{-}\rangle=2|\alpha|^{4},\qquad\langle\hat{J}_{0}\rangle=|\alpha|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}.

(82)

Normal ordering gives

\hat{J}_{+}\hat{J}_{-}=\frac{1}{4}\hat{c}^{\dagger 2}\hat{c}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}(\hat{n}^{2}-\hat{n}),

(83)

with
\langle\hat{n}^{2}\rangle=|\alpha|^{4}+|\alpha|^{2}
.

The rotated parity operator is defined as

\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}=e^{i\phi\hat{J}_{0}}(-1)^{\hat{n}}e^{-i\phi\hat{J}_{0}}.

(84)

Using the coherent expansion,

|\alpha\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle,

(85)

one obtains

\langle(-1)^{\hat{n}}\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-|\alpha|^{2})^{n}}{n!}=e^{-2|\alpha|^{2}}.

(86)

After phase rotation,

\langle\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}\rangle=\sum_{n,m}e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\frac{\alpha^{n}(\alpha^{*})^{m}}{\sqrt{n!m!}}(-1)^{n}e^{i\phi(n-m)/2}.

(87)

Resummation yields

\langle\hat{\Pi}_{\phi}^{(G)}\rangle=\exp\!\left[-2|\alpha|^{2}\sin^{2}\!\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)\right]\exp\!\left[i|\alpha|^{2}\sin\phi\right].

(88)

Expectation values of
\hat{O}_{\phi}
follow from substitution. One obtains

\langle\hat{J}_{0}^{2}\rangle=|\alpha|^{4}+\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}+\frac{1}{16},

(89)

hence

\langle\hat{J}_{0}^{2}-\hat{J}_{0}\rangle=|\alpha|^{4}-\frac{1}{4}|\alpha|^{2}-\frac{3}{16}.

(90)

The resulting expression is

\langle\hat{O}_{\phi}\rangle=8|\alpha|^{4}(1-\cos 2\phi)+\gamma\left[1-e^{-2|\alpha|^{2}\sin^{2}(\phi/2)}\cos(|\alpha|^{2}\sin\phi)\right].

(91)

The first contribution arises from Lie algebra interference, while the second corresponds to parity-modulated coherence suppression.

In the limit
|\alpha|\gg 1
, one obtains

\langle\hat{O}_{\phi}\rangle\simeq 8|\alpha|^{4}(1-\cos 2\phi),

(92)

which depends only on the
\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
geometric structure.

The Green’s-function self-energy correction modifies propagation as

G^{-1}(\omega,\mathbf{k})\rightarrow G^{-1}(\omega,\mathbf{k})-\Sigma(\omega,\mathbf{k}),

(93)

leading to deformation of the algebra through

[\hat{J}_{+},\hat{J}_{-}]=2\hat{J}_{0}+\delta\hat{\Sigma},

(94)

which breaks exact Casimir invariance and introduces decay channels for interference. Phase-sensitive catability is therefore governed by persistence of Lie invariants under self-energy corrections, linking transport kernels with algebraic stability in graphene quantum systems.

VI
Conclusions

The concept of catability establishes a systematic and operational framework for the quantification of macroscopic quantum coherence in superposed coherent states, specifically Schrödinger cat states, without invoking full quantum state tomography. Within this formulation, the central strategy consists of replacing the complete reconstruction of the density matrix, which becomes infeasible in high-dimensional Hilbert spaces, with fluctuation-based observables accessible in experiments and constructed from nonlinear squeezing principles. This construction generalizes standard quadrature squeezing to more involved operator structures, enabling access to non-Gaussian signatures through second-order measurable correlations together with parity-sensitive terms. The operator formulation introduced in
AA
defines a positive semidefinite observable whose lowest eigenstate coincides with an ideal cat state, establishing a correspondence between the spectral properties of the operator and the emergence of macroscopic quantum superposition. The quadratic bosonic contributions quantify phase-space separation between coherent components, whereas parity-dependent terms encode interference structures associated with coherent superposition, rendering the formalism sensitive to amplitude separation and quantum coherence on equal footing. The parameter
\gamma
governs the relative weight between these contributions, providing a tunable mechanism to access distinct regimes of coherence detection. On this basis, catability is defined through an optimization over Gaussian reference states, which ensures a consistent normalization separating non-Gaussian quantum features from classical or Gaussian fluctuations. In this formulation, the resulting quantity functions as a certified witness of Schrödinger-cat-like coherence, where the limit
\xi=0
corresponds to an ideal cat state, intermediate values correspond to partial non-Gaussian coherence, and values larger than unity correspond to loss of sensitivity within the criterion. The approach remains experimentally viable since the required expectation values of operators decompose into moments of the number operator and displaced number statistics, which reduces the measurement overhead compared to full tomography.

The stability analysis under photon-loss decoherence confirms that catability preserves sensitivity to non-Gaussian correlations even in regimes where conventional phase-space diagnostics, such as Wigner negativity, vanish due to dissipative effects. This feature establishes a distinction from fidelity-based measures, which typically degrade rapidly under moderate decoherence, whereas catability retains access to residual interference structures. In practical implementations based on approximate cat states generated from squeezed Fock states, the measure remains robust under realistic noise, supporting its applicability in experimental platforms. Extending this construction to condensed matter systems, in particular graphene-based quantum structures, introduces additional complexity due to Dirac quasiparticles and internal degrees of freedom such as valley and sublattice indices. In confined graphene geometries, coherent superpositions may form across orbital or valley sectors, leading to condensed-matter realizations of cat-like states. In contrast to optical systems, these configurations exhibit intrinsic phase dependence governed by external parameters including magnetic flux, strain-induced gauge fields, and electrostatic potentials. To incorporate this structure, phase-dependent mode operators are introduced, leading to a generalized catability operator containing an explicit phase parameter
\phi
. This phase enters both the coherent displacement structure and the parity operator via a unitary rotation in occupation number space, making the witness responsive to interference modulations controlled by external fields. In graphene quantum rings, the phase
\phi
is directly related to magnetic flux through
\phi=2\pi\Phi/\Phi_{0}
, which establishes a direct link between topological electromagnetic control and coherence diagnostics. Consequently, catability acquires tunability through external parameters, enabling optimization of sensitivity to orbital and valley interference. Expectation values of second-order operators such as
\langle\hat{c}*{m_{0}}^{\dagger 2}\hat{c}*{m_{0}}^{2}\rangle
encode pair correlations, while parity oscillations weighted by occupation probabilities introduce phase-modulated coherence signatures. This structure provides a unified description of cat-like behavior in Dirac materials, where interference between valley states such as
|K\rangle\pm e^{i\theta}|K^{\prime}\rangle
can be quantified within a single framework. Accordingly, phase-dependent catability constitutes a consistent and experimentally relevant tool for characterizing nonclassical coherence in graphene systems, linking operator-based quantum information measures with externally tunable condensed-matter parameters.

Phase-sensitive catability in graphene quantum systems emerges when the low-energy confined dynamics of Dirac quasiparticles are restricted to a single dominant angular momentum channel, producing an exact bosonic representation constructed from quadratic combinations of a single effective mode

Graphene quantum rings constitute a relativistic two-dimensional condensed-matter system where low-energy quasiparticles are governed by an effective Dirac-type Hamiltonian, while confinement on a closed geometry imposes discrete orbital quantization and introduces valley and sublattice degrees of freedom, extending the internal state space into a multi-component algebraic structure framework description included here. In this framework, coherence is not restricted to linear superposition of wave amplitudes but is reformulated through operator algebras whose dynamical closure defines the accessible quantum sectors within algebraic space. The microscopic origin of these expectation values is described using non-equilibrium Green’s functions, where the lesser propagator encodes single-particle correlations and higher-order functions determine pairing contributions in the structure sector. Projection onto the dominant orbital mode connects propagators to Lie generators, establishing a correspondence between transport kernels and algebraic expectation values within the operator representation space framework. In this context, phase-sensitive catability in graphene quantum systems is determined by competition between Lie-algebraic invariants and Green’s-function-induced self-energy corrections, establishing a unified description where algebraic geometry, quantum transport, and coherence stability are linked to framework consistency closure.
