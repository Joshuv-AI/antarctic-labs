Nonequilibrium energetics of sensing and actuation by a smart active particle
Luca Cocconi, Benoît Mahault, Lorenzo Piro

Abstract
Smart active agents must allocate finite energetic resources across distinct functions, yet the underlying thermodynamic trade-offs remain poorly understood. Here, we introduce a minimal model of a self-steering particle with an internal polarity-cue sensor coupled to an external environmental field, decomposing its steady-state entropy production rate into locomotion, actuation, and sensing costs. This separation exposes an energetic bookkeeping structure underlying even the simplest form of embodied navigation. The emergence of Pareto fronts linking energetic expenditure to localisation precision and path-following performance shows that feedback-controlled active motion is constrained by quantitative thermodynamic bounds that persist across distinct task geometries.

Introduction

Many of the most promising applications of active matter, from targeted drug delivery by functionalised colloids
[
1
,
2
]
to swarm nanorobotics
[
3
,
4
]
, rely on the successful integration of self-actuation with the distributed recording, processing, and utilisation of information at the level of individual agents
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Biology offers a wealth of paradigmatic examples in which evolution has woven these functionalities together into remarkably robust behaviours. Classic cases include bacterial chemotaxis
[
8
,
9
]
and quorum sensing
[
10
,
11
]
, where motility and signalling combine either to rectify otherwise unbiased random motion along chemical gradients or to trigger changes in gene expression once a critical population density is exceeded.

Embedding similarly sophisticated information-processing capabilities into synthetic active agents presents a formidable engineering challenge
[
1
]
. This difficulty arises not only from the demands of miniaturisation
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
, but also from the increasingly stringent physical constraints encountered as the characteristic energy scales of actuation, sensing, and thermal fluctuations approach one another
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
(whereas at larger scales the former typically dominates). Under the additional and natural assumption of a limited energetic budget
[
19
]
, it is therefore compelling to ask what generic trade-offs underlie such complex behaviours and, having identified them, how finite thermodynamic resources should be allocated to optimise specific performance metrics
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

Addressing these questions requires a consistent nonequilibrium energetic framework in which all relevant features of the physical model are treated on equal footing. In this respect,
ad hoc
multilevel descriptions, such as those invoking autonomous control via machine-learning modules external to the dynamics
[
23
,
24
]
, may be dynamically informative, but are often thermodynamically ill-posed. The development of analytically tractable models that coherently integrate autonomous actuation, sensing, and decision-making is thus a crucial step towards elucidating the physics of “smart” active matter.

To advance this programme, we introduce a minimal model of a self-steering motile agent equipped with an internal polarity-cue sensor coupled to an external environmental field, such as a concentration or light-intensity gradient (Sec.
2
).
Using tools from stochastic thermodynamics
[
25
]
, we decompose the mean rate of energy dissipation into distinct contributions associated with self-propulsion, steering, and sensing. Having determined how these components partition the overall energetic budget across different parameter regimes (Sec.
3
),
we comment on how the contribution associated with sensing can be related to the rate at which information is collected by the sensor itself (Sec.
4
).
As concrete illustrations, we examine the resulting dissipation–accuracy trade-offs in three prototypical tasks: localisation at a point target, localisation within a target disc, and navigation along a prescribed path (Sec.
5
).

2
Self-steering active particles with explicit sensory feedback

We consider an agent moving in two dimensions at constant self-propulsion speed
w
.
Its state is specified by the center-of-mass position
\bm{r}(t)
and by a heading direction
\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}(t)=(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t))
, with
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
. Circumflexes will henceforth indicate unit vectors.
The agent is additionally equipped with an internal state
\hat{\bm{u}}_{\varphi}(t)
, corresponding to the readout of a polarity cue sensor. This sensor is coupled to a prescribed, stationary vector field
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}(\bm{r})=(\cos\theta^{*}(\bm{r}),\sin\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))
, which encodes the locally preferred direction of motion in space and we thus refer to it as the
steering policy
(see Fig.
1
for a schematic illustration).
The dynamics of the position, heading angle, and sensor readout are governed by the following set of overdamped Langevin equations:

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{r}}(t)

\displaystyle=w\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}(t)+\sqrt{2D_{r}}\bm{\eta}_{r}(t)

(1a)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=\kappa\Gamma_{\theta}(\theta,\varphi)+\sqrt{2D_{\theta}}\eta_{\theta}(t)

(1b)

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=\mu\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi,\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))+\sqrt{2D_{\varphi}}\eta_{\varphi}(t)\,,

(1c)

where the
\eta_{i}
(
i\in\{x,y,\theta,\varphi\}
) are independent and identically distributed Gaussian white noises of unit covariance, while
D_{r}
,
D_{\theta}
, and
D_{\varphi}
denote the one translational and two angular diffusion coefficients, respectively.

The drift term
\kappa\Gamma_{\theta}
represents an internally generated torque that steers the heading angle
\theta
toward the sensor readout
\varphi
, thereby quantifying the strength of actuation.
Conversely, the term
\mu\Gamma_{\varphi}
drives the sensor variable toward alignment with the local policy direction
\theta^{*}(\bm{r})
, with
\mu
setting the sensing rate.
The microscopic origin of this alignment may be equilibrium or nonequilibrium in nature; here, it is treated at the level of effective dynamics.
For concreteness, we assume dipolar alignment interactions and set
\Gamma_{\theta}(s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime})=\Gamma_{\varphi}(s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime})=-\sin(s^{\prime}-s^{\prime\prime})
.
In the absence of steering (
\kappa=0
), the positional dynamics of our model reduces to those of an active Brownian particle (ABP) in two dimensions, a paradigmatic model of active matter
[
26
]
. Related models without an explicit sensing degree of freedom were analysed in Refs.
[
20
]
and
[
27
]
.

(green vector) aligns stochastically with a position-dependent steering policy
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}({\bm{r}})

\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}(r)
(red vector field in the background) points toward a fixed target position denoted by the yellow star. At selected points along the trajectory, the three unit vectors shown in panel (a) — the heading direction
\hat{\bm{u}}(t)
, the sensor readout
\hat{\bm{u}}_{\varphi}(t)
, and the steering policy
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}({\bm{r}})
— are displayed. The trajectory was obtained from numerical integration of the Langevin equations with parameters
\tilde{\mu}=50
,
\tilde{\kappa}=1
,
\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}=0.01
, and
{\rm Pe}=10
.

Activity enters our model through three distinct, independent mechanisms. First, persistent self-propulsion breaks the equilibrium fluctuation-dissipation relation
[
28
]
, representing the canonical form of activity in active matter. This contribution vanishes in the limit
w=0
.
Second, the nonreciprocal coupling between heading angle
\theta
and sensor readout
\varphi
—where the heading “chases” the sensor but not vice versa—violates microscopic reversibility, with reciprocity restored only in the decoupled limit
\kappa=0
. Third, and more subtly
[
29
]
, nonequilibrium persists even for
w=\kappa=0
: the unidirectional link from position
\bm{r}
to sensor dynamics via
\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi,\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))
couples spatial exploration to internal sensory reorientation, precluding detailed balance.

In the following, we rescale space and time as
\bm{r}\to(w/D_{\theta})\bm{r}
and
t\to t/D_{\theta}
, and introduce the dimensionless Péclet number
{\rm Pe}\equiv w^{2}/(D_{r}D_{\theta})
, alignment strengths
\tilde{\kappa}\equiv\kappa/D_{\theta}
and
\tilde{\mu}\equiv\mu/D_{\theta}
, and sensor accuracy
\sigma^{2}_{\varphi}=D_{\varphi}/(D_{\theta}\tilde{\mu})
. Assuming that a statistical steady state for the process exists, the stationary probability density
P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)
satisfies the Fokker-Planck equation

0=\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{J}_{r}+\partial_{\theta}J_{\theta}+\partial_{\varphi}J_{\varphi}

(2)

with the (generically non-vanishing) probability currents

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{r}(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=[\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}-{\rm Pe}^{-1}\bm{\nabla}]P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)

(3a)

\displaystyle J_{\theta}(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=[-\tilde{\kappa}\sin(\theta-\varphi)-\partial_{\theta}]P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)

(3b)

\displaystyle J_{\varphi}(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mu}[-\sin(\varphi-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))-\sigma^{2}_{\varphi}\partial_{\varphi}]P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)~.

(3c)

3
Thermodynamic costs of locomotion, actuation, and sensing

We equip the dynamics with a thermodynamic interpretation by computing the mean rate of entropy production at steady state
[
30
,
25
]
. This is proportional to the mean stochastic work done by the generalized active forces, and can be written in our rescaled units as

\dot{\Sigma}=\int{\rm d}\bm{r}{\rm d}\theta{\rm d}\varphi\ \left[{\rm Pe}\bm{J}_{r}\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}-\tilde{\kappa}\sin(\theta-\varphi)J_{\theta}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}\sin(\varphi-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))J_{\varphi}\right]~.

(4)

The first term in the integrand represents the dissipation from self-propulsion and evaluates exactly to
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm prop}={\rm Pe}
, independent of the steering policy or model parameters. The remaining terms, however, depend non-trivially on the stationary density
P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})
; we derive their approximate forms below under specific conditions.

For clarity, we introduce the shorthand

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\kappa}\int{\rm d}\bm{r}{\rm d}\theta{\rm d}\varphi\ \sin(\theta-\varphi)J_{\theta}~,

(5a)

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}\int{\rm d}\bm{r}{\rm d}\theta{\rm d}\varphi\ \sin(\varphi-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))J_{\varphi}~,

(5b)

where
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act}
captures the thermodynamic cost of actuation/feedback control, while
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}
quantifies the cost of sensing. Before proceeding, we note that by multiplying both sides of Eq. (
2
) by
\cos(\varphi-\theta^{*})
and integrating over all degrees of freedom, one obtains the following alternative form for
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}
:

\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}\left[\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}\cdot\nabla\cos(\varphi-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))\rangle+{\rm Pe}^{-1}\langle\nabla^{2}\cos(\varphi-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))\rangle\right]~,

(6)

where angular brackets
\langle\cdot\rangle
henceforth denote averages with respect to
P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})
.

3.1
Fast-sensing limit and effective dynamics

To derive analytical expressions for the stationary density
P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})
—and thus explicit formulas for the entropy production contributions
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act}
and
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}
—we establish a hierarchy of timescales and apply a multiscale approach
[
31
]
as follows.
In particular, we consider the regime where sensing happens on fast timescales,
which amounts to taking the limit

\tilde{\mu}\to\infty
while keeping the sensor precision
\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}
and all other parameters constant. As a result, we have from the Fokker-Planck equation (
2
) that, to leading order in small
\tilde{\mu}^{-1}
, the joint probability factorises

P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})=P_{\rm eff}(\bm{r},\theta;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})P_{\varphi}(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})+\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\mu}^{-1})

(7)

with

P_{\varphi}(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})=\frac{1}{2\pi I_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}e^{\cos(\varphi-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))/\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}

(8)

the Boltzmann weight obtained by solving
J_{\varphi}=0
at constant
\bm{r}
. Here,
I_{n}
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order
n
.
This marginal also holds, independently of the fast-sensing assumption, for “pinned” problems (e.g., with external potentials halting motion) or spatially uniform policies
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}
. From Eq. (
7
), the key trigonometric moments relative to the policy read

\displaystyle\langle\cos(\varphi-\theta^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})

(9a)

\displaystyle\langle\sin(\varphi-\theta^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=0

(9b)

\displaystyle\langle\cos^{2}(\varphi-\theta^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=1-\langle\sin^{2}(\varphi-\theta^{*})\rangle=1-\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})

(9c)

where we defined
c_{0}(s)=I_{1}(s)/I_{0}(s)
with
0\leq c_{0}(s)<1
. In the limit of fast sensing, the effective dynamics of the heading direction may be written as

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\eta_{\theta}(t)-\int{\rm d}\varphi P_{\varphi}(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})\tilde{\kappa}\sin(\theta-\varphi)

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\kappa}c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})\sin(\theta-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))+\sqrt{2}\eta_{\theta}(t)~,

(10)

mirroring the original Eq. (
1b
) but with renormalised alignment
\tilde{\kappa}\to\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}\equiv\tilde{\kappa}c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})
, with
\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}\leq\tilde{\kappa}
, and direct coupling from policy
\theta^{*}(\bm{r})
to heading
\theta
.

Having established the relaxation of the probability density of the sensor readout as the fastest timescale, we now proceed to introduce an approximation scheme based on a gradient expansion of
P_{\rm eff}(\bm{r},\theta)
in Eq.(
7
).
This enables closed-form evaluation of the steady-state moments in Eq. (
4
) whenever spatial variations in
P_{\rm eff}
remain smooth, effectively imposing a second adiabatic approximation: angular equilibration in
\theta
outpaces positional dynamics in
\bm{r}
. To leading order in
\tilde{\mu}^{-1}
and in gradients, the marginal positional density
\rho(\bm{r})\equiv\int{\rm d}\theta P_{\rm eff}(\bm{r},\theta)
obeys the effective stationary Fokker-Planck equation

\nabla\cdot\left[\left(\langle\bm{u}_{\theta}\rangle_{\theta}-{\rm Pe}^{-1}\bm{\nabla}\right)\rho({\bm{r}})\right]=0

(11)

with the position-dependent average heading direction

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}\rangle_{\theta}

\displaystyle=c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}-\left[c_{1}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\bm{I}+c_{2}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\right]\cdot\nabla\ln\rho

\displaystyle+c_{3}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})+c_{4}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp}(\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp})+{\cal O}(\nabla^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{-1})~,

(12)

where
\bm{I}
is the identity matrix, while
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
encompasses gradients of the density, policy, or flow, and the coefficients
c_{1,2,3,4}
are detailed in
A
.
Inserting (
12
) into (
11
) shows that the combination of sensing and actuation induces: (i) an effective drift along the local policy
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}(\bm{r})
with a renormalised speed
c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})<1
in units of the bare self-propulsion speed; (ii) an anisotropic renormalisation of the diffusion tensor, with enhanced fluctuations along the axis defined by
\theta^{*}(\bm{r})
(
c_{2}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})>0
).
From Eq. (
12
), we additionally note that

\langle\cos(\theta-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))\rangle=\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\rangle=c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})+c_{3}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\langle\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\rangle+O(\nabla^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{-1})~.

(13)

Lastly, the moment expansion yields the approximate identity

\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}^{2}\langle\sin^{2}(\theta-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))\rangle-\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}\langle\cos(\theta-\theta^{*}(\bm{r}))\rangle=-c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\langle\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\rangle+O(\nabla^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{-1})~,

(14)

derived in
A
.

3.2
Approximate analytical forms for actuation and sensing costs

Combining Eqs. (
3b
) and (
5
), by adding and subtracting
\theta^{*}
in the argument of the trigonometric functions, we can write the contribution to the entropy production associated with actuation as

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\kappa}^{2}\langle\sin^{2}[(\theta-\theta^{*})-(\varphi-\theta^{*})]\rangle-\tilde{\kappa}\langle\cos[(\theta-\theta^{*})-(\varphi-\theta^{*})])\rangle

\displaystyle=\tilde{\kappa}^{2}\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})+\tilde{\kappa}^{2}[1-2\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})]\langle\sin^{2}(\theta-\theta^{*})\rangle-\tilde{\kappa}c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})\langle\cos(\theta-\theta^{*})\rangle

(15)

where we have used Eq. (
9
) to evaluate the trigonometric moments with argument
(\varphi-\theta^{*})
, which factorise based on Eq. (
7
). Substituting Eqs. (
13
) and (
14
) into this expression allows us to get rid of the remaining trigonometric moments, leading to

\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act}=\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act,pinned}+\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act,\nabla}+\mathcal{O}(\nabla^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{-1})

(16)

where we have split this contribution into two parts

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act,pinned}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}^{2}\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}{c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}+\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}\left(\frac{1-2\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}{c_{0}^{2}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}-1\right)c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})

(17a)

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act,\nabla}

\displaystyle=-c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\left[1-\left(1-\frac{\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}c_{3}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})}{c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})}\right)\left(\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}-\frac{2\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}{c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}-1\right)\right]\langle\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\rangle

(17b)

of order zero and one in
\nabla
, respectively.
As the notation suggests, the contribution
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act,pinned}
remains finite in the “pinned” problem (
\dot{\bm{r}}=0
), as well as when the policy is trivial, i.e., uniform
\theta^{*}({\bm{r}})={\rm const}
. The existence of such a contribution indicates that, in the limit of fast sensing, a constant thermodynamic cost is incurred by the actuation mechanism as a result of its continuous attempt to enforce alignment of the heading angle to a fluctuating sensor readout, irrespective of the details of the steering policy
[
32
]
.
On the other hand, the contribution
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act,\nabla}\propto-\langle\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\rangle
, which vanishes for homogeneous policies, formalizes the intuition that policies designed to localise the swimmer in the proximity of a target subspace inevitably incur a thermodynamic cost since density accumulates in regions of negative policy divergence (in the parameter regime of interest, the sign of the policy-independent prefactor is positive). Note that evaluating (
5
) only requires knowledge of the marginal density
\rho(\bm{r})
, rather than the full joint density
P({\bm{r}},\theta,\varphi)
, cf. Eq. (
5
).

For sensing, the factorisation (
7
), combined with Eq. (
6
) and (
9
), yields the exact (to all orders in
\nabla
) fast-sensing form

\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}=\frac{c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}{\rm Pe}}\langle|\nabla\theta^{*}(\bm{r})|^{2}\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\mu}^{-1})\ .

(18)

Though formally higher order in gradients than our
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm act}
truncation, its convergence properties—explored below for localisation at a point target—reveal subtleties in the positional density
\rho
. Once again, the evaluation of (
18
) only requires knowledge of the marginal
\rho(\bm{r})
.

4
Thermodynamic cost of sensing as an information flow

Given that
\varphi
represents an internal polarity sensor coupled to the steering policy
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}
, we anticipate a link between its associated entropy production,
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}
, and the rate of information acquisition by the sensor. To quantify this energetics-informatics connection, we evaluate the steady-state multivariate information flow
\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}
into
\varphi
from the remaining degrees of freedom
[
33
,
34
]
:

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{r}d\theta d\varphi\ \ln\frac{P(\varphi)}{P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)}\partial_{\varphi}J_{\varphi}~.

(19)

This is a signed quantity that vanishes at equilibrium and quantifies whether the rest of the system is gaining control over/sending information to (
\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}>0
) or losing control over/receiving information from (
\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}<0
) the variable
\varphi
.
Analogous expressions exist for the information flows into
\bm{r}
and
\theta
. Together, these sum to the time derivative of the multivariate mutual information,
\sum_{s\in\{\bm{r},\theta,\varphi\}}\dot{I}_{\to s}=\dot{I}

[
34
]
, defined here according to the convention
[
35
]

\displaystyle I[P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)]

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{r}d\theta d\varphi\ P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)\ln\frac{P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)}{\rho(\bm{r})P(\theta)P(\varphi)}

\displaystyle={\rm KL}[P(\bm{r},\theta,\varphi)||\rho(\bm{r})P(\theta)P(\varphi)]\,,

(20)

where KL denotes the Kullback-Leibler divergence
[
36
]
, taken between the joint distribution and the product of its marginals.
In the fast sensing limit, using Eq. (
7
),

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{r}d\theta d\varphi\ \left[\ln\frac{P(\varphi)}{P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})}+\ln\frac{1}{P_{\rm eff}(\bm{r},\theta;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})}\right]\partial_{\varphi}J_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{r}d\theta d\varphi\ \ln\frac{P(\varphi)}{P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})}[-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{J}_{r}-\partial_{\theta}J_{\theta}]~,

(21)

where we have used Eq. (
2
) and the fact that
P_{\rm eff}
is independent of
\varphi
.

Upon further manipulation of Eq. (
21
), we find

\displaystyle\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\int d\bm{r}d\varphi\ \ln\frac{P(\varphi)}{P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})}\bm{\nabla}\cdot\left(\int d\theta\ \bm{J}_{r}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\mu}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rm Pe}\int d\bm{r}d\varphi\ \ln\frac{P(\varphi)}{P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})}\rho(\bm{r})\nabla^{2}P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})+\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\mu}^{-1})\,,

(22)

leveraging
\theta
-independence of
P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})
and symmetries in its Boltzmann form (
8
).
Also, through Eq. (
8
) we get

\nabla^{2}P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})=\frac{P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}\left[\frac{\sin^{2}(\varphi-\theta^{*})}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}-\cos(\varphi-\theta^{*})\right]|\nabla\theta^{*}(\bm{r})|^{2}\,,

(23)

and so overall

\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}=\frac{\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{2})}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}{\rm Pe}}\left\langle|\nabla\theta^{*}(\bm{r})|^{2}\right\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\mu}^{-1})\,,

(24)

where

\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{2})\equiv\int d\varphi\ P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})\ln\frac{P(\varphi)}{P(\varphi;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})}\times\left[\frac{\sin^{2}(\varphi-\theta^{*})}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}-\cos(\varphi-\theta^{*})\right]=c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})

(25)

is actually policy-independent (via a change of variable
\varphi\to\varphi^{\prime}+\theta^{*}
) and thus factors from the average.
Comparing with Eq. (
18
), we thus find that the cost of sensing and the stationary information flow into the sensor are exactly equal,
\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}=\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}
, for all values of
\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}
in the fast sensing limit.
The bound
\eta=\dot{I}_{\to\varphi}/\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens}\leq 1
on the efficiency of information flow derived in Ref.
[
34
]
is thus saturated in this system.

5
Unified thermodynamic constraints across navigation tasks

5.1
Localisation at a point target

We first apply our framework to the paradigmatic case of a stationary point target, defined by the steering policy
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}(\bm{r})=-(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{\rm target})/|\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{\rm target}|
designed to localise the agent in the proximity of
\bm{r}_{\rm target}
.
This serves as a minimal model for chemo- or phototaxis, with
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}
representing the normalised gradient of a chemoattractant concentration
[
8
,
9
]
or light intensity
[
37
,
38
]
, respectively.
Translational invariance lets us set
\bm{r}_{\rm target}=\bm{0}
without loss of generality, yielding
|\nabla\theta^{*}|=-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}=r^{-1}
where
r\equiv|\bm{r}|
denotes the radial displacement.

Substituting this policy into the effective Fokker-Planck equation (
11
) and enforcing zero steady-state radial current gives

\frac{{\rm d}\ln\rho(\bm{r})}{{\rm d}r}=-\frac{c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})+r^{-1}c_{3}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})}{{\rm Pe}^{-1}+c_{1}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})+c_{2}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})}~.

(26)

The radial displacement is thus Gamma-distributed

\rho(r)\equiv\int{\rm d}\bm{r}\delta(r-|\bm{r}|)\rho(\bm{r})=\frac{r^{1+\eta}e^{-r/\ell}}{\ell^{2+\eta}\Gamma(2+\eta)}

(27)

\ell=\frac{{\rm Pe}^{-1}+c_{1}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})+c_{2}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})}{c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})},\quad\quad\eta=\frac{c_{3}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})}{{\rm Pe}^{-1}+c_{1}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})+c_{2}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})}~.

All steady-state observables entering the entropy production can be evaluated explicitly from the density profile (

5.2
Regularised point target: localisation within a disc

We modulate the sensory alignment term,
\tilde{\mu}\sin(\varphi-\theta^{*})
in Eq. (
3c
), with a position-dependent factor
\gamma(r)
that vanishes as the particle enters the target.
Physically, such modulation could model an extended domain near
\bm{r}_{\rm{target}}
with safe or nutrient-rich conditions, such that sensing and navigation are no more necessary.
In what follows, we use the hard cutoff
\gamma(r)=\Theta(r-R)
, where
\Theta
is the Heaviside function.

The stationary density is obtained by solving the marginal Fokker-Planck equation (
26
) with a space-dependent effective alignment strength
\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}(r)=\tilde{\kappa}c_{0}(\sigma^{-2}_{\varphi}(r))
which vanishes for
r<R
. This is because the dimensionless sensory error
\sigma^{2}_{\varphi}(r)=D_{\varphi}/[D_{\theta}\tilde{\mu}\gamma(r)]
now diverges for
r<R

c_{0}(0)=0

Solving Eq. (
26

continuity at

\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\eta}e^{-(r-R)/\ell}&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{cases}~,

(32)

where
\Gamma\left(\alpha,x\right)
is the upper incomplete Gamma function.
Accordingly, the stationary probability for the particle to be inside the target disc is

\pi_{\rm in}=\frac{1}{1+2e^{R/\ell}E_{-1-\eta}(R/\ell)}~,

(33)

where
E_{n}(z)=\int_{1}^{\infty}dte^{-zt}t^{-n}

As detailed in
B

18

\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens,reg}=\frac{c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}{\rm Pe}}\;\frac{2e^{R/\ell}}{R^{2}}\;\frac{\left(\frac{\ell}{R}\right)^{\eta}\Gamma\left(\eta,\frac{R}{\ell}\right)}{1+2e^{R/\ell}\left(\frac{\ell}{R}\right)^{2+\eta}\Gamma\left(2+\eta,\frac{R}{\ell}\right)}~,

(34)

R
asymptotes to

\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens,reg}\underset{R\to 0}{\simeq}\frac{c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}{\rm Pe}}\,\frac{\Gamma(\eta)}{\ell^{2}\Gamma(2+\eta)}\left[1-\frac{1}{\eta\,\Gamma(\eta)}\left(\frac{R}{\ell}\right)^{\eta}\right]~.

(35)

It is clear that taking
R\to 0
in Eq. (
35
) recovers the result of the unregularised case.
Using Eq. (
32
), we directly evaluate the variance of the particle displacement from the target center:

\sigma_{r,\rm reg}^{2}=\frac{R^{2}\left(1+4e^{R/\ell}E_{-3-\eta}(R/\ell)\right)}{2+4e^{R/\ell}E_{-1-\eta}(R/\ell)}~,

(36)

which likewise converges to its unregularised form, Eq. (
29

R\to 0
.

To complete the thermodynamic characterisation, we derive the regularised actuation contributions to the total entropy production.
The pinned component, cf. Eq. (
17a

\pi_{\rm in}

(37)

since

The

5.3
Navigation along a target path

We finally consider an agent tasked with maintaining itself near a prescribed 1D target path while navigating along the latter in a preferred direction.
This scenario proves particularly relevant for stochastic optimal navigation, where energy- or time-minimising strategies often reduce to trajectory tracking
[
39
,
23

For tractability, we assume that the path’s typical radius of curvature is large relative to other characteristic length scales of the motion, permitting a straight-line approximation. We further require the policy

{\rm sgn}[\cos\theta^{*}(y)]=-{\rm sgn}(y)

satisfying the assumptions specified above, by

\rho(y;\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})=\rho_{0}\exp\left[\int^{y}dy^{\prime}\frac{c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\cos\theta^{*}(y^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}[c_{3}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})-c_{4}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})]\partial_{y^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\theta^{*}(y^{\prime})}{{\rm Pe}^{-1}+c_{1}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})+c_{2}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\cos^{2}\theta^{*}(y^{\prime})}\right]

), (

) and (

) using (

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\equiv\left\langle\hat{\bm{e}}_{\parallel}\cdot\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}\rangle_{\theta}\right\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\nabla^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{-1})

6
Discussion and conclusion

We introduced a minimal model of a self-steering active particle in which motility, sensing, and actuation are treated on equal thermodynamic footing (Sec.
2
). By resolving the internal sensor dynamics explicitly and embedding them within a stochastic feedback loop, we were able to decompose the steady-state entropy production into physically interpretable contributions associated with locomotion, control, and sensing (Sec.
3
). This separation exposes a concrete energetic bookkeeping structure underlying even the simplest form of embodied navigation. By establishing a quantitative bridge between dissipation and multivariate information flow in this spatially extended agent, we also demonstrate that the energetic cost of sensing can be interpreted as a measurable price of information acquisition (Sec.
4
).

Within a controlled hierarchy of timescales, the fast-sensing limit yields an effective drift–diffusion description in which the steering policy acts as a geometric field shaping both transport and fluctuations. This coarse-grained structure allows analytical access to steady-state densities and leads to explicit dissipation–accuracy trade-offs for representative navigation tasks (Sec.
5
). The emergence of Pareto fronts linking energetic expenditure to localisation precision and path-following performance shows that feedback-controlled active motion is constrained by quantitative thermodynamic bounds. Importantly, the persistence of these trade-offs across distinct task geometries indicates that they are not artefacts of a specific policy, but rather reflect generic features of sensorimotor coupling in smart active matter.

Several natural extensions follow. Allowing the steering policy itself to adapt or be learned
[
23
,
24
,
41
]
would introduce additional thermodynamic costs associated with policy acquisition, placing adaptation within the same accounting structure as sensing and actuation. Likewise, taking into account the energetic price of policy negotiation across agents opens a direct route to multi-agent scenarios, where communication
[
42
,
43
]
and coordination might generate new trade-offs between individual expenditure and collective performance. Such extensions are particularly relevant for the design of autonomous microswimmers
[
1
,
2
]
and swarm robotic systems
[
3
,
4
]
, where sensing, decision-making, and motion must coexist under tight energetic budgets.

Taken together, our results advance a minimal yet analytically tractable framework for the nonequilibrium energetics of smart active matter
[
44
]
. By unifying propulsion, sensing, and feedback control within a single stochastic thermodynamic description, we provide a step toward a quantitative theory in which navigation and decision-making are constrained, organised, and ultimately limited by energy dissipation.

Moment expansion

The approximation scheme introduced in Sec.

20

(42)

where
Q_{i}={\cal O}(\nabla^{i})

\langle\cdot\rangle_{k}\equiv\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rmd\theta\,(\cdot)Q_{k}

as a measure.
Indeed, having integrated out the fast sensory dynamics, the joint probability density
P_{\rm eff}(\bm{r},\theta,t)
in Eq. (
7

\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}=\tilde{\kappa}c_{0}(\sigma^{-2}_{\varphi})

[
20
]

\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}^{2}\langle\sin^{2}(\theta-\theta^{*})\rangle-\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff}\langle\cos(\theta-\theta^{*})\rangle=-c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\langle\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\rangle+O(\nabla^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{-1})~.

(43)

Note that the combination of trigonometric moments in the left-hand side of the above expression had a specific physical interpretation in Ref.
[
20
]
(as a distinct contribution to the total entropy production), which is not the case here.

Going beyond the results of Ref.
[
20
]

\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}\rangle_{\theta}
appearing in Eq. (
11

(44)

\displaystyle{\bm{F}}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=Q_{0}(\theta-\theta^{*})\left[\left(\cos(\theta-\theta^{*})-c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa})\right)\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}+\sin(\theta-\theta^{*})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp}\right],

\displaystyle{\bm{F}}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=-Q_{0}^{\prime}(\theta-\theta^{*})\left[\bm{v}+\cos(\theta-\theta^{*})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}+\sin(\theta-\theta^{*})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp}\right]-Q_{0}(\theta-\theta^{*})c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp}.

\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}\rangle_{1}=\left[\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\bm{c}_{\rho}+\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp}\bm{s}_{\rho}\right]\cdot\nabla\ln\rho_{\rm s}+\left[\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\bm{c}_{\theta}+\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp}\bm{s}_{\theta}\right]\cdot\nabla\theta^{*},

(46)

where

Calculating the averages, we find that the
\bm{c}
and
\bm{s}
coefficients take the forms

\displaystyle\bm{c}_{\rho}=c_{\rho}\hat{\bm{u}}^{*},\qquad\bm{c}_{\theta}=c_{\theta}\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp},\qquad\bm{s}_{\rho}=s_{\rho}\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp},\qquad\bm{s}_{\theta}=s_{\theta}^{v}\bm{v}+s_{\theta}^{*}\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}.

(47)

\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}\rangle_{0}=c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}
and to first order in gradient, we thus find that the average orientation moment takes the general form

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\theta}\rangle_{\theta}

\displaystyle=c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}-\left[c_{1}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\bm{I}+c_{2}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\right]\cdot\nabla\ln\rho-\tilde{\kappa}c_{1}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})(\bm{v}\cdot\nabla)\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}

\displaystyle+c_{3}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}(\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*})+c_{4}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp}(\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp})+{\cal O}(\nabla^{2},\tilde{\mu}^{-1})~,

(48)

where
\bm{I}
denotes the identity matrix and
\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}_{\perp}=\hat{\bm{u}}\left(\theta^{*}+\frac{1}{2}\pi\right)
. The coefficients

cannot be expressed in terms of simple functions

\tilde{\kappa}

For example, up to

Derivation of the dissipation for the regularized target

Here, we derive the expression of the dissipation associated with sensing in the care of the extended target.
We thus consider the Langevin dynamics (
1
) with
\tilde{\mu}\to\gamma(r)\tilde{\mu}
, where
\gamma(r)=\Theta(r-R)
is a step function
\gamma(r)=\Theta(r-R)
over a disc of radius
R
centered at the target.

A suitably modified version of Eq. (
6

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{\rm sens,reg}=\frac{c_{0}(\sigma_{\varphi}^{-2})}{\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}}\Big[

\displaystyle c_{0}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\langle\gamma^{\prime}(r)\rangle+c_{3}(\tilde{\kappa}_{\rm eff})\langle\gamma^{\prime}(r)\nabla\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}^{*}\rangle

(49)
