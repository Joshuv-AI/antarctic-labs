1 LIFT Community of Practice Overview
As the renewable energy sector grows, there is an opportunity to expand access to and shape the
economic, workforce, environmental, and other potential benefits and impacts of renewable
energy development for nearby or affected communities. However, few initiatives exist to
actively bridge the many organizations and groups working in different regions and on different
technologies related to this topic. Without an active network to purposely facilitate these
connections, useful lessons and insights gained by individual groups, including good practices
and processes, remain siloed, and opportunities for collaborating to generate benefits for more
people and places are missed.

This gap is evident in growing renewable energy sectors like wind energy. To address this gap,
the National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL), with the support of the U.S. Department of
Energy (DOE), launched LIFT: A Community of Practice to UpLIFT Participation in Wind
Energy in 2024. This initiative brings together wind energy researchers, practitioners,
stakeholders, agency representatives, Native-led renewable energy initiatives, and other groups
across the United States, to exchange best practices for public participation in wind energy
development, share emerging research, and collaboratively develop innovative approaches for
improving the outcomes of renewable energy projects.

The name of the Community of Practice (CoP), “LIFT,” is inspired by the aerodynamic lift force
that spins a wind turbine’s rotor by overcoming drag through the pressure difference between the
front and back of the turbine blades, thereby producing energy. The overall goal of the LIFT CoP
is to “uplift” participation in decision-making related to the benefits and impacts of renewable
energy development, starting with wind and then expanding to include other renewable energy
sources.

The LIFT CoP convenings and interactive activities in 2024 focused on expanding benefits from
wind energy development. Through interactions in 2024, the LIFT CoP:

 • Identified the multifaceted challenges facing practitioners, agencies, local governments,
 Tribes, and organizers seeking to expand wind energy’s potential benefits.
 • Shared and learned about efforts underway to expand benefits and build capacity through
 both community-led projects (bottom up) and government agency-led efforts (top down).
 • Generated components of a preliminary framework for assessing how community goals
 are met through efforts to expand benefits.
 • Identified next steps for LIFT, including expanding focus to include public participation
 in all renewable energy project technologies across the design, planning, operational, and
 end-of-service phases.
This paper highlights key insights and lessons learned for each of the points above and was
written for a cross-sector audience of practitioners, researchers, agencies, and Tribal
organizations pursuing effective strategies for shaping the impacts of renewable energy projects.

2 Design Framework and Approach
Our first step as a CoP was to develop a framework to better understand the benefits and impacts
of wind energy and renewable energy development to guide the CoP’s effort. This framework
includes five components:

 1. Fair Processes for Stakeholder Participation: ensuring all stakeholders have meaningful
 opportunities to participate in decision-making processes.
 2. Sharing Benefits and Impacts: promoting transparent, fair distribution of both the benefits
 and the burdens of renewable energy development among all affected stakeholders and
 communities.
 3. Learning From and Avoiding Past Mistakes: drawing on lessons from previous projects
 to anticipate, mitigate, and prevent repeating historical harms or exclusions.
 4. Honoring Histories and Agency: recognizing the histories, lived experiences, and
 continued agency of all stakeholders, including Indigenous peoples, in shaping the
 development and governance of renewable energy projects.
 5. Upholding Tribal Sovereignty and Building Authentic Partnerships: recognizing the
 sovereign status of Tribal nations and respecting their rights to self-determination in
 renewable energy development. This includes honoring government-to-government
 relationships; adhering to free, prior, and informed consent principles; integrating Tribal
 knowledge systems and cultural values; supporting Tribal energy self-sufficiency; and
 ensuring Tribal-led participation in the design, development, ownership, and benefit
 structures of renewable energy projects.
With this initial framework, we then convened the LIFT Steering Committee consisting of 9–15
members with broad experience, perspectives, and expertise in wind and other renewable energy
projects. Recognizing the sovereign status and unique interests of Tribal nations, the Steering
Committee includes reserved seats for Tribal government representatives and/or Native-led
organizations to ensure Tribal leadership directly shapes the CoP’s direction, priorities, and
activities. The Steering Committee supported the development of our CoP’s charter, which
describes the above framework, steering committee responsibilities, and the design of full CoP
virtual and in-person convenings.

LIFT’s 2024 convenings were designed to amplify conversations, share knowledge, and
collaboratively identify challenges and opportunities related to expanding wind energy’s
benefits. Both virtual and in-person workshops aimed to provide meaningful learning
opportunities and foster connections between participants. These workshops offered space for
sharing, listening, and collaboration through activities like breakout sessions, lightning talks, and
other participatory methods.

The full LIFT CoP convened three times in 2024. These facilitated and interactive gatherings
generated the following insights:

Inaugural LIFT Virtual Gathering – June 27, 2024. The inaugural LIFT convening brought
together 66 participants from state and local governments, national laboratories, federal agencies,
industry, national organizations, Tribal-serving organizations, and community-based

 2

organizations to introduce the LIFT CoP and explore how stakeholder participation shows up in
participants’ wind energy work. Discussions focused on the role of benefit mechanisms that
provide benefits for communities impacted by wind projects.

LIFT In-Person Gathering, Atlantic City, New Jersey – October 30, 2024. This in-person
LIFT gathering convened 12 participants representing state and local decision makers, industry,
and community-based organizations. Facilitated discussion and interactive activities focused on
challenges and opportunities for connecting stakeholders to wind energy benefits and on methods
for evaluating the impacts of participants’ work.

LIFT Virtual Gathering – December 10, 2024. This virtual convening hosted 28 participants
from research institutions, national laboratories, federal agencies, industry, national
organizations, community-based organizations, and state and local governments in a reflective
discussion on how communities are benefiting from land-based wind development. Participants
shared examples of early successes, persistent challenges, and strategies to address barriers while
also considering what meaningful benefits for host communities look like in practice.

3.1 What Can Be Done To Expand Wind Energy Benefits
Wind energy development can add value to residents, students, workers, Tribal nations, local
governments, nonprofits, and others in communities where energy projects and infrastructure are
located and beyond. These potential beneficial impacts include tax revenues, workforce
development, land lease payments, and manufacturing and supply chain enhancements. A
significant amount of attention in recent years has been paid to benefit mechanisms, which are
mechanisms used by developers to voluntarily provide additional benefits to communities; these
can include direct payments to local governments or community benefit funds providing
investments into community priorities, such as public infrastructure, parks and sports facilities,
education, or more reliable energy. However, there can be a negative perspective on these
benefits, as they may be seen as a form of bribery or a way to placate critical perspectives;
therefore, public participation in decisions about benefits is critically important. Given the
potential risks and rewards as wind energy grows across the United States, the LIFT CoP focused
on identifying opportunities for expanding access to wind energy benefits.

3.1.1 Support Communities Facing the Prospect of New Energy Development
One critical need identified by the CoP is providing support for people confronting possible
changes and challenges resulting from a new energy development project. Energy development
can create new fears and uncertainties, conflict with cultural understandings and preferences,
change the landscape, introduce intracommunity conflicts, and raise concerns about what is best
for the community. In these contexts, there may also be confusion about who local and Tribal
officials and other leaders can trust for reliable and up-to-date information. Communities and
organizations facing the challenges associated with new energy development may also be facing
other types of challenges simultaneously, with varying capacities for responding. So, while new
energy development may offer a range of benefits, it is essential to remember that it also bears
risks for host communities. To effectively work with community and Tribal
leaders/representatives to bridge needs with potential benefits and build capacity to engage,
project developers must acknowledge and work to understand these risks.

 We need to find ways to help communities deal with their fears, concerns, grief,
 and loss that come with new energy infrastructure—especially when unknown.
 —Amy Eshoo, Director, Maine Climate Action, December 2024 LIFT convening
 participant

3.1.2 Expand Access to Wind Benefits to a Broader Array of Beneficiaries
While large-scale energy development such as wind can provide benefits, securing those benefits
for those who most need them is far from automatic. LIFT participants described how optimizing
the benefits of energy development often hinges on active engagement and sustained interaction
between the community leaders seeking benefits and the industry and government decision
makers leading new development projects—and not all host communities have the same access
to the resources and organizational capacity to foster that engagement. Participants described
how wind energy projects may reveal disparities between more and less affluent communities in

 4

terms of accessing benefits. For instance, communities with the capacity to organize and contest
developer plans may be rewarded by robust community benefits, whereas communities with
fewer resources to engage may receive smaller benefits, if any. This means those who could
stand to benefit most from these types of investments may not receive them.

3.1.3 Recognize When Current Approaches Are Not Working
LIFT participants partnering with Tribes shared their experiences with what they described as
systemic barriers. These barriers range from simple issues like norms for communicating to more
complex issues such as staffing limitations or capacity limitations for completing forms, filing
applications, or joining in-person gatherings. When opportunities for participation arise,
impacted groups, including Tribal Nations and associated organizations, must navigate complex
regulatory processes with limited staff and resources, which hampers their ability to advocate for
their needs, ultimately resulting in less access to the benefits generated by wind development.
Tribes, for instance, face legal and structural barriers such as jurisdictional and federal permitting
complexities.

LIFT participants discussed this as the status quo arrangement, placing some groups outside the
pool of those receiving or likely to receive benefits. Simply put, participation in decision-making
processes—about where and who should receive what kinds of benefits, as well as decisions
about technology, siting, energy distribution, etc.—is often out of reach for many groups that
will be affected by those decisions.

3.1.4 Increase Transparency and Access to Decision-Making Processes
Some CoP participants see a need for sustained commitment to increasing transparency and
improving access to decision-making processes related to benefits from wind development. For
example, they advocate for building awareness among policymakers and other decision makers
about the imbalance in who needs and who is likely to receive benefits, as well as the
mechanisms available for broadening the pool of likely recipients. LIFT participants articulated
this as the need for more transparent and accessible decision-making processes that offer better
access and capacity-building opportunities for different stakeholder groups and Tribal nations to
be full participants in decisions about benefits. Full participation in wind energy development
necessitates going beyond opportunities to join a meeting or provide comments; it involves
broader participation in designing or co-designing decision-making processes and outcomes. In
other words, some LIFT CoP participants emphasize the need to move participation in decision-
making on wind benefits further along the spectrum of participation, from what many felt is the
current status quo of “informing” participants on decisions already made to fostering
collaboration and empowering a more inclusive decision-making process open to all participants,
stakeholders, industry, and agencies.

3.1.5 Develop Guidelines for Expanding Participation
LIFT participants noted that the lack of clear engagement or participation guidelines across the
industry hinders full participation in decision-making about benefits. Each case of community
participation is unique, given variations in energy project scale, technology, developer, and
timeline, and the distinct characteristics of the place and people. Modes of public and community
participation also vary, including the extent to which the participation process is designed to
impact final decisions. LIFT participants suggested that learning about what has worked (or not

 5

worked) in different contexts could help to inform a shared, useful framework to guide future
engagement efforts. Determining good approaches for stakeholder or Tribal engagement on wind
energy benefits requires a shared but flexible model capable of incorporating input from different
groups with different histories, visions, and capabilities. For instance, Tribes are not only
stakeholders in participatory processes but sovereign nations requiring consultation and
engagement that recognizes their status.

 There is a notable lack of collaboration … leading to less access to resources
 for Indigenous members. Ensure … communities have access to wind energy
 benefits by removing participation barriers and promoting fair resource
 distribution.
 —Wendolyn Holland, the Alliance for Tribal Clean Energy, LIFT Steering
 Committee member and June 2024 LIFT convening participant

3.1.6 Develop and Share Resources and Tools for Expanding Participation
When discussing the need for capacity-building opportunities for communities facing renewable
energy development opportunities, LIFT participants pointed to the need for informational
resources applicable in different contexts. These resources should include educational materials,
tailored support tools, and access to organizations that offer support to leaders and organizers to
shape wind development in ways that reflect their priorities and needs. Audiences include state
and federal policymakers as well as local leaders. Organizations affiliated with Tribal nations
would benefit from Tribal-specific resources and technical assistance to build skills and capacity
to address the jurisdictional and permitting complexities mentioned above.

 Make educational resources accessible to enable meaningful community
 engagement and promote collaborative governance through co-regulation and
 co-decision-making.
 —June 2024 LIFT convening participant

3.2 Ongoing Efforts of LIFT CoP Participants That Aim To Expand
 Wind Energy Benefits
Several LIFT CoP participants shared their current efforts to expand access to wind energy
benefits. Some projects take a “bottom-up” approach, focusing on connecting local officials or
organizers with state agencies and industry decision makers. Others utilize “top down” strategies
that build capacity for state agencies to better coordinate with impacted communities.

3.2.1 Case Study 1: Foresighting Workshops To Link Needs With Community
 Benefits
The Gulf of Maine Research Institute (GMRI) convened a Strategic Foresighting Workshop to
support coastal fishing communities in envisioning both plausible and preferred future scenarios
in a landscape increasingly shaped by offshore wind energy development. By applying future
thinking, the workshop created a space for fishers to engage deeply with long-term challenges
and opportunities, and to imagine what meaningful, lasting benefits from offshore wind could
look like for the fishing industry.

 6

This effort directly addressed the uncertainty and concern many communities face around the
emerging offshore wind sector—especially nascent floating offshore wind technology—by
providing a structured, facilitated process to explore change and proactively plan for it. The
foresighting approach empowered participants to identify potential risks and opportunities, build
a shared vision for the future, and articulate strategic needs that could be supported by benefit
mechanisms.

Using the foresighting methodology, participants worked through a series of exercises to identify
the major drivers of change shaping the future of the Gulf of Maine fishery—from ecological
shifts and policy changes to economic pressures and intersectoral competition for ocean space.
They then developed alternative future scenarios and collaboratively defined a preferred vision
for a resilient, sustainable, and diverse fishery, despite the known and unknown impacts of
offshore wind development.

With this future vision in mind, participants moved into strategic planning. They identified key
priorities to achieve that future, including protecting and enhancing working waterfront
infrastructure for traditional fishing activities; ensuring stable, multi-species access for individual
fishers; increasing federal investment in the fishing sector, including support for cooperative
research; supporting the next generation of fishers through permit preservation and training;
improving community access to locally sourced seafood; and establishing clear and secure rights
to ocean space that support fishing alongside new uses.

This process not only helped define tangible needs and ideas but also linked them to actionable
strategies—including the development of benefit mechanisms, e.g., fisheries mitigation funds, to
ensure offshore wind development delivers lasting benefits to impacted communities.

Ultimately, the workshop demonstrated how foresighting can build local capacity to plan for
change, bridge communication gaps between communities and developers, and foster long-term
thinking that is grounded in both cultural identity and economic viability. Learn more about
GMRI’s efforts at the GMRI Resource Hub.

 This workshop created a unique space for fishing community leaders,
 scientists, and planners to come together and think beyond the immediate
 challenges—toward long-term resilience. It was powerful to explore future
 scenarios with people who are deeply rooted in the Gulf of Maine, and to co-
 create strategies that reflect the realities and strengths of these communities.
 —Hannah MacDonald, Senior Program Manager, Gulf of Maine Research
 Institute, LIFT Steering Committee member

3.2.2 Case Study 2: Building Capacity for States To Effectively Engage Impacted
 Communities
The Clean Energy States Alliance (CESA) supports state agencies interested in partnering with
local stakeholders to create better and more responsive offshore wind planning and development
processes. The ongoing project was launched in 2021, when several state agencies developing
offshore wind sought CESA’s support in ensuring that local stakeholders saw the benefits of
offshore wind power.

In 2022, CESA published a State of States Report identifying several “levers” that state agencies
can use to help local stakeholders and businesses understand offshore wind development,
participate in the supply chain, and benefit from developer investments. CESA further convened
meetings between local stakeholder organizations and state agencies to identify areas where state

 8

agencies could improve their practices. These areas included encouraging stakeholder
engagement at the state and local levels, prompting information sharing among agencies and
stakeholder groups, building capacity to participate, and encouraging collaboration between state
agencies.

CESA led a working group to finalize several concrete recommendations for state agencies to
improve engagement and build stronger relationships with local stakeholders collected in a
November 2025 report. 1 The resulting report includes recommendations on the following
themes: building capacity among state agencies for stakeholder involvement; promoting greater
transparency for stakeholders into wind development planning, procurement, and permitting; and
creating venues for local stakeholders to provide input on planned and ongoing offshore wind
developments.

 Bringing together state agencies and local organizations has led to extremely
 productive conversations and helped both groups learn so much from one
 another. We believe that working collaboratively between state government and
 local stakeholders is the best way to ensure that large energy projects benefit the
 people that host them.
 —Sam Schacht, Project Director, Clean Energy States Alliance, LIFT Steering
 Committee member

3.2.3 Case Study 3: University of Rhode Island Coastal Resources Center
 Partner Workshops To Meet Information and Resource Needs
In 2023–2024, the University of Rhode Island Coastal Resources Center collaborated with the
Town of Portsmouth and the Rhode Island Saltwater Anglers Association to design and deliver
educational programming that addressed community concerns about offshore energy
development. This included clarifying regulatory processes, providing evidence-based
information on potential local impacts, and facilitating dialogue between local groups and
subject matter experts to support informed public engagement.

 communities, it is viewed as a neutral convener, focused on providing up-to-date,
 evidence-based, and locally relevant information.
 2. Partner-Driven Programming: Involving partner groups in selecting event topics gave
 participants a sense of ownership in the process. When constituents have a voice, they

3.2.4 Case Study 4: Empowering Youth Through Education on Renewable
 Energy
A project led by the nonprofit groups Operation Grow 2 and CE Clean Energy Bright Futures
aims to empower youth through education on renewable energy, leadership, and career-

 Our youth deserve to learn about … renewable energy sources that will have

3.3 Emerging Work and Opportunities To Expand Wind Energy
 Benefits
3.3.1 Community Advisory Boards
Participants shared an array of methods and approaches for expanding access to wind energy
benefits. For example, relying solely on local officials, Tribal council leaders, or municipal staff
to pursue relationships with large energy developers overlooks the many responsibilities these
public servants and officials already manage. In response, one participant shared the model of
Attentive Energy’s Community Advisory Boards (CABs), which consist of individuals with
diverse professional roles and interests, to center local perspectives while pursuing benefits that
reflect local priorities:

Case Study 5: Attentive Energy Offshore Wind Community Advisory Boards

The Attentive Energy CABs were created to enhance local engagement on offshore wind and
support the development process of the Attentive Energy Two project. The CABs aim to
integrate local communities into the offshore wind industry by leaning on a steering committee
of local leaders to facilitate information sharing between the project and residents as well as
advise on the deployment of the project’s community benefits program. Two pilot CABs were
established in Gloucester, Salem, and Union counties, with an initial funding commitment of
$275,000 to support local educational events and a hyperlocal microgrant program that funds
initiatives to increase access to the offshore wind industry.

The development process for the CABs began in early 2023, as the Attentive Energy team sought
to enhance its approach to local engagement and capacity building. With an understanding of the
communities that are at the forefront of much of the onshore infrastructure for the industry in
New Jersey, Attentive Energy deployed its Community Engagement team to connect with
leaders within the towns of Salem, Paulsboro, and Elizabeth who demonstrated an early interest
in the industry and are also involved in local workforce, environmental, and small business

 Learn more about Operation Grow’s efforts at https://www.operationgrowinc.org/.

 11

development activities. Roundtable discussions on the project and the industry were
subsequently held in these communities. The concept for the CABs was also presented to the
participants, and as a result, 10 leaders agreed to form two CABs in collaboration with the
Attentive Energy team.

The CAB members and Attentive Energy have since established a set of operating procedures
and goals for the group. Since their establishment, the CABs have met at least quarterly with the
Attentive Energy team to build their capacity to engage with the industry and to also co-lead
local events and activities to serve residents. This includes roundtable discussions for the public
on the basics of offshore wind development, training on supply chain opportunities for small
business, and sessions at local schools to expose students to the offshore wind workforce. During
meetings, CAB members also provide insights into local priorities and concerns regarding the
development of the industry.

In 2025, Attentive Energy initiated an microgrant initiative with both CABs to support 10
different local programs. This includes initiatives like the creation of learning modules for
participants of the local Boys and Girls Club, purchasing a welding simulator for a local high
school, developing a community garden and environmental stewardship initiative, and several
other efforts focused on small business and workforce development.

The pilot CAB initiative has successfully increased community participation in the project
development process and supported local economic development and workforce opportunities. It
has also helped the Attentive Energy team develop long-lasting and mutually beneficial
relationships with local leaders. As the project team strives to continue advancing Attentive
Energy Two, the CABs will serve as a cornerstone to ensure that team members are aware of
local priorities, as well as expand a coalition of support for the project and industry. Attentive
Energy’s CABs exemplify a proactive approach to community engagement in the offshore wind
industry, playing a crucial role in advancing renewable energy objectives and supporting
community-driven development.

3.3.2 Workforce Development
Other LIFT CoP participant efforts sought to create tangible career pathways for municipalities,
Tribal members, and neighborhoods interested in workforce development. Much like how
shipbuilding historically fostered economic development and shaped identities in coastal
communities, participants discussed how wind development could be leveraged as a means for
cultural and economic rejuvenation. For instance, local hiring, education, and workforce
development programs can allow communities to see themselves as part of the industry’s future.
This could also incorporate colleges and universities, including Tribal colleges. Capacity to do
this work relates to inclusion of workforce priorities with benefit mechanisms for communities.
Some LIFT participants with experience in organizing and building local capacity suggested that
benefit mechanisms should be mandatory, transparent, and tailored to regional and cultural
needs.

 12

 Developing transparent benefit-sharing frameworks ensures tangible, equitable
 outcomes for impacted communities, including financial incentives, education,
 and infrastructure investments.
 —Participant at a LIFT in-person workshop in Atlantic City, New Jersey, October
 2024

3.3.3 Reimagine Wind Energy
Finally, some LIFT CoP participants discussed the opportunity to help impacted communities
see wind energy development through a new lens. While new energy development can provide
access to needed benefits, it can also prompt impacted communities to rethink their current
understanding of wind energy development and make it their own. For instance, some
participants called for reimagining the face of wind energy to be more representative of a broad
range of people and stakeholder groups that can both support and benefit from its development.

 Reimagining isn’t just about changing perceptions—it’s about ensuring
 communities have power in shaping the industry’s future.
 —LIFT workshop participant, October 2024

3.4 Knowing Good Outcomes When We See Them
The 2024 LIFT CoP convenings provided opportunities for LIFT participants to share and
discuss the outcomes of their efforts and ways to evaluate the extent to which desired outcomes
have been achieved. LIFT CoP participants engaged with the question, “What kinds of things

which energy development truly benefits impacted communities. They also recommended that
the framework include both qualitative indicators—such as perceptions of fairness, participation,
influence on the process design, and identification of desired outcomes—and quantitative
metrics, including metrics for job creation, education, environmental impacts, and health
outcomes.

4 Expanding the LIFT CoP To Meet Opportunities
The 2024 LIFT CoP convenings and activities focused on expanding benefits from wind energy
development. Through 2024 activities, the LIFT CoP:

 • Identified the multifaceted challenges facing practitioners, agencies, local governments,
 Tribes, and organizers seeking to expand wind energy’s potential benefits.
 • Shared and learned about efforts underway to expand benefits and build capacity through
 both community-led projects (bottom up) and government agency-led efforts (top down).
 • Identified methods for evaluating outcomes in connecting stakeholders with wind energy
 benefits.

4.1 Participant-Identified Opportunities
While LIFT convenings welcomed Tribal perspectives, including contributions from the Alliance
for Tribal Clean Energy, and aimed to integrate culturally grounded approaches into discussions,
2024 activities only scratched the surface of Tribal perspectives on expanding benefits from
renewable energy development. Going forward, LIFT participants identified incorporating Tribal
perspectives on participation in renewable energy development as a priority topic, along with
exploring opportunities with Tribal Ecological Knowledge and consensus-based decision
processes informed by Tribal perspectives.

Beyond discussions focused on expanding the benefits of wind energy development, LIFT
participants also called for more effective and authentic participation not only during the
planning or approval phase of project development but throughout the entire life cycle of the
energy project. These discussions highlighted the need for participation from project inception
through design and technology selection, operations, and end-of-service phases to ensure local
officials, organizations, Tribes, and community leaders can actively, continuously shape
decisions that impact them directly.

While utility-scale wind energy served as the common technology topic for LIFT CoP activities
throughout 2024, some participants also shared their experience with other renewable energy
technologies, such as solar energy, and other project scales, including distributed energy.
Incorporating additional renewable energy technology systems is an opportunity to continue
building out cross-technology insights within the CoP.

4.2 A Next Phase of LIFT Will Respond Directly to These
 Opportunities
Responding to these opportunities, the next phase LIFT CoP activities will:

 • Expand the technology focus from wind to include learnings from other renewable
 energy technologies, including geothermal, hydrokinetic, solar energy, and biomass
 bioenergy. This includes both utility-scale projects, as well as distributed energy projects
 where power is produced close to where it is used, on a smaller scale. By including
 participants with experience, interest, or expertise in these technologies, we can ask:
 What can we learn from each, and what can we learn through comparison across
 technologies as well as across sectors and regions?

 15

 • Engage a “full life cycle” conceptual framework: Participation in the full life cycle
 of renewable energy projects from design and planning to operations to end-of-
 service. How are stakeholders, communities, and Tribes engaged in decision-making
 across this spectrum? What are the barriers to engaging in operational and end-of-life
 decision-making and how are they overcome? This framework will build on the many
 insights shared by LIFT participants from different sectors and regions about
 opportunities for bolstering participation.
 • Focus on LIFT CoP participant interaction during LIFT gatherings to provide
 opportunities to share work related to the full life cycle framework and hear from others.
 A successful group dynamic will support participants in generating new ideas,
 discovering new collaborative possibilities, building a shared evaluation framework, and
 fostering new––or enhancing existing––relationships across cultures, sectors, regions,
 and technologies.

4.3 2025 LIFT CoP Activities
April 2025: LIFT convened an informal discussion open to all LIFT CoP participants to share
their stories and efforts while identifying needs related to the full life cycle framework.
Discussions informed planning efforts for the 2025 virtual and in-person LIFT convenings.

May 2025: A full CoP virtual gathering was held that included a panel discussion with five
presenters speaking to their experience with public participation in renewable energy technology
design, planning, operation, and end-of-service.

August 2025: LIFT convened an in-person workshop in conjunction with the Rural Sociological
Society Annual Meetings in Salt Lake City, Utah, which welcomed participation from rural
energy researchers, practitioners, and agency representatives on public participation across the
full life cycle framework.

September 2025: The LIFT Team began planning for 2026 activities, which will focus on novel
and innovative public participation and stakeholder engagement processes to enable social
acceptance for renewable energy siting.

Appendix: Research Methods
To gather insights from LIFT convenings and organize the content collected within this report,
the LIFT team captured comprehensive notes during LIFT gatherings, identified themes,
highlighted illustrative quotes, and outlined case studies presented during convenings to describe
both the breadth and depth of ideas, lessons, and efforts shared among the CoP. The LIFT team
drafted this report from those notes. The report was next reviewed by members of the LIFT
Steering Committee for their support in confirming our findings, reviewing key themes, refining
the illustrative examples, and checking that representative quotes and cases were incorporated.
This collaborative review is meant to ensure the accuracy and credibility of our findings.
