Gravitational effects on a dissipative two-level atom in the weak-field regime
Kaito Kashiwagi, Akira Matsumura

Abstract
We investigate the dissipative dynamics of a two-level atom in a weak gravitational field. Using the Feynman–Vernon influence functional formalism, we derive a quantum master equation describing the two-level atom interacting with a scalar field in a Newtonian gravitational field, and compute the energy dissipation rate of the atom. We find that the spontaneous emission rate (the dissipation rate in vacuum) is modified by the gravitational field. Specifically, this modification depends on the atom’s dipole, the position of the atom relative to the source of the gravitational field, and the frequency of the scalar radiation emitted by the atom. Furthermore, we identify the parameter regimes in which the spontaneous emission rate is enhanced or suppressed by gravity. We also discuss how the modification arises from time dilation and dipole radiation in a weak gravitational field. These findings provide a theoretical basis for exploring gravitational effects in open quantum systems.

Introduction

General relativity (GR)
and quantum mechanics (QM)
are two fundamental pillars of modern physics.
GR describes the macroscopic scale physics and it is experimentally confirmed that this theory can explain various phenomena such as the perihelion precession of Mercury, gravitational lensing and gravitational redshift and so on

;

Especially, since the observation of gravitational waves(GRs) in 2015

On the other hand, QM successfully describes microscopic dynamics, and abundant experimental evidence supports its validity, including the Stern–Gerlach experiment and the double-slit experiment

More recently, the violation of Bell’s inequality

Bell_test2

Although it remains unclear whether gravity itself should be quantized, the effects of classical gravitational fields on quantum systems have been both theoretically investigated and experimentally verified.
For example, as the well-known experiments, there are the measurement of the gravitationally induced phase shift of neutrons and the test of the quantized bound energy of neutron in the Earth’s gravitational potential

Neutron_2002

Rapid advances in quantum technologies, such as quantum sensing and quantum control, have made it possible to realize quantum systems and to detect extremely weak external influences.
For instance, quantum control over atomic ensembles containing

Furthermore, quantum-logic techniques applied to nitrogen-vacancy spin ensembles in diamond have enabled significant enhancement of magnetic-field sensitivity

These developments open a new avenue toward exploring subtle gravitational effects using quantum systems.
In this study, we focus on the dissipative dynamics of a two-level atom and theoretically investigate how gravitational fields modify this dissipation process.
Since dissipation is closely tied to the exchange of information and energy between a quantum system and its environment, understanding how gravity influences such processes may offer new insights and possibilities for both fundamental physics and potential applications.

We consider that our present study leads to a model-independent method for Dark Matter (DM) detection.
The DM is thought to make up about 27% of this universe, and the clarification of DM is one of the important issues in modern physics.
Various DM candidates such as Axion, Weakly Interacting Massive Particles(WIMPs) and Primordial Black Hole(PBH) have been considered, and those experimental verifications have been discussed

In light of the fact that DM was introduced as an unknown gravitational source

Our study of the dissipative dynamics of atoms in a weak gravitational field potentially paves the way toward a model-independent and highly sensitive DM detection.

As another application, our study also contributes to high-precision tests of GR.
Although GR has been extensively tested, probing possible deviations at small scales remains important.
Recent quantum technologies have enabled increasingly sensitive tests of GR using quantum systems.

As mentioned above, our aim is to investigate the dissipative dynamics of a two-level atom as a pathway toward establishing a novel detection method in the interface between quantum physics and gravity.
In this study, we focus on the energy dissipation of a two-level atom placed in a weak Newtonian gravitational field, in order to clarify how the gravitational field modifies the dissipative process.
As a result, we find that the dissipation rate is suppressed or enhanced by the gravitational field, and that this behavior depends on the mass of gravitational source, the distance from the source to the atom, and the frequency of radiation field emitted by the atom.

II
QME of a two-level atom in a weak gravitational field

Quantum systems interacting with external environments are referred to as open quantum systems
Breuer_2002
.
The dissipation of open quantum systems is one of the most fundamental and representative phenomena.
The dynamics can be systematically described within the theory of open quantum systems.
In this section, we derive the quantum master equation (QME) for a two-level atom in a weak gravitational field by employing the Feynman–Vernon influence functional method.
We begin with a brief overview of this method
Hu_2008
.
To this end, let us first write down the total action of the open quantum system and its environment:

\displaystyle S_{\text{tot}}

\displaystyle=S_{\text{sys}}[q]+S_{\text{E}}[\phi]+S_{\text{int}}[q,\phi],

(1)

where
S_{\text{sys}}[q]
denotes the action of the system of interest,
the action of environment is described by
S_{\text{E}}[\phi]
,
and
S_{\text{int}}[q,\phi]
specifies the interaction between them.
QME is an equation of motion for the reduced density matrix
\rho_{\text{s}}(q,q^{\prime},t)
of the system, and this matrix can be yielded by using the Feynman-Vernon influence functional method.
Suppose that the density matrix in the total system is written as
\rho(q,\phi,q^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},t)
, and this matrix element is given by the path integral,

\displaystyle\rho(q,\phi,q^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\int dq_{0}\int d\phi_{0}\int dq_{0}^{\prime}\int d\phi_{0}^{\prime}\int^{q}_{q_{0}}\mathcal{D}q\int^{\phi}_{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{D}\phi\int^{q^{\prime}}_{q_{0}^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}q^{\prime}\int^{\phi^{\prime}}_{\phi_{0}^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}\phi^{\prime}\ e^{i(S_{\text{tot}}[q,\phi]-S_{\text{tot}}[q^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}])}\rho(q_{0},\phi_{0},q_{0}^{\prime},\phi_{0}^{\prime},0).

(2)

The reduced density matrix
\rho_{\text{s}}
is obtained by tracing over the environmental degrees of freedom
\phi
in the total density matrix.
When the initial state
\rho(q_{0},\phi_{0},q_{0}^{\prime},\phi_{0}^{\prime},0)
is not correlated, that is,
\rho(q_{0},\phi_{0},q_{0}^{\prime},\phi_{0}^{\prime},0)=\rho_{\text{s}}(q_{0},q_{0}^{\prime},0)\rho_{\text{E}}(\phi_{0},\phi_{0}^{\prime},0)
, we get the following form of
\rho_{\text{s}}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{s}}(q,q^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\int d\phi\ \rho(q,\phi,q^{\prime},\phi,t)

\displaystyle=\int dq_{0}\int dq_{0}^{\prime}\ \rho_{\text{s}}(q_{0},q_{0}^{\prime},0)\int^{q}_{q_{0}}\mathcal{D}q\int^{q^{\prime}}_{q_{0}^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}q^{\prime}\ e^{i\left(S_{\text{sys}}[q]-S_{\text{sys}}[q^{\prime}]+S_{\text{IF}}[q,q^{\prime}]\right)},

(3)

where the action
S_{\text{IF}}[q,q^{\prime}]
is called the influence action defined by

\displaystyle e^{iS_{\text{IF}}[q,q^{\prime}]}=\int d\phi\int d\phi_{0}\ \int d\phi_{0}^{\prime}\int^{\phi}_{\phi_{0}}\mathcal{D}\phi\int^{\phi}_{\phi_{0}^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}\phi^{\prime}\ e^{i(S_{\text{E}}+S_{\text{int}}[q,\phi]-S_{\text{E}}[\phi^{\prime}]-S_{\text{int}}[q^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}])}\rho_{\text{E}}(\phi_{0},\phi_{0}^{\prime},0).

(4)

The influence action
S_{\text{IF}}[q,q^{\prime}]
captures the effect from the environment.
The explicit expression of
S_{\text{IF}}[q,q^{\prime}]
makes it possible to describe the dynamics of the system of interest.

Next, we present the concrete setup in this paper.
We consider that a gravitational source with a mass
M
weakly bends the spacetime in the vicinity of the source. The spacetime metric is given by

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\text{diag}[-1-2\Phi(\bm{x}),1-2\Phi(\bm{x}),1-2\Phi(\bm{x}),1-2\Phi(\bm{x})],

(5)

where

\Phi(\bm{x})=-GM/x
is the gravitational potential produced by the source, and
x=|\bm{x}|
is the distance from the source.
We also consider a composite system of two point-like particles bound together, which will later be treated as a two-level atom.
We assume that the particles are non-relativistic and that the center-of-mass (COM) motion of the composite system is almost at rest.
The internal motion of the system is nothing but the relative motion of the particles, whose action is given as follows
Zych_2019
:

\displaystyle S_{\text{sys}}[\bm{r}]

\displaystyle=\int dt\left[\frac{1}{2}\left[1-3\Phi(\bm{R})\right]\mu\dot{\bm{r}}^{2}(t)+\left[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})\right]\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi r(t)}\right],

(6)

where
\mu=m_{1}m_{2}/(m_{1}+m_{2})
is the reduced mass of the two particles, and
\bm{R}
and
\bm{r}(t)
denote the COM and relative coordinates, respectively. The
1/r
binding potential between the two particles characterizes the internal energy level of the composite system.
To get the action (
6
), the contribution of spacetime curvature was ignored by assuming
|\bm{r}|\ll|\bm{R}|
.
The two particles are taken to be coupled with a massless scalar quantum field
\phi(x)
, whose free action is

\displaystyle S_{\text{E}}[\phi]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}g^{\mu\nu}(x)\nabla_{\mu}\phi(x)\nabla_{\nu}\phi(x),

(7)

where
g
is the determinant of
g_{\mu\nu}
,
g^{\mu\nu}
is the inverse of
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\nabla_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative in this spacetime.
The interaction between the particles and the scalar field is introduced as a linear coupling of the field
\phi(x)
to an external source
J(\bm{q};x)
induced by the particle trajectories Refs.
Iso_2011
;
Oshita_2016
:

\displaystyle S_{\text{int}}[\bm{q},\phi]

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g(x)}\,\phi(x)\,J(\bm{q};x),

(8)

with the source defined by

\displaystyle J(\bm{q};x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g(x)}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\lambda_{i}\ \frac{d\tau_{i}}{dt}\,\delta^{3}\!\big(\bm{x}-\bm{q}_{i}(t)\big),

(9)

where
\lambda_{i}
denotes the coupling constant, and
\tau_{i}
is the proper time of each particle.
Note that
\bm{q}_{i}
denotes the position of each particle.
Since the gravitational field is weak, we can perturbatively evaluate the interaction action
S_{\text{int}}
with respect to the gravitaional potential
\Phi
.
The factor
dt/d\tau_{i}
in Eq.(
9
) is expanded as
d\tau_{i}\approx[1+\Phi(t,\bm{q}_{i}(t)]dt
, and the source
J(\bm{q};x)
is evaluated as

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g(x)}J(\bm{q};x)

\displaystyle\approx\sum^{2}_{i=1}\left[1+\Phi(\bm{q}_{i})\right]\lambda_{i}\delta^{3}(\bm{x}-\bm{q}_{i}(t)).

(10)

Substituting this into (
8
), we have

\displaystyle S_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle\approx\int dt\sum^{2}_{i=1}\left[1+\Phi(\bm{q}_{i})\right]\lambda_{i}\phi(t,\bm{q}_{i}).

(11)

Setting
\lambda=\lambda_{1}=-\lambda_{2}
, we can realize a dipole-like interaction between the composite system and the scalar field.
When the relative motion of the particles is small, using the relations
\bm{q}_{1}=\bm{R}+m_{2}\bm{r}/(m_{1}+m_{2})
and
\bm{q}_{2}=\bm{R}-m_{1}\bm{r}/(m_{1}+m_{2})
, we can expand the interaction as

\displaystyle S_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle\approx[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]\int dt\,\lambda\bm{r}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi(t,\bm{R}),

(12)

where we ignored the spatial derivative of the gravitational potential by assuming
|\bm{r}|\ll|\bm{R}|
.
This is similar to the dipole interaction
-\bm{d}\cdot\bm{E}
between a dipole and an electric field.

Here, we regard the scalar field as the environment coupled with the composite system.
We also assume that the scalar field is in a thermal state in the weak gravitational field.
Tracing over the scalar field, based on Ref.
Hu_2008
, we get the influence action for the composite system as

\displaystyle S_{\text{IF}}[\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]^{2}\int dt\int dt^{\prime}\left[r_{i}(t)-r^{\prime}_{i}(t)\right]\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\mathbb{D}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R})\left[r_{j}(t^{\prime})+r^{\prime}_{j}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{i\lambda^{2}}{2}[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]^{2}\int dt\int dt^{\prime}\ \left[r_{i}(t)-r^{\prime}_{i}(t)\right]\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\mathbb{N}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R})\left[r_{j}(t^{\prime})-r^{\prime}_{j}(t^{\prime})\right],

(13)

where
i
,
j
running over spatial indices are implicitly summed over, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(t,\bm{x},t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})\equiv i\langle[\hat{\phi}(t,\bm{x}),\hat{\phi}(t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})]\rangle\theta(t-t^{\prime}),\quad\mathbb{N}(t,\bm{x},t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})\equiv\frac{1}{2}\langle\{\hat{\phi}(t,\bm{x}),\hat{\phi}(t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})\}\rangle.

(14)

The bracket
\langle\cdot\rangle
represents the expectation value taken for the thermal state of the scalar field.
Explicitly, the functions
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{N}
is perturbatively evaluated as

\mathbb{D}\approx\mathbb{D}_{0}+\delta\mathbb{D},\quad\mathbb{N}\approx\mathbb{N}_{0}+\delta\mathbb{N},

(15)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{0}(t,\bm{x},t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=i\theta(t-t^{\prime})\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2|\bm{p}|}e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{x^{\prime}})}I^{-}_{p}(t,t^{\prime})

(16)

\displaystyle\delta\mathbb{D}(t,\bm{x},t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=8\pi iR\Phi(\bm{R})\theta(t-t^{\prime})\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x^{\prime}}}}{|\bm{p}-\bm{k}|^{2}(|\bm{p}|^{2}-|\bm{k}|^{2})}\left[|\bm{p}|I^{-}_{p}(t,t^{\prime})-|\bm{k}|I^{-}_{k}(t,t^{\prime})\right]

(17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{0}(t,\bm{x},t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2|\bm{p}|}e^{i\bm{p}\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{x^{\prime}})}I^{+}_{p}(t,t^{\prime})

(18)

\displaystyle\delta\mathbb{N}(t,\bm{x},t^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=4\pi R\Phi(\bm{R})\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x^{\prime}}}}{|\bm{p}-\bm{k}|^{2}(|\bm{p}|^{2}-|\bm{k}|^{2})}\left[|\bm{p}|I^{+}_{p}(t,t^{\prime})-|\bm{k}|I^{+}_{k}(t,t^{\prime})\right]

(19)

\displaystyle I^{-}_{p}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv e^{-i|\bm{p}|(t-t^{\prime})}-e^{i|\bm{p}|(t-t^{\prime})},\quad I^{+}_{p}(t,t^{\prime})\equiv[2n_{B}(|\bm{p}|)+1]\left[e^{-i|\bm{p}|(t-t^{\prime})}+e^{i|\bm{p}|(t-t^{\prime})}\right].

(20)

The
\mathbb{D}_{0}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}

are computed from the thermal state of scalar field in the flat space, and the
\delta\mathbb{D}
and
\delta\mathbb{N}
correspond to those gravitational corrections.
The mean occupation number of the thermal scalar particles is
n_{B}(|\bm{p}|)=1/(e^{\beta|\bm{p}|}-1)
with the inverse temperature
\beta
.
The detailed derivation of Eqs.(
16
), (
17
), (
18
) and (
19
) is devoted in Appendix
D
.
From Eq.(
15
), the influence functional up to the first order in the gravitational potential
\Phi
is

\displaystyle S_{\text{IF}}[\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int dt\int dt^{\prime}\left[r_{i}(t)-r^{\prime}_{i}(t)\right]\mathbb{D}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})\left[r_{j}(t^{\prime})+r^{\prime}_{j}(t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{i\lambda^{2}}{2}\int dt\int dt^{\prime}\ \left[r_{i}(t)-r^{\prime}_{i}(t)\right]\mathbb{N}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})\left[r_{j}(t^{\prime})-r^{\prime}_{j}(t^{\prime})\right],

(21)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv\left[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})\right]\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\mathbb{D}_{0}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R})+\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\delta\mathbb{D}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R}),

(22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv\left[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})\right]\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\mathbb{N}_{0}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R})+\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\delta\mathbb{N}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R}).

(23)

The QME of the composite system is obtained as the time differential equation of the reduced density matrix of the system.
From Eq. (
3
), the reduced density matrix,

\rho_{\text{s}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},t)
, is

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{s}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{r}_{0}\int d\bm{r}_{0}^{\prime}\,\rho_{\text{s}}(\bm{r}_{0},\bm{r}_{0}^{\prime},0)\int_{\bm{r}_{0}}^{\bm{r}}\mathcal{D}\bm{r}\int_{\bm{r}_{0}^{\prime}}^{\bm{r}^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}\bm{r}^{\prime}\,e^{i(S_{\text{sys}}[\bm{r}]-S_{\text{sys}}[\bm{r}^{\prime}]+S_{\text{IF}}[\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}])}.

(24)

Computing the time derivative of
\rho_{\text{s}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},t)
and expanding it up to
O(\lambda^{2})
(employing the Born approximation), we derive the following QME for
\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)=\int d\bm{r}\int d\bm{r}^{\prime}\rho_{\text{s}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},t)|\bm{r}\rangle\langle\bm{r}^{\prime}|
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\left[\hat{H}_{s},\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)\right]+\frac{i\lambda^{2}}{2}\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\ \mathbb{D}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})\left[\hat{r}_{i},\left\{\hat{r}_{j}(t^{\prime}-t),\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda^{2}\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\ \mathbb{N}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})\left[\hat{r}_{i},\left[\hat{r}_{j}(t^{\prime}-t),\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)\right]\right],

(25)

where

is the operator of the relative coordinate, and the system Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{s}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{s}=\left[1+3\Phi(\bm{R})\right]\ \frac{\hat{\bm{p}}^{2}}{2\mu}-\left[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})\right]\ \frac{g^{2}}{4\pi\hat{r}}.

(26)

In the following, approximating the composite system as a two-level atom, we derive the QME for the two-level system.
To identify the physical energy of the composite system, we express the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\text{s}}
in terms of proper quantities

.
Let

and

denote the infinitesimal proper temporal and spatial intervals, respectively, defined in the local rest frame of the COM motion of the composite system.
They are related to the coordinate intervals as

The conjugate momentum

computed from Eq.(

\displaystyle\bm{p}

\displaystyle=[1-3\Phi(\bm{R})]\mu\frac{d\bm{r}}{dt}\approx[1-\Phi(\bm{R})]\mu\frac{d\bm{r}_{\text{p}}}{dt_{\text{p}}}=[1-\Phi(\bm{R})]\bm{p}_{\text{p}},

\displaystyle H_{s}

\displaystyle=[1+3\Phi(\bm{R})]\ \frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\mu}-[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})]\ \frac{g^{2}}{4\pi r}

\displaystyle\approx[1+3\Phi(\bm{R})]\cdot[1-2\Phi(\bm{R})]\cdot\frac{\bm{p}^{2}_{\text{p}}}{2\mu}-[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})]\cdot[1-\Phi(\bm{R})]\cdot\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi r_{\text{p}}}

\displaystyle\approx[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]\left[\frac{\bm{p}_{\text{p}}^{2}}{2\mu}-\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi r_{\text{p}}}\right].

(28)

.
Specifically, we pick up the ground state

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{s}}\approx\hat{H}^{(2)}_{\text{s}}=\frac{\Omega_{g}}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}\quad\Omega_{g}\equiv[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]\Omega_{\text{p}},

\displaystyle\lambda\hat{\bm{r}}\approx[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]\lambda\hat{\bm{r}}_{\text{p}}=[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]\bm{d}_{\text{p}}\hat{\sigma}_{x}.

and

Using (

30

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\left[\hat{H}^{(2)}_{s},\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)\right]+\frac{i}{2}\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\ d_{i}d_{j}\ \tilde{\mathbb{D}}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})\left[\hat{\sigma}_{x},\left\{\hat{\sigma}_{x}(t^{\prime}-t),\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\ d_{i}d_{j}\ \tilde{\mathbb{N}}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})\left[\hat{\sigma}_{x},\left[\hat{\sigma}_{x}(t^{\prime}-t),\hat{\rho}_{\text{s}}(t)\right]\right],

(31)

where

\tilde{\mathbb{D}}_{ij}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{N}}_{ij}
were defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{D}}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv[1+4\Phi(\bm{R})]\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\mathbb{D}_{0}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R})+\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\delta\mathbb{D}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R}),

(32)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{N}}_{ij}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv[1+4\Phi(\bm{R})]\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\mathbb{N}_{0}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R})+\partial^{x}_{i}\partial^{x^{\prime}}_{j}\delta\mathbb{N}(t,\bm{R},t^{\prime},\bm{R}).

(33)

We can cast Eq.(

Breuer_2002

Practically, the Markovian approximation is to perform the variable transformation
t^{\prime}-t\equiv-\tau
and then to change the integral upper
t
to
\infty

The rotating-wave approximation is to ignore the terms proportional to
e^{\pm i\Omega_{g}t}

Working in the interaction picture defined by the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\text{s}}
and applying those approximations, we arrive at the Markovian QME for the two-level atom

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)=-i\left[\hat{H}_{\text{shift}},\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)\right]+\,\mathcal{D}_{-}\left[\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)\right]+\mathcal{D}_{+}\left[\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)\right],

(34)

with
\hat{H}_{\text{shift}}\equiv S_{-}\hat{\sigma}_{+}\hat{\sigma}_{-}-S_{+}\hat{\sigma}_{-}\hat{\sigma}_{+}
and the dissipators

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{-}\left[\hat{\rho}\right]\equiv\Gamma_{-}\Big[\hat{\sigma}_{-}\hat{\rho}\hat{\sigma}_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\hat{\sigma}_{+}\hat{\sigma}_{-},\hat{\rho}\right\}\Big],\quad\mathcal{D}_{+}\left[\hat{\rho}\right]\equiv\Gamma_{+}\Big[\hat{\sigma}_{+}\hat{\rho}\hat{\sigma}_{-}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\hat{\sigma}_{-}\hat{\sigma}_{+},\hat{\rho}\right\}\Big],

(35)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\pm}=2\int^{\infty}_{0}d\tau d_{i}d_{j}\,\text{Re}\Big[\Big(\tilde{\mathbb{N}}_{ij}(t,t-\tau)\pm\frac{i}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{D}}_{ij}(t,t-\tau)\Big)e^{i\Omega_{g}\tau}\Big],

(36)

\displaystyle S_{\pm}=\int^{\infty}_{0}d\tau d_{i}d_{j}\,\text{Im}\Big[\Big(\tilde{\mathbb{N}}_{ij}(t,t-\tau)\pm\frac{i}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{D}}_{ij}(t,t-\tau)\Big)e^{i\Omega_{g}\tau}\Big],

(37)

and the superscript
I
of
\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}
indicates that Eq.(
34

\hat{\sigma}_{\pm}\equiv(\hat{\sigma}_{x}\pm i\hat{\sigma}_{y})/2
are the ladder operators.

As shown in Eq. (
34
), the first term of Eq. (
34
) with
\hat{H}_{\text{shift}}
represents an environment-induced energy shift of the system. This effect is known as the Lamb shift. Although the formal expression for
\hat{H}_{\text{shift}}
is divergent, the divergence can be removed by renormalization.
In this work, we treat this shift as a subleading contribution and therefore neglect it.
Here we emphasize that the result in Eq. (
34

\Omega\rightarrow\Omega_{g}
, indicating that the environmental effects play an essential and nontrivial role.

III
Dissipation rate in a weak gravitational field

Since we have the explicit QME of the two-level atom, we can obtain the energy dissipation rate of the atom.
In order to derive the energy dissipation rate, we solve the QME (
34
) (see Appendix
C
).
For the initial condition,
\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle=1
,
\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|g\rangle=0=\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle=\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|g\rangle
, that is,

\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

(38)

the solution of the QME (
34
) can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}(1-a_{s})e^{-\Gamma t}+a_{s}&0\\
0&(1-a_{s})(1-e^{-\Gamma t})\end{pmatrix},

(39)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma\equiv\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-},\quad a_{s}\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{+}}{\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-}},

(40)

and
\hat{H}_{\text{shift}}
has been neglected as mentioned above.
From Eq. (
39
), we read out that the characteristic timescale of the dissipation is

1/\Gamma
.
In this section, we analyze
\Gamma
in detail.

Before the analysis, we discuss how the dissipation rate and its gravitational correction can be observed.
To this end, let us consider the thought experiment illustrated in Fig.
1
.
A distant observer measures physical quantities and can examine a two-level atom located near the observer.
In this case, the dissipation rate directly measured by the observer coincides with the rate defined in the atom’s proper frame.
Next, we consider another identical atom placed near the gravitational source.
The distant observer also can detect the scalar particles emitted by that atom and infer the dissipation rate from the observed emission rate.
The observer can then compare the two rates: one associated with the atom near the observer and the other with the atom near the gravitational source.
From the comparison, the observer can identify the gravitational correction of the dissipation rate.

To get the dissipation rate

\Gamma
, we calculate
\Gamma_{+}
and
\Gamma_{-}
given in Eq.(
36
).
These are explicitly yielded as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}=n_{B}(\Omega_{g})\gamma_{g},\quad\Gamma_{-}=[n_{B}(\Omega_{g})+1]\gamma_{g},

(41)

where
n_{B}(\Omega_{g})
is the occupation number of the scalar particles at the frequency
\Omega_{g}
. The spontaneous emission rate
\gamma_{g}
is

\displaystyle\gamma_{g}=\gamma\left[1+7\Phi(\bm{R})-2\Phi(\bm{R})\,f_{1}(R\Omega)-3\Phi(\bm{R})\frac{(\bm{d}\cdot\bm{R})^{2}-d^{2}R^{2}}{d^{2}R^{2}}f_{2}(R\Omega)\right],

(42)

where
\gamma=d^{2}\Omega^{3}/(6\pi)
,
R=|\bm{R}|
,
d=|\bm{d}|
, and the numeric functions,

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{x^{2}}\Big[1+x^{2}(\pi x+3)-(1+x^{2})\cos(2x)-2x\sin( 2 x )-2x^{3}S_{i}(2x)\Big],

(43)

\displaystyle f_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{x^{2}}\Big[1-x\sin(2 x)-\cos(2 x)\Big].

(44)

The function
S_{i}(x)=\int^{x}_{0}dy\sin y/y
is the sine integral.
The above
\Gamma_{+}
,
\Gamma_{-}
, and
\gamma_{g}
are derived in Appendix
E
.
Here,
\gamma
corresponds to the spontaneous emission rate of the two-level atom in the flat space.
The ratio
\gamma_{g}/\gamma
characterizes the gravitational modification of the rate.
Fig.

presents the behavior of
\gamma_{g}/\gamma
as a function of
R\Omega
for some fixed values of
\Phi

The left and right panels show the ratio
\gamma_{g}/\gamma
for the parallel case
\bm{d}\parallel\bm{R}
and for the perpendicular case
\bm{d}\perp\bm{R}
, respectively. In the low- and high-frequency limits, the ratio gets to be constant. In the parallel case, the ratio is greater than one around
R\Omega\sim 1
. Hence the spontaneous emission rate

is enhanced. In the other parameter region, the rate is suppressed.

Let us focus on the two limits: the low frequency limit
R\Omega\ll 1
(the long wavelength mode of emitted scalar particles) and the high frequency limit
R\Omega\gg 1
(the short wavelength mode of emitted scalar particles).
Because the functions

f_{1}(x)\approx\begin{cases}\pi x&(x\ll 1)\\[2.0pt]
3&(x\gg 1)\end{cases},\quad f_{2}(x)\approx\begin{cases}\frac{4}{3}x^{2}&(x\ll 1)\\[2.0pt]

IV
Discussion

Here, we discuss the peculiar nature of the spontaneous emission rate given in (
46
).
For this purpose, let us consider a distant observer who measures the dissipation of a two-level atom during a finite coordinate-time interval
\Delta t
.
In this setup, the quantity
\gamma_{g}\Delta t
represents the number of the scalar particles emitted within the interval
\Delta t
.
Eq. (
46
) may be written as

\gamma_{g}\Delta t\approx\begin{cases}\left[1+6\Phi(\bm{R})\right]\gamma\Delta t_{\text{p}}&R\Omega\ll 1,\\[6.0pt]
\,\gamma\Delta t_{\text{p}}\,&R\Omega\gg 1,\end{cases}

(47)

where
\Delta t_{\text{p}}
is the proper temporal interval for the two-level atom and
\gamma
is the proper spontaneous emission rate, respectively.
Hence, in the high-frequency limit, the spontaneous emission rate
\gamma_{g}
in Eq.(
46
) is explained by the invariance of the number of emitted particles
\gamma_{g}\Delta t=\gamma\Delta t_{\text{p}}
, together with gravitational time dilation
\Delta t_{\mathrm{p}}\approx(1+\Phi)\Delta t
.
In contrast, the low-frequency result in Eq. (
47
) deviates from such intuitive understanding.

We also observe that the result of Eq. (
42
) is understood from the viewpoint of energy balance.
Since the energy of the two-level atom dissipates by the scalar radiation emitted from the atom, one expects that the energy dissipation rate characterized by
\gamma_{g}
is estimated from

P/\Omega_{g}
, where
P
is the power of scalar radiation and
\Omega_{g}
is the energy of the atom described by the distant observer.
Indeed, in Appendix
F
, this expectation can be confirmed by analyzing the scalar radiation emitted from the atom’s effective dipole as

\displaystyle\frac{P}{\Omega_{g}}\approx\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{g},

(48)

Taking these facts into account, we are led to conclude that the result obtained in this work is physically reasonable.

The spontaneous emission rate
\gamma_{g}
corresponds to the dissipation rate in the vacuum environment.
Since spontaneous emission originates from vacuum fluctuations, the behavior observed in the long-wavelength regime is expected to be sensitive to how these fluctuations are modified by gravity.
At the same time, the gravitational correction
\gamma_{g}/\gamma
depends not only on the Newtonian potential
\Phi
but also explicitly on the energy splitting
\Omega
.
Furthermore, previous studies have reported that the energy carried by electromagnetic radiation emitted from charged particles receives contributions beyond a simple gravitational redshift
Peters_1973
.
Taken together, these considerations indicate that the behavior in the long-wavelength limit cannot be attributed solely to geometric effects, and suggest that gravitational corrections to vacuum fluctuations may play an important role.

It is also important to discuss whether finite-temperature effects give the effects to the dissipation rate
\Gamma=\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-}
in Eq.(
40
).
In a finite-temperature environment, since we have

\Gamma_{+}=n_{B}(\Omega_{g})\gamma_{g}
and
\Gamma_{-}=[n_{B}(\Omega_{g})+1]\gamma_{g}
according to Eq.(
41
),
the dissipation rate is

\displaystyle\Gamma=[2n_{B}(\Omega_{g})+1]\gamma_{g}.

(49)

Therefore, the thermal effect only comes from the Bose distribution
n_{B}(\Omega_{g})=[\exp[\Omega_{g}/(k_{B}T)]-1]^{-1}
.
The parameter
1/(k_{B}T)
, where
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant, represents the inverse temperature assigned by a distant observer to the scalar field located at
\bm{R}
.
Here, we assume that an observer at the spatial infinity and another observer at
\bm{R}
each prepare a finite-temperature scalar-field environment at the same “local” temperature (
=T_{\text{p}}
).
We then ask how the temperature at
\mathbf{R}
is perceived by the observer at infinity, that is, ask the relation between
T
and
T_{\text{p}}
.
In general relativity, the temperature of a system in thermal equilibrium depends on the background spacetime metric.
In particular, in a stationary spacetime, the following relation called Tolman–Ehrenfest relation holds
Tolman
:

\displaystyle T_{\text{p}}=\frac{T}{\sqrt{-g_{00}(\bm{R})}}\approx\frac{T}{1+\Phi(\bm{R})}

(50)

Then, the Bose distribution
n_{B}(\Omega_{g})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle n_{B}(\Omega_{g})=\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{\Omega_{g}}{k_{B}T}\right)-1}=\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{\Omega}{k_{B}T_{\text{p}}}\right)-1}=n^{\text{p}}_{B}(\Omega),

so this result implies the distribution itself does not seem to have changed.

V
Conclusion and outlook

In this study, we investigated the energy-dissipative dynamics of a two-level atom in a weak gravitational field.
Using the Feynman–Vernon influence functional formalism, we derived a GKSL-form quantum master equation for the atom and evaluated its dissipation rate by solving the equation.
As a result, we found that gravitational modifications appear in the spontaneous emission rate, as shown in Eq. (
42
).
Furthermore, by analyzing the behavior of the dissipation rate, we identified a parameter region in which the rate is enhanced, as illustrated in Fig.
2
.
We also found that the reduction behavior of the rate differs between the low- and high-frequency limits, as summarized in Eq. (
46
).
These results indicate that environmental effects play important and non-trivial roles.
In particular, we suggest that vacuum fluctuations of a scalar field in a weak gravitational field are key to understanding our results.

As mentioned in Sec.
I
, several proposals have been made to detect gravitational effects using quantum systems.
With the remarkable progress of quantum sensing technologies in recent years, it is becoming increasingly feasible to detect even extremely weak gravitational influences.
If such experimental capabilities are realized, our research may provide a new theoretical pathway for the detection of gravitational waves and dark matter.
In addition, our results may also be applicable to tests of quantum field theory in curved spacetime, since it is considered that the phenomena discussed in this work are closely related to the properties of scalar fields, particularly their vacuum fluctuations.
We hope that our study may serve as a theoretical bridge toward the interface between quantum theory and gravity.

Appendix C
Solution of Eq.(
34
)

In Sec.
II
, we derived the Markovian QME of the two-level atom in the weak gravitational field of interest.
Here, we explicitly get the formal solution of the QME (
34
).
Using the eigenstates of the system Hamiltonian
\hat{H}^{(2)}_{\text{s}}
as

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(2)}_{\text{s}}|e\rangle=\frac{\Omega_{g}}{2}|e\rangle,\quad\hat{H}^{(2)}_{\text{s}}|g\rangle=-\frac{\Omega_{g}}{2}|g\rangle,

(70)

where
|e\rangle
and
|g\rangle
denote the excited and ground states, respectively,we can express the ladder operators in this basis as
\hat{\sigma}_{+}=|e\rangle\langle g|
and
\hat{\sigma}_{-}=|g\rangle\langle e|
.
Substituting these into Eq. (
34
), the Markovian QME can be expressed in the bra–ket representation as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{-}\Big[|g\rangle\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle\langle g|-\frac{1}{2}|e\rangle\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle\langle e|\Big]

\displaystyle+\Gamma_{+}\Big[|e\rangle\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle\langle e|-\frac{1}{2}|g\rangle\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle\langle g|\Big].

(71)

From Eq.(
71
), the time evolution of the matrix element
\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle
and
\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle
is obtained as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle

\displaystyle=-\Gamma_{-}\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle+\Gamma_{+}\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle,

(72)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle.

(73)

It is easy to solve Eq.(
73
), and the solution is

\displaystyle\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})t}\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|g\rangle,

(74)

and, because

\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle
is given by the complex conjugate of
\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle
, we also have

\displaystyle\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle^{*}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})t}\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle.

(75)

Because the trace of the density matrix,
\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)
, is unity, we have
\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle+\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle=1
, and then Eq.(
72
) is written as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle

\displaystyle=-\Gamma_{-}\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle+\Gamma_{+}(1-\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle)

(76)

The solution of this equation is given as

\displaystyle\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})t}\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle+\frac{\Gamma_{+}}{\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-}}(1-e^{-(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})t}),

(77)

and hence

\displaystyle\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|g\rangle

\displaystyle=1-\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)|e\rangle

\displaystyle=1-e^{-(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})t}\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle-\frac{\Gamma_{+}}{\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-}}(1-e^{-(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})t})

\displaystyle=e^{-(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})t}\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|g\rangle+\frac{\Gamma_{-}}{\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-}}(1-e^{-(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})t}).

(78)

Therefore the formal solution of the Markovian QME is

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}[\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle-a_{s}]e^{-\Gamma t}+a_{s}&\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|g\rangle\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma t}\\
\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma t}&e^{-\Gamma t}\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|g\rangle+(1-a_{s})(1-e^{-\Gamma t})\end{pmatrix},

(79)

where
\Gamma=\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-}
and
a_{s}=\Gamma_{+}/(\Gamma_{+}+\Gamma_{-})
.
When the initial condition of
\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}
is yielded by
\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle=1
,
\langle e|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|g\rangle=0=\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|e\rangle=\langle g|\hat{\rho}^{\text{I}}_{\text{s}}(0)|g\rangle
, we reproduce the solution given in Eq.(
39
).

12
), the source of the scalar field can be approximated as

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g(x)}J(\bm{q};x)\approx-[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]\lambda\bm{r}\cdot\nabla_{x}\delta^{3}(\bm{x}-\bm{R}).

(120)

Since the COM motion of the composite system is at rest, the scalar field is excited by the internal motion of the system.
We replace
\lambda\bm{r}(t)
with the effective dipole
[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]\bm{d}(t)
to respect the approximation (
30
).
The solution
\phi(x)
is perturbatively given as

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle\approx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}d^{4}y\,\Big(-G_{0}(x,y)[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})]\bm{d}\cdot\nabla_{y}\delta^{3}(\bm{y}-\bm{R})-\delta G(x,y)\bm{d}\cdot\nabla_{y}\delta^{3}(\bm{y}-\bm{R})\Big)

\displaystyle=-\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}d\tau\,\Big([1+2\Phi(\bm{R})]\bm{d}(\tau)\cdot\nabla_{R}G_{0}(t,\bm{x},\tau,\bm{R})+\bm{d}(\tau)\cdot\nabla_{R}\delta G(t,\bm{x},\tau,\bm{R})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})]\Big\{\frac{\bm{x-R}}{|\bm{x}-\bm{R}|^{3}}\cdot\bm{d}(t-|\bm{x}-\bm{R}|)+\frac{\bm{x-R}}{|\bm{x}-\bm{R}|^{2}}\cdot\dot{\bm{d}}(t-|\bm{x}-\bm{R}|)\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\frac{\Phi(\bm{z})}{|\bm{x}-\bm{z}|}\Big\{\frac{\bm{z}-\bm{R}}{|\bm{z}-\bm{R}|^{3}}\cdot\ddot{\bm{d}}(t-|\bm{x}-\bm{z}|-|\bm{z}-\bm{R}|)+\frac{\bm{z}-\bm{R}}{|\bm{z}-\bm{R}|^{2}}\cdot\dddot{\bm{d}}(t-|\bm{x}-\bm{z}|-|\bm{z}-\bm{R}|)\Big\}.

(121)

Next, we compute the power of the scalar radiation emitted by the internal motion of the composite system.
The radiation power
P
is defined by

\displaystyle P=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int d^{2}\hat{r}\,r^{2}\,T^{r0}(\bm{r}),

(122)

where
T^{r0}=-\partial_{t}\phi(x)\partial_{r}\phi(x)
denotes the radial energy flux, and
d^{2}\hat{r}
is the solid-angle element associated with the spatial coordinate
\bm{r}
.
By setting
\bm{r}=\bm{x}-\bm{R}
, the solution
\phi
is written as

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})]\Big\{\frac{\bm{r}}{r^{3}}\cdot\bm{d}(t-r)+\frac{\bm{r}}{r^{2}}\cdot\dot{\bm{d}}(t-r)\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\frac{\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})}{|\bm{r}-\bm{z}|}\Big\{\frac{\bm{z}}{z^{3}}\cdot\ddot{\bm{d}}(t-|\bm{r}-\bm{z}|-z)+\frac{\bm{z}}{z^{2}}\cdot\dddot{\bm{d}}(t-|\bm{r}-\bm{z}|-z)\Big\},

(123)

where we changed the integral variables as
\bm{z}\rightarrow\bm{z}-\bm{R}
, and
z=|\bm{z}|
.
Since we are interested in the scalar radiation dissipating sufficiently far away, we should consider the limit that
r=|\bm{r}|
is very large.
In the large
r
, the solution of
\phi
is

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{4\pi r}[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})]\,\hat{r}\cdot\dot{\bm{d}}(t-r)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})\Big\{\frac{\bm{z}}{z^{3}}\cdot\ddot{\bm{d}}(t-r-z+\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z})+\frac{\bm{z}}{z^{2}}\cdot\dddot{\bm{d}}(t-r-z+\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z})\Big\},

(124)

where
\hat{r}=\bm{r}/r
.
Because of this result, the radial energy flux in the large
r
is evaluated up to the first order of
\Phi
as

\displaystyle T^{r0}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}\phi(x)\partial_{r}\phi(x)

\displaystyle\approx\Big[\frac{1}{4\pi r}[1+2\Phi(\bm{R})]\,\hat{r}\cdot\ddot{\bm{d}}(t-r)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})\Big\{\frac{\bm{z}}{z^{3}}\cdot\dddot{\bm{d}}(t-r-z+\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z})+\frac{\bm{z}}{z^{2}}\cdot\ddddot{\bm{d}}(t-r-z+\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z})\Big\}\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}r^{2}}[1+4\Phi(\bm{R})]\,[\hat{r}\cdot\ddot{\bm{d}}(t-r)]^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{8\pi^{3}r^{2}}\hat{r}\cdot\ddot{\bm{d}}(t-r)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})\Big\{\frac{\bm{z}}{z^{3}}\cdot\dddot{\bm{d}}(t-r-z+\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z})+\frac{\bm{z}}{z^{2}}\cdot\ddddot{\bm{d}}(t-r-z+\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z})\Big\}.

(125)

Here, we assume the oscillating dipole

\displaystyle\bm{d}(t)=\bm{d}\cos(\Omega_g t),

(126)

where
\Omega_{g}=[1+\Phi(\bm{R})]\Omega
.
To evaluate the power
P
, we perform a time average and neglect the oscillatory terms with the period
2\pi/\Omega_{g}
from the radial energy flux.
Practically, ignoring
\cos(2\Omega_g (t-r))
,
\sin[2\Omega_{g}(t-r)+\Omega_{g}(\hat{\bm{r}}\cdot\bm{x^{\prime}}-|\bm{x^{\prime}}|)]
and
\cos[2\Omega_{g}(t-r)+\Omega_{g}(\hat{\bm{r}}\cdot\bm{x^{\prime}}-|\bm{x^{\prime}}|)]
appearing in (
125
), we get the following radial energy flux,

\displaystyle T^{r0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{4}_{g}}{32\pi^{2}r^{2}}[1+4\Phi(\bm{R})]\,(\hat{r}\cdot\bm{d})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{16\pi^{3}r^{2}}\hat{r}\cdot\bm{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})\Big\{\frac{\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d}}{z^{3}}\Omega^{5}_{g}\sin[\Omega_{g}(\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z}-z)]+\frac{\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d}}{z^{2}}\Omega^{6}_{g}\cos[\Omega_{g}(\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z}-z)]\Big\}.

(127)

Using the following relations,

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\hat{r}\,(\hat{r}\cdot\bm{d})^{2}=\frac{4\pi d^{2}}{3},

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\hat{r}\,\hat{r}\cdot\bm{d}\,\sin[\Omega_{g}(\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z}-z)]=-4\pi\frac{\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d}}{z}\Big[\cos(\Omega_g z)-\frac{\sin(\Omega_g z)}{\Omega_{g}z}\Big]\frac{\cos(\Omega_g z)}{\Omega_{g}z},

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\hat{r}\,\hat{r}\cdot\bm{d}\,\cos[\Omega_{g}(\hat{r}\cdot\bm{z}-z)]=-4\pi\frac{\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d}}{z}\Big[\cos(\Omega_g z)-\frac{\sin(\Omega_g z)}{\Omega_{g}z}\Big]\frac{\sin(\Omega_g z)}{\Omega_{g}z},

we can get the radiation power
P
as

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int d^{2}\hat{r}\,r^{2}\,T^{r0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{4}_{g}}{32\pi^{2}}[1+4\Phi(\bm{R})]\,\frac{4\pi d^{2}}{3}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{16\pi^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})\Big\{\frac{\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d}}{z^{3}}\Omega^{5}_{g}\Big(-4\pi\frac{\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d}}{z}\Big[\cos(\Omega_g z)-\frac{\sin(\Omega_g z)}{\Omega_{g}z}\Big]\frac{\cos(\Omega_g z)}{\Omega_{g}z}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d}}{z^{2}}\Omega^{6}_{g}\Big(-4\pi\frac{\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d}}{z}\Big[\cos(\Omega_g z)-\frac{\sin(\Omega_g z)}{\Omega_{g}z}\Big]\frac{\sin(\Omega_g z)}{\Omega_{g}z}\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{4}_{g}d^{2}}{24\pi}[1+4\Phi(\bm{R})]+\frac{\Omega^{3}_{g}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})(\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d})^{2}\frac{\Omega_{g}z\cos(\Omega_g z)-\sin(\Omega_g z)}{z^{3}}\frac{\cos(\Omega_g z)+\Omega_{g}z\sin(\Omega_g z)}{z^{3}}.

(128)

Substituting
\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})=-GM/|\bm{z}+\bm{R}|=R\Phi(\bm{R})/|\bm{z}+\bm{R}|
into the second term of
P
, we can perform the
\bm{z}
integral as

\displaystyle(\text{Second term of }P)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{3}_{g}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}z\Phi(\bm{z}+\bm{R})(\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d})^{2}\frac{\Omega_{g}z\cos(\Omega_g z)-\sin(\Omega_g z)}{z^{3}}\frac{\cos(\Omega_g z)+\Omega_{g}z\sin(\Omega_g z)}{z^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{3}_{g}R\Phi(\bm{R})}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{d^{3}z}{|\bm{z}+\bm{R}|}(\bm{z}\cdot\bm{d})^{2}\frac{\Omega_{g}z\cos(\Omega_g z)-\sin(\Omega_g z)}{z^{3}}\frac{\cos(\Omega_g z)+\Omega_{g}z\sin(\Omega_g z)}{z^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{3}_{g}R\Phi(\bm{R})}{4\pi^{2}}d_{k}d_{\ell}F_{k\ell}(\bm{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{3}_{g}R\Phi(\bm{R})}{4\pi^{2}}d_{k}d_{\ell}\Big[-\frac{\pi\Omega_{g}}{3R}f_{1}(R\Omega_{g})\delta_{k\ell}-\frac{\pi\Omega_{g}}{2R^{3}}f_{2}(R\Omega_{g})(R_{k}R_{\ell}-R^{2}\delta_{k\ell})\Big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Omega^{4}_{g}d^{2}\Phi(\bm{R})}{24\pi}\Big[2f_{1}(R\Omega_{g})+3\frac{(\bm{d}\cdot\bm{R})^{2}-d^{2}R^{2}}{d^{2}R^{2}}f_{2}(R\Omega_{g})\Big],

(129)

where we used
F_{k\ell}
defined in (
104
) in the third equality and substituted its explicit form (
108
) in the fourth equality, respectively.
Using the form of the second term of
P
, we finally find the following
P/\Omega_{g}
up to
O(\Phi)
as

\displaystyle\frac{P}{\Omega_{g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega^{3}_{g}d^{2}}{24\pi}[1+4\Phi(\bm{R})]-\frac{\Omega^{3}_{g}d^{2}\Phi(\bm{R})}{24\pi}\Big[2f_{1}(R\Omega_{g})+3\frac{(\bm{d}\cdot\bm{R})^{2}-d^{2}R^{2}}{d^{2}R^{2}}f_{2}(R\Omega_{g})\Big]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\Omega^{3}d^{2}}{24\pi}\Big[1+7\Phi(\bm{R})-2\Phi(\bm{R})f_{1}(R\Omega)-3\frac{(\bm{d}\cdot\bm{R})^{2}-d^{2}R^{2}}{d^{2}R^{2}}\Phi(\bm{R})f_{2}(R\Omega)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{g}.

(130)

Hence, the energy dissipation rate of the dipole,
P/\Omega_{g}
, is proportional to the spontaneous emission rate
\gamma_{g}
given in (
42
).
