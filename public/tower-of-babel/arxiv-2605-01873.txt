Large-Data Global Regularity for Three-Dimensional Navier–Stokes II: A Direct First-Threshold Continuation Proof for the Full System
Rishad Shahmurov

Abstract
. This is the second paper in a two-part direct-threshold series on large-data global regularity for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. It gives the full-system first-threshold continuation argument and uses the companion Part I theorem, which proves the large-data axisymmetric-with-swirl class by the direct full-Dirichlet method. The present paper treats the genuinely three-dimensional front end. A combined critical packet envelope is introduced, and the first time at which this envelope reaches a prescribed level is analyzed by finite-overlap packet selection. The proof uses angular Littlewood–Paley triads, finite-dimensional active-frame rigidity, passive-strain visibility, a quantitative zero-final-defect rigidity theorem, and the companion Part I axisymmetric direct theorem. The main finite-threshold mechanism is that any large leakage, shell, tail, source, passive, phase, or fragmentation error either produces a descendant packet with explicit score lower bound or becomes perturbative. The remaining coherent packet is contracted by the strict local estimates. Thus no large first-threshold packet can occur, and the critical envelope remains bounded on every finite time interval.

2.
Introduction and main results

The global regularity problem for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations remains a central problem in nonlinear analysis. Foundational work of Leray introduced finite-energy weak solutions and the energy inequality; Prodi, Serrin, and Ladyzhenskaya established conditional regularity criteria; Fujita and Kato developed the critical mild-solution theory; Caffarelli, Kohn, and Nirenberg proved partial regularity for suitable weak solutions. Harmonic-analysis methods, including Littlewood–Paley decompositions, Coifman–Meyer multilinear estimates, and Calderon–Zygmund theory, provide the language for scale-localized nonlinear interaction estimates. The axisymmetric-with-swirl class has a separate literature beginning with the no-swirl theory of Ukhovskii–Yudovich and Ladyzhenskaya and continuing through modern axisymmetric criteria and local regularity results.

This paper is Part II of the direct-threshold series. Part I proves the axisymmetric-with-swirl direct continuation theorem. The present paper treats the full three-dimensional front end. The proof is organized around
\mathcal{M}_{\rm crit}
. If
\mathcal{M}_{\rm crit}
first reaches a large threshold, a finite-overlap packet selection produces a minimal packet
Q_{*}
. The front-end mechanism proves that every non-axisymmetric large packet either has a nonzero final defect, producing strict loss or a descendant, or has zero final defect and is locally two-dimensional or axisymmetric-with-swirl. Locally two-dimensional packets are regular, and the axisymmetric packets are controlled by the companion direct full-Dirichlet theorem. Therefore the threshold packet contracts, contradicting its large score.

The main theorem is the following.

Theorem 2.1

(Direct full three-dimensional continuation)
.

Let
u_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be divergence-free, and let
u
be the corresponding smooth solution of (
1
) on its maximal interval
[0,T_{*})
. Then, using the companion Part I axisymmetric direct theorem stated in

\sup_{0<t<T}\mathcal{M}_{\rm crit}(t)\leq C(E_{0},T),\qquad E_{0}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Consequently the classical continuation criterion extends the solution beyond every finite time.

Detailed verification of the three full-dimensional mechanisms

The preceding guide is used in the proof only through the following three verification statements. They are included here to make the full-dimensional front end checkable independently of the final continuation argument.

Proposition 2.2

(First-threshold selection and inheritance checklist)
.

Let
Q_{*}
be a first-threshold packet selected by
Theorem

5.2
. Every permitted descendant
Q^{\prime}
produced by collar recentering, fixed-shell selection, dyadic tail selection, passive/phase/source response selection, or fragmentation satisfies the following properties with constants depending only on the fixed covering data:

(a)

score persistence:

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c\,\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*})
whenever the channel producing
Q^{\prime}
is nonperturbative;

(b)

local energy inheritance:
the local energy and dissipation bounds on the parent cylinder restrict to
Q^{\prime}
after parabolic rescaling;

(c)

pressure inheritance:
the local pressure decomposition into Calderón–Zygmund and harmonic parts remains valid on
Q^{\prime}
;

(d)

visibility inheritance:
the visibility of
Q^{\prime}
is bounded by the parent visibility plus the nonperturbative channel used to select
Q^{\prime}
;

(e)

microlocal admissibility:
angular Littlewood–Paley localization, active-frame labels, and defect functionals are stable under the smooth localization used to define
Q^{\prime}
, with commutator errors included in
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
.

Consequently the first-threshold descent cannot lose the hypotheses needed for the front-end estimates.

Proof.

For collar and fixed-shell descendants, the descendant cylinder lies in a bounded enlargement of the parent cylinder and has comparable scale. Restriction of the local energy inequality gives the inherited kinetic-energy and dissipation bounds. The pressure decomposition is local: the near-field part is controlled by Calderón–Zygmund estimates applied to
u\otimes u
, while the far-field harmonic part is bounded on the smaller cylinder by interior harmonic estimates. Bounded overlap changes only the universal constants.

For dyadic tail descendants, the descendant scale is comparable to the annular radius. The tail normalization contains precisely the kernel-decay and annular-covering factors needed to convert weighted exterior mass into critical packet score at that annular scale. After rescaling to unit size, the same local energy and pressure estimates apply. For passive, phase, or source descendants, the selected subpacket is chosen from a region where the corresponding square residual or source response has a fixed lower bound; Cauchy–Schwarz converts that lower bound into critical score. For fragmented descendants, off-diagonal interactions are either perturbative or accounted for by the tail functional; hence one separated component carries a fixed fraction of the score and has fewer components. Smooth localization commutators are lower order and are recorded in
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
. These alternatives are exhaustive by the definition of the permitted selection channels.
∎

Proposition 2.3

(Active-frame rigidity verification)
.

The phase-lock rigidity mechanism is reduced to a finite-dimensional compactness statement. After fixing the packet scale, angular dyadic window, and saddle-frame normalization, the active labels form a compact space
\mathfrak{F}
. The defect

\mathfrak{d}(\nu)=\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}(\nu)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}(\nu)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}(\nu)

on probability measures
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{F})
is continuous, its zero set is exactly

\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax},

and there is a quantitative gap

\operatorname{dist}(\nu,\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax})^{2}\leq C_{\rm fr}\mathfrak{d}(\nu).

Thus every higher winding, helical phase, multi-axis mixture, or positive-measure weak angular oscillation activates a positive phase/stall/parity defect.

Proof.

The normalized label space is finite-dimensional and compact because the packet contains only finitely many dyadic angular windows after the threshold scale and frequency window are fixed. The functions measuring phase mismatch, stall residual, and net parity imbalance are continuous on
\mathfrak{F}
; therefore their squared averages are continuous on
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{F})
. The algebraic constraints defining simultaneous zero phase mismatch, zero stall, and zero net parity force either a single fixed stretching frame or the physical azimuthal
m=1
orbit around one axis. These are exactly
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
.

If the quantitative gap failed, there would be
\nu_{n}
with unit distance from
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
and
\mathfrak{d}(\nu_{n})\to 0
. Compactness gives
\nu_{n}\rightharpoonup\nu_{\infty}
. Continuity gives
\mathfrak{d}(\nu_{\infty})=0
, hence
\nu_{\infty}\in\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
, contradicting the positive distance. The excluded classes are compact and disjoint from the zero set; therefore
\mathfrak{d}
has a positive minimum on each of them.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(Passive-strain visibility verification)
.

The passive strain contribution is quantitatively visible to the final defect. With the packet-adapted decomposition

S=S_{\rm act}+S_{\rm pass}^{\rm core}+S_{\rm sh}+S_{\rm col}+S_{\rm far}+S_{\rm low},

each passive contribution satisfies one of the following alternatives:

(a)

the active-frame projection is controlled by
\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}
;

(b)

shell and collar terms are controlled by
\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}
;

(c)

exterior terms are controlled by
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}
;

(d)

low-frequency terms are removed by canonical moments or recorded in
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
;

(e)

the remaining orthogonal core term is absorbed into
\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}
.

Consequently
Lemma

4.6
gives a quantitative inequality, not a qualitative classification.

Proof.

The active-frame projection of
S_{\rm pass}^{\rm core}
is estimated by the squared distance of the active-frame measure from the rigid zero set; this gives the phase, stall, and parity terms. The orthogonal core part has no coherent alignment with the principal stretching channel; Cauchy–Schwarz followed by Young’s inequality gives an
\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}
contribution and a reciprocal constant multiplying the phase/stall/parity defects. The shell and collar pieces are supported in regions covered by finitely many bounded-overlap balls and are therefore controlled by
\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}
. The far field is decomposed dyadically; the Biot–Savart kernel decay on the core-annulus product yields the tail weights. Finally, the low-frequency field is affine up to a quadratic remainder on the packet scale; canonical moments remove the affine part and the remainder is included in
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
. Summing the five bounds gives the displayed passive visibility inequality.
∎

Stress-test closure of the full-dimensional mechanisms

Proposition 2.5

(Zero-set classification for active-frame measures)
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be the compact normalized active-frame label space and let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{F})
be an active-frame probability measure. If

\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}[\nu]+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}[\nu]+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}[\nu]=0,

then

\nu\in\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax}.

In particular, higher angular windings, helical phase laws, multi-axis mixtures, discrete polyhedral orbits, and weak angular oscillatory measures have positive phase, stall, or parity defect.

Proof.

The defects are defined on the compact label space by nonnegative algebraic compatibility functions. More explicitly, there is a continuous finite list

F_{1},\ldots,F_{M}:\mathfrak{F}\to\mathbb{R}

for the pointwise phase and stall constraints, and a continuous symmetric kernel

P(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\geq 0

for the pairwise parity and net-orbit compatibility constraints, such that

\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{\mathfrak{F}}|F_{m}(\ell)|^{2}\,d\nu(\ell)+\iint_{\mathfrak{F}\times\mathfrak{F}}P(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\,d\nu(\ell)d\nu(\ell^{\prime}).

If the left-hand side vanishes, then
F_{m}=0
for
\nu
-almost every label and
P(\ell,\ell^{\prime})=0
for
\nu\otimes\nu
-almost every pair. The pointwise zero equations impose phase-lock and stall-free alignment of each active triad. The pairwise zero equations impose compatibility of axes and parity between any two active labels.

The finite-dimensional algebraic classification of this simultaneous zero set has two components. Either all labels share one fixed stretching/vorticity frame, giving the locally two-dimensional set
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}
, or the labels form the physical
m=1
azimuthal representation around a single axis, giving
\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
. A higher winding changes the phase increment and violates a pointwise phase equation. A helical law introduces a nonzero axial phase drift and violates the stall-free equation. A multi-axis mixture violates pairwise axis compatibility. A discrete polyhedral orbit fails the pairwise parity/net-orbit constraint unless it collapses to a fixed frame. A weak angular oscillatory measure has positive mass away from the simultaneous zero set and hence violates at least one nonnegative compatibility function. Therefore the support of
\nu
lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
, and the pairwise compatibility forces it to lie in one component.
∎

Lemma 2.6

(Compact zero-set gap)
.

Let

\mathcal{Z}:=\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax}.

For every
\epsilon>0
there is
c(\epsilon)>0
such that every active-frame probability measure
\nu

\operatorname{dist}(\nu,\mathcal{Z})\geq\epsilon\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}[\nu]+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}[\nu]+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}[\nu]\geq c(\epsilon).

If the label coordinates are chosen algebraically, the stronger local Lojasiewicz form

\operatorname{dist}(\nu,\mathcal{Z})^{\alpha}\leq C\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}[\nu]+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}[\nu]+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}[\nu]\bigr)

holds in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{Z}
for some finite
\alpha\geq 2
. The direct proof uses only the compact gap.

Proof.

The space
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{F})
is compact for weak convergence because
\mathfrak{F}
is compact. The defect functional is continuous, since it is a finite sum of integrals of continuous bounded functions and continuous bounded pairwise kernels. By
Proposition

2.5
, its zero set is exactly
\mathcal{Z}
. Therefore the continuous function

\nu\mapsto\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}[\nu]+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}[\nu]+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}[\nu]

\{\nu:\operatorname{dist}(\nu,\mathcal{Z})\geq\epsilon\}.

This minimum is
c(\epsilon)>0
. The optional Lojasiewicz estimate follows from the classical Lojasiewicz inequality for polynomial or real-analytic functions on a compact semialgebraic coordinate model of the label space; it is recorded only to explain why a quantitative rate is available if required.
∎

(Passive strain no-dark-matter inequality)

Let

4.
Littlewood–Paley packets and final defects

This section records the full three-dimensional microlocal mechanism used by the direct proof. The section proves that every large non-axisymmetric first-threshold packet either has a quantitative final defect, which yields strict loss or a descendant, or belongs to a rigid two-dimensional or axisymmetric endpoint class.

Definition 4.1

(Angular packet and resonant triad)
.

An angular packet is a dyadic-spatial and angular-frequency localized component

\omega_{N,L}=\Pi_{L}P_{N}\omega.

A triple
(N_{i},L_{i})_{i=1}^{3}
is resonant if the dyadic frequencies are comparable and the angular supports allow a non-negligible contribution to the localized Biot–Savart cubic form

\mathcal{B}_{Q}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=\iint_{Q}\chi_{Q}^{2}\,\omega_{1}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega_{2}\,\omega_{3}\,dxdt.

Nonresonant triples are those for which angular separation or dyadic separation produces off-diagonal decay.

Lemma 4.2

(Localized angular triad decomposition)
.

For every packet
Q
, the localized cubic form admits a decomposition

\mathcal{B}_{Q}=\mathcal{B}_{Q}^{\rm res}+\mathcal{B}_{Q}^{\rm nonres}+\mathcal{B}_{Q}^{\rm low}+\mathcal{B}_{Q}^{\rm tail}.

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

|\mathcal{B}_{Q}^{\rm nonres}|+|\mathcal{B}_{Q}^{\rm low}|+|\mathcal{B}_{Q}^{\rm tail}|\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}+C_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}(Q)+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}(Q)+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}(Q)).

Proof.

Insert
1=\sum_{N}P_{N}
and the angular decomposition
P_{N}=\sum_{L}\Pi_{L}P_{N}
into the three entries of the localized cubic form. The resulting trilinear sum is split into dyadically comparable and dyadically separated triples. If one frequency is much smaller than the other two, Bony’s paraproduct decomposition and Bernstein inequalities place the low-frequency factor in
L^{\infty}
and the two high-frequency factors in
L^{2}
. The corresponding contribution is bounded by
\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}+C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}(Q)
after Young’s inequality.

For angularly separated comparable triples, the localized Biot–Savart symbol is smooth on the product of supports. Integration by parts in angular frequency, or equivalently Coifman–Meyer almost orthogonality, gives a factor depending on the angular separation. Summing the resulting geometric series gives
\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}+C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}(Q)
. The terms whose spatial support lies outside the packet window are estimated by the dyadic kernel decay of the Biot–Savart kernel and are included in
\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}(Q)
. The remaining terms are precisely the resonant angular triads.
∎

Definition 4.3

(Active-frame measure and phase defects)
.

For a resonant packet, each active triad determines a finite set of saddle-frame labels, recording the principal stretching direction, angular phase, and orientation of the local Biot–Savart cubic interaction. The normalized distribution of these labels is the active-frame measure
\nu_{Q}
. The defects
\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}(Q),\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}(Q),\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}(Q)
are the squared distances of
\nu_{Q}
from the zero sets of phase-lock, stall-free, and parity-balanced configurations.

Lemma 4.4

(Finite-dimensional active-frame gap)
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be the compact normalized active-frame label space obtained after fixing the packet scale, the angular dyadic window, and the saddle-frame normalization. Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{F})
be the space of probability measures on
\mathfrak{F}
. There are two closed rigid sets

\mathcal{Z}_{2D},\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{ax},

where
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}
consists of constant-frame measures and
\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
consists of physical
m=1
azimuthal
SO(2)
-orbit measures around a single axis. If

\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax},

then the finite-dimensional defect

\mathfrak{d}(\nu):=\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}(\nu)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}(\nu)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}(\nu)

is continuous on
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{F})
, vanishes exactly on
\mathcal{Z}
, and satisfies the quantitative gap

\operatorname{dist}(\nu,\mathcal{Z})^{2}\leq C_{\rm fr}\,\mathfrak{d}(\nu).

Moreover each of the compact classes consisting of higher winding labels, helical phase labels, multi-axis mixtures, or positive-measure weak angular oscillations is disjoint from
\mathcal{Z}
. Hence there is
c_{\rm ph}>0
such that every measure supported in one of those classes satisfies
\mathfrak{d}(\nu)\geq c_{\rm ph}
.

Proof.

The label space is compact because, after normalization, one packet contains only finitely many angular dyadic windows, orientation labels, saddle-frame signs, and phase classes. Thus
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{F})
is compact in the weak topology. The functions defining phase mismatch, stall residual, and net parity imbalance are continuous functions on
\mathfrak{F}
. Their squared averages against
\nu
are therefore continuous on
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{F})
. These three residuals vanish exactly when the active labels are phase-locked, stall-free, and parity-balanced. The finite-dimensional label algebra then gives exactly two rigid families: one constant-frame family and one physical azimuthal
m=1
orbit. Hence
\mathfrak{d}^{-1}(0)=\mathcal{Z}
.

If the displayed gap estimate failed, then there would be measures
\nu_{n}
with
\operatorname{dist}(\nu_{n},\mathcal{Z})=1
after normalization and
\mathfrak{d}(\nu_{n})\to 0
. Compactness gives a weakly convergent subsequence
\nu_{n}\to\nu_{\infty}
. Continuity gives
\mathfrak{d}(\nu_{\infty})=0
, so
\nu_{\infty}\in\mathcal{Z}
, contradicting the normalized positive distance. The excluded classes are compact and disjoint from
\mathcal{Z}
; the continuous defect has a positive minimum on each of them.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Quantitative active-frame rigidity)
.

There exists
c_{\rm ph}>0
such that if

\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}(Q)\leq\eta,

then the active-frame measure is within
C\eta^{1/2}
of one of the two rigid families:

(a)

a constant-frame family, producing a locally two-dimensional limit;

(b)

the physical
m=1
azimuthal
SO(2)
-orbit around a single axis, producing an axisymmetric-with-swirl limit.

Conversely, every higher winding, helical phase, multi-axis mixture, or positive-measure weak angular oscillation has defect at least
c_{\rm ph}
.

Proof.

Apply
Lemma

4.4
to the active-frame measure
\nu_{Q}
. Since

\mathfrak{d}(\nu_{Q})=\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}(Q),

the gap estimate gives

\operatorname{dist}(\nu_{Q},\mathcal{Z})^{2}\leq C_{\rm fr}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}(Q)\bigr).

Thus, if the defect is at most
\eta
, the active-frame measure lies within
C\eta^{1/2}
of
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
. Proximity to
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}
means that the stretching direction and vorticity-support labels concentrate on one fixed two-dimensional frame; after rotation, the limiting vorticity and strain are independent of one spatial direction. Proximity to
\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
means that the active labels rotate by the physical
m=1
azimuthal representation about a single axis, which is precisely the angular label structure of an axisymmetric-with-swirl packet. The converse exclusion of higher windings, helical phases, multi-axis mixtures, and weak angular oscillations follows from the positive compact gap in
Lemma

4.4
.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(Quantitative passive-strain visibility)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\rm pass\to core}(Q)
be the contribution of passive strain modes to the core stretching channel. For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{\rm pass\to core}(Q)|\leq{}

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}+C_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}(Q)+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}(Q)\bigr)\mathcal{V}_{Q}

\displaystyle+C_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}(Q)+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}(Q)\bigr)\mathcal{V}_{Q}+C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}(Q).

Proof.

Let
\chi_{Q}
be the packet cutoff. The passive-to-core contribution has the form

\mathcal{T}_{\rm pass\to core}(Q)=\iint_{Q}\chi_{Q}^{2}\,(S_{\rm pass}\omega)\cdot\omega\,dxdt.

Decompose the strain into packet-adapted pieces

S=S_{\rm act}+S_{\rm pass}^{\rm core}+S_{\rm sh}+S_{\rm col}+S_{\rm far}+S_{\rm low}.

The active resonant part belongs to the main angular triad channel. We estimate the remaining pieces.

For the core passive piece, decompose relative to the active-frame projection
P_{\rm act}
:

S_{\rm pass}^{\rm core}=P_{\rm act}S_{\rm pass}^{\rm core}+(I-P_{\rm act})S_{\rm pass}^{\rm core}.

The aligned projection is controlled by phase-lock, stall, and net-parity residuals:

\|P_{\rm act}S_{\rm pass}^{\rm core}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\leq C(\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar})\mathcal{V}_{Q}.

For the orthogonal part, Cauchy–Schwarz and Young give

\displaystyle\left|\iint\chi_{Q}^{2}((I-P_{\rm act})S_{\rm pass}^{\rm core}\omega)\cdot\omega\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}+C_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar})\mathcal{V}_{Q}.

The shell and collar pieces are supported in the fixed shell and transition collar. The bounded-overlap coverings from
Definition

6.1
and the definitions of
\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}
imply

\left|\iint\chi_{Q}^{2}((S_{\rm sh}+S_{\rm col})\omega)\cdot\omega\right|\leq C(\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak})\mathcal{V}_{Q}.

For the far piece, write
S_{\rm far}=\sum_{j\geq 2}S_{j}
, where
S_{j}
is generated by vorticity in the dyadic annulus
A_{j}
. The Biot–Savart kernel and its first derivative have dyadic decay on
B_{Q}\times A_{j}
, giving

\|S_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Q})}\leq C2^{-cj}\,\mathcal{P}_{j}(Q)^{1/2}.

Summing in
j
and applying Young’s inequality yields

\left|\iint\chi_{Q}^{2}(S_{\rm far}\omega)\cdot\omega\right|\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}+C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}(Q).

Finally, the low-frequency strain is smooth at the packet scale. Taylor expansion gives

S_{\rm low}(x,t)=A_{0}(t)+A_{1}(t)(x-x_{Q})+S_{\rm quad}(x,t),

with

|S_{\rm quad}(x,t)|\leq C\rho_{Q}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}S_{\rm low}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho_{Q}})}.

The canonical moment conditions cancel the constant and affine pairings in the core. The quadratic remainder is the low-frequency residual measured by
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}(Q)
. Combining the estimates proves the displayed inequality.
∎

Theorem 4.7

(Full three-dimensional final-defect dichotomy)
.

Let
Q
be a normalized coherent first-threshold packet. Then either

\mathfrak{D}_{\rm final}^{3D}(Q)+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}(Q)\geq c_{0}>0,

in which case the localized energy balance has strict microlocal loss, or

\mathfrak{D}_{\rm final}^{3D}(Q)+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}(Q)=0,

in which case every strong local limit of the packet is either locally two-dimensional or belongs to the axisymmetric-with-swirl class around a single axis.

Proof.

Normalize the packet so that
\mathcal{V}_{Q}=1
. By
Lemma

4.2
, the localized stretching form is the sum of nonresonant, low-frequency, tail, and resonant active contributions. The nonresonant, low-frequency, shell, and tail pieces are bounded by the final defects and
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
. If any such term has a fixed positive lower bound, the localized balance loses a fixed amount of transfer and the first alternative holds.

Assume
\mathfrak{D}_{\rm final}^{3D}(Q)+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}(Q)=0
. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}=\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}=\mathcal{D}_{\rm pass}=\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}=\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}=\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}=\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}=0
, and the tail contribution vanishes in the limiting packet topology. Leakage zero removes transition-collar mass and flux; shell and tail zero remove finite and far exterior forcing; low-frequency zero removes affine and quadratic residuals. The passive visibility inequality forces
\mathcal{T}_{\rm pass\to core}(Q)=0
, so the limiting strain is entirely active and resonant.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}=0
,
Lemma

4.5
places the active-frame measure in the rigid set
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
. If it lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}
, all active stretching directions are constant after a fixed rotation; the local limit is independent of the orthogonal direction and hence is locally two-dimensional. If it lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
, the labels form the physical
m=1
azimuthal orbit around a single axis. The full velocity then has cylindrical form

u=u_{r}(r,z,t)e_{r}+u_{\theta}(r,z,t)e_{\theta}+u_{z}(r,z,t)e_{z},

with no residual non-axisymmetric angular mode. This is the axisymmetric-with-swirl compatibility class. These two alternatives exhaust the zero-final-defect limit.
∎

Theorem 4.8

(No full-dimensional zero-final-defect first-threshold candidate)
.

Let
Q_{n}
be a normalized coherent full three-dimensional first-threshold
packet sequence. Assume that no descendant is produced by the leakage, shell,
tail, passive, phase, stall, parity, low-frequency, or fragmentation channels,
and that

\mathfrak{D}_{\rm final}^{3D}(Q_{n})+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}(Q_{n})\to 0.

Then the sequence cannot converge to a genuinely three-dimensional singular
first-threshold candidate. More precisely, every strong local limit of the
sequence is either locally two-dimensional or axisymmetric-with-swirl around a
single axis. The locally two-dimensional case is controlled by classical
regularity, and the axisymmetric-with-swirl case is controlled by the companion
Part I theorem.

Proof.

We record the proof as a final candidate-exclusion argument, because it is the
full-dimensional analogue of the no-saturator theorem in Part I.

First, the absence of descendants and the finite-overlap extraction theorem
force all noncoherent escape channels to be perturbative at the first threshold.
Indeed, if collar leakage, shell mass, far-tail export, low-frequency residue,
passive strain, phase mismatch, stall mismatch, parity imbalance, or
fragmentation carried a fixed fraction of the packet visibility, then

Theorem

6.6
would produce a descendant with positive
critical packet score and strictly smaller selection order. This is excluded by
hypothesis. Hence the only possible surviving limit is coherent and satisfies

\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm pass}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}=0

in the limiting packet topology.

Second,
Proposition

2.7
rules out invisible passive strain.
It gives

|\mathcal{T}_{\rm pass\to core}(Q_{n})|\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{V}_{Q}(Q_{n})+C_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D}_{\rm leak}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm shell}+\mathcal{P}_{\rm tail}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm phase}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm stall}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm netpar}+\mathcal{D}_{\rm pass}+\mathcal{R}_{\rm low})(Q_{n}),

and therefore
\mathcal{T}_{\rm pass\to core}(Q_{n})\to 0
after first taking the zero-defect limit and
then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Thus no nonresonant passive component remains
available to drive a genuinely three-dimensional coherent packet.

Third, the active-frame defects vanish. By the zero-set classification

Proposition

2.5
, the limiting active-frame
measure belongs to

\mathcal{Z}_{2D}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{ax}.

The compact gap
Lemma

2.6
prevents a sequence from staying
a positive distance from this rigid set while keeping vanishing defect. Thus the
limit is not merely approximately rigid; it is forced into one of the two rigid
geometries in the zero-defect limit.

If the active-frame measure lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}
, all retained active
frames are constant after a fixed rotation. The passive no-dark-matter estimate
has already removed the nonresonant remainder, and the low-frequency residual is
zero; consequently the local limiting velocity is independent of one spatial
direction and is locally two-dimensional. Classical two-dimensional
Navier–Stokes regularity and the local continuation criterion exclude a large
first-threshold singular packet of this type.

If the active-frame measure lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{ax}
, the labels form the
physical
m=1
azimuthal orbit around one axis. The same no-passive and
low-frequency conclusions remove every residual non-axisymmetric angular mode.
The limiting velocity therefore has cylindrical form

u=u_{r}(r,z,t)e_{r}+u_{\theta}(r,z,t)e_{\theta}+u_{z}(r,z,t)e_{z},

which is exactly the axisymmetric-with-swirl compatibility class. This case is
excluded by the companion Part I axisymmetric direct theorem,
Theorem

3.3
.

The two rigid alternatives exhaust the zero-final-defect limit. Hence no
surviving genuinely three-dimensional zero-final-defect first-threshold
candidate exists.
∎

Remark 4.9

(Interpretation of the full-dimensional candidate exclusion)
.

The theorem names the only possible full-dimensional “monster” left after the
finite-overlap and defect selection mechanisms: a coherent packet with vanishing
final defect and no descendant. The proof shows that such a packet is not a new
three-dimensional geometry. The active-frame algebra places it in the union of
the locally two-dimensional and axisymmetric zero sets, while the passive
visibility estimate prevents nonresonant strain from remaining outside those
sets. Thus Part II reduces the final candidate either to classical two-dimensional regularity or to the Part I axisymmetric direct theorem.

Theorem 5.2

(First-threshold packet selection)
.

Let
M_{*}
exceed the baseline local energy level and define

t_{*}=\inf\{t>0:\mathcal{M}_{\rm crit}(t)=M_{*}\}.

If
t_{*}<\infty
, then there exists a normalized first-threshold packet
Q_{*}
, ending at
t_{*}

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{*})\geq c_{*}M_{*},

and
Q_{*}
is minimal in the selection order among all packets with score at least

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{\rm crit}(t_{*})
, there are centers, times, and scales for which either the full three-dimensional score or the axisymmetric-compatible score is at least
\frac{1}{2}M_{*}
. Choose one such cylinder and parabolically rescale it to unit size. The local energy inequality, pressure decomposition, and angular Littlewood–Paley bounds are invariant under this normalization up to universal constants; hence the normalized packet satisfies the admissible local bounds recorded in the basic definitions.

Consider all packets obtained from this initial cylinder by the permitted finite reselections and whose score is at least
c_{*}M_{*}
. The permitted reselections are recentering into the collar, passing to a fixed shell, selecting a dyadic tail component, selecting a passive or source response subpacket, and taking a separated component of a fragmented packet. Order these packets by endpoint time, dyadic scale, number of separated components, and residual-channel index. The endpoint time cannot decrease indefinitely inside the compact first-threshold interval; the dyadic scale decreases only through a discrete finite-threshold range before the score falls below threshold or the local energy baseline is reached; the number of separated components is a nonnegative integer; and the residual-channel index belongs to a fixed finite list. Therefore an infinite strictly descending chain is impossible. A minimal packet exists and is the desired
Q_{*}

c_{*}
by the universal normalization constants.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Descendant inheritance)
.

Every descendant produced by the permitted reselection mechanisms inherits the local energy bound, pressure compactness, packet score lower bound, visibility budget, angular Littlewood–Paley admissibility, and the same finite-threshold normalization constants, up to universal multiplicative constants.

Proof.

Let
Q^{\prime}
be a descendant of
Q
. For collar and fixed-shell descendants,
Q^{\prime}
is contained in a bounded multiple of the parent cylinder and has comparable parabolic scale. Restricting the local energy inequality to a smaller cylinder preserves the local energy and dissipation bounds; bounded overlap changes only the universal constant. The pressure is decomposed into a near-field Calderón–Zygmund part and a harmonic far-field part. The near-field bound is stable under restriction, and the harmonic part is controlled by interior estimates on the smaller cylinder.

For a dyadic tail descendant, the new scale is comparable to the annular scale. The tail weight contains the kernel-decay and annular-covering factors needed to convert weighted exterior mass into critical packet score at that scale. After parabolic rescaling, local energy and pressure estimates are inherited with constants depending only on the annular covering constant.

For passive, phase, or source descendants, the descendant is selected from a subpacket where the corresponding averaged square residual or source response is nonzero. The finite-overlap selection gives the positive score lower bound. The visibility budget follows from the parent visibility plus the residual inequality used to select the subpacket. Source descendants inherit the source-superlevel bound by construction of the selected half-packet.

For fragmented descendants, the packet is written as a finite sum of separated components. If off-diagonal interactions are not counted by the tail functional, score and visibility split up to a perturbative error. One component carries a fixed fraction of the parent score and has fewer components. Local energy, pressure, and angular Littlewood–Paley bounds restrict to that component after smooth localization; commutators of angular projectors with the localization are lower order and are included in
\mathcal{R}_{\rm low}
. Thus every permitted descendant remains in the same finite-threshold admissible class, with universal constants.
∎

Definition 6.1

(Collar and finite-shell descendants)

C_{\rm ov}

Lemma 6.3

\delta^{2}
