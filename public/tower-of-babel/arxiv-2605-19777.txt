Funnel control with input filter for nonlinear systems with arbitrary relative degree Note: This work was funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – Project-ID 524064985.
Janina Schaa, Thomas Berger

Abstract
This paper addresses output reference tracking with prescribed transient performance for unknown nonlinear multi-input multi-output systems with arbitrary relative degree. We propose a novel derivative-free extension of funnel control based on a collection of filter variables that estimate the output derivatives. The resulting controller ensures that the tracking error evolves within prescribed performance bounds, while avoiding differentiation of the output signal and maintaining a simple structure with only a small number of tuning parameters. The effectiveness of the proposed approach is illustrated by a numerical example.



1
Introduction

In many control applications, achieving output reference tracking with prescribed transient performance for uncertain nonlinear systems is a challenging task. Funnel control, introduced in
[
16
]
, provides an adaptive control framework to address this problem for a broad class of nonlinear multi-input multi-output systems via high-gain feedback, see, e.g.,
[
3
]
. By enforcing the tracking error to evolve within a chosen performance funnel, funnel controllers ensure transient behavior under comparatively mild structural assumptions on the system. As a relative of funnel control, prescribed performance control (see e.g.
[
1
]
) is similar in scope and design.

Due to their simple structure, funnel controllers have been shown to be an appropriate choice for many real-world applications, including DC-link power flow control
[
24
]
, speed control of wind turbine systems
[
13
,
14
]
, control of peak inspiratory pressure
[
23
]
, control of industrial servo-systems
[
19
,
12
,
14
]
and voltage and current control of electrical circuits
[
6
]
.

Despite these advantages, several challenges concerning the practical application of funnel controllers have been investigated in recent research, such as the treatment of input constraints
[
15
,
8
]
, discrete-time systems
[
9
]
and output measurement losses
[
4
]
. A persistent issue, however, is the application of funnel controllers to systems with higher relative degree. Classical funnel control schemes like the approach discussed in
[
5
]
require the availability of the output derivatives, which are generally not accessible in practice, thereby limiting the practical implementability.

This issue has been discussed in several works in the literature. For single-input single-output systems, solutions have been proposed in
[
22
]
for the linear time-invariant case, and in
[
10
]
by the introduction of a virtual output, whose parameters depend on the system dynamics, thereby yielding a design that is not model-free. A filter-based extension has been considered in
[
17
]
, resulting in a complex controller structure including high powers of a potentially large gain function.

Another approach is based on the introduction of a pre-compensator, proposed in
[
7
]
(and with a proof for the general case given in
[
20
]
), in order to estimate the required derivative signals. However, this approach exhibits high complexity due to a large number of auxiliary variables, in particular for systems of higher relative degree. Further, derivative-free funnel control based on sampled data has been investigated for systems of relative degree two, see
[
21
]
. However, this approach requires an appropriate choice of the sampling time, which depends on system-specific properties.

In this paper, we propose a novel extension of funnel control utilizing a collection of filter signals approximating the output derivatives, in order to achieve output tracking with prescribed performance for nonlinear systems of arbitrary relative degree without requiring access to the output derivatives, thereby avoiding numerical differentiation of the output signal. The proposed approach extends previous results for systems of relative degree two, see
[
11
]
. In contrast to the derivative-free design based on a cascade of pre-compensators introduced in
[
7
]
, this approach reduces the number of auxiliary dynamics and hence leads to a lower-dimensional closed-loop system, and involves fewer tuning parameters.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the system class and the control objective. Section 3 presents the controller design and establishes the main result. A simulation example is discussed in Section 4, followed by concluding remarks in Section 5.

2
Problem formulation

2.1
System class

Consider a multi-input multi-output system governed by the differential equation

\displaystyle y^{(r)}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}R_{i}y^{(i)}(t)+f\big(\mathbf{T}(y,\ldots,y^{(r-1)})(t)\big)+\Gamma u(t),

(1)

\displaystyle y|_{[0,t_{0}]}

\displaystyle=y^{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{r-1}([0,t_{0}],\mathds{R}^{n}),

of order
r\geq 2
, where
t_{0}\geq 0
,
R_{i}\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
are matrices,
\Gamma\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
has a positive definite symmetric part
\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\top})
,
u\in L_{\text{loc}}^{\infty}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{n})
is a control input, and
y(t)\in\mathds{R}^{n}
at time
t\geq 0
. Further,
y^{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{r-1}([0,t_{0}],\mathds{R}^{n})
is an initial trajectory,
f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathds{R}^{q},\mathds{R}^{n})
is a nonlinear function, and
{\mathbf{T}:\mathcal{C}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{n})^{r}\to L^{\infty}_{\loc}([t_{0},\infty),\mathds{R}^{q})}
is a nonlinear operator satisfying the following conditions:

1.

Causality
:
\forall\,y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{n})^{r}

\forall\,t\geq t_{0}
:

y_{1}|_{[0,t]}=y_{2}|_{[0,t]}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathbf{T}(y_{1})|_{[t_{0},t]}=\mathbf{T}(y_{2})|_{[t_{0},t]}.

2.

Local Lipschitz
:

\forall\,t\geq t_{0}

\forall\,y\in\mathcal{C}([0,t],\mathds{R}^{n})^{r}

{\exists\,\Delta,\delta,c>0}

\forall\,y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{n})^{r}
with

y_{1}|_{[0,t]}=y_{2}|_{[0,t]}=y

and
\left\|y_{1}(s)-y(t)\right\|<\delta
,
\left\|y_{2}(s)-y(t)\right\|<\delta
for all
s\in[t,t+\Delta]
:

\hskip-5.69054pt\esssup_{\mathclap{s\in[t,t+\Delta]}}\left\|\mathbf{T}(y_{1})(s)\!-\!\mathbf{T}(y_{2})(s)\right\|\!\leq\!c\ \sup_{\mathclap{s\in[t,t+\Delta]}}\ \left\|y_{1}(s)\!-\!y_{2}(s)\right\|\!.

3.

Bounded-input bounded-output (BIBO)
:

\forall\,c_{0}>0

\exists\,c_{1}>0

\forall\,y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathds{R}_{\geq 0},\mathds{R}^{n})
:

\sup_{t\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\left\|y_{1}(t)\right\|\leq c_{0}\\
\Longrightarrow\ \esssup_{t\in[t_{0},\infty)}\left\|\textbf{T}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r})(t)\right\|\leq c_{1}.

The system explicitly contains the linear terms
\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}R_{i}y^{(i)}(t)
, since they are excluded by the BIBO property (iii) of operator
\mathbf{T}
.

Remark 1
.

Observe that property (iii) of the operator
\mathbf{T}
ensures that a bounded system output
y
is sufficient to conclude that the internal dynamics of the system, which are modeled by the operator
\mathbf{T}
, remain bounded. This is a stronger assumption than the BIBO property used in classical funnel control, where the output derivatives are assumed to be available, see
[
5
,
2
]
.

2.2
Control objective

The objective is to design an output feedback control law, which achieves that, for any reference signal
y_{\text{ref}}\in W^{1,\infty}([t_{0},\infty),\mathds{R}^{n})
, the output tracking error
e(t)\coloneq y(t)-y_{\text{ref}}(t)
evolves within a prescribed performance funnel

\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}\coloneq\left\{(t,e)\in[t_{0},\infty)\times\mathds{R}^{n}\mid\varphi(t)\left\|e\right\|<1\right\},

(2)

determined by the (user-defined) choice of the function
\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}([t_{0},\infty),\mathds{R})
such that
\inf_{t\geq t_{0}}\varphi(t)>0
.

3
Controller design

We choose the following output feedback control law

\boxed{\begin{aligned} e(t)&=y(t)-y_{\text{ref}}(t),\\
\dot{\xi}_{r-1}(t)&=-\xi_{r-1}(t)+u(t),\\
\dot{\xi}_{i}(t)&=-(r-i)\xi_{i}(t)+\xi_{i+1}(t),&\forall\,1\leq i<r-1,\\
\theta_{1}(t)&=\xi_{1}(t)-\frac{-e(t)}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|e(t)\right\|^{2}},\\
\theta_{i}(t)&=\xi_{i}(t)-\frac{-\theta_{i-1}(t)}{\hat{\theta}_{i-1}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{i-1}(t)\right\|^{2}},&\forall\,2\leq i\leq r-1,\\
u(t)&=\frac{-\vartheta\theta_{r-1}(t)}{\hat{\theta}_{r-1}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{r-1}(t)\right\|^{2}},&\vartheta>0\end{aligned}}

(3)

with constant initial conditions
\xi_{i}(t)=\xi_{i}^{0}
for all
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
and parameters
\vartheta>0
and
\hat{\theta}_{i}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq r-1
.

Observe that this controller does not use the derivatives
\dot{y},\ldots,y^{(r-1)}
of the output and
\dot{y}_{\text{ref}},\ldots,y^{(r-1)}_{\text{ref}}
of the reference trajectory. Instead, we introduce
r-1
filter variables
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r-1}
, which are qualitatively reproducing the signals
\dot{y},\ldots,y^{(r-1)}
, respectively. The control law
u
is then designed to ensure that the filter variable
\xi_{1}
tracks the reference signal
\frac{-e(t)}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|e(t)\right\|^{2}}
, which corresponds to the funnel control law for systems of relative degree one, except for an error
\theta_{1}
, which will remain bounded by some constant
\hat{\theta}_{1}
, due to the iterative construction of constant funnel bounds for the error signals
\theta_{i}
. The controller has
r
design variables,
\hat{\theta}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\theta}_{r-1}
and
\vartheta
. Moreover, one needs to choose the initial values
\xi_{i}^{0}
. Observe that all parameters can be selected without system knowledge, except for the initial system output
y^{0}(t_{0})
.

A function
(x,\xi):[0,\omega)\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{rn}\times\mathds{R}^{(r-1)n}
,
\omega\in(0,\infty],
is called a solution of the closed loop system (
1
), (
3
), if it satisfies the initial conditions
(x_{1}(t),\xi_{1}(t),\ldots,\xi_{r-1}(t))=(y^{0}(t),\xi_{1}^{0},\ldots,\xi_{r-1}^{0})
for each
t\in[0,t_{0}],
and
(x,\xi)_{|[t_{0},\omega)}
is absolutely continuous and satisfies

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=x_{i+1}(t),\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq r-1

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{r}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}R_{i}x_{i+1}(t)+f\big(\mathbf{T}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})(t)\big)+\Gamma u(t)

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=-(r-i)\xi_{i}(t)+\xi_{i+1}(t),\quad\forall\,1\leq i<r-1

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}_{r-1}(t)

\displaystyle=-\xi_{r-1}(t)+u(t),

with
u
as defined in (
3
) for almost all
t\in[t_{0},\omega)
. We call a solution maximal, if it does not have a right extension, which is also a solution.

.

1

\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}([t_{0},\infty),\mathds{R})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=i+1}^{r}(-i)^{j-1}y^{(r-j+1)}(t)+\Gamma\xi_{r-1}(t)

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{T}(y,\ldots,y^{(r-1)})(t))-iS^{i}(t)+\sum_{j=i}^{r-1}(-i)^{j}y^{(r-j)}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=r-i+1}^{r-1}(-1)^{r-j}(i^{r-j}y^{(j)}(t)-a_{i,j-1}\Gamma\xi_{j}(t))

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=r-i}^{r-1}(-1)^{r-1-j}a_{i,j}\Gamma(r-j)\xi_{j}(t)+iS_{0}(i)y(t)

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{T}(y,\ldots,y^{(r-1)})(t))-iS^{i}(t)+iS_{0}(i)y(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{j=r-i+1}^{r-1}\!\!\!\!(-1)^{r-j}(i^{r-j}y^{(j)}(t)\!-\!(a_{i,j-1}\!+\!a_{i,j}(r-j))\Gamma\xi_{j}(t))

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{i-1}ia_{i,r-i}\Gamma\xi_{r-i}(t)+(-i)\sum_{j=i}^{r-1}(-i)^{j-1}y^{(r-j)}(t)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:aij}}{=}f(\mathbf{T}(y,\ldots,y^{(r-1)})(t))-iS^{i}(t)+iS_{0}(i)y(t)

\displaystyle\quad+(-i)\sum_{j=i}^{r-1}(-i)^{j-1}y^{(r-j)}(t)+(-1)^{i-1}ia_{i,r-i}\Gamma\xi_{r-i}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=r-i+1}^{r-1}(-1)^{r-j}(i^{r-j}y^{(j)}(t)-ia_{i,j}\Gamma\xi_{j}(t))

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{T}(y,\ldots,y^{(r-1)})(t))+iS_{0}(i)y(t)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{i-1}ia_{i,r-i}\Gamma\xi_{r-i}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+(-i)\Bigg(S^{i}(t)+\sum_{j=i}^{r-1}(-i)^{j-1}y^{(r-j)}(t)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{j=r-i+1}^{r-1}(-1)^{r-j-1}(i^{r-j-1}y^{(j)}(t)-a_{i,j}\Gamma\xi_{j}(t))\Bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{def:zeta-i}}{=}f(\mathbf{T}(y,\ldots,y^{(r-1)})(t))+(-1)^{i-1}ia_{i,r-i}\Gamma\xi_{r-i}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+(-i)\Big(\zeta_{i}(t)-(-1)^{i-1}(i^{i-1}y^{(r-i)}(t)-a_{i,r-i}\Gamma\xi_{r-i}(t))

\displaystyle\quad-(-i)^{r-1}y(t)+(-i)^{i-1}y^{(r-i)}(t)\Big)+iS_{0}(i)y(t)

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{T}(y,\ldots,y^{(r-1)})(t))+iS_{0}(i)y(t)

\displaystyle\quad+(-i)\zeta_{i}(t)-(-i)^{r}y(t).

(10)

Using the fact that
y
is bounded along the maximal solution due to the boundness of the tracking error and the reference signal, and by the BIBO property of the operator
\mathbf{T}
and continuity of
f
, we can conclude that
f(\mathbf{T}(y,\ldots,y^{(r-1)})(t))
is bounded along the trajectory of the maximal solution, therefore
\zeta_{i}
remains bounded by a Gronwall argument.

Step 2b:
We show that
\dot{y}-\Gamma\xi_{1}
can be expressed as a linear combination of bounded terms. Define the matrix

M\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\ldots&1\\
1&2&\ldots&r-1\\
1&2^{2}&\ldots&(r-1)^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&2^{(r-3)}&\ldots&(r-1)^{(r-3)}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathds{R}^{(r-2)\times(r-1)}.

(11)

Recall that for any pairwise distinct
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in\mathds{R}
, the Vandermonde matrix

V(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{1}^{2}&\ldots&\alpha_{1}^{r-1}\\
1&\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{2}^{2}&\ldots&\alpha_{2}^{r-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\alpha_{r}&\alpha_{r}^{2}&\ldots&\alpha_{r}^{r-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(12)

is invertible. From this, we can conclude that
\dim\operatorname{ker}M=1
, and moreover, by choosing an arbitrary
0\neq c=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
\vdots\\
c_{r-1}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{ker}M
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}i^{k}=0,\quad\forall\,0\leq k\leq r-3,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}i^{r-2}\neq 0.

(13)

Now, define
Z(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}\zeta_{i}(t)
for
t\in[t_{0},\omega)
. Using the representation of
\zeta_{i}
from (
9
), we can express this as

\displaystyle Z(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}\alpha_{j}(i)\right)y^{(j)}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}\beta_{j}(i)\right)\Gamma\xi_{j}(t)

(14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}A_{j}y^{(j)}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}B_{j}\Gamma\xi_{j}(t),

where the matrices
A_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}\alpha_{j}(i)=0
for each
j\geq 2
due to property (
13
) and the fact that the
\alpha_{j}(s)
are polynomials of degree
r-1-j\leq r-3
. Similarly, we obtain time-independent coefficients
B_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}\beta_{j}(i)=0
for each
j\geq 2
, again by property (
13
) and the fact that the
\beta_{j}(s)
are polynomials of degree
r-1-j\leq r-3
.

However, calculating the remaining coefficients shows that

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}\alpha_{1}(i)=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}((-i)^{r-2}I+S_{1}(i))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}(-i)^{r-2}I\neq 0,

since the degree of
S_{1}(s)
is not larger than
r-3
and by (
13
), and similarly

B_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}\beta_{1}(i)=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}(-1)^{r-1}i^{r-2}=:-q\neq 0.

This shows that

Z(t)=q(\dot{y}(t)-\Gamma\xi_{1}(t))+A_{0}y(t).

(15)

Since
Z(t)
is bounded on
[t_{0},\omega)
as a linear combination of
\zeta_{1}(t),\ldots,\zeta_{r-1}(t)
, which are bounded by Step 2a, we can conclude that

\dot{y}(t)-\Gamma\xi_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{q}(Z(t)-A_{0}y(t))

remains bounded on
[t_{0},\omega)
.

Step 3:
Construct a family
\left(Z_{k}(t)\right)_{1\leq k\leq r-1}
of bounded functions on
[t_{0},\omega)
. Define
Z_{1}(t)\coloneq Z(t)
as in the previous step. Fix
2\leq k\leq r-1
. Choose
c^{(k)}\in\mathds{R}^{r-1}
such that

c^{(k)}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}^{(k)}\\
\vdots\\
c_{r-1}^{(k)}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{ker}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\ldots&1\\
1&2&\ldots&r-1\\
1&2^{2}&\ldots&(r-1)^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&2^{(r-2-k)}&\ldots&(r-1)^{(r-2-k)}\end{pmatrix},

and
\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}i^{r-1-k}=:q_{k}\neq 0.
A vector
c^{(k)}
with these properties exists by the invertibility of the Vandermonde matrix.
Now, define
Z_{k}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}\zeta_{i}(t)
for
t\in[t_{0},\omega)
. This is bounded, since all
\zeta_{i}
are bounded by Step 2. Again, we can express

\displaystyle Z_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\!\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}\alpha_{j}(i)\right)\!y^{(j)}(t)\!+\!\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}\beta_{j}(i)\!\!\right)\!\Gamma\xi_{j}(t)

(16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}A_{j}^{(k)}y^{(j)}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}B_{j}^{(k)}\Gamma\xi_{j}(t),

where
A_{j}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}\alpha_{j}(i)=0
for each
j\geq k+1
due to the choice of
c^{(k)}
and the fact that the
\alpha_{j}(s)
are polynomials of degree
r-1-j\leq r-2-k
. Similarly, we obtain
B_{j}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}\beta_{j}(i)=0
for each
j\geq k+1
, again by the choice of
c^{(k)}
and the fact that the
\beta_{j}(s)
are polynomials of degree
r-1-j\leq r-2-k
.

However, calculating the coefficient for
j=k
shows that

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}\alpha_{k}(i)=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}((-i)^{r-1-k}I+S_{k}(i))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}(-i)^{r-1-k}I=(-1)^{r-1-k}q_{k}I\neq 0,

since the degree of
S_{k}(s)
is not larger than
r-1-k
, and similarly

B_{k}^{(k)}\!\!=\!\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}\beta_{k}(i)\!=\!\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}c_{i}^{(k)}(-1)^{r-k}i^{r-1-k}\!=\!(-1)^{r-k}q_{k}\!\neq\!0.

This shows that

Z_{k}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}A_{j}^{(k)}y^{(j)}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j}^{(k)}\Gamma\xi_{j}(t)

(17)

for all
t\in[t_{0},\omega)
, for some matrix
A_{k}^{(k)}=(-1)^{r-1-k}q_{k}I
that is invertible.

Step 4:
We show that
\varphi(t)\left\|e(t)\right\|\leq\varepsilon
for all
t\in[t_{0},\omega)
, for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1).
Choose
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
\varphi(t_{0})\left\|e(t_{0})\right\|<\varepsilon
, and

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\top}))}\Bigg(\|\dot{y}_{\text{ref}}\|_{\infty}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\left\|\frac{\dot{\varphi}}{\varphi}\right\|_{\infty}

(18)

\displaystyle+\|\varphi\|_{\infty}(\lambda_{\text{max}}(\Gamma)\hat{\theta}_{1}+\|\dot{y}-\Gamma\xi_{1}\|_{\infty})\Bigg)=:\sigma,

where
\lambda_{\text{min}}(\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\top}))>0
is the smallest eigenvalue of the positive definite matrix
\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma+\Gamma^{\top})
, and
\lambda_{\text{max}}(\Gamma)>0
is the absolute value of the largest eigenvalue of the matrix
\Gamma
, and
\sigma
is finite due to Step 2 and the assumptions on
\varphi
and
y_{\text{ref}}
. Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exists
s_{1}\in[t_{0},\omega)
such that
\varphi(s_{1})\left\|e(s_{1})\right\|>\varepsilon
. Since
\varphi(t_{0})\left\|e(t_{0})\right\|<\varepsilon
by choice of
\varepsilon
, there exists
s_{0}\coloneq\sup\{t\in[t_{0},s_{1}]\mid\varphi(t)\left\|e(t)\right\|=\varepsilon\}
.

We will use the fact that

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\xi_{1}(t)+\dot{y}(t)-\Gamma\xi_{1}(t)

(19)

\displaystyle=\Gamma\theta_{1}(t)+\Gamma\frac{-e(t)}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|e(t)\right\|^{2}}+\dot{y}(t)-\Gamma\xi_{1}(t)

holds for all
t\in[t_{0},\omega)
.
Observe that for all
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}\varphi(s)^{2}\!\left\|e(s)\right\|^{2}=\varphi(s)^{2}\langle e(s),\dot{e}(s)\rangle+\varphi(s)\dot{\varphi}(s)\left\|e(s)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\varphi(s)^{2}\langle e(s),\dot{y}(s)\rangle\!+\!\|\dot{y}_{\text{ref}}\|_{\infty}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\!+\!\left\|\frac{\dot{\varphi}}{\varphi}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad\overset{\eqref{eq:doty}}{=}\varphi(s)^{2}\left\langle e(s),\frac{-\Gamma e(s)}{1-\varphi(s)^{2}\left\|e(s)\right\|^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\varphi(s)^{2}\langle e(s),\Gamma\theta_{1}(s)+\dot{y}(s)-\Gamma\xi_{1}(s)\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\|\dot{y}_{\text{ref}}\|_{\infty}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\left\|\frac{\dot{\varphi}}{\varphi}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq-\frac{\lambda_{\text{min}}(\Gamma)\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}}+\sigma\overset{\eqref{def-eps}}{<}0.

This implies that
\varepsilon=\varphi(s_{0})\left\|e(s_{0})\right\|>\varphi(s_{1})\left\|e(s_{1})\right\|>\varepsilon
, a contradiction. Hence, the claim of Step 4 is shown.

Step 5:
We show boundness of
u
,
\xi_{i}
and
y^{(i)}
for each
1\leq i\leq r-1
. By Step 4,
\varphi(t)\left\|e(t)\right\|<\varepsilon<1
, hence
\frac{1}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|e(t)\right\|^{2}}
is bounded. Moreover, since
\|\theta_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq\hat{\theta}_{1}
, it follows that
\xi_{1}(t)=\theta_{1}(t)+\frac{-e(t)}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|e(t)\right\|^{2}}
is bounded. Further, since
\dot{y}-\Gamma\xi_{1}
is bounded by Step 2, we can conclude that
\dot{y}
is bounded. Choose
b_{0}\coloneq\sup_{t\in[t_{0},\omega)}\left\|\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{-e(t)}{1-\varphi(t)^{2}\left\|e(t)\right\|^{2}}\right\|
, which is bounded due to Step 4 and the boundness of
\dot{y}
yielding that
\dot{e}
is bounded. Observe that this shows that
\dot{\theta}_{1}
is bounded. Choose some
\frac{\|\theta_{1}(t_{0})\|}{\hat{\theta}_{1}}<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}<1
such that

\hat{\theta}_{1}((r-1)\|\xi_{1}\|_{\infty}+b_{0}+\hat{\theta}_{2})<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}}{1-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}}.

Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exists
s_{1}\in[t_{0},\omega)
such that
\|\theta_{1}(s_{1})\|>\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}\hat{\theta}_{1}
holds. Due to the condition on the initial condition, there exists
s_{0}\coloneq\sup\{s\in[t_{0},s_{1}]\mid\|\theta_{1}(s)\|=\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}\hat{\theta}_{1}\}
. For each
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
, the estimate

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}\left\|\theta_{1}(s)\right\|^{2}=\left\langle\theta_{1}(s),\dot{\xi}_{1}(s)-\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}\frac{-e(s)}{1-\varphi(s)^{2}\left\|e(s)\right\|^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\langle\theta_{1}(s),-(r-1)\xi_{1}(s)+\xi_{2}(s)\rangle+\left\|\theta_{1}\right\|b_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left\|\theta_{1}\right\|((r-1)\|\xi_{1}\|_{\infty}+b_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left\langle\theta_{1}(s),\theta_{2}(s)+\frac{-\theta_{1}(s)}{\hat{\theta}_{1}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{1}(s)\right\|^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\hat{\theta}_{1}((r-1)\|\xi_{1}\|_{\infty}\!+\!b_{0}\!+\!\hat{\theta}_{2})+\frac{-\left\|\theta_{1}(s)\right\|^{2}}{\hat{\theta}_{1}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{1}(s)\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\hat{\theta}_{1}((r-1)\|\xi_{1}\|_{\infty}+b_{0}+\hat{\theta}_{2})-\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}}{1-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}}<0

holds. By integration, we obtain

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}\hat{\theta}_{1}<\theta_{1}(s_{1})<\theta_{1}(s_{0})=\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}\hat{\theta}_{1},

a contradiction. We can conclude that
\|\theta_{1}(s)\|\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}\hat{\theta}_{1}
for all
s\in[t_{0},\omega)
.

We continue to show the boundness of
y^{(k)},\frac{1}{\hat{\theta}_{k}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{k}\right\|^{2}},\dot{\theta}_{k-1}
and
\xi_{k}
inductively. Fix
2\leq k\leq r-1
. Assume that all
\xi_{i},y^{(i)}
, and
\frac{1}{\hat{\theta}_{i}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{i}\right\|^{2}}
for
1\leq i<k
, and
\dot{\theta}_{i-1}
for
2\leq i<k
are bounded. We show that this implies that
\xi_{k},\frac{1}{\hat{\theta}_{k}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{k}\right\|^{2}},\dot{\theta}_{k-1}
and
y^{(k)}
are bounded.

Observe that
\xi_{k}(t)=\theta_{k}(t)+\frac{-\theta_{k-1}(t)}{\hat{\theta}_{k-1}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{k-1}(t)\right\|^{2}}
is bounded by the induction hypothesis. Further, we can use (
17
) to conclude that

y^{(k)}\!(t)\!=\!\frac{1}{(-1)^{r-1-k}q_{k}}\!\!\left(\!Z_{k}(t)\!-\!\!\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j}^{(k)}\Gamma\xi_{j}(t)\!-\!\!\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}A_{j}^{(k)}y^{(j)}(t)\!\right)\!\!,

which is bounded, since
Z_{k}
is bounded by Step 3, and the functions
y,\ldots,y^{(k-1)}
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k-1}
are bounded by the induction hypothesis, and
\xi_{k}
remains bounded by the previous argument. Observe that
\dot{\theta}_{k-1}(t)=\dot{\xi}_{k-1}(t)-\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{-\theta_{k-2}(t)}{\hat{\theta}^{2}_{k-2}-\left\|\theta_{k-2}(t)\right\|^{2}}
is bounded, since
\xi_{k},\xi_{k-1},\dot{\theta}_{k-2}
and
\frac{1}{\hat{\theta}^{2}_{k-2}-\left\|\theta_{k-2}(t)\right\|^{2}}
are bounded by the induction hypothesis. Define
b_{k-1}\coloneq\sup_{t\in[t_{0},\omega)}\left\|\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{-\theta_{k-1}(t)}{\hat{\theta}_{k-1}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{k-1}(t)\right\|^{2}}\right\|
, which is finite by the induction hypothesis and the previously observed boundness of
\dot{\theta}_{k-1}
. Choose some
\frac{\|\theta_{k}(t_{0})\|}{\hat{\theta}_{k}}<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}<1
such that

\hat{\theta}_{k}((r-k)\|\xi_{k}\|_{\infty}+b_{k-1}+\hat{\theta}_{k+1})<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2}}{1-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2}}.

Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exists
s_{1}\in[t_{0},\omega)
such that
\|\theta_{k}(s_{1})\|>\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}
holds. Due to the condition on the initial condition, there exists
s_{0}\coloneq\sup\{s\in[t_{0},s_{1}]\mid\|\theta_{k}(s)\|=\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}\}
. For each
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
, the estimate

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}\left\|\theta_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}=\left\langle\theta_{k}(s),\dot{\xi}_{k}(s)-\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}\frac{-\theta_{k-1}(s)}{\hat{\theta}_{k-1}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{k-1}(s)\right\|^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\langle\theta_{k}(s),-(r-k)\xi_{k}(s)+\xi_{k+1}(s)\rangle+\left\|\theta_{k}\right\|b_{k-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left\|\theta_{k}\right\|((r-k)\|\xi_{k}\|_{\infty}+b_{k-1})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left\langle\theta_{k}(s),\theta_{k+1}(s)+\frac{-\theta_{k}(s)}{\hat{\theta}_{k}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\hat{\theta}_{k}((r-k)\|\xi_{k}\|_{\infty}\!+\!b_{k-1}\!+\!\hat{\theta}_{k+1})\!+\!\frac{-\left\|\theta_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}}{\hat{\theta}_{k}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\hat{\theta}_{k}((r-k)\|\xi_{k}\|_{\infty}\!+\!b_{k-1}\!+\!\hat{\theta}_{k+1})\!-\!\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2}}{1-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2}}\!<\!0

holds. By integration, we obtain

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}<\theta_{k}(s_{1})<\theta_{k}(s_{0})=\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k},

a contradiction. We can conclude that
\|\theta_{k}(s)\|\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}\hat{\theta}_{k}
for all
s\in[t_{0},\omega)
, finishing the induction.

It remains to show boundness of
u
, which follows from the existence of
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{r-1}<1
satisfying
\|\theta_{r-1}\|\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}_{r-1}\hat{\theta}_{r-1}
, hence
\frac{1}{\hat{\theta}_{r-1}^{2}-\left\|\theta_{r-1}(t)\right\|^{2}}
is bounded, showing the claim of Step 5.

Step 6:
We show that
\omega=\infty
. Assume that
\omega<\infty
. By Step 4 and the existence of
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\varepsilon}_{r-1}<1
as well as boundednes of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r-1}
shown in Step 5, the closure of the graph of the maximal solution is a compact subset of
\mathcal{E}
, contradicting Step 1. Therefore,
\omega=\infty
, showing the existence of a global solution.
∎

Remark 3
.

Note that the filter-based contoller (
3
) uses only
r-1
dynamic variables
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r-1}
and no further assumptions on the gain matrix
\Gamma
are needed, besides positive definiteness of its symmetric part. In contrast, the funnel controller using a cascade of pre-compensators from
[
7
,
20
]
introduces
r(r-1)
dynamic variables and requires knowledge of an estimate close enough to
\Gamma
.

Remark 4
.

For the choice of the tuning parameters
\hat{\theta}_{i}
and the initial values
\xi_{i}^{0}
of the filter variables, note that, although smaller values of
\hat{\theta}_{i}
may improve controller performance, decreasing this parameter requires a precise knowledge of the initial output
y^{0}(t_{0})
in order to guarantee the existence of a consistent choice of initial values
\xi_{i}^{0}
.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we proposed a derivative-free extension of funnel control for nonlinear multi-input multi-output systems with arbitrary relative degree. By employing a collection of filter variables as surrogates for the unavailable output derivatives, the resulting controller avoids the differentiation of the output signal while being of low complexity. The tracking error evolves within the prescribed performance funnel, and all signals remain bounded. Compared to earlier works based on the funnel pre-compensator, the new approach involves much less dynamic variables and tuning parameters and avoids additional assumptions on the input gain matrix
\Gamma
.

The proposed approach remains sensitive to measurement noise and discretization effects, as transient violations of the funnel constraint cannot be excluded. This is inherent to funnel control and not specific to the proposed extension. Future work may address the integration of the novel approach with other funnel-based control schemes, as well as the extension to more complex settings such as networked systems.
