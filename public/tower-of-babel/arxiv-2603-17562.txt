On global dynamics for damped driven


Abstract
The article concerns damped driven Jaynes–Cummings equation which describes quantised one-mode Maxwell field coupled to a two-level molecule. We consider a broad class of damping and pumping which are polynomial in the creation and annihilation operators, and their structures correspond to the theory of completely positive and trace preserving generators (CPTP) of Lindblad and Kossakowski & al. Our main result is the construction of global generalised solutions with values in the Hilbert space of nonnegative Hermitian Hilbert–Schmidt operators in the case of time-dependent pumping. The proofs rely on finite-dimensional approximations of the annihilation and creation operators.

Introduction

The Jaynes–Cummings equation is the basic model of Quantum Optics,
and it is used for description of various aspects of the laser action.
The model without damping and pumping was introduced in

[
16
]
(the survey can be found in
[
19
]
).
Various versions of the pumping are considered in
[
5
,
8
,
12
,
17
,
23
]
.
The damping was introduced analysing quantum spontaneous emission
[
1
]
–
[
3
]
,
[
6
,
7
,
23
,
24
]
.
We construct global solutions for all initial values from the space of Hilbert–Schmidt
operators in the case of time-dependent pumping.

Denote

X=F\otimes{\mathbb C}^{2}
, where
F
is the single-particle Hilbert space
endowed with
an orthonormal basis
|n\rangle
,
n=0,1,\dots
,
and the corresponding annihilation and creation operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
:

a|n\rangle=\sqrt{n}|n-1\rangle,\qquad a^{\dagger}|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle,\qquad[a,a^{\dagger}]=1.

(1.1)

We will consider a damped driven version of the Jaynes–Cummings equation

\dot{\rho}(t)={\mathbb A}\rho(t):=-i[H(t),\rho(t)]+\gamma D(t)\rho(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

(1.2)

where
the density operator
\rho(t)
of the coupled field-molecule system
is a Hermitian operator in
X
.
The Hamiltonian
H(t)
is the sum

H(t)=H_{0}+pH_{1}(t),\qquad H_{0}:=\omega_{c}a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{a}\sigma_{3},\quad H_{1}(t)=(a+a^{\dagger})\otimes\sigma_{1}+A^{e}(t).

(1.3)

Here
H_{0}
is the Hamiltonian of the free field and atom without interaction,
while
pH_{1}(t)
is the interaction Hamiltonian,

\omega_{c}>0
is the cavity resonance frequency,

\omega_{a}>0
is the atomic
frequency, and

p\in{\mathbb R}
is proportional to the molecular dipole moment.
The pumping is represented by selfadjoint operators

A^{e}(t)
,
and

\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{3}
are the Pauli matrices
acting on the factor
{\mathbb C}^{2}
in
F\otimes{\mathbb C}^{2}
, so
[a,\sigma_{k}]=[a^{\dagger},\sigma_{k}]=0
.
Finally,
\gamma>0
, and
D(t)
is a dissipation operator.
We will consider the operator

D_{1}\rho=a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}a\rho-\frac{1}{2}\rho a^{\dagger}a,

(1.4)

used in
[
1
]
–
[
3
]
,
[
6
,
7
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
,
and also its suitable modifications.

Definition 1.1
.

{\rm HS}
is the Hilbert space of Hermitian
Hilbert–Schmidt operators with the inner product
[
21
]

\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle_{\rm HS}=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho_{1}\rho_{2}].

(1.5)

Definition 1.2
.

i)
|n,s_{\pm}\rangle=|n\rangle\otimes s_{\pm}
is the orthonormal basis in
X
,

s_{\pm}\in{\mathbb C}^{2}
and
\sigma_{3}s_{\pm}=\pm s_{\pm}
.

ii)
X_{\infty}
is the space
of all finite linear combinations of the vectors
|n,s_{\pm}\rangle
.

iii)
{\cal D}\subset{\rm HS}
is the subspace of finite rank Hermitian operators

\rho=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm}}\rho_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}|n,s\rangle\otimes\langle n^{\prime},s^{\prime}|.

(1.6)

Our main goal is to construct global nonnegative solutions for the equation (
1.2
)
in the Hilbert space
{\rm HS}
in the case of time-dependent
pumping
A^{e}
. The main issue is that
the operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
are unbounded by (
1.1
), so,
the right hand side of (
1.2
)
is not Lipschitz continuous in
\rho(t)
.
Accordingly, the meaning of the equation (
1.2
) must be adjusted (see Definition
2.3
).

In the case of time-independent pumping
A^{e}(t)=A^{e}
, the Hamilton

H(t)=H
is a selfadjoint operator, so
for
\gamma=0
,
solutions are given by

\rho(t)=e^{-iHt}\rho(0)e^{iHt},\qquad t\geq 0.

(1.7)

In this case, the trace
\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\!\rho(t)
is conserved, and
\rho(t)\geq 0
if
\rho(0)\geq 0
.
However, for time-dependent pumping or
\gamma>0
, the formula for solutions is missing.

We assume that
the pumping
A^{e}(t)
and
the dissipation operator
D(t)

are polynomials in
a
and
a^{\dagger}
. Moreover, the structure of
D(t)
corresponds to
the theory of
completely positive and trace preserving generators (CPTP) developed by
Lindblad
[
20
]
and Gorini, Kossakowski and Sudarshan
[
14
]
.

Examples.

A^{e}=a^{\dagger}+a
,
A^{e}=a^{\dagger}a
, and
D(t)=D_{1}
satisfy all our assumptions.

Our main results are as follows:
there exist
continuous linear operators
U(t):{\rm HS}\to{\rm HS}
with
t\geq 0

such that for each nonnegative
\rho^{0}\in{\rm HS}
, the trajectory
\rho(t)=U(t)\rho^{0}
is
a nonnegative generalised solution
to (
1.2
) with initial condition

\rho(0)=\rho^{0},

(1.8)

and
the a priori bounds hold,

\|\rho(t)\|_{\rm HS}\leq\|\rho^{0}\|_{{\rm HS}},\qquad t\geq 0.

(1.9)

Our strategy relies on
finite-dimensional approximations of the annihilation and creation operators
and the uniform bounds (
1.9
).
The bounds are due to the nonpositivity of the dissipation operators
D(t)

under our assumpions.
The key example of such dissipation operators is
D_{1}

for which the nonpositivity has been established in
[
18
]
.

Note that the nonnegativity of
\rho(t)
cannot be obtained by a straightforward
application of
the theory
[
14
,
20
]
of
completely positive and trace preserving generators (CPTP) since the generator
{\mathbb A}(t)

is an unbounded operator. However, the theory allows us to
establish the nonnegativity for special finite-dimensional approximations
which
keep the CPTP structure of the generator. Then the nonnegativity of
\rho(t)
follows by the
limit transition. In contrast, the trace conservation does not follow in the limit,
though it is conserved for all the approximations.

Let us comment on previous related results.
In the case of bounded generators
{\mathbb A}(t)
,
global solutions for equations of type (
1.2
) obviously exist.
In this case,
Lindblad
[
20
]
and Gorini, Kossakowski, and Sudarshan
[
14
]

found necessary and sufficient conditions on
{\mathbb A}(t)
providing the positivity and trace preservation.

For unbounded generators, the existence of global solutions
to Quantum Dynamical Systems (QDS) is not well-developed,
[
4
, p.110]
.
In
[
11
]
, E.B. Davies considered autonomous quantum-mechanical Fokker–Planck
equations (QFP).
The existence of the corresponding positive
contraction semigroup is established
in the Banach space of self-adjoint trace-class operators.
The uniqueness and trace preservation have not been proved.
The useful sufficient conditions, providing the trace preservation
for QFP equations,
were
found in
[
9
]
. The detailed characterisation of a class of covariant
QDS with unbounded generators is presented in
[
15
]
.
The survey
can be found in
[
10
,
13
]
.

In
[
18
]
, we have constructed the contraction semigroup for the equation
(
1.2
) with
pumping and dissipation operator which do not depend on time, so
{\mathbb A}(t)={\mathbb A}
. In this case, the main ingredient in the proof
is the nonpositivity
{\mathbb A}\leq 0
which implies the existence of
the semigroup
e^{{\mathbb A}t}
by
the Lumer–Phillips theorem.
The nonpositivity
follows from the same property
of the dissipation operator
D
and
implies that the semigroup
is contracting, i.e., the bounds (
1.9
) hold. As the key
example, the nonpositivity is proved for the operator (
1.4
).

Note that
the equation (
1.2
) is non-autonomous, so, the theory of semigroup
is not applicable.
Our approach
allows us to substitute the theory by a systematic application of
the contraction (
1.9
).

Up to our knowledge,
the well-posedness
for nonautonomous damped driven Jaynes–Cummings equations
has not been
established previously.

Notations and main results

Every density operator
\rho\in{\rm HS}

is defined uniquely by its matrix entries

\rho_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\langle n,s|\rho|n^{\prime},s^{\prime}\rangle,\qquad n,n^{\prime}=0,1,\dots,\quad{s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm}}.

(2.10)

The Hilbert–Schmidt norm, corresponding to the inner product (
1.5
), can be written as

\|\rho\|_{\rm HS}^{2}=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits[\rho^{2}]=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm}}|\rho_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}|^{2}<\infty.

(2.11)

Note that
by (
1.1
),
the space

X_{\infty}
is invariant with respect to
a
and
a^{\dagger}
. Hence,
the products
of

\rho\in{\cal D}
with any polynomials of
a
and
a^{\dagger}

are well-defined as operators
in
X_{\infty}
.

We assume that the pumping and the dissipation operator
satisfy the following conditions. Denote by
M_{2}={\mathbb C}^{2}\otimes{\mathbb C}^{2}
the space of

2\times 2
-matrices.

H1.

The pumping
A^{e}(t)
is a
symmetric operator in
X_{\infty}
, and it is a
polynomial in the creation and annihilation operators:

A^{e}(t)=\sum A^{e}_{l,l^{\prime}}(t)a^{{\dagger}l}a^{l^{\prime}},\qquad A^{e}_{l,l^{\prime}}\!\!\in\!C(0,\infty;M_{2}),

(2.12)

H2.

The dissipation operator
D(t)\rho

admits a “double” structure
of the
CPTP theory
of completely positive trace preserving operators
[
14
,
20
,
22
]
:

D(t)\rho=[V(t)\rho,V^{\dagger}(t)]+[V(t),\rho V^{\dagger}(t)],

(2.13)

where
V(t)
is also a
polynomial in the creation and annihilation operators:

V(t)=\sum V_{l,l^{\prime}}(t)a^{{\dagger}l}a^{l^{\prime}},\qquad V_{l,l^{\prime}}\!\!\in\!C(0,\infty;M_{2}),

(2.14)

Remark 2.1
.

i)
H1–H2
imply that

{\mathbb A}(t)\rho\in{\cal D}
for

\rho\in{\cal D}
and
t\geq 0
.

ii)
The operator (
1.4
) admits the structure (
2.14
).

Remark 2.2
.

In
[
17
]
, the pumping

A^{e}(t)=\sigma_{+}e^{-i\omega_{p}t}+\sigma_{-}e^{i\omega_{p}t},\qquad\sigma_{+}=\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\end{array}\!\!\!\right)\!,\quad\sigma_{-}=\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
1&0\end{array}\!\!\!\right)

(2.15)

has been applied
to the study of the collapse and revival
phenomenon (see
[
17
, (2.1)]
).

To formulate our results, we need
give a meaning to
the equation
(
1.2
). The issue is that the operators
{\mathbb A}(t)
are not well-defined on
{\rm HS}
.
The equation admits the treatment via the matrix
entries (
2.10
) as the system

\dot{\rho}_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(t)=[{\mathbb A}(t)\rho(t)]_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}},\qquad t\geq 0,\qquad n,n^{\prime}\geq 0,\quad s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm},

(2.16)

since the right hand side is well-defined for all
\rho(t)\in{\rm HS}
.
Indeed,
by

H1
and (
1.1
),
the operators
{\mathbb A}(t)
are well-defined on the domain
{\cal D}
, and

[{\mathbb A}(t)\rho]_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\sum_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}|k-n|+|k^{\prime}-n^{\prime}|\leq N\\
r,r^{\prime}=s_{\pm}\end{array}}{\mathbb A}_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{k,r;k^{\prime},r^{\prime}}(t)\rho_{k,r;k^{\prime},r^{\prime}},\,\quad n,n^{\prime}\geq 0,s,s^{\prime}=s_{\pm},\quad\rho\in{\cal D},

(2.17)

where
N=\max(2,\deg A^{e},\deg V)
, and

{\mathbb A}_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{k,r;k^{\prime},r^{\prime}}(\cdot)\in C(0,\infty),\qquad\forall n,s,n^{\prime},s^{\prime},k,r,k^{\prime},r^{\prime}.

(2.18)

Finally, since
the summation in (
2.17
) is finite,
the matrix entries admit a unique extension by continuity from
\rho\in{\cal D}

to all
\rho\in{\rm HS}
. The structure (
2.17
) means that the matrix of the generator in the basis
|n,s\rangle
is almost
diagonal.

Let us denote by

{\rm HS}_{w}
the Hilbert space
{\rm HS}
endowed with
the weak topology,
and the space of trajectories

{\cal T}=L^{\infty}(0,\infty;{\rm HS})\cap C(0,\infty;{\rm HS}_{w}).

(2.19)

Definition 2.3
.

A trajectory
\rho(t)\in{\cal T}
is a generalised solution to (
1.2
)
if it
satisfies the system (
2.16
) in the sense of distributions, i.e.,

\rho_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(t)\!-\!\rho_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}(0)=\int_{0}^{t}[{\mathbb A}(\tau)\rho(\tau)]_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}d\tau.

(2.20)

Our main result is the following theorem.
Denote by
{\rm HS}^{+}
the set of nonnegative
\rho\in{\rm HS}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let conditions
H1–H2
hold.
Then
there exist
continuous linear operators
U(t):{\rm HS}\to{\rm HS}
with
t\geq 0

such that

i) For each
\rho^{0}\in{\rm HS}
, the trajectory
\rho(t)=U(t)\rho^{0}\in{\cal T}
is
the generalised solution
to (
1.2
) with initial condition (
1.8
);

ii)
\rho(t)\in{\rm HS}^{+}
for
t\geq 0
if
\rho^{0}\in{\rm HS}^{+}
,
and
the bounds (
1.9
) hold.

The proof relies on finite-dimensional
Faedo–Galerkin approximations defined via
finite-dimensional approximations
of the annihilation and creation operators,
and on uniform a priori bounds in the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

Remark 2.5
.

i)
Theorem

also holds for dissipation operators

where each

admits the structure (

)–(

) with the corresponding

. The proof is unchanged.

ii) The trace conservation holds for every finite-dimensional approximation,
however, we cannot conclude the conservation in the limit.

3
Nonpositivity of the dissipation operator

In this section we prove
the nonpositivity of the dissipation operator
D(t)

satisfying
H2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The operators
D(t)
with the structure (
2.13
), (
2.14
) are nonpositive in
{\cal D}
:

\langle\rho,D(t)\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}\leq 0,\qquad\rho\in{\cal D},\quad t\geq 0.

(3.1)

Proof.

We will omit the dependence on
t
.
For
\rho\in{\cal D}
,

\displaystyle\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\big(\rho D\rho\big)=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\Big(\rho\big(V\rho V^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}V^{\dagger}V\rho-\frac{1}{2}\rho V^{\dagger}V\big)\Big)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\big(\rho V\rho V^{\dagger}-\rho V^{\dagger}V\rho\big)=\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits\big(\rho V\rho V^{\dagger}-V^{\dagger}V\rho^{2}\big).

Now we use the fact that
\rho
is a finite rank Hermitian operator (
1.6
).
Then (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) imply that
the operators
\rho V\rho V^{\dagger}
and
V^{\dagger}V\rho^{2}

have only finite
number of nonzero entries (
2.10
), so their traces
are well-defined.
Moreover,

\rho
admits a finite spectral resolution in the orthonormal basis of its eigenvectors
e_{i}\in X_{\infty}
:

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\rho_{i}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}.

(3.3)

In this basis, the entries
\rho_{ij}=\rho_{i}\delta_{ij}
, and
the entries

V_{jk}=\langle e_{j},Ve_{k}\rangle

and
V^{\dagger}_{kl}=\langle e_{k},V^{\dagger}e_{l}\rangle

of the operators
V
and
V^{\dagger}
are well-defined.
Hence, (
3.2
) implies,
with summation in repeated indices,

\displaystyle\langle\rho,D\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\delta_{ij}V_{jk}\rho_{k}\delta_{kl}V^{\dagger}_{li}-V^{\dagger}_{kl}V_{lj}\rho_{j}^{2}\delta_{jk}=\rho_{i}V_{ik}\rho_{k}V^{\dagger}_{ki}-V^{\dagger}_{kl}V_{lk}\rho_{k}^{2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{i}V_{ik}\rho_{k}V^{\dagger}_{ki}-V^{\dagger}_{ki}V_{ik}\rho_{k}^{2}=V_{ik}V^{\dagger}_{ki}(\rho_{i}\rho_{k}-\rho_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(V_{ik}V^{\dagger}_{ki}(\rho_{i}\rho_{k}-\rho_{k}^{2})+V_{ki}V^{\dagger}_{ik}(\rho_{k}\rho_{i}-\rho_{i}^{2}))=-\frac{1}{2}|V_{ik}|^{2}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{k})^{2}\leq 0

since
V^{\dagger}_{ik}=\overline{V}_{ki}
. Hence, (
3.1
) is proved.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The proof of the nonpositivity
essentially depends on the symmetry of
\rho
.

4
Finite-dimensional approximations
and uniform bounds

Replace the Hilbert space
X
by
its finite-dimensional subspace

X_{\nu}=(|n\rangle\otimes s_{\pm}:n\leq\nu),\qquad\nu=1,2,\dots.

(4.1)

The subspace is invariant under action of the
annihilation operator
a
, and we define by
a_{\nu}
its restriction to
X_{\nu}
:

a_{\nu}[|n\rangle\otimes s]=[a|n\rangle]\otimes s,\qquad 0\leq n\leq\nu.

(4.2)

On the other hand,
X_{\nu}
is not invariant under action of the
creation operator
a^{\dagger}
, so we define
a^{\dagger}_{\nu}
as the adjoint to
a_{\nu}
:

a_{\nu}^{\dagger}[|n\rangle\otimes s]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[a^{\dagger}|n\rangle]\otimes s,&0\leq n<\nu\\
0,&n=\nu\end{array}\right|.

(4.3)

Remark 4.1
.

The definitions (
4.2
), (
4.3
) are equivalent to known matrix representations
of
a_{n,n^{\prime}}
and
a^{\dagger}_{n,n^{\prime}}
restricted to
n,n^{\prime}\leq\nu
.

Denote by
{\cal D}_{\nu}
the space of Hermitian operators (
1.6
) with
n,n^{\prime}\leq\nu
,
and define the finite-dimensional approximations of the JC dynamics
(
1.2
) by

\dot{\rho}_{\nu}(t)={\mathbb A}_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}(t):=-i[H_{\nu}(t),\rho_{\nu}(t)]+\gamma D_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

(4.4)

where
\rho_{\nu}(t)
is a linear operator in
X_{\nu}
,
and
H_{\nu}(t)
and
D_{\nu}(t)
are defined as
H(t)
and
D(t)
with
a
and
a^{\dagger}

replaced by
a_{\nu}
and
a^{\dagger}_{\nu}
respectively.
By the conditions
H1–H2
and (
4.2
)–(
4.3
),

H_{\nu}(t)
and
D_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}

are
Hermitian operators in
X_{\nu}
. Hence,

{\mathbb A}_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}

is also Hermitian, so the linear equation (
4.4
)
admits a unique global Hermitian solution
\rho_{\nu}(t)\in C(0,\infty;{\cal D}_{\nu})
for any Hermitian initial state
\rho_{\nu}(0)\in{\cal D}_{\nu}
.
Since
\rho^{0}\in{\rm HS}
,

\rho^{0}=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\rho_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{0}|n,s\rangle\langle n^{\prime},s^{\prime}|,\qquad\|\rho^{0}\|_{\rm HS}=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}|\rho_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{0}|^{2}<\infty.

(4.5)

Define the approximate initial state by

\rho_{\nu}^{0}=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\leq\nu}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\rho_{n,s;n^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{0}|n,s\rangle\langle n^{\prime},s^{\prime}|.

(4.6)

It is the Hermitian operator since
\rho^{0}
is, and

\|\rho_{\nu}^{0}\|_{\rm HS}\leq\|\rho^{0}\|_{\rm HS}.

(4.7)

Obviously,
\rho_{\nu}^{0}\geq 0
if
\rho^{0}\geq 0
.
Denote by
\rho_{\nu}(t)
the solution to (
4.4
) with the initial state
\rho^{0}_{\nu}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

i) The a priori bounds hold

\|\rho_{\nu}(t)\|_{\rm HS}\leq\|\rho^{0}_{\nu}\|_{\rm HS},\qquad t\geq t_{0},\quad\nu=0,1,\dots.

(4.8)

ii)
\rho_{\nu}(t)\geq 0
for
t\geq 0
if
\rho^{0}_{\nu}\geq 0
.

Proof.

ad i)

It suffices to prove
that
the generator
{\mathbb A}_{\nu}(t)

is nonpositive in
X_{\nu}
:

\langle\rho,{\mathbb A}_{\nu}(t)\rho\rangle_{\rm HS}\leq 0,\qquad\rho\in X_{\nu}.

(4.9)

The generator can be written as

{\mathbb A}_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}=K_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}+\gamma D_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu},

(4.10)

where

\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!K_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}=-i[H_{\nu}(t),\rho_{\nu}],\qquad D_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}=[V_{\nu}\rho_{\nu},V_{\nu}^{\dagger}]+[V_{\nu},\rho_{\nu}V_{\nu}^{\dagger}].

(4.11)

Here
V_{\nu}=V_{\nu}(t)
is obtained from the polynomial
V(t)
by replacement
of
a
and
a^{\dagger}
by
a_{\nu}
and
a_{\nu}^{\dagger}
, respectively, while
V_{\nu}^{\dagger}=(V_{\nu}(t))^{\dagger}
by (
4.3
).
For
\rho_{\nu}\in{\cal D}_{\nu}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{\nu},K_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}\rangle_{\rm HS}=-i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits(\rho_{\nu}^{\dagger}[H_{\nu}(t),\rho_{\nu}])=-i\mathop{\rm tr\,}\nolimits(\rho_{\nu}[H_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}-\rho_{\nu}H(t)])=0.

(4.12)

On the other hand,

\langle\rho_{\nu},D_{\nu}(t)\rho_{\nu}\rangle_{\rm HS}\leq 0,\qquad\rho_{\nu}\in{\cal D}_{\nu}
