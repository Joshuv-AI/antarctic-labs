A very robust Ramsey theorem for matchings
Peter Keevash, Peleg Michaeli

Abstract
Our main result is a robust generalisation of the Cockayne–Lorimer theorem on the multicolour Ramsey number of matchings. It is moreover a generalisation of the transference generalisation of Cockayne–Lorimer, which (informally) says that the random graph G ∼ G ⁡ ( n , p ) G\sim G(n,p) with n ​ p → ∞ np\to\infty whp 1 1 1 With high probability, that is, with probability tending to 1 1 as n → ∞ n\to\infty . has essentially the same Ramsey matching properties as the complete graph K n K_{n} . We show, somewhat surprisingly, that the same is true under the rather weak robustness assumption that G G is an s s -connector (i.e. G ¯ \overline{G} is K s , s K_{s,s} –free) with s = o ⁡ ( n ) s=o(n) . Moreover, we show that such G G has only an additive O ⁡ ( s ) O(s) loss with respect to K n K_{n} for monochromatic matchings, which is essentially sharp. Our proof adapts a compression algorithm based on Gallai–Edmonds decompositions that we developed previously for generalised Ramsey–Turán problems.

3.4

Proof of the main theorem

We conclude this section by proving our main theorem.
For
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}
write
(\mathbf{v})^{+}
for the vector in
\mathbb{N}^{q}

in which the
j
th coordinate is
\max\{v_{j},0\}
.

3.10
.

Let
q,s\geq 1
be integers.
Suppose
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{q})\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
satisfies

\mathbf{z}_{\min}=\min_{j}z_{j}\geq s-1

and
\|\mathbf{z}\|_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{\min}\geq(q+13)(s-1)
.
For all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q}
and
n\geq\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+1
,
if
G
is an
n
-vertex
s
-connector
then
G\to(\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{z})^{+}K_{2}
.

We note that
Theorem

1.1
is immediate from
Lemma

3.10

applied with
\mathbf{z}=7(s-1)\mathbf{1}_{q}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}=\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{z}
,
noting that
\|\mathbf{z}\|_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{\min}=7(q+1)(s-1)\geq(q+13)(s-1)
.

Proof.

By passing to an induced subgraph of order
\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+1
,
which inherits the
s
-connector property from
G
,
we may assume that
n=\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+1
.
Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{0},G_{1},\dots,G_{q})
be a fully-coloured
q
-colouring of
G
.
If there exists
j\in[q]
such that
\nu(G_{j})>n/2-s
, we are done,
since for every
j
,
n\geq 2t_{j}
,
and this would imply
\nu(G_{j})\geq t_{j}-s+1\geq t_{j}-z_{j}
, as required.

We therefore assume that
\nu(G_{j})\leq n/2-s
for all
j\in[q]
.
In particular, the assumption of
Lemma

3.9
hold.
Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime},T,C^{*}=\textsc{Distil}(\mathcal{G})
,
and write
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=(G_{0}^{\prime},G_{1}^{\prime},\dots,G_{q}^{\prime})
.
Let
\eta,\kappa
be the constants from
Lemma

3.9
,
and recall (by
Lemma

3.9
(
1
))
that

\kappa\geq\frac{1}{2}(n-|T|-(q+13)(s-1)-1).

Rearranging, we have
|T|\geq n-(q+13)(s-1)-1-2\kappa
.
We may also assume that
\kappa\leq t_{\eta}-z_{\eta}-1
,
as otherwise, by
Lemma

3.9
(
2
),

\nu(G_{\eta})\geq\nu(G_{\eta}^{\prime})\geq\kappa\geq t_{\eta}-z_{\eta}
, and we are done.
Assume for contradiction that
\nu(G_{j})\leq t_{j}-z_{j}-1
for all

.
Then, by
Lemma

3.9
(
3
),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}-\|\mathbf{z}\|_{1}\geq\nu_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle\geq\kappa+|T|\geq n-(q+13)(s-1)-1-\kappa

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+1-(q+13)(s-1)-t_{\eta}+z_{\eta}

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+1-(q+13)(s-1)+\mathbf{z}_{\min}.

Rearranging, we obtain

,
contradicting the assumption.
∎

4

Discussion

This concluding section discusses some applications
and potential future research directions.

4.1

Sharpness

We did not try to optimise the additive error of
7(q+1)(s-1)
in
Theorem

1.1
.
A slightly more careful analysis in
Lemma

3.10
reduces it to
\sim qs

if
s>1
and
q\to\infty
.
It seems plausible that the true error term should be linear in
s
and independent of
q
.

1
.

Is there a constant
C
such that the following holds:
for all integers
q,s\geq 1
,
all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q}
,
and all
n\geq\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+1+C(s-1)
,
if
G
is an
n
-vertex
s
-connector
then
G\to\mathbf{t}K_{2}
?

On the other hand, for some choices of
\mathbf{t}

the lower bound from
Proposition

1.2
can be improved.
Indeed, fix
q\geq 1
and
s\geq 2
, let
\mathbf{t}=(2,\dots,2)\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q}
,
and let
G
be the complete split graph with a clique of size
q
and an independent set of size
2s-1
.
Then
G
is an
s
-connector and
|V(G)|=q+2s-1=\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+2s-3
.
However,
G\not\to\mathbf{t}K_{2}
:
label the clique vertices by
c_{1},\dots,c_{q}
,
and colour each edge
e
with the minimum
j\in[q]
such that
c_{j}\in e
.
Then each colour class is a star,
so every monochromatic matching has size at most
1
.
Thus any positive answer to
1
would require
C\geq 2
.
We thank an anonymous colleague for drawing our attention to an error in an earlier version of this discussion.

4.2

Transference

Fix
q\geq 1
.
Say that a sequence of
n
-vertex graphs
(G_{n})

is
q
-asymptotically Cockayne–Lorimer

(
q
-
aCL
),
if there exists
\xi(n)=o(n)

such that for every
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q}
,
if
n\geq\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+\xi(n)

then
G_{n}\to\mathbf{t}K_{2}
.
Say further that
(G_{n})
is
asymptotically Cockayne–Lorimer
(
aCL
)
if for every fixed
C>0
it is
q
-
aCL
for every integer
q\in[1,C]
.

Theorem

1.1
implies that sublinear connectors are
aCL
.
It is not hard to see that almost-complete graphs
are sublinear connectors, and hence
aCL
.
This
defect version
of Cockayne–Lorimer
was proved in
[
9
]

and then combined with the sparse regularity lemma
to obtain the transference result that if
np\to\infty

then the binomial random graph
G(n,p)
is
whp

aCL
.
This now follows immediately from
Theorem

1.1
,
since
G(n,p)
with
np\to\infty
is
whp
a sublinear connector.
Moreover, the same observation gives, e.g., that for
d\to\infty
,
the random regular graph
\mathcal{G}_{n,d}
is also
whp

aCL
,
and that for
d\gg\lambda
, an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph (i.e.
d
-regular spectral expander) is
aCL
.

4.3

Beating the pigeonhole bound

A straightforward pigeonhole argument shows that
for every graph
G
and every
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q}
,
if
\nu(G)>\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}
then
G\to\mathbf{t}K_{2}
.
Say that
G
is
q
-weakly Cockayne–Lorimer
(
q
-
wCL
)
if there exists
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q}
with
\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}\geq\nu(G)

such that
G\to\mathbf{t}K_{2}
.
Note that it is monotone in
q
,
in the sense that
q
-
wCL
implies
q^{\prime}
-
wCL
for every
q^{\prime}\geq q
.
We do not know whether this property admits a simple characterisation,
so we will just discuss a few examples.

4.1
.

If
G
is bipartite then it is not
q
-
wCL
for any
q\geq 1
.

Proof.

Write
\nu=\nu(G)
.
Fix
q\geq 1
,
and let
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{q})\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q}
with
\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}\geq\nu
.
By Kőnig’s theorem, there exists a vertex cover
X
of size
\nu
.
Partition
X=X_{1}\cup\dots\cup X_{q}
such that
|X_{j}|<t_{j}
for all
j\in[q]
.
Colour every edge of
G
which is incident to
X_{j}
with colour
j
.
Since
X_{j}
is a vertex cover for the
j
-coloured graph
G_{j}
,
it holds that
\nu(G_{j})\leq|X_{j}|<t_{j}
,
hence
G\not\to\mathbf{t}K_{2}
.
∎

The proof above extends to any graph that is
Kőnig–Egerváry

(has equal matching and cover numbers) such as a
complete split graph
,
constructed by taking a clique of size
n/2
(for even
n
)
and joining it completely to an independent set of size
n/2
.
This graph is Kőnig–Egerváry and thus not
wCL
,
while being very far from being bipartite.
On the other hand:

4.2
.

The odd cycle
C_{\ell}
is
2
-
wCL
for every

.

Proof.

Let
q=2
and write

(so

).
Let

and

, and note that
r,b\leq k
.
Take
\mathbf{t}=(r,b)

\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}=k=\nu(C_{\ell})
.
Consider a
2
-colouring of

,
and let

denote the corresponding colour subgraphs.
Assume for contradiction that
\nu(R)<r\leq k
and
\nu(B)<b\leq k
.
In particular,

.
This implies that

are linear forests,

But this implies that

∎

The triangle, however, is not
q
-
wCL
for any

.
Note that odd cycles only just beat the pigeonhole bound,
so we next ask when the win is more significant, via the parameter

\rho_{q}(G):=\sup\left\{\frac{\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+1}{\nu(G)}:\ \mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q},\ G\to\mathbf{t}K_{2}\right\}.

Thus
\rho_{q}(G)\geq 1
for every graph,
and being
q
-
wCL
is equivalent to having
\rho_{q}(G)>1
.
We are interested in graph sequences
(G_{n})

for which there exists
\varepsilon>0

with
\rho_{q}(G_{n})\geq 1+\varepsilon
for all sufficiently large

.
Formally, say that
(G_{n})
is
q
-wCL

if

Note that

1.1
implies that
if
G
is an
n

-connector
then,
by choosing
\mathbf{t}
as balanced as possible,

\rho_{q}(G)\geq(1-o(1))\frac{2q}{q+1}.

In particular, as
G(n,d/n)
and
\mathcal{G}_{n,d}
are both,
whp
,

\beta n
-connectors for

which tends to

as

the theorem implies that, for sufficiently large

they are
2
-
wCL

whp
.
It does not seem to be obvious, however,
how low can

be (as a function of

)
to guarantee that.

2
.

Is the random cubic graph
\mathcal{G}_{n,3}

whp

2
-
wCL
?

3
.

What is the minimum

for which

is
whp

q

?

Note that in

.

For every integer

Proof sketch.

Choose

Let

4.4

Hypergraphs

The hypergraph analogue of the Cockayne–Lorimer theorem,
due to Alon, Frankl and Lovász
[
1
]
,
shows that if
n\geq(r-1)\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+1

then
K_{n}^{r}\to\mathbf{t}K_{r}^{r}
, which is sharp.
Here
K_{n}^{r}
denotes the complete
r
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph
(so
K_{r}^{r}
is a single
r
-edge).
Defect and transference versions of this result have been obtained in
[
8
]
.
Since for
r=2
our main result reproves the defect and transference versions,
it is natural to ask whether an analogous extension holds for
r>2
.

We propose the following generalisation of the notion of an
s
-connector.
An
r
-uniform hypergraph is an
s
-connector

if for every collection of pairwise disjoint vertex sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{r}

with
|A_{i}|\geq s
for all
i\in[r]
,
there exists an edge
e=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
of
H

such that
v_{i}\in A_{i}
for all
i\in[r]
.

4
.

Let
q,s\geq 1
and
r\geq 2
be integers.
There exists
\varphi=\varphi_{q,r}:(0,1]\to(0,\infty)

with
\varphi(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0

such that the following holds:
For all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{q}
and
n\geq(r-1)\|\mathbf{t}\|_{\infty}+\Lambda_{\mathbf{t}}+1+n\varphi(s/n)
,
if
H
is an
r
-uniform
n
-vertex
s
-connector
then
H\to\mathbf{t}K_{r}^{r}
.

If
4
is true it would be a significant strengthening
of the defect and transference versions of the Alon–Frankl–Lovász Theorem.
