Crossed product functors associated to ℓ p \ell^{p} -pseudofunctions
Jacek Krajczok, Ebrahim Samei, Timo Siebenand, Adam Skalski

Abstract
. We show that the ℓ p \ell^{p} -pseudofunctions, which were recently shown to lead to exotic completions of group C ∗ C^{*} -algebras by Wiersma and the second named author, can be used to construct well-behaved crossed product functors in the sense of Buss, Echterhoff and Willett. The construction proceeds via introducing certain Banach algebras, related to operators acting on Hilbert valued ℓ p \ell^{p} -spaces, which a priori depend on the choice of a Hilbert space representation of the underlying C*-algebra. We prove that, in fact, the resulting algebras are isomorphic (with the isomorphism constant depending only on p p ), and hence their C*-envelopes are isometrically isomorphic. This, in particular, means that the construction genuinely generalises the one studied earlier in the group case. The tools we develop allow us to show that for certain non-amenable actions, the resulting crossed product completions must indeed be exotic.

1.
Introduction

Discrete, or more generally, locally compact groups have been a source of interesting and important examples of operator algebras since the beginning of the theory of the latter in the 1940s. In particular, the constructions of the universal and the reduced C*-algebra of a discrete group
G
serve as archetypical examples of ‘abstract’ and ‘concrete’ C*-algebras, and to the very day play a central position in the study of operator algebras and their interactions with the geometric group theory. Recent years have brought a lot of interest in the so-called ‘exotic’ group C*-algebras, i.e. C*-completions of the group ring
\mathbb{C}[G]
sitting naturally between the universal and the reduced one. One of the starting points of their study was the article
[
BG
]
, where Brown and Guentner introduced the so-called
\ell^{p}
-completions of the group ring, which spurred a lot of further activity (see
[
SW2
]
and references therein). Choosing a completion as above corresponds to fixing a certain family of (weak equivalence classes of) Hilbert space representations of
G
, and indeed most of the constructions of exotic group C*-algebras begin by setting up such a family. The work of the second named author and Wiersma in
[
SW2
]
, inspired by their earlier article
[
SW1
]
, took a different perspective. In the first step, they considered the shift representation of
G
on
\ell^{p}(G)
, with fixed
p\in[1,\infty)
, leading in a natural way to a Banach
*
-algebra
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G)
, and only in the second step the relevant C*-algebra appeared, as the C*-envelope of
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G)
. One should note that in several cases
C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G))
coincides with the Brown-Guentner algebra
C^{*}_{\ell^{p}}(G)
, and in fact it is still not known whether they are ever different.

On the other hand, soon after the work of Brown and Guentner, the article
[
BGM
]
, motivated by looking for an appropriate formulation of the Baum-Connes conjecture with coefficients, initiated a systematic study of the ‘exotic’ crossed products. Again, it had been known for a long time that given an action of
G
on a C*-algebra
A
, one can form canonical universal/reduced crossed product algebras. The key insight of
[
BGM
]
, later fleshed out primarily in
[
BEW
]
, was that one should not look at potentially exotic crossed product completions separately for each C*-dynamical system, but rather view them simultaneously, as a prescription for finding C*-norms for all possible
G
-actions. This has led to a notion of a
crossed product functor
, with several notable examples related to the Brown-Guentner construction, but also to the study of exotic coactions initiated in
[
KLQ
]
. The article
[
BEW
]
and its successors, such as
[
AEES
]
, introduced also several natural and important properties of crossed product functors, among which we would like to distinguish being a
correspondence functor
(the property roughly means that the construction behaves well with respect to
G
-equivariant C*-correspondences, but admits also several equivalent reformulations).

The aim of our paper is to place the construction of the
C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G))
in the framework of crossed product functors. To that end, similarly to the path chosen in
[
SW2
]
, to any action of a discrete group
G
on a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, we associate first a Banach
*
-algebra
\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)
, constructed via a Banach space representation of the relevant convolution algebra on the
\ell^{p}
-space with values in a Hilbert space. Although the construction demands a choice of a representation of
A
, we exploit the Kahane-Khintchine inequality and the Voiculescu’s theorem to prove that different choices lead to isomorphic objects, with the isomorphism bound dependent only on
p
. Then the desired C*-algebra arrives as the C*-envelope of
\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)
. Let
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
denote the functor
C^{*}\circ\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;\cdot)
. The following theorem summarizes some of the main results of our work (see Theorem
3.10
, Corollary
3.12
, Theorem
4.2
, and Theorem
4.6
).

Theorem A
.

The functor
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G

from the category of
G
-C*-algebras to the category of C*-algebras is a crossed product correspondence functor. The C*-algebra
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
is independent from the choice of the representation for
A
. Moreover,
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;\mathbb{C}))
is canonically isometrically isomorphic to the C*-algebra
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G))
introduced by Wiersma and the second named author in
[
SW2
]
.

One should note that this implies, in particular, that the functor
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
is indeed different from the Brown-Guentner functor
\rtimes_{BG_{p}}G
(as the latter is not a correspondence functor). Furthermore, we show that if the action of
G
on
A
admits an invariant state, then the C*-algebra
C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G))
embeds canonically into
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
. This means that we can directly use several results of
[
SW2
]
to show that the functor we consider here is, in general, genuinely exotic, by which we mean that none of the canonical surjections
A\rtimes_{u}G\to C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
and
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))\to A\rtimes_{r}G
is isometric (see Theorem
5.1
). We also exhibit certain instances where we can directly prove, using the random walk and Furstenberg entropy techniques introduced in a similar context in [ASdLSW
1-2
], that
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
are exotic without assuming the existence of an invariant state (see Theorem
5.6
and Remark
5.7
).

The detailed plan of the paper is as follows: after this introduction, in Section
2
we recall the basic aspects of the theory of crossed product functors, mostly following
[
BEW
]
. Section
3
is devoted to the study of Banach algebras
\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)
associated to a
G
-C*-algebra
A
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Here we show that the construction (up to a Banach algebra isomorphism) does not depend on the choice of the representation of
A
, and establish several functorial properties of the functor
\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;\cdot)
. This sets the ground for Section
4
, where we define our C*-algebraic crossed product functor
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
and prove that it is indeed a correspondence functor.
Finally, in Section
5
, we treat several examples where
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
leads to genuinely exotic crossed products.

Throughout
\otimes
denotes the minimal C*-tensor product of C*-algebras. Hilbert space scalar products are linear on the right side. We will use
G
to denote a discrete group.

2.
Crossed product functors and their properties

We shall describe here briefly basic aspects of the theory of crossed product functors, as introduced by Buss, Echterhoff and Willett in
[
BEW
]
. Note that the theory has been developed for general locally compact groups, but we shall stick to the discrete context.

Let
G
be a discrete group. By a
G
-C*-algebra we understand a C*-algebra
A
equipped with a homomorphism
\alpha\colon G\to\textup{Aut}(A)
. We shall denote by
A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G
the
algebraic crossed product
, so the space of finitely supported functions on
G
with values in
A
equipped with the
*
-algebra structure induced by the action
\alpha
; note that sometimes it will be useful to speak also about the corresponding ‘
\ell^{1}
-algebra’, i.e.
\ell^{1}(G;A)
.
We shall often speak of the category of
G
-C*-algebras, with the morphisms given by
G
-equivariant
*
-homomorphisms, and of the category of C*-algebras with
*
-homomorphisms as morphisms.
Note that if
A
is a
G
-C*-algebra as above and

\mathcal{M}(A)
is the multiplier algebra of
A
, then we also obtain a canonical homomorphism
\overline{\alpha}\colon G\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}(A))
. We can thus talk about
G
-equivariant
*
-homomorphisms
\varphi\colon C\to\mathcal{M}(A)

(here
C
is another
G
-C*-algebra with the action
\gamma
) by saying
\varphi
is

G
-equivariant if

\displaystyle\varphi(\gamma_{s}(c))=\overline{\alpha}_{s}(\varphi(c))

for all
s\in G
and
c\in C
.

Let
-\rtimes_{u}G
(resp.
-\rtimes_{r}G
) denote the well-known
universal crossed product functor (resp. reduced crossed product functor) from the category of
G
-C*-algebras to the category of C*-algebras.
There is a natural transformation
\Lambda\colon-\rtimes_{u}G\Rightarrow-\rtimes_{r}G

consisting of surjective
*
-homomorphisms such that

\Lambda_{A}|_{A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G}=\mathrm{id}_{A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G}
.

The notion of a crossed product functor formalizes the idea of having a ‘well-behaved’ method of constructing ‘intermediate’ crossed products.

Definition 2.1
.

A
crossed product functor

-\rtimes_{\mu}G
is a functor
from the category of
G
-C*-algebras to the category of C*-algebras, together with natural transformations

\displaystyle q\colon-\rtimes_{u}G\Rightarrow-\rtimes_{\mu}G\mbox{ and }s\colon-\rtimes_{\mu}G\rightarrow-\rtimes_{r}G

consisting of surjective
*
-homomorphisms such that
s\circ q=\Lambda
.

Note that the
*
-homomorphisms
q_{A}\colon A\rtimes_{u}G\to A\rtimes_{\mu}G
and
s_{A}\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to A\rtimes_{r}G
for a
G
-C*-algebra
A
are just the continuous
extensions of the identity map
\mathrm{id}_{A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G}
. Whenever convenient, we will write also
\rtimes_{\mu}G
instead of
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
.

Let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra. The universal crossed product
A\rtimes_{u}G
comes with
a (universal) covariant representation
(\iota_{A},\iota_{G})\colon(A,G)\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{u}G)
.
Similarly we have the reduced/regular covariant representation
(\iota_{A,r},\iota_{G,r})\colon(A,G)\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{r}G)
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be any crossed product functor. We write

\displaystyle\iota_{A,\mu}

\displaystyle:=q_{A}\circ\iota_{A}\colon A\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G);

\displaystyle\iota_{G,\mu}

\displaystyle:=q_{A}\circ\iota_{G}\colon G\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G).

Furthermore, we write
C^{*}_{\mu}(G)
for
\mathbb{C}\rtimes_{\mu}G
.
As is well known, the maps
\iota_{G}
, and respectively
\iota_{G,r}
, extend canonically to
*
-homomorphisms

\displaystyle\iota_{G}\colon C^{*}_{u}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{u}G),\quad\iota_{G,r}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{r}G)

given by

\displaystyle(\iota_{G,\mu}(f)g)(t)=\sum_{s\in G}f(s)\alpha_{s}(g(s^{-1}t))

for
f\in C_{c}(G)
,
g\in A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G
and
t\in G
.

We are ready to formulate a first (relatively) weak property
for a crossed product functor.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a crossed product functor. We say that
-\rtimes_{\mu}G

(i)

is
functorial for generalised morphisms

if for all
G
-C*-algebras
A
and
B
and every
G
-equivariant
*
-homomorphism

\varphi\colon A\to\mathcal{M}(B)
, there exists a
*
-homomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi\rtimes_{\mu}G\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to\mathcal{M}(B\rtimes_{\mu}G)

given by

\displaystyle((\varphi\rtimes_{\mu}G)(f)g)(t)=\sum_{s\in G}\varphi(f(s))\alpha_{s}(g(s^{-1}t))

for all
f\in A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G
,
g\in B\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G
and

t\in G
;

(ii)

has the
ideal property
if for all

G
-C*-algebras
A
and all
G
-invariant closed ideals
I
of
A
the inclusion map

\iota\colon I\to A
yields an injective
*
-homomorphism

\iota\rtimes_{\mu}G\colon I\rtimes_{\mu}G\to A\rtimes_{\mu}G
.

In fact, the properties above are equivalent.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
BEW
, Lemma 3.3]

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a crossed product functor. Then
\rtimes_{\mu}G
is functorial
for generalised morphisms if and only if
\rtimes_{\mu}G
has the ideal property.

Both the universal
-\rtimes_{u}G
and the reduced
-\rtimes_{r}G
crossed product
functors have the ideal property. We obtain the following simple observation.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a crossed product functor that is functorial for generalised morphisms,
and let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra. Then the group homomorphism

\displaystyle\iota_{G,\mu}\colon G\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G)

extends canonically to a
*
-homomorphism

\displaystyle\iota_{G,\mu}\colon C_{\mu}^{*}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G)

given by

\displaystyle\iota_{G,\mu}(f)g(t)=\sum_{s\in G}f(s)\alpha_{s}(g(s^{-1}t))

for
f\in C_{c}(G)
,
g\in A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G
and
t\in G
.

Proof.

Just use the obvious embedding
\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{M}(A)
.
∎

The next property, dating back to
[
KLQ
]
, is very useful, as it allows us to use ‘good’ functions on
G
as the source of Schur multiplier maps on the relevant crossed products.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a crossed product functor. It is said to
admit
coactions
if for all
G
-C*-algebras
A
the covariant representation

\displaystyle(\iota_{A,\mu}\otimes 1,\iota_{G,\mu}\otimes\iota_{G,u})\colon(A,G)\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G\otimes C^{*}_{u}(G))

yields a
*
-homomorphism

\displaystyle\beta_{A\rtimes_{\mu}G}:=(\iota_{A,\mu}\otimes 1)\rtimes_{\mu}(\iota_{G,\mu}\otimes\iota_{G,u})\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G\otimes C^{*}_{u}(G)).

Here ‘yields’ means that the
*
-homomorphism

(\iota_{A,\mu}\otimes 1)\rtimes_{u}(\iota_{G,\mu}\otimes\iota_{G,u})\colon A\rtimes_{u}G\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G\otimes C^{*}_{u}(G))

associated to
the covariant representation above factors through the quotient map

A\rtimes_{u}G\to A\rtimes_{\mu}G
.

Again, the universal and reduced crossed product functors do admit
coactions.

Remark 2.6
.

Suppose that
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
is a crossed product functor that admits coactions. As a special
case, we obtain a comultiplication like map

\displaystyle\delta_{G}=\iota_{G,\mu}\otimes\iota_{G}\colon C_{\mu}^{*}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(C^{*}_{\mu}(G)\otimes C^{*}_{u}(G)).

Now, suppose
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
is a normalised positive definite function on
G
and
let us denote the corresponding state on
C^{*}_{u}(G)
also by
\phi
. Standard results on strict slice maps on multiplier algebras (
[
Lan
]
) yield by a strict extension a completely positive map
\mathrm{id}_{A\rtimes_{\mu}G}\otimes\phi\colon\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G\otimes C^{*}_{u}(G))\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G)
. Define

\displaystyle m_{\phi}=(\mathrm{id}_{A\rtimes_{\mu}G}\otimes\phi)\circ\beta_{A\rtimes_{\mu}G}\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G)

and note that we have

(2.1)

m_{\phi}(f)=\phi f\qquad(f\in A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G),

so that in fact the map
m_{\phi}
takes values in
A\rtimes_{\mu}G
(and naturally is determined by the formula (
2.1
) above). We shall see later that for even better behaved crossed product functors and certain choices of
\phi
we can lift the map above to a map
\widetilde{m_{\phi}}\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to A\rtimes_{u}G
.

The next property, similarly to the ideal property, admits several equivalent descriptions.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a crossed product functor. We say that
-\rtimes_{\mu}G

(i)

has the
cp-map property
, if for all
G
-C*-algebras
A
and
B
and every
G
-equivariant completely positive map

\varphi\colon A\to B
there is a completely positive map
\varphi\rtimes_{\mu}G\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to B\rtimes_{\mu}G
given by

\displaystyle\varphi\rtimes_{\mu}G(f)=\varphi\circ f

for all
f\in A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G
;

(ii)

has the
projection property
, if for all
G
-C*-algebras

A
and all
G
-invariant projections
p\in\mathcal{M}(A)
the inclusion

j\colon pAp\to A
yields an inclusion
j\rtimes_{\mu}G\colon pAp\rtimes_{\mu}G\to A\rtimes_{\mu}G
;

(iii)

has the
hereditary subalgebra property
if for all

G
-C*-algebras
A
and all
G
-invariant hereditary subalgebras
B
of
A
, the inclusion map

\iota\colon B\to A
yields an injective
*
-homomorphism

\iota\rtimes_{\mu}G\colon B\rtimes_{\mu}G\to A\rtimes_{\mu}G
.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
BEW
, Theorem 4.9]

All the properties in Definition
2.7
are equivalent. A crossed product
functor that satisfies these properties is
called a
correspondence functor
.

Both the reduced
-\rtimes_{r}G
and the universal
-\rtimes_{u}G
crossed product functors are correspondence functors. The first part of the next fact is also contained in
[
BEW
, Theorem 4.9]
, and the second is
[
BEW
, Theorem 5.6]
.

Theorem 2.9
.

A correspondence functor has the ideal property and admits coactions.

The next two results are proved in
[
AEES
]
: the first is
[
AEES
, Proposition 2.8]
, the second is
[
AEES
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a correspondence crossed product functor and let
A
be a C*-algebra equipped with a trivial
G
-action. Then the images of
*
-homomorphisms

\iota_{A,\mu}\colon A\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G)
and

\iota_{G,\mu}\colon C_{\mu}^{*}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{\mu}G)
commute,

\iota_{A,\mu}(a)\iota_{G,\mu}(b)\in A\rtimes_{\mu}G
for all

a\in A
and
b\in C^{*}_{\mu}(G)
and the
*
-homomorphism

\displaystyle\iota_{A,\mu}\odot\iota_{G,\mu}\colon A\odot C_{\mu}^{*}(G)\to A\rtimes_{\mu}G

given by

\displaystyle\iota_{A,\mu}\odot\iota_{G,\mu}(a\otimes b)=\iota_{A,\mu}(a)\iota_{G,\mu}(b)

for
a\in A
and
b\in C_{\mu}^{*}(G)
is injective and has dense image.

In particular,
A\rtimes_{\mu}G
is a C*-tensor product of
A
and
C^{*}_{\mu}(G)
.
We will write
A\otimes_{\mu}C^{*}_{\mu}(G)
for
A\rtimes_{\mu}G
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a correspondence crossed product functor and let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra.
The
*
-homomorphisms

\iota_{A}\otimes_{\mu}1\colon A\to\mathcal{M}((A\rtimes_{u}G)\otimes_{\mu}C_{\mu}^{*}(G))

induced by

\displaystyle(\iota_{A}\otimes_{\mu}1)(a)(b\otimes c)=\iota_{A}(a)b\otimes c

for
a\in A
,
b\in A\rtimes_{u}G
and
c\in C^{*}_{\mu}(G)
,
and

\iota_{G}\otimes_{\mu}\iota_{G,\mu}\colon G\to\mathcal{M}((A\rtimes_{u}G)\otimes_{\mu}C_{\mu}^{*}(G))

induced by

\displaystyle(\iota_{G}\otimes_{\mu}\iota_{G,\mu})(s)(b\otimes c)=\iota_{G}(s)b\otimes\iota_{G,\mu}(s)c

for all
s\in G
,
b\in A\rtimes_{u}G
and
c\in C_{\mu}^{*}(G)

define a nondegenerate covariant representation of
A
. Its integrated form

(\iota_{A}\otimes_{\mu}1)\rtimes_{u}(\iota_{G}\otimes_{\mu}\iota_{G,\mu})\colon A\rtimes_{u}G\to\mathcal{M}((A\rtimes_{u}G)\otimes_{\mu}C_{\mu}^{*}(G))

factors through

, i.e., there is a unique injective
*
-homomorphism

\gamma_{A\rtimes_{\mu}G}:=(\iota_{A}\otimes_{\mu}1)\rtimes_{\mu}(\iota_{G}\otimes_{\mu}\iota_{G,\mu})\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to\mathcal{M}((A\rtimes_{u}G)\otimes_{\mu}C_{\mu}^{*}(G))

such that
(\iota_{A}\otimes_{\mu}1)\rtimes_{u}(\iota_{G}\otimes_{\mu}\iota_{G,\mu})=((\iota_{A}\otimes_{\mu}1)\rtimes_{\mu}(\iota_{G}\otimes_{\mu}\iota_{G,\mu}))\circ q_{A}
.

The above lemma allows us to construct the ‘
\mu
-to-full’ Schur multiplier maps mentioned in Remark
2.6
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a correspondence crossed product functor and let
\phi\in C^{*}_{\mu}(G)^{*}\subseteq B(G)
. Then the prescription

(2.2)

\widetilde{m_{\phi}}(f)=\phi f\qquad(f\in A\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}G),

extends to a completely bounded map (denoted by the same symbol)

\widetilde{m_{\phi}}\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to A\rtimes_{u}G,

with
\|\widetilde{m_{\phi}}\|_{cb}\leq\|\phi\|_{C^{*}_{\mu}(G)^{*}}
.

Proof.

Strict extension yields a completely bounded strict map
\mathrm{id}_{A\rtimes_{u}G}\otimes\phi\colon\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{u}G\otimes C^{*}_{\mu}(G))\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{u}G)
. Use Lemma
2.11
to define a bounded map

\displaystyle\widetilde{m_{\phi}}=(\mathrm{id}_{A\rtimes_{u}G}\otimes\phi)\circ q\circ\gamma_{A\rtimes_{\mu}G}\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to\mathcal{M}(A\rtimes_{u}G),

where
q\colon(A\rtimes_{u}G)\otimes_{\mu}C^{*}_{\mu}(G)\to(A\rtimes_{u}G)\otimes C^{*}_{\mu}(G)
is the obvious quotient map; its existence follows from Takesaki’s theorem saying that the spatial norm is the minimal
C^{*}
-norm on the algebraic tensor product. It is easy to check that
\widetilde{m_{\phi}}
satisfies the condition (
2.2
) and hence actually takes values in
A\rtimes_{u}G
.
∎

Finally we mention an easy observation which tells us that when the action of
G
on
A
admits an invariant state, and
\rtimes_{\mu}
is a

-correspondence functor, then the crossed product
A\rtimes_{\mu}G
‘remembers’
C^{*}_{\mu}(G)
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
-\rtimes_{\mu}G
be a correspondence crossed product functor, and let
G
act on a unital C*-algebra
A
with a
G
-invariant state. Then the canonical map
\iota_{G,\mu}\colon C^{*}_{\mu}(G)\to A\rtimes_{\mu}G
is isometric.

Proof.

A
G
-invariant state is, in particular, a
G
-invariant completely positive map from
A
to
\mathbb{C}
(which admits a
G
-invariant right inverse given by
\lambda\mapsto\lambda 1_{A}
) so that we obtain a completely positive, contractive map
\phi_{\mu}\colon A\rtimes_{\mu}G\to C^{*}_{\mu}(G)
, such that
\iota_{G,\mu}
is its right inverse. Hence
\iota_{G,\mu}
is isometric.
∎

3.
Banach *-algebras
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
and their properties

As in
[
SW2
]

3.1.
Definition of the
\ell^{p}
-convolution crossed product Banach algebra
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a
G
-C*-algebra. A
(Banach space) covariant representation
is a triple

(X,\pi,U)
consisting of a Banach space
X
, a contractive algebra-homomorphism

\pi\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(X)
and a group homomorphism

U\colon G\to\mbox{Isom}(X)
into the isometry group
\mbox{Isom}(X)
of
X
, such that

\displaystyle U_{s}\pi(a)=\pi(\alpha_{s}(a))U_{s}

holds for all
s\in G
and
a\in A
. We say
(X,\pi,U)
is a
C*-covariant representation

if
(X,\pi,U)
is a covariant representation,
X
an Hilbert space,
\pi
is a
*
-homomorphism
and
U(G)\subseteq\mathcal{U}(X)
, where
\mathcal{U}(X)
denotes the group
of unitary operators on
X
.

Remark 3.1
.

i) Let
\pi\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
*
-representation of the
G
-C*-algebra
A

on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
be the
\mathcal{H}
-valued
\ell^{p}
-space for a

p\in[1,\infty)
.
For every
a\in A
and
f\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi}_{p}(a)f\colon G\to\mathcal{H},\quad s\mapsto\pi(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a))(f(s)).

The inequality
\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}(a)f)(s)\|\leq\|a\|\|f(s)\|
for all
s\in G
implies

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\pi}_{p}(a)f\|_{p}\leq\|a\|\|f\|_{p},

so
\widetilde{\pi}_{p}(a)f\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
. The inequality (
3.1
)
yields further that

\widetilde{\pi}_{p}(a)\colon\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})\to\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
is
a well defined linear operator with norm bounded by
\|a\|
. We thus obtain a contractive
algebra-homomorphism
\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))
.
Now let
p>1
and let
q\in(1,\infty)
be the ‘conjugate index’, i.e.
1/p+1/q=1
. For
f\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
,
g\in\ell^{q}(G;\mathcal{H})
, write
\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{t\in G}\langle f(t),g(t)\rangle
for the canonical
sesquilinear
pairing. The operator
\widetilde{\pi}_{p}
is still ‘exterior involutive’, i.e.

(3.2)

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\pi}_{p}(a)f,g\rangle=\sum_{s\in G}\langle\pi(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a))f(s),g(s)\rangle=\sum_{s\in G}\langle f(s),\pi(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a^{*}))g(s)\rangle=\langle f,\widetilde{\pi}_{q}(a^{*})g\rangle.

Let

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}\colon G\to\mbox{Isom}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))

be the group homomorphism given by
(\lambda^{p}(s)f)(t)=f(s^{-1}t)
for
f\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
and
s,t\in G
.
Note that for
f\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
,
g\in\ell^{q}(G;\mathcal{H})
and
s\in G
we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda^{p}(s)f,g\rangle=\sum_{t\in G}\langle f(s^{-1}t),g(t)\rangle=\sum_{t\in G}\langle f(t),g(st)\rangle=\langle f,\lambda^{q}(s^{-1})g\rangle.

It is straightforward to check that
(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}),\widetilde{\pi}_{p},\lambda^{p})
is a covariant representation. We will call this construction the

\pi
-valued
p
-regular covariant representation
. We will occasionally omit the index
p
from
\lambda^{p}
or
\widetilde{\pi}_{p}
.

ii) Let
\varphi\colon A\to B
be a
G
-equivariant
*
-homomorphism between

G
-C*-algebras, let
\pi\colon B\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
*
-representation of
B
on
an Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
p\in[1,\infty)
. Furthermore,
let the triple
(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}),\widetilde{\pi}_{p},\lambda^{p})
be the

\pi
-valued
p
-regular covariant representation. Then

(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}),\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\circ\varphi,\lambda^{p})=(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}),\widetilde{(\pi\circ\varphi)_{p}},\lambda^{p})
.

Let
(A,\alpha)
be a
G
-C*-algebra and let
(X,\pi,U)
be a covariant representation of
(A,\alpha)
. For
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
, using the inequality

\|\pi(f(s))U_{s}x\|\leq\|f(s)\|\|x\|
valid for all
s\in G,x\in X
, we see that the function

G\ni s\mapsto\pi(f(s))U_{s}x\in X
is integrable and

\displaystyle\pi\rtimes U(f)\colon X\to X,\quad x\mapsto\sum_{s\in G}\pi(f(s))U_{s}x

is a linear operator, with the norm bounded by
\|f\|_{1}
. This yields a contractive Banach algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\pi\rtimes U\colon\ell^{1}(G;A)\to\mathcal{B}(X),\quad f\mapsto\pi\rtimes U(f).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be two
G
-C*-algebras and let
\varphi\colon A\to B
be a

G
-equivariant
*
-homomorphism. Then the ‘pointwise extension’ of
\varphi
, denoted
\varphi\rtimes_{1}G
, yields a contractive
*
-homomorphism from
\ell^{1}(G;A)
to
\ell^{1}(G;B)
.
If
\pi\colon B\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a
*
-representation,
p\in[1,\infty)
and

(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}),\tilde{\pi}_{p},\lambda^{p})
is the
\pi
-valued

p
-regular covariant representation, then

\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}((\varphi\rtimes_{1}G)f)=\widetilde{\pi\circ\varphi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f)

for all
f\in\ell^{1}(G;\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
and
g\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
. Then, using
that
\tilde{\pi}(\varphi(a))=\widetilde{\pi\circ\varphi}(a)
for all

, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}((\varphi\rtimes_{1}G)f)g=\sum_{s\in G}\tilde{\pi}(\varphi(f(s)))\lambda^{p}(s)g=\sum_{s\in G}\widetilde{\pi\circ\varphi}(f(s))\lambda^{p}(s)g=\widetilde{\pi\circ\varphi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f)g.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)

A
be a
G
-C*-algebra and let
\pi\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
*
-representation of

a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

(\ell^{p}(G,\mathcal{H}),\widetilde{\pi}_{p},\lambda^{p})

\pi

Let
q\in(1,\infty)

and let

and

using

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(h)f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in G}\langle\widetilde{\pi}_{p}(h(s))\lambda^{p}(s)f,g\rangle=\sum_{s\in G}\langle\lambda^{p}(s)f,\widetilde{\pi}_{q}(h(s)^{*})g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in G}\langle\lambda^{p}(s^{-1})f,\widetilde{\pi}_{q}(h(s^{-1})^{*})g\rangle=\sum_{s\in G}\langle f,\widetilde{\pi}_{q}(\alpha_{s}(h(s^{-1})^{*}))\lambda^{q}(s)g\rangle

(3.4)

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(h)f,g\rangle=\langle f,\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(h^{*})g\rangle.

Using the standard fact that
\|f\|_{p}=\sup\{|\langle f,g\rangle|\mid g\in\ell^{q}(G;\mathcal{H}):\|g\|_{q}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\quad\;\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(h^{*})\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\langle\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(h^{*})f,g\rangle|\mid f\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}),\,g\in\ell^{q}(G;\mathcal{H}):\,\|f\|_{p},\,\|g\|_{q}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\langle f,\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(h)g\rangle|\mid f\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}),\,g\in\ell^{q}(G;\mathcal{H}):\,\|f\|_{p},\,\|g\|_{q}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(h)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{q}(G;\mathcal{H}))}.

This allows us to consider the following Banach

]

[

]
.

Definition 3.4

A

3.2.
(In)dependence of the algebras

of the choice of the representation

We shall show that the Banach algebra

is canonically isometrically isomorphic to the Banach algebra
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G)
introduced in
[

is

to

for any faithful representation
\pi
of
A
. As a consequence, we shall later obtain an isomorphism statement for the corresponding C
∗
-algebras.

First we need to establish a lemma, in whose proof we will use the family
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\LL^{\infty}([0,1])
of Rademacher functions (see
[
DJT
, Page 10]
). The Rademacher functions form an orthonormal set in
\LL^{2}([0,1])
. We will also use the Kahane’s inequality
[
DJT
, Theorem 11.1]
, which says that for any
1\leq p,q<\infty
there is a constant
K_{p,q}>0
such that

(3.5)

\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}r_{k}(t)\bigr\|^{q}dt\bigr)^{1/q}\leq K_{p,q}\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}r_{k}(t)\bigr\|^{p}dt\bigr)^{1/p}

for any Banach space
X
and vectors
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in X
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
A
be a
G
-C
∗
-algebra,
\pi
a non-degenerate
*
-representation of
A
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
and
\sigma=\pi(\cdot)\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\colon A\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K})
its amplification for a non-zero Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{p}\geq 1
depending only on
p
, such that

(3.6)

\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))}\leq\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}))}\leq C_{p}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))}

for
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A).

Proof.

Since representations
\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p},\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p}
are contractive, it is enough to prove (
3.6
) for a fixed finitely supported function
f\colon G\rightarrow A
.

The first inequality in (
3.6
) is straightforward: take
\xi\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
, choose a unit vector
\zeta\in\mathcal{K}
and define
\xi_{\zeta}\colon G\ni g\mapsto\xi(g)\otimes\zeta\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}
. Then
\|\xi_{\zeta}\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K})}=\|\xi\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})}
and

\displaystyle\|(

\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})}=\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G}\pi\bigl(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g))\bigr)\xi(g^{-1}h)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G}\pi\bigl(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g))\bigr)\xi(g^{-1}h)\otimes\zeta\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}}\bigr)^{1/p}=\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|((\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi_{\zeta})(h)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi_{\zeta}\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K})}\leq\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|\|\xi\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})},

which proves the first inequality in (
3.6
). Fix
\xi\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K})
with norm
\leq 1
. To establish the second inequality, we need to prove that

(3.7)

\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K})}\leq C_{p}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})}

with a constant
C_{p}
depending only on
p
. Since
\xi
is
p
-summable, there is a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
such that
\xi(h)\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{0}
for
h\in G
. Then we easily see that (
3.7
) is equivalent to

(3.8)

\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{0})}\leq C_{p}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})},

where by a slight abuse of the notation we write also
\sigma
for the amplification of
\pi
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{0}
. By passing via isometry, we can in fact assume that

\mathcal{K}_{0}=\mathcal{R}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{r_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\LL^{2}(\left[0,1\right]).

For
h\in G
, we write

\xi(h)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\eta_{n}(h)\otimes r_{n},\quad\textnormal{where}\;\;\;\eta_{n}\colon G\ni h\mapsto(\mathrm{id}\otimes r_{n}^{*})(\xi(h))\in\mathcal{H}.

The above series converges in the norm of
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R}
for every
h\in G
. Then also

\bigl\|\xi-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\eta_{n}(\cdot)\otimes r_{n}\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R})}^{p}=\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\eta_{n}(h)\otimes r_{n}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R}}^{p}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0

by the bounded convergence theorem, where the uniform bound is given by

\bigl\|\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\eta_{n}(h)\otimes r_{n}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R}}^{p}=\bigl(\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\bigl\|\eta_{n}(h)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\leq\bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl\|\eta_{n}(h)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}=\|\xi(h)\|^{p}.

This proves that
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\eta_{n}(\cdot)\otimes r_{n}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}\xi
in
\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R})
. With these preliminaries we calculate the left hand side of (
3.8
)

\displaystyle\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigl\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\eta_{n}(\cdot)\otimes r_{n}\bigr)\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G}\bigl(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}(f(g))\lambda^{p}(g)\bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\eta_{n}(\cdot)\otimes r_{n}\bigr)\bigr)(h)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R}}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\pi(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))\eta_{n}(g^{-1}h)\otimes r_{n}\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R}}\bigr)^{1/p}.

Note that the sum over
g\in G
is finite. As
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\LL^{2}([0,1])
, we can calculate the last norm in
\mathcal{H}\otimes\LL^{2}([0,1])\simeq\LL^{2}([0,1];\mathcal{H})
, using (
3.5
):

\displaystyle\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi

\displaystyle\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\pi(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))\eta_{n}(g^{-1}h)r_{n}(t)\bigr\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}dt\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\bigl(K_{p,2}\bigl[\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\pi(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))\eta_{n}(g^{-1}h)r_{n}(t)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}dt\bigr]^{1/p}\bigr)^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=K_{p,2}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)((\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\eta_{n})(h)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}dt\bigr)^{1/p}.

We have switched the order of integral and sum, since the integrand is non-negative and we can restrict to a countable subset of
G
. Then

\displaystyle\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi

\displaystyle\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{p,2}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\eta_{n}\bigr\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})}dt\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq K_{p,2}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)\eta_{n}\bigr\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})}dt\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=K_{p,2}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)\eta_{n}(h)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}dt\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq K_{p,2}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigl(\sum_{h\in G}\bigl(K_{2,p}\bigl[\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)\eta_{n}(h)\bigr\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}dt\bigr]^{1/2}\bigr)^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=K_{p,2}K_{2,p}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\eta_{n}(\cdot)\otimes r_{n}\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R})}

\displaystyle=K_{p,2}K_{2,p}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|\|\xi\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{R})}.

This ends the proof with
C_{p}=K_{p,2}K_{2,p}
.
∎

Next we show that in the case of
A=\mathbb{C}
and trivial action, the constant in the above lemma can be improved to
1
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Take
p\in[1,\infty)
, a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and consider representations
\lambda^{p}\colon G\rightarrow\mbox{Isom}(\ell^{p}(G))
and
\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}\colon G\rightarrow\mbox{Isom}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))
given by the left translation. Then for every
f\in\ell^{1}(G)
we have

\|\lambda^{p}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G))}=\|\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))}.

Proof.

We have established the inequality
\|\lambda^{p}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G))}\leq\|\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))}
as a part of Lemma
3.8
for
A=\mathbb{C}
. Consequently, it is enough to prove the converse inequality. Furthermore, arguing by approximation, we only need to establish this inequality for finitely supported functions.

Assume by contradiction, that we have
\bigl\|\lambda^{p}(f)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G))}<\bigl\|\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}(f)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))}
for a finitely supported
f\in\ell^{1}(G)
. Then we can find
\varepsilon>0
and a finitely supported function
\xi\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
with
\xi(g)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\xi_{g,n}e_{n}\,(g\in G)
for an orthonormal family
\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
such that

(3.9)

\|\xi\|=1,\quad(1+\varepsilon)\bigl\|\lambda^{p}(f)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G))}\leq\bigl\|\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}(f)\xi\bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})}.

Fix
m\geq 2
. For simplicity, we shall write
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in\{1,\ldots,N\}^{m}
and
e_{\mathbf{n}}:=e_{n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{n_{m}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}
. Consider the vector

\eta_{m}\colon G^{\times m}\ni(g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m})\mapsto\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}^{m}}\xi_{g_{1},n_{1}}\cdots\xi_{g_{m},n_{m}}e_{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}

in
\ell^{p}(G^{\times m};\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m})
. As easily checked, it has norm
1
:

\displaystyle\|\eta_{m}\|

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\{1,\ldots,N\}^{m}}\xi_{g_{1},n_{1}}\cdots\xi_{g_{m},n_{m}}e_{\mathbf{n}}\bigr\|^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}\bigl(\sum_{n_{1},\dotsc,n_{m}=1}^{N}\bigl|\xi_{g_{1},n_{1}}\cdots\xi_{g_{m},n_{m}}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}\bigl(\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{N}|\xi_{g_{1},n_{1}}|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\cdots\bigl(\sum_{n_{m}=1}^{N}|\xi_{g_{m},n_{m}}|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1}\in G}\bigl(\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{N}|\xi_{g_{1},n_{1}}|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}\cdots\bigl(\sum_{g_{m}\in G}\bigl(\sum_{n_{m}=1}^{N}|\xi_{g_{m},n_{m}}|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}=1.

Next, define

x_{m}=\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}f(g_{1})\cdots f(g_{m})\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}}(g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m})\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G^{\times m};\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m})).

where
\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}}\colon G^{\times m}\rightarrow\mbox{Isom}(\ell^{p}(G^{\times m};\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}))
is the left translation representation.
We can use Lemma
3.8
for the group
G^{\times m}
and Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}
, to get

(3.10)

\|x_{m}\|\leq C_{p}\bigl\|\lambda^{p}(f^{\otimes m})\bigr\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G^{\times m}))}=C_{p}\|\lambda^{p}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G))}^{m}.

To see the above equality, observe that we have the canonical isometric isomorphism
\ell^{p}(G^{\times m})\simeq\ell^{p}(G)\otimes_{d_{p}}\ell^{p}(G^{\times(m-1)})
, where
d_{p}
is the Chevet-Saphar tensor product (see
[
Che
, Section 4]
and
[
Rya
, Section 6.2]
). Under this isomorphism
\lambda^{p}(f^{\otimes m})\simeq\lambda^{p}(f)\otimes\lambda^{p}(f^{\otimes(m-1)})
, hence the claim holds by induction. On the other hand

\displaystyle\|x_{m}\|

\displaystyle\geq\|x_{m}\eta_{m}\|

\displaystyle=\bigl\|\bigl(\sum_{h_{1},\dotsc,h_{m}\in G}f(h_{1})\cdots f(h_{m})\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}}(h_{1},\dotsc,h_{m})\bigr)\eta_{m}\bigr\|

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{h_{1},\dotsc,h_{m}\in G}f(h_{1})\cdots f(h_{m})\eta_{m}(h_{1}^{-1}g_{1},\dotsc,h_{m}^{-1}g_{m})\bigr\|^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{h_{1},\dotsc,h_{m}\in G}f(h_{1})\cdots f(h_{m})\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\{1,\dotsc,N\}^{m}}\xi_{h_{1}^{-1}g_{1},n_{1}}\cdots\xi_{h_{m}^{-1}g_{m},n_{m}}e_{\mathbf{n}}\bigr\|^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}\bigl(\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\{1,\dotsc,N\}^{m}}\bigl|\sum_{h_{1},\dotsc,h_{m}\in G}f(h_{1})\cdots f(h_{m})\xi_{h_{1}^{-1}g_{1},n_{1}}\cdots\xi_{h_{m}^{-1}g_{m},n_{m}}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}\bigl(\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\{1,\dotsc,N\}^{m}}\bigl|\sum_{h_{1}\in G}f(h_{1})\xi_{h_{1}^{-1}g_{1},n_{1}}\bigr|^{2}\cdots\bigl|\sum_{h_{m}\in G}f(h_{m})\xi_{h_{m}^{-1}g_{m},n_{m}}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\in G}\bigl(\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{N}\bigl|\sum_{h_{1}\in G}f(h_{1})\xi_{h_{1}^{-1}g_{1},n_{1}}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\cdots\bigl(\sum_{n_{m}=1}^{N}\bigl|\sum_{h_{m}\in G}f(h_{m})\xi_{h_{m}^{-1}g_{m},n_{m}}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g\in G}\bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bigl|\sum_{h\in G}f(h)\xi_{h^{-1}g,n}\bigr|^{2}\bigr)^{p/2}\bigr)^{m/p}=\bigl(\sum_{g\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{h\in G}f(h)\xi_{h^{-1}g,n}e_{n}\bigr\|^{p}\bigr)^{m/p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{g\in G}\bigl\|(\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}(f)\xi)(g)\bigr\|^{p}\bigr)^{m/p}=\bigl\|\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}(f)\xi\bigr\|^{m}_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\varepsilon)^{m}\bigl\|\lambda^{p}(f)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G))}^{m},

where the last inequality holds by (
3.9
). Combining this with (
3.10
) gives contradiction for
m
big enough, so that
(1+\varepsilon)^{m}>C_{p}
.
∎

We immediately obtain a conclusion in the case of
A=\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

For every group
G
and
1<p<\infty
we have

\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;\mathbb{C}\right)=\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G)
, where the latter denotes the Banach
*
-algebra introduced in
[
SW2
]
.

Proof.

The definition of
\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G)
in
[
SW1
]
uses the norm

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{id},p}\colon\ell^{1}(G)\to[0,\infty),\quad f\mapsto\mbox{max}\{\|\lambda^{p}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G))},\,\|\lambda^{q}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{q}(G))}\},

whereas the norm in
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;\mathbb{C}\right)
is given by

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;\mathbb{C}\right)}\colon\ell^{1}(G)

\displaystyle\to[0,\infty),

\displaystyle f\mapsto\sup_{\mathcal{H}-\textup{Hilbert space}}\mbox{max}\{\|\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}))},\,\|\lambda^{q,\mathcal{H}}(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{q}(G;\mathcal{H}))}\},

where
\lambda^{p,\mathcal{H}}
is the left translation representation of
G
on
\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
. The independence of the norm of the choice of
\mathcal{H}
is precisely the content of Proposition
3.9
. ∎

Theorem
3.10
can be interpreted by saying that the Banach
*
-algebra
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;\mathbb{C}\right)
is (isometrically) independent from the choice of representation of
\mathbb{C}
. For a general
G
-C
∗
-algebra
A
we have a slightly weaker statement –
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
is independent from such a choice up to a (not necessarily isometric) isomorphism.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
\pi,\sigma
be non-degenerate
*
-representations of
A
on respectively
\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
. Assume that
\pi
is faithful and take
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
. Then

(i)

if
\pi\simeq\aleph_{0}\cdot\pi
, i.e.
\pi
is unitarily equivalent with
\aleph_{0}
-many copies of
\pi
, we have

(3.11)

\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}))}\leq\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\pi}))};

(ii)

in general, there is a constant
C_{p}\geq 1
depending only on
p
, such that

(3.12)

\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}))}\leq C_{p}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\pi}))}.

Proof.

Assume first that
\aleph_{0}\cdot\pi\simeq\pi
. Since
\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p},\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p}
are contractive, it is enough to show (
3.11
) for a fixed finitely supported
f\colon G\rightarrow A
. Next, choose
\xi\in\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\sigma})
of norm
\leq 1
with finite support. To avoid trivialities, assume
f\neq 0,\xi\neq 0
. It is enough to show that

(3.13)

\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\sigma})}\leq\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\pi}))}.

We want to use Voiculescu’s theorem on approximate unitary equivalence of representations, hence we need to make several preparations and reduce to a separable situation.

Let
G_{0}
be the (countable) subgroup of
G
generated by
\text{supp}(f)\cup\text{supp}(\xi)
. Since
\sigma
is non-degenerate, there is
b_{g,r}\in A
such that
\|\sigma(b_{g,r})\xi(g)-\xi(g)\|\leq\tfrac{1}{r}
for
r\in\mathbb{N},g\in G_{0}
. Define a C
∗
-algebra

B=C^{*}(\{\alpha_{h}(f(g)),\alpha_{h}(b_{g,r})\mid g,h\in G_{0},r\in\mathbb{N}\})\subseteq A.

Observe that
B
is
G_{0}
-invariant and separable; choose a countable dense subset
\{a_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\!\subseteq B\setminus\{0\}
. Next, define a separable, closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}
:

\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\sigma(a_{k})\xi(g)\mid g\in G_{0},k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Clearly
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0}
is
\sigma(B)
-invariant, hence we can consider the (co-)restricted representation
\sigma_{0}\colon B\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0})
.

As
\pi
is faithful and
\aleph_{0}\cdot\pi\simeq\pi
, there are unit vectors
\{\zeta_{k,m}^{i}\}_{i,k,m\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
such that
\|\pi(a_{k})\zeta^{i}_{k,m}\|\geq\|a_{k}\|-\tfrac{1}{m}
and
\langle\pi(b)\zeta^{i}_{k,m}|\pi(b^{\prime})\zeta^{i^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\rangle=0
, for any
b,b^{\prime}\in A,\,i,k,m,k^{\prime},m^{\prime},i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
i\neq i^{\prime}
. Finally, define a separable, closed subspace

\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\pi(a_{n})\zeta_{k,m}^{i}\mid i,n,k,m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

Then
\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}
is a
\pi(B)
-invariant subspace. Let
\pi_{0}
be the (co-)restriction
\pi_{0}\colon B\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0})
.

Representation
\pi_{0}
is faithful. Indeed, for
a\in B
we can find indices
n_{j}
such that
a_{n_{j}}\xrightarrow[j\to\infty]{}a
. Then if
(c_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an approximate unit in
B
, choose
m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|a_{m_{k}}-c_{k}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{k}
with
\|c_{k}\|,\|a_{m_{k}}\|\leq 1
. Then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\|\pi_{0}(a_{n_{j}})\|

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\pi_{0}(a_{n_{j}})\pi(a_{m_{k}})\zeta^{1}_{n_{j},k}\|\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\bigl(\|\pi(a_{n_{j}}c_{k})\zeta^{1}_{n_{j},k}\|-\|a_{n_{j}}\|\tfrac{1}{k}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\bigl(\|\pi(a_{n_{j}})\zeta^{1}_{n_{j},k}\|-\|a_{n_{j}}-a_{n_{j}}c_{k}\|-\|a_{n_{j}}\|\tfrac{1}{k}\bigr)\geq\|a_{n_{j}}\|

and we obtain
\|\pi_{0}(a_{n_{j}})\|=\|a_{n_{j}}\|
. Finally

\|a\|\geq\|\pi_{0}(a)\|=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\pi_{0}(a_{n_{j}})\|=\lim_{j\to\infty}\|a_{n_{j}}\|=\|a\|.

Clearly both
\pi_{0},\sigma_{0}
are non-degenerate. Since
b_{g,r}\in B
, we have
\xi\colon G\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0}
.

Finally, we claim that
\pi_{0}(B)\cap\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0})=\{0\}
. Indeed, suppose otherwise. Then there is
a\in B,\|a\|=1
such that
\pi_{0}(a)
is of finite rank. Then we can choose index
n\in\mathbb{N}
so that
\|a-a_{n}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{4}
, hence
\|a_{n}\|\geq\tfrac{3}{4}
. For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\|\pi_{0}(a_{n})\zeta^{i}_{n,4}\|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, hence
\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\|\pi_{0}(a_{n})\zeta^{i}_{n,4}\|\leq\|\pi_{0}(a)\zeta^{i}_{n,4}\|+\tfrac{1}{4}
and
\pi_{0}(a)\zeta^{i}_{n,4}\neq 0
. Since these vectors are pairwise orthogonals for different
i
’s, the operator
\pi_{0}(a)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0})
cannot be of finite rank and we obtain a contradiction.

Consider the C
∗
-algebra
\pi_{0}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0})
. Since
\pi_{0}
is faithful, we can consider a non-degenerate
*
-homomorphism
\sigma^{\prime}\colon\pi_{0}(B)\ni\pi_{0}(a)\mapsto\sigma_{0}(a)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0})
. Voiculescu’s theorem
[
Dav
, Corollary II.5.5]
gives us an approximate unitary equivalence
\mathrm{id}\approx_{a}\mathrm{id}\oplus\sigma^{\prime}
of representations of
\pi_{0}(B)
. This means that there is a sequence of unitary operators
U_{n}\colon\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0}

(n\in\mathbb{N})
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|U_{n}\pi_{0}(a)U_{n}^{*}-\pi_{0}(a)\oplus\sigma_{0}(a)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0})}=0\qquad(a\in B).

With these preliminaries, we can start calculating the left hand side of (
3.13
):

\displaystyle\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})

\displaystyle(f)\xi\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\sigma})}=\sum_{h\in G_{0}}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G_{0}}\sigma(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))\xi(g^{-1}h)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in G_{0}}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G_{0}}\bigl(0,\sigma_{0}(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))\xi(g^{-1}h)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in G_{0}}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G_{0}}\bigl(\pi_{0}(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))\oplus\sigma_{0}(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))\bigr)\,\bigl(0,\xi(g^{-1}h)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in G_{0}}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G_{0}}\lim_{n\to\infty}U_{n}\pi_{0}(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))U_{n}^{*}\bigl(0,\xi(g^{-1}h)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in G_{0}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl\|U_{n}\sum_{g\in G_{0}}\pi_{0}(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))U_{n}^{*}\bigl(0,\xi(g^{-1}h)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,0}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{h\in G_{0}}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G_{0}}\pi_{0}(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))U_{n}^{*}\bigl(0,\xi(g^{-1}h)\bigr)\bigr\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}}^{p}.

Note that the sum over
g
is finite and we use Fatou lemma in the last step. Now, define

\eta_{n}\colon G\ni h\mapsto U_{n}^{*}(0,\xi(h))\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

Clearly
\|\eta_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0})}\leq 1
. We can continue the computation

\displaystyle\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\xi\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\sigma})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{h\in G_{0}}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G_{0}}\pi_{0}(\alpha_{h^{-1}}(f(g)))\eta_{n}(g^{-1}h)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi,0}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|\sum_{g\in G}(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}(f(g))\lambda^{p}(g)\eta_{n})(h)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{h\in G}\bigl\|\bigl((\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\eta_{n}\bigr)(h)\bigr\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\eta_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{p}\leq\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|^{p}_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\pi}))}.

This establishes (
3.13
) and ends the proof of (
3.11
).

The inequality (
3.12
) follows by considering amplification. Consider the representation
\gamma=\pi(\cdot)\otimes I_{\ell^{2}}\colon A\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\ell^{2})
. It is faithful, non-degenerate and
\gamma\simeq\aleph_{0}\cdot\gamma
. Using the inequality (
3.11
) and Lemma
3.8
we obtain

\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\sigma}))}\leq\|(\widetilde{\gamma}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}))}\leq C_{p}\|(\widetilde{\pi}_{p}\rtimes\lambda^{p})(f)\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H}_{\pi}))}

for
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
, as originally claimed.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Take
1<p<\infty
and let
\pi,\sigma
be faithful representations of a
G
-C*-algebra
A
. There exists
D_{p}\geq 1
(depending only on
p
) with the following properties:

(i)

the identity map on
\ell^{1}(G;A)
extends to an isomorphism of Banach
*
-algebras
\mathrm{PF}_{p,\pi}^{*}\left(G;A\right)\rightarrow\mathrm{PF}_{p,\sigma}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
with the norm bounded by
D_{p}
. If
\aleph_{0}\cdot\pi\simeq\pi
and
\aleph_{0}\cdot\sigma\simeq\sigma
, then this isomorphism is isometric.

(ii)

The identity map on
\ell^{1}(G;A)
extends to an isomorphism of Banach
*
-algebras
\mathrm{PF}_{p,\pi}^{*}\left(G;A\right)\rightarrow\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
with the norm bounded by
D_{p}
.

Proof.

The two claims follow immediately from Proposition
3.11
, with
D_{p}=\max(C_{p},C_{q})
, where
q
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
.
∎

3.3.
Further properties of the algebras
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)

We now investigate certain spectral and hereditary properties of Banach algebras
\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)
. We start with the following result, which is a consequence of the Riesz-Thorin Theorem.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
,
p_{0}<p_{1}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
, let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra and let

\pi\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
*
-representation of
A
on a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let

p_{\theta}\in(p_{0},p_{1})
be such that
1/p_{\theta}=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
.
Then for every
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{\theta}}(f)\|\leq\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{0}}(f)\|^{1-\theta}\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{1}}(f)\|^{\theta}

Proof.

Let
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
. For every
g\in\ell^{p_{0}}(G;\mathcal{H})\cap\ell^{p_{1}}(G;\mathcal{H})
the equality

\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{0}}(f)g=\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{1}}(f)g
holds. Therefore

\displaystyle Z\colon\ell^{p_{0}}(G;\mathcal{H})+\ell^{p_{1}}(G;\mathcal{H})\to\ell^{0}(G;\mathcal{H}),\quad g_{0}+g_{1}\mapsto\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{0}}(f)g_{0}+\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{1}}(f)g_{1}

is a well-defined linear map. By the Riesz-Thorin Theorem, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{\theta}}(f)g\|\leq\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{0}}(f)\|^{1-\theta}\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{1}}(f)\|^{\theta}\|g\|_{{p_{\theta}}}

for all
g\in\ell^{p_{\theta}}(G;\mathcal{H})
. In particular,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{\theta}}(f)\|\leq\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{0}}(f)\|^{1-\theta}\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{1}}(f)\|^{\theta}

holds.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
,
p_{0}<p_{1}
and
p\in(p_{0},p_{1})
. Let

\theta\in(0,1)
be such that
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
and let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra. Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p_{0}}(G;A)}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{\textup{PF}_{p_{1}}^{*}(G;A)}^{\theta}

for all
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be any non-degenerate
*
-representation of
A
on a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
q_{i}\in(1,\infty)
be such that
1/p_{i}+1/q_{i}=1
, for
i\in\{0,1\}
and
let
q
be such that
1/q=(1-\theta)/q_{0}+\theta/q_{1}
. Then

1/p+1/q=(1-\theta)(1/p_{0}+1/q_{0})+\theta(1/p_{1}+1/q_{1})=1
. By
Proposition
3.13
, for each
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
we have

\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f)\|\leq\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{0}}(f)\|^{1-\theta}\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p_{1}}(f)\|^{\theta}

and

\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f)\|\leq\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q_{0}}(f)\|^{1-\theta}\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q_{1}}(f)\|^{\theta}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\pi,p}\leq\|f\|_{\pi,p_{0}}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{\pi,p_{1}}^{\theta}

and finally

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p_{0}}(G;A)}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p_{1}}(G;A)}^{\theta}.

∎

The last proposition yields, in particular, the following monotonicity result.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
p,p^{\prime}\in(1,2]
,
p^{\prime}>p
, and let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra. Then, for every
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
, we have

\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p^{\prime}}(G;A)}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)}.

In particular, the identity map on
\ell^{1}(G;A)
extends to a contractive
*
-homomorphism from

\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)
into
\textup{PF}^{*}_{p^{\prime}}(G;A)
.

Proof.

This can be shown as in
[
SW1
, Proposition 4.5]
. Indeed, if we let
q>2
be the conjugate of
p
and choose
\theta\in(0,1)
with
1/p^{\prime}=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q
, then Proposition
3.14
shows that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p^{\prime}}(G;A)}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{\textup{PF}_{q}^{*}(G;A)}^{\theta}=\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)},

as
\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)}=\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{q}(G;A)}
.
∎

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then

\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)
is a
*
-subalgebra of
A\rtimes_{r}G
. In particular,
\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)
is a semisimple Banach
*
-algebra.

Proof.

By Remark
3.7
we may assume that
p\in(1,2)
.
It then follows from Corollary
3.15
that there is a contractive
*
-homomorphism

j_{A,p}\colon\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)\to A\rtimes_{r}G

such that

\textstyle{\ell^{1}(G;A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{A,p}}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{A,2}}
\textstyle{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{A,p}}
\textstyle{A\rtimes_{r}G}

commutes, where
\iota_{A,2}\colon\ell^{1}(G;A)\to A\rtimes_{r}G
and
\iota_{A,p}\colon\ell^{1}(G;A)\to\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
are the corresponding canonical contractive inclusions (see Remark
3.7
). In particular,
\iota_{A,2}
and
\iota_{A,p}
are injective. It remains to show that
j_{A,p}
is injective to complete the proof. Let
x\in\ker j_{A,p}
. Since
\iota_{A,p}
has dense image and
C_{c}(G;A)
is dense in
\ell^{1}(G;A)
, there is a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in C_{c}(G;A)^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\iota_{A,p}(f_{n})\to x
as
n\to\infty
. It is enough to show that for all
*
-representations
\pi\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we have

(3.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f_{n})=0\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f_{n})=0.

So let
\pi\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
*
-representation of
A
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then, for all
g\in C_{c}(G;\mathcal{H})
, we have
\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{2}(f_{n})g\to 0
in
\ell^{2}(G;\mathcal{H})
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore,
\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{2}(f_{n})g\to 0

pointwise (with respect to elements of
G
) as
n\to\infty
for all
g\in C_{c}(G;\mathcal{H})
. Note that we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f_{n})g=\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{2}(f_{n})g=\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f_{n})g

for all
g\in C_{c}(G;\mathcal{H})
.

Since
(\iota_{A,p}(f_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
both (norm) limits in (
3.15
) exist. In particular, for every
g\in C_{c}(G;\mathcal{H})
the sequences

(\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f_{n})g)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f_{n})g)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge in
\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
and, respectively, in
\ell^{q}(G;\mathcal{H})
. It follows that they converge pointwise. Therefore, by equation (
3.16
) and
the fact that
\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{2}(f_{n})g\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f_{n})g=0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f_{n})g=0

for all
g\in C_{c}(G;\mathcal{H})
. Equation (
3.15
) follows.
∎

Next we have the following observation.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra and let
p\in(1,\infty)
. The Banach
*
-algebra

\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
admits a contractive approximate identity.

Proof.

The contractive inclusion

\iota_{A,p}\colon\ell^{1}(G;A)\to\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
has dense image. The Banach
*
-algebra

\ell^{1}(G;A)
admits a contractive approximate identity
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
. Hence
(\iota_{A,p}(u_{\lambda}))_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

is a contractive net. So let
x\in\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
and let

\varepsilon>0
. Then there is
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
such that

\|x-\iota_{A,p}(f)\|<\varepsilon
. Since
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

is an approximate identity in
\ell^{1}(G;A)
, there is a
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda

such that
\|u_{\lambda}f-f\|_{1}<\varepsilon
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. So
we obtain (using the facts that both
(\iota_{A,p}(u_{\lambda}))_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
\iota_{A,p}

are contractive)

\displaystyle\|\iota_{A,p}(u_{\lambda})x-x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\iota_{A,p}(u_{\lambda})x-\iota_{A,p}(u_{\lambda})\iota_{A,p}(f)\|+\|\iota_{A,p}(u_{\lambda}f)-\iota_{A,p}(f)\|+\|\iota_{A,p}(f)-x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x-\iota_{A,p}(f)\|+\|u_{\lambda}f-f\|_{1}+\|\iota_{A,p}(f)-x\|\leq 3\varepsilon

for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. This shows that
(\iota_{A,p}(u_{\lambda}))_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

is a contractive left approximate identity in
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
. Analogously it can be shown that

(\iota_{A,p}(u_{\lambda}))_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a right approximate identity. This completes the
proof.
∎

We shall now discuss certain functorial properties of our construction.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
A
and
B
be
G
-C*-algebras, let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
\varphi\colon A\to B
be a
G
-equivariant

*
-homomorphism. Recall the maps
\iota_{A,p}
defined in Remark
3.7
and
j_{A,p}
introduced in the proof of Proposition
3.16
. Then there is a unique contractive
*
-homomorphism

\varphi\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G\colon\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)\to\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;B)

such that

(3.17)

\textstyle{\ell^{1}(G;A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{A,p}}
\scriptstyle{\varphi\rtimes_{1}G}
\textstyle{\ell^{1}(G;B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{B,p}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G}
\scriptstyle{j_{A,p}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;B\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{B,p}}
\textstyle{A\rtimes_{r}G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi\rtimes_{r}G}
\textstyle{B\rtimes_{r}G}

commutes.

Proof.

The only thing we need to show is that
\varphi\rtimes_{1}G
is continuous with respect to
the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;B\right)}
.
But as the estimate

\displaystyle\|(\varphi\rtimes_{1}G)(f)\|_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;B\right)}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\max\{\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}((\varphi\rtimes_{1}G)(f))\|,\;\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}((\varphi\rtimes_{1}G)(f))\|\}\mid

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mid\pi\ \mbox{is a}\ast\text{-representation
of }B\,\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\max\{\|\widetilde{\pi\circ\varphi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f)\|,\,\|\widetilde{\pi\circ\varphi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f)\|\}\mid\pi\ \mbox{is a}\ast\text{-representation
of }B\,\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{\max\{\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f)\|,\,\|\widetilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f)\|\}\mid\pi\ \mbox{is a}\ast\text{-representation
of }A\,\}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)},

is valid for all
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
, we have that
\varphi\rtimes_{1}G
is contractive
with respect to the
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}
-norms. This completes the proof.
∎

We shall now check that the above morphism remains an isometry when we are dealing with inclusions. This essentially follows from the fact that we can always extend representations from C*-subalgebras, if we allow ourselves to increase the dimension of the representation space.

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra and let
B
be a
G
-invariant
C*-subalgebra of
A
.
Then the inclusion map
\iota\colon B\to A
induces an isometric
*
-monomorphism

\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G\colon\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;B)\to\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\ell^{1}(G;B)
. We have to show that

\|(\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G)(f)\|_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)}=\|f\|_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;B)}
. Since the inequality

\|(\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G)(f)\|_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;B)}
is automatic by Proposition
3.18
,
it suffices to show that

\|(\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G)(f)\|_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)}\geq\|f\|_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;B)}.

In order to do this, let

\varepsilon>0
be given.
By the definition of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;B)}
, there is a non-degenerate
*
-representation
\pi\colon B\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

such that

\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;B)}<\max\{\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f)\|,\,\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f)\|\}+\varepsilon.

Now, there is a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, an isometry
V\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
and a
*
-representation
\rho\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
such that

\pi(b)=V^{*}\rho(b)V
for
b\in B
. Indeed, this follows by applying first the (possibly the non-unital version of) Arveson’s extension theorem to
\pi
, then unitizing the resulting extension via Proposition 2.2.1 or Lemma 2.2.3 in
[
BO
]
and then applying the Stinespring Theorem.

Note that we have an isometric inclusion
V\otimes 1\colon\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})\subseteq\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{K})
and a natural contractive projection
V^{*}\otimes 1\colon\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{K})\to\ell^{p}(G;\mathcal{H})
.
Consequently

\displaystyle\quad\;\|(\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G)(f)\|_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq\max\{\|\tilde{\rho}\rtimes\lambda^{p}((\iota\rtimes_{1}G)(f))\|,\,\|\tilde{\rho}\rtimes\lambda^{q}((\iota\rtimes_{1}G)(f))\|\}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\max\{\|\widetilde{\rho\circ\iota}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f)\|,\|\widetilde{\rho\circ\iota}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f)\|\}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq\max\{\|(V^{*}\otimes 1)(\widetilde{\rho\circ\iota}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f))(V\otimes 1)\|,\|(V^{*}\otimes 1)(\widetilde{\rho\circ\iota}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f))(V\otimes 1)\|\}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\max\{\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{p}(f)\|,\|\tilde{\pi}\rtimes\lambda^{q}(f)\|\}+\varepsilon\geq\|f\|_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;B)}.

Since
\varepsilon
was chosen arbitrary, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;B)}\leq\|(\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G)(f)\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)}.

It follows that
\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
is isometric.
∎

4.
Crossed product functor
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G

In this short, but central section of the paper, we formally introduce the
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
functor, exploiting Banach algebras constructed and studied in the previous section.

Recall that
\ell^{1}(G;\cdot)
is a functor from
the category of
G
-C*-algebra into the category of
*
-semisimple
Banach
*
-algebras and
C^{*}(\cdot)
is functor from the category of
*
-semisimple
Banach
*
-algebras into the category of C*-algebras. Recall also that the latter is constructed by defining, for any
*
-semisimple
Banach
*
-algebra
B
, the new, in general smaller, norm

\|b\|_{C^{*}(B)}:=\sup\{\|\gamma(b)\|\mid\mathcal{H}\textup{ is a Hilbert space and }\gamma\colon B\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\textup{ a}\ast\textup{-homomorphism}\}

and defining
C^{*}(B)
as the completion of
B
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{C^{*}(B)}
.
The composition

C^{*}\circ\ell^{1}(G;\cdot)
is then exactly the universal crossed product functor
-\rtimes_{u}G
. It follows from Lemma
3.16
and Proposition
3.18
that
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;\cdot\right)
is a functor from
the category of
G
-C*-algebra into the category of
*
-semisimple Banach
*
-algebras. Thus we can state the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, denote by
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
the functor
C^{*}\circ\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;\cdot)

from the category of
G
-C*-algebra to the category of C*-algebras. In particular, for every
G
-C*-algebra
A
write

A\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
for the enveloping C*-algebra
C^{*}(\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right))
.

We can immediately record a consequence of Corollary
3.12
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
A
is a
G
-C*-algebra and
\pi\colon A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a faithful
*
-representation of
A
. Set
C^{*}(\mathrm{PF}_{p,\pi}^{*}\left(G;A\right))
to be the enveloping C*-algebra of
\mathrm{PF}_{p,\pi}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
. Then
the identity map on
\ell^{1}(G;A)
extends to an isometric isomorphism of C
∗
-algebras

C^{*}(\mathrm{PF}_{p,\pi}^{*}\left(G;A\right))\simeq C^{*}(\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right))=A\rtimes_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}}G.

Proof.

Take
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
. Then
\|f\|_{C^{*}(\mathrm{PF}_{p,\pi}^{*}\left(G;A\right))}
is the supremum of
\|\gamma(f)\|
, where
\gamma
is a
*
-representation of
\mathrm{PF}_{p,\pi}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
on a Hilbert space. By composing with the isomorphism from Corollary
3.12
(ii), we obtain a
*
-representation of
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
. The same logic holds in the reversed direction, hence in the end we are looking at the supremum of the same family of numbers.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. The functor
\rtimes_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}}G
is a crossed product functor.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra. Then, by Lemma
3.16
and its proof,
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
is a
*
-semisimple Banach
*
-algebra and moreover the canonical contractive
*
-homomorphisms
\iota_{A,p}\colon\ell^{1}(G;A)\to\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
and

j_{A,p}\colon\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)\to A\rtimes_{r}G
are injective and have dense images. Thus, we have surjective
*
-homomorphism

q_{A}=C^{*}(\iota_{A,p})\colon A\rtimes_{u}G\to A\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
and

s_{A}=C^{*}(j_{A,p})\colon A\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G\to A\rtimes_{r}G
.
∎

We will prove in Theorem
4.6
that
\rtimes_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}}G
is a correspondence functor, by establishing its projection property. As a first step towards this goal, we prove that it has the ideal property.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. The functor
\rtimes_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}}G
has the ideal property.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra,
I
an ideal in
A
invariant under the action of
G
and let

\iota\colon I\to A
be the canonical inclusion. By Proposition
3.19
, the map
\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G\colon\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I)\to\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)
is an isometric
*
-monomorphism, hence
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I)
(identified with the image of
\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
) is a closed
*
-ideal in
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
. The map
\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
extends to a
*
-homomorphism, denoted by the same symbol,
\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G\colon C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I))\to C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A))
. It remains to show that it is injective.

Choose
0\neq x\in C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I))
and let
\Pi\colon C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I))\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a non-degenerate
*
-representation of
C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I))
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
such that
\Pi(x)\neq 0
. Let
\Pi_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I)}
be the restriction of
\Pi
to
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I)
, it is also non-degenerate. Since by Proposition
3.17
the algebra
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I)
admits a contractive approximate identity, and
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;I\right)
is an ideal in
\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)
,
\Pi_{\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;I)}
extends to a
*
-homomorphism
\widetilde{\Pi}_{0}\colon\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Finally by the universal property of the C*-envelope,
\widetilde{\Pi}_{0}
extends to a
*
-representation
\widetilde{\Pi}\colon C^{*}(\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A))\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Write
x=\lim_{i\in I}x_{i}
with
x_{i}\in\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;I\right)
. We obtain

\widetilde{\Pi}\bigl((\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G)(x)\bigr)=\lim_{i\in I}\widetilde{\Pi}\bigl((\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G)(x_{i})\bigr)=\lim_{i\in I}\Pi_{\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;I\right)}(x_{i})=\Pi(x)\neq 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next we prove one more auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

,

(4.1)

\textstyle{\ell^{1}(G;A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let
\pi\colon eAe\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

Then, similarly as in the proof of Proposition

, there is a Hilbert space

. A straightforward calculation reveals

and

\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;A\right)\rightarrow\mathrm{PF}_{p}^{*}\left(G;eAe\right)
.
∎

We are now ready for the theorem mentioned above.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. The functor
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
has the projection property, hence it is a correspondence functor.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
G
-C*-algebra and
e\in\mathcal{M}(A)
a
G
-invariant projection. We need to show that the canonical inclusion
\iota\colon eAe\to A
induces a injective
*
-homomorphism
\iota\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G\colon eAe\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G\to A\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
.

By Proposition
3.19
, we can view
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;eAe)
as
a Banach
*
-subalgebra of
\textup{PF}_{p}^{*}(G;A)
. Let

5.
Non-triviality of the construction

In this final section, we shall discuss several examples in which our functor leads to genuinely exotic crossed products.

We begin with a straightforward observation: in the presence of an invariant state on
A
, to show that the crossed products
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
are exotic, one may simply use the corresponding fact for the algebras
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G))
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a unital
G
-C*-algebra which admits a
G
-invariant state. Assume further that
p,p^{\prime}\in[2,\infty),p<p^{\prime}
are such that the canonical contractive map
q\colon C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p^{\prime}}(G))\to C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G))
is not isometric. Then the corresponding map
q_{A}\colon C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p^{\prime}}(G;A))\to C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
is also not isometric.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Proposition
2.13
, Theorem
4.6
and Theorem
3.10
.
∎

The above proposition shows that one can apply the results of
[
SW2
]
(particularly,
[
SW2
, Corollary 4.3 and Corollary 5.12]
) to show that many classes of the crossed products we constructed are, in fact, exotic. We also have a similar fact for trivial actions (which naturally admit invariant states), as follows already from Proposition
2.10
(via Theorem
4.6
and Theorem
3.10
, as we can view
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
as a C*-completion of
A\odot C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G))
. Note that we always have
A\subseteq C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
, as
\rtimes_{\textup{PF}_{p}}G
is a crossed product functor (see the discussion in
[
BEW
, Section 2.1]
).

We would like to show that in some cases we can use the methods developed in
[
SW2
]
also for actions which do not admit invariant states.
We first need a definition, introduced in
[
SW2
, Definition 4.1]
, which presents a modest strengthening of the Haagerup property for groups.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
B(G)=C^{*}(G)^{*}
be the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra of
G
. Then
G
has the
integrable Haagerup property
if

B(G)=\overline{\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}(B(G)\cap\ell^{p}(G))}^{\,\sigma(B(G),C^{*}(G))}.

Equivalently,
G
has the integrable Haagerup property if there exists a net of positive definite functions

\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}\ell^{p}(G)

so that
\phi_{i}\xrightarrow[i\in I]{}1
uniformly on finite subsets of
G
. Again equivalently, there is a proper conditionally negative function
L
on
G
such that for all
\beta\in(0,1)
,

\varphi_{\beta}:=\beta^{L}\in\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}\ell^{p}(G).

We also recall a fact established in
[
SW2
, Lemma 3.2]
:
\ell^{p}
-integrable positive definite functions on
G
give rise to bounded positive functionals on
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G))
.

Before we formulate the next theorem, we should warn the reader that we shall use the notation of the type
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))\neq A\rtimes_{r}G
to say that the corresponding canonical map is not isometric (and not that the C*-algebras in question are not abstractly isomorphic, which seems to be much more difficult to establish).

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose that
A
is a
G
-C*-algebra such that

A\rtimes_{u}G\neq A\rtimes_{r}G.

Then
there is
p\in(2,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle A\rtimes_{u}G\neq C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)).

If moreover
G
has the integrable Haagerup property, then
there is
p\in(2,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))\neq A\rtimes_{r}G.

Proof.

Let
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
. It follows from the interpolation relation in Proposition
3.14
, applied for
p_{0}=2
and
p_{1}
tending to infinity, that for every
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\theta=2/p
, we have

\|f\|_{C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A)}\leq\|f\|^{\theta}_{A\rtimes_{r}G}\|f\|^{1-\theta}_{\ell^{1}(G;A)}.

Hence, by Proposition
3.15
,

\|f\|_{A\rtimes_{r}G}\leq\inf_{p>2}\|f\|_{C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))}=\lim_{p\to 2^{+}}\|f\|_{C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))}\leq\|f\|_{A\rtimes_{r}G}

so that

\|f\|_{A\rtimes_{r}G}=\inf_{p>2}\|f\|_{C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))}.

Thus our hypothesis implies that for some
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
and some
p\in(2,\infty)
, we must have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{A\rtimes_{u}G}\neq\|f\|_{C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))}.

Suppose now that
G
has the integrable Haagerup property and that for every
p\in[2,\infty)
, we have

C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))=A\rtimes_{r}G.

Since
G
has the integrable Haagerup property, there is a proper conditionally negative function
L
on
G
such that for all
\beta\in(0,1)
,

\varphi_{\beta}:=\beta^{L}\in\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}\ell^{p}(G).

Now fix
\beta\in(0,1)
and
p\geq 2
such that
\varphi_{\beta}\in\ell^{p}(G)
. Suppose that
\widetilde{m_{\varphi_{\beta}}}
is the associated map defined in (
2.2
). By Proposition
2.12
and the discussion before the theorem,
\widetilde{m_{\varphi_{\beta}}}
extends to a (completely) contractive map from
A\rtimes_{r}G=C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
into
A\rtimes_{u}G
. Thus, for every
f\in\ell^{1}(G;A)
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{A\rtimes_{u}G}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\beta\to 1^{-}}\|\widetilde{m_{\varphi_{\beta}}}(f)\|_{A\rtimes_{u}G}\leq\|f\|_{A\rtimes_{r}G}.

This is a contradiction, which completes the proof.
∎

It would be interesting to see whether one could strengthen the statement of the previous theorem and show that, in some cases,
C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(G;A))
can produce an exotic crossed product without knowing whether there is an invariant state on
A
. We finish this section by presenting a generic way of producing many such examples. Our method relies on random walks and compact stationary spaces. First, we briefly recall the concepts and the notation we need.

Let
\mu
be finitely supported probability measure on a countable group
G
. We
always
assume that
\mu
is
non-degenerate
, i.e. its support generates
G
as a semigroup. The
Avez entropy
of
\mu
in
G
, denoted by
h(G,\mu)
, is defined as

h(G,\mu):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{n}\sum_{s\in G}\mu^{*n}(s)\,\log\mu^{*n}(s),

where we use the convention that
\mu^{*n}(s)\log\mu^{*n}(s)=0
whenever
s\notin\text{supp}\,\mu^{*n}
. For a sub-additive function
L
on
G
with values in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we define the
speed
of
\mu
in
G
with respect to
L
to be

\ell(G,\mu):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\in G}\mu^{*n}(s)L(s).

Let
X
be a compact metrizable space such that
G
acts continuously on
X
, and let
\mu
be a finitely supported non-degenerate probability measure on
G
. The quasi-invariant probability measure
\xi
on
X
is said to be
stationary
if
\mu*\xi=\xi
. In this case, we say that
(X,\xi)
is a compact
(G,\mu)
-space.
The
Koopman representation
of
G
on

is the map

\displaystyle\pi_{X}\colon G\to\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(X,\xi))

given by

\displaystyle[\pi_{X}(s)f](x)=\left[\frac{d(s\xi)}{d\xi}(x)\right]^{1/2}f(s^{-1}x)

for all

,

and
\xi
-a.e.
x
. Here,

\frac{d(s\xi)}{d\xi}
denotes the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
s\xi
with respect to
\xi
.
The

of
(X,\xi)
with respect to
(G,\mu)

is defined as

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(X,\xi)=-\sum_{s\in G}\mu(s)\int_{X}\log\left[\frac{d(s^{-1}\xi)}{d\xi}(x)\right]d\xi(x).

It is well known that
h_{\mu}(X,\xi)\geq 0
, with equality occuring if and only if
\xi
is
G
-invariant. Also

\displaystyle h_{\mu^{*n}}(X,\xi)=nh_{\mu}(X,\xi)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

and

(5.1)

\displaystyle h(G,\mu)\geq h_{\mu}(X,\xi),

where

is the Avez entropy of
\mu
.
The equality in (

) occurs if and only if
(X,\xi)
is a measure preserving extension of the Poisson boundary of
(G,\mu)

(see
[
FG
]
).

We state the following result which is a consequence of
[
ASdLSW2
, Theorem 3.11]
(and also follows from the proof of
[
ASdLSW1
, Theorem 4.7]
). The proof is straightforward and we present it here for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mu
be a finitely supported, non-degenerate probability measure on a countable group
G
, and let

\omega\colon G\to(0,\infty)
be such that
\omega^{-1}=1/\omega\in\ell^{1}(G)
. Then

\displaystyle h(G,\mu)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\in G}\mu^{*n}(s)\log\omega(s).

Proof.

Let
\eta:=C\omega^{-1}
, where
C=\|\omega^{-1}\|_{1}^{-1}
. Then
\eta
is a probability measure on
G
, and so, by Gibbs’ Inequality, the relative entropy

D(\mu^{*n}||\eta):=\sum_{s\in G}\mu^{*n}(s)[\log\mu^{*n}(s)-\log\eta(s)]\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

is non-negative. Thus

\displaystyle-\sum_{s\in G}\mu^{*n}(s)\log\mu^{*n}(s)

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{s\in G}\mu^{*n}(s)\log\eta(s)

\displaystyle=-C+\sum_{s\in G}\mu^{*n}(s)\log\omega(s).

The final result follows by dividing both sides of the above inequality by
n
and taking the liminf.
∎

Recall the convention introduced before Theorem
5.3
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{k}
be the free group on

(ii)

Let us first fix some terminology. Let

\mathbb{F}_{k}

be the word-length function on
\mathbb{F}_{k}

\mathbb{F}_{k}

By well-known results of Haagerup from
[
Haa

(\rho,\pi_{X})
of
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{k})
and

\mathbb{F}_{k}
and

\mathcal{A}

We are ready to prove the statements in the proposition.

(i) It follows from

(ii) Suppose that

where the last inclusion follows from

\displaystyle\Xi_{X}^{p}\omega_{\alpha}^{-1}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{k}).

It follows from Proposition

\displaystyle h(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}_{k}}\mu^{*n}(s)\log\left[\Xi_{X}(s)^{-p}\omega_{\alpha}(s)\right]

By
[
Haa
]

\displaystyle C^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{k})\to C^{*}_{\pi_{X}}(\mathbb{F}_{k}),\quad f\mapsto f\varphi_{\beta}.

In particular, using again

\displaystyle\Xi_{X}\varphi_{\beta}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{k},\omega_{\alpha}^{-1}).

Similar to part (ii), it again follows from Proposition

\displaystyle h(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)

\displaystyle\leq 2\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{-1}{n}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}_{k}}\mu^{*n}(s)\log\left(\Xi_{X}(s)\beta^{L(s)}\right)

\displaystyle=2\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left[\dfrac{-1}{n}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}_{k}}\mu^{*n}(s)\log\Xi_{X}(s)+\dfrac{-\log\beta}{n}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}_{k}}\mu^{*n}(s)L(s)\right]

where
\ell(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)
is the speed of the random walk generated by
\mu
in
\mathbb{F}_{k}
. By letting
\beta\to(2k-1)^{-1/p}
, we obtain

(5.3)

\displaystyle h(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)\leq h_{\mu}(X,\xi)+\frac{2\ell(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)\log(2k-1)}{p}.

Thus, we can state that

(5.4)

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(X,\xi)<h(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)-\frac{2\log(2k-1)}{p}\ell(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)\Longrightarrow C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{k};C(X)))\neq C(X)\rtimes_{r}\mathbb{F}_{k}.

∎

We can now exhibit further examples where the functors we introduced in the last section lead to exotic crossed products.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{k}
be the free group on
2\leq k<\infty
generators, and let
\mu
be a finitely supported, non-degenerate probability measure on
\mathbb{F}_{k}
. Then there is
p_{0}\geq 2
so that for every
p\geq p_{0}
, there is a compact
(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)
-space
(X,\xi)
with
h_{\mu}(X,\xi)>0
and

\displaystyle C(X)\rtimes_{r}\mathbb{F}_{k}\neq C^{*}(\textup{PF}^{*}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{k};C(X)))\neq C(X)\rtimes_{u}\mathbb{F}_{k}.

Proof.

It is shown in
[
HY
]
(see also
[
Bow
]
,
[
BZ
]
) that for every
\epsilon\in(0,h(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu))
, there is a compact
(G,\mu)
-space
(X,\xi)
such that
h_{\mu}(X,\xi)=\epsilon
. Hence if
p_{0}\geq 2
is chosen such that
h(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)-\frac{2\log(2k-1)}{p_{0}}\ell(\mathbb{F}_{k},\mu)>0
, then for every
p\geq p_{0}
, one can find a compact
(G,\mu)
-space
(X,\xi)
such that
h_{\mu}(X,\xi)
is positive and satisfies both the conditions of Proposition
5.5
(ii) and (iii). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

The preceding theorem can be generalized, with a fairly analogous proof, to a much larger class of groups. To see this, let
G
be a finitely generated, nonamenable group. Suppose further that there is a proper conditionally negative length function
L
on
G
such that
G
has both rapid decay and the integrable Haagerup property with respect to
L
. This class of groups includes free groups, but also many more examples (see
[
SW2
, Examples 5.13, 5.15 and Remark 5.14]
). Under these assumptions for
G
and
L
, Proposition
5.5
can be generalized, with the same proof, to
G
and
\mu
, for any finitely supported non-degenerate probability measure
\mu
on
G
. Hence Theorem
5.6
holds as well, provided that one could find compact
(G,\mu)
-spaces with arbitrary small positive entropy. However, the latter fact is true, as it is a consequence of the failure of the entropy gap for
(G,\mu)
. Indeed, it follows from
[
BHO
]
and
[
Nev
]
that the existence of an entropy gap is equivalent to the group having Property (T). However, a nonamenable group with Haagerup property does not have Property (T) so that
(G,\mu)
does not have an entropy gap. That is, for every
\delta>0
, there is a compact
(G,\mu)
-space
(X,\xi)
such that
h_{\mu}(X,\xi)\in(0,\delta)
. Therefore, Theorem
5.6
holds for
(G,\mu)
.

Finally, we should say that it is not clear to us whether the actions discussed in Theorem
5.6
and the last remark admit invariant states or not. In general it appears difficult to construct natural explicit examples of non-amenable actions with no invariant states (abstractly one may consider the action of a non-exact
G
on its Stone-Čech compactification). In the final theorem, we present one such class of actions; we do not know, however, if our functors lead to exotic crossed products also in this case.

Let
H
be a connected semisimple Lie group with finite center, and let
P
be the minimal parabolic subgroup of
H
so that
H=PK
, where
K
is the maximal compact subgroup of
H
. Then
H/P
is the Furstenberg (Poisson) boundary of
H
, and admits a unique
K
-bi invariant probability measure
\nu_{P}
. More generally, for
P\subseteq Q\subseteq H
with
Q
being a parabolic subgroup of
H
, there is a unique
K
-bi invariant probability measure
\nu_{Q}
on
H/Q
(
[
Fur1
]
,
[
NZ
]
).

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
H
,
P
, and
Q
be as above with
P
a proper subset of
Q
and
G
a lattice in
H
. Then
C(H/Q)\rtimes_{u}G\neq C(H/Q)\rtimes_{r}G
and there is no
G
-invariant state on
C(H/Q)
.

Proof.

It follows from the Furstenberg work in
[
Fur2
]
and
[
Fur3
]
that there is a probability measure
\mu
on
G
with finite first moment w.r.t the Riemannian metric on
H
such that
\nu_{P}
is the unique
\mu
-stationary measure on
H/P
and
(H/P,\nu_{P})
is the Furstenberg boundary of
(G,\mu)
. Moreover,
\nu_{Q}
is the
unique

\mu
-stationary measure on
H/Q
and
(H/Q,\nu_{Q})
is a
\mu
-boundary (in fact, every
\mu
-boundary is of this form). This, in particular, implies that
C(H/Q)
has no
G
-invariant probability measure as every such measure must also be
\mu
-stationary, thus equal to
\nu_{Q}
, which is not possible (
\nu_{Q}
is quasi-invariant but not
G
-invariant). Furthermore, since
(H/Q,\nu_{Q})
is a proper
\mu
-boundary,
[
NS
, Theorem 9.2]
yields that
(H/Q,\nu_{Q})
is not measurably amenable (as a
G
-space). Hence the action of
G
on
H/Q
is not topologically amenable so that
C(H/Q)\rtimes_{u}G\neq C(H/Q)\rtimes_{r}G
.

∎
