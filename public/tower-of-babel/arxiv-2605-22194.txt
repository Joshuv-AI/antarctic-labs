Segre Varieties and Desarguesian Spreads
Antonio Cossidente, Giuseppe Marino, Francesco Pavese, Paolo Santonastaso, John Sheekey

Abstract
Let PG ⁡ ( n − 1 , q ) \mathrm{PG}(n-1,q) denote the ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -dimensional projective space over 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} . We investigate the intersection of two Desarguesian ( h − 1 ) (h-1) -spreads of PG ⁡ ( k ​ h − 1 , q ) \mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q) and show that it is determined by a subgeometry over a suitable extension field. Our approach combines a characterization of subsets of points of PG ⁡ ( k − 1 , q h ) \mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h}) closed under q q -order subgeometries with a matrix model for Desarguesian spreads based on Moore matrices. This leads naturally to the notion of generalized Segre varieties 𝒮 k ​ r − 1 , h − 1 r ​ ( q ) \mathcal{S}^{r}_{kr-1,h-1}(q) and a geometric description of their maximal subspaces. As a main application, we prove that if two distinct Desarguesian ( h − 1 ) (h-1) -spreads of PG ⁡ ( k ​ h − 1 , q ) \mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q) contain a common pseudo-arc of size k + 1 k+1 , then their intersection is precisely the system ℛ h , q r \mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q} of ( h − 1 ) (h-1) -dimensional subspaces of 𝒮 k ​ r − 1 , h − 1 r ​ ( q ) \mathcal{S}^{r}_{kr-1,h-1}(q) , for some proper divisor r r of h h .

1
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a prime power.
An
(h-1)
-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
is a partition of the point set into mutually
disjoint
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces; such a spread exists if and only if

h
divides
n
.
A distinguished class of spreads is formed by the
Desarguesian spreads
.
Their study goes back to the foundational work of B. Segre
[
12
]
. These spreads play a central role in finite geometry, where they arise naturally
in connection with translation planes, blocking sets, linear sets, eggs, translation generalized quadrangles,
and several other incidence structures, see e.g.

[
5
,
8
,
1
]
.

A useful description of a Desarguesian spread is
in terms of its
director spaces
.
More precisely, if
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
is embedded as a subgeometry of

\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
and fixed pointwise by a semilinear collineation
\Psi
of order
h
,
then a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace
\Theta
, such that
\Theta,\Theta^{\Psi},\ldots,\Theta^{\Psi^{h-1}}
span
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
, determines a spread whose elements are the intersections of

\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
with the subspaces

\langle P,P^{\Psi},\dots,P^{\Psi^{h-1}}\rangle_{q^{h}}
,
where
P
ranges over
\Theta
.
The subspaces
\Theta,\Theta^{\Psi},\dots,\Theta^{\Psi^{h-1}}
are called the
director spaces of the spread, and they uniquely determine it

[
1
]
. On the other hand, the Segre variety
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
is the image of the Segre
embedding

\sigma_{k,h}:\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)\times\mathrm{PG}(h-1,q)\longrightarrow\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q),

and consists of the points corresponding to rank-one
k\times h
matrices.
Its geometry is governed by two families of maximal subspaces, the
reguli
,
whose elements are mutually disjoint within each family and intersect pairwise
in exactly one point across the two families.
Segre varieties thus provide the natural geometric model for rank-one tensors
and appear throughout the theory of field reduction, linear sets, and
semifields

[
5
,
8
,
9
]
. A particularly significant link between these two structures is the following:
the elements of a Desarguesian spread corresponding to the points of a
canonical subgeometry inside a director space form precisely the

(h-1)
-regulus of a Segre variety, see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
.

The aim of this paper is to determine the intersection of two
Desarguesian spreads of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
.
We show that such intersections are governed by subfield geometry and can be
described in terms of suitable generalized Segre varieties. Our first result is a characterization of subsets of points of

\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})
that are closed with respect to
q
-order subgeometries.
This extends a theorem of Rottey and Van de Voorde for the projective line and
shows that such sets necessarily arise from subgeometries defined over a
subfield
[
11
]
. Next, we introduce the geometric setting through a matrix model for the Desarguesian spread. More precisely, we define the ambient projective space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{h}}
in terms of the vector space of
h\times k

matrices with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{h}}
. We endow this space with a suitable semilinear
collineation whose fixed-point set is a canonical subgeometry isomorphic to

\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
. In this model, the points of the fixed subgeometry are represented by Moore matrices and we describe the relation between its spread elements and its director
spaces. Within this framework, we introduce the notion of a
generalized Segre
variety

\mathcal{S}^{r}_{kr-1,h-1}(q)
, associated with a proper divisor
r
of

h
. More precisely, if
\theta_{q^{r}}\subset\Theta
denotes the canonical

q^{r}
-subgeometry of a director space, then the corresponding subset

\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
of the Desarguesian spread
\mathcal{D}_{h}
, consisting of the
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces
associated with the points of
\theta_{q^{r}}
, forms one of the two natural
systems of maximal subspaces of
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{kr-1,h-1}(q)
. The generalized
Segre variety is defined as the union of the elements of
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
; moreover,
it is also partitioned by a second system
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{kr,q}
of mutually disjoint

(kr-1)
-dimensional subspaces. In this sense,
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
and
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{kr,q}
play the role of
the two reguli of the classical Segre variety.

Our main theorem determines the structure of the intersection of two distinct
Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spreads of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
containing a common pseudo-arc of
size
k+1
. Precisely, the common
pseudo-arc first determines a common
(h-1)
-regulus of a Segre variety; then, we prove that, via the matrix-geometric framework, any additional common spread element gives rise to a proper
canonical
q^{r}
-subgeometry of a director space containing the canonical

q
-subgeometry. At that point, by making use of
the subgeometry-closure theorem,
we show that the intersection is exactly the system
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
of
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces
of a generalized Segre variety
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{kr-1,h-1}(q)
, for some proper divisor

r
of
h
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall the preliminaries on
Desarguesian spreads and Segre varieties, and we establish the characterization
of point sets closed under
q
-order subgeometries.
Section
3
develops the geometric framework, including
an explicit matrix model for the Desarguesian spread, the introduction of
generalized Segre varieties, and the study of their properties.
In Section
4
, we determine the intersection structure of
two Desarguesian spreads and prove the main theorem.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some definitions and known results that will be
useful throughout the paper. In particular, we briefly recall Desarguesian spreads and Segre varieties, together with some of their basic properties. We also show a characterization result for sets of points having a closure property with respect to
q
-order subgeometries.

2.1
Desarguesian spreads

An
(h-1)
-spread of the projective space
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
is a collection

\mathcal{S}
of pairwise disjoint
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces such that
every point of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
is contained in exactly one element of

\mathcal{S}
. Equivalently, an
(h-1)
-spread induces a partition of the
point set of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
into
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces.
Two spreads
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
are said to be
equivalent if there exists a collineation of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
mapping

\mathcal{D}_{1}
onto
\mathcal{D}_{2}
.

A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of an
(h-1)
-spread
in
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
is that
h
divides
n
, as shown by Segre
[
12
]
.
Accordingly, we write
n=hk
for some positive integer
k
.
A relevant class of spreads is given by the
Desarguesian spreads
, which can
be described as follows; see
[
12
]
. Let
\Sigma\simeq\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
be embedded as a
q
-order subgeometry of

\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
in such a way that
\Sigma
coincides with
the set of fixed points

\Sigma=\mathrm{Fix}(\Psi)

of a semilinear collineation
\Psi
of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
of order
h
.
For any point
P\in\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
, define

\displaystyle X^{*}(P):=\langle P,P^{\Psi},\ldots,P^{\Psi^{h-1}}\rangle_{q^{h}}.

Observe that
X^{*}(P)
has dimension at most
h-1
for every
P\in\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
. Let
\Theta
be a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
satisfying

\langle\Theta,\Theta^{\Psi},\ldots,\Theta^{\Psi^{h-1}}\rangle_{q^{h}}=\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h}).

(2.1)

Then, for every point
P\in\Theta
, Condition (
2.1
)
implies that
X^{*}(P)
is an
(h-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
. By construction, each subspace
X^{*}(P)
with
P\in\Theta
is stabilized by

\Psi
. Hence, by
[
10
, Lemma 1]
, the intersection

\displaystyle X(P):=X^{*}(P)\cap\Sigma

is an
(h-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\Sigma
.
As
P
ranges over
\Theta
, we obtain the family

\mathcal{L}(\Theta):=\{\,X(P)=X^{*}(P)\cap\Sigma:P\in\Theta\,\},

which consists of
q^{h(k-1)}+q^{h(k-2)}+\cdots+q^{h}+1
mutually
disjoint
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces of
\Sigma
, and therefore forms an

(h-1)
-spread of
\Sigma
.

Any
(h-1)
-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
that is
{\rm PGL}
-equivalent to a spread
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
is called a
Desarguesian spread
of

\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
.
Moreover, the
(k-1)
-dimensional subspaces

\Theta,\Theta^{\Psi},\ldots,\Theta^{\Psi^{h-1}}

are uniquely determined by the spread
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
.
More precisely, if
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)=\mathcal{L}(\Theta^{\prime})
for some

(k-1)
-subspace
\Theta^{\prime}
of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
, then necessarily

\Theta^{\prime}=\Theta^{\Psi^{i}}
for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,h-1\}
.
For this reason, the subspaces

\Theta,\Theta^{\Psi},\ldots,\Theta^{\Psi^{h-1}}

are referred to as the
director spaces
of the Desarguesian spread

\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
.
Further details can be found in

[
1
,
10
,
12
,
15
]
.

2.2
Segre varieties

Let
k,h\geq 1
. The
Segre map

\sigma_{k,h}:\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)\times\mathrm{PG}(h-1,q)\longrightarrow\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)

is defined by

\left((x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}),(y_{1},\ldots,y_{h})\right)\mapsto(x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{h},\ldots,x_{k}y_{1},\ldots,x_{k}y_{h}),

where the coordinates
x_{i}y_{j}
are taken in lexicographical order.
The image of the Segre map
\sigma_{k,h}
is an algebraic variety called the
Segre variety
and it is denoted by

\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
.

We may represent the points of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
by the homogeneous coordinates

(x_{11},x_{12},\ldots,x_{1h};x_{21},\ldots,x_{2h};\ldots;x_{k1},\ldots,x_{kh}).

In this way, to each point of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
we can naturally associate a

k\times h
matrix
(x_{ij})
, with
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j\leq h
.
Consider the family of quadrics whose defining equations are given by the

2\times 2
minors of this matrix.
It can be shown that the Segre variety
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
coincides with
the intersection of all quadrics defined in this way, see
[
5
, Theorem 4.94]
.
Equivalently, the points of the Segre variety
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
are precisely
those whose coordinates form a matrix
(x_{ij})
of rank
1
, see
[
5
, Theorem 4.101]
.

The variety
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
contains
two distinguished families of maximal subspaces. Fixing a point of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
and letting the point of
\mathrm{PG}(h-1,q)
vary, we
obtain an
(h-1)
-dimensional subspace entirely contained in

\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
. As the point of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
varies, the resulting
family of pairwise disjoint
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces forms a so called

(h-1)
-regulus

\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
of the Segre variety

\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
. Analogously, fixing a point of
\mathrm{PG}(h-1,q)
and varying the point in

\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q)
yields a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of

\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
. These subspaces form another family, called a

(k-1)
-regulus

\mathcal{R}_{k,q}
of
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
. Distinct subspaces belonging to the same regulus are mutually disjoint, whereas any two
subspaces from different reguli intersect in exactly one point. Moreover, every
maximal subspace contained in
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
belongs to exactly
one of these two reguli. For further details, we refer the reader to

[
4
,
5
]
.

2.3
A preliminary result

In this section, we investigate a higher-dimensional analogue of
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
. More precisely, we study subsets of points of

\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})
satisfying a closure property with respect to

q
-order subgeometries, and we show that such sets necessarily arise from
a subgeometry over a subfield. In
[
11
]
the authors prove the following.

Theorem 2.1

(see
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Assume that
q>2
. If
\mathcal{S}
is a set of at least three points of
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{h})
, such that any three points of
\mathcal{S}
determine a
q
-order subline entirely contained in
\mathcal{S}
, then
\mathcal{S}
defines a
q^{r}
-order subline of
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{h})
for
some divisor
r
of
h
.

A set of
k+1
points of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})
is said to be in
general position
if no
k
of them lie in a hyperplane. Note that
k+1
points of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})
in general position determine a unique
q
-order subgeometry of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})
. Here, we are interested in a generalization of Theorem
2.1
. Precisely, we consider
\mathcal{S}
to be a set containing
k+1
points of

\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})
, with
q>2
, in general position, such that for any choice
of
k+1
points of
\mathcal{S}
in general position, the unique
q
-order
subgeometry determined by them is entirely contained in
\mathcal{S}
.

The following lemmas collect some geometric properties that follow from
the assumptions on
\mathcal{S}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subset of points of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})
such that
\mathcal{S}
contains
k+1
points in general position, and such that for any

k+1

\mathcal{S}

-order

, then

. Also, if

are two intersecting lines such that

.

Proof.

First, we prove that if a line
\ell

\mathcal{S}
, then

.
Let
P_{1}
and

be distinct points of
\mathcal{S}
, and let
\ell

\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})

contains a
q

-order subgeometry determined by

points of

and, by hypothesis, is entirely contained in

\mathcal{S}
. Hence, if a line
\ell
has at least two points in common with

\mathcal{S}
, then

, proving the first part of

To prove the second part, let
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
be two intersecting lines such

for

. Let

. If one of the points

Denote by

the
q
-order subgeometry determined by
k+1
points of

\mathcal{S}

.
Let

\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})

\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
. Then there exists a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
containing
\Xi
and the
(h-1)
-regulus
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
of
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
if and only if there exists a point
P
of
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})
such that
\overline{\Xi}=\left\langle P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\right\rangle_{q^{h}}
. Moreover,
P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
is a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
containing
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
, then by Lemma
3.4
,
\Theta^{\psi^{i}}\in\mathcal{R}_{k,q^{h}}
. Hence, if
\Xi\in\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
, let
P^{\psi^{i}}=\overline{\Xi}\cap\Theta^{\psi^{i}}
,
i=0,\dots,h-1
. In particular,
\overline{\Xi}
is spanned by
P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}
and
P^{\psi^{i}}\in\overline{\Xi}\cap\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})
,
i=0,\dots,h-1
. Viceversa, if
P\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})
, let
\Theta
be the unique member of
\mathcal{R}_{k,q^{h}}
through
P
. Then
\Theta^{\psi^{i}}\in\mathcal{R}_{k,q^{h}}
,
i=0,\dots,h-1
. Observe that
\Xi
belongs to
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
by construction, and the system
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
of
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
is contained in
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
by Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\Xi
be an
(h-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\Pi\simeq\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
disjoint from
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
, and
\overline{\Xi}
its extension in
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
. Let
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
be a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
such that
\Xi\in\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
and
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}\subset\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
. Let
P
denote the point
\Theta\cap\overline{\Xi}
. Then
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
is the unique Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
containing
\Xi
and
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
if and only if
\overline{\Xi}\cap\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})=\left\{P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\right\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
be a further Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
such that
\Xi\in\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
and
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}~\subset~\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
. Then
\Phi\in\mathcal{R}_{k,q^{h}}
and
\Phi\neq\Theta^{\psi^{i}}
,
i=0,\dots,h-1
. Hence
\Phi\cap\Theta^{\psi^{i}}=\emptyset
,
i=0,\dots,h-1
, and
Q=\overline{\Xi}\cap\Phi
is a point of
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})
, with
Q\notin\left\{P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\right\}
. Similarly, if
Q
is a point of
\overline{\Xi}\cap\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})
, with
Q\notin\left\{P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\right\}
, let
\Phi
be the unique member of
\mathcal{R}_{k,q^{h}}
through
Q
. Then
\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
is a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
such that
\Xi\in\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
and
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}~\subset~\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
. Moreover
\mathcal{L}(\Phi)\neq\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
.
∎

We are now in a position to state and prove the main result of this subsection,
which provides a complete geometric characterization of when a Desarguesian
spread contains, besides the
(h-1)
-regulus of a Segre variety, an additional

(h-1)
-dimensional subspace disjoint from it.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\Xi
be an
(h-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\Pi\simeq\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
disjoint from
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
, and
\overline{\Xi}
its extension in
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q^{h})
. Let
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
be a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
such that
\Xi\in\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
and
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}\subset\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
. Let
P
denote the point
\Theta\cap\overline{\Xi}
. Then
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
is the unique Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
containing
\Xi
and
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
if and only if
P
is not contained in a proper canonical subgeometry of
\Theta
containing
\theta_{q}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.6
,
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)
is the unique Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\Pi
containing
\Xi
and
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
if and only if
\overline{\Xi}\cap\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})=\left\{P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\right\}
, where
\overline{\Xi}=\left\langle P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\right\rangle_{q^{h}}
and
P
is defined as in (
3.1
). By (
3.2
),
\overline{\Xi}
is defined by
\overline{\pi}_{v}
. Hence, if
Q\in\overline{\Xi}\cap\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})
there are
\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{h}}
,
i=0,\dots,h-1
, such that
Q
is represented by the matrix

\displaystyle{\rm diag}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{h})M_{v}

of rank one.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{i\mid\alpha_{i}\neq 0\}
. Since
\psi
fixes
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q^{h})
, we may assume, without loss of generality, that
1\in\mathcal{I}
. Notice that since

\displaystyle{\rm rk}\left({\rm diag}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{h})M_{v}\right)=1

we have that

\displaystyle i\in\mathcal{I}\iff v^{\sigma^{i}}\in\langle v\rangle_{q^{h}}.

On the other hand, if
v^{\sigma^{i}},v^{\sigma^{j}}\in\langle v\rangle_{q^{h}}
, then
v^{\sigma^{-i}},v^{\sigma^{i+j}}\in\langle v\rangle_{q^{h}}
.
Hence
\mathcal{I}
must correspond to an additive subgroup of the group of integers modulo
h
. Therefore
|\mathcal{I}|=t
, for some divisor
t
of
h
. If
t=1
, then
Q\in\{P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\}
and
P\in\theta_{q}
. If
t>1
and hence
Q\notin\{P,P^{\psi},\dots,P^{\psi^{h-1}}\}
, let
h=rt
. Then
v^{\sigma^{r}}=\alpha v
for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{h}}
and hence

v=v^{\sigma^{h}}=(v^{\sigma^{r}})^{\sigma^{r(t-1)}}=\left(\left((v^{\sigma^{r}})\right)^{\sigma^{r}}\dots\right)^{\sigma^{r}}=\alpha^{1+q^{r}+\cdots+q^{r(t-1)}}v={\mathrm{N}}_{q^{h}|q^{r}}(\alpha)v,

where
{\mathrm{N}}_{q^{m}|q^{r}}
denotes the field norm function from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{h}}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}
. It follows that
{\mathrm{N}}_{q^{h}|q^{r}}(\alpha)=1
, and so there exists (by Hilbert’s Theorem 90) some
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{h}}
such that
\alpha=\beta^{q^{r}-1}
. Let
v^{\prime}=\beta^{-1}v
. Then

\displaystyle(v^{\prime})^{\sigma^{r}}=\beta^{-q^{r}}\alpha v=\beta^{1-q^{r}}\alpha v^{\prime}=v^{\prime}.

Therefore, the
k
entries of
v^{\prime}
lie in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}
and
v=\beta v^{\prime}
. Hence, the point
P\in\Theta
represented by (
3.1
) lies in
\theta_{q^{r}}
, the canonical
q^{r}
-order subgeometry of
\Theta
.
∎

4
The intersection of two Desarguesian spreads

In this section, using the geometric setting and the results established in the
previous section, we are able to determine how two Desarguesian spreads
intersect.

Consider the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}
, regarded as a vector space of dimension
kh
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}^{*}
, let
g_{\alpha}\in{\rm GL}_{kh}(q)
be given by
g_{\alpha}:x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}\mapsto\alpha x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{G}=\left\{g_{\alpha}\in{\rm GL}_{kh}(q)\mid\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}^{*}\right\}

is a cyclic subgroup of
{\rm GL}_{kh}(q)
isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}^{*}
, and
\tilde{G}
is regular on the vectors of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}^{*}
. The image
G
in
{\rm PGL}_{kh}(q)
of a conjugate of
\tilde{G}
in
{\rm GL}_{kh}(q)
is called Singer group of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
. The following results follow from
[
7
, Corollary 2]
and
[
6
, Theorem 7.3]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

•

A cyclic regular subgroup of
{\rm PGL}_{kh}(q)
is a Singer group of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
.

•

These cyclic regular subgroups form a single conjugacy class in
{\rm PGL}_{kh}(q)
consisting of the Singer groups of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
.

•

The normalizer of a Singer group in
{\rm PGL}_{kh}(q)
has order
kh\frac{q^{kh}-1}{q-1}
.

Let
\tilde{H}
be the subgroup of
\tilde{G}
of order
q^{h}-1
, that is

\displaystyle\tilde{H}=\left\{g_{\alpha}\in{\rm GL}_{kh}(q)\mid\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{h}}^{*}\right\}.

The orbits of
\tilde{H}
on the vectors of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}
form a vector space partition
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{h}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}
, that is a set of
h
-dimensional vector subspaces such that every non-zero vector of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{kh}}
is contained in a unique member of
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{h}
, and
\tilde{G}/\tilde{H}
acts regularly on
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{h}
.
The image
H
in
{\rm PGL}_{kh}(q)
of a conjugate of
\tilde{H}
in
{\rm GL}_{kh}(q)
is called Singer subgroup of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of order
\frac{q^{h}-1}{q-1}
. The set
\mathcal{D}_{h}
, consisting of
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
whose underlying vector spaces are elements of
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{h}
form a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
. Moreover,
H
leaves invariant every element of
\mathcal{D}_{h}
. For more information see
[
3
,
15
]
.

A
pseudo-arc
of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
is a set
\mathcal{P}_{h}
of
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces such that every subset of
k
of them spans the entire space. When
h=1
, this definition reduces to the classical notion of arcs in projective
spaces. We recall the following fundamental bound on the size of a pseudo-arc.

Theorem 4.2

(see
[
14
, Theorem 4.11]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}
be a pseudo-arc of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
. We have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{P}_{h}|\leq\begin{cases}q^{h}+k&\mbox{ if }q\mbox{ is even,}\\
q^{h}+k-1&\mbox{ if }q\mbox{ is odd.}\\
\end{cases}

A pseudo-arc
\mathcal{P}_{h}
is said to be
Desarguesian
if
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{h}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{h}
is a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
. Note that, if
k>2
, the largest known pseudo-arc
\mathcal{P}_{h}
of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
is Desarguesian, it has size
q^{h}+1
and arises from a normal rational curve of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{h})
by field reduction.

Proposition 4.3
.

A pseudo-arc
\mathcal{P}_{h}
of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
is Desarguesian if and only if
\mathcal{P}_{h}
is fixed by a Singer subgroup of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of order
\frac{q^{h}-1}{q-1}
elementwise.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{D}_{h}
is a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
and
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{h}
, then there is a Singer subgroup of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of order
\frac{q^{h}-1}{q-1}
, say
H
, stabilizing
\mathcal{D}_{h}
elementwise. Therefore
\mathcal{P}_{h}
is fixed by
H
elementwise. Viceversa, if
H
is a Singer subgroup of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of order
\frac{q^{h}-1}{q-1}
fixing
\mathcal{P}_{h}
elementwise then the orbits of
H
on points of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
form a Desarguesian spread
\mathcal{D}_{h}
such that
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{h}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}
be a pseudo-arc of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of size
k+1
. Then there exists a unique Segre variety
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
such that
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subset\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}=\{\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\dots,\Sigma_{k},\Sigma_{k+1}\}
. Every point
P
in
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\Sigma_{i}\right)
lies on a unique
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace spanned by the points
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
, where
P_{i}\in\Sigma_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
. Varying
P\in\Sigma_{k+1}
a set of
\frac{q^{h}-1}{q-1}
pairwise disjoint
(k-1)
-dimensional subspaces is constructed. Similarly, by starting from
h+1
of these
(k-1)
-dimensional subspaces so obtained, that form a pseudo-arc of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
, a set of
\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}
pairwise disjoint
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces arises. By
[
13
]
they are the
(k-1)
-regulus and the
(h-1)
-regulus of a Segre variety
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
.
∎

Next, we observe that
{\rm PGL}_{kh}(q)
is transitive on pseudo-arcs of
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of size
k+1
(see also
[
14
, Theorem 1.6]
). Let

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}&\dots&A_{k}\end{pmatrix}

(4.1)

be a block
1\times k
matrix over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
A_{i}
is a square matrix of order
h
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
i=1,\dots,k
. Denote by
\Sigma_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
, the
(h-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
whose underlying vector space is spanned by the rows of (
4.1
) where

\displaystyle A_{j}=\begin{cases}I_{h}&\mbox{ if }j=i,\\
\mathbf{0}_{h}&\mbox{ if }j\neq i.\end{cases}

Here
I_{h}
and
\mathbf{0}_{h}
are the identity and zero matrix of order
h
, respectively. Let
\Sigma
be an
(h-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
such that any
k
of
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\dots,\Sigma_{k},\Sigma
span the whole
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
. Then the underlying vector space of
\Sigma
is spanned by the rows of (
4.1
), where
A_{i}\in{\rm GL}_{h}(q)
. Indeed,

\displaystyle{\rm rk}\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}&\dots&A_{k}\\
\mathbf{0}_{h}&I_{h}&\dots&\mathbf{0}_{h}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbf{0}_{h}&\mathbf{0}_{h}&\dots&I_{h}\end{pmatrix}={\rm rk}\begin{pmatrix}I_{h}&\mathbf{0}_{h}&\dots&\mathbf{0}_{h}\\
A_{1}&A_{2}&\dots&A_{k}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbf{0}_{h}&\mathbf{0}_{h}&\dots&I_{h}\end{pmatrix}=\ldots={\rm rk}\begin{pmatrix}I_{h}&\mathbf{0}_{h}&\dots&\mathbf{0}_{h}\\
\mathbf{0}_{h}&I_{h}&\dots&\mathbf{0}_{h}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{1}&A_{2}&\dots&A_{k}\end{pmatrix}=hk.

Therefore
\Sigma
can be chosen in

\frac{|{\rm GL}_{h}(q)|^{k}}{|{\rm GL}_{h}(q)|}

ways.

Let
H
be the stabilizer in
{\rm PGL}_{kh}(q)
of each of the
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces
\Sigma_{1},\dots,\Sigma_{k}
. Note that a projectivity of
H
is induced by a block matrix given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\mathbf{0}_{h}&\dots&\mathbf{0}_{h}\\
\mathbf{0}_{h}&A_{2}&\dots&\mathbf{0}_{h}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathbf{0}_{h}&\mathbf{0}_{h}&\dots&A_{k}\end{pmatrix},

(4.2)

where
A_{i}\in{\rm GL}_{h}(q)
. Hence the centre
Z(H)
of
H
is
\left\{\rho I_{hk}\mid\rho\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}\right\}
and

|H|=\frac{|{\rm GL}_{h}(q)|^{k}}{q-1}
.

Let
\Sigma_{k+1}
be the
(h-1)
-dimensional subspace whose underlying vector space is spanned by the rows of (
4.1
), where
A_{1}=A_{2}=\ldots=A_{k}=I_{h}
. Then an element of
Stab_{H}(\Sigma_{k+1})
is induced by (
4.2
) where
A_{1}=A_{2}=\ldots=A_{k}
. Moreover
Z(H)\subset Stab_{H}(\Sigma_{k+1})
and

Stab_{H}(\Sigma_{k+1})\simeq\frac{{\rm GL}_{h}(q)}{Z(H)}
.

It follows that
H
is transitive on the
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces
\Sigma
of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
such that any
k
of
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\dots,\Sigma_{k},\Sigma
span the whole
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}
be a pseudo-arc of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of size
k+1
and let
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
be the unique Segre variety such that
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subset\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
. If
\mathcal{D}_{h}
is a Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
such that
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subset\mathcal{D}_{h}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}\subset\mathcal{D}_{h}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}=\{\Sigma_{1},\dots,\Sigma_{k+1}\}
. It is enough to observe that the unique cyclic group of
Stab_{H}(\Sigma_{k+1})
acting regularly on the points of each of the
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathcal{P}_{h}
is a Singer subgroup of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of order
\frac{q^{h}-1}{q-1}
and fixes both
\mathcal{D}_{h}
and
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}
be a pseudo-arc of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of size
k+1
and let
\mathcal{D}_{h}
,
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
be distinct Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spreads of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
such that
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subset\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
. Then
|\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}|=\frac{q^{kr}-1}{q^{r}-1}
for some proper divisor
r
of
h
. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
, where
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
is the system of
(h-1)
-dimensional subspaces of a generalized Segre variety
\mathcal{S}^{r}_{kr-1,h-1}(q)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}
be a pseudo-arc of
\Pi\simeq\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
of size
k+1
and let
\mathcal{L}(\Theta)=\mathcal{D}_{h}
,
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
be distinct Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spreads of
\Pi\simeq\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
such that
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subset\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
. By Lemma
4.4
, there is a Segre variety
\mathcal{S}_{k-1,h-1}(q)
of
\Pi
such that
\mathcal{P}_{h}\subset\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
and by Proposition
4.5
,
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
. If
h
is prime, then
\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
, by Theorem
3.7
. Assume that
\mathcal{R}_{h,q}\subset\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
and let
\Xi\in\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
, with
\Xi\notin\mathcal{R}_{h,q}
. With the same notation used in subsection
3.2
, let
P=\overline{\Xi}\cap\Theta
. By Theorem
3.7
, there exists a proper divisor
r
of
h
such that
h=rt
and
\theta_{q^{r}}
is the canonical
q^{r}
-order subgeometry of
\Theta
containing
P
and
\theta_{q}
. By Proposition
4.5
and Theorem
2.4
, it follows that
\Phi\in\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
, if
\overline{\Phi}\cap\Theta
is a point of
\theta_{q^{r}}
. Hence, with the same notation used in subsection
3.1
,
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
. Then two possibilities arise: either
\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
or
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}\subset\mathcal{D}_{h}\cap\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\prime}
. If the latter case occurs, then the previous argument can be repeated, and the statement follows.
∎

The result in Theorem
4.6
extends and generalizes
[
11
, Corollary 1.6]
. It would be nice to determine the number of Desarguesian
(h-1)
-spreads of
\mathrm{PG}(kh-1,q)
that pairwise intersect in precisely
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
. By Theorem
4.6
such a number equals
\frac{|G|}{|N|}
, where
G
is the stabilizer in
Stab_{{\rm PGL}_{kh}(q)}(\mathcal{S}^{r}_{kr-1,h-1}(q))
of
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{h,q}
and
N
is the normalizer of a Singer cyclic subgroup of order
\frac{q^{h}-1}{q-1}
in
G
.
