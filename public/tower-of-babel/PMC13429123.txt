Clinical Outcomes of Double C-Loop Haptic Intraocular Lenses Across Different Optic Designs: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Camille Bosc, Eloïse Le Malefan, Pauline Boury, Mélanie Bernier
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo assess the visual, refractive, and rotational stability outcomes of double C-loop haptic intraocular lenses (IOLs) across different optic designs.MethodsThis retrospective study included 69 eyes implanted with PODEYE toric, Serenity toric, and FineVision HP toric IOLs (Beaver-Visitec International, Inc. USA). A total of 23 eyes per group were analyzed at 3-months postoperatively. Subjective refraction, monocular uncorrected distance visual acuity and corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA), and rotational stability were evaluated in all cases.ResultsIn the PODEYE toric IOL group, 100% of eyes were within ±0.50D with a mean spherical equivalent (SE) of 0.06±0.26D; in the Serenity toric IOL group, this percentage was 78.26% with a mean SE of –0.28±0.38D, while in the FineVision HP toric IOL group, 86.96% of eyes were within ±0.50D, with a mean SE of 0.22±0.30D (p<0.001). Mean CDVA was –0.02±0.04, –0.02±0.04, and –0.05±0.07 logMAR in the PODEYE toric, Serenity toric, and FineVision HP toric IOLs groups, respectively (p=0.047). In the PODEYE toric IOL group, 23.81% and 100% of eyes achieved a cumulative CDVA of ≥20/16 and ≥20/20, respectively. Corresponding values were 17.39% and 100% for the Serenity toric IOL group, and 52.17% and 95.65% for the FineVision HP toric IOL group. Mean IOL rotation was 1.09±0.79, 1.70±1.72 and 1.78±1.31 degrees for the PODEYE toric, Serenity toric, and FineVision HP toric IOLs groups (p=0.16). 100% of eyes exhibited rotation ≤10 degrees, and 100%, 91.30% and 100% of eyes in the PODEYE toric, Serenity toric, and FineVision HP toric IOLs groups, respectively, showed rotation ≤5 degrees (p=0.108).ConclusionDouble C-loop haptic IOLs provide excellent refractive, distance visual acuity, and rotational stability outcomes across different optic designs.

Introduction

Correcting pre-existing astigmatism with toric intraocular lenses (IOLs) in patients with cataract has enabled surgeons to improve refractive accuracy and visual acuity outcomes following surgery. Achieving emmetropia after both cataract and refractive lens exchange procedures is considered a hallmark of excellence in modern surgical practice. A recent report by the American Academy of Ophthalmology, which analyzed 63 publications evaluating visual and refractive outcomes as well as rotational stability in eyes implanted with toric IOLs, concluded that these lenses are effective in neutralizing corneal astigmatism, resulting in better uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA) and a significant reduction in refractive astigmatism after cataract surgery compared with non-toric IOLs.1 Postoperative alignment of toric IOL is critical for successful astigmatism correction since rotational instability post-surgery may significantly compromise refractive outcomes and reduce visual acuity. Several factors, including preoperative refraction, biological parameters, surgical technique, postoperative care, and long-term management, have been reported to influence IOL rotational stability.2 A systematic review and meta-analysis including 51 studies and 4863 eyes evaluated the rotational stability of toric IOLs across different IOL models and haptic designs.3 The authors concluded that postoperative IOL rotation depends on multiple aspects of IOL material and design. Notably, double-loop haptic designs demonstrated exceptional rotational stability, which may be attributed to the increased number of contact points between the haptics and the capsular bag, thereby increasing the total friction.

A recent review on double C-loop haptic lenses (POD platform [BVI Inc., Waltham, USA]) concluded that this platform provides good rotational stability across different IOL models, including monofocal and trifocal optic designs, and with both hydrophilic and hydrophobic materials.4 Astigmatism correction using toric designs can be implemented across a wide range of IOL models, from monofocal to presbyopia-correcting lenses. The POD platform, characterized by a symmetrical quadripod design with 2-by-2 oppositely oriented haptics, is currently used in several toric IOLs made of hydrophobic material and has demonstrated favourable clinical outcomes in monofocal (PODEYE),5–9 trifocal (FineVision HP),10 and more recently isofocal (Serenity)11,12 optic designs. However, to the best of our knowledge, no clinical studies have directly compared the outcomes of these three IOL types within toric platforms. Therefore, the main aim of this retrospective clinical study was to assess visual acuity, refractive, and rotational stability outcomes following implantation of PODEYE toric, Serenity toric, and FineVision HP toric IOLs after cataract surgery.

Materials and Methods

Design and Surgical Procedure

This retrospective clinical study included 69 eyes from 44 patients who underwent cataract surgery at the Institut Ophtalmologique de l’Ouest (IOO) Jules Verne (Nantes, France), with implantation of PODEYE toric, Serenity toric, or FineVision HP toric IOLs. All eyes received toric IOL models. The study was approved by the IOO Review Board (IOO-2025-2) and conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practices and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki with patients signing the informed consent form. Inclusion criteria comprised patients undergoing cataract phacoemulsification surgery with implantation of PODEYE toric, Serenity toric, or FineVision HP toric IOLs, and availability for a 3-month follow-up visit. Exclusion criteria included the presence of ocular diseases that could compromise postoperative visual outcomes evaluating the clinical records. All surgeries were performed by the same experienced surgeon (CB) using the EVA NEXUS system (DORC Dutch Ophthalmic Research Centre (International) B.V., Zuidland, The Netherlands) and a pop- and pre-chop technique. In all cases, the IOL was implanted in the capsular bag.

Intraocular Lenses

All IOLs are manufactured from the same glistening-free hydrophobic acrylic material (GFY, n=1.53 and Abbe number=42), and incorporate blue light and ultraviolet filters. They share an identical haptic design based on the POD platform, characterized by a double C-loop configuration with Ridgetech and posterior angulation. This platform provides 4 contact points with the capsular bag and allows intraoperative rotation of the lens in both clockwise or counterclockwise directions. The PODEYE toric IOL (PODT49P model) features a biconvex aspheric toric monofocal design, with spherical powers ranging from +6.00D to +30.00D in 0.50D increments. The Serenity toric IOL (PODST49P model) has an aspherical toric isofocal optic design, with spherical powers ranging from +10.00D to +30.00D in 0.50D increments and from +31.00D to +35.00D in 1.00D increments. The FineVision HP toric IOL (PODFT49P model) incorporates a biconvex aspheric toric diffractive design providing 2 additions (+3.50D and +1.75D), with spherical powers ranging from +10D.00 to +35.00D in 0.50D increments. The three IOL models share the same overall diameter (11.40 mm) and optic diameter (6.00 mm) and are available in identical cylindrical powers (1.00, 1.50, 2.25, 3.00, 3.75, 4.50, 5.25 and 6.00D, at the IOL plane).

Pre- and Post-Operative Measurements

Before the surgery, all patients underwent a full preoperative assessment, including optical non-contact biometry using the IOLMaster (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany) or the Tomey OA-2000 (Nagoya, Japan) biometers, monocular logMAR corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) and subjective refraction (sphere, cylinder, and axis). The target refraction was emmetropia in all eyes. Intraocular lens power was calculated using the Barrett toric and BVI and ESCRS’s calculators. Postoperative assessment at 3-months included manifest refraction, monocular logMAR UDVA and CDVA, and IOL rotation. Absolute rotational stability was calculated between baseline and the 3-month postoperative visit. To determine the stability of the toric IOLs after implantation, IOL orientation at baseline was defined as the surgical position recorded intraoperatively using the Callisto system (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany) at the end of the surgery and, at the postoperative follow-up visit, using the slit-lamp.

Statistical Analysis and Sample Size

All the variables (gender, age, UDVA, CDVA, sphere, cylinder and axis, biometric parameters, rotation, and IOL powers) were entered into a database and analyzed using Microsoft Excel (2019, v. 16.43, Microsoft Corporation, USA). Descriptive statistics included mean, standard deviation, range, and 95% confidence intervals. Comparisons between groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by post-hoc Tukey’s tests for multiple comparisons, and Fisher’s exact test. A p value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The sample size was calculated assuming a two‑sided alpha of level of 0.05, 80% statistical power, equal group sizes, and based on previously reported rotation outcomes for the PODEYE toric IOL (2.52±2.59 degrees).7 Under these assumptions, a minimum of 23 eyes per group was required. Refraction accuracy (spherical equivalent and cylinder) and visual acuity outcomes were graphically represented using standard plots.13 In addition, astigmatism analysis was performed using a double-angle plot method,14 incorporating preoperative corneal astigmatism and the postoperative refractive astigmatism data.

Table 2 also summarizes the mean, standard deviation, range, and 95% confidence intervals for UDVA and CDVA across the three groups. Rotation Outcomes

Table 2 also reports the mean, standard deviation, range, and 95% confidence interval values for IOL rotation across the three groups. Statistical analysis did not reveal a significant difference in rotation between groups (p=0.16). Overall, 100% of eyes exhibited a rotation ≤10 degrees. Regarding the ≤5 degrees threshold, 100%, 91.30%, and 100% of eyes in the PODEYE toric, Serenity toric, and FineVision HP toric IOLs groups, respectively, were within this limit. Comparison of the proportions of eyes with rotation ≤5 degrees showed no statistically significant differences between groups (p=0.108).

Discussion

Different clinical studies have reported that the PODEYE toric,5–9 FineVision HP toric,15,16 and Serenity (non-toric)11,12 IOLs provide good refractive outcomes and excellent visual acuity when implanted. The purpose of our study is to compare the outcomes of the three types of toric IOLs based on the POD platform with double C-loop haptics, in order to confirm the stability of the platform across different optical designs.

PODEYE Toric IOL

Several publications have analyzed the outcomes of the PODEYE toric IOL.5–8 Ang et al7 in particular, reported the outcomes for a large cohort of 236 eyes at 12-months post-surgery in a prospective multicenter study conducted across several countries. These authors found that 74.6% and 95.8% of eyes were within ±0.50D and ±1.00D of the target refraction, respectively. Additionally, 78.0% and 97.5% of eyes showed a postoperative refractive cylinder of ≤0.50D and ≤1.00D, respectively. The mean manifest SE and refractive cylinder values were 0.16±0.40D and −0.42±0.33D, respectively. Our results are consistent with those reported in this study, confirming the accuracy of the procedure when the PODEYE toric IOL is implanted. Figure 1A shows that 100% of eyes were within ±0.50D, with a mean SE close to emmetropia (0.06±0.26D). Figure 1B shows that 82.61% and 100% of eyes were ≤0.50D and ≤1.00D, respectively, with a mean refractive cylinder about a quarter of diopter (−0.29±0.33D). Accurate refractive outcomes are expected to be associated with good visual acuity outcomes, as uncorrected astigmatism reduces visual acuity. In our study, 23.81% and 100% of eyes achieved a cumulative CDVA of ≥20/16 and ≥20/20, respectively, while 23.81% and 80.95% of eyes achieve these values for cumulative UDVA (Figure 3A). Mean UDVA and CDVA were better than 20/20 (–0.01±0.09 and –0.02±0.04 logMAR, respectively, see Table 2). Ang et al7 also reported that 89.4% and 94.9% of eyes achieved monocular UDVA and CDVA of ≤0.2 logMAR, respectively. The mean monocular UDVA and CDVA were 0.08±0.11 and 0.01±0.09 logMAR, respectively. It should be noted that the rotational stability of the toric IOL is a key determinant of postoperative effectiveness. Any postoperative IOL rotation may reduce astigmatic correction and, in cases of significant rotation, may even induce astigmatism. A reduction of approximately 15% in cylindrical correction occurs for every 5 degrees of misalignment of a toric IOL.17 Therefore, stable haptics are essential to ensure optimal rotational stability. In the study by Ang et al7 the mean absolute IOL rotation was 2.52±2.59 degrees, with 98.56% of eyes showing a rotation <10 degrees. The multicenter study7 concluded that this toric IOL model provides good visual and refractive outcomes in cataract patients with astigmatism, with a mean absolute rotation of <2.6 degrees. Our findings support this conclusion, with a mean rotation of 1.09±0.79 degrees and 100% of eyes showing a rotation ≤5 degrees.

Serenity Toric IOL

Unfortunately, no peer review studies on the Serenity toric IOL have been published to date; therefore, no direct comparison with our results can be made. However, Daya et al12 analyzed the non-toric model in a prospective multicenter study involving 108 eyes of 54 patients from different countries, with a 3-month follow-up. These authors reported that 79.63% and 98.15% of eyes were within ±0.50D and ±1.00D of the target SE, respectively. The mean SE and cylinder values were –0.06±0.44D and –0.37±0.44D, respectively. Double angle plots revealed a centroid value of 0.20D @ 4 degrees ±0.54D and a mean absolute value of 0.38D±0.43D. Our results for SE showed that 78.26% of eyes were within ±0.50D and 95.65% within ±1.00D. For refractive cylinder, 78.26% of eyes were ≤0.50D and all were ≤1.00D (Figure 1A and B). The mean SE and cylinder were −0.28±0.38D and −0.40±0.26D, respectively. The refractive astigmatism centroid was 0.14±0.46D at 156 degrees, and the mean absolute astigmatism was 0.40±0.25D (Figure 2). Regarding distance visual acuity, Daya et al12 reported that 68.41% and 98.15% of eyes achieved UDVA and CDVA ≥20/20, respectively, while 88.89% and 99.07% achieved ≥20/25, respectively. The mean UDVA and CDVA values were 0.02±0.11 and –0.04±0.05 logMAR, respectively. In our study, the corresponding percentages were 47.62% and 100% for UDVA and CDVA ≥20/20, and 80.98% and 100% for UDVA and CDVA ≥20/25, respectively (Figure 3B), with similar mean values (0.07±0.11 and –0.02±0.04 logMAR, respectively, Table 2). Daya et al12 concluded that eyes implanted with this IOL model achieved good distance visual acuity and accurate refractive outcomes. Our findings with the toric version of the IOL are broadly consistent with these results. The mean rotation in our cohort was 1.70±1.72 degrees, with 91.30% of eyes showing a rotation ≤5 degrees and 100% of eyes ≤10 degrees.

FineVision Toric IOL

Two clinical studies have analyzed the performance of the FineVision HP toric IOL. Daya and Espinosa15 retrospectively analyzed 62 eyes at 6 weeks post-surgery, reporting that 88.71% of eyes were within ±0.50D and 98.39% were within ±1.00D of the SE, with a mean value of 0.09±0.39D. The mean IOL rotation reported was 5.8 degrees. Akahoshi,16 in a similar retrospective study of Japanese eyes (n=66), found that 98.48% of eyes were within ±0.50D of SE and all were within ±1.00D, with a mean value of 0.00±0.21D. Regarding refractive astigmatism, 95.45% of eyes had ≤0.50D and 100% of eyes had ≤1.00D, with a mean value of –0.08±0.23D. Our results also showed favourable outcomes, with 86.96% of eyes within ±0.50D and 100% within ±1.00D of SE, and a mean value about a quarter of diopter (0.22±0.30D). In terms of refractive astigmatism, 100% of eyes were ≤0.50D, with a mean value of −0.14±0.20D. The mean rotation in our cohort was lower than that reported by Daya and Espinosa,15 at 1.78±1.31 degrees, with 100% of eyes showing a rotation ≤5 degrees. Focusing now on visual acuity outcomes, Daya and Espinosa15 reported that 82.26% and 98.39% of eyes achieved UDVA of ≥20/20 and ≥20/25, respectively, while for CDVA these values were 95.16% and 100%, respectively. The mean UDVA and CDVA were 0.01±0.06 and –0.01±0.04logMAR, respectively. Similarly, Akahoshi16 reported good percentages of cumulative visual acuity at distance, with 87.88% and 90.91% of eyes achieving UDVA and CDVA ≥20/20, respectively, and 98.48% and 100% achieving ≥20/25, respectively. Our results also support these favourable distance vision outcomes: 91.30% and 100% of eyes achieved UDVA ≥20/20 and ≥20/25, respectively; while 95.65% and 100% achieved these thresholds for CDVA (see Figure 3C). Mean UDVA and CDVA were better than 20/20 (–0.03±0.06 and –0.05±0.07 logMAR, respectively, Table 2). Both Daya and Espinosa15 and Akahoshi16 concluded that this IOL provides accurate refractive outcomes with excellent visual acuity. Our findings are consistent with these authors.

Comparative Analysis

Our refractive outcomes revealed statistically significant differences among the mean values of three IOL groups (p<0.05, Table 2). However, the difference in SE between groups was about ±0.25D, and even smaller for refractive astigmatism, suggesting that the clinical impact is likely negligible. A similar pattern was observed for UDVA and CDVA. In fact, for CDVA, post hoc analysis did not reveal statistically significant differences between any pair of groups (all p>0.05). These findings indicate that, although a modest overall group effect was detected, the intergroup difference were small and not consistently distinguishable in pairwise comparisons. Therefore, all three IOL types showed comparable distance visual acuity outcomes. Rotational outcomes showed no statistically significant differences among groups, either for mean rotation values (p=0.16) or for the proportion of eyes with rotation ≤5 degrees (p=0.108), indicating comparable rotational stability across all groups. This is consistent with previous studies evaluating the POD platform. Li et al12 reported, after analysing by haptic design, that loop haptics are more stable than plate-haptics. Furthermore, double-loop (quad-haptics) and plate loop (closed-loop) designs demonstrate even greater rotational stability than traditional loop haptics. This improved stability may be attributed to the increased number of contact points between the haptics and the capsular bag. The FineVision POD FT toric IOL has demonstrated excellent rotational stability despite its hydrophilic material (pooled mean absolute rotation: 1.53 degrees).2 It should be noted that the FineVision IOL evaluated in our study corresponds to the HP model, which is hydrophobic. A recent review including 19 clinical studies (1428 eyes implanted with the POD platform) concluded that this platform provides good rotational stability across different IOL models, regardless of whether they are monofocal or trifocal, and irrespective of hydrophilic or hydrophobic material.4 The mean rotation across these studies was 2.61 degrees. Therefore, our findings further support the rotational stability of the POD platform, independently of optical design, and confirm its favourable clinical performance.

Several limitations in our study should be acknowledged. First, the follow-up period was limited to 3 months. However, it is well established that the most significant IOL rotation occurs within the first month after surgery,18 suggesting that rotational stability can be reasonably assessed within this timeframe. Nevertheless, longer follow-up periods in future clinical studies would be desirable to confirm the absence of late postoperative IOL rotation. Second, the sample size was relatively small and the preoperative statistical significance of some parameters. Larger cohorts are needed to further validate these preliminary findings and strengthen the evidence supporting our conclusions.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study suggests that the double C-loop haptic provides excellent refractive, distance visual acuity, and rotational stability for eyes implanted with toric IOLs based on different optic designs. Correct spherical power with appropriate toric IOL provides excellent accuracy of the procedure when the POD platform is used. Surgeons can consider this haptic design for stability independently of the optical design of the IOL model selected during cataract surgery.
