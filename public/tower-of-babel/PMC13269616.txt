Breast cancer metastasis to the colon mimicking primary colorectal carcinoma
Authors: Qingkui Liu, Jianheng Gao, Hongxing Yang, Xiaoshan Chen, Yaqi He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast cancer metastasis to the colon is rare, occurring in less than 1% of patients with metastatic breast cancer. Invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC) exhibits a distinct tropism for the gastrointestinal tract and adnexa, often presenting with atypical imaging features that mimic primary tumors. We report a case of a 58-year-old woman with a history of right breast ILC who presented with incidental pelvic masses on ultrasound five years after mastectomy. 2-[¹⁸F]FDG PET/CT revealed multifocal hypermetabolic lesions in the transverse colon wall and bilateral adnexa. CT enterography and colonoscopy confirmed a stenotic colonic lesion with smooth mucosa. Postoperative pathology following right hemicolectomy, hysterectomy, and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy confirmed metastatic breast carcinoma involving the right colon and bilateral adnexa. This case highlights the critical role of PET/CT in detecting occult gastrointestinal metastases in patients with ILC and underscores the importance of considering metastatic disease in the differential diagnosis of colonic lesions in this population.

A 58-year-old woman with a history of right breast invasive lobular carcinoma (T1 N3a M0, stage IIIB), treated by mastectomy five years prior, presented with an incidental pelvic masses on ultrasound. Whole-body 2-[¹⁸F]FDG PET/CT (a, b) revealed multifocal hypermetabolic lesions in the transverse colon wall and bilateral adnexa. CT enterography (c, * denotes uterus) confirmed focal colonic wall thickening and bilateral adnexal masses. Colonoscopy (d) showed a semicircular nodular, stenotic lesion with smooth mucosa, occupying approximately 80% of the lumen. Postoperative pathology following right hemicolectomy, hysterectomy, and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy confirmed metastatic breast carcinoma involving the right colon and bilateral adnexa. Gastrointestinal metastases occur in less than 1% of patients with metastatic breast cancer (McLemore et al. 2005). Invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC) exhibits a distinct tropism for the GI tract and adnexa, likely due to E-cadherin loss promoting a diffusely infiltrative growth pattern (Ciriello et al. 2015). 2-[¹⁸F]FDG PET/CT is crucial for detecting occult lesions and guiding endoscopic evaluation (Falco et al. 2018). In this case, PET/CT identified an occult colonic metastasis five years after initial diagnosis. The imaging and endoscopic appearance—diffuse infiltration with preserved mucosa—closely mimicked primary colorectal carcinoma, representing a key diagnostic pitfall in patients with ILC
