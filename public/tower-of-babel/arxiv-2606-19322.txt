Existential Inclusions of Bi-exact Groups are Conjugacy Representation Rigid

Abstract
If Λ \Lambda is a non-amenable bi-exact group and Λ ↪ Γ \Lambda\hookrightarrow\Gamma is an existential embedding, then each of the intersections Λ ∩ g ​ Λ ​ g − 1 \Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1} for g g a member of Γ \ Λ \Gamma\backslash\Lambda is amenable. This in conjunction with work of Bekka and Kalantar [ 1 ] demonstrates that in this situation, the weak equivalence class of the quasi-regular representation λ Γ / Λ \lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda} determines Λ \Lambda up to conjugacy among the self-commensurating subgroups of Γ \Gamma .

Introduction

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a countable discrete group, and that
\Lambda
is a subgroup which belongs to a specified class
\mathscr{C}
of subgroups of
\Gamma
. Originating in the area of spectral geometry, it has become a question of interest to compare the quasi-regular representation
\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda}
with those arising from other subgroups in the class
\mathscr{C}
.

Indeed, Sunada
[
12
]
leveraged the comparison of quasi-regular representations in the setting of
\Gamma
a finite group in order to produce two isospectral Riemannian manifolds which are not isometric.

Recently, work in this situation was revived by Bekka and Kalantar
[
1
]
. In this work, they studied subgroups
\Lambda
of a countable discrete group
\Gamma
which exhibit a rigidity phenomenon involving the quasi-regular representation. In particular,
\Lambda
is conjugacy representation rigid in the class
\mathscr{C}
provided that if
\Lambda^{\prime}
is another group belonging to
\mathscr{C}
and
\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda}
is weakly equivalent to
\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda^{\prime}}
, then
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
are conjugate within
\Gamma
(I am referring to their rigidity notion as "conjugacy representation rigidity" in order to avoid ambiguity regarding other notions of representation rigidity in the literature).

Their results concern the situation in which the class
\mathscr{C}
consists of self-commensurating subgroups of
\Gamma
. This is a particularly relevant setup, because the quasi-regular representation associated to a self-commensurating subgroup is irreducible.

Suppose that
\Lambda
is a non-amenable subgroup of
\Gamma
such that
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
is amenable for each member
g
of
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
. In this situation,
\Lambda
is referred to as a-normal
[
1
]
(a-normality does not formally require non-amenability of
\Lambda
, but the amenable case is uninteresting). Then
\Lambda
is self-commensurating, and Bekka and Kalantar proved that
\Lambda
is conjugacy representation rigid in the class of self-commensurating subgroups of
\Gamma

[
1
]
.

The purpose of this brief note is to provide further examples of this situation arising from first order logic. The main mechanism for this is the following Theorem.

Theorem:
Let
\Lambda
denote a bi-exact group and suppose that
\Lambda\hookrightarrow\Gamma
is an existential embedding. Then
\Lambda
is an a-normal subgroup of
\Gamma
.

In work of Perin
[
4
]
, there are given many examples of elementary inclusions
\Lambda\hookrightarrow\Gamma
with
\Gamma
torsion free hyperbolic in which
\Lambda
is actually malnormal in
\Gamma
(i.e. the intersections
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
are trivial for
g
a member of
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
). Recall that by the permanence properties of existential and universal formulas, if
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
is an elementary embedding, and

\displaystyle\Lambda\leq K\leq\Gamma,

then the inclusion
\Lambda\leq K
is existential. Moreover, malnormality of
\Lambda
in
\Gamma
evidently implies malnormality of
\Lambda
within
K
. Therefore, Perin’s work provides numerous examples of the above phenomenon for torsion free hyperbolic groups, such that the main Theorem is partially covered by existing work.

Preliminaries on First Order Logic

Here, the necessary first order logic is introduced following Tent and Ziegler
[
13
]
. Within the language of groups, the terms are words in the variables denoting group elements, their inverses, and the identity element. An atomic formula consists of two terms separated by "
=
".

To describe first order formulae, one combines atomic formulae, logical connectors (
\vee
,
\wedge
,
\neg
) and quantifiers (
\forall,\exists
). Where no free variables are present (i.e. each variable is quantified over), a formula is referred to as a sentence. For example, the following is a sentence in the first order language of groups:

\displaystyle\forall g_{1}\exists g_{2}(g_{1}g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}=1)\wedge\neg(g_{2}=1).

The
elementary theory
of a group
G
is the collection of first order sentences which hold true in
G
. An embedding
H\hookrightarrow G
of groups is called elementary if each first order sentence
\phi
in the language of groups with constants in
H
satisfies
G\models\phi
if and only if
H\models\phi
.

Observe that if
H
is elementarily embedded within
G
, then
H
and
G
have the same elementary theory. Indeed, a first order sentence
\phi
in the language of groups is also a first order sentence in the language of groups with constants in
H
. If
G
and
H
have the same elementary theory, then they are called elementarily equivalent. More precisely,
G
and
H
are elementarily equivalent provided that for each first order sentence
\phi
in the language of groups,
G\models\phi
if and only if
H\models\phi
.

It should be noted that
H
being elementarily embedded within
G
is strictly stronger than
H
being elementarily equivalent to
G
(for instance,
2\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Z}
is not an elementary embedding. See Guirardel, Levitt and Sklinos
[
4
]
3.1.1).

The main focus below is existential embeddings. An existential sentence is a first order sentence
\phi
in the language of groups which takes the following form:

\displaystyle\exists x_{1}\exists x_{2}\dots\exists x_{n}\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

where
\psi
is a quantifier free formula. The
existential theory
of a group
G
consists of the collection of existential sentences which hold true in
G
. As with elementary embeddings, an embedding
H\hookrightarrow G
of groups is called existential provided each existential sentence
\phi
in the language of groups with constants in
H
satisfies
G\models\phi
if and only if
H\models\phi
.

Preliminaries on Bi-exactness

The notion of a bi-exact group was introduced by Ozawa in 2004
[
9
]
towards deformation/rigidity results in the theory of II
1
factors.

A group
\Lambda
is bi-exact provided the
\Lambda\times\Lambda
action by left/right translations on the Stone-Čech remainder
\partial^{\beta}\Lambda\vcentcolon=\beta\Lambda\backslash\Lambda
is amenable. Amenability of the action
\Lambda\times\Lambda\curvearrowright\partial^{\beta}\Lambda
asserts the existence of a sequence of continuous maps
\mu^{n}:\partial^{\beta}\Lambda\rightarrow\text{Prob}(\Lambda\times\Lambda)
such that:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in\partial^{\beta}\Lambda}\|s\cdot\mu^{n}_{x}-\mu^{n}_{s\cdot x}\|_{1}=0

for each
s
belonging to
\Lambda\times\Lambda

[
9
]
. Here,

\displaystyle\text{Prob}(\Lambda\times\Lambda)\vcentcolon=\{\mu\in\ell^{1}(\Lambda\times\Lambda)\,|\,\mu\geq 0\,\text{and}\,\|\mu\|_{1}=1\}

and
(s\cdot\mu)(t)=\mu(s^{-1}t)
. For more equivalent conditions defining bi-exactness, see Brown and Ozawa
[
2
]
. The importance of this notion in the present context comes from the following result, which is present in various forms in the literature.

Proposition (
[
10
]
,
[
3
]
):
Suppose
\Lambda
is a bi-exact group. Then for each non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
on
\mathbb{N}
there holds:

{}_{L(\Lambda)}[L^{2}(L(\Lambda)^{\omega})\ominus\ell^{2}(\Lambda)]_{L(\Lambda)}\prec\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\ell^{2}(\Lambda).

That is, the
L(\Lambda)
-
L(\Lambda)
bimodule on the left hand side is weakly coarse.

Proof.

A proof by literature. It was demonstrated by Ozawa
[
10
]
that the group von Neumann algebra of a bi-exact group satisfies
W^{*}
AO. Now, apply Theorem 7.20 of Ding and Peterson
[
3
]
.
∎

The class of bi-exact groups is sizeable, containing all word hyperbolic groups, discrete subgroups of connected simple Lie groups of rank
1
, small cancellation groups, amenable groups, and the semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
(
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
). Moreover, this class is stable under measure equivalence
[
11
]
,
W^{*}
-equivalence
[
3
]
, free products with finite amalgamation
[
9
]
, and passing to subgroups
[
9
]
.

4
Preliminaries on Representation Rigidity

Following the treatment of Bekka and Kalantar
[
1
]
, the setup for conjugacy representation rigidity is introduced. Suppose that
\Lambda
is a subgroup of a group
\Gamma
. Define the commensurator subgroup
\text{Comm}_{\Gamma}(\Lambda)
to consist of those
g
in
\Gamma
such that
\Lambda
and
g\Lambda g^{-1}
are commensurable. That is, those
g
for which the intersection
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
has finite index in both
\Lambda
and
g\Lambda g^{-1}
.

Observe that
\Gamma
acts by left multiplication on the coset space
\Gamma/\Lambda
. This action induces a unitary representation
\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda}
on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma/\Lambda)
called the quasi-regular representation associated to
\Gamma
. By a Theorem of Mackey (see
[
1
]
2.6), if
\Lambda
is self-commensurating (i.e.
\Lambda=\text{Comm}_{\Gamma}(\Lambda)
), then
\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda}
is an irreducible representation of
\Gamma
.

It is then a natural question to ask when two self-commensurating subgroups
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
of
\Gamma
define unitarily (resp. weakly) equivalent irreducible representations of
\Gamma
.

Towards an answer to this question, Bekka and Kalantar
[
1
]
defined the class
\text{Sub}_{\text{sg}}(\Gamma)
of subgroups of
\Gamma
with spectral gap. This class consists of those subgroups
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
such that the trivial representation
1_{\Lambda}
is isolated in the spectrum of the natural representation of
\Lambda
on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma/\Lambda)
. They then demonstrate that self-commensurating subgroups in this class are conjugacy representation rigid in the following sense.

Theorem A (
[
1
]
):
Suppose
\Lambda_{1}
is a self-commensurating subgroup of a countable group
\Gamma
. If
\Lambda_{1}
has spectral gap, then for all self-commensurating subgroups
\Lambda_{2}
of
\Gamma
, the quasi-regular representations
\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda_{1}}
and
\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda_{2}}
are weakly equivalent if and only if
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are conjugate subgroups of
\Gamma
.

If
\Lambda_{1}
is a self-commensurating subgroup of
\Gamma
which satisfies the conclusion of this Theorem, it is said to be conjugacy representation rigid in the class of self-commensurating subgroups of
\Gamma
. In applying this Theorem, Bekka and Kalantar further introduce a sufficient condition on subgroups which guarantees the spectral gap property. It requires the following definition.

Definition:
Suppose
\Lambda
is a subgroup of a countable group
\Gamma
. Then
\Lambda
is a-normal provided
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
is amenable for each member
g
of
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
.

In their paper, Bekka and Kalantar (
[
1
]
4.11) demonstrate that if a non-amenable subgroup
\Lambda
of a countable group
\Gamma
is a-normal, then it has the spectral gap property. Observe immediately that a non-amenable a-normal subgroup
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
is self-commensurating, such that in this case the spectral gap translates via the above Theorem into a representation rigidity result. In fact, such subgroups
\Lambda
are
strongly self-commensurating
in the sense that the intersections
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
for
g
a member of
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
each have infinite index in
\Lambda
.

5
The Main Theorem

Using a trick identical to that in work of Hayes, Jekel, and Kunnawalkam Elayavalli (
[
6
]
Proposition 3.1) along with some basic representation theory, the main theorem is proved.

Theorem:
Let
\Lambda
denote a bi-exact group and suppose that
\Lambda\hookrightarrow\Gamma
is an existential embedding. Then
\Lambda
is an a-normal subgroup of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Set
M=L(\Lambda)
, the group von Neumann algebra. By the bi-exactness of
\Lambda
and the above Proposition,

\displaystyle L^{2}(M^{\omega})\ominus\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\prec\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\ell^{2}(\Lambda)

for each non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
on
\mathbb{N}
. Now, suppose that
\Lambda\hookrightarrow\Gamma
is an existential embedding. As was demonstrated in work of Kunnawalkam Elayavalli
[
7
]
, there exists a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
on
\mathbb{N}
and an embedding
L(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow M^{\omega}
extending the diagonal embedding of
M
:

\displaystyle M\leq L(\Gamma)\leq M^{\omega}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{{}_{M}}[\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\ominus\ell^{2}(\Lambda)]_{M}\prec L^{2}(M^{\omega})\ominus\ell^{2}(\Lambda),

which is weakly coarse by the above. Fix
g
in
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
. Complete
g\vcentcolon=g_{1}
to a system
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of double coset representatives such that
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
is divided as the disjoint union:

\displaystyle\Gamma\backslash\Lambda=\bigcup_{i}\Lambda g_{i}\Lambda.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{{}_{M}}[\ell^{2}(\Gamma)\ominus\ell^{2}(\Lambda)]_{M}\cong\bigoplus_{i}\ell^{2}(\Lambda g_{i}\Lambda)

as
M-M
bimodules. In particular,
\ell^{2}(\Lambda g\Lambda)
is weakly coarse. Now, observe that
\Lambda\times\Lambda
acts transitively on the double coset
\Lambda g\Lambda
. Therefore, if
H
is the stabilizer of
g
, then:

\displaystyle\ell^{2}(\Lambda g\Lambda)\cong\ell^{2}(\Lambda\times\Lambda/H)=\lambda_{\Lambda\times\Lambda/H},

the quasi-regular representation. Observe here that the
M
-
M
bimodule structure translates into a unitary representation of
\Lambda\times\Lambda
as the map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y^{-1})
is an isomorphism
\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\text{op}}\cong\Lambda\times\Lambda
. Under this identification, the coarse bimodule corresponds to the left regular representation. That
\ell^{2}(\Lambda g\Lambda)
is weakly coarse means that
\lambda_{\Lambda\times\Lambda/H}\prec\lambda_{\Lambda\times\Lambda}
. Recall that
\lambda_{\Lambda\times\Lambda/H}=\text{Ind}_{H}^{\Lambda\times\Lambda}1_{H}
, such that standard induction/restriction arguments (see Bekka and Kalantar
[
1
]
Corollary 2.3) yield
1_{H}\prec\lambda_{H}
. Since the trivial representation is weakly contained in the left regular representation,
H
is amenable. Now, fix
(x,y)
in
H
. By definition,

\displaystyle xgy^{-1}=g.

Rearranging,
y=g^{-1}xg
. Hence, both
x
and
g^{-1}xg
belong to
\Lambda
, such that
x
is a member of
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
. Conversely, it is easily seen that a pair of the form
(x,g^{-1}xg)
for
x
a member of
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
belongs to
H
. In short,
H\cong\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
, so that
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
is amenable. Since
g
was an arbitrary member of
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
, the subgroup
\Lambda
is a-normal.
∎

Using bi-exactness alone, the conclusion of a-normality is the strongest possible. Indeed, this is demonstrated by the case in which both
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
are abelian.

Remark:
Observe that the bimodule
\ell^{2}(\Lambda g\Lambda)
being weakly coarse gave rise to the amenability of the intersection
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
. It should be noted that if the bimodules
\ell^{2}(\Lambda g\Lambda)
for
g
a member of
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
are coarse (i.e. embed into a direct sum of the coarse bimodule
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
), then
\Lambda
is seen to be almost malnormal (
[
5
]
section 2). That is, the intersections
\Lambda\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}
for
g
a member of
\Gamma\backslash\Lambda
are finite.

Now, the representation rigidity Corollary is immediate.

Corollary:
Let
\Lambda
be a non-amenable bi-exact group and
\Lambda\hookrightarrow\Gamma
an existential embedding. Both
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
are assumed to be countable and discrete. Then
\Lambda
is strongly self-commensurating within
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
is conjugacy representation rigid in the class of self-commensurating subgroups of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Apply Bekka and Kalantar
[
1
]
Theorem A and the main Theorem.
∎

While not directly applicable to representation rigidity, it should be noted that another Theorem of Bekka and Kalantar applies in this setting as well. This yields a Corollary of independent interest regarding the ideal structure in the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the quasi-regular representation.

Corollary:
Let
\Lambda
be a non-amenable bi-exact group, and
\Lambda\hookrightarrow\Gamma
an existential embedding. Then
C^{*}_{\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda}}(\Gamma)
contains the ideal
K
of compact operators on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma/\Lambda)
, and
K
is minimal in the sense that it is contained within each nonzero, closed, two-sided ideal of
C^{*}_{\lambda_{\Gamma/\Lambda}}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Apply Bekka and Kalantar
[
1
]
Theorem B and the main Theorem.
∎
