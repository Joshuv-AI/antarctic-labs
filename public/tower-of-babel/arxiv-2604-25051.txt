Families of Eliahou semigroups linked to Farey intervals
Axel Bacher

Abstract
We describe new families of Eliahou semigroups, encompassing previous families described by Delgado, Eliahou and Fromentin, and Bras-Amorós. A crucial parameter is a Farey interval associated to the semigroup. We show that these semigroups probably all satisfy Wilf’s conjecture and describe ways to explicitly construct semigroups belonging to these families. This work is based on an exploration of the numerical semigroup tree giving (conjecturally) all Eliahou semigroups of conductor up to 320 thanks to a new way of representing the semigroups and pruning of unwanted branches.

Introduction

A numerical semigroup is a co-finite sub-semigroup of
(\mathbb{N},+)
. Given
some integers

, we use the
classical notation:

S=\langle\gamma_{0},\dotsc,\gamma_{\ell-1}\rangle_{c}

\gamma_{0},\dotsc,\gamma_{\ell-1}
and all

\geq c
. This construction is unique if

\gamma_{0},\dotsc,\gamma_{\ell-1}
are primitive (they cannot be written as

) and

. In this case, we
say that

is

. We
call
\gamma_{0},\dotsc,\gamma_{\ell-1}

and

conductor
of
S
. Primitive elements beyond

are called

generators
and we denote by

their number; the total number of
generators

is called the
embedding dimension
of
S
. We
also call
rank
of
S
, and denote by
k
, the number of left elements
(elements
<c
). The smallest generator
m=\gamma_{0}
plays a special role
and is called the
multiplicity
. The number
g=c-k
is the number of
gaps in
S
and called the
genus
, but we do not need it here. These
parameters, and the others defined below, are illustrated in
Figure
1
in the next section.

Wilf
[
19

gives a simple conjecture concerning these parameters. Define

of

W=ke-c\text.

(1)

Wilf’s conjecture states that every numerical semigroup satisfies

.
This conjecture is nearly fifty years old and still open. It has been proven
to be true in some special cases, namely

]

]
,

[

]
,

Eliahou

\displaystyle E

,

These searches are based on an exploration of the

Our classification uses two key ingredients. The first is the notion of

3.1

In this section, we compute the Eliahou number of the semigroups defined in

If
S
is collision-free, the number of elements in the sumset
i\Gamma
is:

s_{i}=\mchoose{\ell-1}{i}\text{.}

Let
\omega
be the number of long elements in
S
. From (
5
), we have:

k=\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\floor{\frac{(h-i)a^{\prime}}{b^{\prime}}}s_{i}+\omega\text{.}

(14)

The number
s
of critical elements is:

s=s_{0}+\dotsb+s_{h}=\mchoose{\ell}{h}\text{.}

In the section, we make use of the following two summation identities:

\displaystyle\ell\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\frac{(h-i)s_{i}}{h}=s\text{,}

(15)

\displaystyle\ell\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\frac{is_{i}}{h}=(h-1)s_{h}\text{.}

(16)

The first is a consequence of the multiset version of the Chu-Vandermonde
identity and
\ell\smchoose{\ell+1}{h-1}=hs
, while the second is derived
by writing
is_{i}=(\ell-1)\smchoose{\ell}{i-1}
and, after summation,

\ell(\ell-1)\smchoose{\ell+1}{h-2}=h(h-1)s_{h}
.

Finally, from the
h
–Farey interval, we find
q=\ceil{ha/b}-1=\floor{ha^{\prime}/b^{\prime}}
. Moreover, from Definition
2.2
and (
6
), we
get
\rho=\phi_{h}m+t=\ceil{\phi_{h}m}+\tau
.
If
b
divides
h
, then
\phi_{h}=0
, so we have
\rho=t=\tau
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
S
is collision-free, we have
E=E_{0}+\rho+\ell\omega
, where
E_{0}

has the four equivalent forms:

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=-\ell\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\phi^{\prime}_{h-i}s_{i}+\phi^{\prime}_{h}s\text{,}

(17)

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=-(h-1)s_{h}+\ell\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\phi_{h-i}s_{i}-\phi_{h}s\text{,}

(18)

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=-\floor{h\phi^{\prime}_{1}}s+\ell\sum_{j=1}^{\floor{h\phi^{\prime}_{1}}}\mchoose{\ell}{h-\ceil{j/\phi^{\prime}_{1}}}\text{,}

(19)

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=-(h-1)s_{h}+\floor{h\phi_{1}}s-\ell\sum_{j=1}^{\floor{h\phi_{1}}}\mchoose{\ell}{h-\ceil{j/\phi_{1}}}\text{.}

(20)

It is convenient to use (
20
) if
\phi_{1}
is low, since the sum has few
summands. If
\phi_{1}
is high,
\phi^{\prime}_{1}
is low, so we can use (
19
).

Proof 3.2
.

We use
E=k\ell-qs+\rho
and write
\floor{ia^{\prime}/b^{\prime}}=ia^{\prime}/b^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}_{i}
.
From (
14
), (
15
) and
q=ha^{\prime}/b^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}_{h}
, we get (
17
).

Writing instead
\floor{ia^{\prime}/b^{\prime}}=ia/b+\phi_{i}-1
and
q=ha/b+\phi_{h}-1
, we get:

E=\ell\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}(\phi_{h-i}-1)s_{i}+(1-\phi_{h})s+\rho+\ell\omega\text{.}

Using the sum of (
15
) and (
16
) to compute

\ell\mkern 2.0mu\smash{\sum}s_{i}
, this yields (
18
).

To show (
19
), write
\phi^{\prime}_{h-i}=(h-i)\phi^{\prime}_{1}-\floor{(h-i)\phi^{\prime}_{1}}
. We use (
15
) as well as Abel’s lemma, or
summation by parts. This states that:

\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}u_{i}(v_{i+1}-v_{i})=u_{h}v_{h}-u_{0}v_{0}-\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})v_{i+1}\text{.}

We use
u_{i}=\floor{(h-i)\phi^{\prime}_{1}}
and
v_{i}=\smchoose{\ell}{i-1}
, so
s_{i}=v_{i+1}-v_{i}
. Moreover, we have
u_{h}=v_{0}=0
and
u_{i+1}-u_{i}=-1
if and only if
h-i=\ceil{u_{i}/\phi^{\prime}_{1}}
, which shows (
19
). The
case of (
20
) is identical.

3.2
Conditions for
E<0
and
W<0

Using the formulas of Theorem
3.1
, we give several results concerning
the Eliahou and Wilf numbers. We start by giving some sufficient or necessary
conditions for
E<0
. Since
E\geq E_{0}
, the condition
E_{0}<0
is
necessary.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\ell\geq 3
and
b^{\prime}\neq 1
, then
E_{0}<0
.

The condition
b^{\prime}\neq 1
is equivalent to
\fpart{a/b}\neq 1/h
.

If we write
\rho=\phi_{h}m+t
and
m=s+r
, we find
E=E_{0}+\phi_{h}s+\phi_{h}r+t
. The
rational
E_{0}+\phi_{h}s
is thus a lower bound of
E
that depends only
on
h
,
a/b
and
\ell
and has a nice definition thanks to (
18
). We
conjecture that it is negative under the same conditions. This is significant
if
b\nmid h
, since we have
\phi_{h}=0
otherwise.

Proof 3.4
.

Assume first that
b=h
, so
\phi_{h}=0
. Then
(\phi_{h-i})
is a
permutation of
(i/h)
for
i=0,\dotsc,h-1
. As
\ell\geq 3
, the sequence

(s_{i})
is increasing and as
\fpart{a/b}\neq 1/h
, the permutation is not the
identity. Therefore, by the rearrangement inequality, the sum
of
\phi_{h-i}s_{i}
is bounded by the sum of
is_{i}/h
, which is given
by (
16
).

If
b
divides
h
, the sequence
(\phi_{h-i})
consists of the
numbers
i/b
for
0\leq i<b
, each appearing
h/b
times and is thus bounded
by a permutation of
(i/h)
, which gives the result.

Finally, if
b
does not divide
h
, let
\psi_{1}=\floor{h\phi_{1}}/h
. We
have
\floor{h\psi_{1}}=\floor{h\phi_{1}}
and
\psi_{1}<\phi_{1}<1-1/h
, so
by (
20
), the value
E_{0}
for
\phi_{1}
is lower than the one
for
\psi_{1}
, which is negative from the above reasoning.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
S
is collision-free. If
b^{\prime}=1
, or
b=1
and
S
is short,
or
b^{\prime}=h
and
S
is split, then
E\geq 0
.

We conjecture that this still holds if
b^{\prime}=h
and
S
is short. Moreover,
we believe that the collision-free condition is unnecessary; this is further
discussed below.

Proof 3.6
.

If
b^{\prime}=1
, then all the
\phi^{\prime}_{i}
are zero and (
17
) shows that
E\geq 0
.

If
b=1
and
S
is short, then all the
\phi_{i}
are zero and we have
E=-(h-1)s_{h}+\rho
from (
18
).
Since
h\Gamma\subset[c+m-w,c+m)
and
S
is collision-free, we have
w\geq s_{h}
and (
8
) shows that
E\geq 0
.

If
b^{\prime}=h
and
S
is split, then
\phi^{\prime}_{h}=0
and
\phi_{h}=(b-1)/b
. The
same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
applied to (
17
)
shows that
E\geq-(h-1)s_{h}+\rho
. Using
\rho=\phi_{h}m+t
, (
8
) and

w\geq s_{h}
, we get:

E\geq\frac{(b-1)(m-hs_{h})}{b}\text{.}

We use (
10
) and (
8
) to bound
m
, which gives
E\geq 0
.

The next results show ways to build infinite families of Eliahou semigroups
starting from one.
Similar definitions appear in the literature
[
18
,
2
]
, but the
semigroups described below are different from both of them.

Lemma 3.7
.

The semigroup
S(h,a/b+1,\Delta,\tau,m)
is collision-free if and only
if
S
is collision-free. In this case, it has the same Eliahou number.

This semigroup can also be defined as
\langle m,\Gamma+m\rangle_{c+hm}
.
The result does not hold if
S
is not collision-free: the semigroup
S(3,5/3,\{0,2\},0,8)=\langle 8,11,13\rangle_{32}
has
E=11
, but
S(3,8/3,\{0,2\},0,8)=\langle 8,19,21\rangle_{56}
has
E=17
.

Proof 3.8
.

As both semigroups have the same left generators modulo
m
, one is
collision-free if and only if the other is. Moreover, both have the same
h
,

\ell
,
\phi_{i}
and
\phi^{\prime}_{i}
,
\rho
and
\omega
, so they have the
same
E
if they are collision-free.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
S
is collision-free,
b
divides
h
and
n
is large enough, then the
semigroup
S(h,a/b,\Delta,\tau,m+bn)
is collision-free and has Eliahou
number at most that of
S
.

Proof 3.10
.

Let
S_{n}
be the larger semigroup. Using for example (
17
), it
suffices to show that
S_{n}
is collision-free and has at most as many long
elements as
S
.

Let
\lambda\in i\Gamma
and let
\lambda^{\circ}=\lambda+\ceil{(h-i)a/b}m-m-c
. The number
\lambda^{\circ}
is in
(-m,m)
and
\lambda
is short if
and only if
\lambda^{\circ}\geq 0
.
From Definition
2.2
, the conductor and left generators of
S_{n}

are respectively
c+(ah-b)n
and
\Gamma+an
. This shows that the
element
\lambda_{n}
corresponding to
\lambda
in
S_{n}

satisfies
\lambda^{\circ}_{n}=\lambda^{\circ}+\phi_{h-i}bn
. Therefore,
if
\lambda
is short, then
\lambda_{n}
is short.

Let now
\mu\in j\Gamma
. If
i\nequiv j

modulo
b
, then
\phi_{h-i}\neq\phi_{h-j}
and
\lambda^{\circ}_{n}

and
\mu^{\circ}_{n}
are far apart modulo
m+bn
if

is large.
If
i\equiv j
, then
\lambda^{\circ}_{n}-\mu^{\circ}_{n}=\lambda^{\circ}-\mu^{\circ}
, so
if
S
is collision-free and
n
is large enough,
S_{n}
is collision-free.

The proof shows that
S_{n}
has no more collisions than
S
, so we believe that
the result still holds if
S
is not collision-free.

We now turn our attention to he Wilf number. Since

, proving

Lemma 3.11
.

If

is collision-free and

This condition is probably overkill.

The results of this section all apply to collision-free semigroups.
It may seem that, as semigroups with collisions have a lower rank than
collision-free semigroups with the same parameters, they might have a
lower
E
or
W
. Our last result, while partial, suggests that this is
not the case.

In the following, a
collision
is a pair of sums of at most
h
elements
of
\Gamma
that give the same result modulo
m
. A collision at
\lambda
is

primitive
if one cannot write
\lambda=\mu+\nu
where a
smaller collision occurs at
\mu
.

Lemma 3.13
.

If
S
has one primitive collision, then
E>E_{0}+\rho
.

Proof 3.14
.

Assume that
S
is short and the primitive collision occurs at
\lambda
.
Consider the sum giving
\lambda
with the higher number of generators,
say
h-j
. Then any sum of generators containing that sum is redundant.
There are
s_{i}
such sums in the sumset
(h-j+i)\Gamma
for
0\leq i\leq j
. The
decrease in left elements compared to the collision-free case is thus:

\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\floor{\frac{(j-i)a^{\prime}}{b^{\prime}}}s_{i}\text{.}

The decrease in critical elements is
s_{0}+\dotsb+s_{j}=\smchoose{\ell}{j}
. Using
q=ha^{\prime}/b^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime}_{h}
and (
15
), the change
to the Eliahou number
k\ell-qs+\rho
is:

\left(\frac{(h-j)a^{\prime}}{b^{\prime}}-\phi^{\prime}_{h}\right)\mchoose{\ell}{j}+\ell\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\phi^{\prime}_{j-i}s_{i}\text{.}

Since
a^{\prime}/b^{\prime}\geq 1
and
j<h
(as
\{0\}
does not collide with itself),
this is positive. If
S
is not short, it has more left elements and we have
the same result.

Extending the results of this section to semigroups with collisions is not
straightforward since they rely on lower bounds on
s_{h}
and
k
which might
not hold with collisions. Still, we believe that Lemmas
3.5
,

3.9
and
3.11
remain valid, including with more than one
primitive collision.

3.3
Constructing Eliahou semigroups

Based on the results of Section
3.2
, we show how to
explicitly construct Eliahou semigroups.
Many of the examples in this section are taken from the results of our experimentation (see Table
1
).
Fix
h
,
a/b
and
\ell
. The first
step is to pick a
B_{h}
set
\Delta
of cardinality
\ell-1
. A simple choice
is
\{0,1,h+1,h^{2}+h+1,\dotsc\}
, but tighter sets exist if
\ell\geq 5

[
16
]
. We have three regimes.

•

If
b=h
, then for any
\Delta
, the semigroup
S(h,a/h,\Delta,0,m)
is split if
m
is large enough. By Lemma
3.3
,
it satisfies
E<0
if
b^{\prime}\neq 1
.

•

If
b\mid h
, then Eliahou collision-free semigroups may or may not
exist, but if they do, there exist infinitely many by Lemma
3.9
.

•

If
b\nmid h
, then as
\phi_{h}\neq 0
,
\rho=\phi_{h}m+t
and
E=k\ell-qs+\rho
scale with
m
. This shows that only finitely many
Eliahou semigroups can exist.

Semigroups with
\rho=0
, called
special

[
6
]
, only exist if
b=h
.

Previously published examples for
b=h
include Delgado’s semigroups

[
7
]
with
a=2h-1
and
\Delta=\{0,1\}
, Eliahou and Fromentin’s
semigroups
[
10
]
with
h=3
and
a=5
, and Almíron and
Moyano-Fernández’s semigroups
[
1
]
with
h=4
,

a\in\{7,11\}
and
\ell=4
. Examples for

are Bras-Amorós’s
semigroups
[
5
]
, with
h=4
,
a/b=3/2
,
\Delta=\{0,1\}
,

\rho=5
and

odd. These illustrate the dependency in
m\bmod b
:
we have
E_{0}=-6
in this case and the value

is only possible
when

is odd. No example with

is known to our

\hat{S}(4,5/3,\{0,1\})=\langle 19,30,31\rangle_{106}

.

If

, Eliahou collision-free semigroups always exist if

if

3.5

\hat{S}(h,a/b,\Delta)

on

However, the minimal diameter of

is not known if

is large

[

for bounds). We give below two infinite families, one
with

Proposition 3.15
.

If
b
divides
h
,

,

and

Proof 3.16
.

Definition
2.2
and the formulas (

and

are
invariant when

is replaced by

) to compute

4
Experimental search for Eliahou semigroups

4.1
Double elements algorithm

The algorithm we used to search for Eliahou semigroups is a variant of the one
presented in
[
5
]
to explore the numerical semigroup tree, in
which one goes from parent to child by removing a right generator from the
semigroup. Let
L=S\cap[0,c)
be the set of left elements of
S
and
D=L+L
the set of
double elements
. Since the set of right generators
is
[c,c+m)\setminus D
, knowing
D
is enough to know which elements we may
remove.

In the exploration, we consider semigroups of the form
S=\langle m,\Gamma\rangle_{c}
, but without assuming that
S
is canonically defined; the
rank and other parameters are counted according to this value of
c
, not the
real conductor of
S
. Let
S^{\prime}
be a semigroup with conductor
c^{\prime}=c+1
(and
rank
k^{\prime}
, etc.) and agreeing with
S
on
\{0,\dotsc,c-1\}
. There are three
possibilities.

•

The point
c
is a gap of
S^{\prime}
. This is only possible if
c\notin D
.
In this case, we have
L^{\prime}=L
and
r^{\prime}=r-\mathbb{1}_{c+m\in D}
.

•

The point
c
is in
S^{\prime}
but not a generator. This is only possible
if
c\in D
. In this case, we have
L^{\prime}=L\cup\{c\}
and
D^{\prime}=D\cup(c+L^{\prime})
. Since
c\in D
and
c+m\in D^{\prime}
, we have
r^{\prime}=r
.

•

The point
c
is a left generator of
S^{\prime}
. This is also only possible
if
c\notin D
. In this case, we have
L^{\prime}=L\cup\{c\}
and
D^{\prime}=D\cup(c+L^{\prime})
. Since
c\notin D
and
c+m\in D^{\prime}
, we have
r^{\prime}=r-1
.

In essence, we have replaced the usual numerical semigroup tree, which has
arity bounded by
m
, by a unary-binary tree by adding some intermediary
nodes (the semigroups not canonically defined).

To explore this tree, we track the following variables, with
Algorithm
1
showing the procedures to update them:

•

the integers
c_{S}
,
k_{S}
,
\ell_{S}
and
r_{S}

representing
c
,
k
,
\ell
and
r
;

•

the bitfields
\mathcal{L}_{S}
and
\mathcal{D}_{S}
representing
(L-1)\cap\mathbb{N}
and
(D-c)\cap\mathbb{N}
, subsets of
\{0,\dotsc,c-2\}
.

Algorithm 1

Going from
c
to
c+1

{algorithmic}
\Require

0\notin\mathcal{D}_{S}

\Procedure
add_gap
S

\State
\mathcal{D}_{S}\leftarrow\mathcal{D}_{S}-1

\State
c_{S}\leftarrow c_{S}+1

\If
m-1\in\mathcal{D}_{S}

\State
r_{S}\leftarrow r_{S}-1

\EndIf
\EndProcedure
\Statex

{algorithmic}

\Require
0\in\mathcal{D}_{S}

\Procedure
add_non_gen
S

\State
\mathcal{L}_{S}\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{S}\cup\{c_{S}-1\}

\State
\mathcal{D}_{S}\leftarrow(\mathcal{D}_{S}\setminus\{0\}-1)\cup\mathcal{L}_{S}

\State
c_{S}\leftarrow c_{S}+1

\State
k_{S}\leftarrow k_{S}+1

\EndProcedure

{algorithmic}
\Require

0\notin\mathcal{D}_{S}

\Procedure
add_left_gen
S

\State
\mathcal{L}_{S}\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{S}\cup\{c_{S}-1\}

\State
\mathcal{D}_{S}\leftarrow(\mathcal{D}_{S}-1)\cup\mathcal{L}_{S}

\State
c_{S}\leftarrow c_{S}+1

\State
k_{S}\leftarrow k_{S}+1

\State
\ell_{S}\leftarrow\ell_{S}+1

\State
r_{S}\leftarrow r_{S}-1

\EndProcedure

By starting with semigroups of the form
\langle m\rangle_{m+1}
, the
multiplicity
m
is kept constant throughout the exploration. Moreover, we can
easily compute
q
and thus
E=k\ell+qr-c
from these variables.

Finally, assume we are interested in semigroups with
c\leq c_{\max}
. Since
the reason to track
\mathcal{L}_{S}
and
\mathcal{D}_{S}
is to check if
0
and
m-1
are
in
\mathcal{D}_{S}
and
\mathcal{D}_{S}
is shifted by one every time
c
increases, we only
need the lowest
c_{\max}-c+m
bits of
\mathcal{L}_{S}
and
\mathcal{D}_{S}
. It follows
that we only need
(c_{\max}+m-1)/2
bits. Since Eliahou semigroups
satisfy
c>3m
, we need
(2c_{\max}-2)/3
bits. It seems that we lose some
information on the semigroup this way, but as seen below, we can use a trick
to retain full knowledge of it.

4.2
Depth-first traversal and branch pruning

Following
[
14
,
3
,
5
]
, we
perform a depth-first search of the tree, which uses the least amount of
memory. Since the tree grows impractically fast with
c
, we need to prune
branches that we know do not contain Eliahou semigroups. Consider
S=\langle m,\Gamma\rangle_{c}
and
S^{\prime}=\langle m,\Gamma,\gamma_{\ell},\dots,\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}-1}\rangle_{c}
with
\ell^{\prime}>\ell
.
Assume that
c
lies in a given target interval
c_{\min}\leq c\leq c_{\max}
.
Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be the Eliahou numbers of
S
and
S^{\prime}
, respectively.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k_{\min}
be the rank of the semigroup
\langle m,\Gamma\rangle_{c_{\min}}
. If the inequality
(k_{\min}+1)(\ell+1)\geq c_{\max}
holds, then
E^{\prime}\geq 0
.

Proof 4.2
.

The semigroup
S
has at least
k_{\min}
left elements. Adding one left
generator adds at least one to
k
and
\ell
, so the lemma follows
from
E=k\ell+qr-c
.

Conjecture 4.3
.

If
\gamma_{\ell}\geq c+m-2\gamma_{1}
and
E\geq 0
, then
E^{\prime}\geq 0
.

The conjecture is certainly true for
\gamma_{\ell}\geq c+m-\gamma_{1}
, because adding
\gamma_{\ell}
adds at least
1
to
k

and
\ell
and subtracts at most
1
from
r
, and we have
k\geq q
.
If
\gamma_{\ell}\geq c+m-2\gamma_{1}
, adding
\gamma_{\ell}
can
subtract up to
\ell
right generators (since elements of the
form
\gamma_{\ell}+\gamma_{i}+\gamma_{j}
for
i,j\geq 1
are beyond
c+m

and therefore not right generators). It is not true in general that
E^{\prime}\geq E
:
for example,
S=\langle 23,39,40,42\rangle_{77}
has
E=15
but
S^{\prime}=\langle 23,39,40,42,54\rangle_{77}
has
E^{\prime}=14
. We hope that this does
not happen if
E<0
or at least not unless
S
is very large.

Using both these results (assuming that Conjecture
4.3
holds),
we explore the descendants of a semigroup
\langle m,\Gamma\rangle_{c}
in two
stages.

•

First, we build all semigroups
\langle m,\Gamma\rangle_{c^{\prime}}
for
c\leq c^{\prime}\leq c_{\max}
. We output any Eliahou semigroups found when
c^{\prime}\geq c_{\min}

and compute in passing the number
k_{\min}
.

•

Second, only if
(k_{\min}+1)(\ell+1)<c_{\max}
, we perform the
depth-first traversal: for all
c\leq\gamma_{\ell}<c_{\max}+m-2\gamma_{1}
such that
\gamma_{\ell}\notin D
, we recursively explore the
descendants of the semigroup
\langle m,\Gamma,\gamma_{\ell}\rangle_{\gamma_{\ell}+1}
.

To facilitate this algorithm, we start the exploration with semigroups of the
form
\langle m,\gamma_{1}\rangle_{\gamma_{1}+1}
, so the
bound
c_{\max}+m-2\gamma_{1}
remains fixed. This is described formally in
Algorithm
2
. Note that each recursive call is done with a
higher value of
\ell
, so the condition
(k_{\min}+1)(\ell+1)<c_{\max}

keeps the size of the calling stack under
\sqrt{c_{\max}}
. Moreover, the
left generators of
S
consist of the conductors of all semigroups in the
stack, so we can recover full knowledge of
S
by looking into the stack.
Alternatively, one could track the list of left generators of
S
in a
separate variable.

Algorithm 2

Exploring the descendants of a semigroup

{algorithmic}
\Ensure

all Eliahou descendants of
S
with
c_{\min}\leq c\leq c_{\max}

\Procedure
explore
S,c_{\min},c_{\max}

\State
S^{\prime}\leftarrow S

\While
c_{S^{\prime}}<c_{\max}

\If
c_{S^{\prime}}=c_{\min}

\State
k_{\min}\leftarrow k_{S^{\prime}}

\EndIf
\If
0\in\mathcal{D}_{S^{\prime}}

\State
\Call
add_non_gen
S^{\prime}

\Else
\State
\Call
add_gap
S^{\prime}

\If
c_{S^{\prime}}\geq c_{\min}

and

k_{S^{\prime}}\ell_{S^{\prime}}+\lceil c_{S^{\prime}}/m\rceil r_{S^{\prime}}<c_{S^{\prime}}

\State
\Output
S^{\prime}

\EndIf
\EndIf
\EndWhile
\If

(k_{\min}+1)(\ell_{S}+1)<c_{\max}

\While
c_{S}<c_{\max}+m-2\gamma_{1}

\If
0\in\mathcal{D}_{S}

\State
\Call
add_non_gen
S

\Else
\State
S^{\prime}\leftarrow S

\State
\Call
add_left_gen
S^{\prime}

\State
\Call
explore
S^{\prime},c_{\min},c_{\max}

\State
\Call
add_gap
S

\EndIf
\EndWhile
\EndIf
\EndProcedure

After the exploration, we take all Eliahou semigroups found and try adding
left generators greater than or equal to
c+m-2\gamma_{1}
to see if we find any
more Eliahou semigroups. Given their rarity, this takes negligible time.

Unfortunately, the running time is very sensitive to the bound used
for left generators: shifting it by
1
in either direction affects
performance by around 15%. This means that Conjecture
4.3
is
crucial to our exploration.

4.3
Practical implementation

Following again
[
14
]
, we implemented our algorithm by
taking advantage of the computer architecture. We store the sets
\mathcal{L}_{S}

and
\mathcal{D}_{S}
in 256-bit registers (thus, we may theoretically go up to

c_{\max}=386
) and operate on them using instructions in the AVX2 set. The
right rotation of a register, used to compute
\mathcal{D}_{S}-1
, can be
computed in four instructions (
vpermq
,
vpsrlq
,
vpsllq
,

vpor
). The register representing the set
\{c_{S}-1\}
can be computed in
one instruction (
vpsllvd
) from a vector representing
c_{S}
which can be
incremented in one instruction (
vpaddd
).
In addition, we use the fact that
m
is constant to compute
q
without a
division.

We used intervals
[c_{\min},c_{\max}]
of length
8
, which seemed best in
practice and ran the exploration of
\langle m,\gamma_{1}\rangle_{\gamma_{1}+1}

in parallel for all quadruplets
(m,\gamma_{1},c_{\min},c_{\max})
,
with
c_{\max}
up to
320
. This amounts to 92545 parallel tasks, which we
ran on 10 machines of 40 cores each using a simple MPI distributed program.
The computation took less than five hours.

No extra semigroups were found by adding generators greater
than
c+m-2\gamma_{1}
, but it does eventually happen; an example is the
semigroup

\langle 435,684,711,720,723,724,1346\rangle_{1740}

mentioned in Section
3.3
, which has
E=-3
(but still
satisfies Conjecture
4.3
). Thus, if the conjecture is
true, the Wilf conjecture is true up to conductor
320
.

The code and its output are available at the following address:

https://depot.lipn.univ-paris13.fr/bacher/eliahou-semigroups
