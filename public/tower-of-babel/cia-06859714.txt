Further Delays in Deploying New African Peacekeepers

2. ECOMOG has scaled down its demobilization plan to
allow for only three additional peacekeeping battalions, but
continuing financial and security concerns among potential
contributors and logistic planning delays are holding up
deployment; even if a decision is made before the end of the
month, the soonest new troops could arrive would be early
December. Zimbabwe, Uganda, and Tanzania have indicated a
willingness to send contingents to an expanded ECOMOG, and
each plans to dispatch a reconnaissance mission to Monrovia
within the coming week. Tanzania, however, has said that
its troop commitment is not yet firm, and Dar es Salaam will
wait for the assessment of its reconnaissance team before
making a final decision. No other African countries have
offered troops, despite OAU and ECOWAS appeals. Each of the
three potential donors claims it can dispatch its battalion
with a three to four week lead time, but even so it remains
unclear how long it will take for them to be equipped,
trained, and deployed after arriving in Liberia: Moreover,
under current planning guidelines, the peacekeeping
augmentees would be ill-equipped for a peace enforcement
role, despite the deteriorating security conditions in
Liberia. Deployment of these troops will be further ©
complicated by the need to repair Roberts Airfield--the only
facility in Liberia capable of handling large airlift
operations--and repair and demine key roads and bridges.

Status of the New Transition Government

3. The specific linkage in the peace accord that ties
deployment of new African peacekeepers and UN monitors,
demobilization, and installation of a new interim government
together has so far stymied efforts to establish a new
political administration. Although the five-person
executive council has been selected, the installation of the
new interim government, originally scheduled to take place
in early September concurrent with encampment and
disarmament, is likely to continue to slip. Current interim
government president Sawyer plans to stay in office until

disarmament is well under way

Unrealistic Peace Process Timetable

4. The seven-month transition period called for in
the accord, which ends with national elections in February
1994, is already behind schedule, and a timetable of 18 to
24 months probably is more realistic. Even if new
peacekeeping troops are on the ground by yearend, .
demobilization efforts are likely to proceed slowly because
of deep-seated mistrust, logistic and financial problems,
and a lack of command and control within the Liberian
factions. Election preparations, which are not likely to
begin until next spring, will be hampered by supply and
transportation problems and political infighting, and are
unlikely to be completed before late 1994. Even after
elections, it will be difficult for the peacekeepers to
depart Liberia without first training a new police force and
Army to maintain order.
