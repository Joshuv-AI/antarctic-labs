Abstract
. Given an o-minimal structure, we show that every definable (in this structure) mapping that is Lipschitz with respect to the inner metric can be approximated by 𝒞 1 \mathscr{C}^{1} mappings that are Lipschitz with respect to the inner metric with arbitrarily close bounds for the derivative. When the o-minimal structure admits 𝒞 ∞ \mathscr{C}^{\infty} cell decomposition, we show that the approximation can be required to be 𝒞 ∞ \mathscr{C}^{\infty} and we extend this result to outer Lipschitz mappings. The proof involves the construction of partitions of unity with sharp bounds for the derivative, which can be useful for other approximation problems.

1.
Introduction

Approximation theorems play an important role in many areas of analysis, and in the recent years, the development of o-minimal geometry provided us valuable tools to perform analysis on singular spaces.

In
[
F
]
, A. Fisher shows that Lipschitz definable mappings can be approximated by continuously differentiable definable mappings with approximation of the Lipschitz constant. We show in this article an analogous result for inner Lipschitz definable mappings (Theorem
4.3
). This is the class of mappings having bounded first derivative (almost everywhere), which appears naturally when performing analysis on singular varieties. Definable approximations with bounded derivatives are for instance needed to define weight functions to study solutions of PDEs on singular domains (see for example
[
V2
]
).
The proof of this theorem relies on the construction of definable
\mathscr{C}^{1}
partitions of unity with sharp bounds of the first derivative (Theorem
4.2
), which is of its own interest and can be useful to show other density results. It can be seen as an improvement of a result of
[
K-CPV
]
, where
\Lambda_{p}^{0}
regular partitions of unity were constructed. Somehow, we provide
\Lambda_{1}^{0}
-regular partitions of unity but with a constant which is arbitrarily small.

We also derive
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
approximation results.
All the finiteness properties of o-minimal structures that make them very attractive also make them very rigid.
It is well-known for instance that it is impossible to construct
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
definable partitions of unity when the structure is polynomially bounded. In such structures, any
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
function that has a
0
Taylor series at some point must be
0
on the entire connected component of its domain containing that point, which makes
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
approximation problems much more challenging in this framework.
We strengthen Fisher’s result by showing that it is indeed possible to approximate definable Lipschitz mappings by
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
definable Lipschitz mappings (Theorem
4.6
) in any o-minimal structure admitting
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
cell decomposition.
Moreover we give analogous results for inner Lipschitz mappings (Corollary
4.4
).

We denote by
|.|
the euclidean norm. Given a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
d(x,A)
for the euclidean distance from a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
A
, and
\partial A
for the topological boundary of
A
, i.e.
\partial A:=\overline{A}\setminus A
.
{\bf B}(x,r)
stands for the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x
.

2.
Framework

We recall a few basic facts and definitions about o-minimal structures.
We refer to
[
C
,
D
]
for more.
In the sequel, although
[
V1
]
is only devoted to the subanalytic category, we will also sometimes refer to this survey for results whose proofs go over the framework of o-minimal structures.

2.1.
O-minimal structures.

A
structure
(expanding
(\mathbb{R},+,.)
) is a family
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for each
n
the following properties hold

(1)

\mathcal{D}_{n}
is a Boolean algebra of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2)

If
A\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
and
B\in\mathcal{D}_{k}
then
A\times B
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{n+k}
.

(3)

\mathcal{D}_{n}
contains
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:P(x)=0\}
, where
P\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
.

(4)

If
A\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
then
\pi(A)
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{n-1}
, where
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is the standard projection onto the first
(n-1)
coordinates.

Such a family
\mathcal{D}
is said to be
o-minimal
if in addition:

(5)

Any set
A\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a finite union of intervals and points.

A set belonging to the structure
\mathcal{D}
is called a
definable set
and a map whose graph is in the structure
\mathcal{D}
is called a
definable map
.

A structure
\mathcal{D}
is said to be
polynomially bounded
if for each definable function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, there exist a positive number
a
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|f(x)|<x^{p}
for all
x>a
.

Examples of polynomially bounded o-minimal structures are the semi-algebraic sets and the globally subanalytic sets, but also the so-called
x^{\lambda}
-sets as well as the structures defined by convenient quasi-analytic Denjoy-Carleman classes of functions
[
RSW
]
.

We say that an o-minimal structure
admits
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
cell decomposition
if for each definable function
f
on an open subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is a definable open dense subset of
V
of
U
on which
f
is
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
.
All the just above mentioned examples of polynomially bounded o-minimal structures admit
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
cell decomposition.

Throughout this article,
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
will stand for a fixed polynomially bounded o-minimal structure (expanding
(\mathbb{R},+,.)
). The term “definable” will refer to this structure.

Given
A\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
and
B\in\mathcal{D}_{k}
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}(A,B)
the set of definable mappings
\xi:A\to B
and by
\mathcal{D}(A)
the set of definable functions
\xi:A\to\mathbb{R}
, whereas
\mathcal{D}^{+}(A)
will stand for the set of positive definable continuous functions on
A
.

Given
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, we write
\mathcal{D}^{m}(A)
(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{m}(A,B)
) for the elements of
\mathcal{D}(A)
(resp.
\mathcal{D}(A,B)
) that are
\mathscr{C}^{m}
(a function will be said to be
\mathscr{C}^{m}
on
A
if it extends to a
\mathscr{C}^{m}
function on an open neighborhood of
A
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
).

Given a mapping
f:A\to B
, we denote by
|f|
the function that assigns to
x\in A
the number
|f(x)|
. When
f
is differentiable,
we will write
|df|
for the function defined by
x\mapsto|d_{x}f|
, where
|d_{x}f|:=\sup_{|u|=1}|d_{x}f(u)|
. We then set

|f|_{1}:=|f|+|df|.

Recently
[
VV
, Theorem
4.8
]
, it was proved that if
\mathcal{D}
admits
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
cell decomposition and if
M
is a
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
definable submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
then
for each
f\in\mathcal{D}^{1}(M)
and
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(M)
there is
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\infty}(M)
such that on
M

|f-g|_{1}<\varepsilon.

This result was proved earlier in the semi-algebraic structure by M. Shiota
[
S
]
. The first deep insight in this direction was indeed achieved by G. Efroymson who showed that there is a
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
semi-algebraic function
g
such that
|f-g|<\varepsilon

[
BCR
, Theorem
8.8.4
]
.

If
S
is a definable
\mathscr{C}^{k}
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
k\geq 2
possibly infinite) then there exist a definable open neighborhood
U_{S}
of
S
and a definable
\mathscr{C}^{k-1}

retraction
\pi_{S}:U_{S}\to S
such that for all
x\in U_{S}
we have

d(x,S)=|x-\pi_{S}(x)|.

If
\dim S=n
then
U_{S}=S
and
\pi_{S}(x)=x
for all
x
. Moreover, for
x\in U_{S}\setminus S
we have

\nabla_{x}d(\cdot,S)=\frac{x-\pi_{S}(x)}{|x-\pi_{S}(x)|},

which is always orthogonal to

T_{\pi_{S}(x)}S
(see
[
V1
, Proposition 2.4.1]
).

Given
\delta\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(S)
, we then let

(2.1)

U_{S}^{\delta}:=\{x\in U_{S}:d(x,S)<\delta(\pi_{S}(x))\}.

2.2.
Stratifications

We define the
angle
between two given vector subspaces
E
and
F
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as:

\angle(E,F):=\underset{u\in E,|u|\leq 1}{\sup}d(u,F).

Definition 2.1
.

A
\mathscr{C}^{k}

stratification of
a definable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a finite partition of it into
definable
\mathscr{C}^{k}
submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, called
strata
. A
refinement
of a stratification
\mathcal{S}
is a stratification
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
such that every
S\in\mathcal{S}
is a union of strata of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

Let
S
and
Y
be a couple of strata (of some stratification) and let
z\in S\cap\overline{Y}
.
We will say that
(Y,S)
satisfies the
(w)
condition at
z
(of Kuo-Verdier) if there exists a constant
C
such that for
y\in Y
and
x\in S
in a neighborhood of
z
:

(2.2)

\angle(T_{x}S,T_{y}Y)\leq C|x-y|.

A stratification
\mathcal{S}
is said to be
(w)
-regular
if every couple of strata
(Y,S)
of
\mathcal{S}
satisfies the
(w)
condition at every point of
S\cap\overline{Y}
.
We say that a stratification
\mathcal{S}
of a set
A

satisfies the frontier condition
if the closure in
A
of every
S\in\mathcal{S}
is a union of strata of
\mathcal{S}
. Equivalently, given
S
and
Y
in
\mathcal{S}
, either
\overline{Y}\cap S=\emptyset
or
S\subset\overline{Y}
. When strata are connected (which can always be obtained up to a refinement) and
A
is locally closed, the
(w)
condition entails the frontier condition (see
[
Ve
]
or
[
V1
, Propositions
2.6.3
and
2.6.17
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a couple of manifolds
(Y,S)
satisfies
Whitney’s
(b)
condition
at
x\in S
,
or is
(b)
-regular
at
x
, if for all sequences
x_{i}\in S
and
y_{i}\in Y
with limit
x

such that
T_{y_{i}}Y
tends to
\tau
and the lines
\overline{x_{i}y_{i}}
tend to
\lambda
, one has
\lambda\subset\tau
.
When every pair of strata of a stratification is
(b)
-regular (at each point)
then we say that the stratification is
(b)
-regular
.

It was shown by Ta Le Loi
[
L
]
that every definable set admits a
(w)
-regular stratification and that Kuo-Verdier
(w)
condition implies Whitney’s
(b)
condition.

Remark 2.3
.

If
M
is a definable submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
{x_{0}}\in\overline{M}\setminus M
then
(M,\{{x_{0}}\})
is
(b)
-regular. Indeed, if Whitney’s
(b)
condition failed, it easily follows from curve selection lemma that it would fail along a definable arc
\gamma
in
M
tending to
{x_{0}}
. But since
\gamma
is definable, the angle between
(\gamma(t)-{x_{0}})
and
\gamma^{\prime}(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}M
must tend to
0
, which is a contradiction.

We now recall the notion of horizontally
\mathscr{C}^{1}
stratified mapping due to C. Murolo and D. Trotman
[
MT
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
f:A\to B
be a definable mapping. We say that
f:(A,\mathcal{S})\to(B,\mathcal{T})
is a
stratified mapping
if
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are stratifications of
A
and
B
respectively such that every stratum
S\in\mathcal{S}
is mapped by
f
into an element
T
of
\mathcal{T}
and the restricted mapping
f_{|S}:S\to T
is a
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
submersion.

Given a definable mapping
f:A\to B
, it is possible to construct stratifications
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
of
A
and
B
respectively, making
f
into a stratified mapping. These stratifications may satisfy the
(w)
condition, or more generally any condition for which we can stratify definable sets (see
[
V1
, Proposition 2.6.10]
).

A stratified mapping
f:(A,\mathcal{S})\to(B,\mathcal{T})
is said to be
horizontally

\mathscr{C}^{1}
if for any sequence

(x_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
in a stratum
Y
of
\mathcal{S}
tending to some
point
x
in a stratum
S\in\mathcal{S}
and for any sequence
u_{l}\in T_{x_{l}}Y
tending to a vector
u
in
T_{x}S
, we have

\lim d_{x_{l}}f_{|Y}(u_{l})=d_{x}f_{|S}(u).

Remark 2.5
.

Since we can choose a sequence
u_{l}
that tends to
0
, horizontally
\mathscr{C}^{1}
mappings must have locally bounded derivative. Moreover, when the
(w)
condition holds, the required continuity condition in the above definition entails that every stratum
S
has for each positive number
\mu
a definable neighborhood
U
in
A
on which

(2.3)

|d_{\pi_{S}(x)}f_{|S}|\leq|d_{x}f_{|Y}|+\mu,

where
Y
is the stratum that contains
x
. Conversely,
given a mapping that has locally bounded derivative, it is not difficult to see that there is a stratification of
f
with respect to which
f
is horizontally
\mathscr{C}^{1}
. This stratification is indeed provided by a
(w)
-regular stratification of the restriction of the canonical projection
A\times B\to B
to the graph of
f
. The corresponding stratifications of
A
and
B
can be required to satisfy the
(w)
condition (see
[
V1
, Proposition
2.6.13
]
for a construction of regular horizontally
\mathscr{C}^{1}
stratifications).

2.3.
Rugose vector fields

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
(w)
-regular stratification of a definable set
A
and let
S\in\mathcal{S}
as well as
{x_{0}}\in S
. It was established by Verdier
[
Ve
]
that when the
(w)
condition holds, each germ of smooth tangent vector field on
S
near
{x_{0}}
, say
v:U\cap S\to TS
, has an extension
\tilde{v}:U\cap A\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, tangent to the strata (i.e.
v(x)\in T_{x}Y
for all
x\in U\cap Y
,
Y\in\mathcal{S}
), for which there exists a constant
C
(depending on
x_{0}
) such that for all
x\in A
and
y\in S
in the vicinity of
{x_{0}}
:

(2.4)

|\tilde{v}(x)-\tilde{v}(y)|\leq C|x-y|.

Vector fields satisfying such an inequality are called
rugose

[
Ve
]
. This extension property is actually equivalent to the
(w)
condition, as established by Brodersen and Trotman
[
BT
]
.
For more details on stratifications and rugose vector fields we refer to the David Trotman’s survey
[
T
]
and references therein.

3.
Inner Lipschitz mappings

If
x
and
y
are two points of the same connected component of a definable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

\mathring{d}_{A}(x,y):=\inf\{l(\gamma)\,:\,\gamma\in\mathcal{D}^{0}([0,1],A),\gamma(0)=x,\gamma(1)=y\},

where
l(\gamma)
denotes the length of an arc
\gamma
(defined as
\sup\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}|\gamma(t_{i})-\gamma(t_{i+1})|
, where the supremum is taken over all the partitions
0=t_{0}<\dots<t_{k}=1
). When
x
and
y
do not belong to the same connected component, we put
\mathring{d}_{A}(x,y):=+\infty
. We call
\mathring{d}_{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
, the
inner metric on
A
.

The definition of the inner metric restricts itself to definable arcs because we need a bit of regularity in order to evaluate the length of arcs (definable arcs are piecewise
\mathscr{C}^{1}
), and, as well-known, connected definable arcs are definably path connected. The set
A
being possibly singular, we are not sure that there is always a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
arc connecting points. Lemma
3.3
will establish that
\mathscr{C}^{0}
arcs would give the same metric (see (
3.9
)).

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a mapping
f:A\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is
inner Lipschitz
if there exists a constant
C
such that the following inequality holds for any
x,y\in A

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C\mathring{d}_{A}(x,y).

The smallest such
C
is then called
the inner Lipschitz constant of
f
.

Given a mapping
f:A\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
{x_{0}}\in A
, we set

\mathring{L}_{f}(x_{0}):=\inf_{r>0}\{C\,:\,|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C\mathring{d}_{A}(x,y),\,\mbox{\rm for all}\,x,y\in{\bf B}(x_{0},r)\cap A\}.

It directly follows from the definitions that
f
is inner Lipschitz if and only if
\mathring{L}_{f}
is a bounded function on
A
, and that in this case the inner Lipschitz constant of
f
is equal to the supremum of
\mathring{L}_{f}
.

We stress the fact that the inner distance is not necessarily definable. The lemma below however establishes that
\mathring{L}_{f}
is a definable function.
In this lemma, we consider
d_{x}f
although
f
is not assumed to be differentiable. This is because definable mappings are differentiable on a definable dense subset of their domain.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable set and
f\in\mathcal{D}^{0}(A,\mathbb{R}^{k})
. For all
{x_{0}}\in A
, we have

(3.5)

\mathring{L}_{f}(x_{0})=\inf_{r>0}\sup\{|d_{x}f|\,:\,x\in{\bf B}(x_{0},r)\cap A\}.

Proof.

As
|d_{x}f|
cannot be greater than the inner Lipschitz constant, the right-hand-side cannot be greater than the left-hand-side. It thus suffices to show the reversed inequality.

Take
(w)
-regular stratifications
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
of
A
and
\mathbb{R}^{k}
respectively that make of
f
a horizontally
\mathscr{C}^{1}
mapping, and
assume that
|d_{x}f|\leq L
on
{\bf B}(x_{0},r)\cap A
for some
r
.

Let us show that there exists
\tilde{r}\leq r
such that
f
is
L
-Lipschitz with respect to the inner metric on
{\bf B}(x_{0},\tilde{r})\cap A
.
By (
2.3
), we see that

(3.6)

\sup_{x\in{\bf B}(x_{0},r)\cap S}|d_{x}f_{|S}|\leq L,

for every stratum
S\in\mathcal{S}
meeting this set. As
\mathring{d}_{A}(a,{x_{0}})
tends to
0
as
|a-{x_{0}}|
tends to
0
(see for instance
[
KP
]
or
[
V1
, Proposition
3.2.5
]
, this also directly follows from the next lemma), there is

\tilde{r}\leq\frac{r}{4}
such that for all
a
in

{\bf B}(x_{0},\tilde{r})\cap A

(3.7)

\mathring{d}_{A}(a,{x_{0}})<\frac{r}{4}.

By definition of the inner metric, given
a,b\in A
there is for each
\mu>0
a
\mathscr{C}^{0}
definable arc
\gamma:[0,1]\to A
of length not greater than
\mathring{d}_{A}(a,b)+\mu
connecting
a
and
b
. For
\mu<\frac{r}{4}
and
a
as well as
b
in
{\bf B}(x_{0},\tilde{r})\cap A
, we then have

l(\gamma)\leq\mathring{d}_{A}(a,b)+\mu\leq\mathring{d}_{A}(a,{x_{0}})+\mathring{d}_{A}(b,{x_{0}})+\frac{r}{4}\overset{(\ref{eq_d_axo})}{<}\frac{3r}{4},

which means that such an arc
\gamma
does not leave
{\bf B}({x_{0}},r)\cap A
(since
\gamma(0)\in{\bf B}({x_{0}},\frac{r}{4})
).
Let then
t_{0}=0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{k}=1
be such that
\gamma
stays in the same stratum on
(t_{i},t_{i+1})
for all
i<k
. By (
3.6
) and the Mean Value Theorem, we have for every
i<k
:

|f(\gamma(t_{i}))-f(\gamma(t_{i+1}))|\leq Ll(\gamma_{|[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}),

which shows that
|f(a)-f(b)|
is not bigger than
Ll(\gamma)
. Hence,
|f(a)-f(b)|\leq L\mathring{d}_{A}(a,b)
, as claimed.
∎

The inner distance was defined by taking the infimum among all the
definable
continuous arcs. The proof of Lemma
3.5
will however involve
integration of vector fields, which may generate non definable arcs. We thus need to check that the infimum among all the continuous arcs is not smaller, for which the following lemma will be needed (see (
3.10
)).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable set and
\mu>0
. Every
x_{0}\in A
has a neighborhood
U
in
A
such that each
x\in U
can be connected to
{x_{0}}
by a
\mathscr{C}^{0}
definable arc in
A
of length not greater than
(1+\mu)|x-x_{0}|.

Proof.

We will assume
{x_{0}}=0
and will argue by induction on
n
, the case
n=1
being obvious. Note first that, if
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
are definable sets for which the lemma holds, then the lemma holds for their union. In particular, since
A
can be covered by finitely many sets that are Lipschitz cells after a possible orthonormal change of coordinates that we may identify with the identity (see for instance
[
KP
]
or
[
V1
, Proposition
3.2.5
]
), it is enough to show the result for
A
of type
E\cup\{0\}
, where
E
is a Lipschitz cell satisfying
0\in\overline{E}
.

It suffices to construct a definable mapping
h:U\times[0,1]\to U
, with
U
neighborhood of
0
in
A
, such that

(1)

h(x,0)=0
and
h(x,1)=x

(2)

h(x,t)
is
\mathscr{C}^{1}
with respect to
t
and the angle between the vectors
\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(x,t)\neq 0
and
h(x,t)
tends to zero as
x\to 0
(uniformly in
t\in(0,1]
).

We denote by
h
the mapping obtained by applying induction hypothesis to
\pi(A)
,
where
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is the projection omitting the last coordinate.
We start with the case where
E
is the graph of a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
Lipschitz function
\xi:\pi(E)\to\mathbb{R}
. In this case, we claim that

\tilde{h}(x,t):=(h(x,t),\xi(h(x,t)))

has the required properties. To see this,
let for simplicity
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
respectively denote the vectors
\tilde{h}(x,t)
and
\frac{\partial\tilde{h}}{\partial t}(x,t)
divided by the norms of their respective projections onto
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Denote by
w(x,t)
the vector realizing the distance from

By induction

. As

We now have to address the case where

s\xi_{1}(\pi(x))+(1-s)\xi_{2}(\pi(x)),\;\;s\in(0,1),

we set

To check that it has the required property, set for simplicity for

and

u_{i}(x,t):=\frac{\tilde{h}(x,\xi_{i}(x),t)}{|h(x,t)|}\quad\mbox{and}\quad v_{i}(x,t):=\frac{\frac{\partial\tilde{h}}{\partial t}(x,\xi_{i}(x),t)}{|\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(x,t)|}.

The vectors

tends to zero,

and

(resp.

(resp. to

Thanks to this lemma, we can show that the inner metric could be defined with non necessarily definable arcs, i.e., that given a definable set

(3.9)

\mathring{d}_{A}(x,y)=\inf\{l(\gamma):\gamma\in\mathscr{C}^{0}([0,1],A)\mbox{ joining $x$ and $y$}\}.

If we denote by

\mathring{d}_{A}(x,y)\leq(1+\mu)\tilde{d}_{A}(x,y).

Take for this purpose an arc

and

as well as
\mu>0
. Apply the just above lemma to the point

of

There is a finite partition

such that

The

\mathring{d}_{A}(x,y)\leq l(\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}l(\alpha_{i})\overset{(\ref{eq_alpha_i})}{\leq}(1+\mu)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}|\gamma(t_{i})-\gamma(t_{i+1})|\leq(1+\mu)l(\gamma),

).

Lemma 3.4
.

For all vector subspaces
E,F
, and
T
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

we have

(3.12)

\angle(E\cap T^{\perp},F\cap T^{\perp})\leq 2\;\frac{\angle(E,F)}{\inf_{v\in T,|v|=1}d(v,F^{\perp})}.

Proof.

We denote by
\pi_{V}
the orthogonal projection onto a space
V
. Take
a unit vector
u
in
E\cap T^{\perp}
and let us show that
d(u,F\cap T^{\perp})
is not greater than the right-hand-side of (
3.12
).

If
\pi_{F}(u)=\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)
then
d(u,F\cap T^{\perp})=d(u,F)\leq\angle(E,F)
and the desired fact is clear. Otherwise, as the vector
\pi_{F}(u)-\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)
is the projection of
\pi_{F}(u)
onto the orthogonal complement of
F\cap T^{\perp}
in
F
, which is nothing but
\pi_{F}(T)
, we have,

(3.13)

|\pi_{F}(u)-\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)|=\langle\frac{\pi_{F}(u)-\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)}{|\pi_{F}(u)-\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)|},\pi_{F}(u)\rangle,

and there is a unit vector
v
in
T
such that

(3.14)

\frac{\pi_{F}(v)}{|\pi_{F}(v)|}=\frac{\pi_{F}(u)-\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)}{|\pi_{F}(u)-\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)|}.

Write now

(3.15)

\displaystyle|u-\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|u-\pi_{F}(u)|+|\pi_{F}(u)-\pi_{F\cap T^{\perp}}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\angle(E,F)+|\langle\frac{\pi_{F}(v)}{|\pi_{F}(v)|},\pi_{F}(u)\rangle|,

by the definition of
\angle(E,F)
as well as (
3.13
) and (
3.14
).
Observe now that

|\langle\frac{\pi_{F}(v)}{|\pi_{F}(v)|},\pi_{F}(u)\rangle|=|\langle\frac{v}{|\pi_{F}(v)|},\pi_{F}(u)\rangle|=\frac{|\langle v,u-\pi_{F}(u)\rangle|}{|\pi_{F}(v)|}\leq\frac{\angle(E,F)}{d(v,F^{\perp})},

because

and
|\pi_{F}(v)|=d(v,F^{\perp})
. Plugging the just above inequality into (
3.15
) yields the desired fact.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
(w)
-regular stratification of a locally closed definable set

. For every

and every

there is

such that for all

(3.16)

\mathring{d}_{A}(x,\pi_{S}(x))\leq(1+\mu)d(x,S).

Proof.

Let
\mu>0
and
S\in\mathcal{S}
.
We first construct a function
\delta\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(S)
and a rugose vector field

tangent to the strata (i.e.

for all

, for all

) such that for all

\;\;\langle\nabla_{x}d(\cdot,S),\eta(x)\rangle=1.

(2)

\;\;|\eta(x)|\leq 1+\mu
.

(3)

\;\;d_{x}\pi_{S}(\eta(x))=0

denote the set of all the strata of dimension less than or equal to

. Let

be the minimal integer such that

. We will define

on the elements of

, by induction on

For

, we set for

where

is the orthogonal projection onto

Recall that

and

. Since the

tends to

. By Definable Choice
[

, Theorem 3.1]
, we thus see that there is

such that on

(3.17)

|P_{x}(\nabla_{x}d(\cdot,S))-\nabla_{x}d(\cdot,S)|<\mu.

which yields (
2

) holds by construction. If we divide

we see that the right-hand-side of (

) is only multiplied by a constant close to

by choosing

small enough. This completes the case

.

Take

and
let
\eta
be the vector field given by the induction hypothesis. Denote by

its extension to a rugose vector field tangent to the strata and put

\eta^{\prime}(x):=\frac{P_{x}(\tilde{\eta}(x))}{\langle\nabla_{x}d(\cdot,S),P_{x}(\tilde{\eta}(x))\rangle},

with

as above.
We first check that

is rugose at every

) we derive near

\angle(T_{y}Y\cap T_{\pi_{S}(y)}S^{\perp},T_{y}Y\cap T_{\pi_{S}(x)}S^{\perp})\leq 4\angle(T_{\pi_{S}(y)}S^{\perp},T_{\pi_{S}(x)}S^{\perp})\leq C|y-x|,

for some constant

. Moreover, (

\displaystyle|(P_{x}-Id)_{|T_{y}Y\cap T_{\pi_{S}(y)}S^{\perp}}|=\angle(T_{y}Y\cap T_{\pi_{S}(y)}S^{\perp},T_{x}Y^{\prime}\cap T_{\pi_{S}(x)}S^{\perp})

4.
Approximations and partitions of unity

We will prove simultaneously the two main results of this article (Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
). Constructing partitions of unity will require bump functions, for which the following lemma will be needed.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
\mu>0
, we can find a definable
\mathscr{C}^{1}
function
\psi_{\mu}:[0,+\infty)\times(0,1)\to[0,1]

such that

(1)

\psi_{\mu}(y,z)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{\rm for}\;0\leq y\leq b(z),\quad\mbox{\rm where }b(z)\in(0,z),\\
0&\mbox{\rm for}\;y\geq z,\end{cases}

(2)

\left|\frac{\partial\psi_{\mu}}{\partial y}(y,z)\right|\leq\frac{\mu}{y}
,
for

y>0
,

(3)

\left|\frac{\partial\psi_{\mu}}{\partial z}(y,z)\right|\leq 4+\frac{\mu}{z}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a positive rational number (as it suffices to prove this lemma for arbitrarily small values of
\mu
, we may assume
\mu
to be rational). For
z\in(0,1)
and
y\in(0,z]
, we put

\phi_{\mu}(y,z)=(1+z)\left(1-\left(\frac{y}{z}\right)^{\mu}\right).

We first estimate the partial derivatives of
\phi_{\mu}
. For every
0<y\leq z
, we clearly have

(4.20)

\left|\frac{\partial\phi_{\mu}}{\partial y}(y,z)\right|=(1+z)\frac{\mu}{y}\left(\frac{y}{z}\right)^{\mu}\leq\frac{2\mu}{y}

as well as

(4.21)

\left|\frac{\partial\phi_{\mu}}{\partial z}(y,z)\right|=\left|1-\left(\frac{y}{z}\right)^{\mu}+\frac{(1+z)\mu y^{\mu}}{z^{\mu+1}}\right|\leq 1+\frac{(1+z)\mu}{z}.

Notice that
\phi_{\mu}(z,z)=0
and
\phi_{\mu}(b(z),z)=1
, where
b(z):=\frac{z^{\frac{1}{\mu}+1}}{(1+z)^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}
. As a matter of fact, the function

\psi_{\mu}(y,z):=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $0\leq y<b(z)$}\\
1-(1-\phi_{\mu}(y,z)^{2})^{2}&\mbox{if $b(z)\leq y\leq z$}\\
0&\mbox{if $y>z$}\\
\end{cases}

is
\mathscr{C}^{1}
on
[0,+\infty)\times(0,1)
. Since
\mu
is rational,
\psi_{\mu}
is definable. As, for
y\in(b(z),z)
,

d_{(y,z)}\psi_{\mu}=4\phi_{\mu}(y,z)(1-\phi_{\mu}(y,z)^{2})d_{(y,z)}\phi_{\mu}(y,z),

(
4.20
) and (
4.21
) yield

\left|\frac{\partial\psi_{\mu}}{\partial y}(y,z)\right|\leq\frac{8\mu}{y}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left|\frac{\partial\psi_{\mu}}{\partial z}(y,z)\right|\leq 4+\frac{4(1+z)\mu}{z}\leq 4+\frac{8\mu}{z},

which is satisfying.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

be a locally closed definable set and

,

(ii)

|d_{x}g|\leq\mathring{L}_{f}(x)+\mu

Proof.

Fix

. We prove these two theorems simultaneously by induction on

. Both statements being obvious for

, take

and denote by

and

the respective induction hypotheses of Theorems

and

We first point out a useful consequence of

be a manifold of dimension smaller than

.
By

definable function

on

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{S}
(we recall that
U^{\delta}_{S}=S
for all
\delta
in this case). The above estimate obviously continues to hold.

We now turn to define the desired partition of unity. For
l\leq d
we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{l}
the collection of the strata of
\mathcal{S}
of dimension
\leq l
, and set
X_{l}:=\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}}S
.
We will define
\varphi_{S}
inductively on
\dim S
. Namely, given
l\leq d
,
we will construct
\mathscr{C}^{1}
functions
\varphi_{S}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\partial A\to[0,1]
,
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}
, satisfying

(4.25)

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}}\varphi_{S}(x)\leq 1\mbox{ for all }x\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}}\varphi_{S}(x)=1\mbox{ on a neighborhood of $X_{l}$},

with
\mbox{\rm supp}\,\varphi_{S}\subset U_{S}
for each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}
, as well as for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(\partial A\cup\overline{S})
:

(4.26)

|d_{x}\varphi_{S}|\leq\frac{\mu}{d(x,S)}.

The desired partition of unity is given by the case
l=d
.

If
S
is a
0
-dimensional stratum, just set

\varphi_{S}(x):=\psi_{S}^{\delta}(x),

which for
\delta\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(S)
small enough, has all the required properties. Let us thus now define the functions
\varphi_{S}
,
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
, assuming all the
\varphi_{Y}
,
Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
, to have already been defined.

For
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
and
\delta_{S}:S\to\mathbb{R}
small enough positive
\mathscr{C}^{1}
definable function,
set

\varphi_{S}:=\psi_{S}^{\delta_{S}}\cdot(1-\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}}\varphi_{Y}).

Thanks to the frontier condition, we may assume the
\delta_{S}
to be sufficiently small for the
U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}
,
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
, to be disjoint from each other. As a matter of fact, for every
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
, on
U_{S}^{b\circ\delta_{S}}
we have
\psi_{S}^{\delta_{S}}=1
and
\psi_{S^{\prime}}^{\delta_{S^{\prime}}}=0
if
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
is different from
S
. Hence, on
U_{S}^{b\circ\delta_{S}}
we get

\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l}}\varphi_{Y}=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}}\varphi_{Y}+\varphi_{S}=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}}\varphi_{Y}+(1-\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}}\varphi_{Y})=1.

Moreover, as by induction
\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}}\varphi_{Y}=1
in the vicinity of
X_{l-1}
, we have
\varphi_{S}=0
near this set for each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
, so that near
X_{l-1}
we have

\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l}}\varphi_{Y}=\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{S}_{l-1}}\varphi_{Y}=1.

Furthermore, since
0\leq\psi_{S}^{\delta_{S}}\leq 1
,
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}}\varphi_{S}
cannot exceed
1
, yielding (
4.25
).

To check (
4.26
), observe that if the
\delta_{S}
are sufficiently small to have
d(x,S)\leq d(x,Y)
on
U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}
when
Y
is a stratum comprised in the boundary of a stratum
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
, then the induction assumption entails for
x\in U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}\setminus\overline{S}

|d_{x}\varphi_{Y}|\leq\frac{\mu}{d(x,Y)}\leq\frac{\mu}{d(x,S)}.

Thanks to the above observation, we may require
|d_{x}\delta_{S}|<1
,
so that (
4.24
) entails, together with the just above inequality and the definition of
\varphi_{S}
, for
x\in U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}\setminus\overline{S}
,
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l-1}
,

|d_{x}\varphi_{S}|\leq\frac{\kappa\mu}{d(x,S)}.

for some constant
\kappa
independent of
\mu
, yielding (
4.26
) (
\varphi_{S}
being supported in
U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}
, this inequality continues to hold outside this set).
This completes our induction on
l\leq d
, giving the desired partition of unity and completing the induction step of the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

We now perform the induction step of Theorem
4.3
. Fix
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(A)
. Let
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be
\mathscr{C}^{2}

(w)
-regular stratifications of
A
and
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with respect to which
f
is a horizontally
\mathscr{C}^{1}
stratified mapping. Choose for each
S\in\mathcal{S}
a sufficiently small neighborhood
U_{S}
of
S
for the closest point retraction
\pi_{S}:U_{S}\to S
to be well-defined and
\mathscr{C}^{1}
. Let for
S\in\mathcal{S}

g_{S}(x):=f(\pi_{S}(x)),\quad x\in U_{S}.

Note that, as
f
is continuous, it follows from Definable Choice that if
\delta_{S}\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(S)
is sufficiently small then
|f-g_{S}|<\varepsilon
on
U^{\delta_{S}}_{S}
. As a matter of fact, if we set
g:=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\varphi_{S}g_{S}
, where
(\varphi_{S})_{S\in\mathcal{S}}
is a partition of unity subordinate to the covering
(U_{S}^{\delta_{S}})_{S\in\mathcal{S}}
as given by Theorem
4.2
, then

|f-g|=|\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\varphi_{S}(f-g_{S})|<\varepsilon.

It thus only remains to check that
g
satisfies
(ii)
for small enough functions
\delta_{S}
. For this purpose, fix
\mu>0
and denote by
P_{x}
the orthogonal projection onto
T_{x}S
, for
x\in S\in\mathcal{S}
. Note that since for each
S\in\mathcal{S}
,
d_{x}\pi_{S}=P_{x}
for every
x\in S
, it follows from Definable Choice that for
\delta_{S}
small enough we have for
x\in U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}

(4.27)

|d_{x}\pi_{S}-P_{\pi_{S}(x)}|<\mu.

Set for simplicity
L=\sup_{A}\mathring{L}_{f}
.
Notice also that by the definitions of
g_{S}
and
\mathring{L}_{f}
we have for each
S\in\mathcal{S}
(for
\delta_{S}
small enough) for
x\in U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}

(4.28)

|f(x)-g_{S}(x)|\leq L\cdot\mathring{d}_{A}(x,\pi_{S}(x))\overset{(\ref{eq_lem_strate_inner})}{\leq}L(1+\mu)\cdot d(x,S).

Write now

(4.29)

|d_{x}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}g_{S}\varphi_{S}|\leq|\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}g_{S}(x)d_{x}\varphi_{S}|+|\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\varphi_{S}(x)d_{x}g_{S}|.

Since
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}d_{x}\varphi_{S}=0
on
A
, the first term of the right-hand-side can be estimated as

(4.30)

|\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}g_{S}(x)d_{x}\varphi_{S}|=|\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}(g_{S}-f)(x)d_{x}\varphi_{S}|\overset{(\ref{eq_fmoinsg})}{\leq}L(1+\mu)\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}d(x,S)|d_{x}\varphi_{S}|\overset{(\ref{eq_derpsi_thm})}{\leq}L(1+\mu)\kappa\mu,

where
\kappa
stands for the number of strata of
\mathcal{S}
.
To estimate the second one, take
S\in\mathcal{S}
and write for
x\in U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}

|d_{x}g_{S}|=|d_{\pi_{S}(x)}f\,d_{x}\pi_{S}|\overset{(\ref{eq_d_x_pi})}{\leq}|d_{\pi_{S}(x)}f_{|S}\,P_{\pi_{S}(x)}|+|d_{\pi_{S}(x)}f_{|S}|\mu\leq|d_{\pi_{S}(x)}f_{|S}|(1+\mu).

Hence, for
x\in U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}
(with
\delta_{S}
small enough), denoting by
Y
the stratum that contains
x
, we get after applying (
2.3
) and multiplying by
\varphi_{S}

(4.31)

|\varphi_{S}(x)d_{x}g_{S}|\leq\varphi_{S}(x)(|d_{x}f_{|Y}|+\mu)(1+\mu)\leq\varphi_{S}(x)(\mathring{L}_{f}(x)+\mu)(1+\mu).

If
x\notin U_{S}^{\delta_{S}}
then
\varphi_{S}(x)=0
and this inequality is still valid.
Hence, plugging (
4.30
) and (
4.31
) into (
4.29
), we get

|d_{x}g|\leq L(1+\mu)\kappa\mu+(\mathring{L}_{f}(x)+\mu)(1+\mu)\leq\mathring{L}_{f}(x)+C\mu,

for some constant
C
independent of
\mu
.
∎

Thanks to the theorem proved in
[
VV
]
, we get in addition:

Corollary 4.4
.

If the o-minimal structure admits
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
cell decomposition, then the approximation provided by Theorem
4.3
can be required to be
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(A)
and
\mu>0
, and denote by
g
the mapping provided by Theorem
4.3
.
By
[

, Theorem 4.8]
, there exists
\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{D}^{\infty}(A,\mathbb{R}^{k})
such that
|\tilde{g}-g|_{1}\leq\min(\varepsilon,\mu)
. Clearly,

∎

4.1.
Approximation of Lipschitz definable mappings

Given a Lipschitz mapping
f:A\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we denote by
Lip(f)
its Lipschitz constant, i.e.

Lip(f)=\inf\{L\in\mathbb{R}:\forall\,x,x^{\prime}\in A,\quad\;|f(x)-f(x^{\prime})|\leq L|x-x^{\prime}|\}.

Observation 4.5
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. If
f:\overline{A}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is an inner Lipschitz mapping,
\mathscr{C}^{1}
on
A
and
L
-Lipschitz on
\partial A
then
f
is
L^{\prime}
- Lipschitz on
\overline{A}
, where
L^{\prime}=\max(\sup_{x\in A}|d_{x}f|,L)
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\overline{A}
and define

E:=\{t\in[0,1]:(1-t)x+ty\notin A\},\;\;t_{0}:=\inf E,\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad t_{1}:=\sup E.

If
E
is empty then the desired fact follows from the Mean Value Theorem. Otherwise, the corresponding points
a:=(1-t_{0})x+t_{0}y
and
b:=(1-t_{1})x+t_{1}y
being in the boundary of
A
, we have by assumption
|f(a)-f(b)|\leq L|a-b|
. By the Mean Value Theorem, we have
|f(x)-f(a)|\leq K|x-a|
, where
K
is the bound of the partial derivatives on
A
, and the same inequality holds for
t_{1}
and the points
b
and
y
. Finally, by the triangle inequality, we get

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq|f(x)-f(a)|+|f(a)-f(b)|+|f(b)-f(y)|\leq K|x-a|+L|a-b|+K|b-y|,

which is not greater than

L^{\prime}|x-a|+L^{\prime}|a-b|+L^{\prime}|b-y|=L^{\prime}|x-y|,

where
L^{\prime}=\max(L,K)
.
∎

We first give a result in the case where the domain of the mapping is a definable manifold.

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume that the o-minimal structure admits
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
cell decomposition.
Let
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be definable
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
submanifolds. For any definable Lipschitz mapping
f:M\to N
and any
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(M)
and
\mu>0
, there exists a
(Lip(f)+\mu)
-Lipschitz mapping
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\infty}(M,N)
such that

(i)

|f-g|<\varepsilon

(ii)

|d_{x}g|\leq\mathring{L}_{f}(x)+\mu.

Proof.

We first show that for each
\mu<1
, the mapping
f
can be extended to a definable
(L+\mu)
-Lipschitz mapping
\tilde{f}
on a neighborhood of
M
, which will reduce the proof to the case where
M
is open in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, it suffices to show that
M
and
N
have neighborhoods
V
and
W
such that the closest point retractions
\pi_{M}:V\to M
and
\pi_{N}:W\to N
are well-defined and
(1+\mu)
-Lipschitz, since the desired extension will then be given by
\tilde{f}:=\pi_{N}\circ f\circ\pi_{M}
. We will focus on
M
, the argument for
N
being analogous. Note first that since
d_{x}\pi_{M}
is for every
x\in M
the orthogonal projection onto
T_{x}M
, we have
|d_{x}\pi_{M}|=1
for such
x
. By Definable Choice, there thus must be
\delta\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(M)
such that for each
x\in M
the mapping
\pi_{M}
is
(1+\mu)
-Lipschitz on
{\bf B}(x,\delta(x))
. We claim that
V:=U^{\mu\delta/4}_{M}
is the desired neighborhood. Indeed, if
x
and
y
are two points of
V
such that
|x-y|<\frac{\delta\circ\pi_{M}(x)}{2}
, they both belong to
{\bf B}(\pi_{M}(x),\frac{1+\mu}{2}\delta\circ\pi_{M}(x))
, which for
\mu<1
is included in
{\bf B}(\pi_{M}(x),\delta\circ\pi_{M}(x))
, on which
\pi_{M}
is
(1+\mu)
-Lipschitz. Of course, the same argument applies when

|x-y|<\frac{\delta\circ\pi_{M}(y)}{2}
. Finally, to address the case where
|x-y|\geq\max(\frac{\delta\circ\pi_{M}(x)}{2},\frac{\delta\circ\pi_{M}(y)}{2})
, notice that for all
x
and
y
in
V
we have

|\pi_{M}(x)-\pi_{M}(y)|\leq|x-y|+|x-\pi_{M}(x)|+|y-\pi_{M}(y)|\leq|x-y|+\frac{\mu}{4}\delta\circ\pi_{M}(x)+\frac{\mu}{4}\delta\circ\pi_{M}(y),

which is not greater than
(1+\mu)|x-y|
as soon as
|x-y|\geq\max(\frac{\delta\circ\pi_{M}(x)}{2},\frac{\delta\circ\pi_{M}(y)}{2})
, showing that
\pi_{M}
is
(1+\mu)
-Lipschitz on
V
.
Since it now suffices to prove the theorem for
\tilde{f}:=\pi_{N}\circ f\circ\pi_{M}
with
\tilde{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\circ\pi_{M}
, we will assume from now that
M
is open without changing notations.

Fix
\mu>0
. Possibly replacing
\varepsilon(x)
with the function
\frac{\varepsilon(x)d(x,\partial M)}{\sup\{d(a,\partial M)\,:\,|a|=|x|\}}\leq\varepsilon
, we can also assume that
\varepsilon(x)
goes to
0
as
x
approaches
\partial M
, and possibly replacing it with
\min(\varepsilon,\mu)
, we can assume it to be smaller than
\mu
. By Corollary
4.4
, there exists a definable
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
inner Lipschitz mapping
g:M\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
|f-g|<\varepsilon
and

(4.32)

|d_{x}g|\leq\mathring{L}_{f}(x)+\mu\leq Lip(f)+\mu.

Since
f
is Lipschitz, it continuously extends to a Lipschitz mapping on
\overline{M}
with the same Lipschitz constant. Since
\varepsilon
tends to zero at boundary points,
g(x):=f(x)
extends continuously
g
at boundary points, which means that
g
extends continuously to
\overline{M}
, inducing a
Lip(f)
-Lipschitz mapping on
\partial M
. By Observation
4.5
and (
4.32
),
g
must be
(Lip(f)+\mu)
-Lipschitz on
M
.
∎

Corollary
4.9
will address the case where the mapping is defined on a possibly nonsmooth definable subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Its proof will make use of the definable version of Kirszbraun’s theorem, which is a very delicate result established in
[
AF
]
:

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable set and
f:A\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
a definable
L
-Lipschitz map.
There exists a definable
L
-Lipschitz map
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
\tilde{f}_{|A}=f
.

Remark 4.8
.

In the case
k=1
, such an extension can however easily be obtained by setting
\tilde{f}(x):=\inf\{f(a)+L|x-a|\,:\,a\in A\}
.

Corollary 4.9
.

Assume that the o-minimal structure admits
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
cell decomposition.
Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a definable set. For any definable Lipschitz mapping
f:A\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
and any
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(A)
and
\mu>0
, there exists a
(Lip(f)+\mu)
-Lipschitz mapping
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\infty}(A,\mathbb{R}^{k})
such that
|f-g|<\varepsilon
on
A
.

Proof.

Thanks to Theorem
4.7
, we can extend
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, we can extend
\varepsilon
continuously to a neighborhood of
A
using Riesz’s formula (we may assume
\varepsilon\leq 1
)

\tilde{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{a\in A}\frac{\varepsilon(a)d(x,A)}{|a-x|}&\mbox{\rm for}\;x\notin A\\
\varepsilon(x)&\mbox{\rm for}\;x\in A\end{cases}

The needed approximation is then provided by Theorem
4.6
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

In the case where
f
is bounded, we can require that
Lip(g)=Lip(f)
. See
[
F
, Proof of Corollary
4
]
for details.

Another application of Theorem
4.6
is the following variant of Mostowski’s separation lemma
[
M
]
.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
M
be a definable
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
A,B
be disjoint definable subsets of
M
. If
A
and
B
are closed in
M
then there exists a Lipschitz function
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\infty}(M)
which is positive on
A
and negative on
B
.

Proof.

Let us define a Lipschitz function by setting for
x\in M
,
f(x):=d(x,B)-d(x,A)
. By Theorem
4.6
, there is a Lipschitz function
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\infty}(M)
such that
|f(x)-g(x)|<\frac{d(x,A)+d(x,B)}{2}
. This implies that
g(x)>\frac{d(x,B)}{2}
on
A
, and
g(x)<-\frac{d(x,A)}{2}
on
B
.
∎
