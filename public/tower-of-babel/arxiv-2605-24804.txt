Abstract




Abstract:

In this paper, we apply a self-similar transformation to convert the parabolic equation with a Sobolev-Hardy term

\displaystyle u_{t}-\Delta u=\frac{|u|^{q-2}u}{\left|x\right|^{s}}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),

into the following elliptic equation

-\Delta v-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla v=\alpha v+\frac{|v|^{q-2}v}{|y|^{s}},

where
2<q\leq 2^{*}(s)=\frac{2N-2s}{N-2},0\leq s<2,\alpha=\frac{2-s}{2q-4}
.
For this equation, we establish the
weighted Hardy inequality and Sobolev inequality. Furthermore, by virtue of the variational methods,
we obtain infinitely many solutions in the subcritical case,
and prove the existence of solutions in the critical case.
We also apply the Pohozaev identity to establish the nonexistence of solutions under certain conditions.

Keywords:
Hardy terms, Sobolev inequality, self-similar transformation, variational method.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the following classical semilinear parabolic equation associated with Sobolev-Hardy term in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

u_{t}-\Delta u=\frac{|u|^{q-2}u}{\left|x\right|^{s}},\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),

(1.1)

where
2<q\leq 2^{*}(s)\equiv\frac{2N-2s}{N-2},0\leq s<2
.
Semilinear parabolic partial differential equations with singular potentials constitute a core research branch of
modern nonlinear analysis and evolutionary equation theory. Compared with classical parabolic equations with smooth
coefficients, such equations feature spatially singular terms, which can effectively characterize complex diffusion,
concentration and singular evolution phenomena in physics, mechanics and engineering practice, including anomalous
heat conduction, phase transition dynamics, material singularity response and population evolution with spatial
singularity constraints (see
[
7
,
15
]
. In
[
3
]
, the author established the necessary and sufficient
conditions for initial data lying below or at the ground state, which lead to a complete dichotomy of the dynamical
behavior of solutions: the solution either exists globally in time with its energy decaying to zero as time evolves,
or undergoes blow-up in finite or infinite time.
In
[
12
]
, for spatial dimension
N\geq 5
, by virtue of the Fourier splitting method,
the author derived the precise decay rate of dissipative solutions and demonstrated that the critical norm of such
dissipative solutions vanish as time tends to infinity.
For further research progress on this equation, we refer the
readers to
[
4
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
and the references therein.

The self-similar transformation is defined as follow:

u(x,t)=t^{-\alpha}v(y),\quad y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},

where,

t>0,\alpha=\frac{2-s}{2q-4}.

Through this transformation and the detailed calculation, problem (
1.1
) can be transformed into the following problem

-\Delta v-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla v=\alpha v+\frac{|v|^{q-2}v}{|y|^{s}}.

(1.2)

Set

K(y):=e^{|y|^{2}/4}.

Then we let

Lv:=-\Delta v-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla v=-\frac{1}{K}\nabla\cdot(K\nabla v).

Hence, problem (
1.2
) is equivalent to

Lv=\alpha v+\frac{|v|^{q-2}v}{|y|^{s}}.

When
s=0
, for problem (
1.1
), Tan
[
20
]
adopted the Moser iteration technique and potential well method to establish the existence of classical global solutions for initial data with low energy. Meanwhile, he further investigated the asymptotic behaviors of global solutions arising from high-energy initial data. Based on the existing theories concerning radial solutions, this work precisely describes the asymptotic profiles of unbounded global solutions defined in bounded domains of dimension
N\geq 3
. Escobedo and Kavian in
[
5
]
conducted a systematic investigation into problem (
1.1
) and problem (
1.2
) for
s=0
.
By virtue of the self-similar transformation, problem (
1.1
) can be reduced to problem (
1.2
).
Employing variational methods, including critical point theory and the Mountain Pass Lemma,
they established the existence, multiplicity, symmetry and asymptotic properties of solutions to
the elliptic equation, and further characterized the complete structure of self-similar solutions
for the heat equation. Thereafter, for
s=0
, problem (
1.2
) as well as its more general nonlinear
formulations has been extensively investigated in the literature; see, for instance,

[
2
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.

We denote by
X
the Hilbert space obtained as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
with respect to the norm

\|\nabla v\|_{K,2}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}K(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

which is induced by the inner product

(v_{1},v_{2})_{K}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\nabla v_{1}\cdot\nabla v_{2})K(y)dy.

For each
q\in\left[2,2^{\ast}(s)\right]
, we define
L_{K}^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
and
L_{K}^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},\frac{1}{|y|^{s}}\right)
as follows:

L_{K}^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right):=\left\{v\text{ measurable in }\mathbb{R}^{N}:\|v\|_{K,q}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{q}K(y)dy\right)^{1/q}<\infty\right\},

L_{K}^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},\frac{1}{|y|^{s}}\right):=\left\{v\text{ measurable in }\mathbb{R}^{N}:\left\|v\right\|_{K,q,s}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|y|^{s}}|v|^{q}K(y)dy\right)^{1/q}<\infty\right\}.

The domain
D(L)
of this operator consists of all
v\in L_{K}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that
Lv\in L_{K}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
,
and one has
D(L)=X
; see Lemma 2.1 in
[
13
]
. Moreover,
L
is positive and self-adjoint with a compact inverse.
In particular, the normalized eigenfunctions of
L
constitute a complete orthonormal basis in
L_{K}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.
The first eigenvalue of
L
satisfies
\lambda_{1}=N/2
, which yields the following Poincaré inequality

\lambda_{1}\|v\|_{K,2}^{2}\leqslant\|\nabla v\|_{K,2}^{2},\quad v\in X,

(1.3)

as stated in Proposition 2.3 of
[
5
]
.
A function
v\in X
is a weak solution of
(
1.2
), if for every
\phi\in X
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla v\nabla\phi k(y)dy=\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v\phi K(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v|^{q-2}v}{|y|^{s}}\phi K(y)dy.

Weak solutions of (
1.2
) are exactly the critical points of the functional
E_{K}(v):X\rightarrow R
defined as

\displaystyle E_{K}(v)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}K(y)dy-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{2}K(y)dy-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|y|^{s}}|v|^{q}K(y)dy

(1.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla v|^{2}-\alpha|v|^{2}\right)K(y)dy-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|y|^{s}}|v|^{q}K(y)dy

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}A(v)-\frac{1}{q}B(v).

It is easy to prove that
E_{K}(v)
is Fréchet differentiable in
X
and

\langle E_{K}(v),\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla v\nabla\phi k(y)dy-\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v\phi K(y)dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v|^{q-2}v}{|y|^{s}}\phi K(y)dy.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we establish the weighted Hardy inequality
and the weighted Sobolev inequality. In Section 3, we investigate the Hardy subcritical singular
case and obtain the existence of infinitely many solutions for this case. In Section 4,
we discuss the Hardy critical singular case and derive the existence and nonexistence results of solutions.
In Section 5, we provide an alternative proof for the existence result established in Section 4.

, then for any

and any

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
u\in H_{K}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then we have

(1)

u/|x|\in L^{2}_{K}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.

(2)

(K-Hardy inequality)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}K(x)dx\leqslant C_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}K(x)dx

(2.1)

with
C_{N}=\left(\frac{2}{N-2}\right)^{2}
.

(3)

The constant
C_{N}
is optimal.

Proof
.

By a density argument, it suffices to consider only smooth functions
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then we have

\displaystyle|u(x)|^{2}K(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(|u(\lambda x)|^{2}K(\lambda x)\right)d\lambda

\displaystyle=-2\int_{1}^{\infty}u(\lambda x)\langle x,\nabla u(\lambda x)\rangle K(\lambda x)d\lambda-\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda|x|^{2}}{2}|u(\lambda x)|^{2}K(\lambda x)\right)d\lambda.

By using Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}K(x)dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(\lambda x)}{|x|}\left\langle\frac{x}{|x|},\nabla u(\lambda x)\right\rangle K(\lambda x)dxd\lambda

\displaystyle-\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}|u(\lambda x)|^{2}K(\lambda x)\right)d\lambda.

\displaystyle\leq-2\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{N-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(y)}{|y|}\frac{\partial u(y)}{\partial r}K\left(y\right)dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{N-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(y)}{|y|}\frac{\partial u(y)}{\partial r}K\left(y\right)dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{N-2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|y|^{2}}K\left(y\right)dy\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\frac{\partial u(y)}{\partial r}\right|^{2}K\left(y\right)dy\right)^{1/2}.

And then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}K(x)dx\leq\left(\frac{2}{N-2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}K(x)dx.

Next, we prove that
C_{N}
is the optimal constant.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, we take the following radial function

V(r)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }r\in[0,1],\\
r^{-\gamma}e^{-\frac{|r|^{2}}{4}}&\text{ if }r>1,\end{cases}

where
\gamma=\frac{N-2+2\varepsilon}{2}
. Hence, we have

V^{2}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }r\in[0,1],\\
r^{-2\gamma}e^{-\frac{|r|^{2}}{2}}&\text{ if }r>1.\end{cases}

For
r>1
, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=-\gamma r^{-\gamma-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}+r^{-\gamma}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}\cdot\left(-\frac{r}{2}\right)=-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}r^{-\gamma}\left(\frac{\gamma}{r}+\frac{r}{2}\right)=-V(r)\left(\frac{\gamma}{r}+\frac{r}{2}\right),

\displaystyle|\nabla V(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(V^{\prime}(r)\right)^{2}=V^{2}(x)\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\gamma+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{V^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\nabla V(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\gamma+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}\right)}=\frac{|\nabla V(x)|^{2}}{\left(\gamma^{2}+\gamma|x|^{2}+\frac{|x|^{4}}{4}\right)}\geq\frac{|\nabla V(x)|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}.

Thus, from the above relation, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{V^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}K(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\frac{V^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}K(x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B}\frac{V^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}K(x)dx

(2.2)

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{N}\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-3}K(r)dr+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla V(x)|^{2}K(x)dx.

where
\omega_{N}
is the measure of the
(N-1)
-dimensional unit sphere.

The inequality (
2.2
) implies that as long as the constant
1/\gamma^{2}
is less than
C_{N}
,
we can always construct a function
V(x)
such that the inequality (
2.1
) fails to hold. Consequently,
C_{N}
is shown to be optimal.

Lemma 2.4
.

(K-Sobolev-Hardy Inequality). Assume that
2\leq q\leq 2^{*}(s):=\frac{2N-2s}{N-2}

(1)

There exists a constant

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{q}}{|x|^{s}}K(x)dx\right)^{2}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}K(x)dx\right)^{q}.

(2)

The embedding

is continuous for all

Proof
.

(1) For

.
Therefore, we consider the case of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}K(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{s}}{|x|^{s}}(K(x))^{\frac{s}{2}}\cdot|u|^{2^{*}(s)-s}(K(x))^{{}^{\frac{2-s}{2}}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}K(x)dx\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{\left(2^{*}(s)-s\right)\frac{2}{2-s}}K(x)dx\right)^{\frac{2-s}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}K(x)dx\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}K(x)dx\right)^{\frac{2-s}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}K(x)\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}K(x)\right)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}\cdot\frac{2-s}{2}}

\displaystyle=C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}K(x)dx\right)^{\frac{N-s}{N-2}}

By similar arguments for

, we can also obtain

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{q}}{|x|^{s}}K(x)dx\right)\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}K(x)dx\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}.

(2) Let

\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{K,2}\leqslant 1

,
one can suppose that (by Rellich’s compactness theorem):

u_{n}\rightarrow u\quad\text{ in }L_{\text{loc }}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}\right).

Then , we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}K(x)dx=\int_{|x|\leqslant R}\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}K(x)dx+\int_{|x|>R}\left|u_{n}-u\right|^{2}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}}K(x)dx.

Given an

(2.3)

Let

\displaystyle e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{8}}\nabla u=|x|^{\frac{s}{2}}\nabla v-e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{8}}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2|x|^{2}}\right)xu=|x|^{\frac{s}{2}}\nabla v-|x|^{\frac{s}{2}}v\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2|x|^{2}}\right)x.

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}|x|^{s}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}|x|^{2}|x|^{s}dx

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2|x|^{2}}\right)|x|^{s}x\nabla v^{2}dx.

(2.7)

Then using this together with (

On the other hand, According to Lemma 3.2 of

3
Hardy subcritical singular case

In this section, we deal with the Hardy subcritical singular case and employ the fountain theorem to establish the existence of infinitely many solutions.
Our main result is as follow.

.

Suppose
