Single-letter one-way distillable entanglement for non-degradable states
Rabsan Galib Ahmed 1,2 , Graeme Smith 1,2 and Peixue Wu 1,2 1 Institute for Quantum Computing, University of Waterloo, 200 University Avenue West, Waterloo, ON N2L 3G1, Canada 2 Department of Applied Mathematics, University of Waterloo, 200 University Avenue West, Waterloo, ON N2L 3G1, Canada

Abstract
The one-way distillable entanglement is a central operational measure of bipartite entanglement, quantifying the optimal rate at which maximally entangled pairs can be extracted by one-way LOCC. Despite its importance, it is notoriously hard to compute, since it is defined by a regularized optimization over many copies and adaptive one-way protocols. At present, single-letter formulas are only known for (conjugate) degradable and PPT states. More generally, it has remained unclear when one-way distillable entanglement can still be additive beyond degradability and PPT settings, and how such additivity relates to additivity questions of quantum capacity of channels. In this paper, we address this gap by identifying three explicit families of non-degradable and non-PPT states whose one-way distillable entanglement is nevertheless single-letter. First, we introduce two weakened degradability-type conditions—regularized less-noisy and informationally degradable—and prove that each guarantees additivity and hence a single-letter formula. Second, we show a stability result for orthogonally flagged mixtures: when one component has orthogonal support on Alice’s system and zero one-way distillable entanglement, the mixture remains single-letter, even though degradability is typically lost under such mixing. Finally, we propose a generalized spin-alignment principle for entropy minimization in tensor-product settings, which we establish in several key cases, including a complete Rényi-2 result. As an application, we obtain additivity results for generalized direct-sum channels and their corresponding Choi states.



I
Introduction

Distilling maximally entangled states from many copies of a noisy bipartite state
via local operations and classical communication (LOCC)

[
2
,
5
,
4
]

is a foundational problem in quantum information science, enabling key protocols such as
dense coding and teleportation
[
6
,
3
]
.
When the allowed classical communication is restricted to be one-way (say
A\to B
),
the optimal asymptotic rate at which ebits can be extracted is quantified by the

one-way distillable entanglement

D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})
.
Beyond its operational meaning as an entanglement measure,
D_{\to}
is tightly connected to
communication problems: through channel–state duality, additivity and single-letter
phenomena for one-way distillation mirror (but are not identical to) additivity questions
for coherent information and quantum capacity.

A major obstruction is that
D_{\to}
is generally
non-additive

[
12
,
16
]
, and therefore is defined through a regularization

[
10
]
:

\displaystyle D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}).

Determining when such regularizations collapse to single-letter
formulas is a central theme in quantum information theory.
For (conjugate) degradable and anti-degradable states, one-way distillable entanglement
admits a single-letter expression given by coherent information
[
14
]
.
Likewise, PPT states have vanishing two-way (and hence one-way) distillable entanglement

[
11
]
.

In contrast, explicit families of states that are
non-degradable
, non-PPT, and yet satisfy

D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})

have remained unexplored. Motivated by recent progress on additivity for non-degradable channels in quantum capacity theory

[
15
,
17
]
, we fill this gap by introducing three mechanisms that produce such states:

(i)

states satisfying weaker notions of degradability
,

(ii)

orthogonally mixtures with a “useless” component
, and

(iii)

states governed by a spin-alignment entropy minimization principle
.

A channel-capacity viewpoint and a subtle mismatch.

Given a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, a natural perspective suggested by the Choi correspondence is to compare
additivity phenomena for one-way distillation with those for one-shot channel capacity.
Concretely, let
\Phi_{\rho}^{A\to B}
be the completely positive map induced by
\rho_{AB}
via the
Choi–Jamiołkowski isomorphism (equivalently,
\rho_{AB}
is proportional to the Choi operator of
\Phi_{\rho}
).
One may ask whether additivity of the maximal coherent information
\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Phi_{\rho})

should be equivalent to additivity of
D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB})
.
While these two questions coincide in the (conjugate) degradable setting, in general they are

a priori different
: the optimizations defining the two quantities impose different operational constraints.

Recall
[
10
]
that

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\max\Big\{I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}BM}}:\;\omega_{A^{\prime}BM}=\sum_{m}(K_{m}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K_{m}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\otimes|m\rangle\langle m|,\ \sum_{m}K_{m}^{\dagger}K_{m}=\mathbb{I}_{A}\Big\}.

A natural relaxation is to
drop the completeness constraint

\sum_{m}K_{m}^{\dagger}K_{m}=\mathbb{I}_{A}
,
i.e., allow trace non-preserving operations and renormalize.
For any
K:A\to A^{\prime}
, define the postselected state

\omega_{A^{\prime}B}(K)=\frac{(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}}{\mathrm{Tr}\big[(K^{\dagger}K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}\big]}.

Then one obtains the simplified expression

\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\max_{K}\;I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}B}(K)},

(1)

which upper bounds
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})
by construction.

This relaxation admits an exact channel interpretation.
Let
\rho_{AB}^{\mathcal{N}}
denote the normalized Choi state of a channel
\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}
.
Using the Choi–Jamiołkowski isomorphism,
\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}
becomes precisely the one-shot
quantum capacity:

\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})=\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}\!\big(\rho_{AB}^{\mathcal{N}}\big),

where
\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})
is the maximal coherent information of
\mathcal{N}
.

Consequently, additivity of
\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}
is equivalent (via the Choi correspondence)
to additivity of
\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}
for the associated channels.
In contrast, additivity for
D^{(1)}_{\to}
is intrinsically more delicate, since the optimization is restricted to

complete
one-way instruments
\sum_{m}K_{m}^{\dagger}K_{m}=\mathbb{I}_{A}
rather than arbitrary
postselected filters.
Our three mechanisms provide structured settings in which this completeness constraint can still be controlled,
yielding explicit non-degradable, non-PPT families with single-letter one-way distillable entanglement.

We present three structural mechanisms that yield explicit families of
non-degradable
, non-PPT states
with single-letter one-way distillable entanglement. For each mechanism we first state the guiding principle,
and then give a representative example.

Mechanism 1: additivity from weaker degradability (information dominance)

Let
\rho_{AB}
have a purification
\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE}
. Standard degradability requires that
E
can be simulated
from
B
by a channel; we instead impose only an
information ordering
saying that, under all relevant
pre-processings on Alice, Bob is at least as informative as the purifying system.
Two convenient formulations are:

•

(Regularized less noisy.)
For every
n\geq 1
and every quantum instrument

\mathcal{T}^{A^{n}\to A^{\prime}M}
, letting
\omega_{A^{\prime}B^{n}E^{n}}=\mathcal{T}(\phi_{ABE}^{\otimes n})
,

I(M;B^{n})_{\omega}\geq I(M;E^{n})_{\omega}.

(2)

•

(Informationally degradable.)
For every channel
\mathcal{N}^{A\to A^{\prime}}
and

\omega_{A^{\prime}BE}=\mathcal{N}(\phi_{ABE})
,

I(A^{\prime};B)_{\omega}\geq I(A^{\prime};E)_{\omega}.

(3)

These conditions are strictly weaker than degradability but are still strong enough to force
single-letter behavior, by the same telescoping/chain-rule strategy that underlies many additivity
proofs in capacity theory (cf.
[
17
]
).

Consequence (informal).

If (
2
) holds for all
n
, then

D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB})=I(A\rangle B)_{\rho},

and
D_{\to}^{(1)}
is additive for many copies of such states, i.e., it exhibits weak additivity under tensor products.
Furthermore informationally degradable states satisfy additivity for
D_{\to}^{(1)}
under tensor products i.e., it exhibits strong additivity under tensor products.

Example (flagged mixtures of amplitude damping channels).

Let
{\rm AD}_{\gamma}
be the qubit amplitude damping channel with Kraus operators

E_{0}=|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+\sqrt{1-\gamma}\,|1\rangle\!\langle 1|,\qquad E_{1}=\sqrt{\gamma}\,|0\rangle\!\langle 1|,\qquad\gamma\in[0,1].

Fix parameters
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in[0,1]
and a mixing probability
p\in[0,1]
, and define the flagged channel

\mathcal{N}^{A\to BF}(\rho):=p\,{\rm AD}_{\gamma_{0}}(\rho)\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{F}+(1-p)\,{\rm AD}_{\gamma_{1}}(\rho)\otimes|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{F},

where the classical flag
F
is available to Bob. Its (normalized) Choi state

\rho_{RBF}^{\mathcal{N}}:=(\mathrm{id}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{N})(|\Phi^{+}\rangle\!\langle\Phi^{+}|)
,
with
|\Phi^{+}\rangle=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
, is

\rho_{RBF}^{\mathcal{N}}=p\,\rho_{RB}^{(\gamma_{0})}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{F}+(1-p)\,\rho_{RB}^{(\gamma_{1})}\otimes|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{F},

(4)

where, in the computational basis
\{|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle\}
of
RB
,

\rho_{RB}^{(\gamma)}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&0&\gamma&0\\
\sqrt{1-\gamma}&0&0&1-\gamma\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

This flagged family for some parameter regions provides explicit
non-degradable
Choi states for which the weaker
information-dominance property holds and hence
D_{\to}(\rho_{RBF}^{\mathcal{N}})
is single-letter
[
17
]
.

Mechanism 2: orthogonal flags with a “useless” component

Suppose Alice locally knows which component of a mixture she holds, i.e. the components have orthogonal support
on
A
. Then one-way distillation can be performed conditionally on that classical information.
If, moreover, one component is “useless” for one-way distillation (e.g. anti-degradable / separable, hence
zero one-way distillable entanglement), the mixture inherits a single-letter formula from the “useful”
component. Operationally, orthogonality on
A
turns the problem into a direct-sum decomposition.

Prototype statement (informal).

Let
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}
be bipartite states with
\rho_{0}^{A}\perp\rho_{1}^{A}
and assume the product additivity bound

D_{\to}^{(1)}(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{w_{i}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{w_{i}})
for all words
w^{n}
.
Then for any
p\in[0,1]
,

D_{\to}\big(p\rho_{0}+(1-p)\rho_{1}\big)=D_{\to}^{(1)}\big(p\rho_{0}+(1-p)\rho_{1}\big)=p\,D_{\to}(\rho_{0})+(1-p)\,D_{\to}(\rho_{1}).

Example (a
3\times 3
state with a separable “junk” block).

Consider, for
s\in[0,1]
, the state on
\mathbb{C}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{3}

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}(s)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{3}\Big(|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{A}\otimes\big[s\,|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{B}+(1-s)\,|2\rangle\!\langle 2|_{B}\big]+|0\rangle\!\langle 1|_{A}\otimes\sqrt{s}\,|0\rangle\!\langle 2|_{B}

\displaystyle\hskip 62.59596pt+|1\rangle\!\langle 0|_{A}\otimes\sqrt{s}\,|2\rangle\!\langle 0|_{B}+|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{A}\otimes|2\rangle\!\langle 2|_{B}\Big)+\frac{1}{3}|22\rangle\!\langle 22|_{AB}.

Here the last term
\frac{1}{3}|22\rangle\!\langle 22|
is separable and supported on an
A
-subspace orthogonal to
the support of the first block. The remaining “useful” component is a Choi-type amplitude-damping structure on

{\rm span}\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}_{A}
.
By the above principle,
\rho_{AB}(s)
has single-letter one-way distillable entanglement, and one obtains

D_{\to}(\rho_{AB}(s))=D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB}(s))=\frac{2}{3}\max\!\left\{h\!\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)-h\!\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right),\,0\right\}.

Mechanism 3: spin alignment and generalized direct-sum structure

Certain block-structured channels/states reduce multi-copy optimizations (entropy minimization or coherent
information maximization) to a classical mixture over “which block is used”
[
20
]
.
The key step is a
spin-alignment
entropy minimization rule: when outputs are mixtures of the form

\rho\otimes\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{0}\otimes\rho
, entropy is minimized by aligning the free input
\rho

with maximal-eigenvalue directions of the fixed states
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}
.
This forces optimal
n
-copy inputs to be product-aligned across sites, turning a priori noncommutative
problems into tractable classical ones.

Spin alignment (informal).

Fix
\sigma_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(B_{0})
and
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{D}(B_{1})
and define

\mathcal{N}_{0}(\rho)=\rho\otimes\sigma_{1}
,
\mathcal{N}_{1}(\rho)=\sigma_{0}\otimes\rho
.
Given a distribution
\{p_{\vec{x}}\}
on
\{0,1\}^{n}
, consider

\min_{\{\rho_{\vec{x}}\}}S\!\left(\sum_{\vec{x}}p_{\vec{x}}\,\mathcal{N}_{\vec{x}}(\rho_{\vec{x}})\right).

The conjectured (and partially proved) alignment rule says the optimum is attained by product inputs

\rho_{\vec{x}}=\bigotimes_{i}\tau^{(i)}_{x_{i}}
with each
\tau^{(i)}_{0}
(resp.
\tau^{(i)}_{1}
)
a projector onto a maximal-eigenspace of
\sigma_{0}
(resp.
\sigma_{1}
).
We establish the
n=1
von Neumann case, and the full
n
-copy statement for Rényi-
2
entropy.

Example (a generalized direct-sum state with single-letter
D_{\to}
).

Let
A,B\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}+d_{1}}
and define

\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{2d_{0}d_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{0}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}-1}\Big(|i\rangle|j\rangle+|j+d_{0}\rangle|i+d_{1}\rangle\Big)\Big(\langle i|\langle j|+\langle j+d_{0}|\langle i+d_{1}|\Big).

This state is a canonical “two-block coherent coupling” (a Choi-type generalized direct-sum structure).
Using the alignment principle to control the relevant entropy minimizations, we show that

D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB}),

providing an explicit non-degradable, non-PPT family where one-way distillation is single-letter.

Together, these three mechanisms—information dominance without full degradability,
orthogonal flags with a useless component, and spin-alignment-driven block reduction—yield broad,
explicit sources of additivity and single-letter behavior for
D_{\to}
beyond the degradable/PPT regimes.

Open questions and future work.
An important open problem is to prove the spin alignment conjecture with von-Neumann entropy for arbitrary tensor powers. Although partial progress has been made
[
1
]
, the general case remains unsolved. Futhermore, understanding the comparative additivity of
D^{(1)}_{\to}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}
remains to be explored. Finally, it remains to be understood whether the techniques developed here extend to two-way distillation or to other quantum resource theories.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
II
, we provide the necessary preliminary background for our results. In Section
III
, we have identified the general principles that allow us to show single-letter one-way distillable entanglement for non-degradable states. Finally in Section
IV
, we provide explicit examples for such states. Additionally we show that a large class of generalised direct sum channels exhibit single-letter quantum capacity complying with one of our general principles.

II
Preliminary

II-A

Bipartite quantum states and entanglement

Consider two quantum systems with corresponding Hilbert spaces,
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
abbreviated as
A
(for Alice) and
B
(for Bob). The states of this composite system, called the bipartite states, are described by elements of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
, where
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
is the set of all positive semi-definite operators on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with unit trace.

The set of bipartite states,
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
is further classified into two categories:
separable states
and
entangled states
. We call
\varrho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
a separable state if there exists a probability vector
\{p_{i}\}
and states
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
,
\sigma_{i}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
such that

\displaystyle\varrho_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\;\rho_{i}\otimes\sigma_{i}

(6)

The set of all separable states are often denoted as
\rm{Sep}(A:B)
. The states which cannot be written in this form are called
entangled states
. These are elements of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})\;\backslash\;\rm{Sep}(A:B)
.

II-B

Distillable entanglement

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite state on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
.
We denote by
\mathrm{LOCC}_{A\to B}
the set of protocols implementable by
local operations and classical communication from
A
to
B
only.
For an integer
M
, let

\Phi_{M}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\lvert i\rangle\lvert i\rangle

be a maximally entangled state of Schmidt rank
M
on

\mathbb{C}^{M}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{M}
.
From here onward all the logarithms are taken to be base
2
.

Achievable rate.

A number
R\geq 0
(in ebits per copy) is said to be
one-way
achievable
for
\rho_{AB}
if there exist a sequence of integers
M_{n}

and a sequence of one-way LOCC protocols

\Lambda_{n}\in\mathrm{LOCC}_{A\to B}
such that

\sigma_{n}:=\Lambda_{n}\big(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\big)\in\mathcal{D}\big((\mathbb{C}^{M_{n}}\!\otimes\!\mathbb{C}^{M_{n}})\big)

satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\big\|\sigma_{n}-\Phi_{M_{n}}\big\|_{1}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}\;\geq\;R.

One-way distillable entanglement.

The
one-way distillable entanglement
of
\rho_{AB}
is

D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})\;:=\;\sup\bigl\{R\,\big|\,R\text{ is one-way achievable for }\rho_{AB}\bigr\}.

(7)

Equivalent fidelity formulation.

Equivalently, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
define the

\varepsilon
-error,
n
-blocklength one-way distillable entanglement by

\displaystyle D_{\to}^{(n,\varepsilon)}(\rho_{AB}):=\frac{1}{n}\,\max_{\Lambda_{n}\in\mathrm{LOCC}_{A\to B}}\{\log M:F\bigl(\Lambda_{n}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}),\,\Phi_{M}\bigr)\geq 1-\varepsilon\Bigr\},

where
F(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Uhlmann fidelity. Then

D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})\;=\;\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\;\liminf_{n\to\infty}D_{\to}^{(n,\varepsilon)}(\rho_{AB}).

(8)

Regularization expression

Devetak and Winter
[
10
]
proved the following regularized expression for the one-way distillable entanglement

D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\,D^{(1)}_{\to}\!\left(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\right),

(9)

where

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}):=\max_{\mathcal{T}}\;\sum_{m}\lambda_{m}\,I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\rho_{m}}=\max_{\mathcal{T}}\,I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\mathcal{T}(\rho_{AB})},\quad\rho_{m}:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{m}}\,\mathcal{T}_{m}(\rho_{AB}),\quad\lambda_{m}:=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\mathcal{T}_{m}(\rho_{AB})\right],

(10)

where the instrument
\mathcal{T}=\sum_{m}\mathcal{T}_{m}\otimes\lvert m\rangle\!\langle m\rvert
has a single Kraus operator

\mathcal{T}_{m}(\cdot)=K_{m}(\cdot)K_{m}^{\dagger}
,
K_{m}:A\to A^{\prime}
.

We can obtain an alternative expression in terms of an isometric extension
V:A\to A^{\prime}MN
of the instrument,
\mathcal{T}
, defined by

V:=\sum_{m}K_{m}\otimes\lvert m\rangle_{M}\otimes\lvert m\rangle_{N},

(11)

for a classical register
N\cong M
.
Since
\sum_{m}K_{m}^{\dagger}K_{m}=\mathbb{I}_{A}
, we have
V^{\dagger}V=\mathbb{I}_{A}
,
and
\mathcal{T}(\rho_{AB})=\operatorname{Tr}_{N}\!\big(V\rho_{AB}V^{\dagger}\big)
. For any isometry
V^{A\to A^{\prime}MN}
with the form (
11
), and let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite state with purification
\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE}
, define

\lvert\omega\rangle_{A^{\prime}MNBE}=(V\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{E})\,\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE},

(12)

we have

\displaystyle I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\mathcal{T}(\rho_{AB})}

\displaystyle=I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\omega}

\displaystyle=S(BM)_{\omega}-S(A^{\prime}BM)_{\omega}

\displaystyle=S(BM)_{\omega}-S(EN)_{\omega}

\displaystyle=S(BM)_{\omega}-S(EM)_{\omega},

where the third equality follows from the fact that
\omega
is pure on
A^{\prime}MNBE
, so
S(A^{\prime}BM)_{\omega}=S(EN)_{\omega}
(complementary systems with a joint pure state have equal von Neumann entropy); the last equality follows from
M\!\leftrightarrow\!N
symmetry in the construction of
\omega
(in particular, the joint law of
(E,M)
and
(E,N)
is the same), hence
S(EN)_{\omega}=S(EM)_{\omega}
. Therefore, we have

Lemma II.1
.

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
with purification
\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE}
, we have

D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\max_{V}\,I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\omega}=\max_{V}\,\left[S(BM)_{\omega}-S(EM)_{\omega}\right],\quad\lvert\omega\rangle_{A^{\prime}MNBE}=(V\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{E})\,\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE},

(13)

where
V^{A\to A^{\prime}MN}
is any isometry with the form (
11
).

From the above lemma, it is immediate to see that if
U_{A}
and
V_{B}
are isometries, then

D^{(1)}_{\to}\bigl((U_{A}\otimes V_{B})\,\rho\,(U_{A}^{\dagger}\otimes V_{B}^{\dagger})\bigr)=D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho),\quad D_{\to}\bigl((U_{A}\otimes V_{B})\,\rho\,(U_{A}^{\dagger}\otimes V_{B}^{\dagger})\bigr)=D_{\to}(\rho)

(14)

for every
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(AB)
.

II-C

Degradable states

Definition II.2
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite state with purification
\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE}
.
The state
\rho_{AB}
is called:

1.

degradable
, if there is a quantum channel
\mathcal{D}^{B\to E}
such that

(id\otimes\mathcal{D}^{B\to E})(\rho_{AB})=\phi_{AE}.

(15)

2.

antidegradable
, if there is a quantum channel
\mathcal{D}^{E\to B}
such that

(id\otimes\mathcal{D}^{E\to B})(\phi_{AE})=\rho_{AB}.

(16)

Via data processing inequality, for the state given by
\lvert\omega\rangle_{A^{\prime}MNBE}=(V\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{E})\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE}
, we have
S(BM)_{\omega}-S(EM)_{\omega}\leq S(B)_{\omega}-S(E)_{\omega}=I(A\rangle B)_{\rho}
. Then using Lemma
II.1
, we find that
D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho)=I(A\rangle B)_{\rho}
for degradable state
\rho
. Similarly,
D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho)=0
for anti-degradable state
\rho
. This provides an alternative proof of the results obtained in
[
14
]
. Using the additivity of coherent information under tensor product states, we immediately have

\displaystyle D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\begin{cases}I(A\rangle B)_{\rho},&\text{ when $\rho$ is degradable};\\
0,&\text{ when $\rho$ is anti-degradable}.\end{cases}

(17)

II-D

Connection to the quantum channel capacities

To study the additivity property of
D^{(1)}_{\to}
, we introduce an upper bound of it. Recall (
10
):

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\max\left\{I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}BM}}:\omega_{A^{\prime}BM}=\sum_{m}(K_{m}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K_{m}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}\otimes|m\rangle\langle m|,\quad\forall\{K_{m}^{A\to A^{\prime}}\}\ \text{s.t.}\ \sum_{m}K_{m}^{\dagger}K_{m}=\mathbb{I}_{A}\right\}.

Therefore, a natural upper bound for
D^{(1)}_{\to}
is to withdraw the condition
\sum_{m}K_{m}^{\dagger}K_{m}=\mathbb{I}_{A}
:

Definition II.3
.

For a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, we introduce the quantity

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\max\left\{I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}BM}}:\omega_{A^{\prime}BM}=\frac{\sum_{m}(K_{m}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K_{m}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}\otimes|m\rangle\langle m|}{\sum_{m}\Tr( (K_m^\dagger K_m \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})},\quad\forall\{K_{m}\}\right\}.

Interestingly, the above quantity is actually the maximal coherent information of the channel with Choi operator given by
\rho_{AB}
:

Proposition II.4
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be bipartite state and
\mathcal{N}
be the corresponding completely positive map from
A
to
B
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N})=\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}).

A first step is to simplify the above expression.

Lemma II.5
.

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\max\left\{I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}B}}:\omega_{A^{\prime}B}=\frac{(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}}{\Tr( (K^\dagger K \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})},\quad\forall K:A\to A^{\prime}\right\}.

(18)

Proof.

It is obvious that

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})\geq\max\left\{I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}B}}:\omega_{A^{\prime}B}=\frac{(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}}{\Tr( (K^\dagger K \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})},\quad\forall K:A\to A^{\prime}\right\}.

To show the other direction, suppose
\{K_{m}\}
are the optimal operators achieving
\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})
. Denote

\displaystyle p_{m}=\frac{\Tr( (K_m^\dagger K_m \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})}{\sum_{m}\Tr( (K_m^\dagger K_m \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})},\quad\rho_{A^{\prime}B}^{m}=\frac{(K_{m}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K_{m}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}}{\Tr( (K_m^\dagger K_m \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})}

Then the coherent information of
\omega_{A^{\prime}BM}=\sum_{m}p_{m}\rho_{A^{\prime}B}^{m}\otimes|m\rangle\langle m|
is calculated as

\displaystyle I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}BM}}=\sum_{m}p_{m}I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\rho_{A^{\prime}B}^{m}}\leq I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\rho_{A^{\prime}B}^{m_{0}}},

where
m_{0}
is chosen such that

\displaystyle I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\rho_{A^{\prime}B}^{m_{0}}}=\max_{m}I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\rho_{A^{\prime}B}^{m}}.

Therefore, by choosing
K
as
K_{m_{0}}
, one has

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})\leq\max\left\{I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}B}}:\omega_{A^{\prime}B}=\frac{1}{\Tr( (K^\dagger K \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})}(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}\quad\forall K:A\to A^{\prime}\right\},

which concludes the proof.
∎

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, it naturally induces a completely positive map
\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}
via the following

\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}(\sigma):=d_{A}\,\Tr_{A}((\sigma^{T}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}),\quad\rho_{AB}=d_{A}\,(id\otimes\mathcal{N}^{A\to B})(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|).

(19)

For any completely positive map
\mathcal{N}
, one can define its maximal coherent information by

\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\mathcal{N}):=\max\left\{I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}B}}:\omega_{A^{\prime}B}=\frac{(id\otimes\mathcal{N})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{A^{\prime}A})}{\Tr((id \otimes\mc N)(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{A'A}))},\quad\forall|\psi\rangle_{A^{\prime}A}\right\}.

(20)

Proof of Proposition
II.4
.

Recall that there is a one-to-one correspondence (up to normalization) between pure states
|\psi\rangle_{A^{\prime}A}
on
A^{\prime}A
and operators
K:A\to A^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle_{A^{\prime}A}=(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{A})|\Phi\rangle_{AA}.

Note that

\displaystyle(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}=(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})(id\otimes\mathcal{N})(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}=(id\otimes\mathcal{N})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{A^{\prime}A})),

thus using the definitions (
20
) and (
18
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma II.6
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite state. Then we have,
\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=0\iff D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=0

Proof.

As
\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})\geq D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})\geq 0
, it trivially follows that
\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=0\implies D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=0
. To show the other way around, assume that
\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})>0
. Therefore, there exists
K:A\to A^{\prime}
such that

I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}B}}>0;\qquad\omega_{A^{\prime}B}=\frac{1}{\Tr( (K^\dagger K \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})}(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})^{\dagger}

Now choose
0<c\leq 1
such that
E_{0}:=c(K^{\dagger}K)\leq\mathbb{I}_{A}
. We further perform the following spectral decomposition

\mathbb{I}_{A}-E_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{dim}A}E_{k}

Here
E_{k}
for
k\geq 1
are rank-1 positive operators with
0\leq\mathrm{Tr}(E_{k})\leq 1
. Firstly, we note that
\{E_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\mathrm{dim}A}
forms a POVM, therefore defines an instrument,
\mathcal{T}:A\to A^{\prime}M
where
M\cong\mathbb{C}^{\rm{dim}A+1}
. Secondly, for
k\geq 1
, define Kraus operators,
K_{k}:=U\sqrt{E_{k}}=\lvert\beta\rangle\!\langle\alpha\rvert
for some vectors
\lvert\alpha\rangle\in A,\lvert\beta\rangle\in A^{\prime}
, then the state

\omega^{(k)}_{A^{\prime}B}:=\lvert\beta\rangle\!\langle\beta\rvert_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{1}{\langle\alpha\rvert\rho_{A}\lvert\alpha\rangle}\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\left[(\lvert\alpha\rangle\!\langle\alpha\rvert\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}\right]

is a product state on
A^{\prime}B
, i.e., having
I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\omega^{(k)}_{A^{\prime}B}}=0
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle I(A^{\prime}\rangle BM)_{\mathcal{T}(\rho)}=c\Tr( (K^\dagger K \otimes\mb I_B)\rho_{AB})\;I(A^{\prime}\rangle B)_{\omega_{A^{\prime}B}}>0\implies D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})>0

(21)

By contrapositivity this is equivalent to
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=0\implies\widehat{D}^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=0

∎

III
General principle of non-degradable states with
single-letter one-way distillable entanglement

It is well-known that the one-way distillable entanglement of (anti-)degradable states has a single-letter expression (see
[
14
]
) and is equal to the coherent information. Moreover, two-way (thus one-way) distillable entanglement of PPT states is known to be zero. In this section, we introduce three distinct classes of non-degradable states that exhibit additivity in the one-way distillable entanglement. The first class relies on a weaker notion of degradable states. The second class concerns with states having an useless component. Thirdly, we propose a special class of generalised direct sum states which has a single-letter one-way distillable entanglement.

III-A

States with weaker notion of degradability

Motivated from previous work
[
17
]
, we propose the following two notions of weaker degradability

Definition III.1
.

We say that the bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
with purification
\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE}
is

•

less noisy at level
n
, if for any quantum instrument
\mathcal{T}^{A^{n}\to A^{\prime}M}
, for the state
\omega_{A^{\prime}B^{n}E^{n}}=\mathcal{T}^{A^{n}\to A^{\prime}M}(\phi_{ABE}^{\otimes n})
, we have

I(M;B^{n})\geq I(M;E^{n}).

(22)

We say
\rho_{AB}
is regularized less noisy, if (
22
) holds for any
n\geq 1
.

•

informationally degradable, if for any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}^{A\to A^{\prime}}
, for the state
\omega_{A^{\prime}BE}=\mathcal{N}^{A\to A^{\prime}}(\phi_{ABE})
, we have

I(A^{\prime};B)\geq I(A^{\prime};E).

(23)

Proposition III.2
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a bipartite state which is less noisy at level
n\geq 1
. Then we have

D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=nD_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB})=n\,I(A\rangle B).

(24)

Thus if
\rho_{AB}
is regularized less noisy, we have
D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB})
.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 1
and suppose the optimal quantum instrument for
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
is given by
\mathcal{T}^{A^{n}\to A^{\prime}M}
. Denote
V:A^{n}\to A^{\prime}MN
is the isometry of
\mathcal{T}
, defined as in (
11
). Via Lemma
II.1
and the optimality of the quantum instrument,

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{AB})=S(B^{n}M)_{\omega}-S(E^{n}M)_{\omega},\quad\lvert\omega\rangle_{A^{\prime}MNB^{n}E^{n}}:=V^{A^{n}\to A^{\prime}MN}\lvert\phi\rangle^{\otimes n}_{ABE}.

Note that
I(M;B^{n})_{\omega}\geq I(M;E^{n})_{\omega}
is equivalent to
S(B^{n}M)_{\omega}-S(E^{n}M)_{\omega}\leq S(B^{n})_{\omega}-S(E^{n})_{\omega}
which implies

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{AB})\leq S(B^{n})_{\omega}-S(E^{n})_{\omega}=I(A^{n}\rangle B^{n})_{\rho^{\otimes n}}\;=\;n\,I(A\rangle B)_{\rho}.

where the last two equalities follow from the additivity of von Neumann entropy over the tensor products:
S(B^{n})_{\rho^{\otimes n}}=n\,S(B)_{\rho}
and
S(A^{n}B^{n})_{\rho^{\otimes n}}=n\,S(AB)_{\rho}
, hence
I(A^{n}\rangle B^{n})_{\rho^{\otimes n}}=S(B^{n})-S(A^{n}B^{n})=n\,I(A\rangle B)_{\rho}
. We conclude the proof by recalling that
n\,I(A\rangle B)_{\rho}
is a lower bound for
D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
.

∎

Proposition III.3
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}
be informationally degradable. Then for all
n,k\geq 0
,

D_{\to}^{(1)}\!\big(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\!\otimes\!\sigma_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}^{\otimes k}\big)\;=\;n\,D_{\to}^{(1)}(\rho_{AB})\;+\;k\,D_{\to}^{(1)}(\sigma_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}})=n\,I(A\rangle B)_{\rho}+k\,I(\widetilde{A}\rangle\widetilde{B})_{\sigma}.

(25)

In particular, informationally degradable states are single-letter for one-way distillable entanglement and are additive under tensor products.

Proof.

Let
\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE}
,
\lvert\widetilde{\phi}\rangle_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}\widetilde{E}}
be the purification states of
\rho,\sigma
respectively. Fix
n,k\geq 0
(
0
means empty here), suppose

\mathcal{T}^{A^{n}\widetilde{A}^{k}\to A^{\prime}M}

is the optimal quantum instrument for
D_{\to}^{(1)}\!\big(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\!\otimes\!\sigma_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}^{\otimes k}\big)
. Denote
V:A^{n}\widetilde{A}^{k}\to A^{\prime}MN
is the isometry of
\mathcal{T}
, defined as in (
11
). Via Lemma
II.1
and the optimality of the quantum instrument, we have

\displaystyle D_{\to}^{(1)}\!\big(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\!\otimes\!\sigma_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}^{\otimes k}\big)=S(B^{n}\widetilde{B}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(E^{n}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega},

where

\displaystyle\lvert\omega\rangle_{A^{\prime}MNB^{n}\widetilde{B}^{k}E^{n}\widetilde{E}^{k}}:=V^{A^{n}\widetilde{A}^{k}\to A^{\prime}MN}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B^{n}\widetilde{B}^{k}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{E^{n}\widetilde{E}^{k}}\left(\lvert\phi\rangle_{ABE}^{\otimes n}\otimes\lvert\widetilde{\phi}\rangle^{\otimes k}_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}\widetilde{E}}\right).

(26)

Now denote

\displaystyle B^{n}=B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n},\quad\widetilde{B}^{k}=\widetilde{B}_{1}\widetilde{B}_{2}\cdots\widetilde{B}_{k},

and similar for
E^{n},\widetilde{E}^{k}
. Then via telescoping argument,

\displaystyle S(B^{n}\widetilde{B}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(E^{n}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}

\displaystyle=S(B^{n}\widetilde{B}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(B^{n}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}+S(B^{n}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(E^{n}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(S(\widetilde{B}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)_{\omega}-S(\widetilde{E}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)_{\omega}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(S(B_{i}V_{i}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(E_{i}V_{i}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}\right),

where
V_{i},\widetilde{V}_{j}
are
n-1
,
k-1
subsystems defined by

\displaystyle V_{i}=\begin{cases}B_{2}\cdots B_{n},&i=1,\\[2.0pt]
E_{1}\cdots E_{i-1}\,B_{i+1}\cdots B_{n},&2\leq i\leq n-1,\\[2.0pt]
E_{1}\cdots E_{n-1},&i=n.\end{cases},\quad\widetilde{V}_{j}=\begin{cases}\widetilde{B}_{2}\cdots\widetilde{B}_{k},&j=1,\\[2.0pt]
\widetilde{E}_{1}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{j-1}\,\widetilde{B}_{j+1}\cdots\widetilde{B}_{k},&2\leq j\leq k-1,\\[2.0pt]
\widetilde{E}_{1}\cdots\widetilde{E}_{k-1},&j=k.\end{cases}

Now we claim that

\displaystyle S(\widetilde{B}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)_{\omega}-S(\widetilde{E}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)_{\omega}\leq S(\widetilde{B}_{j})_{\omega}-S(\widetilde{E}_{j})_{\omega},\quad S(B_{i}V_{i}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(E_{i}V_{i}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}\leq S(B_{i})_{\omega}-S(E_{i})_{\omega},\ \forall i,j.

(27)

In fact, for each
1\leq j\leq k
, one can construct a quantum channel
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{\widetilde{A}_{j}\to\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{N}_{j}}(\rho_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}):=\Tr_{(\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)^{c}}\bigg[V^{A^{n}\widetilde{A}^{k}\to A^{\prime}MN}(\lvert\phi\rangle\!\langle\phi\rvert_{ABE}^{\otimes n}\otimes\rho_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}\otimes\lvert\widetilde{\phi}\rangle\!\langle\widetilde{\phi}\rvert^{\otimes(k-1)}_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}\widetilde{E}})(V^{A^{n}\widetilde{A}^{k}\to A^{\prime}MN})^{\dagger}\bigg],

where
(\widetilde{A}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)^{c}
denotes the complement of
\widetilde{A}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{\widetilde{A}_{j}\to\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M}(\lvert\widetilde{\phi}\rangle\!\langle\widetilde{\phi}\rvert_{\widetilde{A}_{j}\widetilde{B}_{j}\widetilde{E}_{j}})=\omega_{\widetilde{B}_{j}\widetilde{E}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M},

where
\omega_{\widetilde{B}_{j}\widetilde{E}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M}
is the reduced density of
\omega
defined in (
26
). Since
\lvert\widetilde{\phi}\rangle_{\widetilde{A}_{j}\widetilde{B}_{j}\widetilde{E}_{j}}
is the purification of the informational degradable state
\sigma_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}
, via (
23
), we have

\displaystyle I(\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M;\widetilde{B}_{j})\geq I(\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M;\widetilde{E}_{j}),

concluding the claim that
S(\widetilde{B}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)_{\omega}-S(\widetilde{E}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)_{\omega}\leq S(\widetilde{B}_{j})_{\omega}-S(\widetilde{E}_{j})_{\omega}
. Similarly, we have

S(B_{i}V_{i}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(E_{i}V_{i}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}\leq S(B_{i})_{\omega}-S(E_{i})_{\omega}.

Therefore, one has

\displaystyle S(B^{n}\widetilde{B}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(E^{n}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(S(\widetilde{B}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)_{\omega}-S(\widetilde{E}_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j}B^{n}M)_{\omega}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(S(B_{i}V_{i}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}-S(E_{i}V_{i}\widetilde{E}^{k}M)_{\omega}\right)

\displaystyle\leq k(S(\widetilde{B})_{\sigma}-S(\widetilde{E})_{\sigma})+n(S(B)_{\rho}-S(E)_{\rho})=k\,I(\widetilde{A}\rangle\widetilde{B})_{\sigma}+n\,I(A\rangle B)_{\rho}.

We conclude the proof by recalling that
D_{\to}^{(1)}\!\big(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}\!\otimes\!\sigma_{\widetilde{A}\widetilde{B}}^{\otimes k}\big)\geq k\,I(\widetilde{A}\rangle\widetilde{B})_{\sigma}+n\,I(A\rangle B)_{\rho}
.
∎

III-B

Non-degradable states with useless component

In this subsection we exhibit a family of generally non-degradable states whose one-way distillable entanglement is single-letter. Heuristically, these states are orthogonally flagged mixtures on Alice, so the additivity inherited from the components persists even though degradability may fail. The formal statement is:

Theorem III.4
.

Let
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
be bipartite states on
AB
satisfying

D^{(1)}_{\to}(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{w_{i}})\;\leq\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{w_{i}})\;=\;(n-|w^{n}|)\,D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{0})+|w^{n}|\,D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{1})

(28)

for all
w^{n}=(w_{1},\cdots w_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, assume
\rho_{0}^{A}\perp\rho_{1}^{A}
. Then for all
p\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle D_{\to}\big(p\rho_{0}+(1-p)\rho_{1}\big)=D^{(1)}_{\to}\big(p\rho_{0}+(1-p)\rho_{1}\big)=pD_{\to}(\rho_{0})+(1-p)D_{\to}(\rho_{1}).

To prove the above theorem, we need the following lemmas:

Lemma III.5
.

Let
\sigma,\tau
be bipartite states on
AB
such that
\sigma^{A}\perp\tau^{A}
, i.e.,
\mathrm{supp}(\sigma^{A})\perp\mathrm{supp}(\tau^{A})
. Then for
p\in[0,1]
, we have

D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho)\geq\;p\,D^{(1)}_{\to}(\sigma)+(1-p)D^{(1)}_{\to}(\tau),\quad D_{\to}(\rho)\geq\;p\,D_{\to}(\sigma)+(1-p)D_{\to}(\tau).

(29)

Proof.

Define
A_{0}:=\mathrm{supp}(\sigma^{A}),A_{1}:=\mathrm{supp}(\tau^{A})
, we have
\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)\subseteq A_{0}B,\ \mathrm{supp}(\tau)\subseteq A_{1}B
. Define an isometry
V:A\to X\otimes A^{\prime}
:

V\big|_{A_{0}}=|0\rangle_{X}\otimes id_{A_{0}},\qquad V\big|_{A_{1}}=|1\rangle_{X}\otimes id_{A_{1}},

where
X=\mathrm{span}\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}
and
A^{\prime}
is a copy of
A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
.
Let

\omega\;:=\;(V\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\,\rho\,(V^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\;=\;p\,|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{X}\otimes\sigma\;+\;(1-p)\,|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{X}\otimes\tau.

Via (
14
), we have
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\omega)=D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho),\ D_{\to}(\omega)=D_{\to}(\rho)
. It remains to show that

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\omega)=D^{(1)}_{\to}(p\,|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{X}\otimes\sigma\;+\;(1-p)\,|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{X}\otimes\tau)\geq p\,D^{(1)}_{\to}(\sigma)+(1-p)D^{(1)}_{\to}(\tau),

(30)

\displaystyle D_{\to}(\omega)=D_{\to}(p\,|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{X}\otimes\sigma\;+\;(1-p)\,|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{X}\otimes\tau)\geq p\,D_{\to}(\sigma)+(1-p)D_{\to}(\tau).

(31)

To show (
30
), suppose
\mathcal{T}^{0}:A\to M_{0}A_{0}^{\prime},\ \mathcal{T}^{1}:A\to M_{1}A_{1}^{\prime}
are the optimal quantum instruments for
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\sigma)
and
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\tau)
, respectively. Then one finds the quantum instrument
\mathcal{T}=\lvert 0\rangle\!\langle 0\rvert\cdot\lvert 0\rangle\!\langle 0\rvert\otimes\mathcal{T}^{0}+\lvert 1\rangle\!\langle 1\rvert\cdot\lvert 1\rangle\!\langle 1\rvert\otimes\mathcal{T}^{1}
provides a lower bound of
D^{(1)}_{\to}(p\,|0\rangle\!\langle 0|_{X}\otimes\sigma\;+\;(1-p)\,|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{X}\otimes\tau)
, which is
p\,D^{(1)}_{\to}(\sigma)+(1-p)D^{(1)}_{\to}(\tau)
.

To show (
31
), for each
n
,
\omega^{\otimes n}
is block-diagonal in the computational basis of
X^{n}
:

\omega^{\otimes n}\;=\;\sum_{x^{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}p^{N_{0}(x^{n})}(1-p)^{N_{1}(x^{n})}\,|x^{n}\rangle\!\langle x^{n}|_{X^{n}}\ \otimes\ \sigma^{\otimes N_{0}(x^{n})}\otimes\tau^{\otimes N_{1}(x^{n})},

where
N_{0}(x^{n})
and
N_{1}(x^{n})=n-N_{0}(x^{n})
denote the type counts (numbers of 0’s and 1’s).
Let
\Delta
be the completely dephasing channel on
X^{n}
in this basis; then
\Delta(\omega^{\otimes n})=\omega^{\otimes n}
.
Therefore prepending
\Delta
to any protocol does not change its action on
\omega^{\otimes n}
.
Hence, without loss of generality, any protocol may be assumed to
measure

X^{n}
in this basis at the beginning and obtain the classical string
x^{n}
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
By the definition of
D_{\to}(\sigma),\ D_{\to}(\tau)
, there exists
K\geq 1
such that for all
N\geq K
there are one-way protocols
\Pi^{\sigma}_{N},\Pi^{\tau}_{N}
on
\sigma^{\otimes N}
and
\tau^{\otimes N}
producing
(N,M^{\sigma}_{N},\varepsilon)
and
(N,M^{\tau}_{N},\varepsilon)
with

\frac{1}{N}\log M^{\sigma}_{N}\;\geq\;D_{\to}(\sigma)-\varepsilon,\quad\frac{1}{N}\log M^{\tau}_{N}\;\geq\;D_{\to}(\tau)-\varepsilon.

(32)

On input
\omega^{\otimes n}
, measure
X^{n}
to obtain
x^{n}
and the conditional state

\sigma^{\otimes N_{0}(x^{n})}\otimes\tau^{\otimes N_{1}(x^{n})}
.
If
N_{0}(x^{n}),N_{1}(x^{n})\geq K
, run
\Pi^{\sigma}_{N_{0}(x^{n})}
on the
\sigma
-positions and
\Pi^{\tau}_{N_{1}(x^{n})}
on the
\tau
-positions; otherwise output a trivial product (zero ebits). For large enough
n
,

\displaystyle n\,D^{(n,\varepsilon)}_{\to}(\omega)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x^{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n},N_{0}(x^{n}),N_{1}(x^{n})\geq K}p^{N_{0}(x^{n})}(1-p)^{N_{1}(x^{n})}\left(\log M^{\sigma}_{N_{0}(x^{n})}+\log M^{\tau}_{N_{1}(x^{n})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x^{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n},N_{0}(x^{n}),N_{1}(x^{n})\geq K}p^{N_{0}(x^{n})}(1-p)^{N_{1}(x^{n})}\left(N_{0}(x^{n})(D_{\to}(\sigma)-\varepsilon)+N_{1}(x^{n})(D_{\to}(\tau)-\varepsilon)\right),

where for the first inequality, we use the one-way protocols consisting of
\Pi^{\sigma}_{N_{0}(x^{n})},\Pi^{\tau}_{N_{1}(x^{n})}
depending on the measurement outcome on
X^{n}
, and the second inequality follows from (
32
). Finally, using

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n},N_{0}(x^{n}),N_{1}(x^{n})\geq K}p^{N_{0}(x^{n})}(1-p)^{N_{1}(x^{n})}N_{0}(x^{n})=pn(1-o_{n}(1)),

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n},N_{0}(x^{n}),N_{1}(x^{n})\geq K}p^{N_{0}(x^{n})}(1-p)^{N_{1}(x^{n})}N_{1}(x^{n})=(1-p)n(1-o_{n}(1)),

we have

\displaystyle D^{(n,\varepsilon)}_{\to}(\omega)\geq p(1-o_{n}(1))(D_{\to}(\sigma)-\varepsilon)+(1-p)(1-o_{n}(1))(D_{\to}(\tau)-\varepsilon)

Let
n\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
, by definition (
8
) we have

\displaystyle D_{\to}(\rho)=D_{\to}(\omega)\geq\;p\,D_{\to}(\sigma)+(1-p)D_{\to}(\tau).

∎

For the other direction, we use the following lemma, which is Proposition 2.7 in
[
14
]
:

Lemma III.6
.

Let
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
be bipartite states on

The minimization problem (

To prove the above result, we first briefly review the majorization of matrices. For a Hermitian matrix

\lambda(X)=(\lambda_{1}^{\downarrow}(X),\dots,\lambda_{d}^{\downarrow}(X))

For two vectors

by

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{\downarrow}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}^{\downarrow}\quad(k=1,\dots,d-1),\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}.

A function

The Shannon entropy

is Schur concave; hence the von Neumann entropy

is Schur concave in the spectrum.

For a positive semidefinite matrix

to be the diagonal matrix (in some fixed reference basis) whose diagonal entries are the eigenvalues of

written in non-increasing order.

We will use the following known tensor rearrangement majorization principle, proved in

Lemma III.9

Let

(40)

IV-C

Generalized direct sum channels and their corresponding states

In this subsection, motivated by
[
20
]
, we present a class of quantum channels and states with single-letter expression for capacities and one-way distillable entanglement. We first show that this class of channels can have single-letter quantum capacity using the spin alignment phenomenon.

We say a completely positive map
\Phi
has a generalized (partially coherent) direct sum structure if

\displaystyle\Phi\begin{pmatrix}X_{00}&X_{01}\\
X_{10}&X_{11}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{0}(X_{00})&\Phi_{01}(X_{01})\\
\Phi_{10}(X_{10})&\Phi_{1}(X_{11})\end{pmatrix}

where
\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}
are completely positive. It is shown in
[
8
]
that
\Phi
admits partially coherent direct sum structure if and only if the Kraus operators of
\Phi
are block diagonal, i.e.,
\Phi(X)=\sum_{k}E_{k}XE_{k}^{\dagger}
with

E_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}E_{k}^{0}&0\\
0&E_{k}^{1}\end{pmatrix}.

(51)

A key observation is that the optimal states achieving the maximal coherent information of
\Phi^{\otimes n}
can be chosen to be block diagonal.

Lemma IV.1
.

Suppose
\Phi
is a generalized direct sum channel with Kraus operators
\begin{pmatrix}E_{k}^{0}&0\\
0&E_{k}^{1}\end{pmatrix}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Phi^{\otimes n})=\max\Big\{I_{c}(\rho_{n},\Phi^{\otimes n}):\ \rho_{n}=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}p_{\vec{x}}\ |\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{x}|\otimes\rho_{\vec{x}},\ \rho_{\vec{x}}\in\mathcal{D}_{d_{0}^{|\vec{x}|}d_{1}^{n-|\vec{x}|}},\ \sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}p_{\vec{x}}=1\Big\}.

Proof.

Using the direct sum structure of the Hilbert space, any
\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{(d_{0}+d_{1})^{n}}
can be decomposed as

\rho_{n}=\sum_{\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}|\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{y}|\otimes\rho_{\vec{x},\vec{y}},\quad\rho_{\vec{x},\vec{y}}\in\mathbb{M}_{d_{0}^{|\vec{x}|}d_{1}^{n-|\vec{x}|}\times d_{0}^{|\vec{y}|}d_{1}^{n-|\vec{y}|}}.

Define the pinching map (conditional expectation onto block-diagonal entries)

\mathcal{P}(\rho_{n}):=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}(|\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{x}|\otimes\mathbb{I})\ \rho_{n}\ (|\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{x}|\otimes\mathbb{I})=\sum_{\vec{x}}|\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{x}|\otimes\rho_{\vec{x},\vec{x}}.

Step 1: the complementary output is unchanged by pinching.

Since each Kraus operator of
\Phi
is block diagonal, every Kraus operator of
\Phi^{\otimes n}
is also block diagonal with respect to the decomposition indexed by
\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. Hence, for
\vec{x}\neq\vec{y}
one has

\Tr\!\big(E_{\vec{k}}\ (|\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{y}|\otimes\rho_{\vec{x},\vec{y}})\ E_{\vec{\ell}}^{\dagger}\big)=0,

and therefore the complementary channel depends only on the diagonal blocks:

(\Phi^{c})^{\otimes n}(\rho_{n})=(\Phi^{c})^{\otimes n}(\mathcal{P}(\rho_{n})).

Step 2: the main output entropy increases under pinching.

Set
\omega:=\Phi^{\otimes n}(\rho_{n})
and
\omega^{\prime}:=\Phi^{\otimes n}(\mathcal{P}(\rho_{n}))
.
By construction,
\omega^{\prime}
is obtained from
\omega
by pinching with respect to the orthogonal projections
\{|\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{x}|\otimes\mathbb{I}\}_{\vec{x}}
on the output space. Equivalently,
\omega^{\prime}
is a convex combination of unitary conjugations of
\omega
(hence the spectrum of
\omega^{\prime}
majorizes that of
\omega
), and since the von Neumann entropy is Schur concave we get

S(\Phi^{\otimes n}(\rho_{n}))=S(\omega)\leq S(\omega^{\prime})=S(\Phi^{\otimes n}(\mathcal{P}(\rho_{n}))).

This is exactly the majorization/pinching trick
[
7
]
.

Combining Step 1 and Step 2,

I_{c}(\rho_{n},\Phi^{\otimes n})=S(\Phi^{\otimes n}(\rho_{n}))-S((\Phi^{c})^{\otimes n}(\rho_{n}))\leq S(\Phi^{\otimes n}(\mathcal{P}(\rho_{n})))-S((\Phi^{c})^{\otimes n}(\mathcal{P}(\rho_{n})))=I_{c}(\mathcal{P}(\rho_{n}),\Phi^{\otimes n}),

so an optimizer exists among block-diagonal states of the claimed form.
∎

Now we show the following using the spin alignment phenomenon, which generalizes the result in
[
19
]
to different dimensions.

Proposition IV.2
.

Assume
\Phi_{0}(X)=\Tr(X)\mathbb{I}_{d_{0}^{\prime}}/d_{0}^{\prime}

Step 1: identify the spin-alignment structure on the complementary channel.

\widetilde{\Phi}^{c}(\rho)=p_{0}\Big(\frac{1}{d_{0}^{\prime}}P_{d_{0}^{\prime}}\Big)\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{A_{0}}\;+\;p_{1}\ \widehat{\rho}_{A_{1}}\otimes\Big(\frac{1}{d_{1}^{\prime}}P_{d_{1}^{\prime}}\Big).

(55)

Step 2: reduce the

By Lemma

n
we may restrict to inputs of the form

\rho_{n}=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}p_{\vec{x}}\ |\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{x}|\otimes\rho_{\vec{x}}.

For our specific choice (
52
) (hence also for
\widetilde{\Phi}
), the main output
\widetilde{\Phi}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{n})
depends on
\rho_{\vec{x}}
only through the weights
p_{\vec{x}}
, because each diagonal branch is completely depolarizing. Therefore, for fixed
\{p_{\vec{x}}\}
, maximizing
I_{c}(\rho_{n},\widetilde{\Phi}^{\otimes n})
is equivalent to minimizing the entropy of the complementary output

(\widetilde{\Phi}^{c})^{\otimes n}(\rho_{n})=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}p_{\vec{x}}\ (\widetilde{\Phi}^{c})^{\otimes n}\big(|\vec{x}\rangle\langle\vec{x}|\otimes\rho_{\vec{x}}\big),

and each summand has the spin-alignment structure inherited from (
55
) on every tensor factor.

Invoking the (multi-copy) Spin Alignment Conjecture for the entropy-minimization problem associated with (
55
), we may choose an optimizer such that, for each
\vec{x}
, the state
\rho_{\vec{x}}
is supported on the tensor product of one-dimensional subspaces spanned by a fixed unit vector in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}}
and a fixed unit vector in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}
(i.e. the “aligned spins”). Equivalently, there exists an optimizer supported on

\Big(\mathrm{span}\{\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert d_{1}\rangle\}\Big)^{\otimes n}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}})^{\otimes n},

(56)

where
\lvert 0\rangle
denotes a unit vector in the first summand
\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}}
and
\lvert d_{1}\rangle
denotes a unit vector in the second summand
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}
(after fixing an orthonormal basis).
Consequently, for every
n
,

\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\widetilde{\Phi}^{\otimes n})=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Psi^{\otimes n}),

(57)

where
\Psi
is the restriction of
\widetilde{\Phi}
to the two-dimensional input subspace
\mathrm{span}\{\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert d_{1}\rangle\}
.

Step 3: the restricted channel
\Psi
is degradable.

On
\mathrm{span}\{\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert d_{1}\rangle\}
, both diagonal blocks
X_{00}
and
X_{11}
are scalars, hence by (
54

\Psi^{c}(\cdot)
depends only on the block traces (and ignores the off-diagonal entry).
Let
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
be the orthogonal projections onto the two output summands of
\widetilde{\Phi}
.
Define a CPTP map
\mathcal{D}
on the output space of
\Psi
by

\mathcal{D}(Y):=\Tr(P_0 Y)\Big(\frac{1}{d_{0}^{\prime}}P_{d_{0}^{\prime}}\Big)\otimes\lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert\;+\;\Tr(P_1 Y)\ \lvert d_{1}\rangle\langle d_{1}\rvert\otimes\Big(\frac{1}{d_{1}^{\prime}}P_{d_{1}^{\prime}}\Big).

(58)

Since
\Tr(P_0\Psi(\rho))=\Tr(\rho_{A_0})
and
\Tr(P_1\Psi(\rho))=\Tr(\rho_{A_1})
, and since
\Psi^{c}
depends only on these two weights on the restricted subspace, one checks directly from (

\Psi^{c}=\mathcal{D}\circ\Psi,

i.e.
\Psi
is degradable. Therefore
\mathcal{Q}(\Psi)=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Psi)
.

Step 4: conclude single-letter capacity for
\Phi
.

Using (

) and degradability of
\Psi
,

\mathcal{Q}(\Phi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Phi^{\otimes n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\widetilde{\Phi}^{\otimes n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Psi^{\otimes n})=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Psi)=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\widetilde{\Phi})=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Phi),

which proves
\mathcal{Q}(\Phi)=\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}(\Phi)

Now we provide the proof of the single-letterization of one-way distillable entanglement for a class of generalised direct sum (GDS) states as an example case of the Spin alignment phenomena.

Proposition IV.3
.

Let
A,B\cong\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}+d_{1}}
and define

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{2d_{0}d_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{0}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}-1}\left(\lvert i\rangle\lvert j\rangle+\lvert j+d_{0}\rangle\lvert i+d_{1}\rangle\right)\left(\langle i\rvert\langle j\rvert+\langle j+d_{0}\rvert\langle i+d_{1}\rvert\right).

Then
\rho_{AB}
has single-letter one-way distillable entanglement:

\displaystyle D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}).

Proof.

We prove that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=n\,D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}),

which implies the claim by regularization.

Step 0: a canonical instrument.

For
0\leq i<d_{0}
and
0\leq j<d_{1}
define the (orthonormal) vectors

\displaystyle\lvert\psi_{ij}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl(\lvert i\rangle\lvert j\rangle+\lvert j+d_{0}\rangle\lvert i+d_{1}\rangle\Bigr),

so that
\rho_{AB}=\frac{1}{d_{0}d_{1}}\sum_{i,j}\lvert\psi_{ij}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{ij}\rvert
is the maximally mixed state on the span of
\{\lvert\psi_{ij}\rangle\}_{i,j}
.
Define an instrument
\mathcal{T}:A\to AM
with Kraus operators

\displaystyle K_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}}}\lvert i\rangle\!\langle i\rvert+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\lvert d_{0}+j\rangle\!\langle d_{0}+j\rvert,\qquad 0\leq i<d_{0},\;0\leq j<d_{1}.

A direct computation gives
\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}^{\dagger}K_{ij}=\mathbb{I}_{A}
, hence
\mathcal{T}
is trace-preserving and
\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n}
is a valid candidate for
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})
.

Step 1: reduction to block-diagonal POVM elements (pinching).

Decompose
A=\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}
and define projectors

\displaystyle\Pi_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{0}-1}\lvert i\rangle\!\langle i\rvert,\qquad\Pi_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}-1}\lvert j+d_{0}\rangle\!\langle j+d_{0}\rvert.

For
\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, set

\displaystyle\Pi_{\vec{x}}=\Pi_{x_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Pi_{x_{n}}.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}:A^{\otimes n}\to A^{\otimes n}M
be any trace-preserving instrument with Kraus operators
\{L_{m}\}_{m}
and POVM elements
E_{m}=L_{m}^{\dagger}L_{m}
.
Consider the pinched POVM elements

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{m}:=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m}\Pi_{\vec{x}}.

Since
\sum_{m}E_{m}=\mathbb{I}
, we still have
\sum_{m}\widetilde{E}_{m}=\mathbb{I}
, so
\{\widetilde{E}_{m}\}_{m}
comes from a trace-preserving instrument
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{1}
.

The GDS structure of
\rho_{AB}
implies that, conditioned on outcome
m
, the joint state of
B^{\otimes n}
and of a purification environment depends only on the diagonal blocks
\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m}\Pi_{\vec{x}}
and is insensitive to the off-diagonal blocks
\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m}\Pi_{\vec{y}}
with
\vec{x}\neq\vec{y}
.
Moreover, on the Bob system the replacement
E_{m}\mapsto\widetilde{E}_{m}
corresponds to pinching the post-measurement state in the direct-sum decomposition indexed by
\vec{x}
, which increases the entropy by the Ky Fan majorization trick
[
7
]
.
Since
I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)=S(B^{\otimes n}M)-S(E^{\otimes n}M)
for a purification, and the environment term is unchanged under the above replacement, we obtain

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{1}(\rho^{\otimes n})}\geq I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{1}(\rho^{\otimes n})}.

It therefore suffices to optimize over block-diagonal instruments.

Step 2: spin alignment within each block.

Fix a block-diagonal instrument (still denoted
\mathcal{T}_{1}
) with POVM elements
E_{m}=\sum_{\vec{x}}\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m}\Pi_{\vec{x}}
.
For each
m
and
\vec{x}
define the weights

\displaystyle\alpha_{m,\vec{x}}:=\Tr(\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_m\Pi_{\vec{x}})\geq 0.

For our state
\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
, the marginal on
B^{\otimes n}M
depends on
E_{m}
only through the collection
\{\alpha_{m,\vec{x}}\}_{\vec{x}}
(intuitively, within a fixed block
\vec{x}
the state is maximally mixed on the corresponding support, so only the total weight matters for Bob’s entropy).
Thus, keeping
\{\alpha_{m,\vec{x}}\}
fixed, to increase coherent information it is enough to
minimize
the entropy contribution coming from the environment.

By the (multi-copy) spin alignment property applied to the entropy-minimization problem induced by
\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
, the environment entropy is minimized when each positive block
\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m}\Pi_{\vec{x}}
is replaced by a rank-one projector with the same weight.
Concretely, define positive elements

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{m}=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\alpha_{m,\vec{x}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\lvert t(x_{i})\rangle\!\langle t(x_{i})\rvert,

where
t(0)=0
and
t(1)=d_{0}
.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{2}
denote the corresponding (possibly non–trace-preserving) instrument (as in Definition
II.3
). Then, by construction,

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{2}(\rho^{\otimes n})}\geq I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{1}(\rho^{\otimes n})}.

Step 3: reduction to a degradable tensor-power state.

Define

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}=(d_{0}d_{1})(K_{00}\otimes\mathbb{I})\rho_{AB}(K_{00}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbb{I}),\qquad\sigma=\sigma_{0}^{\otimes n}.

One verifies (see the appendix) that
\sigma_{0}
is a degradable state, hence so is
\sigma
.
Moreover, from the explicit form of
E^{\prime}_{m}
, one can construct an instrument
\mathcal{T}_{3}
acting on
\sigma
such that the classical-quantum branch structure matches that of
\mathcal{T}_{2}(\rho^{\otimes n})
and

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{2}(\rho^{\otimes n})}=I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{3}(\sigma)}.

Since
\sigma
is degradable, coherent information is maximized by the trivial instrument
[
14
]
. Therefore

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}\geq I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{3}(\sigma)}.

Combining inequalities gives the upper bound

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}\geq D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}).

(59)

Step 4: a matching lower bound from
\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n}
.

For all
x_{i}\in(0,\dots,d_{0}-1)
and
y_{i}\in(0,\dots,d_{1}-1)
, the normalized branch states

\displaystyle(d_{0}d_{1})^{n}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}(K_{x_{i}y_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{I})\rho_{AB}(K_{x_{i}y_{i}}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbb{I})

are related to
\sigma
by local unitaries on
A^{\otimes n}
and
B^{\otimes n}
(they simply relabel the basis vectors within each direct-sum block). Hence they have the same coherent information as
\sigma
, and these branches are precisely the outcomes of
\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n}
. Therefore

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}=I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n}(\rho^{\otimes n})}\leq D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}).

(60)

Combining the bounds yields

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}.

(61)

Since
\sigma=\sigma_{0}^{\otimes n}
,

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}=n\,I(A\rangle B)_{\sigma_{0}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=n\,D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}).

Taking the regularized limit yields

\displaystyle D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho_{AB}).

(62)

∎

Remark IV.4
.

Unlike the GDS channel setting, for general GDS states with different block dimensions on Bob (e.g.

V
Conclusion and Outlook

We identified new structural conditions under which one-way distillable entanglement admits a single-letter formula. In particular, we introduced the notions of
less noisy at level
n
and
informationally degradable
states, and showed that both classes are single-letter for
D_{\to}
. Informationally degradable states are moreover additive under tensor products, i.e., they exhibit strong additivity. These results demonstrate that single-letter behavior extends strictly beyond the degradable regime.

We further proved a general single-letter theorem for mixtures with orthogonal support on Alice’s side, introducing non-degradable states with “useless” components. This yields explicit examples where the distillable entanglement is simply the convex combination of the components, despite the absence of degradability.

A central conceptual ingredient is the spin-alignment phenomenon, which explains why entropy minimization for certain generalized direct-sum channels is achieved by locally aligned inputs. We established this effect for the single-copy von Neumann entropy and for arbitrary block length in the Rényi-2 case. Applying this principle, we showed that a class of generalized direct sum channels has single-letter quantum capacity, and that some particular case of the corresponding states have single-letter one-way distillable entanglement. However, we have found that even though numerical evidences for additivity of
D^{(1)}_{\to}
are present for certain states, proving additivity is difficult.

Our results reveal new mechanisms enforcing additivity and single-letter formulas beyond degradability. An important open problem is to prove the spin alignment conjecture with von-Neumann entropy for arbitrary tensor powers. Understanding the comparative additivity of
D^{(1)}_{\to}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}
remains to be explored. Finally, it remains to be understood whether the techniques developed here extend to two-way distillation or to other quantum resource theories.

Definition .2

Let us consider Hilbert spaces,
A\cong A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
and

\Phi_{i}:\mathcal{B}(A_{i})\to\mathcal{B}(B_{i})

\{F_{k}:=F^{(1)}_{k}\oplus F_{k}^{(2)}\}

\displaystyle J_{\Phi}=\sum_{k}\mathrm{vec}({F^{T}_{k}})\mathrm{vec}({F^{T}_{k}})^{\dagger}.

(67)

For non-zero maps,
\Tr{J_{\Phi}}\neq 0
. Therefore, we can define a corresponding GDS state

\mathcal{T}_{1}:A^{\otimes n}\to A^{\otimes n}M
with POVM elements

\displaystyle E_{m,n}=\sum_{\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\{0,\dots,d_{0}+d_{1}-1\}^{n}}\;e^{m}_{\vec{x},\vec{y}}\;\lvert\vec{x}\rangle\!\langle\vec{y}\rvert.

For all
\vec{x}\in\{0,\dots,d_{0}+d_{1}-1\}^{n}
, define
\vec{s}(\vec{x})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle s_{i}=\begin{cases}0,\quad x_{i}\in\{0,\dots,d_{1}-1\}\\
1,\quad x_{i}\in\{d_{1},\dots,d_{1}+d_{0}-1\}.\end{cases}

(71)

The
(\vec{x},\vec{x})
-th diagonal element of
\rho^{(m)}_{B^{\otimes n}}
is proportional to

\displaystyle\Tr\Pi_{\vec{s}(\vec{x})}E_{m,n}\Pi_{\vec{s}(\vec{x})}.

(72)

Here
\Pi_{\vec{x}}=\Pi_{x_{1}}\otimes\Pi_{x_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\Pi_{x_{n}}
with
\Pi_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{d_{0}-1}\lvert j\rangle\!\langle j\rvert
and
\Pi_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}-1}\lvert j\rangle\!\langle j\rvert
.

The state of
E^{\otimes n}
is proportional to

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}_{x_{i}}(\mathscr{E}_{\vec{x}}),

(73)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\vec{x}}=\Pi_{\vec{x}}E^{T}_{m,n}\Pi_{\vec{x}}.

(74)

Here we treat
\mathscr{E}_{\vec{x}}
to be a positive operator on the space
\bigotimes_{i}\mathbb{C}^{d_{x_{i}}}
. Furthermore, we define,
\mathcal{N}_{0}:B(\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}})\to B(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}})
and
\mathcal{N}_{1}:B(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}})\to B(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{0}})
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{0}(\rho)=\mathbb{I}_{d_{1}}\otimes\rho;\qquad\mathcal{N}_{1}(\sigma)=\sigma\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d_{0}}.

(75)

Note that the state of the environment does not depend on the the
off-diagonal blocks
of the POVM elements, i.e., on
\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m,n}\Pi_{\vec{y}}
for
\vec{x}\neq\vec{y}
. As these blocks appear only in the off-diagonal entries of
B^{\otimes n}
, we can choose POVM elements
E_{m,n}
such that

\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m,n}\Pi_{\vec{y}}=\delta_{\vec{x},\vec{y}}\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m,n}\Pi_{\vec{x}}

without decreasing the coherent information.

Step 2
(Spin-alignment phenomena):
Furthermore, the spin alignment conjecture
III.7
implies that the entropy of the environment is minimum when replace the POVM elements
E_{m,n}
by positive elements

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{m,n}=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{\otimes n}}\Tr(\Pi_{\vec{x}} E_{m,n} \Pi_{\vec{x}})\;\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\lvert t(x_{i})\rangle\!\langle t(x_{i})\rvert,

(76)

where
t(0)=0
and
t(1)=d_{0}
. Let us denote the corresponding trace non-preserving instrument (which we have introduced in the definition
II.3
), by
\mathcal{T}_{2}:A^{\otimes n}\to A^{\otimes n}M
. Therefore, so far we have proven that for any trace-preserving instrument,
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, we can find a (possibly trace non-preserving) instrument,
\mathcal{T}_{2}
such that

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{2}(\rho^{\otimes n})}\geq I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{1}(\rho^{\otimes n})}.

(77)

Step 3
(Matching upper and lower bound on
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n})
)
: Now, we consider the state

\displaystyle\sigma=\sigma^{\otimes n}_{0}=(d_{0}d_{1})^{n}\;((K_{00}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K_{00}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}))^{\otimes n},

(78)

and the instrument
\mathcal{T}_{3}:A^{\otimes n}\to A^{\otimes n}M
given by the POVM elements:

\displaystyle F_{m}=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\frac{1}{d_{0}^{N_{0}(\vec{x})}}\frac{1}{d_{1}^{N_{1}(\vec{x})}}\Tr\Pi_{\vec{x}}E_{m,n}\Pi_{\vec{x}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\lvert t(x_{i})\rangle\!\langle t(x_{i})\rvert;\qquad F=\mathbb{I}_{A^{\otimes n}}-\sum_{m}F_{m}.

It immediately follows that

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{2}(\rho^{\otimes n})}=I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{3}(\sigma)}.

(79)

Now
\sigma_{0}
is a degradable state, as can be seen as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma^{AB}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}}}\lvert 0,0\rangle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\lvert d_{0},d_{1}\rangle\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}}}\langle 0,0\rvert+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\langle d_{0},d_{1}\rvert\right)+\frac{1}{2d_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{1}-1}\lvert 0,j\rangle\!\langle 0,j\rvert+\frac{1}{2d_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{0}-1}\lvert d_{0},i+d_{1}\rangle\!\langle d_{0},i+d_{1}\rvert

(80)

For any purification
\lvert\sigma_{0}\rangle_{ABE}
, by applying a fully amplitude damping channel from
\lvert d_{1}\rangle\to\lvert 0\rangle
on
B
alone, we obtain a state on
AB
that is equivalent to the state
\sigma^{AE}_{0}
, upto local isometries on
B
. Therefore,
\sigma=\sigma_{0}^{\otimes n}
is degradable as well.

We know that for degradable states, the trivial instrument achieves the optimal coherent information. We get the following chain of inequality,

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}\geq I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{3}(\sigma)}=I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{2}(\rho^{\otimes n})}\geq I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}_{1}(\rho^{\otimes n})}

(81)

As
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is any trace-preserving instrument, this inequality implies that

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}\geq D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{AB})

(82)

Now we note that for all
x_{i}\in(0,\dots,d_{0}-1)
and
y_{i}\in(0,\dots,d_{1}-1)
, the states

(d_{0}d_{1})^{n}\;\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}(K_{x_{i}y_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho_{AB}(K_{x_{i}y_{i}}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B})

are related to
\sigma
via local unitaries on
A^{\otimes n}
and
B^{\otimes n}
, and there are
(d_{0}d_{1})^{n}
numbers of such states. Therefore, they all have the same coherent information as
I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}
. Noting that
\bigotimes^{n}_{i=1}K_{x_{i}y_{i}}
are nothing but the Kraus operators of the proposed optimum instrument
\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n}
, we have

\displaystyle I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}=I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n}M)_{\mathcal{T}^{\otimes n}(\rho^{\otimes n})}\leq D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{AB})

(83)

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{AB})
.

Step 4
(Additivity of
D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{AB}
)
)
: From
82
and
83
, we get

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{AB})

\displaystyle=I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma}=I(A^{\otimes n}\rangle B^{\otimes n})_{\sigma^{\otimes n}_{0}}=nI(A\rangle B)_{\sigma_{0}}=nD^{(1)}(\rho_{AB}).

Therefore, we finally have

\displaystyle D_{\to}(\rho_{AB})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{(1)}_{\to}(\rho^{\otimes n}_{AB})=D^{(1)}(\rho_{AB}).

(84)

∎
