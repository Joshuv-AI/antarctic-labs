1983, the U. S. Army's Center of Military History informed us on 1 June
1983 that a package of classified documents we sent them by courier on 26
April 1983 was not delivered until 31 May 1983, and that while the
contents were intact, the package had arrived with its outer envelope
missing and the inner envelope opened. The Army's investigation of this
matter has determined that both the delay and unsealing happened while
the package was in the hands of Army couriers. We cooperated with the
Army investigation, and in the account that follows we have the
information concerning the Army's handling of this package from Special
Agent John P. Hott of the Army's 902nd Military Intelligence Group, Ft.
Meade, Maryland (telephone 301 677 7430).

Demma, a historian in the Army's Center of Military History, releasing
and forwarding to him 28 classified documents (10 Top Secret and 18
Secret), which we had received for CIA review from the John F. Kennedy
Library in Boston. My cover letter and the 28 documents were properly
double-wrapped, marked and sealed, and sent to Dr. Demma by CIA courier
on 26 April 1983 (copy of our receipt attached).

6. Since these documents .do not appear to have been out of
authorized hands at any point, even in their delayed and badly handled
delivery by the Army courier, I am not certain that a damage assessment
report is called for. Although as far as we know none of these 28
documents (all of which concern the Vietnam War and date from 1962 and
1963) from the Kennedy Library has yet been downgraded or declassified, I
suspect that if submitted for classification review, many (and perhaps
all) of these documents would be downgraded or declassified. In any
event, I shall be glad to have your advice on this matter.

7. In my view this incident raises two questions about the Army's
handling of packages of classified material received from CIA's courier
service:

a. Why is there no provision for regular and expeditious
delivery of packages from the Army's security facility at
the Pentagon to the Army's Center of Military History at
the Pulaski Building?

b. Is it in fact normal procedure for Army couriers routinely
to open double-wrapped, sealed, properly marked and
receipted packages of classified material to determine the
classification level of their contents?
