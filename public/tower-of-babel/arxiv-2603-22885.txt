A Heterogeneous Long-Micro Scale Cascading Architecture for General Aviation Health Management Journal: Chinese Journal of Aeronautics
Xinhang Chen, Zhihuan Wei, Yang Hu, Zhiguo Zeng, Kang Zeng, Wei Wang

Abstract
Background: General aviation fleet expansion demands intelligent health monitoring under computational constraints. Real-world aircraft health diagnosis requires balancing accuracy with computational constraints under extreme class imbalance and environmental uncertainty. Existing end-to-end approaches suffer from the receptive field paradox: global attention introduces excessive operational heterogeneity noise for fine-grained fault classification, while localized constraints sacrifice critical cross-temporal context essential for anomaly detection. Methods: This paper presents an AI-driven heterogeneous cascading architecture for general aviation health management. The proposed Long-Micro Scale Diagnostician (LMSD) explicitly decouples global anomaly detection (full-sequence attention) from micro-scale fault classification (restricted receptive fields), resolving the receptive field paradox while minimizing training overhead. A knowledge distillation-based interpretability module provides physically traceable explanations for safety-critical validation. Results: Experiments on the public National General Aviation Flight Information Database (NGAFID) dataset (28,935 flights, 36 categories) demonstrate 4–8% improvement in safety-critical metrics (MCWPM) with 4.2 × \times training acceleration and 46% model compression compared to end-to-end baselines. Conclusions: The AI-driven heterogeneous architecture offers deployable solutions for aviation equipment health management, with potential for digital twin integration in future work. The proposed framework substantiates deployability in resource-constrained aviation environments while maintaining stringent safety requirements.

Data Description

This study employs the NGAFID aviation maintenance dataset as the analytical subject

Comprising 23-dimensional sensor time-series (1 Hz sampling) from over 28,000 flights (31,000 hours) of a Cessna 172 fleet across 36 unscheduled maintenance categories, NGAFID captures real-world operational uncertainty absent in simulation environments. Its sensor network covers whole-aircraft subsystems with multi-system coupling characteristics, constituting a whole-airframe level diagnosis dataset.

The 23-dimensional sensors partition into two complementary observation sets (Table

This binary partition reveals the core characteristic of aviation flight parameter data—monitoring data normal envelopes and anomalous deviations are nested within specific operational patterns, necessitating context-dependent discrimination rather than absolute thresholds.

3.1
Task Formalization and Decomposition Framework Theory

Traditional end-to-end diagnosis models the mapping
f_{D}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
where input
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times D}
produces probability distribution
\mathbf{y}=[y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}]^{\top}
, with
y_{0}
denoting healthy state probability and
y_{i}
the
i
-th fault class probability
Lones2023
. However, this formulation overlooks the Conditional Dependency inherent in aviation data: input
\mathbf{x}=[\mathbf{m}\parallel\mathbf{o}]
comprises monitoring data
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times D_{m}}
(engine, cylinder, electrical indicators) and operational pattern data
\mathbf{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times D_{o}}
(IAS/VSpd/AltMSL/OAT reflecting flight phases and piloting styles). Ignoring operational context
\mathbf{o}
risks spurious correlations, misidentifying normal responses under extreme conditions as faults.

We decompose diagnosis into two subtasks with distinct receptive field requirements:

Anomaly Detection (AD):

f_{AD}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
outputs
\mathbf{p}_{AD}=[p_{h},p_{a}]^{\top}
(healthy/anomalous probabilities), requiring
full-sequence receptive fields
(
\approx L
) to integrate cross-temporal operational context and detect systematic deviations within specific flight phase envelopes.

Fault Classification (FC):

f_{FC}:\mathcal{X}_{a}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
where
\mathcal{X}_{a}=\{\mathbf{x}\mid f_{AD}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{1}_{a}\}
operates on confirmed anomalous samples, requiring
restricted receptive fields
(
\ll L
) to isolate fault-related temporal segments and suppress cross-phase coupling noise for fine-grained fault attribution.

These subtasks exhibit competing Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) maximization objectives: global fields optimize AD via cross-temporal context integration, while local fields optimize FC via fault-signal isolation. The
receptive field paradox
renders single architectures fundamentally incapable of simultaneous optimization—global attention introduces excessive operational heterogeneity noise for FC, while local constraints sacrifice critical context for AD. This necessitates architectural-level explicit separation rather than implicit monolithic trade-offs.

This architectural decoupling circumvents representation learning conflicts, enabling heterogeneous optimization tailored to each subtask’s receptive field requirements.

3.2
LMSD: Architectural Instantiation of DDF

The Long-Micro Scale Diagnostician (LMSD) instantiates DDF through explicit heterogeneous cascading.
Long
denotes the global stage (
f_{AD}
) with receptive fields
\approx L
for operational pattern detection;
Micro
denotes the local stage (
f_{FC}
) with receptive fields
\ll L
for fault-related micro-period extraction.

LMSD implements the meta-architecture operator
\mathcal{M}
via
Hard-Threshold Routing
with Dimensional Isolation. For input
\mathbf{x}=[\mathbf{m}\parallel\mathbf{o}]
:

Stage 1 (Long/Global Screening).
The Health Analyzer
\mathcal{H}_{\theta_{H}}
performs AD, outputting logits
\mathbf{z}_{AD}=[z_{h},z_{a}]^{\top}
. Probability distribution
\mathbf{p}_{AD}=\mathrm{Softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{AD})=[p_{h},p_{a}]^{\top}
determines the routing path.

Stage 2 (Routing and Dimensional Isolation).
LMSD constructs an
(N+1)
-dimensional output vector
\mathbf{z}_{D}
(dimension 0 for healthy,
1:N
for faults). The routing mechanism enforces hard isolation:

1.

Healthy Path
(
z_{h}>z_{a}
): Dimension 0 set to
z_{h}
, remaining dimensions masked to
-\infty
, ensuring zero fault probability mass.

2.

Anomalous Path
(
z_{a}\geq z_{h}
): Dimension 0 masked to
-\infty
, with dimensions
1:N
populated by the Fault Analyzer output
\mathbf{z}_{FC}
.

This guarantees healthy samples never enter the fault classifier’s discrimination space, while fault distributions remain uninfluenced by the healthy category.

Stage 3 (Micro/Local Precise Diagnosis).
The Fault Analyzer
\mathcal{F}_{\theta_{F}}
processes confirmed anomalies, outputting
\mathbf{z}_{FC}=[z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}]^{\top}
. The final vector
\mathbf{z}_{D}=[-\infty,\mathbf{z}_{FC}^{\top}]^{\top}
undergoes Softmax normalization to yield
\mathbf{p}_{D}
.

Decoupled Training Strategy.
Diverging from end-to-end joint optimization, LMSD employs
data-level decomposition
:
\mathcal{H}_{\theta_{H}}
trains on the complete dataset
\mathcal{D}
(leveraging all healthy samples for global pattern learning), while
\mathcal{F}_{\theta_{F}}
trains exclusively on the anomalous subset
\mathcal{D}_{a}=\{(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i}^{FC})\mid y_{i}^{AD}=1\}
(eliminating healthy sample contamination of fault discrimination space).
Optimization-level hard isolation
prohibits gradient backpropagation across the routing boundary, preventing fine-grained FC gradients from interfering with global AD pattern learning. During inference, pre-trained
\mathcal{H}_{\theta_{H}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\theta_{F}}
execute with frozen parameters.

This “large-sample lightweight” (AD) versus “small-sample complex” (FC) strategy reduces training iteration overhead while aligning model complexity with data scale. Heterogeneous hyperparameter configurations (hidden dimensions, encoder depths) accommodate divergent demands of global context modeling versus local feature extraction.

3.3
Component-level Instantiation: Trainable Models

This section instantiates
\mathcal{M}
with heterogeneous backbones: ConvTokMHSA for
\mathcal{H}_{\theta_{H}}

3.3.1
ConvTokMHSA: Global Context Modeling

The Convolutional Tokenizer partitions input
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times D}
into non-overlapping segments of length
p
, extracting local shape features via 1D convolution while embedding segment-wise statistics (
\mu
,
\sigma
) and positional encodings to generate token sequences
\mathbf{X}_{tok}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{tok}\times d_{tok}}
(
N_{tok}=\lceil L/p\rceil
).

Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) operates on these tokens via convolutional projections (Q, K, V generated by 1D convolution), enabling full-sequence interaction:

\mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})=\mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)\mathbf{V}

(2)

This facilitates cross-temporal context integration essential for operational pattern identification. Global attention spans
N_{tok}
tokens; replacing with Sliding Window Local Attention (SWLA) degrades AD performance by losing critical operational context, confirming the necessity of full-sequence fields for the Long stage.

3.3.2
MMK Net: Micro-Scale Local Feature Extraction

MMK Net processes raw sequences directly through Multi-Micro Kernel Convolution. Each MMK Block employs bottleneck dimensionality reduction (1
\times
1 conv) followed by parallel 1D convolutions with kernels
\{1,3,5\}
:

\mathbf{h}^{(l)}=\mathrm{ReLU}\left(\mathrm{LN}\left(\underset{k\in\{1,3,5\}}{\mathrm{Concat}}\left[\mathrm{Conv}_{1d}^{(k)}\left(\mathbf{W}_{bottle}\ast\mathbf{h}^{(l-1)}\right)\right]\right)\right)

(3)

where
\mathbf{h}^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{3F\times L}
denotes the
l
-th block output,
\mathbf{W}_{bottle}
the bottleneck kernel, and LN denotes LayerNorm (stabilizing feature scales under class imbalance).

Micro-kernels (max size 5) ensure strictly restricted receptive fields, isolating fault-related segments while suppressing cross-stage noise. Removal of pooling layers preserves temporal resolution; residual connections every 3 blocks maintain shallow feature sharpness. Global average pooling yields the final classification input.

This architectural heterogeneity—global attention (
\approx L
) versus micro-convolution (
\ll L
)—fundamentally resolves the receptive field paradox, furnishing a deployable pathway for industrial diagnosis.

3.4
Keyness Extraction Layer via Distillation Learning

To furnish physically traceable explanations for LMSD’s two-stage decisions, we introduce the Keyness Extraction Layer (KEL)—a lightweight, model-agnostic interpretability module prepended to either stage. Unlike post-hoc techniques (SHAP, LIME) that struggle to integrate with temporal physical mechanisms, KEL extracts sample-level temporal keyness through knowledge distillation, enabling verification of whether model attention aligns with fault propagation pathways.

Given input sample
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times L\times D}
, KEL generates a Temporal Keyness Vector
\mathbf{w}_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{L/s}
(where
s
denotes temporal resolution) via a lightweight encoder
\mathcal{F}_{enc}
comprising two CNN layers:

\mathbf{w}_{K}=\sigma(\mathcal{F}_{enc}(\mathbf{x};\theta_{K}))\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times(L/s)}

(4)

The keyness matrix
\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times L\times D}
is obtained via temporal expansion (repeat-interpolation) of
\mathbf{w}_{K}
to original sequence length, yielding weighted input
\mathbf{x}_{kw}=\mathbf{x}\odot\mathbf{K}
. Notably, KEL constrains keyness lower bound to 0.5 (via ReLU-pre-sigmoid activation), preserving information accessibility to all temporal segments while highlighting relative criticality—avoiding the absolute suppression pitfalls of traditional attention mechanisms in safety-critical applications.

KEL employs knowledge distillation to ensure faithful reproduction of the original model’s decision logic without disrupting pretrained distributions. The pretrained original model serves as Teacher
\mathcal{T}
(frozen), while the KEL-embedded model serves as Student
\mathcal{S}
. The student minimizes temperature-scaled (
T=1.2
) KL divergence between teacher and student feature distributions, balanced by cross-entropy classification loss:

\mathcal{L}=D_{KL}\left(\mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{T}}}{T}\right)\Big\|\mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{S}}}{T}\right)\right)+\mathcal{L}_{CE}(\mathbf{Y}_{\mathcal{S}},\mathbf{y})

(5)

Upon convergence, the teacher is removed and
\mathbf{w}_{K}
is extracted, enabling acquisition of shallow-layer temporal attention distributions without modifying original model parameters. Coupled with Top-K cosine similarity retrieval of healthy baselines, this keyness information generates contrastive temporal heatmaps that verify whether the model attends to physically relevant segments (e.g., pre-takeoff thermal deviations for rocker cover faults) rather than spurious operational artifacts—substantiating the credibility of LMSD’s safety-critical predictions through mechanistically transparent evidence.

Experimental Results and Discussion

4.1
Data Processing and Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted on the NGAFID Overall dataset (36 classes, 28,935 flights), with the benchmark Subset (19 classes) results provided in Supplementary Material. All variable-length flight segments are normalized to a fixed length of
2048
via Cubic Spline Interpolation, retaining complete 23-dimensional records with
<
10% missing channel rates. Missing values are addressed via Forward Fill. Z-score normalization parameters are computed exclusively from training sets to prevent statistical leakage.

Validation Protocol:
Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation is employed with physical file isolation between folds. For end-to-end models, four folds are used for training (9:1 internal split), with the remaining fold held out for evaluation. For LMSD, the Health Analyzer and Fault Analyzer share the complete training data per fold but execute independent 9:1 training/validation splits internally, ensuring optimization isolation while maintaining test set consistency.

Training Protocols:
Conventional end-to-end methods employ early stopping (patience=3). LMSD adopts hard-isolated decoupled training:
\mathcal{H}_{\theta_{H}}
trains on the complete dataset (leveraging all healthy samples), while
\mathcal{F}_{\theta_{F}}
trains exclusively on the anomalous subset
\mathcal{D}_{a}
, preventing healthy sample contamination of fault discrimination space. Heterogeneous hyperparameter configurations for all models are detailed in
Supplementary Material
.

Class Imbalance Mitigation:
Proportional replication augmentation (
k_{da}=3
) is applied to FC and Diagnosis tasks:
N_{c_{da}}=\min(3\times N_{c},N_{c_{max}})
, preventing distribution distortion while ensuring tail-class learnability.

4.2
Evaluation Metrics

A four-dimensional evaluation system assesses: (1) classification performance; (2) training efficiency; (3) inference overhead; (4) safety-critical metrics.

Classification Metrics:
Accuracy (ACC), macro-averaged F1-score (F1), and Weighted F1 (WF1) for end-to-end tasks.

Efficiency Metrics:
Epoch Training Time (ET), Total Training Time (TTT), Inference Time for 32 Samples (IT32, post GPU warm-up), and Model Size (MSize)—quantifying fleet-level retraining feasibility and edge-node deployability.

Safety-Critical Metrics:
For AD, False Negative Rate (FNR). For end-to-end Diagnosis, Multi-Class Weighted Penalty Metric (MCWPM) formalizes “Safety-over-Economy” constraints:

\mathrm{MCWPM}=\frac{\sum\mathrm{TP}}{\sum\mathrm{TP}+\alpha_{\mathrm{P}}\cdot\mathrm{FN}_{\mathrm{health}}+\beta_{\mathrm{P}}\cdot\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{health}}}

(6)

where
\mathrm{FN}_{\mathrm{health}}
(missed detection) and
\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{health}}
(false alarm) denote fault
\rightarrow
healthy and healthy
\rightarrow
fault misclassifications, respectively. With
\alpha_{\mathrm{P}}=2.5
and
\beta_{\mathrm{P}}=1.0
, MCWPM explicitly prioritizes aviation safety by penalizing missed detections more severely than false alarms.

Implementation:
Python 3.12 with PyTorch 2.7.1+cu126 on Intel i7-13620H (32 GB RAM) and NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU (28 GB VRAM). Fixed computational baseline ensures reproducibility of efficiency metrics critical for industrial deployment.

Limited sensor capacity and feature sparsity.
The Cessna-172’s 23-dimensional 1Hz acquisition pales against modern QAR systems, submerging fault features in process noise. PCA confirms discriminative signals concentrate in the 12th–18th minor components (low-variance subspaces). While LMSD’s receptive field separation mitigates cross-stage noise, it cannot overcome the physical information bottleneck imposed by sensor sparsity.

These tri-coupled constraints indicate that LMSD’s metrics approach the discriminability boundary under current data limitations. Future breakthroughs require: (1) refined maintenance semantic annotation; (2) enhanced flight parameter quality/capacity; (3) component-subsystem-whole-aircraft multi-level architectures with physics-informed constraints.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

To address data uncertainty, task heterogeneity, and computational constraints in general aviation health diagnosis, this paper proposes the Diagnosis Decomposition Framework (DDF) instantiated as the Long-Micro Scale Diagnostician (LMSD). The core contribution is a deployable methodology resolving the receptive field paradox through explicit architectural decoupling: lightweight global analyzers with full-sequence fields (ConvTokMHSA) learn operational patterns from large-scale data, while complex micro-scale analyzers with restricted fields (MMK Net) extract local fault features from anomalous samples. This “large-sample lightweight + small-sample complex” strategy reduces training overhead while ensuring stringent missed detection constraints via hard-threshold routing, formalizing aviation’s “safety-first” ethics into deployable AI design.

NGAFID validation confirms effectiveness across three dimensions: (1)
Task adaptability
—heterogeneous cascading achieves 4–8% MCWPM improvement over end-to-end baselines under class imbalance; (2)
Interpretability
—KEL visualizations confirm physically traceable hierarchical cognition (global operational discrimination enabling microscopic fault localization); (3)
Computational efficiency
—decoupled training reduces TTT by 4.2
\times
versus complex end-to-end alternatives with 46% smaller model size. However, label noise, extreme long-tail distributions, and limited sensor capacity impose performance ceilings, indicating that methodological advances must coordinate with data infrastructure improvements.

Future work advances along three axes: (1)
Methodological
—evolving from hard-threshold to uncertainty-quantified soft routing with physics-informed embeddings for causal reasoning; (2)
Data infrastructure
—enhanced flight parameter capacity via increased sensor dimensions/sampling rates and refined maintenance annotations; (3)
Engineering deployment
—edge computing adaptation, online incremental learning, and cross-domain transfer capabilities for fleet-wide autonomous health management.

Digital Twin Integration.
Future extensions of this work will explore the integration with digital twin frameworks, where the proposed heterogeneous diagnosis architecture can serve as the real-time analytics engine for virtual aircraft replicas, enabling closed-loop health management. In such a framework, the LMSD’s two-stage decision process would feed into continuously updated digital twins, providing both instantaneous fault alerts and long-term degradation predictions. The physically traceable interpretability provided by KEL aligns naturally with digital twin requirements for transparent, auditable decision pathways, while the computational efficiency of decoupled training enables real-time synchronization between physical assets and their virtual counterparts. This convergence of AI-driven diagnosis with digital twin technology represents a promising pathway toward comprehensive aviation intelligent systems.
