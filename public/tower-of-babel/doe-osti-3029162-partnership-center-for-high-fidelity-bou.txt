What follows is the Final Technical Report for DOE cooperative agreement DE-SC0018271. This
project was the University of Colorado, Boulder effort for the Partnership Center for High-
fidelity Boundary Plasma Simulation. The center PI was Dr. C.S. Chang at Princeton Plasma
Physics Laboratory. Work carried out included turbulence spreading from edge to core,
sensitivity to edge microinstabilities to the magnetic equilibrium, detailed global linear and
nonlinear benchmarking between the XGC and GEM gyrokinetic codes. Additional work on
core-edge coupling took place.

Turbulence Spreading from Edge to Core
Global GEM simulations were carried out to investigate if turbulence spreading from the
edge my help explain the transport “shortfall”, in which the ion heat flux Qi predicted by
gyrokinetic simulations is smaller than the experimental value determined by the
transport code ONETWO at the outer core region. For two DIII-D L- mode cases, we find that
the core is dominated by the ITG instability while the edge is dominated by the TEM. Our
simulations do observe significant turbulence spreading from the edge to the core. It appears
that the core is dominated by the ITG turbulence inthe absence of edge. However, when edge is
included, the TEM turbulence spreading from the edge to the core enhances the ion heat flux
level in the core substantially, but the overall ion heat flux is still lower than the experimentally
predicted level. Therefore, we conclude that the effect of TEM edge turbulence alone is not
sufficient to explain the bserved shortfall in the DIII-D L-mode shots. Although no significant
profile relaxation is observed for the nonlinear simulations, the fact that the shortfall cannot be
explained by turbulence spreading from edge in this study indicates a “flux-driven” model may
be required. In fact, a series of follow-up studies using the XGC1 code with particle source and
sink were carried out.

Sensitivity of Edge Micro-instabilities to the Magnetic Equilibrium
We studied the effects of the magnetic equilibrium on gyrokinetic simulations of edge
tokamak microi-nstabilities. We have implemented the general geometry of
the experimental tokamak magnetic equilibrium in GEM using a novel and
mathematically intuitive approach. It is found that compared to general geometry, the
widely-used Miller parameterization of the magnetic equilibrium is a good approximation
in the core region through to the top of the pedestal. Linear simulations indicate that
results with the two geometries agree for r/a ≤ 0.9. However, in the edge region, the
instabilities are sensitive to the magnetic equilibrium in both the L-mode and the H-mode
plasmas. A small variation of the plasma shaping parameters leads to large changes to the
edge instability. The edge region is only a few centimeters wide there can be significant

uncertainties in the magnetic equilibrium obtained from the analysis of the experimental

GEM/XGC Coupling
Addionally work on coupling the XGC edge gyrokinetic simulation to the core gyokinetic
simulation GEM took place. The original code coupling with a blending function was originally
proposed by Prof. Parker. We investigated coupling schemes within the project, and have
successfully coupled GEM and XGC using the Dominski spatial coupling scheme. We have
validated the coupled results to a reference case from XGC and show excellent agreement
between the coupled code and the XGC reference case. We have done this for two tokamak
geometries. First, we implemented a realistic magnetic equilibrium in nearly circular geometry
to develop the coupling model. Next, we coupled the two codes for a realistic DIII-D
equilibrium with a divertor region. In the second case, we included neoclassical effects in both
codes. So far, all coupling has been done using the adiabatic electron approximation, but work
is underway to extend spatial coupling to include kinetic electrons and electromagnetic
perturbations. The lead scientist on the coupling work was Dr. Junyi Cheng, Univ. of Colorado.
