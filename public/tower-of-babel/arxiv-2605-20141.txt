Relativistic Saturation of Coulomb-Limited Electron Coherence
Yury A. Budkov

Abstract
We show that the non-relativistic theory of mutual coherence and localization in Coulomb-disordered media can be extended to relativistic electron beams used in transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Starting from the Dirac equation, we derive a paraxial Schrödinger-like equation for the envelope spinor and obtain an effective coupling constant A rel = ( γ + 1 ) / ( 2 ​ γ ​ ℏ ​ v ) A_{\rm rel}=(\gamma+1)/(2\gamma\hbar v) that governs the disorder-induced phase fluctuations. In the non-relativistic limit γ → 1 \gamma\to 1 this reduces to 1 / ( ℏ ​ v ) 1/(\hbar v) , while for ultra-relativistic electrons it saturates at 1 / ( 2 ​ ℏ ​ c ) 1/(2\hbar c) . The universal relation between the transverse coherence length ρ c \rho_{c} and the single-particle localization length ℓ \ell , namely ρ c ∼ λ D ​ ℓ / L \rho_{c}\sim\lambda_{D}\sqrt{\ell/L} , remains unchanged. We compare the asymptotic behaviour of the phase structure function D ϕ ​ ( ρ ) D_{\phi}(\rho) and the localization length in the non-relativistic and relativistic regimes, and show that the emergent algebraic decay of mutual coherence at large separations, analogous to the wave-structure-function asymptotics in turbulent media, persists in both cases. The results imply that standard TEM energies (100–300 keV) are already close to the optimal regime for minimizing Coulomb decoherence, and that further increasing the beam energy yields diminishing returns. While the asymptotic coherence decay is algebraic rather than exponential, the corresponding exponent can still be large for realistic experimental parameters, so the effect is primarily of conceptual and asymptotic significance.



Central physical result.

We show that relativistic kinematics imposes a saturation of Coulomb-induced phase-noise suppression at high beam energies. Consequently, coherence gains obtained by increasing the accelerating voltage become progressively weaker in the relativistic regime. This establishes an intrinsic saturation of coherence improvement for electron waves propagating through fluctuating Coulomb media, analogous to turbulence-limited wave-front coherence in atmospheric optics.

Introduction

In a series of recent papers
1
;
2
;
3
(Parts I–III), a microscopic theory of disorder-induced localization and mutual coherence degradation was developed for a quantum particle in a classical one-component plasma. The theory predicts a universal relation between the transverse coherence length
\rho_{c}
and the single-particle localization length
\ell
:

\rho_{c}\sim\lambda_{D}\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{L}},

(1)

where
\lambda_{D}
is the Debye screening length and
L
is the sample thickness. This result establishes a direct link between quantum localization and the intrinsic resolution limit in liquid-cell electron microscopy.

The original formulation was non-relativistic. However, actual TEM experiments employ electron beams with kinetic energies of
100
–
300
keV, corresponding to velocities
v\sim 0.5c
–
0.8c
. It is therefore essential to verify whether the universal relation (
1
) survives relativistic corrections, and to determine how the effective coupling to the disorder, as well as the asymptotic behaviour of the coherence function, are modified at high energies.

In this Letter we derive the relativistic paraxial equation for the electron envelope starting from the Dirac equation, extract the effective coupling constant
A_{\rm rel}
, and analyze the asymptotic forms of the phase structure function
D_{\phi}(\rho)
and the localization length in both the non-relativistic and relativistic regimes. We find that
A_{\rm rel}
saturates in the ultra-relativistic limit, implying that standard TEM energies already lie in a near-optimal regime for minimizing Coulomb-induced decoherence. Importantly, this saturation does not impose a fundamental limit on the instrumental resolution itself, which remains governed by the electron wavelength, but rather on the achievable suppression of Coulomb-induced phase noise and coherence degradation.

. A relativistic electron wave propagating through a thermally fluctuating one-component plasma accumulates stochastic phase shifts generated by ionic density fluctuations and the associated random electrostatic potential.

Paraxial reduction of the Dirac equation.

We consider a static electrostatic potential
W(\mathbf{r})
that couples to the electron as the time component of a four-potential. The Dirac equation in Hamiltonian form is

i\hbar\partial_{t}\Psi=\bigl[c\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{p}}+\beta mc^{2}+W(\mathbf{r})\bigr]\Psi.

(2)

For a stationary beam of total energy
\mathcal{E}
we write
\Psi=e^{-i\mathcal{E}t/\hbar}\psi
, obtaining the stationary equation. Decomposing the spinor into upper and lower components
\psi=(\varphi,\eta)^{T}
, and eliminating
\eta
perturbatively for weak fields
|W|\ll\mathcal{E}+mc^{2}
, yields the scalar-like equation for the upper component:

(\mathcal{E}-mc^{2}-W)\varphi=\frac{c^{2}}{\mathcal{E}+mc^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{\,2}\varphi.

(3)

Spin-dependent terms (spin-orbit and Darwin) are suppressed by
1/(\mathcal{E}+mc^{2})^{2}
and are neglected in the leading paraxial approximation
4
;
6
.

We now assume forward propagation along the
z
-axis with momentum
p\gg\hbar k_{\perp}
, and separate the fast phase:

\varphi(\mathbf{r})=e^{ikz}\phi(\mathbf{r}),\qquad k=\frac{p}{\hbar}=\frac{\gamma mv}{\hbar},

where
\gamma=(1-v^{2}/c^{2})^{-1/2}
. Expanding the squared momentum operator and keeping only terms linear in
\partial_{z}
, we obtain after a straightforward reduction the relativistic paraxial equation

i\hbar v\,\partial_{z}\phi=\Bigl[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\gamma m}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}\,W(\mathbf{r})\Bigr]\phi.

(4)

This is the relativistic analogue of the standard paraxial Schrödinger equation: the longitudinal coordinate
z
plays the role of time, the effective transverse mass is
\gamma m
, and the potential enters with a
\gamma
-dependent prefactor
(\gamma+1)/(2\gamma)
, which reduces to unity in the non-relativistic limit
\gamma\to 1
and approaches
1/2
for
\gamma\gg 1
.

Effective coupling and phase structure function.

Dividing Eq. (
4
) by
v
we obtain a standard Schrödinger form with “time”
z
:

i\hbar\,\partial_{z}\phi=\Bigl[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\gamma mv}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma v}W\Bigr]\phi.

The corresponding effective Lagrangian for the transverse motion is

L_{\rm eff}=\frac{\gamma mv}{2}\,\dot{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{2}-\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma v}\,W(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},z).

In the eikonal (straight-line) approximation, the accumulated random phase over a distance
L
is

\Delta\phi=-\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma\hbar v}\int_{0}^{L}dz\,W.

(5)

Hence the effective coupling constant that controls the disorder-induced phase fluctuations is

A_{\rm rel}=\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma\hbar v}.

(6)

In the non-relativistic limit
\gamma\to 1
, this reduces to
A_{\rm NR}=1/(\hbar v)
, which is precisely the coupling used in the non-relativistic theory of Ref.
3
. For ultra-relativistic electrons,
A_{\rm rel}\to 1/(2\hbar c)
.

For Gaussian, statistically homogeneous disorder with correlator
K(r)=\langle W(\mathbf{r})W(0)\rangle
, the mutual coherence function becomes

\gamma(\rho)=\exp\!\Bigl[-\frac{1}{2}D_{\phi}(\rho)\Bigr],

(7)

D_{\phi}(\rho)=4A_{\rm rel}^{2}L\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,\bigl[K(u)-K\!\bigl(\sqrt{u^{2}+\rho^{2}}\bigr)\bigr].

(8)

The structural form of
D_{\phi}(\rho)
is identical in the non-relativistic and relativistic regimes; the only modification is the replacement
A\to A_{\rm rel}
.

Asymptotic behaviour of
D_{\phi}(\rho)
and analogy with turbulent media.

For an electrolyte modeled as a classical one-component plasma, the potential correlator is
K(r)=C(1-e^{-\kappa r})/r
with
C=k_{\rm B}Tq_{0}^{2}/\varepsilon
and
\kappa=1/\lambda_{D}
. In the non-relativistic theory, the phase structure function has the exact form
3

D_{\phi}^{\rm NR}(\rho)=4A_{\rm NR}^{2}LC\int_{0}^{\kappa\rho}dt\,\bigl[t^{-1}-K_{1}(t)\bigr].

(9)

At small separations
\kappa\rho\ll 1
, the expansion of the Bessel function yields a Gaussian-like decay

\gamma(\rho)\simeq\exp\!\bigl[-\tfrac{1}{2}A_{\rm NR}^{2}LC\,\kappa^{2}\rho^{2}\ln(2/\kappa\rho)\bigr],\qquad\rho_{c}\sim\lambda_{D}\sqrt{\frac{\ell_{\rm NR}}{L}}.

At large separations
\kappa\rho\gg 1
,
K_{1}(t)
is exponentially suppressed, leading to a logarithmic growth of
D_{\phi}^{\rm NR}
and hence a power-law tail

\gamma(\rho)\sim(\kappa\rho)^{-2A_{\rm NR}^{2}LC}.

(10)

This algebraic decay is a direct consequence of the unscreened
1/r
tail of the potential correlator.

This behaviour is strikingly analogous to the wave-structure-function results obtained by Tatarskii
8
and Ishimaru
5
for classical waves propagating through random media with long-range correlations, e.g., atmospheric turbulence. In those works, the phase structure function grows as a power law (e.g.,
\rho^{5/3}
for Kolmogorov turbulence), leading to a slow decay of the mutual coherence function. In the present case, the longitudinal accumulation of screened phase fluctuations produces a logarithmic structure function and an algebraic coherence tail, placing the theory within the same conceptual framework of wave propagation in random environments with algebraic correlations. The main difference is that here the disorder is created by equilibrium thermal fluctuations in a plasma, rather than externally imposed turbulence.

The mutual coherence length derived here plays a role analogous to the Fried parameter
r_{0}
in astronomical imaging. In that context, turbulence in the atmosphere limits the effective resolution of ground-based telescopes regardless of their aperture. In the present case, the image degradation in a liquid-cell TEM is governed not only by the electron wavelength and lens aberrations, but also by the stochastic phase fluctuations accumulated during propagation through the fluctuating Coulomb environment. The length
\rho_{c}
thus sets a “Coulomb aperture” for electron microscopy, a fundamental scale that cannot be overcome by improving the optics alone. This perspective unifies the description of coherence loss in two seemingly disparate fields and underscores the universal character of the wave-structure-function formalism
8
;
7
.

In the relativistic case, the coupling constant
A_{\rm NR}
is simply replaced by
A_{\rm rel}
. Therefore, the asymptotic behaviour of
D_{\phi}(\rho)
at both small and large
\rho
is formally identical, with the effective coupling strength reduced by the factor
(\gamma+1)/(2\gamma)
. Specifically, the power-law exponent in Eq. (
10
) becomes
2A_{\rm rel}^{2}LC=2[(\gamma+1)/(2\gamma)]^{2}A_{\rm NR}^{2}LC
. Thus, relativity partially suppresses the loss of coherence, but does not alter the functional form of the decay.

Localization length and universal scaling.

The disorder-averaged envelope Green function decays exponentially with a localization length
\ell
that follows from the same saddle-point analysis as in the non-relativistic case
1
:

\ell_{\rm rel}=\frac{1}{A_{\rm rel}^{2}\,C\,\ln(\kappa L_s)},\qquad C=\frac{k_{\rm B}Tq_{0}^{2}}{\varepsilon}.

(11)

Substituting Eq. (
6
) yields the explicit relativistic form

\ell_{\rm rel}=\frac{4\gamma^{2}\hbar^{2}v^{2}}{(\gamma+1)^{2}\,C\,\ln(\kappa L_s)},

(12)

which reduces to the non-relativistic result
\ell_{\rm NR}=\hbar^{2}v^{2}/[C\ln(\kappa L_s)]
when
\gamma\to 1
. At ultra-relativistic energies,
\ell_{\rm rel}\to 4\hbar^{2}c^{2}/[C\ln(\kappa L_s)]
, so the localization length saturates at a finite value.

The transverse coherence length
\rho_{c}
, defined by
\gamma(\rho_{c})=e^{-1}
, retains the universal form

\rho_{c}\sim\lambda_{D}\sqrt{\frac{\ell_{\rm rel}}{L}}.

(13)

Thus, despite the relativistic modification of the coupling constant and the localization length, the fundamental scaling relation (
1
) is preserved. The asymptotic behaviour of
\rho_{c}
as a function of energy is therefore qualitatively similar in the non-relativistic and relativistic regimes, with
\rho_{c}
growing as
\sqrt{\ell}
and saturating when
\ell
saturates.

Implications for transmission electron microscopy.

For typical TEM energies (
100
–
300
keV,
\gamma\simeq 1.2
–
1.6
), the prefactor
(\gamma+1)/(2\gamma)
ranges from
0.93
to
0.85
, deviating from unity by at most
15\%
. Consequently, the non-relativistic estimates of
\rho_{c}
given in Ref.
3
require only a modest relativistic correction. More importantly, as
v\to c
(
\gamma\to\infty
), the coupling constant saturates:

\lim_{v\to c}A_{\rm rel}=\frac{1}{2\hbar c}.

(14)

This saturation implies that the disorder-induced decoherence cannot be arbitrarily suppressed by increasing the beam energy; the Coulomb fluctuations impose an irreducible “floor” on the coherence loss. Standard TEM therefore already operates near the optimal point, and further gains from higher voltages are marginal from the standpoint of Coulomb decoherence.

The analysis of the asymptotic behaviour of
D_{\phi}(\rho)
shows that the algebraic tail of the coherence function persists in the relativistic regime, with the exponent only slightly reduced. This means that the long-range nature of the Coulomb correlations remains the dominant source of decoherence even for relativistic electrons, and that the slow decay of coherence at large separations is a generic feature of Coulomb-disordered media.

Origin of the algebraic coherence tail.

The algebraic asymptotic behavior of the mutual coherence function should not be interpreted as evidence for an unscreened microscopic Coulomb interaction. At the microscopic level, the electrostatic correlations are exponentially screened on the Debye scale. The logarithmic growth of the phase structure function emerges only after longitudinal integration of screened phase fluctuations accumulated along the propagation direction. Thus, the algebraic coherence tail is an emergent statistical property of the accumulated phase field rather than a direct manifestation of a bare long-range potential.

Conclusion.

We have demonstrated that the relativistic paraxial reduction of the Dirac equation leads to a renormalized coupling constant
A_{\rm rel}=(\gamma+1)/(2\gamma\hbar v)
, which smoothly interpolates between the non-relativistic and ultra-relativistic limits and saturates at
1/(2\hbar c)
. The universal scaling
\rho_{c}\sim\lambda_{D}\sqrt{\ell/L}
remains intact, confirming that the coherence-localization correspondence established in the non-relativistic theory is a robust, Lorentz-invariant feature of Coulomb-disordered media. The asymptotic behaviour of the phase structure function, characterized by a logarithmic growth and an algebraic tail in the coherence function, is preserved in the relativistic regime and closely mirrors the well-known results for wave propagation in random media with long-range correlations
8
;
5
;
7
. These findings place the earlier non-relativistic predictions on a firm relativistic footing and provide a clear theoretical guideline for optimizing electron microscopy under conditions where thermal ionic disorder is the dominant source of decoherence.
