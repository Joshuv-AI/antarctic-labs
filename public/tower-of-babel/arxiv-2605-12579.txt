Phase-resolved field-space distance bounds in ekpyrotic, bouncing and cyclic cosmologies
Marcin Postolak

Abstract
The inflationary Lyth bound relates the primordial tensor amplitude to the inflaton field excursion. There is no analogous universal relationship in the case of ekpyrotic, bouncing, and cyclic models because scalar and tensor perturbations depend on entropy conversion, matching through the bounce and the specific mechanism that violates or evades the null energy condition. Nevertheless, the background kinematics fulfills a useful non-inflationary analogue: a field-space distance budget. In this study, we propose a phase-resolved distance criterion for a non-inflationary smoothing process and decompose the invariant scalar distance into ekpyrotic smoothing, entropy-to-curvature conversion, bounce, and post-bounce contributions. Then, we impose BKL anisotropy suppression as an additional constraint on the ekpyrotic phase. In the canonical phase of the ekpyrotic contraction, we recover the known small-field scaling and generalize it to total budget inequality. We impose three requirements: a BKL (Belinski-Khalatnikov-Lifshitz) anisotropy suppression that is parameterized separately, a phenomenological cutoff-corrected distance budget inspired by tower of states logic, and observational conversion windows from residual isocurvature and non-Gaussianity. Furthermore, we propose a new master condition that provides a lower bound on the value of the parameter ϵ ek \epsilon_{\rm ek} that depends on the remaining distance available after conversion and the cosmological bounce. We also derive a curvature constraint for scale-invariant entropy perturbations in curved field space which shows that the small total distance and the observed red tilt seem to indicate ultra-fast-roll ekpyrosis, sharp turns, short or strongly modified bounces, and/or significant negative sectional curvature of the scalar manifold. Finally, we demonstrate methods for testing the distance budget against observational data. Examples include n s n_{s} running, primordial non-Gaussianity, isocurvature, a stochastic GWs background (SGWB), and late-time dark energy distance constraints. The observational discussion is organized into explicit diagnostic maps, which are used to identify and analyze specific features and patterns in the data. We also apply the formalism to exponential and cyclic ekpyrotic SF potentials, providing a mechanism-resolved interpretation of the bounce or crossover contribution for several specific realizations.



I
Introduction

The Lyth bound
53
is one of the cleanest kinematic connections between an observable quantity and a microscopic field-space distance in early Universe cosmology. In canonical single-field slow-roll inflation, the relations:

r=16\epsilon\quad\land\quad\frac{d\phi}{dN}=M_{\rm Pl}\sqrt{2\epsilon}

(1)

imply:

\frac{\Delta\phi}{M_{\rm Pl}}=\int dN\sqrt{\frac{r(N)}{8}}\,,

(2)

so that a detectable primordial tensor amplitude generically points to a Planckian or super-Planckian inflaton excursion
53
. The relation has been generalized within the effective theory of single-field inflation, where nontrivial dynamics does not automatically eliminate field-range constraints
14
.

Non-inflationary alternatives such as ekpyrotic, bouncing and cyclic cosmologies address the flatness, horizon and anisotropy problems by a phase of slowly contracting ultra-stiff evolution rather than accelerated expansion
42
;
81
;
47
;
13
;
72
. In such scenarios, the role of tensor perturbations is fundamentally different from canonical slow-roll inflation. In minimal ekpyrotic models, the tensor spectrum is typically blue and negligible on cosmic microwave background (CMB) scales
16
;
47
, whereas special constructions, such as S-brane models, may produce observable tensors with model-dependent consistency relations
22
. In non-singular modified gravity bounces, scalar and tensor spectra depend on the detailed bounce sector and transfer functions
41
;
85
. Consequently, there is no model-independent ekpyrotic identity analogous to (
1
).

However, the absence of a universal tensor-to-field range relation does not mean that field distance is irrelevant. Ekpyrotic smoothing itself has a simple field-range cost. Lehners demonstrated that imposing sub-Planckian field excursions can drive scale-free ekpyrosis to the ultra-fast-roll regime, which can lead to observationally relevant running
49
. This study takes a complementary step. We propose that the natural, non-inflationary analogue of Lyth’s logic is a
phase-resolved field-space distance budget
for the entire cosmological history:

d_{\rm tot}=d_{\rm ek}+d_{\rm conv}+d_{\rm b}+d_{\rm post}\,.

(3)

The point is simple. If an effective field theory (EFT) is required to remain within a finite field-space distance, then ekpyrotic smoothing, entropy conversion, the bounce, and post-bounce matching all compete for the same distance.

Relation to previous studies

The ekpyrotic smoothing contribution in equation (
19
) is not claimed to be new. It is closely related to the small-field ekpyrotic argument in
49
, in which imposing a sub-Planckian field range was shown to drive scale-free ekpyrosis toward ultra-fast-roll. The new contribution of this work is extending this one-phase bound to a complete non-inflationary history, including BKL anisotropy suppression, entropy-to-curvature conversion, bounce duration, cutoff loss, and post-bounce evolution. Thus, the result is not an ekpyrotic tensor-to-scalar Lyth bound but rather a field-space distance-budget criterion.

The main results are as follows: First, we rederive the ekpyrotic smoothing contribution in terms of the growth of the comoving Hubble scale. Second, we introduce lower bounds for conversion and bounce. Third, we combine these bounds into a master inequality and solve for the minimum ekpyrotic fast-roll parameter,
\epsilon_{\rm ek}
. Fourth, we connect the same distance budget to observable quantities, particularly
n_{s}
, non-Gaussianity, residual isocurvature, CMB B modes, and late-time scalar field evolution, as suggested by recent dark energy data.

II
Invariant field-space distance

Let us consider
n
scalar fields that are minimally coupled to Einstein’s theory of general relativity (GR):

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R-\frac{1}{2}G_{IJ}(\phi)g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{J}-V(\phi)\right]

(4)

\displaystyle+S_{\rm{b/EFT}}\,,

where
G_{IJ}
is the field-space metric, and
S_{\rm b/EFT}
denotes NEC-violating operators, as well as higher-derivative or modified gravity operators, which are relevant near the bounce. Using an invariant field-space metric and covariant field-space distance follows the standard multifield formalism
37
;
36
;
77
.

The following assumptions are used throughout:

(i)

Einstein-scalar dynamics are assumed during the ekpyrotic smoothing phase but not necessarily during the bounce;

(ii)

The bounce sector is treated kinematically through the effective potential, the bounce action, and the bounce interval, rather than being derived from a specific microscopic Lagrangian;

(iii)

Entropy perturbations are treated in the approximate decoupling limit during their generation;

(iv)

Observational conversion bounds are interpreted as phenomenological consistency windows rather than as model-independent numerical limits.

In the case of spatially flat FLRW background, the line element becomes of the form:

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\,d\vec{x}^{2}\,,

(5)

so that one can define the field-space speed:

\dot{\sigma}^{2}\equiv G_{IJ}\dot{\phi}^{I}\dot{\phi}^{J}\,.

(6)

The invariant scalar distance is:

\Delta s=\int ds_{\rm{field}}=\int\dot{\sigma}\,dt\,,

(7)

and the dimensionless distance takes the form of:

d\equiv\frac{\Delta s}{M_{\rm Pl}}=\frac{1}{M_{\rm Pl}}\int\dot{\sigma}\,dt\,.

(8)

Away from the bounce, assuming ordinary Einstein-scalar dynamics, the Friedmann equations yield:

\dot{H}=-\frac{\dot{\sigma}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\quad\land\quad\epsilon\equiv-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=\frac{\dot{\sigma}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}H^{2}}\,.

(9)

Thus:

\dot{\sigma}=M_{\rm Pl}\left|H\right|\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,,

(10)

and:

d=\int\left|H\right|dt\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,.

(11)

This is the basic kinematic identity underlying the non-inflationary distance budget.

III
Ekpyrotic smoothing distance

We know that during the ekpyrotic contraction:

H<0\quad\land\quad\epsilon>3\quad\land\quad\omega=\frac{2}{3}\epsilon-1\gg 1\,.

(12)

The relevant smoothing measure is not the expansion e-fold number:

N=\ln{a}\,,

(13)

but the growth of the comoving Hubble scale:

\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\equiv\ln{\bigl(a\left|H\right|\bigr)}\,.

(14)

Its differential is:

\displaystyle d\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}

\displaystyle=d\ln{a}+d\ln{\left|H\right|}

(15)

\displaystyle=H\,dt+\frac{\dot{H}}{H}dt

(16)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\epsilon\right)H\,dt\,.

(17)

Since
H<0
and
\epsilon>1
, one can conclude that:

d\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}=\left(\epsilon-1\right)\left|H\right|\,dt\,.

(18)

Using (
11
), the ekpyrotic distance is therefore:

d_{\rm ek}=\int d\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\,\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon}}{\left(\epsilon-1\right)}\,,

(19)

which is the ekpyrotic analogue of the kinematic part of the Lyth bound. For constant
\epsilon_{\rm ek}
:

d_{\rm ek}\simeq\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{\rm ek}}}{\left(\epsilon_{\rm ek}-1\right)}\,\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\,.

(20)

In the ultra-ekpyrotic limit
\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gg 1
:

d_{\rm ek}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\epsilon_{\rm ek}}}\,\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\,.

(21)

Hence, the ekpyrotic smoothing phase alone is sub-Planckian only if:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gtrsim 2\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}^{2}\,.

(22)

For
\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\simeq 60
, this gives:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gtrsim 7200\quad\land\quad\omega_{\rm ek}\gtrsim 4800\,.

(23)

This reproduces the basic ultra-fast-roll implication of small-field ekpyrosis
49
. The novelty developed below is to make the bound phase-resolved.

IV
Anisotropy suppression and a BKL distance bound

Ekpyrotic contraction is necessary for more than just shrinking the comoving Hubble radius, namely, it must also suppress the shear anisotropy underlying the Belinski-Khalatnikov-Lifshitz instability near spacelike singularities
15
;
35
. This yields a second, closely related distance constraint.

For an approximately homogeneous but anisotropic background, the shear contribution scales as:

\rho_{\sigma}\propto a^{-6}\,.

(24)

For an ekpyrotic scalar with a nearly constant equation of state:

\rho_{\phi}\propto a^{-2\epsilon}\,,

(25)

so that:

\frac{\rho_{\sigma}}{\rho_{\phi}}\propto a^{2\epsilon-6}\,.

(26)

Since
a
decreases during contraction, this ratio is suppressed only for:

\epsilon>3\,,

(27)

which is the usual ultra-stiff ekpyrotic condition. This is the same physical mechanism by which ekpyrotic contraction suppresses mixmaster-type chaotic behavior in the:

\omega>1

(28)

regime
35
.

Now, let us define the logarithmic anisotropy suppression requirement of the form:

\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}\equiv\ln{\left[\frac{\left(\frac{\rho_{\sigma}}{\rho_{\phi}}\right)_{i}}{\left(\frac{\rho_{\sigma}}{\rho_{\phi}}\right)_{f}}\right]}\,.

(29)

For a constant
\epsilon
value one gets:

\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}=2\left(\epsilon-3\right)\,\left|\Delta N\right|\,,

(30)

where:

\left|\Delta N\right|=\ln{\left(\frac{a_{i}}{a_{f}}\right)}\,.

(31)

The same phase is described by the exact relations:

\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}=\left(\epsilon-1\right)\,\left|\Delta N\right|\quad\land\quad d_{\rm ek}=\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,\left|\Delta N\right|\,.

(32)

Eliminating
\left|\Delta N\right|
using (
30
), the distance needed to achieve a prescribed BKL suppression is:

d_{\rm ek}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}\sqrt{2\epsilon}}{2\left(\epsilon-3\right)}\,.

(33)

For
\epsilon\gg 1
it yields:

d_{\rm ek}\simeq\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}}{\sqrt{2\epsilon}}\,.

(34)

If only the distance
d_{\rm rem}
is available for the ekpyrotic smoothing phase, then the exact BKL constraint is given by:

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}\sqrt{2\epsilon}}{2(\epsilon-3)}\leq d_{\rm rem}\,.

(35)

Introducing the quantity:

x=\sqrt{\epsilon}

(36)

gives:

d_{\rm rem}\,x^{2}-\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}}{\sqrt{2}}x-3d_{\rm rem}\geq 0\,,

(37)

and therefore:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\geq\left[\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}}{2\sqrt{2}\,d_{\rm rem}}+\sqrt{3+\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}^{2}}{8d_{\rm rem}^{2}}}\right]^{2}\,.

(38)

In the strong suppression limit:

\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}\gg d_{\rm rem}

(39)

one obtains the compact result of:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gtrsim\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}^{2}}{2d_{\rm rem}^{2}}\,.

(40)

Combining the smoothing and anisotropy requirements gives the conservative condition:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gtrsim\max\left[\frac{2\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}^{2}}{d_{\rm rem}^{2}},\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}^{2}}{2d_{\rm rem}^{2}}\right]\,.

(41)

In the simplest constant
\epsilon
model, the two terms are not independent because, at large values of
\epsilon
:

\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}=\frac{2\left(\epsilon-3\right)\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}}{\left(\epsilon-1\right)}\simeq 2\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\,.

(42)

However, they differ when the required anisotropy suppression is determined by initial shear, spatial curvature, or a non-constant ekpyrotic profile. In this sense, the BKL bound converts the distance budget into a criterion that simultaneously smooths and isotropizes.

V
Total distance budget

Let us introduce the notion of the
total invariant distance
:

d_{\rm tot}=d_{\rm ek}+d_{\rm conv}+d_{\rm b}+d_{\rm post}\,,

(43)

where
d_{\rm conv}
is the entropy-to-curvature conversion distance,
d_{\rm b}
is the bounce distance, and
d_{\rm post}
denotes post-bounce matching, reheating or relaxation. A small-field EFT-control criterion is:

d_{\rm tot}\leq d_{\max}\,,

(44)

where
d_{\max}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
corresponds to a Planckian field-space radius.
More generally, the maximum usable distance may be smaller than
d_{\rm max}
if the EFT cutoff decreases along the trajectory. A simple parametrization, motivated by distance-conjecture reasoning in quantum gravity (tower of states)
62
;
61
;
63
:

m_{\rm tower}(d)=m_{0}\,e^{-\alpha d}\quad\land\quad\Lambda_{\rm EFT}(d)=\Lambda_{0}\,e^{-\gamma d}\,,

(45)

where
d
is the dimensionless field-space distance. Requiring the largest curvature or bounce scale
H_{\rm max}
to remain below the cutoff gives:

H_{\rm max}<\Lambda_{0}\,e^{-\gamma d_{\rm tot}}\,.

(46)

Hence:

d_{\rm tot}<d_{\rm cut}\equiv\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{H_{\rm max}}\right)\,.

(47)

The effective distance available to the whole history is therefore:

d_{\rm allowed}\equiv\min\left[d_{\rm max},\frac{1}{\gamma}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{H_{\rm max}}\right)\right]\,.

(48)

This cutoff-corrected budget is particularly important for high-curvature, non-singular bounces. Increasing the bounce scale decreases the available field-space distance, unless the microscopic cutoff is parametrically greater.

Now, we can define:

d_{\rm aux}\equiv d_{\rm conv}+d_{\rm b}+d_{\rm post}\quad\land\quad d_{\rm rem}\equiv d_{\rm allowed}-d_{\rm aux}\,.

(49)

The ekpyrotic phase is possible only if:

d_{\rm rem}>0

(50)

and:

\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{\rm ek}}}{\left(\epsilon_{\rm ek}-1\right)}\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\leq d_{\rm rem}\,.

(51)

Assuming that:

x\equiv\sqrt{\epsilon_{\rm ek}}>1

(52)

yields:

\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\,x}{x^{2}-1}\leq d_{\rm rem}\,,

(53)

or, equivalently:

d_{\rm rem}\,x^{2}-\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\,x-d_{\rm rem}\geq 0\,.

(54)

The positive root of (
54
) is given by the following formula:

x\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}+\sqrt{2\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}^{2}+4d_{\rm rem}^{2}}}{2d_{\rm rem}}\,.

(55)

so that:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\geq\left[\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}}{\sqrt{2}\,d_{\rm rem}}+\sqrt{1+\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}^{2}}{2d_{\rm rem}^{2}}}\right]^{2}\,.

(56)

In the limit of:

\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\gg d_{\rm rem}\,,

(57)

we obtain the more compact form:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gtrsim\frac{2\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}^{2}}{d_{\rm rem}^{2}}\,,

(58)

with:

d_{\rm rem}=d_{\rm allowed}-d_{\rm conv}-d_{\rm b}-d_{\rm post}\,.

(59)

This result is the simplest expression of the distance-budget effect. Conversion and bounce costs decrease the available field-space distance for smoothing. This increases the required ekpyrotic fast-roll parameter.

For the exponential ekpyrotic potential:

V(\phi)=-V_{0}e^{-c\phi/M_{\rm Pl}}\,,

(60)

with the scaling solution:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}=\frac{c^{2}}{2}\,,

(61)

the inequality (
58
) produces:

c\gtrsim\frac{2\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}}{d_{\rm rem}}\,.

(62)

Thus, a complete sub-Planckian history with
d_{\rm allowed}=1
,
\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}=60
, and
d_{\rm aux}=0.5
requires:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gtrsim 2.88\times 10^{4},\quad\land\quad c\gtrsim 240\,,

(63)

which is
four times stronger
in
\epsilon
than the ekpyrotic phase only estimate.

VI
Entropy-conversion distance

In many ekpyrotic models, curvature perturbations are generated from the entropy perturbations
59
;
45
;
23
. Therefore, one usually introduce the tangent and normal unit vectors:

T^{I}=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{I}}{\dot{\sigma}}\quad\land\quad G_{IJ}T^{I}T^{J}=1\,,

(64)

and:

G_{IJ}N^{I}N^{J}=1\quad\land\quad G_{IJ}T^{I}N^{J}=0\,.

(65)

On the other hand, the covariant turn rate is defined by the relation:

D_{t}T^{I}=\Omega\,N^{I}\,.

(66)

The total bending angle is:

\Theta_{\rm conv}=\int\Omega\,dt\,.

(67)

The geodesic curvature of the field-space trajectory is:

\kappa_{g}\equiv\frac{\Omega}{\dot{\sigma}}\,.

(68)

Since:

ds_{\rm field}=\dot{\sigma}dt\,,

(69)

then:

d\Theta=\Omega\,dt=\kappa_{g}\,ds_{\rm field}\,.

(70)

Therefore:

\Theta_{\rm conv}=\int\kappa_{g}\,ds_{\rm field}\,.

(71)

If
\kappa_{g}\leq\kappa_{g,{\rm max}}
, then:

\Theta_{\rm conv}\leq\kappa_{g,{\rm max}}\,\Delta s_{\rm conv}\,.

(72)

Thus, a required bending angle
\Theta_{\rm req}
imposes the relationship:

d_{\rm conv}\geq\frac{\Theta_{\rm req}}{M_{\rm Pl}\,\kappa_{g,{\rm max}}}\,.

(73)

Equivalently, small distance conversion requires:

M_{\rm Pl}\,\kappa_{g,{\rm max}}\gtrsim\frac{\Theta_{\rm req}}{d_{\rm conv}}\,.

(74)

The physical interpretation is transparent: efficient conversion in a sub-Planckian interval requires a sharp bend or a small radius of curvature:

R_{\rm turn}=\kappa_{g}^{-1}\lesssim\frac{M_{\rm Pl}\,d_{\rm conv}}{\Theta_{\rm req}}\,.

(75)

It is also useful to express the same statement in terms of Hubble units. By defining:

\eta_{\perp}\equiv\frac{\Omega}{\left|H\right|}\quad\land\quad dn\equiv\left|H\right|\,dt\,.

(76)

one can write:

\Theta_{\rm conv}=\int\eta_{\perp}\,dn\,.

(77)

For:

\left|\eta_{\perp}\right|\leq\eta_{\perp,{\rm max}}

(78)

we obtain:

n_{\rm conv}\geq\frac{\Theta_{\rm req}}{\eta_{\perp,{\rm max}}}\,.

(79)

On the other hand:

d_{\rm conv}=\int\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,dn\,,

(80)

and for:

\epsilon\geq\epsilon_{{\rm conv},\min}

(81)

we conclude that:

d_{\rm conv}\geq\sqrt{2\epsilon_{{\rm conv},\min}}\,n_{\rm conv}\,.

(82)

Combining both conditions gives the following constraint:

\Theta_{\rm req}\leq\frac{\eta_{\perp,{\rm max}}}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{{\rm conv},\min}}}d_{\rm conv}\,.

(83)

Therefore, a small-field scenario cannot simultaneously have a very long, weakly turning and efficient conversion phase.

VI.1
Conversion windows from isocurvature and non-Gaussianity

The conversion distance is more than just a theoretical cost, it can also be bounded by residual isocurvature and non-Gaussianity. In a two-field transfer formalism one can write:

\mathcal{R}_{f}=\mathcal{R}_{i}+T_{\mathcal{R}S}\,\mathcal{S}_{i}\quad\land\quad\mathcal{S}_{f}=T_{\mathcal{S}S}\mathcal{S}_{i}\,.

(84)

Such a transfer matrix approach is a standard formalism in multifield perturbation theory

37
;
54
. A schematic residual isocurvature fraction is:

\beta_{\rm iso}\simeq\frac{T_{\mathcal{S}S}^{2}}{T_{\mathcal{R}S}^{2}+T_{\mathcal{S}S}^{2}}\,.

(85)

In correlated mixed models, Planck bounds the non-adiabatic contribution at the percent level. The precise number depends on the isocurvature mode and the specific correlation choice
7
. If
\beta_{\rm iso}<\beta_{\rm max}
, then:

\frac{\left|T_{\mathcal{R}S}\right|}{\left|T_{\mathcal{S}S}\right|}\gtrsim\sqrt{\frac{1-\beta_{\rm max}}{\beta_{\rm max}}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\beta_{\rm max}}-1}\,.

(86)

On superhorizon scales, the curvature perturbation is sourced by the turn rate, schematically:

\frac{d\mathcal{R}}{dn}\simeq 2\eta_{\perp}\mathcal{S}\,.

(87)

If
|\eta_{\perp}|\leq\eta_{\perp,{\rm max}}
and the entropy transfer during conversion is summarized by the residual factor
|T_{\mathcal{S}S}|
, a conservative conversion efficiency requirement is:

\Delta n_{\rm conv}\gtrsim\frac{\left|T_{\mathcal{S}S}\right|}{2\eta_{\perp,{\rm max}}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\beta_{\rm max}}-1}\,.

(88)

Using (
80
) gives:

d_{\rm conv}\gtrsim\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{{\rm conv},\min}}}{2\eta_{\perp,{\rm max}}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\beta_{\rm max}}-1}\left|T_{\mathcal{S}S}\right|\,.

(89)

However, the formula (
89
) should not be read as a universal rlation; it is a compact way of saying that CMB adiabaticity imposes a lower cost on conversion once a maximum turn rate is taken into account.

Non-Gaussianity pushes in the opposite direction. A sharp conversion of entropy to curvature can generate significant local-type non-Gaussianity in ekpyrotic and cyclic models
48
, though Planck detects no primordial non-Gaussianity signal
6
.

In a given conversion model, this can be represented as an upper bound of the form:

\left|\eta_{\perp}\right|\leq\eta_{\perp,{\rm NG}}\,.

(90)

Since
\Theta_{\rm req}
is given by the relation (
77
), one obtains:

\Delta n_{\rm conv}\geq\frac{\Theta_{\rm req}}{\eta_{\perp,{\rm NG}}}\,,

(91)

and therefore:

d_{\rm conv}\gtrsim\sqrt{2\epsilon_{{\rm conv},\min}}\frac{\Theta_{\rm req}}{\eta_{\perp,{\rm NG}}}\,.

(92)

Equations (
89
) and (
92
) define an observational conversion window. Isocurvature requires efficient conversion, whereas non-Gaussianity limits the sharpness of the conversion.

VII
Kinematic bounce distance

A non-singular bounce must satisfy the following conditions:

H(t_{b})=0\quad\land\quad\dot{H}(t_{b})>0\,.

(93)

In canonical Einstein-scalar theory:

\dot{H}=-\frac{\dot{\sigma}^{2}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\leq 0\,,

(94)

so a bounce requires NEC violation, modified gravity, ghost-condensate/Galileon/Horndeski/DHOST-type dynamics, loop effects or additional sectors
13
;
20
;
41
;
85
. A universal perturbative Lyth-like formula cannot be assigned to this phase. Nevertheless, its field-space distance is kinematically well defined:

d_{\rm b}=\frac{1}{M_{\rm Pl}}\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\dot{\sigma}\,dt\,.

(95)

Let us also define:

\Delta t_{b}=t_{+}-t_{-}\quad\land\quad\Delta H_{b}=H(t_{+})-H(t_{-})>0\,,

(96)

and a phenomenological
effective bounce coefficient
:

\dot{H}=\frac{\Xi(t)}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\dot{\sigma}^{2}\,,

(97)

with:

\Xi_{\rm eff}\equiv\frac{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\,\Delta H_{b}}{\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\dot{\sigma}^{2}dt}\,.

(98)

In the case of usual canonical evolution:

\Xi=-1\,.

(99)

so that, a successful bounce requires an effective positive contribution. By introducing:

I_{1}\equiv\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\dot{\sigma}\,dt\quad\land\quad I_{2}\equiv\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\dot{\sigma}^{2}\,dt\,,

(100)

and the shape factor:

S_{b}\equiv\frac{I_{1}^{2}}{\Delta t_{b}\,I_{2}}\quad\land\quad 0<S_{b}\leq 1\,,

(101)

where the upper bound follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\left(\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\dot{\sigma}\,dt\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}\dot{\sigma}^{2}\,dt\right)\left(\int_{t_{-}}^{t_{+}}dt\right)=I_{2}\,\Delta t_{b}\,.

(102)

Using the fact that:

I_{2}=2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\frac{\Delta H_{b}}{\Xi_{\rm eff}}

(103)

we get:

d_{\rm b}^{2}=\frac{I_{1}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}=S_{b}\,\Delta t_{b}\frac{I_{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}=\frac{2S_{b}\,\Delta H_{b}\,\Delta t_{b}}{\Xi_{\rm eff}}\,,

(104)

so that:

d_{\rm b}=\left(\frac{2S_{b}\,\Delta H_{b}\,\Delta t_{b}}{\Xi_{\rm eff}}\right)^{1/2}\,.

(105)

For a symmetric bounce from
H_{-}=-H_{B}
to
H_{+}=+H_{B}
, the parameters are given by:

\Delta H_{b}=2H_{B}\quad\land\quad\tau_{b}\equiv H_{B}\Delta t_{b}\,,

(106)

which yields into:

d_{\rm b}=2\sqrt{\frac{S_{b}\,\tau_{b}}{\Xi_{\rm eff}}}\,.

(107)

If only a distance
d_{{\rm rem},b}
remains for the bounce, then:

\tau_{b}\leq\frac{\Xi_{\rm eff}}{4S_{b}}d_{{\rm rem},b}^{2}\,.

(108)

Thus, a small-field bounce is short in units of the bounce scale unless:

\Xi_{\rm eff}\gg 1\quad\land\quad S_{b}\ll 1\,,

(109)

or the bounce itself consumes a distance of the Planckian order.

VII.1
Mechanism-resolved bounce sectors

The parametrization in (
107
) should not be interpreted as a universal dynamical law for all non-singular bounces. It is just a kinematic identity for a scalar field-mediated effective bounces if the quantities
S_{b}
,
\tau_{b}
and
\Xi_{\rm eff}
can be defined. Different microscopic bounce mechanisms assign different meanings to the bounce contribution in the total field-space budget. From that reason, it is useful to write:

d_{\rm b}\mapsto d_{b}^{(\mathcal{B})}\,,

(110)

where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the bounce mechanism. The distance criterion now becomes:

\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}\gg d_{\rm eff}
it yields:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gtrsim\frac{2\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}^{2}}{d_{\rm eff}^{2}}\,.

(134)

The BKL anisotropy requirement gives the additional exact constraint of the form:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\geq\left[\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}}{2\sqrt{2}\,d_{\rm eff}}+\sqrt{3+\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}^{2}}{8d_{\rm eff}^{2}}}\right]^{2}\,.

\epsilon

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\gtrsim\max\left[\frac{2\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}^{2}}{d_{\rm eff}^{2}},\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\rm BKL}^{2}}{2d_{\rm eff}^{2}}\right]\,.

(136)

ekpyrotic smoothing, anisotropy suppression, entropy conversion, and bounce dynamics are not independent from the point of view of field-space control

(154)

(155)

\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}=\frac{q\left(q-1\right)}{\tau^{2}}\quad\land\quad a^{2}H^{2}=\frac{q^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\,.

(156)

Introducing the dimensionless effective entropy mass parameter:

\mu_{s}\equiv\frac{m_{s}^{2}}{H^{2}}\,,

(157)

the relation (

v^{\prime\prime}_{s}+\left[k^{2}+\frac{\mu_{s}\,q^{2}-q\left(q-1\right)}{\tau^{2}}\right]v_{s}=0\,.

(158)

(160)

(161)

one obtains

\mu_{s}\equiv\frac{m_{s}^{2}}{H^{2}}=2-\epsilon-\left(\epsilon-1\right)^{2}\left[\left(\frac{4-n_{s}}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right]\,.

(162)

XII
Limitations

The entropy-curvature relation (
167
) assumes approximately constant
\epsilon
and a decoupled entropy mode during the generation stage. It should be regarded as a controlled analytic estimate, not as a replacement for full numerical perturbation evolution in a complete model. Nevertheless, it captures a robust tension: large
\epsilon
and small field distance require either a strongly tachyonic transverse potential, strong turns, or a highly curved scalar manifold. This is the same type of geometric sensitivity encountered in multifield inflationary systems with curved target spaces
77
.

Finally, the analogy with the Lyth bound should not be overstated. In inflation the field range is tied directly to
r
through slow-roll consistency relations. In ekpyrotic and bouncing cosmology, the more robust statement is the following: large smoothing range with efficient conversion and controlled bounce implies large field-space cost, not a universal relation between
r
and
\Delta\phi
.

XIII
Conclusions

We have formulated a field-space distance budget for ekpyrotic, bouncing and cyclic cosmologies. The central result is the cutoff-corrected master inequality (
131
) which extends the small-field ekpyrotic smoothing constraint to the complete non-inflationary history, which implies the lower bound (
133
). In addition, BKL suppression requires (
135
), or approximately (
136
). If
d_{\rm eff}\leq 0
, no sub-
d_{\rm allowed}
completion is possible regardless of how large
\epsilon_{\rm ek}
is.

The physical implications are direct. A fully small-field ekpyrotic/cyclic history tends to require ultra-fast-roll contraction, strong BKL suppression, sharp or geometrically supported entropy conversion, a short, strongly modified, or mechanism-resolved bounce, a sufficiently high EFT cutoff, and a carefully arranged entropy sector capable of producing the observed red scalar tilt. These requirements can be confronted with present and future data through
n_{s}
, running, non-Gaussianity, residual isocurvature, CMB tensor searches, stochastic GWs backgrounds and late-time dark energy reconstructions, and the cyclic slope hierarchy implied by a DESI-motivated CPL interpretation. In this sense the
distance budget is a non-inflationary analogue of the logic behind the Lyth bound
:
it does not universally predict

r
,
but it identifies the field-space cost of producing a smooth, observationally viable Universe without inflation
.

This appendix gives a minimal worked example, summarized visually in Fig.

It is a local effective description of a smooth bounce core, similar in spirit to ghost-condensate or Galileon realizations in which the NEC-violating stage is carried by scalar dynamics

Toy model scalar-mediated bounce diagnostics for the ansatz
H(t)=H_{B}\tanh(t/t_0)

core, and the normalized bounce distance
d_{b}^{({\rm toy})}/\sqrt{2H_{B}t_{0}/\Xi}

Let us consider a smooth symmetric ansatz:

H(t)=H_{B}\tanh{\left(\frac{t}{t_0}\right)}\,,

(214)

-\kappa t_{0}\leq t\leq\kappa t_{0}\quad\land\quad\kappa>0\,.

(215)

The bounce occurs at
t=0
, where
H=0
and:

\dot{H}(t)=\frac{H_{B}}{t_{0}}\sech^{2}{\left(\frac{t}{t_{0}}\right)}>0\,.

(216)

We impose also that the effective scalar-mediated bounce relation can be written as:

\dot{H}=\frac{\Xi}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\dot{\sigma}^{2}\quad\land\quad\Xi>0\,,

(217)

with approximately constant
\Xi
inside the bounce core. Then:

\dot{\sigma}(t)=M_{\rm Pl}\sqrt{\frac{2H_{B}}{\Xi t_{0}}}\,\sech{\left(\frac{t}{t_0}\right)}\,.

(218)

The dimensionless scalar distance consumed by the bounce is:

d_{b}^{({\rm toy})}(\kappa)=\frac{1}{M_{\rm Pl}}\int_{-\kappa t_{0}}^{\kappa t_{0}}\dot{\sigma}(t)\,dt=\sqrt{\frac{2H_{B}t_{0}}{\Xi}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\kappa}\,,

(219)

where:

\mathcal{I}_{\kappa}\equiv\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\sech{u}\,du=4\arctan\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)\right]\,.

(220)

For
\kappa\to\infty
,
\mathcal{I}_{\kappa}\to\pi
, so the localized bounce contribution approaches:

d_{b}^{({\rm toy})}\to\pi\sqrt{\frac{2H_{B}t_{0}}{\Xi}}\,.

(221)

Thus, the distance cost is reduced by increasing the phenomenological effective NEC-violating coefficient
\Xi
or by making the bounce short in the dimensionless combination
H_{B}t_{0}
.

By defining:

I_{1}=\int_{-\kappa t_{0}}^{\kappa t_{0}}\dot{\sigma}\,dt\quad\land\quad I_{2}=\int_{-\kappa t_{0}}^{\kappa t_{0}}\dot{\sigma}^{2}\,dt\,.

(222)

Using (
218
), one gets:

I_{1}=M_{\rm Pl}\sqrt{\frac{2H_{B}t_{0}}{\Xi}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\kappa}\quad\land\quad I_{2}=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\frac{4H_{B}}{\Xi}\tanh{\kappa}\,.

(223)

The duration and Hubble jump over the same core interval are:

\Delta t_{b}=2\kappa t_{0}\quad\land\quad\Delta H_{b}=2H_{B}\tanh{\kappa}\,.

(224)

The effective coefficient computed from its definition is:

\Xi_{\rm eff}=\frac{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\Delta H_{b}}{I_{2}}=\Xi\,,

(225)

and the shape factor is:

S_{\kappa}=\frac{I_{1}^{2}}{\Delta t_{b}I_{2}}=\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\kappa}^{2}}{4\kappa\tanh\kappa}\,.

(226)

The general bounce distance identity is then verified explicitly:

\left[d_{b}^{({\rm toy})}\right]^{2}=\frac{2S_{\kappa}\Delta H_{b}\Delta t_{b}}{\Xi_{\rm eff}}\,.

(227)

This example also illustrates why the shape factor is useful. The function
\dot{\sigma}(t)
is localized around
t=0
, and enlarging the formal interval by increasing
\kappa
adds little field distance but increases the nominal duration. The decrease of
S_{\kappa}
compensates for this bookkeeping choice.

Inserting the toy result into the mechanism-resolved distance budget gives:

d_{\rm eff}^{({\rm toy})}=d_{\rm allowed}^{({\rm toy})}-d_{\rm conv}-d_{\rm post}-\sqrt{\frac{2H_{B}t_{0}}{\Xi}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\kappa}\,.

(228)

The smoothing bound becomes:

\epsilon_{\rm ek}\geq\left[\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}}{\sqrt{2}\,d_{\rm eff}^{({\rm toy})}}+\sqrt{1+\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\rm sm}^{2}}{2[d_{\rm eff}^{({\rm toy})}]^{2}}}\right]^{2}\,,

(229)

provided that
d_{\rm eff}^{({\rm toy})}>0

d_{\rm eff}^{({\rm toy})}
becomes small or negative and the ekpyrotic fast-roll requirement becomes correspondingly stronger or impossible to satisfy within the assumed finite distance budget.
