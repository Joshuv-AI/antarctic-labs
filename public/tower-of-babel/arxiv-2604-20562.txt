Abstract
. We provide the full classification of equidistant decompositions of a two-dimensional Euclidean plane and a two-dimensional sphere.

53C20, 53C21, 53C23

Introduction

A decomposition of a smooth complete Riemannian manifold
M
into a
collection of pairwise equidistant closed leaves
L_{y}
,
y\in Y
,
is in one-to-one correspondence to the natural quotient map
P:M\to Y
,
where
Y
is a space of leaves.
In the most general case, when the leaves of the decomposition may
have different dimension and perhaps of regularity less then
C^{1,1}
,
the map
P:M\to Y
is called
submetry
.
Submetries were first introduced by Berestovskii
[
3
]

and defined as a map,
that sends balls of a given radius in
M
surjectively onto balls of the same radius in
Y
.
If, however, all the leaves
L_{y}
are
C^{1,1}
but still of different dimension,
the map
P:M\to Y
is called a
transnormal
(or Riemannian) submetry.
If
Y
is a smooth complete Riemannian manifold,
then the map
P
is a Riemannian submersion
[
2
]
.
The quotient map
P:M\to M/G
of the Riemannian manifold
M

associated to an isometric group action
G
provides canonical examples of
submetries that are not necessary submersions.

The structure of submetries
was the primary focus of the investigation in
[
7
]
.
One of their central results establishes that the fibers
L_{y}
are sets of
positive reach

r>0
.
By definition, it means that the closest foot-point projection on
L_{y}

is uniquely defined within a neighborhood
U(L_{y})\subset M
.
In this case, the distance function
d_{L_{y}}:U(L_{y})\to[0,r)
is a submetry itself.

Even though the sets of positive reach were studied in many works

[
5
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
,
examples of low regularity are hard to construct explicitly.

The aim of this paper is to present
the full classification of equidistant decompositions
of the two-dimensional Euclidean space and spherical space,
supplementing the known examples.

Before we state our first result,
let us introduce the following curve in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Let
\Pi_{a}
and
\Pi_{-a}
be two disjoint closed half planes.
Denote
l_{a}:=\partial\Pi_{a}
,
l_{-a}:=\partial\Pi_{-a}

and
h:=dist(l_{a},l_{-a})\geq 0
.
Fix the point
x_{0}\in l_{a}
and choose

y_{0}\in l_{-a}
such that the distance between
y_{0}
and
the orthogonal projection of
x_{0}
to
l_{-a}
is
2a
.
Consider two families of concentric half circles

{\mathcal{F}}_{a}=\Pi_{a}\cap\{S(x_{0},r_{i})\}_{i=0}^{\infty}\,\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,{\mathcal{F}}_{-a}=\Pi_{-a}\cap\{S(y_{0},r_{i})\}_{i=0}^{\infty}

where
S(x,r)
is a circle of radius
r
around
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and

r_{i}=(1+2i)\cdot a
for any
i=0,1,2,\dots
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
be a family of line segments in

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\backslash\left(\Pi_{a}\cup\Pi_{-a}\right)

orthogonal to
l_{\pm a}
connecting pairwise the
endpoints
l_{a}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
and
l_{-a}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{-a}
.
From the choice of
x_{0}
,
y_{0}
and
r_{i}
, we obtain that the union

\sigma_{a,h}:={\mathcal{F}}_{h}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{a}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{-a}

is a
C^{1,1}
curve on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for any real number
h\geq 0
and

a>0
(see Figure

).

Theorem 1
.

Let
P:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to Y
be a submetry with connected fibers
between a two-dimensional Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
a one-dimensional Alexandrov space
Y
.
Then up to isometry one of the following holds

i)

Y={\mathbb{R}}
and
P:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is an orthogonal projection;

ii)

Y=[0,\infty)

and
P
is a distance function to
L=P^{-1}(0)
and
L
is either a point, a closed straight line segment or a half line;

iii)

Y=[-a,a]
and

P
is a signed distance function to the curve
P^{-1}(0)=\sigma_{a,h}

for any
h\geq 0
.

The first two statements are not new. In fact,

i)
follows from more general result, established in
[
2
]

and
ii)
follows from Lemma 5.2 and Example 6.6 in
[
7
]
.
The third statement will be proved as Theorem
4
below.

at distance

Consider two families of concentric half circles

{\mathcal{F}}_{1}={\mathbb{S}}^{2}_{1}\cap\{S(x_{0},r_{i})\}_{i=0}^{k-1}\,\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,{\mathcal{F}}_{-1}={\mathbb{S}}^{2}_{-1}\cap\{S(y_{0},r_{i})\}_{i=0}^{k-1}

where
S(x,r)
are the points on the distance
r
from
x

{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
and

r_{i}=(1+2i)\cdot a

.
By the choice of
r_{i}

Theorem 2
.

Let
P:{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\to Y
be a submetry with connected leaves between
a two-dimensional spherical space
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
and a one-dimensional Alexandrov space
Y
.

Then the base space
Y
is a segment of the length
\pi/k
and

a)

If
k=1
,
then
P
is a quotient map of an isometric group action of
S^{1}
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
.

b)

If
k>1
, then up to isometry,

P
is the signed distance function to the curve
P^{-1}(0)=\sigma_{s}

for some
s
coprime with
k
.

When
k=1
, the submetry
P:{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\to Y^{1}
is transnormal.
In this case, the result coincides with the analogous result for
singular Riemannian foliations on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
due to Radeschi
[
13
]
.

By
[
9
]
, any submetry
P:X\to Y
between Alexandrov spaces admits
a canonical factorization
P=P_{1}\circ P_{0}
, where
the submetry
P_{0}:X\to Y_{0}
has connected fibers
and the submetry
P_{1}:Y_{0}\to Y
has discrete fibers.
All one-dimensional Alexandrov spaces
are the quotients of the real line
Y={\mathbb{R}}/G
by a discrete group of isometries

.
From the result of Lange
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]

follows that
all submetries

with discrete fibers are coverings.
Namely,
Y_{0}={\mathbb{R}}/G_{0}
, where
G_{0}
is a subgroup of
G

(see Subsection
2.3
).
These results in combination with our Theorems
1
and
2

gives us the full classification of the equidistant decompositions
of the two-dimensional Euclidean space and spherical space.

.

.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Definition and basic properties

A submetry
P:X\rightarrow Y
is a map between metric spaces
X
and
Y

which sends balls
B_{r}(x)
of radius
r
around any point
x\in X

surjectively onto the closed
r
-ball around
P(x)
, i.e.

P(B_{r}(x))=B_{r}(P(x))
.
The space
X
is called total space,
Y
is called base space.

Let
d_{A}:X\to[0,\infty)
be a distance function to a subset
A
in
X
.
The following property follows from the definition of the submetry.

Proposition 1.1

(Lemma 2.1.,
[
7
]
)
.

A map
P:X\rightarrow Y
between metric spaces is a submetry if and only if
P

is surjective and, for any
y\in Y
, we have
d_{y}\circ P=d_{P^{-1}(y)}
.

In particular, it means that the submetry
P:X\rightarrow Y

induces an equidistant decomposition of the space
X
.
Indeed, for any two points
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y

the distance between fibers

P^{-1}(y_{1})
and
P^{-1}(y_{2})
is constantly equal to
d(y_{1},y_{2})
on
Y
.
The opposite is also true.
In fact, if metric space
X
admits a decomposition
into a collection of pairwise equidistant leaves
L_{y}
,
y\in Y
,
then the set of leaves
Y
with induced distance
becomes a metric space.
Then the canonical projection
P:X\to Y
is a submetry.

The submetry
P:X\to Y
induces some properties of the total space
X
onto
Y

(see
[
3
, Proposition 1]
).
In particular, if
X
is a smooth complete Riemannian manifold
M
,
then
Y
is a complete and proper metric space.
We may also replace
Y
with its given metric by

P(M)
with an induced length metric.
Due to
[
7
, Corollary 2.10]

P:M\to Y
remains a submetry.
Thus, without loss of generality, we assume that
P:M\to Y

is surjective and
Y
is a length space.

The restriction of a submetry
P:X\rightarrow Y
to
an open subset
A\subset X
might not be a submetry, but it is a local submetry.
The definition of local submetry was given in
[
7
, Definition 2.7]
.
However, for us is no need to localize the definition, since
by
[
7
, Corollary 2.9]
a local submetry
P:M\to Y

from a complete Riemannian manifold
M
is a submetry.

Additionally,
if
P:M\to Y
is a submetry from the Riemannian manifold
M
,
then
Y
is also a locally contractible.
In fact, by
[
7
, Corollary 1.5]
,
the tangent space
T_{y}Y
has a canonical decomposition

T_{y}Y={\mathbb{R}}^{l}\times T_{y}^{0}Y
, for some
l\geq 0
,
where
T_{y}^{0}Y
is the Euclidean cone over
an Alexandrov space of a diameter at most
\pi/2
.
The exponential map
exp_{y}
defines a homeomorphism from the

r
-ball around the origin in
T_{y}Y
to the
r
-ball in
Y
.
Since the ball around the origin in
T_{y}Y
is contractible,
so is the ball in
Y
.

1.2.
Horizontal lift

Given a submetry
P:M\to Y
,
a rectifiable curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
is called
horizontal

if the length of
\gamma
in
M
equals the length of
P\circ\gamma
in
Y
.

Remind, that
M
is a smooth Riemannian manifold
and
Y
is a proper length space.
Then, from
[
7
, Lemma 2.8]

it follows that for any rectifiable curve
\sigma:[a,b]\to Y

starting at
\sigma(a)=y\in Y

there exists a horizontal curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M

such that
\gamma(a)\in P^{-1}(y)
and
\sigma=P\circ\gamma
.
The curve
\gamma
is then called a
horizontal lift
of
\sigma
.
For a locally Lipchitz curves it is equivalent to
the property that the velocities
|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|
and
|\sigma^{\prime}(t)|

coincide for all
t\in[a,b]
.

Following
[
7
]
, we call a curve
\gamma:[0,s]\to M

a
P
-minimal geodesic
if
it is parametrized by arclength and

dist\left(P\left(\gamma(0)\right),P\left(\gamma(s)\right)\right)=s.

The
P
-minimal geodesic
\gamma:[0,s]\to M
, if it exists,
realizes the distance between the fibers through its endpoints.
If
\gamma
is a
P
-minimal geodesic,
then
\gamma
is a geodesic in
M
and

P\circ\gamma
is a geodesic in
Y
.
Moreover, any horizontal lift
of a geodesic
\sigma:[a,b]\to Y
is a
P
-minimal geodesic.
Using this statement in combination with Proposition
1.1
, we get the following.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
M
a complete Riemannian manifold.

(1)

If
P:M\to[a,\infty)
is a submetry onto half line,
then
P
is a distance function to the set
P^{-1}(a)
.

(2)

If
P:M\to[a,b]
is a submetry onto a segment,
then
P
is a distance function to the set
P^{-1}(a)
(or to
P{-1}(b)
),
or, equivalently,
P
is a signed distance to the middle fiber
P^{-1}(\frac{b-a}{2})
.

However, if
\gamma
is a horizontal geodesic in
M
,
then its image
P\circ\gamma

is not necessary a geodesic in
Y
, but a
quasigeodesic (see
[
6
, Proposition 4]
).
Moreover, they have a special form

Proposition 1.2

(Corollary 7.4,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
P:X\to Y
be a local submetry between Alexandrov regions.
Let
\gamma:[0,l]\to X
be a geodesic.
Then
P\circ\gamma
is a quasigeodesic and
there is a partition
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots,<t_{N}\leq l

such that each restriction
\left(P\circ\gamma\right)|_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}

is a locally minimizing geodesic.

1.3.
Sets of positive reach

The following key result was proved in the work by Kapovitch and Lytchak.

Proposition 1.3

(Theorem 1.1
[
7
]
)
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold and
P:M\to Y
is a submetry.
Then any fiber
L=P^{-1}(y)
,
y\in Y
of the submetry
P

is a set of positive reach in
M
.

We recall that the subset
L
of a Riemannian manifold
M

has
positive reach

if the closest point projection on
L
is
uniquely defined in a neighborhood
U(L)
in
M
.
Moreover, the following is also true

Proposition 1.4

(Proposition 6.3
[
7
]
)
.

Let
L\subset M
be a closed subset of positive reach,
nowhere dense in a Riemannian manifold
M
.
Then
d_{L}:U(L)\to[0,\infty)
is a local submetry with
L=P^{-1}(0)
.

The structure of subsets of positive reach is well understood
and it does not depend on the Riemannian metric,
but only on the
C^{1,1}
-atlas.
Such sets were investigated
in particular in
[
5
]
,
[
1
]
,

[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,

[
14
]
,
[
12
]
.

Notice also, that
the topological dimension
\dim L
of a set of positive reach
L

coincides with its Hausdorff-dimension
[
5
]
.
The set of positive reach
L
in
M

has a well-defined
tangent cone

T_{x}L

that is a convex subset of
T_{x}M

for all
x\in L

[
5
, Theorem 4.8]

and
\dim L
is the maximum of the
dimensions of convex cones
T_{x}L
.

Let
L_{reg}
be the set of all points
x\in L
such that
the tangent cone
T_{x}L
is isometric to
{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim L}
.
Then, by
[
14
, Theorem 7.5]
the subset
L_{reg}

is not-empty, open in
L
and it is a
C^{1,1}
-submanifold of
M
.
In case when
L\backslash L_{reg}
is empty,

L
is a
C^{1,1}
-manifold without boundary.
Moreover

Proposition 1.5

(Proposition 1.4.,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
L
be a set of positive reach in
M
.
Then the following are equivalent:

1)

The set
L
is a
C^{1,1}
-submanifold;

2)

The set
L
is a topological manifold;

3)

All tangent spaces
T_{x}L
for
x\in L
are Euclidean spaces.

If
L\backslash L_{reg}
is not empty, then

L\backslash L_{reg}
is locally closed subset of positive reach
of the dimension
\leq\dim L-1
.
The following Lemma is a direct corollary of
[
14
, Theorem D]

and
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
adapted for our case.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
L\neq\emptyset
be a connected
set of positive reach and empty interior in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
or
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
.
Then
L
is either a point or a
C^{1,1}
curve
(one-dimensional manifold) with or without boundary.

2.
Properties and preparations

2.1.
Transnormal regions

Let
P:M\to Y
is a submetry.
for any point
x\in M
consider the fiber
L=P^{-1}(P(x))
passing through
x
.
As we said before, the fiber
L

has a well defined tangent cone
T_{x}L
.
The
normal (or horizontal) cone

N_{x}L
is a polar cone of
T_{x}L
in
T_{x}M
.
In another words,
N_{x}L
is a set of all vectors in
T_{x}M

enclosing angles
\geq\pi/2
with all vectors in
T_{x}L
.

For any point
x\in L
and a unit vector
h\in N_{x}L
,
the geodesic
\gamma_{h}:[0,l)\to M
starting in
x

in the direction
h
and parametrized by arclength
satisfies

(2.1)

dist\left(L,\gamma_{h}(s)\right)=s,

for all
s
such that
\gamma_{h}(s)\in U(L)
.
Recall, that
U(L)
is a maximal open set in
which
L
has positive reach.

Note, that the equation (
2.1
) do not
guarantee, that the geodesic
\gamma_{h}
starting in horizontal direction

h\in N_{x}L
is itself a horizontal curve for any
s\in(0,l)
.
However, it is true locally, meaning, that for any
x\in M

there is
r_{x}\in(0,l)
, such that
the geodesic
\gamma_{h}:[0,r_{x})\to M
is a horizontal curve
and a
P
-minimal geodesic (see
[
7
, Proposition 7.3]
).

We can separate regions in
M
, inside which
\gamma_{h}
would be the global
horizontal curve. Namely,

Definition 2.1
.

Let
P:M\to Y
be a submetry and
let
{\mathcal{V}}
be an open set in
M
.
Let
\gamma_{h}:[0,l)\to M
be a geodesic emanating at
x\in{\mathcal{V}}

in the horizontal direction
h\in N_{x}L
.
Assume
\gamma_{h}
is perpendicular to any fiber it intersects
for all times when
\gamma_{h}
is in
{\mathcal{V}}
.
Then the set
{\mathcal{V}}
is called a
transnormal region
of the
manifold
M
.

If
{\mathcal{V}}=M
, then the submetry
P:M\to Y
is called
transnormal
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
P:M\to Y
be a submetry
and let
L=P^{-1}(P(x))
denote the fiber through any point
x\in M
.
Then an open set
{\mathcal{V}}\subset M
is a transnormal region of
M

if and only if

L\cap{\mathcal{V}}\subset L_{reg}
for any
x\in{\mathcal{V}}
.

Recall, that
L_{reg}
is set of all points
x\in L
such that
the tangent cone
T_{x}L
is isometric to
{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim L}
,
and
L_{reg}
is a
C^{1,1}
-submanifold of
M
.

The proof of Lemma
2.1
is almost
word by word repeating the part of the proof of

[
7
, Proposition 12.5]
.
For the completeness, we include it here as well.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

To prove the forward direction, we proceed by contradiction.
Let
{\mathcal{V}}\subset M
be a transnormal region of the submetry
P:M\to Y
.
Assume now, that there is a point
x\in{\mathcal{V}}
and
a fiber
L
through
x
such that
L\cap{\mathcal{V}}\not\subset L_{reg}
.
It means, there is a point
y\in L\cap{\mathcal{V}}
such that
the tangent space
T_{y}L
is not a vector space.
Therefore, the normal space
N_{x}L
is also not a vector space.
Hence, we can find a unit vector
h\in N_{y}L
such
that
-h\not\in N_{y}L
.
Since
{\mathcal{V}}\subset M
is an open set, then there is a small
neighborhood
{\mathcal{U}}
of the point
y
such that
{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
.
Hence, there is a small
\varepsilon
such that
the geodesics
\gamma_{h}:[0,\varepsilon]\to M
and
\gamma_{-h}:[0,\varepsilon]\to M

are both in
{\mathcal{U}}
.
However,
\gamma_{h}:[0,\varepsilon]\to M
is horizontal geodesic,
but
\gamma_{-h}:[0,\varepsilon]\to M
is not.
If we then take a vector
v=-\gamma^{\prime}_{h}(t)
for
t\in(0,\varepsilon)
,
then the geodesic in the direction
v
does not stay perpendicular
for all fibers it intersect inside
{\mathcal{V}}
, resulting a contradiction.

The sufficiency can be shown also by contradiction.
Assume
\gamma_{h}:[0,l)\to M
is the geodesic
starting at the point
x\in{\mathcal{V}}
in the horizontal direction
h

and lies inside
{\mathcal{V}}
for all times
[0,l)
.
By
[
7
, Proposition 7.3]
, there exist
r\in(0,l]

such that
\gamma_{h}:[0,r]\to M
is a horizontal curve.
Assume
r
is the maximal with this property and
r<l
.
Then
\gamma_{h}
meets the fiber
L_{r}:=(P^{-1}\circ P)(\gamma_{h}(r))

in the horizontal direction
-\gamma^{\prime}_{h}(r)\in N_{\gamma_{h}(r)}L_{r}
.
Moreover, the normal space
N_{\gamma_{h}(r)}L_{r}
is a vector space.
Hence, the opposite direction
\gamma^{\prime}_{h}(r)
is also horizontal.
Since the point
\gamma_{h}(r)\in{\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}
is open, then
there is a small
\varepsilon>0
such that the restriction

\gamma_{h}:[r,r+\varepsilon)\to M
is also a horizontal curve.
Since
r<l
, we get a contradiction to the minimality of
r
.
Hence,
{\mathcal{V}}
is a transnormal region.
∎

The horizontal geodesic
\gamma_{h}
leaves the transnormal region
{\mathcal{V}}

moment when it meets a fiber
L
outside of its regular part,
i.e. when
\gamma_{h}(t)\in L\backslash L_{reg}
.
If it is a case, then the following result holds.

Proposition 2.1

(Theorem 12.4
[
7
]
)
.

Let
P:M\to Y

2.2.
Submetry with connected leaves

When the map

is a fiber bundle,
then using long exact sequence of homotopy groups
one can deduce that
if the total space

is simply connected and
all fibers are connected manifolds,
then the base space
Y
is also simply connected.
Similar statement is true when the map

is a submetry.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
P:M\to Y
be a submetry between a complete,
simply connected Riemannian manifold
M

and a connected metric space
Y
.
If all fibers of
P
are connected, then
Y
is also simply connected.

Proof.

Consider a loop
\tilde{c}(t):[0,l]\to Y

such that
\tilde{c}(0)=\tilde{c}(l)=p
.
Since
Y
is a locally contractible space,

\tilde{c}(t)
can be approximated by a piecewise geodesic,
closed loop
c(t):[0,b]\to Y
homotopic to
\tilde{c}(t)
and
c(0)=c(b)=p
.
The curve
c(t)
is rectifiable and
therefore, by
[
7
, Lemma 2.8]
,
there exists a horizontal lift

\gamma(t):[0,b]\to M
of the curve
c(t)
.
The points
\gamma(0)
and
\gamma(b)
are on the same fiber
L=P^{-1}(p)
but they may not coincide.
Since
L
is connected, then there is a path on
L

connecting points
\gamma(0)
and
\gamma(b)
.
Hence, the curve
\gamma(t)
can be extended to the loop

\tilde{\gamma}:[0,a]\to M
where

\tilde{\gamma}(0)=\tilde{\gamma}(a)
.
Since
M
is simply connected,
then there is a continuous homotopy map

F(t,s):[0,a]\times[0,1]\to M
such that

F(t,0)=\tilde{\gamma}(t)
and
F(t,1)=\{q\}
, i.e.

F
contracts the loop
\tilde{\gamma}

to the point

within
M
.

The map
P\circ F(t,\cdot)
brings the loop from the total space
to the base space for any
s\in[0,1]
.
Hence, this map
P\circ F(t,\cdot)
is the homotopy between the loop
c_{p}(t)
and the point
P(q)
.
Therefore, the space
Y
is also simply connected.
∎

Necessary for us is the following corollary of Lemma
2.2
.

Corollary 2.1
.

There is no submetry

with connected fibers
when

is an Euclidean space

or spherical space
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
of the dimension

Theorem 5
.

Let
P:{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\to Y
be a submetry with connected leaves between
a two-dimensional unite sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
and a one-dimensional Alexandrov space
Y
.

Then
Y
is a segment of the length
\pi/k
and

a)

If
k=1
,
then
P
is a quotient map of an isometric group action of
S^{1}
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
.

b)

If
k>1
, then up to isometry,

P
is the signed distance function to the curve
P^{-1}(0)=\sigma_{s}

for some
s
coprime with
k
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
is compact, the base space

must also be compact
(see
[
7
, Lemma 2.3.]
and
[
3
, Proposition 1]
).
Moreover, from Lemma
2.2
we also know, that

is
simply-connected.
Therefore,
Y
must be a segment
[-a,a]
.

By Lemma
1.1
,
P
can be identified, up to isometry,
with the signed distance function to the middle fiber
L_{0}=P^{-1}(0)
.
By Lemma
1.2
,

L_{0}
is a
C^{1,1}
curve without boundary.

To construct
L_{0}
, we consider the geodesic
\gamma(t):[0,t_{0})\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}

emanating from
x\in L_{0}
in the direction
h_{x}\in N_{x}L
.
The geodesic in the opposite direction
-h_{x}\in N_{x}L

we will write as
\gamma(-t)
,

.
Note that for any horizontal geodesic the time of its existence satisfies

(4.2)

|t_{0}|\leq\pi.

The projection

is a quasigeodesic in
Y
.
If

, then by Proposition
1.2
, there is a partition of
[0,t_{0})
such that

(4.3)

(P\circ\gamma)\left(2ka+(-1)^{k}\varepsilon\right)=\varepsilon,\,\,\,\varepsilon\in[-a,a]

when

such that
2ka+(-1)^{k}\varepsilon\leq t_{0}
.
In the opposite direction same holds replacing

with
-t
and
\varepsilon

-\varepsilon
.
In combination with (
4.2

Before proceeding with the construction of

, we first analyze the boundary fibers

.
By Lemma
1.2

curve that may have boundary.

First, we observe that

{\mathbb{S}}^{2}

\leq a

.
Then

is compact, both

and

must be compact domains.
Furthermore, neither
L_{+}
nor
L_{-}
can be a closed curve;
otherwise the boundary of

Since the boundary of

is the fiber

,
this contradicts its connectedness.
Therefore, each boundary fiber,

and

,
is either a single point or a closed segment.

Case 1.

Suppose the boundary fiber

is a single point

.
Then the fiber

is the points on the distance
a
from

,

is a circle of the radius

.
The points of the fiber

lies on the distance

from

.
If

.

-action on

.
