Delayed single-stage surgical management using a peroneus brevis muscle flap for fracture-related infection after lateral malleolus fixation: a retrospective case series
Authors: Karen Dyreborg, Andreas Ibrahim Jørgensen, Anton A. N. Peterlin, Hans Gottlieb
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFracture-related infection (FRI) following lateral malleolus fracture fixation complicated by soft tissue breakdown represents a challenging clinical problem with no established standard of care. This study evaluates a treatment strategy consisting of prolonged antibiotic suppression until fracture union, followed by delayed single-stage surgical management.MethodsThis retrospective consecutive case series included 11 patients treated between 2020 and 2023 at a multidisciplinary orthopaedic infection unit. All patients were managed according to a standardized protocol involving oral antibiotic suppression until radiologically confirmed fracture consolidation. This was followed by a single-stage surgical procedure including implant removal, radical debridement, deep tissue sampling, local antibiotic therapy, and soft tissue reconstruction using a distally based peroneus brevis muscle flap with split-thickness skin grafting.ResultsEleven consecutive patients (median age 58 years) were treated according to the protocol. Six patients underwent antibiotic suppression for a median duration of 147 days prior to surgery. Among patients available for outcome assessment, infection control was achieved in 10 of 10 surviving patients. One patient died of unrelated causes within 14 days postoperatively and was not classified as treatment failure. Soft tissue complications were common, with partial flap necrosis occurring in 4 patients (36%) and split-skin graft loss in 6 patients (55%), predominantly managed conservatively.ConclusionsDelayed single-stage surgical management following prolonged antibiotic suppression may represent a feasible treatment strategy for selected patients with FRI after lateral malleolus fixation. However, the high rate of soft tissue complications and the limited sample size warrant cautious interpretation. Further comparative studies are needed to define the role of this approach.

Introduction

Fracture-related infection (FRI) has been defined by international consensus, enhancing diagnostic accuracy and standardizing reporting across centres [1]. FRI following internal fixation of lateral malleolus fractures, poses significant management challenges, particularly when accompanied by lateral soft tissue breakdown and exposed implants. Infection rates after ankle fracture surgery range from 7% to 13%, increasing in patients with comorbidities such as diabetes, smoking, or vascular disease [2–5].

Following ankle fractures, FRI is often complicated by inadequate soft tissue coverage, which makes surgical management increasingly complex due to limited local reconstructive options, with the high risk of persistent infection or ultimately amputation [2, 6, 7]. Optimal outcomes require a multidisciplinary approach integrating orthopaedic surgeons, plastic surgeons, microbiologists, and infectious disease expertise [3, 4, 8], i.e. the multidisciplinary team (MDT).

Surgical treatment principles for FRI in lateral malleoli fractures include meticulous debridement, deep tissue samples, implant removal, dead space management, and durable soft tissue coverage [6, 9]. Single-stage protocols have been proposed to streamline care, reduce hospital stays, and minimize reoperation risk. While the debridement, antibiotics, and implant retention (DAIR) approach remain relevant in early infections, its success rates are limited, particularly in the context of mature biofilm formation and unstable fractures [10, 11].

Currently, no evidence-based consensus exists for the management of FRI involving the lateral malleolus complicated by wound dehiscence. The peroneus brevis (PB) muscle flap has been described as a reliable option for local tissue reconstruction in the distal third of the leg [12, 13]. This study presents a retrospective consecutive case series evaluating the clinical outcomes of a standardized single-stage approach, preceded by infection suppression until fracture union, and finalized with PB flap coverage within a dedicated MDT framework.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

This study represents a retrospective consecutive case series conducted at a tertiary orthopaedic infection unit between February 2020 and January 2023.

All patients treated according to a standardized treatment protocol for fracture-related infection (FRI) following lateral malleolus fixation with concomitant lateral wound dehiscence were included.

The indication for this treatment strategy was determined by a multidisciplinary team (MDT) consisting of orthopaedic surgeons, plastic surgeons, and infectious disease specialists.

No patients treated with this protocol during the study period were excluded.

Although consecutive, the cohort represents a selected subgroup of patients considered eligible for this treatment strategy, which may introduce selection bias.

Initial management and fracture consolidation

Patients presenting with non-healed fractures started empiric oral antibiotic therapy (Fig. 1). Clinical and radiological follow-up in an MDT-led outpatient clinic until fracture union was confirmed by computer tomography (CT) imaging (Fig. 2). Surgical planning included preoperative vascular mapping using CT angiography.

Surgical technique

Once fracture healing was confirmed, patients underwent single-stage revision surgery. This included implant removal, five intraoperative bone biopsies, radical debridement of necrotic and infected tissue. Debridement was continued until viable bone was encountered, defined as punctate cortical bleeding (“vital Haversian bleeding”). Revised bone voids were managed using antibiotic-loaded calcium sulphate/hydroxyapatite composites (CERAMENT G: 17.5 mg gentamicin mL-1; CERAMENT V: 66 mg vancomycin mL-1; BONESUPPORT AB, Sweden).

A distally based PB flap was harvested and transposed into the skin defect. Split-thickness skin grafts were applied, and donor sites were closed primarily (Fig. 3). Distal flap necrosis in a PB flap is likely related to marginal perfusion at the distal tip of the flap. Preventive strategies include preservation of distal perforators, minimizing flap length, and avoiding tension.

Antibiotic regimen

Standardized postoperative protocol included 7 days of intravenous antibiotics, followed by 5 weeks of culture-guided oral antibiotics in accordance with the OVIVA protocol [14].

Empirical oral antibiotic suppression was initiated based on institutional guidelines targeting common FRI pathogens and adjusted according to prior microbiological findings when available. In cases with negative intraoperative cultures, postoperative antibiotic therapy was guided by prior microbiology, clinical presentation, and multidisciplinary consensus.

Follow-up and outcome measures

The primary outcome was clinical failure, defined as the need for revision surgery, including further debridement for a nonhealing wound, a persistent sinus tract at the surgical site, or any proximal amputation within 12 months. Secondary outcomes included flap viability or postoperative flap complications, procedures. Patients were seen in the outpatient clinic at two weeks, eight weeks, and 12 months postoperatively.

Statistical analysis

Given the retrospective design and limited sample size, statistical analysis was descriptive. Continuous variables are reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), and categorical variables are presented as absolute numbers and percentages. No comparative or inferential statistical analyses were performed.

A total of 11 consecutive patients (6 males, 5 females) with a median age of 58 years (range 42–76) were treated according to the described protocol during the study period (Table 1).

No recurrence of infection was observed among surviving patients at 12 months fol-low-up. Infection control was achieved in 10 of 10 patients available for outcome as-sessment. One patient died of congestive heart failure within 14 days postoperatively, unrelated to the surgical intervention, and was not classified as treatment failure.

At the time of referral, five patients (45%) had radiologically consolidated fractures and proceeded directly to surgery. The remaining six patients (55%) underwent sup-pressive antibiotic therapy for a median duration of 147 days (range: 63–289) until CT-confirmed fracture union. No patient required additional stabilization at the time of index surgery.

Intraoperative cultures were positive in seven patients (64%). The most frequently isolated pathogen was Staphylococcus aureus (n = 4), followed by Escherichia coli (n = 2), Proteus spp. (n = 2), Pseudomonas aeruginosa (n = 1), and Staphylococcus epidermidis (n = 1).

No recurrence of infection was observed in any of the surviving patients at 12 months follow-up. The overall success rate was 100% (10 of 10 surviving patients); one pa-tient (ASA III, aged 76) died of congestive heart failure within 14 days postoperatively, unrelated to surgical intervention. This case was not classified as treatment failure, as death was unrelated to the surgical intervention and flap viability was maintained. No intraoperative complications occurred in any of the cases.

Partial flap necrosis occurred in four patients (36%), primarily involving the distal por-tion of the muscle. All cases were managed conservatively with local wound care, with complete healing achieved without surgical revision. Split-skin graft loss was observed in six cases (55%), with one requiring re-grafting under general anaesthe-sia. All other cases healed secondarily with outpatient management.

The median length of hospital stay was 11.8 days (IQR: 7–16), primarily determined by the need to adjust intravenous antibiotics based on culture results. All patients re-ceived seven days of relevant intravenous antibiotics, followed by five weeks of oral therapy according to sensitivity profiles.

Discussion

FRIs following ankle fracture fixation remains a complex clinical challenge, particular-ly when accompanied by lateral wound dehiscence and compromised soft tissue coverage. This study reports favourable outcomes using standardized single-stage protocol with definitive soft tissue reconstruction using the PB muscle flap.

Although the surgical intervention was performed in a single stage, the overall treat-ment strategy should be understood as staged, as it relies on prolonged preoperative antibiotic suppression.

The rationale for initial infection suppression prior to surgical intervention is grounded in established treatment principles, as described by Tsang et al., allowing for me-chanical bone union while mitigating the risk of persistent infection during fracture healing [10, 15]. In this study, this strategy facilitated definitive single-stage revision surgery without the need for further stabilization, thereby lowering the cumulative sur-gical burden and hospitalization time.

Our treatment algorithm aligns with recommendations from international expert groups emphasizing the importance of a multidisciplinary team and structured proto-cols in FRI management [2, 6, 16]. The success rate of infection resolution in 10 of 10 patients (100%) corroborates findings from McNally et al., who demonstrated similar efficacy using a comparable one-stage approach involving absorbable, antibiotic-loaded bone void fillers and robust soft tissue management [16]. However, our ob-served complication rates were substantial, with 36% partial flap necrosis and 55% split-skin graft loss. Although most complications were managed conservatively, these findings highlight that this reconstructive approach is associated with consider-able challenges. And also, that some degree of necrosis may be unavoidable in compromised soft tissue environments.

These findings support prior reports by Antonini et al., who demonstrated the reliabil-ity of the PB flap in addressing soft tissue defects of the distal lower limb [12]. Preser-vation of the distal perforator during flap mobilization was paramount, consistent with anatomical and clinical studies emphasizing vascular reliability [12, 13].

Our protocol’s emphasis on achieving fracture union prior to surgical revision may explain the low rate of reoperation and implant failure, contrasting with previous stud-ies where debridement and flap coverage were performed over unhealed fractures. Ovaska et al. reported a higher incidence of secondary surgical interventions when hardware was retained, or fractures were incompletely healed at the time of soft tis-sue reconstruction [17]. In comparison, our strategy of delaying flap coverage until osseous consolidation likely contributed to improved stability, a known factor in infec-tion eradication [2].

Despite a high rate of culture-negative results (36%), clinical resolution of infection suggests that prolonged oral antibiotic suppression can be effective, although it may compromise microbial detection at the time of revision [18, 19]. This phenomenon is consistent with the literature on biofilm-related infections, where long-term suppres-sion can induce a viable but non-culturable state, particularly in Staphylococcus au-reus [20]. While Depypere et al. advocated for early aggressive debridement to pre-vent biofilm maturation [21], our findings demonstrate that delayed one-stage surgery after fracture union remains a viable alternative in select cases, provided infection is well suppressed and managed within an MDT framework.

Our protocol did not include DAIRs, despite its early-intervention advantages. DAIR has shown limited efficacy in fracture settings, with up to 50% of patients requiring multiple procedures to achieve infection control [11]. The absence of implant failure and universal fracture healing in our cohort supports the appropriateness of a de-layed one-stage approach under specific clinical conditions.

Study limitations include the retrospective design, small sample size, and lack of a control group. Therefore, the results should not be interpreted as evidence of superi-ority over alternative treatment strategies. Additionally, extended preoperative antibi-otic use precluded reliable pre-debridement cultures in some patients, limiting bacte-riological correlation. Nonetheless, all surviving patients were followed for a minimum of 12 months, exceeding the 90-day window currently deemed insufficient for FRI surveillance [22].

Conclusion

This retrospective consecutive case series suggests that delayed single-stage surgi-cal management following prolonged antibiotic suppression may represent a feasible treatment strategy in selected patients with fracture-related infection after lateral mal-leolus fixation. However, the high rate of soft tissue complications and potential se-lection bias warrant cautious interpretation. Further prospective comparative studies are required.
