An axially symmetric stationary N-center solution of Einstein’s vacuum equations
Aleksandr A. Shaideman, Jesus D. Arias H, Kirill V. Golubnichiy

Abstract
Using the Euclidon method [ 1 ] - [ 3 ] , a stationary solution of Einstein’s vacuum equations was obtained, describing N rotating axially symmetric masses, which in the absence of rotation describes N arbitrary axially symmetric static masses, for example, N Zipoy masses on the axis of symmetry, and in the absence of distortion, N Kerr-NUT solutions.

3
Basic equations

As was shown by Papapetrou
[
6
]
, the line element, describing a stationary axially symmetric gravitational field without loss of generality can be present in the following canonical form:

ds^{2}=f^{-1}[e^{2\gamma}(d\rho^{2}+dz^{2})+\rho^{2}d\varphi^{2}]-f(dt-\omega d\varphi)^{2}

(3.1)

Here
\rho,\varphi,z
and
t
are canonical Weyl coordinates and time, respectively;
f(\rho,z)
,
\gamma(\rho,z)
and
\omega(\rho,z)
are unknown functions to be determined from the field equations.

The vacuum Einstein equations are given by

R_{ik}=0,

where
R_{ik}
is the Ricci tensor .

Therefore we obtain all the Einstein equations in the case of a stationary axially symmetric gravitational field outside the sources:

f\Delta f=(\overrightarrow{\nabla}f)^{2}-f^{4}\rho^{-2}(\overrightarrow{\nabla}\omega)^{2},

(3.2)

\overrightarrow{\nabla}(f^{2}\rho^{-2}\overrightarrow{\nabla}\omega)=0,

(3.3)

\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\rho}=\frac{1}{4}\rho f^{-2}[(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\rho})^{2}-(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z})^{2}-f^{4}\rho^{-2}((\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\rho})^{2}-(\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial z})^{2})],

(3.4)

\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial z}=\frac{1}{2}\rho f^{-2}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\rho}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}-f^{4}\rho^{-2}\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\rho}\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial z}),

(3.5)

The operators
\overrightarrow{\nabla}
and
\Delta
defined by the formulae

\overrightarrow{\nabla}\equiv\overrightarrow{\rho}_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\overrightarrow{z}_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial z},

\Delta\equiv\overrightarrow{\nabla}^{2}\equiv\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}

(
\overrightarrow{\rho}_{0}
and
\overrightarrow{z}_{0}
being unit vectors), i.e., they are similar to the ordinary Laplacian and gradient operators for flat space expressed in cylindrical coordinates provided that there is no angular coordinate dependence.

It should be noted that the integrability condition of equations
3.4
,
3.5
for determining the function
\gamma(\rho,z)
is the system
3.2
,
3.3
which does not contain
\gamma(\rho,z)
.

If we switch from the rotation potential
\omega
to the new potential
\Phi
by the formulae

\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\rho}=\frac{\rho}{f^{2}}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z},\quad\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial z}=-\frac{\rho}{f^{2}}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\rho},

(3.6)

we obtain the following field equations:

f\Delta f=(\overrightarrow{\nabla}f)^{2}-(\overrightarrow{\nabla}\Phi)^{2},\quad\overrightarrow{\nabla}(f^{-2}\overrightarrow{\nabla}\Phi)=0.

(3.7)

Introducing the Ernst complex potential

\varepsilon=f+i\Phi

(3.8)

gives us, from
3.7
, the equation

(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{*})\Delta\varepsilon=2(\overrightarrow{\nabla}\varepsilon)^{2},

(3.9)

where
\varepsilon^{*}
is the complex conjugate of
\varepsilon
.
