LiWO-SRDN-based EV charging coordination for stable smart grid systems using a single-switch high step-up zeta converter
Authors: M. Siva Ramkumar, Faten Labassi, Jayant Giri, Meraj Ali Khan, Azizbek Ruzmetov, Kiruthika Balakrishnan, J. Isaac JoshuaRamesh Lalvani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Intermittent renewable energy sources and the increasing use of Electric Vehicles (EVs) cause significant challenges for Smart Grid (SG) stability and energy management. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a hybrid EV charging coordination system. It uses a Single-Switch High Step-Up Zeta converter (S2HSZ) along with Leaf in Wind Optimization (LiWO) and Spiking Deep Residual Network (SDRN) in a unified framework. The proposed method is designed to boost grid stability, optimize converter efficiency, minimize charging errors, and improve energy use in systems with PV and wind integration. In this setup, LiWO fine-tunes the PI controller gains, while SDRN predicts ideal operating parameters for smooth performance in dynamic grid conditions. The proposed framework is implemented in MATLAB, and the LiWO-SDRN model is compared with Adaptive Interaction Artificial Neural Networks (AI-ANN), Artificial Neural Network-Particle Swarm Optimization (ANN-PSO), Multi- Agent Deep Neural Network (MADNN), Gannet Optimization Algorithm-Dilated Residual Convolutional Neural Networks (GOA-DRCNN) models. The LiWO-SDRN model delivers coordinated EV charging and steady converter operation. The experiment shows the LiWO-SDRN framework attained improved results: 99.7% converter efficiency, 98.7% energy use efficiency, and a system error of 1.5%. The LiWO-SDRN framework outperformed other methods in terms of stability, efficiency, and charging coordination. These findings demonstrate that the model significantly boosts smart grid operations, especially with EV charging and renewable energy.

Introduction

Background

EVs are essential in transportation since they offer a twofold benefit because of reduced carbon emissions and enhanced environmental sustainability1,2. The increasing demand for EVs requires a corresponding increase in an effective charging system. The management systems are designed to maximize the distribution of electrical power in such a way that the users can have an uninterrupted power supply as the demand varies3,4. Solar PV and wind generation facilities are some of the RESs that will provide sufficient electricity to add to the EV charging stations when demand is on the increase. Green solar panels produce power using sunlight in the day, which enables the generation of clean power, but fluctuating wind energy generates helpful power generation in regions that have a constant wind flow5,6. One of the most common systems of winding power of the wind energy resources is the Doubly Fed Induction Generator (DFIG)7.

Renewable-generated power will help the grid to produce sufficient power to support the growing EV demand, besides lessening reliance on traditional power sources8. A grid-synchronous EV charging system would be needed to make the grid stable, and this system would synchronize EV charging with the availability of renewable power. Demand response applications will allow vehicle owners to adjust their charging schedule to match times of renewable energy excess to the power grid to achieve stability9,10. Vehicles-to-Grid (V2G) technologies provide EVs with the capability of providing stored electricity to the grid during the time of increased power demand, which contributes to the strength of power grids11.

Wind energy that flows through the power grid stabilizes the electricity systems as it encourages the need to reduce the use of fossil fuels. Prediction analysis of both the energy need and renewable power production patterns is more effective in grid power management12,13. When grid operators coordinate the charging activity with the availability of renewable energy, maximum use of clean energy will be achieved, and the environmental impact of electric vehicle charging will be reduced14. The combination of EVs, PV, wind, and V2G systems forms a transportation network, as a result of which sustainability and resilience of the power system will be achieved. The integration will be essential towards developing a clean, dependable energy future due to the increased use of renewable energy15.

Literature survey

Various research investigations on EV charging systems with SG stability have been conducted through diverse techniques and methodologies. The following section includes summaries of selected works.

A P Singh et al.16 proposed a DC Microgrid (MG)-based Electric Vehicle Charging Station (EVCS) that integrates a grid system with an EV and batteries along with solar PV devices and an AI-ANN -powered V2G and Grid-to-Vehicle (G2V) Power Management Controller (PMC). The EVCS facilitates effective power distribution in residential or commercial buildings by supporting two operating modes: The EV works as a power consumer under G2V and as an energy supplier during V2G functions. The AI-ANN-based PMC uses peaceful mode shifts to control energy exchange between the PV system and battery storage, and electric vehicle and grid connection for a sustained DC bus voltage and stronger operations performance.

Nouri et al.17 integrated ANN and PSO in an intelligent fuzzy logic-based charge and discharge management system for EV batteries in V2G systems, which improves energy extraction from solar systems under various irradiation conditions. Aljafari et al.18 have presented a new MADNN that constrains a Markov decision process in a dynamic pricing and scheduling framework for EV charging station management. By taking into account dynamic pricing and other EV characteristics, the MADNN incorporates a queuing model to effectively plan EV charging, allowing for efficient coordination between energy supply networks and EVs as variable loads.

Kalaiselvan et al.19 have developed the combination of GOA with THDCNN, forming the GOA-THDCNN hybrid approach, which delivered effective management solutions for integrated PV-solar and small hydro power and EV charging systems. The approach aimed to reach its objectives through plug-in electric device grid connection, bidirectional grid isolation, and hybrid PV network injection-based grid-tied load management and decentralized technology-enabled smart distribution. Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) operation with control features enables this method to achieve effective performance.

Maguluri et al.20 have introduced that a MADQN algorithm serves as a management approach that optimizes the electrical system by distributing EV loads for balance. Deep reinforcement learning and cooperative decision-making mechanisms within MADQN allow the system to enhance the performance of EV battery charge plans. The system uses dynamic response mechanisms to follow power requirements, renewable energy availability, and system constraints, which enable optimized load management with minimal environmental consequences. Pratap et al.21 have presented that the HAVOGO served as a hybrid optimization method to achieve optimal designs of extensive distribution systems. HAVOGO optimizes the power distribution network integration of EVCSs through a smart fusion of AVO’s structural strength with GO’s flexible design capabilities. This approach addresses the combined process of distributed generation placement, together with distribution static compensator installation and network structural changes. A multi-objective framework optimized power compensating device allocation with network reconfiguration through an optimization process that reflects technical and financial priorities.

Jiang et al.22 have introduced a bilevel stochastic optimization model that addresses both trip planning and charging destination optimization for EV charging navigation. This study introduces an HEDQN. This network consists of two enhanced deep Q networks: one for charging destination optimization and another for charging route path optimization of EVs. Kandaswamy et al.23 have developed the GTOA-DRCNN technique, which combines the GTOA with DRCNN to create a hybrid EM strategy for EV Hybrid Battery Systems (HBS). While the DRCNN models and predicts the vehicle’s power consumption with the goal of lowering operating costs and energy loss, the GTOA optimizes EM within the system. Comparison of grid stability with existing literatures.

The comparative assessment shows that current strategies primarily emphasize addressing targeted tasks such as charging assignment, energy management, load balancing or navigation enhancement separately. However, the optimization of the combination between converter efficiency (CEC), adaptive PI controller tuning, handling of intermittency from renewable energy sources and coordinated EV charging has received limited attention in a smart grid system. On the other hand, the proposed LiWO-SDRN method combines optimization, prediction, and converter-assisted regulation to accurately coordinate charging, minimizing system error, making energy utilization more efficient, and keeping the smart grid stability under the continuously changing operating conditions.

Research gap and motivation

The recent research works of EV charging in smart grids mainly focuses on scheduling, load balancing, and renewable energy consumption, using both AI and optimization methods. Techniques like AI-ANN, ANN-PSO, MADNN, MADQN, and GOA-THDCNN show better performance in coordinating charges and managing energy. Most of these approaches focus on optimization or prediction separately. They do not handle converter-assisted grid stabilization, PI controller tuning, renewable energy intermittency, and coordinated EV charging together in a single framework. Also, existing methods focus less on boosting converter efficiency and adjusting controller gains dynamically based on changing smart grid conditions. Fluctuating PV and wind energy sources create instability, making it way harder to keep EV charging and overall grid operations smooth and steady. To overcome these limitations, this research proposes a hybrid LiWO-SDRN framework combined with an S2HSZ converter for managing EV charging in grids that use both PV and wind power. This method integrates LiWO’s optimization benefits with SDRN’s predictive learning to highlight PI controller gains. As a result, it boosts converter performance, reduces grid instability, and increases energy usage efficiency.

Novelty

The combination of the LiWO algorithm, SDRN, and S2HSZ converter in a single smart grid system to manage EV charging represents the novelty of the proposed research. The research integrates these technologies for coordinated charging within a single framework. Unlike traditional methods focusing only on charging scheduling or energy management alone, this proposed approach simultaneously addresses adaptive controller tuning, converter efficiency enhancement, renewable energy integration, and grid stability improvement. The LiWO algorithm tunes the PI controller gain parameters of the S2HSZ converter to minimize system instability and boost voltage regulation. SDRN predicts the best gain parameters during dynamic conditions, enhancing how EV charging and renewable energy work together. Additionally, the framework combines PV and wind energy sources for better energy management, which lowers system errors more than existing methods.

Proposed research modelling

Fig. 1Structure of RESs integrated SG system with EV charging.

Structure of RESs integrated SG system with EV charging.

Figure 1 shows the structure of RESs that integrates the SG system with EV charging. The system combines PV panels and Wind Turbines (WTs) as the key sources of energy, with the DFIG providing renewable energy. An S2HSZ converter balances and boosts the voltage of the RESs and EV batteries to be in line with the system requirements, and a Voltage Source Inverter (VSI) transforms the regulated DC power into AC power to be integrated into the grid. The proposed LiWO and SDRN system manages the charging of EVs to optimize the movement of power and guarantees the stability of the system. The PI controller with Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) is used to control the output of the Zeta converter so as to operate smoothly. This combined system allows efficient balance between the power generation, EV charging load, and grid stability in the SG environment.

The framework shown in Fig. 1 starts with generating renewable energy from PV and wind systems in a smart grid. This energy is then controlled by the S2HSZ converter to keep stable voltage and power characteristics, ensuring efficient operation. The PI controller manages converter operation continuously, while the LiWO algorithm adjusts the controller gains to minimize on system instability and charging fluctuations. At the same time, the SDRN model predicts the right parameters for changes in grid load, renewable energy supply, and EV charging needs. By using these optimized settings, coordinated EV charging is achieved, boosting energy use efficiency, minimizing charging errors, and improving smart grid stability.

Proposed LiWO-SDRN methodology

In this study, the LiWO-SDRN method is used to organize the charging of EVs in SG systems. LiWO optimizes gain parameters of the PI controller, maximizing energy use and minimizing grid instability, and SDRN is a better predictor of optimum gain parameters, making SGs combined with PV and wind energy sources stable. Converter efficiency is enhanced by the addition of the S2HSZ converter, which will minimize the power imbalance and improve the efficiency of the whole system to coordinate the effective charging of EVs.

Step 7: Termination.

After satisfying the termination condition, the algorithm terminates, and the optimal solution that has been obtained throughout the optimization process is returned as the final output. Otherwise, the algorithm goes back to Step 3 and repeats the procedure, refining the solutions until one of the termination conditions is met.

The LiWO algorithm finetunes the proportional gain and integral gain parameters of the PI controller for better voltage regulation and stability. It starts by randomly selecting initial PI gain combinations to optimize the process. So, the proposed setup enhances control by smartly adjusting these key factors. During each iteration, candidate solutions get updated using a leaf movement mechanism guided by wind direction and adaptive search behavior. Their fitness is assessed based on reducing voltage fluctuation, current oscillation, converter instability, and charging error. The optimization process continues until it meets the stopping criteria or reaches the max number of iterations. Once completed, the optimized gain parameters are given as input to the S2HSZ converter control system to coordinate EV charging.

In this work, the LiWO algorithm was implemented due to its proficiency in adaptively balancing exploration and exploitation in optimization problems that are nonlinear and dynamic. When compared to classical optimization techniques like PSO, GA, and GOA, LiWO performs better in terms of enhancing the diversity of the search, decreasing the occurrence of premature convergence, and increasing the ability to deal with varying smart grid operating conditions. The leaf-movement-inspired adaptive search strategy is capable of effectively adjusting the PI controller gain parameters which results in minimal voltage instability, charging error, and converter oscillation. Thus, these features render the LiWO more appropriate for the coordinated EV charging scenario integrated with renewable energy-based smart grid systems.

Spiking deep residual network (SDRN)

SDRN represents a promising advancement in neural network architectures, leveraging the principles of residual learning while adapting them to Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs)31. Integrate-and-Fire (IF) neurons take the place of the activation function, ReLU layer. Since the IF neuron implicitly implements addition during the integration of spikes, the sum layer is no longer required. A layer of synaptic connections that mimics convolutional action takes the place of the convolution layer. Biases are converted into constant currents injected into spiking neurons, while convolutional weights are mapped directly to the corresponding synaptic layers. Discussion

The proposed model links the S2HSZ converter and optimizes the PI controller gain parameters to enhance the SG systems with the EV charging effectiveness in terms of the stable power grids and effective power management of the PV and wind energy sources. The S2HSZ converter was tested with great results, as it is highly efficient with a rate of 99.7% that was better than competitor converters such as High Gain SEPIC with 97.8% and High Gain Quadratic Boost with 95.4%. This is an extraordinary converter efficiency where it becomes possible to optimize the energy conversion in addition to maintaining stable voltage in the system. LiWO-SDRN approach enhances the performance of the system by its 1.5% error accuracy and the 98.7% energy efficiency, which is more significant than the current system based on AI-ANN and ANN-PSO and MADNN that show high errors with low efficiencies. The proposed approach demonstrates its excellence by proving an amazing ability to minimize operational errors and make SG systems the most efficient energy-wise.

The study of the solar panel, converter, DFIG, EV battery, and grid can be considered as the analysis of system components that provide support to these performance improvements. The solar panel is able to hold a consistent voltage of 80 V and hold temperature as well as irradiance levels at 1000 W/m2, which facilitates constant energy generation. The converters boost voltage output at 0 to 600 V and then stabilize the outputs, which is effective voltage control. When initially connected, the current in the solar panel is more than 5 A but decreases to zero as the system carries out an efficient charge process. The converter current exhibits high load management due to the fact that it attains its steady state when the transient period is very short. The EV battery has a smooth operation and maintains a constant voltage and current profile, although its state of charge remains at 80%, which marks effective energy storage management features. There is a steady 450 V peak-to-peak AC power supply having low distortion since the grid voltage swings between a range of + 450 V and − 450 V. These findings combined confirm the potential of the proposed method to streamline the functionality of SG systems, enhance energy efficiency, and maintain stability in the operation of the grids, especially in the grids with integrated RESs.

The proposed framework shows scalability for large smart grid environments, including multiple EV charging stations and renewable energy sources. Since, LiWO optimization and SDRN prediction work adaptively with changing loads and renewable energy, it can handle more extensive coordinated charging networks with proper resource management. Its modular design, which integrates converter regulation, optimization, and predictive coordination, makes it well-suited for large distributed smart grid systems.

Conclusion

This study proposed a hybrid LiWO-SDRN model combined with an S2HSZ converter for the coordinated EV charging in renewable energy-based smart grids. The designed framework aimed at enhancing grid stability, converter efficiency, charging coordination, and energy utilization in PV- and wind-integrated scenarios. The LiWO algorithm in the proposed method was used for optimizing the PI controller gain parameters, whereas SDRN was applied to forecast appropriate operating conditions for stable converter-supported EV charging coordination. The converter efficiency of the method is 99.7% by matching the power and still maintaining the performance of 97.8% in terms of efficiency. Comparison with other techniques such as AI-ANN, ANN-PSO, MADNN, GOA-THDCNN, and MADQN through simulation results indicated that the proposed model led to more effective converter operation, lesser charging discrepancy, and higher energy utilization performance. The results showed better voltage regulation and steady charging coordination in various conditions. Overall, the proposed framework demonstrated performance improvements, while the large-scale real-time implementation remains a challenge. There is increased computation complexity and increased memory requirement involved while combining optimization algorithms along with the deep learning mechanism, especially in the case of large-scale smart grids incorporating several EV charging points. Moreover, validation of the proposed model was performed using simulations, without considering any practical implementations.

Limitations

The proposed approach has proven to be effective in improving energy efficiency and better grid stability, though it has some constraints on the system. Parameters of PI controller gains require precise adjustment since this affects performance significantly, but the optimization process is difficult under various operating conditions. One of the main weaknesses of this system is that it relies on S2HSZ converters because it lacks flexibility in a variety of grid configurations and hardware configurations.

Future work

The future studies should focus on enhancing the system to better suit a wide range of environmental conditions, with special focus on areas with changing renewable energy resources. The approach needs to be developed to perform better with unforeseen grid disturbances, along with enhanced management of unforeseen load changes. Another strategy that will facilitate the use of proactive energy management systems will be investing in more advanced predictive modelling systems between energy demands and renewable power generation.
