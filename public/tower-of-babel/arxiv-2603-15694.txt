Baryon Asymmetry of the Universe from Preon Confinement and Supersymmetry Breaking
Risto Raitio

Abstract
We propose a mechanism for generating the baryon asymmetry of the universe (BAU) within a supersymmetric preon model based on U ⁡ ( 1 ) × S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) U(1)\times SU(3) internal symmetry. Integrating out massive preons at the confinement scale Λ c ​ r ∼ 10 14 \Lambda_{cr}\sim 10^{14} GeV induces a Chern-Simons (CS) term in the effective gauge action via the Callan-Harvey anomaly inflow mechanism, deriving rather than assuming the topological structure required for the model. The confinement transition, at which supersymmetry breaks intrinsically through the differential condensation of fermionic and bosonic preon composites, provides the necessary departure from thermal equilibrium. The time-varying CS coefficient sources a net topological charge, which the anomaly equation converts directly into baryon number. Electroweak sphalerons are out of equilibrium at Λ c ​ r \Lambda_{cr} , protecting the asymmetry from immediate washout. Matching the observed baryon-to-entropy ratio η ∼ 8.7 × 10 − 10 \eta\sim 8.7\times 10^{-10} constrains the fermion/boson condensation asymmetry to ϵ ≃ 0.022 \epsilon\simeq 0.022 , a value consistent with a one-loop origin. This mechanism is structural rather than statistical, and the electroweak phase transition plays no role.

Introduction

The origin of the baryon asymmetry of the universe (BAU) remains one
of the deepest unsolved problems in cosmology. Any viable mechanism
must satisfy the three Sakharov conditions
[
1
]
:
baryon number (
B

from thermal equilibrium.

The most widely studied scenario is electroweak baryogenesis
(EWBG)
[
2

3

[

The Supersymmetric Preon Model

The model is described in detail in
[
9

10
]

summarize the features relevant for baryogenesis here.

2.1
Preon content and symmetry group

The internal symmetry group is

G=U(1)\times SU(3),

(1)

where
U(1)
acts via phases
e^{i\theta q}
with
q
the electric
charge in units of
e/3
, and
SU(3)
is the preon color group. The
two fundamental fermionic preons are

\displaystyle\psi_{0}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle(0,\,\mathbf{3})\,,\quad\text{neutral color-triplet}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\psi_{1}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3},\,\mathbf{1}\bigr)\,,\quad\text{charged color-singlet}\,.

(3)

Gauge anomaly cancellation requires the minimal completion

\tilde{\psi}_{0}\sim(0,\bar{\mathbf{3}})
and

\psi_{-1}\sim(-\tfrac{1}{3},\mathbf{1})
; all gauge anomalies of the

U(1)\times SU(3)
theory then cancel identically at the preon
level
[
10
]
. In particular, the mixed anomaly

U(1)-SU(3)^{2}
vanishes because color-charged preons are
U(1)
-neutral
and vice versa.

Preons are free above the critical scale
\Lambda_{cr}\sim 10^{14}
GeV,
close to the standard cosmology reheating scale
T_{R}
and below the
GUT scale. Below
\Lambda_{cr}
they form composite states by a confining
interaction. The Standard Model quarks, leptons, and their
superpartners emerge as three-body preon composites; the correspondence
is given in
[
9
]
.

2.2
Supersymmetry breaking at the confinement transition

It has been proposed in
[
10
]
that supersymmetry breaks

automatically
at
\Lambda_{cr}
as a consequence of compositeness.
Fermionic composites (quarks, leptons) and bosonic composites
(squarks, sleptons) are built from preons of different statistics and
therefore condense at different rates during the confinement
transition. The masses of superpartners are set by a different
dynamical scale than the masses of SM particles, and SUSY is strongly
broken below
\Lambda_{cr}
.

A crucial question for the baryogenesis mechanism is whether the
fermionic and bosonic condensates necessarily acquire
different

masses at
\Lambda_{cr}
, or whether some residual symmetry could enforce

m_{f}=m_{b}
. At tree level, the preon-preon interaction potential
is of Yukawa type derived from
t
-channel scalar and gauge boson
exchange
[
11
]
; since this potential depends only on
the spatial separation and not on the statistics of the bound
state constituents, the Schrödinger equation yields the same
binding energy for fermionic and bosonic composites at leading
order. Tree-level degeneracy
\Delta m=0
is therefore the
natural starting point, not a fine-tuned accident.

The mass splitting arises at one loop. Fermion and boson loops
contribute to the effective potential with opposite signs — a
direct consequence of the spin-statistics theorem — so the
one-loop correction to the composite mass takes the form

\Delta m\equiv m_{b}-m_{f}\sim\frac{g^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\,\bar{m}\,,

(4)

where
\bar{m}=(m_{b}+m_{f})/2
is the mean composite mass and

g
is the preon gauge coupling at
\Lambda_{cr}
. This is the leading
non-trivial contribution to the mass splitting, and it is
non-zero for any
g\neq 0
.

We define the
condensation asymmetry parameter
as the
dimensionless ratio

\epsilon\equiv\frac{m_{b}-m_{f}}{m_{b}+m_{f}}=\frac{\Delta m}{2\bar{m}}\,.

(5)

This is the sole free parameter of the baryogenesis mechanism.
Substituting the one-loop estimate (
4
):

\epsilon\sim\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\,,

(6)

which for
g\sim 1
at the strongly coupled confinement scale
gives
\epsilon\sim 6\times 10^{-3}
, within a factor of four
of the observationally required value
\epsilon\simeq 0.022

derived in Section
4
. The one-loop origin
of
\epsilon
is consistent with the observed BAU without
fine-tuning, and provides a first-principles estimate that can
be sharpened once the confinement dynamics is specified.

For baryogenesis the essential feature is that
\epsilon
is

dynamical and time-dependent
during the transition: the
loop-induced splitting builds up as the system evolves through

\Lambda_{cr}
and the preon masses are generated. The parameter
\epsilon

is determined observationally in Section
4
.

2.3
Weak interactions as a later composite phenomenon

In the present model, weak interactions enter only after composite
quarks and leptons have formed at
\Lambda_{cr}
. The
SU(2)_{L}
gauge group
and the Higgs mechanism operate at the electroweak scale

\Lambda_{EW}\sim 246
GeV, many orders of magnitude below
\Lambda_{cr}
. This
separation is important: the BAU is generated entirely within the
preon sector at
\Lambda_{cr}
, and the electroweak phase transition plays no
role in its production. The
SU(2)_{L}
gauge field is present as a
massless degree of freedom in the thermal plasma at
\Lambda_{cr}
, but
sphaleron transitions driven by that field are out of equilibrium at
that epoch, as we show in Section
4
. In the
present framework the Higgs boson is a composite preon
state
[
9
]
, consistent with the view that fundamental
scalar fields and their associated fine-tuning problems may be
artifacts of treating composite objects as elementary.

3
Induced Chern-Simons Term from Preon Integration

3.1
Setup and strategy

At the confinement scale
\Lambda_{cr}
the charged color-singlet preons

\psi_{1}\sim(\frac{1}{3},\mathbf{1})
and

\psi_{-1}\sim(-\frac{1}{3},\mathbf{1})
acquire Dirac masses

m_{f}
and
-m_{f}
respectively through the binding dynamics.
We wish to determine what structure is imprinted on the low-energy
effective action for the
U(1)
gauge field
A_{\mu}
when these
massive fermions are integrated out of the path integral. The
answer is provided by anomaly inflow
[
12
]
: a massive
chiral fermion induces a topological Chern-Simons term in the
effective gauge action, with a coefficient independent of the
fermion mass and determined entirely by its charge and chirality.
A pedagogical derivation is given in
[
15
]
; here we
apply the result to the specific preon charge assignments of the
model.

The Euclidean action for
\psi_{1}
minimally coupled to
A_{\mu}
is

S_{E}=\int d^{4}x\;\bar{\psi}_{1}\bigl(\not{D}+m_{f}\bigr)\psi_{1}\,,\qquad D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-iqA_{\mu}\,,\quad q=\tfrac{1}{3}\,.

(7)

The one-loop effective action obtained by integrating out
\psi_{1}
is

\Gamma_{eff}[A]=-\log\det\bigl(\not{D}+m_{f}\bigr)=-\mathrm{Tr}\log\bigl(\not{D}+m_{f}\bigr)\,.

(8)

We expand
\Gamma_{eff}
in powers of
A_{\mu}
and isolate the

three-point term
— cubic in
A_{\mu}
— which carries the
Chern-Simons structure.

3.2
The triangle diagram

The three-point contribution to
\Gamma_{eff}
arises from the
one-loop Feynman diagram with a single closed fermion loop and three
external gauge boson legs carrying momenta
p
,
q
,
r
with

p+q+r=0
. The amplitude is

\Pi^{\mu\nu\rho}(p,q,r)=(-1)(iq)^{3}\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[\gamma^{\mu}S(k)\,\gamma^{\nu}S(k{+}q)\,\gamma^{\rho}S(k{+}q{+}r)\Bigr]\,,

(9)

where the overall
(-1)
is the fermion loop sign and the massive
Euclidean propagator is

S(k)=\frac{-i(\not{k}-m_{f})}{k^{2}+m_{f}^{2}}\,.

(10)

Both triangle orientations (direct and crossed, related by

\nu\leftrightarrow\rho
,
q\leftrightarrow r
) must be summed to
obtain a symmetric amplitude.

3.3
Gamma matrix trace and the Levi-Civita structure

Substituting (
10
) into (
9
) and expanding
the numerator factors
(-i)^{3}(\not{k}-m_{f})(\not{k}+\not{q}-m_{f})(\not{k}+\not{q}+\not{r}-m_{f})
yields eight terms.
Terms with an odd total number of gamma matrices vanish under the
Dirac trace. The two surviving structures are:

Term A
(three momentum slashes, no mass insertion):

\mathcal{T}_{A}^{\mu\nu\rho}=-\,\mathrm{Tr}\bigl[\gamma^{\mu}\not{k}\,\gamma^{\nu}(\not{k}+\not{q})\,\gamma^{\rho}(\not{k}+\not{q}+\not{r})\bigr]\,.

(11)

Term B
(one momentum slash, two mass insertions):

\mathcal{T}_{B}^{\mu\nu\rho}=m_{f}^{2}\,\mathrm{Tr}\bigl[\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\not{k}+\cdots\bigr]\,.

(12)

The essential identity for Term A in four-dimensional Minkowski space
is
[
13
]

\mathrm{Tr}\bigl[\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma_{5}\bigr]=-4i\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,,

(13)

where
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is the totally antisymmetric
Levi-Civita tensor. This structure arises because, after Feynman
parameterization and symmetric integration over loop momentum, the
only Lorentz-invariant rank-three antisymmetric tensor available is

\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}q_{\sigma}
(with
q_{\sigma}
an external
momentum). The
\gamma_{5}
term does not cancel because the loop
integral itself is odd under the relevant momentum
shifts
[
14
]
. The Levi-Civita tensor

\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is the seed of the Chern-Simons term
in the effective action.

3.4
Loop integration and non-decoupling

After extracting the
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
tensor structure,
the remaining scalar integral — evaluated in the limit of vanishing
external momenta
p,q,r\to 0
, appropriate since
\Lambda_{cr}
far exceeds
any external scale — reduces to

I(m_{f})\Big|_{p\to 0}=m_{f}\int\frac{d^{4}k_{E}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{(k_{E}^{2}+m_{f}^{2})^{3}}=m_{f}\cdot\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{2m_{f}^{2}}=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}m_{f}}\,.

(14)

The factor of
m_{f}
in the numerator of (
14
) originates
from the mass insertion in the propagator; it cancels the
1/m_{f}

from the denominator, yielding a result that is
independent of

|m_{f}|
and retains only its sign:

I(m_{f})\Big|_{p\to 0}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{\mathrm{sgn}(m_{f})}{32\pi^{2}}\,.

(15)

This is the non-decoupling result
[
12
,
15
]
: a
fermion of arbitrarily large mass still contributes a finite,
mass-independent Chern-Simons term to the effective action.
The heavy preon does not decouple. Its entire contribution is
topological, controlled by the
sign
of
m_{f}
rather than
its magnitude.

k_{CS}=\frac{q^{3}}{24\pi^{2}}\,\mathrm{sgn}(m_{f})\,.

(17)

This is the result of anomaly inflow in 3+1

In thermal equilibrium,

The departure is provided by the SUSY-breaking confinement

\Delta k_{CS}=\frac{2\epsilon}{648\pi^{2}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{3}\cdot 3=\frac{\epsilon}{2916\pi^{2}}\simeq 3.5\times 10^{-5}\,\epsilon\,.

(20)

where the factor of 3 counts preon generations.
Three properties of
\Delta k_{CS}
deserve emphasis.

Derivation, not assumption.

The CS term is not introduced into the preon Lagrangian by hand. It
emerges at one loop from the
U(1)
gauge structure and the preon
mass spectrum, as an unavoidable quantum consequence of integrating
out the massive charged preons. The topological character of
baryogenesis in this model is therefore a structural prediction of
the gauge anomaly cancellation conditions, not an independent
hypothesis.

The sign carries the physics.

The coefficient
\Delta k_{CS}
is odd in
\mathrm{sgn}(m_{f})
.
The baryon asymmetry is therefore sensitive not to the magnitude of
the confinement scale but to the relative chirality of fermionic
versus bosonic condensation — a qualitative, discrete feature of the
SUSY-breaking transition. This is why
\epsilon\simeq 0.022

(derived in Section
4
) is a natural rather than
fine-tuned value.

Topological stability.

Because
k_{CS}
is mass-independent, the induced term is stable
against radiative corrections that shift
|m_{f}|
. Only a change of

\mathrm{sgn}(m_{f})
— a global feature of the vacuum structure —
can alter
\Delta k_{CS}
. This gives the baryogenesis mechanism a
robustness unusual among high-scale models.

4
Baryogenesis at the Confinement Transition

4.1
The non-equilibrium source

The time-varying CS coefficient derived in Section
3

is the dynamical source for baryon number generation. During the
confinement transition at
\Lambda_{cr}
, the fermionic preon mass
m_{f}(t)

and the bosonic spreon mass
m_{b}(t)
evolve at different rates,
as established in Section
2.2
. The net CS
coefficient therefore changes in time:

\dot{k}_{CS}(t)\neq 0\quad\text{throughout the transition window }\Delta t\sim\Lambda_{cr}^{-1}\,.

(21)

This is the non-equilibrium condition required by the third Sakharov
criterion. It is not imposed externally — it follows from the
differential condensation of fermions and bosons, which is in turn a
direct consequence of intrinsic SUSY breaking.

The physical origin of the confinement transition was analyzed
in
[
11
]
. The preon-preon interaction potential is
derived from
t
-channel exchange of the Higgs scalar and the massive
gauge field in Maxwell-Chern-Simons QED with spontaneous symmetry
breaking, evaluated in the non-relativistic approximation. The
resulting potential takes a Yukawa form with an SSB-generated
mass for the mediating scalar. We note that the non-relativistic
approximation is marginal at
T\sim\Lambda_{cr}
, where typical preon momenta
satisfy
|\mathbf{p}|\sim T\sim\Lambda_{cr}\sim m_{f}
, and that the exchange
potential is a tree-level result; relativistic and loop corrections
modify the coefficients but not the topological structure of the
transition. The qualitative conclusions relevant for baryogenesis
are robust: the massive gauge field of MCS-QED generates a cubic
term in the finite-temperature effective potential through the
Coleman-Weinberg mechanism
[
16
]
, and a cubic term
drives a
first-order
transition. Consequently, the
confinement transition proceeds by bubble nucleation — the true
vacuum (
\langle\phi\rangle\neq 0
, condensed preon phase)
nucleates inside the false vacuum (
\langle\phi\rangle=0
,
deconfined phase) — and the CS coefficient changes from zero to

\Delta k_{CS}
as the bubble wall passes through a given region.
This is structurally identical to the bubble-wall mechanism of
standard EWBG, but operating at
\Lambda_{cr}
rather than
\Lambda_{EW}
.
Inside the true vacuum, sphaleron activity is suppressed by the
preon condensate in analogy with the EWBG condition

\phi(T_{c})/T_{c}\gtrsim 1
, providing a natural freezing mechanism
for the generated asymmetry. The parameters
\mu^{2}
,
E
, and

\lambda
of
V_{eff}(\phi,T)
are determined in principle by
the preon-preon interaction potential of
[
11
]
, with
the cubic coefficient
E
arising from the same massive Proca
field of MCS-QED that provides the attractive term in the
two-body potential. A fully relativistic treatment of
the preon effective potential is left for future work.

4.2
Baryon number generation via the anomaly equation

The connection between the time-varying CS coefficient and baryon
number is the standard anomaly equation. In the presence of a

U(1)
gauge background with Chern-Simons number
N_{CS}
, baryon
number changes as

\frac{dB}{dt}=N_{f}\,\frac{dN_{CS}}{dt}\,,

(22)

where
N_{f}=3
counts the preon generations. Integrating
through the transition and using the relation between
N_{CS}

and
k_{CS}
, the baryon number density generated is

n_{B}=N_{f}\,\Delta k_{CS}\,T_{c}^{3}\,,

(23)

where
T_{c}\simeq\Lambda_{cr}\sim 10^{14}
GeV is the confinement
temperature and the factor
T_{c}^{3}
provides the appropriate
dimension from the spatial integral over the transition region.
Substituting (
20
):

n_{B}=\frac{3\,\epsilon}{2916\pi^{2}}\,T_{c}^{3}\simeq 1.04\times 10^{-4}\,\epsilon\,T_{c}^{3}\,.

(24)

4.3
Washout protection at the confinement scale

The generated asymmetry is protected from immediate washout by
a straightforward kinematic argument. The electroweak sphaleron
rate in the symmetric phase is
[
17
]

\Gamma_{sph}\sim 25\,\alpha_{W}^{5}\,T\,,

(25)

while the Hubble expansion rate at temperature
T
is

H(T)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{2}g_{*}}{90}}\,\frac{T^{2}}{M_{Pl}}\,,

(26)

with
g_{*}\simeq 200
the effective relativistic degrees of freedom
at
\Lambda_{cr}
and
M_{Pl}=1.22\times 10^{19}
GeV the Planck mass.
Evaluating at
T_{c}\sim 10^{14}
GeV:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{sph}(T_{c})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 25\,(1/30)^{5}\times 10^{14}\,\text{GeV}\sim 10^{8}\,\text{GeV}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle H(T_{c})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{(10^{14})^{2}}{10^{18}}\,\text{GeV}=10^{10}\,\text{GeV}\,.

(28)

Since
\Gamma_{sph}(T_{c})\ll H(T_{c})
, electroweak sphalerons are

out of thermal equilibrium
at the confinement epoch. They
cannot erase the asymmetry generated by the preon transition.
The baryon number produced at
\Lambda_{cr}
is therefore safe from
sphaleron washout at that epoch, without requiring any additional
suppression mechanism.

4.4
The baryon-to-entropy ratio

The entropy density of the thermal bath at
T_{c}
is

s=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{45}\,g_{*}\,T_{c}^{3}\,,

(29)

giving the baryon-to-entropy ratio

\frac{\Delta B}{s}=\frac{n_{B}}{s}=\frac{N_{f}\,\Delta k_{CS}}{2\pi^{2}g_{*}/45}=\frac{45\,N_{f}\,\Delta k_{CS}}{2\pi^{2}\,g_{*}}\,.

(30)

Inserting
N_{f}=3
,
\Delta k_{CS}=\epsilon/(2916\pi^{2})
,
and
g_{*}=200
:

\frac{\Delta B}{s}=\frac{45\times 3\,\epsilon}{2\pi^{2}\times 200\times 2916\pi^{2}}\simeq\frac{\epsilon}{2.52\times 10^{7}}\,.

(31)

The observed baryon-to-entropy ratio is
[
18
]

\left(\frac{\Delta B}{s}\right)_{obs}=\frac{\eta\,s}{n_{\gamma}}\simeq 6.10\times 10^{-10}\times 7.04\simeq 8.7\times 10^{-10}\,.

(32)

Setting (
31
) equal to (
32
) and solving
for the condensation asymmetry:

\boxed{\epsilon\simeq 0.022\,.}

(33)

A value
\epsilon\simeq 2\%
is entirely natural within the model.
It is consistent with a one-loop correction to the effective
potential at the confinement transition — comparable to typical
loop factors
\alpha/4\pi\sim 10^{-3}
–

— and requires
no fine-tuning. Crucially, the smallness of the observed

\eta\sim 10^{-9}
is not explained by a small
\epsilon
alone
but by the combined suppression from
g_{*}

generations, and the cube of the preon charge
q^{3}=(1/3)^{3}

mechanism distributes the hierarchy naturally across these factors.

4.5
Intermediate-scale washout and emergent gauge symmetry

The standard washout concern.

In the Standard Model, EW sphalerons re-enter thermal equilibrium
as the universe cools below
T_{sph}\sim 10^{12}
GeV and remain
active until the EWPT at
T\sim 130
GeV. During this window
sphalerons conserve
B-L
exactly
[
2
]
. If the
asymmetry generated at
\Lambda_{cr}
carries
B-L=0
, sphaleron
equilibration would drive
B\to 0
before the EWPT freezes it in.
This is the standard washout argument, and it applies to any
baryogenesis mechanism that generates
B
without a corresponding

B-L
asymmetry.

Mitigation from the preon framework.

The standard washout argument, however, imports assumptions from
the SM that require reexamination in the preon context. In the
present model, baryon number
B
and lepton number
L
are

emergent
quantum numbers — labels assigned to composite
three-preon states after confinement, not conserved Noether charges
of the fundamental
U(1)\times SU(3)
preon theory. The same
applies to the electroweak gauge group
SU(2)_{L}
: in a fully
composite picture,
SU(2)_{L}
and its gauge bosons
W^{a}_{\mu}
arise
at the composite level below
\Lambda_{cr}
, not as fundamental degrees of
freedom of the preon Lagrangian.
3
3

3

We note that if

SU(2)_{L}
is emergent rather than fundamental, then sphaleron
solutions — which are topological objects in the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge
bundle — are also emergent. The CS term, by contrast, is derived
from the fundamental preon
U(1)
gauge structure via the
Callan-Harvey mechanism and is not emergent. A rigorous treatment
of the distinction between fundamental and emergent gauge symmetry
in the preon framework is left for future work.

The sphaleron, as a classical saddle-point solution of the

SU(2)_{L}
field equations, can only exist where
SU(2)_{L}
is
a dynamical local symmetry with propagating gauge fields. In the
preon model this condition is satisfied only
below

\Lambda_{cr}
,
after composite quarks, leptons, and weak gauge bosons have formed.
By that point the BAU is already encoded in the composite
spectrum — imprinted at
\Lambda_{cr}
by the CS mechanism and inherited
by the composites as they condense. Sphalerons acting on composite
quarks and leptons at
T\lesssim\Lambda_{cr}
are therefore acting on a
spectrum that already carries the asymmetry as an internal
property of its constituents.

The relevant conserved quantity at the preon level is not
B-L

but
preon number
, and the CS mechanism generates an
asymmetry in preon number directly. Whether sphaleron-mediated
processes on the composite spectrum can unwind this preon-level
asymmetry is a non-trivial question that depends on the detailed
mapping between preon number and the emergent
B
and
L
charges.
We expect this mapping to protect the asymmetry, but a rigorous
demonstration requires a more complete treatment of the emergent

SU(2)_{L}
dynamics than is available at present.

Two conservative routes to full protection.

Setting aside the emergent symmetry argument, two routes within
the conventional framework ensure the asymmetry survives to the
EWPT regardless. First, the
SU(8)
extension
of
[
10
,
8
]
naturally contains
representations that generate
B-L\neq 0
at
\Lambda_{cr}
; a single
operator suppressed by
\Lambda_{cr}
suffices to protect the asymmetry
from sphaleron washout by the standard argument. Second, the
first-order character of the preon confinement transition
(Section
4.1
) means that sphaleron activity is
suppressed inside the condensed phase in exact analogy with the
EWBG condition
\phi(T_{c})/T_{c}\gtrsim 1
, providing a dynamical
freezing mechanism independent of
B-L
. We regard the emergent
symmetry argument as the deepest resolution of the washout
problem in this framework, and the two conservative routes as
insurance pending a more complete treatment.

Discussion and Conclusions

5.1
Resolution of the statistical fluctuation problem

A previous attempt to generate the BAU within this preon
framework
[
11
,
19
]
relied on a statistical
fluctuation of the CS background, which is vulnerable to the
objection that the asymmetry is a local accident of our Hubble
patch rather than a structural prediction of the model. The present
mechanism resolves this problem directly. The baryon asymmetry
arises from the Callan-Harvey induction of the CS term — a
one-loop quantum consequence of integrating out the massive charged
preons
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{-1}
— combined with the intrinsic
SUSY-breaking mass splitting
\Delta m\neq 0
. Both ingredients
are universal: every Hubble patch that underwent the same

U(1)\times SU(3)
preon confinement transition with the same
dynamics produces the same asymmetry. The mechanism is structural,
not statistical.

The separation of the three Sakharov conditions across three

5.3
The role of the electroweak phase transition

A central result of this paper is that the EWPT plays

no generative role
in the baryogenesis mechanism.
Although the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge field is present as a massless
degree of freedom in the thermal plasma at
\Lambda_{cr}
, and the
electroweak Chern-Simons number is well-defined in the symmetric
phase, the baryon asymmetry is imprinted on the composite
quark-lepton spectrum at confinement — prior to electroweak
symmetry breaking by eleven orders of magnitude in temperature.
The EWPT is a spectator process. This conclusion holds
independently of whether the EWPT is first-order or a crossover,
and independently of the Higgs mass. It means that the present
mechanism is not constrained by, and does not compete with,
searches for BSM physics at the electroweak scale.

5.4
Open problems and future directions

Three problems remain open and define the programme for future work.

Emergent
SU(2)_{L}
and the washout problem.

As argued in Section
4.5
, the standard
sphaleron washout concern is significantly mitigated in the preon
framework because
B
,
L

SU(2)_{L}
are emergent rather
than fundamental. Sphalerons can only act on the composite
spectrum below
\Lambda_{cr}
, by which point the asymmetry is already
frozen into the composite states. The deepest open question is
therefore not how to protect
B-L
from sphalerons, but whether
the preon-level CS asymmetry is preserved under the mapping from
preon number to the emergent
B
and
L
charges. A rigorous
treatment of emergent
SU(2)_{L}
in the preon framework — and its
implications for topological charge conservation — is the most
pressing item for future work.

Explicit computation of the triangle diagram.

The induced CS coefficient (
17

the standard Callan-Harvey result with the preon charge assignments.
An explicit FeynCalc evaluation of the gamma matrix

The confinement dynamics.

The binding force responsible for preon confinement at
\Lambda_{cr}

remains unspecified in the present model. A concrete proposal —
whether a hypercolor gauge force, a four-fermion interaction, or a

5.5
Conclusions

We have proposed a mechanism for generating the baryon asymmetry
of the universe within a supersymmetric preon model based on

U(1)\times SU(3)
internal symmetry. The main results are as
follows.

The electroweak phase transition plays no role in this mechanism.
This liberates the model from all constraints associated with the
Higgs mass, the order of the EWPT, and the absence of BSM physics
at the electroweak scale.

The Chern-Simons term responsible for topological charge generation
is
derived
from the preon gauge structure via the
Callan-Harvey anomaly inflow mechanism at one loop. It is not
introduced by hand. The induced coefficient
k_{CS}=q^{3}\,\mathrm{sgn}(m_{f})/24\pi^{2}
is mass-independent and
topologically stable.

Supersymmetry breaks intrinsically at the confinement scale
\Lambda_{cr}

through the differential condensation of fermionic and bosonic
preon composites, as proposed in
[
10
]
. The mass
splitting
\Delta m\neq 0
arises at one loop from the opposite
signs of fermion and boson contributions, and is therefore
guaranteed for any nonzero preon gauge coupling.

The confinement transition provides all three Sakharov conditions
simultaneously and independently: baryon number violation through
the anomaly equation, CP violation through the preon chirality
asymmetry
\epsilon
, and departure from equilibrium through the
SUSY-breaking transition itself. Electroweak sphalerons are out of
equilibrium at
\Lambda_{cr}
, protecting the asymmetry from immediate
washout.

Matching the observed baryon-to-entropy ratio

\eta\simeq 8.7\times 10^{-10}
constrains the sole free parameter
to
\epsilon\simeq 0.022
, a value natural for a one-loop
correction to the effective potential. The mechanism is structural
rather than statistical: it operates identically in every Hubble
patch that underwent the same preon confinement transition.
Fixing
\epsilon
from the observed BAU constrains the preon gauge
coupling
g
at
\Lambda_{cr}
via equation (
6
), which
in turn determines the soft SUSY-breaking parameters and allows
predictions for superpartner masses accessible to future collider
experiments.
