PROJECT SCOPE AND OBJECTIVE
Budget Period 1 Scope and Objective: Quantify capacity loss due to degradation and identify the
mechanisms involved. Understanding the mechanisms of degradation can lead to successful
strategies for avoidance or mitigation of degradation.
Budget Period 2 Scope and Objective: Test accelerated aging protocols with the help of extensive
analytical testing to characterize the changes in the direct air capture (DAC) materials.
Budget Period 3 Scope and Objective: Carry out long term (>500 cycles) testing and use the results
of these tests to validate reliable accelerated aging tests for the material types in our set.

 BENEFIT TO DOE MISSION
 This work supports the mission of the U.S. Department of Energy to promote American energy
 leadership, technological innovation, and economic growth through the development of
 technologies for cost effective carbon capture. While capture technologies can provide carbon
 dioxide for enhanced oil recovery, many capture materials suffer from oxidative, hydrolytic
 and/or thermal degradation which leads to capacity loss and unwanted emissions such as
 ammonia. Short lifetimes increase operational costs and cost studies suffer from uncertainty
 because the true lifetimes of many capture materials are unknown. In this study, a suite of
 capture materials were studied to quantify their degradation, provide insight into the
 mechanisms that lead to their uptake loss and provide validated accelerated aging protocols
 for industry.

COMMERCIALIZATION ADVANCEMENTS

  MARKET ADVANCEMENTS

  A major objective of this project was to develop and validate accelerated aging protocols for
  direct air capture materials. Discussions were held with eight direct air capture companies.
  Results of a questionnaire indicated that the majority of companies viewed having a validated
  accelerated aging protocol as a “must have”.

  COMMERCIALIZATION ACHIEVEMENTS AND SUCCESS STORIES
 The topic of oxidative degradation and sorbent lifetime is of great interest to
  commercial enterprises engaged in carbon capture. Engagement with industry during
  the development of the proposal and the first budget period generated strong interest
  from commercial partners both in terms of cost share partnering and interest in the
  outcome of our research. The project was cancelled prior to completing the first
  budget period and therefore, commercial achievements were not realized.

CONCLUSION

  PATH FORWARD
  The project has been cancelled and there are no additional next steps planned.
