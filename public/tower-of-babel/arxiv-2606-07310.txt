On growth rates of infinite and finite sumsets
By Felipe Hernández and Luke Hetzel

Abstract
We study growth rates of infinite and finite sumset patterns in sets of positive density. In the infinite setting, we show that no such rate exists, answering a question of Kra, Moreira, Ritcher, and Robertson. Namely, for any proposed growth rate ℋ : ℕ → ℕ \mathcal{H}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N} tending to infinity, we construct a set A A of lower density 1 1 such that whenever B , C ⊆ ℕ B,C\subseteq\mathbb{N} are infinite and B + C ⊆ A B+C\subseteq A we have the minimum of | B ∩ [ N ] | |B\cap[N]| and | C ∩ [ N ] | |C\cap[N]| is less than ℋ ⁡ ( N ) \mathcal{H}(N) for infinitely many N N . In the finitary setting, we prove that for all δ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \delta\in(0,1) , for all sufficiently large N N , for all subsets A A of { 1 , … , N } \{1,\dots,N\} of proportion δ \delta , one can always find sumset patterns B + C ⊆ A B+C\subseteq A with | B | |B| and | C | |C| of order log ⁡ N \log N , partially resolving a conjecture of Kra, Moreira, Richter, and Robertson. Moreover, we generalize our second result to the case of the k k -fold sum B 1 + B 2 + … + B k ⊆ A B_{1}+B_{2}+\ldots+B_{k}\subseteq A .



1.  Introduction

One of the most famous and celebrated results in Ramsey theory is van der Waerden’s theorem
[
17
]
: for any integers
r,k
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
[N]
is colored with
r
colors, then some color class contains an arithmetic progression of length
k
. A limitation of this theorem is that one cannot specify in advance which color must contain these progressions. In 1953 Roth
[
15
]
showed that for every
\delta\in(0,1]
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
A\subseteq[N]
with
|A|\geq\delta N
, the set
A
contains an arithmetic progression of length
3
. In 1975 Szemerédi
[
16
]
proved that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\in(0,1]
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
A\subseteq[N]
with
|A|\geq\delta N
, the set
A
contains an arithmetic progression of length
k
, resolving a conjecture of Erdős and Turán
[
4
]
.

A direct application of the compactness principle shows that the results of van der Waerden and Szemerédi both have equivalent infinitary formulations. On one hand, equivalent to van der Warden’s theorem is the statement that in every finite coloring of
\mathbb{N}
at least one color contains arbitrarily large arithmetic progressions. On the other hand, Szemerédi’s theorem is equivalent to the statement that every subset of the
\mathbb{N}
with positive upper Banach density (see
Section

2
for the definition) contains arbitrarily large arithmetic progressions.

In 2019, Moreira, Richter, and Robertson
[
14
]
showed that every set of positive upper Banach density contains a set of the form
B+C
for two infinite sets
B
and
C
, proving a long-standing conjecture of Erdős. Subsequent works have established several generalizations, but since we will mainly focus on configurations of the form
B+C
in this article, we refer the reader to

[
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
for further developments.

One may wonder whether it is possible to say something about the size of
B
and
C
in the theorem of Moreira, Richter, and Robertson. In this direction, Host
[
7
]
showed that there exists a set of positive density not containing any sum of a set of positive density and an infinite set. However, one can still ask whether the theorem of Moreira, Richter, and Robertson admits an equivalent finitary formulation, as do the theorems of van der Waerden and Szemerédi. Since positive density corresponds to linear growth, one might instead ask whether
B
and
C
can be forced to grow at some prescribed sublinear rate. In their survey paper, Kra, Moreira, Richter, and Robertson
[
13
]
pose the following question.

Question 1.1

(
[
13

)
.

Given
\delta\in(0,1)
, is there a nondecreasing function
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
with
\mathcal{H}(N)\to\infty

satisfying the following: for any set

with

\min\{|B\cap[N]|,|C\cap[N]|\}\geq\mathcal{H}(N)

The authors in
[

remark that not even the coloring analog of

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a non-decreasing function with
\mathcal{H}(N)\to\infty
as

.

1.

There is a
2
-coloring of
\mathbb{N}
such that for all two infinite sets

with

\max\{|B\cap[N]|,|C\cap[N]|\}<\mathcal{H}(N)

for infinitely many

Remark 1.2
.

We are not sure if we can replace
\min
with
\max
in (
2

N\in\mathbb{N}
” with “cofinite

shows that no growth rate can be found for infinite sumsets. However, a similar question can be asked for finite sumsets. For
\delta\in(0,1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\phi_{\delta}
as the largest integer such that any set

with

contains a set of the form

Conjecture 1.3

(
[

Let

Then

0<\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{\delta}(N)}{\log(N)},

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{\delta}(N)}{\log(N)}<\infty.

The lower bound over the liminf would show that

Remark 1.4
.

In this work, we focus only on

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{\delta}(N)}{\log(N)\log\log(N)^{10+\epsilon}}=0.

(3)

To our knowledge, this the closest result to
Eq.

2

The use of the intersectivity lemma of Bergelson

]
has been crucial for finding infinite sumsets

Theorem B

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and

For every

\frac{1}{\log{\frac{1}{\delta}}}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{\delta}(N)}{\log{N}}.

We remark that we actually obtain a more general version of

2.  Notation

We write
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
as the set of naturals and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
to be the set of non-negative integers. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we denote
[N]:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
[M,N)=\{M,M+1,\ldots,N-1\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}

define the
lower natural density
and
upper natural density
of
A
by

\underline{d}(A):=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap[N]|}{N}\text{ and }\overline{d}(A):=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap[N]|}{N}

respectively. Analogously, we define the
upper Banach density
by

d^{*}(A):=\limsup_{N-M\to\infty}|A\cap[M,N)|/(N-M).

In order to construct the set
A
in
A
(
2
) we make use of the notion of
Schnirelmann density
which is usually defined in infinite sets, but we extend its definition to finite sets in the following way.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the
Schnirelmann density
of a set
A\subseteq[0,N)
as

\sigma_{N}(A)=\min_{k\leq N}\frac{|A\cap[0,k)|}{k}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
w\in\{0,1\}^{[0,n)}
be a finite word, we define
|w|=n
to be the length of
w
. Additionally for
i\in\mathbb{N}
we define
w^{i}=ww\ldots w
to be the concatenation of
w
with itself
i
times. Finally we define
\supp(w)=\{i\in[0,n):w_{i}=1\}

Definition 2.4
.

For eventually positive functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, we say
f(n)=\omega(g(n))
if
f(n)/g(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. For functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
g(n)\neq 0
for
n
sufficiently large , we say
f(n)\thicksim g(n)
if
f(n)/g(n)\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with

\binom{[N]}{\alpha}
to be the set of all subsets of
[N]
of cardinality

.

3.  Growth Rates For Infinite Sumsets

3.1.  Growth rate problem for colorings

In this section, we prove
A
. We first show that for every possible growth rate,
\mathcal{H}
, there is a 2-coloring of the natural numbers such that every infinite sumset fails to follow
\mathcal{H}
infinitely many times. This example contains in embryonic form the ideas that we later use to construct the analogous counterexample for the density version of the problem.

Since part of our proof of
A
(
2
) will involve projecting into
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
it becomes notationally simpler to work in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
instead of
\mathbb{N}
. In order to keep notation consistent between the coloring and density problem, we similarly adapt the proof of
A
(
1
). Since
A
is equivalent in either
\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{N}_{0}
by shift invariance, this does not lose any generality.

Theorem
A

1
.

For any non-decreasing function
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
with
\mathcal{H}(N)\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
there is a
2
-coloring of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that for all two infinite sets
B,C\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with
B+C
monochromatic we have

\max\{|B\cap[N]|,|C\cap[N]|\}<\mathcal{H}(N)

(4)

for infinitely many
N
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there is a non-decreasing
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
with
\mathcal{H}(N)\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
such that for every
2
-coloring of
\mathbb{N}
there are two infinite sets
B,C\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
B+C
is monochromatic and for all sufficiently large
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\max\{|B\cap[N]|,|C\cap[N]|\}\geq\mathcal{H}(N).

Define the function
I:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
as

I(n)=\min\{r\in\mathbb{N}:\mathcal{H}(r)>n\}.

We will build a
2
-coloring
\mathbb{N}_{0}=A_{0}\sqcup A_{1}
that witnesses the failure of
H
by building the indicator function of the color
A_{1}
as the limit of a sequence of finite words
(w_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. To define such sequence, we set
w_{1}=01
and for
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define

w_{i+1}=\begin{cases}w_{i}1^{2(I(|w_{i}|)+|w_{i}|)+1}&\text{ if }w_{i}\text{ ends with }0\\
w_{i}0^{2(I(|w_{i}|)+|w_{i}|)+1}&\text{ if }w_{i}\text{ ends with }1\end{cases}.

Let
W\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the infinite word such that for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
W|_{[|w_{i}|]}=w_{i}
, i.e.
W
starts with the subword
w_{i}
. Define
A_{1}=\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\mid W(i)=1\}
and
A_{0}=\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\mid W(i)=0\}
. We observe that
(A_{0},A_{1})
defines a coloring of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
B,C\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}
be infinite sets such that
B+C
is monochromatic for the coloring
(A_{0},A_{1})
. Without loss of generality, assume that
B+C\subseteq A_{0}
, since the case
B+C\subseteq A_{1}
is analogous. Let
N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that for all
N\geq N^{\prime}
,

\max\{|B\cap[N]|,|C\cap[N]|\}>\mathcal{H}(N).

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|w_{i}|\geq N^{\prime}
and such that
w_{i}
ends with
0
. As
w_{i+1}
extends
w_{i}
by only adding
1

\max(|B\cap[|w_{i+1}|]|,|C\cap[|w_{i+1}|]|)\leq|w_{i}|
. Otherwise, if for example
|C\cap[|w_{i+1}|]|>|w_{i}|
, then
B+C
must contain an element in the interval
\{|w_{i}|+1,\ldots,|w_{i+1}|\}
which is contained in
A_{1}
, a contradiction.

On the other hand, we have that

\mathcal{H}(|w_{i+1}|)\geq\mathcal{H}(I(|w_{i}|))>|w_{i}|\geq\max(|B\cap[|w_{i+1}|]|,|C\cap[|w_{i+1}|]|).

Since
|w_{i+1|}\geq|w_{i}|\geq N^{\prime}
, we contradict the hypothesis over
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

In

4

3.2.  Growth rate problem for sets of positive lower density

As explained by
Remark

3.1
, the example provided by
Theorem

is not good enough to tackle
1.1
. We use the required growth rate to force a shift of the beginning of
A_{1}
to be included in
A_{0}
and force a shift of the beginning of
A_{0}
to be included in
A_{1}
, and continue repeating the process. This says that any monochromatic
B,C
that are satisfying the growth rate will be forced into the wrong color later on. The main issue is that in order to control the cardinalities of
B
and
C
, we forbid potentially super-exponentially increasing intervals for each color. This forces each color to have lower density zero.

The idea to fix this will be to forbid specific patterns instead of a full interval. We prove that for any finite sufficiently large finite pattern, we can find a set with high density that does not include the pattern. We can then iteratively forbid all possible patterns that could have appeared in a candidate for
B
which satisfies the growth rate.

The key proposition that will allow us to forbid specific patterns is the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every
\epsilon>0
there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every finite subset
P\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
|P|\geq K
there exist arbitrarily large
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\subseteq[0,N)
with
\sigma_{N}(A)\geq 1-\epsilon
satisfying
A
does not contain any shift of
P
in
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.

Now we assume
Proposition

3.2
to prove
Item

2
of
A
, which we restate now for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem
A

2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a non-decreasing function with
\mathcal{H}(N)\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
. Then there exists a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\underline{d}(A)=1
such that if

are infinite with

, then there exist infinitely many
N\in\mathbb{N}
for which

\min{\{|B\cap[0,N)|,|C\cap[0,N)|\}}<\mathcal{H}(N).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be non-decreasing with
\mathcal{H}(n)\to\infty
as

. Define the function
I:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
as

Similarly to
Theorem

of lower density 1 by building its indicator function as concatenation of a sequence of finite words
(w_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.
A
is defined as

A=\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:W(n)=1\},

where

is the unique infinite word such that
W|_{[0,|W_{i}|)}=W_{i}
for each

, where
W_{i}=w_{1}\cdots w_{i}
. These finite words will have the following properties:

1.

(Forbidding patterns) If
l\geq 2

Assume that we have found such sequence

. By

2

\underline{d}(A)=1
. For the sake of contradiction, assume that there are infinite sets

3.3.  Proof of
Proposition

3.2

We will find an
N

.
We will need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
M,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
M\geq 2
. Then there exists a
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every sequence of
K
not necessarily distinct natural numbers
p_{1},\ldots p_{K}
, there is a partition
1\leq i_{0}<i_{1}<\ldots<i_{r+1}\leq K
such that either

M\cdot\sum_{j=i_{\ell-1}}^{i_{\ell}-1}p_{j}\leq\sum_{j=i_{\ell}}^{i_{\ell+1}-1}p_{j},\ \forall\ell\in[r],

(5)

or

M\cdot\sum_{j=i_{\ell}}^{i_{\ell+1}-1}p_{j}\leq\sum_{j=i_{\ell-1}}^{i_{\ell}-1}p_{j},\ \forall\ell\in[r].

(6)

Proof.

Let us first prove the statement for
M=1
. Let
K=R_{3}(r+2,2)
the Ramsey number for
3
-subsets with
2
colors. We color each
3
-subset of
[K]
such that for
i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}
, the set
\{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\}
is colored red if

\sum_{j=i_{1}}^{i_{2}-1}p_{j}\leq\sum_{j=i_{2}}^{i_{3}-1}p_{j}

and blue otherwise. By Ramsey’s theorem, there is a set
I:=\{i_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{r+1}\subseteq[K]
such that either every
3
-subset of
I
is red or every
3
-subset of
I
is blue. On one hand, if the color is red, then we obtain
Eq.

5
. On the other hand, if the color is blue, then get
Eq.

6
, concluding the case
M=1
.

For the general case, take
r=M^{r^{\prime}+1}+1
, apply the first case and assume without loss of generality that

\sum_{j=i_{\ell-1}}^{i_{\ell}-1}p_{j}\leq\sum_{j=i_{\ell}}^{i_{\ell+1}-1}p_{j},\ \forall\ell\in[r].

Define for each
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,r^{\prime}+1\}
define
k_{\ell}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}M^{j}
. This way, we get that

M\cdot\sum_{j=k_{\ell-1}}^{k_{\ell}-1}p_{j}\leq\sum_{j=k_{\ell}}^{k_{\ell+1}-1}p_{j},\ \forall\ell\in[r^{\prime}],

concluding.
∎

From
Lemma

3.5
we achieve the following corollary.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
M,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
M\geq 2
. Then there exists a
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
|A|=K
there exists
a_{0}<a_{1}<\ldots<a_{r}\in A
with either

M(a_{i}-a_{i-1})\leq(a_{i+1}-a_{i}),\ \forall i\in[r-1],

or

M(a_{i+1}-a_{i})\leq(a_{i}-a_{i-1}),\ \forall i\in[r-1].

Proof.

Let
K^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
given by
Lemma

3.5
with
M
and
r+1
. Set
K=K^{\prime}+1
. Write in increasing order
A=\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
, and set
p_{j}=b_{j+1}-b_{j}
for
j\in[K-1]
. By the conclusion of
Lemma

3.5
we have that there is a partition
1\leq i_{0}<i_{1}<\ldots<i_{r+1}\leq K
such that either
Eq.

5
or
Eq.

6
hold. Observing that

\sum_{j=i_{l-1}}^{i_{l}-1}p_{j}=\sum_{j=i_{l-1}}^{i_{l}-1}b_{j+1}-b_{j}=b_{i_{l}}-b_{i_{l-1}},

and setting
a_{l}=b_{i_{l}}
for each
l\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
yields the conclusion.
∎

The motivation behind
Corollary

3.6
is that if a set is large enough, we can always obtain a subset with gaps geometrically increasing. Such kind of patterns are manageable to forbid because they can be treated approximately like an arithmetic progression, which is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7

(Forbidding Increasing Gap Sequences)
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
B=\{b_{0}<\ldots<b_{r}\}
with either

\forall i\in[r-1],\ M(b_{i}-b_{i-1})\leq(b_{i+1}-b_{i})\text{ and }b_{1}-b_{0}\geq M

or

\forall i\in[r-1],\ M(b_{i+1}-b_{i})\leq(b_{i}-b_{i-1})\text{ and }b_{r}-b_{r-1}\geq M,

then for infinitely many
N
there is a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{N}
with

\sigma_{N}(A)\geq\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)(1-\epsilon),

such that
A
contains no shift of
B
in
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality take
B=\{0=b_{0}<b_{1}<\ldots<b_{r}\}
such that
M(b_{i}-b_{i-1})<b_{i+1}-b_{i}
for each
i\in[r-1]
. Our goal is to construct a set
A\subseteq[0,b_{1}+b_{r})
such that
A
contains no shifts of
B
mod
b_{1}+b_{r}
. It suffices to construct a set
A
that contains no shifts of the set
B_{1}:=\{0,b_{1},b_{1}+b_{1},b_{2}+b_{1},\ldots,b_{r-1}+b_{1}\}
mod
b_{1}+b_{r}
. For each
0\leq i\leq r-2
let
\ell^{(i)}
be such that for some
0\leq k_{i}<(b_{r-i-1}+(i+2)b_{1})
we have

(\ell^{(i)}+1)(b_{r-i-1}+(i+2)b_{1})+k_{i}=b_{r-i}+(i+1)b_{1}.

Namely,
\ell^{(0)}
is such that such that
(\ell^{(0)}+1)(b_{r-1}+2b_{1})+k_{0}=b_{r}+b_{1}
for some
0\leq k_{0}<b_{r-1}+2b_{1}
. We will partition
[0,b_{1}+b_{r})
into the following
\ell^{(0)}+1
many pieces in the following way:

[0,b_{r}+b_{1})=\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\ell^{(0)}}\left[(i-1)(b_{r-1}+2b_{1}),i(b_{r-1}+2b_{1})\right)\right)\sqcup\left[\ell^{(0)}(b_{r-1}+2b_{1}),b_{r}+b_{1}\right).

Suppose that there exists
A^{1}\subseteq[0,b_{r-1}+2b_{1})
such that
A^{1}
contains no shifts of
B_{1}
mod
b_{r-1}+2b_{1}
. Then build
A
by in each set
[(i-1)(b_{r-1}+2b_{1}),i(b_{r-1}+2b_{1}))
including
(i-1)(b_{r-1}+2b_{1})+A^{1}
, but not including the elements of
\left[\ell^{(0)}(b_{r-1}+2b_{1}),b_{r}+b_{1}\right)
, this is:

A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell^{(0)}}\left((i-1)(b_{r-1}+2b_{1})+A^{1}\right)

We claim
A
contains no shifts of
B
mod
b_{1}+b_{r}
. We prove this by contradiction and dividing in three cases:

Case 1:
Assume that
A
contains a shift
j+B
mod
b_{r}+b_{1}
where
0\leq j\leq b_{r}+b_{1}-1
and
j+b_{r}<b_{r}+b_{1}
.

Note first that
|(b_{r}+b_{1}-1)-(j+b_{r})|\leq b_{1}-1
. Since the length of
\left[\ell^{(0)}(b_{r-1}+2b_{1}),b_{r}+b_{1}\right)
is greater than or equal to
b_{r-1}+2b_{1}
and
b_{1}-1<b_{r-1}+2b_{1}
we have

\ell(b_{r-1}+2b_{1})<j+b_{r}\leq b_{r}+b_{1}-1.

Hence,
j+b_{r}\notin A
and
j+B\not\subseteq A
mod
b_{r}+b_{1}
, a contradiction.

Case 2:
Assume that
A
contains a shift
j+B
mod
b_{r}+b_{1}
where
0\leq j\leq b_{r}+b_{1}-1
,
j+b_{r}\geq b_{r}+b_{1}
and
j+b_{r-1}<b_{r}+b_{1}
. Then, taking
i=j-b_{1}
,
A
contains a set of the form

i+B_{1}=\{i,i+b_{1},i+b_{1}+b_{1},i+b_{2}+b_{1},\ldots,i+b_{r-1}+b_{1}\}.

This way, either
i+b_{r-1}+b_{1}
is in the final partition element
\left[\ell^{(0)}(b_{r-1}+2b_{1}),b_{r}+b_{1}\right)
or
A^{1}
contains a shift of
B_{1}
mod
b_{r-1}+2b_{1}
, a contradiction.

Case 3:
Assume that
A
contains a shift
j+B
mod
b_{r}+b_{1}
where
0\leq j\leq b_{r}+b_{1}-1
,
j+b_{r-1}\geq b_{r}+b_{1}
. Then, either

j+b_{i}\in\left[\ell^{(0)}(b_{r-1}+2b_{1}),b_{k}+b_{1}\right),\text{ or }j+b_{i}\geq b_{r}+b_{1}

for each
i
, in which case since
b_{0}=0
we have that
j\geq b_{r}+b_{1}-1
, a contradiction in either case.

Hence, by exhaustion of all possible cases the result follows. We are left to prove that it is possible to find a set
A^{1}
in
[0,b_{r-1}+2b_{1})
containing no shift of
B_{1}
mod
b_{r-1}+2b_{1}
. For this, we partition
[0,b_{r-1}+2b_{1})
into
\ell^{(1)}+1
many sets

[0,b_{r-1}+2b_{1})=\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\ell^{(1)}}\left[(i-1)(b_{r-2}+3b_{1}),i(b_{r-2}+3b_{1})\right)\right)\sqcup\left[\ell^{(1)}(b_{r-2}+3b_{1}),b_{r-1}+2b_{1}\right).

Similarly to the first iteration, it would suffice to construct a
A^{2}\subseteq[0,b_{r-2}+3b_{1})
that contains no shifts of
B_{2}:=\{0,b_{1},2b_{1},3b_{1},b_{2}+2b_{1},b_{3}+2b_{1},\ldots,b_{r-2}+2b_{1}\}
mod
b_{r-2}+3b_{1}
, and then set

A^{1}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell^{(1)}}\left((i-1)(b_{r-2}+3b_{1})+A^{2}\right).

Continuing this process in the natural way, in each iteration we partition the interval

\displaystyle[0,b_{r-j}+(j+1)b_{1})=

\displaystyle\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\ell^{(j)}}\left[(i-1)(b_{r-(j+1)}+(j+2)b_{1}),i(b_{r-(j+1)}+(j+2)b_{1})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\sqcup\left[\ell^{(j)}(b_{r-(j+1)}+(j+2)b_{1}),b_{r-j}+(j+1)b_{1}\right).

to construct a set
A^{j}\subseteq[0,b_{r-j}+(j+1)b_{1})
containing no shifts of
B_{j}:=\{0,b_{1},2b_{1},\ldots,(j+1)b_{1},b_{2}+jb_{1},\ldots,b_{r-j}+jb_{1}\}
mod
b_{b_{r-j}+(j+1)b_{1}}
such that

A^{j}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell^{(j)}}\left((i-1)(b_{r-(j+1))}+(j+2)b_{1})+A^{j+1}\right),

where
A^{j+1}
is yet to be defined in the next iteration.

Consequently, we reduce to the goal of constructing a
A^{r}\subseteq[0,(r+1)b_{1})
that contains no shifts of
B_{r}=\{0,b_{1},2b_{1},\ldots rb_{1}\}
. To do so, we set
A^{r}:=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots rb_{1}-1\}
. We observe that

\sigma_{(r+1)b_{1}}(A^{r})=\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right).

Moreover, since in each step of our proof
A^{j}
is construction gluing up
\ell^{(j)}
copies of
A^{j+1}
and leaving a small interval empty, we get that for each
j\in[r]:

\sigma_{b_{r-j}+(j+1)b_{1}}(A^{j})\geq\sigma_{b_{r-(j+1)}+(j+2)b_{1}}(A^{j+1})\left(\frac{\ell^{(j)}}{\ell^{(j)}+2}\right)\geq\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)\cdot\min_{i=j,\ldots,r-1}\left(\frac{\ell^{(i)}}{\ell^{(i)}+2}\right)^{r-1}.

We thus have constructed a
A\subseteq[0,b_{1}+b_{r})
that contains no shifts of
B
, and such that

\sigma_{b_{r}+b_{1}}(A)\geq\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)\cdot\min_{i=1,\ldots,r-1}\left(\frac{\ell^{(i)}}{\ell^{(i)}+2}\right)^{r-1}.

Here note that
\min_{i=1,\ldots,r-1}|\frac{\ell^{(i)}}{\ell^{(i)}+2}|
can be made arbitrarily close to
1
by choosing
M
to be large. Hence, we can choose
M
such that

\sigma_{b_{r}+b_{1}}(A)\geq\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)(1-\epsilon),

concluding.
∎

Finally, we prove
Proposition

3.2
.

Proof of
Proposition

3.2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Let

\delta=1-\frac{1-\epsilon}{1-\frac{1}{1+r}}

and
M\in\mathbb{N}
given by
Lemma

3.7
for
\delta
and
r
. Then, let
K\in\mathbb{N}
given by
Corollary

3.6
, and let
P\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with
|P|\geq K
. Then, by
Corollary

3.6
, there is a subset

such that either
\forall i\in[r],\ M(b_{i}-b_{i-1})\leq(b_{i+1}-b_{i})
or
\forall i\in[r],\ M(b_{i+1}-b_{i})\leq(b_{i}-b_{i-1})
. By

3.7
applied with

, and

, we find

and

∎

4.  Asymptotics for finite sumsets

In this section, we prove
B
and its more general version. To build intuition, we first prove
B
before turning to the general result. Finally, we prove
C
by actually providing a growth rate for
k
-fold sumsets
B_{1}+\cdots+B_{k}
.

4.1.  Proof of
B

We recall that for
\delta\in(0,1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\phi_{\delta}
as the largest integer such that any set
A\subseteq[N]
with
|A|\geq\delta N
contains a set of the form
B+C
where
B,C\subseteq[N]
satisfy
\min\{|B|,|C|\}\geq\phi_{\delta}(N)
. Here we restate
B
for readability.

Theorem
B
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and
0<\sigma<1/\log{(\delta^{-1})}
. Let
\alpha>0
and let
M=M(N)=\omega(N^{\alpha})
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, there is
\overline{N}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq\overline{N}
, the following holds: for each
m\in[M]
let
A_{m}\subseteq[N]
satisfying
|A_{m}|\geq\delta N
. Then there is
B\subseteq[M]
with
|B|\geq\sigma\log{N}
such that

\left|\bigcap_{m\in B}A_{m}\right|\geq N^{1-\sigma\log(\delta^{-1})}.

For the sake of brevity, we will omit the explanation of some details. These details will be formalized later in the more general proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

Proof.

Let
n\in[N]
be a uniform random variable in
[N]
. Define

L_{n}=\{m\in[M]:n\in A_{m}\}.

By the hypothesis over
A_{m}
,
m\in[M]
, we have that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(|L_{n}|)=\sum_{m\in[M]}\mathbb{P}(n\in A_{m})\geq\delta M.

Now, pick
B\in\binom{[M]}{\sigma\log{N}}
uniformly at random and independent from
n
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{m\in B}A_{m}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=N\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\mathbb{P}\left(n\in\bigcap_{m\in B}A_{m}\Bigg|n\right)\right)

\displaystyle=N\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\mathbb{P}\left(B\subseteq L_{n}\Bigg|n\right)\right)

\displaystyle=N\binom{M}{\sigma\log{N}}^{-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\binom{|L_{n}|}{\sigma\log{N}}\right).

Using the fact that the function
\Psi
defined as

\Psi(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x<y\\
\binom{x}{y}&\text{ if }x\geq y\end{cases}.

is convex for a fixed
y\in[N]
, we get that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\binom{|L_{n}|}{\sigma\log{N}}\right)\geq\binom{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(|L_{n}|\right)}{\sigma\log{N}}\geq\binom{\delta M}{\sigma\log{N}}.

This way, we have that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{m\in B}A_{m}\right|\right)\geq N\binom{M}{\sigma\log{N}}^{-1}\binom{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(|L_{n}|\right)}{\sigma\log{N}}\geq N\binom{M}{\sigma\log{N}}^{-1}\binom{\delta M}{\sigma\log{N}}.

For
N
sufficiently large we obtain that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{m\in B}A_{m}\right|\right)\geq N(\delta M/M)^{\sigma\log{N}}=N^{1-\sigma\log(\delta^{-1})},

from which the conclusion follows.
∎

4.2.  A generalization of
B

In this section, we will make use of the following fact which is a basic consequence of Stirling’s formula: If
n=\omega(u^{2})
then

\binom{n}{u}\thicksim\left(\frac{ne}{u}\right)^{u}(2\pi u)^{-1/2}.

In particular, if
n_{1},n_{2}=\omega(u^{2})
then

\binom{n_{1}}{u}\binom{n_{2}}{u}^{-1}\thicksim\left(\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}\right)^{u}.

(7)

We now state our generalization of
B
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\delta,\tilde{\delta}\in(0,1)
such that
\tilde{\delta}<\delta
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\sigma<1/\log{(\tilde{\delta}^{-1})}
. Let
\alpha>0
and let
M=M(N)=\omega(N^{\alpha})
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
. There is
\overline{N}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq\overline{N}
, the following holds: for each
\bm{m}\in[M]^{k}
let
A_{\bm{m}}\subseteq[N]
satisfying
|A_{\bm{m}}|\geq\delta N
. Then, there are
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\subseteq[M]
with
|B_{i}|\geq(\sigma\log{N})^{1/k}
for each
i\in[k]
, such that

\left|\bigcap_{\bm{m}\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k}}A_{\bm{m}}\right|\geq N^{1-\sigma\log(\tilde{\delta}^{-1})}.

To prove
Theorem

4.1
, we follow a strategy similar to the one used in the proof of
B
. Let us briefly recall the idea. First, one obtains a lower bound for
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(L_{n})
, where
n\in[N]
is chosen uniformly at random. Second, using Jensen’s inequality, one derives a lower bound for

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{m\in B}A_{m}\right|\right)

in terms of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(L_{n})
, where
B\subseteq[M]
is a set of cardinality
\lceil\sigma\log{N}\rceil
picked at random. The theorem then follows by taking
N
sufficiently large. In
Theorem

4.1
this strategy becomes somewhat more involved. We will write
(m_{j})_{j\neq i}
as short for

(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1},m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{k})\in[M]^{k-1}.

For
n\in[N]
and
(m_{j})_{j\neq i}\in[M]^{k-1}
define

L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i})}=\{m\in[M]:n\in A_{m_{1},\ldots m_{i-1},m,m_{i+1},\ldots m_{k}}\}.

Pick a uniform random variable
n\in[N]
. Also, pick
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\subseteq[N]
sets of cardinality
\lceil\sigma\log{N})^{1/k}\rceil
at random. Our first step in this proof is to find a lower bound for

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k-1}}L_{k,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq k})}\right|\right).

(8)

To find a good bound, we want to use the assumption that the sets
A_{\bm{m}}
have large density. In order to do so, we will fix the coordinates
m_{j}
inductively. For this reason, it is convenient to prove a lower bound for the following sequence of objects: for every
i\in[k]
,
m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{k}\in[M]
, we give a lower bound for

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-1}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i})}\right|\right).

Taking
i=k
then gives the desired bound in
Eq.

8
.
The second step is to apply Jensen’s inequality in order to lower bound

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{\bm{m}\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k}}A_{\bm{m}}\right|\right)

by an expression depending on the quantity in
Eq.

8
. The conclusion then follows by choosing
N
sufficiently large.

Having outlined the overall idea of the proof, we now proceed with the details.

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

We start fixing the parameters formalizing the estimations associated to
Eq.

7
. Let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}\in(0,1]
so that
\tilde{\delta}=\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k}\delta
and for sufficiently large
N
, we have that for each
i\in[k]
,

\binom{M}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}^{-1}\binom{M(\eta_{1}\ldots\eta_{i-1}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(i-2)/k}}}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}

is greater or equal than

\eta_{i}\left((\eta_{1}\ldots\eta_{i-1}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(i-2)/k}}\right)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}.

The fact that such
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k}
and
N
exists comes from the estimate associated to
Eq.

7
with

n_{1}=M(\eta_{1}\ldots\eta_{i-1}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(i-2)/k}},\penalty\ n_{2}=M,\penalty\ \text{ and }u=(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}.

Let
i\in[k]
,
n\in[N]
, and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1},m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{k}\in[N]
. Define

L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i})}=\{m\in[M]:n\in A_{m_{1},\ldots m_{i-1},m,m_{i+1},\ldots m_{k}}\},

this is, all the coordinates
m=m_{i}
such that
n\in A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}}
.

Pick
B_{1}\ldots,B_{k}\in\binom{[M]}{(\sigma\log{N})^{1/k}}
and
n\in[N]
uniformly at random and independent. We claim that for every
i\in[k]
,
m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{k}\in[M]
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-1}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i})}\right|\right)\geq M(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{i-1}\delta)^{(\sigma\log{N})^{(i-1)/k}},

(9)

where
m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1}
are dummy variables. In fact, for
i=1
we take
m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}\in[M]
and compute:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|L_{1,(n,m_{2},\ldots,m_{k})}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}\in[M]}\mathbb{P}(n\in A_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}})

\displaystyle\geq M\delta\geq M\delta^{\prime},

which ultimately gives
Eq.

9
for
i=1
.

Assume that we know the result for
i-1
, where
i\geq 2
. Fix
m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{k}\in[M]
. We have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-1}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i})}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{i}\in[M]}\mathbb{P}\left(m_{i}\in\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-1}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i})}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{i}\in[M]}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\mathbb{P}\left(B_{i-1}\subseteq\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-2})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-2}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i-1})}\Bigg|n,B_{1},\ldots,B_{i-2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{i}\in[M]}\binom{M}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}^{-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\binom{\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-2})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-2}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i-1})}\right|}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}\right),

where the second equality comes from basic properties of the conditional expectation, and in the third equality we used that, conditional to having chosen
n
,
B_{1},\ldots,B_{i-2}
, the probability that
B_{i-1}
is contained in

\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-2})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-2}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i-1})}

is equal to

\binom{M}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}^{-1}\binom{\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-2})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-2}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i-1})}\right|}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}},

by definition
B_{i-1}
.

Define the function
\Psi
as

\Psi(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x<y\\
\binom{x}{y}&\text{ if }x\geq y\end{cases}.

We observe that
\Psi
is convex for a fixed
y\in[N]
. Therefore, by Jensen inequality we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\binom{\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-2})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-2}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i-1})}\right|}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-2})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-2}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i-1})}\right|\right)}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}

\displaystyle\geq\binom{M(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{i-2}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(i-2)/k}}}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}

where we used the induction hypothesis in the last inequality. This way, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{i-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{i-1}}L_{i,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq i})}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m_{i}\in[M]}\binom{M}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}^{-1}\binom{M(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{i-2}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(i-2)/k}}}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}

\displaystyle\geq\eta_{i-1}\sum_{m_{i}\in[M]}\left(\frac{M(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{i-2}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(i-2)/k}}}{M}\right)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}

\displaystyle\geq M(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{i-1}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(i-1)/k}},

concluding the induction. We remark that our algebraic manipulations are all valid since
M(\delta^{\prime})^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(k-1)/k}}\geq\exp(\alpha\log(N)-\log(\delta^{\prime-1})(\sigma\log(N))^{(k-1)/k})\geq N^{\alpha/2}
whenever
N
is sufficiently large.

Now, we can compute the following:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{\bm{m}\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k}}A_{\bm{m}}\right|\right)=N\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\mathbb{P}\left(n\in\bigcap_{\bm{m}\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k}}A_{\bm{m}}\Bigg|n\right)\right)

\displaystyle=N\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\mathbb{P}\left(B_{k}\subseteq\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k-1}}L_{k,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq k})}\Bigg|n\right)\right)

\displaystyle=N\binom{M}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}^{-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\binom{\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k-1}}L_{k,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq k})}\right|}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq N\binom{M}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}^{-1}\binom{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k-1})\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k-1}}L_{k,(n,(m_{j})_{j\neq k})}\right|\right)}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}

\displaystyle\geq N\binom{M}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}^{-1}\binom{M(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k-1}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(k-1)/k}}}{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}

\displaystyle\geq N((\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))^{(k-1)/k}})^{(\sigma\log(N))^{1/k}}

where we used
Eq.

9
with
i=k
in the second to last inequality. Hence, we get

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left(\left|\bigcap_{\bm{m}\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k}}A_{\bm{m}}\right|\right)\geq N(\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{k}\delta)^{(\sigma\log(N))}=N^{1-\sigma\log(\tilde{\delta}^{-1})},

finishing the proof.
∎

4.3.  Lower Asymptotics for
\phi

Let
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
. Define

\phi_{k}(\delta,N)=\min_{A\subseteq[N],|A|\geq\delta N}\max_{B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\subseteq[N],B_{1}+\cdots+B_{k}\subseteq A}\min\{|B_{1}|,\ldots,|B_{k}|\}.

That is,
\phi_{k}(\delta,N)
denotes the largest integer such that any set
A\subseteq[N]
with
|A|\geq\delta N
contains
B_{1}+\cdots+B_{k}
as a subset, where
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\subseteq[N]
satisfy
\min\{|B_{1}|,\ldots,|B_{k}|\}\geq\phi_{k}(\delta,N)
.
We will prove that for a fixed
\delta>0
, for all
\alpha<(1/\log(\delta^{-1}))^{1/(k-1)}
the expression
\phi_{k}(\delta,N)/\log(N)^{1/(k-1)}
is bounded below by
\alpha
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. Equivalently, there is
\overline{N}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq\overline{N}
for all
A\subseteq[N]
with
|A|\geq\delta N
, there are
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\subseteq[N]
with
B_{1}+\cdots+B_{k}\subseteq A
and such that
|B_{1}|,\ldots,|B_{k}|\geq\alpha\log(N)^{1/(k-1)}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For every
\delta>0
, we have

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{k}(\delta,N)}{\log(N)^{1/(k-1)}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{\log(1/\delta)}\right)^{1/(k-1)}.

Proof.

Let
\delta^{\prime}<\tilde{\delta}<\delta
and
\epsilon>0
. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be large enough that
\delta N-(k-1)N^{1-\epsilon}\geq\tilde{\delta}N
. Let
M=N^{1-\epsilon}
and take

\sigma>0
such that

\sigma<\frac{1}{\log(\delta^{\prime-1})}.

Define for each
\bm{m}\in[M]^{k-1}
the set
A_{\bm{m}}=(A-(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k-1}))\cap[N]
. Notice that
|A_{\bm{m}}|\geq\delta N-(k-1)N^{1-\epsilon}
.
By
Theorem

4.1
, we can find sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k-1}\subseteq[M]
with
|B_{1}|,\ldots,|B_{k-1}|\geq(\sigma\log(N))^{1/(k-1)}
such that

\left|\bigcap_{\bm{m}\in B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{k-1}}A_{\bm{m}}\right|\geq N^{1-\sigma\log(\delta^{\prime-1})}.

This implies that there are
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\subseteq[N]
with
|B_{1}|,\ldots,|B_{k}|\geq(\sigma\log(N))^{1/(k-1)}
such that
B_{1}+\cdots+B_{k}\subseteq A
. Thus

\sigma^{1/(k-1)}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{k}(\delta,N)}{\log(N)^{1/(k-1)}}.

Taking
\sigma\nearrow\left(\frac{1}{\log(\delta^{\prime-1})}\right)
we have

\left(\frac{1}{\log(\delta^{\prime-1})}\right)^{1/(k-1)}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{k}(\delta,N)}{\log(N)^{1/(k-1)}}.

Taking
\delta^{\prime}\nearrow\delta
we conclude that

\left(\frac{1}{\log(\delta^{-1})}\right)^{1/(k-1)}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{k}(\delta,N)}{\log(N)^{1/(k-1)}},

finishing the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

C
follows as from
Theorem

4.2
by taking
k=2
.
