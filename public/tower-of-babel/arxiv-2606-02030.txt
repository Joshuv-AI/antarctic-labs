A Domatic Analogue of χ \chi -Bounded Graph Classes and the Gyárfás–Sumner Conjecture
Quentin Chuet, Selma Djelloul, Hoang La, François Pirot, Hossein Zaredehabadi

Abstract
Given a graph G G , a dominating set is a subset X ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) X\subseteq V(G) such that N ⁡ [ X ] = V ⁡ ( G ) N[X]=V(G) . The domatic number of G G , denoted DOM ⁡ ( G ) \dom(G) , is the maximum size of a partition of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) into dominating sets. In analogy with the lower bound of the chromatic number by the clique number, the domatic number satisfies the upper bound DOM ⁡ ( G ) ≤ δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 \dom(G)\leq\delta(G)+1 where δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G) is the minimum degree of G G . Therefore, as an analogue of the notion of χ \chi -bounded graph classes, we say that a class of graphs 𝒢 \mathscr{G} is DOM-bounded if there exists a positive unbounded function f 𝒢 f_{\mathscr{G}} such that for every G ∈ 𝒢 G\in\mathscr{G} , we have DOM ⁡ ( G ) ≥ f 𝒢 ​ ( δ ⁡ ( G ) ) \dom(G)\geq f_{\mathscr{G}}(\delta(G)) . We propose the following conjecture for graphs forbidding a fixed induced subgraph, analogous to the Gyárfás–Sumner Conjecture for χ \chi -bounded graph classes: for every connected graph H H , the class of H H -free graphs is DOM-bounded if and only if H H is a tree of diameter at most 3 3 . We reduce the case of disconnected graphs to the connected setting and show that the conditions on H H are necessary. We show that star-free graphs of minimum degree at least δ \delta have domatic number Ω ⁡ ( δ / log ⁡ δ ) \Omega(\delta/\log\delta) , which is best possible up to a constant factor. We also identify a subclass of star-free graphs for which the domatic number is linear in δ \delta : line graphs of bounded rank hypergraphs. In support of our conjecture in the case of double stars, we prove that P 4 P_{4} -free graphs (i.e. cographs) of minimum degree δ \delta have domatic number at least 1 + δ 2 1+\frac{\delta}{2} , which is best possible.

Introduction

Let
G
be a graph and
k
an integer. Given a
k
-colouring
\phi\colon V(G)\to[k]
, we say that
\phi
is
dominating
if
\phi(N[v])=[k]
for every vertex
v\in V(G)
. Equivalently, the colour classes of
\phi
form a
domatic partition
of
G
, that is, a partition of
V(G)
into dominating sets (recall that a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
is
dominating
if
N[X]=V(G)
). The
domatic number
\dom(G)
of
G
is the maximum
k
such that a dominating
k
-colouring of
G
exists.

A standard upper bound is
\dom(G)\leq\delta(G)+1
, where
\delta(G)
denotes the minimum degree of
G
. If equality holds, then
G
is said to be
domatically full
. The largest known class of domatically full graphs is that of strongly chordal graphs
[
5
]
.
The equality between the chromatic number and the clique in perfect graphs, and more generally, their functional equivalence in
\chi
-bounded classes naturally parallels the relationship between the domatic number and the minimum degree in domatically full graphs, and more broadly, in DOM-bounded classes which we define as follows.
Given a class of graphs
\mathscr{G}
, we say that
\mathscr{G}
is
DOM-bounded
if there exists a positive unbounded function
f_{\mathscr{G}}
such that

\dom(G)\geq f_{\mathscr{G}}(\delta(G))

for every graph
G\in\mathscr{G}
.
We say that
f_{\mathscr{G}}
is a
DOM-binding function of
\mathscr{G}
, and if there is no graph
G\in\mathscr{G}
such that
\dom(G)>f_{\mathscr{G}}(\delta(G))
, we further say that
f_{\mathscr{G}}
is
optimal
.
This condition is trivially satisfied when
\mathscr{G}
has bounded minimum degree; consequently, we restrict our attention to classes with arbitrarily large minimum degree.
In particular, non-trivial minor-closed classes of graphs are not relevant with respect to DOM-boundedness, as every
H
-minor-free graph (for a fixed
t
-vertex graph
H
) is
O(t\sqrt{\log t})
-degenerate
[
10
]
.

Zelinka
[
17
]
proved that the class of all graphs is not DOM-bounded by constructing graphs with domatic number
2
and arbitrarily large minimum degree (see
Section

3
). By modifying Zelinka’s construction (which is bipartite), we obtain split graphs and high-girth graphs with the same property.
Hence, bipartite graphs, split graphs, and graphs of high girth are not DOM-bounded. The objective of this paper is to identify structural constraints that guarantee DOM-boundedness.

We turn our attention to the classes of graphs with forbidden sub-structures.

Forbidden subgraphs

Consider the class
\mathscr{G}
of graphs that do not contain a fixed graph
H
as a subgraph. If
H
contains a cycle of length
\ell
, then
\mathscr{G}
contains the class of graphs of girth greater than
\ell
, which is not DOM-bounded. Otherwise,
H
is a forest, and every graph in
\mathscr{G}
is
(|V(H)|-2)
-degenerate, and therefore has bounded minimum degree. Thus, forbidding a subgraph does not provide a meaningful structural constraint with respect to DOM-boundedness.

Forbidden induced subgraphs

A graph
G
is
H
-free
if it does not contain
H
as an induced subgraph; we denote by
\forb(H)
the class of
H
-free graphs. More generally, given a family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs, we say that
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free if it is
H
-free for every
H\in\mathcal{F}
, and we denote by
\forb(\mathcal{F})
the corresponding class. Our goal is to characterise the graphs
H
for which
\forb(H)
is DOM-bounded.

The analogous problem for
\chi
-boundedness is the Gyárfás–Sumner Conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Gyárfás, 1975
[
9
]
; Sumner, 1981
[
16
]
)
.

For every graph
H
,
\forb(H)
is
\chi
-bounded if and only if
H
is a forest.

As in the case of forbidding
H
as a (non-induced) subgraph, if
H
contains a cycle of length
\ell
, then
\forb(H)
contains the class of graphs of girth greater than
\ell
, which is not DOM-bounded.
If
H
is connected and has diameter at least
4
, then it contains an induced copy of
P_{5}
.
Hence,
\forb(H)
contains the class of split graphs (which are
P_{5}
-free) and is not DOM-bounded.
If
H
has at least two connected components of size at least
2
, then
\forb(H)
contains
\forb(2K_{2})
, which in turn contains the class of split graphs. Hence,
\forb(H)
is not DOM-bounded.
Consequently, when
H
is disconnected,
\forb(H)
can be DOM-bounded only if there exist a connected graph
H_{0}
and a positive integer
k
such that
H
is the disjoint union of
H_{0}
and
k
isolated vertices, and
\forb(H_{0})
is DOM-bounded (since
\forb(H_{0})\subseteq\forb(H)
). We prove that the converse also holds.

Theorem 2
.

Let
H
be a disconnected graph. Then
\forb(H)
is DOM-bounded if and only if
H
has at most one connected component
H_{0}
of order at least
2
, and
\forb(H_{0})
is DOM-bounded.

The remaining graphs to consider are trees of diameter at most
3
.
We conjecture that all such graphs yield DOM-bounded classes.

Conjecture 3
.

For every connected graph
H
,
\forb(H)
is DOM-bounded if and only if
H
is a tree of diameter at most
3
.

A tree of diameter at most
3
is either a star (diameter
2
) or a double star (diameter
3
). We partially resolve
3
by establishing the result when
H
is a star.

Theorem 4
.

Let
k
be an integer such that
k\geq 2
.
If
G
is a
K_{1,k+1}
-free graph of minimum degree
\delta\geq 4^{1+k^{2}}
, then

\dom(G)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{3\log\delta}\right\rfloor.

Note that
K_{1,1}
-free are edgeless graphs, and
K_{1,2}
-free graphs are disjoint unions of cliques, which are domatically full.
Furthermore,
4
is tight up to an absolute constant factor, as shown in
16
.

We then investigate which subclasses of star-free graphs admit a linear DOM-binding function.
We prove it for line graphs of rank-
k
hypergraphs (that is, hypergraphs with edge size at most
k
), which form a subclass of
\forb(K_{1,k+1})
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
k
be an integer such that
k\geq 2
.
If
G
is the line graph of a rank-
k
hypergraph, then

\dom(G)\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{k(\log k+O(\log\log k))}.

Finally, we consider double-star-free graphs in support of
3
. The smallest double star is
P_{4}
, and
\forb(P_{4})
coincides with the class of cographs
[
15
]
. We show that cographs are linearly DOM-bounded and determine the optimal DOM-binding function.

Theorem 6
.

For every cograph
G
,

\dom(G)\geq 1+\frac{\delta(G)}{2}.

The bound in
6
is tight due to complete multipartite graphs where every part has size 2.

2.2
Total domatic number

Given a graph
G
, a
total dominating set
of
G
is a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
such that every vertex
v\in V(G)
has at least one neighbour in
X
. Note that no total dominating set exists in
G
if
G
has an isolated vertex; otherwise
V(G)
is a total dominating set of
G
. Observe also that all total dominating sets of a loopless graph
G
have size at least
2
.
We denote
\tdom(G)
the
total domatic number
of
G
, that is the maximum size of a partition of
V(G)
into total dominating sets, which we call a
total domatic partition of
G
. This corresponds to the colour classes of a
coupon colouring of
G
, as defined in
[
3
]
, which in turn corresponds to a panchromatic colouring
[
11
]
(sometimes also called polychromatic colouring, e.g. in
[
2
]
) of its (open) neighbourhood hypergraph.

Lemma 7
.

For every graph
G
,

\left\lfloor\frac{\dom(G)}{2}\right\rfloor\leq\tdom(G)\leq\dom(G).

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph.
The bound
\tdom(G)\leq\dom(G)
is trivial since a total dominating set of
G
is always, in particular, a dominating set of
G
. Let us prove the other inequality.
The union of two disjoint dominating sets of
G
is a total dominating set. So, given a domatic partition
\mathcal{P}
of
G
of size
k
, one obtains a total domatic partition of
G
of size
\left\lfloor k/2\right\rfloor
by merging the first
2\left\lfloor k/2\right\rfloor-2
parts of
\mathcal{P}
by pairs, and the last two or three ones together.
∎

Observe that the first inequality is best possible, as witnessed by
G=K_{n}
, which has
\dom(G)=n
and
\tdom(G)=\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor
.
The second inequality is also best possible, as witnessed by
G=K_{n,n}
, which has
\dom(G)=\tdom(G)=n
.
One could define the notion of TDOM-boundedness similarly to that of DOM-boundedness;
Lemma

7
implies that these two notions are equivalent.

2.3
Probabilistic tools

We rely on the two following corollaries of the general Lovász Local Lemma, of increasing strength and technicality.

Theorem 8

(LLL)
.

Let
A_{1},...,A_{n}
be events in a probability space
\Omega
with dependency graph
\Gamma
. If there exist real numbers
0\leq x_{1},...,x_{n}<1
such that, for every
i\in[n]
,

\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right]\leq x_{i}\prod_{A_{j}\in N_{\Gamma}(A_{i})}(1-x_{j}),

then

\mathbb{P}\left[\bigwedge_{i\in[n]}\overline{A_{i}}\right]\geq\prod_{i\in[n]}(1-x_{i})>0.

The following corollary appears almost verbatim in
[
14
]
, albeit with a slightly stronger hypothesis. We include its short proof for completeness.

Corollary 9
.

Let
A_{1},...,A_{n}
be events in a probability space
\Omega
with dependency graph
\Gamma
. If, for all
i\in[n]
, one has
\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right]<1
and

\sum_{A_{j}\in N_{\Gamma}(A_{i})}\mathbb{P}\left[A_{j}\right]\leq\frac{1}{4},

(1)

then
\mathbb{P}\left[\bigwedge_{i\in[n]}\overline{A_{i}}\right]>0
.

Proof.

If there is an isolated vertex
i
in
\Gamma
, then
A_{i}
is independent from the other events, so

\mathbb{P}\left[\bigwedge_{j\neq i}\overline{A_{j}}\right]>0\implies\mathbb{P}\left[\bigwedge_{j\in[n]}\overline{A_{j}}\right]>0.

We therefore assume that
\Gamma
contains no isolated vertex. In particular, we have
\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right]\leq 1/4
for all
i\in[n]
.
We let
x_{i}\coloneqq 2\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right]\leq 1/2
for each
i\in[n]
.
Using the inequality
1-x\geq 4^{-x}
for all
0\leq x\leq 1/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle x_{i}\prod_{ij\in E(\Gamma)}(1-x_{j})

\displaystyle\geq x_{i}4^{-\sum_{ij\in E(\Gamma)}x_{j}}=x_{i}4^{-2\sum_{ij\in E(\Gamma)}\mathbb{P}\left[A_{j}\right]}

\displaystyle\geq x_{i}4^{-2\times\frac{1}{4}}=\frac{x_{i}}{2}=\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right],

for all
i\in[n]
. The conclusion follows from
8
.
∎

The following more technical corollary lets us introduce a trade-off between the cumulative probabilities of neighbouring events of a given random event, and its own probability.

Corollary 10
.

Let
A_{1},...,A_{n}
be events in a probability space
\Omega
with dependency graph
\Gamma
. If there exists
\alpha<1
such that, for all
i\in[n]
, one has
\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right]^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and

\sum_{A_{j}\in N_{\Gamma}(A_{i})}\mathbb{P}\left[A_{j}\right]^{\alpha}\leq(1-\alpha)\log_{4}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right]},

(2)

then
\mathbb{P}\left[\bigwedge_{i\in[n]}\overline{A_{i}}\right]>0
.

Proof.

We let
x_{i}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right]^{\alpha}\leq 1/2
for each
i\in[n]
.
Using the inequality
1-x\geq 4^{-x}
for all
0\leq x\leq 1/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle x_{i}\prod_{ij\in E(\Gamma)}(1-x_{j})

\displaystyle\geq x_{i}4^{-\sum_{ij\in E(\Gamma)}x_{j}}=x_{i}4^{-\sum_{ij\in E(\Gamma)}\mathbb{P}\left[A_{j}\right]^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq x_{i}4^{(\alpha-1)\log_{4}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right]}}=\mathbb{P}\left[A_{i}\right],

for all
i\in[n]
. The conclusion follows from
8
.
∎

3
Constructions with small domatic number and high minimum degree

Zelinka
[
17
]
showed that there exist graphs of arbitrarily large minimum degree with bounded (total) domatic number. We repeat the short proof as a warm-up for the next constructions presented in this section, which adapt Zelinka’s construction to satisfy some structural constraints.

Theorem 12

(Zelinka, 1983)
.

For every integer
\delta\geq 2
, there exists a graph of minimum degree
\delta
and total domatic number
1
.

Proof.

Let
n\coloneqq 2d-1
, and let
G_{d}
be the graph with vertex sets
A\cup B
, where
A\coloneqq[n]
and
B\coloneqq\binom{A}{d}
, and edge set
\{ab\in A\times B:a\in b\}
. In any
2
-colouring
\phi
of
G_{d}
, there exists a monochromatic subset
S\subseteq A
of size
d
, which corresponds to an open neighbourhood in
G_{d}
, therefore
\phi
is not a total dominating colouring. We conclude that
\tdom(G_{d})=1
.
∎

Remark 1
.

With a similar argument and
n\geq 3d-2
, we have a graph of minimum degree
d
and domatic number
2
.
We remark that the structure of
G_{d}[A]
does not impact the argument, thus adding any subset of edges to
A
still yields a graph of minimum degree
d
and total domatic number
1
. In particular, adding all possible edges within
A
results in a split graph (
A
is the clique, and
B
is the independent set).

Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, the
bipartite incidence graph
of
\mathcal{H}
is a graph with vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})\cup E(\mathcal{H})
, and such that
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
is adjacent to
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
v\in e
. Observe that the graph
G_{d}
constructed in the proof of
12
is precisely the bipartite incidence graph of
K_{n}^{(d)}
, the complete
d
-uniform hypergraph on
n\coloneqq 2d-1
vertices.

The chromatic number of a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is the minimum number of colours needed to colour
V(\mathcal{H})
in such a way that no edge of
\mathcal{H}
is monochromatic. The proof of
12
relied on the simple fact that
K_{n}^{(d)}
has chromatic number at least
3
if
n\geq 2d-1
, thus attempting to colour the vertices of
K_{n}^{(d)}
with
2
colours produces a monochromatic edge. Zelinka’s construction can be generalised by considering the bipartite incidence graph of any
d
-uniform hypergraphs of
chromatic number
at least
3
. In particular, if said hypergraph has no short “cycles”, then its bipartite incidence graph has large girth.

A
Berge cycle
in a hypergraph is a sequence
(v_{1},e_{1},v_{2},e_{2},\dots,v_{\ell},e_{\ell})
of alternating and distinct vertices and edges, such that
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subseteq e_{i}
for
i\in[\ell-1]
and
\{v_{1},v_{\ell}\}\subseteq e_{\ell}
. The
girth
of a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, denoted
g(\mathcal{H})
, is the length of its smallest Berge cycle.
The existence of
d
-uniform hypergraphs with arbitrarily large girth and chromatic number was first proved by Erdős
[
4
]
. We refer to
[
1
]
for references and a simple construction.

Theorem 13

(Erdős, 1959)
.

For every
k,g,d\geq 2
, there exists a
d
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
of girth at least
g
, and chromatic number at least
k
.

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is called
vertex-critical
if every proper induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
has smaller chromatic number. The degree of a vertex is the number of incident edges.

Lemma 14
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a vertex-critical hypergraph of chromatic number
k
. Then
\mathcal{H}
has minimum degree at least
k-1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
has a vertex
v
of degree less than
k-1
. By vertex-criticality, we have
\chi(\mathcal{H}-v)<k
; let
\phi
be a proper
(k-1)
-colouring of
\mathcal{H}-v
. Adding back
v
and its incident edges, we can extend
\phi
to
v
since there are at most
\deg(v)<k-1
colour constraints for
v
, thus
\mathcal{H}
has chromatic number less than
k
, a contradiction.
∎

Using
d
-uniform hypergraphs of large girth and chromatic number, we construct graphs of large minimum degree and girth with total domatic number
1
.

Proposition 15
.

For every
d\geq 2
and
g\geq 3
, there exists a bipartite graph
G
of minimum degree
d
and girth at least
g
such that
\tdom(G)=1
.

Proof.

We take a
d
-uniform hypergraph of girth
g
and chromatic number at least
d+1
as in the statement of
13
, and we extract a vertex-critical sub-hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with chromatic number
d+1
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{H}
also has girth at least
g
.
Let
G
be the bipartite incidence graph of
\mathcal{H}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}
has edge size and minimum degree both at least
d
,
G
has minimum degree

and girth at least
2g
.
We claim that
\tdom(G)=1
.
Indeed, attempting to colour

with
2

only contains one colour, and

∎

Remark 2
.

It is easy to modify the previous proof so as to have
\dom(G)=2
instead of
\tdom(G)=1
.

In the following proposition, we show that the
\dom
-binding function for star-free graphs in
4
is optimal up to a multiplicative factor.

Proposition 16
.

There exist graphs of independence number
2
with arbitrarily large minimum degree
\delta
, and domatic number at most
(1+o(1))\frac{\delta}{\log\delta}
.

Proof.

Let
n
be a large integer,
t\coloneqq\left\lceil n/(\log n)^{3}+1\right\rceil
, and
p\coloneqq 1/\log n
. Let
G
be constructed as follows: The set of vertices of
G
is
V(G)=A\sqcup B
, where
A=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
and
B=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{t}\}
both induce cliques in
G
; thus
G
has independence number at most
2
. For every
(i,j)\in[n]\times[t]
, the edge
u_{i}v_{j}
is added to
G
independently with probability
p
.

We claim that with nonzero probability,
G
satisfies the following:

•

G
has minimum degree
\delta\geq np=\frac{n}{\log n}
, hence
\delta\leq n\leq(1+o(1))\,\delta\log\delta

•

There is no dominating set
X\subseteq A
of size less than
k\coloneqq\frac{1-p}{p}\log\frac{t}{(\log n)^{4}}\sim(\log n)^{2}
.

We first examine the first desired property. Every vertex in
A
has degree at least
n-1
. As for vertices in
B
, they have
t-1
neighbours in
B
, and their degree in
A
is determined by a binomial random variable of parameters
(n,p)
. By
11
, the probability that
v\in B
has less than
(1-1/(\log n)^{2})\,np
neighbours in
A
is at most
e^{-\frac{np}{2(\log n)^{4}}}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}
. By union bound, the probability that at least one vertex has degree at most
(1-1/(\log n)^{2})\,np
is at most
t/n^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}
. Therefore, with high probability, every vertex in
B
has degree at least
np-\frac{np}{(\log n)^{2}}+t-1\geq np
.

We now examine the second desired property. Let us fix any set
X\subseteq A
of size
k
. Independently for any given vertex
v\in B
, the probability that
X
does not dominate
v
is
(1-p)^{k}\geq e^{-\frac{p}{1-p}k}=\frac{(\log n)^{4}}{t}
, where we used the inequality
1-x\geq e^{-\frac{x}{1-x}}
which holds for all
x<1
. Therefore, the probability that
X
dominates
B
is at most
(1-(1-p)^{k})^{t}\leq e^{-(\log n)^{4}}
. There are
\binom{n}{k}\leq e^{k\log n}
ways to choose such a set
X
, therefore by union bound, the probability that there exists a set
X\subseteq A
of size
k
which dominates
B
is at most
e^{k\log n-(\log n)^{4}}\leq\frac{1}{n}
.

In conclusion, with nonzero probability, both desired properties hold for
G
; in that case,
G
satisfies the statement of
16
. Indeed, in every domatic partition of
G
, at most
t
dominating sets intersect
B
; the others (which must be contained in

) have size at least
k
, and thus
\dom(G)\leq t+\frac{n}{k}\leq(1+o(1))\frac{n}{(\log n)^{2}}\leq(1+o(1))\frac{\delta}{\log\delta}
.
∎

4
Forbidding a disconnected induced subgraph

We first observe that
\forb(2K_{2})
is not DOM-bounded, since it contains the class of split graphs.
We infer that
\forb(H)
can be DOM-bounded only if
H=H_{0}+rK_{1}
for some connected subgraph
H_{0}
and some nonnegative integer

.
In this section, we show that
\forb(H)
is DOM-bounded if and only if
\forb(H_{0})
is.
We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 17
.

Let
G
be a graph of minimum degree
\delta
and let
Z\subseteq V(G)
. There is a partition of
V(G)\setminus Z
into at least
k-|Z|
dominating sets of
G
, where

k\coloneqq\min\left\{\sqrt{\delta/2},\dom(G\setminus Z)\right\}.

Proof.

Let us write
Z=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{t}\}
with
t\leq k
, otherwise there is nothing to prove.
Let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
be a domatic partition of
G\setminus Z
.
We claim that each
z_{i}\in Z
has a set
S(z_{i})
of private neighbours such that

•

|S(z_{i})|=\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{2k}\right\rfloor
,

•

there exists
j\in[k]
such that
S(z_{i})\subseteq D_{j}
, and

•

the sets
\{S(z_{i})\}_{i\in[t]}
are pairwise disjoint.

Those can be constructed iteratively; the set

is selected from the colour class

which has the largest intersection with

By the Pigeonhole Principle, that intersection has size at least
\frac{1}{k}\left(\deg(z_{i})-(i-1)\frac{\delta}{2k}\right)\geq\frac{1}{k}\left(\delta-(t-1)\frac{\delta}{2k}\right)\geq\frac{\delta}{2k}
.
Note that, by definition of

\frac{\delta}{2k}\geq k
.
Without loss of generality, let us assume that
\bigcup_{z\in Z}S(z)\subseteq\bigcup_{j=k-t+1}^{t}D_{j}

For each

we construct

from

By construction, each

So

Theorem 18

Let

Proof.

Let

We construct a domatic partition of

For every nonnegative integer

Proof of claim.

5
Line graphs of rank-
k
hypergraphs

A hypergraph has rank
k
if every edge has size at most
k
. The line graph of a hypergraph is the intersection graph of its edges. Line graphs of rank-
k
hypergraphs are in particular induced-
K_{1,k+1}
-free.
Indeed, if an edge
e
of the hypergraph intersects
k+1
pairwise disjoint edges, then two of these intersections must occur at the same vertex of
e
, forcing the corresponding two neighbours of
e
in the line graph to be adjacent.

In this section, we show that line graphs of rank-
k
hypergraphs are linearly DOM-bounded. We shall first use the following result due to Lovász
[
12
]
concerning the panchromatic number of hypergraphs.

Theorem 19

(Lovász, 1970)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,\mathcal{E})
be a hypergraph. If there exists an integer
\beta\geq 1
such that every nonempty subset
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
verifies

\left\lvert\bigcup\mathcal{F}\right\rvert>(\beta-1)|\mathcal{F}|,

then
\mathcal{H}
has panchromatic number at least
\beta
.

19
suffices to show that the class of line graphs of rank-
k
hypergraphs is linearly DOM-bounded.

Corollary 20
.

Let
G
be a line graph of some rank-
k
hypergraph.
Let
\delta
be the minimum degree of
G
. Then

\dom(G)\geq\frac{\delta}{k^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
H
be a rank-
k
hypergraph such that
G
is the line graph of
H
. For each
v\in V(H)
, we denote
E_{v}
the set of edges of
H
incident to
v
.
Let
V^{+}\coloneqq\left\{v\in V(H):|E_{v}|\geq\delta/k\right\}
; clearly,
V^{+}
is a transversal of
H
. Consider the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with vertex-set
\mathcal{E}(H)
and hyperedge-set
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\left\{E_{v}:v\in V^{+}\right\}
. Observe that each edge
e\in\mathcal{E}(H)
is contained in
|e|\leq k
distinct sets of the form
E_{v}
, one for each vertex of
e
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a non-empty subset of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})
. By double counting, we have

\frac{\delta}{k}|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|F|=\sum_{e\in\bigcup\mathcal{F}}|\{F\in\mathcal{F}:e\in F\}|\leq k\left\lvert\bigcup\mathcal{F}\right\rvert,

hence
\left\lvert\bigcup\mathcal{F}\right\rvert\geq\frac{\delta}{k^{2}}|\mathcal{F}|
for all non-empty subsets
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})
. Thus, by
19
,
\mathcal{H}
has panchromatic number at least
\frac{\delta}{k^{2}}
. Consider any panchromatic
\frac{\delta}{k^{2}}
-colouring of
\mathcal{H}
, and observe that this same colouring of
\mathcal{E}(H)
corresponds to a dominating colouring in
G
: indeed, for every edge
e\in\mathcal{E}(H)
,
e
is incident to some vertex
v\in V^{+}
, and thus is incident to all edges in the set
E_{v}
, which contains every colour. Therefore
\dom(G)\geq\delta/k^{2}
.
∎

In the rest of this subsection, we show that
20
can be quantitatively improved, starting with line graphs of simple graphs (
2
-uniform hypergraphs).

5.1
Line graphs of simple graphs

A
cover decomposition
of a graph is a partition of its edges into edge covers (a subset of edges which covers all vertices). The
cover index
of a graph
G
is the maximum size of a cover decomposition of
G
. Clearly, a graph of minimum degree
\delta
has cover index at most
\delta
. In his thesis
[
7
]
(see also
[
8
]
), Gupta proved that simple graphs of minimum degree
\delta
have cover index at least
\delta-1
.

Theorem 21

(Gupta, 1967)
.

Let
G
be a simple graph. Then
G
has a cover decomposition of size
\delta(G)-1
.

Using this result on cover decompositions, we improve the lower bound
\dom(G)\geq\left\lceil\delta(G)/4\right\rceil
from
20
to
\dom(G)\geq\left\lceil\delta(G)/2\right\rceil-1
, when considering line graphs of simple graphs.

Theorem 22
.

Let
G
be the line graph of a simple graph, and
\delta\coloneqq\delta(G)
. Then

\dom(G)\geq\lceil\delta/2\rceil-1.

Proof.

Let
H
be a simple graph such that
L(H)=G
. Let
V^{+}\subseteq V(H)
be the vertices of
H
of degree at least
\lceil\delta/2\rceil
; observe that
V^{+}
is a vertex cover of
H
. Consider the graph
H^{\prime}
which is a supergraph of
H
where we glue a clique of size
\delta
to every vertex of
V(H)\setminus V^{+}
. Since
H^{\prime}
has minimum degree at least
\lceil\delta/2\rceil
, we apply
21
to obtain a cover decomposition
E^{\prime}_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup E^{\prime}_{r}
of
H^{\prime}
, where
r\geq\lceil\delta/2\rceil-1
. For
i\in[r]
, let
E_{i}\coloneqq E^{\prime}_{i}\cap E(H)
; then
E_{i}
covers
V^{+}
, since no vertex in
V^{+}
is incident to an edge in
E(H^{\prime})\setminus E(H)
, by construction. We conclude that the decomposition
E_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup E_{r}
is a domatic partition of
L(H)=G
.
∎

Remark 3
.

22
is nearly tight: consider the line graph of the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
; one easily shows that
L(K_{n,n})
has degree
2(n-1)
and has domatic number
n
, thus we obtain
\dom(L(K_{n,n}))\leq\delta/2+1
.

5.2
Improved bounds via cover decompositions

The notion of cover decomposition extends naturally to hypergraphs. This was considered by Bollobás et al. in
[
2
]
, where they establish an asymptotically tight lower bound for the size of a cover decomposition in a hypergraph of given rank, as a function of its minimum degree. Note that this result does not require the hypergraph to be simple or loopless (hyperedges of size
1
are allowed).

Theorem 23

(Bollobás, Pritchard, Rothvoß, Scott, 2013)
.

Let
H
be a rank-
k
hypergraph. Then
H
has a cover decomposition of size
\frac{\delta(H)}{\log k+\mathcal{O}(\log\log k)}
.

Using the same strategy as in
22
, we deduce the following bound on the domatic number of line graphs of rank-
k
hypergraphs, improving
20
by a factor
(1+o(1))\frac{k}{\log k}
. The following is a restatement of
5
.

Theorem 24
.

Let
G
be the line graph of some rank-
k
(multi)hypergraph, and
\delta\coloneqq\delta(G)
. Then

\dom(G)\geq\frac{\delta}{k(\log k+O(\log\log k))}.

Proof.

Let
H
be a hypergraph such that
L(H)=G
. Let
V^{+}\subseteq V(H)
be the vertices of
H
of degree at least
\lceil\delta/k\rceil
; observe that
V^{+}
is a vertex cover of
H
. Consider the hypergraph
H^{\prime}
obtained from
H
by adding
\delta
copies of
\{v\}
as an edge for each vertex of
v\in V(H)\setminus V^{+}
. By construction,
H^{\prime}
has minimum degree at least
\lceil\delta/k\rceil
, so we can apply
23
to obtain a cover decomposition
E^{\prime}_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup E^{\prime}_{r}
of
H^{\prime}
, where
r\geq\lceil\delta/k\rceil/(\log k+O(\log\log k))
. For
i\in[r]
, let
E_{i}\coloneqq E^{\prime}_{i}\cap E(H)
; then
E_{i}
covers
V^{+}
, since no vertex in
V^{+}
is incident to an edge in
E(H^{\prime})\setminus E(H)
, by construction. We conclude that the decomposition
E_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup E_{r}
is a domatic partition of
L(H)=G
.
∎

Remark 4
.

24
is tight up to a
(1+o(1))\log k
factor; the complete
k
-partite
k
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with all parts of size
n
has edge-degree
\delta_{e}\geq k(n-1)^{k-1}
and has edge domination number at least

.

6
Cographs

The class of
cographs
corresponds to the class of
P_{4}
-free graphs. Every cograph can be constructed inductively by taking either the disjoint union or the complete join of two smaller cographs, the base case being a single vertex. As a consequence, a cograph can be represented by a
cotree
, that is a binary tree whose internal nodes are labelled either
\wedge
to represent a complete join of the cographs represented by their children, or
\vee
to represent the disjoint union of the cographs represented by their children. The leaves of the cotree are the vertices of the cograph it represents.

We begin with a tight bound for
\tdom
of cographs, with equality when
G
is a clique of odd order.

Theorem 25
.

Let
G
be a cograph with minimum degree
\delta\geq 1
.
We have

\tdom(G)\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{2}.

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on
G
.
When
G
is a single vertex, we have
\delta(G)=0
and
\tdom(G)=0
, so the result holds.
If
G
is not connected, let
C
be a connected component with the smallest value of
\tdom(G[C])
. One has

\tdom(G)=\tdom(G[C])\geq\frac{\delta(G[C])}{2}\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{2},

as desired.
Finally, if
G=G_{0}\wedge G_{1}
with
n_{0}\coloneqq|V(G_{0})|\geq n_{1}\coloneqq|V(G_{1})|
, then

\delta(G)=\min\{\delta(G_{0})+n_{1},\delta(G_{1})+n_{0}\}.

For all
i\in\{0,1\}
, every total dominating set of
G_{i}
is also a total dominating set of
G
, so we have
\tdom(G)\geq\tdom(G_{0})+\tdom(G_{1})
. If we have
\tdom(G_{0})=n_{0}/2
, then by the induction hypothesis we obtain

\tdom(G)\geq\frac{n_{0}}{2}+\frac{\delta(G_{1})}{2}\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{2}.

We now assume that
\tdom(G_{0})<n_{0}/2
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a partition of
V(G_{0})
into
\tdom(G_{0})
total dominating sets; every part of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
has size at least
2
(since total dominating sets have size at least
2
), and since
|\mathcal{P}_{0}|<n_{0}/2
at least one part has size at least
3
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a subset of minimum size such that
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}|Q|\geq n_{1}
. We have
|\mathcal{Q}|\leq\left\lceil(n_{1}-1)/2\right\rceil\leq n_{1}/2
. Let
M
be a matching between
V(G_{1})
and
\bigcup\mathcal{Q}
that saturates
V(G_{1})
. Every
\{u,v\}\in M
is a total dominating set of
G
, so
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq(\mathcal{P}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{Q})\cup M
is a partition of
V(G)
into total dominating sets, of size

|\mathcal{P}|\geq\tdom(G_{0})-|\mathcal{Q}|+n_{1}\geq\frac{\delta(G_{0})}{2}+\frac{n_{1}}{2}\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{2}.\qed

We slightly adapt the above proof to derive a tight bound for the domatic number of cographs. We first need to reduce the universal vertices from a given graph
G
(a vertex
v\in V(G)
is
universal in
G
if
N_{G}[v]=V(G)
).

We begin with the following straightforward observation.

Observation 1
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
v\in V(G)
. Then

\dom(G)\leq 1+\dom(G\setminus v).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a domatic partition of
G
of size
\dom(G)
, and let
D\in\mathcal{P}
be the part containing
v
. Remove
D
from the partition. Then assign each vertex of
D\setminus\{v\}
arbitrarily to one of the remaining parts.
This is a domatic partition of
G\setminus v
, hence
\dom(G\setminus v)\geq\dom(G)-1
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 26
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of universal vertices of
G
.
Then
\dom(G)=|\mathcal{S}|+\dom(G\setminus\mathcal{S})
.

Proof.

We have the upper bound
\dom(G)\leq|\mathcal{S}|+\dom(G\setminus\mathcal{S})
by repeating
|\mathcal{S}|
times
1
. Let us now prove the lower bound.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a partition of
G\setminus\mathcal{S}
into
\dom(G\setminus\mathcal{S})
dominating sets of
G\setminus\mathcal{S}
, and let
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\mathcal{P}_{0}\cup\left\{\{s\}:s\in\mathcal{S}\right\}
.
This is a domatic partition of
G
, hence
\dom(G)\geq\dom(G\setminus\mathcal{S})+|\mathcal{S}|
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 27
.

For every cograph
G
with minimum degree
\delta
,

\dom(G)\geq 1+\frac{\delta}{2}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the number of vertices of
G
.
When
G
has a single vertex, we have
\delta(G)=0
and
\dom(G)=1
, so the result holds.
If
G
is not connected, let
C
be its connected component with the smallest value of
\dom(G[C])
. One has

\dom(G)=\dom(G[C])\geq\frac{\delta(G[C])}{2}+1\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{2}+1,

as desired.
If
G
has at least one universal vertex, let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of universal vertices of
G
. By
Lemma

26
, we have

\dom(G)=|\mathcal{S}|+\dom(G\setminus\mathcal{S}).

If
G\setminus\mathcal{S}
is empty, then
G
is complete, and the result is immediate. Otherwise, by the induction hypothesis applied to
G\setminus\mathcal{S}
, we obtain

\dom(G)\geq|\mathcal{S}|+1+\frac{\delta(G\setminus\mathcal{S})}{2}.

Since
G\setminus\mathcal{S}
is nonempty, we have

\delta(G)=|\mathcal{S}|+\delta(G\setminus\mathcal{S}).

Therefore,

\dom(G)\geq|\mathcal{S}|+1+\frac{\delta(G)-|\mathcal{S}|}{2}=1+\frac{\delta(G)}{2}+\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{2}\geq 1+\frac{\delta(G)}{2}.

We now assume that
G
has no universal vertex, and that
G=G_{0}\wedge G_{1}
with
n_{0}\coloneqq|V(G_{0})|\geq n_{1}\coloneqq|V(G_{1})|
. We have

\delta(G)=\min\{\delta(G_{0})+n_{1},\delta(G_{1})+n_{0}\}.

For all
i\in\{0,1\}
, every dominating set of
G_{i}
is also a dominating set of
G
, so we have
\dom(G)\geq\dom(G_{0})+\dom(G_{1})
. If we have
\dom(G_{0})=n_{0}/2
, then by the induction hypothesis we obtain

\dom(G)\geq\frac{n_{0}}{2}+\frac{\delta(G_{1})}{2}+1\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{2}+1.

We now assume that
\dom(G_{0})<n_{0}/2
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a partition of
V(G_{0})
into
\dom(G_{0})
dominating sets; every part of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
has size at least
2
(since
G
and therefore also
G_{0}
has no universal vertex), and since
|\mathcal{P}_{0}|<n_{0}/2
at least one part has size at least
3
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a subset of minimum size such that
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}|Q|\geq n_{1}
. We have
|\mathcal{Q}|\leq\left\lceil(n_{1}-1)/2\right\rceil\leq n_{1}/2
. Let
M
be a matching between
V(G_{1})
and
\bigcup\mathcal{Q}
that saturates
V(G_{1})
. Every
\{u,v\}\in M
is a dominating set of
G
, so
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq(\mathcal{P}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{Q})\cup M
is a partition of
V(G)
into dominating sets, of size

|\mathcal{P}|\geq\dom(G_{0})-|\mathcal{Q}|+n_{1}\geq\frac{\delta(G_{0})}{2}+1+\frac{n_{1}}{2}\geq\frac{\delta(G)}{2}+1.\qed

Observe that the bound given by
27
is tight, as equality holds when
G
is a complete multipartite graph with all parts of size
2
.

7
Forbidding stars

In this section, we denote by
N^{2}_{G}[u]
the set of vertices at distance at most 2 from a vertex
u
in a graph
G
. We omit
G
when it is clear from context.

We show the existence of dominating colourings of graphs
G
with some well-chosen structural properties through an analysis of the uniformly random
k
-colouring of
G
. This strategy has been successfully applied in
[
6
]
to obtain the following lower bound, which in particular demonstrates that the class of regular graphs is DOM-bounded.

Theorem 28

(Feige, Halldórsson, Kortsarz, Srinivasan, 2002)
.

For every graph
G
,

\dom(G)\geq(1+o(1))\frac{\delta(G)}{\log\Delta(G)}.

Using a similar approach, relying on increasingly technical applications of the LLL, we show that unit disk graphs and, more generally, star-free graphs are DOM-bounded.

Theorem 30
.

For every unit disk graph
G
of minimum degree
\delta\geq e^{44}
,

\dom(G)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{2\log\delta}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
k\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{2\log{\delta}}\right\rfloor
.
For each vertex
v\in V(G)
, we draw a colour
\bm{\sigma}(v)\in[k]
independently and uniformly at random.
For each colour
i\in[k]
and vertex
u\in V(G)
, we let
B_{i,u}
be the random bad event that there is no vertex of colour
i
in
N[u]
.
We show with an application of
9
that, with non-zero probability, no bad event occurs, in which case the realisation of
\bm{\sigma}
is a dominating
k
-colouring of
G
.
Let
u\in V(G)
be a fixed vertex, and
i\in[k]
a fixed colour.
We have

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{i,u}\right]=\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\deg(u)+1}\leq e^{-\frac{\deg(u)+1}{k}}<1.

(3)

Observe that the bad event
B_{i,u}
is entirely determined by the random choices of colours of the vertices in
N[u]
. Hence, for any vertex
v\in V(G)
at distance at least
3
from
u
and every colour
j\in[k]
, since
N[u]
and
N[v]
are disjoint, the bad events
B_{i,u}
and
B_{j,v}
are independent.
Therefore, in the dependency graph
\Gamma
of the bad events
(B_{i,u})_{i\in[k],u\in V(G)}
, we have
N_{\Gamma}(B_{i,u})\subseteq\left\{B_{j,v}:j\in[k],v\in N_{G}^{2}[u]\right\}
.
We observe that, in the embedding of
G
in the plane,
N^{2}[u]
is contained in the disk of radius
2
centred in
u
. By
Lemma

29
, this disk can be partitioned into
22
parts of diameter at most
1
, so

can be partitioned into
22

— since the set of vertices included in a region of diameter at most

induces a clique in a unit-disk graph.
This lets us decompose the sum in the left-hand side of condition (
1
) from
9
as follows:

\sum_{j\in[k],w\in N^{2}[v]}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{j,w}\right]\leq k\sum_{i=1}^{22}\sum_{w\in C_{i}}e^{-\frac{\deg(w)+1}{k}}

We now bound the contribution of each clique
C_{i}
in that sum separately.
Let
t_{i}\coloneqq|C_{i}|
; either
t_{i}\leq\delta
, and

\sum_{w\in C_{i}}e^{-\frac{\deg(w)+1}{k}}\leq\delta e^{-\frac{\delta+1}{k}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta};

otherwise
t_{i}\geq\delta
, and

\sum_{w\in C_{i}}e^{-\frac{\deg(w)+1}{k}}\leq t_{i}e^{-\frac{t_{i}}{k}}\leq\delta e^{-\frac{\delta}{k}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta},

where we have used that
\deg(w)\geq t_{i}-1
since

is a clique, and that the function
x\mapsto xe^{-\frac{x}{k}}
is non-increasing when

.

We conclude that

\sum_{j\in[k],w\in N^{2}[v]}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{j,w}\right]\leq\frac{22k}{\delta}\leq\frac{11}{\log\delta}\leq\frac{1}{4},

which lets us apply
9
and thus end the proof.

for

Theorem 31
.

Let
0<c,p\leq 1
be real constants and
K\geq 1
an integer.
Let
G
be a graph of minimum degree
\delta\geq 4^{e/9+K/p}
.
If, for every vertex
u\in V(G)
,

|\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)|\geq c\cdot\deg(u)
, then

\tdom(G)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{c\delta}{3\log\delta}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
k\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{c\delta}{3\log{\delta}}\right\rfloor
. By the assumptions, we have
\delta\geq 4
, and we may assume that
c\delta\geq 3
, otherwise
k=0
and there is nothing to prove. If
k=1
, then colouring every vertex with the unique colour gives a total-dominating
1
-colouring, since
|\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)|\geq 1
for every
u\in V(G)
. Hence, we may assume that
k\geq 2
.
For each vertex
u\in V(G)
, let us draw a colour
\bm{\sigma}(u)\in[k]
independently and uniformly at random.
For each colour
i\in[k]
and vertex
u\in V(G)
, we let
B_{i,u}
be the random bad event that there is no vertex of colour
i
in
\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)
, and we let
\Gamma
be the dependency graph of those bad events.
Note that
|\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)|\geq c\cdot\deg(u)\geq c\delta\geq 3
by assumption.
We show with an application of
10
that, with non-zero probability, no bad event occurs, in which case the realisation of
\bm{\sigma}
is a total-dominating
k
-colouring of
G
.

Let
u\in V(G)
be a fixed vertex, and
i\in[k]
a fixed colour.
We have

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{i,u}\right]=\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\left\lvert\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)\right\rvert}\leq e^{-\frac{c\cdot\deg(u)}{k}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{3}}.

(4)

Observe that the bad event
B_{i,u}
is entirely determined by the random choices of colours of the vertices in
\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)
. For every
u^{\prime}\notin\bigcup_{v\in\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)}N_{G}(v)
, the sets
\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)
and
\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u^{\prime})
are disjoint, so for every colour
j\in[k]
, the bad events
B_{i,u}
and
B_{j,u^{\prime}}
are independent.
Therefore, in the dependency graph
\Gamma
, we have

N_{\Gamma}(B_{i,u})\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in[k],v\in\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)}\left\{B_{j,w}:w\in N_{G}(v)\right\}.

Although the right-hand side may contain
B_{i,u}
itself, this only enlarges the set over which we sum.
By definition, for each
v\in\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)
,
N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(u)
can be decomposed into
K
parts
X_{1}(v),\ldots,X_{K}(v)
such that
\deg_{G}(w)+1\geq p|X_{\iota}(v)|
for every
\iota\in[K]
and
w\in X_{\iota}(v)
.

Let
\alpha\coloneqq 1-\frac{1}{3\log\delta}<1
. By (
4
), we have

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{i,u}\right]^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{3\alpha}}=\frac{e}{\delta^{3}}<\frac{1}{2}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{B_{j,w}\in N_{\Gamma}(B_{i,u})}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{j,w}\right]^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in[k]}\left(\sum_{w\in N(u)}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{j,w}\right]^{\alpha}+\sum_{v\in\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)}\sum_{\iota\in[K]}\sum_{w\in X_{\iota}(v)}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{j,w}\right]^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq k\left(\frac{\deg(u)}{\delta^{3\alpha}}+\sum_{v\in\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)}\sum_{\iota\in[K]}\sum_{w\in X_{\iota}(v)}e^{-\frac{\alpha c\cdot\deg(w)}{k}}\right).

Let
j\in[k]
,
v\in\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)
, and
\iota\in[K]
be fixed. Let us bound the contribution of
X_{\iota}(v)
in the above sum. We write
t\coloneqq|X_{\iota}(v)|
; either
pt\leq\delta+1
, and

\sum_{w\in X_{\iota}(v)}e^{-\frac{\alpha c\cdot\deg(w)}{k}}\leq\frac{\delta+1}{p}e^{-\frac{\alpha c\delta}{k}}\leq\frac{\delta+1}{p\delta^{3\alpha}}<\frac{3}{p\delta^{2}},

where we have used that
e\,\frac{\delta+1}{\delta}<3
when
\delta\geq 10
;
otherwise
pt\geq\delta+1
. Since
\deg(w)+1\geq pt
for every
w\in X_{\iota}(v)
, we have

\deg(w)\geq pt-1\geq\frac{pt\delta}{\delta+1}.

Hence, setting
\beta\coloneqq\frac{\alpha cp\delta}{k(\delta+1)}
, we get

\sum_{w\in X_{\iota}(v)}e^{-\frac{\alpha c\cdot\deg(w)}{k}}\leq te^{-\beta t}.

The function
x\mapsto xe^{-\beta x}
is non-increasing on
[1/\beta,\infty)
. Moreover,

t\geq\frac{\delta+1}{p}\geq\frac{k(\delta+1)}{\alpha cp\delta}=\frac{1}{\beta},

where we used
k\leq\alpha c\delta
. Therefore,

te^{-\beta t}\leq\frac{\delta+1}{p}e^{-\frac{\alpha c\delta}{k}}\leq\frac{\delta+1}{p\delta^{3\alpha}}<\frac{3}{p\delta^{2}}.

We conclude that

\sum_{B_{j,w}\in N_{\Gamma}(B_{i,u})}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{j,w}\right]^{\alpha}\leq k\left(\frac{e\deg(u)}{\delta^{3}}+\frac{3\left\lvert\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)\right\rvert K}{p\delta^{2}}\right).

Moreover, since
k\geq 2
, using the standard inequality
-\log(1-x)\geq x
for
x\in(0,1)
with
x=1/k
, we obtain

\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left[B_{i,u}\right]}=\left\lvert\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)\right\rvert\log\frac{k}{k-1}\geq\frac{\left\lvert\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)\right\rvert}{k}.

Therefore,

(1-\alpha)\log_{4}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\left[B_{i,u}\right]}\geq\frac{1}{3\log 4\cdot\log\delta}\cdot\frac{\left\lvert\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(u)\right\rvert}{k}.

So (
2
) is satisfied as long as

\frac{k^{2}\log\delta\log 4}{\delta^{2}}\left(\frac{3e}{c\delta}+\frac{9K}{p}\right)\leq 1.

Using the assumption
c\delta\geq 3
, this holds when
\delta\geq 4^{e/9+K/p}
. We may therefore apply
10
and thus end the proof.
∎

We now show that star-free graphs satisfy the setting of
31
. We first rely on the following structural result.

Lemma 32
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer, let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-free graph, and let
X
be the set of vertices of degree more than
\frac{r-2}{r-1}\,n
in

. Then
G[X]
is
K_{r-1}
-free.

Proof.

If
G
is
K_{r-1}
-free, the result is immediate.
Otherwise, let
W
be any copy of
K_{r-1}
in
G
. We have
\bigcap_{w\in W}N(w)=\emptyset
since
G
is
K_{r}
-free. Hence

\sum_{w\in W}\deg(w)\leq(r-2)n.

By the Pigeonhole Principle, we infer that
W\not\subseteq X
, and since
W
was chosen arbitrarily, we conclude that
G[X]
is
K_{r-1}
-free, as desired.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain the following.

Corollary 33
.

Given an integer
r\geq 2
, let
p\coloneqq\frac{1}{r}
and
K\coloneqq r
. Then, for every induced-
K_{1,r+1}
-free graph
G
, every vertex
v\in V(G)
verifies
\widetilde{N}_{p,K}(v)=N(v)
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V(G)
, and let
u\in N(v)
. Since
G
is induced-
K_{1,r+1}
-free, we have
\alpha(G[N(u)])\leq r
. Hence, for the induced subgraph

H\coloneqq G[N(u)\setminus N(v)],

we also have
\alpha(H)\leq r
.

We shall use the following claim.

Claim 33.1
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex graph with bounded independence number
\alpha(H)\leq r
.
Then
V(H)
can be decomposed into
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
such that, for every
i\in[r]
and
v\in X_{i}
,

\deg_{H}(v)\geq\frac{|X_{i}|}{r}-1.

Proof of claim.
Let
n\coloneqq|V(H)|
.
If
\delta(H)\geq\frac{n}{r}-1
, the result trivially holds by taking
X_{1}=V(H)
and
X_{2}=\ldots=X_{r}=\emptyset
. So we assume that there is a vertex
x_{0}
with
\deg_{H}(x_{0})<\frac{n}{r}-1
.
Let
X\coloneqq\left\{x\in V(H):\deg_{H}(x)<\frac{n}{r}-1\right\}
; in particular
x_{0}\in X
so
X\neq\emptyset
.
Since
\alpha(H)\leq r
, the complement
\overline{H}
is
K_{r+1}
-free. Moreover, by the definition of
X
, a vertex
x\in X
has degree more than
\frac{r-1}{r}n
in
\overline{H}
. Therefore, by
Lemma

32
applied to
\overline{H}
with parameter
r+1
, the graph
\overline{H}[X]
is
K_{r}
-free, i.e.
\alpha(H[X])\leq r-1
.

We now proceed by induction on
r
.
In the base case
r=2
, we have
\alpha(H[X])\leq 1
, so
X
is a clique. Moreover, for every
v\in V(H)\setminus X
, by the definition of
X
, we have

\deg_{H}(v)\geq\frac{n}{2}-1\geq\frac{|V(H)\setminus X|}{2}-1.

Thus
X,V(H)\setminus X
is the desired decomposition.

In the general case, by the induction hypothesis applied to
H[X]
, one can decompose
X
into
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r-1}
such that, for every
i\in[r-1]
and
v\in X_{i}
,

\deg_{H[X]}(v)\geq\frac{|X_{i}|}{r-1}-1.

In particular,

\deg_{H}(v)\geq\deg_{H[X]}(v)\geq\frac{|X_{i}|}{r-1}-1\geq\frac{|X_{i}|}{r}-1.

Let
X_{r}\coloneqq V(H)\setminus X
. For every
v\in X_{r}

\deg_{H}(v)\geq\frac{n}{r}-1\geq\frac{|X_{r}|}{r}-1.

Thus
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
is the desired decomposition of

.

\lozenge

Applying the claim to
H=G[N(u)\setminus N(v)]
, we obtain a decomposition

N(u)\setminus N(v)=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{r}

such that, for every

and every

\deg_{H}(w)\geq\frac{|X_{i}|}{r}-1.

Since
\deg_{G}(w)\geq\deg_{H}(w)
, this gives

\deg_{G}(w)+1\geq\frac{|X_{i}|}{r}.

Thus

admits the required decomposition with

.

Fix

Open problems

8.1
Unit disk graphs

In
Section

7
, we first considered the class of unit disk graphs to illustrate a simple application of the Lovász Local Lemma to yield a quasilinear lower bound on the domatic number depending only on the minimum degree, before we moved on to the more abstract setting of
(p,K)
-constrained neighbourhoods we needed to prove DOM-boundedness for the more general classes of induced-star-free graphs. While we proved a quasilinear upper bound for the domatic number of induced-star-free graphs (cf.
16
), we have no reason to believe that our quasilinear lower bound for unit disk graphs (cf.
30
) has the right order of magnitude.

Problem 1
.

Is there a linear DOM-binding function for the class of unit disk graphs?

More generally, we believe DOM-boundedness may be interesting to study in other geometric intersection graphs, such as intersection graphs of balls or boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d
-directional segment graphs, string graphs, …

8.2
Beyond cographs

Another characterisation of cographs corresponds to the class of graphs of cliquewidth at most
2
. One could consider dominating colourings of graphs of bounded cliquewidth to try and extend the present results.

Problem 2
.

Given a fixed integer
t\geq 3
, is the class of graphs of clique-width at most
t
DOM-bounded?
The special case of distance-hereditary graphs (that have clique-width
\leq 3
) can be considered first.

8.3
Forbidden induced subgraphs

As a partial progress towards solving
3
, one could try and solve its restriction to split graphs.
Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the class of split graphs, and
S_{s,t}
the double star with
s+t
leaves.

Problem 3
.

Is
\forb(S_{r,r})\cap\mathcal{S}
DOM-bounded?

8.4
Line graphs

We showed in
22
that if
G
is the line graph of a simple graph, then
\dom(G)\geq\frac{\delta}{2}-1
. We then remarked that this lower bound was nearly tight, as there exist line graphs
G
such that
\dom(G)\leq\frac{\delta}{2}+1
.

We note that the notion of
edge-domatic partitions
(i.e. domatic partition in the line graph) was considered by Zelinka
[
18
]
. In particular, it was claimed that every graph
G
has edge-domatic number at least
\delta(G)
; since
L(G)
has minimum degree at most
\delta(L(G))\leq 2\delta(G)-2
, this would imply the tight lower bound
\dom(L(G))\geq\delta(G)\geq\frac{\delta(L(G))}{2}+1
. However, the proof provided in
[
18
, Theorem 1]
is based on a flawed argument; roughly speaking, the author repeatedly extracts a maximal independent set of edges, ensuring that the minimum degree decreases by at most
1
at each step, but fails to ensure that edges extracted at a given step are dominated by every subsequent colour class. We therefore pose Zelinka’s claimed bound as a problem.

Problem 4
.

Is it true that every graph
G
verifies
\dom(L(G))\geq\delta(G)
?
