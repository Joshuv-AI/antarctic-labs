On the Graphical r r -Stirling Numbers of the First Kind for Specific Graph Families
Daniel Yaqubi ∗, Madjid Mirzavaziri

Abstract
. This paper investigates the graphical r r -Stirling numbers of the first kind , denoted by [ G k ] r \left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{G}{k}\right]_{r} , which enumerate partitions of a vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) into k k disjoint cycles such that r r specified vertices occupy distinct blocks. We establish closed-form expressions and recursive identities for fundamental graph families, including Path ( P n P_{n} ), Cycle ( C n C_{n} ), Star ( S n S_{n} ), Wheel ( W n W_{n} ), and Fan ( F n F_{n} ) graphs. A primary focus of this study is the statistical characterization of the cycle distribution. We derive explicit formulas for the mean and variance of these numbers, extracted from the structural properties of the r r -cycle polynomials. These results provide a rigorous measure of the average cycle density and variability across different graph topologies, bridging the gap between algebraic combinatorics and the structural analysis of restricted permutations. Keywords: Graphical r r -Stirling numbers, r r -cycle polynomials, universal vertices, mean, variance, combinatorial moments.

1.
Introduction

The Stirling numbers of the first kind
{n\brack k}
are fundamental objects in
enumerative combinatorics, counting permutations of an
n
-element set with
exactly
k
disjoint cycles. These numbers play a central role in the study of
permutation statistics, algebraic combinatorics, and probability theory

[
10
,
11
]
. Classical results of Goncharov and Feller
established deep connections between Stirling numbers, generating polynomials,
and asymptotic normality, leading to a Central Limit Theorem for the number of
cycles in random permutations.

An important refinement was introduced by Broder
[
12
]
, who defined
the
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind

{n\brack k}_{r}
by imposing the
restriction that the first
r
elements must lie in distinct cycles. These
restricted Stirling numbers interpolate between classical permutations and more
constrained cycle structures, and they have been studied extensively in
connection with restricted permutations, algebraic identities, and probabilistic
limit laws.

Motivated by the interaction between graph structure and cycle decompositions,
Barghi and DeFord
[
13
]
introduced the
graphical Stirling
numbers of the first kind

{G\brack k}
for an arbitrary graph
G
. These
numbers enumerate partitions of the vertex set
V(G)
into
k
disjoint cycles
supported by the edges of
G
, allowing both
1
-cycles and
2
-cycles. When

G
is the complete graph
K_{n}
, the numbers
{G\brack k}
reduce to the
classical Stirling numbers of the first kind. The sum
\sum_{k}{G\brack k}
,
called the
graphical factorial
of
G
, counts all cycle covers of
G
.
Although cycle cover enumeration is computationally difficult in general,
explicit formulas and structural results can be obtained for sparse or highly
structured graph families.

In this paper, we introduce and study the
graphical
r
-Stirling numbers of
the first kind
, denoted
{G\brack k}_{r}
. These numbers count partitions of

V(G)
into
k
disjoint cycle blocks subject to the additional constraint that

r
specified vertices lie in distinct cycles. This definition simultaneously
generalizes Broder’s
r
-Stirling numbers and the graphical Stirling numbers of
the first kind, providing a unified framework for studying restricted cycle
decompositions in graphs and modeling combinatorial situations in which
designated vertices must participate in closed paths.

Our first objective is to extend enumerative results for
{G\brack k}
to this

r
-restricted setting. We derive explicit formulas, recurrence relations, and

r
-cycle polynomials for several fundamental families of graphs, including
paths (
P_{n}
), cycles (
C_{n}
), stars (
S_{n}
), wheels (
W_{n}
), and fan graphs
(
F_{n}
).

Beyond exact enumeration, we investigate the
algebraic properties
of
graphical
r
-Stirling numbers. Building on Harper’s seminal work on the
real-rootedness and log-concavity of Stirling polynomials
[
14
]
, as
well as subsequent developments by Liu, Yang, and Zhang

[
15
,
16
]
, we study unimodality, log-concavity, and
real-rootedness of the associated
r
-cycle polynomials for various graph
families. These algebraic properties provide strong structural insight and play
a crucial role in probabilistic analysis.

From a probabilistic perspective, classical results of Goncharov

[
10
]
and Feller
[
11
]
established a Central Limit
Theorem for the number of cycles in permutations. More recently, asymptotic
normality and related limit laws have been studied for generalized Stirling
numbers and graph-based combinatorial statistics

[
17
,
18
]
. In this work, we extend these ideas to the
graphical
r
-Stirling setting. Using analytic properties of generating
polynomials and moment methods, we derive explicit formulas for the mean and
variance of the number of cycles and establish Gaussian limiting behavior for
several families of graphs as the number of vertices tends to infinity.

Finally, we examine asymptotic behavior across growing families of graphs and
analyze how sparsity, degree distribution, and structural constraints influence
both enumerative and probabilistic properties. The paper concludes with several
conjectures and open problems concerning asymptotic normality, real-rootedness,
and algebraic behavior of graphical
r
-Stirling polynomials, suggesting
promising directions for future research in graph enumeration, algebraic
combinatorics, and probabilistic graph theory.

Preliminaries and notation

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph with vertex set
V=[n]
and edge set
E
. A
graphical cycle partition
of
G
into
k
blocks is a partition of the vertex set
\pi(G)=\{C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{k}\}
such that for each block
C_{i}
, one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

|C_{i}|=1
(a
1
-cycle) or
|C_{i}|=2
(a
2
-cycle).

(ii)

If
|C_{i}|>2
, the induced subgraph
G|_{C_{i}}
is Hamiltonian; that is, it is connected and contains at least one cycle that visits each vertex in
C_{i}
exactly once.

In the case of
|C_{i}|>2
, we treat
C_{i}
as a specific oriented Hamiltonian cycle within the induced subgraph. The
graphical Stirling number of the first kind
, denoted by
{G\brack k}
, represents the total number of such partitions of
V
into exactly
k
cycles. This definition generalizes the classical Stirling numbers by restricting the cycle formation to the edge set of
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
. The
graphical cycle polynomial

\mathcal{C}(G,x)
is the generating function for the graphical cycle partition numbers
{G\brack k}

\mathcal{C}(G,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{G\brack k}x^{k}

Remark 2.2
.

Although both the matching polynomial and the graphical cycle polynomial of the
first kind are satisfy linear recurrences on some graphs,
they encode fundamentally different combinatorial structures.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a simple graph. The graphical Stirling numbers
{G\brack k}
satisfy the following recursive and structural identities:

(1)

Isolation Shift:
Let
I\subseteq V(G)
be a set of
m
isolated vertices. For
k\geq m
:

{G\brack k}={G-I\brack k-m}

(2)

Disjoint Union Convolution:
Let
G
be the disjoint union of two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Then:

{G_{1}\cup G_{2}\brack k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}{G_{1}\brack j}{G_{2}\brack k-j}

(3)

The graphical cycle polynomial of their union is given by the product of their individual polynomials:

\mathcal{C}(G_{1}\cup G_{2},x)=\mathcal{C}(G_{1},x)\cdot\mathcal{C}(G_{2},x)

Proof.

(1) Let
I=\{v\in V(G):\deg(v)=0\}
. By definition, every block in a graphical cycle partition must induce a cycle
C_{\ell}
. Since vertices in
I
have no incident edges, they cannot participate in induced cycles of length
\ell\geq 2
. Consequently, each vertex
v\in I
must form a trivial 1-cycle (
C_{1}
). Since these
m
isolated vertices are necessarily partitioned into
m
distinct cycles, the problem reduces to partitioning the remaining vertex set
V(G)\setminus I
into
k-m
induced cycles. This yields the identity
{G-I\brack k-m}
.

(2) Since
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are disjoint, any subset of
V(G_{1}\cup G_{2})
that induces a cycle must be a subset of either
V(G_{1})
or
V(G_{2})
. Thus, a partition of the union into
k
cycles is uniquely determined by the union of a
j
-cycle partition of
G_{1}
and a
(k-j)
-cycle partition of
G_{2}
. Summing over all valid
j
yields the discrete convolution.

(3) By the definition of the graphical cycle polynomial and the convolution property of the cycle partition numbers for disjoint unions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(G_{1}\cup G_{2},x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}{G_{1}\cup G_{2}\brack k}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{j}{G_{1}\brack j}{G_{2}\brack k-j}\right)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j}{G_{1}\brack j}x^{j}\right)\left(\sum_{m}{G_{2}\brack m}x^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}(G_{1},x)\cdot\mathcal{C}(G_{2},x)

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be the disjoint union of components
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{m}
. The graphical Stirling numbers satisfy the multinomial convolution:

{G\brack k}=\sum_{j_{1}+\dots+j_{m}=k}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}{G_{i}\brack j_{i}}\right)

Equivalently, the graphical cycle polynomial
\mathcal{C}(G,x)=\sum_{k}{G\brack k}x^{k}
is multiplicative over disjoint components:

\mathcal{C}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}G_{i},x\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}(G_{i},x)

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a simple graph and
v\notin V(G)
. For any
w\in V(G)
, let
G+_{w}v
be the graph formed by attaching
v
to
w
via a pendant edge. The graphical cycle partition numbers satisfy:

{G+_{w}v\brack k}={G\brack k-1}+{G-w\brack k-1}

Proof.

Consider the vertex
v
in
G+_{w}v
. Since
v
is a pendant vertex with
N(v)=\{w\}
, it can only induce a cycle of length 1 or 2.

•

If
v
induces a
C_{1}
, the remaining vertices
V(G)
must be partitioned into
k-1
cycles. There are
{G\brack k-1}
such ways.

•

If
v
induces a
C_{2}
, it must be the edge
vw
. The remaining vertices
V(G)\setminus\{w\}
must be partitioned into
k-1
cycles. This corresponds to the graph
G-w
, yielding
{G-w\brack k-1}
ways.

Summing these disjoint cases gives the result.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(Kaplansky’s Lemma for Circular Arrangements)
.

The number of ways to select
m
non-adjacent edges from a cycle graph
C_{n}
(equivalent to selecting
m
non-consecutive objects from
n
objects arranged in a circular fashion) is given by:

\frac{n}{n-m}\binom{n-m}{m}

where
0\leq m\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.

Theorem 3.6
.

The graphical cycle polynomial of the path graph
P_{n}
is given by:

\mathcal{C}(P_{n},x)=\sum_{k=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{n}\binom{k}{n-k}x^{k}

where the coefficients
\binom{k}{n-k}
correspond to the number of matchings of size
n-k
in
P_{n}
.

Proof.

In the path graph
P_{n}
, we have only 1-cycles and 2-cycles. Thus, any graphical cycle partition of
V(P_{n})
into
k
subsets must consist solely of
m
induced
C_{2}
subgraphs (edges) and
s
induced
C_{1}
subgraphs (vertices).

The total number of vertices is
2m+s=n
and the total number of cycles is
m+s=k
, which implies
m=n-k
. According to Kaplansky’s Lemma
3.5
, the number of ways to choose
j
items from
m
items in a row such that no two are consecutive is given by:

\begin{bmatrix}P_{n}\\
k\end{bmatrix}=\binom{(n-1)-(n-k)+1}{n-k}=\binom{k}{n-k}

Summing over all possible values of
k
(where
n-k\leq k
implies
k\geq n/2
) yields the result.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

The graphical cycle polynomial of the cycle graph
C_{n}
for
n\geq 3
is given by:

\mathcal{C}(C_{n},x)=2x+\sum_{k=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{C_n}{k}x^{k}

where the coefficients
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{C_n}{k}=\frac{n}{k}\binom{k}{n-k}
for
k\geq 2
correspond to the number of matchings of size
n-k
in
C_{n}
.

Proof.

By definition, the graphical cycle polynomial
\mathcal{C}(G,x)
enumerates the partitions of the vertex set
V(G)
into
k
disjoint cycles, where a partition into
k
cycles contributes to the coefficient of
x^{k}
. For the cycle graph
G=C_{n}
, the admissible cycle lengths in a vertex partition are strictly constrained by the graph’s topology.

I. The Case
k=1
(Hamiltonian Cycle):

For
k=1
, the vertex set must be covered by a single cycle of length
n
. While
C_{n}
contains exactly one such subgraph, we account for the two possible orientations (clockwise and counter-clockwise) of the Hamiltonian cycle, consistent with the treatment of cycles in directed contexts (analogous to Stirling numbers of the first kind). This yields the term:

2x^{1}=2x.

II. The Case
k\geq 2
.

For
k\geq 2
, the partition cannot include the Hamiltonian cycle. Furthermore, for
n\geq 3
, the cycle graph
C_{n}
does not contain any vertex-disjoint cycles of length
3\leq\ell<n
. Consequently, any partition of
V(C_{n})
into
k
components must consist exclusively of:

•

m
cycles of length 2 (representing a matching of
m
disjoint edges),

•

s
cycles of length 1 (representing isolated vertices).

To determine the number of components
k
, we consider the following system:

(1)

\displaystyle 2m+s

\displaystyle=n\quad\text{(Vertex constraint)}

(2)

\displaystyle m+s

\displaystyle=k\quad\text{(Component constraint)}

Solving this system yields
m=n-k
. Thus, a vertex partition into
k
components is bijectively related to a matching of size
n-k
in
C_{n}
.

By Kaplansky’s Lemma
3.5
and substituting
m=n-k
we have

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{C_n}{k}=\frac{n}{n-(n-k)}\binom{n-(n-k)}{n-k}=\frac{n}{k}\binom{k}{n-k}

The summation limits are derived from the matching constraint
0\leq m\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. Substituting
m=n-k
leads to
n-\lfloor n/2\rfloor\leq k\leq n
, which simplifies to
\lceil n/2\rceil\leq k\leq n
. Summing over these indices completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(G,1)=\sum_{k}{G\brack k}
denote the total number of graphical cycle partitions of

. The following relations hold:

(1)

For the cycle graph
C_{n}
(

\mathcal{C}(C_{n},1)=L_{n}+1

(2)

For the path graph

\mathcal{C}(P_{n},1)=F_{n+1}

Theorem 3.9
.

For the complete graph
K_{n}
, the graphical cycle partition numbers
{n\brack k}
coincide with the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind
{n\brack k}
. Consequently, the graphical cycle polynomial of
K_{n}
is equivalent to the rising factorial:

\mathcal{C}(K_{n},x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\brack k}x^{k}=x(x+1)(x+2)\dots(x+n-1)

Proof.

Recall that the Stirling number of the first kind counts the number of permutations of
n
elements with
k
disjoint cycles. In the complete graph
K_{n}
, any subset of
m
vertices induces a clique where the number of possible oriented cycles is
(m-1)!
(with the convention that
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
yield 1).

Since every possible partition of the vertex set into
k
subsets can be realized as a collection of
k
cycles in
K_{n}
, the total count is:

{K_{n}\brack k}=\sum_{\{V_{1},\dots,V_{k}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(|V_{i}|-1)!

This summation over all set partitions is the standard formula for the Stirling numbers of the first kind.
∎

Theorem 3.11

(Reduction Theorem)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph and
v\in V(G)
. If
v
is not contained in any induced cycle of length
\ell\geq 3
in
G
, then the graphical Stirling numbers of the first kind satisfy the recurrence:

\begin{bmatrix}G\\
k\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}G-v\\
k-1\end{bmatrix}+\sum_{w\in N(v)}\begin{bmatrix}G-\{v,w\}\\
k-1\end{bmatrix}

In the case where the neighborhood
N(v)
is vertex-transitive within
G
(i.e.,
G-\{v,w_{i}\}\cong G-\{v,w_{j}\}
for all
w_{i},w_{j}\in N(v)
), the recurrence simplifies to:

\begin{bmatrix}G\\
k\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}G-v\\
k-1\end{bmatrix}+\deg(v)\begin{bmatrix}G-\{v,w\}\\
k-1\end{bmatrix}

for any arbitrary neighbor
w\in N(v)
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V(G)
be a vertex that is not contained in any induced cycle of length
\ell\geq 3
. We partition the set of all
k
-cycle partitions of
G
based on the block
B
containing
v
. By the given condition,
B
must induce a Hamiltonian cycle, but since
v
belongs to no induced cycles of length
\ell\geq 3
, the only possibilities are
|B|=1
or
|B|=2
.

•

Case 1 (
|B|=1
):
If

Example 3.22

(Barbell Graph

)

The Barbell graph

is obtained by connecting two disjoint complete graphs

via a bridge

, where

and

are vertices in the respective cliques. By the Bridge Lemma (Lemma

Corollary 3.23
.

The cycle polynomial of the Barbell graph satisfies the recurrence

\mathcal{C}(B_{n},x)=\mathcal{C}(K_{n},x)^{2}+x\,\mathcal{C}(K_{n-1},x)^{2},

and it is real-rooted.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of all cycle partitions of
B_{n}
. Partition
\mathcal{P}
according to the status of the bridge
e=\{u,v\}
:

•

Case 1:
e
is not a block.

The vertices
u
and
v
belong to separate blocks within their respective cliques. The number of such partitions is the product of the partitions of the two independent
K_{n}
graphs:

\mathcal{C}(K_{n},x)\cdot\mathcal{C}(K_{n},x)=\mathcal{C}(K_{n},x)^{2}.

•

Case 2:
e
forms a block.

The bridge forms an independent
K_{2}
block. The remaining
n-1
vertices in each clique are partitioned independently, contributing

x\cdot\mathcal{C}(K_{n-1},x)\cdot\mathcal{C}(K_{n-1},x)=x\,\mathcal{C}(K_{n-1},x)^{2},

where the factor
x
accounts for the new block.

Combining the two cases gives the structural recurrence:

\mathcal{C}(B_{n},x)=\mathcal{C}(K_{n},x)^{2}+x\,\mathcal{C}(K_{n-1},x)^{2}.

Recall that
\mathcal{C}(K_{n},x)=x^{\overline{n}}
, the rising factorial. Using
x^{\overline{n}}=(x+n-1)x^{\overline{n-1}}
, we factor
(x^{\overline{n-1}})^{2}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(B_{n},x)

\displaystyle=\bigl((x+n-1)x^{\overline{n-1}}\bigr)^{2}+x\,(x^{\overline{n-1}})^{2}

\displaystyle=(x^{\overline{n-1}})^{2}\bigl((x+n-1)^{2}+x\bigr)

\displaystyle=(x^{\overline{n-1}})^{2}\left[x^{2}+(2n-1)x+(n-1)^{2}\right].

The quadratic factor has discriminant

\Delta=(2n-1)^{2}-4(n-1)^{2}=4n-3>0,

which ensures that all roots are real. Since
x^{\overline{n-1}}
is also real-rooted, it follows that
\mathcal{C}(B_{n},x)
is real-rooted.
∎

4.
Graphical
r
-Stirling Numbers of the First Kind

Definition 4.1

(Graphical
r
-Stirling Number)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph of order
n
. For
1\leq r\leq k\leq n
, the graphical
r
-Stirling number of the first kind, denoted by
{G\brack k}_{r}
, is defined as the number of partitions of
V(G)
into
k
blocks
\{C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{k}\}
such that:

(1)

Each induced subgraph
G[C_{i}]
is a
1
-cycle, a
2
-cycle, or a Hamiltonian subgraph of order
|C_{i}|\geq 3
.

(2)

The first
r
labeled vertices
\{1,2,\dots,r\}
are contained in distinct blocks.

Definition 4.2

(Graphical
r
-Cycle Polynomial)
.

Let
G
be a simple graph of order
n
. For a non-negative integer
r
such that
0\leq r\leq n
, we define the
graphical
r
-cycle polynomial of the first kind
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{r}(G,x)
, as the generating function:

\mathcal{C}_{r}(G,x)=\sum_{k=r}^{n}{G\brack k}_{r}x^{k},

where
{G\brack k}_{r}
denotes the graphical
r
-Stirling number of the first kind. In the case where
r=1
(or
r=0
), we simply write
\mathcal{C}(G,x)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
R\subseteq V(K_{n})
be a set of
r

1\leq r\leq n
. The following properties hold for the complete graph
K_{n}
:

(i)

Classical Correspondence:
The
r
-restricted graphical Stirling numbers of the first kind coincide exactly with the classical
r
-Stirling numbers:

\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{K_n}{k}\right]_{r}=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{r}.

(ii)

Generating Function:
The
r
-restricted graphical cycle polynomial of
K_{n}
is given by the restricted rising factorial:

\mathcal{C}_{r}(K_{n},x)=\sum_{k=r}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{r}x^{k}=x^{r}\prod_{i=r}^{n-1}(x+i).

(iii)

Recurrence Relation:
For
n>r
, the graphical Stirling numbers satisfy:

\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{K_n}{k}\right]_{r}=\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{K_{n-1}}{k-1}\right]_{r}+(n-1)\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{K_{n-1}}{k}\right]_{r}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
for parts (i) and (iii).

Base case:
For
n=r
, the
r
-restriction requires each vertex in
R
to belong to a distinct cycle. Since there are only
r
vertices total, each must form a 1-cycle. Thus,
\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{K_r}{r}\right]_{r}=1
, which matches the classical boundary condition
\begin{bmatrix}r\\
r\end{bmatrix}_{r}=1
.

Inductive step:
Assume the result holds for
n-1
. Consider
K_{n}
and an unrestricted vertex
v\in V(K_{n})\setminus R
. In any
r
-restricted cycle partition into
k
cycles, vertex
v
has two options:

(1)

v
forms its own 1-cycle (
C_{1}
). The remaining
n-1
vertices are partitioned into
k-1
cycles satisfying
r
restrictions. This gives
\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{K_{n-1}}{k-1}\right]_{r}
ways.

(2)

v
is part of a cycle of length
\ell\geq 2
. In
K_{n}
,
v
can be inserted into any of the
n-1
existing edge-slots of a valid cycle partition of
K_{n-1}
to form a larger cycle. This gives
(n-1)\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{K_{n-1}}{k}\right]_{r}
ways.

Summing these cases yields the recurrence in (iii). Since this recurrence and the boundary conditions are identical to the classical
r
-Stirling numbers, the identity in (i) is proven. Part (ii) follows immediately as the generating function for said coefficients.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

The total number of
r
-restricted cycle partitions of
K_{n}
, denoted

, is given by:

B_{r}(K_{n})=\frac{n!}{r!}.

Proof.

The total number of partitions is found by evaluating the cycle polynomial at

:

B_{r}(K_{n})=\mathcal{C}_{r}(K_{n},1)=1^{r}\prod_{i=r}^{n-1}(1+i)=(r+1)(r+2)\cdots(n)=\frac{n!}{r!}.

∎

The

\binom{K}{N-K}
. Thus, there are
\binom{k-r}{n-r-(k-r)}=\binom{k-r}{n-k}
such partitions.

•

Case II:

C=\{r,r+1\}
is a
2
-cycle. We must then partition the remaining
n-(r+1)
vertices into
k-r
remaining blocks. This results in
\binom{k-r}{n-r-1-(k-r)}=\binom{k-r}{n-k-1}
partitions.

Summing these cases and applying Pascal’s identity, we obtain:

{P_{n}\brack k}_{r}=\binom{k-r}{n-k}+\binom{k-r}{n-k-1}=\binom{k-r+1}{n-k}.

To compute the total number of partitions, we sum over all possible values of
k
:

\sum_{k=r}^{n}{P_{n}\brack k}_{r}=\sum_{k=r}^{n}\binom{k-r+1}{n-k}.

By performing the change of variable
j=k-r
, the sum becomes
\sum_{j=0}^{n-r}\binom{j+1}{n-r-j}
. Using the well-known identity for the sum along the shallow diagonals of Pascal’s triangle,
\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\binom{m-i}{i}=F_{m+1}
, we find:

B_{r}(P_{n})=\sum_{k=r}^{n}{P_{n}\brack k}_{r}=F_{n-r+1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

The
r
-restricted graphical cycle polynomial for the path graph
P_{n}
is defined by:

C_{r}(P_{n},x)=x^{r-1}C(P_{n-r+1},x).

By expanding the sum, the polynomial can be expressed as:

C_{r}(P_{n},x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-r+1}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n-r+1-j}{j}x^{n-j}.

Furthermore, for

, the polynomial satisfies the following recurrence relation:

C_{r}(P_{n},x)=xC_{r}(P_{n-1},x)+xC_{r}(P_{n-2},x),

with the initial conditions

and
C_{r}(P_{r+1},x)=x^{r+1}+x^{r}
.

Proof.

Substituting the result
{P_{n}\brack k}_{r}=\binom{k-r+1}{n-k}
into the definition of the graphical
r
-cycle polynomial yields:

\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n},x)=\sum_{k=r}^{n}{P_{n}\brack k}_{r}x^{k}=\sum_{k=r}^{n}\binom{k-r+1}{n-k}x^{k}.

Applying the change of variable
j=k-r
, such that
k=j+r
, we shift the summation index:

\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n},x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-r}\binom{j+1}{n-r-j}x^{j+r}=x^{r}\sum_{j=0}^{n-r}\binom{j+1}{n-r-j}x^{j}.

To verify the recurrence for
n\geq r+2
, we consider the terminal vertex
v_{n}
. In any
r
-graphical partition,
v_{n}
belongs to a block
C
. Since
P_{n}
is a path,
C
is either a
1
-cycle
\{v_{n}\}
or a
2
-cycle
\{v_{n-1},v_{n}\}
.

•

If
C=\{v_{n}\}
, the remaining vertices form a valid
r
-graphical partition of
P_{n-1}
. This contributes
x\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n-1},x)
.

•

If
C=\{v_{n-1},v_{n}\}
, the remaining vertices form a valid
r
-graphical partition of
P_{n-2}
. Since
n-1>r
, the
r
-restriction is not violated. This contributes
x\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n-2},x)
.

The summation of these cases confirms the recurrence. The base cases follow directly: for
P_{r}
, only
r
singletons are allowed (
x^{r}
); for
P_{r+1}
, we may have
r+1
singletons (
x^{r+1}
) or the edge
\{r,r+1\}
with
r-1
singletons (
x^{r}
).
∎

The complement of the path graph,

Theorem 4.7
.

Let

Consider an

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n}^{c},x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{P_{n-1}^c}{k-1}\right]_{r}+(n-2)\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{P_{n-1}^c}{k}\right]_{r}\right)x^{k}

Corollary 4.8
.

The graphical
r
-cycle polynomial of the first kind for

is given by:

\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n}^{c},x)=x^{r}\prod_{i=r-1}^{n-3}(x+i).

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
for a fixed restriction parameter
r
.
Let
n=r
. For a graph where all vertices are restricted, each vertex must occupy a distinct 1-cycle. Thus,
\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{r}^{c},x)=x^{r}
. Applying the formula:

x^{r}\prod_{i=r-1}^{r-2}(x+i)=x^{r}\cdot(1)=x^{r}.

The base case holds for the empty product.

Assume the identity holds for

\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n-1}^{c},x)=x^{r}\prod_{i=r-1}^{n-3}(x+i).

By the recurrence relation established in Theorem
4.7
, the polynomial satisfies the functional equation:

\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n}^{c},x)=(x+n-2)\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n-1}^{c},x).

Substituting the inductive hypothesis:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n}^{c},x)

\displaystyle=(x+n-2)\left[x^{r}\prod_{i=r-1}^{n-3}(x+i)\right]

\displaystyle=x^{r}\left[\prod_{i=r-1}^{n-3}(x+i)\right]\cdot(x+n-2).

By extending the product to the upper limit

, we obtain:

\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n}^{c},x)=x^{r}\prod_{i=r-1}^{n-2}(x+i).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

It is instructive to compare this result with the polynomial for the complete graph

•

\mathcal{C}_{r}(K_{n},x)=x^{r}(x+r)(x+r+1)\dots(x+n-1)

•

\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n}^{c},x)=x^{r}(x+r-1)(x+r)\dots(x+n-2)

The removal of the path edges

from

effectively shifts every linear factor in the restricted rising factorial down by 1. This algebraic shift reflects the loss of exactly one cycle-insertion site at each vertex-addition step.

Definition 5.1

(Mean and Variance of Graphical Stirling Distributions)
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
X
be a random variable representing the number of blocks in a partition chosen uniformly at random from the set of all
r
-graphical cycle partitions of
G

E_{r}[X]
and variance

are defined as:

(3)

E_{r}[X]=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{r}^{\prime}(G,1)}{\mathcal{C}_{r}(G,1)}=\frac{\sum_{k=r}^{n}k\begin{bmatrix}G\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{r}}{\sum_{k=r}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}G\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{r}}

(4)

\text{Var}_{r}(X)=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{r}^{\prime\prime}(G,1)}{\mathcal{C}_{r}(G,1)}+E[X]-(E[X])^{2}

where
\mathcal{C}_{r}^{\prime}(G,1)
and
\mathcal{C}_{r}^{\prime\prime}(G,1)

.

Remark 5.2

(Extremal Behavior and Structural Entropy)
.

The mean

achieves its supremum in the null graph

(

) and decreases as edge density increases. For forest graphs like

, these moments relate to the Golden Ratio

Proposition 5.3
.

For a Hamiltonian graph
G
, the existence of a spanning cycle (
\begin{bmatrix}G\\
1\end{bmatrix}\geq 1
) reduces the mean

and generally increases
\text{Var}(X)
by extending the distribution’s support to the lower bound of the index
k
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
m=n-r
be the number of unrestricted vertices in the path graph
P_{n}
. The mean
E_{r}(P_{n})
and variance
\text{Var}_{r}(P_{n})
for the number of blocks in an graphical
r
-cycle of the first kind of

are given by:

E_{r}(P_{n})=r+\frac{mL_{m+1}-F_{m+1}}{5F_{m+1}}

\text{Var}_{r}(P_{n})=\frac{5m(m+1)F_{m+1}^{2}-(mL_{m+1}-F_{m+1})^{2}-5(mL_{m+1}-F_{m+1})F_{m+1}}{25F_{m+1}^{2}}

where
F_{k}
and

are the

-th Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, respectively.

Proof.

The graphical
r
-cycle polynomial of the first kind factors as
\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n},x)=x^{r}\mathcal{C}(P_{m},x)
. Moments are derived using the derivatives of the path cycle polynomial
\mathcal{C}(P_{m},x)
at
x=1
, noting that
\mathcal{C}(P_{m},1)=F_{m+1}
. Utilizing the closed-form identities for Fibonacci-type polynomials:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(P_{m},1)

\displaystyle=\frac{mL_{m+1}-F_{m+1}}{5}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(P_{m},1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(5m^{2}-3m-2)F_{m+1}-m(m-1)L_{m+1}}{25}

The expectation is defined by
r+\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(1)}{\mathcal{C}(1)}
. The variance is computed via the identity
\text{Var}(X)=\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{\mathcal{C}(1)}+\mu-\mu^{2}
. Substituting the Lucas-Fibonacci identities and simplifying the resulting expressions algebraically leads to the stated exact formulas.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

As
n\to\infty
, the exact moments of the path graph
P_{n}
converge to linear approximations governed by the golden ratio
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
:

E_{r}(P_{n})\approx\frac{n-r}{\phi+2}+r\approx 0.276(n-r)+r,\quad\text{Var}_{r}(P_{n})\approx\frac{n-r}{5\sqrt{5}}\approx 0.089(n-r)

Proof.

The result follows from the asymptotic properties of the Fibonacci and Lucas sequences, where
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{L_{m}}{F_{m}}=\sqrt{5}
. Alternatively, for large
m=n-r
, the cycle polynomial is dominated by the growth rate
\lambda(x)=\frac{x+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}
.
Applying the logarithmic derivative operators to the asymptotic form
\lambda(x)^{m}
:

E[X]\approx r+m\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(1)}{\lambda(1)},\quad\text{Var}(X)\approx m\left[\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime}(1)}{\lambda(1)}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(1)}{\lambda(1)}-\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(1)}{\lambda(1)}\right)^{2}\right]

Substituting the evaluated derivatives
\lambda(1)=\phi
,
\lambda^{\prime}(1)=\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{5}}
, and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}(1)=\frac{2}{5\sqrt{5}}
yields the constant coefficients
1/\sqrt{5}
and
1/5\sqrt{5}
respectively. Since
\text{Var}_{r}(P_{n})
diverges linearly with

, the distribution of cycle counts is asymptotically normal.
∎

Similar to path graphs, cycle graphs exhibit linear scaling but involve Lucas numbers due to the periodic boundary condition.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let

be the number of unrestricted vertices in the cycle graph

for

and variance

For

The mean is

Corollary 5.7
.

As

The convergence stems from the asymptotic ratio of the underlying sequences:

. Applying this to the exact mean directly yields the result. Furthermore, as

In contrast to the linear scaling of sparse graphs, complete graphs

exhibit logarithmic scaling, mirroring the classical Stirling numbers of the first kind.

Theorem 5.8
.

For the complete graph
K_{n}
, the expected number of blocks
E[X]
and variance

are:

(5)

E_{r}[X]=H_{n-1}-H_{r-1}+r,\quad Var_{r}(X)=\sum_{i=r}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{i}-\frac{1}{i^{2}}\right)

where

is the

Proof.

The
r
-Stirling cycle polynomial for

is given by the shifted rising factorial:

\mathcal{C}_{r}(K_{n},x)=x(x+1)\dots(x+r-1)\frac{\Gamma(x+n)}{\Gamma(x+r)}

Taking the logarithm and differentiating with respect to

\frac{d}{dx}\ln\mathcal{C}_{r}(K_{n},x)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{1}{x+i}+\psi(x+n)-\psi(x+r)

At

.

E_{r}[X]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{i}+(H_{n-1}-H_{r-1})=(H_{r}-H_{r-1})+H_{n-1}+(r-1)\dots

Simplifying the terms related to the

\psi^{\prime}(x)
.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

As

, the expectation and variance grow logarithmically:

E_{r}(K_{n})\approx\ln(n-r)+\gamma+r,\quad\text{Var}_{r}(K_{n})\approx\ln(n-r)+\gamma-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}

is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

Proof.

Using the asymptotic expansion
H_{m}=\ln m+\gamma+O(1/m)
and the convergence

, we substitute

into the exact moments. As the variance
\sigma_{n}^{2}\to\infty
as

∎

5.1.
Dense Graph Theorem

Theorem 5.10

(Dense Graph Asymptotics)
.

Let
\{G_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a family of graphs with
|V(G_{n})|=n
such that

\delta(G_{n})\geq n-C

for some fixed constant
C
. Fix
r\geq 0
. Then:

(1)

The mean and variance of
X_{G_{n},r}
satisfy

\mathbb{E}[X_{G_{n},r}]=\log n+O(1),\qquad\mathrm{Var}(X_{G_{n},r})=\log n+O(1).

(2)

The
r
-cycle polynomial
P_{G_{n},r}(x)
is real-rooted for all sufficiently
large
n
.

(3)

The normalized random variable

\frac{X_{G_{n},r}-\mathbb{E}[X_{G_{n},r}]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(X_{G_{n},r})}}

converges in distribution to the standard normal law.

Proof.

We compare
G_{n}
with the complete graph
K_{n}
. Since

\delta(G_{n})\geq n-C
, the graph
G_{n}
can be obtained from
K_{n}
by deleting at
most
Cn/2
edges.

For
K_{n}
, the
r
-cycle polynomial satisfies the exact recurrence

P_{K_{n+1},r}(x)=(x+n-r)\,P_{K_{n},r}(x),

which yields

\mathbb{E}[X_{K_{n},r}]\sim\log n,\qquad\mathrm{Var}(X_{K_{n},r})\sim\log n

by classical results of Goncharov and Feller.

For
G_{n}
, the insertion of a new vertex contributes

P_{G_{n+1},r}(x)=(x+n-r)\,P_{G_{n},r}(x)+E_{n}(x),

where
E_{n}(x)
is a polynomial whose coefficients are uniformly
bounded in
n
, reflecting the bounded number of forbidden adjacencies.

Taking logarithmic derivatives at
x=1
shows that the contribution of
E_{n}(x)

to both the first and second moments is
O(1)
. Hence,

\mathbb{E}[X_{G_{n},r}]=\log n+O(1),\qquad\mathrm{Var}(X_{G_{n},r}]=\log n+O(1).

The real-rootedness of
P_{K_{n},r}(x)
is classical. Since
P_{G_{n},r}(x)
differs
from
P_{K_{n},r}(x)
by a bounded perturbation in each step, real-rootedness is
preserved for all sufficiently large
n
; see Harper and subsequent refinements
by Liu, Yang, and Zhang. Real-rootedness implies log-concavity and, together with
diverging variance, yields asymptotic normality via Harper’s theorem.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let

Theorem 5.17

(Mean and Variance for

Proof.

Consider the graphical

\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n}^{c},x)=(x+n-2)\mathcal{C}_{r}(P_{n-1}^{c},x)

\displaystyle E_{r}(P_{n}^{c})

6.
Further Work and Open Problems

In this paper, we derived exact expressions and analyzed statistical properties of the graphical Stirling numbers of the first kind for several families of graphs, including star graphs, double stars, lollipop graphs, Tadpole graphs, and Barbell graphs. Using techniques such as bridge decomposition and generating polynomials, we obtained explicit graphical cycle polynomials, closed-form expressions in terms of Fibonacci and Lucas polynomials, and calculated statistical moments such as the mean and variance. These results provide a foundation for studying asymptotic distributions and combinatorial properties of
\begin{bmatrix}G\\
k\end{bmatrix}

We propose several conjectures and open problems to guide further research:
