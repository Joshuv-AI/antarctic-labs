Abstract
. We introduce the concept of generalized reducibility , which provides a flexible framework for analyzing the long-time behavior of solutions to quadratic quantum Hamiltonians. As an application of this notion, for many prescribed sub-exponential growth rates f ⁡ ( t ) f(t) , either monotone or oscillatory, we explicitly construct time-decaying perturbations of the one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator such that the Sobolev norms of solutions grow at the rate f ⁡ ( t ) f(t) .

1.1.
Generalized reducibility

For a specific quantum Hamiltonian
H(t,Z)
with constant coefficients, there exists a straightforward approach to analyzing the propagators of Eq. (
1
). This includes, for instance, Mehler’s formula for the
n-
dimensional QHO
{\mathcal{T}}
defined in (
5
). The qualitative properties of the solutions can then be investigated through direct computations.

Consequently, to efficiently describe the behavior of solutions to the time-dependent equation, it is practical to remove the time-dependence from the original equation via an appropriate transformation. This process is referred to as
reducibility
.
A considerable amount of prior research has been devoted to the reducibility of
PDEs with time-dependent coefficients; see, for example,
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
10
]
and the references therein.
Moreover, the study of the growth of Sobolev norms (defined in Section
1.2
) of solutions is closely connected to the reducibility of the underlying quantum Hamiltonian, particularly in the linear setting.

In the works mentioned above, reducibility is typically achieved through an
L^{2}-
unitary transformation that is
bounded on Sobolev spaces
(defined in Section
1.2
), and the growth of Sobolev norms is subsequently inferred by conjugating the equation to one with constant coefficients.
We propose a different perspective. Instead of requiring the conjugating transformation to be bounded on Sobolev spaces, we allow an
L^{2}-
unitary transformation that is not necessarily Sobolev-bounded. This transformation conjugates the original equation to a constant-coefficient equation whose solutions have uniformly bounded Sobolev norms, more precisely, to the constant-coefficient equation

\frac{1}{{\rm i}}\partial_{t}\psi={\mathcal{T}}\psi\quad{\rm with}\ {\mathcal{T}}\ {\rm defined\ in}\ (\ref{nD-QHO}).

The growth of the Sobolev norms for solutions of the original equation is then recovered through a detailed analysis of the mapping properties of the conjugating transformation itself.
In contrast to reducibility in the classical sense, such an
L^{2}-
unitary conjugation can always be achieved. We refer to this notion as
generalized reducibility
.
To distinguish it from the previously studied notion of reducibility, we refer to reducibility in the previous sense as
normal reducibility
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Generalized reducibility)

The quadratic quantum Hamiltonian (
1
)
is reducible in the generalized sense, i.e., there exists an
L^{2}-
unitary transformation

\psi(t)={\mathcal{U}}(t)\varphi(t),

such that the quantum Hamiltonian (
1
)
is conjugated to the constant-coefficient equation

\frac{1}{{\rm i}}\partial_{t}\varphi={\mathcal{T}}\varphi.

A more detailed description in the Sobolev setting, together with a comparison between normal reducibility and generalized reducibility, will be presented in Section
1.2
.

1.2.
Growth of Sobolev norms

With the generalized reducibility obtained in Theorem
1.1
, we are able to describe the growth of
Sobolev norms
for quadratic quantum Hamiltonian, especially for the quantum Hamiltonians that are irreducible in the normal sense.

The
Sobolev space
(also referred to as
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}-
space
for a given
s\geq 0
) is defined as

(6)

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{s}{2}}u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right\},

with
{\mathcal{T}}
being the QHO defined as in (
5
), and equipped with the
Sobolev norm
(also known as
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}-
norm
)

(7)

\|u\|_{s}:=\left(\|{\mathcal{T}}^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Several equivalent forms of the
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}-
norm will be given in Section
2
.

As an important issue in mathematical physics, the behavior of solutions to Hamiltonian PDEs in Sobolev spaces was first proposed by Bourgain
[
12
]
and subsequently developed through a series of pioneering works (see, e.g.,
[
14
,
18
,
20
,
22
]
). This topic has attracted sustained interest over the past decades.

Beyond the boundedness of Sobolev norms and the existence of “weak” upper bounds (such as
t^{\varepsilon}
or logarithmic) on their growth (see
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
]
and the references therein), a more challenging problem is to establish the existence of unbounded trajectories in Sobolev spaces. This question is naturally related to weak turbulence phenomena and energy cascade mechanisms, as well as to the precise determination of the time growth rates of Sobolev norms. Over the past decades, substantial progress has been made in this direction for a wide class of Hamiltonian PDEs.

As mentioned in the previous subsection, it is effective to study the long-time behavior of solutions through normal reducibility of the original quantum Hamiltonian, especially for the linear Hamiltonians.

•

For a one-dimensional (1D) QHO with a time-periodic linear potential, Bambusi–Grébert–Maspero–Robert
[
5
]
proved polynomial growth of order
t^{s}
for the
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}-
norms by reducing the system to a transport equation.

•

For 1D QHO with time quasi-periodic perturbations, which are quadratic polynomials of
(D,X)
, various growth rates of
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}-
norms have been observed depending on the normal forms of reducibility
[
28
,
30
]
:
t^{s}
polynomial growth for parabolic normal form, exponential growth for hyperbolic normal form, and
t^{2s}
polynomial growth when reducible to the Stark Hamiltonian.

•

More generally, in the
n
-dimensional setting, a complete classification of Sobolev norm growth can be obtained under the assumption of reducibility (see Theorem 1.2 of
[
27
]
). In particular, if the quantum Hamiltonian (
1
) is reducible in the sense of Theorem 1.2 of
[
27
]
, then the long-time growth behavior of
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
-norms can be fully characterized according to the associated normal form.

•

Beyond exact reducibility, the notion of
almost reducibility
also plays a significant role in the study of Sobolev norm growth. In this framework, one typically observes oscillatory growth phenomena, reflecting the presence of persistent but non-removable time dependence; see
[
29
]
.

Another approach commonly used to obtain the unbounded trajectories is to construct specific perturbations towards the growth to infinity.
For 1D QHO, Delort
[
15
]
(followed by a refined proof of Maspero
[
32
]
) constructed time periodic order-zero pseudo-differential operators as the perturbation such that some solutions exhibit
t^{\frac{s}{2}}-
polynomial growth of
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{s}-
norms, and in an abstract setting, Maspero
[
33
,
34
]
exploited further time periodic perturbations and gave sufficient conditions such that some solution exhibits such polynomial growth, using the Mourre estimate. This strategy has also been applied by Maspero
and collaborators to the 1D fractional quasilinear Schrödinger equation
[
35
]
and to completely resonant QHO on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

[
26
]
.

For
2-
dimensional QHO, Faou-Raphaël
[
16
]
constructed a time-decaying perturbation such that, after a suitable “dilation transformation”, the Sobolev norms of some solutions to the transformed equation remains bounded in time.
However, the corresponding solutions of the original system exhibit logarithmic growth in Sobolev norms. We remark that the underlying philosophy is similar to that of generalized reducibility, even though the setting and methods are different.

Based on the study in
[
36
]
for linear Lowest Landau Level equations with a time dependent potential, Thomann
[
37
]
constructed the perturbation given by the projection onto Bargmann-Fock space such that some travelling wave whose Sobolev norm presents polynomial growth with time.
In other settings, the logarithmic growth of Sobolev norms was also shown by Bourgain
[
13
]
for 1D and 2D linear Schrödinger equations with quasi-periodic potential, and by Haus-Maspero
[
23
]
for semiclassical anharmonic oscillators with regular time dependent potentials.

1.2.1.
Classical-quantum correspondence

For the quadratic quantum Hamiltonian

(8)

\frac{1}{{\rm i}}\partial_{t}\psi=({\mathcal{Q}}_{{\mathcal{A}}(t)}(Z)+{\mathcal{L}}_{\ell(t)}(Z))\psi,

with
{\mathcal{A}}(t)\in C_{b}^{0}({\mathbb{R}},\mathrm{sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}}))
and
\ell(\cdot)\in C_{b}^{0}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
, the long-time behavior of its solutions is closely related to the classical affine system

(9)

\dot{z}={\mathcal{A}}(t)z+\ell(t).

According to
[
27
, Theorem 1.4]
, under the reducibility assumption on the quantum Hamiltonian (
8
), the growth of
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}-
norms for the solutions to Eq. (
8
) is determined by the solution to the affine system (
9
). With generalized reducibility stated in Theorem
1.1
, such a classical-quantum correspondence
can be generalized by removing the reducibility assumption.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
s\geq 0
and
t_{0}>0
, consider Eq. (
8
) with initial condition
\psi(t_{0})\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\setminus\{0\}
. There is a constant
C>1
, depending on
\psi(t_{0})
and
z_{*}(t_{0})
, such that
the corresponding solution
\psi(t)
of Eq. (
8
) satisfies

(10)

C^{-1}\leq\frac{\|\psi(t)\|_{s}}{\|z_{*}(t)\|^{s}+\|W(t)\|^{s}}\leq C,\qquad t\geq t_{0},

where
z_{*}(t)
is a particular solution of the affine system (
9
), and

W(t)
is the fundamental solution matrix of the linear system
\dot{z}={\mathcal{A}}(t)z
satisfying

W(t_{0})={\mathbb{I}}_{2n}
.
In particular, if
\ell(\cdot)=0
in (
9
), then

(11)

C^{-1}\leq\frac{\|\psi(t)\|_{s}}{\|W(t)\|^{s}}\leq C,\qquad t\geq t_{0}.

Since Eq. (
8
) is not reducible in general, one cannot compute the growth of Sobolev norms by passing to a normal form. In particular, the lack of reducibility prevents us from conjugating the equation to a time-independent Hamiltonian for which the norm growth can be read off directly.
Generalized reducibility overcomes this difficulty by allowing conjugations that are unitary on
L^{2}
but not necessarily bounded on
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
, thereby still making it possible to determine the growth of Sobolev norms.

The main idea in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is precisely to exploit this generalized conjugation framework in order to transfer the growth mechanism from the transformed equation back to the original one.

Moreover, generalized reducibility differs essentially from normal reducibility in the
diversity of possible growth behaviors
of Sobolev norms.
In
[

1.2.2.
Construction for prescribed growth rate

According to Theorem
1.2
, since the growth of Sobolev norms for quadratic quantum Hamiltonians can be read off from the corresponding classical linear system, it is possible to construct quantum Hamiltonians whose solutions exhibit prescribed growth rates in Sobolev spaces.

Given some
t_{0}>0
, let
{\mathcal{M}}([t_{0},\infty[)
be the class of
C^{2}
non-decreasing functions on
[t_{0},\infty[
satisfying
\displaystyle\inf_{t\geq t_{0}}f(t)>0
, and

f(t)\to\infty,\quad\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)(t)\to 0\ {\rm monotonously},\quad t\to\infty.

It is easy to verify that, for suitable
t_{0}
,
{\mathcal{M}}([t_{0},\infty[)
includes a large class of sub-exponential growth rates such as

(12)

\begin{array}[]{lll}e^{\ln(t)^{a}}\ (a>0),&e^{{\rm li}(t)},&e^{t^{\sigma}}\ (0<\sigma<1),\\[8.53581pt]
t,&\frac{t}{\ln(t)},&\underbrace{\ln\ln\cdots\ln}_{k\ {\rm times}}(t)\ (k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}).\end{array}

Moreover, if
f\in{\mathcal{M}}([t_{0},\infty[)
, then
f^{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{M}}([t_{0},\infty[)
for any

.

.

For

such that the following holds for any given

.

(1)

For the 1D homogeneous quadratic Hamiltonian PDE

(14)

(2)

2.1.
Schrödinger representation

Let
{\bf H_{n}}
be the real
(2n+1)-
dimensional
Heisenberg group
, which is
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+1}
equipped with the group law

(p,q,t)(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\left(p+p^{\prime},q+q^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(\langle p,q^{\prime}\rangle-\langle q,p^{\prime}\rangle)\right),

where
p,q,p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
t,t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The map
\rho
, from
{\bf H_{n}}
to the group of unitary operators on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, is defined as

\rho(p,q,t)=e^{{\rm i}(\langle p,D\rangle+\langle q,X\rangle+t\mathrm{Id})}=e^{{\rm i}t}e^{{\rm i}(\langle p,D\rangle+\langle q,X\rangle)},\quad(p,q,t)\in{\bf H_{n}},

that is, for
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

(21)

\displaystyle\left(\rho(p,q,t)u\right)(x)=e^{{\rm i}t+{\rm i}\langle q,x\rangle+\frac{\rm i}{2}\langle p,q\rangle}u(x+p),

\rho
then becomes an irreducible unitary representation of
{\bf H_{n}}
on the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, known as the
Schrödinger representation
. Since the variable
t
always acts in a straightforward manner, it is often convenient to omit it entirely.

The
Schrödinger representation

\rho
is defined from
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
to
{\mathcal{B}}(L^{2})
. For
p,q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, it can be explicitly formulated as

(22)

\left(\rho(v)u\right)(x)=e^{{\rm i}\langle q,x\rangle+\frac{\rm i}{2}\langle p,q\rangle}u(x+p),\quad v=\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix},\quad u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

It is easy to verify that
\rho(v)
is
L^{2}-
unitary and belongs to
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}^{s})
for any
s\geq 0
. Moreover, for
p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, a straightforward computation shows that

(23)

\rho\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}\\
q_{1}\end{pmatrix}\rho\begin{pmatrix}p_{2}\\
q_{2}\end{pmatrix}=e^{\frac{\rm i}{2}(\langle p_{1},q_{2}\rangle-\langle q_{1},p_{2}\rangle)}\rho\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}+p_{2}\\
q_{1}+q_{2}\end{pmatrix},

which implies

\rho(v)^{-1}=\rho(-v)
for any
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
.

2.2.
Metaplectic representation

The
symplectic group
, denoted by
\mathrm{Sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
, comprises
2n\times 2n
real matrices that preserve the symplectic form. Specifically, a matrix
{\mathbb{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&F\end{pmatrix}
belongs to
\mathrm{Sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
, with
A
,
B
,
C
, and
F
being its four
n\times n
blocks, if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

A^{*}C=C^{*}A,\quad B^{*}F=F^{*}B,\quad A^{*}F-C^{*}B={\mathbb{I}}_{n}.

Such a matrix
{\mathbb{A}}\in\mathrm{Sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is referred to as a
symplectic matrix
.

The
symplectic Lie algebra
,
\mathrm{sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
, consists of all matrices
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\rm gl}(2n,{\mathbb{R}})
such that
e^{t{\mathcal{A}}}\in\mathrm{Sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In other words,
{\mathcal{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&F\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
, with
A
,
B
,
C
, and
F
as the four
n\times n
blocks of
{\mathcal{A}}
, if and only if the following hold:

F=-A^{*},\quad B=B^{*},\quad C=C^{*}.

A matrix
{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathrm{sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is known as a
Hamiltonian matrix
.

The
Metaplectic representation

{\mathcal{M}}
of
\mathrm{Sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is defined as follows. Given
{\mathbb{A}}\in\mathrm{Sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}})
, it induces an automorphism
T_{{\mathbb{A}}}
of the Heisenberg group
{\bf H_{n}}
by

T_{{\mathbb{A}}}(p,q,t)=\left({\mathbb{A}}\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix},t\right),\qquad p,q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since the Schrödinger representation
\rho
, defined in (
21
), is an irreducible unitary representation of
{\bf H_{n}}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, the composite
\rho\circ T_{{\mathbb{A}}}
is also an irreducible unitary representation of
{\bf H_{n}}
, satisfying

By the Stone-von Neumann theorem, the two irreducible unitary representations

Therefore,

modulo
{\pm\,\mathrm{Id}}
and is referred to as the

of

. In explicit formulations, the ambiguity of
\pm 1

4.2.

To show Theorem
1.5
, let us first define the following class of functions.
For given
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, let

be the class of

functions

on
[t_{0},\infty[
with derivatives satisfying

and

G^{(j)}(t)\to 0\ {\rm as}\ t\to\infty,\quad 0\leq j\leq N-1.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
G\in{\mathcal{A}}_{N}([t_{0},\infty[)
with some
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, we have

\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\left\{\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos(2s)G(s)\,ds\right|,\ \left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\sin(2s)G(s)\,ds\right|\right\}<\infty.

Proof.

By integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos(2s)G(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\sin(2s)G(s)\right]_{s=t_{0}}^{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\sin(2s)G^{\prime}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\cos\left(2s-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)G(s)\right]_{s=t_{0}}^{t}

\displaystyle-\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos\left(2s-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)G^{\prime}(s)\,ds.

Let us show by recurrence that, for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos(2s)G(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2^{l}}\left[\cos\left(2s-\frac{l\pi}{2}\right)G^{(l-1)}(s)\right]_{s=t_{0}}^{t}

\displaystyle+\,\frac{(-1)^{j}}{2^{j}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos\left(2s-\frac{j\pi}{2}\right)G^{(j)}(s)\,ds.

Assume that the above equality holds for
j=k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
.
Through integration by parts,

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{k}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos\left(2s-\frac{k\pi}{2}\right)G^{(k)}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{k+1}}\left[\cos\left(2s-\frac{(k+1)\pi}{2}\right)G^{(k)}(s)\right]_{s=t_{0}}^{t}

\displaystyle+\,\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{2^{k+1}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos\left(2s-\frac{(k+1)\pi}{2}\right)G^{(k+1)}(s)\,ds,

then we have that it also holds for
j=k+1
, and hence for every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
with
j\leq N
.
In particular, for
j=N
,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos(2s)G(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2^{l}}\left[\cos\left(2s-\frac{l\pi}{2}\right)G^{(l-1)}(s)\right]_{s=t_{0}}^{t}

\displaystyle+\,\frac{(-1)^{N}}{2^{N}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos\left(2s-\frac{N\pi}{2}\right)G^{(N)}(s)\,ds.

Since
G\in{\mathcal{A}}_{N}([t_{0},\infty[)
,
G^{(N)}\in L^{1}([t_{0},\infty[)
, we have that

\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\cos(2s)G(s)\,ds\right|<\infty.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\sin(2s)G(s)\,ds\right|<\infty
.∎
