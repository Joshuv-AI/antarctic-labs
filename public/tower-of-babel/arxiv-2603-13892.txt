Global Well-Posedness for the Fourth-Order Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation with Potential in the Energy-Critical Case
Hikaru Nakayama

Abstract
We consider the fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equation with potential in the energy-critical case. In this paper, we prove global well-posedness and scattering for radial initial data.



1
Introduction and Main Result

1.1
Introduction

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the following fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta^{2}u+Vu+\lambda|u|^{p-1}u=0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.1)

Here
\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}^{2}
and
\Delta^{2}=\Delta\circ\Delta
. Moreover,
V
is a real-valued potential. Define
2^{\sharp}=2n/(n-4)
. When
p=2^{\sharp}-1
, the equation is called energy-critical, and when
1<p<2^{\sharp}-1
, it is called energy-subcritical. Also, when
\lambda>0
, the equation is called defocusing. Throughout this paper,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the
L^{2}
inner product, and
\langle\cdot\rangle=(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{1/2}
. We say that
u\in C(I,H^{2})
is a solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}=u(0)
if it satisfies the integral equation

u(t)=e^{itH}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)H}|u|^{p-1}u(s)\,ds,

where
H=\Delta^{2}+V
.

Pausader
[
24
]
proved global well-posedness and scattering for radial initial data in the energy-critical case for the fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equation obtained from (
1.1
) by taking
V=0
. In this paper, using the method of
[
24
]
, we obtain an analogous result for the fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equation with potential (
1.1
). In
[
24
]
, Strichartz estimates play an essential role in the proof. Therefore, in the present paper we need Strichartz estimates with potential, which were established in
[
23
]
. However, when a potential is present, the propagator
e^{it(\Delta^{2}+V)}
cannot in general be represented by the Fourier transform. Thus, in order to obtain Strichartz estimates in Sobolev norms, we need the equivalence of Sobolev norms

\|(\Delta^{2}+V)^{s/4}u\|_{L^{p}}\sim\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{p}}.

This is a major difference from the case
V=0
. We discuss this equivalence in detail in Section 3, and by using it we derive Strichartz estimates in Sobolev norms in Section 4. In addition,
[
24
]
uses
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
decay estimates; in order to obtain their counterparts in the presence of a potential, we use the boundedness of wave operators, which is also treated in Section 4. Furthermore, in Section 8 we derive a Morawetz-type estimate with potential, which is needed to prove the existence of global solutions. Then, in Section 9, we prove global existence. Moreover, for nonlinear Schrödinger equations with potential, besides the question of whether a solution
u
asymptotically approaches a linear solution
e^{it(\Delta^{2}+V)}u^{\pm}
, it is also important to ask whether it asymptotically approaches a free linear solution
e^{it\Delta^{2}}u^{\pm}
corresponding to the case
V=0
. This is discussed in Section 10 on scattering.

1.2
Main result

In order to state the main theorems of this paper, we impose the following assumptions.

Assumption 1.1
.

Let
V
be a real-valued potential, and let
\mathcal{F}
denote the Fourier transform. Assume that
n\geq 5
.

1.

|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}
holds for some
\beta>n+3
when
n
is odd, and for some
\beta>n+4
when
n
is even.

2.

When
n=7
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{1+\varepsilon}V(\cdot)\|_{H^{\varepsilon}}<\infty
.

3.

There exist
0<\delta\ll 1
and
\sigma>\frac{2n-8}{n-1-\delta}
such that

\|\mathcal{F}(\langle\cdot\rangle^{\sigma}V(\cdot))\|_{L^{\frac{n-1-\delta}{n-4-\delta}}}<\infty.

4.

H=\Delta^{2}+V
has no positive eigenvalues, and zero energy is regular.

Here, “zero energy is regular” means that there is no nontrivial solution to
H\psi=0
satisfying
\langle x\rangle^{\frac{n}{2}-4-\varepsilon}\psi(x)\in L^{2}
.

Assumption 1.2
.

Let
V
be a
C^{1}
real-valued potential.

1.

x\cdot\nabla V\leq 0
.

2.

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}V(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}\langle x\rangle^{-\beta-|\alpha|}
for
|\alpha|=0,1
and some
\beta\geq 4
.

Assumption 1.3
.

Let
V
be a real-valued potential.

1.

V\geq 0
.

2.

V\in L^{\infty}\cap L^{\frac{n}{4}}
.

3.

There exists a small
\delta_{n}>0
such that
\|V\|_{L^{\frac{n}{4},\infty}}\leq\delta_{n}
.

The following two theorems are the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 5
,
\lambda>0
, and
p\in(1,\,2^{\sharp}-1]
. Let
V\in L^{\infty}\cap L^{\frac{n}{4}}
be a radial real-valued potential satisfying Assumptions
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
. Then, for any radial initial data
u_{0}\in H^{2}
, there exists a unique global solution
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{2})
to (
1.1
) satisfying
u(0)=u_{0}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 5
, and let
V
be a radial real-valued potential satisfying Assumptions
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
. Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{2})
be any radial solution to (
1.1
) with
p=2^{\sharp}-1
and
\lambda>0
. Then there exist radial functions
u^{\pm}\in H^{2}
such that

\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta^{2}}u^{\pm}\|_{H^{2}}\rightarrow 0,\ \ t\rightarrow\pm\infty.

These results extend the result of
[
24
]
, where (
1.1
) was studied in the case
V=0
. Assumption
1.1
is used to derive the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
decay estimates introduced in Section 4, Assumption
1.2
is used to derive the Morawetz-type estimate introduced in Section 8, and Assumption
1.3
is used to establish the equivalence of Sobolev norms

\|H^{s/4}u\|_{L^{p}}\sim\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{p}},

which is discussed in Section 3.

The following gives an example of a potential satisfying the assumptions of the main theorems.

Example 1.6
.

Let
\beta>n+4
and let
0<c\ll 1
be sufficiently small. Define

V(x)=c\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}.

Then it is easy to see that
V
is radial and satisfies Assumptions
1.2
and
1.3
. It is also immediate that Assumption
1.1
(1) and (2) hold. For Assumption
1.1
(3), by the Hausdorff–Young inequality, using a sufficiently small constant
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, we obtain

\|\mathcal{F}\left(\langle x\rangle^{\frac{2n-8}{n-1}-\beta+\varepsilon}\right)\|_{L^{1+\frac{3}{n-4}+\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|\langle x\rangle^{\frac{2n-8}{n-1}-\beta+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{(1+\frac{3}{n-4})^{\prime}-\varepsilon}}<\infty,

so Assumption
1.1
(3) is satisfied. Assumption
1.1
(4) follows from Remark
4.2
.

1.3
Related background

For the second-order nonlinear Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\lambda|u|^{p-1}u=0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \ t\in\mathbb{R},

there are many results in the energy-critical case
p=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
and the defocusing case, namely
\lambda<0
. Historically, Bourgain
[
3
]
first proved global well-posedness for radial solutions when
n=3,4
, and then Tao
[
30
]
generalized Bourgain’s result to arbitrary
n\geq 3
. Furthermore, J. Colliander, M. Keel, G. Staffilani, H. Takaoka, and T. Tao
[
6
]
obtained global well-posedness for general initial data when
n=3
, and finally Ryckman and Visan
[
27
]
,
[
32
]
extended this to
n\geq 4
. Thus, the study of global well-posedness for the usual second-order nonlinear Schrödinger equation has the above background.

The fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equation was introduced by Karpman
[
15
]
and by Karpman and Shagalov
[
16
]
in order to describe fourth-order dispersion effects for intense laser beams propagating through a medium with Kerr nonlinearity. Subsequently, well-posedness for fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equations was studied by Pausader
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
, and by C. Hao, L. Hsiao, and B. Wang
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
, among others. In particular, global well-posedness and scattering in the energy-critical case were investigated by Pausader
[
24
]
,
[
26
]
, and by Miao, Xu, and Zhao
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
. More recently, Miao and Zheng
[
22
]
, as well as Dinh
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
, studied the
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
-critical problem and obtained results on scattering theory. Here,

s_{c}=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{4}{p-1}.

As for previous work on the fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equation with potential, namely the case
V\neq 0
in (
1.1
), Feng, Wang, and Yao
[
12
]
proved global well-posedness and scattering in the energy-subcritical case.

Notations

Let
L^{q}=L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the usual Lebesgue space, and let
L^{r}(I,L^{q})
denote the space of measurable functions from an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
to
L^{q}
such that the norm

\|u\|_{L^{r}(I,L^{q})}=\left(\int_{I}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}}^{r}dt\right)^{1/r}

is finite.

Next we define generalized Sobolev spaces. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, define

H^{s,q}:=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\|u\|_{H^{s,q}}:=\|\langle\Lambda\rangle^{s}u\|_{L^{q}}<\infty\right\},\ \ \Lambda=\sqrt{-\Delta}.

Here
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
denotes the space of tempered distributions. In particular, we write
H^{s,2}=H^{s}
. We also denote by
L^{r,\infty}\ (1\leq r<\infty)
the weak-
L^{r}
space, defined by

L^{r,\infty}=\left\{f:\|f\|_{L^{r,\infty}}=\sup_{\gamma>0}\gamma\left|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};|f(x)|>\gamma\}^{1/\gamma}\right|<\infty\right\}.

For later use, we recall the weak-type Hölder inequality. If
\dfrac{1}{p}=\dfrac{1}{q}+\dfrac{1}{r}
with
1<p,q,r<\infty
, and if
f\in L^{r,\infty}
and
g\in L^{q}
, then

\|fg\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{p,q,r}\|f\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}.

(2.1)

Here
A\lesssim_{u}B
means that there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
u
, such that
A\leq CB
. Next we define two important conserved quantities, the mass
M(u)
and the energy
E(u)
:

M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{2}dx

(2.2)

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(|\Delta u(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{p+1}|u(x)|^{p+1}\right)\,dx

(2.3)

We introduce the norms used in the following arguments:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{M(I)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n-4}}(I,L^{\frac{2n(n+4)}{n^{2}+16}})}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W(I)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n-4}}(I,L^{\frac{2n(n+4)}{n^{2}-2n+8}})}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Z(I)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n-4}}(I,L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n-4}})}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{N(I)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(I,L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}})}.

For
u\in H^{2}
, define the functional
\mathcal{E}
by

\mathcal{E}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta u(x)|^{2}dx.

(2.4)

Let
\mathbb{B}(X,Y)
denote the space of bounded operators from
X
to
Y
, and in particular write
\mathbb{B}(X,X)=\mathbb{B}(X)
. We denote the operator norm by
\|\cdot\|_{X\rightarrow Y}:=\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{B}(X,Y)}
. Also, if
H
is a self-adjoint operator, then
\mathcal{H}_{ac}(H)
denotes the absolutely continuous spectral subspace of
H
, and
P_{ac}(H)
denotes the projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{ac}(H)
. We also write
\sigma(H)
for the spectrum of
H
.

3
Equivalence of Homogeneous Sobolev Norms on
L^{p}

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
q_{0}>2
. Assume that there exist constants
a,b>0
such that

\|Vf\|_{L^{q_{0}}}\leq a\|\Delta^{2}f\|_{L^{q_{0}}}+b\|f\|_{L^{q_{0}}},\ \ f\in H^{4,q_{0}}.

By
[
8
]
, the following
L^{p}
boundedness of the Riesz transform is known.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 5
and set
H=\Delta^{2}+V
. Assume that
V
satisfies Assumption
3.1
for some
2<q_{0}<\frac{n}{2}
. Then there exists
\delta_{q_{0}}>0
such that if

\|V\|_{L^{\frac{n}{4},\infty}}\leq\delta_{q_{0}},

(3.1)

then the Riesz transform
\nabla^{2}H^{-1/2}
is bounded on
L^{q}
for every
\dfrac{nq_{0}^{\prime}}{n+2q_{0}^{\prime}}<q<q_{0}
.

Assumption 3.3
.

Let
V
be a real-valued potential. Assume that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\|Vf\|_{L^{1}}\leq\varepsilon\|\Delta^{2}f\|_{L^{1}}+C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{1}},\ \ f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
H=\Delta^{2}+V
. Assume that
V\in L^{\frac{n}{4}}
and
V\geq 0
. Then the following two statements hold.

•

If
V\in L^{\infty}
and the weak
L^{\frac{n}{4}}
norm of
V
is sufficiently small, then for every
1<p<\dfrac{n}{2}
and
0\leq s\leq 2
,

\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|H^{s/4}f\|_{L^{p}}.

(3.2)

•

Assume that
V
satisfies Assumption
3.3
. Then for every
1<p<\dfrac{n}{s}
and
0\leq s\leq 4
,

\|H^{s/4}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}.

(3.3)

Proof.

First we prove (
3.2
). Since
V\in L^{\infty}
, Assumption
3.1
holds for any
2<q_{0}<\frac{n}{2}
. Therefore, by applying Proposition
3.2
, we obtain for
1<p<\frac{n}{2}
that

\||\nabla|^{2}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|H^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}}.

(3.4)

Hence it remains to prove the case
0<s<2
. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, set

T_{x+iy}:=|\nabla|^{2x+iy}H^{-\frac{x}{2}+iy}.

Then we show the
L^{p}
boundedness of
T_{1+iy}=|\nabla|^{iy}|\nabla|^{2}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}H^{iy}
. By (
3.4
), it suffices to prove the
L^{p}
boundedness of
H^{iy}
. Since
V\in L^{\infty}
by assumption, Assumption
3.3
is satisfied. Hence, by
[
7
]
, the semigroup
e^{-tH}
extends to an analytic semigroup
e^{zH}
on
L^{1}
with angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
, and when
V\geq 0
its kernel satisfies

|e^{-tH}(x,y)|\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{4}}\exp\left\{-c\dfrac{|x-y|^{\frac{4}{3}}}{t^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right\}.

(3.5)

Therefore, by applying Blunck’s abstract spectral multiplier theorem
[
2
]
to
H
, we obtain for
1<p<\infty
that

\|H^{iy}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C_{p}\langle y\rangle^{2}.

Hence the case
0<s<2
follows from Stein’s interpolation theorem
[
28
]
.

Next we prove (
3.3
). Set
H_{0}=\Delta^{2}
and define

T_{z}=H^{z}H^{-z}_{0},\ \ z=x+iy.

We already know that
H^{iy}
and
H^{-iy}_{0}
are bounded on
L^{q}
for
1<q<\infty
. Thus, taking
z=iy
, we have for
1<q<\infty
,

\|T_{iy}\|_{L^{q}\rightarrow L^{q}}\lesssim\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}.

(3.6)

Next take
z=1+iy
. For
1<p<\frac{n}{4}
, by Hölder and Sobolev inequalities,

\displaystyle\|Hf\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|H_{0}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|Vf\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|H_{0}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{n}{4}}}\|H_{0}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|H_{0}f\|_{L^{p}}.

Therefore, for
1<p<\frac{n}{4}
,

\|T_{1+iy}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\lesssim\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}

(3.7)

holds. Hence, by (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and Stein’s interpolation theorem, for
\theta\in[0,1]
,

\|T_{\theta}\|_{L^{r}\rightarrow L^{r}}\lesssim 1

holds, where
1<r<\frac{n}{4\theta}
. Writing
s=4\theta
, we obtain for
s\in[0,4]
and
1<r<\frac{n}{s}
that

\|H^{s/4}f\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L^{r}}.

∎

By Proposition
3.4
, under Assumption
1.3
, since
V\in L^{\infty}
implies Assumption
3.3
, we obtain the equivalence of Sobolev norms

\|H^{s/4}u\|_{L^{p}}\sim\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{p}}

for any
1<p<\frac{n}{2}
.

The following proposition asserts the equivalence of inhomogeneous Sobolev norms and will be used in Section 10 on scattering. The difference from Proposition
3.4
is that Assumption
1.3
(1) and (3) are not needed.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, assume
V\in L^{\infty}
, and let
E>0
be sufficiently large. Then for any
0<s\leq 4
,

(\Delta^{2}+E)^{s/4}(H+E)^{-s/4}
and
(H+E)^{s/4}(\Delta^{2}+E)^{-s/4}
are bounded on
L^{p}
.

Proof.

First we show that
(\Delta^{2}+E)(H+E)^{-1}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p})
. Since
H
is bounded from below, there exists
E_{0}>0
such that for
E\geq E_{0}
,
H+E
is a positive self-adjoint operator. In this case we may write

(H+E)^{-1}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tH}e^{-Et}fdt,\ \ f\in L^{2}.

By
[
7
]
, the semigroup
e^{-tH}
extends to an analytic semigroup
e^{zH}
on
L^{1}
with angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
, and its kernel satisfies

|e^{-tH}(x,y)|\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{4}}\exp\left\{-c\dfrac{|x-y|^{\frac{4}{3}}}{t^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\omega t\right\},\ \ t>0,

(3.8)

where
c,\omega>0
. For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}t^{-\frac{n}{4}}\exp\left\{-c\dfrac{|x-y|^{\frac{4}{3}}}{t^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\omega t\right\}dy\lesssim e^{\omega t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s}s^{\frac{3}{4}n-1}ds\leq Ce^{\omega t}\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}n\right).

Hence

\|e^{-tH}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\lesssim e^{\omega t}

follows. Therefore, if
E>\max(E_{0},\omega)
, then for
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
,

\|(H+E)^{-1}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-Et}\|e^{-tH}f\|_{L^{p}}dt\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(E-\omega)t}dt\|f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim|E-\omega|^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Thus
(H+E)^{-1}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}
. Moreover,

\Delta^{2}(H+E)^{-1}f=(H+E-V-E)(H+E)^{-1}f=\{1-(V+E)(H+E)^{-1}\}f

so that
\|\Delta^{2}(H+E)^{-1}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(1+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}})\|f\|_{L^{p}}
for
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
. By density, we obtain
(\Delta^{2}+E)(H+E)^{-1}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p})
. The case
0<s<4
follows from Stein’s interpolation theorem exactly as in Proposition
3.4
.

Next we show
(H+E)(\Delta^{2}+E)^{-1}
. Similarly,
(\Delta^{2}+E)^{-1}
is bounded on
L^{p}
. Furthermore,

(H+E)(\Delta^{2}+E)^{-1}f=\{1+V(\Delta^{2}+E)^{-1}\}f

so that
\|(H+E)(\Delta^{2}+E)^{-1}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(1+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}})\|f\|_{L^{p}}
for
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}
. Hence
(H+E)(\Delta^{2}+E)^{-1}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p})
. The case
0<s<4
is the same.
∎

4
Strichartz Estimates and
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
Decay Estimates

For an external force term
h\in L_{loc}^{1}(I,H^{-4})
and initial data
u_{0}\in L^{2}
, define

u(t)=e^{itH}u_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)H}h(s)ds.

(4.1)

We say that a pair
(q,r)
is Schrödinger admissible (abbreviated S-admissible) if
2\leq q,r\leq\infty
,
(q,r,n)\neq(2,\infty,2)
, and

\dfrac{2}{q}+\dfrac{n}{r}=\dfrac{n}{2}.

We say that a pair
(q,r)
is biharmonic admissible (abbreviated B-admissible) if
2\leq q,r\leq\infty
,
(q,r,n)\neq(2,\infty,4)
, and

\dfrac{4}{q}+\dfrac{n}{r}=\dfrac{n}{2}.

In order to state the assumptions on
V
, define

\begin{array}[]{lcr}H_{\ell}=4^{\ell}\Delta^{2},&V_{\ell}(x)=(-x\cdot\nabla_{x})^{\ell}V(x),&\ell=1,2.\end{array}

(4.2)

Assumption 4.1
.

Let
V
be a real-valued potential satisfying
V\in L^{\frac{n}{4},\infty}
. Assume that for each
\ell=0,1,2
, we have
(x\cdot\nabla)^{\ell}V\in L^{1}_{loc}
and
|(x\cdot\nabla)^{\ell}V|^{1/2}(\Delta^{2}+1)^{-1/2}
is bounded on
L^{2}
. Furthermore, assume that for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\langle(\Delta^{2}+V)u,u\rangle

\displaystyle\gtrsim\langle\Delta^{2}u,u\rangle,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\langle(H_{1}+V_{1})u,u\rangle

\displaystyle\gtrsim\langle\Delta^{2}u,u\rangle,

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\langle(H_{2}+V_{2})u,u\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle(H_{1}+V_{1})u,u\rangle.

(4.5)

The following Strichartz estimates for (
4.1
) were proved in
[
23
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
n\geq 5
, and let
H=\Delta^{2}+V
satisfy Assumption
4.1
. Then the following statements hold for
u
given by (
4.1
).

•

(Standard Strichartz estimate)

Let
(p_{1},q_{1})
and
(p_{2},q_{2})
be B-admissible. Then

\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}(I,L^{q_{1}})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h\|_{L^{p_{2}^{\prime}}(I,L^{q_{2}^{\prime}})}.

(4.6)

•

(Improved Strichartz estimate)

Let
(p,q)
and
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
be S-admissible. Then

5
Local and Global Existence in the Subcritical Case

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
V\in L^{\infty}
satisfies Assumption
1.3
and Assumption
4.1
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{2}
, and let
n\geq 5
and
p\in(1,2^{\sharp}-1)
. Then, for any such
u_{0}
and
p
, there exist
T>0
and a solution
u\in C([0,T],H^{2})
to (
1.1
) satisfying
u(0)=u_{0}
. Moreover, the conservation of mass and energy,

\displaystyle M(u(t))

\displaystyle=M(u_{0}),

(5.1)

\displaystyle E(u(t))

\displaystyle=E(u_{0}),

hold for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Let
M=C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}
, and define the mapping
\Phi
by

\Phi(u)=e^{itH}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)H}|u|^{p-1}u(s)\,ds.

Next, define the complete metric space
X_{M}
by

X_{M}=\left\{u\in C(I,H^{2});\ \|\Delta u\|_{S(I)}\leq 2M\right\}.

Here,

\|u\|_{S(I)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(q,r):\text{B-admissible}\\
r<n/2\end{subarray}}\|u\|_{L^{q}(I,L^{r})},

and the distance on
X_{M}
is defined by this norm.
By the Strichartz estimate (
4.8
), together with Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev’s inequality, we have for any
u\in X_{M}
,

\displaystyle\|\Delta\Phi(u)\|_{S(I)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}+C\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{2(p-1)}(I,L^{\frac{n(p-1)}{2}})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}})}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}+C\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{2(p-1)}(I,L^{\frac{n(p-1)}{2}})}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,L^{2})}.

Also, by Sobolev’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, for
1<p<2^{\sharp}-1
, we obtain

\|u\|_{L^{2(p-1)}(I,L^{\frac{n(p-1)}{2}})}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{(p-1)}-\frac{n-4}{8}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\frac{8(p-1)}{(n-4)(p-1)-4}}(I,L^{\frac{n(p-1)}{2p}})}.

(5.3)

From (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), setting

\theta=\frac{1}{(p-1)}-\frac{n-4}{8}>0,

we obtain

\|\Delta\Phi(u)\|_{S(I)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}+CT^{\theta}\|\Delta u\|^{p}_{S(I)}\leq M+CT^{\theta}M^{p}.

Therefore, if we choose
T
sufficiently small so that
CT^{\theta}M^{p}\leq M
, namely,

T\leq CM^{\frac{1-p}{\theta}},

then
\Phi
maps
X_{M}
into itself.
Furthermore, for any
u,v\in X_{M}
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{S(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1})|u-v|\|_{L^{2}(I,L^{\frac{2n}{n+4}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{2(p-1)}(I,L^{\frac{n(p-1)}{2}})}+\|v\|^{p-1}_{L^{2(p-1)}(I,L^{\frac{n(p-1)}{2}})}\right)\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}(I,L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\theta}\left(\|\Delta u\|^{p-1}_{S(I)}+\|\Delta v\|^{p-1}_{S(I)}\right)\|u-v\|_{S(I)}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\theta}M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{S(I)}.

Thus, by taking
T
sufficiently small,
\Phi
becomes a contraction mapping on
X_{M}
.
The conservation of mass and energy follows from Chapter 3 of
[
4
]
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
n\geq 5
,
p\in(1,2^{\sharp}-1)
, and assume that
V\geq 0
and
u_{0}\in H^{2}
. Let
u
be the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}
.
Then the solution

can be extended globally in time on
\mathbb{R}
in each of the following cases:

(a)

\lambda\geq 0
,

(b)

\lambda<0,\ p<1+\frac{8}{n}
,

(c)

Cases (b) and (c) follow from the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality together with the conservation of mass and energy.

\|u-v\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}}(I,L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}})}\leq C\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{H^{2}}.

Substituting this into (

) yields (

).
Finally, (
6.3
) follows by applying the Strichartz estimates once more.
The conservation of mass and energy follows from
[
4
]
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
p=2^{\sharp}-1
, and assume that
V
satisfies Assumptions
1.3
and
4.1
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for any

satisfying
\mathcal{E}(u_{0})\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, there exists a unique global solution
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{2})
to (

) with initial data

.

Proof.

Assume that
u
exists on
[0,t_{0}]
and that the

-norm of

is sufficiently small. Then, by the Strichartz estimate (

), condition (

) holds at

, and hence

can be extended to
[t_{0},t_{0}+1]
.

Therefore, to prove global existence, it suffices to show that
\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}
remains small on the interval of existence.
By Sobolev’s inequality and the weak-type Hölder inequality (
2.1
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)|u(x)|^{2}dx\lesssim\|V\|_{L^{\frac{n}{4},\infty}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2^{\sharp}}}^{2}\lesssim\|V\|_{L^{\frac{n}{4},\infty}}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}.

(6.6)

Moreover, by (
6.6

E(u(t))=E(u_{0})\leq C(\mathcal{E}(u_{0})+\mathcal{E}(u_{0})^{2^{\sharp}/2}).

(6.7)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H^{2}}}

\displaystyle=2E(u(t))-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)|u(x)|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{n-4}{n}\lambda|u(x)|^{2^{\sharp}}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2E(u_{0})+|\lambda|\dfrac{n-4}{n}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2^{\sharp}}}^{2^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}(u_{0})+\mathcal{E}(u_{0})^{2^{\sharp}/2}+\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H^{2}}}^{2^{\sharp}}.

It follows from (

) that, if

is sufficiently small, then

remains small in the

-norm.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Assume that
V
satisfies Assumptions
1.3
and
4.1
. Let
n\geq 5
and
p=2^{\sharp}-1
.
Let
u\in C([0,T),H^{2})
be a solution to (
1.1
) satisfying
\|u\|_{Z([0,T])}<+\infty
.
Then there exists a constant
K=K(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}},\|u\|_{Z([0,T])})
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}}([0,T],L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\dot{H^{2}})}+\|u\|_{M([0,T])}\leq K,

(6.9)

and
u
can be extended to a solution
\tilde{u}\in C([0,\tilde{T}),H^{2})
of (
1.1
) for some
\tilde{T}>T
.

Proof.

Take a small
\eta>0
, and let
B=\|u\|_{Z([0,T])}
. For
x\geq 0
, let
[x]
denote the largest integer not exceeding
x
.
Set

N=\left[(B/\eta)^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n-4}}\right]+1.

Then the interval
[0,T]
can be decomposed into
N
subintervals
I_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,N
, such that

\|u\|_{Z(I_{j})}=\eta\quad(1\leq j\leq N-1),\qquad\|u\|_{Z(I_{N})}\leq\eta.

Applying the Strichartz estimate (
4.8
) on
I_{j}=[t_{j},t_{j+1}]
, we obtain for any

,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{M([t_{j},t])}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t_{j})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}+C\|u\|_{Z(I_{j})}^{\frac{8}{n-4}}\|u\|_{M([t_{j},t])}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t_{j})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}+C\eta^{\frac{8}{n-4}}\|u\|_{M([t_{j},t])}.

Also, applying (
4.6
) on

and using conservation of mass, we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}}([t_{j},t],\ L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t_{j})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|u\|_{Z([t_{j},\ t])}^{\frac{8}{n-4}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}}([t_{j},t],\ L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+C\eta^{\frac{8}{n-4}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}}([t_{j},t],\ L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}})}.

\eta

) and (

) imply

\displaystyle\|u\|_{M(I_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t_{j})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}}(I_{j},\ L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, by (

),

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{j},\ \dot{H}^{2})}\leq C\|u(t_{j})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}},

\|u(t_{j+1})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}\leq C\|u(t_{j})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}.

Thus, for every

,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}}([0,T],\ L^{\frac{2(n+4)}{n}})}\leq N^{\frac{n}{2(n+4)}}C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

(6.12)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\ \dot{H}^{2})}\leq C^{N}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{M([0,T])}\leq C^{N}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}.

Hence (
6.9
) follows from (
6.12
).

Now take
t_{0}\in I_{N}
. By Duhamel’s formula and the Strichartz estimate (
4.8
), for any
t

\displaystyle\|e^{i(t-t_{0})H}u(t_{0})\|_{W([t_{0},T])}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{W([t_{0},T])}+C\||u|^{\frac{8}{n-4}}u\|_{N([t_{0},T])}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{W([t_{0},T])}+C\|u\|_{W([t_{0},T])}^{\frac{n+4}{n-4}}.

Since the

Therefore the left-hand side of (

0
as

Hence, for any
\delta>0
, there exist
t_{1}\in(0,T)
and

such that

and

Then, by Proposition
6.2
, there exists a solution

of (
1.1
) with

.
By uniqueness,
u=v
on

, and thus

can be extended to
[0,T^{\prime}]
.
∎

8

In this section we establish the almost conservation of localized mass and a localized Morawetz-type estimate.
We begin with the almost conservation of localized mass.

Let

for

For

(8.1)

where

By Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev’s inequality,

M(u,B_{x_{0}}(R))\lesssim\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}R^{4}.

(8.2)

We now show that, for a solution

to (
1.1

Lemma 8.1
.

Let

and

. Let

and let

Proof.

By translation invariance, we may assume

Using (
1.1
) and integration by parts,

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}M(u(t),B_{0}(R))

\displaystyle=2\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}i((\Delta^{2}u)\bar{u}+V|u|^{2}+\lambda|u|^{p})\chi_{R}^{4}(x)dx

\displaystyle=2\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}i(\Delta^{2}u)\bar{u}\chi_{R}^{4}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\dfrac{16}{R}\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}i\Delta u\nabla\bar{u}(\nabla\chi)_{R}\chi_{R}^{3}dx

\displaystyle+\dfrac{8}{R^{2}}\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}i\Delta u\left(\chi_{R}^{3}(\Delta\chi)_{R}+3\chi_{R}^{2}(\nabla\chi)_{R}^{2}\right)\bar{u}dx.

We estimate the two terms in the last line separately.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta u\nabla\bar{u}(\nabla\chi)_{R}\chi_{R}^{3}dx\right|\leq\|(\nabla\chi)_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\chi_{R}^{6}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Also, integrating by parts,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\chi_{R}^{6}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{u}\left((\Delta u)\chi_{R}^{6}+\nabla u\dfrac{6}{R}(\nabla\chi)_{R}\chi_{R}^{5}\right)dx.

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}\chi_{R}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{6}{R}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}\chi_{R}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}(\nabla\chi_{R})^{2}\chi_{R}^{6}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}M(u(t),B_{0}(R))^{\frac{1}{2}}+\dfrac{1}{R}M(u(t),B_{0}(R))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}dx\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla\chi_{R})^{n}\chi_{R}^{3n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}M(u(t),B_{0}(R))^{\frac{1}{2}}+\dfrac{1}{R}M(u(t),B_{0}(R))^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}R

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}M(u(t),B_{0}(R))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, for the second term in (

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta u\chi_{R}^{2}\bar{u}\left(\chi_{R}(\Delta\chi)_{R}+3(\nabla\chi)_{R}^{2}\right)dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta u\chi_{R}^{2}\bar{u}dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}\chi_{R}^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}M(u(t),B_{0}(R))^{\frac{1}{2}}

Since

Assumption 10.1
.

Let

, where

and

(H1)

The wave operators

exist on

.

(H2)

The inverse wave operators

\Omega_{\pm}=s-\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}e^{itH_{0}}e^{-itH}P_{ac}(H)

exist on

.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}(X,Y)
denote the space of compact operators from
X
to
Y
, and write
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}(X)=\mathbb{B}_{\infty}(X,X)
.
Also, define
L^{\infty,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the set of all
f\in L^{\infty}
such that
f(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
.

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
V\in L^{\infty,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

[\psi(H_{0})-\psi(H)]\varphi(H_{0})\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}(L^{2},H^{2})

for any
\psi,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}[\psi(H_{0})-\psi(H)]\varphi(H_{0})\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}(L^{2}).

By the Helffer–Sjöstrand formula,

\displaystyle\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}

\displaystyle[\psi(H_{0})-\psi(H)]\varphi(H_{0})

(10.1)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\partial\tilde{\varphi}}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}[(H_{0}-z)^{-1}-(H-z)^{-1}]\varphi(H_{0})dzd\bar{z},

where
\tilde{\varphi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is an almost-analytic extension of
\varphi
satisfying

\partial_{\bar{z}}\tilde{\varphi}(z)=O(\langle z\rangle^{-N}|\text{Im}z|^{N})

for any
N\geq 1
.

We claim that

\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}[(H_{0}-z)^{-1}-(H-z)^{-1}]\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}(L^{2}).

Indeed, by the resolvent identity,

\displaystyle\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}[(H_{0}-z)^{-1}-(H-z)^{-1}]

\displaystyle=\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}(H-z)^{-1}V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}\langle H\rangle^{-1/2}\langle H\rangle^{1/2}(H-z)^{-1}V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}.

Now
\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}\langle H\rangle^{-1/2}
and
\langle H\rangle^{1/2}(H-z)^{-1}
are bounded on
L^{2}
, and since
V\in L^{\infty,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the operator

V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\in\mathbb{B}_{\infty}(L^{2})

is compact. Therefore the integrand in (
10.1
) is compact. Also, since
\varphi(H_{0})\in\mathbb{B}(L^{2})
, the full integrand is compact.

Writing
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}}
, the spectral theorem and Proposition
3.5
imply

\displaystyle\|\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}[(H_{0}-z)^{-1}-(H-z)^{-1}]\varphi(H_{0})\|

(10.2)

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\varphi(H_{0})\|+\|\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}(H-z)^{-1}\varphi(H_{0})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}\varphi(H_{0})\|+\|(H-z)^{-1}\langle H\rangle^{1/2}\varphi(H_{0})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\|\|\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}\varphi(H_{0})\|+\|(H-z)^{-1}\|\|\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}\varphi(H_{0})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(H_{0})}|\lambda-z|^{-1}\|\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}\varphi(H_{0})\|+\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(H)}|\lambda-z|^{-1}\|\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}\varphi(H_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq C|\text{Im}z|^{-1}.

Thus the integral in (
10.1
) converges in operator norm, and the result follows.
∎

Proposition 10.3
.

Assume
V\in L^{\infty,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then the following hold:

1.

If (H1) holds, then

W_{\pm}=s-\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}e^{itH}e^{-itH_{0}}

exist on
H^{2}
.

2.

If (H2) holds, then

\Omega_{\pm}=s-\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}e^{itH_{0}}e^{-itH}P_{ac}(H)

exist on
H^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{2}
and set
W(t)=e^{itH}e^{-itH_{0}}
. By Proposition
3.5
,

\|\langle\Delta\rangle W(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|e^{itH}\langle H\rangle^{1/2}e^{-itH_{0}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}},

so
W(t)
is uniformly bounded on
H^{2}
with respect to
t
.

To prove existence, it suffices to show that for any sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
, the sequence
\{W(t_{n})u_{0}\}
is Cauchy in
H^{2}
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
, and choose
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}
such that
\varphi\equiv 1
near the origin. Let
\varphi_{R}(\lambda)=\varphi(\lambda/R)
. Then

\|u_{0}-\varphi_{R}(H_{0})u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\sigma(H_{0})}|1-\varphi(\lambda/R)|^{2}d\|E_{H_{0}}(\lambda)u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}\rightarrow 0,\qquad R\rightarrow\infty

by the dominated convergence theorem.
In particular, for some
R\geq 1
,

\sup_{n,m}\|(W(t_{n})-W(t_{m}))(u_{0}-\varphi_{R}(H_{0})u_{0})\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}-\varphi_{R}(H_{0})u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}<\varepsilon.

Hence we may replace
u_{0}
by
u_{R}=\varphi_{R}(H_{0})u_{0}
.

Now choose
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}
such that
\psi\varphi\equiv\varphi
. Then

\displaystyle[W(t_{n})-W(t_{m})]u_{R}=\psi_{R}(H)[W(t_{n})-W(t_{m})]u_{R}+(1-\psi_{R})(H)[W(t_{n})-W(t_{m})]u_{R}.

Since
\|\langle H_{0}\rangle^{1/2}\psi_{R}(H)\|_{L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}}\lesssim R
, assumption (H1) implies

\|\psi_{R}(H)[W(t_{n})-W(t_{m})]u_{R}\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim R\|[W(t_{n})-W(t_{m})]u_{R}\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0,\qquad m,n\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover,

(1-\psi_{R})(H)\varphi_{R}(H_{0})=[\psi_{R}(H_{0})-\psi_{R}(H)]\varphi_{R}(H_{0}),

and for any
f\in L^{2}
, one has weak convergence
e^{itH_{0}}f\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{2}
as
t\to\pm\infty
. Therefore, by Lemma
10.2
,

\displaystyle\|(1-\psi_{R})(H)[W(t_{n})-W(t_{m})]u_{R}\|_{H^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\|[\psi_{R}(H_{0})-\psi_{R}(H)]e^{-it_{n}H_{0}}u_{R}\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|[\psi_{R}(H_{0})-\psi_{R}(H)]e^{-it_{m}H_{0}}u_{R}\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,\qquad

\displaystyle m,n\rightarrow\infty.

Hence
\{W(t_{n})u_{R}\}
is Cauchy in
H^{2}
.
The proof for
\Omega_{\pm}
is the same.
∎

Appendix A.1

(Abstract bootstrap principle,
[
29
]
)
.

Let
I
be a time interval, and suppose that for each
t\in I
there are two statements: a “hypothesis”
\textbf{H}(t)
and a “conclusion”
\textbf{C}(t)

Assume that the following four properties hold.

(a)

If
\textbf{H}(t)
holds for some

, then
\textbf{C}(t)
also holds for that

.

(b)

If
\textbf{C}(t)
holds for some
t\in I
, then
\textbf{H}(t^{\prime})
holds for all
t^{\prime}
in a neighborhood of
t
.

(d)

If a sequence
t_{n}\in I
converges to
t\in I
and
\textbf{C}(t_{n})
holds for all
n
, then
\textbf{C}(t)
also holds.

(e)

\textbf{H}(t)
holds for at least one point

.

Then
\textbf{C}(t)
holds for every
t\in I
.

Appendix A.2

(Product rule,
[
5
]
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
, and let

satisfy

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{i}}+\frac{1}{q_{i}},\qquad(i=1,2).

Then

\||\nabla|^{s}(fg)\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\||\nabla|^{s}g\|_{L^{q_{1}}}+\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|g\|_{L^{q_{2}}}

holds.

Appendix A.3

(Fractional chain rule for a Hölder continuous function,
[
32
]
)
.

Let

.
Then, for any

,

, and

\||\nabla|^{s}G(u)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\||u|^{\alpha-\frac{s}{\sigma}}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\||\nabla|^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{\frac{s}{\sigma}p_{2}}}^{\frac{s}{\sigma}}

holds, where

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\qquad\left(1-\frac{s}{\alpha\sigma}\right)p_{1}>1.
