 Modeling of spin squeezing in OPMs and performance
 improvements
Executive summary August 11, 2025

Prepared by Eduardo Ibarra-Garcı́a-Padilla
Team members Eduardo Ibarra-Garcı́a-Padilla, Brandon Ruzic, Jonathan Bainbridge,
 Peter Schwindt
Sandia National Laboratories, Albuquerque, NM 87123 USA

Thrust
We propose to develop an optically pumped magnetometer (OPM) that implements spin squeezing
to achieve better than standard quantum limit (SQL) performance while operating in mission
relevant environments. This performance enhancement would allow us to increase the useable
bandwidth by a factor > 2 while maintaining world class magnetic sensitivity, an order of magnitude
better than commercially available options.

Theory objective
We develop a theoretical model to explore the underlying dynamics of the hot vapor cell on which
the OPM is based so we may best predict and optimize squeezing performance. Our theoretical
approach focuses on:

 I) Calculating the relevant physical mechanisms that limit the sensitivity and bandwidth of the
 OPM in terms of experimentally accessible parameters such as the temperature at which the
 vapor cell is operated and the probe laser detuning, amongst others.

  II) Deriving Cramer-Rao lower bounds (CRLB) for the sensitivity of the OPM as functions
 of the experimentally accessible parameters under the assumption that we are performing
 quantum non-demolition measurements and the relevant noise channels correspond to the
 photon shot noise and spin projection noise.

 III) Incorporating effects of squeezing in the CRLB and compute both the sensitivity and band-
 width of the OPM for different experimentally accessible parameters. The effects of squeez-
 ing are introduced by assuming the spin-projection noise is now exponentially correlated in
 time and its associated noise floor level increases as the squeezing degrades in time.

 1

Theory conclusions
The main results of this work are summarized in Fig. 1.
 In the absence of squeezing we explore the temperature, filling pressure, and detuning depen-
dence on the Faraday rotation signal of a scalar OPM operating in Earth’s magnetic field. Our
findings indicate that the lowest sensitivity can be attained at an operation temperature of 120 ◦ C
and a filling pressure of N2 at 115 Torr. A detuning of 98 GHz from the D2 line ensures a large
Faraday optical rotation response for a single pass cubic cell with linear size of 1 cm while keeping
undesired non-linear light-matter couplings are below 0.1%.
 When effects of squeezing are introduced our results show that although attaining an absolute
lower sensitivity and larger bandwidth seems unrealistic experimentally due to the short lifetime of
the squeezed state, at short time scales the bandwidth can be enhanced by a factor of ∼ 1.4x.
This improvement in the bandwidth makes it useful for field applications where fast detection is
needed. In conclusion, our results address a question relevant in the field regarding the usability
of spin squeezed states in OPM for magnetic field sensing.

Organization of report
The theory report is organized as follows:

  1. We present the relevant Hamiltonian that describes light-matter interaction for the OPM.

  2. We compute the D1 and D2 line dynamical polarizabilities, which allows to determine at
 which values of the laser probe detuning the system does not exhibit undesired non-linear
 light-matter couplings.

  3. We calculate the spin-exchange, destruction, and diffusion rates for 87 Rb, which are relevant
 for describing how the the spin polarization of the atom ensemble decays in time as function
 of temperature and the buffer gas filling pressure.

  4. We compute the strength of the Faraday rotation signal as a function of temperature and
 detuning. We then calculate the output signal detectable using balanced polarimetry.

  5. We present our derivation of the relevant equations for the CRLB to compute the magnetic
 field sensitivity and discuss their behaviors in the unsqueezed regime.

  6. We propose a model on how to incorporate squeezing in our calculations, and derive the
 relevant corrections to the CRLBs.

  7. We present results as we explore different levels of squeezing and compare their sensitivities
 and bandwidth against the unsqueezed results.

  8. We conclude and detailed derivations are presented in the Appendix.

 Abstract
 In this notes we discuss our approach to calculating the magnetic field sensitivity of an optical
magnetometer operated in scalar configuration in which quantum non-demolition measurements
are made, and a pulsed probe is used to prepare spin-squeezed states for magnetic field sensing.
Whenever an approximation is made it will be highlighted in blue so these are easy to track.

I. THE SYSTEM

 The system under consideration is an ensemble of atoms in a cell which are in the presence
of a magnetic field which induces Larmor precesion. These atoms are illuminated by laser
fields which couple the polarization of the probing light with the spin degree of freedom of
the atoms. The atomic system will be described by collective spin operators, while the light
in terms of Stokes operators, both of which obey the SU(2) algebra of angular momentum
variables 1 .

II. INTERACTION

 In light-matter interfaces, light will be either absorbed or dispersed. The absorption curve
as a function of detuning is proportional to 1/∆2 , while the dispersion curve is proportional
to 1/∆. In the limit of large detunings, so that absorption is suppressed, only dispersion
effects need to be considered. Dispersion effects will change the polarization state of light
if the sample is birefringent. This means that when linearly polarized light passes through
the atomic spin in the z-direction, the circular birefringence caused by the spin component
Jˆz will rotate the polarization of the light [1, 2].
 Experimentally, the probe laser is tuned to either the D1 or the D2 line 2 . We define the
detuning ∆ to be measured with respect to the F = 2 → F ′ = J ′ + I transition (i.e. the
maximum allowed F ′ value for the given excited manifold).

by the spin-exchange Rse and spin destruction Rsd rates.

 Since a QND measurement is performed, we only consider two sources of noise:

 • Photon shot + electronic noise. These exhibit a white noise spectra.

 • Spin-projection noise. In the presence of squeezing we model as non-white noise since
 by introducing entanglement intro the system via squeezing, the noise should be corre-
 lated. We consider a model where the noise is exponentially correlated with correlation
 time Tc . Further details will be discussed below.

In the following simulations consider a white noise bandwidth identical to the Larmor
frequency fn = ν0 , and for the correlated non-white noise a bandwidth given by fnw =
ν0 /(1 + 2Tc ν0 ), which corresponds to summing in parallel 1/fnw = 1/ν0 + 1/(1/2Tc ) such
that the appropriate limits when Tc → 0 and Tc → ∞ are recovered. In particular, in the
limit when Tc → 0 the spin-projection noise should be white noise with the same noise
bandwidth as the photon shot noise.
 In addition, given that only zero crossings in phase with a sine wave oscillating at the
Larmor period can be detected via the pulse-probed scheme, we consider a detuning for
which the a2 /a1 ratio is small while ensuring no wrapping occurs in the signal.
 Since the squeezing will degrade over time (see for example Fig. 7) the noise spectral
density should change over time, being smaller at short times when the state is maximally
squeezed, and then larger as the squeezing degrades and then becomes anti-squeezed. We
propose a model on how to do that and define the correlation time Tc as the time the system
losses all of its squeezing and then begins to anti-squeeze.

                                ξ 2 (t = Tc ) = 1                                       (40)
                                    ρnw (t) = ξ 2 (t)ρnw (t = 0)                        (41)

This model then captures that the squeezing degrades as a function of time and eventually
your error gets worst.

VI. CONCLUSIONS

 In these notes we derived a theoretical model to explore the underlying dynamics of the
hot vapor cell on which the OPM is based so we may best predict and optimize squeezing
performance. We have:

 • Calculated the relevant physical mechanisms that limit the sensitivity and bandwidth
 of the OPM in terms of experimentally accessible parameters such as the temperature
 and filling pressure at which the vapor cell is operated, as well as the probe laser
 detuning, amongst others.

 • Derived Cramer-Rao lower bounds (CRLB) for the sensitivity of the OPM as func-
 tions of the experimentally accessible parameters under the assumption that we are
 performing quantum non-demolition measurements and the relevant noise channels
 correspond to the photon shot noise + electronic noise and spin projection noise.

 • Incorporated effects of squeezing in the CRLB and compute both the sensitivity and
 bandwidth of the OPM for different experimentally accessible parameters and different

 20

 squeezing models.

  Our results illustrate the temperature, filling pressure and detuning dependence on the
Faraday rotation signal of a scalar OPM operating in Earth’s magnetic field. Our findings
indicate that the lowest sensitivity can be attained at an operation temperature of 120 ◦ C
and a filling pressure of N2 at 115 Torr. A detuning of 48 GHz from the D2 line ensures a
large Faraday optical rotation response for a single pass cubic cell with linear size of 1 cm
while keeping undesired non-linear light-matter couplings are below 0.2%.
  We then introduced the effects of squeezing, which we have done by proposing the spin-
projection noise is now exponentially correlated in time and the noise increases as the
squeezing degrades in time. Our results show that although attaining an absolute lower
sensitivity and larger bandwidth seems unrealistic experimentally due to the short lifetime
of the squeezed state, at short time scales the bandwidth can be enhanced by a factor of
∼ 1.4x which makes it useful for field applications where fast detection is needed. In con-
clusion, our results address a question relevant in the field regarding the usability of spin
squeezed states in OPM for magnetic field sensing.
