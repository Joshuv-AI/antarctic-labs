1.
Introduction

Call two sequences
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
defined in the Banach spaces
(B_{1},\|\cdot\|_{B_{1}})
, resp.
(B_{2},\|\cdot\|_{B_{2}})

equivalent
if for some constant
K>0
we have

K^{-1}\big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\big\|_{B_{1}}\leq\big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}y_{i}\big\|_{B_{2}}\leq K\big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\big\|_{B_{1}}

for every
n\geq 1
and every
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By a classical theorem of Kadec and
Pełczynski
[
17
]
, for any normalized weakly null sequence

(x_{n})
in
L_{p}(0,1)
,
p>2

there exists a subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})
which is
equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{2}
or
\ell_{p}
. Thus every infinite
dimensional closed subspace of
L_{p}(0,1)
,
p>2
contains a
subspace isomorphic to
\ell_{2}
or
\ell_{p}
.
In the case when
\{|x_{n}|^{p},\,n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable,
the first alternative holds, while if the functions
(x_{n})
have disjoint
support, the second alternative holds trivially. The general case follows via a subsequence splitting argument
as in
[
17
]
.

The proofs in
[
17
]
depend on the study of subspaces of
L_{p}
spanned by independent or lacunary sequences of random variables which, in turn, requires sharp inequalities for the norm of partial sums of such sequences. Classical examples are the Khinchin, Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund, Rosenthal inequalities and their variants, see e.g.
[
18
,
24
,
25
,
29
,
30
]
. In recent decades, the theory has been extended to rearrangement invariant, in particular Orlicz spaces, see e.g.
[
4
]
and the references therein. In particular, there is a characterization of rearrangement invariant spaces in which the Khinchin and Marcinkiewicz inequalities hold, see
[
3
,
4
,
28
]
. A simple and direct proof in the special cases of Lorentz and Orlicz spaces was given by the authors in a preprint; see
[
9
]
. For a general overview and further results, see
[
5
,
20
,
23
]
and the references therein.

For
1\leq p<2
the situation becomes more complex and many basic problems about the subspace structure of
L_{p}
remain open until today.
But in
[
10
]
a necessary and sufficient condition was given for the first alternative in the Kadec-Pełczyinski theorem for the case
1\leq p<2
, which we formulate below. Using the terminology of
[
8
]
, call a sequence of
(X_{n})
of random variables
determining
if it has a limit distribution relative to any set
A
in the probability space with
P(A)>0
, i.e.,
there exists a distribution function
F_{A}
such that
\lim P(X_{n}\leq t|A)=F_{A}(t)
as
n\to\infty
for all continuity points
t
of
F_{A}
. Here
P(\cdot|A)
denotes conditional probability given
A
. As is shown in
[
2
,
8
]
, there exists a random measure
\mu
(i.e. a measurable map from the underlying probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},P)
to
(\mathcal{M},\pi)
, where
{\mathcal{M}}
is the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
and
\pi
is the Prohorov distance), such that for any
A
with
P(A)>0
and any continuity point
t
of
F_{A}
we have
F_{A}(t)=\mathbb{E}_{A}(\mu(-\infty,t]),
where
\mathbb{E}_{A}
denotes conditional expectation given
A
. We call
\mu
the
limit random measure
of
(X_{n})
. Let
(Y_{n})
be a sequence of random variables on
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},P)
such that, conditionally on
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,\mu)
, the
Y_{n}
are i.i.d. with conditional distribution
\mu
. See
[
2
]
, p. 72; note that the construction of
(Y_{n})
requires an enlargement of the probability space. Clearly,
(Y_{n})
is an exchangeable sequence, we call it the
limit exchangeable sequence
of
(X_{n})
.

A Young function, or Orlicz function, is a convex mapping

\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

satisfying

\psi(x)/x\to\infty
as
x\to\infty,

and

\psi(x)/x\to 0
as
x\to 0.

In particular, every Young function is non-decreasing, continuous and locally Lipschitz on
(0,\infty)
, and hence differentiable almost everywhere. Moreover, its left and right derivatives, denoted by
\psi^{\prime}_{-}
and
\psi^{\prime}_{+}
, exist at every point of
(0,\infty)
, see e.g.
[
19
, ch. 1]
.

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},P)
be a probability space. The Orlicz norm of a measurable function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
is given by

(1.1)

\|f\|_{L^{\psi}(Y)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\ :\ \int_{\Omega}\psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)dP(x)\leq 1\right\}.

The
Orlicz-space

L^{\psi}
with assigned Young-function
\psi
consists of the set of measurable functions
f
such that
\|f\|_{L^{\psi}}
is finite.
For
1\leq p<\infty
, if
\psi(x)=x^{p}
, then
L^{\psi}=L^{p}
.
Since we only consider finite-measure spaces, we have
\psi\ll\varphi
if and only if
L^{\varphi}\subset L^{\psi}
and then

(1.2)

\|\cdot\|_{L^{\psi}}\ll\|\cdot\|_{L^{\varphi}},

see e.g. Chapter
2
of
[
31
]
. The notation
f\ll g
means that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
|f|\leq Cg
in the range under consideration.

In
[
10
]
the following result was proved:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and let
(X_{n})
be a determining sequence of random variables such that
\|X_{n}\|_{p}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{|X_{n}|^{p},n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable and
X_{n}\to 0
weakly in
L^{p}
. Let
\mu
be the limit random measure of
(X_{n})
.
Then there exists a subsequence
(X_{n_{k}})
equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell^{2}
if and only if

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}d\mu(x)\in L^{p/2}.

The basic step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
was the following asymptotic result for lacunary series:

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
there exists, for every
\varepsilon>0
, an increasing sequence
(n_{k})
of positive integers such that

(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\leq\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}X_{n_{i}}\|_{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})

for every
k\geq 1
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where

(1.4)

\rho_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}Y_{i}\|_{p}.

Clearly,
\rho_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is a symmetric function and thus Theorem
1.2
implies the results on almost symmetric sequences in
L_{p}
spaces in
[
7
,
15
,
16
]
.

Note that for
p\geq 2
the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
imply that the limit distribution
F
of
(X_{n})
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{p}dF(x)<\infty
, and thus

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{p}d\mu(x)\right)<\infty,

which implies (
1.3
) by the monotonicity of the
L^{p}
norm. Actually, for
p\geq 2
the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
is easily to prove and the statement was known
in the literature as early as in the 1930’s.

The purpose of the present paper is to extend Theorem
1.2
for general nonlinear functionals
f_{k}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{k}X_{n_{k}})
of lacunary sequences
(X_{n_{k}})
. We will namely prove the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X_{n})
be a determining sequence of r.v.’s
with limit exchangeable sequence
(Y_{n})
with limit random measure
\mu
having mean 0 a.s. and expectation satisfying

(1.5)

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}d\mu(x)\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

Let
(S,d)
denote the space of probability measures with mean
0
and finite second moment equipped with the Wasserstein
W_{2}
metric

d(\nu,\lambda)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}(F_{\nu}^{-1}(x)-F_{\lambda}^{-1}(x))^{2}dx\right)^{1/2},

where
F_{\nu}
and
F_{\lambda}
denote the distribution functions belonging to
\nu
and
\lambda
.
Let
f_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
(
k=1,2,\ldots)
be nonnegative measurable functions satisfying the condition

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}f_{k+1}(a_{1}x_{1},a_{2}x_{2},\ldots,a_{j-1}x_{j-1},a_{j}t,a_{j+1}\xi_{j+1}^{(\nu)},\ldots,a_{k}\xi_{k}^{(\nu)})

\displaystyle\phantom{999999999999}-\mathbb{E}f_{k+1}(a_{1}x_{1},a_{2}x_{2},\ldots,a_{j-1}x_{j-1},a_{j}t^{\prime},a_{j+1}\xi_{j+1}^{(\lambda)},\ldots,a_{k}\xi_{k}^{(\lambda)})|

(1.6)

\displaystyle\leq a_{j}|t-t^{\prime}|+\mathbb{E}f_{k}(a_{1}Y_{1},\ldots,a_{k}Y_{k})\,d(\nu,\lambda)

for every
k\geq 1
,
1\leq j\leq k
,
\nu,\lambda\in S
, real numbers
t,t^{\prime},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1}

and
i.i.d.
sequences
(\xi_{n}^{(\nu)}),(\xi_{n}^{(\lambda)})
with
respective distributions
\nu
and
\lambda
. (The finiteness of the expectations in (
1.3
) is assumed here.)
Define, generalizing (
1.4
),

(1.7)

\rho_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}):=\mathbb{E}f_{k}(a_{1}Y_{1},\ldots,a_{k}Y_{k}),

and assume that

(1.8)

\rho_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\geq c|a_{j}|\ \ 1\leq j\leq k

for some constant
c>0
.
Assume finally that
\{|X_{n}|,\,n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable and

\mathbb{E}f(a_{1}X_{1},\ldots a_{n}X_{n})<\infty\ \ \text{for all}\ \ n\geq 1\ \ \text{and}\ \ (a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then there exists, for every
\varepsilon>0
, a subsequence
(X_{n_{k}})
satisfying

(1.9)

(1-\varepsilon)\rho_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\leq\mathbb{E}f_{k}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{k}X_{n_{k}})\leq(1+\varepsilon)\rho_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})

for any
k\geq 1
and any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
.

By de Finetti’s theorem, exchangeable sequences are conditionally i.i.d. with respect to their tail
\sigma
-algebra and consequently they satisfy all limit theorems of i.i.d. random variables in a mixed form. By a heuristic principle
(’subsequence principle’)
, formulated by Chatterji in
[
13
]
,
the same holds for sufficiently thin subsequences of any sequence of r.v.’s satisfying norm boundedness conditions. Limit theorems for a sequence
(X_{n})
of random variables are asymptotic results for a sequence of functionals
f_{k}(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots)

(k=1,2,\ldots)
of the sequence
(X_{n})
and under suitable technical assumptions on the functionals
f_{k}
, Aldous
[
2
]
gave a rigorous proof of
the subsequence principle.
Note that relation (
1.9
) in Theorem
1.3
is also an asymptotic relation, except that it involves weighted functionals
f_{k}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{k}X_{n_{k}})
and the limit relation is uniform in
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)
, a fact crucial for applications in Banach space theory.
The uniformity of the result is guaranteed by the equicontinuity relation (
1.3
).

In the case when
f_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=|x_{1}+\ldots+x_{k}|
, the equicontinuity relation (
1.3
) was verified in
[
10
]
using the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality

(1.10)

C\|\xi\|_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\xi_{i}\right\|_{p}\leq\|\xi\|_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for
p=1
; here
(\xi_{n})
is an i.i.d. sequence with mean zero, and finite second moment. This yields Theorem 1.2 for
p=1
. For
f_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=|x_{1}+\ldots+x_{k}|^{p}
,
1\leq p<2
, an estimate for the left hand side of (
1.3
) is given in
[
10
]
, page 2068, but this estimate is of a different form as (
1.3
). Thus, purely formally,
Theorem
1.2
is not a special case of Theorem
1.3
for
1<p<2
. Still, the proof of Theorem
1.3
works in this case, since by the estimate for
|g^{{\bf a},\ell}(t,\nu)-g^{{\bf a},\ell}(t^{\prime}\ \nu^{\prime})|
in
[
10
]
, p. 2061 the class of functions in (
2.10
) satisfies assumptions (a), (b) of the subsequent Lemma
2.1
, and this is all we need for the argument.

For nonlinear functionals
f_{k}
the verification of (
1.3
) is generally technically complicated, but intuitively it is clear that (
1.3
) holds for a very large class of functionals
f_{k}
. Note that (
1.3
) holds trivially for
f_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|x_{j}|.

A further interesting consequence of Theorem 1.3 is the following

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\psi
be a Young function with
x\ll\psi(x)\ll x^{p}
,
p\in[1,2)
and let
(X_{n})
be a determining sequence such that
\|X_{n}\|_{L^{\psi}}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, ,
\{\psi(|X_{n}|),n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable and
X_{n}\to 0
weakly in
L^{\psi}
. Let
\mu
be the limit random measure of
(X_{n})
.
Then there exists a subsequence
(X_{n_{k}})
equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell^{2}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}d\mu(x)\in L^{\sqrt{\psi}}.

We also note that under
\psi(x)\gg x^{p}
,
p>2
, Kadec-Pełczynski-type results also hold under the
L^{\psi}
norm, see e.g
[
1
,
22
]
.

By a result of Komlós
[
21
]
, every sequence
(X_{n})
of random variables with bounded
L_{2}
norms satisfying
X_{n}\to 0
weakly in
L_{2}
has a subsequence
(X_{n_{k}})
which is an unconditional convergence sequence, i.e.
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}X_{n_{k}}
converges a.s. after any permutation of its terms provided
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}^{2}<\infty
. This was an unsolved problem originating from the 1930’s, see Ulyanov
[
32
]
, p. 54. A simple proof of Komlós’ result was given by Aldous
[
2
]
, using an equicontinuity argument. A similar method was used in
[
11
]
to prove uniform limit theorems for normed lacunary sums
A_{N}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}X_{n_{k}}
with limit distribution having Fourier transform
\exp(-c|t|^{\alpha})
,
0<\alpha<2
,
c>0
.

We follow the argument in

We will need the following

Lemma 2.1

(Ranga Rao

Let now

, that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}t,a_{2}\xi_{2}^{(\nu)},\ldots,a_{\ell}\xi_{\ell}^{(\nu)})|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(0,a_{2}\xi_{2}^{(\nu_{0})},\ldots,a_{\ell}\xi_{\ell}^{(\nu_{0})})|+|a_{1}t|+|\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}Y_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})|d(\nu,\nu_{0})

\displaystyle=|a_{1}t|+|\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}Y_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})|d(\nu,\nu_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\ell}({\bf{a}})|t|+\rho_{\ell}({\bf{a}})d(\nu,\nu_{0}),

where
\nu_{0}
denotes the probability measure concentrated at 0 and thus the
\xi_{j}^{(\nu_{0})}
are identically 0. Hence to verify assumption (b) of Lemma
2.1
we need to check that if
(X_{n},\nu)\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\rightarrow}(Y_{1},\nu)
, then

\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{n}|+d(\nu,\nu_{0})\right)\longrightarrow\mathbb{E}\left(|Y_{1}|+d(\nu,\nu_{0})\right).

This follows, however, from the uniform integrability of the
X_{n}
and the fact that
d(\nu,\nu_{0})=\mathbb{E}(\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}d\nu(x))^{1/2}<\infty
by (
1.5
).
We thus proved relation (
2.9
) and thus also
(
2.5
) whence it follows (note again that the right side of
(
2.5
) equals 1) that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}({\bf a})^{-1}\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}X_{n},a_{2}Y_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})

(2.12)

\displaystyle\to\rho_{\ell}({\bf a})^{-1}\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}Y_{1},a_{2}Y_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})\ \hbox{ as }\ n\to\infty

uniformly in
\ell,{\bf a}
. Hence we can choose
n_{1}
so large
that

\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},a_{2}Y_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})-\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}Y_{1},a_{2}Y_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})\big|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\rho_{\ell}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})

for every
{\bf a}\neq{\bf 0}
and
\ell\geq 1
. The last relation clearly holds for
\bf a={\bf 0}
, and thus the first induction step is completed.

Assume now that
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k-1}
have already been chosen.
Exactly in the same way as we proved (
2.11
), just putting
a_{k}X_{n}
at the
k
-th location,
it follows that for
\ell>k

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}({\bf a})^{-1}\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{k-1}X_{n_{k-1}},a_{k}X_{n},a_{k+1}Y_{k+1},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\rho_{\ell}({\bf a})^{-1}\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{k-1}X_{n_{k-1}},a_{k}Y_{k},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})\ \hbox{
as }\ n\to\infty

uniformly in
a
and
\ell
. Hence we can choose
n_{k}
so
large that
n_{k}>n_{k-1}
and

\displaystyle\big|\ \mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{k-1}X_{n_{k-1}},a_{k}X_{n_{k}},a_{k+1}Y_{k+1},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{k-1}X_{n_{k-1}},a_{k}Y_{k},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})\big|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}}\rho_{\ell}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})

for every
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
\ell>k
. This
completes the
k
-th induction step; the so constructed sequence

(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
clearly satisfies

\big|\ \mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}X_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{\ell}X_{n_{\ell}})-\mathbb{E}f_{\ell}(a_{1}Y_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}Y_{\ell})|\leq\varepsilon\rho_{\ell}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})

for every
\ell\geq 1
and

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.
The last relation is equivalent to (
2.4
) and thus Theorem
1.3
is proved.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since
\psi
is convex and non-decreasing, the right derivative
\psi_{+}^{\prime}(t)
exists for every
t>0
, and for

0<x<y
we have

\frac{\psi(y)-\psi(x)}{y-x}\leq\psi_{+}^{\prime}(y).

Moreover,

\psi_{+}^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{\psi(2t)-\psi(t)}{t}\leq\frac{\psi(2t)}{t}\ll t^{p-1},\qquad t>0.

Hence, for
0<x<y
,

\psi(y)-\psi(x)\ll(y-x)y^{p-1}.

Since

y^{p-1}\leq x^{p-1}+y^{p-1},

it follows that

\psi(y)-\psi(x)\ll|x-y|\bigl(x^{p-1}+y^{p-1}\bigr).

Interchanging
x
and
y
if necessary, we obtain

(2.13)

|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|\ll|x-y|\bigl(x^{p-1}+y^{p-1}\bigr),\qquad x,y>0.

Letting

(2.14)

h^{{\bf a},l}(t,\nu)=\mathbb{E}\psi\bigl(|a_{1}t+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}a_{i}\xi_{i}^{(\nu)}\bigr|\bigr),

relation (
2.13
) implies that the estimates for
|g^{{\bf a},l}(t,\nu)|
and
|g^{{\bf a},l}(t,\nu)-g^{{\bf a},l}(t^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})|
obtained in

[
10
]
, page 2060 remain valid for
|h^{{\bf a},l}(t,\nu)|
and
|h^{{\bf a},l}(t,\nu)-h^{{\bf a},l}(t^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})|

and consequently the class of functions

\left\{\frac{h^{{\bf a},\ell}(t,\nu)}{\|{\bf a}\|_{2}^{p}}\right\}

satisfies conditions (a) and (b) of Lemma 2.1. Thus the induction procedure on pp. 2060-2062 of
[
10
]
remains valid and leads to a subsequence
(X_{n_{k}})
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\mathbb{E}\psi\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}X_{n_{i}}\right|\right)\overset{1+\varepsilon}{\approx}\mathbb{E}\psi\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}Y_{i}\right|\right),

where the symbol
\overset{1+\varepsilon}{\approx}
means that the ratio of the two sides is
between
1-\varepsilon
and
1+\varepsilon
.
Replacing
\psi(x)
by
\psi(x/C)
for any fixed
C>0
means only a scale change and thus the subsequence
(X_{n_{k}})
provided by the induction procedure will satisfy
also

\mathbb{E}\psi\left(\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}X_{n_{i}}\right|}{C}\right)\overset{1+\varepsilon}{\approx}\mathbb{E}\psi\left(\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}Y_{i}\right|}{C}\right)

for all
C>0
simultaneously. Let now

f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}X_{n_{i}},\qquad g=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}Y_{i}.

Since
\psi
is continuous, if
C^{*}
denotes the
L^{\psi}
-norm of
g
, then

\mathbb{E}\,\psi\!\left(|g|/C^{*}\right)=1,

and thus

(2.15)

1-\varepsilon\leq\mathbb{E}\psi(|f|/C^{*})\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Let
C^{**}
denote the
L^{\psi}
norm of
f
, then

(2.16)

\mathbb{E}\psi(|f|/C^{**})=1.

Let

G(c):=\mathbb{E}\psi(c|f|),\qquad a:=\frac{1}{C^{**}},\qquad b:=\frac{1}{C^{*}}.

Then

G(a)=1,\qquad 1-\varepsilon\leq G(b)\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Since
G
is convex, which follows from the convexity of
\psi
and since
G(0)=0
, the function

c\mapsto\frac{G(c)}{c}

is nondecreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Indeed, for
0<x<y
, and by convexity of
G
,

G(x)=G\!\left(\frac{x}{y}\,y+\left(1-\frac{x}{y}\right)0\right)\leq\frac{x}{y}G(y)+\left(1-\frac{x}{y}\right)G(0)=\frac{x}{y}G(y).

Dividing by
x>0
, we obtain

\frac{G(x)}{x}\leq\frac{G(y)}{y},

which proves the claim.

If
b\leq a
, then

\frac{G(b)}{b}\leq\frac{G(a)}{a}=\frac{1}{a},

so

1-\varepsilon\leq G(b)\leq\frac{b}{a}=\frac{C^{**}}{C^{*}}\leq 1.

If
b\geq a
, then

\frac{G(b)}{b}\geq\frac{G(a)}{a}=\frac{1}{a},

so

1\leq\frac{C^{**}}{C^{*}}=\frac{b}{a}\leq G(b)\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Therefore,

1-\varepsilon\leq\frac{C^{**}}{C^{*}}\leq 1+\varepsilon,

i.e. the ratio of the
L^{\psi}
norm of
f
and
g
is between positive constant bounds.
In other words,

(2.17)

K_{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}Y_{i}\Bigr\|_{L^{\psi}}\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}X_{n_{i}}\Bigr\|_{L^{\psi}}\leq K_{2}\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}Y_{i}\Bigr\|_{L^{\psi}}

for all
k\geq 1
and all
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with some positive constants
K_{1},K_{2}
.
We now claim

(2.18)

C\|Y\|_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}Y_{i}\right\|_{L^{\psi}}\leq\|Y\|_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for some constant
C>0
. In the case when the
Y_{j}
are i.i.d. random variables with mean 0 and finite variance, the upper bound in (
2.18
) follows from the fact that by the relation
x\ll\psi(x)\ll x^{2}
and the embedding relation (
1.2
), the
L^{2}
norm dominates the
L^{\psi}
norm. The lower bound, on the other hand, follows from the fact that the
L^{\psi}
norm dominates the
L^{1}
norm, thereby reducing the problem to the already proven inequality (
1.10
) for
p=1
. Since the limit exchangeable sequence
(Y_{j})
is conditionally i.i.d. with respect to its tail
\sigma
-algebra with conditional mean zero and conditional finite variance, the validity of (
2.18
) in the exchangeable case follows from the i.i.d. case by integration. Combining (
2.17
) and (
2.18
we get

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\ll\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}X_{n_{i}}\right\|_{L^{\psi}}\ll\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

and thus the subspace spanned by
(X_{n_{k}})
in
L^{\psi}
is equivalent to
\ell^{2}
.

The necessity part of Theorem 1.1 in
[
10
]
was deduced from the relation

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}X_{n_{k}}\right\|_{p}=O(1)

on p. 2062. Since by the above explanation the last relation remains valid under the Orlicz norm, the proof of the necessity part of Theorem
1.4
follows again the same way as in
[
10
]
.
∎
