Intra-articular delivery of VEGF targeting miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p ameliorates synovitis and pain in a rat model of post-traumatic knee osteoarthritis
Authors: Tatsumi Tanaka, Eriko Toyoda, Makoto Ogawa, Ryoka Uchiyama, Miyu Tamaki, Masahiko Watanabe, Masato Sato
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/objectiveKnee osteoarthritis (KOA) is the most common joint disorder and causes pain and functional limitation. Patients who are refractory to conservative treatment or unsuitable for surgery owing to advanced age or comorbidities have limited options. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is implicated in synovitis, angiogenesis and pain in KOA. microRNA (miRNA) based therapies have recently attracted attentions. This study aimed to investigate whether intra-articular delivery of VEGF-targeting miRNA can reduce synovitis and pain.MethodsHuman synovial sarcoma cell line HS-SY-II was used for screening of VEGF-targeting miRNAs and primary synovial fibroblasts derived from KOA patients was used for confirmation of the efficacy. miRNA mimics were transfected and VEGF production was quantified. In vivo analyses, KOA was induced by anterior cruciate ligament transection and medial meniscectomy (ACLT model). Wistar rats were randomly allocated to 5 groups in 2 cohorts (n = 5 per group per cohort): normal (no ACLT), ACLT + negative control miRNA (NC), ACLT + miR-126-3p, ACLT + miR-140-5p, and ACLT + a combination of miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p (miR-Mix). miRNA mimics were injected intra-articularly. Hindlimb weight-bearing was assessed using incapacitance tester. Synovitis and cartilage degeneration were evaluated histologically. VEGF levels in synovial fluid, synovium and cartilage were quantified by Simple Western analysis. Non-parametric tests and repeated-measures analyses were performed, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.ResultsCombination of miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p suppressed VEGF production in HS-SY-II cells than either miRNA alone and the suppressive activity on VEGF production was confirmed in primary synovial fibroblasts from KOA patients. In ACLT rats, synovial VEGF levels were increased in NC group compared with normal rats and tended to be lower in all miRNA-treated groups. Hindlimb weight-bearing ratios were reduced to about 30% after ACLT and remained low in the NC group, whereas they gradually improved in the miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p monotherapy groups and almost recovered in the miR-Mix group. At final time point, weight-bearing ratio was significantly higher in miR-Mix group than NC group. Histologically, Krenn scores were tend to lower in miR-126-3p and miR-Mix groups than NC group, indicating amelioration of synovitis, whereas OARSI histopathology scores for femoral cartilage did not differ significantly among groups and tended to be higher in miR-treated groups. There was a strong negative correlation between Krenn synovitis score and hindlimb weight-bearing ratio (Spearman's ρ = −0.7, p < 0.001).ConclusionIntra-articular administration of VEGF-suppressive miR-Mix ameliorated synovitis and pain-related behaviour. In highly unstable traumatic model, however, structural protection of articular cartilage was not observed and cartilage degeneration even tended to be worse in miR-treated groups, possibly due to increased weight-bearing following pain relief.The translational potential of this articleThese findings suggest intra-articular delivery of VEGF-suppresive miRNAs may represent novel analgesic strategies for KOA by reducing synovitis within joint. miRNA-based modulation of VEGF production could potentially be translated into future disease-modifying and analgesic treatments, however further studies using non-traumatic KOA models, safety evaluation and clarifying molecular mechanisms are required.

Introduction

Knee osteoarthritis (KOA) is the most common joint disorder and a leading cause of pain, stiffness and reduced activities of daily living in older adults [1]. Its global prevalence has been reported to exceed 10% [2]. The pathogenesis of KOA involves mechanical and inflammatory factors, including ageing, obesity-related cartilage wear, congenital or acquired joint deformities and post-traumatic inflammation [3].

Non-surgical treatment options are broadly divided into pharmacological and non-pharmacological approaches. Pharmacological options include oral analgesics and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, as well as intra-articular injections of hyaluronic acid [4]. Non-pharmacological approaches include exercise therapy aimed at strengthening periarticular muscles, weight management through dietary interventions, use of braces or insoles, and gait modification with walking aids. Surgical options for advanced KOA include high tibial osteotomy and total knee arthroplasty [5]. However, these procedures are invasive and associated with potential complications such as bleeding, infection, stiffness and deep vein thrombosis, which can be life-threatening [6]. Moreover, some patients are medically unfit or unwilling to undergo surgery, highlighting an unmet need for novel non-surgical therapies.

At the molecular level, several mediators including Runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2), interleukin-1 (IL-1) and matrix metalloproteinase-3 (MMP-3) have been implicated in KOA progression [[7], [8], [9]]. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) has been reported to contribute to KOA progression through promotion of angiogenesis, osteophyte formation and synovial inflammation [[10], [11], [12]]. VEGF produced by synovial cells and chondrocytes is associated with joint pain and enhances the release of other inflammatory cytokines, thereby accelerating KOA [13]. Nagai et al. showed that systemic and intra-articular administration of the anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody bevacizumab inhibited OA progression and improved cartilage repair in animal models [14,15].

Recently, nucleic acid-based approaches, including small non-coding RNAs, have been investigated as potential therapies for KOA [[16], [17], [18]]. Among them, miRNAs are attractive because each miRNA can regulate multiple target genes within a signaling pathway, offering the potential for robust disease modulation [[19], [20], [21]]. Several studies have reported that specific miRNAs are upregulated or downregulated in KOA and may serve as therapeutic targets or biomarkers [16,[18], [19], [20], [21]]. For example, miR-140-5p has been shown to suppress OA progression by targeting transcription factors involved in cartilage degeneration and its expression is reduced in synovial fluid from patients with KOA in association with disease severity.

Using miRTarBase, we identified VEGF-targeting miRNAs and confirmed that miR-126-3p suppresses VEGF production in synovial cells. We further found that combining miR-126-3p with miR-140-5p more effectively suppresses VEGF production than either miRNA alone. Based on these findings, we hypothesised that intra-articular delivery of VEGF-targeting miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p would reduce synovitis and pain in KOA. Therefore, we investigated the effects of intra-articular miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p mimics in a rat ACLT model of post-traumatic KOA, focusing on VEGF expression, synovitis, cartilage degeneration and pain-related behaviour.

All experiments involving human tissue were conducted in accordance with the Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Biological Research Involving Human Subjects in Japan and were approved by the Ethics Committee of Tokai University (approval no. 09R-070). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. All animal experiments were performed in accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of Tokai University and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Tokai University (approval no. 222048).

In vitro analysis of VEGF suppression by miRNA induction

Cell lines and culture conditions

Human synovial sarcoma cell line HS-SY-II (RIKEN Cell Bank, #RCB2231) were used solely as an initial, standardised fibroblast-like cell-based platform for screening because they provide stable characteristics, minimal concern regarding phenotypic alterations caused by dedifferentiation, and lower variability for parallel transfection of multiple candidate miRNAs. Cells were seeded at 2 × 105 cells/well in Falcon 24 well plates and cultured in low-glucose DMEM containing pyruvate (Gibco #11885) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; NICHIREI BIOSCIENCES INC.) and Antibiotic–Antimycotic Solution (Fujifilm 161 23181) under standard conditions (37°C, 5% CO2). MiRIDIAN miRNA mimics were transfected using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Thermo Fisher Scientific) at a final concentration of 10 nM. After overnight incubation, the medium was replaced 48 h after transfection. Then, cells were cultured in the replaced medium for an additional 24 h, after which supernatants were collected for VEGF analysis.

As an OA model with clinical relevance, we employed primary synovial fibroblasts obtained from patients with KOA for the validation. The synovial fibroblasts were isolated from discarded synovial tissue obtained during total knee arthroplasty in patients with KOA. Synovial tissues were digested with 5 mg/mL collagenase type I (Worthington #CLS 1) in 20% FBS DMEM/F12 (Gibco #11320) at 37°C for 2 h with shaking. Isolated cells were expanded for 3 passages in α MEM GlutaMAX (Gibco #32561) containing 10% FBS and Antibiotic–Antimycotic Solution (10% FBS MEM medium), and then cryopreserved in CELLBANKER 1 (Zenoaq) until use. For experiments, cells were seeded at 2 × 105 cells/well in 12 well plates and transfected with miRNA mimics or NC using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX. After overnight incubation, the medium was replaced and culture supernatants were collected on days 2, 4 and 8 for VEGF measurement. VEGF concentrations were measured using a Human VEGF SimpleStep ELISA kit (Abcam), and cell numbers were assessed with a CellTiter-Glo 2.0 Cell Viability Assay (Promega) to calculate VEGF production per cell.

Gene expression analysis

Primary synovial cells were seeded in 96-well culture dishes at a density of 2 × 104 cells per well. After transfection of miRNA mimics, the cells were cultured under normoxic or hypoxic conditions for 72 h. Cells were then lysed and cDNA was prepared using the TaqMan™ Gene Expression Cells-to-CT™ Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, cultured synovial cells were lysed and reverse transcribed, followed by quantitative PCR using TaqMan® Fast Advanced Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and TaqMan® Gene Expression Assays for VEGFA (Assay ID: Hs00900055_m1,Thermo Fisher Scientific) and for ACTB (Assay ID: Hs01060665_g1, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Real-time PCR was performed on QuantStudio3™ Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the ΔΔCt method after normalization to ACTB gene.

Quantification of miRNA expression

Primary synovial cells were seeded in 24-well culture dishes at a density of 2 × 105 cells per well. After transfection of miRNA mimics, the cells were cultured under standard conditions (37°C, 5% CO2). Cells were lysed 1, 4, 7, 14 days after transfection using QIAzol Lysis Agent (QIAGEN). Total RNA was extracted using miRNAeasy Mini kit (QIAGEN). RNA concentration was determined using a NanoDrop™ Lite spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The complementary DNA was synthesized using the TaqMan™ Advanced miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and miRNA expression levels were determined by quantitative PCR using TaqMan advanced miRNA assay (Applied Biosystems) for hsa-miR-106b-3p (Assay ID: 477866_mir), hsa-miR-140-5p (477909_mir), hsa-miR-126-3p (Assay ID: 477887_mir) and hsa-miR-140-3p (Assay ID: 477908_mir). Real-time PCR was performed on QuantStudio3™ Real-Time PCR System. Relative miRNA expression levels were calculated using the ΔΔCt method after normalization to hsa-miR-106b-3p which was selected as an internal control based on FANTOM5 miRNA expression profile.

Rat ACLT model of knee osteoarthritis

Fifty male Wistar rats (13 weeks old; Japan Clea, Tokyo, Japan) were acclimatized for 2 weeks under specific pathogen-free conditions with free access to food and water, housed 1–2 animals per cage. Rats were block-randomized into five groups based on body weight. Ten rats were allocated as normal group. A total of 40 rats underwent surgical procedures to induce traumatic osteoarthritis.

Under isoflurane anesthesia with oxygen, the right knee joint was approached through a medial parapatellar incision (∼1 cm). With the knee flexed at 90°, the patella was dislocated laterally to expose the joint. The anterior cruciate ligament was transected with scissors, and the medial meniscus was removed using forceps (ACLT procedure). The patella was reduced and the joint capsule and skin were closed. In the normal group, the joint capsule was opened and closed without ligament transection or meniscectomy.

One week after ACLT, hindlimb weight-bearing ratio was measured using an incapacitance meter to ensure balanced allocation of animals. Rats were then randomised into 4 miRNA treated groups: NC, miR-126-3p, miR-140-5p and miR-Mix (miR-126-3p + miR-140-5p). Two cohorts (Group I and Group II) were used, each consisting of 25 rats (n = 5 per group), for a total of 50 rats. Group I was used to evaluate the distribution of miRNA mimics and in vivo effects of intra-articular miRNA mimic administration on VEGF expression. GroupII was used to examine the effect on weight-bearing ratio and to conduct histological evaluation of the joint in the ACLT model rats. miRNA mimics were complexed with in vivo jetPEI® (Polyplus Transfection; Funakoshi Japan) according to the manufacturer's protocol to achieve a final dose of 2 nmol/30 μL per injection. For the miR-Mix group, 1 nmol of each miRNA in 30 μL (total 2 nmol) was used. In vivo jetPEI is known to enhance delivery of nucleic acids to target tissues and has been used in various in vivo studies [[25], [26], [27]].

Intra-articular injection procedure

For intra-articular injections, a medial parapatellar skin incision (∼1 cm) was made on the right knee under isoflurane anesthesia. With the knee flexed to 90°, a 29 gauge needle was inserted just above the patellar tendon into the joint cavity, and 30 μL of the miRNA mimic solution was injected. The knee was then extended and the incision closed.

In vivo distribution of Cy5-labelled miRNA

In Group I, the intra-articular distribution and retention of miRNA mimics were evaluated using Cy5-labelled miRNA (2 nmol/30 μL; Horizon Discovery Ltd., USA). Immediately after intra-articular injection, fluorescence signals were monitored with an IVIS® Lumina imaging system (Lumina-II with near infrared excitation and emission filters; PerkinElmer, USA) under isoflurane anesthesia (2.5–3% at 3 L/min). Imaging was performed daily for 7 days after injection with the following settings: excitation 620 nm, emission 710 nm, exposure time 5 s, medium binning, f stop 2. Ten days after injection, the femur and synovium were harvested, embedded in OCT compound (Sakura Finetek Japan), cryosectioned at 7 μm using Cryofilm (Leica Microsystems, Japan) according to Kawamoto's method and examined by fluorescence microscopy BZ-X800 (Keyence, Japan) [28].

Simple Western analysis of VEGF expression

In Group I, rats received intra-articular injections of miRNA mimics at 2 and 4 weeks after ACLT and were euthanised at 5 weeks. Synovial fluid, synovium and cartilage were collected for VEGF analysis (Fig. 2a). Synovial fluid was obtained by injecting 100 μL of saline into the joint cavity and aspirating the lavage fluid as described previously after passively flexing and extending the knee joint 10 times [29]. The synovium was dissected from the joint capsule, and femoral articular cartilage was carefully shaved from the distal femur to avoid damaging the subchondral bone. Cartilage and synovial tissues were homogenised in Tris-based lysis buffers containing protease inhibitors (150 mM NaCl, 50 mM Tris–HCl pH 7.4; or 1 M NaCl, 10 mM EDTA, 50 mM Tris–HCl pH 7.4) and proteins were sequentially extracted according to Claudia et al. [30] Cartilage samples were further treated with RIPA buffer and sonicated using a Bioruptor (BM Bio, Japan) for 3 times with 30 s cycles. Protein concentrations were determined with a DC Protein Assay kit (Bio-Rad, Japan). For Simple Western analysis (WES; ProteinSimple, San Jose, CA, USA), all samples were concentrated by acetone precipitation and 300 ng of protein per lane was loaded using a 12–230 kDa separation module following the manufacturer's instructions. Anti VEGFA antibody (rabbit, Abcam ab214424) was used at 1:10, and horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti rabbit IgG (ProteinSimple detection module). Signals were quantified using Compass software (ProteinSimple), and VEGF peak areas were normalized to total protein using the Total Protein Assay (ProteinSimple).

Pain assessment (hindlimb weight-bearing test)

In Group II, miRNA-treated groups were received intra-articular administration once per week from 1 to 4weeks after ACLT. Hindlimb weight-bearing ratio was assessed twice weekly, from before surgery to 1-5 weeks after ACLT using an incapacitance meter (BrainScience Idea Co., Ltd., Japan), as described previously [31]. Rats were placed so that each hind paw rested on a separate force plate, and 10 measurements were recorded for each time point. All measurements were conducted by four investigators who were blinded to the group assignments. The weight-bearing ratio (%) of the affected limb was calculated as: affected limb weight (g)/[affected limb weight (g) + contralateral limb weight (g)] × 100.

Macroscopic cartilage assessment

In Group II, rats received weekly intra-articular injections of miRNA mimics from 1 to 4 weeks after ACLT and were sacrificed 10 days after the fourth injection. The distal femur was exposed, the joint surface was stained with indigo carmine and macroscopic cartilage damage was evaluated according to the method of Nomura et al. [32] Macroscopic image data were assigned randomized ID numbers, after which the scores were averaged. The extent and depth of cartilage damage on the distal femoral surface were assessed by three blinded observers.

Histological evaluation

The distal femur and synovium (Group II) were fixed in 20% formalin (Wako, Japan), femur were decalcified. Specimens were embedded in paraffin and sectioned at 3 μm. Sections were deparaffinised and stained with haematoxylin and eosin, Safranin O staining (0.1%).

Cartilage degeneration in the distal femur was graded according to a modified OARSI scoring system (grade and stage; total score 0–24, higher scores indicate more severe cartilage degeneration) [33]. Synovitis was evaluated using the Krenn scoring system (0–9 points; higher scores indicate more severe synovitis) [34]. Sections were assigned randomized ID numbers, all histological evaluations were performed by 3 blinded observers, after which the scores were averaged.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

For the detection of VEGF, antigen retrieval was performed using Target Retrieval Solution pH6 (DAKO) at 95°C for 20 min. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 0.3% hydrogen peroxide. Immunostaining was performed using ImmPRESS Anti-Mouse IgG Reagent HRP (Vector Laboratories) according to manufactures’ instructions. Briefly, sections were incubated with mouse anti-VEGF IgG1 (VG-1) (5 μg/mL; abcam (Cambridge, UK)) or mouse IgG1(abcam) at 4°C overnight. Following incubation with a HRP-conjugated secondary antibody, immunoreactivity was visualized using a DAB substrate. Sections were counterstained with haematoxylin. All images were obtained using a BZ-X810 fluorescence microscope (Keyence, Osaka, Japan).

Immunofluorescence staining

For the detection of CD31 and alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), antigen retrieval was performed using Target Retrieval Solution pH9 (Agilent-DAKO) at 95°C for 20 min. The sections were permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 in BlockAid Blocking Solution (ThermoFisher) for 10 min at room temperature. The sections were incubated overnight at 4°C with rabbit monoclonal antibody against CD31 (1:2000; EPR17259, abcam). After washing, the sections were incubated with Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:200) and FITC-conjugated mouse monoclonal anti-α-SMA (1:100; 1A4, Merck, Germany) for 1 h at room temperature. For isotype control, rabbit IgG (abcam) or mouse IgG2a-FITC isotype control (Merck) were used. Nuclei were counterstained with VECTASHIELD Hard Set Mounting Medium with DAPI (Vector laboratories). Images were acquired using BZ-X810 fluorescence microscope (Keyence, Osaka, Japan).

Image analysis

Quantification of immunofluorescence staining was performed using an AI-assisted image analysis workflow with BZ-X Analyzer software BZ-H4A (Keyence, Osaka, Japan) and Fiji/ImageJ 1.54t. Representative images were used to train the machine-learning algorithm to distinguish positive staining from background tissue. After automated segmentation, the positive area was measured and expressed as the percentage of total tissue area within the ROI. The same trained model and analysis settings were applied to all samples. The areas positive for CD31 andα-SMA were quantified using an AI-assisted image analysis and expressed as the percentage of the synovial tissue area. Synovial fluorescence images from NC group (n = 5), miR-Mix group (n = 5), and normal group (n = 4) were analysed exploratorily. CD31 was used as an endothelial/angiogenic marker, and α-SMA was used as a perivascular or mature vessel-associated marker. Signal-derived area metrics, CD31- α-SMA proximity, and an α-SMA coverage of CD31 index were summarized by group. The analysis was performed as an exploratory assessment using the current image dataset.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, USA) and EZR software(Saitama, Japan). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For comparisons of hindlimb weight-bearing ratio, histological scores and VEGF levels among groups, Kruskal–Wallis tests followed by Dunn's multiple comparisons test, Bonferroni-corrected tests were used, as appropriate. In vitro experiments were analysed using Mann–Whitney U test, Kruskal–Wallis tests and two-way repeated-measures ANOVA and adjusted using the Tukey post hoc test. Correlations between Krenn scores and weight-bearing ratios were evaluated using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Next, to validate the synergistic effect in a clinically relevant pathological context, we employed primary synovial fibroblasts obtained from patients with KOA. In primary synovial fibroblasts, miRNA mimics was introduced and VEGF production per 24 h period was assessed over time. miR-Mix significantly suppressed VEGF production on days 2, 4 and 8 after transfection compared with NC, demonstrating that the VEGF-suppressive effect of miRNA mimics in synovial cells persisted for at least 8 days (Fig. 1c). Concurrently decreased mRNA levels of VEGF gene were shown under both normal and hypoxic conditions by quantitative PCR (Fig. 1d). Furthermore, analysis of intra-cellular miRNA expression levels of miRNA-140-5p and miRNA-126-3p over time by quantitative PCR indicated that the intracellular level of both miRNAs in miR-Mix group increased by over 200-fold compared with NC group and subsequently, the level decreased to approximately one-fifth of the peak level by one week and remained approximately 30-fold higher than basal levels even at two weeks after treatment (Fig. 1e).

In vivo distribution and tissue uptake of miRNA mimics

To assess the effect of intra-articular delivery of VEGF-suppressive miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p on synovitis and pain in KOA, we conducted intra-articular administration of miRNA mimics to the rat ACLT model because VEGF-A production in intra-articular tissues is known to be increased in this model [35]. Immediately after intra-articular injection of Cy5-labelled miRNA mimics, strong fluorescence was observed around the injected (right) knee joint by IVIS imaging (Fig. 2b). The fluorescence signal decreased the following day and was no longer detectable in the joint region 1 week after injection (Fig. 2b). At 10 days after injection, fluorescence microscopy of frozen sections revealed Cy5 -positive signals in the articular cartilage surface of the distal femur and within the synovium of the injected knee, indicating uptake of miRNA mimics into these tissues (Fig. 2c,d). No fluorescence was detected in the contralateral femur or synovium. These findings suggest that although miRNA mimics are rapidly cleared from the joint cavity, miRNAs taken up by cartilage and synovial cells persist for at least 10 days consistent with the findings of in vitro transfection to synovial cells (Fig. 1e).

Effects of intra-articular miRNA mimics on VEGF expression in joint tissues

In ACLT rats of Group I, miRNA mimics were administered intra-articularly at 2 and 4 weeks after surgery, and tissues were collected at 5 weeks (Fig. 2a). Synovial VEGF levels in the NC group were elevated compared with the Normal group, confirming ACLT-induced upregulation of VEGF. In contrast, the miR-126-3p, miR-140-5p and miR-Mix groups tended to show lower VEGF levels in synovial tissue than the NC group, indicating a suppressive effect of intra-articular miRNA administration on synovial VEGF production (Fig. 2e).

Discussion

To our knowledge, although numerous studies have reported the therapeutic efficacy of the single miRNA administration in KOA [[16], [17], [18]], this is the first report demonstrating that combinatorial miRNA administration can enhances their therapeutic efficacy. If the combined administration of multiple microRNAs becomes feasible, the ability to combine microRNAs with distinct biological functions may provide a foundation for personalized, tailor-made therapeutic strategies based on each patient's condition.

The mechanisms of pain in KOA involve multiple inflammatory mediators, including IL- 1, cyclo-oxygenase-2 (COX-2) and IL-8 [36]. Articular cartilage is aneural and is therefore unlikely to be the primary source of pain [37]. Instead, synovial tissue, which is richly innervated and vascularised, is thought to play a central role. Synovitis can stimulate nociceptive nerve fibres and induce pain [38]. Indeed, moderate to severe synovitis is frequently observed on MRI and arthroscopic biopsies of knees from patients with KOA [39]. Synovitis has been implicated not only in pain but also in structural progression of KOA, making it an important therapeutic target [40]. In PRP therapy on KOA, it has reported that higher VEGF concentration in PRP results in poor clinical outcome [41]. In the present study, through the combined intra-articular administration of miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p significantly ameliorated synovitis and improved weight-bearing, whereas structural protection of articular cartilage was not achieved. Our main findings can be summarized as follows: (i) candidate miRNA screening in a fibroblast-like cell line; (ii) validation of synergistic effect of miRNA combination in primary KOA synovial fibroblasts; (iii) intra-articular biodistribution after local delivery; and (iv) the dissociation between anti-synovitis/pain-related effects and structural cartilage outcomes in a post-traumatic model. Intra-articularly injected Cy5-conjugated miRNA mimics were taken up by synovium and cartilage and persisted within these tissues for at least 10 days. Although the possibility of deconjugation of the fluorescent dye was excluded, VEGF production in synovium was reduced in miRNA-treated groups compared with NC suggests the miRNA mimics are considered to retain its activity. Synovitis scores were tend to be improved in the miR-126-3p and miR-Mix groups and pain-related behaviour improved, particularly in the miR-Mix group, with a strong negative correlation between synovitis severity and weight-bearing ratio. Although the exploratory findings in immunostaining of synovial tissue should be interpreted with caution due to the lack of statistical significance, the observed trend of CD31 directionally consistent with the previously demonstrated VEGF-suppressive effects of miR-Mix and may indicate a potential inhibitory effect on VEGF-dependent angiogenesis.

In contrast, the preserved αSMA-positive structures and CD31-αSMA association suggest that mature vessel-like structures were not depleted. These findings support the possibility that miR-Mix may attenuate VEGF-dependent angiogenic processes, as reflected by the reduced CD31-positive vascular signal.

Role of VEGF in synovitis and pain

Inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1 and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and matrix metalloproteinases including MMP-3 and MMP-13 released from chondrocytes and synovial cells, contribute to synovitis in KOA [40].VEGF, a key angiogenetic factor, is also produced by these cells and promotes osteophyte formation and synovial inflammation, thereby accelerating KOA [42]. Among VEGF family members (VEGF A, VEGF B, VEGF C, VEGF D and placental growth factor), VEGF A is believed to be the principal isoform involved in KOA [43]. Therefore, we targeted VEGF A using miRNAs. Previous studies have demonstrated that inhibition of VEGF signaling can alleviate pain and cartilage degeneration in OA models [[10], [11], [12], [13], [14], [15]]. Takano et al. reported that suppression of VEGF production produced more effective analgesia than other interventions [44]. In our study, strong suppression of VEGF production was observed in vitro, and a suppressive trend was observed in vivo. Although not statistically significant, the expression of angiogenic markers in synovial tissue showed a trend toward reduced angiogenesis in miR-Mix group. The strong negative correlation between Krenn score and weight-bearing ratio suggests that pain-related behaviour correlated more closely with synovitis severity than with cartilage degeneration in this model. Inconsistent with the previous report of Nagai et al. , that reported protective effect of anti-VEGF antibody on surgical KOA model [14,15],no significant histological alteration were detected in the cartilage. Liao et al. reported that synovitis-induced NGF release sensitize nociceptive receptors and aggravates pain in KOA [45]. Pain sensitization is increasingly recognized as a critical determinant of OA associated pain and involves not only peripheral changes within the joint but also alterations in the dorsal root ganglia (DRG) and spinal cord. Therefore, future studies evaluating both peripheral and central sensitization mechanisms, will be important for clarifying the mechanisms underlying the potential analgesic effects of miR-Mix.

miRNA-based therapy for KOA

Nucleic acid-based therapeutic approaches for KOA, including small non-coding RNAs such as miRNAs and tRNA-derived fragments, are being actively explored [[16], [17], [18]]. miRNAs can regulate multiple target genes and pathways, making them attractive therapeutic candidates [[19], [20], [21],[46], [47], [48]]. Shi et al. reported that miR-140-5p suppressed KOA progression by targeting transcription factors involved in cartilage degradation [49]. Fei et al. showed that repeated early intra-articular injections of miR-140-5p in a rat OA model effectively inhibited disease progression [48]. VEGF targeting miRNA-29a also reported has protective effects against synovitis in KOA models [50].

In our study, we used miRTarBase to identify VEGF-targeting miRNAs and confirmed that combining miR-126-3p with miR-140-5p enhanced VEGF suppression, both in vitro and in vivo, and intra-articular administration of these miRNAs improved pain associated behaviour. These findings support the concept that miRNA-based regulation of VEGF signaling in synovial tissue could be a promising approach for KOA pain management. Rong et al. reported that VEGFA derived from synovial tissue activates the MAPK pathway, thereby promoting the release of inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1, and COX-2, which in turn accelerates the progression of KOA [51]. The relationship between VEGF reduction and the amelioration of pain-related behavior in our study remains unclear, and clarification of the underlying molecular mechanisms is required before clinical application. Additionally, further studies on the direct target genes involved in combinatorial use of miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p, including 3′UTR validation, rescue experiments, and analysis of VEGF family-member specificity are also required.

Lack of structural cartilage protection and model considerations

Fei et al. reported that miR-140-5p exerts significant regenerative effects on cartilage in the early stages of knee osteoarthritis [48]. Zhou et al. reported that miR-126-3p may inhibit the progression of KOA by suppressing signaling pathways that promote chondrocyte apoptosis and inflammatory responses [52]. In our study, despite significant improvement in synovitis and pain associated behaviour, we did not observe structural protection of articular cartilage; OARSI scores did not differ significantly among groups and tended to be worse in the miR-Mix group. However, several methodological differences may account for the discrepancy between our findings and previous reports. For example, compared with the study by Fei et al. for miR-140-5p [48], differences in the administered dose (5 nmol vs. 2 nmol) and the delivery strategy, which employed miRNA agomirs, may have influenced therapeutic efficacy. Wilson et al. employed a mouse OA model for examination of miR-126-3p [53]. In addition, Shi et al. [49] and Zhou et al. [2,5]. administered exosomes derived from cells transfected with miR-126-3p or miR-140-5p, respectively. In these studies, exosome-mediated intracellular delivery may have enabled more efficient uptake of miRNAs by target cells, resulting in differences in intracellular miRNA levels, trafficking, stability, and degradation. By contrast, miRNA mimics in the present study were delivered using in vivo-jetPEI, cationic polymer-based transfection reagent, which is not expected to provide the tissue- or cell-selective delivery properties often attributed to exosomes. Differences in experimental design may also have contributed to the divergent outcomes. In the study by Fei et al., forced exercise was applied following surgical induction of OA, potentially reducing the influence of spontaneous locomotor activity on disease progression. In contrast, Shi et al. induced joint injury bilaterally, which may have resulted in a greater reduction in overall activity than in our unilateral model. Furthermore, differences in treatment timing and evaluation time points may also have influenced the observed therapeutic effects.

Taken together, differences in animal models, delivery methods, dosing regimens, treatment schedules, and outcome assessment protocols may collectively explain the lack of chondroprotective effects observed in our study. Timothy et al. reported that administration of the analgesic agent tanezumab in a rat model of meniscal injury resulted in accelerated cartilage damage [54]. They suggested that early pain relief, initiated within 2 weeks after model induction, improved gait impairment but might also increase the risk of cartilage degeneration. These findings are consistent with the results of the present study and may help explain the dissociation between pain relief and structural joint preservation observed in our model. Furthermore, the ACLT model used in this study is a highly unstable post-traumatic OA model in which ACL transection and medial meniscectomy lead to mechanical overload and direct impingement between the femur and tibia, resulting in rapid cartilage wear. As locomotor activity monitoring was not conducted in this study, further studies are needed to investigate the relationship between animal activity levels and cartilage damage. Also, there is a possibility of off-target effects of miR-Mix cause cartilage damage. To establish its clinical application as therapeutic agent, further evaluation on safety and potential adverse effects is essential.

In addition, therapeutic effects in other OA models include chemically induced models using monosodium iodoacetate (MIA) or collagenase, which produce OA without gross joint instability [55] need to be studied. The MIA model has been reported to more closely mimic the chronic course of human OA in terms of cartilage damage [56]. Hyaluronic acid injection has been shown to improve arthritis in MIA-induced OA [57]. Udo et al. reported that MIA induces arthritis and cartilage disorganization [58]. Wilson et al. reported that miR-126-3p acts on VEGF in synovial and bone tissues, attenuating synovitis and mitigating cartilage degeneration [53]. In future studies evaluating intra-articular miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p, using non-traumatic models such as the MIA model and avoiding direct collision between the femur and tibia may yield different results regarding cartilage structure.

Safety considerations and limitations

VEGF-targeting therapies such as bevacizumab are widely used in oncology and ophthalmology but are associated with systemic adverse effects, including thromboembolic events, hypertension and organ toxicities [59]. Local intra-articular administration of miRNAs that selectively suppress VEGF in synovial tissue may offer a safer alternative, but potential off-target effects of miRNA therapies must be carefully considered. Combinations of miRNAs may increase the risk of off-target effects because each miRNA regulates multiple genes. Although, no severe adverse reactions were observed in our study, our combinatorial miRNA approach, while effective in suppressing VEGF, may require further optimisation to balance efficacy and safety.

This study has several limitations. First, the present study should be regarded as a proof-of-concept study for local treatment; not as evidence of clinical readiness and long-term efficacy or safety were not evaluated. We did not extensively optimise the dose and dosing intervals of miRNA mimics; different regimens may produce stronger or more durable effects. Second, we assessed weight-bearing only over a relatively short period and did not evaluate long-term pain behaviour. Third, potential long-term adverse effects of miRNA administration, including immunogenicity and off-target gene regulation, as well as systemic and local uptake, and direct context-dependent effects of the miRNA combination in subchondral bone or other vascular joint tissueswere not investigated. Fourth, we did not directly quantify activity levels; thus, we could not formally assess whether pain relief led to overuse of the joint.

Fifth, the involvement of reduction in VEGF to alleviation of pain remains to be elucidated. The angiogenic and nociceptive pathway analysis should be done as an important next step by accurately evaluating in vivo. The mechanism underlying the synergistic effects of the miRNA combination on VEGF production using luciferase reporter assays and other methods also needs to be clarified. Finally, extrapolating findings from a traumatic ACLT model to primary KOA in humans should be done with caution. Future studies using non-traumatic OA models and long-term follow-up are needed to clarify the structural impact and safety of repeated intra-articular miRNA administration.

Conclusion

In this study, VEGF-suppressive miR-126-3p and miR-140-5p mimics were administered intra-articularly to rats with ACLT-induced KOA. Intra-articular miRNA delivery increased hindlimb weight-bearing ratios and significantly improved synovitis, while cartilage structure was not protected and even tended to be worse in the miR-Mix group. These findings suggest that intra-articular miRNA therapy can alleviate synovitis-associated pain in a traumatic KOA model by suppressing VEGF production in synovial tissues, but further optimisation of dosing, model selection and safety evaluation is required before clinical translation.
