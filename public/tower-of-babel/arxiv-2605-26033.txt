Lattice point counting problems on step-two nilpotent Lie groups
Sheng-Chen Mao

Abstract




Abstract.

We develop the theory of lattice point counting on connected and simply connected nilpotent Lie groups of step-two, endowed with the parabolic type dilation and a family of homogeneous norms
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x,t)=\left(|M_{1}x|^{\alpha}+|M_{2}t|^{\alpha/2}\right)^{1/\alpha}
adapted to the dilation structure, where
\alpha>0
and
M_{1},M_{2}
are invertible matrices. With appropriate notions of lattices, the domains to be counted are balls associated to these norms, and explicit counting discrepancy estimates are deduced for all possible dimensions and all
\alpha>0
. The bounds are sharp when the group center is unidimensional and
\alpha=2
, in certain rational sense. Our study also generalizes and even quantitively improves previous results on Heisenberg groups obtained by Garg–Nevo–Taylor
[
17
,
Ann. Inst. Fourier
, 2015]
:
(i) In dimension
5
, the exponent of logarithmic factor is lowered from
2/3
to
{1}/{3}
if
\alpha\in(3,4)
or
\alpha=1
; and the factor
\log^{2/3}R
is dropped if
\alpha=4
(i.e., the Cygan–Korányi norm case) or
\alpha\in(2,3]
. (ii) In dimension
3
, the estimation is upgraded from
O_{\epsilon}(R^{5/2+\epsilon})
to
O(R^{2}\log^{1/2}R)
for
\alpha=1
, and to
O(R^{{19}/{8}})
for
\alpha\in(1,2)
; and the factor
\log R
is removed for
\alpha>4
. Moreover, as a byproduct, we extend the lattice counting near Heisenberg spheres, recently considered by Campolongo–Taylor
[
8
,
Matematica
, 2023]
and Srivastava–Taylor
[
58
,
J. Fourier Anal. Appl.
, 2026]
, to the above step-two group setting with arbitrary dimensional group center, where some quantitive improvements are also attained. Our method relies upon Poisson’s summation formulas, oscillatory integral estimates and asymptotic properties as well as recursion formulas of Bessel functions.

Introduction

The problem of lattice point counting is an important branch of analytic number theory, with a rich history of over 200 years; see
[
36
,
1
]
. Given an Euclidean ball
\mathcal{B}_{n}(r)
of radius
r>0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, one hopes to approximate the discrete quantity — the number of integer points contained in the ball, by the continuous quantity — its volume, for large
r
, in which the difficulty lies is to determine the correct order of the error term (called the
discrepancy
).
More specifically, we aim to establish the following asymptotic

\#\left({\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\cap r\,\mathcal{B}_{n}(1)\right)-r^{n}\operatorname{vol}(\mathcal{B}_{n}(1))=O_{\theta}(r^{\theta}),\qquad\mathrm{as}\,\,\,r\to\infty,

(1.1)

with
\theta<n
as small as possible,
where
\mathcal{B}_{n}(1)
denotes the standard unit closed ball and the infimum of all such
\theta
is called the
correct order
. This goal has been achieved for dimension
n\geq 4
, with the correct order equal to
n-2
. For the notorious cases of dimensions 3 and 2, also known as the sphere
problem and Gauss circle problem, the solutions remain wide open; and the correct orders are conjectured to be
1
and
1/2
, respectively. The best available exponents are
21/16
for
n=3
by D.R. Heath-Brown
[
30
]
, and
0.628966\cdots
for
n=2
by Li–Yang
[
43
]
.

It is natural to study similar problems for general domains
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in (
1.1
) besides balls
\mathcal{B}_{n}(1)
, under suitable conditions. Extensive researches have been done in this direction, and it continues to be an active area to this day. The reader is referred to
[
40
,
39
,
15
,
34
,
36
,
61
,
26
]
for the developments. We mention that when
\Omega
is compact convex with smooth boundary and nonzero Gaussian
curvature everywhere, the problem is well understood relatively; see
[
29
,
33
,
36
]
. However, it is not yet solved for any
n\geq 4
, where the conjectured correct order coincides with the ball case, i.e.,
n-2
. Regarding the case that the curvature may vanish, which includes
l^{p}
-norm ball (
p\in(2,\infty)
) as a canonical example, cf. e.g.
[
36
,
55
,
4
]
. For non-convex domain such as
l^{p}
-norm ball (
p\in(0,1)
); see e.g. the work of W.-G. Nowak
[
49
,
50
,
51
]
. There are also many other extensions of (
1.1
) with deep insights on the underlying groups and their lattices as well as the domains to be counted. See e.g.
[
24
,
25
,
2
]
and the references therein for lattice counting on topological groups. However, in such framework the correct order has not been determined even for any simple Lie group yet, to our knowledge. On the other hand, the research on nilpotent case seems rather limited, being restricted exclusively to the Heisenberg group
[
17
,
18
,
8
,
58
]
, etc, which is usually recognized as the simplest example of non-Abelian and non-compact Lie groups.

Nilpotent Lie groups constitute a basic subject of contemporary mathematical research, with connections to differential geometry (including sub-Riemannian geometry), complex analysis, non-commutative harmonic analysis and PDEs; see
[
41
,
22
,
3
,
14
,
13
]
and the references therein. This context, particularly the step-two case, has received much attention very recently; see e.g.
[
57
,
44
,
56
,
47
,
11
]
. In addition, nilpotent Lie groups and their discrete subgroups are significant in the investigation of semisimple Lie groups and arithmetic subgroups of linear semisimmple Lie groups, cf.
[
37
]
, which also can be traced back to many number-theoretic problems, especially in the modular function theory and the reduction theory of quadratic forms; cf. e.g.
[
48
,
54
,
53
]
.

In this paper, we endeavor to develop the lattice counting theory in nilpotent Lie groups, focusing mainly on the step-two class equipped with a homogeneous structure of parabolic type. As shall be seen, this setting affords a clear analogue of the classical lattice point counting problem, and makes it possible to achieve a complete understanding of the distribution of lattice points within a non-Euclidean frame, in the sense of obtaining the optimal discrepancy exponent, as in some known cases on Heisenberg groups.

Recall the Heisenberg group
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\times{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by the group law

(x,t)\circ(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\langle{\mathbb{J}}x,x^{\prime}\rangle\right)\quad\mbox{with}\quad{\mathbb{J}}:=2\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&{\mathbb{I}}_{d}\\
-{\mathbb{I}}_{d}&0\end{array}\right).

(1.2)

In 2015, by combining the Poisson summation formula, van der Corput lemma and the asymptotics of Bessel functions, Garg, Nevo and Taylor
[
17
]
examined an analogue of (
1.1
) on
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
and arrived at that, for all
R\geq 10
,

\left|\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{2d+1}\cap\mathscr{B}_{R}^{\alpha,A}\right)-\operatorname{vol}\left(\mathscr{B}_{1}^{\alpha,A}\right)R^{2d+2}\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}R^{2d},&\mbox{as}\ d\geq 1,\,\alpha=2;\\
R^{2}\log R,&\mbox{as}\ d=1,\,2<\alpha\leq 4;\\
R^{2+\frac{2\alpha-8}{3\alpha-4}}\log R,&\mbox{as}\ d=1,\,\alpha>4,\\
\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\mathscr{B}_{R}^{\alpha,A}:=\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{d}:|x|^{\alpha}+A|t|^{\alpha/2}\leq R^{\alpha}\}
with
\alpha,A>0
. Moreover, from a slicing argument and the Euler-MacLaurin formula, they also obtained that, for any
\alpha>0
,

\left|\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{2d+1}\cap\mathscr{B}_{R}^{\alpha,A}\right)-\operatorname{vol}\left(\mathscr{B}_{1}^{\alpha,A}\right)R^{2d+2}\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}R^{2d},&\mbox{as}\ d\geq 3;\\
R^{4}\log^{\frac{2}{3}}R,&\mbox{as}\ d=2;\\
R^{\frac{5}{2}+\epsilon},&\mbox{as}\ d=1,\\
\end{cases}

(1.4)

where the last estimate used implicitly the conjectured exponent for Gauss’s circle problem. Note that when
d=1
, the second estimate (
1.4
) is better than (
1.3
) as
\alpha>12
; while it is inferior to (
1.3
) as
\alpha\in(4,12]
. Fix
A=1
now. The bound in (
1.3
) is shown by
[
17
]
to be optimal as
\alpha=2
for any
d\geq 1
. In the Cygan–Korányi ball case (i.e. as
\alpha=4
) the task of finding the correct order turns out to be more intricate, and has been conducted by Y.A. Gath alone. In 2020, Gath
[
18
]
proved that the order
2
in (
1.3
) is the correct one for
d=1
by means of the Mellin transforms and Riesz mean estimates of
[
9
]
; later in 2022, for
d\geq 3
Gath
[
19
]
demonstrated that the correct order belongs to
[{2d-1},{2d-2/3}]
, enhancing the exponent
2d
in (
1.4
) substantially, and from a sharp second moment estimate he conjectured that
2d-1
should be the one. Gath’s method
[
19
]
is based on a delicate slicing argument, reductions to weighted integer lattice counting estimates in Euclidean domains, and technical tools in estimating exponential sums from analytic number theory.
As to
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
, there is no further progress beyond the initial result of
[
17
]
. Notwithstanding, in this work we shall make the first improvement by removing the logarithmic factor. Moreover, refinements for some other cases are also acquired and listed in Remark
1.3
.

It is also of independent interest to study the distribution of lattices near the hypersurface in nilpotent groups. Recently, Campolongo and Taylor
[
7
,
8
]
have inspected this topic for integer points near Heisenberg spheres, then Srivastava and Taylor
[
58
]
considered an average version along with the group translation. For the Euclidean counterparts, see e.g.
[
6
,
35
,
42
,
32
]
. As a byproduct of our results, the above regime of
[
7
,
8
,
58
]
can be extended to the step-two group setting; see Theorem
1.7
. The detailed comparison between their results and ours are provided in Remark
1.8
below. For other pertinent results concerning lattice point counting on Heisenberg groups, the reader can consult e.g.
[
46
,
5
,
20
,
21
]
.

1.1
Notation

We denote the set of natural numbers by
{\mathbb{N}}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
, and put
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}={\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
. The symbol
\pm
appearing in the summation serves to avoid repetition; e.g.,
\sum_{\pm}f_{\pm}h_{\pm}:=f_{+}h_{+}+f_{-}h_{-}.
Given a set
\Omega
, we use
\#(\Omega)
to denote its cardinality and
\chi_{\Omega}
for the associated characteristic function. The notation
\mathbf{1}\{E\}
stands for the indicator function of an event
E
, which equals
1
if
E
happens and
0
otherwise. We utilize
{\mathbb{I}}_{n}
to signify the
n
-th order identity matrix. The transpose of a matrix is written as
\cdot^{\mathsf{T}}.

This paper employs the usual asymptotic notation. The letter
C
and its possible variants are adopted to represent implicit positive constants which may change between lines. Let
w
be a non-negative function. We mean
|f|\leq Cw
by
f=O(w)
, and
f\lesssim w
(resp.
f\gtrsim w
) by
f\leq Cw
(resp.
f\geq C\,w
) when
f
is also non-negative. If both
f\lesssim g
and
f\gtrsim g
hold, we will write
f\sim g
for short. A subscript on which parameters these constants depend shall be specified (if needed) like
O_{\gamma},\lesssim_{\gamma},\sim_{\gamma}
.

1.2
Step-two nilpotent Lie groups

Let us first recall some basic facts of step-two nilpotent Lie groups; for more details the readers are referred to e.g.
[
3
,
14
,
13
]
. Suppose that
q,m\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
, and that
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
is a connected and simply connected Lie group whose real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
obeys that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\qquad\{0\}\neq[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subset\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}),

where

\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}=q,\,\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}=m
. We endow
\mathfrak{g}
with a natural parabolic dilation structure
\{\delta_{r}\}_{r>0}
as follows:

\delta_{r}(v_{1},v_{2}):=(r\,v_{1},r^{2}\,v_{2}),\quad\forall\,v_{1}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2},r>0,

(1.5)

such that each
\delta_{r}
is a morphism of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
This makes
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
a homogeneous group of step 2. One then can identify
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
and
\mathfrak{g}
via the Lie group exponential map. In this way,
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
can be viewed as
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with the following group law

\displaystyle(x,t)\circ(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}):=\left(x+x^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbb{U}}x,x^{\prime}\rangle\right),\quad(x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m},

(1.6)

where

\langle{\mathbb{U}}x,x^{\prime}\rangle:=(\langle U^{(1)}x,x^{\prime}\rangle,\ldots,\langle U^{(m)}x,x\rangle)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},

with
m
real
q
-th order matrices
U^{(j)}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard Euclidean inner product. In this coordinate, the identity coincides with the origin, and the inverse of
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
equals
(x,t)^{-1}=(-x,-t+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\mathbb{U}}x,x\rangle)
. The above dilation (
1.5
) on
\mathfrak{g}
induces the corresponding automorphism
\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{r}\}_{r>0}
on
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
:

\boldsymbol{\delta}_{r}(x,t):=(rx,r^{2}t),\qquad\forall\,(x,t)\in{\mathbb{G}}(q,m),r>0.

Under (
1.6
), a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
(called the
Jacobian basis
) is given by (see e.g.
[
3
, §3.2]
)

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{X}_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q}U_{i,j}^{(k)}x_{j}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}},\quad\mathrm{~T}_{l}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{l}},\qquad\mbox{for}\ i\leq q\ \mbox{and}\ l\leq m.\end{gathered}

(1.7)

Now we fix the (bi-invariant) Haar measure
\operatorname{vol}
on
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
lifted by the exponential map. Here are some examples of step-two groups.

Example 1.1
.

(i)

Heisenberg groups
. Taking
q=2d,\,m=1
and
{\mathbb{U}}:=2\,{\mathbb{J}}
in (
1.6
) reverts the well-known Heisenberg group
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
defined by (
1.2
). We also recall the polarized Heisenberg group
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}_{\mathrm{pol}}
with
{\mathbb{U}}
specified as

{\mathbb{J}}_{\mathrm{pol}}:=2\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
{\mathbb{I}}_{d}&0\end{array}\right)
. The isomorphism between
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
and
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}_{\mathrm{pol}}
is given by

(x,t)\mapsto\left((x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}),\ \frac{t}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\langle x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\rangle\right),\ \mbox{where}\ x:=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(ii)

Heisenberg-type groups
{\mathbb{H}}(2n,m)
. Let
q=2n
and
{\mathbb{U}}
satisfy the following condition:

•

U^{(j)}
is skew-symmetric and orthogonal for any
1\leq j\leq m
;

•

U^{(i)}U^{(j)}=-U^{(j)}U^{(i)}
for each
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
with
i\neq j
.

Then we obtain the Heisenberg-type groups
{\mathbb{H}}(2n,m)
, which contain the class of Heisenberg groups.
More discussions on
{\mathbb{H}}(2n,m)
can be found in
[
3
, §3.6 and §18]
.

(iii)

Free step-two Carnot groups
{\mathbb{F}}_{q,2}
. Suppose that
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
is fixed with
i>j
, and
S(i,j)
is the
q
-th order skew-symmetric matrix whose entries are
-1
in the
(i,j)
position,
+1
in the
(j,i)
position and 0 elsewhere. We set
m:=q(q-1)/2
, which equals exactly the number of such
S(i,j)
. Then the free step-two Carnot group
{\mathbb{F}}_{q,2}
is formed by taking
U^{(k)}(1\leq k\leq m)
in (
1.6
) to be these

m
matrices. The Lie algebra of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q,2}
is recognized as the most non-Abelian as possible, from which the attribute “free” comes. See
[
3
, §3.3 and §14]
for more details.

Throughout this paper, the number
Q:=q+2m
will be designated as the
homogeneous dimension
of
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
, whose topological dimension readily equals
q+m
. We mention that the nilpotency of
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
forces
q\geq 2
, as can be seen from (
1.6
).

Suppose
\alpha>0
and
M:=\operatorname{diag}(M_{1},M_{2})
, where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
q
-th and
m
-th order invertible matrices respectively. The homogeneous norm
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
on
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
is defined by

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x,t)=\left(|M_{1}x|^{\alpha}+|M_{2}t|^{\alpha/2}\right)^{1/\alpha},\quad\forall\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}(q,m),

(1.8)

which is
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{r}
-homogeneous of degree 1, that is,

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}\left(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{r}(x,t)\right)=r\,\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x,t),\qquad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}(q,m),\ r>0.

It is a quasi-norm which fits the inequality (cf. e.g.
[
13
, Proposition 3.1.38]
)

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}((x,t)\circ(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\leq C_{0}\,(\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x,t)+\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))

(1.9)

for some universal constant
C_{0}>0
. Nevertheless, any homogeneous Lie group can admit a quasi-norm
which satisfies the triangle inequality; see
[
13
, Theorem 3.1.39]
or
[
31
, THEOREM 1]
. In the case of Heisenberg-type group
{\mathbb{H}}(2n,m)
, the celebrated Cygan–Korányi norm, i.e., when
M_{1}={\mathbb{I}}_{2n},\,M_{2}=4\,{\mathbb{I}}_{m}
and
\alpha=4
in (
1.8
), serves as a typical example; cf. e.g.
[
3
, p. 705]
. We denote the
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
-ball
of radius
R>0
by

B_{R}^{\alpha,M}:=\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{G}}(q,m):\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x,t)\leq R\}.

Note that

\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\right)=\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{1}^{\alpha,M}\right)R^{Q},\quad\forall\,R>0.

(1.10)

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group. A discrete subgroup
\Gamma
of
{\mathbb{G}}
is called cocompact (resp. cofinite) if
\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{G}}
is compact (resp. of finite invariant measure). In fact, the two definitions are equivalent (see
[
54
, Theorem 2.1]
) in this case. Subsequently, a cofinite lattice will be simply referred to as the lattice. According to Mal’cev’s criterion (see
[
45
]
, and also
[
54
, Theorem 2.12]
),
{\mathbb{G}}
admits a lattice if and only if its Lie algebra admits a
basis carrying rational structure constants, that is,
[w_{i},w_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathbb{G}}}c^{k}_{ij}w_{k}
with rational
c^{k}_{ij}
, for any
w_{i},w_{j}
belong to this basis. Notice that not every nilpotent group can admit a lattice; for a counterexample, see
[
54
, Remark 2.14]
. On the other hand, as to our group
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
, from (
1.7
) and a simple evaluation it follows that

\left[{\mathrm{X}}_{i},{\mathrm{X}}_{j}\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{2}\left(U_{j,i}^{(k)}-U_{i,j}^{(k)}\right){\mathrm{T}}_{k},\quad[{\mathrm{X}},{\mathrm{T}}_{l}]=0,\quad\forall\,i,j\leq q,\,{\mathrm{X}}\in\mathfrak{g},\,\mbox{and}\ l\leq m.

This implies that
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
admits a lattice whenever each
U^{(j)}
has only rational entries, by means of Mal’cev’s criterion. As a result, Heisenberg groups and free step-two Carnot groups in Example
1.1
admit lattices. The existence of lattices on Heisenberg-type groups is ensured by
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, which states that the structure constants for
\mathfrak{g}
of
{\mathbb{H}}(2n,m)
can take values solely in
\{0,1,-1\}
by choosing suitable bases for
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.

A nature problem is to establish a complete classification for all kinds of lattices when
{\mathbb{G}}
admits ones, about which, however, little is known; see
[
16
]
for related development. In the case of Heisenberg groups, it is solved. As a matter of fact, the following subgroups

\Gamma_{b}:=\{((x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}),t)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{d}_{\mathrm{pol}}:x^{\prime}\in b\,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},x^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},t\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},\quad b\in({\mathbb{N}}^{*})^{d},\,b_{j}|b_{j+1},\,\forall\,j\leq d-1,

(1.11)

with
b\,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:=\{(b_{1}k_{1},\ldots,b_{d}k_{d}):(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\}
, classify all types of lattices of
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}_{\mathrm{pol}}
up to automorphism, cf.
[
23
, §2]
.

Within the present framework, the lattices of our interest are as follows. Let
L=\operatorname{diag}(L_{1},L_{2})
be a real matrix
with
L_{i}(i=1,2)
the
q,m
-th order invertible matrices respectively, such that the discrete set

\Gamma_{L}:=L{\mathbb{Z}}^{q+m}=\{(L_{1}k^{\prime},L_{2}k^{\prime\prime}):(k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{q+m}\}

(1.12)

constitutes a subgroup of
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
. This is possible; for instance, assuming each
U^{(j)}
consists of rational entries, then we can take
L_{2}
to be the form
L_{2}=\operatorname{diag}\left(l_{2,1},l_{2,2},\ldots,l_{2,m}\right)
, and choose an
L_{1}
so that
\frac{1}{2}\,l_{2,j}^{-1}L_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}U^{(j)}L_{1}
is integral matrix for any
j=1,2,\ldots,m
. It is routine to validate that the set
L\,[0,1)^{q+m}
is a fundamental domain for
\Gamma_{L}
, whose covolume thus equals
|\det(L)|\in(0,\infty)
. Hence
\Gamma_{L}
is indeed a lattice of
{\mathbb{G}}(q,m)
. We point out that, (
1.12
) have encompassed all forms of lattices given by (
1.11
) in the setting of
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}_{\mathrm{pol}}
, as easily verified, which are pivotal in the research of Weyl’s law on Heisenberg manifolds, cf. e.g.
[
60
,
38
,
52
]
.

Having laid the groundwork above, one is accordingly led to the following lattice point counting problem: given the ball family
B_{R}^{\alpha,M}
, how much do we know about the asymptotic behavior of

\#\left(\Gamma_{L}\cap B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\right),\qquad\mathrm{as}\ R\to\infty?

(1.13)

In this paper, we contribute a preliminary answer to this question. Going back to the simplest Heisenberg group case considered by
[
17
]
, note that here we have incorporated non-radial homogeneous norms and more general type of lattices; and importantly, we have improved upon the results of
[
17
]
in many respects (see Remark
1.3
). We also remark that owing to the global coordinate
{\mathbb{R}}^{q+m}
, the “lattice” counting problem (
1.13
) still makes sense, even when
\Gamma_{L}=L{\mathbb{Z}}^{q+m}
is not a subgroup of

1.4
Method and outline of this paper

The correct order of a lattice point problem is intimately tied to the geometry of the underlying domain. Unlike the classical Euclidean
l^{\alpha}
-norm ball (
\alpha>0
), the
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
-ball loses the rotational symmetry and instead display the anisotropy, which is compatible with the dilation structure of the underlying group. When
\alpha<4
, the spherical surface
\partial B_{1}^{\alpha,M}
fails to be of class
C^{2}
; and when
\alpha\geq 4
it is
C^{2}
, but the Gaussian curvature of
\partial B_{1}^{\alpha,M}
vanishes everywhere on the equator
\{t=0\}
as well as the pole
\{x=0\}
, which also could be of different dimensions and different degenerate behaviors. This feature exhibits a greater complexity than the degeneracy of
l^{\alpha}
-hypersphere (the boundary of
l^{\alpha}
-norm ball) in Euclidean spaces as
\alpha
varies; see
[
8
, §3]
for more details on
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
. So far, the correct order is known only for
\alpha=2,d\geq 1
and
\alpha=4,d=1
, in the Heisenberg group
{\mathbb{H}}^{d}
case. Note that for
l^{2}
-norm ball, the famous Gauss circle problem and sphere problem are still unsolved.

There are several existing approaches in the setting of Heisenberg groups. The methods of Gath
[
18
,
19
]
are effective but limited to only
\alpha=4
, which also heavily rely on the arithmetic structure of the Cygan–Korányi norm (such as the choice of slicing). So it seems difficult to apply them to general dimensions
q,m
, matrix
M
and index
\alpha
. Garg, Nevo and Taylor in
[
17
]
have introduced two methods for larger range of
\alpha
. The first uses classical Poisson’s summation formulas to transform the problem into the spectral estimates for Heisenberg balls. This method only analyzes the case
\alpha\geq 2
, where, in order to overcome the difficulty arising from the vanishing curvature, they utilized the radial property of the homogeneous norm, thereby reducing the high-dimensional oscillatory integral to an one-dimensional problem. Their second method uses a hyperplane slicing argument and results in Euclidean lattice point counting to turn the problem on Heisenberg groups into an one-dimensional summation estimation, which is then converted into an oscillatory integral via the Euler-MacLaurin formula.

Observe that the first method in
[
17
]
lacks of understanding for
\alpha<2
. In this regime, the
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
-ball is no longer convex; moreover, when
\alpha\in(0,1)
the homogeneous norm loses subadditivity; whereas for
\alpha\in(1,2)
the reduced one-dimensional oscillatory integral may admit degenerate stationary points — a situation that does not occur for
\alpha\geq 2
. As for the second method in
[
17
]
, the Euler-MacLaurin formula is not applicable to general step-two groups with higher central dimensions. On the other hand, when the central dimension of a step-two group increases, the one-dimensional integral (produced by radiality) will involve two Bessel functions, and the singularity of the integrand will become much more complicated. Recall that Bessel functions are a class of special functions with broad applications, whose great abstruseness of properties are prominent to mathematicians and physicists in their research; see e.g.
[
63
,
27
]
. Furthermore, another new difficulty as well as interesting feature is that, due to the anisotropy of our group structure, the concentrations of lattice points on the first layer
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
and the second layer
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
will be distinct, and when
q
and
m
are large enough, different characteristics should exhibit (see, for instance, (
2.5
) in Lemma
2.4
).
This point does not seem to be captured by the Heisenberg framework considered in
[
17
]
and
[
18
,
19
]
, nor by the methods therein. In the present paper, we barely give a rudimentary study for this aspect; one would like to revisit this issue in subsequent work to investigate discrepancy estimates uniformly in
q
and
m
.

Our method builds upon
[
17
]
and introduces some original ideas.
The novel ingredients include using a partition of unity to separate singularities of the amplitude, recurrence formulas for Bessel functions, and refined analysis of the phase functions appearing in the spectral estimates of the
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
-ball in conjunction with its anisotropy (which is not fully appreciated in
[
17
]
). These tools have a wider scope of applicability adapted to the homogeneous structure, which exploit the cancellation more efficiently along with the integration by parts, permitting us to extend the relevant results from Heisenberg groups to our step-two nilpotent Lie groups, and even to make many quantitative improvements as in Remark
1.3
; see also the beginning of Section
5
for further details. Moreover, such improvements naturally lead to further advances beyond the estimates for the lattice point counting near Heisenberg spheres of
[
8
,
58
]
, via a “ball-to-shell” argument as said in Remark
1.8
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we list some preliminary tools which is of immense importance to establish the main results. But the proof of Lemmas
2.2
-
2.4
will be postponed to Sections
4
and
5
respectively, in view of its lengthy content. In Section

we will first prove Theorem
1.2
via some classical process (including Poisson’s formulas and estimating the resulting oscillating integrals) in lattice point problems for
\alpha\geq 1
, where
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
is subadditive. And then in Subsection

we prove Theorem
1.4
by using a similar argument and the subadditivity of
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}^{\alpha}
as a replacement when
\alpha\in(0,1)
, where this moment
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
fails to retain this critical property, though. Next, in Subsection

we will only sketch the proof of Theorem

, which follows from a standard slicing argument in the literature. In Subsection

, we simply prove Remark

(2) by reductio ad absurdum, where the original concept
\boldsymbol{\delta}
-rationality for matrices, adapted to the homogeneous structure of our groups, comes into play. Incidentally, the
\boldsymbol{\delta}
-irrational case is fundamentally distinct, and has been partially discussed by the author and S. Yang in

In the last two sections, we derive the spectral estimates for the

-ball in Section

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
M
be invertible. Then the homogeneous norm
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
is subadditive with respect to the Euclidean addition, that is,

\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}((x,t)+(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\leq\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x,t)+\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),\qquad\forall\,(x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{G}}(q,m),

(2.1)

if and only if
\alpha\geq 1
. Moreover, the
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
-ball

B_{1}^{\alpha,M}
is Euclidean-convex if and only if
\alpha\geq 2
.

Proof.

The proof is simple. When
\alpha\geq 1
, from the elementary inequality
(a+b)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq a^{\frac{1}{2}}+b^{\frac{1}{2}},\forall\,a,b\geq 0
and the triangle inequality in the Banach space
l^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}\left((x,t)+\left(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left|M_{1}\left(x+x^{\prime}\right)\right|^{\alpha}+\left|M_{2}\left(t+t^{\prime}\right)\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\left(\left|M_{1}x\right|+\left|M_{1}x^{\prime}\right|\right)^{\alpha}+\left(\left|M_{2}t\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left|M_{2}t^{\prime}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left|M_{1}x\right|^{\alpha}+\left|M_{2}t\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+\left(\left|M_{1}x^{\prime}\right|^{\alpha}+\left|M_{2}t^{\prime}\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x,t)+\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}\left(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\right),

whence (
2.1
) holds. If this conclusion were true for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, by taking

(x,t)=(M_{1}^{-1}({\mathbb{O}}_{q-1}^{\mathsf{T}},1)^{\mathsf{T}},{\mathbb{O}}_{m}),\qquad(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})=({\mathbb{O}}_{q},M_{2}^{-1}({\mathbb{O}}_{m-1}^{\mathsf{T}},\epsilon)^{\mathsf{T}}),

where
\epsilon>0
and
{\mathbb{O}}_{\nu}
denotes the zero vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}
, we would have

\left(1+\epsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq 1+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\,\epsilon>0.

But from the classical inequality
(1+a)^{p}\geq 1+p\,a,\forall\,a\geq 0,p\geq 1
, it must yield that

1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\epsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq 1+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\,\epsilon>0.

This leads to a contradiction as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

To show the second claim, we use the convexity of the function
s\mapsto s^{p}
on
[0,\infty)
for
p\geq 1
and conclude that, whenever
\alpha\geq 2
,
(x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in B_{1}^{\alpha,M}
and
u\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}\left(u(x,t)+(1-u)\left(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\left(u\left|M_{1}x\right|+(1-u)\left|M_{1}x^{\prime}\right|\right)^{\alpha}+\left(u\left|M_{2}t\right|+(1-u)\left|M_{2}t^{\prime}\right|\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[u\left|M_{1}x\right|^{\alpha}+(1-u)\left|M_{1}x^{\prime}\right|^{\alpha}+u\left|M_{2}t\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+(1-u)\left|M_{2}t^{\prime}\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\left[u\,\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x,t)^{\alpha}+(1-u)\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq 1,

as required. However, when
\alpha<2
, this is not the case. Consider two boundary points
((M_{1}^{-1}({\mathbb{O}}_{q-1}^{\mathsf{T}},1)^{\mathsf{T}},{\mathbb{O}}_{m})
and
((M_{1}^{-1}({\mathbb{O}}_{q-1}^{\mathsf{T}},s)^{\mathsf{T}},M_{2}^{-1}({\mathbb{O}}_{m-1}^{\mathsf{T}},s^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}})
with

s^{\alpha}+(s^{\prime})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}=1,\qquad s,s^{\prime}\in(0,1).

Then their midpoint is outside the ball
B_{1}^{\alpha,M}
when
s
is near
1

s_{0}=s_{0}(\alpha)\in(0,1)
such that

1<\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)^{\alpha}+\left(\frac{s^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}=\frac{(1+s)^{\alpha}+2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(1-s^{\alpha})}{2^{\alpha}}=:\frac{f(s)}{2^{\alpha}},\qquad\mbox{for}\ s_{0}<s<1.

Notice that

f^{\prime}(s)=\alpha s^{\alpha-1}[(s^{-1}+1)^{\alpha-1}-2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}].

Let
s_{0}
be the unique zero point of

on
(0,1)

\alpha\in(0,1]

as desired. The proposition is thereby proved. We remark that the two counterexamples above are new even in Heisenberg groups.

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\chi_{B_{1}^{\alpha,M}}}(w,0)\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}\left(1+|w|)^{-q-\alpha},\right.&\mathrm{as}\ q<\frac{4m}{\alpha}-2\alpha+1,\\
(1+|w|)^{-\frac{q+1}{2}-\frac{2m}{\alpha}},&\mathrm{as}\ q\geqslant\frac{4m}{\alpha}-2\alpha+1.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Furthermore, when
\alpha\in 2{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
this bound can be improved to
(1+|w|)^{-\frac{q+1}{2}-\frac{2m}{\alpha}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
. We have

\left|\widehat{\chi_{B_{1}^{\alpha,M}}}(0,s)\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}(1+|s|)^{-m-\frac{\alpha}{2}},&\,\,\mathrm{as}\,\,q>\frac{m+\alpha-1}{2}\alpha,\\
(1+|s|)^{-\frac{m+1}{2}-\frac{q}{\alpha}},&\,\,\mathrm{as}\,\,q\leq\frac{m+\alpha-1}{2}\alpha.\end{cases}

Furthermore, when
\alpha\in 4{\mathbb{N}}^{*}

(1+|s|)^{-\frac{m+1}{2}-\frac{q}{\alpha}}

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha>0,c_{1},c_{2}>0

|w|\geq c_{1},|s|\geq c_{2}
. Then for

, we have

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\chi_{B_{1}^{\alpha,M}}}(w,s)\right|\lesssim|w|^{-\frac{q}{2}}|s|^{-\frac{m}{2}}|(w,s)|^{-\sigma}.

(2.4)

Furthermore, if

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\chi_{B_{1}^{\alpha,M}}}(w,s)\right|\lesssim|w|^{-\frac{q-1}{2}}|s|^{-\frac{m}{2}}|(w,s)|^{-\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}.

The implicit constants in (

Proof of main results

Note that

\displaystyle\#\left(\Gamma_{L}\cap B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\right)=\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}\cap B_{R}^{\alpha,\widetilde{M}}\right),\qquad\frac{\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\right)}{|\det(L)|}={\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{R}^{\alpha,\widetilde{M}}\right)},

(3.1)

where
\widetilde{M}:=ML=\operatorname{diag}\{M_{1}L_{1},M_{2}L_{2}\}
. Hence we only need to cope with the special case where
L={\mathbb{I}}_{q+m}
and
M
is invertible, from which the general case will follow immediately.

3.1
Proof of Theorem
1.2

At present, our target is to analyze

\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}\cap B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\right)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}}\chi_{B_{R}^{\alpha,M}}(j).

For that, recall the Poisson summation formula (see e.g.
[
28
, Theorem 3.2.8]
), for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{q+m})
,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}}f(j)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}}\widehat{f}(k).

(3.2)

Since when
\alpha\geq 1
,
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
is subadditive by Proposition
2.1
, then one can examine that

\chi_{B_{R-\epsilon}^{\alpha,M}}*\rho_{\epsilon}(x,t)\leq\chi_{B_{R}^{\alpha,M}}(x,t)\leq\chi_{B_{R+\epsilon}^{\alpha,M}}*\rho_{\epsilon}(x,t),\quad\forall\,(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q+m}.

(3.3)

Here,
\epsilon\in(0,1)
is to be determined later;
*
denotes the usual Euclidean convolution;
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{q+m})
is non-negative and supported in
{B_{1}^{\alpha,M}}
with integral value 1; and
\rho_{\epsilon}:=\epsilon^{-Q}\rho(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\epsilon^{-1}}(\cdot))
. To show (
3.3
), first observe that the convolution terms in (
3.3
) both take values only in
[0,1]
. Then it is enough to exploit the following facts:

(x,t)\notin B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\Rightarrow(x,t)\notin\left(B_{R-\epsilon}^{\alpha,M}+B_{\epsilon}^{\alpha,M}\right),\quad(x,t)\in B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\Rightarrow(x,t)\in\left(B_{R+\epsilon}^{\alpha,M}-B_{\epsilon}^{\alpha,M}\right).

We next use
\chi_{B_{R\pm\epsilon}^{\alpha,M}}*\rho_{\epsilon}
to replace
\chi_{B_{R}^{\alpha,M}}
, and plug them into the Poisson summation formula, and finally sum up the
the resulting errors. Precisely, by (
3.2
)-(
3.3
), on one hand,

\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}\cap B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\right)\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}}\left(\chi_{B_{R+\epsilon}}*\rho_{\epsilon}\right)^{\wedge}(k)=\sum_{k\neq 0}\widehat{\chi_{B^{\alpha,M}_{R+\epsilon}}}(k)\widehat{\rho}_{\epsilon}(k)+\operatorname{vol}\left(B^{\alpha,M}_{R+\epsilon}\right);

and on the other,

\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}\cap B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\right)\geq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}}\left(\chi_{B_{R-\epsilon}}*\rho_{\epsilon}\right)^{\wedge}(k)=\sum_{k\neq 0}\widehat{\chi_{B^{\alpha,M}_{R-\epsilon}}}(k)\widehat{\rho}_{\epsilon}(k)+\operatorname{vol}\left(B^{\alpha,M}_{R-\epsilon}\right).

From (
1.10
) we see that, for all
R\geq 10
,

\left|\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{q+m}\cap B_{R}^{\alpha,M}\right)-\operatorname{vol}\left(B^{\alpha,M}_{R}\right)\right|\lesssim\sum_{\pm}\sum_{k\neq 0}\left|\widehat{\chi_{B^{\alpha,M}_{R\pm\epsilon}}}(k)\right||\widehat{\rho}_{\epsilon}(k)|+R^{Q-1}\epsilon.

(3.4)

Because
\widehat{\rho}
is of Schwartz class and (through a routine verification)

\widehat{\chi_{B^{\alpha,M}_{R}}}(k)=R^{Q}\,\widehat{\chi_{B^{\alpha,M}_{1}}}(Rk^{\prime},R^{2}k^{\prime\prime}),\qquad\widehat{\rho_{\epsilon}}(k)=\widehat{\rho}(\epsilon k^{\prime},\epsilon^{2}k^{\prime\prime}),

then by (
3.4
), we have after a normalization that

\mathcal{P}_{q,m}^{\alpha,M}(R)\lesssim\sum_{\pm}\mathbf{S}(R\pm\epsilon,\epsilon)+R^{-1}\epsilon,

(3.5)

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}(R,\epsilon)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\neq 0}\left|\widehat{\chi_{B^{\alpha,M}_{1}}}(Rk^{\prime},R^{2}k^{\prime\prime})\right|\left(1+\epsilon\left|k^{\prime}\right|+\epsilon^{2}\left|k^{\prime\prime}\right|\right)^{-N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq 0,\,k^{\prime\prime}=0}\cdots+\sum_{k^{\prime}=0,\,k^{\prime\prime}\neq 0}\cdots+\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq 0,\,k^{\prime\prime}\neq 0}\cdots

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathbf{S}_{j}(R,\epsilon),

where
N>0
is a large constant to be chosen (indeed
N=q+m+1
will suffice). Thus we are reduced to the estimation of each
\mathbf{S}_{j}(R,\epsilon)
for large
R
and small
\epsilon
. In fact, we will prove that, whenever
R\geq 10
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{1}(R,\epsilon)\lesssim R^{-\beta_{1}}\mathcal{E}(q,\beta_{1}),\quad\mbox{for}\ \alpha>0,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{2}(R,\epsilon)\lesssim R^{-2\beta_{2}}\,\mathcal{E}(2m,2\beta_{2}),\quad\mbox{for}\ \alpha>0,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{3}(R,\epsilon)\lesssim\begin{cases}R^{-\frac{Q}{2}-2\sigma}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathcal{E}(m,2\sigma),&\mbox{for $\alpha\geq 2-m$},\\
R^{-\frac{Q+1}{2}-2\sigma}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\mathcal{E}(m-1,2\sigma),&\mbox{for}\ \frac{q}{2}+1\geq\alpha\geq 3-m,\\
\end{cases}

(3.8)

where
\sigma
is given by (
1.14
), and

\beta_{1}:=\frac{q}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{q+2\alpha,\frac{4m}{\alpha}+1\right\},\quad\beta_{2}:=\frac{m}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{m+\alpha,\frac{2q}{\alpha}+1\right\},

and
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
is defined by, for
\epsilon\in(0,1)

\mathcal{E}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}):=\begin{cases}(\epsilon^{-1})^{\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}},&\mbox{as}\ \eta_{1}>\eta_{2},\\
\log(\epsilon^{-1}),&\mbox{as}\ \eta_{1}=\eta_{2},\\
1,&\mbox{as}\ \eta_{1}<\eta_{2},\\
\end{cases}\qquad\forall\,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}.

To show (
3.6
), by Lemma
2.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{1}(R,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\beta_{1}}\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq 0}\left|k^{\prime}\right|^{-\beta_{1}}\left(1+\epsilon\left|k^{\prime}\right|\right)^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\beta_{1}}\int_{\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}:\,|z|\geq 1\}}|z|^{-\beta_{1}}(1+\epsilon|z|)^{-N}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\beta_{1}}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-\beta_{1}+q-1}(1+\epsilon r)^{-N}dr

\displaystyle\leq R^{-\beta_{1}}\left(\int_{1}^{\epsilon^{-1}}r^{-\beta_{1}+q-1}dr+\epsilon^{-N}\int_{\epsilon^{-1}}^{\infty}r^{-\beta_{1}+q-1-N}dr\right),

which gives the desired estimation if
N>q-\beta_{1}
. To show (
3.7
), we see from Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{2}(R,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k^{\prime\prime}\neq 0}R^{-2\beta_{2}}\left|k^{\prime\prime}\right|^{-\beta_{2}}\left(1+\epsilon^{2}\left|k^{\prime\prime}\right|\right)^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-2\beta_{2}}\int_{\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}:\,|z|\geq 1\}}|z|^{-\beta_{2}}\left(1+\epsilon^{2}|z|\right)^{-N}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-2\beta_{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-\beta_{2}+m-1}(1+\epsilon^{2}r)^{-N}dr

\displaystyle\leq R^{-2\beta_{2}}\left(\int_{1}^{\epsilon^{-2}}r^{-\beta_{2}+m-1}dr+\epsilon^{-2N}\int_{\epsilon^{-2}}^{\infty}r^{-\beta_{2}+m-1-N}dr\right),

which implies our claim provided that
N>\max\{q-\beta_{1},m-\beta_{2}\}
. To show (
3.8
), via (
2.4
) of Lemma
2.4
it yields that

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{3}(R,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k^{\prime}\neq 0,\,k^{\prime\prime}\neq 0}R^{-\frac{q}{2}}\left|k^{\prime}\right|^{-\frac{q}{2}}R^{-m}\left|k^{\prime\prime}\right|^{-\frac{m}{2}}\left(R\left|k^{\prime}\right|+R^{2}\left|k^{\prime\prime}\right|\right)^{-\sigma}\left(1+\epsilon\left|k^{\prime}\right|+\epsilon^{2}\left|k^{\prime\prime}\right|\right)^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{|z^{\prime}|\geq 1,|z^{\prime\prime}|\geq 1}\left|z^{\prime}\right|^{-\frac{q}{2}}\left|z^{\prime\prime}\right|^{-\frac{m}{2}}\left(R\left|z^{\prime}\right|+R^{2}\left|z^{\prime\prime}\right|\right)^{-\sigma}\left(1+\epsilon\left|z^{\prime}\right|+\epsilon^{2}\left|z^{\prime\prime}\right|\right)^{-N}dz^{\prime}dz^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}r_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}r_{2}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(Rr_{1}+R^{2}r_{2}\right)^{-\sigma}\left(1+\epsilon r_{1}+\epsilon^{2}r_{2}\right)^{-N}dr_{1}dr_{2}

\displaystyle\leq R^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\left[\int_{1}^{\epsilon^{-1}}\int_{1}^{\epsilon^{-2}}r_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}r_{2}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(R^{2}r_{2}\right)^{-\sigma}dr_{1}dr_{2}\right.

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{\epsilon^{-1}}\int_{\epsilon^{-2}}^{\infty}r_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}r_{2}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(R^{2}r_{2}\right)^{-\sigma}\left(\epsilon^{2}r_{2}\right)^{-N}dr_{1}dr_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\epsilon^{-1}}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\epsilon^{-2}}r_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}r_{2}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(R^{2}r_{2}\right)^{-\sigma}\left(\epsilon r_{1}\right)^{-N}dr_{1}dr_{2}

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{\epsilon^{-1}}^{\infty}\int_{\epsilon^{-2}}^{\infty}r_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}r_{2}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(R^{2}r_{2}\right)^{-\sigma}\left(\epsilon r_{1}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\left(\epsilon^{2}r_{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}dr_{1}dr_{2}\right],

which readily leads to the first estimate in (
3.8
) as long as
N>\max\{q,m\}
; while the second one can also be justified likewise by using (
2.5
) instead (with
N>\max\{q+1,m\}
).

The remaining proof now can be finished by determining suitable
\epsilon
to control the resulting errors, based on various conditions. For (i) of Theorem
1.2
, we use (
3.5
)-(
3.7
) and the second estimate in (

) to get

\mathcal{P}_{q,m}^{\alpha,M}(R)\lesssim R^{-\beta_{1}}\mathcal{E}(q,\beta_{1})+R^{-2\beta_{2}}\,\mathcal{E}(2m,2\beta_{2})+R^{-\frac{Q+1}{2}-2\sigma}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\mathcal{E}(m-1,2\sigma)+R^{-1}\epsilon.

To balance the four quantities we let
\epsilon=R^{-1}
, and this will imply (
1.15
) after some tedious verifications. Let us inspect the situation that

as an example. Recall

, and the first three resulting terms (denoted by

{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\lesssim\begin{cases}R^{-\frac{4m}{\alpha}-1},&\mbox{as}\ q>\beta_{1},\\
R^{-\frac{4m}{\alpha}-1}\log R,&\mbox{as}\ q=\beta_{1},\\
R^{-\beta_{1}},&\mbox{as}\ q<\beta_{1},\\
\end{cases}\quad{\mathbf{B}}_{2}\lesssim R^{-\gamma},\quad{\mathbf{B}}_{3}\lesssim\begin{cases}R^{-2-2\sigma}\log R,&\mbox{as}\ m\geq 2,\\
R^{-1-2\sigma},&\mbox{as}\ m=1,\end{cases}

respectively, where

For the rest cases, we conclude from (

)-(

\mathcal{P}_{q,m}^{\alpha,M}(R)\lesssim R^{-\beta_{1}}\mathcal{E}(q,\beta_{1})+R^{-2\beta_{2}}\,\mathcal{E}(2m,2\beta_{2})+R^{-\frac{Q}{2}-2\sigma}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathcal{E}(m,2\sigma)+R^{-1}\epsilon.

Similarly, the desired conclusions can be drawn by choosing

as follows and plugging them into the above estimation:

•

•

for (iii) we take

Proof of Theorem

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Although by Proposition
2.1
,
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}
is not subadditive anymore for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, which plays an important part in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we can consider
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha,M}^{\alpha}
as a substitute, whose subadditivity is indeed a simple outcome of (
2.1
) and the primary inequality

(a+b)^{\alpha}\leq a^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha},\qquad\ \forall\,a,b\geq 0,\ \forall\,\alpha\in(0,1].

Consequently, we may still obtain a counterpart of (
3.3
) which reads, for
R\geq 10
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\chi_{B_{(R^{\alpha}-\epsilon^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}}^{\alpha,M}}*\rho_{\epsilon}(x,t)\leq\chi_{B_{R}^{\alpha,M}}(x,t)\leq\chi_{B_{(R^{\alpha}+\epsilon^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}}^{\alpha,M}}*\rho_{\epsilon}(x,t),\quad\forall\,(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q+m}.

Then by appealing to Poisson’s summation formula as in the proof Theorem
1.2
, with the fact

\operatorname{vol}\left(B^{\alpha,M}_{(R^{\alpha}\pm\epsilon^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}}\right)=\operatorname{vol}\left(B^{\alpha,M}_{1}\right)R^{Q}+O(R^{-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}),

we arrive at that, for
R\geq 10
,

\mathcal{P}_{q,m}^{\alpha,M}(R)\lesssim\sum_{\pm}\mathbf{S}((R^{\alpha}\pm\epsilon^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha},\epsilon)+R^{-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}.

This, together with (
3.6
)-(
3.8
), implies that, for
m=2
(using the first estimation of (
3.8
)),

\mathcal{P}_{q,m}^{\alpha,M}(R)\lesssim R^{-\beta_{1}}\mathcal{E}(q,\beta_{1})+R^{-2\beta_{2}}\,\mathcal{E}(2m,2\beta_{2})+R^{-\frac{Q}{2}-1}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}-1}+R^{-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha},

and for

(using the second one),

\mathcal{P}_{q,m}^{\alpha,M}(R)\lesssim R^{-\beta_{1}}\mathcal{E}(q,\beta_{1})+R^{-2\beta_{2}}\,\mathcal{E}(2m,2\beta_{2})+R^{-\frac{Q+1}{2}-1}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)^{\frac{Q+1}{2}-2}+R^{-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Finally, from the choices that

Proof of Theorem

Theorem
1.5
is a direct consequence of the classical lattice counting problem in ellipsoids on
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
combining a slicing argument. This procedure is almost identical to that of
[

, Theorem 1.1 (3)]
, so we shall omit the details. The main difference is that instead of discrepancy estimates for standard Euclidean balls, we employ the ellipsoid version. Recall that if

is an

\left|\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap R\,\mathcal{B}_{n}\right)-{\operatorname{vol}\left(\mathcal{B}_{n}\right)}R^{n}\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}R^{\frac{2}{3}},&\mbox{as}\ n=2,\\
R^{\frac{231}{158}},&\mbox{as}\ n=3,\\
R^{2}\log R,&\mbox{as}\ n=4,\\
R^{n-2},&\mbox{as}\ n\geq 5.\end{cases}

For
n=2
the result can be found in e.g.
[
36
, p. 9]
; for
n=3
see
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
; for
q\geq 4
the estimates are contained in
[
26
, Theorem 1.5(i)]
(
n\geq 5
) and its proof (
n=4
); Likewise, the upper bounds in

can be improved if we impose finer conditions on
\widetilde{M}
and appeal to stronger conclusions accordingly as mentioned in Remark

.

3.4
Proof of Remark
1.3
(2)

The proof is classical and based on an argument by contradiction. Fix
c>0
such that
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{c}\widetilde{M}
is an integral matrix. We are required to show that
\mathcal{P}_{q,1}^{2,M}(R)\neq o(R^{-2})
. Were this false, in view of (
3.1
) we would have that

\left|\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{q+1}\cap B_{R}^{2,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{c}\widetilde{M}}\right)-{\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{R}^{2,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{c}\widetilde{M}}\right)}\right|={\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{c^{-1}R}^{2,\widetilde{M}}\right)}\,\mathcal{P}_{q,1}^{2,M}(c^{-1}R)=o(R^{q}),\quad\mbox{as}\ R\to\infty.

Notice that presently,

\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{q+1}\cap B_{\sqrt{N}}^{\alpha,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{c}\widetilde{M}}\right)=\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{q+1}\cap B_{\sqrt{N+1/2}}^{\alpha,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{c}\widetilde{M}}\right),\quad\forall\,N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}.

{\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{\sqrt{N}}^{2,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{c}\widetilde{M}}\right)}={\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{\sqrt{N+1/2}}^{2,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{c}\widetilde{M}}\right)}+o(N^{\frac{q}{2}}),\quad\mbox{as}\ N\to\infty,

). This completes the proof.

3.5
Proof of Theorem
1.7

The socalled “ball-to-shell” argument refers to that one can straightforwardly derive the estimates for lattice counting in a thin spherical shell from that in a pair of large balls which surround it. To be more precise, denote by
E(R)
the corresponding upper bounds for
\mathcal{P}_{q,m}^{\alpha,M}
established in Theorems
1.2
,
1.4
and
1.5
. Then one can find

such that, for all
(x,t)\in\Gamma_{L}
and

\displaystyle\#\left(\Gamma_{L}\cap\left(B_{R+\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\backslash B_{R-\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\#\left(\Gamma_{L}\cap B_{R+\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\right)-\#\left(\Gamma_{L}\cap B_{R-\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\right)

\displaystyle=\#\left(\Gamma_{L}\cap B_{R+\delta}^{\alpha,M}\right)-\#\left(\Gamma_{L}\cap B_{R-\delta}^{\alpha,M}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{1}^{\alpha,M}\right)}{|\det(L)|}((R+\delta)^{Q}-(R-\delta)^{Q})

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{0}(E(R+\delta)+E(R-\delta))

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{Q-1}\delta+E(R),

which gives (

) as required. For (
1.7
), one directly uses (
1.16
) (which holds for

) and the following observation:

\displaystyle\mbox{LHS of \eqref{aveLp}}

\displaystyle\leq T^{-Q}\sum_{(x,t)\in\Gamma_{L}(T)}\#\left(\Gamma_{L}(T)\cap\left(B_{R+2\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\backslash B_{R-2\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{-Q}\,T^{Q}\max_{(x,t)\in\Gamma_{L}(T)}\#\left(\Gamma_{L}(T)\cap\left(B_{R+2\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\backslash B_{R-2\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(x,t)\in\Gamma_{L}}\#\left(\Gamma_{L}\cap\left(B_{R+2\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\backslash B_{R-2\delta}^{\alpha,M}(x,t)\right)\right).

The proof of Theorem
1.7
is therefore finished.

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}^{\alpha}}e^{-2\pi i(w\cdot x+s\cdot t)}dxdt=\int_{|x|\leq 1}e^{-2\pi iw\cdot x}\left(\int_{|t|\leq\left(1-|x|^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}e^{-2\pi is\cdot t}dt\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq 1}e^{-2\pi iw\cdot x}(1-|x|^{\alpha})^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}|s|^{-\frac{m}{2}}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(2\pi(1-|x|^{\alpha})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}|s|\right)dx

\displaystyle=2\pi|w|^{-\frac{q}{2}+1}|s|^{-\frac{m}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}(2\pi|w|r)J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(2\pi|s|\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)r^{\frac{q}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}dr

(4.2)

\displaystyle=2\pi|w|^{-\frac{q}{2}}|s|^{-\frac{m}{2}+1}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(2\pi|s|r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(2\pi|w|\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)r^{\frac{m}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2\alpha}}dr.

(4.3)

Here in the last step we have used similar argument as in the proof of (
4.2
), but integrating the
x
-variable first, and
J_{\nu}
is the Bessel function of order
\nu
, defined by (first for
\Re(\nu)>-\frac{1}{2}
)

J_{\nu}(r)=\frac{\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{\nu}}{\Gamma\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_{-1}^{+1}e^{irs}\left(1-s^{2}\right)^{\nu}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}},\quad r>0.

Applying the power series,
J_{\nu}
has an entire
extension in
\nu
on the complex plane for all
r>0
; see e.g.
[
28
, §B]
and
[
63
]
for more details. As to our applications, we always assume that
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let us recall some useful properties of Bessel functions as follows:

•

Recursion formulas (
[
27
, §8.471]
):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{\nu}J_{\nu}(r)\right)=r^{\nu}J_{\nu-1}(r),\quad\nu\in{\mathbb{R}},\,r>0,

(4.4)

\displaystyle J_{-n}(r)=(-1)^{n}J_{n}(r),\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,r>0.

(4.5)

•

Small argument estimates (
[
28
, §B]
):

|J_{\nu}(r)|\leq C_{\nu}\,r^{\nu},\quad\forall\,r\leq 1;\ \forall\,\nu>-\frac{3}{2}.

(4.6)

•

Large argument estimates (
[
27
, §8.451]
):

|J_{\nu}(r)|\leq C_{\nu}^{\prime}\,r^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\,r\geq 1;\ \forall\,\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(4.7)

•

Asymptotics at infinity (
[
27
, §8.451]
):

J_{\nu}(r)\sim r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\pm}e^{\pm ir}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{\nu,j}^{\pm}\,r^{-j},\quad\mathrm{as}\,\,r\to\infty,

(4.8)

where
a^{\pm}_{\nu,j}
’s are complex numbers that can be determined exactly, and the asymptotic sum holds in the sense that, for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

J_{\nu}(r)-r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\pm}e^{\pm ir}\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{\nu,j}^{\pm}\,r^{-j}=O(r^{-N-\frac{3}{2}}),\quad\mathrm{as}\,\,r\to\infty.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Note that by
[

\lim_{r\to 0+}J_{\nu}(r)r^{-\nu}=\frac{1}{2^{\nu}\,\Gamma(\nu+1)},\qquad\forall\,\nu>-\frac{1}{2}.

(4.9)

Then letting
s\to 0
in (
4.2
) gives

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi_{B_{1}^{\alpha}}}(w,0)=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{m}{2}+1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{m}{2}+1\right)}|w|^{-\frac{q}{2}+1}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}(2\pi r|w|)r^{\frac{q}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}}dr.

Since the desired estimates are trivial for
|w|\lesssim 1
, we are left with large
|w|
. Let
\lambda=2\pi|w|\geq 10
. We write

\mathcal{I}(\lambda):=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{m}{2}+1\right)}{2^{\frac{q}{2}}\pi^{\frac{q+m}{2}}}\lambda^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\widehat{\chi_{B_{1}^{\alpha}}}(w,0)=\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}(\lambda r)\,r^{\frac{q}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}}dr.

To control it, the points
r=0
and
r=1
are treated separately. Fixing a non-negative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\varphi(r)=\begin{cases}0,&r\leq\frac{1}{4},\\
1,&r\geq\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

(4.10)

then the integral can be splitted as follows:

\mathcal{I}=\int_{0}^{1}\varphi\cdots+\int_{0}^{1}(1-\varphi)\cdots=:\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}.

In the sequel, to get Lemma
2.2
we will show that

|\mathcal{I}_{1}(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{2m}{\alpha}-\frac{3}{2}},\qquad|\mathcal{I}_{2}(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}-\alpha-1},

(4.11)

and for any
\alpha\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
that

|\mathcal{I}_{2}(\lambda)|\lesssim_{k}\lambda^{-k},\quad\forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(4.12)

For
\mathcal{I}_{1}
, the new amplitude function behaves well near
0
, but there is a singularity of order
2m/\alpha
at point
1
. For that, we let

\varphi_{0}(r):=\left(\frac{1-r^{\alpha}}{1-r}\right)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}},\quad r\in[0,1),\qquad\varphi_{0}(1):=\alpha^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}}.

It is easily seen that
\varphi_{0}\in C^{\infty}([0,1]).

Then we can write

\mathcal{I}_{1}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}(\lambda r)(1-r)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}}\varphi(r)\varphi_{0}(r)r^{\frac{q}{2}}dr,

which, after plugging (
4.8
) into it, can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}^{(N)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}C_{j}\lambda^{-j-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\pm}\left|\int_{0}^{1}e^{\pm i\lambda r}(1-r)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}}\tilde{\varphi}_{j}(r)dr\right|+C_{N}\lambda^{-N-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}}\tilde{\varphi}_{N}(r)dr

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j=0}^{N}C_{j}\lambda^{-j-\frac{1}{2}}\ \mathcal{I}_{1,j}(\lambda).

where
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}

will be chosen soon,
C_{j}>0
is suitable constant and

\tilde{\varphi}_{j}(r):=\varphi(r)\varphi_{0}(r)r^{\frac{q}{2}-\frac{1}{2}-j},\quad j=0,1,\ldots,N.

For
\mathcal{I}_{1,N}
, it is obvious that
\mathcal{I}_{1,N}(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda^{-N-\frac{1}{2}}
. For

\mathcal{I}_{1,j}(0\leq j\leq N-1)
, since
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}^{(k)}(0)=0,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
, by
the standard oscillatory integral theory with singularities in the integrands (see e.g.
[
12
, pp. 47-48]
), we have
\mathcal{I}_{1,j}(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{2m}{\alpha}-1}
. As a result, one can choose an integer
N
larger than
2m/\alpha+1
, and obtain

\left|\mathcal{I}_{1}(\lambda)\right|\leq\mathcal{I}_{1}^{(N)}(\lambda)\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda^{-j-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\lambda^{-\frac{2m}{\alpha}-1}+\lambda^{-N-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{2m}{\alpha}-\frac{3}{2}}.

For
\mathcal{I}_{2}
, the asymptotic expansions of
J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}
are not available near
0
. However, we can use the recursion formula (
4.4
) of Bessel functions to perform integration by parts, which will lead to a better decay. Specifically, by (
4.4
) we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}(\lambda)=\left.\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}}\frac{(\lambda r)^{\frac{q}{2}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}(\lambda r)}{\lambda}(1-\varphi)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}}\right|_{0}^{1}-\frac{\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}}}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{1}(\lambda r)^{\frac{q}{2}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}(\lambda r)\left(-\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\right)dr,

where
\varphi_{1}=\varphi^{\prime}(r)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}}
and
\varphi_{2}=2m(1-\varphi(r))r^{\alpha-1}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2m}{\alpha}-1}.
Note that the first item in RHS equals 0 by (
4.6
) and the definition of
\varphi
, whence

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(\lambda r)^{\frac{q}{2}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}(\lambda r)\varphi_{j}(r)dr=:\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\mathcal{I}_{2,j}(\lambda).

The estimation of
\mathcal{I}_{2,1}
is easy. Since
\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi_{1}\subset[\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}]
, there is no singularity in the amplitude. We then define inductively

\varphi_{1,k+1}(r):=r^{-1}\varphi_{1,k}^{\prime}(r),\qquad\varphi_{1,1}:=\varphi_{1},\qquad k\geq 1,

and set

\mathcal{I}_{2,1}^{(k)}(\lambda):=(-1)^{k-1}\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}+1-2k}\int_{0}^{1}(\lambda r)^{\frac{q}{2}+k}J_{\frac{q}{2}+k-1}(\lambda r)\,\varphi_{1,k}(r)\,dr.

Utilizing (
4.4
) and integration by parts repeatedly we see that
\mathcal{I}_{2,1}=\mathcal{I}_{2,1}^{(k)},\,\forall\,k\geq 1
. One can also easily verify that

\left|\varphi_{1,k}(r)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,m,k},\qquad\left|J_{\frac{q}{2}+k-1}(\lambda r)\right|\leq C_{q,k,1},\qquad\forall\,k\geq 1,

which implies that

\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,1}(\lambda)\right|=\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,1}^{(k)}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}+1-2k}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\frac{q}{2}+k}r^{\frac{q}{2}+k}dr\lesssim\lambda^{-k+1},\quad\forall\,k\geq 1.

(4.13)

To estimate
\mathcal{I}_{2,2}
, more care is necessary for singularities of the type
r^{\alpha-1}
. Likewise, we define

\varphi_{2,k+1}(r):=r^{-1}\varphi_{2,k}^{\prime}(r),\quad\varphi_{2,1}:=\varphi_{2},\quad k\geq 1

and put

\mathcal{I}_{2,2}^{(k)}(\lambda):=(-1)^{k-1}\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}+1-2k}\int_{0}^{1}(\lambda r)^{\frac{q}{2}+k}J_{\frac{q}{2}+k-1}(\lambda r)\varphi_{2,k}(r)dr.

For any
k\geq 1
, through direct calculations we have that
\mathcal{I}_{2,1}=\mathcal{I}_{2,1}^{(k)}
, and that

\left|\varphi_{2,k}(r)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,m,k}^{\prime}\ r^{\alpha+1-2k},\qquad\forall r\in(0,1).

(4.14)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,2}(\lambda)\right|=\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,2}^{(k)}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-k+1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+\frac{q}{2}+1-k}\left|J_{\frac{q}{2}+k-1}(\lambda r)\right\rvert\,dr

\displaystyle=:\lambda^{-k+1}\left(\int_{0}^{\lambda^{-1}}\cdots+\int_{\lambda^{-1}}^{1}\cdots\right).

The first integral in the brace can be controlled by

\lambda^{\frac{q}{2}+k-1}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{-1}}r^{\alpha+q}dr\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}-\alpha-2+k},

if we invoke (
4.6
); while for the second one by (
4.7
) it yields that

\left|\int_{\lambda^{-1}}^{1}\cdots\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\lambda^{-1}}^{1}r^{\alpha+\frac{q}{2}+\frac{1}{2}-k}dr\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}-\alpha-2+k}.

where we have chosen an
k>\alpha+\frac{q+3}{2}
. These estimates above give that
\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,2}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}-\alpha-1},
from which, together with (
4.13
), it follows that

\left|\mathcal{I}_{2}(\lambda)\right|\leq\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,1}(\lambda)\right|+\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,2}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{q}{2}-\alpha-1}

as required in (
4.11
).

Finally, we are left to the case that
\alpha\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
. Indeed at this moment, from the definition of
\varphi_{2,k}
, (
4.14
) can be improved to

\left|\varphi_{2,k}(r)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,m,k}^{\prime\prime},\qquad\forall k\geq 1,\ \forall r\in(0,1).

Then a similar argument as in the estimation of
\mathcal{I}_{2,1}^{(k)}
will lead to that

\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,2}(\lambda)\right|=\left|\mathcal{I}_{2,2}^{(k)}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-k+1},\qquad\forall\,k\geq 1,

which shows that
\left|\mathcal{I}_{2}(\lambda)\right|=O\left(\lambda^{-k}\right)
. This proves (
4.12
) and hence Lemma
2.2
.

To treat (

becomes less effective since there occur two Bessel functions in the integrand. A natural idea is to use a resolution of the identity, which covers the interval

by an intermediate part and two end parts; the latter are usually neglectable or easy to control because of the short lengths; while for the former, one resorts to the asymptotics of Bessel functions and reduces matters to the following type of oscillatory integral with singularities at two ends formally:

\displaystyle\phi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r):=\lambda_{1}r\pm\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},\qquad r\in(0,1).

\phi_{\lambda}^{-}

for all

On the other hand, due to the anisotropy of the

-ball

\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
that we encounter are in fact mutually independent. Unfortunately, this aspect seems to have been overlooked by

In fact, we find it necessary to distinguish these cases. And a practical division is that:
\lambda_{1}\gtrsim\lambda_{2}

(van der Corput)
.

Let

A straightforward computation gives:

\displaystyle(\phi_{\lambda}^{+})^{\prime}(r)=\lambda_{1}-2\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}r^{\alpha-1},

\displaystyle(\phi_{\lambda}^{+})^{\prime\prime}(r)=-2\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-2}r^{\alpha-2}(\alpha-1-r^{\alpha}),

\displaystyle(\phi_{\lambda}^{+})^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)=-2\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-3}r^{\alpha-3}[(\alpha-1-r^{\alpha})(\alpha-2+\alpha r^{\alpha})-\alpha(1-r^{\alpha})r^{\alpha}].

When
\alpha\in(0,1]\cup[2,\infty)
, the monotonicity of
(\phi_{\lambda}^{+})^{\prime}
is obvious. Let
r_{*}=r_{*}(\alpha,\lambda)\in(0,1)
be the unique solution of the equation

\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4\lambda_{2}}=\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}r^{\alpha-1}.

(5.6)

For
\alpha\geq 2
, we have

\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(r)\geq\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}\left(r_{*}\right)=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2},\qquad\forall\,r\in\left(0,r_{*}\right).

On
(r_{*},1)
, since the function
r\mapsto\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}r^{\alpha-1}
is increasing and
\alpha-1-r^{\alpha}\geq 1-r^{\alpha}
,
then

\left|\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime}(r)\right|\geq\left.2\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}r^{\alpha-1}\right|_{r=r_{*}}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}.

For
\alpha\leq 1
, the rest conclusions of (ii) can be proved in a similar manner, with the simple fact that
r^{\alpha}+1-\alpha\geq r^{\alpha}
.

When
\alpha\in(1,2)
, we let
r_{0}=(\alpha-1)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\in(0,1)
, which is the unique stationary point of
\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}
on
(0,1)
. This implies the monotonic properties in (iii) trivially. By calculations we have

\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(r_{0})=\lambda_{1}-\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}\lambda_{2}\geq 0,\qquad\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}(r_{0})=4\lambda_{2}(2-\alpha)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-2}(\alpha-1)^{\frac{2\alpha-3}{\alpha}}\alpha>0.

Hence there is an interval
(r_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime\prime})\subset(0,1)
containing
r_{0}

and depending only on
\alpha
such that

\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)/\lambda_{2}\sim_{\alpha}1,\qquad\mbox{on}\ (r_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime\prime}),

(5.7)

due to the continuity of
\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}.

We denote

C_{\alpha}^{\prime}:=\lambda_{2}^{-1}\max\left\{\lambda_{1}-\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}\left(r_{0}^{\prime}\right),\lambda_{1}-\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}\left(r_{0}^{\prime\prime}\right)\right\}>0.

It follows from the last assertion in (iii) that
C_{\alpha}^{\prime}<\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}
. And it is simple to verify that
C_{\alpha}^{\prime}
indeed depends only on
\alpha
. There are two possible cases: If
\lambda_{1}-\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}\lambda_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1},
then
\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(r)\geq\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}\left(r_{0}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}
on
(0,1)
. Conversely, if
\lambda_{1}-\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}\lambda_{2}<\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}
(so
\lambda_{1}\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{2}
), then by the definition of
C_{\alpha}^{\prime}
and the monotonic properties of
\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}
we obtain

\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(r)\geq\lambda_{1}-C_{\alpha}^{\prime}\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{1}-\frac{C_{\alpha}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}}\lambda_{1}\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{1},\quad\forall\,r\in\left(0,r_{0}^{\prime}\right)\cup\left(r_{0}^{\prime\prime},1\right).

Lastly, by (
5.7
) it yields that
|\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)|\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{2}\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{1}
on
(r_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime\prime})
.
These lead to (iii). Lemma
5.2
is therefore proved.

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.
We calculate that

\displaystyle\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(r)=\lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1},

\displaystyle\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime}(r)=-\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-2}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-2}\left(\alpha-2+r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)=-\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{1}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-3}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-3}\left[\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-2-(\alpha-2)r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\left(\alpha-2+r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{2}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\right].

When
\alpha\in(0,1]\cup[2,\infty)
, the desired conclusions can be proved similarly as in Lemma
5.3
(i)-(ii). We only mention some cruxes here: Firstly,
R_{*}=R_{*}(\alpha,\lambda)\in(0,1)
is the unique solution of the equation

\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}=\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}.

(5.8)

Secondly, for
\alpha\geq 2
it holds that
r^{-1}\left(\alpha-2+r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\geq\frac{\alpha}{2},\,\forall\,r\in(0,1)
. Thirdly, for
\alpha\leq 1
, one has
\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(2-\alpha-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\geq 1,\,\forall\,r\in(0,1)
.

When
\alpha\in(1,2)
, put
R_{0}=(2-\alpha)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\in(0,1)
, the unique stationary point of
\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}
on
(0,1)
. Then the monotonic properties in (iii) hold by our calculations of the derivatives. It is easily seen that the equation
\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(R_{0})
or

\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}}=\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}

(5.9)

has exactly two solutions
R_{j}=R_{j}(\alpha,\lambda),j=1,2
in
(0,1)
with
R_{1}<R_{0}<R_{2}
. On the other hand, we get via a direct computation that

\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(R_{0})=\lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}}\lambda_{1}>0,\qquad-\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}(R_{0})=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{8}{\alpha}(\alpha-1)(2-\alpha)^{2-\frac{6}{\alpha}}>0.

Denote by
R_{0}^{\prime}=R_{0}^{\prime}(\alpha)
and
R_{0}^{\prime\prime}=R_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)
the solutions of (
5.9
) with LHS replaced by
2/\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}
. Then we have
R_{1}<R_{0}^{\prime}<R_{0}<R_{0}^{\prime\prime}<R_{2}
and

-\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)/\lambda_{1}\sim_{\alpha}1,\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\,\,r\in(R_{0}^{\prime},R_{0}^{\prime\prime}).

(5.10)

Now we meet two situations: If
\lambda_{2}-\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2},
then

\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(r)\geq\min\{\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}\left(R_{1}\right),\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}\left(R_{2}\right)\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime}(R_{0})\geq\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{2},\quad\forall\,r\in(R_{1},R_{2}).

(5.11)

Moreover, since the function
r\longmapsto\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}
is decreasing on
(0,R_{1})
and increasing on
(R_{2},1)
, respectively, which is a direct consequence of the last assertion in (iii), then by (
5.9
) we have, for all
r\in(0,R_{1})\cup(R_{2},1)

\displaystyle\left|\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime}(r)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-2}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-2}\left|r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\alpha-2\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4}\min_{r\in\{R_{1},R_{2}\}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left|r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\alpha-2\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{2}}{4}\min_{r\in\{R_{0}^{\prime},R_{0}^{\prime\prime}\}}\left|r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\alpha-2\right|\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{2}.

Conversely, if
\lambda_{2}-\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}^{-1}\lambda_{1}<\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}
(so
\lambda_{1}\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{2}
), then for
\alpha\in(1,2)
we obtain

|\left(\psi_{\alpha}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime}(r)|\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4}\min_{r\in\{R_{0}^{\prime},R_{0}^{\prime\prime}\}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left|r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\alpha-2\right|\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{2},\quad\forall\,r\in(0,R_{0}^{\prime})\cup(R_{0}^{\prime\prime},1).

While on
(R_{0}^{\prime},R_{0}^{\prime\prime})
, from (
5.10
) it follows that
|\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)|\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{1}\sim_{\alpha}\lambda_{2}
. These estimates imply (iii) and hence the proof of Lemma
5.3
is completed.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.
This lemma can be proved in a simpler way and by some slight modifications of the partial proofs of Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
. So we shall omit the details.

5.2
Proof of (
2.4
)

We will divide the discussion into the following two cases: Case (i):
\lambda_{1}\geq\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}\lambda_{2}
and Case (ii):
\lambda_{1}<\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}\lambda_{2}
.

5.2.1
Proof of Case (i):
\lambda_{1}\geq\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}\lambda_{2}

Note that we now have
\lambda_{1}\sim_{\alpha}|\lambda|
, which will be used implicitly in what follows. Applying integration by parts with (
4.4
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)\right]^{\prime}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left[\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right]^{\frac{m}{2}}dr

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)J_{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+2}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-1}dr

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots(1-\varphi)dr+2\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\varphi dr

\displaystyle=:2\sum_{k=1}^{2}{\mathcal{J}}_{k}(\lambda),

where
\varphi
is defined by (
4.10
) as before. In view of this, we merely have to show that

|{\mathcal{J}}_{1}(\lambda)|\lesssim|\lambda|^{-1-\sigma},\qquad|{\mathcal{J}}_{2}(\lambda)|\lesssim|\lambda|^{-1-\sigma}

holds uniformly for large
|\lambda|
.

Estimation of
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
.
Before going further, we remark that the methods in this part actually provide a paradigm, which will be transplanted to other situations. Core portions in the proof of (
2.5
) are also performed in such a way, with more delicate discussions.

Now we write

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)J_{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+2}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-1}(1-\varphi)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta}\cdots+\int_{\delta}^{1}\cdots

\displaystyle=:{\mathcal{J}}_{1,1}(\lambda)+{\mathcal{J}}_{1,2}(\lambda).

Here
\delta=\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{q+2\alpha-1}}\in(\lambda_{1}^{-1},1)
. The reason of this choice will be clear soon.

For
{\mathcal{J}}_{1,1}
, from (
4.6
)-(
4.7
) we see that, for all
r\in\left[0,1/2\right]
,

\left|J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)\right|\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)^{\frac{q}{2}},&r\leq\lambda_{1}^{-1},\\
\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},&r\geq\lambda_{1}^{-1},\end{array}\qquad\left|J_{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\right|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.\right.

Then by the fact that
\operatorname{supp}(1-\varphi)\cap[0,1]\subset[0,1/2]
it yields

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{J}}_{1,1}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\left(\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{1}^{-1}}r^{q+\alpha-1}dr+\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\lambda_{1}^{-1}}^{\delta}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\left(\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{q}{2}-\alpha}+\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{q+2\alpha-1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sim|\lambda|^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Note that we have chosen
\delta
as above to balance the quantities in second the line.

For
{\mathcal{J}}_{1,2}
, since
\operatorname{supp}\varphi\cap[0,1]\subset[1/4,1]
, by our choice we have
\lambda_{1}\delta\gtrsim 1
, whence one can substitute the expansion (
4.8
) into its formula and obtain that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}_{1,2}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\cdot\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\left|\int_{\delta}^{1}e^{i\phi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+1}{\alpha}-1}(1-\varphi)dr\right|\right.

\displaystyle+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{5}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+1}{\alpha}-1}(1-\varphi)dr

\displaystyle\left.+\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1}{\alpha}-1}(1-\varphi)dr\right].

The third term in the bracket is seen to be controlled by
\lambda_{2}^{-1}
; while the second can be bounded by
\lambda_{1}^{-1/2}
, since

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{5}{2}}dr\lesssim\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{as}\ \alpha>\frac{3-q}{2},\\
\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\log\lambda_{1},&\mbox{as}\ q=2,\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Turning to the first term (designated by
FI(\lambda)
), we will employ Lemma
5.1
of van der Corput extensively, and so as to improve the integrability of the amplitude in some singular case, the skill of integration by parts (with the differential property (
4.4
) of Bessel functions) will also play an important part.

Note that
\phi_{\lambda}^{-}
behaves better than
\phi_{\lambda}^{+}
as in Lemma
5.4
, subsequently we will merely deduce the case with the latter phase. Once this is done, the former case can be proved similarly and more simply. This step will be proceeded repeatedly by default in such situation throughout Subsection
5.2
, without extra explanation. Similar convention will be adopted to Subsection
5.3
.

If
\alpha\geq\frac{3-q}{2}
, according to Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
we have

FI(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\sigma}\left(1+\int_{\delta}^{1}\left|\left[r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+1}{\alpha}-1}(1-\varphi)\right]^{\prime}\right|dr\right),

(5.12)

where
\sigma
is given by (
1.14
). Then
FI(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\sigma}
. Otherwise, it must hold that
\alpha<\frac{3-q}{2}
or equivalently,
q=2
and
\alpha<1/2
(since
q\geq 2
as explained in Subsection
1.2
). This can be splitted into two possible cases:
\delta\geq r_{*}
and
\delta<r_{*}
, where
r_{*}
is defined by (
5.6
). Note that
\sigma=1/2
at this moment.

(1) Let
q=2,\alpha<1/2
and
\delta\geq r_{*}
.
By Lemma
5.2
(ii), the decay
\lambda_{1}^{-\sigma}
in (
5.12
) can be improved to
\lambda_{1}^{-1}
. However,

\int_{\delta}^{1}\left|\left[r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+1}{\alpha}-1}(1-\varphi)\right]^{\prime}\right|dr\lesssim\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}dr\sim\delta^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha},

which implies that
FI(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Through these estimates we arrive at that
|{\mathcal{J}}_{1,2}(\lambda)|\lesssim|\lambda|^{-1-\sigma}
.

(2) Let
q=2,\alpha<1/2
and
\delta<r_{*}
.
In this case, by (
5.6
) one has
r_{*}\sim\left(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}
, from which, the condition
\delta<r_{*}
readily means that

\lambda_{1}\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1+2\alpha}{3\alpha}}.

(5.13)

Additionally, we need a new expression for
{\mathcal{J}}_{1,2}
.
Using the recursion formula (
4.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{1,2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}\left[J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}\right]^{\prime}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)r^{\frac{q}{2}}(1-\varphi)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}^{-1}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-\delta^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-\delta^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}\delta\right)\delta^{\frac{q}{2}}(1-\varphi(\delta))

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)r^{\frac{q}{2}}\varphi^{\prime}dr

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\delta}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)r^{\frac{q}{2}}(1-\varphi)dr.

The first resulting term has the upper bound
\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{q}{2}}\cdot\left(\lambda_{1}\delta\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\delta^{\frac{q-1}{2}}.
Since
\operatorname{supp}\varphi^{\prime}\subseteq\left[\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}\right]
, then the second term can be controlled by

\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}dr\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Regarding the third term (denoted by
T_{3}(\lambda)
), plugging (
4.8
) into it (with
1
-term and
N
-term expansions for
J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)
and
J_{\frac{m}{2}}(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{2/\alpha})
respectively) produces that

\displaystyle|T_{3}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left|\int_{\delta}^{1}e^{i\phi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1}{\alpha}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)^{-j}(1-\varphi)dr\right|\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-3}{\alpha}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)^{-N}(1-\varphi)dr\,\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{q-3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1}{\alpha}}(1-\varphi)dr\right]

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j=1}^{3}T_{3,j}(\lambda)

where
N\geq 2
is to be chosen. Then by (
5.13
) and fixing an integer
N>\frac{1+2\alpha}{3\alpha}
we have

\displaystyle T_{3,2}(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{2}^{-N}\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{q-1}{2}}dr\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-N}\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}},

\displaystyle T_{3,3}(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{q-3}{2}}dr\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

For
T_{3,1}(\lambda)
, since
\delta<r_{*}
, the integral inside is written as

\int_{\delta}^{1}\cdots=\int_{r_{*}}^{1}\cdots+\int_{\delta}^{r_{*}}\cdots,

whose associated quantities are denoted by
T_{3,1}^{\prime}
and
T_{3,1}^{\prime\prime}
respectively, so that
T_{3,1}=T_{3,1}^{\prime}+T_{3,1}^{\prime\prime}
.
For
T_{3,1}^{\prime}
, one acquires by using again Lemma
5.2
(ii) that

\displaystyle T_{3,1}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-j}\left(1+\int_{r_{*}}^{1}\left\lvert\,\left[r^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1}{\alpha}}\left(\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)^{-j}(1-\varphi)\right]^{\prime}\right|dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-j}\left(1+\int_{r_{*}}^{1}r^{\frac{q-1}{2}-1}dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

For
T_{3,1}^{\prime\prime}
, noting that at present
r_{*}^{-1}\sim\left({\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}^{-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\gtrsim{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}^{-1}}
, then the second estimation in Lemma
5.2
(ii) can be optimized as follows:

\left|\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime}(r)\right|\geqslant\left.\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-2}r^{\alpha-1}\cdot r^{-1}\right|_{r=r_{*}}\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1},\quad\forall\,r\in(0,r_{*}).

Hence from the van der Corput Lemma it follows that

\displaystyle T_{3,1}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda_{2}^{-j}\left(\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[1+\int_{\delta}^{r_{*}}\left\lvert\,\left[r^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1-2j}{\alpha}}(1-\varphi)\right]^{\prime}\right\lvert dr\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda_{2}^{-j}\left(\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\int_{\delta}^{r_{*}}r^{\frac{q-1}{2}-1}dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda_{2}^{-j}\left(\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Finally, putting these estimates together, we conclude that
|{\mathcal{J}}_{1}(\lambda)|\lesssim|\lambda|^{-1-\sigma}
as required.

Estimation of

.
The method is analogous to that of
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
.
We first write

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)J_{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+2}{\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-1}\varphi(r)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}\cdots+\int_{r_{\delta}}^{1}\cdots

\displaystyle=:{\mathcal{J}}_{2,1}(\lambda)+{\mathcal{J}}_{2,2}(\lambda),

with
\delta=\bar{c}\,\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{m+1}}<1
(

is a positive constant and
c_{2}
is assumed already in Lemma
2.4
), where we have put

For
{\mathcal{J}}_{2,2}
, observing that
\operatorname{supp}\,\varphi\cap[0,1]\subset[1/4,1]
. Then by the choice of
\delta
and (
4.6
)-(
4.7

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{J}}_{2,2}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\cdot\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int^{1}_{1-\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\left(\lambda_{2}(1-r)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}-1}(1-r)^{\frac{m+2}{\alpha}-1}dr

\displaystyle+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\cdot\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\int_{r_{\delta}}^{1-\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\left(\lambda_{2}(1-r)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-r)^{\frac{m+2}{\alpha}-1}dr\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sim|\lambda|^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

To dominate
{\mathcal{J}}_{2,1}

4.8
) and Lemma
5.1

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{J}}_{2,1}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\cdot\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\left|\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}e^{i\phi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+1}{\alpha}-1}\varphi(r)dr\right|\right.

\displaystyle+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{5}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+1}{\alpha}-1}\varphi(r)dr

\displaystyle\left.+\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1}{\alpha}-1}\varphi(r)dr\right].

The second and third resulting terms in the bracket are well controlled by
\lambda_{1}^{-1}
and
\lambda_{2}^{-1}\log\lambda_{2}
respectively. Regarding the first term (denoted by
FI(\lambda)
as before), using Lemma
5.1

FI(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\sigma}\left(1+\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}\left|\left[r^{\frac{q}{2}+\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m+1}{\alpha}-1}\varphi(r)\right]^{\prime}\right|dr\right).

(5.14)

As a result, if
\alpha\leq m+1

, which implies
\left|{\mathcal{J}}_{2,1}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}
. As for the rest case that
\alpha>m+1
(so
\alpha>2
and
\sigma=1/2
), there are two possible subcases:
r_{\delta}\leq r_{*}
and
r_{\delta}>r_{*}
, to which we next turn.

(1) Let
\alpha>m+1
and

.
Note that

\left(1-r_{*}^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\sim\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\alpha}}

(5.15)

by (

\lambda_{1}\gtrsim\lambda_{2}^{\frac{\alpha+2m}{2+2m}}

which also leads to the desired estimate for
{\mathcal{J}}_{2,1}(\lambda)
.

(2) Let
\alpha>m+1
and
r_{\delta}>r_{*}
.
Note also at this point it holds that

\lambda_{1}\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{\frac{\alpha+2m}{2+2m}}.

(5.16)

A new expression of
{\mathcal{J}}_{2,1}
is derived from an integration by parts with (
4.4
) as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{2,1}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}^{-1}\left[J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r_{\delta}^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r_{\delta}^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r_{\delta}\right)r^{\frac{q}{2}}\varphi\right]\right|_{r=r_{\delta}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)r^{\frac{q}{2}}\varphi^{\prime}dr

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m}{\alpha}}J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)r^{\frac{q}{2}}\varphi dr.

The first two terms are bounded by
\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{3}{2}}
as easily verified. For the third term (denoted by
T_{3}(\lambda)
), applying (
4.8
) with
1
-term and
N
-term expansions for
J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}r\right)

\displaystyle\left|T_{3}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\sum_{j=0}^{{N-1}}\lambda_{2}^{-j}\left|\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}e^{i\phi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{q}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1-2j}{\alpha}}\varphi(r)dr\right|\right.

\displaystyle+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}r^{\frac{q}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1}{\alpha}}\varphi(r)dr

\displaystyle\left.+\int^{r_{\delta}}_{0}r^{\frac{q}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-3}{\alpha}}\left(\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\right)^{-N}\varphi(r)dr\right],

where
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
is to be chosen. In fact, the second term in the bracket is bounded by
\lambda_{1}^{-1}
; by fixing
N>\frac{\alpha+2m}{1+m}
and using (
5.16
) we see the last one can be majorized by
\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}N}\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1}.
Thus, one can reduce matters to showing that

\left|\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}e^{i\phi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{q}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{m-1-2j}{\alpha}}\varphi(r)dr\right|\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{j}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}.

This can be done as follows. Firstly, note that

\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\geq\delta\gtrsim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mbox{for all}\ r\in(0,r_{\delta}).

Secondly, split the integral into two parts:
(0,r_{*})
and
(r_{*},r_{\delta})
. Lastly, for
(0,r_{*})
part we employ Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
(i); while for
(r_{*},r_{\delta})
part, we use again Lemma
5.1
with the following fact (by (
5.15
))

\left|\left(\phi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime}(r)\right|\geq\left.\left(2(\alpha-2)\lambda_{2}\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}r^{\alpha-1}\cdot\left(1-r^{\alpha}\right)^{-1}\right)\right|_{r=r_{*}}=\frac{\alpha-2}{2}\lambda_{1}\cdot(1-r_{*}^{\alpha})^{-1}\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}.

which is an improvement of the second estimation in Lemma
5.2
(i). As a result, we conclude that
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}(\lambda)\lesssim|\lambda|^{-1-\sigma}
as required, and the proof of
Case (i)
is finished.

5.2.2
Proof of Case (ii):
\lambda_{1}<\mathbf{C_{\alpha}}\lambda_{2}

Note that
\lambda_{2}\sim_{\alpha}|\lambda|
and recall (
5.2
). Following a similar line of
reasoning, one can scrutiny under the condition where
\alpha+m\geq 2
, it holds that
|{\mathcal{K}}(\lambda)|\lesssim|\lambda|^{-1-\sigma}
. We sketch the main steps, and the details will be provided only when major differences arise.

Step 1:
Performing integration by parts with (

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-1}dr

(5.17)

Thus, it remains to show that

Step 2:
To dominate
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
, we split the integral into two parts

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{1,1}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\lambda_{2}^{-1}}\lambda_{2}^{\frac{m}{2}}r^{\frac{m}{2}}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-1}dr+\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\lambda_{2}^{-1}}^{\delta}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}^{\frac{m}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha+2m}{2}}+\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\delta^{\frac{\alpha+m-1}{2}}\right)

(5.19)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

In order to assess
{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}
, we use (
4.8
) and obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\left|\int_{\delta}^{1}e^{i\psi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}(1-\varphi)dr\right|\right.

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{5}{2}}(1-\varphi)dr

\displaystyle\left.+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}(1-\varphi)dr\right].

For the first term (denoted by

Thus the case where

\lambda_{2}\gtrsim\lambda_{1}
). The main difficulty lies in the remaining case that

. If
\alpha\in(0,1]
then the desired conclusion can be drawn from a similar argument as in the estimation of
{\mathcal{J}}_{1,2}
in Subsubsection
5.2.1
, with the roles of
r_{*}
replaced by
R_{*}
defined as in (
5.8
), and of Lemma
5.2
(ii) replaced by Lemma
5.3
(ii), respectively.

Now focus on the case

. Recall the numbers

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle-\left.\lambda_{2}^{-1}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2\alpha}}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)r^{\frac{m}{2}}(1-\varphi)\right|_{\delta}^{1}

\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{3}{2}}
. For the third term (denoted by

), from (

) it follows that, for

to be chosen,

\displaystyle|TI(\lambda)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left|\int_{\delta}^{1}e^{i\psi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2\alpha}}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)^{-j}(1-\varphi)dr\right|\right.

5.3
, where the case that

5.3
Proof of (

We divide the problem into two cases as well:

Recall from (

(5.23)

respectively, under the conditions that
\alpha+m\geq 3
and
\alpha\leq\frac{q}{2}+1
.

5.3.1
Estimation of
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}

Let

. Retain the notation as in (
5.18

5.19
) and the assumption we obtain that
|{\mathcal{K}}_{1,1}(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which is better than the desired bound since

. To assess
{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}
, we use (
4.8
) to find that

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\left|\int_{\delta}^{1}e^{i\psi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2\alpha}-1}(1-\varphi)dr\right|\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{5}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2\alpha}-1}(1-\varphi)dr

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2\alpha}-1}(1-\varphi)dr\right].

Then, for
\alpha+m\geq 3
, from Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
it follows that

\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}^{-\sigma}+\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\right).

Hence when
\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}^{2}
it holds that
|{\mathcal{K}}_{1}(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{3}{2}-\sigma}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Consider now
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}^{2}
. Performing integration by parts with (
4.4
) we have

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}\,{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}(\lambda)=\lambda_{2}^{-1}J_{\frac{m}{2}+1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)\left.J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-1}(1-\varphi)\right|_{\delta}^{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}+1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-1}\varphi^{\prime}dr

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\int_{\delta}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}+1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}-2}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+4}{2\alpha}-2}r^{\frac{2\alpha+m}{2}-2}(1-\varphi)dr

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{\delta}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}+1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-2}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-2+r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)(1-\varphi)dr.

Note that
\lambda_{2}r\gtrsim 1
on
[\delta,1]
. Thus, using the conditions that
\alpha+m\geq 3
and
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}^{2}
, together with (
4.7
) it is easy to examine that
\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. These results therefore lead to the first estimate in (
5.23
).

5.3.2
Estimation of
{\mathcal{K}}_{2}

Let
\alpha\leq\frac{q}{2}+1
. Using an integration by parts with (
4.4
), we can write

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-1}\varphi(r)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}\cdots+\int_{r_{\delta}}^{1}\cdots dr

\displaystyle=:{\mathcal{K}}_{2,1}(\lambda)+{\mathcal{K}}_{2,2}(\lambda).

Here
r_{\delta}=\left(1-\delta^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}
with
\delta={c}_{0}\,\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{q}{q+1}}
and
{c}_{0}<(2\pi c_{1})^{q/(q+1)}
fixed.
We distinguish the following comparison relations:
\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}^{q}
and
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}^{q}
.

Case (I):
\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}^{q}
.
Notice that
\operatorname{supp}\varphi\cap[0,1]\subset[1/4,1]
. Then through (
4.6
)-(
4.7
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2,2}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}^{1}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}(1-r)^{\frac{q-2}{2\alpha}+\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-1}dr+\int_{r_{\delta}}^{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-r)^{\frac{q+1}{2\alpha}-1}dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{1}\cdot(\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\lambda_{1}^{-q}+\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{\frac{q+1}{2}}),

which shows that
\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2,2}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For
{\mathcal{K}}_{2,1}
we use (
4.8
) and acquire that

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2,1}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\cdot{\lambda_{2}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left|\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}e^{i\psi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2\alpha}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)^{-j}\varphi dr\right|\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{5}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2\alpha}-1}\varphi dr

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2\alpha}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)^{-N}\varphi dr\right],

where
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
is to be determined later. Note that under our assumption

\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\geq\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{q+1}}\gtrsim\lambda_{2}^{\frac{1}{q^{2}+q}},\quad\mbox{for all}\ r\in[0,r_{\delta}].

(5.24)

Then invoking Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
produces that

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2,1}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{j}{q^{2}+q}}\left(\chi_{(0,\frac{q+1}{2}]}(\alpha)+\delta^{\frac{q+1-2\alpha}{2}}\chi_{(\frac{q+1}{2},\infty)}(\alpha)\right)\lambda_{2}^{-\sigma}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\lambda_{2}^{-1}+\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{N}{q^{2}+q}}\right),

(5.25)

which, together with the choice that
N=\lfloor q^{2}+q\rfloor+1
, implies the desired estimate for
\alpha\leq\frac{q+1}{2}.
For the remaining case where
\frac{q+1}{2}<\alpha\leq\frac{q+2}{2}
, we exploit the skills developed in the previous subsection. And there arise two situations:
\alpha\geq 2
and
\alpha<2.

(1) Let
\alpha\geq 2
and
\frac{q+1}{2}<\alpha\leq\frac{q+2}{2}
.
First note that
\sigma=1/2
at this point. Via (
5.8
) one has

\left(1-R_{*}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sim_{\alpha}{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{-1}}.

(5.26)

Hence from the proof of Lemma
5.3
(i) we may conclude that, for all
r\in(0,R_{*})
,

\left|\left(\psi_{\lambda}^{+}\right)^{\prime\prime}(r)\right|\geq\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-2}r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\geq\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{2}\left(1-R_{*}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{-1}\gtrsim\lambda_{2}\left({\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{-1}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}.

(5.27)

Next, we further divide the problem into two subcases:
\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{1}^{2}
and
\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{1}^{2}
.

Focus on the first subcase
\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{1}^{2}
. If
R_{*}\geq r_{\delta}
, then the
\lambda_{2}^{-\sigma}
decay in (
5.25
) can be improved to
\lambda_{2}^{-1}
by Lemma
5.3
(i). But in this subcase

\lambda_{2}^{-1}\cdot\delta^{\frac{q+1-2\alpha}{2}}\sim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q(2\alpha-q-1)}{2(q+1)}}\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}},

whence we obtain
\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2,1}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
by choosing the same
N
as before; If
R_{*}<r_{\delta}
, then we split the integral into two parts,
(0,R_{*})
and
(R_{*},r_{\delta})
. The first part can be handled by the previous line of reasoning; for the second part, the decay
\lambda_{2}^{-\sigma}
can be improved to
\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}^{-1})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-2}}

if we use the van der Corput Lemma with (
5.27
). Therefore the
required estimate follows since it also holds that

\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}^{-1})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-2}}\cdot\delta^{\frac{q+1-2\alpha}{2}}\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

in this subcase with our assumptions.

Focus on the second subcase
\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{1}^{2}
. Using integration by parts we get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{2,1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\left.=-\lambda_{2}^{-1}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2\alpha}}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)r^{\frac{m}{2}}\varphi\right|_{0}^{r_{\delta}}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2\alpha}}J_{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)r^{\frac{m}{2}}\varphi^{\prime}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{2\alpha}}J_{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)r^{\frac{m}{2}}\varphi dr.

The first resulting term can be bounded by
\lambda^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
if we use (
4.7
); while the second can also be controlled by this amount via Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
(i) (note that
\operatorname{supp}\varphi^{\prime}\subset[1/4,1/2]
bringing no singularity to the integrand). To assess the first term, we employ (
4.8
) with
N
-term and
2
-term expansions for
J_{\frac{q}{2}}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)
and
J_{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)
respectively) and find that, for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\left|{{\mathcal{K}}_{2,1}}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{\pm}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sum_{k=0}^{1}\left|\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}e^{i\psi_{\lambda}^{\pm}(r)}r^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2\alpha}}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)^{-j}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)^{-k}\varphi dr\right|\right.

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}^{-2}\int_{0}^{r_{\delta}}r^{\frac{m-5}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2\alpha}}\varphi dr

\displaystyle\left.+\int^{r_{\delta}}_{0}r^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-3}{2\alpha}}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)^{-N}\varphi dr\right].

Similarly, fixing large
N
and using (
5.24
) the last two terms are majorized by
\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{5}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.
Turn to the first term (denoted by
FI(\lambda)
). If
R_{*}\geq r_{\delta}
, then by Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
(i) with (
5.24
) we have in this subcase

FI(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
R_{*}<r_{\delta}
, we split the integration interval as
(0,R_{*})\cup(R_{*},r_{\delta})
. The first part can be handled by a similar argument; while the second is bounded by

\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sum_{k=0}^{1}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{j}{q+1}}\lambda_{2}^{-k}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}^{-1})^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-2}}\left(1-R_{*}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2\alpha}}

via Lemmas
5.1
(with (
5.27
)) and (
5.24
), which is majorized by

\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left({\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{-1}}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha+q-1}{2\alpha-2}}\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}

from (
5.26
).

(2) Let
\alpha<2
and
\frac{q+1}{2}<\alpha\leq\frac{q+2}{2}
.
Note that we have
q=2
,
\alpha\in(3/2,2)
and
\sigma=1/3
. The proof is similar to that of the former situation; cf. also the estimation of
{\mathcal{K}}_{1,2}
in
Step 2
of Subsubsection
5.2.2
. We omit the details.

Case (II):
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}^{q}
.

Recall that
q\geq 2
and we always assume that
\alpha\leq\frac{q}{2}+1
in this subsubsection. An integration by parts with (
4.4
) gives

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\left[\lambda_{2}^{-1}J_{\frac{m}{2}+1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)\left.J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-1}\varphi\right|_{0}^{1}\right.

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}+1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-1}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-1}\varphi^{\prime}dr

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}+1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}-2}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+4}{2\alpha}-2}r^{\frac{2\alpha+m}{2}-2}\varphi dr

\displaystyle\left.\quad-\lambda_{2}^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\frac{m}{2}+1}\left(\lambda_{2}r\right)J_{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-2}r^{\frac{\alpha+m}{2}-2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-2+r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\varphi dr\right]

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j=1}^{4}{\mathcal{K}}_{2}^{(j)}(\lambda).

Note that
\operatorname{supp}\varphi\cap[0,1]\subset[1/4,1]
. By (
4.6
) we have

\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2}^{(1)}(\lambda)\right|+\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2}^{(2)}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\left(\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{3}{2}}+\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{3}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

And from (
4.6
)-(
4.7
) one sees that

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2}^{(4)}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}^{1}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+2}{2\alpha}-2+\frac{q-2}{2\alpha}}\left|\frac{\alpha}{2}-2+r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right|dr\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\int_{0}^{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2\alpha}-2}dr\right).

The second integral in the brace is actually bounded under our assumption that
\alpha\leq\frac{q}{2}+1
. For the first one, when
\alpha<q
it has the upper bound

\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}^{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{\alpha}-2}dr\sim\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\cdot\lambda_{1}^{\alpha-q}\lesssim 1;

alternatively when
\alpha\geq q
, we must have
\alpha=q=2
, whence
\frac{\alpha}{2}-2+r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}=r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-1
and the first integral then can be controlled by

\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\int_{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}^{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)dr\lesssim 1.

This implies that
\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2}^{(4)}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For
{\mathcal{K}}_{2}^{(3)}
, using (
4.6
)-(
4.7
) again we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2}^{(3)}(\lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1})^{2}\cdot\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}^{1}\left|J_{\frac{q}{2}-2}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\right|\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q+4}{2\alpha}-2}dr\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\int_{0}^{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{\frac{q+3}{2\alpha}-2}dr\right).

The second term in the brace is majorized by
\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. For the first one, when
\alpha<q
it has the upper bound

\int_{1-\lambda_{1}^{-\alpha}}^{1}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q-4}{2}}\left(1-r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q-4}{2\alpha}+\frac{q+4}{2\alpha}-2}dr\sim\lambda_{1}^{\frac{q-4}{2}-q+\alpha}\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1},

since
\alpha\leq\frac{q}{2}+1
; when
\alpha\geq q
(so
\alpha=q=2
), noting that
J_{-1}=-J_{1}
via (
4.5
), thus it can be bounded by

\int_{1-\lambda_{1}^{-2}}^{1}\lambda_{1}(1-r)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(1-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dr\lesssim\lambda_{1}^{-1}.

These estimates and the assumption
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}^{q}
then lead to that

\left|{\mathcal{K}}_{2}^{(3)}(\lambda)\right|\lesssim(\lambda_{2}^{-1}\lambda_{1})^{2}\cdot\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\lambda_{2}^{-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, we have verified (
5.23
) and completed the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
