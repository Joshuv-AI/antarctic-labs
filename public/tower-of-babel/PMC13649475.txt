High-affinity ELR+ CXC chemokines display balanced G protein-, GRK- and β-arrestin-associated transducer engagement at the viral G protein-coupled receptor ORF74

Qianqian Kong, Brent Van Bosstraeten, Steven De Jonghe, Dominique Schols, Tom Van Loy

Abstract

Open reading frame 74 (ORF74) is the single G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) encoded in the genome of Kaposi’s sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (KSHV). Due to its high constitutive activity and broad chemokine binding profile, ORF74 contributes to KSHV-associated diseases through autocrine and paracrine mechanisms that promote cell proliferation, inflammation and angiogenesis. Previous studies showed that ELR+ CXC chemokines (i.e., CXCL1–3 and CXCL5–8), which contain a conserved Glu-Leu-Arg motif, bind ORF74 with high affinity. Among these chemokines, CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8 act as full or partial agonists in ORF74-mediated G protein signaling and β-arrestin recruitment, whereas CXCL5 behaves as a neutral ligand. However, whether these chemokines preferentially engage specific intracellular transducers remains unclear. We therefore applied a unified NanoLuc Binary Technology (NanoBiT) platform in HEK293A cells to systematically compare how ELR+ CXC chemokines modulate ORF74–transducer proximity across the G protein–GRK–β-arrestin axis. CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8, but not CXCL5, induced concentration-dependent ORF74-specific responses involving miniGi recruitment, Gβ1 proximity, GRK2/3/5/6 proximity and β-arrestin1/2 recruitment. Using CXCL1 as the reference ligand, bias analysis showed that the active ELR+ CXC chemokines engage ORF74 in a largely balanced manner across these proximal transducer readouts.

Introduction

G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) are membrane proteins that mediate cellular responses to diverse extracellular stimuli, including hormones, neurotransmitters, peptides, lipids and sensory cues. Upon activation, GPCRs engage intracellular signaling machinery, most notably heterotrimeric G proteins (Gαβγ), G protein-coupled receptor kinases (GRKs) and β-arrestins, thereby initiating complex signaling networks that regulate physiological processes (). Accumulating evidence demonstrates that GPCRs can adopt multiple active conformations, each enabling distinct engagement with signal transducers and resulting in different signaling outcomes. This dynamic nature of GPCR conformations supports the concept of ligand bias, also referred to as biased agonism, since different ligands may stabilize distinct conformations of the same receptor and thus promote preferential coupling to particular G proteins, GRKs or β-arrestins ().

Ligand bias is particularly relevant for promiscuous signaling systems. Human chemokines are small proteins that, through interaction with their cognate GPCRs, regulate immune cell migration and activation. Deregulation of the chemokine–chemokine receptor signaling axis is associated with many inflammatory diseases and cancer (; ). Human chemokine receptors frequently interact with multiple chemokines, which may elicit ligand-specific signaling outputs (). For several receptors, such as CXCR1 (; ), CXCR2 (; ; ), CXCR3 (; ), and CCR7 (; ), ligand-dependent differences in G protein activation, GRK and/or β-arrestin recruitment, and receptor trafficking have been reported. These findings highlight the prevalence of biased signaling within the chemokine receptor family and suggest that it may serve as an additional layer of chemokine receptor regulation.

Several human herpesviruses have acquired chemokine receptor homologues in their genomes during co-evolution with their hosts. Expression and activity of these receptors support viral persistence and dissemination and contribute to infection-related pathogenesis (; ; ). Open reading frame 74 (ORF74) is the single GPCR encoded by Kaposi’s sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (KSHV), also known as human herpesvirus 8 (HHV8) (). ORF74 is most closely related to the human chemokine receptor CXCR2, displays high constitutive activity and interacts with a broad ligand repertoire, comprising at least fourteen chemokines from the CXC and CC subfamilies that modulate receptor activity as agonists, inverse agonists or antagonists (; ; ). In particular, ELR+ CXC chemokines (i.e., CXCL1–3 and CXCL5–8), which contain a conserved Glu-Leu-Arg motif, bind ORF74 with high affinity and, with the exception of CXCL5, act as ORF74 agonists (). Among them, CXCL1 displays the highest apparent affinity for ORF74, and recent cryo-EM structures of CXCL1-bound ORF74–Gi complexes have revealed its ligand-binding mode and Gi-coupled active conformation (; ).

This broad and functionally diverse ORF74 ligand repertoire raises the possibility that individual chemokines may stabilize distinct receptor conformations, differentially regulate downstream signaling pathways and fine-tune ORF74-mediated signaling (). However, it remains unclear whether individual ligands exhibit genuine signaling bias through preferential engagement of specific intracellular transducers, as observed for human chemokine receptors (). To address this question, we employed a unified NanoLuc Binary Technology (NanoBiT) platform in a common cellular background to investigate ORF74-associated transducer responses induced by ELR+ CXC chemokines. This analysis confirmed that CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8 activate ORF74 across miniGi, Gβ1, GRK2/3/5/6 and β-arrestin1/2 readouts, but revealed no significant ligand bias relative to CXCL1, indicating largely balanced ORF74 engagement across the G protein–GRK–β-arrestin transducer axis.

Materials and methods

Chemokines, reagents and plasmids

Recombinant human CXCL1 (#300-11), CXCL2 (#300-39), CXCL3 (#300-40), CXCL5 (#300-22B), CXCL6 (#300-41), CXCL7 (#300-14), and CXCL8 (#200-08M) were purchased from PeproTech. The pcDNA3.1(+)-ORF74 plasmid was generated in-house as described previously (). ORF74-LgBiT and ORF74-SmBiT were constructed from pcDNA3.1(+)-ORF74 by fusing LgBiT or SmBiT, respectively, to the C terminus of ORF74 through a linker (GNSSSGGGGSGGGGSSG). SmBiT-Gβ1, SmBiT-β-arrestin1, and SmBiT-β-arrestin2 were constructed in the pEF1α-IRES vector by fusing SmBiT to the N terminus of Gβ1, β-arrestin1, or β-arrestin2, respectively, through a linker (GSSGGGGSGGGG). miniGi is an engineered GTPase domain of the Gαi subunit (). LgBiT-miniGi and LgBiT-GRK2/3/5/6, which carry an N-terminal LgBiT tag, were kindly provided by Dr. Andy Chevigné (Luxembourg Institute of Health) (; ).

Cell culture

HEK293A cells were kindly provided by Prof. A. Inoue (Tohoku University, Sendai, Japan) and maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium, high glucose (DMEM; Thermo Fisher Scientific, #41965-039) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Cytiva, #SV30160.03) at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

NanoBiT based transducer proximity assays

Ligand-induced proximity between ORF74 and miniGi, Gβ1, GRK2/3/5/6 or β-arrestin1/2 was monitored using a NanoBiT complementation assay. HEK293A cells were transiently co-transfected in suspension using FuGENE® HD Transfection Reagent (Promega, #E2312) with the following biosensor pairs at a 1:1 ratio: ORF74-SmBiT with LgBiT-miniGi or LgBiT-GRK2/3/5/6; and ORF74-LgBiT with SmBiT-Gβ1 or SmBiT-β-arrestin1/2. Cells were seeded in poly-D-lysine-coated white 96-well plates (Corning, #3610; 2.5 × 104 cells/well; 50 ng DNA per well) and cultured for 48 h. Next, cells were washed once with assay buffer [Hanks’ Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS; Thermo Fisher Scientific), 20 mM HEPES, 0.5% FBS, pH 7.4] and incubated with 80 μL/well of a 1:200 diluted Nano-Glo® Live Cell Substrate (Promega, #N2012) working solution for 30 min at 37 °C. Luminescence was recorded using the FLIPR Penta system (Molecular Devices), with baseline recording followed by addition of serial chemokine dilutions (final concentration range, 0.06–250 nM) and monitoring for 20 min.

Real-time data were normalized to baseline and corrected by subtracting the vehicle control. Responses were quantified as area under the curve (AUC) over 0–20 min after chemokine addition, with signal changes above and below baseline defined as positive and negative responses, respectively. Data are expressed as normalized AUC (%), with the maximal CXCL1 response within each assay set to 100% for positive responses and −100% for negative responses.

Data analysis and ligand bias assessment

Data are reported as mean ± SEM from three or five independent experiments, each performed in duplicate. NanoBiT concentration–response curves were fitted using a three-parameter logistic model in GraphPad Prism v10.4.2, from which pEC50 and Emax values were derived. For negative NanoBiT responses, Emax values were reported as negative values. Ligands that did not produce clear concentration-dependent responses, with absolute signal changes below 20% of the maximal CXCL1 response, were not fitted and were reported as not determined. Statistical comparisons of pEC50 and Emax values were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Dunnett’s multiple comparisons test, with CXCL1 as the reference ligand.

Equimolar comparison plots were generated from the normalized concentration–response data by plotting responses measured at the same ligand concentrations against each other for selected pairs of ORF74-associated NanoBiT readouts. For readouts with negative NanoBiT responses, response direction was inverted for visualization and absolute Emax values were used for bias calculations. Ligand bias was assessed using log(Emax/EC50) as a transduction coefficient, with Emax values expressed as fractions and EC50 values expressed in molar concentrations ().

For each readout, Δlog(Emax/EC50) values were calculated by subtracting the log(Emax/EC50) value of the reference ligand CXCL1 from that of each test ligand:
1. Δ⁡logEmaxEC50=logEmaxEC50lig−logEmaxEC50ref

The bias index was calculated as ΔΔlog(Emax/EC50) by subtracting the Δlog(Emax/EC50) value for the comparator pathway from that for the pathway of interest.
2. ΔΔ⁡logEmaxEC50=Δ⁡logEmaxEC50P1−Δ⁡logEmaxEC50P2

Statistical significance of ligand bias was determined using multiple unpaired Welch’s t-tests with Holm–Šídák correction for multiple comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

ELR+ CXC chemokines induce ORF74-associated transducer responses

We previously showed that ELR+ CXC chemokines interact with ORF74 with apparent potencies in the nanomolar range. CXCL1 was the most potent ligand, while CXCL2, CXCL3 and CXCL6 were only slightly less potent. CXCL5, CXCL7 and CXCL8 showed substantially lower and broadly similar potencies (). We aimed to investigate whether these ELR+ CXC chemokines differentially engage proximal ORF74-associated transducers and thereby produce measurable ligand bias. For reference, representative raw luminescence traces for all NanoBiT biosensor pairs, recorded before and for 20 min after the addition of 250 nM CXCL1 or vehicle, are shown in .

Given previous evidence supporting Gi coupling by ORF74, we first monitored ligand-induced miniGi recruitment as a Gi-associated activation readout. miniGi is an engineered Gαi-derived probe that recognizes active GPCR conformations while lacking the N-terminal membrane anchors and Gβγ-binding interface of native Gα subunits (). CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8 induced concentration-dependent increases in ORF74–miniGi luminescence, whereas CXCL5 had little effect, despite its previously observed binding to ORF74. Among the active ligands, CXCL1 and CXCL3 behaved as full agonists, whereas CXCL2 and CXCL6–8 displayed partial agonist activity. CXCL1–3 showed the highest potency, while CXCL7 was the least potent active ligand (; ).

In parallel, we monitored ligand-induced ORF74–Gβ1 proximity as a complementary readout that may reflect receptor–G protein engagement and/or conformational rearrangement (). Similar to the miniGi readout, CXCL1–3 induced robust concentration-dependent increases in ORF74–Gβ1 luminescence, whereas CXCL6–8 produced weaker responses with lower potency and/or efficacy (; ). CXCL5 again failed to induce a clear concentration-dependent response, consistent with its minimal activity in the miniGi assay ().

GRKs are key regulators of GPCR signaling that phosphorylate activated receptors, thereby promoting β-arrestin recruitment and subsequent receptor desensitization and trafficking. Cytosolic GRK2/3 are recruited to the plasma membrane through free Gβγ subunits released upon G protein activation, whereas the membrane-associated GRK5/6 are less dependent on Gβγ availability and can engage receptors with reduced dependence on G protein activation (). We next assessed how ELR+ CXC chemokines modulate ORF74–GRK2/3/5/6 NanoBiT responses.

For GRK2 and GRK3, active ELR+ CXC chemokines induced concentration-dependent increases in luminescence, with an overall pattern similar to that observed for ORF74–Gβ1. CXCL1–3 produced robust ORF74–GRK2/3 responses, whereas CXCL6–8 showed more variable and generally weaker responses (; ). The response direction was reversed for GRK5 and GRK6, with chemokines producing concentration-dependent decreases in ORF74–GRK luminescence. CXCL1–3 induced substantial negative responses, whereas CXCL6–8 also produced marked decreases but with lower potency (; ). Across all four GRK readouts, CXCL5 failed to induce clear dose-dependent changes in ORF74–GRK luminescence (). The negative GRK5/6 responses likely reflect ligand-induced changes in ORF74–GRK5/6 proximity and/or tag orientation rather than direct evidence of GRK5/6 dissociation from ORF74 ().

Finally, ligand-induced β-arrestin recruitment to ORF74 was assessed. These data include three independent experiments reported previously (), combined with two additional independent experiments performed for CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8 in the present analysis. CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8 induced concentration-dependent recruitment of both β-arrestin1 and β-arrestin2, whereas CXCL5 failed to induce clear responses up to 250 nM and showed a largely neutral profile (). For β-arrestin1 recruitment, CXCL1 and CXCL3 showed the highest efficacy, whereas CXCL2 and CXCL6–8 behaved as partial agonists. A similar ligand ranking was observed for β-arrestin2 recruitment, although CXCL2 and CXCL6–8 showed higher efficacy than for β-arrestin1 recruitment (; ).

Equimolar comparison plots reveal similar ORF74-associated transducer profiles among active ELR+ CXC chemokines

To visualize how ELR+ CXC chemokines modulate ORF74-associated responses across different NanoBiT readouts, equimolar comparison plots were generated for selected pairs, with the maximal CXCL1 response set to 100% for each readout. In these plots, the dotted line indicates equal normalized responses between the two compared readouts (; ). For CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8, miniGi and Gβ1 responses were largely aligned with the line of equal normalized response (). These chemokines also showed closely matched responses within the GRK2/3 and GRK5/6 subfamilies, respectively (). In contrast, the GRK3–GRK5 comparison showed a shift toward higher ORF74–GRK5 response (). Gβ1 responses were comparable to those of GRK3 (), whereas GRK5 responses were again higher in the Gβ1–GRK5 comparison (). Comparisons of miniGi or Gβ1 with β-arrestin1/2 showed a general shift toward more pronounced β-arrestin recruitment at matched ligand concentrations (), while β-arrestin1 and β-arrestin2 recruitment was largely similar (). Subtle ligand-dependent differences were also observed, particularly in the miniGi–β-arrestin1/2 plots. In the miniGi–β-arrestin1 comparison, CXCL7 remained close to the line of equal normalized response, indicating similar miniGi and β-arrestin1 responses, whereas CXCL1 showed a pronounced shift toward β-arrestin1 recruitment. This difference implies a relative shift of CXCL7 toward miniGi compared with CXCL1 (). Collectively, these equimolar comparison plots indicate broadly matched responses for CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8 in the miniGi–Gβ1, GRK2–GRK3, GRK5–GRK6 and β-arrestin1–β-arrestin2 comparisons, while also revealing shared readout-dependent differences in comparisons involving GRK5 or β-arrestin1/2.

Active ELR+ CXC chemokines do not display pronounced G protein-, GRK- or β-arrestin-selective bias relative to CXCL1 at ORF74

While the visual comparisons () provided an overview of ORF74-associated transducer profiles, ligand-specific bias relative to CXCL1 was further assessed by quantitative bias index analysis. Bias index values were calculated using the ΔΔlog(Emax/EC50) approach (), with CXCL1 as the reference ligand. For example, in the miniGi–Gβ1 comparison, positive values indicate preference toward miniGi, whereas negative values indicate preference toward Gβ1. However, no significant bias relative to CXCL1 was observed for CXCL2, CXCL3, CXCL6, CXCL7, or CXCL8 in this comparison (). Comparison of miniGi with β-arrestin1/2 recruitment similarly revealed no major G protein-versus β-arrestin-associated bias, although CXCL7 showed a small but significant preference toward miniGi over β-arrestin1 relative to CXCL1 (). We next assessed comparisons within the Gβ1–GRK–β-arrestin axis, using GRK3 and GRK5 as representative readouts for the GRK2/3 and GRK5/6 subfamilies, respectively. No significant bias was detected in comparisons of Gβ1 with GRK3/5 or β-arrestin1/2, or between GRK3 and GRK5 (). Among β-arrestin isoforms, only CXCL7 showed a small but significant preference for β-arrestin2 over β-arrestin1 (). Overall, in the HEK293A NanoBiT system, CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8 displayed largely balanced engagement of ORF74 across the G protein–GRK–β-arrestin transducer axis relative to CXCL1.

Discussion

Many human chemokine receptors interact with multiple chemokine ligands, creating a complex ligand–receptor network that regulates immune cell activation, migration, and inflammatory responses. Rather than representing simple redundancy, such promiscuous ligand recognition can generate ligand-dependent differences in receptor signaling (). Whether this concept can be extended to viral GPCRs remains unclear. Notably, ligand-dependent functional selectivity has been described for the human cytomegalovirus (HCMV)-encoded chemokine receptor US28, where different chemokines and engineered CX3CL1 variants differentially modulate G protein-associated calcium signaling and migration responses (). ORF74 is another example of this complexity, since it interacts with at least fourteen chemokines, including the seven ELR+ CXC chemokines CXCL1–3 and CXCL5–8 (; ). Previous studies have shown that these ELR+ CXC chemokines display varying degrees of ORF74 agonism, whereas other ORF74-interacting chemokines can behave as neutral ligands or inverse agonists. Moreover, pharmacological inhibitor studies showed that ORF74-mediated chemotaxis is highly Gi-dependent, whereas responses induced by individual ELR+ CXC chemokines differed in their sensitivity to Gβγ and phospholipase C (PLC) inhibition (). These findings raised the question of whether the differences in signaling potency, efficacy, and downstream inhibitor sensitivity within this ELR+ CXC chemokine agonist series are accompanied by distinct ORF74-associated transducer interaction profiles and ligand bias.

We used NanoBiT-based proximity assays in HEK293A cells to compare how ELR+ CXC chemokines modulate ORF74 proximity to miniGi, Gβ1, GRK2/3/5/6 and β-arrestin1/2. Their experimental tractability and high transfection efficiency make HEK293A cells well suited for transient NanoBiT biosensor assays. By applying the same readout and cellular background across these transducer assays, this approach minimizes experimental variability and enables direct comparison of ORF74-associated transducer profiles. Overall, CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8 induced concentration-dependent responses across multiple ORF74–transducer pairs, whereas CXCL5 failed to produce robust responses. Together with its weak agonistic activity in calcium mobilization and little to no activity in phosphatidylinositol turnover and chemotaxis (; ), these findings suggest that CXCL5 binds to ORF74 but has only limited capacity to promote productive ORF74 signaling. Among the active ELR+ CXC chemokines, signaling potency was broadly consistent with the apparent binding potency rank order, with CXCL1–3 and CXCL6 generally showing higher potencies than CXCL7 and CXCL8. However, potency and maximal efficacy were not strictly coupled. CXCL6 produced lower maximal responses than CXCL1–3 in the NanoBiT assays, with efficacies more in line with those of CXCL7 and CXCL8.

While CXCL1-induced β-arrestin recruitment and receptor trafficking have been described for ORF74 (; ), the involvement of GRKs in ORF74 regulation remains poorly understood. Previous GRK knockout studies demonstrated that GPCRs can display distinct GRK dependency for β-arrestin recruitment, with some receptors showing broad GRK2/3/5/6-mediated regulation and others preferentially depending on GRK2/3 (). We therefore extended our NanoBiT analysis beyond miniGi recruitment to the Gβγ–GRK–β-arrestin axis. To our knowledge, this is the first study to show that ELR+ CXC chemokines modulate ORF74 proximity to GRK2/3/5/6 in a ligand-dependent manner. The increased ORF74–GRK2/3 signals are consistent with ligand-induced recruitment of cytosolic GRK2/3 to activated ORF74. In contrast, the decreased ORF74–GRK5/6 signals should not be taken as direct evidence for GRK5/6 dissociation from ORF74. Additional experiments would be required to determine whether ORF74 and GRK5/6 display basal proximity before ligand stimulation. Given the distinct membrane association of GRK2/3 and GRK5/6, and the dependence of NanoBiT complementation on both protein proximity and tag orientation, reduced ORF74–GRK5/6 luminescence may instead reflect ligand-induced changes in ORF74 conformation or in the relative positioning of ORF74 and GRK5/6. This distinction is important because increasing evidence indicates that GRK subtype usage is both receptor- and ligand-dependent, influencing β-arrestin recruitment and downstream signaling (; ; ). In particular, GRK2/3-mediated β-arrestin recruitment is closely linked to G protein activation through its dependence on Gβγ, whereas GRK5/6-mediated regulation is less dependent on free Gβγ and may therefore be more compatible with β-arrestin-biased signaling (). In this context, recent cell-based platforms provide practical tools for profiling and screening GRK subfamily-selective inhibitor candidates ().

The equimolar comparison plots provided an initial visual overview of ORF74-associated transducer profiles. CXCL1 showed the highest apparent binding potency and consistently robust activity across the ORF74-associated readouts examined, supporting its selection as the reference ligand for calculating ΔΔlog(Emax/EC50), with concentration-response curves fitted using a fixed Hill slope of 1 (). However, the response profile of CXCL1 was not uniformly aligned across all readout comparisons, with visible differences observed in pairs such as miniGi–β-arrestin1/2, GRK3–GRK5 and Gβ1–GRK5. CXCL1 is therefore not completely unbiased across the ORF74-associated readouts, and the equimolar plots suggested that active ELR+ CXC chemokines may share qualitative pathway preferences. When quantified across selected transducer pairs, bias index values remained within ±0.5 log units, corresponding to less than ∼3-fold shifts in relative pathway preference among chemokines. Overall, although shared pathway preferences may exist, the active ELR+ CXC chemokines did not display pronounced ligand-specific bias relative to CXCL1.

Several aspects of the present study should be considered when interpreting these findings. All NanoBiT experiments were performed in HEK293A cells, whereas ORF74 signaling in KSHV-associated diseases occurs mainly in endothelial and B-cell contexts (; ; ). Cellular background may influence GPCR signaling readouts and the interpretation of ligand bias (; ). Similarly, altered effector expression, such as GRK expression, can influence both agonist-induced and basal receptor–β-arrestin interactions (). In addition, receptor overexpression in engineered systems may distort apparent ligand bias (). Different LgBiT/SmBiT assignments may affect signal amplitude and complementation efficiency across biosensor pairs. However, using CXCL1 as the reference ligand within each assay for both response normalization and ligand bias calculation reduces the influence of the resulting system bias (). Despite these limitations, the use of a common NanoBiT platform facilitated a systematic comparison of how ELR+ CXC chemokines regulate the ORF74–Gi/Gβ–GRK–β-arrestin axis. Together, our findings indicate that the active ELR+ CXC chemokines CXCL1–3 and CXCL6–8, but not the largely neutral ligand CXCL5, engage ORF74 in a largely balanced manner across these proximal signaling readouts, thereby expanding the current understanding of ORF74 ligand pharmacology.
