Rashba Spin Demons in Two-Dimensional d d -Wave Altermagnets and their Electrostatic Control
Muhammad Irfan Sarwar, Mohsin Raza, Kashif Sabeeh

Abstract
Spin demons in d d -wave altermagnets are acoustic collective excitations produced by nearly out-of-phase motion of spin-split quasiparticle populations. We identify a new mode, Rashba spin demon mode, in 2D d d -wave altermagnets in the presence of Rashba Spin-Orbit Coupling (RSOC). The pristine spin demon mode in altermagnets, without RSOC, is a charge-dark excitation which is invisible to charge-sensitive probes. We show that Rashba spin demon mode is a spin-dominated acoustic mode that is distinct from the pristine mode and has a finite charge density spectral weight. RSOC rotates the quasiparticle spin texture in momentum space, generating mixed charge-spin coherence factors that give the longitudinal spin pole a charge residue. We also show that a nearby metallic gate provides a complementary electrostatic knob by screening the Coulomb interaction and tuning the pole dispersion and linewidth. Within a charge-spin random-phase approximation, we identify regimes where the Rashba mode is underdamped, charge visible, and still predominantly longitudinal-spin-like. Our results establish RSOC and electrostatic screening as complementary controls for tuning the spin and charge character of altermagnetic spin demons and making them accessible to charge probes without destroying their spin-dominated character.

This Letter addresses the following questions: How is the pristine spin demon mode modified in the presence of RSOC and electrostatic gate? Can one brighten the charge dark pristine spin demon mode to make it charge visible without losing its spin identity? And can one externally tune the Rashba spin demon mode and change its spin and charge character?

To answer the first question, we show that Rashba spin demon mode is a spin-dominated acoustic mode that is distinct from the pristine mode and has a finite charge density spectral weight. In response to the the rest of the questions, a useful control mechanism must satisfy two conditions. It must create enough spin-charge mixing to give the mode a finite density footprint, but it must not hybridize the acoustic spin demon into an ordinary charge plasmon. It should also tune the collective denominator, and therefore the mode velocity and linewidth, without relying only on microscopic changes of the altermagnetic band splitting. Single-particle model and spin texture.—

We model the low-energy quasiparticles of a two-dimensional

d
-wave altermagnet by

H(\mathbf{k})=\epsilon_{0}(\mathbf{k})\mathbb{I}+\Delta_{d}(\mathbf{k})\sigma_{z}+\alpha_{R}(k_{y}\sigma_{x}-k_{x}\sigma_{y}),

(1)

where

\epsilon_{0}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m_{0}},\qquad\Delta_{d}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{\ast}}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2}).

(2)

The term
\Delta_{d}(\mathbf{k})\propto k^{2}\cos 2\phi
carries the

d
-wave altermagnetic symmetry: it changes sign under a

\pi/2
rotation and vanishes along the nodal directions

k_{x}=\pm k_{y}
. In the spin-conserving limit, this produces
spin-resolved Fermi contours elongated along orthogonal axes.
A density perturbation with fixed propagation direction then
projects differently onto the two spin populations, creating
separated particle-hole continua. This kinematic separation is
the window in which the pristine altermagnetic spin demon is
formed.

Rashba coupling changes this reference problem in a more
subtle way than a simple band shift. Writing

\begin{split}H(\mathbf{k})&=\epsilon_{0}(\mathbf{k})\mathbb{I}+\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\bm{\sigma},\\
\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})&=\bigl(\alpha_{R}k_{y},-\alpha_{R}k_{x},\Delta_{d}(\mathbf{k})\bigr),\end{split}

(3)

the two Rashba-altermagnetic bands are

E_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})=\epsilon_{0}(\mathbf{k})+\lambda|\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})|,\qquad\lambda=\pm 1.

(4)

The spin texture is

\langle\bm{\sigma}\rangle_{\lambda}=\lambda\hat{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{k}).

(5)

Thus Rashba coupling simultaneously reshapes the Fermi
contours and rotates the quasiparticle spin texture away from
the fixed
\sigma_{z}
axis. The first effect changes the
available particle-hole phase space, while the second changes
the charge-spin coherence factors entering the response
function. This spinor rotation is the microscopic origin of the
Rashba-induced charge visibility discussed below.
The explicit dimensionless Fermi-contour equation, the Rashba branch condition, and the spin-texture components are derived in the Supplemental Material
33
. These formulas are used to generate Fig.
2
. The key point is that Rashba coupling does not simply distort the Fermi contours; it also changes the spinor overlaps that enter the response function. This spinor deformation is the microscopic origin of Rashba brightening.

More explicitly, the pristine limit has two kinds of structure that must be kept separate. The Fermi contours are spin resolved, but the spin quantization axis is fixed. Rashba coupling changes both features at once: it deforms the contours and rotates the spin axis in momentum space. The contour deformation controls which particle-hole transitions are available, whereas the spin rotation controls the coherence factors of those transitions. The latter is what allows a density operator to acquire overlap with a longitudinal spin excitation.

Charge-spin response and collective poles.—

The gate-screened Coulomb interaction is

v_{q}^{\mathrm{gate}}=\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{r}q}\left(1-e^{-2qd_{g}}\right).

(6)

For
qd_{g}\ll 1
the gate cuts off the long-range
1/q
tail, whereas for
qd_{g}\gg 1
the bare two-dimensional interaction is recovered. The gate therefore changes the Coulomb feedback but not the single-particle spin texture.

Because Rashba coupling makes spin nonconserved, the response must be formulated in the charge-spin basis
a,b\in(n,S_{x},S_{y},S_{z})
. With vertices
\Gamma_{n}=\mathbb{I}
and
\Gamma_{i}=\sigma_{i}
, the band projectors entering the response are

P_{\lambda\mathbf{k}}\equiv P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}+\lambda\hat{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\bm{\sigma}\right],\qquad\lambda=\pm 1.

(7)

The bare response is then the generalized Kubo/Lindhard function
23
;
24
;
26

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}_{ab}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\frac{f_{\lambda\mathbf{k}}-f_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}{\hbar\omega+E_{\lambda\mathbf{k}}-E_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}+i\Gamma}

(8)

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[P_{\lambda\mathbf{k}}\Gamma_{a}P_{\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\Gamma_{b}\right].

The projector trace is the coherence factor. It is gauge independent and contains both the ordinary Fermi-surface phase space and the relative rotation of spin texture between
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}
. In the Supplemental Material we show explicitly that Eq. (
8
) reduces to the spin-resolved Lindhard response in the
\alpha_{R}\rightarrow 0
limit and yields finite mixed charge-spin components when Rashba spinors are present
33
.

The Coulomb interaction couples only to charge density. The RPA Dyson equation therefore reduces to

\chi^{\rm RPA}_{ab}=\chi^{(0)}_{ab}+\frac{v_{q}^{\mathrm{gate}}\chi^{(0)}_{an}\chi^{(0)}_{nb}}{1-v_{q}^{\mathrm{gate}}\chi^{(0)}_{nn}}.

(9)

Equation (
9
) is the mathematical core of the mechanism. The collective pole is governed by the density-like denominator
1-v_{q}^{\mathrm{gate}}\chi_{nn}^{(0)}
, but the spectral residue in any channel is controlled by the numerator. A mode can therefore remain strongest in
A_{S_{z}}=-\mathrm{Im}\,\chi^{\rm RPA}_{zz}
while acquiring a finite density footprint in
A_{n}=-\mathrm{Im}\,\chi^{\rm RPA}_{nn}
. We quantify this footprint and the internal spin character by

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{ch}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{n}(q,\omega_{d})}{A_{S_{z}}(q,\omega_{d})},

(10)

\displaystyle W_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{S_{i}}(q,\omega_{d})}{A_{S_{x}}(q,\omega_{d})+A_{S_{y}}(q,\omega_{d})+A_{S_{z}}(q,\omega_{d})}.

Here
\omega_{d}
is the tracked acoustic spin-demon frequency. We track the acoustic branch continuously from the pristine spin-demon limit and evaluate both its spectral sharpness and spin character at the same pole; the details of this procedure are given in the Supplemental Material
33
.

The emergence of the Rashba spin demon can already be anticipated at the bare-response level. Rashba spinor mixing gives the density and longitudinal-spin vertices a finite overlap in the same low-energy window where the acoustic demon branch is formed; the corresponding continuum analysis is given in the Supplemental Material
33
. These plots establish that Rashba coupling produces mixed charge-spin spectral weight in the same region where the pristine spin demon lives. Separately, the gate-only analysis in the Supplemental Material shows how electrostatic screening tunes the acoustic branch and quality factor even when
\alpha_{R}=0

33
. Thus Rashba modifies the residues, while the gate modifies the collective denominator.

In the spin-conserving benchmark, the anisotropic spin-
\sigma
band can be mapped to an isotropic two-dimensional Lindhard problem by using the projected momentum
\bar{q}_{\sigma}=\eta_{\sigma}(\theta)q/k_{F}
. The spin-resolved continuum edges are then separated whenever
\eta_{\uparrow}\neq\eta_{\downarrow}
. In the long-wavelength limit the pristine acoustic spin-demon branch is

x_{d}^{(0)}=\frac{\hbar\omega_{d}^{(0)}}{E_{F}}=\frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\eta_{\min}\frac{q}{k_{F}},

(11)

with
\eta_{\min}=\min(\eta_{\uparrow},\eta_{\downarrow})
. We use this branch only as a reference guide in the figures; the full Rashba-plus-gate pole is extracted from the interacting spectral function. The derivation of Eq. (
11
), the continuum edges, and the gate-screened analytical limit is given in the Supplemental Material
33
.

The behavior in Fig.

\bar{\alpha}_{R}

Finally, Fig.

W_{z}

stays subleading. The full spin decomposition and representative spin spectral cuts in the Supplemental Material show the same conclusion in more detail

Physical interpretation and outlook.—

The results separate two roles that are often entangled in spin-charge collective modes. Rashba coupling acts at the level of quasiparticle spinors and response-function residues: it gives the spin-demon pole charge visibility. Gate screening acts at the level of the Coulomb denominator: it tunes the pole position and quality factor. This is why the mode in Fig.
4
is neither a conventional charge plasmon nor the completely dark pristine spin demon. It remains distinct from the pristine spin demon branch, but it acquires a finite charge handle.

This distinction is important experimentally. A purely charge-dark spin demon is naturally suited to spin-sensitive probes, whereas a finite
A_{n}(q,\omega_{d})
opens a route to density-sensitive spectroscopy. The visibility ratio
V_{\mathrm{ch}}
is therefore more than a formal quantity: it measures how much of a spin-dominated collective excitation leaks into the experimentally accessible charge channel. Candidate systems should combine a sizable
d
-wave altermagnetic splitting, a pristine two-dimensional conducting channel, inversion-breaking Rashba coupling, and a controllable dielectric environment. Material-specific band structures, disorder, phonons, domain structure, and additional spin-orbit terms will affect the quantitative linewidths and visibility, but they do not alter the main results.

This distinction suggests a concrete experimental reading of the spectra. A purely charge probe should see very little spectral weight at the pristine spin-demon frequency. After Rashba brightening, the same probe can acquire a finite response at the acoustic pole, while a spin-sensitive probe should still see the stronger longitudinal spin peak. Observing both features in the same low-energy window would be a clear signature of a charge-visible spin demon rather than an ordinary plasmon. The gate dependence provides an additional check, because moving the gate should tune the collective pole without changing the underlying
d
-wave altermagnetic spin splitting.

Conclusions.—

We have identified a mode, Rashba spin demon mode, which is distinct from the pristine spin demon mode and is long lived in Rashba 2D
d
-wave altermagnets. It is an acoustic mode which is predominantly a spin mode with a charge character. We have also shown that Rashba coupling and electrostatic gate screening provide complementary controls of spin demons in two-dimensional
d
-wave altermagnets. The pristine spin demon originates from separated spin-resolved continua and is nearly charge dark. Rashba spin-orbit coupling converts the pure-spin problem into a charge-spin response problem, producing a finite density residue at the acoustic spin-demon pole. Gate screening tunes the Coulomb denominator and hence the dispersion and quality factor. The resulting excitation remains predominantly longitudinal-spin-like while becoming charge visible. This establishes Rashba-brightened spin demons as tunable spin-charge collective modes in altermagnetic heterostructures.

(S18)

\displaystyle M^{ab}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{k})\Gamma_{a}P_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\Gamma_{b}\right].

The trace factor is gauge independent and keeps the spinor overlaps explicitly.

Let

\mathbf{n}=\hat{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{k}),\qquad\mathbf{n}^{\prime}=\hat{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}).

(S19)

Using the Pauli identities

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}]

\displaystyle=2\delta_{ij},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}]

\displaystyle=2i\epsilon_{ijk},

(S20)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{l}]

\displaystyle=2(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}-\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}),

one obtains

\displaystyle M_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{nn}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\right),

(S21)

\displaystyle M_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{ni}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\lambda n_{i}+\lambda^{\prime}n_{i}^{\prime}+i\lambda\lambda^{\prime}(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{n}^{\prime})_{i}\right],

\displaystyle M_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{in}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\lambda n_{i}+\lambda^{\prime}n_{i}^{\prime}-i\lambda\lambda^{\prime}(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{n}^{\prime})_{i}\right],

\displaystyle M_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta_{ij}+\lambda\lambda^{\prime}(n_{i}n^{\prime}_{j}+n_{j}n^{\prime}_{i}-\delta_{ij}\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{\prime})+i\epsilon_{ij\ell}(\lambda n_{\ell}-\lambda^{\prime}n^{\prime}_{\ell})\right].

For the longitudinal spin channel,

M_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{zz}=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\lambda\lambda^{\prime}(2n_{z}n^{\prime}_{z}-\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{\prime})\right].

(S22)

The mixed factors
M^{ni}
and
M^{in}
contain the essential Rashba information. Their real parts depend on the spin projection carried by the two states, while the imaginary parts are controlled by the relative rotation of the spin texture between
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}
. These terms are absent in a globally spin-collinear description and are responsible for the density residue of the Rashba spin demon.

B. RPA response and the spin-conserving check

The Coulomb interaction is density-density only,

V_{ab}(\mathbf{q})=v_{q}\delta_{an}\delta_{bn}.

(S23)

The RPA equation is

\chi^{\rm RPA}=\chi^{(0)}+\chi^{(0)}V\chi^{\rm RPA}.

(S24)

Since
V
has only one nonzero component,

\chi^{\rm RPA}_{ab}=\chi^{(0)}_{ab}+v_{q}\chi^{(0)}_{an}\chi^{\rm RPA}_{nb}.

(S25)

Setting
a=n
gives

\chi^{\rm RPA}_{nb}=\frac{\chi^{(0)}_{nb}}{1-v_{q}\chi^{(0)}_{nn}},

(S26)

and therefore

\chi^{\rm RPA}_{ab}=\chi^{(0)}_{ab}+\frac{v_{q}\chi^{(0)}_{an}\chi^{(0)}_{nb}}{1-v_{q}\chi^{(0)}_{nn}}.

(S27)

The two responses used most directly in the paper are

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm RPA}_{nn}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi^{(0)}_{nn}}{1-v_{q}\chi^{(0)}_{nn}},

(S28)

\displaystyle\chi^{\rm RPA}_{zz}

\displaystyle=\chi^{(0)}_{zz}+\frac{v_{q}\chi^{(0)}_{zn}\chi^{(0)}_{nz}}{1-v_{q}\chi^{(0)}_{nn}}.

Thus the pole denominator is common, but the residue is channel dependent. This is the formal reason why the same collective pole can be spin dominated and still acquire a finite charge signal.

The spin-conserving limit verifies the construction. Define

S=\chi_{\uparrow}^{(0)}+\chi_{\downarrow}^{(0)},\qquad D=\chi_{\uparrow}^{(0)}-\chi_{\downarrow}^{(0)}.

(S29)

Then

\chi_{nn}^{(0)}=\chi_{zz}^{(0)}=S,\qquad\chi_{nz}^{(0)}=\chi_{zn}^{(0)}=D.

(S30)

Substitution into Eq. (
S28
) yields

\chi^{\rm RPA}_{zz}=S+\frac{v_{q}D^{2}}{1-v_{q}S}=\frac{S-4v_{q}\chi_{\uparrow}^{(0)}\chi_{\downarrow}^{(0)}}{1-v_{q}S},

(S31)

where
D^{2}-S^{2}=-4\chi_{\uparrow}^{(0)}\chi_{\downarrow}^{(0)}
has been used. Equation (
S31
) is the spin-resolved RPA form of the pristine altermagnetic spin-demon response
17
;
32
.

III
Spin-resolved continua and acoustic reference branch

For a perturbation momentum
\mathbf{q}=q(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
, the anisotropic spin-
\sigma
band maps to an isotropic two-dimensional Lindhard problem with projected momentum

\displaystyle\bar{q}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\bar{q}\,\eta_{\sigma}(\theta),\qquad\bar{q}=\frac{q}{k_{F}},

(S32)

\displaystyle\eta_{\sigma}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{m_{\rm DOS}}{m_{x}^{\sigma}}\cos^{2}\theta+\frac{m_{\rm DOS}}{m_{y}^{\sigma}}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{1/2}.

The dimensionless frequency is
x=\hbar\omega/E_{F}
, and we define

\nu_{\sigma,\mp}=\frac{x}{2\bar{q}_{\sigma}}\mp\frac{\bar{q}_{\sigma}}{2}.

(S33)

The zero-temperature two-dimensional Lindhard function is
39
;
15

\frac{\chi_{\sigma}^{(0)}}{N_{0}}=-\left(A_{\sigma}^{2D}+iB_{\sigma}^{2D}\right),

(S34)

with

\displaystyle A_{\sigma}^{2D}=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{\bar{q}_{\sigma}}\Big[\Theta(\nu_{\sigma,-}^{2}-1)\operatorname{sgn}(\nu_{\sigma,-})\sqrt{\nu_{\sigma,-}^{2}-1}

(S35)

\displaystyle-\Theta(\nu_{\sigma,+}^{2}-1)\operatorname{sgn}(\nu_{\sigma,+})\sqrt{\nu_{\sigma,+}^{2}-1}\Big],

\displaystyle B_{\sigma}^{2D}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{q}_{\sigma}}\Big[\Theta(1-\nu_{\sigma,-}^{2})\sqrt{1-\nu_{\sigma,-}^{2}}

\displaystyle-\Theta(1-\nu_{\sigma,+}^{2})\sqrt{1-\nu_{\sigma,+}^{2}}\Big].

The particle-hole continuum is the region in which
B_{\sigma}^{2D}
is nonzero. In the small-
\bar{q}_{\sigma}
regime used in the spectra, the upper edge is

x_{+,\sigma}=\bar{q}_{\sigma}^{2}+2\bar{q}_{\sigma}.

(S36)

When
\eta_{\uparrow}\neq\eta_{\downarrow}
, the two spin-resolved continuum edges separate. This separation opens the low-damping interval that supports the acoustic spin demon. In the long-wavelength strong-coupling limit, the acoustic reference branch is

\displaystyle x_{d}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar\omega_{d}^{(0)}}{E_{F}}=\frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\eta_{\min}\bar{q},\qquad\eta_{\min}=\min(\eta_{\uparrow},\eta_{\downarrow}),

(S37)

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{\min}}{\eta_{\max}}

\displaystyle<\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\qquad Q_{\rm pristine}=\frac{3\sqrt{3\eta_{\max}^{2}-4\eta_{\min}^{2}}}{\eta_{\min}},\qquad\eta_{\max}=\max(\eta_{\uparrow},\eta_{\downarrow}).

The first line gives the guide branch. The second line gives the existence condition and the corresponding analytical quality-factor estimate for the spin-conserving case. In the Rashba-plus-gate problem, Eq. (
S37
) is used only to identify the acoustic branch; the actual pole is extracted from the full
\chi_{zz}^{\rm RPA}
.

in the same frequency range, providing the channel through which the longitudinal spin pole gains density spectral weight after RPA feedback.

IV
Gate screening and gate-only analytical control

A metallic gate at distance
d_{g}
produces image-charge screening of the two-dimensional Coulomb interaction
8
;
9
;
40
;
42
. The interaction used in the paper is

\displaystyle v_{q}^{\rm gate}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{r}q}\left(1-e^{-2qd_{g}}\right),

(S38)

\displaystyle\frac{v_{q}^{\rm gate}}{v_{q}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=1-e^{-2qd_{g}}.

The limiting forms are

\displaystyle v_{q}^{\rm gate}

\displaystyle\rightarrow v_{q}^{(0)},

\displaystyle qd_{g}

\displaystyle\gg 1,

(S39)

\displaystyle v_{q}^{\rm gate}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{e^{2}d_{g}}{\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{r}},

\displaystyle qd_{g}

\displaystyle\ll 1.

Thus the gate cuts off the small-
q
Coulomb singularity without changing the Rashba spin texture.

The dimensionless screened coupling can be written as

\alpha_{\rm gate}(\bar{q},\bar{d}_{g})=v_{q}^{\rm gate}N_{0}=g_{C}\frac{1-e^{-2\bar{q}\bar{d}_{g}}}{\bar{q}},\qquad g_{C}=\frac{e^{2}N_{0}}{2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{r}k_{F}},\qquad\bar{d}_{g}=k_{F}d_{g}.

(S40)

In the spin-conserving gate-only limit, the acoustic pole is

\displaystyle x_{d}^{\rm gate}

\displaystyle=2\bar{q}\,u_{g}\eta_{\min},

(S41)

\displaystyle u_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{g}}{\sqrt{C_{g}^{2}-1}},\qquad C_{g}=2+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\rm gate}},

\displaystyle Q_{\rm gate}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{1-(\delta u_{g})^{2}}}{\delta(u_{g}^{2}-1)^{3/2}},\qquad\delta=\frac{\eta_{\min}}{\eta_{\max}}.

For
\alpha_{\rm gate}\rightarrow\infty
,
u_{g}\rightarrow 2/\sqrt{3}
, and Eq. (
S41
) reduces to the acoustic branch in Eq. (
S37
). This limit checks the normalization of the screened interaction.

suppresses the long-range Coulomb feedback, softens the acoustic branch, and changes the quality factor. This confirms that the gate controls the collective denominator, whereas Rashba coupling controls the response residues through spinor coherence factors.

V
Pole extraction and spectral diagnostics

The Rashba spin demon is identified as the low-energy acoustic pole continuously connected to the spin-conserving altermagnetic spin demon. The pole is tracked in

A_{S_{z}}(q,\omega)=-\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{zz}^{\rm RPA}(q,\omega).

(S42)

At fixed
q
, we define

\omega_{d}(q)=\arg\max_{\omega\in W_{d}}A_{S_{z}}(q,\omega),

(S43)

where
W_{d}
is a low-energy window around the acoustic branch. The restriction to
W_{d}
prevents a higher-energy Rashba-mixed charge feature from being misidentified as the spin demon.

The dielectric function used for the linewidth estimate is

\epsilon(q,\omega)=1-v_{q}^{\rm gate}\chi_{nn}^{(0)}(q,\omega).

(S44)

For a sharp pole, the spectral maximum agrees with
\mathrm{Re}\,\epsilon(q,\omega_{d})=0
. The linewidth, quality factor, charge visibility, and spin weights are then evaluated at the same pole:

\displaystyle\gamma_{x}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,\epsilon(q,x_{d})}{\partial_{x}\mathrm{Re}\,\epsilon(q,x)|_{x=x_{d}}}\right|,

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{d}}{\gamma_{x}},

\displaystyle x_{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar\omega_{d}}{E_{F}},

(S45)

\displaystyle V_{\rm ch}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{n}(q,\omega_{d})}{A_{S_{z}}(q,\omega_{d})},

\displaystyle A_{n}(q,\omega)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_{nn}^{\rm RPA}(q,\omega),

\displaystyle W_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{S_{i}}(q,\omega_{d})}{A_{S_{x}}(q,\omega_{d})+A_{S_{y}}(q,\omega_{d})+A_{S_{z}}(q,\omega_{d})},

\displaystyle W_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=W_{x}+W_{y}.

A charge-visible Rashba spin demon is therefore characterized by finite
V_{\rm ch}
and dominant longitudinal spin weight
W_{z}
.
