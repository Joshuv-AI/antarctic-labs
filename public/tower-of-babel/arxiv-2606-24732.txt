Addison Day, Jonathan R. Kujawa

Abstract
. We introduce and study a one-parameter family of algebras that naturally generalize the Schur algebras. We show the Schur algebra is canonically a quotient when the parameter is a nonnegative integer, characterize when they are semisimple, show they are based quasi-hereditary, and that their category of representations is a highest weight category that can be identified as a subcategory of parabolic category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} for the general linear Lie algebra.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 17B10, 16G99. Secondary 20B30, 20C30.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Let
\mathds{k}
be a field of characteristic zero. Let
V_{n}
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-vector space. There is an action of the symmetric group on
d
letters,
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, on the
d
-fold tensor product
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
by permutation of the tensor factors. For more than a century, the Schur algebra

S(n,d):=\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\left(V_{n}^{\otimes d}\right)

has played a central role in representation theory. Via Schur–Weyl duality it acts as a bridge between the representation theories of the symmetric and general linear groups. It is also the prototypical example of a quasi-hereditary algebra and its representations are the prototypical example of a highest weight category.

The purpose of this paper is to introduce and study a family of algebras,
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
, where
t\in\mathds{k}
. These algebras and their representations situate the classical Schur algebras within a one-parameter family in much the same way Deligne’s category,
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathfrak{S}_{t})
, does for the representation theory of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}

[
6
]
. Indeed, a key ingredient for this paper is the approach to
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathfrak{S}_{t})
based on oligomorphic groups recently developed by Harman–Snowden
[
16
]
.

An oligomorphic group is a group
G
acting faithfully on a set
X
in such a way that, for all
r\geq 0
,
G
has finitely many orbits under the diagonal action on the
r
-fold product of
X
with itself. For example,
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
acting on the natural numbers is an oligomorphic group. Given an oligomorphic group, there is a notion of a measure and, hence, a theory of integration. In the case of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
, there is a unique measure
\mu_{t}
for each
t\in\mathds{k}
. Given the data of an oligomorphic group with a measure, Harman–Snowden define a category of so-called permutation modules where the composition operation is given by convolution with respect to the measure. For a fixed
t\in\mathds{k}
we define
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
to be the path algebra of a subcategory of the corresponding permutation category for
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
:

\mathbb{S}(n,t)=\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}}\Hom_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}},\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\right).

See
Section

4.4
for a detailed definition. The goal of the paper is to study the structure and representation theory of
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
.

1.2.
Main Results

Our first significant results are
Propositions

4.4.2
and
4.5.5
. They give a double coset basis for
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
along with a combinatorial product rule that generalizes a known basis and product rule for
S(n,d)
. As a consequence,
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is seen to be the path algebra of a category first introduced by Harman in his thesis
[
17
]
and generalized in
[
22
]
. That the oligomorphic definition coincides with these earlier constructions will not surprise experts (e.g., the authors of
[
16
]
). These results provide a crucial connection to
S(n,d)
that is used throughout the paper.

The category
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
is a spherical
\mathds{k}
-linear tensor category. We can therefore consider the semisimplification,
\overline{\operatorname{Perm}}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
, obtained by taking the quotient of
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
by the tensor ideal of so-called negligible morphisms. When
n\geq 2
,
Corollary

5.1.2
shows that the subcategory of
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
that defines
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is not semisimple if and only if
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. In the case when
t=d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
Theorem

5.2.1
demonstrates that the classical Schur algebra is the path algebra of the semisimplification:

S(n,d)\cong\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{d}}\Hom_{\overline{\operatorname{Perm}}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{d})}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}},\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\right).

In particular, there is a canonical quotient map
\mathbb{S}(n,d)\twoheadrightarrow S(n,d)
.

In
Section

6
we establish that
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
has a basis that parallels Green’s codeterminant basis for
S(n,d)
. This gives
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
the structure of an upper finite based quasi-hereditary algebra in the sense of
[
3
]
and makes the category of locally finite-dimensional
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules a highest weight category in the sense of
[
5
]
. In particular, there are standard modules
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
for
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
, where
\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
is the set of all “partitions” of
t
with
n
parts. Each standard module has simple head
L(\bm{\lambda})
and
\left\{L(\bm{\lambda})\mid\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}\right\}
is a complete, irredundant set of simple
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules. Moreover,
Theorem

7.5.1
classifies when
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is semisimple: it is semisimple if and only if
n=1
or if
n\geq 2
and
t\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Another consequence of the codeterminant basis is that
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
admits two natural integral forms. There is the naive one given by the span of the double coset basis. The second and more interesting one is given by the span of the codeterminant basis. This form should be viewed as the Schurification of
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
in the sense of
[
19
]
and we expect this second form to have favorable properties (e.g., with respect to base change). Also of interest,
Sections

5.3
and
6.2.5
demonstrate for
\mathds{k}=\mathbb{Q}(T)
, the field of rational functions, that
\mathbb{S}(n,T)
admits analogous forms over the ring of integer-valued polynomials.

Finally, in
Section

8
we show that
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
can be realized as the quotient of an idempotented form of the enveloping algebra for the general linear Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
. This gives rise to an inflation functor from
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules to
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-modules which allows the category of finitely generated
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules to be identified as a full subcategory of an appropriate parabolic category
\mathcal{O}
corresponding to the Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{1}(\mathds{k})\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}(\mathds{k})
. A connection between Deligne’s category and this parabolic category
\mathcal{O}
was previously observed in
[
1
]
.

1.3.
Future Directions

The results of this paper suggest a number of natural directions of investigation that we look forward to pursuing. There should be an interpolation,
\mathbb{S}_{q}(n,t)
, of the quantized Schur algebras
S_{q}(n,d)
. The oligomorphic group framework of
[
16
]
can be used to define interpolations of various generalizations of the Schur algebra (e.g., the cyclotomic Schur algebras). More exotic would be to formulate and study Delannoy Schur algebras
[
15
]
and Schur algebras for other interesting oligomorphic groups (e.g., see
[
16
, Section 1.3.7]
for a list). In a different direction, we anticipate that
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
can be used to study the stable (aka reduced) Kronecker coefficients. A related question is to establish a Schur–Weyl duality relationship between
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
and Deligne’s category. Lastly, it is known that one can define
\operatorname{Rep}(GL_{r})
for any
r\in\mathds{k}
and thereby meaningfully interpolate the dimension of
V_{n}
. There should be a corresponding two-variable interpolation of the Schur algebra,
\mathbb{S}(r,t)
, where
r,t\in\mathds{k}
.

2.
Notation and Combinatorics

Let us establish notation, conventions, and combinatorics that will be used throughout the paper.

2.1.
Notation

Let
\mathds{k}
denote a field of characteristic zero. Let
\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
denote the natural numbers and let
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
denote the nonnegative integers. Given
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
with
x\leq y
, let
[x,y]=\left\{x,x+1,\dotsc,y\right\}
and let
[x,\infty]=\left\{x,x+1,x+2,\dotsc\right\}
. If
x>y
we set
[x,y]=\emptyset
. Given a set
X
, write
|X|
for the cardinality of
X
. For
k\geq 1
, let
X^{k}
be the set of
k
-tuples of elements from
X
, and let
X^{(k)}
be the set of all subsets of
X
of size
k
. If
X
and
Y
are
G
-sets for some group
G
, then
G
acts diagonally on
X\times Y
. If
X
is a
G
-set for some group
G
, then
G
acts on
X^{(k)}
and on set partitions of
X
by acting on the entries of each subset.

Let
R
be a commutative ring with
1
. Given
t\in R
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, set

(2.1.1)

\binom{t}{k}=\binom{t}{t-k,k}=\frac{t(t-1)(t-2)\dotsb(t-k+1)}{k!}.

By convention,
\binom{t}{0}=1
. More generally, if
m,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geq\ell
, and
k_{2},\dotsc,k_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, then set

(2.1.2)

\binom{t-\ell}{t-m,k_{2},\dotsc,k_{r}}=\frac{(t-\ell)(t-\ell-1)(t-\ell-2)\dotsb(t-m+1)}{k_{2}!k_{3}!\dotsb k_{r}!}.

We will generally assume that
R
and
t
are such that all expressions of the form
Eqs.

2.1.1
and
2.1.2
are elements of
R
. For example, this is automatic if
R
contains
\mathbb{Q}
, or if
t
is an integer and
m,\ell,k_{2},\dotsc,k_{r}
are chosen so that the above expressions are integer-valued polynomials in the variable
t
.

2.2.
Ring of Integer-Valued Polynomials

Let

\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle=\left\{p(T)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]\mid p(\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\right\}

denote the ring of integer-valued polynomials in the variable
T
. If
p(T)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]
has degree
d
, then
p(T)
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
if and only if
p(a)
is an integer for
d+1
consecutive integers
a
. It is also well known that
p(T)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
if and only if

p(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_{k}\binom{T}{k}

for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Note that if
m,\ell,k_{2},\dotsc,k_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
m=\ell+k_{2}+\dots+k_{r}
, then

(2.2.1)

\binom{T-\ell}{T-m,k_{2},\dotsc,k_{r}}

is an integer-valued polynomial because it computes a multinomial coefficient when
T
is evaluated at all sufficiently large integers.

2.3.
Classical Combinatorics

2.3.1.
Compositions and Partitions

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, a
composition
of
d
with
n

parts
is a sequence of nonnegative integers
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dotsc,\gamma_{n})
such that
|\bm{\gamma}|:=\sum_{k\in[1,n]}\gamma_{k}=d
. Note that when
n=\infty
, this implies the sequence
\bm{\gamma}
is eventually
0
. Given a composition
\bm{\gamma}
, a
part
of
\bm{\gamma}
is one of the entries of the sequence. We emphasize that we allow parts to equal zero and those parts are not to be ignored. A
partition
of
d
is a composition
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})
of
d
that satisfies
\gamma_{1}\geq\dotsb\geq\gamma_{n}
. For short, write
\bm{\gamma}\models d
(resp.,
\bm{\gamma}\vdash d
) when
\bm{\gamma}
is a composition (resp., partition) of
d
. There is a unique empty composition with zero parts and it is considered to be a partition of zero.

Given a composition
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})
of
d
, there is an associated set partition
\left\{\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{1},\dots,\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{n}\right\}
of
[1,d]
given by

\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{k}=\left[1+\sum_{i\in[1,k-1]}\gamma_{i},\sum_{i\in[1,k]}\gamma_{i}\right]

for
k\in[1,n]
.
In particular,
|\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{k}|=\gamma_{k}
for all
k
. Also, note that since we allow the parts of
\bm{\gamma}
to equal zero, our definition of a set partition allows for the empty subset and it might appear multiple times.

Example 2.3.1
.

The composition
\bm{\gamma}=(3,6,2,0,0)
is a composition of
d=11
with
n=5
parts. The set partition of
[1,11]
corresponding to
\bm{\gamma}
is

\left\{\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{1}=\{1,2,3\},\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{2}=\{4,5,6,7,8,9\},\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{3}=\{10,11\},\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{4}=\emptyset,\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{5}=\emptyset\right\}.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, write

\displaystyle\Lambda(n,d)

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{\gamma}\models d\mid\text{$\bm{\gamma}$ has $n$ parts}\right\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{+}(n,d)

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{\gamma}\vdash d\mid\text{$\bm{\gamma}$ has $n$ parts}\right\},

\displaystyle\Lambda(n)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\Lambda(n,d),

\displaystyle\Lambda^{+}(n)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\Lambda^{+}(n,d).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
, let

X(n)=\bigoplus_{i\in[1,n]}\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_{i}

denote the free abelian group on the set
\left\{\varepsilon_{i}\right\}_{i\in[1,n]}
. We identify
\Lambda(n)
and its various subsets with their images in
X(n)
under the embedding

\bm{\gamma}\mapsto\sum_{r\in[1,n]}\gamma_{r}\varepsilon_{r}.

As we did here, we sometimes leave it to the reader to infer that
\gamma_{r}
denotes the
r
th part of
\bm{\gamma}
(and likewise for
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta},\dotsc
).

2.3.2.
Matrices and Arrays

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
. Given compositions
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)
, let
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
denote the set of
n\times n
matrices
q=(q_{i,j})_{i,j\in[1,n]}
which satisfy the following conditions:

•

for all
i,j\in[1,n]
,
q_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
;

•

\sum_{i,j\in[1,n]}q_{i,j}=d
;

•

for each
j\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{i\in[1,n]}q_{i,j}=\beta_{j}
;

•

for each
i\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{j\in[1,n]}q_{i,j}=\delta_{i}
.

Observe that
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
is a finite set for any
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)
. Given
q=(q_{i,j})\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
, let
q^{*}=(q_{j,i})\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})
denote the transpose matrix. Set

\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)=\bigcup_{\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)}\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta}).

Let
\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d)
and
\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
denote the subsets of upper triangular matrices. Likewise, let
\operatorname{Mat}^{-}(n,d)
and
\operatorname{Mat}^{-}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
denote the subsets of lower triangular matrices.

For any
q,r,s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
, write
A(n,q,r,s)
for the set of arrays
A=(A_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k\in[1,n]}
which satisfy the following conditions:

•

for all
i,j,k\in[1,n]
,
A_{i,j,k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
;

•

for each pair
i,j\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{k\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=q_{i,j}
;

•

for each pair
j,k\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{i\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=r_{j,k}
;

•

for each pair
i,k\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=s_{i,k}
.

For a fixed
q,r,s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
, the set of arrays
A(n,q,r,s)
is finite.

2.3.3.
Semistandard Fillings of Young Diagrams and Semistandard Matrices

Given a partition
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
of
d
, there is the associated
Young diagram of shape
\bm{\lambda}
,
Y^{\bm{\lambda}}
. This is an array of one-by-one boxes with
\lambda_{i}
boxes in the
i
th row, where the boxes are left justified. A
Young tableau of shape
\bm{\lambda}
is a filling of the boxes of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}}
with natural numbers. Call the filling
row semistandard
if the entries are weakly increasing from left-to-right along rows, and call it
semistandard
if it is weakly increasing from left-to-right along rows and strictly increasing from top-to-bottom along columns.

Let
\bm{\lambda}
be a partition of
d
and
\bm{\beta}
a composition of
d
. Let
q=(q_{i,j})\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})
. There is a unique tableau of shape
\bm{\lambda}
which is row semistandard and has
q_{i,j}
entries equal to
j
in row
i
of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}}
. For short, we call this
the filling of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}}
by
q
. Observe that a filling of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}}
cannot give a semistandard tableau if a
k
appears anywhere in rows
k+1,\dotsc,n
of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}}
. That is, if the filling of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}}
by
q
is semistandard, then
q
must be upper triangular.

2.3.4.
Tuples and Weights

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let

I(n,d)=\left\{\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d})\mid i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d}\in[1,n]\right\}

be the set of
d
-tuples with entries from
[1,n]
. The symmetric group on
d
letters,
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, has a right action on
I(n,d)
via place permutation. That is, the action is given by

(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\cdot\sigma=(i_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,i_{\sigma(d)})

for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
and all
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in I(n,d).

Given
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})\in I(n,d)
, the
weight
of
\mathbf{i}
is
\wt(\mathbf{i})=\sum_{k\in[1,d]}\varepsilon_{i_{k}}\in X(n)
. Observe that
\mathbf{i}
and
\mathbf{j}
are in the same
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
-orbit if and only if
\wt(\mathbf{i})=\wt(\mathbf{j})
. Let
\mathfrak{O}(n,d)
denote the set of
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
-orbits in
I(n,d)\times I(n,d)
. Write
\overline{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})}\in\mathfrak{O}(n,d)
for the orbit containing
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})\in I(n,d)\times I(n,d)
.

There is a well-defined function

\displaystyle\mathfrak{O}(n,d)

\displaystyle\to\Lambda(n,d)\times\Lambda(n,d),

\displaystyle\overline{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})}

\displaystyle\mapsto(\wt(\mathbf{i}),\wt(\mathbf{j})).

For
(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})\in\Lambda(n,d)\times\Lambda(n,d)
, write
\mathfrak{O}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
for the set of orbits in
\mathfrak{O}(n,d)
which go to
(\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu})
under this map.

2.4.
Combinatorics with a
t

2.4.1.
Compositions and Partitions with a
t

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Given a fixed
t\in R
, let

\Lambda(n)_{t}=\left\{\bm{\beta}=\left(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dotsc,\beta_{n}\right)\in\left(t-\mathbb{Z}\right)\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n-1}\;\left|\;\sum_{i\in[1,n]}\beta_{i}=t\right.\right\},

and let

\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}=\left\{\bm{\beta}=\left(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dotsc,\beta_{n}\right)\in\Lambda(n)_{t}\mid\beta_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq\beta_{n}\right\}.

We extend these definitions to
n=\infty
by assuming all but finitely many
\beta_{i}
equal zero.

Given
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dotsc,\gamma_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, there is an associated set partition
\left\{\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{1},\dots,\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{n}\right\}
of
\Omega:=\mathbb{N}
given by setting

\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{k}=\left[1+\sum_{i\in[2,k-1]}\gamma_{i},\sum_{i\in[2,k]}\gamma_{i}\right]

for
k\in[2,n]
, and

\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{1}=\Omega\backslash\bigcup_{k\in[2,n]}\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{k}.

Observe that
\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{1}
is an infinite set and
|\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{k}|=\gamma_{k}
when
k\in[2,n]
. Also observe that we allow for parts of
\bm{\gamma}
to equal zero and for the corresponding set partition of
\Omega
to include empty subsets.

Example 2.4.1
.

Consider the composition
\bm{\gamma}=(t-11,3,6,2,0,0)\in\Lambda(6)_{t}
. The corresponding set partition of
\Omega
is

\left\{\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{1}=\{12,13,14,\dots\},\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{2}=\{1,2,3\},\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{3}=\{4,5,6,7,8,9\},\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{4}=\{10,11\},\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{5}=\emptyset,\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{6}=\emptyset\right\}.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and
t\in R
, let

X(n)_{t}=\left\{\left.\bm{\lambda}=\sum_{i\in[1,n]}\lambda_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\in\bigoplus_{i\in[1,n]}R\varepsilon_{i}\;\right|\;\text{$\lambda_{1}\in t-\mathbb{Z}$, and $\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}$ for $i\in[2,n]$ }\right\}.

There is an embedding

(2.4.1)

\Lambda(n)_{t}\hookrightarrow X(n)_{t}

given by
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dotsc,\gamma_{n})\mapsto\sum_{i\in[1,n]}\gamma_{i}\varepsilon_{i}
. We identify
\Lambda(n)_{t}
and its subsets with their image under this map.

2.4.2.
Matrices and Arrays with a
t

Given
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, let
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
denote the set of matrices
q=(q_{i,j})_{i,j\in[1,n]}
which satisfy the following conditions:

•

q_{1,1}\in t-\mathbb{Z}
;

•

for all other
(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}
,
q_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
;

•

\sum_{(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}}q_{i,j}=t
;

•

for each
j\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{i\in[1,n]}q_{i,j}=\beta_{j}
;

•

for each
i\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{j\in[1,n]}q_{i,j}=\delta_{i}
.

Observe that
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
is a finite set for each pair
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
. Given
q=(q_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
, let
q^{*}=(q_{j,i})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})_{t}
denote the transpose matrix. Set

\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)=\bigcup_{\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}}\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}.

Let
\mathcal{M}at_{n}^{+}(t)
and
\mathcal{M}at_{n}^{+}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
denote the subsets of upper triangular matrices. Likewise, let
\mathcal{M}at_{n}^{-}(t)
and
\mathcal{M}at_{n}^{-}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
denote the subsets of lower triangular matrices.

For
q,r,s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)
, let
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
be the set of arrays
A=(A_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k\in[1,n]}
which satisfy:

•

A_{1,1,1}\in t-\mathbb{Z}
;

•

for all other
i,j,k\in[1,n]
,
A_{i,j,k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
;

•

for each pair
i,j\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{k\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=q_{i,j}
;

•

for each pair
j,k\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{i\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=r_{j,k}
;

•

for each pair
i,k\in[1,n]
,
\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=s_{i,k}
.

For a fixed
q,r,s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)
, the set of arrays
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
is finite.

Remark 2.4.2
.

Observe that
\Lambda(n,d)
is naturally a subset of
\Lambda(n)_{d}
:

\Lambda(n,d)=\left\{\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dotsc,\gamma_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{d}\mid\gamma_{1}\geq 0\right\}.

Likewise, for
\bm{\gamma},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)
,
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\gamma},\bm{\delta})
is the subset of
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\delta})_{d}
consisting of matrices
q=(q_{i,j})
such that
q_{1,1}\geq 0
. For
q,r,s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
,
A(n,q,r,s)
is the subset of
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{d}
consisting of arrays
A=(A_{i,j,k})
such that
A_{1,1,1}\geq 0
.

2.4.3.
Linear Translation

Given
t_{1},t_{2}\in R
, it will be convenient to have notation for the operation of linearly translating from
t_{1}
-combinatorics to
t_{2}
-combinatorics. For example, if
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{t_{1}}
, then we write
\bm{\beta}(t_{2})
for
(\beta_{1}+t_{2}-t_{1},\beta_{2},\dotsc,\beta_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{t_{2}}
. Clearly,
\bm{\beta}\mapsto\bm{\beta}(t_{2})
defines a bijection
\Lambda(n)_{t_{1}}\to\Lambda(n)_{t_{2}}
. There are entirely similar bijections
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t_{1}}\to\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta}(t_{2}),\bm{\beta}(t_{2}))_{t_{2}}
given by
q\mapsto q(t_{2})
,
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t_{1}}\to\mathcal{A}_{n}(q(t_{2}),r(t_{2}),s(t_{2}))_{t_{2}}
given by
A\mapsto A(t_{2})
, etc.

Remark 2.4.3
.

In the special case when
t_{2}=d
is an integer and
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t_{1}}
, then
\bm{\beta}(d)
is an element of
\Lambda(n,d)
whenever
d
is large enough to ensure
\bm{\beta}(d)_{1}\geq 0
.
Likewise, for
q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t_{1})
and sufficiently large
d
,
q(d)
can and will be viewed as an element of
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
; given
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t_{1}}
,
A(d)
can and will be viewed as an element of
A(n,q(d),r(d),s(d))
.

Since for any
t\in\mathds{k}
and any
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
the set
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
is finite, the previous paragraph implies that the map

\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}\to\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta}(d),\bm{\beta}(d))

given by
q\mapsto q(d)
will be a bijection for
d\gg 0
. Likewise, since for any
q,r,s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)
the set
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
is finite, the map

\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}\to A(n,q(d),r(d),s(d))

given by
A\mapsto A(d)
will be a bijection for
d\gg 0
.

2.4.4.
Dominance Order

Given
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{n}),\bm{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, write
\bm{\beta}\preceq\bm{\delta}
if

(2.4.2)

\sum_{i\in[1,k]}\left(\delta_{i}-\beta_{i}\right)\geq 0

for all
k\in[1,n]
. This defines a partial order on
\Lambda(n)_{t}
which we call the
dominance order
. Observe that this partial order is preserved under linear translation from
\Lambda(n)_{t_{1}}
to
\Lambda(n)_{t_{2}}
, and that it restricts to the usual dominance order on
\Lambda(n,d)
.

2.4.5.
Semistandard Shifted Matrices with a
t

Given
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
,
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, and
q=(q_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{t}
, then we say
q
is
semistandard
if and only if the filling of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}(d)}
by
q(d)
is semistandard for
d\gg 0
.

The following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 2.4.4
.

Given
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
and
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, the matrix
q=(q_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{t}
is semistandard if and only if, for all sufficiently large
d
,
q(d)=(q_{i,j}(d))
satisfies

(2.4.3)

\sum_{j\in[1,y]}q_{x,j}(d)\leq\sum_{j\in[1,y-1]}q_{x-1,j}(d)\text{ for all }(x,y)\in[2,n]\times[1,n].

For example, if
q
is semistandard, then
q_{2,1}
is bounded by an empty sum and must equal zero. Likewise,
q_{3,1}=0
. And
q_{3,2}=0
because
q_{2,1}=0
. Continuing in this way shows that
q
will be upper triangular. As another example, note that
Eq.

2.4.3
imposes no conditions on
q_{1,y}
when
y\in[2,n]
. Also observe that
Eq.

2.4.3
is automatically satisfied for
q_{2,y}
when
y\in[2,n]
because
d\gg 0
.

3.
The Classical Schur Algebra

We recall various well known results about the Schur algebra. These results will be used, both literally and inspirationally, in what follows.

3.1.
Double Cosets

Given a composition
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})
of
d
, write
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{\gamma_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathfrak{S}_{\gamma_{n}}
for the corresponding Young subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. Given compositions
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)
, there is a bijection
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})\to\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{d}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
given by

q\mapsto\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}},

where
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
is any permutation with the property that, for all
i,j\in[1,n]
,

q_{i,j}=\left|\{\bm{\delta}\}_{i}\cap\sigma\left(\{\bm{\beta}\}_{j}\right)\right|.

Given
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)
, there is also a bijection

\varphi:\mathfrak{O}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})\to\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})

given as follows. For
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{d})\in I(n,d)
and
x\in[1,n]
, let
[\mathbf{k}]_{x}=\left\{r\in[1,d]\mid k_{r}=x\right\}
be the set of subscripts where that entry of
\mathbf{k}
has value
x
. Let
\overline{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})}\in\mathfrak{O}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
. Then the bijection is given by

(3.1.1)

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\overline{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})}\right)=\left(q_{x,y}\right)_{x,y\in[1,n]},

(3.1.2)

\displaystyle q_{x,y}=\left|[\mathbf{j}]_{x}\cap[\mathbf{i}]_{y}\right|.

That is,
q_{x,y}
is a count of the number of positions which are simultaneously equal to
x
in
\mathbf{j}
and equal to
y
in
\mathbf{i}
.

3.2.
The Classical Schur Algebra

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
V_{n}=\mathds{k}^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-vector space with distinguished basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[1,n]}
. Then
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
has distinguished basis

\left\{v_{\mathbf{i}}=v_{i_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{i_{d}}\mid\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d})\in I(n,d)\right\}.

There is a right
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
-action on
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
induced by the place permutation action on
I(n,d)
. The classical
Schur algebra
is defined to be the endomorphism algebra

S(n,d)=S_{\mathds{k}}(n,d)=\operatorname{End}_{\mathds{k}\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\left(V_{n}^{\otimes d}\right).

For
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in I(n,d)
write
f_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i}}:V_{n}^{\otimes d}\to V_{n}^{\otimes d}
for the
\mathds{k}
-linear map given by
f_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i}}(v_{\mathbf{k}})=\delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{j}}
for all
\mathbf{k}\in I(n,d)
. Here
\delta_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{k}}
denotes the Kronecker function. Given
\overline{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})}\in\mathfrak{O}(n,d)
, let
\xi_{\overline{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})}}=\sum_{(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})\in\overline{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})}}f_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}}
. We abuse notation and write
\xi_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i}}
for
\xi_{\overline{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})}}
.

It is well known that

(3.2.1)

\left\{\xi_{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i}}\mid\overline{(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{i})}\in\mathfrak{O}(n,d)\right\}

forms a basis for
S(n,d)
. For example, see
[
13
, Section 2.3]
. It will be convenient to use an alternate indexing of this basis using the function
\varphi
from
Section

3.1
(cf.
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
).

Given
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n,d)
, let
V_{n,\bm{\beta}}^{\otimes d}
denote the span of
\left\{v_{\mathbf{i}}\mid\mathbf{i}\in I(n,d),\wt(\mathbf{i})=\bm{\beta}\right\}
. Then,

V_{n}^{\otimes d}=\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n,d)}V_{n,\bm{\beta}}^{\otimes d}

is a decomposition into
\mathds{k}\mathfrak{S}_{d}
-submodules. Furthermore,
V_{n,\bm{\beta}}^{\otimes d}\cong M^{\bm{\beta}}
, where
M^{\bm{\beta}}
is the permutation module obtained by linearizing the
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
-set
X^{\bm{\beta}}=\mathfrak{S}_{d}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
.

Correspondingly, the Schur algebra has the direct sum decomposition

(3.2.2)

S(n,d)\cong\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)}\Hom_{\mathds{k}\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\left(M^{\bm{\beta}},M^{\bm{\delta}}\right).

Given
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)
, observe that if
q\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
, then
\xi_{\varphi^{-1}(q)}\in\Hom_{\mathds{k}\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\left(M^{\bm{\beta}},M^{\bm{\delta}}\right)
. For short, write
\xi_{q}
for
\xi_{\varphi^{-1}(q)}
. From this and
Eq.

3.2.1
, it follows that

(3.2.3)

\left\{\xi_{q}\mid q\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})\right\}

is a basis for
\Hom_{\mathds{k}\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\left(M^{\bm{\beta}},M^{\bm{\delta}}\right)
and

\left\{\xi_{q}\mid q\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)\right\}

is a basis for
S(n,d)
.

3.3.
Classical Structure Constants

Using this indexing the structure constants for the Schur algebra can be described combinatorially. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Let
q,r,s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
. Let
A(n,q,r,s)
be as in
Section

2.3.2
.

For
A=(A_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k\in[1,n]}\in A(n,q,r,s)
, define

(3.3.1)

v_{A}=\prod_{i,k\in[1,n]}\binom{\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}}{A_{i,1,k},\dotsc,A_{i,n,k}}=\prod_{i,k\in[1,n]}\frac{(\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k})!}{\prod_{j\in[1,n]}(A_{i,j,k}!)}.

For
q,r,s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
, set

(3.3.2)

c_{q,r}^{s}=\sum_{A\in A(n,q,r,s)}v_{A}.

Remark 3.3.1
.

Observe that
A(n,q,r,s)
is empty unless
q\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\gamma},\bm{\delta})
,
r\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
, and
s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})
for some
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta},\bm{\gamma}\in\Lambda(n,d)
. Set
c_{q,r}^{s}=0
if
A(n,q,r,s)
is empty.

The structure constants for
S(n,d)
are given by the following result found in
[
23
, Proposition 2.3]
or
[
22
, Proposition 6]
.

Proposition 3.3.2
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta},\bm{\gamma}\in\Lambda(n,d)
. For
q\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\gamma},\bm{\delta})
and
r\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
, the product in
S(n,d)
is given by

\xi_{q}\xi_{r}=\sum_{s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})}c_{q,r}^{s}\xi_{s}.

3.4.
Green’s Codeterminant basis for
S(n,d)

Green gave an alternate basis for
S(n,d)
in
[
12
]
called the codeterminant basis, which we now describe. Given
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n,d)
and
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)
, set

X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})=\left\{\xi_{r}\mid\text{$r\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})$ and the filling of $Y^{\bm{\lambda}}$ by $r$ is semistandard}\right\}

and

Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})=\left\{\xi_{q}\mid\text{$q\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})$ and the filling of $Y^{\bm{\lambda}}$ by $q^{*}$ is semistandard}\right\}.

The set

\operatorname{CB}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta}):=\left\{\xi_{q}\xi_{r}\left|(\xi_{q},\xi_{r})\in\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n,d)}Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})\times X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})\right.\right\}

is Green’s codeterminant basis for
\Hom_{\mathds{k}\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\left(M^{\bm{\beta}},M^{\bm{\delta}}\right)
. The set

\operatorname{CB}(n,d):=\bigcup_{\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n,d)}\operatorname{CB}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})

is Green’s codeterminant basis for
S(n,d)
. This basis makes the classical Schur algebra a based quasi-hereditary algebra in the sense of
[
19
]
.

Remark 3.4.1
.

The results of this section remain true when
n=\infty
if one takes, as we do, the right hand side of
Eq.

3.2.2
as the definition of
S(\infty,d)
.

4.
The Interpolating Schur Algebra

We now define the main object of study in this paper.

4.1.
The Infinite Symmetric Group and Double Cosets

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
be the group of bijections from
\Omega:=\mathbb{N}
to itself.

Given
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dotsc,\beta_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
there is an associated Young subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\leq\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
isomorphic to

\mathfrak{S}_{\beta_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta_{n}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}.

There is also the corresponding
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-set of left cosets,
\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}}:=\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
.

Given
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, the set of double cosets
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
is in bijection with the set
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
via the map

(4.1.1)

q\mapsto\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}},

where
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
is any permutation with the property that, for all
i,j\in[1,n]
except
(i,j)=(1,1)
,

q_{i,j}=\left|\{\bm{\delta}\}_{i}\cap\sigma\left(\{\bm{\beta}\}_{j}\right)\right|.

Note that the set of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-orbits in
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
is in bijection with the set of double cosets
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
. The map is given on orbit representatives by

(4.1.2)

(x\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}},y\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})\mapsto\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}x^{-1}y\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}.

We will freely identify these sets via this bijection. Note that we can and will assume orbit representatives have the form
(\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}},z\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})
for some
z\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. The bijection then becomes
(\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}},z\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})\mapsto\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}z\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
.

4.2.
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
as an Oligomorphic Group

The action of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
on
\Omega
is faithful and the diagonal action of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
on
\Omega^{r}
has finitely many orbits for all
r\geq 1
. This makes the pair
(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty},\Omega)
an oligomorphic group. We refer the reader to
[
16
,
4
]
for details. Here we only recall enough to make sense of the concepts we use.

There is a topology on
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
given by declaring that the subgroups of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
which fix a finite subset of
\Omega
are a base of open neighborhoods of the identity. The action of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
on a set
X
is
smooth
if every point stabilizer is an open subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. In this paper, all
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-sets are assumed to be smooth. An
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-set is called
finitary
if the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
has finitely many orbits. Note that if
X
and
Y
are finitary
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-sets, then
X\times Y
is a finitary
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-set under the diagonal action (see
[
16
, Proposition 2.8(b)]
).

More generally, if there exists an open subgroup
U\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
acting smoothly on some set
X
(which may or may not be a
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-set), then we call
X
a
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-set. If the action of
U
has finitely many orbits, then we say it is a finitary
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-set. Being a finitary
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-set does not depend in an important way on the choice of open subgroup
U
. See
[
16
, Section I.2]
. Note that, because Young subgroups are open,
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
is a finitary
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-set for every
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
. Write
\mathcal{M}^{\bm{\beta}}
for the linearization of
\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}}=\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
.

Recall that
\mathds{k}
denotes a characteristic zero field. Harman–Snowden define the notion of a
\mathds{k}
-valued measure
associated to an oligomorphic group such as
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. This is a
\mathds{k}
-valued function on the collection of finitary
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-sets subject to various axioms. See
[
16
, Section I.3]
for specifics. Important for this paper is the fact that for each
t\in\mathds{k}
there exists a unique measure
\mu_{t}
satisfying
\mu_{t}(\Omega)=t
. A description of
\mu_{t}
is given as follows.

Example 4.2.1
.

For
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
\mathfrak{S}(p)
be the subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
which fixes
[1,p]
pointwise. As discussed in
[
16
, Example 3.4]
, for a finitary
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-set
X
there is a polynomial
p_{X}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]
such that

p_{X}(p)=\left|X^{\mathfrak{S}(p)}\right|

for all sufficiently large
p
. The measure
\mu_{t}
satisfies
\mu_{t}(X)=p_{X}(t)
for all
t\in\mathds{k}
.

With this description it is straightforward to see that

\mu_{t}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\right)=\binom{t}{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dotsc,\beta_{n}}

for all
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
.

4.3.
The Category
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})

Let
X
be a finitary
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-set, where
U
is an open subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
which acts smoothly on
X
with finitely many orbits. If
f:X\to\mathds{k}
is a function that is constant on
U
-orbits, then it is called a
Schwartz function on
X
. Note that Schwartz functions are defined for non-finitary sets in
[
16
]
, but that generality is not needed for this paper. If
f
is a Schwartz function on
X
and
c_{1},\dotsc,c_{z}\in\mathds{k}
are the distinct values of
f
, then the integral of
f
on
X
with respect to the measure
\mu_{t}
is defined to be

\int_{X}f(x)\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{z}c_{i}\mu_{t}(f^{-1}(c_{i})).

Given finitary
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-sets
X
,
Y
, and
Z
, and Schwartz functions
f:Y\times X\to\mathds{k}
and
g:Z\times Y\to\mathds{k}
, then
g\circ f:Z\times X\to\mathds{k}
is the Schwartz function given by their convolution:

(4.3.1)

(g\circ f)(z,x)=\int_{Y}g(z,y)f(y,x)\,dy.

The category
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
is the category of all finitary
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-sets with
\Hom_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}(X,Y)
being the
\mathds{k}
-vector space of Schwartz functions on the finitary
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-set
Y\times X
. Composition of morphisms in
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
is the composition of Schwartz functions given in
Eq.

4.3.1
.

4.4.
The Interpolating Schur Algebra

Definition 4.4.1
.

Fix
t\in\mathds{k}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
. Let
\mu_{t}
be the
\mathds{k}
-valued measure on finitary
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-sets which is described in
Example

4.2.1
. The Schur algebra associated to this data is the path algebra for the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
consisting of objects
\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}}
for
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
:

(4.4.1)

\mathbb{S}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)=\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}}\Hom_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}\left(\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}},\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\delta}}\right).

We frequently leave the field of definition implicit and write
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
for
\mathbb{S}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)
.

There is an obvious basis for
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
. Namely, given
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, consider
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
. Since this is a finitary
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-set, the space of Schwartz functions has a
\mathds{k}
-basis given by the indicator functions for the orbits. By
Section

4.1
these are indexed by
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
. Let

\xi_{q}\in\Hom_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}\left(\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}},\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\delta}}\right)

denote the indicator function which is
1
on the orbit labelled by
q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
and
0
on all other orbits. Then the following result holds.

Proposition 4.4.2
.

Fix
t\in\mathds{k}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
. For each
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
,

\left\{\xi_{q}\mid q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}\right\}

is a
\mathds{k}
-basis for
\Hom_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}\left(\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}},\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\delta}}\right)
and each such summand given in
Eq.

4.4.1
is finite-dimensional as a
\mathds{k}
-vector space.

Moreover, the set

\mathcal{SB}(n)_{t}:=\left\{\xi_{q}\mid q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)\right\}

is a
\mathds{k}
-basis for
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
.

4.5.
Structure Constants

We next derive a combinatorial description of the structure constants for
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
.

Throughout this section let
q=(q_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\delta})_{t}
and
r=(r_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
. Consider

(4.5.1)

\xi_{q}\circ\xi_{r}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})_{t}}C_{q,r}^{s}(t)\xi_{s},

where
C_{q,r}^{s}(t)\in\mathds{k}
.

For
s=(s_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})_{t}
,
\xi_{s}
is the indicator function for some double coset, say
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
. It follows that

(4.5.2)

C_{q,r}^{s}(t)=(\xi_{q}\circ\xi_{r})(\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})=\int_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}}\xi_{q}(\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}},\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}})\xi_{r}(\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}},\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})\,d\left(\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\right).

However, the function

\xi_{q}(\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}},-)\xi_{r}(-,\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}):\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\to\mathds{k}

only takes on values
0
and
1
. Therefore, the integral in
Eq.

4.5.2
is simply the measure of the set of cosets
\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
such that the double coset
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
has indicator function
\xi_{q}
and, simultaneously, the double coset
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\theta^{-1}\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
has indicator function
\xi_{r}
.
We let
X(q,r,s)
denote this set of cosets. Then,

(4.5.3)

C_{q,r}^{s}(t)=\mu_{t}(X(q,r,s)).

To compute the measure of
X(q,r,s)
requires some preparatory lemmas.

It is straightforward to verify the following result.

Lemma 4.5.1
.

The subgroup
U(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta})=\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\cap\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\sigma^{-1}
acts on
X(q,r,s)
by left translation. The stabilizer of
\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\in X(q,r,s)
is equal to
V(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta},\theta,\bm{\delta})=U(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta})\cap\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\theta^{-1}
.

Lemma 4.5.2
.

The
U(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta})
-orbits on
X(q,r,s)
are in bijection with
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
.

Proof.

For short, write
U=U(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta})
. Define a map

\displaystyle X(q,r,s)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}

\displaystyle\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}

\displaystyle\mapsto(A_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k\in[1,n]},

where

A_{i,j,k}=\begin{cases}\left|\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}\right|,&\text{if }(i,j,k)\neq(1,1,1);\\
t-\sum_{(i,j,k)\in[1,n]^{3}\backslash\{(1,1,1)\}}A_{i,j,k},&\text{for }(i,j,k)=(1,1,1).\end{cases}

That
A=(A_{i,j,k})
lies in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
is a straightforward verification. We next claim the function is constant on
U
-orbits. Say
u\in U
satisfies
u\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}=\pi\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}.
Then
u\theta=\pi x
for some
x\in\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
. Using that
u
is an element of
U=\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\cap\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\sigma^{-1}
and applying
u
to the set
\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}
yields

\left|\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}\right|=\left|\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}\right|

for all
(i,j,k)\neq(1,1,1)
. Thus the array defined using
\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
is the same as the array defined using
\pi\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
. Now consider the induced function from the set of
U
-orbits to
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
.

We first show the map is injective. Suppose
\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
and
\pi\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
map to the same array. Then, for all
i,j,k\in[1,n]
,

\left|\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}\right|=\left|\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}\right|.

The collections

\left\{\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}\mid(i,j,k)\in[1,n]^{3}\right\}

and

\left\{\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}\mid(i,j,k)\in[1,n]^{3}\right\}

are two set partitions of
\Omega
. By assumption, the subsets associated to
(i,j,k)
in each set partition have the same cardinality. Therefore there is an element
u\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
which takes each subset in the first set partition to its mate in the second set partition. That is,

(4.5.4)

u(\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k})=\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}

for all
i,j,k\in[1,n]
.

We claim
u\in U=\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\cap\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\sigma^{-1}
. First note that, for any
i\in[1,n]
,

\displaystyle u\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}

\displaystyle=u\bigcup_{j,k\in[1,n]}\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j,k\in[1,n]}u(\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j,k\in[1,n]}\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k},

\displaystyle=\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}.

Hence
u\in\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}
. On the other hand, for any
k\in[1,n]
,

\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}=\bigcup_{i,j\in[1,n]}\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}.

Applying
u
to both sides yields

\displaystyle u\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}

\displaystyle=u\bigcup_{i,j\in[1,n]}\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i,j\in[1,n]}u(\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i,j\in[1,n]}\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}.

This shows
u\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}=\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}
and, hence,
u\in\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\sigma^{-1}
. So
u\in U=\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\cap\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\sigma^{-1}
, as claimed.

We next show that
u\pi\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}=\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
. Since
u\in U
satisfies
Eq.

4.5.4
, it follows that for all
j\in[1,n]
,

\displaystyle u\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i,k\in[1,n]}\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap u\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i,k\in[1,n]}u\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap u\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap u\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k},

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i,k\in[1,n]}u(\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\pi\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i,k\in[1,n]}\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k},

\displaystyle=\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}.

But
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
is the stabilizer of the set partition
\{\{\bm{\delta}\}_{1},\dotsc,\{\bm{\delta}\}_{n}\}
and so we conclude
\theta^{-1}u\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
. Hence,
u\pi\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}=\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
, as claimed.

Finally, we show the map is surjective. Let
A=(A_{i,j,k})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
. For any
i,k\in[1,n]
, set
X_{i,k}=\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}
. On the one hand,
\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
is the double coset corresponding to the indicator function
\xi_{s}
. Applying
Eq.

4.1.1
, this shows that the cardinality of
X_{i,k}
equals
s_{i,k}
when
(i,k)\neq(1,1)
and the cardinality of
X_{1,1}
is infinite. On the other hand,
\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=s_{i,k}
for all
i,k\in[1,n]
. Taken together, this implies that for each pair
(i,k)
, there exists a set partition of
X_{i,k}
,
\left\{X_{i,j,k}\mid j\in[1,n]\right\}
, such that
X_{i,j,k}
has cardinality
A_{i,j,k}
for
(i,j,k)\neq(1,1,1)
and
X_{1,1,1}
is infinite. Observe that
\{X_{i,j,k}\mid i,j,k\in[1,n]\}
provides a set partition of
\Omega
. Also observe that for
j\in[2,n]
,

|\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}|=\sum_{i,k\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=\left|\bigcup_{i,k\in[1,n]}X_{i,j,k}\right|,

and both
\{\bm{\delta}\}_{1}
and
\bigcup_{i,k\in[1,n]}X_{i,1,k}
are infinite. Choose
\theta\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
with the property that it maps
\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}
bijectively to
\bigcup_{i,k}X_{i,j,k}
for all
j\in[1,n]
. By construction,
|\{\bm{\gamma}\}_{i}\cap\theta\{\bm{\delta}\}_{j}\cap\sigma\{\bm{\beta}\}_{k}|=A_{i,j,k}
. That is,
\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\mapsto A
, as required.
∎

Using the fact that any conjugate of a Young subgroup is an open subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
, it follows that any
U(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta})
-orbit in
X(q,r,s)
is a finitary
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-set and, hence, it makes sense to take its measure. The following result computes this measure explicitly.

To state the result, we need to introduce the following expression. Given
t\in R
,
q,r,s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)
, and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
, define

(4.5.5)

V_{A}(t)=\prod_{i,k\in[1,n]}\binom{\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}}{A_{i,1,k},\dotsc,A_{i,n,k}}=\prod_{i,k\in[1,n]}\frac{(\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k})!}{\prod_{j\in[1,n]}(A_{i,j,k}!)}.

Going forward we assume
R
and
t
are such that
V_{A}(t)\in R
for all such arrays (e.g.,
R=\mathds{k}
).

Lemma 4.5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{A}
be the
U(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta})
-orbit in
X(q,r,s)
associated to
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
by the bijection given in the proof of the previous lemma. Then,

\mu_{t}\left(\mathcal{O}_{A}\right)=V_{A}(t).

Proof.

Say
\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\in X(q,r,s)
is chosen so that
\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
maps to the array
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
in the previous proof. For short, let
U=U(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta})
and let
V=V(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta},\theta,\bm{\delta})
be the stabilizer of
\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}
for the action of
U
on
X(q,r,s)
. Then
\mathcal{O}_{A}
is isomorphic to
U/V
as
\widehat{\mathfrak{S}}_{\infty}
-sets so we may instead compute the measure of
U/V
thanks to
[
16
, Definition 3.1(a)]
.

By property
[
16
, Definition 3.1(e)]
, since the canonical map
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/V\to\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/U
is a surjective map of transitive
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-sets with fiber
U/V
, for all
t\in\mathds{k}
we have

\mu_{t}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/V)=\mu_{t}(U/V)\mu_{t}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/U).

On the other hand, as discussed in
Example

4.2.1
, there are polynomials
p_{X}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]
for
X\in\{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/V,\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/U,U/V\}
such that
p_{X}(t)=\mu_{t}(X)
for all
t\in\mathds{k}
. Combining these observations shows that

(4.5.6)

p_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/V}(T)=p_{U/V}(T)p_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/U}(T)

in
\mathbb{Q}[T]
.

Note that
V=V(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta},\theta,\bm{\delta})=\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\cap\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\sigma^{-1}\cap\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\delta}}\theta^{-1}
is a Young subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. From the proof of
Lemma

4.5.2
it follows that this Young subgroup is defined by the composition
\left(A_{i,j,k}\right)\in\Lambda(n^{3})_{t}
. Replacing
t
with
T
in
A_{1,1,1}
and applying
Example

4.2.1
yields

p_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/V}(T)=\binom{T}{A_{1,1,1},A_{1,1,2},\dots,A_{n,n,n}}=\frac{T(T-1)\cdots(T-m+1)}{\prod_{(i,j,k)\in[1,n]^{3}\backslash\{(1,1,1)\}}(A_{i,j,k}!)},

where
m=\sum_{(i,j,k)\in[1,n]^{3}\backslash\{(1,1,1)\}}A_{i,j,k}
.

Likewise, note that
U=U(\bm{\gamma},\sigma,\bm{\beta})=\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\gamma}}\cap\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\sigma^{-1}
is a Young subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
. Moreover, if for
(i,k)\in[1,n]^{2}
we set
\rho_{i,k}=\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}
, then
\left(\rho_{i,k}\right)\in\Lambda(n^{2})_{t}
is the composition which defines this Young subgroup. Replacing
t
with
T
in
\rho_{1,1}
and applying
Example

4.2.1
yields

p_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/U}(T)=\binom{T}{\rho_{1,1},\rho_{1,2},\dots,\rho_{n,n}}=\frac{T(T-1)\cdots(T-\ell+1)}{\prod_{(i,k)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}(\rho_{i,k}!)},

where
\ell=\sum_{(i,k)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}\rho_{i,k}=\sum_{(i,j,k)\in[1,n]^{3}\backslash\{(1,1,1)\}}A_{i,j,k}-\sum_{j\in[2,n]}A_{1,j,1}
.

Substituting these into
Eq.

4.5.6
and simplifying demonstrates that

\displaystyle p_{U/V}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{(T-\ell)(T-\ell-1)\dots(T-m+1)}{\prod_{j\in[2,n]}(A_{1,j,1}!)}\prod_{(i,k)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}\frac{\rho_{i,k}!}{A_{i,1,k}!\dots A_{i,n,k}!}

(4.5.7)

\displaystyle=\binom{T-\ell}{T-m,A_{1,2,1},\dots,A_{1,n,1}}\prod_{(i,k)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}\binom{\rho_{i,k}}{A_{i,1,k},\dots,A_{i,n,k}}

\displaystyle=V_{A}(T).

Evaluating
p_{U/V}(T)
at
T=t
proves the assertion.
∎

Remark 4.5.4
.

If
R=\mathbb{Q}[T]
and
t=T
, the formulation of
V_{A}(T)
given in
Eq.

4.5.7
along with the discussion in
Section

2.2
shows that
V_{A}(T)
is an integer-valued polynomial.

Theorem 4.5.5
.

Let
q,r,s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)
. Then,

C_{q,r}^{s}(t)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}}V_{A}(t).

Proof.

This follows immediately from the lemmas. Namely,

C_{q,r}^{s}(t)=\mu_{t}\left(X(q,r,s)\right)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}}\mu_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{A})=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}}V_{A}(t).

∎

Recall that for
q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
,
q^{*}\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})_{t}
denotes the transpose matrix.

Corollary 4.5.6
.

The map
\tau:\mathbb{S}(n,t)\to\mathbb{S}(n,t)
defined by
\xi_{q}\mapsto\xi_{q^{*}}
is an involutive anti-automorphism of
\mathds{k}
-algebras.

Proof.

Let
A=(A_{i,j,k})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
. There is a bijection
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}\to\mathcal{A}_{n}(r^{*},q^{*},s^{*})_{t}
given by
A=(A_{i,j,k})\mapsto A^{*}=(A_{k,j,i})
. The formula given in
Eq.

4.5.5
can be used to show that
V_{A}(t)=V_{A^{*}}(t)
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{t}
and, hence,
C_{q,r}^{s}(t)=C_{r^{*},q^{*}}^{s^{*}}(t)
for all
q,r,s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)
. The claim follows.
∎

Given a composition
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, let
q_{\bm{\beta}}=(q_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
be the matrix which has
q_{i,i}=\beta_{i}
and
q_{i,j}=0
for
i\neq j
. An elementary application of the formula for
C_{q,r}^{s}(t)
establishes the following result.

Corollary 4.5.7
.

For each
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
the element
1_{\bm{\beta}}:=\xi_{q_{\bm{\beta}}}\in\Hom_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}(\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}},\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}})
is the identity morphism. Thus,
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is a locally unital
\mathds{k}
-algebra with distinguished set of pairwise orthogonal idempotents
\left\{1_{\bm{\beta}}\mid\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}\right\}
. These idempotents are fixed by the anti-involution
\tau
.

5.
Negligible Morphisms, Semisimplification, and the Classical Schur Algebra

5.1.
Negligible Morphisms

By
[
16
, Corollary 8.7]
,
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
is a
\mathds{k}
-linear rigid tensor category. The tensor product on objects is given by the direct product of finitary
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-sets. Every object of
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
is self-dual and
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
is spherical. We refer the reader to
[
10
]
for definitions and standard results for such categories.

Such categories have a
\mathds{k}
-linear
categorical trace
map

\operatorname{tr}=\operatorname{tr}_{X}:\operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}(X)\to\mathds{k}

for every object
X
in
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
. In this setting the categorical trace has a concrete description
[
16
, Proposition 8.10]
. Namely, given
f\in\operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}(X)
(i.e., a Schwartz function
f:X\times X\to\mathds{k}
), then

(5.1.1)

\operatorname{tr}(f)=\int_{X}f(x,x)\;\;dx.

A morphism
f:X\to Y
is called
negligible
if for all morphisms
g:Y\to X
,
\operatorname{tr}(f\circ g)=\operatorname{tr}(g\circ f)=0
. For every pair of objects
X,Y
in
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
, let

\mathcal{N}(X,Y)=\left\{f\in\Hom_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}(X,Y)\mid\text{$f$ is negligible}\right\}.

By
[
10
, Exercise 8.18.9]
or
[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
,
\mathcal{N}
is a tensor ideal of
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
and the quotient category
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})/\mathcal{N}
is a semisimple
\mathds{k}
-linear rigid tensor category. This is known as the
non-degenerate quotient
or
semisimplification
of
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
.

Recall that
\xi_{q_{\bm{\beta}}}
is the identity morphism in
\operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})}(\mathcal{X}^{\bm{\beta}})
.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

In
\operatorname{Perm}(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty};\mu_{t})
the following categorical trace values hold:

(1)

Given
q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
,

\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q})=\delta_{q,q_{\bm{\beta}}}\binom{t}{\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{n}};

(2)

Given
q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})_{t}
and
r\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
, let
m=\sum_{(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}q_{i,j}
. Then there exists
z\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
such that

(5.1.2)

\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q}\circ\xi_{r})=\delta_{r,q^{*}}\prod_{i\in[1,n]}\frac{\left(\sum_{j\in[1,n]}q_{i,j}\right)!}{q_{i,1}!q_{i,2}!\dotsb q_{i,n}!}=\delta_{r,q^{*}}t(t-1)(t-2)\dotsb(t-m+1)z,

Proof.

To prove (1), choose
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
so that the
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
which contains
(\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}},\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})
corresponds to
q
as described in
Section

4.1
. For short, write
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}
for that orbit. We compute the trace using
Eq.

5.1.1
,

\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q})=\int_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}}\xi_{q}(\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}},\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})\;\;d\left(\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\right).

Since
\xi_{q}
is the indicator function for
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}
and since
(\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}},\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})
is in
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}
if and only if
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}
, this integral is identically zero unless
q=q_{\bm{\beta}}
. In that case,

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q_{\bm{\beta}}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}}\xi_{q_{\bm{\beta}}}(\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}},\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}})\;\;d\left(\theta\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{t}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}/\mathfrak{S}_{\bm{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\binom{t}{\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{n}},

where the last equality was given in
Example

4.2.1
.

To prove (2), note that the
\mathds{k}
-linearity of the trace function along with (1) immediately implies that

\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q}\circ\xi_{r})=C_{q,r}^{q_{\bm{\beta}}}(t)\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q_{\bm{\beta}}}),

where
C_{q,r}^{q_{\bm{\beta}}}(t)
is the structure constant described by
Theorem

4.5.5
. We next claim that
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,q_{\bm{\beta}})
contains exactly one element if
r=q^{*}
and is otherwise empty. Let
A=(A_{i,j,k})
be an array in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,q_{\bm{\beta}})
. From the fact that the entries of
A
are nonnegative integers (excepting
A_{1,1,1}
, of course), the condition that
\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=(q_{\bm{\beta}})_{i,k}=0
for
i\neq k
implies that if
i\neq k
, then
A_{i,j,k}=0
for all
j\in[1,n]
. But then, since
\sum_{k\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=q_{i,j}
, it follows that for all
i,j\in[1,n]
we have
A_{i,j,k}=q_{i,j}
if
k=i
, and
A_{i,j,k}=0
if
k\neq i
. That is,
A
is completely determined by
q
and
q_{\bm{\beta}}
, if it exists. Hence, there is at most one such array. And if the array exists, then
r_{j,k}=\sum_{i\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=A_{k,j,k}=q_{k,j}
and so
r=q^{*}
. In particular,
\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q}\circ\xi_{r})=0
if
r\neq q^{*}
.

Now assume
r=q^{*}
and set
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,q_{\bm{\beta}})
to be the unique array determined in the previous paragraph. Applying
Theorem

4.5.5
,
Eq.

4.5.5
, and claim (1) yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q}\circ\xi_{r})

\displaystyle=V_{A}(t)\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q_{\bm{\beta}}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i,k\in[1,n]}\frac{\left(\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}\right)!}{A_{i,1,k}!A_{i,2,k}!\dotsb A_{i,n,k}!}\operatorname{tr}(\xi_{q_{\bm{\beta}}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in[1,n]}\frac{\left(\sum_{j\in[1,n]}q_{i,j}\right)!}{q_{i,1}!q_{i,2}!\dotsb q_{i,n}!}\binom{t}{\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{n}}.

Isolating the terms which contain
t
yields

(5.1.3)

(t-\ell)(t-\ell-1)(t-\ell-2)\dotsb(t-m+1)t(t-1)(t-2)\dotsb(t-u+1)z,

where
z\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
,
\ell=\sum_{(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}q_{i,j}-\sum_{j\in[2,n]}q_{1,j}
,
m=\sum_{(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}q_{i,j}
, and
u=\sum_{i\in[2,n]}\beta_{i}
. Using
\sum_{i,j\in[1,n]}q_{i,j}=t
and the row-sum
\sum_{j\in[1,n]}q_{1,j}=\beta_{1}
, both
\ell
and
u
equal
t-\beta_{1}
. Therefore, after replacing
\ell
with
u
and rearranging terms,
Eq.

5.1.3
becomes

t(t-1)(t-2)\dotsb(t-u+1)(t-u)\dotsb(t-m+1)z.

The claim follows.
∎

Corollary 5.1.2
.

Let
q=(q_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
. Then,
\xi_{q}
is negligible if and only if
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
q_{1,1}<0
.

Proof.

Let
\ell=\sum_{(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}q_{i,j}-\sum_{j\in[2,n]}q_{1,j}
and
m=\sum_{(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}q_{i,j}
. It follows from
Lemma

5.1.1
that the morphism
\xi_{q}
is negligible if and only if
t
is a root of
Eq.

5.1.2
. That is,

t=0,1,\dotsc,m-1.

Or, equivalently, if and only if
t
is a nonnegative integer and
t-m=q_{1,1}
is negative.
∎

5.2.
\mathbb{S}(n,d)
and
S(n,d)

Throughout this section fix
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,

, and assume that
t=d
. Recall from
Remark

2.4.2
that
\Lambda(n,d)
is a subset of
\Lambda(n)_{d}
,
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
is a subset of
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(d)

5.1.1

Theorem 5.2.1
.

Let

and

. Then

\mathbb{S}(n,d)/\mathcal{I}(n,d)\cong S(n,d)

as
\mathds{k}
-algebras.

Proof.

By construction,
\mathbb{S}(n,d)/\mathcal{I}(n,d)
has a basis indexed by the set

M:=\left\{q=(q_{i,j})\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(d)\mid q_{1,1}\geq 0\right\}.

However, this set coincides with
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
and so also indexes the basis for
S(n,d)
. Define a vector space isomorphism

\mathbb{S}(n,d)/\mathcal{I}(n,d)\to S(n,d)

by
\xi_{q}+\mathcal{I}(n,d)\mapsto\xi_{q}
for all

.

Let
s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
, so
s_{1,1}\geq 0
. The set
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{d}
splits into arrays

of two types: those with

4.5.7

identifies with

and, moreover,

. These observations imply that

C_{q,r}^{s}(d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{d}\\
A_{1,1,1}\geq 0\end{subarray}}V_{A}(d)=\sum_{A\in A(n,q,r,s)}v_{A}=c_{q,r}^{s}.

Therefore, this map is also a
\mathds{k}
-algebra isomorphism.
∎

5.3.
The algebra
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)

Consider
\mathds{k}=\mathbb{Q}(T)
and set
t=T
. Let

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}(n,T)

denote the free
\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
-submodule of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}(n,T)
with basis
\left\{\xi_{q}\mid q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(T)\right\}
. Since the structure constants

Remark 5.3.1
.

When
t=d
is a nonnegative integer and
\mathds{k}
is a characteristic zero field, write

for the composite

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_{d}}\mathbb{S}_{\mathds{k}}(n,d)\xrightarrow{f^{\mathds{k}}_{n,d}}S_{\mathds{k}}(n,d).

is asymptotically injective. That is, for any fixed nonzero

,

will be nonzero for all sufficiently large

.

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)
and

that parallel Green’s codeterminant bases for

and

6.1.
Codeterminant Index Sets with a
t

For

6.2.
Codeterminant Basis for
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)

Theorem 6.2.1
.

Let
\mathds{k}=\mathbb{Q}(T)
and fix
t=T
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. For each
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{T}
, the set

\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}=\left\{\xi_{q}\xi_{r}\left|(\xi_{q},\xi_{r})\in\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{T}}Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})_{T}\times X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{T}\right.\right\}

is a
\mathbb{Q}(T)
-basis for
1_{\bm{\delta}}\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}(n,T)1_{\bm{\beta}}
.

Proof.

Recall from
Remark

5.3.1
that there is a ring homomorphism:

F_{n,d}^{\mathbb{Q}}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)\to S_{\mathbb{Q}}(n,d).

Recalling that
F_{n,d}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\xi_{q})
is zero if
q(d)_{1,1}<0
and is otherwise the basis element of
S_{\mathbb{Q}}(n,d)
indexed by
q(d)\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
, we abuse notation for the duration of the proof by writing
\xi_{q(d)}\in S_{\mathbb{Q}}(n,d)
for
F_{n,d}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\xi_{q})
.

For a fixed
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{T}
, the sets

\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{T}}Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})_{T}\times X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{T}

and

\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}

are finite. Therefore, as discussed in
Remark

2.4.3
, for all sufficiently large
d
it is true that:

(1)

the map

\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{T}}Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})_{T}\times X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{T}\to\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n,d)}Y(\bm{\delta}(d),\bm{\lambda}(d))\times X(\bm{\lambda}(d),\bm{\beta}(d))

given by

is a bijection;

(2)

the map

\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}\to\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta}(d),\bm{\beta}(d))

given by
q\mapsto q(d)
is a bijection;

(3)

the filling of the Young diagram
Y^{\bm{\lambda}(d)}
by
r(d)
and by
q^{*}(d)
are both semistandard for all
(\xi_{q},\xi_{r})
in
\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{T}}Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})_{T}\times X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{T}
.

Under those conditions,

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{T}}Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})_{T}\times X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{T}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n,d)}Y(\bm{\delta}(d),\bm{\lambda}(d))\times X(\bm{\lambda}(d),\bm{\beta}(d))\right|,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta}(d),\bm{\beta}(d))\right|,

and the set

\operatorname{CB}(n,d,\bm{\delta}(d),\bm{\beta}(d))=\left\{\xi_{q(d)}\xi_{r(d)}\left|(\xi_{q(d)},\xi_{r(d)})\in\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}(d)\in\Lambda^{+}(n,d)}Y(\bm{\delta}(d),\bm{\lambda}(d))\times X(\bm{\lambda}(d),\bm{\beta}(d))\right.\right\}

is Green’s codeterminant basis for
\Hom_{\mathbb{Q}\mathfrak{S}_{d}}(M^{\bm{\beta}(d)},M^{\bm{\delta}(d)})=1_{\bm{\delta}(d)}S_{\mathbb{Q}}(n,d)1_{\bm{\beta}(d)}
.

To show linear independence, let
\xi_{q_{1}}\xi_{r_{1}},\ldots,\xi_{q_{p}}\xi_{r_{p}}
be a finite collection of distinct elements from
\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}
and say

(6.2.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}(T)\xi_{q_{i}}\xi_{r_{i}}=0,

where
r_{i}(T)=a_{i}(T)/b_{i}(T)
for some
a_{i}(T),b_{i}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]
with
b_{i}(T)\neq 0
. By clearing denominators we may assume without loss of generality that
r_{i}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]
for all
i\in[1,p]
and, hence,
Eq.

6.2.1
lies in
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)
. Applying

to

yields

\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}(d)\xi_{q_{i}(d)}\xi_{r_{i}(d)}=0,

in

for all

. However,

for all

and all

. Thus,

for all

, as required.

For spanning, since

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{\mathds{k}}\left(1_{\bm{\delta}}\mathbb{S}_{\mathds{k}}(n,T)1_{\bm{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}|

\displaystyle=|\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta}(d),\bm{\beta}(d))|

∎

Remark 6.2.2

Corollary 6.2.3

The set

to

is defined over the integers.

Proposition 6.2.4
.

Let
\mathds{k}=\mathbb{Q}(T)
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Say
\xi_{q}\xi_{r}\in\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}
. If one writes

\xi_{q}\xi_{r}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}}C_{q,r}^{s}(T)\xi_{s}

in

for all

.

Proof.

Since
\xi_{q}\in Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})_{T}
, the discussion after

2.4.3
shows that

is lower triangular. Let
s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}
and let

be an array in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q,r,s)_{T}
. Since
\sum_{k\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=q_{i,j}=0
for

, this implies
A_{i,j,k}=0
for all
k\in[1,n]
as long as
i<j
. In particular,
A_{1,j,1}=0
for
j\in[2,n]
. But then
\sum_{j\in[2,n]}A_{1,j,1}=0
and

, so the leading binomial
\binom{T-\ell}{T-m,0,\dots,0}
in the formula for

given in

4.5.7
equals one. Hence,
V_{A}(T)
is an integer. This fact along with
Theorem

4.5.5
proves the claim.
∎

Let

(6.2.2)

\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}(n,T)

be the
\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
-span of
\mathcal{CB}(n)_{T}
. The next result shows this is a
\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
-form for
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}(T)}(n,T)
.

Proposition 6.2.5
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)
is a
\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
-algebra.

Proof.

All that needs to be verified is that
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)
is closed under multiplication. Let
\xi_{q_{1}}\xi_{r_{1}}\in\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\delta})_{T}
and
\xi_{q_{2}}\xi_{r_{2}}\in\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{T}
. Since
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)
is contained in
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)
, a
\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
-algebra, we have

(6.2.3)

(\xi_{q_{1}}\xi_{r_{1}})(\xi_{q_{2}}\xi_{r_{2}})=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})_{T}}a_{s}(T)\xi_{s},

for
a_{s}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
.
Proposition

6.2.4
implies that each
\xi_{s}
can be written as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of elements from
\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})_{T}
. Substituting these combinations into
Eq.

6.2.3
yields

(\xi_{q_{1}}\xi_{r_{1}})(\xi_{q_{2}}\xi_{r_{2}})=\sum_{\xi_{t}\xi_{u}\in\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})_{T}}b_{t,u}(T)\xi_{t}\xi_{u},

for
b_{t,u}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]
. Taking
d\gg 0
and applying
F_{n,d}^{\mathbb{Q}}
yields

(\xi_{q_{1}(d)}\xi_{r_{1}(d)})(\xi_{q_{2}(d)}\xi_{r_{2}(d)})=\sum_{\xi_{t}\xi_{u}\in\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})_{T}}b_{t,u}(d)\xi_{t(d)}\xi_{u(d)}

in
S_{\mathbb{Q}}(n,d)
.

Green’s codeterminant basis has integral structure constants
[
12
,
26
]
. That is,
b_{t,u}(d)
is an integer for all
d\gg 0
and
b_{t,u}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
for all
\xi_{t}\xi_{u}\in\mathcal{CB}(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})_{T}
, as desired.
∎

Let
\mathds{k}
be a characteristic zero field and fix
t\in\mathds{k}
. Then
\mathds{k}
is a
\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle
-module via the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}_{t}:\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle\to\mathds{k}
given by
\operatorname{ev}_{t}(p(T))=p(t)
. There are canonical
\mathds{k}
-algebra isomorphisms

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}\mathds{k}\cong\mathbb{S}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)\cong\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}(n,T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\langle T\rangle}\mathds{k}.

In particular, via these isomorphisms it follows that the set

(6.2.4)

\mathcal{CB}(n)_{t}=\left\{\xi_{q}\xi_{r}\left|(\xi_{q},\xi_{r})\in\cup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}}Y(\bm{\lambda})_{t}\times X(\bm{\lambda})_{t}\right.\right\}

defines a
\mathds{k}
-basis for
\mathbb{S}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)
.

7.
Representation Theory of
\mathbb{S}(n,t)

In this section we establish that
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is a symmetrically based quasi-hereditary algebra and, hence, its representations define a highest weight category.

7.1.
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-Modules

Let
t\in\mathds{k}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Recall that
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is a locally unital algebra with distinguished idempotents
\{1_{\bm{\beta}}\mid\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}\}
. By
Proposition

4.4.2
, it is
locally finite-dimensional
:
1_{\bm{\delta}}\mathbb{S}(n,t)1_{\bm{\beta}}
is a finite-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-vector space for all
\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
.

By definition, an
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-module is a module,
M
, for the associative
\mathds{k}
-algebra
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
which also satisfies
M=\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}}1_{\bm{\beta}}M
, where the direct sum is a direct sum of
\mathds{k}
-vector spaces. We call elements of
M_{\bm{\beta}}:=1_{\bm{\beta}}M

weight vectors
of
weight

\bm{\beta}
and call
M_{\bm{\beta}}
a
weight space
. We call
M
locally finite-dimensional if every weight space of
M
is a finite-dimensional
\mathds{k}
-vector space. Let
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}
denote the category of all
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules, let
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}_{\text{lfd}}
denote the full subcategory of locally finite-dimensional
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules, and let
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}_{\text{fg}}
denote the full subcategory of finitely generated
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules. Note that finitely generated modules for a locally finite-dimensional algebra are always locally finite-dimensional.

There is a contravariant equivalence between right and left locally finite-dimensional
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules given by the graded dual:
M\mapsto M^{\circledast}=\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}}M_{\bm{\beta}}^{*}
, where
M_{\bm{\beta}}^{*}=\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(M_{\bm{\beta}},\mathds{k})
. There is a contravariant autoequivalence of
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}_{\text{lfd}}
given by
M\mapsto M^{\vee}
, where
M^{\vee}=M^{\circledast}
as a
\mathds{k}
-vector space and the action of
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
given by
(a.f)(m)=f(\tau(a)m)
for all
f\in M^{\vee}
,
a\in\mathbb{S}(n,t)
, and
m\in M
, where
\tau
is the involutive anti-automorphism defined in
Corollary

4.5.6
.

7.2.
Highest Weight Combinatorics

Recall the dominance partial order on
\Lambda(n)_{t}
given by
Eq.

2.4.2
. Call a partially ordered set
(X,\leq)

upper finite
if
[x,\infty):=\{y\in X\mid x\leq y\}
is a finite set for all
x\in X
. The following lemma records some of the basic properties of
(\Lambda(n)_{t},\preceq)
.

Lemma 7.2.1
.

The following statements are true:

(1)

The partially ordered set
(\Lambda(n)_{t},\preceq)
is upper finite.

(2)

For
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
and
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
, the sets
X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{t}
and
Y(\bm{\beta},\bm{\lambda})_{t}
are empty unless
\bm{\beta}\preceq\bm{\lambda}
.

(3)

For
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
,
X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\lambda})_{t}=Y(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\lambda})_{t}=\{\xi_{q_{\bm{\lambda}}}=1_{\bm{\lambda}}\}
.

Proof.

To prove (1), let
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{n})\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
and consider
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{n})\in[\bm{\mu},\infty)
. Then

\left(t-\sum_{i\in[2,n]}\lambda_{i}\right)-\left(t-\sum_{i\in[2,n]}\mu_{i}\right)=\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}\geq 0.

Consequently,

\sum_{i\in[2,n]}\mu_{i}\geq\sum_{i\in[2,n]}\lambda_{i}.

However,
\lambda_{2},\dotsc,\lambda_{n}
are nonnegative integers and only finitely many
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
satisfy this condition for a fixed
\bm{\mu}
.

To prove (2), consider
q\in X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{t}
and
d\gg 0
. Since
q(d)
is upper triangular, for each
p\in[1,n]
it is true that

\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta(d)_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{p}q(d)_{i,j}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{p}q(d)_{i,j}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{n}q(d)_{i,j}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda(d)_{i}.

Therefore
\bm{\beta}\preceq\bm{\lambda}
, as claimed. A similar argument applies to
Y(\bm{\beta},\bm{\lambda})_{t}
.

To prove (3), observe that there is one and only one filling of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}(d)}
by a
q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\lambda})_{t}
which is semistandard. Namely, since
\sum_{i\in[1,n]}q(d)_{i,1}=\bm{\lambda}(d)_{1}
, a filling of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}(d)}
by
q(d)
will have
\bm{\lambda}(d)_{1}
ones. For the filling to be semistandard, they must exactly be used to fill the
\bm{\lambda}(d)_{1}
boxes in the first row of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}(d)}
. Similarly,
\sum_{i\in[1,n]}q(d)_{i,2}=\bm{\lambda}(d)_{2}
and a filling of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}(d)}
by
q(d)
will have
\bm{\lambda}(d)_{2}
twos. For the filling to be semistandard, they must exactly be used to fill the
\bm{\lambda}(d)_{2}
boxes in the second row of
Y^{\bm{\lambda}(d)}
. And so on. Thus
q=q_{\bm{\lambda}}
, as claimed. Likewise, for
q^{*}
.
∎

7.3.
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is an upper finite symmetrically based quasi-hereditary algebra

Theorem 7.3.1
.

Let
\mathds{k}
be a characteristic zero field, fix
t\in\mathds{k}
, and fix
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Consider
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
with the anti-involution
\tau
defined in
Corollary

4.5.6
. Set
I=\Lambda(n)_{t}
and
\Lambda=\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
, both equipped with the dominance order
\preceq
. For
\bm{\mu}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
and
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
, let
X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})_{t}
,
X(\bm{\lambda})_{t}
,
Y(\bm{\mu},\bm{\lambda})_{t}
, and
Y(\bm{\lambda})_{t}
be as in
Section

6.1
.

This data makes
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
an upper finite symmetrically based quasi-hereditary algebra in the sense of
[
3
, Definition 5.1]
.

Proof.

The reader can easily confirm that the six defining properties of an upper finite, based quasi-hereditary algebra given in
[
3
, Definition 5.1]
have already been established. Namely,
\mathbb{S}(n,t)=\bigoplus_{\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta}\in I}1_{\bm{\delta}}\mathbb{S}(n,t)1_{\bm{\beta}}
is a locally unital
\mathds{k}
-algebra which satisfies:

(1)

there is a subset of special idempotents
\left\{1_{\bm{\lambda}}\mid\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda\right\}
for a fixed subset
\Lambda\subseteq I
;

(2)

\Lambda
is upper finite with respect to
\preceq
(see
Lemma

7.2.1
);

(3)

there exist subsets
X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{t}\subseteq 1_{\bm{\lambda}}\mathbb{S}(n,t)1_{\bm{\beta}}
and
Y(\bm{\delta},\bm{\lambda})_{t}\subseteq 1_{\bm{\delta}}\mathbb{S}(n,t)1_{\bm{\lambda}}
for all
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda
and all
\bm{\delta}\in I
;

(4)

the products
yx
over all
(y,x)\in\bigcup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda}Y(\bm{\lambda})_{t}\times X(\bm{\lambda})_{t}
form a basis for
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
(see
Eq.

6.2.4
);

(5)

for
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in\Lambda
, the sets
X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})_{t}
and
Y(\bm{\mu},\bm{\lambda})_{t}
are empty unless
\bm{\mu}\preceq\bm{\lambda}
(see
Lemma

7.2.1
);

(6)

X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\lambda})_{t}=Y(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\lambda})_{t}=\{1_{\bm{\lambda}}\}
for each
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda
(see
Lemma

7.2.1
).

All that remains is to verify that
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is an upper finite
symmetrically
based quasi-hereditary algebra. However, this follows from observing that
\tau\left(1_{\bm{\beta}}\right)=1_{\bm{\beta}}
by
Corollary

4.5.7
and
\tau\left(X(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\beta})_{t}\right)=Y(\bm{\beta},\bm{\lambda})_{t}
for all
\bm{\beta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}
and
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
by definition.
∎

As remarked in
[
3
, Section 5]
, thinking of

as a
\mathds{k}
-linear category with this structure fits it into the theory of cellular categories
[
25
,
9
]
.

7.4.
Standard, Costandard, and Simple Modules for
\mathbb{S}(n,t)

The results of
[
3
]
can be used to give
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}_{\text{lfd}}
the structure of a highest weight category and can be used to give a construction of the simple
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules.

For
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
, let
\mathbb{S}(n,t)_{\npreceq\bm{\lambda}}
be the quotient of
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
by the two-sided ideal,
\mathcal{I}(\npreceq\bm{\lambda})
, generated by the set

\left\{1_{\bm{\mu}}\mid\bm{\mu}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t},\bm{\mu}\npreceq\bm{\lambda}\right\}.

Write
\bar{a}
for the coset
a+\mathcal{I}(\npreceq\bm{\lambda})
. Let
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
denote the
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-module
\mathbb{S}(n,t)_{\npreceq\bm{\lambda}}\bar{1}_{\bm{\lambda}}
and let
\nabla(\bm{\lambda})
denote the
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-module
(\bar{1}_{\bm{\lambda}}\mathbb{S}(n,t)_{\npreceq\bm{\lambda}})^{\circledast}
. Call
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
and
\nabla(\bm{\lambda})
the standard and costandard modules, respectively, labelled by
\bm{\lambda}
.

For each
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
,
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
has a unique proper maximal submodule
R(\bm{\lambda})
. Write

L(\bm{\lambda})=\Delta(\bm{\lambda})/R(\bm{\lambda})=\operatorname{head}(\Delta(\bm{\lambda}))

for the head of
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
. Write
\operatorname{socle}(N)
for the socle of an
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-module
N
.

The following is
[
3
, Theorem 5.9]
applied to our setting.

Theorem 7.4.1
.

The category
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}_{\text{lfd}}
is a highest weight category with standard and costandard modules
\left\{\Delta(\bm{\lambda})\right\}_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}}
and
\left\{\nabla(\bm{\lambda})\right\}_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}}
, respectively. The set

\left\{L(\bm{\lambda})=\operatorname{head}(\Delta(\bm{\lambda}))\cong\operatorname{socle}(\nabla(\bm{\lambda}))\mid\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}\right\}

is a complete, irredundant set of simple
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-modules.

7.5.
Semisimplicity

Theorem 7.5.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}
is a semisimple category if and only if
n=1
or
n\geq 2
and
t\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

If
n=1
, then
\mathbb{S}(1,t)=\mathds{k}
for all
t\in\mathds{k}
and semisimplicity is trivially true. Therefore, we assume
n\geq 2
for the remainder of the proof.

First, assume
t=d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and consider the surjective
\mathds{k}
-algebra map
f_{n,d}:\mathbb{S}(n,d)\to S(n,d)
. By inflation through this map the simple modules for
S(n,d)
define finite-dimensional simple modules for
\mathbb{S}(n,d)
.

However, for all
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
,
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
has a unique maximal proper submodule and therefore is indecomposable. As noted in the discussion just before
[
3
, Theorem 5.9]
,
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
has a basis indexed by
Y(\bm{\lambda})_{t}
. However,
Y(\bm{\lambda})_{t}
is easily seen to be an infinite set. For example, for all
k\geq 0
,
Y(\bm{\delta}^{k},\bm{\lambda})_{t}
is nonempty when
\bm{\delta}^{k}=(\lambda_{1}-k,\lambda_{2}+k,\lambda_{3},\dotsc,\lambda_{n})
. Namely, define
q=(q_{i,j})
by

\displaystyle q_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}-k,

\displaystyle q_{2,1}

\displaystyle=k,

\displaystyle q_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}\lambda_{i},\text{ otherwise.}

This is easily seen to be an element of
Y(\bm{\delta}^{k},\bm{\lambda})_{t}
.

If
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}
were a semisimple category when
t=d
, then
L(\bm{\lambda})=\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
for all
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
and all simple modules would be infinite-dimensional. But this contradicts the fact that finite-dimensional simple modules are known to exist.

Now assume
t\in\mathds{k}
is not a nonnegative integer. As discussed in
[
2
, Section 2.2]
, because
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is locally finite-dimensional, it will be semisimple if and only if the Jacobson radical,

J(\mathbb{S}(n,t)):=\bigcap_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}}\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathbb{S}(n,t)}\left(L(\bm{\lambda})\right),

is zero. Since

J(\mathbb{S}(n,t))=\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}}1_{\bm{\beta}}J(\mathbb{S}(n,t))1_{\bm{\delta}},

it will suffice to show each
1_{\bm{\beta}}J(\mathbb{S}(n,t))1_{\bm{\delta}}
is equal to zero.

Let
x\in 1_{\bm{\beta}}J(\mathbb{S}(n,t))1_{\bm{\delta}}
and write

x=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})_{t}}c_{q}\xi_{q}.

But then, for any fixed
r^{*}\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})_{t}
,

x\xi_{r^{*}}=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})_{t}}c_{q}\xi_{q}\xi_{r^{*}}

is an element of
1_{\bm{\beta}}J(\mathbb{S}(n,t))1_{\bm{\beta}}
.

Because
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is locally finite-dimensional over
\mathds{k}
, it is also algebraic over
\mathds{k}
. Therefore the Jacobson radical is a nil ideal (e.g., see
[
21
, Lemma 3.12(iv)]
or
[
20
, Corollary 4.19]
). However, if
x\xi_{r^{*}}
is nilpotent, then
[
16
, Corollary 14.8, Theorem 7.11]
imply that

\operatorname{tr}\left(x\xi_{r^{*}}\right)=0.

On the other hand, by
Lemma

5.1.1
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(x\xi_{r^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\left(\sum_{q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})_{t}}c_{q}\xi_{q}\xi_{r^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})_{t}}c_{q}\operatorname{tr}\left(\xi_{q}\xi_{r^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{r}t(t-1)(t-2)\dotsb(t-m+1)z,

for some
z\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
and nonnegative integer
m
. Since
t\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, this expression equaling zero implies
c_{r}=0
. Since this is true for all
r\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\beta},\bm{\delta})_{t}
,
x
must equal zero. Therefore,
J(\mathbb{S}(n,t))=0
and
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
is semisimple.
∎

8.
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
as a Quotient of a Modified Enveloping Algebra for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})

8.1.
The General Linear Lie Algebra

For
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and a characteristic zero field
\mathds{k}
, consider the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
with entries from
\mathds{k}
(where all but finitely many entries are assumed to be zero in the case
n=\infty
). Let
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{n}
denote the Cartan subalgebra consisting of diagonal matrices,
\mathfrak{b}^{+}=\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{+}
the Borel subalgebra consisting of upper triangular matrices, and
\mathfrak{b}^{-}=\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{-}
the opposite Borel subalgebra consisting of lower triangular matrices.

If we let
\varepsilon_{i}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathds{k}
denote the linear functional which selects the
i
th diagonal entry when given an element of
\mathfrak{h}
, then
X(n)
from
Section

2.3.1
is the integral weight lattice for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
; more generally,
X(n)_{t}
from
Section

2.4.1
is a
t
-shifted integral weight lattice for any
t\in\mathds{k}
.

For
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, let
\bm{\alpha}_{i,j}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
, and for
i\in[1,n-1]
let
\bm{\alpha}_{i}=\bm{\alpha}_{i,i+1}
. Observe that
\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i,j}
is an element of
X(n)_{t}
for all
\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t}
and all
\bm{\alpha}_{i,j}
. The set
\Phi=\{\bm{\alpha}_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}
is the set of roots for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
. The sets
\Phi^{\pm}=\{\pm\bm{\alpha}_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\}
and
\Delta=\{\bm{\alpha}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
are the choice of positive, negative, and simple roots corresponding to our choice of Borel subalgebra. The matrix unit
E_{i,j}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
is our fixed choice of root vector corresponding to
\bm{\alpha}_{i,j}\in\Phi
, and
e_{i}:=E_{i,i+1}
and
f_{i}:=E_{i+1,i}
are our fixed choice of simple root vectors.

8.2.
A Locally Unital Enveloping Algebra

Fix
t
in a commutative ring
R
. Using the notation of the previous section, we define a locally unital
R
-algebra as follows.

Definition 8.2.1
.

Let
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}(n,t)=\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(n,t)
be the locally unital associative
R
-algebra with generators

\{e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}},f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}\mid\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t},i\in[1,n-1]\}\cup\{1_{\bm{\lambda}}\mid\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t}\}

and, for all
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\in X(n)_{t}
and all

(U0)

;

(U1)

e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}1_{\bm{\mu}}=\delta_{\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}}e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}
,
1_{\bm{\mu}}e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}=\delta_{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i},\bm{\mu}}e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}
;

(U2)

f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}1_{\bm{\mu}}=\delta_{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i},\bm{\mu}}f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}
,
1_{\bm{\mu}}f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}=\delta_{\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}}f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}
;

(U3)

e^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{i}e^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{i}e^{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}-2e^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{i}e^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{j}e^{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}+e^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{j}e^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{i}e^{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}=0
, if
|i-j|=1
;

(U4)

f^{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{i}f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{j}-2f^{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{j}f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{i}+f^{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{i}f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{i}=0
, if
|i-j|=1
;

(U5)

e^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{i}e^{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}-e^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{j}e^{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}=0
, if
|i-j|\neq 1
;

(U6)

f^{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}_{j}-f^{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{i}=0
, if
|i-j|\neq 1
;

(U7)

e^{\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{i}f^{\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{j}-f^{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}-\bm{\alpha}_{j}}_{j}e^{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}=\delta_{i,j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})1_{\bm{\lambda}}
.

Note that the only defining relation influenced by the choice of
t
is (U7). Also note that
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(n,t)
admits an anti-involution,
\tau
, given on generators by
\tau(1_{\bm{\lambda}})=1_{\bm{\lambda}}
,
\tau(e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}})=f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}
, and
\tau(f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}})=e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}
.

Remark 8.2.2
.

The algebra
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(n,t)
is a locally unital version of the enveloping algebra of the general linear Lie algebra,
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
, with
t
-shifted integral weights. In particular,
e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}
and
f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}
are locally unital versions of the simple root vectors
e_{i}
and
f_{i}
, and the defining relations for
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(n,t)
are locally unital versions of the defining relations for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
. By definition, a
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(n,t)
-module is a module,
M
, for the associative
R
-algebra
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(n,t)
which also satisfies
M=\bigoplus_{\bm{\beta}\in X(n)_{t}}1_{\bm{\beta}}M
, where the direct sum is a direct sum of
R
-modules.

Remark 8.2.3
.

If
R=\mathds{k}
is a characteristic zero field, then the category of modules for the locally unital algebra
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)
coincides with the category of weight modules for the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
whose weights lie in
X(n)_{t}
.

We briefly sketch the correspondence. If
M=\bigoplus_{\bm{\gamma}\in X(n)_{t}}M_{\bm{\gamma}}
is a weight module for
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
, then define the action of
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)
on
M
by declaring for any
m\in M_{\bm{\gamma}}
that
1_{\bm{\lambda}}m=\delta_{\bm{\lambda},\bm{\gamma}}m
,
e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}m=\delta_{\bm{\lambda},\bm{\gamma}}e_{i}m
, and
f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}m=\delta_{\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\alpha}_{i},\bm{\gamma}}f_{i}m
. Conversely, given a
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)
-module,
M=\bigoplus_{\bm{\gamma}\in X(n)_{t}}1_{\bm{\gamma}}M
, declare
1_{\bm{\gamma}}M
to be the
\bm{\gamma}
-weight space for the Cartan subalgebra, and declare the action of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
to be given on an
m\in 1_{\bm{\gamma}}M
by
e_{i}m=e_{i}^{\bm{\gamma}}m
and
f_{i}m=f_{i}^{\bm{\gamma}-\bm{\alpha}_{i}}m
.

We will freely go between these two notions in what follows.

8.3.
Borel and Borel–Schur Subalgebras

Given
t\in R
, let
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathfrak{b}^{+},t)=\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}^{+},t)
denote the locally unital
R
-algebra generated by
\left\{1_{\bm{\lambda}}\mid\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t}\right\}
and
\left\{e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}\mid\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t},i\in[1,n-1]\right\}
subject to relations (U0), (U1), (U3), and (U5). Likewise, let
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathfrak{b}^{-},t)=\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}^{-},t)
denote the locally unital
R
-algebra generated by
\left\{1_{\bm{\lambda}}\mid\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t}\right\}
and
\left\{f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}\mid\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t},i\in[1,n-1]\right\}
subject to relations (U0), (U2), (U4), and (U6).

Because the relevant defining relations do not involve the parameter
t
, there is an isomorphism for every
t
,

\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm}):=\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm},0)\xrightarrow{\cong}\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm},t),

given by
1_{\bm{\lambda}}\mapsto 1_{\bm{\lambda}(t)}
,
e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}\mapsto e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}(t)}
, and
f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}\mapsto f_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}(t)}
.
If
R
contains
\mathbb{Q}
, then,

\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R\cong\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm}).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
t\in R
, let
\mathbb{S}^{\pm}_{R}(n,t)
be the subalgebra of
\mathbb{S}_{R}(n,t)
spanned by
\xi_{q}
for all
q\in\mathcal{M}at^{\pm}_{n}(t)
. Similarly, let
S^{\pm}_{R}(n,d)
denote the subalgebras of
S_{R}(n,d)
spanned by
\xi_{q}
for all
q\in\operatorname{Mat}^{\pm}(n,d)
. The fact that these are subalgebras follows from
Theorems

4.5.5
and
3.3.2
(see the proof of
Proposition

8.3.1
for a similar argument).

Proposition 8.3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
t\in R
. There are homomorphisms of locally unital algebras,

\displaystyle\iota_{n,t}^{U^{\pm}}:\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\pm})\to\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(n,t),

\displaystyle\iota_{n,d,t}^{S^{\pm}}:S^{\pm}_{R}(n,d)\to\mathbb{S}^{\pm}_{R}(n,t).

Proof.

We only do the
+
cases since the
-
cases are essentially identical. The map
\iota_{n,t}^{U^{+}}
is the obvious one given on generators by
1_{\bm{\lambda}}\mapsto 1_{\bm{\lambda}(t)}
and
e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}}\mapsto e_{i}^{\bm{\lambda}(t)}
for all
\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{0}
and
i\in[1,n-1]
. Since the defining relations of
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{\pm})
hold in
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(n,t)
, it is immediate that this determines a well-defined homomorphism.

The map
\iota_{n,d,t}^{S^{+}}
is given on basis elements by
\xi_{q}\mapsto\xi_{q(t)}
for
q\in\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d)
. This evidently defines an embedding of
R
-modules. It remains to verify the map is multiplicative.

First, we claim that for all
\bm{\delta},\bm{\gamma}\in\Lambda(n,d)
the map
q\mapsto q(t)
is a bijection between
\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\gamma})
and
\mathcal{M}at^{+}_{n}(\bm{\delta}(t),\bm{\gamma}(t))_{t}
. That this is an injective function between the stated sets is immediate. It remains to see that it is surjective. To do so, it suffices to verify
r(d)_{1,1}\geq 0
for all upper triangular
r\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta}(t),\bm{\gamma}(t))_{t}
. However,

r(d)_{1,1}=d-\sum_{(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}r(d)_{i,j}=d-\sum_{(i,j)\in[1,n]^{2}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}r_{i,j}=d-\sum_{j\in[2,n]}\delta_{j}=\delta_{1}\geq 0,

because
r
is upper triangular and
\bm{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{n})\in\Lambda(n,d)
.

We next claim that if
q,r\in\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d)
and
C_{q(t),r(t)}^{s}(t)\neq 0
for some
s\in\mathcal{M}at_{n}(\bm{\delta}(t),\bm{\beta}(t))_{t}
, then
s
is upper triangular. Say
A=(A_{i,j,k})\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q(t),r(t),s)
is an array used to calculate
C_{q(t),r(t)}^{s}(t)
, then
\sum_{k\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=q_{i,j}=0
whenever
i>j
and
\sum_{i\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=r_{j,k}=0
whenever
j>k
. This implies
A_{i,j,k}=0
whenever
i>j
or
j>k
. However, if
i>k
, then this implies the nonnegative integer
s_{i,k}
satisfies

s_{i,k}=\sum_{j\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}\leq\sum_{j\in[1,i-1]}A_{i,j,k}+\sum_{j\in[k+1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=0.

That is,
s
is upper triangular. The same argument shows that if
q
and
r
are upper triangular and
c_{q,r}^{s}\neq 0
for some
s\in\operatorname{Mat}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})
, then
s
is upper triangular.

Let
r\in\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d,\bm{\gamma},\bm{\beta})
and
q\in\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\gamma})
. Then,

(8.3.1)

\xi_{q(t)}\xi_{r(t)}=\sum_{\tilde{s}\in\mathcal{M}at^{+}_{n}(\bm{\delta}(t),\bm{\beta}(t))_{t}}C_{q(t),r(t)}^{\tilde{s}}(t)\xi_{\tilde{s}}=\sum_{s\in\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d,\bm{\delta},\bm{\beta})}C_{q(t),r(t)}^{s(t)}(t)\xi_{s(t)},

where the last equality follows from the preceding discussion.
The map
\iota^{S^{+}}_{n,d,t}
will be multiplicative as long as

C_{q(t),r(t)}^{s(t)}(t)=c_{q,r}^{s}

for all upper triangular
q,r,s\in\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d)
.

First, we claim that the function
A(n,q,r,s)\to\mathcal{A}_{n}(q(t),r(t),s(t))_{t}
given by
A\mapsto A(t)
is a bijection. Like before, the map is clearly injective. Surjectivity follows if we verify that
B(d)_{1,1,1}\geq 0
for all
B\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q(t),r(t),s(t))_{t}
. However, since
q(t)
is upper triangular,
B_{i,1,1}(d)=B_{i,1,1}=0
for
i\in[2,n]
. As a consequence,

B(d)_{1,1,1}=\sum_{i\in[1,n]}B_{i,1,1}(d)=r_{1,1}\geq 0,

where the last inequality holds because
r
was assumed to be an element of
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)
.

We next claim

V_{A}(t)=v_{A(d)}

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(q(t),r(t),s(t))_{t}
. In comparing the two, we see there is only one term in the combinatorial formulas for
V_{A}(t)
and
v_{A(d)}
that might differ due to the shift in the
(1,1,1)
-entry. However,
r(t)
is upper triangular and this implies

\sum_{i\in[1,n]}A_{i,j,k}=r(t)_{j,k}=0

whenever
j>k
. Thus,
A_{i,j,k}=A(t)_{i,j,k}=0
for
j>k
. In particular,
A_{1,j,1}=A(t)_{1,j,1}=0
for
j\in[2,n]
. Applying this observation to the combinatorial formulas for
V_{A}(t)
and
v_{A(d)}
shows that the one term where they could differ is actually equal to
1
in both of them. Hence the two formulas agree and
V_{A}(t)=v_{A(d)}
.

From this it follows that the structure constants
C_{q(t),r(t)}^{s(t)}
and
c_{q,r}^{s}
are equal, as desired.
∎

Remark 8.3.2
.

The family of maps
\left\{\iota_{n,d,t}^{S^{\pm}}\right\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
are asymptotically surjective in the sense that, given
y\in\mathbb{S}^{\pm}(n,t)
, for all sufficiently large

there exists
x\in S^{\pm}(n,d)
such that
\iota^{S^{\pm}}_{n,d,t}(x)=y
. This follows from the fact that any finite subset
\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)
is in the image of the map
\operatorname{Mat}(n,d)\to\mathcal{M}at_{n}(t)
given by
q\mapsto q(t)
, once
d
is sufficiently large.

8.4.

Since the

-module

has weights which lie in

as a

The restriction of these maps define surjective maps

in

.

If

contains
\mathbb{Q}

\pi_{n,d}:\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b}^{\pm})\to S_{R}^{\pm}(n,d).

For all

takes generators to generators, it also takes root vectors to root vectors, products of root vectors to products of root vectors and, hence these PBW elements to PBW basis elements in

, the family of maps
\{F_{n,d}^{\mathbb{Q}}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
is asymptotically injective. Therefore, showing
\mathtt{r}
equals zero is equivalent to showing that
F_{n,d}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathtt{r})=0
for all large
d
. It follows from the discussion in the first paragraph that

F_{n,d}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathtt{r})=\pi_{n,d}\left(\operatorname{ev}_{d}\left(e_{i}^{\bm{\gamma}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}e_{j}^{\bm{\gamma}}-e_{j}^{\bm{\gamma}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}e_{i}^{\bm{\gamma}}\right)\right)

as maps on
V_{n}^{\otimes d}
. However, this map is zero since
e_{i}^{\bm{\gamma}+\bm{\alpha}_{j}}e_{j}^{\bm{\gamma}}-e_{j}^{\bm{\gamma}+\bm{\alpha}_{i}}e_{i}^{\bm{\gamma}}
equals zero in
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)
by relation (U5). Therefore,
\mathtt{r}=0
in
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)
, as desired. The same argument applies equally well to the other defining relations.
∎

Theorem 8.6.2
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Q}[T]
and
t=T
. The map of
\mathbb{Q}[T]
-algebras

u_{n}:\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)\to\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)

is surjective.

Proof.

Let
q\in\mathcal{M}at^{+}_{n}(T)
. As stated in
Remark

8.3.2
,
\iota^{S^{+}}_{n,d,T}:S^{+}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,d)\to\mathbb{S}^{+}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)
is asymptotically surjective. That is, we may choose
d
large enough to ensure that
q(d)
is an element of
\operatorname{Mat}^{+}(n,d)
and also that
\iota^{S^{+}}_{n,d,T}(\xi_{q(d)})=\xi_{q}
. The map
\pi_{n,d}:\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(\mathfrak{b}^{+})\to S^{+}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,d)
given in
Eq.

8.4.1
is surjective. Therefore, we can choose
X_{q(d)}\in\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(\mathfrak{b}^{+})
such that
\pi_{n,d}(X_{q(d)})=\xi_{q(d)}
and, hence,

u_{n}\left(\iota^{U^{+}}_{n,T}\left(X_{q(d)}\right)\right)=\iota^{S^{+}}_{n,d,T}\left(\pi_{n,d}\left(X_{q(d)}\right)\right)=\xi_{q}.

Therefore
\mathbb{S}^{+}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)
lies in the image of
u_{n}
. A similar argument shows
\mathbb{S}^{-}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)
lies in the image of
u_{n}
. Finally, since the codeterminant basis
\mathcal{CB}(n)_{T}
is a subset of
\mathbb{S}^{-}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)\mathbb{S}^{+}_{\mathbb{Q}[T]}(n,T)
, we conclude that
u_{n}
is surjective.
∎

Notice that the map
u_{n}
satisfies
u_{n}\circ\tau=\tau\circ u_{n}
. For a characteristic zero field
\mathds{k}
and
t\in\mathds{k}
, the previous result, along with base change along the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}_{d}:\mathbb{Q}[T]\to\mathds{k}
at
T=t
, yields the following.

Corollary 8.6.3
.

There is a surjective map of locally unital
\mathds{k}
-algebras

(8.6.1)

\psi=\psi_{n,t}:\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and
t\in\mathds{k}
.

Remark 8.6.4
.

Like in
[
8
]
, the above result should provide a presentation for
\mathbb{S}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)
by generators and relations. We expect the kernel of the map given in
Corollary

8.6.3
will be the two-sided ideal generated by the set
\left\{1_{\bm{\lambda}}\mid\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t}\backslash\Lambda(n)_{t}\right\}
. See
[
22
]
for related results. Since we do not need this description, we leave it as a question.

8.7.
Parabolic Category
\mathcal{O}

Throughout this section
\mathds{k}
is a characteristic zero field and
t\in\mathds{k}
. Let
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}=\left\{\bm{\alpha}_{i,j}\mid 2\leq i\neq j\leq n\right\}
and let
\mathfrak{l}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{1}(\mathds{k})\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}(\mathds{k})
be the Levi subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
corresponding to the subroot system
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}
be the parabolic subalgebra corresponding to
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}}=\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}\cup\Phi^{\pm}
. Likewise, let
\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}
be the nilpotent subalgebra corresponding to
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}}\backslash\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}}
.

Corresponding to these choices, parabolic category
\mathcal{O}^{\mathfrak{p}}
is defined to be the full subcategory of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-modules
M
that satisfy the following conditions:

(P1)

M
is finitely generated as a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-module;

(P2)

M
is finitely semisimple when restricted to
U(\mathfrak{l})
(i.e.,
M
is isomorphic to a direct sum of finite-dimensional simple
U(\mathfrak{l})
-modules);

(P3)

M
is locally
U(\mathfrak{u}^{+})
-finite (i.e., every element of
M
generates a finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{u}^{+})
-module).

For a fixed
t\in\mathds{k}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{t}
be the full subcategory of finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-modules which have a weight space decomposition with weights lying in
\Lambda(n)_{t}
.

Lemma 8.7.1
.

The category
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}^{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

Condition (P1) is obviously satisfied.

Let
N
be a module in
\mathcal{P}_{t}
. If
N
is restricted to the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{l}
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}(\mathds{k})
, then the weights of
N
lie in
\Lambda(n-1)
. For
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let

N_{p}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\mu}\in\Lambda(n)_{t}\\
\sum_{i\in[2,n]}\mu_{i}=p\end{subarray}}N_{\bm{\mu}}.

The presentation given in
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
demonstrates that
N_{p}
is a
S(n-1,p)
-module and, hence, is a direct sum of finite-dimensional simple
S(n-1,p)
-modules. Therefore, via the canonical surjective map
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}(\mathds{k}))\to S(n-1,p)
,
N_{p}
is a direct sum of finite-dimensional simple
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}(\mathds{k}))
-modules. However, since
N_{p}
is spanned by weight vectors, it follows that
N_{p}
is a finitely semisimple
U(\mathfrak{l})
-module. Therefore

N=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}N_{p}

is a finitely semisimple
U(\mathfrak{l})
-module. Therefore, condition (P2) holds.

Finally, to show
N
satisfies condition (P3) it suffices to show that each weight vector
x\in N
generates a finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{u}^{+})
-module. Since all weights of
N
lie in
\Lambda(n)_{t}
, weight considerations show that only finitely many PBW basis vectors in
U(\mathfrak{u}^{+})
can be applied to a weight vector of
N
and still be nonzero. Therefore,
U(\mathfrak{u}^{+})x
is finite-dimensional, as desired.
∎

Via the surjective map
\psi
given in
Eq.

8.6.1
and the identification of
\dot{\mathbf{\mathcal{U}}}_{\mathds{k}}(n,t)
-modules and
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))
-modules with a weight space decomposition as described in
Remark

8.2.3
, there is a fully faithful inflation functor

\Psi=\Psi_{n,t}:\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}\to U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))\text{-mod}.

Lemma 8.7.2
.

The functor
\Psi
restricts to define a fully faithful functor

\Psi:\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}_{\text{fg}}\to\mathcal{P}_{t}.

Proof.

It is immediate that the inflation of any finitely generated
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
-module is finitely generated, has a weight space decomposition, and the weights lie in
\Lambda(n)_{t}
. Therefore, the image of
\Psi
lies in
\mathcal{P}_{t}
. The fact that
\Psi
is fully faithful follows from the fact that
\psi
is surjective.
∎

Remark 8.7.3
.

If the presentation of
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
given in
Remark

8.6.4
is valid, then the category
\mathbb{S}(n,t)\text{-mod}_{\text{fg}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{P}_{t}
via the functor
\Psi
.

We fix
\mathfrak{h}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{0}=\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{l}
as our choice of Cartan and Borel subalgebras of
\mathfrak{l}
, respectively. Given
\bm{\lambda}\in X(n)_{t}
, let
L_{\mathfrak{l}}(\bm{\lambda})
be the simple
\mathfrak{l}
-module of highest weight
\bm{\lambda}
with respect to these subalgebras of
\mathfrak{l}
. Let

V(\bm{\lambda})=U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k}))\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p}^{+})}L_{\mathfrak{l}}(\bm{\lambda})

be the generalized (or parabolic) Verma module for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
with highest weight
\bm{\lambda}
. Let
\widehat{L}(\bm{\lambda})
be the simple head of
V(\bm{\lambda})
.

Proposition 8.7.4
.

Let
t\in\mathds{k}
and let
\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
, then

\Psi_{n,t}(\Delta(\bm{\lambda}))\cong V(\bm{\lambda}).

Proof.

By construction,

\Delta(\bm{\lambda})=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\mu}\in X(n)_{t}\\
\bm{\lambda}\not\preceq\bm{\mu}\end{subarray}}\Delta(\bm{\lambda})_{\bm{\mu}}.

The condition
\bm{\lambda}\not\preceq\bm{\mu}
is precisely the requirement that
\bm{\mu}
is not greater than
\bm{\lambda}
in the dominance order on weights of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{k})
. Thus, by construction,
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
is generated by
\bar{1}_{\bm{\lambda}}
, a highest weight vector of weight
\bm{\lambda}
which generates an
\mathfrak{l}
-composition factor isomorphic to
L_{\mathfrak{l}}(\bm{\lambda})
. By the universal property of
V(\bm{\lambda})
, there is a surjective homomorphism

(8.7.1)

V(\bm{\lambda})\twoheadrightarrow\Delta(\bm{\lambda}).

It is well known (e.g., by
[
18
, Theorem 9.12]
) that
V(\bm{\lambda})
is simple whenever
t
is not an integer and, hence, the map is an isomorphism in this case. In particular,

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathds{k}}\left(V(\bm{\lambda})_{\bm{\mu}}\right)=\operatorname{dim}_{\mathds{k}}\left(\Delta(\bm{\lambda})_{\bm{\mu}}\right)

for all
\bm{\mu}\in X(n)_{t}
. If we show these dimensions are always finite and are independent of
t
, then the map given in
Eq.

8.7.1
is an isomorphism for all
t
. However,
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathds{k}}\left(V(\bm{\lambda})_{\bm{\mu}}\right)
can be computed using the parabolic version of Kostant’s partition function. On the other hand,
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathds{k}}\left(\Delta(\bm{\lambda})_{\bm{\mu}}\right)=|Y(\bm{\mu},\bm{\lambda})_{t}|
. Therefore, both dimensions are finite numbers that can be computed combinatorially without reference to
t
.
∎

The functor
\Psi
sends the head of
\Delta(\bm{\lambda})
to the head of
V(\bm{\lambda})
. With this in mind, the following result is immediate.

Corollary 8.7.5
.

For all
\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}
in
\Lambda^{+}(n)_{t}
,
\Psi_{n,t}\left(L(\bm{\lambda})\right)=\widehat{L}(\bm{\lambda})
and the composition multiplicities for
\mathbb{S}(n,t)
satisfy:

\left[\Delta(\bm{\lambda}):L(\bm{\mu})\right]=\left[V(\bm{\lambda}):\widehat{L}(\bm{\mu})\right].
