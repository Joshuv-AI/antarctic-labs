Project Overview
Despite flexible, continuous representations with high-fidelity reconstruction of Implicit Neural
Representations (INRs) and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), these approaches require substantial
computational resources and long training times for challenging computed tomography (CT)
reconstructions such as dynamic 4DCT. This project explored recently introduced Gaussian
Splatting (GS) as a computationally efficient alternative that can achieve reasonable
reconstruction fidelity with reduced computational cost for 4DCT. Our method extends 3D
Gaussian Splatting by incorporating a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to model the time-dependent
motion and deformation of 3D Gaussians in 4D time-space domain. During optimization, our 4D
Gaussian representation is adaptively refined through splitting, merging, and pruning based on
Gaussian size, density, and motion, as illustrated in Figure 1. We also extended the camera
models to support both parallel-beam and cone-beam CT geometries. We demonstrated the
feasibility of our proposed approach using the LLNL MPM-based 4DCT dataset representing
object deformation over time, as shown in Figure 2. Our method is computationally more
efficient, requiring less than several hours on a single GPU, compared with INR approaches which
requires 24+ hours across dozens of GPUs.

Mission Impact
The outcome will provide a foundation for GS-driven reconstruction across a broad range of
challenging computational imaging and inverse problems where conventional methods are
infeasible, and INR approaches are less practical due to the computing resource requirements.
This approach has the potential to advance computational imaging and non-destructive
evaluation capabilities across core mission areas at LLNL, including advanced manufacturing and
materials characterization, automatic threat recognition, and high energy density science.
