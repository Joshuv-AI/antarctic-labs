Vacancy-Enhanced N – N Bonding and Deep Level Complex Defect Formation in \upbeta - Ga 2 ​ O 3 \text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
Asiyeh Shokri, Yevgen Melikhov, Yevgen Syryanyy, Maryna Chernyshova, Iraida N. Demchenko

Abstract
The formation and electronic properties of nitrogen-related defect complexes in \upbeta - Ga 2 ​ O 3 \text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} are investigated using first-principles calculations. Starting from the energetically favorable N i 9 ​ – N OI \text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}} configuration, nitrogen atoms exhibit a strong tendency toward co-localization, leading to reduced N – N separation. However, analysis of bond lengths and electron localization function shows that these configurations do not fully attain molecular N 2 \text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} character. The role of intrinsic defects is further examined by introducing oxygen and gallium vacancies. Vacancy-assisted configurations enhance local lattice relaxation and further decrease the N – N distance. Formation energy calculations indicate that several vacancy-assisted complexes are thermodynamically favorable, while binding energy analysis confirms their stability against dissociation. Despite this, the density of states analysis reveals that all configurations introduce localized electronic states within the band gap. These states originate primarily from hybridized N- 2 ​ p 2p and O- 2 ​ p 2p orbitals and remain energetically separated from the band edges. Spin density analysis further confirms strong localization. Overall, these defect complexes act as deep trapping centers, limiting carrier transport in \upbeta - Ga 2 ​ O 3 \text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and thereby promoting semi-insulating behavior and current blocking characteristics.

1
Introduction

Nitrogen incorporation in
\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
has
been extensively investigated as a strategy to tailor the electronic
properties of this ultra-wide band gap (UWBG) oxide semiconductor

[
14
,
4
,
15
]
. In particular, nitrogen has been considered as a potential
acceptor dopant when substituting for oxygen lattice sites
[
25
]
.
However, both theoretical and experimental studies consistently show
that nitrogen-related defect levels are typically deep within the band
gap, which limits their effectiveness for
p
-type doping and instead
promotes carrier compensation
[
17
,
23
]
.

Beyond isolated substitutional defects, nitrogen incorporation under
non-equilibrium conditions, such as ion implantation, significantly
alters the electrical behavior of

\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

[
2
]
. Specifically, Nitrogen
implantation has been reported to increase resistivity and enable
semi-insulating behavior, which is beneficial for current-blocking
layers in high-voltage devices
[
24
,
13
]
. While these observations
indicate that nitrogen-related defects act as carrier traps, the
microscopic origin of this behavior remains not fully understood.

From a defect physics perspective, the atomic configuration of nitrogen
in
\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is expected to depend
strongly on the incorporation pathway. While near-equilibrium growth
favors isolated defects
[
19
]
, ion implantation introduces a high
density of intrinsic point defects, such as vacancies and interstitials,
which can stabilize complex configurations
[
3
]
. In such
environments, interactions between multiple nitrogen atoms and native
defects become increasingly relevant and can significantly modify both
structural and electronic properties
[
4
,
2
]
. In this context,
nitrogen co-localization and the formation of
N
–
N
configurations have
attracted growing attention
[
1
]
. Previous studies suggest that,
analogous to ZnO
[
20
,
22
]
, nitrogen in

\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
may form molecular
configurations or small
N
–
N
complexes
[
2
]
.

A particularly significant configuration is the

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}
complex. Under Ga-rich
conditions, this configuration has been identified as the most
energetically favorable
[
21
]
. However, analysis of the electron
localization function (ELF) indicated that this structure does not
correspond to a true
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecule, but rather to a
partially bonded configuration involving nitrogen and neighboring Ga
atoms. Specifically, while the bond length in configurations like

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}
(
\approx 1.27\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}
) is close
to molecular
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
\approx 1.10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}
), the electronic
density shows significant interaction between one N atom and the gallium
sub-lattice
[
21
]
, preventing the formation of a fully isolated,
"true" molecular state.

This suggests that additional mechanisms, such as the involvement of
intrinsic vacancies, may be required to further stabilize stronger
N
–
N

interactions or modify the carrier trapping behavior of these complexes.
It should be noted that the ubiquitous presence of background hydrogen
in oxide semiconductors can also significantly influence defect
chemistry, often passivating deep levels or participating in complex
formation
[
16
]
. While the potential interplay between hydrogen and
nitrogen-related effects cannot be definitively ruled out, the scope of
this investigation is deliberately restricted to the fundamental
interactions between nitrogen and native intrinsic defects.

Motivated by these considerations, this work investigates
nitrogen-related defect complexes derived from the

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}
configuration, with a particular
emphasis on vacancy-assisted structures, including oxygen and gallium
vacancies. Thermodynamic stability is assessed through formation and
binding energies, while electronic properties are examined through
density of states and spin-density analyses. The tendency for nitrogen
co-localization is further evaluated by comparing configurations with
varying
N
–
N
separation.

Our results demonstrate that while vacancies significantly stabilize the

N
–
N
interactions, they introduce localized electronic states deep within
the band gap. These states, primarily derived from N-
2p
and O-
2p

orbitals, act as deep trapping centers and lead to carrier localization.
Consequently, nitrogen-related defect complexes in

\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
are more relevant for
semi-insulating and charge-blocking applications than for achieving
shallow acceptor
p
-type conductivity.

Materials and Methods

All calculations were performed using the projector augmented wave (PAW)
method, as implemented in the VASP software package
[
11
,
9
,
10
,
8
]
. The
plane-wave cutoff energy was set to
510\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
. Exchange-correlation effects
were treated within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using
the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional
[
18
]
. To obtain an
accurate description of band gap, the Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof (HSE06)
hybrid functional was utilized with a fixed screening parameter of

0.2\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}
and a Hartree-Fock exchange fraction of
35\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
,
yielding band gap values in good agreement with experimental data

[
6
]
.

A
1\text{\times}4\text{\times}2\text{\,}
supercell containing 160 atoms, with fixed lattice parameters

a=$12.22\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
,
b=$12.112\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
, and
c=$11.58\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
, was used to
model the
\beta
phase of
\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. Brillouin
zone integrations were performed using the
\Gamma
-centered Monkhorst-Pack
k-point mesh of
2\text{\times}8\text{\times}4\text{\,}
for the conventional unit cell and
1\text{\times}1\text{\times}1\text{\,}
for the

1\text{\times}4\text{\times}2\text{\,}
supercell calculations. Gaussian smearing with a width of
0.05\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}

was applied. Structural relaxations were performed at fixed volume until
the residual forces on each atom were below

0.03\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}
, and the electronic self-consistency
threshold was set to
1\text{\times}{10}^{-4}\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
. Spin polarization was
included in all calculations. Note that this supercell size was chosen
to minimize finite-size effects in the calculation of defect formation
energies.

The formation energy of a defect
X
in charge state is given by
[
5
]

E^{f}\left(X_{q}\right)=\ E_{tot}\left(X_{q}\right)-E_{tot}(bulk)-\sum_{i}{n_{i}\mu_{i}}+q\ \left(E_{F}+E_{V}\right)+\ E_{c},

(1)

where
E_{tot}(X_{q})
and

E_{tot}(bulk)
are the total energies of the
defective and pristine supercells, respectively. The term

n_{i}
represents the number of atoms of species

i
added (
n_{i}>0
) or removed
(
n_{i}<0
), and
\mu_{i}

is the corresponding chemical potential.
The Fermi level
E_{F}
is referenced to the valence band maximum
E_{V}
.
The correction term
E_{c}
accounts for finite-size effects associated with charged defects under periodic boundary conditions
[
5
]
.

The chemical potentials are constrained by the thermodynamic stability
condition of
\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
:

2\mu_{\text{Ga}}+3\mu_{\text{O}}=\Delta H_{f}(\text{Ga}_{2}\text{O}_{3}),

(2)

and both Ga-rich (O-poor) and O-rich (Ga-poor) conditions are considered.
Under Ga-rich conditions,
\mu_{Ga}
is referenced to bulk Ga, while under O-rich conditions,
\mu_{O}
is referenced to half the total energy of an

\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecule.
The allowed range of chemical potentials is chosen to avoid the formation of competing phases.

For nitrogen incorporation, the nitrogen chemical potential is
referenced to molecular
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. To prevent the formation of
secondary phases such as GaN, additional thermodynamic constraints are
applied to ensure phase stability.

The thermodynamic charge transition level between charge states

q_{1}
and
q_{2}
is defined as

[
12
]
:

\varepsilon(q_{1}/q_{2})=\frac{E_{f}(X_{q_{1}})-E_{f}(X_{q_{2}})}{q_{2}-q_{1}},

(3)

and this quantity corresponds to the Fermi level at which the defect
changes its stable charge state.

Charged defects were modeled by adding or removing electrons from the
supercell, with a compensating uniform background charge to maintain
overall neutrality. Finite-size corrections were applied to account for
spurious electrostatic interactions between periodic images. Formation
energies were evaluated for multiple charge states, and the most stable
configuration at a given Fermi level was determined by the lowest
formation energy.

3
Results

3.1
Energetic preference of nitrogen co-localization and emergence of
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-like bonding

In a previous study, the
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}

configuration was identified as the energetically most favorable
nitrogen-related defect complex in

\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
under equilibrium conditions

[
21
]
. Motivated by this result, it is examined whether interstitial

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}
and substitutional nitrogen interaction can lead to
the formation of an
N
–
N
bond with
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-like character.

To address this question, multiple configurations were constructed by
placing substitutional nitrogen at different oxygen sites surrounding
the
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}
interstitial defect. In this way, systematic
variations in
N
–
N
separation and total energy were obtained. All
structures were fully relaxed to determine their equilibrium geometries
and total energies. A clear correlation is observed between the total
energy and the
N
–
N
distance (Figure
1
). Configurations with shorter
N
–
N

separations are consistently found to be lower in energy, indicating a
strong energetic preference for nitrogen co-localization near the

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}
site. Among all configurations,

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}
exhibits both the lowest total
energy and the shortest
N
–
N
distance and is therefore identified as the
ground-state configuration.

3.2
Electronic properties of defect configurations

The formation energies of the selected defect configurations were
calculated for charge states ranging from -2 to +2 (Figure
3
).
Among the considered models, the
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{GaI}}}
configuration is found to exhibit the low formation energy under Ga-poor conditions over a wide range of Fermi levels, which is comparable to that of an isolated N atom substituted at O site (
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{O}}}
).
Besides, in Ga-rich conditions model,

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{GaII}}}
and

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{2}\,\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{Ga}}}
have higher
energy compared to

energy gain associated with the formation of a defect complex from its
isolated constituents and is defined as the difference between the sum
of the formation energies of the individual defects and that of the

(4)

where

lower or negative value suggests reduced stability

.

dissociation into isolated defects. In particular, the complexes can

lattice.

Conclusions

First-principles calculations were performed to investigate
nitrogen-related defect complexes in

\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, focusing on the interaction
between interstitial
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}
and substitutional nitrogen
(
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}
), as well as the role of intrinsic vacancies. The
results show that nitrogen atoms exhibit a strong tendency toward
co-localization, leading to the formation of energetically favorable

\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{i\/9}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{OI}}}
complexes. Vacancy-assisted
configurations further enhance this behavior by reducing local
structural constraints and significantly decreasing the
N
–
N
separation.
In particular, Ga-vacancy-containing models promote stronger
N
–
N

interaction, in some cases approaching bond lengths comparable to
molecular nitrogen.

Formation energy and binding energy analyses indicate that the
considered defect complexes are thermodynamically stable with respect to
isolated defects, especially in vacancy-assisted configurations where
lattice relaxation is enhanced. However, electronic structure
calculations reveal that all configurations introduce deep defect states
within the band gap. These states are primarily derived from N-
2p
and
O-
2p
orbitals and remain strongly localized, preventing the formation of
shallow acceptor levels. Although the Fermi level shifts toward the
valence band maximum in Ga-vacancy-rich structures, the persistence of
localized gap states limits their effectiveness for
p
-type conductivity.

Spin density and electron localization function analyses further confirm
strong localization of charge around nitrogen and neighboring oxygen
atoms, with vacancy incorporation modifying but not eliminating defect
localization. Overall, although nitrogen incorporation and vacancy
engineering promote structural and energetic stabilization of N-related
complexes, the resulting electronic states remain deep and localized.
Consequently, these defects act as carrier trapping centers, suppressing
transport and favoring charge blocking behavior rather than enabling
shallow acceptor-mediated conductivity in

\text{$\upbeta$}\text{-}\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
.
