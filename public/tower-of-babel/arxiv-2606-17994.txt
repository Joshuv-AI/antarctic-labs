Constraints on the Sum of Neutrino Masses from ACT DR6 and DESI DR2 Considering Isocurvature Initial Conditions
Hongsheng Hou, Sai Wang, Zhi-Chao Zhao, Xin Zhang

Abstract
We present a robust assessment of cosmological constraints on the sum of neutrino masses ( ∑ m ν \sum m_{\nu} ) when relaxing the standard assumption of purely adiabatic primordial initial conditions. Allowing for a neutrino density isocurvature (NDI) component alongside the adiabatic mode, we analyse the latest CMB-SPA combination (Planck 2018, ACT DR6, and SPT-3G), DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillation data, and the DES Year 5 supernova sample. Within the Λ \Lambda CDM model, the 95% upper limit weakens only marginally from ∑ m ν < 0.052 \sum m_{\nu}<0.052 eV (purely adiabatic) to < 0.057 <0.057 eV (including NDI), with the NDI amplitude consistent with zero. In the CPL dynamical dark energy model, the adiabatic limit is < 0.111 <0.111 eV, shifting to < 0.115 <0.115 eV with NDI, yet the isocurvature mode remains undetected. While these limits are robust against the inclusion of isocurvature perturbations, they are highly sensitive to both the assumed dark energy equation of state and the prior lower bound on ∑ m ν \sum m_{\nu} . Notably, the adiabatic Λ \Lambda CDM limit of 0.052 0.052 eV lies below the minimum sum required by the normal neutrino mass hierarchy ( 0.05878 0.05878 eV), indicating that this bound is an artifact of the statistical prior extending to zero. Imposing a physically motivated hierarchy-informed prior raises the limit to < 0.092 <0.092 eV. Our results demonstrate that current data show no evidence for NDI modes and that the inferred neutrino mass upper limit is robust against this extension, but a definitive, model-independent bound requires addressing prior dependencies and dark energy uncertainties. This work provides the first joint constraint on ∑ m ν \sum m_{\nu} and NDI using the full CMB-SPA+DESI DR2+DES dataset.

In this paper, we take full advantage of these state-of-the-art data sets to systematically investigate the robustness of cosmological upper limits on
\sum m_{\nu}
against the assumption of initial conditions. We analyze the high-resolution cosmic microwave background measurements from Atacama Cosmology Telescope Data Release 6 and South Pole Telescope third-generation, the Planck 2018 cosmic microwave background data, the baryon acoustic oscillation measurements from Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Data Release 2, and the Type Ia supernova sample from the Dark Energy Survey Year 5

Our findings will demonstrate the degree to which current cosmological constraints on neutrino masses are model-dependent, or conversely, whether they exhibit strong robustness against extensions to the initial condition sector. This work provides crucial guidance for interpreting cosmological neutrino mass limits in the context of particle physics and for planning future surveys aimed at uncovering the absolute neutrino mass scale and its hierarchy.

II
Theory

Massive neutrinos affect cosmological observables primarily through their free-streaming behavior. In the standard
\Lambda
CDM model with adiabatic initial conditions, neutrinos with mass sum
\sum m_{\nu}
suppress the growth of matter density perturbations on scales smaller than their free-streaming length, reducing the matter power spectrum and damping the small-scale cosmic microwave background anisotropies, particularly at high multipoles
\ell
where the Silk damping tail is modified
Lesgourgues and Pastor 2006
;
Wong 2011
. See left panels of Figure
1
. This suppression becomes more pronounced as
\sum m_{\nu}
increases
Lesgourgues and Pastor 2006
. However, the effect is degenerate with other parameters such as the matter density
\Omega_{m}
and the Hubble constant
H_{0}

Aghanim et al. 2020
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025a
. These degeneracies can be broken by combining cosmic microwave background data with baryon acoustic oscillation measurements, which provide a precise standard ruler at low redshifts
Aghanim et al. 2020
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025a
. baryon acoustic oscillation observables, specifically the angular diameter distance
D_{A}(z)
and the Hubble expansion rate
H(z)
, are sensitive to the overall matter density and the expansion history, thereby helping to isolate the signature of neutrino mass
Abdul Karim et al. 2025a
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025b
. See left panels of Figure
2
. Hence, joint analyses of cosmic microwave background and baryon acoustic oscillation data yield tight upper limits on
\sum m_{\nu}
under the assumption of purely adiabatic initial conditions
Aghanim et al. 2020
;
Louis et al. 2025
;
Camphuis et al. 2026
.

In addition to the adiabatic mode, primordial perturbations may contain an neutrino density isocurvature component, where the photon-to-neutrino density ratio varies spatially while the total density perturbation vanishes initially
Bucher et al. 2002
. The neutrino density isocurvature mode modifies the cosmic microwave background power spectrum in ways distinct from the effect of neutrino mass. Unlike the adiabatic mode, which produces a pronounced Sachs–Wolfe plateau at low multipoles, the neutrino density isocurvature contribution rises from
\ell\sim 10
, peaks around
\ell\sim 100
, and then declines, thereby suppressing the large-scale cosmic microwave background anisotropy and altering the relative heights of the acoustic peaks
Bucher et al. 2002
;
Buckley et al. 2025
. See right panels of Figure
1
. In the matter power spectrum, the neutrino density isocurvature component causes a scale-dependent suppression on intermediate wavenumbers (
k\sim 0.01
–
0.1\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}
) because the gravitational potential decays more rapidly after horizon entry in the absence of an initial curvature perturbation
Buckley et al. 2025
. See right panels of Figure
2
. For baryon acoustic oscillation observables, the neutrino density isocurvature component shifts the sound horizon at recombination
r_{s}
and modifies the late-time distance-redshift relation
D_{A}(z)
, leading to a different redshift dependence of the baryon acoustic oscillation angular scale
\theta_{\rm BAO}(z)=r_{s}/D_{A}(z)
compared to the pure adiabatic case
Buckley et al. 2025
. In this work, we consider a mixed scenario where an neutrino density isocurvature component is present alongside the dominant adiabatic mode, and we investigate how the inclusion of neutrino density isocurvature affects the derived upper limit on
\sum m_{\nu}
from cosmic microwave background and baryon acoustic oscillation data.

To quantitatively model the primordial perturbations, we adopt a general parameterization that includes both the adiabatic mode and the neutrino density isocurvature mode, following the formalism of Ref.
Bucher et al. 2002
. Instead of assuming a power-law form for the power spectra, we treat the amplitudes at two distinct pivot scales as independent free parameters. Specifically, we choose
k_{1}=0.002\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}
and
k_{2}=0.1\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}
, following Ref.
Akrami et al. 2020
. These two scales are selected to capture the complementary sensitivity of different cosmological probes:
k_{1}
lies in the regime of the cosmic microwave background Sachs–Wolfe plateau and the first acoustic peaks (
\ell\sim 10
–
100
), where neutrino density isocurvature modes leave their most prominent signature;
k_{2}
corresponds to smaller scales relevant for the cosmic microwave background damping tail (
\ell\gtrsim 500
) and the matter power spectrum wavenumbers probed by baryon acoustic oscillation and weak lensing (
k\sim 0.1\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}
), where the effects of neutrino free-streaming become significant. For each wavenumber
k
, the primordial power spectrum is described by a real symmetric matrix

\mathcal{P}_{AB}(k)
in the
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{I})
basis, where

A,B\in\{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{I}\}
and the independent components are

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{I}}
, and

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}

Akrami et al. 2020
.
where the diagonal entries
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}(k)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}(k)
are interpolated between the two pivot scales using a piecewise constant or linear interpolation in
\ln k
, and the off-diagonal term
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{I}}(k)
is parameterized by a correlation angle
\Delta(k)
such that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{I}}(k)=\sqrt{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}(k)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}(k)}\cos\Delta(k)
. The relative contribution of the NDI mode is conveniently expressed by the isocurvature fraction
\beta(k)={\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}(k)}/(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}(k)+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}(k)),

which may in general be scale-dependent. In this work, we consider the simple case of uncorrelated modes, i.e.,
\cos\Delta(k)=0
for all
k
, which is a common assumption in the literature and is realized in many inflationary models with orthogonal field directions
Langlois 1999
;
Gordon et al. 2000
. The free parameters for the primordial power spectra are therefore the adiabatic amplitudes
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}^{(1)}\equiv\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}(k_{1})
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}^{(2)}\equiv\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}(k_{2})
, and the neutrino density isocurvature amplitudes
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}\equiv\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}(k_{1})
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}^{(2)}\equiv\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}(k_{2})
.

The choice of piecewise interpolation (constant or linear) between the two pivots is a pragmatic simplification. We have not performed an explicit comparison of different interpolation schemes (e.g., log-linear vs. cubic spline) or tested the sensitivity of our results to the exact location of the pivot scales. A more flexible treatment, such as allowing the spectral indices of the neutrino density isocurvature and adiabatic modes as free parameters, would be desirable in future work but is beyond the scope of this paper. Nevertheless, given that the posterior constraints on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}^{(2)}
are both consistent with zero, our main conclusion about the robustness of
\sum m_{\nu}
limits against neutrino density isocurvature is unlikely to be significantly affected by these modeling choices.

The remaining cosmological parameters are the same as in the standard
\Lambda
CDM model: the physical baryon density
\omega_{b}=\Omega_{b}h^{2}
, the physical cold dark matter density
\omega_{c}=\Omega_{c}h^{2}
, the angular size of the sound horizon at recombination
\Theta_{s}
, the optical depth to reionization
\tau
, and the sum of neutrino masses
\sum m_{\nu}
. In total, our parameter space consists of
\{\omega_{b},\omega_{c},\Theta_{s},\tau,\sum m_{\nu}\}
plus the four amplitude parameters
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}^{(2)}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}^{(2)}
. To study the robustness of neutrino mass constraints against the initial condition assumption, we compare two scenarios: (i) a pure adiabatic model where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}^{(1)}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}^{(2)}=0
; and (ii) a mixed model where these neutrino density isocurvature amplitudes are allowed to vary freely. In the mixed model, we adopt uniform priors on
\log\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}}
and
\log\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}}
. All other parameters are assigned the same priors in both models to enable a fair assessment of how the inclusion of neutrino density isocurvature modes affects the upper limit on
\sum m_{\nu}
.

Recent cosmological observations, particularly from Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument and Dark Energy Survey, have provided hints that dark energy may not be a strict cosmological constant but rather a dynamical component
Lodha et al. 2025
;
Popovic et al. 2026
. These analyses have shown mild tensions with the standard
\Lambda
CDM model and a preference for an evolving equation of state at low redshifts. To assess the robustness of our neutrino mass constraints in the context of dynamical dark energy, we also perform our analysis within the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder parameterization
Chevallier and Polarski 2001
;
Linder 2003
. In the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder model, the dark energy equation of state is given by
w(a)=w_{0}+w_{a}(1-a)=w_{0}+w_{a}{z}/{(1+z)}
, where
z
stands for cosmological redshift defined as
z=1/a-1
with
a
being the cosmic scale,
w_{0}
is the equation of state at the present day (
z=0
) and
w_{a}
describes its redshift evolution. The background expansion history is modified accordingly, altering the angular diameter distance and the growth of structure, which in turn affects the cosmic microwave background and baryon acoustic oscillation observables. In addition to the parameters already defined in our baseline
\Lambda
CDM model, the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder model introduces two independent parameters:
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
. For the pure adiabatic and mixed neutrino density isocurvature scenarios, we derive constraints on
\sum m_{\nu}
both under the assumption of a cosmological constant (
w_{0}=-1
,
w_{a}=0
) and under the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder parameterization with flat priors
w_{0}\in[-3,1]
and
w_{a}\in[-3,2]
. This allows us to examine whether the robustness against initial conditions persists when the dark energy sector is allowed to vary.

In addition to
\sum m_{\nu}
, the neutrino mass hierarchy, i.e., the ordering of the three mass eigenstates, might affect cosmological observables. In the normal hierarchy, the three masses satisfy
m_{1}<m_{2}<m_{3}
; in the inverted hierarchy,
m_{3}<m_{1}<m_{2}
. The difference manifests in the contribution of each eigenstate to the total
\sum m_{\nu}
and in the free-streaming history, because the lightest eigenstate in inverted hierarchy is heavier than in normal hierarchy for a fixed
\sum m_{\nu}
, altering the suppression scale of the matter power spectrum. However, given the sensitivity of current data, the impact of hierarchy is subtle and often degenerate with other parameters
Lesgourgues and Pastor 2006
;
Wong 2011
;
Huang et al. 2016
;
Wang et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2016
;
Du et al. 2025b
;
Yang et al. 2017
;
Guo et al. 2018
;
Du et al. 2025a
. To assess whether our conclusions on
\sum m_{\nu}
depend on the assumed ordering, we perform our analysis under three scenarios: normal hierarchy, inverted hierarchy, and a degenerate hierarchy where all three masses are set equal (
m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{3}=\sum m_{\nu}/3
) with the same prior on
\sum m_{\nu}
as in normal hierarchy/inverted hierarchy. The degenerate hierarchy case serves as a reference for minimal hierarchy information. By comparing the posterior distributions and upper limits on
\sum m_{\nu}
obtained from normal hierarchy, inverted hierarchy, and degenerate hierarchy, we quantify the robustness of our neutrino mass constraints against the unknown mass ordering.

III
Data and Method

For our primary cosmic microwave background data, we adopt the cosmic microwave background-SPA likelihood
Camphuis et al. 2026
, which combines high-resolution temperature, polarization, and lensing measurements from Planck, Atacama Cosmology Telescope, and South Pole Telescope third-generation. Specifically, we include the Planck 2018 low-
\ell
TT and EE likelihoods for large angular scales, the Atacama Cosmology Telescope Data Release 6 primary cosmic microwave background likelihood for small-scale anisotropies, and the South Pole Telescope third-generation two-year delensed EE and lensing data to further sharpen the constraints on the damping tail and gravitational lensing potential
Carron et al. 2022
;
Ge et al. 2025
;
Camphuis et al. 2026
;
Qu et al. 2026
;
Louis et al. 2025
. To avoid double counting and reduce correlations between the Planck and Atacama Cosmology Telescope datasets, we also use the Planck–Atacama Cosmology Telescope cut combination that imposes appropriate multipole cuts, thereby ensuring a conservative and unbiased joint analysis
Louis et al. 2025
;
Calabrese et al. 2025
.

baryon acoustic oscillation measurements provide a robust standard ruler for probing the expansion history of the Universe. We use the most recent baryon acoustic oscillation data from Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Data Release 2, which combines Lyman-
\alpha
forest auto- and cross-correlations with galaxy and quasar clustering at lower redshifts
Abdul Karim et al. 2025b
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025a
. The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Data Release 2 sample is based on three years of observations, comprising over 14 million galaxies and quasars, and achieves a combined precision of
0.65\%
on the isotropic baryon acoustic oscillation scale at an effective redshift of
z_{\rm eff}=2.33
. The full Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Data Release 2 baryon acoustic oscillation likelihood covers a wide redshift range, providing a strong complement to cosmic microwave background data by breaking degeneracies between
\sum m_{\nu}
and other parameters.

To further constrain the expansion history at moderate redshifts and to help break residual parameter degeneracies, we include the Dark Energy Survey Year 5 supernova sample
Abbott et al. 2024
;
Popovic et al. 2026
. This sample comprises approximately 1,500 spectroscopically confirmed and photometrically classified Type Ia supernovas spanning redshifts
0.1<z<1.2
, and is the largest and deepest SN Ia sample to date. Including the supernova data helps to tighten constraints on
\Omega_{m}
and the dark energy equation of state.

We perform parameter inference using MontePython
Audren et al. 2013
;
Brinckmann and Lesgourgues 2019
interfaced with the Cosmic Linear Anisotropy Solving System Boltzmann solver
Blas et al. 2011
;
Lesgourgues and Tram 2011
. For each cosmological model, Cosmic Linear Anisotropy Solving System computes the theoretical cosmic microwave background temperature and polarization power spectra, the matter power spectrum, and the lensing potential, which are then passed to MontePython. MontePython implements a Markov chain Monte Carlo sampler to explore the posterior distribution of the parameters. Cosmic Linear Anisotropy Solving System is configured with high-precision settings to ensure accurate predictions up to the maximum multipoles of the cosmic microwave background data. For the neutrino sector, the neutrino density isocurvature mode amplitude is implemented as an additional primordial power spectrum component within Cosmic Linear Anisotropy Solving System.

V
Conclusions and Discussion

In this work, we have investigated the robustness of cosmological upper limits on the sum of neutrino masses against the assumption of primordial initial conditions. Specifically, we considered the possibility of an neutrino density isocurvature component in addition to the standard adiabatic mode, and we analyzed the latest observational data sets: the cosmic microwave background-SPA combination (Planck 2018, Atacama Cosmology Telescope Data Release 6, and South Pole Telescope third-generation), Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Data Release 2 baryon acoustic oscillation, and the Dark Energy Survey Year 5 supernova sample. Our analysis was performed within both the standard
\Lambda
CDM framework and the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder dynamical dark energy model, and we also examined the impact of the neutrino mass hierarchy (normal hierarchy, inverted hierarchy, and degenerate hierarchy) as well as the effect of the prior lower bound on
\sum m_{\nu}
.

Our main findings are as follows. First, within
\Lambda
CDM, the
95\%
upper limit on
\sum m_{\nu}
under purely adiabatic initial conditions is
0.052
eV (assuming degenerate hierarchy and a prior lower bound of
0
eV). When an neutrino density isocurvature component is added, the bound increases only marginally to
0.057
eV, and the neutrino density isocurvature amplitude is consistent with zero at
95\%
confidence. Hence, the neutrino mass constraint is robust against the inclusion of isocurvature modes in a
\Lambda
CDM universe. Second, in the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder model, the adiabatic upper limit loosens to
0.111
eV, and adding neutrino density isocurvature further relaxes it to
0.115
eV, indicating a
50\%
increase relative to
\Lambda
CDM. This demonstrates that the inferred neutrino mass scale is highly sensitive to assumptions about the dark energy equation of state. Nevertheless, even in the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder model, the neutrino density isocurvature amplitude remains consistent with zero, and the isocurvature component does not significantly alter the
\sum m_{\nu}
bound. Third, we studied the influence of the neutrino mass hierarchy. Current cosmological data cannot distinguish the mass ordering. However, the prior lower bound on
\sum m_{\nu}
has a notable effect: enforcing a positive minimum (e.g.,
0.05878
eV) raises the upper limit by
0.03
–
0.04
eV. Therefore, when quoting upper limits, it is essential to specify the prior range, especially the lower bound.

The implications of our results are twofold. On the one hand, the robustness of the
\sum m_{\nu}
upper limit against neutrino density isocurvature modes suggests that current cosmological bounds are reliable even if the primordial perturbations are not purely adiabatic, provided that the neutrino density isocurvature amplitude is small. On the other hand, the strong dependence on the dark energy model underscores the need for a precise determination of the late-time expansion history before a definitive, model-independent neutrino mass limit can be claimed.

Our finding that neutrino density isocurvature modes do not significantly weaken the
\sum m_{\nu}
bound raises the question: is this robustness generic, or does it arise because the current limit lies close to the minimal mass allowed by the neutrino hierarchy? The minimum
\sum m_{\nu}
is
0.05878
eV for normal hierarchy and
0.09892
eV for inverted hierarchy. Our
\Lambda
CDM adiabatic upper limit (
0.052
eV) sits just below the normal hierarchy floor; imposing a physically motivated lower bound (e.g.,
0.05878
eV) raises the limit to
0.092
eV, while the neutrino density isocurvature
-
allowed bound becomes
0.094
eV, indicating an increase of only
0.002
eV. This tiny shift reveals that the data already constrain
\sum m_{\nu}
to a narrow window above the hierarchy minimum. In this regime, the degeneracy between
\sum m_{\nu}
and the neutrino density isocurvature amplitude is limited: a larger
\sum m_{\nu}
would over‑suppress structure, and the distinct scale‑dependent signature of neutrino density isocurvature cannot fully compensate. Hence, even allowing neutrino density isocurvature hardly relaxes the bound. The same holds for the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder model, where the absolute limits are higher (
0.111
–
0.115
eV) but the relative weakening due to neutrino density isocurvature remains minimal (
0.004
eV).

Physically motivated priors should at least incorporate the minimum mass implied by the neutrino mass hierarchy, i.e., 0.05878 eV for normal hierarchy and 0.09892 eV for inverted hierarchy. Our adiabatic
\Lambda
CDM upper limit of
0.052
eV lies below the normal hierarchy minimum, indicating that this bound is an artifact of the statistical prior extending to zero rather than a physically allowed mass range. Consequently, we recommend reporting two limits simultaneously: one with a zero lower bound (to facilitate comparison with previous work) and one with a hierarchy-informed lower bound (to reflect physical plausibility). For instance, in
\Lambda
CDM with adiabatic initial conditions, the
95\%
upper limit becomes
<0.092
eV when imposing
\sum m_{\nu}\geq 0.05878
eV, compared to
<0.052
eV under the zero lower bound. In summary, the observed robustness is intimately tied to the fact that current data already confine
\sum m_{\nu}
to a narrow range just above the minimal hierarchical mass, leaving little room for isocurvature modes to alter the limit, but the absolute value of the upper limit is strongly prior-dependent and should be interpreted with the hierarchy minimum in mind.

Although our analysis shows that current data disfavor a significant neutrino density isocurvature component, other new physics can mimic or degenerate with the effects of massive neutrinos. Dark radiation with self-interactions, for instance, can produce scale-dependent signatures in the cosmic microwave background and matter power spectra that partially resemble those of neutrino density isocurvature modes
Chang et al. 2026
. When the neutrino energy density is allowed to vary, cosmological data prefer massless free-streaming dark radiation over massive neutrinos, significantly relaxing the neutrino mass bound
Chang et al. 2026
. Moreover, neutrino self-interactions beyond the Standard Model have profound cosmological implications and can alter cosmic microwave background anisotropies in ways that may degenerate with neutrino density isocurvature signatures
Berryman et al. 2023
. Given that our constraints on the neutrino density isocurvature amplitude are consistent with zero, a joint analysis combining neutrino density isocurvature with extended neutrino sectors (e.g.,
N_{\mathrm{eff}}
or self-interactions) would be a natural next step.

Next-generation surveys will dramatically sharpen constraints on both
\sum m_{\nu}
and neutrino density isocurvature modes. The combination of cosmic microwave background-S4 and LiteBIRD is forecast to reduce isocurvature error bars by up to
70\%
for neutrino isocurvature modes
Montandon et al. 2021
. In the large-scale-structure sector, the Euclid alone is expected to achieve
\sigma(\sum m_{\nu})\approx 56
meV in
\Lambda
CDM, and combined with Planck this improves to
\approx 23
meV, offering evidence of a non-zero neutrino mass to at least
2.6\sigma

Archidiacono et al. 2025
. Including future cosmic microwave background data from LiteBIRD and cosmic microwave background-S4 could push this to a
4\sigma
detection
Archidiacono et al. 2025
. Should a non-zero neutrino density isocurvature component exist, these future surveys would have the sensitivity to detect it; otherwise, they will firmly establish the adiabatic nature of primordial perturbations.
