Computing the free energy of quantum Coulomb gases and molecules via quantum Gibbs sampling
Simon Becker, Cambyse Rouzé, Robert Salzmann

Abstract
. We develop a quantum algorithm for estimating the free energy as well as the total Gibbs state of interacting quantum Coulomb gases and molecular systems in dimensions d ∈ { 2 , 3 } d\in\{2,3\} at finite temperature. These systems lie beyond the reach of existing methods due to their singular interactions and infinite-dimensional Hilbert space structure. First, we show that the free energy of the full many-body Hamiltonian can be approximated by that of the same Hamiltonian with a finite-rank low-energy truncation of the interaction, with an explicit error bound polynomial in the particle number. This reduces the problem to a controlled finite-rank perturbation problem. Second, we introduce a quantum Gibbs sampling scheme tailored to this truncated system, based on a class of quantum Markov semigroups. Our main analytical result establishes that the associated generator has a strictly positive spectral gap for every truncation, implying exponential convergence to the target Gibbs state. This provides, to our knowledge, the first rigorous mixing-time guarantee for Gibbs sampling in a Coulomb interacting continuous-variable quantum system. Finally, we give an explicit quantum circuit implementation of the dynamics and derive an end-to-end complexity bound for approximating the free energy and the Gibbs state itself. Our results provide a mathematically rigorous route to quantum algorithms for free energy estimation in interacting quantum systems, without relying on classical approximations such as the Born–Oppenheimer reduction.



1.
Introduction

Free energy plays a foundational role in thermodynamics and has become an essential tool for describing complex chemical, biological, and physical phenomena.
In practice, the free-energy landscape connecting an initial configuration to a final one governs the likelihood and timescale of transitions between states: large free-energy barriers strongly suppress such transitions, whereas lower barriers make them more accessible. A familiar example is provided by chemical reactions in solution, including processes occurring inside living cells, where reaction rates are often controlled by the need to overcome intermediate free-energy obstacles. Similar ideas also arise in quantum settings, where free-energy landscapes help characterize thermal states of many-body systems, metastability, or the difficulty of steering a quantum system from one phase or configuration to another.

(2.4)

\log\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{X+Y})}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{X})}\geq\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{X}Y)}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{X})}

Hence

Next, applying (

Inserting these estimates into (

with respect to

:

Lemma 2.2

(Product cutoff estimate without scaling assumption)
.

Given
d\in\{2,3\}
, for all
n,M
and
\psi\in Q(H_{0,n})
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle|W_{n}[\psi]|

\displaystyle\lesssim\,B_{n}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle,

(2.7)

\displaystyle|W_{n}[\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\psi]|

\displaystyle\lesssim\,B_{n}(\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/2}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle,

(2.8)

\displaystyle|\Delta_{n,M}[\psi]|

\displaystyle\lesssim\,B_{n}(\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/4}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle.

Proof.

Fix
1\leq i<j\leq n
and
d=3
. Introduce the relative variable
r:=2^{-1/2}{(x_{i}-x_{j})}
, so that
{|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-1}}=2^{-1/2}{|r|^{-1}}
. By Hardy’s inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\left\|\frac{u}{|r|}\right\|^{2}\leq 4\|\nabla_{r}u\|^{2}.

Hence, by Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, for every
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\left\langle u,\frac{1}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|}u\right\rangle=2^{-1/2}\left\langle u,\frac{1}{|r|}u\right\rangle\leq\sqrt{2}\,\|u\|\,\|\nabla_{r}u\|

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|\nabla_{r}u\|^{2}+(2\eta)^{-1}\|u\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta\|\nabla_{r}u\|^{2}+C_{\eta}\|u\|^{2},

where we defined
C_{\eta}=(2\eta)^{-1}
in the last equality.
Since

\|\nabla_{r}u\|^{2}\leq\langle u,(-\Delta_{x_{i}}-\Delta_{x_{j}})u\rangle\leq\langle u,\bigl(-\Delta_{x_{i}}-\Delta_{x_{j}}+|x_{i}|^{2}+|x_{j}|^{2}\bigr)u\rangle,

we obtain

(2.9)

\frac{1}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|}\leq\eta\bigl(-\Delta_{x_{i}}-\Delta_{x_{j}}+|x_{i}|^{2}+|x_{j}|^{2}\bigr)+(2\eta)^{-1}

as quadratic forms. Taking
\eta=1
, and using
h_{i}+h_{j}+1\leq 2(H_{0,n}+1)
, we get

|W_{n}[\psi]|\lesssim\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|\langle\psi,(h_{i}+h_{j}+1)\psi\rangle\lesssim B_{n}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle.

In dimension
d=2
, let
u\in Q(h_{1}+h_{2})
. Fix
0<\varepsilon<1
. Since

|\log r|\leq C_{\varepsilon}\bigl(1+r^{-\varepsilon}+r^{\varepsilon}\bigr),\qquad r>0,

it suffices to estimate these three terms separately. First,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy=\|u\|^{2}\leq\langle u,(h_{1}+h_{2}+1)u\rangle.

Next, using the change of variables
r=x-y
,
s=x+y
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x-y|^{-\varepsilon}|u(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|r|^{-\varepsilon}|u(r,s)|^{2}\,dr\,ds.

Set

F(r):=\|u(r,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{s})}.

Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|r|^{-\varepsilon}|u(r,s)|^{2}\,dr\,ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|r|^{-\varepsilon}F(r)^{2}\,dr.

Choose
p>1
such that
\varepsilon p<2
, and let
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
its Hölder conjugate. Since

|r|^{-\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
for
B_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Hölder’s inequality in the
r
-variable yields

\int_{|r|\leq 1}|r|^{-\varepsilon}F(r)^{2}\,dr\leq\bigl\||r|^{-\varepsilon}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\|F^{2}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=C\|F\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}.

Now
2q<\infty
, so by the Sobolev embedding

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{r};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{s}))\hookrightarrow L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{r};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{s}))

we obtain

\|F\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|u\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}_{r}^{2},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{s}^{2}))}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{r};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{s}))}.

Hence

\int_{|r|\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|r|^{-\varepsilon}|u(r,s)|^{2}\,ds\,dr\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{r};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{s}))}^{2}.

Since
|r|^{-\varepsilon}\leq 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we also control

\int_{|r|>1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|r|^{-\varepsilon}|u(r,s)|^{2}\,ds\,dr\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}.

Therefore

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|r|^{-\varepsilon}|u(r,s)|^{2}\,dr\,ds\lesssim\Bigl(\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{r};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{s}))}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}\Bigr).

By the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
,

\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}\lesssim\langle u,(h_{1}+h_{2}+1)u\rangle.

Finally, since
0<\varepsilon<2
, there exist a constants
C_{\varepsilon},C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}>0
such that

|x-y|^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\bigl(1+|x|^{\varepsilon}+|y|^{\varepsilon}\bigr)\leq C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\bigl(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\bigr),

and therefore

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x-y|^{\varepsilon}|u(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\langle u,(h_{1}+h_{2}+1)u\rangle.

Putting the three estimates together yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\log|x-y||\,|u(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy\lesssim\langle u,(h_{1}+h_{2}+1)u\rangle.

Summing over all combinations of
x=x_{i},y=x_{j}
yields then

|W_{n}[\psi]|\lesssim B_{n}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle.

This ends the proof of (
2.6
). For (
2.7
), we may proceed for both
d=2,3
as follows:
instead of bounding the interaction form entirely by the energy right away, we first apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the interaction potential. For
d=3
, using Hardy’s inequality
\||x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-1}\varphi\|\lesssim\langle\varphi,(h_{i}+h_{j})\varphi\rangle^{1/2}
, and for
d=2
using the analogous interpolation from the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality derived above, we obtain the fractional form bound for any

|W_{n}[\varphi]|\lesssim B_{n}\|\varphi\|\langle\varphi,(H_{0,n}+1)\varphi\rangle^{1/2}.

We evaluate this bound on the high-energy projection

. Since
(H_{0,n}+1)\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\geq(\lambda_{M+1}+1)\Pi_{M}^{\perp}
, the norm decays as:

\|\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\psi\|\leq(\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/2}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle^{1/2}.

Furthermore, since
[\Pi_{M}^{\perp},H_{0,n}]=0
and
\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\leq 1
, we have
\langle\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\psi\rangle\leq\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle
. Substituting these into our fractional form bound yields

\displaystyle|W_{n}[\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\psi]|

\displaystyle\lesssim B_{n}\|\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\psi\|\langle\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\Pi_{M}^{\perp}\psi\rangle^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq B_{n}\left((\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/2}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle^{1/2}\right)\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle^{1/2}

(2.10)

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/2}B_{n}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle,

which ends the proof of (
2.7
).

For (
2.8
), we associate to

the sesquilinear form

W_{n}(\varphi,\chi):=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{n,i,j}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}w_{d}(x_{i}-x_{j})\,\varphi(x)\,\overline{\chi(x)}\,dx,

so that

. Hence, writing

and

W_{n}[\psi]-W_{n,M}[\psi]=W_{n}[P\psi+Q\psi]-W_{n}[P\psi]=W_{n}[Q\psi]+2\Re W_{n}(P\psi,Q\psi).

Similarly, if
\widetilde{W}_{n}
denotes the positive sesquilinear form with kernel

|w_{d}|
and coefficients
|\alpha_{n,i,j}|
, then combining the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (

) applied to

, and (

) applied to

, we get

\displaystyle|W_{n}(P\psi,Q\psi)|

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{W}_{n}[P\psi]^{1/2}\widetilde{W}_{n}[Q\psi]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim B_{n}(\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/4}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle.

Finally, combining the last bound with the estimate for

derived in (

) yields the claimed bound (

).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space, bounded from below with smooth partition function. Then, for every
t>0
,

(2.12)

\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(K)K)\leq 2F_{t}(K)-F_{t/2}(K).

Proof.

Writing
U_{K}(t)=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(K)K)
,
we immediately get

-\partial_{t}\log\mathcal{Z}_{t}(K)=-\frac{\partial_{t}\mathcal{Z}_{t}(K)}{\mathcal{Z}_{t}(K)}=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(Ke^{-tK})}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tK})}=U_{K}(t).

Moreover, by the quotient rule,

(2.13)

\displaystyle U_{K}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(Ke^{-tK})^{2}-\operatorname{Tr}(K^{2}e^{-tK})\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tK})}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tK})^{2}}=-\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(K)K^{2})+\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(K)K)\bigr)^{2}.

Expanding
(K-U_{K}(t))^{2}
yields
\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\sigma_{t}(K)(K-U_{K}(t))^{2}\bigr)=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(K)K^{2})-U_{K}(t)^{2}
. Thus

U_{K}^{\prime}(t)=-\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\sigma_{t}(K)(K-U_{K}(t))^{2}\bigr)\leq 0,

This proves that
t\mapsto U_{K}(t)
is decreasing. Finally, fix
t>0
. Since
U_{K}
is decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, we have

U_{K}(t)\leq\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}U_{K}(s)\,ds.

Using
U_{K}(s)=-\partial_{s}\log\mathcal{Z}_{s}(K)
, we obtain

\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}U_{K}(s)\,ds=-\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}\partial_{s}\log\mathcal{Z}_{s}(K)\,ds=\frac{2}{t}\Bigl(\log\mathcal{Z}_{t/2}(K)-\log\mathcal{Z}_{t}(K)\Bigr),

which is exactly (
2.12
).
∎

Next, we control the free energy difference obtained in the previous Lemma. In what follows, we
denote the one-body
\rho_{t}(x):=\langle x|\sigma_{t}(h)|x\rangle
. Since
h=-\Delta+|x|^{2}
, the kernel
\rho_{t}(x):=\langle x|\sigma_{t}(h)|x\rangle
of
e^{-th}
is given by the Mehler formula. In particular,
\rho_{t}(x)
is smooth, strictly positive, and has Gaussian decay as
|x|\to\infty
:

\rho_{t}(x)\leq C_{t}e^{-c_{t}|x|^{2}}

for suitable constants
c_{t},C_{t}>0
. Therefore, for any
d\in\{2,3\}
,

\widetilde{I}_{d,t}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|w_{d}(x-y)|\,\rho_{t}(x)\rho_{t}(y)\,dx\,dy<\infty.

Lemma 2.4

(Upper bounds on the free energy via free Gibbs states)
.

Given
d\in\{2,3\}
, fix
t>0
. Then

(2.14)

F_{t}(H_{n})\leq nF_{t}(h)+B_{n}\widetilde{I}_{d,t},\qquad\text{ and }\qquad F_{t}(H_{n,M})\leq nF_{t}(h)+B_{n}\widetilde{I}_{d,t}.

Proof.

By a use of the Bogoliubov inequality (
2.4
), with
X=-tH_{0,n}
and
Y=-t(H_{n}-H_{0,n})
,

F_{t}(H_{n})\leq{n}F_{t}(h)+\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})W_{n}).

Next, we estimate the interaction term: for each pair
i<j
one has

\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\,w_{d}(x_{i}-x_{j})\bigr)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}w_{d}(x-y)\rho_{t}(x)\rho_{t}(y)\,dx\,dy,

independently of
i<j
. Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})W_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{n,i,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}w_{d}(x-y)\rho_{t}(x)\rho_{t}(y)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|w_{d}(x-y)|\,\rho_{t}(x)\rho_{t}(y)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=B_{n}\widetilde{I}_{d,t}.

The proof for
H_{n,M}
is almost identical: by (
2.4
),

F_{t}(H_{n,M})\leq nF_{t}(h)+\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})W_{n,M}).

Then, since
\Pi_{M}
commutes with
H_{0,n}
,
\Pi_{M}
also commutes with
\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})
and

\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})W_{n,M})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{n,i,j}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\Pi_{M}\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\Pi_{M}\,w_{d}(x_{i}-x_{j})\bigr).

Since
\Pi_{M}\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\Pi_{M}\geq 0
is trace class, we may use
\bigl|\operatorname{Tr}(AB)\bigr|\leq\operatorname{Tr}(A|B|)

for
A\geq 0
trace class to get

\bigl|\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\Pi_{M}\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\Pi_{M}\,w_{d}(x_{i}-x_{j})\bigr)\bigr|\leq\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\Pi_{M}\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\Pi_{M}\,|w_{d}(x_{i}-x_{j})|\bigr).

Again, since
\Pi_{M}
commutes with
\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})
, one has

0\leq\Pi_{M}\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\Pi_{M}=\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\Pi_{M}\leq\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n}).

Hence

\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\Pi_{M}\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\Pi_{M}\,|w_{d}(x_{i}-x_{j})|\bigr)\leq\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\sigma_{t}(H_{0,n})\,|w_{d}(x_{i}-x_{j})|\bigr)=\widetilde{I}_{d,t}

which proves the second bound.
∎

Similarly, we derive lower bounds on the free energies

and

Lemma 2.5

(Lower bounds on the free energy via free Gibbs states)
.

For

and

we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle F_{t}(H_{n}),\,F_{t}(H_{n,M})\geq nF_{t/2}(h)-CA_{n}B_{n}\,.

For some universal constant
C\geq 0.

Proof.

We start with the case
d=3
, so
w_{3}(x)=|x|^{-1}\geq 0
. For every
\eta>0
, the estimate proved in (

) gives

\frac{1}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|}\leq\eta\bigl(-\Delta_{x_{i}}-\Delta_{x_{j}}+|x_{i}|^{2}+|x_{j}|^{2}\bigr)+(2\eta)^{-1}

Summing over all pairs

Choosing

we get

H_{n}=H_{0,n}+W_{n}\geq(1-\eta A_{n})H_{0,n}-(2\eta)^{-1}B_{n}=\frac{1}{2}H_{0,n}-A_{n}B_{n}

Exactly the same estimate holds for

. Indeed, since
\Pi_{M}
commutes with

because

and
\Pi_{M}(h_{i}+h_{j})\Pi_{M}\leq h_{i}+h_{j}
on the form domain. Summing again over pairs yields and taking the same choice for

H_{n,M}\geq\frac{1}{2}H_{0,n}-A_{n}B_{n}.

Now (

implies

\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH_{n}}),\,\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH_{n,M}})\leq e^{tA_{n}B_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH_{0,n}/2}),

which directly yields

F_{t}(H_{n}),\,F_{t}(H_{n,M})\geq-A_{n}B_{n}+nF_{t/2}(h).

Now assume
d=2
, so

w_{2}(x)=-\log|x|
. This kernel is not pointwise nonnegative, so the previous argument must be modified. We use the following bound, whose proof we postpone to Lemma
2.6
below: for any
\eta>0
we have that

(2.18)

|\,\log|x-y|\,|\leq\eta(h_{x}+h_{y})+C\,\eta^{-1}

as quadratic forms on

and for some universal constant

As a consequence, if

W_{n}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{n,i,j}\,(-\log|x_{i}-x_{j}|),\qquad B_{n}:=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|,

then for every

as quadratic forms. Thus, we obtain

(2.20)

H_{n}=H_{0,n}+W_{n}\geq\big(1-\eta A_{n}\big)H_{0,n}-C\,\eta^{-1}B_{n}.

Choosing once again

we see that (
2.20
) becomes

(2.21)

\displaystyle H_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}H_{0,n}-2CA_{n}B_{n}.

Again, exactly the same estimate holds for
H_{n,M}
, using that
\Pi_{M}
commutes with
H_{0,n}
. Thus, (

) together with the monotonicity of

implies

\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH_{n}}),\,\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH_{n,M}})\leq e^{t2CA_{n}B_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-tH_{0,n}/2}),

which directly yields

(2.22)

F_{t}(H_{n}),\,F_{t}(H_{n,M})\geq-2CA_{n}B_{n}+nF_{t/2}(h).

Lemma 2.6

(Two-sided logarithmic form bound in dimension

)
.

For

(2.23)

\bigl|\langle u,(-\log|x-y|)u\rangle\bigr|\leq\eta\,\langle u,(h_{x}+h_{y})u\rangle+C\,\eta^{-1}\|u\|^{2}\qquad\text{ for all }u\in Q(h_{x}+h_{y}),

Equivalently,

(2.24)

-|\,\log|x-y|\,|\leq-\log|x-y|\leq|\,\log|x-y|\,|\leq\eta(h_{x}+h_{y})+C\,\eta^{-1}

as quadratic forms on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in Q(h_{x}+h_{y})
and introduce the orthogonal change of variables

r:=2^{-1/2}(x-y)
,
s:=2^{-1/2}(x+y)
, and define
v(r,s):=u\big(\frac{r+s}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{s-r}{\sqrt{2}}\big)
. Moreover, the map
(x,y)\mapsto(r,s)
satisfies

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},\qquad-\Delta_{x}-\Delta_{y}=-\Delta_{r}-\Delta_{s},\qquad|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}=|r|^{2}+|s|^{2}.

Hence

(2.25)

\langle u,(h_{x}+h_{y})u\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\Bigl(|\nabla_{r}v|^{2}+|\nabla_{s}v|^{2}+(|r|^{2}+|s|^{2})|v|^{2}\Bigr)\,dr\,ds.

Also, since
|x-y|=\sqrt{2}\,|r|
,

|\log|x-y||\leq C+|\log|r||.

Therefore it suffices to prove that for every
\eta>0
,

(2.26)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|\log|r||\,|v(r,s)|^{2}\,dr\,ds\leq\eta\,\langle u,(h_{x}+h_{y})u\rangle+C\eta^{-1}\|u\|^{2}.

We split the integral into the regions
|r|\leq 1
and
|r|>1
. For the region
|r|>1
, we use that for every
\eta>0
we can bound

\log t=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\eta t^{2}/2\right)+\frac{1}{2}\log(2/\eta)\leq\eta t^{2}+\eta^{-1},

where for the last inequality we have used the elementary bound
\log x\leq x.

Hence

(2.27)

\displaystyle\int_{|r|>1}|\log|r||\,|v|^{2}=\int_{|r|>1}\log|r|\,|v|^{2}\leq\eta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|r|^{2}|v|^{2}\,dr\,ds+\eta^{-1}\|v\|^{2}.

For the region
|r|\leq 1
, fix
0<\delta<2
. Since

-\log t\leq C_{\delta}\,t^{-\delta}\qquad(0<t\leq 1),

we obtain

\int_{|r|\leq 1}|\log|r||\,|v(r,s)|^{2}\,dr\,ds\leq C_{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{s}}\int_{|r|\leq 1}|r|^{-\delta}|v(r,s)|^{2}\,dr\,ds.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and set
f_{s}(r):=v(r,s)
. Choose
p>1
such that
\delta p<2
, and let
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Since
|r|^{-\delta}\in L^{p}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, Hölder’s inequality gives

\int_{|r|\leq 1}|r|^{-\delta}|f_{s}(r)|^{2}\,dr\leq\||r|^{-\delta}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\|f_{s}\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}.

Now by the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality in dimension
2
,

\|f_{s}\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq C_{q}\|\nabla_{r}f_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2/p}\|f_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2(1-1/p)}.

Using Young’s inequality, for every
\eta>0
,

\|\nabla_{r}f_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2/p}\|f_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2(1-1/p)}\leq\frac{\eta}{C_{\delta}C_{q}\||r|^{-\delta}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}}\,\|\nabla_{r}f_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+c_{p,\delta}\,\eta^{-1/(p-1)}\|f_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}.

Therefore, choosing
\delta=1/2
and
p=2,
yields after integrating in
s
,

(2.28)

\int_{|r|\leq 1}|\log|r||\,|v(r,s)|^{2}\,dr\,ds\leq\eta\|\nabla_{r}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}+C^{\prime}\,\eta^{-1}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}.

Combining (
2.27
) and (
2.28
), and using (
2.25
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|\log|r||\,|v(r,s)|^{2}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\eta\Bigl(\|\nabla_{r}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}+\||r|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}\Bigr)+C\eta^{-1}\|v\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\,\langle u,(h_{x}+h_{y})u\rangle+C\eta^{-1}\|u\|^{2},

which proves (
2.26
), hence (
2.23
). The operator inequalities in (
2.24
) follow immediately.
∎

We are ready to prove the required bounds on the average energy appearing in (
2.11
):

Lemma 2.7

(Bounding the average oscillator energy)
.

Fix
d\in\{2,3\}
. Then there exists a constants
C_{\beta}<\infty
and independent of
n
and
M
, such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})H_{0,n})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}\,\left(n+(A_{n}+1)B_{n}\right),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n,M})H_{0,n})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}\,\left(n+(A_{n}+1)B_{n}\right).

Proof.

Using the upper bound for
F_{\beta}(H_{n})
derived in Lemma
2.4
and the lower bound for
F_{\beta}(H_{n})
from Lemma
2.5
, combined with Lemma
2.3
with
K=H_{n}
, we get

\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})H_{n})\leq 2nF_{\beta}(h)+2B_{n}\widetilde{I}_{d,\beta}-nF_{\beta/4}(h)+CA_{n}B_{n}.

Finally, from (
2.21
) and (
2.16
) we have

(2.29)

H_{n}=H_{0,n}+W_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}H_{0,n}-CA_{n}B_{n}

from which we can conclude

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})H_{0,n})\leq 2\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})H_{n})+2CA_{n}B_{n}\leq 4nF_{\beta}(h)+4B_{n}\widetilde{I}_{d,\beta}-2nF_{\beta/4}(h)+4CA_{n}B_{n}.

Once again, the same proof yields an identical bound for
\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n,M})
and concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to state and prove the main theorem of this section:

Theorem 2.8

(Free energy perturbation bound)
.

Fix
d\in\{2,3\}
,
\beta>0
. There exists a constant
C_{\beta}<\infty
, independent of
n,M\geq 1
, such that

|F_{\beta}(H_{n})-F_{\beta}(H_{n,M})|\leq C_{\beta}\,\left(n^{3}\alpha^{\max}_{n}+n^{5}\left(\alpha^{\max}_{n}\right)^{3}\right)M^{-\frac{1}{4d}},

where we defined
\alpha^{\max}_{n}:=\max_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|.

Proof.

From (
2.11
), Lemma
2.7
and the fact that
\lambda_{M}=\Omega(M^{1/d}),
we see

\displaystyle|F_{\beta}(H_{n})-F_{\beta}(H_{n,M})|\leq C_{\beta}\left(nB_{n}+(A_{n}+1)B^{2}_{n}\right)M^{-\frac{1}{4d}}.

Using that
B_{n}\leq n^{2}\alpha^{\max}_{n}
and
A_{n}\leq n\alpha^{\max}_{n}
finishes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Relative entropy and trace-distance convergence

In this section, we use the relative entropy to obtain a trace-distance bound on the distance between the two full and approximated Gibbs state.
Given two density operators
\rho,\sigma
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the (quantum) relative entropy of
\rho
with respect to
\sigma
is defined by

D(\rho\|\sigma):=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\bigr),&\text{if }\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subset\mathrm{supp}(\sigma),\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is nonnegative and vanishes if and only if
\rho=\sigma
. Moreover, it controls the trace norm via Pinsker’s inequality,

\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}^{2}\leq 2D(\rho\|\sigma).

Lemma 2.9

(Relative entropy bound for Gibbs states under relative form perturbations)
.

Let
H_{0}
be self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}
, bounded from below with compact resolvent. Let
W,W^{\prime}
be symmetric quadratic forms on
Q(H_{0})
with relative form bound strictly smaller than
1
, and define
H=H_{0}+W
,
H^{\prime}=H_{0}+W^{\prime}
such that the respective Gibbs states
\sigma_{\beta}(H),\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})
exist. Set
\Delta:=H-H^{\prime}
and assume that for some
\varepsilon,c\geq 0
,

(2.30)

|\Delta[\psi]|\leq\varepsilon\langle\psi,H_{0}\psi\rangle+c\|\psi\|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\psi\in Q(H_{0}).

Then the Gibbs states
\sigma_{\beta}(H),\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})
satisfy the relative entropy bounds

(2.31)

D(\sigma_{\beta}(H)\|\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})),\,D(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})\|\sigma_{\beta}(H))\leq\beta\Bigl(\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)H_{0})+\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})H_{0})+2c\Bigr),

and consequently

(2.32)

\|\sigma_{\beta}(H)-\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\beta\Bigl(\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)H_{0})+\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})H_{0})+2c\Bigr)}.

Proof.

Self-adjointness, lower bounds, and compact resolvent follow from the form assumptions; hence, the Gibbs operators are trace class. Using
\log\sigma_{\beta}(H)=-\beta H-\log\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}(H)
, we compute

\begin{split}D(\sigma_{\beta}(H)\|\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime}))&=\beta\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)(H^{\prime}-H))+\log\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}(H^{\prime})-\log\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}(H)\\
&=\beta\bigl(F_{\beta}(H)-F_{\beta}(H^{\prime})-\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)\Delta)\bigr),\end{split}

and similarly

D(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})\|\sigma_{\beta}(H))=\beta\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})\Delta)-(F_{\beta}(H)-F_{\beta}(H^{\prime}))\bigr).

By the Bogoliubov inequality as in Lemma
2.1
,

\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)\Delta)\leq F_{\beta}(H)-F_{\beta}(H^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})\Delta),

hence both relative entropies are bounded by
\beta(\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})\Delta)-\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)\Delta))
. Using the spectral decomposition
\sigma_{\beta}(H)=\sum_{k}p_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k}|
and using (
2.30
),

|\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)\Delta)|=\Bigl|\sum_{k}p_{k}\Delta[\psi_{k}]\Bigr|\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)H_{0})+c,

and analogously for
\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})
. Therefore

\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})\Delta)-\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)\Delta)\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H)H_{0})+\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H^{\prime})H_{0})+2c,

which yields the entropy bounds. The trace norm estimate follows from Pinsker
\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}^{2}\leq 2D(\rho\|\sigma)
.
∎

Specifying this to the case of Coulomb interactions yields then:

Theorem 2.10

(Trace norm perturbation bound)
.

Fix
d\in\{2,3\}
,
\beta>0
. Then there exists
C_{\beta}<\infty
, independent of
n,M
, such that

(2.33)

\|\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})-\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n,M})\|_{1}\leq C_{\beta}\sqrt{n^{3}\alpha^{\max}_{n}+n^{5}(\alpha^{\max}_{n})^{3}}\ M^{-\frac{1}{8d}},

where we defined
\alpha^{\max}_{n}:=\max_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

|\Delta_{n,M}[\psi]|\lesssim B_{n}(\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/4}\langle\psi,(H_{0,n}+1)\psi\rangle,

hence there exists
\varepsilon_{n,M},c_{n,M}>0
such that

|\Delta_{n,M}[\psi]|\leq\varepsilon_{n,M}\langle\psi,H_{0,n}\psi\rangle+c_{n,M}\|\psi\|^{2},\quad\varepsilon_{n,M}\sim c_{n,M}\sim B_{n}(\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/4}.

Applying Lemma
2.9
with
H=H_{n}
,
H^{\prime}=H_{n,M}
,
H_{0}=H_{0,n}
, we obtain

\|\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})-\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n,M})\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\beta\Bigl(\varepsilon_{n,M}\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})H_{0,n})+\varepsilon_{n,M}\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n,M})H_{0,n})+2c_{n,M}\Bigr)}.

By Lemma
2.7
, and the fact that
B_{n}\leq n^{2}\alpha^{\max}_{n}
and
A_{n}\leq n\alpha^{\max}_{n}
we see

\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})H_{0,n})\,,\,\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n,M})H_{0,n})\leq C_{\beta}(n+n^{3}(\alpha^{\max}_{n})^{2})\quad

and hence

\|\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})-\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n,M})\|_{1}\lesssim_{\beta}\Bigl((n^{3}\alpha^{\max}_{n}+n^{5}(\alpha^{\max}_{n})^{3})(\lambda_{M+1}+1)^{-1/4}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Using
\lambda_{M+1}\gtrsim M^{1/d}
, we conclude

\|\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n})-\sigma_{\beta}(H_{n,M})\|_{1}\leq C_{\beta}\sqrt{n^{3}\alpha^{\max}_{n}+n^{5}(\alpha^{\max}_{n})^{3}}\ M^{-\frac{1}{8d}}.

∎

3.
Spectral gap analysis

In this section, we study the spectral gap of the generators
L_{H_{n,M}}
. The proof of Theorem
1.4
is based on a perturbative analysis of the reference generator
L_{H_{0,n}}
.

3.1.
Quadratic Hamiltonians

In order to study the generator of the dynamics corresponding to
H_{0,n}
, we consider the quantum Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup converging to the Gibbs state of the number operator
N:=a^{\dagger}a\equiv\frac{1}{2}(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+x^{2}-1)
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, with associated ladder operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
, whose generator on
\mathscr{T}_{1}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
is given by
[
14
]

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N}(\rho)=

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(1)|^{2}\,\Big(a^{\dagger}\rho a-\frac{1}{2}\{aa^{\dagger},\rho\}\Big)+|\widehat{f}(-1)|^{2}\,\Big(a\rho a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{a^{\dagger}a,\rho\}\Big).

This generator coincides with
\mathcal{L}_{(H_{0,n=1}-1)/2}
in the case of a single one-dimensional particle, i.e.
d=1
. Denoting the birth rate
\nu_{+}:=|\widehat{f}(1)|^{2}
and death rate
\nu_{-}:=|\widehat{f}(-1)|^{2}
, the associated self-adjoint generator on
\mathscr{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
takes the form
[
14
]
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle L_{N}(x)=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\nu_{-}+\nu_{+}}{2}(Nx+xN)+\nu_{+}x\right)+\sqrt{\nu_{+}\nu_{-}}(axa^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}xa).

In later parts of the paper, we shall also use the lower bound

(3.3)

\displaystyle L_{N}\;\geq\;L_{\operatorname{LB}},

in the sense of quadratic forms, as shown in
[
14
, Proof of Thm. 6.3]
. Here
L_{\operatorname{LB}}
is the so-called
ladder-block
operator defined by

(3.4)

L_{\operatorname{LB}}(x):=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{+}-\nu_{-})^{2}(Nx+xN)\;-\;\nu_{+}(\nu_{-}-\nu_{+})\,x.

The operator
L_{\operatorname{LB}}
admits the spectral decomposition

(3.5)

L_{\operatorname{LB}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\kappa_{k}\,R_{k}\qquad\text{ where }\kappa_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{+}-\nu_{-})^{2}\,k\;-\;\nu_{+}(\nu_{-}-\nu_{+}),

and
R_{k}
is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
\operatorname{span}\{\,|n\rangle\langle m|:n+m=k\,\}
, where
\ket{n}
denotes the Fock state with
N\ket{n}=n\ket{n}
. This decomposition provides a simpler spectral structure than that of
L_{N}
, making it a useful comparison operator for later perturbative spectral analysis.

Next, to motivate our unperturbed Hamiltonian
H_{0,n}
in the context of Coulomb interacting systems, we consider a model of
d=2
dimensional particles in a constant magnetic field with a quadratic trapping potential

(3.6)

\displaystyle H_{0}\equiv h_{i}=(-i\nabla_{x_{i}}-\vec{A}(x_{i}))^{2}+\langle x_{i},Mx_{i}\rangle.

Furthermore, we set
{A}(x_{i})=\frac{1}{2}(-Bx_{i2},Bx_{i1})^{\mathsf{T}}
the vector potential for a constant magnetic field
B
and coordinates
x_{i}:=(x_{i1},x_{i2})
in the orthogonal direction and assume that
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is a positive-definite matrix.

Next, we study the spectral gap of
L_{H_{0}}
for the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
introduced in (
3.6
). First, the spectral decomposition
H_{0}
follows the method introduced in
[
28
]
: we note that
H_{0}=X^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}X
, where
X=(x_{1},D_{x_{1}},x_{2},D_{x_{2}})
, where
D_{x_{i}}:=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
and

\mathcal{H}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}+\frac{B^{2}}{4}&0&M_{12}&-\frac{B}{2}\\
0&1&\frac{B}{2}&0\\
M_{12}&\frac{B}{2}&M_{22}+\frac{B^{2}}{4}&0\\
-\frac{B}{2}&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

We then notice that
[X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}]=-\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}
, where
\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{2}).
Next, we compute

\begin{split}[X,H_{0}]&=\sum_{k}X_{k}\sum_{i,j}X_{i}\mathcal{H}_{ij}X_{j}e_{k}-\sum_{i,j}X_{i}\mathcal{H}_{ij}X_{j}X_{k}e_{k}\\
&=\sum_{i,j,k}(X_{i}\mathcal{H}_{ij}X_{k}X_{j}+[X_{k},X_{i}]\mathcal{H}_{ij}X_{j})e_{k}-\sum_{i,j}X_{i}\mathcal{H}_{ij}X_{j}X_{k}e_{k}\\
&=\sum_{i,j,k}X_{i}\mathcal{H}_{ij}[X_{k},X_{j}]e_{k}-\Sigma_{ki}\mathcal{H}_{ij}X_{j}e_{k}=-\sum_{i,j,k}X_{i}\mathcal{H}_{ij}\Sigma_{kj}e_{k}+\Sigma_{ki}\mathcal{H}_{ij}X_{j}e_{k}\\
&=-\sum_{i,j,k}e_{k}\Sigma_{kj}\mathcal{H}_{ji}X_{i}+e_{k}\Sigma_{ki}\mathcal{H}_{ij}X_{j}=-2\Sigma\mathcal{H}X.\end{split}

Thus, we have
[iH_{0},X]=2\Omega X
, where

\Omega:=i\Sigma\mathcal{H}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-\frac{B}{2}&0\\
-M_{11}-\frac{B^{2}}{4}&0&-M_{12}&-\frac{B}{2}\\
\frac{B}{2}&0&0&1\\
-M_{12}&\frac{B}{2}&-M_{22}-\frac{B^{2}}{4}&0\end{pmatrix},

the characteristic polynomial of
\Omega
reads

\det(\Omega-\lambda I)=\lambda^{4}+\lambda^{2}(\operatorname{Tr}(M)+B^{2})+\operatorname{det}(M).

Since the characteristic polynomial is invariant under flips
\lambda\to-\lambda
, the eigenvalues of
\Omega
are of the form

\operatorname{Sp}(\Omega)=\operatorname{diag}(\pm i\sigma_{1},\pm i\sigma_{2})

with

\begin{split}\sigma_{1}&=\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(M)+B^{2}+\sqrt{(\operatorname{Tr}(M)+B^{2})^{2}-4\det(M)}}{2}}\,~,\\
\sigma_{2}&=\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(M)+B^{2}-\sqrt{(\operatorname{Tr}(M)+B^{2})^{2}-4\det(M)}}{2}}\end{split}

which are both real and strictly positive since
\operatorname{Tr}(M),\det(M)>0.
We then have left and right eigenvectors

\Omega^{\top}u_{i}=-i\sigma_{i}u_{i}\text{ and }\Omega v_{i}=-i\sigma_{i}v_{i}.

Taking the complex conjugate of this equation and using the fact that all entries in
\Omega
are real, we find

\Omega^{\top}\overline{u_{i}}=i\sigma_{i}\overline{u_{i}}\text{ and }\Omega\overline{v_{i}}=i\sigma_{i}\overline{v_{i}},

We find that

i\sigma_{i}\langle v_{j},u_{i}\rangle=\langle v_{j},\Omega^{\top}u_{i}\rangle=\langle\Omega v_{j},u_{i}\rangle=-i\sigma_{j}\langle v_{j},u_{i}\rangle.

Thus,
\langle v_{j},u_{i}\rangle=0
, and by the same computation
\langle\overline{v_{j}},u_{i}\rangle=\delta_{ij}.

In the following, we write
\Sigma_{y}=\Sigma
and introduce
\Sigma_{z}:=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{3},\sigma_{3}).
We thus find that using
\Sigma_{y}^{2}=1
and
\Sigma_{y}^{-1}=\Sigma_{y}=-\Sigma_{y}^{\top}

\Sigma_{y}v_{i}=\frac{i\Sigma_{y}\Omega v_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}=-\frac{\mathcal{H}v_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}=-\frac{\mathcal{H}\Sigma_{y}(\Sigma_{y}v_{i})}{\sigma_{i}}=-\frac{i\Omega^{\top}(\Sigma_{y}v_{i})}{\sigma_{i}}.

This shows that
\Sigma_{y}v_{i}
is also an eigenvector with eigenvalue
i\sigma_{i}
of
\Omega^{\top}
, and we conclude that
\overline{u_{i}}\propto\Sigma_{y}v_{i}.
Thus, we may choose
\overline{u_{i}}:=-\Sigma_{y}v_{i}
, and therefore
\Sigma_{y}\overline{u_{i}}=-\Sigma_{y}^{2}v_{i}=-v_{i}
, and normalize
u_{i}
such that
\langle u_{i},\overline{v_{i}}\rangle=-u_{i}^{\top}\Sigma_{y}\overline{u_{i}}=1.

Defining
Q=(v_{1},\overline{v_{1}},v_{2},\overline{v_{2}})
and
Q^{-1}=(u_{1},\overline{u_{1}},u_{2},\overline{u_{2}})^{\top}
, we find

Q^{-1}\Omega Q=\operatorname{diag}(-i\sigma_{1},i\sigma_{1},-i\sigma_{2},i\sigma_{2}).

We also observe that

\begin{split}Q^{\dagger}&=(\overline{v_{1}},v_{1},\overline{v_{2}},v_{2})^{\top}=(\Sigma_{y}\overline{u_{1}},-\Sigma_{y}u_{1},\Sigma_{y}\overline{u_{2}},-\Sigma_{y}u_{2})^{\top}=(-\overline{u_{1}},u_{1},-\overline{u_{2}},\Sigma_{y}u_{2})^{\top}\Sigma_{y}=-\Sigma_{z}Q^{-1}\Sigma_{y}.\end{split}

We then define
a_{i}:=u_{i}^{\top}X
and
a_{i}^{\dagger}=u_{i}^{\dagger}X
and observe that

\begin{split}[a_{i},a_{j}^{\dagger}]&=\sum_{n,m}(u_{i})_{n}(\overline{u_{j}})_{m}[X_{n},X_{m}]=-\sum_{n,m}(u_{i})_{n}\Sigma_{nm}(\overline{u_{j}})_{m}=\delta_{ij}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}[a_{i},a_{j}]&=\sum_{n,m}(u_{i})_{n}(u_{j})_{m}[X_{n},X_{m}]=-\langle\Sigma_{y}\overline{u_{i}},u_{j}\rangle=\langle v_{i},u_{j}\rangle=0.\end{split}

Defining the operator-valued vector
A=(a_{1},a_{1}^{\dagger},a_{2},a_{2}^{\dagger})^{\top}
, we have, by construction,
A=Q^{-1}X
, and thus
X=QA.
We may thus write the Hamiltonian as:

\begin{split}H_{0}&=X^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}X=A^{\dagger}Q^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}QA=-A^{\dagger}\Sigma_{z}Q^{-1}\Sigma_{y}\mathcal{H}QA=iA^{\dagger}\Sigma_{z}Q^{-1}\Omega QA\\
&=iA^{\dagger}\Sigma_{z}\operatorname{diag}(-i\sigma_{1},i\sigma_{1},-i\sigma_{2},i\sigma_{2})A=A^{\dagger}\Sigma_{z}\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},-\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},-\sigma_{2})A\\
&=A^{\dagger}\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{2})A=2\sigma_{1}(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+1)+2\sigma_{2}(a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{2}+1).\end{split}

Thus, we have found

\operatorname{Sp}(H_{0})=\sigma_{1}(2\mathbb{N}_{0}+1)+\sigma_{2}(2\mathbb{N}_{0}+1).

We thus see that

H_{0,n}=\sum_{j\in[n]}h_{j}=\sum_{j\in[n]}\sum_{k\in[2]}h_{jk}

with
h_{jk}=2\sigma_{k}(a_{jk}^{\dagger}a_{jk}+1)
satisfying

[h_{jk},h_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}]=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
where
j
labels the particle. Hence, the generator
L_{H_{0,n}}
defined by
H_{0,n}
, the Hamiltonian of the non-interacting particle, is a quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
[
14
]
. Combining with the spectral analysis carried in
[
14
]
, we find

Theorem 3.1
.

The associated generator of the Gibbs sampler in
\mathscr{T}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
is a family of quantum Ornstein-Uhlenbeck generators with coefficients
\nu_{-,k}:=|\widehat{f}(-2\sigma_{k})|^{2}
,
\nu_{+,k}:=|\widehat{f}(2\sigma_{k})|^{2}
and
with spectrum

\operatorname{Sp}(L_{H_{0,n}})=-\sum_{k,j}\left\{\ell\left(\frac{\nu_{-,k}-\nu_{+,k}}{2}\right);\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

From here onwards, we assume for simplicity that the unperturbed Hamiltonian
H_{0}
is already in diagonal form, i.e.
H_{0,n}:=\sum_{j\in[n]}\sum_{k\in[d]}h_{jk}
with
\sigma_{k}=1
for all
k
.

3.2.
Finite rank perturbative stability

In order to establish the spectral gap of
L_{H_{n,M}}
, we now show that finite-rank perturbations of the
H_{0,n}
only produce finite-rank corrections in the structure of the
\mathscr{T}_{2}
generator. In the next section, we will show that this is sufficient to preserve discreteness of the spectrum and hence a spectral gap. Our first Lemma below shows that perturbing the Hamiltonian in a finite-dimensional sector does not affect the jump operators on the infinite-dimensional bulk, and only creates a finite-rank correction.

Lemma 3.2

(Finite-rank stability under exponential conjugation)
.

Let
H_{0}
be a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, bounded from below, with discrete spectrum. Let
P
be a finite-rank orthogonal projection such that
[P,H_{0}]=0,

and set
Q:=1-P
. Let
R=PRP
be bounded and self-adjoint, and define

H:=H_{0}+R.

Then
H
is reduced by the decomposition

\mathcal{H}=Q\mathcal{H}\oplus P\mathcal{H},\text{ such that }H=QH_{0}Q\oplus(PH_{0}P+R).

Let
A^{\alpha}
be a possibly unbounded operator such that

P\mathcal{H}\subset D(A^{\alpha})\qquad\text{and}\qquad PA^{\alpha}\text{ extends to a bounded operator on }\mathcal{H}.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that the operators

\begin{split}L_{s}^{\alpha}(H)&:=\sum_{E,E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Sp}(H)}e^{s(E-E^{\prime})}\widehat{f}(E-E^{\prime})\,P_{E}(H)A^{\alpha}P_{E^{\prime}}(H)\text{ and }\\
L_{s}^{\alpha}(H_{0})&:=\sum_{E,E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Sp}(H_{0})}e^{s(E-E^{\prime})}\widehat{f}(E-E^{\prime})\,P_{E}(H_{0})A^{\alpha}P_{E^{\prime}}(H_{0})\end{split}

are well-defined, and denote
L^{\alpha}(H):=L^{\alpha}_{0}(H)
, resp.
L^{\alpha}(H_{0}):=L^{\alpha}_{0}(H_{0})
. Then

L_{s}^{\alpha}(H)=L_{s}^{\alpha}(H_{0})+\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha},\qquad Q\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}Q=0,

and
\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}
is finite rank. In particular, if the conjugated operators

e^{sH}L^{\alpha}(H)e^{-sH},\qquad e^{sH_{0}}L^{\alpha}(H_{0})e^{-sH_{0}}

are well-defined and coincide with the above series expansions, then with
\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}
finite rank

e^{sH}L^{\alpha}(H)e^{-sH}=e^{sH_{0}}L^{\alpha}(H_{0})e^{-sH_{0}}+\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha},\qquad Q\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}Q=0.

Proof.

Since
[P,H_{0}]=0
, the subspaces
P\mathcal{H}
and
Q\mathcal{H}
reduce
H_{0}
. Since
R=PRP
, one has
RQ=QR=0.

Hence
H=H_{0}+R
is block diagonal with respect to the orthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{H}=Q\mathcal{H}\oplus P\mathcal{H},

namely

H=QH_{0}Q\oplus(PH_{0}P+R).

We now describe the spectral projections of
H_{0}
and
H
relative to this decomposition. Since
Q\mathcal{H}
reduces
H_{0}
, we may write

QH_{0}Q=\sum_{\mu\in\operatorname{Sp}(QH_{0}Q)}\mu\,Q_{\mu},

where the
Q_{\mu}
are pairwise orthogonal spectral projections satisfying
Q_{\mu}\leq Q
. On the finite-dimensional space
P\mathcal{H}
, the operator
PH_{0}P
has a spectral decomposition

PH_{0}P=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\nu_{\ell}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell},

where
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}\leq P
, the projections
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}
are pairwise orthogonal, and

\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}=P.

Likewise, since
PH_{0}P+R
acts on the finite-dimensional space
P\mathcal{H}
, it has a spectral decomposition

PH_{0}P+R=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}\Pi_{j},

with
\Pi_{j}\leq P
, pairwise orthogonal, and

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Pi_{j}=P.

It follows that the spectral projections of
H_{0}
are precisely
\{Q_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in\operatorname{Sp}(QH_{0}Q)}\cup\{\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{r},

while the spectral projections of
H
are precisely
\{Q_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in\operatorname{Sp}(QH_{0}Q)}\cup\{\Pi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}.

Indeed, on
Q\mathcal{H}
, the operators
H
and
H_{0}
coincide, whereas on
P\mathcal{H}
the operator
H_{0}
is replaced by
PH_{0}P+R
.
Now expand
L_{s}^{\alpha}(H)
according to this decomposition. Since the spectral projections of
H
are
\{Q_{\mu}\}\cup\{\Pi_{j}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{s}^{\alpha}(H)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu,\nu}e^{s(\mu-\nu)}\widehat{f}(\mu-\nu)\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}Q_{\nu}+\sum_{\mu,j}e^{s(\mu-\lambda_{j})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\lambda_{j})\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j,\mu}e^{s(\lambda_{j}-\mu)}\widehat{f}(\lambda_{j}-\mu)\,\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}Q_{\mu}\qquad+\sum_{j,k}e^{s(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})}\widehat{f}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})\,\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{k}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle L_{s}^{\alpha}(H_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu,\nu}e^{s(\mu-\nu)}\widehat{f}(\mu-\nu)\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}Q_{\nu}+\sum_{\mu,\ell}e^{s(\mu-\nu_{\ell})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\nu_{\ell})\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell,\mu}e^{s(\nu_{\ell}-\mu)}\widehat{f}(\nu_{\ell}-\mu)\,\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}Q_{\mu}+\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}e^{s(\nu_{\ell}-\nu_{\ell^{\prime}})}\widehat{f}(\nu_{\ell}-\nu_{\ell^{\prime}})\,\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell^{\prime}}.

Subtracting these two expressions yields

L_{s}^{\alpha}(H)-L_{s}^{\alpha}(H_{0})=\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu,j}e^{s(\mu-\lambda_{j})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\lambda_{j})\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j}-\sum_{\mu,\ell}e^{s(\mu-\nu_{\ell})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\nu_{\ell})\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j,\mu}e^{s(\lambda_{j}-\mu)}\widehat{f}(\lambda_{j}-\mu)\,\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}Q_{\mu}-\sum_{\ell,\mu}e^{s(\nu_{\ell}-\mu)}\widehat{f}(\nu_{\ell}-\mu)\,\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}Q_{\mu}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j,k}e^{s(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})}\widehat{f}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})\,\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{k}-\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}e^{s(\nu_{\ell}-\nu_{\ell^{\prime}})}\widehat{f}(\nu_{\ell}-\nu_{\ell^{\prime}})\,\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell^{\prime}}.

The
Q
-
Q
block cancels completely, because
H
and
H_{0}
have the same spectral projections on
Q\mathcal{H}
. Therefore

Q\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}Q=0.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}
is finite rank. Since
P\mathcal{H}\subset D(A^{\alpha})
, the operator
A^{\alpha}P
is well-defined on all of
\mathcal{H}
. Because
P\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional, the range of
A^{\alpha}P
is contained in the finite-dimensional space
A^{\alpha}(P\mathcal{H})
. Hence
A^{\alpha}P
is finite rank. Since
\Pi_{j},\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}\leq P
, it follows that

A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j}=(A^{\alpha}P)\Pi_{j},\qquad A^{\alpha}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}=(A^{\alpha}P)\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}

are finite-rank operators. Consequently,

Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j},\qquad Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}

are finite rank as well. Next, by assumption, the densely defined operator
PA^{\alpha}
extends to a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
, which we again denote by
PA^{\alpha}
. Since
\Pi_{j},\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}\leq P
, we have on
D(A^{\alpha})
,

\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}=\Pi_{j}(PA^{\alpha}),\qquad\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}=\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}(PA^{\alpha}).

Hence
\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}
extend continuously to all of
\mathcal{H}
. Their ranges are contained in

\Pi_{j}\mathcal{H}\subset P\mathcal{H},\qquad\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}\mathcal{H}\subset P\mathcal{H},

which are finite-dimensional. Thus
\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}
are finite rank. Therefore

\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}Q_{\mu},\qquad\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}Q_{\mu},\qquad\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{k},\qquad\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell^{\prime}}

are all finite rank. Since there are only finitely many indices
j,k,\ell,\ell^{\prime}
, the last two lines involve only finitely many finite-rank terms. For the mixed terms, note that

\sum_{j}\Pi_{j}=P,\qquad\sum_{\ell}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}=P.

Hence

\sum_{\mu,j}e^{s(\mu-\lambda_{j})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\lambda_{j})\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j}=\Big(\sum_{\mu}e^{s\mu}\widehat{f}(\mu-\lambda_{1})Q_{\mu},\ldots\Big)

is more cleanly viewed term-wise as a sum of operators, each factoring through the finite-dimensional space
P\mathcal{H}
. Indeed, for each fixed
j
,

\sum_{\mu}e^{s(\mu-\lambda_{j})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\lambda_{j})\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j}=\Big(\sum_{\mu}e^{s(\mu-\lambda_{j})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\lambda_{j})\,Q_{\mu}\Big)A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j}.

By well-definedness of
L_{s}^{\alpha}(H)
, this operator is well-defined, and since
A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j}
is finite rank, it is finite rank. Summing over the finitely many
j
shows that

\sum_{\mu,j}e^{s(\mu-\lambda_{j})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\lambda_{j})\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\Pi_{j}

is finite rank. The same argument applies to

\sum_{\mu,\ell}e^{s(\mu-\nu_{\ell})}\widehat{f}(\mu-\nu_{\ell})\,Q_{\mu}A^{\alpha}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell},

and similarly, using that
\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}
are finite rank, to the terms

\sum_{j,\mu}e^{s(\lambda_{j}-\mu)}\widehat{f}(\lambda_{j}-\mu)\,\Pi_{j}A^{\alpha}Q_{\mu},\qquad\sum_{\ell,\mu}e^{s(\nu_{\ell}-\mu)}\widehat{f}(\nu_{\ell}-\mu)\,\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ell}A^{\alpha}Q_{\mu}.

Therefore, every group of terms in
\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}
is finite rank, and hence
\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}
itself is finite rank. Finally, suppose that the conjugated operators
e^{sH}L^{\alpha}(H)e^{-sH}
and
e^{sH_{0}}L^{\alpha}(H_{0})e^{-sH_{0}}
are well-defined and agree with the above spectral expansions. Then, using

e^{sH}P_{E}(H)=e^{sE}P_{E}(H),\qquad P_{E^{\prime}}(H)e^{-sH}=e^{-sE^{\prime}}P_{E^{\prime}}(H),

we obtain

e^{sH}L^{\alpha}(H)e^{-sH}=\sum_{E,E^{\prime}}e^{s(E-E^{\prime})}\widehat{f}(E-E^{\prime})\,P_{E}(H)A^{\alpha}P_{E^{\prime}}(H)=L_{s}^{\alpha}(H),

and similarly

e^{sH_{0}}L^{\alpha}(H_{0})e^{-sH_{0}}=L_{s}^{\alpha}(H_{0}).

The stated identity, therefore, follows from the first part of the proof.
∎

Next, we establish that the finite-rank stability of Lemma
3.2
propagates to the quadratic expressions appearing in the generator
L_{H_{n,M}}
(cf. (
1.8
)). This is crucial since the generator depends on terms such as
(L^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L^{\alpha}
and
L^{\alpha}(L^{\alpha})^{\dagger}
.

Corollary 3.3

(Finite-rank stability of quadratic expressions)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.2
, set
L:=L_{s}^{\alpha}(H)
,
L_{0}:=L_{s}^{\alpha}(H_{0})
, and
\mathcal{R}:=\mathcal{R}_{s}^{\alpha}
, so that
L=L_{0}+\mathcal{R}
with
\mathcal{R}
finite rank. Assume there exists a dense subspace
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{H}
with
\mathcal{D}\subset D(L)\cap D(L_{0})
,
Lx=L_{0}x+\mathcal{R}x
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}
, and such that all expressions below are well-defined on
\mathcal{D}
. If moreover
\operatorname{Ran}(\mathcal{R})\subset D(L^{\dagger})\cap D(L_{0}^{\dagger})
, then on
\mathcal{D}

L^{\dagger}L-L_{0}^{\dagger}L_{0}=L_{0}^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}L_{0}+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}\mathcal{R},

and
K:=L^{\dagger}L-L_{0}^{\dagger}L_{0}
is finite rank on
\mathcal{D}
. Likewise, if
\operatorname{Ran}(\mathcal{R}^{\dagger})\subset D(L)\cap D(L_{0})
, then on every dense subspace
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subset D(L^{\dagger})\cap D(L_{0}^{\dagger})
such that
L^{\dagger}x=L_{0}^{\dagger}x+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}x
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
and all expressions are well-defined,

LL^{\dagger}-L_{0}L_{0}^{\dagger}=L_{0}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}L_{0}^{\dagger},

and
\widetilde{K}:=LL^{\dagger}-L_{0}L_{0}^{\dagger}
is finite rank on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{R}
is finite rank, it is bounded, hence
\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}
is bounded. For
x\in\mathcal{D}
one has
Lx=L_{0}x+\mathcal{R}x
, thus
L^{\dagger}Lx=L^{\dagger}L_{0}x+L^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}x
, and subtracting
L_{0}^{\dagger}L_{0}x
gives
(L^{\dagger}L-L_{0}^{\dagger}L_{0})x=(L^{\dagger}-L_{0}^{\dagger})L_{0}x+L^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}x
. On
D(L^{\dagger})\cap D(L_{0}^{\dagger})
one has
L^{\dagger}=L_{0}^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}
, and since
\operatorname{Ran}(\mathcal{R})\subset D(L^{\dagger})\cap D(L_{0}^{\dagger})
, this yields
L^{\dagger}L_{0}x=L_{0}^{\dagger}L_{0}x+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}L_{0}x
and
L^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}x=L_{0}^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}x+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}x
, hence
L^{\dagger}L-L_{0}^{\dagger}L_{0}=L_{0}^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}L_{0}+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}
on
\mathcal{D}
. Finite-rankness follows since
\operatorname{Ran}(\mathcal{R})
is finite-dimensional and contained in
D(L_{0}^{\dagger})
, so
L_{0}^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}
is finite rank;
\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}L_{0}
is finite rank; and
\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}
is finite rank.

For the second identity, let
x\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. Then
L^{\dagger}x=L_{0}^{\dagger}x+\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}x
, hence
LL^{\dagger}x=LL_{0}^{\dagger}x+L\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}x
, and subtracting
L_{0}L_{0}^{\dagger}x
gives
(LL^{\dagger}-L_{0}L_{0}^{\dagger})x=(L-L_{0})L_{0}^{\dagger}x+L\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}x=\mathcal{R}L_{0}^{\dagger}x+L\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}x
. Since
\operatorname{Ran}(\mathcal{R}^{\dagger})\subset D(L)\cap D(L_{0})
, one has
L\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}x=L_{0}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}x+\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}x
, hence
LL^{\dagger}-L_{0}L_{0}^{\dagger}=L_{0}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}L_{0}^{\dagger}
on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. Again, each term is of finite rank, so
\widetilde{K}
is of finite rank.
∎

We can now combine these two ingredients to study the full perturbed generator
L_{H_{n,M}}
: all modifications remain confined to finite-rank coefficients. We recall that conjugation by
e^{\pm\beta H/4}
leads to the generator on
\mathscr{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
: on
\mathscr{F}:=\operatorname{span}\{|E\rangle\langle E^{\prime}|:E,E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Sp}(H)\}
, denoting
\Gamma_{\tau}(X):=e^{\tau H}Xe^{-\tau H}
and a finite set of bare jumps
\{A^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
,

\displaystyle L_{H}=-i(B_{+,H}\bullet-\bullet B_{-,H})+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\Big(L^{\alpha}_{+,H}\,\bullet\,(L^{\alpha}_{-,H})^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}K^{\alpha}_{+,H}\bullet-\tfrac{1}{2}\bullet K^{\alpha}_{-,H}\Big),

with

\displaystyle B_{H}

\displaystyle:=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{E,F,G\in\operatorname{Sp}(H)}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{\beta(F-E)}{4}\Big)\,\overline{\widehat{f}(G-F)}\,\widehat{f}(G-E)\,P_{F}(A^{\alpha})^{\dagger}P_{G}A^{\alpha}P_{E}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{E,F\in\operatorname{Sp}(H)}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{\beta(F-E)}{4}\Big)\,\,P_{F}L^{\alpha}(H)^{\dagger}L^{\alpha}(H)P_{E}

and

(3.8)

\displaystyle L^{\alpha}_{\pm,H}:=\Gamma_{\pm\beta/4}(L^{\alpha}(H)),\quad K^{\alpha}_{\pm,H}:=\Gamma_{\pm\beta/4}\big((L^{\alpha}(H))^{\dagger}L^{\alpha}(H)\big),\quad B_{\pm,H}:=\Gamma_{\pm\beta/4}(B_{H}).

Analogously, we denote the same operators associated with
H_{0}
by
L_{\pm,0}^{\alpha}
and
B_{\pm,0}
. Invoking Lemma
3.2
, we further denote
L_{\pm,H}^{\alpha}=L_{\pm,0}^{\alpha}+\mathcal{R}_{\pm}^{\alpha}
with
\mathcal{R}_{\pm}^{\alpha}
having finite rank.

Proposition 3.4

(Finite-rank perturbation of the conjugated generator)
.

With the notations of the above paragraph,

L_{\operatorname{pert}}=L_{H}-L_{H_{0}}=-i\big((B_{+,H}-B_{+,0})\bullet-\bullet(B_{-,H}-B_{-,0})\big)+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\big(\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}\big),

where

\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha}\bullet(L_{+,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}+L_{+,0}^{\alpha}\bullet(\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha}\bullet(\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha})^{\dagger},

\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}=\big((L_{-,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,H}^{\alpha}-(L_{-,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,0}^{\alpha}\big)\bullet\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}(x)=\bullet\big(L_{-,H}^{\alpha}(L_{+,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}-L_{-,0}^{\alpha}(L_{+,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}\big).

Moreover, on any common domain where all products are defined,

(3.9)

\begin{split}(L_{-,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,H}^{\alpha}-(L_{-,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,0}^{\alpha}&=(L_{-,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha}+(\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,0}^{\alpha}+(\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha},\\
L_{-,H}^{\alpha}(L_{+,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}-L_{-,0}^{\alpha}(L_{+,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}&=L_{-,0}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha}(L_{+,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}.\end{split}

In particular, all coefficient differences are finite rank under the same range assumptions as in Corollary
3.3
.

Proof.

Insert
\rho=e^{-\beta H/4}xe^{-\beta H/4}
into
\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{f},H}
and conjugate by
e^{\beta H/4}
. The commutator gives
e^{\beta H/4}[B_{H},\rho]e^{\beta H/4}=B_{+,H}x-xB_{-,H}
. The gain term becomes
e^{\beta H/4}L^{\alpha}(H)\rho(L^{\alpha}(H))^{\dagger}e^{\beta H/4}=L_{+,H}^{\alpha}x(L_{+,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}
. For the anticommutator, note
(L_{-,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}=e^{\beta H/4}(L^{\alpha}(H))^{\dagger}e^{-\beta H/4}
, hence

\displaystyle(L_{-,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,H}^{\alpha}=e^{\beta H/4}(L^{\alpha}(H))^{\dagger}L^{\alpha}(H)e^{-\beta H/4}

\displaystyle L_{-,H}^{\alpha}(L_{+,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}=e^{-\beta H/4}L^{\alpha}(H)(L^{\alpha}(H))^{\dagger}e^{\beta H/4}

which yields the displayed form. The same identities hold for
H_{0}
, and subtraction gives the difference formula. Using
L_{+,H}^{\alpha}=L_{+,0}^{\alpha}+\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha}
, expand
(L_{+,H}^{\alpha})x(L_{+,H}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}
to obtain
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}
. Similarly, combining
L_{-,H}^{\alpha}=L_{-,0}^{\alpha}+\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha}
and
L_{+,H}^{\alpha}=L_{+,0}^{\alpha}+\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha}
gives the expansions for
\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}
. The explicit identities for the coefficient differences follow by direct expansion on a common domain where all products are defined. Since
\mathcal{R}_{\pm}^{\alpha}
are finite rank, all purely quadratic terms are finite rank, and the mixed terms are finite rank under the same domain and range assumptions as in Corollary
3.3
.
∎

3.3.
Spectral gap for finite-rank perturbation of quadratic Hamiltonians

Next, we make use of the structural result of Proposition
3.4
in the special case of a Gaussian reference Hamiltonian, i.e.
H_{0}\equiv H_{0,n}
,
H\equiv H_{n,M}=H_{0,n}+W_{n,M}
, and the bare jumps are chosen as
A^{\alpha}:=a_{ij}^{\sigma}
, for
\alpha=(i,j,\sigma)\in[n]\times[d]\times\{\emptyset,\dagger\}
, to show the positivity of the spectral gap of
L_{H_{n,M}}
. The proof uses the combined fact that the unperturbed generator is explicit, and the perturbation inherits a very rigid structure.

First, we can easily verify that the unperturbed filtered jumps introduced in (
3.8
) for
H=H_{0,n}
take the form, for any
\alpha=(i,k,\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle L_{\pm,0}^{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widehat{f}(-2)\,e^{\mp\beta/4}a_{ik}&\sigma=\emptyset\\
&\widehat{f}(2)\,e^{\mp\beta/4}a^{\dagger}_{ik}&\sigma=\dagger\end{aligned}\,\,\,\right..

Moreover, by the detailed balance condition (
1.3
),
\widehat{f}(-2)e^{-\beta/2}=\widehat{f}(2)e^{\beta/2}
, and thus, denoting
\nu_{-}:=|\widehat{f}(-2)|^{2}
,
\nu_{+}:=|\widehat{f}(2)|^{2}
and
n_{ik}=a_{ik}^{\dagger}a_{ik}
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle L_{H_{0,n}}=\sum_{i\in[n]}\sum_{k\in[d]}-\Big(\frac{\nu_{-}+\nu_{+}}{2}(n_{ik}\bullet+\bullet n_{ik})+\nu_{+}\bullet\Big)+\sqrt{\nu_{+}\nu_{-}}\big(a_{ik}\bullet a_{ik}^{\dagger}+a_{ik}^{\dagger}\bullet a_{ik}\big).

Therefore, from the observation made in (
3.3
), we see that

(3.11)

\displaystyle L_{H_{0,n}}\geq\sum_{j\in[n]}\sum_{k\in[d]}\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{+}-\nu_{-})^{2}(n_{jk}\bullet+\bullet n_{jk})\;-\;\nu_{+}(\nu_{-}-\nu_{+})\,\bullet\equiv L_{\operatorname{LB},n}\,.

Next, we consider general non-positive perturbations of the Gaussian generator
L_{H_{0,n}}
of the form

L^{\prime}=L_{H_{0,n}}+L_{\operatorname{pert}}\leq 0,

To prove the existence of a spectral gap for
L^{\prime}
, we use the following lemma with the operator
L_{\mathrm{LB}}
in (
3.4
), along with the many-particle operator
L_{\text{LB},n}=\oplus_{i\in[n]}\oplus_{k\in[d]}L_{\text{LB}}.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\|L_{\operatorname{pert}}(L_{\operatorname{LB},n}-\lambda)^{-1}\|<1

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then
L^{\prime}
has a discrete spectrum and hence is gapped. In particular, it is enough to assume that
L_{\operatorname{pert}}
is

L_{\mathrm{LB},n}
-bounded with relative bound
0
, i.e.
D(L_{\mathrm{LB},n})\subset D(L_{\mathrm{pert}})

and for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
such that

\|L_{\mathrm{pert}}x\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\|L_{\mathrm{LB},n}x\|_{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|x\|_{2},\qquad x\in D(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}).

Proof.

Since
L^{\prime}\leq 0
, it suffices to show that it has a compact resolvent.
For this, we use that by (
3.3
)
L_{H_{0,1}}\geq L_{\text{LB}}
implies
L_{H_{0,n}}\geq L_{\text{LB},n}
. We then define
L_{\text{LB},n}^{\prime}:=L_{\text{LB},n}+L_{\text{pert}}
and have the resolvent identity

(L_{\text{LB},n}^{\prime}-\lambda)^{-1}=(L_{\text{LB},n}-\lambda)^{-1}(1+L_{\operatorname{pert}}(L_{\text{LB},n}-\lambda)^{-1})^{-1}

if
\|L_{\operatorname{pert}}(L_{\operatorname{LB},n}-\lambda)^{-1}\|<1
for some
\lambda.
Thus
L_{\text{LB},n}^{\prime}
has compact resolvent as long as the resolvent of
L_{\operatorname{LB},n}
is defined, since the resolvent of
L_{\operatorname{LB},n}
is compact.
Below, we use the essential spectrum of a self-adjoint operator
(T,D(T))
, denoted
\operatorname{Sp}_{\text{ess}}(T)
, which is the set of real numbers
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
T-\lambda I
is not a Fredholm operator. We recall that an operator is Fredholm if it is closed with a finite dimensional kernel and cokernel. By the monotonicity of operators, we have

\inf\operatorname{Sp}_{\text{ess}}(L^{\prime})\geq\inf\operatorname{Sp}_{\text{ess}}(L^{\prime}_{\operatorname{LB},n})=\infty.

Thus, the spectrum of
L^{\prime}
is purely discrete.

Next, to see the second condition, fix
\varepsilon>0
, and choose
C_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
such that

\|L_{\mathrm{pert}}x\|\leq\varepsilon\|L_{\mathrm{LB},n}x\|+C_{\varepsilon}\|x\|,\qquad x\in D(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}).

For
y\in\mathscr{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
, let
x:=(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}y.

Since
L_{\mathrm{LB},n}
is self-adjoint, we have
x\in D(L_{\mathrm{LB},n})
, and therefore

\|L_{\mathrm{pert}}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}y\|=\|L_{\mathrm{pert}}x\|\leq\varepsilon\|L_{\mathrm{LB},n}x\|+C_{\varepsilon}\|x\|.

Thus

\|L_{\mathrm{pert}}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}y\|\leq\varepsilon\|L_{\mathrm{LB},n}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}y\|+C_{\varepsilon}\|(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}y\|.

Taking the supremum over
\|y\|=1
, we obtain

\|L_{\mathrm{pert}}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}\|\leq\varepsilon\|L_{\mathrm{LB},n}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}\|+C_{\varepsilon}\|(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}\|.

Since
L_{\mathrm{LB},n}
is self-adjoint,

\|(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{|\mu|},

and

\|L_{\mathrm{LB},n}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}\|=\sup_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Sp}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n})}\frac{|\lambda|}{|\lambda-i\mu|}\leq 1.

Hence

\|L_{\mathrm{pert}}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}\|\leq\varepsilon+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{|\mu|}.

Choose
\varepsilon<1
. Then for
|\mu|
sufficiently large we have

\varepsilon+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{|\mu|}<1,

which proves

\|L_{\mathrm{pert}}(L_{\mathrm{LB},n}-i\mu)^{-1}\|<1.

∎

Next, we specialize our discussion to the case of
L^{\prime}=L_{H_{n,M}}
. The following constitutes the main result of this section:

Theorem 3.6
.

The generator
L_{H_{n,M}}
exhibits discrete spectrum and thus
\operatorname{gap}(L_{H_{n,M}})>0
.

Proof.

We start by specializing the structural result of Proposition
3.4
to the present Gaussian unperturbed setting: we recall that
H_{0}\equiv H_{0,n}=nd+2\sum_{i\in[n]}\sum_{k\in[d]}n_{ik}
and
H\equiv H_{n,M}=H_{0,n}+W_{n,M}
, where
W_{n,M}=\Pi_{M}W_{n}\Pi_{M}
is bounded and self-adjoint for
\Pi_{M}:=P_{M}^{\otimes n}
with
[\Pi_{M},H_{0,n}]=0
.
Moreover,

L_{H_{n,M}}-L_{H_{0,n}}=-i\big((B_{+,H_{n,M}}-B_{+,0})\bullet-\bullet(B_{-,H_{n,M}}-B_{-,0})\big)+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\big(\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}\big),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha}\bullet(L_{+,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}+L_{+,0}^{\alpha}\bullet(\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha}\bullet(\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}\equiv\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{(1)}+\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{(2)}+\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}=\big((L_{-,H_{n,M}}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,H_{n,M}}^{\alpha}-(L_{-,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,0}^{\alpha}\big)\bullet

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}=\bullet\big(L_{-,H_{n,M}}^{\alpha}(L_{+,H_{n,M}}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}-L_{-,0}^{\alpha}(L_{+,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}\big).

Moreover, on any common domain where all products are defined,

A_{\alpha}:=(L_{-,H_{n,M}}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,H_{n,M}}^{\alpha}-(L_{-,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,0}^{\alpha}=(L_{-,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha}+(\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}L_{+,0}^{\alpha}+(\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha},

B_{\alpha}:=L_{-,H_{n,M}}^{\alpha}(L_{+,H_{n,M}}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}-L_{-,0}^{\alpha}(L_{+,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}=L_{-,0}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha}(L_{+,0}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}+\mathcal{R}_{-}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{R}_{+}^{\alpha})^{\dagger}.

Next, we verify the relative boundedness condition of Lemma
3.5
. We first argue that for each
\alpha
,
\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}
are both relatively bounded with respect to
L_{\operatorname{LB}}
with relative bound
0
: indeed, since they correspond to left and right multiplication operators with respect to a finite rank operator by Corollary
3.3
, we get directly that the anticommutator parts
A_{\alpha}
and
B_{\alpha}
are all finite rank, and thus for all
x\in\mathscr{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H}_{n})
,

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}x\|_{2}\leq\|A_{\alpha}\|\,\|x\|_{2}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}x\|_{2}\leq\|x\|_{2}\,\|B_{\alpha}\|\,.

The same type of bound also holds for the last summand
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{(3)}
of
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}
. The same argument also holds for the coherent term by (
3.7
).

In contrast to this, the perturbation of the jump operator is still unbounded. The corresponding unbounded contributions
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{(1)}
and
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{(2)}
are of the form
Z_{1}xZ_{2},
where either
Z_{1},Z_{2}
is always a finite rank operator and the other one is proportional to
a_{ik}
or
a_{ik}^{\dagger}
. Fortunately, terms of the latter type are still zero bounded with respect to
L_{\operatorname{LB},n}
, so that Lemma
3.5
can be applied. To show this, we make use of the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}:=\sum_{i\in[n]}\sum_{k\in[d]}n_{ik}\bullet+\bullet n_{ik}\equiv\sum_{i\in[n]}\sum_{k\in[d]}\mathcal{N}_{ik}.

Then, consider for instance a term proportional to
a_{ik}xZ
for some bounded operator
Z
and
x\in D(\mathcal{N}_{ik}^{1/2})
. Then,

\|a_{ik}xZ\|_{2}\leq\|Z\|\,\|a_{ij}x\|_{2}.

Moreover,

\|a_{ik}x\|_{2}^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((a_{ik}x)^{\dagger}(a_{ik}x)\bigr)=\operatorname{Tr}(x^{\dagger}a_{ik}^{\dagger}a_{ik}x)=\operatorname{Tr}(x^{\dagger}n_{ik}x).

Since

\langle x,\mathcal{N}_{ik}(x)\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(x^{\dagger}(n_{ik}x+xn_{ik})\bigr)=\operatorname{Tr}(x^{\dagger}n_{ik}x)+\operatorname{Tr}(x^{\dagger}xn_{ik}),

and both terms on the right are nonnegative, it follows that

\operatorname{Tr}(x^{\dagger}n_{ik}x)\leq\langle x,\mathcal{N}_{ik}(x)\rangle_{2}.

Therefore

\|a_{ik}xZ\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|Z\|^{2}\,\|a_{ik}x\|_{2}^{2}=\|Z\|^{2}\,\langle x,\mathcal{N}_{ik}(x)\rangle\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\|a_{ik}xZ\|_{2}\leq\|Z\|\,\|\mathcal{N}_{ik}^{1/2}(x)\|_{2}\,.

Next, by the scalar inequality
\sqrt{t}\leq\varepsilon t+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}
,
t\geq 0
and functional calculus, since
\mathcal{N}_{ik}\geq 0
,

\|\mathcal{N}_{ik}^{1/2}(x)\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\|\mathcal{N}_{ik}(x)\|_{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\|x\|_{2},\qquad x\in D(\mathcal{N}_{ik}).

Hence

\|a_{ik}xZ\|_{2}\leq\|Z\|\,\|\mathcal{N}_{ik}^{1/2}(x)\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\|Z\|\,\|\mathcal{N}_{ik}(x)\|_{2}+\frac{\|Z\|}{4\varepsilon}\|x\|_{2}.

Finally, since
\mathcal{N}_{ik}\leq\mathcal{N}
, and since
L_{\operatorname{LB},n}=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{+}+\nu_{-})^{2}\mathcal{N}-nd\nu_{+}(\nu_{-}-\nu_{+})\bullet
, we get constants
c_{ik}
and
d_{ik}
such that for any
\varepsilon<1
,

\displaystyle\|a_{ik}xZ\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\,c_{ik}\|L_{\operatorname{LB},n}(x)\|_{2}\,+d_{ik}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,\|x\|_{2}.

The same control can be found for any of the terms
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{(1)}
and
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{(2)}
. Thus, after summation and up to re-normalizing
\varepsilon
accordingly, we find that the conditions of Lemma
3.5
are satisfied, and thus
L_{H_{n,M}}
is gapped.

∎

3.4.
Uniform gaps for weakly interacting gases

In the weakly interacting regime, where
B_{n}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, obtained by choosing coupling strengths
\lvert\alpha_{n,i,j}\rvert=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon/n^{2})
, the generator
L_{\sigma_{E},H_{nM}}
has a positive spectral gap that is bounded uniformly in the particle number
n
. Consequently, in this regime, the algorithm approximates the free energy densities efficiently and uniformly across all particle numbers. For our discussion of uniform gaps, we need a standard observation based on the min-max principle.

Proposition 3.7

(Stability of low eigenvalues under form-small perturbations)
.

Let
A
be a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, bounded from below, with a compact resolvent. Let
C
be a symmetric quadratic form with domain
Q(A)
such that for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, one has

(3.12)

|\langle u,Cu\rangle|\leq\varepsilon\langle u,(A+1)u\rangle,\qquad u\in Q(A).

Then the form sum
A+C
is well-defined, self-adjoint, bounded from below, and has compact resolvent. Moreover, if

\lambda_{1}(A)\leq\lambda_{2}(A)\leq\cdots,\qquad\lambda_{1}(A+C)\leq\lambda_{2}(A+C)\leq\cdots

denote the eigenvalues counted with multiplicity, then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.13)

(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{n}(A)-\varepsilon\leq\lambda_{n}(A+C)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{n}(A)+\varepsilon.

In particular, for the first spectral gap
\mathrm{gap}(A):=\lambda_{2}(A)-\lambda_{1}(A)
,
\mathrm{gap}(A+C):=\lambda_{2}(A+C)-\lambda_{1}(A+C)
, one has

(3.14)

\mathrm{gap}(A)-\varepsilon\bigl(\lambda_{1}(A)+\lambda_{2}(A)+2\bigr)\leq\mathrm{gap}(A+C)\leq\mathrm{gap}(A)+\varepsilon\bigl(\lambda_{1}(A)+\lambda_{2}(A)+2\bigr).

Proof.

By (
3.12
),

(3.15)

(1-\varepsilon)\langle u,Au\rangle-\varepsilon\|u\|^{2}\leq\langle u,(A+C)u\rangle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\langle u,Au\rangle+\varepsilon\|u\|^{2},\qquad u\in Q(A).

Since
\varepsilon<1
, the form of
A+C
is closed and lower bounded on
Q(A)
by the KLMN theorem
[
41
]
, hence it defines a self-adjoint operator bounded from below. Because the form domain is the same as that of
A
, the compactness of the resolvent is preserved.
Now, apply the min-max principle. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{n}(A+C)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\dim V=n\\
V\subset Q(A)\end{subarray}}\ \sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in V\\
\|u\|=1\end{subarray}}\langle u,(A+C)u\rangle.

If we use the upper quadratic form bound (
3.15
), we see that

\lambda_{n}(A+C)\leq\inf_{\dim V=n}\ \sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in V\\
\|u\|=1\end{subarray}}\bigl((1+\varepsilon)\langle u,Au\rangle+\varepsilon\bigr)=(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{n}(A)+\varepsilon,

while the lower quadratic form bound yields

\lambda_{n}(A+C)\geq\inf_{\dim V=n}\ \sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in V\\
\|u\|=1\end{subarray}}\bigl((1-\varepsilon)\langle u,Au\rangle-\varepsilon\bigr)=(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{n}(A)-\varepsilon.

This proves (
3.13
).
To estimate the gap, combine the lower bound for
\lambda_{2}(A+C)
with the upper bound for
\lambda_{1}(A+C)
:

\mathrm{gap}(A+C)=\lambda_{2}(A+C)-\lambda_{1}(A+C)\geq\bigl((1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{2}(A)-\varepsilon\bigr)-\bigl((1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(A)+\varepsilon\bigr).

After rearranging,

\mathrm{gap}(A+C)\geq\mathrm{gap}(A)-\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}(A)+\lambda_{2}(A)+2).

The upper bound follows analogously from the upper bound for
\lambda_{2}(A+C)
and the lower bound for
\lambda_{1}(A+C)
.
∎

Now, by Theorem
2.8
, the free energy densities

f_{\beta}(H_{n}):=\frac{F_{\beta}(H_{n})}{n}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad f_{\beta}(H_{n,M}):=\frac{F_{\beta}(H_{n,M})}{n}

satisfy, for
d\in\{2,3\}
,
\beta>0
, and interaction strengths obeying for some
\gamma\geq 1

(3.16)

|\alpha_{n,i,j}|\lesssim n^{-\gamma}\qquad(1\leq i<j\leq n),

the estimate

\bigl|f_{\beta}(H_{n})-f_{\beta}(H_{n,M})\bigr|\leq C_{\beta}\,n^{2-\gamma}M^{-\frac{1}{4d}}.

In particular, if
\gamma\geq 2
, the right-hand side can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
M
sufficiently large, independently of
n
. We may thus choose
\gamma=2
in the sequel. It is meaningful to study free energy densities, as
F_{\beta}(H_{0,n})=nF_{\beta}(h),
i.e. the densities are the unit-size objects in our scaling.
For our argument, it is convenient to write

H_{n,M}=H_{0,n}+W_{n,M}=H_{0,n}+\Pi_{M}\sum_{i<j}\alpha_{n,i,j}V_{ij}\Pi_{M}\text{ with }V_{ij}(x)=\begin{cases}-\log|x_{i}-x_{j}|&\text{ if }d=2\\
|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-1}&\text{ if }d=3\end{cases}

such that in this scaling
\|W_{n,M}\|
is uniformly bounded in
n
.

Proposition 3.8

(Locality yields an
n^{-1}
bound for the truncated remainder)
.

Fix a site
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and let
A_{i}^{\alpha}
be a local creation or annihilation operator acting only on the
i
-th tensor factor. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define

A_{i}^{\alpha}(t):=e^{itH_{0,n}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{0,n}}.

Assume that

(3.17)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\neq j\leq n\\
t\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\big\|[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]\big\|\leq C_{M},

for some constant
C_{M}<\infty
depending only on
M
. Assume moreover that uniformly in j

(3.18)

\sup_{1\leq i\leq n}\sum_{j\neq i}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|\leq\frac{\kappa}{n}

for some
\kappa>0
. Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},(1+|t|)\,dt)
, we consider
L^{\alpha}_{i}(H):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,e^{itH}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH}\,dt

for
H=H_{0,n}
and
H=H_{n,M}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{M,f}>0
, independent of
n
and
i
, such that

\big\|L_{i}^{\alpha}(H_{n,M})-L_{i}^{\alpha}(H_{0,n})\big\|\leq\frac{C_{M,f}}{n}\text{ with }C_{M,f}:=\kappa C_{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|t|\,|f(t)|\,dt.

If, in addition, for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
the conjugated operators
L_{i,s}^{\alpha}(H):=e^{sH}L_{i}^{\alpha}(H)e^{-sH}

are well-defined for
H=H_{0,n},H_{n,M}
, and if

(3.19)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\neq j\leq n\\
t\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\big\|[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},e^{itH_{0,n}}(e^{sH_{0,n}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-sH_{0,n}})e^{-itH_{0,n}}]\big\|\leq C_{M,s},

then

\big\|L_{i,s}^{\alpha}(H_{n,M})-L_{i,s}^{\alpha}(H_{0,n})\big\|\leq\frac{C_{M,s,f}}{n},\qquad C_{M,s,f}:=\kappa C_{M,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|t|\,|f(t)|\,dt.

Proof.

We first prove the non-conjugated statement. By Duhamel’s formula, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

e^{itH_{n,M}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{n,M}}-e^{itH_{0,n}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{0,n}}=i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-u)H_{n,M}}[W_{n,M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(u)]e^{-i(t-u)H_{n,M}}\,du

for
t\geq 0
, while for
t\leq 0
the same identity holds with the integral taken over
[t,0]
. Hence, in either case,

\big\|e^{itH_{n,M}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{n,M}}-e^{itH_{0,n}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{0,n}}\big\|\leq\int_{0}^{|t|}\big\|[W_{n,M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(\tau)]\big\|\,d\tau.

It is therefore enough to estimate the commutator
[W_{n,M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]
.

Since
A_{i}^{\alpha}
, and thus
A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)
, acts only on the
i
-th tensor factor and
V_{jk}
acts only on the
j
-th and
k
-th factors, we have

[\Pi_{M}V_{jk}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]=0\qquad\text{whenever }i\notin\{j,k\}.

Therefore, only the interaction terms touching site
i
survive:

[W_{n,M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]=\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{n,i,j}\,[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)].

Taking norms and using (
3.17
), we obtain

\big\|[W_{n,M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]\big\|\leq\sum_{j\neq i}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|\,\big\|[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]\big\|\leq C_{M}\sum_{j\neq i}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|.

By (
3.18
),

\big\|[W_{n,M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]\big\|\leq\frac{\kappa C_{M}}{n},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Substituting this into the Duhamel estimate yields

\big\|e^{itH_{n,M}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{n,M}}-e^{itH_{0,n}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{0,n}}\big\|\leq\frac{\kappa C_{M}}{n}\,|t|.

Multiplying by
|f(t)|
and integrating over
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we get

\displaystyle\big\|L_{i}^{\alpha}(H_{n,M})-L_{i}^{\alpha}(H_{0,n})\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|\,\big\|e^{itH_{n,M}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{n,M}}-e^{itH_{0,n}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{0,n}}\big\|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa C_{M}}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|t|\,|f(t)|\,dt.

This proves the first claim.

For the conjugated statement, we proceed along the same lines and define

B_{i,s}^{\alpha}:=e^{sH_{0,n}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-sH_{0,n}}.

Applying exactly the same argument to
B_{i,s}^{\alpha}
in place of
A_{i}^{\alpha}
, and using the assumption (
3.19
), gives the analogous estimate

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\Big(e^{itH_{n,M}}B_{i,s}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{n,M}}-e^{itH_{0,n}}B_{i,s}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{0,n}}\Big)\,dt\Big\|\leq\frac{\kappa C_{M,s}}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|t|\,|f(t)|\,dt.

Under the stated identification of the conjugated operators with these time-averaged expressions, this is precisely the desired estimate for the difference

L_{i,s}^{\alpha}(H_{n,M})-L_{i,s}^{\alpha}(H_{0,n}).

∎

In the next Lemma we directly verify the conditions of the previous result for our setting.

Lemma 3.9

(Verification of the local commutator bound for truncated Coulomb interactions)
.

Let

\mathcal{H}_{n}:=\bigotimes_{r=1}^{n}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad H_{0,n}:=\sum_{r=1}^{n}h_{r},

where each
h_{r}
is a d-dimensional harmonic oscillator, with
d\in\{2,3\}
acting on the
r
-th tensor factor.
For
1\leq i<j\leq n
, let

V_{ij}(x):=\begin{cases}-\log|x_{i}-x_{j}|&\text{ if }d=2\\
|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-1}&\text{ if }d=3.\end{cases}

Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and let
A_{i}^{\alpha}
be a local creation or annihilation operator acting on the
i
-th tensor factor. Then, for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C_{M}<\infty
, independent of
n
,
i
,
j
, and
t
, such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\neq j\leq n\\
t\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\bigl\|[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]\bigr\|\leq C_{M}.

More precisely, one may take

\begin{split}C_{M}&=\Bigl\|\Bigl[(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M}),\,A^{\alpha}\otimes 1\Bigr]\Bigr\|\leq 2\,\bigl\|(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})\bigr\|\,\|A^{\alpha}P_{M}\|\\
&\lesssim\bigl\|(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})\bigr\|\ \sqrt{M+1}.\end{split}

Proof.

Since
H_{0,n}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}h_{r}
and
A_{i}^{\alpha}
acts only on the
i
-th tensor factor, its free Heisenberg evolution is determined entirely by the one-particle oscillator on that factor:

A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)=e^{itH_{0,n}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-itH_{0,n}}=e^{ith_{i}}A_{i}^{\alpha}e^{-ith_{i}}.

For the harmonic oscillator, the creation and annihilation operators are eigenoperators of the Heisenberg dynamics. Thus, there exists
\theta_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)=e^{i\theta_{\alpha}t}A_{i}^{\alpha},

and therefore
\bigl\|[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]\bigr\|=\bigl\|[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}]\bigr\|.

It follows that it is enough to estimate the commutator at time
t=0
.

We now use the tensor-product structure. Since
V_{ij}
acts only on the
i
-th and
j
-th variables and
\Pi_{M}=P_{M}^{\otimes n}
, the compressed interaction
\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M}
acts trivially on all tensor factors except the
i
-th and
j
-th. Likewise,
A_{i}^{\alpha}
acts only on the
i
-th tensor factor. Hence, after identifying the
i
- and
j
-factors with two copies of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one may write

\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M}=I_{\widehat{ij}}\otimes\bigl((P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})\bigr),

and

A_{i}^{\alpha}=I_{\widehat{ij}}\otimes(A^{\alpha}\otimes 1),

where
I_{\widehat{ij}}
denotes the identity, on
\operatorname{ran}(\Pi_{M})
, on all tensor factors other than
i
and
j
. Consequently,

[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}]=I_{\widehat{ij}}\otimes\Bigl[(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M}),\,A^{\alpha}\otimes 1\Bigr].

Taking norms yields

\bigl\|[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}]\bigr\|=\Bigl\|\Bigl[(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M}),\,A^{\alpha}\otimes 1\Bigr]\Bigr\|.

The right-hand side depends only on the two-particle compressed operator and is therefore independent of
n
,
i
, and
j
. This proves the claimed reduction.

It remains to show that this norm is finite. Set
E_{M}:=P_{M}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Since
P_{M}
is a spectral projection of the harmonic oscillator onto energies
\leq M
, the space
E_{M}
is finite-dimensional. Therefore,
E_{M}\otimes E_{M}
is finite-dimensional as well, and

(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})

is a bounded operator on
E_{M}\otimes E_{M}
. Moreover,
A^{\alpha}P_{M}
is bounded because it acts on the finite-dimensional space
E_{M}
. It follows that the commutator

\Bigl[(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M}),\,A^{\alpha}\otimes 1\Bigr]

is bounded on
E_{M}\otimes E_{M}
, and hence has finite norm. Defining

C_{M}:=\Bigl\|\Bigl[(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M}),\,A^{\alpha}\otimes 1\Bigr]\Bigr\|,

we conclude that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\neq j\leq n\\
t\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\bigl\|[\Pi_{M}V_{ij}\Pi_{M},A_{i}^{\alpha}(t)]\bigr\|\leq C_{M}.

To see where the explicit bound comes from, we write

X_{M}:=(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M}).

Then

[X_{M},A^{\alpha}\otimes 1]=X_{M}(A^{\alpha}\otimes 1)-(A^{\alpha}\otimes 1)X_{M},

so by the triangle inequality,

\|[X_{M},A^{\alpha}\otimes 1]\|\leq\|X_{M}(A^{\alpha}\otimes 1)\|+\|(A^{\alpha}\otimes 1)X_{M}\|\leq 2\|X_{M}\|\,\|A^{a}P_{M}\|.

This proves

C_{M}\leq 2\,\bigl\|(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})V_{ij}\,(P_{M}\otimes P_{M})\bigr\|\,\|A^{\alpha}P_{M}\|.

Finally, for a one-particle creation or annihilation operator
A^{\alpha}
, the standard ladder-operator bounds on the oscillator basis imply

\|A^{\alpha}P_{M}\|\lesssim\sqrt{M+1},

which gives the last claim.
∎

We have thus established that each perturbation of the
j
-th Lindblad operator is a finite-rank operator with a norm bound
\mathcal{O}(\sum_{i\neq j}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|).
This allows us to conclude that the spectral gap of the unperturbed Lindbladian associated with
H_{0,n}
persists, as long as the sum of their norms does not exceed the size of the gap.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\alpha_{n,i,j}\lesssim\varepsilon(M)/n^{2}
for
\varepsilon(M)>0
sufficiently small and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};(1+|t|)\ dt)
, then the Lindbladian
L_{H_{n,M}}
associated with the Hamiltonian
H_{n,M}
has a uniform gap in
n
.

Proof.

When studying the Lindbladian associated with
H_{n,M}
, the perturbations to the anti-commutator and commutator terms in the Lindbladian associated with
H_{0,n}
involve products of creation and annihilation operators with the finite rank operator difference of the respective Lindblad operators; see (
3.9
). Since the norm of this difference for the
j
-th Lindblad operator is, by Proposition
3.8
and Lemma
3.9
, bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\sum_{i\neq j}|\alpha_{n,i,j}|)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon(M)/n)
. Summing
n
many such terms still leads only to a perturbation of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon(M))
, which does not close the unit-size gap of the unperturbed Lindbladian by choosing
\varepsilon(M)
sufficiently small.

It is only the perturbation of the jump operator that involves unbounded perturbations, but we may proceed as in the proof of Theorem
3.6
:

Denoting the perturbation obtained from the jump operators of the
j
-th particle Lindblad operator by
C_{j}
, we see that each of them satisfies the relative bound with respect to
A_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{+}-\nu_{-})^{2}\{N_{j},x\}
for
\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon/n)

(3.20)

|\langle u,C_{j}u\rangle|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\langle u,A_{j}u\rangle+\delta\|u\|^{2}.

This computation makes up the last part of the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
Then, from
[
14
, Proof of Thm. 6.3]
, we may use that
Ax=L_{\operatorname{LB}}^{\oplus_{i=1}^{n}}x+n\nu_{+}(\nu_{-}-\nu_{+})x\leq L_{H_{0,n}}
, we have

\sum_{i}|\langle u,C_{i}u\rangle|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\langle u,L_{H_{0,n}}u\rangle+\left(\delta n+\varepsilon^{\prime}n\nu_{+}|\nu_{-}-\nu_{+}|\right)\|u\|^{2}

such that for
\eta=\max\{\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta n+\varepsilon^{\prime}n\nu_{+}|\nu_{-}-\nu_{+}|\}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
we have

\sum_{i}|\langle u,C_{i}u\rangle|\leq\eta\langle u,(L_{H_{0,n}}+1)u\rangle=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\langle u,(L_{H_{0,n}}+1)u\rangle).

We thus see that we can apply Proposition
3.7
to the self-adjoint Lindbladian on the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators, as the unperturbed operator
L_{H_{0,n}}
exhibits a uniform gap, as it is just a
n
-fold direct sum, to conclude the existence of a uniform gap for the perturbed one as well.
∎
