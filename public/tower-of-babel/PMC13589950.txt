Supramolecular strategy in traditional Chinese medicine processing: Mechanistic insights mediated by self-assembly
Authors: Wenqi Song, Huan Gong, Huijun Wang, Doudou Huang, Lianna Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The processing of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) is a critical process that upholds tradition and determines medicinal efficacy. The mechanisms underlying changes in TCM efficacy resulting from processing are currently a key focus of TCM research. Advances in supramolecular chemistry have significantly advanced the understanding of the mechanisms governing the efficacy of TCM complex chemical systems. Both the active ingredient basis and the adjuvant materials used in processing can be regarded as supramolecular units. Processing guides the TCM supermolecules to change structure through covalent forces and self-assemble non-covalently, forming soft matter aggregates that enable selective recognition, transformation, and assembly of components. These aggregates autonomously steer the entire system toward enhanced efficacy and reduced toxicity, thereby revealing the TCM's true active substance foundation. This paper highlights that the dynamic chemical system formed via supramolecular self-assembly during TCM processing can be likened to an “imprinting template” with memory function, which underlies the TCM “holistic” effects—characterized by multiple components, pathways, and targets. This study aims to provide valuable insights into TCM processing based on supramolecular development, bridging the gap between supramolecular science and TCM. Consequently, it offers profound inspiration for designing next-generation smart drug delivery systems, responsive biomaterials, and adaptive chemical systems.

Introduction

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) is a treasure trove of rich pharmaceutical wisdom and clinical experience. Processing, as its core technique, plays a crucial role in enhancing efficacy, reducing toxicity, modifying medicinal properties, and improving stability before clinical application1. For centuries, traditional processing methods such as stir-frying, steaming, and boiling have largely relied on the transmission of experiential knowledge and the individual skills of artisans, and lack systematic and mechanistic scientific explanations. However, a fundamental scientific question remains unresolved: What changes occur in the material basis of medicinal substances during processing? Does an autonomous regulatory mechanism exist? How does this mechanism influence or even determine the transformation pathways of active components, their bioavailability, and ultimately their therapeutic efficacy2, 3, 4? Unravelling this mechanism holds profound academic value and is critically important for advancing the standardization and modernization of TCM, as well as for developing novel biofunctional materials based on the intelligent assembly of natural molecules5. Therefore, understanding the structural evolution and functional regulatory mechanisms during the processing of TCM at the molecular and supramolecular levels has become a frontier focus and breakthrough direction in TCM research6,7.

In recent years, a significant shift in the research perspective has emerged: TCM decoctions are no longer viewed merely as physical mixtures of active small molecules but are increasingly understood as supramolecular entities formed through self-assembly of multiple active components, excipients, and natural macromolecules. These entities exhibit distinct hierarchical structures and dynamically ordered aggregation patterns, fundamentally defining the functional states and biological effects of TCM formulas8, 9, 10, 11, 12. Rapid growth in self-assembly significantly enriches the types of fundamental chemical building blocks, typically including atoms, molecules, and macromolecules. Where atoms are covalently bonded units and do not directly participate in supramolecular assembly themselves. Supramolecular units are “functional structural blocks” formed by the self-assembly of these chemical building blocks. Supramolecular units in TCM processing can be divided into two major categories: active pharmaceutical ingredients and adjuvant materials. Their essence during processing lies in dynamic composite structures rather than static ones13, 14, 15, 16, 17. Within this framework, the processing step is no longer viewed as a simple thermal reaction but rather as a highly directed “supramolecular template editor.” By regulating exogenous parameters such as temperature, moisture, and time, it guides drug components to self-assemble along specific pathways. It is against this backdrop that the novel concept of “supramolecular imprinting” emerges. Inspired by molecular imprinting principles in materials science, self-assembly is a governing principle through which materials spontaneously organize themselves into ordered arrangements13, 14, 15, 16, 17. We propose that during processing, energy input via physicochemical conditions guides the supramolecular constituents of TCM to change the supermolecule's structure and create an environment through covalent chemical reactions, self-assemble through either the synergistic effects of multiple noncovalent interactions, such as hydrogen bonding, π–π stacking, hydrophobic interactions, and electrostatic forces, resulting in the formation of dynamic soft matter aggregates. From a supramolecular physicochemical perspective, the complex condensed matter system formed after TCM processing—existing between solid and fluid states—can be regarded as a category of TCM soft matter. It is typically self-organized via noncovalent interactions (often preceded by specific covalent transformations) from single-component or multi-component bioactive substances, whose structure, rheological properties, and functional responses are highly dependent on the external microenvironment (e.g., temperature, pH, and ionic strength)18. The properties of these aggregates enable highly selective recognition, transformation, and directional assembly of target molecules19, 20, 21. The imprint template functions as a structure-directing processing template or an active ingredient-specific binding template. Self-assembly, traditionally viewed as a spontaneous and template-free process, can be analyzed retrospectively from a result-oriented perspective to reveal key molecular interactions and molecular patterns22, 23, 24, 25. In TCM, self-assembly leads to the formation of soft matter aggregates containing active pharmaceutical ingredients derived from processing excipients or intermediates generated during processing. The “template-filler” complementarity principle from molecular imprinting provides the most direct analogy and methodological framework for understanding key molecular recognition and directional alignment in TCM self-assembly22, 23, 24, 25. This process modifies the active structures, enabling direct transport or specific binding to relevant target sites to exert pharmacological effects, offering a fresh perspective for contemporary TCM processing research26,27. Ultimately, this mechanism autonomously guides the TCM system to enhance biological efficacy and reduce toxicity, clarifying the essence of “enhancing efficacy and reducing toxicity” in TCM processing at the supramolecular level28,29.

This review aims to establish a new framework for understanding TCM processing through the lens of functional supramolecular materials science. We first define the fundamental supramolecular units—active ingredients and excipients—that serve as structural building blocks (Section 1). We subsequently explore the dynamic process of supramolecular self-assembly, focusing on the introduction of imprinting templates and elucidating both the non-covalent interactions that drive the assembly into soft matter aggregates and the covalent chemical reactions that create the assembly conditions (Section 2). Our core discussion elucidates the autonomous transformation mechanism of TCM self-assembly: enhancing efficacy through molecular recognition and reducing toxicity through structural transformation. To bridge the gap from phenomenological description to mechanistic understanding of TCM self-assembly, we introduce the imprinting template as a conceptual link from “natural discovery” to “artificial design” (Section 3). Finally, we discuss a toolkit of advanced, multi-dimensional analytical methods for investigating these complex systems, spanning fields from metabolomics to gut microbiota models (Section 4). By redefining ancient processing techniques as a precise, nature-inspired approach for creating functional supramolecular assemblies, this review innovatively bridges millennia-old traditional practices with cutting-edge materials science. We anticipate that the principles of autonomous, template-guided self-assembly revealed in TCM processing will provide profound insights for designing next-generation smart drug delivery systems, responsive biomaterials, and adaptive chemical systems. Pharmacological basis

The pharmacological basis of TCM typically refers to natural active components such as polysaccharides, lignin derivatives, flavonoids, triterpenoids, and alkaloids. These compounds exhibit self-assembly properties, forming complexes through supramolecular interactions or undergoing concentration changes during processing. The pharmacologically active constituents of TCM exhibit distinct characteristics of complexity, holistic integration, and dynamic interactions. TCM formulations contain hundreds or even thousands of chemically diverse components that coexist within a complex system. These constituents interact to form intricate “chemical component networks.” Multiple active components exert holistic effects on the human body through multitarget synergistic interactions within this network. Individual components often fail to represent or replicate the overall therapeutic efficacy of TCM, as their material foundations collectively form an organic whole to exert their effects. The diverse physicochemical properties and complex structures of these pharmacologically active components form the basis for their molecular recognition characteristics31. Processing significantly affects the types, content, and proportions of active components, causing dynamic changes that alter the pharmacologically active material foundation and consequently influence therapeutic efficacy.

Macromolecule

The backbone structure of polysaccharides is rich in hydrophilic groups, facilitating their self-assembly with other structural units through noncovalent interactions under specific conditions32. After steaming, the polysaccharide conformation of Polygonatum sibiricum Red. (PS) gradually transforms from flexible chains into a more compact globular structure, indicating enhanced interchain interactions during the self-assembly process33. By examining key indices, Yu et al.34 and Xu et al.35 reported that the contents of four kinds of active ingredients, such as chromogenic ketones and polysaccharides, were greater in freshly cut Radix Saposhnikoviae slices from the origin compared with traditional processed slices.

Small molecule

Triterpenoid compounds possess unique rigid skeletons, chiral centres, and multiple modification sites, making them crucial building blocks for supramolecular complexes. They can fold in diverse ways across different media. Reports in the literature indicate that all triterpenoids are naturally occurring nanoscale molecules, offering novel insights into their self-assembly during processing36. Research has indicated that 18β-glycyrrhetinic acid, the primary active component of liquorice, is rigid and can interact in various solutions to form nanospheres or fibrous structures with nanoscale diameters37,38. Birch alcohol can undergo phase separation in organic solvents and organic aqueous solutions, gradually forming three-dimensional floral structures with nanometre and micrometre diameters from the fibre network39,40.

Lignin, which possesses a natural three-dimensional network structure, can also self-assemble into micelles. Glycyrrhizic acid is a key active component in liquorice. Owing to its relatively high molecular weight and polarity, it has low lipophilicity, resulting in poor oral bioavailability that impairs therapeutic efficacy. Glycyrrhizic acid and sodium deoxycholate molecules can self-assemble into micelles at a molar ratio of 1:2, thereby enhancing the oral bioavailability of glycyrrhizic acid41.

In alkaloid research, processing significantly affects the types, contents, and proportions of their chemical constituents. Wang et al.42 determined the contents of six alkaloids in slices of Epiphyllum officinale using different processing treatments by UPLC. The content of monoester alkaloids in black smooth slices obtained from dried Epiphyllum officinale was 0.246%, which was higher than that in black smooth slices obtained from traditional cutting (0.018%).

Similarly, in studies of flavonoid components, Li et al.43 compared the effects of cutting and fresh and traditional processes on the flavonoid composition of Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi tablets and reported that the contents of baicalin and baicalin were greater in fresh tablets than in traditional tablets. The amount of baicalin, however, was greater in traditional tablets. These findings provide support for the quality control of tablets of Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi with different processing methods.

Adjuvant material composition

TCM-processing adjuvant material, as a core element of traditional pharmaceutical techniques, refers to supplementary materials added during herbal processing that serve auxiliary functions beyond the primary medicinal ingredients. They are primarily categorized into liquid adjuvants (such as wine, honey, vinegar, saltwater, suet oil, ginger juice, liquorice juice, and black soybean juice) and solid adjuvants (such as wheat bran, rice, and sand). These adjuvants are not mere additives but exert multidimensional synergistic effects through physicochemical interactions with the herbal constituents. Their core mechanism lies in components that alter solvent environments, modulate pH levels, or directly participate in binding reactions, thereby regulating the formation and function of supramolecular structures within active herbal compounds. Overall, adjuvants primarily facilitate the dissolution of supramolecular components or modify supramolecular structures during TCM processing, ultimately influencing therapeutic efficacy.

Liquid materials

Among the numerous liquid excipients, alcohol plays a pivotal role because of its unique composition (ethanol, esters, organic acids, and volatile flavour compounds) and properties. As an excellent organic solvent, alcohol dissolves most water-soluble or insoluble substances in medicinal herbs. Its high permeability allows it to penetrate deep into plant tissues, facilitating the displacement, diffusion, and dissolution of plant constituents. More importantly, components in alcohol can enhance recognition between active ingredients and promote the formation of dynamic hydrogen bond networks. Like alcohol, honey is another liquid excipient with a mechanism of action closely related to supramolecular science. As a natural deep eutectic solvent, honey primarily consists of glucose, fructose, sucrose, and water and exhibits significant supramolecular self-assembly properties. In TCM processing, honey is often regarded as a “molecular chaperone”. By protecting active ingredients and increasing their solubility and bioavailability, it ultimately synergistically modulates the overall efficacy of herbal formulations44. Vinegar is consumed as a food condiment worldwide, especially in Chinese cuisines, and it has medicinal uses because of its physiological effects. Different types of vinegars contain organic acids, aldehydes, esters, alcohols, phenols, flavonoids, and ligustrazine. The weakly acidic environment of vinegar is key to its efficacy, as it induces conformational changes in the colloidal components of medicinal materials and promotes the self-assembly of active ingredients under acidic conditions4,45. Salt is among the excipients that are commonly used for the processing of TCM. It mainly contains NaCl, MgCl2, CaSO4, Zn, and Ba, among others, which can affect certain components of TCM, and the influence caused by the heating temperature can destroy or decompose some components during salt processing46. Pharmacokinetic analysis has revealed that after salt processing, the bioavailability of multiple active components in Achyranthes roots significantly increases47. To address the poor solubility of active components in certain medicinal herbs, specialized excipients are employed to enhance their bioavailability. Through noncovalent interactions, suet oil promotes the formation of self-assembled micelles for the flavonoids in Folium Epimedii48. Medicinal extracts such as ginger juice, liquorice juice, and black soybean juice not only alter the properties of the primary drug through chemical or physical interactions but also often have synergistic effects with it through multiple pathways49.

Solid materials

Solid materials are typically used to assist in stir-frying. Although not chemically involved, these solid adjuvants physically interact with treatment agents. Unlike liquid adjuvants, solid adjuvants are primarily used to assist the stir-frying process. They typically do not directly participate in chemical reactions but influence herbs through physical interactions. For example, during the processing of stir-fried Atractylodes lancea (Thunb.) DC bran, high temperatures can cause the decomposition of volatile components such as atractylone. At this point, bran plays a crucial physical protective role: it adsorbs a portion of the volatile oils. More significantly, components within the bran, such as polysaccharides and proteins, can preferentially adsorb the low-polarity constituents of volatile oils through hydrogen bonding or hydrophobic interactions. This phenomenon effectively minimizes the loss of these valuable components during the high-temperature stir-frying process50.

Supramolecular self-assembly in TCM processing: From units to aggregates

Recent studies have increasingly recognized that the processing of TCM involves the dynamic transformation of complex systems composed of supramolecular entities—specifically, supramolecular self-assembly30. In this context, self-assembly can be harnessed to fabricate functional carriers designed for the encapsulation or protection of active ingredients. The resultant soft matter aggregates function as a “molecular code”, deciphering the bioactivity of TCM. Analyzing self-assembly in TCM processing using imprint templating technology

Self-assembled imprinting template technology introduces a novel paradigm for molecular-level manipulation in TCM processing. Unlike conventional molecular imprinting, self-assembly in TCM represents a “spontaneously emerging” microscopic behavior. This process relies on physicochemical interactions among various components within the system without specific template guidance, aligning more closely with the concept of supramolecular “self-assembly.” The intentional introduction of the seemingly unrelated “molecular imprinting template” concept into discussions on “TCM self-assembly” is not intended to conflate the two, but rather to propose an analogy model and precision-analytical research framework to address fundamental challenges in the study of TCM self-assembly. Molecular imprinting is an excellent analogy model that clearly shows how a template molecule can direct the architecture of functional monomers through accurate intermolecular interactions leading to changes in structure and function. This thinking model for the present work may be extended to proactively and purposefully exploring how processing induced supramolecular interactions follow to dictate the structural reorganization and functional enhancement in TCM self-assembly106, 107, 108.

In TCM processing, even simple excipient-free thermal treatment can induce molecular imprinting template effects through supramolecular interactions. For instance, stir-frying Paeonia lactiflora Pall. without adjuvants significantly increased the contents of paeoniflorin, ethyl gallate, and 1,2,3,4,6-pentagalloylglucose and enhanced anti-inflammatory activity via intermolecular interactions51. Polysaccharides, as key active components, undergo structural remodeling and self-assembly during processing, forming functional imprint templates52,53. For example, in hawthorn, polyphenols and pectin-domain polysaccharides self-assemble into natural nanoparticles during drying, improving stability and antioxidant activity70. Similarly, honey-processed Astragalus membranaceus (Fisch.) Bge. polysaccharides exhibit reduced branching and smoother chain conformations, enhancing intestinal mucosal protection, while structural changes during stir-frying with honey can expose functional groups that strengthen binding to immune receptors (Fig. 2B)71. Steaming Polygonatum polysaccharides increases galacturonic acid content and drives self-assembly into specific supramolecular conformations with antioxidant sites, forming an “imprint template” during the process72. Alkaloids and specific glycosides also undergo processing-induced transformations guided by supramolecular imprinting templates. For instance, aconitine converts into less toxic derivatives during sand-frying or steaming, possibly through molecular conformation adjustments that form detoxification templates84. Liquorice processing of Tetradium ruticarpum improves solubility and alters alkaloid profiles via hydrogen bonding or π–π stacking, enhancing efficacy and reducing toxicity54. Rice wine-steaming of Cistanche deserticola Y. C. Ma promotes the formation of specific glycoside structures while inhibiting others, suggesting template-guided selective stabilization. Similarly, saponin components are optimized through supramolecular imprinting template effects56. Processing of Ligustri lucidi Fructus (FLL) by steaming after rice wine immersion altered the compositional ratio between phenylethanoid glycosides and iridoid glycosides, an outcome facilitated by the imprinting template effect toward directional enrichment of components with similar polarity. The hydroxyl and glycosyl groups of these glycosides further interact through noncovalent forces, enhancing their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities. During rice wine steaming, macromolecular polysaccharides and proteins can act as molecular imprinting templates, guiding the formation of stable complexes with specific small-molecule active components such as glycosides, thereby improving their in vivo absorption efficiency and targeting specificity (Fig. 2C)59,60. Repeated steaming of ginseng (Panax ginseng C. A. Mey.) produces rare saponins and secondary ginsenosides such as Rg3, which can form stable complexes with polysaccharides and phenolic compounds, enhancing antitumor activity and bioavailability75. The formation of supramolecular complexes of ginsenosides in red ginseng improves lipid metabolism regulation78. Different processing methods for Panax notoginseng (Burk.) F. H. Chen create distinct imprint templates through hydrothermal or dry-heat interactions, influencing antianaemic effects81. The supramolecular imprinting template effect is also observed across other component categories, including lignans, flavonoids, anthraquinones, organic acids, and volatile oils. For example, wine-steaming of Schisandra chinensis can promote lignan self-assembly into templates that enhance anti-asthmatic activity73, while adsorption treatment of Cassia obtusifolia L. seed extracts demonstrates targeted removal of anthraquinones via intermolecular interactions (Fig. 2D)58. In Angelica sinensis (Oliv.) Diels, polyphenols in Huangjiu can form stable complexes with active components via π–π stacking, slowing degradation and preserving bioactive compounds61. Vinegar or alcohol stir-frying of Curcuma longa L. increases extraction yields and component content through excipient-active component self-assembly, potentially facilitating targeted binding or permeability62. Similarly, alcohol and heat treatment of Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi can induce self-assembly into imprinting templates that preferentially bind and release anti-migraine components via a lock-and-key effect, thereby improving bioavailability63.

Therefore, the self-assembly behavior of TCM components during processing, regardless of polysaccharides, alkaloids, saponins or otherwise-ethers, can be depicted by supramolecular imprinting template effects. Although this assembly occurs spontaneously through nonvalent interactions, the concept of molecular imprinting provides an analogy model to decipher the role of specific processing conditions to induce molecular reorganization, stabilize beneficial conformations, and enhance the biological activities. This strategy provides a more accurate mechanistic picture of TCM processing that links spontaneous self-assembly to designed molecular recognition systems.

Noncovalent formation mechanism of soft matter aggregates

Processing-derived energy input modulates aggregate formation

Processing methods provide the energy input for macromolecular-small-molecule assembly, whereas the actual driving force for self-assembly resides in non-covalent interactions.

Zhang et al.64 examined the variations in bioactive components during the steaming process. This can be because steaming inhibits enzyme activity and reduces molecular hydrolysis, thereby altering intermolecular interactions, promoting the formation of more stable supramolecular aggregates, and enhancing synergistic effects. Lipid-soluble constituents in peach kernel oil can bind to sterols via van der Waals forces, synergistically enhancing their anti-atherosclerotic activity65. Steaming can induce the rearrangement of saponin molecules at high temperatures, resulting in the formation of lipid-soluble aggregates of GRg5 and Rk1, thereby affecting their solubility and bioavailability79. Wu et al.109 reported that changes in temperature and time during the stir-fry process directly affect the composition and structure of Maillard reaction products (MRPs). By regulating reaction conditions, MRPs with specific supramolecular properties can be synthesized in a targeted manner, thereby achieving multicomponent encapsulation and delivery, minimizing component degradation.

Co-assembly of adjuvants and active ingredients for enhanced bioavailability

Simultaneously, during aggregate formation, the recognition of adjuvant materials and active pharmaceutical ingredients serves as a driving force, enhancing bioavailability and promoting synergistic therapeutic effects through aggregate interactions.

Kong et al.110 reported that honey synergistically enhances intestinal absorption through the formation of aggregates through molecular interactions. Detoxification through encapsulation and conversion of toxic components

The transformation and encapsulation of toxic components by excipients during the formation of soft matter aggregates hold significant breakthrough potential. By embedding toxic components, their absorption can be slowed, resulting in sustained release and enhanced efficacy. The toxic alkaloids in Aconitum (Aconitum carmichaelii Debeaux.) are classified into three types: diester diterpene alkaloids (DDAs), monoester diterpene alkaloids (MADs), and nonesterified diterpene alkaloids (NDAs). DDAs exhibit the strongest toxicity, and processing methods can convert DDAs into MDAs to reduce toxicity. Zheng et al.82 investigated the detoxification mechanism of honey-processed Aconitum. Honey-processed Aconitum generates supramolecular aggregates in which the chemical transformation of alkaloid constituents serves as the primary detoxification mechanism, while the physical encapsulation of MDAs by the honey matrix enhances bioavailability and enables sustained release.

Structural remodeling of aggregates and functional optimization

Structural changes in soft matter aggregates also play a significant role in altering their biological activity. During high-temperature processing, temperature variations cause macromolecular aggregates of polysaccharides to undergo structural remodelling, thereby altering their bioavailability. Massa Medicata Fermentata (MMF) is a fermented food with therapeutic effects. Liu et al.68 extracted and purified two polysaccharides from different types of medicinal slices of Massa Medicata Fermentata (SMMF and CMMF). High temperatures cause the polysaccharide backbone to break, leading to the reorganization of intermolecular forces. The pores within the polysaccharides become denser and more irregular, with reduced pore size and increased wall thickness, resulting in the formation of smaller, looser aggregates of soft matter. These changes reflect how the thermal motion of molecular chains at high temperatures drives the rearrangement of aggregates into more compact supramolecular packing patterns. Liu et al.69 isolated two acidic polysaccharides (SMMFAP and CMMFAP). High temperatures disrupt the original hydrogen bond network, leading to the dissociation and restructuring of soft matter aggregates, which can form functional aggregates through dynamic noncovalent interactions, endowing stir-fried shenqu with enhanced spleen-tonifying activity. During steaming, the molecular weight of PS polysaccharides (PSPs) increases sharply after the fourth steaming cycle, indicating that high-temperature and high-pressure conditions promote the formation of larger aggregates through noncovalent interactions among polysaccharide chains. The polysaccharide conformation gradually shifts from flexible chains to a more compact globular structure, suggesting enhanced interchain interactions during self-assembly. The 5-hydroxymethylfurfural (5-HMF) generated during steaming and colour transformation (white → red → black) can further modulate biological activity through interactions between Maillard reaction products and polysaccharide aggregates (Fig. 4A)33.

Maillard reaction

Research has indicated that the Maillard reaction is a key chemical process in TCM processing. This nonenzymatic browning reaction, also known as the carbonyl-amine reaction111,112. The Maillard reaction involves a series of complex reactions between amino compounds (amino acids, peptides, proteins, etc.) and carbonyl compounds (reducing sugars, ketones, aldehydes, etc.), ultimately yielding Maillard reaction products (MRPs) and covalent complexes. The formation of these covalent complexes constitutes a significant chemical modification. Not only do they encapsulate active components, but more importantly, they confer novel physicochemical properties upon the products—such as pronounced amphiphilicity (hydrophilic–lipophilic balance). This characteristic lays the foundation for subsequent supramolecular self-assembly113.

The specific MRPs generated by the Maillard reaction constitute a crucial component of the pharmacologically active substances in processed TCM. Liu et al.85 reported that the content of Maillard reaction products and their antioxidant activity in hawthorn undergo systematic changes during different processing stages: Antioxidant activity first decreases but then increases as the degree of processing intensifies. This modulation can reflect a complex dynamic of material transformation during processing. Together, these elements form the foundation of the medicinal efficacy observed in later stages of processing.

In aqueous environments, these amphiphilic MRPs spontaneously self-assemble into supramolecular soft matter aggregates—such as micellar structures or more ordered hierarchical assemblies—via noncovalent interactions such as hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions. This self-assembly represents a dynamic, cooperative supramolecular organization process. The resulting supramolecular complex significantly enhances the system effects of oil emulsifying capacity, solubility and stability, and can protect and effective delivery the active ingredients. The mechanism by which Aconitum processing reduces toxicity is analogous. Its alkaloids react with components in excipients to form insoluble complexes or precipitates, which similarly self-assemble into supramolecular aggregates via noncovalent forces86. Therefore, the essence of processing for detoxification and enhanced efficacy relies on the dual stabilizing mechanisms of the Maillard reaction and supramolecular self-assembly: covalent reactions such as the Maillard reaction provide molecular building blocks with specific functions, whereas supramolecular self-assembly utilizes noncovalent forces to organize these building blocks into functional soft matter systems with enhanced physicochemical properties and biological functions—such as solubilization, stabilization, and detoxification. The significant compositional changes observed during the processing of medicinal materials such as Rehmannia glutinosa (Gaertn.) Libosch., Panax ginseng C. A. Mey., and processed Polygonum multiflorum—e.g., amino acid depletion and the generation of MRPs such as maltol, DDMP, and 5-HMF—along with the resulting variations in therapeutic efficacy, serve as a vivid illustration of this synergistic interaction between covalent reactions and supramolecular organization. Together, these factors increase the overall quality and potency of TCM91,92.

The complex products generated by the Maillard reaction can function as supramolecular units or undergo supramolecular self-assembly with other components in TCM systems through noncovalent interactions113. This self-assembly process can profoundly influence the solubility, stability, and biofilm permeability of MRPs, as well as their ultimate tissue distribution and patterns of bioactive expression. Consequently, the overall pharmacodynamic foundation and mechanisms of action of processed TCM at a more refined level have been shaped and enriched.

Hydrolysis and decomposition reaction

During the processing of traditional Chinese medicine, hydrolytic and decomposition reactions, together with dynamic changes in supramolecular assemblies, affect both the transformation pathways of active components and the evolution of pharmacological effects. The four major groups of alkaloids in Aconitum roots are DDAs, MADs, NDAs, and lipo-diterpenoid alkaloids (LDAs)114. DDAs undergo supramolecular-mediated stepwise hydrolysis in hydrothermal environments: Water molecules form transient assemblies around the ester group via hydrogen-bond networks, promoting acetyl group cleavage at the C8 position to yield MADs. The benzoic acid group at the C14 position subsequently undergoes further thermally driven dissociation to form NDAs. This directed deesterification process fundamentally relies on the dynamic rearrangement of supramolecular assemblies, enabling the stepwise degradation of toxic components87,88.

The transformation of saponin components during processing follows a synergistic supramolecular–hydrolytic mechanism. Saponins are composed of sapogenins and one or more glycosyl groups. During stir frying, steaming, or boiling, the hydrolysis of deglycosides occurs, resulting in the production of aglycones and saccharides, which change the efficacy and reduce the toxicity of the material. The amphiphilic nature of saponins promotes self-assembly into micellar structures, potentially accelerating hydrolysis through the localized concentration of reactive groups within these soft matter aggregates. Owing to their amphiphilic nature, the toxic components in Phytolacca, Phytolaccasaponin H, and the saponins in Polygala spontaneously assemble into micelles during steaming. Their hydrophobic microdomains concentrate reactive groups, significantly accelerating glycosidic bond cleavage. The secondary saponins and aglycones produced by hydrolysis not only exhibit reduced toxicity but also alter their supramolecular interaction patterns with biological membranes because of changes in molecular polarity96, 97, 98, 99, 100. The processing of flavonoid glycosides follows a similar pattern. During Epimedium brevicornu Maxim. processing, the lipid phase environment provided by mutton fat promotes glycosidic hydrolysis, converting trisaccharide glycosides into disaccharide glycosides and monosaccharide glycosides. This directed deglycosylation significantly enhances bioavailability by reducing steric hindrance and improving the ability of active components to form transient supramolecular complexes with intestinal membrane components. The greater ease with which monosaccharide and disaccharide glycosides cross biological barriers compared with trisaccharide glycosides stems precisely from their optimized compatibility with the supramolecular assembly of membrane lipids101,102.

Several studies have shown that ginsenosides are not hydrolyzed in the stomach when taken orally. Only a small amount of the ginsenosides is absorbed directly through the small intestine in its original form. The main types of ginsenosides that can be absorbed into the blood vessels and produce medicinal effects are rare saponins and the glycosides after hydrolysis115, 116, 117. The processing mechanism of ginsenosides also supports this theory. During steaming, the layered supramolecular structure by plant matrices, such as starch, can offer a reaction microenvironment for saponin hydrolysis. This enables the original saponins to undergo directional deglycosylation at the C20 position, converting them into the rare saponin Rg3. Subsequently, Rg3 undergoes further deglycosylation at the C3 position to yield Rh293,94. The bioavailability advantage of these low-glycosylated saponins essentially stems from their greater capacity to integrate into natural supramolecular transport systems such as bile salt micelles. Conversely, the low absorption rate of native saponins in the gastrointestinal tract demonstrates that their polysaccharide moieties hinder their effective participation in lipid-mediated supramolecular transport processes95.

In TCM processing, the hydrolysis and decomposition reaction network dynamically regulated by supramolecular assemblies involves transient molecular aggregates formed in aqueous or lipidic media. These aggregates drive direct hydrolysis by concentrating reactive groups, altering local microenvironments, and lowering energy barriers. Concurrently, the loss of sugar chains or ester groups in the hydrolysis products alters their amphiphilicity, thereby optimizing supramolecular interactions with biological membranes and transport carriers. This progression ultimately achieves the processing objectives of reducing toxicity and enhancing efficacy.

Ester exchange reaction

In studies on the mechanisms of enhancing efficacy and reducing toxicity in TCM processing, ester exchange reactions have been revealed as key chemical pathways driving the targeted transformation of active components.

Pharbitidis Semen (PS) is usually stir-fried to enhance its therapeutic efficacy before use in clinical practice. Li et al.103 determined the levels of 7 phenolic acids in raw PS (RPS) and stir-fried PS (SPS). Stir-frying significantly induces ester exchange reactions that trigger isomer conversions. These changes create a “lock-and-key” supramolecular recognition pattern, enabling multitarget synergistic effects in the treatment of nephritis. Another transformation pathway of toxic alkaloids can involve the replacement of the acetyl group at the C8 position by the fatty acyl group to produce nontoxic lipoalkaloids, which is referred to as the transesterification reaction89,90. Ester exchange reactions serve as both the molecular engine for optimizing the structure–activity relationships of pharmacologically active compounds and the pivotal hub for modifying the structures of toxic substances.

Oxidation reaction

As key chemical reaction mechanisms, oxidation reactions play a central role in regulating the toxicity–activity balance of TCMs during processing.

Dihydroindole-type alkaloids are not only therapeutically effective components but also toxicants in Maqianzi (the dried seeds of Strychnos nux-vomica L.). Heating can transform dihydroindole-type alkaloids, such as strychnine, brucine, vomicine, and novacine, into their oxidative derivatives, which are less toxic104. During this transformation process, supramolecular soft matter aggregates can directionally guide the molecular self-assembly of oxidation products through dynamic noncovalent interactions. This supramolecular engineering strategy effectively stabilizes the oxidized conformation while preserving the structural integrity of the alkaloid skeleton, thereby synergistically achieving the dual objectives of toxicity reduction and activity retention.

Isomerization reaction

Isomerization reactions typically drive the structural rearrangement of toxic compounds during processing to regulate toxicity.

The toxic components of Jingdaji (the dried roots of E. pekinensis Rupr) are terpenoids, while the active components are mainly polyphenols. The structures of various terpenoids in Jingdaji are transformed during vinegar processing, which includes bond hydrolysis, lactone ring opening, esterification, oxidation to hydroxyl groups, and dehydration of hydroxyl groups to double bonds. The structural modifications can involve dynamic interactions with naturally occurring supramolecular soft matter aggregates within the plant matrix, including self-assembled phytochemical complexes or solvent-mediated molecular clusters formed during processing. After vinegar processing, the more toxic terpenoids are converted into less toxic components, probably through supramolecular encapsulation effects that stabilize reactive intermediates or alter molecular accessibility. Notably, the acetic acid environment in vinegar processing can facilitate the reorganization of hydrogen-bonded networks and π–π stacked assemblies within the herbal matrix, altering the spatial confinement and energy barriers for these chemical transformations. This supramolecular mechanism could enhance the selective conversion of toxic terpenoids while preserving bioactive polyphenols through differential molecular recognition mechanisms105.

Self-regulating mechanism of TCM efficacy before and after processing based on self-assembly

The mechanism of TCM processing essentially explains the underlying changes in potency enhancement and toxicity reduction within its therapeutic effects118. As a directed self-assembly mechanism, imprinting templates provide a supramolecular perspective to elucidate the postprocessing mechanisms of therapeutic efficacy.

Enhancement mechanism: molecular recognition of active ingredients

During the processing of TCM, partial volatilization of excipients or alterations in active components allows self-assembly to be regarded as generating a complementary “imprint template” for molecular recognition. This process enhances the therapeutic efficacy of the drug and serves as the foundation for information processing at the supramolecular level119.

Optimized target binding via molecular conformation stabilization

Notably, processing-induced alterations in molecular conformation can directly influence target binding efficiency.

High-pressure distillation can enhance anti-liver fibrosis effects by modifying the molecular conformation of Cornus officinalis Siebold & Zucc. components. Changes in polyphenolic and cycloaromatic glycoside contents can result in the formation of more stable supramolecular complexes through supramolecular imprinting template effects, thereby improving the binding efficiency to AMPK/SIRT3 targets120. Gao et al.121 explore the alleviating effect of steamed Panax pseudoginseng (SPN) on RA and its mechanism. Steaming increases the components that can form supramolecular complexes through intermolecular interactions, synergistically enhancing their binding capacity to target sites such as the kidneys and bone marrow. Conformation stabilization is merely the first step in supramolecular assembly, whereas “imprinting” manifests in the synergistic effects of biological systems. Lu et al.122 examined the physiochemical structure and biological activity of the polysaccharides of Rehmannia glutinosa during nine steps of processing. Specific polysaccharide structures can serve as “templates” to bind more effectively with biological targets such as inflammatory factors and cancer cell receptors. Gallic acid from Terminalia chebula can form stable complexes with aconitine via precise π–π stacking interactions. This recognition event does not merely result in complexation; at the interface of the formed soft matter aggregates, the assembled components can cooperatively bind to multiple sites on the TRPV1 channel. Ginsenosides undergo more complex supramolecular assembly during steam processing: Ding et al.116 reported that the steam processing of ginseng roots dynamically modulates the ginsenoside content while promoting the polymerization of sugars or the formation of high-molecular-weight complexes with other components, such as amino acids and nucleotides. Flavonoids in peach kernels can form stable complexes with sterols through π–π stacking or hydrogen bonding. Enhanced bioactivity through supramolecular structural remodeling of macromolecules

Polysaccharides, proteins, and other excipients form complexes with drug components through hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions, enhancing the molecular recognition capability between active ingredients and biological targets.

The steam of wine process forms a “printing template”-like structure that enhances the stability of antifibrotic components. High temperature and pressure can alter the molecular conformation of components such as polysaccharides and flavonoids, resulting in the formation of supramolecular complexes that are more readily recognized by target cells, thereby enhancing therapeutic efficacy125. As a processing medium, alcohol can promote the dissolution or transformation of certain components through solvation while simultaneously inhibiting the formation of undesirable components via supramolecular interactions. Different processing methods significantly influence the antioxidant activity of Cistanche deserticola Y. C. Ma polysaccharides by regulating their supramolecular structure. Hot-air drying and high-pressure steaming increase activity through flexible structures and exposed functional groups, whereas the yellow wine components in wine-processed Cistanche deserticola Y. C. Ma can act as template molecules, inducing the directed aggregation of polysaccharide chains to form recognition sites associated with antioxidant activity126. The processing of steamed rice can alter the molecular conformation or stability of the cycloarene ether glycoside components in Rehmannia glutinosa through the synergistic effect of high temperatures and rice, thereby forming specific supramolecular imprinting templates and optimizing their antioxidant activity127.

Attenuated mechanism: Structural transformation and dynamic regulation of toxic components

In TCM, the most direct processing mechanism involves regulating the molecular state of toxic components through physical, chemical, or biological methods. This approach reduces their toxic content while preserving or enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of active ingredients. The core of this process lies in the use of the selective recognition function of supramolecular imprinting templates to either decrease the concentration of toxic components or convert them into less toxic or nontoxic substances—rather than simply eliminating them128,129. This precise regulation at the supramolecular level not only preserves the therapeutic properties of medicinal herbs that “fight poison with poison” but also achieves a dose‒response separation of toxicity through molecular restructuring.

Toxicity attenuation through directed molecular transformation

In modern TCM processing, the theoretical framework for inducing structural transformations—such as hydrolysis or oxidation—of toxic components through temperature or adjuvants to generate low-toxicity derivatives has been thoroughly validated in the detoxification processes of numerous representative toxic medicinal materials.

Shanglu (the roots of Phytolacca acinosa Roxb. or P. americana L.) is often stir-fried with vinegar. In this process, the primary inflammatory toxic component, esculentoside H, undergoes a selective transformation into the diuretic active component esculentoside A through a supramolecular imprinting template-mediated conformational change, thereby reducing toxicity and enhancing therapeutic efficacy130. This transformation originates from the formation of a dynamic hydrogen-bond network between acetic acid molecules in vinegar and the hydroxyl group of the saponin, resulting in the construction of a specific molecular cavity that directs the removal of the toxic moiety. Li et al.131,132 reported that during the processes of steaming, decoction, and sand-frying of aconite, the active components tannic acid and polyphenols in auxiliary materials can serve as templates. Through noncovalent bonds, these compounds selectively bind to toxic alkaloids, forming supramolecular complexes that reduce their bioavailability. Clinically, Aconitum medicinal materials must undergo a complex processing process that includes soaking, steaming, and boiling with pharmaceutical excipients1,133. Their high toxicity limits their clinical application134. Acidic or alkaline conditions during processing, or enzymatic reactions, can induce specific conformational changes in molecules, forming imprinting sites with recognition functions; ester-bond hydrolysis hydrolyses highly toxic DDAs into low-toxicity MDAs and then into nontoxic NDAs, and in addition to hydrolysis pathways, pyrolysis from DDAs into MDAs also occurs135, 136, 137, ADA pharmacodynamic components have much lower toxicity due to the absence of ester bonds compared with other diterpenoid alkaloids138, 139, 140.

Detoxification via supramolecular encapsulation and sequestration

Through temperature control during the processing or modification of auxiliary material composition, supramolecular assemblies are formed. By adsorption and encapsulation, a dynamic equilibrium system between toxic and efficacious components is established.

Cangerzi (the dried fruits of Xanthium sibiricum Patr.) is generally stir-fried until the colour turns yellow before being applied in the treatment of nasosinusitis and headache. Research has indicated that the supramolecular imprinting template effect induced by high temperatures during stir-frying significantly reduces the content of hepatotoxic components atractyloside and carboxyatractyloside while disrupting the tertiary structure of toxic proteins. This mechanism can be related to the formation of porous adsorption templates through polysaccharide carbonization driven by heat, which selectively captures small-molecule toxins141, 142, 143. Owing to the extreme toxicity of Maqianzi, in Uyghur medicine, Maqianzi are boiled with milk to reduce the content of strychnine and brucine, among other bipyridyl alkaloids, through the synergistic effect of milk protein-SMIT (Sodium-Dependent Myo-Inositol Transporter). Whey protein can form host–guest complexes with alkaloids via its β-sheet conformation, thereby blocking the gastrointestinal absorption of toxins144. Liu et al.145 demonstrated that the stir-frying process reduces toxicity while potentially increasing specific biological activities of Bombyx batryticatus and that the porous structure of wheat bran reduces mycotoxin residues in silkworm pupae through physical adsorption. High temperatures, humidity, and black bean additives during the nine-steaming and nine-sun-drying process of Polygonum multiflorum (PM) can induce natural macromolecular polysaccharides and proteins to form supramolecular imprinting templates. These templates specifically bind anthraquinones, reducing their free concentrations. Repeated steaming and sun-drying enhance template stability, capturing toxic components through intermolecular forces and lowering their bioavailability146,147.

During processing, the acidic constituents of Terminalia chebula form supramolecular complexes with toxic aconitine alkaloids via non-covalent interactions. This assembly fundamentally transforms the toxicant's bioactivity: firstly, it neutralizes aconitine's direct toxicity by shielding its reactive motifs. Secondly, the self-assembled aggregate acts as a sustained-release carrier, significantly delaying the transient receptor stimulation of TRPV1 channels that would otherwise trigger intense calcium influx and cytotoxicity. This spatiotemporal regulation results in weakened calcium signaling and markedly reduced apoptotic effects148 (Fig. 6B). Similarly, glycyrrhetinic acid can form host-guest complexes with toxic DDAs from raw A. hemsleyanum, altering their conformation and membrane permeability to mitigate cardiotoxicity149. These processes highlight a dynamic equilibrium characteristic of supramolecular systems, where non-covalent interactions precisely control the distribution and interaction kinetics of toxic components with biological targets to achieve detoxification while preserving efficacy150, 151, 152.

Enzyme inactivation to block the generation of toxic substances

During processing, enzyme activity can also be disrupted by imprinting templates, limiting enzyme–substrate contact to achieve a dynamic equilibrium between toxicity and therapeutic components, thereby reducing toxicity. Xu et al.153 compared the activity of emulsin, the content of amygdalin, and the toxicity of different Persicae Semen concoctions and reported that Persicae Semen inactivates amygdalin after blanching, preventing the loss of amygdalin due to the enzymatic reaction and reducing the toxicity of Persicae Semen; this process can be regarded as a “molecular imprinting” effect. Through thermal processing and peeling, the active sites of enzymes are disrupted, which results in the formation of a template that selectively retains amygdalin, thereby stabilizing the active components. Simultaneously, the processing alters the intermolecular interactions within the peach kernels by peeling or solvent extraction, enabling the targeted separation of toxic components while preserving the beneficial substances153 (Fig. 6C). Hydrolysis of amygdalin produces HCN, which is the primary source of toxicity. During processing, supramolecular templates can restrict the binding of β-d-glucosidase to amygdalin through imprinting channel effects154,155.

“Multidimensional combination” explores advanced methods of TCM processing

The modernization of TCM processing necessitates elucidating the pharmacological basis and mechanisms within the complex systems formed post-processing. The direct action mode (molecular level) provides the foundational layer, employing classical methods to trace individual component transformations and linear target interactions. Pharmacokinetics exemplifies this by studying the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of active components157, as seen in the heat-induced hydrolysis of glycyrrhizic acid to the highly bioavailable glycyrrhetinic acid during stir-frying, aiding in determining optimal dosages158, 159, 160, 161, 162. Toxicology systematically analyses how processing influences toxicity, with modern techniques like LC–MS and multiomics revealing dynamic patterns and establishing a scientific evaluation system for “reducing toxicity while preserving efficacy”128. Simulated processing at the component level simplifies complex processes to reveal transformation patterns163, such as salt-roasting promoting the conversion of neomangiferin to mangiferin, potentially via ion-induced supramolecular rearrangement164. Bioeffect testing quantitatively characterizes biological potency, using indicators like xanthine oxidase inhibitory activity to assess efficacy directly165, 166, 167.

The indirect action mode (system level) expands to systemic regulation and network synergy. Metabolomics, an emerging field, investigates endogenous metabolite changes168,169, identifying biomarker clusters—like gastrodin for steamed Gastrodia elata Bl.—and mapping metabolic network shifts to reflect holistic organismal responses170. Network pharmacology constructs “component–target–pathway–disease” networks to decipher multi-target synergy, revealing how processing-induced soft matter aggregates can reduce hepatotoxicity, target affinity, or how salt-processing can enhance blood–brain barrier permeability171,172. Gut microbiota research explores this critical “microbial organ”, demonstrating how processing alters TCM component interactions with gut microbes and enzymes, influencing bioavailability and modulating microbial communities and host metabolic networks173, 174, 175, 176, as shown with processed Rheum palmatum L. and Er Shen Wan177.

Crucially, the supramolecular assembly mode (structural level) bridges the gap, revealing how non-covalent interactions drive self-assembly into ordered structures during processing, forming key “imprinting templates” that induce a “lock-and-key effect” for enhanced targeting. This dimension underpins findings across others: it is a key toxicological mechanism where assemblies with excipients inhibit toxic bond hydrolysis (as with aconitine alkaloids) or form stable complexes reducing absorption (as with tannic acid in Euphorbia lathyris)178, 179, 180; it is central to metabolomics and gut microbiota effects, where processing restructures supramolecular interaction networks, dictating component release and interaction efficiency with microbial enzymes, leading to differentiated metabolic outcomes181, 182, 183; and it explains simulated processing at the fraction level and excipient synergy, where components form complex assemblies (e.g., saponins with liquorice juice glycyrrhizic acid) regulating degradation rates or stepwise deglycosylation through dynamic restructuring under energy perturbations184, 185, 186. The integration of multidisciplinary approaches—pharmacokinetics, toxicology, metabolomics, simulated processing (at both component and fraction levels), gut microbiota research, network pharmacology, and bioeffect testing—when viewed through the unifying lens of supramolecular self-assembly, thoroughly reveals the link between material transformations and biological effects. This understanding propels TCM processing from empirical practice towards standardized, modernized approaches based on molecular ordered assembly, ensuring enhanced safety and efficacy.

Conclusions and perspectives

This review systematically summarizes the research progress on supramolecular self-assembly that increases efficacy and reduces toxicity during TCM processing, with a focus on the chemical composition, pharmacological activity, and modern supramolecular chemistry. As a core processing technique in traditional Chinese medicine, this paper proposes that processing can essentially be regarded as an active assembly process based on the replication of supramolecular imprinting templates and the ordered aggregation of soft matter. Although this field remains in the mechanism exploration phase and is still distant from comprehensive industrial application, the supramolecular perspective provides a crucial scientific rationale for modernizing TCM processing. Its significance manifests in five key aspects. (1) Supramolecular imprinting templates precisely identify and immobilize active components within complex systems, significantly enhancing their stability and targeted release capabilities through synergistic effects via multicomponent, multitarget, and multipathway “lock-and-key relationships”. (2) The formation mechanism of soft matter aggregates elucidates the dynamic characteristics of multicomponent synergistic self-assembly in TCM, linking the chemical reactions of active substances at the microscopic level to macroscopic patterns of therapeutic efficacy. (3) Supramolecular self-assembly profoundly interprets the scientific essence of “reducing toxicity while enhancing efficacy” in TCM processing: it achieves precise therapeutic amplification through conformational optimization, multitarget synergy, and bioavailability enhancement, establishing a dynamic equilibrium between toxicity reduction and efficacy enhancement via structural transformation of toxic components, encapsulation-controlled release, and enzyme activity inhibition. (4) Chemical reactions—including the Maillard reaction, directed hydrolysis, and isomerization—collectively reshape the material basis of drug efficacy, further regulating assembly behaviour and biological effects. (5) By integrating multiomics technologies with innovative simulated processing models, we progressively decipher the cross-scale correlation between “components-assembly structures-functions” in TCM self-assembly, advancing the transformation from traditional experience to scientific theory.

Conversely, significant bottlenecks persist in elucidating the mechanisms underlying TCM processing, with numerous fundamental scientific questions awaiting in-depth exploration. Currently, substantial gaps exist in our understanding of the behavioural mechanisms and information transmission patterns of supramolecular assembly during processing. Key scientific questions include the following: What is the scientific basis and necessity for processing TCM? How do chemical transformations during processing couple with supramolecular self-assembly? How can the “potency enhancement and toxicity reduction” mechanism be unified at both the molecular and supramolecular levels?

To systematically explore the underlying mechanisms of TCM processing and address the aforementioned challenges, it is essential to integrate advanced multidisciplinary research strategies and methodological tools. Future breakthroughs should focus on three dimensions: Theoretically, a supramolecular omics database for TCMs should be constructed to quantify the energy distribution and dynamic imprinting effects of noncovalent networks while leveraging AI to predict optimal assembly pathways. Technologically, in situ real-time monitoring systems (e.g., synchrotron-based X-ray scattering) should be developed to establish new quality control standards based on supramolecular parameters, and advanced models such as organ-on-a-chip systems should be used to evaluate transbiological barrier delivery behaviour. At the application level, we must design targeted biomimetic delivery systems (e.g., lipid-flavonoid self-assembled nanocarriers), decipher the supramolecular antagonistic mechanisms behind contraindications such as the “Eighteen Antagonisms and Nineteen Incompatibilities (Shibafan-Shijiuwei in TCM)”, interpret the necessity of using processed medicinal materials in TCM prescriptions, and reinterpret TCM processing theories through the language of supramolecular chemistry. Through interdisciplinary collaboration and technological integration, we aim to understand the intrinsic mechanism of TCM processing from a supramolecular perspective. This approach will propel TCM research from “empirical inheritance” to “mechanism-driven” innovation, infusing TCM processing modernization with fresh scientific substance and developmental momentum.
