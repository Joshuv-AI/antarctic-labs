 Executive Summary
The U.S. Department of Energy Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility’s
Precipitation Best-Estimate (PrecipBE) Value-Added Product integrates multiple precipitation
datastreams, accounting for data quality and instrument limitations, to deliver comprehensive
per-precipitation event properties alongside ancillary ARM data set data. PrecipBE bundles all valid
surface rainfall samples into artificial intelligence (AI)-ready tabular and time-series formats, reporting
bundle means and uncertainty ranges. This per-event structure provides an insightful and easy-to-use
resource for researchers analyzing precipitation characteristics.

1.0 Introduction
 Precipitation measurements are critical for understanding aerosol-cloud interactions (ACI) and serve as
 crucial benchmarks in earth system model (ESM) evaluation. Detailed case studies using surface
 precipitation data often require temporally evolving precipitation rate and accumulation data. These time
 series data serve as target quantities (benchmarks) for model simulations or analytical models. ESM
 evaluation studies, on the other hand, often rely on bulk statistics or data subsets and therefore use
 isolated precipitation-event statistics after conditioning on quantities such as surface temperature.

 The ARM User Facility operates multiple types of precipitation-sensing instruments, including impact,
 video, and laser disdrometers, as well as tipping and weighing buckets. Each instrument tends to have
 higher sensitivity and/or better accuracy at certain precipitation conditions. However, the availability of
 independent studies evaluating the performance of precipitation instruments under strict laboratory
 conditions is still scarce. Moreover, comprehensive analyses of precipitation errors as a function of
 various background conditions (high winds, low temperatures, etc.) and deployment configurations, let
 alone snowy conditions, is still limited and require additional research. In the interim, however, deciding
 how to determine the “true” precipitation properties or to weight different ARM instrument samples based
 on the current literature is prone to unpredictable errors and biases. Therefore, straightforward statistics
 combining data from measurements collected (per deployment) ostensibly provide the best estimates of
 precipitation event properties (onset and ending, accumulation, precipitation rates, etc.). Here, we
 describe the Precipitation Best Estimate (PrecipBE) Value-Added Product (VAP). PrecipBE streamlines
 both process understanding and model evaluation studies using ARM precipitation data. While currently
 generated only for rainfall events, planned VAP enhancements include integrating solid-phase
 observations to produce equivalent snowfall event data sets. Additional information about PrecipBE can
 be found in a recent journal article (Silber et al. 2026).

2.0 Processing and Data Sets
 PrecipBE includes two datastreams, elaborated on below, streamlining both process understanding and
 model evaluation studies using ARM precipitation data. The first datastream (precipbetseries) provides
 time series (evolving) precipitation data, whereas the second datastream (precipbestats) includes per-event
 statistics in an easy-to-use, one-dimensional (tabular) format. The VAP processing, which is performed
 per precipitation event, leverages existing ARM measurement capabilities, depending on instrument
 availability (per deployment; see Table 1 in Silber et al. 2026) . That is, all valid surface precipitation
 samples are integrated into event characterization and processing. Here, “valid” refers to samples that
 passed quality control (QC) tests, do not exhibit unlikely values (see Table 1), and are not associated with
 data quality reports (DQRs) labeled as “Incorrect”. If one or more samples in a precipitation event
 identified with a given instrument does not pass one or more of these conditions, the entire precipitation
 event for that instrument is excluded from measurement bundling and additional processing of
 PrecipBE’s final data. Because processing depends on DQR availability:
 1. c-level data sets that are integrated into the precipBE processing are mapped to their parent
 datastreams from which DQRs are pulled, i.e., we effectively introduce DQR inheritance. For
 example, when Video Disdrometer Quantities (VDISQUANTS) and/or Laser Disdrometer Quantities

 1

 (LDQUANTS) VAP data are available, the PrecipBE algorithm pulls DQRs from the video
 disdrometeor and laser disdrometeor b1-level datastreams, respectively.
2. PrecipBE datastreams are set to level c1 if at least one year has passed since the data collection to the
 processing day in the case of ARM’s fixed sites and active (long-term) deployments of the third ARM
 Mobile Facility (AMF3), or if more than one year has passed since the last day of any AMF1, AMF2,
 or AMF3 deployment. Otherwise, the PrecipBE datastream level will be set to c0. This approach
 increases the likelihood that sufficient time has passed to allow instrument mentors, community
 members, and users to identify most, if not all, issues, so that the related DQRs will be reflected in the
 final product (by excluding from processing, as needed). During routine operation, c0 data will be
 revisited at reasonable intervals and reprocessed at the c1 level. However, reprocessing will be
 performed if critical issues impacting the final product are identified.

2.1 precipbetseries – Precipitation Event Time Series Data Set
precipbetseries provides the temporally evolving instrument-mean, minimum, maximum, and standard
deviation of event-cumulative precipitation and 1-min precipitation rates. Each timestamp indicates the
number of instruments used, and flags are provided for events detected using only a single instrument.
The time series files also include bit-packed flag arrays for instrument availability, invalid instrument
samples, and instrument DQRs. The time series data file provides information about which instruments
were available via its bit-packed available_instruments field. One can also leverage this data set
to examine all input data converted to convenient and uniform mm/min units, in the
precipitation_amount_all field, thereby streamlining instrument cross-comparison. This
standardized per-instrument time series is accompanied by a corresponding QC flag under the
qc_precipitation_amount_all field and a field indicating the indices corresponding to each
instrument (precipitation_amount_all_source). precipbetseries generates daily files
regardless of precipitation occurrence.

2.2 Precipbestats – Precipitation Event Tabular Statistics Data Set
Unlike the precipbetseries data set, (daily) precipbestats data files are generated only for days with one or
more precipitation event onsets, with the number of timestamps equal to the number of precipitation
events that started on a given day. In each timestamp, precipbestats informs about statistics of the given
event such as the instrument-mean, minimum, maximum, and standard deviation of onset, end time,
period, total amount, mean precipitation rate, 1-min-averaged maximum precipitation rate, and

 2

 precipitation rate standard deviation. precipbestats also provides various flags (for DQRs,
 single-instrument events, strong winds, etc.) and information such as which instrument recorded the
 highest precipitation rate or smallest total amount for that event. The data files also contain statistical
 information about the surface-level atmospheric state during precipitation events, with data harvested
 from (in order of preference) the surface meteorological instrumentation (MET), the automatic weather
 station (MAWS), or one of the Vaisala WXT systems operated by ARM, as well as drop distribution
 moment data derived using the VDISQUANTS or LDQUANTS VAPs, depending on availability.

4.0 Output Data
 PrecipBE produces between one and two daily output files: one for precipbetseries, generated daily
 regardless of whether precipitation was detected on a given day, and one for precipbestats, generated only
 on days with one or more precipitation event onsets. The corresponding names of the output files are:

 SSSprecipbestatsXX.LL.YYYYMMDD.hhmmss.nc

 and

 SSSprecipbetseriesXX.LL.YYYYMMDD.hhmmss.nc

 Where:
  • SSS is the site
  • XX is the facility
  • LL is the level (c0 or c1; see Section 2.0)
  • YYYY is the year
  • MM is the month
  • DD is the day
  • hh is the hour
  • mm is the minute
  • ss is the second
