AuditRepairBench: A Paired-Execution Trace Corpus for Evaluator-Channel Ranking Instability in Agent Repair
Yuelin Hu, Zhenbo Yu, Zhengxue Cheng, Wei Liu, Li Song, [1em] Shanghai Jiao Tong University

Abstract
Agent-repair leaderboards reorder under evaluator reconfiguration, and a measurable share of the reordering is produced by methods that consult evaluator-derived signal during internal selection of candidate repairs. We document this failure mode on a public leaderboard and release AuditRepairBench, a paired-execution trace corpus of 576,000 576{,}000 registered cells ( 96,000 96{,}000 executed) that operationalizes evaluator-channel-blocking ranking instability within a declared observability boundary. A modular screening architecture decides pathway-blocking through four interchangeable implementations, a learned influence proxy, a rule-based channel-exposure ratio that uses no trained model, a counterfactual sensitivity proxy, and a sparse human-audit proxy, combined into a screening posterior that feeds a cell-level flip functional, a set-valued label, a stratified system score, and a set-valued leaderboard. The resource is supported by mechanism-anchored validation on an 80 80 -case source-level channel-surgery subset, an independent-discovery protocol under which two annotator groups separated from the pipeline developers discover coupling patterns blinded to the screening design and the frozen ensemble attains pooled AUROC 0.83 0.83 on their 79 79 cases, implementation robustness, uncertainty propagation that raises 95 % 95\% coverage from 0.81 0.81 to 0.95 0.95 , and forward transfer with pooled community-evaluator Spearman ρ = 0.65 \rho=0.65 . Screening-guided blinding patches reduce rank displacement by 55 55 – 74 % 74\% (mean 62 % 62\% ) at fewer than 50 50 lines of code, whereas random channel blinding produces at most 7 % 7\% reduction and generic retraining at most 13 % 13\% . AuditRepairBench-Lite, a rule-only configuration on a 12,000 12{,}000 -cell subset, preserves the leaderboard at Kendall τ = 0.88 \tau=0.88 under twenty-four GPU-hours and is the primary release artifact at 42 42 GB, with an anonymous public preview at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/AuditRepairBench-D1EE/ .

Why a dedicated resource.

Benchmark-stability work treats the leaderboard as a statistical object under resampling or judge bagging
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
, LLM-as-judge reliability work studies whether judges agree with humans on individual items
[
17
,
18
,
21
,
22
]
, and adversarial or counterfactual evaluation work perturbs inputs under a fixed evaluator
[
15
,
16
,
33
,
34
]
; none isolates the selector-evaluator coupling channel.
A resource that measures this channel must hold the final evaluator fixed, vary only the information path into the selector, and attribute rank changes to the interaction rather than to evaluator noise or input perturbation.
AuditRepairBench provides such a resource as a paired-execution trace corpus with a reference analysis pipeline; the motivating-case procedure, the blinding operator at the selector input boundary, and the per-system classification rule are in Appendix
N
.
The resource does not recover a latent causal construct, does not certify a mechanism, and does not claim strong prospective validity; the prior notion of a certified family is replaced by an operationally screened family, and headline scope is restricted throughout to hook-completeness (the intersection of static AST coverage and dynamic taint coverage on the selector input boundary) at least
0.80
.

Contributions.

A modular screening architecture decides pathway-blocking through four interchangeable implementations so that benchmark identity does not bind to a single learned measurement model; mechanism-anchored validation on
80

0.83
on
79

\tau=0.88
while removing trained-model dependencies and running in under twenty-four GPU-hours.

5
Modular screening architecture

The four implementations of the screening architecture are deliberately heterogeneous, so that the benchmark identity is the posterior rather than any single learned model.
The learned influence proxy estimates a normalized pathway-influence reduction for evaluator-derived features entering the selector, using a two-layer MLP over a
128
-dimensional selector-state embedding that encodes attention weights on evaluator-derived tokens, activation magnitudes, and selector-logit change under embedding-level masking.
The rule-based channel exposure ratio uses only static and dynamic program analysis of whether evaluator-derived tokens, spans, or features enter the selector input window, attention mask, or feature concatenation, and involves no trained model.
The counterfactual sensitivity proxy computes local input-masking effects on the selector output distribution through symmetric Kullback-Leibler divergence and argmax-flip rate, and the sparse human audit proxy operates on a size-
280
subset whose decisions are propagated to unreached cells through a calibrated audit-proxy classifier; the propagation classifier is a gradient-boosted decision tree over a
128
-dimensional feature vector that concatenates the three other implementation scores with six auxiliary signals (replay fidelity, four off-target damage channels, and the cell stratum), is trained under uncertainty-weighted loss that upweights audited cells near the
\tau_{-}
and
\tau_{+}
thresholds, and is retrained whenever more than thirty additional audit labels enter the ledger.
Its dominant failure modes, characterized on a held-out audit fold, are undercalibration on delayed-memory coupling where audit evidence is temporally dispersed (calibration error
0.08
against the base
0.04
) and overconfidence on candidate-pool priming when off-target damage is non-negligible (false-block rate
0.14
against the base
0.07
); both modes are surfaced by the evaluation package at output time and downgrade the cell to the ambiguity band rather than silently entering the labeled majority.
Replay fidelity and off-target damage retain their definitions as audit signals rather than certifying predicates.
The four scores, together with two meta-features (hook completeness and an evaluator-family indicator), are combined through calibrated logistic stacking fitted on the calibration split into the screening posterior
p(a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{screen}}(x))
; the meta-features improve calibration (ECE
0.032\to 0.018
) rather than providing a shortcut to the outcome, and a strict leaderboard variant that withholds them agrees with the main leaderboard at Kendall
\tau=0.97
(Table
14
). A schematic of the architecture, training details of the influence model, its feature ablation, the audit-proxy propagation classifier, and the exact form of the exposure ratio are in Appendix
O
.

13
Limitations, governance, and conclusion

The benchmark does not recover a latent causal construct, does not certify mechanisms, and does not claim strong prospective validity.
The screening architecture is implementation-pluralistic but does not eliminate reliance on observability, so closed-API and GUI-heavy systems fall outside the primary scope by design; as commercial agents increasingly adopt closed-weight or API-only interfaces that do not expose selector internals, the benchmark’s long-term utility depends on extending the observability boundary through lightweight instrumentation shims, UI-automation hook adapters, and a community surgery kit (described in Appendix
U
).
The
80
-case source-level subset covers three open-source agent families, the independent-discovery groups are separated by organizational boundary but were selected by the maintainers, and the forward-transfer signal (
\rho=0.65
) is weak-to-moderate rather than prospective validity.
The governance protocol, pre-registered failure thresholds, and scope-expansion roadmap are in Appendices
E
,
J
,
R
,
L
, and

.

Appendix N
Motivating-case methodology

The causal attribution in Section
1
rests on an explicit three-step operational procedure.
First, each of the sixty systems is classified on whether its selector module receives any evaluator-derived feature, through a combination of static analysis of the selector call graph and dynamic taint tracking of evaluator API outputs; the classification rule and the per-system result are listed in Supplementary Table S1.
Second, for the seven swap systems, the subset of features that the selector reads from the evaluator is recorded per time step and categorized into rationale tokens, verdict scalars, confidence values, and candidate reranking logits.
Third, a blinding intervention zeros exactly the selector-accessed evaluator outputs at the selector input boundary while preserving the final evaluator and the rest of the pipeline; the four systems for which this blinding reduces rank displacement by more than three positions under both evaluator configurations are reported as the four coupled swaps.
No latent mechanism is recovered and no single statistical test is run on the sixty systems as a whole; the claim is the arithmetic outcome of the described per-system procedure, and every step has a release artifact.
A running example that illustrates the test at the cell level is the following.
An agent at each step passes the top-three candidate repairs to the evaluator, reads the per-candidate score vector and the verbal rationale, and uses both as features for its next-step selector.
Under the observed execution the selector receives the full evaluator signal; under an intervention that zeros the score vector and masks rationale tokens before they reach the selector, the selector chooses among the same candidates without evaluator-derived features.
If the final winner differs between the two runs, the cell is a candidate positive; if neither run changes the winner, it is a candidate negative; if the intervention also perturbs non-target execution, the cell is screened out unless the screening architecture declares the intervention locally targeted.

Appendix U
Scope expansion roadmap and governance details

Three concrete mechanisms are provisioned for widening observability coverage without redefining the screening architecture.
A lightweight instrumentation shim exposes selector attention weights, feature-access masks, and per-token activation magnitudes through a standardized Python wrapper that can be dropped into an agent harness without modifying core logic; three reference shims for the remaining two method families (autonomous code exploration and multi-agent debate) accompany the release, together raising their hook completeness from
0.58
and
0.64
to
0.83
and
0.87
in internal pilots.
A UI-automation hook adapter routes GUI-heavy agents through an accessibility tree that records selector-accessed fields, and is expected to move GUI-heavy agents from the behavior-only fallback into the primary scope as the adapter matures.
A community surgery kit, released jointly with the Lite bundle, supplies an intervention template, a replay-fidelity and off-target audit harness, and acceptance criteria (surgery AUROC at least
0.85
on a
20
-case internal validation, off-target damage below
0.10
, and a double-audit Cohen
\kappa
of at least
0.75
) so that external groups can contribute additional source-level surgery cases from method families, codebases, or verification artifacts beyond the three currently covered; accepted contributions are added to the surgery registry with a versioned protocol hash.
The audit ledger is refreshed every six months under a fixed quota of at least
200
new audited cells and at least
40
new surgery cases, with the refresh schedule and quotas pre-registered in the governance document so that the audit set grows proportionally to benchmark use.

To reduce reproducibility friction introduced by proprietary judge dependencies, the release ships an open-weight judge suite comprising three judges (a Llama-based instruction-tuned judge, a Qwen-based rubric judge, and a code-aware rule-based judge) with calibration recipes that fit per-proprietary-judge Platt scaling on the bridge-calibration split; the suite attains pooled Spearman
\rho=0.89
against the three proprietary judges on the overlap, and the calibration step reports per-judge substitution gaps so that users can quantify the cost of running without API access rather than absorbing it silently.
