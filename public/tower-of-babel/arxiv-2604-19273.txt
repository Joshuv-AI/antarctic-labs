Abstract
In this letter, we propose a sparsification method for precoding codebooks that reduces the peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR) while preserving the achievable rate. By exploiting the fact that precoder matrices lie on the Grassmann manifold, we formulate a codebook design problem that enables sparsification without modifying the existing feedback mechanism. We develop two sparsification approaches, namely exact sparsification via unitary transformation and approximate sparsification via sparse principal component analysis (SPCA), and integrate them into a unified design algorithm. The proposed sparsified codebooks incur negligible performance loss while reducing PAPR by more than 1 ​ dB 1~\text{dB} in uplink scenarios.



I
Introduction

IN the evolution toward 6G systems, uplink traffic is expected to increase significantly due to the growing number of connected devices
[
5
]
. In uplink systems, power amplifier (PA) efficiency is inherently constrained at user equipment, and the high peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR) remains a major challenge in orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) signals, which are commonly used in modern wireless communication standards.
Extensive studies have characterized the statistical properties of PAPR and developed reduction techniques for OFDM and discrete Fourier transform spread OFDM (DFT-s-OFDM)
[
10
,
7
]
, which are adopted in current 5G uplink systems. To further enhance both uplink capacity and coverage in beyond-5G and 6G systems, extending DFT-s-OFDM to support multi-stream transmission has attracted increasing interest, since the current 5G standard restricts it to single-stream transmission.

Spatial precoding plays an essential role in multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems.
In 5G uplink, codebook-based precoding is employed, where the codebooks composed of multiple precoders are standardized in
[
1
]
for different numbers of antenna ports and data streams.
The precoding matrices can be interpreted as points on a Grassmann manifold
[
8
,
9
]
, and under uncorrelated Rayleigh fading, codebook design is equivalent to subspace packing on the Grassmann manifold. However, existing codebooks adopt dense precoder structures designed to maximize array gain
and their design does not take into account the use of multi-stream DFT-s-OFDM.
When applied to multi-stream transmission, such dense precoders break the single-carrier property of DFT-s-OFDM, resulting in increased PAPR. Recent work
[
2
]
addressed this issue by designing codebooks consisting exclusively of sparse precoders and demonstrated their effectiveness in reducing PAPR for multi-stream DFT-s-OFDM.

In this letter, we propose a sparsification method for precoding codebooks that reduces PAPR while maintaining the achievable rate. Unlike
[
2
]
, which designs new codebooks, the proposed method sparsifies existing codebooks standardized in
[
1
]
while preserving the original codebook indices.
This approach enables effective PAPR reduction without affecting existing standard specifications, since dense precoders can be replaced with sparse precoders through a user-equipment-only implementation.
By exploiting the Grassmannian representation of precoder matrices, sparse precoders are constructed either via unitary transformations of the original precoders or via sparse principal component analysis (SPCA)
[
3
,
11
]
. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

We propose a codebook-based precoding framework that uses sparse precoders instead of dense precoders for PAPR reduction in DFT-s-OFDM without performance degradation and without modifying the existing feedback mechanism.

2.

We develop two closed-form sparsification methods on the Grassmann manifold, based on unitary transformations and SPCA.

II
System Model

In this section, we describe the limited-feedback MIMO system
[
8
,
9
]
with codebook-based precoding.

We consider a single-user uplink limited-feedback MIMO system with
N_{t}
transmit and
N_{r}
receive antennas, where
N_{s}\leq\min(N_{t},N_{r})
denotes the number of data streams. Let
\mathbf{H}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{r}\times N_{t}}
denote the channel matrix for the
k
-th subcarrier,
k=1,\ldots,K
. Let
\mathbf{s}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}\times 1}
denote the transmitted symbol vector on the
k
-th subcarrier, drawn from phase shift keying (PSK) or quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) constellations and satisfying
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{s}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}]=\mathbf{I}_{N_{s}}
. The received signal
\mathbf{y}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{r}\times 1}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{k}=\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{i}\mathbf{s}_{k}+\mathbf{v}_{k},

(1)

where
\mathbf{W}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times N_{s}}
is selected from a finite codebook
\mathcal{W}={\mathbf{W}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{W}_{|\mathcal{W}|}}
,
\mathbf{H}_{k}
is the channel matrix, and
\mathbf{v}_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{v}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N_{r}})
is additive white Gaussian noise.

We consider wideband precoding as in current 5G uplink systems, where a common precoder is applied across all subcarriers. Each precoder satisfies
\mathbf{W}_{i}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{W}_{i}=\mathbf{I}_{N_{s}}
. With perfect channel state information at the receiver,
the precoder is selected to maximize the achievable rate

\displaystyle R(\mathbf{W}_{i})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}~\log_{2}\det\left(\mathbf{I}_{N_{s}}+\frac{\rho}{N_{s}}\mathbf{W}_{i}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{i}\right),

(2)

where
\rho=1/\sigma_{v}^{2}
denotes the average signal-to-noise ratio (SNR).
As seen from (
2
),
R(\mathbf{W}_{i})
is invariant under right multiplication of
\mathbf{W}_{i}
by any unitary matrix. Together with the orthonormality constraint, this implies that
\mathbf{W}_{i}
can be regarded as a point on the Grassmann manifold
\mathcal{G}(N_{t},N_{s})
.
The receiver feeds back the index
\hat{i}
corresponding to the optimal precoder as

\displaystyle\hat{i}=\underset{1\leq i\leq|\mathcal{W}|}{\text{argmax}}~R(\mathbf{W}_{i}),

(3)

using
\lceil\log_{2}|\mathcal{W}|\rceil
bits.

III
Proposed Sparsification Methods

In this section, we describe a new problem formulation for reducing PAPR without modifying the existing feedback mechanism and the corresponding codebook design procedure.

III-A

Problem Formulation

To reduce PAPR in multi-stream DFT-s-OFDM, we propose a pre-mapped sparse codebook design that operates within the existing limited-feedback framework.
An overview of the system is summarized as follows:

•

The UE independently selects a precoder from the pre-mapped sparse codebook using the same feedback index as the original dense codebook.

•

The selected precoder reduces PAPR with little performance loss in multi-stream DFT-s-OFDM due to its sparse structure.

In this system, the pre-mapped sparse codebook should preserve the performance and spatial characteristics (e.g., main lobe directions) of the original dense precoder at each index, since the UE independently selects the precoder without modifying the feedback mechanism. From the viewpoint of codebook design, each precoder represents a point on the Grassmann manifold
\mathcal{G}(N_{t},N_{s})
. Hence, the performance preservation problem can be interpreted as approximating each original precoder by a sparse precoder on the Grassmann manifold. On the Grassmann manifold, the distance between two points is defined as the chordal distance. Specifically, the chordal distance between two points
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{P}
, denoted by
d_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{P})
, is defined as

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{P})=\sqrt{N_{s}-\|\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{P}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{F}}}.

(4)

Let the original dense codebook be denoted as
\mathcal{W}=\{\mathbf{W}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{|\mathcal{W}|}\}
and the pre-mapped sparse codebook as
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathbf{P}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{P}_{|\mathcal{P}|}\}
.
Based on the results demonstrated in
[
2
]
, we set the number of nonzero elements of each precoder matrix
\mathbf{P}_{i}
to
N_{t}
, i.e.,
\|\mathbf{P}_{i}\|_{0}=N_{t}
, taking into account the trade-off between sparsity and performance.
Since the optimization can be carried out independently for each index
i\in\{1,\cdots,|\mathcal{W}|\}
, the pre-mapped sparse codebook design reduces to the following per-index problem:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\underset{\mathbf{P}}{\operatorname{minimize}}\;&d_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{P})\\
\text{s.t.}\;&\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{G}(N_{t},N_{s}),\;\|\mathbf{P}\|_{0}=N_{t}.\end{split}

(5)

Thus, the index
i
is omitted hereafter without loss of generality, since each precoder is optimized independently.

III-B

Exact Sparsification via Unitary Transformation

Regarding the problem defined in (
5
), when the optimal sparse precoder
\mathbf{P}
exists, that is, the chordal distance between
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{P}
is
0
, they span the same subspace, and there exists a unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}\times N_{s}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{U}.

(6)

In this work, we demonstrate that the derivation of such a unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}
and corresponding sparse precoder
\mathbf{P}
can be obtained in closed form for a given dense precoder
\mathbf{W}
.

Let
I_{j}\subset\{1,\cdots,N_{t}\}
be the support set of
\mathbf{p}_{j}
, i.e.,
\text{supp}(\mathbf{p}_{j})
, which represents the set of indices corresponding to the nonzero elements of each vector
\mathbf{p}_{j}
, where
\mathbf{p}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times 1}
denotes the
j
-th column vector of
\mathbf{P}
.
Furthermore, we define
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1},\cdots,I_{N_{s}}\}
as the collection of support sets
I_{j}
for a given sparse matrix
\mathbf{P}
, that is, the set of the positions of all nonzero elements in the matrix. In this letter, we refer to
\mathcal{I}
as a sparsity pattern.
Since all sparse matrices
\mathbf{P}
must be on the Grassmann manifold,
\mathbf{P}
must satisfy the orthogonality constraint
\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{I}_{N_{s}}
. Thus, the support sets for an arbitrary sparsity pattern
\mathcal{I}
must be mutually disjoint, i.e.,

I_{j}\cap I_{k}=\varnothing
for all
j,k\in\{1,\cdots,N_{s}\}
. When we let
n(N_{t},N_{s})
denote the number of candidates for sparsity patterns that satisfy this constraint for a given matrix size
N_{t}\times N_{s}
,
n(N_{t},N_{s})
can be simply derived as

\displaystyle n(N_{t},N_{s})=\frac{1}{N_{s}!}\sum_{m=0}^{N_{s}}(-1)^{m}\binom{N_{s}}{m}(N_{s}-m)^{N_{t}}.

(7)

This corresponds to the Stirling number of the second kind, which gives the total number of ways to partition
N_{t}
distinct elements into
N_{s}
nonempty subsets.
For instance, when the matrix size is given by
(N_{t},N_{s})=(4,2)
, the number of candidates is obtained as
n(4,2)=7
, and the resulting sparsity patterns are denoted as

\mathcal{I}_{1}=\{\{1,2\},\{3,4\}\},~\mathcal{I}_{2}=\{\{1,3\},\{2,4\}\},

\mathcal{I}_{3}=\{\{1,4\},\{2,3\}\},~\mathcal{I}_{4}=\{\{1\},\{2,3,4\}\},

\mathcal{I}_{5}=\{\{1,3,4\}\{2\}\},

\mathcal{I}_{6}=\{\{1,2,4\},\{3\}\},
and
\mathcal{I}_{7}=\{\{1,2,3\},\{4\}\}
.

For a given sparsity pattern
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1},\cdots,I_{N_{s}}\}
defined in (
7
), let
J_{j}
denote the complement of each support set
I_{j}
, i.e.,
J_{j}=I_{j}^{c}
. For each column vector
\mathbf{p}_{j}
, the entries corresponding to the indices
J_{j}
must be zero, i.e.,
\mathbf{p}_{j}[J_{j}]=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{|J_{j}|\times 1}
. When the unitary matrix representation defined in (
6
) holds, the constraint

\displaystyle(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{u}_{j})[J_{j}]=\mathbf{0},

(8)

is satisfied, where
\mathbf{u}_{j}
is the
j
-th column vector of
\mathbf{U}
. The constraint (
8
) can be equivalently rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{j}\in\text{ker}(\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:]),

(9)

where
\text{ker}(\cdot)
denotes the null space of a matrix. Regarding (
9
), a feasible unitary vector
\mathbf{u}_{j}
exists when the null space of
\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:]
has a nonzero dimension.
Additionally, since
\mathbf{U}
is a unitary matrix,
the following orthonormality constraints must hold for any pair of column indices
1\leq j<k\leq N_{s}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{u}_{k}=0,~\|\mathbf{u}_{j}\|=1.\end{split}

(10)

To make the above conditions explicit and verifiable, we state the following proposition.

Proposition 1

For a given dense precoder
\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{G}(N_{t},N_{s})
and a sparsity pattern
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1},\cdots,I_{N_{s}}\}
, there exists a unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}=[\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{N_{s}}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}\times N_{s}}
satisfying (
6
) if and only if one can sequentially construct vectors
\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{N_{s}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{j}\in\text{ker}(\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:]),~\mathbf{u}_{j}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{u}_{k}=0~(j\neq k),~\|\mathbf{u}_{j}\|=1,

(11)

for all
j,k\in\{1,\cdots,N_{s}\}
. Equivalently, the sparsity pattern
\mathcal{I}
is feasible if and only if, at each step
j
, there exists a nonzero vector in
\text{ker}(\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:])\bigcap\text{span}\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{j-1}\}^{\perp}
.

Based on Proposition
1
, the feasibility of a given sparsity pattern can be verified constructively. When the above condition is satisfied, the desired unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}
can be obtained in closed form by the following procedure. Let
\mathbf{Q}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}\times N_{s}}
denote the projection matrix onto the null space of
\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:]
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{j}=\mathbf{I}_{N_{s}}-\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:]^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:],

(12)

where
(\cdot)^{\dagger}
denotes the pseudoinverse. For an arbitrary column vector
\mathbf{x}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{s}}
, the vector
\mathbf{Q}_{j}\mathbf{x}_{j}
must lie in the null space of
\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:]
, i.e.,
\ker(\mathbf{W}[J_{j},:])
.
Next, to satisfy the orthonormality condition given by (
10
), we perform the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization process, which is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{j}=\frac{\left(\mathbf{I}_{N_{s}}-\sum_{m=1}^{j-1}\mathbf{u}_{m}\mathbf{u}_{m}^{\mathrm{H}}\right)\mathbf{Q}_{j}\mathbf{x}_{j}}{\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}_{N_{s}}-\sum_{m=1}^{j-1}\mathbf{u}_{m}\mathbf{u}_{m}^{\mathrm{H}}\right)\mathbf{Q}_{j}\mathbf{x}_{j}\right\|}.

(13)

By iteratively applying this procedure for
j=1,\cdots,N_{s}
, the desired unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}=[\mathbf{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{N_{s}}]
is obtained, when the feasibility condition in Proposition
1
is satisfied. In this case, each step yields a nonzero vector, and thus the Gram-Schmidt procedure does not degenerate.

For example, we consider the sparsification of the original dense precoder
\mathbf{W}
, which is denoted as

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&-\mathrm{j}&1&-\mathrm{j}\\
1&-\mathrm{j}&-1&\mathrm{j}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}}.

(14)

When the sparsity pattern
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1},I_{2}\}
is given as
I_{1}=\{1,2\}
and
I_{2}=\{3,4\}
, their complement subsets are denoted as
J_{1}=\{3,4\}
and
J_{2}=\{1,2\}
respectively. Thus, the submatrices corresponding to these complement subsets are denoted as

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}[J_{1},:]=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\
-\mathrm{j}&\mathrm{j}\end{bmatrix},~\mathbf{W}[J_{2},:]=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
-\mathrm{j}&-\mathrm{j}\end{bmatrix},

(15)

respectively. Here, each null space is also denoted as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\ker\left(\mathbf{W}[J_{1},:]\right)=\mathrm{span}\left([1,1]^{\mathrm{T}}\right),\\
&\ker\left(\mathbf{W}[J_{2},:]\right)=\mathrm{span}\left([1,-1]^{\mathrm{T}}\right).\end{split}

(16)

Since these null spaces are mutually orthogonal, we automatically obtain the orthonormal column vectors
\mathbf{u}_{1}
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}
as

\mathbf{u}_{1}=1/\sqrt{2}~[1,1]^{\mathrm{T}}
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}=1/\sqrt{2}~[1,-1]^{\mathrm{T}}
respectively.
Finally, the desired unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}
is obtained as
\mathbf{U}=[\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2}]
, and the corresponding sparse precoder
\mathbf{P}
is also obtained as

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{U}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1&-\mathrm{j}&0&0\\
0&0&1&-\mathrm{j}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}}.

(17)

At this point,
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{W}
represent the same subspace on the Grassmann manifold, and the resulting chordal distance between these two points is
0
.

III-C

Approximate Sparsification via SPCA

In this subsection, we describe a process for approximating the sparsification problem in (
5
) based on SPCA when no unitary matrix
\mathbf{U}
exists for the problem defined in (
5
).
For a given dense matrix
\mathbf{W}
, minimizing the chordal distance to
\mathbf{P}
can be expressed as the following maximization problem based on the inner product of
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{P}
:

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{P}}{\operatorname{maximize}}~\|\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{P}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

(18)

This formulation corresponds to searching for a sparse matrix
\mathbf{P}
that maximizes the inner product with the original dense matrix
\mathbf{W}
, and this problem can be formulated as SPCA, a well-known problem in the literature
[
3
,
11
]
. In this sense, the sparsification of a point on the Grassmann manifold can be interpreted as an SPCA problem. According to the SPCA formulation, (
18
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{P}}{\operatorname{maximize}}~\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\mathbf{p}_{j}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{p}_{j}.

(19)

Regarding (
19
), we consider the problem of maximizing the inner product
\mathbf{p}_{j}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{p}_{j}
for each column vector
\mathbf{p}_{j}
, subject to the constraints
\text{supp}(\mathbf{p}_{j})=I_{j}\in\mathcal{I}
and
\|\mathbf{p}_{j}\|=1
.
For a given support set
I_{j}
, the optimal nonzero entries of
\mathbf{p}_{j}
can be obtained in closed form. Specifically, this problem reduces to the Rayleigh quotient maximization problem.
Here, let
\mathbf{A}_{j}=(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{H}})[I_{j},I_{j}]\in\mathbb{C}^{|I_{j}|\times|I_{j}|}
denote the submatrix corresponding to the rows and columns indexed by
I_{j}
, and let
\mathbf{b}_{j}=\mathbf{p}_{j}[I_{j}]\in\mathbb{C}^{|I_{j}|\times 1}
denote the subvector of
\mathbf{p}_{j}
restricted to the support set
I_{j}
.
Then, the optimal subvector
\hat{\mathbf{b}_{j}}
that maximizes the inner product
\mathbf{b}_{j}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{j}\mathbf{b}_{j}
is given by the eigenvector associated with the largest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}_{j}
, denoted by
\lambda_{\text{max}}(\mathbf{A}_{j})
, from the Rayleigh quotient maximization result. Hence, the corresponding optimal vector
\hat{\mathbf{p}_{j}}
that maximizes the inner product
\mathbf{p}_{j}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{p}_{j}
is obtained by embedding the subvector
\hat{\mathbf{b}_{j}}
into the indices
I_{j}
and setting all remaining elements to zero.

Consequently, the SPCA optimization problem defined in (
19
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{I}_{k}}{\operatorname{argmax}}~\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s}}\lambda_{\text{max}}(\mathbf{A}_{j}),~\forall k\in\{1,\cdots,n(N_{t},N_{s})\}.

(20)

That is, the problem is formulated as a combinatorial optimization problem of selecting the optimal sparsity pattern
\mathcal{I}
from
n(N_{t},N_{s})
candidates. Finally, the optimal matrix
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
is given by
\hat{\mathbf{P}}=[\hat{\mathbf{p}_{1}},\cdots,\hat{\mathbf{p}_{N_{s}}}]
, and the resulting optimal codebook
\mathcal{P}
is also obtained by performing this optimization independently for a given codebook size
|\mathcal{P}|
.

V
Conclusion

In this letter, we proposed a sparsification method for precoding codebooks that preserves the achievable rate while reducing PAPR. By exploiting the Grassmannian property of the precoder matrices, we formulated a codebook design problem that enables sparsification without modifying the existing feedback mechanism. Two methods were developed, namely exact sparsification via unitary transformation and SPCA-based approximation, within a unified design algorithm. The proposed codebooks incurred negligible performance loss while reducing PAPR in DFT-s-OFDM systems.
