Dense Ionized Outflow with Five Narrow Components in a Dust-obscured Galaxy
Taketo Yoshida, Tohru Nagao, Yoshiki Toba, Naomichi Yutani, Thomas Bohn, Kohei Shibata, Nozomu Tamada

Abstract
We present our discovery of a complex ionized outflow in SDSS J101034.28+372514.7 (J1010+3725), a dust-obscured galaxy (DOG) at z = 0.282 z=0.282 . The SDSS optical spectrum of J1010+3725 shows five narrow components with one broad component in [O iii ] λ \lambda 5007, which represents one of the most complex outflow structures observed among dusty active galactic nuclei. Spectrum fitting shows that the five narrow components have a wide range of velocity shifts (from − 1475 -1475 to + 507 +507 km s -1 ). The possible multiple peaks are also observed in [O iii ] λ \lambda 4363 and [Ne iii ] λ \lambda 3868, which allows us to investigate the physical condition of the outflowing gas by comparing the measured emission-line flux ratios with photoionization models. The comparison suggests that the five outflowing components are characterized by high hydrogen densities ( ≳ 10 5 \gtrsim 10^{5} cm -3 ). Our results imply that the five highly dense gas components may be outflowing with multiple bulk velocities at the innermost part of the narrow-line region in J1010+3725.

I
Introduction

It has been shown that the mass of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) strongly correlates with their host galaxy properties such as velocity dispersion and bulge mass, suggesting that they have co-evolved (e.g., Magorrian et al.
27
; Marconi & Hunt
28
; Kormendy & Ho
21
).
A major merger of gas-rich galaxies is often assumed to explain this co-evolution (e.g., Sanders et al.
47
; Hopkins et al.
18
).
In this scenario, the merging/interaction of gas-rich galaxies induces a burst of
star-formation (SF) and the infall of gas onto the nucleus (e.g., Barnes and Hermquist
2
), the latter of which ignites the active galactic nucleus (AGN) at the center of the galaxy (hereafter the major merger scenario).
During this phase, the star forming regions and AGN are typically enshrouded by a large amount of dust.
Eventually, the surrounding gas and dust are blown out by powerful radiation and/or outflow from the AGN.

Numerical simulations have suggested that gas-rich major mergers of galaxies can be classified as dust-obscured galaxies (DOGs: Dey et al.
10
) around the peak of their SF rate and AGN accretion (Narayanan et al.
37
; Yutani et al.
73
).
DOGs are selected by their extreme optical to infrared (IR) color (
i-[22]\geq 7
: Toba et al.
60
; see also Dey et al.
10
), and include both dusty SF galaxies and dusty AGNs.
These two populations of DOGs can be separated by their characteristic features in their spectral energy distributions (SEDs: Dey et al.
10
; Melbourne et al.
30
; Toba et al.
60
; Noboriguchi et al.
40
; Suleiman et al.
52
; Yu et al.
72
; Yoshida et al.
71
).
SF-dominated DOGs are often called “bump DOGs” owing to their characteristic bump feature in their SEDs at near-infrared (NIR) wavelength (e.g., Desai et al.
9
; Melbourne et al.
30
; Yu et al.
72
).
On the contrary, AGN-dominated DOGs are characterized by power-law SED shapes from optical to mid-IR (MIR) wavelengths (e.g., Bussmann et al.
5
; Melbourne et al.
30
; Zou et al.
76
; Yu et al.
72
), and are thus called “power-law (PL) DOGs”.

Some AGN-dominated DOGs are characterized by their hotter dust emission from relatively strong AGN activity (
\gtrsim
60K; Wu et al.
69
; Fan et al.
14
), and they are known as Hot DOGs (Eisenhardt et al.
13
; Wu et al.
69
).
They are characterized by higher IR luminosities and obscuration than those of classical DOGs (e.g., Tsai et al.
63
; Toba et al.
61
,
62
; Li et al.
25
).
Based on the major merger scenario, dusty AGNs including PL DOGs and Hot DOGs are expected to host strong outflows, which have the potential to eject gas and dust to the outer regions of the galaxy.
In fact, both observations and simulations suggest that these dusty AGNs often show complex [O
iii
] emission profiles, which are more significant in more heavily obscured AGNs (e.g, Zakamska et al.
75
; Toba et al.
57
; Perrotta et al.
41
; Finnerty et al.
15
; Jun et al.
20
; Yutani et al.
74
).

Yoshida et al. (in preparation) identified DOGs having both a Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS: York et al.
70
) spectrum and a NIR counterpart to investigate the relation between their SED shape and the properties of their SDSS spectrum.
Within this sample, we report the serendipitous discovery of multi-peaked [O
iii
]
\lambda
\lambda
4959, 5007 emission profiles, as well as possible multiple peaks in [Ne
iii
]
\lambda
3868 and [O
iii
]
\lambda
4363, in SDSS J101034.28+372514.7 (J1010+3725), indicative of a multi-component outflow.
In this paper, we report the unique nature and possible implications of this extraordinary outflow feature found in the spectrum of a luminous DOG.

In Section

Unless otherwise noted, all magnitudes refer to the AB system.

As for the complex narrow-line structure, although it does not significantly differ from those of other AGNs in terms of velocity shift and velocity dispersion, it is clearly resolved into five distinct components.
Although the detailed geometrical structure of those five components cannot be discussed using the currently available datasets, integral-field spectroscopic observations with a high angular resolution may reveal their spatial distribution.
As for the broad component, we are probably observing an outflowing component whose characteristics are similar to those seen in other AGNs.

IV.2
Physical Nature of the Outflowing Gas

To investigate the physical condition of the outflowing gas, we compared the observed and model-predicted line ratios of the narrow components, specifically [O
iii
]
\lambda
5007/[O
iii
]
\lambda
4363 and [O
iii
]
\lambda
5007/[Ne
iii
]
\lambda
3868.
The observed ratios and their uncertainties were derived from our best-fit spectral model.
Among the five components, only the rest one yielded reliable measurements (S/N
>3
) for both line ratios.
Thus, we mainly focused on this main component to discuss the physical nature.

We performed photoionization modeling using the
CLOUDY
version 23.01 (Chatzikos et al.
7
; Gunasekera et al.
17
).
For the calculations, we adopted gas metallicities of
Z_{\odot}
and
2Z_{\odot}
.
For the ionization parameter (
U
) and hydrogen density of the cloud (
n
), we set ranges of
10^{-3.5}
–
10^{-1.5}
and
10^{3}
–10
7
cm
-3
with a step size of 1.0 dex, respectively.
Orion-type graphite and silicate grains (Baldwin et al.
1
) were included, along with the effects of dust on heavy-element depletion and radiative transfer.
The AGN SED was assumed to be as follows:

f_{\nu}=\nu^{\alpha_{\rm UV}}{\rm exp}(-h\nu/kT_{\rm BB}){\rm exp}(kT_{\rm IR}/h\nu)+b\nu^{\alpha_{\rm X}}

(1)

Here, we took the two types of SED parameters originally considered for broad-line Seyfert1 galaxies (BLS1s;
\alpha_{\rm UV}=-0.5
,
T_{\rm BB}=1.18\times 10^{6}
K,
kT_{\rm IR}=0.01
Ryd,
\alpha_{\rm X}=-1.15
, and
b
is a constant yielding
\alpha_{\rm OX}=-1.35
) and narrow-line Seyfert1 galaxies (NLS1s;
\alpha_{\rm UV}=-0.5
,

Our results imply that the five highly dense gas components are outflowing with multiple (line-of-sight) bulk velocities.
On the narrow-line region (NLR) scale, outflows are mostly driven by radiation pressure from AGNs, and theoretical models in which they are launched from the dusty torus as a dusty wind have also been proposed (see, e.g., Kudoh et al.
