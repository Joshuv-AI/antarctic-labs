Endoscopic sinus surgery combined with nasal corticosteroids and surgery alone for chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps: a meta-analysis
Authors: Yalian Chen, Xianfang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe purpose of this meta-analysis was to assess whether the outcomes of endoscopic sinus surgery in chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps could be improved by adjunctive corticosteroid therapy.MethodsThis meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA statement. We systematically searched PubMed, Web of Science, and CNKI for clinical studies comparing ESS combined with corticosteroid therapy and ESS alone in patients with CRSwNP.ResultsEight clinical studies with an aggregate of 776 patients were included. Meta-analysis revealed: (1) A lower rate of polyp recurrence in the ESS plus corticosteroid therapy group (OR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.33–0.97, P = 0.04); (2) Better sinonasal quality of life (SNOT-22: SMD = − 1.09, 95% CI: − 1.30 to − 0.89, P < 0.00001); (3) Decrease symptom score (VAS: MD = − 3.80, 95% CI: − 4.53 to − 3.07, P < 0.00001); (4) Better endoscopic outcome (Lund-Kennedy: SMD = − 2.05, 95% CI: − 2.27 to − 1.83, P < 0.00001). Heterogeneity was low (I² = 0%–4%), and funnel plots were generated exploratorily because of the limited number of studies.ConclusionESS combined with adjunctive corticosteroid therapy was associated with lower recurrence and improved postoperative SNOT-22, VAS, and Lund-Kennedy scores compared with surgery alone in patients with CRSwNP. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously because endotype-specific effects, corticosteroid delivery methods, surgical extent, and long-term symptomatic recurrence were not consistently reported.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-03909-w.Keywords: Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps, Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, Nasal Corticosteroids, Meta-Analysis, Polyp Recurrence, Therapeutic Efficacy

Introduction

Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) is a common chronic inflammatory disease of the upper airways [1], and is associated with the symptoms of repeated nasal blockage, hyposmia, and mucopurulent rhinorrhea, etc [2]. Recent epidemiological studies indicate that CRSwNP represents a clinically important subtype of chronic rhinosinusitis [3]. In China, chronic rhinosinusitis imposes a substantial disease burden at the population level [4]. Besides the physical symptoms, CRSwNP places a considerable burden on the patients’ quality of life (QoL) [5, 6], and may result in the notable reduction in work productivity [6], an increased risk of comorbid asthma [7], as well as the severely increased rate of sleep disorders [8]. Taken together, these clinical burdens underscore the need for effective therapeutic strategies.

Management of CRSwNP has been progressing toward a multimodal approach, with endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) and adjunctive corticosteroid therapy being important components of treatment [9]. ESS eliminates the mechanical barrier, allowing normalization of mucociliary function by removing polyps and reestablishing physiologic sinus ventilation and drainage [10], which is essential for the control of acute symptoms and inflammation caused by the retention of nasal secretion stasis [11]. Nonetheless, ESS does not address the fundamental inflammatory pathogenesis of CRSwNP: the ongoing eosinophilic infiltration and mucosal edema that sustains polyp regrowth [12]. In a prospective cohort study, Rizzi et al. (2023) have shown that 30–40% of patients undergoing ESS alone have polyp recurrence within 5 years, with recurrence rates surpassing 90% in eosinophilic CRSwNP as a major phenotypic subset [13].

In contrast, corticosteroid therapy has strong anti-inflammatory effects through the inhibition of cytokine secretion such as IL-5 and IL-13 [14], which leads to a decrease in eosinophil chemotaxis and inhibition of mucosal hyperplasia, and these processes are tightly linked to the immunopathologic mechanisms of CRSwNP [15]. Recent clinical trials suggest additive benefits when ESS is combined with corticosteroid therapy: ESS and mometasone furoate nasal spray treatment following surgery halves 12-month recurrence rates compared to ESS alone, improves nasal patency and olfactory performance [16]. Similarly, Kang et al. demonstrated a significant reduction in postoperative symptom severity, as measured by the Visual Analog Scale (VAS), in patients who received budesonide irrigation after ESS compared with those who underwent ESS alone [17].

Nonetheless, descriptive gaps are critical in current evidence and still await fulfillment. Previous meta-analyses regarding ESS combined with corticosteroid therapy for CRSwNP, however, most of the research suffered from small sample sizes and limited outcome measures. Thus, limited conclusions could be drawn. Secondly, there has been minimal head-to-head comparison of efficacy of different corticosteroid delivery methods and analysis of long-term (> 2 years) outcomes, which leaves open question of best length and formulation for medication use. Third, variability in reported outcomes among trials, for instance inconsistent definitions of “recurrence” or non-uniform use of QOL tools, all could hinder the translation of the findings into clinical practice guidelines.

To overcome these limitations, the current meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of ESS combined with adjunctive corticosteroid therapy versus ESS alone for CRSwNP based on the following outcome parameters:

Rate of polyp recurrence: The recurrence rate was analyzed as an objective disease-control outcome, reflecting postoperative polyp regrowth where such information was available. To improve comparability across studies, recurrence was defined as polyp regrowth requiring revision surgery or an endoscopic grade of ≥2, where such information was available.

By integrating data from the included clinical studies and prospective cohort studies (n = 776 patients), this meta-analysis aimed to: (1) quantify the magnitude of benefit of ESS combined with adjunctive corticosteroid therapy over ESS alone across key outcomes; and (2) provide an updated synthesis of available comparative evidence and identify evidence gaps related to endotype-specific effects, corticosteroid delivery methods, surgical extent, and long-term outcomes. Rather than redefining established international treatment standards, this review aimed to clarify the extent to which the available comparative evidence, including Asian and recent studies, supports the combined strategy and to identify the clinical factors that remain insufficiently reported for individualized postoperative management.

Materials and methods

Literature search and study selection

This meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA statement. A systematic literature search was performed in PubMed, Web of Science, and CNKI from database inception to 05 October 2025. The PubMed search strategy was as follows: (“chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps” OR CRSwNP OR “nasal polyps”) AND (“endoscopic sinus surgery” OR ESS OR FESS OR “functional endoscopic sinus surgery”) AND (corticosteroid* OR glucocorticoid* OR steroid* OR “nasal corticosteroid*” OR “intranasal corticosteroid*” OR budesonide OR mometasone OR fluticasone). Similar combinations of subject headings and free-text terms were used in Web of Science. For CNKI, equivalent Chinese terms related to chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, endoscopic sinus surgery, and nasal corticosteroids were used. Two reviewers independently screened the titles and abstracts of the retrieved records and then assessed the full texts of potentially eligible studies. Disagreements were resolved by discussion and consensus.

Exclusion criteria

Studies meeting any of the following criteria were excluded: non-clinical studies; studies involving coexisting sinonasal diseases such as fungal sinusitis or sinonasal tumors; and studies with incomplete, unusable, or unextractable outcome data. Risk of bias assessment

Risk of bias in the included studies was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (version 5.1.0) in the following seven domains: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, completeness of outcome data, selective reporting, other potential sources of bias including baseline imbalance. Bias information of each domain was rated as “low”, “high” or “unclear”. Two reviewers independently performed these assessments and discrepancies between them were resolved by discussion. Statistical analysis

This meta-research was conducted using Review Manager (RevMan) Version 5.4 [18]. Dichotomous results were analyzed by using the Mantel–Haenszel fixed-effect model, and the effect was presented odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Continuous outcomes were analyzed as either mean differences (MD) or standardized mean differences (SMD) with 95% CI: MD for VAS (as a uniform 0–10 scale) and SMD for SNOT-22 and Lund-Kennedy scores (to correct for differences in scales).

I² and χ2 test were used to quantify heterogeneity. When heterogeneity was not significant (I² ≤ 50%, P > 0.1), the fixed-effect model was employed; otherwise, the random-effect model was chosen. A p value of < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. Because not all included studies reported all outcomes, each meta-analysis was conducted using studies with extractable data for the corresponding outcome. Therefore, the number of included studies and patients varied across recurrence, SNOT-22, VAS, and Lund-Kennedy analyses. Funnel plots were generated only as exploratory assessments because only limited studies were available for the outcome.

Results

Study selection and characteristics

A total of 560 records were identified through database searching, including 428 from PubMed, 113 from Web of Science, and 19 from CNKI. After removal of 86 duplicate records, 474 records remained for title and abstract screening. Of these, 206 records were excluded. The full texts of 268 articles were assessed for eligibility. Finally, 8 studies were included in the meta-analysis. These studies included 776 patients in total, with 370 patients in the surgery and corticosteroids group and 406 patients in the surgery group. Postoperative polyp recurrence

Four studies provided extractable data on postoperative polyp recurrence. As shown in Fig. 3, recurrence occurred in 41 of 117 patients in the surgery and corticosteroids group and in 65 of 132 patients in the surgery group. The pooled analysis showed that the surgery and corticosteroids group had a lower recurrence rate than the surgery group (OR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.33–0.97, P = 0.04).

Postoperative SNOT-22 Scores

Postoperative SNOT-22 results were described in three separate trials with 454 participants overall [20, 23, 24]. There was little heterogeneity (χ² = 0.44, df = 1, P = 0.51, I² = 0%). As illustrated in Fig. 4, the overall SMD was − 1.09 (95% CI: − 1.30 to − 0.89, P < 0.00001).

Forest plot of the postoperative SNOT-22 scoring between the combined therapy and surgery solely

Postoperative VAS Scores

Three eligible studies with a total of 109 patients were included for the assessment of symptom severity measured by VAS [20, 25, 26]. Low heterogeneity was observed (χ² = 0.96, df = 2, P = 0.62, I² = 0%), thus a fixed-effects model was implemented. As shown in Fig. 5, the combined MD was − 3.80 (95% CI: − 4.53 to − 3.07, P < 0.00001).

Forest plot of the postoperative VAS scoring between the combined therapy and surgery solely

Postoperative Lund–Kennedy Scores

Three studies including 476 patients were eligible for analysis with respect to endoscopic outcomes using the Lund-Kennedy scoring system [22–24]. The heterogeneity test showed a low variation (χ² = 2.08, df = 2, P = 0.35, I² = 4%). As shown in Fig. 6, the pooled SMD was − 2.05 (95% CI: − 2.27 to − 1. 83, P < 0.00001), indicating that ESS combined with adjunctive corticosteroid therapy was associated with significantly better mucosal recovery and fewer residual lesions under endoscopy than ESS alone.

Funnel plots were generated exploratorily for postoperative polyp recurrence, SNOT-22, VAS, and Lund-Kennedy scores (Fig. 7). Because only limited studies were available for each outcome, the funnel plots could not reliably exclude publication bias. Therefore, publication bias assessment should be interpreted cautiously.

Descriptive synthesis of clinically relevant modifiers

In addition to the pooled analyses, we descriptively summarized clinically relevant modifiers reported by the included studies. These modifiers are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. Reporting of inflammatory phenotype or endotype was inconsistent, and most studies did not provide extractable outcome data stratified by type 2/eosinophilic versus non-type 2/non-eosinophilic disease. Corticosteroid delivery methods varied across studies, including intranasal spray, corticosteroid irrigation, steroid-eluting dressing or stent, and short-course systemic corticosteroids. The extent of surgery was also not uniformly described, and most studies did not clearly distinguish between limited polypectomy, standard FESS, targeted ESS, or extended/complete surgery. Finally, recurrence was reported mainly as endoscopic or polyp recurrence, whereas long-term symptomatic recurrence and recurrence requiring revision surgery were not consistently reported.

Discussion

This meta-analysis of 8 clinical studies involving 776 patients with CRSwNP provides evidence that ESS combined with adjunctive corticosteroid therapy was associated with better outcomes than ESS alone across the analyzed endpoints. However, the included studies used different corticosteroid delivery routes, and the present analysis could not determine whether treatment effects differed by delivery method. For instance, Zhao et al. suggested that local corticosteroid administration directly targets residual inflammatory foci in sinus cavities, a niche inaccessible to surgical resection alone [27]. Further, the observed improvement in Lund-Kennedy scores suggests better endoscopic mucosal outcomes in the combined-therapy group, although the magnitude of benefit should be interpreted cautiously because of differences in study design and intervention protocols. This is particularly relevant for patients with recurrent CRSwNP, as sustained mucosal integrity has been linked to lower long-term revision surgery rates, a critical unmet need addressed by the current study’s pooled data. Short-course systemic corticosteroids should also be distinguished from topical maintenance therapy. When systemic corticosteroids are used as rescue or perioperative anti-inflammatory treatment, cumulative exposure and potential systemic complications should be considered. Because the included studies did not uniformly report cumulative systemic steroid dose or adverse events, the safety profile of systemic corticosteroid use could not be fully assessed in this meta-analysis.

The cumulative analysis showed a lower postoperative polyp recurrence rate in the surgery and corticosteroids group than in the surgery group, with 41 and 65 recurrence events, respectively (OR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.33–0.97, P = 0.04). This finding reiterates the significance of adjunctive corticosteroid therapy in breaking the “inflammation-polyp regrowth” cycle, especially for eosinophilic CRSwNP, a subtype associated with a markedly higher recurrence risk in absence of sufficient anti-inflammatory maintenance [28]. In line with this a trial investigating the use of mometasone furoate nasal spray as an adjunct to ESS performed better in terms of faster mucosal epithelialization and a reduced recurrence rate, demonstrating that local corticosteroid administration addresses residual inflammation in sinus cavities that are not surgically resectable [16].

For PROs, the combination therapy yielded a large, clinically meaningful improvement in sinonasal QOL, as reflected by a SMD in SNOT-22 scores. This aligns with evidence that ESS alone improves nasal ventilation and olfactory function while adjunctive corticosteroid therapy may further enhance these benefits by reducing mucosal edema and mucus hypersecretion [29]. Similarly, the MD in VAS scores indicates substantially alleviated postoperative symptoms, which is a synergy of ESS’s minimally invasive tissue preservation and adjunctive corticosteroid therapy.

Endoscopic outcomes, measured via Lund-Kennedy scores, showed an even more pronounced benefit of combined therapy, reflecting fewer residual lesions and better mucosal healing. This is attributed to ESS’s ability to restore sinus drainage while preserving normal tissue [10], paired with the role of adjunctive corticosteroid therapy in promoting epithelial repair and suppressing inflammatory cell infiltration. This thus becomes a key advantage for chronic disease management. The low statistical heterogeneity observed across outcomes supports the consistency of the pooled estimates, although clinical heterogeneity remained.

An important issue that deserves further consideration is the heterogeneity of CRSwNP endotypes. In Western populations, eosinophilic or type 2-high disease is more common and is generally considered more responsive to corticosteroid therapy, whereas Asian populations have historically shown a higher proportion of non-eosinophilic or neutrophilic inflammation, although a shift toward eosinophilic disease has increasingly been reported. Because the included studies did not consistently provide endotype-specific data, we were unable to perform subgroup analyses according to eosinophilic versus non-eosinophilic disease. Therefore, the pooled benefit observed in this study should be interpreted as an overall treatment effect across mixed CRSwNP populations rather than definitive evidence for any single inflammatory endotype. The therapeutic context of CRSwNP has also changed with the introduction of biologics for severe or refractory type 2 disease. In Europe and North America, biologics are increasingly considered for patients with uncontrolled CRSwNP despite appropriate surgery and topical anti-inflammatory treatment, particularly when type 2 inflammation, asthma, repeated systemic corticosteroid use, or recurrent disease is present. Therefore, the present findings should not be interpreted as competing with biologic therapy. Rather, ESS combined with postoperative corticosteroid therapy represents a conventional anti-inflammatory strategy, whereas biologics may be considered for selected refractory patients. The included studies did not provide sufficient data to determine how postoperative corticosteroid strategies interact with subsequent biologic use, and this remains an important area for future research.

In addition, SNOT-22 should be regarded as the most clinically relevant patient-reported outcome among the endpoints analyzed in this review. Endoscopic recurrence of nasal polyps does not necessarily indicate a need for revision surgery, particularly when symptoms are mild or quality-of-life impairment is limited. Therefore, recurrence should be interpreted together with patient-reported symptoms, endoscopic severity, and the need for additional treatment.

Another clinically relevant source of heterogeneity is the route of corticosteroid administration. The included studies used different delivery methods, such as intranasal spray, irrigation, steroid-eluting stents, and short-course oral corticosteroids. These approaches may differ in local drug distribution, tissue penetration, duration of action, and patient adherence. The present meta-analysis was not designed to compare these strategies directly, and future head-to-head studies are needed to determine the optimal postoperative corticosteroid delivery method.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the included studies ranged from 2004 to 2025 and varied in sample size, corticosteroid regimen, and follow-up duration, and long-term outcome data were still limited. Second, the included studies did not consistently stratify outcomes according to CRSwNP phenotype or inflammatory endotype. This is clinically relevant because eosinophilic CRSwNP is associated with a higher recurrence risk and may respond differently to corticosteroid-based therapy than non-eosinophilic disease. The lack of phenotype-specific reporting limited our ability to determine whether the magnitude of benefit was similar across inflammatory subtypes. Third, the extent and style of surgery were not uniformly reported across studies, and variability in surgical approach may also have influenced postoperative outcomes. Fourth, only a limited number of studies systematically reported adverse events, which restricted a comprehensive evaluation of the risk-benefit profile of the combined strategy. Polypectomy, standard FESS, targeted surgery, and extended or complete sinus surgery are not equivalent procedures. The extent of surgery may influence the ability of topical corticosteroids to reach the postoperative sinonasal cavity and may modify postoperative recurrence risk.

From a clinical perspective, these findings support considering postoperative corticosteroid therapy as part of inflammatory control after ESS in selected patients with CRSwNP. However, the choice of delivery method, treatment duration, and escalation to biologic therapy should be individualized according to inflammatory phenotype, symptom burden, endoscopic findings, prior systemic corticosteroid exposure, asthma comorbidity, and recurrence pattern. Early endoscopic monitoring remains important, but endoscopic recurrence should be interpreted together with patient-reported symptoms and quality-of-life impairment rather than being regarded as an automatic indication for revision surgery.

Long-term clinical studies are needed to assess the durability of ESS combined with postoperative corticosteroid therapy, particularly in Asian populations and in patients with non-type 2, type 2, or mixed inflammatory patterns. Future studies should report endotype-specific outcomes, standardized corticosteroid delivery methods, surgical extent, symptomatic recurrence, revision surgery, biologic use, cumulative systemic corticosteroid exposure, and cost-effectiveness of different postoperative anti-inflammatory strategies.

Conclusion

In summary, this meta-analysis showed that ESS combined with adjunctive corticosteroid therapy was associated with lower recurrence and improved quality of life, symptom relief, and endoscopic mucosal outcomes compared with surgery alone in patients with CRSwNP. However, the findings should be interpreted cautiously because endotype-specific effects, corticosteroid delivery methods, surgical extent, and long-term symptomatic recurrence were not consistently reported across the included studies.

The clinical value of this review lies mainly in summarizing available comparative evidence and highlighting the need for better stratified studies, particularly in Asian populations and in patients with non-type 2 or mixed inflammatory patterns.
