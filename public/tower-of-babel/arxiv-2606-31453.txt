Spectral Multipartite Entanglement
Vahid Azimi-Mousolou

Abstract
We introduce a unified, computable measure of multipartite entanglement based on the spectral properties of an entanglement graph and its associated entanglement matrix. This framework quantifies quantum correlations among arbitrary subsystems and partitions of a composite system. We prove that the resulting spectral entanglement measure satisfies the fundamental requirements of entanglement measures. Furthermore, we derive a generic multipartite monogamy relation that extends residual entanglement beyond qubit systems and introduces spectral residual entanglement for arbitrary multipartite states.



Introduction.—
Quantum entanglement, originally introduced in foundational debates
Einstein1935
;
Schrodinger1935
;
Schrodinger1936
, is now recognized as a central resource in quantum information science, enabling advantages in quantum communication, computation, and metrology

;
Nielsen2010
;
Giovannetti2011
;
Chitambar2019
.
While bipartite entanglement is well studied within a relatively mature theoretical framework, the characterization and quantification of multipartite entanglement remain major open problems

;
Ma2024
.

Existing entanglement measures, subject to standard consistency requirements
Vedral1997
;
Vidal2000
;
Donald2002
, include concurrence
Hill1997
;
Wootters1998
, entanglement of formation
Wootters1998
, relative entropy of entanglement
Vedral1998
, negativity
Vidal2002
, and related measures
Plenio2007
;
Horodecki2009
. Although successful for characterizing bipartite entanglement in quantum systems, these measures do not capture the full structure of multipartite quantum correlations.
Multipartite entanglement exhibits a qualitatively richer correlation structure, with features absent in the bipartite setting. Even for few-body systems, inequivalent entanglement classes arise
Dur2000
;
Verstraete2002
, and their classification rapidly becomes intractable as system size increases

. Moreover, multipartite quantum correlations are genuinely collective and obey nontrivial constraints—such as monogamy relations

;
Osborne2006
and hierarchical structures

;
Guhne2009
—that cannot, in general, be reduced to bipartite contributions. Despite significant progress, a physically meaningful, computable, and scalable framework for quantifying multipartite entanglement is still lacking.

In this Letter, we introduce a spectral measure of multipartite entanglement based on an entanglement graph representation of quantum states. The measure is constructed from the spectral properties of this graph and captures genuinely many-body correlations beyond bipartite contributions. We prove that it satisfies the fundamental requirements of an entanglement measure and is scalable to large systems. Moreover, it reveals global and residual quantum correlation structures that are inaccessible to existing measures.

The key idea is to represent the multipartite entanglement structure of a quantum state as a weighted graph whose nodes correspond to subsystems or partitions and whose edges encode entanglement between disjoint nodes.
As illustrated in Fig.

for three- and four-body systems, this representation naturally captures the full connectivity of multipartite quantum correlations. The spectral characteristics of the resulting graph provide a unified and physically meaningful quantification of entanglement in complex many-body quantum systems.

Definition.—

Consider an arbitrary mixed state
\rho
of an
N
-partite quantum system whose
total Hilbert space is

\mathcal{H}=\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathcal{H}_{i},\qquad I=\{1,2,\ldots,N\},

(1)

where each
\mathcal{H}_{i}
denotes the Hilbert space of subsystem
i\in I
.
Let
1<n\leq 2^{N}-2
and
1<M\leq N
, and consider a family of nontrivial
subsets of
I
,

\Lambda=\{I_{1},I_{2},\ldots,I_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(I)\setminus\{\emptyset,I\},

(2)

where
\mathcal{P}(I)
denotes the power set of
I
and

I_{\Lambda}:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}I_{k}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{M}\}\subseteq I.

(3)

For a given bipartite entanglement measure
E

Vedral1998
;
Plenio2007
;
Horodecki2009
, we define the
entanglement graph

G(\rho,\Lambda,E)
associated with the state
\rho
and partition
\Lambda
as follows: As shown in Fig.
1
for two illustrative cases, each vertex associated with an element
I_{k}\in\Lambda
represents a composite subsystem whose Hilbert space is

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{I_{k}}=\bigotimes_{i\in I_{k}}\mathcal{H}_{i}.

(4)

The edge weight between two vertices
I_{k}
and
I_{l}
is then given by the bipartite entanglement between the corresponding disjoint subsystems, i.e.,

E_{I_{k}|I_{l}}=\begin{cases}E(\rho_{I_{k}I_{l}}),&\text{if }I_{k}\cap I_{l}=\varnothing,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(5)

Here

\rho_{I_{k}I_{l}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{I\setminus(I_{k}\cup I_{l})}[\rho]

denotes the reduced density matrix obtained by tracing out all subsystems
\mathcal{H}_{i}
such that
i\notin I_{k}\cup I_{l}
.

The weighted
n\times n
adjacency matrix
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)
of the entanglement graph
G(\rho,\Lambda,E)
is referred to as the
entanglement matrix
.
In the following, we show that
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)
provides a natural way to quantify
M
-partite entanglement among the subsystems
\mathcal{H}{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{H}{i_{M}}
in the state
\rho
with respect to the partition
\Lambda
.

The entanglement matrix
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)=[m_{kl}]_{k,l=1}^{n}
is real, nonnegative, symmetric, and hollow

m_{kl}=E_{I_{k}|I_{l}}\geq 0,\quad\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)=\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}(\rho),\quad\text{Tr}[\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)]=0,

(6)

which implies that all eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
are real and satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}=0
. This allows us to define the
spectral
M
-partite entanglement
among the subsystems
\mathcal{H}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{i_{M}}
as the largest eigenvalue of the entanglement matrix
note

E_{\Lambda}(\rho)=\lambda_{\max}\bigl[\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)\bigr].

(7)

Using Eq. (
6
) and the Rayleigh quotient
Horn2012
, the spectral entanglement can be equivalently expressed as

Symmetric quadratic form:

E_{\Lambda}(\rho)=\max_{\|{\bf x}\|_{2}=1}{\bf x}^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho){\bf x},\qquad{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(8)

Spectral radius:

E_{\Lambda}(\rho)=r\bigl[\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)\bigr]:=\max_{i}\bigl|\lambda_{i}[\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)]\bigr|,

(9)

where
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)
,

Spectral norm:

E_{\Lambda}(\rho)=\|\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)\|_{2}:=\max_{\|{\bf x}\|_{2}=1}\|\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho){\bf x}\|_{2},

(10)

for
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the Euclidean norm.

Network interpretation.—

The spectral radius of the entanglement graph captures the global connectivity, or “entanglement spread”, across subsystem nodes and thus encodes the spectral
M
-partite quantum correlation and entanglement.

Geometric interpretation.—

The norm representation in Eq. (
10
) provides a geometric interpretation of
E_{\Lambda}(\rho)
as the largest stretching factor of the linear map
{\bf x}\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho){\bf x}
acting on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
. Equivalently, it corresponds to the length of the major semi-axis of the ellipsoid
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)[S^{n-1}]
, the image of the unit sphere under the action of the entanglement matrix.

Proposition
.

The spectral entanglement satisfies the following properties, establishing it as a valid multipartite entanglement measure
Vedral1997
;
Horodecki2009
;
Donald2002
:

1.

Nonnegativity:

E_{\Lambda}(\rho)\geq 0
,
and
E_{\Lambda}(\rho)=0
if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)=0
,
meaning all bipartite entanglements between disjoint parties vanish.

2.

Consistency:

In the case of two parties,
\Lambda=\{I_{1},I_{2}\}
, one obtains
E_{\Lambda}(\rho)=E_{I_{1}|I_{2}}(\rho)
, showing that the spectral entanglement coincides with the original bipartite entanglement measure.

3.

Permutation invariance:

E_{\Lambda}(\rho)
is invariant under any permutation in
\Lambda
. This follows from the fact that a permutation of any pair of subsystems
I_{k}
and
I_{l}
in
\Lambda
corresponds to swapping the
k
-th and
l
-th rows and the same columns of the entanglement matrix
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{E,P\Lambda}(\rho)=P\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)P^{T},

(11)

where
P
is a permutation matrix. Such a transformation leaves the eigenvalues, and hence the spectral entanglement, unchanged. This property shows that the spectral entanglement is independent of the order of the subsystems within
\Lambda
.

4.

Invariant under local unitaries:
Consider a local unitary transformation
\tilde{\rho}=\left[\left(\otimes_{i\in I_{\Lambda}}U_{i}\right)\otimes V\right]\rho\left[\left(\otimes_{i\in I_{\Lambda}}U_{i}\right)\otimes V\right]^{\dagger}
,
where each
U_{i}
represents a unitary acting on the subsystem
\mathcal{H}_{i}
for
i\in I_{\Lambda}
and
V
is a unitary on the rest of the system, i.e.,
\otimes_{i\notin I_{\Lambda}}\mathcal{H}_{i}
. For such transformation, one can obtain

\tilde{\rho}_{I_{k}I_{l}}=\left[\left(\otimes_{i\in I_{k}}U_{i}\right)\otimes\left(\otimes_{i\in I_{l}}U_{i}\right)\right]\rho_{I_{k}I_{l}}\left[\left(\otimes_{i\in I_{k}}U_{i}\right)\otimes\left(\otimes_{i\in I_{l}}U_{i}\right)\right]^{\dagger}
.Thus, the fact that bipartite entanglement measures are invariant under local unitaries
Vedral1997
;
Horodecki2009
;
Donald2002
implies

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}(\tilde{\rho})=E_{\Lambda}(\rho).

(12)

5.

Convexity:

E_{\Lambda}(\rho)
satisfies the convexity relation

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}\left(\sum_{l}w_{l}\rho^{(l)}\right)\leq\sum_{l}w_{l}E_{\Lambda}(\rho^{(l)}),

(13)

for any convex combination
\rho=\sum_{l}w_{l}\rho^{(l)}
of a set of density matrices
\rho^{(l)}
with weights
w_{l}\geq 0
such that
\sum_{l}w_{l}=1
. To see this, note that
\rho_{I_{k}I_{l}}=\sum_{l}w_{l}\rho^{(l)}_{I_{k}I_{l}}
, following from the linearity of the partial trace. Then the convexity relation of the bipartite entanglement measure
E
, i.e.,

\displaystyle E\left(\sum_{l}w_{l}\rho^{(l)}_{I_{k}I_{l}}\right)\leq\sum_{l}w_{l}E(\rho^{(l)}_{I_{k}I_{l}}),

(14)

for each pair
I_{k}
and
I_{l}
, implies

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)\leq\sum_{l}w_{l}\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho^{(l)}).

(15)

This equation, along with Eq. (
10
), as well as the monotonicity and triangle inequality of the spectral norm
Horn2012
, proves the convexity relation in Eq. (
13
).

6.

Monotonicity:

E_{\Lambda}(\rho)
is monotone under LOCC, that is, for any quantum operation
\Phi
given by local operations and classical communication, one has
Vedral1997
;
Horodecki2009
;
Donald2002

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}(\Phi[\rho])\leq E_{\Lambda}(\rho).

(16)

This inequality follows directly from Eq. (
10
), the monotonicity of the spectral norm
Horn2012
, and the relation

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\Phi[\rho])\leq\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho).

(17)

To prove Eq. (
17
), note that for each pair
I_{k},I_{l}\in\Lambda
, the map

\tilde{\Phi}[\rho_{I_{k}I_{l}}]:=\left(\Phi[\rho]\right)_{I_{k}I_{l}}
—defined by composing the partial trace with the quantum operation
\Phi
—is itself an LOCC operation
Vidal2000
.
By the monotonicity of the bipartite entanglement measure
E
under LOCC, it follows that

\displaystyle E\big(\tilde{\Phi}[\rho_{I_{k}I_{l}}]\big)=E\big((\Phi[\rho])_{I_{k}I_{l}}\big)\leq E(\rho_{I_{k}I_{l}}).

(18)

The left- and right-hand sides of this inequality correspond to the entries of the entanglement matrices
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\Phi[\rho])
and
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda}(\rho)
,
respectively, thereby proving Eq. (
17
).

Beyond the properties established above, spectral entanglement satisfies the following
inclusion monotonicity
, a fundamental feature underlying its multipartite structure. This property induces a natural hierarchy of correlations within a quantum system and provides a way to characterize the genuinely multipartite nature of spectral entanglement through the generalized residual entanglement introduced below.

Lemma
(Inclusion monotonicity).

For families of nontrivial subsets
\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\Lambda_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(I)
, the spectral entanglement satisfies

E_{\Lambda_{1}}(\rho)\leq E_{\Lambda_{2}}(\rho),

(19)

for any state
\rho
of an
N
-partite quantum system with Hilbert space defined in Eq. (
1
).

Proof.

From the definition above, it is clear that the entanglement graph
G(\rho,\Lambda_{1},E)
is a subgraph of the entanglement graph
G(\rho,\Lambda_{2},E)
. Therefore, the corresponding entanglement matrix
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda_{1}}(\rho)

is a principal submatrix of the entanglement matrix
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Lambda_{2}}(\rho)
, as defined in Eq. (
6
).
The lemma then follows from the definition of spectral entanglement in Eq. (
9
) (equivalently Eq. (
10
)) and the monotonicity of the spectral radius (equivalently, the spectral norm) for nonnegative matrices under principal submatrices
Horn2012
.

To demonstrate the multipartite character of the spectral entanglement measure, we examine its connection to the residual three-tangle
Coffman2000
and show that spectral multipartite entanglement provides a natural extension of the concept of residual entanglement from pure tripartite states to general mixed states in multipartite systems.

Let us consider a tripartite system with the total Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}

(20)

For the choice
\Delta=\{\{A\},\{B\},\{C\},\{B,C\}\}
,
a bipartite entanglement measure
E
, and a tripartite density matrix
\rho
, the corresponding entanglement matrix is

\mathcal{M}_{E,\Delta}(\rho)=\begin{pmatrix}0&E_{A|B}&E_{A|C}&E_{A|BC}\\
E_{A|B}&0&E_{B|C}&0\\
E_{A|C}&E_{B|C}&0&0\\
E_{A|BC}&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(21)

Following the three-qubit monogamy relation
Coffman2000
;
Osborne2006
,

E_{A|B}^{2}+E_{A|C}^{2}\leq E_{A|BC}^{2},

(22)

the
three-tangle
\tau_{A|B|C}
, defined as

\tau_{A|B|C}=E_{A|BC}^{2}-E_{A|B}^{2}-E_{A|C}^{2},

(23)

quantifies tripartite entanglement. For pure states, when
E
is the concurrence
Hill1997
;
Wootters1998
,
\tau_{A|B|C}
is known as the
residual tangle
Coffman2000
.

By using Eq. (
23
),
the characteristic polynomial of the entanglement matrix in Eq. (
21
) reads

\displaystyle P(\lambda,\tau_{A|B|C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{4}-(2E_{A|B}^{2}+2E_{A|C}^{2}+E_{B|C}^{2}+\tau_{A|B|C})\lambda^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle-(2E_{A|B}E_{A|C}E_{B|C})\lambda

\displaystyle+E_{B|C}^{2}(E_{A|B}^{2}+E_{A|C}^{2}+\tau_{A|B|C}).

Since all eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
of
\mathcal{M}_{E,\Delta}(\rho)
satisfy
P(\lambda_{i},\tau_{A|B|C})=0
, the corresponding spectral entanglement

E_{\Delta}(\rho)=\lambda_{\max}=\max\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}\}
is an implicit function of the residual tangle
\tau_{A|B|C}
. Thus, the implicit function theorem gives

\frac{\partial E_{\Delta}(\rho)}{\partial\tau_{A|B|C}}=\frac{\partial\lambda_{\max}}{\partial\tau_{A|B|C}}=-\frac{\partial P/\partial\tau_{A|B|C}}{\partial P/\partial\lambda}\bigg|_{\lambda=\lambda_{\max}}.

(25)

The quadratic form in Eq. (
8
) implies that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}=\max_{\|{\bf x}\|_{2}=1}{\bf x}^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{E,\Delta}(\rho){\bf x}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(0,1,1,0)\mathcal{M}_{E,\Delta}(\rho)(0,1,1,0)^{T}

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{B|C}

and hence

\frac{\partial P}{\partial\tau_{A|B|C}}\Big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{\max}}=-\lambda_{max}^{2}+E_{B|C}^{2}\leq 0.

(27)

Moreover, since
P(\lambda,\tau_{A|B|C})
has positive leading coefficient and all roots are real, the derivative at the largest root satisfies

\frac{\partial P}{\partial\lambda}\Big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{\max}}=\prod_{i\neq\max}(\lambda_{\max}-\lambda_{i})>0.

(28)

Combining Eqs. (
25
), (
27
), and (
28
), we conclude that

\frac{\partial E_{\Delta}(\rho)}{\partial\tau_{A|B|C}}=\frac{\lambda_{\max}^{2}-E_{B|C}^{2}}{\prod_{i\neq\max}(\lambda_{\max}-\lambda_{i})}>0

(29)

and therefore, the spectral entanglement
E_{\Delta}(\rho)
is an increasing function of the three-tangle
\tau_{A|B|C}
. This demonstrates that the spectral entanglement is, in fact, a tripartite entanglement measure integrating two- and three-tangles into a single unified measure.

Note that the monogamy relation and inclusion monotonicity express the same principle of entanglement distribution: extending a collection of partitions can only increase the associated entanglement. Consequently, inclusion monotonicity implies a spectral analogue of the monogamy inequality,

E_{\Lambda_{1}}(\rho)\leq E_{\Lambda_{2}}(\rho),

(32)

\tau_{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}}=E_{\Lambda_{2}}(\rho)-E_{\Lambda_{1}}(\rho).

(33)

\mathcal{S}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{N-1}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(I),

\tau_{n}=E_{\mathcal{S}_{n-1}}(\rho)-E_{\mathcal{S}_{n-2}}(\rho),\qquad n=2,3,\ldots,N,

with the convention

.
Each

quantifies the genuinely

Conclusions.—

We have introduced a unified spectral framework for quantifying multipartite entanglement by representing quantum states as entanglement graphs and analyzing the spectrum of the associated entanglement matrix. The resulting spectral entanglement measure is computable, scalable, and satisfies the standard requirements of an entanglement measure. It is compatible with existing bipartite entanglement measures and naturally gives rise to a multipartite monogamy structure, which in turn justifies the definition of spectral residual entanglements for general multipartite states. This framework provides a new route for classifying entangled states in many-body quantum systems.
