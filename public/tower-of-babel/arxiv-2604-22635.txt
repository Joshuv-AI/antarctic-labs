Decent actions of groups on restricted products
Chris Karpinski

Abstract
An action of a group G G on a set X X is called “decent” if every subgroup of G G with a finite orbit in X X fixes a point in X X and every finitely generated subgroup of G G such that every element of the subgroup fixes a point of X X must itself have a global fixed point. In this article, we study conditions on when actions of groups on restricted products are “decent”. We prove that the action of the automorphism group of a restricted product with base space the projective plane ℙ 2 ​ ( k ) \mathbb{P}^{2}(k) over a field k k is decent, generalizing a result of Lonjou–Przytycki–Urech.

Introduction

This paper examines actions of groups with various “fixed point” properties.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X_{0}
be a set. An action of a group
G
on
X_{0}
is
elliptic
(respectively,
purely elliptic
) if
G
has a global fixed point on
X_{0}
(respectively, each element of
G
fixes a point of
X_{0}
). An element of
G
(respectively, a subgroup of
G
) is called
elliptic
if it fixes a point of
X_{0}
.

A recent question of interest has been to identify and study actions of groups on spaces having the property that every subgroup of the group that locally fixes points must have a global fixed point.

Definition 1.2
.

An action of a group
G
on a set
X_{0}
is
decent
if:

1.

Every subgroup of
G
with a finite orbit on
X_{0}
fixes a point.

2.

Every
finitely generated
subgroup of
G
acting purely elliptically on
X_{0}
fixes a point.

The terminology of “decent actions” was first introduced in
[
6
, Definition 1.2]
, where they appeared in the study of subgroups of the plane Cremona group
\mathrm{C}_{2}(k)
, which is the group of all birational transformations of the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}(k)
over a field
k
. However, such actions have been studied in the literature before their apparition in
[
6
]
(see, for instance,
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
).

Decent actions appear in many natural examples. For instance, any isometric action of a group on a tree is decent (see, for instance,
[
1
, Proposition 2.5]
). For
d=2,3
, any isometric action of a group on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the Euclidean metric is decent (
[
2
, Proposition 9.2]
). Also, by
[
3
, Chapter II.2, Corollary 2.8(1)]
, item 1 in Definition
1.2
holds for any isometric action of a group on a complete CAT(0) space, however, item 2 in Definition
1.2
may fail even for actions on Hilbert spaces (for every
n\geq 2
, examples of finitely generated groups of isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with the Euclidean metric acting purely elliptically but not elliptically on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
were constructed in
[
2
, Example 9.1]
).

Haettel and Osajda conjecture in
[
5
]
that every action of a group on a “finite-dimensional non-positively curved complex” is decent. See
[
5
, Definition 2.1]
for the precise definition of finite-dimensional and non-positively curved complex.

In this paper, we generalize results of
[
6
]
on establishing decentness of actions of groups on
restricted products
(see Definition
2.1
below).

The main result of Lonjou–Przytycki–Urech (
[
6
]
) that we aim to generalize is
[
6
, Theorem 1.10]
(see Section
2.1
for the definition of
G^{\oplus}
and
\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p})
below).

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
6
, Theorem 1.10]
)

Let
G_{0}
be a group acting decently on a set
X_{0}
, with basepoint
x_{0}
. Let
P
be the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over an algebraically closed field
k
, and let
H=Aut(P)=PGL_{2}(k)
. Then
G^{\oplus}
acts decently on
\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p})
, with
X_{p}=X_{0}
and
x_{p}=x_{0}
for all
p\in P
.

Note that by
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
, item 1 in Definition
1.2
for
G^{\oplus}\curvearrowright\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p})
follows from item 1 for
G_{0}\curvearrowright X_{0}
. Therefore, it suffices to prove item 2 for
G^{\oplus}\curvearrowright\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p})
, i.e. that each finitely generated subgroup
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
acting purely elliptically on
\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p})
acts elliptically.

Also, note that while stated for algebraically closed fields
k
, Theorem
1.3
holds for arbitrary fields. Indeed, if
K
is an arbitrary field and
k
is an algebraic closure of
K
, then denoting
P=\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
,
P^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
,
G^{\oplus}=\prod_{P}^{r}G_{0}\rtimes PGL_{2}(K)
,
G^{\prime\oplus}=\prod_{P^{\prime}}^{r}G_{0}\rtimes PGL_{2}(k)
,
X=\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p})
and
X^{\prime}=\oplus_{p\in P^{\prime}}(X_{p},x_{p})
, we can identify
P<P^{\prime}
and we have natural embeddings:

\phi:X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}

and

\psi:G^{\oplus}\hookrightarrow G^{\prime\oplus}

defined by
\phi(x)=z
where
z_{p}=x_{p}
for each
p\in P
and
z_{q}=x_{0}
for each
q\in P^{\prime}\setminus P
, and
\psi((g_{p})_{p\in P},h)=((g_{q}^{\prime})_{q\in P^{\prime}},h)
where
g^{\prime}_{p}=g_{p}
for each
p\in P
and
g^{\prime}_{q}=1
for each
q\in P^{\prime}\setminus P
, with
\phi
being
\psi
-equivariant.

Thus, if
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
is finitely generated and acts purely elliptically, then the same is true for
\psi(\Gamma)
, and hence by Theorem
1.3
, we have that
\psi(\Gamma)
fixes a point
y\in X^{\prime}
. But since
G<PGL_{2}(K)
, we can choose
y\in\phi(X)
. Indeed, given a fixed point
y^{\prime}
of
\psi(\Gamma)
in
X
, we define
y_{p}=y^{\prime}_{p}
for each
p\in P
and
y_{p}=x_{0}
for
p\in P^{\prime}\setminus P
. Then
y\in\phi(X)
and
y
is still a fixed point of
\psi(\Gamma)
. Indeed, given
\gamma\in\Gamma
, write
\psi(\gamma)=((g_{p}^{\prime})_{p\in P^{\prime}},h)
where
g_{p}^{\prime}=1
for each
p\in P^{\prime}\setminus P
. Then, for
p\in P^{\prime}\setminus P
, since
G
preserves
P^{\prime}\setminus P
we have

(\psi(\gamma)y)_{p}=y_{h^{-1}(p)}=x_{0}=y_{p}

and for each
p\in P
, since
y^{\prime}
is a fixed point of
\psi(\Gamma)
, we have have

(\psi(\gamma)y)_{p}=g_{p}^{\prime}y_{h^{-1}(p)}=g_{p}^{\prime}y^{\prime}_{h^{-1}(p)}=y_{p}^{\prime}=y_{p}

Hence, by
\psi
-equivariance of
\phi
, we have that
\Gamma
fixes a point of
X
.

We generalize Theorem
1.3
to the following, which is the main result of this paper:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G_{0}
be a group acting decently on a set
X_{0}
, with basepoint
x_{0}
. Let
P
be the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
over a field
k
, and let
H=Aut(P)=PGL_{3}(k)
. Then
G^{\oplus}
acts decently on
\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p})
, with
X_{p}=X_{0}
and
x_{p}=x_{0}
for all
p\in P
.

For the same reason as noted for Theorem
1.3
, item 1 in Definition
1.2
for
G^{\oplus}\curvearrowright\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p})
follows automatically from item 1 for
G_{0}\curvearrowright X_{0}
, and it suffices to prove Theorem
1.4
for algebraically closed fields, hence we will assume that
k
is algebraically closed in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

As an application of Theorem
1.3
, Lonjou–Przytycki–Urech are able to deduce local to global results on subgroups of
\mathrm{C}_{2}(k)
, most notably
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, stating that every finitely generated subgroup of
\mathrm{C}_{2}(k)
all of whose elements are algebraic is bounded (see
[
6
]
for the definitions of “algebraic” elements and “bounded” subgroups of
\mathrm{C}_{2}(k)
). This is achieved by studying the action of
\mathrm{C}_{2}(k)
on a restricted product of simplicial trees called the “Jonquières complex”, and showing that this action is decent. It is then shown that algebraic elements of
\mathrm{C}_{2}(k)
are precisely the elliptic elements with respect to this action, and that the bounded subgroups are precisely the elliptic subgroups with respect to this action.

An important ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.3
is to establish decentness of actions on restricted products where the acting subgroup has abelian projection to
PGL_{2}(k)
. See Section
2.1
for an explanation of the notation and terminology below.

Lemma 1.5
.

(
[
6
, Lemma 4.7]
)
Let
G_{0}
be a group acting decently on
X_{0}
. Let
H
be a group acting on
P
, and let
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
be finitely generated and act purely elliptically on
X=X_{0}^{\oplus P}
. Suppose that
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
is abelian. If
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)<H
is trivial or it contains an element that has only finitely many finite orbits on
P
, then
\Gamma
fixes a point of
X
.

We generalize this to the following:

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
G_{0}
be a group acting decently on
X_{0}
. Let
H
be a group acting on
P
, and let
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
be finitely generated and act purely elliptically on
X
. If
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
is virtually nilpotent, then
\Gamma
fixes a point of
X
.

Note that we are able to remove the assumption of the existence of an element of
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
with only finitely many finite orbits on
P
.

In another direction, we prove the following:

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
G_{0}
be a group acting decently on
X_{0}
. Let
H
be a group acting on
P
, and let
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
be finitely generated and acting purely elliptically on
X
.

If
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)<H
is trivial or has an abelian normal subgroup that contains an element with only finitely many finite orbits on
P
, then
\Gamma
fixes a point of
X
.

As a corollary, we deduce the following for solvable projections:

Corollary 1.8
.

If
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
has a normal series
G_{1}<G_{2}<\ldots<G_{n}=\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
such that each
G_{i}/G_{i-1}
is abelian and such that there exists
t\in G_{1}
with only finitely many finite orbits on
P
, then
\Gamma
fixes a point of
X
.

We still do not know if Lemma
1.6
generalizes to the case of when
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
is virtually solvable for arbitrary groups
H
acting on arbitrary sets
P
. However, for the case of when
P=\mathbb{P}^{2}(k)
over a field
k
and
H=PGL_{3}(k)\curvearrowright P
, we are able to prove the following:

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
G_{0}
be a group acting decently on
X_{0}
. Let
P=\mathbb{P}^{2}(k)
over a field
k
and
H=PGL_{3}(k)\curvearrowright P
, and let
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
be finitely generated and act purely elliptically on
X
. If
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
is virtually solvable, then
\Gamma
fixes a point of
X
.

We deduce Lemma
1.9
as a corollary of the following more general condition on
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
acting on
P
and Borel’s theorem (see, for instance,
[
4
, 14.65]
).

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
G_{0}
be a group acting decently on
X_{0}
. Let
P=\mathbb{P}^{2}(k)
over a field
k
and
H=PGL_{3}(k)\curvearrowright P
, and let
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
be finitely generated and act purely elliptically on

. If
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
has a finite index subgroup that fixes a point of

, then
\Gamma
fixes a point of

.

Preliminaries

2.1
Restricted products

Definition 2.1
.

Let
P
be a set. For each
p\in P
, let
X_{p}
be a set with a base point
x_{p}
. The
restricted product of
(X_{p},x_{p})
is the set:

X=\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{p},x_{p}):=\{(y_{p})_{p}\in\prod_{P}X_{p}:y_{p}=x_{p}\text{ for all but finitely many }p\}

In this paper, we will specialize to the case of when the based spaces
(X_{p},x_{p})
are all one fixed based space
(X_{0},x_{0})
. In this case, we will denote
X_{0}^{\oplus P}=\oplus_{p\in P}(X_{0},x_{0})
when we do not wish to distinguish the basepoint
x_{0}
, and we will also sometimes use the notation
\prod^{r}_{P}X_{0}
for the restricted product
X_{0}^{\oplus P}
.

Suppose that
G_{0},H
are groups such that
H\curvearrowright P
and
G_{0}\curvearrowright X_{0}
. Then the (unrestricted) wreath product:

G_{0}\wr_{P}H:=\prod_{P}G_{0}\rtimes H

acts on
\prod_{P}X_{0}
via, for each
(x_{p})_{p}\in\prod_{P}X_{0}
and each
g\in\prod_{P}G_{0},h\in H
:

(ghx)_{p}=g_{p}x_{h^{-1}p}

Denote
G^{\oplus}
the subgroup of
G_{0}\wr_{P}H
preserving
X
. We will also denote

\prod_{P}^{r}G_{0}=\{(g_{p})_{p}\in\prod_{P}G_{0}:g_{p}\in\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{0}}(x_{0})\ \text{for all but finitely many $p\in P$}\}

Note that for any
\Gamma<G_{0}\wr_{P}H
, we have a group homomorphism
\psi:\Gamma\to H
given by projecting onto the
H
factor in the semi-direct product
\prod_{P}G_{0}\rtimes H
. We will denote the image of
\Gamma
in
H
by
\mathrm{proj}_{H}(\Gamma)
.

Similarly, note that any
\Gamma<G_{0}\wr_{P}H
acts on
P
via the
H
coordinate. For any
p\in P
, the projection
\mathrm{Stab}_{\Gamma}(p)\to G_{0}
of the stabilizer in
\Gamma
of
p
onto the
p
th coordinate in the product
\prod_{P}G_{0}
is a group homomorphism.

We record the following lemma, which we will use in Section
3.2
and Section
4
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
t\in G^{\oplus}
and let
O
be an infinite orbit of
t
on
P
. Then for any fixed points
y,z
of
t
in
X
, we have
y_{p}=z_{p}
for each
p\in O
.

Proof.

Let
z
be a fixed point of
t=gh
in
X
for
g\in\prod_{P}G_{0}
and
h\in H
. We will show that the coordinates of
z
in
O
are uniquely determined.

Fixing
p\in O
, we can enumerate the elements of
O
as
O=\{t^{n}p:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Since
O
is infinite, the map
\mathbb{Z}\ni n\mapsto t^{n}p
is a bijection, we can identify
O
with
\mathbb{Z}
and write the
t
-action on
O
as
t^{n}i=i+n
for any
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We will also write
z_{t^{n}p}(=z_{h^{n}p})
as
z_{n}
and
g_{t^{n}p}(=g_{h^{n}p})
as
g_{n}
.

Since
t(z)=z
, we have for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
that
g_{n}z_{n-1}=z_{n}
. Denote
S=\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{0}}(x_{0})
. Since
t\in G^{\oplus}
, there exists
N\leq M
in
\mathbb{Z}
such that
g_{n}\in S
for all
n\leq N
and for all
n\geq M
. Up to re-indexing
O
, we can assume that
N=0
.

We claim that
z_{n}=x_{0}
for all
n\leq 0
. Indeed, since
z\in X
, there exists
K\leq 0
such that for all
n\leq K
,
z_{n}=x_{0}
. By induction, for each
K\leq n\leq 0
, we have that

z_{n}=g_{n}z_{n-1}=g_{n}g_{n-1}z_{n-2}=\cdots=g_{n}g_{n-1}\cdots g_{K}z_{K}

Since
g_{i}\in S
for all
i\leq 0
, the latter expression is in
S\{z_{K}\}=S\{x_{0}\}=\{x_{0}\}
. Therefore,
z_{n}=x_{0}
for all
K\leq n\leq 0
, and hence for all
n\leq 0
(since
K
was chosen such that
z_{n}=x_{0}
for all
n\leq K
). Note that a symmetric argument yields that
z_{n}=x_{0}
for all
n\geq M
, but we will not need this.

Now for each
n>0
, by induction we have:

z_{n}=g_{n}z_{n-1}=g_{n}g_{n-1}z_{n-2}=\cdots=g_{n}g_{n-1}\cdots g_{1}z_{0}=g_{n}g_{n-1}\cdots g_{1}x_{0}

Thus, the coordinates of
z
on
O
depend only on
g
and
x_{0}
, hence are uniquely determined.

2.2
Biregularity

We review the notion of biregularity of maps as defined in
[
6
, Section 4.1]
.

Let
(X_{0},x_{0})
be a based set and let
X
be the restricted product of
(X_{0},x_{0})
over a set
P
. Let
G_{0}\curvearrowright X_{0}
and
H\curvearrowright P
, and
G^{\oplus}
the subgroup of
G_{0}\wr_{P}H
preserving
X
.

Definition 2.3
.

Fix
z\in X
. We say that
f\in G^{\oplus}
is
biregular
over
p\in P
with respect to
z
if
f(z)_{f(p)}=z_{f(p)}
. Equivalently, for
f=(g,h)
with
g\in\prod_{P}G_{0}
and
h\in H
, we have
g_{p}(z_{h^{-1}(p)})=z_{p}
. Otherwise, we say that
f
is
singular
over
p
with respect to
z
.

An element
f\in G^{\oplus}
has
persistent fibre
over
p\in P
if there exists
l\geq 1
such that for all
n\geq l
, we have that
f^{n}
is singular over
p
with respect to
z
and
f^{-n}
is biregular over
p
with respect to
z
.

Remark 2.4
.

(
[
6
, Remark 4.5]
)
A map
f\in G^{\oplus}
fixes a point
z\in X
if and only if
f
is biregular over each
p\in P
with respect to
z
.

Remark 2.5
.

(
[
6
, Remark 4.6]
)
If
f\in G^{\oplus}
has persistent fibre over
p\in P
, then it does not fix a point of
X
.

2.3
Topology and dynamics on
\mathbb{P}^{n}

In this section, we review the canonical topological and metric structure of the projective space
P=\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{K})
over a normed field
\mathbb{K}
and study various dynamical behaviour of automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, introducing the notions of
proximal
and
very proximal
elements. We follow
[
4
, Section 2.9]
.

We will typically work with
local
fields
\mathbb{K}
, that is, fields equipped with a norm whose associated metric induces a locally compact topology on
\mathbb{K}
. The norm on
\mathbb{K}
induces a product norm on
V=\mathbb{K}^{n+1}
. If
\mathbb{K}
is archimedean (i.e.
\{|n\cdot 1_{\mathbb{K}}|:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is unbounded, equivalently
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
), then we equip
V
with the
\ell^{2}
norm:

|(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})|=\sqrt{|x_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|x_{n+1}|^{2}}

For each
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in V
.

Otherwise, if
\mathbb{K}
is non-archimedean (i.e.
\{|n\cdot 1_{\mathbb{K}}|:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded), then we equip
\mathbb{K}
with the
\ell^{\infty}
norm:

|(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})|=\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n+1}|\}

for each
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in V
.

If
\mathbb{K}
is archimedean, we equip the wedge product
V\wedge V:=(V\otimes V)/\mathrm{Span}(v\otimes w-w\otimes v:v,w\in V)
with the Euclidean norm by declaring the basis vectors
e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
to be orthonormal, so that:

|v\wedge w|^{2}=|v|^{2}|w|^{2}-|\langle v,w\rangle|^{2}

for each
v,w\in V
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard Euclidean/Hermitian inner product on
V
.

If
\mathbb{K}
is non-archimedean, we equip
V\wedge V
with the max norm, so that:

|v\wedge w|=\max_{i<j}\{|x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}|\}

for
v=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}),w=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1})
.

We then define the following distance function on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, denoting
[v]
the equivalence class in
\mathbb{P}^{n}=\mathbb{P}(V)
of a vector
v\in V
:

Definition 2.6
.

The
chordal metric
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}=\mathbb{P}(V)
is defined by:

d([v],[w])=\frac{|v\wedge w|}{|v||w|}

By
[
4
, Theorem 2.77]
,
d
is a metric on
P
whose associated topology is the quotient topology on
P
induced from
V\setminus\{0\}
and the quotient map
V\setminus\{0\}\ni v\mapsto[v]
(see
[
4
, Exercise 2.78]
). We will not need to explicitly use this metric, however, we still record its definition for the sake of completeness.

We now discuss a class of well-behaved automorphisms of
P
, the
proximal
and
very proximal
automorphisms. Recall that every
g\in GL(V)
induces a projective automorphism of

G
be a counterexample of minimal nilpotency class, so that every element
g\in G
has a finite orbit on
P
and
G
has minimal nilpotency class among finitely generated nilpotent groups exhibiting such an action. By the orbit stabilizer theorem, there exists an infinite index subgroup
H
of
G
such that
P=G/H
. By Zorn’s lemma, since
G
is finitely generated, we have that
H
is contained in a maximal infinite index subgroup, so we can assume that
H
is maximal infinite index in
G
. Let
N=Z(G)
be the center of
G
. Then
G/N
has strictly smaller nilpotency class than
G
. If
N<H
, then
G/N
acts on
G/H=P
with the same properties as
G\curvearrowright P
, which contradicts the minimality of the nilpotency class of
G
. We therefore have that
N
is not a subgroup of
H
, and so
NH
properly contains
H
, hence
NH
has finite index in
G
. Thus,
NH/H
is infinite and
NH\curvearrowright NH/H
has the same properties as
G\curvearrowright P
, so we can assume that
G=NH
. Since
N=Z(G)
, we have that
H\triangleleft NH
. Thus, the property of an element having a finite orbit on
P
is equivalent to the property of having every orbit finite on
P
. Hence, every element of
G
has all orbits finite on
P
. But this means that the group
G/H
is finitely generated nilpotent and torsion, hence finite. This contradicts that
H
has infinite index in
G
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Throughout the proof, we will abuse notation and use the same letter to denote an element of
\Gamma
and its image in
G
.

First, suppose that
G
is nilpotent. Let
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}
be a finite set of generators of
\Gamma
, writing each
\gamma_{i}=(g_{i},h_{i})
for some
g_{i}\in\prod_{P}G_{0}
and
h_{i}\in G
. Since
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
, we have that for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, there are only finitely many
p\in P
such that
(g_{i})_{p}\notin Stab_{G_{0}}(x_{0})
. Letting
Q
be the union of the
G
-orbits of such
p\in P
, for each
x\in X
such that
x_{p}=x_{0}
for all
p\notin Q
, we have that
(\gamma_{i}x)_{p}=x_{0}=x_{p}
for each

and each

. Thus, it suffices to show that
\Gamma
fixes a point of

. Since
Q
is a union of finitely many
G

P
is a single
G

is transitive. If

fixes a point of
X
by
[
6

, so we can assume that

is infinite. Then by Lemma
4.2
, there exists

all of whose orbits on

are infinite. Let

on
X
.

By subdividing a central series for

, there exists a normal series
\{1\}=G_{0}<G_{1}<G_{2}<\ldots<G_{n}=G
for
G
where
G_{i+1}/G_{i}
is cyclic for each

. Denote a lift of a generator of

to

G_{i}=\langle g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{i}\rangle

. Let
\Gamma_{i}=\psi^{-1}(G_{i})
for each

.

For each
f\in\Gamma
, let
B_{f}=\{b\in P:f\text{ or $f^{-1}$ is singular over }b\text{ with respect to }z\}
and denote
\Gamma_{i}^{2}=\langle f^{2}:f\in\Gamma_{i}\rangle
for each

. First, we note that for each

, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any

G\curvearrowright G_{i+1}/G_{i}=\langle g_{i+1}\rangle

g\cdot hG_{i}=ghg^{-1}G_{i}

for each

and each

(this action is well-defined since
G_{i+1}

are both normal in

). Since
G_{i+1}/G_{i}
is cyclic, its automorphism group is finite and hence there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
g\in G
,
g^{N}
acts trivially on

, so that
[g^{N},h]\in G_{i}
for each

(if
G_{i+1}/G_{i}

).

We will show by induction on

\Gamma_{i}^{2}(z)=z
for each

and that
G_{i}B_{f^{2}}
is finite for each
f\in\Gamma
.

For the base case
i=0
, let
f\in\Gamma_{0}=\ker\psi
. Then on

, we have
f=1
. We need to show that
f

with respect to

. Let

. Since
\langle t\rangle p
is infinite and since

is finite, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all

, we have
(t^{n}f^{2})^{M}(p)=(t^{n})^{M}(p)\notin B_{f^{2}}
. Since

, we have that

with respect to

. Therefore,
\Gamma_{0}^{2}(z)=z

That

and

is finite for each

.

Now suppose that
\Gamma_{i}^{2}(z)=z

is finite for each

. First, we show that
\Gamma_{i+1}^{2}(z)=z
. Let

. Recall that there exists

such that
[t^{N},f^{2}]\in G_{i}
. To simplify notation below, denote

.

We will show that there exists

such that for all

,

(s^{n}f^{2})^{M}(p)\notin B_{f^{2}}

We consider two cases.

First, suppose that there exist

. Since

\displaystyle(s^{n}f^{2})^{M}(p)

\displaystyle\in s^{nM}(f^{2})^{M}G_{i}p\text{, since $[s,f^{2}]\in G_{i}$}

\displaystyle=s^{nM}(f^{2})^{j}(f^{2})^{ql}G_{i}p\text{, writing }M=ql+j\text{ for some $q\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $0\leq j<|l|$}

\displaystyle=s^{nM}(f^{2})^{j}G_{i}s^{qk}(p)\text{, since $s^{k}p=(f^{2})^{l}p$ and $G_{i}\triangleleft G$}

\displaystyle=s^{nM+qk}(f^{2})^{j}G_{i}p

We present another general case of when
G^{\oplus}
acts decently on
X
, based on the algebraic structure of
G=\psi(\Gamma)
, though we will not need the following lemma in the proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G_{0}\curvearrowright X_{0}
be a decent action of the group
G_{0}
on the set
X_{0}
. Let
H\curvearrowright P
be an arbitrary action of the group
H
on the set
P
. Let
\Gamma<G^{\oplus}
be finitely generated and act purely elliptically on
X
. Suppose that there exists an abelian normal subgroup
N\triangleleft G:=\psi(\Gamma)
and
t\in N
with only finitely many finite orbits on
P
. Then
\Gamma
fixes a point of
X
.

Proof.

We may assume that
t
has all orbits infinite on
P
. Indeed, let
Q
be the union of all finite orbits of
t
on
P
. Since
N
is abelian, we have that
NQ=Q
. It then follows that
GQ=Q
, since for any
g\in G
and any
q\in Q
, we have:

\langle t\rangle gq=g\langle g^{-1}tg\rangle q\subset gNQ=gQ

and the latter set is finite. Therefore,
Q
is a finite
G
-invariant subset of
P
, so by
[
6
, Remark 4.2]
, we have that
G
fixes a point of
X_{0}^{\oplus Q}
. Thus, it suffices to find a fixed point of
G
on
X_{0}^{\oplus P\setminus Q}
, and
t
has all orbits infinite on
P\setminus Q
. Thus, we may assume that
t
has all orbits infinite on
P
.

Let
z
be an arbitrary fixed point of
t
on
X
. Since
N
is abelian and
t
has all orbits infinite on
P
, the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 4.7]
or Lemma
4.1
shows that
\Gamma_{N}:=\psi^{-1}(N)
fixes
z
. Since
N\triangleleft G
, we have
\Gamma_{N}\triangleleft\Gamma
and so
\Gamma
acts on the fixed point set of
\Gamma_{N}
. Since
t
has all orbits infinite, by Lemma
2.2
, we have that
z
is the unique fixed point of
t
, hence of
\Gamma_{N}
, so that the fixed point set of
\Gamma_{N}
is
\{z\}
. Thus,
\Gamma
acts on
\{z\}
, and hence fixes
z
.
∎

We do not know if Lemma
4.1
generalizes to virtually solvable projections for arbitrary actions
H\curvearrowright P
, and we leave this problem for interested readers to explore.
