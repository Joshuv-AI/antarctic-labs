Two-bump axisymmetric solutions of the Nirenberg problem
YanYan Li, Luc Nguyen, Bo Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we revisit the axisymmetric Nirenberg problem for certain prescribed scalar curvature functions whose critical points include the north and south poles and whose order of flatness at both points is n − 2 n-2 . It has been known for some time that the solution set is compact when the parameter points ( K 1 , K 2 , a 1 , a 2 ) ∈ ℝ 4 (K_{1},K_{2},a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{4} , determined by the first two leading expansions of the prescribed scalar curvature functions at the poles, stay away from a critical three-dimensional hypersurface Σ 0 \Sigma^{0} , and that loss of compactness occurs near Σ 0 \Sigma^{0} . We construct, in dimensions n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , two-bump axisymmetric solutions for parameter points sufficiently close to Σ 0 \Sigma^{0} from one side, where the relevant side is determined by the next-order flatness terms. This gives a one-sided refinement of the previously known blow-up phenomenon near the critical hypersurface Σ 0 \Sigma^{0} : two-bump blow-up occurs from this side, whereas the solution set remains compact on the opposite side.

Let

A.1

Lemma A.1
.

c_{0}=\langle\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}},\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}\rangle=2^{n-2}n(n-2)|{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}dr}{(1+r^{2})^{n}},

(A.5)

\left\langle\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}},\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}}\right\rangle=\frac{2^{n-4}n(n-2)^{2}(n+2)}{n+1}|{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}dr}{(1+r^{2})^{n}}t_{i}^{-2}.

(A.6)

Proof.

We will only consider
i=1
. Recall that
-L_{g_{0}}\delta_{P,t}=\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\delta_{P,t}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}
and
-L_{g_{0}}\frac{\partial\delta_{P,t}}{\partial t}=\frac{n(n+2)}{4}\delta_{P,t}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\frac{\partial\delta_{P,t}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{P_{1},t_{1}},\delta_{P_{1},t_{1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\delta_{P_{1},t_{1}}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}dv_{g_{0}}=2^{n-2}n(n-2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{t_{1}^{-n}dy}{(1+t_{1}^{-2}|y|^{2})^{n}},

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{1},t_{1}}}{\partial t_{1}},\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{1},t_{1}}}{\partial t_{1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n+2)}{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\delta_{P_{1},t_{1}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\big(\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{1},t_{1}}}{\partial t_{1}}\big)^{2}dv_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=2^{n-4}n(n-2)^{2}(n+2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{t_{1}^{-n-2}(1-t_{1}^{-2}|y|^{2})^{2}dy}{(1+t_{1}^{-2}|y|^{2})^{n+2}}.

Making the change of variables

A.5

(A.7)

To see this, we use the integration-by-parts identity

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{a+1}}{(1+r^{2})^{b+1}}dr=-\frac{1}{2b}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{a}\frac{d}{dr}\Big(\frac{1}{(1+r^{2})^{b}}\Big)dr=\frac{a}{2b}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{a-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{b}}dr,

which holds for

. This implies

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{a-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{b+1}}dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{r^{a-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{b}}-\frac{r^{a+1}}{(1+r^{2})^{b+1}}\Big)dr=\frac{2b-a}{2b}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{a-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{b}}dr.

In particular,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n+1}}{(1+r^{2})^{n+2}}dr=\frac{n}{2(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{n+1}}dr=\frac{n}{4(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{n}}dr.

The identity (
A.7
) follows from the above identiy and the algebraic identity

\frac{(1-r^{2})^{2}r^{n-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{n+2}}=\frac{r^{n-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{n}}-\frac{4r^{n+1}}{(1+r^{2})^{n+2}}.

The proof is complete
∎

e_{2}:=\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}},

e_{3}:=\delta_{P_{j},t_{j}}-\frac{\langle\delta_{P_{j},t_{j}},e_{1}\rangle}{\langle e_{1},e_{1}\rangle}e_{1}-\frac{\langle\delta_{P_{j},t_{j}},e_{2}\rangle}{\langle e_{2},e_{2}\rangle}e_{2},

and

e_{4}:=\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{j},t_{j}}}{\partial t_{j}}-\frac{\langle\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{j},t_{j}}}{\partial t_{j}},e_{1}\rangle}{\langle e_{1},e_{1}\rangle}e_{1}-\frac{\langle\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{j},t_{j}}}{\partial t_{j}},e_{2}\rangle}{\langle e_{2},e_{2}\rangle}e_{2}-\frac{\langle\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{j},t_{j}}}{\partial t_{j}},e_{3}\rangle}{\langle e_{3},e_{3}\rangle}e_{3}.

Then
(1-\Pi_{t})(\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{i}})
can be represented as

(1-\Pi_{t})(\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{i}})=\frac{\langle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{i}},e_{1}\rangle}{\langle e_{1},e_{1}\rangle}e_{1}+\frac{\langle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{i}},e_{2}\rangle}{\langle e_{2},e_{2}\rangle}e_{2}+\frac{\langle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{i}},e_{3}\rangle}{\langle e_{3},e_{3}\rangle}e_{3}+\frac{\langle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{i}},e_{4}\rangle}{\langle e_{4},e_{4}\rangle}e_{4}.

Then (
A.49
) follows from the above equality and the estimates in Lemma
A.1
and
A.2

A direct computation gives that

\displaystyle\left\langle v,\frac{\partial^{2}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}L_{g_{0}}(\frac{\partial^{2}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}^{2}})v=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{i}^{2}}(-L_{g_{0}}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}})v=\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\frac{\partial^{2}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}}{\partial t_{i}^{2}}v

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n+2)}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}\left(\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}^{2}}+\frac{4}{n-2}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}-1}(\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}})^{2}\right)v.

Recall that

\Big|\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}}\Big|\leq C(n)t_{i}^{-1}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}},~~\Big|\frac{\partial^{2}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}^{2}}\Big|\leq C(n)t_{i}^{-2}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}.

Then (
A.50
) can be obtained by the above estimates and Hölder’s inequality.
∎

Lemma A.17
.

Let

,

\displaystyle\leq O(1),

and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}K\left(|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u-(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{2}\bar{\alpha}_{l}\delta_{P_{l},t_{l}})^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\right)\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}}\right|

(A.53)

Proof.

\|\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}\|+|\alpha_{i}|+|\alpha_{i}|^{-1}+\|v\|+\|u\|=O(1).

(A.54)

This implies (

).

For the remaining two estimates, we only need to prove (

\displaystyle I:=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}K\left(|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u-\bar{\xi}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\right)\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{n-2}\alpha_{i}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}K\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}}v

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{n+2}{n-2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}K\left(\xi^{\frac{4}{n-2}}-(\alpha_{i}\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}})^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\right)v\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}K\left(|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u-\xi^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}-\frac{n+2}{n-2}\xi^{\frac{4}{n-2}}v\right)\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n}}K\left(\xi^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}-\bar{\xi}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}-\frac{n+2}{n-2}\bar{\xi}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}(\xi-\bar{\xi})\right)\frac{\partial\delta_{P_{i},t_{i}}}{\partial t_{i}}
