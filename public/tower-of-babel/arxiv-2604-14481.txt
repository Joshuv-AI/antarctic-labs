Entropy considerations in Many-Body Gravity and General Relativity, and the impact on cosmic inflation
S. Ganesh

Abstract
Many body gravity (MBG) is a novel modified theory of gravity formulated in a 5-D space-time-temperature framework, in which the variation in temperature is recast as a variation in the 5-D metric. Previous work on MBG has shown that it can reproduce galaxy rotation curves, radial acceleration relation and the weak gravitational lensing of the bullet cluster, without the inclusion of dark matter. In this work we show that MBG can reproduce cosmic inflation, and in the process, analyze fundamental relations between interaction, time and gravity. To analyze cosmic inflation using interacting massless scalar fields, we first analyze theoretically a hypothetical universe with a single massive particle, or a collection of non-interacting massive particles. A quantitative relation between time and interaction is developed using Quantum Field Theory (QFT), which suggests that the notion of time becomes ill-defined for such a universe. The mass terms in MBG and General Relativity cause a discrepancy with the QFT results. An interacting massless scalar field then becomes a necessity to resolve the issue at the onset of inflation. However, the entropic terms in the MBG field equations are seen to be consistent with the QFT results and further accelerate inflation. The slow-roll condition is shown to be a natural consequence of the Euler-Lagrange equations of motion governing the massless scalar field in 5-D space-time-temperature, during the early phase of inflation. Finally, the MBG field equations are solved in the context of a Friedmann metric, leading to inflation. The matter era is also investigated. Keywords : Many body gravity , Friedmann universe, dark matter, Modified gravity, QFT, Cosmic Inflation PACS numbers : 04.50.Kd, 11.10.Wx, 11.10.-z,

II.1
The 5-D thermal field theory

In Ref.
gans6
, the Quantum field theoretic aspects of a non-time varying thermal field with spatial variations, were developed. In Ref.
gans7

(t,x,y,z,i\beta)

We now summarize the Quantum field theoretic aspects of the more generic case of a time varying thermal field, developed in Ref.
gans7
.
The field theory is developed for a Lagrangian in 5-D space-time-temperature. The Lagrangian density in a 5-D space, for a neutral scalar field would be,

\mathcal{L}(\hat{\phi},\partial_{a}\hat{\phi})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{a}\hat{\phi}\partial^{a}\hat{\phi}-m^{2}\hat{\phi}^{2}\right),

(1)

with the index
a=0,1,2,3,4
corresponding to the dimensions
[t,x,y,z,\tau]
.

\tau
is the inverse temperature and varies from
0
to
\beta
.
The sign convention used is (+,-,-,-,-).
This gives rise to the equation of motion:

\partial_{a}\partial^{a}\hat{\phi}+m^{2}\hat{\phi}=0.

(2)

The constraining 5 momentum delta function would be,

\delta(E^{2}-\omega^{2}-{\bf{p}}^{2}-m^{2}),

(3)

and the 5-D integral measure is:

\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{dE}{2\pi}2\pi\delta(E^{2}-\omega_{n}^{2}-{\bf{p}}^{2}-m^{2})\\
=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2E},

(4)

where, the Matsubara frequency,
\omega_{n}=\frac{2n\pi}{\beta}
, for a boson.
As mentioned in Ref.
gans6
,
E
, may be considered the original intrinsic energy of a particle, and
\omega_{n}=iE_{c}
, can be considered as the interaction energy of the particle with the thermal medium. The variable,
\omega_{n}
, determines the decay or enhancement of a particle wave-function with temperature (for example, the Dirac spinor in Ref.
gans6
).
Since
E
and
\omega_{n}
lie in orthogonal dimensions (conjugate momenta to time and temperature respectively), the magnitude of the total energy is then

=\sqrt{E^{2}-\omega_{n}^{2}}.

(5)

It is intuitive, that a particle’s 3-momentum would be affected by both
E
and
\omega_{n}
, and not just
E
.
Thus, one may consider

E^{2}-\omega_{n}^{2}={\bf{p}}^{2}+m^{2}.

(6)

A portion of the particle’s original energy,
E
, is lost due to interaction with the thermal medium. This provides an intuition behind the delta function,
\delta(E^{2}-\omega_{n}^{2}-{\bf{p}}^{2}-m^{2})
.

The operator for a neutral scalar field in 5-D space-time-temperature is,

\hat{\phi}({\bf{x}},\tau,t)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{p}}}\\
\times\sum_{s}\left(a^{\dagger}_{{\bf{p}},\omega_{n}}e^{-ipx}e^{-i\omega_{n}\tau}+a_{{\bf{p}},\omega_{n}}e^{ipx}e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}\right).

(7)

The operator,
a^{\dagger}_{{\bf{p}},\omega_{n}}
, creates a particle with 3-momentum
{\bf{p}}
, and Matsubara frequency
\omega_{n}
.

One may premise the below commutation relation:

[a_{{\bf{p}}_{1},\omega_{n1}},a^{\dagger}_{{\bf{p}}_{2},\omega_{n2}}]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}({\bf{p}}_{1}-{\bf{p}}_{2})\zeta(\beta)\delta_{n1,n2},

(8)

where,
\zeta(\beta)
is a scalar normalization function, and needs to be determined.
Let us define,

\displaystyle a_{{\bf{p}}}=\sum_{n}f(\omega_{n})a_{{\bf{p}},\omega_{n}},

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}_{{\bf{p}}}=\sum_{n}f^{*}(\omega_{n})a^{\dagger}_{{\bf{p}},\omega_{n}}.

(9)

Eq.
II.1
can be interpreted in the following way. When a momentum state
|{\bf{p}}\rangle
is created, then
|{\bf{p}}\rangle
itself can be treated as a superposition of the momentum-Matsubara eigenstates
|{\bf{p}},\omega_{n}\rangle
, with probability amplitudes
f(\omega_{n})
.
Since
f(\omega_{n})
is a probability amplitude,
\sum_{n}|f(\omega_{n})|^{2}=1
.
In Ref.
gans7
, it was shown that the commutator,

[a_{{\bf{p}}_{1}},a^{\dagger}_{{\bf{p}}_{2}}]=\sum_{n1}(2\pi)^{3}\zeta(\beta)\delta^{3}({\bf{p}}_{1}-{\bf{p}}_{2})|f(\omega_{n1})|^{2}.

(10)

Since
\sum_{n1}|f(\omega_{n1})|^{2}=1
, let us assign
\zeta(\beta)=1
, in Eq.
10

[a_{{\bf{p}}_{1}},a^{\dagger}_{{\bf{p}}_{2}}]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}({\bf{p}}_{1}-{\bf{p}}_{2}).

(11)

Thus, the usual commutation relation between the 3-momentum annihilation and creation operator is recovered.

III
Interaction and time

We now explore the relationship between particle interaction and time using QFT. The particle interaction is encapsulated in the form of the coupling constant.
We will look at the three fundamental forces of quantum electrodynamics (QED), quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the weak force, and finally the Higgs mechanism for generating mass.
Most of the basic QFT formulations are based on Ref.
PnS
. The basic formulations are modified for the metric below:

h_{\mu\nu}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c c}h_{00}&0\\
0&-\delta_{ij}\\
\end{array}\right],

(27)

\forall i,j=1,2,3
.
Referring to Fig.
1
, we assume that
h_{00}
is roughly constant within each of the three regions.
The three regions are sufficiently large, in comparison to the wavelength of the particles, so that
h_{00}
can be kept constant. We could have considered the metric
h_{00}dt^{2}-h_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}
.
But, this does not offer any additional insight or generality and only exacerbates the mathematical complexity.
We will show that even this fairly simple treatment with
h_{00}
as a constant in a given region leads to profound implications.
It reveals the discrepancy with Einstein’s gravity in the case of no interactions.

We first model spinors and relations related to spinors. These would then be useful for both QED and QCD.

III.1
Spinors

III.1.1
Dirac Equation

The Dirac equation in curved space time is

i\gamma^{\rho}e^{\mu}_{\rho}D_{\mu}\psi-m\psi=0,

(28)

where,
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-\frac{i}{4}\omega_{\mu}^{\rho\alpha}\sigma_{\rho\alpha}
,
\sigma_{\rho\alpha}=\frac{i}{4}[\gamma_{\rho},\gamma_{\alpha}]
and
e^{\rho}_{\mu}
is the vierbein.
If the metric terms
h_{\mu\nu}
are almost constants, then
\frac{i}{4}\omega_{\mu}^{\rho\alpha}\sigma_{\rho\alpha}=0
, The Dirac equation simplifies to

i\gamma^{\rho}e^{\mu}_{\rho}\partial_{\mu}\psi-m\psi\approx 0,

(29)

Let the solution to Eq.
29
be

\psi=a_{p}u(p)\exp(ih_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}x^{\mu}),

(30)

where
a_{p}
is the annihilation operator.
Substitute
\psi
in Eq.
29
, and
after some algebra, the solution for
u(p)
is given by,

u{(p)}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c }\sqrt{h_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho}e_{\rho}^{\nu}p^{\mu}}\zeta\\
\sqrt{h_{\mu\nu}\overline{\sigma}^{\rho}e_{\rho}^{\nu}p^{\mu}}\zeta\\
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c }\sqrt{p.\sigma}\zeta\\
\sqrt{p.\overline{\sigma}}\zeta\\
\end{array}\right],

(31)

where,
p.\sigma=h_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\rho}e_{\rho}^{\nu}p^{\mu}
,
\overline{\sigma}=(\sigma^{0},-\sigma^{1},-\sigma^{2},-\sigma^{3})
and
\zeta
ia a
2\times 1
spinor.
The spinor product,
\sum_{s}u^{s}(p)\overline{u}^{s}(p)
is given by:

\sum_{s}u^{s}(p)\overline{u}^{s}(p)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c c}m&{h_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{l}e_{l}^{\nu}p^{\mu}}\\
{h_{\mu\nu}\overline{\sigma}^{l}e_{l}^{\nu}p^{\mu}}&m\\
\end{array}\right]=\not{p}+m,

(32)

where,
\not{p}=h_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\rho}e_{\rho}^{\nu}p^{\mu}
.
The results of quantum field theory can be reused with the generalization of the ”dot” and the ”Feynman slash” notations.

III.1.2
Invariance of product of
\gamma
matrices on
h_{00}

If the polarization vector is normalized as

\epsilon_{\mu r}\epsilon^{\mu}_{s}=\delta_{rs},

(33)

where
r,s=1,2
. Then,

\displaystyle h_{00}{\epsilon^{0}_{r}}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\epsilon^{i}}^{2}=1,

(34)

or

\sqrt{h_{00}}\epsilon^{0}_{r}=\sqrt{1+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\epsilon^{i}}^{2}}.

(35)

Similarly, if
k_{\mu}k^{\mu}=m^{2}
,

\sqrt{h_{00}}k^{0}=\sqrt{m^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{k^{i}}^{2}}.

(36)

Finally,

\epsilon_{\mu r}k^{\mu}=\sqrt{1+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\epsilon^{i}}^{2}}\sqrt{m^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{k^{i}}^{2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\epsilon^{i}_{r}k^{i},

(37)

which is independent of
h_{00}
.
This generally true for any inner product,

a_{\mu}b^{\mu}=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{a^{i}}^{2}}\sqrt{\beta^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{b^{i}}^{2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}a^{i}b^{i},

(38)

as long as it satisfies the constraints:

\displaystyle a_{\mu}a^{\mu}=\alpha^{2};~~~~~b_{\mu}b^{\mu}=\beta^{2},

(39)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are constants.
Constraints like those mentioned above would be satisfied by the momenta of on-shell particles.
Finally, for terms like
\not{p}=\gamma^{a}e^{\mu}_{a}p^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu}
, we must square
M
and obtain the trace. For odd number of
\gamma
matrices, the trace is zero.
For a product of two
\gamma
matrix,

\displaystyle tr{\not{p}_{1}\not{p}_{2}}=tr\Big[(\gamma^{a}e^{\mu}_{a}p_{1}^{\nu}h_{\mu\nu})(\gamma^{b}e^{\alpha}_{b}p_{2}^{\beta}h_{\alpha\beta})\Big]

\displaystyle=tr[\gamma^{a}\gamma^{b}]e^{\mu}_{a}e^{\alpha}_{b}p_{1}^{\nu}p_{2}^{\beta}h_{\mu\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=4\eta^{ab}e^{\mu}_{a}e^{\alpha}_{b}h_{\mu\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}p_{1}^{\nu}p_{2}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=4h^{\mu\alpha}h_{\mu\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}p_{1}^{\nu}p_{2}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=4h_{\nu\beta}p_{1}^{\nu}p_{2}^{\beta}.

(40)

Finally,

tr{\not{p}_{1}\not{p}_{2}}=4p_{1\mu}p_{2}^{\mu}=4\Big[\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{p_{1}^{i}}^{2}}\\
\times\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{p_{2}^{i}}^{2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}p^{i}_{1}p^{i}_{2}\Big].

(41)

Thus, from Eq.
41
,
tr{\not{p}_{1}\not{p}_{2}}
is seen to be independent of
h_{00}
.

For a product of
n

\gamma
matrices,

tr(\not{a}_{1}\not{a}_{2}...\not{a}_{n})=(a_{1}.a_{2})tr(\not{a}_{3}...\not{a}_{n})\\
-(a_{1}.a_{3})tr(\not{a}_{2}\not{a}_{4}...\not{a}_{n})...+(a_{1}.a_{n})tr(\not{a}_{2}...\not{a}_{n-1}).

(42)

If
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
, … ,
a_{n}
satisfy Eq.
39
, then,
based on Eq.
38
, the inner product
(a_{i}.a_{j})
is independent of
h_{00}
. If the trace of
n-2
gamma matrices on the R.H.S. is independent of
h_{00}
, then the trace of
n
gamma matrices on the L.H.S. is independent of
h_{00}
.
But from Eq.
41
, we know that the trace of two gamma matrices is independent of
h_{00}
. Therefore, by mathematical induction, the trace of product of
n
gamma matrices is independent of
h_{00}
for even
n
.
For odd
n
, the trace of odd number of gamma matrices is zero.
Thus, the trace of product of any number of gamma matrices (even or odd) is independent of
h_{00}
.

For spin polarized spinors, the probability amplitude calculation can involve terms that include
\gamma^{5}=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}
. Let us say we have a term like

\gamma^{5}\not{p}_{1}...\not{p}_{n}.

If
p_{1}
, …
p_{n}
are on shell momenta, then, the constraint in Eq.
39
is satisfied as
p_{r\,\mu}p_{r}^{\mu}=m^{2}
.
Subsequently, for the metric in Eq.
27
, one has,

\sqrt{h_{00}}p_{r}^{0}=\sqrt{m^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}p_{r}^{i}p_{r}^{i}}.

(43)

Any term,
\not{p}_{r}
, is of the form
\gamma^{b}e_{b}^{\mu}p_{r\,\mu}=\sqrt{h_{00}}\gamma^{0}p_{r}^{0}+\sum_{i}\gamma^{i}p^{i}_{r}
.
One may replace all occurrences of
\sqrt{h_{00}}p_{r}^{0}
with
\sqrt{m^{2}+\sum_{i}p_{r}^{i}p_{r}^{i}}
in the expression
\gamma^{5}\not{p}_{1}...\not{p}_{n}
.
Consequently,
\gamma^{5}\not{p}_{1}...\not{p}_{n}
becomes independent of
h_{00}
.

III.5
Electroweak theory

For the current purpose, the calculations of the weak force are similar to those of QCD calculations.
The SU(3) generators

There are nuances related to the left-handed and right-handed fermions, which are not present in QED or QCD.
The left and the right-handed particles live in different representations of the gauge group.
However, these do not impact our results on the scaling of
\lambda

III.9

So far, we have examined the relation between the coupling constant,

The coupling constant,

From Heisenberg’s uncertainty relation,

This leads to a different inverse temperature,

We then infer a direct relation between the coupling constant,
\lambda
, time, and the inverse temperature,
\beta

The MBG theory incorporates the variation of

in its laws of gravity.
The inverse temperature,
\beta({\bf x},t)

As seen in Eq.
12

To wrap up, during interaction, the interacting wave-functions evolve.
When the coupling constant is smaller, the interaction will evolve more slowly, which means time will be slower.
The interaction is not a point impact as in a classical picture.
The wave-functions are smeared in space-time, and the interaction is a smeared continuous process.
All particles are interacting with each other.
The system of interacting wave-functions can be viewed as smeared coalesced wave-functions evolving together. These interacting wave-functions are spread in the fabric of space-time and manifests space-time itself.
It seems that interactions and time evolution are two sides of the same coin.

\rho^{E}_{0}v^{h}_{0}v^{h}_{0}
and
\rho^{E}_{0}v^{h}_{1}v^{h}_{1}
.
If the entropic term in MBG is equivalent to dark matter in
\Lambda CDM
cosmology, then
the amount of dark matter inferred from the rotation curves and the gravitational lensing should be different.
In fact, the literature does contain references to such scenarios
(
rot_lens
)
.
Another example is that the dark matter inferred via gravitational lensing for NGC6505 is about 11.1%
(
ngc6505
)
, while the dark matter inferred via rotation curves for the Milkyway is more than 90%
(
sofue_mw
)
.
In the MBG theory, the galaxy rotation curve and the gravitational lensing are seen as arising from different components of a tensor.

V.5
Newtonian behavior of
\rho^{E}_{0}
for weak fields at far distances

In Ref.
gans8
;
gans9
, it was shown that
s\propto\frac{1}{\phi}
for systems such as a galaxy.
This led to
\frac{kc^{2}}{2s}\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}s\rightarrow\frac{kc^{2}}{2}\phi\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\phi}
for such systems.
With this representation, we can examine the behavior of
\rho^{E}_{0}
.
For a purely interacting system (
\rho\rightarrow 0
), after replacing
k
by
k_{l}
, the MBG equation, Eq.
26
, becomes:

\nabla^{2}\phi\approx-k_{l}c^{2}\frac{\phi}{2}\nabla^{2}\frac{1}{\phi}.

(169)

We note that Eq.
169
is of the form:

\nabla^{2}\phi\approx 4\pi G\rho^{E}_{0},

(170)

which can be seen as Newton’s equation of gravity with entropic mass density,
\rho^{E}_{0}
.
At far distances, a system such as a galaxy can be treated as a point mass.
This results in a spherical symmetry.
Under spherical symmetry, the Eq.
169
can be written as:

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}r^{2}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\approx K\frac{\phi}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\frac{1}{\phi},

(171)

where,

K=-\frac{k_{l}c^{2}}{2}.

(172)

The solution to the equation mentioned above was derived in Ref.
gans8
. For ease of reference, the solution is reproduced in the Appendix
1
.
1
1
footnotetext:
Typographical errors in the solution are also corrected.
From Eq.
198
, the solution to the above equation is:

\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}-2K\phi\ln(|\phi|)-\phi=\frac{c_{1}}{r}+c_{2},

(173)

where,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are constants of integration.

It’s reasonable to assume that
\phi
will become very small at a large
r
.
This means,
c_{2}\neq 0
, due to the
\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}
term. In the limit of small
\phi
we have,

\lim_{\phi\rightarrow 0}\phi\ln(|\phi|)=0.

(174)

Then,
\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}-2K\phi\ln(|\phi|)-\phi\approx\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}
.
Subsequently, Eq.
173
becomes:

\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}\approx\frac{c_{1}}{r}+c_{2},

(175)

Differentiating and rearranging,

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=\frac{c_{1}\phi^{2}}{K^{2}r^{2}},

(176)

Substituting
\phi
from Eq.
175
,

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=\frac{c_{1}K^{2}}{(c_{1}+c_{2}r)^{2}},

(177)

For large
r
, Eq.
177
simplifies to

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=\frac{c_{1}K^{2}}{c_{2}^{2}r^{2}}.

(178)

This result indicates that at large
r
, the gravitational potential behaves like a Newtonian field with an equivalent point mass,
M_{N}^{eq}
, where

M_{N}^{eq}=\frac{c_{1}K^{2}}{Gc_{2}^{2}},

(179)

and
G
is Newton’s gravitational constant.
As a case in point, a galaxy, along with both baryonic mass,
\rho
, and entropic mass or pseudo mass,
\rho^{E}_{0}
(the equivalent of dark matter in the MBG theory), should behave like a particle with Newtonian gravity at distances far away from the galaxy.
Interestingly, a similar behavior is observed at the outermost edge of the Milkyway. While a flat rotation curve is seen for most of the galaxy, the velocity of stars at the very edge of the Milkyway galaxy is seen to decrease
(
hammer
)
. As a caution, the results may also depend on the methodology used to obtain the rotation curve.
Secondly, it remains to be seen whether Milky Way is a one-off phenomenon.

Let us now investigate the relationship between
M_{N}
and
k
.
From Eq.
169
, since the factor
\phi\nabla^{2}\frac{1}{\phi}
is invariant w.r.t. scaling of
\phi
by a constant, it can easily be seen that,

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\phi\propto k_{l},~~~~\phi\propto k_{l}.

(180)

Since,
K\propto k_{l}
, Eqs.
180
leads to,

\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}\propto k_{l}.

(181)

Finally, from Eq.
175
, we have

c_{1},c_{2}\propto\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}\propto k_{l}.

(182)

Using the relations in Eqs.
172
and
182
in Eq.
179
, we obtain,

M_{N}^{eq}\propto k_{l},

(183)

where,
M_{N}^{eq}
is the equivalent Newton mass.
Since
M_{N}^{eq}
is an outcome of the term
\phi\nabla^{2}\frac{1}{\phi}
, which in turn is derived from
\frac{1}{s}\nabla^{2}s
(refer Eqs.
24
and
26
), one may infer that
\frac{1}{s}\nabla^{2}s
acts as a Newtonian particle for a time invariant weak field and at far away distances.

As
k_{l}\rightarrow 0
, it is seen that
M_{N}^{eq}\rightarrow 0
. Thus, when there are no interactions, like in the case of a single particle, there is no equivalent mass,
M_{N}^{eq}
. Consequently, there is no impact to time.

V.6
Matter era

The result of Sec.
V.4
can be utilized to model the Friedman universe deep in the matter era.
Let us start from the MBG equations, i.e. Eq
136

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}ks^{2}R=\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}kP_{1}s^{2},

(184)

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{k}{s}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}s-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R+\frac{k}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}s=\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}T_{\mu\nu},

where,

T_{\mu\nu}=\left(\rho+\frac{kP_{1}}{c^{2}}\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+kg_{\mu\nu}P_{1}.

(186)

Here,
k
,
s
and
g_{\mu\nu}
refer to the values in the Friedman universe.
But we now redefine
\rho
as,

\rho=\rho_{b}+\rho^{E}_{0}.

(187)

where,
\rho_{b}
is the baryonic mass density, and
\rho_{0}^{E}
is based on Eq.
168
.
\rho_{0}^{E}
refers to the entropic mass found in any local body like a galaxy, galaxy cluster etc.
As discussed in Sec.
V.1
, if much after inflation during the matter era, we were to take
k\sim 0
, then Eq.
V.6
would become,

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}T_{\mu\nu},

(188)

where,

T_{\mu\nu}=\rho u_{\mu}u_{\nu}.

(189)

It is important to note that
\rho_{0}^{E}
continues to be non-zero as
k_{l}\neq k
and
k_{l}\neq 0
.
It is also seen that in Eq.
189
, there is no pressure.
Deep in the matter era, the pressure,
P_{1}\sim 0
, as the baryonic matter is non-relativistic.
For a non-relativistic system, pressure can be neglected.
The inclusion of the cosmological constant in Eq.
188
, results in:

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}T_{\mu\nu}-\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}.

(190)

This is exactly the same equation as the
\Lambda CDM
model, with dark matter replaced by
\rho^{E}_{0}
.
The cosmological constant has been used as a placeholder because dark energy has not yet been analyzed within the MBG framework.
In Ref.
gans8
, it was shown that the pseudo mass (
\rho^{E}_{0}
in this paper) is capable of reproducing the Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) curve for dark matter.
This leads credence to the claim that we can replace
\rho_{d}
with
\rho^{E}_{0}
, where
\rho_{d}
is the dark matter density.
Thus, in the deep matter era, if
\rho^{E}_{0}
is taken to be of the same amount as
\rho_{d}
, then, MBG should reproduce to a fair extent the results of
\Lambda CDM
, for the phenomena that are confined to the matter era.

As a passing note, the modeling of the radiation era and the era close to matter radiation equality may be more intricate, as
k
may not be taken as 0. Also, the pressure due to radiation is not zero or negligible.
Another aspect that has not been covered is the transition from inflation to the radiation era, via reheating etc.
These require further analysis and are planned for future work.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we have shown conceptually and analytically that interaction or entropy seems to be an ingredient in the laws of gravity.
In General Relativity, for the case of cosmic inflation, interaction is captured as an external potential,
V(\hat{\phi})
.
In the case of MBG, interaction is captured by the
5^{th}
dimension of the massless scalar field and the entropic terms of MBG.
In previous work
gans8
;
gans9
, it was shown that for galaxy rotation curves and the gravitational lensing of the bullet cluster, the entropic terms of MBG are able to replace dark matter.
This raises the question as to whether dark matter is actually the contribution of entropy to gravity.
We have shown that a massless scalar field is required for consistency with the QFT results, and fuels inflation in the MBG framework also.
The entropic terms of MBG seem to accelerate inflation above and beyond what is scoped by General Relativity.

The theory of MBG not only reproduces cosmic inflation, but it also hints at the possibility of accelerated inflation at the beginning of inflation.
However, there are certain aspects that yet remain to be covered.
A rigorous criterion for ending inflation and the power spectrum of the curvature perturbation needs to be established.
The matter-radiation equality and the radiation era need to be modeled, which would enable cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation analysis.
These are planned for future work.

Appendix A
The solution of MBG equation with no mass density term

For an observer far away from the system we are trying to model (say a galaxy), , one may approximate the mass to be centered at the origin. As such,
one may assume a spherical symmetry as an approximation.
We now determine the solution to the asymptotic equation, Eq.
169
in spherical co-ordinates. In spherical co-ordinates, Eq.
169
becomes

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}r^{2}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\approx-\frac{kc^{2}}{2}\frac{\phi}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\frac{1}{\phi}.

(191)

Substitute
r=e^{t}
, and subsequently, assign
\zeta(\phi)=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}
. This gives

\zeta^{\prime}-\frac{2K}{\phi^{2}+K\phi}\zeta=-1,

(192)

where
K=\frac{kc^{2}}{2}
. The integration factor for this is

f(\phi)=\left(\frac{K+\phi}{\phi}\right)^{2}.

(193)

Multiplying the integration factor on both sides of Eq.
192
, and solving, we get

f(\phi)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=-\int f(\phi)d\phi.

(194)

After solving, we get:

-\int f(\phi)d\phi=\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}-2K\phi\ln(|\phi|)-\phi-c_{2}.

(195)

One may again rewrite Eq.
194
as:

\frac{f(\phi)d\phi}{\int f(\phi)d\phi}=-dt.

(196)

Solving the integration and reverse substituting
t=\ln(r)
,

\int f(\phi)d\phi=-\frac{c_{1}}{r}.

(197)

Comparing Eqs.
195
and
197
, we finally obtain:

\frac{K^{2}}{\phi}-2K\phi\ln(|\phi|)-\phi=\frac{c_{1}}{r}+c_{2},

(198)

where,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are constants of integration.
