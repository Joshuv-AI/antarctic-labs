Impact of the 2024 flood event on trace elements contamination in floodplain sediments at the mouth of the Nysa Kłodzka river into the Odra valley
Authors: Agnieszka Kałmykow-Piwińska, Katarzyna Witkowska
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates the impact of the September 2024 flood on the trace elements concentration in the floodplain sediments at the mouth of the Nysa Kłodzka River into the Odra Valley. The research integrated geological mapping, sediment sampling (112 samples from 56 locations), laboratory analyses of trace element concentrations (44 representative samples from 22 locations), statistical evaluation (Mann–Whitney U test), and calculation of the geochemical and ecotoxicological indices (Enrichment Factor, Geoaccumulation Index, Contamination Factor, Degree of Contamination, Single Index of Ecological Risk Factor, Potential Ecological Risk Index). The results indicate that the 2024 flood event caused an increase in the concentrations of trace elements in surface sediments compared to subsurface, pre-flood layers (significant increase of Pb and Cd). The applied geochemical and ecotoxicological indices confirmed that the 2024 flood event enhanced both contamination levels and ecological risk in floodplain sediments. Overall, the findings emphase the important role of flood events in the mobilization, transport, and redistribution of contaminants within river floodplains.Keywords: Nysa Kłodzka, Odra, River valley, Flood, Sediment quality, Floodplain, Trace elements

Introduction

Flooding represents one of the most destructive natural hazards worldwide, causing substantial human and economic losses on an annual basis. Between 1994 and 2013, floods accounted for approximately 43% of the numer of all recorded global natural hazards, affecting nearly 2.5 billion people and resulting in about 160,000 fatalities (Venegas-Cordero et al., 2022). Ongoing global warming, combined with increasing anthropogenic pressure, has led to a rise in both the frequency and intensity of extreme precipitation events, which consequently results in more frequent and severe flooding (IPCC, 2021; Ponting et al., 2021).

The frequency and intensity of floods influence the dynamics within fluvial sediments. During these events, the change in the physical and chemical conditions of the sediments impacts the mobilization of trace elements, leading to their release into the aquatic environment (Ibragimow et al., 2012; Zhou et al., 2022).

As industrialization continues to grow, the concentrations of heavy metals and other pollutants in various environmental components are steadily increasing, posing a growing threat to both flora and fauna (Ali et al., 2019; Karnwal et al., 2024; Merian, 1984). Heavy metals, recognized as key environmental contaminants, are of particular concern due to their toxicity, with many classified as carcinogenic to humans (Ociepa-Kubicka & Ociepa, 2012). Their spatial distribution in soils is highly variable and depends on factors such as parent material composition, as well as natural and anthropogenic sources of contamination, including road traffic, industrial activities, and agricultural practices (Michalak et al., 2021).

Flooding has been widely recognized as a diffuse source of trace element contamination in sediments, due to the mobilization, transport, and redeposition of contaminated material during flood events (Zhao & Marriott, 2013; Guo et al., 2014; Todorovic & Breton, 2014; Falkowska & Falkowski, 2015; Antić-Mladenović et al., 2019, Babcsányi et al., 2020, Crawford et al., 2022, Weber et al., 2023). Consequently, heavy‑metal contamination of floodplain sediments represents a widespread environmental concern because these deposits act both as sinks that store pollutants and as secondary sources that release them during flooding or erosion (Asowata & Akinwumiju, 2020; Falkowska & Falkowski, 2011). Heavy-metal contamination posees a risk to the environment, due to possible transfer of metals into the food chain and their leaching into surface and groundwater (Kawałko et al., 2023). Moreover, the environmental risk associated with heavy metals in floodplain sediments is strongly influenced by their bioavailability, toxicity, and persistence (Iordache et al., 2022).

To assess these risks, several contamination indices based on the total concentrations of potentially toxic elements in sediments, have been developed. The most widely applied indices include the Enrichment Factor (EF), the Geoaccumulation Index, and the Potential Ecological Risk Index (RI) (Kawałko et al., 2023).

Throughout more than a millennium of Poland’s history, numerous natural water-related disasters have been documented, primarily floods and droughts. Among these, floods constitute the most significant natural hazard in Poland and remain a major subject of scientific and societal concern (Kundzewicz et al., 2012).

The documented history of floods in the Nysa Kłodzka catchment dates back to the thirteenth century. From the 13th to the nineteenth century, recurrent major floods contributed substantially to sediment accumulation on valley floors, particularly within the flat-bottomed valleys of the Kłodzko Basin. This process was intensified by widespread deforestation in mountainous areas, which enhanced the supply of slope-derived material to river channels (Łach, 2009 ).

The flood event of 2024 in southwestern Poland was caused by prolonged and exceptionally intense rainfall occurring in September. The area-averaged precipitation total for Poland in September 2024 reached 67.9 mm, exceeding the 1991–2020 long-term average (climate norm) by 10.5 mm.

Flooding of the Odra River and its tributaries resulted from sustained and extremely heavy rainfall between 13 and 15 September 2024. The most intense rainfall was recorded in the upper and middle Odra basin (13 September: Złoty Potok—161.5 mm; 14 September: Śnieżnik Massif—218.8 mm; 15 September: Mała Kopa—91.4 mm), during which cumulative precipitation totals locally exceeded monthly averages several times. The area-averaged precipitation total for the Nysa Kłodzka catchment and the upper Odra River basin upstream of the Nysa Kłodzka confluence reached approximately 160 mm in September 2024, corresponding to 280% of the 1991–2020 long-term average precipitation. These conditions led to rapid surface runoff, widespread local inundation, abrupt and extreme rises in river water levels, and numerous exceedances of warning and alarm thresholds. At the mouth of the Nysa Kłodzka River to the Odra River, the flood peak water level was recorded on 18 September, exceeding the alarm level of 530 cm by 173 cm, while the estimated peak discharge reached approximately 2000 m3/s. The average flood occurrence probability corresponds to a return period of 100–500 years (Banasiak, 2024; Państwowe Gospodarstwo Wodne Wody Polskie, 2025).

Unstable weather conditions and insufficient flood protection infrastructure led to serious consequences of the September 2024 flood for Poland. In total 10522 residential buildings and 814 critical pablic facilities were flooded, while 421 buildings were completely destroyed.

In southwestern Poland, the flood damaged 3633 km of roads, 229 bridges, 127 km of bicycle paths, and 1823 culverts. Two major water reservoirs failures occurred in the Nysa Kłodzka catchemnt (the Stronie Śląskie and the Topola). The flood also damaged infrastructure posing potential risks to human health, including cemeteries, landfills, wastewater treatment plants, drinking water facilities, sewage networks and water supply networks. Agricultural losses were also significant, with 3144 flooded farms and more than 29000 ha of affected cropland. Additionally, the flood affected both fauna and flora, which may have long-term consequences for local ecosystems.

Total material losses are estimated at over 13 billion PLN (approximately 3.5 billion USD), while the most severe impacts were human-related, including 9 fatalities, 238,045 affected individuals, and more than 4400 evacuations (Państwowe Gospodarstwo Wodne Wody Polskie, 2025; Springer, 2025).

The aim of the study was to assess the impact of the September 2024 flood event on the concentration of trace elements in floodplain sediments at the mouth of the Nysa Kłodzka River within the Odra Valley. The specific objectives were to quantify the concentrations of trace elements in surface and subsurface (up to 60 cm) sediments in order to distinguish 2024 flood-related inputs from pre-flood concentrations, evaluate the effect of the 2024 flood event on sediment contamination using selected geochemical and ecotoxicological indices and determine the statistical significance of differences between surface and subsurface sediment layers using the Mann–Whitney U test.

The trace elements selected for the study include lead (Pb), copper (Cu), nickel (Ni), zinc (Zn), cobalt (Co), arsenic (As), strontium (Sr), cadmium (Cd), chromium (Cr), and barium (Ba). With the exception of As, these elements are classified as heavy metals. Previous studies (Bojakowska et al., 1997, Bojakowska et al., 1998, Adamiec & Helios-Rybicka, 2002, Müller et al., 2002, Aleksander-Kwaterczuk & Helios-Rybicka, 2009, Ibragimow et al., 2010, Adamiec, 2013) have repeatedly demonstrated that the concentrations of these elements in the sediments (suspended, bottom, and floodplain) of the Odra River catchment exceed regional geochemical background levels (Lis et al., 2012).

Study area

The study area is located at the mouth of the Nysa Kłodzka River into the Odra River in south-western Poland, within the Opole Voivodeship, between the major cities of Opole and Wrocław (Fig. 1).

Location of the study area against the background of a Digital Terrain Model (DTM) with a 5-m resolution, current as of 2023

According to the physical–geographical regionalization of Poland proposed by Kondracki (1980), the area lies within the Silesian Lowland macroregion, specifically in the Wrocław Ice-Marginal Valley mesoregion. The Silesian Lowland is an extensive lowland plain covering approximately 12734 km2, traversed by the Odra River, which flows from the southeast to the northwest. Within this macroregion, the Odra valley typically reaches a width of 8–10 km and gradually descends from about 180 m above sea level in the southern part to approximately 90 m above sea level toward the north-western section (Kondracki, 1980). In the vicinity of the confluence with the Nysa Kłodzka River, the shared valley of both rivers expands to more than 8 km in width.

The Odra River is the second longest river in Poland in terms of total length, after the Vistula River. The Odra River catchment covers an area of 136,528 km2, of which 84.9% lies within Poland, 10.4% in Germany, and 4.7% in the Czech Republic. The river originates in the Odra Mountains in the Czech Republic at an elevation of approximately 632 m above sea level and flows for 854.3 km before entering the Szczeciński Lagoon, then the Pomeranian Bay, and ultimately the Baltic Sea. Environmental contamination in the Odra River Valley is driven by a combination of geological conditions and intensive anthropogenic pressure, particularly related to coal and copper mining and processing industry, non-ferrous metallurgy, electroplating, as well as emissions from the energy sector (Adamiec & Helios-Rybicka, 2002; Adamiec, 2013; Helios-Rybicka et al., 2005).

The Nysa Kłodzka River is one of the largest left-bank tributaries of the Odra River in terms of mean annual discharge (38.3 m3/s at its mouth). It is 189 km long and drains a catchment area of 4570.3 km2 (Kotusz et al., 2009). The river originates in the Śnieżnik Massif, on the slopes of Trójmorski Wierch, at elevations exceeding 1000 m above sea level. In its upper course, it flows through the Upper Nysa Trough, which functions as a natural drainage corridor. The river then enters the Kłodzko Basin, where it maintains a mountain character until it exits the Bardzkie Mountains. Downstream of this section, the Nysa Kłodzka begins to meander and gradually acquires the characteristics of a lowland river before ultimately joining the Odra River (Perz, 2019). Due to steep slopes, high channel gradients, and intensive urbanization in the river valley bottoms, the Nysa Kłodzka catchment responds rapidly to intense precipitation events, resulting in short-lived but high-magnitude flood waves. Major flood events typically persist for two to three days, whereas smaller floods may last from several hours to several tens of hours (Łach, 2009). Potential contaminant sources in the Nysa Kłodzka River basin include predominantly anthropogenic inputs such as industrial emissions, municipal wastewater, road traffic, and agricultural activities. Industrial emissions and municipal wastewater represent the largest contributors to metal enrichment in sediments. Additional inputs originate from diffuse sources such as road traffic and urban waste, leading to elevated concentrations of Pb, Cu, and Zn in more urbanized areas. Agricultural practices, including the use of fertilizers and plant protection products, also contribute to metal inputs to the river system (Helios-Rybicka & Strzebońska, 1999; Kanasiewicz, 1993). Previous studies and field measurements indicate that arsenic (As) is a key anthropogenic element transported within the river system, originating from mining sites and waste deposits in the Złoty Stok area (Drewniak et al., 2010; Marszałek & Wąsik, 2000).

Methods

Field sampling

Field studies were carried out in April 2025 and were conducted along the valleys of the Nysa Kłodzka and Odra rivers. To delineate the spatial extent of the September 2024 flood within the study area, official flood hazard maps were used (Copernicus EMS flood extent data (EMSR756, 2024), NewsMap, 2024). The flood depth on most of the study area was below 0.5 m, but in places it was up to 6 m (Fig. 2).

The selection of sampling sites was preceded by a preliminary spatial analysis based on archival and contemporary cartographic materials. These included the Detailed Geological Map of Poland at a scale of 1:50000 (Winnicka, 1999), orthophotomaps at a scale of 1:5000 (2023), and a Digital Terrain Model (DTM) with a spatial resolution of 5 m (2023) from the national Geoportal (Główny Urząd Geodezji i Kartografii, 2025). The sampling sites were selected to ensure that they were located within the areas inundated during the 2024 flood event and to provide a spatially representative coverage of the flooded valley along the studied river reach (Copernicus EMS flood extent data (EMSR756, 2024), NewsMap, 2024).

Sediment sampling included both surface and subsurface layers. Surface samples (up to 20 cm) were collected to assess the most recent deposition of sediments and associated contaminants following the 2024 flood event. Subsurface samples were obtained from depths of up to 60 cm to characterize pre-flood conditions and to identify potential historical accumulation of trace elements within fluvial sediments. Surface and subsurface samples were separated based on field macroscopic observations, including visible changes in color, texture, and structure of the sediment layers. In general, surface sediment layers were visibly darker in colour, less consolidated, more porous and with higher moisture content compared to subsurface deposits. The applied methodology of comparing surface sediments with subsurface sediments on the base of field observations is a well-established approach for isolating the impact of individual extreme events (event-based sampling). A similar approach, using subsurface layers as a representation of pre-flood conditions, has been employed by Schalk and Jacobson (1997) for flooding on the Missouri River, by Hudson (2022) for flooding on the Maas River and by Khan et al. (2013) for the Mississippi River, based on the stratigraphic separation of fresh alluvium from the stable substrate. All samples were collected using an Eijkelkamp liner sampler set with a diameter of 90 mm. The collected samples were stored in zipper plastic bags.

Sampling locations were positioned in the field using code-based DGPS receivers integrated with ArcPad (Mobile GIS) software. All spatial data were stored and managed within a GIS database. During fieldwork, 112 sediment samples were collected from 56 distinct locations. From this dataset, 44 representative samples were selected for laboratory analyses, including 22 surface samples and 22 subsurface samples. The samples were selected to ensure comprehensive spatial coverage of the entire study area. Additionally, surface and subsurface samples were paired to be as lithologically comparable as possible (in most cases macroscopic analysis indicated the same sediment type), in order to minimize the influence of grain-size variability on the comparison of trace element concentrations. Among the analyzed samples, 14 originated from the Odra River valley, while 30 were collected from the Nysa Kłodzka valley (Fig. 2).

Laboratory analyses

Laboratory analyses of trace metal concentrations were performed by accredited laboratory – Bureau Veritas Mineral Laboratories (Kraków, Poland). Samples were air-dried, crushed (≥ 70% passing 2 mm) and pulverized (250 g; ≥ 85% passing 75 μm). A sieve test was used to monitor the of sample selection and randomization at the primary crushing and grinding stage, as well as monitor the wear surfaces of plates, bowls and other equipment problems. Then samples (15 g) were subjected to a modified aqua regia digestion (HNO3:HCl:H2O2 at a ratio of 1:1:1), followed by inductively coupled plasma emission and mass spectrometry (ICP-ES/MS). Reporting Detection Limits were: 0.1 mg/kg for Cu, Pb, Ni, Co and Cd, 0.5 mg/kg for As, and 1 mg/kg for Zn, Sr, Cr and Ba.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses included a nonparametric Mann–Whitney U test, which was applied to evaluate differences in trace metal concentrations between surface and subsurface sediment layers. For small datasets and in cases where the assumption of normality is not met, methodological literature recommends the use of nonparametric tests as a robust and reliable alternative to parametric approaches (Conover, 1999). In soil and water quality studies, nonparametric tests are widely applied due to their simplicity and their ability to reduce errors associated with incorrect normality assumptions in heavy metal analyses (Przysucha et al., 2025). Consequently, comparative nonparametric procedures such as the Mann–Whitney U test are commonly used when classical parametric assumptions are not satisfied. This approach allowed assessment of whether trace metal concentrations in sediments deposited during the 2024 flood differed significantly from those in older deposits. Statistical analyses were performed using Statistica 12 software.

Geochemical and ecotoxicological indices

Geochemical and ecotoxicological indices, including the Enrichment Factor (EF), the Geoaccumulation Index (I geo), the Contamination Factor (CF), the Degree of Contamination (Cd), the Single Index of Ecological Risk Factor (ER) and the Potential Ecological Risk Index (RI), were calculated to quantify the degree of sediment contamination and to assess the potential ecological risk associated with trace element accumulation.

The Enrichment Factor (EF) is commonly applied to assess the influence of anthropogenic inputs on heavy metal concentrations (Buat-Menard & Chesselet, 1979; Chester & Stoner, 1973). It is calculated using the formula:

(Ae—the average content of the element in the sample; Ac—Clarke number of the element to be determined (Clarke, 1924; Parker, 1967, Zoler et al. 1974); Be—the average content of the reference element in the sample; Bc—Clarke number of the reference element.)

There are five classes of EF (Inengite et al., 2015): depletion of mineral enrichment (EF < 2); moderate enrichment (EF = 2–5); significant enrichment (EF = 5–20); very high enrichment (EF = 20–40); extremely high enrichment (EF > 40).

The Geoaccumulation Index (I geo) evaluates sediment contamination by comparing measured metal concentrations with pre-industrial background levels (Müller, 1979; Zgłobicki et al., 2016) using the formula:

(Ce—the average content of the element in the sample; GB—geochemical background of the element (Lis et al., 2012); 1.5—natural variations in the content of a given element in the environment, with little anthropogenic impact.)

There are seven classes of I geo (Müller, 1979): unpolluted (I geo < 0); from unpolluted to moderately polluted (I geo = 0–1); moderately polluted (I geo = 1–2); from moderately polluted to strongly polluted (I geo = 2–3); Strongly polluted (I geo = 3–4); from strongly polluted to extremely polluted (I geo = 4–5); extremely polluted (I geo > 5).

The Contamination Factor (CF) quantifies the degree of contamination based on the extent to which geochemical background values are exceeded (Håkanson, 1980). It is calculated using the formula:

[Ci—average content of element in samples taken from at least 5 sites; Cn—pre-industrial reference level for the element, e.g. geochemical background (Lis et al., 2012)].

There are four classes of CF (Håkanson, 1980): Low Contamination Factor (CF = 1); Moderate Contamination Factor (CF = 1–3); Considerable Contamination Factor (CF = 3–6); Very High Contamination Factor (CF > 6).

The Degree of contamination (Cd), representing the cumulative contamination level as the sum of individual contamination factors (Håkanson, 1980), is calculated using the formula:

(CFi—Contamination Factor for the particular element; m—the number of analyzed elements.)

There are four classes of Cd (Håkanson, 1980): low degree of contamination (Cd < m); moderate degree of contamination (Cd = m–2 m); considerable degree of contamination (Cd = 2 m–4 m); very high degree of contamination (Cd > 4 m).

The Single Index of Ecological Risk Factor (ER), allowing a quantitative assessment of the potential ecological risk of the studied trace elements (Håkanson, 1980), is calculated using the formula:

(Tri—toxic-response factor for the particular element; CFi—Contamination Factor for the particular element.)

There are five classes of ER (Håkanson, 1980): low potential ecological risk (ER < 40); moderate potential ecological risk (ER = 40–80); considerable potential ecological risk (ER = 80–160); high potential ecological risk (ER = 160–320); very high potential ecological risk (ER > 320).

The Potential Ecological Risk Index (RI) provides an integrated assessment of trace element contamination (Håkanson, 1980), using the formula:

(ERi– Single Index of Ecological Risk Factor for the particular element; m—the number of analyzed elements.)

There are four classes of RI (Håkanson, 1980): low ecological risk (RI < 150); moderate ecological risk (RI = 150–300); considerate ecological risk (RI = 300–600); very high ecological risk (RI > 600).

Geochemical background of the elements was determined for the study area by Lis et al. (2012), whereas Clarke numbers were adopted as the average abundance of elements in the Earth’s continental crust after Taylor (1964), as reported by Parker (1967). ER an RI were calculated for eight trace elements (Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, Co, As, Cd and Cr) for which toxic-response factors were available in the literature (Cu = 5, Pb = 5, Zn = 1, Ni = 5, Co = 8, As = 10, Cd = 30, Cr = 2)(Håkanson, 1980; Dauvalter and Rognerud, 2001; Zgłobicki et al., 2016).

Results and discussion

Trace element concentrations in surface and subsurface sediments

The average concentrations of trace elements in the surface sediment samples in the study area are as follows: Ba > Zn > Pb > Cr > Sr > Cu > Ni > As > Co > Cd. In subsurface samples, the average concentrations exhibit a slightly different pattern: Ba > Zn > Cr > Ni > Pb > Cu > Sr > As > Co > Cd (Fig. 3.).

Compared to the data from 1998 for the confluence of the Nysa Kłodzka and the Odra River (Helios-Rybicka & Strzebońska, 1998), the concentration of Cu, Pb and Zn decreased (in both surface and subsurface samples).

The results of the nonparametric Mann–Whitney U test revealed consistent differences between surface and subsurface sediments across the study area, although their magnitude and statistical significance varied depending on location. However, the relatively limited number of samples may have reduced the statistical power of the analysis; with a larger dataset, statistically significant differences could potentially be identified for a greater number of elements.

For the entire study area, higher concentrations in surface samples were observed for Pb, Cd, Zn, Ba, Sr, Cu, As, and Cr, (Table 1, Fig. 3) with statistically significant differences identified only for Pb (p = 0.005814) and Cd (p = 0.001295). In contrast, Ni and Co exhibited slightly higher concentrations in subsurface samples; however, these differences were minor and not statistically significant.

Spatial variability of trace element concentrations in the Nysa Kłodzka and Odra floodplains

To further examine spatial variability, the Mann–Whitney U test was also performed on samples grouped according to their location within the study area. Two subsets were distinguished: samples from the Nysa Kłodzka floodplain (15 sampling sites) and samples from the Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence (6 sampling sites).

One additional sampling site located on the Odra floodplain upstream of the confluence was excluded from the statistical analysis due to its singular representation. Nevertheless, it is worth noting that, at this site, concentrations of Cu, Pb, Zn, As, and Cd were higher in surface sediments than in subsurface samples, whereas the concentrations of Ni, Co, Sr, and Ba were lower in the surface layer (Sample N37 in dataset—Kałmykow-Piwińska, 2026). These results differ from the patterns observed between surface and subsurface samples at other locations. However, as they are based on a single sampling site, they are insufficient to support any general conclusions.

In the Nysa Kłodzka Valley, surface samples showed elevated concentrations of Cd, Cu, Pb, Zn, and Sr (Fig. 4A), with statistical significance detected only for Cd (p = 0.045153). Concentrations of As, Cr, and Ba were comparable between surface and subsurface layers, while Ni and Co were slightly lower in surface samples, although these differences were not statistically significant.

In the Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence, all analyzed elements exhibited higher concentrations in surface sediments compared to subsurface layers (Fig. 4B), with statistically significant differences observed for Pb (p = 0.045328) and Cd (p = 0.027769).

Notably, in the Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence, Ni and Co concentrations in surface sediments exhibited a pattern opposite to that observed in other analyses, with higher concentrations in surface samples than in subsurface layers.

A comparison between the Nysa Kłodzka Valley and the Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence revealed that, in surface samples, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, Co, Cd, and Ba were more enriched in the Odra floodplain, whereas As and Cr showed similar concentrations in both areas, and Sr was higher in the Nysa Kłodzka Valley. In subsurface samples, Cu, Pb, Zn, Ni, Co, Sr, and Cr displayed comparable concentrations between the two areas, while Cd and Ba were higher in the Odra floodplain and As was higher in the Nysa Kłodzka Valley.

Sources, historical contamination, and flood-driven remobilisation of trace elements

The observed enrichment of trace elements in surface sediments likely reflects the recent input of contaminated material associated with the 2024 flood event. The particularly consistent pattern observed in the Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence suggests enhanced accumulation of flood-derived sediments in this zone, a role of the Odra floodplain as the place where trace elements accumulates that has been previously highlighted by Ibragimow et al (2010).

This pattern likely reflects the accumulation of metals in Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence with the Nysa Kłodzka, supplied from both the Nysa Kłodzka catchment and the upper Odra basin upstream of the confluence, associated with historical mining and industrial activities. The inputs form the upper Odra catchment are primarily linked to long-term industrial activity, including coal, zinc–lead and copper ore mining and metallurgy, as well as coke production, power generation, and chemical industries in the Upper Silesian region and the Karviná–Ostrava Coal Basin. In addition, the discharge of highly mineralized mine waters from deep underground workings represents an important source of dissolved constituents, including Ba and Sr, which are commonly enriched in saline waters and subsequently introduced into river systems (Bojakowska et al., 1998; Jones et al., 2013). Although industrial emissions have significantly decreased in recent decades, remobilisation of previously contaminated sediments and, to a lesser extent, ongoing discharges continue to influence the geochemistry of the river system (Bojakowska et al., 1997, 1998, Aleksander-Kwaterczuk & Helios-Rybicka, 2009). The observed decline in industrial pollution is reflected in the lower heavy metal concentrations recorded in both surface and subsurface samples compared to those reported by Helios-Rybicka and Strzebońska (1998). In contrast, the remobilisation of historically contaminated material is indicated by generally higher trace element concentrations in surface sediments relative to subsurface deposits. This pattern has also been documented in other river systems (Strzebońska et al., 2017).

Elevated concentrations of As and other trace metals observed in the study area may be partially attributed to the upstream supply of contaminated material from the Nysa Kłodzka catchment. A particularly important source is the former gold and arsenic mine in Złoty Stok (middle Nysa Kłodzka catchment), where mining activities conducted from the thirteenth century until 1962 have led to long-term environmental contamination (Karczewska et al., 2013). Previous studies have documented extremely high arsenic concentrations in alluvial sediments in this area, reaching up to 10300 mg/kg, along with elevated levels of other metals such as Pb, Zn, Ni, and Cr (Karczewska & Duszyńska, 2007; Karczewska et al., 2013; Krysiak & Karczewska, 2007; Stachnik et al., 2020). These contaminated sediments are subject to remobilization and downstream transport, particularly during high-flow events, thereby contributing to the metal load in the Nysa Kłodzka River system.

The higher As concentrations observed in subsurface sediments of the Nysa floodplain compared to those of the Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence may reflect historical inputs of arsenic-rich material derived from from mining activities in the Nysa Kłodzka catchment, which become diluted downstream by sediments transported by the Odra River. In contrast, the similar As concentrations observed in surface sediments of both floodplains, as well as the relatively uniform As distribution between surface and subsurface layers in the Nysa valley, may indicate a reduced contemporary input of arsenic. This pattern is consistent with the cessation of mining activity and suggests that present-day contamination is primarily driven by the redistribution of previously deposited, contaminated sediments rather than ongoing point-source emissions (Stachnik et al., 2020). However, elevated As concentrations have also been reported in natural soils around Złoty Stok, indicating a combined geogenic and anthropogenic origin of As in this area, which may constitute an additional source of As mobilized during flood events (Krysiak & Karczewska, 2007).

Geochemical and ecotoxicological assessment of floodplain sediments

The analysis of geochemical indices further confirmed higher contamination levels in sediments affected by the 2024 flood and are consistent with the results of the Mann–Whitney U test. Elements for which statistically significant differences between surface and subsurface samples were identified (Cd and Pb) also showed a clear deterioration in contamination levels reflected in both geochemical and ecotoxicological index classifications. For other elements (Cu, Zn, As, Sr, and Ba), an increase in contamination was also observed; however, it was less pronounced and did not result in substantial changes in index classes. Ni and Co, which exhibited slightly higher concentrations in subsurface samples (without statistical significance), did not show any changes in geochemical index values (Table 2).

The Enrichment Factor (EF) increased in surface sediments for Pb and Zn from moderate to significant enrichment, and for Cd from moderate to significant enrichment (Table 2). For As, the EF indicated very high enrichment in both surface and subsurface layers, confirming its predominantly anthropogenic origin (Karczewska & Duszyńska, 2007; Karczewska et al., 2013; Krysiak & Karczewska, 2007; Stachnik et al., 2020).

Similarly, for Pb and Cd, the Geoaccumulation Index (I geo) values increased from the ‘unpolluted’ class (subsurface samples) to the ‘from unpolluted to moderately polluted’ class (surface samples). In the case of Cu, the contamination level rose from ‘from unpolluted to moderately polluted’ to ‘moderately polluted’, while for As it increased from ‘moderately polluted’ to ‘from moderately to strongly polluted’ (Table 2). For Pb and Cd the Contamination Factor (CF) values rose from low contamination factor to moderate contamination factor, for Cu from moderate contamination factor to considerate contamination factor, and for As from considerate contamination factor to very high contamination factor. The significant contamination with Cd and Zn, as well as moderate pollution with Pb, As, and Cu was reported in previous studies in the Odra River catchment (Adamiec, 2013).

The analysis of ecotoxicological indices indicated an increase in ecological risk following the 2024 flood event. The significant change of the single ecological risk factor (ER) was observed for Cd, for which the risk level shifted from low to moderate.

These results highlight the continuing importance of historical contamination in the study area, particularly with respect to Cd and Pb, but also Zn, Cu, and As (Bojakowska et al., 1997, Bojakowska et al., 1998, Helios-Rybicka & Strzebońska, 1999, Marszałek & Wąsik, 2000, Adamiec & Helios-Rybicka, 2002, Müller et al., 2002, Helios-Rybicka et al., 2005, Aleksander-Kwaterczuk & Helios-Rybicka 2009, Drewniak et al., 2010, Ibragimow et al., 2010, Adamiec, 2013). The elevated concentrations of trace elements observed in surface sediments indicate the remobilisation and redeposition of previously contaminated material during the 2024 flood event. This demonstrates that historical mining and industrial pollution remains an important secondary source of contamination within the river system, despite the reduction in direct industrial emissions in recent decades.

Particularly concerning is the behaviour of Cd, for which increased concentrations in post-flood surface sediments were consistently reflected by all applied geochemical and ecotoxicological indices. Moreover, the ecological risk factor (ER) for Cd increased from the low-risk category in subsurface sediments to the moderate-risk category in surface deposits, indicating a measurable increase in potential ecological hazard associated with flood-driven sediment remobilisation.

An increase in pollution levels associated with flood impacts has also been reported by Babcsányi et al. (2020), who compared contamination indices (Igeo, PLI) in oxbow-lake sediments located on the active floodplain with those from oxbow lakes situated outside flood embankments. Oxbow lakes regularly inundated by floods exhibited significantly higher values of contamination indices.

Overall, the results indicate that floodplain sediments deposited after the 2024 flood are more enriched in trace elements than pre-flood sediments. This pattern is consistent with earlier findings reported by Budek et al. (2004), who documented a significant increase in heavy metal concentrations in Serafa River waters following the 1997 flood (Poland, tributary of the Vistula River), as well as with studies on floodplain sediments conducted in the Meuse River Valley in the Netherlands (Albering et al., 1999) and in the Umela River Valley in Japan (Andarani et al., 2021).

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, due to financial constraints, the analysis of trace element concentrations was conducted on a limited number of samples. A larger dataset would likely improve the statistical robustness of the results and could reveal significant differences between surface and subsurface samples for a wider range of elements. Moreover, an increased number of samples would allow for more detailed comparative analyses, including grouping according to floodplain zones (e.g., the Nysa and Odra floodplains upstream of the confluence, Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence) and examining relationships with flood depth.

Second, the distinction between surface (post-flood) and subsurface (pre-flood) sediments was based primarily on field, macroscopic observations, as funding limitations precluded the application of sediment dating techniques. This introduces a degree of uncertainty in the temporal interpretation of the sampled layers. In addition, no geochemical fractionation analysis was performed, which limits the assessment of trace element mobility and bioavailability in the studied sediments.

Finally, grain size characteristics were assessed only macroscopically, while detailed granulometric analyses are still ongoing. Since sediment granulometry can significantly influence trace element distribution, this represents an additional source of uncertainty.

Despite these limitations, the study provides clear evidence of elevated concentrations of trace elements in surface sediments compared to subsurface layers, indicating an increase in contamination of floodplain deposits at the Nysa Kłodzka–Odra confluence following the 2024 flood. These findings contribute to the understanding of contaminant dynamics in floodplain environments and highlight the need for further, more comprehensive investigations.

Conclusion

The results demonstrate that the 2024 flood event led to an increase in trace element concentrations in floodplain sediments. The Mann–Whitney U test revealed consistent differences between surface and subsurface sediments across the study area; however, their magnitude and statistical significance varied depending on location. Statistically significant differences were identified primarily for Pb and Cd, while most other elements showed higher concentrations in surface samples without reaching statistical significance. This may be partly related to the limited number of samples, which could have reduced the statistical power of the analysis.

At the scale of the entire study area, surface sediments were generally enriched in Pb, Cd, Zn, Ba, Sr, Cu, As, and Cr, whereas Ni and Co showed slightly higher concentrations in subsurface layers, although these differences were minor and not statistically significant. Spatial variability was observed: in the Nysa Kłodzka Valley, elevated concentrations in surface sediments were observed mainly for Cd, Cu, Pb, Zn, and Sr, while in the Odra floodplain downstream of the confluence all analyzed elements showed higher concentrations in surface layers. Surface sediments in the Odra floodplain were generally more enriched than those in the Nysa Kłodzka Valley, highlighting this zone as a preferential area of contaminant accumulation.

Geochemical and ecotoxicological indices confirmed an increase in contamination levels and ecological risk following the 2024 flood. The observed enrichment of trace elements in surface layer reflects the input and redistribution of contaminated material during the flood event and supports the role of extreme floods as key drivers of trace element mobilization and deposition in river systems.

The spatial patterns of trace element concentration indicate a combined influence of recent flood processes and historical contamination. Elevated metal concentrations in the Odra floodplain are associated with long-term industrial activity in the upper catchment, including mining, metallurgy, and the discharge of saline mine waters enriched in elements such as Ba and Sr. In turn, elevated As concentrations are strongly influenced by upstream inputs from the Nysa Kłodzka catchment, particularly historical mining in Złoty Stok. The persistence of elevated As levels suggests that present-day contamination is largely controlled by the remobilization of legacy sediments.

Overall, the study highlights the combined role of historical pollution and contemporary flood processes on trace element distribution in floodplain sediments and underscores the importance of extreme flood events in controlling contaminant dynamics in riverine environments.
