Daily alcohol consumption trajectories and user retention during a national temporary abstinence campaign: real-world data from a smartphone application
Authors: Mickael Naassila, Nicolas Simon, Judith André, Méléna Dreinaza, Raphaël Trouillet, Amine Benyamina, Bernard Basset, Laure Grellet, Pascal Perney
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTemporary Abstinence Challenges (TAC), such as Dry January, are widely implemented public health initiatives and have been associated with short- and medium-term reductions in alcohol consumption. Smartphone applications are increasingly recommended to support participation, yet little is known about real-world user engagement and day-to-day drinking trajectories during these campaigns. This study examined patterns of app use, retention, and alcohol consumption among users of a smartphone application during the 2024 national “Défi de janvier” campaign in France.MethodsWe conducted an observational analysis of anonymized, app-based self-reported data collected between 1 and 31 January 2024. Daily alcohol consumption and engagement were analyzed among 2,032 adult users according to age, sex, and baseline World Health Organization (WHO) drinking-risk level assessed during a reference week preceding the campaign. User retention was examined using Cox proportional hazards models, and changes in alcohol consumption were analyzed using daily trajectories and linear mixed-effects models.ResultsHigher baseline WHO drinking-risk levels and younger age were independently associated with lower retention, whereas sex and binge drinking during the reference week were not. Across all drinking-risk groups of users who remained engaged, alcohol consumption followed a stable weekly pattern with peaks on Fridays and Saturdays. Among users with available data over time, mean self-reported daily alcohol consumption decreased over January across baseline risk categories. Absolute reductions were greatest among users with higher baseline consumption, while individuals with lower-risk drinking showed smaller but consistent decreases.ConclusionsIn this large real-world sample of smartphone application users participating in a national temporary abstinence challenge, alcohol consumption declined among users who remained engaged and across all baseline drinking-risk levels, despite substantial variability in user retention. These findings provide real-world evidence on engagement patterns and self-reported drinking trajectories among users of a digital tool during a national Temporary Abstinence Challenge, and highlight opportunities to optimize future interventions, particularly for younger users and those with higher baseline drinking levels.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-026-04996-5.Keywords: Alcohol, Digital intervention, Dry January, Temporary Abstinence Challenges, User retention

Background

Among available public health campaigns to reduce alcohol-related harm, Temporary Abstinence Challenges (TAC) have become increasingly visible and widely adopted in many countries. Numerous studies have demonstrated health benefits and reductions in alcohol consumption among TAC participants, with effects persisting several months after the campaign in some cases [1]. However, it remains unclear whether increasing participation in TAC initiatives translates into meaningful reductions in alcohol consumption at the population level [2]. Evidence suggests that the success of TACs is partly linked to positive and empowering messaging focused on the health benefits of abstinence rather than solely on the risks associated with alcohol use [3]. Early research has also highlighted the role of social contagion, via websites, email communication, and social media, in promoting participation and adherence [4]. Digital tools have therefore become a central component of most TACs, with the Try Dry® application developed for the Dry January campaign serving as a prominent example of how digital support may enhance engagement.

The long-term impact of TACs appears to depend, at least in part, on sustained participant engagement through campaign websites, social media, and other digital supports providing prevention and motivational messages [4, 5]. Smartphone applications that combine alcohol consumption monitoring with tailored feedback are increasingly recommended as supportive tools during TACs. However, the applications most commonly used do not systematically deliver personalized content adapted to individual drinking patterns, and empirical data describing real-world usage, adherence, and drinking trajectories during TAC participation remain limited. Such data are essential to better understand how these campaigns function in practice and to inform their future optimization.

TAC initiatives were first developed in Australia in 2008 [6] and have since been implemented in multiple countries, including France, Belgium, Québec, New Zealand, and Australia [6]. In England, the Dry January campaign, launched in 2013, is the most extensively studied. While Dry January promotes abstinence during the month of January, it also explicitly aims to encourage greater control over drinking by reducing both the quantity and frequency of alcohol consumption [2].

Several studies have shown that TAC participants reduce both drinking frequency and total alcohol intake compared with their own baseline consumption, and in some cases compared with non-participants [7]. These reductions may persist for several months, typically up to six months or longer [8]. For example, longitudinal data indicate that individuals who complete Dry January report significant reductions in drinking frequency, total alcohol units consumed, and heavy-drinking episodes at six months [5]. These longer-term effects appear to be mediated by increases in drink-refusal self-efficacy (DRSE) and shifts toward more intentional drinking behaviors [5]. DRSE refers to an individual’s perceived ability to refuse alcohol in social, emotional, or opportunistic contexts [9], and is associated with lower alcohol-related harm. Qualitative studies similarly report that participants attribute sustained reductions in alcohol consumption to increased self-awareness, enhanced self-efficacy, and greater intentionality around drinking [10].

Comparable findings have been reported for other TACs. The Belgian Tournée Minérale campaign has been associated with significant reductions in weekly alcohol consumption and decreases in both risk-level drinkers and binge drinking [11]. In Thailand, the nationwide Buddhist Lent abstinence campaign, implemented since 2003, has been associated with an approximately 10% reduction in population-level alcohol consumption during campaign periods [12]. However, not all population-level indicators converge. For example, increased participation in Dry January has not consistently been associated with reductions in overall alcohol consumption at the national level in England [2], and short-term reductions in alcohol sales observed in January do not necessarily translate into sustained changes over time [13].

Reductions in alcohol use during TAC participation can produce rapid health benefits, including decreased liver fat and improvements in metabolic parameters [14], supporting their role as accessible harm-reduction strategies for the general population [6]. Longitudinal and population-level evidence further suggests that repeated exposure to TACs over multiple years may contribute to sustained reductions in alcohol consumption [12]. Using monthly per capita data on alcohol consumption for Thailand based on taxation records over 23 years, this study demonstrated that the Buddhist Lent campaign in effect since 2003 was associated with average reductions of drinking of approximately 10%, compared with the average drinking amount during Buddhist Lent before the campaign was initiated [12].

In addition, participants frequently report improvements in physical and mental well-being, increased confidence in navigating social situations without alcohol, and greater engagement in non-alcohol-related activities [3, 5, 15, 16].

Despite the growing popularity of TACs and encouraging early evidence [4, 5, 17], substantial heterogeneity exists among participants with respect to age, sex, drinking patterns, goals, and adherence [6, 18].

Identifying factors associated with lower engagement and characterizing changes in alcohol consumption across the challenge period are therefore key steps toward improving the design and impact of future TACs. In January 2024, the French Society of Alcohology and Addictology deployed the MyDéfi® smartphone application for the first time as part of the national “Défi de janvier” campaign. The application enables users to track alcohol consumption daily, receive personalized feedback, and engage with motivational content throughout the challenge, while allowing for anonymous extraction of aggregated data.

The aim of this study was to analyze user retention and daily alcohol consumption trajectories among MyDéfi® users during the 2024 “Défi de janvier” campaign, with a specific focus on age, sex, and baseline World Health Organization drinking-risk levels.

Methods

Study design and data source

This study is an observational analysis of anonymized, self-reported data collected via the MyDéfi® smartphone application during the national “Défi de janvier” Temporary Abstinence Challenge (TAC) in France, conducted from 1 to 31 January 2024. The analysis was not preregistered.

The MyDéfi® application allows users to record daily alcohol consumption and to receive personalized feedback throughout the challenge. Data were extracted anonymously and aggregated for research purposes by the application developer, in compliance with General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) requirements.

Participants

All individuals who downloaded the MyDéfi® application and entered at least one alcohol consumption record between 1 and 31 January 2024 were eligible for inclusion. Upon installation of the application, users were informed that anonymized self-reported data could be used for research purposes and provided informed consent under these conditions.

The present study did not involve active recruitment, targeted outreach, or predefined sampling strategies. All individuals who downloaded the MyDéfi® smartphone application and entered at least one alcohol consumption record during the observation period were eligible for inclusion. In particular, no recruitment quotas were applied with respect to sex, age, or baseline drinking level; the observed demographic distribution reflects natural uptake and use of the application under real-world conditions rather than a recruitment design. Upon installation, users were informed that anonymized self-reported data could be used for research purposes in accordance with General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) requirements, and data were analyzed only for users who agreed to these conditions. However, because data collection was fully anonymized and implemented as part of a nationwide public health deployment, individual-level information on users who declined consent or opted out was not retained, and the exact proportion of informed users who opted in or out cannot be quantified. For the purposes of the present analysis, the analytic sample was further restricted to users whose first day of protocol entry (Day 1) occurred during the official “Défi de janvier” period (January 1–31, 2024). This restriction was applied to ensure that all included participants initiated the challenge within the same temporal window. As a result, the transition from the initial dataset to the final analytic sample reflects a single inclusion criterion based on timing of first entry, rather than sequential exclusions or attrition steps.

Although registration for the “Défi de janvier” campaign was possible through the campaign website without downloading the application, the present study includes only participants who used the MyDéfi® smartphone application. No exclusion criteria were applied based on age, sex, or baseline drinking level.

Ancillary online follow-up survey (independent dataset)

In parallel with the app deployment, an independent online follow-up survey was conducted among “Défi de janvier” participants to assess weekly alcohol consumption at baseline and at 1 and 3 months after the challenge. Respondents reported whether they used MyDéfi® during January and whether they continued using it thereafter. These survey data were independent from the app database and are presented as complementary observational evidence; comparisons are non-causal due to self-selection into app use and follow-up attrition. Additional methodological details are provided in Supplementary Methods.

Variables and measures

Demographic characteristics

Demographic variables extracted from the database included age and sex assigned at birth, as reported by users at registration.

Alcohol consumption

Alcohol consumption was self-reported daily using the application’s consumption diary and expressed in units of alcohol (UA), where one unit corresponds to 10 g of pure ethanol. Users were able to enter consumption either daily or retrospectively (typically weekly).

Baseline alcohol consumption was assessed during a reference period corresponding to the seven days preceding the user’s first use of the application. To reduce variability related to holidays or atypical weeks, users were instructed to report their alcohol consumption for a typical week rather than strictly adhering to calendar dates.

Baseline alcohol consumption was categorized according to World Health Organization (WHO) drinking-risk levels. For men, very-high-risk drinking was defined as > 100 g/day, high-risk as > 60–100 g/day, medium-risk as > 40–60 g/day, and low-risk as 1–40 g/day. For women, corresponding thresholds were > 60 g/day, > 40–60 g/day, > 20–40 g/day, and 1–20 g/day, respectively.

Abstinence refers to days with zero reported alcohol units, whereas non-drinking days reflect the absence of any alcohol intake regardless of overall consumption trajectory.

Binge drinking

When a recorded consumption reached ≥ six units in women or ≥ seven units in men, users were prompted to indicate the time period over which the drinks had been consumed. If intake occurred within two hours or less, the episode was classified as binge drinking, in line with previously proposed conceptualizations [19].

User retention and adherence

Daily completion of the alcohol consumption diary was used as a proxy measure of user engagement. Days without entries were treated as missing data and contributed to the operational definition of dropout.

Retention was defined as the presence of at least 15 days with completed diary entries during January. Dropout was defined as more than five consecutive days without recorded entries. No imputation was performed for missing data.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics are presented as means and standard deviations for continuous variables and as counts and percentages for categorical variables.

Predictors of dropout

Predictors of dropout were examined using Cox proportional hazards regression models. Two multivariable models were initially tested: the first included age, sex, and baseline WHO drinking-risk level; the second replaced WHO drinking-risk level with the presence or absence of binge drinking during the reference week. Because the model including WHO drinking-risk levels demonstrated superior explanatory power, it was retained.

Covariates with p-values > 0.05 were subsequently removed. Sex did not reach statistical significance (HR 0.99, 95% CI [0.88–1.12], p = 0.94) and was excluded from the final model, which included age and baseline WHO drinking-risk level. Proportional hazards assumptions were assessed using Schoenfeld residuals.

Changes in alcohol consumption

Daily alcohol consumption trajectories were examined separately for each baseline WHO drinking-risk category. In addition, changes in mean daily alcohol consumption were analyzed using linear mixed-effects models fitted with restricted maximum likelihood (REML).

The primary model assessed mean alcohol consumption as a function of time (baseline reference week vs. last week of January), baseline WHO drinking-risk level, and their interaction, using the following specification:

Mean alcohol consumption ~ Week × Baseline WHO drinking-risk level.

Standardized coefficients were obtained by fitting the model to standardized data. Confidence intervals were computed using Wald t-approximations. Model performance was assessed using marginal and conditional R² values.

The model demonstrated substantial explanatory power (conditional R² = 0.78; marginal R² = 0.62). The intercept, corresponding to the baseline week and low-risk group, was 1.45 (95% CI [1.34–1.55], p < 0.001). Standardized coefficients were obtained by fitting the model to a standardized dataset. Confidence intervals were computed using Wald t-approximations.

Characteristics of the Mydéfi® smartphone application

MyDéfi® is the official application of the French Society of Alcohology & Addictology (SF2A), designed to support alcohol-reduction efforts through personalized algorithm-driven messages. Initially developed as a 12-week brief intervention tool [20], the application was adapted for the “Défi de janvier” campaign, including a condensed four-week program with specific messages tailored to temporary abstinence objectives. A gamified element—a tree gradually blooming as participants achieved daily abstinence—was added during the challenge.

At the start of the intervention, users entered their age, sex assigned at birth, and their alcohol consumption over the preceding seven days. The app then provided personalized feedback based on WHO drinking-risk categories, highlighted drinking patterns (e.g., binge episodes), and proposed entry into the program. All data are encrypted to preserve anonymity. Personalized feedback is based on motivational interviewing [21] and cognitive-behavioral therapy [22] as foundational frameworks for the app’s algorithms.

The algorithm determined the most appropriate program (reduction of chronic consumption or reduction of binge drinking in adults or young adults ≤ 25 years). For younger users, messages were adapted in tone and content. The algorithm accounts for multiple factors, including daily consumption, sex, and the stage of the program (early, middle, or late phase). Users recorded consumption daily or retrospectively (weekly on Sundays). Users received daily messages that depended on the different data previously mentioned allowing highly personalized messages. Daily messages included: (i) Advice for reducing consumption, ensuring gradual reduction to prevent withdrawal symptoms. (ii) Educational messages on the physical, psychological, social, and professional harms of alcohol, and on the benefits of reducing intake. (iii) Motivational messages, supporting intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy. (iv) Messages promoting adherence, encouraging consistent engagement with the application. (v) During January, daily reinforcement of alcohol-free days.

The app also provided visual summaries and dynamic representations of consumption patterns that evolve over time.

Statistics

This observational analysis was not preregistered. Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.1.2) [23] with the tidyverse [24], survival and survminer [25] packages, and lme4 (version 1.1–27.1) for linear mixed-effects models. The lmerTest package (version 3.1-3) was used to compute p-values based on Satterthwaite’s method. Figures were generated using ggplot2 (version 3.3.5).

Continuous variables are presented as means, standard deviations, and medians with ranges; categorical variables as counts and percentages.

Retention was defined as ≥ 15 days of data entry, while dropout was defined as > 5 consecutive days without entries. Daily diary entries were analyzed as observed; missing days were not imputed and were incorporated into the operational definition of dropout. Covariates associated with dropout risk were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression. Proportional hazards assumptions were examined using Schoenfeld residuals.

A linear mixed-effects model assessed predictors of mean daily alcohol consumption, using the formula described above.

Discussion

Principal findings

Using daily real-world data collected via a smartphone application during a national Temporary Abstinence Challenge, this study provides a detailed characterization of user retention and alcohol consumption trajectories throughout the month of January. Three main findings emerge. First, user retention declined over time and was strongly associated with baseline drinking-risk level and age, with younger participants and those reporting higher baseline consumption being more likely to disengage. Second, alcohol consumption followed a highly stable weekly rhythm across all drinking-risk categories, with consistent peaks on Fridays and Saturdays. Third, mean daily alcohol consumption decreased significantly over the course of the challenge in all baseline WHO risk groups, with the largest absolute reductions observed among participants with higher initial consumption.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to simultaneously examine daily engagement and day-to-day alcohol consumption trajectories among smartphone application users during a Temporary Abstinence Challenge. By leveraging high-frequency digital data collected under real-world conditions, the present analysis complements the existing TAC literature, which has largely relied on retrospective or sparsely sampled self-reported measures [4–6]. This approach provides novel insights into both behavioral dynamics and engagement processes during abstinence-oriented public health campaigns.

User characteristics and engagement

The demographic profile of participants indicates that the “Défi de janvier” attracted a broad adult population, with comparable participation among men and women and a wide age range extending well beyond middle adulthood. This contrasts with earlier TAC studies reporting a predominance of women among participants [15, 26], but is consistent with more recent evidence suggesting a progressive diversification of participant profiles over time [6].

Although most users reported low-risk alcohol consumption at baseline, a substantial proportion met criteria for high or very-high WHO drinking-risk levels. This finding aligns with prior research showing that TACs often attract individuals who already perceive their drinking as problematic and who display greater readiness to change [6, 17, 27]. From a public health perspective, this reinforces the relevance of TACs as harm-reduction strategies capable of reaching individuals at elevated risk of alcohol-related harm, even if sustained engagement remains uneven across subgroups.

Retention analyses revealed that higher baseline drinking-risk levels were associated with an increased likelihood of dropout, while older age predicted better engagement. Similar patterns have been reported in previous TAC studies, in which younger individuals and heavier drinkers were less likely to complete abstinence challenges [1, 6, 7]. In contrast, binge drinking during the reference week was not independently associated with dropout in our analyses, although this may reflect limited sensitivity related to the short baseline assessment window and the episodic nature of binge drinking behaviors [19].

Taken together, these findings highlight the need for digital interventions embedded within TACs to be better tailored to younger users and individuals with higher-risk drinking patterns. These groups may face greater cognitive, social, or motivational barriers to sustained engagement during abstinence-oriented initiatives and may therefore benefit from more flexible goals, adaptive messaging, and enhanced support features.

Alcohol consumption trajectories

Daily monitoring revealed a remarkably stable weekly rhythm in alcohol consumption across the entire month, characterized by systematic peaks on Fridays and Saturdays. This pattern was consistent across all baseline WHO drinking-risk categories of users who remained engaged, with absolute consumption levels scaling proportionally with baseline risk. Such temporal regularity underscores the persistence of socially structured drinking habits, even during periods of intentional abstinence or reduction.

Despite this stable weekly pattern, a significant overall reduction in alcohol consumption was observed in all baseline risk groups over the course of the challenge. Reductions were proportional to initial consumption levels: participants with lower baseline intake exhibited the greatest relative decreases, whereas those with high and very-high-risk drinking achieved the largest absolute reductions. Although regression to the mean may partially contribute to these findings, the magnitude and consistency of reductions suggest a meaningful behavioral change associated with TAC participation.

This pattern has important implications for harm reduction. While heavier drinkers were more likely to disengage, those who remained engaged experienced the most substantial absolute reductions in consumption, indicating that TAC-linked digital interventions may offer considerable benefit for high-risk individuals who sustain participation. Similar observations have been reported in other abstinence-focused initiatives, such as the Belgian Tournée Minérale, where most participants pursued abstinence goals but meaningful reductions were also observed among those with higher baseline consumption [28]. Likewise, recent French data indicate that the majority of Dry January participants aim for abstinence, with a smaller but significant proportion targeting reduction rather than complete cessation [27].

In addition to the app-based analyses, the ancillary follow-up survey provides complementary insights into short- and medium-term drinking outcomes among “Défi de janvier” participants according to self-reported app use. While no difference in weekly alcohol consumption change was observed between app users and non-users at 1 month, a significantly greater reduction was reported at 3 months among participants who continued to use the application. Although these results must be interpreted with caution due to self-selection into app use and attrition during follow-up, the observed temporal pattern is noteworthy. The absence of group differences at 1 month suggests that initial reductions may largely reflect shared campaign-related motivation, social norms, or seasonal effects associated with January abstinence challenges. In contrast, the emergence of a difference at 3 months is consistent with the hypothesis that continued engagement with a digital support tool may help consolidate behavioral changes beyond the immediate challenge period. This delayed divergence aligns with theoretical models of behavior change, according to which sustained reductions in alcohol consumption are mediated by progressive increases in self-efficacy, self-monitoring, and intentional drinking practices rather than by short-term abstinence alone. Continued app use may facilitate these processes by reinforcing awareness of drinking patterns, providing ongoing motivational feedback, and supporting goal maintenance after the campaign has ended. Importantly, these ancillary findings do not provide causal evidence of app effectiveness and should not be interpreted as such. Rather, they offer contextual observational support suggesting that continued digital engagement may be associated with more durable reductions in alcohol consumption among motivated participants. Taken together with the main app-based trajectory analyses, these results highlight the potential relevance of sustained post-challenge support as a key determinant of longer-term outcomes in TACs.

Strengths and limitations

This study is, to our knowledge, the first to describe day-to-day engagement and alcohol consumption changes among users of a smartphone application deployed during a national TAC. The large sample size and the availability of daily consumption data provide an unprecedented level of temporal resolution for understanding drinking behaviors and engagement dynamics during abstinence challenges 6).

Several limitations should nevertheless be acknowledged. Alcohol consumption was self-reported, which introduces potential reporting bias, particularly in the context of an abstinence-oriented challenge. The present analyses therefore focus on within-individual trajectories of reported consumption rather than absolute intake levels. Selection bias is likely at multiple stages, including self-selection into the campaign, voluntary app download, and continued app use over time. As a result, the findings may not be generalizable to all TAC participants or to the broader drinking population. Data from non-app users were unavailable, and the observational design without a control group precludes causal inference regarding the effectiveness of the application itself. While the main analyses lacked a contemporaneous non-app comparator within the app dataset, an independent online follow-up survey conducted among Défi de janvier participants provided a partial within-campaign comparison between app users and non-users at 1 and 3 months. The absence of differences at 1 month and the larger reductions at 3 months among continued users are compatible with a potential medium-term association, but remain subject to strong confounding by motivation and self-selection and therefore do not establish effectiveness. Importantly, complementary survey-based comparisons between app users and non-users are observational and subject to strong self-selection; they should be interpreted as contextual evidence rather than as estimates of intervention effectiveness. Finally, baseline consumption may have been influenced by holiday-related drinking patterns, and downloading the app does not necessarily imply full or sustained participation in the TAC.

Conclusions

Daily monitoring of participants during the 2024 “Défi de janvier” has yielded valuable insights for future TAC interventions. Complementary survey data suggest that continued engagement with a digital support tool after the challenge may be associated with more sustained reductions in alcohol consumption, underscoring the importance of post-challenge support strategies and warranting controlled evaluation. To enhance their impact, future applications should particularly target younger users and heavy drinkers, two groups at elevated risk of disengagement, while also reinforcing end-of-week engagement through personalized messaging and gamified features. These findings describe trajectories of self-reported alcohol consumption among app users under real-world conditions and should be interpreted as descriptive evidence rather than as evidence of biologically verified reductions in alcohol intake.
