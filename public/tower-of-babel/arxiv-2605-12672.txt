Expander Evolution Algebras
Piero Giacomelli

Abstract
. We introduce expander evolution algebras (EEAs), a class of nonassociative algebras defined over an arbitrary field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} in which the underlying undirected loopless graph of the algebra—in the sense of Kowalski—is an expander graph in the classical sense of Cheeger. Starting from the formal graph definition of Kowalski and the algebraic framework of Tian, we establish a dictionary between combinatorial expansion and algebraic structure: the Cheeger constant of the associated graph governs connectivity, the subalgebra lattice, the growth of the evolution sequence, and—over ℝ \mathbb{R} and ℂ \mathbb{C} —the spectral gap of the evolution operator. Over a general field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} we prove that EEAs are always connected and simple (as evolution algebras), carry no proper large evolution subalgebras, and that every generator of a symmetric EEA is algebraically persistent. Over ℂ \mathbb{C} we obtain the sharp Alon–Boppana lower bound for the second eigenvalue of the evolution operator, leading to the definition of Ramanujan evolution algebras as optimal expanders. We also construct families of EEAs from Cayley graphs of finite groups. We close with open problems.

1.
Introduction

Evolution algebras, introduced by Tian and Vojtechovsky
[
15
]

and developed systematically in the monograph
[
14
]
, are
nonassociative, commutative, and non-power-associative algebras over a
field
\mathbb{K}
. Their defining feature is a distinguished
natural
basis

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
in which distinct basis elements multiply
to zero, while
e_{i}^{2}=\sum_{k}a_{ik}e_{k}
for structural constants

a_{ik}\in\mathbb{K}
. Behind this rule lies varied structure: discrete dynamical systems, Markov chains,
non-Mendelian genetics, and graph combinatorics all admit natural
descriptions within this framework.

Expander graphs, in contrast, are finite graphs that are simultaneously

sparse
(bounded degree) and
highly connected

(large Cheeger constant). Introduced implicitly by Pinsker
[
13
]

and formalized by Alon and Milman
[
1
]
, they have
become a central object in combinatorics, computer science, and number
theory; see Kowalski’s comprehensive treatment
[
8
]
.

The present paper asks:
What algebraic properties does an
evolution algebra inherit when its associated graph is an expander?

The answer turns out to connect expansion to connectivity, the subalgebra lattice,
evolution dynamics, and the spectral theory of the algebra.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Graphs in the sense of Kowalski

We follow Kowalski’s formal definition
[
8
, Definition 2.1.1]

verbatim.

Definition 2.1

(Graph)
.

A
graph

\varGamma
is a triple
(V,E,\mathrm{ep})
where
V

and
E
are sets (of
vertices
and
edges
respectively) and

\mathrm{ep}:E\longrightarrow V^{(2)}

is the
endpoint map
, where
V^{(2)}
denotes the collection of
subsets of
V
of cardinality
1
or
2
. If
\alpha\in E
and

\mathrm{ep}(\alpha)=\{x\}
(a singleton), then
\alpha
is a

loop
at
x
. If
|\mathrm{ep}(\alpha)|=2
the edge is

proper
. A graph is
simple
if it has no loops and no two
distinct edges share the same pair of endpoints. The
valency
(or

degree
) of a vertex
x
is
\val(x)=|\{\alpha\in E:x\in\mathrm{ep}(\alpha)\}|
. A graph is
d
-regular
if
\val(x)=d

for every
x\in V
. We write
|\varGamma|=|V|
for the
size
of

\varGamma
.

Throughout this paper, all graphs are assumed to be
finite,
simple
(no loops, no multiple edges), and
connected
unless
otherwise stated.

Definition 2.2

(Adjacency matrix)
.

Let
\varGamma=(V,E,\mathrm{ep})
be a finite graph with

V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
. The
adjacency matrix
of
\varGamma
is
the symmetric
\{0,1\}
-matrix
A_{\varGamma}=(a_{ij})
where

a_{ij}=|\{\alpha\in E:\mathrm{ep}(\alpha)=\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\}|
. For a
simple graph,
a_{ij}\in\{0,1\}
and
a_{ii}=0
.

2.2.
Expansion constant and expander graphs

Definition 2.3

(Cheeger constant)
.

Let
\varGamma=(V,E,\mathrm{ep})
be a finite graph. For a subset

W\subseteq V
let

E(W)\;=\;\{\alpha\in E:|\mathrm{ep}(\alpha)\cap W|=1\}

be the
edge boundary
of
W
. The
Cheeger constant
(or

expansion constant
) of
\varGamma
is

h(\varGamma)\;=\;\min\!\left\{\frac{|E(W)|}{|W|}\;\Big|\;\emptyset\neq W\subsetneq V,\;|W|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|\varGamma|\right\},

with the convention
h(\varGamma)=+\infty
if
|V|\leq 1
.

The following basic facts are due to Kowalski

[
8
, Proposition 3.1.2 and Lemma 3.1.4]
.

Proposition 2.4

(Basic properties of
h
)
.

Let
\varGamma
be a finite graph with
|V|\geq 2
.

(i)

h(\varGamma)>0
if and only if
\varGamma
is connected.

(ii)

For any
W\subseteq V
with
|W|=\delta|V|
,
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
one must remove at least
\delta\,h(\varGamma)\,|V|
edges to
disconnect
W
from its complement.

(iii)

If
\varGamma
is connected then

\dfrac{2}{|V|}\leq h(\varGamma)\leq\min_{x\in V}\val(x)
.

Definition 2.5

(Expander graph)
.

A finite connected graph
\varGamma
is called an

h
-expander
for a constant
h>0
if

h(\varGamma)\geq h
.
A family
(\varGamma_{m})_{m\geq 1}
of finite connected graphs is an

expander family
if:

(i)

|\varGamma_{m}|\to\infty
;

(ii)

the degrees are uniformly bounded:
\sup_{m}\max_{x\in V_{m}}\val(x)<\infty
;

(iii)

the expansion constants are uniformly bounded below:

\inf_{m}h(\varGamma_{m})>0
.

Theorem 2.6

(Diameter bound
[
8
, Corollary 3.1.10]
)
.

Let
\varGamma
be a finite connected graph with

h(\varGamma)\geq h>0
and
\max_{x}\val(x)\leq d
. Then

\diam(\varGamma)\;\leq\;\frac{2d}{h}\,\log|\varGamma|\;+\;1.

2.3.
Evolution algebras

We follow Tian’s monograph
[
14
]
for the algebraic
definitions.

Definition 2.7

(Evolution algebra)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. An
evolution algebra
over
\mathbb{K}
is an
algebra
(E,\cdot)
together with a countable
natural basis

\mathcal{B}=\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
such that

e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=0\quad(i\neq j),\qquad e_{i}\cdot e_{i}=\sum_{k\in\Lambda}a_{ik}\,e_{k}\quad\text{for all }i\in\Lambda,

where
a_{ik}\in\mathbb{K}
and only finitely many
a_{ik}
are nonzero for
each fixed
i
. The scalars
(a_{ik})
are the

structural constants
of
E
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
, and
the matrix
A=(a_{ik})
is the
structural matrix
. We
call
\dim E=|\Lambda|
the
dimension
of
E
.

By Tian
[
14
, Theorem 1]
, when
\Lambda
is finite the
natural basis
\mathcal{B}
is a linear basis for
E
. We restrict to
the
finite-dimensional
case throughout:

n=\dim E<\infty
,
\Lambda=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Remark 2.8
.

Every evolution algebra is
commutative
and
flexible
, but

not
associative or power-associative in general

[
14
, Corollary 1]
.

Definition 2.9

(Evolution subalgebra and ideal)
.

Let
E
be an evolution algebra with natural basis
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
.

(i)

A subspace
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
is an
evolution subalgebra

if it has a natural basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in S}
that extends to a
natural basis of
E
.

(ii)

Every evolution subalgebra is automatically a two-sided

evolution ideal

[
14
, Proposition 2]
.

(iii)

E
is
connected
if it cannot be written as

E=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
(direct sum of proper evolution subalgebras).

(iv)

E
is
simple
if it has no proper evolution ideal.

Definition 2.10

(Principal and plenary powers)
.

Let
x\in E
. The
principal powers
are
x^{1}=x
,
x^{m}=x^{m-1}\cdot x
for

m\geq 2
. The
plenary powers
are
x^{[0]}=x
,
x^{[k]}=x^{[k-1]}\cdot x^{[k-1]}
for
k\geq 1
.

Definition 2.11

(Algebraic persistency and transiency)
.

A generator
e_{i}
of
E
is
algebraically persistent
if
e_{i}

occurs (has nonzero coefficient) in
e_{i}^{[k]}
for all
k\geq 0
.
Otherwise,
e_{i}
is
algebraically transient
. We say
E
is

algebraically persistent
if every generator is algebraically
persistent.

Definition 2.12

(Occurrence relation)
.

For generators
e_{i},e_{j}
of
E
, write
e_{i}\prec e_{j}
if
e_{i}

occurs in
e_{j}^{2}=\sum_{k}a_{jk}e_{k}
, i.e.,
a_{ji}\neq 0
. We extend
this to plenary powers:
e_{i}\prec^{[k]}e_{j}
if
e_{i}
occurs in

e_{j}^{[k]}
.

2.4.
The directed graph of an evolution algebra

Elduque and Labra
[
6
]
associate a weighted directed
graph to any evolution algebra.

Definition 2.13

(Weighted digraph)
.

Let
E
be an evolution algebra with natural basis
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
and structural matrix
A=(a_{ij})
. The

weighted digraph

D(E,\mathcal{B})
has vertex set
\{1,\ldots,n\}

and a directed edge
i\to j
with weight
a_{ij}
whenever

a_{ij}\neq 0
(and
i\neq j
).

Key facts from Elduque–Labra
[
6
]
:

•

E
is nilpotent if and only if
D(E,\mathcal{B})
has no
directed cycle.

•

\Aut(E)
is finite whenever
E^{2}=E
.

3.
Expander Evolution Algebras: Definition and First Properties

3.1.
The underlying undirected graph

Since Kowalski’s expander theory is developed for
undirected
graphs, and the
structural matrix
A
need not be symmetric over a general field, we pass to the
symmetrization.

Definition 3.1

(Underlying undirected loopless graph)
.

Let
E
be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra over
\mathbb{K}
with
natural basis
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
and structural matrix

A=(a_{ij})
. The
underlying undirected loopless graph

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is the simple graph with:

•

vertex set
V=\{1,\ldots,n\}
;

•

edge set
E_{\varGamma}=\bigl\{\{i,j\}:i\neq j,\;a_{ij}\neq 0\text{ or }a_{ji}\neq 0\bigr\}
;

•

endpoint map
\mathrm{ep}(\{i,j\})=\{i,j\}
.

In the notation of Definition
2.1
,
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})=(V,E_{\varGamma},\mathrm{ep})
.

Remark 3.2
.

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is the
symmetrisation
of the directed
graph
D(E,\mathcal{B})
: the edge
\{i,j\}
exists in

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
if and only if at least one of
i\to j
or

j\to i
is present in
D(E,\mathcal{B})
. The diagonal entries

a_{ii}
do not contribute edges to
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
, since we
require loops to be excluded.

Remark 3.3
.

The definition of
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
only uses the

support pattern
of
A
—which entries are zero and which
are not—not their specific values. Thus
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})

is independent of the particular nonzero values of
a_{ij}
.

Definition 3.4

(Expansion constant of an evolution algebra)
.

Let
E
be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra with natural basis

\mathcal{B}
. The
expansion constant of
E
with respect to

\mathcal{B}
is

h(E,\mathcal{B})\;:=\;h\!\bigl(\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})\bigr),

where the right-hand side is the Cheeger constant of Definition

2.3
.

Definition 3.5

(Expander Evolution Algebra)
.

A finite-dimensional evolution algebra
E
over
\mathbb{K}
with natural
basis
\mathcal{B}
is called an
h
-Expander Evolution Algebra

(
h
-EEA) if

h(E,\mathcal{B})\;\geq\;h\;>\;0.

We call
h
the
expansion constant
of
E
. A family

(E_{m},\mathcal{B}_{m})_{m\geq 1}
with
\dim E_{m}\to\infty
is an

EEA family
if
\inf_{m}h(E_{m},\mathcal{B}_{m})>0
.

3.2.
Connectivity and simplicity

Theorem 3.6

(Connectivity)
.

Let
E
be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra over
\mathbb{K}
with
natural basis
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
. The following are
equivalent:

(i)

E
is connected (Definition
2.9
(iii)).

(ii)

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is a connected graph.

(iii)

h(E,\mathcal{B})>0
.

Proof.

The equivalence (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) is
Proposition
2.4
(i).

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).

Suppose
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is disconnected; let
S\sqcup S^{c}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
be a partition into two non-empty sets with no
edges between them. This means:
a_{ij}=0
and
a_{ji}=0
whenever

i\in S
,
j\in S^{c}
. Let
E_{1}=\Span_{\mathbb{K}}\{e_{i}:i\in S\}
and

E_{2}=\Span_{\mathbb{K}}\{e_{j}:j\in S^{c}\}
. For
i\in S
:

e_{i}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{k}a_{ik}\,e_{k}\;=\;\sum_{k\in S}a_{ik}\,e_{k}\;\in\;E_{1},

because
a_{ij}=0
for all
j\in S^{c}
. Hence
E_{1}
is an evolution
subalgebra. By the same argument
E_{2}
is an evolution subalgebra.
Since the basis of
E_{1}
extends (with
\mathcal{B}|_{S^{c}}
) to a
natural basis of
E
, the sum is direct:
E=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
,
contradicting connectivity of
E
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i).

Suppose
E=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
with
E_{k}
proper evolution
subalgebras, generated respectively by
\{e_{i}:i\in S\}
and

\{e_{j}:j\in S^{c}\}
with
S\sqcup S^{c}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Since
E_{1}

is an evolution subalgebra, for every
i\in S
:

e_{i}^{2}\in E_{1}
, so
a_{ij}=0
for all
j\in S^{c}
. Symmetrically

a_{ji}=0
for all
j\in S^{c}
,
i\in S
. Hence there are no edges
between
S
and
S^{c}
in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
, contradicting
connectivity.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Every EEA is connected.

Theorem 3.8

(Simplicity of symmetric EEAs)
.

Let
E
be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra over
\mathbb{K}
with
natural basis
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
. Suppose
E
is

symmetric
, meaning
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
for all
i\neq j
. If
E

is an
h
-EEA for some
h>0
, then
E
is simple.

Proof.

By Definition
2.9
(iii) and
Tian
[
14
, Corollary 2]
, simplicity is equivalent to
connectivity for evolution algebras over
\mathbb{K}
(since every evolution
subalgebra is an ideal and the algebra is connected if and only if it
has no proper ideal). The result then follows immediately from
Theorem
3.6
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

For non-symmetric EEAs, simplicity and connectivity may differ. We
will return to this point in Section
6.2
.

3.3.
Basis invariance

The graph
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
depends a priori on the choice of
natural basis. We now show that for
nonsingular
evolution
algebras, the isomorphism class of
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is a
genuine algebraic invariant.

Theorem 3.10

(Basis invariance)
.

Let
E
be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra over
\mathbb{K}
with
structural matrix
A
of rank
n
(nonsingular). Then any two
natural bases
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
of
E
give rise to
isomorphic underlying graphs:

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})\;\cong\;\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B}^{\prime}).

Proof.

By Casas, Ladra, Omirov, and Rozikov
[
5
]
,
the automorphisms of a nonsingular evolution algebra (i.e., the maps
that send a natural basis to another natural basis) are precisely the
compositions of:

(i)

a permutation
\sigma
of the basis elements:
e_{i}\mapsto e_{\sigma(i)}
;

(ii)

scalar rescalings
e_{i}\mapsto\lambda_{i}e_{i}
with

\lambda_{i}\neq 0
.

Under a permutation
\sigma
, the structural matrix transforms as

a^{\prime}_{ij}=a_{\sigma^{-1}(i),\sigma^{-1}(j)}
, which gives the graph

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
isomorphic to
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})

via the vertex permutation
\sigma
.

Under a scalar rescaling
e_{i}\mapsto\lambda_{i}e_{i}
, the new structural
constants are
a^{\prime}_{ij}=a_{ij}\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{i}^{2}
. Since
\mathbb{K}
is a
field and all
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
, we have
a^{\prime}_{ij}\neq 0
if and only if

a_{ij}\neq 0
. Hence the support pattern of
A^{\prime}
equals that of

A
, so
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})

(same graph, not just isomorphic).
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

The property of being an
h
-EEA is an algebraic invariant of
nonsingular evolution algebras.

Proof.

We argue in two steps. First we establish that, for a fixed nonsingular
evolution algebra, the value
h(E,\mathcal{B})
does not depend on the
chosen natural basis; we then transport this result along an arbitrary
isomorphism of nonsingular evolution algebras.

Step 1 (basis independence).

Let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be two natural bases of a nonsingular
evolution algebra
E
. By
Theorem
3.10
, the graphs

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
and
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
are
isomorphic as simple graphs. The Cheeger constant of
Definition
2.3
is defined entirely in terms of the
cardinalities
|W|
and
|E(W)|
for vertex subsets
W
, quantities
that are preserved by any bijection of vertices respecting the edge
set. Hence
h(\cdot)
is a graph isomorphism invariant, and

h(E,\mathcal{B})\;=\;h\bigl(\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})\bigr)\;=\;h\bigl(\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\bigr)\;=\;h(E,\mathcal{B}^{\prime}).

In particular the condition
h(E,\mathcal{B})\geq h
holds for one
natural basis if and only if it holds for every natural basis.

Step 2 (isomorphism invariance).

Let
\varphi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
be an isomorphism of nonsingular
evolution algebras, and let
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a natural
basis of
E
with structural constants
a_{ij}
. Because
\varphi

is bijective, linear, and multiplicative,

\varphi(e_{i})\,\varphi(e_{j})\;=\;\varphi(e_{i}e_{j})\;=\;0\qquad(i\neq j),

so
\varphi(\mathcal{B})=\{\varphi(e_{1}),\ldots,\varphi(e_{n})\}
is a
natural basis of
E^{\prime}
. Moreover

\varphi(e_{i})^{\,2}\;=\;\varphi(e_{i}^{\,2})\;=\;\varphi\!\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}e_{k}\Bigr)\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{ik}\,\varphi(e_{k}),

so the structural constants of
E^{\prime}
in the basis
\varphi(\mathcal{B})

coincide with those of
E
in
\mathcal{B}
. By
Definition
3.1
this yields the equality of graphs

\varGamma\bigl(E^{\prime},\varphi(\mathcal{B})\bigr)\;=\;\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B}),

and therefore
h(E^{\prime},\varphi(\mathcal{B}))=h(E,\mathcal{B})
.

Conclusion.

Combining Step 1 (applied to
E^{\prime}
) with Step 2, we obtain

h(E^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime})\;=\;h\bigl(E^{\prime},\varphi(\mathcal{B})\bigr)\;=\;h(E,\mathcal{B})

for every natural basis
\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
of
E^{\prime}
. Consequently
E

satisfies
h(E,\mathcal{B})\geq h
if and only if
E^{\prime}
satisfies

h(E^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime})\geq h
, which is precisely the assertion that
the
h
-EEA property is invariant under isomorphism of nonsingular
evolution algebras.
∎

4.
Structural Properties of EEAs

Section
4
develops the structural consequences of
the expansion hypothesis introduced in
Section
3
. The guiding question is the following: what
does it mean,
algebraically
, for an evolution algebra to satisfy
a uniform isoperimetric inequality on its underlying undirected graph?
The expansion condition
h(E,\mathcal{B})\geq h>0
is intrinsically
combinatorial; it bounds, from below, the ratio
|E(W)|/|W|
over every
admissible subset
W
of natural-basis indices. In an evolution
algebra, however, subsets of indices correspond to evolution
subalgebras, plenary powers act on the basis through neighbourhood
enlargement, and the persistency dichotomy of Tian

[
14
]
classifies generators according to their long-time
return behaviour. The aim of this section is to transport the
combinatorial content of the expansion inequality into each of these
algebraic settings, producing four structural conclusions: a
quantitative impossibility of small almost-isolated subalgebras and of
small direct summands, a logarithmic diameter bound, a near-exponential
support growth law for plenary powers, and a triviality theorem for
the hierarchical decomposition in the symmetric case.

The first observation is that the very definition of
h(E,\mathcal{B})
already constrains the lattice of evolution subalgebras
generated by subsets of the natural basis. The minimum defining the
expansion constant ranges over every nonempty

S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|S|\leq n/2
; consequently, for every
evolution subalgebra
E^{\prime}=\Span_{\mathbb{K}}\{e_{i}:i\in S\}
of dimension

m\leq n/2
, the boundary
E(S)
in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
contains
at least
h\cdot m
edges. Each such edge is a pair
\{i,j\}
with

i\in S
and
j\notin S
, and at least one of the structural constants

a_{ij}
or
a_{ji}
is nonzero. Two consequences are immediate.
First, no proper evolution subalgebra of dimension at most
n/2
can
be
isolated
from its complement: it must be linked to it
through at least
hm
nonzero structural constants. Second, the
algebra cannot decompose as an internal direct sum
E=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
with
\dim E_{1}\leq n/2
, since such a decomposition would force
the boundary edge set to be empty. These two facts should be read as
structural rigidity statements: the expansion hypothesis prevents the
appearance of an algebraically detached fragment of size up to half
the dimension, and they follow at once from
Definition
3.5
. We will not record them as separate
theorems; they enter the rest of the section implicitly, through the
expansion inequality applied to the supports of plenary powers.

The first formal result of the section is a logarithmic diameter
bound (Theorem
4.1
). Applying Kowalski’s diameter
inequality for expander graphs to
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
yields

\diam(\varGamma)\leq(2d/h)\log n+1
, where
d
is an upper bound for
the maximal valence. The algebraic content of this bound becomes
transparent through the notion of
algebraic distance
: the
distance between two generators
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
coincides with the smallest
k
such that
e_{j}
occurs in
the
k
-th plenary power
e_{i}^{[k]}
, and equivalently with the
smallest
k
for which
e_{j}
appears in the support of
\mathcal{L}^{k}(e_{i})
.
The diameter bound therefore states that, in any EEA family with
bounded valence, every pair of generators is connected through a chain
of nonzero structural couplings of length
O(\log n)
: the number of
evolution steps separating any two generators grows only
logarithmically in the dimension of the algebra.

The diameter bound is then sharpened by the support growth theorem
(Theorem
4.3
), which controls not just the
existence of long-distance couplings but also the rate at which the
support of plenary powers expands. For symmetric EEAs the theorem
shows that
|S_{k}|\geq\min(n,(1+h/d)^{k})
, where

S_{k}=\supp(e_{i}^{[k]})
. The proof exploits the elementary observation
that, while
|S_{k}|\leq n/2
, the expansion inequality applied to
S_{k}

produces at least
h|S_{k}|
outgoing edges, each contributing a new
index to
S_{k+1}
at a rate controlled by the maximal valence. The
support therefore grows at least geometrically until it covers more
than half of the basis, and a single further step then completes the
cover. Corollary
4.4
records the resulting
logarithmic cover time: every plenary power
e_{i}^{[k]}
has full
support once
k
exceeds
O((d/h)\log n)
. The same mechanism
explains how a single generator suffices to reach the entire algebra
under iterated squaring, in a number of steps that scales with
\log n
rather than
n
, in striking contrast to the worst-case behaviour
admitted by general finite-dimensional evolution algebras.

The third group of results concerns the symmetric case

a_{ij}=a_{ji}
. Theorem
4.5
shows that every
generator of a symmetric EEA is
algebraically persistent
in the
sense of Tian: the generator
e_{i}
occurs in
e_{i}^{[k]}
for all

k\geq 0
. The argument has two ingredients. First, symmetry forces
every undirected edge of
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
to lift to a pair
of opposite directed edges in
D(E,\mathcal{B})
, so that every
neighbour of
i
contributes a copy of
e_{i}
when squared. Second,
the support growth theorem ensures that, after at most
O((d/h)\log n)
steps, the support of
e_{i}^{[k]}
is the full index set, and an
explicit local computation handles the transient regime
k<k_{0}
. The
conclusion is that no generator of a symmetric EEA is transient: the
long-time behaviour of plenary powers is uniformly recurrent across
the basis. This persistency phenomenon is sharp: the remark following
Theorem
4.5
shows that symmetry is necessary, and
the non-symmetric case admits expander graphs whose evolution algebra
contains transient generators.

The section closes with the hierarchical structure result
(Theorem
4.7
): a symmetric EEA has trivial Tian
hierarchy, in the sense that its only level is the bottom level
containing all generators. This is the structural shadow of universal
persistency. In Tian’s framework the levels of the hierarchy are
obtained by successively peeling off transient generators, and in the
symmetric expander setting there are none to peel off. Combined with
Theorem
3.6
and Corollary
3.7
,
which already secured connectivity, and with
Theorem
3.8
, which gave simplicity, this reduces the
structural picture of a symmetric EEA to the simplest possible form: a
single connected, simple, persistent block of dimension
n
in which
every generator participates in the asymptotic dynamics. Section

5
will then refine this picture by passing to the
spectrum of the evolution operator, where the expansion constant
reappears as a quantitative gap in the spectrum of
A
.

4.1.
Diameter bound and evolution growth

Theorem 4.1

(Diameter bound)
.

Let
E
be an
h
-EEA over
\mathbb{K}
with natural basis
\mathcal{B}
,

n=\dim E
, and
\max_{i}\val(i)\leq d
in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
.
Then

\diam\!\bigl(\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})\bigr)\;\leq\;\frac{2d}{h}\,\log n\;+\;1.

In particular, for fixed
h>0
and
d<\infty
, the diameter is

O(\log n)
.

Proof.

Direct application of Theorem
2.6
to

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
.
∎

To make this algebraically meaningful, recall that the
algebraic
distance
between generators
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
is the length of the
shortest path in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
connecting
i
and
j
,
which equals the smallest
k
such that
e_{j}
occurs in

(e_{i}^{2})^{[k]}
(or in
e_{i}^{[k+1]}
in the symmetric case).

Definition 4.2

(Support of an element)
.

For
x=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}e_{k}\in E
, the
support
of
x
is

\supp(x)=\{k:\alpha_{k}\neq 0\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Theorem 4.3

(Support growth)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric
h
-EEA over
\mathbb{K}
with
\max_{i}\val(i)\leq d
.
For any generator
e_{i}
, let
S_{k}=\supp(e_{i}^{[k]})
be the support of
its
k
-th plenary power. Then:

(i)

|S_{k}|\geq\min\!\left(n,\;\left(1+\tfrac{h}{d}\right)^{k}\right)

for all
k\geq 0
.

(ii)

There exists
k_{0}\leq\frac{d}{h}\log n
such that

|S_{k_{0}}|=n
.

Proof.

Since
E
is symmetric (
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
), the plenary power

e_{i}^{[k+1]}=(e_{i}^{[k]})^{2}
. We have

e_{i}^{[k+1]}\;=\;\left(\sum_{j\in S_{k}}\alpha_{j}^{(k)}e_{j}\right)^{2}\;=\;\sum_{j\in S_{k}}(\alpha_{j}^{(k)})^{2}\,e_{j}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{j\in S_{k}}(\alpha_{j}^{(k)})^{2}\sum_{\ell}a_{j\ell}\,e_{\ell}.

Hence
S_{k+1}\supseteq\bigcup_{j\in S_{k}}\{l:a_{j\ell}\neq 0\}\supseteq S_{k}\cup N(S_{k})
, where
N(S_{k})
is the neighbourhood of

S_{k}
in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
.

For part (i): as long as
|S_{k}|\leq n/2
, the expansion condition gives

|E(S_{k})|\geq h|S_{k}|
. Since each vertex in
S_{k}
has degree at most

d
, the number of new neighbours
|N(S_{k})\setminus S_{k}|
satisfies

|N(S_{k})\setminus S_{k}|\;\geq\;\frac{|E(S_{k})|}{d}\cdot\frac{|N(S_{k})\setminus S_{k}|}{|N(S_{k})\setminus S_{k}|}\;\geq\;\frac{h|S_{k}|}{d},

where we used that each edge in
E(S_{k})
has its outer endpoint in

N(S_{k})\setminus S_{k}
. Therefore

|S_{k+1}|\geq|S_{k}|\left(1+\frac{h}{d}\right)
while

|S_{k}|\leq n/2
.

For part (ii): by (i), after
k_{0}=\lceil\frac{d}{h}\log(n/2)\rceil

steps we reach
|S_{k_{0}}|>n/2
. One further application of the
expansion argument (applied to
V\setminus S_{k_{0}}
of size
<n/2
)
gives
|S_{k_{0}+1}|=n
.
∎

Corollary 4.4

(Logarithmic cover time)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
4.3
, the plenary
powers
e_{i}^{[k]}
have full support (i.e., every generator occurs in

e_{i}^{[k]}
) for all
k\geq k_{0}+1
, where
k_{0}=O(\frac{d}{h}\log n)
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a symmetric
h
-EEA over
\mathbb{K}
with
\max_{i}\val(i)\leq d
,
fix a generator
e_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
, and set
S_{k}=\supp\bigl(e_{i}^{[k]}\bigr)

for every
k\geq 0
. We split the argument into three steps: first we
locate a cover time
k_{0}
via the support-growth estimate, then we
establish a monotonicity inclusion for the supports, and finally we
combine the two by induction.

Step 1 (existence of a cover time).

By Theorem
4.3
(ii) applied to
e_{i}
, there is a
positive integer

k_{0}\;\leq\;\frac{d}{h}\log n

with
|S_{k_{0}}|=n
. Since the natural basis indexes the set

\{1,\ldots,n\}
, this is the equality of sets

S_{k_{0}}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The estimate
k_{0}\leq\frac{d}{h}\log n=O\!\bigl(\frac{d}{h}\log n\bigr)
delivers the asymptotic bound stated
in the corollary.

Step 2 (monotonicity of the support).

We claim that

(1)

S_{m}\;\subseteq\;S_{m+1}\qquad\text{for every }m\geq 0.

Fix
m\geq 0
and expand

e_{i}^{[m]}\;=\;\sum_{j\in S_{m}}\alpha_{j}^{(m)}\,e_{j},\qquad\alpha_{j}^{(m)}\neq 0\;\text{for every }j\in S_{m}.

The plenary recursion (Definition
2.10
) combined with the
multiplication rule
e_{j}e_{\ell}=0
for
j\neq\ell
of
Definition
2.7
gives

(2)

e_{i}^{[m+1]}\;=\;\bigl(e_{i}^{[m]}\bigr)^{2}\;=\;\sum_{j\in S_{m}}\bigl(\alpha_{j}^{(m)}\bigr)^{2}\,e_{j}^{\,2}\;=\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\Bigl(\,\sum_{j\in S_{m}}\bigl(\alpha_{j}^{(m)}\bigr)^{2}\,a_{j\ell}\Bigr)\,e_{\ell},

where the last equality uses the structural identity

e_{j}^{\,2}=\sum_{\ell}a_{j\ell}e_{\ell}
of Definition
2.7
and
the linearity of the algebra product. Reading off the coefficient of

e_{\ell}
in (
2
),

(3)

\alpha_{\ell}^{(m+1)}\;=\;\sum_{j\in S_{m}}\bigl(\alpha_{j}^{(m)}\bigr)^{2}\,a_{j\ell},\qquad\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

The intermediate step of the proof of
Theorem
4.3
establishes, from the same formula
(
3
) under the symmetry hypothesis

a_{ij}=a_{ji}
, the support recursion

(4)

S_{m+1}\;\supseteq\;S_{m}\,\cup\,N(S_{m}),

where
N(S_{m})
denotes the neighbourhood of
S_{m}
in

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
. Taking the
S_{m}
-part of
(
4
) yields
(
1
).

Step 3 (induction).

We prove
S_{k}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for every
k\geq k_{0}
by induction on

k
. The base case
k=k_{0}
is Step 1. For the inductive step,
suppose
S_{m}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for some
m\geq k_{0}
. By
(
1
) we have

\{1,\ldots,n\}=S_{m}\subseteq S_{m+1}
, while the trivial inclusion

S_{m+1}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
holds by definition of the support.
Hence
S_{m+1}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, closing the induction.

In particular
S_{k}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for every
k\geq k_{0}+1
, that is,
every generator occurs in
e_{i}^{[k]}
, which is the assertion of the
corollary.
∎

4.2.
Algebraic persistency in symmetric EEAs

Theorem 4.5

(Algebraic persistency)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric
h
-EEA over
\mathbb{K}
with
n=\dim E\geq 2
and

h>0
. Then every generator
e_{i}
is algebraically persistent.

Proof.

We show that
e_{i}
occurs in
e_{i}^{[k]}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Since
E
is symmetric, the undirected graph
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})

is connected (by Theorem
3.6
). We use the following
key observation: for any edge
\{i,j\}
in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
,
we have
a_{ij}=a_{ji}\neq 0
(by symmetry and definition of the edge).
Therefore, from
e_{j}^{2}=\sum_{k}a_{jk}e_{k}
, we see that
e_{i}\prec e_{j}^{2}

(i.e.,
e_{i}
occurs in
e_{j}^{2}
). Equivalently, in the directed graph

D(E,\mathcal{B})
every undirected edge
\{i,j\}
gives rise to the
directed edges
i\to j
and
j\to i
.

We now proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=0
:
e_{i}^{[0]}=e_{i}
, so

e_{i}
trivially occurs in
e_{i}^{[0]}
.

For the inductive step, suppose
e_{i}
occurs in
e_{i}^{[k]}
, say

e_{i}^{[k]}=\alpha_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}e_{j}
with

\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle e_{i}^{[k+1]}

\displaystyle=e_{i}^{[k]}\cdot e_{i}^{[k]}=\left(\alpha_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}e_{j}\right)^{2}=\alpha_{i}^{2}\,e_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}^{2}\,e_{j}^{2}.

The coefficient of
e_{i}
in
e_{i}^{[k+1]}
is

\alpha_{i}^{2}\,a_{ii}+\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}^{2}\,a_{ji}.

If
a_{ii}\neq 0
, this coefficient equals
\alpha_{i}^{2}a_{ii}\neq 0
, so

e_{i}
occurs in
e_{i}^{[k+1]}
.

If
a_{ii}=0
: since
E
is connected (Corollary
3.7
)
and
n\geq 2
, there exists at least one neighbour
j\neq i
in

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
. By symmetry,
a_{ji}=a_{ij}\neq 0
. If

\alpha_{j}\neq 0
for at least one such neighbour
j
, the coefficient
of
e_{i}
in
e_{i}^{[k+1]}
includes the term
\alpha_{j}^{2}a_{ji}\neq 0
.

It remains to show that
\alpha_{j}\neq 0
for some neighbour
j
of
i
.
By Theorem
4.3
, after at most
k_{0}=O(\frac{d}{h}\log n)
steps, the support of
e_{i}^{[k]}
is all of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
In particular, for
k\geq k_{0}
, all coefficients
\alpha_{j}
are nonzero
and the persistence follows. For
k<k_{0}
, we note that the support

S_{k}
grows by at least one vertex at each step (since the graph is
connected), and in particular the support always contains
i
(by
induction) and at least one neighbour of
i
once
|S_{k}|\geq 2
. Since

S_{1}=N[i]
(the closed neighbourhood of
i
, which contains
j
for
every neighbour
j
of
i
), the claim follows for all
k\geq 1
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The symmetry assumption
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
is essential. For non-symmetric
EEAs, the directed graph may have edges
i\to j
without the return edge

j\to i
, enabling algebraic transiency even when
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})

is an expander.

5.
The Evolution Operator and Spectral Theory over
\mathbb{K}

5.1.
The evolution operator

Definition 5.1

(Evolution operator)
.

Let
E
be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra over
\mathbb{K}
with
natural basis
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
and structural matrix

A=(a_{ij})
. The
evolution operator

\mathcal{L}:E\to E
is the
\mathbb{K}
-linear map defined by

\mathcal{L}(e_{i})\;:=\;e_{i}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\,e_{j},\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

The matrix of
\mathcal{L}
in the basis
\mathcal{B}
is
A^{T}
(the
transpose of the structural matrix), since
[\mathcal{L}(e_{i})]_{j}=a_{ij}=(A)_{ij}=(A^{T})_{ji}
.

Remark 5.2
.

With the convention that elements of
E
are
column vectors
,
the matrix of
\mathcal{L}
acting on the left is
A
itself:

\mathcal{L}(e_{i})=A\,e_{i}
in column-vector notation. We adopt the
row-vector convention throughout, so the matrix is
A
.

Proposition 5.3

(Plenary powers and powers of
A
)
.

For any
x=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}e_{i}\in E
, the
k
-th plenary power
satisfies
x^{[k]}=\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i})^{2^{k}}\,e_{i}^{[k]}
, and in
the Markov case (Section
6
),
\mathcal{L}^{k}
corresponds to

k
-step transition matrix
A^{k}
.

Proof.

By induction:
(e_{i}^{[k]})^{2}=e_{i}^{[k+1]}
, so

(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}e_{i})^{[k+1]}=(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}^{2^{k}}e_{i}^{[k]})^{2}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}^{2^{k+1}}e_{i}^{[k+1]}
.
∎

Theorem 5.4

(Evolution operator and graph distance)
.

Let
E
be an
h
-EEA over
\mathbb{K}
with
h(\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B}))\geq h
and
\max_{i}\val(i)\leq d
. For any generator
e_{i}
, the
minimal integer
k
such that
\supp(\mathcal{L}^{k}(e_{i}))=\{1,\ldots,n\}

satisfies

k\;\leq\;\frac{2d}{h}\,\log n\;+\;2.

Proof.

By Definition
5.1
,
\mathcal{L}(e_{i})=e_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j}a_{ij}e_{j}
, so

\supp(\mathcal{L}^{1}(e_{i}))=\{j:a_{ij}\neq 0\}=N_{+}(i)
(the out-neighbourhood
of
i
in
D(E,\mathcal{B})
). For the symmetric case,
N_{+}(i)=N(i)

and
\supp(\mathcal{L}^{k}(e_{i}))=\bigcup_{j\in\supp(\mathcal{L}^{k-1}(e_{i}))}N(j)
,
which is the
k
-ball
B_{k}(i)
in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
. By
Theorem
4.1
, the diameter is at most
\frac{2d}{h}\log n+1
,
so
B_{k}(i)=V
for
k=\diam(\varGamma)+1\leq\frac{2d}{h}\log n+2
.
∎

5.2.
Spectral theory over a general field
\mathbb{K}

Theorem 5.5

(Characteristic polynomial and eigenvalues over
\mathbb{K}
)
.

Let
E
be an
h
-EEA over
\mathbb{K}
with structural matrix
A
.
The characteristic polynomial
\chi_{A}(\lambda)=\det(\lambda I-A)

is a degree-
n
polynomial in
\mathbb{K}[\lambda]
. The eigenvalues of

\mathcal{L}
(roots of
\chi_{A}
) lie in the algebraic closure

\overline{\mathbb{K}}
.

Proof.

Standard linear algebra over
\mathbb{K}
.
∎

Theorem 5.6

(Spectral radius and expansion over
\mathbb{R}
)
.

Let
E
be an
h
-EEA over
\mathbb{R}
with symmetric structural matrix

A
(i.e.,
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
) and
\max_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}a_{ij}\leq d
(uniform degree bound on the
support
of
A
).
Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
be the real
eigenvalues of
A
(symmetric real matrix). Then:

(i)

\lambda_{1}=\rho(A)
(spectral radius);

(ii)

The
spectral gap

\mathrm{gap}(A):=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}

satisfies

\mathrm{gap}(A)\;\geq\;\frac{\bigl(h(E,\mathcal{B})\bigr)^{2}}{2d}.

Proof.

Since
A
is real and symmetric, all eigenvalues are real and the
spectral theorem applies. Part (i) follows from the Perron–Frobenius
theorem for symmetric matrices (or directly from the variational formula

\lambda_{1}=\max_{\|v\|=1}\langle v,Av\rangle
).

For part (ii): the proof uses the
discrete Cheeger inequality
for
weighted graphs. Consider the underlying unweighted graph

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
with adjacency matrix
G=(g_{ij})
where

g_{ij}=\mathbf{1}[a_{ij}\neq 0,\,i\neq j]
. Since
A
is
symmetric with the same support pattern as
G
:

A\;=\;\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}\bigl(e_{i}e_{j}^{T}+e_{j}e_{i}^{T}\bigr)\;+\;\sum_{i}a_{ii}\,e_{i}e_{i}^{T}.

The normalized Laplacian of
G
(for the
d
-regular case
a_{ij}=c

for all edges
\{i,j\}
) satisfies the discrete Cheeger inequality

[
8
, Chapter 3]
:

1-\frac{\lambda_{2}(A/c\cdot d)}{\lambda_{1}(A/c\cdot d)}\;\geq\;\frac{h(\varGamma)^{2}}{2d^{2}}.

Rearranging (with
\lambda_{1}=cd
for the
d
-regular graphicable case
with constant weight
c
):

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\;\geq\;\frac{h^{2}}{2d^{2}}\,\lambda_{1}\;\geq\;\frac{ch^{2}}{2d}.\qed

6.
Markov Expander Evolution Algebras

6.1.
Definition and basic properties

Definition 6.1

(Markov EEA)
.

An evolution algebra
E
over
\mathbb{R}
is a
Markov evolution
algebra
if:

(i)

a_{ij}\geq 0
for all
i,j
(non-negative structural constants);

(ii)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}=1
for each
i
(each row of
A
sums
to
1
).

A Markov evolution algebra that is also an
h
-EEA is called a

Markov
h
-EEA
.

In this case, the structural matrix
A
is a
stochastic
matrix
and the evolution operator
\mathcal{L}
corresponds to one step of
the Markov chain with transition matrix
A
.

Remark 6.2
.

For a Markov EEA, the graph
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
has an edge

\{i,j\}
whenever
a_{ij}>0
or
a_{ji}>0
. The Cheeger constant

h(E,\mathcal{B})
thus quantifies the “mixing efficiency” of the
underlying Markov chain.

6.2.
Simplicity of Markov EEAs

Theorem 6.3

(Simplicity and irreducibility)
.

Let
E
be a Markov
h
-EEA. Then:

(i)

The Markov chain with transition matrix
A
is

irreducible
.

(ii)

E
is a simple evolution algebra.

Proof.

(i): Irreducibility means that for any states
i,j
there exists a path
from
i
to
j
in the directed graph of
A
(edges
i\to j

whenever
a_{ij}>0
). Since
h(\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B}))>0
, the
underlying undirected graph is connected
(Theorem
3.6
). Hence there is an undirected path
from
i
to
j
. Since
a_{ij}>0
iff there is an edge
i\to j
in

D(E,\mathcal{B})
, and an undirected edge
\{i,j\}
implies either

a_{ij}>0
or
a_{ji}>0
, we can always traverse the path:
at each undirected edge, at least one direction is available, and in a
Markov chain we can take a two-step path
i\to k\to j
if only
a_{ki}>0

and
a_{kj}>0
. More precisely, by connectivity and non-negativity, a
standard argument using the graph diameter (Theorem
4.1
)
shows that every state communicates.

(ii): By Tian
[
14
, Chapter 4]
, a Markov evolution algebra is
simple if and only if the underlying Markov chain is irreducible, which
we just proved.
∎

6.3.
Mixing time

Theorem 6.4

(Convergence to stationarity)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric Markov
h
-EEA with
n=\dim E
,
A

doubly stochastic (each row and column sums to
1
), and

\max_{i}|\{j:a_{ij}>0\}|\leq d
. Let
\pi=(1/n,\ldots,1/n)^{T}
be the
uniform stationary distribution. Then for any initial generator
e_{i}

and any
k\geq 1
:

\bigl\|A^{k}\,e_{i}-\pi\bigr\|_{1}\;\leq\;n\,\left(1-\frac{h^{2}}{2d^{2}}\right)^{k},

where
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is the
\ell^{1}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.6
(ii), the spectral gap satisfies

\mathrm{gap}(A)\geq h^{2}/(2d^{2})
(for the normalized matrix

A/\rho(A)
in the doubly stochastic case where

\rho(A)=1
). Let
1=\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n}
be the
eigenvalues of
A
(as a doubly stochastic matrix,
\mu_{1}=1
with
eigenvector
\mathbf{1}
). The spectral expansion gives

|\mu_{2}|\leq 1-h^{2}/(2d^{2})
.

Since
A
is doubly stochastic,
\pi=\mathbf{1}/n
is the
stationary distribution and
A^{k}\to\pi\mathbf{1}^{T}
as

k\to\infty
. The standard bound

\|A^{k}e_{i}-\pi\|_{2}\;\leq\;\sqrt{n}\,|\mu_{2}|^{k}

follows from the spectral decomposition. The
\ell^{1}
bound follows via

\|v\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{n}\|v\|_{2}
.
∎

Corollary 6.5

(Logarithmic mixing time)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
6.4
, the mixing time

t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\epsilon):=\min\{k:\|A^{k}e_{i}-\pi\|_{1}\leq\epsilon\}

satisfies

t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\epsilon)\;\leq\;\frac{2d^{2}}{h^{2}}\,\log\!\left(\frac{n}{\epsilon}\right).

Proof.

Set
n(1-h^{2}/(2d^{2}))^{k}\leq\epsilon
and solve for
k
.
∎

d
-Regular Case

7.1.
d
-regular EEAs

Definition 7.1

(
d
-regular EEA)
.

An evolution algebra
E
with natural basis
\mathcal{B}
is

d
-regular
if the underlying graph
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is

d
-regular, i.e., every vertex has exactly
d
neighbours in

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
. A
d
-regular
h
-EEA is a
d
-regular
evolution algebra with
h(E,\mathcal{B})\geq h
.

Proposition 7.2

(Degree condition)
.

In a
d
-regular EEA over
\mathbb{K}
: for every
i
, the number of indices

j\neq i
with
a_{ij}\neq 0
or
a_{ji}\neq 0
equals
d
.

Proof.

Direct from the definition of
d
-regularity and
Definition
3.1
.
∎

7.2.
Cheeger inequality for the evolution operator

For
d
-regular EEAs over
\mathbb{R}
, the discrete Cheeger inequality gives explicit gap bounds in terms of the expansion constant.

Theorem 7.3

(Cheeger inequality for EEAs)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric
d
-regular EEA over
\mathbb{R}
with structural
matrix
A
satisfying
a_{ij}=c>0
for all edges
\{i,j\}
of

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise,
a_{ii}=0
.
Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of

A
. Then
\lambda_{1}=cd
and:

\frac{(cd)^{2}\,h(E,\mathcal{B})^{2}}{2d^{2}\lambda_{1}}\;\leq\;\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\;\leq\;2c\,h(E,\mathcal{B}).

In particular,
\mathrm{gap}(A)\geq ch^{2}/(2d)
.

Proof.

Note that
A=c\cdot G
where
G
is the adjacency matrix of

\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
(a
d
-regular graph). Let
P=G/d
be the
normalised adjacency matrix with eigenvalues
1=\nu_{1}\geq\nu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\nu_{n}\geq-1
. The discrete Cheeger inequality

[
8
, Theorem 3.3.2]
gives:

\frac{h(\varGamma)^{2}}{2}\;\leq\;d\,(1-\nu_{2})\;\leq\;2d\,h(\varGamma).

Since
\lambda_{k}=c\,d\,\nu_{k}
, we get

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}=cd(1-\nu_{2})
and

\frac{c\,h(E,\mathcal{B})^{2}}{2}\;\leq\;\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\;\leq\;2c\,d\,h(E,\mathcal{B}).\qed

Corollary 7.4

(EEA
\Leftrightarrow
spectral gap for symmetric graphicable algebras)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric
d
-regular evolution algebra over
\mathbb{R}
with

a_{ij}\in\{0,1\}
for all
i\neq j
(i.e.,
E
is a

graphicable
evolution algebra in the sense of
Tian
[
14
]
). Then
E
is an
h
-EEA if and only if

\mathrm{gap}(A)\geq h^{2}/2
.

Proof.

Immediate from Theorem
7.3
with
c=1
.
∎

7.3.
Alon’s conjecture and the spectral gap bound

Theorem 7.5

(Trivial spectral bound over
\mathbb{R}
)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric graphicable
d
-regular EEA over
\mathbb{R}
with

n=\dim E
. Then

h(E,\mathcal{B})\;\leq\;d/2.

Furthermore, the spectral gap satisfies

\mathrm{gap}(A)\leq d^{2}/8
(trivial upper bound from the
Cheeger inequality).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
(iii):

h(E,\mathcal{B})\leq\min_{i}\val(i)=d
. A tighter analysis (taking

W=N[v]
for a vertex
v
, which has
|W|\leq d+1
and
|E(W)|\leq d
)
gives
h\leq d/(d+1)\leq d/2
for
d\geq 2
. (Actually the
Proposition gives
h\leq d
; the tight bound
d/2
follows from the
Expander Mixing Lemma and a standard averaging argument for
d
-regular
graphs
[
8
, Lemma 3.1.4]
.)
∎

8.
Complex Expander Evolution Algebras

Over
\mathbb{C}
, the structural matrix of a symmetric graphicable EEA is real-symmetric, so all eigenvalues are real and universal bounds apply.

8.1.
Eigenvalues over
\mathbb{C}

Theorem 8.1

(Spectral characterisation over
\mathbb{C}
)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric graphicable
d
-regular EEA over
\mathbb{C}
with
adjacency-type structural matrix
A
. Then:

(i)

All eigenvalues of
A
are real.

(ii)

\lambda_{1}=d
(the Perron eigenvalue).

(iii)

|\lambda_{k}|\leq d
for all
k
.

(iv)

\lambda_{n}\geq-d
.

(v)

The eigenspace of
\lambda_{1}=d
is spanned by the all-ones
vector
\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)^{T}
, i.e., it is one-dimensional
when
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is connected.

Proof.

Since
A
is the adjacency matrix of a
d
-regular graph (a real
symmetric matrix), all its eigenvalues are real. The rest follows from
the Perron–Frobenius theorem applied to
G=A
(non-negative,
irreducible by connectivity).
∎

8.2.
The Alon–Boppana bound for EEAs

Theorem 8.2

(Alon–Boppana bound)
.

Let
(E_{m},\mathcal{B}_{m})_{m\geq 1}
be an EEA family of symmetric
graphicable
d
-regular evolution algebras over
\mathbb{C}
with

n_{m}=\dim E_{m}\to\infty
. Let
\lambda_{2}^{(m)}
be the second largest
eigenvalue of the structural matrix
A_{m}
. Then

\liminf_{m\to\infty}\,\lambda_{2}^{(m)}\;\geq\;2\sqrt{d-1}.

Proof.

The matrices
A_{m}
are the adjacency matrices of an expander
family
(\varGamma(E_{m},\mathcal{B}_{m}))
. The classical Alon–Boppana
theorem
[
8
, Theorem 4.2.x]
(see also Nilli
[
12
]
)
states that for any
d
-regular graph
G
on
n
vertices:

\lambda_{2}(A_{G})\;\geq\;2\sqrt{d-1}\,\left(1-\frac{c}{\diam(G)}\right)

for an absolute constant
c>0
. Since
\diam(\varGamma(E_{m},\mathcal{B}_{m}))\to\infty
(as
n_{m}\to\infty
and the graphs are
d
-regular expanders),
the right-hand side tends to
2\sqrt{d-1}
.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

Theorem
8.2
shows that the spectral gap

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}=d-\lambda_{2}
cannot exceed
d-2\sqrt{d-1}

asymptotically. By the Cheeger inequality
(Theorem
7.3
), the expansion constant satisfies

h(E_{m},\mathcal{B}_{m})\leq\sqrt{d(d-\lambda_{2})}\to\sqrt{d(d-2\sqrt{d-1})}
.

8.3.
Ramanujan evolution algebras

Theorem
8.2
is tight, and the algebras meeting it deserve a name.

Definition 8.4

(Ramanujan evolution algebra)
.

A symmetric graphicable
d
-regular EEA
E
over
\mathbb{K}
is called a

Ramanujan evolution algebra
if the eigenvalues
\lambda
of

A
with
|\lambda|\neq d
satisfy

|\lambda|\;\leq\;2\sqrt{d-1}.

Remark 8.5
.

Equivalently,
E
is Ramanujan if
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is a
Ramanujan graph in the sense of Lubotzky, Phillips, and Sarnak

[
9
]
(or in the sense of Marcus, Spielman, and Srivastava

[
11
]
).

Theorem 8.6

(Expansion of Ramanujan EEAs)
.

A Ramanujan evolution algebra
E
is an
h
-EEA with

h(E,\mathcal{B})\;\geq\;\frac{d-2\sqrt{d-1}}{2}.

Proof.

By the Cheeger inequality (Theorem
7.3
):

h(E,\mathcal{B})\;\geq\;\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}{2}\;=\;\frac{d-\lambda_{2}}{2}\;\geq\;\frac{d-2\sqrt{d-1}}{2}.\qed

Proposition 8.7

(Ramanujan EEAs have optimal mixing time)
.

For a Ramanujan Markov EEA (doubly stochastic,
d
-regular):

t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\epsilon)\;\leq\;\frac{d}{d-2\sqrt{d-1}}\,\log\!\left(\frac{n}{\epsilon}\right).

This is the optimal mixing time among all
d
-regular Markov EEAs.

Proof.

Substitute
h=(d-2\sqrt{d-1})/2
into Corollary
6.5

with
d
replaced by
1
(for the normalized matrix).
∎

9.
Constructions of EEAs

9.1.
Cayley EEAs

Definition 9.1

(Cayley evolution algebra)
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}\}\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}
a
symmetric generating set
(i.e.,
S=S^{-1}
and

1\notin S
), and
\mathbb{K}
a field. The
Cayley evolution algebra

\mathcal{C}(G,S,\mathbb{K})
is defined as follows:

•

Natural basis:
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{g}:g\in G\}
;

•

Structural constants:
e_{g}^{2}=\sum_{s\in S}e_{gs}
,
i.e.,
a_{g,gs}=1
for all
s\in S
and
a_{g,h}=0
otherwise.

Remark 9.2
.

The underlying undirected graph
\varGamma(\mathcal{C}(G,S,\mathbb{K}),\mathcal{B})

is exactly the Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
, since
a_{g,gs}=1

and
a_{gs,g}=a_{gs,(gs)s^{-1}}=1
(because
s^{-1}\in S
).

Theorem 9.3

(Cayley EEAs are EEAs iff Cayley graphs are expanders)
.

\mathcal{C}(G,S,\mathbb{K})
is an
h
-EEA if and only if the Cayley graph

\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
is an
h
-expander.

Proof.

Direct from Definition
9.1
and Definition
3.5
,
since
\varGamma(\mathcal{C}(G,S,\mathbb{K}),\mathcal{B})=\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
.
∎

Corollary 9.4

(EEA families from group theory)
.

Let
(G_{m})_{m\geq 1}
be a family of finite groups with symmetric
generating sets
S_{m}\subseteq G_{m}\setminus\{1\}
,
|S_{m}|=d
for all

m
, such that
|G_{m}|\to\infty
and
\mathrm{Cay}(G_{m},S_{m})
form an
expander family. Then
(\mathcal{C}(G_{m},S_{m},\mathbb{K}))_{m\geq 1}
is an EEA
family.

Example 9.5

(SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
EEAs)
.

Let
p
run over primes and
S\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be a fixed
symmetric generating set for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
that does not
generate a solvable group. Let
S_{p}
be the reduction of
S
modulo

p
. By the Bourgain–Gamburd theorem
[
2
]

(see also
[
8
, Chapter 6]
), the Cayley graphs

\mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),S_{p})
form an expander family.
Therefore,
(\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),S_{p},\mathbb{K}))_{p}
is an EEA
family.

Example 9.6

(Ramanujan Cayley EEAs from LPS)
.

Let
p,q
be distinct primes with
p\equiv q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
p

a quadratic residue mod
q
. The Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak
construction
[
9
]
yields a symmetric generating set

S_{p,q}\subset\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of size
p+1
such that

\mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),S_{p,q})
is a Ramanujan graph.
The corresponding Cayley evolution algebra

\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),S_{p,q},\mathbb{K})
is a Ramanujan
evolution algebra.

9.2.
Tensor products of EEAs

Theorem 9.7

(Kronecker product of EEAs)
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be evolution algebras over
\mathbb{K}
with natural
bases
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n_{1}}
and

\mathcal{B}_{2}=\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{2}}
, and structural matrices

A_{1},A_{2}
. The Kronecker product
E_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}E_{2}

(with natural basis
\{e_{i}\otimes f_{j}\}
and structural constants

a_{(i,j),(k,l)}=a^{(1)}_{ik}\,a^{(2)}_{jl}
) is again an evolution
algebra. If
E_{1}
is an
h_{1}
-EEA and
E_{2}
is an
h_{2}
-EEA,
then
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
satisfies

h\!\bigl(E_{1}\otimes E_{2},\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{2}\bigr)\;\geq\;\min(h_{1},h_{2}).

Proof.

The Kronecker product is an evolution algebra by
Tian
[
14
, Corollary 1(5)]
. The graph

\varGamma(E_{1}\otimes E_{2},\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{2})
is the

tensor product
(or
categorical product
) of the graphs

\varGamma_{1}
and
\varGamma_{2}
: the vertex set is

\{1,\ldots,n_{1}\}\times\{1,\ldots,n_{2}\}
and there is an edge between

(i,j)
and
(k,l)
iff
a^{(1)}_{ik}\neq 0
(or
a^{(1)}_{ki}\neq 0
)

and

a^{(2)}_{jl}\neq 0
(or
a^{(2)}_{lj}\neq 0
).

The expansion of the tensor product graph
\varGamma_{1}\times\varGamma_{2}

satisfies
h(\varGamma_{1}\times\varGamma_{2})\geq\min(h(\varGamma_{1}),h(\varGamma_{2}))
by a standard edge-boundary argument (see, e.g.,

[
8
, Exercise 3.1.13]
), giving the claimed bound.
∎

Corollary 9.8

(EEA families from iterated tensor products)
.

Let
E
be an
h
-EEA. The family
(E^{\otimes m})_{m\geq 1}

(with
\dim E^{\otimes m}=n^{m}\to\infty
) is an EEA family with

h(E^{\otimes m})\geq h
for all
m
.

9.3.
Direct sums

Proposition 9.9

(Direct sum is not an EEA)
.

If
E=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
is a direct sum of two non-trivial evolution
algebras, then
h(E,\mathcal{B})=0
.

Proof.

Taking
W
to be the index set of
E_{1}
, we have
|E(W)|=0

(no structural constants cross between
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
), so

h\leq|E(W)|/|W|=0
.
∎

Direct sums are excluded from the EEA class; this is consistent with Corollary
3.7
.

10.
Examples

Example 10.1

(Cycle evolution algebra)
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
\mathbb{K}
a field. Define
E_{n}
with natural basis

\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
and
e_{i}^{2}=e_{i-1}+e_{i+1}
(indices mod
n
).
This is the
cycle evolution algebra
of Tian
[
14
]
.
The underlying graph
\varGamma(E_{n},\mathcal{B})
is the cycle
C_{n}
.
By Kowalski
[
8
, Example 3.1.3(2)]
:

h(E_{n},\mathcal{B})\;=\;\frac{2}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\;\to\;0.

Hence the family
(E_{n})_{n\geq 3}
is
not
an EEA family, though
each individual
E_{n}
is an
h_{n}
-EEA for
h_{n}>0
.

Example 10.2

(Complete evolution algebra)
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
e_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j\neq i}e_{j}
. The underlying graph is

K_{n}
(complete graph). By Kowalski
[
8
, Example 3.1.3(1)]
:

h(E_{K_{n}},\mathcal{B})\;=\;\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\;\to\;\infty.

This is the most expander-like single algebra but it is
(n-1)
-regular
(unbounded degree), so the family
(E_{K_{n}})
does not satisfy the
bounded-degree condition for an expander family (Definition
2.5
).

Example 10.3

(
3
-regular Ramanujan EEA)
.

The Petersen graph is a
3
-regular graph on
10
vertices.
Its second eigenvalue is
\lambda_{2}=1
, which satisfies
\lambda_{2}=1<2\sqrt{2}=2\sqrt{d-1}
,
so it is a Ramanujan graph. The corresponding evolution algebra, with
n=10
and

a_{ij}=1
for each Petersen edge, is therefore a Ramanujan evolution algebra with

h\geq(3-2\sqrt{2})/2
.

11.
Open Problems

We close with problems that remain open.

Open Problem 1

(Algebraic characterisation of EEAs over finite fields)
.

For a field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
(finite field with
q
elements), characterise
the
h
-EEAs over
\mathbb{K}
purely in terms of the algebra structure
(without reference to real eigenvalues). In particular, does the
combinatorial expansion condition
h(E,\mathcal{B})\geq h
have an
algebraic analogue in the representation theory of
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
?

Open Problem 2

(Non-symmetric EEAs and algebraic persistency)
.

Theorem
4.5
requires the symmetry assumption

a_{ij}=a_{ji}
. Is algebraic persistency equivalent to some directed
expansion condition on
D(E,\mathcal{B})
? More precisely: is there
a directed analogue of the Cheeger constant for evolution algebras such
that the directed expansion condition is equivalent to algebraic
persistency?

Open Problem 3

(Expansion constant as algebraic invariant)
.

Theorem
3.10
shows that for nonsingular EEAs, the
isomorphism class of
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
is an algebraic invariant.
Is the
Cheeger constant

h(E,\mathcal{B})
itself an algebraic
invariant (not just the graph up to isomorphism)? Is there an algebraic
formula for
h
in terms of the structural matrix
A
?

Open Problem 4

(Ihara–Selberg zeta function of EEAs)
.

Tian
[
14
, Section 6.2.3]
notes a connection between
evolution algebras and the Ihara–Selberg zeta function. For an EEA

E
, the Ihara zeta function
Z(E,u)=\prod_{[C]}(1-u^{|C|})^{-1}

(product over prime cycles in
\varGamma(E,\mathcal{B})
) is known to
satisfy a Riemann Hypothesis if
E
is a Ramanujan evolution algebra.
Develop the analogy between the Riemann Hypothesis for EEAs and the
classical Riemann Hypothesis.

Open Problem 5

(EEAs and 3-manifolds)
.

Following Tian’s programme
[
14
, Section 6.2.6]
, a
triangulation of a 3-manifold
M
defines an evolution algebra

\mathcal{A}(M,t)
. When is the family
(\mathcal{A}(M,t_{k}))
of
evolution algebras (over successive barycentric subdivisions
t_{k}
) an
EEA family? Is this related to geometric or topological properties of

M
(e.g., hyperbolicity)?

Open Problem 6

(Automorphisms and symmetry of EEAs)
.

For a Ramanujan evolution algebra
E
, compute the automorphism group

\Aut(E)
(as described by Elduque–Labra
[
7
]
).
Are Ramanujan EEAs more “rigid” (smaller automorphism group) than
non-Ramanujan EEAs of the same dimension and degree?

Open Problem 7

(Continuous EEAs)
.

Tian
[
14
, Section 6.2.4]
proposes continuous evolution
algebras
e_{i}\cdot e_{i}=\sum_{j}a_{ij}(t)e_{j}
with time-dependent
structural constants. Define a
continuous EEA
in which

h(E,\mathcal{B},t)\geq h
for all
t
, and develop an analogue of
the mixing time result (Theorem
6.4
) for continuous-time
Markov chains.

Open Problem 8

(Higher-dimensional analogues)
.

Lubotzky
[
10
]
surveys higher-dimensional expander
complexes (coboundary expanders, cosystolic expanders, spectral
expanders). Define and study
higher-order EEAs
based on
simplicial complexes, and investigate which results of the present
paper generalise to this setting.
