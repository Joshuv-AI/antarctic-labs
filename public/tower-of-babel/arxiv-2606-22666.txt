Minimum Virtual Proper Time and Finite Mass–Charge Matching in QED
Mustafa Bakr

Abstract
We formulate a finite-proper-time version of QED defined by a gauge-covariant generating functional in which every Schwinger proper-time integral has a physical lower endpoint s 0 = Λ − 2 s_{0}=\Lambda^{-2} that is not removed. Closed fermion loops are defined by the gauge-covariant heat-kernel determinant, and open fermion lines are obtained by functional differentiation of the same finite-proper-time open-line kernel. The Ward–Takahashi identity follows exactly from gauge covariance to all orders. The vacuum polarisation is finite and exactly transverse. The on-shell mass relation gives m 0 ≃ 0.482 m_{0}\simeq 0.482 MeV \,\mathrm{MeV} for an illustrative choice Λ = 13 ​ TeV \Lambda=13\,\mathrm{TeV} at one loop. The free finite-proper-time propagators are proved to satisfy Osterwalder–Schrader reflection positivity, with a positive Källén–Lehmann spectral function and no additional ghost states. The on-shell residue Z 2 > 0 Z_{2}>0 at one loop confirms unitarity of the fermion sector, and the perturbative spectral function of the dressed propagator is positive. A worldline bound Q ≤ 0 Q\leq 0 gives fixed-order Euclidean UV finiteness. The one-loop vertex correction gives a finite anomalous magnetic moment, a calculable O ⁡ ( m 2 / Λ 2 ) O(m^{2}/\Lambda^{2}) prediction absent in standard QED.

The present work develops a finite-proper-time formulation of QED. The central postulate is that internal virtual histories represented by Schwinger proper-time integrals have a minimum proper-time duration,

T\geq s_{0}=\Lambda^{-2}.

(1)

The parameter

II
Unified Finite-Proper-Time Path-Integral Definition

The physical argument in this paper is as follows: every internal virtual history that is represented by a Schwinger proper-time integral is defined with the lower endpoint removed:

\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\quad\longrightarrow\quad\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}ds,\qquad s_{0}=\Lambda^{-2}.

(2)

The resulting exponential factors in momentum space are therefore derived from the path integral. They are not added to propagators or vertices by hand.

For a positive Euclidean kinetic operator
P
, the ordinary inverse is
P^{-1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,e^{-sP}
. The finite-proper-time inverse is

P^{-1}_{\Lambda}=\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}ds\,e^{-sP}=e^{-s_{0}P}P^{-1}.

(3)

Thus, for the free Euclidean photon in Feynman gauge,
P=-\partial_{E}^{2}
, one obtains

D_{\mu\nu,\Lambda}(k)=\delta_{\mu\nu}\frac{e^{-s_{0}k_{E}^{2}}}{k_{E}^{2}}.

(4)

This is the proper-time representation of the photon inverse with the endpoint
s=0
excluded. It is not an independently postulated Gaussian form factor.

Closed charged histories are defined by the gauge-covariant determinant

\Gamma_{1,\Lambda}[A]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\,\mathrm{Tr}e^{-s\Delta_{A}},

(5)

where
\Delta_{A}
is the squared Euclidean Dirac operator. Open charged histories are defined by the gauge-covariant open-line kernel

S_{\Lambda}[A](x,y)=(m-\not\!D_{x})\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}dT\,e^{-m^{2}T}\langle x|e^{-TH_{A}}|y\rangle.

(6)

The one-photon vertex, the two-photon contact kernel, endpoint terms, and the self-energy are functional derivatives of Eq. (
6
). All sectors used below are derived from the same finite-proper-time path-integral prescription.

The finite-proper-time covariance (
4

VII
. The results proved here are perturbative UV-finiteness and finite matching statements derived from the unified finite-proper-time path integral.

Theorem 2

(Worldline negativity)
.

For Euclidean momenta satisfying

Q=-\frac{T}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f_{n}|^{2}}{n^{2}}\leq 0,\qquad f_{n}=\sum_{i}k_{i}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}n\tau_{i}/T}.

(27)

Proof.

The Fourier representation of the Green function is
G_{B}=T/6-(T/\pi^{2})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos[2\pi n(\tau-\tau^{\prime})/T]/n^{2}

cancel the constant pieces and give (

∎

The identity gives

For every fixed number of external photon insertions, the contracted Euclidean worldline vertex grows at most polynomially in the external momenta, since the worldline integrand consists of a tensor polynomial in

Proof.

The integral has the form
I_{G}=\int\prod_{\ell}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{\ell}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\prod_{j}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-q_{j}^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}}{q_{j}^{2}}\prod_{v}V_{v}
. By the amplitude-level vertex bound,
\prod_{v}V_{v}
is bounded by a polynomial in the loop momenta. Since every loop momentum passes through at least one photon covariance, there exists a constant
c_{G}>0
such that
\sum_{j}q_{j}^{2}\geq c_{G}\sum_{\ell}k_{\ell}^{2}
in the UV region. The integrand is therefore bounded by
C_{G}(1+|k|)^{N_{G}}\exp(-c_{G}|k|^{2}/\Lambda^{2})
, which is integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{4L}
for every finite
N_{G}
.
∎

The Gaussian covariance is an efficient Euclidean UV completion, but it is not assumed to define a reflection-positive physical photon Hilbert space. The Euclidean damping factor is not, by itself, a proof of Osterwalder–Schrader reflection positivity or of Lorentzian unitarity. Entire-function nonlocal theories can admit generalised spectral representations, but these depend on the analytic-contour prescription and do not follow automatically from Euclidean UV damping alone. Therefore this theorem is a Euclidean fixed-order UV statement, not a proof of a nonperturbative unitary Lorentzian theory.

As an explicit two-loop check, the self-energy-insertion contribution to the two-loop vacuum polarisation can be evaluated through the mass derivative of the one-loop result:
\Pi^{(2)}_{\rm SE}(q^{2})=(\partial\Pi^{(1)}/\partial m^{2})\times 2m\,\delta m_{\Lambda}
. This is a chain-rule identity applied to the exact one-loop vacuum polarisation (

\delta m
inside the one-loop VP loop, the transverse

contribution is exactly the mass derivative of
\Pi^{(1)}

\Pi^{(2)}_{\rm SE}(0)=9.0\times 10^{-5}

O(\alpha/\pi)
relative to

\Pi^{(2)}/\Pi^{(1)}

3.5\times 10^{-3}
at

at

The two-loop mass correction from the iterated self-energy is likewise finite:
\delta m^{(2)}=(\mathrm{d}\,\delta m/\mathrm{d}m)\,\delta m^{(1)}

\delta m^{(2)}/m=3.4\times 10^{-3}

\delta m^{(2)}/\delta m^{(1)}=0.055

\delta m(m+\delta m^{(1)})-\delta m(m)

VIII
Finite Matching, Charge Running, and Physical Observables

The finite-proper-time formulation replaces divergent renormalisation by finite matching. In the Thomson scheme,
\Pi_{R}(q_{E}^{2})=\Pi(q_{E}^{2})-\Pi(0)
with
\Pi_{R}(0)=0
. In the mass sector,
m_{\rm phys}=m_{0}+\delta m_{\Lambda}(m_{0},\Lambda)
with
\delta m_{\Lambda}
finite. The finite-proper-time parameter
\Lambda
must be determined experimentally or embedded in a deeper theory.

For spacelike momentum transfer
Q^{2}=q_{E}^{2}>0
, such as in electron–muon scattering, the dressed photon propagator is well defined in the Euclidean formulation. The bare inverse is
D_{0,\Lambda}^{-1}(Q^{2})=Q^{2}\mathrm{e}^{Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}
, and the fermion loop contributes a term
-Q^{2}\Pi_{R}(Q^{2})
. Therefore the dressed photon propagator is

D_{\Lambda}^{\rm dressed}(Q^{2})=\frac{1}{Q^{2}\left[\mathrm{e}^{Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}-\Pi_{R}(Q^{2})\right]}.

(28)

At low
Q^{2}\ll\Lambda^{2}
this reproduces the standard QED result
1/\{Q^{2}[1-\Pi_{R}(Q^{2})]\}
. At
Q^{2}\gg\Lambda^{2}
the exponential dominates and the spacelike amplitude is exponentially suppressed.

For timelike processes such as high-mass Drell–Yan production,
q^{2}=m_{\ell\ell}^{2}>0
, the situation is more subtle. The Euclidean Gaussian factor
\mathrm{e}^{-Q_{E}^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}
becomes
\mathrm{e}^{+m_{\ell\ell}^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}
under naive analytic continuation, which enhances rather than suppresses the amplitude. A direct real-time Schwinger-parameter prescription would produce a phase factor
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s_{0}(q^{2}+\mathrm{i}0)}
rather than Euclidean damping; whether this is the physical collider prescription requires a separate Lorentzian construction. This paper does not resolve the Lorentzian continuation; the results established here are Euclidean.

Existing high-mass dilepton searches
CMS2019
show no significant nonresonant deviation from the Standard Model. Their published limits apply to specific contact-interaction and ADD-graviton templates, not to a finite-proper-time photon propagator. In the low-energy limit the finite-proper-time modification induces an effective contact coefficient of order
4\pi\alpha/\Lambda^{2}
, which corresponds to an effective compositeness scale
\Lambda_{\rm CI,eff}\sim\Lambda/\sqrt{\alpha}\simeq 150\,\mathrm{TeV}
for
\Lambda=13\,\mathrm{TeV}
, well above the CMS 95% CL limits of 20–32 TeV. This estimate ignores chiral structure,
Z
-exchange,
\gamma
–
Z
interference, and quark electric-charge factors, and is intended only as an order-of-magnitude comparison. A direct bound on the finite-proper-time scale from collider data requires a dedicated recast using the Lorentzian continuation of the finite-proper-time photon propagator. Precision low-energy QED observables, including constraints from the muon anomalous magnetic moment
Muong2_2023
, provide complementary sensitivity at the
O(m^{2}/\Lambda^{2})
level. The benchmark value
\Lambda=13\,\mathrm{TeV}
is illustrative and is not derived in this paper.

VIII.1
Anomalous magnetic moment

The one-loop vertex correction provides a concrete prediction that distinguishes the finite-proper-time framework from standard QED. The derivation proceeds in four steps: Feynman parametrization of the vertex integral with FPT propagators, Gaussian loop integration, extraction of
F_{2}(0)
via the Gordon decomposition, and evaluation of the leading correction.

The one-loop vertex correction is

\Lambda^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)=e^{2}\!\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,\gamma^{\nu}S_{\Lambda}(p^{\prime}\!+\!k)\gamma^{\mu}S_{\Lambda}(p\!+\!k)\gamma_{\nu}D_{\Lambda}(k),

(29)

with
S_{\Lambda}(p)=(m-\mathrm{i}\not\!p)\,R(p^{2}+m^{2})
and

x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
with
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=1
for the two fermion and one photon propagators, and combining into a total Schwinger time
T

x_{i}T\geq s_{0}
for each propagator gives
T\geq s_{0}/\min(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
. After completing the square in
k
and performing the Gaussian loop integral, the combined Euclidean denominator at
q=p^{\prime}-p\to 0
(on shell,
p^{2}=p^{\prime 2}=m^{2}
, massless photon) is
\Delta=m^{2}(1-x_{3})^{2}

F_{2}
projection of the Dirac numerator
PeskinSchroeder1995
gives

\int_{T_{\min}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}T\,\mathrm{e}^{-T\Delta}=\mathrm{e}^{-T_{\min}\Delta}/\Delta
, where
T_{\min}=s_{0}/\min(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

F_{2}^{\rm FPT}(0)=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\int_{0}^{1-z}\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{4z}{1-z}\\
\times\exp\!\left(-\frac{m^{2}(1-z)^{2}}{\min(x,1{-}x{-}z,z)\Lambda^{2}}\right).

(30)

In the limit
\Lambda\to\infty

\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\int_{0}^{1-z}\mathrm{d}x\,4z/(1-z)=\int_{0}^{1}4z\,\mathrm{d}z=2

The correction

\min(x,y,z)\to 0

\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,[1-\exp(-a/x)]\simeq a[\ln(1/a)+1-\gamma_{E}]
for small

scaling with
\mu=m^{2}/\Lambda^{2}
. By symmetry the

\delta a_{e}\simeq-\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\times 2\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{4z}{1-z}\,a(z)\bigl[\ln\tfrac{1}{a(z)}+1-\gamma_{E}\bigr]\\
=-\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,z(1-z)\Bigl[\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}(1-z)^{2}}+1-\gamma_{E}\Bigr].

(31)

Splitting the logarithm
\ln[\Lambda^{2}/(m^{2}(1-z)^{2})]=\ln(\Lambda^{2}/m^{2})+2\ln[1/(1-z)]
and evaluating the elementary integrals
\int_{0}^{1}z(1-z)\,\mathrm{d}z=1/6
,
\int_{0}^{1}z(1-z)\ln[1/(1-z)]\,\mathrm{d}z=5/36

\delta a_{e}=-\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\biggl[\frac{1}{6}\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}+\frac{5}{18}+\frac{1-\gamma_{E}}{6}\biggr]\\
+\,O\!\left(\frac{m^{4}}{\Lambda^{4}}\ln^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}\right).

(32)

The
(1-\gamma_{E})/6

O(m^{2}/\Lambda^{2})
without a logarithmic enhancement and is absorbed, together with
5/18

O(m^{2}/\Lambda^{2})

33

\delta a_{e}=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\,\frac{2}{3}\,\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}+O\!\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right).

(33)

The coefficient

IX
Spectral Properties and the Optical Theorem

The Lorentzian spectral content of the vacuum polarisation provides the most direct test of whether the finite-proper-time framework gives physical spectral functions. In standard QED, the imaginary part of
\Pi
above the pair-production threshold
q^{2}>4m^{2}
is fixed by the optical theorem
Cutkosky1960
;
PeskinSchroeder1995
:

\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi(q^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)=-\frac{\alpha}{3}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^{2}}{q^{2}}}\left(1+\frac{2m^{2}}{q^{2}}\right).

(34)

In the finite-proper-time Feynman-parameter representation,
\Pi
involves the incomplete gamma function
\Gamma(0,s_{0}[m^{2}+x(1-x)q^{2}])
. For spacelike
q_{E}^{2}>0
the argument is positive and
\Pi
is real. For timelike
q^{2}>4m^{2}

m^{2}-x(1-x)q^{2}
is negative in the interval
x_{-}<x<x_{+}
where
x_{\pm}=(1\pm\sqrt{1-4m^{2}/q^{2}})/2
. The imaginary part of
\Gamma(0,z)
for
z<0
arises from the branch cut of the logarithm:
\mathrm{Im}\,\Gamma(0,z-\mathrm{i}\epsilon)=\pi
for
z<0
, independently of the value of
s_{0}
. Therefore

\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi^{\rm FPT}(q^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)=-2\alpha\int_{x_{-}}^{x_{+}}\mathrm{d}x\,x(1-x)=\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi^{\rm std}(q^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon).

(35)

The imaginary part is unchanged by the finite-proper-time cutoff. The spectral function
\rho(s)=-\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi(s)/\pi
is non-negative and identical to the standard QED spectral function. The optical theorem is satisfied: the Cutkosky rules
Cutkosky1960
give the same imaginary part whether or not the proper-time cutoff is present.

The finite-proper-time modification affects only the real part of
\Pi
through the
s_{0}
-dependent piece of
\Gamma(0,s_{0}\Delta)
. The subtracted vacuum polarisation admits the decomposition

\Pi_{R}(Q_{E}^{2})=\int_{4m^{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{\rho(s)\,Q_{E}^{2}}{s(Q_{E}^{2}+s)}+\Delta\Pi_{\Lambda}(Q_{E}^{2}),

(36)

where the first term is the standard spectral integral with
\rho\geq 0
, and
\Delta\Pi_{\Lambda}
is an entire function of
Q_{E}^{2}
with no spectral support that encodes the finite-proper-time matching. At low
Q_{E}^{2}\ll\Lambda^{2}
the matching function is a constant; at high
Q_{E}^{2}\gg\Lambda^{2}
it provides the polynomial
\Lambda^{4}/Q_{E}^{4}
tail.

The significance of this decomposition is that the reflection-positivity question is localised. The fermion-loop vacuum polarisation has a positive spectral function and satisfies the optical theorem. As shown in Sec.
X
, the finite-proper-time propagators themselves satisfy Osterwalder–Schrader reflection positivity, so that the Euclidean framework admits a unitary Hilbert-space reconstruction in the sense of the OS axioms
OsterwalderSchrader1973
;
OsterwalderSchrader1975
.

The on-shell residue of the dressed fermion propagator provides a complementary unitarity check. The dressed inverse propagator
S_{\Lambda}^{-1}(p)-\Sigma(p)
has a pole near
p^{2}=-m_{\rm phys}^{2}
, with residue
Z_{2}
. At one loop,
Z_{2}^{-1}=1-\Sigma_{V}(m^{2})-2m^{2}[\Sigma_{S}^{\prime}(m^{2})+\Sigma_{V}^{\prime}(m^{2})]
, where
\Sigma=m\Sigma_{S}+\mathrm{i}\not\!p\Sigma_{V}
and primes denote
d/dp^{2}
. The imaginary part of the self-energy for timelike momenta is also identical to standard QED, because
\mathrm{Im}\,\Gamma(0,s_{0}z+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)=\pi
for
z<0
independently of
s_{0}
(the sign is fixed by the Feynman

X
Reflection Positivity of the Finite-Proper-Time Propagators

A central question for any nonlocal or higher-derivative field theory is whether the modified propagators satisfy Osterwalder–Schrader reflection positivity
OsterwalderSchrader1973
;
OsterwalderSchrader1975
, which guarantees the existence of a unitary Hilbert space and a positive Hamiltonian in the reconstructed Lorentzian theory. Arici
et al.

Arici2018
proved that rational modifications of the propagator, such as those arising in higher-derivative gravity and Lee–Wick theories, generically violate reflection positivity, because the additional poles in the complex
p^{2}
-plane introduce ghost states with negative spectral weight. The finite-proper-time propagator contains an entire function
\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}p^{2}}
, which has no zeros or poles. We now prove that it satisfies OS2 explicitly.

Let
\Theta
denote reflection in Euclidean time:
(\Theta f)(t,\vec{x})=f(-t,\vec{x})
. For a scalar two-point function
\Delta(p)
, OS2 requires that the partial Fourier transform in the time component,

\widetilde{\Delta}(\vec{p},\,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{4}}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}p_{4}t}\Delta(p_{4},\vec{p}),

(37)

satisfy
\widetilde{\Delta}(\vec{p},\,t)\geq 0
for all
t
and all
\vec{p}
.

For the finite-proper-time photon propagator
\Delta_{\Lambda}(p)=\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}p^{2}}/p^{2}
(massless case; the massive fermion case is identical with
\omega^{2}=\vec{p}^{\,2}+m^{2}
), write

\Delta_{\Lambda}(p_{4},\vec{p})=\frac{1}{p_{4}^{2}+\omega^{2}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}p_{4}^{2}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}\vec{p}^{\,2}},

(38)

where
\omega=|\vec{p}\,|
. The partial Fourier transform in
p_{4}
of a product is the convolution:

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\Lambda}(\vec{p},\,t)=\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}\vec{p}^{\,2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\;\underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\omega|\tau|}}{2\omega}}_{\displaystyle G_{0}(\tau)\,\geq\,0}\;\;\underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(t-\tau)^{2}/(4s_{0})}}{\sqrt{4\pi s_{0}}}}_{\displaystyle K_{s_{0}}(t-\tau)\,\geq\,0}\,.

(39)

Here
G_{0}
is the standard free-propagator time kernel and
K_{s_{0}}
is the heat kernel (the Fourier transform of
\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}p_{4}^{2}}
). Since both factors under the integral are non-negative, and the prefactor
\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}\vec{p}^{\,2}}>0
, the convolution is non-negative for all
t
:

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\Lambda}(\vec{p},\,t)\geq 0\qquad\forall\;\vec{p},\;t.

(40)

This proves that the free finite-proper-time propagators satisfy OS2.

The same argument applies to the seagull (contact) interaction
V_{2}^{\mu\nu}=-2e^{2}\delta^{\mu\nu}
, which contributes a term proportional to
\delta(t)
in position space. The OS2 bilinear form gives
\langle\Theta f,\delta\cdot f\rangle=\int d^{3}x\,|f(0,\vec{x})|^{2}\geq 0
, so the contact term is also reflection-positive.

The spectral content is equally transparent. The Källén–Lehmann decomposition gives

\Delta_{\Lambda}(p^{2})=\frac{Z}{p^{2}+m^{2}}+h_{\Lambda}(p^{2}),

(41)

where
Z=\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}m^{2}}
is a finite positive residue and
h_{\Lambda}(p^{2})=[\mathrm{e}^{-s_{0}(p^{2}+m^{2})}-Z]/(p^{2}+m^{2})
is an entire function with no singularities or branch cuts (the apparent pole at
p^{2}=-m^{2}

\rho(\mu^{2})=Z\,\delta(\mu^{2}-m^{2})\geq 0.

(42)

The finite-proper-time modification adds only a spectral-free entire function
h_{\Lambda}
—it introduces no ghosts, no additional poles, and no negative spectral weight.

The free-theory RP proof does not, by itself, establish unitarity of the interacting theory. At one loop, the dressed fermion propagator
S_{\rm dressed}(p)=[i\not\!p-m-\Sigma(p)]^{-1}
has a spectral decomposition near the physical mass pole
p^{2}=-m_{\rm phys}^{2}

\rho_{\rm dressed}(\mu^{2})=Z_{2}\,\delta(\mu^{2}-m_{\rm phys}^{2})+\rho_{\rm cont}(\mu^{2}).

(43)

Unitarity of the interacting theory requires
Z_{2}>0
and
\rho_{\rm cont}(\mu^{2})\geq 0
. For the first condition, the one-loop residue
Z_{2}
computed in Sec.
IX
gives
Z_{2}=1.17
; positive. For the second, the continuum spectral function is determined by the imaginary part of the self-energy across the pair-production cut:
\rho_{\rm cont}(\mu^{2})\propto\mathrm{Im}\,\Sigma(\mu^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)

\mathrm{Im}\,\Gamma(0,\,z+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)=\pi\qquad\text{for }z<0,\;\;\forall\;s_{0}>0,

(44)

which follows from
\Gamma(0,z)=-\mathrm{Ei}(-z)
and the branch cut of
\mathrm{Ei}(x)
at
x>0

\mathrm{i}\epsilon
prescription selects the upper branch, giving
+\pi
independently of the prefactor
s_{0}
multiplying

\rho_{\rm cont}^{\rm FPT}=\rho_{\rm cont}^{\rm std}\geq 0

\widetilde{S}_{\rm dressed}(\vec{p},t)=Z_{2}\,e^{-\omega|t|}/(2\omega)+\int\mathrm{d}\mu^{2}\,\rho_{\rm cont}\,e^{-\sqrt{\vec{p}^{\,2}+\mu^{2}}|t|}/(2\sqrt{\cdots})\geq 0

For interacting theories, perturbative reflection positivity in the coincidence limit has been established for nonlocal theories with entire-function form factors by Christodoulou and Modesto

XI
Quantum-Gravity Outlook

The finite-proper-time idea suggests a natural perturbative UV mechanism for gravity. In higher-derivative gravity
Stelle1977
and nonlocal gravity programmes
Modesto2012
;
Biswas2012
, UV finiteness is achieved at the cost of unitarity or additional degrees of freedom. In a background-field treatment with
g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+\kappa h_{\mu\nu}
, the one-loop gravitational effective action has the schematic form
\Gamma_{\rm grav}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\ln\Delta_{2}-\mathrm{Tr}\ln\Delta_{\rm gh}+\cdots
, where
\Delta_{2}
is the graviton fluctuation operator and
\Delta_{\rm gh}
the ghost operator. In a finite-proper-time version,
\int_{0}^{\infty}ds/s\to\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}ds/s
with

. The standard heat-kernel expansion
\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathrm{e}^{-s\Delta}\sim(4\pi s)^{-2}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,\mathrm{Tr}(a_{0}+sa_{1}+s^{2}a_{2}+\cdots)
shows that ordinary UV divergences arise from

. With
s\geq s_{0}
, the quartic, quadratic, and logarithmic divergences become finite contributions proportional to powers and logarithms of
\Lambda_{G}
. At fixed loop order, the same Schwinger-parameter bound would be expected to give perturbative UV finiteness, although the graviton derivative couplings require a separate vertex-bound analysis beyond the QED worldline bound proved here.

This is only a perturbative UV-finiteness mechanism. It is not a complete quantum-gravity theory. It does not solve background independence, the conformal-factor problem, Lorentzian unitarity beyond perturbation theory, or the cosmological-constant problem. The appropriate claim is that the finite-proper-time principle may convert perturbative gravitational UV divergences into finite curvature matching terms.
