Malliavin calculus for signatures with applications to finance
Eduardo Abi Jaber, Clément Rey, Dimitri Sotnikov

Abstract
Malliavin calculus is a powerful and general framework for the analysis of square-integrable random variables, but it often suffers from a lack of tractability and explicit representations. To address this limitation, we focus on a subclass of random variables given by finite linear combinations of time-extended Brownian motion signatures. The class remains rich due to the universal approximation properties of signatures. Leveraging the algebraic structure of signatures, we first derive explicit formulas for the Malliavin derivative of signatures of continuous Itô processes. As a consequence, we obtain closed-form expressions for the Clark–Ocone representation, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and its generator, as well as the integration-by-parts formula within the class of Brownian signature variables. These results provide purely algebraic formulations of the classical operators of Malliavin calculus. As an application, we compute Greeks for general path-dependent options under signature volatility models, and numerically compare different choices of Malliavin weights.

60H07, 60H30

Keywords:

Path signatures, Malliavin calculus, Integration by parts, Greeks.

Introduction

A central problem in probability is to establish differentiability of the mapping

\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}\big[f(G^{\theta})\big],

where
G^{\theta}
is a random variable depending on a parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
and to derive tractable representations of the associated parameter sensitivity

\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathbb{E}\big[f(G^{\theta})\big].

The

Malliavin 1978
calculus

1

We refer to
Nualart 2006
;
Alós et al. 2021
for a comprehensive treatment of Malliavin calculus and its applications.
, which serves as a stochastic analogue of the calculus of variations, provides a powerful tool to address this problem whenever
G^{\theta}
is an
\mathcal{F}^{W}_{T}
-measurable random variable with
W
a Brownian motion. Under suitable regularity and non-degeneracy assumptions, the Malliavin integration-by-parts formula yields the representation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathbb{E}\big[f(G^{\theta})\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[f(G^{\theta})\pi_{T}\big],

(1.1)

where
\pi_{T}
denotes the associated Malliavin weight. An important application of such formulas is the derivation of explicit expressions for the density of random variables. In particular,
Bismut 1981
introduced a probabilistic proof of Hörmander’s theorem based on integration by parts on the Wiener space, establishing the smoothness and lower bounds of densities for a broad class of diffusion processes
(
Kusuoka and Stroock 1985
;
Kohatsu-Higa 2003
;
Nualart 2006
;
Bally 2006
;
Nourdin and Viens 2009
)
. Furthermore, the representation (
1.1
) is particularly attractive for Monte Carlo methods, as it avoids differentiating the function
f
.
Integration-by-parts techniques have proven useful in stochastic optimal control
(
Gobet and Munos 2005
)
, gradient estimation in machine learning
(
Oden 2025
)
, and the improvement of learning procedures
(
Pidstrigach et al. 2025
)
.
In mathematical finance, the computation of sensitivities (the so-called Greeks) via Malliavin integration by parts was first developed within the Black–Scholes framework
(
Fournié et al. 1999
)
. This approach was later extended to stochastic volatility models
(
El-Khatib 2009
)
, including hybrid
(
Yilmaz 2018
)
, jump-diffusion
(
El-Khatib and Privault 2003
;
Davis and Johansson 2006
;
Petrou 2008
)
, and non-Markovian settings
(
Al-Foraih et al. 2023
)
, and to more complex derivatives such as barrier, lookback
(
Gobet and Kohatsu-Higa 2003
)
, and American options
(
Bally et al. 2003
)
.

However, in general, the Malliavin weight
\pi_{T}
is either not explicit or difficult to compute numerically, except in very specific configurations where additional structure is available.
This naturally leads to the following question:

Can one identify a structural and generic class of random variables
G^{\theta}
for which the Malliavin weight admits an explicit and numerically tractable form?

A natural solution comes from the theory of path signatures, introduced by
Chen 1957
as sequences of iterated integrals, which provide a systematic way to represent functionals of continuous paths.
More precisely, the signature
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}
of the time-augmented Brownian motion
\widehat{W}_{t}=(t,W_{t})
,
t\geq 0
, is defined as the sequence

\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\left(1,\begin{pmatrix}t\\
W_{t}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\frac{t^{2}}{2!}&\int_{0}^{t}s\,dW_{s}\\
\int_{0}^{t}W_{s}\,ds&\frac{W_{t}^{2}}{2!}\end{pmatrix},\ldots\right),

and serves as a universal approximator: it naturally appears in stochastic Taylor expansions of Markovian
(
Ben Arous 1989
;
Kloeden and Platen 1992
)
and non-Markovian systems
(
Fliess 1983
;
Abi Jaber et al. 2024
)
, and satisfies a Stone–Weierstrass–type theorem for functionals of Brownian motion. This highlights the role of signatures as analogues of polynomials on path space and underlines their foundational importance in the theory of rough paths initiated by Terry Lyons (see, e.g.,
Lyons et al. 2007
).

The interplay between Malliavin calculus and the theory of rough paths and signatures has received considerable attention. For instance,
Friz and Victoir 2010
apply Malliavin calculus to establish the existence of smooth densities for rough differential equations (RDEs). Further developments include the study of Malliavin differentiability of RDE solutions by
Inahama 2014
, the analysis of Malliavin differentiability and existence of smooth densities for rough SDEs by
Bugini et al. 2024
, and the computation of the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion of Gaussian-process signatures by
Cass and Ferrucci 2024
. Moreover, Malliavin calculus was used to establish the densities of the truncated path signatures themselves. See
Kusuoka and Stroock 1987
for the case of Brownian motion signature and
Baudoin et al. 2020
for the fractional Brownian motion case. Regarding the computation of Greeks,
Teichmann 2006
proposed an approach relying on the cubature on Wiener space introduced by
Lyons and Victoir 2004
. Most existing approaches start from an SDE, use a stochastic Taylor expansion to bring out the signature, and then, under conditions such as Hörmander’s, apply Malliavin calculus. A comprehensive framework for applying Malliavin calculus directly to the Brownian signature
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
is not yet fully developed. We instead isolate the Malliavin/signature component, aiming at a unified and generic treatment that exploits the algebraic structure of signatures and extends beyond the SDE setting to non-Markovian dynamics.

Going back to our question, and motivated by the universal approximation properties of path signatures, we restrict our attention to random variables that are linear in the signature of Brownian motion, of the form

G^{\theta}=\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{\theta},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle,

where the coefficients
\ell^{\theta}
are deterministic. A first crucial observation is that this representation decouples the stochastic noise from the parameter dependence. As a consequence, the sensitivity analysis reduces to first understanding the Malliavin calculus of the generic object
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}
and then applying integration-by-parts formulas to the class of linear functionals. Compared to the related literature, a key feature of our approach is that all fundamental Malliavin operations on the signature are systematically encoded algebraically as linear operators on the (extended) tensor algebra. This allows us to exploit the rich algebraic structure of signatures to derive explicit expressions for the Malliavin weights, while the computability of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}
enables the design of numerically tractable implementations.

To implement this strategy, we first develop a probabilistic perspective on the path signature of stochastic processes, which allows us to derive results for the Malliavin derivative of the signature of general Itô processes (Theorem
4.4
) echoing the calculus of variations for rough differential equations
(
Marie 2015
)
, but using a purely probabilistic approach. In Theorem
4.3
, we further identify a class of processes for which the Stratonovich integral is Malliavin differentiable, and we derive a formula for its Malliavin derivative.

By combining this viewpoint with the powerful algebraic properties of the signature, we then
show that several fundamental operations from Malliavin calculus admit explicit and tractable representations when applied to Brownian signatures. In particular, we derive explicit expressions for the Skorokhod integral (Proposition
5.7
), the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup (Theorem
5.14
), chaos expansions (Proposition
5.9
), and ultimately to an integration-by-parts formula (Theorem
6.8
) for functionals of the form
f(G^{\theta})
, where the Malliavin weights are determined explicitly and belong to a class of random variables stable under integration by parts. More precisely, this stable class turns out to be the class of rational functions of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}
, that is, ratios of two linear functionals of the signature.

As an application, we use our results to derive and compute model sensitivities (Greeks) of path-dependent payoffs in signature volatility models in Section
7
.
Signature volatility models form a universal and flexible class of volatility models as shown by
Arribas et al. 2020
;
Cuchiero et al. 2025a
;
Abi Jaber and Gérard 2025a
, capable of representing and approximating a wide range of (possibly non-Markovian) dynamics while preserving computational tractability for pricing and hedging derivatives. Within this setting, our Malliavin integration-by-parts formula yields explicit and reusable expressions for Greeks of a large class of path-dependent payoffs.
In particular, any payoff of the form

f\!\left(\left\langle\ell,{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle\right),

where
{\mathbb{X}}_{T}
denotes the signature of the time-augmented log-price process
(t,\log S_{t})
, can be treated within a unified framework.
The resulting sensitivity formula depends only on the deterministic coefficient
\ell
, without requiring additional model-specific derivations, which makes the approach particularly robust and attractive for practical implementation. For completeness, we perform numerical comparisons of different choices of Malliavin weights for European and Asian options.

The results presented in this paper can be useful in a variety of settings where signatures are employed. Beyond volatility modeling, signatures have found wide-ranging applications across various domains; see
Chevyrev and Kormilitzin 2025
for a comprehensive overview of the signature method. In particular, they have been used to solve stochastic control problems
(
Bayer et al. 2023
;
Bayer et al. 2025
;
Abi Jaber et al. 2025b
)
to capture memory effects and nonlinearities in machine learning for time series
(
Levin et al. 2016
;
Drobac et al. 2025
;
Abi Jaber and Gérard 2025b
)
, and in finance
(
Lyons et al. 2020
;
Cartea et al. 2022
)
.

We finally mention that an alternative framework to Malliavin calculus is Functional Itô Calculus, introduced by
Dupire 2019
and subsequently employed by
Dupire and Tissot-Daguette 2022
to prove the functional Taylor expansion. These results were then extended by
Cuchiero et al. 2025b
to functionals of càdlàg rough paths, leveraging signature approximation properties, and by
Bielert 2025
using rough integration theory. The connection between the functional (vertical) derivative and the Malliavin derivative was established in
Cont and Fournié 2013
. Building on these ideas,
Jazaerli and F. Saporito 2017
proposed an approach to Greek computation based on integration by parts with functional derivatives. However, their tractable formula applies only to so-called weakly path-dependent functionals, which exclude many path-dependent derivative contracts of practical interest.

2.1
Notations

From now on, let
T>0
be fixed. Throughout this paper, we work on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
supporting a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion
W
, where
(\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W})_{t\in[0,T]}
denotes the filtration generated by
W
.

We will denote by
\circ
the Stratonovich integrals. Namely, for two continuous semimartingales
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, the Stratonovich integral is defined by

\int_{0}^{\cdot}Y_{s}\circ dX_{s}=\int_{0}^{\cdot}Y_{s}\,dX_{s}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle_{\cdot},

where the integral on the right-hand side is the standard Itô integral
\langle X,Y\rangle
denotes the quadratic covariation between
X
and
Y
.

We will denote by
C^{k}_{p}(V,W)
(resp.
C^{k}_{b}(V,W)
) the space of
k
times differentiable functions from
V
to
W
with all derivatives of polynomial growth (resp. bounded).

Path signature.

We introduce the key objects needed to present the main results of this work. For a more thorough introduction to path signatures, we refer to
Chevyrev and Kormilitzin 2025
. We start with the definition of the path signature of a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued semimartingale
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, which will be denoted by
\mathbb{X}
. The
path signature

{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}
of
X
over
[s,t]
for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
is the infinite sequence of iterated Stratonovich integrals:

{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}:=\left({\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}\right)_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}\in V}=\left(\ \,\idotsint\limits_{s\leq u_{1}\leq\ldots\leq u_{n}\leq t}\circ dX_{u_{1}}^{i_{1}}\ldots\circ dX_{u_{n}}^{i_{n}}\right)_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}\in V}.

(2.1)

Here,
V
denotes the set of all words in the alphabet
A=\{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{1}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{d}}}\}
. We will also use the standard notation
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}:={\mathbb{X}}_{0,t}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Throughout this paper, we will often consider the
time-augmented
paths
\widehat{X}\colon t\mapsto(t,X_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and their signatures, denoted by
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
. In this case, it is convenient to consider the alphabet
\{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{1}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{d}}}\}
, where the letter
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}
corresponds to integration with respect to the time component, while the letters
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
correspond to integration with respect to

for
i=1,\ldots,d
.

\blacksquare

Extended tensor algebra.

For
n\geq 0
, each word
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}\in V
of length
|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n
, with
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{k}}}}\in A,\,k=1,\ldots,n
, can be identified with a basis vector
e_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{i_{n}}
of
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes n}
, where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
form an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We use the convention
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes 0}\cong\mathbb{R}
and denote the corresponding empty word by
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}
. This leads to the definition of the
extended tensor algebra

T((\mathbb{R}^{d})):=\prod_{n\geq 0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes n}=\left\{\bm{\ell}=(\bm{\ell}_{0},\bm{\ell}_{1},\ldots)\colon\ \bm{\ell}_{n}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes n},\ n\geq 0\right\}.

(2.2)

Since each element
\bm{\ell}_{n}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes n}
admits a representation
\bm{\ell}_{n}=\sum_{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n}\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}
, each element
\bm{\ell}\in T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
can be written as the formal sum

\bm{\ell}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n}\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}.

(2.3)

In particular, the path signature defined in (
2.1
) belongs to
T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and admits the representation

{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n}{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}.

Tensor algebra and duality.

We denote by
T(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
the
tensor algebra

T(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes n},

i.e. the subspace of sequences
\bm{\ell}\in T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
having only finitely many non-zero components. Note that
T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the topological dual of
T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
(equipped with the product topology), with pairing given by

\langle\bm{\ell},\mathbb{X}\rangle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n}\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}\mathbb{X}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}},\quad\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\mathbb{X}\in T((\mathbb{R}^{d})),

where the sum is well-defined since only finitely many terms are nonzero. Moreover, given a continuous linear operator
{\bf A}\colon T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, we call
{\bf A}^{*}\colon T(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
its
adjoint
if

\langle\bm{\ell},{\bf A}\mathbb{X}\rangle=\langle{\bf A}^{*}\bm{\ell},\mathbb{X}\rangle,

for all
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathbb{X}\in T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Algebraic structure.

The extended tensor algebra
T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
becomes an algebra with unit element
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}
when equipped with the standard operations of addition, scalar multiplication, and the tensor product defined by

\bm{\ell}\otimes\bm{p}\in T((\mathbb{R}^{d})),\qquad(\bm{\ell}\otimes\bm{p})_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\bm{\ell}_{k}\otimes\bm{p}_{n-k},\quad n\geq 0.

A key algebraic property of the path signature, known as the
Chen 1957
identity, states that the signature of a path over
[s,t]
is equal to the product of its signatures over
[s,u]
and
[u,t]
for all
s\leq u\leq t
, namely,

{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}={\mathbb{X}}_{s,u}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{u,t}.

(2.4)

In particular, it follows from the uniqueness result of
Boedihardjo et al. 2016
that the signature of a path
X
concatenated with its time-reversal
\overleftarrow{X}^{[0,T]}\colon t\mapsto X_{T-t}
is trivial. By Chen’s identity,

{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\otimes\overleftarrow{\mathbb{X}}_{T}^{[0,T]}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}},

and hence the inverse group element of
{\mathbb{X}}_{T}
is given by the signature of the time-reversed path,
{\mathbb{X}}_{T}^{-1}=\overleftarrow{\mathbb{X}}_{T}^{[0,T]}
.

Malliavin derivative of path signatures.

We introduce the space of simple functionals as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}:=\left\{F\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\colon\ F(\omega)=f(W_{t_{k+1}}(\omega)-W_{t_{k}}(\omega),\ k=0,\ldots,2^{n}-1),\ f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{p}((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2^{n}},\mathbb{R}),\ t_{k}=\frac{kT}{2^{n}},\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

For
F\in\mathcal{S}
, we define the
Malliavin derivative
of
F
as an element of
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
with components

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}^{j}_{t}F=\partial_{x^{j}_{l}}f(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}},k=0,\ldots,2^{n}-1),\quad j=1,\ldots,d,

(2.5)

for
t\in(t_{l},t_{l+1}]
, where
\partial_{x^{j}_{l}}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to the
j
-th component of the argument
x_{l}
, associated with the increment
W_{t_{l+1}}-W_{t_{l}}
.
We use the convention
\mathbf{D}_{0}^{j}F=0
.

Higher-order Malliavin derivatives are defined recursively as

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{t_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{t_{\ell}}F\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes\ell}.

Notice that
\mathcal{S}
is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and
\mathbf{D}
is a closable operator from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
k=1,2,\ldots
, the space
\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
is defined as the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|F\|_{\mathbb{D}^{k,2}}^{2}:=\mathbb{E}\left[|F|^{2}\right]+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\ldots\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{D}_{t_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{t_{m}}F|_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes m}}^{2}\,dt_{1}\ldots dt_{m}\right].

In particular,
\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
is equipped with the inner product

\displaystyle\langle F,G\rangle_{k,2}:=\mathbb{E}\left[FG\right]+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\ldots\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{D}_{t_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{t_{m}}F,\mathbf{D}_{t_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{t_{m}}G\rangle_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes m}}\,dt_{1}\ldots dt_{m}\right].

and hence forms a Hilbert space. We also define
\mathbb{D}^{\infty,2}:=\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
.

The Malliavin derivative satisfies the chain rule. For a random vector
F=(F_{1},\dots,F_{k})
such that
F_{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
for all
i
, and for a function
\varphi\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{k},\mathbb{R})
, the random variable
\varphi(F)
is also Malliavin differentiable, and

\mathbf{D}(\varphi(F))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(F)\mathbf{D}F_{i}.

(2.6)

When the signature components
{\mathbb{X}}_{T}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}
of an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued process
X
belong to
\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
for all
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V
, we denote by
\mathbf{D}_{t_{1}}^{j_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{t_{k}}^{j_{k}}{\mathbb{X}}_{T}
the element-wise application of the Malliavin derivative:

\mathbf{D}_{t_{1}}^{j_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{t_{k}}^{j_{k}}{\mathbb{X}}_{T}:=\left(\mathbf{D}_{t_{1}}^{j_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{t_{k}}^{j_{k}}{\mathbb{X}}_{T}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}\right)_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V}\in T((\mathbb{R}^{m})).

(2.7)

Then the element
\underbrace{\mathbf{D}\cdots\mathbf{D}}_{k\text{ times}}{\mathbb{X}}_{T}
belongs to

L^{2}\!\left(\Omega\times[0,T]^{k},\,(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}\otimes T((\mathbb{R}^{m}))\right).

Main results

Malliavin derivative of signatures of Itô processes.

Consider a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued Itô process of the form

dX_{t}=a_{t}^{0}\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{t}^{k}\circ dW_{t}^{k}=A_{t}\circ d\widehat{W}_{t},

where
a_{t}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,d
, are square-integrable adapted processes satisfying additional Malliavin differentiability and regularity conditions specified in Assumption
4.1
, which together form the columns of the matrix
A_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(d+1)}
, and
\widehat{W}_{t}=(t,W_{t})
. We show that the Malliavin derivative of
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
, the signature of
X
, when exists, is given explicitly by

\mathbf{D}_{s}{\mathbb{X}}_{t}={\mathbb{X}}_{s}\otimes A_{s}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}\;+\;\int_{s}^{t}{\mathbb{X}}_{u}\otimes\left(\mathbf{D}_{s}A_{u}\circ d\widehat{W}_{u}\right)\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{u,t},\quad s\leq t,

(2.8)

see Theorem
4.4
. In particular, when
X_{t}=\widehat{W}_{t}=(t,W_{t})
, this implies

\mathbf{D}_{s_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots\mathbf{D}_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s_{1}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s_{1},s_{2}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{2}}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s_{n},t},\quad 0\leq s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n}\leq t.

(2.9)

The “pierced” formula (
2.9
) shows that iterated Malliavin derivatives of the Brownian signature correspond to inserting the letters

{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}

into the signature at the differentiation times. The formula admits a natural geometric interpretation, as explained in the next remark.

Remark 2.2
.

By definition (
2.5
), the Malliavin derivative

\mathbf{D}^{i}_{s},\,s\geq 0
of a functional
F((W_{u})_{u\in[0,t]})
can be formally viewed as the directional derivative of
F
in the direction of the perturbation
\mathds{1}_{\left\{[s,+\infty)\right\}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
, that is,

\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}F\big((W_{u}+\varepsilon\,\mathds{1}_{\left\{u\geq s\right\}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})_{u\in[0,t]}\big).

Of course, the Malliavin derivative can be rigorously interpreted as a directional derivative only along directions in the Cameron–Martin space, which does not contain indicator functions. Therefore, this remark is purely formal and is intended only as a geometric illustration of the result.
Geometrically, this variation corresponds to inserting a short “vertical” segment of length
\varepsilon
in the direction
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
between the two parts
(W_{u})_{u\in[0,s]}
and
(W_{u})_{u\in[s,t]}
of the Brownian path (see Figure
1

The signature of this vertical segment is
\exp^{\otimes}(\varepsilon\,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}):=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{n}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}^{\otimes n}}{n!}
.
Hence, by Chen’s identity, the signature
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
of the perturbed path satisfies

\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes\exp^{\otimes}(\varepsilon\,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}.

Differentiating at
\varepsilon=0
yields

\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes\exp^{\otimes}(\varepsilon\,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}\big)=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}.

This provides a clear geometric link between the Malliavin derivative and the vertical (or pathwise) functional derivatives of the signature functionals considered by
Cuchiero et al. 2025b

Integration of Malliavin derivatives.

The algebraic structure of the “pierced” signature (
2.9
) allows us to rewrite certain functionals that appear in the Malliavin calculus applied to
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
as linear functionals. Motivated by (
2.9
), we show in Theorem
5.3
that for a general
d
-dimensional path
X
,

\int_{0\leq s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n}\leq t}\left({\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1},s_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{n},t}\right)\circ dX_{s_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots\circ dX_{s_{n}}^{j_{n}}=\mathbf{\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{n}}}}}({\mathbb{X}}_{t}),

(2.10)

where
\mathbf{\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{n}}}}}:T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is an operator acting on the extended tensor algebra by replacing the letters
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}
with
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{n}}}}
, as described in Definition
5.2
.
Its adjoint is shown to be
\mathbf{\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{n}}}}}
, so that

\langle\bm{\ell},\,\mathbf{\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{n}}}}}({\mathbb{X}})\rangle=\left\langle\mathbf{\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{n}}}}}(\bm{\ell}),{\mathbb{X}}\right\rangle,\qquad\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ {\mathbb{X}}\in T((\mathbb{R}^{d})),

as shown in Proposition
5.5
.

This allows us to identify certain Malliavin-calculus operations applied to the Brownian signature
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
with operators on
T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
and to show that the class of random variables

\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}):=\left\{\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle\;\colon\;\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\right\},

(2.11)

defined as linear functionals of the Brownian signature with finitely many non-zero coefficients, is closed under these operators. For example,

\int_{0\leq s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n}\leq t}\mathbf{D}_{s_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots\mathbf{D}_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}}\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\rangle\circ d\widehat{W}_{s_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots\circ d\widehat{W}_{s_{n}}^{j_{n}}=\left\langle\mathbf{\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}\ldots j_{n}}}}}(\bm{\ell}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle.

The class (
2.11
) can be viewed as a class of non-commutative path-dependent polynomials of Brownian motion. It is important for applications due to its approximation properties (see, for instance,
(
Levin et al. 2016
)
or
(
Cuchiero et al. 2023
, Subsection 2.2)
for the continuous semimartingale framework) and its ubiquitous role in stochastic Taylor expansions
(
Kloeden and Platen 1992
;
Ben Arous 1989
;
Abi Jaber et al. 2024
)
.

Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and its generator.

We next consider the generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup, which is a family of operators acting on

L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T}^{W},\mathbb{P})
.
Note that any random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T}^{W},\mathbb{P})

can be represented as a measurable functional of the Brownian motion, that is,

F=F(W)
. For
\bm{\kappa}=(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, the corresponding Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup is then defined by

{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}F(W)=\mathbb{E}[F(B^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}})\mid\mathcal{F}^{W}_{T}],

(2.12)

where

B^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}_{t}:=\left(e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}W_{t}^{i}+\sqrt{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}W_{t}^{\perp,i}\right)_{i=1,\ldots,d},\quad t\in[0,T],\quad\vartheta\geq 0,

and
W^{\perp}
is a standard Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
independent of
W
.
The infinitesimal generator of the semigroup
\left({\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}\right)_{\vartheta\geq 0}
is defined by

{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}=\dfrac{d}{d\vartheta}\bigg|_{\vartheta=0}{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}.

(2.13)

Theorem
5.14
shows that, for
F(W)=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
,
the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and its generator, viewed as linear operators acting on
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T}^{W},\mathbb{P})
,
can be identified with linear operators on the tensor algebra:

\displaystyle{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\right)\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t},

(2.14)

\displaystyle{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}({{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}-{\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}})\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t},

(2.15)

and that
{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}
and
{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}
map
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t})
to itself:

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}\right)(\bm{\ell}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}({{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}-{\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}})(\bm{\ell}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle.

Here, the diagonal operator
{\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}
multiplies each coordinate
\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}
by the number
|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}
of occurrences of
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
in
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}
, and
{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\vartheta}
multiplies the coordinates
\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}
by
e^{-|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}\vartheta}
.

We also discuss the relation

{\bf L}^{i}:={\bf L}^{e_{i}}=-\delta^{i}\mathbf{D}^{i},

between the generator, the Malliavin derivative, and the Skorokhod integral (defined in Section
5.2
).

Integration by parts.

Integration by parts is one of the key applications of Malliavin calculus. Typically, it establishes formulae of the form

\mathbb{E}\left[f^{\prime}(G)F\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f(G)\pi_{T}\right],

(2.16)

which hold for
f\in C^{1}_{b}
and random variables
F,G
satisfying suitable integrability conditions. However, the Malliavin weight
\pi_{T}
is often intractable and cannot be computed explicitly. To address this issue, we introduce an operation

\diamond:T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\times T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\to T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}),

which allows for the linearization of the
L^{2}
scalar product between the Malliavin derivatives of random variables

F=\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
and
F^{\prime}=\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
from
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
:

\left\langle\mathbf{D}F,\mathbf{D}F^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle.

(2.17)

This leads to the integration by parts formula (
2.16
) established in
Theorem
6.8
for the case

G=\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle,\quad F=\frac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle},\quad\bm{\ell}^{G},\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).

(2.18)

Furthermore, we show that the Malliavin weight
\pi_{T}
belongs to the same class as
F
and provide explicit and tractable formula for it, in contrast with the typically implicit weights arising in classical Malliavin calculus.

Finally, we illustrate this result by providing formulas for the sensitivities of path-dependent options within the signature volatility framework of
Abi Jaber and Gérard 2025a
. More precisely, we consider the class of payoffs representable as linear functionals of the log-price signature, expressing the Malliavin weight as a rational function of the Brownian signature.

3
Properties of path signatures

In this subsection, we present some properties of path signatures that will be used throughout this paper.

Signature dynamics.

It follows immediately from the definition (
2.1
) that for each
n\geq 1
, every coordinate of the signature satisfies

{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{X}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n-1}}}}}\circ dX_{s}^{i_{n}}.

In compact tensor form, this yields the signature dynamics

d{\mathbb{X}}_{t}={\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes\circ dX_{t},\qquad{\mathbb{X}}_{0}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}.

(3.1)

As already mentioned in Subsection
2.1
, the inverse element
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}
with respect to the tensor product is the signature of the time-reversed path
\overleftarrow{X}^{[0,t]}\colon u\mapsto X_{t-u}
,
u\in[0,t]
.
The next lemma provides the dynamics of this inverse signature.

Proposition 3.1
.

The inverse signature
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}
is a continuous semimartingale (elementwise) satisfying

d{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}=-\,dX_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1},\qquad{\mathbb{X}}_{0}^{-1}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Differentiating the identity

{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}

and using the product rule yields

d{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}+{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes d{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}={\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes dX_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}+{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes d{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}=0.

Multiplying on the left by
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}
gives (
3.2
).
∎

Variation of constants for signatures.

Since the group increment

{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}={\mathbb{X}}_{s}^{-1}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}

plays the role of the fundamental solution, it can be used to obtain a variation-of-constants formula for
T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
-valued linear SDEs of the form

d{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}={\mathbb{Y}}_{t}\otimes\circ dX_{t}+d\bm{\sigma}_{t}.

(3.3)

We interpret (
3.3
) elementwise: for any truncation order
N
, the truncated equation is a finite-dimensional linear Stratonovich SDE whose coefficients depend only on the first
N
levels of
{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}
.
Thus, if each truncated system admits a unique strong solution, we say that (
3.3
) admits a unique strong solution.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued semimartingale with signature
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
.
Suppose that each component
\bm{\sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}
,
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V
, is a continuous semimartingale.
Then (
3.3
) admits a unique strong solution
{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}
, and this solution admits the following representation:

{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}={\mathbb{Y}}_{s}\,{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}+\int_{s}^{t}\circ d\bm{\sigma}_{u}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{u,t}.

(3.4)

Proof.

For any
{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}
satisfying (
3.3
), observe that

\displaystyle d({\mathbb{Y}}_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1})

\displaystyle=d{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}+{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}\otimes d{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}

\displaystyle=({\mathbb{Y}}_{t}\otimes dX_{t}+d\bm{\sigma}_{t})\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}-{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}\otimes(dX_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1})

\displaystyle=d\bm{\sigma}_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}.

Hence,

{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{-1}={\mathbb{Y}}_{s}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s}^{-1}+\int_{s}^{t}d\bm{\sigma}_{u}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{u}^{-1}.

Multiplying on the right by
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
and using the identity
{\mathbb{X}}_{u}^{-1}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t}={\mathbb{X}}_{u,t}
yields (
3.4
). This proves uniqueness. Existence follows from the fact that the process
{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}
defined by (
3.4
) satisfies (
3.3
), which can be verified by direct differentiation.
∎

Shuffle property.

Another key feature of signatures is that products of linear functionals is itself a linear functional, as a consequence of the shuffle property.
The shuffle product
\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}
is a bilinear mapping
T(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times T(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For words
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}\in V
and letters
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}\in A
, the shuffle product is defined recursively by

{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}=({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}){\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}+({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}){\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},

with the base case
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}
.
Since
\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}
is bilinear, the definition extends to
T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
: for
\bm{\ell},\bm{p}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{p}:=\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}\in V}\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}\bm{p}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}).

Proposition 3.3

(Shuffle property)
.

Let
X
be a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued semimartingale with signature
{\mathbb{X}}
.
Then for all
\bm{\ell},\bm{p}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\left\langle\bm{\ell},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle\,\left\langle\bm{p},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle=\left\langle\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{p},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle.

Proof.

See
(
Lyons et al. 2007
, Theorem 2.15)
.
∎

The shuffle product also linearizes certain entire functions.
For
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the shuffle exponential is defined by

\exp^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}}(\bm{\ell}):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\bm{\ell}^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}n}}{n!}\in T((\mathbb{R}^{d})),

(3.5)

where
\bm{\ell}^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}n}
is the
n
-fold shuffle product.
Using the decomposition

\exp^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}}(\bm{\ell})=e^{\bm{\ell}^{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(\bm{\ell}-\bm{\ell}^{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}})^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}n}}{n!},

(3.6)

with the convention
\bm{p}^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}0}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}
,
we see that each component contains only finitely many terms, and the shuffle exponential is well defined.
Formula (
3.6
) also yields a more efficient computational method.

Expected signature of Brownian motion.

The expected signature

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}],\quad t\in[0,T],

of the time-extended Brownian path
\widehat{W}\colon t\mapsto(t,W_{t}^{1},\ldots,W_{t}^{d})
can be computed explicitly.
By
Fawcett 2003
,

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}=\exp^{\otimes}\left({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}\right),

(3.7)

where
\exp^{\otimes}(\bm{\ell}):=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\dfrac{\bm{\ell}^{\otimes n}}{n!}
for
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
.

4
Malliavin derivative of an Itô process signature

Let
a^{0},a^{1},\ldots,a^{d}
be adapted
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued adapted square-integrable processes. Let us define the
m
-dimensional Itô process
X
by

dX_{t}=a_{t}^{0}\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{t}^{k}\circ dW_{t}^{k}=A_{t}\circ d\widehat{W}_{t},

(4.1)

where
(a^{j}_{t})_{j=0,\ldots,d}
form the columns of the matrix
A_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(d+1)}
and
\widehat{W}_{t}=(t,W_{t})
.
We first aim to establish the following formula for the Malliavin derivative of
X
: for
\mathbb{P}\otimes ds\otimes dt\text{-almost every }(\omega,s,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]^{2},

\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}X_{t}=a_{s}^{i}\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(s)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}a_{u}^{0}\,du+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}a_{u}^{k}\circ dW_{u}^{k},

(4.2)

similar to the corresponding formula in the Itô integral case
(
Alós et al. 2021
, Section 3.2.1)
. Treating Stratonovich integrals requires stronger regularity assumptions on the integrands
a^{k}
than in the Itô case.

Assumption 4.1
.

We assume that

1.

a^{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{1,2}
, that is,
a^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
such that
a^{0}_{t}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, and

\|a^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1,2}}^{2}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|a_{t}^{0}|^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{D}_{s}a^{0}_{t}|^{2}\,ds\,dt\right]<\infty.

2.

For
k=1,\ldots,d
,
a^{k}\in\mathbb{L}^{2,2}
, that is,
a^{k}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
,
a_{t}^{k}\in\mathbb{D}^{2,2}

5
Malliavin calculus for

We now focus on the signature of the time-augmented Brownian motion

5.1
Iterated integrals of Malliavin derivatives of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}

We have already seen that the higher-order Malliavin derivative of the Brownian signature reduces to the “insertion” of letters in Chen’s identity, see (
4.10
). Our goal is to show that this operation can be, in some sense, inverted by integrating
\mathbf{D}_{s_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots\mathbf{D}_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
over the simplex
0\leq s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n}\leq t
with respect to the components of
d\widehat{W}
.

In particular, this will allow us to show in Theorem
5.6
that if
F=\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t})
, recall (
2.11
), then

\int_{0\leq s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n}\leq t}\mathbf{D}_{s_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots\mathbf{D}_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}}\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle\circ d\widehat{W}_{s_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots\circ d\widehat{W}_{s_{n}}^{j_{n}}\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}),

and its coefficients can be expressed explicitly in terms of
\bm{\ell}
. This property will be crucial for deriving closed-form formulae for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and integration by parts.

We start with the simplest case
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}
, which can be written as

\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}=\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}\in V}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}).

(5.1)

The behavior of the products
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}}
under integration with respect to
s
is described in the following lemma, which holds for general path signatures.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be an

-valued continuous semimartingale. Then, for all
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}\in V
and

\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{X}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}}{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}}\circ dX_{s}^{j}={\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}jv_{2}}}}},\quad t\in[0,T].

(5.2)

Consequently, for
\bm{\ell}_{1},\bm{\ell}_{2}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(5.3)

Proof.

For
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}\in V
, set

We proceed by induction on
|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}|
to show that

(5.4)

If
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{\varnothing}}}
, (
5.4
) holds by definition of the signature. Otherwise, suppose that

for some

, and that (

) holds for

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}jv_{2}}}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{X}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}}{\mathbb{X}}_{s,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}}\circ dX_{s}^{j}=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{X}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}{\mathbb{X}}_{s,u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}}\circ dX_{u}^{k}\right)\circ dX_{s}^{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{u}{\mathbb{X}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}}{\mathbb{X}}_{s,u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}}\circ dX_{s}^{j}\right)\circ dX_{u}^{k}=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{Y}}_{u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}jv_{2}^{\prime}}}}}\circ dX_{u}^{k}

Integrating (

Definition 5.2
.

Fix

by

{\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}\colon\mathbbm{x}\mapsto\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{n}}}}}\mathbbm{x}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}w_{1}v_{1}\ldots w_{n}v_{n}}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}\ldots{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{n}}}}).

(5.5)

Note that
{\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}
can be equivalently defined by its action on the basis vectors:

{\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{n}}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}w_{1}v_{1}\ldots w_{n}v_{n}}}}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\end{subarray}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}u_{1}v_{1}\ldots u_{n}v_{n}}}},\quad{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V.

(5.6)

Hence, the switching operator
{\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}
admits a natural interpretation: it finds all possible sequences of consecutive and non-intersecting sub-strings

{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}
in the word
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}
, and replaces them with
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}
.
The result is the sum of all such modified words. In particular,
{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}
replaces each occurrence of the letter
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}
by the letter
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
, one at a time.
For example,

{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{1}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{01101}}})={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{00101}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{01001}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{01100}}}.

The switching operator allows us to rewrite compactly the iterated integrals we are interested in.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued continuous semimartingale and let
{\mathbb{X}}
denote its signature. Then,

\int_{0\leq s_{1}\leq\ldots\leq s_{n}\leq t}\Big({\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1},s_{2}}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{n},t}\Big)\circ dX_{s_{1}}^{j_{1}}\ldots\circ dX_{s_{n}}^{j_{n}}={\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{n}}}}}({\mathbb{X}}_{t}).

(5.7)

Proof.

It follows directly from the definition of the tensor product that

{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1},s_{2}}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{n},t}=\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{n}}}}\in V}{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}}}}}{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1},s_{2}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}}\ldots{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{n},t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{n}}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}i_{1}v_{1}\ldots i_{n}v_{n}}}}).

Integrating and applying Lemma
5.1

n
times concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Theorem
5.3
still applies when the order of the iterated integral differs from the number of “holes” in the “pierced” signature. More precisely, if the integral is taken only over a subset of consecutive variables
s_{k_{0}+1}\leq\ldots\leq s_{k_{0}+m}
for some
0\leq k_{0}\leq\ldots\leq k_{0}+m\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{s_{k_{0}}\leq s_{k_{0}+1}\leq\ldots\leq s_{k_{0}+m}\leq s_{k_{0}+m+1}}\Big({\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1},s_{2}}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{n},t}\Big)\circ dX_{s_{k_{0}+1}}^{j_{k_{0}+1}}\ldots\circ dX_{s_{k_{0}+m}}^{j_{k_{0}+m}}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{X}}_{s_{1}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{k_{0}}}}}\otimes{\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{k_{0}+1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{k_{0}+m}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{k_{0}+1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{k_{0}+m}}}}}({\mathbb{X}}_{s_{k_{0}},s_{k_{0}+m+1}})\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{k_{0}+m+1}}}}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{s_{n},t},

where we set
s_{0}=0
and
s_{n+1}=t
.

\blacksquare

We will also be interested in computing the linear functionals of objects of the form (
5.7
) and to write them as linear functionals of
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
. In other words, we would like to compute the adjoint of
{\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}}}}}
. The following proposition answers this question.

Proposition 5.5
.

The adjoint operator
({\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}})^{*}
is given by

({\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}})^{*}={\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}.

(5.8)

That is, for
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\langle\bm{\ell},\,{\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}({\mathbb{X}}_{t})\rangle=\left\langle{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}(\bm{\ell}),{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle.

Proof.

The proof follows from observing that

\langle\bm{\ell},\,{\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}({\mathbb{X}}_{t})\rangle=\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{n}}}}}\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}u_{1}v_{1}\ldots u_{n}v_{n}}}}}{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{0}w_{1}v_{1}\ldots w_{n}v_{n}}}}}=\left\langle{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{n}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{n}}}}}(\bm{\ell}),{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle.

∎

Theorem
5.3
and Proposition
5.5
imply immediately the following result.

Theorem 5.6
.

For all
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and
j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
, we have

\int_{0\leq s_{1}\leq\ldots\leq s_{n}\leq t}(\mathbf{D}_{s_{1}}^{i_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t})\circ d\widehat{W}_{s_{1}}^{j_{1}}\ldots\circ d\widehat{W}_{s_{n}}^{j_{n}}={\bf\Psi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}}}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}}}}}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}).

Moreover, for all
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,

\int_{0\leq s_{1}\leq\ldots\leq s_{n}\leq t}\mathbf{D}_{s_{1}}^{i_{1}}\ldots\mathbf{D}_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}}\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\rangle\circ d\widehat{W}_{s_{1}}^{j_{1}}\ldots\circ d\widehat{W}_{s_{n}}^{j_{n}}=\left\langle{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j_{1}\ldots j_{n}}}}}(\bm{\ell}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle.

5.2
Skorokhod integration and the representation of random variables in
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})

We now illustrate how the derived results can be applied to obtain explicit representations of the (anticipative) Skorokhod integral, the Clark–Ocone formula, and the chaos expansion for random variables
F\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
.

Skorokhod integration.

The Skorokhod integral
\delta
is defined by the dual relationship

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{D}_{t}F,h_{t}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,dt\right]=\mathbb{E}[F\,\delta(h)],

(5.9)

for
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
h\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
, where

\mathrm{Dom}(\delta):=\left\{h\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon\ \left|\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{D}_{t}F,h_{t}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,dt\right]\right|\leqslant C\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\ \forall F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}\right\}.

Moreover, for
h=(h^{1},\ldots,h^{d})\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
we may write

\displaystyle\delta(h)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\delta^{j}(h^{j}),

where, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and all
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{D}^{j}_{t}F\,h^{j}_{t}\,dt\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[F\,\delta^{j}(h^{j})\right].

If
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
h\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
are such that
Fh\in L_{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
Fh\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
and the following equality
(
Nualart 2006
, Proposition 1.3.3)
holds:

\displaystyle\delta(Fh)=F\,\delta(h)-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{D}_{s}F,h_{s}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,ds,

(5.10)

provided the right-hand side is square integrable. This equality plays an important role, in particular, for proving the integration by parts results discussed in Section
6
.

We show that the Skorokhod integral of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}
, and hence of any random variable in
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
, can be expressed using the switching operator
{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}
.

Proposition 5.7
.

For
i=1,\ldots,d
, we have

\delta^{i}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})=W_{T}^{i}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}-{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}).

(5.11)

Moreover, if
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, then

\delta^{i}\left(\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)=\left\langle\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}-{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle.

(5.12)

Proof.

Applying the integration-by-parts formula (
5.10
) element-wise with
F=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}
and
h_{t}\equiv 1
yields

\displaystyle\delta^{i}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})

\displaystyle=W_{T}^{i}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}-\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{D}_{t}^{i}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\,dt

\displaystyle=W_{T}^{i}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}-{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}),

where we used Theorem
5.6
to compute the integral.
Equation (
5.12
) then follows from Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
5.5
.
∎

Clark–Ocone formula.

The classical Clark–Ocone formula refines the martingale representation theorem. It states that any
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
admits the representation

F=\mathbb{E}[F]+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{t}^{i}F\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W}]\,dW_{t}^{i}.

(5.13)

If the random variable
F
lies in
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
, the integrand in (
5.13
) can be computed explicitly.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
F=\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
with
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Then,
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and

F=\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{T}\right\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{T-t}\right\rangle\,dW_{t}^{i},

(5.14)

where
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
denotes the expected signature given by (
3.7
).

Proof.

Equation (
4.9
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_{t}^{i}F\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t,T}\right\rangle\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W}\right]=\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{T-t}\right\rangle,

(5.15)

since
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W}
-measurable and
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t,T}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W}
, with
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t,T}\overset{d}{=}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T-t}
.
∎

Chaos expansion.

The chaos expansion
(
Nualart 2006
, Theorem 1.1.2)
expresses any square-integrable random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}^{W},\mathbb{P})
as an infinite series of iterated Wiener–Itô integrals:

F=\mathbb{E}[F]+\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}\in V_{n}}\ \,\idotsint\limits_{0<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{n}<t}f_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\,dW_{s_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots dW_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}}.

Moreover, when
F
is infinitely Malliavin differentiable (i.e.
F\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty,2}
), the kernel functions
f_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}
can be computed
(
Nualart 2006
, Exercise 1.2.6)
via

f_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}):=\frac{1}{n!}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{D}_{s_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots\mathbf{D}_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}}F\right],\qquad{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}\in V\setminus\{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}\}.

It follows from Corollary
4.7
that
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})\subset\mathbb{D}^{\infty,2}
.
The following proposition shows that for
F\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
, the kernel functions can be expressed explicitly.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
F=\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
with
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
F\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty,2}
and the kernel functions
f_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}
in the chaos expansion are given by

f_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\left\langle\bm{\ell},\,\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}-s_{1}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{2}}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{n}-s_{n-1}}\right\rangle.

(5.16)

In particular, recalling that
|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}
denotes the number of occurrences of the letter
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
in
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V
, we have

f_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\equiv 0,\quad n>N=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}\colon\ {\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V,\ \bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}\neq 0\right\},

so that
F
has a finite chaos expansion of order
N
.

Proof.

The result follows from (
4.10
) and the independence of

\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s_{1}},\ \widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s_{1},s_{2}},\ \ldots,\ \widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s_{n-1},s_{n}}
.
∎

5.3
The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and its generator

In this section we establish explicit formulas for the action of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and its generator on the Brownian signature.
The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup
{\bf T}_{\vartheta}
(see, for instance,
Nualart 2006
) and its generator are usually introduced using the splitting of the Brownian motion

B^{\vartheta}:=e^{-\vartheta}W+\sqrt{1-e^{-2\vartheta}}W^{\perp},\quad\vartheta\geq 0,

where
W^{\perp}
is an independent copy of
W
. The semigroup is then defined by

{\bf T}_{\vartheta}F(W)=\mathbb{E}[F(B^{\vartheta})\mid\mathcal{F}^{W}_{T}],\quad\vartheta\geq 0,

and its generator is given by

{\bf L}=\dfrac{d}{d\vartheta}\bigg|_{\vartheta=0}{\bf T}_{\vartheta}=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\delta^{i}\mathbf{D}^{i},

(5.17)

where the last identity follows from
(
Nualart 2006
, Proposition 1.4.3)
.

However, for later applications to integration-by-parts formulae, it may be convenient to split to all components of
W
, but only some of them. This leads to the following generalization of
{\bf T}_{\vartheta}
.

Definition 5.10
.

Fix
\bm{\kappa}=(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
and set

B^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}_{t}:=\left(e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}W_{t}^{i}+\sqrt{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}W_{t}^{\perp,i}\right)_{i=1,\ldots,d},\quad t\in[0,T],\quad\vartheta\geq 0.

The
\bm{\kappa}
-Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and its generator are defined by

{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}F(W)=\mathbb{E}[F(B^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}})\mid\mathcal{F}^{W}_{T}],\qquad{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}=\dfrac{d}{d\vartheta}\bigg|_{\vartheta=0}{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}.

(5.18)

In particular, when
\bm{\kappa}=(1,\ldots,1)
, the definition coincides with the standard Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup (
5.18
). When
\bm{\kappa}=e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
for some
i=1,\ldots,d
, this corresponds to splitting only the
i
-th component of
W
. We will denote the corresponding semigroup and generator by
{\bf T}^{i}_{\vartheta}
and
{\bf L}^{i}
.
Note that the operators
\mathbf{T}_{\vartheta}^{i}
and
\mathbf{L}^{i}
,
can be viewed as the standard Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup and generator associated with the
one-dimensional Brownian motion
W^{i}
, conditional on the remaining components

W^{1},\ldots,W^{i-1},W^{i+1},\ldots,W^{d}
. In particular, this implies that

\mathbf{L}^{i}=-\delta^{i}\mathbf{D}^{i},

which leads to the linear decomposition of the generator:

\mathbf{L}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbf{L}^{i}.

Remark 5.11
.

Note, however, that the splitting of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup itself is not linear:

\mathbf{T}_{\vartheta}\neq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbf{T}_{\vartheta}^{i}.

For instance, one can easily verify that, for
F=W_{T}^{1}W_{T}^{2}
,

\mathbf{T}_{\vartheta}\bigl(W_{T}^{1}W_{T}^{2}\bigr)=e^{-2\vartheta}W_{T}^{1}W_{T}^{2}\neq 2e^{-\vartheta}W_{T}^{1}W_{T}^{2}=(\mathbf{T}_{\vartheta}^{1}+\mathbf{T}_{\vartheta}^{2})\bigl(W_{T}^{1}W_{T}^{2}\bigr).

\blacksquare

Our goal is to compute
{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}F(W)
and
{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}F(W)
in the special case
F(W)=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
. We recall that in this case
{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
and
{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
are understood as elements of
T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, with the operators applied component-wise:

{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\left({\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}\right)_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V},\qquad{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\left({\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}\right)_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V}.

Remark 5.12
.

The domain of
\mathbf{L}
coincides with the space
\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
of random variables that are twice Malliavin differentiable
(
Nualart 2006
, Section 1.4.2)
.
Therefore,
\mathbf{L}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
is well-defined, since

\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty,2}
by Corollary
4.7
. We will show that

{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}\delta^{i}\mathbf{D}^{i},

so that
\mathrm{Dom}({\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}})=\mathrm{Dom}({\bf L})
and
{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}

\blacksquare

Definition 5.13
.

Fix
\vartheta\geq 0
and a letter
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\in A
. We define the operators

{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\vartheta}\colon T((\mathbb{R}^{d+1}))\to T((\mathbb{R}^{d+1}))

and

{\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}\colon T((\mathbb{R}^{d+1}))\to T((\mathbb{R}^{d+1}))

by

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\vartheta}\colon\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n}\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\mapsto\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n}e^{-|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}\,\vartheta}\,\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}},

\displaystyle{\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}\colon\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n}\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\mapsto\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|=n}|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}\,\bm{\ell}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}},

where
|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}
denotes the number of occurrences of the letter
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
in the word
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}
.

We note that both operators are diagonal, and they satisfy

{\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}=-\dfrac{d}{d\vartheta}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\vartheta}\big|_{\vartheta=0}.

Theorem 5.14
.

The
\bm{\kappa}
-Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup operator of the Brownian signature is given by

{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}\,{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\right)\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}.

(5.19)

Moreover, we have
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\in\mathrm{Dom}({\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}})
and

{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}\bigl({{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}-{\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}\bigr)\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}.

(5.20)

Their adjoint operators are

({\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}})^{*}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\right)\exp\!\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}\,{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}\right),\qquad({\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}})^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}({{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}-{\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}).

(5.21)

Proof.

Step 1. Semigroup
{\bf T}_{\vartheta}^{\bm{\kappa}}
.

We denote the time-augmented signature of
B^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}
by
\widehat{\mathbb{B}}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}
and write the dynamics equation (
3.1
) for it:

d\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}=(\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}})\,dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\circ(e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\,dW_{t}^{i}+\sqrt{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}\,dW_{t}^{\perp,i}).

(5.22)

Writing the dynamics of
(\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})
in a similar way, one can easily verify that

d\bigl\langle\widehat{\mathbb{B}}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},\,\sqrt{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}W^{\perp,i}\bigr\rangle_{t}=({1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}})(\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}})\,dt,\quad i=1,\ldots,d.

Using the relationship between Itô and Stratonovich integrals for

(\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\circ\sqrt{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}\,dW_{t}^{\perp,i}
in (
5.22
)
and rearranging the terms, we obtain

d\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}=\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes\!\left({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}}{2}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}\right)dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\circ e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\,dW_{t}^{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\,\sqrt{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}\,dW_{t}^{\perp,i}.

Taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,|\mathcal{F}^{W}_{T}]
yields a linear SDE for

{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathbb{B}}_{t}^{\vartheta,\bm{\kappa}}\,|\mathcal{F}^{W}_{T}]:

d{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}={\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\otimes\!\left({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}\right)dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\otimes(e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\circ dW_{t}^{i}.

Applying Picard iteration, one obtains a solution linear in
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
:

{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}=\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}}\bigl(\bm{u}^{\vartheta,{\bm{\kappa}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}}}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\bm{u}^{\vartheta,{\bm{\kappa}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{n}}}})\bigr)\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}}},

where

\bm{u}^{\vartheta,{\bm{\kappa}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}})={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}},\qquad\bm{u}^{\vartheta,{\bm{\kappa}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})=e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},\qquad i=1,\ldots,d.

(5.23)

Hence, we can define an operator
{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}\colon T((\mathbb{R}^{d+1}))\to T((\mathbb{R}^{d+1}))
by

{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}}})=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\bm{u}^{\vartheta,{\bm{\kappa}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i_{j}}}}),

so that
{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t})
coincides with the action of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup.

Since for all
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}\in\{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{1}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{d}}}\}
,

{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}})=\mathds{1}_{\left\{j=0\right\}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}},\qquad{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}})=\begin{cases}e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}},&j=i,\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}},&j\neq i,\end{cases}

and

\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}})=\begin{cases}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}},&j=0,\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}},&j\neq 0,\end{cases}

equation (
5.23
) reads

\bm{u}^{\vartheta,{\bm{\kappa}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}})=\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\right)({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}})={\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}),\qquad{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}\in\{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{1}}},\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{d}}}\}.

Moreover, each
{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}
satisfies the Leibniz rule,

{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u}}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}})={{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u}}})\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u}}}\otimes{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}),

and the same holds for
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}
, so that
its exponential is a tensor algebra homomorphism:

\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u}}}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}})=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u}}})\otimes\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}).

Moreover, for all
i=1,\ldots,d
,
{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}
is a homomorphism as well.
Thus, the identity

{\bf T}^{\bm{\kappa}}_{\vartheta}=\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\right)

is proved, yielding (
5.19
).

Step 2. Generator
{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}
.

By definition,

{\bf L}^{\bm{\kappa}}=\left[\frac{d}{d\vartheta}\left(\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)\right)\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)\left(\frac{d}{d\vartheta}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}}^{\kappa_{j}\vartheta}\right)\prod_{k\neq j}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}^{\kappa_{k}\vartheta}\right]_{\vartheta=0}.

A direct computation gives

\frac{d}{d\vartheta}\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)\Bigg|_{\vartheta=0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}},\qquad{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\kappa_{i}\vartheta}\big|_{\vartheta=0}=\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa_{i}\vartheta}}{2}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}\right)\Bigg|_{\vartheta=0}=\mathrm{Id}.

Recall that
\frac{d}{d\vartheta}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}}^{\kappa_{j}\vartheta}\Big|_{\vartheta=0}=-\kappa_{j}{\bf\Lambda_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}}}

so (
5.20
) follows.

Finally, the adjoint formulas (
5.21
) follow from Proposition
5.5

Remark 5.16
.

The semigroup
{\mathbf{T}}_{\vartheta}
can equivalently be defined in terms of chaos expansions; see
(
Nualart 2006
, Definition 1.4.1)
. Namely, if
F\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}^{W},\mathbb{P})
, then

F=\sum_{n\geq 0}J_{n}F,

where
J_{n}F
denotes the projection of
F
onto the
n
-th Wiener chaos. The semigroup then acts as

{\mathbf{T}}_{\vartheta}F=\sum_{n\geq 0}e^{-n\vartheta}J_{n}F.

Using the recursive definition of the Wiener chaos and the Itô signature, a simple induction argument based on Fubini’s theorem shows that
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{\mathrm{Ito},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}
belongs to the Wiener chaos of order
\sum_{i=1}^{d}|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|_{i}
.

{\mathbf{T}}_{\vartheta}\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{\mathrm{Ito}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\bf J}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}^{\vartheta}\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{\mathrm{Ito}}\right\rangle.

Comparing this with (
5.25
), we observe that the operator

\exp\!\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\vartheta}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}\right)

is related to the conversion of the Stratonovich signature into the Itô signature. Indeed, by writing the dynamics of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot}
in terms of Itô integrals and proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
5.14
, one can show that

\left\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{\mathrm{Ito}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}\right)\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle,\qquad

which provides a concise operator representation of the Itô–Stratonovich relationship for signatures similar to
Ben Arous 1989
.

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V
and consider the
T_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
-valued process

{\mathbb{X}}_{s}=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t},\qquad s\leq t.

Then
{\mathbb{X}}_{\cdot}\in\mathbb{L}_{2}^{1,2}
and, for
s\leq t
,

(\mathbf{D}^{+,i}{\mathbb{X}}_{\cdot})_{s}=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{iv}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t},\qquad(\mathbf{D}^{-,i}{\mathbb{X}}_{\cdot})_{s}=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{vi}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}.

Proof.

For
0<s<u<t
, we have

\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}\big)=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}.

We claim that for all
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}\in V
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}\big)^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}}-(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{iv}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t})^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}}\right)^{2}\right]\to 0,

as
|u-s|\to 0
. Indeed, we have

\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}\big)-\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{iv}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t}=\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}-{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}\big)\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t},

so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\mathbf{D}_{s}^{i}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}\big)^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}}-(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{iv}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,t})^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}}\right)^{2}\right]

(5.30)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime\prime}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{3}}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}iw_{2}^{\prime}vw_{3}=w_{1}ivw_{2}^{\prime\prime}w_{3}=w}}}\end{subarray}}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}}}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime}}}}}-\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime\prime}}}}}\big)\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{3}}}}}\right)^{2}\right]

(5.31)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime\prime}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{3}}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}}|,|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{3}}}}|<|w|}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0<|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime}}}}|,|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime\prime}}}}|<|w|}}}\end{subarray}}\big|\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}}}\big|\big|\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime}}}}}-\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}^{\prime\prime}}}}}\big|\big|\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{3}}}}}\big|\right)^{2}\right]

(5.32)

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}}|<|w|}}}}\big|\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}}}\big|\right)^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{3}}}}|<|w|}}}}\big|\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{3}}}}}\big|\right)^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0<|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}}}}|<|w|}}}}\big|\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}}}}}\big|\right)^{2}\right]

(5.33)

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}}|<|w|}}}}\big|\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{1}}}}}\big|\right)^{2}\right]^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0<|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}}}}|<|w|}}}}\big|\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s,u}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w_{2}}}}}\big|\right)^{2}\right],

(5.34)

where, in the last inequality, we used the diffusive scaling of the signature.
The last expression depends only on
|u-s|
and converges to
0
as
|s-u|\to 0
. Hence, the limit in (
4.4
) vanishes. The proof for
0<u<s<t
and
\mathbf{D}^{-,i}
is analogous.
∎

Applying Lemma
5.18
to
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot,t}
yields

\left(\mathbf{D}^{+,i}\Big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot,t}\Big)\right)_{u}+\left(\mathbf{D}^{-,i}\Big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot,t}\Big)\right)_{u}=2\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}\big).

The Skorokhod integral (
5.28
) can therefore be expressed in terms of the Stratonovich integral using (

):

\displaystyle\delta^{i}\Big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{\cdot,t}\Big)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}\big)\circ dW_{u}^{i}-\int_{0}^{t}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{u,t}\big)\,du

(5.35)

\displaystyle={\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t})-{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t})

\displaystyle={\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t})-{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}),

(5.37)

where we used Theorem
5.6
and observed that

{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}={\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}
.
This recovers the formula obtained in Corollary

.

6
Integration by parts

For conciseness of notation, we will denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
the scalar product in the space
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
. That is, for
h=(h^{1},\ldots,h^{d})
and
g=(g^{1},\ldots,g^{d})
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we set

\langle h,g\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int_{0}^{T}\langle h_{t},g_{t}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,dt.

The following proposition (a multidimensional version of
Nualart 2006
) allows for computation of Malliavin weights in the integration-by-parts formula (
2.16
).

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
G
and
F
be random variables such that
G\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
h\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}\not=0
a.s. and
\dfrac{Fh}{\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}}\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
. Then, for any
f\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
we have

\mathbb{E}[f^{\prime}(G)F]=\mathbb{E}\left[f(G)\delta\left(\dfrac{Fh}{\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}}\right)\right].

(6.1)

Moreover, if
\dfrac{F}{\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
h\in\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)
, then

\mathbb{E}\left[f^{\prime}(G)F\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f(G)\left(\dfrac{F\delta(h)}{\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}}-\dfrac{\langle\mathbf{D}F,h\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}}+\dfrac{F\left\langle\mathbf{D}\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}},h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}^{2}}\right)\right].

(6.2)

Proof.

By the chain rule,
\left\langle\mathbf{D}f(G),h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=f^{\prime}(G)\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}
, so that

f^{\prime}(G)=\dfrac{\left\langle\mathbf{D}f(G),h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}},

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f^{\prime}(G)F]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\left\langle\mathbf{D}f(G),h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}F\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f(G)\delta\left(\dfrac{Fh}{\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}\right)\right],

(6.3)

where we used the definition of the Skorokhod integral (
5.9
). The formula (
5.10
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f^{\prime}(G)F]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[f(G)\left(\dfrac{F\delta(h)}{\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}-\left\langle\mathbf{D}\left(\dfrac{F}{\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}\right),h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right)\right],

(6.4)

and (
6.2
) follows from a direct computation of the Malliavin derivative inside the integral.
∎

6.1
Linearization of the
L^{2}
scalar product

Typically, computing
\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}
and
\left\langle\mathbf{D}\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}},h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}
, which appear in (
6.2
), is non-trivial, and their numerical evaluation may present a significant challenge. However, this computation is significantly simplified if the process
h
is chosen in such a way that it allows one to linearize
\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}
, i.e., to express it as a linear functional of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}
. Assuming additionally that both
G
and
F
are linear or rational (i.e., ratios of two linear functionals) functions of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}
makes it possible to rewrite the weight given by (
6.2
) as a rational function of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}
as well.

We start by considering two random variables
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
with corresponding coefficients
\bm{\ell},\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, and we introduce an operation
\diamond\colon T(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times T(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that allows us to linearize the scalar product
\left\langle\mathbf{D}F,\mathbf{D}F^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}
, i.e., to express it as

\left\langle\mathbf{D}F,\mathbf{D}F^{\prime}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\mathbf{D}\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\rangle,\mathbf{D}\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\rangle\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle.

(6.5)

Definition 6.2
.

Fix three words
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{2}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}\in V
. For two words
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v^{\prime}}}}
, define
\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{2}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}}:T(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times T(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{2}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v^{\prime}}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}:\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}u_{1}v_{2}}}}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}:\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}u_{2}v_{2}^{\prime}}}}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v^{\prime}}}}\end{subarray}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}}}})\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{w}}}\otimes({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}).

(6.6)

For two tensor sequences
\bm{\ell},\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the operator is extended by bilinearity. We also define
\diamond:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}
.

The proposition below clarifies the link between this algebraic operation and the integration of the product of linear functionals of two “pierced” signatures
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
X
be an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued continuous semimartingale. Then, for all words
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{2}}}}\in V
, letter
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}\in A
, and
\bm{\ell},\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\bm{\ell},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle\,\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{2}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle\circ dX_{t}^{k}=\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{2}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}\bm{\ell}^{\prime},{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle.

(6.7)

Proof.

Since the integral in (
6.7
) is a bilinear functional of
\bm{\ell}
and
\bm{\ell}^{\prime}
, it suffices to consider the case
\bm{\ell}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\in V
and
\bm{\ell}^{\prime}={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v^{\prime}}}}\in V
. Then the integrand expands as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle\,\langle{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v^{\prime}}}},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{2}}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle\circ dX_{t}^{k}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}:\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}u_{1}v_{2}=v}}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}:\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}u_{2}v_{2}^{\prime}=v^{\prime}}}}\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}}{\mathbb{X}}_{t}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}}}}}{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}}{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}}\circ dX_{t}^{k}

(6.9)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}:\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}u_{1}v_{2}=v}}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}:\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}u_{2}v_{2}^{\prime}=v^{\prime}}}}\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}}}},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle\,\left\langle{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}},{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\right\rangle\circ dX_{t}^{k}

(6.10)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}:\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}u_{1}v_{2}=v}}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}:\\
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}u_{2}v_{2}^{\prime}=v^{\prime}}}}\end{subarray}}\left\langle({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{1}^{\prime}}}}){\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v_{2}^{\prime}}}}),{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle

(6.11)

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{1}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{u_{2}}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v^{\prime}}}},{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle,

(6.12)

where Lemma
5.1
is used in the third equality. This proves (
6.7
).
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
with the coefficients
\bm{\ell},\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Then

\langle\mathbf{D}F,\mathbf{D}F^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle.

(6.13)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{D}F,\mathbf{D}F^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\bm{\ell},\mathbf{D}^{i}_{t}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\rangle\,\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\mathbf{D}^{i}_{t}\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\bm{\ell},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t,T}\rangle\,\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t,T}\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle=\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle,

where we used Proposition
6.3
and the definition of
\diamond
.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Fix
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}\in A
and
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Then
\bm{\ell}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}={\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell})
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to observe that for all continuous semimartingales
X
, we have

\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}},{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\bm{\ell},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle\,\underbrace{\langle{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}},{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle}_{=1}\circ dX_{t}^{k}=\left\langle{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell}),{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle,

where the last equality follows from Theorem
5.3
.
∎

Although it enjoys the desired linearization property (
6.13
), the direct computation of
\diamond
via its definition (
6.6
) is rather cumbersome and numerically intricate. The following proposition shows that
\diamond
can be expressed in terms of the switching operators
{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}
, enabling a significantly more efficient numerical implementation.

Proposition 6.6
.

If
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}\in A
and
\bm{\ell},\bm{\ell}^{\prime}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, then the diamond product
\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}
can be written in carré du champ form:

\bm{\ell}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}\bm{\ell}^{\prime}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left({\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime})-{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell})\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime}-\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{\prime})\right).

(6.14)

In particular, we have

\bm{\ell}\diamond\bm{\ell}^{\prime}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left({\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime})-{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell})\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime}-\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{\prime})\right).

(6.15)

Proof.

For
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon>0
, take an arbitrary continuous semimartingale
X
and define

h_{t}(\varepsilon):=\langle\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes\exp^{\otimes}(\varepsilon{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle.

Since
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes\exp^{\otimes}(\varepsilon{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}
is group-like (i.e., corresponds to the signature of a path as a product of signatures), it satisfies the shuffle property:

h_{t}(\varepsilon)=\langle\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes\exp^{\otimes}(\varepsilon{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle=\langle\bm{\ell},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes\exp^{\otimes}(\varepsilon{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes\exp^{\otimes}(\varepsilon{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle.

Differentiating both sides twice with respect to
\varepsilon
at
\varepsilon=0
gives

\displaystyle\dfrac{d^{2}}{d\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}h_{t}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{\ell},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\rangle+\langle\bm{\ell},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\rangle\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle\bm{\ell},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\otimes{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}\otimes{\mathbb{X}}_{t,T}\rangle.

Integrating this identity with respect to
\circ dX_{t}^{k}
over
[0,T]
and applying Theorem
5.3
, Proposition
5.5
, and (
6.7
) yields

\left\langle{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime}),{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell}),{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{\prime},{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle+\left\langle\bm{\ell},{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{\prime}),{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}\bm{\ell}^{\prime},{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle,

so that

\left\langle\bm{\ell}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}\bm{\ell}^{\prime},{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime})-{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell})\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{\prime}-\bm{\ell}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{k}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{\prime}),{\mathbb{X}}_{T}\right\rangle

Finally, noting that this equality remains true for all possible
X
, we conclude that
(
6.14
) holds.
∎

6.2
Integration by parts formula

The linearization property (
6.13
), proved in the previous subsection, suggests that taking
h=\mathbf{D}H
for some
H\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
is a natural choice for the function
h
in Proposition
6.1
. However, one can obtain a much larger class of admissible functions
h
by taking

h=\left(\mathbf{D}^{i}\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{h}_{i},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)_{i=1,\ldots,d},\qquad\bm{\ell}^{h}_{i}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}),\quad i=1,\ldots,d,

(6.16)

so that we associate with each function
h
a collection of coefficients

\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}=(\bm{\ell}_{1}^{h},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{d}^{h})\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})^{d}.

A natural extension of the “diamond” product
\diamond
to vectors of coefficients is

\vec{\bm{\ell}}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}^{\prime}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{\ell}_{i}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}\bm{\ell}^{\prime}_{i},\qquad\vec{\bm{\ell}},\vec{\bm{\ell}^{\prime}}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})^{d}.

Hence, for
G=\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
and
h
of the form (
6.16
), we obtain

\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle,

(6.17)

where we identify
\bm{\ell}^{G}
with the vector
\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{G}=(\bm{\ell}^{G},\ldots,\bm{\ell}^{G})
.

Before stating the main theorem of this section, we introduce the class
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
of random variables that can be written as a ratio of two linear functionals of the Brownian signature.

Definition 6.7
.

We say that a random variable
F
belongs to
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
if there exist
\bm{\ell}_{1},\bm{\ell}_{2}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\left\langle\bm{\ell}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\neq 0
a.s. and

F=\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}\quad\text{a.s.}

Note that
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
forms an algebra.

Theorem 6.8
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
6.1
, suppose that

1.

G\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
with the coefficient
\bm{\ell}^{G}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.

F\in\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
, i.e.,
F=\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}
for some
\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

3.

h
is a process of the form (
6.16
).

4.

The coefficients
\bm{\ell}^{G},\bm{\ell}^{F_{1}},\bm{\ell}^{F_{2}},\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}
are such that
\dfrac{F}{\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}}=\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
.

Then,

\mathbb{E}\left[f^{\prime}(G)F\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f(G)\pi_{T}\right],

(6.18)

where
\pi_{T}\in\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
is given by

\displaystyle\pi_{T}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{d}({\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}-{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}})(\bm{\ell}^{h}_{i}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}-\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}

(6.19)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle^{2}\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}+\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle^{2}}.

Proof.

The proof relies on formula (
6.2
), obtained in Proposition
6.1
. Our goal is to show that each term in the Malliavin weight given by (
6.2
) can be written as a fraction of two random variables in
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
.

First, all the terms in (
6.2
) containing
\left\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}
can be computed via (
6.17
).

To compute
\left\langle\mathbf{D}F,h\right\rangle
, we apply the chain rule (
2.6
):

\mathbf{D}F=\mathbf{D}\left(\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}\right)=\dfrac{\mathbf{D}\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}-\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\mathbf{D}\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle^{2}},

so that, using (
6.17
),

\left\langle\mathbf{D}F,h\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}-\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle^{2}}.

(6.20)

As for the term
\delta(h)
, we observe that

\delta^{i}(h^{i})=\delta^{i}\!\left(\mathbf{D}^{i}\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{h}_{i},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)=-{\bf L}^{i}\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{h}_{i},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle=\left\langle({\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}-{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}})(\bm{\ell}^{h^{i}}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle,

(6.21)

as follows from Corollary
5.17
.

Combining (
6.17
), (
6.20
), and (
6.21
) and substituting the results into (
6.2
) yields (
6.19
).

It remains to notice that
\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\neq 0
a.s. since
F\in\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
, and that

\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle=\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}\neq 0\quad(a.s.),

by the assumption of Proposition
6.1
, so that
\pi_{T}
belongs to
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
.
∎

Remark 6.9
.

Theorem
6.8
states that the integration by parts formula (
6.18
) preserves the class
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
. This implies that if
f\in C^{k}
for some
k\geq 2
, then one can iterate the formula to obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[f^{(k)}(G)F\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f(G)\pi_{T}^{k}\right],

(6.22)

where
\pi_{T}^{k}\in\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
is given recursively by (
6.19
), replacing
\bm{\ell}^{F}_{m}
by
\bm{\ell}^{\pi_{T}^{k-1}}_{m}
for
m\in\{1,2\}
and
k\geq 2
.

\blacksquare

Remark 6.10
.

The formula (
6.19
) can be written explicitly in the compact form

\pi_{T}=\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{\pi_{T}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{\pi_{T}}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle},\quad\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{\pi_{T}}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\neq 0,

(6.23)

with

\displaystyle\bm{\ell}^{\pi_{T}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}({\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}-{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}})(\bm{\ell}^{h^{i}})\right)\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}_{1}^{F}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}_{2}^{F}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\left(\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\right)-\left(\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\right)\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}_{2}^{F}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\left(\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\right)

(6.24)

\displaystyle+\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\left(\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\right)\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\left(\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\right)+\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\left(\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\right),

(6.25)

\displaystyle\bm{\ell}^{\pi_{T}}_{2}

\displaystyle=(\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2})^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}2}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}\left(\bm{\ell}^{G}\diamond\vec{\bm{\ell}}^{h}\right)^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.0mu\sqcup}2}.

(6.26)

Although this makes the implementation of the recursive formula (
6.22
) straightforward, it has an important practical disadvantage: computing
\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{\pi_{T}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{\pi_{T}}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
requires evaluating
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}^{\leq N}
for a much higher order
N
than would be necessary when using the “decomposed” formula (

).

Example 6.11
.

The following examples show that two widely used choices of
h
are covered by Theorem
6.8
.

1.

Consider first the simplest choice
h^{i}\equiv\mathds{1}_{[0,T]}
,
i\in I
, for some set of indices
I\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}
, as considered in
(
Al-Foraih et al. 2023
)
. Note that
\mathds{1}_{[0,T]}\equiv\mathbf{D}^{i}\left\langle{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
, so that
\bm{\ell}^{h}_{i}=\mathds{1}_{\left\{i\in I\right\}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
. Since, by Corollary
6.5
,
\bm{\ell}\diamond^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}={{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell})
for all
\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and since
({\bf\Lambda}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}-{{\bf\Psi}}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{ii}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}})({\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}})={\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}
by direct computation, the expression (
6.19
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\pi_{T}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left\langle\sum_{i\in I}{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\sum_{i\in I}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{G}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}F\;-\;\dfrac{\left\langle\sum_{i\in I}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\,\left\langle\sum_{i\in I}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{G}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}

(6.27)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{1},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\,\left\langle\sum_{i\in I}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{F}_{2},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle^{2}\,\left\langle\sum_{i\in I}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{G}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}+\dfrac{\left\langle\sum_{i,j\in I}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}({\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{j}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{G})),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\sum_{i\in I}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{G}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle^{2}}F.

Despite the relative simplicity of this formula, it is necessary to justify that
\left\langle\sum_{i\in I}{\bf\Psi}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{i}}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{0}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{G}),\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\neq 0
a.s. If this cannot be guaranteed, one might prefer the second option.

2.

The second natural choice is
h^{i}=\mathbf{D}^{i}G
(i.e.
\bm{\ell}^{h}_{i}=\bm{\ell}^{G}
) for
i\in I
. It is often used to establish the regularity of densities (see, for instance,
Nualart 2006
). This choice ensures that

\langle\mathbf{D}G,h\rangle_{L^{2}}=\sum_{i\in I}\|\mathbf{D}^{i}G\|^{2}\geq 0.

(6.28)

The expression (
6.28
) is strictly positive if a.s. there exist
i\in I
and
t\in[0,T]
such that
\mathbf{D}_{t}^{i}G\neq 0
, which is much easier to verify than the corresponding condition in the first case. Note that, when
I=\{1,\ldots,d\}
, the condition
\langle DG,h\rangle_{L^{2}}\neq 0
represents the standard non-degeneracy condition for the Malliavin covariance matrix, which reduces to a scalar in this setting.

\blacksquare

Remark 6.12
.

The assumptions on the process
h
in Theorem
6.8
allow us not only to encompass the most common choices of
h
, as shown in the example above, but also to express the Malliavin weight using the unified formula (
6.19
). However, in general, other classes of processes
h
may be considered, and the corresponding integration by parts formulae can be derived using similar arguments.

\blacksquare

7
Greeks in the signature volatility model

We illustrate our results by computing the Greeks within the Signature Volatility model. Throughout this section, we assume that
d=2
. The asset price under the risk-neutral measure in the Signature Volatility model is given by

\displaystyle\dfrac{dS_{t}}{S_{t}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{t}dB_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],

(7.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle.

(7.2)

where
\bm{\sigma}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\rho\in[-1,1]
,
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
denotes the signature of
\widehat{W}_{t}=(t,W_{t}^{1},W_{t}^{2})
, and
B_{t}=\rho W_{t}^{1}+\bar{\rho}W_{t}^{2}
with
\bar{\rho}=\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}
. We assume the interest rate is
r=0
and that the volatility process is independent of
W^{2}
, meaning no letter
{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{2}}}
appears in
\bm{\sigma}
. To ensure the martingale property of the price process
S
established in
(
Abi Jaber et al. 2025a
, Theorem 3.1)
, we take odd truncation orders
N
(that is,
\bm{\sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}}=0
for all
|{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{v}}}|>N
) and impose the condition
\rho\,\bm{\sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}{\mathbf{1}}}^{\otimes N}}<0
, unless the volatility process is linear in
W
.

This model was shown to be universal in the sense that a large class of Markovian and non-Markovian stochastic volatility models can be exactly represented in the form (
7.1
) with an infinite collection of coefficients
\bm{\sigma}
, and can be well approximated by such models with finitely many coefficients
\bm{\sigma}
; see
(
Abi Jaber and Gérard 2025a
)
. This highlights the universality of the approach proposed in this paper, which can be applied to any model that can be written in the form (
7.1
) without any modification of the pricing or Greeks formulae.

We are interested in general path-dependent options with maturity
T>0
that depend on the signature
{\widehat{\mathbb{X}}}_{T}
of the time-augmented log-price
\widehat{X}:t\mapsto(t,\log S_{t})
for
t\in[0,T]
. We show in Proposition
7.2
below that
X_{t}
can be represented as a linear functional
\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{X},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle
of the Brownian signature. Hence, each element of
{\widehat{\mathbb{X}}}_{T}
can be expressed as a linear functional of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}
as well; see
(
Cuchiero et al. 2023
, Theorem 3.16)
for an explicit formula. Therefore, we can assume the payoff is written in the form
f\left(G\right),\ G=\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle
, where
\bm{\ell}^{G}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. The option price is given by
\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(G\right)\right]
. Our goal is to calculate the sensitivities

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(G\right)\right],

(7.3)

with respect to the model parameters
\theta
, where
\theta\in\{S_{0},\rho,\bm{\sigma}\}
.

Assuming for a moment that
f\in C^{1}_{b}
and that we can exchange the expectation and the differentiation, the Greek (
7.3
) is given by

\mathbb{E}\left[f^{\prime}\left(\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)\left\langle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)\pi_{T}\right].

(7.4)

Note that
\left\langle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})\subset\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T})
, so that Theorem
6.8
applies and the weight
\pi_{T}
is given by (
6.19
). This weight is independent of the function
f
, but it does depend on the option payoff through the coefficient
\bm{\ell}^{G}
.

Remark 7.1
.

The integration-by-parts result given by Theorem
6.8
requires the function
f
to be differentiable with a bounded derivative. However, functions of practical interest are typically non-differentiable, such as call option payoffs, or even discontinuous, such as digital option payoffs. In these cases, equation (
7.4
) no longer makes sense.
Nevertheless, for a square-integrable function
f
, in the sense that

\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)^{2}\right]<\infty,

assuming that the maps
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)^{2}\right]
and
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}[\pi_{T}^{2}]
are continuous, one can prove that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\left\langle\bm{\ell}^{G},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{T}\right\rangle\right)\pi_{T}\right].

(7.5)

This is achieved by approximating
f
in
L^{2}
by a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of compactly supported, infinitely differentiable functions for which (
7.5
) holds, and then passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
. A rigorous proof of this approximation argument can be obtained by a straightforward adaptation of the proof of
(
Fournié et al. 1999
, Proposition 3.2(ii))
. Note that, for the examples considered below, the continuity assumptions are trivially satisfied.

Before considering concrete examples, we rewrite
\log S_{T}
as a linear functional of the Brownian signature.

Appendix A
Malliavin differentiability of Stratonovich integrals

For simplicity of notation, we present the proof of Theorem
4.3
for the process

X_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}a_{s}\circ dW_{s},

where
a
satisfies (
4.3
)–(
4.5
) and
W
is a one-dimensional Brownian motion. The general case can be treated analogously.

The proof is an adaptation of the results in
(
Nualart 2006
, Section 3.1.1)
to the case of Stratonovich integrals considered as processes
\int_{0}^{\cdot}a_{s}\circ dW_{s}
, rather than random variables
\int_{0}^{T}a_{s}\circ dW_{s}
, and also establishes the existence of the derivative
(r,t)\mapsto\mathbf{D}_{r}\int_{0}^{t}a_{s}\circ dW_{s}
as an element of
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T]^{2})
. Our goal is therefore to show that

X\in\mathbb{L}^{1,2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{D}_{r}X_{t}=a_{r}\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(r)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{D}_{r}a_{s}\circ dW_{s}.

Recall that for a square-integrable process
h
, the Stratonovich integral
\int_{0}^{T}h_{s}\circ dW_{s}
, when it exists, is defined as the limit in probability of the Riemann sums

S^{\pi}(h_{\cdot})=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}\left(\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}h_{s}\,ds\right)\left(W_{t_{j+1}}-W_{t_{j}}\right),

along partitions
\pi=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}=T\}
as their diameter

|\pi|:=\sup_{j=0,\ldots,n-1}|t_{j+1}-t_{j}|\to 0
; see, e.g.,
Nualart 2006
.

Let
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\nu)
be a measurable space with a
\sigma
-finite atomless measure
\nu
, and let
h\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T])
. In what follows, we will need to define the Stratonovich integrals
\int_{0}^{T}h(y,s)\circ dW_{s}
, depending on a parameter
y\in Y
, as elements of
L^{2}(\Omega\times Y)
.

We denote by
P^{\pi}h
the stepwise approximation of
h
corresponding to the partition
\pi
:

P^{\pi}h(y,\cdot)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}h(y,s)\,ds\;\mathds{1}_{(t_{j},t_{j+1}]}(\cdot),

which, for almost every
(\omega,y)\in\Omega\times Y
, is the orthogonal projection of
h(y,\cdot)
onto the space of step functions generated by
\{\mathds{1}_{(t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\}_{j=0}^{n-1}
associated with the partition
\pi
.

The following lemma extends
(
Nualart 2006
, Lemma 3.1.2)
.

Lemma A.1
.

If
h\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T])
, then

\|P^{\pi}h-h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T])}\xrightarrow{|\pi|\to 0}0.

Proof.

For almost every
(\omega,y)\in\Omega\times Y
, we have
h(y,\cdot)\in L^{2}([0,T])
, and by the density of step functions,

\int_{0}^{T}|P^{\pi}h(y,s)-h(y,s)|^{2}\,ds\xrightarrow{|\pi|\to 0}0.

Moreover,

\int_{0}^{T}|P^{\pi}h(y,s)-h(y,s)|^{2}\,ds\leq 2\int_{0}^{T}|P^{\pi}h(y,s)|^{2}\,ds+2\int_{0}^{T}|h(y,s)|^{2}\,ds\leq 4\int_{0}^{T}|h(y,s)|^{2}\,ds,

where we used the projection property

\|P^{\pi}h(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}([0,T])}\leq\|h(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}([0,T])}
.
Integrating this bound with respect to
\mathbb{P}\otimes\nu(dy)
yields

\|P^{\pi}h-h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T])}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_{Y}\int_{0}^{T}|P^{\pi}h(y,s)-h(y,s)|^{2}\,ds\,\nu(dy)\right]\leq 4\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T])}^{2}.

The dominated convergence theorem then implies

\|P^{\pi}h-h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T])}^{2}\xrightarrow{|\pi|\to 0}0.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

If
h_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T])
and
\mathbf{D}h_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T]^{2})
as
n\to\infty
, then

\|\delta(s\mapsto h_{n}(\cdot,s))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y)}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,

where we recall that
\delta
is the Skorokhod integral defined in Subsection
5.2
.

Proof.

The proof relies on the standard
L^{2}
-bound for
\delta(u)
, where
u\in\mathbb{L}^{1,2}
, that is,

\mathbb{E}[\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T])}^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{D}u\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{2})}^{2}]<\infty.

In this case, the following bound holds

\mathbb{E}[|\delta(u)|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T])}^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{D}u\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{2})}^{2}].

(A.1)

For the proof, we refer to
(
Nualart 2006
, Proposition 1.3.1)
.

Applying (
A.1
) to
\delta(h_{n}(y,\cdot))
, integrating with respect to
\nu(dy)
and applying Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\|\delta(s\mapsto h_{n}(\cdot,s))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y)}^{2}\leq\|h_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T])}^{2}+\|\mathbf{D}h_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y\times[0,T]^{2})}^{2}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

∎

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Y}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<u<T\\
|u-s|\leq 1/n\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\big|\mathbf{D}_{s}h(y,u)-(\mathbf{D}^{+}h)(y,s)\big|^{2}\right]ds\,\nu(dy)

(A.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Y}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<u<s\\

We first note that the Riemann sums can be rewritten as

By Lemma

The convergence of the second term in (

Appendix B
Greeks via the characteristic function

If the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

the model admits a computable characteristic function
\phi(u):=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{iu\log\frac{S_{T}}{S_{0}}}\right],\ u\in\mathbb{R}
;

2.

for a given payoff
f
, a Fourier inversion formula is available,

then one can obtain a quasi-analytic Fourier representation for Greeks with respect to
S_{0}
, computable via numerical integration without the need for Monte Carlo simulation. The characteristic function
\phi(u)
in the signature volatility model can be computed by solving an infinite-dimensional Riccati equation; see
(
Abi Jaber and Gérard 2025a
, Section 5)
.

For example, for a vanilla call option, Lewis’ formula yields

C(T,K)=\mathbb{E}[(S_{T}-K)^{+}]=S_{0}-\frac{K}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\mathfrak{Re}\left(e^{i\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)\log\frac{S_{0}}{K}}\phi\!\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)\right)\frac{du}{u^{2}+\frac{1}{4}},

(B.1)

so that the option Delta is given by formal differentiation with respect to
S_{0}
:

\Delta(T,K)=1-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{K}{S_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\mathfrak{Re}\left(i\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)e^{i\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)\log\frac{S_{0}}{K}}\phi\!\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)\right)\frac{du}{u^{2}+\frac{1}{4}},

which can be computed much faster than Monte Carlo methods.

Similarly, using the relationship
C_{\mathrm{digital}}(T,K)=-\partial_{K}C(T,K)
between digital and vanilla call options, one obtains a Fourier representation for the digital option price by differentiating (
B.1
) with respect to
K
:

C_{\mathrm{digital}}(T,K)=\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\left\{S_{T}\geq K\right\}}]=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\mathfrak{Re}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-iu\right)e^{i\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)\log\frac{S_{0}}{K}}\phi\!\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)\right)\frac{du}{u^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}.

(B.2)

Taking the derivative with respect to
S_{0}
, we obtain the Delta of the digital option:

\Delta_{\mathrm{digital}}(T,K)=\frac{1}{\pi S_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\mathfrak{Re}\left(e^{i\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)\log\frac{S_{0}}{K}}\phi\!\left(u-\frac{i}{2}\right)\right)\,du.

(B.3)

This approach is rather formal, since we do not justify the differentiation of the integrals with respect to parameters, and no rigorous result concerning the existence of solutions to the infinite-dimensional Riccati equations is currently available. Hence, we emphasize that the Greeks obtained by this method are used only as sanity checks to demonstrate consistency with other methods.
