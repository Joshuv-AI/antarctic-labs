Dual Space Preconditioning for Gradient Descent in the Overparameterized Regime
Reza Ghane, Danil Akhtiamov, Babak Hassibi

Abstract
In this work we study the convergence properties of the Dual Space Preconditioned Gradient Descent, encompassing optimizers such as Normalized Gradient Descent, Gradient Clipping and Adam. We consider preconditioners of the form ∇ K \nabla K , where K : ℝ p → ℝ K:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R} is convex and assume that the latter is applied to train an over-parameterized linear model with loss of the form ℓ ⁡ ( 𝐗𝐖 − 𝐘 ) \ell(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{Y}) , for weights 𝐖 ∈ ℝ d × k \mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k} , labels 𝐘 ∈ ℝ n × k \mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k} and data 𝐗 ∈ ℝ n × d \mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d} . Under the aforementioned assumptions, we prove that the iterates of the preconditioned gradient descent always converge to a point 𝐖 ∞ ∈ ℝ d × k \mathbf{W}_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k} satisfying 𝐗𝐖 ∞ = 𝐘 \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_{\infty}=\mathbf{Y} . Our proof techniques are of independent interest as we introduce a novel version of the Bregman Divergence with accompanying identities that allow us to establish convergence. We also study the implicit bias of Dual Space Preconditioned Gradient Descent. First, we demonstrate empirically that, for general K ⁡ ( ⋅ ) K(\cdot) , 𝐖 ∞ \mathbf{W}_{\infty} depends on the chosen learning rate, hindering a precise characterization of the implicit bias. Then, for preconditioners of the form K ⁡ ( 𝐆 ) = h ⁡ ( ‖ 𝐆 ‖ F ) K(\mathbf{G})=h(\|\mathbf{G}\|_{F}) , known as isotropic preconditioners , we show that 𝐖 ∞ \mathbf{W}_{\infty} minimizes ‖ 𝐖 ∞ − 𝐖 0 ‖ F 2 \|\mathbf{W}_{\infty}-\mathbf{W}_{0}\|_{F}^{2} subject to 𝐗𝐖 ∞ = 𝐘 \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_{\infty}=\mathbf{Y} , where 𝐖 0 \mathbf{W}_{0} is the initialization. Denoting the convergence point of GD initialized at 𝐖 0 \mathbf{W}_{0} by 𝐖 GD , ∞ \mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},\infty} , we thus note 𝐖 ∞ = 𝐖 GD , ∞ \mathbf{W}_{\infty}=\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},\infty} for isotropic preconditioners. Finally, we show that a similar fact holds for general preconditioners up to a multiplicative constant, namely, ‖ 𝐖 0 − 𝐖 ∞ ‖ F ≤ c ​ ‖ 𝐖 0 − 𝐖 GD , ∞ ‖ F \|\mathbf{W}_{0}-\mathbf{W}_{\infty}\|_{F}\leq c\|\mathbf{W}_{0}-\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},\infty}\|_{F} for a constant c > 0 c>0 .

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{i}=\mathbf{w}_{i-1}-\eta\nabla K\left(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}_{i-1})\right)

Where

In this paper, we consider the following optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W})

And use Dual Space Preconditioned GD to minimize the loss:

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{i-1}-\eta\nabla K\left(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})\right)

(1)

Where

,
18

We focus on convex loss functions of the form

where
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
is the data matrix,
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
is the weight matrix for which we are optimizing and

is not strictly convex and does not have a unique minimizer
. It should be noted that most works in the literature assume strict convexity, making our proof of convergence for Dual Space Preconditioned GD novel in this setting.

Since

has many solutions due to the over-parametrization, it is natural to further inquire into the characterizing properties of
\mathbf{W}_{\infty}
among this set of solutions, usually known as

in the literature. As an example of an algorithm displaying implicit regularization, it has been shown
[
1

that for the loss

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}}D_{\psi}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{0})

\displaystyle s.t\quad\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{Y}

Different choices of

2

1

As outlined in the abstract, the remainder of our paper is devoted to analyzing implicit bias of Dual Space Preconditioned GD, with particular attention to the case of isotropic preconditioners. In addition, we establish exponential convergence rate of the weights for isotropic preconditioners.

As for relevant works,
[

There have been works extending the results of

II
Main Results and Applications

We use bold-faced letters to denote matrices and vectors.
n\geq 1
denotes the number of datapoints in the data matrix
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
d>n
denotes the number of features. Moreover,
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
denotes the labels. We begin with the following basic definitions.

Definition 1

(Strong Convexity)
.

f:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-strongly convex if

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{W}_{2})

\displaystyle\geq f(\mathbf{W}_{1})+\text{Tr}\Bigl(\nabla f(\mathbf{W}_{1})^{T}(\mathbf{W}_{2}-\mathbf{W}_{1})\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|\mathbf{W}_{1}-\mathbf{W}_{2}\|_{F}^{2}

Definition 2
.

(Fenchel Dual)
We define
f^{\ast}:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to be the Fenchel Dual of
f:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
if

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(\mathbf{Z})=\max_{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}}\text{Tr}(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{Z})-f(\mathbf{X})

Definition 3
.

(Lipschitz Gradient)
A differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
has
L
-Lipschitz gradient if for
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
:

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(\mathbf{A})-\nabla f(\mathbf{B})\|_{F}\leq L\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\|_{F}

We define
K:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\to\mathbb{R}
to be a convex function resulting in a preconditioner
\nabla K:\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W})
to be the training loss achieved by the weights
\mathbf{W}
.

Definition 4

(Bregman Divergence)
.

For any
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the standard Bregman Divergence is defined as

\displaystyle D_{f}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}):=

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{A})-f(\mathbf{B})-\text{Tr}{\left(\nabla f(\mathbf{A})^{T}(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})\right)}

The main contribution of the present paper is defining a new form of the Bregman Divergence, that we will call
Adjusted Bregman Divergence
, and deriving related fundamental identities necessary for analysing convergence of (
1
).

Definition 5

(Adjusted Bregman Divergence)
.

For any
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the Adjusted Bregman Divergence is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{f}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}):=

\displaystyle f^{*}(\nabla f(\mathbf{A}))-f^{*}(\nabla f(\mathbf{B}))

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}(\nabla f(\mathbf{A})-\nabla f(\mathbf{B}))\right)}

We define the interpolating manifold
\mathcal{M}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=\{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}:\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{Y}\}

(2)

We formulate the required assumptions as follows:

Assumptions 1
.

1.

The dual reference function
K:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is differentiable and convex. Furthermore for any
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
such that
K(\mathbf{A})=0
implies
\nabla K(\mathbf{A})=0
.

2.

The loss function

Moreover, define

In item

6.

II-A

Examples and Discussion

To delineate the utility of our results, in this section, we will focus on a few selected examples. We consider the squared loss which amounts to taking
\mu=M=1

1) Normalized Gradient Descent:
We consider the case where

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{i-1}-\eta\frac{\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})}{\epsilon+\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})\|_{F}}

5

2) Gradient Clipping:
Gradient Clipping is another popular
[
26
]

\displaystyle K(\mathbf{Z})=\begin{cases}\frac{\|\mathbf{Z}\|_{F}^{2}}{2}&\|\mathbf{Z}\|_{F}\leq\epsilon\\

Which yields

5

3) Adam:
To illustrate the utility of the second part of Theorem
2
, let us consider

which yields

, where

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{i-1}-\eta\frac{\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})}{\epsilon+|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})|}

(7)

Note that the choice of

follows the update rule of Adam
[
11
]
without weight decay and momentum.
Generally, the weights are initialized such that for every
(j,j^{\prime})\in[d]\times[k]
, we have that
|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{0})^{(j,j^{\prime})}|>0
. Therefore, we may assume that throughout the iterations, no entry of the gradient of the loss is exactly zero. Setting
m_{K}=\frac{\epsilon}{(\epsilon+\max_{j,j^{\prime}}|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{0})^{(j,j^{\prime})}|)^{2}}
and

We would also like to provide intuition for the dynamics of (

[

III
Outline of the proofs

III-A

Convergence

As noted earlier, the proof of convergence is based on the result of Proposition
1
, whose proof is presented in the Supplementary Materials. Using Proposition
1
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{0})=\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{t})+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{t}K(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i}))

(8)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\Bigl[\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W}_{i},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})-\eta D_{K}(\mathbf{W}_{i},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{t}D_{K}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})

(9)

By taking
t\rightarrow\infty
, as the left hand side of (
8
) is independent of
t
and finite, and every term on the right hand side is positive, we have that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}K(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i}))<\infty
which implies
K(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{t}))\rightarrow 0
. By the Assumption 1.1 on
K
, it follows that
\nabla K(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{t}))\rightarrow 0
and this concludes the convergence argument.

III-B

Proximity to GD

In the case that
K(\cdot)
is not isotropic, for instance in the third example of Section II.A, we cannot apply the reasoning as in Section III.B. To this end, we introduce the following auxiliary variable
\hat{\mathbf{W}}
. Consider
\{\mathbf{W}_{i}\}_{i=1}
generated according to the update rule in (
1
), initialized from
\mathbf{W}_{0}
.
Furthermore, consider

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{i-1}-\eta\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})

Consider the following GD updates, initialized from the same
\mathbf{W}_{0}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i}=\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1}-\eta\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1})

We use
\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{i}
to analyze
\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i}-\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{F}
. By algebraic manipulations, we observe that

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i}-\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{i}\|_{F}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\|\mathbf{W}_{j}-\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{j}\|_{F}

\displaystyle+\eta\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\Bigl(\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i})\|_{F}+\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})\|_{F}\Bigr)

Then we note that by combining the triangle inequality and
L_{K}
-Lipschitzness of
\nabla K
:

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{i}-\mathbf{W}_{i}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\eta\Bigl\|\nabla K\left(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})\right)-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})\Bigr\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\eta(L_{K}+1)\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i-1})\|_{F}

Then combining this identity with the bounds in Lemma 4, yields the results of Theorem
2
.

Lemma 1
.

For any
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and any differentiable
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{A})

\displaystyle+\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})-\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}{\left((\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{A})^{T}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}))\right)}

Proof.

Using the definition of
\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, we expand each term on the left-hand side:

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{A})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C}))-\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}))

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{A}^{T}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}))\right)}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}))-\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}))

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}))\right)}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C}))-\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}))

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}))\right)}

Computing
\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{A})+\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})-\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{B})

First, the
\mathcal{L}^{*}

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C}))-\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}))]+[\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}))-\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}))]

\displaystyle\quad-[\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C}))-\mathcal{L}^{*}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}))]=0

Second, the trace terms:

\displaystyle-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{A}^{T}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}))\right)}-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}))\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B}))\right)}

\displaystyle=-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{A}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})\right)}+\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{A}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A})\right)}-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A})\right)}

\displaystyle+\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B})\right)}+\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})\right)}-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{B})\right)}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})\right)}-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{A}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})\right)}-\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A})\right)}

\displaystyle+\text{Tr}{\left(\mathbf{A}^{T}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A})\right)}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}{\left((\mathbf{B}^{T}-\mathbf{A}^{T})\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})\right)}-\text{Tr}{\left((\mathbf{B}^{T}-\mathbf{A}^{T})\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A})\right)}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}{\left((\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{A})^{T}(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}))\right)}

.

Lemma 5
.

If
D_{K}(\mathbf{W}_{i},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})-\alpha\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W}_{i},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})\geq 0
then

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i})\leq(1-\alpha\eta)\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})

Proof.

We have from the fundamental identity

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})-\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i})=\eta K(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i}))

\displaystyle+\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W}_{i},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})-\eta D_{K}(\mathbf{W}_{i},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})+\eta D_{K}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}\eta K(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{i}))+\eta D_{K}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\geq}}\eta D_{K}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})

Where in
(a)
we have used the assumption on
\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}-\eta D_{K}
being nonnegative.
(b)
follows from the nonnegativity of
K(\cdot)
. Then by the assumption

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})-\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i})\geq\alpha\eta\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})

Then we observe that

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i})\leq(1-\alpha\eta)\tilde{D}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}_{i-1})

∎

Lemma 6

(Gradient Descent Rate)
.

We have that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\frac{M\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{T})}{n}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{0})}

\displaystyle\cdot\Bigl(1-\eta\frac{\mu\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{T})}{n}\Bigr)^{i}

Proof.

In this proof we consider the mapping
\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W})\leftarrow vec(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}))
where
vec(\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}))\in\mathbb{R}^{kd}
.
Using the fundamental theorem of calculus

\displaystyle\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i})-\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}ds\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}\Bigl(s\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i}+(1-s)\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1}\Bigr)(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i}-\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1})

\displaystyle=-\eta\int_{0}^{1}ds\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}\Bigl(s\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i}+(1-s)\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1}\Bigr)\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1})

Under the vectorization mapping, the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}(\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{kd\times kd}
will be block-diagonal. Hence

\displaystyle\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i})

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\mathbf{I}-\eta\int_{0}^{1}ds\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}\Bigl(s\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i}+(1-s)\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1}\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\cdot\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1})

Then we note that for each block
\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1})\in\mathcal{S}
, hence constrained to
\mathcal{S}
, by assumptions for each block of the Hessian matrix

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{T})}{n}\|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1})\|_{2}\leq\|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i-1})\|_{2}

As

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{T})}{n}\mathbf{I}\preceq\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}\Bigl|_{\mathcal{S}}\preceq\frac{M\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{T})}{n}\mathbf{I}

Which implies by the Descent Lemma
7
:

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{GD},i})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\frac{M\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{T})}{n}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}_{0})}

\displaystyle\cdot\Bigl(1-\eta\frac{\mu\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{T})}{n}\Bigr)^{i}

∎

Lemma 7
.

(Descent Lemma) For any convex
L
-smooth function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
we have for
\eta_{L}<\frac{2}{L}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\eta_{L}-\frac{L\eta_{L}^{2}}{2}\Bigr)\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|_{F}^{2}\leq f(\mathbf{x})-\min_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

Which implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|_{F}^{2}\leq 2L\Bigl(f(\mathbf{x})-\min_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}}f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\Bigr)

Proof.

We have for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the definition of smoothness:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{y})\leq f(\mathbf{x})+\text{Tr}\Bigl(\nabla f(\mathbf{x})^{T}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})\Bigr)+\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\|_{F}^{2}

Take
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}-\eta_{L}\nabla f(\mathbf{x})
, then

\displaystyle f\Bigl(\mathbf{x}-\eta_{L}\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\Bigr)\leq f(\mathbf{x})+\Bigl(\frac{L\eta_{L}^{2}}{2}-\eta_{L}\Bigr)\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|_{F}^{2}

Then as
f\Bigl(\mathbf{x}-\eta_{L}\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\Bigr)\geq\min_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
we arrive at

\displaystyle\Bigl(\eta_{L}-\frac{L\eta_{L}^{2}}{2}\Bigr)\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|_{F}^{2}\leq f(\mathbf{x})-\min_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}}f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

∎
