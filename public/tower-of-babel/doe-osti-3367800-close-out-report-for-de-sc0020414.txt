1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
 This report documents the accomplishments of the University of Tennessee – Knoxville (UTK)
team under award DE-SC0020414 for the project period August 15, 2022 through August 14, 2025.
This award supported UTK's contribution to a multi-institutional U.S. collaboration on the WEST
(Tungsten Environment in Steady-State Tokamak) experiment at CEA-Cadarache, France. The
overall collaboration was led by Dr. Ezekial Unterberg of ORNL, with UTK serving as a key
partner alongside ORNL, the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), Princeton
Plasma Physics Laboratory (PPPL), the Pennsylvania State University (PSU), and the
Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).
 The project addressed the impact of tungsten (W) as the primary plasma-facing material in a
long-pulsed, fully-tungsten, actively-cooled divertor tokamak. The research was organized around
three Key Science-Driver Questions (KSQs):
 1. KSQ-1: Characterize W surface morphology under reactor-like fluences
 2. KSQ-2: Determine main W source locations through synthetic diagnosis
 3. KSQ-3: Use impurity powder dropping to aid in control and sustainment of long-pulses
 The UTK team was primarily involved in KSQ-1 and KSQ-2. Over the three-year award
period, the UTK team made significant contributions including: leading dedicated experiments on
WEST during the C7, C9, C10, and C11 operational campaigns studying impurity sourcing from
multiple device regions; developing and deploying an integrated synthetic diagnostic workflow
using the OEDGE/DIVIMP impurity transport codes constrained by multiple WEST diagnostics;
installing and analyzing impurity collector probe samples and high-field-side wall coupons;
developing boron calibration standards through cross-calibration of LAMS and NRA; performing
high-fidelity plasma-surface interaction modeling of helium plasma exposure to tungsten single
crystal samples; conducting first-principles density functional theory calculations of intrinsic
defects and helium behavior in tungsten borides and tungsten oxide; and providing on-site
experimental leadership through a full-time UTK staff scientist stationed at WEST.
 The project produced 14 peer-reviewed journal articles, over 20 conference presentations, and
contributed to one M.S. thesis (Jake Maeker), two Ph.D. theses (Wendy Garcia, Davis Easley), and
two in-progress Ph.D. theses (Sean Kosslow, Brian Putra). This work contributed to the training
of five graduate students and three staff scientists. International collaboration with CEA and the
WEST team was sustained throughout, with UTK personnel obtaining long-term site access and
contributing to experimental planning, execution, and analysis across multiple WEST campaigns.

2. ACCOMPLISHMENTS
2.1 Project Goals
The major goals of the project, as outlined in the original proposal and renewal, were organized
around the following three KSQs:
 1. Characterize W surface morphology under reactor-like fluences — Understanding
 how long-pulse, high-fluence plasma exposure affects tungsten surface morphology and
 subsequently changes scrape-off layer (SOL) contamination pathways, including the
 effects of boron and oxygen surface layers.
 2. Determine main W source locations through synthetic diagnosis — Identifying the
 dominant pathways through the SOL for impurity transport and determining whether the
 SOL can effectively screen impurities from contaminating the main plasma, with particular
 attention to contributions from multiple plasma-facing component (PFC) locations.
 3. Use impurity powder dropping to aid in control and sustainment of long-pulses —
 Evaluating whether active (real-time) methods of PFM surface conditioning using the
 Impurity Powder Dropper (IPD) can alleviate W SOL contamination and allow for long-
 pulse discharges.
The UTK team's specific responsibilities focused on KSQ-1 and KSQ-2, with supporting
contributions to KSQ-3 through sample analysis and boron transport characterization.

2.2 Accomplishments by Key Science Question
KSQ-1: Characterize W Surface Morphology Under Reactor-Like Fluences
Goal Status: Substantially Achieved
The UTK team, in partnership with ORNL and UIUC, completed simulations of plasma-surface
interactions on WEST using a comprehensive multi-scale, multi-physics modeling framework.
Key accomplishments include:
o High-fidelity PSI modeling of He plasma exposure (FY23–FY24): Dr. Ane Lasa Esquisabel
 published a manuscript in Nuclear Fusion describing the results of an integrated modeling
 framework including SOLPS-ITER (SOL plasma), hPIC2 (plasma sheath), RustBCA (ion-
 surface interactions), and Xolotl (sub-surface material evolution). The framework was applied
 to interpret WEST C4 campaign experiments in which five single-crystal W samples were
 placed in a reciprocating collector probe and exposed to He plasma. Two scenarios were
 modeled to elucidate the role of impurities: pure He plasma and 95% He / 5% O. The SOLPS
 predictions matched experimental reciprocating probe measurements, providing confidence in
 the plasma conditions used as inputs. The presence of 5% oxygen decreased incident particle
 and heat fluxes by 15–20% while increasing sputtering yields by an order of magnitude. In the
 presence of O, model predictions of the experimental LAMS signal showed near-surface He
 concentrations consistent with experimental measurements.

 tungsten collector probe samples during the WEST C4 He campaign,” Nuclear Fusion 64,

KSQ-2: Determine Main W Source Locations Through Synthetic Diagnosis
Goal Status: Substantially Achieved
The UTK team made major advances in understanding impurity transport in the WEST SOL
through a combination of experimental leadership, diagnostic development, and computational
modeling:
o C7 Campaign Experiments (FY23): A dedicated L-mode experiment was designed,
 proposed, and executed on WEST during the C7 campaign to characterize W and light impurity
 sources. The separatrix was driven ~45 mm away from the upper divertor during discharges to
 assess the impact on impurity sourcing from multiple regions (upper/lower divertor, antenna).
 Results were presented at APS DPP 2023 and PSI 2024 and served as the scientific motivation
 for C9 experiments.
o C9 Campaign Experiments (FY24): Building on C7 results, a new experiment was proposed
 and executed during C9 by UTK Staff Scientist Dr. Alex Grosjean, who served as experimental
 session leader. Dr. Grosjean developed novel plasma shapes through his training as a WEST
 "pilot" (plasma operation controller), enabling first-of-their-kind scans of the gap distances
 between the primary and secondary separatrices and the upper divertor. The experiments
 utilized the newly commissioned McPherson spectrometer (installed by ORNL) to measure

 WI and WII emission. Four different plasma shapes were investigated for their impact on W
 net/gross erosion, and the role of the secondary separatrix in impurity sourcing was assessed.
o Impurity Collector Probe Experiments (FY24): UTK installed four tungsten samples on the
 fast-reciprocating impurity collector probe (ICP) during the C9 campaign. The ICP sampled
 the far-SOL plasma to assess boron and other impurity content following injection from the
 Impurity Powder Dropper (operated by PPPL) and after wall conditioning conducted as part
 of normal operations. Samples were prepared by UTK graduate student Sean Kosslow and
 installed by Dr. Grosjean. The samples were successfully exposed for more than 10 plunges
 and are undergoing LAMS analysis at ORNL.
o Main Chamber Wall Coupons (FY24-FY25): Twenty tungsten coupons were prepared and
 installed in sample holders at twelve separate poloidal and toroidal locations on the high-field
 side wall during C9 (FY24). These samples were exposed for the full campaign and were
 analyzed by the broader U.S. collaboration (ORNL electron microscopy, PSU XPS, MIT ion
 beam analysis, ORNL/UTK TDS). A second set of samples was installed during C10 and C11
 (FY25) using the same sample holders to compare the effect of the IPD on main chamber and
 material evolution.
o Boron Calibration Standards (FY24): Mr. Kosslow led the development of calibration
 standards for boron areal density measurements through cross-calibration of LAMS and
 Nuclear Reaction Analysis (NRA) in collaboration with Dr. Kevin Woller of MIT.
o OEDGE/DIVIMP Modeling (FY23–FY25): The OEDGE interpretive modeling suite was
 applied to WEST geometries and constrained using multiple diagnostics including divertor
 Langmuir probes, reciprocating Langmuir probes, interferometry/reflectometry, and visible
 spectroscopy. DIVIMP impurity transport simulations demonstrated that while the divertor
 target experiences the highest gross W erosion, it is well screened, whereas other PFC regions
 (e.g., the pumping baffle) experience lower erosion but higher proportional contamination of
 the near-SOL. The background plasma and impurity transport models were iteratively refined
 using the synthetic diagnosis workflow.
o CHERAB Synthetic Diagnostic Framework (FY23–FY25): Mr. Kosslow utilized a
 synthetic diagnosis workflow using the CHERAB code package for WEST bolometry and
 spectroscopic diagnostics. This framework uses 3D CAD geometries and ray-tracing to
 produce synthetic measurements for comparison with OEDGE/DIVIMP impurity transport
 simulations.
o 3DLIM Implementation (FY24): Mr. Kosslow implemented the 3DLIM synthetic diagnostic
 tool to interpret ICP measurements, including interfacing 3DLIM with WEST magnetic field
 line tracing tools and implementing relevant wall geometry to determine ICP sampling lengths.
o Collaboration with SOLEDGE modeling teams (FY23–FY25): UTK is serving as a liaison
 between the U.S. collaboration and CEA computational modelers with significant work by
 UTK graduate student, Mr. Brian Putra, utilizing the ERO2.0 and SOLEDGE code suites,
 preparing for cross-code comparisons and validation activities.

6. IMPACT
6.1 Impact on the Principal Discipline
 This project provided an important validation opportunity for the high-fidelity, integrated
plasma surface interaction modeling framework developed through the PSI SciDAC program on
the all-tungsten WEST long-pulse tokamak. It also provided unique opportunities to study the role
of boronization during plasma wall conditioning on plasma-surface interactions and tungsten
surface chemistry evolution.
 WEST is one of the few tokamak experiments in the world that has devoted a significant
portion of its experimental program to PSI studies. It is equipped with a wide array of spectroscopic
tools for assessing impurities in the plasma, making it an ideal platform for advancing impurity
transport models. The lessons learned regarding impurity transport in the SOL plasma contribute
directly to understanding how to control and mitigate contamination of the core plasma by high-Z
impurities. The advances in both theory and modeling have direct relevance to ITER and future
fusion pilot plants by increasing both interpretive and predictive modeling capabilities.
 Key scientific contributions include:
 • Demonstration that oxygen impurities (at ~5% concentration) increase tungsten sputtering
 yields by an order of magnitude and must be included in simulations to accurately predict
 erosion
 • First observations of the impact of plasma shaping on non-divertor impurity sources in a
 fully-tungsten tokamak
 • Validation of the SOLPS–hPIC2–RustBCA–Xolotl integrated modeling framework against
 experimental LAMS measurements of He depth profiles in plasma-exposed W single
 crystals
 • Development of a synthetic diagnosis workflow coupling OEDGE/DIVIMP impurity
 transport simulations with CHERAB ray-tracing for validation against spectroscopic
 measurements

6.2 Impact on Other Disciplines
 The development and application of spectroscopic techniques for distinguishing charge states
of tungsten (WI, WII) and light impurities has broader applicability to plasma environments
beyond fusion, including plasma thrusters and plasma processing where low charge states of high-
Z materials may be encountered. The focus on WI and WII spectroscopy is critical to
understanding net vs. gross erosion in any application of tungsten as a plasma-facing component.
 The study of wall conditioning methods on a high-Z tungsten-walled device provides insights
into surface chemistry processes involving tungsten, oxygen, carbon, nitrogen, and boron mixtures
that may be of interest to the plasma processing industry.

6.3 Impact on Human Resources
 This project contributed to the training and development of five UTK graduate students and
three staff scientists. These individuals gained experience in experimental analysis, computational
modeling, international collaboration, and presentation of results at major conferences. The project
fostered extensive interaction with colleagues across the United States (ORNL, UIUC, PPPL, PSU,
MIT) and internationally with collaborators in France, providing exposure to a diverse array of
expertise and research methodologies.
 Dr. Grosjean's career trajectory from UTK postdoc to staff scientist to trained WEST Scientific
Coordinator and pilot represents a particularly notable example of the workforce development
impact of this award.

6.4 Impact on Infrastructure
 The project developed strong institutional ties between the collaborating U.S. partners (UTK,
ORNL, UIUC, PPPL, PSU, MIT) and the WEST team at CEA. UTK trained five graduate students
and three staff scientists in computational modeling for impurity transport and integration of
diagnostic data from WEST into models. The CHERAB synthetic diagnostic framework developed
for WEST represents an institutional capability that can be applied to other plasma experimental
devices.
 UTK supplied tungsten substrates for the fast-reciprocating impurity collector probe and main
chamber sample coupon holders during the C9 and C11 campaigns. The joint UTK-ORNL
LAMS/TDS facility was upgraded with a femtosecond laser, providing enhanced capability for
future plasma-exposed sample characterization.

7. CHANGES AND PROBLEMS
7.1 Changes in Approach
The project continued largely on schedule throughout the three-year period. The full U.S.
collaboration worked together effectively under the coordination of Lead PI Dr. Unterberg. The
primary changes in approach were:
 1. Expanded sample exposure program: UTK became more involved in sample exposures
 on WEST than originally envisioned, enabled by Dr. Grosjean's on-site presence and the
 support of CEA collaborators.
 2. Evolution of experimental focus: C7 results on primary separatrix movement informed
 the design of more comprehensive C9 experiments that added secondary separatrix scans
 and expanded plasma shaping, representing a natural and productive evolution of the
 research.
 3. Increased emphasis on boron transport characterization: The development of boron
 calibration standards and the installation of ICP samples for boron transport studies became
 a larger component of the work than initially planned.

7.2 Problems and Delays
 1. STC Agreement disruption (FY23): The re-negotiation of the DOE-CEA Science and
 Technology Cooperation Agreement temporarily restricted site access. Analysis was able
 to continue through the WEST Partner Zone, and long-term access was subsequently
 restored.
 2. Sample shipment logistics: Radiation screening and clearance procedures for shipping
 plasma-exposed samples from WEST to the U.S. required significant time and
 coordination. Dr. Grosjean's on-site presence was essential for shepherding samples
 through the process.

 the C4 campaign and ITER-like PFU samples from WEST.
