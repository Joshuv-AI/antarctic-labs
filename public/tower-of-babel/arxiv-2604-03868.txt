Risk-Constrained Belief-Space Optimization for Safe Control under Latent Uncertainty
Clinton Enwerem, John S. Baras, Calin Belta

Abstract
Many safety-critical control systems must operate under latent uncertainty that sensors cannot directly resolve at decision time. Such uncertainty, arising from unknown physical properties, exogenous disturbances, or unobserved environment geometry, influences dynamics, task feasibility, and safety margins. Standard methods optimize expected performance and offer limited protection against rare but severe outcomes, while robust formulations treat uncertainty conservatively without exploiting its probabilistic structure. We consider partially observed dynamical systems whose dynamics, costs, and safety constraints depend on a latent parameter maintained as a belief distribution, and propose a risk-sensitive belief-space Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control framework that plans under this belief while enforcing a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) constraint on a trajectory safety margin over the receding horizon. The resulting controller optimizes a risk-regularized performance objective while explicitly constraining the tail risk of safety violations induced by latent parameter variability. We establish three properties of the resulting risk-constrained controller: (1) the CVaR constraint implies a probabilistic safety guarantee, (2) the controller recovers the risk-neutral optimum as the risk weight in the objective tends to zero, and (3) a union-bound argument extends the per-horizon guarantee to cumulative safety over repeated solves. In physics-based simulations of a vision-guided dexterous stowing task in which a grasped object must be inserted into an occupied slot with pose uncertainty exceeding prescribed lateral clearance requirements, our method achieves 82% success with zero contact violations at high risk aversion, compared to 55% and 50% for a risk-neutral configuration and a chance-constrained baseline, both of which incur nonzero exterior contact forces.

Problem Formulation

We consider a partially-observed stochastic system

x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t},\theta,w_{t}),\qquad z_{t}=g(x_{t},\theta,v_{t}),

where

is the terminal cost. Since

is unknown and

is a random variable whose distribution is induced by

and the process noise over the horizon.

(Shorthand Notation)
.

Hereafter, we write

,

, and

Definition 3

(Belief and Latent Parameter)
.

Since
\theta
is not directly observable, the controller maintains a posterior belief

b_{t}(\theta)\;:=\;p\bigl(\theta\mid x_{0:t},\,z_{1:t}\bigr),

where

is the joint probability law over
(\theta,x_{0:t},z_{1:t})

is the information available up to time

Exact inference over

We therefore use a particle filter, which can represent multimodal beliefs, handle hybrid continuous-discrete latent states, and is simple to implement, requiring only the number of particles

to be specified
[
31
,
22
]

Definition 4

(Particle Belief)
.

We approximate the belief
b_{t}(\theta)
by a set of

weighted particles
\{(\theta^{(i)},\omega_{t}^{(i)})\}_{i=1}^{N_{p}}
, with
\omega_{t}^{(i)}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}\omega_{t}^{(i)}=1
. Upon applying control

and receiving observation

, each particle is propagated through the process model (
3
),

x_{t+1}^{(i)}=f\bigl(x_{t},u_{t},\theta^{(i)},w_{t}^{(i)}\bigr),\ w_{t}^{(i)}\sim p_{w},

(6)

and the

are updated via the likelihood induced by the measurement model (
3
)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{t+1}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\omega_{t}^{(i)}\cdot p\bigl(z_{t+1}\mid\theta^{(i)},x_{t+1}^{(i)}\bigr),

(7)

\displaystyle\omega_{t+1}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{t+1}^{(i)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N_{p}}\tilde{\omega}_{t+1}^{(j)}},

(8)

with resampling applied when the effective sample size

Definition 5

(Safety Function)
.

We encode safety by a continuously-differentiable function

that defines the parameter-dependent safe set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon h(x,\theta)\geq 0\}

\mathrm{Int}\,\mathcal{S}(\theta)
, and boundary
\partial\mathcal{S}(\theta)
. At time,

h(x_{t},\theta)\begin{cases}>0,&x_{t}\in\mathrm{Int}\,\mathcal{S}(\theta),\\
=0,&x_{t}\in\partial\mathcal{S}(\theta),\\
<0,&x_{t}\notin\mathcal{S}(\theta).\end{cases}

(9)

The function

may represent physical safety margins such as obstacle clearance or contact force limits (see
Section

V
). Since instantaneous safety does not capture safety over the task horizon, we next define a trajectory-level safety notion.

Definition 6

(Trajectory Safety Margin)
.

Given a trajectory
x_{t:t+H}{\equiv}\,\mathbf{x}
(induced by
\mathbf{u}
and
\theta
via (
3
)) with horizon

starting at time

, its safety margin is

M_{H}(\mathbf{x},\theta)=\min_{k=0,\dots,H}\,h(x_{t+k},\theta).

(10)

For notational simplicity, hereafter, we will drop the
\mathbf{x}
and
\theta
arguments in (
10
) and simply write
M_{H}
(the exceptions in
Remark

1
carry over). A positive

indicates the trajectory remains in
\mathcal{S}(\theta)
, while

indicates a violation, hence
{-}{M_{H}}
may be interpreted as a

.

Problem 1

(Risk-Constrained Belief-Space Control)
.

Given a stochastic system with latent parameters

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\star}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{H}}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{b_{t}}[J_{H}]+\lambda_{r}\,\mathrm{CVaR}_{\beta_{c}}(J_{H})

\displaystyle\mathrm{CVaR}_{\beta_{s}}({-}{M_{H}})\leq 0.

Remark 2

(Underlying Distribution and Problem Data)

In

Since

Theorem 1

(CVaR Safety Implication)

Let

Proof.

Remark 4
.

Let

2

\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
is a closed subset of the compact set
\mathcal{U}^{H}
, hence compact.
Write the objective (
11
) as
\varphi(\mathbf{u},\lambda_{r})\,{=}\,\mathbb{E}_{b_{t}}[J_{H}(\mathbf{u})]+\lambda_{r}\,\mathrm{CVaR}_{\beta_{c}}(J_{H}(\mathbf{u}))
.
Under
2
,
J_{H}(\mathbf{u})
depends continuously on
\mathbf{u}
, so
\mathrm{CVaR}_{\beta_{c}}(J_{H}(\cdot))
is continuous on
\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
.
Since
\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
is compact,
\mathrm{CVaR}_{\beta_{c}}(J_{H}(\cdot))
attains a finite supremum
C<\infty
on
\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
, and therefore

\hskip-7.5pt\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})}\bigl|\lambda_{r}\,\mathrm{CVaR}_{\beta_{c}}(J_{H}(\mathbf{u}))\bigr|\;{\leq}\;{\lambda_{r}}C\;\to\;0\ \text{as }\lambda_{r}\;{\downarrow}\;0.

(22)

Hence
\varphi(\cdot,\lambda_{r})\to\mathbb{E}_{b_{t}}[J_{H}(\cdot)]
uniformly on
\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
.
Since
\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
is compact, every sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{\star}(\lambda_{r})\}
has a cluster point
\bar{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
. For any such cluster point and any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
, the minimality of
\mathbf{u}^{\star}(\lambda_{r})
gives
\varphi(\mathbf{u}^{\star}(\lambda_{r}),\lambda_{r})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{v},\lambda_{r})
. Taking the limit along the subsequence realizing
\mathbf{u}^{\star}(\lambda_{r})\to\bar{\mathbf{u}}
, and by uniform convergence
[
32
]
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{b_{t}}[J_{H}(\bar{\mathbf{u}})]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\lambda_{r}\;{\downarrow}\;0}\varphi(\mathbf{u}^{\star}(\lambda_{r}),\lambda_{r})

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\lim_{\lambda_{r}\;{\downarrow}\;0}\varphi(\mathbf{v},\lambda_{r})=\mathbb{E}_{b_{t}}[J_{H}(\mathbf{v})].

(23)

Since we chose
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})
arbitrarily, it follows that
\bar{\mathbf{u}}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{H}(\beta_{s})}\mathbb{E}_{b_{t}}[J_{H}(\mathbf{u})]
.
∎

Theorem 3

(Cumulative Receding-Horizon Safety)
.

Suppose the controller re-solves (
11
)–(
12
) at
T
successive time steps with horizon
H
and safety level
\beta_{s}\in(0,1)
, satisfying (
12
) at every re-solve. Let
M^{(j)}_{H}
denote the safety margin at the
j
-th solve. Then

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathrm{Pr}\!\Bigl(\bigcap_{j=1}^{T}\bigl\{M^{(j)}_{H}\geq 0\bigr\}\Bigr)\;\geq\;1-T(1-\beta_{s}).\end{array}

(24)

Proof.

By
Theorem

1
,
\mathrm{Pr}(M^{(j)}_{H}\,{<}\,0)\leq 1-\beta_{s}
for each
j
. A union bound
[
32
]
gives

\mathrm{Pr}\!\Bigl(\bigcup_{j=1}^{T}\{M^{(j)}_{H}\,{<}\,0\}\Bigr)\leq T(1-\beta_{s}),

and taking the complement yields (
24
).
∎

Remark 5
.

The bound is non-trivial while
T<1/(1-\beta_{s})
; for
\beta_{s}=0.90
this permits up to
T=9
solves before it degenerates. For larger
T
, one can tighten
\beta_{s}
adaptively or apply Boole–Fréchet corrections
[
33
]
when violations are positively correlated. In our experiments (
T\leq 200
,
\beta_{s}=0.90
), the bound is loose, but the empirical collision rate remains well below it due to strong correlation of margins across overlapping horizons.

i.

ii.

iii.

:
The state

iv.

:
We use the stage cost

\ell(x_{t},u_{t},\theta)=\|p_{t}^{\mathrm{obj}}-p_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta)\|_{\bar{Q}}^{2}+\|u_{t}\|_{\bar{R}}^{2},

We define the safety function as the minimum of three physically meaningful margins,

h(x_{t},\theta)=\min\!\left\{\begin{aligned} &d_{\mathrm{env}}(x_{t},\theta)-d_{\min},\\

Chance-Constrained Baseline

We compare our method against a chance-constrained baseline (
CcMppi
) that uses the same dynamics model, belief representation, stage cost, terminal cost, and MPPI rollout budget, but replaces the CVaR-based safety constraint with a probability-of-violation constraint, omits the risk term in the cost (
11

M_{H}
using only the posterior mean
\bar{\theta}=\mathbb{E}_{b_{t}}[\theta]
as a point estimate of the slot geometry, discarding the full belief distribution. At each time step,

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{H}}\quad

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{b_{t}}[J_{H}]

(29)

subject to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\bigl(M_{H}<0\bigr)\leq\delta_{H},

(30)

where the probability is taken with respect to the belief over
\theta
and the process noise over the horizon, and
\delta_{H}\in(0,1)
is the prescribed violation tolerance. In practice,
CcMppi

empirically from the same trajectory rollouts used to estimate

12

\lambda_{r}{=}0

V-D

CVaR Computation

The per-candidate scores in (
15

16

. For the performance CVaR,

VI-C

Discussion

Safety-Performance Tradeoff

Tables

and
III
confirm a safety-performance tradeoff: increasing
\beta_{s}
eliminates exterior contact entirely (27% to 0% contact rate) and improves success rate from 55% to 82%, consistent with

CVaR vs. Chance Constraint

Both
CcMppi
(
\delta_{H}{=}0.050
) and CVaR-MPPI (
\beta_{s}{=}0.90
) achieve zero contact, yet CVaR-MPPI attains a substantially higher success rate (

\beta_{s}{=}0.95

82\%
vs.
50\%
. The chance constraint controls only the
probability
of violation, whereas the CVaR constraint penalizes its
magnitude
, directing the optimizer toward trajectories that maintain safer margins during critical insertion instants and thus complete insertion more reliably.

Computational Cost

All configurations run at approximately
1{,}200
ms per control step on a single CPU core (
Table

), dominated by the
K\times N_{p}
forward rollouts in MuJoCo (
1{,}024
total). The CVaR estimation overhead is negligible relative to simulation.

Limitations

Our current evaluation assumes a rigid grasp and uses a simplified clearance model with a finite set of surface points. Contact dynamics during the final stowing phase may require higher-fidelity models. The particle filter also assumes independent noise across observations, implying that correlated sensor errors may degrade belief quality.

VII
Conclusion

We presented a belief-space MPC framework for safe control under latent uncertainty. By solving a risk-regularized finite-horizon optimal control problem with a CVaR safety constraint on the trajectory margin, the controller produces actions that optimize expected performance while bounding the probability of safety violations. The CVaR constraint implies a probabilistic safety guarantee (
Theorem

1
), the formulation recovers risk-neutral control in the appropriate limit (
Theorem

2
), and a union-bound argument extends the per-horizon guarantee to cumulative safety across receding-horizon re-solves (
Theorem

3
). Ablation studies on the vision-guided dexterous insertion task confirm a monotonic safety-performance tradeoff as the safety risk confidence level increases, and comparison with a chance-constrained baseline demonstrates the benefit of CVaR’s tail sensitivity. Future work will address correlated observation noise and extension to time-varying latent parameters.
