Orthonormal Spectral Cluster Bounds on Manifolds with Nonpositive Curvature Thanks: © 2026 by the authors. This paper may be reproduced, in its entirety, for non-commercial purposes.
Jean-Claude Cuenin, Ngoc Nhi Nguyen, Xiaoyan Su

Abstract
. Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a closed n n -dimensional Riemannian manifold with nonpositive sectional curvature. We prove sharp, logarithmically improved spectral cluster bounds for orthonormal systems in the supercritical range. More precisely, for spectral windows of size ( log ⁡ λ ) − 1 (\log\lambda)^{-1} , we obtain the orthonormal analogue of the logarithmically improved L q L^{q} estimates of Hassell–Tacy. Our argument combines the universal orthonormal spectral cluster bounds of Frank–Sabin with Bérard-type kernel estimates and a generalization of the Bourgain–Shao–Sogge–Yao multiplier estimate to the orthonormal setting.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Universal bounds

Let
(M,{\rm g})
be a closed
n
-dimensional manifold and
-\Delta_{\rm g}
be the associated Laplace–Beltrami operator, for
n\geq 2
.
For
\lambda\geq 2
and
\varepsilon(\lambda)\in(0,1]
, the spectral projector with window size
\varepsilon
and its corresponding eigenspace are defined, using the spectral theorem, as

\Pi_{\lambda,\varepsilon(\lambda)}\coloneqq\mathbf{1}(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\rm g}}\in[\lambda,\lambda+\varepsilon(\lambda))),\quad E_{\lambda,\varepsilon(\lambda)}\coloneqq\mathop{\rm Ran}\Pi_{\lambda,\varepsilon(\lambda)}.

For
\varepsilon(\lambda)=1
, Sogge
[
11
]
proved the universal bounds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\delta(q)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)},\quad u\in E_{\lambda,1}

(1)

where, for
2\leq q\leq\infty
,

\displaystyle\delta(q)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}),&2\leq q\leq q_{c},\\
n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{1}{2},&q_{c}\leq q\leq\infty,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad q_{c}\coloneqq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}.

(2)

As was shown in
[
12
]
, the universal bounds (
1
) are sharp on
any closed manifold.

More recently, Frank–Sabin
[
7
, Theorem 2]
proved the following generalization of (
1
): for any orthonormal system
(u_{j})_{j\in J}\subset E_{\lambda,1}
and coefficients
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j\in J}\subset\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j\in J}\nu_{j}|u_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{q/2}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{2\delta(q)}\|\nu\|_{\ell^{\alpha(q)}},

(3)

where the implicit constant is independent of
\lambda
,
J
,
\nu
and the orthonormal system, and

\displaystyle\alpha(q)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{2q}{q+2}&2\leq q\leq q_{c},\\
\frac{q(n-1)}{2n},&q_{c}\leq q\leq\infty.\end{cases}

(4)

Here and in the following,
J
denotes any countable index set. By contrast, combining (
1
) with the triangle inequality yields (
3
) with
\alpha=1
, even without the orthogonality assumption. The key point is that, for
q>2
, Frank–Sabin’s inequality holds with some
\alpha>1
. This is in line with the Lieb–Thirring philosophy that orthogonality needs space.

1.2.
Logarithmically improved bounds

We now assume that
(M,g)
has nonpositive curvature, in the sense that all the sectional curvatures are nonpositive. In this setting, for
\varepsilon(\lambda)=(\log\lambda)^{-1}
, Hassell and Tacy
[
9
]
obtained the sharp bounds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(M)}\lesssim_{q}(\log\lambda)^{-1/2}\lambda^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)},\quad u\in E_{\lambda,(\log\lambda)^{-1}}

(5)

for all
supercritical
exponents
q>q_{c}
. A well-known orthogonality argument shows that (
5
) cannot be improved.

The case
p=\infty
in (
5
) follows from earlier results of Bérard
[
1
]

and is related to improved remainder estimates in the Weyl law,

\displaystyle N(\lambda)\coloneqq\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{1}({-\Delta_{\rm g}}<\lambda^{2}))=|M|\frac{|B^{n}|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\lambda^{n-1}}{\log\lambda}\right).

(6)

The whole range of supercritical
L^{q}
bounds was later established under a weaker dynamical assumption in the work of Canzani and Galkowski
[
4
]
.

The aim of this paper is to generalize (
5
) to systems of orthonormal functions. The following theorem is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, all of whose sectional curvatures are nonpositive, and let
q>q_{c}
and
1\leq\beta<\frac{q(n-1)}{2n}
. Then for any orthonormal system
(u_{j})_{j\in J}\subset E_{\lambda,(\log\lambda)^{-1}}
and coefficients
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j\in J}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j\in J}\nu_{j}|u_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{q/2}(M)}\lesssim_{q,\beta}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1}\|\nu\|_{\ell^{\beta}}.

(7)

The theorem is optimal in the following two senses:
first, the factor
(\log\lambda)^{-1}\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1}
cannot be improved. This follows from the optimality of (
5
) by taking
\#J=1
.
Second, if
q<\infty
, the inequality fails for all
\beta\geq\frac{q(n-1)}{2n}
. This is seen by taking an orthonormal basis
(u_{j})_{j\in J}
of the eigenspace
E_{\lambda,(\log\lambda)^{-1}}
and
\nu_{j}=1
for all
j\in J
. Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|M|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{1-n}(\log\lambda)\bigg\|\sum_{j\in J}|u_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{q/2}(M)}\geq\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{1-n}(\log\lambda)\bigg\lVert\sum_{j\in J}|u_{j}|^{2}\bigg\rVert_{L^{1}(M)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{1-n}(\log\lambda)\dim E_{\lambda,(\log\lambda)^{-1}}=\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{1-n}(\log\lambda)(N(\lambda+(\log\lambda)^{-1})-N(\lambda))>0

where the final inequality follows from the elementary fact that
\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n}N(\lambda)>0
.
On the other hand,
\|\nu\|_{\ell^{\beta}}=(\#J)^{1/\beta}
.
Combining these observations, we see that (
7
) can only hold if

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{n-1}}{\log\lambda}\lesssim\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\Big(\frac{\lambda^{n-1}}{\log\lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}.

(8)

It follows from the relation
n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1+(n-1)\frac{2n}{q(n-1)}=n-1
that we must have
\beta<\frac{q(n-1)}{2n}
if
q<\infty
. For
q=\infty
, (
7
) holds with
\beta=\infty
. This was proved by Ren and Zhang
[
10
]
. Alternatively, it follows from (
5
) and, e.g.,
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Notation

The notation
A\lesssim B
means
A\leq CB
, for some unspecified
constant
C
that may depend on fixed quantities such as
M,q
and
\beta
but not on
\lambda,J,\nu
or the orthonormal system (or on
W,W_{1},W_{2}
in the dual estimates). To emphasize the dependence on parameters, we sometimes use subscripts, e.g.
A\lesssim_{q,\beta}B
. When we write
A\lesssim_{N}B_{N}
, we mean that for every
N>0
there exists a constant
C_{N}
such that
A\leq C_{N}B_{N}
.

For an operator
S:L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)
, we denote its operator norm by
\|S\|
and its
p
-Schatten norm by
\|S\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}}\coloneqq(\tr(S^{*}S)^{\frac{p}{2}})^{\frac{1}{p}}
for
1\leq p<\infty
. We often omit
M
from the notation, e.g. we write
L^{p}
instead of
L^{p}(M)
.

2.
Spectral multiplier theorem for orthonormal systems

In this section, we prove a spectral multiplier estimate that only uses the universal bounds of Frank–Sabin as input. This is a generalization of the Bourgain–Shao–Sogge–Yao multiplier estimate
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
to the orthonormal setting and may be of independent interest.

We set

\displaystyle P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{\rm g}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Pi_{k,1}\coloneqq\mathbf{1}(P\in[k,k+1))\ \mbox{for }k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(M,{\rm g})
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
, let
m:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a bounded Borel function and
2\leq q\leq\infty
. Let
\delta(q)
and
\alpha(q)
be given by (
2
) and (
4
), respectively. Assume that

\displaystyle K_{q}(m)\coloneqq\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[k,k+1)}|m(\tau)|^{2}(1+k)^{2\delta(q)}\bigg)^{1/2}<\infty.

Then for any orthonormal system
(u_{j})_{j\in J}\subset L^{2}(M)
and coefficients
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j\in J}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j\in J}\nu_{j}|m(P)u_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{q/2}(M)}\lesssim K_{q}(m)^{2}\|\nu\|_{\ell^{\alpha(q)}}.

(9)

Moreover, for all
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}(M)
,

\displaystyle\|W_{1}m(P)W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha(q)^{\prime}}(L^{2}(M))}\lesssim K_{q}(|m|^{1/2})^{2}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}(M)}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}(M)}.

(10)

Proof.

By Frank–Sabin’s duality principle
[
6
, Lemma 3]
, (
9
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\|Wm(P)\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\lesssim K_{q}(m)^{2}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}},\quad W\in L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}.

(11)

Assume first that
\supp m\subset[0,N+1]
for some
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we define

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k,\varepsilon}^{-1}\Pi_{k,1}m(P),\quad c_{k,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\sup_{\tau\in[k,k+1)}|m(\tau)|+\varepsilon.

(12)

Using orthogonality,

\displaystyle\Pi_{k,1}\Pi_{k^{\prime},1}=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\Pi_{k,1},\quad k,k^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}},

(13)

and the support assumption on
m
,
we observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k,\varepsilon}\Pi_{k,1}S_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\Pi_{k,1}m(P)=m(P).

(14)

By the spectral theorem,

\displaystyle\|S_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq 1.

(15)

Thus

\displaystyle\|Wm(P)\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k,\varepsilon}W\Pi_{k,1}\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}=\bigg\|\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k,\varepsilon}^{2}W\Pi_{k,1}\overline{W}\bigg\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k,\varepsilon}^{2}\|W\Pi_{k,1}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[k,k+1)}|m(\tau)|^{2}(1+k)^{2\delta(q)}+\varepsilon^{2}N^{2\delta(q)+1}\Big)\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}

where we used
\|AB\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}}\leq\|A\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}}\|B\|
and (
15
) in the first inequality, followed by

\|A\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2p}}^{2}=\|AA^{*}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}}
and orthogonality of spectral projectors to eliminate cross terms. The final inequality
uses the Frank–Sabin bound (
3
) for spectral projectors, or more precisely, the dual inequality

\|W\Pi_{k,1}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\lesssim(1+k)^{2\delta(q)}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}},

see
[
7
, (16)]
. Passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
yields (
11
).

To remove the support assumption, observe that, since
P
is a self-adjoint operator with discrete spectrum,
\mathbf{1}_{[0,N+1]}(P)
is a sequence of monotonically increasing finite-dimensional orthogonal projections that converges strongly to the identity operator on
L^{2}(M)
, as
N\to\infty.
Setting
m_{N}(P):=m(P)\mathbf{1}_{[0,N+1]}(P)
, it follows by
[
8
, Theorem 5.2]
that

\|Wm(P)\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\leq\sup_{N}\|Wm_{N}(P)\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\lesssim K_{q}(m_{N})^{2}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}\leq K_{q}(m)^{2}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}.

The bound (
10
) follows from (
11
) and its dual, applied to
|m|^{1/2}
, since

\displaystyle\|W_{1}m(P)W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert W_{1}|m|^{1/2}(P)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\bigg\lVert\frac{m}{|m|}(P)\bigg\rVert\left\lVert|m|^{1/2}(P)W_{2}\right\lVert_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}

and
\frac{m}{|m|}(P)
is a partial isometry.
∎

We record the following application of the multiplier theorem to resolvent powers.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
(M,{\rm g})
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
. Let
2\leq q\leq\infty
and
\gamma>1/2
. Then for any orthonormal system
(u_{j})_{j\in J}\subset\mathop{\rm Ran}\mathbf{1}_{[0,2\lambda]}(P)
and coefficients
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j\in J}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
,

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j\in J}\nu_{j}|(-\Delta_{{\rm g}}-(\lambda+i)^{2})^{-\gamma}u_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{q/2}(M)}\lesssim_{\gamma}\lambda^{2\delta(q)-2\gamma}\|\nu\|_{\ell^{\alpha(q)}}.

Proof.

Consider the function

m_{\gamma}(\tau)\coloneqq(\tau^{2}-(\lambda+i)^{2})^{-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{[0,2\lambda]}(\tau).

Then, since
\gamma>1/2
,

K_{q}(m_{\gamma})\lesssim\lambda^{\delta(q)-\gamma}\bigg(\sum_{k\leq 2\lambda}(1+|k-\lambda|)^{-2\gamma}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim_{\gamma}\lambda^{\delta(q)-\gamma}.\qed

Remark 2.3
.

The case
\gamma=1
also follows from
[
5
, Proposition 3.1]
. The elliptic part of resolvent powers, corresponding to the projection onto
\mathop{\rm Ran}\mathbf{1}_{[2\lambda,\infty)}(P)
, can be handled by
[
7
, Proposition 24]
, combined with
[
5
, Lemma 2.4]
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In the following, let
T=c_{0}\log\lambda
, where
c_{0}
is a small constant to be fixed later.
We adapt an argument of Bourgain–Shao–Sogge–Yao
[
3
]
to the case of orthonormal systems. Let
q>q_{c},
and
1\leq\beta<\frac{q(n-1)}{2n}
. By duality, to prove (
7
) it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\|W\Pi_{\lambda,T^{-1}}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{q,\beta}\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}.

(16)

Let
a
be a nonnegative Schwartz function on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle a\geq\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}\quad\text{and}\quad\supp(\widehat{a})\subset[-1,1].

(17)

Since
a\geq\mathbf{1}_{[-1,1]}
, it follows that

\displaystyle Wa(T(P-\lambda))\overline{W}\geq W\Pi_{\lambda,T^{-1}}\overline{W}

(18)

in the quadratic form sense.
Since for positive compact operators
0\leq B\leq A
, one has
\|B\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}}\leq\|A\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}}
, (
16
) would thus follow from

\displaystyle\|Wa(T(P-\lambda))\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}\lesssim\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}.

(19)

By Fourier inversion, we can write

\displaystyle a(T(P-\lambda))=\frac{1}{2\pi T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{a}(t/T){\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}{\rm e}^{\mathrm{i}tP}{\rm d}t.

(20)

Using that
2\cos(tP)=e^{itP}+e^{-itP}
and discarding the smoothing operator
a(-T(P+\lambda))
arising from the
e^{-itP}
term (see Remark
3.2
below), it suffices to consider

\displaystyle A\coloneqq\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{a}(t/T){\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}\cos(tP){\rm d}t

(21)

and to prove

\displaystyle\|WA\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}\lesssim\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}},\quad q>q_{c},\quad\beta<\frac{q(n-1)}{2n}.

(22)

We now split
A
into a local and global part,
A=A_{\rm loc}+A_{\rm glo}
, where

\displaystyle A_{\rm loc}\coloneqq\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}b(t)\widehat{a}(t/T){\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}\cos(tP){\rm d}t,\quad A_{\rm glo}\coloneqq\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1-b(t))\widehat{a}(t/T){\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}\cos(tP){\rm d}t

(23)

and
b\in C_{c}^{\infty}([-2,2])
is such that
b=1
on
[-1,1]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Estimate for the local part)
.

For
2\leq q\leq\infty
,

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm loc}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{q}\lambda^{2\delta(q)}(\log\lambda)^{-1}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}},\quad W\in L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}(M),

(24)

where
\delta(q)
and
\alpha(q)
are given by (
2
) and (
4
), respectively.

Proof.

We write
A_{\rm loc}
as a spectral multiplier,

\displaystyle A_{\rm loc}=m_{\rm loc}(P),\quad m_{\rm loc}(\tau)\coloneqq\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}b(t)\widehat{a}(t/T){\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}\cos(t\tau){\rm d}t.

(25)

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle|m_{\rm loc}(\tau)|\lesssim_{N}T^{-1}((1+|\lambda-\tau|)^{-N}+(1+|\lambda+\tau|)^{-N}).

(26)

Using Theorem
2.1
and observing that

\displaystyle K_{q}(|m_{\rm loc}|^{1/2})\lesssim_{N}T^{-1/2}\lambda^{\delta(q)}+\lambda^{-N},

(27)

yields the claimed bound.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Since

|a(-T(\tau+\lambda)|\lesssim_{N}(1+|\lambda+\tau|)^{-N},

the same proof yields

\|Wa(-T(P+\lambda))\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{N}\lambda^{-N}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}.

Lemma 3.3

(Estimate for the global part)
.

Under the nonpositive curvature assumption and for
q>q_{c}
, there exists
\epsilon(q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm glo}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1-\epsilon(q)}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}},\quad W\in L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}(M).

(28)

Moreover, for any
\beta<\frac{q(n-1)}{2n}
there exists
\epsilon(q,\beta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm glo}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{q,\beta}\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1-\epsilon(q,\beta)}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}},\quad W\in L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}(M).

(29)

Proof of (
28
).

Again we write
A_{\rm glo}
as a spectral multiplier,

\displaystyle A_{\rm glo}=m_{\rm glo}(P),\quad m_{\rm glo}(\tau)\coloneqq\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1-b(t))\widehat{a}(t/T){\rm e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda}\cos(t\tau){\rm d}t.

(30)

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle|m_{\rm glo}(\tau)|\lesssim_{N}(1+|\lambda-\tau|)^{-N}+(1+|\lambda+\tau|)^{-N}.

(31)

Using Theorem
2.1
and the estimate

\displaystyle K_{q_{c}}(|m_{\rm glo}|^{1/2})\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{q_{c}}}

(32)

yields

\displaystyle\|W_{1}A_{\rm glo}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{n+1}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{n+1}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{n+1}}.

(33)

We recall that
A_{\rm glo}
depends on
T=c_{0}\log\lambda
. Given
\eta>0
we may now fix
c_{0}
such that the pointwise kernel estimate

\displaystyle\|A_{\rm glo}\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times M)}\lesssim_{\eta}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\eta}

(34)

holds, where we denoted the kernel of
A_{\rm glo}
by the same symbol as the operator. This follows from
[
3
, Eq. (5.7)]
and is also implicit in earlier work of Bérard
[
1
]
and Hassell–Tacy
[
9
]
.
As an immediate consequence of (
34
), we have the Hilbert–Schmidt bound

\displaystyle\|W_{1}A_{\rm glo}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}}\lesssim_{\eta}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\eta}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

(35)

We interpolate the bilinear map

\mathcal{T}(W_{1},W_{2})\coloneqq W_{1}A_{\rm glo}W_{2}.

By (
33
), we have

\|\mathcal{T}(W_{1},W_{2})\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{n+1}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{n+1}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{n+1}},

while (
35
) implies

\|\mathcal{T}(W_{1},W_{2})\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}}\lesssim_{\eta}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\eta}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence, by bilinear complex interpolation
(see, e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem 4.4.1]
),
for every
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(W_{1},W_{2})\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{r_{\theta}}}\lesssim_{\eta}\lambda^{(1-\theta)\frac{n-1}{n+1}+\theta(\frac{n-1}{2}+\eta)}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{p_{\theta}}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{p_{\theta}}},

(36)

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{n+1}+\frac{\theta}{2},\qquad\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{n+1}+\frac{\theta}{2}.

(37)

Here we used
[
2
, Theorem 4.4.1]
with

A_{0}^{(1)}=A_{0}^{(2)}=L^{n+1}
,
A_{1}^{(1)}=A_{1}^{(2)}=L^{2}
,
B_{0}=\mathfrak{S}^{n+1}
,
B_{1}=\mathfrak{S}^{2}
and the fact that
L^{p}
and Schatten spaces are interpolation spaces, with

(L^{p_{1}},L^{p_{2}})_{\theta}=L^{p_{\theta}}
and
(\mathfrak{S}^{p_{1}},\mathfrak{S}^{p_{2}})_{\theta}=\mathfrak{S}^{p_{\theta}}
for
\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{2}}
and
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
.

Choosing

\theta=1-\frac{q_{c}}{q},

we obtain

p_{\theta}=r_{\theta}=2(q/2)^{\prime}

in (
37
).
Therefore, setting
W_{1}=W
and
W_{2}=\overline{W}
in (
36
),

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm glo}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{\eta}\lambda^{(n-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})+\eta(1-\frac{q_{c}}{q})}\|W\|_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}^{2}.

(38)

Fixing
\eta:=\frac{n-1}{4}
, this shows that
(
28
) holds with

\epsilon(q)=\frac{n-1}{4}\Big(1-\frac{q_{c}}{q}\Big).\qed

Proof of (
29
).

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi=1
on
[-1,1]
, and
\supp\chi\subset[-2,2]
. For
R\in[1,\lambda]
, to be fixed later, define

m_{\rm near}^{(R)}(\tau)\coloneqq\chi\Big(\frac{\tau-\lambda}{R}\Big)m_{\rm glo}(\tau),\qquad m_{\rm far}^{(R)}(\tau)\coloneqq\Big(1-\chi\Big(\frac{\tau-\lambda}{R}\Big)\Big)m_{\rm glo}(\tau),

and

A_{\rm near}^{(R)}\coloneqq m_{\rm near}^{(R)}(P),\qquad A_{\rm far}^{(R)}\coloneqq m_{\rm far}^{(R)}(P).

(39)

Then
A_{\rm glo}=A_{\rm near}^{(R)}+A_{\rm far}^{(R)}
. We will collect some properties of these operators in Lemma
3.4
below.

We first estimate the near part. In view of (
28
), we may assume that
\beta^{\prime}<2(q/2)^{\prime}
. Using
(
43
) with

s^{\prime}=2(q/2)^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}\lesssim(R\lambda^{n-1})^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}\|WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}.

(40)

Since
A_{\rm near}^{(R)}
is a spectral multiplier satisfying the same pointwise
bound (
31
) as
A_{\rm glo}
, the estimate
(
38
) applies to
A_{\rm near}^{(R)}
as well. Thus, after some straightforward arithmetic simplifications, using
\alpha(q)=\frac{q(n-1)}{2n}
for
q\geq q_{c}
,

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1+\eta(1-\frac{q_{c}}{q})-(n-1)(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{2n}{q(n-1)})}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}.

We now set

\displaystyle\epsilon(q,\beta)\coloneqq\frac{n-1}{4}\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{2n}{q(n-1)}\Big),\quad\eta(q,\beta)\coloneqq\frac{2\epsilon(q,\beta)}{1-\frac{q_{c}}{q}},\quad R(\lambda,q,\beta)\coloneqq\lambda^{\frac{\epsilon(q,\beta)}{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}}

and fix
\eta=\eta(q,\beta)
,
R=R(\lambda,q,\beta)
to obtain

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{n(1-\frac{2}{q})-1-\epsilon(q,\beta)}\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}.

(41)

Using (
45
) with
N
sufficiently large yields a better estimate than (
41
) for the far part
A_{\rm far}^{(R)}
.
Combining the near and far bounds, we obtain (
29
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

With the above notation, the following properties hold.

(i)

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}(WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W})\lesssim R\,\lambda^{n-1}

(42)

and if
1\leq\beta^{\prime}\leq s^{\prime}\leq\infty
, then

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}\lesssim(R\lambda^{n-1})^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\|WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{s^{\prime}}}.

(43)

(ii)

For every
q\geq 2
and every
N>0
,

\displaystyle K_{q}\big(|m_{\rm far}^{(R)}|^{1/2}\big)\lesssim_{N}R^{-N}\lambda^{\delta(q)}.

(44)

Consequently, Theorem
2.1
implies

\displaystyle\|WA_{\rm far}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha(q)^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{N}R^{-N}\lambda^{2\delta(q)}\|W\|_{L^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}(M)}^{2}.

(45)

Proof.

Part (i) follows by definition immediately, since
m_{\rm near}^{(R)}
is supported where

|\tau-\lambda|\leq 2R
, so

\operatorname{rank}A_{\rm near}^{(R)}\leq\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda-2R,\lambda+2R]}(P)\lesssim R\,\lambda^{n-1},

where the last inequality follows from the Weyl law (
6
); the logarithmic improvement is irrelevant here.

By (
42
) and Hölder,

\|WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}\lesssim(R\lambda^{n-1})^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\|WA_{\rm near}^{(R)}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{s^{\prime}}}.

For (ii), on the support of
m_{\rm far}^{(R)}
one has
|\tau-\lambda|\geq R
. Therefore, by the assumption (
31
),

|m_{\rm far}^{(R)}(\tau)|\lesssim_{N}R^{-N/2}(1+|\lambda-\tau|)^{-N/2}+(1+|\lambda+\tau|)^{-N}.

Hence

\displaystyle K_{q}\big(|m_{\rm far}^{(R)}|^{1/2}\big)^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[k,k+1)}|m_{\rm far}^{(R)}(\tau)|(1+k)^{2\delta(q)}\lesssim_{N}R^{-N/2}\lambda^{2\delta(q)}+\lambda^{-N}.

After replacing
N
by
2N
, this gives (
44
). Inequality (
45
) follows from Theorem
2.1
.
∎
