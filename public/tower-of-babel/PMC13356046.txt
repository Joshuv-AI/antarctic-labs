Assessment of Dose Proportionality and the Effect of Food on a Once-Daily Extended-Release Tablet Formulation of Deutetrabenazine in Healthy Participants
Authors: Eva-Maria Sunzel, Giulia Ghibellini, Maria Gutierrez, Debra Ruffo, Shailendra Chavan, Nagnath G Birajdar, Frank Schneider, Pippa Loupe, Mark Forrest Gordon, Laura Rabinovich-Guilatt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectiveDeutetrabenazine is approved as a treatment in adults for tardive dyskinesia and chorea associated with Huntington’s disease. A once-daily tablet (Austedo XR® Teva) was recently approved in addition to the twice-daily tablet (Austedo®), and studies were conducted to evaluate dosage strength proportionality and food effect of the once-daily formulation.MethodsIn a randomized cross-over study of dosage strength proportionality, healthy participants (n = 116) received single doses of the once-daily deutetrabenazine formulation and a power model was fitted to describe the relationship between dose and pharmacokinetic parameters (maximum observed plasma drug concentration [Cmax], area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time 0 to 36 h [AUC0–36h], and area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time 0 extrapolated to infinity [AUC0–∞]). In the second cross-over study, a single 24-mg dose of the once-daily formulation was given in fasted and fed conditions to healthy participants (n = 84) and the effect of food was evaluated by constructing 90% confidence intervals of the geometric mean ratios for Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–∞.ResultsDosage strength proportionality for the once-daily formulation was demonstrated for the dosage strengths 6 mg, 12 mg, and 24 mg; and across the approved dose range up to 48 mg for the pharmacokinetic parameters of all analytes (deutetrabenazine, and metabolites; deuterated alpha-dihydrotetrabenazine and beta-dihydrotetrabenazine), individually and as a sum). In the food effect study, the Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–∞ geometric mean ratios for deutetrabenazine and deuterated metabolites all fell within the 90% confidence interval of 80–125.ConclusionsThe once-daily formulation of deutetrabenazine exhibited dosage strength proportional pharmacokinetics for doses up to 48 mg and there was no observed effect of food.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40261-026-01566-8.

Key Points

Following administration of the once-daily formulation of deutetrabenazine (Austedo XR®), the plasma concentrations of deutetrabenazine and its deuterated active metabolites increase proportionally with increasing doses.The once-daily formulation of deutetrabenazine (Austedo XR®) can be administered with or without meals as there is no effect of food on the plasma concentrations of deutetrabenazine and its deuterated active metabolites.

Introduction

Deutetrabenazine is a vesicular monoamine transporter 2 inhibitor, approved as a treatment in adults for tardive dyskinesia (TD) and chorea associated with Huntington’s disease (HD) [1–3]. Deutetrabenazine is structurally related to tetrabenazine (Xenazine®; Bausch Health Companies Inc.) [4]. Following oral administration, peak concentrations of deutetrabenazine and tetrabenazine are reached within 3 h post-dosing, and both are rapidly and extensively converted during first-pass hepatic metabolism by carbonyl reductase to their active metabolites, alpha-dihydrotetrabenazine (α-HTBZ) and beta-dihydrotetrabenazine (β-HTBZ). The concentrations of deutetrabenazine and tetrabenazine are very low, thus the active metabolites are considered primarily responsible for pharmacological activity [5, 6]. For tetrabenazine, the active metabolites α-HTBZ and β-HTBZ have very short half-lives as they are rapidly biotransformed because of O-demethylation at their two methoxy groups [7]. Therefore, tetrabenazine has to be dosed frequently [5]. Deutetrabenazine was rationally designed using deuteration at a selected molecular position to slow down the cytochrome P450 (CYP)-dependent metabolism of deuterated α-HTBZ and β-HTBZ to their less potent O-desmethyl metabolites [8, 9]. This targeted placement of the C–D bonds in deutetrabenazine results in increased exposure of deuterated α-HTBZ and β-HTBZ with prolonged half-lives [10, 11].

Deutetrabenazine (Austedo®; Teva) was originally designed as a twice-daily (BID) formulation; earlier studies on the BID formulation [12] determined that the pharmacokinetics of the deuterated metabolites following administration of the BID formulation under fed conditions were linear and approximately proportional when given single doses of 6–24 mg and repeated doses of 7.5–22.5 mg administered BID. Exposure to deutetrabenazine and its deuterated metabolites increased in a generally dose-proportional manner for the 24–72 mg dose range; however, data were highly variable. They also found that the composition of the meal did not affect the pharmacokinetics of the deutetrabenazine BID formulation [12]. The recently approved extended-release deutetrabenazine (Austedo XR®; Teva) was developed as a once-daily (QD) formulation. The QD formulation is based on an osmotic drug-delivery system that provides drug release in a controlled and sustained manner such that plasma concentrations are maintained for several hours after administration [13].

The recommended clinical dose range for the BID and QD formulations is 12–48 mg per day for the treatment of adults with TD and chorea associated with HD [13]. For those switching from the tetrabenazine 12.5-mg dose, a 6-mg dose of deutetrabenazine is available, and for those who are poor CYP2D6 metabolizers or taking strong CYP2D6 inhibitors, 36 mg/day is the maximum recommended dose [13]. An earlier study compared the bioavailability and bioequivalence of deutetrabenazine and its active metabolites following administration of the BID versus QD formulation at steady state [14]. In this study, peak concentrations at steady state for the QD formulation were similar to the BID formulation for deutetrabenazine and were slightly lower for the metabolites. The time to reach peak concentrations at steady state and the extent of exposure (area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time 0–24 h at steady state) were similar for the BID and QD formulations for all analytes [14].

In addition to a bioequivalence study comparing the BID and QD formulations, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recommends that a dosage strength equivalence assessment or a dosage strength proportionality assessment of the QD formulation be performed [15]. The study described herein was a single-dose crossover study and had two objectives; one objective was to determine whether the pharmacokinetics of the QD formulation of deutetrabenazine and the deuterated metabolites showed dosage strength proportionality following administration of dose strengths of 6-mg, 12-mg, and 24-mg tablets of the QD formulation, similar to what was shown previously with the BID formulation. The second objective was to determine whether the pharmacokinetics of deutetrabenazine and the deuterated metabolites showed dosage strength proportionality for doses 6–48 mg.

To extend the flexibility of dosing regimens for patients, a study was conducted to determine whether the QD formulation of deutetrabenazine required food consumption. The QD formulation is an osmotic pressure tablet that uses osmotic pressure to deliver deutetrabenazine at a steady controlled rate [13]. Its release is independent of gastric conditions [16] and a single-dose comparison study is considered sufficient to evaluate the food effect for osmotic release tablets [16–19]. Therefore, the effect of food consumption was evaluated in a phase I study in which a single dose of the QD formulation, 24 mg was administered to healthy adult participants in both fed and fasted conditions to determine the effect of food on the pharmacokinetics of deutetrabenazine and its metabolites.

Methods

Study Designs

The first study (Study TV50717-PK-10175), was a phase I, open-label, randomized, four-period, four-sequence crossover study designed to assess the dosage strength proportionality of deutetrabenazine, and deuterated metabolites, α-HTBZ and β-HTBZ (individually and as a sum) as recommended in the FDA guidance for bioavailability studies [15] with relevant adaptations as described in the statistical section. Healthy male and female non-smoking adults aged 18–55 years who were extensive or intermediate CYP2D6 metabolizers were eligible for screening. The CYP2D6 genotyping was confirmed by testing prior to enrollment (either at screening or based on historical documentation). In case the CYP2D6 genotype was not previously determined, buccal swab collection samples were analyzed for CYP2D6 metabolizer status by Worldwide Clinical Trials, Austin, TX, USA. Those subjects meeting inclusion/exclusion criteria were randomly assigned to receive treatment in one of four treatment sequences of the QD formulation doses. The administered doses, 2 × 6 mg, 1 × 12 mg, 1 × 24 mg, and 2 × 24 mg were given under fed conditions to cover the recommended clinical dose range (6–48 mg) per day. See the statistical section for an explanation on why 2 × 6 mg for the 6-mg dosage strength and 2 × 24 mg doses were used for the 48-mg dosage strength.

Participants received a single dose of one of the deutetrabenazine doses, per their treatment sequence 30 min after intake of a standardized high-calorie, high-fat breakfast (approximately 1000 calories). Subjects had to remain seated for a minimum of 4 h after drug administration, except for positional changes required for protocol specified measurements. They were permitted to have non-mineral water ad libitum, except for 1 h prior to and 1 h after drug administration (240 mL of water provided with dosing and fluid provided with the standardized high-calorie, high-fat breakfast). In each administration period, blood samples (4 mL) were collected within approximately 30 min before breakfast and after deutetrabenazine administration at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, and 96 h. There was a 6-day washout between each administration period.

The second study (Study TV50717-BE-10165) was a phase I, single-dose, open-label crossover study. Healthy male and female non-smoking adults aged 18–55 years who were extensive or intermediate CYP2D6 metabolizers (as described previously) were eligible for screening; those meeting inclusion/exclusion criteria were randomly assigned to receive three treatments: a deutetrabenazine 24-mg QD formulation in the fed state (QD fed), a deutetrabenazine 24-mg QD formulation in the fasted state (QD fasted), and a deutetrabenazine BID formulation at a dose of 12 mg administered twice in a fed state in one of six treatment sequences. This article only describes the effect of food on the pharmacokinetics of deutetrabenazine and active metabolites following the QD fed (reference) and QD fasted (test) treatments. The fed state was chosen as a reference and fasted as a test because of the current dosing recommendations for the BID tablets to be taken with food, and the previous bioequivalence study compared the BID and QD deutetrabenazine tablets in the fed state [14].

All participants began fasting the evening before drug administration at least 10 h prior to dose with water available except for 1 h prior and 1 h after dose administration. Participants in the QD fed group were administered the dose in the morning, 30 min after consuming a high-calorie, high-fat breakfast (approximately 1000 calories). Participants in the QD fasted group were administered the drug dose with 240 mL of water in the morning like those in the QD fed group; however, they remained fasting until 4 h after dose administration. All subjects remained seated for a minimum of 4 h after the dose administration with the exception of positional changes required for protocol-specified measurements. Subjects were permitted to have non-mineral water ad libitum except for 1 h prior to and 1 h after drug administration. Blood samples (4 mL) were collected for determination of deutetrabenazine, deuterated α-HTBZ, and deuterated β-HTBZ metabolites before study drug administration on day 1 of periods 1, 2, and 3 and frequently over 96 h after administration of deutetrabenazine QD formulation, following the same sampling schedule as in the first study. There was a 6-day washout between each period. The pre-dose blood sample was taken within approximately 45 min of dosing for both the fed and fasted groups (≤15 min before the start of breakfast in the QD fed group). All blood samples were stored in a deep freezer set at − 70 °C (− 60 to − 80°C) at the clinic site, they were shipped to the bioanalytical site in dry ice to maintain temperatures below − 70 °C and stored at the bioanalytical site in a deep freezer set at − 70 °C (− 60 to − 80 °C) until analyzed.

Safety Assessments

Safety assessments were conducted throughout both studies either during the treatment period clinic visits or via telephone calls including adverse event reporting, vital signs, electrocardiograms, physical and neurological examinations, laboratory tests, assessment of suicidality, and recording of concomitant medication use.

Bioanalytical Procedures

Plasma concentrations of deutetrabenazine, deuterated α-HTBZ, and β-HTBZ were determined by a liquid chromatography-tandem mass-spectrometry analysis using solid-phase extraction at the Teva facility WPPL (Watson Pharma Pvt Ltd., Navi Mumbai, India). Further optimization of the bioanalytical methods was performed to ensure the sensitivity of the methods to detect deutetrabenazine and individual metabolites concentrations for the lowest doses. For the study on dosage strength proportionality, a 6500-mass spectrometry instrument interfaced with a high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) system fitted with a reverse-phase HPLC column was used and the mobile phase was organic solvent: buffer solution (60:40, v/v). For the second study on food effect, a 6500-mass spectrometry instrument interfaced with a HPLC system fitted with a reverse-phase HPLC column was used and the mobile phase was organic mixture: buffer solution (75:25 v/v). The developed chromatographic method was validated in terms of linearity, sensitivity, matrix effect, carry-over, accuracy, precision, recovery, robustness, and stability in accordance with FDA and European Medicines Agency guidelines [20, 21].

The method was validated for the first study, wherein linearity for deutetrabenazine, deuterated α-HTBZ, and β-HTBZ in plasma was obtained over the concentration range of 1–750, 30–60,000, and 30–60,000 pg/mL, respectively, with a coefficient of linear correlation (r) of >0.99. The lower limits of quantification were identifiable and reproducible at 1.070, 30.179, and 30.645 pg/mL. The precision of Matrix factor for analyte and internal standard was found to be less than 7%. No carryover was observed for all analytes and its internal standards. The within- and between-run precision values were below 11% and the accuracy had a range of 91.61–114.90%, 92.89–110.33%, and 96.68–105.00% at all three quality-control sample levels for the parent drug deutetrabenazine and its two deuterated metabolites. Overall recoveries were 80.80% and 87.38% for deutetrabenazine and its internal standard, 90.40% and 96.15% for deuterated α-HTBZ and its internal standard; and 90.11% and 93.55% for deuterated β-HTBZ and its internal standard. The precision of recovery for all analytes and internal standards was found to be less than 5%. Deutetrabenazine, deuterated α-HTBZ, and β-HTBZ were found to be stable in plasma samples under six freeze-thaw cycles, long-term storage (14 days at − 30 °C and 140 days at − 70 °C), short-term storage (20 h at room temperature), autosampler stability (161 h at − 5 °C), and stock-solution stability (13 days at 5 °C).

Similarly, the method was validated for the second study, wherein linearity for deutetrabenazine, deuterated α-HTBZ and β-HTBZ in plasma was obtained over the concentration ranges of 2.9–750, 255–100,000, and 257–100,000 pg/mL, respectively, with a coefficient of linear correlation (r) of >0.99. The lower limits of quantification were identifiable and reproducible at 2.905, 255.548, and 257.841 pg/mL. The precision of the Matrix factor for analyte and internal standard was found to be less than 7%. No carryover was observed for all analytes and its internal standards. The within-run and between-run precision values were below 12% and the accuracy ranges were 91.96–110.88%, 94.13–107.72%, and 91.37–110.41% at all three quality-control samples levels for the parent and its two metabolites. Overall recovery was 83.75% and 88.01% for deutetrabenazine and its internal standard; 87.74% and 86.58% for deuterated α-HTBZ and its internal standard; and 81.89% and 81.95% for deuterated β-HTBZ and its internal standard. Deutetrabenazine, deuterated α-HTBZ, and β-HTBZ were found to be stable in plasma samples under nine freeze-thaw cycles, long-term storage (181 days at − 30 °C and 180 days at − 70 °C), short-term storage (22 h at room temperature), autosampler stability (208 h at 5 °C), and stock-solution stability (14 days at 5 °C).

Pharmacokinetic (PK) Assessments

Pharmacokinetic (PK) parameters for deutetrabenazine, deuterated α-HTBZ and β-HTBZ (individually and as a sum) were calculated using standard non-compartmental methods of Phoenix WinNonlin Software (Version 7.0). Determination of deuterated total (α + β) HTBZ plasma concentrations was conducted by summing deuterated α-HTBZ and β-HTBZ plasma concentrations. If a sample only had values for 1 of the active metabolites (α-HTBZ or β-HTBZ), then total (α + β) HTBZ was assigned as “missing” for that timepoint and the sample was omitted in the PK analysis. For calculating pharmacokinetic parameters, a below lower limit of quantification value at time 0, at a sampling time before the first quantifiable concentration, or at a sampling time between quantifiable concentrations was treated as zero. All other below lower limit of quantification values (i.e., those occurring post-dose) were treated as missing. Missing values were ignored in PK parameter calculations (see the Methods section in the Electronic Supplementary Materials [ESM] for the number of missing values).

Statistical Analyses

In the first study, the primary objective was to assess the dose proportionality of exposure to deutetrabenazine, deuterated α-HTBZ and β-HTBZ (metabolites individually and as a sum) after single doses of the 6 mg, 12 mg, and 24 mg QD formulation tablets under fed conditions. The dosage strength proportionality study was based on the FDA recommendation to perform, in addition to the aforementioned study comparing the bioequivalence and bioavailability of the BID versus QD formulation [14], a dosage strength equivalence assessment or a dosage strength proportionality assessment in accordance with the guidance [15]. We chose a dosage strength proportionality study but modified it owing to limitations of the bioanalytical method that could affect the full PK characterization following administration of a single 6-mg tablet. Per the protocol and statistical analysis plan, it was required for the 2 × 6 mg dose to be found to be similar to the 1 × 12 mg dose to conclude dosage strength equivalence. This was a pre-requisite for these “halved-concentrations” to be included in the dosage strength proportionality analyses. For the assessment of dosage strength equivalence, comparisons of Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–∞ between two tablet strengths (2 × 6 mg vs 1 × 12 mg) were carried out using a mixed model with fixed effect terms for sequence, period, treatment/tablet strength, and a random effect of subject within sequence. The difference between the 2 × 6 mg (test) and 1 × 12 mg (reference) was evaluated by constructing 90% CIs for the test/reference ratios, based on the least-square means from the mixed model for the log transformed Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–∞. The geometric mean ratio (GMR) and the associated 90% confidence intervals (CIs) of the mixed model on the log scale were back-transformed to obtain the GMR between the two tablet strengths and the 90% CI for this ratio.

Following the assessment of the dose strength equivalence for 2 × 6 mg and 1 × 12 mg doses, the PK parameters for deutetrabenazine and its active metabolites following the 2 × 6 mg dose were dose normalized to reflect the strength of 1 × 6 mg rather than the 2 × 6 mg. For the assessment of dosage strength proportionality, a power model was fitted to describe the relationship between dose and PK parameters using a mixed model with fixed effects of sequence, period, and dose and a random effect of subject. For each PK parameter (Cmax, AUC0–36h and AUC0–∞), the slope of the regression line β was presented along with the 90% CI of the slope [22]. The primary analysis included three dose levels of 1 × 6 mg, 1 × 12 mg, and 1 × 24 mg and the acceptable interval for the 90% CI was 84–116%. A similar approach including data from the 2 × 24 mg treatment arm was used to demonstrate dosage strength proportionality up to the 48-mg dose level (including four dose strengths: 1 × 6 mg, 1 × 12 mg, 1 × 24 mg, and 2 × 24 mg), which was a secondary objective of the trial and the acceptable interval for the 90% CI was 84-111%. At the time of the study, a 48-mg tablet of the QD formulation was not available so the dosage strength of 48 mg was assessed using 2 × 24 mg administration. A subsequent study confirmed the dosage strength bioequivalence of the 2 × 24 mg and 48 mg administration (data on file, Teva Pharmaceuticals).

In the second study, the evaluation of an effect of food on the pharmacokinetics of all analytes between the QD fasted (test) and QD fed (reference) groups was based on comparisons of Cmax, AUC0–t and AUC0–∞ between treatments using a separate parametric mixed model for each PK parameter with sequence, period, and treatment as fixed effects and subject as a random effect. The choice of using the data from the fed state as a reference was based on the current dosing recommendations to take the deutetrabenazine BID formulation with food. The difference between the fed and fasted states were evaluated by constructing 90% CIs for the GMRs of the QD fasted(test)/QD fed(reference) least-square means for the log transformed Cmax, AUC0–t, and AUC0–∞.

Results

Participant Demographics, Disposition, and Safety

For the first study, 116 participants (56% male; 44% female) met entry criteria, received at least one dose of the QD formulation, and were evaluated for safety (demographics are provided in Table S1 of the ESM). The mean age of the participants was 40.9 years (range 19–55 years) and the majority were white [n = 99] (85%); 17 (15%) were black. Enrolled participants had a mean weight of 75.2 kg (range 50–99 kg) and a mean body mass index of 26.7 kg/m2 (range 19.8–29.9 kg/m2). A total of 103 completed the study and received all four doses. The 13 participants who discontinued had the following reasons: seven withdrawals by participant, two because of adverse events, and four due to other reasons. The adverse events that led to discontinuation were vomiting and coronavirus disease 2019 infection. The incidence of adverse events was similar when comparing the four dose administrations, with adverse events reported by 5 (4%), 3 (3%), 2 (2%), and 4 (4%) participants receiving 2 × 6 mg, 1 × 12 mg, 1 × 24 mg, and 2 × 24 mg doses, respectively. The only reports of treatment-related adverse events occurred in the 2 × 6 mg group (<1%); one incidence of headache and one of dizziness, both rated as mild in severity. Adverse events that occurred in more than one subject were influenza-like illness in three (3%) participants, and back pain in two (2%) participants.

For the second study, 84 participants met eligibility criteria; all 84 received all doses and completed the study (Table S1 of the ESM). The mean age of the participants was 38.4 years (range 18–55 years), the majority were white [n = 74] (88%); ten (12%) were black; and the percentages of men and women were similar (56% and 44%, respectively). Enrolled participants had a mean weight of 76.3 kg (range 53–99 kg) and a mean body mass index of 26.87 kg/m2 (range 18.6–30.0 kg/m2). The incidence of adverse events was overall low in the study and similar between the QD formulation 24-mg dose administrations in the fed and fasted conditions, two (2%) and four (5%) respectively. One treatment-related adverse event, headache mild in severity occurred in the 24-mg QD fed group (1%) and two events were reported in the 24-mg QD fasted group (2%), both headaches, moderate in severity. The most common adverse events reported in total were headache (three participants, 4%) and presyncope (two participants, 2%).

For both studies, there were no clinically meaningful trends observed for any clinical laboratory variables, vital sign parameters, or electrocardiogram findings. No deaths or serious adverse events, and no suicidal ideation or suicide attempts were reported.

Pharmacokinetics

Dose Proportionality of Three Dosage Strengths and Across Recommended Clinical Dose Range

The plasma concentrations for deutetrabenazine and the sum of the deuterated active metabolites (total [α + β]-HTBZ) following QD formulation administration using different dosage strengths in the first study is depicted in Fig. 1. The shapes of the mean plasma concentration–time profiles were similar across the clinical dose range and demonstrated increasing concentrations with increasing doses of the QD formulation. For all doses, the rise in plasma concentrations was smooth for deutetrabenazine and total (α + β)-HTBZ, and the median tmax was approximately 3 h post-dose administration. After reaching a plateau with a duration of approximately 12 h, the disposition of both deutetrabenazine and deuterated total (α + β)-HTBZ was bi-phasic. Notably, the plasma concentration–time curves after administration of 2 × 6 mg and 1 × 12 mg doses of the QD formulation were essentially superimposable.

The Effect of Food on the Pharmacokinetics of the Deutetrabenazine Once-Daily (QD) Formulation

In the food-effect study, participants were administered single doses of the deutetrabenazine QD formulation in fed (reference) and fasted (test) conditions to assess the effect of food on the pharmacokinetics of deutetrabenazine and the deuterated metabolites (geometric means and coefficients of variation are shown in Table S3 of the ESM). Plots of deutetrabenazine and deuterated total (α + β)-HTBZ mean plasma concentrations are shown in Fig. 3. For both analytes, in the fasted state, two peaks were discernable, with peak plasma concentrations slightly lower in the fasted state compared with those in the fed state. In the fed state, the rise in concentration of deuterated total (α + β)-HTBZ occurred more smoothly and resulted in a single peak; the decline in total (α + β)-HTBZ plasma concentrations after a plasma concentration plateau of several hours was biphasic and similar in both fasted and fed states.

The effect of food on the exposure of the QD formulation of deutetrabenazine was assessed by assessing the relative bioavailability for Cmax, AUC0–t and AUC0–∞ following single-dose administration of the QD formulation 24-mg dose under the fasted (test) and fed (reference) conditions. Discussion and Conclusions

These two studies occurred during the development of the QD formulation of deutetrabenazine and were part of its basis of approval by the FDA in 2023. Using a modified dosage strength proportionality assessment, the dosage strength proportionality of the new QD formulation was evaluated for three dosage strengths (6 mg, 12 mg, and 24 mg) and across the approved dose range (6–48 mg) and the effect of food on exposure was characterized to inform dosing recommendations.

Following single-dose administration of the QD formulation mean plasma concentration–time profiles were similar for all analytes across the dosage strengths and the clinical dosing range, and demonstrated an initial rise in plasma concentrations, followed by sustained plateau of several hours at each dose level with a multi-phasic disposition phase. Dosage strength proportionality was shown for the three dosage strengths for AUC0-36h and AUC0-∞ for deutetrabenazine and the deuterated metabolites. In the dosage strength proportionality analyses of the full clinical range, the 90% CIs for the PK parameters of the parent drug, deutetrabenazine, did not include 1; however, it was entirely within the prespecified bounds, and the study met the dose proportionality criterion for the evaluated dose range. The GMR of 0.96 is close to 1, indicating near proportionality with a slight trend toward sub‑proportionality. This trend is not considered to be clinically relevant. It is important to keep in mind that deutetrabenazine is rapidly metabolized to its deuterated metabolites and shows an exposure that is <1% of the exposure of the metabolites. Furthermore, exposure of the parent drug is more variable. The 90% CIs for the PK parameters for the deuterated metabolites that are responsible for the treatment effect include 1. Additionally, as dose proportionality was demonstrated across the full clinical range for the deuterated metabolites, the dosing recommendations for the QD formulation can follow the same titration schedule as the BID formulation.

Another important attribute is that patients can take the QD formulation with or without food. In the second study, the effect of food on the pharmacokinetics of the QD formulation was assessed to see whether there were differences in exposure when taken in the presence of food. A previous study of the BID formulation of deutetrabenazine found that food had no effect on the exposure (AUC0–t and AUC0–∞) of the deuterated active metabolites; however, there was an approximately 50% increase in the Cmax in the presence of food [12]. Consequently, the dosing regimen used in the clinical program for the approved BID formulation is recommended to be administered in the fed state. However, for the osmotic tablet technology used in the QD formulation, it was hypothesized that the absence of food would have a minimal impact on exposure for the QD formulation [16]. In the present study, following the administration of a single QD formulation tablet 24 mg under fasted and fed conditions, all analytes met bioequivalence criteria. As no significant food effect on the pharmacokinetics of deutetrabenazine or its active deuterated metabolites was evident, the QD formulation can be taken irrespective of meals. This flexibility in taking the QD formulation of deutetrabenazine with or without food may contribute to the convenience of the medication. In a survey of patients newly initiated to the deutetrabenazine QD formulation, preferences for when they took their medication and whether they took it with or without food varied, and more than half of participants reported that they always or usually took it without food [23, 24]. Overall, most participants in the survey reported that the QD formulation of deutetrabenazine was easy to use and to include in their daily routine.

In both studies, described here, the overall incidence of adverse events following single-dose administration of the QD formulation was very low and there were no reports of serious adverse events. No clinically meaningful trends were reported for clinical laboratory results, vital sign measurements, and electrocardiogram results. No new safety issues are expected with the administration of the QD formulation. As the BID formulation and QD formulation of deutetrabenazine follow the same dosing regimens, with a 12-mg starting dose and a once-weekly dose increase based on individual tolerability and pharmacological response, the more sustained plasma concentrations over a dosing interval from the QD tablets are not anticipated to alter the tolerability or safety of the drug. Additionally, in exposure analyses using phase III data in patients with TD or HD chorea, the predicted efficacy of the QD formulation on abnormal movement severity was similar to that of the BID formulation administered at comparable exposures [25]. Taken together, these analyses support the multiple dosing and administration options of deutetrabenazine.

There are several methods described in the literature to evaluate dose proportionality [22, 26, 27] and a power model using a mixed model with fixed and random effects was selected for this study. Although our findings advance insights on the dose proportionality of deutetrabenazine and its metabolites, the study should be interpreted in light of certain limitations. It must be acknowledged that PK data were collected using a 2 × 6 mg dose instead of a 1 × 6 mg dose, thus this analysis holds true under the assumption of dose proportionality from 6 to 12 mg. To overcome the bioanalytical limitations of the predicted low concentrations following a single 6-mg tablet, the dosage strength equivalence was evaluated for the lowest two dose strengths before dose proportionality was assessed [15]. Hence, the dosage strength equivalence was first demonstrated between 2 × 6 mg and 1 × 12 mg, allowing for the evaluation of the proportionality for the three dosage strengths and over the full clinical dose range.

It is also important to note that at the time of these studies, a 48-mg tablet was not available; a dosage strength of 48 mg was subsequently developed, and a dosage strength equivalence of 2 × 24 mg and 48 mg was confirmed in a later study (data on file, Teva Pharmaceuticals). The studies included additional limitations. The participant populations were healthy volunteers who were largely (≥85%) white and had mean ages of approximately 40 years with an upper limit of 55 years. This may limit the generalizability of the results to individuals with different racial/ethnic backgrounds, or older patients. The studies only used a standardized high-calorie, high-fat meal as the fed condition and did not investigate other meal types; however, in a previous study, a single 18-mg dose of deutetrabenazine administered with a high-fat, high-calorie meal demonstrated PK parameters that were bioequivalent to those observed with a standard meal [12].

Overall, the results of these two studies indicate that the QD formulation of deutetrabenazine exhibits dose-proportional pharmacokinetics for dosage strengths up to 48 mg, and no new safety concerns emerged. Additionally, following QD formulation administration, there was no effect of food on the pharmacokinetics of deutetrabenazine and its deuterated metabolites, thus it can be taken with or without food.
