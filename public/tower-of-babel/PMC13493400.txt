Trends and future projections of Hodgkin and Non-Hodgkin lymphoma burden in Chinese children and adolescents (1990–2043): insights from the GBD 2023 database
Authors: Yanchao Li, Zhenglian Yao, Yiming Yang, Jianjuan Ma, Honglan Yang, Fufei Dai, Linqiang Shen, Changqun He, Rong Gao, Junxiang Li, Xiaoshan Shao, Xiaoyan Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To analyze the spatiotemporal trends and predict the future burden of Hodgkin lymphoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma among children and adolescents in China from 1990 to 2023, using data from the Global Burden of Disease 2023 database. Data on the incidence, prevalence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years for Hodgkin Lymphoma and Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma were extracted from the Global Burden of Disease 2023 dataset. The analysis targeted children and adolescents aged under 19 years, stratified by sex and age groups: under 5 years, 5–9 years, 10–14 years, and 15–19 years. The average annual percentage change and its 95% uncertainty interva were calculated using Joinpoint regression analysis to evaluate trends. Additionally, time-series modeling and forecasting were performed using the autoregressive integrated moving average model to analyze the age-standardized incidence rate, age-standardized prevalence rate, age-standardized mortality rate, and age-standardized DALY rate. The incidence and mortality rates of Hodgkin Lymphoma decreased significantly from 1990 to 2023, with age-standardized incidence rate declining from 0.650 per 100,000 to 0.322 per 100,000 (95% UI: -2.237 to -1.780) and age-standardized mortality rate from 0.531 per 100,000 to 0.107 per 100,000 (95% UI: -4.914 to -4.448). However, the prevalence of Hodgkin Lymphoma increased, reflecting improved survival rates. Higher burden was observed in males, particularly in the 15–19 age group. The incidence and prevalence of Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma increased significantly, with age-standardized incidence rate rising from 3.896 per 100,000 to 5.174 per 100,000 (95% UI: 0.615 to 1.390) and age-standardized prevalence rate from 12.476 per 100,000 to 34.793 per 100,000 (95% UI: 3.009 to 3.395). Mortality and DALY rates declined, but the absolute number of deaths increased due to population growth. Higher burden was observed in males across all age groups. Time series modeling using the autoregressive integrated moving average model predicts that over the next two decades, the incidence, mortality, and DALY rates of Hodgkin Lymphoma and Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma will continue to decline. This study highlights the distinct epidemiological patterns of Hodgkin Lymphoma and Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma among children and adolescents in China. While the incidence and mortality rates of Hodgkin Lymphoma decreased significantly from 1990 to 2023, Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma exhibited an increasing incidence, though its mortality rate also declined over the same period. The results offer valuable insights for policymaking and the development of health service delivery strategies aimed at optimizing acute therapeutic access for Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma and long-term survivorship care for Hodgkin Lymphoma in pediatric lymphoma patients.

Introduction

China, with a population exceeding 1.4 billion and approximately 250 million children and adolescents under 14 years of age, represents the world’s largest pediatric population [1]. Consequently, disease burden patterns in this country carry substantial global implications for healthcare resource allocation and policy planning. In recent years, the incidence of malignant lymphoma has significantly increased, ranking among the top ten most prevalent cancers globally [2]. Lymphoma is a relatively common type of cancer, ranking among the top 10–15 most frequently diagnosed cancers globally and in China [3, 4]. A report from the National Central Cancer Registry of China (NCCRC) estimated that in 2015, lymphoid neoplasms accounted for 2.1% of all newly diagnosed cancer cases (88,200 cases) and 1.9% of all cancer-related deaths (52,100 deaths) [4]. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), a hematologic malignancy originating from lymphocytes, accounts for 90% of all lymphoma cases [5]. Pediatric lymphoma is a highly aggressive malignant tumor that often presents with nonspecific early symptoms, hindering timely detection and resulting in delayed diagnosis for a significant proportion of cases [6, 7]. Unlike solid tumors, lymphomas and other non-solid malignancies typically exhibit extensive systemic involvement, including bone marrow infiltration and central nervous system dissemination, which further complicates treatment strategies [6]. Lymphoma not only imposes a tremendous emotional and financial burden on affected children but also on their families and the broader healthcare system [8, 9].

Previous Global Burden of Disease (GBD)-based studies have predominantly examined the global burden of lymphoma in adults, and while some have explored childhood lymphoma at the worldwide level, research specifically focusing on Chinese children and adolescents remains scarce [10–13]. Consequently, this study aims to evaluate the burden of lymphoma among children and adolescents in China. While GBD analyses have documented worldwide lymphoma trends, China’s distinctive demographic scale, rapid economic development, and unique healthcare delivery system necessitate dedicated national evaluation. The findings from this study not only inform domestic policy but also contribute to global understanding of lymphoma burden in large, transitioning populations.

This study aims to conduct a comprehensive analysis of lymphoma in China utilizing the most recent data from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023. Specifically, we examined the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR), age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR), and age-standardized disability-adjusted life year (DALY) rate (ASDR). Furthermore, we made future projections, including predicted values for ASIR, ASPR, ASMR, and ASDR related to Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) and non-Hodgkin lymphoma by the year 2043.

Materials and methods

Data source

The data utilized in this study were extracted from the GBD 2023 dataset, a comprehensive database that documents the incidence, prevalence, mortality, and DALYs rates. This study examined the national burden of HL and NHL in China, with a particular emphasis on children. The analysis stratified the data by sex and age groups, specifically targeting those under 5 years, 5–9 years, 10–14 years, and 15–19 years.

The data pertaining to leukemia in this study were obtained from the Global Health Data Exchange (GHDx) and its associated tools (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool). We employed the GBD tool to extract data on the incidence rate, prevalence rate, mortality rate, and DALYs for China from 1990 to 2023, which served as metrics for evaluating the burden of HL and NHL. This study utilised publicly available, de-identified data from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023. No individual patient data were accessed. As this research constitutes secondary analysis of existing, anonymised population-level data, ethical review by an institutional review board and informed consent were not required, in accordance with standard guidelines for research using publicly available datasets.

In this study, disease burden was quantified using four age-standardized metrics from GBD framework. Incidence refers to the number of newly diagnosed cases within a specific time period, and ASIR represents the number of new cases per 100,000 population after adjusting for age structure. Prevalence denotes the total number of existing cases at a given point in time, encompassing both newly diagnosed and previously identified individuals; ASPR measures existing cases per 100,000 population after age standardization, indicating the size of the population requiring clinical surveillance and follow-up care. Mortality refers to the number of deaths attributable to a specific disease, and ASMR represents deaths per 100,000 population following age adjustment, capturing the fatality risk of the disease. DALYs quantify the total healthy life years lost due to premature mortality and disability combined; ASDR expresses these total healthy life years lost per 100,000 population after age standardization, serving as a composite indicator of the overall health impact. Together, these four metrics captured distinct yet complementary dimensions of lymphoma burden—ASIR reflected disease onset risk, ASPR reflected the cumulative patient population requiring healthcare engagement, ASMR reflected lethal outcomes, and ASDR provided a comprehensive measure of total population health loss—enabled a nuanced assessment of temporal trends and facilitating cross-population comparisons by eliminating confounding from differential age structures.

Statistical analysis

We analyzed the incidence, prevalence, mortality, DALYs, and ASIR, ASPR, ASMR, and ASDR of HL and NHL in China using data from the GBD database. Additionally, we examined the crude incidence rate (CIR), crude prevalence rate (CPR), crude mortality rate (CMR), and crude DALY rate (CDR) for each age group. The average annual percentage change (AAPC) and its corresponding 95% uncertainty interval (95% UI) were calculated using Joinpoint software (National Cancer Institute, Rockville, MD, USA) to assess the trends in disease burden. The logarithmic age-standardized indicators were fitted into a regression model represented by ln(y) = α + βx + ε, where y denotes the respective age-standardized indicator, x denotes the calendar year, α is the intercept, β is the slope coefficient (i.e., the average annual change in ln(y) per unit increase in x), and ε represents the random error term assumed to follow a normal distribution with mean zero. The AAPC was calculated as 100 × (exp(β) − 1), with the 95% uncertainty interval (UI) also derivable from the model. An AAPC estimate with a 95% UI > 0 indicated an increasing trend in the age-standardized indicator, < 0 indicates a decreasing trend, and if it includes 0, it signified a stable trend. Additionally, we employed the autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model to conduct time-series modeling and forecasting for four age-standardized indicators of HL and NHL in China from 1990 to 2023: ASPR, ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR. Residual normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. While some models showed significant deviation from normality (p < 0.05), this was common in epidemiological time series data characterized by skewed distributions and structural changes. ARIMA models were known to be robust to moderate non-normality, particularly with sample sizes > 30, as the asymptotic properties of maximum likelihood estimators remained valid. Furthermore, the Ljung-Box test confirmed white noise residuals for all models, indicating adequate model specification. Bootstrap methods were considered for confidence interval estimation if strict normality is required, but this was deemed unnecessary given the satisfactory diagnostic performance.

The statistical analysis and visualization of the data in this study were conducted using R statistical software (version 4.5.1) and Joinpoint software (version 4.5.0). A p-value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

Incidence of HL and NHL

China, with a population exceeding 1.4 billion and approximately 250 million children and adolescents under 14 years of age, represents the world’s largest pediatric population [1]. The total number of HL cases in China decreased from 6,639 (95% UI: 3,012–9,043) in 1990 to 5,841 (95% UI: 3,882–7,678) in 2023, indicating a cumulative reduction of 12.02%. The ASIR of HL in China declined from 0.650 (95% UI: 0.295–0.894) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 0.322 (95% UI: 0.219–0.426) per 100,000 population in 2023. Concurrently, the AAPC of the incidence rate of HL in China decreased by 2.009% (95% UI: −2.237 to −1.780) from 1990 to 2023 (Table 1).

The total number of NHL cases in China increased from 38,267 (95% UI: 31,009–48,523) in 1990 to 108,328 (95% UI: 72,251–157,786) in 2023, indicating a cumulative increase of 183.08%. The ASIR of NHL in China rose from 3.896 (95% UI: 3.154–4.945) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 5.174 (95% UI: 3.437–7.549) per 100,000 population in 2023. Concurrently, the AAPC of the incidence rate of NHL in China increased by 1.002% (95% UI: 0.615 to 1.390) from 1990 to 2023 (Table 1).

Prevalence of HL and NHL

The prevalence of HL in China increased from 17,830 (95% UI: 7,852–25,198) in 1990 to 32,456 (95% UI: 22,189–45,418) in 2023, with a cumulative increase of 82.03%. The ASPR of HL in China rose from 1.582 (95% UI: 0.693–2.236) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 1.937 (95% UI: 1.322–2.744) per 100,000 population in 2023. The AAPC of the prevalence rate of HL in China increased by 0.762% (95% UI: 0.583 to 0.940) from 1990 to 2023 (Table 1).

The prevalence of NHL in China increased from 131,490 (95% UI: 82,676–212,826) in 1990 to 715,197 (95% UI: 394,476–1,150,336) in 2023, with a cumulative increase of 443.92%. The ASPR of NHL in China rose from 12.476 (95% UI: 7.840–20.174) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 34.793 (95% UI: 19.453–55.540) per 100,000 population in 2023. The AAPC of the prevalence rate of NHL in China increased by 3.201% (95% UI: 3.009 to 3.395) from 1990 to 2023 (Table 1).

Mortality of HL and NHL

The number of HL-related deaths in China decreased from 5,094 (95% UI: 2,325–6,960) in 1990 to 2,243 (95% UI: 1,493–2,963) in 2023, reflecting a cumulative decrease of 55.97%. The ASMR of HL in China also showed a decline, from 0.531 (95% UI: 0.246–0.734) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 0.107 (95% UI: 0.072–0.141) per 100,000 population in 2023. Furthermore, the AAPC of the mortality rate of HL in China decreased by 4.682% (95% UI: −4.914 to −4.448) from 1990 to 2023 (Table 1).

While the absolute number of NHL-related deaths in China increased from 25,918 (95% UI: 21,870–30,855) in 1990 to 39,905 (95% UI: 33,970–46,638) in 2023, the ASMR exhibited a contrasting downward trend. Specifically, the ASMR declined from 2.758 (95% UI: 2.311–3.276) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 1.851 (95% UI: 1.590–2.166) per 100,000 population in 2023. This improvement in survival risk was further evidenced by the AAPC, which showed a significant annual decrease of 1.154% (95% UI: −1.407 to −0.900) over the study period (Table 1).

DALYs of HL and NHL

The number of DALYs caused by HL in China decreased from 207,715 (95% UI: 94,737–286,633) in 1990 to 67,380 (95% UI: 46,774–89,518) in 2023, indicating a cumulative reduction of 67.56%. The ASDR of HL in China declined from 18.972 (95% UI: 8.681–25.774) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 3.659 (95% UI: 2.639–4.823) per 100,000 population in 2023. The AAPC of the DALY rate of HL in China decreased by 4.813% (95% UI: −5.021 to −4.605) from 1990 to 2023 (Table 1).

The number of DALYs caused by NHL in China increased from 1,034,453 (95% UI: 876,108–1,242,438) in 1990 to 1,158,268 (95% UI: 992,048–1,343,536) in 2023, reflecting a cumulative increase of 11.97%. The ASDR of NHL in China declined from 97.082 (95% UI: 82.521–116.631) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 59.588 (95% UI: 51.544–69.309) per 100,000 population in 2023. The AAPC of the DALY rate of NHL in China decreased by 1.447% (95% UI: −1.686 to – 1.208) from 1990 to 2023 (Table 1).

Trends in the burden of HL and NHL

The age-standardized rates of HL among children and adolescents in China demonstrated a significant decline over the study period. The ASIR decreased from approximately 0.650 per 100,000 in 1990 to about 0.322 per 100,000 in 2023, indicating a substantial reduction in new cases. Conversely, the age-standardized rates of NHL exhibited an upward trend. The ASIR for NHL rose from about 3.896 per 100,000 in 1990 to approximately 5.174 per 100,000 in 2023, suggesting an increase in new cases. The ASPR for NHL also increased significantly, indicating a growing prevalence of this disease. The DALYs rate for NHL initially decreased significantly but had slowed in recent years (Figs. 3).

Burden of HL and NHL in different age groups

From 1990 to 2023, the ASIR, ASPR, ASMR, and DALYs rate of HL and NHL exhibited distinct trends across various age groups. For HL, adolescents aged 15 to 19 years experienced significant increases in ASIR and ASPR, while ASMR and ASDR also displayed upward trends, indicating a growing disease burden within this demographic. In comparison to 1990, all metrics, except for ASPR—including ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR—showed marked declines by 2023. In contrast, the changes in ASMR and ASDR for NHL across all age groups exhibited a more complex, wave-like pattern. Compared to 1990, both ASMR and ASDR in 2023 showed significant declines (Figs. 4 and 5).

Discussion

This study provides the assessment of the significant and increasing burden of lymphoma in China. The standardized estimation methodology for lymphoma metrics utilized in the GBD study facilitates a comparative analysis between HL and NHL. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive evaluation of the incidence, prevalence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years associated with HL and NHL in China over the past 30 years, utilizing data from the GBD 2023 database. Furthermore, we projected the trends for the next 20 years. We analyzed the differences in disease burden between HL and NHL across various age groups and sexes in China. The results indicate that from 1990 to 2023, China experienced declines in ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR for HL, while ASPR exhibited an upward trend. This pattern is consistent with global trends observed for HL [14]. In contrast, the ASMR and ASDR for NHL demonstrated a downward trend, whereas the ASIR and ASPR for NHL showed an upward trend, reflecting global changes in NHL [10].

In our study, the burden patterns of HL and NHL among Chinese adolescents varied by age and sex. Similar to most cancers in China, ASIR, ASPR, ASMR, and ASDR of HL gradually increased with age, while the incidence and prevalence of NHL exhibited an opposite trend, decreasing with advancing age. In all age groups of HL and NHL, males exhibited significantly higher ASIR, ASPR, ASMR, and ASDR compared to females [10, 15]. Notably, lymphoma has been identified as one of the cancer types among both males and females aged 0–14 years [15, 16]. Previous studies have demonstrated that the incidence and mortality rates of HL and NHL are predominantly higher in males [15]. Given the limited established modifiable risk factors for childhood lymphoma, emphasis should be placed on timely diagnostic evaluation, standardised treatment protocols, and long-term survivorship care—particularly for the expanding HL survivor population and the rising number of NHL cases requiring complex therapeutic intervention.

In comparison to 1990, the burden of HL among the 0–19 age group in China in 2023 exhibited divergent trends across metrics: the age-standardized incidence rate, mortality rate, and DALY rate all declined significantly (AAPC: −2.009%, −4.682%, and −4.813%, respectively), while the age-standardized prevalence rate has increased by 0.762%. Notably, the age-standardized mortality rate of HL in 2023 has decreased to 0.107 per 100,000, which is less than a quarter of the rate in 1990. As of 2023, the age-standardized incidence rate of NHL reached 5.174 per 100,000, representing a 1.002% increase since 1990, while the prevalence demonstrated a more pronounced rise (average annual percentage change +3.201%). The divergence between rising ASPR and modest ASIR growth is consistent with prolonged survival among NHL patients, resulting in an expanding survivor population requiring ongoing clinical surveillance. However, the absolute number of NHL-related deaths increased from 25,918 to 39,905. This suggests that despite the declining age-standardized rates indicating improved survival, the actual burden on clinical resources and healthcare expenditures remains substantial and is intensifying due to population growth [17].

From 1990 to 2023, the persistent increase in the prevalence of HL and NHL may be inherently multifactorial, driven by several contributing factors. Furthermore, the globalization of Western diets has resulted in a significant rise in obesity rates. Instead, efforts should focus on improving recognition of early clinical manifestations—such as persistent lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, or systemic symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss)—among primary care physicians and pediatricians, coupled with streamlined referral pathways to pediatric oncology centers.

Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) is a widely prevalent viral pathogen that affects approximately 90% of the global population [18]. Among hematologic malignancies, EBV is closely associated with both HL and NHL [19–21]. Given the close association between lymphoma and immune dysfunction, particularly in relation to viral infections such as EBV, it is plausible that improvements in public health interventions have significantly influenced these trends [22, 23]. In recent years, multiple studies have demonstrated that CAR-T cell therapy, PD-1 inhibitors, immunosuppressants, and EBV vaccines exhibit significant efficacy and favorable safety profiles in lymphoma treatment [24–26]. Emerging immune-targeted therapies, such as bispecific T cell engagers and CAR-T cell therapy, have proven effective in the treatment of relapsed and refractory lymphoma [27]. In NHL, a pivotal trial of epcoritamab (CD3xCD20 bispecific antibody) enrolled 36 patients, with three-year progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) estimates of 25% and 39% in the overall population, compared with 53% and 71% among complete responders, highlighting durable benefit in patients achieving deep remission [25]. EBV-associated DLBCL exhibits higher PD-L1 expression levels compared with EBV-negative tumors, suggesting the presence of immune evasion mechanisms in this malignancy [28]. Due to the upregulation of the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway in EBV-associated DLBCL, PD-1/PD-L1 blockade is anticipated to enhance the efficacy of CAR T-cell therapy [29]. A meta-analysis including 16 studies involving a total of 1,347 patients demonstrated that CAR-T cell therapy improved the CR rate in NHL [30]. A clinical study confirmed that after CAR T cell infusion, circulating PD-1 + CAR+ CD8 + T cells are highly correlated with response to CAR T cell therapy in NHL [31]. Through epigenetic modification, enhancing anti-PD-1 efficacy in HL to eliminate persistent Hodgkin and Reed-Sternberg cell (HRSC) populations, thereby achieving successful treatment of HL patients [32]. Additionally, EBV-targeted vaccine approaches and T-cell engineering therapies directed against EBV latent proteins LMP1, LMP2, and/or EBNA1 aim to enhance the specificity and efficacy of treatment for EBV-associated malignancies [33]. In summary, these three therapeutic approaches have substantially advanced the prognosis of lymphoma.

In China, the ASIR of childhood HL is decreasing, while the cure rate using modern risk - adapted protocols is high. This epidemiological characteristic shifts the clinical focus to the care of long - term survivors. In contrast, the incidence rate of childhood NHL is increasing, but much lower than that of HL. Furthermore, the annual prevalence growth rate of NHL, which stands at 3.201%, significantly surpasses the global average, indicating the presence of unique gene-environment interactions that urgently necessitate dedicated prospective etiological cohort studies in children [10]. Therefore, the management of NHL in China places greater emphasis on timely diagnostic evaluation, access to centralized multidisciplinary care, and intensive treatment protocols, rather than long - term chronic management.

We analyzed actual GBD 2023 estimates for the period 1990–2023 to characterize historical trends in four age-standardized indicators (ASIR, ASPR, ASMR, ASDR). Subsequently, we fitted ARIMA time series models to project these trends forward to 2043, generating forecasts with 80% and 95% uncertainty intervals. The ASPR of HL increased during 1990–2023 and is projected to continue increasing annually, while the ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR declined during the study period and are expected to gradually decline further. In contrast, the ASIR of NHL remained relatively stable during 1990–2023 and is predicted to remain stable, whereas the ASPR, ASMR, and ASDR decreased during the study period and are anticipated to decrease year by year until 2043. Although the incidence and prevalence rates of both HL and NHL increased in the most recent years of the study period, they are projected to decline steadily after 2023.

These projections carry uncertainties; actual future trends may be influenced by changes in healthcare delivery, diagnostic practices, or demographic structure. These statistical extrapolations should not be interpreted as causal predictions. The expanding pediatric oncology capacity in China may influence future lymphoma outcomes, though this cannot be quantified with the present data.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the ARIMA forecasting model is inherently more accurate for short-term projections; our 20-year horizon (2024–2043) introduces increasing uncertainty as temporal distance from the observed data extends, with widening prediction intervals that may limit practical utility. Second, the divergence between declining age-standardized mortality rates and rising absolute death numbers for NHL reflects the interplay between population growth and epidemiological trends—a pattern that our model describes but does not explain causally. Third, the interpretation of rising HL prevalence alongside declining incidence as “improved survival” represents an inference from population-level data rather than direct evidence from individual patient outcomes or treatment records. Finally, GBD estimates for pediatric lymphoma in China may be affected by regional underreporting or diagnostic variability, though this limitation pertains to the input data rather than our analytical approach.

Conclusion

This study presents an analysis of the spatiotemporal variation in the burden of HL and NHL among children and adolescents in China from 1990 to 2023, utilizing data from the GBD 2023 database. Our findings reveal distinct epidemiological patterns and trends for HL and NHL in this population. The prevalence of HL has increased alongside declining incidence and mortality rates, a pattern consistent with prolonged survival in this patient group. Higher incidence and mortality rates were observed in males, particularly in the 15–19 age group. While age-standardized mortality and DALY rates of NHL have declined, the absolute number of NHL-related deaths has increased, which is associated with population growth and rising incidence. Similar to HL, higher incidence rates of NHL were observed in males across all age groups.

The findings of this study provide insights into the burden of lymphoma among children and adolescents in China and facilitate the monitoring of temporal trends within this population. The observed sex disparities and the distinct epidemiological patterns between HL and NHL underscore the need for further investigation into the underlying determinants. These results may inform policymaking regarding disease surveillance, healthcare resource allocation, and the development of management strategies, particularly in addressing the increasing number of NHL cases and the follow-up care needs of the growing HL survivor population.
