1. Source commented that Regiment 2h had captured 12 American soldiers after
five engagements during March through May 1966 in KONTUM Province, SVN,
Four soldiers were taken near National Route LWA in TU MA DONG District
(a Highland name for the area TOUMOURONG on the map). Three Americans
were captured in DAC TO District. The remaining five were caught in May,
three at TU MA DONG District and two in District 80.

2. Cadre leaders told the Source's unit that three POWs were seized approxi-
mately 2) or 25 May during the VC attack of TU MA DONG Post. The soldiers
were members of a relief element of "US Airlift Division One" which had
been flown into the area by helicopters after the VC attacked the Post.

3. Source's company captured two US soldiers on or about 28 - 31 May in
District €0, KONTUM Province. NGOC LINH Mountain was the only prominent
reference point in the District which Source remembered. The POWs were
also members of "US Airlift Division One". Source commented that both
soldiers wore patches on their sleeves which resembled "the blade of a
sword" and ‘an upside down: V". Source saw these two POWs. One POW was
Caucasian and had brown hair. Source did not remember the color of his
eyes. He was over six feet tall and weighed less than 190 pounds. Source
recalled a scar or an "unsmooth surface" on one cheek of the individual.
This POW was not wounded. The other POW was either of Caucasian or Oriental
descent. He had a very high nose but had eyes "like a Vietnamese". He
had dark black hair, brown eyes, was over six feet tall and weighed over
200 pounds. This soldier was wounded in his right arm and leg, but his °
injury was described as non-critical. Source said he heard that the
majority of the 12 prisoners were wounded, but he did not know the serious-.

ness of their injuries.

lh. Source did not know the ultimate disposition of the captives. Unit policy
prohibited their detaining prisoners for more than five days. The two
POWs he saw were taken to District 80. He surmised that they would be. .
sent from there either to NVN or to a detention camp in Military Region V
(MR-5) in the Western Highlands. Cadres tgld the Source's unit that there
was a POW camp in MR-V which contained over 300 US personnel.
