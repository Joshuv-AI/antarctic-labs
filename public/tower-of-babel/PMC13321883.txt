Efficacy and safety of cabozantinib plus nivolumab in advanced non-clear cell renal cell carcinoma: a nationwide multicenter study
Authors: Chang Gon Kim, Junkyu Kim, In-Ho Kim, Eun Hee Jung, Hyeon-Su Im, Byung Woog Kang, Ji Hyun Park, Joo-Hwan Park, Jungmin Jo, Eo Jin Kim, Seung Hoon Beom, Sun Young Rha, Se Hyun Kim, Inkeun Park, Se Hoon Park, Jae-Lyun Lee, Sang Joon Shin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTNon-clear cell renal cell carcinoma (nccRCC) represents a biologically heterogeneous group of tumors for which optimal systemic treatment strategies remain poorly defined despite recent therapeutic advances. To address this gap, we evaluated the clinical activity and safety of cabozantinib plus nivolumab in patients with advanced nccRCC. In this nationwide, multicenter retrospective cohort study conducted in the Republic of Korea, 62 patients received the combination across multiple lines of therapy, with most treated in the first-line setting. Papillary RCC was the most common histologic subtype (50.0%), followed by FH-deficient (22.6%), TFE3-rearranged (9.7%), chromophobe (6.5%), collecting duct (6.5%), and unclassified tumors (4.8%). With a median follow-up of 22.2 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 15.4–29.1), the objective response rate (ORR) was 45.2% (95% CI, 32.4–57.9) and the disease control rate (DCR) was 93.6% (95% CI, 87.3–99.8). Responses were seen across histologic subtypes (ORR = 48.4% in papillary, 50.0% in FH-deficient, 50.0% in TFE3-rearranged, 25.0% in chromophobe, 25.0% in collecting duct, and 33.3% in unclassified tumors; P = 0.876). Median progression-free and overall survival were 9.7 months (95% CI, 7.7–11.6) and 22.0 months (95% CI, 15.2–28.7), respectively. Grade 3–4 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 46.8% of patients, and treatment discontinuation due to toxicity was reported in 6.5%. These findings demonstrate that cabozantinib plus nivolumab provides clinically meaningful and durable antitumor activity with manageable toxicity across diverse histologic subtypes in advanced nccRCC, supporting further prospective evaluation of cabozantinib plus nivolumab in advanced nccRCC.

Introduction

Nonclear cell renal cell carcinoma (nccRCC) accounts for approximately 20–25% of all renal cell carcinoma (RCC) cases and encompasses a remarkably heterogeneous group of tumors. 1 , 2 In the 2022 WHO 5th edition classification, more than a dozen distinct molecular and histologic nccRCC entities are recognized—including papillary, chromophobe, fumarate hydratase–deficient (FH-deficient), translocation-associated, and collecting duct carcinomas, as well as multiple newly defined molecularly driven subtypes. 3 , 4 These tumors exhibit unique genetic alterations, biological behaviors, and prognostic implications. 5‐7 However, therapeutic strategies are still largely extrapolated from clear cell RCC due to the limited development of subtype-specific evidence. Prospective studies rarely enroll sufficient patients with individual nccRCC subtypes, and most available data derive from small, single-arm phase II trials lacking randomized comparisons. 8 , 9

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) monotherapy targeting programmed death-1 (PD-1), such as nivolumab or pembrolizumab, has shown only modest activity in advanced nccRCC, typically yielding objective response rates (ORRs) below 30%. 10 , 11 Similarly, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as sunitinib, axitinib, pazopanib, or cabozantinib have demonstrated limited and heterogeneous efficacy across nccRCC subtypes. 12‐17 In contrast, regimens combining PD-1 inhibition with multitargeted TKIs have yielded substantially improved outcomes. In the phase II study conducted at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSKCC), cabozantinib plus nivolumab achieved an ORR of 48% and a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 13 months in papillary, unclassified, and translocation-associated nccRCC, although activity remained limited in chromophobe RCC. 18 , 19 The KEYNOTE-B61 trial further demonstrated robust and consistent antitumor activity of pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib with an ORR of 51% and a median PFS of 18 months across multiple nccRCC histologies. 20 , 21 Collectively, these findings position ICI–TKI combinations as preferred regimens for advanced nccRCC with clinically meaningful efficacy and safety. 22

Nevertheless, important evidence gaps persist. Although recent trials have established the efficacy of ICI–TKI combinations in nccRCC, uncertainty remains regarding treatment selection and expected outcomes in routine clinical practice, particularly across diverse patient populations. To address this unmet need, we conducted a nationwide Korean multicenter retrospective study (KCSG-GU25-01) evaluating the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib plus nivolumab in patients with advanced nccRCC.

Patients and methods

Study design and patient population

This nationwide, multicenter, retrospective cohort study evaluated the clinical outcomes of cabozantinib plus nivolumab in patients with advanced or metastatic nccRCC treated across 11 tertiary institutions in the Republic of Korea. Consecutive patients with histologically confirmed nccRCC who initiated cabozantinib plus nivolumab between March 2022 and December 2024 were identified through institutional databases at each participating center using predefined study eligibility criteria. Patients were eligible if they had measurable disease per investigator assessment and received cabozantinib plus nivolumab in any line of systemic therapy. Exclusion criteria included prior treatment with cabozantinib, a predominant clear-cell component, or insufficient clinical or follow-up data. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board of each participating center and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients in the study started treatment after writing the informed consent.

Treatment exposure and dose modifications

Cabozantinib was administered orally at a starting dose of 40 mg once daily, and nivolumab was given intravenously at 240 mg every 2 weeks, following routine institutional practice. Dose reductions, interruptions, and discontinuation were performed according to physician judgment and toxicity profiles. Treatment was continued until radiographic progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient/physician decision.

Clinical assessments and endpoints

Radiologic tumor assessments were performed according to routine clinical practice and evaluated using RECIST v1.1 criteria. 23 The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete or partial response. The disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the proportion of patients with complete response, partial response, or stable disease. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from treatment initiation to radiographic progression or death from any cause. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from treatment initiation to death from any cause; surviving patients were censored at the date of last follow-up. Safety was evaluated using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0, based on clinical documentation and laboratory findings during routine care.

Data collection

Baseline demographic, pathological, and clinical characteristics—including age, sex, RCC histologic subtype, International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium (IMDC) risk category, metastatic sites, laboratory parameters, and prior therapies—were collected via standardized data abstraction forms across all participating centers. Pathologic confirmation was performed at each institution. Prespecified exploratory subgroup analyzes included comparisons by histologic subtype and line of therapy, and were considered hypothesis-generating.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyzes were conducted using R version 4.2.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Categorical variables were compared using the Chi-square test or Fisher's exact test, and continuous variables using the t-test or Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Confidence intervals for ORR and DCR were calculated using the Clopper–Pearson method. Two-sided P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 62 patients with advanced or metastatic nccRCC who received cabozantinib plus nivolumab were included in the analysis (Table 1). The median age at treatment initiation was 55 y (range: 24–84 y), and 61.3% of patients were male. Papillary RCC was the most common histologic subtype (50.0%), followed by FH-deficient (22.6%), TFE3-rearranged (9.7%), chromophobe (6.5%), collecting duct (6.5%), and unclassified tumors (4.8%). At baseline, the lymph nodes (61.3%), lung (51.6%), bone (50.0%), and liver (30.6%) were the most frequently involved metastatic sites. The distribution of IMDC risk categories was 3.2% favorable, 43.5% intermediate, and 53.2% poor risk. Prior nephrectomy had been performed in 40.3% of patients. Most patients (79.0%) received cabozantinib plus nivolumab as first-line therapy, whereas 12.9% and 8.1% initiated treatment in the second-line and third-or later line settings, respectively.

Efficacy

Among the 62 evaluable patients (Figure 1A), the ORR was 45.2% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 32.4–57.9; Figure 1B). Complete responses were observed in 1.6% (N = 1), and partial responses in 43.5% (N = 27). Responses were observed across histologic subtypes, including papillary (48.4%), FH-deficient (50.0%), translocation-associated (50.0%), unclassified (33.3%), chromophobe (25.0%), and collecting duct carcinoma (25.0%), with no statistically significant difference among subtypes (P = 0.876). An additional 48.4% achieved stable disease as their best response, resulting in a DCR of 93.6% (95% CI, 87.3–99.8; Figure 1C). Disease control was significantly different according to histologic subtypes (P = 0.013), with the lowest DCR in chromophobe histology (50.0%). ORR and DCR were not significantly different according to line of treatment (Supplementary Figure 1A and B) and IMDC risk group (Supplementary Figure 2A and B). Primary progressive disease occurred in 6.5% of patients.

Survival

With a median follow-up duration of 22.2 months (95% CI, 15.4–29.1), the median PFS was 9.7 months (95% CI, 7.7–11.6; Figure 2A). One- and two-year PFS rates were 39.0% and 23.4%, respectively. PFS outcomes were generally consistent across histologic subgroups (P = 0.116; Figure 2B), although patients with chromophobe and collecting duct carcinoma exhibited numerically shorter median PFS (1.9 and 5.0 months, respectively) compared to other histologic subtypes. The median OS was 22.0 months (95% CI, 15.2–28.7; Figure 2C) and 1-y and 2-y OS rates were 72.1% and 41.5%, respectively. Although there was no significant difference in OS according to histologic subtypes (P = 0.085; Figure 2D), the median OS of patients with chromophobe histology was numerically shorter (5.2 months) compared to other histologic subtypes. Although PFS and OS did not differ significantly according to line of treatment (Supplementary Figure 1C and D), patients with favorable/intermediate IMDC risk had significantly longer PFS than those with poor IMDC risk (Supplementary Figure 2C). A trend toward longer OS was also observed in patients with favorable/intermediate IMDC risk compared with those with poor IMDC risk, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (Supplementary Figure 2D).

Safety

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) of any grade occurred in all patients (Table 2). The most frequent TEAEs were elevations in AST (71.0%) and ALT (56.5%), followed by anemia (45.2%), fatigue (41.9%), hypothyroidism (33.9%), hand-food syndrome (32.3%), and platelet decreased (30.6%). Grade 3–4 TEAEs occurred in 46.8% of patients, including hemoglobin decreased (11.3%), AST elevation (8.1%), and ALT elevation (8.1%). Immune-related adverse events requiring a steroid more than physiologic dose (more than 10 mg of prednisolone/d) occurred in 25.8% of patients. Treatment discontinuation due to toxicity occurred in 6.5% of patients and no treatment-related deaths were reported.

Discussion

In this nationwide multicenter cohort, cabozantinib plus nivolumab demonstrated clinically meaningful activity in patients with advanced nccRCC, suggesting that it may represent a reasonable treatment option in routine clinical practice. In a heterogeneous population largely treated in the first-line setting, the combination achieved an ORR of 45.2% and a DCR of 93.6%, with a median PFS of 9.7 months and a median OS of 22.0 months. Importantly, treatment was generally well tolerated, with a low rate of discontinuation due to toxicity (6.5%), a finding of particular relevance in daily practice where treatment sustainability is critical. Clinical responses were observed across multiple histologic subtypes, although the magnitude of benefit varied, underscoring the need for individualized treatment selection in this biologically diverse disease.

Our observations align with and expand upon evidence from prior prospective trials of cabozantinib plus nivolumab. The phase II trial from MSKCC evaluated 47 patients with nccRCC (40 in cohort 1 and 7 in cohort 2) and reported an ORR of 47.5% and a median PFS of 12.5 months in cohort 1, while no objective responses were observed in chromophobe RCC within cohort 2. 12 The updated analysis focusing on 40 patients with papillary, unclassified, or translocation-associated RCC demonstrated an ORR of 48%, median PFS of 13 months, and median OS of 28 months, with durable responses extending beyond 2 y in a substantial proportion of responders. 13 These consistent signals across both prospective and real-world settings underscore the robust activity of the cabozantinib plus nivolumab combination in several clinically relevant nccRCC subtypes.

Beyond the MSKCC experience, additional data have suggested meaningful activity of cabozantinib-based regimens in nccRCC. In a multicenter international retrospective study including 112 patients with advanced nccRCC, cabozantinib achieved an ORR of 27% and a median PFS of 7.0 months across diverse histologic subtypes. 24 More recently, a multicenter study by the Turkish Oncology Group demonstrated longer PFS with cabozantinib than with sunitinib (9.2 vs. 6.6 months) and a numerically higher ORR (38.6% vs. 26.3%). 25 In the ARON-1 study, patients treated with cabozantinib plus nivolumab achieved an ORR of 40% and a median PFS of 18.4 months. 26 Taken together, these datasets support the activity of cabozantinib-based regimens in nccRCC while highlighting the substantial heterogeneity across histologic subtypes. Our study adds granularity by providing histology-specific outcomes from a uniformly treated cohort that includes aggressive and underrepresented entities such as FH-deficient, translocation-associated, and collecting duct RCC. Importantly, our study includes 62 patients—larger than the MSKCC cabozantinib/nivolumab cohorts and representing, to our knowledge, the largest real-world dataset evaluating this combination in nccRCC.

Our results should also be interpreted within the broader landscape of immune checkpoint inhibitor–TKI combinations for nccRCC. The KEYNOTE-B61 trial, the largest prospective trial in this setting (N = 158), demonstrated an ORR of 51% and consistent benefit across multiple histologies with pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib. 20 , 21 Although cross-trial comparisons must be approached cautiously, ICI–TKI regimens including cabozantinib/nivolumab and pembrolizumab/lenvatinib consistently achieve ORRs of ~45–50% and PFS in the 9–13-month range across independent datasets, representing a substantial improvement over historical outcomes with VEGF-targeted TKIs or PD-1 inhibitor monotherapy (typically ORR < 30% and PFS < 6 months). Consistent with these observations, a recent systematic analysis incorporating available phase II and phase III datasets further supports that IO–TKI combinations provide superior clinical outcomes compared with TKI monotherapy in advanced nccRCC. 27

nccRCC comprises a biologically and clinically heterogeneous group of tumors, with substantial variability in underlying molecular drivers and therapeutic responsiveness across histologic subtypes. Papillary RCC, translocation-associated RCC, and FH-deficient RCC have shown relatively favorable responses to combined treatment with ICI-TKI, likely reflecting higher angiogenic signaling and/or increased immunogenicity in these subtypes. In contrast, chromophobe RCC and collecting duct carcinoma have consistently demonstrated limited benefit from ICI-TKI combinations, underscoring fundamental biological differences. Chromophobe RCC is characterized as an immunologically “cold” tumor driven predominantly by mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic reprogramming rather than angiogenic or immune-mediated pathways. 28 Collecting duct carcinoma exhibits an aggressive, urothelial-like biology and has historically been treated with cytotoxic chemotherapy. 29 However, the optimal systemic treatment strategy remains uncertain, although the BONSAI trial (NCT03354884) suggested potentially comparable outcomes with cabozantinib. 30 In our cohort, chromophobe RCC and collecting duct carcinoma showed relatively limited activity, with ORRs of 25.0% in both subtypes and median PFS of 1.9 and 5.0 months, respectively. Taken together, these findings highlight the heterogeneity of nccRCC and suggest that ICI–TKI combinations should not be uniformly applied across all histologic subtypes, supporting the need for biology-driven treatment strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

The safety findings in our cohort are also consistent with prior studies. Elevations in hepatic enzymes, anemia, fatigue, and diarrhea were among the most common adverse events, comparable to toxicity patterns reported in the MSKCC phase II trial and other ICI–TKI combinations. Notably, the discontinuation rate in our study (6.5%) was lower than in the MSKCC trial, possibly reflecting differences in patient characteristics, toxicity-management strategies, ethnicity, or dose-modification patterns. Nevertheless, no new safety signals emerged, confirming the regimen's tolerability in heterogeneous real-world populations.

Our study has limitations inherent to its retrospective multicenter design, including potential selection bias related to differences in referral patterns, treatment accessibility, and institutional practice patterns across participating centers, as well as a lack of centralized radiologic review and variability in follow-up intervals. Histologic classification was performed at individual institutions without centralized pathology verification, and molecular profiling was not uniformly available, limiting the ability to explore genomic predictors of response. Furthermore, subgroup analyzes by histologic subtype were constrained by small sample sizes in certain rare entities. In addition, the findings derive from a Korean population, which may limit generalizability to other ethnic or geographic groups. Lastly, similar to the MSKCC phase II trial and KEYNOTE-B61 trial, our study also employed a single-arm design, limiting the ability to directly compare the ICI–TKI combinations against TKI or ICI monotherapy and precluding definitive conclusions regarding the comparative superiority or broad applicability of this regimen across all nccRCC subtypes. Ultimately, randomized trials such as STELLAR-304 (NCT05678673) are expected to provide definitive evidence regarding the comparative superiority of ICI–TKI regimens in nccRCC. 31

In conclusion, this nationwide study reinforces the clinical activity and manageable safety profiles of cabozantinib plus nivolumab in advanced nccRCC and provides one of the largest real-world assessments of this regimen to date. The consistency of outcomes across multiple studies—including prospective trials and now a large real-world dataset—suggests that cabozantinib plus nivolumab may be an active treatment option across several nccRCC histologies. Prospective, biomarker-driven studies are warranted to refine patient selection and optimize treatment strategies; meanwhile, our findings may inform treatment selection in routine clinical practice for patients with advanced nccRCC.
