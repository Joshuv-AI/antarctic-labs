Integrating single-cell and bulk transcriptomics to identify a SUMOylation-related prognostic signature and the oncogenic role of VPS72 in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Shenzhi Li, Jie Wang, Liqin Wang, Tingmei Bian, Shaogui Qian, Xin Zhu, Xiaofeng Zhou, Zheng Wang, Lei Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Integrated Landscape: Characterized the SUMOylation landscape in HCC using integrated single-cell and bulk transcriptomics.•Cellular Taxonomy: Stratified malignant cells into five distinct SUMOylation subtypes with unique biological functions.•Robust Prediction: Developed a 13-gene prognostic signature (SUMOylation.Sig) validated across multiple independent cohorts.•Functional Validation: Confirmed that VPS72 knockdown inhibits HCC cell proliferation and migration.•Therapeutic Guidance: Demonstrated the signature’s efficacy in predicting tumor immune microenvironment and immunotherapy response.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is recognized as the most prevalent form of primary liver cancer, accounting for approximately 90% of liver cancer cases and ranking among the leading causes of cancer-related mortality globally. Each year, HCC results in over 800,000 new diagnoses and >700,000 deaths, presenting a bleak five-year survival rate that falls below 20% [1]. The poor prognosis associated with HCC can largely be attributed to the almost universal emergence of drug resistance, which significantly hampers effective treatment outcomes. Recent therapeutic advancements, including surgical resection, liver transplantation, and targeted therapies such as sorafenib, have not substantially improved survival rates. In addition to traditional chemotherapeutics, immunotherapy, particularly immune checkpoint inhibitors, has surfaced as a potentially effective treatment approach for HCC. However, the efficacy of single-agent immunotherapy remains modest, with response rates ranging from 10–20% [2]. This limited effectiveness underscores the presence of primary resistance pathways that undermine antitumor immune responses within the HCC microenvironment [3]. This highlights the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies and biomarkers to improve patient survival.

To address these challenges, recent research has heavily focused on identifying novel molecular targets, prognostic signatures, and immunotherapeutic strategies. Extensive bioinformatics analyses have been utilized to screen for crucial gene targets and biomarkers that mediate HCC progression [4]. For instance, the aberrant expression of specific genes, such as PRDX6, has been found to profoundly affect the proliferation, migration, and invasion of HCC cells, emphasizing the prognostic value of novel biomarkers [5]. Concurrently, novel therapeutic agents are being actively explored; studies have identified ESR1 as a critical target for Icaritin, a traditional medicine with estrogen-like activities, which successfully inhibits malignant proliferation in HCC [6]. Furthermore, the unique immune microenvironment of the liver has positioned immunotherapy as a revolutionary treatment for advanced HCC, while its potential for downstaging tumors prior to liver transplantation is also being rigorously investigated [7]. Understanding these intricate molecular, targeted, and immunological landscapes sets the stage for exploring crucial post-translational modifications.

SUMOylation, a reversible posttranslational modification (PTM), plays a critical role in cancer biology by covalently attaching a small ubiquitin-related modifier (SUMO) protein to lysine residues on target proteins [8,9]. The SUMOylation process is regulated by a series of conserved proteins, including SAE1, SAE2, Ubc9, SUMO E3 ligases, and SENPs. This process is distinct from ubiquitination, which typically results in proteasomal degradation; instead, SUMOylation primarily modulates protein activity, localization, and interactions without inducing degradation. It is essential for various cellular processes such as transcription, DNA repair, stress responses, and protein-protein interactions, all of which are vital for maintaining cellular function and responding to environmental changes [[8], [9], [10]]. Research indicates that SUMOylation is essential for modulating cellular proliferation and the preservation of genomic integrity, leading to its emerging significance in cancer. Notably, components of the SUMOylation pathway frequently exhibit elevated expression levels in cancerous tissues, correlating with poor patient outcomes [[11], [12], [13]]. Research has indicated that the suppression of SUMOylation can induce cell death, suppress tumor cell invasion, and reduce migration rates [[13], [14], [15]], while also enhancing sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents [16,17]. Conversely, elevated SUMO-2/3 modifications in breast cancer cells could potentially augment their metastatic abilities by influencing a range of cellular functions, such as glycolytic activity, cellular migration, cell cycle progression, stress response mechanisms, inflammatory processes, gene transcription, and the interplay between the SUMO and ubiquitin pathways [18]. Thus, exploring the implications of SUMOylation in HCC not only enhances our comprehension of the disease but also paves the way for developing targeted therapies that could improve clinical outcomes for patients suffering from this aggressive malignancy.

This study aims to analyze malignant epithelial cells and their microenvironment in HCC patients using scRNA-seq technology. By identifying prognostic genes associated with SUMOylation, we seek to construct a risk-scoring model that can predict patient survival rates and response to immunotherapy. The integration of various bioinformatics approaches, including ssGSEA, Cox regression analysis, and machine learning algorithms, enhances the reliability and accuracy of our findings. This research holds significant potential for improving prognostic assessments and therapeutic strategies in HCC, ultimately contributing to better patient outcomes.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

The scRNA-seq data for HCC patients were sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (GSE149614), which includes over 70,000 single-cell transcriptomes derived from 10 HCC patients. For our analysis, six HCC samples were selected. RNA-seq expression data, clinical information, and somatic mutation of HCC were downloaded from TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), GEO, and ICGC (https://dcc.icgc.org) databases, including the TCGA-LIHC dataset, GSE14520 dataset, ICGC-LIRI-JP dataset. TCGA-LIHC dataset served as the training set, while the other two datasets served as external validation sets.

Single-cell data preprocessing

The analysis of HCC scRNA-seq data was performed using the “Seurat” R package (version 4.4.0). Cell quality assessment was conducted using two key indicators: the count of genes identified within each cell and the percentage of mitochondrial genes present. Cells that had fewer than 100 or >6000 detected genes, along with those exhibiting mitochondrial gene proportions exceeding 15% were excluded. A comprehensive analysis led to the identification of 2000 genes exhibiting high variability through the utilization of the “FindVariableFeatures” function. Next, the “ScaleData” function was employed to normalize the data, thereby reducing the influence of technical noise. Dimensionality reduction was achieved using principal component analysis (PCA), where the top 40 principal components (PCs) were selected for ensuing uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) analysis. Cell clustering was conducted through the “FindNeighbors” and “FindClusters” functions, with a resolution parameter set at 0.05 to discern distinct cell subpopulations. Marker genes for each cluster were identified utilizing the “FindAllMarkers” function in Seurat, with the criteria set for log2[fold change (FC)] exceeding 0.25 and an adjusted P-value of <0.05. The automated annotations produced by the SingleR were integrated with established canonical marker genes from previous studies, alongside manual corrections to enhance the accuracy of the annotations.

Identification of differentially expressed SUMOylation-related genes (SRGs)

A total of 20 SRGs were collected from a previous study [19]. To identify genes showing significant variation between HCC and paratumoral normal samples, we applied the “limma” R package (version 3.64.3) to the TCGA-LIHC cohort, using filtering parameters of |log2FC|> 1 and an adjusted P-value < 0.05. The resulting differentially expressed SRGs were further filtered through univariate Cox regression analysis to extract those with significant prognostic potential. Additionally, the ssGSEA was employed to calculate a SUMOylation-related score.

Cancer cell malignancy recognition by InferCNV

The “infercnv” R package (version 1.24.0) was employed to calculate copy number variations (CNVs) in scRNA-seq data. This analysis is specifically designed to evaluate relative gene expression levels in conjunction with chromosomal location data, thereby allowing for the inference of CNV status in individual cells. In this study, B/plasma cells were selected as the reference group because they represent a robust, non-malignant, and diploid cell population within the tumor microenvironment. As immune-lineage cells distinct from the epithelial-derived tumor cells, they serve as an ideal baseline for CNV inference, effectively minimizing the background noise of the normal epithelial transcriptome and providing a clear signal for the identification of malignant epithelial cells. Through this methodology, we successfully differentiated between malignant tumor cells and healthy cells, identifying tumor-derived epithelial cells as those exhibiting elevated CNV scores. This comprehensive analysis is crucial for understanding the genetic landscape of tumors and their cellular composition. In accordance with this classification, the expression patterns of prognostic SRGs were analyzed across different cellular subtypes. The assessment of SUMOylation activity within individual cells was conducted employing various algorithms, such as UCell, ssgsea, singscore, AUCell, and AddModuleScore.

SUMOylation subtypes identification by non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) algorithm

To comprehensively analyze the effects of SUMOylation on malignant epithelial cells, we utilized the NMF algorithm to facilitate dimensionality reduction of the expression data pertaining to prognostic SRGs. To elucidate biological pathways that are uniquely associated with SUMOylation subtypes in HCC, the “FindAllMarkers” function alongside the “limma” R package was utilized to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from the single-cell data. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis for each subtype was conducted utilizing the identified DEGs for each cellular subtype.

Transcription factor prediction

The “pySCENIC” package (version 1.3.1) was utilized to reconstruct gene regulatory networks. The SCENIC framework, which is based on co-expression and motif analysis, enabled us to uncover key gene regulatory mechanisms and assess transcription factor (TF) activity. To evaluate the enrichment of TFs and the activity of regulatory factors, we constructed an AUCell matrix. This matrix allowed us to score the regulon activity for each cell, facilitating the clustering of cells based on shared activity within a regulatory subnetwork. As a result, we could effectively identify distinct cell types and states through the analysis of activated regulons in each cell type, using a raw count matrix as input for the pySCENIC pipeline.

Cell-cell communication analysis and metabolic analysis

The R package “CellChat” (version 2.2.0) was utilized to explore intricate cell-cell communications and to develop regulatory networks grounded in the expression levels of ligands and receptors. This analysis allowed us to infer intercellular communication dynamics, particularly within the tumor microenvironment (TME), encompassing both ligand-receptor interactions as well as signaling pathways. In addition, the “scMetabolism” package (version 0.2.1) was used to determine the metabolic activity of different SUMOylation cell subtypes.

Development and validation of a risk signature

We utilized the TCGA-LIHC dataset as our training cohort and subsequently confirmed our results in the GSE14520 and ICGC datasets. To quantify SUMOylation-related activity, enrichment scores were generated using the ssGSEA algorithm based on the expression profiles of specific markers for each SUMOylation cluster. To assess the role of the ssGSEA score on the overall survival (OS) of patients with HCC, we applied the optimal cutoff value, which allowed us to stratify the patients into two distinct groups.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) characteristic of the SUMOylation-C4 cluster were identified using a significance filter of an adjusted P-value < 0.05 and a |log2FC| >1. We employed univariate Cox regression analysis to initially discern genes that may hold prognostic significance within the TCGA set. To build and validate a machine learning-based prognostic signature (SUMOylation.Sig) through a comprehensive approach that integrated 101 combinations of 10 distinct machine learning algorithms in TCGA. The algorithms employed included CoxBoost, Elastic Net (Enet), Stepwise Cox, Supervised Principal Component Analysis (SuperPC), Generalized Boosted Regression Modeling (GBM), Lasso, plsRcox, Ridge Regression, Random Survival Forest (RSF), and survival-SVM. To ensure the reproducibility and robustness of the machine learning pipeline, hyperparameter tuning for each algorithm, particularly the RSF model, was performed using a grid search strategy. For the RSF model, we specifically optimized the ‘ntree’ (number of trees) and ‘mtry’ (number of variables to split at each node) hyperparameters. The ‘ntree’ was tuned within the range of 500–2000, and the ‘mtry’ parameter was determined based on the square root of the number of input variables. We analyzed candidate prognostic genes utilizing Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) to mitigate potential overfitting. To ensure the robustness of our findings, all models were subjected to further cross-validation using the GSE14520 and ICGC cohorts. Finally, Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) was computed across all validation datasets, identifying the model with the highest average C-index as the optimal prognostic signature.

Subsequently, patients in both the TCGA-LIHC training set and two validation cohorts were categorized into low- and high-risk groups according to the optimal threshold, facilitating a comprehensive assessment of clinical outcomes. To evaluate survival rates between these groups, we employed Kaplan-Meier survival analysis alongside the log-rank test, which underscored the clinical prognostic significance of SUMOylation.Sig. Additionally, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was utilized to verify the predictive precision of the signature.

Nomogram development and evaluation

We performed univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses to assess the independence of the risk scores from other clinical traits, reinforcing the robustness of the prognostic model. Next, a nomogram was developed utilizing the “rms” package (version 8.1-0) in R to quantitatively predict the prognosis for patients with HCC. This nomogram incorporates critical clinicopathologic characteristics and the risk score. To assess the precision and predictive accuracy of the nomogram, calibration curves, and ROC curves were generated. The calibration curves assessed the model’s fit, while the ROC curves examined its discrimination ability, thereby providing a comprehensive evaluation of the nomogram’s effectiveness in predicting patient survival outcomes.

Analysis of tumour-infiltrating immune cells

To explore the correlation between HCC and the abundance of immune infiltrating cells, we computed the immune cell infiltration and immune-related functions for HCC patients through the ssGSEA approach, using the “GSVA” R package (version 1.46.0). Furthermore, we applied the CIBERSORT algorithm to quantitatively assess the relative abundances of 22 distinct immune cell types within the HCC samples. Furthermore, we employed the ESTIMATE algorithm to analyze each tumor sample, focusing on the levels of immune infiltration, stromal components, and tumor purity.

Prediction of response to immunotherapy

To estimate the likelihood of immunotherapy success in HCC patients, we implemented the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) methodology. The TIDE score, derived from standardized transcriptome data, serves as a critical metric indicating the likelihood of immune escape; higher scores correlate with reduced benefits from immunotherapy. Subsequently, the immunophenotype score (IPS) was utilized to further evaluate immunotherapy outcomes. The IPS for HCC was downloaded from the Cancer Immunome Atlas (TCIA) database (https://tcia.at/). An increased IPS score correlates with a more pronounced immunoreactivity phenotype. Additionally, the Submap algorithm was utilized to forecast the reactions of various risk groups to treatments involving anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 antibodies [20]. The IMvigor210 dataset, which includes patients who were treated with PD-1 or CTLA4 inhibitors for urothelial cancer, was utilized to verify the role of SUMOylation.Sig in forecasting the advantages of immunotherapy.

Correlation between SUMOylation.Sig with TMB and drug sensitivity

Somatic mutation data for HCC patients were sourced from the TCGA database for the purpose of assessing individual tumor mutation burden (TMB) scores [26]. Subsequently, we compared the TMB differences between these groups and performed a survival analysis to evaluate the influence of TMB on patient outcomes. Additionally, the “oncoPredict” R package (version 0.2) was employed to calculate the IC50 values for each HCC patient. This analysis utilized the data derived from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/), enabling a comprehensive assessment of drug sensitivity across various malignancies.

Cell culture

Human HCC cells HepG2 and LM3 and human normal hepatocytes L-O2 were acquired from Icellbioscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd., (Shanghai, China). L-O2 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium with 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Massachusetts, USA) and 1% antibiotics. All of the cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2.

Quantitative real-time PCR

To isolate total RNA from the various HCC cell lines, we employed the Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, USA; Takara, Japan). The resulting RNA was then converted into complementary DNA (cDNA) through reverse transcription using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Takara, Japan), adhering strictly to the manufacturer’s provided protocols. For the analysis of gene expression levels, quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) was conducted with TB Green Premix Ex Taq (Takara, Japan). The relative expression levels of genes were quantitatively assessed using the 2-ΔΔCt method, utilizing GAPDH as an internal control for normalization.

Cell transfection

To knock down the expression of VPS72, small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting VPS72 or a negative control siRNA were transfected into HepG2 and LM3 cells using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer’s protocol. The cells were cultured in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

Colony formation assay

The proliferative capacity was assessed via colony formation assay. Briefly, transfected cells in the logarithmic growth phase were digested and seeded into 6-well plates at a density of approximately 1000 cells per well. After approximately two weeks of incubation, cells were fixed with methanol for 15 min and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 10 min. The number of colonies was then photographed and counted.

Wound healing assay

To evaluate cell migration, a wound healing assay was performed. Transfected cells were seeded in 6-well plates and grown to 90% confluence. A sterile pipette tip was used to create a linear scratch. Images of the “wound” were captured at 0, 24, and 48 h using an inverted microscope to calculate the migration rate.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.3.3). The Wilcoxon test was employed to evaluate differences between two preselected groups or paired samples, while the Kruskal-Wallis test was utilized for comparisons among three groups. The relationship between pairs of variables was analyzed using either Spearman’s rank correlation or Pearson’s correlation methods. Survival analysis was conducted using Kaplan-Meier curves, with the log-rank test applied to assess survival disparities among various risk groups. P < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant, signifying notable distinctions in the evaluated parameters.

To comprehensively reveal the cellular landscape of HCC, we obtained scRNA-seq data from 6 HCC samples. Following quality control screening, a total of 17,683 cells were selected for in-depth examination. We used SingleR, along with known canonical marker genes from previously published studies, to annotate cell types (Fig. 1B). This process ultimately identified 8 types of cells, including B/plasma cells, endothelial cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, macrophages, mast cells, neutrophils, and NK/T cells (Fig. 1B). The bar chart visually illustrated the composition ratio of the number of 8 types of cells among 6 HCC samples (Fig. 1C). We also presented the expression patterns of these cell marker genes for these 8 cell types (Fig. 1D). The heatmap illustrated in Fig. 1E showcases the expression and functional enrichment of marker genes across various cell types. This representation elucidates the functional attributes of these cell types in critical biological processes, including T-cell activation, antigen processing and presentation, and complement activation.

SUMOylation subtype identification based on prognostic SRGs

By utilizing the expression signatures of 10 prognostic SRGs, we successfully utilized the NMF clustering algorithm to classify malignant epithelial cells into 5 SUMOylation-related malignant cell subpopulations. We performed gene differential expression analysis among 5 SUMOylation-related malignant cell subpopulations, with the top 10 markers illustrated in Fig. 2D Subsequently, we performed GO enrichment analyses on these DEGs. GO enrichment analysis showed a notable enrichment of the SUMOylation-C1 subtype in protein metabolism and quality control and steroid metabolic process, while the SUMOylation-C2 subtype exhibited significant enrichment in gene expression and translation process (Fig. 2E). SUMOylation-C3 and SUMOylation-C4 were predominantly concentrated in the biological processes of small molecule metabolism and translation machinery biogenesis (Fig. 2E). To assess the distribution of these SUMOylation-associated cell types at the bulk transcriptomic level, we performed ssGSEA on TCGA samples using representative marker genes for each malignant subset. The analysis demonstrated that, compared to adjacent normal tissues, the estimated abundance of most SUMOylation-related cell populations was significantly lower in HCC tumor tissues (Fig. 2F).

Cell-cell communication analysis

By employing CellChat analysis, we successfully developed intricate intercellular communication networks that included a diverse array of cell types, including endothelial cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, mast cells, neutrophil, NK/T cells, macrophages, B/Plasma cells, and 5 SUMOylation cell subpopulations. The interactions’ quantity and strength between 5 SUMOylation cell subpopulations and all other cell types were comprehensively summarized (Fig. 3C). Next, We depicted the communication dynamics, both incoming and outgoing, using bubble plots to make predictions about these interactions. The results demonstrated that the main ligands linked to the output of 5 SUMOylation cell subtypes, when functioning as signal senders, were CD74 and PPIA. Conversely, the primary signal receivers comprised BSG and SDC2 (Fig. 3D and 3E). Furthermore, the heat map analysis demonstrated that within the incoming signaling patterns, the MIF, CypA, and MK signaling pathways were strongly expressed in most 5 SUMOylation cell subtypes. In contrast, the outgoing signaling patterns were predominantly associated with the IGFBP and EGF signaling pathways in relation to these cell subtypes (Fig. 3F).

Establishment and validation of a novel prognostic signature

By leveraging the expression levels of marker genes linked to five different cellular subtypes associated with SUMOylation, we calculated enrichment scores via the ssGSEA method. Based on the determined optimal cutoff, patients were categorized into two risk categories for the purpose of conducting a survival analysis. Data derived from the TCGA training dataset, along with two additional validation datasets, revealed that individuals classified within the high SUMOylation-C4 group demonstrated markedly enhanced survival outcomes in comparison to their counterparts in the low SUMOylation-C4 group (Fig. 4B-D). Subsequently, univariate Cox regression analysis from both training and validation cohorts demonstrated that the SUMOylation-C4 cell subtype could function as a protective factor impacting patient prognosis in HCC (Fig. 4E).

Next, a total of 1388 DEGs were detected according to the established criteria of |log2FC| > 1 and adjusted P-value ≤ 0.05. Furthermore, we utilized the “FindAllMarkers” function within Seurat to isolate 599 marker genes linked to the SUMOylation-C4 cell subtype. The overlap between these DEGs and the identified marker genes led to the discovery of 212 differentially expressed marker genes. After intersecting 1388 DEGs and 599 marker genes of SUMOylation-C4 cell subtype, 149 genes were selected for the identification of genes associated with the survival of HCC patients. To explore the potential prognostic value of these intersection genes, we performed a univariate Cox regression analysis utilizing the TCGA-LIHC cohort. The findings from this analysis indicated that 30 genes serve as prognostic markers that impact the OS of HCC patients. Subsequently, these 30 genes were incorporated into an integrated machine learning program, resulting in the evaluation of 101 different combinations of machine learning algorithms on the TCGA, GSE14520, and ICGC datasets. Among the various algorithms assessed, the RSF algorithm exhibited the most superior predictive capabilities, attaining a mean C-index of 0.701, thus identifying it as the optimal model (Fig. 4F).

Based on this optimal model, we developed the SUMOylation.Sig signature and calculated risk scores for each sample across the TCGA, GSE14520, and ICGC cohorts. Patients in all three cohorts were stratified into high and low-risk score groups guided by an optimal cutoff value. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that those in the high-risk category had markedly reduced survival rates compared to the low-risk individuals (Fig. 4G, I, K). Furthermore, the predictive accuracy of SUMOylation.Sig was validated by ROC analysis, which showed satisfactory performance for forecasting patient outcomes (Fig. 4H, J, L).

Nomogram development and evaluation

The results of the correlation analysis indicated a significant association between grade, tumor stage of HCC, and risk score (Fig. 5A). Initial univariate evaluations identified the TNM stage and risk score as significant predictors of HCC patient outcomes (Fig. 5B). In the multivariate Cox regression analysis, we found that the risk score and TNM stage remained an independent prognostic factor for HCC (Fig. 5C). To enhance prognostic stratification, we developed nomograms that integrate the risk score along with TNM stage for HCC (Fig. 5D). The nomograms effectively estimated 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates, showing a strong correlation between total score and patient survival outcomes. The nomogram exhibited robust performance, as evidenced by the AUC values of 0.966, 0.980, and 0.982 for predictions of OS at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively (Fig. 5E). This performance was further validated by the calibration plots, which illustrated a strong concordance between the observed and predicted OS rates across the three-time points (Fig. 5F). Knockdown of VPS72 inhibits HCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion

To further explore the biological function of the signature genes, we selected VPS72 for functional validation in HepG2 and LM3 cell lines. We performed siRNA-mediated knockdown using two independent sequences (si-VPS72-1 and si-VPS72-2) targeting different regions of the VPS72 gene to ensure the specificity of the knockdown. As shown in Fig. 9N, qRT-PCR analysis confirmed significant and consistent knockdown efficiency for both siRNAs in both cell lines (P < 0.001). Given its superior knockdown efficiency, si-VPS72-1 was selected for subsequent functional assays. The colony formation assay revealed that the proliferative ability of HepG2 and LM3 cells was significantly reduced following VPS72 knockdown (Fig. 9O). The wound healing assay demonstrated that silencing VPS72 markedly slowed the migration of these cells at 24 and 48 h compared to the control group (Fig. 9P). These experimental results suggest that VPS72 acts as an oncogene in HCC by promoting tumor cell growth and metastasis.

Discussion

HCC remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with current treatment strategies often limited by late-stage diagnosis and heterogeneous tumor biology. The complexity of HCC necessitates innovative approaches that integrate tumor phenotypes with molecular mechanisms to enhance therapeutic decision-making and patient outcomes. Our study highlights the critical role of SUMOylation-related cell subtypes in the pathogenesis and progression of HCC, underscoring the potential of targeting SUMOylation pathways for therapeutic intervention.

Our findings reveal a significant correlation between the expression of SRGs and OS of HCC patients, with the identification of 10 differentially expressed prognostic SRGs. The elevated SUMOylation scores in malignant cells suggest a pivotal role in tumor progression. By integrating the expression profiles of SRG, we identified five cell subpopulations associated with SUMOylation. We found that the SUMOylation-C4 cell subtype significantly impacts patient prognosis. By employing comprehensive analyses, including scRNA-seq and machine learning algorithms, we established a novel prognostic signature, SUMOylation.Sig, which demonstrated strong predictive performance across multiple cohorts. This signature not only correlates with overall survival but also demonstrates potential in predicting responses to immunotherapy. This model not only emphasizes the importance of SUMOylation in HCC but also provides a valuable tool for stratifying patients and guiding personalized treatment strategies.

Beyond the computational model, in this study, we not only developed a robust prognostic signature but also performed experimental validation of a key gene, VPS72. Our findings demonstrated that knockdown of VPS72 significantly inhibited the proliferation, migration, and invasion of HepG2 and LM3 cells. These results provide experimental evidence that VPS72, a core component of our SUMOylation.Sig, is associated with the malignant phenotype of HCC in vitro, further supporting the potential of our signature as a tool for identifying therapeutic targets.

Extending this biological validation, the other 12 genes within our SUMOylation.Sig (TUBG1, G6PD, CPS1, SPP1, STIP1, PON1, FTCD, ITGB1BP1, MRPL9, SLC2A2, RDH16, and ALDH2) also possess established biological relevance to HCC tumorigenesis. Several of these genes function as oncogenic drivers that promote aggressive phenotypes. G6PD is a rate-limiting enzyme in the pentose phosphate pathway; its upregulation, often triggered by stress-induced stabilization via SUMOylation, sustains redox balance and nucleotide biosynthesis to fuel HCC proliferation [21]. SPP1 (Osteopontin) is well-documented to facilitate tumor metastasis, promote immune evasion, and influence the tumor immune microenvironment [22]. STIP1 acts as a chaperone molecule that mediates heat-induced metastasis and EMT through the regulation of Snail1 [23]. TUBG1 has been demonstrated to promote HCC growth, migration, and cell-cycle progression [24]. Furthermore, MRPL9 and LPCAT1 have been identified as mitochondrial and membrane-remodeling proteins that support metabolic adaptation and progression in HCC by enhancing AKT phosphorylation [25].

In contrast, other signature genes function primarily as metabolic regulators or tumor suppressors and are frequently downregulated in HCC. CPS1, a rate-limiting enzyme of the urea cycle, is often lost in HCC; its deficiency potentiates metastasis by reducing aspartate levels and redirecting metabolic flux toward pyrimidine biosynthesis [26]. FTCD acts as a tumor suppressor by maintaining lipid metabolic balance; its loss upregulates PPARγ and SREBP2 signaling, leading to lipid accumulation and hepatocarcinogenesis [27]. ALDH2 is crucial for detoxifying ethanol metabolites, and its deficiency is strongly linked to alcohol-associated HCC development and poor prognosis through the activation of oncogenic pathways [28]. Similarly, the antioxidant enzyme PON1 is downregulated in HCC, and its low expression level serves as a robust predictor of poor survival and microvascular invasion [29]. Other signature genes, including SLC2A2 (GLUT2) and RDH16, are essential for glucose and retinol metabolism, and their suppression is linked to the loss of hepatocyte differentiation and increased stemness in HCC [30,31]. While ITGB1BP1 requires further functional characterization in HCC, its inclusion in our signature suggests it contributes to the coordinated metabolic and structural dysregulation characterizing high-risk HCC. Collectively, these genes integrate essential metabolic, signaling, and structural components that support the biological validity of our SUMOylation.Sig model. Overall, our study advocates for the urgent need to explore SUMOylation-targeted therapies as a promising avenue for improving HCC management.

The study identified a total of 19 differentially expressed SRGs in HCC tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues, with 10 of these genes being prognostically significant. Among these, SAE1 (SUMO-activating enzyme subunit 1) was notably upregulated in HCC, correlating with poor patient prognosis, suggesting its role in promoting tumor growth and progression through mechanisms such as cell cycle regulation and proliferation [32]. Another significant gene, UBA2 (ubiquitin-associated-2 domain), was also found to be overexpressed, indicating its involvement in the SUMOylation process that modulates various cellular functions [33]. The expression of SENP1 (SUMO-specific protease 1) was linked to enhanced tumorigenesis, as it regulates the deSUMOylation of target proteins, thereby influencing pathways critical for cancer progression [34,35]. Furthermore, the study highlighted the role of SUMO2, which was significantly elevated in HCC tissues and associated with the activation of oncogenic pathways, reinforcing its potential as a therapeutic target [36]. The findings also revealed that the SUMOylation score, calculated through ssGSEA analysis, was markedly higher in HCC samples, indicating a heightened level of SUMOylation activity in malignant cells compared to normal tissues. This suggests that the dysregulation of SUMOylation may contribute to the malignant phenotype of HCC, emphasizing the importance of these SRGs in the tumor microenvironment and their potential as prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets in HCC management. Overall, the upregulation of these SUMO-related genes underscores their critical role in the pathogenesis of HCC and their potential utility in developing targeted therapies for improved patient outcomes.

The analysis of SUMOylation-related pathways revealed distinct functional enrichments associated with different cellular subtypes in HCC. Specifically, the enrichment of the SUMOylation-C1 subtype in protein metabolism and quality control indicates that SUMOylation could play a vital role in maintaining protein homeostasis, which is crucial for cancer cell survival and proliferation. Furthermore, the SUMOylation-C2 subtype’s significant association with gene expression and translation processes underscores the importance of SUMOylation in regulating transcriptional activity and protein synthesis, both of which are essential for tumor growth and metastasis. These findings underscore the importance of SUMOylation in the regulation of tumor biology, as the differential enrichment of pathways highlights how distinct SUMOylation patterns can influence cellular behavior and contribute to the heterogeneity observed in HCC. The elevated SUMOylation scores in malignant cells, as demonstrated through ssGSEA analysis, further support the notion that SUMOylation is a critical post-translational modification that may promote tumor proliferation and metastasis by improving the stability and activity of oncogenic proteins. This study not only elucidates the functional implications of SUMOylation in HCC but also provides a foundation for future investigations aimed at targeting SUMOylation pathways as a therapeutic strategy, potentially improving patient outcomes in HCC treatment.

The analysis of immune cell infiltration revealed notable disparities among the two risk groups, with the former exhibiting markedly elevated levels of immune cell presence. This observation supports the hypothesis that a robust immune response is crucial for effective tumor control, as evidenced by the enhanced response rates to immunotherapy observed in the low-risk group. The elevated immune scores in the low-risk group indicate a more favorable immune landscape, characterized by increased infiltration of cytotoxic T cells and natural killer (NK) cells, which are essential for effective anti-tumor responses [37,38]. Conversely, the high-risk group demonstrated a predominance of immunosuppressive cell types, such as M2 macrophages, which are known to facilitate tumor progression and immune evasion [39,40].

This dichotomy underscores the importance of understanding the immune microenvironment in HCC, as it may provide insights into potential therapeutic strategies aimed at enhancing immune responses. Furthermore, the study identified a correlation between SUMOylation-related gene expression and the immune landscape, suggesting that SUMOylation may play a pivotal role in modulating the TIME. This modulation could influence the efficacy of immunotherapeutic strategies, as tumors with a more immunogenic profile are likely to respond better to such treatments. The implications of these findings are profound, as they suggest that targeting the SUMOylation pathway could enhance the immune response in HCC, potentially leading to improved patient outcomes. Understanding the interplay between SUMOylation and immune cell dynamics not only sheds light on the mechanisms of immune evasion in HCC but also opens avenues for developing novel therapeutic strategies aimed at reprogramming the tumor microenvironment to favor anti-tumor immunity. This research underscores the importance of considering the immune context when evaluating treatment options for HCC, particularly in the design of personalized immunotherapy approaches.

Our analysis further identified prominent cell-cell communication networks, highlighting CD74 and PPIA as key outgoing ligands, with BSG and SDC2 acting as primary receptors within the SUMOylation-related subtypes. These molecular interactions likely underpin the aggressive phenotype of these subtypes. Specifically, the PPIA-BSG (CD147) axis has been well-documented to promote tumor progression in HCC by activating the MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways, thereby enhancing cell migration, invasion, and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) secretion. Furthermore, PPIA acts as an extracellular signaling molecule that, upon binding to BSG, facilitates a pro-inflammatory and pro-angiogenic microenvironment, reinforcing the malignant landscape. Simultaneously, CD74, as a high-affinity receptor for MIF, plays a critical role in promoting tumor cell survival and inhibiting apoptosis through its interaction with CD44. The upregulation of these pathways within our identified SUMOylation subtypes suggests that they coordinate a complex signaling crosstalk that likely shapes the immunosuppressive TIME. The interaction between SDC2 and these ligands further emphasizes the role of cell-surface proteoglycans in mediating focal adhesion and signaling transduction, which are essential for tumor cell plasticity and communication with surrounding stromal cells. Together, these ligand-receptor pairs constitute a functional signaling module that potentially contributes to the poor prognosis observed in high-risk patients.

However, it is crucial to acknowledge the complexity of SUMOylation in the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), as existing literature has reported contradictory findings. While our signature serves as a robust predictor for immunotherapy response, several studies have linked aberrant SUMOylation directly to immune suppression. For instance, SUMOylation has been shown to be essential for the differentiation, stability, and immunosuppressive function of regulatory T cells (Tregs) [41]. Furthermore, hyper-SUMOylation in HCC can suppress Type 1 Interferon (IFN-1) signaling, leading to impaired dendritic cell maturation and reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration [42]. Additionally, specific SUMOylation events have been reported to drive immune evasion by upregulating PD-L1 expression and inducing CD8+ T cell metabolic dysfunction [43]. This duality suggests that SUMOylation exerts highly context-dependent effects on anti-tumor immunity. Interestingly, our finding that the low-risk group exhibits superior responsiveness to immunotherapy is largely consistent with the notion that excessive SUMOylation activity contributes to an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Acknowledging these conflicting reports highlights the necessity for future investigations to delineate the precise, context-dependent mechanisms by which individual SUMOylation events modulate immune responses in HCC.

Conclusions

The results of this study not only elucidate the complex cellular landscape of HCC but also emphasize the importance of SUMOylation in cancer biology. The identification of distinct cell types and their associated SUMOylation profiles may pave the way for novel therapeutic strategies targeting SUMOylation pathways in HCC. However, it is essential to acknowledge the limitations of this research, such as the relatively small sample size, the requirement for further validation in larger cohorts, and the need to explore the functional implications of the identified cell types. Furthermore, while our in vitro assays demonstrate that VPS72 promotes HCC cell proliferation and migration, the lack of in vivo experimental evidence limits our ability to definitively confirm its role in driving complex tumor progression. Future in vivo studies using animal models are warranted to fully elucidate the oncogenic mechanisms of VPS72 in HCC. Future studies should focus on exploring the mechanistic underpinnings of SUMOylation in HCC and its potential as a biomarker for treatment response.
