Toxicological evaluation of herbo-mineral formulation: Acute and sub-acute oral toxicity in rats
Authors: Mukul S Tambe, Akshay M Baheti, Arti G Swami, Anil T Pawar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundToxicity evaluation is an essential component of the preclinical safety assessment for herbal and herbo-mineral formulations intended for repeated use.ObjectivesThe present study was undertaken to assess the acute and subacute oral toxicity of an Ayurveda-based herbo-mineral formulation (HMF) comprising Tinospora cordifolia (Thunb.) Miers, Withania somnifera (L.) Dunal, and Kukkutandatvak bhasma in rats.MethodsAn acute toxicity of a prepared HMF was conducted at a single oral dose of 2000 mg/kg in rats as per OECD guideline 423. Sub-acute toxicity of HMF was evaluated at 250, 500, and 1000 mg/kg by oral administration in rats in accordance with OECD Test Guideline 407.ResultsNo mortality and clinical signs of toxicity were produced in rats by a single oral administration of a 2000 mg/kg dose of HMF. In the subacute toxicity study, body weight gain and food consumption patterns in the HMF-treated groups were comparable to those of the vehicle control group. Hematological and serum biochemical parameters remained within physiological limits. The relative organ weights did not differ significantly between the control and HMF-treated groups. Histopathological evaluation of the organs of HMF-treated rats revealed normal findings. No delayed or residual toxicity was observed in the satellite group.ConclusionHMF was found to be safe following a single 2000 mg/kg dose and at a 28-days repeated dose of 1000 mg/kg by the oral route in rats. The study findings support the safety of the HMF and provide a scientific basis for further preclinical and clinical development.Keywords: Herbo-mineral formulation, OECD guidelines, Repeated dose oral toxicity, Safety evaluation, Subacute oral toxicity

Introduction

The traditional systems of medicine, especially Ayurveda, rely largely on medicinal plants and herbo-mineral preparations for the prevention and treatment of chronic diseases and disorders. Since these preparations are used for a long time, it is important to assess their safety profile. Although these traditional systems of medicine have long been in use, many of their preparations have not been assessed for toxicological safety [1].

Herbo-mineral preparations are a distinct form of Ayurvedic medicine (Rasaushadhi) that combines plant bioactives with mineral or animal derived compounds. These compounds are further processed using traditional methods like Shodhana (purification) and Marana (incineration). These preparations are believed to have a synergistic effect and improved bioavailability [2]. However, the use of minerals, particularly bhasmas, has come under scrutiny due to potential toxicity [3]. In view of such toxicities, the World Health Organization and other governing bodies worldwide require rigorous toxicological testing in accordance with established guidelines before any medicinal claim can be made [4,5].

Ayurvedic physicians use a combination of Tinospora cordifolia (Thunb.) Miers (Common name: Guduchi), Withania somnifera (L.) Dunal (Common name: Ashwagandha), and Kukkutandatvak bhasma for the treatment of inflammatory conditions and other conditions that are associated with inflammation. Tinospora cordifolia (Thunb.) Miers (Tinospora cordifolia) is an established medicinal herb that has immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and hepatoprotective effects [6]. Similarly, Withania somnifera (L.) Dunal (Withania somnifera) is an established adaptogenic herb that has anti-inflammatory, anti-stress, neuroprotective, and immunomodulatory effects [7]. Tinospora cordifolia and Withania somnifera have demonstrated significant therapeutic potential in several experimental models, including carrageenan-induced paw edema, adjuvant-induced arthritis, and lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced inflammation, and have been widely used in Ayurvedic formulations for chronic inflammatory diseases [8,9]. Kukkutandatvak bhasma, is a calcium-rich compound prepared from hen eggshells [10]. It possesses anti-inflammatory properties and is a natural source of bioavailable calcium. The rationale for using this combination is that it may have synergistic effects, but the long-term safety of this herbo-mineral combination has not been properly explored.

While each ingredient in the formulation is individually reported to be safe, it is not possible to conclude the toxicity of the combined herbo-mineral formulation from its components [[11], [12], [13]]. There may be interactions between the plant-derived ingredients and mineral components that could cause systemic toxicity. Thus, a toxicity study of the herbo-mineral formulation (HMF) is necessary. Acute and subacute toxicity studies in laboratory animals are essential components of preclinical safety evaluation and are recommended by international regulatory guidelines such as the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) [14,15].

Acute toxicity studies provide preliminary information on lethality and immediate toxic effects, while subacute repeated-dose toxicity studies help to identify potential target organ toxicity, cumulative effects, and reversibility of toxic manifestations following prolonged exposure. This study aimed to investigate the acute and 28-day repeated oral toxicity of a herbo-mineral formulation consisting of a combination of aqueous extracts of Tinospora cordifolia and Withania somnifera, and Kukkutandatvak bhasma in Wistar rats.

Materials and methods

Procurement and authentication of plant materials

The plant materials, Tinospora cordifolia and Withania somnifera were collected from local region of Satara district, Maharashtra, India. The collected plant specimens were authenticated by the Botanical Survey of India, Pune, Maharashtra, India (Authentication letter no. BSI/WRC/IDEN./CER.2021/H1; Dated: 13 December 2021).

Extraction of plant materials

The dried powder of the bark of Tinospora cordifolia and the roots of Withania somnifera was extracted using the decoction method with distilled water, as described in standard Ayurvedic texts [16,17]. The dried aqueous extracts of Tinospora cordifolia and Withania somnifera were stored in air-tight containers.

Preparation of herbo-mineral formulation

Aqueous extracts of the stem of Tinospora cordifolia (ATC) and roots of Withania somnifera (AWS), and Kukkutandatvak bhasma were accurately weighed in the ratio of 1:6:6 and mixed to prepare the herbo-mineral formulation (HMF). The ATC and AWS ratios were selected based on previously reported analgesic and anti-inflammatory doses, 50 mg/kg [18] and 300 mg/kg [19], respectively. Kukkutandatvak bhasma has been reported to have significant anti-inflammatory activity at 300 mg/kg in rats [20], and this dose was selected to provide a natural calcium source in the HMF.

Standardization of HMF

The ATC and AWS were reported to contain berberine and withaferin A, respectively. Kukkutandatvak bhasma is a natural source of bioavailable calcium. Thus, the HMF was analyzed for the berberine, withaferin A and calcium contents. Withaferin A and berberine were analyzed by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS), whereas inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) was used for calcium estimation.

For LC-MS/MS analysis, the weighed sample (391 mg) of HMF was dissolved in 10 ml of methanol, filtered through a 0.22 μm syringe filter, and then analyzed by LC-MS/MS. For analysis, an HPLC system (Nexera, Shimadzu, Milano, Italy) and a mass spectrometer (API-4000, QT-Sciex, Brugherio, Italy) were used. Chromatographic separation was performed using a Phenomenex Luna (250 × 4.6 mm, 5 μm) column. Samples were examined by LC-MS/MS by injecting 20 μl solution at 30 °C. For sample separation, the mobile phase consisted of 0.1 % formic acid in water (A) and acetonitrile (B) run in gradient mode. The flow rate was 1 ml/min. The run time was 30 min, and the gradient program started with 80% A and 20% B for the first 10 min, gradually changing to 60% A and 40% B over the next 8 min, then 20% A and 80% B for 4 min, and finally returning to 80% A and 20% B for the last 8 min.

Chemicals and equipment

The biochemical analyses, including sodium, potassium, glucose, cholesterol, urea, uric acid, triglycerides, bilirubin, creatinine, glutamate oxaloacetate transaminase (SGOT), glutamate pyruvate transaminase (SGPT), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), total protein, and albumin, were carried out using biochemistry kits from Meril Diagnostics India, Pvt. Ltd., India. The equipment used in this study were refrigerated centrifuge (BioEra, Model: BE/CI/RMC-260605), clinical chemistry analyzer (BeneSphera, Model: C61), haematoanalyser (Nihon Kohden, Model: Celltac MEK-1305), and light microscope (Olympus, Model: HSA).

Animals

A total of 56 Wistar rats (25male and 31 female rats) procured from the National Institute of Biosciences, Pune, Maharashtra, India, were used for the study. All animal housing and experimentation procedures, such as housing, dosing, and sacrifice, were performed as per the standard operating procedures and the guidelines provided by the Committee for the Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CCSEA), New Delhi, India. The study was approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC Approval No.: SOPMITWPU/IAEC/2021-22/M1/5; Dated10 July 2021).

Acute oral toxicity study

The acute oral toxicity of the HMF was performed as per OECD 423 guidelines [14]. A total of six female Wistar rats (Age: 8-10 weeks; body weight: 170-185 g) were used for the study. The rats were fasted for 14 h before the commencement of this study. Rats were divided into two experimental groups, each containing three rats. Group I was the vehicle control group, which received vehicle (0.5% w/v carboxymethyl cellulose). Group II was the HMF-treated group that received 2000 mg/kg of HMF. The vehicle and HMF (2000 mg/kg) were administered once orally. The starting oral dose of 2000 mg/kg was chosen for the acute oral toxicity of the HMF since mortality is unlikely at this dose level due to reported safety profile of the constituents of the HMF. Following administration, the rats were closely monitored for the first 30 min and then observed periodically over the next 24 h, with particular attention during the initial 4 h. Thereafter, animals were observed once daily for 14 consecutive days to detect any signs of toxicity or mortality. At the end of the 14 days observation period, rats of the vehicle control and HMF-treated groups were humanely sacrificed by overdose of ketamine-xylazine anesthesia followed by cervical dislocation in accordance with CCSEA guidelines. Death of the rats was confirmed by the cessation of respiration and heartbeat, as well as the absence of corneal reflex. Gross necropsy was performed on all euthanized rats.

Subacute oral toxicity study

The subacute oral toxicity of the HMF was assessed according to OECD test guideline 407 [15]. A total of 25male and 25 female Wistar rats were randomly divided into five experimental groups, each containing five male and five female rats. The study consisted of treatment and satellite groups. The treatment groups received vehicle (0.5% carboxymethyl cellulose), lower dose (250 mg/kg), intermediate dose (500 mg/kg), and higher dose ( 1000 mg/kg) of HMF once daily for 28 days by oral route. The satellite group received HMF at 1000 mg/kg once daily for 28 days by oral route, and then were observed for an additional 14 days without further dosing.

Mortality and clinical signs

All rats were daily observed for mortality and clinical signs such as changes in skin, fur, eyes, mucous membranes, respiratory, circulatory, autonomic, and central nervous system. The behavioral and somatomotor functions were also closely observed. Special emphasis was given to the signs of tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhea, lethargy, sleep changes, or coma. Rats in the HMF-treated groups were observed for the treatment period of 28 days, while those in the vehicle control and satellite treatment groups were further observed for 14 days post-treatment to explore the possibility of reversibility or delayed onset of any of the toxicity symptoms.

Body weight, food consumption and water intake

The body weights of both male and female rats were measured once a week. The food and water intake were measured daily by weighing the amount of food and water left after 24 h.

Hematology and serum biochemistry

On day 29, rats in the treatment groups were anesthetized with ketamine and xylazine, and blood samples were obtained by retro-orbital sinus puncture using ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) and non-EDTA tubes. Rats in the vehicle and satellite treatment group were anesthetized as above on day 43, and a blood samples were obtained by retro-orbital sinus puncture. Blood in an EDTA tube was analyzed for hematological parameters such as white blood cells (WBCs), red blood cells (RBCs), haemoglobin (HGB), platelets (PLT), mean corpuscular volume (MCV), mean corpuscular haemoglobin (MCH), mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration (MCHC), haematocrit (HCT), and reticulocytes using a haematoanalyzer. The blood in the non-EDTA tube was centrifuged at 7500 rpm for 15 min to obtain serum. Serum was estimated for biochemical parameters such as sodium, potassium, glutamate oxaloacetate transaminase (SGOT), glutamate pyruvate transaminase (SGPT), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), glucose, cholesterol, triglycerides, creatinine, albumin, urea, uric acid, total bilirubin, and total protein.

Necropsy, organ collection and histopathology

The anesthetized rats of the treatment groups were humanely euthanized by cervical dislocation in accordance with CCSEA guidelines on day 29, whereas the anesthetized rats of the vehicle control and satellite treatment groups were euthanized in the same way on day 43 for gross necropsy. Death of the rats was confirmed by the cessation of respiration and heartbeat, as well as the absence of corneal reflex. The weights of the major organs were recorded. The brain, pituitary gland, heart, lungs, thymus gland, liver, spleen, kidneys, adrenal glands, stomach, intestine, pancreas, sciatic nerve, and spinal cord were isolated. The reproductive organs, such as the testes, epididymis, and seminal vesicles, were isolated from male rats, and the ovaries and uterus were isolated from female rats. The isolated tissues were fixed in 10% formalin solution and processed for tissue histopathology. Tissue sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and examined under a light microscope.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software version 8.0. The data of HMF-treated and satellite groups were compared with the vehicle control group by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by post-hoc Dunnett's test. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Standardization of HMF

The berberine, withaferin A, and calcium contents of HMF were 0.0007%, 0.43%, and 25.8% w/w, respectively.

Acute toxicity study

There was no mortality after a single oral administration of HMF at 2000 mg/kg, and all rats survived at the end of 14 days. No other signs of toxicity were observed. The gross necropsy examination conducted at the end of the experiment revealed no lesions in organs such as the brain, heart, lungs, liver, spleen, kidneys, adrenal glands, stomach, intestines, ovaries, or uterus. This indicates that the HMF is non-toxic at a single oral dose of 2000 mg/kg and can be considered safe for subacute toxicity studies.

Sub-acute toxicity study

Effect on mortality and clinical signs

There was no mortality in the vehicle control, HMF-treated, or satellite treatment groups during the observation period. The rats in the treatment groups showed no changes in general appearance or clinical signs, including no changes in skin, fur, eyes, mucous membranes, respiration, circulation, autonomic and central nervous system function, somatomotor activity, or behavioural patterns. Additionally, there were no signs of tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhea, lethargy, sleep disturbances, or coma. No delayed or reversible toxic effects were observed in the satellite group during the post-treatment observation period.

Effect on body weight, food consumption and water intake

The body weight of the control, HMF-treated, and satellite treatment groups increased progressively during the observation period. No statistically significant differences were observed in the body weight of HMF-treated and satellite treatment groups as compared to the vehicle control group at any point of the observation (Table 1). There was no significant difference in food consumption between the HMF-treated and satellite treatment groups in either sex as compared to the vehicle control group (Table 2). The water intake of the vehicle control, HMF-treated, and satellite treatment groups was statistically the same (Table 3).

Effect on organ weights

There were no statistically significant differences in the absolute weights of the key organs, such as liver, kidneys, heart, spleen, thymus, adrenal glands, and brain of the HMF-treated and satellite treatment groups compared to the vehicle control group (Table 6). Further, there were no significant changes in the weights of the reproductive organs, such as testes, epididymis, and seminal vesicles in males and ovaries and uterus in females, in the HMF-treated groups as compared to the vehicle control group.

Effects on histopathology

Microscopic evaluation of all examined organs from the vehicle control group revealed normal histoarchitecture, which was comparable to those of the HMF-treated as well as the satellite treatment groups (Supplementary Fig. S1–S19). No evidence of cellular degeneration, necrosis, inflammatory infiltration, fibrosis, vascular congestion, edema, or architectural distortion was observed in any of the examined organs. The bain and spinal cord had normal neuronal morphology with normal cellular architecture and the absence of gliosis and vacuolization (Supplementary Fig. S1 and S2). No abnormality was detected in the morphology of the sciatic nerve (Supplementary Fig. S3). The heart had normal myocardial fibers without evidence of degeneration or inflammation (Supplementary Fig. S4). The liver had normal lobular architecture with normal hepatocytes, normal sinusoids, and the absence of fatty changes, necrosis, and inflammation (Supplementary Fig. S5). Likewise, the kidneys had normal glomeruli and renal tubules without evidence of tubular degeneration and interstitial inflammation (Supplementary Fig. S6). Histological sections of the lungs had normal alveolar architecture without congestion and inflammatory cell infiltration (Supplementary Fig. S7). The spleen and thymus had normal lymphoid architecture, which indicated the absence of immunotoxicity (Supplementary Fig. S8 and S9). The pancreas had normal acinar and islet architecture without evidence of degeneration and inflammation (Supplementary Fig. S10). The stomach had normal mucosal and glandular architecture without erosion and ulceration (Supplementary Fig. S11 and S12). Histological sections of the endocrine organs, such as the adrenal and pituitary glands, had normal cellular morphology and zonal architecture (Supplementary Fig. S13 and S14). The reproductive organs of both sexes, including the ovaries, uterus, testes, epididymis, and seminal vesicles, had normal histological architecture without evidence of treatment-induced abnormalities (Supplementary Fig. S15–S19).

Discussion

Herbo-mineral formulations are extensively used in traditional systems of medicine, especially Ayurveda, for the treatment of chronic diseases. Although these formulations are processed using traditional techniques and have a long history of use in medicine, their safety cannot be assumed solely on the basis of traditional knowledge [21,22]. The presence of mineral ingredients, variability in raw materials and processing techniques, and the possibility of repeated or chronic administration make it necessary to conduct systematic toxicological studies on the final product. Moreover, the interaction of herbal bioactive compounds with mineral ingredients may affect pharmacokinetic properties and toxicity profiles, which cannot be predicted from individual ingredient studies [23]. Hence, preclinical toxicity evaluation of herbo-mineral formulations is necessary to determine safety, target organ toxicity, and provide a scientific basis for their rational clinical use.

In the Ayurvedic clinical practice, the combination of Tinospora cordifolia (Guduchi), Withania somnifera (Ashwagandha), and Kukkutandatvak bhasma has been used in the treatment of inflammatory diseases based on their immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory properties. Nevertheless, despite their common use in combination, there is a lack of safety information on the final herbo-mineral formulation after repeated exposure. Thus, the current study was conducted to assess the safety profile of single and repeated oral administration of the herbo-mineral formulation (HMF) based on a thorough evaluation of mortality, signs of toxicity, body weight change, food consumption, water intake, hematological parameters, serum biochemistry, absolute organ weights changes, and histopathology.

Acute toxicity study at a single oral dose of HMF at 2000 mg/kg revealed no mortality and clinical signs of toxicity during the observation period. This indicates that the approximate lethal dose of the HMF is higher than 2000 mg/kg in rats. Based on the outcomes of the acute oral toxicity study, three different dose levels of HMF, i.e., 250, 500 and 1000 mg/kg, were selected for the subacute oral toxicity study.

Subacute toxicity studies in rodents, especially rats, are a basic part of preclinical safety evaluation and are commonly used to assess the adverse effects of repeated exposure to chemical or biological agents over a fixed period. These studies are intended to establish the toxicological profile of a test substance, providing critical information regarding systemic toxicity, dose response, and possible cumulative or delayed toxicity that could occur as a consequence of continuous exposure [15]. In the present study, a common vehicle control group was employed for both the treatment and satellite groups of the herbo-mineral formulation (HMF), as our previous study demonstrated the absence of toxicological effects associated with the vehicle (0.5% w/v carboxymethyl cellulose) [24]. The use of a shared control group also contributed to reducing the number of experimental animals in accordance with the Reduction principle of the 3Rs in animal experimentation.

Body weight, food consumption, and water intake are regarded as sensitive and early indicators of systemic toxicity in repeated-dose toxicity studies. The consistent and progressive increase in body weight across all experimental groups, similar to that of the vehicle control, indicates that HMF did not interfere with the normal growth pattern. There was also no difference in food consumption or water intake between the HMF-treated groups and the vehicle control group. This suggests that the HMF did not have any adverse effect on the basic metabolic processes of the experimental animals.

In addition to general physiological parameters, assessment of hematological parameters offers important information regarding the potential impact of repeated exposure on the hematopoietic and immune systems. Blood is a type of connective tissue that distributes toxic substances in the body. These toxic substances cause damage to the blood cells, leading to an abnormal regulation of the body's function [25]. Hematological analysis is essential in understanding the impact of toxic substances on the hematopoietic system and the immune system [26]. In the current study, the parameters of red blood cell indices, hemoglobin, white blood cell count, platelet count, and reticulocyte percentage were all within the normal physiological range for all the experimental groups. The absence of significant hematological changes indicates that there were no myelotoxic, hemolytic, or immunotoxic effects from the HMF.

Biochemical parameters in serum are valuable tools in toxicology studies for evaluating the mechanism of toxicity, functional status of major organ systems, and identification of target organs (liver, kidney, hematopoietic, immune systems, etc.) [27]. In the current study, the liver function tests (SGOT, SGPT, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, albumin, and total protein) did not reveal any treatment-related or dose-dependent alterations, thus ruling out the possibility of hepatocellular damage. The renal function parameters, including urea and uric acid, remained within normal ranges, thus indicating the integrity of the kidneys. The lack of alterations in glucose, lipid profile, and electrolyte concentrations further strengthens the metabolic safety of the HMF.

Organ weight changes are often the first manifestation of chemical exposure, making them important endpoints in toxicity studies [28]. In the current study, there were no significant differences in the weights of the vital organs, such as the liver, kidneys, heart, spleen, thymus, brain, endocrine organs, and reproductive organs. The results indicate that the repeated treatment with the HMF did not cause hypertrophy, atrophy, or edema of the these organs. The organ weights were also within the normal range, thus further confirming the lack of target-organ toxicity.

Histopathology is regarded as the gold standard for the determination of toxicological effects and the identification of microscopic changes in tissues induced by toxic exposure [29]. In the current study, histopathological analysis of a wide range of organs, such as the brain, spinal cord, heart, liver, kidneys, lungs, pancreas, spleen, thymus, stomach, adrenal gland, pituitary gland, and reproductive organs, showed normal tissue architecture in vehicle control, HMF-treated, and satellite treatment groups. No evidence of cellular degeneration, necrosis, inflammation, fibrosis, or vascular abnormalities was observed. The absence of histopathological changes in both endocrine and reproductive organs suggests that the HMF does not exert endocrine-disrupting or reproductive toxic effects.

The harmony between organ weights, functional parameters (hematology and biochemistry) and microscopic findings (histopathology) provides strong evidence for the overall safety profile of the HMF. Such consistency across multiple toxicological endpoints is widely regarded as a robust indicator of non-toxicity in repeated-dose studies. Furthermore, the absence of delayed or residual toxic effects in the satellite group supports the lack of cumulative toxicity associated with the HMF.

In the current study, the repeated oral dose administration of the HMF at doses of 250, 500, and 1000 mg/kg for 28 days did not result in any mortality during the observation period. The clinical signs were normal, and there was no significant impact of the HMF on neurological signs. Thus, the HMF was found safe when administered orally to Wistar rats of both sexes at a dose of 1000 mg/kg/day for 28 days. Hence, the no-observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) of HMF in the sub-acute oral toxicity study is 1000 mg/kg/day.

Conclusion

Acute and subacute studies of the herbo-mineral formulation (HMF) did not show any treatment-related adverse findings. Acute toxicity study demonstrated no mortality and clinical signs of toxicity at a single oral administration of a 2000 mg/kg dose of HMF. Subacute toxicity study showed that repeated 28-day oral administration of the HMF did not produce systemic, organ-specific, hematological, biochemical, or histopathological toxicity up to 1000 mg/kg in rats. No delayed or residual toxic effects were observed, indicating the lack of cumulative toxicity associated with the HMF. Thus, the approximate lethal dose (ALD) of the HMF was found to be higher than 2000 mg/kg body weight, and the no-observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) was found to be 1000 mg/kg body weight in albino Wistar rats. These findings support the safety and tolerability of the HMF comprising Tinospora cordifolia, Withania somnifera, and Kukkutandatvak bhasma. This study provides a strong experimental basis for its further preclinical development and future clinical translation.
