Excitons and Polaritons in Semiconductor
 Nanorods and Nanowires
 Project report

 Serguei Goupalov

 Abstract

 We have developed a new tool for modeling colloidal semiconductor nanocrystals
within the effective mass approximation by adapting the xyz algorithm widely used
to find eigenfrequencies for vibrational modes of both macroscopic elastic objects and
nanoparticles and based on the Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle. We demonstrated
its extreme efficiency and versatility by studying the influence of crystal structure and
shape anisotropies on valence-band states of InP colloidal nanocrystals destined to re-
place highly toxic cadmium and lead contained quantum dots in numerous applications.
We showed that, while the isotropic approximation gives qualitatively valuable results
for one or two lowest levels of a hole confined in a spherical InP nanocrystal, it cannot
correctly predict the symmetry, the degeneracy, and the order of the levels starting
already from the second or third level due to the strong anisotropy of the underlying
crystal lattice. Thus, the xyz algorithm will play a decisive role in modeling ellipsoidal
quantum dots and nanorods of III - V and II – VI semiconductors. We have also
calculated dispersion of transverse polariton in a CsPbBr3 nanowire and submitted a
CINT rapid-access proposal involving collaboration between Jackson State University,
LANL and the University of Bordeaux.

1 Introduction
Colloidal semiconductor nanocrystals (NCs) have demonstrated a huge potential for opto-
electronic and photocatalytic energy conversion applications.
 The effective-mass approximation (EMA) allows one to describe semiconductor nanos-
tructures using parameters experimentally determined from the constituent bulk materials.
Another advantage of the EMA is its ability to provide analytical or semi-analytical ex-
pressions for energy dispersion and wave functions. However, this comes at a price of addi-
tional approximations being made. The primary ones are negligence of the crystal structure
anisotropy stemming from the cubic symmetry of the underlying crystal lattice and the as-
sumption of the spherical shape of NCs. It is, therefore, important to find a way to test
sensitivity of theoretical predictions towards relaxation of underlying approximations.
 Meanwhile, a very similar situation has been encountered in the problem of finding free
vibrational modes of inhomogeneous and anisotropic elastic objects, where an analytical
solution is known only for the simplest case of an elastically isotropic sphere. Back in 1991
Visscher et al. [1] of the Los Alamos National Laboratory proposed a very efficient algorithm
(dubbed by its authors as the xyz algorithm) based on the Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle
with the basis functions in the form of the product of the powers of Cartesian coordinates.
The main advantage of this method is that it allows one to express all involved matrix
elements in terms of the overlap integral that can be evaluated analytically for ellipsoidal
and many other shapes [1, 2]. During the summer of 2025 we generalized the xyz algorithm
so that it could be applied to vibrations of restrained NCs with the rigid boundary condition
implying zero displacement on the boundary [3].
 The similarities in mathematical description of acoustic vibrations within the approxima-
tion of elastic continuum and valence-band holes in III-V and II-VI semiconductors within
the effective mass approximation have long been noted [3, 4]. In this project we applied the
xyz algorithm to the valence-band holes in InP NCs thereby testing the method. Further ap-
plication of the xyz algorithm will allow us to model nanorods in the shape of spheroids and
ellipsoids and will, therefore, substantially advance our capabilities in modeling nanorods.
 The exciton fine structure of semiconductor NCs is governed by electron-hole exchange

 1

interactions, crystal-field effects and quantum confinement. In conventional CdSe nanocrys-
tals, the exciton ground state is dark. Recent theoretical work predicts that shape anisotropy
in wurtzite nanorods can strongly modify the exciton fine structure and may induce an in-
version between dark and bright exciton states [5]. Our hypothesis is that the aspect ratio
of CdSe/CdS nanorods can be used as a tuning parameter to control the ordering of the
lowest excitonic states. One of the goals of this project is to determine experimentally how
the exciton fine structure evolves with nanorod geometry and to identify the aspect-ratio
regime where bright-dark inversion occurs.
 Nanorods considered in Ref. [5] had length smaller than the wavelength of light. In this
limit effect of retardation on electron-hole coupling can be neglected. In longer nanorods
and/or other materials this might not be so and the retardation effect needs to be accounted
for.
 In the limit of infinitely long nanowires, their optical properties are dominated by exciton
polaritons, or hybridized exciton-photon excitations [6–9]. Studies of dispersion of one-
dimensional exciton polaritons have been limited to organic semiconductors [6] and MBE-
grown structures [7–9] while colloidal nanowires have their own pecularities.

3 Future work
The submitted CINT user proposal implies the following future work:

  Work Performed at CINT

 1. Synthesis of CdSe and/or CdSe/CdS nanorods with aspect ratios from 2 to 10,
 diameters 3–6 nm and lengths 8–50 nm.

 2. TEM characterization to determine dimensions, aspect-ratio distributions and
 structural quality.

 3. Ensemble absorption, photoluminescence, PLQY and fluorescence lifetime mea-
 surements to identify the highest-quality batches.

 4. Transfer of selected samples to the University of Bordeaux for single-particle
 magneto-optical studies. Magnetic-field-induced brightening of dark excitons and
 temperature-dependent spectroscopy will be used to determine bright-dark split-
 tings, exciton g-factors, exchange energies, radiative decay rates, spin-flip rates
 and exciton-phonon coupling strengths. The expected outcome is a complete
 determination of the band-edge exciton fine structure as a function of nanorod
 geometry and identification of the aspect-ratio regime where bright-dark inversion
 occurs.

  Work Performed at the User Institution

 At Jackson State University (JSU) we will perform theoretical modeling of the nanos-
 tructures and determine the range of parameters where a reversal of the order of bright
 and dark states occurs.

 At the University of Bordeaux, France, we will perform cryogenic single-particle op-
 tical spectroscopy on individual nanorods, including high-resolution photolumines-
 cence, polarization-resolved spectroscopy, time-resolved fluorescence measurements,

 4

 and magneto-photoluminescence studies under magnetic fields up to several Tesla. By
 monitoring the evolution of emission spectra and fluorescence dynamics as a function of
 magnetic field and temperature, we will determine the complete band-edge exciton fine
 structure, including bright-dark exciton splittings, anisotropy-induced fine-structure
 splittings, exchange interaction energies, exciton g-factors, radiative decay rates, spin-
 relaxation processes, and exciton-phonon coupling strengths. These measurements
 will establish quantitative correlations between nanorod geometry and excitonic-state
 ordering and will provide the experimental signatures required to identify a possible
 inversion between dark and bright exciton ground states.

 We will apply the xyz algorithm to various semiconductor NC systems where the crystal
structure and/or shape anisotropy play a significant role including HgT e, HgS, and HgSe
NCs.

5 Conclusions
We have developed a new tool for modeling colloidal semiconductor nanocrystals within the
effective mass approximation by adapting the xyz algorithm widely used to find eigenfre-
quencies for vibrational modes of both macroscopic elastic objects and nanoparticles and
based on the Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle. We demonstrated its extreme efficiency
and versatility by studying the influence of crystal structure and shape anisotropies on
valence-band states of InP colloidal nanocrystals destined to replace highly toxic cadmium
and lead contained quantum dots in numerous applications. We showed that, while the
isotropic approximation gives qualitatively valuable results for one or two lowest levels of a

hole confined in a spherical InP nanocrystal, it cannot correctly predict the symmetry, the
degeneracy, and the order of the levels starting already from the second or third level due
to the strong anisotropy of the underlying crystal lattice. Thus, the xyz algorithm will play
a decisive role in modeling ellipsoidal quantum dots and nanorods of III - V and II – VI
semiconductors and will be particularly valuable for cubic-structure nanocrystals.
