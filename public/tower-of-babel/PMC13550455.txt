Mechanism of matrine inhibiting retinoblastoma cell growth by downregulating NR1D2 to regulate phosphorylation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway
Authors: Li Kong, Yangrui Du, Xiaomei Lan, Qian Zheng, Qingqing Hu, Tao Li, Li Bin, Zhiyu Du
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study explored the mechanism of matrine-induced apoptosis in human retinoblastoma (RB) cell. Network pharmacology and proteomics approaches were employed to screen the intersection between the pharmacodynamic targets of matrine and the pathogenic targets of RB, and to identify the key signaling pathways underlying the anti-RB effect of matrine. Cytological experiments were used to evaluate cell proliferation, apoptosis, and the expression of PI3K/AKT pathway and apoptosis-related proteins. Results showed matrine inhibited Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell growth time- and dose-dependently, suppressed migration, and induced apoptosis. Integrated omics analysis identified Nuclear Receptor Subfamily 1 Group D Member 2 (NR1D2) as a primary responsive target. Matrine markedly downregulated NR1D2 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels. Matrine treatment and NR1D2 interference significantly promoted apoptosis (P < 0.05), while NR1D2 overexpression antagonized the pro-apoptotic effect of matrine. Total PI3K and AKT levels were unchanged, but their phosphorylated forms were altered; matrine-induced apoptosis correlated with Bax, Cleaved Caspase-3 upregulation and Bcl-2 downregulation. Thus, matrine inhibits RB Y79 cell growth by downregulating NR1D2, inhibiting PI3K/AKT phosphorylation and inducing RB cell apoptosis.

Introduction

Retinoblastoma (RB) has the highest morbidity among primary intraocular cancers during infancy 1, with an annual incidence rate of about 1:16,000–1:18,000 in newborns2. The incidence rate of RB is increasing3,4. In developed countries, a 95% 5-year survival rate of RB can be reached5, but the average survival rate in developing countries is only 50%6–9. The methods used to treat RB include chemotherapy, eyeball enucleation, radiotherapy, etc10. Especially, the application of chemotherapeutic drugs substantially increases the eye protection rate in RB patients, and in some patients, vision has even been preserved. In the early stage, VEC chemotherapy schemes based on vincristine (VCR), etoposide (VP16), and carboplatin (Carbo) were applied systemically11. However, to reduce systemic chemotherapeutic drug-related adverse reactions, the main treatment methods include selective ophthalmic artery chemotherapy combined with TTT or intraocular injection chemotherapy12–16. However, irrespective of the kind of chemotherapy administered, chemoresistance remains the major cause of failure of RB treatment or disease recurrence9,17; therefore, new, safe, and effective drugs with low side effects are needed for treating RB.

Chinese medicine has attracted much attention because of its many targets and low adverse reactions. Matrine has different pharmacological activities, like anti-inflammation, anti-tumor, bacteriostasis, and immunomodulation18, and is used for treating chronic hepatitis along with hepatocellular carcinoma19,20. Matrine has been extensively explored for its anti-tumor activity, but its effect and mechanism in RB are unclear. In this study, we investigated RB to determine how matrine affects RB growth and apoptosis. We also assessed its regulation on phosphatidylinositol 3 kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT) pathway, thus providing the theoretical foundation to treat RB with matrine.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Human RB Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells (Cat. No. iCell-h330; iCell Bioscience Inc., Shanghai, China) underwent culture within RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA) that contained 20% fetal bovine serum (Gibco,USA) as well as 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco,USA) under 37 °C, 5% CO2, and 95% saturated humidity. After a stable subculture for 2–3 generations, logarithmically growing cells were used in the experiment.

Detection of cell proliferation (CCK-8 assay) and migration assays

Logarithmic-phase Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells (2 × 103/well) were inoculated within 96-well plates. Next, different doses of matrine (0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4 mmol/L) were administered into experimental group, while cell suspension medium and cell-free medium were added in control group. Following 24, 48, and 72 h of culture, every well was introduced with 10 μL of CCK-8 solution, followed by 30 min of incubation. Next, an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay was conducted for measuring absorbance (OD) value at 450 nm, and cell survival rate (%) was determined by: [(experimental group OD-blank group OD)/(control group OD-blank group OD)] × 100%.

Cell migration capacity was assessed utilizing Transwell chamber assays. Cells pre-treated with serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chambers, while complete medium containing chemoattractants was added to the lower chambers. After a 24-h incubation period, the cells that migrated through the membrane were fixed, stained with crystal violet, and photographed under a microscope for quantitative analysis.

Network pharmacology detection

Potential targets of matrine and RB-related genes were retrieved from public databases, including PubChem, SwissTargetPrediction, and GeneCards. A Venn diagram was generated to identify the overlapping genes, and a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed utilizing the STRING database to screen for potential core targets. To further characterize the expression patterns of candidate target genes in RB, the GSE97508 dataset was downloaded from the GEO database to compare transcriptional differences between tumor tissues and normal retinal tissues. Molecular docking was performed to evaluate the potential binding capacity between matrine and the core target NR1D2.

Proteomic analysis

Following 24 h of 1 mM matrine treatment, Y79 cells were harvested by centrifugation, followed by freezing in liquid nitrogen. The protein was extracted by pyrolysis, the BCA approach was conducted to analyze protein level, and SDS-PAGE was subsequently conducted. Next, 100 µg of protein sample was hydrolyzed by protease, desalted, and quantified by peptide, and then detected by DIA mass spectrometry. The original data were imported into the Spectronaut™18 software system for searching the database and analyzing the data. p < 0.05 represented statistical significance, while proteins whose difference was greater than 1.5 were used as the screening criterion for differentially expressed proteins. GO function annotation analysis (biological process, cell composition, and molecular function) and KEGG enrichment were implemented on the differentially expressed proteins.

Plasmid construction, cell transfection, and lentivirus infection

First, NR1D2 shRNA was synthesized and connected to the linearized pLVX-shRNA2-Puro vector to obtain the target vector plasmid. Then, 293 T cells underwent co-transfection using target vector plasmid and packaging vector along with pseudotype (VSV-g) envelope vector. After 48 h, the virus supernatant was collected and filtered through centrifugation. After the virus titer was determined, Y79 cells and WERI-Rb-1 (six-well plate, 2 × 105 cells/well) received infection at MOI = 50. Following infection for 48 h, the complete medium containing puromycin (1 μg/mL) was replaced, and stably transfected cells were obtained after 4–6 days of screening.

Apoptosis detection through flow cytometry

First, Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells were classified in control, matrine (1 mM, 24 h), NR1D2 interference, NR1D2 overexpression, and NR1D2 overexpression + matrine groups. After collection and PBS washing, cells were subjected to resuspension within 195 μL of binding solution, followed by 10–20 min of incubation using Annexin V-FITC (5 μL) as well as PI (10 μL) successively at ambient temperature away from light. When these cells were incubated in an ice bath, a computer was adopted for determining the apoptotic cell proportion.

Detection of protein expression via Western blotting

First, Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells were classified into the control, matrine (1 mM, 24 h), NR1D2 interference, NR1D2 overexpression, and NR1D2 overexpression + matrine groups. Then, cells were collected before pyrolysis for separating total proteins; the proteins were quantified by the BCA method. A 10% SDS-PAGE gel was performed using the same amount of protein, followed by wet transfer onto the membrane after electrophoresis. After 1.5 h of blocking using 5% defatted milk powder under ambient temperature, the membrane received overnight primary antibody incubation under 4 °C. After TBST washing, the membrane underwent another 1.5 h of secondary antibody incubation under ambient temperature. After samples were washed with TBST, they were developed in the dark, and the ImageJ software was used to analyze the gray values.

Statistical analysis

Results were examined by GraphPad Prism 10.0 software, while measurement variables were represented by mean ± standard deviation (SD). T-tests were applied for analyzing between-group differences, while single-factor ANOVA for among-group counterparts. P < 0.05; *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ****P < 0.0001 stood for statistical significance.

Results

Matrine inhibits Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell proliferation

Matrine at different doses (0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, and 4 mmol/L) inhibited the proliferation of Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells to varying degrees after 24, 48, and 72 h. According to microscopic observations, Y79 cells of control group were abundant, uniform and round in size, clustered, and grew in suspension. After 24 h of matrine treatment, with the increasing matrine concentration, the Y79 cell density slowly declined, the morphology was gradually irregular, cells changed from clustered to dispersed, the suspension decreased, the cells were gradually deposited at the culture plate bottom, and the broken cell number gradually increased (Fig. 1). Network pharmacology detection

The 2D/3D chemical structures of matrine were retrieved from the PubChem database (Fig. 4A). Through integrating GeneCards, OMIM, and DisGeNET databases, Venn diagram analysis identified 22 overlapping targets (the intersection of matrine-related targets and RB-associated pathogenic targets), which were used for subsequent PPI network construction (Fig. 4B). Four core hub targets (HDAC2, HDAC3, HDAC6, and NR1D2) were screened from the PPI network (Fig. 4C). Validation based on the GEO dataset GSE97508 showed that NR1D2 was significantly upregulated in RB tumor tissues (p < 0.01), while other hub genes showed no significant expression differences (Fig. 4D). Molecular docking (AutoDock Vina) confirmed stable binding between matrine and NR1D2 (minimum binding energy: − 7.24 kcal/mol, far below the − 5.0 kcal/mol threshold), with specific binding mediated by hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions (Fig. 4E). Proteomic analysis revealed the downregulation of NR1D2

Proteomic analysis was conducted, and the results were compared between the cells in matrine-treated and control groups. As suggested through principal component analysis (PCA), these two groups showed clear separation in PC1 and PC2 dimensions (Fig. 5A), which suggested that matrine significantly affected the protein expression of Y79 cells after intervention. Differential analysis revealed 790 differentially expressed proteins after matrine treatment, including 311 upregulated proteins and 479 downregulated proteins (Fig. 5B). Volcano plot analysis of the differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) specifically highlighted a marked downregulation of core targets, including NR1D2, following matrine exposure (Fig. 5C). As suggested by the KEGG enrichment, those downregulated proteins were mostly associated with PI3K-Akt, MAPK, and Wnt pathways, and other key pathways associated with the growth and survival of tumors, indicating that matrine probably suppresses the growth and survival of Y79 cells by modulating these pathways (Fig. 5D). Matrine promotes the apoptosis of Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells by downregulating NR1D2

Previously, matrine treatment significantly reduced the NR1D2 protein level in Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells. We further explored the mechanism by which NR1D2 regulated matrine-induced Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell apoptosis. The expression of NR1D2 was regulated by transfection interference and overexpression plasmids, and Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell models with NR1D2 interference (shNR1D2) and overexpression (exNR1D2) were constructed. The apoptosis rates in the different groups at 24 h post-treatment were measured by conducting flow cytometry assays. We found that the Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 apoptosis rates in the matrine group and the NR1D2 interference group were evidently higher than control group (P < 0.05), that in NR1D2 overexpression group apparently declined versus control group (P < 0.05), while that in NR1D2 overexpression + matrine group remarkably elevated relative to control group but dramatically declined compared with matrine group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7A). As revealed by multiple comparisons, the apoptosis rate significantly differed across the matrine group, NR1D2 interference group, NR1D2 overexpression group, and NR1D2 overexpression + matrine group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7B). From the above results, matrine effectively enhances Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell apoptosis, while knocking down NR1D2 simulates matrine’s apoptosis-promotion efficiency, whereas NR1D2 overexpression antagonizes its apoptosis-promotion effect. After matrine was added to Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells overexpressing NR1D2, the decreasing trend of the apoptosis rate was reversed, which suggested that downregulating NR1D2 is a key mechanism by which matrine promoted Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell apoptosis.Fig. 7Function of matrine in Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell apoptosis. Matrine inhibits the phosphorylation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway by downregulating NR1D2.

To investigate matrine’s effect on PI3K/AKT pathway, key proteins within Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells were detected (Fig. 8). Compared to control group, total PI3K and AKT protein levels within Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells in the matrine, NR1D2 interference, NR1D2 overexpression, and NR1D2 overexpression + matrine groups were not markedly different (P > 0.05) (Fig. 8A and B). However, the phosphorylated proteins p-PI3K and p-AKT contents in PI3K/AKT pathway changed in each treatment group. Levels of these proteins in Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells in matrine and NR1D2 interference groups remarkably declined compared with control group (P < 0.05). In contrast, their contents within Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells in NR1D2 overexpression group apparently elevated compared with control group (P < 0.05). In NR1D2 overexpression + matrine group, NR1D2 overexpression substantially reversed matrine’s ability to inhibit p-PI3K and p-AKT, with markedly increased levels relative to matrine group, but their contents exhibited no obvious difference relative to control group (Fig. 8A and B). According to the above findings, matrine and direct interference with NR1D2 specifically suppressed PI3K/AKT pathway phosphorylation and activation but did not affect total protein level. Matrine regulates the expression of apoptosis-related proteins downstream of the PI3K/AKT pathway.

For assessing the downstream mechanism related to matrine-related apoptosis, key apoptotic proteins downstream of PI3K/AKT pathway within Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells were detected (Fig. 9). Bax (a pro-apoptotic protein) and Cleaved Caspase-3 (a apoptotic execution protein) levels in matrine group and NR1D2 interference group were apparently higher than control group (P < 0.05), whereas Bcl-2 (an anti-apoptotic protein) expression significantly decreased (P < 0.05). Bax and Cleaved Caspase-3 expression levels apparently decreased compared with control group (P < 0.05), whereas Bcl-2 contents remarkably increased (P < 0.05) in NR1D2 overexpression group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 9A and B). Relative to matrine group, Bax and Cleaved Caspase-3 contents markedly declined (P < 0.05), whereas Bcl-2 levels apparently increased (P < 0.05) in NR1D2 overexpression + matrine group. Overexpressing NR1D2 reverses matrine-related increased Bax and Cleaved Caspase-3 levels whereas declined Bcl-2 expression. According to the above results, matrine treatment and NR1D2 interference significantly up-regulated Bax and Cleaved Caspase-3 but down-regulated Bcl-2. NR1D2 overexpression blocked matrine-mediated regulation of these apoptosis-related proteins. Discussions

Applying chemotherapeutic drugs is the most important way to treat RB. Although these drugs significantly improve the eye protection and survival rates in RB patients, there are still cases of resistance to chemotherapy and side effects on the whole body or only the eyes. Therefore, novel, efficient, and low-toxicity anti-RB drugs need to be developed. Matrine is a bio-alkaline active component extracted from Sophora flavescens; as the tetracyclic quinacridine alkaloid, it has many physiological and pharmacological effects, comprising anti-inflammation, antioxidation, and anti-tumor. Matrine exerts a unique dual regulatory role in cancer biology and immune metabolism: it not only directly targets tumor cells to exert anti-tumor activity but also indirectly suppresses tumor progression by modulating the body’s immune-metabolic microenvironment, a characteristic that makes it a promising candidate molecule for the development of novel anti-tumor agents in clinical practice21. Matrine has anticancer effects on different cancer cells, such as those of breast cancer, multiple myeloma, liver cancer, gastric cancer, and glioma22–27. Matrine can play an anti-tumor role in many ways, mainly by blocking tumor-related pathways, including the PI3K/AKT/mTOR, JAK/STAT, NF-κB, Wnt/β-catenin and MAPK pathways, up-regulating the pro-apoptotic protein Bax, thus exerting strong anticancer effects, including suppressing cell growth, promoting autophagy and apoptosis, suppressing cell invasion and migration, and inhibiting tumor angiogenesis28. Compared to traditional chemotherapeutic drugs, matrine is much less toxic and has fewer side effects on the whole body. Therefore, determining whether matrine also has anticancer effects on RB provides new ideas for treating RB using drugs.

In this study, matrine strongly suppressed Y79 cell proliferation and accelerated apoptosis, and such effect increased as the duration of matrine treatment increased and the drug concentration increased, which agrees with matrine’s inhibition against other tumor cell proliferation. Flow cytometry analysis revealed that matrine treatment significantly elevated the apoptosis rate of Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells compared with the control group, confirming its apoptosis-inducing effect on these retinoblastoma cells. Transwell assays further verified that matrine inhibits the migration of Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells, thereby impairing their migratory capacity. Collectively, these results indicate that matrine holds potential as a candidate active ingredient for the development of anti-retinoblastoma agents.

Traditional Chinese medicine is featured by multiple components, targets and pathways, making it difficult to fully clarify its intrinsic mechanism of action. Network pharmacology constructs drug–target–disease interaction networks and analyzes topological characteristics, which can systematically reveal the holistic regulatory pattern of drugs on disease networks. It is highly consistent with the holistic concept and syndrome differentiation of TCM, and is well applicable to the mechanism research of TCM formulas and active monomers. PPI network analysis identified four hub genes: HDAC2, HDAC3, HDAC6 and NR1D2. These genes were closely interconnected in the network, suggesting their core regulatory roles in the anti-retinoblastoma (RB) action of matrine. GEO transcriptome validation confirmed significant upregulation of NR1D2 in RB tissues, while other hub genes showed no consistent expression changes; aberrant NR1D2 overexpression is closely linked to RB occurrence and progression, indicating its clinical relevance as a potential therapeutic target. Molecular docking revealed that the binding affinity of matrine to NR1D2 was − 7.24 kcal/mol, below the effective threshold of − 5.0 kcal/mol, and matrine stably bound to the active site of NR1D2 via hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions with key amino acid residues, which validates the direct high-affinity interaction between matrine and NR1D2 and supports NR1D2 as a direct target for matrine against RB.

Proteomics provides a high-throughput strategy to explore drug targets and signaling pathways. In the present study, 790 differentially expressed proteins were screened in matrine-treated Y79 cells, among which downregulated proteins were mainly enriched in PI3K-Akt, MAPK and Wnt pathways critical for tumor proliferation, survival, migration and invasion. These pathway alterations were consistent with matrine’s anti-RB phenotype, implying that matrine exerts anti-tumor effects by regulating multiple oncogenic cascades. Notably, matrine markedly downregulated NR1D2 protein expression, which cross-validated network pharmacology predictions and further verified the central role of NR1D2 in mediating matrine’s anti-RB effect. NR1D2 (also called REV-ERBβ) is one of the nuclear receptor superfamily members. REV-ERB can regulate many biological processes that depend on circadian rhythm and metabolism and can participate in many physiological and pathological processes as an inhibitory regulator, including tumor development, metabolism, proliferation, and inflammation; therefore, it has a very wide regulatory role. A recent study revealed its high expression within tumors, which is tightly related to tumor growth and metastasis, degree of malignancy, patient prognosis, and other factors29. To determine the function of NR1D2 in matrine, we constructed Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell models with NR1D2 interference or overexpression. Specifically, knocking down NR1D2 significantly enhanced Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell apoptosis, and NR1D2 overexpression had the opposite effect. Matrine reversed the resistance to apoptosis induced by the overexpression of NR1D2, suggesting that NR1D2 is the key mediator by which matrine suppresses Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell proliferation, whereas matrine accelerates Y79 cell apoptosis by decreasing NR1D2 expression.

Matrine suppresses PI3K/AKT pathway to suppress proliferation of breast cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, gastric cancer and liver cancer cells30. The PI3K/AKT signaling pathway exists extensively across all kinds of cells and exerts an important regulatory effect on signaling activities correlated with cell proliferation, invasion, and apoptosis31,32. Abnormal activation of this pathway can activate various downstream substrates through phosphorylation, thereby leading to healthy cell conversion in malignant cells, enhancing cell proliferation, invasion, and migration, while inhibiting apoptosis33. This study also analyzed whether NR1D2 regulation by matrine also enhances Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell apoptosis via PI3K/AKT pathway. Matrine does not affect PI3K or AKT total protein level but can strongly inhibit their phosphorylation, which ultimately elevating Bax, declining Bcl-2 expression, while activating Caspase-3 to execute the apoptotic program, thereby promoting the apoptosis of Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells. In functional rescue experiments, the overexpression of NR1D2 can antagonize the inhibition induced by matrine against PI3K/AKT phosphorylation, which further suggests that the downregulation of NR1D2 inhibits matrine-mediated PI3K/AKT phosphorylation. In this study, matrine inhibited the phosphorylation of PI3K/AKT and promoted apoptosis, which was consistent with the finding that matrine inhibits glioblastoma cell, triple-negative breast cancer cell, and liver cancer cell proliferation34–36.

Despite the mechanistic insights provided in this study, several inherent limitations remain to be addressed in subsequent research. First, pharmacological inhibition targeting the PI3K/AKT signaling axis was not implemented to functionally verify the proposed regulatory cascade, which is indispensable for definitive confirmation of the pathway-mediated antitumor effects of matrine in retinoblastoma cells. Second, promoter activity assessment and in-depth transcriptional regulatory validation were absent in the present work, hindering the systematic delineation of upstream transcriptional mechanisms governing NR1D2 expression and function in retinoblastoma. Furthermore, in vivo animal models were not established to corroborate the antitumor efficacy and underlying molecular mechanism of matrine under physiological microenvironment conditions.Future investigations will prioritize overcoming these experimental deficiencies through comprehensive functional validations including pathway inhibitor intervention, promoter-related transcriptional assays, and in vivo preclinical studies. These follow-up explorations are expected to refine the mechanistic elucidation of matrine against retinoblastoma and generate more robust preclinical evidence to facilitate its potential translational and clinical application.

Conclusion

To summarize, this study revealed that matrine remarkably suppresses RB Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cell proliferation, promotes their apoptosis. Matrine restrains the PI3K/AKT pathway phosphorylation activation through decreasing NR1D2 expression, thereby promoting Bax while suppressing Bcl-2 expression, ultimately over-expressing Caspase-3 to promote the apoptosis of Y79 and WERI-Rb-1 cells. This mechanism explains the anti-proliferative effect of matrine on RB, and lays the theoretical foundation for developing new anti-RB treatments. Although our study revealed that matrine suppresses PI3K/AKT pathway through decreasing NR1D2 expression, the NR1D2 downstream regulatory network and its interaction with other apoptosis-related signaling pathways have not been identified. Further multitarget interaction studies are needed, and these mechanisms should be investigated in future studies.
