Prospective exploratory pilot study of 18 F-FMISO PET/CT for assessing tumor hypoxia in soft tissue sarcomas
Authors: Ronnie Sebro, Ashley M. Fuller, T.S. Karin Eisenger-Matheson, Christina Dulal, Austin R. Pantel, Jacob Shabason, William Levin, Kumarasen Cooper, Paul Zhang, Daniel A. Pryma, Michael D. Farwell, Jacob G. Dubroff, David A. Mankoff
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hypoxia is associated with poor outcomes in soft tissue sarcoma (STS) and is linked to increased expression of hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α), carbonic anhydrase IX (CAIX), and lysyl hydroxylase 2 (PLOD2). These factors are potential therapeutic targets. This exploratory study aimed to investigate the correlation between 18F-fluoromisonidazole (18F-FMISO) positron emission tomography (PET) imaging, a marker of hypoxia, and the expression of these biomarkers in STS. This imaging phase II clinical trial (NCT #03730077) recruited 5 patients with suspected soft tissue sarcoma (STS) and imaged these patients with 18F-Fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) and 18F-FMISO within 2 weeks of each other. STS tissue was analyzed for HIF-1α, CAIX, and PLOD2 expression using immunohistochemistry and western blotting. Collagen formation was assessed by Masson’s trichrome. In this small sample, a correlation was found between STS size and PLOD2 expression (r = 0.996, P = 0.004). STS grade correlated positively with 18F-FMISO T/M (tumor/muscle) SUVmax (r = 0.894, P = 0.041). 18F-FDG correlated positively with 18F-FMISO SUVmax (r = 0.994, P = 0.006). Positive but non-significant associations were noted between 18F-FMISO T/M SUVmax and CAIX (r = 0.95, P > 0.05) and PLOD2 (r = 0.93, P > 0.05). These preliminary observations in a small sample are hypothesis-generating at best and must be interpreted with substantial caution due to the extremely limited number of patients, which precludes robust statistical inference and generalizability. No definitive conclusions can be drawn regarding the relationship between 18F-FMISO uptake and hypoxia biomarker expression. Larger, adequately powered prospective studies are essential to validate these findings, clarify potential correlations, and determine the clinical utility of 18F-FMISO PET/CT as a noninvasive hypoxia biomarker in STS.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s40644-026-01069-x.

Introduction

Soft tissue sarcomas (STS) are rare tumors of mesenchymal origin with approximately 13,000 newly diagnosed cases each year in the United States [1]. Prognosis depends on the stage at patient presentation [2, 3]. Patients with localized disease have a 5-year survival of approximately 81%, whereas patients who present with metastatic disease have a 5-year survival of approximately 15% [2, 3]. The lungs are the most frequent site of metastasis and understanding the tumor biology of metastasis could identify important targets that could arrest sarcomas at the local disease stage and prevent the development of metastasis and improve disease prognosis [4–6].

Intratumoral hypoxia plays a critical role in metastasis [7–9]. In sarcomas, high levels of intratumoral hypoxia and/or hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) expression are associated with increased resistance to radiation therapy, shorter time to metastasis, and worse overall prognosis [10–12]. High expression levels of HIF-1α targets such as lysyl hydroxylase 2 (PLOD2), a collagen-modifying enzyme, and carbonic anhydrase IX (CAIX), a regulator of intra/extracellular pH, are also prognostic for reduced long-term survival among sarcoma patients [13–15]. Mechanisms by which these factors promote STS metastasis represent active areas of investigation. In particular, aberrant PLOD2 upregulation facilitates both early and late stages of pulmonary metastasis by disrupting the organization of fibrillar collagen molecules in the STS tumor microenvironment, enhancing local sarcoma cell migration, compromising lung endothelial integrity, and promoting sarcoma cell transmigration and lung colonization [13, 16]. Therefore, identification of non-invasive clinical biomarkers of these hypoxia-associated factors/processes could improve assessments of disease progression and metastatic risk among STS patients.

18F-Fluoromisonidazole (18F-FMISO) is a radiolabeled nitroimidazole positron emission tomography (PET) tracer has been studied in several tumors [14, 15], and has been used to non-invasively measure loco-regional tumor hypoxia in vivo [16–23]. Here, we hypothesize that (1) hypoxia levels would vary by sarcoma tumor grade, (2) that sarcoma hypoxia is correlated with glycolysis and that PET/CT hypoxia tracer (18F-FMISO) tumor-to-muscle (T/M) uptake ratios will be correlated with the maximum standardized uptake values (SUVmax) obtained from 18F-Fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) PET/CT, (3) that 18F-FMISO T/M uptake ratios correlate with intra-tumoral markers of hypoxia, including HIF-1α, CAIX, and PLOD2 expression levels in subjects with STS and (4) that we can identify the best predictor of intra-tumoral PLOD2 expression levels using 18F-FMISO and 18F-FDG PET/CT uptake values, HIF-1α and CAIX. The novel goals, not investigated in prior STS PET studies, are (1) to examine the association of 18F-FMISO as a non-invasive indicator of the expression of PLOD2, to guide biopsy tissue sampling in future and (2) as a potential imaging diagnostic test for PLOD2-targeted therapy.

Materials and methods

This was a prospective phase II imaging clinical trial registered with United States National Library of Medicine (NLM) at the National Institutes of Health (NIH) (registry: www.clinicaltrials.gov; trial registration #NCT03730077; date of registration: 2/1/2019). The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the local Institutional Review Board (IRB) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Investigational New Drug (IND) committee for the use of the investigational imaging agent 18F-FMISO. Each subject provided signed informed consent at the time of entry into the study.

Histopathology

Histopathological tumor diagnosis and tumor grade were determined by a pathologist board-certified by the American Board of Pathology (ABP).

Tissues were first sectioned to 5 μm in thickness using a microtome. Immunohistochemistry was performed on a Leica Bond Rxm automated IHC/ISH stainer using the Bond Refined Detection kit and antibodies to HIF-1α (1:250; #100066421, Cayman Chemical, Ann Arbor, MI, USA), CAIX (1:500; #NB-100-417, Novus Biologicals, Centennial, CO, USA), and PLOD2 (1:200; #21214-1-AP, Proteintech, Chicago, IL, USA). Nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded biopsy sections were stained with Masson’s Trichrome according to standard protocols to visualize fibrillar collagen fibers. Stained slides were scanned into digital images using an Aperio VERSA scanner at 20x magnification (Leica Biosystems, Heidelberg, Germany) and analyzed with Aperio ImageScope software (positive pixel count algorithm for all markers). The Aperio ImageScope software was used to objectively and reliably quantify the pixel counts for HIF-1α, CAIX, and PLOD2.

Imaging methods

PET imaging was performed using a Big-Bore whole-body PET/CT scanner (Philips Medical Systems, Netherland). The scan length depended on the size of the area to be imaged as the field of view (FOV) was selected based on the location and size of the tumor.

PET images were reconstructed using a time-of-flight–enabled ordered subsets expectation maximization algorithm (BLOB-OS-TF; Philips Medical Systems implementation) on a Big Bore whole-body PET/CT scanner. Standard reconstruction parameters consisted of 3 iterations and 31 subsets, consistent with commonly used clinical protocols for this platform.

18F-FMISO imaging protocol

Radiolabeling

18F fluoride is delivered from the cyclotron trapped on an ion-exchange cartridge. It is then eluted into the reaction vial (RV) using a Kryptofix-222/potassium carbonate solution. The contents of the reaction vial are evaporated to dryness at 105 °C for 4 min under nitrogen flow and vacuum. A solution of the radiolabeling precursor in 1 mL of extra-dry acetonitrile is added, and the mixture is heated at 100 °C for 4 min to perform the radiolabeling reaction. After labeling, the protecting group is removed by adding 0.5 mL of 1 N HCl diluted in 0.5 mL water and heating at 90 °C for 3 min. The reaction is subsequently quenched with 2.5 mL of water containing 0.5 mL of 1 N NaOH and passed through an Alumina N cartridge before being transferred to the work-up station for HPLC purification.

Purification and formulation

For purification and formulation, the crude reaction mixture is injected onto a Phenomenex Synergi 5 μm (25 × 100 mm) HPLC column and eluted with 0.01 M phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 5% ethanol at a flow rate of 4 mL/min. The product fraction is collected and diluted with 50 mL of water, then passed through a solid-phase extraction Sep-Pak cartridge (Oasis HLB, 540 mg) under nitrogen pressure. The cartridge is washed with 5 mL of water, and the product is eluted with 1.5 mL of ethanol into the final product vial (FPV). Finally, the cartridge is rinsed with 15 mL of water containing approximately 1.8 mg of sodium ascorbate, and the rinse is also collected in the FPV.

18F-FMISO scans were obtained within 2 weeks of percutaneous biopsies. An intravenous (IV) line was placed for the injection of the radiotracer. A dose of 5–10 mCi (185–370 MBq) of 18F-FMISO was administered. Subjects were imaged in the supine position, approximately 60 min after the injection of the tracer at 3 min per bed. All 18F-FMISO scans were research scans. These scans had to cover the abdomen (to include the liver) and the entire tumor. The transaxial FOV was ~ 70 cm and the axial FOV varied from 15 to 25 cm. A low-dose computed tomography (CT) scan (100–140 kVp, variable mA, 5 mm slice thickness) was acquired for attenuation correction and anatomical localization of findings in the PET scan.

18F-FMISO measurements

A circular region of interest (ROI) was centered over the biopsied area based on the biopsy tract to include the entire tumor, and the sarcoma maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) was automatically calculated using MIM software (Cleveland, OH, USA). The lesion ROI size was not fixed but based on the extension of the morphological lesion on CT. Another circular ROI was placed over adjacent normal skeletal muscle matched to the site of the STS and the muscle SUVmax was calculated following the method described by Rajendran et al. [16].

18F-FDG imaging protocol

18F-FDG PET/CT studies were obtained within 2 weeks of percutaneous biopsies, and at least 24 h before or after the 18F-FMISO PET/CT studies. For the 18F-FDG PET/CT studies that were utilized in this study, subjects had to fast for at least 4 h prior to the study and had to have a blood glucose level less than 200 mg/dl (11.1 mmol/L) prior to the injection of the radiotracer. For clinical studies, the field of view (FOV) was from the skull-base to the mid-thighs. The subject was given approximately 225 ml of dilute oral contrast (approximately 9 g barium sulphate) (Readi-Cat2, Bracco, NJ, USA) approximately 30 min before, and then after the intravenous administration of the radiotracer. Subjects #1 and #2 had research 18F-FDG PET/CTs, Subject #3 had a clinical 18F-FDG PET/CTs, Subject #4 did not have a 18F-FDG PET/CT and Subject #5 had a simulation 18F-FDG PET/CT for radiation treatment planning. The FOV was the whole body (vertex to toes) if it was a clinical/research study (x3) or low abdomen to mid leg for the simulation study. The transaxial FOV was ~ 70 cm and the axial FOV varied from 15 to 25 cm. The FOV size was 50–60 cm to ensure the entire body contour was included for attenuation correction. Subjects were injected with between 8 and 20 mCi (296–740 MBq) of 18F-FDG and imaged in the supine position approximately 60 min after the injection at 3 min per bed. A low-dose CT scan (100–140 kVp, variable mA, 5 mm slice thickness) was also acquired for attenuation correction and anatomical localization of findings in the PET scan. A 512 × 512 reconstruction matrix was utilized.

18F-FDG measurements

A circular ROI was centered over the biopsied area, in the same location at the 18F-FMISO scan and the SUVmax of the sarcoma was calculated. Again, the lesion ROI size was not fixed but based on the extension of the morphological lesion on CT. All measurements were made by an imaging researcher with 2 years of experience supervised by a board-certified radiologist without knowledge of the subject’s clinical history and data.

Statistical analysis

First, descriptive statistics of the demographic and clinical variables were evaluated.

Primary analyses

Our primary goal was to assess whether in vivo measures of hypoxia measured by 18F-FMISO were correlated with in vitro measures of hypoxia which were assessed using HIF-1α, CAIX, and PLOD2. We used Pearson’s correlation coefficients to assess these associations.

Exploratory analyses

We evaluated how in vivo measures of hypoxia measured by 18F-FMISO were correlated with tumor size, tumor grade, in vivo measures of anerobic glycolysis measured by 18F-FDG and collagen synthesis measured by MT. Pearson’s correlations were used to evaluate associations between quantitative variables and Spearman’s correlations were used to evaluate associations between quantitative and qualitative/ordinal variables.

All test statistics were two-sided, and the a priori Type I error rate was set to 0.05. Statistics were performed using Rv3.4 (https://www.r-project.org).

Discussion

This preliminary study investigated 18F-FMISO as an integrated biomarker to direct PLOD2-related treatment, however we found no statistically significant associations between 18F-FMISO T/M SUVmax and HIF-1α, CAIX, and PLOD2 levels. Exploratory analyses suggested a positive association between sarcoma size and PLOD2 expression. The exploratory analysis also suggested that there was a positive correlation between tumor grade and 18F-FMISO T/M SUVmax. In vivo tumor glycolysis measured by 18F-FDG SUVmax was positively correlated with the in vivo measure of hypoxia (18F-FMISO T/M SUVmax) in the exploratory analyses.

The positive association detected between 18F-FMISO uptake measures and tumor grade in the exploratory analysis, has not been shown previously. Similarly, the positive association between tumor size and PLOD2 expression levels detected in the exploratory analysis is novel. While Rajendran et al. noted a non-significant correlation between 18F-FDG and 18F-FMISO pixel counts of 0.49 [16], we found an association between 18F-FDG uptake and 18F-FMISO uptake in our exploratory analysis.

The exploratory analysis suggested that tumor size was positively correlated with both tumor PLOD2 levels and 18F-FDG SUVmax. We hypothesize that as sarcomas get larger, they may outstrip their vascular supply, increasing both intra-tumoral hypoxia and aerobic glycolysis. The exploratory analysis also suggested that tumor grade was positively correlated with both 18F-FMISO SUVmax, and 18F-FMISO T/M SUVmax. Based on this result we hypothesized that higher grade tumors showed increased intra-tumoral hypoxia in our study. A known downstream product of the increased hypoxia is increased PLOD2 levels, which affect collagen fibrin formation [13].

The study has a few limitations. The sample size limits the power of the study to detect associations between in vivo imaging and in vitro tissue measures. However this is an expected challenge given the rarity of patients with soft tissue sarcomas, and the ability to prospectively recruit these patients into trials. Only lesions measuring 2 cm or greater were included in this study to comply with the registered prospective protocol. Metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) are important tumor metrics in determining lesion aggressiveness and differentiation grade. We did not study these metrics because the study protocol stated that we would use the SUVmax and tumor-to-background ratio (TBR) as the outcomes of interest, and post-hoc analysis using these metrics would either inflate the Type I error rate or decrease the power of the study after adjusting for multiple comparisons. Further research is needed to confirm these associations and to investigate the predictive values of MTV and TLG in this setting.

In summary, the small study size limits conclusions about the association of PLOD2 and 18F-FMISO uptake levels to support 18F-FMISO PET as a companion diagnostic for PLOD2-targeted therapy. Exploratory analyses found positive associations between PLOD2 levels and tumor size, 18F-FMISO uptake and tumor grade, and 18F-FMISO uptake with 18F-FDG uptake. These associations need to be confirmed in a future study in a larger study population.
