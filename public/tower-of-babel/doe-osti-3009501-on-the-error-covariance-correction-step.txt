subject: On the Error Covariance Correction Step of an ESKF Attitude Update

 The attitude states of an error-state Kalman filter (ESKF) behave differently than most other
 states in the system due to their multiplicative (rather than additive) nature. One way in which
 they differ is an error covariance correction step after an ESKF error reset, which is not
 required for, for example, position and velocity states [1, 2, 3, 4]. This covariance correction
 step is not intuitive, and it has only been recently derived for coordinate transform matrices
 [1]. The author of this memo, however, found the provided derivation in [1] confusing due to
 a lack of clarity surrounding the invoked reference frames, and clarity is required as there are
 at least 4 different ways to parameterize small-angle attitude errors in an ESKF. Furthermore,
 while reproducing the work, the author of this memo found a more straightforward derivation
 that provides additional insight into the correction step. This memo offers a derivation of the
 attitude error covariance correction step of an ESKF, which pays specific attention to the
 coordinate reference frames.

This position error state example was simple and straightforward because the error state

 Concluding Remarks

 This memo derived the correction step for the attitude error covariance of an error-state Kalman
 filter. The derived equations are only appropriate for the attitude convention adopted in this
 text, and readers should take note of any differences in convention before applying the
 equations. Some insight was provided into the difference between the pre- and post-reset
 frames of reference and how this difference affects the final error covariance expressions. This
 begs the question: is a similar covariance correction step necessary for position and velocity
 states, which are estimated in the pre-reset frame of reference?
