Universal criticality of entropy production in chemical reaction networks
Kyota Tamano, Keiji Saito

Abstract
Stochastic thermodynamics gives universal relations for microscopic entropy production, yet its critical behavior at macroscopic nonequilibrium transitions remains unclassified. We study well-mixed reversible chemical reaction networks in the macroscopic-first limit, where transitions arise as local bifurcations of mass-action dynamics. Using linear-noise formulas, center-manifold normal forms, and Floquet theory, we obtain generic exponents for entropy-production fluctuations and responses at pitchfork, transcritical, saddle-node, and Hopf bifurcations. Beyond this low-order classification, a trajectory-space Cramér–Rao type bound yields the universal scaling inequality α − 2 ​ β ≥ 0 \alpha-2\beta\geq 0 . Hence divergent responses require divergent fluctuations, but not conversely, making entropy-production fluctuations a sharper probe of nonequilibrium criticality.

\displaystyle{\partial_{\theta}\sigma}

\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
. One can readily verify that the generic exponents listed in Table
1
satisfy this relation. Furthermore, for the Schlögl model
Schlögl 1972
;
Vellela and Qian 2008
, which exhibits a cusp bifurcation, recent numerical computations along a path through the critical point estimated the critical exponents to be
\alpha=1.3\pm 0.2
and
\beta=0.65\pm 0.05

Remlein and Seifert 2024
. These results strongly suggest that the equality in relation (
13

\partial_{\theta}\sigma
diverges (i.e.,
\beta>0
),
{\rm Var}\sigma
must strictly diverge (i.e.,
\alpha>0
). The converse, however, is not necessarily true, as corroborated by the specific critical exponents in Table
1
. This implies that fluctuations reflect criticality more sensitively than parametric responses.

Concluding remarks.—

In this paper, we elucidate the generic critical behavior of entropy production at macroscopic bifurcation points. By deriving general formulas for the entropy production variance Eqs. (
9
)-(
10
) and the parametric response (
11
), we derive the universality-class classification on the critical exponents
\alpha
and
\beta
using
center manifold theory
, as shown in Table
1
. The general mechanism for divergence was also clarified. Moreover, we establish a universal inequality valid for any bifurcation (Eq. (
13
)), dictating that a diverging response (
\beta>0
) strictly necessitates diverging fluctuations (
\alpha>0
), but not vice versa. This indicates that fluctuations are more sensitive indicators of criticality than responses.

We finally remark on the order of limits between the volume scale
\Omega
and the observation time
\tau
. We here take the macroscopic-scale limit
\Omega\to\infty
first before taking long observation time, as in (
8
) and (
11
). On the other hand, recent study for the Schlögl model reports the exponentially large fluctuation of the entropy production with respect to the size for the saddle-node bifurcation
Nguyen and Seifert 2020
, which never occurs in our setup. This effect emerges because they look at the long time limit first before taking the macroscopic-scale limit. The critical behavior can be sensitive on the relative scales of observation time and system size, which somewhat reminds us of Keizer’s paradox in the chemical reaction
Keizer 1979
;
Vellela and Qian 2007
. It is intriguing to elucidate the observation-time dependence in the critical phenomena.

S.I
Well-mixed chemical reaction

We consider a well-mixed reversible chemical reaction network, where spatial inhomogeneities are neglected so that the state of the system is fully characterized by the concentrations of the chemical species, without reference to spatial coordinates. , we can consider the dynamics of the deviation from the deterministic motion
{\bm{x}}(t)
in the form

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(\Gamma)/\Omega

\displaystyle\to x_{\ell}(t)+y_{\ell}(t)/\sqrt{\Omega}\,,

(S.22)

where the deterministic part
x_{\ell}(t)
obeys the equation
\dot{x}_{\ell}=F_{\ell}({\bm{x}})=\sum_{\rho}\nabla^{\ell}_{\rho}J_{\rho}({\bm{x}})
and
y_{\ell}(t)/\sqrt{\Omega}
is the fluctuating deviation part. We again emphasize that this decomposition is mathematically justified by rigorous analysis by Kurtz
Kurtz 1972
;
Kurtz 1971
. Likewise, one can also consider the fluctuating deviation from the systematic cumulative number of occurrences per volume in the form

\displaystyle Z_{\rho}(\Gamma)/\Omega

\displaystyle\to z_{\rho}(t)+w_{\rho}(t)/\sqrt{\Omega}\,,

(S.23)

where the deterministic part
z_{\rho}(t)
obeys the equation
\dot{z}_{\rho}=J_{\rho}({\bm{x}})
.

The Langevin equations with respect to
{\bm{y}}(t)
and
{\bm{w}}(t)
are readily obtained by considering the Fokker-Planck equation for the variable
\bm{y}
defining

\displaystyle f({\bm{y}},t)

\displaystyle:={1\over\Omega^{|{\cal S}|/2}}\sum_{{\bm{n}}}P({\bm{n}},t)\prod_{\ell\in{\cal S}}\delta(n_{\ell}-\Omega(x_{\ell}(t)+y_{\ell}/\sqrt{\Omega}))\,.

(S.24)

The time-evolution of the distribution
f({\bm{y}},t)
is computed as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f({\bm{y}},t)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Omega}\sum_{\ell}\dot{x}_{\ell}{\partial\over\partial y_{\ell}}f({\bm{y}},t)+{1\over\Omega^{|{\cal S}|/2}}\sum_{{\bm{n}}}\partial_{t}P({\bm{n}},t)\prod_{\ell\in{\cal S}}\delta(n_{\ell}-\Omega x_{\ell}(t)-\sqrt{\Omega}y_{\ell})\,.

(S.25)

Performing the formal Taylor’s expansion and using integration by parts several times, one arrives at the following expression in the large
\Omega
limit:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f({\bm{y}},t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}{\partial\over\partial y_{\ell}}\sum_{\rho}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell}{y}_{\ell^{\prime}}{\partial\over\partial x_{\ell^{\prime}}}({J}_{+\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))-{J}_{-\rho}({\bm{x}}(t)))f({\bm{y}},t)

\displaystyle+{1\over 2}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}{\partial^{2}\over\partial y_{\ell}\partial y_{\ell^{\prime}}}\sum_{\rho}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}({J}_{+\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))+{J}_{-\rho}({\bm{x}}(t)))f({\bm{y}},t)\,.

(S.26)

Through this Fokker-Planck equation, one can identify the corresponding Langevin equation:

\displaystyle\dot{y}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}{\partial{F_{\ell}}({\bm{x}}(t))\over\partial x_{\ell^{\prime}}}y_{\ell^{\prime}}+\sum_{\rho}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell}\sqrt{{\cal A}_{\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))}\,\xi_{\rho}(t)\,,

(S.27)

\displaystyle\dot{w}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}{\partial{J}_{\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))\over\partial{x}_{\ell^{\prime}}}y_{\ell^{\prime}}+\sqrt{{\cal A}_{\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))}\,{\xi}_{\rho}(t)\,.

(S.28)

Here,
\xi_{\rho}(t)
is a Gaussian white noise satisfying
\langle\!\langle\xi_{\rho}(t)\rangle\!\rangle=0
and
\langle\!\langle\xi_{\rho}(t)\xi_{\rho^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\rangle\!\rangle=\delta_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\delta(t-t^{\prime})
, where
\langle\!\langle...\rangle\!\rangle
implies a noise average. The amplitude on the noise
{\cal A}_{\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(\bm{x}(t))

\displaystyle:=\left[{J}_{+\rho}(\bm{x}(t))+{J}_{-\rho}(\bm{x}(t))\right]\,.

(S.29)

S.III.2
Fluctuation of entropy production

We consider the entropy fluctuation based on the physical environment entropy (
S.13
). We aim to derive the formal expression of fluctuation of the entropy production per unit time, defined as

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\sigma

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\lim_{\Omega\to\infty}{1\over\Omega}\,{\rm Var}\Sigma_{\rm e}=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\lim_{\Omega\to\infty}{1\over\Omega}\left[\Bigl<\left(\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}Z_{\rho}(\Gamma)\right)^{2}\Bigr>-\langle\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}Z_{\rho}(\Gamma)\rangle^{2}\right]\,,

(S.30)

where
{\rm Var}\Sigma_{\rm e}
implies the variance of the environment entropy production. Here, note that we take the thermodynamic limit first and we next consider the long time average. The order of the limitation is crucial, since the physical properties around the bifurcation point are very sensitive on the order. Note that taking the thermodynamic first is a standard procedure to consider the phase transition behavior.

Since we consider macroscopic-scale fluctuation of the entropy-production, we employ the Langevin equations (
S.27
) and (
S.28
). The formal solution of
{\bm{y}}(t)
is readily obtained for the initial conditions
{{\bm{y}}(t=0)}={\bm{0}}
:

\displaystyle{y}_{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\Phi_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(t,s)\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(\bm{x}(s))}\,{\xi}_{\rho}(s)\,,

(S.31)

\displaystyle\Phi(t,s)

\displaystyle:={\cal T}\exp\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}ds^{\prime}{\partial{\bm{F}}(\bm{x}(s^{\prime}))\over\partial{\bm{x}}}\Bigr]\,,

(S.32)

where
{\cal T}
is the time-ordering operator. Using this solution, under the condition
{\bm{w}}(t=0)={\bm{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle{w}_{\rho}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds\,G_{\rho,\,\rho^{\prime}}(\tau,s)\,\xi_{\rho^{\prime}}(s)\,,

(S.33)

where the Green’s function matrix is given by

\displaystyle G_{\rho,\,\rho^{\prime}}(\tau,s)

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\int_{s}^{\tau}dt\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}{{J}_{\rho}(\bm{x}(t))\over\partial{x}_{\ell}}\Phi_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(t,s)\nabla^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}}+\delta_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\Bigr]\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{\rho^{\prime}}(\bm{x}(s))}

(S.34)

Note that the target quantity (
S.30
) is obtained as

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\sigma

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\lim_{\Omega\to\infty}{1\over\Omega}\left[\Bigl<\left(\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}Z_{\rho}(\Gamma)\right)^{2}\Bigr>-\langle\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}Z_{\rho}(\Gamma)\rangle^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\sum_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\mu_{\rho}\langle\!\langle w_{\rho}(\tau)w_{\rho^{\prime}}(\tau)\rangle\!\rangle\mu_{\rho^{\prime}}\,,

(S.35)

where we use the fact that the path-probability average is replaced by the noise average in the Langevin picture. The covariance matrix with respect to the variables
{\bm{w}}
is given as

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{1}{\tau}\langle\!\langle w_{\rho}(\tau)w_{\rho^{\prime}}(\tau)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds\sum_{\rho^{\prime\prime}}G_{\rho,\,\rho^{\prime\prime}}(\tau,s)\,G_{\rho^{\prime},\,\rho^{\prime\prime}}(\tau,s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds\sum_{\rho^{\prime\prime}}\left[\int_{s}^{\tau}dt_{1}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}{\partial J_{\rho}({\bm{x}}(t_{1}))\over\partial x_{\ell}}\Phi_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(t_{1},s)\nabla^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime\prime}}+\delta_{\rho,\,\rho^{\prime\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~\times\mathcal{A}_{\rho^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{x}(s))\left[\int_{s}^{\tau}dt_{2}\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}{\partial J_{\rho^{\prime}}({\bm{x}}(t_{2}))\over\partial x_{m}}\Phi_{m,m^{\prime}}(t_{2},s)\nabla^{m^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime\prime}}+\delta_{\rho^{\prime},\,\rho^{\prime\prime}}\right]\,.

(S.36)

After manipulation, we arrive at the following formula when the stable steady solution of Eq. (
S.6
) is a fixed point such as in pitchfork, transcritical, and saddle-node bifurcations:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm Var}\sigma&=\sum_{\rho\in{\cal R}}v_{\rho}^{2}(\bar{\bm{x}}){\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}})\,,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~[{\rm Fixed~point~case}]\\
v_{\rho}&=\mu_{\rho}-\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in{\cal S}}{\partial_{\bar{x}_{\ell}}\sigma(\bar{\bm{x}})}[{\mathbb{S}}^{-1}(\bar{\bm{x}})]_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\,,\end{split}

(S.37)

where
\bar{\bm{x}}
is the stable fixed point solution in Eq.(
S.6
), and
\mathbb{S}(\bar{\bm{x}})
is the stability matrix (Jacobian) evaluated at the fixed point, with elements
[\mathbb{S}(\bar{\bm{x}})]_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=\partial F_{\ell}/\partial x_{\ell^{\prime}}|_{\bm{x}=\bar{\bm{x}}}
.
Conversely, when the stable steady solution of (
S.6
) is a limit cycle (LC), typically arising from a Hopf bifurcation, the formula is given by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm Var}\sigma&=\!\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\!{1\over\tau}\!\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!ds\!\sum_{\rho}\!v_{\rho}^{2}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s)){\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))\,,~~~~~~~[{\rm Limit~cycle~case}]\,,\\
v_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))&=\!\mu_{\rho}+\!\!\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\!\int_{s}^{\tau}\!\!dt\partial_{\bar{x}_{\ell}}\sigma(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))\Phi_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}(t,s)\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\,,\end{split}

(S.38)

where
\bar{\bm{x}}(s)
is a periodic solution (limit cycle) of Eq.(
S.6
).

Note that the term
{\cal A}_{\rho}
never diverges due to the Property 1 in Sec.
S.II.4
. Divergence of the fluctuation emerges from the term
v_{\rho}
.

S.IV
Classification of diverging exponents in entropy fluctuation

In the chemical reaction, we consider the control parameter
\theta
defined through the
concentration of chemostatted species:

\displaystyle{\bm{a}}

\displaystyle={\bm{a}}^{(0)}+\theta\hat{\bm{a}}\,,

(S.39)

where
\hat{\bm{a}}=(\hat{a}_{\ell})_{\ell\in{\cal S}_{c}}
is the unit vector which defines the increasing direction of the concentrations of the chemostatted chemical species from the initial concentration vector
{\bm{a}}^{(0)}
. The parameter
\theta
is the amplitude along the direction. The parameter
\theta
is a control parameter to govern the bifurcations. The final aim here is to classify the exponent of the divergence of the fluctuation of the entropy production according to the types of bifurcation:

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\sigma

\displaystyle\propto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\theta-\theta_{c})^{-\alpha_{+}}&\theta>\theta_{c}\\
(\theta_{c}-\theta)^{-\alpha_{-}}&\theta<\theta_{c}\\
\end{array}\right.

Note that exponents
\alpha_{\pm}
does not depend on the choice of the functional form on the parameter
\theta
, as long as the function is differentiable around the bifurcation point.

S.IV.1
Typical bifurcations and their normal forms: A review

Before classifying the diverging exponents at bifurcation points, we briefly review the local dynamical structure near several standard bifurcations. Let us consider an
n
-dimensional state vector
{\bm{x}}
obeying the deterministic dynamics

\dot{\bm{x}}={\bm{F}}_{\theta}({\bm{x}})\,.

The subscript
\theta
emphasizes that the vector field depends on a control parameter
\theta
. The linearized dynamics around a solution
{\bm{x}}(t)
is governed by the stability matrix
\mathbb{S}_{\theta}({\bm{x}}(t))
:

\displaystyle\delta\dot{\bm{x}}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{S}_{\theta}({\bm{x}}(t))\,\delta{\bm{x}}(t)\,,\qquad\mathbb{S}_{\theta}({\bm{x}}(t)):=\left.\frac{\partial{\bm{F}}_{\theta}}{\partial{\bm{x}}}\right|_{{\bm{x}}={\bm{x}}(t)}.

(S.42)

Let
\theta_{c}
be a bifurcation point, and let
{\bm{x}}_{c}
be an equilibrium (stable fixed point solution) at
\theta=\theta_{c}
, namely

{\bm{F}}_{\theta_{c}}({\bm{x}}_{c})={\bm{0}}.

The eigenvalues of the linearization
{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta_{c}}({\bm{x}}_{c})
determine the decomposition of the tangent space

\displaystyle T_{{\bm{x}}_{c}}\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle=E^{\rm c}\oplus E^{\rm s}\oplus E^{\rm u}\,,

(S.43)

where
E^{\rm c}
is the center subspace associated with eigenvalues of zero real part, and
E^{\rm s}
and
E^{\rm u}
denote the stable and unstable subspaces, respectively. For
\theta
close to
\theta_{c}
, the local dynamics relevant to the bifurcation is governed by the flow restricted to the center manifold. The
Center Manifold Theorem

Kuznetsov 2023
guarantees the existence of a locally invariant center manifold tangent to
E^{\rm c}
at
{\bm{x}}_{c}
.

Let
{\bm{e}}_{1}^{\rm c},\cdots,{\bm{e}}_{d_{\rm c}}^{\rm c}
be basis vectors for the center subspace
E^{\rm c}
, and let
{\bm{e}}^{\rm su}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{e}}^{\rm su}_{d_{\rm su}}
be basis vectors for the stable and unstable subspaces
E^{\rm s}\oplus E^{\rm u}
. Here,
d_{\rm c}
and
d_{\rm su}
are the dimensions of
E^{\rm c}
and
E^{\rm s}\oplus E^{\rm u}
, respectively, and hence
d_{\rm c}+d_{\rm su}=n
. These vectors are not necessarily orthogonal. We construct the matrix
{\cal P}
by taking these vectors as its columns:

\displaystyle{\cal P}

\displaystyle=({\bm{e}}_{1}^{\rm c},\cdots,{\bm{e}}_{d_{\rm c}}^{\rm c},{\bm{e}}^{\rm su}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{e}}^{\rm su}_{d_{\rm su}})\,.

(S.44)

Then, for
\theta
near
\theta_{c}
, we introduce linear coordinates
({\bm{c}}^{T},{\bm{s}}^{T})^{T}
by

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}_{c}

\displaystyle={\cal P}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bm{c}}\\
{\bm{s}}\end{array}\right)\,,

where
{\bm{c}}=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{d_{\rm c}})^{T}
parameterizes the center directions, while
{\bm{s}}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{d_{\rm su}})^{T}
parameterizes the stable and unstable directions.

Introducing the unfolding parameter

\mu:=\theta-\theta_{c}\,,

the
Center Manifold Theorem
guarantees that, for
|\mu|\ll 1
, the center manifold can be represented locally as a graph

{\bm{s}}={\bm{h}}({\bm{c}},\mu)

with
{\bm{h}}({\bm{0}},0)={\bm{0}}
and
\partial_{\bm{c}}{\bm{h}}({\bm{0}},0)={\bm{0}}
. Accordingly, the variables
{\bm{c}}
and
{\bm{s}}
obey equations of the form

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{c}}

\displaystyle={\bm{g}}_{\rm c}({\bm{c}},{\bm{s}},\mu)\,,

(S.47)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{s}}

\displaystyle={\bm{g}}_{\rm su}({\bm{c}},{\bm{s}},\mu)\,,

(S.48)

and the reduced dynamics on the center manifold is

\dot{\bm{c}}={\bm{g}}_{\rm c}({\bm{c}},{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}},\mu),\mu)\,.

To flatten the center manifold, we introduce the nonlinear coordinate transformation

{\bm{w}}={\bm{s}}-{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}},\mu)\,.

With this transformation, the center manifold is given by
{\bm{w}}={\bm{0}}
.
When the system starts at the initial condition
({\bm{c}}(0),{\bm{w}}(0)={\bm{0}})
, then the state at time
t
remains in the manifold
({\bm{c}}(t),{\bm{w}}(t)={\bm{0}})
for any time
t
. We define the flattened coordinates by
{\bm{\zeta}}=({\bm{c}}^{T},{\bm{w}}^{T})^{T}
. Then

{\bm{x}}={\bm{x}}_{c}+{\cal P}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bm{c}}\\
{\bm{w}}+{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}},\mu)\end{array}\right),

and the Jacobian matrix of this coordinate map is

\displaystyle{\cal J}({\bm{c}},\mu)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial{\bm{x}}}{\partial{\bm{\zeta}}}={\cal P}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathbb{I}}&{\bm{0}}\\
\partial_{\bm{c}}{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}},\mu)&{\mathbb{I}}\end{array}\right)\,.

At
({\bm{c}},\mu)=({\bm{0}},0)
, it coincides with
{\cal P}
. Let
\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\theta}({\bm{\zeta}})
denote the transformed vector field in the flattened coordinates satisfying

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\zeta}}=\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\theta}({\bm{c}},{\bm{w}})\,.

(S.51)

We also define its stability matrix as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}({\bm{\zeta}})=\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}({\bm{c}},{\bm{w}})

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\bm{\zeta}}\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\theta}({\bm{c}},{\bm{w}})\,.

(S.52)

Since the set
{\bm{w}}={\bm{0}}
is invariant, the lower component of the transformed vector field satisfies
\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\theta}^{({\rm su})}({\bm{c}},{\bm{0}})={\bm{0}}
for

\forall{\bm{c}}
. Therefore, the stability matrix along the center manifold has the block upper-triangular form

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}({\bm{c}},{\bm{w}}={\bm{0}})

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm c})}({\bm{c}})&\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm csu})}({\bm{c}})\\
{\bm{0}}&\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm su})}({\bm{c}})\end{array}\right)\,.

Moreover, if

\bar{\bm{\zeta}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\bm{c}}\\
{\bm{0}}\end{array}\right)

is an equilibrium of the dynamics (
S.51
), then the corresponding equilibrium of the original system is

\bar{\bm{x}}={\bm{x}}_{c}+{\cal P}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\bm{c}}\\
{\bm{h}}(\bar{\bm{c}},\mu)\end{array}\right),

and the two stability matrices are related to each other as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle={\cal J}(\bar{\bm{c}},\mu)\,\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{c}},{\bm{w}}={\bm{0}})\,{\cal J}(\bar{\bm{c}},\mu)^{-1}\,.

(S.55)

Hence, the eigenvalues at equilibria on the center manifold are determined by the diagonal blocks
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm c})}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm su})}
. The eigenvalues associated with
E^{\rm s}\oplus E^{\rm u}
remain
O(1)
and stay away from the imaginary axis near the bifurcation.

In the case of
d_{\rm c}=1
, the reduced dynamics on the center manifold is one-dimensional. Then
{\bm{c}}
becomes a scalar
c
, and the eigenvalue associated with the center direction converges to
0
as
\theta\to\theta_{c}
. Expanding the reduced vector field in powers of
c
and
\mu
, we obtain

\dot{c}=g_{\rm c}(c,\mu)=a_{10}\mu+a_{01}c+a_{11}\mu c+a_{02}c^{2}+a_{03}c^{3}+\cdots.

At the bifurcation point,
a_{01}=\partial_{c}g_{\rm c}(0,0)=0
. The bifurcation type is determined by the lowest-order nonvanishing nonlinear term together with the nondegeneracy conditions with respect to the unfolding parameter. After smooth coordinate changes and rescalings of
c
,
\mu
, and time, one obtains the standard normal forms.

If an additional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry
c\mapsto-c
is present, then the reduced vector field is odd in
c
, namely
g_{\rm c}(-c,\mu)=-g_{\rm c}(c,\mu)
, so the constant and even-order terms vanish. If, moreover, the coefficients of
\mu c
and
c^{3}
are nonzero, a pitchfork bifurcation occurs. For definiteness, the supercritical normal form is written as

\displaystyle\dot{c}

\displaystyle=g_{\rm c}(c,\mu)=\mu c-c^{3}.

(S.56)

If
a_{10}=0
,
a_{11}\neq 0
, and
a_{02}\neq 0
, a transcritical bifurcation occurs:

\displaystyle\dot{c}

\displaystyle=g_{\rm c}(c,\mu)=\mu c-c^{2}.

(S.57)

If
a_{10}\neq 0
and
a_{02}\neq 0
, a saddle-node bifurcation occurs:

\displaystyle\dot{c}

\displaystyle=g_{\rm c}(c,\mu)=\mu-c^{2}.

(S.58)

In the case of
d_{\rm c}=2
, the center block of the linearization has a pair of complex conjugate eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,2}^{\rm c}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\theta)\pm i\omega(\theta)\,,

(S.59)

such that
\alpha(\theta_{c})=0
,
\omega(\theta_{c})=\omega_{0}\neq 0
, and
\frac{d\alpha}{d\theta}(\theta_{c})\neq 0
, while all other eigenvalues have negative real parts. By the Center Manifold Theorem, the dynamics reduces locally to a two-dimensional system. After smooth coordinate transformations and rescalings, the reduced system can be written in complex form as

\dot{z}=(\mu+i\omega_{0})z-\gamma z|z|^{2}+\cdots\,,

where
z=c_{1}+ic_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mu=\theta-\theta_{c}
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
determines the criticality (for simplicity, we suppress the nonlinear frequency shift). Truncating at cubic order, we obtain the Hopf normal form in the two variables
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
:

\begin{split}\dot{c}_{1}&=g_{{\rm c},1}(c_{1},c_{2})=\mu c_{1}-\omega_{0}c_{2}-\gamma c_{1}(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2})+\cdots\,,\\
\dot{c}_{2}&=g_{{\rm c},2}(c_{1},c_{2})=\omega_{0}c_{1}+\mu c_{2}-\gamma c_{2}(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2})+\cdots\,.\end{split}

(S.60)

Writing
z=re^{i\phi}
, we obtain the equations

\displaystyle\dot{r}

\displaystyle=\mu r-\gamma r^{3}+O(\mu^{2}r+\mu r^{3}+r^{5}),\qquad\dot{\phi}=\omega_{0}+O(\mu+r^{2}).

(S.61)

If
\gamma>0
, the Hopf bifurcation is supercritical, and a stable limit cycle of radius emerges for
\mu>0
(equivalently,
\theta>\theta_{c}
):

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mu/\gamma}\,,~~~T(\mu)={2\pi\over\omega_{0}}+O(\mu)\,,

(S.62)

where
T(\mu)
is the period of the limit cycle. If
\gamma<0
, the Hopf bifurcation is subcritical, and an unstable limit cycle exists for
\mu<0
(equivalently,
\theta<\theta_{c}
).

S.IV.2
Fluctuation of the entropy production for fixed-point cases

Let us discuss the generic properties for the case
d_{\rm c}=1
, where the center manifold is one-dimensional. In this case, the relevant stable states are equilibria, and hence we use the formula for the fixed-point case in (
S.37
). We write

\mu:=\theta-\theta_{c}
for the unfolding parameter. Let
\bar{\bm{x}}_{\pm}(\theta)
denote a stable equilibrium approached from the sides
\theta\to\theta_{c}\pm 0
, respectively, whenever the corresponding limit exists. We note each element of the entropy gradient vector
\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial x_{\ell}}
never diverges due to the property 3 in Sec.
S.II.4
. We consider the
generic
situation where the entropy-gradient vector has a finite, nonzero overlap with the center direction.
In the generic case, the divergence of the fluctuation of entropy production is controlled by the singular part of the inverse stability matrix along the center-manifold equilibrium. Let

\bar{\bm{\zeta}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{c}\\
0\end{array}\right)

be a stable equilibrium in the flattened coordinates, and let
\bar{\bm{x}}
be the corresponding equilibrium in the original coordinates. Then, from the block upper-triangular structure of the transformed stability matrix on the center manifold, we obtain

\displaystyle[{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{x}})]^{-1}

\displaystyle={\cal J}(\bar{c},\mu)\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{\zeta}})^{-1}[{\cal J}(\bar{c},\mu)]^{-1}

\displaystyle={\cal J}(\bar{c},\mu)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda^{-1}&-\lambda^{-1}\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm csu})}(\bar{c})[\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm su})}(\bar{c})]^{-1}\\
{\bm{0}}&[\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm su})}(\bar{c})]^{-1}\end{array}\right)[{\cal J}(\bar{c},\mu)]^{-1}\,,

where

\lambda:=\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm c})}(\bar{c},\mu)=\partial_{c}g_{\rm c}(\bar{c},\mu)

is the center eigenvalue at the equilibrium. Near the bifurcation point, the diverging contribution comes from
\lambda^{-1}
because
\lambda\to 0
as
\theta\to\theta_{c}
, while the elements of
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm csu})}
and the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm su})}
remain
O(1)
. Let
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
denote the values of
\lambda
on the stable branches for
\mu>0
and
\mu<0
, respectively. In the generic case described above, the fluctuation of entropy production scales as

\mathrm{Var}\,\sigma\propto\lambda_{\pm}^{-2}

on each side where a stable equilibrium branch exists. We now classify this generic behavior for each bifurcation class.

S.IV.2.1
Pitchfork and transcritical bifurcations

For the pitchfork bifurcation, we use the normal form (
S.56
),

\dot{c}=\mu c-c^{3}\,.

The stable equilibria are given by
\bar{c}=\pm\sqrt{\mu}
for
\mu>0
and
\bar{c}=0
for
\mu<0
. Hence,

\displaystyle\lambda_{+}

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}g_{\rm c}(\bar{c},\mu)=\mu-3\bar{c}^{2}=-2\mu\qquad(\mu>0)\,,

(S.65)

\displaystyle\lambda_{-}

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}g_{\rm c}(0,\mu)=\mu=-|\mu|\qquad(\mu<0)\,.

(S.66)

Consequently, the generic behavior of the fluctuation of entropy production is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\,\sigma

\displaystyle\propto\begin{cases}\dfrac{C_{+}}{\mu^{2}}&\cdots\ \mu>0\,,\\[5.16663pt]
\dfrac{C_{-}}{\mu^{2}}&\cdots\ \mu<0\,,\end{cases}

(S.67)

where
C_{\pm}
are finite constants. Thus, the exponents are
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(2,2)
for the pitchfork bifurcation. We have some remark on the symmtery in the pictchfork bifurcation.
The normal form has
c\to-c
symmetry. However, we emphasize that this symmetry does not imply
\sigma(c)=\sigma(-c)
. The entropy production is a physical observable expressed in the original concentrations and reservoir affinities, and it generally contains an odd component in the center-manifold coordinate,
\sigma(c)=\sigma_{0}+s_{1}c+s_{2}c^{2}+\cdots
with
s_{1}\neq 0
. Only when the full chemical reaction network has an additional exact symmetry that exchanges the two branches and leaves the environment entropy production invariant is the odd coefficient
s_{1}
forbidden. Such symmetry-protected cases are nongeneric in the present sense and may exhibit reduced exponents.

We next consider the transcritical bifurcation in the same manner. We use the normal form (
S.57
),

\dot{c}=\mu c-c^{2}\,,

which has the stable equilibria
\bar{c}=\mu
for
\mu>0
and
\bar{c}=0
for
\mu<0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lambda_{+}

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}g_{\rm c}(\mu,\mu)=\mu-2\mu=-\mu\qquad(\mu>0)\,,

(S.68)

\displaystyle\lambda_{-}

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}g_{\rm c}(0,\mu)=\mu=-|\mu|\qquad(\mu<0)\,.

(S.69)

Hence, we arrive at the same type of divergence for the fluctuation of entropy production as in (
S.67
). Therefore, the exponents are again
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(2,2)
for the transcritical bifurcation.

The pitchfork and transcritical bifurcations are both continuous transitions in the sense that the stable equilibria change continuously as a function of
\theta
. In this generic setting, the continuous bifurcation cases show the same exponents on both sides, namely
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(2,2)
.

S.IV.2.2
Saddle-node bifurcation

In contrast to the pitchfork and transcritical bifurcations, the saddle-node bifurcation shows a sudden loss of the local stable equilibrium branch. Using the normal form (
S.58
),

\dot{c}=\mu-c^{2}\,,

one finds the stable equilibrium
\bar{c}=\sqrt{\mu}
for
\mu>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lambda_{+}

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}g_{\rm c}(\bar{c},\mu)=-2\bar{c}=-2\sqrt{\mu}\qquad(\mu>0)\,.

(S.70)

Hence, the generic divergence of the entropy-production fluctuation on the side where the stable equilibrium exists is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\,\sigma

\displaystyle\propto\frac{C^{\prime\prime}_{+}}{\mu}\qquad(\mu>0)\,,

(S.71)

where
C^{\prime\prime}_{+}
is a finite constant.

For
\mu<0
, the local normal form has no nearby equilibrium, so the center-manifold analysis does not provide a universal singular contribution on that side. Any behavior for
\theta<\theta_{c}
is determined by the global structure of the full system.
Therefore, from the local bifurcation analysis, one concludes the generic exponents
(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})=(1,\varnothing)
for the saddle-node bifurcation. Here,
\varnothing
implies that the exponent depends on the specific models.

The saddle-node bifurcation is discontinuous in the sense that the local stable equilibrium branch terminates at the bifurcation point. This is reminiscent of a first-order equilibrium phase transition. However, unlike the equilibrium first-order case, the bifurcation can still show one-sided scaling behavior through the vanishing center eigenvalue. We also remark on the difference from the argument in Ref.
Nguyen and Seifert 2020
, where exponentially large fluctuations are observed for finite-volume chemical reactions. As emphasized before, we focus only on the thermodynamic limit (
S.30
). Hence, our target quantity is different from the one considered in Ref.
Nguyen and Seifert 2020
.

Fluctuation of the entropy production for limit-cycle case

time-periodic limit cycle in the regime

S.IV.3.1
The case of
\theta>\theta_{c}

We here discuss the divergence behavior for the regime
\mu:=\theta-\theta_{c}>0
near the Hopf bifurcation point. Since the periodic motion appears in this regime, we use the monodromy matrix
{\bm{M}}_{\theta}
defined as

\displaystyle{\bm{M}}_{\theta}

\displaystyle={\cal T}\exp\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}dt\,{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t))\Bigr]\,,

(S.72)

where
\bar{\bm{x}}(t)
is the stable periodic solution of Eq.(
S.6
).
T
is the period of the limit cycle which depends on the parameter
\theta
as

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle={2\pi\over\omega_{0}}+O(\mu)\,.

(S.73)

See Eq.(
S.62
) in the review part of the bifurcations in Sec.
S.IV.1
. We define the left and right eigenvectors for the monodromy matrix
{\bm{M}}_{\theta}
:

\displaystyle{\bm{M}}_{\theta}\bm{v}_{j}

\displaystyle=e^{\nu_{j}T}\bm{v}_{j}\,,\quad\bm{w}^{\dagger}_{j}{\bm{M}}_{\theta}=e^{\nu_{j}T}\bm{w}^{\dagger}_{j}\,.

(S.74)

We can show the following lemma for the monodromy matrix, the proof of which is provided in the sec.
S.IV.3.3
below.

Lemma 1
.

Assume a generic supercritical Hopf bifurcation at
\theta=\theta_{c}
, and let

\mu=\theta-\theta_{c}>0
.
Then the Floquet exponents of the limit cycle born from the Hopf bifurcation satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nu_{1}&=0,\qquad\nu_{2}=-2\mu+O(\mu^{2}),\\
\nu_{j}&=\lambda_{j}(\theta_{c})+O(\mu),\qquad j=3,\dots,n,\end{split}

(S.75)

where
\Re\lambda_{j}(\theta_{c})\neq 0
for
j\geq 3
.
Namely, one exponent is zero, one is of order
O(\theta-\theta_{c})
,
and the remaining
n-2
exponents are of order
O(1)
.

The properties
\bm{w}_{j}^{\dagger}\bm{v}_{m}=\delta_{jm}
and
\sum_{j}\bm{v}_{j}\bm{w}^{\dagger}_{j}={\mathbb{I}}
are satisfied. Then, we can write
\Phi_{\theta}(t,0)=\sum_{j}e^{\nu_{j}t}\tilde{\bm{v}}_{j}(t)\bm{w}^{\dagger}_{j}
, where
\tilde{\bm{v}}_{j}(t):=e^{-\nu_{j}t}\Phi_{\theta}(t,0)\bm{v}_{j}
. To correctly decompose the matrix as
\Phi_{\theta}(t,s)=\Phi_{\theta}(t,0)\Phi_{\theta}(0,s)
, we define the periodic left eigenvectors as
\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{j}(t):=e^{\nu_{j}t}\bm{w}^{\dagger}_{j}\Phi_{\theta}(0,t)
. Note that
\tilde{\bm{v}}_{j}(t)
and
\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{j}(t)
are both periodic functions with the period
T
. One can then write the matrix
\Phi_{\theta}(t,s)
as

\displaystyle\Phi_{\theta}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Phi_{\theta,j}(t,s)\,,\quad\Phi_{\theta,j}(t,s)=e^{\nu_{j}(t-s)}\tilde{\bm{v}}_{j}(t)\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{j}(s)\,.

(S.76)

In order to investigate
{\rm Var}\sigma
, let us focus on the term
v_{\rho}-\mu_{\rho}
in the formula (
S.38
):

\displaystyle v_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))-\mu_{\rho}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{s}^{\tau}dt\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}{\partial\sigma(\bm{x}(t))\over\partial x_{\ell}}[\Phi_{\theta}(t,s)]_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}[g_{j}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}\,,

(S.77)

\displaystyle[g_{j}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{\tau}dt\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}{\partial\sigma(\bm{x}(t))\over\partial x_{\ell}}[\Phi_{\theta,j}(t,s)]_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\,.

(S.78)

We first consider the term
g_{j=1}
. Note that
\nu_{1}=0
and
\tilde{\bm{v}}_{1}(t)\propto\dot{\bm{x}}(t)
. Hence we observe

\displaystyle[g_{1}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle\propto\int_{s}^{\tau}dt\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}{\partial\sigma(\bm{x}(t))\over\partial x_{\ell}}\dot{x}_{\ell}(t)[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{1}(s)]_{\ell^{\prime}}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}=\left[\sigma(\bm{x}(\tau))-\sigma(\bm{x}(s))\right]\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{1}(s)]_{\ell^{\prime}}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\,.

(S.79)

From the property 2 in Sec.
S.II.4
, this term never contributes to the divergence. Hence, we next consider the other cases
j\geq 2
. To this end, we write the term dependent on
t
in the Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}{\partial\sigma({\bm{x}}(t))\over\partial x_{\ell}}[\tilde{\bm{v}}_{j}(t)]_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}{\cal F}_{j,k}(\theta)e^{i\omega kt}\,,\quad\omega=2\pi/T\,,

(S.80)

where we explicitly write the
\theta
-dependence in the Fourier transform. Then, we have

\displaystyle[g_{j}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}[g_{j,k}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}\,,

(S.81)

\displaystyle[g_{j,k}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}

\displaystyle={{\cal F}_{j,k}(\theta)\over\nu_{j}+i\omega k}(e^{i\omega k\tau}e^{\nu_{j}(\tau-s)}-e^{i\omega ks})\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{j}(s)]_{\ell^{\prime}}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\,.

(S.82)

Note that
\omega
is finite at and near the bifurcation point, and hence this structure tells us that the component
k=0
and
j=2
gives singular behavior, since
\nu_{2}=O(\mu)
while
\nu_{j}=O(1)
for
j\geq 3
. Hence, the fluctuation is dominated by
[{\rm Var}\sigma]\sim[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}
, where
[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}
is given by the component
g_{j=2,k=0}
as

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds\sum_{\rho}|[g_{2,0}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}+\mu_{\rho}|^{2}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))=[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(1)}+[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(2)}+{\rm const}.\,,

(S.83)

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds\sum_{\rho}[g_{2,0}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}[g_{2,0}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}^{\ast}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))\,,

(S.84)

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}{\rm Re}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds[g_{2,0}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))\,.

(S.85)

where the subscript ‘D’ stands for the dominant contribution of the fluctuation.

We first consider the term
[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(1)}
, which is explicitly written as

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds\sum_{\rho}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}{|{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)|^{2}\over|\nu_{2}|^{2}}|e^{\nu_{2}(\tau-s)}-1|^{2}[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{2}(s)]_{\ell}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{2}(s)]_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\ast}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\,.

(S.86)

Let use the Fourier transform as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho,\ell,\ell^{\prime}}[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{2}(s)]_{\ell}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{2}(s)]_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\ast}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}=-\infty}^{\infty}{\cal D}_{k^{\prime}}(\theta)e^{i\omega k^{\prime}s}\,.

(S.87)

Then, we have

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}=-\infty}^{\infty}\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds{|{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)|^{2}\over|\nu_{2}|^{2}}|e^{\nu_{2}(\tau-s)}-1|^{2}{\cal D}_{k^{\prime}}(\theta)e^{i\omega k^{\prime}s}

\displaystyle={|{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)|^{2}\over|\nu_{2}|^{2}}{\cal D}_{0}(\theta)\,,

(S.88)

by using the fact that
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1/\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds|e^{\nu_{2}(\tau-s)}-1|^{2}e^{i\omega k^{\prime}s}=\delta_{k^{\prime},0}
. Note that the term
{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)
and
{\cal D}_{0}(\theta)
are written as

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}dt\sum_{\ell}{\partial\sigma(\bar{\bm{x}}(t))\over\partial x_{\ell}}[\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}(t)]_{\ell}\,,

(S.89)

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{0}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}dt\,\sum_{\rho,\ell,\,\ell^{\prime}}[\tilde{\bm{w}}_{2}^{\dagger}(t)]_{\ell}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t))\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}[\tilde{\bm{w}}_{2}^{\dagger}(t)]_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\ast}\,.

(S.90)

The term
{\cal D}_{0}(\theta)
neither diverges due to
|{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t))|<\infty
from the property 1 in Sec.
S.II.4
, nor is infinitesimal since
\theta\to\theta_{c}
leads to a finite value. On the other hand, as we show below,
{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)={O}(\sqrt{\mu})
.
To show this, we expand the limit cycle as
{\bm{x}}(t)={\bm{x}}_{c}+\sqrt{\mu}{\bm{x}}^{({\rm cm})}(t)+\cdots
, where
{\bm{x}}_{c}
is the fixed point at
\theta=\theta_{c}
and
{\bm{x}}^{({\rm cm})}(t)
stands for the periodic motion. Then, we we use the lemma
2
and
3
which is explained below, to obtain

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}dt\sum_{\ell}\Bigl[{\partial\sigma\over\partial x_{\ell}}({\bm{x}}_{c})+{\sqrt{\mu}}\sum_{m}\sum_{\ell}\,{\partial^{2}\sigma({\bm{x}}_{c})\over\partial x_{\ell}\partial x_{m}}x_{m}^{({\rm cm})}(t)+\cdots\Bigr]\times\Bigl[[\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(0)}(t)]_{\ell}+\sqrt{\mu}[\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(1)}(t)]_{\ell}+\cdots\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mu}{\cal F}_{2,0}^{(1)}+\cdots\,,

(S.91)

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{2,0}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi/\omega_{0}}dt\,\sum_{\ell,m}{\partial^{2}\sigma({\bm{x}}_{c})\over\partial x_{\ell}\partial x_{m}}x_{m}^{({\rm cm})}(t)[\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(0)}(t)]_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell}{\partial\sigma({\bm{x}}_{c})\over\partial x_{\ell}}[\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(1)}(t)]_{\ell}\,,

(S.92)

where we have used
\int_{0}^{2\pi/\omega_{0}}dt\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(0)}(t)=0
and
T={2\pi/\omega_{0}}+O(\mu)
(See Eq.(
S.73
)). From the Lemma
2
, we have
\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(0)}(t)={\cal P}\left(e^{-i\omega_{0}\sigma_{y}t}{\bm{\alpha}}^{(0)},{\bm{0}}\right)^{T}
for the vector
{\bm{\alpha}}^{(0)}
. Hence we obtain the behavior:

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=O(\mu^{-1})=O((\theta-\theta_{c})^{-1})\,.

(S.93)

Lemma 2
.

The left and right eigenvectors for
j=2
in the original coordinates are expanded as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bm{v}}_{2}(\theta)&={\bm{v}}_{2}^{(0)}+\sqrt{\mu}{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(1)}+\cdots\,,\\
{\bm{w}}_{2}(\theta)&={\bm{w}}_{2}^{(0)}+\sqrt{\mu}{\bm{w}}_{2}^{(1)}+\cdots\,.\end{split}

(S.94)

Lemma 3
.

The periodic vectors
\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}(t)
and
\tilde{\bm{w}}_{2}(t)
are expanded as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}(t)&=\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(0)}(t)+\sqrt{\mu}\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(1)}(t)+\cdots\,,\\
\tilde{\bm{w}}_{2}(t)&=\tilde{\bm{w}}_{2}^{(0)}(t)+\sqrt{\mu}\tilde{\bm{w}}_{2}^{(1)}(t)+\cdots\,,\end{split}

(S.95)

where

\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(0)}(t)=\Phi_{\theta_{c}}(t,0){\bm{v}}_{2}(\theta_{c})
and
\tilde{\bm{w}}_{2}^{(0)}{}^{\dagger}(t)={\bm{w}}_{2}^{\dagger}(\theta_{c})\Phi_{\theta_{c}}(0,t)
.

Next, we consider the term
[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(2)}
which is given as follows:

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}{\rm Re}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds\sum_{\rho}[g_{2,0}(\tau,s)]_{\rho}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}{\rm Re}\int_{0}^{\tau}ds{{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)\over\nu_{2}}(e^{\nu_{2}(\tau-s)}-1)\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{2}(s)]_{\ell^{\prime}}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))\,.

(S.96)

We introduce the Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\dagger}_{2}(s)]_{\ell^{\prime}}\nabla_{\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}{\cal A}_{\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(s))=\sum_{k^{\prime}=-\infty}^{\infty}Q_{k^{\prime}}(\theta)e^{i\omega k^{\prime}s}\,,

(S.97)

which leads to

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(2)}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\Bigl[{{\cal F}_{2,0}(\theta)\over\nu_{2}}Q_{0}(\theta)\Bigr]\,.

(S.98)

Since

, the zeroth-order contribution to

vanishes, and therefore

. Using also

, we obtain

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=O(1)\,.

(S.99)

Thus we conclude

\displaystyle[{\rm Var}\sigma]

\displaystyle\sim[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}\sim[{\rm Var}\sigma]_{\rm D}^{(1)}\propto(\theta-\theta_{c})^{-1}\,.

(S.100)

S.IV.3.2
The case of
\theta<\theta_{c}

For the regime of
\theta<\theta_{c}
, the stable solution is not the periodic motion, but the fixed point
\bar{\bm{x}}
, and hence we use the formula in (
S.37
) to discuss the fluctuation of entropy production. Note that the stability matrix has finite eigenvalues as in (
S.59
). Eigenvalues in the stable+unstable manifold are
O(1)
, and eigenvalues in the center manifold are near
\pm i\omega_{0}
for the parameter near

S.IV.3.3
Proofs for the lemmas 1-3

The proof of the lemma
1
is as follows.

Proof.

Let
\mu:=\theta-\theta_{c}>0
, and let

\bar{\bm{\zeta}}(t)=\bigl(\bar{\bm{c}}(t)^{T},{\bm{0}}^{T}\bigr)^{T}

be the
T(\mu)
-periodic orbit in the flattened coordinates

{\bm{\zeta}}=({\bm{c}}^{T},{\bm{w}}^{T})^{T}
, where

{\bm{w}}={\bm{s}}-{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})
. The corresponding orbit in the original
coordinates is denoted by

\bar{\bm{x}}(t)=\Psi(\bar{\bm{\zeta}}(t)).

Here
\Psi
is the smooth coordinate map from the flattened coordinates to the original coordinates. We define the Jacobian for a later use:

\displaystyle{\cal J}(t):=\partial_{\bar{\bm{\zeta}}}\Psi(\bar{\bm{\zeta}}(t)).

(S.101)

We first clarify the relation between the variational equations in the original and flattened coordinates. In the original coordinates, the variational equation along
\bar{\bm{x}}(t)
is

\displaystyle\delta\dot{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t))\,\delta{\bm{x}},\qquad{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t)):=\partial_{\bm{x}}{\bm{F}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t)).

(S.102)

Equivalently, the same matrix is obtained by first transforming the nonlinear
vector field. In the flattened coordinates the vector field is given by

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{c}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm c}({\bm{c}},{\bm{w}}):={\bm{g}}_{\rm c}({\bm{c}},{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})+{\bm{w}}),

(S.103)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm w}({\bm{c}},{\bm{w}}):={\bm{g}}_{\rm su}({\bm{c}},{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})+{\bm{w}})-\partial_{\bm{c}}{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})\,{\bm{g}}_{\rm c}({\bm{c}},{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})+{\bm{w}}).

(S.104)

The stability matrix is computed as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(t)=\partial_{\bm{\zeta}}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm c}\\
\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm w}\end{pmatrix}\Bigg|_{({\bm{c}},{\bm{w}})=(\bar{\bm{c}}(t),{\bm{0}})}.

(S.105)

Since the center manifold
{\bm{s}}={\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})
is invariant, one has

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}_{\rm su}({\bm{c}},{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}}))=\partial_{\bm{c}}{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})\,{\bm{g}}_{\rm c}({\bm{c}},{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})).

(S.106)

Therefore

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm w}({\bm{c}},{\bm{0}})={\bm{0}}\qquad\text{for all ${\bm{c}}$}.

(S.107)

Differentiating this identity with respect to
{\bm{c}}
yields

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{c}}\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm w}({\bm{c}},{\bm{0}})={\bm{0}}.

(S.108)

Consequently, the stability matrix
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(t)
has the block upper triangular form

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{\rm c}(t)&B(t)\\
{\bm{0}}&A_{\rm su}(t)\end{pmatrix},

(S.109)

where
A_{\rm c}(t)=\partial_{\bm{c}}\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm c}(\bar{\bm{c}}(t),{\bm{0}})
,
B(t)=\partial_{\bm{w}}\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm c}(\bar{\bm{c}}(t),{\bm{0}})
, and

A_{\rm su}(t)=\partial_{\bm{w}}\hat{\bm{g}}_{\rm w}(\bar{\bm{c}}(t),{\bm{0}})
.

Let
\tilde{\Phi}_{\theta}(t,0)
be the fundamental matrix of the flattened
variational equation, and write

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\theta}(t,0)=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\Phi}_{11}(t,0)&\tilde{\Phi}_{12}(t,0)\\
\tilde{\Phi}_{21}(t,0)&\tilde{\Phi}_{22}(t,0)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\tilde{\Phi}_{\theta}(0,0)=\mathbb{I}.

(S.110)

From (
S.109
), the lower-left block satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{\Phi}_{21}(t,0)=A_{\rm su}(t)\tilde{\Phi}_{21}(t,0),\qquad\tilde{\Phi}_{21}(0,0)={\bm{0}}.

(S.111)

By uniqueness of solutions, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{21}(t,0)\equiv{\bm{0}}.

(S.112)

Thus the fundamental matrix is written in the following form:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\theta}(t,0)=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm c}(t,0)&*\\
{\bm{0}}&\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm su}(t,0)\end{pmatrix},

(S.113)

where

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm c}(t,0)

\displaystyle=A_{\rm c}(t)\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm c}(t,0),\qquad\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm c}(0,0)=\mathbb{I},

(S.114)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm su}(t,0)

\displaystyle=A_{\rm su}(t)\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm su}(t,0),\qquad\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm su}(0,0)=\mathbb{I}.

(S.115)

The monodromy matrix in the flattened coordinates is written in the following form

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\theta}=\tilde{\Phi}_{\theta}(T,0)=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\bm{M}}^{({\rm c})}_{\theta}&*\\
{\bm{0}}&\tilde{\bm{M}}^{({\rm su})}_{\theta}\end{pmatrix},

(S.116)

where
\tilde{\bm{M}}^{({\rm c})}_{\theta}=\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm c}(T,0)
and
\tilde{\bm{M}}^{({\rm su})}_{\theta}=\tilde{\Phi}_{\rm su}(T,0)
.
Hence

\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\theta})=\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\bm{M}}^{({\rm c})}_{\theta})\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}-\tilde{\bm{M}}^{({\rm su})}_{\theta})
.
The Floquet multipliers of
\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\theta}
are the union of those of the center and su (stable+unstable) blocks.

We now relate this result to the monodromy matrix in the original coordinates.
Let
\Phi_{\theta}(t,0)
be the fundamental matrix in the original coordinate. The fundamental matrices in the two coordinates satisfy the relation

\displaystyle\Phi_{\theta}(t,0)={\cal J}(t)\,\tilde{\Phi}_{\theta}(t,0)\,{\cal J}^{-1}(0).

(S.117)

Since the orbit is periodic, we have
\bar{\bm{\zeta}}(T)=\bar{\bm{\zeta}}(0)
and
{\cal J}(T)={\cal J}(0)
. Therefore the monodromy matrix originally defined by

\displaystyle{\bm{M}}_{\theta}={\cal T}\exp\left[\int_{0}^{T}dt\,{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t))\right]=\Phi_{\theta}(T,0)

(S.118)

is conjugate to the flattened monodromy matrix:

\displaystyle{\bm{M}}_{\theta}={\cal J}(0)\,\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\theta}\,{\cal J}^{-1}(0).

(S.119)

Thus
{\bm{M}}_{\theta}
and
\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\theta}
have the same Floquet multipliers. The connection term affects the generator
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}(t)
, but it does not invalidate the above spectral decomposition, because the block triangular structure follows from the invariance of the flattened center manifold
{\bm{w}}=0
.

We next analyze the center block. For a generic supercritical Hopf bifurcation,
the reduced dynamics on the two-dimensional center manifold can be written,
after a smooth local change of coordinates, in polar coordinates
(r,\phi)
as

\displaystyle\dot{r}

\displaystyle=\mu r-\gamma r^{3}+R_{r}(r,\mu),

(S.120)

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}+R_{\phi}(r,\mu),

(S.121)

where
\gamma>0
. The functions
R_{r}
and
R_{\phi}
are respectively defined as
R_{r}(r,\mu)=O(\mu^{2}r+\mu r^{3}+r^{5})
and
R_{\phi}(r,\mu)=O(\mu+r^{2})
. For
\mu>0
sufficiently small, there exists a periodic orbit on the center manifold with

\displaystyle r=\bar{r}(\mu)=\sqrt{\mu/\gamma}+O(\mu^{3/2}),\qquad T(\mu)=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{0}}+O(\mu).

(S.122)

Linearizing the radial equation along this orbit gives

\displaystyle\delta\dot{r}

\displaystyle=\left[\mu-3\gamma\bar{r}^{2}(\mu)+\partial_{r}R_{r}(\bar{r}(\mu),\mu)\right]\delta r=\left[-2\mu+O(\mu^{2})\right]\delta r,

(S.123)

because
\bar{r}^{2}(\mu)=\mu/\gamma+O(\mu^{2})
and

\partial_{r}R_{r}(\bar{r}(\mu),\mu)=O(\mu^{2})
. Therefore the nontrivial
Floquet exponent in the center directions is

\displaystyle\nu_{2}=-2\mu+O(\mu^{2})=-2(\theta-\theta_{c})+O((\theta-\theta_{c})^{2})\,.

(S.124)

The other center Floquet exponent is exactly zero. Indeed, differentiating the
equation of motion for the periodic orbit on the center manifold gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\dot{\bar{\bm{c}}}(t)=A_{\rm c}(t)\dot{\bar{\bm{c}}}(t).

(S.125)

Since
\bar{\bm{c}}(t)
is
T
-periodic, so is
\dot{\bar{\bm{c}}}(t)
, and hence

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{M}}^{({\rm c})}_{\theta}\,\dot{\bar{\bm{c}}}(0)=\dot{\bar{\bm{c}}}(0).

(S.126)

Thus one center Floquet multiplier equals
1
, corresponding to the Floquet
exponent

\displaystyle\nu_{1}=0.

(S.127)

Finally, we consider the su (stable+unstable) block. As
\mu\to 0^{+}
, the periodic orbit
shrinks to the Hopf fixed point, and therefore

\displaystyle A_{\rm su}(t)=A_{{\rm su},0}+O(\sqrt{\mu}),\qquad A_{{\rm su},0}:=\tilde{\mathbb{S}}^{({\rm su})}_{\theta_{c}}({\bm{0}}),

(S.128)

uniformly for
t\in[0,T(\mu)]
. The eigenvalues of
A_{{\rm su},0}
are the
eigenvalues of the linearization at the Hopf point restricted to

E^{\rm s}\oplus E^{\rm u}
, and hence all of them have nonzero real parts.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{M}}^{({\rm su})}_{\theta}=e^{T_{0}A_{{\rm su},0}}+O(\sqrt{\mu}),\qquad T_{0}:=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{0}}.

(S.129)

Choosing the branches of the logarithm continuously from
\mu=0
, standard
perturbation theory for periodic linear systems gives, for
j=3,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle\nu_{j}(\theta)=\lambda_{j}(\theta_{c})+o(1),

(S.130)

where
\lambda_{j}(\theta_{c})
are the eigenvalues of
A_{{\rm su},0}
. In
particular, these
n-2
exponents remain of order
O(1)
.

Collecting the center and su spectra, we conclude that for

\theta>\theta_{c}
sufficiently close to
\theta_{c}
, the Floquet exponents
consist of one zero exponent, one exponent of order

O(\theta-\theta_{c})
, and
n-2
exponents of order
O(1)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The proof of the lemma
2
is as follows.

Proof.

We consider the flattened
{\bm{\zeta}}
coordinate. The monodromy matrix in the
{\bm{\zeta}}
coordinate has the following structure

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm c}\,,&\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm csu}\\
{\bm{0}}\,,&\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm su}\end{array}\right)={\cal J}^{-1}(0){\bm{M}}_{\theta}{\cal J}(0)\,.

Let us denote the right eigenvector of
\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\theta}
as
{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}
, which is related to the full space vector via
{\bm{v}}_{2}(\theta)={\cal J}(0){\bm{\zeta}}_{2}
. We decompose the vector
{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}
as

\displaystyle{\bm{\zeta}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{\alpha}}\\
{\bm{\beta}}\end{array}\right).

The eigenvalue equation reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm c}{\bm{\alpha}}+\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm csu}{\bm{\beta}}&=e^{\nu_{2}T}{\bm{\alpha}}\,,\\
\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm su}{\bm{\beta}}&=e^{\nu_{2}T}{\bm{\beta}}\,.\end{split}

(S.135)

Since
\nu_{2}={O}(\mu)\ll 1
, we have
e^{\nu_{2}T}\sim 1
. The second equation implies that
{\bm{\beta}}
must be a zero vector, i.e.,
{\bm{\beta}}={\bm{0}}
, because the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm su}
are
O(1)
and bounded away from
1
. The eigenvalue equation is then reduced to
\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm c}{\bm{\alpha}}=e^{\nu_{2}T}{\bm{\alpha}}
. Because
\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm c}
is expanded as
\tilde{\bm{M}}_{\rm c}\sim{\mathbb{I}}+{O(\mu)}
, we have

{\bm{\alpha}}={\bm{\alpha}}^{(0)}+{O}(\mu){\bm{\alpha}}^{(1)}
.

To obtain the eigenvector in the original coordinates, we multiply it by the transformation Jacobian:

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}_{2}(\theta)

\displaystyle={\cal J}(0)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{\alpha}}\\
{\bm{0}}\end{array}\right)={\cal P}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathbb{I}}&{\bm{0}}\\
\partial_{\bm{c}}{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}}(0))&{\mathbb{I}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{\alpha}}^{(0)}+{O}(\mu){\bm{\alpha}}^{(1)}\\
{\bm{0}}\end{array}\right).

On the limit cycle of radius
r\sim\sqrt{\mu}
, the derivative of the center manifold scales as
\partial_{\bm{c}}{\bm{h}}({\bm{c}})={O}(|{\bm{c}}|)={O}(\sqrt{\mu})
. Hence, the nonlinear transformation introduces an
O(\sqrt{\mu})
correction to the full-space eigenvector:

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}_{2}(\theta)

\displaystyle={\cal P}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{\alpha}}^{(0)}\\
{\bm{0}}\end{array}\right)+\sqrt{\mu}{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(1)}+\cdots={\bm{v}}_{2}^{(0)}+\sqrt{\mu}{\bm{v}}_{2}^{(1)}+\cdots\,.

A similar argument applies to the left eigenvector, yielding
{\bm{w}}_{2}(\theta)={\bm{w}}_{2}^{(0)}+\sqrt{\mu}{\bm{w}}_{2}^{(1)}+\cdots
.
∎

The proof of the lemma

is as follows.

Proof.

Note that we consider

and

. We map the state transition matrix back to the original coordinates using the Jacobian of the transformation evaluated along the limit cycle,
{\cal J}(t)

\tilde{\Phi}_{\theta}(t,0)={\cal J}^{-1}(t)\Phi_{\theta}(t,0){\cal J}(0)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}(t)

By expanding the exponential as in standard perturbation theory, we note:

\displaystyle\hat{T}\exp\Bigl[\int_{0}^{t}ds\tilde{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{({\rm c})}(s)\Bigr]

(S.148)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{v}}_{2}(t)

The same logic as this derivation is applied to obtain the relation for

S.V
Parametric response of entropy production rate

As explained in Sec.
S.IV
, we consider the case where the concentration
a_{\ell}
in the chemostat so that one can control the amplitude of the parameter
\theta
. The parameter
\theta
is a control parameter to govern the bifurcations. In this setup, we discuss
general
diverging behavior of the response of the average entropy production against the change of parameter
\theta
by defining the exponents.
We define the parametric response as

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\sigma

\displaystyle={\partial\over\partial\theta}\lim_{\tau\to}{1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\sigma(t)\,.

(S.152)

We then look at the diverging behavior with the exponents
\beta_{\pm}
:

\displaystyle{\partial_{\theta}\sigma}

\displaystyle\propto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\theta-\theta_{c})^{-\beta_{+}}&\theta>\theta_{c}\\
(\theta_{c}-\theta)^{-\beta_{-}}&\theta<\theta_{c}\\
\end{array}\right.

S.V.1
Fixed-point cases

In the case of fixed points, we write the entropy production as
\sigma(\theta,\bar{\bm{x}})
, where
\bar{\bm{x}}
is a
\theta
-dependent fixed point. Hence one can write the parametric response as

\displaystyle{\partial_{\theta}\sigma}

\displaystyle={\partial\sigma(\theta,\bar{\bm{x}})\over\partial\theta}+\sum_{\ell}{\partial\sigma(\theta,\bar{\bm{x}})\over\partial\bar{x}_{\ell}}{\partial\bar{x}_{\ell}\over\partial\theta}\,.

\displaystyle={\partial\sigma(\theta,\bar{\bm{x}})\over\partial\theta}+[{\rm grad}\sigma]^{\top}{\cal J}(\bar{\bm{c}}){\partial\bar{\bm{\zeta}}\over\partial\theta}\,.

(S.155)

From the property 3 in Sec.
S.II.4
,
[{\rm grad}\sigma]^{\top}
is finite, and hence the singular behavior can appear from the term
{\partial\bar{\bm{\zeta}}/\partial\theta}
.

In the case of pitchfork bifurcation, we have the solution
\bar{c}=\pm\sqrt{\mu}
for
\mu:=\theta-\theta_{c}>0
, and
0
for
\mu<0
, and hence we have
(\beta_{+},\beta_{-})=(1/2,0_{-})
.

In the case of transcritical bifurcation, we have the solution
\bar{c}=\mu
for
\mu>0
, and
0
for
\mu<0
, and hence we have
(\beta_{+},\beta_{-})=(0_{-},0_{-})
.

In the case of saddle-node bifurcation, we have the solution
\bar{c}=\pm\sqrt{\mu}
for
\mu>0
, and hence we have
(\beta_{+},\beta_{-})=(1/2,\varnothing)
.

S.V.2
Hopf bifurcation

In the case of a supercritical Hopf bifurcation, the normal form yields the solution

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{c}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bm{0},&\quad\text{for }\mu<0,\\
\sqrt{\mu}\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\omega t)\\
\sin(\omega t)\end{pmatrix},&\quad\text{for }\mu>0\,.\end{array}\right.

For
\mu>0
, the solution is periodic with period
T=2\pi/\omega
. Accordingly, we consider

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(J_{+\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t))-J_{-\rho}(\bar{\bm{x}}(t))\right)dt.

(S.158)

By the law of mass action,
J_{\pm\rho}
are polynomials in the components of
\bar{\bm{c}}(t)
. Therefore, they can be expressed as polynomials of
\sqrt{\mu}\cos(\omega t)
,
\sqrt{\mu}\sin(\omega t)
, and constants.
Using the identity

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\cos x)^{m}(\sin x)^{n}\,dx=0,\quad\text{if either $m$ or $n$ is odd},

(S.159)

it follows that the term

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(J_{+\rho}(\bm{x}(t))-J_{-\rho}(\bm{x}(t))\right)dt

contains only integer powers of
\mu
, and no half-integer powers. Consequently, we obtain
(\beta_{-},\beta_{+})=(0_{-},0_{-})
.

S.VI
General inequality between
\alpha
and
\beta

In this section, we derive the general inequality between
\alpha
and
\beta
, valid for any kinds of bifurcation in the chemical reactions. We use the environment entropy (
S.13
). One can easily find the following identity:

\displaystyle{\partial_{\theta}}\langle\Sigma_{\rm e}(\Gamma)\rangle-\langle\partial_{\theta}\Sigma_{\rm e}(\Gamma)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int d\Gamma\,{\cal P}(\Gamma){\partial\ln{\cal P}(\Gamma)\over\partial\theta}\Sigma_{\rm e}(\Gamma)\,.

(S.160)

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality leads to the Cramer-Rao type inequality

\displaystyle\Bigl[{\partial_{\theta}}\langle\Sigma_{\rm e}(\Gamma)\rangle-\langle\partial_{\theta}\Sigma_{\rm e}(\Gamma)\rangle\Bigr]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq I(\theta,\tau)\,{\rm Var}\Sigma_{\rm e}\,,

(S.161)

where
I(\theta,\tau)
and
{\rm Var}\Sigma_{\rm e}
are respectively defined as

\displaystyle I(\theta,\tau)

\displaystyle=\Bigl<-{\partial^{2}\over\partial\theta^{2}}\ln{\cal P}(\Gamma)\Bigr>\,,

(S.162)

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\Sigma_{\rm e}

\displaystyle=\Bigl<\Bigl[\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}Z_{\rho}(\Gamma)\Bigr]^{2}\Bigr>-\Bigl<\sum_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}Z_{\rho}(\Gamma)\Bigr>^{2}\,.

(S.163)

The quantity
I(\theta,\tau)
is the Fisher information computed through the path trajectory. We define the following scaled Fisher information

\displaystyle i(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\lim_{\Omega\to\infty}{1\over\Omega}I(\theta,\tau)\,.

(S.164)

We obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle\Bigl[{\partial_{\theta}\sigma}-\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\lim_{\Omega\to\infty}{1\over\Omega}\langle\partial_{\theta}\Sigma_{\rm e}(\Gamma)\rangle\Bigr]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq i(\theta)\,{\rm Var}\sigma\,.

(S.165)

We first discuss the property of the second part in the left hand side. Using the exact micro-macro correspondence proven by Kurtz
Kurtz 1972
;
Kurtz 1971
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\lim_{\Omega\to\infty}{1\over\Omega}\langle\partial_{\theta}\Sigma_{\rm e}(\Gamma)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho}({\partial_{\theta}\mu_{\rho}})\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\,J_{\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))\,,

(S.166)

From the property 1 in Sec.
S.II.4
, the long time average of
J_{\rho}
must be finite, and hence this term does not show the divergence.

We next consider the general properties of the scaled Fisher information as follows. We first observe the following expression for the Fisher information.

\displaystyle I(\theta,\tau)

\displaystyle=-\int d\Gamma\,\Bigl[{\partial^{2}\over\partial\theta^{2}}\ln{\cal P}(\Gamma)\Bigr]{\cal P}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\rho}\left[\sum_{{\bm{n}}\neq{\bm{n}}^{\prime}}\left({\partial^{2}\over\partial\theta^{2}}\ln W_{{\bm{n}},{\bm{n}}^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right)W_{{\bm{n}},{\bm{n}}^{\prime}}^{\rho}P({\bm{n}}^{\prime},t)-\sum_{{\bm{n}}\neq{\bm{n}}^{\prime}}\left({\partial^{2}\over\partial\theta^{2}}W_{{\bm{n}},{\bm{n}}^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right)P({\bm{n}}^{\prime},t)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho}\sum_{{\bm{n}}\neq{\bm{n}}^{\prime}}\left({\partial\over\partial\theta}\ln W_{{\bm{n}},{\bm{n}}^{\prime}}^{\rho}\right)^{2}W_{{\bm{n}},{\bm{n}}^{\prime}}^{\rho}P({\bm{n}}^{\prime},t)\,.

(S.167)

Now we use the detailed expression of the transition rate (
S.4
) to obtain

\displaystyle{\partial\over\partial\theta}\ln W^{\rho}_{\bm{n},\bm{n}\mp\nabla_{\rho}}

\displaystyle={\partial\over\partial\theta}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}{\nabla_{\pm\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}}\ln a_{\ell^{\prime}}=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}{\nabla_{\pm\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}}{\hat{a}_{\ell^{\prime}}\over a_{\ell^{\prime}}}=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}{\nabla_{\pm\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\over\theta+a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{(0)}/\hat{a}_{\ell^{\prime}}}\,,

(S.168)

where we use the definition of
\theta
in (
S.39
). The scaled Fisher information is thus given as follows

\displaystyle i(\theta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\sum_{\rho}\left[\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}{\nabla_{+\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\over\theta+a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{(0)}/\hat{a}_{\ell^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}J_{+\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))+\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}{\nabla_{-\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\over\theta+a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{(0)}/\hat{a}_{\ell^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}J_{-\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))\right]

\displaystyle\leq c_{\rm max}\lim_{\tau\to\infty}{1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\sum_{\rho}\left[J_{+\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))+J_{-\rho}({\bm{x}}(t))\right]\,,

(S.169)

where

\displaystyle c_{\rm max}

\displaystyle:=\max_{\rho}\left\{\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}{\nabla_{+\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\over\theta+a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{(0)}/\hat{a}_{\ell^{\prime}}}\right)^{2},\left(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{c}}{\nabla_{-\rho}^{\ell^{\prime}}\over\theta+a_{\ell^{\prime}}^{(0)}/\hat{a}_{\ell^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}\right\}\,.

(S.170)

The inequality (
S.169
) indicates that the scaled Fisher information is related to the activity per unit volume in the thermodynamic limit. From the property 1 in Sec.
S.II.4
, the scaled Fisher information generically does not show the divergence.

As a result, the inequality (
S.165
) immediately leads to the following inequality on the exponents

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\geq 2\beta\,,

(S.171)

for
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
.
