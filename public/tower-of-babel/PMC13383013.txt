Amiodarone use prior to cardiac transplant in children is associated with increased need for post-transplant chronotropic support
Authors: Melodie M Lynn, Alexander Alali, Timothy J Humlicek, Kyle D Hope, Kriti Puri, Taylor S Howard, Tam Dan Pham, Hari P Tunuguntla, Swati Choudhry, Christina Y Miyake, Santiago O Valdes, Jack F Price, Susan W Denfield, William J Dreyer, Jeffery J Kim, Joseph A Spinner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe use of amiodarone prior to cardiac transplant in adults is associated with worse post-transplant outcomes. Data are scarce in pediatrics regarding the association of pre-amiodarone use with post-transplant outcomes. We aim to describe and compare pediatric post-transplant outcomes based on use of pre-transplant amiodarone at a large pediatric cardiac transplant center.MethodsWe performed a retrospective cohort study of pediatric patients 18 years or younger who underwent cardiac transplant between 2015 – 2021. Pre-transplant amiodarone use was defined as use/administration of amiodarone within two weeks pre-transplant. Pre-transplant characteristics and post-transplant outcomes including mortality and markers of morbidity were compared between the two groups.ResultsSix percent (10/156) of the study population were on amiodarone pre-transplant. There were no differences in mortality between the two groups. Severe primary graft dysfunction (PGD) incidence was higher in the pre-transplant amiodarone group (p = 0.04). The use of post-transplant isoproterenol (p = 0.01) and temporary pacing (p = 0.01) were higher in the pre-transplant amiodarone group. There was no association between either amiodarone dose or length therapy with post-transplant outcomes.ConclusionsHigher rates of PGD, increased use of isoproterenol, and need for temporary pacing post-transplant among patients receiving pre-transplant amiodarone suggests that amiodarone may contribute to inadequate chronotropy in the acute post-operative period. The need for chronotropic support occurred irrespective of amiodarone dose or length of therapy.Keywords: amiodarone, pediatric heart transplant, chronotropic incompetence, primary graft dysfunction, post-transplant outcomes

Background

Amiodarone is often the preferred antiarrhythmic medication in patients with decreased cardiac function due to its antiarrhythmic properties in the absence of frequent or significant cardio-depressant effects unlike other antiarrhythmic medications. Due to its long half-life, side effect profile, and potential for interactions with other medications, there has been concern about the use of amiodarone in patients waiting for heart transplantation (HTx).1, 2, 3

Due to potential effects of amiodarone on the transplanted graft, multiple studies have been performed to evaluate the risk of amiodarone use pre-HTx on post-HTx outcomes. Single center and larger database studies evaluating this question among adult HTx recipients have demonstrated mixed results. The use of amiodarone pre-HTx has been associated with worse early post-HTx outcomes in the adult HTx literature.4, 5, 6, 7, 8 These outcomes include an increased risk of severe primary graft dysfunction7, 8, 9, 10 and lower 30-day and 1-year survival rates among those patients who received pre-HTx amiodarone.4, 5, 6 In contrast, other studies did not identify a difference in post-HTx outcomes.11, 12, 13

The data exploring the effects of pre-HTx amiodarone among pediatric HTx recipients remains scarce. Butts et al. previously described that pre-HTx amiodarone use was not associated with adverse outcomes using International Society of Heart and Lung Transplantation (ISHLT) International Thoracic Organ Registry data.14 However, given the limitations of this database, they were not able to discern the need for higher inotropic or chronotropic support. They were also unable to describe if dose or length of amiodarone therapy affected post-HTx outcomes.

We aim to describe and compare pediatric HTx outcomes based on use of pre-HTx amiodarone at a large pediatric HTx center.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

We performed a retrospective cohort study of pediatric patients 18 years of age or younger who underwent HTx between 2015 and 2021 at a large pediatric HTx center. The study was approved by the Baylor College of Medicine Institutional Review Board. If a subject required a second HTx within the same study period, the second HTx episode was excluded. Any patient undergoing multiorgan transplantation was also excluded from this analysis.

Study outcomes and definitions

Pre-HTx amiodarone use was defined as a patient receiving amiodarone within two weeks of HTx. Two weeks captured the subjects who received amiodarone closest to time of transplant. Patients that received amiodarone further from transplant were less likely to have clinical meaningful exposure, so were excluded from the study. The primary outcome was early post-HTx mortality defined as 30-day and 1-year mortality. The secondary outcomes were markers of increased morbidity and included severe primary graft dysfunction (PGD), median vasoactive-inotropic score (VIS) within 30 days post-HTx, the use of isoproterenol, need for antiarrhythmic therapy within 30 days post-HTx, need for temporary pacing within 30 days post-HTx, intensive care unit (ICU) length of stay (LOS), and post-HTx hospital LOS. Severe PGD was defined by use of extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) or ventricular assist device (VAD) within 24 h post-HTx.

Pre-HTx characteristics and post-HTx outcomes were compared between those who received amiodarone pre-HTx versus those who did not. Within the amiodarone cohort, the association of dose and length of amiodarone therapy were also analyzed as predictors of post-HTx outcomes.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using 2025 JMP® 19. JMP Statistical Discovery LLC, Cary, NC. All pre-HTx characteristics and post-HTx outcomes are displayed as percentages for categorical variables and median with interquartile ranges for continuous variables. We compared pre-HTx characteristics and post-HTx outcomes using chi-square or Fisher’s Exact Test for categorical variables or Wilcoxon rank sum test for continuous variables as appropriate. Mortality data were evaluated using Kaplan-Meier analyses.

Results

Patient characteristics and outcomes

There were 156 patients 18 years or younger who underwent HTx at our center between 2015 and 2021. Ten patients (6%) were treated with amiodarone pre-HTx. The median duration of amiodarone use was 130 days [41 – 415 days]. The median dose of amiodarone received was 8.25 mg/kg/day [5.75 – 12.75 mg/kg/day]. Ventricular arrhythmias followed by atrial arrhythmias were the most common reasons for amiodarone use.

There were no differences in age at HTx, sex, race, ethnicity, weight, or underlying cardiac diagnosis (Table 1). Ventricular assist device (VAD) at time of transplant was higher in the amiodarone group (p-value 0.01).

Amiodarone dose or length of therapy was not associated with differences in the incidence of severe PGD. The need for isoproterenol or temporary pacing occurred irrespective of amiodarone dose or length of therapy in our study population. No one in our study required permanent pacemaker placement post-HTx.

Discussion

Amiodarone use

Amiodarone use pre-HTx was uncommon (6%) among children who underwent HTx at our institution during the study period, which is similar to proportion of patients who received pre-HTx amiodarone (10%) in the Butts et al. ISHLT registry study.14 Pre-amiodarone use is much more common in adult HTx recipients with rates reported at 30–40%.4, 5, 6 This variation between adult and pediatric HTx may partly be related to the different indications for HTx; adults who undergo HTx are much more likely to have previously experienced a sudden cardiac arrest prompting listing for HTx.15 The amiodarone side-effect profile, including thyroid dysfunction, ocular side effects, liver dysfunction, and skin changes, likely also contributes to its more limited use in the pediatric population.16, 17, 18, 19, 20

It is possible that a different impact of amiodarone on post-transplant outcomes in adults compared to children is due, in part, to differences in metabolism of amiodarone. Some studies suggest that the metabolism of amiodarone is faster in children than in adults. This may result in a quicker elimination of amiodarone and less impactful effect on the new graft.21, 22, 23, 24

Post transplant mortality

In the context of very low overall early 30-day and 1-year post-HTx mortality at our institution, there were no differences in the 30-day or 1-year post-HTx mortality between those that received amiodarone pre-HTx vs those who did not. The 1-year post-HTx mortality among patients who did receive pre-HTx amiodarone (10%) was similar to the previously published pediatric data (11%).14 This is a clear divergence compared to multiple studies among adult HTx recipients which have reported that pre-HTx amiodarone is associated with an increase in early post-HTx mortality.4, 5, 6

Chronotropic incompetence

In comparison to the ISHLT registry study by Butts et al, this single center study was able to assess more granular post-HTx outcomes (not captured by registry data) that could have been affected using pre-HTx amiodarone.14 Patients who did receive pre-HTx amiodarone were more likely to require either temporary pacing or isoproterenol compared to those who did not. These findings suggest that amiodarone may contribute to inadequate chronotropy in the acute post-operative period. This finding is consistent among adult HTx studies which have reported pre-HTx amiodarone is associated with bradycardia and need for temporary pacing, isoproterenol, and permanent pacemaker placement.3, 5, 9, 25, 26 Pre-HTx amiodarone was an independent predictor for the need for chronotropic support post HTx in Ustunkaya’s study of over 800 patients.27

Severe primary graft dysfunction

Pre-HTx amiodarone was associated significantly with severe PGD. This finding suggests that amiodarone may increase the risk of PGD in pediatric patients. Most of the amiodarone cohort were supported with VAD at time of transplant. Durable VAD support is also a known risk factor for severe PGD.28, 29 Chinnadurai et al found that both continuous-flow left ventricular assist device and amiodarone use were independent risk factors for severe PGD, but the combination of both risk factors was associated with increased incidence of PGD compared to either factor alone.7 We did not identify an increased need of vasoactive support between our two groups, again which suggests amiodarone most significant hemodynamic effect is on chronotropy of the transplant graft. Isoproterenol may be helpful to ameliorate the risk of PGD, especially those at increased risk of chronotropic incompetency.

Limitations

Due to the retrospective nature of our study, there are several possible limitations in these analyses. We could only assess correlation and not causality when assessing the impact of pre-HTx amiodarone on post-HTx outcomes. The low number of patients receiving amiodarone also limits the power of our study. As a single-center study, these findings may not be generalizable to the broader pediatric heart transplant population. Larger, multicenter studies are needed to better evaluate the amiodarone effects on post-transplant outcomes in the pediatric population.

Conclusions

Pre-HTx amiodarone use was uncommon in this single-center pediatric HTx cohort. Pre-HTx amiodarone appears to be associated with severe primary graft dysfunction, increased use of isoproterenol, and need for temporary pacing post-HTx suggesting that it may cause chronotropic incompetency in the acute post-operative period. Pre-HTx amiodarone was not associated with post-HTx mortality or need for permanent pacemaker placement. This contrasts with the relationship between pre-HTx amiodarone and post-HTx outcomes in adults, and it suggests that we should not be deterred to use amiodarone, when indicated, in pediatric patients awaiting HTx.
