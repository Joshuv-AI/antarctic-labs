Abstract
. Let K K be a field of characteristic zero. Motivated by the conjecture that an enveloping algebra U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) U(\mathfrak{g}) is Noetherian only if 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is finite dimensional, we define the notion of weakly Noetherian Lie algebras. The main result, Theorem A, states that weakly Noetherian Lie algebras have a very constrained structure. In the specific case of ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} graded Lie algebras, it implies an explicit classification of the perfect strictly weakly Noetherian Lie algebras, stated in Theorem B. The proofs of both theorems are quite long, and uses concrete results due to Tits, Formanek, Razmyslov, Grabowski and the author. The first theorem provides some insight on the desired conjecture. The second one implies the conjecture for all perfect ℤ n {\mathbb{Z}}^{n} -graded Lie algebras, improving a celebrated theorem of Sierra and Walton.

Introduction

0.1.
General introduction

Over a field
K
of characteristic 0,
any finite dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

admits a Levi decomposition

\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\mathfrak{r}
, where
\mathfrak{s}
is
semisimple and
\mathfrak{r}
is a solvable ideal.
Motivated by the conjecture
mentionned in the abstract, we pose the
very naive question:

Do infinite dimensional Lie algebras
admit a structure

theory similar to the finite dimensional case?

In general the answer is obviously negative, but we can expect a positive answer for some classes of infinite dimensional Lie algebras. For example, Aldosray, Amayo and Stewart

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
32
]

has proved some general results for Lie algebras satisfying some Artinian-Noetherian conditions.
Here we try a different approach:
we say that a Lie algera
\mathfrak{G}
is
weakly Noetherian
if

(a)

for any subalgebra
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{G}
,
the space
\mathfrak{m}/[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]
is finite dimensional.

If, in addition

(b)

any chain of
characteristic ideals in
\mathfrak{G}
stabilizes,

\mathfrak{G}
is called
Noetherian
. Moreover
\mathfrak{G}
is called

strictly weakly Noetherian
if
\mathfrak{G}\otimes L
is weakly Noetherian, for any finite extension
L
of
K
.
Typical examples of Noetherian Lie algebras are the Krichever-Novikov Lie algebras
\mathrm{Vect}_{X}
of vector fields on a smooth algebraic curve
X
, as it is proved in
Section
4
.

At first glance, the notion of
weakly Noetherian Lie algebras looks like an abstract nonsense notion.
In fact, we will see that it
is connected with interesting concrete algebraic theories, as explained in Subsection

0.3
.

First we consider the case of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebras,

\mathcal{L}=\oplus_{{\bf m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\bf m}
, where is is assumed that all homogenous components have finite dimensions. Given a finite extension
L
of
K
, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Witt}(L)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Der}\,L[t]

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vir}(L)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Der}\,L[t,t^{-1}],

and we denote as
\widehat{\mathrm{Vir}}(L)
the Virasoro algebra, that is the universal central extension of
\mathrm{Vir}(L)
. For
perfect
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra, there is an explicit classification, up to a finite dimensional factor.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a perfect strictly
weakly Noetherian

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra
and let
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
be its its universal central extension. Then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\simeq\mathfrak{g}\,\oplus\,\big[\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Witt}(E_{i})\big]\,\oplus\,\big[\oplus_{j=1}^{m}\widehat{\mathrm{Vir}}(F_{j})\big]

where
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\mathfrak{r}
is a (perfect) finite dimensional Lie algebra,

n
,
m
are integers and

E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
are finite extensions of
K
.

In order to state the structure result in
general we first need some additional definitions.
Recall that the
radical

\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{G})
of a Lie algebra

\mathfrak{G}
is the intersection of all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of codimension
>1
. Assume now that

\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra satisfying
the following simplifying hypothesis

(h1)

\mathfrak{G}=[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
, and

(h2)

\mathfrak{G}
does not admit finite dimensional simple quotients.

Then we define, by transfinite induction, a decreasing sequence,
indexed by ordinals
\alpha
,
of Lie subalgebras

\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{G}_{(0)}\supset\mathfrak{G}_{(1)}\supset\ldots\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}\supset\ldots

as follows:

(a)

if the ordinal
\alpha
is a successor,
that is
\alpha=\beta+1
for some
\beta
, then

\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}=[\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{G}_{(\beta)}),\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{G}_{(\beta)})]

(b)

If
\alpha>0
is a limit ordinal, then

\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}=[\cap_{\beta<\alpha}\,\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{G}_{(\beta)}),\cap_{\beta<\alpha}\,\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{G}_{(\beta)})].

In its simplified form,
the main result is as follows:

Theorem A
.

(simplified version) Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra
satisfying (h1) and (h2).
Then, for any ordinal
\alpha
:

(a)

the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}

is a perfect ideal,

(b)

\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}/\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha+1)}

is a central extension of

\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}/\operatorname{rad}\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}
by a finite
dimensional center,

(c)

any
simple quotient of
\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}
is infinite dimensional,
and

(d)

for
\alpha\geq 1
, no Krichever-Novikov algebras occur as a quotient of
\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}
.

Moreover if
\mathfrak{G}
is Noetherian,
then
\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}=0
for
\alpha
big enough.

Therefore, Theorem A shows that Noetherian Lie algebras
\mathfrak{G}
have a very
constrained structure. Roughly speaking,
\mathfrak{G}

is a "tower" of central extensions of
infinite dimensional simple Lie algebras.

0.2.
Relation with the Sierra-Walton conjecture

Our investigation is motivated by the following:

Sierra-Walton Conjecture 1
.

[
31
]

If
\mathfrak{g}
is infinite dimensional, then
U(\mathfrak{g})
is not Noetherian.

This question has been stated by several authors since the 70’s, among them
R. K. Amayo, I. Stewart, J. Dixmier, V. Latishev, K. A. Brown
[
3
,
13
]

A breakthrough result was obtained in 2013 by S. Sierra and Ch. Walton:

Theorem 2
.

[
31
]

The enveloping algebra of the Witt algebra
W(K)

is not Noetherian.

Consequently, S. Sierra and Ch. Walton stated this question as a conjecture.
From Sierra-Walton Theorem, one can derived the same result for all Krichever-Novikov algebras
[
14
]
.
The conjecture was confirmed recently for some other classes of Lie algebras

[
7
,
8
]
and
[
5
]
. The old work
[
35
]
is also
connected with these questions.
As a consequence of Theorem B, we obtain the following generalization of Sierra-Walton Theorem.

Corollary
65
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a perfect

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra.
The algebra
U(\mathcal{L})
is left Noetherian
only if
\mathcal{L}
has finite dimension.

A refined version of Theorem A is proved in section

8
. As a consequence we deduce:

Corollary
45
.

Any infinite dimensional Noetherian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
contains two characteristic ideals
\mathfrak{p}\supset\mathfrak{q}
with

\displaystyle\mathrm{codim}

\displaystyle\,\mathfrak{p}<\infty

\displaystyle\mathrm{codim}\,\mathfrak{q}=\infty

such that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}

is just-infinite and either

(type A)

\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}
is simple, or

(type B)

\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}
is residually nilpotent, or

(type C)

\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}
is perfect, residually semi-simple
and does not satisfies any polynomial identity.

By
[
18
]
, any Krichever-Novikov algebra

\mathrm{Vect}_{X}
is simple, that is of type A. Given any point

P
on the curve
X
, the Lie subalgebra
\mathrm{Vect}_{X}^{(2)}
of vector fields vanishing twice at
P
is residually nilpotent,
that is of type B. However, we do not know examples of Noetherian Lie algebras of type
C
.

It follows from Corollary

45
that the Sierra-Walton conjecture can be reduced to these three types of Lie algebras.

Hovewer Theorem A and some intuitive arguments, briefly outlined in Subsection
9.2
, suggest that the conjecture could be undecidable for some simple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{S}
.

0.3.
The ideas of the proofs

The proof of Theorem A is quite long. Although its statement looks formal, its proof is based on concrete results.
The core of the proof consists of the following result:

Theorem
41
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra.

Any maximal ideal of codimension
>1
is characteristic.

The difficult point in the proof of Theorem
41
concerns maximal ideals of finite codimension. It is based on
the theory of affine Lie algebras, as
initiated in
[
33
,
34
]
, and the next result:

Theorem
25
.

(simplified version) Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra and let
d>1
be an integer.

Then
\mathfrak{G}
contains only finitely many maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of codimension
d
.

Its proof combines the theory of central identities of Formanek
[
15
]
and Razmyslov
[
29
]
, together with the finitness of the dimension of centroids, i.e. Proposition
20
.

Obviously the proof of Theorem A runs by transfinite induction, and we will illustrate the use of Theorem
41

to go to the second step. Using hypotheses (h1) and (h2), it is quite easy to prove that

\mathfrak{G}_{(1)}
is perfect and that
\mathfrak{G}_{(0)}/\mathfrak{G}_{(1)}
is a central extension of
\mathfrak{G}_{(0)}/\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak{G}_{(0)})
. To continue
by induction, we consider a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathfrak{G}_{(1)}

and the corresponding simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{G}_{(1)}/\mathfrak{m}
.
Using Theorem
41
, we show that
the Lie algebra
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{S})
of outer derivations of
\mathfrak{S}

is nonzero. Thus
by
[
12
]
,
\mathfrak{S}
is infinite dimensional, which allows to pursue the induction. Moreover by
a theorem of Grabowski

[
19
]
,
\mathfrak{S}
is not a
Krichever-Novikov Lie algebra either.

The proof of Theorem B starts with the following theorem,
whose proof is essentially based on author’s result

[
26
,
27
,
28
,
5
]
for
algebraically closed fields. The descent
argument from
\overline{K}
to
K
is an easy
consequence of Hilbert’s 90 Theorem.

Theorem
57
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a simple
infinite-dimensional
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded
Lie algebra over
K
.

If
\mathcal{L}
is strictly Noetherian,
then
\mathcal{L}
is isomorphic to

\mathrm{Witt}(L)
or
\mathrm{Vir}(L)
for some
finite extension
L
of
K
.

Given an arbitrary weakly Notherian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
, the
definition of the filtration

\mathfrak{G}\supset\mathfrak{G}_{(0)}\supset\mathfrak{G}_{(1)}\supset\ldots\mathfrak{G}_{(\alpha)}\supset\ldots

is generalized in Section
8
. However, when

\mathfrak{G}
does not satisfy (h1) or (h2), we have

\mathfrak{G}\neq\mathfrak{G}_{(0)}
. Nevertheless,
\mathfrak{G}_{(0)}
satisfies the conclusion of Theorem A.

Now, let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem B. Theorem A(d) and Theorem
57
easily imply that
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}=0
.
Moreover, we also deduce
that
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}/\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L}_{(1)})
is a finite sum
of copies of Witt and Virasoro Lie algebras.
Using an argument about representation theory of
finite dimensional Lie algebras,
we also prove that
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
is finite dimensional.
Then Grabowskiś theorem
[
19
]
is used to show that, up to isogeny,
\mathcal{L}
is the direct sum of

\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
and
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
, which concludes the proof of Theorem B.

1.
Main Definitions and Notations

From now on, we denote by
K
a field of characteristic zero, and we write
\overline{K}
for its algebraic closure.

Depending upon the context, we will
use symbols like
\mathfrak{G}
,
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathcal{L}
for Lie algebras
and
\mathfrak{m}
,
\mathfrak{r}\ldots
for ideals of Lie algebras. Usually
\mathfrak{G}
denotes a Lie algebra of
arbitrary dimension,
\mathfrak{g}
a Lie algebra of finite dimension and
\mathcal{L}
a graded Lie algebra.

We collect now the main definitions, which mostly concern the infinite dimensional Lie algebras.

1.1.
Simple Lie algebras and radicals

As usual, a one-dimensional Lie algebra
is not considered as a simple Lie algebra.
Accordingly, the

radical
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
, denoted by
\operatorname{rad}\mathfrak{G}
,
is the intersection of all its maximal ideals of codimension
>1
. Its finite radical,
denoted as
\operatorname{rad}^{f}\mathfrak{G}
, is the intersection of all its maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
with

1<\dim\mathfrak{m}<\infty.

We define a
semisimple
Lie algebra as a
finite dimensional Lie algebra
which is a direct sum of simple Lie algebras. With our restrictive convention, neither a simple Lie algebra of infinite dimension nor an infinite sum
of simple finite dimensional Lie algebra is semisimple.

1.2.
Derived and descending central series

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a Lie algebra of arbitrary dimension. Recall that
\mathfrak{G}
is called
perfect
if
\mathfrak{G}=[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
.

The descending central series of
\mathfrak{G}
is
defined by induction by

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{1}\mathfrak{G}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathfrak{G},

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{k+1}\mathfrak{G}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\mathfrak{G},\mathscr{C}^{k}\mathfrak{G}]\noindent{\rm\,\,for\,\,}k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\cap_{k\geq 1}\mathscr{C}^{k}\mathfrak{G}

and the derived series by

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{0}\mathfrak{G}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathfrak{G}

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{k+1}\mathfrak{G}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G},\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}]\noindent{\rm\,\,for\,\,}k\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\cap_{k\geq 1}\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}.

With these conventions we have

[\mathscr{C}^{k}\mathfrak{G},\mathscr{C}^{\ell}\mathfrak{G}]\subset\mathscr{C}^{k+\ell}\mathfrak{G}
and

\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}\subset\mathscr{C}^{2^{k}}\mathfrak{G}
.

We say that the derived series

\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}

stabilizes
if
there is an integer
N
such that

\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}=\mathscr{D}^{N}\mathfrak{G}\,\,\forall k\geq N,

which is equivalent to

\mathscr{D}^{N+1}\mathfrak{G}=\mathscr{D}^{N}\mathfrak{G}
. The statement that the central
series
\mathscr{C}^{k}\mathfrak{G}
stabilizes is similarly defined. In general, the Lie algebra

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}(\mathfrak{G})
is not perfect, and , for any ordinal
\alpha
we could define
\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{G}
.
In the paper, we are only interested in the last term of the series, say
\mathscr{D}^{*}\mathfrak{G}
, which can be simply defined without using ordinals.
Indeed
\mathscr{D}^{*}\mathfrak{G}
is the sum of all perfect ideals, which is obviously
the largest perfect ideal in
\mathfrak{G}
.
When
\mathscr{D}^{*}\mathfrak{G}=0
, the Lie algebra is called

purely imperfect
.

We shall use repeatedly the following obvious facts.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a Lie algebra.

(a)

If
\mathfrak{G}/[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
has finitedimension and its central series stabilizes, then
\mathfrak{G}/\mathscr{C}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}
is a finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra.

(b)

If the derived series of
\mathfrak{G}
stabilizes, then

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}
is a perfect Lie algebra.

1.3.
Residual and strict properties

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a certain property of Lie algebras.
When a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
satisfies
\mathcal{P}
, we say
that
\mathfrak{G}
is
\mathcal{P}
.
Given
\mathfrak{G}
a Lie algebra, we say that

(a)

\mathfrak{G}
is
residually
\mathcal{P}

if, for any
x\in\mathfrak{G}
, there is an ideal

\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{G}
with
x\notin\mathfrak{m}
such that

\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}
is
\mathcal{P}
,

(b)

\mathfrak{G}
is
strictly
\mathcal{P}

if
\mathfrak{G}\otimes L
is
\mathcal{P}
for any finite extension
K
of
L
.

For exemple,
\mathfrak{G}
is
residually nilpotent
if
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}=0
.
Furthermore,
\mathfrak{G}
is
residually semi-simple
if
\operatorname{rad}^{f}\mathfrak{g}=0
.
Equivalently, there is a collection
\{\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in A\}
of finite dimensional simple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}
such that

\mathfrak{G}
is a dense subalgebra of
\prod_{\alpha\in A}\,\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}
.

The previous two properties are not exclusive each other. For example, the Lie
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)\otimes tK[t]
. is simultaneously residually semi-simple and residually nilpotent.

1.4.
Other definitions

We now recollect standard definitions for
arbitrary Lie algebras
\mathfrak{G}
.

(a)

Given another Lie algebras
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
,
we say that
\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
are
commensurable
if they contain the same subalgebra of finite codimension.

(b)

A Lie algebra

\mathfrak{s}
is called a
section
of
\mathfrak{G}
if there is a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and an ideal

\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathfrak{p}
such that

\mathfrak{s}\simeq\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}
.

(c)

The
centralizer
of a subset
S\subset\mathfrak{G}
is the Lie subalgebra

C_{\mathfrak{G}}(S)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathfrak{G}\mid[x,S]=0\}.

The
normalizer
of a subalgebra

\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{G}
is the Lie subalgebra

N_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{a})\coloneqq\{x\in\mathfrak{G}\mid[x,\mathfrak{a}]\subset\mathfrak{a}\}.

(d)

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
is called
just-infinite
if
\dim\,\mathfrak{G}=\infty
but any nonzero ideal
of
\mathfrak{G}
has finite codimension.

(e)

Given an arbitrary algebra
A
, we denote as
\operatorname{Der}\,A
the Lie algebra of derivation of
A
.
For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
, the Lie algebra of
outer derivations
is

\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{G}):=\operatorname{Der}\mathfrak{G}/\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{G}).

(f)

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
and an ideal

\mathfrak{m}
, we say that
\mathfrak{G}
is an
extension
of

\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}
by
\mathfrak{m}
. When
\mathfrak{m}
is a central ideal,

\mathfrak{G}
is called a
central extension of

\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}
.

(g)

For a perfect Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
,
we write
\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}
for its universal central extension.

(h)

An ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is called a

characteristic ideal
if

\partial\,\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{m}
for any
derivation
\partial
of
\mathfrak{G}
.

Recall the following folklore result.

Lemma 4
.

Any perfect ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is characteristic.

Proof.

For any derivation
\partial

of
\mathfrak{G}
, we have

\partial\,[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]=[\partial\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\subset[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{m},

which proves the claim.
∎

We will also use the following technical lemma:

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}

and let
\mathfrak{z}
be the center of
\mathfrak{a}
. Assume that

\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{z})=0
.

(a)

If
\mathfrak{z}=0
, then as Lie algebras

\mathfrak{G}=C_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{a})\oplus\mathfrak{a}\simeq\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{a}.

(b)

If
\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathfrak{a}
are perfect, then as Lie algebras

\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}\simeq\widehat{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}.

Proof.

We first prove Assertion (a). Consider the natural Lie algebra homorphism

\pi:\mathfrak{G}\to\operatorname{Der}\mathfrak{a},z\mapsto\operatorname{ad}(x)\mid_{\mathfrak{a}}.

By hypothesis we have
\operatorname{Der}\mathfrak{a}\simeq\mathfrak{a}

and therefore
\mathfrak{G}/\operatorname{Ker}\pi\simeq\mathfrak{a}
. Since

\operatorname{Ker}\pi=C_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{a})
, we deduce

\mathfrak{G}=C_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{a})\oplus\mathfrak{a}\simeq\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{a},

which proves the claim.

We now prove Assertion (b). Let
\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}
be the space of all
elements
z\in\mathfrak{a}
whose image in
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{z}
is central. Thus for

z\in\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}
, we have

\operatorname{ad}(x)\operatorname{ad}(y)(z)=0,\,\,\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{a}.

Since
\mathfrak{a}
is perfect we have

[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}]=[[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}],\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}]\subset[\mathfrak{a},[\mathfrak{a}],\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}]]=0,

therefore
\mathfrak{z}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{z}
, which means that
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{z}
is centerless. We have just prove that

\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{z}\simeq\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{z}
, therefore we have

\widehat{\mathfrak{G}}\simeq\widehat{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{a}},

which completes the proof.
∎

1.5.
Structure theory of finite dimensional Lie algebra

The following classical result is attributed to E. Cartan, cf.

[
12
]
§5.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional Lie algebra.

(a)

The radical
\operatorname{rad}\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the maximal solvable ideal.

(b)

Set
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{g}/\operatorname{rad}\mathfrak{g}
. Then

\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\operatorname{rad}\mathfrak{g}
.

(c)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is perfect, then
\operatorname{rad}\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent.

(d)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is solvable, then
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is nilpotent.

(e)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, then
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})=0
.

2.
Examples of non-Noetherian Lie algebras

We provide examples of non-Noetherian Lie algebras which
underline the specificity of Noetherian Lie algebras.
In what follows,
\mathfrak{G}
denotes a Noetherian Lie algebra
and
\mathfrak{H}
will designate a non-Noetherian Lie algebra.
For simplicity, we will always assume that
\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathfrak{H}

\mathrm{F}_{i}=[\mathrm{F}_{i+1},\mathrm{F}_{i+1}]

\mathfrak{H}

In conclusion, we ask

Does a perfect weakly Noetherian Lie algebra

3.
Various Noetherianity properties for Lie algebras

3.1.
Definition of Noetherianity for Lie algebras

We define three notions of Noetherianity for Lie algebras:

(1)

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
is called
weakly Noetherian
if

\dim\,\mathfrak{m}/[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]<\infty

for all subalgebras
\mathfrak{m}
in
\mathfrak{G}
.

(2)

A weakly Noetherian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
is called

Noetherian
if satisfies the ascending chain condition (ACC in brief) on
the characteristic ideals.

(3)

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
is called
strongly Noetherian
if all its subalgebras are finitely generated.

We observe that the notion of Noetherianity for
the associative algebras is preserved by finite
extensions, as shown by the following
well-known statement.

Lemma 7
.

If an associative algebra
A

is left Noetherian, then
A
is strictly left Noetherian.

Proof.

Let
L\supset K
be a finite extension. Obviously,

A\otimes L
is
a left free
A
-module of finite rank.
Thus
A\otimes L
satisfies the ACC condition on left
A
-submodules. We deduce that
A\otimes L
satisfies the ACC on left
A\otimes L
-modules, that is on the left ideals.
Therefore

A\otimes L
is left Noetherian.
∎

By contrast, the notions of Noetherianity for the Lie algebras are not obviously preserved by finite extensions. For clarification, let state the following obvious Corollary.

Corollary 8
.

The various notions of Noetherianity are logically connected by the following scheme:

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{g})\text{\,\,strictly Noetherian}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\hskip 48.36967pt

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{g})\text{\,\, Noetherian}

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt\Downarrow

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt\Downarrow

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\text{\,\,strictly strongly Noetherian}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\hskip 48.36967pt

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\text{\,\, strongly Noetherian}

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt\Downarrow

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt\Downarrow

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\text{\,\,strictly Noetherian}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\hskip 48.36967pt

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\text{\,\, Noetherian}

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt\Downarrow

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt\Downarrow

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\text{\,\,strictly weakly Noetherian}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\hskip 48.36967pt

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\text{\,\, weakly Noetherian}

3.2.
Elementary properties of weakly Noetherian Lie algebras

We have collected the elementary properties
of weakly Noetherian Lie algebras in the
following Lemma.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra.

(a)

Any section

\mathfrak{s}
of
\mathfrak{G}
is also weakly Noetherian.

(b)

Any abelian section of
\mathfrak{G}
is finite dimensional.

(c)

\mathfrak{G}
does not contain nonabelian free subalgebras.

(d)

If
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
is commensurable with
\mathfrak{G}
, then

\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
is also weakly Noetherian.

(e)

The
k
-th term
\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}
of the derived series has finite codimension
in
\mathfrak{g}
for any
k\geq 1
.
In particular, if
\mathfrak{G}
is solvable, then
then
\dim\mathfrak{G}<\infty
.

(f)

We have
\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}=\mathscr{C}^{\omega}[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
.

(g)

If
\mathfrak{G}
is perfect and
\mathfrak{m}
is an ideal of finite codimension,
then
\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{m}=\mathscr{C}^{\omega}\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

Assertions (a) and (b) are obvious.

We prove Assertion (c) by contradiction.
Otherwise,
\mathfrak{G}
contains a free Lie algebra
over two generators
x
and
y
. The
subalgebra
F
generated by the set

S:=\{\operatorname{ad}(x)^{n}(y)\mid n\geq 0\}
is
the free Lie algebra over
S
. Therefore

F/[F,F]
is an infinite dimensional
abelian section,
which contradicts weak Noetherianity.

Assertion (d) amounts to prove that if

\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
contains
\mathfrak{G}
as a subalgebra
of finite codimension, then

\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
is also weakly Noetherian.
Let
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
be an arbitrary subalgebra
of
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
.
Set
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{G}
. In view of the short exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{a}/[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]\to\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}/[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]\to\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{a}\to 0,

we deduce that

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}/[\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle\leq\dim\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}/[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]

\displaystyle=\dim\mathfrak{a}/[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]+\dim\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\mathfrak{a}/[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]+\dim\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{G}<\infty,

which proves that
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
is weakly Noetherian.

Assertion (e) follows from the fact that

\mathfrak{G}/\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}
has a composition series consisting of
k
abelian sections, thus

\mathfrak{G}/\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}
has finite dimension.

We now prove Assertion (f).
Let
k
be any integer. It has been proved that
the solvable Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}/\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}
is
finite dimensional. By Theorem
6
(d),

[\mathfrak{G}/\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}/\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}]
is nilpotent.
Therefore

\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{G}\supset\mathcal{C}^{\omega}[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}],

thus

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}\supset\mathcal{C}^{\omega}[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
.
Since obviously

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}=\mathscr{D}^{\omega}[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]\subset\mathscr{C}^{\omega}[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}],

we finally deduce that

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}=\mathscr{C}^{\omega}[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}].

Finally, we prove Assertion (g).
Set
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{G}/\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{m}
. By the previous
considerations,
\mathfrak{g}
is
of finite dimensional. Since
\mathfrak{m}/\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{m}
is a solvable ideal of the perfect Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, Theorem
6
(a)(c) asserts that

\mathfrak{m}/\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{m}
is nilpotent. Therefore

\mathscr{D}^{k}\mathfrak{m}\supset\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\mathfrak{m},

for all integers
k
. It follows that

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{m}\supset\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\mathfrak{m}
and therefore

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{m}=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\mathfrak{m},

as claimed.
∎

3.3.
Finite closure of ideals

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a Lie algebra, and let
\mathfrak{m}
be an ideal. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the set of all
ideals
\mathfrak{r}\supset\mathfrak{m}
such that

\dim\mathfrak{r}/\mathfrak{m}<\infty
.
We define the
finite closure

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})
of
\mathfrak{m}

in
\mathfrak{G}
as the ideal

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})=\sum_{\mathfrak{r}\in\mathcal{F}}\,\mathfrak{r}.

For an ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of finite codimension, we have
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})=\mathfrak{G}
,
therefore this notion
is only interesting for ideals
of infinite codimension.
An ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is called

closed
if
\mathfrak{m}=\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})
.
In general,
\mathcal{F}
has no maximal
element, thus
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathfrak{m}
could be infinite dimensional.

Lemma 10
.

Any chain of finite dimensional ideals
in a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
stabilizes.

Moreover, any ideal
\mathfrak{m}
satisfies

(a)

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathfrak{m}
is finite
dimensional, and

(b)

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})
is a characteristic ideal.

Proof.

We first prove the first assertion
by contradiction. Assume otherwise, thus there
is an increasing chain

0=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{2}\subset\ldots

of finite dimensional ideals. We can
select a subchain in a way that

\dim\mathfrak{m}_{k}>\dim\mathfrak{m}_{k-1}^{2}.

Since
C_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}(\mathfrak{m}_{k-1})
is the kernel of the Lie algebra morphism

\operatorname{ad}:\mathfrak{m}_{k}\to\mathfrak{g}l(\mathfrak{m}_{k-1}),x\mapsto\operatorname{ad}(x)\mid_{\mathfrak{m}_{k-1}},

dimension considerations show that there is an element

x_{k}\in C_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}(\mathfrak{m}_{k-1})
which
belongs to
\mathfrak{m}_{k}\setminus\mathfrak{m}_{k-1}
.
By construction, the elements
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots

are linearly independent and they pairwise commute, which contradicts that
\mathfrak{G}
is weakly Noetherian.

We now prove Assertion (a). Since any chain
of finite dimension ideals in
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}
stabilizes,
we conclude
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}}(0)
is finite dimensional.
Since

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathfrak{m}\simeq\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}}(0),

we conclude that
\dim\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathfrak{m}<\infty.

In order to prove Assertion (b), we can assume that

\mathfrak{m}
is closed. Let

\partial
be a derivation of
\mathfrak{g}

and set
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}+\partial\mathfrak{m}
.
We have

\partial[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\subset[\partial\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\subset[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{m}.

It follows that

\dim\,\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{m}\leq\dim\mathfrak{m}/[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]<\infty.

Since
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
is an ideal and
\mathfrak{m}
is closed, we have

\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}
which amounts to

\partial\,\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{m}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}
is stable by any derivation
\partial
, we conclude that

\mathfrak{m}
is characteristic.
∎

Corollary 11
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a Noetherian Lie algebra and let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal.

(a)

\mathfrak{a}
satisfies the ACC condition on chains of ideals,

(b)

In particular, any quotient of
\mathfrak{a}
is a Noetherian Lie algebra.

Proof.

Let

\mathfrak{m}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{2}\subset\ldots

be a chain of ideals of
\mathfrak{a}
. By Assertion (b) of Lemma
10
,

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{m}_{k})
is an
ideal of
\mathfrak{G}
, for any integer
k
.
By Assertion (b) of Lemma
10

the chain

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{m}_{1}))\subset\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{m}_{2}))\subset\ldots.

consist of characteristic ideals of
\mathfrak{G}
. Hence the chain stabilizes,
so there exist an integer
N
such that

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{m}_{k}))=\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{m}_{N})),

for any
k\geq N
. Hence the chain

\mathfrak{m}_{k}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{k+1}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{k+2}\ldots

is nested between
\mathfrak{m}_{k}
and

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{m}_{k}))
. By Assertion (a)
of Lemma
10
,
\mathfrak{m}_{k}
has finite codimension in

\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{m}_{k}))
. We deduce that the
chain
\mathfrak{m}_{k}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{k+1}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{k+2}\ldots
stabilizes, which proves that
\mathfrak{a}
satisfies the ACC condition on ideals,
that is Assertion (a).

It follows that any quotient of
\mathfrak{a}
is Noetherian.
∎

Corollary 12
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a Noetherian Lie algebra.

If
\mathfrak{G}

\mathfrak{G}

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a nonzero ideal

Throughout the section, we will use the following notation. Given a graded Lie algebra

4.1.

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
endowed with a filtration

0=\mathfrak{g}(-1)\subset\mathfrak{g}(0)\subset\mathfrak{g}(1)\subset\cdots

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{g}(n),\mathfrak{g}(m)]

\displaystyle\subset\mathfrak{g}(n+m)

\displaystyle\cup_{n}\mathfrak{g}(n)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{g}

The associated graded Lie algebra is

\mathfrak{G}=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\,\,\mathfrak{G}_{n}

\mathfrak{G}_{n}=\mathfrak{g}(n)/\mathfrak{g}(n-1)

Here
we do not assume that the homogenous component
\mathfrak{G}_{0}=\mathfrak{g}(0)

However, the hypotheses of the lemma will
imply that, for

\mathfrak{G}_{n}

Lemma 13
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a filtered Lie algebra, with associated graded algebra
\mathfrak{G}

(a1)

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}_{\geq 1}

is weakly Noetherian, and

(a2)

the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}(0)

then the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is strongly Noetherian.

Moreover, if

(b1)

\mathfrak{B}

\mathfrak{G}_{\geq 1}

\mathfrak{B}
, and

(b2)

\mathfrak{g}(0)

\mathfrak{g}

Proof.

Any subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathfrak{g}
admits a filtration

0=\mathfrak{b}(-1)\subset\mathfrak{b}(0)\subset\mathfrak{b}(1)\subset\ldots

defined by
\mathfrak{b}(n)=\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{g}(n)
for all

n\geq 1
. Let
\mathfrak{B}
be the associated graded

We now prove the first assertion, namely
that
\mathfrak{b}
is finitely generated.
Since
\mathfrak{B}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{G}
, Hypothesis (a1)
implies that

\dim\mathfrak{B}_{\geq 1}/[\mathfrak{B}_{\geq 1},\mathfrak{B}_{\geq 1}]<\infty.

Since
\mathfrak{B}_{\geq 1}
is positively graded, we deduce that
\mathfrak{B}_{\geq 1}
is generated by a finite set

y_{1},\dots,y_{m}
of homogenous elements of degree respectively

d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}
. Hence, there are elements
x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}\in\mathfrak{b}
such that

x_{i}\in\mathfrak{b}(d_{i})

y_{i}=x_{i}\mod\mathfrak{b}(d_{i}-1)

for all

Let
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
be the subalgebra generated by

. It is clear that, as a vector space we have

\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{b}(0).

Both subalgebras
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{b}(0)

\mathfrak{b}
is finitely generated, which proves the first assertion.

We now turn to the second assertion, that is that
\dim\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{m}<\infty
for any nonzero ideal

\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathfrak{b}
. Let

0=\mathfrak{m}(-1)\subset\mathfrak{m}(0)\subset\mathfrak{m}(1)\subset\ldots

\mathfrak{m}

\mathfrak{M}
be the associated graded Lie algebra.

It is obvious that
\mathfrak{m}(0)
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{b}(0)
and it is clear that
\mathfrak{M}_{\geq 1}
is an ideal
of
\mathfrak{B}_{\geq 1}
. Therefore

\mathfrak{b}(0)/\mathfrak{m}(0)
and
\mathfrak{B}_{\geq 1}/\mathfrak{M}_{\geq 1}
are finite dimensional by hypotheses. We deduce that
\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{m}
is finite dimensional, which proves the second assertion.

(b)

Theorem 15
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine curve.
The Krichever-Novikov algebra
\mathrm{Vect}_{X}
is strongly Noetherian.

Moreover any Lie subalgebra is just infinite.

Proof.

The proof of both assertions runs by induction on the number of point at infinity of

Since for any field extension

\mathrm{Vect}_{X}\otimes L{\rm\,\,Noetherian}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{X}{\rm\,\,Noetherian},

we can assume that all these points are rational.

If

is a projective curve of genus

\mathrm{Vect}_{X}
is finite dimensional. Indeed
it is three-dimensional if

and
\mathrm{Vect}_{X}=0

Since
X
is affine, it admits
a point

at infinity. Set

and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Vect}_{X}
. For

\mathfrak{g}(n)
the space of vector fields with a pole of order at most

\mathfrak{G}
be the graded space associated with the filtration

\mathfrak{g}(0)\subset\mathfrak{g}(1)\subset\cdots.

Let

\xi,\,\eta

and
m-1

\displaystyle\xi=

\displaystyle t^{1-n}\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t+o(t^{1-n})

\displaystyle\eta=

\displaystyle t^{1-m}\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t+o(t^{1-m}).

Hence we have

\displaystyle[\xi,\eta]=(n-m)t^{1-m-n}\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t+o(t^{1-m-n})

shows.

By definition,

The proof that any subalgebra of

5.
Centroids of weakly Noetherian Lie algebras

We now prove that the centroids of weakly
Noetherian Lie algebras are finite dimensional, see Proposition
20
.
This result will be used in the next section.

Recall that the
centroid
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, denoted as
\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})
, is the algebra

\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G}):=\{\theta\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{G})\mid\theta([x,y])=[x,\theta(y)]\,\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{G}\}.

For simplicity, we will simply denote as
\theta\,x
the action of an operator

\theta\in\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})
on an element
x\in\mathfrak{G}
.

Lemma 17
.

For any

\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})
, we have

\theta\theta^{\prime}\,z=\theta^{\prime}\theta\,z\,\,\,\forall z\in[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
.

Proof.

By skew symmetry of the bracket,
we have

\theta\,[x,y]=[\theta\,x,y]=[x,\theta\,y],

for any
\theta\in\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{g})
and

x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. It follows that

\theta^{\prime}\theta\,[x,y]=[x,\theta^{\prime}\theta\,y]=[\theta\theta^{\prime}\,x,y]=\theta\theta^{\prime}\,[x,y],

which proves that

\theta\theta^{\prime}\,z=\theta^{\prime}\theta\,z\,\forall z\in[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
.
∎

For a subset
S\subset\mathfrak{G}
, the left ideal

\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}(S):=\{a\in\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})\mid aS=0\}

is called the
annihilator
in
\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})
of S
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra. The annihilator

\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}([\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}])

is a two-sided ideal of
\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})

of finite dimension.

Proof.

The left ideal
\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}([\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}])
is also
a right ideal by Lemma
17
.

Let
\mathfrak{z}
be the center of

\mathfrak{G}
.
For
a\in\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}([\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}])
, we have

[a\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]=a[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]=0,

hence
a\mathfrak{G}
lies in
\mathfrak{z}
. It follows easily that

\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}([\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}])=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{G}/[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}],\mathfrak{z}).

Since
\mathfrak{G}
is
weakly Noetherian, its abelian sections
\mathfrak{G}/[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
and
\mathfrak{z}

are finite dimensional, thus the ideal

\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}([\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}])
is finite dimensional, which completes the proof.
∎

We now state our last preliminary result.

Lemma 19
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra, and let
I\subset\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})
be a linear subspace.

If
I^{2}=0
, then
I
is finite dimensional.

Proof.

Set
\mathfrak{a}=I\mathfrak{G}
.We have

[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]=[I\mathfrak{G},I\mathfrak{G}]=I^{2}[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]=0,

hence the commutative ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is finite dimensional. Therefore its commutant

\mathfrak{m}:=C_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{a}),

has finite codimension. Since

\mathfrak{m}/[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{m}]
is an abelian section,
the ideal
[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{m}]
also have finite codimension.

We have

I[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{m}]=[I\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{m}]=[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m}]=0

Hence there is a natural embedding

I\subset\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{G}/[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{m}],\mathfrak{a})
, which proves that
I
is finite dimensional.
∎

Proposition 20
.

The centroid

\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})
of a
weakly Noetherian Lie algebra
is finite dimensional.

Proof.

Assume otherwise, that is

\dim\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})=\infty
.
By lemma
18
, the algebra

R:=\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})/\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}([\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]).

is infinite dimensional.

We now find,
by induction, an infinite
sequence
z_{1},z_{2}\cdots
of elements
in
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
such that the
ideals

I_{n}:=\cap_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}(z_{i})
satisfy

I_{n}^{2}z_{n+1}\neq 0.

To start the induction, we observe that
R\neq 0
, hence there exists a nonzero element
z_{1}\in[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
. Since
I_{0}=R
, we have
I_{0}^{2}z_{1}\neq 0
.

Next, assume now by induction that we have already found the elements
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}
.
Since for
z\in[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
, the space
Rz
is an abelian Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{G}
, the
ideal
\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}(z)
has finite codimension. Therefore

I_{n}:=\cap_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})}(z_{i})

has infinite dimension. By
Lemma
19
we have
I^{2}_{n}\neq 0
, and therefore
we can find
z_{n+1}\in[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]
such that

I^{2}_{n}\,z_{n+1}\neq 0
. Thus we have established the existence of the sequence
z_{1},z_{2}\cdots
.

By Lemma
17
, the algebra
R
is commutative, so
I^{2}_{n}
is the linear span
of the set
\{a^{2}\mid a\in I_{n}\}
. Therefore there exists elements

a_{1}\in I_{0},a_{2}\in I_{1},\ldots
such that

a_{n}^{2}z_{n}\neq 0,\forall n\geq 1.

Set
y_{n}=a_{n}z_{n}
. To conclude the proof, it is enough to show that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
generated by the
set
\{y_{n}\mid n\geq 1\}
is an infinite dimension abelian Lie algebra.

Since

R
is commutative, we observe that
for
n<m
, we have
a_{m}a_{n}z_{n}=a_{n}a_{m}z_{n}=0
.
Therefore

[y_{n},y_{m}]=[a_{n}x_{n},a_{m}x_{m}]=[a_{m}a_{n}x_{n},x_{m}]=0,

which proves that
\mathfrak{a}
is abelian. It remains to prove that the elements

y_{n}
are linearly independant. Let

Y:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,c_{i}y_{i}

be any linear combination, where

c_{i}
are scalars and
c_{n}\neq 0
.
As before, we have

a_{n}y_{i}=a_{n}a_{i}x_{i}=a_{i}a_{n}x_{i}=0

for
i<n
.
Thus
a_{n}.Y=c_{n}a_{n}.y_{n}=c_{n}a_{n}^{2}.z_{n}\neq 0
,
which shows that the set
\{y_{i}\mid i\geq 1\}
is linearly independent.
∎

Corollary 21
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}
be a weakly Noetherian simple
Lie algebra. Then

(a)

Its centroid
L
is a finite field extension of
K
, hence
L\subset\overline{K}
, and

(b)

\mathfrak{S}\otimes_{L}\overline{K}
is a simple
Lie algebra.

Proof.

The centroid
L
of
\mathfrak{S}
is obviously a division algebra. Since
\mathfrak{S}=[\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{S}]
, the algebra
L
is commutative by Lemma
17
.
By Proposition
20
,
L
is a finite extension of
K
, which proves Assertion (a).

Assertion (b) follows from Jacobson density Theorem
[
22
]
applied to the simple
U(\mathfrak{S})
-module
\mathfrak{S}
.

∎

stated in the introduction, namely

6.1.
Generalities about simple quotients

We recall two elementary well-known lemmas.

Lemma 22
.

Let
R
be a unital associative
algebra, and let
M,M_{1},\cdots,M_{n}
be a finite
collection of distinct maximal two-sided ideals.
Then the natural map

M_{1}\cap M_{2}\cap\cdots\cap M_{n}\to R/M

is surjective.

Proof.

Obviously we have

\displaystyle M+\big(M_{1}\cap\cdots\cap M_{n}\big)

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle M+M_{1}M_{2}\cdots M_{n}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle(M+M_{1})(M+M_{2})\cdots(M+M_{n})

However, by maximality assumption,
we have
M+M_{i}=R
, for all positive integer
i\leq n
.
Therefore
(M+M_{1})(M+M_{2})\cdots(M+M_{n})=R
.
It follows that

M+\big(M_{1}\cap\cdots\cap M_{n}\big)=R,

which amounts to the surjectivity of

M_{1}\cap M_{2}\cap\cdots\cap M_{n}\to R/M.

∎

For an integer

and a field

,
let
\mathrm{Mat}_{d}(L)
be the algebra of

matrices over

.

Lemma 23
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra
of finite dimension

over its centroid

.

Then, the image of

in
\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to

Proof.

The assertion is a consequence of Wedderburn’s Theorem applied to the
simple finite dimensional

-module

.

We will now state an important result of Formanek and Razmyslov. Let

Theorem 24
.

[

]

6.3.
Finite dimensional simple quotients

We now prove the main result of the section.

Theorem 25
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra and let
d>1
.

Then
\mathfrak{G}
contains only finitely many maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}
is a simple Lie algebra of dimension
d
over its centroid
L=\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m})
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise. We will show that it contradicts Proposition
20

Thus there is
an infinite sequence

\mathfrak{m}_{1},\mathfrak{m}_{2}\ldots

of distinct ideals such that

\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is a simple Lie algebra of dimension
d
over its centroid

L_{i}:=\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}_{i})
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that

\cap_{i=1}^{\infty}\,\mathfrak{m}_{i}=0
, therefore there is an embedding

\mathfrak{G}\subset\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\,\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}.

Let
P_{d}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})
be a central identity, which does exist by Theorem
24
.
For any
m
-uple

{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in U(\mathfrak{G})^{m}
,
the element
\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}))
lies in

\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{G})
. Since

\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\,L_{i},

\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}))
is represented as an infinite sequence of scalars

(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
, where each
x_{i}
belongs to
L_{i}
.

Set
I_{n}=\cap_{i=1}^{n}\,\mathrm{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak{G})}(\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}_{i})
. By Lemmas
22
and
23
, there is

{\bf a}^{(n)}\in I_{n-1}
such that

\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}^{(n)}))
acts on

\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{m}_{n}
as the identity. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}^{(1)}))

\displaystyle=(1,x_{2},\ldots)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}^{(2)}))

\displaystyle=(0,1,y_{3},\ldots)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}^{(3)}))

\displaystyle=(0,0,1,z_{4},\ldots)

\displaystyle\ldots

where
x_{2},\ldots,y_{3},\ldots,z_{4},\ldots
are some undetermined scalars.
Since

\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}^{(1)})),\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}^{(2)})),\mathrm{Ad}(P_{d}({\bf a}^{(3)})),\ldots

are linearly independent, we conclude that
\mathrm{Cent}(U\mathfrak{g})
is
infinite dimensional, which contradicts Proposition

20
.
∎

6.4.
Lie Polynomial Identities

A
Lie polynomial

P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
is an element
of the free Lie
K
-algebra over the Lie
variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
. A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}

satisfies the polynomial identity
P

if

P(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})=0,\,\forall a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in\mathfrak{G},

see the monograph
[
6
]
. The following Lemma is well-known.

Lemma 26
.

Let
P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
be a Lie
polynomial, and

(\mathfrak{s}_{1},\mathfrak{s}_{2},\ldots)
be a countable family of finite dimensional simple Lie algebras such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\dim_{L_{i}}\,\mathfrak{s}_{i}=\infty,

where
L_{i}:=\mathrm{Cent}(\mathfrak{s}_{i})
.

Then
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
does not satisfies
P
for
i\gg 0
.

Proof.

Indeed it is an easy consequence of
the celebrated paper of Amitsur and Levitzki

[
4
]
. For the sake of the reader, a quick proof is given below.

First we prove that

\mathfrak{g}l_{N_{0}}
does not satisfies

P
for a certain integer
N_{0}>0
.
Let
F_{m}
be the free Lie algebra over

x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}
. Since
F_{m}
is residually finite dimensional, there is a finite codimensional ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset F_{m}
such that

P\notin\mathfrak{m}
. By Ado’s theorem
[
12
]
, there is an embedding

F_{m}/\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{g}l_{N_{0}}
for some integer
N_{0}
.
Thus
\mathfrak{g}l_{N_{0}}
does not satisfies
P
.

Next we observe that any polynomial identity
of
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
is also a polynomial identity for
the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}_{i}\otimes_{L_{i}}\overline{K}
. So we can add the simplifying hypothesis that
K
is algebraically closed.
When
\dim\mathfrak{s}_{i}>248
, the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{s}_{i}
is isomorphic to

\mathfrak{sl}(n+1),\mathfrak{s}o(2n+1),\mathfrak{s}p(2n)
or
\mathfrak{s}o(2n)

for some
n
. Each of these Lie algebras contains
\mathfrak{g}l_{N_{0}}
whenever
n\geq N_{0}
, therefore
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
does not satisfies
P
for
i\gg 0
.
∎

Corollary 27
.

Any residually semisimple weakly Noetherian
Lie algebra

does not satisfy any polynomial identity, except if

is finite dimensional.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathfrak{G}
is infinite dimensional.
By theorem

,

\mathfrak{G}
admits countably many simple quotients
of finite dimension, say
\mathfrak{s}_{1},\mathfrak{s}_{2},\ldots
.
Moreover

where

. Thus by

,

does not satisfy any polynomial identity.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a weakly Noetherian Lie algebra.
In the present section we prove Theorem

, namely
that any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of

>1
is characteristic, which is
a crucial step in the proof of Theorem A.
When
\mathfrak{m}
has infinite codimension, the result is a simple consequence of Lemma

(b).

The case when
\mathfrak{m}
has finite codimension is the
difficult part of the proof. It combines
the previous Theorem

with some considerations about affine Lie algebras, defined below. The proof slighty differs from
the explanations of the Introduction. However, in Remark

, we briefly explains why the simplified version of Theorem

7.1.
Affine Lie algebras

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra. A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
is called
affine
if

(a)

\mathfrak{G}
contains
\mathfrak{s}
, and

(b)

viewed as an
\mathfrak{s}
-module,
\mathfrak{G}
is a direct sum of adjoint modules.

This kind of Lie algebras first appeared in
Tits work
[
33
]
[
34
]
.
Over an algebraically closed field, or, more generally
when
\mathfrak{s}
is a split simple Lie algebra,
affine Lie algebras are special
case of the Lie algebras graded by a root system
(LAGRS), which have been extensively investigated.
We can mention the early works of Koecher

[
25
]
, Kantor
[
23
]
for rank one root system, and, in the general case, there are a lot of papers, including those of Berman and Moody
[
10
]
or Benckart and Zelmanov
[
9
]
. The main difference with the
axioms of
[
33
]
[
34
]
is the fact that affine Lie algebras do not contain trivial representations of
\mathfrak{s}
. Also, at the difference of other precited works, we do not assume that
\mathfrak{s}
is split, so there are no root decomposition in our setting.

Henceforth, it will be more easy to give direct proofs than extracting results from previous works.

The first step consists in the computation
of the space

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s})

of all equivariant products on

\mathfrak{s}
. The Lie bracket

[,]
is a skew symmetric equivariant product.
When
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
, there is a also a
symmetric product
\cdot
defined by

x\cdot y:=1/2(xy+yx)-1/n{\mathrm{Tr}}(xy)1_{n}

for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.

Here
xy
is the usual product of
n\times n
matrices,
1_{n}
denotes the identity of

\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(K)
, and the additional term
1/n{\mathrm{Tr}}(xy)1_{n}
insures that

x\cdot y
is in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
. For
n\geq 3
, the product
\cdot
is not zero.

The starting point is the following
well-known observation:

Lemma 28
.

Assume that
K=\overline{K}
is algebraically closed.

If
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
with
n\geq 3
, then

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s})
has dimension two, with basis
the Lie bracket
[,]
and the symmetric product
\cdot
.

Otherwise
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s})
is the one-dimensional space generated by the Lie bracket
[,]
.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i},f_{i},h_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be the standard Chevalley
generators of
\mathfrak{s}
, let

\mathfrak{h}=\oplus_{i\in I}\,K.h_{i}
be the Cartan subalgebra and let

\mathfrak{b}
be Borel subalgebra generated by

\mathfrak{h}
and the elements
e_{i}
. Let
\theta
be the highest root
and let
e_{\theta}
be the corresponding root element.

Let
\pi:\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}
be any nonzero equivariant product. Since the
\mathfrak{s}
-module
\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s}
is generated by
Ke_{\theta}\otimes\mathfrak{s}
, we have

\pi(Ke_{\theta}\otimes\mathfrak{s})\neq 0
.

Since

Ke_{\theta}
is the unique simple
\mathfrak{b}
-submodule of
\mathfrak{g}
,
the
\mathfrak{b}
-module
\pi(Ke_{\theta}\otimes\mathfrak{s})
intersects
Ke_{\theta}
. By weight considerations, we conclude that

\pi(Ke_{\theta}\otimes\mathfrak{h})\neq 0
.

Set
J:=\{i\in I\mid\exists\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s})\,\phi(e_{\theta}\otimes h_{i})\neq 0\,\}
.
Since
\pi(Ke_{\theta}\otimes\mathfrak{h})
lies in the one-dimensional space
Ke_{\theta}
, we deduce that

\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{s}}(\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s})\leq\mathrm{Card\,}\,J
.

For
i\in I
with
\theta(h_{i})=0
, we have
[e_{\theta},f_{i}]=0
. Obviously

\pi(e_{\theta}\otimes e_{i})=0
, hence

0=[\pi(e_{\theta}\otimes e_{i}),f_{i}])=\pi(e_{\theta}\otimes h_{i})
.

It follows that

J\subset\{i\in I\mid\theta(h_{i})\neq 0\}
.

If
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
with
n\geq 3
, the set

\{i\in I\mid\theta(h_{i})\neq 0\}
is a doubleton,
which proves the claim in that case. Otherwise,
the set

\{i\in I\mid\theta(h_{i})\neq 0\}
is a singleton,
which concludes the proof.
∎

We will now extend the result over
nonalgebraically closed fields. Let
L
be the centroid of the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
. By Lemma
17
, the division algebra
L
is a field.

We can multiply any

Let

Lemma 32
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a simple Lie algebra
with centroid
L
, and let

\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{s}\otimes_{L}A
be an affine Lie algebra.

Any derivation
\partial
of
\mathfrak{G}
is of the form

x(a)\mapsto[X,x(a)]+x(\delta a),

where
X
belongs to
\mathfrak{G}
and

\delta
is a derivation of
A
.

Proof.

The map
\partial|_{\mathfrak{s}}

x\in\mathfrak{s}\mapsto\partial x\in\mathfrak{G}

is a derivation of
\mathfrak{s}
. By Cartan’s Theorem
6
(e), we have

H^{1}(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{G})=H^{1}(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s})\otimes_{L}A=\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{s})\otimes_{L}A=0.

Hence, there is

such that

\partial x=[X,x],\,\forall x\in\mathfrak{s}.

Set
\partial^{\prime}=\partial-\operatorname{ad}(X)
.
Since
\partial^{\prime}\mathfrak{s}=0
, the linear map

\partial^{\prime}
is
\mathfrak{s}
-equivariant. Thus

\partial^{\prime}
is
L
-linear and

\partial^{\prime}x(a)=x(\delta a)

for some

L
-linear map
\delta:A\to A
.

We now show that
\delta
is a derivation of

A
. Indeed
we will only consider the case where

\overline{K}\otimes_{L}\mathfrak{s}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
for some

\circ
of

, where
\circ_{1}
is commutative and
\circ_{2}
is skew-symmetric. We will show that

\circ_{1}
and
\circ_{2}

Let

[x(a),y(b)]+[x(b),y(a)]=2[x,y](a\circ_{1}b),

we easily deduce that

Similarly, consider an element

∎

For a given derivation

.

7.3.
Maximal ideals of finite codimension

In this subsection, we finally prove that, given a weakly
Noetherian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
,
any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
with

We start with some preparatory results.

Lemma 36
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a simple finite dimensional Lie algebra with centroid
L
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{s}
be a Lie subalgebra which contains a
L
-basis of
\mathfrak{s}
.

Then

(a)

\mathfrak{a}
is a simple Lie algebra,

(b)

its centroid

satisfies

, and

(c)

\mathfrak{a}\otimes_{L^{\prime}}L=\mathfrak{s}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{r}
be a minimal ideal
of
\mathfrak{a}
, and let
L.\mathfrak{r}
be the
L
-vector space generated by
\mathfrak{r}
. Since
\mathfrak{a}
contains an
L
-basis of
\mathfrak{s}
, the space
L.\mathfrak{r}
is an

\mathfrak{s}
, thus
L.\mathfrak{r}=\mathfrak{s}
.

We claim that
\mathfrak{r}
is a simple Lie algebra.
Since
L.\mathfrak{r}=\mathfrak{s}
, the ideal
\mathfrak{r}
cannot be abelian.
By minimality of
\mathfrak{r}
, we conclude that
\mathfrak{r}
is simple.

By Cartan Theorem
6
(e), we have
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{r})=0
.
Therefore by Lemma
5
, the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{a}
is a direct sum

\mathfrak{a}\simeq C_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{r})\oplus\mathfrak{r}.

Since
\mathfrak{r}
contains a basis of
\mathfrak{s}
, its centralizer

is trivial, from which we deduce that

\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{r}
. Therefore we have proved that
\mathfrak{a}
is simple.

It is clear that

L^{\prime}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a})\subset\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{s})=L,

therefore
L^{\prime}
is a subfield of

. Moreover,
by Corollary

\mathfrak{a}\otimes_{L^{\prime}}L
is a simple Lie algebra, therefore the homomorphism

\mathfrak{a}\otimes_{L^{\prime}}L\to\mathfrak{s}
is an isomophism, which completes the proof.
∎

Given a simple finite dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
, we view

\mathfrak{s}\otimes K[[t]]=\varprojlim\,\mathfrak{s}\otimes K[t]/(t^{n})

Lemma 37

Any dense Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{G}\subset\mathfrak{s}\otimes K[[t]]

In fact a Lie subalgebra

Corollary 38

No dense subalgebra

Let
\mathfrak{G}

.
By Lemma

First, we claim that there is an open dense subset

By definition, we have

9.1.
Reduction of Sierra-Walton Conjecture to three cases

Lemma 44
.

Any Noetherian Lie algebra

\mathfrak{G}

\mathfrak{m}

Proof.

By Corollary

\mathfrak{m}
in the set of ideals
of infinite codimension.

By Lemma

\mathfrak{m}
is closed. By Lemma

\mathfrak{m}
is characteristic,

Assume now that
\mathfrak{G}
is just infinite.
If

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}=0
and

is residually

.
∎

Corollary 45
.

Any infinite dimensional Noetherian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
contains two characteristic ideals

Proof.

By Lemma

\mathfrak{G}
contains a characteristic ideal
\mathfrak{m}
such that

\mathfrak{G}
, we can assume that that
\mathfrak{m}=0

If

(b)
we can assume that

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\mathfrak{G}

\mathfrak{G}

In

(b)

The Sierra Walton conjecture

Next set

\displaystyle\{(\bf m,k)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\mid\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{(\bf m,k)}\neq 0\}

.

11.
Simple
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebras.

In this section, we classify
strictly Noetherian simple
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebras.
We deduce that, for any Noetherian

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, we
have
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}=0
.

11.1.
L
-forms of
\mathrm{Witt}
and
\mathrm{Vir}

Let
\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}
be a simple Lie algebra
over
\overline{K}
and let
L
be a finite extension of
K
. A simple Lie algebra

\mathfrak{G}_{L}
with centroid
L
is called an
L
-form of

\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}
if

\mathfrak{G}_{L}\otimes_{L}\,{\overline{K}}\simeq\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}.

In general,
L
-forms of
\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}
are classified by the nonabelian Galois cohomology

H^{1}({\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{K}/L),\mbox{\rm Aut\,}(\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}})),

see
[
30
]
.

Assume now that the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}
is a

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra. A simple

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra

\mathfrak{G}_{L}
with centroid
L
is called a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
L
-form of
\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}
if

\mathfrak{G}_{L}\otimes_{L}\,{\overline{K}}\simeq\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}\,\,\text{ as a ${\mathbb{Z}}$-graded Lie algebra}.

Obviously,
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
L
-forms are classified by

H^{1}({\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{K}/L),\mbox{\rm Aut\,}_{0}(\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}),

where

\mbox{\rm Aut\,}_{0}(\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}})
is the group of
grading-preserving automorphisms of
\mathfrak{G}_{\overline{K}}
.

Lemma 48
.

Let
L
be a finite
extension of
K
.

(a)

Any
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
L
-form of
\mathrm{Witt}(\overline{K})
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Witt}(L)
, and

(b)

Any
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
L
-form of
\mathrm{Vir}(\overline{K})
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Vir}(L)
.

Proof.

Clearly any automorphism
in
\mbox{\rm Aut\,}_{0}(\mathrm{Witt}(\overline{K}))

or in
\mbox{\rm Aut\,}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}(\overline{K}))
is of the form

t^{n}\frac{t\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mapsto a^{n}t^{n}\frac{t\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t},

for some nonzero scalar
a
.
Therefore

\mbox{\rm Aut\,}_{0}(\mathrm{Witt}(\overline{K}))=\mbox{\rm Aut\,}_{0}(\mathrm{Vir}(\overline{K}))=\overline{K}^{*}.

Since, by Hilbert 90 Theorem
[
21
]
,

H^{1}({\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{K}/L),\overline{K}^{*})=\{1\},

we deduce that

\mathrm{Witt}(\overline{K})
and

\mathrm{Vir}(\overline{K})
admits only one
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
L
-form,
namely
\mathrm{Witt}(L)
and
\mathrm{Vir}(L)
, which completes the proof.
∎

11.2.
Cartan subalgebras and splitting fields.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\oplus\mathcal{L}_{n}
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra. As usual, we assume that all
components
\mathcal{L}_{n}
are finite dimensional.
A subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathcal{L}_{0}
is called
a
Cartan subalgebra
if
\mathfrak{h}
is nilpotent
and
N_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\mathcal{L}\mid\,(\operatorname{ad}(h)-\alpha(h))^{N}(x)=0\quad\forall h\in\mathfrak{h}\text{ and }N\gg 0\}

The spaces
\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)}
are called the
generalized
\mathfrak{h}
-weightspaces
. They admit a decomposition

\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)}=\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{L}^{((\alpha,n))},

where
\mathcal{L}^{((\alpha,n))}=\mathcal{L}_{n}\cap\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)}
. We say that the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
is
split
if
\mathcal{L}
is a direct sum of generalized weightspaces,
that is:

\mathcal{L}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}\,\,\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)}.

Lemma 49
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a finitely generated

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra, and let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
.

(a)

There exists a finite extension
E
of
K
such that
\mathfrak{h}\otimes E
is a split Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}\otimes E
.

(b)

Assume moreover that
\mathcal{L}
is simple with centroid
L
.
Then

(b1)

L
is a finite extension of
K
,

(b2)

\mathfrak{h}
is a
L
-vector subspace of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, and

(b3)

there exists a finite extension
E
of
L
such that
\mathfrak{h}\otimes_{L}E
is a split Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}\otimes_{L}E
.

Proof.

We prove Assertion (a). By hypothesis, there is
a finite dimensional
\mathfrak{h}
-module
M
which generates
\mathcal{L}
. There is a finite extension

E
of
K
such that
the
\mathfrak{h}\otimes E
-module
M\otimes E
is a direct sum of generalized weightspaces. Set
\mathcal{L}_{E}=\mathcal{L}\otimes E
. Since

[\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)},\mathcal{L}^{(\beta)}]\subset\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha+\beta)}\,\,\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{h}^{*},

we concude that
\mathcal{L}_{E}
is a direct sum of generalized weight spaces, therefore
\mathfrak{h}\otimes E
is a split Cartan subalgebra.

We now prove Assertion (b1). We claim that each
homogenous component
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is a
L
-vector space.
Otherwise, we can find an element
a\in L

of degree
k\neq 0
, that is satisfying

a\mathcal{L}_{n}\subset\mathcal{L}_{n+k},\,\,\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

It follows easily that

\mathcal{L}
is a free
K[a,a^{-1}]
-module of finite rank,
which contradicts that

1+a
is invertible. Therefore each
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is a
L
-vector space, which implies that
L
is a finite extension of
K
.

For the proof of Assertion (b2), write
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
for the
L
-vector space generated by
\mathfrak{h}
. Clearly
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra. Since
N_{\tilde{h}}(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}
, we deduce that
\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{h}
, which means that
\mathfrak{h}
is a
L
-vector space.
Moreover Assertion (b3) follows from Assertion (a) applied to

\mathcal{L}
, viewed as a
Lie algebra over
L
.
∎

11.3.
Rank of simple
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebras

We now assume that
\mathcal{L}
is a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded simple Lie algebra with centroid
L
and let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathcal{L}_{0}
be a Cartan subalgebra.

For any extension
E
of
L
, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{E}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{L}\otimes_{L}E

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{E}

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{h}\otimes_{L}E

The field
E
is called a
splitting field
if

\mathfrak{h}_{E}
is a split Cartan subalgebra.
By Lemma
49
(b2), there are finite extensions
E
of
L

which are splitting fields. Given a splitting field,
the
set of roots
of

\mathcal{L}_{E}
is:

\Delta\coloneqq\{\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{E}\times{\mathbb{Z}}\mid\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\widetilde{\alpha}}\neq 0\},

where, for
\widetilde{\alpha}=(\alpha,n)\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{E}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\mathcal{L}_{E}^{(\widetilde{\alpha})}:=\mathcal{L}_{E}^{(\alpha)}\cap(\mathcal{L}_{E})_{n}.

We will see that
\mathcal{L}_{0}\neq 0
and, with our nonstandard definition,
(0,0)
is a root, called
the trivial root
.
A root
\widetilde{\alpha}=(\alpha,n)
is called

real
if
\alpha\neq 0
and
imaginary
otherwise. Let
\Delta_{\operatorname{re}}
be the set of real roots.

The
root lattice
is the subgroup

Q\subset\mathfrak{h}_{E}^{*}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
generated by
\Delta
.
As an abstract group,
Q
is independent of the choice of a splitting field. We define the rank

\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{L}
of

\mathcal{L}
as the rank of
Q
. When
\mathcal{L}
is infinite dimensional,
we have obviously
\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{L}\geq 1
. Indeed it is also true when
\mathcal{L}
is finite dimensional, see Lemma
59
.
Since it is assumed that
\mathcal{L}
is finitely generated,

\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{L}
is finite.

Remark 50
.

Assume that

\mathfrak{h}
is a split Cartan subalgebra of

\mathcal{L}
. Any element
x\in\mathcal{L}_{0}^{(\alpha)}

with
\alpha\neq 0
acts locally nilpotently on
\mathcal{L}
, thus

\exp\operatorname{ad}(x)

is a well defined automorphism in
\mbox{\rm Aut\,}_{0}(\mathcal{L})
.
The subgroup
\mathrm{Elem}(\mathcal{L})\subset\mbox{\rm Aut\,}_{0}(\mathcal{L})
generated by those automorphisms is called the group of
elementary automorphism of
\mathcal{L}
. It follows from
[
12
]
that any two split Cartan subalgebras of
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
are conjugated by an elementary automorphism.

Therefore
Q
and
\operatorname{rk}\mathcal{L}

are indeed independent of the choice of a split Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
. Since we do not need
this fact, we will not provide more details.

11.4.
Simple Lie algebras of rank one

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a finitely generated infinite dimensional

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded simple Lie algebra
of rank one, let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
. Set
L=\mathrm{Cent}(\mathcal{L})
and let
E
be a splitting field.

By definition of the rank, there is

\tilde{\alpha}=(\alpha,1)\in(\mathfrak{h}_{E}^{*},{\mathbb{Z}})
such that

\mathcal{L}_{E}=\oplus\mathcal{L}_{E}^{(n\tilde{\alpha})}.

There are two cases

(a)

\alpha=0
, that is all roots are imaginary, and

(b)

\alpha\neq 0
, that is all roots are reals, except the trivial root.

The first case is impossible:

Lemma 51
.

[
26
, Lemma 22]

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a simple
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra. If
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{K}}
is finitely generated, then
\Delta
contains
at least on real root.

We turn now our attention to the second case, that is
\alpha\neq 0
.
The following result is implicitly proved in

[
28
]
, and more detailled account is given in
[
5
, theorem 5.8]
. The appearance of a free Lie algebra in Assertion (b) is connected with the
Gabber-Kac Theorem
[
17
]
for the contragredient Lie algebra
G(^{2\,2}_{2\,2})
.

Lemma 52
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
finitely generated simple
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra of rank one.
Then

(a)

Either
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{K}}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\overline{K})
,
\mathrm{Witt}(\overline{K})
, or
\mathrm{Vir}(\overline{K})
,

(b)

or
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{K}}
contains
a nonabelian free Lie algebra.

We deduce:

Corollary 53
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a simple
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra of rank one. If
\mathcal{L}
is
strictly Noetherian
and infinite dimensional, then

\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathrm{Witt}(L)\rm{\,\,or\,\,}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathrm{Vir}(L),

for some finite extension
L
of
K
.

Proof.

By By Corollary
47
,
\mathcal{L}
is finitely generated.

If
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{K}}
contains a noncommutative free Lie algebra, it contains a free
Lie algebra over two generators
x
and
y
.
These generators belong to

\mathcal{L}_{E}
for some finite extension

E
of
K
. By Lemma
9
(c),

\mathcal{L}_{E}
is not Noetherian, which contradicts that
\mathcal{L}
is strictly Noetherian.

Therefore, by Lemma
52
,

\mathcal{L}_{\overline{K}}
is isomorphic to

\mathrm{Witt}(\overline{K})
, or
\mathrm{Vir}(\overline{K})
.
Thus by Lemma
48
, we have

\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathrm{Witt}(L)\rm{\,\,or\,\,}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathrm{Vir}(L),

where
L
is the centroid of
\mathcal{L}
.
∎

11.5.
Simple Lie
\overline{K}
-algebras of rank
\geq 2

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a simple

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra of rank
\geq 2

over
\overline{K}
and let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{G}_{0}
.
We now define two hypothetical properties, and we will see that
any such
\mathfrak{G}
satisfies one of them.
By the end of the section it will be clear that these properties are mutually exclusive.

To start with, we define the notion of a string. Let
\widetilde{\alpha}\in Q
and

\widetilde{\beta}\in\Delta
.
There are
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\pm\infty\}

with
a<0<b
such that

(i)

\widetilde{\beta}+k\widetilde{\alpha}
belongs to
\Delta
for any
k\in]a,b[
, but

(ii)

neither

\widetilde{\beta}+a\widetilde{\alpha}
nor

\widetilde{\beta}+b\widetilde{\alpha}
belongs to

\Delta
.

The set

\{\widetilde{\beta}+k\widetilde{\alpha}\mid k\in]a,b[\}
is called the

\widetilde{\alpha}
-string through
\widetilde{\beta}
. Obviously, the string is infinite if

a=-\infty
or
b=\infty
.

The first hypothetical property

(\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{re}})
is the following:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\text{There exist }\widetilde{\alpha}\in\Delta_{\operatorname{re}},\quad\widetilde{\beta}\in\Delta,\quad\widetilde{\beta}\notin{\mathbb{Q}}.\widetilde{\alpha},\text{ such that}\\
&\text{the $\widetilde{\alpha}$-string through $\widetilde{\beta}$ is infinite.}\end{aligned}

(
\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{re}}
)

The second hypothetical property is the notion of weak integrability.
Following
[
28
]
, we say that
\mathcal{\mathfrak{G}}
is
weakly integrable
if,
for any
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\Delta_{\operatorname{re}}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigcap_{n\geq 0}\,\mathrm{Ad}(\mathcal{\mathfrak{G}}^{\widetilde{\alpha}})^{n}(\mathcal{\mathfrak{G}})=0.

The following result has been proved in

[
5
]
, see Lemma 6.5.

Lemma 54
.

Let
\mathcal{\mathfrak{G}}
be a simple
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
\overline{K}
-algebra of
rank
\geq 2
. Then either

(a)

\mathcal{\mathfrak{G}}
satisfies the hypothesis

(\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{re}})
, or

(b)

\mathcal{\mathfrak{G}}
is weakly integrable.

The following result has been proved in
[
27
]

and
[
28
]
, see Theorem 4.

Theorem 55
.

If
\mathcal{\mathfrak{G}}
is weakly integrable, then
\mathfrak{G}
is an affine Lie algebra.

Remark
The Lie algebra

\mathfrak{G}
is called
integrable
if
for any real root
\tilde{\alpha}
,

ad(\mathfrak{G}^{(\tilde{\alpha})})
is locally nilpotent.
This definition of integrability is more natural, and Theorem
55

is proved in
[
27
]
under the condition that
\mathfrak{G}
is integrable.

However the notion of weak integrability is more
adapted to the proofs, and it is proved
in
[
28
]
that weak integrability implies integrability.

11.6.
Simple Lie algebras of rank
\geq 2
over the nonalgebraically closed field
K

The previous subsection did involve
simple
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebras of rank
\geq 2

over
\overline{K}
.

We now investigate

{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebras
\mathcal{L}
of rank
\geq 2

over
K
. We also assume that
\mathcal{L}
is simple as a non-graded Lie algebra.

Lemma 56
.

No simple finitely generated simple Lie algebra

\mathcal{L}
of rank
\geq 2
is
strictly Noetherian.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a simple finitely generated simple Lie algebra of rank
\geq 2
and let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
.

Any affine Lie algebra is not simple as an abstract
Lie algebra, and its centroid is infinite dimensional,
therefore
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{K}}
is not affine.
By Lemma
54
, we conclude that
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{K}}
satisfies hypothesis

(\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{re}})
.

By Lemma
49
, there exists a finite extension
E
of
L
such that

\mathfrak{h}_{E}
is split. We observe that

\mathcal{L}_{E}
admits the same root system as

\mathcal{L}_{\overline{K}}
. Therefore there is a real root

\tilde{\alpha}\in\Delta_{\operatorname{re}}

and another root

\tilde{\beta}\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}\alpha
such that the
\tilde{\alpha}
-string going through
\tilde{\beta}
is infinite.
Set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}=

\displaystyle\oplus_{k=1}^{\infty}\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,\mathcal{L}_{E}^{k\tilde{\beta}+n\tilde{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}=

\displaystyle\oplus_{k=2}^{\infty}\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,\mathcal{L}_{E}^{k\tilde{\beta}+n\tilde{\alpha}}

Obviously,

\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}\simeq\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,\mathcal{L}_{E}^{\tilde{\beta}+n\tilde{\alpha}}
is an infinite dimensional abelian section of
\mathcal{L}_{E}
. Thus

\mathcal{L}
is not strictly Noetherian.
∎

11.7.
Strictly Noetherian simple
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebras

As a conclusion of the section, we obtain

Theorem 57
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an
infinite-dimensional
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded
Lie algebra over
K
.

If
\mathcal{L}
is simple and strictly Noetherian,
then
\mathcal{L}
is isomorphic to

\mathrm{Witt}(L)
or
\mathrm{Vir}(L)
for some
finite extension
L
of
K
.

Proof.

By Lemma
46
, we can assume that
n=1
.
By Lemma
56
, the Lie algebra has rank one. Thus
the Theorem follows from Corollary
53
.
∎

Corollary 58
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a strictly Noetherian

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra. Then

\mathcal{L}_{(1)}=0.

Proof.

Assume otherwise. By
Corollary
11
,
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
satisfies the ACC on ideals, therefore
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
admits a maximal ideal. By Theorem
41
,
\mathfrak{m}
is a graded ideal.

Thus by Theorem
57
,
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}/\mathfrak{m}
is either isomorphic to

\mathrm{Witt}(L)
or
\mathrm{Vir}(L)
or
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}/\mathfrak{m}
is finite dimensional.

This contradicts Assertions (d) and (e) of Theorem A,
which asserts that no simple quotient of

\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
is a finite dimensional Lie algebra or is a Krichever-Novikov
Lie algebra. We conclude that
\mathcal{L}_{(1)}=0
.
∎

12.
The structure Theorem for perfect Noetherian graded Lie algebras

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a strictly Noetherian

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra.

In Corollary

58
, we have already shown that

\mathcal{L}_{(1)}=0
.
In Subsection
12.1
, we determine
the structure of

\mathcal{L}_{(0)}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{(0)}/\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
.
Furthermore, assume that
\mathcal{L}
is perfect.
In Subsection
12.3
, we show that

\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
is finite dimensional.
We conclude in the last subsection that,
up to isogeny,
\mathcal{L}
is a direct sum of

\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
and
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
, which
proves Theorem B.

12.1.
Simple quotients of
\mathcal{L}

In this subsection, we show that any strictly Noetherian

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
admits only finitely many maximal ideals of codimension
>1
. Then we deduce the structure of

\mathcal{L}_{(0)}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{(0)}/\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
.

Lemma 59
.

Let

\mathfrak{s}=\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,\mathfrak{s}_{n}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra endowed with a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading.

Then any Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
is a
Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{s}
.

Proof.

Since by Cartan theorem
6
(e),
we have
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{s})=0
, there is an element
D\in\mathfrak{s}
such that

[D,x]=nx\,\,\forall x\in\mathfrak{s}_{n}.

Clearly,
D
belongs to
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
. Since

D
is central in
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
, it belongs to

\mathfrak{h}
.

Since
N_{D}(\mathfrak{s})=\mathfrak{s}_{0}
, we conclude that

N_{\mathfrak{s}}(\mathfrak{h})=N_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(\mathfrak{h})

therefore
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{s}
.
∎

Lemma 60
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a strictly Noetherian

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra.
Then
\mathcal{L}
admits only finitely many maximal ideals.

Proof.

By Lemma
46
,
we can assume that
\mathcal{L}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded.
Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
,
let
\mathfrak{m}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{m}_{m}
be a finite family of distinct maximal ideals, and let

\mathfrak{h}_{i}
be the image of
\mathfrak{h}
in
\mathcal{L}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}
.

By Theorem
41
, we observe that all ideals

\mathfrak{m}_{i}
are graded ideals. When
\mathcal{L}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is finite dimensional,

\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is a Cartan subalgra of
\mathcal{L}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}
by
Lemma
59
.
Otherwise,
\mathcal{L}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is isomorphic to

\mathrm{Witt}(L)
or
\mathrm{Vir}(L)
by Proposition

57
and
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is isomorphic to
L
, the zero part of
\mathrm{Witt}(L)
or
\mathrm{Vir}(L)
.

Therefore the number of maximal ideals is bounded by
\dim\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

Let
E
be a finite extension of
K
. Recall that

\mathrm{Witt}(E)
is centrally closed
[
16
]
and that

\widehat{\mathrm{Vir}(E)}
is a central extension of

\mathrm{Vir}(E)
by
E

[
16
]
.

Corollary 61
.

We have

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}\simeq\,\big[\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Witt}(E_{i})\big]\,\oplus\,\big[\oplus_{j=1}^{m}\widehat{\mathrm{Vir}}(F_{j})\big]

where

n
,
m
are integers and

E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
are finite extensions of
K
.

Proof.

By Lemma
60
and Theorem
57
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{(0)}/\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L}_{(0)})\simeq\,\big[\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Witt}(E_{i})\big]\,\oplus\,\big[\oplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{Vir}}(F_{j})\big]

for some integers

n
,
m
and some finite extensions

E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
of
K
.
By Theorem A (b),
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}/\mathcal{L}_{(1)}
is a central extension
of
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}/\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L}_{(0)})
and by
Corollary
58
we have

\mathcal{L}_{(1)}=0
. It follows that

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}\simeq\,\big[\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Witt}(E_{i})\big]\,\oplus\,\big[\oplus_{j=1}^{m}\widehat{\mathrm{Vir}}(F_{j})\big].

∎

12.2.
Quasi-minuscule weights

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a finite dimensional semi-simple Lie algebra,
and let
\mathfrak{h}
be a split Cartan subalgebra.
We implicity assume that a Borel subalgebra is given, therefore the notion of
positive roots and dominant weights are well
defined. For any dominant weight, we denote by

L(\omega)
the simple module with highest weight

\omega
.

First, assume that
\mathfrak{s}
is simple and let

W
be its Weyl group. If the set of weight
of
L(\omega)
is
W.\omega)

or
W.\omega\cup\{0\}
, the weight
\omega
is called
quasi-minuscule
. The latter case occurs only when
\omega
is the highest root if
\mathfrak{s}
is of type ADE, or the highest short
root otherwise.

We will now extend the classical definition
of quasi-minuscule weights to the
case where
\mathfrak{s}
is semi-simple, that is

\mathfrak{s}=\oplus_{i=1}^{m}\,\mathfrak{s}_{i},

where each summand
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
is simple.
A weight
\mu
is called

quasi-minuscule
if

\mu=\sum_{i\in I}\,\omega_{i},

where
I
is a non-empty subset
of
\{1,\ldots,m\}
and each
\omega_{i}
is a
quasi-minuscule weight for
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
.

Lemma 62
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
\mathfrak{s}
-module. Assume that

V^{(\mu)}=0

for any quasi-minuscule weight
\mu
. Then
V
is a trivial
\mathfrak{s}
-module, that is
\mathfrak{s}.V=0
.

Proof.

Clearly the statement amounts to the fact that whenever
V
is a nontrivial
simple
\mathfrak{s}
-module
V
, we have
V^{(\mu)}\neq 0
, for some quasi-minuscule weight
\mu
.

Indeed
V
is of the form

V=\otimes_{i\in I}\,V_{i},

where
I
is a non-empty subset
of
\{1,\ldots,m\}
and each
V_{i}
is a
nontrivial simple
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
-module. Therefore for

j\not\in I
,
\mathfrak{s}_{j}
acts trivially.

By
[
12
]
Chapter 8, for each

i\in I
, there is a quasi-minuscle weight

\omega_{i}
such that
V_{i}^{(\omega_{i})}\neq 0
.
Thus we have

V^{(\omega)}\neq 0,

where
\omega
is the quasi-minuscule weight
\omega=\sum_{i\in I}\,\omega_{i}
.
∎

12.3.
Proof that
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}{(0)}
is finite dimensional

We will now use that
\mathcal{L}
is assumed to be perfect.

Lemma 63
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a strictly
Noetherian perfect
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra.
Then

(a)

\mathcal{L}/\operatorname{rad}^{f}(\mathcal{L})
is finite dimensional, and

(b)

there is an integer
M
such that

\mathcal{C}^{M+1}\operatorname{rad}^{f}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{C}^{M}\operatorname{rad}^{f}(\mathcal{L}).

Proof.

By Lemma
60
,
\mathcal{L}
admits only finitely many maximal ideals, thus
\mathcal{L}/\operatorname{rad}^{f}(\mathcal{L})
is finite dimensional, which proves Assertion (a)

We now prove Assertion (b).
Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra
of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
. By Lemma
49
(a),
there is a finite extension
E
of
K
such that

\mathfrak{h}\otimes E
is a split Cartan subalgebra
in
\mathcal{L}\otimes E
.

We observe that

\operatorname{rad}^{f}(\mathcal{L}\otimes E)=\operatorname{rad}^{f}(\mathcal{L})\otimes E.

Even if it means choosing
\mathcal{L}\otimes E
instead of
\mathcal{L}
, we may assume that
\mathfrak{h}
is split.

Therefore
\mathcal{L}
admits a generalized weight decomposition

\mathcal{L}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)},

where
\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)}=\{x\in\mathcal{L}\mid(\operatorname{ad}(h)-\alpha(h))^{N}(x)=0{\rm\,\,for\,}N\gg 0\}
.

We claim that
\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)}
is finite dimensional whenever
\alpha\neq 0
.
Set
\mathfrak{p}=\oplus_{k\geq 1}\,\mathcal{L}^{(k\alpha)}

and
\mathfrak{q}=\oplus_{k\geq 2}\,\mathcal{L}^{(k\alpha)}
.
Then
\mathcal{L}^{(\alpha)}
is isomorphic to the abelian section
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{q}
, which proves the claim.

Set
\mathfrak{r}=\operatorname{rad}^{f}(\mathcal{L})
,
\mathfrak{s}=\mathcal{L}/\mathfrak{r}

and, for any
k\geq 1
, set

V_{k}=\mathcal{C}^{k}\mathfrak{r}/\mathcal{C}^{k+1}\mathfrak{r}
.
We have already proved that
\mathfrak{s}
is a
(finite dimensional) semisimple Lie algebra.
Since they are abelian sections, each component
V_{k}
is a finite dimensional
\mathfrak{s}
-module.

Since the Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}/[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{r}]
is finite dimensional,
Theorem
6
(b) implies that

\mathcal{L}/[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{r}]\simeq\mathfrak{s}\ltimes V_{1}.

Since
\mathcal{L}
is perfect
and
V_{1}
is an abelian ideal, we deduce that

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{s},V_{1}].

(2)

Let
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
be the image of
\mathfrak{h}

in
\mathfrak{s}
. Since each
V_{k}
is an
\mathfrak{s}
-module,

\mathfrak{h}
acts diagonally on
V_{k}

and the action factors trough
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}
.
Thus for any quasi-minuscule weight

\omega
we can consider the weightspace

\,V_{k}^{(\omega)}=\{x\in V_{k}\mid h.x=\omega(h)x\,\forall h\in\mathfrak{h}\}.

We have proved that
\mathcal{L}^{(\omega)}
is finite dimensional, henceforth

\oplus_{k{\geq 1}}\,V_{k}^{(\omega)}

is finite dimensional. Therefore there
is an integer
N
, such that

V_{k}^{(\omega)}=0,

for all
k\geq N
and all
quasi-minuscule weight
\omega
. By Lemma

62
, we obtain that

\mathfrak{s}
acts trivially on
V_{k}
for
k\geq N
.

The Lie bracket

\mathfrak{r}\times\mathcal{C}^{N}\,\mathfrak{r}\to C^{N+1}\,\mathfrak{r}

induces a surjective

\mathfrak{s}
-equivariant map

\mu:V_{1}\otimes V_{N}\to V_{N+1}.

We have proved that
V_{1}=\mathfrak{s}.V_{1}

and
\mathfrak{s}.V_{N}=\mathfrak{s}.V_{N+1}=0
. It follows that

\displaystyle V_{N+1}

\displaystyle=\mu(V_{1}\otimes V_{N})

\displaystyle=\mu(\mathfrak{s}.V_{1}\otimes V_{N})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{s}.\mu(V_{1}\otimes V_{N})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{s}.V_{N+1},

from which we conclude that
V_{N+1}=0
, that is

\mathcal{C}^{M}\,\mathfrak{r}=\mathcal{C}^{M+1}\,\mathfrak{r},

where
M=N+1
, which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 64
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a strictly
Noetherian perfect
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie algebra.

Then
\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
is finite dimensional.

Proof.

By Lemma
63
(a),

\operatorname{rad}^{f}(\mathcal{L})
has finite codimension,
therefore by Lemma
9
(g) we have

\mathscr{D}^{\omega}\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L})

By Lemma
63
(b), there is an integer
M

such that

\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{C}^{M}\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L}).

Hence the descending derived series stabilizes. It implies that
\mathcal{C}^{M}\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L})
is perfect, which means that

\mathcal{L}_{(0)}=\mathcal{C}^{M}\operatorname{rad}(\mathcal{L}),

which implies the claim.
∎

12.4.
Proof of Theorem B

We finally prove the following result:

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a perfect strictly Noetherian

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra
and let
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
be its its universal central extension. Then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\simeq\mathfrak{g}\,\oplus\,\big[\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Witt}(E_{i})\big]\,\oplus\,\big[\oplus_{j=1}^{m}\widehat{\mathrm{Vir}}(F_{j})\big]

where
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{s}\ltimes\mathfrak{r}
is a (perfect) finite dimensional Lie algebra,

n
,
m
are integers and

E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
are finite extensions of
K
.

Proof.

Let

\mathfrak{z}
be the center of
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
.
By Corollary
61
,
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}/\mathfrak{z}
is a direct sum
of Witt and Virasoro Lie algebras. Thus by
Grabowski Theorem
16
, we have

\mathrm{Out}(\mathcal{L}_{(0)}/\mathfrak{z})=0
. Since
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}_{(0)}
are perfect,
by Lemma
5
(b),
we have

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}_{(0)})}+\widehat{\mathcal{L}_{(0)}}.

By Corollary
64
,

\mathfrak{g}:=\widehat{\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}_{(0)})}
is finite dimensional.
Moreover by Corollary
61
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{(0)}\simeq\,\big[\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Witt}(E_{i})\big]\,\oplus\,\big[\oplus_{j=1}^{m}\widehat{\mathrm{Vir}}(F_{j})\big]

for some integers

n
,
m
and some finite extensions

E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
of
K
. This completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

Corollary 65
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a perfect

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Lie algebra.
The algebra
U(\mathcal{L})
is left Noetherian
only if
\mathcal{L}
has finite dimension.

Proof.

Assume that
U(\mathcal{L})
is left Noetherian.
Then by Corollary
8
the Lie algebra

\mathcal{L}
is strictly Noetherian.
By Sierra Walton Theorem
2
,
\mathcal{L}
does not admit a section
isomorphic to
\mathrm{Witt}(K)
. Thus by Theorem B,

\mathcal{L}
is finite dimensional.
∎
