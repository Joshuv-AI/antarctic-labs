Quality of life with palbociclib plus tamoxifen in hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer: results from PATHWAY, an Asian international, double-blind, randomized phase 3 trial
Authors: Takashi Yamanaka, Joohyuk Sohn, Kazuki Sudo, Hirofumi Mukai, Naohito Yamamoto, Chi-Feng Chung, Yen-Shen Lu, Kyung-Hun Lee, Soo-Chin Lee, Tsutomu Iwasa, Hiroji Iwata, Kenichi Watanabe, Kyung Hae Jung, Yuko Tanabe, Seok Yun Kang, Hiroyuki Yasojima, Kenjiro Aogi, Eriko Tokunaga, Sung Hoon Sim, Yoon Sim Yap, Koji Matsumoto, Ling-Ming Tseng, Yoshiko Umeyama, Emi Noguchi, Tomomi Hata, Aya Kuchiba, Taro Shibata, Kenichi Nakamura, Yasuhiro Fujiwara, Kenji Tamura, Kan Yonemori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn the Asian international PATHWAY trial, palbociclib–tamoxifen demonstrated improved progression-free survival compared with placebo–tamoxifen in patients with HR+/HER2− locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer (ABC). This analysis compared quality of life (QoL) between treatment arms.MethodsPre/peri or postmenopausal women with HR+/HER2− ABC were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive palbociclib–tamoxifen or placebo–tamoxifen. Patient-reported outcomes were assessed on day 1 of cycles 1 (baseline), 4, and 7, and at the end of treatment (EOT) using the EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23 questionnaires. Least-square mean (LSM) changes in scores from baseline were calculated. Kaplan–Meier method was used to calculate time to deterioration (TTD) in the minimally important difference (MID) in the pain subscale.ResultsIn the comparison between treatment arms, no significant differences were observed in LSM changes in scores from baseline in global QoL (cycle 4: 0.70 [95% confidence interval (CI): −5.00, 6.41]; cycle 7: 2.17 [95% CI: −4.14, 8.48]; EOT: −5.19 [95% CI: −11.64, 1.26]). For both the EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23 functional and symptom subscales, no clinically meaningful differences between treatment arms were found in the LSM changes in scores from baseline. Median TTD in the MID in the pain subscale was 32.5 months (95% CI: 11.7, not estimable) for the palbociclib–tamoxifen arm and 21.2 months (95% CI: 5.5, 43.5) for the placebo–tamoxifen arm (hazard ratio = 0.729 [95% CI: 0.467, 1.138]).ConclusionsThe combination of palbociclib and tamoxifen delayed deterioration in patients’ QoL while they experienced reduced risk for disease progression.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03423199; study registration date: February 6, 2018.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s12282-026-01865-0.Keywords: HR+/HER2−, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer, palbociclib, patient-reported outcomes, tamoxifen

Introduction

Among women worldwide, breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer and leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. In both low to medium Human Development Index countries (e.g., India) and high-income countries (e.g., Japan, Republic of Korea) in Asia, rapid increases in breast cancer incidence and mortality have occurred [1]. Reported in the 2019 Global Burden of Disease Study of 21 evaluated geographic regions, East Asia had the highest number of breast cancer cases, while South Asia, East Asia, and Southeast Asia had the highest breast cancer-related disability-adjusted life-year burden [2, 3]. Factors contributing to the disproportionate breast cancer burden in Asian countries include that the peak age for diagnosis is near 50 years of age, while it is ~ 70 years of age in Western populations [4], ~ 50% of patients diagnosed with breast cancer are pre or perimenopausal [2], and up to 25% are initially diagnosed with de novo metastatic disease compared with only 3%−5% of those in Europe and the US [5].

Cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors, including palbociclib, in combination with endocrine therapy (ET) are recommended for the treatment of hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2−) locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer (ABC) [2, 6, 7]. The safety and efficacy of palbociclib plus ET were investigated in the pivotal PALOMA-2 and PALOMA-3 phase 3 trials of patients with estrogen receptor-positive (ER+)/HER2− or HR+/HER2− ABC, in which palbociclib in combination with letrozole or fulvestrant significantly prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) compared with placebo plus ET [8, 9]. In these trials, Asian patients represented 15%−21% of those who received palbociclib [8, 9]. In subgroup analyses of Asian patients, palbociclib plus ET also prolonged PFS in PALOMA-2 and PALOMA-3 [10, 11]. The PALOMA-4 trial specifically assessed palbociclib efficacy and safety in Asian populations (mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Taiwan, and Thailand), and found consistent results with the PALOMA-2 outcomes [8], with Asian postmenopausal patients having significantly prolonged median PFS with first-line palbociclib plus letrozole versus placebo plus letrozole (hazard ratio [HR] = 0.68 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.53, 0.87]; P = 0.0012) [12].

Patient-reported outcome (PRO) measures of quality of life (QoL) represent key secondary outcomes of oncology research and are particularly important considering patients with breast cancer across a multitude of countries are living longer, including those in some Asian countries; however, the disease remains incurable [13–17]. Guidelines/policies of the European School of Oncology and European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) [6], the Pan-Asian adapted ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines [2], and the ABC Global Alliance [18] have all prioritized the incorporation of PRO assessments into oncology research and routine patient care. In PALOMA-4, patient-reported QoL was shown to be maintained in Asian patients treated with palbociclib plus letrozole versus placebo plus letrozole when assessed with the Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy-Breast (FACT-B) and EuroQoL five-dimension three-level (EQ-5D-3L) questionnaires, which were protocol-specified secondary outcomes [12, 19]. These results were consistent with those reported in the predominantly Western population in PALOMA-2, which used similar QoL assessment tools [20].

The combination of tamoxifen, a selective estrogen receptor modulator, and luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone agonists for ovarian function suppression is one of the ET options for pre/perimenopausal patients with HR+/HER2− ABC [6, 21]. Tamoxifen is also an ET option for postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2– ABC after treatment with aromatase inhibitors or when they are intolerable to treatment with them [6, 21]. Resistance, primary or acquired, to ET remains a challenge, which is particularly true for premenopausal or perimenopausal patients, who are more prevalently diagnosed with HR+/HER2– ABC in Asian countries compared with Western populations [22], further emphasizing the necessity for alternative ET options for Asian patients. The phase 3 PATHWAY trial (NCCH1607, NCT03423199), thus, investigated the efficacy and safety of palbociclib or placebo plus tamoxifen in 184 pre/perimenopausal or postmenopausal women with HR+/HER2– ABC from Japan, Republic of Korea, Taiwan, and Singapore [21]. After a median duration of follow-up of approximately 41 months, palbociclib plus tamoxifen (median PFS: 24.4 months [95% CI: 13.1, 32.4]) significantly reduced the risk of disease progression or death by 40% versus placebo plus tamoxifen (median PFS: 11.1 months [95% CI: 7.4, 14.6]; one-sided P = 0.002) overall, and in subgroups of patients treated with first-line or second-line ET, and pre/perimenopausal and postmenopausal patients [21]. The OS data had not matured, and median OS was not reached in either study arm at the time of the PFS analysis; however, a trend favoring the palbociclib plus tamoxifen arm was observed; the overall reduction in risk of death was 27% with palbociclib plus tamoxifen versus placebo plus tamoxifen (HR = 0.73 [95% CI: 0.44, 1.21]) [21]. Here, we report the prespecified secondary outcomes of patient-reported QoL of the PATHWAY trial, assessed with the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire-Core 30 (EORTC QLQ-C30) and its companion assessment module, the Breast Cancer-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (QLQ-BR23).

Patients and methods

Study design

PATHWAY is an international, multi-center, double-blind, randomized phase 3 trial being conducted in Asia (22 sites in Japan, the Republic of Korea, Taiwan, and Singapore; NCT03423199; study registration date: February 6, 2018). The protocol was approved by the institutional review board at each trial site and each patient provided written informed consent. This study was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patients and treatment

Pre/peri or postmenopausal women with HR+/HER2– ABC who were candidates to receive tamoxifen as first-line or second-line ET for ABC were enrolled in PATHWAY. Exclusion criteria included prior treatment with a CDK4/6 inhibitor or tamoxifen. Patients with disease progression > 12 months after the completion of adjuvant therapy with tamoxifen and/or one previous line of chemotherapy for ABC were eligible. Patients were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive palbociclib plus tamoxifen (palbociclib arm) or placebo plus tamoxifen (placebo arm) [21]. Patients were stratified by treatment history with ET (first-line or second-line) and by menopausal status (pre/perimenopausal versus postmenopausal) at randomization. Study treatment that was given after recurrence during treatment or within 12 months after completion of adjuvant ET was defined as second-line ET. Patients received either palbociclib (starting dose: 125 mg/day) or placebo orally once daily (3 weeks on and 1 week off) plus tamoxifen 20 mg orally once daily (continuously). Pre/perimenopausal women received goserelin (administered subcutaneously). Patients continued to receive treatment until progressive disease (PD), clinically diagnosed symptomatic deterioration, unacceptable toxicity, death, or patient’s withdrawal of consent, whichever occurred first.

Patient-reported outcomes

Protocol-specified secondary endpoints of the PATHWAY study included PROs assessed with the EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23 instruments on day 1 of cycle 1 (baseline), day 1 of cycles 4 and 7, and at the end of treatment/withdrawal (EOT). Questionnaires were available in the local language at study sites and completed by patients prior to having any tests and any discussion of their progress with healthcare personnel at the site on the visit day and mandatorily before study treatment administration.

The EORTC QLQ-C30 was developed specifically for patients with cancer and has been utilized in clinical trials of palbociclib to understand the impacts on QoL and daily activities [23, 24]. The EORTC QLQ-C30 is a 30-item questionnaire composed of a global health QoL domain, five multi-item functional subscales (physical, role, emotional, cognitive, and social functioning), three multi-item symptom subscales (fatigue, nausea & vomiting, and pain), and six single items assessing other cancer-related symptoms (dyspnea, insomnia, appetite loss, constipation, diarrhea, and the financial impact of cancer) [25]. The questionnaire uses 28 4-point Likert scales with responses ranging from “not at all” to “very much” and two 7-point Likert scales for global health and overall QoL [25–27]. The QLQ-BR23 is a 23-item breast cancer-specific companion module to the EORTC QLQ-C30, consisting of four functional subscales (body image, sexual functioning, sexual enjoyment, and future perspective) and four symptom subscales (systemic side effects, breast symptoms, arm symptoms, and upset by hair loss) [28]. Responses to all questionnaire items were converted to a 0 to 100 scale using a standard scoring algorithm [29]. For global QoL and functional subscales, higher scores represent a better level of functioning/QoL than lower scores. For symptom subscales, higher scores represent greater symptom severity. A change ≥ 10 points in scores from baseline was considered clinically meaningful [30]. For single items on questionnaires, in cases where two answers were given, the more severe answer was counted. If at least half of the items for the multi-item scales were answered, then the score for that scale was pro-rated based on non-missing items. For subsequent analyses, missing items were not imputed.

Statistical analyses

All PRO analyses were performed based on the PRO-evaluable population, defined as patients in the intent-to-treat population with a baseline and ≥ 1 post baseline PRO assessment before the end of study treatment. Questionnaire completion rates were summarized at baseline, cycles 4 and 7, and EOT. The primary PRO analysis for comparison of treatment arms was based on least-square mean (LSM) changes in scores from baseline to cycle 4, cycle 7, and EOT based on longitudinal repeated measures analysis using a mixed effects model, with treatment, time, treatment-by-time, and baseline score as covariates. Parameter estimates were based on a restricted maximum likelihood method and an unstructured covariance matrix was used. No adjustments for multiple comparisons were made. Time to deterioration (TTD) in the EORTC QLQ-C30 was analyzed for the prespecified secondary outcomes of TTD in the minimally important difference (MID) in the symptom subscale of pain and TTD in a composite endpoint, defined as the first occurrence of death, PD, clinically diagnosed symptomatic deterioration, or MID in pain. MID was defined as an increase (i.e., greater symptom severity) in score of ≥ 10 points from baseline [30]. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to calculate TTD. One-sided P-value was calculated by stratified log-rank test. All statistical analyses were conducted using the Statistical Analysis System software (SAS Institute; Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Patients

A total of 184 patients were randomly assigned to palbociclib arm (n = 91) or placebo arm (n = 93) between February 15, 2018 and July 30, 2019. Baseline characteristics were generally balanced between the palbociclib and placebo arms (Table 1); median ages, respectively, were 60 and 61 years, 61.5% and 60.2% received first-line ET, and 72.5% and 71.0% were postmenopausal. Among the patients who received palbociclib compared with placebo, a greater proportion had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 (79.1% vs. 65.6%).

Patient-reported outcomes

The PRO-evaluable population included 88 patients in the palbociclib arm and 93 patients in the placebo arm. In the palbociclib arm, 90.9%, 76.1%, and 72.7% completed the EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23 questionnaires at cycle 4, cycle 7, and EOT, respectively; in the placebo arm, 76.3%, 63.4%, and 84.9% completed both questionnaires at cycle 4, cycle 7 and EOT, respectively (Online Resource 1).

EORTC QLQ-C30

Baseline mean scores for the EORTC QLQ-C30 global QoL domain were generally similar for the palbociclib (65.3 [95% CI: 61.1, 69.4]) and placebo (63.3 [95% CI: 58.6, 67.9]) arms, as were baseline mean scores for all functional subscales (Online Resource 2). For the EORTC QLQ-C30 global QoL and functional subscales, LSM changes in scores from baseline to cycles 4 and 7 and EOT were similar between treatment arms (Fig. 1). Some improvement in emotional functioning was seen at cycles 4 and 7 for both treatment arms, while there was worsening in cognitive functioning for both treatment arms throughout the course of treatment. Social functioning was improved at cycles 4 and 7 in the palbociclib arm; no such improvement was seen at these visits in the placebo arm. At EOT, global QoL and functioning in all subscales had worsened in both treatment arms than at previous time points. In the comparison between treatment arms, no significant differences were observed in LSM changes in scores from baseline in global QoL (cycle 4: 0.70 [95% CI: −5.00, 6.41]; cycle 7: 2.17 [95% CI: −4.14, 8.48]; EOT: −5.19 [95% CI: −11.64, 1.26]) and all functional subscales throughout the course of treatment (Fig. 2).

TTD

For the TTD in the EORTC QLQ-C30 symptom subscale of pain, a total of 40 patients (45.5%) in the palbociclib arm versus 40 patients (43.0%) in the placebo arm had an event of MID at the data cutoff of September 15, 2022. The median TTD for pain was 32.5 months (95% CI: 11.7, not estimable) for the palbociclib arm and 21.2 months (95% CI: 5.5, 43.5) for the placebo arm (HR = 0.729 [95% CI: 0.467, 1.138]; Fig. 6). For the TTD in the composite endpoint, a total of 68 patients (77.3%) in the palbociclib arm versus 81 patients (87.1%) in the placebo arm had an event at the data cutoff; these events included PD (42 patients [47.7%] vs. 54 patients [58.1%], clinically diagnosed symptomatic deterioration (3 patients [3.4%] vs. 4 patients [4.3%]), and MID in the pain subscale (23 patients [26.1%] vs. 23 patients [24.7%]). The median TTD was 10.3 months (95% CI: 5.8, 24.4) for the palbociclib arm and 5.5 months (95% CI: 3.6, 7.6) for the placebo arm (Online Resource 6). Patients in the palbociclib arm had a 40% significant reduction in the risk of deterioration compared with those in the placebo arm (HR = 0.601 [95% CI: 0.433, 0.834]; one-sided P = 0.001).

Discussion

In this assessment of patient-reported QoL of patients with HR+/HER2− ABC enrolled in the international Asian PATHWAY trial, no clinically meaningful differences in QoL, as assessed by EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23, were observed between patients who received palbociclib plus tamoxifen and those who received placebo plus tamoxifen. Although it does not meet the ≥ 10-point threshold for clinical significance, the improvement in pain scores was observed at cycles 4 (not statistically significant) and 7 (statistically significant) from baseline among patients who received palbociclib versus placebo. Furthermore, among the patients in the palbociclib arm, TTD in the MID in the pain subscale of the EORTC QLQ-C30 was prolonged by approximately 11 months compared with those in the placebo arm. Delaying pain may positively influence patient QoL, considering pain significantly negatively impacts QoL in patients with ABC [24, 31]. In addition, TTD in the composite endpoint, including death, PD, clinically diagnosed symptomatic deterioration, and MID in the pain subscale of the EORTC QLQ-C30 was prolonged by approximately 5 months compared with those in the placebo arm. These findings observed in the PATHWAY trial are noteworthy, considering that patients with HR+/HER2− ABC frequently receive multiple lines of sequential therapies and are living longer with their metastatic disease [13–17, 21], although it still remains incurable.

Our PRO findings are consistent with those of the international PALOMA-2 and -3 trials and the PALOMA-4 trial conducted in Asia [12, 19, 20, 24]. Both the PALOMA-2 and PALOMA-4 trials only included postmenopausal patients with ER+/HER2− ABC who received first-line treatment with palbociclib plus letrozole or placebo plus letrozole and reported that patient-reported QoL was maintained while on treatment with palbociclib relative to control treatment [12, 19, 20]. Additionally, in these two trials, pain scores, assessed with the FACT-B or FACT-General instruments, showed greater improvement in patients who received palbociclib compared with those who received control treatment [19, 20]. Similar to the PATHWAY trial, the PALOMA-3 trial included both pre/perimenopausal and postmenopausal patients, but all patients with HR+/HER2− ABC had progressed on prior ET; among the patients treated with palbociclib plus fulvestrant, a significantly greater improvement in EORTC QLQ-C30 global QoL, as well as in pain scores, was found compared with the control treatment [24].

Patients in the PATHWAY trial who were treated with palbociclib plus tamoxifen also experienced a delay in the pain deterioration endpoint (32.5 vs. 21.2 months with placebo plus tamoxifen; HR = 0.729 [95% CI: 0.467, 1.138]). In PALOMA-3, the estimated median TTD in pain score was 8.0 months in the palbociclib plus fulvestrant arm versus 2.8 months in the control treatment arm (HR = 0.642 [95% CI: 0.487, 0.846]) [24]. Findings from PALOMA-3 included a significantly greater delay in deterioration of the EORTC QLQ-C30 global QoL in the palbociclib plus fulvestrant arm versus the control treatment arm (HR = 0.641 [95% CI: 0.451, 0.910]) [24]. In the PALOMA-2 and PALOMA-4 trials, TTD in FACT-B total scores did not significantly differ among treatment arms; both trials did report that patients treated with palbociclib without disease progression had significantly longer median TTD/TTD-definitive definition (TTD-DD; a decrease of 7 points with no subsequent observation of a < 7-point decrease) compared with those who had disease progression (PALOMA-2 TTD: 27.6 vs. 22.2 months; HR = 0.525 [95% CI: 0.380, 0.725]; PALOMA-4 TTD-DD: 60.1 vs. 28.0 months; P < 0.001) [19, 20].

The nearly 5-month delay in the composite deterioration endpoint among patients treated with palbociclib plus tamoxifen in the PATHWAY trial correlates with their longer duration of PFS [21]. In PATHWAY, palbociclib plus tamoxifen demonstrated a significant and clinically meaningful benefit with over a 13-month increase in median PFS compared with placebo plus tamoxifen (24.4 vs. 11.1 months; HR = 0.60 [95% CI: 0.43, 0.85]; one-sided P = 0.002) [21]. The PATHWAY study findings are also relatively consistent with those seen in the international MONALEESA-7 trial (~ 30% Asian of a total of 672 patients), in which premenopausal patients with HR+/HER2− ABC treated with ribociclib plus tamoxifen (n = 87) had a 41% reduced risk of disease progression or death versus those treated with placebo plus tamoxifen (n = 90) (median PFS: 22.1 vs. 11.0 months; HR = 0.59 [95% CI: 0.39, 0.88]) [32]. Furthermore, those who received ribociclib plus ET and control treatment had similar EORTC QLQ-C30 global QoL, but TTD in global QoL (HR = 0.67 [95% CI: 0.52, 0.86]) and pain (HR = 0.65 [95% CI: 0.45, 0.92]) were prolonged [33].

In the PATHWAY trial, the reported adverse events (AEs), were generally consistent with the known safety profile of palbociclib plus other ET in predominately Western populations [8, 9, 21], and that seen in the subgroup analyses of Asian populations in the PALOMA-2 and PALOMA-3 trials [10, 11]. The most common AE, neutropenia, had an incidence of 91.2% (75.8% grade 3; 13.2% grade 4) [21]. Similar AE findings were seen in the PALOMA-4 trial conducted in Asia, which had a reported neutropenia incidence of 98.2% (72.0% grade 3; 12.5% grade 4) [12]. In neither of these trials were new palbociclib safety concerns identified in the Asian study populations [12, 21]. In the analysis of patient-reported QoL in PALOMA-4, patients with and without neutropenia did not show any significant differences in QoL [19], which was consistent with that reported in PALOMA-2 [20]. Although neutropenia was common among patients who received palbociclib in the PATHWAY trial, QoL scores during the course of treatment remained similar to those of patients who received placebo in combination with tamoxifen who had a low incidence of neutropenia (2.2% any grade) [21]. Palbociclib dose reductions were frequent among patients treated with palbociclib in the PATHWAY trial, with 67% requiring at least one dose reduction [21], which may have diminished any significant impact of AEs on QoL.

The PATHWAY trial only included patients of Asian origin and study findings may not generalize to other racial/ethnicity populations with HR+/HER2– ABC. However, no significant racial and ethnicity differences in palbociclib efficacy were reported in the PALOMA-2 and PALOMA-3 trials [8–11]. Although pre/perimenopausal patients were included in the PATHWAY trial, the sample sizes were small, with 25 and 27 patients in the palbociclib and placebo arms, respectively. During the course of treatment in the PATHWAY trial, some attrition occurred with completion of the EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23 questionnaires; however, at all assessed time points, completion rates were relatively high and at EOT, completion rates of both questionnaires were 73% and 85% of the palbociclib and placebo PRO-evaluable population, respectively. The significant, but not clinically meaningful, change from baseline seen in the pain subscale in the palbociclib arm versus the placebo arm at cycle 7 should be interpreted with caution due to the potential impact of repeated measures over time. Including disease progression in the composite endpoint in which TTD was measured may inherently favor the palbociclib arm since the treatment is already known to significantly prolong PFS (i.e., the resulting delay in TTD may reflect delayed clinical progression rather than a genuine preservation of patient-perceived QoL). Lastly, a greater proportion of patients who received palbociclib compared with placebo had an ECOG performance status of 0 (79.1% vs. 65.6%), which may have potentially confounded the study findings.

Conclusions

In the PATHWAY trial, no clinically meaningful differences in patient-reported QoL were observed between Asian patients with HR+/HER2– ABC who received palbociclib plus tamoxifen and those who received placebo plus tamoxifen, as assessed with the EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-BR23 instruments. The TTD in the MID in the pain subscale of the EORTC QLQ-C30 was prolonged in patients treated with palbociclib plus tamoxifen compared with those treated with placebo plus tamoxifen. As the choice and optimal sequencing of ET is complex for both pre/perimenopausal and postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2− breast cancer that progresses to ABC, it is important that alternative ET options are clinically tested with standard CDK4/6 inhibitor treatments. The combination of palbociclib and tamoxifen provides value by delaying deterioration in patients’ QoL while they experience reduced risk for disease progression and less pain and expands the treatment options beyond palbociclib combinations with an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant.
