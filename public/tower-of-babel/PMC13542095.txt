Financial time series forecasting with a hybrid VMD–CSA–BiT framework
Authors: Guiyan Zhao, Jiayuan Ouyang, Jianhui Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Financial time series forecasting faces significant challenges due to inherent nonlinearity, non-stationarity, and high levels of noise. To address these issues, this study proposes VMD–CSA–BiT, an integrated framework that combines variational mode decomposition (VMD), convolutional self-attention (CSA), and bidirectional transformers (BiT) to enhance prediction robustness. The methodology first decomposes raw price series into interpretable intrinsic mode functions via VMD. It then employs the CSA module to refine pointwise representations at individual time steps and applies the BiT network to model bidirectional long-term temporal dependencies. Evaluated on a range of financial assets using a comprehensive set of market features, the proposed framework demonstrates consistent performance improvements over multiple benchmark models, including traditional statistical methods, machine learning models, and advanced deep learning architectures, achieving significant reductions in key error metrics. The results indicate that VMD–CSA–BiT offers superior forecasting accuracy and stability, with visual analyses showing that its predictions generally align with actual market movements. This study shows that VMD–CSA–BiT is a promising and effective approach for financial time series forecasting. Future research will focus on further architectural optimizations and extending the framework to additional financial applications.

Introduction

Regression analysis of financial time series is a widely employed method to examine the interrelationships among various factors in financial markets and their impact on indicators such as asset prices. As a complex system, the financial market can be better understood through regression analysis, which helps uncover trends and correlations, for instance, how economic indicators affect stock prices or how interest rates influence exchange rates, thereby enhancing the accuracy and effectiveness of decision-making.

Nevertheless, financial markets, as complex dynamic systems, often exhibit characteristics such as nonlinearity, non-stationarity, and high noise. Traditional linear models fail to adequately capture intricate nonlinear relationships. Moreover, financial data are highly time-sensitive, with statistical properties like mean and variance changing frequently over time, which complicates the extraction of informative features and limits forecasting performance. Additionally, financial markets behave as chaotic systems containing substantial random fluctuations and noise. Unidirectional temporal models are easily disturbed by such complexity, making it difficult to predict genuine volatility trends accurately1.

The long short-term memory (LSTM) network2, a classical deep learning model, has achieved notable success in time-series forecasting. However, it struggles to simultaneously capture short-term fluctuations and long-term trends in financial data, and its prediction deviations increase in highly volatile regions. The Transformer network3, built on the self-attention mechanism, excels at modeling long-range dependencies and shows potential for financial time-series forecasting. Yet, a single Transformer structure cannot fully learn both forward and backward temporal dependencies.

To address this, the present study modifies the Transformer encoder architecture and proposes a Bidirectional Transformer (BiT) designed to deeply extract bidirectional temporal dependencies. The network employs parallel encoders to respectively capture forward and backward features, and adopts a hierarchical regression framework that integrates features across layers via residual connections.

To further improve forecasting performance, suppress noise, and overcome the Transformer’s limitation in multiscale feature extraction, we incorporate variational mode decomposition (VMD)4 for feature decomposition. VMD decomposes the original signal into multiple intrinsic mode function (IMF) components, thereby mitigating non-stationarity and transforming the data into more regularized components. By separating high- and low-frequency IMFs, multiple BiT modules can independently learn features at different time scales. To adapt VMD-derived features to the BiT input dimension, a one-dimensional convolutional-based pre-processing self-attention module is designed. This module combines local feature extraction via convolution with global dependency modeling via self-attention, improving feature composition. Experiments demonstrate that the CSA module enhances local feature extraction through position-dependent linear transformation, which in turn improves regression performance.

The principal contributions of this work are summarized as follows: (1) A hybrid VMD–CSA–BiT forecasting framework is proposed. Tailored to the nonlinear, non-stationary, and high-noise nature of financial time series, it integrates signal decomposition, local feature extraction, and global temporal modeling. The framework outperforms several benchmark models across multiple forecasting tasks. (2) An improved convolutional self-attention (CSA) mechanism is proposed. By incorporating a one-dimensional convolutional preprocessing stage for VMD-derived high- and low-frequency components, it refines pointwise representations at individual time steps, thereby augmenting the self-attention module’s capacity to capture global dependencies and effectively model multiscale patterns in financial data. (3) A feature learning approach combining VMD and BiT is introduced. VMD decomposition reduces non-stationarity and noise interference, while the bidirectional Transformer simultaneously learns forward and backward temporal dependencies, improving sequential feature learning.

It details the mathematical principles of VMD and explains the designed CSA module and the standard Transformer architecture. “VMD–CSA–BiT network architecture” section describes the proposed model’s design and implementation, outlining its overall architecture, workflow, and modular details. “Experiments” section details the experiments, covering data preparation, setup, evaluation metrics, comparative tests, and ablation studies. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach compared to baseline methods. “Conclusions” section concludes the paper by summarizing the work, discussing the model’s strengths in handling financial data, and suggesting directions for future research.

Literature review

Given the inherent complexity of financial markets, traditional linear models often struggle to effectively capture the nonlinear relationships within them. In contrast, machine learning and deep learning methodologies are better suited for modeling such intricate nonlinear dynamics, enabling a more accurate fit to the complex patterns and trends embedded in financial time series data5. Research in this domain has advanced considerably in recent years, with scholars proposing a variety of approaches that have yielded notable outcomes. These include methods such as Random Forests (RF)6, Support Vector Machine (SVM)7, eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)8, Backpropagation Neural Networks9, LSTM networks10, and various hybrid models11. Kobiela et al.12 compared the forecasting performance of the Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) model and the LSTM model on NASDAQ data. Their results indicated that the ARIMA model outperformed the LSTM when using a single feature (historical price) over longer forecasting horizons, particularly for predicting average prices over 30 days, 3 months, and 9 months. Li et al.13 investigated the application of LSTM models to several technology stocks, demonstrating LSTM’s potential in capturing complex stock price patterns and generating reasonably accurate predictions. Zhu et al.14 compared ARIMA, XGBoost, and LSTM models in forecasting Amazon stock prices, finding that while the LSTM model achieved the best fit, its predictive performance required further refinement to reduce bias. Oyewola et al.15 proposed an LSTM network integrated with a quasi-Pareto sequence sampling optimization and an attention layer. Experimental validation across multiple stock indices proved this method significantly superior to conventional deep learning models.

To further enhance the predictive efficacy of deep learning techniques for financial stock data, researchers have introduced the attention-based Transformer network16. This architecture has demonstrated exceptional performance in diverse time series analysis tasks, owing to its robust capacity for capturing long-range dependencies. Kehinde et al.17 evaluated multiple deep learning models in forecasting several major global stock indices, observing that the Transformer network’s performance markedly surpassed that of other traditional deep learning methods. This finding underscores the Transformer’s effectiveness in financial market applications. Following this, several novel attention-based architectures have been proposed and applied within finance. Koval et al.18 employed the Longformer network to analyze annual reports of financial companies, aiming to predict long-term corporate risk and correlations, thereby assessing the model’s ability to identify complex cross-document relationships containing subtle signals. Their experiments demonstrated that Longformer could enhance predictive performance through fine-grained modeling of cross-document interactions. Ge et al.19 utilized the FEDformer network to construct a novel commodity price forecasting model. Comparative analysis with other econometric models revealed that FEDformer achieved a significantly lower mean absolute error than baseline approaches. Addressing the challenges of nonlinearity and non-stationarity in financial time series, Kehinde et al.20 proposed a Transformer network integrated with Holt-Winters exponential smoothing, named Helformer. This method employs Optuna for Bayesian hyperparameter optimization and has been verified to possess strong generalization capability and forecasting performance. Babu et al.21 applied the Reformer network to social media data analysis for predicting market trends and consumer demand, with experiments confirming the method’s efficacy and substantial potential for market applications.

Although single models have demonstrated value in financial time series forecasting, they exhibit limitations when handling data characterized by complex nonlinearity, non-stationarity, and high noise. A notable shortcoming is their frequent inability to simultaneously and effectively capture both short-term fluctuations and long-term trends. Consequently, integrating techniques such as signal decomposition with deep learning can better extract complex patterns from financial time series, thereby improving predictive accuracy. Empirical mode decomposition (EMD)22, a classical time-frequency domain signal processing method, decomposes signals based on their intrinsic time scales, facilitating the handling of non-stationary and nonlinear temporal data. In financial time series analysis, EMD and its variants are often combined with various deep learning models to enhance forecasting performance. Ali et al.23 employed a model combining an EMD algorithm based on Akima spline interpolation with an LSTM network for stock prediction, which outperformed baseline models across several statistical metrics. Regarding the Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD) method, Cai et al.24 integrated a CNN-BiLSTM-Attention network to propose an EEMD-based stock return forecasting approach. Features decomposed by EEMD demonstrated satisfactory performance in predicting high-frequency components, revealing the method’s considerable potential in finance. To address the inherent challenges of financial temporal data, Sun et al.25 used the Complete Ensemble EMD with Adaptive Noise (CEEMDAN) algorithm, which precisely selects time windows, to improve LSTM’s forecasting accuracy. Assisted by Bayesian optimization, the CEEMDAN-BO-LSTM network could handle various challenges in financial time series, effectively capturing market volatility and long-term trends even under highly volatile conditions. Ghosh et al.26 combined EEMD with Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) clustering to decompose stock time series data, subsequently employing XGBoost regression and support vector regression (SVR) for prediction. This method was proven to possess commendable effectiveness and robustness. For multi-scale pattern prediction in financial markets, deep learning methods integrated with wavelet decomposition have also shown strong adaptability and forecasting performance. Yan et al.27 introduced a multi-level attention mechanism and adaptive fuzzy granulation technology based on discrete wavelet transform (DWT), utilizing support vector regression for enterprise bankruptcy risk prediction. Their results were significantly superior to other baseline models.

However, feature decomposition methods based on the EMD framework face certain challenges when processing financial temporal data. For instance, the resulting IMF components may contain features from different frequency scales, leading to distorted trend representations. Additionally, the recursive decomposition process is prone to generating edge effects (divergence errors) at the data boundaries. As for wavelet transform methods, issues such as a lack of strict translation invariance and spectral leakage may adversely affect subsequent model predictions. In contrast, the VMD method has demonstrated superior decomposition efficacy in addressing the aforementioned challenges28. Consequently, in financial research, VMD-based feature decomposition methods have been adopted in various forecasting models due to their effectiveness. Guo et al.29 constructed a VMD-ARIMA-TEF hybrid model to enhance the accuracy and stability of financial time series forecasting. This model decomposes the time series via VMD, uses ARIMA to predict each linear component, and finally aggregates the subsequence predictions to form a complete forecast. Experiments proved this method offers significant improvement over other hybrid models. Gao et al.30 adopted a VMD-LSTM hybrid model to improve overall forecasting accuracy. Yao et al.31 combined VMD, SVM, and Backpropagation neural networks to construct a hybrid financial data forecasting model. By leveraging the BP neural network to enhance the VMD algorithm’s sensitivity to fluctuations, this method effectively increased data utilization, thereby improving model prediction accuracy.

In conclusion, despite considerable progress through the extensive exploration of various methods, ranging from traditional models to deep learning and hybrid approaches that incorporate signal decomposition, significant challenges remain in financial time series forecasting. Critical among these are developing more robust signal processing methods, effectively combining the advantages of different models, and concurrently capturing both long- and short-term temporal dependencies. These challenges are particularly acute given the characteristic high noise, non-stationarity, and multi-scale nature of financial data. Addressing these limitations provides the fundamental motivation and entry point for the study undertaken in this work.

Related methods

Signal decomposition for enhanced data reliability via VMD

Financial time series are characterized by pronounced nonlinearity, non-stationarity, and significant market noise. Traditional signal decomposition methods, such as EMD and its variants (e.g., EEMD, CEEMDAN), employ a recursive sifting process. When processing intermittent signals, these methods are highly susceptible to mode mixing, where extracted trend components become entangled with noise, severely compromising the accuracy of subsequent predictive models. While wavelet transforms offer good time-frequency localization, their non-adaptive nature means decomposition efficacy heavily depends on the a priori selection of a wavelet basis function, making them less suitable for the diverse and evolving patterns in financial data.

In contrast, VMD demonstrates distinct advantages for financial data processing. Its design inherently mitigates mode mixing by decomposing a signal into a predefined number K of band-limited IMFs. This is achieved by solving a constrained variational optimization problem that minimizes the sum of the estimated bandwidths of all modes. This frequency-domain constraint effectively separates high-frequency random noise from mid- and low-frequency market trends. Furthermore, the quadratic penalty term α in the VMD formulation functions analogously to a Wiener filter, enhancing its robustness to noise and yielding cleaner modal components for subsequent model learning. Therefore, VMD is employed in this phase to denoise and stabilize the closing price time series, a critical step for mitigating noise-induced unreliability in sustainability-sensitive financial data (e.g., energy futures volatility). Enhanced convolutional self-attention mechanism

The CSA mechanism proposed in this study aims to adapt the high- and low-frequency features derived from VMD decomposition to the input feature dimensions of the BiT network. Simultaneously, it addresses the limitation of standard self-attention in extracting local patterns, thereby better accommodating the complex multi-scale characteristics inherent in financial time series. While standard self-attention excels at capturing global dependencies, VMD-decomposed financial data encompass both high-frequency short-term fluctuations and low-frequency long-term trends. By incorporating a one-dimensional convolutional preprocessing stage, the CSA module effectively extracts local, fine-grained features from the VMD components and integrates them with the global relational modeling capacity of self-attention. In our implementation, the convolution uses a kernel size of 1 (pointwise convolution), performing a learnable linear transformation at each time step independently. This refines the representation of individual positions without aggregating neighboring time steps, while the subsequent self-attention module captures longer-range temporal dependencies. This complementary design, convolution for local feature extraction coupled with attention for global dependency capture, not only enhances the processing of information across different frequency modes but also improves the utilization of key influencing factors through optimized feature composition, ultimately leading to superior prediction accuracy.

Self-attention mechanisms represent a prevalent methodology for sequential data processing, dynamically allocating differential attention weights through inter-element relational analysis to extract and represent sequential features32. This paradigm fundamentally learns inter-element correlations, synthesizing final sequence representations via weighted aggregation of elemental features33. Bidirectional transformer network

The Transformer is a neural architecture based on the attention mechanism, which effectively captures long-range dependencies and offers considerable advantages for processing lengthy sequential data37. Following large-scale pre-training, this framework demonstrates strong generalization capabilities when adapted to various natural language processing tasks38,39. Adjusting key architectural parameters, such as model depth, width, and attention configurations, enables it to accommodate tasks of differing complexity.

To address the limitations of standard Transformer models in managing long sequences and intricate dependencies, this work proposes the BiT network. By integrating a bidirectional structure with the self-attention mechanism, the model directly captures correlations between any two positions in a sequence. This approach effectively mitigates the loss of long-range dependencies, thereby providing a more global perspective of financial market dynamics. Crucially, the model employs parallel forward and backward Transformer blocks to simultaneously learn bidirectional dependencies within the time series. This design allows the model not only to utilize historical information but also to comprehensively understand sequential context through its backward pass, resulting in a more robust feature representation. When processing the multi-scale financial data derived from Variational Mode Decomposition, the integration of this bidirectional modeling with a hierarchical regression architecture achieves a more precise fit to the nonlinear and non-stationary characteristics of the data. Consequently, the proposed framework demonstrates significantly superior prediction accuracy and trend-tracking capability compared to traditional deep learning models such as LSTM.Fig. 2The architecture of the transformer network.

Experiments

Data preparation

All experimental datasets were uniformly sourced from Yahoo Finance to ensure standardization and consistency. Leveraging the platform’s structured data format, we constructed a unified set of 15 endogenous market features, including opening price, high price, low price, trading volume, and turnover. While distinct asset classes are influenced by heterogeneous external factors, gold prices by macroeconomic expectations, equities by corporate fundamentals, the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) posits that such information is largely internalized in historical price and volume data41. Consequently, by learning the nonlinear and non-stationary patterns within this endogenous feature set, the model can capture asset-specific dynamics without relying on asset-specific exogenous variables. This approach supports the adaptability of the framework across diverse financial instruments under a generalized input representation.

To strictly avoid data leakage, the dataset was partitioned into training, validation, and testing subsets prior to any data preprocessing steps. The VMD was applied independently to these subsets. Specifically, the optimization of VMD parameters was determined solely based on the characteristics of the training set, ensuring that no information from the test set was embedded in the model training phase. The specific partition ratios and look-back windows vary by experimental objective, as detailed below.

It should be noted that the benchmark experiment in “Comprehensive performance evaluation against benchmark models” section and the supplementary analyses in “Ablation study on framework component efficacy”, “Multi-timescale analysis via sequence decomposition” and “Robustness and directional accuracy evaluation across varying configurations” sections adopt different experimental protocols because they serve different analytical purposes. “Comprehensive performance evaluation against benchmark models” section is designed to evaluate the overall forecasting performance of the proposed framework across multiple representative financial instruments; therefore, it uses a chronological 90%/10% training/test split and a 60-day look-back window. In contrast, “Ablation study on framework component efficacy”, “Multi-timescale analysis via sequence decomposition” and “Robustness and directional accuracy evaluation across varying configurations” sections focus on ablation analysis, decomposition-based analysis, and robustness evaluation, where the data are chronologically divided into training, validation, and test subsets with a 60%/20%/20% ratio to support explicit validation and unbiased testing. Therefore, the numerical results reported in these sections are not directly comparable across sections; comparative interpretations should be made only within the same experimental setting.

Multi-timescale analysis via sequence decomposition

This section examines the capacity of the VMD–CSA–BiT framework to capture and disentangle multi-scale dynamics in financial time series. The experiment decomposes aggregate price series into distinct components, long-term trend, medium-term cycle, and short-term fluctuation, for independent forecasting. This decomposition-based perspective is motivated by prior evidence that different time-scale components of stock prices are driven by heterogeneous factors such as macroeconomic shocks, market uncertainty, and speculative behavior42. The analysis focuses on two renewable energy stocks: NextEra Energy (NEE, wind and solar power generation), with data from 3 January 2012 to 1 December 2025, and Sunrun Inc. (RUN, residential solar and storage), covering 5 August 2015 to 1 December 2025. Each original price series is adaptively decomposed via Variational Mode Decomposition into a set of Intrinsic Mode Functions, which are grouped by their center frequencies into long-, medium-, and short-term components. These component groups are then independently processed by the forecasting models. To ensure a temporally unbiased evaluation, the data for each stock and component group are chronologically partitioned into training (60%), validation (20%), and test (20%) subsets. The comparative models align with those used in the ablation study, namely the standalone BiT, a standard Transformer, VMD-BiT, VMD-CSA-Transformer, and VMD-Transformer. The evaluation employs MAE, and RMSE.

Conclusions

This study addresses the challenges of nonlinearity, non-stationarity, and high noise in financial time series forecasting by proposing a hybrid framework termed VMD–CSA–BiT, which integrates VMD, an enhanced CSA mechanism, and a BiT. The principal conclusions and contributions are summarized as follows: 1) The design goes beyond a mere combination of existing models; it is specifically tailored to the multi-scale nature of financial data. The novel introduction of an improved Convolutional Self-Attention mechanism strengthens the extraction of local, high-frequency features via one-dimensional convolutional preprocessing, while the Bidirectional Transformer captures long-range global dependencies. This integrated approach enables comprehensive feature learning from microscopic fluctuations to macroscopic trends. 2) Experimental results demonstrate that VMD effectively decomposes the original non-stationary price signal into subcomponents with distinct frequency characteristics. This decomposition supplies subsequent deep learning models with cleaner and more regularized inputs, thereby contributing to enhanced robustness in prediction. 3) Across various financial instruments, the VMD–CSA–BiT model achieves stronger performance on key metrics, including MAE, RMSE, MAPE, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textrm{R}^2$$\end{document}, MD, and DA, compared to the benchmark models considered. It exhibits relatively strong tracking capability and stability during periods of high market volatility.

Several limitations of this study warrant consideration for practical applications: 1) The integration of signal decomposition and a complex neural network architecture results in a high number of parameters and substantial computational demands for both training and inference. Consequently, the framework may face constraints in scenarios requiring ultra-low latency, such as high-frequency trading. 2) Model performance is contingent on the selection of VMD parameters (e.g., the number of modes K and the penalty factor α). Applying the model to new asset classes with divergent characteristics may necessitate parameter re-optimization to avoid the risks of under- or over-decomposition. 3) The current model primarily relies on historical transactional data. The exclusion of external factors, such as macroeconomic policies, breaking news, and investor sentiment, limits its capacity to provide early warnings for abrupt, extreme market events.

Future research will focus on the following extensions: 1) Incorporating unstructured data from financial news and social media sentiment through text-mining techniques to develop a multimodal forecasting model, thereby improving the framework’s sensitivity to market sentiment and unforeseen events. 2) Employing intelligent optimization algorithms to adaptively tune the critical parameters of VMD, enhancing the model’s generalizability and level of automation across different assets. 3) Exploring model compression techniques, such as pruning or knowledge distillation, to reduce computational complexity while maintaining predictive accuracy, thereby facilitating deployment in real-time risk management and high-frequency trading environments. 4) Incorporating multi-criteria evaluation frameworks, such as the S.A.F.E AI Lens44, to assess predictive efficiency, sustainability, and predictor contributions simultaneously, thereby enriching the evaluation of model performance in future studies.
