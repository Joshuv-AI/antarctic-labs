Order-disorder trade-off in dirty quantum systems
Jinmin Yi, Chong Wang

Abstract
We prove a trade-off theorem for order and disorder parameters in one-dimensional quantum spin systems with quenched disorder. For a disordered ensemble with exact Ising symmetry and average translation symmetry, any gapped ensemble must have one and only one of the following: an O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) order parameter or an O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) disorder parameter with even parity, both of the Edwards-Anderson type. The result extends to nearly gapped ensembles that accommodate Griffiths-type rare-region effects. These results offer a powerful and rigorous framework to understand the disorder effects beyond perturbative approaches. As applications, we (1) establish the existence of string order parameters for SPT phases; (2) derive a Lieb-Schultz-Mattis-type constraint for disordered ensembles, which requires a nearly gapped ensemble to spontaneously break the symmetry; and (3) discuss similar trade-off relations for disordered fermion chains, leading to an improved understanding of certain “intrinsically disordered” topological phases.



I
Introduction

Quenched disorder plays a central role in condensed matter physics. In realistic dirty materials, its effects are both unavoidable and profound, ranging from disorder-induced localization to the quantized Hall effect. A key challenge in understanding disorder effects
Sachdev 2011
is that disorder can give rise to intrinsically non-perturbative phenomena like rare region effects that lie beyond the reach of perturbative approaches
Griffiths 1969
;
Vojta 2006
;
Fisher 1995
;
IGLOI and MONTHUS 2005
;
Yi and Burkov 2025
. Developing a rigorous framework for phases of dirty quantum systems therefore requires tools that can handle such non-perturbative effects.

In this work, we establish a rigorous framework for understanding the effects of quenched disorder in the context of one-dimensional quantum spin chain. We focus on systems with an exact Ising symmetry and average translation symmetry, and we study the order and disorder parameters for such systems. The order and disorder parameters are powerful tools for understanding both the symmetry breaking phases and the symmetric topological phases in 1D clean systems, which also provides a useful foundation for the recent advance of the topological holography principle and symmetry topological field theory (SymTFT)
Ji and Wen 2020
;
Chatterjee and Wen 2023
;
Kong et al. 2020
;
Huang and Cheng 2025
.

In a clean chain with Ising symmetry
S=\prod_{i}S_{i}
, we say the system has an order parameter if there exist parity-odd local operators
O_{i}
such that

\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\langle O_{i}O_{j}\rangle\neq 0,

(1)

detecting spontaneous symmetry breaking. Analogously, the system has a disorder parameter if there exist
O_{i}
,
O_{j}
with the same parity under
S
such that the string correlator

\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\langle O_{i}O_{j}\prod_{i<k<j}S_{k}\rangle\neq 0,

(2)

detecting the symmetric phase. Levin
Levin 2020
proved rigorously that any gapped, translationally invariant Ising chain has either an order parameter or a disorder parameter with even parity, but not both.

In this work, we extend this dichotomy to quenched disordered systems. Since correlators can fluctuate in sign across disorder realizations, the correct diagnostic is the disorder average of the absolute value—the analogue of the Edwards-Anderson order parameter
Edwards and Anderson 1975
. Thus for a disorder ensemble
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
with Ising symmetry
S=\prod_{i}S_{i}
we say it has an order parameter if there exist parity-odd local operators
O_{i}
such that

\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\!\left(\left|\langle\Omega_{\omega}|O_{i}O_{j}|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\right|\right)\neq 0,

(3)

where
\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
denotes the average over the ensemble,
and analogously, it has a disorder parameter if there exist local operators
O_{i}
,
O_{j}
such that

\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\!\left(\left|\langle\Omega_{\omega}|O_{i}O_{j}\prod_{i<k<j}S_{k}|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\right|\right)\neq 0.

(4)

Our main result is that the clean dichotomy survives for gapped ensembles (all ground states uniformly gapped in its symmetry sector and in the same phase): any such ensemble has either an order parameter or a disorder parameter, but not both, and the endpoint operators of any disorder parameter must be even under
S
. The proof combines two ingredients: the principle that local perturbations perturb locally (LPPL)
Bachmann et al. 2011
;
De Roeck and Schütz 2015
, which ensures that local order/disorder indicators depend only on nearby disorder variables, and a probabilistic analysis that relates the probabilities of local ordering and disordering events to the existence of global disorder-averaged parameters. The result extends to nearly gapped ensembles that accommodate rare Griffiths regions occurring with exponentially small probability in their size.

Physically, the necessity of taking the absolute value before disorder averaging is straightforward to understand. For the order parameter, a spin-glass phase in which
\langle O_{i}O_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}
acquires random signs across the ensemble causes the first moment to vanish, while the Edwards–Anderson correlator remains finite. Similarly, for the disorder parameter, an “Anderson insulator” phase with localized yet randomly distributed
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
charge produces fluctuating signs of the disorder parameter, so that only the absolute moment survives disorder averaging. In our framework, both the random magnet and the Anderson insulator are viewed as simple gapped ensembles.

Disorder also has a rich interplay with topological phases of matter. In this work we provide a rigorous handle on three examples:

1.

For symmetry-protected topological (SPT)
Chen et al. 2012
phases in
1d
, we show that the Edwards-Anderson type disorder-averaged string order parameters
Kennedy and Tasaki 1992
;
Pérez-García et al. 2008
;
Pollmann and Turner 2012
;
Haegeman et al. 2012
remain well-behaved diagnostics for different phases.

2.

We derive a Lieb-Schultz-Mattis-type constraint
Lieb et al. 1961
;
Oshikawa 2000
;
Hastings 2004
;
Cheng et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2025
;
Yi et al. 2025
;
Gioia and Wang 2022
;
Zou and Cheng 2026
for disordered ensembles
Kimchi et al. 2018
;
Ma and Wang 2023
;
You and Oshikawa 2024
;
Xu and Jian 2025
;
Panahi et al. 2026
;
Li et al. 2026
: if a disorder ensemble has an average translation symmetry and an exact
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry with a projective representation on each site, it cannot be nearly gapped without long-range order—it must develop an order parameter and break some symmetry.

3.

Using Jordan-Wigner transform, the trade-off result can be translated to a similar result on fermion chains, now constraining two types of disorder operators, dressed by bosonic and fermionic operators, respectively. This result can be used to understand a class of “intrinsically disordered” topological phase proposed in Ref.
Ma et al. 2025
.

While many of the above results have been anticipated in some form in earlier literature based on physical arguments, our work puts them on a solid footing – this is particularly valuable for strongly disordered and interacting systems, where non-perturbative effects, such as rare regions, often render conventional methods uncontrolled. Indeed, without a proper understanding of rare-region effects, it was difficult even to formulate the correct statements, let alone establish them rigorously. From this perspective, one important implication of our work is that the notion of a “nearly gapped ensemble” provides the natural generalization of the familiar concept of a “gapped state” in clean systems.

III.1
fixes technical notation, Sec.
III.2

III.6
establish the three parts of the main theorem (existence, mutual exclusivity, and parity of the disorder parameter). In Sec.

we extend these results to nearly gapped ensembles with rare-region effects. In Sec.
V
we present the applications to average SPT string order (Sec.
V.1
), the LSM-type constraint (Sec.
V.2
) and fermion chains (Sec.
V.3
). We conclude with a summary and outlook. Several appendixes contain technical details.

II
Preliminaries and Summary of results

This section sets up the framework for our main results and provides a summary of the key statements.
In Sec.
II.1
we describe the physical setting and introduce the notion of a gapped ensemble. In Sec.
II.2
we define order and disorder parameters for dirty systems. In Sec.
II.3
we review the principle that local perturbations perturb locally (LPPL) and how we model the rare region effects. Sec.
II.4
summarizes the main results.

II.1
Gapped disordered systems

In this work, we focus on spin systems on a 1D lattice with periodic boundary conditions. We consider a Hilbert space with a local tensor product structure
\mathcal{H}=\otimes_{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}
, where
i
labels lattice sites. We study quenched disorder by considering an ensemble of local Hamiltonians
{H_{\omega}}
depending on the disorder realization
\omega
. Concretely, the Hamiltonian
H_{\omega}
for each realization
\omega
takes the form

H_{\omega}=H_{0}+\sum_{i}(v_{i}^{\omega}\mathcal{O}_{i}+h.c.),

(5)

where
v_{i}^{\omega}
denotes the disorder potential at site
i
for realization
\omega
, and
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is a local operator supported near
\omega
. We assume the disorder is at most short-range correlated, namely that
\overline{v_{i}^{*}v_{j}}
decays exponentially with
|i-j|
.

We focus on systems with an exact Ising symmetry

S=\prod_{i}S_{i},\qquad S_{i}^{2}=1,

(6)

so that
[H_{\omega},S]=0
for every realization
\omega
. We also assume average lattice translation symmetry: the clean Hamiltonian
H_{0}
commutes with the translation operator
\hat{T}
,
[H_{0},\hat{T}]=0
, and the disorder distribution is invariant under lattice translations. Thus, while a given realization
H_{\omega}
need not be translationally invariant, the joint distribution of the random potentials is. A standard example is an independent, identically distributed disorder, with the marginal distribution
\mathbb{P}(v_{i})
the same for every site
i
.

We denote by
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
the corresponding ensemble of ground states, which we take to be even under
S
for every realization
\omega
. We call
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
a
gapped ensemble
if, for every realization
\omega
,
H_{\omega}
is gapped in the even symmetry sector with a gap lower-bounded by some constant
\epsilon
, and all
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
are smoothly connected by a quasi-adiabatic evolution, i.e., they lie in the same gapped phase. This rules out mixtures of states from different phases (phase separation), which is also a natural assumption when
v_{i}^{\omega}
is a continuous variable. Note that this notion excludes Griffiths-type rare regions that lead to small gaps; in Sec.
II.3
we relax this to a
nearly gapped ensemble
, where such rare regions are allowed but occur with exponentially small probability in their size.

The assumption that every
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
is even under
S
is purely technical: it ensures the ground state is uniquely determined by
\omega
without having to track degeneracy. Even if
H_{\omega}
undergoes spontaneous symmetry breaking for some
\omega
, the even- and odd-sector ground states are locally indistinguishable — no local operator can tell them apart. Fixing the even-sector ground state therefore does not affect the physical content of our results.

In our notion of a gapped ensemble, the requirement that
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
is a gapped ground state in its own symmetry sector is weaker than the SRE-ensemble condition in the literature of average SPT
Ma et al. 2025
, where each
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
is required to be short-range entangled. Our setting allows, for example, an Ising ferromagnet with
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
in a GHZ sector.

II.2
Order and disorder parameters

We now give precise definitions of order and disorder parameters for a disordered ensemble. Intuitively, this is just the ensemble average of the absolute value of the order and disorder parameters defined for a single state as addressed in the introduction, in the spirit of the Edwards-Anderson order parameter for spin glasses
Edwards and Anderson 1975
. We now state the following quantitative definitions for the order parameter. Throughout,
\left\|\cdot\right\|
denotes the operator norm, i.e., the largest singular value of an operator.

Definition 1

(Order parameter)
.

A disorder ensemble
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
is called to have a
(\delta,\ell)
order parameter if, for any two sites
i
and
j
with
\mathrm{dist}(i,j)>2\ell
, there exist operators
O_{i,j}
, such that

1.

O_{i,j}
is odd under
S
.

2.

O_{i}
is supported on
R_{i}=[i+1,i+\ell]
and likewise for
O_{j}
.

3.

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(|\langle\Omega_{\omega}|O_{i}O_{j}|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle|)\geq\delta
, where
\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
is the ensemble average over disorder realizations.

4.

\left\|O_{i,j}\right\|\leq 1
.

Similarly, we can define the disorder parameter:

Definition 2

(Disorder parameter)
.

A disorder ensemble
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
is called to have a
(\delta,\ell)
disorder parameter if, for any two sites
i
and
j
with
\mathrm{dist}(i,j)>2\ell
, there exist operators
O_{i,j}
, such that

1.

O_{i}
and
O_{j}
are both even under
S
or both odd under
S
.

2.

O_{i}
is supported on
R_{i}=[i+1,i+\ell]
and likewise for
O_{j}
.

3.

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\left|\langle\Omega_{\omega}|O_{i}O_{j}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j}S_{k}|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\right|\right)\geq\delta
, where
\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
is the ensemble average over the disorder distribution.

4.

\left\|O_{i,j}\right\|\leq 1
.

The endpoint operators
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
are either both even or both odd under
S
, so their parity is well-defined. We say the disorder parameter has
even parity
if
O_{i}
is even under
S
, and
odd parity
if
O_{i}
is odd under
S
. For brevity, we say a system has an
O(1)
order (resp. disorder) parameter if it has a
(\delta,\ell)
order (resp. disorder) parameter for some
\delta,\ell=O(1)
independent of system size.

In the clean limit, where there is no disorder potential, these definitions reduce to the order and disorder parameters introduced in Ref.
Levin 2020
, which characterize the symmetry-broken and symmetric phases, respectively. In particular, Ref.
Levin 2020
showed that for a translational symmetric clean system that is gapped in each symmetry sector, the symmetric ground state must admit either an order parameter or a disorder parameter, but not both, and any disorder parameter must be parity even.

II.3
LPPL and rare region effects

One key assumption of this work is the principle of “local perturbation perturbs locally” (LPPL)
Bachmann et al. 2011
;
De Roeck and Schütz 2015
, which we now review. Precisely, suppose the disorder strengths
v_{i}^{\omega}
are continuously tunable and the spectral gap remains uniformly bounded below throughout. If one modifies the disorder strengths only on a region
R
, passing from realization
\omega
to
\omega^{\prime}
, then the ground states before and after the change are related by a symmetric quasi-local unitary
U_{R}
supported near
R

Hastings and Wen 2005
;
Bravyi et al. 2010
;
Bachmann et al. 2011
;
Haah et al. 2021
;
Yi et al. 2026
,

|\Omega_{\omega^{\prime}}\rangle=U_{R}\,|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle,

(7)

with
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
and
|\Omega_{\omega^{\prime}}\rangle
being the parity even ground states of
H_{\omega}
and
H_{\omega^{\prime}}
, respectively.

Consequently, if one considers any local operator
O_{R}
supported in the region
R
, its expectation value
\langle\Omega_{\omega}|O_{R}|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
should only depend on the value of
v_{i}^{\omega}
for sites
i
sufficiently close to the region
R
, i.e.,

i\in R^{+\xi}\coloneqq\{j|\mathrm{dist}(j,R)\leq\xi\}\;,

(8)

An immediate consequence is that the expectation value of any operator
O_{R}
supported on
R
depends only on the disorder strengths within the enlarged region
R^{+\xi}\coloneqq\{j\mid\mathrm{dist}(j,R)\leq\xi\}
, where
\xi
is the correlation length: modifying
v_{i}^{\omega}
outside
R^{+\xi}
induces a unitary supported outside
R
, which commutes with
O_{R}
and leaves
\langle\Omega_{\omega}|O_{R}|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
unchanged. Throughout, we approximate
U_{R}
as strictly local on a finite neighborhood of
R
, dropping its exponentially decaying tail. This does not affect our results, and a more precise estimate of the approximation error can be found in Refs.
Haah et al. 2021
;
Yi et al. 2026
.

We now specify the class of rare regions we consider. We define a rare region as a spatial region
R
on which the system fails to be gapped locally, but where the ground state outside of
R
still resembles a gapped ground state. For simplicity, we assume that the probability of such a rare region to occur is exponentially small in its size, i.e., as
p^{|R|}
for some small
p<1
. More precisely, if
|\Omega^{\prime}\rangle
denotes a state containing a rare region supported on
R
, we model it as

|\Omega^{\prime}\rangle=U_{R}|\Omega\rangle,

(9)

where
|\Omega\rangle
is a parity-even gapped ground state and
U_{R}
is a symmetric unitary supported on region
R
and is not necessarily quasi-local within
R
. This decomposition allows rare regions to act as defects on the region
R
while preserving the gapped structure elsewhere. The physical example we have in mind is the Griffiths phase in Ising model away from critical point, where a large region
R
may realize a different phase than the rest of the system due to rare disorder configurations. Although the probability of the rare region effect decays with
|R|
, its effect enhances as
R
becomes larger – for example, the gap of the system may decay rapidly with
R
, rendering the system effectively gapless. We therefore call such ensembles
nearly gapped
. More precisely,

Definition 3
.

An ensemble of ground states is nearly gapped if it is a mixture of parity-even gapped states and rare-region states, with the probability of the rare-region states decaying exponentially in the size of the rare region.

As we shall discuss later in Sec.
IV
, the results in this work is unaffected by such rare regions and hold generally for
nearly gapped ensembles
.

The exponential suppression of the rare region probability
p^{|R|}
is closely related to the LPPL principle: if LPPL holds, then the appearance of a large rare region
R
requires all the random potential inside
R
to take some rare value, which must come with probability
\sim p^{|R|}
. A class of counter examples is the infinite-randomness fixed points, including the random-singlet state
Fisher 1994
and the critical random transverse-field Ising model
Fisher 1995
, in which the probability for establishing a large rare region only decays algebraically, and is therefore not considered as nearly gapped states.

II.4
Summary of results

We now summarize the key results in this work. We also point out the sections that discuss these statements in detail. This section can be viewed as a road map of the work.

(1) A gapped ensemble of disordered systems must admit either an
O(1)
order parameter or an
O(1)
disorder parameter, but not both. Moreover, any disorder parameter must be parity even.

Theorem 1
.

A gapped ensemble
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
has one and only one of the following: an
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter or an
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter with even parity, for large enough
O(1)
constants
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
, where
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
satisfy the following trade-off relation:

\displaystyle c_{\eta}\sqrt{\eta}+c_{\eta}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime 1/3}>1.

(10)

where
c_{\eta}
and
c_{\eta}^{\prime}
are
O(1)
constants independent of the system size.

The precise requirements on these
O(1)
constants are given in Proposition
1
in Sec.
III.4.3
.

The theorem has three parts: existence (the ensemble has at least one of the two: order parameter or disorder parameter, see Sec.
III.4
), mutual exclusivity (it cannot have both, see Sec.
III.5
), and the parity constraint (any disorder parameter must be even-parity, see Sec.
III.6
).

(2) Our trade-off theorem still holds with the presence of rare regions, as long as the probability of rare regions decays sufficiently fast with their size, as shown in Sec.
IV
. Similar to the gapped case, in Sec.
IV.1
we provide the proof of the existence of at least one of the two parameters, and in Sec.
IV.2
we show that they cannot coexist. The parity constraint for disorder parameters also continues to hold in the presence of rare regions, as shown in Sec.
IV.3
.

(3) Our definition of the disorder parameters can be applied to characterize SPT phases. In particular, we demonstrate this for a disordered system in an SPT phase with exact
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry and average translational symmetry. Precisely, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 1
.

Let
\{H_{\omega}\}
be a disordered average-SPT ensemble with average translational
symmetry and exact
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry
S^{e}
and
S^{o}
, with

S^{e}=\prod_{i}Z_{2i},\quad S^{o}=\prod_{i}Z_{2i+1}.

(11)

Then, for any sites
i<j
, there exist local operators

O_{i}
and
O_{j}
, supported near
i
and
j
respectively, such that

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\left|\bigl\langle O_{i}O_{j}\prod_{i<2k<j}Z_{2k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\right)=O(1).

(12)

Similarly, there exist local operators
O_{i}^{\prime}
and
O_{j}^{\prime}
, supported near

i
and
j
respectively, such that

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\left|\bigl\langle O_{i}^{\prime}O_{j}^{\prime}\prod_{i<2k+1<j}Z_{2k+1}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\right)=O(1).

(13)

Moreover, for any fixed ensemble
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
, the symmetry parities of these disorder parameters are uniquely determined. More precisely,
the operators
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
must be even under
S^{e}
, and the operators

O_{i}^{\prime}
and
O_{j}^{\prime}
must be even under
S^{o}
. In addition, the parities of

O_{i},O_{j}
under
S^{o}
and of
O_{i}^{\prime},O_{j}^{\prime}
under
S^{e}
are the same, and this parity is uniquely fixed by the ensemble.

This provides a concrete application of our main theorem and suggests that string order parameters remain a powerful diagnostic tool for characterizing SPT phases even in dirty quantum systems. See Sec.
V.1
for the proof and further discussion.

(4) Our framework also allows us to derive a Lieb-Schultz-Mattis-type constraint for disordered ensembles. Precisely, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 2
.

Let
\{H_{\omega}\}
be a nearly gapped disordered ensemble with average translational symmetry and an exact
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry
S^{x}
and
S^{z}
on a periodic chain of even length, with

S^{x}=\prod_{i}X_{i},\quad S^{z}=\prod_{i}Z_{i},

(14)

Then
\{H_{\omega}\}
must have an
O(1)
order parameter for either
S^{x}
or
S^{z}
.

This result can be viewed as a disordered analogue of the Lieb-Schultz-Mattis (LSM) theorem, in the spirit that under certain symmetry constraints, the system cannot be gapped and symmetric without long-range order. In this case, the presence of an average translation symmetry and a projective representation of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry on each site forces the system to develop an order parameter, ruling out the possibility of a symmetric, gapped phase without long-range order. See Sec.
V.2
for the proof and further discussion.

(5)Through a Jordan-Wigner transform, we can translate our results on Ising spin chains to fermion chains. Specifically, we have

Corollary 3
.

For a fermion chain in a nearly gapped ground state ensemble, there is always some disorder parameter

\lim_{|i_{1}-i_{2}|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|\langle O_{1}O_{2}\prod_{i_{1}\leq k\leq i_{2}}F_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|=O(1),

(15)

where
F_{k}
is the local fermion parity on site
k
. For a given nearly gapped ensemble, the dressing local operators
O_{1,2}
are either always bosonic (a trivial phase) or always fermionic (a Kitaev chain).

This shows that string operators remain well-behaved diagnostics for
1d
fermionic topological phases.

The above result can be used to understand a class of “intrinsically disordered” topological phase proposed in Ref.
Ma et al. 2025
. The simplest example is a Kitaev chain, but with an additional average
U(1)
symmetry
c_{i}\to e^{i\alpha}c_{i}
. This average symmetry would forbid a first-moment fermionic disorder parameter:
\lim_{|i_{1}-i_{2}|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\langle O_{1}O_{2}\prod_{i_{1}\leq k\leq i_{2}}F_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}=0
, but does not forbid the Edwards-Anderson type of disorder parameter. This is why such phase can only appear in disordered ensembles, where the
U(1)
symmetry only holds on average and first-moment and Edwards-Anderson disorder parameters can behave very differently.

For more details on fermion chains see Sec.
V.3
.

III
Order-disorder trade-off for gapped ensembles

In this section we prove our main theorem constraining the order and disorder parameters for gapped ensemble without rare region effects. The argument proceeds in three steps. First, we show that the system must exhibit either an order parameter or a disorder parameter. Second, we show that these two possibilities are mutually exclusive. Third, we prove that any disorder parameter must be parity even.

The proof is probabilistic in nature. We regard the disorder potentials
\{v_{i}^{\omega}\}
as random variables, and local observables evaluated in a fixed ground state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
as events that depend only on the disorder variables near their support.

In Sec.
III.1
, we establish the technical setup, introducing notions of strong and weak order and disorder on disjoint intervals and reviewing the foundational lemmas. In Sec.
III.2
, we define two families of probabilistic events
\{A_{i}\}
and
\{B_{i}\}
, corresponding to the ground state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
being ordered or disordered near site
i
. In Sec.
III.3
, we show that the complementary events
\overline{A_{i}}
and
\overline{B_{i}}
are mutually incompatible, i.e., at least one of
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
must occur for each
i
. In Sec.
III.4
, we use these events to prove that the system must have either an
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter or an
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter, with
\eta,\eta^{\prime},\ell,\ell^{\prime}
being
O(1)
constants independent of system size. In Sec.
III.5
, we show that the system cannot simultaneously possess both an order and a disorder parameter. In Sec.
III.6
, we show that any disorder parameter must be parity even.

III.1
Technical setup for the proof

We begin by establishing key definitions and notations required for the proof. Following Ref.
Levin 2020
, we define notions of strong and weak order and disorder on disjoint intervals for an individual state. These concepts are essential for characterizing the occurrence of order and disorder parameters. We also review several foundational lemmas that underpin our main arguments.

For any subset
X\subset\{1,\ldots,L\}
, define the restricted symmetry operator

S_{X}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in X}S_{i}\;.

(16)

We now introduce the notion of strong or weak ordering for an individual state
|\psi\rangle
:

Definition 4
.

A state
|\psi\rangle
is
\delta
strongly-ordered on disjoint intervals
I_{1},I_{2}
if there exist operators
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
such that:

1.

O_{1}
and
O_{2}
are supported on
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.

2.

O_{1}
and
O_{2}
are odd under
S
.

3.

\left|\left\langle O_{1}O_{2}\right\rangle_{\psi}\right|\geq\delta.

4.

\left\|O_{1}\right\|,\left\|O_{2}\right\|\leq 1
.

Definition 5
.

A
state
|\psi\rangle
is
\delta
weakly-ordered on disjoint intervals
I_{1},I_{2}
if there exists an operator A such that:

1.

A
is supported on
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
.

2.

A
is odd under
S_{I_{1}}
and
S_{I_{2}}
.

3.

\left|\langle A\rangle_{\psi}\right|\geq\delta
.

4.

\|A\|\leq 1
.

Note that the only difference between the strong or weak ordering is that for the weak ordering, we do not require a tensor product structure of the operator
A
.

The notion of strong or weak disorder is defined similarly:

Definition 6
.

A state
|\psi\rangle
is
\delta
strongly-disordered on disjoint, non-adjacent intervals
I_{1},I_{2}
if there exist operators
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
such that

1.

O_{1}
and
O_{2}
are supported on
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.

2.

O_{1}
and
O_{2}
are both even or both odd under
S
.

3.

\left|\left\langle O_{1}O_{2}S_{J}\right\rangle_{\psi}\right|\geq\delta
where
J
is the interval between
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, going clockwise.

4.

\left\|O_{1}\right\|,\left\|O_{2}\right\|\leq 1
.

Notice that
O_{1},O_{2}
are either both even or both odd under
S
: in the former case, we will say that
|\psi\rangle
is
\delta
strongly-disordered with even parity, while in the latter case we will say that
|\psi\rangle
is
\delta
strongly-disordered with odd parity.

Definition 7
.

A state
|\psi\rangle
is
\delta
weakly-disordered on disjoint, non-adjacent intervals
I_{1},I_{2}
if there exists an operator
A
such that

1.

A
is supported on
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
.

2.

A
is even under both
S_{I_{1}},S_{I_{2}}
or odd under both
S_{I_{1}},S_{I_{2}}
.

3.

\left|\left\langle AS_{J}\right\rangle_{\psi}\right|\geq\delta
where
J
is the interval between
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, going clockwise.

4.

\|A\|\leq 1
.

Similarly to above, we will say that
|\psi\rangle
is
\delta
weakly-disordered with even or odd parity depending on whether
A
is even or odd under
S_{I_{1}},S_{I_{2}}
.

Adapted from Ref.
Levin 2020
. Example of
I^{\pm}
notation: the spins in the interval
I
are shown in black; the spins in
I^{-}
and
I^{+}
are shown in red and yellow; and the spins in
I^{\prime}\equiv\left(I^{-}\cup I\cup I^{+}\right)^{c}
are shown in blue. The region
I^{\prime}
is buffered from
I
by
I^{\pm}
.

We will also need the following notation for intervals. Given an interval
I\subset\{1,\ldots,L\}
and an interval
I^{\prime}
not adjacent to
I
, let
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
denote the two intervals between
I
and
I^{\prime}
in the clockwise and counterclockwise directions, respectively (see Fig.
1
). In general, we take
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
to be of the same size and larger than
I
.

Fix an integer
\ell>0
and
\ell^{\prime}=O(\ell)
, both of the same order as the correlation length and to be chosen later, and for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,L\}
, define

\displaystyle I_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[i+1,i+\ell]

(17)

\displaystyle I_{i}^{-}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[i-\ell^{\prime}+1,i]

(18)

\displaystyle I_{i}^{+}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[i+\ell+1,i+\ell+\ell^{\prime}]

(19)

\displaystyle I_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(I^{-}_{i}\cup I_{i}\cup I_{i}^{+})^{c}

(20)

where we take the periodic boundary condition.

It is worth highlighting that for a gapped ground state, the weak (dis)order can be upgraded to a strong (dis)order, as demonstrated by the following lemma:

Lemma 1

(Lemma 2, Ref.
Levin 2020
)
.

For every
\delta>0
, there exists a length
\lambda_{\delta}=\tilde{O}\left(1/\varepsilon^{2}\right)
such that for an
S
-symmetric ground state
|\Omega\rangle
with gap
\epsilon
in its symmetry sector, if it is
\delta
weakly-ordered on
I_{1},I_{2}
, then it is

\delta/2
strongly-ordered on
I_{1},I_{2}
for all
I_{1},I_{2}
that are separated by
a distance of at least
\lambda_{\delta}
. Likewise, if
|\Omega\rangle
is
\delta

weakly-disordered on
I_{1},I_{2}
with even (odd) parity, then it is
\delta/2

strongly-disordered on
I_{1},I_{2}
with even (odd) parity for all
I_{1},I_{2}

separated by at least
\lambda_{\delta}
.

We remark that the weak-to-strong upgrade for the order parameters is included here for completeness only, following the framework of Ref.
Levin 2020
. The rest of this work only relies on the fact that a weak disorder parameter can be upgraded to a strong disorder parameter.

III.2
Probabilistic events for order and disorder

Consider the disorder potential
\{v_{i}^{\omega}\}
as a set of random variables which uniquely determines the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
in the disorder ensemble, we define the following two sets of probabilistic events
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
that are conditions on local observables for the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
.

We first define the family of events associated with ordering. Let
A_{i}
be the event that, for a disorder realization
\omega
, the corresponding state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
has mutual information between
I_{i}
and
I_{i}^{\prime}
bounded from below:

\begin{split}I_{\omega}(I_{i}:I_{i}^{\prime})&=S_{\omega}(I_{i})+S_{\omega}(I_{i}^{-}I_{i}I_{i}^{+})-S_{\omega}(I_{i}^{-}I_{i}^{+})\\
&>\frac{1}{32}\;,\end{split}

(21)

where
S_{\omega}(I)
denotes the von Neumann entropy of the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
restricted to the region
I
. The specific choice of the lower bound
1/32
is made for convenience in the later analysis.

Next, we define the family of events associated with disordering. Let
B_{i}
be the event that

\max_{\mathrm{supp}(U)\subset I_{i}^{-}\cup I_{i}^{+}}\left|\langle\Omega_{\omega}|US_{I_{i}}|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(22)

Again, the choice of threshold
1/2
is for later convenience.

Both
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
depend on
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
only through its reduced density matrix on the region
I_{i}^{-}\cup I_{i}\cup I_{i}^{+}
. By the LPPL principle, this reduced density matrix depends only on the disorder variables
v_{j}^{\omega}
with
j
close to that region, namely within the distance
O(\ell+\ell^{\prime}+\xi)=O(\ell)
from the site
i
. Therefore, if
\mathrm{dist}(i,j)\gg\ell
, the events
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
depend on disjoint sets of disorder variables and are independent; the same holds for
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
.

In the following, we discuss in detail the physical meaning of events
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
, and how they relate to the ordering and disordering of the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
near the site
i
.

III.2.1
The events for ordering:
\{A_{i}\}

We first summarize the physical content of
A_{i}
before giving the rigorous proof. Informally, if
A_{i}
occurs, the state has nontrivial long-range correlations between
I_{i}
and its complement
I_{i}^{\prime}
, signaling local ordering near site
i
. Moreover, if both
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
occur for two well-separated points
i
and
j
, the local ordering near
i
and
j
induces an order parameter on
I_{i}
and
I_{j}
.

Suppose
A_{i}
occurs for a state
|\Omega\rangle
; for simplicity, we suppress the disorder label
\omega
. Then

I_{\Omega}(I_{i}:I_{i}^{\prime})=S(I_{i}|I_{i}^{\prime})_{\Omega_{I_{i}}\otimes\Omega_{I_{i}^{\prime}}}-S(I_{i}|I^{\prime}_{i})_{\Omega}>\frac{1}{32}

(23)

where the conditional entropy of a bipartite state
\sigma_{AB}
is defined by

S(A|B)_{\sigma}\coloneqq S_{\sigma}(AB)-S_{\sigma}(B),

(24)

with
S_{\sigma}(X)
the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix
\sigma_{X}
on subsystem
X
.

The conditional entropy is continuous in the density matrix
Alicki and Fannes 2004
;
Winter 2016
, as quantified by the following Fannes-type inequality:

Lemma 2

(Lemma 2, Ref.
Winter 2016
)
.

For states
\rho
and
\sigma
on a Hilbert space
A\otimes B
if
\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}\leq\epsilon\leq 2
, then

\left|S(A|B)_{\rho}-S(A|B)_{\sigma}\right|\leq\epsilon\log|A|+2H_{2}\left(\epsilon/2\right)

(25)

where
H_{2}(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)
, and the trace-norm
\left\|A\right\|_{1}\coloneqq{\rm tr}\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}
.

Since mutual information is nonnegative, Lemma.
2
implies:

I_{\Omega}(I_{i}:I_{i}^{\prime})\leq\varepsilon\ell+2H_{2}(\varepsilon/2)

(26)

where
\varepsilon=\left\|\Omega_{I_{i}}\otimes\Omega_{I_{i}^{\prime}}-\Omega_{I_{i}I_{i}^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}
. Combining (
21
) and (
26
), we have

\varepsilon\ell+2H_{2}(\varepsilon/2)>\frac{1}{32}\;,

(27)

where

\varepsilon\coloneqq\left\|\Omega_{I_{i}}\otimes\Omega_{I_{i}^{\prime}}-\Omega_{I_{i}I_{i}^{\prime}}\right\|_{1},

(28)

and we denote the solution to this inequality as
\varepsilon>\varepsilon_{\ell}
.

The trace norm
\varepsilon
can in turn be bounded by connected correlation functions.

Lemma 3

(Lemma 20 of Ref.
Brandão and Horodecki 2014
)
.

For every bounded operator
L\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{D})
with
d\leq D
,

\|L\|_{1}\leq d^{2}\max_{\|X\|\leq 1,\|Y\|\leq 1}\left|{\rm tr}\bigl((X\otimes Y)L\bigr)\right|.

(29)

Applying this with

L=\Omega_{I_{i}}\otimes\Omega_{I_{i}^{\prime}}-\Omega_{I_{i}I_{i}^{\prime}},

(30)

we find that there exist unit-norm operators
X
and
Y
supported on
I_{i}
and
I_{i}^{\prime}
, respectively, such that

\begin{split}&|\langle\Omega|XY|\Omega\rangle-\langle\Omega|X|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|Y|\Omega\rangle|\\
\geq&2^{-2\ell}\left\|\Omega_{I_{i}}\otimes\Omega_{I_{i}^{\prime}}-\Omega_{I_{i}I_{i}^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}>2^{-2\ell}\varepsilon_{\ell}\end{split}

(31)

We now decompose
X
and
Y
into components with definite parity under the Ising symmetry:

\displaystyle X_{\pm}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(X\pm SXS),

(32)

\displaystyle Y_{\pm}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(Y\pm SYS).

(33)

Since
|\Omega\rangle
has a definte parity, the expectation value of any odd operator vanishes. Therefore,

\begin{split}&\left|\langle\Omega|XY|\Omega\rangle-\langle\Omega|X|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|Y|\Omega\rangle\right|\\
&=\Bigl|\langle\Omega|X_{-}Y_{-}|\Omega\rangle+\langle\Omega|X_{+}Y_{+}|\Omega\rangle-\langle\Omega|X_{+}|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|Y_{+}|\Omega\rangle\Bigr|\\
&\geq|\langle\Omega|X_{-}Y_{-}|\Omega\rangle|-\left|\langle\Omega|X_{+}Y_{+}|\Omega\rangle-\langle\Omega|X_{+}|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|Y_{+}|\Omega\rangle\right|.\end{split}

(34)

The first term is of the form expected for an order parameter, while the second is a connected correlator of parity-even operators and should decay exponentially with separation.

To bound the second term, we use the cluster decomposition theorem for even operators.

Lemma 4

(Proposition 2 of Ref.
Levin 2020
)
.

Let
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
be operators that are even under
S
, have norm at most
1
, and are supported on intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
separated by distance at least
\ell^{\prime}
. Then

\left|\langle O_{1}O_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}-\langle O_{1}\rangle_{\Omega}\langle O_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\leq(6+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{g(\ell^{\prime})}+g(\ell^{\prime}),

(35)

where

g(\ell^{\prime})=\mathrm{poly}(\ell^{\prime},\epsilon^{-1})e^{-c_{1}\epsilon\ell^{\prime}}

(36)

for some numerical constant
c_{1}>0
, and
\epsilon
is the energy gap within the even subspace.

Since
\mathrm{supp}(X_{+})\subset I_{i}
and
\mathrm{supp}(Y_{+})\subset I_{i}^{\prime}
, the separation between their supports is at least
\ell^{\prime}
. Because
g(\ell^{\prime})
decays exponentially, for any fixed
\ell
one can choose
\ell^{\prime}=O(\ell)
such that

(6+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{g(\ell^{\prime})}+g(\ell^{\prime})\leq 2^{-2\ell-1}\varepsilon_{\ell}.

(37)

With this choice of
\ell^{\prime}
, Eq. (
34
) implies that there exist odd operators
X_{-}
and
Y_{-}
such that

\begin{split}|\langle\Omega|X_{-}Y_{-}|\Omega\rangle|&\geq 2^{-2\ell}\varepsilon_{\ell}-\left((6+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{g(\ell^{\prime})}+g(\ell^{\prime})\right)\\
&\geq 2^{-2\ell-1}\varepsilon_{\ell}.\end{split}

(38)

Thus the event
A_{i}
implies a nontrivial odd correlator between
I_{i}
and
I_{i}^{\prime}
. Since both
\ell
and
\varepsilon_{\ell}
are
O(1)
, this is an
O(1)
lower bound. However,
I_{i}^{\prime}
has support comparable to the system size, so this correlator is not yet an order parameter in the sense of Definition.
1
.

To convert it into a genuine order parameter for the state
|\Omega\rangle
, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 5

(Extension of Lemma 3, Ref.
Levin 2020
)
.

Let
O_{1},O_{1}^{\prime},O_{2},O_{2}^{\prime}
be operators supported on intervals
I_{1},I_{1}^{\prime},I_{2},I_{2}^{\prime}
respectively, all either odd or even under
S
, with norm at most
1
. Define

\ell_{\min}=\min\bigl(\mathrm{dist}(I_{1},I_{1}^{\prime}),\mathrm{dist}(I_{1}^{\prime},I_{2}^{\prime}),\mathrm{dist}(I_{1},I_{2})\bigr).

(39)

Then

\left|\langle O_{1}O_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\geq\left|\langle O_{1}O_{1}^{\prime}\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\left|\langle O_{2}O_{2}^{\prime}\rangle_{\Omega}\right|-f(\ell_{\min}),

(40)

and

\begin{split}&\left|\left\langle O_{1}O_{2}S_{J\cup I_{2}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\\
\geq&\left|\left\langle O_{1}O_{1}^{\prime}S_{I_{1}^{+}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\left|\left\langle O_{2}O_{2}^{\prime}S_{I_{2}^{+}}\right\rangle_{\Omega}\right|-f(\ell_{\min}),\end{split}

(41)

where
f(\ell)=\mathrm{poly}(\ell,\epsilon^{-1})e^{-c\epsilon\ell}
,
J
is the interval between
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
in the clockwise direction, and
I_{1(2)}^{+}
is the interval between
I_{1(2)}
and
I_{1(2)}^{\prime}
in the clockwise direction.

The proof of this extension is essentially identical to that of Lemma 3 in Ref.
Levin 2020
, with the generalization that we do not impose constraints on the separation between regions
I_{i}
and
I_{i}^{\prime}
. We therefore omit the details here.

Now suppose two events
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
occur with
\mathrm{dist}(i,j)\geq\ell^{\prime}
. We choose
O_{i},O_{i}^{\prime}
to be the odd operators
X_{-},Y_{-}
obtained above for site
i
, and similarly define
O_{j},O_{j}^{\prime}
for site
j
. If
\ell^{\prime}
is chosen large enough that

f(\ell^{\prime})\leq\frac{(2^{-2\ell-1}\varepsilon_{\ell})^{2}}{2},

(42)

which is possible if one takes
\ell^{\prime}/\ell=O(1)
sufficiently large since
f(\ell^{\prime})
decays exponentially, then Lemma
5
yields odd operators
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
such that

\left|\langle O_{i}O_{j}\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\geq 2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}.

(43)

Therefore, whenever two well-separated events
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
occur, the state exhibits an order parameter between the corresponding regions.

III.2.2
The events for disordering:
\{B_{i}\}

We first summarize the physical content of
B_{i}
before giving the rigorous argument. Informally, if
B_{i}
occurs, the state has a nontrivial disorder parameter on
I_{i}^{-}
and
I_{i}^{+}
, signaling that the system is disordered near site
i
. Moreover, if
B_{i,j,k}
all occur for three well-separated sites
i
,
j
,
k
, the local disordering near those sites induces a disorder parameter on at least two of the three regions
I_{i,j,k}^{-}
.

To see that the system is disordered near site
i
when
B_{i}
occurs, recall that if
B_{i}
occurs, then

\max_{\mathrm{supp}(U)\subset I_{i}^{-}\cup I_{i}^{+}}|\langle\Omega_{\omega}|US_{I_{i}}|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle|\geq\frac{1}{2}\;.

(44)

To single out the disorder parameters with definite parity, we can decompose
U
into components with even and odd parity on
I_{i}^{\pm}
, i.e.,

U_{\pm}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\left(U_{*}+SU_{*}S\right)\pm\frac{1}{4}S_{I_{i}^{+}}\left(U_{*}+SU_{*}S\right)S_{I_{i}^{+}}\;,

(45)

where
U_{*}
is the maximal choice of
U
. By assumption,

\left|\left\langle\left(U_{+}+U_{-}\right)S_{I_{i}}\right\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq 1/2
. Hence either

\left|\left\langle U_{+}S_{I_{i}}\right\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq 1/4
or

\left|\left\langle U_{-}S_{I_{i}}\right\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq 1/4
.
Let
U_{a}
denote whichever of
U_{+}
or
U_{-}
gives the larger expectation value.
It is straightforward to check that
U_{a}
is supported on
I_{i}^{-}\cup I_{i}^{+}
,
has definite even/odd parity under
S_{I_{i}^{-}}
and
S_{I_{i}^{+}}
, and satisfies

\|U_{a}\|\leq 1
. Thus
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
is 1/4 weakly-disordered on
I_{i}^{-},I_{i}^{+}
. By Lemma
1
, if
\ell
is chosen large enough that
\ell\geq\lambda_{1/4}
, then
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
is
1/8
strongly-disordered on
I_{i}^{-},I_{i}^{+}
with the same parity as
U_{a}
.

Similarly to the case for
\{A_{i}\}
, we can upgrade this local disordering to a non-local disordering when multiple events occur at the same time. Suppose three events
B_{i,j,k}
occur with the minimum distance between any two events larger than
\ell
, by the pigeonhole principle, there must be two events, say
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
, such that
O_{i}^{\pm}
and
O_{j}^{\pm}
share the same parity. We can then apply Lemma.
5
to obtain a disorder parameter between
I_{i}^{-}
and
I_{j}^{-}
. Concretely, if
\ell
is chosen large enough that

f(\ell)\leq\frac{1}{128},

(46)

then Lemma
5
implies for operators
O_{i}^{-}
and
O_{j}^{-}
,

\begin{split}&\left|\langle O_{i}^{-}O_{j}^{-}S_{J\cup I_{j}^{-}}\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\\
\geq&\left|\langle O_{i}^{-}O_{i}^{+}S_{I_{i}}\rangle_{\Omega}\right|\left|\langle O_{j}^{-}O_{j}^{+}S_{I_{j}}\rangle_{\Omega}\right|-f(\ell)\geq\frac{1}{128}.\end{split}

(47)

Thus the occurrence of three well-separated events
B_{i}
,
B_{j}
and
B_{k}
implies the existence of a disorder parameter between at least two of the three regions
I_{i}^{-},I_{j}^{-},I_{k}^{-}
.

III.3
Incompatibility of the events
\overline{A_{i}}
and
\overline{B_{i}}

We now show that the events
\overline{A_{i}}
and
\overline{B_{i}}
are not compatible with each other, i.e., at least one of
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
must happen. The proof is identical to that in Ref.
Levin 2020
, and we include it here for completeness.

We prove by contradiction. Assume that both
\overline{A_{i}}
and
\overline{B_{i}}
occur for a specific state
|\Omega\rangle
. Consider another state
|\Omega^{\prime}\rangle=S_{I_{i}}|\Omega\rangle
and consider their reduced density matrix on
X=I_{i}\cup I_{i}^{\prime}
. By the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality
Fuchs and van de
Graaf 1998

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\Omega_{X}-\Omega_{X}^{\prime}\right\|_{1}+F(\Omega_{X},\Omega_{X}^{\prime})\geq 1\;,

(48)

where the fidelity
F(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\left({\rm tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}\right)^{2}
.

For the first term, note that

\begin{split}\left\|\Omega_{X}-\Omega_{X}^{\prime}\right\|_{1}&=\max_{\mathrm{supp}(A)\subset X}|\langle\Omega|A|\Omega\rangle-\langle\Omega^{\prime}|A|\Omega^{\prime}\rangle|\\
&=\max_{\mathrm{supp}(A)\subset X}|\langle\Omega|A-S_{I_{i}}AS_{I_{i}}|\Omega\rangle|\end{split}

(49)

and the second term can be related to event
B_{i}
by considering the Uhlmann’s theorem
Uhlmann 1976
, i.e.,

\begin{split}F(\Omega_{X},\Omega_{X}^{\prime})&=\max_{\mathrm{supp}(U)\subset I_{i}^{-}\cup I_{i}^{+}}|\langle\Omega|U|\Omega^{\prime}\rangle\\
&=\max_{\mathrm{supp}(U)\subset I_{i}^{-}\cup I_{i}^{+}}|\langle\Omega|US_{I_{i}}|\Omega\rangle|\;.\end{split}

(50)

Suppose that event
B_{i}
does not occur, i.e.,

\max_{\mathrm{supp}(U)\subset I_{i}^{-}\cup I_{i}^{+}}|\langle\Omega|US_{I_{i}}|\Omega\rangle|<\frac{1}{2}\;

(51)

by (
48
), we have
Levin 2020
:

\max_{\operatorname{supp}(A)\subset I_{i}\cup I_{i}^{\prime}}\left|\left\langle\frac{1}{2}\left(A-S_{I_{i}}AS_{I_{i}}\right)\right\rangle_{\Omega}\right|>\frac{1}{2}

(52)

namely,
|\Omega\rangle
is
1/2
weakly-ordered on
I_{i}
,
I_{i}^{\prime}
. As demonstrated in Lemma.
1
, if we take
\ell^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{1/4}
, then
|\Omega\rangle
is
1/4
strongly-ordered on
I_{i}
,
I_{i}^{\prime}
, i.e., there exist odd operators
O_{i}
(resp.
O_{i}^{\prime}
), supported on
I_{i}
(resp.
I_{i}^{\prime}
) such that

\left|\left\langle O_{i}O_{i}^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\Omega}\right|>\frac{1}{4}.

(53)

Note that the mutual information is lower bounded by the correlation function
Wolf et al. 2008
, thus

I_{\Omega}(I_{i}:I_{i}^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\left\langle O_{i}O_{i}^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\Omega}\right|^{2}>\frac{1}{32}

(54)

However, if
A_{i}
does not occur, then
I_{\Omega}(I_{i}:I_{i}^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{32}
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\overline{A_{i}}
and
\overline{B_{i}}
cannot occur simultaneously, which completes the proof.

III.4
Trade-off between order and disorder

We are now ready to show that for any gapped ensemble
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
, there must be either an
O(1)
order parameter or a disorder parameter. Concretely, we will assume that the system has neither a
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter nor a
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter, and obtain a trade-off between
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
. By choosing
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
appropriately, we can show that at least one of
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
must be
O(1)
.

The key observation is that if both
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are small, then the system is neither ordered nor disordered, and hence the probability of the events
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
must be small. By the union bound, the probability that least one event
A_{i}
or
B_{i}
occurs is also small. However, we have shown above that at least one of
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
must occur for each
i
, so

\mathbb{P}(A_{i})+\mathbb{P}(B_{i})\geq 1\;,

(55)

leading to a contradiction.

In the following, we estimate
\mathbb{P}(A_{i})
and
\mathbb{P}(B_{i})
in terms of
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
. The estimation is based on the LPPL principle, which implies that different events
A_{i}
(or
B_{i}
) are independent if they are sufficiently separated. We also make use of the translational invariance of the disorder ensemble, so
\mathbb{P}(A_{i})
and
\mathbb{P}(B_{i})
are independent of
i
.

III.4.1
Estimation of
\mathbb{P}(A_{i})

For the estimation of
\mathbb{P}(A_{i})
, consider two events
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
with
\mathrm{dist}(i,j)\gg\ell^{\prime}
such that they are independent. Suppose both
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
occur for a random disorder realization
\omega
. Recall that, as shown in Sec.
III.2.1
, this implies that the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
has an order parameter between
I_{i}
and
I_{j}
. More explicitly, there exist odd operators
O_{i}[\omega]
and
O_{j}[\omega]
supported on
I_{i}
and
I_{j}
respectively, that depend on the disorder realization
\omega
, such that

\left|\langle O_{i}[\omega]O_{j}[\omega]\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq 2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}.

(56)

For small
\eta
, the probability that such an order parameter exists is correspondingly small. More concretely, by the definition of order parameter, since the ensemble does not have an
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter, for any fixed choice of
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left[\left|\langle O_{i}O_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\right]<\eta.

(57)

Recall Markov’s inequality: if
X
is a nonnegative random variable and
a>0
, then

\mathbb{P}(X\geq a)\leq\mathbb{E}[X]/a.

(58)

This can be verified by noting that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[X]=&\mathbb{E}\big[X\mathbf{1}_{\{X<a\}}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[X\mathbf{1}_{\{X\geq a\}}\big]\geq a\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}_{\{X\geq a\}}\big]\\
=&a\mathbb{P}(X\geq a).\end{split}

(59)

For any operators
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
, applying Markov’s inequality to the random variable
X=|\langle O_{i}O_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|
with threshold

a=\varepsilon_{0}\coloneqq 2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}/3,

(60)

we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\langle O_{i}O_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\varepsilon_{0}\right)<\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon_{0}}=\frac{2^{4\ell+3}\cdot 3\eta}{\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}}.

(61)

Thus, for any fixed odd operators
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
, the probability that they form an order parameter is upper bounded. On the other hand, if
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
occur, there must exist odd operators
O_{i}[\omega]
and
O_{j}[\omega]
that form an order parameter, though these operators may depend on the disorder realization
\omega
. Therefore, to estimate
\mathbb{P}(A_{i})
, we need to take a union bound over all possible choices of
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
.

To this end, let
\mathcal{N}_{i}
be an
\varepsilon_{0}
-net
Papaspiliopoulos 2020
for local operators on
I_{i}
: a finite subset of the operator unit ball such that every local operator
O
on
I_{i}
with
\left\|O\right\|\leq 1
lies within operator-norm distance
\varepsilon_{0}
of some
O^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
(see Appendix
A
). The key observation is that whenever
A_{i}\cap A_{j}
occurs, the
\omega
-dependent witnesses
O_{i}[\omega]
,
O_{j}[\omega]
with
|\langle O_{i}[\omega]O_{j}[\omega]\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|\geq 3\varepsilon_{0}
are each approximated by some
fixed
net element
O_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
,
O_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}
satisfying
\left\|O_{i}^{\prime}-O_{i}[\omega]\right\|,\left\|O_{j}^{\prime}-O_{j}[\omega]\right\|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. By the triangle inequality, this fixed pair still witnesses the order parameter:

|\langle O_{i}^{\prime}O_{j}^{\prime}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|\geq|\langle O_{i}[\omega]O_{j}[\omega]\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|-2\varepsilon_{0}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

(62)

Thus
A_{i}\cap A_{j}
implies that some pair in
\mathcal{N}_{i}\times\mathcal{N}_{j}
achieves
|\langle O_{i}^{\prime}O_{j}^{\prime}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|\geq\varepsilon_{0}
. Since
|I_{i}|=\ell
, as shown in Appendix
A
, each net has size

N_{\ell}(\varepsilon_{0})\leq\bigl(1+\frac{2^{\ell/2+1}}{\varepsilon_{0}}\bigr)^{2^{2\ell+1}}

(63)

independently of the site index, a union bound over all
N_{\ell}(\varepsilon_{0})^{2}
pairs gives

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(A_{i}\cap A_{j})&\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(\exists\,O_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{i},\,O_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}:\,\bigl|\langle O_{i}^{\prime}O_{j}^{\prime}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\bigr|\geq\varepsilon_{0}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{2^{4\ell+3}\cdot 3\eta}{\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}}\,N_{\ell}(\varepsilon_{0})^{2}.\end{split}

(64)

Since
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
are independent, we have
\mathbb{P}(A_{i}\cap A_{j})=\mathbb{P}(A_{i})^{2}
, so

\mathbb{P}(A_{i})\;<\;2^{2\ell+1}\sqrt{6\eta}\;\frac{N_{\ell}\left(2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}/3\right)}{\varepsilon_{\ell}}.

(65)

III.4.2
Estimation of
\mathbb{P}(B_{i})

We now estimate
\mathbb{P}(B_{i})
. As in the
A_{i}
case, we consider three independent events
B_{i}
,
B_{j}
,
B_{k}
with
\mathrm{dist}(i,j),\mathrm{dist}(j,k),\mathrm{dist}(i,k)\gg\ell
. If all three occur, there exist
\omega
-dependent disorder-parameter witnesses in at least two of the regions
I_{i}^{-}
,
I_{j}^{-}
,
I_{k}^{-}
; taking these to be
I_{i}^{-}
and
I_{j}^{-}
without loss of generality, there exist operators
O_{i}^{-}[\omega]
and
O_{j}^{-}[\omega]
supported on
I_{i}^{-}
and
I_{j}^{-}
respectively such that

\left|\langle O_{i}^{-}[\omega]O_{j}^{-}[\omega]S_{J\cup I_{j}^{-}}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{128},

(66)

where
J
is the interval between
I_{i}^{-}
and
I_{j}^{-}
in the clockwise direction. Since the ensemble has no
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter, for any fixed
O_{i}^{-}
,
O_{j}^{-}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\!\left[\left|\langle O_{i}^{-}O_{j}^{-}S_{J\cup I_{j}^{-}}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\right]<\eta^{\prime},

(67)

and Markov’s inequality with threshold
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}\coloneqq 1/384
gives

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left|\langle O_{i}^{-}O_{j}^{-}S_{J\cup I_{j}^{-}}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}\right)<384\eta^{\prime}.

(68)

As in the
A_{i}
case, the witnesses
O_{i}^{-}[\omega]
,
O_{j}^{-}[\omega]
depend on
\omega
, so we introduce
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}
-nets
\mathcal{N}_{i}^{-}
,
\mathcal{N}_{j}^{-}
for local operators on
I_{i}^{-}
,
I_{j}^{-}
, each of size

N_{\ell^{\prime}}(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0})\leq\bigl(1+\frac{2^{\ell^{\prime}/2+1}}{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}}\bigr)^{2^{2\ell^{\prime}+1}}.

(69)

Whenever
B_{i}\cap B_{j}\cap B_{k}
occurs, the witnesses are approximated by fixed net elements
O_{i}^{-\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{i}^{-}
,
O_{j}^{-\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}^{-}
with
\left\|O_{i}^{-\prime}-O_{i}^{-}[\omega]\right\|,\left\|O_{j}^{-\prime}-O_{j}^{-}[\omega]\right\|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}
, and the triangle inequality gives

\left|\langle O_{i}^{-\prime}O_{j}^{-\prime}S_{J\cup I_{j}^{-}}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{128}-2\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}=\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}.

(70)

Thus
B_{i}\cap B_{j}\cap B_{k}
implies that some pair in
\mathcal{N}_{i}^{-}\times\mathcal{N}_{j}^{-}
achieves threshold
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}
. A union bound over all
N_{\ell^{\prime}}(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0})^{2}
pairs, combined with independence
\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\cap B_{j}\cap B_{k})=\mathbb{P}(B_{i})^{3}
, gives

\mathbb{P}(B_{i})<\left(384\eta^{\prime}\,N_{\ell^{\prime}}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{384}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/3}.

(71)

III.4.3
Main trade-off result

We are now ready to prove our main trade-off result.

Proposition 1
.

Let
f
,
g
,
\varepsilon_{\ell}
,
N_{\ell}
, and
\lambda_{1/4}
be as defined below. A gapped ensemble
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
has either an
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter or an
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter, provided
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
satisfy

1.

f(\ell)\leq\frac{1}{128}
,

2.

\ell^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{1/4}
,

3.

f(\ell^{\prime})\leq\frac{(2^{-2\ell-1}\varepsilon_{\ell})^{2}}{2}
,

4.

(6+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{g(\ell^{\prime})}+g(\ell^{\prime})\leq 2^{-2\ell-1}\varepsilon_{\ell}
.

Moreover,
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
satisfy the trade-off relation

2^{2\ell+1}\sqrt{6\eta}\;\frac{N_{\ell}\!\left(2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}/3\right)}{\varepsilon_{\ell}}+\left(384\eta^{\prime}N_{\ell^{\prime}}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{384}\right)^{\!2}\right)^{1/3}\geq 1.

(72)

Here the auxiliary quantities are defined as follows. The functions
f
and
g
decay exponentially,

\displaystyle f(\ell)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{poly}(\ell,\epsilon^{-1})\,e^{-c\epsilon\ell},

(73)

\displaystyle g(\ell)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{poly}(\ell,\epsilon^{-1})\,e^{-c_{1}\epsilon\ell},

(74)

where
c,c_{1}>0
are numerical constants and
\epsilon
is the uniform gap lower bound for the ensemble. The quantity
\varepsilon_{\ell}\in(0,1)
solves

\ell+2H_{2}(\varepsilon_{\ell}/2)=\frac{1}{32},

(75)

N_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
satisfies

N_{\ell}(\varepsilon)\leq\left(1+\frac{2^{\ell/2+1}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2^{2\ell+1}},

(76)

and
\lambda_{1/4}=\tilde{O}(1/\varepsilon^{2})
depends only on the gap.

Proof.

We prove by contradiction. Assume that the system has neither an
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter nor an
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter, then the probability of the events
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
are upper bounded:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{i})

\displaystyle<2^{2\ell+1}\sqrt{6\eta}\;\frac{N_{\ell}\left(2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}/3\right)}{\varepsilon_{\ell}},

(77)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{i})

\displaystyle<\left(384\eta^{\prime}N_{\ell^{\prime}}(\frac{1}{384})^{2}\right)^{1/3}.

(78)

However, since for any
i
at least one of
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
must occur, we have
\mathbb{P}\left(A_{i}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(B_{i}\right)\geq 1
, thus

2^{2\ell+1}\sqrt{6\eta}\;\frac{N_{\ell}\left(2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}/3\right)}{\varepsilon_{\ell}}+\left(384\eta^{\prime}N_{\ell^{\prime}}(\frac{1}{384})^{2}\right)^{1/3}>1,

(79)

a contradiction. Therefore, the system must have either an
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter or an
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter, which completes the proof.
∎

It is worth noting that the conditions on
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
can be satisfied simultaneously when
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
are sufficiently large
O(1)
constants, since
g(\ell)
and
f(\ell)
decay exponentially. In this case, all factors in the trade-off relation are
O(1)
constants, so at least one of
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
must be
O(1)
, which means that the system must have either an
O(1)
order parameter or an
O(1)
disorder parameter.

III.5
Mutually exclusivity of order and disorder

We have already shown that a gapped ensemble must have either an order parameter or a disorder parameter. We now show that these two possibilities are mutually exclusive: the existence of an order parameter rules out the existence of a disorder parameter. Therefore, a gapped ensemble has exactly one of the two.

To this end, we recall that such constraints have been shown for the clean case without disorder average in Ref.
Levin 2020
, as demonstrated by the following Lemma:

Lemma 6

(Theorem 3, Ref.
Levin 2020
)
.

If an Ising-symmetric ground state
|\Omega\rangle
with gap
\epsilon
in its symmetry sector has a
(\delta,\ell)
order parameter defined at two points
i_{1},i_{3}
and a
(\delta,\ell)
disorder parameter defined at two points
i_{2},i_{4}
with
i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}<i_{4}
, then

\min_{k,l}\left[\operatorname{dist}\left(i_{k},i_{l}\right)\right]\leq 2\ell+\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\log\delta^{-1}}{\epsilon}\right)

(80)

For a translationally invariant state
|\Omega\rangle
, this suffices to show that if
|\Omega\rangle
has an
O(1)
order parameter, then it cannot have an
O(1)
disorder parameter, and vice versa. Assume the contrary, we can take
i_{1,2,3,4}
to be evenly spaced in the ring geometry, such that

\min_{k,l}\left[\operatorname{dist}\left(i_{k},i_{l}\right)\right]=\frac{L}{4},

(81)

where
L
is the system size. For sufficiently large
L
, this contradicts the bound in Lemma
6
, thus at least one of the order parameter and disorder parameter must be absent.

Note that without additional structure for the Hamiltonians
\{H_{\omega}\}
in the disorder ensemble, this mutual exclusivity cannot be generalized to the case with disorder average. To see this, consider an ensemble
\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
where
H_{1}
is in the Ising ordered phase and
H_{2}
is in the disordered phase, then the ensemble has both an order parameter and a disorder parameter, even though each individual Hamiltonian has only one of the two.

However, we now argue that this does not happen in our setting. Recall that we assumed
H_{\omega}
to take the form

H_{\omega}=H_{0}+\sum_{i}(v_{i}^{\omega}\mathcal{O}_{i}+{\rm h.c.}),

(82)

and that
H_{\omega}
remains gapped in every symmetry sector with a uniform gap
\epsilon
for all
\omega
. We further assume that
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
lies in the same phase for every
\omega
. This is automatically guaranteed when
v_{i}^{\omega}
varies continuously, since one can then interpolate between any two ground states via a quasi-adiabatic evolution. This same-phase condition rules out the counterexample above.

We now prove the mutual exclusivity for a gapped ensemble. Assume that the ensemble has a
(\delta,\ell)
order parameter and an
(\delta,\ell)
disorder parameter, with
\delta
and
\ell
being
O(1)
constants. By definition, there exists odd operators
O_{i_{1}}
and
O_{i_{3}}
supported near sites
i_{1}
and
i_{3}
respectively, such that

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\left|\langle O_{i_{1}}O_{i_{3}}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\right)\geq\delta.

(83)

Similarly, there exists an operator
O_{i_{2}}
and
O_{i_{4}}^{-}
supported near sites
i_{2}
and
i_{4}
respectively, such that

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\left|\langle O_{i_{2}}O_{i_{4}}\prod_{k=i_{2}+1}^{i_{4}}S_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\right)\geq\delta.

(84)

Consider the event
A
that

\left|\langle O_{i_{1}}O_{i_{3}}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\frac{\delta}{2},

(85)

i.e., the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
has a
(\delta/2,\ell)
order parameter defines on two points
i_{1}
and
i_{3}
, and the event
B
that

\left|\langle O_{i_{2}}O_{i_{4}}\prod_{k=i_{2}+1}^{i_{4}}S_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\frac{\delta}{2},

(86)

i.e., the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
has a
(\delta/2,\ell)
disorder parameter defines on two points
i_{2}
and
i_{4}
.

Note that while
A
only depends on the reduced density matrix near site
i_{1}
and
i_{3}
, the event
B
involves a non-local operator supported on the region between
i_{2}
and
i_{4}
. Therefore, unlike in previous sections, the events
A
and
B
are not necessarily independent, but we can still show that both events have an
O(1)
probability to occur.

Concretely, since the expectation value of the order parameter is at most 1, we have

\delta\leq\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\left|\langle O_{i_{1}}O_{i_{3}}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\right)\leq\mathbb{P}(A)\cdot 1+\mathbb{P}(\overline{A})\cdot\frac{\delta}{2},

(87)

thus
\mathbb{P}(A)\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
. Similarly,
\mathbb{P}(B)\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
. Therefore, there exists two states
|\Omega_{1}\rangle
and
|\Omega_{2}\rangle
such that
|\Omega_{1}\rangle
has a
(\delta/2,\ell)
order parameter defined on two points
i_{1}
and
i_{3}
, and
|\Omega_{2}\rangle
has a
(\delta/2,\ell)
disorder parameter defined at two points
i_{2}
and
i_{4}
. Since they are in the same phase, there exists a quasi-adiabatic evolution
U
that connects
|\Omega_{1}\rangle
and
|\Omega_{2}\rangle
. Since
U
is symmetric and can be approximated by a finite-depth circuit
Hastings and Wen 2005
;
Bravyi et al. 2010
;
Bachmann et al. 2011
;
Haah et al. 2021
;
Yi et al. 2026
, it will only dress the order parameter
O_{i_{1}}
and
O_{i_{3}}
quasi-locally, and
|\Omega_{2}\rangle
will also have a
(O(\delta),O(\ell+\xi))
order parameter defined on two points
i_{1}
and
i_{3}
, where
\xi
is the correlation length, in addition to the
(\delta/2,\ell)
disorder parameter defined at two points
i_{2}
and
i_{4}
.

This contradicts Lemma
6
, thus at least one of the order parameter and disorder parameter must be absent, which completes the proof.

III.6
Parity of disorder parameter

We now finish with the final ingredient for the proof of the main theorem, which is that if the gapped ensemble does have a disorder parameter, then it must have even parity. This can be shown by a similar argument as in the proof of the mutual exclusivity.

To this end, we recall that such constraints have been shown for the clean case without disorder average in Ref.
Levin 2020
, as demonstrated by the following Lemma:

Lemma 7

(Theorem 4, Ref.
Levin 2020
)
.

If an Ising-symmetric ground state
|\Omega\rangle
with gap
\epsilon
in its symmetry sector has a
(\delta,\ell)
disorder parameter with odd parity under
S
, defined at
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}
, then

\min_{k,l}\left[\operatorname{dist}\left(i_{k},i_{l}\right)\right]\leq 2\ell+\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\log\delta^{-1}}{\epsilon}\right)

(88)

Similar to the previous section, for a translational invariant state
|\Omega\rangle
, this suffices to show that if
|\Omega\rangle
has an
O(1)
disorder parameter, it cannot be odd under
S
, since otherwise we can take
i_{1,2,3,4}
to be evenly spaced in the ring geometry, and their minimum distance should not have an
O(1)
upper bound.

We now generalize this result to the case with disorder average. Assume that the ensemble has a
(\delta,\ell)
disorder parameter. Choose
2n
points
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{2n}
evenly spaced in the ring geometry, with
n
being an
O(1)
constant to be chosen later, such that
\mathrm{dist}(i_{k},i_{l})\gg\ell
for all
k\neq l
. By definition, there exist operators
O_{i_{k}}
supported near site
i_{k}
, such that

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\left|\langle O_{i_{k}}O_{i_{k}+n}\prod_{m=i_{k}+1}^{i_{k+n}}S_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\right)\geq\delta.

(89)

We now define a series of events
B_{k}^{\prime}
for
k=1,2,\cdots,n
such that

\left|\langle O_{i_{k}}O_{i_{k}+n}\prod_{m=i_{k}+1}^{i_{k+n}}S_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.

(90)

i.e., the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
has a
(\delta/2,\ell)
disorder parameter defined on two points
i_{k}
and
i_{k+n}
. Note that these events depend non-locally on
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
, so they are not necessarily independent, but similar to the previous section, we can still show that their probability is an
O(1)
constant. Concretely, since the expectation value of the disorder parameter is at most 1, we have

\begin{split}\delta&\leq\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\left|\langle O_{i_{k}}O_{i_{k}+n}\prod_{m=i_{k}+1}^{i_{k+n}}S_{m}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\right)\\
\leq&\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime})\cdot 1+\mathbb{P}(\overline{B_{k}^{\prime}})\cdot\frac{\delta}{2},\end{split}

(91)

thus
\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime})\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
.

Now we proceed to show that there exists two events
B_{k}^{\prime}
and
B_{l}^{\prime}
such that their intersection has positive probability. We can consider the indicator variables
X_{k}=\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}^{\prime}}
for
k=1,2,\cdots,n
. Since
\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime})\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(X_{k})=\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime})\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
. Defining
S=\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(S^{2})&=\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(X_{i})+\sum_{i\neq j}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(X_{i}X_{j})\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(S)+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\max_{kl}\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{l}^{\prime})\;.\end{split}

(92)

On the other hand, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(S^{2})=\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(S)^{2}+\mathrm{Var}(S)\geq\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(S)^{2},

(93)

where
\mathrm{Var}(S)
is the variance of
S
. Thus we have

\max_{k,l}\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{l}^{\prime})\geq\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(S)^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(S)\right).

(94)

Since

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(S)=\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(X_{k})\geq n\delta/(2-\delta),

(95)

if we take
n>\frac{2-\delta}{\delta}
, then
\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{l}^{\prime})>0
is an
O(1)
constant for some
k\neq l
. Therefore, there exists a state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
that has a
(\delta/2,\ell)
disorder parameter with odd parity defined at two pairs of points
i_{k}
,
i_{k+n}
, and
i_{l}
,
i_{l+n}
. By Lemma
7
, this is not possible for sufficiently large
L
, a contradiction. Therefore, the disorder parameter must be even under
S
, which completes the proof.

IV
Rare region effects

In this section, we discuss the rare region effect and demonstrate that it does not invalidate our main theorem. Specifically, we show that even for a nearly gapped ensemble with the presence of rare regions, the system still has one and only one of the two: an
O(1)
order parameter or an
O(1)
disorder parameter with even parity.

The key idea of the proof is to also denote the absence of rare region effect as an event and control the probability correspondingly. More concretely, we can introduce a new series of events
C_{i}
such that
C_{i}
occurs if there is no rare region effect in the region near site
i
. Concretely,
C_{i}
occurs if for the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
, the rare region effect can be represented by a unitary supported outside of the region
I_{i}^{-}I_{i}I_{i}^{+}
, i.e.,

|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle=U_{I_{i}^{\prime}}|\Omega\rangle,

(96)

where
|\Omega\rangle
is a state with a uniform gap
\epsilon
in the symmetry sector, and
U_{I_{i}^{\prime}}
is a symmetric unitary operator supported in the region
I_{i}^{\prime}
, which is the complement of
I_{i}^{-}I_{i}I_{i}^{+}
. By the LPPL principle, the event
C_{i}
should only depend on the disorder realization near the site
i
, so it is independent of the events
A_{j}
,
B_{j}
and
C_{j}
for
j
with
\mathrm{dist}(i,j)\gg\ell^{\prime}
. Moreover, we expect for all
O(1)
constants
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
,

\mathbb{P}(C_{i})>p_{0},

(97)

for some
O(1)
constant
p_{0}
, depending on
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
that is very close to 1.

The fact that
\mathbb{P}(C_{i})
is an
O(1)
constant, independent of the system size
L
, is an important feature of nearly gapped ensemble, with Griffiths-like rare regions that have exponentially small probability. In contrast, for the infinite-randomness fixed points
Fisher 1994
;
Fisher 1995
,
\mathbb{P}(C_{i})
vanishes as
L\to\infty
, and our results below will not apply.

Since the proof closely follows the gapped case without rare regions, we will only sketch it, focusing on verifying that the key lemmas remain valid even in the presence of rare regions.

IV.1
Trade-off for nearly gapped ensembles

We start by showing that for any nearly gapped ensemble

IV.1.3
Trade-off relation

We begin by generalizing the incompatibility condition for the gapped case without the rare region effect. Recall that in that case, the incompatibility between the events
\overline{A_{i}}
and
\overline{B_{i}}
is shown by Lemma.
1
. For the case with rare region effect, we can similarly show that if the event
\overline{B_{i}}\cap C_{i}
occurs, then

I_{\Omega}(I_{i}:I_{i}^{\prime})>\frac{1}{32}

(116)

which implies that the event
\overline{A_{i}}
cannot occur. Therefore, we have
C_{i}\subset A_{i}\cup B_{i}
, and thus

\mathbb{P}(C_{i})\leq\mathbb{P}(A_{i}\cap C_{i})+\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\cap C_{i}).

(117)

We are now ready to prove the trade-off relation for nearly gapped ensembles. Assume that the ensemble does not have an
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter and an
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{i}\cap C_{i})

\displaystyle<2^{2\ell+1}\sqrt{6\eta}\;\frac{N_{\ell}\left(2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}/3\right)}{\varepsilon_{\ell}},

(118)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\cap C_{i})

\displaystyle<\left(384\eta^{\prime}N_{\ell^{\prime}}(\frac{1}{384})^{2}\right)^{1/3}.

(119)

On the other hand, we have
\mathbb{P}(C_{i})>p_{0}
is some
O(1)
constant. Therefore, if we take

\begin{split}&2^{2\ell+1}\sqrt{6\eta}\;\frac{N_{\ell}\!\left(2^{-4\ell-3}\varepsilon_{\ell}^{2}/3\right)}{\varepsilon_{\ell}}\\
+\,&\left(384\eta^{\prime}\,N_{\ell^{\prime}}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{384}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/3}=p_{0},\end{split}

(120)

then we have
\mathbb{P}(C_{i})>\mathbb{P}(A_{i}\cap C_{i})+\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\cap C_{i})
, a contradiction. Therefore, a nearly gapped ensemble must have either an
(\eta,\ell)
order parameter or an
(\eta^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
disorder parameter, which completes the proof of the trade-off relation for nearly gapped ensembles.

IV.2
Mutual exclusivity for nearly gapped ensembles

We now show that a nearly gapped ensemble cannot have both an
O(1)
order parameter and an
O(1)
disorder parameter.

Suppose for contradiction that both a
(\delta,\ell)
order parameter and a
(\delta,\ell)
disorder parameter exist. Define events
A
and
B
as in Sec.
III.5
:
A
is the event that a disorder realization
\omega
yields a state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
satisfying

\left|\langle O_{i_{1}}O_{i_{3}}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\frac{\delta}{2},

(121)

and
B
is the event that

\left|\langle O_{i_{2}}O_{i_{4}}\prod_{k=i_{2}+1}^{i_{4}}S_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.

(122)

Since the probabilistic argument of Sec.
III.5
does not depend on whether rare regions are present, it applies unchanged and gives
\mathbb{P}(A)\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
and
\mathbb{P}(B)\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
.

We can now pick
i_{1,2,3,4}
to be evenly spaced in the whole geometry, and let
R_{k}
denote the event that there exists a rare region covering the site
i_{k}
of size at least
L/12
. By the nearly gapped assumption,

\mathbb{P}(R_{k})\sim p^{L/12}\;.

(123)

Consider the event
R
that there is a rare region that spans one of the four sites, i.e.,
R\coloneq\bigcup_{k}R_{k}
. By the union bound,

\mathbb{P}(R)\leq\sum_{k}\mathbb{P}(R_{k})\sim O(p^{L/12})\;.

(124)

We now show that events
A
and
B
are not merely artifacts of large rare regions smearing out local operators near
i_{1,2,3,4}
. By the union bound,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(A\cap\overline{R})&\geq\mathbb{P}(A)-\mathbb{P}(R)\geq\frac{\delta}{2-\delta}-O(p^{L/12}),\\
\mathbb{P}(B\cap\overline{R})&\geq\mathbb{P}(B)-\mathbb{P}(R)\geq\frac{\delta}{2-\delta}-O(p^{L/12}).\end{split}

(125)

Taking the system size to be large, both right-hand sides give
O(1)
constant lower bounds.

However, for any realization
\omega
for which
A\cap\overline{R}
occurs, there exists a
(\delta/2,L/12)
order parameter on sites
i_{1,3}
for some gapped state. Similarly, for any realization
\omega
for which
B\cap\overline{R}
occurs, there exists a
(\delta/2,L/12)
disorder parameter on sites
i_{2,4}
for some gapped state. This violates the absence of phase separation condition for our ensemble, a contradiction.

This completes the proof that a nearly gapped ensemble cannot have both an
O(1)
order parameter and an
O(1)
disorder parameter in the presence of rare regions.

IV.3
Parity of disorder parameter for nearly gapped ensembles

We now show that any disorder parameter of a nearly gapped ensemble must parity even.

The key idea is similar to Sec.
III.6
, and we treat the rare region effects as in Sec.
IV.2
.

Suppose for contradiction that the ensemble has a
(\delta,\ell)
disorder parameter with odd parity. Place
n
pairs of sites
(i_{k},i_{k+n})
for
k=1,\ldots,n
evenly in the geometry, each pair separated by
L/2
. Define
B_{k}^{\prime}
as the event that the realization
\omega
yields a state satisfying

\left|\left\langle O_{i_{k}}O_{i_{k+n}}\prod_{m=i_{k}+1}^{i_{k+n}}S_{m}\right\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}\right|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.

(126)

Since the probabilistic argument of Sec.
III.5
remains valid, we have
\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime})\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
for each
k
. Choosing
n
large enough that
\sum_{k}\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime})>1
, the second-moment argument of Sec.
III.6
then produces indices
k\neq l
with all four sites
i_{k},i_{k+n},i_{l},i_{l+n}
pairwise well-separated with a distance
L/2n
, such that
\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{l}^{\prime})=O(1)
.

Since
\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{l}^{\prime})=O(1)
, these events cannot merely come from rare regions near the four sites. For each
j\in\{i_{k},i_{k+n},i_{l},i_{l+n}\}
let
R_{j}
denote the event that a rare region of size at least
L/(6n)
covers site
j
, and let
R=\bigcup_{j}R_{j}
. By the nearly gapped assumption,

\mathbb{P}(R_{j})\sim p^{L/(6n)},

(127)

and by the union bound,

\mathbb{P}(R)\leq\sum_{j}\mathbb{P}(R_{j})\sim O(p^{L/(6n)}).

(128)

Hence

\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{l}^{\prime}\cap\overline{R})\geq\mathbb{P}(B_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{l}^{\prime})-\mathbb{P}(R)>0

(129)

for sufficiently large
L
. However, for any realization
\omega
for which
B_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{l}^{\prime}\cap\overline{R}
occurs, the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
has two odd-parity disorder parameters at the well-separated pairs
(i_{k},i_{k+n})
and
(i_{l},i_{l+n})
, contradicting Lemma
7
. Therefore the disorder parameter must be parity-even, which completes the proof.

V
Applications

In this section, we discuss some applications of our main theorem. We first extend our theorems to the case of symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phase, and demonstrate a well-defined string order parameter for SPT in dirty systems in Sec.
V.1
. We then use our main theorem to show that there is a Lieb-Schultz-Mattis-type constraint for average ensemble in Sec.
V.2
. In Sec.
V.3
we apply our results to fermion chains through the Jordan-Wigner transform and obtain trade-off relations between different disorder parameters.

V.1
String order parameter for SPT in dirty systems

While traditionally the concept of SPT phases is defined for clean systems, it can be naturally extended to dirty systems
Ma and Wang 2023
;
Ma et al. 2025
. For the clean systems, the string order parameter
Kennedy and Tasaki 1992
;
Pérez-García et al. 2008
;
Pollmann and Turner 2012
;
Haegeman et al. 2012
serves as a powerful tool to characterize the SPT phases, which is defined as the expectation value of two operators connected by a string of restricted symmetry operators. Note that this coincides with our definition of disorder parameter in the clean case, so it is natural to ask whether our definition in the disordered case can also be used to characterize SPT phases. In this section, we show that this is indeed the case, and we can use our main theorem to show that there is a well-defined string order parameter for some disordered SPT phases.

For simplicity, we focus on the case of
1d
SPT phases protected by an exact
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, which is the simplest non-trivial case. We also assume an average lattice translational symmetry, even though it is not involved directly in the topological protection. More explicitly, we consider a family of disordered Hamiltonians
\{H_{\omega}\}
that each
H_{\omega}
is symmetric under the following symmetry:

S^{e}=\prod_{i}Z_{2i},\quad S^{o}=\prod_{i}Z_{2i+1}\;,

(130)

and the ensemble
\{H_{\omega}\}
is translationally invariant.

For
\{H_{\omega}\}
to be an average SPT, we expect that there is no spontaneous symmetry breaking, or equivalently, there is no order parameter for our ensemble. This can also be a natural consequence if we assume that
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
is an SRE ensemble
Ma et al. 2025
, i.e., the state
|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle
is short-range correlated for almost all
\omega
. Thus by our main theorem, there must be a disorder parameter for
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
, precisely, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 4
.

Let
\{H_{\omega}\}
be a disordered average-SPT ensemble with exact

S^{e}
and
S^{o}
symmetry and average translational
symmetry. Then, for any sites
i<j
, there exist local operators

O_{i}
and
O_{j}
, supported near
i
and
j
respectively, such that

\bigl\langle O_{i}O_{j}\prod_{i<2k<j}Z_{2k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega}=O(1).

(131)

Similarly, there exist local operators
O_{i}^{\prime}
and
O_{j}^{\prime}
, supported near

i
and
j
respectively, such that

\bigl\langle O_{i}^{\prime}O_{j}^{\prime}\prod_{i<2k+1<j}Z_{2k+1}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega}=O(1).

(132)

Moreover, for any fixed ensemble
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
, the symmetry parities of these disorder parameters are uniquely determined:
the operators
O_{i},O_{j}
need to be even under
S^{e}
, and the operators

O_{i}^{\prime},O_{j}^{\prime}
need to be even under
S^{o}
. In addition, the parity of

O_{i},O_{j}
under
S^{o}
and the parity of
O_{i}^{\prime},O_{j}^{\prime}
under
S^{e}
are equal, and is uniquely fixed by the ensemble.

We now provide the proof of this corollary. Since
\{H_{\omega}\}
is an average SPT, there is no order parameter for
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
under the symmetry
S^{e}
and
S^{o}
, so by our main theorem, there must be a disorder parameter for
\{|\Omega_{\omega}\rangle\}
. This gives the existence of the operators
O_{i},O_{j},O_{i}^{\prime},O_{j}^{\prime}
. Moreover, as shown in Sec.
III.6
, as the disorder parameter for
S^{e}
,
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
must be even under
S^{e}
, and as the disorder parameter for
S^{o}
,
O_{i}^{\prime}
and
O_{j}^{\prime}
must be even under
S^{o}
.

To show that the parity of
O_{i},O_{j}
under
S^{o}
and the parity of
O_{i}^{\prime},O_{j}^{\prime}
under
S^{e}
are unique, assume the contrary, without loss of generality, that
O_{i}
and
O_{j}
are odd under
S^{o}
but
O_{i}^{\prime}
and
O_{j}^{\prime}
are even under
S^{e}
. Making use of the translational symmetry, we can then choose four sites
i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}<i_{4}
such that
\mathrm{dist}(i_{k},i_{l})\gg\ell
for all
k\neq l
, with the operator
O_{k}
defined near site
i_{k}
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|\bigl\langle O_{1}O_{3}\prod_{i_{1}<2k<i_{3}}Z_{2k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|=\delta

(133)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|\bigl\langle O_{2}O_{4}\prod_{i_{2}<2k+1<i_{4}}Z_{2k+1}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|=\delta,

(134)

where
\delta
is some
O(1)
constant,
O_{1,3}
are odd under
S^{o}
and even under
S^{e}
, and
O_{2,4}
are even under
S^{o}
and even under
S^{e}
.

We first prove that this is impossible for a gapped clean system without disorder averaging. To this end, we invoke the following lemma:

Lemma 8

(Proposition 1, Ref.
Levin 2020
)
.

Let
A
be an operator that is even under
S
, has norm of at most 1 and is supported on
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
where
I_{1},I_{2}
are two intervals separated by a distance of at least
\ell
. Then,
for any symmetric state
|\Omega\rangle
with gap
\epsilon
in its symmetry sector, we have

\|P_{\tilde{I}_{1}}(\ell)P_{\tilde{I}_{2}}(\ell)AS_{J}|\Omega\rangle-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|AS_{J}|\Omega\rangle\|\leq\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{g(\ell)})

(135)

where
P_{\tilde{I}_{1}}(\ell)
is a projection operator that is even under
S
and supported on
\tilde{I}_{j}\equiv\left\{i:\operatorname{dist}\left(i,I_{j}\right)<\ell/2\right\}
,
J
denotes the interval between
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
(going clockwise), and
g(\ell)
is defined as in Lemma.
4
.

Applying this lemma to operators
O_{1,2,3,4}
and let
\ell_{\min}=\min_{k,l}[\mathrm{dist}(i_{k},i_{l})]-2\ell
, we can define

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=P_{1}P_{3}O_{1}O_{3}\prod_{i_{1}<2k\leq i_{3}}Z_{2k}\;,

(136)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=P_{2}P_{4}O_{2}O_{4}\prod_{i_{2}<2k+1\leq i_{4}}Z_{2k+1}\;,

(137)

where
P_{k}\coloneqq P_{\tilde{I}_{k}}(\ell_{\min})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|U|\Omega\rangle-\delta|\Omega\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle\leq O(\sqrt{g(\ell_{\min})})\;,

(138)

\displaystyle\left\|V|\Omega\rangle-\delta|\Omega\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle\leq O(\sqrt{g(\ell_{\min})})\;.

(139)

It then follows that

\displaystyle\left\|UV|\Omega\rangle-\delta^{2}|\Omega\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle\leq O(\sqrt{g(\ell_{\min})})

(140)

\displaystyle\left\|VU|\Omega\rangle-\delta^{2}|\Omega\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle\leq O(\sqrt{g(\ell_{\min})})\;.

(141)

However, by the parity of the operators
O_{1,2,3,4}
, we have
UV=-VU
, which contradicts the above inequalities for large enough
\ell_{\min}
. We thus complete the proof for the clean case.

For the dirty case, we can apply the same argument as in Sec.
III.5
. Suppose the contrary, that there exist operators
O_{1,2,3,4}
as described above, we can consider the events
B_{13}
that

|\bigl\langle O_{1}O_{3}\prod_{i_{1}<2k<i_{3}}Z_{2k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|\geq\delta/2,

(142)

and
B_{24}
that

|\bigl\langle O_{2}O_{4}\prod_{i_{2}<2k+1<i_{4}}Z_{2k+1}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|\geq\delta/2,

(143)

with probability
\mathbb{P}(B_{13})\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
and
\mathbb{P}(B_{24})\geq\delta/(2-\delta)
.

We can also take the rare region effects into account by the same argument as in Sec.
IV.2
. We can take
i_{1,2,3,4}
evenly spaced in the whole geometry, and let
R_{i}
denote the event that there exists a rare region of size at least
L/12
that covers site

, and let
R=\bigcup_{i}R_{i}
. Following the arguments in Sec.

, we have

\mathbb{P}(B_{13}\cap\overline{R})=O(1)\;,\quad\mathbb{P}(B_{24}\cap\overline{R})=O(1)\;,

(144)

for large enough system size
L
. Thus for any realization
\omega
for which
B_{13}\cap\overline{R}
occurs, there exists a
(\delta/2,L/12)
disorder parameter on sites
i_{1,3}
for some gapped state with the same parity as
O_{1,3}
. Similarly, for any realization
\omega
for which
B_{24}\cap\overline{R}
occurs, there exists a
(\delta/2,L/12)
disorder parameter on sites
i_{2,4}
for some gapped state with the same parity as
O_{2,4}
. These two gapped states can be connected by a quasi-adiabatic evolution without changing the endpoint parity (see Appendix.
B
). By the same argument as in the clean case, this is not possible for sufficiently large system size, a contradiction. Therefore, the parity of
O_{i},O_{j}
under
S^{o}
and the parity of
O_{i}^{\prime},O_{j}^{\prime}
under
S^{e}
are unique, which completes the proof of this corollary.

V.2
Lieb-Schultz-Mattis-type constraint for disordered ensembles

The Lieb-Schultz-Mattis (LSM) theorem
Lieb et al. 1961
and its generalizations
Oshikawa 2000
;
Hastings 2004
;
Liu et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025
;
Yi et al. 2025
;
Gioia and Wang 2022
provide powerful constraints on the low-energy physics of quantum many-body systems with certain symmetries and filling constraints. In this section, we show that our main theorem provides a powerful tool to derive LSM-type constraints for disordered ensembles
Ma and Wang 2023
;
You and Oshikawa 2024
;
Panahi et al. 2026
;
Kimchi et al. 2018
;
Xu and Jian 2025
;
Li et al. 2026
.

The key idea is to show that under certain symmetries, the system cannot have a disorder parameter, and thus by our main theorem, if the disorder ensemble is nearly gapped, it must have an order parameter, which implies spontaneous symmetry breaking.

For simplicity, we focus on the case of 1D spin chains with an exact
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry and average translational symmetry. More explicitly, we consider a family of disordered Hamiltonians
\{H_{\omega}\}
defined on a ring geometry with even number of qubits, that each
H_{\omega}
is symmetric under the following symmetry:

S^{x}=\prod_{i}X_{i},\quad S^{z}=\prod_{i}Z_{i}\;,

(145)

and the ensemble
\{H_{\omega}\}
is translationally invariant. Note that on a single site, the representation of the symmetry group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is projective (
X_{i}Z_{i}=-Z_{i}X_{i}
), so we expect that there is a LSM-type constraint for this system. More explicitly, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 5
.

Let
\{H_{\omega}\}
be a nearly gapped ensemble with exact
S^{x}
and
S^{z}
symmetry and average translational symmetry. Then
\{H_{\omega}\}
must have an
O(1)
order parameter for either
S^{x}
or
S^{z}
.

To show this, we just need to show that
\{H_{\omega}\}
cannot have an
O(1)
disorder parameter for both
S^{x}
and
S^{z}
. Assume the contrary, that
\{H_{\omega}\}
has an
O(1)
disorder parameter for both
S^{x}
and
S^{z}
, we can then choose four sites
i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}<i_{4}
such that
\mathrm{dist}(i_{k},i_{l})\gg\ell
for all
k\neq l
, with the operator
O_{k}
defined near site
i_{k}
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|\bigl\langle O_{1}O_{3}\prod_{i_{1}\leq k<i_{3}}X_{k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|=\delta,

(146)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|\bigl\langle O_{2}O_{4}\prod_{i_{2}\leq k<i_{4}}Z_{k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|=\delta,

(147)

where
\delta
is some
O(1)
constant,
O_{1,3}
are even under
S^{x}
, and
O_{2,4}
are even under
S^{z}
. Without loss of generality, we can also assume that
i_{1,2,3,4}
are all even sites. By the average translational symmetry of the disorder ensemble, we then also have

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|\bigl\langle\hat{T}\left(O_{2}O_{4}\prod_{i_{2}\leq k<i_{4}}Z_{k}\right)\hat{T}^{\dagger}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|=\delta,

(148)

thus
O_{2}^{\prime}=\hat{T}O_{2}\hat{T}^{\dagger}Z_{i_{2}}
and
O_{4}^{\prime}=\hat{T}O_{4}\hat{T}^{\dagger}Z_{i_{4}}
also form a disorder parameter for
S^{z}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
O_{2,4}
have a definite parity under
S^{x}
, and thus
O_{2}^{\prime}
and
O_{4}^{\prime}
have the opposite parity under
S^{x}
because of the additionally dressed
Z
operators.

We can now apply a similar argument as in Sec.
V.1
to show that this is not possible for a sufficiently large system size. We start with the clean case.

To this end, we apply Lemma.
8
to operators
O_{1,2,3,4}
and let
\ell_{\min}=\min_{k,l}[\mathrm{dist}(i_{k},i_{l})]-2\ell
, we can define

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=P_{1}P_{3}O_{1}O_{3}\prod_{i_{1}<k\leq i_{3}}X_{k}\;,

(149)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=P_{2}P_{4}O_{2}O_{4}\prod_{i_{2}<k\leq i_{4}}Z_{k}\;,

(150)

\displaystyle V^{\prime}

\displaystyle=P_{2}P_{4}O_{2}^{\prime}O_{4}^{\prime}\prod_{i_{2}<k\leq i_{4}}Z_{k}\;,

(151)

where
P_{k}\coloneqq P_{\tilde{I}_{k}}(\ell_{\min})
. By Lemma.
8
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|U|\Omega\rangle-\delta|\Omega\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle\leq O(\sqrt{g(\ell_{\min})})\;,

(152)

\displaystyle\left\|V|\Omega\rangle-\delta|\Omega\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle\leq O(\sqrt{g(\ell_{\min})})\;,

(153)

\displaystyle\left\|V^{\prime}|\Omega\rangle-\delta|\Omega\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle\leq O(\sqrt{g(\ell_{\min})})\;.

(154)

However, since
O_{2}
and
O_{2}^{\prime}
have opposite parity under
S^{x}
, we have either
UV=-VU
or
UV^{\prime}=-V^{\prime}U
, which contradicts the above inequalities for large enough
\ell_{\min}
. We thus complete the proof for the clean case.

We now move on to the dirty case. We can apply the same argument as in Sec.
III.5
. Suppose the contrary, that there exist operators
O_{1,2,3,4}
as described above, we can consider the events
B_{13}
that

|\bigl\langle O_{1}O_{3}\prod_{i_{1}\leq k<i_{3}}X_{k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|\geq\delta/2,

(155)

and
B_{24}
that

|\bigl\langle O_{2}O_{4}\prod_{i_{2}\leq k<i_{4}}Z_{k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|\geq\delta/2.

(156)

The average translational symmetry of the disorder ensemble also implies that there is an event
B_{24}^{\prime}
that

|\bigl\langle O_{2}^{\prime}O_{4}^{\prime}\prod_{i_{2}\leq k<i_{4}}Z_{k}\bigr\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|\geq\delta/2,

(157)

Similar to the analysis in Sec.
III.5
, since the expectation values of the disorder parameters cannot be larger than 1, we can show that the probability

\mathbb{P}(B_{13}),\mathbb{P}(B_{24}),\mathbb{P}(B_{24}^{\prime})\geq\delta/(2-\delta).

(158)

We can also take the rare region effects into account by the same argument as in Sec.
IV.2
. We can take
i_{1,2,3,4}
evenly spaced in the whole geometry, and let
R_{i}
denote the event that there exists a rare region of size at least
L/12
that covers site
i
, and let
R=\bigcup_{i}R_{i}
. Following the arguments in Sec.
IV.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{13}

\displaystyle\cap\overline{R})=O(1)\;,

(159)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{24}

\displaystyle\cap\overline{R})=O(1)\;,

(160)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{24}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\cap\overline{R})=O(1)\;,

(161)

for large enough system size
L
. Thus there exists three gapped states with, respectively, a
(\delta/2,L/12)
disorder parameter on sites
i_{1,3}
with the same parity as
O_{1,3}
, a
(\delta/2,L/12)
disorder parameter on sites
i_{2,4}
with the same parity as
O_{2,4}
, a
(\delta/2,L/12)
disorder parameter on sites
i_{2,4}
with the same parity as
O^{\prime}_{2,4}
.

These three gapped states can be connected by a quasi-adiabatic evolution without changing the endpoint parity (see Appendix.
B
). By the same argument as in the clean case, this is not possible for sufficiently large system size, a contradiction.

Therefore,
\{H_{\omega}\}
cannot have an
O(1)
disorder parameter for both
S^{x}
and
S^{z}
. By our main theorem, if
\{H_{\omega}\}
is nearly gapped, it must have an
O(1)
order parameter for either
S^{x}
or
S^{z}
, which completes the proof of this corollary.

V.3
Fermion chain and intrinsic average SPT

Our results on the Ising spin chain can be readily translated to fermion chains through the Jordan-Wigner transformation. In this section, we demonstrate that the trade-off between order and disorder parameters in the spin chain translates to a trade-off between disorder parameters with bosonic or fermionic endpoint operators in fermionic systems. We then apply this result to understand a class of “intrinsically disordered” topological phase proposed in Ref.
Ma et al. 2025
.

In the following, we denote the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry as generated by

S=\prod_{i}X_{i}.

(162)

The Jordan-Wigner transform maps the Ising spin chain to a fermion chain, with two Majorana fermions
\gamma_{2i-1},\gamma_{2i}
for every unit cell
i
:

\displaystyle X_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\gamma_{2i-1}\gamma_{2i}=F_{i},

\displaystyle Z_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\prod_{j<i}F_{j})\gamma_{2i-1},

(163)

where
F_{i}=\pm 1
is the fermion parity on site
i
. The
\gamma
fermions couple to a dynamical
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field – in
1d
the only gauge-invariant degree of freedom of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field is the gauge flux (holonomy) along the entire closed
1d
ring, which sets the spatial boundary condition for the fermions. In the Jordan-Wigner transform, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge flux is given by the total Ising charge
S
. The significance of having exact Ising symmetry is that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field is effectively non-dynamical once we fix the total
S
charge, and it suffices to consider a genuine fermion chain. This allows us to translate our results on Ising chain to the fermion chain.

First, we consider a disorder operator in the Ising chain. The undressed disorder operator maps to

S_{12}:=\prod_{i_{1}\leq k\leq i_{2}}X_{k}=\prod_{i_{1}\leq k\leq i_{2}}F_{i}:=F_{12},

(164)

which is a disorder operator in fermion parity. For a dressed disorder operator
O_{1}O_{2}S_{12}
, if
O_{1,2}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-even, we have

O_{1}O_{2}S_{12}=\tilde{O}_{1}\tilde{O}_{2}F_{12},

(165)

where
\tilde{O}_{1,2}
are some bosonic local operators in the fermion chain. If
O_{1,2}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-odd, then in the fermion picture an additional flux-changing operator has to be inserted, leading to

O_{1}O_{2}S_{12}=f_{1}f_{2},

(166)

where
f
are fermion operators. For the simplest choice of
O_{i}=Z_{i}
, the
f_{i}
operators are nothing but the
\gamma
fermions. More generally they can take forms like
\gamma_{a}\gamma_{b}\gamma_{c}
. Combining the two results, the order parameters
O_{1}O_{2}
(
O_{1,2}
being
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
odd) in the Ising chain maps to

O_{1}O_{2}=f_{1}f_{2}F_{12},

(167)

where again
f_{1,2}
are fermion operators. In a fermion chain, the above disorder operator dressed with fermion operators is used to detect nontrivial topological superconductivity in the Kitaev phase.

For a nearly gapped ensemble in a fermion chain, we immediately conclude from Theorem
1
:

1.

There always exists some disorder parameter

\lim_{|i_{1}-i_{2}|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|\langle O_{1}O_{2}\prod_{i_{1}\leq k\leq i_{2}}F_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|=O(1).

(168)

The dressing local operators
O_{1,2}
are either always bosonic or always fermionic. The former corresponds to a trivial phase while the latter corresponds to a Kitaev chain.

2.

The fermion two-point correlator must decay:

\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\langle f_{i}f_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}=0.

(169)

In Ref.
Ma et al. 2025
a simple example of
intrinsically disordered
topological phase was proposed in a fermion chain. The proposed phase is essentially a disordered Kitaev chain, but with an additional average
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
symmetry
c_{i}\to ic_{i}
(where
c_{i}=\gamma_{2i-1}+i\gamma_{2i}
is the complex fermion on each site). In fact the average symmetry can even be enlarged to a full
U(1)
:
c_{i}\to e^{i\alpha}c_{i}
. In the Ising chain picture this
U(1)
is generated by charge
Q=\sum_{i}(1-X_{i})/2
. Having this average symmetry unbroken requires the vanishing of the first-moment (not Edwards-Anderson) order parameter for any local operator
O_{i}
that is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-odd (hence also charged under
U(1)
):

\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\langle O_{i}O^{\dagger}_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}=0.

(170)

Translating this to the fermion picture means that the first-moment fermion-dressed disorder parameter must vanish:

\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\langle f_{i}f^{\dagger}_{j}\prod_{i\leq k\leq j}F_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}=0.

(171)

This is why in a clean system with a
U(1)
symmetry, the Kitaev phase cannot be realized as a gapped state. But in a dirty system, our results indicates that we should instead examine the Edwards-Anderson disorder parameter. In this light, a Kitaev phase with

\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}|\langle f_{i}f^{\dagger}_{j}\prod_{i\leq k\leq j}F_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\omega}}|=O(1),

(172)

is perfectly compatible with the average (but not exact)
U(1)
symmetry. This is why the phase is considered “intrinsically disordered”.

VI
Summary and Outlook

In this work, we defined notions of order and disorder parameters for quenched disordered spin chains and established a fundamental trade-off between them. Specifically, we showed that: (i) any gapped ensemble must exhibit either an order parameter or a disorder parameter; (ii) the two cannot coexist; and (iii) any disorder parameter must be even under the symmetry. These results extend naturally to nearly gapped ensembles in the presence of rare-region effects.

Order and disorder parameters play a central role in the understanding of phases of matter without disorder averaging. Our results provide a firm foundation for their role in quenched disordered systems as well. Rare-region effects have long been difficult to treat in the study of disordered systems. Based on the LPPL principle, our work gives a systematic understanding of how such effects influence order and disorder parameters, which may offer broader insight into the role of rare regions in disordered phases.

Our results also have concrete applications to disordered systems. First, we prove the existence of well-defined string order parameters for SPT phases in dirty systems. Second, our theorem yields a Lieb-Schultz-Mattis-type constraint for disordered ensembles with anomalous symmetry. Third, our results can be extended to fermion systems and provide a more rigorous understanding of certain “intrinsically disordered” topological phases.

More broadly, this work highlights the importance of order and disorder parameters in the study of quenched disordered systems. Promising directions for future work include generalizing these ideas beyond Ising symmetries and extending them to higher dimensions. It would also be interesting to formulate analogous notions of order and disorder parameters for mixed-state phases, such as decohered average SPT phases.

Note added
: for complementary perspective on order-disorder relation in the context of mixed quantum states, developed in parallel to ours by Divi, Lessa and Wang, see Ref.
Divi et al. 2026
.

-net
for
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
if for every
O\in\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
there exists
O^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\varepsilon)
such that
\left\|O-O^{\prime}\right\|\leq\varepsilon
.

We now estimate the covering number
|\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\varepsilon)|
. Let the local Hilbert space on an interval of length
\ell
have dimension
d=2^{\ell}
. Every operator
O\in\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
can be expanded in the orthonormal Pauli basis
\{P_{k}\}
as
O=\sum_{k}c_{k}P_{k}
, with Frobenius norm

\left\|O\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\coloneqq\sqrt{{\rm tr}(O^{\dagger}O)}=\sqrt{\sum_{k}|c_{k}|^{2}}\,.

(174)

Collecting the real and imaginary parts of the coefficients identifies every operator
O\in\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
with a point of
\mathbb{R}^{2d^{2}}
. The Frobenius and operator norms satisfy

\left\|O\right\|\leq\left\|O\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\sqrt{d}\,\left\|O\right\|\,,

(175)

so every
O\in\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
corresponds to a point in the Euclidean ball of radius
\sqrt{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d^{2}}
, and
\left\|O-O^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\varepsilon
implies
\left\|O-O^{\prime}\right\|\leq\varepsilon
. Hence an
\varepsilon
-net for the Euclidean ball induces an
\varepsilon
-net for
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
, and applying the covering-number estimate above with
r=\sqrt{d}
and
n=2d^{2}
gives an estimation for the cover number
N_{\ell}(\varepsilon)\coloneqq|\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\varepsilon)|
.

N_{\ell}(\varepsilon)\leq\Bigl(1+\frac{2\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2d^{2}}=\Bigl(1+\frac{2^{\ell/2+1}}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2^{2\ell+1}}\,.

(176)

Although this grows rapidly with
\ell
, it does not depend on the system size, and since
\ell
is an
O(1)
constant independent of system size, the net size remains bounded by a system-size-independent constant.

Appendix B
Quasi-adiabatic evolution for gapped symmetry sector

In this appendix we review the quasi-adiabatic evolution
Hastings and Wen 2005
;
Bravyi et al. 2010
;
Bachmann et al. 2011
and its implication in this work.

B.1
Construction

For two local Hamiltonians
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
smoothly connected by a path
H_{s}
along which the gap remains open, the ground-state subspaces of the two systems can be connected by a quasi-adiabatic evolution defined as follows:

U\coloneqq\mathcal{S}\exp\left\{i\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{~d}s\mathcal{D}_{s}\right\}

(177)

where
\mathcal{S}
denotes an
s
-ordered exponential, and the quasi-adiabatic continuation operator
\mathcal{D}_{s}
is given by

\mathcal{D}_{s}\coloneqq i\int\mathrm{~d}tF(t)\exp\left(iH_{s}t\right)\left(\partial_{s}H_{s}\right)\exp\left(-iH_{s}t\right)

(178)

with
F(t)
being a super-polynomially decaying function.

This quasi-adiabatic construction extends to the case where the spectral gap is required only within the parity-even symmetry sector, rather than across the full Hilbert space. Indeed, since
\partial_{s}H_{s}
is parity-even, it cannot connect states of opposite parity, so the sector-restricted gap suffices to control the evolution.

Although the quasi-adiabatic evolution is generated by an almost-local Hamiltonian, it can be well approximated by a quantum circuit of
O(\log L)
depth
Haah et al. 2021
;
Yi et al. 2026
. For the purpose of estimating how local operators transform under the evolution, it was also shown
Yi et al. 2026
that the evolution can be approximated by a finite-depth quantum circuit, with errors controlled by the locality of the gates. In particular, if
H_{s}
is a sum of local terms each commuting with
S
, the approximating circuit can be chosen so that every gate commutes with
S
; symmetry is thus preserved gate-by-gate, not merely in for the whole circuit. In what follows we exploit this and simply treat
U
as a finite-depth,
S
-symmetric quantum circuit.

B.2
Quasi-adiabatic evolution for disorder parameters

We now show that the parity of the endpoint operators of a disorder parameter is invariant under quasi-adiabatic evolution. This is used in Sec.
III.6
to establish that any disorder parameter must be parity-even, and underlies our applications to SPT phases and the disorder-averaged LSM theorem in Sec.
V
.

Recall that a disorder parameter involves a symmetry string
S_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\prod_{j\in\Gamma}S_{j}
over a contiguous interval
\Gamma
, together with endpoint operators
O_{L}
and
O_{R}
localized near the left and right endpoints of
\Gamma
, respectively. As argued above, the quasi-adiabatic evolution
U
can be approximated by a finite-depth quantum circuit whose gates all commute with
S
. We factor this circuit as
U=U_{R}U_{0}U_{L}
, where
U_{L}
(resp.
U_{R}
) consists of all gates in the lightcone of the left (resp. right) endpoint of
\Gamma
, and
U_{0}
contains the remaining bulk gates. Since the bulk gates commute with
S_{\Gamma}
, they pass through the string unchanged, and the dressed disorder operator decomposes as

U^{\dagger}\!\left(O_{L}S_{\Gamma}O_{R}\right)U=\widetilde{O}_{L}\,S_{\Gamma}\,\widetilde{O}_{R},

(179)

where the dressed endpoint operators are

\displaystyle\widetilde{O}_{L}

\displaystyle=U_{L}^{\dagger}O_{L}U_{L}\cdot U_{L}^{\dagger}S_{\Gamma}U_{L}S_{\Gamma}^{-1},

(180)

\displaystyle\widetilde{O}_{R}

\displaystyle=S_{\Gamma}^{-1}U_{R}^{\dagger}S_{\Gamma}U_{R}\cdot U_{R}^{\dagger}O_{R}U_{R}.

(181)

Here the extra factors
U_{L}^{\dagger}S_{\Gamma}U_{L}S_{\Gamma}^{-1}
and
S_{\Gamma}^{-1}U_{R}^{\dagger}S_{\Gamma}U_{R}
results from the action of
U_{L}
and
U_{R}
in the undressed disorder operator.

It remains to check that the parity is preserved. Suppose
SO_{L}S^{-1}=p_{L}O_{L}
. Since both
U_{L}
and
S_{\Gamma}
commute with
S
, we have

\begin{split}S\widetilde{O}_{L}S^{-1}&=S(U_{L}^{\dagger}O_{L}U_{L})S^{-1}\cdot S(U_{L}^{\dagger}S_{\Gamma}U_{L}S_{\Gamma}^{-1})S^{-1}\\
&=p_{L}\,\widetilde{O}_{L},\end{split}

(182)

so
\widetilde{O}_{L}
carries the same parity as
O_{L}
. An identical argument applies to
\widetilde{O}_{R}
. The same reasoning extends to systems with multiple
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetries, as long as the bare disorder operator and all gates in the quasi-adiabatic evolution commute with all symmetries. This is indeed the case for the applications in Sec.
V
.
