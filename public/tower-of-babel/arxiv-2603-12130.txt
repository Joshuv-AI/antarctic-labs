Entanglement cost of bipartite quantum channel discrimination under positive partial transpose operations
Chengkai Zhu, Shuyu He, Gereon Koßmann, Xin Wang

Abstract
Quantum channel discrimination is a fundamental task in quantum information processing. In the one-shot regime, discrimination between two candidate channels is characterized by the diamond norm. Beyond this basic setting, however, many scenarios in distributed quantum information processing remain unresolved, motivating notions of distinguishability that capture the power of the available resources. In this work, we formulate a theory of testers for bipartite channel discrimination, leading to the concept of the entanglement cost of bipartite channel discrimination: the minimum Schmidt rank k k of a shared maximally entangled state required for local protocols to achieve the globally optimal success probability. We introduce k k -injectable testers as a tester-based description of entanglement-assisted local discrimination and, in particular, study the class of k k -injectable positive-partial-transpose (PPT) testers, which constitutes a numerically tractable relaxation of the practically relevant class of LOCC testers. For every k k , we derive a semidefinite program (SDP) for the optimal success probability, which in turn yields an efficiently computable one-shot PPT entanglement cost. To render these optimization problems numerically feasible, we prove a symmetry-reduction principle for covariant channel pairs, thereby reducing the effective dimension of the associated SDPs. Finally, by dualizing the SDP, we derive bounds on the composite channel-discrimination problem and illustrate our framework with proof-of-principle examples based on the depolarizing channel, the depolarized SWAP channel, and the Werner–Holevo channels.



1
Introduction

Quantum channel discrimination is a fundamental primitive in quantum information processing, with a wide range of applications (see, e.g.,
[
DFY07
,
DFY08
,
Pir11
,
WTLB17
,
ZP20
]
and subsequent works). The basic setting assumes an ensemble of quantum channels, each occurring with a specified probability, and allows the tester to use an arbitrary input state, possibly entangled with an untouched memory register, as well as an arbitrary measurement on the joint output register. The goal is to determine the optimal input state and measurement that maximize the probability of correctly identifying the implemented channel. In the special case of discriminating between two channels, this problem admits a well-known and widely applicable solution in terms of the
diamond-norm distance
, first introduced by Kitaev
[
Kit97
]
.

A closer look at the general setup of channel discrimination naturally raises the question of the role of the memory register, which may be viewed as providing entanglement assistance. The usefulness of entanglement in channel discrimination has been studied extensively (see, e.g.,
[
CPR00
,
Ros04
,
Sac05a
,
Sac05b
,
BBCW13
]
). A seminal result of Piani and Watrous
[
PW09
]
shows that every entangled state is useful for some channel discrimination task. Beyond this qualitative advantage, it is also important to understand the
quantitative
entanglement required, namely the minimum ancilla dimension sufficient for optimal discrimination. It is known that an ancilla with dimension equal to the
input
dimension is always sufficient
[
Kit97
]
, and in some cases also necessary
[
PW17
]
. By contrast, an ancilla with dimension equal to the
output
dimension is not always sufficient
[
PW17
]
. However, no general and efficient method is currently known for determining the exact ancilla dimension required for arbitrary pairs of channels.

Moreover, many information theoretic tasks naturally involve
bipartite quantum channels
, that is, channels with two input and two output registers that act jointly on inputs provided by both Alice and Bob, while restricting the allowed probe states and measurements to a practically relevant subclass such as local operations and classical communication (LOCC) or related classes
[
CLM+14
,
CLL06
]
. In
Figure

1
, we show the general case of a bipartite channel discrimination task. It is readily seen that the standard point-to-point channel is recovered as a special case when Bob’s input and Alice’s output are trivial one-dimensional systems. In the bipartite setting, if Alice and Bob, although spatially separated, are allowed to prepare entangled probe states and perform joint measurements, then the optimal performance in binary channel discrimination is again characterized by the diamond-norm distance, just as in the point-to-point case (see
Figure

1
(a)). However, physically motivated constraints often limit Alice and Bob to LOCC in both probe-state preparation and measurement (see
Figure

2
). Strategies subject to such locality constraints are known to be strictly less powerful than global ones
[
MPW10
]
. Nevertheless, a quantitative understanding of the gap between local and global strategies for general bipartite channels remains largely lacking. This leads to the central question of this work:

How much shared entanglement is required to optimally discriminate a bipartite channel in the distributed setting via LOCC?

We term this quantity the entanglement cost of bipartite channel discrimination. This quantity is particularly
acute in a scenario of immense practical relevance to quantum networks and distributed quantum computing, where entanglement distribution is
costly and must be optimized. Specifically, as illustrated in
Figure

1
(b), we aim to quantify the minimum dimension
k
of a shared maximally entangled state
\Phi_{k}
, that allows Alice and Bob to achieve the optimal discrimination probability given by the diamond norm. In the point-to-point limit, our framework naturally recovers two settings: configuration (c) corresponds to utilizing entanglement to enhance the LOCC norm defined in Ref.
[
MPW10
]
to the diamond norm, while configuration (d) corresponds to the problem of determining the minimum ancilla dimension required for the input probe to achieve the diamond norm.

1.1
Contributions

In this work, we introduce the
entanglement cost of bipartite channel discrimination
and develop the mathematical framework of
k
-injectable testers
(cf. Section
3
). These testers formalize discrimination strategies assisted by the shared entanglement of local dimension
k
. By additionally specifying the type of communication allowed in the final measurement stage, one obtains more refined classes, such as
k
-injectable PPT testers. This framework provides a systematic way to quantify the exact entanglement resources required for optimal channel discrimination under locality constraints (cf. Section
4
). A natural problem is then to determine the entanglement cost and success probability for the class of
k
-injectable LOCC testers. However, since the set of LOCC operations is in general not even closed (see
[
CLM+14
]
), it appears extremely difficult to characterize this class numerically. A tractable outer approximation is given by the set of separable testers, that is, testers with separable measurements, and a further outer approximation is provided by the set of PPT testers. Since characterizing separable testers essentially requires solving the separability problem, which is itself computationally hard (see, e.g.,
[
Faw21
]
), we focus in our numerical analysis on the set of PPT testers.

Focusing therefore on the class of PPT operations, which, as discussed above, provides a standard computable relaxation of LOCC, we prove that for fixed entanglement dimension

, the success probability can be computed via a semidefinite program (SDP) (cf. Theorem

. We further generalize our framework from discriminating individual channels to discriminating

Furthermore, we establish that discriminating bipartite depolarized SWAP channels also incurs a PPT entanglement cost of 1 ebit across arbitrary dimensions. Finally, for a pair of

\log_{2}d
ebits.

Organization

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
establishes the notation and reviews the preliminaries of quantum channel discrimination and the diamond norm. In Section

We denote finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces associated with the systems of Alice and Bob as

to note LOCC-operations between
AA^{\prime}
and

. For a POVM

is separable for all

, and it is called a PPT POVM if
(M_{AB}^{(j)})^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}\geq 0
for all

A quantum channel is a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map
{\cal N}_{A\to B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\cal L}(A)\rightarrow{\cal L}(B)
. We denote by

the set of all quantum channels from
{\cal L}(A)
to
{\cal L}(B)
. The Choi-Jamiołkowski operator of a quantum channel

is defined as
J_{A^{\prime}B}^{{\cal N}}\coloneqq\sum_{jk}|j\rangle\!\langle k|_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal N}_{A\to B}(|j\rangle\!\langle k|)
where

. We denote by
\mathrm{id}_{A}
the identity channel on
{\cal L}(A)
. For a bipartite quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\rightarrow A_{1}B_{1}}
, we assume that both input and output systems are shared between Alice and Bob, where we use the subscript ‘
0
’ to denote the input system and the subscript ‘

For any two quantum processes, or, more generally, linear maps, they can be composed whenever the input system of one matches the output system of the other. After their connection, the Choi operator of the whole process can be derived through the

The link product admits both associative and commutative properties, i.e.,

2.2
One-shot quantum channel discrimination

In the task of minimum-error channel discrimination, one is given access to an unknown quantum channel

In this section, we introduce the task of bipartite quantum channel discrimination under locality constraints. This setting arises naturally in distributed quantum computing and quantum
networks, where spatially separated parties cannot perform global operations. The central challenge is quantifying how much entanglement must be shared to overcome this locality restriction. To this end, we formalize the notion of

3.1
Problem formulation

Specifically, consider the physically relevant scenario where two spatially separated parties, Alice and Bob, are restricted to LOCC
[

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{suc}}\Big(\Big\{(\lambda,{\cal N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\rightarrow A_{1}B_{1}}^{(0)}),(1-\lambda,{\cal N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\rightarrow A_{1}B_{1}}^{(1)})\Big\}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\Big\|\lambda\,{\cal N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\rightarrow A_{1}B_{1}}^{(0)}-(1-\lambda)\,{\cal N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\rightarrow A_{1}B_{1}}^{(1)}\Big\|_{\Diamond}\Big).

3.2
Tester under locality constraints

Under the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism, the discrimination process in
Figure

2
can be described by the link product (see Eq. (
1
)) of all input states and physical operations, excluding the unknown channel
[
CDP09
]
. More precisely, for fixed input states
\rho_{A}\in{\cal D}(A),\sigma_{B}\in{\cal D}(B)
, encoding
\operatorname{LOCC}(A\!\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\!B)
channel
{\cal E}_{AB\to A^{\prime}A_{0}B_{0}B^{\prime}}
and a
\operatorname{LOCC}(A_{1}A^{\prime}\!\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\!B_{1}B^{\prime})

\big\{M_{A^{\prime}A_{1}B^{\prime}B_{1}}^{(j)}\big\}_{j}

\big\{T_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}^{(j)}\big\}_{j}
of the channel discrimination task in
Figure

T_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}^{(j)}\coloneqq\rho_{A}\star\sigma_{B}\star J_{ABA_{0}B_{0}A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}^{{\cal E}}\star M_{A^{\prime}A_{1}B^{\prime}B_{1}}^{(j)}.

(9)

Although an LOCC tester, constructed from an LOCC encoding
{\cal E}
and an LOCC POVM
\{M^{(j)}\}_{j}
, represents the most physically relevant class, characterizing such testers mathematically is notoriously difficult (see, e.g.,
[

]

To make the problem tractable, we consider relaxations of the LOCC constraint. A natural relaxation is to replace the LOCC encoding with SEP operations
[
BDF+99
,
Rai99
]
, i.e., channels with product Kraus operators between
A
and
B

{\cal E}_{AB\to A^{\prime}A_{0}B_{0}B^{\prime}}
is a SEP operation if and only if its Choi-Jamiołkowski operator is separable
[

]

J_{ABA_{0}B_{0}A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}^{{\cal E}}=\sum_{x}p_{x}\alpha^{x}_{AA_{0}A^{\prime}}\otimes\beta^{x}_{BB_{0}B^{\prime}}.

Meanwhile, we can relax the LOCC measurement
M^{(j)}
to the SEP POVM, i.e.,

M_{A^{\prime}A_{1}B^{\prime}B_{1}}^{(j)}=\sum_{y}q_{y}\zeta^{y}_{A^{\prime}A_{1}}\otimes\mu^{y}_{B^{\prime}B_{1}}.

Therefore, we arrive at a relaxation of the LOCC tester

\widehat{T}_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}^{(j)}=\sum_{x}\sum_{y}p_{x}q_{y}\operatorname{Tr}_{A_{0}A_{1}}\Big[(\zeta_{A^{\prime}A_{1}}^{y})^{\mathsf{T}}\Big(\alpha^{x}_{AA_{0}A^{\prime}}\rho_{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\Big)\Big]\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_{B_{0}B_{1}}\Big[(\mu_{B^{\prime}B_{1}}^{y})^{\mathsf{T}}\Big(\beta^{x}_{BB_{0}B^{\prime}}\sigma_{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\Big)\Big].

Notice that
\widehat{T}_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}^{(j)}
is a separable operator between
A_{0}A_{1}
and
B_{0}B_{1}
, which motivates the definition of a SEP tester as follows.

Definition 1

(SEP tester)
.

A set of linear operators
\big\{T^{(j)}_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}\big\}_{j=0}^{m-1}
is called an SEP tester if there exists a state
\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}

where

for all

Unfortunately, even the set of separable testers is hard to describe, as it requires basically a solution to the separability problem, which is already known to be computationally intractable for quantum states
[
Faw21
]
. A well-known relaxation, which is even SDP representable, i.e., can be incorporated in a semidefinite program, for the set of separable states, is the positive-partial-transpose (PPT) condition. It is based on the observation that every separable state needs to stay positive under local positive maps, which are not completely positive. The PPT is a well-known work-horse for outer approximations of the set of separable states, as it is positive but not completely positive. We thus take the definition of a SEP tester and relax its separability condition in the following definition for a PPT tester to a computationally tractable condition using the PPT map.

Definition 2

(PPT tester)
.

A set of linear operators
\big\{T^{(j)}_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}\big\}_{j=0}^{m-1}
is called a PPT tester if there exists a state
\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}
such that
T^{(j)}_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}\geq 0,\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}T^{(j)}_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}=\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}
, and each element satisfies

\big(T^{(j)}_{A_{0}B_{0}A_{1}B_{1}}\big)^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}B_{1}}}\geq 0.

Concluding the discussion on different testers under locality constraints, we note the strict inclusions between the following sets

\text{LOCC testers}\subsetneq\text{SEP testers}\subsetneq\text{PPT testers}\subsetneq\text{All testers}.

4.3
Composite quantum channel discrimination

Up to this point, our analysis has assumed that the candidate quantum channels are perfectly known to the discriminating parties. In practical distributed quantum networks, however, channel dynamics are often subject to partial characterization, correlated noise, or systematic uncertainties. In such scenarios, the unknown channel is drawn from one of two possible sets of channels, e.g.,
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
, rather than being a single specific channel. The task of distinguishing between these
sets
is known as
composite quantum channel discrimination

[
BDS25
]
.

In composite quantum channel discrimination, the discriminating parties (Alice and Bob) know the sets
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
, but they do not know exactly which specific channels
{\cal N}
and
{\cal M}
from those sets will be handed to them. Because they must choose their input states, encodings, and POVMs, which together form the tester, before the unknown channel is applied, their choice of tester must be universal for the entire set. Therefore, given prior probabilities
\lambda
and
1-\lambda
, when
k
ebits of shared entanglement assist Alice and Bob, the optimal worst-case success probability of discriminating
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
is defined by

P_{\mathrm{suc},e}^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}(\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1};k,\lambda)\coloneqq\max_{\{T^{(j)}_{k\text{-inj}}\}\in{\operatorname{PPT}}}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\cal N}\in\Omega_{0},\\
{\cal M}\in\Omega_{1}\end{subarray}}\left[\lambda\operatorname{Tr}\big[T_{k\text{-inj}}^{(0)}(\Phi_{k}\otimes J^{{\cal N}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})\big]+(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Tr}\big[T_{k\text{-inj}}^{(1)}(\Phi_{k}\otimes J^{{\cal M}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})\big]\right],

(28)

where the minimization ranges over all possible channels in the two sets. Suppose both
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
are convex and compact. Notice that the set of
k
-injectable PPT testers is convex and compact, and the trace objective function above is bilinear, i.e., linear in
T_{k\text{-inj}}^{(j)}
and linear in the channel. We can therefore use Sion’s minimax theorem
[
Sio58
]
to exchange the
\min
and
\max
first, and then leverage SDP duality to absorb the inner maximization into the outer minimization in Eq. (
28
), resulting in a single, tractable SDP. To this end, we first derive the dual problem of (
23
).

Let us introduce positive semidefinite Lagrange multipliers
C_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},D_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},E_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},G_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},H_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},K_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\geq 0
for the operator inequalities, and scalar multipliers
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
for the trace constraints on the marginals. The Lagrangian function of the primal problem is given by

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=1-\lambda+\operatorname{Tr}\big[W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\big(\lambda J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal N}}-(1-\lambda)J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal M}}\big)\big]

(29)

\displaystyle+\langle C_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\rangle+\langle D_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle E_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}-(1-k)Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}}\rangle+\langle G_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},(1+k)Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}}-W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle H_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}-(1-k)(\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-Q_{AB}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}})\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle K_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},(1+k)(\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}})-(\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}})\rangle

\displaystyle+\alpha(1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}})+\beta(1-\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}})

\displaystyle=1-\lambda+\alpha+\beta

\displaystyle+\Big\langle\lambda J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal N}}-(1-\lambda)J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal M}}-C_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}+E_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}-G_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}-H_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}+K_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}},W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+\Big\langle\!\!-D_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}-(1-k)E_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}+(1+k)G_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}+(1-k)H_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}}-(1+k)K_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{T_{\mathbf{B}}},Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+\Big\langle\operatorname{Tr}_{A_{1}B_{1}}\big(C_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}+H_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}-K_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}\big)-\alpha{\mathds{1}}_{A_{0}B_{0}},\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+\Big\langle\operatorname{Tr}_{A_{1}B_{1}}\big(D_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}+(k-1)H^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}+(k+1)K^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\big)-\beta 1_{A_{0}B_{0}},\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}}\Big\rangle.

The dual function is obtained by taking the supremum over the primal variables
W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}},\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}}\geq 0

Setting all variables proportional to the identity operator provides a strictly feasible interior point for the primal problem (

\displaystyle P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal N},{\cal M};k,\lambda)=\min

\displaystyle 1-\lambda+\alpha+\beta

(30)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{A_{1}B_{1}}\Big[D_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}+(k-1)H_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}+(k+1)K_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}\Big]\leq\beta{\mathds{1}}_{A_{0}B_{0}}.

\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{suc},e}^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}(\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1};k,\lambda)=\min

\displaystyle 1-\lambda+\alpha+\beta

(31)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle C_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},~D_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},~E_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},~G_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},~H_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},~K_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\geq 0,

5.1
Covariant channel

For a finite group
{\cal G}
and every

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}\Big(\big(U_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{0}}(g)\big)\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}\big(U_{A_{0}}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes V_{B_{0}}^{\dagger}(g)\big)\Big)

(33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(U_{A_{1}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{1}}(g)\Big){\cal N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}(\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}})\Big(U_{A_{1}}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes V_{B_{1}}^{\dagger}(g)\Big),\quad\forall\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}\in{\cal D}(A_{0}B_{0}).

•

(U_{A_{0}},V_{B_{0}})
-cross-covariant if for all
g\in{\cal G}

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}\Big(\big(U_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{0}}(g)\big)\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}\big(U_{A_{0}}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes V_{B_{0}}^{\dagger}(g)\big)\Big)

(34)

\displaystyle\Big(V_{A_{1}}(g)\otimes U_{B_{1}}(g)\Big){\cal N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}(\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}})\Big(V_{A_{1}}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes U_{B_{1}}^{\dagger}(g)\Big),\quad\forall\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}\in{\cal D}(A_{0}B_{0}).

.

Let

Proof.

This readily follows from the definition of the Choi-Jamiołkowski operator and Eq. (

When
B_{0}
and

are one-dimensional trivial subsystems, the channel covariance reduces to

We now apply this framework to the task of discriminating between two covariant channels,

Proposition 5.2

(Symmetry reduction under channel covariance)
.

Let
{\cal G}
be a compact group with Haar measure
\mu
and two unitary representations
g\mapsto U_{A_{0}}(g)
on
{\cal H}_{A_{0}}
and
g\mapsto V_{B_{0}}(g)
on
{\cal H}_{B_{0}}

{\cal N}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}

(U_{A_{0}},V_{B_{0}})
-covariant, i.e., for all
g\in{\cal G}
and
\rho\in{\cal D}(A_{0}B_{0})

{\cal C}\Big(\big(U_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{0}}(g)\big)\rho\big(U_{A_{0}}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes V_{B_{0}}^{\dagger}(g)\big)\Big)=\Big(U_{A_{1}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{1}}(g)\Big){\cal C}(\rho)\Big(U_{A_{1}}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes V_{B_{1}}^{\dagger}(g)\Big),\forall{\cal C}\in\{{\cal N},{\cal M}\},

and
{\cal H}_{A_{0}}\cong{\cal H}_{A_{1}},{\cal H}_{B_{0}}\cong{\cal H}_{B_{1}}
.
Define

\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(g)\coloneqq\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{0}}(g)\otimes U_{A_{1}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{1}}(g).

(35)

Then the optimal value of SDP (
22
) is unchanged if one adds the invariance constraints: for all
g\in{\cal G}
:

i).

\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(g)W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(g)^{\dagger}=W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}
,

ii).

\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(g)Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(g)^{\dagger}=Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}
,

iii).

\Big(\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{0}}(g)\Big)\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}\Big(\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{0}}(g)\Big)^{\dagger}=\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}
,

\Big(\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{0}}(g)\Big)\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}}\Big(\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{0}}(g)\Big)^{\dagger}=\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}}

Proof.

Define the twirls as follows

{\cal S}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(X)\!\coloneqq\!\!\int_{G}\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(g)X\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\dagger}(g)\,d\mu(g),~{\cal S}_{A_{0}B_{0}}(Y)\!\coloneqq\!\!\int_{G}\Big(\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{0}}(g)\Big)Y\Big(\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{0}}(g)\Big)^{\dagger}\,d\mu(g).

Both maps are
unital
,
completely positive
,
trace preserving
, self-adjoint w.r.t. the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, and idempotent. By Lemma
5.1
, we have that
{\cal S}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(J^{{\cal N}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}-J^{{\cal M}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})=J^{{\cal N}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}-J^{{\cal M}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}
. Therefore, for any feasible
(W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}},\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}},\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}})
of SDP (
22

\operatorname{Tr}\Big[W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(J^{{\cal N}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}-J^{{\cal M}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})\Big]=\operatorname{Tr}\Big[{\cal S}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})\,{\cal S}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(J^{{\cal N}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}-J^{{\cal M}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})\Big]=\operatorname{Tr}\Big[{\cal S}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})\,(J^{{\cal N}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}-J^{{\cal M}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})\Big],

i.e.,
{\cal S}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})
yields the same objective value. Now, let

\bar{W}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\coloneqq{\cal S}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(W_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}),~\bar{Q}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\coloneqq{\cal S}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(Q_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}),~\bar{\rho}_{A_{0}B_{0}}\coloneqq{\cal S}_{A_{0}B_{0}}(\rho_{A_{0}B_{0}}),~\bar{\sigma}_{A_{0}B_{0}}\coloneqq{\cal S}_{A_{0}B_{0}}(\sigma_{A_{0}B_{0}}).

(36)

Since the positivity and operator order are preserved under unitary conjugation and convex averaging, it follows that

0\leq\bar{W}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\leq\bar{\rho}_{A_{0}B_{0}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}},\quad 0\leq\bar{Q}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\leq\bar{\sigma}_{A_{0}B_{0}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}},

(37)

and
\operatorname{Tr}\bar{\rho}_{A_{0}B_{0}}=\operatorname{Tr}\bar{\sigma}_{A_{0}B_{0}}=1
. For the
\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}
-constraints, notice that

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{0}}(g)\otimes U_{A_{1}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{1}}(g)\Big)X\Big(\overline{U}^{\dagger}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes\overline{V}^{\dagger}_{B_{0}}(g)\otimes U^{\dagger}_{A_{1}}(g)\otimes V^{\dagger}_{B_{1}}(g)\Big)\Big]^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}B_{1}}}

(38)

\displaystyle\Big(\overline{U}^{\dagger}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{0}}(g)\otimes U_{A_{1}}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes\overline{V}_{B_{1}}(g)\Big)X^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}B_{1}}}\Big(\overline{U}_{A_{0}}(g)\otimes V^{\dagger}_{B_{0}}(g)\otimes U_{A_{1}}(g)\otimes V_{B_{1}}^{\dagger}(g)\Big).

(1-k)\bar{Q}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}}\leq\bar{W}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}}\leq(1+k)\bar{Q}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}},

(39)

because we can exchange the order of the integral and
\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}B_{1}}

(1-k)(\bar{\sigma}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}_{A_{0}B_{0}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-\bar{Q}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}})\leq\bar{\rho}_{A_{0}B_{0}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-\bar{W}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}}\leq(1+k)(\bar{\sigma}_{A_{0}B_{0}}^{\mathsf{T}_{B_{0}}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}-\bar{Q}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{B}}}).

(40)

Thus,
(\bar{W},\bar{Q},\bar{\rho},\bar{\sigma})
is also feasible of SDP (
22

satisfy
\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(g)(\cdot)\Gamma_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\dagger}(g)=(\cdot)
and

satisfy

for all

5.1.1
Depolarizing channel

As a canonical example of covariant channels, we consider the depolarizing channel, which serves as the standard theoretical model for uniform quantum noise. We first investigate the bipartite setting, where the noise acts globally across the joint input of Alice and Bob, and subsequently, the point-to-point setting.

For a bipartite input system
A_{0}B_{0}
and a bipartite output system
A_{1}B_{1}
where
{\cal H}_{A_{0}}\cong{\cal H}_{A_{1}},{\cal H}_{B_{0}}\cong{\cal H}_{B_{1}}
, a bipartite depolarizing channel
{\cal D}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}(\cdot)
with noise parameter
p
is defined by

{\cal D}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}(X)=(1-p)X+p\operatorname{Tr}(X)\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}}{d_{A_{1}B_{1}}}.

(42)

The Choi operators of the depolarizing channels are given by

J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal D}^{p}}=d_{A_{0}}d_{B_{0}}(1-p)\Phi_{d_{A_{0}}}\otimes\Phi_{d_{B_{0}}}+p\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}}{d_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}}.

(43)

By applying the general symmetry reduction established in Proposition
5.2
, we can significantly simplify the optimization complexity. The full SDP formulation reduces to a tractable linear program over the isotropic subspace, as detailed in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.4

(Symmetry reduction for bipartite depolarizing channels)
.

Let
{\cal D}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}
and
{\cal D}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}^{q}
be two bipartite depolarizing channels with distinct noise parameters
p
and
q
, respectively. The maximal
k
-ebit-assisted success probability of discriminating
{\cal D}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}},{\cal D}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}^{q}
via a
k
-injectable PPT tester is given by the following linear program.

\displaystyle P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};k,\frac{1}{2})=\max

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(p-q)\left(d_{A_{0}B_{0}}-\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}\right)(x-y)

(44)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle x,y,u,v\in[0,1/d_{A_{0}B_{0}}],

\displaystyle(1-k)u\leq x\leq(1+k)u,

\displaystyle(1-k)v\leq y\leq(1+k)v,

\displaystyle(1-k)\left(\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}-u\right)\leq\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}-x\leq(1+k)\left(\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}-u\right),

\displaystyle(1-k)\left(\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}-v\right)\leq\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}-y\leq(1+k)\left(\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}-v\right).

Proof.

See Appendix
B
.
∎

) for sets of depolarizing channels. Suppose the noise parameters of the channels are not perfectly characterized, but are known to belong to two disjoint compact sets

and

Corollary 5.5

(Composite discrimination of depolarizing channels)
.

Let
\Omega_{0}=\{{\cal D}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}p\in P\}
and
\Omega_{1}=\{{\cal D}^{q}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}q\in Q\}
be two sets of bipartite depolarizing channels defined by compact parameter sets
P,Q\subset[0,1]
such that
p>q
for all
p\in P
and
q\in Q
. The
k
-ebit-assisted optimal worst-case success probability of discriminating
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}

(45)

subject to the same constraints on
(x,y,u,v)
as in Proposition
5.4
, where
\Delta_{P,Q}\coloneqq\min_{p\in P,q\in Q}(p-q)
is the minimum noise gap between the two channel sets.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.4
, we have

P_{\mathrm{suc},e}^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}\Big(\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1};k,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\max_{(x,y)\in{\cal D}_{k}}\min_{p\in P,q\in Q}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(p-q)\left(d_{A_{0}B_{0}}-\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}\right)(x-y)\right],

(46)

where
\mathcal{D}_{k}
is the feasible region of the scalar variables
(x,y,u,v)
parameterized by
k
and
d_{A_{0}B_{0}}
. Because
{\cal D}_{k}
is independent of
P
and
Q
, and the expression
\left(d_{A_{0}B_{0}}-\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}B_{0}}}\right)(x-y)
is strictly non-negative for any optimal choice of
x
and
y
, the inner minimization strictly evaluates to minimizing
(p-q)
over
P\times Q
. Taking
\Delta_{P,Q}=\min_{p\in P,q\in Q}(p-q)
completes the proof.
∎

Using the linear program reduction in Proposition
5.4
, we show that for a pair of bipartite depolarizing channels, the one-shot PPT entanglement cost of minimum-error discrimination is
0
ebits.

Proposition 5.6

(PPT entanglement cost of bipartite depolarizing channels)
.

For any bipartite depolarizing channels
{\cal D}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}},{\cal D}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}^{q}
with distinct noise parameters
p,q
and equiprobable priors, the one-shot PPT entanglement cost of minimum-error discrimination is
0
ebits.

Proof.

From Proposition
5.4
, when
k=1
, let

x\coloneqq\frac{1}{d_{A_{0}}d_{B_{0}}},\quad y\coloneqq 0,\quad u=v\coloneqq\frac{1}{2d_{A_{0}}d_{B_{0}}}.

(47)

By simple calculation, these solutions are feasible. Thus we have

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}},{\cal D}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}^{q};k,\frac{1}{2}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d_{A_{0}}^{2}d_{B_{0}}^{2}-1)}{2d_{A_{0}}^{2}d_{B_{0}}^{2}}.

Consider the dual SDP (
5
), let

\alpha\coloneqq\frac{(p-q)(d_{A_{0}}^{2}d_{B_{0}}^{2}-1)}{2d_{A_{0}}^{2}d_{B_{0}}^{2}},\quad C_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}\coloneqq\frac{(p-q)({\mathds{1}}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}-\Phi_{d_{A_{0}}}\otimes\Phi_{d_{B_{0}}})}{2d_{A_{0}}d_{B_{0}}}.

(48)

It can be checked that these solutions are also feasible for dual SDP. Hence, we conclude that

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};k,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=P_{\mathrm{suc}}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d_{A_{0}}^{2}d_{B_{0}}^{2}-1)}{2d_{A_{0}}^{2}d_{B_{0}}^{2}}.

∎

This result is physically intuitive because the bipartite depolarizing channel acts globally and symmetrically across Alice and Bob’s joint system; the optimal discrimination strategy does not require them to establish any further non-local correlations. However, the situation changes drastically when we consider point-to-point depolarizing channels, where the noise acts purely locally on a system transmitted from Alice to Bob. First, we can also have the symmetry reduction for the point-to-point case based on Proposition
5.4
as follows.

Proposition 5.7

(Symmetry reduction for point-to-point depolarizing channels)
.

Let
{\cal D}_{A\to B}^{p}
and
{\cal D}_{A\to B}^{q}
be two depolarizing channels with distinct noise parameters
p
and
q
, respectively where
d_{A}=d_{B}=d
. The maximal
k
-ebit-assisted success probability of discriminating
{\cal D}_{A\to B}^{p}
and
{\cal D}_{A\to B}^{q}
via a
k
-injectable PPT tester is given by the following linear program.

\displaystyle P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal D}_{A\to B}^{p},{\cal D}_{A\to B}^{q};k,\frac{1}{2})=\max

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(p-q)\left(d-\frac{1}{d}\right)(x-y)

(49)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle x,y,u,v\in[0,1/d],

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}\coloneqq y\pm\frac{x-y}{d},\qquad\mu_{\pm}\coloneqq v\pm\frac{u-v}{d},

\displaystyle(1-k)\mu_{\pm}\leq\lambda_{\pm}\leq(1+k)\mu_{\pm},

\displaystyle(1-k)\left(\frac{1}{d}-\mu_{\pm}\right)\leq\frac{1}{d}-\lambda_{\pm}\leq(1+k)\left(\frac{1}{d}-\mu_{\pm}\right).

Proof.

See Appendix
C
.
∎

Utilizing Proposition
5.7
, we demonstrate that the PPT entanglement cost for a pair of distinct point-to-point depolarizing channels is nonzero. Moreover, surprisingly, one ebit suffices for optimal discrimination between any two different point-to-point depolarizing channels of arbitrary dimension via PPT testers.

Proposition 5.8

(PPT entanglement cost of point-to-point depolarizing channels)
.

For any
d
-dimensional point-to-point depolarizing channels
{\cal D}^{p}_{A\to B},{\cal D}_{A\to B}^{q}
with distinct noise parameters
p,q
and equiprobable priors, the one-shot PPT entanglement cost of minimum-error discrimination is
1
ebit.

Proof.

First, we will show that when
k=2
,

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};2,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=P_{\mathrm{suc}}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d^{2}-1)}{2d^{2}},

which means
k=2
is sufficient. Next, we will show that when
k=1
,

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};1,\frac{1}{2}\Big)<P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};2,\frac{1}{2}\Big),

which indicates that
k=2
is necessary.

When
k=2
, for Proposition
5.7
, let

x\coloneqq\frac{1}{d},\quad y\coloneqq 0,\quad u=v\coloneqq\frac{1}{d^{2}}.

(50)

It follows that

\lambda_{\pm}=\pm\frac{1}{d^{2}},\quad\mu_{\pm}=\frac{1}{d^{2}},

and constitute a feasible solution since

-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\leq\pm\frac{1}{d^{2}}\leq\frac{3}{d^{2}},~-\Big(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{d}\mp\frac{1}{d^{2}}\leq 3\Big(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\Big).

Thus we have

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};2,\frac{1}{2}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d^{2}-1)}{2d^{2}}.

Further, we consider the dual SDP (
5
). Let

C_{AB}\coloneqq\frac{(p-q)(d^{2}-1)}{2d}\Phi_{d},~\alpha\coloneqq\frac{(p-q)(d^{2}-1)}{2d^{2}}.

(51)

It can be checked that these solutions are feasible, and we have

P_{\mathrm{suc}}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};\frac{1}{2}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d^{2}-1)}{2d^{2}}.

Since
P_{\mathrm{suc}}({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};1/2)\geq P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};2,\frac{1}{2}\Big)
, we have

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};2,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=P_{\mathrm{suc}}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d^{2}-1)}{2d^{2}}.

When
k=1
, let

x\coloneqq\frac{1}{d},\quad y\coloneqq\frac{1}{d(d+1)},\quad u\coloneqq\frac{1}{d},\quad v\coloneqq\frac{d+2}{2d(d+1)}.

(52)

It follows that

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{1\pm 1}{d(d+1)},\quad\mu_{\pm}=\frac{d+2\pm 1}{2d(d+1)},

and constitute a feasible solution since

0\leq\frac{1\pm 1}{d(d+1)}\leq\frac{d+2\pm 1}{d(d+1)},\quad 0\leq\left(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1\pm 1}{d(d+1)}\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{d+2\pm 1}{2d(d+1)}\right).

Thus, we have

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};1,\frac{1}{2}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d-1)}{2d}.

Now consider the dual SDP (
30
) for point-to-point channels

\displaystyle P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal N},{\cal M};k,\lambda)=\min

\displaystyle 1-\lambda+\alpha+\beta

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle C_{AB},~D_{AB},~E_{AB},~G_{AB},~H_{AB},~K_{AB}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\lambda J_{AB}^{{\cal N}}-(1-\lambda)J_{AB}^{{\cal M}}\leq H_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}-K_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}+G_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}-E_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}+C_{AB},

\displaystyle(1+k)(G_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}-K_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}})+(1-k)(H_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}-E_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}})\leq D_{AB},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}(C_{AB}+H_{AB}-K_{AB})\leq\alpha{\mathds{1}}_{A},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\big[D_{AB}+(k-1)H_{AB}+(k+1)K_{AB}\big]\leq\beta{\mathds{1}}_{A}.

Construct

\alpha\coloneqq\frac{(p-q)(d-1)}{2d},~C_{AB}\coloneqq\frac{(p-q)(d-1)}{2}\Phi_{d},~E_{AB}\coloneqq\frac{(p-q)}{2d}({\mathds{1}}-F).

(53)

It can be checked that these solutions are feasible, and we have

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};1,\frac{1}{2}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d-1)}{2d}.

Thus, we conclude that

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};1,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(p-q)(d-1)}{2d}.

Hence, we have shown that

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};1,\frac{1}{2}\Big)<P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};2,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=P_{\mathrm{suc}}\Big({\cal D}^{p},{\cal D}^{q};\frac{1}{2}\Big),

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

A channel
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
is said
to be PPT if
\mathsf{T}\circ{\cal N}
is a quantum channel, where
\mathsf{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\cal L}({\cal H}_{B})\to{\cal L}({\cal H}_{B})
is the transpose map
[
HHH00
]
. Equivalently,
{\cal N}_{A\to B}
is PPT if and only if its Choi operator is PPT, i.e.,
(J_{AB}^{{\cal N}})^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}\geq 0
. Notably, PPT channels can distribute bound entanglement but never distillable entanglement.
A point-to-point depolarizing channel
{\cal D}^{p}_{A\to B}(X_{A})=(1-p)X_{A}+p\operatorname{Tr}(X_{A})\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{A}}{d_{A}}
is PPT if and only if
p\geq\frac{d}{1+d}
. Proposition
5.8
demonstrates that for any two PPT depolarizing channels
{\cal D}^{p}_{A\to B}
and
{\cal D}^{q}_{A\to B}
with
p,q\geq\frac{d}{1+d}
, optimal discrimination requires nontrivial entanglement resources, even though these channels themselves cannot generate non-positive partial transpose (NPT) entanglement.

5.1.2
Depolarized SWAP channel

Bipartite communication channels that actively exchange information between Alice and Bob naturally exhibit cross-covariance. A paradigmatic example of such dynamics is the depolarized SWAP channel, which applies a global SWAP operation subject to uniform white noise, i.e.,

{\cal S}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}(X)=(1-p)F_{A_{0}B_{0}}XF_{A_{0}B_{0}}+p\operatorname{Tr}(X)\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{A_{1}B_{1}}}{d_{A_{1}B_{1}}},

(54)

where
F_{A_{0}B_{0}}
is the SWAP operator between
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
. In the following, we apply Proposition
5.2
to reduce the SDP formulation for a pair of distinct depolarized SWAP channels to a tractable linear program.

Proposition 5.10

(Symmetry reduction for depolarized SWAP channels)
.

Let
{\cal S}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}
and
{\cal S}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}^{q}
be two depolarized SWAP channels with distinct noise parameters
p
and
q
, respectively, where
d_{A}=d_{B}=d
. The maximal
k
-ebit-assisted success probability of discriminating
{\cal S}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}},{\cal S}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}^{q}
via a
k
-injectable PPT tester is given by the following linear program.

P_{suc,e}^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}({\cal S}^{p},{\cal S}^{q};k,\frac{1}{2})=\max\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-q}{2}\left[\left(d^{2}-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\right)w_{1}-\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{2}}(w_{2}+w_{3})-\frac{(d^{2}-1)^{2}}{d^{2}}w_{4}\right]

subject to
w_{i},q_{i}\in[0,1/d^{2}]
for
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, and for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in\{+1,-1\}
:

i).

(1-k)\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}(Q)\leq\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}(W)\leq(1+k)\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}(Q)
,

ii).

(1-k)\left[\frac{1}{d^{2}}-\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}(Q)\right]\leq\frac{1}{d^{2}}-\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}(W)\leq(1+k)\left[\frac{1}{d^{2}}-\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}(Q)\right]
,

where the eigenvalue functions
\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}(W)
and
\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}(Q)
are defined by

\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}(W)\coloneqq w_{4}+s_{1}\frac{w_{2}-w_{4}}{d}+s_{2}\frac{w_{3}-w_{4}}{d}+s_{1}s_{2}\frac{w_{1}-w_{2}-w_{3}+w_{4}}{d^{2}},

and
\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}(Q)
takes the exact same form substituting
w_{i}
with
q_{i}
.

Proof.

See Appendix
D
.
∎

By analytically evaluating the linear program derived in Proposition
5.10
, we prove that the exact PPT entanglement cost for discriminating depolarized SWAP channels is
1
ebit for arbitrary dimension.

Proposition 5.11

(PPT entanglement cost of depolarized SWAP channels)
.

For any
d
-dimensional depolarized SWAP channels
{\cal S}^{p}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}
and
{\cal S}^{q}_{A_{0}B_{0}\to A_{1}B_{1}}
with distinct noise parameters
p>q
and equiprobable priors, the one-shot PPT entanglement cost of minimum-error discrimination is
1
ebit.

Proof.

First, we will show that when
k=2
,

P_{\mathrm{suc},e}^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}\Big({\cal S}^{p},{\cal S}^{q};2,\frac{1}{2}\Big)=P_{\mathrm{suc}}\Big({\cal S}^{p},{\cal S}^{q};\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-q}{2}\frac{d^{4}-1}{d^{4}},

which means
k=2
is sufficient to achieve the global optimum. Next, we will show that when
k=1
, the success probability is strictly bounded by
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-q}{2}\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{2}}
, indicating that
k=2
is necessary.

When
k=2
, in the linear program of Proposition
5.10
, let

w_{1}\coloneqq\frac{1}{d^{2}},\quad w_{2}=w_{3}=w_{4}\coloneqq 0,\quad q_{1}=q_{2}=q_{3}=q_{4}\coloneqq\frac{1}{d^{4}}.

Substituting these values into the eigenvalue functions yields

\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}(W)=s_{1}s_{2}\frac{1}{d^{4}},\quad\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}(Q)=\frac{1}{d^{4}}\quad\forall s_{1},s_{2}\in\{+1,-1\}.

We then verify the feasibility of this assignment. The boundary constraints
0\leq w_{i}\leq 1/d^{2}
and
0\leq q_{i}\leq 1/d^{2}
are satisfied since
1/d^{4}\leq 1/d^{2}
. For
k=2
, the primary PPT constraints
(1-k)\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}\leq\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}\leq(1+k)\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}
evaluate exactly to

-\frac{1}{d^{4}}\leq\pm\frac{1}{d^{4}}\leq\frac{3}{d^{4}},

which holds. The complementary PPT constraints
(1-k)\left(\frac{1}{d^{2}}-\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{d^{2}}-\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}\leq(1+k)\left(\frac{1}{d^{2}}-\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}\right)
become

-\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{4}}\leq\frac{1}{d^{2}}\mp\frac{1}{d^{4}}\leq\frac{3(d^{2}-1)}{d^{4}}

For
\lambda=1/d^{4}
, the inequality
-\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{4}}\leq\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{4}}\leq\frac{3(d^{2}-1)}{d^{4}}
holds for any
d\geq 1
. For
\lambda=-1/d^{4}
, the inequality
-\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{4}}\leq\frac{d^{2}+1}{d^{4}}\leq\frac{3(d^{2}-1)}{d^{4}}
simplifies on the right-hand side to
d^{2}+1\leq 3d^{2}-3\implies 2d^{2}\geq 4
, which holds for any valid local dimension
d\geq 2
. Thus, this constitutes a feasible solution that reaches the maximum possible trace objective.

When
k=1
, the PPT constraints reduce to
0\leq\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}\leq 2\mu_{s_{1},s_{2}}
. This strictly requires
\lambda_{s_{1},s_{2}}(W)\geq 0
for all sign combinations. Specifically, evaluating the cross-sign cases
\lambda_{+-}
and
\lambda_{-+}
gives

\displaystyle\lambda_{+-}

\displaystyle=w_{4}+\frac{w_{2}-w_{4}}{d}-\frac{w_{3}-w_{4}}{d}-\frac{w_{1}-w_{2}-w_{3}+w_{4}}{d^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\Big[(d^{2}-1)w_{4}+d(w_{2}-w_{3})+w_{2}+w_{3}-w_{1}\Big]\geq 0

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{-+}

\displaystyle=w_{4}-\frac{w_{2}-w_{4}}{d}+\frac{w_{3}-w_{4}}{d}-\frac{w_{1}-w_{2}-w_{3}+w_{4}}{d^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\Big[(d^{2}-1)w_{4}+d(w_{3}-w_{2})+w_{2}+w_{3}-w_{1}\Big]\geq 0.

Summing these two inequalities yields

\lambda_{+-}+\lambda_{-+}=\frac{2(d^{2}-1)w_{4}+2(w_{2}+w_{3})-2w_{1}}{d^{2}}\geq 0,

which provides the strict algebraic requirement

w_{2}+w_{3}+(d^{2}-1)w_{4}\geq w_{1}.

We now evaluate the trace objective under this condition. Factoring the objective function

\operatorname{Tr}[W(J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal S}^{q}}-J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal S}^{p}})]=(p-q)\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{2}}\left[(d^{2}+1)w_{1}-(w_{2}+w_{3})-(d^{2}-1)w_{4}\right].

Applying the necessary condition
-(w_{2}+w_{3})-(d^{2}-1)w_{4}\leq-w_{1}
, we strictly bound the objective by

\operatorname{Tr}[W(J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal S}^{q}}-J_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{{\cal S}^{p}})]\leq(p-q)\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{2}}\left[(d^{2}+1)w_{1}-w_{1}\right]=(p-q)(d^{2}-1)w_{1}\leq(p-q)\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{2}}.

where we used the condition
w_{1}\leq 1/d^{2}
in the inequality. Therefore, the maximal success probability for
k=1
is strictly bounded by

P_{\mathrm{suc},e}^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}\left({\cal S}^{p},{\cal S}^{q};1,\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-q}{2}\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{2}}.

Since
\frac{d^{2}-1}{d^{2}}=\frac{d^{4}-d^{2}}{d^{4}}<\frac{d^{4}-1}{d^{4}}
for any
d\geq 2
, the unassisted
k=1
strategy is strictly suboptimal. Thus, 1 ebit of entanglement is necessary to close the gap.
∎

5.2
Werner-Holevo channel

Beyond covariant channels, we further consider another class of fundamental channels, the Werner-Holevo channels
[
WH02
]
. For an arbitrary dimension
d\geq 2
, the Werner-Holevo channels, denoted as
{\cal W}_{0}
and
{\cal W}_{1}
, are defined via the transpose map by

{\cal W}_{0}(\rho)=\frac{1}{d+1}\left[\operatorname{Tr}(\rho){\mathds{1}}+\rho^{\mathsf{T}}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal W}_{1}(\rho)=\frac{1}{d-1}\left[\operatorname{Tr}(\rho){\mathds{1}}-\rho^{\mathsf{T}}\right].

(55)

Leveraging the primal and dual SDP formulations established in Theorem
4.1
, we precisely quantify the entanglement resources required for this quantum channel discrimination task.

Proposition 5.12
.

For the
d
-dimensional Werner-Holevo channels
{\cal W}_{0}
and
{\cal W}_{1}
, the one-shot PPT entanglement cost of minimum-error discrimination is
\log_{2}d
ebits.

Proof.

First, we show that when
k=d
, we have that
P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal W}_{0},{\cal W}_{1};d,\lambda)=1
.
The Choi operators of the Werner-Holevo channels are given by

J^{{\cal W}_{0}}_{AB}=\frac{1}{d+1}({\mathds{1}}_{AB}+F_{AB}),\quad J^{{\cal W}_{1}}_{AB}=\frac{1}{d-1}({\mathds{1}}_{AB}-F_{AB}),

(56)

where
d_{A}=d_{B}=d
. Let us construct

W_{AB}^{(0)}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2d}({\mathds{1}}_{AB}+F_{AB}),~W_{AB}^{(1)}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2d}({\mathds{1}}_{AB}-F_{AB}),~Q_{AB}^{(0)}=Q_{AB}^{(1)}\coloneqq\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{AB}}{2d},~\sigma_{A}=\rho_{A}\coloneqq\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{A}}{d}.

(57)

We have that

(1-\lambda)+\Big[\lambda\operatorname{Tr}(W_{AB}^{(0)}J^{{\cal W}_{0}}_{AB})-(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Tr}(W_{AB}^{(0)}J^{{\cal W}_{1}}_{AB})\Big]=(1-\lambda)+\lambda\Big[\frac{1}{d(d+1)}\operatorname{Tr}({\mathds{1}}_{AB}+F_{AB})\Big]=1,

and

(W_{AB}^{(0)})^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}=\frac{1}{2d}({\mathds{1}}_{AB}+d\Phi_{AB}),~(W_{AB}^{(1)})^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}=\frac{1}{2d}({\mathds{1}}_{AB}-d\Phi_{AB}).

Since
{\mathds{1}}_{AB}\geq\Phi_{AB}
and
1-k=1-d<0
, it can be checked that

\displaystyle\rho_{A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B}-(1+d)(\sigma_{A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B}-Q_{AB}^{(0)})^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}=\frac{(1-d){\mathds{1}}_{AB}}{2d}\leq\frac{({\mathds{1}}_{AB}+d\Phi_{AB})}{2d}=(W_{AB}^{(0)})^{\mathsf{T}_{B}},

\displaystyle\rho_{A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B}-(1-d)(\sigma_{A}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{B}-Q_{AB}^{(0)})^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}=\frac{(1+d){\mathds{1}}_{AB}}{2d}\geq\frac{{\mathds{1}}_{AB}+d\Phi_{AB}}{2d}=(W_{AB}^{(0)})^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}.

Thus, the operators in Eq. (
57
) constitute a feasible solution to SDP (
22
) which yields that
P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal W}_{0},{\cal W}_{1};d,\lambda)\geq 1
. As
P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal W}_{0},{\cal W}_{1};d,\lambda)\leq 1
by definition, we conclude that

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal W}_{0},{\cal W}_{1};d,\lambda)=1.

In the following, we shall show that
P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal W}_{0},{\cal W}_{1};k,\lambda)<1
for any
2\leq k<d
, by considering the dual SDP (
30
)

\displaystyle P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal N},{\cal M};k,\lambda)=\min

\displaystyle 1-\lambda+\alpha+\beta

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle C_{AB},~D_{AB},~E_{AB},~G_{AB},~H_{AB},~K_{AB}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\lambda J_{AB}^{{\cal N}}-(1-\lambda)J_{AB}^{{\cal M}}\leq H_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}-K_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}+G_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}-E_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}+C_{AB},

\displaystyle(1+k)(G_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}-K_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}})+(1-k)(H_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}-E_{AB}^{\mathsf{T}_{B}})\leq D_{AB},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}(C_{AB}+H_{AB}-K_{AB})\leq\alpha{\mathds{1}}_{A},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\big[D_{AB}+(k-1)H_{AB}+(k+1)K_{AB}\big]\leq\beta{\mathds{1}}_{A}.

Case 1:

0\leq\lambda\leq(d+1)/2d
. Let
C=D=E=K=0
and

\beta=(k-1)\alpha\coloneqq\frac{\lambda(k-1)(k+1)}{k(d+1)},~G\coloneqq\frac{\lambda(k-1)d\Phi_{AB}}{k(d+1)}\geq 0,~H\coloneqq\frac{\lambda(k+1)d\Phi_{AB}}{k(d+1)}\geq 0.

(58)

We will verify that these operators constitute a feasible solution to the dual SDP above.
Noticing
(1+k)G^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}+(1-k)H^{\mathsf{T}_{B}}=0,~{\mathds{1}}_{AB}\geq F_{AB}
, and
0\leq\lambda\leq(d+1)/2d
, we have that

Further, notice that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}(C_{AB}+H_{AB}-K_{AB})=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}H_{AB}=\frac{\lambda(k+1)}{k(d+1)}{\mathds{1}}_{A}=\alpha{\mathds{1}}_{A},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\big[D_{AB}+(k-1)H_{AB}+(k+1)K_{AB}\big]=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[(k-1)H_{AB}]=\frac{\lambda(k-1)(k+1)}{k(d+1)}{\mathds{1}}_{A}=\beta{\mathds{1}}_{A}.

Hence, the operators in Eq. (
58
) constitute a feasible solution to the dual, which yields that when
k<d
,

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal W}_{0},{\cal W}_{1};k,\lambda)\leq 1-\lambda+\frac{\lambda(k+1)}{(d+1)}<1.

Case 2:

(d+1)/2d<\lambda\leq 1
, let

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(2d\lambda-d-1)k+(1-\lambda)(k+1)}{k(d-1)},~\beta\coloneqq\frac{(1-\lambda)(k+1)(k-1)}{k(d-1)},

(59)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(2d\lambda-1-d)({\mathds{1}}_{AB}+F_{AB})}{(d-1)(d+1)},~G\coloneqq\frac{(1-\lambda)(k-1)d\Phi_{AB}}{k(d-1)},~H\coloneqq\frac{(1-\lambda)(k+1)d\Phi_{AB}}{k(d-1)}.

Further, notice that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}(C_{AB}+H_{AB}-K_{AB})=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}(C_{AB}+H_{AB})=\frac{(2d\lambda-d-1)k+(1-\lambda)(k+1)}{k(d-1)}{\mathds{1}}_{A}=\alpha{\mathds{1}}_{A},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\big[D_{AB}+(k-1)H_{AB}+(k+1)K_{AB}\big]=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}[(k-1)H_{AB}]=\frac{(1-\lambda)(k+1)(k-1)}{k(d-1)}{\mathds{1}}_{A}=\beta{\mathds{1}}_{A}.

Hence, the operators in Eq. (

) constitute a feasible solution to the dual, which yields that when
k<d

P^{{\operatorname{PPT}}}_{\mathrm{suc},e}({\cal W}_{0},{\cal W}_{1};k,\lambda)\leq 1-\lambda+\frac{2d\lambda-d-\lambda+k(1-\lambda)}{d-1}<1.

∎
