Immunoregulatory strategies of MOF-based nanodrug delivery systems: Advances in cancer therapy applications and future perspectives
Authors: Linxi Wang, Fei Li, Zhaoyan Zhao, Minggao Zhao, Lanxin Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCancer immunotherapy has emerged as a new paradigm for the treatment of malignant tumors, demonstrating significant potential for clinical translation. Although notable breakthroughs have been achieved with approaches such as immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and cell-based therapies, several major challenges remain. Specifically, treatment-related toxicities, tumor immune escape mechanisms (e.g., impaired antigen presentation and T cell dysfunction), and the immunosuppressive nature of the tumor microenvironment (TME) remain major challenges. To address these limitations, metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have provided an innovative platform for developing next-generation immune delivery systems, owing to their tunable structural design, high drug loading and controlled release capabilities, and immunomodulatory properties. This review systematically elaborates on the advantages of MOFs as nanodrug delivery systems (NDDS), including high payload capacity, targeting ability, and biosafety. It further extends to their innovative applications in the co-delivery of immunomodulatory agents, such as the synergistic delivery of ICIs and tumor antigens. Beyond these foundational aspects, it also covers MOF-based combination therapy strategies, which specifically involve spatiotemporal synergy with chemodynamic therapy (CDT) and photodynamic therapy (PDT), while simultaneously addressing the challenges and future directions associated with their clinical translation. Notably, the review places special emphasis on the fact that MOFs can significantly enhance therapeutic efficacy through key mechanisms, including the induction of immunogenic cell death (ICD) and the remodeling of the immune microenvironment. Looking ahead, with the deepening integration of materials science and tumor immunology, MOFs are well-positioned to drive advancements in the development of cancer immunotherapy.Keywords: Metal-organic frameworks, Cancer, Immunotherapy, Nanodrug delivery systems, Combination therapy

Introduction

As reported by the Global Cancer Statistics 2022, almost 20 million new cancer cases emerged globally, resulting in a death toll that soared to approximately 9.7 million [1]. Malignant tumors pose a severe challenge to global human health today. Because of negative traits like drug resistance, the ability to metastasize easily, and elevated recurrence rates, conventional approaches to cancer treatment—such as surgery, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy—show considerable recurrence rates and notable side effects in the majority of cancer therapies [2]. The 2018 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine recognized contributions to the discovery of negative immune regulation therapies for cancer treatment, marking a significant advancement in humanity's shift from traditional therapies to personalized immunotherapy in the fight against cancer [3].

Cancer immunotherapy refers to the treatment methods that utilize principles and techniques from immunology to specifically eliminate minimal residual tumor lesions, prevent tumor progression, and disrupt immune tolerance through the activation of immune cells and the amplification of the body's anti-tumor immune response. With an increasing understanding of oncology and fundamental immunology, modern cancer immunotherapy has established a solid theoretical foundation. Currently, the strategies for cancer immunotherapy primarily include cytokine therapy, antibody therapy, cancer vaccines, adoptive cell therapy, and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) [4]. Cancer immunotherapy can enhance the synergistic effects of radiotherapy (RT), photodynamic therapy (PDT), and photothermal therapy (PTT), thereby improving anti-cancer efficacy. However, cancer immunotherapy still faces major challenges, including immune-related toxicities, dysfunction of the tumor microenvironment (TME), and low immune response rates. These limitations are largely associated with inefficient antigen presentation, insufficient T-cell infiltration, immunosuppressive TME, and nonspecific systemic distribution of immunotherapeutic agents. Advanced biomaterials and nanodrug delivery systems can address these issues through defined molecular immunological mechanisms. For example, nanocarriers can overcome spatial barriers in solid tumors, such as dense extracellular matrix, abnormal vasculature, and high interstitial pressure, thereby improving intratumoral penetration and immune-cell infiltration [5]. They can also be engineered to target antigen-presenting cells (APCs), including dendritic cells and macrophages, to enhance antigen uptake, MHC-I/MHC-II antigen presentation, dendritic cell maturation, and tumor-specific T-cell activation. Moreover, nanocarriers enable the co-delivery and controlled release of antigens, adjuvants, ICIs, and nucleic acid therapeutics, thereby coordinating innate immune activation with adaptive immune priming while reducing off-target toxicity [6].

Among various nanodrug delivery systems (NDDS), liposomes [7], polymeric nanoparticles [8], mesoporous silica (MS) [9], and inorganic nanoparticles [10], each possesses distinct advantages and application potential. However, their practical translation remains challenged by several limitations, such as limited drug-loading efficiency, difficulties in integrating multiple functions synergistically, and insufficient capacity to modulate the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME). In contrast, metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), owing to their highly tunable pore structures, diverse metal nodes and organic ligands, and excellent functionalization capability, exhibit notable advantages in efficient drug loading, multimodal therapeutic integration, and immune regulation. Advantages of MOFs compared with other delivery systems.

MOFs are a class of porous crystalline materials formed through the coordination-driven self-assembly of metal ions or metal clusters with organic ligands, typically exhibiting two-dimensional or three-dimensional periodic network structures [11]. Owing to their highly tunable pore sizes, large specific surface areas, abundant active sites, and excellent structural designability, MOFs enable the efficient loading and delivery of diverse therapeutic molecules, including chemotherapeutic agents, photosensitizers, nucleic acids, proteins, and immunomodulators. In addition, their tumor-targeting capability toward tumor tissues or specific cell types can be enhanced through surface functionalization or nanoscale engineering. MOFs can also exploit the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect to improve accumulation at tumor sites, thereby enhancing antitumor therapeutic efficacy [12].

MOFs have been progressively developed and applied in the biomedical field. Owing to their high specific surface area, tunable pore size, and favorable biocompatibility, MOFs have been widely investigated for drug delivery, PDT, and other biomedical applications. In 2016, Qu and co-workers further engineered ZIF-8-type nanoscale MOFs as cancer vaccine platforms for the co-delivery of antigens and adjuvants. This strategy promoted the uptake of nanovaccines by APCs and enabled processed antigenic peptides to be presented on the cell surface through major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules, thereby activating antigen-specific T-cell responses [13]. Specifically, Qu et al. encapsulated ovalbumin (OVA) in ZIF-8 and adsorbed CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (CpG ODNs), as immunoadjuvants, onto the MOF surface. The resulting nanovaccine induced pronounced immune activation and elicited effective immunological memory responses [14]. In the same year, Lin and co-workers constructed a chlorin-based nanoscale MOF (TBC-Hf) and combined TBC-Hf-mediated PDT with an indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase (IDO) small-molecule inhibitor loaded within the MOF channels to induce systemic antitumor immunity. Upon light irradiation, this system efficiently generated singlet oxygen (1O2), eradicated primary tumors, and induced immunogenic cell death (ICD), thereby promoting the release of tumor-associated antigens (TAAs). Meanwhile, the IDO inhibitor alleviated the immunosuppressive TME. These two effects synergistically enhanced systemic antitumor immune responses, resulting in primary tumor eradication and significant inhibition of distant untreated tumor growth in a colorectal cancer model. This study demonstrated that MOFs can simultaneously function as photosensitizing scaffolds, drug carriers, and immunomodulatory platforms, thus laying an important foundation for MOF-mediated PDT combined with immunotherapy. Nevertheless, certain limitations remain. In particular, the MOF system was administered by local injection, making it difficult to achieve systemic delivery and targeted accumulation via intravenous administration, thereby restricting its therapeutic potential for widely metastatic tumors.

To address these challenges, subsequent studies have gradually shifted the design focus of MOFs from single local therapeutic systems toward multifunctional nanoplatforms capable of systemic administration, targeted accumulation, and stimuli-responsive release. On the one hand, researchers have improved the blood circulation time and tumor penetration ability of MOFs by regulating their particle size, surface charge, and stability. On the other hand, strategies such as PEGylation, modification with tumor-targeting ligands, and cell membrane coating have been employed to further enhance MOF accumulation at tumor sites and reduce clearance by the reticuloendothelial system (RES). In addition, by exploiting characteristic features of the TME, including acidic pH, overexpressed glutathione (GSH), reactive oxygen species (ROS), and specific enzymes, stimuli-responsive MOFs can be constructed to enable the precise release of antigens, adjuvants, immunomodulators, or chemotherapeutic drugs at tumor sites, thereby improving therapeutic efficacy while reducing systemic toxicity [15].

With the deepening understanding of the mechanisms underlying cancer immunotherapy, the functions of MOF platforms have gradually expanded from antigen delivery and PDT to TME remodeling. Tumor tissues are commonly characterized by hypoxia, acidity, infiltration of immunosuppressive cells, and high expression of immune checkpoints, all of which can impair T-cell infiltration and effector function. To overcome these barriers, MOFs can alleviate tumor hypoxia by catalyzing hydrogen peroxide decomposition to generate oxygen or by regulating redox reactions, thereby enhancing ICD induced by PDT and RT. Moreover, MOFs can deliver immunoadjuvants, cytokines, IDO inhibitors, or ICIs to promote dendritic cell (DC) maturation, enhance cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) infiltration, and suppress immunosuppressive components such as regulatory T cells and myeloid-derived suppressor cells. Ultimately, these effects facilitate the conversion of “cold tumors” into “hot tumors” that are more responsive to immunotherapy.

In recent years, MOFs have demonstrated considerable potential in cancer immunotherapy, with their advantages mainly reflected in the following aspects. 1) MOFs can serve as efficient NDDS. Their unique porous architectures and large specific surface areas allow the effective encapsulation or adsorption of various therapeutic agents, such as chemotherapeutic drugs, photosensitizers, immunoadjuvants, gene therapeutics, and immune checkpoint modulators, thereby improving drug stability, bioavailability, and tumor-localized delivery efficiency. 2) Certain MOFs exhibit favorable responsiveness to the TME, particularly pH responsiveness. Because the TME and intracellular lysosomes are typically weakly acidic, pH-sensitive MOFs can undergo structural degradation at tumor sites or within cells, enabling the controlled release of therapeutic payloads, improving therapeutic selectivity, and reducing nonspecific toxicity to normal tissues [16]. 3) MOFs possess remarkable structural designability. By modulating metal nodes, organic ligands, pore characteristics, and surface modifications, their loading capacity, release kinetics, targeting ability, and biocompatibility can be precisely optimized [17]. 4) MOFs themselves may serve as functional immunomodulatory agents. In the absence of exogenous drug loading, specific metal ions or functional ligands within MOFs can regulate oxidative stress, activate immune-related signaling pathways, or serve as imaging components for cancer theranostics [18]. 5) Rationally designed and surface-modified MOFs generally exhibit favorable biocompatibility and degradability, which may help minimize nonspecific immune activation and systemic toxicity [19]. 6) MOF-based NDDS are particularly suitable for combinational immunotherapy. By integrating chemotherapy, PDT, PTT, chemodynamic therapy (CDT), radiosensitization, and immune checkpoint blockade (ICB), MOF-based NDDS can induce ICD, remodel the immunosuppressive TME, and potentiate systemic antitumor immune responses, thereby enabling bimodal or multimodal synergistic cancer therapy [20].

Overall, MOFs exhibit numerous advantages in cancer immunotherapy, establishing them as promising nanomaterials in this domain. This review systematically summarizes MOFs from several perspectives: the characteristics of MOFs as NDDS; the targeted delivery of ICIs, cancer vaccines, and gene therapy agents utilizing MOFs; the synergistic enhancement of MOFs in combined cancer immunotherapy alongside other modalities, including chemotherapy, phototherapy, sonodynamic therapy (SDT), RT, and starvation therapy; as well as the prospects and challenges of MOF-based NDDS in tumor immunotherapy (Fig. 1). These systems are anticipated to become powerful tools for addressing the challenges faced in cancer immunotherapy.

Literature search methodology

A structured literature search was conducted to identify relevant studies on MOF-based NDDS for cancer immunotherapy. The literature search was performed using major scientific databases, including PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar, ScienceDirect, and ACS Publications. Additional relevant references were identified by manually screening the reference lists of highly cited reviews and key original research articles.

The literature search covered publications from June 2003 to May 2026, with particular emphasis on recent studies published within the last five years. Earlier landmark studies related to MOF synthesis, structural characteristics, and biomedical applications were also included when necessary to provide historical and mechanistic context.

The following keywords and search phrases were used alone or in combination:

“metal-organic framework” OR “MOF” OR “nanoscale metal-organic framework” OR “nano-MOF”, combined with “cancer immunotherapy,” “tumor immunotherapy,” “immune checkpoint inhibitor,” “PD-1,” “PD-L1,” “CTLA-4,” “cancer vaccine,” “antigen delivery,” “dendritic cell,” “T cell activation,” “immunogenic cell death,” “ICD,” “tumor microenvironment,” “TME remodeling,” “nanodrug delivery system,” “stimuli-responsive release,” “photodynamic therapy,” “photothermal therapy,” “chemodynamic therapy,” “sonodynamic therapy,” “radiotherapy,” “starvation therapy,” “combination therapy,” “biosafety,” “biocompatibility,” and “clinical translation.”

Studies were included if they met the following criteria: (1) original research articles or high-quality review articles published in peer-reviewed journals; (2) studies focusing on MOF-based NDDS for cancer therapy, particularly cancer immunotherapy; (3) studies involving MOF-based NDDS of immunotherapeutic agents, including ICIs, tumor antigens, adjuvants, nucleic acids, or cancer vaccines; (4) studies discussing MOF-based combination strategies capable of enhancing antitumor immune responses, such as PDT, PTT, CDT, SDT, RT, chemotherapy, or starvation therapy combined with immunotherapy; (5) articles providing mechanistic insights into immunogenic cell death, antigen presentation, immune checkpoint regulation, TME remodeling, or cancer-immunity-cycle modulation; (6) studies addressing biosafety, pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, degradability, or clinical translation of MOF-based nanomedicines.

Studies were excluded if they: (1) were not related to MOFs or MOF-based nanomaterials; (2) focused solely on non-cancer applications; (3) described cancer therapy without any relevance to immunotherapy, immune modulation, or TME regulation; (4) were conference abstracts, editorials, patents, dissertations, or non-peer-reviewed reports without sufficient methodological detail; (5) were not available in English; (6) contained insufficient experimental or mechanistic information relevant to the scope of this review.

The final selection of references was based on relevance to the scope of the review, scientific quality, novelty, mechanistic depth, and contribution to understanding MOF-based NDDS in cancer immunotherapy. The study selection process is summarized using a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (Fig. 2).

MOFs as delivery platforms: classification, synthetic strategies, and key advantages

MOFs have attracted increasing attention as highly promising biomedical nanodelivery platforms. By rationally selecting metal nodes and organic ligands, diverse types of MOFs can be designed, enabling precise control over their morphology, porosity, stability, and responsiveness to specific biological stimuli. Meanwhile, various synthetic strategies, including solvothermal synthesis, nanoprecipitation, microwave-assisted synthesis, and biomimetic mineralization, provide flexible routes for the customized fabrication of MOFs to meet different therapeutic requirements. Compared with conventional delivery systems, MOFs offer unique advantages in terms of high drug-loading capacity, co-delivery of multiple therapeutic agents, stimuli-responsive release, and the integration of targeting, and immunomodulatory functions.

Comparative analysis of MOF types for cancer immunotherapy

According to the combination modes of metal nodes and organic ligands, as well as the topology of the framework, MOF materials can be further classified into several representative categories, including porous coordination networks or pocket-channel frameworks (PCNs), University of Oslo (UiO) series, zeolitic imidazolate frameworks (ZIFs), Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) series, Materials of Institute Lavoisier (MILs), and isoreticular MOFs (IRMOFs).

Representative MOFs, including PCN-224, UiO-66, ZIF-8, HKUST-1, MIL-101, and MOF-5(Fig. 3), were selected for a comprehensive comparison in terms of structural features, drug-loading capacity, degradation behavior, immunomodulatory mechanisms, biocompatibility, and applicable therapeutic scenarios. PCNs are assembled from metal ions or metal clusters and organic ligands through coordination bonds, and generally possess well-defined three-dimensional porous frameworks, cage-channel architectures, high specific surface areas, and facile functionalization. Among them, Zr-porphyrin PCNs have been widely applied in cancer therapy owing to their combined structural stability and photo-/sonosensitizing properties. MOFs constructed using photosensitizers as ligands can facilitate the ordered arrangement of photosensitizers and markedly reduce aggregation-induced quenching [21,22]. In addition, the porous channels within MOFs can accelerate the diffusion of ROS [23].

PCN-224

Tian et al. developed an HA-modified, rapamycin-loaded multifunctional PCN-224 nanodrug delivery platform, denoted R@BP@HA, in which bovine serum albumin (BSA) was used to improve the aqueous solubility of rapamycin, while the porous structure of PCN-224 enabled efficient drug loading [24]. PCN-224 functions both as an efficient photodynamic agent and as a drug carrier. It can generate abundant ROS to kill tumor cells and synergize with rapamycin to potently induce ICD. Meanwhile, HA-mediated targeting promotes tumor accumulation, leading to robust proliferation of CTLs, enhanced lymphocyte infiltration into tumor tissues, and the establishment of long-term antitumor immune memory, thereby achieving synergistic chemo-PDT-immunotherapy. Chen et al. constructed an erythrocyte membrane-coated PCN-224 nanosonosensitizer loaded with LY364947, termed LPM. Under ultrasound irradiation, the PCN-224 sonosensitizer efficiently generated ROS, activated the caspase-3/GSDME pathway, and induced tumor cell pyroptosis, thereby releasing tumor antigens and inflammatory factors to initiate antitumor immune responses. As a nanocarrier, PCN-224 enabled LY364947 loading, while erythrocyte membrane camouflage promoted tumor-targeted accumulation. In the acidic TME, LPM achieved responsive drug release, degraded collagen, loosened the extracellular matrix (ECM), and significantly enhanced T-cell infiltration, ultimately leading to potent activation of antitumor immunity and the establishment of long-term immune memory [25]. Overall, owing to its tailorable porous framework, excellent photo-/sonosensitizing activity, and efficient drug-loading and delivery capabilities, PCN-224 can induce ICD or pyroptosis, enhance T-cell infiltration, and remodel the TME. These properties highlight its considerable potential in multimodal combinatorial cancer immunotherapy.

UiO-66

Taking UiO-66 as an example, UiO-66 is composed of Zr4+ ions and terephthalic acid (BDC), forming a three-dimensional porous network. Jiang et al. designed a cancer cell membrane-coated biomimetic UiO-66 nanodelivery system, MCM@UN, co-loaded with a bromodomain-containing protein 4 (BRD4) inhibitor and a Toll-like receptor 7/8 (TLR7/8) agonist. In this system, UiO-66 serves as a carrier that enables efficient loading and pH-responsive release of the BRD4 inhibitor, enhances intracellular delivery efficiency, and provides structural support for the biomimetic targeted delivery platform, thereby acting as a key component for achieving synergistic immunotherapy [26]. UiO-66 possesses a well-defined and stable porous architecture, good biocompatibility, excellent structural stability, and pH-responsive properties. Their Zr-O clusters and open metal sites can provide effective anchoring sites for drug molecules. In addition, UiO-66 exhibits a certain degree of amphiphilicity, allowing it to cross cell membranes and undergo cellular internalization, thereby facilitating efficient anticancer drug loading, intracellular delivery, and stimuli-responsive release.

ZIF-8

ZIF-8 is assembled from Zn2+ and 2-methylimidazole(2-MI) through coordination interactions [27]. It has attracted considerable attention in the field of cancer immunotherapy, mainly owing to its abundant porous structure, tunable pore size, good biocompatibility, and pronounced pH-responsive properties. As a nanodrug delivery platform, ZIF-8 can efficiently load chemotherapeutic agents and facilitate the intracellular delivery of protein antigens, nucleic acids, and immunoadjuvants, while enabling selective release in the acidic TME [28]. In addition, the role of ZIF-8 in cancer immunotherapy is not limited to that of a pH-responsive drug carrier. It can also remodel the TIME and amplify antitumor immune responses by inducing ICD and pyroptosis, releasing Zn2+ to activate immune-related pathways, promoting the release of Damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), enhancing antigen presentation, regulating macrophage polarization, and synergizing with ICB. Therefore, ZIF-8 represents a multifunctional immunotherapeutic nanoplatform with broad application prospects [29,30]. MIT@ZIF-8 nanoparticles were synthesized via a simple aqueous one-pot method. This platform integrates the dual functions of its components: MIT acts as a chemotherapeutic agent and ICD, while the ZIF-8 carrier enhances drug uptake and triggers cell pyroptosis. Together, they achieve synergistic immune activation. In both “hot” (colon) and “cold” (prostate) tumor models, MIT@ZIF-8 demonstrated superior antitumor efficacy, favorable safety, and the ability to establish systemic immune memory. Mechanistically, it remodeled the TME by increasing CD8+ effector T cells and decreasing Tregs, ultimately re-sensitizing refractory prostate cancer to anti-CTLA-4 therapy [31].

HKUST-1

HKUST-1 is a porous Cu-based MOF formed through the coordination of Cu2+ with 1,3,5-benzenetricarboxylic acid (H3BTC) [32]. A HA-modified HKUST-1 nanozyme platform, HHOC, was constructed to co-load chlorin e6 (Ce6) and oxaliplatin. Through HA-CD44-mediated targeting, HHOC can selectively accumulate in tumor cells and generate photodynamic and photothermal effects under laser irradiation. Meanwhile, HKUST-1 releases Cu2+/Cu+, which consumes GSH and catalyzes H2O2 to produce ·OH, thereby amplifying ROS-mediated oxidative stress. The released oxaliplatin further exerts chemotherapeutic effects, enabling synergistic CDT, PDT, PTT, and chemotherapy, and effectively inhibiting colon cancer growth [33].

MIL-101

MIL-series materials are constructed from metal nodes such as Fe3+, Al3+ and various carboxylate ligands. They exhibit diverse structural types, tunable pore sizes, and adjustable framework flexibility. Among them, Fe-based MILs can generate ROS through Fenton-like reactions, induce ICD, and activate antitumor immunity. Wang et al. developed a HA-coated MIL-100(Fe) nanodelivery system (CRMH NPs) co-loaded with the chemotherapeutic agent irinotecan (CPT-11) and the TLR7 agonist R848. MIL-100 mediated ICD induction and cGAS-STING pathway activation, thereby remodeling the TIME. When combined with ICB, this platform achieved efficient and safe chemoimmunotherapy for colorectal cancer [34]. Yan et al. used MIL-53(Fe) as a precursor and prepared a quasi-MOF nanozyme, Q-MIL-53(Fe), through controllable partial ligand removal. Owing to its multiple enzyme-mimicking activities, GSH-depleting capability, and mitochondria-damaging effects, induced ferroptosis and ICD, activated potent antitumor immunity, and, when combined with ICB, significantly suppressed tumor growth and metastasis. This work opens new avenues for the application of MOFs in tumor catalytic immunotherapy [35]. Zhang et al. used NMIL-101 nanoparticles as a carrier to load Glucose Oxidase (Gox), thereby preparing an NMIL-101/GOx nanoinducer. In 4T1 breast cancer cells, NMIL-101/GOx efficiently depleted glucose and converted the generated hydrogen peroxide into highly toxic •OH via the Fenton reaction, while simultaneously consuming intracellular GSH to induce ICD. In treated mice, the proportion of mature DCs increased significantly, and the numbers of tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells and CD4+ T cells were also markedly elevated. By disrupting glucose metabolism and redox homeostasis in tumor cells, NMIL-101/GOx achieved efficient tumor cell killing [36].

MOF-5

MOF-5 is composed of Zn2+ and the ligand BDC, with a specific surface area reaching up to 2900 m2/g, possessing a highly ordered porous structure and excellent framework stability.

To evaluate the biosafety of metal ions, the median lethal dose (LD50) serves as a crucial metric. Owing to their favorable safety profiles, metals including zinc, zirconium, and iron are highly favored for constructing NDDS, demonstrating oral LD50 values of 0.35, 4.1, and 0.45 g/kg, respectively [37]. The intrinsic variations in metal-ligand combinations shape their unique physicochemical properties, including specific surface areas, pore volumes, and degradation kinetics. Ultimately, these structural features dictate their specific mechanisms and overall performance in biomedical applications. Common synthesis methods for MOFs as NDDS

When MOFs are used for NDDS, the materials are generally required to have nanoscale dimensions, good dispersibility, biocompatibility, degradability, high drug-loading capacity, and controllable release properties. Therefore, the commonly used synthesis methods differ from traditional MOF single-crystal synthesis and are more focused on mild, rapid preparation strategies that allow controllable particle size and are suitable for drug loading (Table 3).

Significant advantages of MOFs in cancer immunotherapy

Structural tunability

MOFs are formed through the coordination-driven self-assembly of metal ions or metal clusters with organic ligands. Their framework architecture, pore size, channel morphology, specific surface area, surface charge, and degradation behavior can be precisely engineered by tuning the metal nodes, ligand types, and synthetic conditions. For example, the selection of different metal centers, such as Zr4+, Fe3+, Zn2+, Cu2+, and Mn2+, can endow MOFs with distinct stability, catalytic activity, imaging capability, or immunomodulatory functions [15]. Meanwhile, the incorporation of functional ligands, including carboxylate-, imidazolate-, and porphyrin-based ligands, can further modulate their photoresponsiveness, hydrophilicity/hydrophobicity, drug-binding capacity, and biocompatibility. According to the number of coordination sites, organic linkers can be broadly divided into ditopic, tritopic, tetratopic, and multitopic linkers. Ditopic linkers, such as BDC and 2-MI, usually bridge metal ions through carboxylate-metal or imidazolate-metal coordination bonds, giving rise to representative frameworks such as UiO-66, MIL-101, MOF-5, and ZIF-8. Tritopic linkers, such as BTC, can coordinate with multiple metal clusters in different directions and generate highly connected porous structures, as exemplified by HKUST-1. Tetratopic linkers, such as tetrakis(4-carboxyphenyl)porphyrin (TCPP), coordinate with Zr4+ clusters to construct porphyrinic MOFs such as PCN-224, in which the porphyrin core further provides photoresponsive and ROS-generating capability. Multitopic or functionalized linkers containing amino, hydroxyl, sulfonate, phosphate, or alkynyl groups can introduce additional post-synthetic modification sites and regulate hydrophilicity, surface charge, bioconjugation capacity, and framework stability.

The geometry, rigidity, length, and functional group density of organic linkers directly affect MOFs morphology and structure [45]. Rigid aromatic linkers generally favor highly crystalline and stable frameworks with ordered pore channels, whereas flexible linkers may lead to more diverse morphologies or interpenetrated structures. Longer linkers can enlarge pore size and increase cargo-loading capacity but may reduce framework stability, while shorter linkers often generate compact frameworks with improved robustness but limited space for macromolecular drugs. Moreover, the number and spatial orientation of coordination groups determine the nucleation and growth kinetics of MOF crystals, thereby regulating particle size, shape, pore architecture, defect density, and surface chemistry. Accordingly, rational linker engineering enables the construction of MOF-based nanodrug delivery systems with optimized loading capacity, stimulus-responsive degradation, tumor targeting, and immune-modulatory functions. In addition, by adjusting the solvent system, reaction temperature, precursor concentration, pH, modulators, and reaction time, the nucleation and growth processes of MOFs nanoparticles can be controlled, thereby yielding materials with different sizes, morphologies, and pore structures [50]. Therefore, the structural tunability of MOFs improves drug loading and delivery efficiency and provides a versatile material basis for cancer immunotherapy.

Surface modifiability

Conventional nanomedicines are often rapidly recognized and cleared by the RES, which limits their blood circulation time, tumor accumulation, and therapeutic efficacy. In this context, the abundant surface metal sites, functional organic ligands, and post-synthetic modification capacity of MOFs provide a versatile platform for precise surface engineering. Through rational surface functionalization, MOFs can acquire multiple properties, including immune evasion, prolonged circulation, enhanced colloidal stability, active targeting, barrier penetration, and stimuli-responsive delivery. These features collectively improve their pharmacokinetic profiles, biodistribution, tumor retention, and local exposure of immunotherapeutic agents, thereby enhancing the overall efficacy of cancer immunotherapy [[51], [52], [53]].

Importantly, in cancer immunotherapy, the role of surface modification extends beyond improving drug delivery efficiency [54]. It also enables MOFs to actively participate in the TIME regulation. Tumor tissues usually exhibit distinct pathological characteristics, including abnormal pH gradients, overexpression of tumor-associated receptors such as folate receptor (FR), epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), and CD44, dense ECM, abnormal vasculature, and immunosuppressive cellular components. By exploiting these differences, surface-engineered MOFs can achieve selective accumulation in tumor lesions or immune-related cell populations through ligand-receptor recognition, receptor-mediated endocytosis, biomimetic membrane camouflage, or size/charge-adapted transport. As a result, ICIs, immunoadjuvants, tumor antigens, nucleic acid therapeutics, and photo-/sonosensitizers can be delivered more precisely to tumor sites, antigen-presenting cells, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), or even subcellular organelles.

Current surface modification strategies for targeted MOFs can be broadly classified into covalent modification, non-covalent modification, coordination-based modification and Biomimetic (Table 4) [58]. Covalent conjugation usually provides high binding stability and durable targeting effects, making it suitable for anchoring antibodies, peptides, carbohydrates, and small-molecule ligands [59,60]. Non-covalent modification relies on hydrogen bonding, π-π stacking, electrostatic interactions, and van der Waals forces; although facile and flexible, it may suffer from relatively weak binding affinity and limited stability under complex physiological conditions [61]. Coordination-based modification exploits open metal sites on MOF surfaces to form coordination bonds with ligands containing donor atoms such as N, O, S, or P, thereby offering a mild, controllable, and often reversible approach for functionalization [50]. By integrating targeting agents such as antibodies, peptides, aptamers, carbohydrates, cell membranes, folic acid (FA), or triphenylphosphonium (TPP), MOFs can achieve precise delivery to tumor tissues, specific immune cell subsets, and subcellular organelles, thereby reducing off-target toxicity and improving immunotherapeutic outcomes [58].

The value of antibody-modified MOFs extends beyond “recognition”, as certain antibodies themselves possess therapeutic functions. An acid-responsive αPD-1-MOF@H/D nanodelivery system was constructed, in which ZIF-8 served as the carrier to co-encapsulate hyaluronidase (HAase) and decitabine (DEC). After surface modification with an acid-sensitive polymer, anti-PD-1 antibodies were covalently conjugated onto the nanoparticle surface. The resulting nanoplatform could efficiently bind to the surface of TILs through the specific PD-1/αPD-1 interaction, while this binding was dissociated under acidic conditions, enabling efficient release of both the drugs and antibodies. The released anti-PD-1 antibodies blocked the inhibitory PD-1/PD-L1 signaling axis, thereby preventing TIL exhaustion and maintaining their cytotoxic activity and cytokine-secretion capacity. In synergy with HAase-mediated ECM degradation and DEC-induced CCL5 upregulation for T-cell recruitment, this system established a self-amplifying cycle of T-cell infiltration, resulting in a 12-fold increase in intratumoral TIL infiltration. This strategy provides a translationally promising paradigm for the immunotherapy of “cold” tumors such as pancreatic cancer [62].

Protein and peptide modification has emerged as an important functionalization strategy for MOF-based NDDS. By introducing peptide motifs such as arginine-glycine-aspartic acid (RGD) and Angiopep-2(Ang), MOFs can be endowed with tumor receptor recognition, tissue-penetration, or intracellular delivery capabilities, thereby enhancing drug accumulation at tumor sites and improving therapeutic efficacy. The RGD peptide is one of the most extensively investigated integrin-targeting ligands and can specifically recognize integrin receptors—primarily αvβ3 and αvβ5—that are highly expressed on the surface of tumor cells and tumor neovascular endothelial cells [63]. In a representative Zr-MOF-based theranostic platform, DNA aptamers were stably anchored on the MOF surface through Zr-phosphate coordination, which improved colloidal stability under physiological conditions and enabled fluorescence quenching via π-π stacking. Upon specific recognition of target molecules on tumor cell membranes, aptamer conformational changes triggered fluorescence recovery, allowing target-responsive imaging. Moreover, as photosensitizer carriers, Zr-MOFs generated 1O2 under light irradiation, while aptamer-mediated targeting enhanced tumor cell uptake and significantly improved PDT-induced cytotoxicity and ICD [64].

Carbohydrate-modified MOFs also show notable advantages in tumor targeting and immunotherapy-oriented combination treatment, because many tumor cells and immune cells express receptors that recognize specific glycan structures. Hyaluronic acid (HA), for example, can specifically bind CD44 receptors and simultaneously improve the aqueous dispersibility, biological stability, and intravenous administrability of MOFs. A HA-modified Hf-MOF nanoplatform, UiO-66-Hf(2OH)-C/B@HA, was developed for enhanced radioimmunotherapy against MYC-amplified hepatocellular carcinoma. In this system, HA-mediated CD44 targeting markedly improved tumor accumulation and cellular uptake. The platform delivered telaglenastat (CB-839) and buthionine sulfoximine (BSO), which synergistically inhibited glutamine metabolism and glutathione synthesis, enhanced radiosensitivity, and induced ICD. These effects promoted DC maturation and T-cell infiltration. When combined with PD-1 blockade, this strategy further amplified antitumor immune responses and significantly inhibited tumor growth [65]. This example highlights that carbohydrate modification can transform MOFs from passive delivery vehicles into active platforms for radio-immunotherapy synergy.

Biomimetic modification further expands the immunotherapeutic potential of MOFs. Cell membrane coating, one of the most widely used biomimetic strategies, involves cloaking MOFs with membranes derived from tumor cells, immune cells, or stem cells. This approach provides natural “self” signals and membrane-associated receptors, allowing MOFs to evade immune clearance, prolong circulation, and achieve homologous or inflammation-guided tumor targeting. For example, Zhang et al. developed a macrophage-membrane-coated copper-based MOF nanoplatform (denoted ZCESM@mem) for precision copper ion delivery and synergistic tumor therapy. Copper-based MOFs offer high Cu2+ loading capacity and intrinsic pH-responsive degradation, enabling tumor-selective drug release in the acidic TME. Specifically, macrophage membranes express chemokine receptors and specific cell adhesion proteins that can selectively bind inflammatory chemokines or vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 (VCAM-1), which are highly expressed by various metastatic cancer cells. Together with the EPR effect, these membrane-associated interactions facilitate active tumor targeting, immune evasion, and prolonged circulation. To overcome the challenge of intracellular Cu2+ trafficking, the platform encapsulates elesclomol (ES), a copper ionophore that promotes mitochondrial Cu2+ accumulation. Upon cellular internalization, the acidic intracellular environment triggers MOFs disassembly and releases Cu2+ and ES. Subsequently, endogenous H2S reacts with Cu2+ to generate CuS, enabling PTT under near-infrared (NIR) irradiation. Concurrently, ES-mediated mitochondrial Cu2+ enrichment induces cuproptosis, while the combined PTT/cuproptosis effect activates antitumor immune responses [57]. Ultimately, effectively ablates primary tumors and suppresses tumor recurrence, highlighting the advantages of biomimetic MOFs surface modification for targeted and immune-synergistic cancer therapy.

Small molecules such as FA and TPP have also become important components of targeted MOFs. FA targets folate receptor (FRs) on the surface of tumor cells and represents a small-molecule targeting ligand with considerable potential for clinical translation. An FA-targeted Ir1@FA@MOFs nanoplatform was constructed for precise photoimmunotherapy. In this system, an iridium (III) complex (Ir1) was integrated into folate receptor-targeted Cu-based MOFs, enabling a transformation of the photodynamic therapeutic mechanism. The resulting nanoplatform effectively depleted intracellular GSH and promoted ROS generation, thereby inducing cuproptosis, ferroptosis, and ICD. These synergistic effects activated systemic antitumor immune responses and converted immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors. Moreover, FA-mediated targeting enabled selective accumulation of the nanoplatform at tumor sites through specific recognition of FRs overexpressed on tumor cells, leading to efficient suppression of tumor growth. Overall, this strategy provides a promising direction for MOF-based precision photoimmunotherapy. Peng et al. constructed the ZIF-67@DOX-TPP nanorobotic system by encapsulating the DOX-TPP complex within the ZIF-67 carrier. This system utilizes cobalt ions to catalyze the decomposition of H2O2, generating propulsion. TPP+ targets the mitochondria, accelerating drug release and degradation in the acidic and high H2O2 TME. By disrupting mitochondrial function and inducing apoptosis, it inhibits tumor growth and metastasis in both cellular and animal models [66]. The design strategy of post-synthetic modification of MOFs materials provides a new approach for the development of intelligent NDDS.

Stimuli-responsiveness

MOFs have become highly promising materials for the development of NDDS that respond to stimuli. This is due to their significant specific surface area, adjustable pore structure and dimensions, simple methods of synthetic modification, and remarkable biocompatibility [67]. MOFs exhibit excellent stimulus responsiveness and controllable degradation properties, enabling them to respond to exogenous stimuli such as light, temperature, ultrasound, and ionizing radiation, as well as endogenous stimuli including pH, ATP, GSH, ions, glucose, H2S, and enzymes [57]. These features allow spatiotemporally controlled drug release, thereby minimizing premature leakage and reducing damage to normal tissues [68].

Immuno-combination therapy refers to a therapeutic strategy that integrates immunotherapy with other treatment modalities or combines different types of immunotherapeutic agents to synergistically enhance anti-tumor efficacy, overcome drug resistance, and improve patient prognosis. MOFs can serve as ideal multifunctional therapeutic platforms to facilitate these strategies by mediating CDT, RT, PTT, PDT, and SDT, which further enhances antitumor immunotherapy by inducing ICD and modulating the TME.

Moreover, MOFs can serve as multifunctional therapeutic platforms to mediate CDT, RT, PTT, PDT, and SDT, and can further enhance antitumor immunotherapy by inducing ICD and modulating the TME. Combination cancer immunotherapy typically involves diverse therapeutic components and multiple immunomodulatory stages. However, conventional delivery systems are often hindered by limited drug-loading capacity, difficulties in multi-component co-delivery, insufficient tumor targeting, uncontrollable release kinetics, and elevated systemic toxicity. MOFs, characterized by their porous crystalline structures and highly tunable compositions, enable the efficient integration of various therapeutic agents—such as ICIs, tumor antigens, immunoadjuvants, chemotherapeutics, photosensitizers, sonosensitizers, or radiosensitizers—within a single platform. These payloads can be released on-demand in response to internal or external stimuli, including pH, GSH, enzymes, light, ultrasound, or radiation. Crucially, the metal nodes and functional ligands of MOFs can actively participate in reactive ROS generation, GSH depletion, oxygen regulation, or activation of immune-related signaling pathways. These intrinsic properties enhance ICD, promote DC maturation, increase effector T-cell infiltration, and effectively reverse the immunosuppressive TME.

According to the source of stimulation, MOF-based NDDS can be broadly classified into two categories: endogenous stimuli-responsive systems and exogenous stimuli-responsive systems.

Endogenous stimuli-responsive MOFs primarily exploit the characteristic physiological features of the TME that distinguish it from normal tissues to achieve selective drug release, such as acidic pH, elevated GSH levels, aberrant enzyme expression, and alterations in specific metabolite concentrations. These endogenous cues can induce degradation of the MOF framework, opening of pore channels, or cleavage of chemical bonds, thereby enabling more precise drug delivery at tumor sites. Because tumor tissues generally exhibit an acidic milieu and an enhanced reductive state, pH-responsive and GSH-responsive MOFs have become the most extensively investigated types. In addition, enzyme-responsive MOFs can also be selectively activated by the overexpression of tumor-associated enzymes, further improving therapeutic selectivity and safety. In a study by Huang et al., an acid-responsive manganese-based MOF (AMS MOFs) nanoplatform was developed for the co-delivery of the hypoxia-activated prodrug (AQ4N) and the STING agonist SR-717. The core advantage of this system lies in its remarkable pH-dependent degradation: while remaining stable at physiological pH, the framework rapidly dissociates in the acidic TME to release its cargo, enabling precise spatiotemporal control of drug delivery. When combined with high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) therapy, the intensified post-operative hypoxia triggers the activation of AQ4N to induce ICD. Concurrently, the released Mn2+ and SR-717 synergistically activate the STING pathway, significantly promoting DCs maturation and antigen presentation [69]. In bilateral 4T1 breast cancer models, this strategy suppressed both primary and distant tumors (extending beyond 90% inhibition) and established long-term immune memory. Consequently, MOFs can successfully coordinate local tumor ablation with systemic immune activation, thereby improving immunotherapy response rates and providing an ideal nanoplatform for multimodal combination cancer immunotherapy.

On the other hand, exogenous stimuli-responsive MOFs mainly respond to externally applied physical signals, such as light, temperature, ultrasound, and ionizing radiation. Compared with endogenous stimuli, exogenous stimuli offer greater controllability, allowing precise regulation of the timing, intensity, and location of activation during treatment; therefore, they are particularly suitable for on-demand drug release and combination therapy. In particular, NIR light has been widely used in MOF-based systems for photothermal and photodynamic therapy because of its relatively strong tissue penetration capability. Ultrasound, on the other hand, enables noninvasive activation in deep tissues and can be combined with sonodynamic therapy or local ablation. Meanwhile, ionizing radiation-responsive MOFs can work synergistically with radiotherapy to enhance tumor destruction and strengthen immune responses. Mao et al. constructed a hafnium-based nanoscale metal–organic framework by covalently grafting the Toll-like receptor agonist R848 onto MOF ligands through ROS-sensitive carbamate linkers, ultimately yielding a composite nanosystem termed R-MOF. This platform integrates dual functions of photoresponsiveness and immune activation, enabling efficient tumor cell killing, tumor growth inhibition, and enhanced immune cell infiltration, while maintaining favorable biosafety. Therefore, it provides a new strategy for combined photodynamic–immunotherapeutic antitumor therapy [70].

In developing NDDS, one can leverage the distinct physiological traits of the TME, which contrast sharply with those of normal tissues—these include acidic pH levels, elevated GSH concentrations, the overproduction of particular enzymes, and the ability for NIR light penetration— to create MOFs that exhibit high specificity and enhanced multi-responsiveness. This ultimately enables the precise release of drugs and enhances antitumor efficacy.

Biocompatibility and biodegradability

The biocompatibility of MOFs is fundamentally determined by the selection of metal ions and organic ligands, which together govern their toxicity, degradability, and overall biosafety. MOFs associated with Fe3+, Co3+, and Al3+ generally exhibit relatively low toxicity; those related to Zr4+, Mg2+, and Zn2+ are mostly of moderate toxicity; whereas MOFs associated with Cu2+, Cr3+, and Mn2+ tend to be more toxic [58,71]. However, even for these relatively safe metals, high doses or repeated administration may still exceed the body's metabolic clearance capacity, resulting in off-target deposition and potential immunomodulatory side effects, such as unintended activation or suppression of innate immune signaling pathways, including the cGAS-STING pathway [72]. Therefore, the selection of metal nodes must strike a balance between therapeutic efficacy and intrinsic toxicity. For clinical translation, priority should be given to MOFs constructed from biocompatible metals with well-defined metabolic fates, together with rigorous long-term toxicological evaluations covering metal biodistribution, excretion kinetics, genotoxicity, and immunotoxicity [73]. In addition, surface functionalization of MOFs with targeting moieties such as polyethylene glycol, HA, FA, and antibodies can effectively reduce immune clearance while substantially enhancing tumor-targeting capability and biocompatibility [74,75]. Sheng et al. developed a targeted nano-drug system based on biodegradable iron-based MOFs, specifically MIL-100. This system co-loads tyrosinase and tyrosine within MIL-100 and utilizes surface-modified HA to target CD44-overexpressing 4T1 breast cancer cells, achieving efficient tumor accumulation. In the TME, MIL-100 can convert H2O2 to O2 to enhance enzyme catalysis and produce ·OH through the Fenton reaction while depleting GSH, thereby synergistically enhancing the efficacy of chemotherapeutic kinetics [76]. GSH-degradable MIL-100 exhibits good long-term biocompatibility. MOFs with particle sizes below 5 nm are readily eliminated by renal clearance, while larger particles are metabolized in the liver. Such hepatic processing tends to induce particle accumulation within reticuloendothelial system (RES) organs (liver and spleen), thereby lowering the overall in vivo metabolic clearance efficienc. Geng et al. utilized ultrasound fragmentation to prepare Gd3+-porphyrin framework nanodots (GdHF-NDs) with a particle size of approximately 5 nm, which can generate a large amount of ROS and demonstrate high performance in cancer SDT, while being rapidly excreted through the renal pathway, showcasing high biocompatibility [77]. MOFs possess biocompatibility and biodegradability, and can be synthesized using biosafe metal ions and organic ligands, thus enabling their safe application in cancer immunotherapy. Their degradation products can be safely metabolized by the organism, with almost no physiological side effects. Based on the aforementioned properties of MOFs, this review summarizes the research progress regarding their application as NDDS for cancer immunotherapy.

Design strategies for MOF-based immunomodulatory platforms

In summary, the rational design of highly efficient MOF-based NDDS for cancer immunomodulation requires a comprehensive balance among their material properties, structural compositions, and anticipated biological effects. At the construction level, synthesis and encapsulation strategies should be flexibly tailored to the dimensions of the guest molecules, prioritizing stable and highly biocompatible metal nodes (e.g., Zr4+, Fe3+, and Zn2+). Furthermore, MOFs incorporating porphyrinic ligands (e.g., TCPP, DBP) can inherently act as photosensitizers to facilitate PDT under light irradiation. This enables the efficient generation of ROS to induce ICD, serving as a crucial prerequisite for ensuring systemic biosafety and exerting multimodal therapeutic functions. Depending on diverse administration routes and targeting objectives, the physicochemical properties of MOFs must be precisely engineered; for instance, nanocarriers smaller than 100 nm with spiked morphologies or specific geometries can significantly facilitate lymph node homing and macrophage polarization [78]. Regarding systemic delivery and circulation, the implementation of surface modifications—such as PEGylation, biomimetic cell membrane camouflage, or targeting peptide conjugation—effectively prevents premature cargo leakage, prolongs blood circulation, and achieves immune evasion, thereby precisely targeting tumor cells. Concurrently, integrating the passive EPR effect with ligand-mediated active recognition drastically augments cellular internalization by antigen-presenting cells APCs while mitigating systemic toxicity. Ultimately, the integration of multiplexed stimuli-responsive mechanisms driven by the endogenous TME (e.g., acidic pH, elevated GSH) or exogenous fields (e.g., light, ultrasound) enables the spatiotemporally controlled release of ICIs or adjuvants (Fig. 4). Such a holistic design paradigm, which seamlessly unifies on-demand precision delivery, intrinsic immune activation (such as the built-in adjuvant effects of metal nodes), and multimodal combinatorial therapy, establishes a robust foundation for developing highly potent and low-toxicity.

NDDS in cancer immunotherapy.

MOFs as NDDS for cancer immunotherapy

In cancer immunotherapy, NDDS often face several challenges, including insufficient drug-loading capacity, difficulty in co-delivering multiple therapeutic agents, poor tumor targeting, nonspecific drug release, limited immune activation efficiency, and systemic toxicity. MOFs, owing to their unique structural and functional advantages, provide effective solutions to these limitations. First, MOFs possess ultrahigh porosity and exceptionally large specific surface areas. Their porous crystalline architectures enable excellent loading capacities, particularly for small-molecule drugs and immunoadjuvants, with drug-loading efficiencies often exceeding those of liposomes and polymeric nanocarriers. This feature facilitates the efficient co-delivery of ICIs, tumor antigens, immunoadjuvants, and small-molecule therapeutics. Second, the metal nodes and organic ligands of MOFs are highly designable. For example, metal nodes such as Mn2+, Cu2+, Fe2+, Zr4+, and Hf4+, as well as functional ligands such as porphyrins and TCPP, can endow MOFs with catalytic, photosensitizing, sonosensitizing, or radiosensitizing properties, thereby enabling the integration of CDT, PDT, SDT, RT, and immunotherapy within a single platform.

In addition, MOFs exhibit TME-responsive degradation behavior. They commonly undergo structural disassembly under the weakly acidic, glutathione-rich, or H2O2-enriched conditions of the TME, thereby enabling precise drug release while remaining relatively stable in normal tissues. This property reduces the nonspecific distribution of immunotherapeutic agents and associated adverse effects. Notably, certain metal ions released from degraded MOFs possess intrinsic immunomodulatory activity. For instance, Mn2+ can activate the cGAS-STING pathway, whereas Zn2+ and Cu2+ can induce mitochondrial damage and ICD. Thus, MOFs can enhance tumor immunogenicity and promote the conversion of “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors even without additional adjuvants. Meanwhile, the surfaces of MOFs can be readily functionalized through PEGylation, HA modification, antibody conjugation, or peptide coupling, enabling active targeting and prolonged circulation, improving tumor accumulation, and reducing off-target toxicity.

The uniform and ordered crystalline structures of MOFs allow the spatially precise arrangement of metal nodes and functional ligands, thereby facilitating spatiotemporal synergy among RT, CDT, ICD induction, and immune activation. Moreover, the in situ encapsulation capability of MOFs can effectively protect biomacromolecules such as Small interfering RNA (siRNA), DNA, protein antigens, and antibodies from enzymatic degradation, thereby improving vaccine delivery stability and antigen presentation efficiency. Finally, some MOFs can amplify antitumor immune responses through cascade reactions, such as GSH depletion, ROS elevation, and mitochondrial damage induction, which progressively remodel the immunosuppressive TME and enhance ICD and subsequent immune responses. Collectively, MOFs integrate high loading capacity, programmable assembly, TME responsiveness, intrinsic immunomodulation, targetable surface modification, and cascade amplification, offering an important strategy for constructing efficient, precise, and low-toxicity NDDS for cancer immunotherapy.

Mechanistic basis of MOF-mediated ICD and TME remodeling

Concept of ICD and its central role in cancer immunotherapy

ICD is a form of programmed cell death capable of eliciting adaptive immune responses against TAAs. Unlike conventional apoptosis, which is generally considered immunologically silent or even tolerogenic in most contexts, ICD is characterized by the release or surface exposure of a series of DAMPs from dying or stressed tumor cells. Representative DAMPs include the translocation of calreticulin (CRT) to the cell surface, the secretion of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), and the release of high-mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) [[79], [80], [81]].

These DAMPs play essential roles in the initiation of antitumor immunity. Specifically, surface-exposed CRT functions as an “eat-me” signal, promoting the phagocytosis of dying tumor cells by DCs and macrophages [82]. ATP acts as a “find-me” signal that recruits APCs and promotes inflammasome activation [83]. HMGB1 can bind to pattern recognition receptors such as Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), thereby enhancing tumor antigen processing and cross-presentation. Collectively, these events promote DC maturation, tumor antigen cross-presentation, and the activation of CTLs, thereby converting local tumor cell death into a systemic antitumor immune response [81]. Therefore, ICD is regarded as a critical bridge linking local tumor eradication with systemic immune activation and represents an important mechanistic basis by which many MOF-based therapeutic strategies convert “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors.

Major molecular pathways of MOF-induced ICD

Depending on their metal nodes, organic ligands, and loaded components, MOFs can induce ICD in tumor cells through multiple mechanisms, mainly involving ROS-mediated endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, cuproptosis, and ferroptosis.

First, ROS-mediated ER stress represents one of the classical mechanisms by which MOFs induce ICD. Many MOFs contain photosensitive organic ligands, such as the porphyrin-based ligand TCPP, or metal nodes with Fenton-like catalytic activity, such as Fe3+, Cu2+, and Mn2+. Upon external light irradiation, ultrasound stimulation, or exposure to endogenous H2O2 in the TME, these MOFs can efficiently generate ROS, including 1O2, ·OH, and superoxide anions (O2−). Excessive ROS disrupt protein-folding homeostasis and induce ER stress, which further leads to hallmark ICD events, including CRT exposure, ATP secretion, and HMGB1 release.

Second, metal ions released from degraded MOFs can induce mitochondrial dysfunction and further trigger ICD. Certain metal ions, such as Cu2+, Zn2+, and Mn2+, can accumulate in tumor cells and interfere with mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, resulting in mitochondrial membrane potential dissipation, excessive ROS generation, and cytochrome C release, thereby initiating the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. When accompanied by sufficient exposure or release of DAMPs, this process can exhibit pronounced immunogenicity. In addition, the recently identified copper-dependent form of cell death, known as cuproptosis, provides a new mechanistic explanation for MOF-induced ICD. Cu-based MOFs can release Cu2+ in the TME, promote copper accumulation in mitochondria, and induce the aggregation of lipoylated tricarboxylic acid cycle-related proteins, particularly dihydrolipoamide S-acetyltransferase (DLAT), thereby causing proteotoxic stress and mitochondrial metabolic dysfunction [84]. This intense cellular stress can further promote DAMP release and enhance tumor immunogenicity.

Third, ferroptosis-related pathways also constitute an important route for MOF-induced ICD. Iron-based MOFs, such as MIL-101(Fe), can catalyze H2O2 to generate ·OH through Fenton-like reactions while simultaneously consuming GSH, thereby impairing the GSH peroxidase 4 (GPX4)-mediated antioxidant defense system and ultimately leading to lipid peroxide accumulation and ferroptosis. Increasing evidence indicates that ferroptosis is not only a metabolism-dependent form of cell death but also an important inducer of ICD. Lipid peroxidation (LPO) products, oxidative stress signals, and the concomitant release of DAMPs can collectively promote DC activation, antigen presentation, and T-cell infiltration. Therefore, iron-based MOFs provide an important strategy for enhancing cancer immunotherapy by coupling ferroptosis with ICD.

MOF-mediated remodeling of the TIME

TME is a complex ecosystem in which tumor cells interact with immune cells, stromal cells, blood vessels, and the ECM, collectively influencing tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis. Under malignant conditions, the TME exhibits a range of immunosuppressive features, including hypoxia, acidosis, elevated levels of ROS, GSH expression, and extensive infiltration of immunosuppressive cells [85]. Together, these characteristics establish barriers that impede antitumor immune responses, particularly contributing to the formation of so-called immunologically “cold” tumors, which are characterized by insufficient cytotoxic T-cell infiltration and enrichment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and Tregs. Remodeling this suppressive microenvironment and converting it into an immunologically “hot” state has therefore become one of the central strategies in cancer immunotherapy.

Hypoxia, a hallmark of solid tumors, can upregulate Hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α), thereby promoting aberrant angiogenesis, tumor invasion, and the infiltration of immunosuppressive cells such as Tregs and MDSCs, while also compromising the efficacy of oxygen-dependent therapies such as PDT. Enzyme-mimetic MOFs, such as Fe-MOFs, exhibit peroxidase (POD)-like and GSH oxidase (GSHox)-like activities, enabling them to catalyze intratumoral hydrogen peroxide to generate ·OH. This process amplifies intracellular ROS levels, alleviates hypoxia-mediated immunosuppression, and induces ferroptosis in cancer cells [86].

Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are important immune cells in the TME. M2-like TAMs can promote tumor progression and immune escape by secreting factors such as IL-10, TGF-β, and VEGF. Metal ions, including Mn2+, Zn2+, and Cu2+, released from the degradation of MOFs can regulate intracellular oxidative stress and inflammatory signaling in macrophages, thereby activating pathways such as NF-κB and STAT1 and promoting the polarization of TAMs from the M2 phenotype toward the M1 phenotype. M1-like TAMs can secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-12, TNF-α, and Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), which enhance antigen presentation, CTLs recruitment, and tumor immune killing [87].

The cGAS-STING pathway is a key innate immune signaling axis that links cytosolic DNA sensing to type I interferon production. Owing to their tunable porosity, MOFs can precisely deliver DNA, cGAMP, or STING agonists to tumor sites. In addition, MOFs can directly modulate the cGAS-STING pathway through their metal components, such as Zn2+ and Mn2+. For example, Cu2+/Fe2+-MOFs can promote the generation/release of mitochondrial DNA, thereby activating STING signaling and inducing the production of type I interferons and chemokines. This process further enhances DC maturation, antigen cross-presentation, and CD8+ T cell infiltration [88].

In summary, MOF-based NDDS can exert antitumor immunotherapeutic effects through a synergistic regulatory network involving ICD induction and TME remodeling. These mechanisms do not operate independently; rather, they mutually reinforce one another and synergistically amplify antitumor immune responses through multilayered interactions. This not only highlights the multifunctional advantages of MOFs in tumor immune modulation but also provides an important theoretical basis for their further application in the delivery of ICIs, cancer vaccines, and gene therapy agents, as well as in the construction of multimodal combination therapeutic platforms. Moreover, MOF-based nanoplatforms can suppress tumor progression through multiple interactions, including TME remodeling, enhanced oxidative stress, ICD induction, immune activation, and synergistic integration with immune checkpoint blockade. These findings further support the potential of MOFs as versatile platforms for multimodal tumor therapy [[89], [90], [91], [92]]. The following section will systematically discuss specific MOF-based NDDS and their applications in cancer immunotherapy.

MOFs for delivering ICIs

Tumor cells promote immune checkpoint signaling pathways that lead to the inactivation of T cells and the activation of programmed cell death, which allows them to escape immune surveillance and facilitates their growth and metastasis [4,93]. ICIs have the ability to obstruct pathways like programmed cell death protein 1 and its ligand 1 (PD-1/PD-L1), cytotoxic T lymphocyte-associated antigen 4 (CTLA-4), and IDO [94,95]. This action reactivates T cell functionality and boosts immune responses against tumors. Nonetheless, the use of ICIs as a single-agent treatment encounters obstacles including inadequate targeting, systemic immune-related side effects, and the development of resistance, all of which restrict their effectiveness in clinical settings [[96], [97], [98]].

Recently, MOFs have become vital vehicles for enhancing the administration of ICIs, thanks to their adjustable pore designs, significant drug loading capabilities, and straightforward functional modification. This development offers novel pathways to address the challenges associated with ICIs. On one hand, MOFs can achieve precise delivery of ICIs through ligand or antibody modification, significantly reducing off-target toxicity. For instance, Jiang et al. designed a PD-L1/CTLA-4 bispecific antibody KN046 loaded on ZIF-8 (KN046@19F-ZIF-8), which significantly enhanced tumor immune responses while reducing systemic toxicity [99]. Qin et al. constructed an engineered Escherichia coli (EcN) co-expressing anti-PD-L1 and anti-CD9 nanobodies (EcN-Nb), forming a hybrid system of EcN-Nb-ZIF-8CHO-ICG, which is loaded with the photothermal agent Indocyanine Green (ICG) and protected by ZIF-8CHO. This system enables precise spatiotemporal control for immunotherapy. The NIR activation of the ZIF-8 layer disassembles it, releasing the PD-L1/CD9 antibodies, which target and inhibit the migration of tumor-derived exosomes (TDE) secreted by tumor cells to promote tumor necrosis. This procedure alters TAMs by shifting them from the M2 phenotype to the M1 phenotype, stimulates T lymphocytes, inhibits tumor growth and metastasis, and improves the effectiveness of PD-L1 therapy [100].

On the other hand, MOFs can integrate PDT, PTT, chemotherapy, or microwave (MW) therapy to enhance the efficacy of ICIs by inducing ICD. The core-shell structured nanosystem (M@O-A) developed by Zhang et al. utilizes PCN-224 loaded with oxaliplatin (OXA) as the core, and is modified with PD-L1 aptamer on the outer layer. This system not only generates 1O2 through the photosensitizer MOFs to achieve PDT, but also delivers OXA and aptPD-L1, synergistically improving tumor accumulation efficiency while reducing the immunotoxicity of chemotherapy, effectively inhibiting both primary and distal colorectal tumors (Fig. 5A). After irradiation with a 640 nm laser, tumors in mice from the three M@O-A NP-treated groups were significantly inhibited (Fig. 5B), and the longest survival was achieved (Fig. 5C). Moreover, the proportions of antitumor CD3+(total T cells), CD3+ CD4+(Th cells), and CD3+ CD8+(cytotoxic T cells) T cells were markedly increased (Fig. 5D–E). In contrast, the proportions of inhibitory immune CD25+CD4+ regulatory T cells (Tregs) and MDSCs were substantially reduced (Fig. 5F-G), thereby triggering a potent antitumor immune response [101]. Liang et al. also utilized PCN-224 to deliver the PD-L1 inhibitor (BMS-202), with an outer layer modified by HA - polyethylene glycol (HA-PEG). Under laser irradiation, it induces tumor necrosis and ICD through PDT, thereby synergistically altering the TME to inhibit tumor growth [102]. Wu et al. constructed a targeted MnCa-MOF nanoactivator, MPPT, capable of co-delivering pyrotinib and a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor, while exploiting Mn2+ to activate the cGAS-STING pathway and strengthen innate antitumor immunity [103]. This platform integrates microwave hyperthermia, chemotherapy, ICB, and STING activation to achieve synergistic tumor eradication. Microwave treatment not only enhances local tumor ablation but also relieves tumor hypoxia, thereby reshaping the immunosuppressive microenvironment. In parallel, pyrotinib inhibits Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) downstream signaling pathways, including PI3K/AKT/mTOR and Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)/ERK, further improving the therapeutic efficacy of PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. As a result, MPPT synchronously suppresses primary and metastatic tumors and elicits long-term immune memory.

MOFs encapsulating IDO inhibitors and other metabolic regulators can reverse immune suppression and stimulate anti-tumor immune responses, providing superior strategies for cancer treatment. Recent advancements have focused on developing stimuli-responsive MOFs that ensure precision drug release while synergizing chemotherapy with ICB. The team led by Ding et al. utilized MOF to encapsulate the hydrophobic IDO inhibitor (NLG919), anchoring small chloramphenicol prodrug (CLB) loaded gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) on the surface of the MOFs, which were further modified with PEG-targeted peptides to create phosphate-responsive MOFs (RPMANB NPs) [104]. In a high phosphate environment within cells, the release of the NLG919 is achieved, and the prodrug chloramphenicol is activated in situ by AuNPs, facilitating the synergy between chemotherapy and the reversal of immune suppression. Building on the concept of microenvironmental responsiveness, other researchers have targeted the hypoxic nature of tumors to trigger immune activation. The hypoxia-responsive MOFs system (AFN MOFs) developed by Li et al. integrates Fe3+, the AQ4N, and NLG919 to initiate a cascade reaction within the hypoxic microenvironment after HIFU treatment. In AFN MOFs, Fe3+induces pyroptosis and triggers ICD via the activation of the caspase-1/GSDMD pathway. Simultaneously, AQ4N is selectively activated under hypoxic conditions to kill tumor cells through chemotherapy, while NLG919 relieves immunosuppression by inhibiting IDO-1 enzyme activity. Together, these three components exert a synergistic anti-tumor effect through a combined chemo-pyroptosis-immunotherapy approach [105].

Beyond triggered chemotherapy, MOFs are increasingly employed to integrate metabolic starvation and physical therapies into the immunotherapeutic framework. Dai et al. developed an Mn-MOF-based nanocarrier drug delivery system (PCP-Mn-DTA@GOx@1-MT) for the co-delivery of GOx and the IDO inhibitor 1-methyltryptophan (1-MT), enabling a cascade-amplified effect in tumor starvation/oxidative immunotherapy (Fig. 6A). In a murine melanoma model, PCP-Mn-DTA@GOx@1-MT significantly increased the proportion of tumor-infiltrating CTLs and maximally reduced the number of Tregs (p < 0.01). These findings indicate that, by virtue of IDO pathway blockade and efficient drug delivery, this system exerts potent immunomodulatory effects, thereby effectively activating antitumor immune responses and suppressing tumor immune escape (Fig. 6B-C) [106]. Similarly, the integration of light-responsive elements allows for enhanced control over ROS production. The team led by Dua et al. constructed a core-shell structure UCNP@MOFs (UPIS), where the Cu-MOFs shell serves as a photosensitizer and nanoenzyme to increase ROS generation. Cu2+ reacts with H2O2 and GSH in the TME to amplify the ROS effect; the pore-loaded IDO inhibitor and S-nitroso-N-acetylpenicillamine (SNAP) activate T cells via NO to treat tumors. UPIS exhibits a significant PDT effect under NIR light irradiation, demonstrating a synergistic immunotherapeutic effect [107]. Finally, the application of these MOF-based immune regulators is expanding from systemic nanocarriers to specialized, multifunctional scaffolds designed for tissue regeneration. Ma et al. integrated the synthesized microwave-responsive nanomaterial ZIF-8·DOX@IDO into a smart multifunctional composite scaffold made from 3D printed titanium. This scaffold possesses both microwave thermosensitization and TEM responsiveness, enabling tumor ICD via a combination of microwave thermal therapy and chemotherapy. Additionally, the released IDO inhibitor activates immune cells, while the Zn2+ generated from the breakdown of ZIF-8 encourages the osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells and facilitates new bone formation, leading to a synergistic effect in the management of osteosarcoma and the regeneration of bone [108].

In summary, MOFs, with their tunable structural characteristics and multifunctional integration capabilities, not only address the limitations of ICIs in terms of poor targeting and resistance in monotherapy but also provide an efficient and safe new strategy for tumor immunotherapy through precise delivery and multimodal synergy.

MOFs for cancer vaccine delivery

Cancer vaccines, as a highly promising approach in tumor immunotherapy, functioning mainly through the cooperative effect of immunogenic antigens alongside immune adjuvants. This approach improves the efficiency of antigen presentation, stimulates the body's specific immune response, and modifies the immunosuppressive microenvironment of tumors, ultimately leading to a strong anti-tumor immune response. Various immune adjuvants commonly utilized include Toll-like receptors (TLRs) and stimulator of interferon genes (STING). Among TLR adjuvants, a notable class of novel microbial nucleic acid adjuvants is unmethylated CpG ODNs, which can induce Th1-type immune responses, promote the swift expansion of CTLs, and effectively stimulate the release of IFN-γ, thereby enhancing the immunogenicity of the vaccine and improving immune efficacy [109].

However, the challenges of low antigen delivery and internalization efficiency, as well as insufficient immunogenicity, encountered in clinical applications significantly limit their efficacy. In this context, MOFs, with their unique structural and functional advantages, have emerged as key carriers for optimizing cancer vaccine delivery, providing innovative solutions to overcome the aforementioned bottlenecks. The core advantages of MOFs as vaccine delivery systems include: protecting antigens and adjuvants from rapid degradation, enhancing targeted delivery capabilities through functional modification, promoting the endocytosis of APCs, and enabling the co-delivery of antigens and adjuvants, thereby efficiently triggering adaptive immune responses and significantly improving the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy [110]. The NDDS of MOFs loads cancer vaccines and precisely targets tumor sites, exhibiting high stability and biocompatibility. This method successfully promotes adaptive immune responses and significantly improves the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy [111].In recent times, several research teams have developed nano-vaccine systems based on MOFs. By precisely regulating immune responses and coordinating multi-dimensional therapeutic strategies, these systems offer new insights for overcoming tumor immune tolerance and enhancing anti-cancer efficacy.

In specific applications, MOFs often enable the precise release of antigens and adjuvants via pH-responsive design, thereby inducing a potent cellular immune response. Zhang et al. first encapsulated OVA using pH-responsive ZIF-8 nanoparticles and adsorbed CpG ODNs, facilitating the simultaneous delivery and release of antigens and adjuvants within the same APCs. This vaccine successfully induced robust humoral and cellular immunity [112]. The cancer vaccine delivery system SOM-ZIF-8/HDSF, created by Zuo et al., effectively encapsulates antigens by utilizing the ordered mesoporous molecular sieve ZIF-8 (SOM-ZIF-8) alongside hexadecylsulfonyl fluoride (HDSF), which serves to enhance lysosomal pH and safeguard the antigens from degradation due to acidity. This further enhances immune activation, eliciting a potent antigen-specific T cell response in the B16 melanoma model and effectively inhibiting tumor growth [113]. Duan et al. utilized MOFs to encapsulate TAAs, which decompose and release TAAs in an acidic environment to enhance antigen cross-presentation. Furthermore, they introduced CpG via Watson-Crick base pairing, which further promoted the CD8+ T cell response and efficiently recruiting tumor-killing immune cells, thereby improving anti-tumor efficacy [114]. The pH-responsive MS@MOF nano cancer vaccine developed by Li et al. integrates tumor antigens (OVA) and immunoadjuvants (polyIC) into MS nanoparticles with a ZIF-8 shell using an ultra-fast low-temperature self-assembly technique. This approach utilizes pH-responsive targeted delivery of antigens/adjuvants to APCs and lymph nodes, enhancing efficiency while reducing off-target risks. In the E.G7-OVA tumor-bearing mouse model, the synergistic effect of the MS@MOF vaccine enabled anti-PD-1 antibodies to maintain antitumor efficacy comparable to that of the original dose even when administered at only one-tenth of the monotherapy dose [115]. Mg-MOFs loaded with cisplatin (DDP) and vincristine (VCR) have been engineered to create a bio-mineralized Lactococcus lactis in situ vaccine (D&V@FOLactis), which expresses Fms-like tyrosine kinase 3 ligand (Flt3L) (DCs growth factor) and OX40L (T cell co-stimulatory factor) to activate immune cells. This system exhibits pH-responsive drug release behavior following intratumoral administration, enhancing antigen exposure through the induction of ICD, thereby attracting immune cells and eliciting long-term anti-tumor immunity [116]. D&V@FOLactis can reshape the TME, activate T cell responses, and exert inhibitory effects on both “cold” and “hot” tumors, producing distant effects and extending survival.

By organically combining PDT/PTT/SDT with nano-vaccines, a multifunctional synergistic therapeutic platform has been constructed, significantly enhancing the efficacy of PD-1 antibodies in the treatment of solid tumors. This strategy not only overcomes the limitations of monotherapy but also reshapes the TIME through multimodal synergistic effects, providing new insights for tumor treatment. Liu et al. utilized Cu-MOF (Cu-THBQ/AX) to load alkaline phosphatase substrates along with the ERK5 inhibitor XMD8-92 (extracellular signal-regulated kinase 5 inhibitor) to construct therapeutic vaccines in situ, achieving dual-pathway synergistic anti-tumor effects (Fig. 7A). This system can inhibit macrophage-mediated efferocytosis, thereby reducing immunosuppressive clearance of apoptotic cells and promoting the abundant release of TAAs and DAMPs. Meanwhile, it drives the polarization of TAMs from the M2 phenotype toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype, significantly increases the proportion of mature DCs, and potently enhances the tumor infiltration and activation of CTLs. In in vivo models, this nanovaccine not only markedly suppresses orthotopic tumor growth but also elicits an abscopal effect to inhibit distant tumors, reduces pulmonary metastasis, and induces durable immune memory, effectively preventing tumor recurrence and demonstrating immunotherapeutic efficacy through in situ tumor vaccination (Fig. 7B-D) [117]. Cai et al. developed a cancer vaccine (PCN-ACF-CpG@HA) using MOFs as carriers. This vaccine is designed for targeted delivery by loading the hypoxia inhibitor ACF and the immune adjuvant CpG, and by modifying HA. Under the hypoxic environment induced by PDT, ACF can block the HIF-1α signaling pathway, while CpG enhances the immunogenic properties of the antigen, working together to promote anti-tumor immune responses and to suppress the metastasis and recurrence of tumors [118]. Zhu et al. developed a transdermal nano-vaccine based on dissolvable microneedles, utilizing a TA/Mn2+ coordination framework to deliver β-catenin silencing DNAzyme and polyserotonin (PST) core. Upon the release of the vaccine from the microneedles, PTT induces ICD, while Mn2+ activates the DNAzyme to promote DC aggregation. The PST captures antigens to enhance cross-presentation, thereby significantly inhibiting the progression of melanoma [119]. The development of MOF-based cancer vaccines has expanded into specialized delivery formats, such as transdermal patches and biomimetic microcrystals, to construct a durable immune microenvironment. Zhan et al. constructed a Mn-MOF nano-vaccine, cMnMOF@CM, coated with the membranes of melanoma cells, which, in conjunction with the CpG adjuvant and SDT, effectively reduces tumor hypoxia and induces ICD. It enhances the maturation of DCs and stimulates T cell activation. Furthermore, when administered alongside anti-PD-1 therapy, it can trigger a systemic immune response and establish enduring immune memory, thereby significantly inhibiting both tumor growth and the risk of recurrence [120]. In addition, Liu et al. constructed manganese microcrystals (Mn/Zr-MOF-shY1-CM) that load the target YTH N6-methyladenosine RNA binding protein 1 (Ythdf1) for a biomimetic nano-vaccine [121]. The bimetallic MOFs microcrystals serve as an endogenous adjuvant delivery system, allowing for the sustained release of TAAs and Mn2+. This system aggregates in situ to form a pro-inflammatory immune niche, regulating the spatiotemporal immunity of DCs, such as recruiting DC1s, activating them, and facilitating lymph node metastasis. The combined effect of Mn2+ stimulating the cGAS-STING pathway along with the downregulation of Ythdf1 enhances antigen cross-presentation, this vaccine demonstrates good preventive effects against melanoma and colorectal tumors, and it can also inhibit the postoperative local recurrence of colorectal cancer and metachronous liver metastasis.

According to the research outlined above, MOF-based nanovaccines have shown promise in suppressing tumor development, metastasis, and recurrence, offering an innovative approach to personalized cancer immunotherapy. As the research on material design and immune mechanisms deepens, MOFs nanovaccines are expected to serve as a bridge connecting precise delivery with efficient immune activation, thereby advancing the development of cancer treatment.

MOFs for gene therapy drug delivery

MOFs offer distinct benefits for nucleic acid drug delivery due to their nanoscale porous architecture and tunable surface properties providing innovative solutions for gene therapy. As carriers of genetic information, nucleic acids (DNA/RNA) are highly susceptible to degradation during the delivery process. However, the protective structures formed through the biomineralization process of MOFs can effectively shield nucleic acids from enzymatic degradation [122]. This carrier system not only protects the integrity of nucleic acids through physical encapsulation but also significantly enhances cellular uptake efficiency due to its nanoscale pores. More importantly, through surface modification and charge regulation, MOFs can further enhance the stability of nanoparticles, thus serving as an excellent medium for the delivery of DNA, siRNA, and nucleic acid aptamers.

Leveraging the inherent metal ions within the MOF framework as co-factors to activate genetic tools while simultaneously triggering prodrug conversion. The bimetallic MOFs system designed by Wang et al. ingeniously combines metal ion catalysis with the gene editing capabilities of DNAzymes [123]. In the tumor micro-acidic environment, the Cu2+ released by this system can catalyze the conversion of prodrugs, resulting in the synthesis of drugs within cells that effectively kill cancer cells while minimizing side effects on normal biological organisms. Meanwhile, Zn2+ acts as a cofactor to activate the cleavage activity of DNAzyme, inhibiting tumor proliferation and metastasis by targeting and cleaving oncogene substrates. Similarly, Guo J utilized Fe3+/Mn2+-tannic acid (TA) coordination to form a multifunctional core-shell structure with a PLGA inner layer loaded with DPCPX (an adenosine A1 receptor inhibitor) and a MOFs outer layer, encapsulating the anti-Pd-L1 DNAzyme (Dz) [124]. This nanosystem functions as a vehicle for the simultaneous delivery of DPCPX and Dz, and it also aids in the release of Mn2+, which activates Dz to degrade PD-L1 mRNA. This results in a dual blockade of ADORA1 and PD-L1. This synergistic inhibition strategy significantly enhances the ICD effect, effectively suppressing both primary and metastatic tumors, and improving the immunotherapeutic efficacy against melanoma.

MOFs are frequently employed to deliver siRNA aimed at silencing resistance-related genes, thereby sensitizing recalcitrant tumors to conventional chemotherapy. In the face of the challenge of drug resistance, He et al. employ MOFs to construct a co-delivery system for cisplatin and siRNA. MOFs safeguard siRNA from nuclease degradation, effectively silencing various genes associated with multiple drug resistance (MDR). This process increases the sensitivity of drug-resistant ovarian cancer cells to cisplatin, thereby amplifying the cytotoxic impact of cisplatin on these cancer cells [125]. Chen et al. designed a nanoparticle-modified MIL-101(Fe) delivery system utilizing selenium (Se) and ruthenium (Ru) to target the silencing of MDR genes and to disrupt microtubule (MT) dynamics within paclitaxel-resistant MCF-7/T cells. This approach aims to improve the effectiveness of siRNA therapy [126]. This system significantly enhances the nuclease resistance of siRNA and promotes its escape from lysosomes, achieving efficient silencing of resistant genes. By activating the p53, MAPK, and PI3K/Akt signaling pathways, it induces apoptosis, disrupts MT dynamics, and interferes with mitotic spindle assembly, demonstrating significant tumor suppression effects with low toxicity in MCF-7/T xenograft models. In other systems, MOFs serve primarily as delivery vehicles, while the loaded payloads (e.g., gene editing tools or chemotherapeutics) drive immune activation, with MOF components providing synergistic effects. Huang et al. developed a HA-modified Mn2+/Zn2+ bimetallic MOF nanosystem delivering a CRISPR plasmid targeting the SLC43A2 gene. This platform effectively knocked down SLC43A2 via CRISPR-mediated gene editing, blocking excessive methionine uptake by tumor cells and alleviating their metabolic competition with T cells. Consequently, T cells acquired sufficient methionine, restoring their function and reducing apoptosis. Meanwhile, Mn2+/Zn2+ released from the degraded MOFs induced ROS burst, causing mitochondrial damage and mtDNA release, which robustly activated the cGAS-STING pathway. This activation markedly upregulated p-TBK1, IFN-β, and CXCL10, and promoted DC maturation. STING activation enhanced T-cell infiltration, while methionine metabolism regulation restored T-cell function. Ultimately, this system significantly increased intratumoral infiltration of CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and CD4+ helper T cells, elevated proinflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IFN-β), reversed the immunosuppressive microenvironment, inhibited both orthotopic and distant tumors, and prolonged mouse survival [127].

By coordinating gene silencing with immune stimulation, MOF-based NDDS can reshape the TIME to foster durable anti-tumor responses. Wang et al. developed a MOFDOX&siATR NDDS for the synergistic co-delivery of doxorubicin (DOX) and ATR-targeting siRNA. In this system, DOX disrupts DNA structure and induces DNA damage and mutations, whereas siATR suppresses ATR protein expression, thereby blocking DNA repair. The synergistic action of DOX and siATR significantly increased the tumor mutational burden and promoted the generation of the neoantigen Atp8b1, which effectively activated DCs maturation and CD8+ T-cell immune responses, remodeled the TIME, and established immune memory. This strategy provides a promising new approach for the immunotherapy of poorly immunogenic pancreatic cancer (Fig. 8) [128]. Similarly, Zeng et al. created a delivery system that targets dual receptors utilizing MOFs, which synergistically delivers discoidin domain receptor 2 (DDR2) specific siRNA (siDDR2) and ITGAV specific siRNA (siITGAV) to specifically interfere with the ECM of tumor cells [129]. They respectively target the DDR2-regulated collagen-cell interaction pathway and the ITGAV-mediated TGF-β1 activation pathway, achieving dual regulation of the ECM. In a model of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), the inhibition of DDR2 and ITGAV leads to a marked decrease in collagen accumulation, an enhancement of CD8+ T cell infiltration, a reduction in PD-L1 expression, and, as a result, a reduction in tumor size. In addition to structural remodeling, MOFs can act as high-efficiency conduits for activating innate immune sensing pathways. Chen and colleagues integrated non-methylated cytosine-phosphate-guanine oligodeoxynucleotides (CpG-ODNs) with the vascular disrupting agent 5,6-dimethylxanthenone-4-acetic acid (DMXAA, an agonist for STING) alongside MOF-801 to form nanoparticles that self-assemble, referred to as MOF-CpG-DMXAA [130]. The MOF-801 functions not only as a highly effective carrier for enhancing the cellular absorption of CpG ODNs and DMXAA but also stimulates the cGAS-STING-NF-κB signaling pathway via the recognition of Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) at remarkably low concentrations (70 ng/mL). Furthermore, the activation of the STING pathway is enhanced synergistically by three elements that contribute to reprogramming TAMs, facilitating the maturation of DCs, and altering tumor vasculature, which in turn leads to an improved TME. MOF-CpG-DMXAA synergistically exerted immunostimulatory functions, achieving favorable therapeutic outcomes against orthotopic and recurrent liver cancer.

MOFs have broad application prospects in the field of nucleic acid drug delivery. Through sophisticated material design and functional integration, MOFs carriers not only address the key bottlenecks in nucleic acid drug delivery but also achieve multi-modal synergy for chemotherapy, gene therapy, and immunotherapy, providing new strategies for tumor treatment.

MOFs NDDS as immunomodulators

Owing to their excellent biocompatibility and highly efficient delivery performance, MOFs have been widely recognized as an innovative class of nanocarrier systems that seamlessly integrate drug delivery capabilities with immunomodulatory effects.

MOFs can directly function as intrinsic immunomodulators. Zheng et al. synthesized an immunostimulatory Mn2+-MOF (ISAM-MOF), which is capable of releasing a substantial amount of Mn2+ in the acidic TME. Compared with free MnCl2, this material more effectively activates the cGAS-STING signaling pathway, thereby enhancing antitumor efficacy in the B16F10 melanoma model and promoting the infiltration of APCs and cytotoxic T cells into tumor tissues [131]. Bimetallic MOFs function as immune modulators, greatly improving anti-tumor immune effectiveness by harnessing the combined effects of metal ions. In addition, the two metal centers perform distinct yet cooperative roles: the Gd/Zn bimetallic MOF (Gd-MOF-5) synthesized by Dai et al. can downregulate the immunosuppressive phosphatidylserine signaling, inducing ICD. Gd3+ competitively inhibits the externalization of phosphatidylserine, improving the immune microenvironment; Zn2+ overload leads to mitochondrial dysfunction and endoplasmic reticulum stress. This synergistic effect allows Gd-MOF-5 to function both as an immunomodulatory agent that suppresses immunoinhibitory signals and as an inducer of ICD that enhances immunostimulatory cues, thereby markedly improving the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy [132]. Cu-based MOF nanocarriers possess intrinsic immunomodulatory functions. Du et al. constructed a self-amplifying bimetallic mitochondrial inhibitor, termed Mito-Jammer, using MOF-199 as the carrier and co-loading DOX and CaO2 (Fig. 9A). This system undergoes TME-responsive degradation and releases Cu2+, leading to a marked increase in intracellular Cu2+ levels, which promotes DLAT oligomerization, downregulates Ferredoxin 1 (FDX1), triggers aberrant mitochondrial protein lipoylation and proteotoxic stress, and ultimately induces cuproptosis. Furthermore, cuproptosis can provoke potent immunogenic cell death, as evidenced by calreticulin exposure and the release of HMGB1 and ATP, thereby recruiting and promoting the maturation of DCs (Fig. 9B). The matured DCs subsequently initiate antigen presentation, activate CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, and upregulate the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ, ultimately eliciting antitumor immune responses and suppressing tumor metastasis [84].

In summary, metal ions (e.g., Mn2+, Zn2+, Cu2+, etc.) released during the degradation of MOFs can enhance immune responses synergistically by activating the cGAS-STING pathway or inducing ICD. Future research ought to prioritize enhancing the structural design and functionalization techniques of MOFs to promote their clinical application as immunometallic therapeutics, thus providing an innovative framework for combined cancer immunotherapy.

MOF-based combination therapies

Owing to the immunosuppressive nature of the TME and the lack of adequate T cell infiltration, the effective response rate of single checkpoint blockade immunotherapy (CBI) is only 20%–30%, and most patients develop secondary resistance [133,134]. Combining CBI with CDT, optical therapies (PTT/PDT)), SDT, and RThas been suggested as a strategy to address immune tolerance and improve antitumor immune responses. Recent advances in malignant tumor immunotherapy have revealed that multi-mechanism synergistic intervention strategies are preferred for cancer immunotherapy, as appropriate combinations can achieve better antitumor efficacy than monotherapies [135]. Utilization of MOF-derived nano-drug delivery systems in cancer immunotherapy integrated with multimodal approaches.

Combination with CDT

CDT has surfaced as an innovative approach for targeted cancer therapy, where its therapeutic mechanism entails the transformation of H2O2 into extremely harmful ·OH via Fenton-like reactions driven by metal ions (Fe2+, Cu2+, Mn2+etc.). This process consequently triggers apoptosis and necrosis in cancer cells [158,159]. However, the therapeutic efficacy of CDT is significantly compromised due to the low levels of H2O2 and the weakly acidic pH present in the TME. In recent years, researchers have successfully overcome the limitations of traditional CDT by designing multifunctional MOFs as NDDS, in combination with immunotherapy strategies, thereby opening new pathways for synergistic enhancement in tumor treatment.

Amid this wave of research, the synergistic therapeutic strategy leveraging cuproptosis and ICB is particularly striking. Multiple research teams have developed MOF-based NDDS to improve the effectiveness of immunotherapy. Lu et al. prepared a PEG-modified NDDS loaded with Elisarom (ES) (ES@Cu (II)-MOF), which possesses both peroxidase (POD) and GSH peroxidase (GSH-Px) like activities. This system can generate ·OH through Fenton reactions and convert Cu2+ into highly toxic Cu + to deplete intracellular GSH. The combined build-up of Cu+ and ES within tumor cells can trigger the clustering of DLAT protein and reduce the levels of Fe-S cluster proteins, ultimately resulting in cell death caused by copper. When this system is used in conjunction with aPD-L1, it significantly inhibits the growth of breast tumors in 4T1 tumor-bearing mice and improves survival rates (Fig. 10A). On this basis, cuproptosis further elicited potent ICD, promoting the abundant release of DAMPs.

These DAMPs subsequently recruited and activated APCs, thereby markedly enhancing tumor immunogenicity and initiating systemic antitumor immunity. This combination strategy not only promoted the infiltration of total CD3+ T cells, CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, NK cells, and B cells into tumor tissues, but also effectively reduced the proportion of immunosuppressive Tregs. Meanwhile, the combination treatment significantly upregulated the serum levels of proinflammatory cytokines, including IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-6, thereby achieving robust activation of antitumor immune responses (Fig. 10B-D) [136]. Hu et al. constructed a dual-responsive nanoplatform (PCu-HA-DQ) based on copper MOFs (PCu-MOFs) loaded with a disulfiram prodrug (DQ) and modified with a HA outer layer [137]. The PCu-HA-DQ specifically targets the CD-44 receptor, enriching in TNBC, guided by magnetic resonance imaging. High concentrations of phosphates and overexpressed H2O2 in tumor cells serve as inducers for the combined therapy; H2O2 reacts with DQ to release DTC, which then binds with Cu2+ to create Cu (DTC)2. This complex redefines the p97-NPL4-UFD1 signaling pathway and inducing apoptosis in tumor cells. Cu (DTC)2 and 1O2 contribute to an efficient ICD, constructing a ‘hot’ tumor and reshaping the TIME. When used alongside anti-PD-1 antibodies, the effectiveness of ICIs is improved, leading to a reduction in tumor growth. Li et al. developed HA-modified MOF materials FCS@LOX@HA (FCSLH), which achieve efficient loading of lactate oxidase (LOX) through the regulation of the Fe/Cu ratio [138]. This system exhibits a dual synergistic mechanism in the TME: on one hand, the CDT mediated by Fe/Cu ions and the LOX-catalyzed conversion of lactate into H2O2 create a cascading amplification effect; on the other hand, lactate metabolism inhibits the HIF-1α/SLC1A1 pathway, leading to LPO and the activation of ICD in cancer cells, while also reducing the polarization of M2-type TAMs through the NF-κB/HIF-1α signaling pathway. The combination with PD-L1 antibody significantly enhances the anti-tumor effect, revealing new insights into the synergistic treatment of metabolic-immunological dual pathways mediated by ferroptosis. Xu et al. utilized spiky MOFs (S@Cu-MOF) loaded with chlorophyll I (PPI) in combination with α PD-1 antibodies to attain anti-tumor effects through multiple mechanisms [139]. The S@Cu-MOF/PPI releases copper ions that interfere with mitochondrial activity, induce copper death, and trigger the cGAS/STING pathway. Consequently, this results in the initiation of ICD, supports the recruitment of DCs, and boosts T cell-driven anti-tumor immune responses, significantly inhibiting in vitro tumors and reducing lung metastasis. S@Cu-MOF/PPI is expected to serve as an α PD-1 sensitizer, activating the copper death mechanism and the cGAS/STING pathway to improve cancer efficacy.

Additionally, the Vk3@Co-Fc cascade nanoplatform developed by Wang et al. focus on CDT induced by H2O2. It is based on ferrocene (Fc, a catalyst for the Fenton reaction) and the MOF vitamin K3 (Vk3) to form the loaded cascade catalytic nanoplatform (Vk3@Co-Fc) [90]. Once released, Vk3 is processed by the highly expressed redox enzyme-1 (NQO1) in tumor cells, resulting in the production of significant amounts of H2O2, which triggers an excessive Fenton reaction, leading to ICD of the tumor. This process subsequently promotes the maturation of DCs, ultimately increasing T cell infiltration into the tumor site. Following the implementation of combined PD-1 blockade, the immune response associated with tumors in breast cancer is amplified, thereby reducing tumor metastasis to the lungs and improving survival rates of mice. It is worth noting that there are novel initiatives aimed at restructuring the TIME. Significantly, novel initiatives are underway to reshape the immune microenvironment associated with tumors. The NDDS R848@rMOF-MA@Gel was developed by modifying NH-MIL-88B through reduction and maleimide (MA) grafting to yield rMOF-MA. This modified framework was subsequently loaded with the immune adjuvant Resiquimod (R848) and encapsulated in a a hydrogel that adheres to tissue, which consists of human serum albumin (HSA) and ortho-phthalaldehyde-terminated 4-arm polyethylene glycol (4aPEG-OPA) [47]. When co-incubated with macrophages, rMOF-MA promotes an increase in intracellular ROS and iron accumulation via Fe2+ generation, GSH depletion, and endocytosis, which together facilitate the repolarization of macrophages from the M2 phenotype to the M1 phenotype. In conjunction with anti-PD-1 antibody treatment, this system markedly enhances antitumor efficacy, elicits a robust immune response, and effectively suppresses lung metastasis. Furthermore, it establishes long-term antitumor immune memory in mice that have undergone surgical intervention.

Collectively, these innovative studies demonstrate that MOF-based nanoplatforms, by skillfully integrating CDT with immunotherapeutic strategies, not only address the drawbacks of traditional CDT but also realize a collaborative antitumor impact via various mechanisms—inducing ICD, remodeling the TIME, and promoting immune cell activation. This provides novel insights and directions for future tumor therapy.

Combination with optical therapy

Phototherapy involves using particular wavelengths of light to stimulate biomolecules or materials for therapeutic purposes, including PDT and PTT. It offers advantages such as non-invasiveness, minimal side effects, and controllability in both time and space. Therefore, the development of NDDS capable of activating PDT or PTT activities is essential for achieving more precise cancer treatment. MOFs have become ideal carriers for phototherapeutic drugs because of their substantial specific surface area, adjustable pore architectures, and multifunctional modification capabilities.

A significant challenge in phototherapy is the attenuation of light in deep tissues and the presence of tumor hypoxia, which limits the generation of ROS. Recent MOF designs address these by optimizing light-harvesting properties and using hypoxia-activated mechanisms. Li et al. developed an innovative core-shell nanocomposite, TPZ/UCSs. This composite features PCN-224 as the outer layer and upconversion nanoparticles (UCNPs) at its core, which is infused with the hypoxia-activated prodrug Tirapazamine (TPZ) [160]. This system efficiently generates 1O2 for PDT by transferring energy to the porphyrin MOF through NIR light excitation of UCNPs; the TPZ is specifically activated within the hypoxic conditions of the TME, improving the effectiveness of chemotherapy. When combined with ICB therapy, both chemotherapy and PDT significantly promote the infiltration of cytotoxic T cells, effectively inhibiting systemic tumors. To optimize the photophysical properties of MOFs for enhanced therapeutic efficacy, Jing et al. prepared a spindle-shaped MOF material PCN-222-SO3H with enhanced NIR absorption properties via sulfonation modification [145]. The introduction of sulfonic acid groups facilitates the formation of strong intramolecular hydrogen bonds, triggering conformational distortion of the porphyrin ring, which significantly enhances the material's light-harvesting ability in the NIR region by narrowing the highest occupied molecular orbital-lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (HOMO-LUMO) energy gap. Compared to the original PCN-222, the optimized PCN-SU exhibits a stronger 1O2 generation efficiency and PDT effect. This material can efficiently trigger ICD in cancer cells and stimulate the maturation of DCs. When used in conjunction with anti-PD-1 ICB treatment, it can markedly increase the infiltration of CTLs at the tumor location under NIR light activation, resulting in in vivo anti-tumor activity. Wang et al. constructed a MOF-based nano-immunomodulator (CMZM). This modulator was prepared by partially replacing the 2-MI ligand in ZIF-8 with 4-(trifluoromethyl)-1H-imidazole (TFMI), doping with Mn2+, and finally coating with 4T1 cell membranes (Mem). CMZM exhibits multi-enzyme activities, including superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase, POD, and GSHOx. On the one hand, it generates O2 to alleviate hypoxia and promotes the polarization of M2 macrophages toward the M1 phenotype. On the other hand, it produces ROS and depletes GSH, exposing necrotic cell debris and reversing the immunosuppressive TME, thereby inducing dendritic cell maturation and CTL infiltration. In 4T1 mouse models, CMZM in combination with an anti-PD-L1 antibody inhibited tumor growth and enhanced T cell infiltration through the synergistic effects of CDT, PDT, and ICB [140].

MOFs can be engineered to disrupt tumor metabolism, making the cells more sensitive to thermal or oxidative stress. Wei et al. constructed a nano-immunoadjuvant based on MOFs (CuS/Z@M4T1). CuS/Z@M4T1 utilizes Zn2+-bonded ZIF-8 engineered with Cu nanoparticles and tumo cell membrane modifications to regulate metabolism and amplify NIR-II photothermal immunotherapy [146]. The photothermal effect in the NIR-II spectrum of CuS NDs, along with ZIF-8's responsiveness to acidic environments, swiftly triggers an excess accumulation of intracellular Zn2+ pools. This disruption affects the metabolic processes within 4T1 cells, which notably reduces the expression of heat shock proteins and improves the effectiveness of PTT. When combined with treatment using aPD-1 antibodies, this approach attracts a greater number of CTLs to the tumor site in mice, decreases the activity of regulatory T cells, efficiently killing the in situ tumor while also inhibiting distal tumors and lung metastasis. Alternatively, the physical structure of the MOF can be used to synchronize the release of immune adjuvants with light-triggered cell death. Xiong et al. developed a TME-activated biomimetic photosensitive nanoplatform, PEG2000-SiNcTI-Ph/CpG-ZIF-8@CM, for precise photodynamic immunotherapy of colon cancer. This platform was constructed using an in-house-developed NIR photosensitizer as the core therapeutic component. ZIF-8 was employed to co-load the photosensitizer and the CpG adjuvant, followed by coating with a PD-1/CD47 dual-functional cell membrane, enabling prolonged circulation, tumor targeting, and selective activation of the photodynamic effect only within the acidic and ATP-rich TME (Fig. 11AB). Upon 808 nm laser irradiation, the nanoplatform generated abundant ROS, leading to precise photodynamic tumor cell killing and potent induction of ICD. This process activated DCs, promoted CD8+ T-cell infiltration, and remodeled the immunosuppressive TEM (Fig. 11EF). When combined with PD-L1 blockade, the platform significantly inhibited both orthotopic and distant tumors, prevented lung metastasis, and established immune memory, thereby achieving highly effective photodynamic immunotherapy (Fig. 11CD) [141]. Similarly, Fan et al. constructed a multimodal diagnostic and therapeutic platform, ICG-CpG@MOF, centered around MIL101-NH2. By co-loading the photosensitizer ICG with the immune adjuvant CpG, this platform simultaneously achieves tumor precision diagnosis and treatment guided by multimodal imaging [142]. This nanosystem passively targets tumors through the EPR effect and generates synergistic PDT and PTT under 808 nm laser excitation, enabling synergistic tumor phototherapy. The ICG-CpG@MOF promotes the controlled release of immune adjuvants, regulated by GSH, into the TME. The released TAAs, along with CpG, have the potential to activate the immune response, facilitating a change in tumor cells from a cold state to a hot state. This transformation significantly boosts tumor cytotoxicity and results in high cure rates while minimizing side effects. Furthermore, another investigation developed a nanoplatform called Fe-THBQ/SR by embedding the STING agonist (SR-717) within an iron-tetrahydroxy-1,4-benzoquinone (Fe-THBQ) MOF [143]. As a photosensitizing agent, Fe-THBQ is capable of producing a significant amount of ROS when exposed to laser light. This leads to a reduction in the levels of heat shock proteins, thereby facilitating mild photothermal therapy (mild PTT) and triggering ferroptosis through the depletion of GSH and the action of GPX4. Additionally, Fe-THBQ/SR releases SR-717 when stimulated by GSH, and this agonist works in concert with ROS-mediated leakage of double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) to enhance STING activation. As a result, this mechanism promotes the normalization of tumor vasculature and reduces hypoxia, which in turn boosts the effectiveness of PDT. Concurrently, it heightens the immunogenic properties of tumor cells, restructures the TIME, and increases the infiltration of T lymphocytes, ultimately leading to better therapeutic outcomes. Ni et al. focused on the triple synergistic therapy of PTT, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy. They used MIL-100 as a carrier to load the chemotherapeutic drug mitoxantrone (MTO) and constructed MMH NPs via surface modification with HA. This approach utilizes the specific binding of HA to the CD44 receptors on tumor cell surfaces to achieve active targeting [144]. The dual stimulation of PTT and chemotherapy enhances ICD, promoting the activation of DCs. ICD and αOX40 reshape the TIME into an immunoactive TME. The combination of MMH NPs and αOX40 facilitates a synergistic treatment involving PTT, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy, improving therapeutic efficacy and eliciting a robust in vitro effect that suppresses tumor metastasis.

In conclusion, these MOF-based nanosystems have generally achieved deep synergy between phototherapy and immunotherapy. They not only enable efficient elimination of primary tumors while simultaneously preventing metastasis and recurrence of tumors by means of mechanisms such as inducing ICD and promoting DCs maturation. This provides diverse strategies for addressing therapeutic challenges including tumor heterogeneity and immunosuppressive microenvironment, and also lays a crucial foundation for the development of precise and low-toxicity comprehensive tumor therapeutic technologies in the future.

Combination with SDT

SDT induces sonochemical reactions through ultrasound activation of sonosensitizers, triggering multiple cytotoxic processes, including ROS production, cavitation effects, and localized thermal effects, thereby achieving non-invasive tumor treatment. SDT induces strong ICD, triggering the release of TAAs and DAMPs, which in turn promote the directional recruitment and activation of DCs, driving antigen-specific T cell immune responses [161,162]. Currently, commonly used organic sonosensitizers include porphyrin derivatives (such as TCPP), methylene blue, and indocyanine green. The utilization of MOFs, characterized by substantial specific surface area and high porosity, can prevent the self-quenching of organic sonosensitizers, promote the rapid diffusion of ROS, improve the delivery efficiency of sonosensitizers, and boost their concentration within tumors.

By targeting the mitochondria—the cell's metabolic core and a primary site for ICD initiation—researchers can amplify the pro-inflammatory impact of ultrasound. Luo et al. developed an innovative nano-delivery system known as Zr-TCPP(TPP)/R837@M, which is founded on triphenylphosphine (TPP) modified MOFs. This system utilizes MOFs as carriers to load the immunoadjuvant R837, and is coated with homologous tumor cell membranes, achieving a high drug loading capacity and mitochondrial targeting functionality [147]. The system not only enhances the SDT effect to promote tumor antigen release but also synergistically facilitates the maturation of DCs through the sustained release of the TLR7 agonist R837. Functionalized MOFs can be effectively taken up by homologous tumor cells, precisely targeting mitochondria, and inducing ICD under ultrasound stimulation, thereby activating DCs cells. When combined with CTLA-4 antibody therapy, this system can synergistically reverse the TIME, improving the effects of SDT while eliciting a strong anti-tumor immune response. This modality moves beyond simple ROS production, using the MOF's degradation products—specifically metal ions—to trigger complex physiological “overloads”. Tang et al. focused on the multi-mechanism synergistic design of MOF-based nanoreactors, and developed a MOF nanoreactor CaCuZC co-assembled with IR780 carbon dots (IR780 CDs) and calcium peroxide (CaO2) (Fig. 12A) This system generates H2O2 in situ through the decomposition of CaO2, activating Cu2+-mediated chemical kinetic therapy, while the overload of Ca2+ synergistically amplifies intracellular oxidative stress through the sonodynamic effect. The dual ions (Cu2+/Ca2+) collaboratively trigger a cascade of mitochondrial damage: ultrasound activation of IR780 CD produces ROS, exacerbating oxidative stress, Cu2+ overload induces the abnormal aggregation of DLAT leading to copper-induced cell death, while GSH depletion and LPO accumulation jointly drive ferroptosis (Fig. 12B). The CaCuZC nanosystem potently triggered ICD, emitting “eat-me” signals to effectively recruit APCs. Meanwhile, it significantly increased the proportion of CD80+CD86+ mature DCs in both tumors and tumor-draining lymph nodes, thereby establishing a critical foundation for the initiation of T-cell immunity. This system also markedly increased CD8+ cytotoxic T cells in tumor tissues and spleens, reduced the proportion of immunosuppressive Tregs, and concomitantly enhanced the infiltration of NK cells and B cells (Fig. 12D) [148]. In addition, the abundant secretion of the proinflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-2 converted “cold tumors” into “hot tumors.” By integrating the cross-regulation of copper death and ferroptosis with the effect of Ca2+ overload, this system innovatively achieves mitochondrial-targeted multimodal immunotherapy, significantly enhancing anti-tumor efficacy (Fig. 12C). For MOF-based nano-epigenetic therapy, Wang et al. prepared the targeted nano-sonosensitizer CRUPPA19 for the precision therapy of specific tumor types, which is applied in ultrasound-driven multi-stage immunotherapy for B-cell lymphoma [149]. The copper (Cu) complex of the FTO demethylation enzyme inhibitor Rhein was encapsulated in UiO-66-NH2 to synthesize the sonosensitizer CuR@UiO-66, which was further modified with mPEG-PO3 and anti-CD19 antibodies to achieve lymphoma tissue-specific targeting. CRUPPA19 is specifically internalized into B lymphoma cells through CD19 targeting, inducing ROS generation and apoptosis under ultrasound activation, and triggering autophagy-dependent release of Cu and Rhein, which increases protein lipidation and global mRNA methylation, leading to copper-induced cell death and transcriptional repression of PD-L1, respectively. The activation of systemic anti-tumor immunity occurs through the enhancement of DC maturation and the cross-activation of CD86 T cells. This significantly hampers the progression of both primary and metastatic lymphoma while also eliminating bone marrow infiltration lesions, showcasing the benefits of a multi-stage synergistic therapeutic approach. The most sophisticated platforms represent a highly integrated approach where the physical structure of the MOF (such as a heterojunction) and its ionic components (like manganese) work in a feedback loop with the body's own immune signaling. Yang et al. developed an in situ Z-type heterojunction nanoplatform MTO (MnxOy/(A/R)TiO2), innovatively integrating SDT/CDT/immunotherapy strategies [150]. The synergistic effect of MTO enhances therapeutic efficacy: the heterojunction and MnxOy components respectively drive SDT and CDT, collaboratively inducing ICD; GSH depletion reverses the immunosuppressive microenvironment, while the released tumor antigens and Mn2+ activate the DC -T cell immune axis via the STING pathway; the IFN-γ secreted by T cells further enhances the Mn3+/Mn2+ cycle, triggering a ferroptosis cascade through a LPO storm. After combined PD-L1 blockade, this platform exhibits vaccine-like functions, synergistically inhibiting primary and metastatic tumors through the tri-modal approach of SDT/CDT/immunotherapy and establishing long-term immune memory.

In summary, through the construction of multifunctional MOF-based nanoplatforms, these studies have gradually deepened the synergistic mechanism between SDT and immunotherapy. This not only significantly enhances the antitumor efficacy but also provides an important direction for precision-targeted combined therapeutic strategies.

Combination with RT

RT is a common method in clinical tumor treatment, and its mechanism of action primarily relies on the generation of ROS induced by high-energy ionizing radiation and subsequent DNA damage. However, the current clinical application of RT faces limitations: it insufficiently damages tumor DNA while causing harmful effects on normal tissues [163]. RT can serve as an adjunctive treatment for immune modulation. The use of MOFs can enhance the production of a large quantity of ROS, while also sensitizing RT-induced DNA damage [151].

MOFs can co-deliver multiple immune adjuvants for macrophage-targeted therapy, thereby inducing systemic immune responses and achieving anti-tumor effects. Ni et al. constructed a multifunctional nanosystem, IMD@Hf-DBP/αCD47, via sequential surface modification, loading of the TLR-7 agonist IMD, and adsorption of the αCD47 antibody [151]. Upon X-ray irradiation, this system exerts a strong RT-RDT effect, inducing ICD of tumor cells and synergistically enhancing the immunomodulatory functions of TLR-7 agonism and CD47 blockade. Further studies demonstrated that combination with anti-PD-L1 therapy could completely eliminate both primary and metastatic tumors in a bilateral colorectal cancer model, exhibiting a systemic immune activation effect. Another important direction is to reshape the TIME, especially by modulating macrophage polarization and DC activation. In terms of regulating the TIME, Zhang et al. investigated petal-like MOFs loaded with R848 [152]. R848@petal-like MOFs efficiently promote the repolarization of TAMs from the M2 phenotype to M1, as well as the maturation of DCs, significantly enhancing the therapeutic efficacy RT by triggering antitumor responses. The combination of R848@petal-like MOFs, RT, and αPD-L1 elicits potent in vitro effects, effectively inhibiting tumor metastasis. MOFs can not only act as efficient radiosensitizers to significantly enhance DNA damage induced by RT, but also effectively load STING agonists by virtue of their porous properties or coordination capabilities, thereby synergistically boosting the antitumor immune response [153]. Luo et al. constructed a STING agonist GA-conjugated nanoradiosensitizer (GA-MOF), achieving tumor immune remodeling via dual mechanisms of synergistic radiosensitization and STING pathway activation [153]. This system forms artificial leukocyte-like immune nodules that dynamically transform into immune activation hotspots under RT, triggering a robust antitumor immune response. When used in conjunction with ICB therapy, it activates systemic immunity and suppresses distant tumors. Similarly, other STING-activating MOF platforms have been designed to combine high-Z element-mediated radiosensitization with biochemical immune activation, enabling effective therapy even under low-dose irradiation. Wang et al. studied a bifunctional MOF (MOF/MSA-2) based on Hf6 secondary building units (SBUs) and six (4′-carboxy[1,1′-biphenyl]-4-yl) phenyl bridging ligands, which integrates radiation sensitization and immune regulation to achieve efficient RT and immunotherapy [154]. The MOF/MSA-2 enhances the effects of X-ray RT due to its high Z characteristics; it induces strong STING activation and effective tumor regression when combined with low-dose X-ray irradiation. Furthermore, in conjunction with αPD-L1, it triggers systemic anti-tumor immunity, simultaneously inhibiting both the primary tumor and distant metastases. MOF-based radioimmunotherapy has advanced from simple drug loading toward precise material engineering. By modulating the metal composition and electronic structure of MOFs, researchers can further improve X-ray energy deposition, ROS generation, and RT-RDT synergy. Li et al. developed a Ta/Zr co-doped nano-MOF (TZM) using TCPP as a photosensitive ligand, integrating both radiosensitization and immune activation functions. The three-dimensional heavy-metal porous structure of TZM efficiently absorbs X-ray energy and promotes hydroxyl radical generation. Ta-Zr co-doping narrows the highest occupied molecular orbital-lowest unoccupied molecular orbital HOMO-LUMO energy gap, facilitating excited-state energy transfer to TCPP and significantly enhancing 1O2 generation efficiency for synergistic RDT (Fig. 13A). TZM induces ICD through RT-RDT synergy, activates the cGAS-STING pathway to promote DCs maturation and pro-inflammatory cytokine release, and upregulates PD-L1 expression to enhance the efficacy of ICIs, ultimately leading to systemic activation of CD8+ T-cell immunity and suppression of osteosarcoma growth and metastasis (Fig. 13BC) [155].

In summary, these studies have deeply integrated radiosensitization and immune regulation strategies through multifunctional MOF platforms, achieving a leap from local RT to systemic immune activation. Their common mechanisms include the induction of ICD, activation of innate immune signaling pathways (e.g., STING), remodeling of the immune microenvironment, and the generation of a synergistic effect with ICB—providing promising design concepts and experimental evidence for the next generation of radioimmunotherapy combinations.

Combination with other therapies

Cancer cells require substantial energy to sustain their uncontrolled proliferation; thus, glucose-depletion-based starvation therapy has emerged as an effective approach for suppressing tumor growth. This strategy can either disrupt the tumor energy supply using vascular-disrupting agents or induce cellular starvation through rapid intracellular glucose consumption mediated by Gox. Based on this glucose-depletion strategy, Luo et al. developed a novel metabolic nanomodulator, D/B/CQ@ZIF-8@CS, by loading ZIF-8 with the glycolysis inhibitor 2-deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG), the glucose transporter 1 inhibitor BAY-876, and the autophagy inhibitor chloroquine [156] (Fig. 14). BAY-876 inhibits GLUT1-mediated glucose uptake, while 2-DG disrupts the glycolytic process, achieving a dual blockade of energy supply in tumor cells, and CQ inhibits autophagy. Furthermore, blocking both the uptake of glucose and aerobic glycolysis successfully counteracts the immunosuppressive TME characterized by high levels of lactate and low glucose, leading to a decrease in regulatory T cell function and improving the effectiveness of anti-CTLA-4 immunotherapy. This starvation-immunity dual-dimensional therapy strategy offers new insights for overcoming tumor metabolic heterogeneity.

Chen et al. designed a biomimetic gallium-based MOF (Ga-MOF) with in-situ encapsulation of the thymidylate synthase inhibitor 5-fluorouracil (5-FU). This framework is functionalized with an outer layer of a red blood cell-platelet fusion membrane (RPM), resulting in the composite 5-FU@GaMOF@RPM (5-FUGR) [157]. This composite serves as an efficient inducer of ICD for enhanced MW immunotherapy. The 5-FUGR achieves prolonged circulation and tumor-targeted accumulation through the RPM biomimetic membrane. The released Ga3+ ions synergize with MW to disrupt the balance of intracellular ions, resulting in significant oxidative stress and retention of calcium within the cells, which in turn promotes tumor cell apoptosis. The combined therapy of 5-FUGR and MW completely eradicates primary 4T1 tumors, induces effective ICD, primes the immune response, and establishes memory effects, while also suppressing tumor metastasis and recurrence.

Based on the above studies, MOF-based NDDS have constructed a new paradigm of multi-modal synergistic therapy by efficiently integrating immunotherapy with various therapeutic modalities, including CDT, PTT/PDT, SDT, and RT. While efficiently inhibiting primary and metastatic tumors, these systems effectively induce ICD and activate specific T-cell immune responses, thereby establishing long-term anti-tumor immune memory. By precisely regulating the TME, improving antigen presentation, and synergizing with ICB, the systems significantly improve the specificity of therapy and systemic anti-tumor efficacy, providing a robust theoretical and experimental foundation for the development of next-generation highly effective and low-toxicity tumor immunotherapy combination strategies.

Perspectives: challenges in cancer immunotherapy and the application prospects of MOF-based NDDS

Core challenges in cancer immunotherapy

Although immunotherapy has fundamentally revolutionized the landscape of cancer treatment, its clinical translation remains hindered by multifactorial constraints, thereby limiting the overall therapeutic efficacy and narrowing the spectrum of cohorts that benefit from it. These core challenges can be categorized as follows:

Limited objective response rates and “cold” tumor phenotypes

A limited objective response rate represents one of the most prominent bottlenecks in current immunotherapeutic regimens. Owing to insufficient immunogenicity, low tumor mutational burden, or inefficient antigen presentation, numerous solid tumors exhibit characteristic “cold tumor” phenotypes, rendering them largely refractory to ICB therapies [164]. Blockades can occur at multiple stages of the cancer-immunity cycle—including antigen generation, cross-presentation, DCs maturation, T-cell activation, and subsequent tumor infiltration—all of which severely undermine the elicitation of robust antitumor immune responses.

IrAEs and systemic toxicity

Furthermore, irAEs pose a critical limitation to broad clinical applications. Conventional systemic administration lacks tissue- and cell-specific selectivity, leading to the non-specific biodistribution of immunotherapeutic agents in healthy tissues. This off-target accumulation often triggers systemic immune hyperactivation, autoimmune-like inflammatory cascades, and multiorgan toxicities [165]. Consequently, striking an optimal balance between potentiating antitumor immunity and mitigating systemic toxicities remains a central prerequisite for optimizing immunotherapeutic paradigms.

Profound intra- and intertumoral heterogeneity

In addition, profound intratumoral and intertumoral heterogeneity further exacerbates the complexity of treatment. Pronounced discrepancies exist across diverse tumor histological subtypes, individual patients, and even distinct spatiotemporal regions within a single solid tumor. These variations manifest in antigen expression profiles, immune checkpoint levels, immune cell infiltration gradients, and metabolic phenotypes [166]. This heterogeneity renders “one-size-fits-all” monotherapies largely ineffective, thereby emphasizing an urgent need for the development of personalized and precision immunotherapeutic strategies.

The highly immunosuppressive TME

Concurrently, the highly immunosuppressive TME severely abrogates antitumor immune responses. The TME is typically enriched with immunosuppressive components, including Tregs, MDSCs, TAMs, and various inhibitory cytokines. These cellular and molecular barriers are further compounded by adverse physicochemical hallmarks such as hypoxia, acidosis, aberrant vasculature, and severe metabolic competition for nutrients [85]. Synergistically, these interconnected factors impair antigen presentation, impede intratumoral T-cell infiltration, downregulate effector cell functions, and ultimately facilitate tumor immune evasion. Thus, remodeling the immunosuppressive TME and triggering ICD within tumor tissues represent pivotal directions for rescuing and enhancing immunotherapeutic efficacy.

Inefficient drug delivery and formulation vulnerabilities

Inefficient drug delivery persists as the fundamental technological bottleneck in contemporary immunotherapy. A myriad of immunotherapeutic agents—ranging from TAAs and neoantigens to adjuvants, nucleic acid-based agonists, monoclonal antibodies, and cytokines—suffer from intrinsic physicochemical vulnerabilities [167]. These include poor in vivo stability, short circulation half-lives, susceptibility to enzymatic degradation, suboptimal targeted tropism, and uncontrollable biodistribution profiles. Conventional administration platforms frequently fail to achieve effective accumulation of therapeutics within target sites (e.g., tumor parenchyma, draining lymph nodes, or defined immune cell populations). This necessitates the administration of higher therapeutic doses and increased dosing frequencies, which inevitably exacerbate off-target toxicities and limit clinical compliance.

Application prospects of MOF-based NDDS

To circumvent the aforementioned hurdles, MOF-based NDDS emerge as a highly promising solution for overcoming current immunotherapeutic bottlenecks. In comparison to traditional nanomaterials, MOFs offer distinctive advantages, including atomic-scale structural precision, modular designability, tunable pore sizes and porosity, exceptional surface areas, superior drug loading capacities, facile surface functionalization, and inherent biodegradability. These attributes enable them to serve as versatile, multifunctional platforms for the efficient encapsulation, protection, targeted delivery, and spatiotemporally controlled release of immunotherapeutic agents.

Enhancing tumor immunogenicity

MOFs function as highly efficient platforms for tumor vaccine delivery. Their hierarchical porous architecture allows for the co-encapsulation of tumor antigens, neoantigens, and immunological adjuvants. Furthermore, strategic surface modifications facilitate lymph node homing or specific APC targeting, thereby promoting antigen uptake, cross-presentation, DCs maturation, and T-cell activation. Such mechanisms orchestrate the vigorous conversion of “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors, effectively amplifying tumor immunogenicity and eliciting robust, specific antitumor immune responses.

Mitigating irAEs

MOFs allow for the rational modulation of metal nodes, organic linkers, particle size, and surface chemistry. These optimizations refine in vivo circulation kinetics and biodistribution, ensuring preferential enrichment within the tumor parenchyma, draining lymph nodes, or target immune cell populations, thereby minimizing exposure to healthy tissues and attenuating systemic toxicity. Furthermore, the capacity of MOFs for preferential accumulation at target sites permits a reduction in dosing frequency and therapeutic concentrations, further lowering the risk of off-target side effects.

Addressing tumor heterogeneity

The high degree of designability inherent in MOFs allows for personalized customization tailored to specific malignancies and patient-specific molecular profiles. For instance, the intrinsic immunogenicity of particular metal ions and linkers can be harnessed during localized administration to augment therapeutic outcomes; conversely, these components can be screened out for systemic delivery to evade clearance by the RES. By meticulously selecting metal-ligand combinations with innate immuno-activating functions, individualized MOF-based immunotherapeutic regimens can be developed for specific cancer subtypes.

Modulation of the TME

MOFs can be engineered to co-deliver ICIs, metabolic modulators, chemotherapeutics, photosensitizers, photothermal agents, sonosensitizers, or radiosensitizers. This integrated approach triggers ICD, promotes the subsequent release of tumor antigens, enhances effector immune cell infiltration, and effectively reverses the immunosuppressive milieu. Beyond acting as passive carriers, MOFs can serve as active immunological adjuvants to directly reshape the TME, facilitating antigen presentation to T cells and dismantling immune suppression while simultaneously priming the initiate immune response.

Optimizing drug delivery efficiency

MOFs demonstrate significant superiorities in nanomedicine. Their well-defined crystalline structures and high drug-loading efficiency are instrumental for rigorous consistency assessment and quality control management—critical requirements for clinical translation that are often unattainable by other nanomaterials. By incorporating “stealth” coatings for prolonged circulation, tumor-homing ligands, biomimetic membrane camouflage, and stimuli-responsive designs (e.g., pH, redox, enzyme, light, ultrasound, or radiation-responsive), MOFs achieve precise, on-demand drug release, significantly elevating local therapeutic concentrations while sparing normal physiological compartments.

Multimodal synergistic therapy

MOFs are uniquely suited for the construction of integrated, cooperative therapeutic platforms. Their highly tunable frameworks allow for the simultaneous integration of diverse therapeutic modalities—such as chemotherapy, PDT, PTT, and RT—with immunotherapy within a single nanoplatform. This “theranostic” integration enables multifaceted intervention across the entire cancer-immunity cycle, providing a more potent induction of tumor immunogenicity, improving overall therapeutic response rates, and counteracting potential drug resistance or immune evasion.

Future directions and key scientific challenges

Notwithstanding the significant potential of MOF -based NDDS, their application in cancer immunotherapy remains in its nascent stages. Future research must prioritize addressing the following critical scientific and technical challenges:

Elucidating fundamental immunological mechanisms

Current research predominantly focuses on utilizing MOFs as passive carriers for immunotherapeutic molecules. However, the active role and specific mechanisms of MOFs throughout the entire cancer-immunity cycle—including antigen generation, cross-presentation, lymphatic transport, APC maturation, and T-cell activation—remain under-explored. Future studies should expand their scope beyond T cells and macrophages to encompass B cells, DCs, and the broader systemic immune network, thereby establishing a holistic understanding of MOF-mediated interactions at each stage of the immune response.

Optimizing rational design for combinatorial therapy

While MOFs facilitate the integration of diverse therapeutic modalities, haphazard combinations do not necessarily result in true synergy and may even trigger drug antagonism or deleterious hyper-inflammation. Given the complexity of the manufacturing process and potential pharmacological interference in vivo, empirical combinations must be avoided. Instead, synergistic systems should be rationally designed based on clear immunological rationales. This requires precise determination of drug ratios, release sequences, and dosing intervals to achieve a “1 + 1 > 2” effect while mitigating the risk of acute systemic immune toxicity.

Maintaining structural integrity and biological activity

Coordinative interactions between metal ions and immunotherapeutic payloads (e.g., antigenic peptides, PD-1 antibodies, and nucleotide agonists) may undermine the fundamental assembly of the MOF, compromising its structural integrity. Furthermore, such coordination and the subsequent release of payloads may alter their spatial conformation, leading to diminished bioactivity. Strategy should shift toward the rational design of immunotherapeutic agents as ligands that directly coordinate with metal nodes to form the framework. Simultaneously, a delicate balance between physiological stability and controlled biodegradability must be achieved to ensure structural persistence during circulation while preventing long-term endogenous accumulation through on-demand degradation within the TME.

Diversifying immunomodulatory payloads

To date, MOF-encapsulated immunomodulators have been largely restricted to small-molecule drugs and antigens. Research into the delivery of biomacromolecules with potent immunomodulatory functions, such as siRNA, aptamers, and cytokines, remains limited. Future efforts should focus on developing MOF platforms capable of delivering diverse nucleic acid and protein-based therapeutics to further enrich the repertoire of available immunotherapeutic strategies.

Developing stimuli-responsive and “smart” nano-platforms

As intelligent biomaterials, stimuli-responsive functionalized MOFs can significantly enhance delivery efficiency while minimizing systemic toxicity and drug wastage. The rational incorporation of moieties responsive to internal or external stimuli—enabling sequential, cascade, or intermittent release from multi-layered or yolk-shell MOF architectures—is essential. Such biomimetic strategies for spatiotemporally programmed drug delivery aligned with complex physiological processes deserve profound investigation.

Strengthening biocompatibility and comprehensive toxicological assessment

While some MOFs demonstrate favorable biodegradability, their long-term toxicity, immunogenicity, and systemic toxicity require further evaluation [168,169]. The biosafety of MOFs remains a paramount concern, particularly regarding the potential toxicity of metal ions and organic ligands that may accumulate in the liver and spleen. Given the significant toxicity variations across different metal bases (e.g., Fe3+/Zn2+/Zr4+-based MOFs exhibit lower toxicity compared to Cu2+/Mn2+-based ones), priority should be given to highly biocompatible building blocks or endogenous organic linkers and bioactive coatings. Moreover, while cytotoxicity is frequently studied, more attention must be paid to genotoxicity, which is critical for long-term clinical safety. Toxicological evaluations should transcend in vitro assays and simple animal models, moving toward human-mimetic models to investigate metabolic pathways, excretion mechanisms, and long-term systemic impact.

Furthermore, establishing a biosafety assessment framework for MOFs in compliance with ISO 10993 standards is of paramount importance. Under the guidance of this framework, the immunotoxicity of MOF-based NDDS should be systematically evaluated, encompassing key endpoints such as hemocompatibility, irritation and sensitization, systemic toxicity and pyrogenicity, genotoxicity, and toxicokinetics. For immunoregulatory nanoplatforms, mechanistic immunotoxicity assessments should also be incorporated, including cytokine release profiling, complement activation, immune cell phenotyping, and the evaluation of unintended immune activation or immunosuppression.

Ensuring reproducibility and scalable production

Precision surface engineering and scalable synthesis are core prerequisites for the industrialization and clinical translation of targeted MOFs. It is imperative to optimize synthetic protocols to enhance loading efficiency and structural stability. Robust, controllable surface modification methods must be developed to eliminate batch-to-batch variations, providing a reliable material foundation for clinical use. The precise structural definition and high loading capacity of MOFs offer peerless advantages for consistency assessment and quality control in clinical settings.

Implementing multi-modal theranostic platforms

The integration of imaging and tracing capabilities within immunomodulatory MOFs offers vast prospects for concurrent diagnosis and treatment. Future MOF platforms should feature multi-modal imaging (e.g., MRI, fluorescence, or CT) to facilitate real-time monitoring of drug distribution and therapeutic assessment, thereby advancing the paradigm of precision immunotherapy.

Bridging the translational gap

Limitations of Current Preclinical Models A fundamental limitation in the current landscape of MOF-based immunotherapy research is the overwhelming reliance on rapidly growing, subcutaneous murine tumor models (e.g., B16F10, 4T1). These simplistic models fail to accurately recapitulate the complex, chronically established immunosuppressive stroma, profound heterogeneity, and sophisticated immune evasion mechanisms characteristic of authentic human solid tumors. Consequently, while these preclinical studies provide crucial preliminary proof-of-concept evidence, extrapolating these findings directly to clinical success remains highly premature. Moving forward, the safety and efficacy of these highly engineered MOF systems in humanized mouse models or large animals, as well as their ultimate clinical translation potential, require rigorous and systematic evaluation.

Notably, RiMO-301, a MOF based on hafnium, has demonstrated combined effects of radiosensitization and ICIs in preclinical studies, without any observable systemic toxicity. This drug has emerged as the first MOF-based candidate to advance to human clinical phase I (NCT03444714), greatly increasing the confidence of researchers engaged in MOF-based NDDS [170].

In summary, cancer immunotherapy continues to face formidable challenges, including suboptimal response rates, irAEs, tumor heterogeneity, an immunosuppressive microenvironment, and inefficient drug delivery. MOF drug delivery systems, characterized by their high designability, superior loading capacities, stimuli-responsive release, and multi-functional integration, are poised to play a transformative role in enhancing tumor immunogenicity, optimizing targeting, and remodeling the TME.

Conclusion

Functionalized MOFs exhibit notable benefits in the area of immunotherapy for NDDS. Leveraging their highly tunable structure, expansive specific surface area, and precise porous architecture, MOFs facilitate efficient loading and targeted delivery of immunotherapeutic agents, such as ICIs, tumor antigens, nucleic acid drugs, and cancer vaccines. Additionally, stimuli-responsive release strategies greatly improve the localized accumulation of drugs within the TME, while also reducing systemic toxicity. MOFs also function as effective platforms for multimodal immune combination therapies. Furthermore, through the development of bio-based ligands, intelligent targeting designs, and the integration of interdisciplinary technologies, MOFs are poised to advance personalized immunotherapy toward greater precision, efficiency, and reduced toxicity. These advancements offer novel therapeutic strategies for tumor immunotherapy, expedite clinical translation, and address the challenges faced in cancer immunotherapy.
