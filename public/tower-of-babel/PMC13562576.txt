GinkgoSense-Net: A method for characterization and real-time detection of ginkgo fruits in complex field environments
Authors: Dongwei Yan, Guozhen Chen, Yiqi Wang, Xiaodong Liu, Jianbo Wang, Yaxu Xue, He Zhang, Shaowei Lan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAutomatic picking of ginkgo fruits is beneficial for prolonging their freshness and ensuring quality during storage. Accurate identification of ginkgo fruits is a critical prerequisite for enabling stable and reliable operation of the end-effector during the harvesting process. However, due to the similarities in color and size between ginkgo fruits and leaves, traditional recognition methods are prone to being affected by variations in lighting, occlusion, and background interference. To solve these challenges, this paper presents an accurate identification method for ginkgo fruits based on GinkgoSense-Net. Specifically, EMA and SAF are integrated into the YOLOv11n framework, and the bounding box regression function is replaced with EIoU. At the same time, methods such as Grad-CAM visualization, ablation experiments, and comparative experiments are used to validate the efficacy of GinkgoSense-Net. The experimental results demonstrate that GinkgoSense-Net has a precision of 95.0%, a recall of 92.1%, and an mAP of 93.6% in the detection of ginkgo fruits, and its real-time performance reaches 74.9. These findings indicate that the proposed model achieves an effective balance among detection accuracy, computational efficiency, and model complexity. Introduction

Ginkgo biloba L., as one of the oldest extant relict plants, possesses not only high ecological and ornamental value, but its fruit is also an important agroforestry product with concurrent medicinal, edible, and economic value1. Consequently, stable ginkgo production is crucial for safeguarding human health and ensuring the sound development of the agricultural economy. With the rapid advancement of smart agriculture technologies, the efficient harvesting of ginkgo fruits has become a crucial part of the industrial chain. However, traditional ginkgo fruit harvesting mainly depends on manual experience, which makes it difficult to achieve large-scale and standardized operations2. Therefore, the automatic picking technology of robots has increasingly emerged as a significant alternative to manual labor. Among them, the recognition of ginkgo fruits is the key link to realizing robot automatic picking. However, ginkgo fruit trees are usually tall, with a wide range of fruit distribution and uneven density, and their color is very similar to that of leaves during harvest, which brings great challenges to the identification of ginkgo fruit3. Therefore, the development of an automated, accurate, and real-time ginkgo fruit recognition technology holds significant application value for automated harvesting, precise yield estimation, and growth status monitoring. Moreover, it can provide valuable technical support and reference for research in smart agriculture.

Some crop identification techniques have been established, providing essential support for the advancement of precision agriculture. These methods mainly encompass conventional image processing techniques4 and recognition approaches based on physical signals5,6. Traditional image processing methods usually rely on appearance features and threshold segmentation. This method can achieve better results under ideal conditions, such as a simple background and constant illumination. However, for natural scenes such as ginkgo fruit, which have variable scales, different shapes and are often mixed with complex backgrounds, its generalization ability and robustness are seriously insufficient7. In addition, physical signal-based recognition techniques such as near-infrared spectroscopy or hyperspectral imaging can provide rich internal component information, but their equipment costs are high, the data acquisition process is complex, and it is difficult to perform real-time detection without belonging to mobile platforms8. This greatly limits its large-scale application in practical scenarios. In recent years, researchers have developed deep learning methods based on machine vision, showing unparalleled superiority. It can automatically extract multi-level and high-dimensional image features from massive labeled data through end-to-end learning. It has strong robustness and adaptability in scenes such as illumination changes, scale scaling, partial occlusion, and background interference9. At the same time, the deep learning model can reason within extremely short time intervals, which fully meets the strict standards of real-time detection applications in terms of timeliness. Therefore, the introduction of machine vision based on deep learning into the real-time recognition of ginkgo fruit is the most promising choice to achieve accurate, efficient and automated operations.

In recent years, numerous studies have investigated object recognition and identification to achieve precise localization of crop fruits. Zhao et al.10, He et al.11, and Zhang et al.12 have demonstrated that, by enhancing the feature extraction capability and structural design of deep learning networks, high-accuracy and robust recognition of fruits such as apples, oranges, and mangoes can be achieved under complex natural conditions, thereby meeting the requirements of real-world operational scenarios. Overall, the field of agricultural vision has gradually established a general paradigm for object detection in recent years. This paradigm emphasizes the incorporation of attention mechanisms to strengthen feature representation, the adoption of multi-scale feature fusion architectures to improve the perception of small targets, and the optimization of bounding box regression losses to enhance localization performance13,14. In particular, within studies on improvements to the YOLO series models, researchers commonly embed channel attention, spatial attention, or hybrid attention mechanisms into the Backbone or Neck components to reinforce feature responses while suppressing background interference15. For instance, prior studies have introduced SE, CBAM, and their improved variants into models such as YOLOv5 and YOLOv8, significantly enhancing both the accuracy and robustness of tasks including crop-weed discrimination and disease detection16,17. Although the aforementioned studies provide valuable references for agricultural visual perception tasks, they have primarily focused on typical commercial crops such as apples, oranges, and mangoes. In contrast, research on the automatic recognition of ginkgo fruits remains relatively limited. Therefore, conducting studies on ginkgo fruit recognition in complex natural environments is of great significance for expanding the application scope of agricultural visual perception technologies to specialty economic forest crops. However, the task of ginkgo fruit recognition presents more substantial challenges. The color of ginkgo fruits is highly similar to that of surrounding leaves, making it difficult for traditional color space-based methods to effectively extract fruit regions. Meanwhile, individual ginkgo fruits are relatively small and occupy only a limited number of pixels in images. Their subtle morphological features are easily affected by multiple factors, including imaging noise, illumination variations, and resolution constraints, which consequently weakens the model’s feature representation capability18. In addition, ginkgo fruits typically grow in clusters, and occlusions among fruits, pedicels, and leaves often cause them to adhere to one another in images. Therefore, it is imperative to develop a robust and interference-resistant recognition method capable of accurately identifying ginkgo fruits in complex environments, thereby providing a critical technical foundation for automated harvesting and intelligent management of ginkgo orchards.

In view of the above challenges, this study focuses on ginkgo fruits in complex orchard environments, addressing typical agricultural scenarios characterized by significant illumination variation and severe occlusion. By collaboratively optimizing the attention mechanism, feature fusion strategy, and bounding box regression function, a target detection framework better suited for ginkgo fruit recognition is developed. The main contributions and innovations of this study are as follows:

(1) Establish a ginkgo fruit image data set that covers different lighting conditions, different shooting angles, and different background complexity.

(2) The feasibility of the convolutional neural network model for recognizing ginkgo fruits in complex scenarios was validated.

(3) Through systematic ablation experiments and comparative experiments, GinkgoSense-Net is compared with the original YOLOv11n and various mainstream target detection models on the self-constructed dataset. The effectiveness of each proposed improvement module was systematically analyzed, and the overall performance of the model was comprehensively validated.

(4) The GinkgoSense-Net model was evaluated across diverse scenarios to qualitatively and quantitatively assess its discriminative capability and practical application potential.

Data acquisition

Image acquisition

In this study, a ginkgo fruit image dataset covering multi-scene and multi-illumination conditions was constructed to meet the demand for automatic picking of ginkgo fruit. The image acquisition work was carried out in Baihe Town, Song County, Luoyang City, Henan Province, China (see Fig. 2), and the acquisition time was from August to October 2025. All images were acquired under complex, multi-view natural conditions, encompassing, but not limited to, variations in individual trees, solar elevation angles, illumination intensity, and degrees of visual similarity. This design aims to realistically capture the impact of illumination variability on visual recognition systems in real-world operational environments. The acquisition device uses a high-resolution camera, and the output image size is 3024 × 4032 pixels. The camera lens and the vertical plane form various shooting angles, such as 0°, 30°, 90° and random angles, respectively, and the shooting distance is 20–30 cm to obtain the ginkgo fruit image with clear structure and details. In addition, in order to deal with various complex scenes that may be encountered in the actual picking process, this study obtained images from six different scenes, such as normal lighting conditions, branch occlusion, and leaf occlusion. Finally, a total of 1550 images of ginkgo fruits were collected, as illustrated in Fig. 3.

Data augmentation

Data augmentation19 is an important preprocessing strategy, which enhances the adaptability of the model to illumination changes, target pose diversity and background interference in complex natural environments by artificially expanding the diversity and scale of training samples. In this study, we performed image processing and data augmentation on PyCharm 2021 software (JetBrains, Prague, The Czech Republic), including changing brightness, adding noise, horizontal mirroring, vertical mirroring, rotating at any angle, rotating 180°, image translation and image cropping, to fully cover various forms and conditions that may occur in the actual environment of ginkgo fruit, as shown in Fig. 4. All data augmentation operations were applied exclusively to the training set, while 620 original images were retained as the validation and test sets to objectively evaluate the model’s ability to process real-world information. Through data augmentation, the 930 training images were expanded to 2,480, resulting in a total of 3,100 ginkgo fruit images. Finally, the dataset was divided into training, validation, and test sets in an 8:1:1 ratio, comprising 2,480, 310, and 310 images, respectively.

Dataset establishment

In this study, the image annotation tool LabelImg (https://github.com/tzutalin/labelImg) on the GitHub open-source platform was used to annotate the ginkgo fruit image dataset. After running the LabelImg script, the ginkgo fruit in each input image is labeled one by one, and the label category is Ginkgo. After labeling, an XML format annotation file corresponding to each image is generated. The file includes the categorical characteristics of each ginkgo fruit in the image, along with their corresponding spatial coordinates, as illustrated in Fig. 5. At the same time, each ginkgo fruit is represented by a rectangular bounding box, where the parameters xmin and ymin represent the coordinate position of the top left corner vertex of the rectangular box in the image pixel coordinate system, while xmax and ymax correspond to the position coordinates of the lower right corner vertex. It is worth noting that image enhancement does not change the spatial distribution and relative position relationship of ginkgo fruit in the original image, but through the reconstruction of image pixel space and set conversion, the position coordinate information of ginkgo fruit in the XML file can be changed.

GinkgoSense-Net network structure

Construction of GinkgoSense-Net model

The YOLO series of algorithms effectively extract multi-scale object features by stacking multiple convolutional layers and incorporating various data augmentation strategies, achieving a favorable balance between detection accuracy and inference speed20,21. As a lightweight variant of this series, YOLOv1122 (https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics) further enhances high-level semantic representation and multi-scale modeling capabilities while maintaining low computational complexity. Considering the requirements for real-time ginkgo fruit recognition in terms of inference speed and deployment efficiency, this study adopts YOLOv11n as the foundational network framework23. Specifically, YOLOv11n comprises four main components: Input, Backbone, Neck, and Detection head. The Input module resizes input images of varying resolutions to a unified size and applies Mosaic data augmentation, which randomly combines four training images. The Backbone utilizes a lightweight CSPDarknet architecture coupled with a Focus module. CSPDarknet performs preliminary feature mapping of the input images, while Focus downsamples the input without information loss. The Neck module propagates high-level semantic information to lower-level features in a top-down manner. The Detection head employs a decoupled design to separate classification and regression tasks. In the context of ginkgo fruit recognition, environmental factors can significantly interfere with feature extraction, potentially causing information loss or confusion in conventional models during feature extraction and fusion stages. To address this, the proposed GinkgoSense-Net integrates YOLOv11n modules into both the Backbone and Neck, and incorporates the EIoU metric to reduce boundary regression loss, thereby enhancing detection robustness (see Fig. 6; Table 1).

EMA attention mechanism

To further enhance the model’s capability in representing fine-grained features of ginkgo fruits and to improve target discrimination in backgrounds with similar color distributions, this study integrates a multi-scale attention (EMA) mechanism24 into the backbone network, as illustrated in Fig. 7. The EMA module strengthens the response of critical regions through multi-scale feature interaction and cross-spatial modeling, while avoiding channel dimensionality reduction and thereby mitigating the loss of detailed information. Given that ginkgo fruit targets are typically small in size, exhibit indistinct boundaries, and are subject to significant background interference, the module is designed with parallel branches. Specifically, a 1 × 1 convolution is employed for channel recalibration to emphasize global discriminative information, while a 3 × 3 convolution is utilized to capture local spatial structures and domain-specific textures. This combination achieves an effective balance between receptive field expansion and computational cost, making it well-suited to the dual requirements of detection accuracy and efficiency.

SAF module

The traditional path aggregation feature pyramid network (PAFPN) is widely used in the YOLO series network structure, but it has obvious limitations in effectively and adaptively integrating high-level semantic features with low-level spatial details. In order to solve this problem, this paper adds a superficial assisted fusion (SAF) module25(see Fig. 8) to Neck of YOLOv11n. This module enhances the fusion between low-level spatial information and high-level semantic features, thereby improving the model’s ability to localize small-scale and densely distributed targets. Owing to the discrepancies in resolution and channel representations across different feature scales, SAF is designed with scale-aligned upsampling and downsampling operations, along with channel regulation strategies tailored to adjacent feature levels. This design ensures an effective balance between information integration and computational cost, ultimately leading to improved stability and accuracy in ginkgo fruit detection. Software and hardware configuration

To guarantee the consistency and comparability of the experimental outcomes, all models were trained and evaluated under the same hardware and software environment. The test platform uses a computing system based on an Intel (R) i5-9300 H processor. The CPU frequency is 2.4 GHz, equipped with 16GB DDR4 memory and an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1650 graphics card. In terms of software environment, a high-performance computing framework based on CUDA 11.8 and CUDNN 8.6 is built, combined with Python 3.8 programming environment and PyTorch 2.0 deep learning library. To achieve a favorable balance between detection accuracy and inference speed (Supplementary Table S3) and to meet the requirements of real-time recognition in natural environments, the input image resolution in this study was set to 640 × 640. Regarding hyperparameter configuration, the initial learning rate was set to 0.01 based on preliminary experiments, which enables stable and sufficient gradient updates during the early stages of training, thereby avoiding oscillations or slow convergence. A weight decay coefficient of 0.0005 was adopted to introduce L2 regularization, effectively mitigating overfitting without excessively compromising the model’s representational capacity. The momentum factor was set to 0.937 to accumulate historical gradient information, smooth the optimization trajectory, and accelerate convergence. In addition, the batch size was set to 8 to balance training efficiency while enhancing the stochasticity of gradient estimation. The model was trained for 200 epochs, and repeated experiments confirmed that it fully converged and that the validation performance stabilized.

Results and discussion

Analysis of visualization results under different improved models

In order to further explore the model’s decision-making process, this study employed Grad-CAM43 and Eigen-CAM44 for visualization analysis of the input images. These techniques are interpretability methods that provide intuitive spatial representations of data features and their variation patterns, thereby enhancing the clarity and comprehensibility of the results. In the resulting heatmaps, red regions indicate high-weight features that play a decisive role in the model’s final predictions, yellow regions correspond to features of moderate importance, and blue regions represent background features with minimal contribution. The Grad-CAM and Eigen-CAM heatmap visualization results are presented in Fig. 9 and Supplementary Fig. S1.

Grad-CAM visualization results of different improved models in various scenarios.

The Eigen-CAM visualization results indicate that, across different complex scenarios, the response regions of all models exhibit a generally strong continuity. However, the salient regions are relatively dispersed, and the high-response areas show a certain degree of spatial diffusion. Specifically, the classical YOLOv11n model displays relatively vague response regions for ginkgo fruits under Eigen-CAM, making it difficult for the model to form a stable and concentrated response core, thereby demonstrating strong background dependency. This observation suggests that, although Eigen-CAM can reflect the overall trend of feature activation, it remains limited in distinguishing fine-grained differences between the target and the background. As EMA and SAF modules are progressively integrated, Eigen-CAM shows increasing attention toward the fruit regions, yet issues such as overly large response areas and unclear boundaries persist. In contrast, Cases4–6 exhibit more compact attention, with response regions gradually converging toward the fruit centers, thereby enhancing the models’ feature representation capabilities to some extent. In the Grad-CAM heatmaps, the classical YOLOv11n model presents diffuse and weak attention to the fruits, failing to effectively capture the main features of the fruit bodies. This limitation is particularly evident in scenarios involving branch obstruction, where the model cannot reliably detect all ginkgo fruits. Compared with YOLOv11n, Case1 demonstrates an increased attention to certain highly salient regions of the fruits. However, it fails to effectively recognize fruits with colors similar to the background or those of a smaller size. Similar phenomena are observed between Case3 and Case5, as well as Case6 and GinkgoSense-Net, indicating that the incorporation of EMA moderately enhances the model’s recognition capability, albeit with certain limitations. Cases2 and 3 also exhibit comparable issues, as they do not focus adequately on ginkgo fruits. Nevertheless, these two models generate extensive yellow patches over most fruits and even produce red spots in some critical regions. In contrast, Cases4–6 show significant improvements in both the accuracy and robustness of fruit recognition. The heatmap of Case4 illustrates a clearer ability to capture fruit contours, with red and yellow regions effectively covering the core areas of the fruits. The heatmaps for Cases5 and 6 exhibit excellent anti-interference performance, clearly delineating the contours of ginkgo fruits even under severe occlusion from branches and leaves. Notably, the GinkgoSense-Net model demonstrates the strongest feature extraction capability across both visualization methods, with Grad-CAM providing a particularly intuitive representation of its advantages. These visualization results suggest that the integration of EMA and SAF enables GinkgoSense-Net to more comprehensively extract and leverage the features of ginkgo fruits. However, while Grad-CAM and Eigen-CAM heatmaps allow for preliminary analysis of the image feature extraction process, the resulting visualizations alone remain insufficient to effectively differentiate the characteristic performance of the various improved models.

With the advancement of the training process, the value of the loss function shows a downward trend as a whole, and it shows a dynamic and stable state after reaching the minimum value. Specifically, the convergence process of the classical YOLOv11n model is relatively gentle, but the final convergence value is low. The performance curve of Case 2 exhibits an initial convergence followed by pronounced oscillations, reflecting that the complex structure introduced by the SAF module may bring optimization difficulties. In contrast, the EIoU loss function effectively improves the stability and convergence characteristics of training by constraining the distance error between the prediction and ground-truth boxes. The Case4 model is more stable than Case2, indicating that EMA plays a stabilizing role in the optimization process of SAF. Although the training convergence speed of GinkgoSense-Net is not the fastest, it demonstrates relatively stable performance in the middle and later stages, reflecting a certain degree of optimization stability and generalization capability. In the regression loss curve of the validation set, the loss of GinkgoSense-Net is the smallest, followed by Case5 and Case6, and the loss of Case1 and classical YOLOv11n is the largest. This is consistent with the phenomenon presented in Fig. 10, once again showing the superiority of the GinkgoSense-Net model. However, in the verification set location loss and classification loss, they cannot be effectively distinguished, which may be due to the difference in data distribution.

Comparative evaluation of detection performance variations across different enhanced models under diverse application scenarios

This study used the ginkgo fruit dataset in different environments to test various models. The specific results are shown in Fig. 11. Under normal light conditions, all models can effectively achieve relatively complete detection. Among them, although the basic framework of YOLOv11n can cover most of the targets, its confidence is generally low, and there is a slight overlap detection phenomenon. In contrast, Case1 combined with EMA significantly improves the confidence level. Under the counter light condition, the missed detection rate of Case2 is reduced by 42% compared with the classical YOLOv11n model, indicating that the SAF module can effectively alleviate the feature degradation problem caused by uneven illumination. For Case3, it shows the best positioning accuracy under the condition of branch occlusion, achieving a significant improvement compared with the classical YOLOv11n model. The detection results of Case1-3 further show that EMA, SAF, and EIoU can effectively improve the identification accuracy of classical YOLOv11n. In the two-module combination model, the detection results of Case4-6 are better than those of Case1-3, indicating that the combination of modules can effectively make up for the defects between modules. It is worth noting that GinkgoSense-Net consistently maintains strong overall performance across various scenarios. However, the model still has a certain false detection rate in areas with similar colors of fruits and branches, which may be related to the lack of sensitivity of the current feature extraction network to chromaticity differences.

In addition, ten repeated experiments were conducted to ensure the reliability of the results, as presented in Table 4, Supplementary Table S1–2, and Fig. 12. The experimental results indicate that, after applying data augmentation, the model’s precision and mAP decrease to some extent, whereas recall improves. These findings suggest that data augmentation may introduce noise or samples that deviate from the true distribution of ginkgo fruits, thereby increasing the difficulty of model learning and leading to a reduction in precision-related metrics. However, due to the increased diversity of training samples in terms of scale variation, illumination conditions, and object poses, the model is able to learn more comprehensive feature representations, resulting in enhanced robustness in complex natural environments, including conditions with occlusion, illumination variation, and background interference.

The performance comparison results of this study are presented in Table 5; Fig. 13. From the comparative analysis presented in Table 5, distinct variations in the effectiveness of different deep learning models in the ginkgo fruit recognition task can be clearly observed. Moreover, the model has a lightweight architecture, with a parameter size of only 6.90 MB and an inference speed of 62.11FPS. These results indicate that YOLO-CWD is well-suited for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices.

Analysis of detection performance differences with various classical models in different scenarios

Although this study has established an image dataset covering ginkgo fruits in various field environments, the adverse effects of weather conditions change and image blurring on system performance cannot be ignored in actual scenes. In order to systematically assess how real-world environmental factors affect model robustness, this work focused on the performance of GinkgoSense-Net in ginkgo fruit recognition and detection tasks under different environmental conditions, and compared it with several mainstream detection models such as DETR, Faster R-CNN, and CenterNet.

Based on the observations presented in Fig. 14, the evaluated models exhibit marked variability in performance across the different application scenarios. Specifically, CenterNet and SSD models have the worst recognition effect among all models, characterized by a high rate of missed detections. Moreover, only a limited proportion of the detected ginkgo fruit bounding boxes achieve confidence scores exceeding 85%, indicating their relatively weak detection reliability. This is similar to the phenomenon reflected in Table 3; Fig. 13, which once again shows that the two models are not suitable for the task of real-time recognition of ginkgo fruit. Compared with these two models, the DETR, RT-DETR, and Faster R-CNN models demonstrate relatively high detection accuracy across diverse environments. However, all three models exhibit both false positives and missed detections under several challenging conditions, including normal light, branch occlusion, dark light, and image blur. Consequently, they are unable to achieve reliable and comprehensive identification of individual ginkgo fruits. The YOLOv4 model is the worst of all YOLO models, and there are missed detections in both normal light and image blurring. However, the model has high confidence in the ginkgo fruit recognition box in various scenarios, which is much higher than DETR, Faster R-CNN, CenterNet, and SSD models. In comparison, YOLO-CWD demonstrates superior detection performance among all YOLO-based models. The confidence scores of most ginkgo fruit detection bounding boxes exceed 0.80, with no instances of missed or false detections observed. Its overall recognition performance is second only to that of GinkgoSense-Net. Among all the scenes of ginkgo fruit, the recognition effect is the worst in the scenes of branch occlusion, leaf occlusion, and image blurring. This may be because branches and leaves directly cover most of the information of the fruit, and image blurring will weaken the texture details of the fruit and background. GinkgoSense-Net can accurately detect each ginkgo fruit, and the confidence of most detection frames is above 0.85. This performance surpasses that of the classical YOLOv11n model, demonstrating its superior detection capability. Meanwhile, the model can still maintain stable recognition ability in a variety of complex scenarios, providing strong support for the research and development of intelligent agricultural picking robots.

Discussion

This study addresses the challenges of ginkgo fruit recognition in complex natural environments by developing a recognition approach tailored for practical agricultural applications. The method was systematically validated across multiple typical scenarios. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach maintains stable recognition accuracy and robust performance under diverse conditions, meeting the efficiency and reliability requirements of field-based automated operations. This research provides effective technical support for the automatic localization and recognition of ginkgo fruits.

The results of this study demonstrate that GinkgoSense-Net achieves strong detection performance in the task of ginkgo fruit recognition. Among them, the introduction of EMA is the key to strengthening the model’s feature extraction capability. The color of the ginkgo fruits and leaves is similar. EMA makes the network focus on the subtle difference between the fruit and background by parallel processing global channel information and local spatial context. In the Grad-CAM visualization results, the model heat map fused with EMA showed a more concentrated and stronger activation response in the fruit area, which effectively suppressed the interference of the complex background45. To mitigate the difficulties arising from the small scale and highly clustered distribution of ginkgo fruits, the integration of SAF directly and efficiently integrates the shallow output of Backbone into Neck, so that the detection head can use deep semantic overview and shallow precise positioning information at the same time when predicting. This improvement enhances the model’s ability to detect small-scale targets and partially occluded fruits, while effectively reducing missed detections. In addition, the overlap and adhesion of bounding boxes are common problems in fruit-dense areas, and the penalty term of aspect ratio in CIoU can hinder convergence in some cases46. EIoU decouples the width-height loss and calculates it independently, making the bounding box regression smoother and more accurate. This contributed to an improvement in the mAP values of the Case 3 and Case 5 models, while also yielding relatively high detection confidence.

In this research, GinkgoSense-Net effectively detected ginkgo fruits in different scenarios by combining the color characteristics of the ginkgo fruit surface. The results based on ablation tests show that our model achieves a good recognition effect for ginkgo fruit recognition precision, recall, mAP, and FPS of 95.0%, 92.1%, 93.6% and 74.9, respectively. To improve the accuracy and robustness of fruit recognition, researchers have proposed a variety of advanced technical solutions in recent years. Huang et al.47 improved the model to identify and crop fruit regions, and then fed these regions into a discrimination module for recognition, achieving an identification accuracy of 92.6%. Zhang et al.48 employed a multimodal neural network framework to accurately identify various fruit types and their maturity levels, achieving an identification accuracy of 77.8%. Yi et al.49 developed a YOLOv9-based fruit volume estimation framework for a complex orchard environment, and achieved 73.5%, 84.6%, 91.1%, and 89.4% accuracy on apple, pear, grapefruit, and kiwifruit datasets, respectively. In this study, the GinkgoSense-Net model was used to identify ginkgo fruits. The accuracy rate was 95.0%, which was 2.4%, 17.2% and 3.9% higher than the above studies, respectively. These results indicate that GinkgoSense-Net possesses strong recognition capability. In addition, the GinkgoSense-Net model demonstrates a notable advantage in recognition speed, achieving 74.9FPS.

This study demonstrates that GinkgoSense-Net achieves high detection accuracy and speed on the ginkgo fruit dataset. It can effectively adapt to complex field environments and enable real-time operation, thereby significantly reducing labor costs. In addition, it provides critical data support for yield estimation, automated harvesting, and precision orchard management. However, this study still has several limitations. On the one hand, the dataset constructed in this study was primarily collected within the same period, region, and under relatively limited environmental conditions. It has not yet been validated across datasets obtained from different acquisition times, geographic regions, years, or extreme environmental scenarios. On the other hand, although GinkgoSense-Net maintains high inference speed and recognition accuracy on resource-constrained devices, the current evaluation is mainly based on static image data, and continuous testing in real-world agricultural video stream scenarios has not yet been conducted. Future research will address these limitations by constructing more diverse datasets that span multiple regions, time periods, and years, thereby enabling a comprehensive evaluation of the model’s adaptability across various conditions. Furthermore, subsequent work will focus on developing a video stream processing system in real agricultural operational environments, facilitating real-time deployment and continuous performance validation. These efforts are expected to further enhance the practical applicability of the model in intelligent agricultural equipment and provide robust technical support for the sustainable and high-quality development of agricultural production.

Conclusions

Based on machine vision technology and the YOLOv11n framework, combined with EMA, SAF, and EIoU, this work presents an enhanced GinkgoSense-Net model for the real-time identification of ginkgo fruits in complex scenes. Based on the experimental findings, several key conclusions can be drawn:

(1) The GinkgoSense-Net model achieved precision, recall, and mAP scores of 95.0%, 92.1%, and 93.6%, respectively, which were 3.6%, 4.7%, and 4.9% higher than those of the original YOLOv11n model. Moreover, the model maintains the real-time detection speed of 74.9FPS, indicating that it achieves a favorable balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency.

(2) The Grad-CAM visualization and detection results indicate that the EMA attention mechanism, SAF module, and EIoU loss function can enhance the model’s real-time detection performance. These components effectively mitigate the impact of complex backgrounds and variable lighting conditions, thereby improving the model’s adaptability and stability across various environmental scenarios.

(3) The comparative evaluation indicates that GinkgoSense-Net outperforms several classical object detection models, including DETR, SSD, YOLOv4, and YOLOv12n. At the same time, the model weight is only 10.21 MB, which demonstrates a lightweight architecture suitable for execution on mobile platforms and devices with limited computational capacity.

The GinkgoSense-Net model developed in this study demonstrates strong real-time detection capability for ginkgo fruits across diverse scenarios. It satisfies the accuracy and responsiveness requirements of agricultural harvesting robots and contributes to advancing the implementation of intelligent agricultural systems.
