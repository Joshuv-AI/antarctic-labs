Project Overview

Many physics simulation codes spend much of their computing time solving very large systems
of linear equations. Algebraic multigrid (AMG) is one of the most effective methods for these
systems, but its performance depends on a collection of operators and parameters that are difficult
to design for every application. This project combined machine learning (ML) with established
numerical principles to improve AMG components. We developed methods that learn sparse
coarse-level operators, interpolation and smoothing procedures, approximate-inverse and multilevel
preconditioners, and strategies for selecting coarse variables. We also created AMGLearn, a common
software environment for developing and comparing the new algorithms. A complementary effort
applied data-driven optimization to smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH), a meshfree simulation
method used for problems involving shocks, material motion, and large deformation.
The AMG studies demonstrated that learned components can reduce computational complexity
and improve convergence while preserving the numerical properties required by an effective solver.
In representative tests, learned coarse operators reduced coarse-level complexity by at least 44
percent without a corresponding loss of solver effectiveness; other methods reduced iteration counts,
improved convergence across broad matrix collections, or transferred to related problem sizes and
parameters. The SPH study showed that optimizing the interpolation kernel and initial particle
distribution can reduce error on a standard shock-wave benchmark. Together, these results provide
a practical foundation for adaptive numerical methods that use data to improve simulation accuracy,
efficiency, and robustness.

Mission Impact

This project strengthens Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) capabilities in high-
performance computing, simulation, and data science. Large linear systems are a major compu-
tational cost in multiphysics simulations supporting stockpile stewardship, high-energy-density
science, and other missions of the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) and National Nuclear Security
Administration (NNSA). The project developed new approaches for reducing multilevel storage and
communication, improving solver robustness, and increasing the accuracy of meshfree hydrodynam-
ics calculations. AMGLearn provides a reusable environment for testing these ideas with LLNL
applications and assessing integration with scalable solver libraries such as hypre. The resulting
algorithms and expertise can improve future mission simulations and are relevant to other federal
programs that depend on scalable equation solvers and high-fidelity computational modeling.
