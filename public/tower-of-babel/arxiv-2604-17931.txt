LiteResearcher: A Scalable Agentic RL Training Framework for Deep Research Agent
Wanli Li 1,2,∗ , Bince Qu 1,2,∗ , Bo Pan 1 , Jianyu Zhang 1 , Zheng Liu 3,† , Pan Zhang 2 Wei Chen 1 , Bo Zhang 1,2,† 1 Zhejiang University 2 Simplex AI 3 The Hong Kong Polytechnic University {wanli_li@zju.edu.cn, tonyzhang@simplexai.com} https://github.com/simplex-ai-inc/LiteResearcher https://huggingface.co/simplex-ai-inc/LiteResearcher-4B

Abstract
Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful training paradigm for LLM-based agents. However, scaling agentic RL for deep research remains constrained by two coupled challenges: hand-crafted synthetic data fails to elicit genuine real-world search capabilities, and real-world search dependency during RL training introduces instability and prohibitive cost, which limits the scalability of Agentic RL. LiteResearcher is a training framework that makes Agentic RL scalable: by constructing a lite virtual world that mirrors real-world search dynamics, we enable a continuously improving training recipe that empowers a tiny search agent to outperform large-scale open-source and commercial models (e.g., Tongyi DeepResearch and Claude-4.5 Sonnet). Specifically, on common benchmarks such as GAIA and Xbench, our LiteResearcher-4B achieves open-source state-of-the-art results of 71.3% and 78.0% respectively, demonstrating that scalable RL training is a key enabler for Deep Research Agents.

1
Introduction

Recent advances in reinforcement learning (RL) on reasoning models
(
Liu et al. 2025b
;
Guo et al. 2025
;
Team et al. 2025a
)
have demonstrated that continuously scaling model capabilities is achievable within stable training environments and with data of appropriate difficulty. However, when transitioning to agentic RL, where reasoning must be integrated with tool use, prior efforts have struggled to achieve similar sustained improvements. We identify the primary bottleneck as the inherent noise and instability of the real-world environment. In the domain of Coding Agents
(
Wei et al. 2025b
;
Jain et al. 2025
)
, this challenge has been effectively addressed through the use of isolated sandboxes configured to simulate production environments while filtering out infrastructure-level noise, thereby enabling scalable training.

However, existing works in the domain of Deep Research have yet to realize such scalable RL training. Online RL frameworks
(
Zheng et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025d
)
interact directly with the live internet, introducing significant training variance and prohibitive costs that limit continuous capability growth. Local retrieval-based systems
(
Jin et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025c
;
Sun et al. 2025
)
are typically confined to a narrow corpus, making it impossible to replicate the complex search dynamics of the diverse internet. Meanwhile, current real-web graph-based data synthesis methods
(
Jin et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025a
;
Gao et al. 2025
)
rely on over-engineered reasoning structures, focusing on handcrafted logic while ignoring the diversity of atomic search techniques required for deep research, such as cross-verification and enumeration.

We argue that scaling agentic RL for Deep Research requires a virtual world that mirrors the structure of the live internet but is isolated in execution: the twin architecture ensures that policies optimized in the virtual environment can generalize to the open web, while isolation shields the training process from the high variance and stochasticity of environmental noise.

In this paper, we present
LiteResearcher
, an Agentic RL training framework designed to construct a
virtual world that mirrors real-world web architecture
. We follow these steps to realize the scaling potential of agentic RL: (1) Synthesize real search data; (2) Simulate search environment; (3) Achieve sustained improvements through curriculum RL.

The foundation of this virtual world is generating realistic training tasks. To capture the diversity of information seeking in real-world user queries, we prioritize the scale of information sources in our virtual world. We find that by scaling up information sources, even a
simple-but-effective
data synthesis can yield a realistic distribution of training tasks.

Crucially, handling these synthetic tasks requires a corresponding execution environment. We simulate a realistic search environment by enriching our corpus with these tasks. For every synthetic task, we perform a targeted expansion of the local webpage corpus by searching and fetching related real-world webpages, which then construct the local search and browse tool. This ensures that for any given training task, the local search and browse tools provide search dynamics analogous to the open web, allowing the agent to obtain real-web atom capabilities in a controlled environment.

Even with synthetic tasks and tools, training can still saturate if the model gets trapped at a certain difficulty threshold, correctly solving easy tasks while completely failing complex ones. We apply a difficulty-aware curriculum RL which progressively enhances both task difficulty and context length across training stages. At each stage, we only keep the synthetic tasks that the current model can partially solve, discarding both trivially easy and impossibly hard instances, thereby providing consistent training signal for improvement.

Our tiny search agent
LiteResearcher-4B
achieves 71.3% on GAIA
(
Mialon et al. 2023
)
and 78.0% on Xbench
(
Chen et al. 2025
)
, surpassing open-source agents up to
8\times
larger such as Tongyi DeepResearch 30B (70.9%/75.0%) and WebSailor 30B (53.2%/53.3%), while matching or exceeding proprietary systems including Claude-4.5-Sonnet (71.2%/66.0%) and GLM-4.6 (71.9%/70.0%). These results demonstrate that scalable RL can yield a powerful on-device deep research agent.

Related Work

Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning

The pursuit of advanced reasoning in LLMs initially relied on inference-time heuristics such as Chain-of-Thought prompting
(
Wei et al. 2022
)
and Tree of Thoughts
(
Yao et al. 2023
)
. A paradigm shift occurred with the demonstration that RL can internalize reasoning capabilities directly into model weights. DeepSeek-R1
(
Guo et al. 2025
)
showed that RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is sufficient to unlock emergent reasoning behaviors such as self-verification and backtracking, and subsequent work has rapidly advanced optimization
(
Yu et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025b
)
, reward design
(
Cui et al. 2025
)
, and scaling
(
Hu et al. 2025
;
Yang et al. 2025
)
, establishing RLVR as a general-purpose post-training paradigm. However, all these methods operate as closed-world reasoners: they rely solely on parametric knowledge and lack the ability to interact with external dynamic environments—a critical limitation for tasks requiring real-time information retrieval.

Agentic Deep Research Models

Recent research has pivoted towards Agentic Deep Research, where agents interact with external search environments through iterative query formulation and page navigation. This line of work can be grouped by environment design. Online real-world RL systems
(
Zheng et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025d
;
Li et al. 2025a
)
train directly on the live web, offering realistic interactions but incurring high cost and non-deterministic reward signals. Local retrieval-based systems
(
Jin et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025c
;
Gao et al. 2025
)
construct environments from Wikipedia or similar corpora, providing fast, deterministic interactions but remaining confined to narrow, homogeneous domains. Simulated systems such as ZeroSearch
(
Sun et al. 2025
)
replace search engines by training an LLM to mimic search engine outputs, but sacrifice page-level fidelity. Recent lightweight explorations such as AgentCPM-Explore
(
Chen et al. 2026
)
further show the capability of small deep-research agents, but still fail to realize scalable RL training. In contrast, our framework builds a local environment with real-world data and a search engine, enabling scalable RL training.

3
Preliminaries: Agentic Framework

We model deep research as a sequential decision-making process under the ReAct framework
(
Yao et al. 2022
)
. Given an initial user query
q
, the agent interacts with the environment over multiple steps by alternately generating reasoning thoughts, executing actions, and receiving observations. The history up to step
t
is denoted by

\mathcal{H}_{t}=(q,\tau_{1},a_{1},o_{1},\dots,\tau_{t},a_{t},o_{t}),

where
\tau_{i}
,
a_{i}
, and
o_{i}
denote the thought, action, and environmental observation at step
i
, respectively. At each step, the agent first generates a reasoning thought
\tau_{t}
conditioned on the preceding history, and then selects an executable action
a_{t}
conditioned on both
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
and
\tau_{t}
:

\tau_{t}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1}),\qquad a_{t}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1},\tau_{t}).

Action Space.
The action space
\mathcal{A}
consists of two primitive actions:

•

Search
(q^{\prime})
: submits a natural-language query
q^{\prime}
to the search engine and returns a ranked list of retrieved snippets and URLs.

•

Browse
(u,q^{\prime})
: visits a selected URL
u
and returns a query-conditioned summary of the page content.

After collecting sufficient information, the agent terminates environment interaction and generates a final response to the original user query
q
.

4
Methods: Towards Scalable Training

To explore the scaling potential of Agentic Deep Research, we aim to establish a scalable training framework as a mandatory first step. Current paradigms that rely on high-latency, unstable online interactions fundamentally bottleneck this process. In this section, we present our LiteResearcher framework, which decouples agent evolution from the open web while ensuring generalization to real-world web dynamics. As illustrated in Figure
2
, the ecosystem is built upon three core pillars:

(1)
Co-construct Training Data and Corpus Pipeline
: To achieve
diversity
in the data distribution and
scalability
in data quantity, we propose a unified framework that co-evolves the training data and the local corpus.

(2)
Stable Local Tool Environment
: Leveraging the enriched webpage corpus, we construct a Local Search Engine and Local Browse Tool that mirror the behavior of real-world search dynamics within the synthesized data.

(3)
Difficulty-Aware Curriculum Learning
: To continuously expand the model’s capability boundaries, we leverage our unlimited synthetic data pool to implement a multi-stage curriculum that serves the model with data of appropriate difficulty, thereby ensuring sustainable performance improvements.

4.1
Co-construct Training Data and Corpus Pipeline

We identify that complex real-world deep research trajectories are fundamentally composed of five atomic search capabilities:

•

Direct Information
: Directly finding a specific piece of information via search.

•

Aggregation
: Locating a target answer based on multiple identifying attributes.

•

Enumeration
: Listing and counting all entities that satisfy specific constraints.

•

Cross-verification
: Validating and triangulating claims across independent sources.

•

Statistics
: Performing comprehensive calculations and extracting numerical metrics.

To construct a dataset with both capability
diversity
and
scalability
, rather than relying on complex, handcrafted generation logic, we first scale up the underlying information sources to comprehensively cover the diverse search patterns of the real world. Subsequently, we filter this vast information pool into solvable, high-quality tasks. Specifically:

QA generation from Seed Corpus

The data construction process begins with a high-quality
Seed Corpus
including Wikipedia and BBC News articles. We then prompt an LLM with the template in Appendix
A.1

to process this raw content and extract structured question-answer (QA) pairs that target key factual entities within the content. These initial QA pairs serve as the foundational “seed tasks” that trigger the subsequent iterative expansion of the local environment.

Information Source Masking

To prevent the agent from relying on trivial shortcuts, we implement a
source masking
mechanism. After a QA pair is generated, we deliberately delete its original information source from the local corpus. This forces the agent to navigate the expanded corpus to discover alternative, non-trivial search pathways, naturally eliciting the complex atomic search capabilities detailed in Table
1
.

Strict Quality and Difficulty Filtering

To guarantee the quality and solvability of the dataset, the candidate QA pairs undergo a rigorous LLM-based evaluation guided by a
7-point rubric
(Appendix
A.2
). As detailed in Appendix
A.2
, this rubric contains dimensions such as independence, ambiguity, answer verifiability, etc. A data point is retained only if it strictly satisfies all seven criteria. Furthermore, prior to each training session, we apply an additional
difficulty filter
to ensure the tasks are appropriately calibrated to the agent’s current capability (see Section 4.3). This final distillation process provides a
diverse and realistic data distribution
for subsequent training.

Continuously Expanding Corpus

For each filtered QA pair, we utilize a search engine to fetch related webpages from the live internet. These newly retrieved pages are continuously appended to the
Enriched Corpus
. This iterative expansion creates a corpus covering the vast and diverse information distribution of the real world.

4.2
Stable Local Tool Environment

Leveraging the enriched corpus from Section
4.1
(
\sim
32M pages, 1M+ domains), we build a fully local tool environment that replaces online interaction during RL training. Unlike standard RAG pipelines that split each page into many chunks, we adopt
page-level indexing
: each webpage is represented by a single vector (title + summary). This keeps the index small enough to serve hundreds of concurrent rollouts at low latency, which would be infeasible with a chunk-level index that is
\sim
10
\times
larger. During training, the system sustains hundreds of concurrent tool requests, accumulating to 73.2M calls over the full RL run (Section
5
). The corpus is served through two purpose-built services (infrastructure details in Appendix
D
):

•

Local Search Engine
: We adopt BGE-M3
(
Chen et al. 2024
)
for hybrid retrieval, which produces both dense and learned sparse embeddings in a single forward pass. The two signals are fused at query time through the built-in hybrid search of Milvus
(
Wang et al. 2021
)
. All embeddings are stored on disk and accessed via DiskANN
(
Jayaram Subramanya et al. 2019
)
with mmap, achieving
\sim
0.15 s/query (
\sim
10
\times
faster than online search engine).

•

Local Browse Tool
: Full webpage content is stored as Markdown in PostgreSQL, keyed by URL and tuned for 1,000 concurrent connections; pages are returned directly upon request at
\sim
0.17 s/page (
\sim
46
\times
faster than Jina Reader).

4.3
Difficulty-Aware Curriculum Learning

A fundamental challenge in Agentic RL is training saturation, where the reward curve converges because the model becomes trapped at a difficulty threshold, correctly solving easy tasks while completely failing complex ones, resulting in no meaningful learning gradient.

Difficulty-Aware Data Filtering

To address training saturation, before each RL stage, we evaluate the difficulty of training queries. For each query, we generate
K=8
rollouts (pass@8) and strictly retain only those where the number of correct responses
c
satisfies
1\leq c\leq 7
. Queries with
c=8
are discarded as trivial (yielding zero useful gradient), while those with
c=0
are deemed impossible or excessively noisy.

On-Policy GRPO Curriculum Learning

We employ the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
(
Shao et al. 2024
)
framework. Given a query
q
and sampled outputs
\{o_{1},\dots,o_{K}\}
, the advantage
A_{i}
is computed by normalizing the reward of
o_{i}
against the group mean and standard deviation. Stripped of KL and entropy terms, our objective function is defined strictly as the clipped surrogate loss:

\mathcal{J}_{GRPO}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{q\sim P(Q),\{o_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}\sim\pi_{\theta_{old}}}\left[\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\min\left(r_{i}(\theta)A_{i},\text{clip}\left(r_{i}(\theta),1-\epsilon_{low},1+\epsilon_{high}\right)A_{i}\right)\right]

where
r_{i}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(o_{i}|q)}{\pi_{\theta_{rollout}}(o_{i}|q)}
is the probability ratio of training engine and rollout engine. While standard implementations perform multiple mini-batch updates over a replay buffer, we enforce a strictly
on-policy
update. We present a detailed ablation study regarding this completely on-policy formulation in Section
5
.

Experimental Setup

On-Policy vs. Off-Policy Training.

We compare a strictly on-policy algorithm, where each rollout batch is consumed in a single update and then discarded, with a more off-policy algorithm, where each rollout batch is split into multiple mini-batches (e.g., 256 samples into 4 mini-batches) and used for several successive updates. As shown in Figure
3
, the off-policy variant gains reward much earlier but eventually declines, whereas on-policy training continues to improve more steadily throughout training. Figure
3
shows that on-policy training achieves higher GAIA validation accuracy (68.9%) than off-policy training (66.8%).

These results suggest that long-horizon Agentic RL is particularly sensitive to off-policy updates. We hypothesize that multiple updates on the same rollout batch introduce policy lag, leading to increasing mismatch between the sampled trajectories and the current policy. In long-horizon search tasks, such mismatch can accumulate along the trajectory, harming optimization stability and ultimately limiting the achievable performance ceiling.

Effectiveness of Multi-Stage Training.

Single-stage RL saturates once the model exhausts tasks at its current difficulty level; deliberate difficulty escalation is the key to continued improvement. Figure
4
shows the learning curves of our two-stage strategy. In Stage 1, the model reaches a plateau at 64.7% GAIA accuracy. After transitioning to Stage 2 with adjusted data distribution and targeted complexity, training continues to improve, reaching 68.3% (+3.6%). This indicates that the second stage helps sustain learning beyond the saturation point of the first, enabled by our unlimited synthetic data pool.

). As shown in Table

, SFT alone yields 55.6% on GAIA—still 15.3 points below the teacher’s own 70.9%. The RL stage not only closes this gap but surpasses it, reaching 71.3% (+15.7 over SFT), with consistent gains across all eight benchmarks. This indicates that the primary performance driver is the RL training framework rather than teacher distillation.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we presented
LiteResearcher
, an agentic RL training framework that resolves the critical bottlenecks of data diversity and environment stability by constructing an isolated virtual world. Crucially, our work yields three key insights for the future of deep research agents. First, we demonstrate that
Agentic RL can drive sustained capability improvement
. The training saturation observed in prior works is due to reliance on high-variance live environments or a narrow local corpus, rather than a limitation of the RL paradigm itself. Second, we reveal that a
simple but scalable synthetic data pipeline
, driven by the continuous expansion of real-world information sources, is significantly more effective than over-engineered, handcrafted reasoning structures. Finally, trained entirely locally at zero marginal cost, our compact LiteResearcher-4B achieves 71.3% on GAIA and 78.0% on Xbench-DS. By matching or exceeding commercial models, our results suggest that
scalable RL training, beyond model scale alone, is a critical enabler for strong deep research capabilities
. The entire training framework, including data synthesis pipelines, local environment infrastructure, and RL training code, will be open-sourced to facilitate future research.
