Comparison of the hadronic vacuum polarization between
hadronic

Abstract




We compare the isospin-one, vector-current hadronic vacuum polarization (HVP)
obtained from isospin-symmetric lattice QCD with that obtained from a
dispersive representation employing inclusive hadronic
\tau
decay
data corrected for isospin breaking. We consider the subtracted
HVP evaluated at squared Euclidean momenta ranging from

0.5
GeV
2
to
12
GeV
2
, together with the light-quark-connected HVP contribution
to the muon anomalous magnetic moment and the short-, intermediate- and
long-distance RBC/UKQCD window components thereof. Dispersive
contributions from the region of hadronic invariant masses above the
\tau
mass are
evaluated using perturbative QCD. We also consider dispersive
determinations using
\tau
data only for contributions from two-pion,
or two-pion and four-pion, modes, and evaluating the remaining contributions
using exclusive-mode
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mbox{hadrons}
cross sections up
to about 2 GeV, lessening the dependence on perturbation theory. We find
generally good agreement between lattice and
\tau
-based results. However, a
comparison of
\tau
-based window-quantity contributions for
the two four-pion modes to expectations for those contributions based on the
Pais relations and
e^{+}e^{-}
four-pion cross sections, reveals
significant differences for the
2\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
mode.

to future work. Here we investigate this
additional source of IB using the chiral-perturbation-theory (ChPT)-inspired
model of Ref.
CEN
;
Guerrero:1997ku
for which

f^{\rm model}_{+}(s)=\frac{m_{\rho}^{2}}{m_{\rho}^{2}-s-im_{\rho}\Gamma_{\rho}(s)}\,\mbox{exp}[2\tilde{H}_{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}(s)+\tilde{H}_{K^{-}K^{0}}(s)]\ ,

(22)

in which

\Gamma_{\rho}(s)=\frac{m_{\rho}s}{96\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}\left(\beta_{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}^{3}(s)\theta(s-(m_{\pi^{+}}+m_{\pi^{0}})^{2})+{1\over 2}\beta_{K^{-}K^{0}}^{3}(s)\theta(s-(m_{K^{-}}+m_{K^{0}})^{2})\right)\ ,

(23)

and

\tilde{H}_{ij}(s)=\frac{1}{f_{\pi}^{2}}\,{\rm Re\,}h_{ij}(s,m_{\rho})\ ,

(24)

with
h_{ij}(s,\mu)
, which encapsulates one-loop effects from ChPT, given
in App.
B
for masses
m_{i}
and
m_{j}
.

In order to correct the
\tau
-data-based parts of Eq. (
7
) to the world of
isoQCD, we thus need to also multiply
\rho_{[\tau^{-}\to\pi^{-}\pi^{0}\nu_{\tau}(\gamma)]}(s)
by the
ratio of the form factor (
22
) evaluated with both pion masses equal to the neutral
pion mass and both kaon masses equal to the kaon mass of isoQCD,
to that evaluated with “real-world” pion and kaon masses.
The complete correction factor is thus

F_{\rm IB}(s)=\frac{S_{\rm EW}}{S_{\rm EW}^{\pi\pi}}\,\frac{1}{G_{\rm EM}(s)}\,\frac{\beta_{\pi^{0}\pi^{0}}^{3}(s)}{\beta_{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}^{3}(s)}\,\frac{|f^{\rm model}_{+}(s)[m_{\pi^{-}}\to m_{\pi^{0}};\ m_{K^{-},K^{0}}\to m_{K^{\rm iso}}]|^{2}}{|f^{\rm model}_{+}(s)[\mbox{real\ world}]|^{2}}\ .

(25)

The factor
S_{\rm EW}
in the numerator accounts for the fact that all spectral
data employed in this paper have already been corrected by the factor
S_{\rm EW}
,
so only the relative difference between
S_{\rm EW}
and
S_{\rm EW}^{\pi\pi}
still
needs to be accounted for.

Finally, the threshold

for

of Eq. (

that the thus-obtained isoQCD spectral function matches very well to the lowest
data points, after they have been corrected as described above. It also turns out
that this threshold correction is so small that it can be safely neglected.

The use of the model of Ref.
CEN
for the form-factor

is the weakest
step in modeling IB corrections. To explore the impact of possible changes in the form
used for the

we have replaced the factor

also no uncertainties due to EM effects.
It turns out that this is a significant effect, as discussed in Ref.
WP25
, and we will
use the size of this
effect as an estimate of the systematic error associated with using Eq. (

)
in Sec.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered the isospin-1 component of the HVP,
which is relevant for high-precision computations of quantities such as the muon
anomalous magnetic moment and the hadronic contribution

\Delta\alpha_{\rm had}
to the running of the EM coupling
\alpha
. We compared
values obtained dispersively using
I=1
hadronic
\tau
-decay data with those
obtained in lattice QCD. Specifically, we considered the subtracted HVP
{\overline{\Pi}}(Q^{2})
,
as well as the lqc contributions to the SD, W1 and LD window quantities and

a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP}
itself. As shown in
Fig.
7
, generally good agreement was found between
the
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP,lqc}
and lqc window quantities produced using the

\tau
-based representation and lattice results for these same quantitities
reported and averaged in Ref.
WP25
. We note that lattice
errors are smaller for the SD and W1 window quantities, while
the situation is reversed for the LD window and
a_{\mu}^{\rm HVP,lqc}
.
For
{\overline{\Pi}}(Q^{2})
, with
0.5
GeV
{}^{2}\leq Q^{2}\leq 12
GeV
2
, we found

\tau
-based values which lie
2-3\sigma
below those of Ref.
Mainz25
, and
are in better agreement with the smaller number of results reported
in Ref.
BMW20
.

In order to make the comparisons, the
\tau
-data-based quantities had to be corrected
for IB, which we assumed to be dominated by those of the two-pion mode. While
radiative corrections appear to be under control
CCHH
, IB also affects the
form factor
f_{+}(s)
, for which no model-independent analysis of IB effects exists
at present. For this form factor, we relied on the ChPT-inspired model of Ref.
CEN

with what we believe to be conservative errors,
following recommendations from Ref.
WP25
.

Finally, we compared the four-pion spectral distributions obtained
using
\tau
data with expectations for those distributions produced
using electroproduction data and the isospin-based Pais relations.
Particularly interesting comparisons based on these results are
between the four-pion contributions to the
SD, W1 and LD window quantities, where we find significant differences between the

\tau
-based
2\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
exclusive-mode contributions
and their
e^{+}e^{-}
-based counterparts.
These differences do not readily show up in our all-contribution totals for these
lqc quantities, as the

2\pi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
differences, though themselves statistically
significant when all correlations are taken into account, are smaller than the errors
on these totals.
