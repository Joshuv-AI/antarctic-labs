Geometric Formulation of Power-Efficiency Bounds in Carnot-like Engines
R. X. Zhai (翟若迅)

Abstract
We formulate the power-efficiency constraint of Carnot-like heat engines as a geometric optimization problem in the plane of normalized branch dissipations. Efficiency contours are straight lines in this plane, so maximizing efficiency at fixed power reduces to bounding the slope of an admissible line. We apply this framework to branch-resolved power-law dissipation, where the irreversible loss on each isothermal branch decays with the branch duration with a common exponent rather than following the standard inverse-time law. After optimizing over the dissipation-asymmetry parameter, the fixed-power attainable set becomes a two-dimensional region, and the resulting slope-bound problem reduces to linear programming. The framework yields the exact power-efficiency frontier within this model and gives closed-form constraints for representative dissipation exponents, including the maximum-power limit.



I
Introduction

A Carnot heat engine operates between two reservoirs and converts part of the absorbed heat into work
Huang 1987
;
Ma 2004
. Its efficiency is bounded by the Carnot efficiency, which is attained only in the quasistatic limit. In this limit the cycle time diverges, so the output power vanishes. Finite-time thermodynamics addresses this compromise between reversibility and useful power
Salamon and Berry 1983
;
Andresen et al. 1984
. A central question is therefore the power-efficiency constraint: at a given power, what efficiency is attainable, and at a given efficiency, what power is attainable?

The power-efficiency constraint is the direct extension of the familiar single-point benchmark set by the efficiency at maximum power. Early endoreversible models and later finite-time engines showed that maximum-power operation generally occurs below the Carnot efficiency
Yvon 1955
;
Chambadal 1957
;
Novikov 1958
;
Curzon and Ahlborn 1975
;
Tu 2012
.
This question has since been studied in linear-response and stochastic models, as well as in quantum, thermoelectric, and Rankine-type heat-engine settings
Van den Broeck 2005
;
Schmiedl and Seifert 2008
;
Tu 2008
;
Izumida and Okuda 2008
;
Allahverdyan et al. 2008
;
Rutten et al. 2009
;
Sheng and Tu 2015
;
Brandner and Seifert 2015
;
Tu 2014
;
Martinez et al. 2015
;
Rossnagel et al. 2016
;
Josefsson et al. 2018
;
Josefsson et al. 2019
;
Liang et al. 2025
;
Cui and Dong 2025
. In the low-dissipation regime, bounds on the efficiency at maximum power were derived in Ref.
Esposito et al. 2010
.
For low-dissipation heat engines where the irreversible entropy production is proportional to
1/\tau
, fixed-power bounds and universal constraints were developed in Refs.
Holubec and Ryabov 2016
;
Ryabov and Holubec 2016
;
Ma et al. 2018
;
Zhai et al. 2026
, and related results have been obtained for steady-state, stochastic, minimally nonlinear, shortcut-assisted, chemical, and fluctuation-constrained engines
Long and Liu 2016
;
Shiraishi et al. 2016
;
Holubec and Ryabov 2017
;
Pietzonka and Seifert 2018
;
Chen 2022
;
Zhao et al. 2022
;
Zhai and Dong 2025
. Experiments have tested both the low-dissipation scaling and the resulting power-efficiency trade-off
Ma et al. 2020
;
Zhai et al. 2023
.

Usually, the inverse-time low-dissipation law assumes that the irreversible entropy production of a finite-time isothermal branch is proportional to the inverse branch duration. When a driven system crosses a critical region or a first-order transition, irreversible entropy production, excess work, or hysteresis indicators can obey nonstandard finite-time scaling
Deffner 2017
;
Wu et al. 2025
;
Chen et al. 2026
. When the system-bath coupling is controlled during an isothermal transformation, the total dissipation can decay with tunable powers of the protocol time
Pancotti et al. 2020
. When memory kernels or relaxation laws are algebraic, finite-time corrections need not be governed by a single relaxation time
Lutz 2001
;
Burov and Barkai 2008
. These examples motivate, without deriving microscopically, a branch-resolved power-law dissipation model.

Finite-time engines beyond the inverse-time law have been studied through generalized dissipative Carnot-like models
Wang and Tu 2012
;
Wang and Tu 2013
. A recent unified approach derived power-efficiency trade-off relations for generic thermal machines from a cycle-level power-law irreversibility
Ma and Fu 2025
; related finite-time refrigerators, heat pumps, and heat devices have also been optimized
Wang et al. 2012
;
de Tomas et al. 2013
;
Holubec and Ye 2020
;
Ye and Holubec 2022
;
Ye and Holubec 2025
. The present work is complementary to these cycle-level approaches: it focuses on the branch-resolved attainable region and the geometry of its power-efficiency frontier.

We do not introduce a new heat-engine mechanism. Instead, we give a geometric method for Carnot-like engines with branch-resolved power-law dissipation. The normalized irreversible entropy productions on the hot and cold isothermal branches are used as coordinates
(\sigma_{h},\sigma_{c})
. At fixed normalized power, the admissible protocols form a set in this plane. Efficiency level curves are straight lines, so maximizing efficiency at fixed power becomes a problem of bounding the slope of an admissible line.

This formulation also clarifies the role of the time-allocation asymmetry parameter
\xi
. If
\xi
is fixed, a prescribed normalized power selects a one-dimensional closed curve in the
(\sigma_{h},\sigma_{c})
plane, and the efficiency bound follows from tangency with an efficiency line. For the low-dissipation case, this fixed-
\xi
construction corresponds to the exact bound of Ref.
Zhai et al. 2026
. Here
\xi
is optimized as well, so the family of fixed-
\xi
curves projects onto a two-dimensional attainable region.

For power-law dissipation, the resulting slope-bound problem reduces to linear programming. This yields the exact power-efficiency frontier within the model and gives closed-form constraints for representative dissipation exponents, including the maximum-power limit.

II
Geometry of Power-Efficiency Constraints

We consider a Carnot-like cycle operating between two heat reservoirs at temperatures
T_{h}>T_{c}
. The cycle consists of two adiabatic branches and two finite-time isothermal branches. During the finite-time contacts of durations
\tau_{h}
and
\tau_{c}
, the heats exchanged with the hot and cold reservoirs are
Q_{h,c}=\pm T_{h,c}\Delta S^{\text{(re)}}-Q_{h,c}^{(\text{ir})}(\tau_{h,c})
.
Here,
\Delta S^{(\text{re})}\geq 0
is the isothermal entropy change in the corresponding reversible cycle. We assume that the irreversible heat transfer scales with
\tau
as a power law,
Q_{h,c}^{\text{(ir)}}=M_{h,c}\tau_{h,c}^{-\alpha}
.
For
\alpha=1
, this recovers the well-studied low-dissipation case. The power and efficiency are defined as

P=\frac{(T_{h}-T_{c})\Delta S^{\text{(re)}}-M_{h}\tau_{h}^{-\alpha}-M_{c}\tau_{c}^{-\alpha}}{\tau_{h}+\tau_{c}},

(1)

and

\eta=\frac{(T_{h}-T_{c})\Delta S^{\text{(re)}}-M_{h}\tau_{h}^{-\alpha}-M_{c}\tau_{c}^{-\alpha}}{T_{h}\Delta S^{\text{(re)}}-M_{h}\tau_{h}^{-\alpha}}.

(2)

We now rewrite these quantities in terms of three dimensionless parameters,
\xi
,
\sigma_{h}
, and
\sigma_{c}
. The variables
\sigma_{h}
and
\sigma_{c}
are the dimensionless irreversible heats for the finite-time isothermal processes, defined as

\sigma_{h,c}\equiv\quantity( M_{h,c} / T_h \Delta S^{\text{(re)}} )\tau_{h,c}^{-\alpha}\geq 0
. The variable
0\leq\xi\leq 1
, defined by
\xi\equiv M_{h}^{1/(1+\alpha)}/\quantity(M_h^{1/(1+\alpha)} + M_c^{1/(1+\alpha)})
, characterizes the dissipation asymmetry between the two isothermal branches.
For later convenience, we set
\epsilon\equiv 1/\alpha
.
In these variables, the efficiency becomes

\eta=\frac{\eta_{C}-\sigma_{h}-\sigma_{c}}{1-\sigma_{h}}.

(3)

By normalizing the power by its maximum with respect to
\sigma_{h}
and
\sigma_{c}
, i.e.,
\tilde{P}\equiv P/P_{\max}
, we obtain

\tilde{P}(\sigma_{h},\sigma_{c},\xi)=\frac{(1+\epsilon)^{1+\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{\epsilon}\eta_{C}^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{\quantity(\eta_C - \sigma_h - \sigma_c)\sigma_{h}^{\epsilon}\sigma_{c}^{\epsilon}}{(1-\xi)^{1+\epsilon}\sigma_{h}^{\epsilon}+\xi^{1+\epsilon}\sigma_{c}^{\epsilon}}.

(4)

For heat-engine operation, the positive-work condition requires
\eta_{C}-\sigma_{h}-\sigma_{c}>0
. With the above notation, the mathematical problem of determining the power-efficiency constraint becomes the following: under the constraint that the function in Eq. (
4
) takes the value
\tilde{P}=\tilde{P}_{0}
, find the maximum and minimum values of the efficiency
\eta
in Eq. (
3
).

Since the efficiency depends only on
\sigma_{h}
and
\sigma_{c}
, its level curves form a family of straight lines passing through the point
\sigma_{h}=1
,
\sigma_{c}=\eta_{C}-1
. The value of
\eta
is completely determined by the slope
\kappa
of the line. For a given normalized power

\tilde{P}(\sigma_{h},\sigma_{c},\xi)=\tilde{P}_{0},

(5)

this equation constrains the possible values of
\sigma_{h}
and
\sigma_{c}
in the two-dimensional plane
(\sigma_{h},\sigma_{c})
. When
\xi
is fixed, Eq. (
5
) restricts
\sigma_{h}
and
\sigma_{c}
to a closed curve in this plane. In this case, the maximum and minimum efficiencies are obtained when the efficiency level lines are tangent to the curve. Determining the power-efficiency constraint therefore becomes the problem of finding tangent lines to a curve through a fixed point. For
\epsilon=1
, this analysis recovers the result of Ref.
Zhai et al. 2026
.

In this paper, we focus on a more general case in which
\xi
is also treated as a tunable variable. Then Eq. (
5
) no longer selects a single curve in the
(\sigma_{h},\sigma_{c})
plane. Instead, as
\xi
varies, these curves sweep out a two-dimensional region. In the following analysis, we show that the power-efficiency constraint problem is thereby transformed into a linear-programming problem.

III
From Attainable Regions to Linear Programming

To analyze the two-dimensional region described above, we rewrite Eq. (
5
) as

\frac{\eta_{C}^{1+\epsilon}\epsilon^{\epsilon}}{(1+\epsilon)^{1+\epsilon}}\tilde{P}_{0}=(\eta_{C}-\sigma_{h}-\sigma_{c})\sigma,

(6)

where

\sigma\equiv\frac{\sigma_{h}^{\epsilon}\sigma_{c}^{\epsilon}}{(1-\xi)^{1+\epsilon}\sigma_{h}^{\epsilon}+\xi^{1+\epsilon}\sigma_{c}^{\epsilon}}.

(7)

Taking the supremum and infimum of
\sigma
with respect to
\xi
gives
\min\{\sigma_{h}^{\epsilon},\sigma_{c}^{\epsilon}\}\leq\sigma\leq(\sigma_{h}+\sigma_{c})^{\epsilon}
.
Combining the upper bound with Eq. (
6
), we obtain the inequality

F(S)\equiv(S-\eta_{C})S^{\epsilon}+\frac{\eta_{C}^{1+\epsilon}\epsilon^{\epsilon}}{(1+\epsilon)^{1+\epsilon}}\tilde{P}_{0}\leq 0,

(8)

where
S\equiv\sigma_{h}+\sigma_{c}
. The positive-work condition and the second law imply
0<S<\eta_{C}
.
The solution of inequality (
8
) is

S_{-}(\tilde{P}_{0})\leq S\leq S_{+}(\tilde{P}_{0}),

(9)

where
S_{\pm}
are the two solutions of
F(S)=0
. This gives the allowed lower and upper values of
\sigma_{h}+\sigma_{c}
. Together with the condition
\sigma_{h},\sigma_{c}>0
, inequality (
9
) defines the isosceles trapezoidal region shown in Fig.
1
. We have so far used only the upper bound of
\sigma
. The lower bound of
\sigma
can further shrink the region, but it does not affect the upper and lower bounds of the efficiency. A detailed discussion of this point is given in Appendix
A
.

Figure 1:
Isosceles trapezoidal attainable region for
\sigma_{h}
and
\sigma_{c}
.

The above analysis shows that, for Carnot-like heat engines with arbitrary power-law dissipation, fixing the normalized power reduces the efficiency-bound problem to finding the bounds on the slope of a straight line that passes through a fixed point and intersects a trapezoidal region. From inequality (
9
), we obtain the power-efficiency constraint

\eta_{-}(\tilde{P}_{0})\leq\eta\leq\eta_{+}(\tilde{P}_{0}),

(10a)

where

\eta_{+}(\tilde{P}_{0})=\frac{\eta_{C}-S_{-}(\tilde{P}_{0})}{1-S_{-}(\tilde{P}_{0})},\quad\eta_{-}(\tilde{P}_{0})=\eta_{C}-S_{+}(\tilde{P}_{0}).

(10b)

A detailed derivation of this bound is provided in Appendix
A
.
It is also worth noting that this bound is consistent with the aggregate approach of Ref.
Ma and Fu 2025
; in particular, it can be recovered by further optimizing their result.
In particular, for
\tilde{P}_{0}=1
, namely at maximum power, the efficiency bounds can be obtained analytically as

\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\eta_{C}\leq\eta_{\text{MP}}\leq\frac{\alpha\eta_{C}}{1+\alpha-\eta_{C}}.

(11)

This result was previously obtained in
Wang and Tu 2012
.

IV
Exact Bounds for Certain Index

To obtain an explicit expression for the exact bound, one needs to solve the power equation

(S-\eta_{C})S^{\epsilon}+\frac{\eta_{C}^{1+\epsilon}\epsilon^{\epsilon}}{(1+\epsilon)^{1+\epsilon}}\tilde{P}_{0}=0.

(12)

In general, this equation does not admit a closed-form analytical solution. For special values of
\alpha
, or equivalently
\epsilon
, however, more explicit results can be obtained. In this section, we present the analytical power-efficiency constraints obtained by our method. Some of them recover existing results, while others are new.

For
\epsilon=1
, the finite-time dissipation is linear in
1/\tau
, namely
\Delta Q\propto 1/\tau
. In this case, the model reduces to the low-dissipation model
Esposito et al. 2010
;
Holubec and Ryabov 2016
;
Ryabov and Holubec 2016
;
Ma et al. 2018
, and we obtain

S_{\pm}(\tilde{P}_{0})=\frac{\eta_{C}}{2}\quantity(1 \pm\sqrt{1-\tilde{P}_0}).

(13)

Substituting this result into Eqs. (
10a
)-(
10b
), we recover the classical power-efficiency constraint for the low-dissipation model.

When the evolution of the working substance during the finite-time isothermal processes has long-tailed memory effects, or when critical slowing down is present, sublinear finite-time dissipation may arise. For Carnot-like heat engines, however, the exact power-efficiency constraint in this regime has received less attention.

For
\epsilon=2
, we have

S_{+}(\tilde{P}_{0})=\frac{\eta_{C}}{3}\quantity[
1 + 2\cos\qty(
\frac{1}{3}\arccos(1 - 2\tilde{P}_0)
)
],

(14)

and

S_{-}(\tilde{P}_{0})=\frac{\eta_{C}}{3}\quantity[
1 + 2\cos\qty(
\frac{2\uppi}{3} - \frac{1}{3}\arccos(1 - 2\tilde{P}_0)
)
].

(15)

For
\epsilon=3
, we have

S_{\pm}(\tilde{P}_{0})=\frac{\eta_{C}}{4}\quantity[
1 + \sqrt{u} \pm\sqrt{3 - u + \frac{2}{\sqrt{u}}}
],

(16)

where

u=1+\frac{3\tilde{P}_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}}{2}\quantity[
\qty(1 + \sqrt{1 - \tilde{P}_0})^{\frac{1}{3}}
+
\qty(1 - \sqrt{1 - \tilde{P}_0})^{\frac{1}{3}}
].

(17)

When
\alpha=2
, or equivalently
\epsilon=1/2
, the irreversibility is superlinear. We have

S_{-}(\tilde{P}_{0})=\frac{4\eta_{C}}{3}\cos^{2}\quantity[
\frac{1}{3} \arccos( - \tilde{P}_0)
- \frac{2\uppi}{3}
],

(18)

and

S_{+}(\tilde{P}_{0})=\frac{4\eta_{C}}{3}\cos^{2}\quantity[
\frac{1}{3} \arccos( - \tilde{P}_0)
].

(19)

Substituting these results into Eqs. (
10a
)-(
10b
) gives explicit power-efficiency constraints for different types of dissipation. For other values of
\epsilon
, Eq. (
12
) may not be solvable explicitly. Nevertheless, all cases share the same linear-programming structure.

V
Conclusion

The trade-off between power and efficiency in finite-time heat engines is a central problem in finite-time thermodynamics. In this work, we have reformulated the power-efficiency constraint of Carnot-like heat engines as a geometric linear-programming problem. This formulation not only provides a unified derivation of existing bounds for this class of heat engines, but also extends them to exact power-efficiency constraints for special dissipation exponents. In particular, for Carnot-like heat engines whose irreversible entropy production scales with the operation time as
1/t^{\alpha}
, our geometric method places the corresponding power-efficiency constraints in a unified framework. This method may also provide a useful route for optimizing heat engines with other nonlinear dissipation laws and other finite-time thermal machines, including refrigerators and heat pumps.

Linear Programming for Efficiency

In this appendix, we solve the linear-programming problem described in the main text.
The efficiency in Eq. (

\eta=1+\frac{(\eta_{C}-1)-\sigma_{c}}{1-\sigma_{h}}=1+\kappa,

where
\kappa
is the slope of the straight line passing through the point with
\sigma_{h}=1
and
\sigma_{c}=\eta_{C}-1<0
. We denote this point by
K
.

The attainable isosceles trapezoidal region is expressed as

S_{-}\leq\sigma_{h}+\sigma_{c}\leq S_{+},\quad\sigma_{h},\sigma_{c}>0.

It is straightforward to see that the point
K
lies outside this region. As shown in Fig.

\sigma_{h}^{+}=S_{-},\quad\sigma_{c}^{+}=0,

(22)

and the point giving the lower bound is

\sigma_{h}^{-}=0,\quad\sigma_{c}^{-}=S_{+}.

(23)

Substituting these points into the efficiency in Eq. (
