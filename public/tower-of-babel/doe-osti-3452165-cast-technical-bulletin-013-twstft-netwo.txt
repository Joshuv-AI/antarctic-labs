Conclusion and Future Work

In this experiment, a Fme transfer network was established over satellite, linking ﬁve diﬀerent
sites together. In this network, Fme was successfully transferred between several sites and the
accuracy of synchronizing the clocks at ORNL and INL together using received Fme was quanFﬁed.
When synchronizing these two sites, the oﬀset at any given Fme was no more than ﬁve
nanoseconds in magnitude over a period of approximately ten days. AddiFonally, when
comparing the Fme diﬀerence individually between ORNL/INL and the clock at NIST and the
clocks at the two Microchip sites, the plots were nearly the same, further demonstraFng that the
clocks at ORNL and INL are closely synchronized together. These results demonstrate that a
TWSTFT network can synchronize clocks at remote sites to an accuracy comparable to that of a
GNSS.

While these results demonstrate that clocks at diﬀerent sites can be symphonized over satellite
with an incredibly low discrepancy over a long Fme, there are several considerable caveats
associated with the TWSTFT network built for this experiment. Firstly, the required equipment is
expensive. The modem alone costs more than $50,000 and the network requires addiFonal
equipment to be fully operaFonal. This makes the overall cost of implemenFng a TWSTFT network
possibly cost prohibiFve when many sites must be synchronized. This network also requires
satellite bandwidth to be leased, which adds an addiFonal and considerable recurring cost. While
the individual cost per site for leased satellite bandwidth decreases linearly as sites are added to
the network, it remains a major cost that must be shouldered. These combined costs make
widespread adopFon of TWSTFT networks unlikely, and future work should focus on any possible
methods to reduce operaFng costs for equipment and/or satellite bandwidth. Finally, the ATS
6502 modem requires an iniFal GNSS connecFon to successfully iniFalize, even though it can
operate without a connecFon aber iniFalizaFon. While this is a trivial problem when GNSS are
abundant and fully funcFonal, any phenomena that degrades or destroys the funcFonal capacity
of GNSS may also prevent or limit the funcFon of a TWSTFT network unless the network has
