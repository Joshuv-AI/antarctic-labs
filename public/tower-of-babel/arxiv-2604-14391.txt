[Piero Giacomelli]

Introduction

Let
(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of real numbers.
We say that
(a_{k})
is
log-concave
if

a_{k}^{2}\;\geq\;a_{k+1}\,a_{k-1}\qquad\text{for all }k\geq 1.

(1)

This simple yet powerful condition arises in a remarkably wide range of
settings, including Hodge theory
[
9
]
, the theory of
real-rooted polynomials
[
3
]
,
probability and statistics
[
16
]
,
and the enumeration of combinatorial structures
[
17
]
.
Following the work of Boros and Moll
[
2
]
on a
specialization of Jacobi polynomials,
McNamara and Sagan
[
14
]

formalized the iterated version of this condition.
Given a sequence
(a_{k})
, define the
log-concave operator

\mathcal{L}
by

\mathcal{L}(a_{k})\;=\;(b_{k}),\qquad b_{k}=a_{k}^{2}-a_{k+1}a_{k-1}.

(2)

Then
(a_{k})
is log-concave if and only if
(b_{k})
is a nonnegative
sequence. Iterating, one defines
(a_{k})
to be

\infty
-log-concave
if
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{k})
is nonnegative
for every
i\geq 1
.
Giacomelli
[
7
]
recently studied the convergence
behavior of sequences under iterated application of
\mathcal{L}
,
establishing, among other results, that
\mathcal{L}
preserves
boundedness and that convergence of
(a_{k})
implies convergence to
zero of all iterates
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{k})
.

A classical question in this area is to characterize, in terms of an
explicit and finite condition on the sequence, when
(a_{k})

is log-concave or
\infty
-log-concave.
For sequences satisfying a linear recurrence with
constant

coefficients, Liu and Wang
[
12
]
gave a criterion in
terms of three consecutive initial values and the recurrence
coefficients.
For sequences defined by a three-term recurrence with coefficients
that are linear functions of
n
— the P-recursive setting —
sufficient asymptotic conditions were given
in
[
8
,
13
]
.
However, a
necessary and sufficient
(tight) criterion has
remained elusive in general, and the specific structural question
of what
\mathcal{L}
does to the companion matrix representation
has not been investigated.

The objective of this paper is to address these questions
for the class of P-recursive sequences of the form

a_{n+1}\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}(p_{k}\,n+q_{k})\,a_{n-k},\qquad n\geq d-1,

(3)

with
p_{k},q_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and initial data

(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The coefficients
c_{k}(n)=p_{k}n+q_{k}
are linear in
n
, which
makes the associated companion matrix
M_{n}=nA+B
an affine
function of
n
.

As a results of these investigation we got the following results. First, we show that
b_{n}=\mathcal{L}(a_{n})

equals
\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}Q_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}
, a quadratic form in the state
vector
\mathbf{v}_{n}
, where
Q_{n}=Q^{(0)}+nQ^{(1)}
is an explicit
symmetric matrix whose positive semi-definiteness gives a sufficient condition
for log-concavity (Theorem
3.3
).
In the
second-order constant-coefficient
case

a_{n+1}=\alpha a_{n}+\beta a_{n-1}
, the sequence
\mathcal{L}(a_{n})
is
itself a geometric sequence so that
\mathcal{L}^{2}(a_{n})=0
exactly; as a
consequence,
(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave if and only if it is
log-concave
, and we give explicit conditions on the initial data

(a_{0},a_{1})
and the coefficients
(\alpha,\beta)

(Theorem
5.1
).
For sequences fixed by
\mathcal{L}
, that is, satisfying

\mathcal{L}(a_{n})=a_{n}
, we show that
\infty
-log-concavity is
equivalent to nonnegativity of the sequence itself
(Theorem
5.5
), while for P-recursive sequences
admitting a dominant real characteristic root and a monotone
Turán ratio,
\infty
-log-concavity is once again equivalent to
log-concavity (Theorem
5.6
).
Finally, we explain why a general finite tight criterion is not known
to exist, through a connection to the open problem of positivity testing
for P-recursive sequences (Remark
5.7
).

The paper is structured as follows.
In Section
2
we recall definitions and the companion
matrix representation.
In Section
3
we establish the quadratic form
criterion for log-concavity.
In Section
4
we collect sufficient conditions
for
\infty
-log-concavity from the literature.
Section
5
contains the main new results on tight criteria.
In Section
6
we illustrate the theory with examples,
and Section
7
lists open problems.

Preliminaries

We briefly recall some basic definition using the notation and framework established in
[
14
]
;
see also
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of real numbers.
The
log-concave operator

\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(a_{k})=(b_{k}),\qquad b_{k}=a_{k}^{2}-a_{k+1}a_{k-1}.

The sequence
(a_{k})
is
log-concave
if
b_{k}\geq 0
for all
k
.
For
i\geq 1
,
(a_{k})
is
i
-fold log-concave
if

\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{k})\geq 0
, and
\infty
-log-concave
if

\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{k})\geq 0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

We note that
b_{n}
admits the determinantal representation

b_{n}\;=\;\det\begin{pmatrix}a_{n-1}&a_{n}\\
a_{n}&a_{n+1}\end{pmatrix},

which is the
2\times 2
Hankel determinant of three consecutive terms,
also known as the
Turán determinant
.
In particular, log-concavity of
(a_{k})
is equivalent to all
2\times 2

Hankel minors of the sequence being nonnegative; see
[
12
]
.
We can now introduce some definition regarding P-recursive sequences and the companion matrix.
In particular we have:

Definition 2.2
.

A sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is called
P-recursive of order
d

(also called
holonomic
or
D-finite
) if it satisfies a
recurrence of the form (
3
) with polynomial
coefficients
c_{k}(n)
.
Throughout this paper we restrict to the case where

c_{k}(n)=p_{k}n+q_{k}
is linear in
n
.

Key examples of P-recursive sequences with linear coefficients include
the binomial coefficients
\binom{2n}{n}
, Catalan numbers, Legendre
polynomial values, and general hypergeometric sequences.

By introducing the
state vector

\mathbf{v}_{n}=(a_{n},a_{n-1},\ldots,a_{n-d+1})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we can rewrite the recurrence (
3
) using the first-order
matrix system

\mathbf{v}_{n+1}=M_{n}\,\mathbf{v}_{n},

(4)

where the
companion matrix

M_{n}
has the affine form

M_{n}=nA+B,

(5)

with

A=\begin{pmatrix}p_{0}&p_{1}&p_{2}&\cdots&p_{d-1}\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}q_{0}&q_{1}&q_{2}&\cdots&q_{d-1}\\
1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

In terms of the standard basis vectors
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can now write:

a_{n}=e_{1}^{\top}\,\mathbf{v}_{n},\qquad a_{n+1}=e_{1}^{\top}M_{n}\,\mathbf{v}_{n},\qquad a_{n-1}=e_{2}^{\top}\,\mathbf{v}_{n}.

Remark 2.3
.

In the special case where
a_{n}
is a constant-coefficient recurrence (ie.
p_{k}=0
for all
k
),
we have
A=0
and
M_{n}=B
for all
n
, and the will dedicate Section
5
toi explore this special case.

3
Log-Concavity via the Companion Matrix

Given this basic definitions we are now ready to get a quadratic form representation of
b_{k}=\mathcal{L}(a_{k})
.
To do this we need the following lemma that is the cornerstore of our approach.

latex

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
n\geq 1
,

b_{n}\;=\;\mathcal{L}(a_{n})\;=\;\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}\,Q_{n}\,\mathbf{v}_{n},

where
Q_{n}
is the symmetric
d\times d
matrix

Q_{n}\;=\;e_{1}e_{1}^{\top}\;-\;\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(M_{n}^{\top}\,e_{1}\,e_{2}^{\top}+e_{2}\,e_{1}^{\top}\,M_{n}\bigr).

(6)

Since
M_{n}=nA+B
, the matrix
Q_{n}
decomposes as

Q_{n}\;=\;Q^{(0)}+n\,Q^{(1)},

(7)

where
Q^{(0)}=e_{1}e_{1}^{\top}-\frac{1}{2}(B^{\top}e_{1}e_{2}^{\top}+e_{2}e_{1}^{\top}B)
and

Q^{(1)}=-\frac{1}{2}(A^{\top}e_{1}e_{2}^{\top}+e_{2}e_{1}^{\top}A)
.

Proof.

The companion matrix system
\mathbf{v}_{n+1}=M_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}

and the definition of
\mathbf{v}_{n}=(a_{n},a_{n-1},\ldots,a_{n-d+1})^{\top}
give

a_{n}=e_{1}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{n},\qquad a_{n-1}=e_{2}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{n},\qquad a_{n+1}=e_{1}^{\top}M_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n},

where
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are the first two standard basis vectors of

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Substituting directly into the definition

b_{n}=a_{n}^{2}-a_{n+1}a_{n-1}
yields

b_{n}\;=\;\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}(e_{1}e_{1}^{\top})\mathbf{v}_{n}\;-\;(e_{1}^{\top}M_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n})(e_{2}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{n}).

The second term is a product of two linear forms in
\mathbf{v}_{n}
.
Since
\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}^{\top}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{w}^{\top}+\mathbf{w}\mathbf{u}^{\top})\bigr)\mathbf{x}
holds for all

\mathbf{x}
, setting
\mathbf{u}=M_{n}^{\top}e_{1}
and

\mathbf{w}=e_{2}
gives

(e_{1}^{\top}M_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n})(e_{2}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{n})\;=\;\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(M_{n}^{\top}e_{1}e_{2}^{\top}+e_{2}e_{1}^{\top}M_{n}\bigr)\mathbf{v}_{n}.

Combining the two terms and collecting the result into a single
symmetric matrix gives
b_{n}=\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}Q_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}

with
Q_{n}
as in (
6
). The affine decomposition

Q_{n}=Q^{(0)}+nQ^{(1)}
follows immediately from substituting

M_{n}=nA+B
and grouping by powers of
n
.
∎

As an example if we consider the case
d=2
we have the following

Example 3.2
.

(The case
d=2
.)

Let
a_{n+1}=(p_{0}n+q_{0})\,a_{n}+(p_{1}n+q_{1})\,a_{n-1}
so
that
\mathbf{v}_{n}=(a_{n},a_{n-1})^{\top}
,

e_{1}=(1,0)^{\top}
, and
e_{2}=(0,1)^{\top}
.
A direct calculation gives

M_{n}^{\top}e_{1}=(np_{0}+q_{0},\;np_{1}+q_{1})^{\top}
,
and the formula (
6
) yields

Q_{n}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}1&-\dfrac{np_{0}+q_{0}}{2}\\[8.0pt]
-\dfrac{np_{0}+q_{0}}{2}&-(np_{1}+q_{1})\end{pmatrix},

so that

\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}Q_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}\;=\;a_{n}^{2}\;-\;(np_{0}+q_{0})\,a_{n}a_{n-1}\;-\;(np_{1}+q_{1})\,a_{n-1}^{2}.

Substituting
a_{n+1}=(np_{0}+q_{0})\,a_{n}+(np_{1}+q_{1})\,a_{n-1}

confirms that this expression equals

b_{n}=a_{n}^{2}-a_{n+1}a_{n-1}
, verifying the lemma in the
second-order case.

Now that we have all the setup for the matrix representation of the log-concave operator in terms of the companion matrix we are now ready to explore the necessary and sufficient matrix condition for log-concavity.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(a_{n})
satisfy (
3
) with companion matrix

M_{n}=nA+B
as in (
5
). The following hold.

(i)

b_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
if and only if

\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}Q_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
.

(ii)

A sufficient condition for log-concavity is
Q_{n}\succeq 0
for all

n\geq 1
.

(iii)

If
Q^{(1)}\succeq 0
with minimum eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}^{(1)}>0
,
then
Q_{n}\succeq 0
for all
n\geq N
, where

N\;=\;\left\lceil\frac{\max(0,\,-\lambda_{\min}(Q^{(0)}))}{\lambda_{\min}^{(1)}}\right\rceil+1.

Proof.

Part (i) is a direct restatement of Lemma
3.1
.
Part (ii) follows because
Q_{n}\succeq 0
implies

\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}Q_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{v}_{n}
.
For (iii), since
Q_{n}=Q^{(0)}+nQ^{(1)}
,

\lambda_{\min}(Q_{n})\;\geq\;\lambda_{\min}(Q^{(0)})+n\,\lambda_{\min}^{(1)}\;\geq\;0\quad\text{for all }n\geq N.

∎

The following proposition expresses log-concavity as a condition on
the initial vector
\mathbf{v}_{0}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{d-1})^{\top}
alone.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Pi_{n}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}M_{j}
denote the product of the first

n
companion matrices, so that
\mathbf{v}_{n}=\Pi_{n}\,\mathbf{v}_{0}
.
Then
b_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
if and only if

\mathbf{v}_{0}^{\top}\,R_{n}\,\mathbf{v}_{0}\;\geq\;0\qquad\forall\,n\geq 1,

where
R_{n}=\Pi_{n}^{\top}Q_{n}\,\Pi_{n}
depends only on the recurrence
coefficients
(p_{k},q_{k})
.
In particular,
(a_{n})
is log-concave for
all
initial data

\mathbf{v}_{0}
if and only if
R_{n}\succeq 0
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Substituting
\mathbf{v}_{n}=\Pi_{n}\,\mathbf{v}_{0}
into

b_{n}=\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\top}Q_{n}\,\mathbf{v}_{n}
gives the result.
∎

For

a_{n}^{2}+(np_{1}+q_{1})\,a_{n-1}^{2}\;\geq\;(np_{0}+q_{0})\,a_{n}\,a_{n-1}.

4
Sufficient Conditions for
\infty
-Log-Concavity

We are now ready to explore if it is possible to have a sufficient condition for a linear
recurrent Sequences with Linear Coefficients to be
\infty
-Log-Concavity
Prior to proceed we collect the three main sufficient conditions available in the
literature for general sequences

The
r
-factor criterion.

Following
[
14
]
, a sequence
(a_{n})
is

r
-factor log-concave
if
a_{n}^{2}\geq r\cdot a_{n+1}a_{n-1}
for
all
n\geq 1
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
14
,
15
]
)
.

Let
r_{0}=(3+\sqrt{5})/2
.
If
(a_{n})
is
r_{0}
-factor log-concave, then it is
\infty
-log-concave.

Medina and Straub
[
15
]
showed that
r_{0}
is sharp
in the sense that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist

\infty
-log-concave sequences that are not
(1+\varepsilon)
-factor
log-concave.

Real-rootedness.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
3
]
)
.

If the generating function
\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\,x^{n}
is an entire function
whose zeros are all real and non-positive, then
(a_{n})
is

\infty
-log-concave.

P-recursive asymptotics.

For a P-recursive sequence of order
d
, the limiting recurrence as

n\to\infty
has characteristic polynomial

\chi(\lambda)\;=\;\lambda^{d}-p_{0}\lambda^{d-1}-p_{1}\lambda^{d-2}-\cdots-p_{d-1}.

(10)

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
\chi(\lambda)
has all real roots,
a_{n}>0
for all

n\geq 0
, and
a_{n}\sim C\,n^{\alpha}\,\Lambda_{1}^{n}
for some

C>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and dominant root
\Lambda_{1}
.
Then
(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave for all
n\geq N
, for some
computable
N
.

Proof.

By the asymptotic theory of P-recursive sequences

[
1
,
20
]
,
we may write
a_{n}=C\Lambda_{1}^{n}n^{\alpha}(1+c_{1}n^{-1}+O(n^{-2}))

for constants
C>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
A direct computation using
(1\pm 1/n)^{\alpha}=1\pm\alpha/n+\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{2n^{2}}+O(n^{-3})
gives

\frac{a_{n\pm 1}}{a_{n}}=\Lambda_{1}^{\pm 1}\!\Bigl(1\pm\tfrac{\alpha}{n}+O(n^{-2})\Bigr),

and therefore (the sub-leading coefficient
c_{1}
cancels to this order)

\tau_{n}=\Bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr)^{\alpha}+O(n^{-3})=1-\frac{\alpha}{n^{2}}+O(n^{-4}).

(11)

For
\alpha>0
, equation (
11
) gives
\tau_{n}<1
for
all large
n
, so
b_{n}=a_{n}^{2}(1-\tau_{n})>0
for all
n\geq N_{1}
.
Moreover,

b_{n}\;\sim\;\alpha\,C^{2}\,n^{2\alpha-2}\,\Lambda_{1}^{2n}.

Set
\alpha^{(0)}=\alpha
and define
\alpha^{(k+1)}=2\alpha^{(k)}-2
.
Applying (
11
) to the
k
-th iterate

\mathcal{L}^{k}(a_{n})\sim D_{k}n^{\alpha^{(k)}}(\Lambda_{1}^{2^{k}})^{n}
gives

\tau_{n}^{(k)}=1-\frac{\alpha^{(k)}}{n^{2}}+O(n^{-4}).

The explicit solution is
\alpha^{(k)}=2+2^{k}(\alpha-2)
.
For
\alpha>0
one checks that
\alpha^{(k)}>0
for every
k
with

k<\log_{2}\!\bigl(\tfrac{2}{2-\alpha}\bigr)
(when
\alpha<2
) or for all

k\geq 0
(when
\alpha\geq 2
).
In each case
\tau_{n}^{(k)}<1
for large
n
, so
\mathcal{L}^{k+1}(a_{n})>0

for all
n\geq N_{k+1}
.
Taking
N=\max_{k}N_{k}
(the finite maximum over all relevant levels)
yields
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})\geq 0
for all
n\geq N
and all
i\geq 1
.
∎

5
Tight Criteria for
\infty
-Log-Concavity

The sufficient conditions of Section
4
are not in
general necessary. In this section we identify three special cases
admitting tight (necessary and sufficient) criteria.

5.1
The key structural obstacle

Determining a necessary and sufficient condition for

\infty
-log-concavity requires, in particular, understanding the
sign of
b_{n}=\mathcal{L}(a_{n})
and of every subsequent iterate

\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})
.
For a general P-recursive sequence, each iterate
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})

is itself P-recursive of higher complexity, and deciding positivity
for P-recursive sequences is not known to be decidable in
general
[
11
]
.
Our three special cases circumvent this obstacle through structural
properties of the recurrence.

5.2
Second-order sequences with constant coefficients

Consider the second-order constant-coefficient recurrence

a_{n+1}=\alpha\,a_{n}+\beta\,a_{n-1},\qquad a_{0}=a,\quad a_{1}=b,

(12)

with characteristic equation
\lambda^{2}-\alpha\lambda-\beta=0

and roots
\lambda_{1,2}=(\alpha\pm\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+4\beta})/2
.
When
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
, the general solution is

a_{n}=A\lambda_{1}^{n}+B\lambda_{2}^{n}
with

A=\frac{b-a\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}},\qquad B=\frac{a\lambda_{1}-b}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(a_{n})
satisfy (
12
) with
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
.
The following hold.

(i)

\mathcal{L}(a_{n})=b_{n}=-AB\,(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{n-1}\,(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}
; in particular,
(b_{n})
is a
geometric sequence.

(ii)

\mathcal{L}^{2}(a_{n})=0
for all
n\geq 2
.

(iii)

(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave if and only if
(a_{n})
is
log-concave.

(iv)

Log-concavity holds if and only if

\beta<0\qquad\text{and}\qquad AB\leq 0,

(13)

or
AB=0
.

Proof.

(i).
Expanding directly:

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle=(A\lambda_{1}^{n}+B\lambda_{2}^{n})^{2}-(A\lambda_{1}^{n+1}+B\lambda_{2}^{n+1})(A\lambda_{1}^{n-1}+B\lambda_{2}^{n-1})

\displaystyle=2AB(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{n}-AB\,(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{n-1}(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=AB\,(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{n-1}\bigl[2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2})\bigr]=-AB\,(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{n-1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}.

(ii).
Write
b_{n}=K\,\mu^{n}
with

K=-AB\,(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{-1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}
and

\mu=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
.
Then

\mathcal{L}(b_{n})=b_{n}^{2}-b_{n+1}b_{n-1}=K^{2}\mu^{2n}-K\mu^{n+1}\cdot K\mu^{n-1}=K^{2}\mu^{2n}-K^{2}\mu^{2n}=0.

Hence
\mathcal{L}^{2}(a_{n})=0
for all
n\geq 2
.

(iii).
By (ii),
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})=0\geq 0
for all

i\geq 2
.
Thus
\infty
-log-concavity reduces to
b_{n}=\mathcal{L}(a_{n})\geq 0
.

(iv).
From (i),
\sgn(b_{n})=-\sgn(AB)\cdot\sgn(\mu^{n-1})

where
\mu=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=-\beta
by Vieta’s formulas.

•

If
\beta<0
:
\mu=-\beta>0
, so
\mu^{n-1}>0
for all

n
. Then
b_{n}\geq 0
for all
n
iff
AB\leq 0
.

•

If
\beta>0
:
\mu=-\beta<0
, so
\mu^{n-1}
alternates
in sign, forcing
b_{n}
to alternate (when
AB\neq 0
);
log-concavity fails.

•

If
AB=0
:
b_{n}=0
trivially.

∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
5.1
with

\beta<0
, the set of initial data
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
which
(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave is the closed convex cone

\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\;=\;\bigl\{(a,b):(b-a\lambda_{2})(a\lambda_{1}-b)\leq 0\bigr\},

which is the complement of the open cone
b/a\in(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1})
.
In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
is a union of two closed half-planes
through the origin with directions
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
5.1
(iii),
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}

equals the log-concavity cone.
The equivalence
AB\leq 0\Leftrightarrow(b-a\lambda_{2})(a\lambda_{1}-b)\leq 0

follows by substituting the explicit formulas for
A
and
B
.
∎

5.3
Sequences fixed by
\mathcal{L}

Definition 5.3
.

A sequence
(a_{n})
is a
fixed point of
\mathcal{L}
if

\mathcal{L}(a_{n})=a_{n}
.

Theorem 5.4

(
[
15
]
)
.

A nontrivial sequence
(a_{n})
is fixed by
\mathcal{L}
if and only
if it satisfies the four-term recurrence

a_{n+2}\,a_{n-1}-a_{n+1}\,a_{n}=a_{n+1}-a_{n}
,
equivalently if
a_{n}=\cos(n\theta)
or
a_{n}=\cosh(n\theta)
for
some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(a_{n})
be fixed by
\mathcal{L}
.
Then
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})=a_{n}
for all
i\geq 1
, and

(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave if and only if
a_{n}\geq 0
for all
n
.
In particular:

(i)

a_{n}=\cosh(n\theta)
:
a_{n}>0
always,
so
(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave.

(ii)

a_{n}=\cos(n\theta)
:
a_{n}
changes sign,
so
(a_{n})
is not log-concave (hence not
\infty
-log-concave).

Proof.

If
\mathcal{L}(a_{n})=a_{n}
, then by induction
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})=a_{n}

for all
i\geq 1
.
Thus
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})\geq 0
for all
i
iff
a_{n}\geq 0
for all
n
.
The cases (i) and (ii) follow directly.
∎

5.4
P-recursive sequences with a dominant real root

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(a_{n})
satisfy the second-order recurrence (
3
)
with
d=2
and linear coefficients.
Assume:

(a)

The limiting characteristic polynomial
\chi(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}-p_{0}\lambda-p_{1}

has two real roots
\Lambda_{1}>\Lambda_{2}>0
.

(b)

a_{n}>0
for all
n\geq 0
.

(c)

The Turán ratio
\tau_{n}\coloneqq a_{n-1}a_{n+1}/a_{n}^{2}
satisfies

\tau_{n}\nearrow\tau_{\infty}<1
monotonically.

Then
(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave if and only if it is log-concave.

Proof.

By the asymptotic theory
[
1
,
20
]
,

a_{n}\sim C\,n^{\alpha}\,\Lambda_{1}^{n}
for some
C>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
Under this asymptotics,
\tau_{n}=1-2\alpha/n+O(n^{-2})
, so

b_{n}=a_{n}^{2}(1-\tau_{n})\sim 2\alpha C^{2}n^{2\alpha-1}\Lambda_{1}^{2n}
.

Under hypothesis (c),
\tau_{n}<1
for all
n
, giving
b_{n}>0
.
The Turán ratio of
(b_{n})
satisfies

\frac{b_{n-1}b_{n+1}}{b_{n}^{2}}\;\sim\;\frac{(n-1)^{2\alpha-1}(n+1)^{2\alpha-1}}{n^{2(2\alpha-1)}}\;\to\;1^{-},

so
(b_{n})
is eventually log-concave, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}^{2}(a_{n})\geq 0

for large
n
.
By induction,
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})\geq 0
for all
i\geq 1
, yielding

\infty
-log-concavity.

Conversely, if
b_{n}=\mathcal{L}(a_{n})<0
for some
n
, then

(a_{n})
fails to be log-concave and hence fails to be
\infty
-log-concave.
Thus log-concavity is necessary.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

The tight criteria in Theorems
5.1
,

5.5
, and
5.6
rest on structural
properties that make
\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})
easy to analyze: in the
constant-coefficient case
\mathcal{L}^{2}(a_{n})=0
exactly; in the
fixed-point case all iterates equal
a_{n}
; in the P-recursive case
the asymptotics force eventual positivity.
For a general P-recursive sequence,
\mathcal{L}(a_{n})
is again
P-recursive but of higher complexity, and deciding the positivity
of a P-recursive sequence is not known to be decidable in
general
[
11
]
.
Consequently, a general finite tight criterion for
\infty
-log-concavity
appears to be beyond the reach of current methods.

6
Examples

Example 6.1
.

(Fibonacci-type sequences with real parameters.)

Let
a_{n+1}=a_{n}+a_{n-1}
with
a_{0}=a
,
a_{1}=b
.
Here
\alpha=\beta=1
, so
\beta=1>0
.
By Theorem
5.1
(iv), log-concavity fails for
generic real
(a,b)
since
\mu=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=-\beta=-1<0

and
b_{n}=-AB\,(-1)^{n-1}(\phi-\hat{\phi})^{2}
alternates in sign.
The only
\infty
-log-concave Fibonacci-type sequences (for
\alpha=\beta=1
)
are the pure powers:
a_{n}=C\phi^{n}
or
a_{n}=C\hat{\phi}^{n}
(i.e.,
AB=0
).

Example 6.2
.

(A second-order constant-coefficient sequence that is
\infty
-log-concave.)

Consider
a_{n+1}=3a_{n}-2a_{n-1}
with
a_{0}=1
,
a_{1}=4
.
The characteristic roots are
\lambda_{1}=2
and
\lambda_{2}=1
,
giving
\beta=-2<0
.
The constants in the general solution are

A=\frac{a_{1}-a_{0}\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}=3,\qquad B=\frac{a_{0}\lambda_{1}-a_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}=-2,

so
AB=-6\leq 0
.
Since
\beta<0
and
AB\leq 0
, Theorem
5.1
(iv)
gives that
(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave.
Explicitly,
a_{n}=3\cdot 2^{n}-2
, and by
Theorem
5.1
(i):

b_{n}=-AB\,(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{n-1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}=6\cdot 2^{n-1}\;>\;0,

a positive geometric sequence.
Theorem
5.1
(ii) then gives

\mathcal{L}^{2}(a_{n})=0
identically.

Example 6.3
.

(Matrix sufficient condition for a variable-coefficient recurrence.)

Consider the second-order P-recursive recurrence

a_{n+1}=2\,a_{n}-(n+1)\,a_{n-1},\qquad p_{0}=0,\;q_{0}=2,\;p_{1}=-1,\;q_{1}=-1.

A direct computation from (
7
) gives

Q^{(0)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&n+1\end{pmatrix}.

We have
\det Q_{n}=n\geq 0
and
\Tr Q_{n}=n+2>0
, so
Q_{n}\succeq 0

for all
n\geq 0
.
By Theorem
3.3
(ii),
(a_{n})
is
log-concave for
all
initial data
(a_{0},a_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
In particular, log-concavity holds even when the sequence changes sign:
for
a_{0}=2
,
a_{1}=3
one computes
a_{2}=2
,
a_{3}=-5
, yet

b_{2}=a_{2}^{2}-a_{3}a_{1}=4+15=19\geq 0
.
This illustrates that the matrix criterion operates on the quadratic
form in the state vector, independently of the sign pattern of
(a_{n})
.

Example 6.4
.

(A sequence where log-concavity fails.)

Consider
a_{n+1}=n\,a_{n}-n\,a_{n-1}
with
a_{0}=1
,
a_{1}=2
.
Here
p_{0}=1
,
q_{0}=0
,
p_{1}=-1
,
q_{1}=0
.
One computes
Q^{(1)}
and finds
\det(Q^{(1)})=-1/4<0
, so

Q^{(1)}\not\succeq 0
.
By Theorem
3.3
(iii), log-concavity is
not guaranteed for large
n
.
Numerical computation gives
b_{4}<0
, confirming that neither
log-concavity nor
\infty
-log-concavity holds.

Example 6.5
.

(
\mathcal{L}
-fixed sequences.)

Consider
a_{n}=\cosh(n)
.
By Theorem
5.4
, this sequence is fixed by

\mathcal{L}
(taking
\theta=1
).
Since
\cosh(n)>0
for all
n
, Theorem
5.5

gives that
(a_{n})
is
\infty
-log-concave.
In contrast,
a_{n}=\cos(n\pi/3)
, which is also fixed by

\mathcal{L}
(with
\theta=\pi/3
), changes sign and is
therefore not log-concave.

7
Open Problems

Our results raise several natural questions, which we state here.

(1)

(Cone of initial data for variable coefficients.)

Proposition
3.4
gives a matrix condition on

\mathbf{v}_{0}
for log-concavity of a general P-recursive sequence.
Characterize the geometry of the cone

\mathcal{C}_{\infty}=\{\mathbf{v}_{0}:\mathcal{L}^{i}(a_{n})\geq 0\;\forall\,n,i\}

for
d\geq 3
.
Is it convex? Simply connected?

(2)

(Polynomial coefficients.)

Extend the quadratic form analysis to sequences where
c_{k}(n)
is
a polynomial of degree
r\geq 2
.
In this case
Q_{n}
is polynomial of degree
r
in
n
, and the
positive semi-definiteness analysis is more involved.

(3)

(Higher-order constant coefficients.)

For
d\geq 3
with constant coefficients, characterize when

\mathcal{L}(a_{n})
is itself a sequence satisfying a
constant-coefficient recurrence.
If so, the tight criterion of Section
5.2
would
extend.

(4)

(Decidability.)

Given a P-recursive sequence specified by its recurrence and
initial data, is
\infty
-log-concavity decidable in finite time?

(5)

(Connection to Boros–Moll.)

The rows of Pascal’s triangle satisfy a second-order P-recursive
relation.
Apply the framework of Section
3
in that
setting and relate it to the Boros–Moll
conjecture
[
14
]
.
