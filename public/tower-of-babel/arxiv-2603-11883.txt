Finiteness of non-decomposable critically 4 and 5-frustrated signed graphs
Zhiqian Wang

Abstract
A signed graph ( G , σ ) (G,\sigma) is a graph G G with a signature σ \sigma labeling each edge with a positive or negative sign. Two signatures of G G are switching equivalent if one is obtained from the other by changing the signs of all edges in an edge-cut. The frustration index of a signed graph ( G , σ ) (G,\sigma) is the minimum number of negative edges among all signatures equivalent to σ \sigma . A signed graph is critically k k -frustrated if it has frustration index k k , and the removal of any edge decreases its frustration index. A critically k k -frustrated signed graph is prime if it has no subdivided edge (including multiedge) and none of its subgraphs is the edge-disjoint union of critically frustrated signed graphs. Steffen and Naserasr et al. conjectured that for any positive integer k k , there are finitely many prime critically k k -frustrated signed graphs. The cases k = 1 , 2 , 3 k=1,2,3 have been proved to be true recently by Cappello et al.. In this paper, we show that the conjecture holds when k = 4 k=4 and 5 5 .

2.2
Finiteness of boundary faces

In this part, we will exploit the properties of small cuts we have proposed in the last subsection to restrict the cardinality of boundary faces in canonical embeddings. For the convenience of analysis, we first standardize the canonical embedding of a signed graph in
\mathcal{S}^{*}(k)
. We say a canonical embedding is

Proposition 2.10
.

For
k\geq 4
, every signed graph
(G,\sigma)\in S^{*}(k)
admits a normal embedding up to switching equivalence.

Proof.

For
(G,\sigma)\in S^{*}(k)
, we arbitrarily fix a canonical embedding of it. Let
F
be a bridge face if it truly exists, and denote the
C_{4}
-face adjacent to

by

according to Proposition

. Let the two vertices of
\mathcal{R}
on

be

and
x_{2}
without loss of generality. We switch at the equilibrated cut
\partial_{G}(\{x_{1},x_{2}\})
, and as explained in the proof of Corollary

, it is equivalent to clipping off the

formed by

on the plane and then move it to the other side inversely.

We assert that in the resulting canonical embedding, the number of bridge faces has been strictly decreased by the switching. If not, then the only possible case is that the antipodals of

and
x_{2}

y_{1}
and
y_{2}
are on the boundary cycle of another boundary face, and furthermore,

induces a

enclosing a face embedded in the cross cap, denoted by

. Consequently, the two adjacent

-faces

and

generate a cut consisting of four edges in

So starting from the initial canonical embedding, by sequentially switching at the

-cuts formed by

Recall that a

of an integer

Cyclic compositions
of an integer

. By Lemma

2.3
Transmission of finiteness

For

(k\geq 4)

Lemma 2.13
.

Every internal face is adjacent to at least two boundary faces.

Proof.

The number of positive edges in an equilibrated cut containing the common edge of two internal faces is at least five, so the claim is obvious when

. We only consider the case

must include at least six positive edges, which is impossible for a signed graph in

Arbitrarily take an internal face

, and let

be a boundary face adjacent to it. Since

is cubic,

and

and

and

Notice that

In the end, it remains to determine a certain value for the upper bound

corresponds to a cyclic composition of

[

, the generating function of cyclic composition number was derived by Hadjicostas.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and
c_{A}(n)
be the number of cyclic compositions of

such that every integer in the sum is contained in

. The generating function of

is

\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}c_{A}(n)x^{n}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\frac{\phi(n)}{n}\log\frac{1}{1-\sum\limits_{s\in A}x^{sn}},

where
\phi(n)
is Euler’s totient function at
n
.

Set
A=\{1,2\}
, and apply Taylor series
\log(1+x)=\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{i}x^{i}
to the generating function, then we get the equation

\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}c_{A}(n)x^{n}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}\frac{\phi(n)}{n}\cdot\frac{x^{ni}(1+x^{n})^{i}}{i}.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, comparing the coefficients of
x^{k}
on both sides, we have

c_{A}(k)=\sum\limits_{d|k}\sum\limits_{j=0}\frac{\phi(d)\binom{\frac{k}{d}-j}{j}}{k-dj}.

Taking
0
s into consideration, by Proposition
2.11
, we set

when

and
M=3\cdot 4^{5}
when
k=5
.

By the construction of

is exactly the value
|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|
in the normal embedding of
(G,\sigma)
. We finally prove the finiteness of
\mathcal{S}^{*}(4)
and
\mathcal{S}^{*}(5)
by showing that the face number of the members is limited.
