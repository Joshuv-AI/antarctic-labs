Thermodynamic Formalism for Quasimorphisms: Lattices in Higher Rank Semisimple Lie Groups
Pablo D. Carrasco, Federico Rodríguez-Hertz

Abstract
. We give a proof, based on thermodynamic formalism, of a theorem in bounded cohomology extending a foundational result of Burger and Monod: if Γ \Gamma is an irreducible uniform lattice in a non-compact connected semisimple Lie group of real rank at least 2 2 , then for any finite-dimensional representation π : Γ → O N \pi:\Gamma\to\On_{N} , every π \pi -quasimorphism (that is, a map with bounded defect with respect to π \pi ) is bounded.



1.
Introduction

For certain classes of groups
\Gamma
, we are interested in understanding their bounded cohomology. Fix an orthogonal representation
\pi:\Gamma\to\On_{N}
, let

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{b}}(\Gamma^{n},\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{f:\Gamma^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N},f\text{ bounded}\}

\displaystyle n\geq 1

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{b}}(\Gamma^{0},\mathbb{R}^{N})=\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle n=0

and
d_{\pi}^{n}:C_{\mathrm{b}}(\Gamma^{n-1},\mathbb{R}^{N})\to C_{\mathrm{b}}(\Gamma^{n},\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\displaystyle d_{\pi}^{n}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\pi(x_{1})f(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}).

It follows that
d_{\pi}^{n+1}\circ d_{\pi}^{n}=0
; the bounded cohomology of
\Gamma
(with coefficients in
\pi
) is the cohomology of the cocomplex

\left\{H^{n}_{\mathrm{b},\pi}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\frac{\ker d_{\pi}^{n+1}}{\mathrm{Im}d_{\pi}^{n}}\right\}.

The inclusion
C_{\mathrm{b}}(\Gamma^{n},\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset C(\Gamma^{n},\mathbb{R}^{N})\coloneqq\{f:\Gamma^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}\}
induces a natural map, called the comparison map,
c_{n}:H^{n}_{\mathrm{b},\pi}\to H^{n}_{\pi}
, where the last space corresponds to the regular group cohomology (with coefficients given by
\pi
). For
n=2
the vector space
H^{2}_{\mathrm{b},\pi}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Banach space; this is the most interesting and studied case, and it is the one that we are focusing on here.

Note that
\Ker c_{2}
measures the additional information carried by
H^{2}_{\mathrm{b},\pi}
compared to its algebraic counterpart
H^{2}_{\pi}
. We mention some relevant cases:

•

when
\Gamma
is amenable, then
\Ker c_{2}=\{0\}
;

•

when
\Gamma
is Gromov hyperbolic and
\pi
is trivial, then
\Ker c_{2}
is infinite dimensional;

•

when
\Gamma
is an irreducible cocompact lattice in a semisimple Lie group
G
of rank
\geq 2
and
\pi
is trivial, then
\ker c_{2}=\{0\}
.

The first result is an almost direct consequence of the definitions and was originally observed by Johnson
[
1
]
. The second is more subtle: it was first proved by Brooks and Series
[
2
]
, and subsequently refined by various authors (
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
). In fact, using dynamical systems techniques, we recently obtained a complete characterization of the space
\Ker c_{2}

[
6
]
, which in particular allows one to derive precise asymptotics for (untwisted) quasimorphisms. The third is a delicate result due to Burger and Monod
[
7
]
(see also
[
8
]
), and is the main focus of this paper.

A central part of their proof consists in relating the second bounded cohomology group of
\Gamma
to the continuous bounded cohomology of the ambient group
G
, with coefficients in the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
L^{2}\!\left(G\backslash\Gamma\right)
. Our contribution is to provide a new proof of their theorem, based on a dynamical approach that applies to arbitrary finite-dimensional orthogonal representations.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a non-compact connected real semisimple Lie group of rank at least
2
, and let
\Gamma<G
be an irreducible uniform lattice. Let
\pi:\Gamma\to\On_{N}
be an orthogonal representation. Then every
\pi
-quasimorphism
L:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is bounded. Equivalently,

\Ker\left(c_{2}:H^{2}_{\mathrm{b},\pi}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})\to H^{2}_{\pi}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)=\{0\}.

In the case of trivial coefficients, this recovers the classical result of Burger and Monod.

The work of Burger and Monod mentioned above is foundational in the theory, and the result stated here, while an important component, represents only part of their broader framework. Nevertheless, our approach is based on a different philosophy (see below), and we expect that it can be adapted to other settings. Techniques of this type have already proven very effective in the rank one case
[
6
]
.

1.1.
Skeleton of the proof

From now on we assume that
G
is a connected semisimple Lie group which is not compact (although it may have compact factors), with real rank
r\geq 2
, and
\Gamma<G
is a uniform irreducible lattice. By this we mean the following.

Definition 1.1
.

The lattice
\Gamma<G
is irreducible if its projection onto every rank
0
and rank
1
factor of
G
is dense.

We refer the reader to
[
9
]
for the necessary background.

Let
L\in C(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
[d^{2}_{\pi}L]\in\ker c_{2}
. Then, a direct computation shows that

\lVert\delta_{\pi}L\rVert:=\sup_{x,y\in\Gamma}|L(xy)-L(x)-\pi(x)L(y)|<\infty.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
L
is a
\pi
-quasimorphism if
\lVert\delta_{\pi}L\rVert<\infty
. If
\pi
is the trivial representation we say that
L
is a quasimorphism.

We say that
L
is cohomologically trivial if it is bounded, and we say that two
\pi
-quasimorphisms are cohomologous if their difference is cohomologically trivial.

The cohomology of quasimorphisms can be identified with the second cohomology group of what we will call the
unbounded cohomology
of
\Gamma
(with coefficients given by
\pi
),
\{H^{n}_{\mathrm{ub},\pi}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})\}
: this is the cohomology of the cocomplex
\left\{\frac{C(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})}{C_{\mathrm{b}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right\}
. See Section
3
of
[
6
]
for a discussion, and in particular for a natural way to norm
H^{2}_{\mathrm{ub},\pi}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})
1
1

1

The arguments are given in the untwisted case, but readily adapt to the setting that we are discussing
.

To prove the main theorem we will show that if
\Gamma
is as in the hypotheses, then any
\pi
-quasimorphism
L:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is cohomologically trivial. What we propose is that cohomology classes of quasimorphisms are associated and uniquely determined by certain probabilities related to natural dynamical systems in the group, or in a related homogeneous space (such as
\Gamma\backslash G
). Uniqueness here is understood in the sense that if
L,L^{\prime}
have the same associated measure, then they are cohomologous.

In the rank one case
[
6
]
, the dynamics is hyperbolic and admits many invariant measures. In contrast, in the higher-rank setting considered here, rigidity phenomena imply that essentially only one invariant measure arises, which leads to the desired conclusion.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. In
Section

2
we discuss the geometric setting, and show that a
\pi
-quasimorphism induces naturally a quasicocycle
{S}
for the action of
G
on some manifold
M^{\prime}
; a quasicocycle is the coarse version of a cocycle over an action. From this construction it follows that now one can look at the action of particular elements
a\in G
on
M^{\prime}
. These actions are foliated, and we are interested in elements which behave hyperbolically on the transverse directions to the orbit foliation: we will refer to these elements as “center isometries”. Then in
Section

3
we develop ideas of thermodynamic formalism for center isometries. In the end, we prove that given a center isometry and a quasicocycle (for example, the restriction of
{S}
to the corresponding cyclic group of the center isometry), one can associate uniquely an invariant measure satisfying a Gibbs-like property. This property in particular shows that
{S}
is bounded in some directions.

We remark here that it is possible to build an analogue theory of these center isometries in the context of
p
-adic groups, and the methods of this article also work in those related contexts, namely for lattices on semisimple
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
groups acting irreducibly on the corresponding Bruhat-Tits building. Details will appear elsewhere.

Finally, in
Section

4
we show how the geometry of the action
G\curvearrowright M^{\prime}
permits us to conclude that
S
is uniformly bounded: this is a consequence of Ratner’s measure classification theorem. To conclude, we use the structure of
G
to show that the above implies that
L
is bounded.

2.
Quasicocycles and twisted quasimorphisms

Let
M=\Gamma\backslash G
, and consider the natural right action
G\curvearrowright M
. For the representation
\pi:\Gamma\to\On_{N}
we denote
\mathcal{C}=\overline{\mathrm{Im}\pi}
and let
G^{\prime}=G\times\mathcal{C}
. The group
\Gamma
acts on the left on
G^{\prime}
by
\gamma\cdot(g,u)=(\gamma g,\pi(\gamma)u)
; alternatively, we consider the irreducible uniform lattice

\Gamma^{\prime}:=\{(\gamma,\pi(\gamma)):\gamma\in\Gamma\}<G^{\prime}

with the usual left action, and denote
M^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}
. The action
G\curvearrowright M
extends naturally to an action
G^{\prime}\curvearrowright M^{\prime}
.

It will be convenient to use the following equivalent description of the above action. Let
\Sigma\subset G
be a fundamental domain for
M
, and
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
a fundamental domain for
M^{\prime}
. With no loss of generality, we assume that
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\times\mathcal{C}
.

Define the integer part maps
[\cdot]:G\to\Gamma,[\cdot]:G^{\prime}\to\Gamma^{\prime}
be such that

\displaystyle[g]^{-1}g\in\Sigma

\displaystyle[(g,u)]^{-1}(g,u)\in\Sigma^{\prime}.

Note that
[g,u]=([g],\pi([g]))
, and the abuse of notation of calling both maps the same is harmless. The following is clear.

Lemma 2.1
.

The functions
[\cdot]:G\to\Gamma,[\cdot]:G^{\prime}\to\Gamma^{\prime}
are
\Gamma
- and
\Gamma^{\prime}
-equivariant: for
\gamma\in\Gamma,g\in G,u\in\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle[\gamma g]=\gamma[g]

\displaystyle[(\gamma,\pi(\gamma))\cdot(g,u)]=(\gamma,\pi(\gamma))[g,u].

Using the map
[\cdot]
, we define a right action of
G^{\prime}
on
\Sigma^{\prime}
as follows. For
x^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
, set

x^{\prime}*g^{\prime}:=[x^{\prime}g^{\prime}]^{-1}x^{\prime}g^{\prime}.

Lemma 2.2
.

The above formula defines an action of
G^{\prime}
on
\Sigma^{\prime}
.

It is enough to show that for
x\in\Sigma,g\in G
,
x*g:=[xg]^{-1}xg
defines a right action of
G
on
\Sigma
. Note that
xg=[xg]x*g
, thus

\displaystyle[x(g_{1}g_{2})]x*(g_{1}g_{2})

\displaystyle=x(g_{1}g_{2})=(xg_{1})g_{2}=([xg_{1}]x*g_{1})g_{2}=[xg_{1}]((x*g_{1})g_{2})

\displaystyle=[xg_{1}][(x*g_{1})g_{2}]\left((x*g_{1})*g_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=[xg_{1}][[xg_{1}]^{-1}(xg_{1})g_{2}]\left((x*g_{1})*g_{2}\right)=[xg_{1}g_{2}]\left((x*g_{1})*g_{2}\right)

and
x*(g_{1}g_{2})=(x*g_{1})*g_{2}
.
∎

It follows that in particular that
\Sigma^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}
is a
G^{\prime}-
principal bundle; note also that
G^{\prime}
acts (on the left) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the action corresponding to the representation
\pi
. Now given
L:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
a
\pi
-quasimorphism, we will show that it defines naturally a function on the vector bundle associated to
\pi
.

Define a map
S:\Sigma^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

S((x,A),(g,B))=A^{-1}L([xg]).

We will show that
S
is a quasicocycle, as per the next definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a group acting on a space
E
, and let
\rho:G\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a representation. A map
S:E\times G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
\rho
-quasicocycle if

\sup_{g_{i}\in G,p\in E}|S(p,g_{1}g_{2})-\rho(g_{1})S(pg_{1},g_{2})-S(p,g_{1})|<\infty.

If
\rho
is the trivial representation, we simply say that
S
is a quasicocycle for the action.

For
a,b\in\Gamma
we write

\delta_{\pi}(L)(a,b)=L(ab)-(\pi(a)L(b)+L(a))

and let

C(a):=\pi(a)L(a^{-1})+L(a)=L(e)-\delta_{\pi}L(a,a^{-1});

notice that
C(a)
it is uniformly bounded. We now make the explicit computations: for
(x,A)\in\Sigma^{\prime}
and
(g_{i},B_{i})\in G^{\prime}
,
i=1,2
, we have

\displaystyle S((x,A),(g_{1}g_{2},B_{1}B_{2}))=A^{-1}L([xg_{1}g_{2}])

\displaystyle S((x,A),(g_{1},B_{1}))=A^{-1}L([xg_{1}])

and

\displaystyle B_{1}S\left((x,A)*(g_{1},B_{1}),(g_{2},B_{2})\right)=B_{1}S\left([xg_{1},AB_{1}]^{-1}(xg_{1},AB_{1}),(g_{2},B_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=B_{1}S\left([xg_{1}]^{-1}xg_{1},\pi([xg_{1}])^{-1}AB_{1},(g_{2},B_{2})\right)=A^{-1}\pi([xg_{1}])L\left([xg_{1}]^{-1}[xg_{1}g_{2}]\right)

\displaystyle=A^{-1}\pi([xg_{1}])\left(L\left([xg_{1}]^{-1}[xg_{1}g_{2}]\right)-\pi([xg_{1}])^{-1}L([xg_{1}g_{2}])-L([xg_{1}]^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle+A^{-1}\pi([xg_{1}])\left(\pi([xg_{1}])^{-1}L([xg_{1}g_{2}])+L([xg_{1}]^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{A^{-1}\pi([xg_{1}])\delta_{\pi}L([xg_{1}]^{-1},[xg_{1}g_{2}])}_{\text{bounded}:=K_{1}}+S((x,A),(g_{1}g_{2},B_{1}B_{2}))+A^{-1}\pi([xg_{1}])L([xg_{1}]^{-1})

\displaystyle=K_{1}+S((x,A),(g_{1}g_{2},B_{1}B_{2}))+\underbrace{A^{-1}C([xg_{1}])}_{\text{bounded}:=K_{2}}+S((x,A),(g_{1},B_{1})).

Let
K:=K_{1}+K_{2}
. We have proven the following.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a constant
K>0
such that for all
(x,A)\in\Sigma^{\prime}
and
(g_{i},B_{i})\in G^{\prime}
,
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\left|S\left((x,A),(g_{1}g_{2},B_{1}B_{2})\right)-\rho\left((g_{1},B_{1})\right)S\left((x,A)*(g_{1},B_{1}),(g_{2},B_{2})\right)-S\left((x,A),(g_{1},B_{1})\right)\right|\leq K

where
\rho\left((g,B)\right)=B^{-1}
. That is,
S:\Sigma^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a
\rho-
quasicocycle.

In particular,
S
is a quasicocycle for the action of
G=G\times\{1\}<G^{\prime}
on
\Sigma^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}
.

Quasicocycles are natural analogues of cocycles for actions, and play a central role in our arguments. Observe that in particular a quasicocycle for a
\mathbb{Z}-
action on a space
X
(thus, given by some invertible map
T:X\toit
) is equivalent to a sequence of functions
(S_{n}:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n})
so that,

(\forall n,m),\quad\lVert S_{n+m}-S_{n}-S_{m}\circ T^{n}\rVert_{\scriptscriptstyle\!\ell^{\infty}}\leq C

for some uniform constant
C
. Similarly for semi-actions of
\mathbb{N}
. Thus, in this case we will refer to the sequence
\bm{S}=(S_{n})
as the quasicocycle
\bm{S}
.

Remark 2.5

(Non-uniform lattices.)
.

The results given in this section also hold for non-uniform lattices. To achieve this one can use that the word and Riemannian metrics on
\Gamma
are uniformly comparable
[
10
]
.

3.
Equilibrium states for Center Isometries

We will study the action of certain elements
a\in G
on
\Sigma^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}
. In this section we introduce such elements and develop a thermodynamic formalism adapted to this setting. To avoid unnecessary technicalities, we work in a more general framework.

Consider a laminated space
(X,{W})
, whose leaves are
c
-dimensional smooth manifolds. We assume that
T{W}
is a continuous bundle over
X
, equipped with a continuous Riemannian metric whose induced distance is locally comparable to that of
X
. For
\delta>0
and
x\in X
, we denote by
B^{{W}}_{\delta}(x)
the local plaque centered at
x
of radius
\delta
, where distance is measured intrinsically along
{W}(x)
.

Let
T:X\to X
be a homeomorphism preserving
{W}
.

Definition 3.1
.

The pair
(T,{W})
is called plaque expansive if there exists
C_{\mathrm{exp}}>0
so that

(\forall x,y\in X,\forall 0<\delta\leq C_{\mathrm{exp}}):\ \distop_{X}(T^{n}x,T^{n}y)\leq\delta\Rightarrow y\in B^{{W}}_{3\delta}(x).

Since
{W}
will be fixed, we will simply say that
T
is plaque expansive
. This notion was introduced by Hirsch, Pugh and Shub
[
11
]
in the context of partially hyperbolic dynamics, which will be our main example.

Definition 3.2

(Center Isometry)
.

Suppose that
X
is a closed smooth manifold. A
\mathcal{C}^{1}
diffeomorphism
T:X\toit
is a center isometry if there exists a
DT-
invariant splitting
TX=E^{s}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{u}
, and a Riemannian metric on
X
so that

(1)

DT|E^{c}
is an isometry.

(2)

\lVert DT|E^{s}\rVert,\lVert DT^{-1}|E^{u}\rVert<1
.

If
T
is a center isometry we refer to the bundles
E^{\mathrm{s}},E^{\mathrm{u}},E^{\mathrm{c}},E^{\mathrm{cs}}=E^{\mathrm{c}}\oplus E^{\mathrm{s}},E^{\mathrm{cu}}=E^{\mathrm{c}}\oplus E^{\mathrm{u}}
as the
stable
,
unstable
,
center
,
center stable
, and
center unstable
, respectively.

Example 3.3
.

In the setting of the introduction, there are always elements
a\in G
so that its induced map
T_{a}:M^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}\toit
is a center isometry. See the last Section for a discussion.

The reader is referred to
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
for more on center isometries. For us it will be important that
each bundle
E^{*},*\in\{s,c,u,cs,cu\}
is integrable to some (
T-

\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{*}}
of smooth leaves. See
[
13
]
.

Notation
.

If
T
is a center isometry we write

B_{\varepsilon}^{*}(x)=B_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{*}}}(x)=\{y\in\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{*}}(x):\distop_{\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{*}}}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}\qquad*\in\{s,c,u,cs,cu\}.

Above
\distop_{\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{*}}}(x,y)
denotes the intrinsic distance inside
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{*}}(x)
.

Our strategy is the following. Starting from a
\pi
-quasimorphism
L:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have shown how to construct a quasicocycle
{S}
for the action of
G
on
M^{\prime}
. Restricting this quasicocycle along the dynamics generated by some particular center isometry, we will show that
{S}
is bounded, and hence deduce that

The method consists of adapting ideas from thermodynamic formalism, in particular developing a Gibbs-type framework for center isometries. While similar ideas were used in
[
6
]
in the hyperbolic setting, the present context requires a different approach, as the dynamics here is far from uniformly hyperbolic.

Some weak hyperbolicity however is given by plaque expansivity. We need the following.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
13
]
)
.

If
T
is a center isometry, then
(T,\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{c}})
is plaque expansive.

For studying certain invariant measures associated to the system we will use the next property.

Definition 3.5
.

We say that
T:X\to X
has the specification property if for each
\delta>0
there exists
M(\delta)
such that the following holds: if
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}
are intervals with
d_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{i},I_{j})\geq M(\delta)
for
i\neq j
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in X
,
I_{i}=[a_{i},b_{i}]
,
b_{i}-a_{i}=n_{i}
, then there exists
z\in X
so that

\distop(T^{j-a_{i}}z,T^{j-a_{i}}x_{i})<\delta\text{ for }j\in I_{i}.

If
T
preserves
{W}
, we say that
T:X\to X
has the quasiperiodic specification property if
z
can be chosen so that
T^{l}z\in B_{\delta}^{{W}}(z)
, for
l=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}+kM
.

The specification property is due to Bowen (cf.
[
14
]
), where he required furthermore that the specifying point

In the cases that we are interested, periodic points are not guaranteed to exist, which is the reason for introducing a natural substitute, as it will allow us to work with quasiperiodic points (defined below); it will be shown that these always exist for center isometries.

Definition 3.6
.

A finite orbit segment
\{x,Tx,\ldots,T^{n-1}x\}
is called
(n,\delta)-
quasiperiodic if
T^{n}(x)\in B_{\delta}^{{W}}(x)
.

We say that

(n,\delta)
-quasiperiodic if it belongs to an

quasiperiodic orbit segment, and that it is
\delta-
quasiperiodic if it is

quasiperiodic, for some

.

The following is clear.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
(T,{F})
has the quasiperiodic specification property.

(1)

For
\delta>0
sufficiently small, the set of

quasiperiodic points is dense.

(2)

T
is topologically mixing, meaning: for
U,V\subset X

such that
\forall n\geq n_{U,V}

Next we give our main example of systems having the quasiperiodic specification property. Fix
T:X\toit
a center isometry, and define the sets (

J^{csu}_{d}(\varepsilon,x)=\bigcup_{y\in B^{cs}_{\varepsilon}(x)}T^{-d}B^{u}_{\varepsilon}(T^{d}y).

Below we adapt the proof of specification for ergodic group automorphism of compact Abelian groups due to Lind

Lemma 3.8

Suppose that

Proposition 3.9

In the hypotheses above,

Fix

\diam(T^{j}C_{i})\leq 2\varepsilon\;\;(0\leq j\leq d_{i},1\leq i\leq k)

3.1.

We now introduce a notion of pressure adapted to quasicocycles. In this part we assume that

Notation

•

Note that since

16

Given

\displaystyle Z_{n}(\varepsilon)=\sup\left\{\sum_{x\in E}\exp(S_{n}(x)):E\;\mbox{is}\;(n,\varepsilon)-\mbox{separated}\right\}.

Definition 3.11

It will be useful to consider a rough equivalence relation on quasicocycles.

Definition 3.12

We say that a quasicocycle

From now on

Immediate from the quasicocycle property.

is a homeomorphism that commutes with
T
and acts isometrically on
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{c}}
, then it also preserves
\nu
.

Observe that if
{E}
is a
\delta
-quasiperiodic family for
T
, then
R{E}=\{RE_{n}\}_{n}
is also a
\delta
-quasiperiodic family. It follows that

\displaystyle R_{*}\nu=R_{*}\lim_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T_{*}^{j}\mu_{n}^{{E}}=\lim_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(T^{j})_{*}\mu_{n}^{R{E}}=\nu

and
\nu
is
R-
invariant.

4.
Proof of the main theorem

We return to the setting introduced in the first two sections. Recall that
M=\Gamma\backslash G,M^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}
and suppose that we are given a
\pi
-quasimorphism
L:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
. We aim to show that
L
is bounded.

Let
\theta
be a Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
, with associated decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
, where
\mathfrak{k}
is the Lie algebra of a maximal compact subgroup
K\subset G
and
\mathfrak{p}=\ker(\theta+\Id)
. Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{p}
be a maximal
\mathbb{R}
-split abelian subalgebra and set
A=\exp(\mathfrak{a})
. Denote by
\Sigma
the corresponding root system and write

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},

with
\mathfrak{n}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
N=\exp(\mathfrak{n})
. We use the Iwasawa decomposition
G=KAN
and the Cartan decomposition
G=KA^{+}K
, with Cartan projection
\mu:G\to A^{+}
.

We choose
a=\exp(H_{\alpha})\in A
, where
H_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{a}
is a coroot of
\alpha\in\Sigma
: in particular its centralizer contains some
U\approx\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})<G
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The element
a
induces center isometries
T_{a}:M^{\prime}\toit,T_{a}:M\toit
, where the first is an isometric extension of the second. Both center isometries have minimal unstable lamination.

In fact, every non-zero element
a^{\prime}\in A
induces a center isometry; this follows from standard arguments (see
[
20
]
). For our choice of
a
(which is not regular), the center foliation strictly contains
A
.

Minimality of the unstable lamination follows from irreducibility of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
(and of
\Gamma
in
G
).
∎

Next we fix
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
a linear functional. Let also
S
be the quasicocycle for the action
G\curvearrowright\Sigma^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}
given on
Proposition

2.4
, and denote by
(S^{a,\ell}_{n}(\cdot))=(\ell(S(\cdot,a^{n})))
the (real) quasicocycle over the action of
T_{a}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

(S^{a,\ell}_{n})_{n}
has the Bowen property.

Since
M^{\prime}
is a manifold, the fundamental domain
\Sigma^{\prime}
can be chosen to be a polyhedron. If
\tilde{p},\tilde{q}
lie in the same Bowen ball in
M^{\prime}
, then their lifts
p,q\in G^{\prime}
lie in nearby translates of
\Sigma^{\prime}
, say
p\in\gamma_{p}\Sigma^{\prime}
and
q\in\gamma_{q}\Sigma^{\prime}
with
\gamma_{p}\Sigma^{\prime}\cap\gamma_{q}\Sigma^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Since there are only finitely many such
\gamma
, the quasimorphism property of
L
yields the result.
∎

As observed at the end of
Section

3
, the equilibrium measure
\nu
associated to
(S^{a,\ell}_{n})
is invariant under
T_{b}
for every
b
commuting with
a
. By our choice of
a
, this implies that
\nu
is invariant under a non-trivial unipotent subgroup
U\subset G
. By irreducibility of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, Moore’s ergodicity theorem implies that
U\curvearrowright M^{\prime}
is ergodic. Hence, by Ratner’s measure classification theorem
[
21
]
,
\nu
is homogeneous. Since
\nu
has full support, it must coincide with Haar measure.

Let

H(a)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int S(x,a^{n})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
denotes Haar measure on
M^{\prime}
. It is immediate that
H(a^{n})=nH(a)
, and so
H
is a homogeneous quasimorphism on
A
: since
A
is abelian,
H
is a homomorphism.

We apply the same reasoning to
H
. By using different linear functionals and by
Corollary

3.31
, we deduce that for every
a\in A
,

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lVert S(\cdot,a^{k})-H(a^{k})\rVert_{\scriptscriptstyle\!\ell^{\infty}}<\infty

On the other hand, the quasicocyle property along
A
gives that for every
a\in A

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\lVert S(\cdot,a^{t})-H(a^{t})\rVert_{\scriptscriptstyle\!\ell^{\infty}}<\infty

Taking a basis for
A
and using again the quasicocycle property, we deduce that

C_{1}:=\sup_{a\in A}\lVert S(\cdot,a)-H(a)\rVert_{\scriptscriptstyle\!\ell^{\infty}}<\infty.

We may choose the fundamental domain
\Sigma
to be right
K
-invariant, i.e.
\Sigma K=\Sigma
, by taking a fundamental domain for the left
\Gamma
-action on
G/K
. It follows that
[xk]=e
for every
k\in K
and
(x,z)\in\Sigma^{\prime}
, and hence

S((x,z),(k,c))=z^{-1}L(e).

In particular,
|S((x,z),(k,c))|=|L(e)|
.

Next we identify
\Sigma^{\prime}
with
\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}
and use the left
G
action on
\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}
. Using the
G=KA^{+}K
decomposition and the quasicocycle property, we see that there is a constant
C_{0}
(
=2\|\delta_{\pi}(L)\|
) so that for
g=k_{1}ak
,
k_{i}\in K
,
a\in A^{+}
,
(x,z)\in\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\Big|S(\Gamma^{\prime}(x,z),(k_{1}ak_{2},e))-

\displaystyle S(\Gamma^{\prime}(xk_{1}a,z),(k_{2},e))-S(\Gamma^{\prime}(xk_{1},z),(a,e))-S(\Gamma^{\prime}(x,z),(k_{1},e))\Big|\leq C_{0}.

Hence we get that

\left|S(\Gamma^{\prime}(x,z),(k_{1}ak_{2},e))-S(\Gamma^{\prime}(xk_{1},z),(a,e))\right|\leq C_{0}+2|L(e)|.

which in turn implies

\left|S(\Gamma^{\prime}(x,z),(g,e))-H(\mu(g))\right|\leq C_{0}+2|L(e)|+C_{1}

for every
g\in G,x\in G
,
z\in\mathcal{C}
(recall that
\mu(g)\in A^{+}
is Cartan projection).

From this we get

\left|z^{-1}L([xg])-H(\mu(g))\right|\leq C_{0}+2|L(e)|+C_{1}:=C_{2}

for every
g\in G,x\in G
,
z\in\mathcal{C}
. In particular,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left|\left(z^{-1}-1\right)L([xg])\right|\leq 2C_{2}.

At the beginning we split
\mathbb{R}^{N}=E\oplus F
so that
\pi
acts on
E
as the identity, and
\bigcap_{z\in\mathcal{C}}\ker(z-1)|F=\{0\}
. Thus, on the
F
part we have that
L
is uniformly bounded, and we can focus on the
E
part: with no loss of generality we assume that
N=1
and
\pi
is trivial.

As it is well known, any quasimorphism is cohomologous to a homogeneous one, thus with no loss of generality, we assume that
L(a^{n})=nL(a),n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Using that
[xg]^{n}=\left[[xg]^{n-1}xg\right]
in (
4.1
), we thus get

L([xg])\in\bigcap_{z\in\mathcal{C}}\ker(z-1).

On the one hand, for
a\in A
,

\left|H(a\mu(a)^{-1})\right|=\left|H(a)-H(\mu(a))\right|\leq 2C_{2}

which means that
a\mu(a)^{-1}\in B_{C_{3}}(\ker H)
, for some uniform
C_{3}
. Remember that the Weyl Group is
W=N_{K}(A)/Z_{K}(A)
where
N_{K}(A),Z_{K}(A)
are the normalizer and centralizer of
A
in
K
, respectively.
W
acts on
A
by conjugation, and for
a\in A
there exists a unique
w\in W
so that
w(a)\in A^{+}
.

It follows that if
w\in W
then
\mu(w(a))=\mu(a)
for every
a\in A
and hence
|H(a\cdot w(a)^{-1})|\leq C_{3}
. Applying the same to
a^{n}\cdot w(a^{-n})
, we obtain that
a\cdot w(a)^{-1}\in\ker H
for every
a\in A,w\in W
. It is simpler to lift everything to
\mathfrak{a}
using the exponential, and use that
W
contains (and in fact is generated by) the reflections
s_{\alpha}
on
\ker\alpha
,
\alpha\in\Sigma
. For
a\in\mathfrak{a}
the element
a-s_{\alpha}(a)
is in the (one-dimensional) orthogonal complement of

. Thus,
H
vanishes on

\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\ker(\alpha)=\left(\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\ker(\alpha)\right)^{\perp}=\{0\}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{a}

i.e.
H\equiv 0
. So,
|L([xg])|\leq C_{2}
for every
x\in\Sigma,g\in G
hence

|L(\gamma)|\leq C_{2}

for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, that is,

is bounded.
