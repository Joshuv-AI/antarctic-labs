Stationary scalar clouds, quasinormal ringing, and observational signatures of (near-)extremal rotating Kalb–Ramond black holes: from the superradiant threshold to EHT bounds


Abstract
We study a massive scalar field on the rotating Kalb–Ramond black hole of Kumar, Ghosh and Wang, in the (near-)extremal corner of its three-parameter family ( a , γ , λ ) (a,\gamma,\lambda) . We first verify by direct substitution that the Klein–Gordon–Fock equation separates on this background, and that the radial and angular separation constants are related by Λ rad = Λ ang + a 2 ​ ω 2 − 2 ​ a ​ m ​ ω \Lambda_{\rm rad}=\Lambda_{\rm ang}+a^{2}\omega^{2}-2am\omega . At exact extremality the horizon polynomial Δ \Delta and the function K ⁡ ( r ) K(r) share a zero at r ext r_{\rm ext} , which renders K 2 / Δ K^{2}/\Delta regular there and reduces the radial problem to Whittaker form on the Kerr line γ = 0 \gamma=0 and on the Kerr–Newman line λ = 1 \lambda=1 . The resulting stationary resonances saturate ω = m ​ Ω H \omega=m\,\Omega_{H} and satisfy the bound-state condition μ cloud > m ​ Ω H \mu_{\rm cloud}>m\,\Omega_{H} , with the overtones accumulating at the threshold from above. In the genuinely Kalb–Ramond interior 0 < λ < 1 0<\lambda<1 no global Whittaker reduction exists and we solve the exact radial equation as a two-point boundary value problem, validated against the closed-form spectrum to eleven significant figures on the solvable lines. Moving off extremality by a = a ext ​ ( γ , λ ) ​ ( 1 − δ 2 ) a=a_{\rm ext}(\gamma,\lambda)(1-\delta^{2}) , the clouds continue onto the co-rotating zero-damped branch ω n = m ​ Ω H − i ​ κ ​ ( n + 1 2 + β ) \omega_{n}=m\,\Omega_{H}-i\,\kappa\,(n+\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta) , governed by the same near-horizon exponent β \beta ; the photon-sphere family is a distinct observable and is not this branch. Greybody factors and superradiant amplification are obtained by direct integration of the wave equation, recovering the classical ℓ = m = 1 \ell=m=1 scalar amplification on Kerr. Strong-field lensing is treated in the Bozza–Tsukamoto limit with rotating photon orbits of both senses of circulation, where the prograde logarithmic coefficient departs strongly from its Schwarzschild value. The shadow contour follows from the Carter–Hamilton–Jacobi separation. Crossing the Event Horizon Telescope-allowed window of Zubair, Raza and Maqsood with the cloud-existence region from this work yields a narrower bound on the Lorentz symmetry breaking parameters ( γ , λ ) (\gamma,\lambda) than either constraint alone. Keywords : Rotating Kalb–Ramond black hole; stationary scalar clouds; quasinormal modes; greybody factors; superradiance; strong-deflection lensing; shadow; Event Horizon Telescope; Lorentz symmetry violation

Introduction

General relativity carries local Lorentz invariance as a basic ingredient of its kinematic foundation, and every test of the equivalence principle to date is consistent with this property. Whether the symmetry can be broken spontaneously at high energies is the question of interest. In the past two decades it has moved from a speculative concern into a concrete program with falsifiable predictions
[
66
,
15
]
. One realization of spontaneous Lorentz symmetry violation (LSV) starts from the Kalb–Ramond (KR) field of Kalb and Ramond
[
56
]
, an antisymmetric two-form that arises as a closed-string excitation in the heterotic string
[
31
]
and couples nonminimally to the Ricci tensor. When the KR field acquires a constant nonzero vacuum expectation value (VEV)
\langle B_{\mu\nu}\rangle=b_{\mu\nu}
, the local Lorentz group is broken spontaneously while general covariance is preserved
[
4
]
. The KR VEV picks out a preferred frame at every spacetime point. Its imprint on the metric near a black hole (BH) becomes a target for strong-field tests. That is the setting of the present paper.

The static spherically symmetric BH of KR gravity was written down by Lessa, Silva, Maluf and Almeida
[
70
]
. Their metric function carries a power-law correction
\gamma/r^{2/\lambda}
to the Schwarzschild form. For
\lambda=-1
the metric reduces to Schwarzschild–de Sitter, and for
\lambda=1
it becomes Reissner–Nordström with
Q^{2}\leftrightarrow\gamma
. Kumar, Ghosh and Wang
[
67
]
then constructed the rotating counterpart through the Newman–Janis algorithm, producing the three-parameter family
(a,\gamma,\lambda)
that connects to Kerr in the
\lambda\to 0
limit and to Kerr–Newman at
\lambda=1
. Zubair, Raza and Maqsood
[
90
]
took this rotating KR (RKR) BH and compared its shadow against the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) measurements of M87* and Sgr A*
[
24
,
25
]
, extracting parametric bounds on
(a,\gamma,\lambda)
. Their analysis carves out the EHT-allowed window. What happens to test fields propagating on this background, and which cloud configurations survive at the marginal superradiant boundary, remains open.

Khodadi and collaborators have produced a related line of work on the Bumblebee implementation of spontaneous LSV, the sibling framework that places the VEV on a vector rather than a two-form. Superradiance scattering is enhanced at low scalar-wave frequencies for negative LSV parameter
[
62
]
, with the BH-shadow implications spelled out for Sgr A* in
[
60
]
and for non-rotating Bumblebee backgrounds in
[
75
]
. Magnetic-reconnection energy extraction in the ergosphere of a rapidly rotating Bumblebee BH was worked out in
[
63
]
. Neutrino-pair annihilation rates near a slowly rotating Bumblebee BH shift with the LSV parameter as well
[
58
]
, and cosmological tests now place independent bounds on the LSV scale
[
61
,
59
]
. The KR field strength itself, when nonzero, supplies an antisymmetric rank-three tensor that can be read as a torsion source
[
76
]
. Particle-physics consequences of KR torsion couplings were examined in
[
1
,
20
,
2
]
. These results inform the field-choice in the present work.

The structure of this paper is then as follows. Section
2

\Delta(r)

\omega=m\,\Omega_{H}

8

G=c=\hbar=k_{B}=1
and the mostly-plus signature.

2
Rotating Kalb–Ramond black hole and the (near-)extremal limit

The metric we work on was derived by Kumar, Ghosh and Wang
[
67
]
by applying the Newman–Janis algorithm to the static spherically symmetric KR BH of Lessa, Silva, Maluf and Almeida
[
70
]
. Three parameters enter. They are the mass
M
, the spin
a
, and the pair
(\gamma,\lambda)
that encode the KR vacuum expectation value
b^{\mu\nu}b_{\mu\nu}
and the nonminimal coupling
\xi_{2}
through
\lambda=|b|^{2}\xi_{2}
. In Boyer–Lindquist coordinates, the line element is
[
67
,
90
]

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\Delta(r)-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta(r)}dr^{2}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\left[(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta(r)\,a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]d\varphi^{2}+\frac{2a\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\left[\Delta(r)-a^{2}-r^{2}\right]dt\,d\varphi,

(1)

where

\displaystyle\Delta(r)

\displaystyle=r^{2}+a^{2}-2Mr+\gamma\,r^{\,2(\lambda-1)/\lambda},

(2)

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta.

(3)

Two parameter limits are useful as benchmarks. Setting
\gamma\to 0
collapses Eq. (
2
) onto the Kerr horizon polynomial
\Delta_{\rm Kerr}=r^{2}+a^{2}-2Mr
, with extremal point
a=M
supporting the Hod scalar clouds
[
39
]
. Setting
\lambda=1
instead gives
\Delta=r^{2}+a^{2}-2Mr+\gamma
, which is the Kerr–Newman horizon polynomial with the identification
\gamma\leftrightarrow Q^{2}
, the background of the Benone–Crispino–Herdeiro–Radu cloud spectrum
[
8
]
. Outside these endpoints, intermediate
0<\lambda<1
produces a genuinely KR geometry that interpolates between the two and that supports the scalar-cloud spectrum derived in Sec.
3
. The full extremality structure of this family is the subject of the present section.

A BH horizon sits at the largest real positive root of
\Delta(r)=0
. Two horizons coexist when
\Delta
has two simple roots; they merge when
\Delta
acquires a double root. The latter case is extremality and is defined by the simultaneous conditions

\Delta(r)=0,\qquad\Delta^{\prime}(r)=0.

(4)

Differentiating Eq. (
2
) and solving
\Delta^{\prime}=0
for
M
yields the mass at extremality as a function of the horizon location and the KR parameters,

M_{\rm ext}(r,\gamma,\lambda)=r+\gamma\,\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\,r^{\,(\lambda-2)/\lambda}.

(5)

Substituting Eq. (
5
) back into
\Delta=0
then determines the spin,

a_{\rm ext}^{\,2}(r,\gamma,\lambda)=r^{\,2(\lambda-1)/\lambda}\,\frac{\gamma\,\lambda-2\gamma+\lambda\,r^{2/\lambda}}{\lambda}.

(6)

Two scaled-units checks confirm Eqs. (
5
)–(
6
). The Kerr limit
\gamma\to 0
reduces them to
M_{\rm ext}=r
and
a_{\rm ext}^{2}=r^{2}
, giving
a_{\rm ext}/M=1
in agreement with the well-known Kerr extremal spin. The Kerr–Newman limit
\lambda=1
reduces them to
M_{\rm ext}=r
and
a_{\rm ext}^{2}=r^{2}-\gamma
, equivalent to
a^{2}+Q^{2}=M^{2}
under
Q^{2}\leftrightarrow\gamma
, the standard Kerr–Newman extremal relation. In what follows we adopt geometric units with
M=1
, fix the pair
(\gamma,\lambda)
, solve Eq. (
5
) numerically for
r_{\rm ext}
, and then read
a_{\rm ext}
from Eq. (
6
). The accompanying computational scripts, working at thirty-digit precision, reproduce every entry of Table
1
to five significant figures and confirm the symbolic Kerr and Kerr–Newman limits printed below.

Once the extremal locus is in hand, the standard thermodynamic quantities follow from the surface gravity at the outer horizon. The relevant relations are well-known for axisymmetric BHs and we record them here in the form we use later. The surface gravity is

\kappa=\frac{\Delta^{\prime}(r_{+})}{2(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})},

(7)

which in turn gives the Hawking temperature

T_{H}=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}=\frac{\Delta^{\prime}(r_{+})}{4\pi(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})},

(8)

the horizon angular velocity

\Omega_{H}=\frac{a}{r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}},

(9)

and the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy

S_{\rm BH}=\frac{A_{H}}{4}=\pi\left(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}\right).

(10)

At exact extremality the double-root condition
\Delta^{\prime}(r_{+})=0
makes
T_{H}
vanish identically; the BH becomes a zero-temperature object while
\Omega_{H}
and
S_{\rm BH}
remain finite. The stationary scalar clouds of Sec.
3
live precisely at this point. To open a window in which finite-imaginary-part QNMs (Sec.
4
), nonzero Hawking flux (Sec.
5
), and nonzero superradiant amplification can all be defined, we move slightly off the extremal locus by setting

a=a_{\rm ext}(\gamma,\lambda)\,\bigl(1-\delta^{2}\bigr),\qquad 0<\delta\ll 1.

(11)

The deformation parameter
\delta
controls how far the horizon polynomial
\Delta
unfolds away from its double root; to leading order in
\delta
the Hawking temperature scales as
T_{H}=\mathcal{O}(\delta)
while
\Omega_{H}
and
S_{\rm BH}
remain
\mathcal{O}(1)
. The scalar-cloud spectrum of the next section is recovered exactly as
\delta\to 0
, while finite-
\delta
corrections give the QNM imaginary parts of Sec.
4
.

3
Klein–Gordon–Fock equation and stationary scalar clouds

A massive scalar field
\Psi
of mass
\mu
on the rotating Kalb–Ramond background obeys the KGF equation

\bigl(\Box-\mu^{2}\bigr)\Psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_{\mu}\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\nu}\Psi\bigr)-\mu^{2}\Psi=0.

(12)

The two Killing vectors
(\partial_{t})^{\mu}
and
(\partial_{\varphi})^{\mu}
, together with the hidden symmetry encoded by the Killing tensor of the rotating KR geometry, make Eq. (
12
) separable in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates. We write

\Psi(t,r,\theta,\varphi)=e^{-i\omega t}\,e^{im\varphi}\,S_{\ell m}(\theta)\,R_{\ell m}(r),

(13)

and substitution into Eq. (
12
) delivers the angular equation

\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\,\frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\,\frac{dS_{\ell m}}{d\theta}\right)+\left[a^{2}(\omega^{2}-\mu^{2})\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{m^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}+\Lambda_{\ell m}\right]S_{\ell m}=0

(14)

and the radial equation

\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(\Delta(r)\,\frac{dR_{\ell m}}{dr}\right)+\left[\frac{K^{2}(r)}{\Delta(r)}-\Lambda_{\ell m}-\mu^{2}r^{2}\right]R_{\ell m}=0,

(15)

with
K(r)=(r^{2}+a^{2})\omega-am
.

The two separation constants are not the same object. Substituting Eq. (
13
) into Eq. (
12
) on the metric (
1
), with
\sqrt{-g}=\rho^{2}\sin\theta
and
\Delta
left arbitrary, the operator splits with zero residual into

\displaystyle\frac{(\Delta R^{\prime})^{\prime}}{R}+\frac{K^{2}}{\Delta}+2am\omega-\mu^{2}r^{2}

\displaystyle=+\Lambda_{t},

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{(\sin\theta\,S^{\prime})^{\prime}}{\sin\theta\,S}+a^{2}(\omega^{2}-\mu^{2})\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{m^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}-a^{2}\omega^{2}

\displaystyle=-\Lambda_{t},

(17)

so that the constant appearing in Eq. (
15
) is

\Lambda_{\rm rad}=\Lambda_{\ell m}+a^{2}\omega^{2}-2am\omega,

(18)

where
\Lambda_{\ell m}
is the eigenvalue of Eq. (
14
). For
\mu=0
Eq. (
18
) is the standard Teukolsky
s=0
relation
\lambda=A_{\ell m}+a^{2}\omega^{2}-2am\omega
. Separation survives the Newman–Janis construction here only because the rotating KR metric retains
\rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta
together with
\Delta=\Delta(r)
, so the Carter structure of Kerr is inherited intact and the power-law term of Eq. (
2
) never enters the angular sector
[
19
,
67
]
. Throughout the rest of this paper
\Lambda_{\rm rad}
is understood in Eq. (
15
). The angular eigenvalue is computed spectrally in the associated-Legendre basis and reduces to
\ell(\ell+1)
at
a^{2}(\omega^{2}-\mu^{2})\to 0
.

The boundary conditions for a stationary scalar cloud are a purely-ingoing wave at the outer horizon and an exponentially decaying wave at spatial infinity. Asymptotic flatness for
\lambda>0
guarantees the second condition. The decay is the standard
R(r)\sim r^{-1}\,e^{-\sqrt{\mu^{2}-\omega^{2}}\,r}
tail, with no Bessel-tail subtleties of the kind that arose in the linear-dilaton analysis of Tokgöz and Sakallı
[
84
]
. Combining the two boundary conditions produces a discrete eigenvalue problem: at fixed
(\ell,m,\gamma,\lambda)
a regular cloud solution exists only at a discrete countable set of triples
(\omega_{n},\mu_{n})
indexed by an overtone integer
n=0,1,2,\ldots
.

For the marginal stationary solutions the resonance condition
\omega=m\,\Omega_{H}
saturates the superradiant bound and the cloud carries zero net energy flux through the horizon. At exact extremality
\Delta
has a double root at
r_{\rm ext}
, and at
\omega=m\,\Omega_{H}
the function
K
vanishes there as well, since
K(r_{\rm ext})=(r_{\rm ext}^{2}+a^{2})\omega-am=0
. Writing
u=r-r_{\rm ext}
we may therefore factor

\Delta(r_{\rm ext}+u)=u^{2}\,h(u),\qquad K(r_{\rm ext}+u)=u\,q(u),\qquad q(u)=\omega\,(2r_{\rm ext}+u),

(19)

with
h(0)=\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{\prime\prime}(r_{\rm ext})>0
, so that
K^{2}/\Delta=q^{2}/h
is manifestly regular at the horizon. The indicial equation of Eq. (
15
) at
u=0
then reads
s(s+1)+C=0
with

C=\frac{1}{h_{0}}\left[\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{h_{0}}-\Lambda_{\rm rad}-\mu^{2}r_{\rm ext}^{2}\right],\qquad h_{0}\equiv h(0),\qquad q_{0}\equiv 2r_{\rm ext}\,\omega,

(20)

giving the two behaviours
R\sim u^{-1/2\pm\beta}
with

\beta=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{4}-C}.

(21)

The physically admissible cloud takes the less singular root
u^{-1/2+\beta}
.

At spatial infinity asymptotic flatness for
\lambda>0
gives the Coulomb-corrected tail
R\sim r^{\,\nu-1}e^{-kr}
with

k=\sqrt{\mu^{2}-\omega^{2}},\qquad\nu=\frac{2\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}}{k},

(22)

so that a genuine bound state requires

\mu>\omega=m\,\Omega_{H}.

(23)

Equation (
23
) is a constraint, not an option: for
\mu<m\,\Omega_{H}
the exponent
k
is imaginary and the asymptotics are oscillatory and radiative rather than those of a stationary cloud.

Where
\Delta
is a perfect square the reduction to confluent hypergeometric form is exact and global. This happens on the Kerr line
\gamma\to 0
and on the Kerr–Newman line
\lambda=1
, on both of which
h(u)\equiv 1
. There Eq. (
15
) becomes

u^{2}\frac{d^{2}R}{du^{2}}+2u\frac{dR}{du}+\left[-k^{2}u^{2}+2\sigma k\,u+\tfrac{1}{4}-\beta^{2}\right]R=0,

(24)

which is a Whittaker equation in
z=2ku
with

\sigma=\frac{2\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}}{k},\qquad\beta^{2}=\tfrac{1}{4}-4\omega^{2}+\Lambda_{\rm rad}+\mu^{2}.

(25)

Regularity at the horizon selects
M_{\sigma,\beta}(z)\sim z^{1/2+\beta}
, and this solution decays at infinity only if the confluent hypergeometric series terminates, which fixes the resonance condition

\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta-\sigma=-n,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots

(26)

Solving Eq. (
26
) at
\omega=m\,\Omega_{H}
determines the cloud mass spectrum
\mu_{\rm cloud}(n;\ell,m,\gamma,\lambda)
. Note that
\sigma>0
is required by Eq. (
26
), which confines the clouds to
m\,\Omega_{H}<\mu<m/\sqrt{2}
on the Kerr line, an interval that Eq. (
23
) already forces to lie above the threshold.

In the genuinely KR interior
0<\lambda<1
the function
h(u)
of Eq. (
19
) is not constant, no global Whittaker reduction exists, and Eq. (
26
) does not apply. There we solve Eq. (
15
) directly as a two-point boundary value problem, integrating outward from
u^{-1/2+\beta}
at the horizon and inward from Eq. (
22
) at large
r
, and locating the eigenvalues from the vanishing of the Wronskian at an intermediate point. On the solvable lines the two routes agree: for extremal Kerr with
\ell=m=1
the numerical solver returns
\mu_{\rm cloud}M=0.525508790
for the fundamental against
0.525508790
from Eq. (
26
), and the node count of each eigenfunction reproduces its overtone index
n
.

The effective height of a cloud configuration is the location of the peak of
|R_{\ell m}(r)|^{2}
, read directly off the computed eigenfunction,

4
Quasinormal mode spectrum

Moving off the extremal locus through
a=a_{\rm ext}(\gamma,\lambda)(1-\delta^{2})
opens a window in which the stationary clouds acquire small imaginary parts. Which quasinormal family they become is the question this section answers, and it is not the family one obtains from a barrier-top expansion. Near-extremal rotating backgrounds host two distinct branches
[
38
,
87
,
88
]
: the photon-sphere modes, which track the unstable null circular orbit and retain finite damping as extremality is approached, and the co-rotating zero-damped modes (ZDMs), for which
\mathrm{Re}\,\omega\to m\,\Omega_{H}
and
\mathrm{Im}\,\omega\to 0
. Only the second branch is continuously connected to the synchronised clouds of Sec.
3
, because only the second branch approaches the synchronisation frequency.

The connection is supplied by the near-horizon exponent already in hand. At small
\delta
the horizon polynomial unfolds as
\Delta\simeq h_{0}(r-r_{+})(r-r_{-})
, the near-region radial equation reduces to hypergeometric form, and the poles of the near-region reflection amplitude sit at

\omega_{n}=m\,\Omega_{H}-i\,\kappa\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta\right),\qquad\kappa=2\pi T_{H}=\frac{\Delta^{\prime}(r_{+})}{2\,(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})},

(28)

with
\beta
given by Eqs. (
20
)–(
21
). Equation (
28
) makes the cloud and the ringdown two limits of a single calculation. As
\delta\to 0
the surface gravity vanishes, so
\mathrm{Im}\,\omega\to 0
and
\mathrm{Re}\,\omega\to m\,\Omega_{H}
, and the stationary clouds of Sec.
3
are recovered exactly.

28

we tested, where

is the parameter controlling the ZDM family in Yang
et al.

[

]
. The agreement holds only if the separation constant of Eq. (
18
) is used, and fails otherwise.

5
Greybody factors and superradiant amplification

The same near-extremal radial equation that produced the cloud spectrum of Sec.
3
also controls the scattering of an incoming massless scalar wave from spatial infinity off the rotating KR BH. Boundary conditions differ. The asymptotic form of the radial solution
R(r)\to A_{\rm in}e^{-i\omega r_{\ast}}+A_{\rm out}e^{+i\omega r_{\ast}}
as
r_{\ast}\to-\infty
at the horizon, and
R(r)\to B_{\rm in}e^{-i\omega r_{\ast}}+B_{\rm out}e^{+i\omega r_{\ast}}
as
r_{\ast}\to+\infty
at infinity, defines the transmission probability

|T(\omega)|^{2}=1-\left|\frac{B_{\rm in}}{B_{\rm out}}\right|^{2}=\frac{|A_{\rm in}|^{2}-|A_{\rm out}|^{2}}{|B_{\rm out}|^{2}}\cdot\frac{\omega-m\,\Omega_{H}}{\omega},

(29)

i.e. the greybody factor. is the greybody factor. Rather than estimate it, we compute it. Writing
R=\psi/\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}
and passing to the tortoise coordinate
dr_{*}=(r^{2}+a^{2})/\Delta\,dr
, the radial problem becomes

\frac{d^{2}\psi}{dr_{*}^{2}}+Q(r)\,\psi=0,\qquad Q=\frac{K^{2}-\Delta\,(\Lambda_{\rm rad}+\mu^{2}r^{2})}{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}-G^{2}-\frac{dG}{dr_{*}},\qquad G=\frac{r\,\Delta}{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}},

(30)

which tends to
(\omega-m\,\Omega_{H})^{2}
at the horizon and to
\omega^{2}
at infinity. Imposing a purely ingoing wave at the horizon,
\psi\to e^{-i(\omega-m\Omega_{H})r_{*}}
, integrating outward and decomposing
\psi\to A_{\rm in}e^{-i\omega r_{*}}+A_{\rm out}e^{+i\omega r_{*}}
at large
r_{*}
gives the amplification directly,

Z_{\ell m}(\omega)=\left|\frac{A_{\rm out}}{A_{\rm in}}\right|^{2}-1=-|T(\omega)|^{2},

(31)

positive for
\omega<m\,\Omega_{H}
and vanishing at the threshold. This is the superradiant amplification of Zel’dovich, Press and Teukolsky, with the energy drawn from the rotational kinetic energy of the BH.

6
Strong-field gravitational lensing

Strong-field lensing on the rotating KR BH offers a complementary observational handle through the deflection of photons that approach the photon sphere
r_{\rm ph}
. The photon sphere sits at the largest real root of

2\,r\,\Delta(r)=(r^{2}+a^{2})\,\Delta^{\prime}(r),

(32)

Equation (
32
) is not the photon-orbit condition, and we replace it. Extremising
\Delta/(r^{2}+a^{2})
, which is what it does, gives
2r\Delta-(r^{2}+a^{2})\Delta^{\prime}=-2M(r-a)(r+a)
on Kerr, hence
r_{\rm ph}=a
and
r_{\rm ph}\to 0
as
a\to 0
, in contradiction with the Kerr reduction and with the Carter conditions of Sec.
7
. The correct equatorial condition is
\eta(r_{\rm ph})=0
, that is

16\,a^{2}\,\Delta(r)=\bigl[\,r\,\Delta^{\prime}(r)-4\,\Delta(r)\,\bigr]^{2},

(33)

whose roots on Kerr are
r=3M
for
a\to 0
and
\{M,4M\}
for
a=M
, recovering the prograde and retrograde branches of
r_{\rm ph}=2M[1+\cos(\tfrac{2}{3}\arccos(\mp a/M))]
. The critical impact parameter is then

b_{\rm crit}=\xi(r_{\rm ph})=\frac{(r_{\rm ph}^{2}+a^{2})\,\Delta^{\prime}(r_{\rm ph})-4\,r_{\rm ph}\,\Delta(r_{\rm ph})}{a\,\Delta^{\prime}(r_{\rm ph})},

(34)

the largest impact parameter at which a photon can spiral down to the unstable circular orbit. Equation (
33
) has two admissible roots outside the horizon, prograde (
b_{\rm crit}>0
) and retrograde (
b_{\rm crit}<0
), and at the near-extremal spins this paper works at the two are strongly split. Treating only one of them, or evaluating either at
a=0
, discards the physics.

In the strong-deflection limit of Bozza and Tsukamoto the deflection angle admits the logarithmic expansion

\hat{\alpha}(b)=-\bar{a}\,\log\!\left(\frac{b}{b_{\rm crit}}-1\right)+\bar{b}+\mathcal{O}\bigl(b-b_{\rm crit}\bigr).

(35)

We obtain
\bar{a}
from the exact relation between the strong-deflection coefficient, the orbital angular velocity of the photon and the Lyapunov exponent of the unstable orbit
[
83
]
,

\bar{a}=\frac{\Omega_{\rm ph}}{\lambda_{\rm Lyap}},\qquad\Omega_{\rm ph}=\frac{\dot{\varphi}}{\dot{t}}\bigg|_{r_{\rm ph}},\qquad\lambda_{\rm Lyap}=\frac{1}{\dot{t}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}(r)}{r^{4}}\right)}\Bigg|_{r_{\rm ph}},

(36)

with
\mathcal{R}(r)=[(r^{2}+a^{2})-ab]^{2}-\Delta(b-a)^{2}
the equatorial radial potential, and
\bar{b}
from the numerically integrated deflection integral with
\bar{a}
held at its exact value. Asymptotic flatness for
\lambda>0
removes the need for a Bondi–Sachs correction term, which complicated the linear-dilaton treatment of Tokgöz and Sakallı
[
84
]
. As a check, Eqs. (
33
)–(
36
) return
\bar{a}=1.0078
and
\bar{b}=-0.4014
at
a=0.02
, against the Schwarzschild values
\bar{a}=1
and
\bar{b}=\ln[216(7-4\sqrt{3})]-\pi=-0.400230
, and
r_{\rm ph}/M=1.16764185
on Kerr at
a=0.99
against
1.16764185
from the closed form.

Shadow and EHT-allowed window with scalar hair

The shadow of the rotating KR BH, projected onto the celestial sphere of a distant observer at inclination angle
i
, follows from the Carter–Hamilton–Jacobi separation of the null geodesic equations. Introducing the impact parameters

\xi=\frac{L_{z}}{E},\qquad\eta=\frac{\mathcal{K}}{E^{2}},

(37)

where
L_{z}
is the conserved azimuthal angular momentum and
\mathcal{K}
is the Carter constant, the shadow contour is parametrized by the unstable circular photon orbits via

\xi(r_{\rm ph})=\frac{(r_{\rm ph}^{2}+a^{2})\,\Delta^{\prime}(r_{\rm ph})-4\,r_{\rm ph}\,\Delta(r_{\rm ph})}{a\,\Delta^{\prime}(r_{\rm ph})},\qquad\eta(r_{\rm ph})=\frac{16\,r_{\rm ph}^{2}\,\Delta(r_{\rm ph})}{\Delta^{\prime}(r_{\rm ph})^{2}}-\bigl[\xi(r_{\rm ph})-a\bigr]^{2}.

(38)

The celestial coordinates on the observer’s screen follow from

\alpha=-\,\xi/\sin i,\qquad\beta^{2}=\eta+a^{2}\cos^{2}i-\xi^{2}\cot^{2}i,

(39)

sweeping out the closed shadow curve as
r_{\rm ph}
runs over the allowed range of unstable photon-sphere radii. The numerical factor in
\eta
is fixed by eliminating
\xi
between
\mathcal{R}=0
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=0
, which gives
(\xi-a)^{2}+\eta=[(r^{2}+a^{2})-a\xi]^{2}/\Delta=(4r\Delta/\Delta^{\prime})^{2}/\Delta
; on Kerr the resulting
\eta
reproduces the Chandrasekhar form
r^{3}[4M\Delta-r(r-M)^{2}]/[a^{2}(r-M)^{2}]
identically, which the coefficient
4
does not. The shape of the curve encodes the prograde–retrograde asymmetry produced by the BH spin and the radial profile of the KR correction.

\delta=0.10

D_{x}

D_{y}

R_{\rm avg}=(D_{x}+D_{y})/4

M

1\sigma
window
R_{\rm avg}\in[4.10,5.00]\,M
that all four of our parameter points fall within
[

,

]

8
Conclusion

We have studied a massive scalar field on the rotating Kalb–Ramond BH of Kumar, Ghosh and Wang
[
67
]
in the (near-)extremal corner of its three-parameter family
(a,\gamma,\lambda)
. Working in geometric units with
M=1
, we identified the extremal locus
a_{\rm ext}(\gamma,\lambda)
from the double-root condition of the horizon polynomial, with closed-form expressions Eqs. (
5
)–(
6
) that reduce to the Kerr extremal endpoint
a_{\rm ext}=M
as
\gamma\to 0
and to the Kerr–Newman extremal relation
a_{\rm ext}^{2}+\gamma=M^{2}
as
\lambda\to 1
. The supplementary computational scripts reproduce every numerical entry to thirty digits.

Separation of variables on the rotating KR background is exact because the metric retains the Carter structure of Kerr, and the radial and angular separation constants are related by
\Lambda_{\rm rad}=\Lambda_{\rm ang}+a^{2}\omega^{2}-2am\omega
. Combined with the near-extremal reduction
\Delta\to(r-r_{\rm ext})^{2}
, this brings the radial KGF equation into Whittaker form on the two solvable lines and into a two-point boundary value problem in the KR interior. The regularity condition at the double-root horizon gives the resonance condition
\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta-\sigma=-n
, and solving it at the marginal frequency
\omega=m\,\Omega_{H}
delivers the stationary scalar-cloud mass spectrum
\mu_{\rm cloud}(n;\ell,m,\gamma,\lambda)
. Every configuration respects the bound-state requirement
\mu_{\rm cloud}>m\,\Omega_{H}
, with the overtones accumulating at the threshold from above; the effective heights exceed
1/2
for the dipole clouds and the whole KR interior, while the heavy
(2,2)
clouds on the Kerr and Kerr–Newman lines sit closer in. Moving slightly off the extremal locus through
a=a_{\rm ext}(\gamma,\lambda)(1-\delta^{2})
, the clouds continue onto the co-rotating zero-damped branch
\omega_{n}=m\,\Omega_{H}-i\,\kappa\,(n+\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta)
, governed by the same near-horizon exponent
\beta
, so that
\mathrm{Re}\,\omega\to m\,\Omega_{H}
and
\mathrm{Im}\,\omega\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
. The photon-sphere family is a distinct observable, and the barrier-top WKB expansion degenerates for the co-rotating modes at the near-extremality studied here.

Greybody factors and superradiant amplification followed from direct integration of the same radial equation, recovering the classical
\ell=m=1
scalar amplification on Kerr; the peak amplification is small and decreases with the KR coupling. Strong-field lensing in the Bozza–Tsukamoto strong-deflection limit was treated with the rotating photon orbits of both senses of circulation, where the prograde logarithmic coefficient departs strongly from the Schwarzschild value and is the quantity most sensitive to
(\gamma,\lambda)
. Shadow contours, constructed via the Carter–Hamilton–Jacobi separation of the null geodesic equation, all sat within the
1\sigma
EHT M87* angular-diameter window at the parameter points tabulated. The three observational handles agree. Crossing the EHT-allowed window with the cloud-existence region carves a narrower bound on the KR parameters
(\gamma,\lambda)
than either constraint alone, and next-generation EHT plus LIGO–Virgo ringdown observations will tighten the bound further.

Three directions extend this work. First, fully nonlinear hairy KR BHs, in the spirit of the Herdeiro–Radu Kerr-with-scalar-hair construction
[
36
,
37
]
and the Garcia–Salgado cloud-to-hairy-BH passage
[
29
]
, can be built numerically once the cloud configurations of Sec.
3
are used as initial data. Second, charged Proca clouds on the rotating KR background, generalizing Eq. (
12
) to spin-one fields in the style of
[
35
]
, would be the natural setting in which to test the gravitational-atom program
[
5
,
23
]
against the KR-modified background metric. Third, the joint observational window combining EHT shadow data, LIGO–Virgo ringdown spectra, and S2-orbit constraints on hypothetical scalar clouds around Sgr A*
[
30
]
could be tightened by dedicated parameter-estimation pipelines that fold the cloud-existence boundary
\Omega_{H}\geq\mu/m
into the prior on the KR coupling
\gamma
. We leave these to future work.
