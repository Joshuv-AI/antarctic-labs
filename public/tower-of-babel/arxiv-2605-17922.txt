Logarithmic Hilbert schemes of curves as weighted blow-ups and their integral Chow rings
Veronica Arena, Terry Dekun Song

Abstract
. The logarithmic Hilbert scheme of a logarithmic curve parametrizes subschemes on the expanded degenerations of the curve that are transverse to the boundary. We prove that the logarithmic Hilbert scheme of points on a smooth pointed curve is an iterated weighted blow-up of the symmetric product of the underlying curve. In doing so, we explicitly identify the blow-up centers, weights, and give them modular interpretations. As applications, we calculate their integral Chow rings in terms of those of the symmetric products. Key ingredients in our work include two recent results: the integral Chow ring formula of weighted blow-ups and a weighted analogue of Castelnuovo’s criterion for blow-downs. We recover the folklore result the logarithmic Hilbert scheme of toric ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} is a toric stack, and the Appendix by Dhruv Ranganathan outlines a complementary approach using Chow quotients.



1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth, projective curve over a field
k
of characteristic zero. Distinct closed points
p_{1},\dots,p_{\ell}\in C
give
C
a divisorial logarithmic structure as a pair
(C|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}p_{i})=(C|D).
The
logarithmic Hilbert scheme
of
n
points on
(C|D),
denoted as
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D),
parametrizes pairs
(\tilde{C},Z)
where

(1)

\tilde{C}
is a semistable model of
C
obtained from iterated deformations to the normal cone along
D,

(2)

Z\subset\tilde{C}
is a length-
n
closed subscheme that is logarithmically flat and has finite automorphism over
C
.

The precise definitions are recalled in Section
2
. The space
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
is representable by a Deligne–Mumford stack with logarithmically smooth logarithmic structure and non-trivial stacky structure. It is also smooth as an algebraic stack with projective coarse moduli space that compactifies
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C\setminus D).
Broader interests in general logarithmic Hilbert schemes come from studying enumerative geometry of curves and sheaves via degenerations
[
19
,
23
,
16
,
24
]
, and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
serves as a local model of some of the more general constructions.

This work concretely describes the logarithmic Hilbert scheme of points on a curve via the geometry of
weighted blow-ups
(c.f.
[
27
]
). Given
(\tilde{C},Z)\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
, taking the scheme-theoretic image along the collapsing map
\tilde{C}\to C
induces a map
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C)
from the logarithmic moduli space to the classical one. Our main geometric result is as follows.

Theorem
A
.

The map
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C)=\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C)
factors through a sequence of
\ell\cdot n
weighted blow-ups

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)\to\cdots\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}}\to\cdots\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C)

where
\bm{i}
belongs to a totally ordered subset of all
\bm{i}
satisfying
\mathbf{0}\leq\bm{i}\leq\mathbf{n}=(n,\dots,n)
entry-wise. Each
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}}
is a moduli space of subschemes on expanded degenerations of
C
along
D
satisfying intermediate stability conditions.

The moduli spaces
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}}
are constructed in Section
3
. The blow-up centers and weights are explicitly described in Section
5
. The edge cases
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\mathbf{0}}
and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\mathbf{n}}
recover
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C)
respectively. Interpolating between them are
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}}
for
\mathbf{0}\lneq\bm{i}\lneq\mathbf{n}
. Given
\bm{i}_{1}\leq\bm{i}_{2},
the map

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}_{2}}\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}_{1}}

can be seen as improving the transversality of subschemes parametrized by
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}_{1}}
at the cost of introducing more expanded degenerations in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}_{2}}.

Our proof of the theorem reduces to the case
D=p,
which we now spell out. In this case, each non-trivial semistable model
\tilde{C}\to C
is
C
attached with a chain of rational curves bubbling off from
p\in C
. Let
\tilde{p}\in\tilde{C}
be the proper transform of
p\in C.
It is the unique smooth point with non-trivial logarithmic structure.

Theorem
B
.

For
0\leq i\leq n,
define
\mathcal{Z}_{i}\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|p)_{\leq i}
as the closed substack parametrizing
(\tilde{C},Z)
where exactly
i
points are supported on
\tilde{p}.
Then
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|p)_{\leq i-1}\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|p)_{\leq i}
is the weighted blow-up with center
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
and weights
(1,\dots,i).

To prove this in Section
5
, we identify a divisor
\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\subset\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|p)_{\leq i-1}
and prove that it satisfies the smooth weighted blow-down criterion of
[
5
]
.

1.1.
Toric geometry on
\mathbb{P}^{1}

We note that the geometry of the logarithmic Hilbert stacks is particularly concrete when
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
D\leq 0+\infty.
In this case,

Corollary
C
.

The logarithmic Hilbert stacks
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0)
and
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)
are toric stacks.

Indeed, the above logarithmic Hilbert stacks are weighted blow-ups of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
along torus-invariant strata. Tracking the weights in the blow-ups, we identify the toric fan of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0)
as the successive star subdivisions of the fan of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
along the rays
(1,2,\dots,n)
,
(0,1,2,\dots,n-1)
,
\dots
,
(0,0,\dots,1,2).
Each ray represents the weighted blow-up along the toric stratum of the zero locus of some symmetric functions on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and the weights of the blow-up coincide with the degrees of the symmetric functions.

In particular, we describe the explicit construction of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0)
as weighted blow-up in Section
4
. This section gives a self-contained and accessible illustration of the geometric picture.

1.2.
Integral Chow ring calculation

After the geometric description of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
given by Theorem
A
, we leverage the weighted Keel’s formula in
[
6
]
, which gives a presentation of the integral Chow rings of the moduli spaces
\mathrm{CH}^{\star}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bf i})
as generated by
\mathrm{CH}^{\star}(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C))
and the exceptional divisors
[\mathcal{E}_{\bm{i}}]
introduced at each weighted blow-up.

To state our result, we set up the following notation. For
p_{r}\in D,
define line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{p_{r}}
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C)
as the normal bundle along the embedding
s_{n,n+1}^{(p_{r})}:\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{n+1}(C)
given by
Z\mapsto Z+p_{r}.
The line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{p_{r}}
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C)
are preserved under pullback maps
(s_{n,n+1}^{(p)})^{*}
for any
p\in C.

Theorem
D
.

Let
\epsilon_{j}^{(r)}\in\mathrm{CH}^{1}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}})
be the exceptional divisor corresponding to
j
points supported on the last bubble along
p_{r}.
The Chow ring
\mathrm{CH}^{\star}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}})
has a presentation

\mathrm{CH}^{\star}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}})=\frac{\mathrm{CH}^{\star}(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C))[\epsilon^{(r)}_{j}\mid i_{r}+1\leq j\leq n,r=1,\dots,\ell]}{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{for each }j=n,\dots,i_{r}+1:\ Q_{n,j}^{(r)}(\epsilon^{(r)}_{n},\dots,\epsilon^{(r)}_{j});\\
\ker({(s^{(p_{r})}_{(n-j),n})}^{*})\cdot\epsilon^{(r)}_{j};\\
\text{for each }k=n-j,\dots,1:\ Q_{n-j,k}^{(r)}(\epsilon^{(r)}_{n},\dots,\epsilon^{(r)}_{j+k})\cdot\epsilon^{(r)}_{n-j}\end{array}\right\}_{r=1,\dots,\ell}}

where
Q_{m,h}
are multivariable polynomials (each corresponding to a top Chern class) with coefficients in
\langle c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{p_{r}}):r=1,\dots,\ell\rangle\subset\mathrm{CH}^{\star}(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(C))
defined as:

(1)

Q_{m,h}(t_{m},\dots,t_{h+1},t):=\prod_{k=1}^{h}(kt+c_{1}(\mathcal{L})-\sum_{j=1}^{m-h}(k+j)t_{j+h})
when
m>h>0,

(2)

Q_{m,m}(t):=\prod_{k=1}^{h}(kt+c_{1}(\mathcal{L}))
when
m=h,

(3)

formally
Q_{0,k}=0
for all
k.

1.3.
Euler characteristics

In Section
8
, we use the stratification of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
to compute the generating function of the classes
[\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)]\in K_{0}(\mathsf{Var}),
the Grothendieck ring of varieties.

Theorem
E
.

We have

\sum_{n\geq 0}[\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)]t^{n}=Z_{C}(t)\left(\frac{(1-\mathbb{L}t)(1-t)}{1-(\mathbb{L}+1)t}\right)^{\ell},

where
Z_{C}(t)=\sum_{m\geq 0}[\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(C)]t^{m}\in K_{0}(\mathsf{Var})[\![t]\!]
is the motivic Zeta function of
C.

The calculation specializes to, for instance, the generating function of Euler characteristics

\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D))t^{n}=(1-t)^{2g-2}\left(\frac{(1-t)^{2}}{1-2t}\right)^{\ell}.

1.4.
Perspectives

While logarithmic moduli spaces are primarily motivated by applications in enumerative geometry, it is of independent interest to relate their geometry to the well-studied non-logarithmic moduli spaces. Our work gives a concrete first example in this direction.

Let
\mathscr{C}\to\operatorname{Exp}(C|D)
be the universal curve over the stack
\operatorname{Exp}(C|D)
of expanded degenerations, and let
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathscr{C})
be the relative Hilbert scheme of length
n,
so that the morphism
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathscr{C})\to\operatorname{Exp}(C|D)
is representable. While being universally closed, the stack
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathscr{C})
is neither quasi-compact nor separated and has positive dimensional stabilizers.

The intermediate moduli stacks
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)_{\leq\bm{i}}
are open substacks of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathscr{C})
that are smooth, proper, and contain
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C\setminus D)
as a dense open substack. In other words, they are candidates to produce well-behaved moduli spaces from the stack
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathscr{C}).
From this point of view, the iterated weighted blow-up descriptions in Theorem
A
set up a VGIT-style wall-crossing between logarithmic and classical Hilbert scheme of points on a smooth marked curve, in which the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-quotients arise from action by the rubber torus. Instances of weighted blow-ups as wall-crossing morphisms include Shah’s work on degree-2 K3 surfaces
[
29
]
and its application to intersection theory
[
10
]
, K-moduli of plane curves
[
8
]
, and moduli of stable genus one curves
[
5
, §7]
.

More generally, given a moduli stack
\mathscr{M}
over (fine and saturated) logarithmic schemes, there often exists a piecewise linear space
\mathscr{S}
–this is a stack over fs log schemes that admit log modifications by Artin fans
[
16
]
–and a morphism
\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{S}
that is representable by an algebaric stack. Producing proper open substacks of
\mathscr{M}
and studying their wall-crossing morphisms is a systematic way to relate logarithmic moduli spaces and their non-logarithmic parallels.

1.5.
Related work

The general moduli problem of logarithmic Hilbert schemes is motivated by approaching enumerative geometry via degenerations: they are constructed for smooth pairs by Li–Wu
[
19
]
, for curves in simple normal crossings pair three-folds by Maulik–Ranganathan
[
23
]
and in full generality by Kennedy-Hunt
[
16
]
. Examples and their concrete geometric descriptions point to connections to the toric geometry of secondary polytope by work of Kennedy-Hunt on linear systems of curves in toric surfaces
[
17
]
and geometric invariant theory by work of Tschanz and Shafi–Tschanz on logarithmic Hilbert schemes of points on degenerations of K3 surfaces
[
30
,
28
]
. We also acknowledge the forthcoming work of Suraj Dash, which gives a combinatorial classification of GIT quotients arising from the relative Hilbert schemes of points over the stack of expansions in a more general setting.

1.6.
Future directions

As logarithmic Hilbert schemes are motivated by the study of moduli spaces via degeneration, it is natural to investigate logarithmic Hilbert and quot schemes of nodal curves as well as the relative logarithmic moduli spaces of the universal curve over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Analogous wall-crossing morphisms relating them to the ordinary moduli spaces can potentially lead to comparison formulas between enumerative invariants coming from the logarithmic and non-logarithmic moduli spaces.

The weighted blow-up picture explored in this work seems to be applicable to general logarithmic symmetric products and has application to the theory of logarithmic 0-cycles. In the surfaces case, combining this approach with
[
24
, §4]
should produce a complete description of the cohomology and Chow ring of the logarithmic Hilbert schemes of points on surfaces.

Viewing smooth points as divisors on curves, it will be worthwhile to study the moduli of logarithmic hypersurfaces from the perspective of wall-crossing. For instance, it will be interesting to produce intermediate moduli spaces that interpolate between the logarithmic linear system of curves on toric surfaces studied by Kennedy-Hunt
[
17
]
and the ordinary linear system.

2.1.
Expanded degenerations

Logarithmic Hilbert schemes of a curve parametrize subschemes on

5.1.
Relevant results for weighted blow-ups

In this subsection we will assume the basic definitions of weighted blow-ups following the work of Quek–Rydh
[

. A shorter introduction to weighted blow-ups can be found in
[

Intuitively, weighted blow-ups can be thought of as birational transformations replacing a chosen center
\mathcal{Z}
by a divisor
\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{Z}
has the structure of a bundle of weighted projective stacks
\mathcal{P}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})

Choosing the (strictly positive integer) weights
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
can be a subtle task, as it requires to assign
\mathbb{G}_{\operatorname{m}}
-weights to local trivializations of the normal bundle of
\mathcal{Z}
in
\mathcal{X}
that are compatible across the open cover. In this paper we will restrict our attention to weighted blow-ups with extra regularity properties, namely what Quek–Rydh defined as regular weighted blow-ups.

Remark 5.1
.

As a weighted blow-up is obtained as a quotient of the deformation to the weighted normal cone via the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\operatorname{m}},
the weighted normal cone is at the heart of understanding the geometry and intersection theory of weighted blow-ups. In our setting, the weighted blow-up centers are regularly embedded, and we will study their weighted normal bundles both in this section (to prove the weighted blow-up construction) and in Section
6.1
, where we compute its top Chern class to apply the weighted analogue of Keel’s formula. It is important to remark that the weighted normal bundle is a twisted weighted vector bundle (using the definition of
[
27
]
) and it is not necessarily isomorphic to the normal bundle.

The main goal of this section is to prove the maps of Theorem
A
are, indeed, weighted blow-ups. Constructing weighted blow-ups from the base is a rather complicated endeavor. However, as long as the objects in questions are smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks, checking whether or not a given map is a weighted blow-up is a significantly easier matter, thanks to the following weighted blow-down criterion.

Lemma 6.13

Let

Generating function of Euler characteristics

We determine the Euler characteristics of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
by calculating their classes in the Grothendieck ring of varieties. We use the locally closed stratification on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
itself, which is along the lines of §

Let
Z_{C}(t)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(C)t^{m}\in K_{0}(\mathsf{Var})[\![t]\!]
be the motivic Zeta function of
C.
We determine classes of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
in terms of
Z_{C}(t).

Theorem E
.

We have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)t^{n}=Z_{C}(t)\left(\frac{(1-\mathbb{L}t)(1-t)}{1-(\mathbb{L}+1)t}\right)^{\ell}.

Proof.

From the previous section,
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)
is stratified by

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)^{(m,\underline{\bm{\nu}})}\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(C\setminus D)\times\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i}}[\mathrm{Sym}^{\nu_{i}^{(j)}}(\mathbb{C}^{\star})/\mathbb{C}^{\star}]

for all tuples
(m,\underline{\bm{\nu}})
where
0\leq m\leq n,
and
m+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|\bm{\nu}_{i}|=n.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m,\underline{\bm{\nu}}):\\
m+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|\bm{\nu}_{i}|=n\end{subarray}}[\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(C|D)^{(m,\underline{\bm{\nu}})}]\cdot t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m,\underline{\bm{\nu}})}(\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(C\setminus D)\cdot t^{m})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\sum_{\underline{\bm{\nu}}}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i}}[\mathrm{Sym}^{\nu_{i}^{(j)}}(\mathbb{C}^{\star})/\mathbb{C}^{\star}]\cdot t^{\nu_{i}^{(j)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(C\setminus D)\cdot t^{m}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[\mathrm{Sym}^{j}\mathbb{C}^{\star}/\mathbb{C}^{\star}]\cdot t^{j}\right)^{k}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{m\geq 0}\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(C\setminus D)\cdot t^{m}\right)\frac{1}{\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[\mathrm{Sym}^{j}\mathbb{C}^{\star}/\mathbb{C}^{\star}]\cdot t^{j}\right)^{\ell}}

Since
Z_{(\text{-})}(t)
is motivic, we have
Z_{C}(t)=Z_{C\setminus D}(t)\left(Z_{\mathrm{pt}}(t)\right)^{\ell}=Z_{C\setminus D}(t)\cdot(1-t)^{-\ell}.
Also, the quotient stack

Using these two facts, the generating function simplifies to

{(1-t)^{\ell}\cdot Z_{C}(t)}\cdot{\left(\frac{1-(\mathbb{L}+1)t}{1-\mathbb{L}t}\right)^{-\ell}}

as desired.
∎

Appendix A
Toric structure of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)
via Chow quotients

by Dhruv Ranganathan

Corollary
C
of this paper states that that the logarithmic Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)
is toric; indeed, it is presented as a sequence of toric blowups of a toric variety. A closely related variant of this space is a well-known toric variety: the toric variety associated with the secondary polytope of the length-
n
interval. See results of Kennedy-Hunt
[
17
]
and also
[
23
, §6]
. Using this, one can check that the logarithmic Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)
is toric. Since
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
is
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, which is also toric, one might guess the intermediate case
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0)
. We briefly sketch this and leave many details to the reader.

Consider the “rubber” logarithmic Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}_{\mathrm{rub}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)
, parameterizing cycles on expansions of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
along
0
and
\infty
, but where two points are identified if they differ by
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-scaling in the main component. The moduli problem is represented by a Deligne–Mumford stack with logarithmic structure, by the same methods as
[
23
]
.

Separately, the linear system
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree-
n
divisors on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. There is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-scaling action on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
induced by the standard
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
H
denote the (normalized) Chow quotient of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
by this action. The Chow quotient parameterizes isomorphism classes of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant degenerations of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with a degree-
n
divisor. This follows from, for instance, the “finite case” of Alexeev’s work on complete semi-abelian moduli
[
4
, §2.10-12]
, or by work of Ascher–Molcho
[
9
]
. As such, there is a moduli map:

h:\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}_{\mathrm{rub}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)\to H

Proposition A.1
.

The morphism
h
is an isomorphism on coarse moduli spaces.

Proof.

By the moduli interpretation, one easily checks that
h
is finite. It is also clearly birational, since the interior parameterizes
n
points on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. The Chow quotient is normal by construction, so the map is an isomorphism by Zariski’s main theorem. ∎

As mentioned above, the Chow quotient of the linear system is the toric variety associated with the secondary polytope of the length-
n

[
15
]
. Therefore,

Corollary A.2
.

The coarse moduli space of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}_{\mathrm{rub}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)
is a toric variety.

One can adapt this argument to the non-rubber moduli space using the approach of Kennedy-Hunt
[
17
]
; we do not pursue this here. Nevertheless, inspecting the map

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)\to\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}_{\mathrm{rub}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty),

it is straightforward see that it is a flat family of chains
\mathbb{P}^{1}

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0+\infty)

One note of caution is that while

(\mathbb{P}^{1}|0)
