 ABSTRACT

Yttrium hydride (YHx) is a promising neutron moderator for advanced microreactor and space reactor
applications because of its high hydrogen density; however, its performance under neutron irradiation
remains insufficiently understood. To address this knowledge gap, Oak Ridge National Laboratory and Los
Alamos National Laboratory initiated an irradiation campaign under the US Department of Energy’s
Nuclear Science User Facilities (NSUF) program to evaluate the effects of irradiation temperature and
neutron fluence on the performance of YHx. Six irradiation capsules containing YHx specimens were
successfully irradiated in the High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR) at three target temperatures and two fast
fluence levels. In this FY, irradiation was completed, and capsules were transferred to the Irradiated
Materials Examination and Testing (IMET) facility for disassembly. The irradiated specimens and silicon
carbide thermometry were successfully recovered and transferred to the Low Activation Materials
Development and Analysis (LAMDA) laboratory. This report summarizes the completed irradiation
campaign, capsule disassembly activities, and the planned post-irradiation examination that will establish
the experimental irradiation temperatures and characterize irradiation-induced changes in the YHx
specimens.

 1. INTRODUCTION

Advanced nuclear systems for terrestrial microreactors and space reactor applications require moderator
materials capable of maintaining high hydrogen retention, dimensional stability, and efficient neutron
moderation under neutron irradiation [1][2][3]. Yttrium hydride (YHx) is a promising candidate due to its
high hydrogen density and thermal stability [4]. However, its irradiation behavior is insufficiently
understood, particularly with respect to irradiation-induced hydrogen redistribution, microstructural
evolution, and changes in material properties [5][6]. To address these knowledge gaps, under the
US Department of Energy’s Nuclear Science User Facilities (NSUF) program, Oak Ridge National
Laboratory (ORNL) and Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) initiated a collaborative irradiation
campaign in the High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR). The experiment consists of six irradiation capsules
containing YHx disk specimens irradiated at 300°C, 400°C, and 500°C and two fast fluence levels,
providing a systematic matrix to evaluate the effects of irradiation temperature and irradiation damage on
material performance [7].

The irradiation experiment leverages an existing HFIR capsule design containing 16 YHx disk specimens
and 8 silicon carbide (SiC) passive thermometry (TM) to confirm the irradiation temperature of the
experiment post irradiation. The capsule design, including the internal holder material and outer diameter,
gas fill, and HFIR irradiation location, was optimized to achieve the desired irradiation temperatures and
fast fluence levels. YHx specimens (with an average H/Y ratio of 1.9) were fabricated at LANL and
characterized through hydrogen content measurements, dimensional inspection, optical imaging, and x-ray
diffraction (XRD) prior to irradiation. Detailed descriptions of the experiment design, specimen
characterization, and capsule fabrication are available in Le Coq et al. [7].

This report summarizes the work completed during FY 2026, including completion of the HFIR irradiation
campaign, capsule disassembly, and preparations for post-irradiation examination (PIE).

 2. HFIR IRRADIATION

By the end of FY 2025, four YHx capsules (YHL01, YHL02, YHL04, YHL05) were successfully delivered
to HFIR for insertion in cycle 513 [7]. During FY 2026, the other two capsules (YHL03 and YHL06) were
successfully assembled, welded, tested, and delivered to HFIR for insertion in cycle 514.

 1

The irradiation campaign was designed to achieve two different levels of fast fluence (E > 0.1 MeV) using
two HFIR irradiation facilities [8]. The lower and higher levels of fast fluence (on average 8.7 × 1020 and
2.1 × 1021 n/cm2) were obtained using HFIR’s hydraulic tube (HT) and target rod rabbit holder (TRRH)
irradiation facilities. The HT facility enables capsule irradiation for partial HFIR cycles, whereas TRRH
irradiations span a full reactor cycle. This six-capsule irradiation campaign was successfully completed
following HFIR cycle 514 in March 2026. Table 1 summarizes the irradiation conditions for each capsule.

 3. CAPSULE DISASSEMBLY

Following irradiation, the capsules were transferred to ORNL’s Irradiated Materials Examination and
Testing (IMET) hot cell facility for capsule disassembly and recovery of the YHx disk specimens and SiC
TM. Each capsule was first identified and sectioned using a low-speed saw to recover the internal holder.
The holder was subsequently opened, and the internal components were removed. The recovered
component layout in cell enabled confirmation of the complete recovery of the 16 YHx disk specimens and
the 8 SiC TM contained in each capsule. All YHx disk specimens and TM were visually examined using an
in-cell microscope to document their identification and condition following irradiation and disassembly.
Figure 1 illustrates the principal steps of the disassembly process, and Table 2 summarizes the recovery
results. All six capsules were successfully disassembled, and every YHx disk specimen and TM was
recovered.

 4. PLANNED PIE ACTIVITIES

Following capsule disassembly, the recovered YHx disk specimens and SiC TM were transferred to ORNL’s
Low Activation Materials Development and Analysis (LAMDA) laboratory for PIE. Planned PIE activities
of the YHx specimens include optical imaging and mass measurements for all specimens as well as
evaluation of thermal properties and microstructural characterizations on selected specimens. Optionally,
high-energy XRD tests are also in consideration depending on synchrotron beamline availability.
Approximately half of the SiC TM from each capsule will be analyzed via dilatometry [9][10] to determine
the experimental TM temperature. The experimental temperature of the YHx specimens will be derived
from the dilatometry results and from the thermal analysis predicted temperature.

 5. CONCLUSIONS

During FY 2026, the NSUF YHx irradiation campaign advanced from experiment fabrication to PIE. All
six irradiation capsules were successfully irradiated in HFIR under the targeted range of temperatures and
fast neutron fluence levels and were subsequently disassembled in the IMET hot cell facility; the irradiated
YHx specimens and SiC TM were successfully recovered and transferred to LAMDA. The project has
therefore completed the irradiation phase and entered the examination phase.

 3

Planned PIE activities include SiC TM analysis to determine the experimental irradiation temperatures,
together with optical characterization, mass measurements, and XRD of the irradiated YHx specimens.
These examinations will provide the irradiation conditions and material characterization data needed to
evaluate the performance of YHx under neutron irradiation. Completion of the PIE campaign is planned for
FY 2027.
