Optimal upper and lower sequence spaces with applications
Sergey V. Astashkin, Per G. Nilsson

Abstract
. We study the optimal upper X U X_{U} and lower X L X_{L} sequence spaces that can be assigned to each Banach lattice X X . These spaces are symmetric, have the Fatou property and the unit vector basis has in these spaces very special properties. Determined by the order structure of X X the spaces X U X_{U} and X L X_{L} turn out to be very useful when studying Banach lattices. Among other results, in terms of these constructions, we identify Banach lattices that satisfy equal-norm upper and lower p p -estimates, give a characterization of L p ​ ( μ ) L_{p}(\mu) -spaces, derive some properties of the tensor product operator in Lorentz and Orlicz spaces, identify Orlicz spaces in which the unit vector basis is upper semi-homogeneous.

1.
Introduction

Recently, in the paper
[
1
]
, the authors have initiated the study of the optimal upper/lower sequence spaces assigned to Banach lattices. The introduction of these concepts was motivated primarily by the study of connections between upper/lower estimates for Banach lattices and the notion of
s
-decomposibility (see
[
1
]
for the definition), which has roots in the interpolation theory of operators. The use of the spaces
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
constructed by a Banach lattice
X
made it possible to obtain in
[
1
]
a characterization of relatively
s
-decomposable Banach lattices. As an application of the latter result, it was obtained in particular an orbital factorization
of relative
K
-functional estimates for Banach couples
\vec{X}=(X_{0},X_{1})
and
\vec{Y}=(Y_{0},Y_{1})
through some suitable couples of weighted
L_{{p}}
-spaces provided if
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
are relatively
s
-decomposable for
i=0,1
.

The first aim of this paper is a continuation of the study of properties of the optimal upper/lower sequence spaces
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
which was began in
[
1
]
. We show that these spaces are symmetric for each Banach lattice
X
, have the Fatou property and satisfy some duality type embeddings.

The second line of our research is connected with some applications of the above constructions to the study of Banach lattices. First, in terms of embeddings of the spaces
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
, we identify Banach lattices that satisfy equal-norm upper and lower
p
-estimates (see Proposition
3.8
) and give a characterization of
L_{p}(\mu)
-spaces (see Theorem
3.9
). Also, in Theorem
3.13
, we prove that a symmetric sequence space
E
coincides with a space
X_{U}
(resp.
X_{L}
) for some Banach lattice
X
if and only if the unit vector basis is upper (resp. lower) semi-homogeneous in
E
.
Recall that a basis
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in a Banach space
X
is called upper (resp.
lower
)
semi-homogeneous
if it is bounded and every bounded block basic sequence
\{y_{k}\}
of
\{x_{k}\}
is dominated by (resp. dominates)
\{x_{k}\}
.

In the final part of the paper we apply the obtained results to the study of symmetric Lorentz and Orlicz sequence spaces. Under some conditions we identify upper/lower optimal spaces for them that allows us in particular to recover some properties of the tensor product operator in these spaces. Among other results, in Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
, we prove that the unit vector basis is upper (resp. lower) semi-homogeneous in the Orlicz space
{\ell_{N}}
if and only if
N(uv)\leq CN(u)N(v)
(resp.
N(u)N(v)\leq CN(uv)
) for some constant
C>0
and all
0<u,v\leq 1
. Recall that a similar criterion for the lower semi-homogeneity of the Lorentz sequence spaces was obtained by Casazza and Bor-Luh Lin in
[
5
]
(by using a completely different method).

2.
Preliminaries.

2.1.
Banach lattices, upper and lower estimates for
disjoint elements.

We will assume that the reader is familiar with the definition of Banach
lattices, their basic properties and some basic terminology (see, for
instance,
[
17
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
24
]
). For definiteness, all Banach spaces and lattices considered in this paper are assumed to be real and infinite dimensional.

Let us start with recalling the notions of lower and upper estimates in
Banach lattices; see
[
17
, Definition 1.f.4]
.
A Banach lattice
X
is
said to
satisfy an upper (resp. a lower)
p
-estimate
, where
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
, if for some constant
M<\infty
and all finite
sequences of pairwise disjoint elements
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq X
it holds

(2.1)

\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big\|_{X}\leq M\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

(resp.

(2.2)

\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq M\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big\|\;).

The infimum of all
M
, satisfying the above inequality, is denoted by
M^{\left[p\right]}\left(X\right)
and
M_{\left[p\right]}\left(X\right)
, respectively. Note that every Banach lattice admits (trivially) an upper
1
-estimate and a lower
\infty
-estimate.

Recall that the Grobler-Dodds indices
\delta(X)
and
\sigma(X)
of a
Banach lattice
X
are defined by

\delta(X):=\sup\{p\geq 1:\,X\;\text{satisfies an upper $p$-estimate}\}

and

\sigma(X):=\inf\{p\geq 1:\,X\;\text{satisfies a lower $p$-estimate}\}.

For every Banach lattice
X
we have
1\leq\delta(X)\leq\sigma(X)\leq\infty
. Moreover, the following duality relations
hold:

\frac{1}{\delta(X)}+\frac{1}{\sigma(X^{\ast})}=1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\frac{1}{\sigma(X)}+\frac{1}{\delta(X^{\ast})}=1.

Further, the following somewhat weaker estimates for disjoint elements in
Banach lattices will be also of interest for us. A Banach lattice
X
is
said to
satisfy an equal-norm upper (resp. an equal-norm lower)
p
-estimate
, where
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
, if estimate
(
2.1
) (resp. (
2.2
)) holds for some constant
M>0
and all
finite sequences of pairwise disjoint elements
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq X
such that
\|x_{i}\|_{X}=1
,
i=1,\dots,n
. In other
words, this means that

\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big\|_{X}\leq Mn^{1/p}\;\;\mbox{(resp.}\;\;M\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big\|\geq n^{1/p}).

Clearly, every Banach lattice that admits an upper (resp. a lower)
p
-estimate admits an equal-norm upper (resp. an equal-norm lower)
p
-estimate as well. Moreover, it is known (see
[
8
, Lemma 2.3]
or
[
3
, Theorem 2]
) that

\delta(X)=\sup\{p\geq 1:\,X\;\text{satisfies an equal-norm upper $p$-estimate}\}

and

\sigma(X)=\inf\{p\geq 1:\,X\;\text{satisfies an equal-norm lower $p$-estimate}\}.

At the same time, there are Banach lattices which admit an equal-norm upper
(resp. an equal-norm lower)
p
-estimate, but fail to admit an upper (resp.
a lower)
p
-estimate. We refer, as an example, to the space
V_{\gamma,p}
, introduced by Tzafriri in
[
25
]
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We say that
{\ell_{p}}
is
finitely
lattice representable
in a Banach lattice
X
whenever for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and each
\varepsilon>0
there exist pairwise disjoint elements
x_{i}\in X
,
i=1,2,\dots,n
, such that for any
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\dots,n
we have

(2.3)

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|a_{i}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\right\|_{X}\leq\left(1+\varepsilon\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|a_{i}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Recall that a Banach lattice
X
is
order continuous
if, for every downward directed set
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}\subset X
with
\bigvee_{\alpha\in A}x_{\alpha}=0
, we have
\lim_{\alpha}\|x_{\alpha}\|_{X}=0
.

2.2.
Banach function spaces

Let
(T,\Sigma,\mu)
be a complete
\sigma
-finite measure space. The set of all a.e. finite real-valued functions (of equivalence classes) defined on
T
with natural algebraic operations and the topology of convergence in measure
\mu
on sets of finite measure is a linear topological space. We denote it by
S(T,\Sigma,\mu)
. The notation
x\leq y
, where
x,y\in S(T,\Sigma,\mu)
, means that there is a set
A\subset T
such that
\mu(T\setminus A)=0
and
x(t)\leq y(t)
for
t\in A
(we say that
x\leq y
a.e. on
T
).

A linear subspace
X\subset S(T,\Sigma,\mu)
is said to be a
function lattice
if
\{x\in S(T,\Sigma,\mu):\,|x|\leq|y|\}\subset X
for every
y\in X
. Any function lattice
X
, equipped with a norm such that
\|x\|\leq\|y\|
whenever
x,y\in X
and
|x|\leq|y|
, is called a
normed function space
. If
X
is complete as a normed linear space,
X
is said to be a
Banach function space (or lattice)
.

Any Banach function space
X
is linearly and continuously embedded into the corresponding linear topological space
S(T,\Sigma,\mu)
(see e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 4.3.1]
). This means that from the convergence in norm it follows the convergence in measure on all subsets of
T
of finite measure.

If
X
is a Banach function space, then the
Köthe dual

(or
associated
) function lattice
X^{\prime}
consists of all
y\in S(T,\Sigma,\mu)
such that

\|y\|_{X^{\prime}}:=\sup\,\biggl\{\int_{T}{x(t)y(t)\,d{\mu}}:\;\;\|x\|_{X}\,\leq{1}\biggr\}\,<\,\infty.

Any Köthe dual space
X^{\prime}
is complete with respect to the norm
y\mapsto\|y\|_{X^{\prime}}
and is embedded isometrically into (Banach) dual space
X^{*}.
Moreover,
X
is continuously embedded into its second Köthe dual
X^{\prime\prime}
, and
\|x\|_{X^{\prime\prime}}\leq\|x\|_{X}
for
x\in X
. A Banach function space
X
is embedded into
X^{\prime\prime}
isometrically if and only if the norm of
X
is
order semi-continuous
, i.e., if the conditions
x_{n}\in X,

n=1,2,\dots,

x\in{X}
, and
x_{n}\to{x}
a.e. on
T
imply that
||x||_{X}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{||x_{n}||_{X}}

[
13
, Theorem 6.1.6]
. A Banach function space
X

has the Fatou property
(or
maximal
) if from
x_{n}\in X,

n=1,2,\dots,

\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\|x_{n}\|_{X}<\infty
,
x\in S(T,\Sigma,\mu)
and
x_{n}\to{x}
a.e. on
T
it follows that
x\in X
and
||x||_{X}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{||x_{n}||_{X}}.
Observe that a Banach function lattice
X
has the Fatou property if and only if the natural inclusion of
X
into
X^{\prime\prime}
is a surjective isometry
[
13
, Theorem 6.1.7]
.

In the case when the measure space
(T,\Sigma,\mu)
is the set of positive integers with the counting measure
\mu
defined by
\mu(\{k\})=1,

k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, we will say about
Banach sequence spaces (or lattices)
. If
E
is such a space, the
Köthe dual
sequence lattice
E^{\prime}
is the set of all sequences
y=(y_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

\|y\|_{E^{\prime}}:=\sup\,\biggl\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}y_{k}:\;\;\|x\|_{E}\,\leq{1}\biggr\}<\infty.

For a detailed exposition of the theory of Banach function and sequence spaces see the monographs
[
2
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
17
]
.

2.3.
Symmetric sequence spaces

Let
(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence of real numbers. In what follows,
(a_{k}^{\ast})_{k=1}^{\infty}
denotes the nonincreasing
permutation of the sequence
(|a_{k}|)_{k=1}^{\infty}
defined by

a_{k}^{\ast}:=\inf_{\mathrm{card}\,A=k-1}\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}\setminus A}|a_{i}|,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}.

A Banach sequence space
E
is called
symmetric
if

E\subset{\ell_{\infty}}
and the conditions
y_{k}^{\ast}=x_{k}^{\ast}
,
k=1,2,\dots
,
x=(x_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in E
imply that
y=(y_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in E
and
\|y\|_{E}=\|x\|_{E}
.

For every symmetric sequence space
E
the following one norm continuous embeddings hold:

{\ell_{1}}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}E\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}{\ell_{\infty}}

[
15
, Theorem II.4.1]
.
Moreover, if
a=(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in E
and
\pi
is an arbitrary permutation of
\mathbb{N}
, then the sequence
a_{\pi}=(a_{\pi(k)})_{k=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
E
and
\|a_{\pi}\|_{E}=\|a\|_{E}.

Let
E
be a symmetric sequence space. The
fundamental function
of

E
is defined by the formula

\phi_{E}(n):=\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\Big\|_{E},\;\;n=1,2,\dots.

In what follows,
e_{k}
are the canonical unit vectors, i.e.,
e_{k}=(e_{k}^{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
,
e_{k}^{i}=0
for
i\neq k
and
e_{k}^{k}=1
,
k,i=1,2,\dots
.
For every symmetric space
E
,
\phi_{E}
is a nondecreasing and positive function such that
\varphi_{E}(n)/n
is nonincreasing.

Let
\phi_{E}=\phi_{E}(n)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be the fundamental function of a
symmetric sequence space
E
. We introduce the following
dilation functions
of
\phi_{E}
:

M_{E}^{0}(n):=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\phi_{E}(m)}{\phi_{E}(mn)}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;M_{E}^{\infty}(n):=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\phi_{E}(mn)}{\phi_{E}(m)},\;\;n\in\mathbb{N},

and the
fundamental indices
of
E
by

(2.4)

\mu_{E}=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}M_{E}^{0}(2^{n})\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\nu_{E}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}M_{E}^{\infty}(2^{n})

[
2
, Chapter 3, Exercise 14, p. 178]
. We have always that
0\leq\mu_{E}\leq\nu_{E}\leq 1
(see
[
15
, § II.4.4]
).

The most important examples of symmetric sequence spaces are the
{\ell_{p}}
-spaces,
1\leq p\leq\infty,
with the usual norms

{\|a\|}_{{\ell_{p}}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\left({\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}{|a_{k}|}^{p}\right)}^{1/p}\;,&1\leq p<\infty\\
\sup\limits_{k=1,2,\dots}|a_{k}|\;,&p=\infty\end{array}.\right.

Their generalization, the
{\ell_{p,q}}
-spaces,
1<p<\infty,

1\leq q\leq\infty,
are equipped with the quasi-norms

\|a\|_{p,q}:=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(a_{k}^{*})^{q}(k^{q/p}-(k-1)^{q/p})\Big)^{1/q}<\infty\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;q<\infty,

and

\|a\|_{p,\infty}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}n^{1/{p-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{*}<\infty

(see e.g.
[
9
]
).
The functional
a\mapsto\|a\|_{{\ell_{p,q}}}
does not
satisfy the triangle inequality for
p<q\leq\infty
, but it is equivalent to
some symmetric norm (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem 4.4.3]
). Clearly,
\ell_{p,p}={\ell_{p}}
,
1<p<\infty,
isometrically.
Moreover, for every
1<p<\infty,

1\leq q\leq\infty,
we have
({\ell_{p,q}})^{\prime}=\ell_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
, where
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
,
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
.

If the fundamental function
\phi_{E}
of a symmetric sequence space
E
satisfies
the estimate
\phi_{E}(m)\leq Cm^{1/p}
(resp.
m^{1/p}\leq C\phi_{E}(m)
) for
some
C>0
and all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
{\ell_{p,1}}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}E
(resp.
E\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}{\ell_{p,\infty}}
) (see
[
15
, Lemma II.5.2]
).

In turn, the
\ell_{p,q}
-spaces (when
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
) belong to a wider
class of the Lorentz spaces. Let
1\leq q<\infty
, and let
\{w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a nonincreasing sequence of positive numbers such that
w_{1}=1
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{k}=0
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{k}=\infty
.
The Lorentz space
\lambda_{q}(w)
(see, e.g.,
[
18
]
or
[
16
, Chapter 4e]
) consists of all sequences
a=(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}

satisfying

\|a\|_{\lambda_{q}(w)}:=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(a_{k}^{\ast})^{q}w_{k}\Big)^{1/q}<\infty.

Since the classical Hardy-Littlewood inequality (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem 2.2.2]
) yields

\|a\|_{\lambda_{q}(w)}=\sup_{\pi}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{\pi(k)}|^{q}w_{k}\Big)^{1/q},

where the supremum is taken over all permutations
\pi
of the set of
positive integers, the functional
a\mapsto\|a\|_{\lambda_{q}(w)}

defines on the space
\lambda_{q}(w)
a symmetric norm. Every Lorentz
sequence space is separable and has the Fatou property.

Clearly, the fundamental function of
\lambda_{q}(w)
is defined by

(2.5)

\phi_{\lambda_{q}(w)}(n)=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\Big)^{1/q},\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore, by (
2.4
), the fundamental indices of
\lambda_{q}(w)
can be calculated by the
formulae

(2.6)

\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{j}w_{k}}{\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}j}w_{k}}\right)^{1/q}.

and

(2.7)

\nu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}j}w_{k}}{\sum_{k=1}^{j}w_{k}}\right)^{1/q}.

Another natural generalization of the
\ell_{p}
-spaces are the Orlicz spaces
(see
[
14
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
). Let
N
be
an Orlicz function, that is, an increasing convex continuous function on
[0,\infty)
such that
N(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}N(t)=\infty
. The
Orlicz sequence space

l_{N}
consists of all sequences
a=(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

\|a\|_{l_{N}}:=\inf\left\{u>0:\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}N\Big(\frac{|a_{k}|}{u}\Big)\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

Without loss of generality, we will assume that
N(1)=1
. In particular, if
N(s)=s^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, we obtain
\ell^{p}
with the usual norm.

Observe that the definition of the space
\ell_{N}
depends (up to equivalence of norms) only on the behaviour of the function
N
near zero.

Every Orlicz space
{\ell_{N}}
has the Fatou property and it is separable if and
only if the function
N
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero
, i.e.,

\limsup_{u\to 0}\frac{N(2u)}{N(u)}<\infty.

Since the fundamental function
\phi_{{\ell_{N}}}
is defined by

(2.8)

\phi_{{\ell_{N}}}(n)=\frac{1}{N^{-1}(1/n)},\;\;n\in\mathbb{N},

where
N^{-1}
is the inverse function for
N
, one can readily check that
the fundamental indices of
{\ell_{N}}
can be calculated by the formulae:

(2.9)

\mu_{{\ell_{N}}}=\sup\Big\{1/q:\,\inf_{0<s,t\leq 1}\frac{N(st)}{N(s)t^{q}}>0\Big\}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\nu_{{\ell_{N}}}=\inf\Big\{1/q:\,\sup_{0<s,t\leq 1}\frac{N(st)}{N(s)t^{q}}<\infty\Big\}

(similar formulae for Orlicz spaces on
[0,1]
can be found in
[
17
, Proposition 2.b.5]
).

Finally,
\ell_{N}^{\prime}=\ell_{\tilde{N}}
, with the
Young conjugate function
\tilde{N}
.

2.4.
Lower/upper semi-homogeneous bases in Banach spaces

A basis
\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in a Banach space
X
is called
lower
(resp.
upper
)
semi-homogeneous
if it is bounded and every bounded block basic sequence
\{y_{k}\}
of
\{x_{k}\}
dominates (resp. is dominated by)
\{x_{k}\}
. In particular, if a basis is both lower and upper semi-homogeneous, then it is perfectly homogeneous and so it is equivalent, by a result of Zippin
[
26
]
, to the unit vector basis of
c_{0}
or
{\ell_{p}}
,
1\leq p<\infty
.

Let
E
be a symmetric sequence space. Then, if the unit vector basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is lower (resp. upper) semi-homogeneous in
E
, we have, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and every pairwise disjoint elements
u_{k}\in E
,
k=1,2,\dots,m
, that

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}u_{k}\Big\|_{E}\leq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|u_{k}\|_{E}e_{k}\Big\|_{E}

(resp.

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|u_{k}\|_{E}e_{k}\Big\|_{E}\leq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}u_{k}\Big\|_{E}\;).

In what follows, we call a Banach sequence lattice
E

lower (resp. upper) semi-homogeneous
if the unit vector basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is lower (resp. upper) semi-homogeneous in
E
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two positive functions (quasinorms). We write
F_{1}\asymp F_{2}
if there exists a positive constant
C
that does not depend
on the arguments of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
such that
C^{-1}F_{1}\leq F_{2}\leq CF_{1}
.
Finally, for any finite set
E\subset\mathbb{N}
by
|E|
we denote cardinality
of
E
.

3.
Optimal Upper and Lower Sequence Lattices

3.1.
Definitions and general properties

We start with recalling the definition and some general properties of a special kind of sequence spaces generated by some appropriate sequences of norms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see
[
1
]
).

Let
X
be an (infinite dimensional) Banach lattice,
S_{X}:=\{x\in X:\,\|x\|_{X}=1\}
. For each positive integer
n,
let
\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)
denote the set
of all sequences
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq S_{X}
of pairwise disjoint elements. Observe that these sets are non-empty for every
n
(see Lemma 3.2 in
[
1
]
). Based on
X
, we construct the
upper

X_{U}
and the
lower

X_{L}

optimal sequence spaces
that satisfy the following norm one continuous
embeddings:

(3.1)

{\ell_{1}}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}X_{U}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}X_{L}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}{\ell_{\infty}}.

To construct
X_{U}
we define first, for each fixed positive integer
n
, the
following norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\left\|(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{X_{U}\left(n\right)}:=\sup\left\{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\right\|_{X}:\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)\right\}.

Let
X_{U}
then be the space of all real-valued sequences
a=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
, for which

\left\|a\right\|_{X_{U}}:=\sup_{n}\left\|(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{X_{U}\left(n\right)}<\infty.

Since

\left\|(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{X_{U}\left(n\right)}\leq\left\|(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{\ell_{1}^{n}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|a_{i}\right|,\;\;n\in\mathbb{N},

we get the left-hand side embedding in
\left(\ref{embeddings}\right)
. Clearly, if
X
satisfies an
upper
p
-estimate, we have

\left\|a\right\|_{X_{U}}\leq M^{\left[p\right]}\left(X\right)\left\|a\right\|_{{\ell_{p}}}.

The first step in the definition of the space
X_{L}
is the introduction of
the functional
\Phi
, defined for
a=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\Phi_{n}\left(a\right):=\inf\left\{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\right\|_{X}:\,\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)\right\}.

Next, we set

\left\|a\right\|_{X_{L}\left(n\right)}:=\inf\left\{\sum_{k\in F}\Phi_{n}\left(a^{k}\right):\,F\subseteq\mathbb{N},\left|F\right|<\infty,a^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},a=\sum_{k\in F}a^{k}\right\}.

Since

\left\|a\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{n}}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|a_{i}\right|\leq\Phi_{n}\left(a\right),

the functional
a\mapsto\left\|a\right\|_{X_{L}\left(n\right)}
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Finally, we define
X_{L}
to be the space
of all real-valued sequences
a=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
, for
which

\left\|a\right\|_{X_{L}}:=\sup_{n}\left\|(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{X_{L}\left(n\right)}<\infty.

One can easily see that the second and third embeddings in
\left(\ref{embeddings}\right)
hold and moreover

\left\|a\right\|_{{\ell_{p}}}\leq M_{\left[p\right]}\left(X\right)\left\|x\right\|_{X_{L}},

whenever the Banach lattice
X
satisfies a lower
p
-estimate.

Note that the construction, which leads to the space
X_{L}
, is close to the one
developed by Junge in
[
10
]
.

Next, we proceed with proving some properties of the spaces
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
, which complement the results obtained in the paper
[
1
]
. The first result can be treated as a sharp version of Theorem 3.3 from
[
1
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every Banach lattice
X
, the spaces
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
are symmetric.

Proof.

Observe that this result for the space
X_{L}
was obtained in
[
1
]
in full, while for
X_{U}
it was proved under the additional condition that
{\ell_{\infty}}
is not finitely representable in
X
(see Section
2.1
). Thus, it remains to show that this assumption is in fact superfluous.

Indeed, from an inspection of the proof of
Theorem 3.3 in
[
1
]
it follows that the above assumption is used only in
the proof of Lemma 6.2 in the same paper. Specifically, we have to select,
for every positive integer
m
, pairwise disjoint elements
w_{1}^{(m)},\dots,w_{m}^{(m)}\in X
such that
\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}^{(m)}\|_{X}=1
and

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\min_{j=1,\dots,m}\|w_{j}^{(m)}\|_{X}=0.

Clearly, the latter
holds if we have the following:

(3.2)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup\Big\{\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\Big\|_{X}:\,\left\{x_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)\Big\}=\infty.

Assuming that (
3.2
) is not the case, for some
C>0

and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
\left\{x_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)
and
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
,
k=1,\dots,n
, we get

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}x_{k}\Big\|_{X}\leq C\max_{k=1,\dots,n}|a_{k}|=C\|(a_{k})_{k=1}^{n}\|_{\ell_{\infty}}.

Hence,
X_{U}={\ell_{\infty}}
, and the result, we wished, follows again.
∎

Denote by
X_{L}^{0}
(resp.
X_{U}^{0}
) the closed linear span of
all finitely supported sequences in
X_{L}
(resp.
X_{U}
).

Corollary 3.2
.

If
X
is an arbitrary Banach lattice, then the canonical unit vectors
e_{k}
,
k=1,2,\dots
, form a symmetric normalized basis in both spaces
X_{U}^{0}
and
X_{L}^{0}
.

In particular, if the support of a sequence
a=(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is finite, say,
\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\,a_{k}\neq 0\}\subset\{1,2,\dots,n\}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
\|a\|_{X_{L}}=\|a\|_{X_{L}(n)}
and
\|a\|_{X_{U}}=\|a\|_{X_{U}(n)}
.

The spaces
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
do not need to be order continuous even if
X
possesses this property (for instance,
(c_{0})_{U}=(c_{0})_{L}={\ell_{\infty}}
; see
[
1
, Example 3.5]
). At the same
time, the construction of the optimal upper and lower sequence spaces
ensures that they
always
have the Fatou property.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every Banach lattice
X
, the spaces
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
have the Fatou property.

Proof.

We start with proving this result for the optimal upper space
X_{U}
. Let
a^{n}=(a_{k}^{n})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in X_{U}
,
a_{k}^{n}\geq 0
,
\sup_{n}\|a^{n}\|_{X_{U}}<\infty

and
a_{k}^{n}\uparrow a_{k}
as
n\to\infty
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We have
to prove that
a=(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in X_{U}
and
\|a\|_{X_{U}}\leq\sup_{n}\|a^{n}\|_{X_{U}}
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. From the
assumption it follows that for all sufficiently large
n
we have

(3.3)

0\leq a_{k}-a_{k}^{n}\leq\varepsilon,\;\;k=1,2,\dots,m.

Hence, for every
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}\in\mathfrak{B}_{m}\left(X\right)
we obtain

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}u_{k}\Big\|_{X}\leq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}(a_{k}-a_{k}^{n})u_{k}\Big\|_{X}+\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}^{n}u_{k}\Big\|_{X}\leq\varepsilon m+\|(a_{k}^{n})_{k=1}^{m}\|_{X_{U}(m)},

whence

(3.4)

\|(a_{k})_{k=1}^{m}\|_{X_{U}(m)}\leq\varepsilon m+\|a^{n}\|_{X_{U}}

if
n
is sufficiently large. Therefore, since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary,
we get

\|(a_{k})_{k=1}^{m}\|_{X_{U}(m)}\leq\sup_{n}\|a^{n}\|_{X_{U}},

which implies that

\|a\|_{X_{U}}\leq\sup_{n}\|a^{n}\|_{X_{U}}<\infty.

Thus,
a\in X_{U}
.

Now, we proceed with proving the same property for
X_{L}
. Again, assume that
a^{n}=(a_{k}^{n})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in X_{L}
,
a_{k}^{n}\geq 0
,
\sup_{n}\|a^{n}\|_{X_{L}}<\infty

and
a_{k}^{n}\uparrow a_{k}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. For a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
we find a positive integer
n_{0}
such that inequality
\left(\ref{add0b}\right)
holds for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Let
n\geq n_{0}
be fixed.
There is a representation
(a_{k}^{n})_{k=1}^{m}=\sum_{i\in A}b^{i}
,
b^{i}=(b_{k}^{i})_{k=1}^{m}
,
|A|<\infty
, such that

(3.5)

\|(a_{k}^{n})_{k=1}^{m}\|_{X_{L}(m)}\geq\sum_{i\in A}\Phi_{m}(b^{i})-\varepsilon.

Setting
d_{k}^{i}:=b_{k}^{i}+\frac{1}{|A|}(a_{k}-a_{k}^{n})
,
i\in A
,
k=1,\dots,m
, we obtain

\sum_{i\in A}d_{k}^{i}=\sum_{i\in A}b_{k}^{i}+a_{k}-a_{k}^{n}=a_{k}^{n}+a_{k}-a_{k}^{n}=a_{k},\;\;k=1,\dots,m,

that is,
(a_{k})_{k=1}^{m}=\sum_{i\in A}d^{i}
, where
d^{i}=(d_{k}^{i})_{k=1}^{m}
. Furthermore, in view of
\left(\ref{add0b}\right)
, we have

|d_{k}^{i}-b_{k}^{i}|=\frac{1}{|A|}(a_{k}-a_{k}^{n})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{|A|},\;\;i\in A,\;k=1,\dots,m.

Consequently, as above, for every
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}\in\mathfrak{B}_{m}\left(X\right)
and each
i\in A
, we get

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}d_{k}^{i}u_{k}\Big\|_{X}\leq\frac{m\varepsilon}{|A|}+\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}^{i}u_{k}\Big\|_{X}.

By the definition of the functional
\Phi_{m}(\cdot)
, this
implies that

\Phi_{m}(d^{i})\leq\frac{m\varepsilon}{|A|}+\Phi_{m}(b^{i}),\;\;i\in A,

and hence from inequality
\left(\ref{add0c}\right)
it follows that

\|(a_{k})_{k=1}^{m}\|_{X_{L}(m)}\leq\sum_{i\in A}\Phi_{m}(d^{i})+\varepsilon\leq\sum_{i\in A}\Phi_{m}(b^{i})+\varepsilon(m+1)\leq\|(a_{k}^{n})_{k=1}^{m}\|_{X_{L}(m)}+\varepsilon(m+2).

Thus, since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}

\|(a_{k})_{k=1}^{m}\|_{X_{L}(m)}\leq\|a^{n}\|_{X_{L}}.

Hence,

\|a\|_{X_{L}}=\sup_{m}\|(a_{k})_{k=1}^{m}\|_{X_{L}(m)}\leq\sup_{n}\|a^{n}\|_{X_{L}}<\infty,

that is,
a\in X_{L}
, and the proof is completed.
∎

In view of embeddings (
3.1
),
X_{U}

and
X_{L}
are both normalized intermediate spaces with respect to the
Banach couple
\left({\ell_{1}},\ell_{\infty}\right).
Moreover, from Proposition

3.3
and Calderón-Mityagin theorem (see
[
2
, Theorem 3.2.12]
or
[
15
, Theorem II.4.9]
) we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.4
.

For every Banach lattice
X
, the optimal sequence lattices
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
are exact interpolation spaces with respect to the couple
\left({\ell_{1}},\ell_{\infty}\right).

3.2.
Duality type properties of optimal sequence spaces

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be an order semi-continuous Banach function space on some measure space
(T,\Sigma,\mu)
. The following embeddings hold:

\displaystyle(X^{\prime})_{L}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}(X_{U})^{\prime}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;(X^{\prime})_{U}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}\left(X_{L}\right)^{\prime}.

Proof.

To prove the first embedding, we need to show that for every
b=(b_{k})\in(X^{{}^{\prime}})_{L}
the functional

\langle a,b\rangle:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}b_{k},\;\;a=(a_{k})\in X_{U},

is bounded on the space
X_{U}
and
\|b\|_{(X_{U})^{\prime}}\leq\|b\|_{(X^{{}^{\prime}})_{L}}
. One can easily see that it suffices
to check that for each
\varepsilon>0
and all
a=(a_{k})\in X_{U}
,
b=(b_{k})\in(X^{{}^{\prime}})_{L}
such that
a_{k}\geq 0
,
b_{k}\geq 0

the following inequality holds:

(3.6)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}b_{k}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|b\|_{(X^{{}^{\prime}})_{L}}\|a\|_{X_{U}}.

To this end, select
\delta>0
such that
\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}<1+\varepsilon
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by the definition of the functional
\Phi_{n}^{X^{\prime}}(\cdot)
, we choose
\left\{v_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X^{{}^{\prime}}\right)
,
v_{k}\geq 0
, such that

(3.7)

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}v_{k}\Big\|_{X^{\prime}}\leq(1+\delta)\cdot\Phi_{n}^{X^{{}^{\prime}}}((b_{k})_{k=1}^{n}).

Further, there exist
u_{k}\in X
,
u_{k}\geq 0
,
k=1,\dots,n
, which satisfy the
conditions:
\|u_{k}\|_{X}=1
and

1-\delta<\left\langle u_{k},v_{k}\right\rangle=\int\nolimits_{T}u_{k}(t)v_{k}(t)\,d\mu\leq 1,1\leq k\leq n.

Let us take for
w_{k}
the restriction of
u_{k}
to the support of
v_{k}
. Then,
\left\|w_{k}\right\|_{X}\leq 1
and

\left\langle w_{k},v_{k}\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{k},v_{k}\right\rangle>1-\delta,\;1\leq k\leq n.

Therefore, since
\left\|w_{k}\right\|\leq 1,

by
\left(\ref{add3}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}b_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\delta}\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}w_{k},\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}v_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\delta}\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left\|w_{k}\right\|_{X}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\frac{w_{k}}{\left\|w_{k}\right\|}\right\|_{X}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}v_{k}\right\|_{X^{{}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\left\|\left(a_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{n}\right\|_{X_{U}\left(n\right)}\Phi_{n}^{X^{{}^{\prime}}}\left(\left(b_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\left\|\left(a_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{n}\right\|_{X_{U}\left(n\right)}\Phi_{n}^{X^{{}^{\prime}}}\left(\left(b_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{n}\right).

Assume now that
(b_{k})_{k=1}^{n}=\sum_{i\in A}d^{i}
, where
d^{i}=(d_{k}^{i})_{k=1}^{n}
and
|A|<\infty
. Then, as above, choosing functions
v_{k}
and
u_{k}
in an appropriate way, we get

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}d_{k}^{i}<(1+\varepsilon)\left\|\left(a_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{n}\right\|_{X_{U}\left(n\right)}\Phi_{n}^{X^{{}^{\prime}}}\left(d^{i}\right),\;\;i\in A,

and hence

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}b_{k}=\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}d_{k}^{i}<(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{i\in A}\Phi_{n}^{X^{{}^{\prime}}}(d^{i})\|(a_{k})_{k=1}^{n}\|_{X_{U}(n)}.

Passing to the infimum over above representations of
(b_{k})_{k=1}^{n}
, we
conclude that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}b_{k}<(1+\varepsilon)\|(b_{k})_{k=1}^{n}\|_{(X^{{}^{\prime}})_{L}(n)}\|(a_{k})_{k=1}^{n}\|_{X_{U}(n)}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|b\|_{(X^{{}^{\prime}})_{L}}\|a\|_{X_{U}},

which implies
\left(\ref{add3a}\right)
. Thus, since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, the embedding
(X^{\prime})_{L}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}(X_{U})^{\prime}
is proved.

To prove the second embedding, let us apply the first one to the Köthe dual space
X^{{}^{\prime}}.
It follows that

(3.8)

(X^{\prime\prime})_{L}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}((X^{{}^{\prime}})_{U})^{{}^{\prime}}.

Being order semi-continuous,
X
is isometrically embedded as a sublattice into
X^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
(see Section
2.2
). Therefore,
\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)\subset\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X^{\prime\prime}\right)
and
so
X_{L}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}(X^{\prime\prime})_{L}
. Combining this embedding together with
(
3.8
), we obtain that
X_{L}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}\left(\left(X^{{}^{\prime}}\right)_{U}\right)^{{}^{\prime}}.
Passing to the Köthe duals and using the fact that
(X^{\prime})_{U}
has the Fatou property (see Proposition
3.3
), we deduce
that

(X^{\prime})_{U}=\left((X^{\prime})_{U}\right)^{\prime\prime}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}\left(X_{L}\right)^{\prime}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Optimal sequence spaces and
order estimates of Banach lattices

As it follows from results obtained in
[
1
]
, the properties of optimal sequence spaces
X_{U}
and
X_{L}
are largely determined by the fact which upper/lower estimates are fulfilled in
X
and, in particular, by the
Grobler-Dodds indices of
X
.

Proposition 3.6
.

[
1
, Proposition 3.9 and Corollary 3.11]

For every Banach lattice
X
we have:

(i)
X_{U}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}\ell_{\delta(X)}
and
{\ell_{p}}\hookrightarrow X_{U}
for every
p<\delta(X)
;

(ii)
\ell_{\sigma(X)}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}X_{L}
and
X_{L}\hookrightarrow\ell_{q}
for every
q>\sigma(X)
;

(iii)
X_{U}={\ell_{p}}
if and only if
p=\delta(X)
and
X
admits an upper

\delta(X)
-estimate;

(iv)
X_{L}=\ell_{q}
if and only if
q=\sigma(X)
and
X
admits a
lower
\sigma(X)
-estimate;

(v)
\delta(X_{U})=\delta(X)
and
\sigma(X_{L})=\sigma(X)
.

Example 3.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and let
{\ell_{p,q}}
be the Lorentz sequence spaces (see Section
2.3
).
It is well known that
\delta({\ell_{p,q}})=\min(p,q)
,
\sigma({\ell_{p,q}})=\max(p,q)
, and moreover, that
{\ell_{p,q}}
admits an upper
\delta({\ell_{p,q}})
-estimate and a
lower
\sigma({\ell_{p,q}})
-estimate (see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 3]
).
Consequently, by Proposition
3.6
,
({\ell_{p,q}})_{U}=\ell_{\min(p,q)}
and
({\ell_{p,q}})_{L}=\ell_{\max(p,q)}
.

Here, we prove a compliment to Proposition
3.6
, which characterizes Banach lattices
X
that satisfy an equal-norm
upper (resp. lower)
p
-estimate in terms of embeddings of the optimal upper
space
X_{U}
(resp. optimal lower space
X_{L}
). Observe that equal-norm
estimates were used in
[
8
]
for the classification of
decomposable pairs of Banach function spaces.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
X
be a Banach lattice. Then, we have:

(a)
X
satisfies an equal-norm upper
p
-estimate with constant
C

if and only if
{\ell_{p,1}}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}X_{U}
;

(b) if
X_{L}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}{\ell_{p,\infty}}
, then
X
satisfies an equal-norm lower
p
-estimate with constant
C
.

Assume that
X
is an order semi-continuous Banach function space. Then,
X
satisfies an equal-norm lower
p
-estimate with constant
C
if and only if
X_{L}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}{\ell_{p,\infty}}
.

Proof.

(a) Let
{\ell_{p,1}}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}X_{U}
. By the
definition of
X_{U}
and the fact that the fundamental function
\phi_{{\ell_{p,1}}}
of
{\ell_{p,1}}
is equal to
n^{1/p}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}u_{k}\Big\|_{X}\leq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\Big\|_{X_{U}}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\Big\|_{{\ell_{p,1}}}=Cn^{1/p},

which implies that
X
admits an equal-norm upper
p
-estimate with constant

C
(see Section
2.1
).

Conversely, assume that
X
satisfies an equal-norm upper
p
-estimate with constant
C
. Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\varepsilon>0

there is
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)

such that

\phi_{X_{U}}(n)=\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\Big\|_{X_{U}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}u_{k}\Big\|_{X}\leq C(1+\varepsilon)n^{1/p},\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence, as was observed in Section
2.3
, it follows the embedding
{\ell_{p,1}}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}X_{U}
.

(b) By the definition of the
X_{L}
-norm, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)
, we have

n^{1/p}=\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\Big\|_{p,\infty}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\Big\|_{X_{L}}\leq C\Phi_{n}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\Big)\leq C\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}u_{k}\Big\|_{X}.

Therefore,
X
satisfies an equal-norm lower
p
-estimate with constant
C
.

To complete the proof, it remains to show that the converse to assertion (b) holds whenever
X
is an order semi-continuous Banach function space.

Let
X
admit an equal-norm lower
p
-estimate. Since
X^{\prime}
satisfies then an equal-norm upper
q
-estimate,
1/p+1/q=1
, with the same constant
(see, for instance,
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
), by the part (a) of this proposition, we obtain that
\ell_{q,1}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}(X^{\prime})_{U}
.
Hence, by duality (see Section
2.3
), we get

((X^{\prime})_{U})^{\prime}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}(\ell_{q,1})^{\prime}={\ell_{p,\infty}}.

On the other hand, since
X
is order semi-continuous, it is isometrically embedded as a sublattice into
X^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)\subset\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X^{\prime\prime}\right)
, which implies that

X_{L}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}(X^{\prime\prime})_{L}
. Moreover, the dual space
X^{\prime}
is order semi-continuous, and so from Proposition
3.5
it follows that
(X^{\prime\prime})_{L}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}((X^{\prime})_{U})^{\prime}
.
Combining the last embeddings, we conclude that
X_{L}\overset{C}{\hookrightarrow}{\ell_{p,\infty}}
, and the proof is completed.
∎

3.4.
A
characterization of
L_{p}(\mu)
-spaces in terms of optimal sequence spaces.

One can easily see that if
X=L_{p}(\mu)
for some
\sigma
-finite measure space
(T,\Sigma,\mu)
and
1\leq p<\infty
, then
X_{U}=X_{L}(={\ell_{p}})
. We show here that, under some conditions, the converse holds as well (for a similar result see
[
8
]
).

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
X
be an order continuous Banach lattice with the Fatou property such that
X_{U}=X_{L}
with equivalence of norms.
Then
X
is order isomorphic either to
L_{p}(\mu)
-space, for some
1\leq p<\infty
and measure space
(T,\Sigma,\mu)
, or to
c_{0}(\Gamma)
, for some set
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of pairwise disjoint elements from
X
. Then, from the definition of optimal upper and lower spaces it follows that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|u_{k}\|_{X}e_{k}\Big\|_{X_{L}}\leq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}u_{k}\Big\|_{X}\leq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|u_{k}\|_{X}e_{k}\Big\|_{X_{U}}.

Since by the assumption
X_{U}=X_{L}
, there exists a constant
C>0

3.5.
A characterization of optimal sequence spaces.

Let
E
be an order semi-continuous Banach sequence lattice. According to
[
1
, Proposition 3.8(a)]
,
E\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}E_{L}
and
E_{U}\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}E
.
In this section, we will be interested in identification of Banach sequence lattices
E
such that the opposite embeddings hold. In other words, we intend to find conditions, under which
E=X_{L}
or
E=X_{U}
for some Banach lattice
X
. Observe that, in view of Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.3
, this is possible only if
E
is order isomorphic to a symmetric sequence space with the Fatou property.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
E
be a Banach sequence lattice satisfying the following property: there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
,
k=1,\dots,n
,
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(E\right)
there is an injective mapping
\pi:\,\{1,\dots,n\}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

(3.9)

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}e_{\pi(k)}\Big\|_{E}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}u_{k}\Big\|_{E}.

Then,
E_{L}
is the minimal symmetric space with the Fatou property that contains
E
. More precisely, if
F
is a symmetric space with the Fatou property such that
E\overset{C_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}F
, then the embedding
E_{L}\overset{CC_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}F
holds.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a=(a_{k})_{k=1}^{n}
be arbitrary. From (
3.9
) and the definition of the functional
\Phi_{n}(\cdot)
it follows that

\Phi_{n}(a)\geq\frac{1}{C}\inf_{\pi}\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}e_{\pi(k)}\Big\|_{E},

where the infimum is taken over all injective mappings
\pi:\,\{1,\dots,n\}\to\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore, assuming that
F
is a symmetric space satisfying
E\overset{C_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}F
, for every
\varepsilon>0
and some representation
a=\sum_{i\in A}a^{i}
, where
a^{i}=(a^{i}_{k})
and
|A|<\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|a\|_{E_{L}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{i\in A}\Phi_{n}(a^{i})\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{C}\sum_{i\in A}\inf_{\pi}\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{i}e_{\pi(k)}\Big\|_{E}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\varepsilon)}{CC_{1}}\sum_{i\in A}\inf_{\pi}\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{i}e_{\pi(k)}\Big\|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\varepsilon)}{CC_{1}}\sum_{i\in A}\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{i}e_{k}\Big\|_{F}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\varepsilon)}{CC_{1}}\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i\in A}a_{k}^{i}e_{k}\Big\|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\varepsilon)}{CC_{1}}\|a\|_{F}.

Since
F
has the Fatou property and
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, this implies that
E_{L}\overset{CC_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}F
. Hence, the proof is completed.
∎

3.6.
Tensor product in semi-homogeneous symmetric spaces

Let
a=(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
b=(b_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be two bounded equences of real numbers. By
a\otimes b
we will denote the
tensor product
of
a
and
b
, i.e., the sequence
(a_{i}b_{j})_{i,j=1}^{\infty}
.

Let
E,F
and
G
be symmetric sequence spaces. The tensor product operator defined by

(3.13)

(a,b)\mapsto a\otimes b

is said to be bounded from direct product
E\times F
in
G
if there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
a\in E
and
b\in F
it holds:

\|(a\otimes b)^{*}\|_{G}\leq C\|a\|_{E}\|b\|_{F},

where
(a\otimes b)^{*}
is the permutation of the sequence
(|a_{i}b_{j}|)_{i,j=1}^{\infty}
in decreasing order.

Following
[
10
]
, we represent the tensor product for finitely supported sequences slightly in a different way. Let

a=(a_{k})
and
b=(b_{k})
be two finitely supported sequences, say,
a_{k}=b_{k}=0
if
k>n
. Then the tensor product of
a
and
b
may be defined as follows:

a\otimes b:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\tilde{b}^{i},

where
\tilde{b}^{i}
are the following ”shifts” of the sequence
b
:

\tilde{b}^{i}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}e_{(i-1)n+k}

(in particular,
\tilde{b}^{1}=b
).

Let us show that operator (
3.13
) has some special properties in semi-homogeneous symmetric spaces.

Recall that
\phi_{E}
is the fundamental function of a symmetric sequence space
E

(see Section
2.3
).

Proposition 3.15
.

(a) If
E
is an upper semi-homogeneous symmetric sequence space, then operator
(
3.13
) is bounded from
E\times E
into
E
.

In particular, for some
C>0

\phi_{E}(mn)\leq C\phi_{E}(n)\phi_{E}(m),\;\;n,m\in\mathbb{N}.

(b) If
E
is a lower semi-homogeneous symmetric sequence space, we have

\|a\|_{E}\|b\|_{E}\leq C\|a\otimes b\|_{E}.

In particular,

\phi_{E}(n)\phi_{E}(m)\leq C\phi_{E}(mn),\;\;n,m\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

We prove only assertion (b) (the proof of (a) follows by the same lines).

By Theorem
3.13
(a),
E=E_{L}
and hence, thanks to Proposition
3.3
, the space
E
has the Fatou property. Consequently, it suffices to consider
only finitely supported sequences
a
and
b
.

Assume that
a_{k}=b_{k}=0
for all
k>n
. Then, taking into account that
\|\tilde{b}^{i}\|_{E}=\|b\|_{E}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
, by the assumption, applied to the sequences
\tilde{b}^{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, we get

\|a\|_{E}\|b\|_{E}=\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\|\tilde{b}^{i}\|_{E}e_{i}\Big\|_{E}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\tilde{b}^{i}\Big\|_{E}=C\|a\otimes b\|_{E}.

Thus, the first assertion is proved. Since the second assertion is an immediate consequence of the first one, the proof is completed.
∎

4.
Applications to Lorentz and Orlicz sequence spaces

4.1.
Lorentz spaces

Let
\{w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a nonincreasing sequence of positive numbers such that
w_{1}=1
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{k}=0
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{k}=\infty
,
1\leq q<\infty
, and let
\lambda_{q}(w)
be the Lorentz sequence space (see Section
2.3
). Recall also that
\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}
and
\nu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}
are the lower and upper fundamental indices of the fundamental function
\phi_{\lambda_{q}(w)}
(see formulae (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
)).

Proposition 4.1
.

We have the following:

(i)
(\lambda_{q}(w))_{U}={\ell_{q}}
;

(ii)
(\lambda_{q}(w))_{L}=\ell_{1/\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}}
(equivalently,
(\lambda_{q}(w))_{L}={\ell_{p}}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
) if and only if
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
n,l\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

(4.1)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\leq Cl^{-q\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}}\sum_{k=1}^{ln}w_{k}.

In particular, in the case when
\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}=1/q
inequality (
4.1
)
is fulfilled if and only if
w
is equivalent to a constant sequence, that is,
\lambda_{q}(w)={\ell_{q}}
.

Proof.

(i) Since
\lambda_{q}(w)
is the
q
-convexification of the space
\lambda_{1}(w)
(see e.g.
[
17
, 1d, p. 53]
),
\lambda_{q}(w)
satisfies an upper
q
-estimate. Moreover, it does not satisfy an upper
r
-estimate for any
r>q
(see
[
23
]
,
[
4
]
or
[
12
, Theorem 3]
). Thus,
\delta(\lambda_{q}(w))=q
. Combining this fact with Proposition
3.6
(iii), we get the desired result.

(ii) By
[
4
]
(see also
[
12
, Theorem 10]
),
\lambda_{q}(w)
satisfies a lower
p
-estimate if and only if
the sequence
n^{-q/p}S_{n}
, where
S_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}
, is equivalent to an increasing sequence. Clearly, this holds if and only if there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that for all positive integers
j,l
we have

(4.2)

S_{j}\leq C_{p}l^{-q/p}S_{lj}.

We claim that inequality (
4.2
) holds if
p>1/\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}
. Indeed, combining the last condition with formula (
2.6
), we obtain

S_{j}\leq C_{p}^{\prime}2^{-nq/p}S_{2^{n}j}

for some
C_{p}^{\prime}>0
and all
n=0,1,\dots
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
, choosing
n
so that
2^{n}\leq l<2^{n+1}
and taking into account that
\{w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is nonincreasing, we infer

S_{j}\leq C_{p}^{\prime}2^{-(n+1)q/p}S_{2^{n+1}j}\leq C_{p}^{\prime}l^{-q/p}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{lj}w_{k}+\sum_{k=lj+1}^{2^{n+1}j}w_{k}\Big)\leq 2C_{p}^{\prime}l^{-q/p}S_{lj},

and our claim is proved.
Moreover, from (
2.6
) it follows that the opposite inequality
p<1/\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}
implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2^{nq/p}S_{j}}{S_{2^{n}j}}=\infty.

Hence, then (
4.2
) fails to hold.

As a result of the above discussion, we see that the lower Grobler-Dodds index
\sigma(\lambda_{q}(w))
is equal to
1/\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}
.
Therefore, inequality (
4.1
) holds if and only if the space
\lambda_{q}(w)
satisfies a lower
\sigma(\lambda_{q}(w))
-estimate. Thus, applying Proposition
3.6
(iv), we conclude that (
4.1
) is equivalent to the fact that
(\lambda_{q}(w))_{L}=\ell_{1/\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}}
(or
(\lambda_{q}(w))_{L}={\ell_{p}}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
).

Finally, since
\{w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a nonincreasing sequence, inequality (
4.1
) may be valid only if
\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}\leq 1/q
. Hence, assuming that
\mu_{\lambda_{q}(w)}=1/q
, we obtain the last assertion of the proposition.

∎

From Proposition
4.1

4.2.
Orlicz spaces

Let
N
be an Orlicz function on
[0,\infty)
such that
N(0)=0
and
N(1)=1
and let
{\ell_{N}}
be the Orlicz sequence space (see Section

).

We start with giving rather simple necessary and sufficient conditions under which
{\ell_{N}}
is a lower (resp. an upper) semi-homogeneous space.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
N
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero. The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)
{\ell_{N}}
is lower semi-homogeneous;

(b)
{\ell_{N}}=({\ell_{N}})_{L}
;

(c)
{\ell_{N}}=X_{L}
for some Banach lattice
X
;

(d) there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all
a,b\in{\ell_{N}}
it holds

\|a\|_{{\ell_{N}}}\|b\|_{{\ell_{N}}}\leq C\|a\otimes b\|_{{\ell_{N}}};

(e) there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that for all
0<s,t\leq 1
we have

N(s)N(t)\leq C_{1}N(st).

Proof.

Observe first that the implication
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.13
(a), while the implications
(b)\Rightarrow(c)
and
(d)\Rightarrow(e)
are obvious.

(c)\Rightarrow(d)
. From (c), Theorem
3.13
(a) and Proposition
3.15
(b) it follows that

\phi_{{\ell_{N}}}(n)\phi_{{\ell_{N}}}(m)\leq C^{\prime}\phi_{{\ell_{N}}}(mn),\;\;n,m\in\mathbb{N}.

Now, combining this together with equality (
2.8
), the
\Delta_{2}
-condition and convexity of
N
, one easily gets (d).

(e)\Rightarrow(a)
.
We need to show that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}\left(X\right)
and
a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
,
k=1,\dots,n
it holds

(4.4)

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}e_{k}\Big\|_{{\ell_{N}}}\leq C_{1}\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}u_{k}\Big\|_{{\ell_{N}}},

where
C_{1}
is the constant from condition (e).
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a_{k}\geq 0
,
u_{k}=\sum_{i\in A_{k}}b_{i}e_{i}
, where
A_{k}
are pairwise disjoint sets of positive integers and
b_{i}\geq 0
for all

. Moreover, by homogeneity, it can be assumed also that
a_{k}\leq 1
,

. Then, since
\|u_{k}\|_{{\ell_{N}}}=1
, we have
\sum_{i\in A_{k}}N(b_{i})=1,

(in particular, this implies that

because of the fact that

is an increasing function and

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}u_{k}\Big\|_{{\ell_{N}}}=1,

from the assumption it follows that

Since

and

is an Orlicz function, this implies that

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}e_{k}\Big\|_{{\ell_{N}}}\leq C_{1}.

Thus, (

Proposition 4.6

(c)
{\ell_{N}}=X_{U}
for some Banach lattice
X
;

(d) there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all
a,b\in{\ell_{N}}
it holds

\|a\otimes b\|_{{\ell_{N}}}\leq C\|a\|_{{\ell_{N}}}\|b\|_{{\ell_{N}}};

(e) there exists a constant
C_{1}\geq 1
such that for all
0<s,t\leq 1
we have

N(st)\leq C_{1}N(s)N(t).

It is known (see e.g.
[
11
]
) that the Orlicz space
{\ell_{N}}
admits an upper
p
-estimate (resp. a lower
p
-estimate) if and only if there exists a constant
K>0
such that for all
v\geq 1
and
0<u\leq vu\leq 1
we have

N(v)u^{p}\leq K\cdot N(vu)

(resp.

N(vu)\leq K\cdot N(v)u^{p}\;).

Assume that
N
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero. Then, from easy calculations it follows that the latter inequalities hold if and only if
there exists a constant
K^{\prime}>0
such that for all
0<s,t\leq 1

N(st)\leq K^{\prime}\cdot N(s)t^{p}

(resp.

N(s)t^{p}\leq K^{\prime}\cdot N(st)\;).

Hence, taking into account formulae (
2.9
), we conclude that
\delta({\ell_{N}})=1/\nu_{{\ell_{N}}}
and
\sigma({\ell_{N}})=1/\mu_{{\ell_{N}}}
. Consequently, applying Proposition
3.6
(iii),(iv), we get the following result.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
N
be an Orlicz function satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero. The following conditions (a), (b) and (c) (resp. (a’), (b’) and (c’)) are equivalent:

(a)
({\ell_{N}})_{U}={\ell_{p}}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
;

(b)
({\ell_{N}})_{U}=\ell_{1/\nu_{{\ell_{N}}}}
;

(c) there exists a constant
K^{\prime}>0
such that for all
0<s,t\leq 1
we have

N(st)\leq K^{\prime}\cdot N(s)t^{1/\nu_{{\ell_{N}}}};

(a’)
({\ell_{N}})_{L}={\ell_{p}}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
;

(b’)
(\ell_{N})_{L}=\ell_{1/\mu_{\ell_{N}}}
;

(c’) there exists a constant
K^{\prime}>0
such that for all
0<s,t\leq 1
we have

N(s)t^{1/\mu_{{\ell_{N}}}}\leq K^{\prime}\cdot N(st).

Example 4.8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
N
be an Otlicz function on
[0,\infty)
such that
N(t)\asymp t^{p}\log^{a}(e/t)
for
0<t\leq 1
.

One can easily check that
\mu_{{\ell_{N}}}=\nu_{{\ell_{N}}}=1/p
. Then, if
a\geq 0
, from Propositions
4.6
and
4.7
, respectively, it follows that conditions (e) and (c’) are fulfilled. Hence,
({\ell_{N}})_{U}={\ell_{N}}
and
({\ell_{N}})_{L}={\ell_{p}}
. Similarly, in the case when
a\leq 0
, conditions (e) and (c) from Propositions
4.5
and
4.7
, respectively, hold. Therefore, we have:
({\ell_{N}})_{L}={\ell_{N}}
and
({\ell_{N}})_{U}=\ell_{p}
.

Finally, let
N(t)\asymp t\log^{a}(e/t)
,
0<t\leq 1
, where
a\leq 0
. Then, in the same way, applying Propositions
4.5
and
4.7
, we get
({\ell_{N}})_{L}={\ell_{N}}
and
({\ell_{N}})_{U}=\ell_{1}
.
