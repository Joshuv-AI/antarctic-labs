On the Convergence of Numerical Index via Operator Openings and Ultraproducts
Monika, Tattwamasi Amrutam, Priyadarshi Dey

Abstract
. The numerical index of a Banach space is a geometric constant relating the numerical radius of bounded linear operators to their standard operator norm. In this paper, we study the continuity of the numerical index under two distinct notions of subspace convergence. First, we establish a full limit theorem in the operator opening topology: if { X n } n ∈ ℕ \{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} and X X are closed subspaces of a Banach space Y Y with X n → X X_{n}\to X in the operator opening, then lim n → ∞ n ⁡ ( X n ) = n ⁡ ( X ) \lim_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})=n(X) . Second, we develop ultraproduct methods for the numerical index, proving that the numerical radius is exactly preserved by ultraproduct operators, i.e., v ⁡ ( ( T n ) 𝒰 ) = lim 𝒰 v ⁡ ( T n ) v((T_{n})_{\mathcal{U}})=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}v(T_{n}) . As a consequence, we show that n ⁡ ( X 𝒰 ) ≤ n ⁡ ( X ) n(X_{\mathcal{U}})\leq n(X) for every ultrapower 𝒰 \mathcal{U} .



1.
Introduction

The numerical index of a Banach space
X
, denoted
n(X)
, is a geometric constant that relates the numerical radius of bounded linear operators on
X
to their standard operator norm. First introduced by Lumer in 1968, the numerical index has since played a central role at the intersection of Banach space geometry and operator theory. However, explicit computation of
n(X)
remains difficult, particularly for infinite-dimensional spaces.

A natural approach for addressing this difficulty is approximation: given a Banach space
X
, can we approximate it by a family of simpler subspaces
\{X_{m}\}
and compute
n(X)
via the limit of the numerical indices
n(X_{m})
? This approach has been explored in the literature, most notably by Martín et al.
[
12
]
, who obtained limsup inequalities under the existence of norm-one complemented subspaces. Subsequently, Aksoy and Lewicki
[
1
]
established full limit theorems under Local and Global Characterization Conditions (LCC and GCC). Conditions such as LCC and GCC depend on the existence of norm-one projections compatible with norming functionals. While these hypotheses are satisfied in many classical settings, they exclude a broad class of Banach spaces in which such projections are difficult to construct, such as hereditarily indecomposable spaces
[
8
,
11
]
.

In this paper, we propose two approaches that overcome these limitations, neither of which requires bounded projections.

Our first approach replaces the projection-based framework with a topological one. Rather than projecting from
X
onto
X_{m}
, we ask how close
X_{m}
is as a subspace of
X
, measured by the
operator opening
. For closed subspaces
Y
,
Z
of a Banach space
X
, the operator opening is defined by

r(Y,Z)=\max\{r_{0}(Y,Z),\,r_{0}(Z,Y)\},

where

r_{0}(Y,Z)=\inf\bigl\{\|C-I\|:C\in GL(X),\,C(Y)=Z\bigr\},

with the convention
r_{0}(Y,Z)=1
when no such invertible operator exists. The topology induced by the operator opening majorizes the topology induced by the geometric (gap) opening
[
13
, Theorem 4.2(c)]
. Our first main result establishes the full continuity of the numerical index under this topology.

Theorem A
.

Let
Y
be a Banach space and let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
X
be closed subspaces of
Y
. If
X_{n}\to X
in the operator opening topology, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})=n(X).

The proof proceeds in two steps. First, we establish norm bounds on the invertible operators present in the operator opening condition (Lemma
2.3
). Second, we apply the Bishop-Phelps-Bollobás theorem to approximate the norming functionals. Together, these tools allow us to prove both the inequalities
\limsup_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})\leq n(X)
and
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})\geq n(X)
, yielding the full limit. The core advantage of the operator-opening topology is the existence of near-identity isomorphisms, which allow us to conjugate optimal operators between
X_{n}
and
X
, thereby circumventing the need for projections or operator extensions.

While the operator opening topology provides a clean, universal framework, it is naturally stronger than the classical gap (or geometric) opening topology. A fundamental open question is whether geometric convergence in the gap topology alone is sufficient to guarantee the continuity of the numerical index. We address this question via ultraproduct methods.

Our second main result establishes the exact preservation of the numerical radius under the ultraproduct construction.

Theorem B
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of Banach spaces and let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a free ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. For any uniformly bounded sequence of operators
T_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{n})
,

v\big((T_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\big)=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}v(T_{n}).

A key consequence of this result is the inequality
n(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n})\leq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}n(X_{n})
, and in particular
n(X_{\mathcal{U}})\leq n(X)
for every ultrapower. When combined with the observation that gap topology convergence
X_{n}\to X
inside an ambient space
Y
implies the ultraproduct identification
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}=X_{\mathcal{U}}
inside
Y_{\mathcal{U}}
(Remark
2.7
), we obtain the following reduction.

Theorem C
.

Let
Y
be a Banach space and let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
X
be closed subspaces of
Y
with
X_{n}\to X
in the gap topology. Then for every free ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
,

n(X_{\mathcal{U}})\leq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}n(X_{n}).

Consequently, if
n(X_{\mathcal{U}})=n(X)
holds for the space
X
, then
n(X)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})
.

This reduces the gap topology convergence problem to the following purely intrinsic question about ultrapowers (see Question
3.7
). We note that the answer is affirmative when
X
is lush (since lushness is preserved by ultraproducts
[
5
]
, giving
n(X_{\mathcal{U}})=n(X)=1
) and trivially when
n(X)=0
. For general Banach spaces, the question remains open.

2.
Preliminaries

Given a Banach space
X
, we write
B_{X},S_{X}
, and
X^{*}
to denote the unit ball, unit sphere, and the dual space of
X
and define

\Pi(X):=\{(x,x^{*}):x\in S_{X},x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}},x^{*}(x)=1\}.

We briefly recall the notions of numerical range, numerical radius, and numerical index and refer the readers to the classical monographs by Bonsall and Duncan
[
3
,
4
]
for more details.

Definition 2.1

(Numerical range, Numerical radius, and Numerical index)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space over
\mathbb{K}
and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
be a bounded linear operator.
The numerical range of
T
is the set

V(T):=\left\{x^{*}(Tx):\ x\in S_{X},\ x^{*}\in S_{X^{\ast}},x^{\ast}(x)=1\right\}.

Then, the numerical radius of
T
is the supremum of absolute values of the elements in the numerical range, i.e.,

v(T):=\sup\left\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in V(T)\right\}.

Finally, the numerical index of
X
is defined by taking the infimum of the numerical radius of norm one operators on
X
, that is

n(X):=\inf\left\{v(T):\ T\in\mathcal{L}(X),\ \|T\|=1\right\}.

Finally, we need the following easy modification of the well-known Bishop-Phelps-Bollóbas Theorem
[
2
, Theorem 1]
. We record it for completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose there exists
u\in B_{X}
and
u^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
such that

\text{Re }u^{*}(u)=1-\delta,

where
|\delta|<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}
. Then there exists a state pair
(y,y^{*})\in\Pi(X)
such that

\|u-y\|<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\|u^{*}-y^{*}\|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
|\delta|<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}
and
\text{Re }u^{*}(u)=1-\delta
,

\text{Re }u^{*}(u)=1-\delta\geq 1-|\delta|>1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

By the Bishop-Phelps-Bollóbas Theorem for the closed unit ball
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
, there exists a pair
(y,y^{*})\in\Pi(X)
with
\|u-y\|<\varepsilon
and
\|u^{*}-y^{*}\|<\varepsilon
. The claim follows.
∎

2.1.
Gap Topology

For a Banach space
X
, let
G(X)
denote the collection of all closed subspaces of
X
. For any
x\in X
and subset
A\subseteq X
, recall that the distance is defined as
\text{dist}(x,A)=\inf_{a\in A}\|x-a\|
.

For
Y,Z\in G(X)
, the geometric (or gap) opening between
Y
and
Z
is defined as

Q(Y,Z)=\max\left\{\sup_{y\in S_{Y}}\text{dist}(y,S_{Z}),\sup_{z\in S_{Z}}\text{dist}(z,S_{Y})\right\}.

For a sequence
Y_{m}\in G(X)
and
Y\in G(X)
, we say that
Y_{m}\to Y
in the gap topology (in the sense of Ostrovskii
[
13
]
) if
\lim_{m\to\infty}Q(Y_{m},Y)=0
. If a sequence of subspaces
X_{m}\to X
in the gap topology inside an ambient space
Y
, the condition
Q(X_{m},X)\to 0
yields two crucial approximation properties for their unit spheres that we will use extensively.

(1)

Because
\sup_{x\in S_{X}}\text{dist}(x,S_{X_{m}})\to 0
, any fixed vector on the unit sphere of the limit space can be approximated by vectors from the unit spheres of the sequence. Specifically, for any fixed
x_{0}\in S_{X}
, there exists a sequence
x_{m}\in S_{X_{m}}
such that
\|x_{m}-x_{0}\|_{Y}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.

(2)

Because
\sup_{z\in S_{X_{m}}}\text{dist}(z,S_{X})\to 0
, any sequence of vectors picked from the unit spheres of
X_{m}
eventually gets arbitrarily close to the unit sphere of
X
. Specifically, for any sequence
x_{m}\in S_{X_{m}}
, there exists a corresponding sequence
u_{m}\in S_{X}
such that
\|x_{m}-u_{m}\|_{Y}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.

2.2.
Operator Opening Topology

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
G(X)
denote the set of closed subspaces of
X
. By
GL(X)
we denote the group of all invertible linear operators on
X
. For
Y,Z\in G(X)
, let

r_{0}(Y,Z)=\inf\{\|C-I\|:C\in GL(X),C(Y)=Z\},

if the set over which the infimum is taken is not empty, and
r_{0}(Y,Z)=1
otherwise.
The
operator opening
between
Y
and
Z
is defined by

r(Y,Z)=\max\{r_{0}(Y,Z),r_{0}(Z,Y)\}.

The topology induced by the operator opening majorizes the topology induced by the geometric opening
[
13
, Theorem 4.2(c)]
. We show below that if
X_{m}\to X
in the operator opening topology, then
\lim_{m\to\infty}n(X_{m})=n(X)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Y
be a Banach space. Let
X
and
Z
be closed subspaces of
Y
. Suppose that there exists
C\in GL(Y)
with
C(X)=Z
and
\|C-I\|<\frac{\eta}{2}
for some
0<\eta<2
. Then,

\frac{1}{\|C\|\|C^{-1}\|}\geq\frac{2-\eta}{2+\eta}.

Proof.

To begin with, since
\|C-I\|<1
, the Neumann series gives
C^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(I-C)^{k}
and hence

\|C^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\|I-C\|}\leq\frac{2}{2-\eta}.

Moreover, by the triangle inequality, we get that

\|C\|\leq\|I\|+\|C-I\|<1+\frac{\eta}{2}=\frac{2+\eta}{2},

which in turn implies that

\|C\|\|C^{-1}\|\leq\frac{2+\eta}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{2-\eta}=\frac{2+\eta}{2-\eta},

which is equivalent to the one claimed.
∎

2.3.
Ultraproducts of Banach Spaces

We briefly recall the Banach space ultraproduct construction and refer the reader to Heinrich
[
10
]
for a comprehensive treatment.

Definition 2.4

(Banach space ultraproduct)
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of Banach spaces and let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a free ultrafilter on
{\mathbb{N}}
. The ultraproduct of
\{X_{n}\}
with respect to
{\mathcal{U}}
is the quotient
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}=\ell_{\infty}(\{X_{n}\})/c_{0,{\mathcal{U}}}(\{X_{n}\})
, where
c_{0,{\mathcal{U}}}(\{X_{n}\})=\{(x_{n})\in\ell_{\infty}(\{X_{n}\}):\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|x_{n}\|=0\}
. The equivalence class of
(x_{n})
is denoted
(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}
, and the quotient norm satisfies
\|(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\|=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|x_{n}\|
. When all
X_{n}=X
, the ultraproduct is called the ultrapower
X_{\mathcal{U}}
, and the diagonal embedding
\delta(x)=(x,x,\ldots)_{\mathcal{U}}
is an isometry.

Definition 2.5

(Ultraproduct of operators)
.

For
T_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{n})
with
\sup_{n}\|T_{n}\|<\infty
, the ultraproduct operator
(T_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}((x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}})=(T_{n}x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}
satisfies
\|(T_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\|=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|T_{n}\|
.

Remark 2.6

(The dual of an ultraproduct)
.

The natural map
\Phi\colon\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}^{*}\to(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n})^{*}
defined by
\Phi((f_{n})_{\mathcal{U}})((x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}})=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}f_{n}(x_{n})
is an isometric embedding. In general
\Phi
is not surjective, but
\Phi(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}^{*})
is always a
1
-norming subspace: for every
(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
,

(1)

\|(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\|=\sup\big\{|\lim_{\mathcal{U}}f_{n}(x_{n})|:(f_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}^{*},\;\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|f_{n}\|\leq 1\big\}.

Indeed, choosing
f_{n}\in S_{X_{n}^{*}}
with
f_{n}(x_{n})=\|x_{n}\|
achieves the supremum.

Remark 2.7
.

If
X_{n}\to X
in gap topology inside an ambient Banach space
Y
, then
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}\cong X_{\mathcal{U}}
isometrically inside
Y_{\mathcal{U}}
. For the inclusion
(\subseteq)
: let
(x_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}X_{n}
with
x_{n}\in X_{n}
. We may assume, without loss of generality that
x_{n}\neq 0
for
{\mathcal{U}}
-almost all
n
. Let
x_{n}=\|x_{n}\|\cdot\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}.
For
\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}\in S_{X_{n}}
, gap convergence (property 2 from Subsection
2.1
) yields
v_{n}\in S_{X}
with

\bigg\|\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}-v_{n}\bigg\|_{Y}\leq\sup_{z\in S_{X_{n}}}dist(z,S_{X})\to 0.

Set
u_{n}=\|x_{n}\|v_{n}\in X
. Since
\|u_{n}\|_{Y}=\|x_{n}\|_{Y}\leq M
, and

\|x_{n}-u_{n}\|_{Y}=\|x_{n}\|_{Y}\bigg\|\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}-v_{n}\bigg\|_{Y}\leq M\cdot\sup_{z\in S_{X_{n}}}dist(z,S_{X})\to 0.

Hence
(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}=(u_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\in X_{\mathcal{U}}
. The reverse inclusion
(\supseteq)
follows symmetrically using property 1.

3.
Proof of Main Results

We begin by establishing the full limit theorem for the numerical index under the operator opening topology, which serves as the formal proof for Theorem
A
. The core advantage of this topology is algebraic in that there are near-identity isomorphisms, which allow us to conjugate optimal operators on
X_{n}
to valid test operators on
X
(and vice versa). By strictly controlling the condition numbers of these conjugating maps via Lemma
2.3
, we ensure that the numerical radius and the operator norm are asymptotically preserved, circumventing the need for global Hahn-Banach extensions altogether.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
Y
be a Banach space and let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
X
be closed subspaces of
Y
. If
X_{n}\to X
in the operator opening topology, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})=n(X).

Proof.

Let
1>\varepsilon>0
. Since
X_{n}\to X
in the operator opening topology,
\exists~n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
s.t
\forall n\geq n_{0}
,
r(X_{n},X)<\frac{\eta}{2}
, where
\eta=\min\{\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\}
. This in turn implies that
r_{0}(X_{n},X)<\frac{\eta}{2}
and
r_{0}(X,X_{n})<\frac{\eta}{2}
. Since
\varepsilon<1
, there exists
C_{n},D_{n}\in GL(Y)
such that

C_{n}(X_{n})=X,\quad\|C_{n}-I\|<\frac{\eta}{2},\quad D_{n}(X)=X_{n},\quad\|D_{n}-I\|<\frac{\eta}{2}.

Claim 1:

\limsup_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})\leq n(X)
.

Choose an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
with
\|T\|=1
such that
v(T)<n(X)+\varepsilon
. For each
n
, define the operator
T_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{n})
by

T_{n}:=C_{n}^{-1}\circ T\circ C_{n}\big|_{X_{n}}.

Since
T=C_{n}\circ T_{n}\circ C_{n}^{-1}
, we see that
1=\|T\|\leq\|C_{n}\|\|T_{n}\|\|C_{n}^{-1}\|
which together with Lemma
2.3
implies that

(2)

\|T_{n}\|\geq\frac{1}{\|C_{n}\|\|C_{n}^{-1}\|}\geq\frac{2-\eta}{2+\eta}.

Let
(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\in\Pi(X_{n})
be arbitrary. Define

x:=C_{n}x_{n}\in X\quad\text{and}\quad x^{*}:=x_{n}^{*}\circ C_{n}^{-1}\in X^{*}.

Observe that
x^{*}(x)=x_{n}^{*}(C_{n}^{-1}C_{n}x_{n})=x_{n}^{*}(x_{n})=1
. Also,

(3)

x_{n}^{*}(T_{n}x_{n})=x_{n}^{*}(C_{n}^{-1}TC_{n}x_{n})=x^{*}(Tx).

Observe that

\|x\|\leq\|C_{n}\|\|x_{n}\|\leq\frac{2+\eta}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|x^{*}\|\leq\|x_{n}^{*}\|\|C_{n}^{-1}\|\leq\frac{2}{2-\eta}.

Let
u:=\frac{x}{\|x\|}\in S_{X}
and
u^{*}:=\frac{x^{*}}{\|x^{*}\|}\in S_{X^{*}}
. Then

u^{*}(u)=\frac{x^{*}(x)}{\|x\|\|x^{*}\|}=\frac{1}{\|x\|\|x^{*}\|}\geq\frac{1}{\big(\frac{2+\eta}{2}\big)\big(\frac{2}{2-\eta}\big)}=\frac{2-\eta}{2+\eta}>1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

Thus,
u^{*}(u)=1-\delta
where
\delta<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}
. By Lemma
2.2
, there exists a state
(y,y^{*})\in\Pi(X)
such that
\|u-y\|<\varepsilon
and
\|u^{*}-y^{*}\|<\varepsilon
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle|u^{*}(Tu)|

\displaystyle\leq|y^{*}(Ty)|+\|u^{*}\|\|T\|\|u-y\|+\|u^{*}-y^{*}\|\|T\|\|y\|

\displaystyle<v(T)+2\varepsilon.

Relating this back to equation (
3
), we see that

|x_{n}^{*}(T_{n}x_{n})|=|x^{*}(Tx)|=\|x\|\|x^{*}\||u^{*}(Tu)|\leq\frac{2+\eta}{2-\eta}\big(v(T)+2\varepsilon\big).

Since
(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\in\Pi(X_{n})
was arbitrary, taking the supremum on both sides gives

v(T_{n})\leq\frac{2+\eta}{2-\eta}\big(v(T)+2\varepsilon\big).

Since
n(X_{n})\leq\frac{v(T_{n})}{\|T_{n}\|}
, using (
2
) yields

\displaystyle n(X_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{v(T_{n})}{\|T_{n}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2+\eta}{2-\eta}\cdot\frac{1}{\|T_{n}\|}\big(v(T)+2\varepsilon\big)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2+\eta}{2-\eta}\right)^{2}\big(v(T)+2\varepsilon\big)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2+\eta}{2-\eta}\right)^{2}(n(X)+3\varepsilon).

Taking the limit superior as
n\to\infty
on both sides, we get that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})\leq\left(\frac{2+\eta}{2-\eta}\right)^{2}(n(X)+3\varepsilon).

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
gives us that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})\leq n(X)
.

Claim 2:

\liminf_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})\geq n(X)
.

For each
n\geq n_{0}
, choose an operator
T_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{n})
with
\|T_{n}\|=1
such that
v(T_{n})<n(X_{n})+\varepsilon
.
Define
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
by

T:=D_{n}^{-1}\circ T_{n}\circ D_{n}\big|_{X}.

Using Lemma
2.3
, as before,

\|T\|\geq\frac{\|T_{n}\|}{\|D_{n}\|\|D_{n}^{-1}\|}\geq\frac{2-\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}.

Let
(x,x^{*})\in\Pi(X)
be an arbitrary state. Define
x_{n}:=D_{n}x\in X_{n}
and
x_{n}^{*}:=x^{*}\circ D_{n}^{-1}\in X_{n}^{*}
.
We have
x_{n}^{*}(x_{n})=1
and
x^{*}(Tx)=x_{n}^{*}(T_{n}x_{n})
.
Observe that

\|x_{n}\|\leq\|D_{n}\|\leq\frac{2+\eta}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|x_{n}^{*}\|\leq\frac{2}{2-\eta}.

Letting
u_{n}:=\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
and
u_{n}^{*}=\frac{x_{n}^{*}}{\|x_{n}^{*}\|}
, we see that

u_{n}^{*}(u_{n})\geq\frac{2-\eta}{2+\eta}=1-\frac{2\eta}{2+\eta}>1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

Using Lemma
2.2
, there exists
(y_{n},y_{n}^{*})\in\Pi(X_{n})
such that
\|u_{n}-y_{n}\|<\varepsilon
and
\|u_{n}^{*}-y_{n}^{*}\|<\varepsilon
. Therefore,

|u_{n}^{*}(T_{n}u_{n})|\leq|y_{n}^{*}(T_{n}y_{n})|+2\varepsilon\leq v(T_{n})+2\varepsilon.

Therefore, we can bound our initial evaluation:

|x^{*}(Tx)|=\|x_{n}\|\|x_{n}^{*}\||u_{n}^{*}(T_{n}u_{n})|\leq\frac{2+\eta}{2-\eta}\big(v(T_{n})+2\varepsilon\big).

Taking the supremum over all
(x,x^{*})\in\Pi(X)
gives
v(T)\leq\frac{2+\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}\big(v(T_{n})+2\varepsilon\big)
.
Since
n(X)\leq\frac{v(T)}{\|T\|}
, we get that

n(X)\bigg(\frac{2-\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}\bigg)\leq v(T)\leq\frac{2+\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}\big(n(X_{n})+3\varepsilon\big).

Consequently,

n(X)\bigg(\frac{2-\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}\bigg)^{2}-3\varepsilon\leq n(X_{n}).

Taking the limit inferior as
n\to\infty
gives us that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})\geq n(X)\bigg(\frac{2-\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}\bigg)^{2}-3\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
was arbitrary, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
gives
n(X)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})
. The claim follows. Combining Claim 1 and Claim 2 yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})=n(X).

∎

It is important to emphasize that the families of subspaces considered in
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]
do not, in general, converge in the operator opening topology, as the following example demonstrates.

Example 3.2
.

Let
Z=\ell_{2}
and for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
let

Z_{m}:=\text{span}\;\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}\},

where
(e_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is the standard orthonormal basis of
\ell_{2}
. Then
(Z_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies that
\overline{\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}Z_{m}}=\ell_{2}.

Moreover, there exists a projection
P_{m}:\ell_{2}\to Z_{m}
, and therefore, each
Z_{m}
is
1
-complemented. However,
(Z_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
does not converge to
\ell_{2}
in operator opening topology. Suppose, by way of contradiction,
Z_{m}\to Z
in operator opening topology. Then there exists an invertible operator
C\in GL(\ell_{2})
such that
C(Z_{m})=\ell_{2}
. Thus

Z_{m}=C^{-1}(C(Z_{m}))=C^{-1}(\ell_{2})=\ell_{2},

which is a contradiction to the fact that
Z_{m}
is finite dimensional and proper subspace of
\ell_{2}
. Therefore the set
\{C\in GL(\ell_{2}):C(Z_{m})=\ell_{2}\}
is empty, and hence by definition
r_{0}(Z_{m},\ell_{2})=1
. Therefore,
Z_{m}\nrightarrow\ell_{2}
in operator opening topology.

It is worth noting that the topologies induced on
G(X)
by the geometric and operator openings are generally different. As demonstrated in
[
9
]
, if
K
is a complemented subspace of
Y
and
L
is an isomorphic uncomplemented subspace of
Z
, the geometric and operator opening topologies induced on
G(Y\oplus Z)
do not coincide.

While the operator opening topology yields clean convergence results, it is too restrictive for many natural approximation schemes. The weaker gap topology is more widely applicable, but the absence of near-identity isomorphisms prevents direct operator transport between subspaces. To bridge this gap, we now develop an ultraproduct approach. The key insight is that gap topology convergence
X_{n}\to X
induces a canonical identification at the ultraproduct level (Remark
2.7
), allowing us to compare numerical indices without explicitly transporting operators. The technical engine of this approach is the following theorem, which establishes that the numerical radius behaves well under ultraproducts.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be Banach spaces,
{\mathcal{U}}
a free ultrafilter on
{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\{T_{n}\}
a uniformly bounded family with
T_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{n})
. Then

v((T_{n})_{\mathcal{U}})=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}v(T_{n}).

Proof.

Set
T=(T_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}
and
Z=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
and
M=\sup_{n}\|T_{n}\|<\infty
. We use throughout that if
\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}a_{n}=a
and
a>c
, then
a_{n}>c
for
{\mathcal{U}}
-almost all
n
, and that representatives of elements of
\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}X_{n}
may be modified on a
{\mathcal{U}}
-null set without changing the equivalence class.

We first establish
v(T)\geq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}v(T_{n})
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. For each
n
, choose
(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\in\Pi(X_{n})
with
|x_{n}^{*}(T_{n}x_{n})|>v(T_{n})-\varepsilon
. Set
\mathbf{x}=(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\in S_{Z}
and
\mathbf{f}=\Phi((x_{n}^{*})_{\mathcal{U}})\in{Z^{*}}
. Since
\|x_{n}^{*}\|=1
for all
n
, we have
\|\mathbf{f}\|\leq 1
. Also
\mathbf{f(x)}=\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}x_{n}^{*}(x_{n})=1
forces
\|\mathbf{f}\|=1
, so
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{f})\in\Pi(Z)
. Since
|\cdot|
is continuous and ultralimits commute with the continuous functions on bounded sets,

v(T)\geq|\mathbf{f}(T\mathbf{x})|=|\lim_{\mathcal{U}}x_{n}^{*}(T_{n}x_{n})|=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}|x_{n}^{*}(T_{n}x_{n})|\geq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}v(T_{n})-\varepsilon,

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
gives
v(T)\geq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}v(T_{n})
.

We now prove
v(T)\leq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}v(T_{n})
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{g})\in\Pi(Z)
with
\mathbf{x}=(x_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. Since
\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}\|x_{n}\|=1
, replacing
x_{n}
by
\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
on the set
\{n:x_{n}\neq 0\}\in{\mathcal{U}}
does not change the equivalence class, so we may assume that
\|x_{n}\|=1
for all
n
. By equation (
1
),
\Phi(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}^{*})
is
1
-norming for
Z
, and the bipolar theorem gives
\overline{\Phi(B_{\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}^{*}})}^{w^{*}}=B_{Z^{*}}
(see
[
10
, Section 1]
). So there exists
(g_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}X_{n}^{*}
with
\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}\|g_{n}\|\leq 1
such that

(4)

|\lim_{\mathcal{U}}g_{n}(x_{n})-1|<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\quad\text{and}\quad|\Phi((g_{n})_{\mathcal{U}})(T\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{g}(T\mathbf{x})|<\varepsilon.

From the first inequality in (
4
),
\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\text{Re}\,g_{n}(x_{n})>1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}
, and since
\text{Re}\,g_{n}(x_{n})\leq\|g_{n}\|
pointwise, we get
\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}\|g_{n}\|>0
. Hence
\|g_{n}\|>0
for
{\mathcal{U}}
-almost all
n
, and set
\hat{g}_{n}=g_{n}/\|g_{n}\|\in S_{X_{n}^{*}}
. Since
\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}\|g_{n}\|>0
, we have

\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\text{Re}\,\hat{g}_{n}(x_{n})=\frac{\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\text{Re}\,g_{n}(x_{n})}{\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|g_{n}\|}\geq\frac{1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}}{1}>1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

Therefore
\text{Re}\,\hat{g}_{n}(x_{n})>1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}
for
{\mathcal{U}}
-almost all
n
. Lemma
2.2
then yields
(y_{n},y_{n}^{*})\in\Pi(X_{n})
with

(5)

\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\|\hat{g}_{n}-y_{n}^{*}\|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for}\;{\mathcal{U}}\;\text{-almost all}\;n.

Writing
\hat{g}_{n}(T_{n}x_{n})=(\hat{g}_{n}-y_{n}^{*})(T_{n}x_{n})+y_{n}^{*}(T_{n}(x_{n}-y_{n}))+y_{n}^{*}(T_{n}y_{n})
and using
\|x_{n}\|=\|y_{n}^{*}\|=1,\|T_{n}\|\leq M,|y_{n}^{*}(T_{n}y_{n})|\leq v(T_{n})
, and (
5
),

|\hat{g}_{n}(T_{n}x_{n})|\leq M\|\hat{g_{n}}-y_{n}^{*}\|+M\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|+v(T_{n})<2M\varepsilon+v(T_{n}).

Since
\|g_{n}\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
for
{\mathcal{U}}
-almost all
n
, we obtain

|g_{n}(T_{n}x_{n})|=\|g_{n}\||\hat{g_{n}}(T_{n}x_{n})|\leq(1+\varepsilon)(v(T_{n})+2M\varepsilon)\quad\text{for}\,{\mathcal{U}}\text{-almost all}\,n.

Taking ultralimits and using the continuity of
|\cdot|
,

\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}|g_{n}(T_{n}x_{n})|\leq(1+\varepsilon)(\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}v(T_{n})+2M\varepsilon).

Since
\Phi((g_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}})(T\mathbf{x})=\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}g_{n}(T_{n}x_{n})
, the triangle inequality and (
4
) give

|\mathbf{g}(T\mathbf{x})|\leq\varepsilon+\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}|g_{n}(T_{n}x_{n})|\leq\varepsilon+(1+\varepsilon)\left(\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}v(T_{n})+2M\varepsilon\right).

Since
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{g})\in\Pi(Z)
was arbitrary,
v(T)\leq\varepsilon+(1+\varepsilon)\left(\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}v(T_{n})+2M\varepsilon\right)
, and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we conclude
v(T)\leq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}v(T_{n})
.
∎

The exact preservation of the numerical radius under ultraproducts has immediate consequences for the numerical index. Since the numerical index is defined as an infimum of numerical radii over operators of norm one, and the ultraproduct operator norm satisfies
\|(T_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\|=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|T_{n}\|
, we obtain a one-sided bound.

Corollary 3.4
.

For any Banach spaces
\{X_{n}\}
and free ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
,

n\bigg(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}\bigg)\leq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}n(X_{n}).

Proof.

For each
n
, choose
T_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{n})
with
\|T_{n}\|=1
and
v(T_{n})<n(X_{n})+1/n
. Since
\|(T_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\|=\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}\|T_{n}\|=1
, Theorem
3.3
gives

n\left(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}\right)\leq v((T_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}})=\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}v(T_{n})\leq\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}\left(n(X_{n})+\frac{1}{n}\right)=\lim_{{\mathcal{U}}}n(X_{n}).

∎

Specializing to the case where all spaces coincide, we obtain a bound for ultrapowers.

Corollary 3.5
.

For any Banach space
X
and free ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
,

n(X_{\mathcal{U}})\leq n(X).

Proof.

Apply Corollary
3.4
with
X_{n}=X
for all
n
.
∎

We now connect these ultraproduct bounds to the gap topology convergence problem. The key observation is that gap convergence
X_{n}\to X
inside an ambient space
Y
induces the identification
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}\cong X_{\mathcal{U}}
inside
Y_{\mathcal{U}}
(Remark
2.7
). This allows us to reduce the gap topology convergence question to a purely intrinsic property of ultrapowers.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
X_{n}\to X
in gap topology inside
Y
. Then

(i)

n(X_{\mathcal{U}})\leq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}n(X_{n})
for every free ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
.

(ii)

If
n(X_{\mathcal{U}})=n(X)
, for every free ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
, then
n(X)\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n})
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from Remark
2.7
and Corollary
3.4
. For part (ii), let
\{X_{k_{j}}\}
be a subsequence with
n(X_{k_{j}})\to\liminf_{n}n(X_{n})
. The family
\{k_{j}:j\geq N\}_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has finite intersection property, hence by the ultrafilter lemma, it extends to a free ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
{\mathbb{N}}
containing each set
\{k_{j}:j\geq N\}
. Since
X_{n}\to X
in gap topology, we have from part (i),

n(X)=n(X_{{\mathcal{U}}})\leq\lim_{\mathcal{U}}n(X_{n})=\lim_{j}n(X_{k_{j}})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}n(X_{n}).

∎

Theorem
3.6
reduces the gap topology convergence problem to the following fundamental question about the stability of the numerical index under ultrapowers.

Question 3.7
.

Does
n(X_{\mathcal{U}})=n(X)
hold for every Banach space
X
and every free ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
?

An affirmative answer to Question
3.7
would immediately imply, via Theorem
3.6
, that the numerical index is
lower semicontinuous
under the gap topology convergence, that is,
n(X)\leq\liminf_{n}n(X_{n})
whenever
X_{n}\to X
in gap topology. Full continuity would require an upper bound
\limsup_{n}n(X_{n})\leq n(X)
, which is a separate question. We conclude by recording the cases where Question
3.7
is known to have an affirmative answer and discussing why the general case remains delicate.

Remark 3.8
.

Question
3.7
has an affirmative answer in the following cases:

(1)

When
X
is lush, since lushness is preserved by ultraproducts
[
5
, Corollary 4.5]
, so
n(X_{\mathcal{U}})=n(X)=1
.

(2)

When
n(X)=0
: Corollary
3.5
gives
0\leq n(X_{{\mathcal{U}}})\leq n(X)=0
, and hence
n(X_{{\mathcal{U}}})=0
.

The general case remains open. We also note that the numerical index is not a local property: the inequality
n(X)>n(X^{*})
can occur
[
6
]
. This suggests that Question
3.7
may have a subtle answer in general, as the ultrapower construction can interact nontrivially with duality and local structure.
