Pair creation amplitudes for a real scalar field coupled to a time-dependent surface in d + 1 d+1 dimensions
C. D. Fosco, B. C. Guntsche

Abstract
We study the pair creation phenomenon for a real scalar field φ \varphi in the presence of a surface that undergoes time-dependent deformations, while imposing Dirichlet-like boundary conditions. Including terms up to fourth order in the departure of the surface from an infinite plane, we present results for the angular dependence of the emission rate for the vacuum-to-pair process as a function of the geometry and the dynamics of the surface, as well as of the momenta of the emitted pair. We check the consistency of the leading contribution with previous results obtained from the imaginary part of the effective action, and clarify how the relation between exclusive probabilities and the imaginary part of the effective action is modified at fourth order by the opening of a two-pair channel.

2.1

We consider a massless real scalar field
\varphi(x)
in

g_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,\ldots,-1)

Space-time indices

The scalar field interacts with a space-time surface
\Sigma

x_{\shortparallel}\equiv(x^{\alpha})_{\alpha=0}^{d-1}

\Sigma\;:\;x_{d}\,=\,\psi(x_{\shortparallel})\;,

(1)

{\mathcal{S}}(\varphi;\psi)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d^{d+1}x\,\Big[\partial_{\mu}\varphi(x)\,\partial^{\mu}\varphi(x)-\lambda\,\delta\big(x^{d}-\psi(x_{\shortparallel})\big)\big(\varphi(x)\big)^{2}\Big]\;.

(2)

The constant

penalizing the development of nonvanishing values for the field
\varphi
on

\Sigma

\varphi\big(x_{\shortparallel},\,\psi(x_{\shortparallel})\big)=0
appear when
\lambda\to+\infty

of the calculation. In this vein, since
\lambda
plays an auxiliary role and
in general we do not attempt here to obtain final results for finite

\lambda
, except in a
d=1
example, where the simplicity of the final
expressions warrants their presentation.

With the aim of finding the probability distributions of the pairs created
by the moving surface, we shall evaluate the transition amplitudes using perturbation theory in the interaction representation. Here we take
as the perturbation the deformation of the surface, which in our setup is
characterized by the single function
\psi
.
Thus, we split up the action
{\mathcal{S}}
into its free (
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
) and
interaction (
{\mathcal{S}}_{I}
)
parts:

{\mathcal{S}}(\varphi;\psi)\;=\;{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(\varphi)\,+\,{\mathcal{S}}_{I}(\varphi;\psi)\;,

(3)

such that:

{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(\varphi)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d^{d+1}x\,\Big[\partial_{\mu}\varphi(x)\,\partial^{\mu}\varphi(x)-\lambda\,\delta(x^{d})\,\big(\varphi(x)\big)^{2}\Big]\;,

(4)

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{I}(\varphi;\psi)\,=\,-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int d^{d+1}x\,\Big[\delta\big(x^{d}-\psi(x_{\shortparallel})\big)-\delta\big(x^{d}\big)\Big]\big(\varphi(x)\big)^{2}\;.

(5)

In the interaction representation

2.2
Free field states and Feynman propagator

The space of states into which the vacuum
|0\rangle
may decay is
built by studying the solutions to the free equation of motion,

[\Box+\lambda\delta(x^{d})]\varphi(x)=0
.
A convenient way to construct such a set is by taking advantage of the
translation invariance along
{\mathbf{x}}_{\shortparallel}\equiv(x^{1},\ldots\,x^{d-1})
. Namely, introducing the momentum,
k_{\shortparallel}\equiv(k^{0},{\mathbf{k}_{\shortparallel}})
, a convenient basis of solutions is then
given by
the following functions:

g^{(o,e)}_{k}(x)\,=\,{\mathcal{N}}_{k}^{(o,e)}\,e^{-ik_{\shortparallel}\cdot x_{\shortparallel}}\,f_{k^{d}}^{(o,e)}(x^{d})

(6)

where
{\mathcal{N}}_{k}^{(o,e)}
are normalization constants and

k_{\shortparallel}\cdot x_{\shortparallel}\equiv\sum_{\alpha=0}^{d-1}k_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
, satisfies
k_{\shortparallel}^{2}=(k^{d})^{2}
(on-shell condition).
Finally, the
f_{k^{d}}^{(o,e)}
functions denote the (odd and even,
respectively) functions:

\displaystyle f_{k^{d}}^{(o)}(x^{d})

\displaystyle=\;\sin(k^{d}x^{d})

\displaystyle f_{k^{d}}^{(e)}(x^{d})

\displaystyle=\;\sin(k^{d}|x^{d}|)\,+\,\frac{2k^{d}}{\lambda}\cos(k^{d}x^{d})\;,

(7)

defined for
|x^{d}|\leq\frac{L}{2}
,
L
being the side length of a
spatial box. As usual, this is introduced to avoid the
infinities associated with a continuum spectrum
2
2

2

On the other
hand, any reference to the finite size shall be eliminated at the end of the
calculation, when evaluating probability densities.
. Indeed, assuming
periodic boundary conditions at
x^{d}=\pm\frac{L}{2}
, the allowed values of
the momentum become:
k^{d}=\frac{2n\pi}{L}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for the odd and even functions.
On the other hand, using the same
L
for the other directions:
{\mathbf{k}_{\shortparallel}}=\frac{2\pi}{L}\,{\mathbf{n}_{\shortparallel}}
,
{\mathbf{n}_{\shortparallel}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{(d-1)}
.

The normalization factors become

{\mathcal{N}}_{k}^{(o)}\,=\,\Big(L^{d}|k^{0}|\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\;,\;\;{\mathcal{N}}_{k}^{(e)}\,=\,\Big\{L^{d}|k^{0}|\big[1+(\frac{2k^{d}}{\lambda})^{2}\big]\Big\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\;,

(8)

and the resulting free field decomposition can be put in the form:

\varphi(x)\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}_{\shortparallel}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1},n^{d}\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{\alpha=o,e}\Big[a_{\mathbf{k}}^{(\alpha)}\,g_{\mathbf{k}}^{(\alpha)}(x)\,+\,(a_{\mathbf{k}}^{(\alpha)})^{\dagger}\,(g_{\mathbf{k}}^{(\alpha)})^{*}(x)\Big]\,

(9)

where
{\mathbf{k}}\equiv\frac{2\pi}{L}(n^{1},\ldots,n^{d})\equiv\frac{2\pi}{L}{\mathbf{n}}
.

With the normalization we have chosen we have:

\big(g_{\mathbf{k}}^{(\alpha)},g_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{(\alpha^{\prime})}\big)=\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\delta_{{\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}}
, with

\big(f,g\big)\,\equiv\,i\int_{\mathbf{x}}f^{*}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\partial_{0}}}g(x)\;,

(10)

and as a consequence
[a_{\mathbf{k}}^{(\alpha)},a_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{(\alpha^{\prime})\dagger}]=\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\delta_{{\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}}
. We have used in (
10
) a shorthand notation for the integral,
whereby rather than writing differentials, the integrated variables appear
as sub-indices for the integral symbol. In the case above, the range
for that integration is the spatial hypercube of side
L
.

On the other hand, in order to evaluate the scattering amplitudes via Feynman
rules, we also need the free Feynman propagator,

G(x,x^{\prime})\,\equiv\,\langle 0|{\mathrm{T}}[\varphi(x)\varphi(x^{\prime})]|0\rangle\;,

(11)

which satisfies the equation

\big[-\Box_{x}-\lambda\delta(x^{d})\big]G(x,x^{\prime})\,=\,i\delta^{d+1}(x-x^{\prime})\;.

(12)

Fourier transforming with respect to the
x_{\shortparallel}
coordinates,

G(x,x^{\prime})\,\equiv\,\int\frac{d^{d}p_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}e^{-ip_{\shortparallel}\cdot(x_{\shortparallel}-x_{\shortparallel}^{\prime})}\widetilde{G}_{p_{\shortparallel}}(x^{d},x^{\prime d})\;,

(13)

the problem becomes one-dimensional, and we find:

\widetilde{G}_{p_{\shortparallel}}(x^{d},x^{\prime d})\,=\,\frac{1}{2i\kappa(p_{\shortparallel})}\left[e^{-\kappa(p_{\shortparallel})|x^{d}-x^{\prime d}|}\,-\,r(p_{\shortparallel})\,e^{-\kappa(p_{\shortparallel})(|x^{d}|+|x^{\prime d}|)}\right]\;,

(14)

where
\kappa(p_{\shortparallel})\equiv\sqrt{-p_{\shortparallel}^{2}-i\epsilon}

and

r(p_{\shortparallel})=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+2\kappa(p_{\shortparallel})}
.

2.3
Emission Probabilities from the Perturbative Amplitudes

We denote by
{\mathcal{A}}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)
the amplitude from the vacuum
|0\rangle
to a
2
-particle

state
|{\mathbf{k}}\alpha,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\alpha^{\prime}\rangle
which, in the
interaction representation, is obtained as follows:

{\mathcal{A}}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\,=\,\langle{\mathbf{k}}\alpha,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\alpha^{\prime}|T\big(e^{i:{\mathcal{S}}_{I}:}\big)|0\rangle\;,

(15)

T
being the time-ordering operator and
:{\mathcal{S}}_{I}:
the normal
ordered version of (
5
).

A clarification about (
15
) will be important when, in
Sec.
4
, we relate these amplitudes to the imaginary part
of the effective action. When the right-hand side is evaluated perturbatively
through Wick’s theorem, each contraction factorizes into a piece attached to
the two external legs and a set of disconnected vacuum subdiagrams; the latter
exponentiate into the vacuum persistence amplitude

{\mathcal{V}}\equiv\langle 0|S|0\rangle
, so that

\langle{\mathbf{k}}\alpha,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\alpha^{\prime}|S|0\rangle={\mathcal{V}}\,{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime})
. Throughout this work

{\mathcal{A}}
denotes the
connected
amplitude: the vacuum bubbles are
factored out, which amounts to dividing by
{\mathcal{V}}
,

{\mathcal{A}}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\,=\,\frac{\langle{\mathbf{k}}\alpha,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\alpha^{\prime}|S|0\rangle}{\langle 0|S|0\rangle}\;.

(16)

For a
stable
vacuum
{\mathcal{V}}
is a pure phase and this division is
immaterial for any probability; the moving surface, however, destabilizes the
vacuum and
|{\mathcal{V}}|^{2}=e^{-2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma}<1
is no longer trivial.
Consequently
\tfrac{1}{2}|{\mathcal{A}}|^{2}
in (
17
) is the

connected
probability, which coincides with the true single-pair
probability only at leading order; the factor
|{\mathcal{V}}|^{2}
is not lost but
reinstated in Sec.
4
[see (
57
) and the
probabilities
p_{1},p_{2}
that follow it], where it encodes the depletion of the
vacuum into the multi-pair channels that open at fourth order.

The probabilities of detecting particles as functions of their momenta and of
the deformation of the surface result from taking the modulus
squared of the amplitudes. The latter will be evaluated as power
series in
\psi
and, to get probabilities up to fourth order,
we only need the amplitudes up to third order in
\psi
. Indeed, the
probability for a particle pair with momenta
{\mathbf{k}}
and

{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
, within infinitesimal volume elements
d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}

d^{d}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
is:

dP\big({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,\big|{\mathcal{A}}\big({\mathbf{k}};{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)\big|^{2}\,\frac{L^{d}d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\,\frac{L^{d}d^{d}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,,

(17)

where the factor
\frac{1}{2}
is the usual Bose-statistics factor for final
states of identical particles, and we have used the shorthand

\big|{\mathcal{A}}\big({\mathbf{k}};{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)\big|^{2}\,\equiv\,\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}=o,e}\,\big|{\mathcal{A}}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\big|^{2}

(18)

for the incoherent sum over the parities (
e
,
o
) of the emitted
quanta, states which are mutually orthogonal. Also note that the powers of
L
will cancel with the ones coming from the
normalization factors of the final states.

To understand the relationship between the expansion of amplitudes and that of
the probabilities, we expand
{\mathcal{A}}
in powers of

\psi
,

{\mathcal{A}}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\,=\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)+{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)+{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)+\ldots

(19)

To find the probabilities, one needs to consider:

\displaystyle\big|{\mathcal{A}}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\,\big|{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\,2\,{\mathrm{R}e}\Big[{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big){\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle+\,\big|{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\big|^{2}\,+\,2\,{\mathrm{R}e}\Big[{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big){\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle+\,\ldots

(20)

where, on the right-hand side, the first line contributes to the probability at second order
in
\psi
, the second line at third order, etc. It is therefore clear
that the probability to fourth order requires the amplitude only up to
third order. Moreover, a subtler point is noteworthy, and applies to terms
involving products of amplitudes of different orders (like the third-order
one, which involves the second- and first-order amplitudes).
The point is that the factors in one such product have the same quantum
numbers, in particular the parity. Thus, if the factors have different selection
rules regarding parity, the product vanishes. This happens, as we shall see
for the first- and second-order amplitudes.

2.4
Perturbative Expansion for the Amplitudes

Let us then calculate each
{\mathcal{A}}^{(i)}
, which requires us to
expand both the interaction action and its exponential.

We first note that, using an index to denote the respective order in
\psi
,
we have

{\mathcal{S}}_{I}={\mathcal{S}}_{I}^{(1)}+{\mathcal{S}}_{I}^{(2)}\,+\,\ldots
, with

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{I}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\,-\lambda\int d^{d}x_{\shortparallel}\,\psi(x_{\shortparallel})\,\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)\partial_{d}\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{I}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int d^{d}x_{\shortparallel}\,\big(\psi(x_{\shortparallel})\big)^{2}\,[\partial_{d}\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)\partial_{d}\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)+\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)\partial^{2}_{d}\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{I}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{\lambda}{6}\,\int d^{d}x_{\shortparallel}\,\big(\psi(x_{\shortparallel})\big)^{3}\,[3\,\partial_{d}\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)\partial^{2}_{d}\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)+\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)\partial^{3}_{d}\varphi(x_{\shortparallel},0)]

\displaystyle\ldots

(21)

The amplitudes at each order are obtained by combining different powers of the interaction, and suitable orders in the
expansion of the interaction itself.

The only case in which one does not need to combine those two expansions is the
first-order term. Introducing Fourier transforms according to the
convention

\widetilde{\psi}(p_{\shortparallel})=\int d^{d}x_{\shortparallel}\,e^{ip_{\shortparallel}\cdot x_{\shortparallel}}\,\psi(x_{\shortparallel})
, the first-order amplitude becomes, for any
\lambda
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\,=\,-i\lambda\,{\mathcal{N}}_{k}^{(\alpha)}{\mathcal{N}}_{k^{\prime}}^{(\alpha^{\prime})}\,\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})\,

\displaystyle\Big[\big(f_{k^{d}}^{(\alpha)}\partial_{d}f_{{k^{\prime}}^{d}}^{(\alpha^{\prime})}\big)|_{x^{d}=0}

\displaystyle+(k^{d},\alpha)\leftrightarrow({k^{\prime}}^{d},\alpha^{\prime})\big]\;.

(22)

We note that, for the factor between square brackets to be nonvanishing, the
parities under
x^{d}\to-x^{d}
of the emitted particles,
\alpha
and

\alpha^{\prime}
, have to be different: one odd and the other even.
More explicitly,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o\big)

\displaystyle=\,-2i\,{\mathcal{N}}_{k}^{(e)}{\mathcal{N}}_{k^{\prime}}^{(o)}\,\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})k^{d}{k^{\prime}}^{d}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o;{\mathbf{k}},e\big)\;,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},e\big)

\displaystyle=\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},o;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o\big)=0\;.

(23)

The second-order term consists of two contributions: the first comes from
the second-order term in the expansion of
{\mathcal{S}}_{I}
; the second
comes from the square of the first-order term. Important
cancellations occur between those two terms. We simply write down the sum
of them, in the
\lambda\to\infty
limit:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\,=\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(2,1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\,+\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(2,2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle=\,-2i\,\big({\mathcal{N}}_{k}\big)^{2}\;\int\frac{d^{d}p_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}-p_{\shortparallel})\,\sqrt{-p_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(p_{\shortparallel}+k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\,k^{d}{k^{\prime}}^{d}\,\delta^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\;,

(24)

(no sum over
\alpha
,
\alpha^{\prime}
) where
{\mathcal{N}}_{k}={\mathcal{N}}_{k}^{(\alpha)}\Big|_{\lambda\to\infty}=\Big(L^{d}|k^{0}|\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

It then becomes evident, without further ado, that the third-order contribution
to the decay probability vanishes. Indeed, it involves the product of
amplitudes to first and second orders, with the same initial and final
states. However, the first-order term requires the parities to be different, while
the second-order one vanishes unless they are equal.

Finally, the third-order amplitude is nonvanishing only when the parities are
different, as was the case for the first-order one. We therefore write it
directly for one of the nontrivial cases, and also in the

\lambda\to\infty
limit:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}\big({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o\big)

\displaystyle=\,i\,\big({\mathcal{N}}_{k}\big)^{2}\,k^{d}{k^{\prime}}^{d}\,\Big\{\frac{1}{3}\,\widetilde{\psi^{3}}(k_{\shortparallel}+k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\,(k^{\prime d})^{2}

\displaystyle+\,\int\frac{d^{d}p_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}-p_{\shortparallel})\,p_{\shortparallel}^{2}\,\widetilde{\psi^{2}}(p_{\shortparallel}+k_{\shortparallel})\,

\displaystyle-\,2\,\int\frac{d^{d}p_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{d^{d}q_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}-p_{\shortparallel})\,\sqrt{-p_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(p_{\shortparallel}-q_{\shortparallel})\,\sqrt{-q_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(q_{\shortparallel}+k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\Big\}\,.

(25)

3
Results and Applications

3.1
Results

The second-order probabilities are obtained from the first-order
amplitudes. Recalling (
2.4
),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o\big)

\displaystyle=\,-2iL^{-d}\,\frac{\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})\,k^{d}{k^{\prime}}^{d}}{\big(|k^{0}||{k^{\prime}}^{0}|\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,\big[1+(\frac{2{k}^{d}}{\lambda})^{2}\big]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\;,

(26)

the probability of pair creation at second order in
\psi
, within a
volume
d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}\,d^{d}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
around
{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
,
is

\displaystyle dP^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\big|{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o\big)\big|^{2}\,+\,\big|{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}\big({\mathbf{k}},o;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},e\big)\big|^{2}\right]\,\frac{L^{d}d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{L^{d}d^{d}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle=\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})\big|^{2}\,

\displaystyle\frac{2|k^{d}|^{2}|{k^{\prime}}^{d}|^{2}}{|k^{0}||{k^{\prime}}^{0}|}\Big\{\big[1+(\frac{2{k}^{d}}{\lambda})^{2}\big]^{-1}+\big[1+(\frac{2{k^{\prime}}^{d}}{\lambda})^{2}\big]^{-1}\Big\}\frac{d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{d^{d}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\;.

(27)

Note that the
d^{\rm th}
component of
k^{\mu}
can assume only
positive values, but the corresponding free particle states are not
plane waves. Indeed, they are mixtures of plane waves having
positive and negative momenta. All the other components run over positive and
negative values.

It is possible to obtain a more covariant looking representation for the
probability:

\displaystyle dP^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=\,8\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})\big|^{2}\,|k^{d}|^{2}|{k^{\prime}}^{d}|^{2}\,\Theta(k^{0})\delta(k^{2})\,\Theta({k^{\prime}}^{0})\delta({k^{\prime}}^{2})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big\{\big[1+(\frac{2{k}^{d}}{\lambda})^{2}\big]^{-1}+\big[1+(\frac{2{k^{\prime}}^{d}}{\lambda})^{2}\big]^{-1}\Big\}\frac{d^{d+1}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{d^{d+1}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,,

(28)

where
\Theta
denotes Heaviside’s step function.
This gives the equivalent form

\displaystyle dP^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=\,2\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})\big|^{2}\;\theta(k^{0})\,\sqrt{k_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\;\Theta({k^{\prime}}^{0})\,\sqrt{{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big\{\big[1+\frac{4k_{\shortparallel}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\big]^{-1}+\big[1+\frac{4{k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\big]^{-1}\Big\}\frac{d^{d}k_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{d^{d}{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,,

(29)

where, on the left-hand side,
k^{d}
and
{k^{\prime}}^{d}
are assumed to be determined
by the respective mass shell conditions:
k^{d}=\sqrt{k_{\shortparallel}^{2}}
and

{k^{\prime}}^{d}=\sqrt{{k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}}^{2}}
.
In the Dirichlet limit this becomes:

dP^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\,{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)\,=\,4\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})\big|^{2}\,\Theta(k^{0})\sqrt{k_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\Theta({k^{\prime}}^{0})\sqrt{{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\frac{d^{d}k_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{d^{d}{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\;.

(30)

A useful expression, at this order, for the probability
of detecting a particle at a given
k^{\mu}
is the following:

dP^{(2)}({\mathbf{k}})\,=\,4\,\frac{d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{|k^{d}|^{2}}{|k^{0}|}\,\int\frac{d^{d}{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\Theta(k^{\prime 0})\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})\big|^{2}\,|{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}|\;.

(31)

As already mentioned, the third-order probability for the
vacuum to decay to a pair vanishes.
Indeed, it is evident that the third-order contributions will involve
the product of a second-order amplitude and a first-order one; namely,

{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}\big({\mathbf{k}},\alpha;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},\alpha^{\prime}\big)

(32)

and its complex conjugate. But, as we have mentioned in the previous section,
the first-order term is nonzero only when
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
,
while the second-order one only appears when
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
.
Thus, the probability density, to this order, vanishes.

Finally, we consider the fourth-order contribution,
which is obtained by collecting all the
O(\psi^{4})
pieces of

\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{A}}|^{2}
. Using the parity selection rules
[
{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)},{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}
require
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
, while

{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}
requires
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
, cf. Sec.
2.4
], the only
surviving terms are

\displaystyle dP^{(4)}\big({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=\,\Big\{\tfrac{1}{2}\big|{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},e\big)\big|^{2}\,+\,\tfrac{1}{2}\big|{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}\big({\mathbf{k}},o;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o\big)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\,{\mathrm{R}e}\Big[{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}\big({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o\big)\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}\big({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o\big)\Big]

\displaystyle+\,{\mathrm{R}e}\Big[{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}\big({\mathbf{k}},o;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},e\big)\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}\big({\mathbf{k}},o;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},e\big)\Big]\Big\}\,\frac{L^{d}d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{L^{d}d^{d}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\;,

(33)

where the overall Bose factor has already been
applied; note that it multiplies the squared amplitudes

|{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}|^{2}
as well, so each enters with weight
\tfrac{1}{2}
. Since

{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},e)={\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}({\mathbf{k}},o;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o)
in the Dirichlet limit, the
two
square moduli may be merged into a single term

|{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},e)|^{2}
.

It is convenient to introduce the loop kernel appearing in

{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}
and the three-vertex object appearing in

{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}
,

\displaystyle J(k_{\shortparallel},k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})

\displaystyle\equiv\int\frac{d^{d}p_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}-p_{\shortparallel})\,\sqrt{-p_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(p_{\shortparallel}+k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\;,

\displaystyle\Phi(k_{\shortparallel},k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})

\displaystyle\equiv\tfrac{1}{3}(k^{\prime d})^{2}\,\widetilde{\psi^{3}}(k_{\shortparallel}+k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})+\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}p_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\widetilde{\psi}(k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}-p_{\shortparallel})\,p_{\shortparallel}^{2}\,\widetilde{\psi^{2}}(p_{\shortparallel}+k_{\shortparallel})

\displaystyle\quad-2\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}p_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{d^{d}q_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}-p_{\shortparallel})\sqrt{-p_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(p_{\shortparallel}-q_{\shortparallel})\sqrt{-q_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\widetilde{\psi}(q_{\shortparallel}+k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\;,

(34)

so that, in the Dirichlet limit,

{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},e)=-2i\,{\mathcal{N}}_{k}{\mathcal{N}}_{k^{\prime}}\,k^{d}k^{\prime d}\,J(k_{\shortparallel},k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})
and

{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o)=i\,{\mathcal{N}}_{k}{\mathcal{N}}_{k^{\prime}}\,k^{d}k^{\prime d}\,\Phi(k_{\shortparallel},k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})
, with
{\mathcal{N}}_{k}=(L^{d}|k^{0}|)^{-1/2}
.
Inserting these into (
3.1
) and using

{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}({\mathbf{k}},e;{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},o)
from
(
2.4
),
the powers of
L
cancel against the phase-space measure and one obtains the
explicit fourth-order spectral probability

\displaystyle dP^{(4)}\big({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=\,4\,\frac{(k^{d})^{2}(k^{\prime d})^{2}}{|k^{0}|\,|k^{\prime 0}|}\,\Big[\big|J(k_{\shortparallel},k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\big|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathrm{R}e}\!\big(\widetilde{\psi}^{*}(k_{\shortparallel}+k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\,\Phi(k_{\shortparallel},k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\big)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathrm{R}e}\big(\widetilde{\psi}^{*}(k_{\shortparallel}+k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel})\,\Phi(k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel},k_{\shortparallel})\big)\Big]\,\frac{d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{d^{d}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\;,

(35)

where
k^{d}=\sqrt{k_{\shortparallel}^{2}}
,
k^{\prime d}=\sqrt{k^{\prime 2}_{\shortparallel}}
are
fixed
by the mass-shell conditions, and the two
{\mathrm{R}e}
terms are the

(e,o)
and
(o,e)
interferences, which differ only by the exchange

{\mathbf{k}}\leftrightarrow{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
in
\Phi
. Equation
(
3.1
) has a first term which is the modulus squared of the
one-loop (“box-type”) amplitude

{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}
, positive definite; the second collects the
interference of the tree-level amplitude
{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}
with the
three-vertex amplitude
{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}
, whose sign makes
the net fourth-order correction to the single-pair spectrum negative on
average (vacuum-persistence depletion, see Sec.
4
).

3.2
Applications

We consider here several applications of the general formulas obtained in
the previous subsection.

3.2.1
Oscillating bump

As a first example, let us evaluate the second-order probability
for a particular case: a small-size deformation, with single frequency
K_{s}

characterizing its time dependence. By small size, we mean that the
surface remains planar almost everywhere, except for a region
with small support where it varies. To be more explicit, we assume:

\psi(x_{\shortparallel})\;=\;\xi({\mathbf{x}_{\shortparallel}})\,\cos(K_{s}t)\;,

(36)

where
\xi
is concentrated around a spatial region of size
l
, which we shall
assume much smaller than the distances from which we want to observe the
properties of the emitted particles.
Introducing this into (
31
), we see that the probability becomes
proportional to the total evolution time
T
, as it should be for a periodic
motion. The rate
dW^{(2)}=\frac{dP^{(2)}}{T}
is:

dW^{(2)}({\mathbf{k}})\,=\,\frac{d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,|k^{d}|\,\Theta(K_{s}-k^{0})\,\int_{U}\frac{d^{d-1}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\,\big|\widetilde{\xi}({\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}})\big|^{2}\,\sqrt{(K_{s}-k^{0})^{2}-({\mathbf{k}}_{\shortparallel}-{\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}})^{2}}\;,

(37)

where

U=\{{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}:|{\mathbf{k}}_{\shortparallel}-{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}|<K_{s}-k^{0}\}.

(38)

Now, we apply the hypothesis regarding the localization of the
system: at this level, it will mean

({\mathbf{k}}_{\shortparallel}-{\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}})^{2}\,\simeq\,({\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}})^{2}\;,

(39)

an approximation that can be expected to be valid, at least
for not very large values of
\theta
, the angle between
{\mathbf{k}}
and
the
d
axis (using spherical coordinates in
d
dimensions).
Recalling that
|{\mathbf{k}}|=k^{0}
, we see that:

dW^{(2)}({\mathbf{k}})\,\simeq\,\frac{(k^{0})^{d}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\cos\theta\,d\Omega\,\Theta(K_{s}-k^{0})\,\int_{U}\frac{d^{d-1}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\,\big|\widetilde{\xi}({\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}})\big|^{2}\,\sqrt{(K_{s}-k^{0})^{2}-({\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}})^{2}}\;,

(40)

where
\Omega
is the solid angle.
The probability of detecting a particle per unit of solid angle
per unit time results from integrating over energies:

\frac{dP(\theta)}{d\Omega}\,\simeq\,C\,\cos\theta\,\;,

(41)

with

C\,\equiv\,\int_{0}^{K_{s}}dk^{0}\frac{(k^{0})^{d}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{U}\frac{d^{d-1}{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\,\big|\widetilde{\xi}({\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}})\big|^{2}\,\sqrt{(K_{s}-k^{0})^{2}-({\mathbf{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}})^{2}}\;.

(42)

The angular pattern
dP/d\Omega\propto\cos\theta
is the dipole-like
distribution shown in Fig.
1
. The intensity is maximal
along the normal to the surface (
\theta=0
) and vanishes tangentially
(
\theta=\pi/2
); the structure is reminiscent of Lambertian emission
from an aperture and is dictated entirely by the kinematical factor

|k^{d}|=k^{0}\cos\theta
in (
37
). The pattern is invariant
under
x^{d}\to-x^{d}
, so an identical lobe (dotted in
Fig.
1
) is emitted into the
x^{d}<0
half-space.

Figure 1:
Angular distribution of the emitted particles in the localized,
small-source regime, Eq. (
41
): a
\cos\theta
lobe along
the normal to the surface. The dotted curve is the mirror image emitted
into the
x^{d}<0
half-space.

We conclude the discussion of this example by mentioning that, had
we wished to consider the radiated power, the qualitative features of
the results would have been the same. Indeed, to obtain, for instance,
the radiated energy per unit of solid angle, per unit time, one just
multiplies (
41
) by
k^{0}
, which is the energy of the
emitted quanta.

3.2.2
Oscillating plane

This is an example which, in its electromagnetic version, has
been considered in
[
7
]
, so it is worth considering
the analogous situation here, for the sake of the comparison.
A plane oscillating with an amplitude
b
and a frequency
K_{s}

corresponds to
\psi(x_{\parallel})=b\cos(K_{s}t)
,

Inserting this into the general expression for the probability, and multiplying
by the energy of the emitted quanta, we get the power per unit of
solid angle, energy, and per area of the oscillating plane:

\frac{1}{A_{\shortparallel}}\,\frac{dW^{(2)}}{dk^{0}\,d\Omega}\,=\,\frac{b^{2}(k^{0})^{5}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\big(\frac{k^{0}}{K_{s}}\big)^{4}\,\cos^{2}\theta\,\sqrt{1-2\frac{k^{0}}{K_{s}}+\big(\frac{k^{0}}{K_{s}}\big)^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}\;.

(43)

The
\cos^{2}\theta
profile and the kinematic square-root edge reproduce, for
the scalar field, the structure found for the electromagnetic field
in
[
7
]
: our Dirichlet result is the analog of their
transverse-electric (Dirichlet) polarization, the twin quanta being emitted
with opposite parallel momenta and energies adding up to the drive frequency

K_{s}
, exactly as in their twin-photon spectra (their transverse-magnetic,
i.e. Neumann, channel would correspond to the complementary boundary
condition).

3.2.3
Second-harmonic and complementary first-harmonic emissions at
fourth order

The single-frequency drive makes the harmonic content of each amplitude
explicit: every insertion of
\widetilde{\psi}
delivers an energy
\pm K_{s}
at
vanishing transverse momentum, so
{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}
feeds pairs with

k^{0}+k^{\prime 0}=K_{s}
, while
{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}
, which carries two insertions, feeds
pairs with
k^{0}+k^{\prime 0}=2K_{s}
. Inspecting the fourth-order probability
(
3.1
) channel by channel, the box term

|{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}|^{2}
is supported
entirely
on the second harmonic

k^{0}+k^{\prime 0}=2K_{s}
, a process kinematically forbidden at second order,
whereas the interference
2\,{\rm Re}\,[{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}]

stays at the first harmonic
K_{s}
and merely provides an
O(b^{4})
correction to
the leading rate, negative on average (the vacuum-persistence depletion
discussed in Sec.
4
). The genuinely new fourth-order
feature is therefore the opening of the
2K_{s}
line.

The second-harmonic channel is self-contained, as it receives no interference,
and can be evaluated in closed form. Inserting

\widetilde{\psi}(p_{\shortparallel})
into the loop kernel

J
of (
3.1
) forces the transverse momenta to be opposite,

\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=0
, and the energies to share the second harmonic,

k^{0}+k^{\prime 0}=2K_{s}
; the loop momentum left over is

p_{\shortparallel}^{\star}=(k^{0}-K_{s},\mathbf{k})
, whose weight in

|{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}|^{2}
is

|\!-\!p_{\shortparallel}^{\star 2}|=|\mathbf{k}^{2}-(k^{0}-K_{s})^{2}|
. Writing

u\equiv k^{0}/K_{s}
and
r\equiv|\mathbf{k}|/K_{s}
, the total second-harmonic rate
per unit transverse area of the plane is

\frac{P^{(4)}_{2K_{s}}}{A_{\shortparallel}\,T}\,=\,\frac{4\pi^{4}\,\Omega_{d-2}}{(2\pi)^{d+4}}\,{\mathcal{I}}_{d}\;b^{4}K_{s}^{d+4}\;,

(44)

with
\Omega_{d-2}=2\pi^{(d-1)/2}/\Gamma(\tfrac{d-1}{2})
the area of the unit

(d{-}2)
-sphere and

{\mathcal{I}}_{d}\,=\,\int_{0}^{2}du\int_{0}^{\min(u,2-u)}\!\!dr\;r^{d-2}\,\sqrt{u^{2}-r^{2}}\,\sqrt{(2-u)^{2}-r^{2}}\;\big|\,r^{2}-(u-1)^{2}\,\big|\;.

(45)

The
d=1
case, lacking transverse momentum, is handled directly. The angular
integral is rational in low dimensions
(
{\mathcal{I}}_{2}=\tfrac{1}{9}
,
{\mathcal{I}}_{4}=\tfrac{1}{45}
, and the
d=1

reduction yields
\tfrac{4}{15}
), so that

\frac{P^{(4)}_{2K_{s}}}{A_{\shortparallel}T}\,=\,\begin{cases}\;\dfrac{b^{4}K_{s}^{5}}{30\pi}\,,&d=1\,,\\[8.61108pt]
\;\dfrac{b^{4}K_{s}^{6}}{72\pi^{2}}\,,&d=2\,,\\[8.61108pt]
\;\dfrac{{\mathcal{I}}_{3}}{16\pi^{2}}\,b^{4}K_{s}^{7}\,\simeq\,2.54\times 10^{-4}\,b^{4}K_{s}^{7}\,,&d=3\,,\\[8.61108pt]
\;\dfrac{b^{4}K_{s}^{8}}{720\pi^{3}}\,,&d=4\,,\end{cases}

(46)

with
{\mathcal{I}}_{3}=0.040053\ldots
(no elementary closed form). The new
channel is suppressed relative to the leading emission by the square of the
expansion parameter
bK_{s}\sim v_{\rm max}
, the maximal velocity of the
surface;
in
d=1
the comparison with the leading rate (
56
) is
exact,

\frac{P^{(4)}_{2K_{s}}}{P^{(2)}_{K_{s}}}\,=\,\frac{2}{5}\,(bK_{s})^{2}\,.

(47)

Physically, the plane driven at
K_{s}
radiates twin quanta that share the
energy
2K_{s}
with opposite transverse momenta, the second-harmonic
counterpart of the first-harmonic twin photons of
[
7
]
, and
its
b^{4}
scaling identifies it as a genuinely nonlinear imprint of the surface
dynamics.

The complementary, first-harmonic piece of the fourth-order probability is the
interference
2\,{\rm Re}[{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}]
, which by the
same harmonic counting remains at
k^{0}+k^{\prime 0}=K_{s}
and thus corrects the

leading
rate at
O(b^{4})
; it is the explicit, oscillating-plane face of
the vacuum-persistence depletion of Sect.
4
. In
d=1
it
can
be evaluated in closed form: every momentum is an energy,
k^{d}=k^{0}
, the loop
factors enter as real pairs,

\sqrt{-p_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\sqrt{-q_{\shortparallel}^{2}}=-|p^{0}||q^{0}|
, and the
three contributions to
\Phi
in (
3.1
) — the

\widetilde{\psi^{3}}
term, the
\widetilde{\psi}\,\widetilde{\psi^{2}}
term and
the triple-
\widetilde{\psi}
convolution — collapse onto the first harmonic.
With
u\equiv k^{0}/K_{s}\in[0,1]
,

\Phi\big|_{K_{s}}\,=\,\frac{\pi b^{3}}{4}\,\big(6u^{2}-8u+9\big)\,K_{s}^{2}\;\delta(k^{0}+k^{\prime 0}-K_{s})\;.

(48)

Inserting this into (
3.1
) and integrating over the energy
sharing gives the
O(b^{4})
correction to the first-harmonic emission rate,

\frac{P^{(4)}_{K_{s}}}{T}\,=\,-\,\frac{17}{120\pi}\,b^{4}K_{s}^{5}\,=\,-\,\frac{17}{10}\,(bK_{s})^{2}\,\frac{P^{(2)}}{T}\,,

(49)

negative, as anticipated. The two fourth-order effects are therefore explicit
in
d=1
: the first-harmonic line is depleted by a relative

O\big((bK_{s})^{2}\big)
, Eq. (
49
), while a second-harmonic line
opens with the positive rate (
46
). Both scale as
b^{4}K_{s}^{5}

and are controlled by the same expansion parameter
bK_{s}\sim v_{\rm max}
;
their
sum, the net connected fourth-order rate, is
-\tfrac{13}{120\pi}\,b^{4}K_{s}^{5}
.

3.2.4
One spatial dimension (
d=1
)

This is a useful example in its own right, since the lack of angular dependence allows
for the evaluation of rather general expressions, even for a finite

\lambda
.
Here, we see that
k^{0}
and
k^{d}=k^{1}
are both positive and identical. Then

dP^{(2)}(k^{1})\,=\,dP^{(2)}(k^{0})\,=\,4\frac{dk^{1}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{d{k^{\prime}}^{1}}{2\pi}\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k^{1}+{k^{\prime}}^{1})\big|^{2}\,k^{1}{k^{\prime}}^{1}\;.

(50)

Thus, we may write the spectral probability density, as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{dP^{(2)}(\omega)}{d\omega}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(\omega+\nu)\big|^{2}\,\omega\,\nu

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\,\omega\,\int_{\omega}^{\infty}d\nu\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(\nu)\big|^{2}\,(\nu-\omega)\;.

(51)

In the particular case of harmonic motion,

\psi(t)\,=\,b\,\cos(K_{s}t)

(52)

the probability density per unit time is:

\gamma(\omega)\,\equiv\,\Big[\frac{1}{T}\frac{dP^{(2)}(\omega)}{d\omega}\Big]_{T\to\infty}\,=\,\frac{b^{2}}{2\pi}\,\Theta(K_{s}-\omega)\;\omega\,(K_{s}-\omega)\;.

(53)

Finally, for any deformation, we can always consider
the total probability, obtained by integrating over the possible
values of
\omega
,

\displaystyle\int d\omega\frac{dP^{(2)}(\omega)}{d\omega}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(\nu)\big|^{2}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\omega\,\Theta(\nu-\omega)\,(\nu-\omega)

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}d\nu\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(\nu)\big|^{2}\,\nu^{3}=\,\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(\nu)\big|^{2}\,|\nu|^{3}\;.

(54)

For a finite
\lambda
, and the same oscillatory configuration we had
before, the probability density per unit time is:

\gamma(\omega)\,=\,\frac{b^{2}}{4\pi}\,\Theta(K_{s}-\omega)\;\omega\,(K_{s}-\omega)\,\Big\{\big[1+(\frac{2\omega}{\lambda})^{2}\big]^{-1}+\big[1+(\frac{2(K_{s}-\omega)}{\lambda})^{2}\big]^{-1}\Big\}\;.

(55)

The shape of
\gamma(\omega)
is shown in Fig.
2

for several values of the dimensionless ratio
\lambda/K_{s}
.

) (in units of

\frac{P^{(2)}}{T}\,=\,\frac{b^{2}K_{s}^{3}}{4\pi}\,\bigg[\frac{r^{2}}{4}\ln\!\Big(1+\frac{4}{r^{2}}\Big)\,-\,\frac{r^{2}}{2}\,+\,\frac{r^{3}}{4}\arctan\!\frac{2}{r}\bigg]\;,\qquad r\equiv\frac{\lambda}{K_{s}}\;.

4
Consistency with previous results

In order to compare our results for the imaginary part of the effective action

\Gamma
with the ones obtained in
[
6
]
, we shall denote by
\Gamma_{[\mathrm{FG}]}
the
effective action used as reference in this comparison. We first carefully
consider the relation between the imaginary part of the effective action and the
exclusive probability of emission of a pair. In particular, we shall see that

P^{(2)}=2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma^{(2)}
is modified in an instructive way, since a

four-particle
(two-pair) channel opens at precisely the same order in

\psi
.

We shall start from the observation that, for an interaction quadratic in

\varphi
, the reduction
formulas
[
8
]
imply that the only connected amplitudes
involve two particles. The full amplitude is the exponential of the
connected parts
[
9
]
; the resulting Gaussian (Bogoliubov,
squeezed-vacuum) structure of the in-out
S
-matrix is standard for theories
quadratic in the field
[
13
]
. We use that
property to write the action of the
S
-matrix on the vacuum in the
following way:

S\,|0\rangle\,=\,{\mathcal{V}}\,\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}{\mathcal{A}}(i;j)\,a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}\Big)|0\rangle\;,\qquad{\mathcal{V}}\equiv\langle 0|S|0\rangle\;,

(57)

where we have used a shorthand notation for the exact two-particle amplitudes,
and the creation operators; i.e., the latter create a quantum in the mode
i\equiv({\mathbf{k}},\alpha)
. The kernel
{\mathcal{A}}(i;j)
is nothing other
than the connected pair amplitude of Sec.
3
(it is symmetric
under interchange of its indices).

Projecting (
57
) on a two-particle state:

{\mathcal{A}}(i;j)\,=\,\frac{\langle i,j|S|0\rangle}{\langle 0|S|0\rangle}\,=\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}(i;j)+{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}(i;j)+{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}(i;j)+\ldots\;,

(58)

where we introduced the perturbative expansion for each coefficient. This is the very amplitude defined in (
15
): the division by
{\mathcal{V}}=\langle 0|S|0\rangle
in (
58
) is exactly the removal of the disconnected vacuum subdiagrams anticipated there, so the kernel in (
57
) is the one whose perturbative coefficients
{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)},{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)},{\mathcal{A}}^{(3)}
were computed in Sec.
3
.

Projecting (
57
) on four-particle states yields,

\langle i,j,k,l|S|0\rangle\,=\,{\mathcal{V}}\,\big[\,{\mathcal{A}}(i;j){\mathcal{A}}(k;l)+{\mathcal{A}}(i;k){\mathcal{A}}(j;l)+{\mathcal{A}}(i;l){\mathcal{A}}(j;k)\,\big]\;,

(59)

i.e., the four-particle (or two-pair) amplitude is the symmetrized product of
pair amplitudes (the three terms are the three ways of grouping four identical
bosons into two pairs).

So far, we have not used the property that
S
is unitary. This fixes the
modulus of
{\mathcal{V}}

|{\mathcal{V}}|^{2}\,\big(1+{\mathcal{P}}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathcal{P}}^{2}+{\mathcal{Q}}+\ldots\big)=1

(60)

where:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}|{\mathcal{A}}(i;j)|^{2}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j,k,l}{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(i;j){\mathcal{A}}(j;k){\mathcal{A}}^{*}(k;l){\mathcal{A}}(l;i)\;,

(61)

and we kept terms with up to four powers of the
amplitudes. We have abbreviated
\sum_{i}\equiv\sum_{\alpha=o,e}\int L^{d}d^{d}{\mathbf{k}}/(2\pi)^{d}
, so that every
sum over indices above involves a phase-space integral plus a sum over the
e

and
o
indices.

With the above notation, we see that the
exact
probability of
detecting a
single pair,
p_{1}
, and two pairs,
p_{2}
, are then written in a rather compact
fashion:

p_{1}\,=\,|{\mathcal{V}}|^{2}{\mathcal{P}}\;\;,\;\;\;p_{2}\,=\,|{\mathcal{V}}|^{2}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathcal{P}}^{2}+{\mathcal{Q}}\big)\;,

(62)

which, using (
60
), leads to:

p_{1}\,=\,{\mathcal{P}}-{\mathcal{P}}^{2}+\ldots\;\;,\;\;\;p_{2}\,=\,\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathcal{P}}^{2}+{\mathcal{Q}}+\ldots\;,

(63)

where we have kept terms with up to four amplitudes. We remark that except for
the latter, no perturbative expansion for the amplitudes themselves has been
used yet. Expanding the amplitudes perturbatively,

\displaystyle p_{1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\,{\mathcal{P}}^{(2)}\;\;,\;\;\;p_{1}^{(4)}\,=\,{\mathcal{P}}^{(4)}-({\mathcal{P}}^{(2)})^{2}\;,

\displaystyle p_{2}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{1}{2}({\mathcal{P}}^{(2)})^{2}+{\mathcal{Q}}^{(4)}\;.

(64)

Finally, to check against the imaginary part of the effective
action
\Gamma
, we note that

2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma\,=\,-\ln|{\mathcal{V}}|^{2}\,=\,{\mathcal{P}}\,+\,{\mathcal{Q}}\,+\,\ldots\;.

(65)

Introducing the expansion of
\Gamma
, one obtains:

2\,{\rm Im}\Gamma^{(2)}\,=\,{\mathcal{P}}^{(2)}\;\;,\;\;2\,{\rm Im}\Gamma^{(4)}\,=\,{\mathcal{P}}^{(4)}+{\mathcal{Q}}^{(4)}

(66)

It follows that

{\mathcal{P}}^{(2)}=2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma^{(2)}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}|{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}(i;j)|^{2}\,,

(67)

which we verify explicitly below.

On the other hand, for the total fourth-order decay probability of the vacuum,
we need to add the corresponding probabilities at that order.
We find:

p_{1}^{(4)}+p_{2}^{(4)}\,=\,2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma^{(4)}\,-\,2\big({\rm Im}\,\Gamma^{(2)}\big)^{2}

(68)

which is exactly the
O(\psi^{4})
term of
1-e^{-2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma}
.
The nonlinear subtraction
-2({\rm Im}\,\Gamma^{(2)})^{2}
embodies the
familiar fact that
2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma
is not the probability of producing a
pair but the (mean) inclusive yield: the two coincide only to leading order and
differ once the multi-pair channels are populated, the vacuum being
correspondingly depleted
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

The second- and fourth-order terms in the expansion of
{\mathcal{P}}
are obtained by integrating the differentials we have already calculated;
explicitly,
{\mathcal{P}}^{(2)}=P^{(2)}\equiv\int dP^{(2)}
and

{\mathcal{P}}^{(4)}=P^{(4)}\equiv\int dP^{(4)}
are the integrated spectral
probabilities (
31
), (
3.1
) of
Sec.
3
, the calligraphic symbol merely emphasizing their
origin in the unitarity sum (
60
).
On the other hand,

{\mathcal{Q}}^{(4)}\,=\,\tfrac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j,k,l}{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}(i;j)\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}(j;k)\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)*}(k;l)\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}(l;i)\;.

(69)

The comparison at second order can then be summarized in the relation

P^{(2)}\,=\,2\,\mathrm{Im}\big[\Gamma^{(2)}_{[\textrm{FG}]}\big]\;,

(70)

the leading-order form of the standard relation
P=2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma
.

For any dimension
d
, integrating (
30
) over the two outgoing momenta we get

P^{(2)}\,=\,\int dP^{(2)}\big(k_{\shortparallel},{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}\big)\,=\,4\,\int\frac{d^{d}k_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\theta(k^{0})\,\sqrt{k_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\,\int\frac{d^{d}k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel})\big|^{2}\,\theta({k^{\prime}}^{0})\,\sqrt{{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\;,

(71)

where the on-shell positivity of
k_{\shortparallel}^{2}
and

{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}^{2}
is implicit in the requirement that the square roots
be real.

The double integral may be reduced to a
single
integral over the
parallel momentum
q\equiv k_{\shortparallel}+{k^{\prime}}_{\shortparallel}
carried
by
\psi
. Performing the shift
k^{\prime}_{\shortparallel}\to q-k_{\shortparallel}
at
fixed
k_{\shortparallel}
,

P^{(2)}\,=\,4\int\frac{d^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(q)\big|^{2}\,F_{d}(q)\;,

(72)

where the kernel

F_{d}(q)\,\equiv\,\int\frac{d^{d}k_{\shortparallel}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\Theta(k^{0})\sqrt{k_{\shortparallel}^{2}}\;\Theta(q^{0}-k^{0})\sqrt{(q-k_{\shortparallel})^{2}}

(73)

is a Lorentz-invariant function of
q
, supported on the forward light cone
of the
d
-dimensional parallel space-time. By Lorentz invariance and
dimensional analysis,

F_{d}(q)\,=\,c_{d}\,(q^{2})^{(d+2)/2}\,\Theta(q^{0})\,\Theta(q^{2})\;,

(74)

with a dimensionless coefficient
c_{d}
which we compute now.

In the rest frame of
q^{\mu}=(Q,\mathbf{0})
,
Q=\sqrt{q^{2}}
, the standard
spectral representation

\Theta(k^{0})\theta(k^{2})\sqrt{k^{2}}\,=\,\int_{0}^{\infty}2m^{2}\,dm\;\Theta(k^{0})\,\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})

(75)

reduces (
73
) to a weighted two-particle phase-space integral.
After scaling
m=Qx
,
m^{\prime}=Qy
with
0\leq x,y

x+y\leq 1
, one obtains

F_{d}(q)\,=\,\frac{(q^{2})^{(d+2)/2}}{2^{2d-4}\,\pi^{(d+1)/2}\,\Gamma\!\big((d-1)/2\big)}\,I_{d}\,\Theta(q^{0})\Theta(q^{2})\;,

(76)

I_{d}\,\equiv\,\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\geq 0\\
x+y\leq 1\end{subarray}}dx\,dy\;x^{2}y^{2}\,\Big[\big(1-(x+y)^{2}\big)\big(1-(x-y)^{2}\big)\Big]^{(d-3)/2}\;,

(77)

where the last bracket is the Källén triangle function for the on-shell
two-particle decomposition. Therefore

c_{d}\,=\,\frac{I_{d}}{2^{2d-4}\,\pi^{(d+1)/2}\,\Gamma\!\big((d-1)/2\big)}\;.

(78)

The integral
I_{d}
is rational for odd
d
(where
(d-3)/2
is a
non-negative integer) and a rational multiple of
\pi^{2}
for even
d
. The

d=1

.
Combining (

\;P^{(2)}\,=\,\mathcal{C}_{d}\,\int d^{d}q\,\Theta(q^{0})\,\Theta(q^{2})\,\big|\widetilde{\psi}(q)\big|^{2}\,(q^{2})^{(d+2)/2}\;,\quad\mathcal{C}_{d}\,=\,\frac{8^{2-d}\,\pi^{-(3d+1)/2}I_{d}}{\Gamma\!\big((d-1)/2\big)}\;.

(79)

For
d=1

79
) reproduces the result quoted in
Sec.

, in full agreement with Eq. (
3.2.4
).

(81)

Since

5
Conclusions

We have computed the exclusive transition amplitudes from the vacuum to a
two-particle state for a massless real scalar field coupled to a time-dependent
Dirichlet surface in
d+1
dimensions, working perturbatively in the surface
deformation
\psi
within the interaction picture and taking the Dirichlet limit

\lambda\to\infty
at the end. The amplitudes were obtained explicitly up to
third order in
\psi
(
2.4
), (
2.4
), which is all that is
needed for the decay probabilities up to fourth order.

At second order the spectral pair-creation probability
(
30
) is a convolution of
|\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}
with two on-shell
weights; in the localized, small-source regime it produces a dipole-like

\cos\theta
angular pattern peaked along the normal to the surface
(Fig.
1
). For a single-frequency drive we obtained the rate

\gamma(\omega)
in closed form, both in the Dirichlet limit and at finite

\lambda
, where a mild bimodal structure develops and the total rate saturates
toward its Dirichlet value near
\lambda\sim K_{s}
(Fig.
2
). The total second-order probability was evaluated in
closed form for arbitrary dimension, Eq. (
79
), and shown to
reproduce
exactly
twice the imaginary part of the effective action of the
companion paper
[
6
]
. This is the
dynamical-Casimir realization of the optical theorem: the discontinuity of the
one-loop bubble equals the on-shell phase-space integral of
|{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}|^{2}
.

At fourth order a two-pair channel opens, and the naive identification

P^{(4)}=2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma^{(4)}
no longer holds. Using the Gaussian
(squeezed-vacuum) structure of the
S
-matrix, dictated by the quadratic
coupling, we showed that the exact decay probability exponentiates as

1-e^{-2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma}
and that, at
O(\psi^{4})
, the one- and two-pair
probabilities satisfy
p_{1}^{(4)}+p_{2}^{(4)}=2\,{\rm Im}\,\Gamma^{(4)}-2({\rm Im}\,\Gamma^{(2)})^{2}
, Eq. (
68
).

Several directions invite further work. First, the closed-form evaluation of the
fourth-order phase-space integrals, the analogs of the
I_{d},c_{d}

coefficients of Sec.
4
, would turn (
68
)
into an explicit check of the
\zeta_{d}
coefficients of the effective action.
Second, the finite-
\lambda
spectra obtained here suggest that the imperfect
(non-Dirichlet) regime, relevant to realistic boundaries, deserves a systematic
treatment; the angular and spectral signatures we found are, in principle,
observable imprints of the geometry and dynamics of the radiating surface. Finally, extending the analysis to more singular potentials, such as those implementing Neumann boundary conditions within the same approach, may shed light on the nontrivial treatment of singularities in this context.
