Abstract
. We prove the existence of optimal separators for intersection graphs of balls and spheres in any dimension d d . One of our results is that if an intersection graph of n n spheres in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} has m m edges, then it contains a balanced separator of size O d ​ ( m 1 / d ​ n 1 − 2 / d ) O_{d}(m^{1/d}n^{1-2/d}) . This bound is best possible in terms of the parameters involved. The same result holds if the balls and spheres are replaced by fat convex bodies and their boundaries.



1.
Introduction

For a graph
G=(V,E)
, a
balanced separator
is a vertex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq V
such that each connected component of
G\setminus\mathcal{X}
contains at most
\tfrac{2}{3}|V|
vertices. In 1979, Lipton and Tarjan
[
15
]
showed that
n
-vertex planar graphs have balanced separators of size
O(\sqrt{n})
. There are many different characterizations of planar graphs that each lead to a different extension of the Lipton–Tarjan separator theorem. For example, Wagner’s theorem
[
23
]
states that a graph is planar if and only if it does not contain
K_{5}
or
K_{3,3}
as a minor. The family of graphs that can be embedded in a fixed surface has a finite list of forbidden minors by the Robertson–Seymour theorem
[
19
]
. Gilbert, Hutchinson, and Tarjan
[
9
]
proved that every
n
-vertex graph which can be embedded in a surface of genus
g
has a separator of size
O(\sqrt{gn})
. Later, Alon, Seymour, and Thomas
[
1
]
proved that for every graph
H
, every
n
-vertex graph that does not contain
H
as a minor has a separator of size at most
O_{H}(\sqrt{n})
.

Another important characterization of planar graphs is the Koebe–Andreev–Thurston circle packing theorem, which says that a graph is planar if and only if it is the intersection graph of interior-disjoint disks in the plane. (This means that there is a collection of interior-disjoint disks, one corresponding to each vertex, so that two vertices are connected by an edge if and only if the corresponding disks are tangent.) This leads to another way to try to extend the Lipton–Tarjan separator theorem, to intersection graphs of geometric objects. A
string graph
is an intersection graph of arcwise-connected sets in the plane, so planar graphs are string graphs. The Lipton–Tarjan separator theorem has been extended in several works studying separators for string graphs
[
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
,
17
]
, and we now know that every string graph with
m
edges has a balanced separator of size
O(\sqrt{m})
.

Graphs with a small balanced separator are poor expanders, and one can utilize the separator for many applications, including in fast divide-and-conquer type algorithms for many algorithmically hard graph problems (see, for example,
[
16
]
), extremal applications (see, for example,
[
8
,
14
]
), and enumerative problems (see, for example,
[
5
]
).

In this paper, we are interested in finding small separators for intersection graphs of geometric objects in higher dimensions. Miller, Teng, Thurston, and Vavasis
[
18
]
proved a separator theorem for the intersection graph of balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in which no point is in more than
k
of the balls. Generalizing this, we prove that every intersection graph of
n
balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
m
edges has a balanced separator of size
O_{d}(m^{1/d}n^{1-2/d})
. This bound is best possible in terms of the parameters involved. (Here and throughout the paper, we use the notation
O_{d}(\cdot)
to hide a multiplicative constant that depends only on the dimension
d
.)

Proving a separator theorem for intersection graphs of spheres turns out to be more challenging as such graphs can have significantly more complicated combinatorial structure. Recently, Davies, Georgakopoulos, Hatzel, and McCarty
[
3
]
studied this problem under the constraint that the intersection graph is
K_{t,t}
-free, proving that if an intersection graph of
n
spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
K_{t,t}
-free, then it has a balanced separator of size
\tilde{O}_{d}(t^{10}n^{1-1/(2d+8)})
. We first study the problem in general. Despite their more complicated structure, we show that intersection graphs of spheres have the same size separators as intersection graphs of balls.

Theorem 1.1
.

An intersection graph of
n
spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
m
edges has a balanced separator of size
O_{d}(m^{1/d}n^{1-2/d})
.

As a corollary, we show that every
K_{t,t}
-free intersection graph of
n
spheres has a separator of size
O_{d}(t^{1/d}n^{1-1/d})
(see Corollary
3.9
). This verifies a strengthening of a conjecture of Davies, Georgakopoulos, Hatzel and McCarty
[
3
, Conjecture 20]
. Both Theorem
1.1
and the corollary are best possible up to the constant factor depending on the dimension
d
(see Proposition
4.1
). We also prove a refinement of Theorem
1.1
which gives a separator whose size depends on the degree sequence of the intersection graph. As a corollary, we prove that every
K_{t,t}
-free intersection graph of spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has average degree
O_{d}(t)
. It follows that
K_{t,t}
-free intersection graphs of spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
have chromatic number
O_{d}(t)
.

We further study intersection graphs of other geometric objects in Section
4
. We generalize our results from balls and spheres to fat convex bodies and their boundaries. Unlike the planar case, we show that both the fatness and convexity assumptions are necessary in dimensions three and larger: no non-trivial separator exists for the intersection graph of general convex bodies or general fat bodies.

Finally, as separators have many algorithmic applications, in Section
5
we show that there are efficient randomized algorithms for finding such separators of intersection graphs of balls or spheres.

2.
Separators for intersection graphs of balls

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a collection of balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The
intersection graph
of
\mathcal{B}
, denoted
G(\mathcal{B})
, is the graph with vertex set
\mathcal{B}
where two balls are adjacent if they intersect. The collection
\mathcal{B}
is
k
-ply
if every point of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is contained in at most
k
balls. Miller, Teng, Thurston, and Vavasis
[
18
]
proved the following separator theorem for intersection graphs of balls.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a
k
-ply collection of
n
balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
G(\mathcal{B})
has a balanced separator of size
O_{d}(k^{1/d}n^{1-1/d})
.

A more recent proof of this result was given by Har-Peled
[
10
]
. Using his techniques, we are able to give a more refined version of the Miller–Teng–Thurston–Vavasis separator theorem. (Similar techniques also appeared in an earlier work of Smith and Wormald
[
20
]
.)

Given a collection of balls
\mathcal{B}
, for each point
x\in\mathcal{B}
, the
ply
of
x
, denoted
p(x)
, is the number of balls in
\mathcal{B}
which contain
x
. For each ball
B\in\mathcal{B}
, the
ply
of
B
, denoted

, is the maximum over
p(x)
over all points
x\in B
. With this definition we strengthen Theorem
2.1
as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a collection of
n
balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
G(\mathcal{B})
has a balanced separator of size

O_{d}\left(\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}p(B)^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}\right)^{1-\tfrac{1}{d}}\right).

Since every ball has ply at most one more than its degree in the intersection graph, we have the following corollary which bounds the size of the separator in terms of the degree sequence of the graph, or simply its number of edges.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a collection of
n
balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
G(\mathcal{B})
has a balanced separator of size

O_{d}\left(\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\deg(B)^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}\right)^{1-\tfrac{1}{d}}\right).

In particular, if
G(\mathcal{B})
has
m
edges, then it has a balanced separator of size
O_{d}(m^{1/d}n^{1-2/d})
.

The second claim follows from the first via an application of Hölder’s inequality since

\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\deg(B)^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}\leq\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\deg(B)\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}1\right)^{\tfrac{d-2}{d-1}}=(2m)^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}n^{\tfrac{d-2}{d-1}}.

To construct a balanced separator, we first construct a separator with slightly worse quantitative properties and then iterate a bounded number of times to find the desired separator.

Definition 2.4
.

For a graph
G=(V,E)
and
c<1
, a
c
-balanced separator
is a vertex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq V
such that each connected component of
G\setminus\mathcal{X}
contains at most
c|V|
vertices.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose graph
G=(V,E)
is such that every induced subgraph has a
c

has a balanced separator of size at most
\lceil\log_{c}(2/3)\rceil s

Proof.

Set

3.
Separators for intersection graphs of spheres

The main difficulty in generalizing the results of the previous section to intersection graphs of spheres is that the notion of ply is not useful for sphere intersection graphs. In particular, one can nest spheres so that the corresponding collection of balls would have large ply yet the sphere intersection graph has few or no edges. Furthermore, there are dense graphs (in particular, complete bipartite graphs) that are intersection graphs of spheres but are
2
-ply in the sense that no point lies on more than two spheres. (To see this, take two nested families of spheres that are arranged so that every sphere from one family intersects every sphere from the other family.) This shows that no analogue of the argument in Remark
Remark
applies to sphere intersection graphs. Despite these difficulties, we show that separators of the same size exist.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a collection of
n
spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
G(\mathcal{S})
has a balanced separator of size at most

O_{d}\left(\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\deg(S)^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}\right)^{1-\tfrac{1}{d}}\right).

As with Corollary
2.3
, this result implies Theorem
1.1
by an application of Hölder’s inequality. We start with an elementary lemma that is needed for the proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a collection of spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
G(\mathcal{S})
has maximum degree at most
\Delta
and that there exists a point
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
contained in the interior of at least
2s
spheres. Then there exists a set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
with
|\mathcal{X}|\leq\Delta
such that every connected component of
G(\mathcal{S})\setminus\mathcal{X}
has size at most
|\mathcal{S}|-s
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
be the set of spheres containing
p
in their interior. For any two spheres
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, either they intersect or one contains the other in its interior. Consider the partial order on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
defined by containment. Take any linear extension of this poset and let
S_{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be the median element of this linear extension. This means that there are at least
s-1
spheres which either intersect
S_{0}
or are contained in its interior and there are at least
s-1
spheres which either intersect
S_{0}
or contain it in their interior.

Now let
\mathcal{X}=N(S_{0})
, the neighborhood of
S_{0}
in

, i.e., the set of spheres which intersect
S_{0}
. We claim that every connected component of
G(\mathcal{S})\setminus\mathcal{X}
has size at most
|\mathcal{S}|-s
. To see this, note that no sphere in
\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{X}
intersects
S_{0}
, so any connected component of
G(\mathcal{S})\setminus\mathcal{X}
consists of spheres either entirely inside
S_{0}
or entirely outside
S_{0}
. However, we showed that there are at least
s

S_{0}
and the

s

G(\mathcal{S})\setminus\mathcal{X}
has size at most
|\mathcal{S}|-s

Intersection graphs of spheres are an example of semialgebraic graphs. Using known bounds on the number of edges in
K_{t,t}
-free semialgebraic graphs
[
21
]
, we deduce the following weak bound for separators in
K_{t,t}
-free sphere intersection graphs.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a collection of
n
spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
G(\mathcal{S})
is
K_{t,t}
-free, then it has a balanced separator of size
O_{d}(t^{2/d(d+2)}n^{1-2/d(d+2)})
.

Proof.

Represent the sphere centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
r
by the point
(x,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Now two spheres
(x,r)
and
(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})
intersect if and only if
(r-r^{\prime})^{2}\leq\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{2}\leq(r+r^{\prime})^{2}
. These are polynomial inequalities of degree
O(1)
, meaning that
G(\mathcal{S})
can be represented by a semialgebraic graph in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
of total degree
O(1)
. (See
[
21
, Definition 1.1]
for the full definition.)

By
[
21
, Theorem 1.6]
(a bound on the Zarankiewicz problem for semialgebraic graphs), if
G(\mathcal{S})
is
K_{t,t}
-free, then it has
O_{d}(t^{2/(d+2)}n^{2(d+1)/(d+2)})
edges. Applying Theorem
1.1
with this bound shows that
G(\mathcal{S})
has a balanced separator of size

O_{d}\left(\left(t^{2/(d+2)}n^{2(d+1)/(d+2)}\right)^{1/d}n^{1-2/d}\right)=O_{d}(t^{2/d(d+2)}n^{1-2/d(d+2)}).\qed

We can bootstrap this weak bound to get a better bound on the number of edges in a
K_{t,t}
-free sphere intersection graph. This follows from
[
6
, Lemma 8]
which turns a bound on the size of separators into a bound on the number of edges for any hereditary family of graphs. (See
[
8
, Section 3]
for another application of this method.)

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a collection of
n
spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
G(\mathcal{S})
is
K_{t,t}
-free, then it has
O_{d}(tn)
edges.

Proof.

We aim to apply
[
6
, Lemma 8]
. This result requires a monotone function
\phi
so that any
n
-vertex
K_{t,t}
-free intersection graph of spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
contains a balanced separator of size at most
n\phi(n)
. By Corollary
3.7
, we can take
\phi(n)=C_{d}(t/n)^{2/d(d+2)}
where
C_{d}
is the constant in that result. Clearly
\phi(n)
is monotone.

Set
n_{0}=(12C_{d})^{d(d+2)/2}t
so that
\phi(n_{0})=1/12
. Then
[
6
, Lemma 8]
implies that every
K_{t,t}
-free intersection graph of spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices has at most
Cn_{0}n/2
edges where

C=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(1+\phi\left(\left\lceil(4/3)^{i}n_{0}\right\rceil\right)\right).

We can bound

C\leq\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\phi\left(\left\lceil(4/3)^{i}n_{0}\right\rceil\right)\right)\leq\exp\left(2C_{d}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{i}\frac{t}{n_{0}}\right)^{2/d(d+2)}\right).

As

, showing that

. This gives the desired bound on the number of edges when
n\geq n_{0}

Note that if
n<n_{0}
, then we can trivially bound the number of edges by
n^{2}<n_{0}n=O_{d}(tn)
.

We also note that both of the assumptions, convexity and fatness, are necessary. Indeed, for
d\geq 3
, every graph is the intersection graph of convex bodies in

\mathbb{R}^{d}

Proposition 4.4
.

For
d\geq 3
,
\epsilon>0
, and any graph
G
, there exists a collection of
(1+\epsilon)
-fat connected bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose intersection graph is
G
.

Proof.

Let
B_{k}
be the ball centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
r_{k}=(2+2\epsilon^{-1})^{k}
. We will define
X_{k}
to be a
(1+\epsilon)
-fat connected body that is contained in
B_{k}
. The set
X_{k}
will be a “balloon” consisting of a large sphere in
\inter(B_{k})\setminus\inter(B_{k-1})
that is tangent to
B_{k-1}
at a point
p_{k}
and a “string” in
B_{k-1}
: a curve starting at
p_{k}\in\partial B_{k-1}
, passing through
\partial B_{0}
at a point
q_{k}
, and then continuing into
B_{0}
. We will define these sets
X_{k}
so that they are disjoint in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\inter(B_{0})
. See Figure
5
for an illustration of this construction.

Suppose we have defined
B_{0},\ldots,B_{k-1}
and
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k-1}
. Let
p_{k}
be a point on
\partial B_{k-1}
. Say
B_{k-1}
has radius
r_{k-1}
. Let
Y_{k}
be the ball of radius
\epsilon^{-1}r_{k-1}
, centered at
(1+\epsilon^{-1})p_{k}
. Then
B_{k-1}\cup Y_{k}
is contained in the ball of radius
(1+\epsilon)\epsilon^{-1}r_{k-1}
centered at
\epsilon^{-1}p_{k}
. Since

, we see that
Y_{k}
is contained in
\inter(B_{k})
. We will define

to be

where
\gamma_{k}
is a curve starting from

which is contained in
B_{k-1}
. Whatever our choice of
\gamma_{k}
, we have ensured that
X_{k}
is
(1+\epsilon)
-fat and connected.

Pick

to be a point in
\partial B_{0}\setminus\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{k-1}\}
. Let
\gamma^{\prime}_{k}
be a curve from
p_{k}
to

whose interior is contained in

and let

5.
Algorithmic implementations

Suppose we are given
\mathcal{B}
as a collection of centers and radii of balls. We give a randomized linear time algorithm that computes a small separator for
G(\mathcal{B})
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The separator for intersection graphs of balls given in Theorem
2.2
can be computed by a randomized linear time algorithm that succeeds with probability at least
1/2
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{P}
for the centers of the balls of
\mathcal{B}
. Let
r
be the smallest radius such that there exists a ball
B_{0}
of radius
r
that contains at least
9^{-d}n
points of
\mathcal{P}
. Fix such a ball
B_{0}
. Then for any
p\in\mathcal{P}\cap B_{0}
, the ball of radius
2r
centered at
p
contains
B_{0}
and thus contains at least
9^{-d}n
points of
\mathcal{P}
.

Our first goal is to efficiently find an approximation for this ball
B_{0}
. Fix a large constant
C_{d}
and sample
C_{d}\cdot 9^{d}
points
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots\in\mathcal{P}
uniformly at random. For each point
p_{i}
, compute the smallest
r_{i}
such that the ball of radius
r_{i}
centered at
p_{i}
contains
9^{-d}n
points of
\mathcal{P}
. This can be computed in linear time for each
p_{i}
. Say we succeed if
\min_{i}r_{i}\leq 2r
. By the discussion above, we succeed if some
p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}\cap B_{0}
, which occurs with probability at least

1-\left(1-9^{-d}\right)^{C_{d}\cdot 9^{d}}\geq 1-e^{-C_{d}}.

Pick
1\leq i\leq C_{d}\cdot 9^{d}
such that
r_{i}
is minimal. As in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, pick
r_{\mathrm{rand}}\in[r_{i},2r_{i}]
uniformly at random and let
\mathcal{X}
be the set of balls
B\in\mathcal{B}
which intersect the sphere of radius
r_{\mathrm{rand}}
centered at
p_{i}
. This set can be computed in linear time. Now we still have

\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{X}|]\leq 4\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}p(B)^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}\right)^{1-\tfrac{1}{d}},

so by Markov’s inequality,

\Pr\left[|\mathcal{X}|\leq 4C_{d}\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}p(B)^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}\right)^{1-\tfrac{1}{d}}\right]\geq 1-C_{d}^{-1}.

Now the set
\mathcal{X}
is a
(1-9^{-d})
-balanced separator for
G(\mathcal{B})
. To see this, note that the ball of radius
r_{i}
centered at
p_{i}
contains
B_{0}
which contains
\left\lceil 9^{-d}n\right\rceil
points of
\mathcal{P}
. Furthermore, the ball of radius
2r_{i}
centered at
p_{i}
can be covered by at most
8^{d}
translates of
B_{0}
, so it contains at most
8^{d}\left\lceil 9^{-d}\right\rceil n<(1-9^{-d})n
points of
\mathcal{P}
.

Thus we have a randomized linear time algorithm which computes a small
(1-9^{-d})
-balanced separator for
G(\mathcal{B})
with probability at least
1-C_{d}^{-1}
. By Lemma
2.5
, for
C_{d}
sufficiently large, we can iterate this algorithm
\left\lceil\log_{1-9^{-d}}(2/3)\right\rceil
times to produce a
\tfrac{2}{3}
-balanced separator for
G(\mathcal{B})
still in linear time with success probability at least
\tfrac{1}{2}
.
∎
