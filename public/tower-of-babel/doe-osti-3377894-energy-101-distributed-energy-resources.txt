 What Are Distributed Energy Resources?

• A distributed energy resource (DER) is a source of electric power that is not directly
 connected to a bulk power system and loads, includes both generators and energy
 storage technologies capable of exporting active power to the power system.
    (IEEE, 2018)

• Examples of DERs include:

 o Solar photovoltaic (PV)

 o Wind

 o Combined heat and power (CHP)

 o Energy storage

 o Small hydro

 What Are Controllable Loads?

• Controllable loads can be modified through direct control or price-based
 mechanisms to respond to grid signals for operational or economic objectives.
    (NIST, 2014)

• Examples include:

 o Demand response (DR)

 o Electric vehicles (EVs)

 o Energy efficiency (EE)

 o Smart thermostats

 o Water heaters

 Energy Storage Technologies

Energy storage broadly refers to any
technology that stores energy for later use.
Available technologies:
• Pumped storage hydropower (PSH)
• Lithium-ion battery energy storage
 system (BESS)
• Sensible thermal storage (molten salt)
• Compressed air energy storage (CAES)
• Flow batteries.

                                                    National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   15

 Pumped Storage Hydropower (PSH)

• The system sends water from a
  higher-elevation reservoir to a
  lower-elevation one through
  pipes—turning electricity-
  generating turbines along the way.
• Water is pumped back up to the
  upper reservoir for later use
  during periods of low energy
  demand (e.g., night-time), or to
  manage excess generation (e.g.,
  high-penetration solar).
• Can serve as a battery in smaller
  systems.
 Image: DOE

 Battery Energy Storage System (BESS)

• A BESS is a device that charges or collects
 energy and then discharges that energy
 later to provide electricity when needed.
 o Lithium-ion batteries are the most common
 technology.
• BESS example uses:
 o When fluctuating sources like wind and
 solar are not producing power
 o During grid outages
 NLR and Raytheon perform system-level testing on the Miramar ZnBr
 o Batteries typically last for 1-6 hours. Flow Battery.
 Photo: Dennis Schroeder, NREL

 o When electricity rates are high to save
 money.

 Demand Response and Ancillary Service Markets

In addition to directly lowering their utility bill through peak shaving and energy arbitrage, battery storage
system owners can be compensated through utility or regional programs for providing a service.
 • Demand Response Programs offered by
 certain utility providers compensate
 customers for lowering demand (by
 discharging battery systems) at certain
 times.
 • Capacity Markets regional programs
 (RTO/ISO) compensate battery systems
 for delivering energy when dispatched.
 • Frequency Regulation Markets
 (regulation-up and regulation-down)
 compensate battery system owners for
 responding to automatic control signals.
 • Participation in these programs
 doesn’t always align with utility bill
 reduction opportunities Image: Elgqvist (2018)

 Types of Energy Storage

 Electrochemical: Storage of electricity in batteries or supercapacitors utilizing
 various materials for the anode, cathode, electrode and electrolyte

 Mechanical: Direct storage of potential or kinetic energy; typically pumped
 storage hydropower, CAES, or flywheel

 Thermal: Storage of excess energy as heat or cold for later use; can involve
 sensible (temperature change) or latent (phase change) thermal storage

 Chemical: Storage of electrical energy by creating hydrogen through
H2 electrolysis of water; hydrogen may also be produced (with emissions) from
 natural gas by steam-methane reforming

 Power vs. Energy Capacity

 • The maximum instantaneous output of the battery
 Power • Measured in kW or MW.

 • How much energy you have available
 Energy • Measured in kWh or MWh.

 • Ratio of power vs. energy; need to specify both
Power : Energy • Typical configurations include 1 MW:2 MWh,
 Ratio equivalent to a 2-hour battery.

The purpose of the battery impacts the system size and ratio.
