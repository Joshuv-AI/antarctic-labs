Analytic Cut in Epistemic Logics with Distributed Knowledge
Ryo Murai, Sizhuo Liu, Katsuhiko Sano

Abstract
Distributed knowledge is a notion of group knowledge studied in multi-agent epistemic logic. Semantically, the distributed knowledge of a group is interpreted via an accessibility relation given by the intersection of the epistemic accessibility relations of the agents in that group. This paper investigates sequent calculi for epistemic logics of distributed knowledge based on 𝐊𝟒𝟓 \mathbf{K45} , 𝐊𝐃𝟒𝟓 \mathbf{KD45} , and 𝐒𝟓 \mathbf{S5} . While cut elimination holds in existing sequent calculi for modal logics 𝐊𝟒𝟓 \mathbf{K45} and 𝐊𝐃𝟒𝟓 \mathbf{KD45} , it fails in all the systems mentioned above. Instead, we establish the analytic cut property for all three systems by adapting Takano’s (2018) strategy, which restricts the cut formulas to the set of subformulas of the conclusion of the cut rule. As a corollary, the Craig interpolation theorem holds for all logics considered. We also show that all proof-theoretic results remain valid when the empty group is allowed for the distributed-knowledge operator, in which case the distributed knowledge for the empty group is interpreted as the global modality.

4
Analytic Cut Property of
\mathsf{G}(\mathbf{K45}_{D})
,
\mathsf{G}(\mathbf{KD45}_{D})
and
\mathsf{G}(\mathbf{S5}_{D})

This section establishes the analytic cut property, i.e., every application of the cut rule in
\mathsf{G}(\Lambda)
can be replaced with an application of the cut rule such that the cut formula is in the set of
subformulas
of the conclusion of the cut. The set
\mathsf{Sub}(\varphi)
of subformulas of an
L
-formula
\varphi
is defined as usual. In particular,
\mathsf{Sub}(D_{G}\varphi)=\mathsf{Sub}(\varphi)\cup\{D_{G}\varphi\}
. For any multiset
\Gamma
of formulas,
\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma)
is defined as
\bigcup_{\varphi\in\Gamma}\mathsf{Sub}(\varphi)
. Moreover, we say that
\Xi
is
subformula-closed
if
\mathsf{Sub}(\Xi)\subseteq\Xi
. Let
\Lambda\in\{\mathbf{K45}_{D},\mathbf{KD45}_{D},\mathbf{S5}_{D}\}
in this section.

Definition 4.1
.

A cut

\Gamma,\Pi\Rightarrow\Delta,\Sigma\lx@proof@logical@and\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,\varphi\varphi,\Pi\Rightarrow\Sigma

is
analytic
if
\varphi\in\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma,\Pi,\Delta,\Sigma)
. The sequent calculus
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)
is the same calculus as
\mathsf{G}(\Lambda)
except that
(Cut)
is always analytic.
In what follows, when
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
is derivable in
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}({\Lambda})
, we denote
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}({\Lambda})\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
.

Let
\Xi
be a subformula-closed finite set of formulas in what follows. The following definition is taken from
[
31
]
, whose notion originates from
[
30
]
.

Definition 4.2
.

A pair
(\Gamma,\Delta)
is a
\Xi
-partial valuation
in
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)
if all the following hold:

(1)

\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\not\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
;

(2)

\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma,\Delta)=\Gamma\cup\Delta
;

(3)

\Gamma\cup\Delta\subseteq\Xi
.

Remark 4.3
.

It is worth noting that the condition for
\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma,\Delta)
highlights the distinction between semantically eliminating only non-analytic cuts and eliminating all cuts. In fully cut-free sequent calculi (for example,
\mathbf{S4}
), the equality
\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma,\Delta)=\Gamma\cup\Delta
may
not
hold; instead, the inclusion
\Gamma\cup\Delta\subseteq\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma,\Delta)
is utilized. This is precisely the difference between the approach used in this paper to establish the analytic cut property and the one applied in
[
31
]
to prove semantic completeness for completely cut-free systems.

The following lemma can be established similarly to Lindenbaum’s Lemma (cf.
[
34
, Lemma 1]
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Gamma\cup\Delta\subseteq\Xi
. If
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\not\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
, then there exists a
\Xi
-partial valuation
(\Gamma^{+},\Delta^{+})
such that
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{+}
,
\Delta\subseteq\Delta^{+}
and
\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma,\Delta)=\Gamma^{+}\cup\Delta^{+}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}
be an enumeration of all formulas in
\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma,\Delta)
. In what follows we inductively construct a sequence
(\Gamma_{k},\Delta_{k})(1\leq k\leq m)
such that
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\not\vdash\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k}
,
\Gamma_{k}\subseteq\Gamma_{k+1}
and
\Delta_{k}\subseteq\Delta_{k+1}
for all

. For base step, let

. It is clear that
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\not\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
. For inductive step, we define

as follows:

\begin{cases}\Gamma_{k+1}=\Gamma_{k}\text{ and }\Delta_{k+1}=\Delta_{k}\cup\{\varphi_{k}\}&\text{if }\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\not\vdash\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k},\varphi_{k}\\
\Gamma_{k+1}=\Gamma_{k}\cup\{\varphi_{k}\}\text{ and }\Delta_{k+1}=\Delta_{k}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Moreover, it is not the case that
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\vdash\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k},\varphi_{k}
and
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\vdash\varphi_{k},\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k}
. Suppose it is the case for contradiction. Then we can derive
\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k}
by

\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k}\Gamma_{k},\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k},\Delta_{k}\lx@proof@logical@and\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k},\varphi_{k}\varphi_{k},\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k},

where

holds clearly since

and
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{k}
,
\Delta\subseteq\Delta_{k}
. Hence we have

, which contradicts the induction hypothesis. Therefore, either
\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k},\varphi_{k}
or
\varphi_{k},\Gamma_{k}\Rightarrow\Delta_{k}
is underivable in
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)
. Finally, let

\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma,\Delta)=\mathsf{Sub}(\Gamma^{+},\Delta^{+})=\Gamma^{+}\cup\Delta^{+}
. Moreover

,
\Delta\subseteq\Delta^{+}
and
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\not\vdash\Gamma^{+}\Rightarrow\Delta^{+}
by our definition.
∎

D_{G_{1}}\varphi_{1},\dots,D_{G_{m}}\varphi_{m},\mathsf{A}\Pi\Rightarrow\mathsf{A}\Sigma,D_{H_{1}}\psi_{1},\dots,D_{H_{n}}\psi_{n},D_{G}\chi D_{G_{1}}\varphi_{1},\dots,D_{G_{m}}\varphi_{m},\mathsf{A}\Pi\Rightarrow\mathsf{A}\Sigma,D_{H_{1}}\psi_{1},\dots,D_{H_{n}}\psi_{n},\chi\quad D_{G}\varphi,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta\varphi,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta.

where
\bigcup^{m}_{i=1}G_{i}\cup\bigcup^{n}_{j=1}H_{j}\subseteq G
in
(\Rightarrow D^{\mathbf{S5}_{D\mathsf{A}}})
.
The following proposition can be established similarly as Proposition
3.2
.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
is derivable in
\mathsf{G}(\Lambda)
then
\bigwedge\Gamma\to\bigvee\Delta
is valid in the corresponding class
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
of pseudo frames to
\Lambda
.

In what remains in this section, let
\Lambda\in\{\mathbf{K45}_{D\mathsf{A}},\mathbf{KD45}_{D\mathsf{A}},\mathbf{S5}_{D\mathsf{A}}\}
, and explain that the proof-theoretic results, namely the analytic cut property and Craig interpolation theorem for
\mathsf{G}(\Lambda)
can be obtained in a similar way as in Theorems
4.9
and
5.5
, respectively, except that a modification of the pseudo-model is required to establish the analytic cut property.

Definition 6.2
.

Define
M^{\Lambda}_{\Xi}=(W^{\Lambda}_{\Xi},(R^{\Lambda}_{G})_{G\in\mathsf{Grp}},V^{\Lambda}_{\Xi})
to be the pseudo model derived from
\Xi
in the same way as in Definition
4.5
.
Define
\sim_{\mathsf{A}}
on
W^{\Lambda}_{\Xi}
as follows:

(\Gamma,\Delta)\sim_{\mathsf{A}}(\Pi,\Sigma)\text{ iff }\left\{\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi}\,|\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Gamma}\,\right\}=\left\{\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi}\,|\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Pi}\,\right\}.

It follows that
\sim_{\mathsf{A}}
is reflexive, transitive, symmetric and Euclidean.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
(\Phi,\Psi)
be a
\Xi
-partial valuation. The pseudo model

M^{\Lambda}_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)}=(W^{\Lambda}_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)},(R^{\Lambda}_{G})_{G\in\mathsf{Grp}},V^{\Lambda}_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)})

derived from
\Xi
and
(\Gamma,\Delta)
is defined as:

•

W^{\Lambda}_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)}=\left\{\,{(\Gamma,\Delta)\in W^{\Lambda}_{\Xi}}\,|\,{(\Phi,\Psi)\sim_{\mathsf{A}}(\Gamma,\Delta)}\,\right\}
;

•

R^{\Lambda}_{G}
is defined depending on the choice of
\Lambda
:

–

\Lambda=\mathbf{K45}_{D\mathsf{A}}
:
(\Gamma,\Delta)R^{\mathbf{K45}_{D\mathsf{A}}}_{G}(\Pi,\Sigma)
iff

\forall H\subseteq G(\left\{\,{\varphi,D_{H}\varphi}\,|\,{D_{H}\varphi\in\Gamma}\,\right\}\subseteq\Pi
and
\left\{\,{D_{H}\varphi}\,|\,{D_{H}\varphi\in\Pi}\,\right\}\subseteq\Gamma
)

and
\left\{\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi}\,|\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Gamma}\,\right\}=\left\{\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi}\,|\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Pi}\,\right\}
,

–

\Lambda=\mathbf{KD45}_{D\mathsf{A}}
:
R^{\mathbf{KD45}_{D\mathsf{A}}}_{G}:=R^{\mathbf{K45}_{D\mathsf{A}}}_{G}
,

–

\Lambda=\mathbf{S5}_{D\mathsf{A}}

(\Gamma,\Delta)R^{\mathbf{S5}_{D\mathsf{A}}}_{G}(\Pi,\Sigma)
iff
\forall H\subseteq G(\left\{\,{D_{H}\varphi}\,|\,{D_{H}\varphi\in\Gamma}\,\right\}=\left\{\,{D_{H}\varphi}\,|\,{D_{H}\varphi\in\Pi}\,\right\})

and
\left\{\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi}\,|\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Gamma}\,\right\}=\left\{\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi}\,|\,{\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Pi}\,\right\}
;

•

(\Gamma,\Delta)\in V^{\Lambda}_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)}(p)
iff
p\in\Gamma
.

The above definition of
W^{\Lambda}_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)}
ensures that the global modality
\mathsf{A}
means “for all states” by specifying the root
(\Phi,\Psi)
. All the necessary lemmas can be proved in a manner similar to those for distributed knowledge logic without the global modality, with the exception of the following:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(\Gamma,\Delta)
be a
\Xi
-partial valuation in
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)
.
Then
(\Gamma,\Delta)
satisfies the following:

(\mathsf{A}\Rightarrow)

\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Gamma
implies
\varphi\in\Pi
for all
(\Pi,\Sigma)\in W_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)}^{\Lambda}
;

(\Rightarrow\mathsf{A})

\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Delta
implies
\varphi\in\Sigma
for some
(\Pi,\Sigma)\in W_{\Xi(\Phi,\Psi)}^{\Lambda}
;

(D)

D_{G}\varphi\in\Delta
implies
\varphi\in\Sigma
for some

such that
(\Gamma,\Delta)R^{\Lambda}_{G}(\Pi,\Sigma)
;

For item

, to find a desired witness

reachable from
(\Phi,\Psi)
, adding the contexts
\mathsf{A}\Pi
and
\mathsf{A}\Sigma
to the inference rules
(\Rightarrow D^{\mathbf{K45}_{D}})
,

(D^{\mathbf{KD45}_{D}})
and

(\Rightarrow D^{\mathbf{S5}_{D}})
of
D_{G}
plays a crucial role. By applying this lemma, we can prove the semantic completeness theorem, following the same line of argument as in the case without the global modality:

Theorem 6.5
.

For any sequent
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
, if
\bigwedge\Gamma\to\bigvee\Delta
is valid in the class
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
of all pseudo frames which satisfy the properties corresponding to
\Lambda
, then
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
.

Corollary 6.6
.

For any sequent
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
, if

\mathsf{G}(\Lambda)\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
, then
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
.

For the rules
(\Rightarrow\mathsf{A})
and
(\mathsf{A}\Rightarrow)
, we can employ the same argument in terms of Maehara’s method as used for the modal logic
\mathbf{S5}
(see, e.g.,
[

]
). Moreover,
adding the contexts
\mathsf{A}\Pi
and
\mathsf{A}\Sigma
to the inference rules
(\Rightarrow D^{\mathbf{K45}_{D}})
,

(D^{\mathbf{KD45}_{D}})
and

(\Rightarrow D^{\mathbf{S5}_{D}})
of
D_{G}

does not change the outline of Maehara’s argument or the information regarding the common vocabulary. Therefore, we obtain the following:

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\Lambda\in\{\mathbf{K45}_{D\mathsf{A}},\mathbf{KD45}_{D\mathsf{A}},\mathbf{S5}_{D\mathsf{A}}\}
.
Suppose
\mathsf{G}(\Lambda)\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
. Then for any partition
\langle(\Gamma_{1}:\Delta_{1});(\Gamma_{2}:\Delta_{2})\rangle
for the sequent
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
, there exists a formula
\varphi
that satisfies the following:

\Gamma_{1}\Rightarrow\Delta_{1},\varphi
and
\varphi,\Gamma_{2}\Rightarrow\Delta_{2}
are derivable in
\mathsf{G}(\Lambda)
and
\mathsf{X}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{X}(\Gamma_{1},\Delta_{1})\cap\mathsf{X}(\Gamma_{2},\Delta_{2})
for all
\mathsf{X}\in\{\mathsf{Agt},\mathsf{Prop}\}
.
Therefore, the Craig interpolation theorem holds for
\mathsf{G}(\Lambda)
.

The reader may wonder if all the sequent calculi equipped with the global modality are semantically complete in terms of models, i.e., pseudo models
M
=
(W,(R_{G})_{G\in\mathsf{Grp}},V)
such that
R_{G}

=

\bigcap_{a\in G}R_{\left\{{a}\right\}}
holds for all
G\in\mathsf{Grp}
.
This can be done in terms of a technique called “tree unraveling”, which transforms a pseudo-model into one that satisfies
\bigcap_{a\in G}R_{\left\{{a}\right\}}=R_{G}
.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
\Lambda\in\{\mathbf{K45}_{D\mathsf{A}},\mathbf{KD45}_{D\mathsf{A}},\mathbf{S5}_{D\mathsf{A}}\}
.
If a formula
\varphi
is valid in the class
\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}
of all frames that satisfy the corresponding properties to
\Lambda
then
\varphi
is valid in the class
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
of all pseudo frames that satisfy the properties corresponding to
\Lambda
.
Therefore, for any sequent
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
, if
\bigwedge\Gamma\to\bigvee\Delta
is valid in the class
\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}
, then
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{a}}(\Lambda)\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
.

Proof.

(Outline) The latter part follows immediately from the former part by Theorem
6.5
. In what follows, we outline the proof of the former part. The following definitions and arguments are adapted from
[
9
,
4
,
19
]
.
Suppose that a formula
\varphi
is valid in the class
\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}
. To show that
\varphi
is valid in the pseudo-frame class
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
, let us fix an arbitrary pseudo-frame
F=(W,(R_{G})_{G\in\mathsf{Grp}})
in
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
. Our goal is to establish the validity of
\varphi
on
F
.
Let us fix an arbitrary valuation
V
and a state
w\in W
.
We will show that
M,w\models\varphi
, where
M:=(F,V)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
M
is point-generated by
w
with respect to the binary relations
(R_{G})_{G\in\mathsf{Grp}}
.
We define the tree unraveling
\mathtt{Tree}^{\Lambda}(M,w)
of
M
around
w
as follows:

•

\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w):=\{

\displaystyle\langle w_{0},G_{1},w_{1},\dots,G_{m},w_{m}\rangle\mid{}

\displaystyle m\geq 0,\ G_{i}\in\mathsf{Grp},\ w_{0}=w,w_{i-1}R_{G_{i}}w_{i}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq m\,\}.

We refer to an element of
\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w)
as a “path (from state
w
)” and denote it by
\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{u}
, etc.

•

We define a binary relation
\mathcal{R}_{G}
on
\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w)
as follows:
\overrightarrow{w}\mathcal{R}_{G}\overrightarrow{v}
iff
\overrightarrow{v}=\overrightarrow{w}{}^{\frown}(H,\mathtt{tail}(\overrightarrow{w}))
for some
H\supseteq G
, where
\mathtt{tail}(\overrightarrow{w})
denotes the last element of
\overrightarrow{w}
, and
⌢
denotes concatenation. We use
\mathcal{R}_{G}^{+}
(or
\mathcal{R}_{G}^{\ast}
) to mean the transitive closure (or the reflexive and transitive closure) of
\mathcal{R}_{G}
.

•

Depending on our choice of
\Lambda
, we define
\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{G}
as follows:

–

For
\Lambda\in\left\{{\mathbf{K45}_{D\mathsf{A}},\mathbf{KD45}_{D\mathsf{A}}}\right\}
, define
\overrightarrow{w}\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{G}\overrightarrow{v}
iff there exists
\overrightarrow{u}\in\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w)
such that
\overrightarrow{u}\mathcal{R}_{G}^{\ast}\overrightarrow{w}
and
\overrightarrow{u}\mathcal{R}_{G}^{+}\overrightarrow{v}
;

–

For
\Lambda=\mathbf{S5}_{D\mathsf{A}}
, define
\overrightarrow{w}\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{G}\overrightarrow{v}
iff there exists
\overrightarrow{u}\in\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w)
such that
\overrightarrow{u}\mathcal{R}_{G}^{\ast}\overrightarrow{w}
and
\overrightarrow{u}\mathcal{R}_{G}^{\ast}\overrightarrow{v}
.

•

\mathcal{V}(p)=\left\{\,{\overrightarrow{v}\in\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w)}\,|\,{\mathtt{tail}(\overrightarrow{v})\in V(p)}\,\right\}
for every
p\in\mathsf{Prop}
.

•

Define
\mathtt{Tree}^{\Lambda}(M,w):=(\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w),(\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{G})_{G\in\mathsf{Grp}},\mathcal{V})
.

It is straightforward to verify that the frame part
(\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w),(\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{G})_{G\in\mathsf{Grp}})
of
\mathtt{Tree}^{\Lambda}(M,w)
still belongs to
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
. Moreover,
we note that
\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{G}

=

\bigcap_{a\in G}\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{\left\{{a}\right\}}
holds.
We can also establish that the mapping
\mathtt{tail}:\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w)\to W
, which sends each
\overrightarrow{v}
to its last element
\mathtt{tail}(\overrightarrow{v})
, is a surjective bounded morphism between pseudo-models. Here, surjectivity is necessary for preserving the truth of formulas of the form
\mathsf{A}\psi
(along the bounded morphism). Then we obtain
\mathtt{Tree}^{\Lambda}(M,w),\langle w\rangle\models\varphi
iff
M,\mathtt{tail}(\langle w\rangle)\models\varphi
, where
\mathtt{tail}(\langle w\rangle)=w
.
Since
\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{G}

=

\bigcap_{a\in G}\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{\left\{{a}\right\}}
holds,

(\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w),(\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{\left\{{a}\right\}})_{a\in\mathsf{Ag}})\in\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}
.
Since
\varphi
is valid in
\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}
, we obtain

(\mathtt{Finpath}(M,w),(\mathcal{R}^{\Lambda}_{\left\{{a}\right\}})_{a\in\mathsf{Ag}},\mathcal{V}),\langle w\rangle\models\varphi
, hence

\mathtt{Tree}^{\Lambda}(M,w),\langle w\rangle\models\varphi
in terms of pseudo models. Therefore, it follows from the above equivalence that
M,w\models\varphi
, as desired.
∎

7
Conclusion

There are several directions for further research. First, there have been attempts to combine epistemic logic with coalition logic
\mathbf{CL}
, which is a logic that concerns coalitional power (see
[
2
]
). It would be interesting to develop sequent calculi for
\mathbf{CL}
with distributed knowledge and to investigate whether analytic cut properties can be established. Second, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, a syntactic proof of the analytic cut property in the style of
[
29
]
is
not
available for our sequent calculi. The main obstacle appears to lie in the parameterization of the operator by a group
G
. However, a Gentzen-style sequent calculus for
\mathbf{S5}
was introduced in
[
13
]
, in which the operator is not parameterized by groups. It would therefore be interesting to investigate whether Takano’s syntactic strategy can be adapted to systems combining this calculus with
\mathbf{CL}
. Third, Hilbert systems and cut-free sequent calculi for intuitionistic
\mathbf{K}
,
\mathbf{KT}
,
\mathbf{KD}
,
\mathbf{K4}
,
\mathbf{K4D}
, and
\mathbf{S4}
with distributed knowledge were introduced in
[
19
]
. Building on this line of work, public announcement expansions of intuitionistic
\mathbf{K}
,
\mathbf{KT}
,
\mathbf{K4}
, and
\mathbf{S4}
with distributed knowledge were studied in
[
21
]
. It would therefore be worthwhile to investigate intuitionistic versions of
\mathbf{K45}
,
\mathbf{KD45}
, and
\mathbf{S5}
with distributed knowledge, and to examine whether these intuitionistic systems can be expanded with public announcement logic via the approach used in
[
15
]
, as well as whether the main proof-theoretic results are preserved under such extensions. Finally, an axiomatization for comparative knowledge was introduced in
[
5
]
, which allows one to express that one group’s (distributed) knowledge includes
all
of another group’s (distributed) knowledge. It would therefore be interesting to develop sequent calculi for this expansion of distributed knowledge and examine whether the main proof-theoretic results established in this paper still hold.
