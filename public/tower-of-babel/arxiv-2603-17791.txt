On the size of gluon occupancies in saturation Preprint: APS/123-QED
A. H. Mueller

Abstract
The size of gluon occupancies, or equivalently the nuclear gluon TMD, at gluon transverse momentum k ⟂ ≤ Q s ​ ( Y ) k_{\perp}\leq Q_{s}(Y) is evaluated. Without Sudakov corrections the occupations can become arbitrarily large while Sudakov effects lead to maximum occupancies of size ( 1 / α ) 3 / 2 (1/\alpha)^{3/2} . Results are the same for running coupling and fixed coupling dynamics. The coherent (elastic) TMD and inelastic gluon TMD are the same in the gluon saturation region. The saturated gluons in the light cone wavefunction seem to have little or no interaction among themselves.

Introduction

(16)

Thus the maximum occupancies occur at

k_{\perp}^{2}\simeq Q_{s}^{2}(Y)e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\alpha N_{c}}}}

(17)

with occupancy on the order of
\alpha^{-3/2}
. Of course there is very little phase space near the
k_{\perp}^{2}
in (
17
) so the great majority of saturated gluons are around
k_{\perp}^{2}=Q_{s}^{2}(Y)
.

V.2
Sudakov effects; running coupling

We are briefly going to redo the discussion of Sec.
V.1
but now using a running coupling for both BFKL and for Sudakov effects. We continue to focus on coherent reactions with incoherent reactions to be discussed later in this note.

We start with the lowest order Sudakov term in (

) but now we use running coupling

\alpha(l_{\perp}^{2})=\frac{1}{b\ln(l_\perp^2/\Lambda^2)}

(18)

where

so that (

\mathit{Sud}^{(1)}=-\frac{N_{c}}{\pi b}\int_{k_{\perp}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\frac{\differential{l_\perp^2}}{l_{\perp}^{2}}\frac{1}{\ln(l_\perp^2/\Lambda^2)}\int_{l_{\perp}^{2}/q_{+}}^{k_{-}}\frac{\differential{l_-}}{l_{-}}

Eq. (

\mathit{Sud}^{(1)}=-\frac{N_{c}}{\pi b}\quantity[\ln\frac{Q^2}{\Lambda^2} \ln(\frac{\ln(Q^2/\Lambda^2)}{\ln(k_\perp^2/\Lambda^2)}) - \ln\frac{Q^2}{k_\perp^2}]

(20)

which can be written as

\mathit{Sud}^{(1)}=-\frac{N_{c}}{\pi b}\quantity[\ln\frac{Q^2}{\Lambda^2} \ln(1+\frac{\ln(Q^2/k_\perp^2)}{\ln(k_\perp^2/\Lambda^2)}) - \ln\frac{Q^2}{k_\perp^2}]

(21)

which we will use just below.

Also, in the running coupling case we should use
Mueller and Triantafyllopoulos 2002

\ln\frac{Q_{s}^{2}(Y)}{\Lambda^{2}}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{4N_{c}}{\pi b}\frac{\chi(\lambda_{0})}{1-\lambda_{0}}Y}

(22)

rather than (
11
). With running coupling the picture in Fig.
5
changes to that of Fig.
8
. When

\bar{Y}

Y

VI
Other issues

In this section we briefly discuss inelastic reactions as well as the role of
Q^{2}
, in particular as it affects Sudakov corrections.

VI.1
Inelastic TMD’s and gluon distributions

So far we have only considered coherent
\gamma^{*}
-nucleus scattering and the associated coherent gluon distributions. The generalization to inelastic reactions is straightforward. As an example consider the coherent reaction of Fig.
3
. As we have discussed earlier one may view the process as the scattering of the state
\ket{q\bar{q}g(+)}
on a nucleus. (In Fig.
3
the nucleus is a simple MV nucleus, but as we have seen earlier there is not a lot of difference between a fully evolved QCD nucleus and a MV nucleus.) So far we have considered the elastic scattering of
\ket{q\bar{q}g(+)}
on the nucleus, but in the strong scattering regime we know that in general the coherent scattering and the inelastic reactions are identical which, in the current circumstance means that the coherent occupation given by (
8
) is the same as the corresponding incoherent occupation. Similarly Sudakov effects for elastic and inelastic TMD’s are the same.

VI.2
The role of
Q^{2}

In the scattering process, in the projectile frame, the
q\bar{q}
system is required to be of transverse size
1/Q
. That is we choose events not in the aligned jet region. The overall cross section is of size
1/Q^{2}
, however, our interest is not in the cross section but in the gluon TMD of the target. Any
Q^{2}
significantly greater than
Q_{s}^{2}(Y)
allows the
q\bar{q}
pair, in the target frame to be a good measurer of a gluon of transverse momentum
k_{\perp}<Q_{s}(Y)
. Eq. (
14
) shows that the gluon transverse momentum dependence of the target TMD depends strongly on the value of
Q^{2}
when
\alpha\ln[2](Q^2/k_\perp^2)
is greater than 1. This is very natural. When
Q^{2}
is very large it is a strong requirement to demand that there be no gluon emissions having transverse momentum between
k_{\perp}
and
Q
. On the other hand it is important that
Q
be greater than
Q_{s}(Y)
otherwise our whole picture of the projectile frame scattering will not be that of a
\ket{q\bar{q}g(+)}
state scattering on a target but, if
Q<Q_{s}(Y)
, that of a
\ket{q\bar{q}(+)}
state scattering on a target. In our detailed discussion of Sudakov efects we have taken
Q^{2}\gg Q_{s}^{2}(Y)
in order that the
q\bar{q}
coming from the
\gamma^{*}
be a good probe of the target gluon distribution, but
Q^{2}/Q_{s}^{2}(Y)
not too large in order to minimize Sudakov effects.
