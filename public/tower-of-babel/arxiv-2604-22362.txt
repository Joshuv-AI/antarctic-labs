Convergence of Timed-Metric Spaces and Causality Thanks: R. Perales was funded by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) [Grant DOI: 10.55776/EFP6]. M. Che was funded by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) [Grant DOI: 10.55776/STA32].
Mauricio Che, Raquel Perales

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of timed Gromov–Hausdorff distance for timed-metric spaces. We prove that this distance is bilipschitz equivalent to the intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distance of Sakovich–Sormani, and therefore induces the same notion of convergence. We establish a compactness theorem for the timed Gromov–Hausdorff distance, obtained as a straightforward consequence of Gromov’s classical compactness theorem. We then investigate the causal structure of timed-metric spaces and the stability of causality under intrinsic timed-Hausdorff convergence. We further analyze causally-null timed-metric spaces and develop several of their basic properties. As a curiosity, we include in an appendix Gromov’s original proof of his compactness theorem, as presented in his paper on groups of polynomial growth.



1.
Introduction

We recall that a space-time consists of a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold, and that the development of notions of low-regularity space-times, as well as of weak convergence in this setting, has been an active area of research in recent years; see, for example,
[
11
,
14
,
5
,
4
]
. In particular, Sakovich–Sormani introduced the notion of a timed-metric space
[
18
]
and defined several distances between such spaces, including the intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distance.

In this manuscript, we work within the framework of Sakovich–Sormani, and we now recall some results in this setting. Given a space-time, Sakovich–Sormani
[
18
]
used the cosmological time of Andersson–Galloway–Howard
[
1
]
together with the null distance of Sormani-Vega
[
19
]
to associate a timed-metric space to any smooth causally null-compactifiable space-time. They further proved that the intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distance is definite, in the sense that the distance between two causally null-compactifiable space-times vanishes if and only if there exists a bijection preserving both the distance and the time. In the smooth setting, this is equivalent to the existence of a Lorentzian isometry between the original manifolds, by results of Sakovich–Sormani
[
17
]
, Burtscher–Garcia Heveling
[
3
]
, and Galloway
[
9
]
. Moreover, Che–Perales–Sormani
[
6
]
established a compactness theorem for the intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distance using addresses, which we will recall later; Perales showed in
[
15
]
that, as conjectured by Sakovich–Sormani, intrinsic timed-Hausdorff convergence implies convergence with respect to some of the other notions introduced by Sakovich–Sormani; and in the upcoming work
[
16
]
, conditions on space-times implying intrinsic timed-Hausdorff convergence will be addressed. We now proceed to state our main results.

Definition 1.1
.

A timed-metric space,

(X,d,\tau)
, is a metric space,

(X,d)
,
endowed with a
1
-Lipschitz function,

\tau:X\to[0,\infty),

which we call time function.

A useful example of a timed-metric space is the max-product space, implicitly defined in
[
18
, Propositions 4.24 and 4.25]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a metric space
(Z,d_{Z})
and
\tau_{max}>0
, we define
the max-product space of the compact interval,
[0,\tau_{max}]
, with

(Z,d_{Z})

as follows:

(1)

\left([0,\tau_{max}]\times Z,\,d_{prod},\,\tau_{prod}\right),

where

(2)

\tau_{prod}(t,z)=t

and

(3)

d_{prod}\Big((t_{1},z_{1}),(t_{2},z_{2})\Big)=\max\Big\{|t_{1}-t_{2}|,\,d_{Z}(z_{1},z_{2})\Big\}.

We now use the max-product space construction to define an analogue of the Gromov–Hausdorff distance for timed-metric spaces.

Definition 1.3
.

The
timed-Gromov–Hausdorff distance
between two
compact timed-metric spaces is

d_{\tau-GH}\bigg((X_{1},d_{1},\tau_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2})\bigg)=\inf d_{H}^{[0,\tau_{max}]\times Z}\bigg(\varphi_{1}(X_{1}),\varphi_{2}(X_{2})\bigg)

where the infimum is over all compact metric spaces,
Z
, and over all time and distance preserving maps,

\varphi_{j}:(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})\to([0,\tau_{max}]\times Z,d_{prod},\tau_{prod}),

where
\tau_{max}=\max\{\tau_{1,\max},\tau_{2,\max}\}
and
\tau_{j,\max}=\max\{\tau_{j}(x):\,x\in X_{j}\}
.

The
timed-Gromov–Hausdorff distance between two
compact timed-metric spaces is symmetric and
takes finite values. In fact,

d_{\tau-GH}\bigg((X_{1},d_{1},\tau_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2})\bigg)\leq d_{GH}\bigg((X_{1},d_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2})\bigg)+\tau_{max}.

In Theorem
3.2
, we prove that this notion is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
the intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distance defined by
Sakovich–Sormani in
[
18
, Definition 4.22]
. This equivalence was first conjectured in
[
18
, Remark 4.26]
.
Since Sakovich–Sormani’s distance is definite, our timed-Gromov–Hausdorff distance is definite as well, this means that

d_{\tau-GH}\bigg((X_{1},d_{1},\tau_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2})\bigg)=0

if and only if there is a distance and time preserving bijection
between the timed-metric spaces. We prove in Theorem
3.4
that
this notion also satisfies the triangle inequality. In the Appendix
A
, we reprove Gromov’s Compactness Criterion from
[
10
]
, and as a straightforward application, we prove a compactness theorem for timed Gromov–Hausdorff distance. However, Che–Perales–Sormani’s compactness result for intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distance
[
6
]
can also be seen as a compactness theorem for timed Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

Theorem 1.4

(Compactness Theorem)
.

If
(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})
is a sequence of compact timed-metric spaces that are uniformly equibounded
and equicompact, and there exists
\tau_{\max}\geq 0
such that

(4)

\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\tau_{j}(X_{j})\subset[0,\tau_{max}].

Then a subsequence converges in the timed-Gromov–Hausdorff sense to a compact timed-metric space
(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\tau_{\infty})
. In fact, there exist
compact
Z
and distance and time preserving maps,

(5)

\varphi_{j}=(\tau_{j},\xi_{j}):X_{j}\to[0,\tau_{max}]\times Z

where
\xi_{j}:X_{j}\to Z
is
distance preserving
, such that the Hausdorff distance between the images converges to zero:

(6)

d_{H}^{[0,\tau_{max}]\times Z}\bigg(\varphi_{j}(X_{j}),\varphi_{\infty}(X_{\infty})\bigg)\to 0.

Similarly to the convergence of points under the Gromov–Hausdorff distance, we obtain convergence of both points and “times” under the timed-Gromov–Hausdorff and
intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distances, c.f. Remark
1.8
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose a sequence of compact
timed-metric spaces converges
in the intrinsic timed-Hausdorff sense to a compact timed-metric space,

(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau-\textrm{GH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\tau_{\infty}).

Then for a subsequence
j_{k}
the conclusions of Theorem
1.4

hold and the following is satisfied.
Given any sequence of points
p_{j}\in X_{j}
, there exists
p_{\infty}\in X_{\infty}
and a subsequence, that we do not relabel, such that

(7)

\varphi_{j}(p_{j})=(\tau_{j}(p_{j}),\xi_{j}(p_{j}))\to\varphi_{\infty}(p_{\infty})=(\tau_{\infty}(p_{\infty}),\xi_{\infty}(p_{\infty})).

Conversely, given
p_{\infty}\in X_{\infty}
there is a sequence
p_{j}\in X_{j}
such that

(8)

\varphi_{j}(p_{j})\to\varphi_{\infty}(p_{\infty}).

Since causality plays a crucial role in the Lorentzian setting, we define a causal structure in our setting.

Definition 1.6
.

For a timed-metric space
(X,d,\tau)
we define a
causal structure
as follows: For
p,q\in X
we say that
p
is in the
causal future
of
q
, written
p\in J^{+}(q)
, iff

(9)

\tau(p)-\tau(q)=d(p,q).

If this holds, we also say that
q
is in the
causal past
of
p
,
written
q\in J^{-}(p)
. When necessary, we write
J^{+}_{X}
and
J^{-}_{X}
in each case. We say that
p
and
q
are
causally related
if

p\in J^{+}(q)
or
q\in J^{+}(p)
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.4
we obtain the following corollary describing how causality behaves under timed-Gromov–Hausdorff convergence. The first part shows stability of causality.

Corollary 1.7
.

Suppose a sequence of compact
timed-metric spaces converges
in the timed-Gromov–Hausdorff sense to a compact timed-metric space,

(10)

(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau-\textrm{GH}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\tau_{\infty}).

Then for a subsequence
j_{k}

the conclusions of Theorem
1.4

hold and the following is satisfied:

(1)

Given two sequences
p_{j},q_{j}\in X_{j}
such that

p_{j}\in J^{+}_{X_{j}}(q_{j})
, there exists

p_{\infty},q_{\infty}\in X_{\infty}
such that

(11)

p_{\infty}\in J^{+}_{X_{\infty}}(q_{\infty})

and
\varphi_{j}(p_{j})\to\varphi_{\infty}(p_{\infty})
,
\varphi_{j}(q_{j})\to\varphi_{\infty}(q_{\infty})
.

(2)

If
p_{\infty}\notin J^{+}_{X_{\infty}}(p^{\prime}_{\infty})

then there exist two sequences of points

p_{j},p^{\prime}_{j}\in X_{j}
such that

\varphi_{j}(p_{j})\to\varphi_{\infty}(p_{\infty})
and

\varphi_{j}(p^{\prime}_{j})\to\varphi_{\infty}(p^{\prime}_{\infty})
, and for

j
sufficiently large it holds,

(12)

p_{j}\notin J^{+}_{X_{j}}(p^{\prime}_{j}).

Remark 1.8
.

In Corollary
1.5

and Corollary
1.7
we do have convergence of points in the following sense. Since the maps
\xi_{j}:X_{j}\to Z
are distance preserving, it is clear that
for any sequence
x_{k}\in X_{j}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x\in X_{j}
,

x_{k}\to x\Longleftrightarrow\xi_{j}(x_{k})\to\xi_{j}(x).

Note that by
Lemma
3.1
, the class of functions considered in the definition of
d_{\tau\text{-}GH}
is larger than the class consisting of all functions
(\tau,\zeta):X\to[0,\tau_{\max}]\times Z
for which
\zeta
is distance-preserving. The proofs of both corollaries also hold under
intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distance, since in this case the class of functions
used to calculate
d_{\tau\text{-}H}
consists precisely of all maps
(\tau,\kappa):X\to[0,\tau_{\max}]\times Z
with
\kappa
a Fréchet map, which is distance-preserving. Moreover, when using addresses in the sense of Definition
2.12
, the sequences appearing in the final parts of these corollaries
are automatically given by the properties of the addresses, c.f. Corollary
4.7
.

Using the causal structure defined above, we can also define an associated
null distance
, in analogy to

[
19
]
. We prove that this null distance is idempotent, whenever it defines a metric.

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
(X,d,\tau)
be a timed-metric space.
Then the null distance
\hat{d}_{d,\tau}
, given in Definition
5.1
, is an extended distance such that

(13)

\hat{d}_{d,\tau}\geq d,

and
\tau
is
1
-Lipschitz with respect to
\hat{d}_{d,\tau}
.
Moreover, if
\hat{d}_{d,\tau}
only takes finite values then
(X,\hat{d}_{d,\tau},\tau)
is a timed-metric space and
\hat{d}_{\hat{d}_{d,\tau},\tau}=\hat{d}_{d,\tau}
.

When a timed-metric space
(X,d,\tau)
is such that
\hat{d}_{d,\tau}=d
, we say that
(X,d,\tau)
is
causally null
. Thus, Proposition
1.9

says that, for any timed-metric space
(X,d,\tau)
such that
\hat{d}_{d,\tau}
is a distance, the timed-metric space
(X,\hat{d}_{d,\tau},\tau)
is causally
null.

Background

We briefly review
basic material about metric spaces, see
[
2

for details. Moreover, we state

[
18
]
and Che–Perales–Sormani in

[

2.1.
Metric spaces and GH distance

Given a metric space
(Z,d_{Z})
, the Hausdorff distance between two subsets
U,W\subset Z

is given by

d_{H}^{Z}\bigg(U,W\bigg)=\inf\left\{r>0:\begin{array}[]{c}\forall u\in U,\exists w\in W\textrm{ s.t. }d_{Z}(u,w)<r\\
\forall w\in W,\exists u\in U\textrm{ s.t. }d_{Z}(u,w)<r\end{array}\right\}.

Given two compact metric spaces
(X_{j},d_{j})
,
j=1,2
,
the Gromov–Hausdorff (GH) distance between them is
defined by

d_{GH}\bigg((X_{1},d_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2})\bigg)=\inf d_{H}^{Z}\bigg(\varphi_{1}(X_{1}),\varphi_{2}(X_{2})\bigg)

where the infimum is over all
distance preserving maps,

\varphi_{j}:X_{j}\to Z
, into all possible metric spaces,
Z
.
Furthermore, a map between metric-spaces,

\varphi:(X,d_{X})\to(Z,d_{Z})
, is said to be
distance preserving if

d_{Z}(\varphi(x),\varphi(x^{\prime}))=d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\qquad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in X.

A particular class of distance preserving maps
are Fréchet maps, to define them recall that
(\ell^{\infty},d_{\ell^{\infty}})

is the Banach space defined as

(14)

\ell^{\infty}=\{(s_{1},s_{2},...)\,:s_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}},\,d_{\ell^{\infty}}((s_{1},s_{2},...).(0,0,...))<\infty\}

where

(15)

d_{\ell^{\infty}}((s_{1},s_{2},...),(r_{1},r_{2},...))=\sup\{|s_{i}-r_{i}|\,:\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a separable metric-space,
(X,d)
,
for any countable dense collection of points
\mathcal{N}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\}
of
X
,
the map

\kappa_{X}=\kappa_{X,\mathcal{N}}:(X,d)\to(\ell^{\infty},d_{\ell^{\infty}})

given by

(16)

\kappa_{X}(x)=(d(x_{1},x),d(x_{2},x),...)\subset\ell^{\infty}.

is called a Fréchet map.

Fréchet maps are distance-preserving (see
[
8
]
and
[
18
]
).
Adopting the notation of Sakovich–Sormani, given
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
compact metric spaces, let

d_{\kappa\text{-}GH}\big((X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y})\big):=\inf\,d_{H}^{\ell^{\infty}}\big(\kappa_{X}(X),\kappa_{Y}(Y)\big),

where the infimum is taken over all pairs of Fréchet maps
\kappa_{X}
and
\kappa_{Y}
.
By
[
18
, Proposition 2.7]
,

d_{GH}
is biLipschitz equivalent to
d_{\kappa\text{-}GH}
.
In analogy with this, in Theorem
3.2
we show bilipschitz
equivalence between
d_{\tau-H}
and
d_{\tau-GH}
.

2.2.
Timed-metric spaces and intrinsic timed-Hausdorff distance

We now briefly review some results and definition from Sakovich–Sormani in

[
18
]
:

Definition 2.2
.

Given a compact timed-metric space,
(X,d,\tau)

and a countably dense collection of points,

{\mathcal{N}}
,
we define the
timed-Fréchet map as

\kappa_{\tau,X}=\kappa_{\tau,X,{\mathcal{N}}}:X\to[0,\tau_{max}]\times\ell^{\infty}\subset\ell^{\infty},\qquad\kappa_{\tau,X}=(\tau,\kappa_{X,{\mathcal{N}}}).

Proposition 2.3

(
[
18
]
)
.

Any
timed-Fréchet map,

\kappa_{\tau,X}:X\to[0,\tau_{max}]\times\ell^{\infty}\subset\ell^{\infty},

is distance preserving.

Definition 2.4

(
[
18
, Definition 4.22]
)
.

The
intrinsic
timed-Hausdorff
distance

between two compact timed-metric spaces,

(X_{i},d_{i},\tau_{i})
,
i=1,2
, is given by

(17)

d_{\tau-H}\Big((X_{1},d_{1},\tau_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2})\Big)=\inf\,d^{\ell^{\infty}}_{H}(\kappa_{\tau_{1},X_{1}}(X_{1}),\kappa_{\tau_{2},X_{2}}(X_{2}))

where the infimum is taken over all possible
timed-Fréchet maps,

\kappa_{\tau_{1},X_{1}}:X_{1}\to\ell^{\infty}
and

\kappa_{\tau_{2},X_{2}}:X_{2}\to\ell^{\infty}
, which are
found by considering all countable dense collections of points in the
X_{i}
and all reorderings of these collections of points.

The intrinsic timed-Hausdorff
distance satisfies the triangle inequality (see
[
6
, Proposition 4.4]
) and is definite in the following sense.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
18
, Theorem 5.14]
)
.

Let

(X_{i},d_{i},\tau_{i})
,
i=1,2
, be
two compact timed-metric spaces.
Then,

(18)

d_{\tau-H}\Big((X_{1},d_{1},\tau_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2})\Big)=0

if and only if there is a
distance and time preserving bijection

(19)

\varphi:(X_{1},d_{1},\tau_{1})\to(X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2}).

We note that a map between two timed-metric spaces,
\varphi:(X_{1},d_{1},\tau_{1})\to(X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2})
, is said to be

time preserving
if
\tau_{2}(\varphi(x))=\tau_{1}(x)
for all
x\in X_{1}
.

2.3.
Correspondences

Let us recall the notion of
correspondences
between sets, which yields a standard characterization of bounds for the Hausdorff distance. The results here will be used in the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Definition 2.6
.

Given two sets
A
and
B
,
a correspondence between them, denoted by
\mathcal{C}
, is a set
\mathcal{C}\subset A\times B
such that

\forall a\in A,\,\exists b\in B\,s.t.\,(a,b)\in{\mathcal{C}}

and

\forall b\in B,\,\exists a\in A\,s.t.\,(a,b)\in{\mathcal{C}}.

Additionally, if
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
are metric spaces, such that

A\subset X
and
B\subset Y
, the distortion of

\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{C})=\sup\{|d_{X}(a,a^{\prime})-d_{Y}(b,b^{\prime})\,|\,(a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}\}.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset A\times B
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset B\times C
be correspondences. Define
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}\subset A\times C
by

\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}=\{(a,c)\in A\times C:\exists b\in B\ \text{such that}\ (a,b)\in\mathcal{C}\ \text{and}\ (b,c)\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\}.

Then it is immediate to verify that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
is a correspondence between
A
and
C
. This is the composition of correspondences
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, and we denote it by
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{C}
.

By the triangle inequality, it follows that

(20)

\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{C})\leq\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})+\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{C}).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
Z
be a metric space.
Given two subsets
A,B\subset Z
, it holds

d_{H}^{Z}(A,B)<R

if and only if

\mathcal{C}=\Bigl\{(a,b)\,|\,d_{Z}(a,b)<R\Bigr\}\subset A\times B

is a correspondence between
A
and
B
. Moreover, in this case
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{C})<2R
.

The following lemma will be used in the proof of Theorem
3.2
to establish the equivalence of different definitions of timed-Hausdorff distance between timed-metric spaces.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be two compact metric spaces.
If there is a correspondence
\mathcal{C}
such that
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{C})<R
, then there exist a pair of
Fréchet maps

\kappa_{X}:X\to\ell^{\infty}

and

\kappa_{Y}:Y\to\ell^{\infty}

such that for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}

(21)

d_{\ell^{\infty}}(\kappa_{X}(x),\kappa_{Y}(y))<R,

and thus,
d_{H}^{\ell^{\infty}}(\kappa_{X}(X),\kappa_{Y}(Y))<R
.

Proof.

Since
X
and
Y
are compact,
there exists
R^{\prime}<R
such that

\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{C})<R^{\prime}
.
Let
\{x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},...\}\subset X

and
\{y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2},...\}\subset Y

be countably dense collection of points.
For each
y_{i}^{\prime}
take

(22)

x_{y_{i}^{\prime}}\in X\,s.t.\,(x_{y_{i}^{\prime}},y_{i}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}\subset X\times Y

and, analogously, for each
x_{i}^{\prime}
take

(23)

y_{x_{i}^{\prime}}\in Y\,s.t.\,(x^{\prime}_{i},y_{x_{i}^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{C}\subset X\times Y.

Define

{\mathcal{N}}_{X}=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}...\}=\{x_{1}^{\prime},x_{y_{1}^{\prime}},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{y_{2}^{\prime}},x_{3}^{\prime},x_{y_{3}^{\prime}},...\}\subset X

and

{\mathcal{N}}_{Y}=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},....\}=\{y_{x_{1}^{\prime}},y_{1}^{\prime},y_{x_{2}^{\prime}},y_{2}^{\prime},y_{x_{3}^{\prime}},y_{3}^{\prime},...\}\subset Y.

Then the Fréchet maps
\kappa_{X}=\kappa_{X,{\mathcal{N}}_{X}}:X\to\ell^{\infty}

and

\kappa_{Y}=\kappa_{Y,{\mathcal{N}}_{Y}}:Y\to\ell^{\infty}
are given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{X}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d_{X}(x_{1}^{\prime},x),d_{X}(x_{y_{1}^{\prime}},x),d_{X}(x_{2}^{\prime},x),d_{X}(x_{y_{2}^{\prime}},x),\dots),

\displaystyle\kappa_{Y}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d_{Y}(y_{x_{1}^{\prime}},y),d_{Y}(y_{1}^{\prime},y),d_{Y}(y_{x_{2}^{\prime}},y),d_{Y}(y_{2}^{\prime},y),\dots).

If
(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}
,
then by (
22
) the odd coordinates of

\kappa_{X}(x)-\kappa_{Y}(y)\in\ell^{\infty}
satisfy

|d_{X}(x_{i}^{\prime},x)-d_{Y}(y_{x_{i}^{\prime}},y)|\leq R^{\prime}.

Analogously, by
(
23
) the even coordinates satisfy

|d_{X}(x_{y_{i}^{\prime}},x)-d_{Y}(y_{i}^{\prime},y)|\leq R^{\prime}.

Hence, each coordinate of

\kappa_{X}(x)-\kappa_{Y}(y)\in\ell^{\infty}
,
is smaller than or equal to
R^{\prime}
.
Therefore,

d_{\ell^{\infty}}(\kappa_{X}(x),\kappa_{Y}(y))\leq R^{\prime}<R.

From this, and recalling that
\mathcal{C}
is a correspondence, we get

d_{H}^{\ell^{\infty}}(\kappa_{X}(X),\kappa_{Y}(Y))<R
.
∎

2.4.
Che–Perales–Sormani’s compactness theorem

Recall that a sequence
\{(X_{j},d_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of metric spaces is
equibounded
if the diameters are uniformly bounded

(24)

\exists D>0\,s.t.\,\forall j\in\mathbb{N}:\operatorname{diam}_{d_{j}}(X_{j})\leq D,

and
equicompact
if the spaces are uniformly compact

(25)

\forall R\in(0,D]\,\,\exists N(R)\in\mathbb{N}\,s.t.\,\forall j\in\mathbb{N}\,\,\exists x^{j}_{R,i}\in X_{j}\,\,s.t.\,\,X_{j}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N(R)}B_{d_{j}}(x^{j}_{R,i},R).

Proposition 2.10

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\{(X_{j},d_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an equibounded and equicompact sequence of metric spaces. Define
\varepsilon_{i}=2^{-i}
and
N_{i}=N(\varepsilon_{i})
.

For each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
A_{i}
be the finite set,

(26)

A_{i}=\Bigl\{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{i}):\,1\leq a_{j}\leq N_{i},j=1,\ldots,i\Bigr\},

p_{i}:A_{i+1}\to A_{i}
the projection maps

p_{i}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1})=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i})

and

(27)

A=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}.

Then there exist countable dense subsets

(28)

{\mathcal{N}}_{j}=\{x_{a}^{j}:\,a\in A\}\subset X_{j}

and maps

(29)

I^{j}_{i}\colon A_{i}\to X_{j}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad I^{j}\colon A\to X_{j},

given by

(30)

I^{j}(a)=I^{j}_{i}(a)=x_{a}^{j}\in X_{j}\qquad\forall a\in A_{i}\subset A,

such that

(31)

X_{j}\subset\bigcup_{a\in A_{i}}B_{d_{j}}(x^{j}_{a},\varepsilon_{i})

and

(32)

I^{j}_{i+1}(a)\in B_{d_{j}}(I^{j}_{i}(p_{i}(a)),2\varepsilon_{i})\subset X_{j}.

Theorem 2.11

(Timed Gromov’s Compactness Theorem,
[
6
]
)
.

If
\{(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a equibounded and equicompact sequence of compact timed-metric spaces that have a uniform bound on their
1
-Lipschitz functions:

\tau_{j}(X_{j})\subset[0,\tau_{max}],

then a subsequence converges in the intrinsic timed-Hausdorff sense to a compact timed-metric space
(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\tau_{\infty})
. In fact,
up to a subsequence,
there exist distance and time preserving
timed-Fréchet maps,

\varphi_{j}=\kappa_{\tau_{j},\mathcal{N}_{j}}\colon X_{j}\to[0,\tau_{max}]\times Z\subset\ell^{\infty},

where
Z
is compact, such that

(33)

d_{H}^{[0,\tau_{max}]\times Z}(\varphi_{j}(X_{j}),\varphi_{\infty}(X_{\infty}))\to 0.

With the notation introduced in Proposition
2.10
,
in combination with the following notion, introduced in
[
6
, Definition 3.3]
, we can get a better description of the Hausdorff convergence above, in terms of uniform convergence.

Definition 2.12
.

Using the notation from Proposition
2.10
, let
\mathcal{A}
be the set of ordered sequences

\alpha=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},...\}\subset A

that satisfy
\alpha_{i}\in A_{i}\subset A
and

p_{i}(\alpha_{i+1})=\alpha_{i}
for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We call elements of
\mathcal{A}

addresses
.

Thus, besides (
33
), by
[
6
, Proposition 3.4]
the following holds:

(34)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}}d_{\ell^{\infty}}\Big(\varphi_{j}({\mathcal{I}}^{j}(\alpha)),\varphi_{\infty}({\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(\alpha))\Big)=0,

for some
{\mathcal{I}}^{j}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to X_{j}
surjective maps.
In particular, from (
34
), uniform convergence of time and distances,

(35)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}}|\tau_{j}({\mathcal{I}}^{j}(\alpha))-\tau_{\infty}({\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(\alpha))|=0,

(36)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}}|d_{j}({\mathcal{I}}^{j}(\alpha),{\mathcal{I}}^{j}(\alpha^{\prime}))-d_{\infty}({\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(\alpha),{\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(\alpha^{\prime}))|=0,

immediately follow.

3.
Timed-Gromov–Hausdorff distance

Here we prove
that

is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the

d_{\tau-GH}
.
We also show that
d_{\tau-GH}
satisfies the triangle inequality, and establish a compactness theorem for

3.1.
Distance and time preserving maps into max-product spaces

Recall the notion of a max-product space from Definition
1.2
. The following lemma will be used to establish that
d_{\tau\text{-}GH}
satisfies the triangle inequality. Moreover, observe that this lemma implies that the class of functions considered in the definition of
d_{\tau\text{-}GH}
is larger than the class consisting of all functions
(\tau,\zeta):X\to[0,\tau_{\max}]\times Z
for which
\zeta
is distance-preserving.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(X,d,\tau)
be a compact timed-metric space,

\tau_{max}=\max\{\tau(x)\,|\,x\in X\}
,

Z
a compact metric space
and

(37)

\varphi:X\to[0,\tau_{max}]\times Z

a distance and time preserving map into the
max-product space,
[0,\tau_{max}]\times Z
.
Then
\varphi=(\tau,\zeta)
, where

\zeta:X\to Z
is
1
-Lipschitz. Moreover,
d_{Z}(\zeta(p),\zeta(q))=d(p,q)
for any
p,q\in X
non causally related.

Proof.

By the definition of a product space,
we know that
\varphi(x)=(\tau_{Z}(x),\zeta_{Z}(x))

where
\tau_{Z}:X\to[0,\tau_{max}]

and
\zeta_{Z}:X\to Z
. By the definition of
\tau_{prod}
and since

\varphi
is time preserving, we have

(38)

\tau_{Z}(x)=\tau_{prod}(\tau_{Z}(x),\zeta_{Z}(x))=\tau(x).

Moreover,

d_{Z}(\zeta_{Z}(x),\zeta_{Z}(y))\leq d_{prod}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))=d_{X}(x,y),

which proves that
\zeta_{Z}
is
1
-Lipschitz.

For the last claim, assume that
p,q\in X
are not causally related. This is equivalent, by the
1
-Lipschitzness of
\tau
, to

|\tau(p)-\tau(q)|<d(p,q).

Since
d(p,q)=d_{prod}(\varphi(p),\varphi(q))
, it follows that

d(p,q)=d_{Z}(\zeta_{Z}(p),\zeta_{Z}(q)).\qed

3.3.
Triangle inequality

We now prove that the timed-Gromov–Hausdorff distance
d_{\tau-GH}
, as in Definition

, satisfies the triangle inequality.

Theorem 3.4
.

Given three compact timed-metric spaces
(X_{i},d_{i},\tau_{i})
,
i=1,2,3
, we have the triangle
inequality

(54)

\begin{split}d_{\tau-GH}((X_{1},d_{1}&,\tau_{1}),(X_{3},d_{3},\tau_{3}))\leq\\
&d_{\tau-GH}((X_{1},d_{1},\tau_{1}),(X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2}))+d_{\tau-GH}((X_{2},d_{2},\tau_{2}),(X_{3},d_{3},\tau_{3})).\end{split}

Proof.

We will abuse notation and only write
X_{i}
instead of the tripe
(X_{i},d_{i},\tau_{i})
.
Let

(55)

D_{12}=d_{\tau-GH}(X_{1},X_{2})\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{23}=d_{\tau-GH}(X_{2},X_{3}),

\tau_{\max 12}=\max\{\max_{x\in X_{1}}\tau_{1}(x),\max_{x\in X_{2}}\tau_{2}(x)\}
,
and similarly define
\tau_{\max 23}
and
\tau_{\max 13}
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. Then there exist compact metric spaces
Z_{12},Z_{23}
and pairs of time and distance preserving maps,

(56)

\varphi_{j}:(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})\to([0,\tau_{\max 12}]\times Z_{12},d_{12},\tau_{12})\qquad j=1,2,

and

(57)

\phi_{j}:(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})\to([0,\tau_{\max 23}]\times Z_{23},d_{23},\tau_{23})\qquad j=2,3,

such that

(58)

d_{H}^{[0,\tau_{\max 12}]\times Z_{12}}(\varphi_{1}(X_{1}),\varphi_{2}(X_{2}))<D_{12}+\epsilon.

and

(59)

d_{H}^{[0,\tau_{\max 23}]\times Z_{23}}(\phi_{2}(X_{2}),\phi_{3}(X_{3}))<D_{23}+\epsilon.

By Lemma

3.1
, we know that
\varphi_{j}=(\tau_{j},\xi_{j})
,
j=1,2
,
and
\phi_{j}=(\tau_{j},\chi_{j})
,
j=2,3
.
We construct a compact metric space
Z
by gluing

and
Z_{23}
along

:

(60)

Z=Z_{12}\sqcup Z_{23}/\sim,\qquad\text{ where }\ z\sim z^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow{\exists x\in X_{2}\,s.t.\,z=\xi_{2}(x),\,z^{\prime}=\chi_{2}(x)},

with metric given by

(61)

\displaystyle d_{Z}(z,z^{\prime})=\begin{cases}d_{Z_{12}}(z,z^{\prime})&\quad z,z^{\prime}\in Z_{12}\\
d_{Z_{23}}(z,z^{\prime})&\quad z,z^{\prime}\in Z_{23}\\
\inf_{x\in X_{2}}\{d_{12}((0,z),\varphi_{2}(x))+d_{23}(\phi_{2}(x),(0,z^{\prime}))\}&\quad z\in Z_{12},\,z^{\prime}\in Z_{23}\\
d_{Z}(z^{\prime},z)&\quad z^{\prime}\in Z_{12},\,z\in Z_{23}.\end{cases}

The fact that

is symmetric and non-negative is immediate from its definition. To check

is definite, the only interesting case is when

and

are such that

, which by compactness of

and the definition of

implies there is some

such that

thus

d_{Z_{12}}(z,\xi_{2}(x))=d_{Z_{23}}(\chi_{2}(x),z^{\prime})=0,

i.e.
z\sim z^{\prime}
. For the triangle inequality, consider the case
z,z^{\prime}\in Z_{12}
and
z^{\prime\prime}\in Z_{23}
. Then, for any

we have:

\displaystyle d_{Z}(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=d_{12}((0,z),(0,z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq d_{12}((0,z),\varphi_{2}(x))+d_{12}(\varphi_{2}(x),\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime}))+d_{12}(\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime}),(0,z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=d_{12}((0,z),\varphi_{2}(x))+d_{23}(\phi_{2}(x),\phi_{2}(x^{\prime}))+d_{12}(\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime}),(0,z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq d_{12}((0,z),\varphi_{2}(x))+d_{23}(\phi_{2}(x),(0,z^{\prime\prime}))+d_{23}((0,z^{\prime\prime}),\phi_{2}(x^{\prime}))+d_{12}(\varphi_{2}(x^{\prime}),(0,z^{\prime}))

and by taking the infimum over

, we get

d_{Z}(z,z^{\prime})\leq d_{Z}(z,z^{\prime\prime})+d_{Z}(z^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime}).

On the other hand

\displaystyle d_{Z}(z,z^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\leq d_{12}((0,z),\varphi_{2}(x))+d_{23}(\phi_{2}(x),(0,z^{\prime\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq d_{12}((0,z),(0,z^{\prime}))+d_{12}((0,z^{\prime}),\varphi_{2}(x))+d_{23}(\phi_{2}(x),(0,z^{\prime\prime}))

\displaystyle=d_{Z}(z,z^{\prime})+d_{12}((0,z^{\prime}),\varphi_{2}(x))+d_{23}(\phi_{2}(x),(0,z^{\prime\prime})),

and by taking the infimum over

, we get

The rest of the cases are analogous or easier.

The maps

(63)

where

Since
\epsilon>0
was taken arbitrarily, this will establish the triangle inequality.

By proposition
2.8

there are correspondences

Similarly for

Let

This correspondence gives the claimed estimate. Indeed, for any

\displaystyle|\tau_{2}(x_{2})-\tau_{3}(x_{3})|<D_{23}+\epsilon.

By definition of

It follows that for all

4.
Understanding causality

In this section, we show that the causal structure for a timed-metric space introduced in Definition
1.6
defines a partial order, and study when this structure is preserved in the limit.

We start with a result that formalizes the fact that the causal structure for timed-metric spaces is a generalization of the standard causal structure of space-times.

Proposition 4.1
.

Given a timed-metric space
(X,d,\tau)
, the induced causal structure defines a partial order.

Proof.

For any
p\in X
it is clear that
p\in J^{+}(p)
, since

\tau(p)-\tau(p)=0=d(p,p).

On the other hand, if
p\in J^{+}(q)
and
q\in J^{+}(p)
then

\tau(p)-\tau(q)=d(q,p)=d(p,q)=\tau(q)-\tau(p),

which implies
d(p,q)=0
, i.e.
p=q
. Finally, if
p\in J^{+}(q)
and
q\in J^{+}(r)
, then

\tau(p)-\tau(q)=d(q,p)\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(q)-\tau(r)=d(r,q)

which by the triangle inequality yields

\tau(p)-\tau(r)=d(q,p)+d(r,q)\geq d(r,p)

Hence,
\tau(p)-\tau(r)\geq d(r,p)
. The reversed inequality
follows from the fact that
\tau
is
1
-Lipschitz. Hence,
p\in J^{+}(r)
. This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Example 4.2
.

Consider the timed-metric space
(X,d,\tau)
where
\tau=d(\cdot,p)
for some
p\in X
. In this example, the causal relation
y\in J^{+}(x)
is equivalent to
p
,
x
, and
y
being aligned, i.e.

(86)

d(y,p)=d(y,x)+d(x,p).

In particular, if
X
is non-branching and geodesic, the set
J^{+}(x)
, for
x\neq p
, is the image of the maximal geodesic starting at
x
that can be extended to
p
, whereas
J^{+}(p)=X
.

Remark 4.3
.

Observe that we could define chronological futures and pasts as

I^{\pm}(p):=\interior(J^{\pm}(p)).

With this definition, the relation
q\in I^{-}(p)
is transitive, and therefore, it could be used to define a chronological structure in
X
, which
agrees with the canonical chronological structure for Lipschitz space-times,
but for lower regularity, such as
C^{0}
,
the chronological structures may not coincide due to the existence of bubbling (see
[
13
]
).

Since we have a causal structure on timed-metric spaces, we can define causal curves.

Definition 4.4
.

Given a timed-metric space
(X,d,\tau)
,
we say that a non-constant continuous curve,
\gamma\colon I\to X
, is a
future-directed causal curve
if for any
s\leq t
in
I
,
\gamma(s)\in J^{-}(\gamma(t))
. Analogously, we define
past-directed causal curves. Causal curves are curves that are either future-directed or past-directed.

The following two propositions are respectively analogous to
[
19
, Corollary 3.19]
and to part of
[
19
, Lemma 3.20]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(X,d,\tau)
be a timed-metric space. Then any causal curve of
X
is a distance realizer between any of its points.

We recall that for a metric space
(X,d)
and points
x,y\in X
, a continuous curve
\gamma:[s,t]\to X
from

x
to
y
is a distance realizer if

d(x,y)=\sup_{s=s_{0}<\dots<s_{N}=t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}d(\gamma(s_{i-1}),\gamma(s_{i})).

Proof.

Let
\gamma\colon I\to X
be a causal curve, and without loss of generality, assume that
\gamma
is future-directed. Let
s<t
in
I
. Then for any
s=s_{0}<\dots<s_{N}=t
,

\gamma(s_{i-1})\in J^{-}(\gamma(s_{i}))

for
i=1,\dots,N
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}d(\gamma(s_{i-1}),\gamma(s_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau(\gamma(s_{i}))-\tau(\gamma(s_{i-1}))

\displaystyle=\tau(\gamma(t))-\tau(\gamma(s))=d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)).

Taking the supremum over all possible partitions of

[s,t]
, we get that

\gamma
is a distance realizer between
\gamma(s)
and
\gamma(t)
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(X,d,\tau)
be a timed-metric space. Let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to X
be a piecewise causal curve, i.e., there exists a partition
a=s_{0}<\dots<s_{N}=b
such that
\gamma|_{[s_{i-1},s_{i}]}
is causal for
i=1,\dots,N
. If
\gamma
is a distance realizer between any of its points then either
\gamma
is causal or for some subpartition
a=s_{0}^{\prime}<\dots s^{\prime}_{N^{\prime}}=b
of
a=s_{0}<\dots<s_{N}=b
the following holds:
\gamma|_{[s_{i-1}^{\prime},s_{i}^{\prime}]}
and
\gamma|_{[s_{i}^{\prime},s_{i+1}^{\prime}]}
have opposite causal orientation and no point in
\gamma|_{[s_{i-1}^{\prime},s_{i}^{\prime})}
is causally related to points in
\gamma|_{(s_{i}^{\prime},s_{i+1}^{\prime}]}
, for
i=1,\dots,N-1
.

Proof.

Assume that
\gamma
is not causal. Thus, without loss of generality, we can assume that for some
s_{i}
,
\gamma|_{[s_{i-1},s_{i}]}
is future-directed causal and
\gamma|_{[s_{i},s_{i+1}]}
is past-directed causal. Let
u\in[s_{i-1},s_{i})
and
v\in(s_{i},s_{i+1}]
. If
\gamma(u)\in J^{-}(\gamma(v))
then

\displaystyle d(\gamma(u),\gamma(v))

\displaystyle=\tau(\gamma(v))-\tau(\gamma(u))

\displaystyle<\tau(\gamma(s_{i}))-\tau(\gamma(u))+\tau(\gamma(s_{i}))-\tau(\gamma(v))

\displaystyle=d(\gamma(u),\gamma(v)),

where the strict inequality follows from the fact that
\gamma|_{[s_{i},s_{i+1}]}
is non-constant and past-directed, therefore
\tau(\gamma(s_{i}))-\tau(\gamma(v))=d(\gamma(v),\gamma(s_{i}))>0
. This is a contradiction, and we get an analogous contradiction assuming
\gamma(u)\in J^{+}(\gamma(v))
. Therefore,
\gamma(u)
and
\gamma(v)
are not causally related. By letting
a=s^{\prime}_{0}<\dots<s^{\prime}_{N^{\prime}}=b
be the ordered values of
s_{i}
such that
\gamma
changes causal orientation, the result follows.
∎

5.
Causally-null timed-metric spaces

We now introduce the null distance on a timed-metric space, based on the notion of null distance on a Lorentzian manifold introduced by Sormani–Vega

[

, and also introduce the notion of causally-null timed-metric space based on the smooth notion given by Sakovich–Sormani

Definition 5.1
.

Given a timed-metric space
(X,d,\tau)
,
we define
\hat{d}_{d,\tau}:X\times X\to[0,\infty]

(107)

\hat{d}_{d,\tau}(p,q)=\inf\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\tau(p_{i})-\tau(p_{i-1})|

are causally related, possibly alternating past

\hat{d}_{d,\tau}(p,q)=\infty

the associated

of

, although it is an extended distance in general. If

is

1.9

First we prove that

is an extended distance. It is clear that

Now take

∎

The following example shows that, in general, we should not expect the property of being causally null to be stable under intrinsic timed-Hausdorff, nor timed Gromov–Hausdorff, convergence.

Example 5.4
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X_{j}
be set

(118)

X_{j}=\left\{\left(\frac{k}{j},0\right)\right\}_{k=0}^{j}\cup\left\{\left(\frac{2k-1}{2j},0\right)\right\}_{k=1}^{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

endowed with the metric

(119)

d_{j}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))=|x-x^{\prime}|

and the time function

(120)

\tau_{j}(x,y)=y.

It is easy to verify that
(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})
is causally null and that
(X_{j},d_{j},\tau_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau-\textrm{H}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(X,d,\tau)
where

(121)

X=[0,1],\quad d(x,x^{\prime})=|x-x^{\prime}|,\quad\tau(x)=0.

However, the null distance in
X
is infinite, since for any
x\neq x^{\prime}
,
x,x^{\prime}\in X
there is no finite (not even a countably infinite) chain of causally related points connecting
x
to
x^{\prime}
. Therefore,
X
is not causally null.

Remark 5.5
.

One may argue that the previous counterexample is pathological because the spaces
X_{j}
are discrete, or because the time function in the limit is identically zero. However, it is possible to construct counterexamples where the sequence consists of (regular) space-times. This will be done in the upcoming work
[
16
]
.

We conclude this section by studying the relationship between
null distances that encode causality in the sense of
[
17
]
and our definition of causally-null timed-metric spaces.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
N
be a space-time and consider a time function
\tau\colon N\to[0,\infty)
whose null-distance encodes causality in the sense of
[
17
]
and defines a timed-metric space
(N,\hat{d}_{\tau},\tau)
. Then
(N,\hat{d}_{\tau},\tau)
is causally null in the sense of Definition
5.1
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{d}_{\tau}
be the null distance on
N
associated to the time function
\tau
. The fact that
\hat{d}_{\tau}
encodes causality means that
x\leq_{N}y
, where
\leq_{N}
denotes the causal order in
N
, if and only if

\tau(y)-\tau(x)=\hat{d}_{\tau}(x,y).

However, this
coincides with the definition of causality
for the timed-metric space
(N,\hat{d}_{\tau},\tau)
, see (
9
). Therefore,

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{\hat{d}_{\tau},\tau}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\tau(p_{i})-\tau(p_{i-1})|:x=p_{0},\dots,p_{N}=y,\ p_{i}\in J^{\pm}(p_{i-1})\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\tau(p_{i})-\tau(p_{i-1})|:x=p_{0},\dots,p_{N}=y,\ p_{i}\leq_{N}p_{i-1}\ \text{or}\ p_{i-1}\leq_{N}p_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\hat{d}_{\tau}(x,y),

for any
x,y\in N
, and the claim follows.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

If
N
is a path-connected space-time and

\tau\colon N\to\mathbb{R}
is a generalized proper time function which is locally anti-Lipschitz in the sense of
[
7
]
, then,
by
[
17
]
,
(N,\hat{d}_{\tau},\tau)
is a timed-metric space and
\hat{d}_{\tau}
encodes causality. In particular,
(N,\hat{d}_{\tau},\tau)
is a causally-null timed-metric space in the sense of Definition
5.1
.

Example 5.8
.

Observe that, in general, Proposition
5.6
does not imply that the completion of
(N,\hat{d}_{\tau},\tau)
is causally null in the sense of Definition
5.1
, not even when
N
is a causally-null compactifiable space-time, in the sense of
[
18
]
, and
\tau
is the cosmological time function, as was pointed out to us by Omar Zoghlami.

Indeed, let
N
be the space-time consisting of the interior of the triangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with vertices
(0,0)
,
(1,0)
and
(0.75,0.25)
, endowed with the restriction of the Minkowski metric (where the second coordinate has negative signature). This space-time is clearly causally-null compactifiable, with
\left(\bar{N},\bar{\hat{d}}\right)
isometric to the corresponding closed triangle. However, under this isometry, one can see that the point
(0,0)\in\bar{N}
is not causally related to any other point in
\bar{N}
, in the sense of Definition
9
, which implies
\hat{d}_{\bar{d},\bar{\tau}}((0,0),(x,y))=\infty\neq\bar{d}((0,0),(x,y))
for any

, where
\bar{\tau}
and
\bar{d}
are the canonical extensions of the cosmological time function and the associated null distance in
N
to
\bar{N}
.

Remark 5.9

Conditions granting that the metric completion of a causally-null timed-metric space is also causally null will be addressed in the upcoming work

Appendix A
Gromov’s compactness theorem

Here we reprove Gromov’s Compactness Criterion from
[
10
]
adding as many details as possible. Note that as a straightforward application, we gave the proof of our compactness theorem in Section
3
.

Theorem A.1
.

If
(X_{j},d_{j})
is a sequence of compact timed-metric spaces that is equibounded and equicompact, as in (
24
) and (
25
)
Then there exists a subsequence of the
(X_{j},d_{j})
that converges in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense to a compact metric space
(Y_{\infty},d_{\infty})
. In fact, there exist compact
F
and
Y_{\infty}
, distance preserving maps, up to a subsequence that we do not relabel,

h_{j}:X_{j}\to F\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{N},

and
h_{\infty}:Y_{\infty}\to F
distance preserving,
such that

(122)

d_{H}^{F}(h_{j}(X_{j}),h_{\infty}(Y_{\infty}))\to 0.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
2.10

to obtain a
countable index set
A=\sqcup A_{i}
, and
a countable dense collection of points,

(123)

I^{j}(A)=\{x^{j}_{a}=I^{j}(a):\,a\in A\}\subset X_{j}\qquad j\in\mathbb{N},

that satisfy (
31
) and (
32
).

Let

(124)

F^{\prime}=\Bigl\{f:A\to\mathbb{R}\,:f\,\text{bounded}\Bigr\}

endowed with the sup norm,

\|f\|=\sup_{a\in A}|f(a)|

(125)

where conditions (
126
) and (
127
) are given by

(126)

f(A_{1})\subset[0,D]

and

(127)

|f(a)-f(p_{i}(a))|\leq 2\varepsilon_{i}\,\,\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\,\forall a\in A_{i+1}.

We first claim that for each
x\in X_{j}
, the map
(h_{j}(x)):A\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(128)

(h_{j}(x))(a)=d_{j}(x,I^{j}_{i}(a)),\qquad\forall a_{i}\in A_{i}\subset A

is contained in
F
.
By the equiboundedness condition, (
24
),
it follows that
(h_{j}(x))\leq D
.
Thus,
(h_{j}(x))\in F^{\prime}
, and in particular,
(h_{j}(x))
satisfies (
126

Now, by (
32

I^{j}_{i+1}(a)\in B_{d_{j}}(I^{j}_{i}(p_{i}(a)),2\varepsilon_{i})\subset X_{j},

Secondly, we claim that the maps

Now we claim that

For
