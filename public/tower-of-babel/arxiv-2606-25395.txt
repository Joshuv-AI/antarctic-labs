Wold-type decomposition and Beurling-Type Theorem for Covariant Representations
Dimple Saini, Azad Rohilla *

Abstract
. Using operator inequalities, we study a Wold-type decomposition of covariant representations. Building on this decomposition, we prove a Beurling-type theorem showing that every nonzero invariant subspace is uniquely determined by its wandering subspace. Our results extend classical theorems of Beurling and subsequent developments for left-invertible operators to the setting of covariant representations of C ∗ C^{*} -correspondences, providing a unified framework for invariant subspace theory under operator inequalities.

1.
Introduction

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space and
B(\mathcal{H})
denote the algebra of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
T\in B(\mathcal{H})
, we write
R(T)
and
N(T)
for the range space and kernel space of
T
, respectively. A closed subspace
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is called
T
-invariant if
T(\mathcal{K})\subseteq\mathcal{K}
. For a given subspace
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{H}
, we define
[\mathcal{K}]_{T}:=\bigvee_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}\mathcal{K},

which represents the smallest closed
T
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}
that contains
\mathcal{K}
. The operator
T
is said to be pure whenever
\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}\mathcal{H}=\{0\}.
We say that
T
has the wandering subspace property if
\mathcal{H}=[\mathcal{H}\ominus T\mathcal{H}]_{T}.
Moreover,
T
is called contractive if
\|Th\|\leq\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and expansive if
\|Th\|\geq\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. In particular, every expansive operator is left invertible and admits a contractive left inverse.

Understanding the structure of operators through representations, classifications, and invariant subspaces is a central theme in operator theory on Hilbert spaces. A classical result in this direction is due to Wold
[
18
]
, who showed that any isometry on a Hilbert space can be described either as a shift, as a unitary operator, or as a unique orthogonal sum of these two types. Another fundamental contribution is Beurling’s theorem
[
1
]
, which characterizes invariant subspaces of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
over the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
: every closed
M_{z}
-invariant subspace is of the form
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for some inner function
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
. This result was later extended by Halmos
[
5
]
in the form of the wandering subspace theorem.

Richter
[
12
]
investigated a class of operators known as concave operators and established a corresponding wandering subspace theorem. An operator
T\in B(\mathcal{H})
is called concave if it satisfies
\|T^{2}x\|^{2}+\|x\|^{2}\leq 2\|Tx\|^{2}
for every
x\in\mathcal{H}.
Subsequently, Shimorin
[
15
]
obtained a Wold-type decomposition for concave operators and presented a simpler proof of Richter’s result. Later, Olofsson
[
9
]
introduced a growth condition for expansive operators and generalized Richter’s theorem as stated below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T\in B(\mathcal{H})
and satisfying:

(i)

T
is pure and expansive.

(ii)

there exist positive constants
d
and
d_{m}
with
\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{1}{d_{m}}=\infty
and

d_{m}(\|Th\|^{2}-\|h\|^{2})+d\|h\|^{2}\geq\|T^{m}h\|^{2},\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

Then
T
possesses the wandering subspace property.

Sun and Zheng
[
16
]
later revisited Beurling-type results by deriving new identities in the Bergman space. Building on these ideas, Izuchi, Izuchi, and Izuchi
[
6
]
established the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T\in B(\mathcal{H})
and satisfying:

(1)

\|Th\|^{2}+\|T^{*2}Th\|^{2}\leq 2\|T^{*}Th\|^{2}
for
h\in\mathcal{H}.

(2)

T
is bounded below.

(3)

T
is contractive.

(4)

\|T^{*n}h\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for
h\in\mathcal{H}.

Then
T
has the wandering subspace property.

Within the framework of noncommutative multivariable operator theory, Cuntz
[
2
]
introduced a
C^{*}
-algebra generated by a row isometry, that is, a tuple
(T_{1},\dots,T_{n})
of isometries on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
whose ranges are mutually orthogonal, equivalently satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}T_{i}^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
. Later, Frazho
[
4
]
proved a Wold decomposition for two such isometries, and Popescu
[
11
]
extended this result to infinite families of isometries with orthogonal ranges. Pimsner
[
10
]
further broadened this framework by constructing Cuntz–Krieger type algebras via isometric covariant representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences. Subsequently, Muhly and Solel
[
8
]
obtained an analogue of Popescu’s Wold decomposition for row isometries in the context of isometric covariant representations, using the Fock module approach.

Operator theory and function theory are closely intertwined fields that have developed through numerous interactions with other areas of mathematics. While classical questions remain influential, many recent advances involve extending foundational results to broader and more abstract settings. One such direction is the study of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules through
C^{*}
-correspondences (over a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
) and their covariant representations. In the special case where
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}
and the correspondence is
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the concept of an isometric covariant representation reduces to that of a row isometry. Consequently, working in this more general framework requires the development of new methods and techniques.

Covariant representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences appear naturally in the study of operator algebras, noncommutative dynamical systems, and dilation theory. They provide a flexible framework for analyzing operator tuples and non-selfadjoint operator algebras associated with correspondences. The main objective of this paper is to extend Beurling-type invariant subspace results to the setting of covariant representations by employing a structural method based on operator inequalities. Using these inequalities, we establish a theory of wandering subspaces for covariant representations.

The results obtained here generalize several classical theorems concerning weighted shifts and operators that are close to isometries to the broader context of covariant representations associated with
C^{*}
-correspondences. In addition, the approach presented in this work offers a unified perspective for investigating invariant subspaces in noncommutative environments where traditional techniques are not directly applicable. This viewpoint highlights the importance of operator inequalities as a substitute for strict isometric assumptions and suggests further directions in multivariable and non-selfadjoint operator theory.

2.1.
C^{*}
-Correspondences

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra. A
C^{*}
-correspondence
over
\mathcal{A}
is a right Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module
E
equipped with a nondegenerate
*
-homomorphism

\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(E),

where
\mathcal{L}(E)
is the
C^{*}
-algebra of adjointable operators on
E
. The left module action of
\mathcal{A}
on
E
is given by
\varphi(a)\xi
, often written simply as
a\cdot\xi
.

2.2.
Covariant Representations

Suppose that
E
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A}
. A
covariant representation
of
E
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
consists of a
*
-representation

\sigma:\mathcal{A}\to B(\mathcal{H})

and a linear map

T:E\to B(\mathcal{H})

satisfying the bimodule property

T(c\cdot x\cdot d)=\sigma(c)\,T(x)\,\sigma(d)\quad\text{for all }c,d\in\mathcal{A},x\in E.

Then we say that
(\sigma,T)
is a
covariant representation
. If
T
is completely bounded (resp. completely contractive), then the pair
(\sigma,T)
is called completely bounded covariant representation (resp. completely contractive covariant representation). For simplicity we used c.b.c-representation instead of completely bounded covariant representation.

The following result is due to Muhly and Solel
[
7
]
which is useful to classify the c.b.c. representations of a
C^{*}
-correspondence:

Lemma 2.1
.

The mapping
(\sigma,T)\mapsto\widetilde{T}
establishes a one-to-one correspondence between all c.b.c. (respectively, completely contractive covariant) representations
(\sigma,T)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
and all bounded (respectively, contractive) linear operators
\widetilde{T}:E\otimes_{\sigma}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
defined by

\widetilde{T}(x\otimes h)=T(x)h\quad\quad(h\in\mathcal{H},x\in E),

with
\widetilde{T}(\phi(c)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\sigma(c)\widetilde{T}
for every
c\in\mathcal{A}
. Furthermore,
\widetilde{T}
is an isometry if and only if the representation
(\sigma,T)
is isometric.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\sigma,T)
be a c.b.c-representation of
{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
A closed subspace
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{H}
is called
(\sigma,T)
-
invariant

(resp.(\sigma,T)
-
reducing
) (cf.
[
17
]
) if it is
\sigma(\mathcal{A})
-invariant and (resp. both
\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
) is invariant by each operator
T(\xi)
for all
\xi\in E.
The restriction
(\sigma,T)|_{\mathcal{K}}
gives a new representation of
E
on
\mathcal{K}.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}(=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})
,

E^{\otimes m}=E\otimes_{\phi}\dots\otimes_{\phi}E
(
m
-times) (here
E^{\otimes 0}=\mathcal{A}
) is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A}
, with the left module action of
\mathcal{A}
on
E^{\otimes m}
defined as

\phi_{m}(c)(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{n})=cx_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{m},\>\>\>\>c\in\mathcal{A},x_{i}\in E.

For
m\in\mathbb{N},
define
\widetilde{T}_{m}:E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by

\widetilde{T}_{m}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{m}\otimes h)=V(x_{1})\dots V(x_{m})h,\quad x_{i}\in E,h\in\mathcal{H}.

The
Fock module
of
E
,
\mathcal{F}(E)=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}E^{\otimes m},
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A},
where the left module action of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{F}(E)
is defined by

\phi_{\infty}(c)\left(\oplus_{m\geq 0}x_{m}\right)=\oplus_{m\geq 0}\phi_{m}(c)x_{m},\>\>x_{m}\in E^{\otimes m},c\in\mathcal{A}.

For
\xi\in E,
the
creation operator

V_{\xi}
on
\mathcal{F}(E)
is defined by

V_{\xi}(x)=\xi\otimes x,\>\>x\in E^{\otimes m}.

Assume
(\sigma,T)
to be a c.b.c-representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
with closed range. The
Moore-Penrose inverse

\widetilde{T}^{\dagger}
of
(\sigma,T)
is defined by

(2.1)

\widetilde{T}^{\dagger}:=\widetilde{T}_{0}^{-1}P_{R(\widetilde{T})},

where
\widetilde{T}_{0}=\widetilde{T}|_{N(\widetilde{T})^{\perp}}:{N(\widetilde{T})^{\perp}}\to R(\widetilde{T})
and
P_{R(\widetilde{T})}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
{R(\widetilde{T})}.
Equivalently, the Moore-Penrose inverse of
(\sigma,T)
can be characterized as the unique operator satisfying the following four equations:

\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{\dagger}\widetilde{T}=\widetilde{T},\quad\widetilde{T}^{\dagger}\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{\dagger}=\widetilde{T}^{\dagger},\quad(\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{\dagger})^{*}=\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{\dagger},\quad(\widetilde{T}^{\dagger}\widetilde{T})^{*}=\widetilde{T}^{\dagger}\widetilde{T}.

Notation 2.3
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N},
define
\widetilde{T}^{\dagger(m)}:\mathcal{H}\to E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{H}
by

\widetilde{T}^{\dagger(m)}:=(I_{E^{\otimes m-1}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{\dagger})(I_{E^{\otimes m-2}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{\dagger})\dots(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{\dagger})\widetilde{T}^{\dagger}.

.

[

Assume
(\sigma,T)

Then the representation

3.
Wandering Subspaces for Covariant Representations

The notion of a wandering subspace plays a central role in classical invariant subspace theory. In the setting of isometries, wandering subspaces provide a complete description of invariant subspaces via Beurling’s theorem
[
1
]
. This idea was later extended to weighted shifts and operators satisfying suitable operator inequalities
[
6
,
15
]
. In this section, we introduce and analyze wandering subspaces for covariant representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences.

Definition 3.1
.

A closed subspace
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is said to be a
wandering subspace
for
(\sigma,T)
if
\mathcal{W}\perp\widetilde{T}_{m}(E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{W})
for
m\geq 1
. The wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}
is called
generating
(or
(\sigma,T)
is said to have the GWS-property) if

\mathcal{H}=[\mathcal{W}]_{\widetilde{T}}:=\bigvee_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{T}_{m}(E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{W}).

The following result from
[
17
, Theorem 3.13]
is a generalization of Shimorin’s Wold-type decomposition
[
15
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
(\sigma,T)
to be a c.b.c-representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H},
which satisfies any one of the following conditions:

(a)

\|\widetilde{T}_{2}\zeta\|^{2}+\|\zeta\|^{2}\leq 2\|(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{T})\zeta\|^{2},\quad\quad\zeta\in E^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{H},
that is,
(\sigma,T)
is concave.

(b)

\|(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{T})\zeta+\eta\|^{2}\leq 2(\|\zeta\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{T}\eta\|^{2}),\quad\zeta\in E^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{H},\eta\in E\otimes\mathcal{H}.

Then
(\sigma,T)
admits Wold-type decomposition. That is, there exists a wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}
of
(\sigma,T)
which uniquely decomposes
\mathcal{H}
into the direct sum of two
(\sigma,T)
-reducing subspaces

\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{T}_{m}(E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{W})\bigoplus\bigcap_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{T}_{m}(E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{H}),

such that
(\sigma,T)|_{\bigcap_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{T}_{m}(E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{H})}
is isometric as well as co-isometric representation.
In particular, if
(\sigma,T)
is analytic (that is,
{\bigcap_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{T}_{m}(E^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{H})}=\{0\}
), then
(\sigma,T)
has GWS-property.

The following theorem is a generalization of
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
(\sigma,T)
to be a c.b.c-representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
with satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\|\widetilde{T}\eta\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{T}_{2}^{*}\widetilde{T}\eta\|^{2}\leq 2\|\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T}\eta\|^{2},\quad\eta\in E\otimes\mathcal{H}.

(2)

(\sigma,T)
is bounded below.

(3)

(\sigma,T)
is completely contractive covariant representation.

(4)

\|\widetilde{T}^{*}_{n}h\|\to 0,
as
n\to\infty
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}.

Then
(\sigma,T)
has GWS-property.

Proof.

Suppose that
M=\mathcal{H}\ominus[\mathcal{H}\ominus\widetilde{T}(E\otimes\mathcal{H})]_{\widetilde{T}},
we want to prove
M=\{0\}.
Let
h\in M,
then
h\perp\widetilde{T}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes(\mathcal{H}\ominus\widetilde{T}(E\otimes\mathcal{H})))
for all
n\geq 0.
Since
R(\widetilde{T})
is closed,
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}h\in E^{\otimes n}\otimes R(\widetilde{T}),
then there exists
\eta_{n+1}\in E^{\otimes n+1}\otimes\mathcal{H}
such that
\widetilde{T}^{*}_{n}h=(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T})\eta_{n+1}.
If
\widetilde{T}
is contractive, then
\|\widetilde{T}_{n+1}^{*}h\|\leq\|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}h\|.
From Equation
1
, it is easy to verify that

\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T})x\|^{2}-2\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T})x\|^{2}+\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{*}_{2}\widetilde{T})x\|^{2}\leq 0,

for all
x\in E^{\otimes n+1}\otimes\mathcal{H}.

Let
c_{n}=\|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}h\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{T}_{n+1}^{*}h\|^{2},
then

\displaystyle c_{n}-c_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}h\|^{2}-2\|\widetilde{T}_{n+1}^{*}h\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{T}_{n+2}^{*}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T})\eta_{n+1}\|^{2}-2\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T})\eta_{n+1}\|^{2}+\|(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{T}^{*}_{2}\widetilde{T})\eta_{n+1}\|^{2}\leq 0.

This implies that
0\leq c_{n}\leq c_{n+1},
from Equation
4
we get

c_{n}=\|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}h\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{T}_{n+1}^{*}h\|^{2}\to 0~~~~\mbox{as}~~n\to\infty.

Thus
c_{n}=0
for all
n\geq 0.
Observe that
\|h\|^{2}=\|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}h\|^{2}
for all
n\geq 0.
Again using Equation
4
, we obtain
h=0.
Therefore
\mathcal{H}=[\mathcal{H}\ominus\widetilde{T}(E\otimes\mathcal{H})]_{\widetilde{T}}.

∎

Now we are study a relation between the conditions of Theorem
(b)
and Theorem
3.3
.

Remark 3.4
.

Assume
(\sigma,T)
to be a c.b.c-representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H},
then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\|(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{T})\zeta+\eta\|^{2}\leq 2(\|\zeta\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{T}\eta\|^{2}),\quad\zeta\in E^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{H},\eta\in E\otimes\mathcal{H}.

(ii)

(\sigma,T)
is bounded below and
(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{*})+(\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T})^{-1}\leq 2I_{E\otimes\mathcal{H}}.

(iii)

(\sigma,T)
is bounded below and
\|\widetilde{T}\eta\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{T}_{2}^{*}\widetilde{T}\eta\|^{2}\leq 2\|\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T}\eta\|^{2},\quad\eta\in E\otimes\mathcal{H}.

Proof.

From
[
17
, Theorem 3.13]
we get
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii).

Let
(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{*})+(\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T})^{-1}\leq 2I_{E\otimes\mathcal{H}}
if and only if

\langle(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{T}\widetilde{T}^{*})z,z\rangle+\langle(\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T})^{-1}z,z\rangle\leq 2\|z\|^{2}~~~\mbox{for all}\quad z\in E\otimes\mathcal{H}.

Since
\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T}
is invertible, substituting
(\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T})^{-1}z=y,
then the above inequality is equivalent to

\|\widetilde{T}y\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{T}_{2}^{*}\widetilde{T}y\|^{2}\leq 2\|\widetilde{T}^{*}\widetilde{T}y\|^{2},\quad\quad y\in E\otimes\mathcal{H}.

This implies that
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii).

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(\sigma,T)
be a c.b.c-representation of

on
\mathcal{H}
which satisfies any one condition of

3.4
, then
(\sigma,T)
admits Wold-type decomposition.

(\sigma,T)

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume
(\sigma,T)
to be a bounded below covariant representation of

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume
(\sigma,T)
to be a c.b.c-representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}.
If
(\sigma,T)
satisfies the conditions

and

(\sigma,T)

Proof.

From Remark
3.4

(\inf_{m\geq 0}w_{i,m})\|x\|\leq\|V_{i}x\|\leq(\sup_{m\geq 0}w_{i,m})\|x\|\quad for\quad i\in I_{n}.

We know that
V_{i}
is bounded below if and only if
0<\inf_{m\geq 0}w_{i,m}\leq\sup_{m\geq 0}w_{i,m}<\infty
for
i\in I_{n}.
By definition of the Moore-Penrose inverse of
V_{i},

V_{i}^{\dagger}(e_{j})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{w_{i,\frac{j-i}{n}}}e_{\frac{j-i}{n}}&\text{if }{j\in B_{i}};\\
0&\text{if }{j\notin B_{i}}.\end{cases}

Lemma 5.1
.

For
x=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}e_{m},
we get

(1)

\|V_{i}V_{i}^{\dagger}x\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in B_{i}}|a_{j}|^{2},

(2)

\|V_{i}^{*}x\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in B_{i}}|a_{j}|^{2}w_{i,\frac{j-i}{n}}^{2},

(3)

\|V_{i}^{*2}x\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in C_{i}}|a_{j}|^{2}w_{i,\frac{j-i}{n}}^{2}w_{i,\frac{j-ni-i}{n^{2}}}^{2}.

If
(\rho,S^{w})
satisfies Equation
(1)
of
Remark
4.5
if and only if

\sum_{j\in C_{i}}|a_{j}|^{2}w_{i,\frac{j-i}{n}}^{2}w_{i,\frac{j-ni-i}{n^{2}}}^{2}+\sum_{j\in B_{i}}|a_{j}|^{2}\leq 2\sum_{j\in B_{i}}|a_{j}|^{2}w_{i,\frac{j-i}{n}}^{2}~~~~~\mbox{for every}~~~i\in I_{n},

it is also equivalent to

(5.1)

\begin{cases}|a_{j}|^{2}(w_{i,\frac{j-i}{n}}^{2}w_{i,\frac{j-ni-i}{n^{2}}}^{2}+1-2w_{i,\frac{j-i}{n}}^{2})\leq 0~~~~~~~~\mbox{for all}~~~{j\in C_{i}},i\in I_{n}\\
|a_{j}|^{2}(1-2w_{i,\frac{j-i}{n}}^{2})\leq 0~~~~~~~~\mbox{for all}~~~{j\in B_{i}\setminus C_{i}},i\in I_{n}\end{cases}.

From Corollary

and
