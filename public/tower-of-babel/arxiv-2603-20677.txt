Nuclear weighted conditional expectation operators
A. Ommi and Y. Estaremi

Abstract
. We provide a characterisations of nuclear weighted conditional expectation operators between different L p ​ ( μ ) L^{p}(\mu) -spaces. As a consequence, when the underlying measure space is non-atomic, the only nuclear weighted conditional expectation operator between different L p ​ ( μ ) L^{p}(\mu) -spaces is the zero operator.



1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a complete
\sigma
-finite measure space. All sets and functions statements are to be interpreted as holding up to sets of measure zero.
For a
\sigma
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
of
\Sigma
, the conditional expectation operator associated with
\mathcal{A}
is the mapping
f\rightarrow E^{\mathcal{A}}f,
defined for all non-negative
f
as well as for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F})=L^{2}(X,\mathcal{F},\mu)
, where
E^{\mathcal{A}}f
is the unique
\mathcal{A}
-measurable function satisfying

\int_{A}(E^{\mathcal{A}}f)d\mu=\int_{A}fd\mu\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall A\in\mathcal{A}.

We will often write
E
for
E^{\mathcal{A}}
. This operator will play a major role in our
work.
We now list fundamental prperties of conditional expectation operator
E
when considered on the space
L^{p}(\mu)
. For any
f,g\in L^{p}(\mu)
the following holds :

(1)

If
g
is
\mathcal{A}
-measurable, then
E(fg)=E(f)g
.

(2)

|E(f)|^{p}\leq E(|f|^{p})
.

(3)

If
f\geq 0
, then
E(f)\geq 0
; if
f>0
, then
E(f)>0
.

(4)

|E(fg)|\leq E(|f|^{p})^{1/p}E(|g|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
(Hölder inequality).

(5)

For each
f\geq 0
,
S(E(f))
is the smallest
\mathcal{A}
-set containing
S(f)
, where
S(f)=\{x\in X:f(x)\neq 0\}
.

For a comprehensive treatment of these properties, we refer the reader to
[
16
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

An element
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mu(A)>0
is called an
\mathcal{A}
-atom of the measure
\mu
if, for each
F\in\mathcal{A}
with
F\subseteq A
, either
\mu(F)=0
or
\mu(F)=\mu(A)
. A measure space
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
that contains no atoms is said to be non-atomic measure space.

It is a standard fact that every
\sigma
-finite measure space
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu|_{\mathcal{A}})
can be uniquely written as a disjoint union

X=\left(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i}\right)\cup B,

where each
A_{i}
is an atom of
\mu
and
B
is non-atomic measurable set(i.e,
B
contains no atom of positive measure) (see
[
19
]
).

More over if the
\sigma
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
coinsides with the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the sequence of measurable sets
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
with positive measures, then for every
f\in\mathcal{D}(E)
,the conditional expection admits the explicit representation

E(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(A_{i})}\int_{A_{i}}f\,d\mu\right)\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}.

Definition 1.2

(Weighted Conditional Expectation (WCE) Operator)
.

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\sigma
-subalgebra of
\Sigma
, such that
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
is also
\sigma
-finite. The corresponding conditional expectation operator relative to
\mathcal{A}
is defined by
E:=E^{\mathcal{A}}(f):L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{p}(\mathcal{A})
where
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Given measurable functions
w,u\in L^{0}(\mu)
, assume that for every
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
the product
uf
is conditionable. The weighted conditional expectation (WCE) operator with the weights
w
and
u
is the linear map
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{0}(\mu)
defined by
T(f)=w\cdot E(uf)
.

Our interest in operators of the form of WCE operators stems from the fact that such products tend to appear often
in the study of those operators related to conditional expectation. WCE operators appear in
[
1
]
, where it is shown that every contractive projection on certain
L^{1}
-spaces can be
decomposed into an operator of the form
M_{w}EM_{u}
and a nilpotent operator. In
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
operators that are
representable as products involving multiplications and conditional expectations are studied. Also, in [9],
S.T.C. Moy has characterized all operators on
L^{p}
-spaces of the form
EM_{u}
and
M_{w}EM_{u}
. Some classical properties
of the operator
EM_{u}
on
L^{p}
-spaces are characterized in
[
12
]
and [8]. The authors have characterized
boundedness of
T
between two different
L^{p}
spaces, polar decomposition and some other classical properties of
T

on
L^{2}(\Sigma)
in
[
8
]
. Compactness of WCE operators between different
L^{p}
spaces is characterized in
[
9
]
. In this paper we investigate the class of Nuclear WCE operators from
L^{p}
into
L^{q}
-spaces in cases
p>q
and
p<q
.

2.
Main Results

In this section first we recall the definition of nuclear and absolutely summing operators. Also, we recall some facts about nuclear operators that will be used in our main results.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and
X^{*}
be the dual of
X
.
A bounded operator
T:X\to Y
is
nuclear
if it admits a representation

(2.1)

Tx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\,y_{n},\qquad x\in X,

for some
(f_{n})\subset X^{*}
and
(y_{n})\subset Y
with

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{X^{*}}\,\|y_{n}\|_{Y}<\infty
.
The
nuclear norm
is

\|T\|_{N}:=\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{X^{*}}\,\|y_{n}\|_{Y}:\ T=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}\otimes y_{n}\right\},

where
(f\otimes y)(x):=f(x)\,y
. We refer to
[
15
,
3
]
for background on nuclear and summing operators,
including the ideal properties and standard consequences used implicitly below.

An operator
T:X\to Y
is
absolutely
1
–summing
if there exists
M>0
such that for every finite family

x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in X
,

(2.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|T(x_{i})\|\leq M\,\sup_{f\in X^{*},\,\|f\|\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f(x_{i})|.

The least such
M
is denoted by
\pi_{1}(T)
. Using the weak
\ell_{1}
–norm identification, (
2.2
) is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|T(x_{i})\|\leq M\,\sup_{|\alpha_{i}|=1}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\right\|.

In the following we recall the Pietsch’s domination theorem that will be used in the proof of our main results.

Theorem 2.1

(Pietsch domination theorem
[
3
, Theorem 2.12]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
T:X\to Y
be absolutely
p
–summing.
Then there exist
C>0
and a Borel probability measure
\mu
on the weak
∗
–compact unit ball
B_{X^{*}}
such that

\|Tx\|\leq C\left(\int_{B_{X^{*}}}|x^{*}(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(x^{*})\right)^{1/p},\qquad x\in X.

In particular, for
p=1
,

\|Tx\|\leq C\int_{B_{X^{*}}}|x^{*}(x)|\,d\mu(x^{*}).

Here we recall the characterizations of compactness of WCE operators between different
L^{p}
-spaces
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
. Then the followings hold:

•

If
1<q<p<\infty
and let
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
be conjugate component to
p
and
q
, respectively, then the WCE operator
T
is compact if and only if

(1)

(E|w|^{q})^{1/q}(E|u|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}=0
a.e. on
B

(2)

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(E(|w|^{q}))^{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}/(q^{\prime}-p^{\prime})}(A_{i})(E(|u|^{p}))^{q^{\prime}/(q^{\prime}-p^{\prime})}(A_{i})\mu(A_{i})<\infty
.

•

If
1<p<q<\infty
, then
T
is compact if and only if

(1)

(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}(E(|w|^{q}))^{1/q}=0)
a.e. on
B

(2)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i})(E(|w|^{q}))^{p^{\prime}/q}(A_{i})}{\mu(A_{i})^{(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})/q^{\prime}}}=0
, when the number of
\mathcal{A}
-atoms is not finite.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{1}(\Sigma)
. Then the followings hold:

•

For
1<p<\infty
, the WCE operator
T:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{1}(\Sigma)
is compact if and only if
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i})(E(|w|))^{p^{\prime}}(A_{i})\mu(A_{i})<\infty
and
E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}E(|w|)=0
a.e. on
B
.

•

Let
(X,A,\mu)
be a non-atomic measure space. Since for each
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
,
\sigma(w)=\sigma(|w|^{\alpha})\subseteq\sigma(E(|w|^{\alpha}))=\sigma((E(|w|^{\alpha}))^{\beta})
, then by the previous results, the WCE operator
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
with
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q<\infty
is compact if and only if it is a zero operator.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{1}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
. Then the followings hold:
Let
1<q<\infty
and let
X=(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}C_{i})\cup C
, where
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a countable collection of pairwise disjoint
\Sigma
-atoms and
C\in\Sigma
, being disjoint from each

•

If the WCE operator
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{1}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
is compact, then

(1)

E(|u|^{q^{\prime}})^{1/q^{\prime}}(E(|w|^{q}))^{1/q}=0
a.e. on
B

(2)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{E(|u|^{q^{\prime}})(A_{i})(E(|w|^{q}))^{q^{\prime}/q}(A_{i})}{\mu(A_{i})}=0
, when the number of
\mathcal{A}
-atoms is not finite.

•

The WCE operator
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{1}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
is compact, if the following conditions hold:

(1)

u(E(|w|^{q}))^{1/q}=0
a.e. on
C
, and

(2)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{E(|u|^{q^{\prime}})(C_{i})(E(|w|^{q}))^{q^{\prime}/q}(C_{i})}{\mu(C_{i})}=0
, when the number of
\Sigma
-atoms is not finite.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
and let the measure space
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a non-atomic. Then the WCE operator
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
is nuclear if and only if
T=0
.

Proof.

Suppose
T=M_{w}EM_{u}
is nuclear WCE operator. Since every nuclear operator is compact,
T
is compact . Then by Remark 2.3 of
[
9
]
we have
T=0
.

The converse is obvious.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a measure space with the finite subset property that is not purely atomic and let
1\leq p,q<\infty
.If the restriction of the WCE operator
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
to the non-atomic part of the measure space is non-zero operator, then
T
can not be nuclear.

Proof.

By contrary suppose that T be nuclear.Then T is compact and by
[
9
]
we have

k
:=
(E|w|^{q})^{1/q}(E|u|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}=0
a.e. on
B
where
1\leq q<p<\infty

h
:=
E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}(E(|w|^{q}))^{1/q}=0
a.e. on
B
where
1\leq p<q<\infty

Since the measure space
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
is not purely atomic, there exists at least one non-atomic subset
D
such that
\mu(D)>0
. By hypothesis, the operator
T
is nonzero on
D
. Consequently, the functions
k
and
h
must be strictly positive on a subset
D
with positive measure (contained in
B
) which is contradiction. Therefore,
T
can not be compact, and Hence,
T
can not be nuclear.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
1\leq q<p<\infty
and let
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
be conjugate components to
p
and
q
respectively. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma
be a
\sigma
-finite subalgebra such that the measure space
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu|_{\mathcal{A}})
is purely atomic. Then the bounded WCE operator
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
is nuclear if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}<\infty,

where
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a collection of pairwise disjoint
\mathcal{A}
-atoms satisfying
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}
, and the exponent
r
given by
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}
.

Proof.

It is standard fact that every
\mathcal{A}
-measurable function on
X
is constant on each
\mathcal{A}
-atom. Hence for every
f\in L^{p}(\Sigma)
we have

\displaystyle Tf

\displaystyle=w\cdot E(uf)=w\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}E(uf)(A_{i})\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}

\displaystyle=w\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(A_{i})}\int_{A_{i}}E(uf)\,d\mu\right)\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}

\displaystyle=w\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(A_{i})}\int_{A_{i}}uf\,d\mu\right)\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}.

Assume that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}<\infty.

Define for each
i\in\mathbb{N}

\phi_{i}(f)=\int_{X}(u\cdot\chi_{A_{i}})f\,d\mu,g_{i}=\frac{w\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}}{\mu(A_{i})}.

Then
u\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Sigma)
and
g_{i}\in L^{q}(\Sigma)
. Clearly, each
\phi_{i}
is a bounded linear functional on
L^{p}(\Sigma)
. More over one can verify that

\left\|g_{i}\right\|_{q}=(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{({1/q})-1}

, in which
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}={1}
(we assume that
\frac{1}{\infty}={0}
) and

\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}.

Therefore,

T=M_{w}EM_{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\phi_{i}\otimes g_{i},

and

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\left\|g_{i}\right\|_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}<\infty.

This implies that
T
is nuclear.

Conversely, let the WCE operator
T=M_{w}EM_{u}
be nuclear. Then
T
is absolutely summing and so by Pietsch’s theorem 2.12 of
[
3
]
, there is a Borel probability measure
\nu
on the unit ball
B_{p^{\prime}}
of
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu)\simeq(L^{p}(\mu))^{*}
such that for some
M>0
,

\left\|Tf\right\|_{q}\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}|l(f)|\,d\nu(l),\qquad\forall f\in L^{p}(\mu).

By Riesz representation theorem, for each continuous linear functional
l\in(L^{p}(\mu))^{*}
, there exists
g_{l}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu)
such that

l(f)=\int_{X}f\cdot g_{l}\,d\mu,\qquad f\in L^{p}(\mu).

Applying Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}|l(f)|\,d\nu(l)

\displaystyle=M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\int_{X}|f.g_{l}|\,d\mu\,d\nu(g_{l})

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{p}\,d\nu(g_{l}).

Taking the sequence of disjoint atoms
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
,we define for each
i\in\mathbb{N}

f_{i}=\frac{\overline{u}|u|^{{p^{\prime}}-2}(E(|w|^{q}))^{({p^{\prime}}-1)/q}}{\left\|T\right\|^{{p^{\prime}}/p}(\mu(A_{i}))^{(r-{p^{\prime}})/pr}}\chi_{A_{i}}.

One readly checks that
f_{i}\in L^{p}(\Sigma)
and
\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{p}\leq 1
. Hence we have

\left\|Tf_{i}\right\|_{q}^{q}=\frac{1}{\left\|T\right\|^{p^{\prime}q/p}}\int_{A_{i}}\frac{1}{\mu(A_{i})^{{(r-p^{\prime}q)}/r}}\left((E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}\right)^{p^{\prime}q}\,d\mu.

Therefore,

\left\|Tf_{i}\right\|_{q}=\frac{1}{\left\|T\right\|^{{p^{\prime}}/p}}\left((E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}\right)^{p^{\prime}}.

Combining this identity with the Pietsch domination bound established earlier, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|Tf_{i}\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\|T\right\|^{{p^{\prime}}/p}}\left((E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}\right)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{p}\,d\nu(g_{l})

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l}).

And so

(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}\left(\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}.

Since the atoms
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
are disjoint, we have the norm decomposition
\left\|g_{l}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}\left(\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}\left(\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}.

Consequently,

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}<\infty.

Which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.8

and

Conversely, let the WCE operator

\nu
on the unit ball

and

\left\|Tf\right\|_{q}\leq M\int_{B_{\infty}}|l(f)|\,d\nu(l),\qquad\forall f\in L^{1}(\mu).

By Riesz representation theorem, for each continuous linear functional
l\in(L^{p}(\mu))^{*}
, there exists
g_{l}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mu)
such that

l(f)=\int_{X}f\cdot g_{l}\,d\mu,\qquad f\in L^{p}(\mu).

Applying Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}|l(f)|\,d\nu(l)

\displaystyle=M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\int_{X}|f.g_{l}|\,d\mu\,d\nu(g_{l})

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{p}\,d\nu(g_{l}).

Taking the sequence of disjoint atoms
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
,we define for each
i\in\mathbb{N}

f_{i}=\frac{\overline{u}|u|^{{p^{\prime}}-2}(E(|w|^{q}))^{({p^{\prime}}-1)/q}}{\left\|T\right\|^{{p^{\prime}}/p}(\mu(A_{i}))^{({p^{\prime}}+r)/pr}}\chi_{A_{i}}.

One readly checks that
f_{i}\in L^{p}(\Sigma)
and
\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{p}\leq 1
. Hence we have

\left\|Tf_{i}\right\|_{q}^{q}=\frac{1}{\left\|T\right\|^{p^{\prime}q/p}}\int_{A_{i}}\frac{1}{\mu(A_{i})^{({r+p^{\prime}q})/r}}\left((E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}\right)^{p^{\prime}q}\,d\mu.

Therefore,

\left\|Tf_{i}\right\|_{q}=\frac{1}{\left\|T\right\|^{{p^{\prime}}/p}}\left((E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{-1/r}\right)^{p^{\prime}}.

Combining this identity with the Pietsch domination bound established earlier, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|Tf_{i}\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\|T\right\|^{{p^{\prime}}/p}}\left((E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{-1/r}\right)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{p}\,d\nu(g_{l})

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l}).

And so

(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{-1/r}\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}\left(\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}.

It is clear that
\left\|g_{l}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}
and
\left\|g_{l}\right\|_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{\infty}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{-1/r}

\displaystyle\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}\left(\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{l}\cdot\chi_{A_{i}}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}\left(\int_{B_{p^{\prime}}}\left\|g_{l}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\,d\nu(g_{l})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}.

This implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{-1/r}\leq M^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\left\|T\right\|^{1/p}<\infty.

Which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma
be a
\sigma
-finite subalgebra. Then the bounded WCE operator
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
is nuclear (where
1\leq q<p<\infty
) if and only if

(i)

(E|u|^{p^{\prime}})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(E|w|^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}=0

\mu
-a.e. on
B
, and

(ii)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/{p^{\prime}}}(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}<\infty
,

in which
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
are pairwise disjoint
\mathcal{A}
-atoms and
B
is non-atomic such that
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}\cup B
and the exponent r determined by
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}
.

Proof.

The statement follows directly by combining Theorem
2.7
and Corollary
2.5
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma
be a

-finite subalgebra. Then the bounded WCE operator

is nuclear (where

) if and only if

(i)

(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(E(|w|^{q}))^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}}=0

\mu
-a.e. on

, and

(ii)

Example 2.11
.

Consider the measure space
(\mathbb{N},2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu)
where
\mu
is the counting measure.
A sub‑
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is generated by the following partition of
\mathbb{N}
:

•

Odd atoms:

\{1\},\{3\},\{5\},\dots
each of measure
1
;

•

Even atoms:

A_{1}=\{2\}
,
A_{2}=\{4,6\}
,
A_{3}=\{8,10,12\}
,
A_{4}=\{14,16,18,20\}
,
\dots

where
\mu(A_{n})=n
and each
A_{n}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-atom consisting of
n
consecutive even numbers.
The first element of
A_{n}
is
2k_{n}
with
k_{n}=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+1
(hence
k_{n}\geq n
).

There is no non‑atomic part.

The conditional expectation operator
E=E^{\mathcal{A}}
acts as follows:

•

On an odd atom
\{2k-1\}
:
E(f)(2k-1)=f(2k-1)
;

•

On an even atom
A_{n}
:
E(f)
is constant on
A_{n}
and equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f\bigl(2(k_{n}+j)\bigr)
.

Two weight sequences are introduced:

\displaystyle u(n)=n,\qquad w(n)=\frac{1}{n^{3}}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

The WCE operator
T
is defined by

\displaystyle Tf=w\cdot E(uf),\qquad f\in L^{p}(\Sigma),

where
\Sigma=2^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Assume
1\leq p<q<\infty
. Set
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
and let
r
be determined by
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}
(i.e.
r=\frac{pq}{q-p}
).
In our setting
B=\varnothing
, so part (i) of Theorem
2.10
is automatic.
We now verify (ii).

Odd atoms:

Since

\displaystyle E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(\{2k-1\})=(2k-1)^{p^{\prime}},\qquad E(|w|^{q})(\{2k-1\})=(2k-1)^{-3q},

therfore we have:

\displaystyle(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}=2k-1,\qquad(E(|w|^{q}))^{1/q}=(2k-1)^{-3}.

The measure factor
\mu(\{2k-1\})^{-1/r}=1
. Hence the contribution is
(2k-1)^{-2}
.
The series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2k-1)^{-2}
converges.

Even atoms
A_{n}
(
\mu(A_{n})=n
):

We only need an upper bound. Since all indices in
A_{n}
are at least
2k_{n}
and
k_{n}\geq n
,

\displaystyle(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{n}))^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq\max_{j=0}^{n-1}2(k_{n}+j)\leq 2(k_{n}+n)\leq 4k_{n},

\displaystyle(E(|w|^{q})(A_{n}))^{1/q}\leq((2k_{n})^{-3q})^{1/q}=(2k_{n})^{-3}.

Thus

\displaystyle(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}}))^{1/p^{\prime}}(E(|w|^{q}))^{1/q}\leq 4k_{n}\cdot(2k_{n})^{-3}=\frac{1}{2k_{n}^{2}}.

Moreover
\mu(A_{n})^{-1/r}=n^{-1/r}
and
k_{n}\sim n^{2}/2
. Consequently the term for
A_{n}
is bounded by a constant times
n^{-4}\cdot n^{-(q-p)/(pq)}=n^{-4-\frac{q-p}{pq}}
, which is summable because the exponent is
<-4
.
Hence the whole series converges. Theorem 2.10 immediately yields that
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
is nuclear for every
1\leq p<q<\infty
.

Now assume
1\leq q<p<\infty
. Again set
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
and define
r
by
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}
(so
r=\frac{pq}{p-q}
).
Theorem
2.9
states that a bounded operator
T:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
is nuclear iff

(i)

(E|u|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}(E|w|^{q})^{1/q}=0
on
B
;

(ii)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}})(A_{i}))^{1/p^{\prime}}\,(E(|w|^{q})(A_{i}))^{1/q}\,(\mu(A_{i}))^{1/r}<\infty
.

Again (i) is trivial because
B=\varnothing
.
We check (ii).

Odd atoms:
The same computation as before gives
(2k-1)^{-2}
multiplied by
\mu(\{2k-1\})^{1/r}=1
. The series converges.

Even atoms
A_{n}
:
The estimate for the product
(E(|u|^{p^{\prime}}))^{1/p^{\prime}}(E(|w|^{q}))^{1/q}
is still
\leq\frac{1}{2k_{n}^{2}}
.
Now
\mu(A_{n})^{1/r}=n^{1/r}=n^{(p-q)/(pq)}
.
Using
k_{n}\sim n^{2}/2
, the term is bounded by a constant times
n^{-4}\cdot n^{\frac{p-q}{pq}}=n^{-4+\frac{p-q}{pq}}
.
Since
\frac{p-q}{pq}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}<\frac{1}{q}\leq 1
, the exponent satisfies
-4+\frac{p-q}{pq}\leq-3
. Hence the series converges absolutely.
Therefore Theorem 2.9 applies and
T=M_{w}EM_{u}:L^{p}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)
is nuclear for all
1\leq q<p<\infty
.
