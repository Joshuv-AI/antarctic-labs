A note on small cap square function and decoupling estimates for the parabola
Jongchon Kim, Liang Wang, Chun Keung Yeung

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove small cap square function and decoupling estimates for the parabola, where the small caps are essentially axis-parallel rectangles of dimensions δ × δ β \delta\times\delta^{\beta} for 0 ≤ β ≤ 1 0\leq\beta\leq 1 . Our estimates complement the known results for 1 ≤ β ≤ 2 1\leq\beta\leq 2 and are sharp up to polylogarithmic factors.

42B10

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate small cap square function and decoupling estimates for the parabola.
Let
\delta
be a small positive constant and
0\leq\beta\leq 2
. We denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}(\delta^{\beta})
the
\delta^{\beta}
-neighborhood of the unit parabola

\mathbb{P}_{1}:=\bigl\{(\xi,\xi^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\xi|\leq 1\bigr\}.

We then consider a disjoint partition
\{\gamma\}
of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}(\delta^{\beta})
, denoted by
\mathcal{P}(\delta)
, where each partition element
\gamma
is defined as

\gamma:=\bigl\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}(\delta^{\beta}):\xi_{1}\in I\bigr\},

for an interval
I\subset[-1,1]
of length
\delta
.
The collection
\mathcal{P}(\delta)
consists of almost rectangular boxes
\gamma
with dimensions
\delta\times\delta^{\beta}
. When
\beta=2
, we call each
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)
a canonical cap. When
1\leq\beta<2
, each
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)
corresponds to a small cap of dimensions
R^{-\alpha}\times R^{-1}
in the literature (e.g.
[
3
]
), where
\delta=R^{-\alpha}
and
\alpha=\beta^{-1}
.

In this paper, we are primarily concerned with the range
0\leq\beta\leq 1
; in this regime,
\gamma
is essentially an axis-parallel rectangle with dimensions
\delta\times\delta^{\beta}
. Let
f
be an arbitrary function whose Fourier transform is supported in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}(\delta^{\beta})
. For any
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we define
f_{\gamma}
as the inverse Fourier transform of
\chi_{\gamma}\widehat{f}
, namely

f_{\gamma}:=(\chi_{\gamma}\widehat{f})^{\vee}.

Let
S_{p,\delta,\beta}
and
D_{p,\delta,\beta}
denote the best constants for the following estimates:

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq S_{p,\delta,\beta}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\qquad\text{(Square function estimate)}

and

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq D_{p,\delta,\beta}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad\text{(Decoupling inequality)},

which hold for all
f
whose Fourier transform is supported in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}(\delta^{\beta})
.

For the canonical caps,
\beta=2
, the Córdoba-Fefferman argument
[
4
,
2
]
yields the square function estimate

S_{4,\delta,2}\lesssim 1
. For decoupling inequality, Bourgain and Demeter, in their landmark paper
[
1
]
, proved that
D_{6,\delta,2}\leq C_{\epsilon}\delta^{-\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
. Guth, Maldague, and Wang
[
8
]
later improved this estimate to
D_{6,\delta,2}\lesssim|\log\delta|^{c}
for some constant
c>0
.

Motivated by exponential sum estimates over slabs, such as the one appearing in Corollary
1.3
below, Demeter, Guth, and Wang
[
3
]
introduced the concept of small cap decoupling inequalities. In particular, they proved that for
1\leq\beta\leq 2
and
p\geq 2
, the inequality

D_{p,\delta,\beta}\leq C_{\epsilon}\delta^{-\epsilon}\bigg(\delta^{-\bigl(1-\frac{2+\beta}{p}\bigr)}+\delta^{-\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}\bigg)

holds for any
\epsilon>0
. The
\delta^{-\epsilon}
loss was improved to
|\log\delta|^{c}
for some constant
c>0
by Johnsrude
[
9
]
.
As an analogue of small cap decoupling inequalities, Gan
[
6
]
established small cap square function estimates: for
1\leq\beta\leq 2
and
p\geq 2
,

S_{p,\delta,\beta}\leq C_{\epsilon}\delta^{-\epsilon}\bigg(\delta^{-\bigl(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\bigr)\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}+\delta^{-\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}\bigr)}\bigg).

Both of the above bounds are sharp up to the
\delta^{-\epsilon}
(or
|\log\delta|^{c}
) factor.

In this paper, we consider the small cap square function and decoupling estimates for the case
0\leq\beta\leq 1
. Specifically, we establish the following small cap square function estimate:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
0\leq\beta\leq 1
and
p\geq 2
, we have the following small cap square function estimate:

S_{p,\delta,\beta}\leq C_{p}|\log\delta|^{c}\left(\delta^{-\left({1-\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\right)}+\delta^{-\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}\right)}\right),

where
C_{p}
is a constant depending only on
p
, and
c>0
is a positive constant. The bound is sharp up to the
|\log\delta|^{c}
factor.

We remark that for
0\leq\beta\leq 1
, the inequality

\left(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1+\beta}{p}

holds if and only if
p\leq 6
.

Our proof yields Theorem
1.1
with
c=1+\frac{1}{p}
. Our argument in the proof of Theorem
1.1
remains valid in the case
1\leq\beta\leq 2
considered in
[
6
]
and can be used to strengthen the result of
[
6
]
by replacing the
\delta^{-\epsilon}
loss with a
|\log\delta|^{c}
loss.

We further establish the following small cap decoupling inequality:

Theorem 1.2
.

For
0\leq\beta\leq 1
and
p\geq 2
, the following estimate holds:

D_{p,\delta,\beta}\leq C_{p}|\log\delta|^{c}\bigg(\delta^{-(2-\beta)\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}+\delta^{-(1-\frac{2+\beta}{p})}\bigg),

where
C_{p}
is a constant depending only on
p
, and
c>0
is a positive constant. The bound is sharp up to the
|\log\delta|^{c}
factor.

We remark that for
0\leq\beta\leq 1
, the inequality

(2-\beta)\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)\geq 1-\frac{2+\beta}{p}

holds if and only if
p\leq 4
. Theorem
1.2
for the critical exponent
p=4
is a direct consequence of the small cap decoupling inequality for the case
\beta=1
due to Johnsrude
[
9
]
and the flat decoupling inequality.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1.3
.

For
1\leq\sigma\leq 2
and
2\leq p<\infty
, the following inequality holds:

\bigg(\int_{[0,1]\times[0,N^{-\sigma}]}\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}e\bigl(x_{1}k+x_{2}k^{2}\bigr)\bigg|^{p}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim(\log N)^{c}\bigg(N^{\frac{\sigma}{2}(1-\frac{4}{p})}+N^{1-\frac{4}{p}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|a_{k}|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
are arbitrary complex numbers and
c>0
is a positive constant. Here, we adopt the standard notation
e(t):=e^{2\pi it}
.

Corollary
1.3
was established in
[
12
]
with an
N^{\epsilon}
loss for any
\epsilon>0
using the small cap decoupling estimate
[
3
]
for
\beta=1
, and the result is sharp up to this
N^{\epsilon}
factor (see
[
12
, Section 1.1]
). In the present work, Corollary
1.3
is obtained as an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
. The improvement of the
N^{\epsilon}
loss to the logarithmic factor
(\log N)^{c}
is due to the logarithmic improvement in the small cap decoupling estimate
[
9
]
. We refer the reader to
[
3
]
for sharp bounds for the range
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
.

Notation

We summarize below the notations that are frequently used throughout this paper.

(1)

We write
A\lesssim B
to mean that there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
.
Furthermore, we write
A\sim B
if both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold.

(2)

Let
\delta,\beta,\kappa>0
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\kappa)
a disjoint partition
\{\tau\}
of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}(\delta^{\beta})
, where each element
\tau
of the partition is given by

\tau:=\bigl\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\xi_{1}\in I,\ |\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}|\leq\delta^{\beta}\bigr\},

with
I\subset[-1,1]
being an interval of length
\kappa
. Furthermore, we denote by
\widetilde{\tau}
the union of
\tau
and its neighboring elements
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\kappa)
.

(3)

For a rectangle
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with side lengths
a\times b
, its dual rectangle
T^{*}
is defined as the rectangle centered at the origin, with side lengths
a^{-1}\times b^{-1}
, and whose sides are parallel to the corresponding sides of
T
. Additionally, we denote by
w_{T}
a smooth function satisfying
w_{T}\sim 1
on
T
, decaying rapidly outside
T
, and
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{w_{T}}\subset T^{*}
.

2.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we focus on the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
It suffices to prove the local estimate

(1)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq C_{p}|\log\delta|^{c}\Bigl(\delta^{-\left({1-\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\right)}+\delta^{-\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}\right)}\Bigr)\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(w_{T})},

where
T
is an axis-parallel rectangle of dimensions
\delta^{-1}\times\delta^{-\beta}
. We obtain Theorem
1.1
by summing (
1
) over translates of
T
partitioning
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

2.1.
Broad-Narrow reduction

In this subsection, we decompose
f
into broad and narrow parts, which revises the argument in
[
3
]
by adopting the idea in
[
8
,
9
]
; this ensures that the small cap square function estimate incurs only a loss of
|\log\delta|^{c}
. Specifically, let
K=8
and
m
be an integer satisfying
K^{m}\leq\delta^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}<K^{m+1}
. Since
K^{-m}\sim\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
, we henceforth identify
\mathcal{P}(K^{-m})
with
\mathcal{P}\bigl(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\bigr)
. Note that each
\theta\in\mathcal{P}\bigl(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\bigr)
is a canonical cap of dimensions
\delta^{\beta/2}\times\delta^{\beta}
. We establish the following estimate:

(2)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq C_{p}|\log\delta|\Big(\text{Narrow}+\text{Broad}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

(3)

\text{Narrow}=\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}\bigl(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\bigr)}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p},

and

(4)

\text{Broad}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i-1})\\
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset\widetilde{\tau}\\
\text{dist}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq K^{-i-1}\end{subarray}}\left\|{\left|{f_{\tau_{1}}f_{\tau_{2}}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}.

Here, for each
\tau\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i})
,
\widetilde{\tau}
denotes the union of
\tau
and its neighboring elements
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i})
.

To prove estimate (
2
), we require the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a sequence of complex numbers, and let
C\geq 1
be a positive constant. Then, at least one of the following estimates holds:

(5)

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|\leq 2Cn\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j:|i-j|>1\end{subarray}}|a_{i}a_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}},

and

(6)

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|\leq\left(1+C^{-1}\right)\max_{i}\bigg|\sum_{j:|i-j|\leq 1}a_{j}\bigg|.

Proof.

Suppose that the following inequality holds:

(7)

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|>\left(1+C^{-1}\right)\max_{i}\bigg|\sum_{j:|i-j|\leq 1}a_{j}\bigg|.

Let
i^{*}
and
i^{**}
be indices such that
|a_{i^{*}}|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|a_{i}|
and
|a_{i^{**}}|=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i:|i-i^{*}|>1\end{subarray}}|a_{i}|
. By the triangle inequality, we have

(8)

\bigg|\sum_{j:|i^{*}-j|\leq 1}a_{j}\bigg|\geq\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|-n|a_{i^{**}}|.

Combining inequalities (
7
) and (
8
), we derive

\displaystyle n|a_{i^{**}}|

\displaystyle\geq\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|-\bigg|\sum_{j:|i^{*}-j|\leq 1}a_{j}\bigg|

\displaystyle>\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|-\frac{1}{1+C^{-1}}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C+1}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|.

Note that

|a_{i^{**}}|^{2}\leq|a_{i^{*}}a_{i^{**}}|\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j:|i-j|>1\end{subarray}}|a_{i}a_{j}|,

which implies
|a_{i^{**}}|\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j:|i-j|>1\end{subarray}}|a_{i}a_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Substituting this into the above inequality, we conclude

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\bigg|\leq 2Cn\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j:|i-j|>1\end{subarray}}|a_{i}a_{j}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The following pointwise bound holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
p>0
:

(9)

|f(x)|\leq C_{p}|\log\delta|\bigg(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}\bigl(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\bigr)}|f_{\theta}(x)|^{p}+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i-1})\\
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset\widetilde{\tau}\\
\operatorname{dist}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq K^{-i-1}\end{subarray}}\left|{f_{\tau_{1}}(x)f_{\tau_{2}}(x)}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
C_{p}
is a constant depending only on
p
and
c
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

For each
\tau\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i})
with
0\leq i\leq m-1
, we first observe the decomposition

f_{\widetilde{\tau}}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i-1})\\
\tau_{1}\subset{\widetilde{\tau}}\end{subarray}}f_{\tau_{1}}(x).

Applying Lemma
2.1
to this sum with parameters
n=K=8
and
C=|\log\delta|
(note that
C=|\log\delta|\geq 1
, which satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
2.1
), we conclude that at least one of the following two estimates holds:

(10)

|f_{\widetilde{\tau}}(x)|^{p}\leq\left(16|\log\delta|\right)^{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i-1})\\
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset{\widetilde{\tau}}\\
\operatorname{dist}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq K^{-i-1}\end{subarray}}\left|{f_{\tau_{1}}(x)f_{\tau_{2}}(x)}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}},

and

(11)

|f_{\widetilde{\tau}}(x)|^{p}\leq\left(1+|\log\delta|^{-1}\right)^{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i-1})\\
\tau_{1}\subset{\widetilde{\tau}}\end{subarray}}|f_{\widetilde{\tau_{1}}}(x)|^{p}.

Using this for the sum
f=\sum_{\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-1})}f_{\tau_{1}}
, we obtain

(12)

\displaystyle|f(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(16|\log\delta|\right)^{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-1})\\
\operatorname{dist}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq K^{-1}\end{subarray}}\left|{f_{\tau_{1}}(x)f_{\tau_{2}}(x)}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}+\left(1+|\log\delta|^{-1}\right)^{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-1})\end{subarray}}|f_{\widetilde{\tau_{1}}}(x)|^{p}.

Next, for any
\tau\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-1})
, we apply the same reasoning to obtain

(13)

\displaystyle|f_{\widetilde{\tau}}(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(16|\log\delta|\right)^{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-2})\\
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset\widetilde{\tau}\\
\operatorname{dist}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq K^{-2}\end{subarray}}\left|{f_{\tau_{1}}(x)f_{\tau_{2}}(x)}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}+\left(1+|\log\delta|^{-1}\right)^{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-2})\\
\tau_{1}\subset\widetilde{\tau}\end{subarray}}|f_{\widetilde{\tau_{1}}}(x)|^{p}.

Combining inequalities (
12
) and (
13
), we find

\displaystyle|f(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{1}\left(1+|\log\delta|^{-1}\right)^{pi}\left(16|\log\delta|\right)^{p}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i})}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i-1})\\
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset{\widetilde{\tau}}\\
\operatorname{dist}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq K^{-i-1}\end{subarray}}\left|{f_{\tau_{1}}(x)f_{\tau_{2}}(x)}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(1+|\log\delta|^{-1}\right)^{2p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-2})\end{subarray}}|f_{\widetilde{\tau_{1}}}(x)|^{p}.

Extending this iterative process to
m
steps, we arrive at

\displaystyle|f(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left(1+|\log\delta|^{-1}\right)^{pi}\left(16|\log\delta|\right)^{p}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i})}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i-1})\\
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset{\widetilde{\tau}}\\
\operatorname{dist}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq K^{-i-1}\end{subarray}}\left|{f_{\tau_{1}}(x)f_{\tau_{2}}(x)}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(1+|\log\delta|^{-1}\right)^{mp}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\mathcal{P}\bigl(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\bigr)\end{subarray}}|f_{\widetilde{\theta}}(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}|\log\delta|^{p}\bigg(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}\bigl(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\bigr)}|f_{\theta}(x)|^{p}+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(K^{-i-1})\\
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset\widetilde{\tau}\\
\operatorname{dist}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq K^{-i-1}\end{subarray}}\left|{f_{\tau_{1}}(x)f_{\tau_{2}}(x)}\right|^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg).

Here, the final inequality uses the facts that
m<|\log\delta|
and
\left(1+|\log\delta|^{-1}\right)^{|\log\delta|}
is bounded by
e
.
∎

Then, the estimate (
2
) follows from Lemma
2.2
. Therefore, it suffices to estimate the narrow and broad parts separately.

2.2.
Broad-Narrow analysis

In this subsection, we aim to establish estimates for the narrow part and the broad part. To this end, we first state the following basic lemmas, which will be essential for our subsequent analysis.

Lemma 2.3

(Local
L^{2}
-orthogonality; see e.g.
[
6
]
, Lemma 2)
.

Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i}
be a sequence of functions, and let
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a rectangle such that the family
\{\text{supp}\widehat{f_{i}}+T^{*}\}_{i}
has finite overlap. Then the estimate

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bigg|\sum_{i}f_{i}w_{T}\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{i}|f_{i}w_{T}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

holds, where
w_{T}
denotes a smooth function satisfying
w_{T}\sim 1
on
T
, decaying rapidly outside
T
, and
\text{supp}\widehat{w_{T}}\subset T^{*}
.

Lemma 2.4

(Narrow part)
.

Let
\theta\in\mathcal{P}(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}})
and
T
be an axis-parallel rectangle of dimensions
\delta^{-1}\times\delta^{-\beta}
. Then the following inequality holds for
p\geq 2
:

\left\|{f_{\theta}}\right\|_{L^{p}(T)}\leq C\delta^{-\left({1-\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\right)}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)\\
\gamma\subset\theta\end{subarray}}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(w_{T})}.

Proof.

We decompose
{T}
into a disjoint union of rectangles
B
with dimensions
\delta^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\times\delta^{-\beta}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}({T})}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{B\subset{T}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(B)}^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim|B|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{B\subset{T}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(w_{B})}^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\qquad\text{(by Bernstein's inequality)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3\beta}{2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}\bigg(\sum_{B\subset{T}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(w_{B})}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3\beta}{2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(w_{{T}})}.

By the local
L^{2}
-orthogonality (Lemma
2.3
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(T)}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3\beta}{2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(w_{{T}})}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{3\beta}{2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)\\
\gamma\subset\theta\end{subarray}}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{2}(w_{{T}})}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and using the fact
|T|\sim\delta^{-(1+\beta)}
, we derive

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)\\
\gamma\subset\theta\end{subarray}}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{2}(w_{{T}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|{T}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)\\
\gamma\subset\theta\end{subarray}}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(w_{{T}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-(1+\beta)\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)\\
\gamma\subset\theta\end{subarray}}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(w_{T})}.

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-\bigl(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\bigr)\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bigr)}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)\\
\gamma\subset\theta\end{subarray}}|f_{\gamma}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(w_{T})}.

Lemma 2.5

(2)

) follows directly from Hölder’s inequality. For

Lemma 2.6

(Broad part)
.

Let
T
be an axis-parallel rectangle of dimensions
\delta^{-1}\times\delta^{-\beta}
. Fix

We apply the parabolic rescaling: for a fixed

Example 1

For each
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)
, let
\widehat{f_{\gamma}}
be a bump function supported on
\gamma
, and define

f=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}f_{\gamma}.

On the one hand, observe that
|f(x)|\gtrsim\delta^{\beta}
when
x
is near the origin, which implies that

(27)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\gtrsim\delta^{\beta}\cdot|B(0,1)|=\delta^{\beta}.

On the other hand, we can find, via direct computation, that
|f_{\gamma}|\sim|\gamma|=\delta^{1+\beta}
on the dual rectangle
\gamma^{*}
, which can be identified as
\gamma^{*}=[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]\times[-\delta^{-\beta},\delta^{-\beta}]
for all
\gamma
, and
|f_{\gamma}|
decays rapidly outside of
\gamma^{*}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\delta^{-1}\cdot\delta^{(1+\beta)(p-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\delta^{-\frac{1}{p}+(1+\beta)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)},

\displaystyle\left\|{\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\left|{f_{\gamma}}\right|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\gamma|\cdot\left\|{\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}1\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\gamma^{*})}\lesssim\delta^{1+\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\delta^{-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}=\delta^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}.

Therefore, we deduce that

\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}{\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{\beta}}{\delta^{1+\beta-\frac{2+\beta}{p}}}=\delta^{-\left(1-\frac{2+\beta}{p}\right)},

and

\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}{\left\|{\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\left|{f_{\gamma}}\right|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{\beta}}{\delta^{\frac{1}{2}+\beta-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}}=\delta^{-\left({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}\right)}.

Example 2

Fix a canonical cap
\theta\in\mathcal{P}(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}})
. For each
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)
, let
\widehat{f_{\gamma}}
be a bump function supported on
\gamma
if
\gamma\subset\theta
, and set
\widehat{f_{\gamma}}=0
otherwise. We then define

f=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}f_{\gamma}.

Then we have
|f_{\gamma}|\sim|\gamma|=\delta^{1+\beta}
on
\gamma^{*}
and decays rapidly outside the dual rectangle
\gamma^{*}
, additionally,
|f|\sim|\theta|=\delta^{\frac{3\beta}{2}}
on
\theta^{*}
and decays rapidly outside the dual rectangle
\theta^{*}
.
We now have

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\gtrsim\ |\theta|\cdot|\theta^{*}|^{\frac{1}{p}}=\delta^{\frac{3\beta}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)},

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\left(\delta^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}\cdot\delta^{(1+\beta)(p-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\delta^{\beta\left(1-\frac{1}{2p}\right)+1-\frac{2}{p}},

and

\left\|{\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\left|{f_{\gamma}}\right|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim|\gamma|\cdot\left\|{\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\subset\theta}1\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\gamma^{*})}=\delta^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5\beta}{4}-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}.

Therefore, we deduce that

\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}{\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{\frac{3\beta}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}}{\delta^{\beta\left(1-\frac{1}{2p}\right)+1-\frac{2}{p}}}=\delta^{-(2-\beta)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)},

and

\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}{\left\|{\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\delta)}\left|{f_{\gamma}}\right|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{\frac{3\beta}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}}{\delta^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5\beta}{4}-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}}=\delta^{-(1-\frac{\beta}{2})\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}.
