Spectral rigidity among ellipses, Bialy’s conjecture and local extrema of Mather’s beta function
Corentin Fierobe

Abstract
In this paper we prove Bialy’s conjecture, which states that if two ellipses ℰ \mathscr{E} and ℰ ′ \mathscr{E}^{\prime} satisfy β ℰ ​ ( ρ 0 ) = β ℰ ′ ​ ( ρ 0 ) \beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{0})=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}(\rho_{0}) and β ℰ ​ ( ρ 1 ) = β ℰ ′ ​ ( ρ 1 ) \beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{1})=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}(\rho_{1}) for two distinct rotation numbers ρ 0 , ρ 1 ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ] \rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in(0,1/2] , then they must coincide up to isometries. We also prove that the same conclusion holds when only one rotation number is prescribed, provided the two ellipses have the same perimeter. Finally, we discuss consequences for local extremizers of Mather’s beta function, building on a recent result of Baranzini, Bialy, and Sorrentino.

Introduction

In the title of a seminal 1966 paper
[
12
]
, Kac asked the question
”Can one hear the shape of a drum?”
which has since become famous. It asks whether the shape of a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is uniquely determined by the spectrum of the Laplacian inside the domain with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions.

This question found a similar formulation in billiards dynamics, where one studies the movement of a particle inside a domain
\Omega
, bouncing off its boundary according to the law of reflection
angle of incidence = angle of reflection
. The spectrum of the Laplacian is replaced by the closure of the set of perimeters of periodic orbits, or
length spectrum

\mathscr{L}(\Omega)
, and one asks whether it determines the domain. Andersson and Melrose
[
1
]
proved that the two questions are related for generic strictly convex domains.

In the planar case, the question admits another, non-equivalent formulation by imposing a
marking
. More precisely, given a strictly convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a rational number
p/q\in[0,1/2]
, one can define

\beta_{\Omega}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=-\frac{1}{q}L_{p/q}

where
L_{p/q}
is the maximal perimeter of an orbit of rotation number
p/q
, where
q
is the number of bounces and
p
is the winding number of the orbit. The map
\beta_{\Omega}
, which extends to a convex map of the interval
[0,1/2]
is called Mather beta function and has many interesting properties relating the length spectrum to billiard’s dynamics.

The question whether
\mathscr{L}(\Omega)
or
\beta_{\Omega}
alone allow recovering a strictly convex planar domain remains open, although some partial results are known.
Generalizing
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
to the length spectrum,
[
23
]
proved that the sets of isospectral domains are compact in the
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
-topology.

[
21
,
22
]
showed that Mather’s beta function, resp. its Taylor coefficients, allow one to recognize disks from any other domain. In fact, one needs only the data of the domain’s perimeter and the value of
\beta_{\Omega}
at a generic rotation number; see
[
2
]
.
Recent developments towards the proof of Birkhoff’s conjecture, see
[
4
,
13
]
and in particular
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
, have shown that
one can recognize ellipses from any other centrally symmetric strictly convex domain with
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-smooth boundary using the values of
\beta_{\Omega}
at rotation numbers in
(0,1/4]
.
In particular, the result holds among ellipses, but the proof works with the knowledge of
\beta_{\Omega}
at an infinite family of rational rotation numbers.

This problem can also be formulated in terms of deformations and has been studied in several works; see, for example,
[
5
,
7
,
10
,
11
]
. We emphasize that the variational techniques developed in
[
7
]
for the study of Mather beta function inspired the results of the present paper. We refer to
[
8
]
for a recent overview of these problems.

In this paper, we are interested in studying the question of recovering
\Omega
from the knowledge of
\beta_{\Omega}(\rho)
on a
finite
set of rotation numbers. This seems impossible to hold for general domains, hence we investigate this problem either among ellipses, or locally near a fixed general domain. This problem was recently investigated in two different works
[
2
,
3
]
, and this paper addresses questions arising from these works in the two cases previously mentioned.

In
[
3
]
, the author formulates an explicit expression of Mather’s beta function associated to an ellipse, and provides remarkable computations that describe fully the billiard dynamics in an ellipse. He also proves using a geometric argument that two ellipses
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
satisfying

\beta_{\mathscr{E}}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{\mathscr{E}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)

for a given rational rotation number
p/q\in(0,1/2)
, then
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
must be the same. He conjectures that the result holds if one replaces
p/q
and
1/2
by any two distinct rotation numbers:

Conjecture 1

(Bialy
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
be two distinct rotation numbers in
(0,1/2]
. Two ellipses
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
satisfying

\beta_{\mathscr{E}}\left(\rho_{0}\right)=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}\left(\rho_{0}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{\mathscr{E}}\left(\rho_{1}\right)=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}\left(\rho_{1}\right)

are the same up to isometries.

We will prove Bialy’s conjecture:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in(0,1/2]
be distinct. Assume that
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
are two ellipses satisfying

\beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{0})=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}(\rho_{0})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{1})=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}(\rho_{1}).

Then
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
up to isometries.

The proof shows that given
\rho_{0}\in(0,1/2]
and an ellipse
\mathscr{E}
, there is an analytic one-parameter family of ellipses
(\mathscr{E}_{e})_{e\in[0,1)}
such that each ellipse
\mathscr{E}_{e}
is the only ellipse of eccentricity
e
satisfying
\beta_{\mathscr{E}_{e}}(\rho_{0})=\beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{0})
up to isometries. Then it shows that for any other
\rho_{1}\neq\rho_{0}
, the map

e\in[0,1)\mapsto\beta_{\mathscr{E}_{e}}(\rho_{1})

is strictly monotone.

The same techniques allow us to show a similar result which can be described as follows. Given
p>0
we consider the family of ellipses
(\mathscr{E}_{e})_{e\in[0,1]}
such that
\mathscr{E}_{e}
is the unique ellipse of perimeter
p
and eccentricity
e
up to isometries. The case

corresponds to a disk, and the case

corresponds to the degenerate case of a flat ellipse. We prove the following result:

Theorem 2
.

Given
\rho\in(0,1/2]
, the map

e\in[0,1]\mapsto\beta_{\Omega_{e}}(\rho)

(1)

is strictly decreasing.

In fact, if
\rho=0
the map (
1
) is identically equal to
0

Corollary 1
.

Let
\rho\in(0,1/2]
. Assume that
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
are two ellipses with the same perimeter such that

\beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho)=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}(\rho).

Then
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
up to isometries.

Previous results can be interpreted in terms of the local maxima of Mather’s beta function. Before giving an explicit formulation for this interpretation, let us recall a recent result by Bialy, Baranzini and Sorrentino, see
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
, stating that for any rotation number
\rho
outside a set of zero measure, one can characterize a given domain
\Omega
as a disk uusing only the data of its perimeter
\partial\Omega
and
\beta_{\Omega}
. More precisely, the result can be stated as follows, where we introduce
\mathbb{D}
as the unit disk:

Theorem 3

(Bialy-Baranzini-Sorrentino
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a strictly convex domain. Then

\beta_{\Omega}(\rho)\leq\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{2\pi}\beta_{\mathbb{D}}(\rho),\qquad\forall\rho\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right].

(2)

If the inequality in (
2
) is an equality at a certain
\rho
, then the billiard in
\Omega
has an invariant curve of constant angle associated to
\rho
. Moreover, there is a set
\mathscr{R}\subset[0,1/2]
containing
\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1/2)
whose complement is dense and of zero measure, such that if equality in (
2
) holds at a
\rho\in\mathscr{R}

\Omega
is a disk.

We recall what it means for a billiard map in a strictly convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
to have an invariant curve of constant angle associated to
\rho\in[0,1/2]
. This notion was introduced and studied by Gutkin
[
9
]
to study the so-called
floating problem
.

The boundary of
\Omega
is assumed to be
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-smooth. The billiard map
T_{\Omega}
in
\Omega
is defined on the set of pairs
(q,\varphi)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,\pi]
:
in fact
T_{\Omega}(q,\varphi)=(q^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
if and only if the oriented line starting at
q
and making an angle
\varphi
with the boundary intersects the boundary at another point
q^{\prime}
,
and bounces off it with an angle
\varphi^{\prime}
.
The map
T_{\Omega}
is said to have
an invariant curve of constant angle associated to
\rho
if there is
\varphi_{0}\in[0,\pi]
such that the set
\mathscr{C}:=\partial\Omega\times\{\varphi_{0}\}
is invariant by
T
, and
\left.T_{\Omega}\right|_{\mathscr{C}}
has rotation number
\rho
.

Disks are natural examples of billiard table with invariant curves of constant angle associated to any rotation number in
[0,1/2]
. Gutkin
[
9
, Corollary 2]
showed that there is a set, which is exactly the set
\mathscr{R}
of Theorem
3
, such that the only domains having an invariant curve of constant angle associated to a
\rho\in\mathscr{R}
are the circular billiards. He also showed that if
\rho\notin\mathscr{R}
, there are non-circular domains having an invariant curve of constant angle associated to
\rho
.

As a consequence of Theorem
3
, disks are global maximizer of the function

\beta(\rho):\Omega\in\mathscr{D}_{p}\mapsto\beta_{\Omega}(\rho)

(3)

defined for a fixed
\rho
over the set
\mathscr{D}_{p}
of strictly convex domains of a given perimeter
p>0
. Moreover, the maximum is strict for all except a countable set of rotation numbers.

In this paper we also intend to address the question of the
local
extrema of
\beta(\rho)
, to which Theorem
2
and Corollary
1
are related, see Corollary
2
. Given an integer

\mathscr{C}^{r}
-smooth domain

Given a domain
\Omega
, we denote by
\varrho
a radius of curvature of the domain associated to a given parametrization by arc-length. A set
\mathscr{S}_{r}
of domains with
\mathscr{C}^{r}
-smooth boundaries is said to be bounded if there is

for any radii of curvature

Theorem 4
.

There exists

with the following property. Given a
\mathscr{C}^{r}
-bounded set

of domains, there exists a positive measure set

\rho\in\mathscr{R}^{\prime}
then

1.

\beta(\rho)
has no local
\mathscr{C}^{r}
-minimizers ;

2.

the only possible local
\mathscr{C}^{r}
-maximizers of
\beta(\rho)
lying in
\mathscr{S}_{r}
are disks.

Note that this result holds in particular when
\mathscr{S}_{r}
is reduced to a single domain
\{\Omega\}
. In that case,
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}
is a positive measure set in
(0,1/2]
– depending on
\Omega
– such that for any
\rho\in\mathscr{R}^{\prime}
,
\Omega
is not a local
\mathscr{C}^{r}
-minimizer of
\beta(\rho)
, and not a local
\mathscr{C}^{r}
-maximizer of
\beta(\rho)
, unless
\Omega
is a disk.

Remark 1
.

\mathscr{R}^{\prime}
contains so-called Diophantine numbers corresponding to the existence of KAM curves. We don’t know whether
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}
contains other numbers, such as rational numbers.

Remark 2
.

In Theorem
4
, the set
\mathscr{S}_{r}
doesn’t need to be open. A domain

is a

Since ellipses are natural generalizations of disks, one may ask whether ellipses can be local maximizers of

Corollary 2
.

Let

and
\rho\in(0,1/2]
. A non-circular ellipse is not a local

Definition 1

Assume that the billiard map

Remark 3
.

Not all invariant curves admit an action-parametrization. Although it is always the case for irrational rotation numbers and sufficiently smooth domains, this can fail for rational ones.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that the billiard map in
\Omega

\beta_{\Omega}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{1}S(\varphi(\Theta),\varphi(\theta+\rho))d\theta=-2\int_{0}^{1}h(\psi(\Theta))\sin\delta(\Theta)d\theta.

Define for

\Sigma_{q}(\Theta)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}S(\varphi(\Theta+k\rho),\varphi(\Theta+(k+1)\rho)).

Integrating

\int_{0}^{1}\Sigma_{q}(\Theta)d\Theta=\int_{0}^{1}S(\varphi(\Theta),\varphi(\Theta+\rho))d\Theta.

Now since

\int_{0}^{1}\Sigma_{q}(\Theta)d\Theta\longrightarrow\beta_{\Omega}(\rho),\qquad q\longrightarrow+\infty

Proof of Bialy’s conjecture – Theorem
1

In this section, two ellipses that differ only by translations or rotations are considered identical, since these transformations do not change Mather’s beta function.

We begin with a proposition describing the structure of ellipses
\mathscr{E}
having a prescribed value of
\beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho)
for a given
\rho\neq 0
. We call an ellipse
non-degenerate
if it is not flat, that is, if its eccentricity satisfies
e<1
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\rho\in(0,1/2]
and
c<0
. The set of non-degenerate ellipses
\mathscr{E}
such that
\beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho)=c
consists of the elements of an analytic family
(\mathscr{E}_{e}^{\rho})_{e\in[0,1)}
where

\mathscr{E}_{e}
is an ellipse of eccentricity
e
.

Proof.

First note that given
\rho\in(0,1/2]
, two ellipses
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
