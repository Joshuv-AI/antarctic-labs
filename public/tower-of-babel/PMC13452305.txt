Protocol for optogenetic stimulation of cells under physical confinement
Authors: Qinling Yuan, Alice Amitrano, Debanik Choudhury, Konstantinos Konstantopoulos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryOptogenetic approaches enable spatiotemporal control of signaling proteins, yet their integration with microfluidic assays to study confined cell migration remains challenging. Herein, we present a protocol for the optogenetic activation of PI3K/Akt signaling in confined cells. We detail procedures for applying stimulation to induce localized Akt activation at the cell’s leading edge. This protocol enables real-time manipulation of subcellular signaling dynamics during confined migration.For additional information on the use of this protocol, please refer to Lee et al.1Subject areas: Biophysics, Cell Biology, Cancer, Biotechnology and bioengineering

Before you begin

Optogenetic tools are genetically encoded, light-sensitive proteins that enable precise spatiotemporal control of protein dynamics at single cell resolution.2,3,4,5,6 This protocol describes the steps required to regulate the spatiotemporal activation of Akt in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) cells migrating in confinement, using the cryptochrome 2 (CRY2)/CIBN light-gated dimerization system.1 With appropriate optimization, this workflow can be extended to other cell types, proteins, optogenetic constructs, and microenvironmental conditions.

In the configuration described in this protocol, 488 nm light stimulation drives CRY2-mCherry-iSH2 recruitment to the CIBN-GFP-CAAX anchor at the stimulated site of the plasma membrane, enabling precise, localized enrichment of the PI3K catalytic subunit (p110α) at the cell leading edge and subsequent downstream Akt phosphorylation.1 Before beginning, users should be familiar with the operating principles of the CRY2-CIBN system and with standard methods for fabricating microfluidic devices. This protocol was developed using the Nikon instrumentation and NIS elements software. Other hardware and software configurations can be employed with appropriate adjustments.

Innovation

The key advancement of this protocol is the integration of optogenetic systems with microfluidic assays to directly interrogate how localized signaling proteins govern directed cell migration. Although optogenetic tools have been widely used to manipulate signaling in two-dimensional cultures,7,8,9,10 applying these approaches in confined microenvironments, where cells navigate geometrically restrictive channels resembling physiological tissue spaces, remains technically challenging.4 Previous studies have used optogenetic approaches to modulate intracellular signaling molecules, such as RhoA and Ras,6,8 as well as membrane-associated proteins, including SWELL1 and parapinopsin,6,11 under confinement; however, a detailed experimental protocol is still lacking. This protocol addresses this gap by enabling spatially targeted 488 nm light stimulation at the leading edge of single cells migrating through confining microchannels, allowing acute and reversible activation of PI3K/Akt signaling at a defined subcellular location with single cell resolution. This level of spatiotemporal precision cannot be achieved with conventional pharmacological or genetic perturbations, which act globally and lack real-time control of migration dynamics. Using this workflow, we demonstrate that selective optogenetic activation of PI3K/Akt at the leading-edge rescues directed migration under acidic extracellular conditions, establishing a direct causal link between localized signaling activity and motility under confinement. This platform can be readily adapted to other optogenetic constructs, proteins, cell types, and microenvironmental conditions to dissect the spatial regulation of directed cell migration, including cell reversal dynamics under confinement.6,8

Expected outcomes

Stimulation is considered successful when sustained enrichment of opto-iSH2 signals is observed at the site of stimulation. Under physiological (pH=7.4) conditions, localized optogenetic stimulation of opto-iSH2 at the leading edge of MDA-MB-231 cells is expected to have little or no effect on cell migration velocity, indicating that PI3K/Akt/NHE1 signaling is already maintained at an optimal level. In contrast, under acidic (pH=6.4) conditions, where cell motility is impaired, this optogenetic stimulation is expected to rescue impaired migration and increase cell velocity by promoting PI3K/Akt/NHE1 enrichment at the leading edge. The detailed underlying mechanism and experimental findings supporting these observations are discussed in our related manuscript.1

Quantification and statistical analysis

Images are processed using ImageJ/Fiji, and cell movement is identified using a custom MATLAB script. Velocity values are calculated in Microsoft Excel. The before-stimulation velocity is defined as the average cell velocity over the 4-min period prior to stimulation, whereas the after-stimulation velocity is calculated as the average of the last eight velocity measurements following stimulation. Statistical analysis can be performed in GraphPad Prism. Data distribution is first assessed using normality/lognormality tests, followed by the appropriate test (e.g., t test or Mann-Whitney) for comparison between before- and after-stimulation velocities.

Limitations

The CRY2-CIBN-based optogenetic system can limit the ability to monitor the dynamic spatiotemporal behavior of additional molecules of interest in live cells, as the system requires delivery of two constructs and occupies two fluorescence channels (488 nm and 568 nm). Adding a live-cell reporter introduces a third construct, which may further reduce transfection/transduction efficiency, increase cellular burden, and limit the available fluorophore choices for imaging. One potential strategy to reduce the number of constructs is to use multicistronic vectors that enable expression of two proteins from a single construct, for example, by linking the coding sequences with a self-cleaving 2A peptide.13,14

The relative expression levels of the optogenetic components are also critical for efficient light-induced recruitment.15,16 Although increasing each component expression may increase the absolute amount of recruited protein, it may also enhance basal association, such that higher expression does not necessarily result in greater fold enrichment. Efficient recruitment therefore requires balanced expression of the two components and identification of an optimal expression range through experimental optimization or mathematical modeling. Systematic determination of these optimal expression levels and ratios will require further investigation.

The optimal stimulation setup can vary substantially depending on the cell type, construct delivery efficiency, and confocal microscope performance. As a result, multiple rounds of optimization experiments may be required to determine appropriate parameters such as laser power, stimulation interval, and total stimulation duration. Successful implementation often requires substantial optimization, technical practice, and experimental effort.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

No enrichment may be observed throughout the stimulation period.

Potential solution

This is likely due to cell-to-cell heterogeneity, including low or absent expression of CIBN-GFP-CAAX in some cells. When no enrichment is detected, the stimulation should not be considered successful, and the cell should be excluded from further analysis. If this issue is observed in many cells, or if opto-iSH2 signal intensity is consistently low across the population, consider re-transducing or re-transfecting the cells followed by puromycin/blasticidin selection or fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) to improve construct expression.

Problem 2

Some custom constructs do not show a detectable response after stimulation.

Potential solution

Although opto-iSH2 is a relatively small construct that redistributes rapidly within cells upon stimulation, not all optogenetic constructs respond at the same timescale. For example, we previously developed an optoSWELL1 construct, in which the ion channel SWELL1 is fused to the optogenetic module.4 Owing to the larger size and distinct biological properties of SWELL1, its response time is substantially slower than that of smaller constructs such as opto-iSH2. In such cases, it may be necessary to optimize the stimulation conditions by increasing the total stimulation duration, increasing the stimulation frequency (e.g., from every 30 s to every 10 s), and/or increasing the laser power to achieve robust localized enrichment of SWELL1.

Problem 3

Custom constructs designed based on signaling molecules do not show a consistent trend after stimulation.

Potential solution

Direct recruitment of a signaling molecule does not always produce a robust or reproducible downstream response. In some cases, recruiting an upstream activator is more effective. For example, we initially tested an opto-Akt construct by directly fusing Akt to CRY2-mCherry, but this construct produced a heterogeneous response after stimulation. A likely explanation is that membrane recruitment of Akt does not necessarily result in efficient local generation of its activated, phosphorylated form. We therefore adopted opto-iSH2, which contains the iSH2 region of p85α and recruits the associated p110α catalytic subunit to initiate PI3K signaling and promote downstream Akt phosphorylation. Similarly, optogenetic activation of Cdc42 and Rac1 is often achieved by recruiting their upstream GEFs, such as ITSN1 for Cdc42 and Tiam1 for Rac1, rather than recruiting the GTPases directly. In addition, variability may arise from differences in the local signaling state and feedback regulation. These spatial effects should be considered together with construct design when troubleshooting heterogeneous responses.

Methods video S1. Stimulation process, related to steps 14, 17, 18, and 20

During the first 4 min pre-stimulation period, the ROI was positioned away from the cell to prevent unintended activation. At the end of this pre-stimulation period, the ROI was repositioned to the cell leading edge, stimulation was initiated, and the ROI was continuously adjusted as the cell migrated, ensuring that stimulation was maintained only at the cell leading edge for the subsequent 12 min.
