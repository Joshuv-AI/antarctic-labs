Arnaud Mayeux

Abstract:
We give a formula for
X

\mathbb{F}_{1}

1
Introduction

Tits heuristic idea of the field with one element
\mathbb{F}_{1}
and Deitmar’s monoid-based
\mathbb{F}_{1}
-geometry both aim to describe aspects of algebraic geometry through underlying combinatorial structures.
Algebraic magnetism follows a similar philosophy by encoding geometric dynamics via commutative monoids.
In this note, we answer a purely magnetic question in terms of
\mathbb{F}_{1}
-geometry. This note builds on and complements
[
3
]
. Our starting point is the following question: given submonoids of an abelian group
N_{1},\ldots,N_{d}\subset M
, viewed as magnets for an algebraic action of
D(M)
on an algebraic space
X
, can we understand and describe

X^{N_{1}}\times_{X}\cdots\times_{X}X^{N_{d}}\,?

We provide an answer to this question in the case where
N_{1},\ldots,N_{d}
satisfy
N_{1}^{\mathrm{gp}}=\cdots=N_{d}^{\mathrm{gp}}
. In this situation, the
N_{i}
together give rise to a scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, denoted
\underline{\mathcal{N}}
(Proposition
3.2
), and to a scheme
A(\mathcal{N})
(Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
) endowed with an action of
D(M)
, such that

X^{N_{1}}\times_{X}\cdots\times_{X}X^{N_{d}}\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{D(M)}\bigl(A(\mathcal{N}),X\bigr)~~~~~~\text{ (Theorem \ref{thm:fiberprodmono})}.

We then indicate that the space
X^{\mathcal{N}}:=\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{D(M)}\bigl(A(\mathcal{N}),X\bigr)
enjoys good geometric properties. We also provide examples and relate it to existing constructions in the literature (Example
4.4
).

2
Schemes over
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}

Schemes over
\mathbb{F}_{1}

[
1
]
, also called monoschemes
[
4
]
, are sheaves of monoids on topological spaces that are built locally from commutative monoids. In the following, we recall some main structural definitions of these objects, and refer to
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
for details.

Definition 2.1
.

If
Q
is a monoid, then

a_{Q}:=\mathrm{spec}(Q)

is the (locally) monoidal space consisting of the set of prime ideals of

Q
, endowed with the Zariski topology and the sheaf of monoids defined in
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A monoidal space is a pair
(X,{\mathcal{M}}_{X})
, where
X
is a topological
space and
{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
is a sheaf of commutative monoids on
X.
A morphism of
monoidal spaces

(f,f^{\sharp}):(X,{\mathcal{M}}_{X}),(Y,{\mathcal{M}}_{Y})

is a pair
(f,f^{\sharp})
, where
f:X\to Y
is a continuous function and

f^{\sharp}:f^{-1}({\mathcal{M}}_{Y})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{X}

is a homomorphism of sheaves of monoids such that for each point
x\in X
,
the stalk
f_{x}^{\sharp}:{\mathcal{M}}_{Y,f(x)}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{X,x}
of
f^{\sharp}
at
x
is a local homomorphism of monoids.

Definition 2.3
.

A locally monoidal space is affine if it is isomorphic to

\mathrm{spec}(Q)
for some monoid
Q.

A monoscheme (or a scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{1}
) is a locally monoidal space that
admits an open cover by affines, and the category of monoschemes is the full
subcategory of the category of locally monoidal spaces consisting of such
spaces.

Given a scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, say
X
, one can associate to it a scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
(cf.
[
1
]
). This construction is denoted by

X\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}\mathbb{Z},

and may be viewed as an extension of scalars from
\mathbb{F}_{1}
to
\mathbb{Z}
.
On affines, the construction is particularly simple. If

X=\operatorname{spec}(Q)

for a commutative monoid
Q
, then

X\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}\mathbb{Z}\;=\;\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[Q]),

where
\mathbb{Z}[Q]
denotes the monoid algebra of
Q
over
\mathbb{Z}
. The general construction is obtained by gluing these affine pieces.

3
Monoids,
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}
-schemes and schemes

If
N
is a commutative monoid, recall that
A(N)={\operatorname{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}}[N])

denotes the diagonalizable monoid scheme over
{\mathbb{Z}}
attached to it
[
3
]
. If
N
is moreover a group,
A(N)
is endowed with a group scheme structure denoted
D(N)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated abelian group.
Let
{\mathcal{N}}=\{N_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}
be a set of fine submonoids of
M
such that
N_{\tau}^{gp}=N_{\tau^{\prime}}^{gp}=:{\mathcal{N}}^{gp}
for all
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}
. Note that for all
\tau\in\mathscr{A}
,
D({\mathcal{N}}^{gp})\to A(N_{\tau})
is an open immersion (cf. e.g.
[
4
, p67]
).

Proposition 3.1

The diagram

D(\mathcal{N}^{{gp}})\longrightarrow A(N_{\tau}),\qquad\tau\in\mathscr{A},

admits a colimit in the category of schemes; this colimit is denoted by
A(\mathcal{N})
.

Proof.

Since each
D(\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{gp}})\to A(N_{\tau})
is an open immersion, the schemes
A(N_{\tau})
glue along the common open subscheme
D(\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{gp}})
; by the standard universal property of gluing along open immersions, this glued scheme is the colimit of the diagram.
∎

Proposition 3.2

There exists a scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, denoted
\underline{{\mathcal{N}}}
such that
\underline{{\mathcal{N}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}{\mathbb{Z}}\cong A({\mathcal{N}})
.

Proof.

Define
\underline{\mathcal{N}}
by gluing the affine monoschemes
\operatorname{spec}(N_{\tau})
along the common open subspace
\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{gp}})
. This is well-defined since all
N_{\tau}
have the same group completion. Hence
\underline{\mathcal{N}}
is a monoscheme, i.e. a scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{1}
.
Applying base change
N\mapsto\mathbb{Z}[N]
(cf.
[
1
]
), localization of monoids commutes with monoid algebras, so the gluing diagram becomes the one defining
A(\mathcal{N})
. Therefore

\underline{\mathcal{N}}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}\mathbb{Z}\cong A(\mathcal{N}).

∎

We now put
Z={\mathcal{N}}^{gp}\subset M
. For each
\tau
, the morphism
D(Z)\to A(N_{\tau})
is
D(Z)
-equivariant. We thus obtain an action of
D(Z)
on
A({\mathcal{N}})
.

Proposition 3.3

The diagram

D(\mathcal{N}^{{gp}})\longrightarrow A(N_{\tau}),\qquad\tau\in\mathscr{A},

admits a colimit in the category of schemes with a
D(M)
-action. This colimit is isomorphic to
A(\mathcal{N})
as scheme.

Proof.

Each
A(N_{\tau})
carries a natural action of
D(M)
(since
N_{\tau}\subset M
), and the morphisms

D(Z)\to A(N_{\tau})

are
D(M)
-equivariant.
The gluings along
D(Z)
are compatible with the
D(M)
-actions, hence
A(\mathcal{N})
inherits a
D(M)
-action such that all maps

A(N_{\tau})\to A(\mathcal{N})

are
D(M)
-equivariant.
Now let
X
be a
D(M)
-scheme and suppose given
D(M)
-equivariant morphisms

f_{\tau}:A(N_{\tau})\to X

compatible on
D(Z)
. By the universal property in schemes, they glue to a unique morphism

f:A(\mathcal{N})\to X.

Since the
f_{\tau}
are
D(M)
-equivariant and the action on
A(\mathcal{N})
is defined by gluing, it follows that
f
is
D(M)
-equivariant.
Thus
A(\mathcal{N})
satisfies the required universal property in the category of
D(M)
-schemes.
∎

4
X
-product of attractors

We proceed with the setting of the previous section.
We work over
S={\operatorname{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}})
. We use the notation of
[
3
]
, in particular, in the following, the notation
X^{N}
means
\underline{{\operatorname{Hom}}}^{D(M)}(A(N),X)
(i.e. the functor
(T\to S)\mapsto{\operatorname{Hom}}_{T}^{D(M)_{T}}(A(N)_{T},X_{T})
).

Theorem 4.1

Let
X
be an algebraic space endowed with an action of
D(M)
. Assume that one of the following conditions holds

1.

X
is quasi-separated over
S
and
D(M/Z)
has connected fibers (e.g.
Z=M
), or

2.

X
is separated.

Then

{\prod_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}}{}_{X}X^{N_{\tau}}=...\times_{X}X^{N_{\tau}}\times_{X}X^{N_{\tau^{\prime}}}\times_{X}...=\underline{{\operatorname{Hom}}}^{D(M)}(A({\mathcal{N}}),X).

Proof.

Let
T\to S
be a test scheme. For each
\tau
, let
g_{\tau}:D(M)\to A(N_{\tau})
be the morphism of diagonalizable monoid schemes dual to
N_{\tau}\subset M
, and let
h_{\tau}:D(Z)\to A(N_{\tau})
be the morphism of diagonalizable monoid schemes dual to
N_{\tau}\subset Z
. Similarly, let
k:D(M)\to D(Z)
be the morphism dual to
Z\subset M
. Since
X^{M}\cong X
(cf.
[
3
]
), we have:

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Hom}}(T,...\times_{X}X^{N_{\tau}}\times_{X}X^{N_{\tau^{\prime}}}\times_{X}...)\cong{\operatorname{Hom}}(T,...\times_{X^{M}}X^{N_{\tau}}\times_{X^{M}}X^{N_{\tau^{\prime}}}\times_{X}...)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\{\{f_{\tau}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}^{D(M)_{T}}(A(N_{\tau})_{T},X_{T})\}_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}|f_{\tau}\circ g_{\tau}=f_{\tau^{\prime}}\circ g_{\tau^{\prime}}~\forall\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}\}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\{\{f_{\tau}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}^{D(M)_{T}}(A(N_{\tau})_{T},X_{T})\}_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}|f_{\tau}\circ h_{\tau}\circ k=f_{\tau^{\prime}}\circ h_{\tau^{\prime}}\circ k~\forall\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}\}.

We now prove that we have

\{\{f_{\tau}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}^{D(M)_{T}}(A(N_{\tau})_{T},X_{T})\}_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}|f_{\tau}\circ h_{\tau}\circ k=f_{\tau^{\prime}}\circ h_{\tau^{\prime}}\circ k~\forall\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}\}

\cong\{\{f_{\tau}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}^{D(M)_{T}}(A(N_{\tau})_{T},X_{T})\}_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}|f_{\tau}\circ h_{\tau}=f_{\tau^{\prime}}\circ h_{\tau^{\prime}}~\forall\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}\}.

It is enough to prove that
X^{Z}\to X=X^{M}
is a monomorphism.
If
X
is separated over
S
, this is proved in
[
3
]
.
If
X
is quasi-separated over
S
and
D(M/Z)
has connected fibers, it is enough to observe that
X^{Z}=X^{D(M/Z)}
(cf.
[
3
, 3.33]
) and apply
[
3
, Theorem 6.8]
.
Now Proposition
3.3
implies that

\{\{f_{\tau}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}^{D(M)_{T}}(A(N_{\tau})_{T},X_{T})\}_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}|f_{\tau}\circ h_{\tau}=f_{\tau^{\prime}}\circ h_{\tau^{\prime}}~\forall\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}\}\cong{{\operatorname{Hom}}}^{D(M)_{T}}(A({\mathcal{N}})_{T},X_{T}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

The functor
\underline{{\operatorname{Hom}}}^{D(M)}(A({\mathcal{N}}),X)
is denoted
X^{\mathcal{N}}
and is called the attractor associated to
\underline{{\mathcal{N}}}
.

Proposition 4.3

Assume that one of the following conditions holds

1.

X\to S
is affine, or

2.

the set
\mathscr{A}
is finite, each
X^{N_{\tau}}
is representable, and one of the following conditions holds

(a)

X
is quasi-separated over
S
and
D(M/Z)
has connected fibers (e.g.
Z=M
), or

(b)

X
is separated.

then
X^{\mathcal{N}}
is representable by an algebraic space over
S
.

Moreover, if
X\to S
is affine, then
X^{\mathcal{N}}=X^{\cap_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}N_{\tau}}
.

Proof.

If
X\to S
is affine, then each
X^{N_{\tau}}
is representable by a closed subscheme
V(J_{\tau})
of
X={\operatorname{Spec}}(A)
where the ideal
J_{\tau}\subset A
is given explictly in
[
3
]
, namely
J_{\tau}=\langle A_{m}|m\in M\setminus N_{\tau}\rangle
. This explicit description of
J_{\tau}
implies that
X^{\mathcal{N}}(\cong{\operatorname{Spec}}(\otimes_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}~A}~A/J_{\tau})\cong{\operatorname{Spec}}(A/(\sum_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}J_{\tau}))
equals
X^{\cap_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}N_{\tau}}
and is representable.
In the second case, by
[
3
]
, each
X^{N_{\tau}}
is representable. Since
\mathscr{A}
is finite, Theorem
4.1
implies that
X^{\mathcal{N}}
is representable.
∎

Example 4.4
.

The formula
X^{\mathcal{N}}=X^{\cap_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}N_{\tau}}
does not extend to the non-affine case.
Let
M={\mathbb{Z}}
,
Z={\mathbb{Z}}
,
N_{\tau}=\tau{\mathbb{N}}~\tau\in\{+,-\}
).
Let
{\mathcal{N}}
be
\{N_{\tau}=\tau{\mathbb{N}}|\tau\in\{+,-\}\}
.
Then
A({\mathcal{N}})\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Let us take
X=A({\mathcal{N}})
.
We have
X^{{\mathcal{N}}}\cong X~~\not\cong~~X^{-{\mathbb{N}}\cap+{\mathbb{N}}}\cong\{0,\infty\}
.
Example of attractors of the form
Y^{\mathcal{N}}
where
{\mathcal{N}}=\{N_{\tau}=\tau{\mathbb{N}}|\tau\in\{+,-\}\}
appear in
[
2
, §1.9, Proposition 1.9.4, Remark 1.9.5]
.

Proposition 4.5

Assume that
\mathscr{A}
is finite.
If
X/S
is separated, then
X^{\mathcal{N}}\to X
is a monomorphism.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
\#\mathscr{A}
. If
\#\mathscr{A}=1
, this is proved in
[
3
]
. Otherwise we have
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A^{\prime}}\sqcup\{\tau\}
with
\tau\in\mathscr{A}
.
We have
X^{{\mathcal{N}}}\cong X^{{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}}\times_{X}X^{N_{\tau}}
. By induction,
X^{{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}}\to X
is a monomorphism, so by
[
5
,
Tag 042P
]
,
X^{{\mathcal{N}}}\cong X^{{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}}\times_{X}X^{N_{\tau}}\to X^{N_{\tau}}
is a monomorphism. By
[
3
]
,
X^{N_{\tau}}\to X
is a monomorphism. So by
[
5
,
Tag 042O
]
,
X^{\mathcal{N}}\to X
is a monomorphism.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The interpretation of
X^{\mathcal{N}}
via schemes over
\mathbb{F}_{1}
implies smoothness results (via formal smoothness as in
[
3
]
). Assume that
\mathscr{A}
is finite.
The formula
X^{\mathcal{N}}=\underline{{\operatorname{Hom}}}^{D(M)}(A({\mathcal{N}}),X)
, implies that if
X\to Y
is a smooth morphism then
X^{\mathcal{N}}\to Y^{\mathcal{N}}
is smooth (under the same assumptions on
X
and
Y
as in
[
3
]
). Indeed it is enough to use
[
3
, §10]
and adapt
[
3
, §11]
(replace
A(N)
by
A({\mathcal{N}})
). In particular
X^{\mathcal{N}}\to S
is smooth if
X\to S
is smooth. The smoothness of
X^{\mathcal{N}}
does not follow immediately from the formula
X^{\mathcal{N}}=X^{N_{1}}\times_{X}\ldots\times_{X}X^{N_{d}}
and the smoothness of
X^{N_{k}}
already established in
[
3
]
.
Indeed, if
X_{1}\to S
,
X_{2}\to S
, and
X_{3}\to S
are smooth morphisms and
X_{1}\to X_{2},X_{3}\to X_{2}
are
S
-morphisms, it does not follow that
X_{1}\times_{X_{2}}X_{3}
is smooth over
S
. For example, let
X_{2}=\mathbb{A}^{2}
,
X_{1}=V(y)
, and
X_{3}=V(y-x^{2})
. Then
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
are smooth, but

X_{1}\times_{X_{2}}X_{3}=V(y,y-x^{2})=V(y,x^{2})\simeq\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{Z}}[x]/(x^{2})),

which is not smooth.
