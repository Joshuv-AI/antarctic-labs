Abstract
. An r r -rooted (possibly infinite) digraph D = ( V , E ) D=(V,E) is a flame if for every v ∈ V ∖ { r } v\in V\setminus\{r\} there exists a set of edge-disjoint paths from r r to v v in D D that covers all ingoing edges of v v . Flames were first studied by Lovász in his investigation of edge-minimal subgraphs of a rooted digraph that preserve all the local edge-connectivities from the root. He showed that these subgraphs are always flames. Szeszlér later proved a common generalisation of Lovász’ result and Edmonds’ disjoint arborescence theorem. In this paper we focus on infinite flames and prove the following constructive characterisation. Every (possibly infinite) flame can be constructed transfinitely, starting from the empty edge set and adding a single edge at each step in such a way that every intermediate digraph is again a flame.

1.
Introduction

An
r
-rooted digraph is a (possibly infinite) digraph with a prescribed root vertex
r
that has no ingoing edges. A flame is an
r
-rooted digraph

D=(V,E)
such that for every
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
there exists a set of edge-disjoint paths from
r
to
v
that covers all ingoing edges of

v
.
Recall that the local edge-connectivity
\lambda_{D}(u,v)
is the maximal number of edge-disjoint paths from
u
to
v
.
Lovász and
Calvillo-Vives independently investigated the edge-minimal subgraphs of finite rooted digraphs preserving all the local edge-connectivities from
the
root. Let
D=(V,E)
be a finite
r
-rooted digraph and suppose that

F\subseteq E
is
minimal with respect to the property that for the spanning
subdigraph
D(F):=(V,F)
we have
\lambda_{D(F)}(r,v)=\lambda_{D}(r,v)
for each
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
. Clearly,
F
must contain at least

\lambda_{D}(r,v)
ingoing edges of
v
for
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
. Lovász and
Calvillo-Vives showed (see
[
5
, Theorem 2]
and
[
2
]
) independently that
F
contains

exactly

\lambda_{D}(r,v)
ingoing edges of
v
for each
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
. In other
words, every spanning subdigraph that preserves all the local edge-connectivities from the root and is edge-minimal with respect to this property is
a
flame. Recall that an
arborescence is a directed rooted tree in which every vertex is reachable from the root by a directed path. The union of vertex-disjoint
arborescences is called a branching. Szeszlér later proved
the following strengthening of the theorem of Lovász and Calvillo-Vives:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
6
, Theorem 8]
)
.

Let
D=(V,E)
be a finite
r
-rooted digraph and
m:=\max_{v\in V\setminus\{r\}}\lambda_{D}(r,v)
. Then there are

F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{m}\subseteq E
such that for every
i\in[m]
:
D_{i}:=(V,F_{i})
is a flame with

\lambda_{D_{i}}(r,v)=\min\{\lambda_{D}(r,v),i\}
for
v\in V\setminus\{r\}

and
F_{i}\setminus F_{i-1}
forms a branching (where
F_{0}:=\emptyset
).

Note that the flame
D_{m}
preserves all the local edge-connectivities from the root. Furthermore, if

k:=\min_{v\in V\setminus\{r\}}\lambda_{D}(r,v)
, then for every
i\in[k]
,
F_{i}\setminus F_{i-1}
must form a spanning arborescence. The latter is the classical
‘disjoint arborescence’ theorem of Edmonds (see
[
3
]
). Theorem
1.1
, when applied to flames, allows us to
construct flames as increasing chains of certain subflames. In this paper we prove that for every (possibly infinite) flame it is possible to find an
increasing
chain of
subflames growing by a single edge in each step. More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

For every flame
D=(V,E)
, there exists a well-order

<
on
E
of order type
\left|E\right|
such that for every subset
F\subseteq E
that corresponds to an initial segment of
<
, the spanning subdigraph
(V,F)
is
a flame.

The special case of Theorem
1.2
where
E
is finite follows from our previous result that the edge sets of the
flame subgraphs of a finite rooted digraph form a greedoid (see
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
). The general case requires more
sophisticated tools, mainly because an infinite flame may admit a proper subflame with exactly the same edge-connectivities from the root, which
renders our key lemma
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
ineffective in the infinite setting.

The paper is organized as follows. In the following section we introduce our terminology. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of the
lemmas
we need. Finally, in Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Notation and terminology

The digraphs

considered in this paper may have parallel edges but no loops.
For

.
If an edge

For

A

together with a specified vertex

.
If a rooted digraph

is fixed, then, to simplify notation, we identify any set
F\subseteq E
with the spanning subdigraph

, which
is considered to have the same root
r
.

If

and

Its vertex set is

.
Edges whose head and tail both lie in

and head in

have

Edges with head in

and tail in

have

as their new head and keep their original tail.
Edges with neither endpoint in

keep their original head and tail.
If

is rooted, then the resulting digraph is rooted at the same vertex.
Moreover, if vertices are contracted to the root
r
, then any resulting ingoing edges of

are deleted.

By a
path

P
we always mean a finite directed path, and we identify paths with their edge sets.
An
XY
-path is a path that starts in
X
, terminates in
Y
, and has no internal vertices in
X\cup Y
.
The initial and terminal segments of a path

are the subpaths of
P
that share, respectively, the first and the last edge of
P
.
For singletons we omit brackets; for example, we write
\delta_{D}(v)
instead of

and

-path instead of

-path.

For a rooted digraph
D
, we define

of edge-disjoint
rv
-paths in
D
that covers
I
.
A rooted digraph
D=(V,E)
is a

if
\delta_{D}(v)\in\mathcal{G}_{D}(v)
for every
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
.
A
transversal
of a set
\mathcal{P}
of edge-disjoint paths is a set of edges containing exactly one edge from each path in
\mathcal{P}
.

For a nonempty family
\mathcal{F}
of sets, we write
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
and
\bigcap\mathcal{F}
for the union and the intersection of the
sets in
\mathcal{F}
, respectively. Let

denote
\{1,2,\dots,k\}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
The variables
\alpha
,
\beta
, and

denote ordinal numbers.
The order type of a well-order is the unique ordinal

such that
(\alpha,\in)
is isomorphic to it.
The cardinality

of a set

is the smallest ordinal
\alpha
such that

\alpha
.

. Since
e\notin\bigcup\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, we must have

e\notin\delta_{D^{\prime}}(X)
, and thus
u\in X
. The initial segments of the paths in

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
up to their first edges in
X
witness that
X
is fillable in
D^{\prime}
.
Since
T\supseteq X
is fillable in
D
, it follows that
X
is fillable in
D
as well.
Finally,
X
must be a proper subset of
T
, because the heads of ingoing edges of
T

that are not used by
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
cannot lie in
X
. This contradicts the fact that
T

is the smallest fillable set containing
\{u,v\}
.

∎

Corollary 3.11
.

If
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
and
e\in\delta_{D}(T_{v,D-e})
, then
T_{v,D-e}
is fillable in

D
(and not only in
D-e
).

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that this is not the case. Let
D^{\prime}
be the auxiliary
digraph obtained from
D
by contracting
T_{v,D-e}=:T
to
v
. To obtain a contradiction,
we will apply Lemma
3.10
to find a strictly larger
v
-tight set,
contradicting the maximality of
T_{v,D-e}
.

By construction,
\delta_{D^{\prime}-e}(v)=\delta_{D-e}(T)
. Since
T_{v,D-e}
is fillable in

D-e
by Observation
3.3
, we have

\delta_{D^{\prime}-e}(v)\in\mathcal{G}_{D^{\prime}-e}(v)
. By our assumption,

\delta_{D^{\prime}}(v)\notin\mathcal{G}_{D^{\prime}}(v)
, and therefore
Lemma
3.10
yields a
v
-tight set
T^{\prime}
in
D^{\prime}-e
that contains the
tail
u
of
e
.

Let
T^{\prime\prime}
be the vertex set in
D
corresponding to
T^{\prime}
, obtained by expanding
the vertex
v\in T^{\prime}
to the set
T
. Then
T^{\prime\prime}\supsetneq T
, since

u\in T^{\prime\prime}\setminus T
. To obtain a contradiction, we show that
T^{\prime\prime}
is tight in
D-e
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a path system in
D-e
witnessing

\delta_{D-e}(v)\in\mathcal{G}_{D-e}(v)
. Since
T
is
v
-tight in
D-e
, the set

\delta_{D-e}(T)=\delta_{D^{\prime}-e}(v)
is a transversal of
\mathcal{P}
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}

consist of the initial segments of the paths in
\mathcal{P}
up to their first edges in

\delta_{D-e}(T)
. Then
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a system of edge-disjoint paths in
D^{\prime}-e

witnessing
\delta_{D^{\prime}-e}(v)\in\mathcal{G}_{D^{\prime}-e}(v)
.

Since
T^{\prime}
is tight in
D^{\prime}-e
, the set
\delta_{D^{\prime}-e}(T^{\prime})
is a transversal of

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. As
\delta_{D^{\prime}-e}(T^{\prime})=\delta_{D-e}(T^{\prime\prime})
, it follows that

\delta_{D-e}(T^{\prime\prime})
is a transversal of
\mathcal{P}
. Hence
T^{\prime\prime}
is tight in
D-e
,
which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

If
F\subseteq E
is a flame,
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
, and
e\in E\setminus F
such that

e\in\delta_{D}(T_{v,F})
, then
F\cup\{e\}
is a flame.

Proof.

Let
v
be the head of
e
. By Corollary
3.11
(applied to

F\cup\{e\}
), the set
T_{v,F}
is fillable in
F\cup\{e\}
. Fix a path system

\mathcal{P}
witnessing this, and let
\mathcal{Q}
be a path system witnessing

\delta_{F}(v)\in\mathcal{G}_{F}(v)
. Let
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
consist of the terminal segments of

\mathcal{Q}
starting from their last edges in

.

For each
Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
, unite

with the unique path
P_{Q}\in\mathcal{P}
whose

, and also include the unique path

.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Assume that
D=(V,E)
is a flame. For each
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
, let

\mathcal{P}_{v}=\{P_{e}:e\in\delta_{D}(v)\}

be a path system witnessing
\delta_{D}(v)\in\mathcal{G}_{D}(v)
, and let

\mathcal{P}:=\bigcup_{v\in V\setminus\{r\}}\mathcal{P}_{v}
. Note that for each
e\in E
there is a unique path
P_{e}\in\mathcal{P}
whose last
edge is
e
. A set
F\subseteq E
is called
\mathcal{P}
-respecting
if
P_{e}\subseteq F
for
every
e\in F
. Every
\mathcal{P}
-respecting set
F
is a flame, since for each

v\in V\setminus\{r\}
the paths
\{P_{e}:e\in\delta_{F}(v)\}
lie in
F
and witness
\delta_{F}(v)\in\mathcal{G}_{F}(v)
.

Observation 4.1
.

The union of a
\subseteq
-chain of
\mathcal{P}
-respecting flames is again a

\mathcal{P}
-respecting flame.

We call a set
F\subseteq E

almost
\mathcal{P}
-respecting
if there are only
finitely many edges
e\in F
such that
P_{e}\not\subseteq F
. An almost

\mathcal{P}
-respecting set is not necessarily a flame.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
F
is an almost
\mathcal{P}
-respecting flame and
e\in E\setminus F
, then there
exists a finite set
\{e_{i}:i\leq n\}\subseteq E\setminus F
containing
e
such that

F\cup\{e_{i}:i\leq m\}
is a flame for every
m\leq n
.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that the statement is false, and let the quadruple

(D,\mathcal{P},F,e)
be a counterexample. Let
u
and
v
be the tail and head of
e
,
respectively. Define

A:=\{e\}\cup\{f\in\delta_{F}(v):P_{f}\not\subseteq F\}\quad\text{and}\quad A^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{f\in A}P_{f}.

Since
F
is almost
\mathcal{P}
-respecting,
A
is finite and thus so is
A^{\prime}
. We assume
that the counterexample is chosen so that

n(D,\mathcal{P},F,e):=\lvert A^{\prime}\setminus F\rvert

is minimal.

To obtain a contradiction, it suffices to show that there exists an edge

g\in A^{\prime}\setminus F
such that
F\cup\{g\}
is a flame. Indeed, we must then have

g\neq e
, since
(D,\mathcal{P},F,e)
is a counterexample. Furthermore, replacing
F
by

F\cup\{g\}
yields another counterexample with

n(D,\mathcal{P},F\cup\{g\},e)=n(D,\mathcal{P},F,e)-1,

contradicting the minimality of
n(D,\mathcal{P},F,e)
.

By Lemma
3.10
, the set
T_{v,F}=:T
contains
u
. By
Corollary
3.12
, for any edge
g\in\delta_{D}(T)\setminus F
, the digraph

F\cup\{g\}
is a flame. Thus it remains to show that

\delta_{D}(T)\cap(A^{\prime}\setminus F)\neq\emptyset.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\delta_{D}(T)\cap(A^{\prime}\setminus F)=\emptyset
. Then for
every
f\in A
we have
P_{f}\cap\delta_{D}(T)\subseteq F
. Note that the sets
P_{f}\cap\delta_{D}(T)=P_{f}\cap\delta_{F}(T)
for
f\in A

are nonempty and pairwise disjoint. Therefore,

\left|\bigcup_{f\in A}P_{f}\cap\delta_{F}(T)\right|\geq|A|,

and hence
|\delta_{F}(T)\cap A^{\prime}|\geq|A|
.

Let

\mathcal{Q}:=\{P_{f}:f\in\delta_{F}(v)\setminus(A\setminus\{e\})\}.

On the one hand,
\mathcal{Q}
covers all but
|A|-1
ingoing edges of
v
in
F
. On the
other hand, there are at least
|A|
edges in

\delta_{F}(T)
that are not used by
\mathcal{Q}
because
\bigcup_{f\in A}P_{f}\cap\delta_{F}(T)
consists of such edges. This contradicts
Corollary
3.9
applied to
F
and the terminal segments of the paths in
\mathcal{Q}
from their last edges in
\delta_{F}(T)
.
∎

Let
\prec
be a well-order of
E
of order type
|E|
. The special case of
Theorem
1.2
where
E
is countable follows from
Lemma
4.2
by a straightforward recursion.

Indeed, suppose that a finite flame

F_{k}=\{f_{0},\dots,f_{m}\}\subseteq E

has already been defined. Since finite flames are clearly
\mathcal{P}
-respecting, we
may apply Lemma
4.2
to
F_{k}
and the
\prec
-least element
e
of

E\setminus F_{k}
. Let
\{e_{i}:i\leq n\}
be the resulting set. We define

f_{m+i}:=e_{i}
and

F_{k+1}:=\{f_{0},\dots,f_{m},\dots,f_{m+n}\}.

The recursion is complete, and the enumeration

F=\{f_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}

Now suppose that
E
is uncountable and let
\kappa:=|E|
. We require the following

Lemma 4.3
.

If
F\subseteq E
is
\mathcal{P}
-respecting and

, then there exists
a set
\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq E\setminus F

\mathcal{P}
-respecting.

Proof.

The lemma follows by iteratively applying Lemma
4.2
together with

(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of almost
\mathcal{P}
-respecting flames such that:

(1)

F_{0}=F
;

(2)

e\in F_{1}
;

(3)

\mathcal{P}
-respecting.

Let
\{e^{1}_{1},\dots,e^{1}_{k_{1}}\}
be obtained by applying Lemma
4.2
to
F

and
e
, and set
F_{1}:=F_{0}\cup\{e^{1}_{1},\dots,e^{1}_{k_{1}}\}
. Suppose
F_{n}
is defined for

. If
F_{n}
is
\mathcal{P}
-respecting, we set
F_{m}:=F_{n}
for all

with

.
Choose
f_{n}
such that the unique index
m_{n}
with

Apply Lemma
4.2
to

, and
