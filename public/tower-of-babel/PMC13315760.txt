Single-dose dual-indication nanoparticle vaccine co-displaying Pseudomonas aeruginosa antigen and tetanus toxin fragment confers durable protection against mixed exposure
Authors: Zifan Zhu, Jinqiong Yan, Jiqing Wan, Meilin Wu, Ziyi Liao, Xiao Peng, Yating Wang, Yuhang Li, Ying Wang, Xinglong Liu, Bin Wang, Quanming Zou, Jing Zhu, Jiang Gu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTrauma, disaster, and conflict-related injuries can create clinically important co-exposure to Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA) and tetanus toxin, where disrupted barriers and contaminated wounds enable simultaneous bacterial infection and toxin entry. Until now no licensed PA vaccine exists and multi-dose schedules are difficult to complete in emergency settings, thus a single-dose, dual-indication vaccine that provides rapid and durable protection is urgently needed. Here, we engineered and characterized a lumazine synthase (LS) nanoparticle vaccine (rePO-TTFC@LS) that co-displays a PA antigen (rePO; PcrV-OprI fusion) and tetanus toxin fragment C (TTFC). In BALB/c mice, rePO-TTFC@LS enhanced antigen uptake and dendritic-cell activation relative to soluble antigens. After a single intramuscular immunization, rePO-TTFC@LS elicited rapid, high anti-rePO and anti-TTFC IgG responses together with increased IFN-γ/IL-4/IL-17A-secreting splenocytes. Vaccination reduced lung bacterial burden and pulmonary IL-1β/IL-6 and improved survival and clinical outcomes in an acute PA pneumonia model, which provided complete protection against lethal tetanus toxin challenge, and decreased PA colonization with improved survival in a femoral fracture-implant co-infection model. Elevated antibody titers and dual protection persisted to 28 weeks after one dose, and biosafety assessments showed no meaningful hemolysis or cytotoxicity and no abnormalities in hematology/serum biochemistry or multiorgan histopathology. Collectively, these results support rePO-TTFC@LS as a single-dose, durable dual-indication nanoparticle vaccine and highlight a modular strategy for combination vaccines suited to mixed-exposure emergency scenarios.Keywords: Bivalent nanoparticle vaccine, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Tetanus toxin C-Fragment (TTFC), Lumazine synthase scaffold, Single-dose durable protection

Introduction

Trauma, surgical procedures, and device implantation frequently expose patients to environmental pathogens while disrupting epithelial and mucosal barriers, creating conditions in which pulmonary and wound infections can emerge and progress rapidly [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. Among bacterial threats, Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA) is particularly problematic owing to intrinsic antimicrobial resistance, robust biofilm formation on airway and implant surfaces, and its association with severe pneumonia and hardware-related infections [5,6]. Notably, PA is among the predominant pathogens isolated from wound infections in conflict and disaster setting [[7], [8], [9], [10]]. In parallel, contaminated traumatic wounds can permit entry of tetanus toxin; globally, tetanus accounts for approximately 1 million cases annually with case-fatality rates of 30-50%, and up to 100% in certain high-risk populations [11,12]. These dual hazards underscore the need for vaccination strategies that rapidly induce neutralizing antitoxin antibodies while providing broad, durable protection against bacterial infection, ideally deliverable as a single-dose when follow-up is uncertain.

Despite more than 60 PA vaccine candidates having entered development, none has yet achieved licensure [13]. PcrV, a pivotal component of the type III secretion system (T3SS), resides at the needle tip and mediates translocation of effector toxins into host cells [14,15]. OprI is a highly conserved outer-membrane lipoprotein that, together with other porins, contributes to nutrient transport and membrane integrity in PA [16]. In a previous study, researchers fused PcrV with OprI to generate a PA subunit vaccine rePO that, after a three-dose schedule, conferred robust protection in both an acute PA pneumonia model and a post-fracture PA infection model in mice [17,18]. Conventional tetanus vaccines are produced by formalin-detoxifying tetanus toxin and adsorbing the resulting toxoid onto aluminum adjuvant; although effective, immunization can be associated with mild adverse reactions and residual safety concerns [12,19,20]. By contrast, the recombinant C-terminal fragment of tetanus toxin (TTFC) elicits immune responses comparable to toxoid-based vaccines while markedly reducing safety risks [21].

Subunit vaccines provide favorable safety profiles and streamlined manufacturing but typically depend on adjuvants and multi-dose schedules to achieve robust protection. Nanoparticle scaffolds help overcome these limitations by increasing antigen valency, enhancing uptake by antigen-presenting cells, and promoting efficient trafficking to lymphoid tissues [[22], [23], [24]]. For example, the lumazine synthase (LS) nanoparticle has been widely used to construct dendritic-cell vaccines, influenza vaccines such as M2e-LS, and HIV-related candidates, including an LS-displayed gp120 vaccine that has advanced to a phase I clinical trial (NCT05414786) [[25], [26], [27]]. Building on this platform, we employed a modular LS nanoparticle together with the SpyTag/SpyCatcher covalent ligation system to co-display two rationally selected antigens—rePO and TTFC [[28], [29], [30]]. The resulting bivalent particle, rePO-TTFC@LS, enables successful co-assembly of bacterial and toxoid antigens on a single self-assembling scaffold while maintaining stability and manufacturability.

Here, we describe the design and physicochemical characterization of rePO-TTFC@LS. We then evaluate immunogenicity in mice, quantifying antigen-specific IgG kinetics, as well as T-cell associated cytokine responses. To establish functional relevance, we assess protection in PA pulmonary infection, tetanus toxin challenge, and an orthopedic fracture–implant co-infection model that reflects real-world contamination scenarios.

Methods

Ethics statement

All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines and approved by the Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of the Army Medical University (No. AMUWEC20250100).

Plasmids, cell lines, and bacteria

The lumazine synthase (LS) nanoparticle scaffold was engineered by genetic fusion (Fig. 1a). The coding sequence for SpyCatcher was fused to the 5′ end of the LS open reading frame to generate pET-21a (+) SpyCatcher-Lumazine Synthase (LS). Building on this backbone, the coding sequence for the recombinant PA rePO (PcrV–OprI fusion) was fused to the LS sequence to construct pET-21a (+) SpyCatcher-PcrV-OprI-Lumazine Synthase (rePO@LS). Plasmids encoding SpyTag-fused antigens—pET-28a (+) TTFC-SpyTag (reTTFC) and pET-28a (+) SpyCatcher-PcrV-OprI-SpyTag (rePO)—were synthesized by GenScript (Nanjing, China).

Protein expression, purification, and conjugation reactions

Bacterial cultures harboring the expression plasmids were grown in LB broth supplemented with the appropriate antibiotic and induced overnight with 200 μM IPTG. Cells were harvested by centrifugation, lysed by probe sonication on ice, and clarified lysates were subjected to Ni–NTA affinity chromatography to obtain purified proteins. Purified reTTFC, LS, and rePO@LS were diluted to equal concentrations, then mixed in defined molar ratios of rePO@LS (or LS) to reTTFC at 1:1, 2:1, 3:1, 1:2, and 1:3 in separate tubes; PBS was added to equalize final volumes. Reactions were incubated at 4°C without agitation, and aliquots were taken at 6, 12, 24, and 48 h for SDS-PAGE to assess conjugation under each condition. Band intensities for LS, rePO@LS, reTTFC, reTTFC@LS, and rePO-TTFC@LS were quantified using ImageJ, and conjugation efficiency was calculated from grayscale density values for each condition.

Characterization of vaccine nanoparticles

Hydrodynamic diameter and zeta potential of LS, rePO@LS, reTTFC@LS, and rePO-TTFC@LS were measured on a Zetasizer Nano after diluting samples to appropriate concentrations; measurements were performed at 25°C in water as the dispersant, with three technical replicates per sample. Thermal stability was evaluated on a NanoTemper Tycho NT.6, reporting the inflection temperature (Ti) for protein unfolding, with three replicates per construct. Size-exclusion chromatography was performed on an ÄKTA avant system using a Superose 6 Increase 10/300 GL column at 0.5 mL/min with PBS as the mobile phase; elution volumes were recorded to estimate oligomeric state. For negative-stain TEM, samples were diluted to 0.01 mg/mL, applied to glow-discharged carbon-coated copper grids, stained with 1% phosphotungstic acid, and imaged to visualize particle morphology. Double antibody sandwich ELISA was used to detect rePO-TTFC@LS. Anti-LS antibody served as the capture antibody, and the following antigens were tested: LS, rePO, reTTFC, rePO@LS, reTTFC@LS, and rePO-TTFC@LS. HRP-labeled detection antibodies specific to the LS scaffold, rePO, or reTTFC were then applied independently. The absorbance was measured at OD450, and the background signal at OD690 was subtracted.

Animal immunization

SPF BALB/c mice (6–8 weeks old), SPF guinea pig (250-300 g) or SPF rabbit (2-3 kg) were randomly assigned to three groups and immunized intramuscularly (100 μL per mouse, 200 μL per guinea pig or 2 mL per rabbit). Each mouse received LS (10 μg LS), rePO (12.21 μg rePO), reTTFC (10 μg reTTFC), rePO@LS (10 μg LS, 12.21 μg rePO), reTTFC@LS (10 μg LS and 10 μg reTTFC), or rePO-TTFC@LS (10 μg LS, 12.21 μg rePO, 10 μg reTTFC). Each guinea pig received rePO (12.21 μg rePO), reTTFC (10 μg reTTFC), or rePO-TTFC@LS (10 μg LS, 12.21 μg rePO, 10 μg reTTFC). Each rabbit received rePO (200 μg rePO), reTTFC (200 μg reTTFC), or rePO-TTFC@LS (165 μg LS, 200 μg rePO, 200 μg reTTFC). For the three-dose regimen, mice received injections on days 0, 7, and 14 at the indicated doses without any adjuvant. For the single-dose regimen, mice, guinea pig or rabbit received a single injection on day 0 at the same dose levels without any adjuvant.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Plates (Corning) were coated overnight at 4°C with rePO or reTTFC (0.6 μg/well). Plates were blocked with PBST containing 1% BSA (w/v) for 2 h at 37°C, then incubated with serial twofold dilutions of mouse sera for 1 h at 37°C (staring 1:100 or 1:1000). After washing, HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (1:7500) was added for 45 min at 37°C, followed by TMB development and measurement of absorbance at 450/690 nm. Mouse total IgG (Abcam, Cat #ab97265, RRID: AB_10680426) titers were measured 7 days after each dose in the three-dose regimen and on days 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 after the single-dose regimen. IgG subclass assays followed the same procedure using HRP-conjugated isotype-specific secondary antibodies (IgG1, IgG2a, IgG2b, Abcam, Cat#ab97240, ab97245, ab97250, RRID: AB_10695944, AB_10680049, AB_10695945). Guinea pig or rabbit total IgG (ORIGENE, Cat#ZB-2301, RRID: AB_2747412; Solarbio, Cat#SE240) titers were measured on days 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 14 after the single-dose regimen.

Enzyme-linked immunospot assay (ELISpot)

Ten days after the immunization, spleens were harvested, single-cell suspensions were prepared, and cells were resuspended in complete RPMI-1640 at 1 × 106 cells/mL. For each well, 100 μL of cells (1 × 105 cells) was added together with an equal volume of rePO peptide pool (30 μg/mL); DMSO served as the vehicle control. Antigen-specific cytokine-secreting cells were quantified using IFN-γ, IL-4, and IL-17A ELISpot kits according to the manufacturers’ instructions (MABTECH,Cat#3321-4APW-10, 3311-4APW-10, 3521-4APW-10).

Antigen uptake and dendritic-cell activation assay

rePO and reTTFC were fluorescently labeled using the FluoReporter FITC Protein Labeling Kit and assembled into nanoparticle formulations rePO@LS, reTTFC@LS, and rePO-TTFC@LS. DC2.4 cells were seeded at 5 × 105 cells per well, incubated with the corresponding FITC-labeled proteins, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature, permeabilized with 0.5% (v/v) Triton X-100, counterstained with DAPI (5 μg/mL), and imaged by laser-scanning confocal microscopy. Bone-marrow–derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) were isolated from mice, plated at 1 × 106 cells per well in 24-well plates, and stimulated with rePO, rePO@LS, rePO-TTFC@LS, reTTFC@LS, reTTFC, LPS, or PBS. After 6 h, cells were stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies (listed in Supplementary Table S4), washed with PBS, and analyzed on a BD FACSCanto II flow cytometer.

Protection assessment

To evaluate protection against PA, mice were challenged 10 days after the immunization with PA strain XN-1 delivered intratracheally at 2× LD50 or 10× LD50 and monitored for 7 days, with survival recorded every 12 h. In a parallel sublethal cohort (0.5× LD50), clinical status was assessed every 12 h for 7 days, including piloerection, posture/locomotion, nasal/ocular discharge, respiratory pattern, and body-weight change; clinical disease was scored 0-2 for each item, with death assigned 10 points. At 24 h after the 0.5× LD50 challenge, lung bacterial burden (CFU per lung) was quantified, and IL-1β and IL-6 concentrations in lung homogenates were measured by ELISA. To further evaluate breadth of protection, immunized mice were challenged with three clinical PA isolates representing distinct serotypes (PA464, PA451, and PA ZNJ004) as described previously [31] (Supplementary Table S5).

To assess antitoxin protection, mice were challenged 10 days after immunization by subcutaneous injection in the inguinal region with tetanus toxin at 1× LD100 (as specified for each experiment) and observed for 14 days. Clinical scoring comprised: asymptomatic (0), severity of neck/abdominal spasms (0-4), hind-limb spasms scored per limb (single leg 0-2; both legs 2-4), and death (10). Body weight and survival were recorded throughout the monitoring period.

To model mixed threats relevant to trauma, 10 days after the immunization a ∼1-cm incision was made over the left thigh, the femur was bluntly exposed, transected at the proximal-mid third, and stabilized with a 0.8-cm Kirschner wire; the wound was debrided and closed. Animals then received local injections of PA XN-1 at 0.5× LD50 and tetanus toxin at 1× LD50. At 48 h post-challenge, bacterial loads (CFU) were enumerated from peri-fracture soft tissue, femur, and the implant surface. Clinical disease scores, body-weight change, and survival were recorded daily for 7 days.

To determine long-term efficacy, mice immunized once with rePO-TTFC@LS were challenged at 28 weeks post-vaccination using the acute PA pneumonia model as described above. In addition, the lethal tetanus toxin challenge was also applied with survival recorded as the primary endpoint.

To evaluate the protective capacity of vaccine-induced antibodies, mice received 200 μg per mouse of anti-rePO-TTFC@LS polyclonal antibodies via intravenous (tail-vein) injection, and antibacterial protection was assessed 2 h later using an acute PA pneumonia challenge with strain XN-1. In parallel, antitoxin neutralizing titers of the administered antibodies were determined according to Method 3508 of the Chinese Pharmacopoeia (2020 edition, Volume III). The neutralizing antibody activity against PA was assessed using a cell lysis inhibition assay. Human bronchial epithelial cells (A549) were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 104 cells/mL (100 μL per well) and cultured overnight. The cells were then infected with the PA103 strain at a multiplicity of infection of 3, and simultaneously treated with 100 μL of 10-fold diluted mouse serum. After an additional 2-h incubation, cell viability was determined using the CCK-8 assay.

Safety assessment

A 5% suspension of mouse or rabbit erythrocytes was prepared and incubated with rePO-TTFC@LS diluted to 10, 25, 50, 100, or 200 μg/mL; PBS and distilled water served as negative and positive controls, respectively. After incubation at 37°C for 2 h, samples were centrifuged for 5 min and hemolysis was assessed spectrophotometrically at 545/570 nm, with hemolysis percentage calculated relative to the two controls. For cytotoxicity, DC2.4 and 293T cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 1 × 105 cells/well and exposed to rePO-TTFC@LS at 25-200 μg/mL for 24 h; cell viability was then determined using CCK-8 and absorbance at 450 nm, and viability was expressed as a percentage of vehicle-treated controls. To evaluate in vivo safety, BALB/c mice were bled from the retro-orbital plexus on days 3, 10, and 28 after immunization with rePO-TTFC@LS for complete blood counts and serum biochemistry. Hearts, livers, spleens, lungs, and kidneys were collected at the same time points, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, paraffin-embedded, sectioned, stained with H&E, and examined by light microscopy for histopathological changes.

Statistical analysis

Data were organized in Microsoft Excel and analyzed with GraphPad Prism 9.5.1. Unless otherwise specified, results are presented as mean ± SD. Two-group comparisons used unpaired t tests, while multiple-group comparisons were evaluated using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post-hoc test for pairwise comparisons. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Construction and characterization of the bivalent nanoparticle vaccine rePO-TTFC@LS

LS-based nanoparticle proteins were constructed by genetic fusion to SpyCatcher, generating SpyCatcher-LS (LS) and SpyCatcher-rePO@LS (rePO@LS). Each nanoparticle was covalently ligated to reTTFC-SpyTag (reTTFC) through SpyCatcher/SpyTag chemistry to produce reTTFC@LS and rePO-TTFC@LS (Fig. 1a). Results from SDS-PAGE showed clear upward shifts in apparent molecular mass after mixing with reTTFC, indicating formation of covalent conjugates (Fig. 1b). Reaction optimization identified a 2:1 input molar ratio and a 48 h incubation as yielding the highest amount of conjugated products (Supplementary Fig. S1). Dynamic light scattering demonstrated that conjugation increased the hydrodynamic diameter of LS and rePO@LS by approximately 8.7 nm and 5.8 nm, respectively (Fig. 1c; Supplementary Table S1). Zeta-potential measurements indicated that all four nanoparticles carried net negative surface charge (Fig. 1d). Thermal-shift analysis revealed a significant increase in the melting temperature of rePO-TTFC@LS relative to rePO@LS and rePO (Fig. 1e). Size-exclusion chromatography on Superose 6 Increase 10/300 GL resolved LS, rePO@LS, reTTFC@LS, and rePO-TTFC@LS at elution volumes of 12.5 mL, 9.8 mL, 12.4 mL, and 9.8 mL, respectively (Fig. 1f). Consistent with nanoparticle assembly according to molecular-mass standards, and negative-stain transmission electron microscopy visualized discrete, well-formed nanoparticles for all constructs (Fig. 1g). The bivalent rePO-TTFC@LS group yielded robust, uniquely positive colorimetric signals across all three distinct detection channels (Supplementary Fig. S3b). Together, these results demonstrate the successful preparation of a monodisperse, bivalent nanoparticle vaccine, rePO-TTFC@LS.

Immunogenicity of the bivalent nanoparticle vaccine rePO-TTFC@LS

In a three-dose regimen, mice received intramuscular vaccinations on days 0, 7, and 14, and sera were collected longitudinally. Across all time points, rePO-TTFC@LS elicited significantly higher anti-rePO IgG titers than the rePO monomer after each immunization; likewise, anti-reTTFC IgG titers in the rePO-TTFC@LS group exceeded those of the reTTFC monomer after the first dose, indicating that nanoparticle presentation enhances responses to both antigens (Supplementary Fig. S4b–c). Thus, we compared the antibody responses between three-dose monomer immunization regimen and one-dose rePO-TTFC@LS immunization (Fig. 2a). As expected, anti-rePO titers increased after each monomeric rePO immunization at D0, D7, and D14 (Fig. 2b). Notably, a single-dose of rePO-TTFC@LS achieved by day 14 titers comparable to those observed after three monomer doses, with a similar pattern observed for anti-reTTFC (Fig. 2c). Building on these findings, subsequent experiments used a single-dose regimen (Fig. 2d). Following one immunization, anti-rePO IgG titers in the rePO-TTFC@LS and rePO@LS groups were significantly higher than in the rePO group at D2, D4, D6, D8, and D10, with rePO-TTFC@LS exceeding rePO@LS at D8 and D10 (Fig. 2e). Subclass analysis at D10 showed that anti-rePO IgG1, IgG2a, and IgG2b titers in rePO-TTFC@LS and rePO@LS were significantly above those in the rePO group, while IgG2a/IgG1 ratios did not differ among groups (Fig. 2f and g).

For TTFC, rePO-TTFC@LS and reTTFC@LS induced faster and higher anti-reTTFC IgG responses than the reTTFC monomer (Fig. 2h). At D10, IgG2a and IgG2b titers in rePO-TTFC@LS (and in reTTFC@LS for several comparisons) were significantly above those in the reTTFC group, whereas IgG1 titers were comparable across groups (Fig. 2i). The IgG2a/IgG1 ratio for anti-TTFC was significantly higher in rePO-TTFC@LS than in the other groups (Fig. 2j). ELISpot on splenocytes harvested after single-dose immunization showed that rePO-TTFC@LS generated more IFN-γ- and IL-17A-secreting cells than the rePO monomer and rePO@LS upon rePO peptide restimulation (Fig. 2k and l). IL-4-secreting cell frequencies were similar between rePO-TTFC@LS and rePO@LS but exceeded those in the rePO monomer group (Fig. 2m).

To further assess cross-species performance, the single-dose regimen was evaluated in guinea pigs and rabbits. As anticipated, a single immunization with rePO-TTFC@LS elicited substantially higher anti-rePO and anti-TTFC IgG titers than the corresponding monomeric antigens in both species (Fig. 2n–p). Collectively, these data indicate that one injection of rePO-TTFC@LS is sufficient to induce robust dual-antigen humoral responses, accompanied by cellular immunity against rePO.

Enhanced antigen uptake and dendritic-cell activation induced by rePO-TTFC@LS

To elucidate how rePO-TTFC@LS elicits potent immune responses, we first assessed antigen uptake by antigen-presenting cells (APCs). FITC-labeled rePO or TTFC was incubated with DC2.4 cells, and confocal imaging revealed markedly stronger intracellular green fluorescence with rePO@LS and rePO-TTFC@LS than with soluble rePO (Fig. 3a). an analogous pattern was observed for TTFC, with reTTFC@LS and rePO-TTFC@LS exceeding soluble reTTFC (Fig. 3b). We next evaluated APC activation by stimulating bone-marrow–derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) in vitro and quantifying activation markers by flow cytometry. Among CD11c + BMDCs, the proportion of CD80+CD86+ cells was highest after exposure to rePO-TTFC@LS (81.4 ± 1.3%), significantly exceeding the levels induced by rePO, rePO@LS, reTTFC, and reTTFC@LS (Fig. 3c). Consistent trends were observed for MHC II and CD40 expression, with rePO-TTFC@LS driving the largest fractions of MHC II+ and CD40+ BMDCs (Fig. 3d and e).

Protective efficacy of single-dose rePO-TTFC@LS against PA pneumonia and tetanus toxin challenge

To determine whether the dual-antigen nanoparticle confers functional protection in vivo, mice received a single intramuscular dose of rePO-TTFC@LS, rePO@LS, or the corresponding monomer antigens, followed by intrapulmonary challenge with PA XN-1 (Fig. 4a). Compared with monomeric rePO and LS controls, rePO-TTFC@LS markedly improved survival and mitigated infection-associated morbidity, as evidenced by faster recovery of body weight and persistently lower disease scores throughout the 7-day observation period (Fig. 4b–e). At the acute phase post-infection, lungs from rePO-TTFC@LS–immunized mice contained substantially fewer bacteria than those from the rePO and rePO@LS groups (Fig. 4f), accompanied by reduced concentrations of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6 in lung homogenates (Fig. 4g). Histopathology corroborated these findings, with rePO-TTFC@LS exhibiting the least alveolar consolidation, inflammatory infiltrates, and interstitial edema among the tested groups (Fig. 4h). In a separate toxin-challenge model, a single immunization with rePO-TTFC@LS afforded superior protection against tetanus toxin compared with soluble reTTFC and reTTFC@LS, reflected by higher survival, improved maintenance of body weight, and lower disease scores over a 14-day monitoring period (Fig. 4i–k). To further evaluate breadth of protection, we challenged mice with three clinical PA isolates representing distinct serotypes (PA464, PA451, and PA ZNJ004), and rePO-TTFC@LS conferred robust protection across all corresponding pneumonia models (Supplementary Fig. S6a–c).

To evaluate antibody-mediated protection, mice received 200 μg of anti-rePO-TTFC@LS polyclonal antibodies (pAb) by intravenous injection and were challenged in the acute PA XN-1 pneumonia model 2 h later. Relative to PBS controls, pAb-treated mice exhibited prolonged survival, significantly reduced lung bacterial burdens and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6), and improved histopathology, with corresponding benefits in body-weight trajectories and composite clinical disease scores (Supplementary Fig. S7b–g). Moreover, anti-rePO-TTFC@LS pAb exhibited superior neutralizing activity, maintaining approximately 90% A549 cell viability and outperforming the group of monomeric rePO and rePO@LS nanoparticles (Supplementary Fig. S7h). In parallel, the antitoxin neutralizing titer of the anti-rePO-TTFC@LS pAb was significantly higher than that observed in the reTTFC-immunized group (Supplementary Fig. S8), indicating that vaccine-elicited antibodies afford dual protection against both PA infection and tetanus toxin.

Protective efficacy of rePO-TTFC@LS in a murine open-fracture model co-challenged with PA and tetanus toxin

To better approximate clinical scenarios, we established a murine femoral-fracture model in which animals were co-challenged with PA and tetanus toxin after a single immunization, and we compared prophylaxis with rePO-TTFC@LS, reTTFC@LS, rePO@LS, or controls (Fig. 5a). All mice vaccinated with reTTFC@LS or rePO-TTFC@LS survived the co-challenge, whereas the rePO monomer, rePO@LS, and PBS groups succumbed (Fig. 5b). Despite the high survival rate in both the reTTFC@LS and rePO-TTFC@LS groups, their clinical trajectories differed significantly. The rePO-TTFC@LS produced more favorable clinical trajectories than reTTFC@LS, with smaller early weight loss and faster recovery (Fig. 5c) and consistently lower disease scores (Fig. 5d). In contrast, the reTTFC@LS group suffered from severe morbidity, evidenced by pronounced weight loss and persistently higher disease scores. To assess antibacterial effects at the infection site, we quantified CFU in peri-fracture soft tissue, femoral bone, and the implant. Bacterial burdens in the rePO-TTFC@LS group were comparable to those in rePO@LS and were significantly lower than in PBS/LS controls (Fig. 5e–g). Conversely, bacterial burdens in the reTTFC@LS group remained as high as those in the PBS and LS controls across all compartments, which explains the severe morbidity as observed in the reTTFC@LS survivors. These data demonstrate while single-antigen formulations provided limited defense against this dual-threat model, a single immunization of bivalent rePO-TTFC@LS conferred comprehensive dual protection by preventing both systemic acute intoxication and clearing localized bacterial infection.

Durable dual protection conferred by rePO-TTFC@LS over a 28-week interval

Next, we evaluated the longevity of humoral immunity and sustained protective efficacy elicited by rePO-TTFC@LS by longitudinally monitoring serum anti-rePO and anti-TTFC IgG for 28 weeks, followed by pathogen challenges at week 28 (Fig. 6a). Across the observation period, IgG titers against both antigens remained stable without appreciable decline (Fig. 6b). Upon sublethal pulmonary infection with PA XN-1, mice immunized with rePO-TTFC@LS exhibited markedly reduced bacterial burdens in lung homogenates and lower pulmonary concentrations of IL-1β and IL-6 compared with PBS controls (Fig. 6c and d). In a separate lethality model initiated 28 weeks after immunization, all rePO-TTFC@LS vaccinated mice survived tetanus toxin challenge over the 14-day monitoring window (Fig. 6e), with superior body-weight trajectories and consistently lower global disease scores than PBS recipients (Fig. 6f and g). These data indicate that a single immunization with rePO-TTFC@LS maintains durable humoral responses and confers long-term protection against both PA pulmonary infection and tetanus toxin exposure.

Initial in vitro and in vivo biosafety evaluation of rePO-TTFC@LS

We assessed the biosafety profile of rePO-TTFC@LS across complementary in vitro and in vivo assays. Incubating graded concentrations of rePO-TTFC@LS (10-200 μg/mL) with rat or rabbit whole blood did not induce detectable hemolysis across the range tested (Fig. 7a and b). Co-culture of rePO-TTFC@LS (10-200 μg/mL) with 293T cells or DC2.4 dendritic cells showed no appreciable reduction in cell viability relative to vehicle controls (Fig. 7c). To examine systemic safety in mice, whole blood and serum collected at days 3, 10, and 28 after immunization were analyzed for hematology and biochemistry; values for vaccinated mice remained within reference ranges and were comparable to those of healthy, non-immunized animals (Fig. 7d). Histopathological examination of heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney at the same time points revealed no treatment-associated lesions, with tissue architecture indistinguishable from healthy controls (Fig. 7e). These data showed that the rePO-TTFC@LS is of good biosafety in vitro and in vivo.

Discussion

Most nanoparticle vaccines under development present a single antigen or, at most, a multivalent array derived from different serotypes of the same pathogen, thereby eliciting potent but pathogen-restricted immunity [25,27,32,33]. In contrast, we co-displayed antigens from two distinct threats rePO and TTFC, by covalently coupling them to the LS scaffold to create the bivalent nanoparticle vaccine rePO-TTFC@LS. This dual-indication design addresses the mixed-exposure scenarios common after trauma, particularly in disaster and conflict settings where PA infection and Clostridium tetani contamination can coincide, and provides a practical template for developing combination vaccines on nanoparticle platforms. Our rePO-TTFC@LS vaccine holds significant translational potential across several clinical and emergency scenarios. In the context of disaster relief or combat casualty care, a single intramuscular injection could be administered with initial wound debridement and stabilization. Furthermore, prophylactic administration could be integrated into emergency orthopedic protocols. For example, a single injection administered in the emergency department for patients with open fractures would provide essential immunity during both the high-risk, two-week postoperative window for PA infection and the incubation period for tetanus. Finally, this vaccine offers a strategy for the pre-exposure protection of high-risk occupational groups, including firefighters and explosive ordnance disposal units.

Compared with monomeric subunits, nanoparticle vaccines approximate the size and repetitive architecture of pathogens, facilitating recognition and phagocytic uptake by antigen-presenting cells and thereby accelerating and amplifying downstream immune activation [[34], [35], [36]]. Consistent with this principle, we observed that rePO@LS, reTTFC@LS, and the bivalent rePO-TTFC@LS were internalized by DC2.4 cells with substantially higher efficiency than their soluble rePO and reTTFC counterparts, and they elicited greater maturation of BMDCs. While direct visualization of in vivo cellular trafficking was not performed in the current study, many literature have established that nanoparticles within this size range can rapidly drain to local lymph nodes and are highly efficiently captured by antigen presenting cells (APCs) in vivo [37]. In our study, the rapid onset of robust, high-titer humoral and cellular immunity, along with the outstanding functional protection demonstrated across multiple stringent challenge models, inherently validates the in vivo bioavailability and engagement of rePO-TTFC@LS. Mechanistically, this efficient in vivo uptake and presentation is the fundamental prerequisite for the downstream protective efficacy as we observed.

A single intramuscular dose of rePO-TTFC@LS elicited a rapid and robust antigen-specific immune response in mice. For the rePO arm, rePO-TTFC@LS induced significantly higher anti-rePO IgG titers than either rePO@LS or soluble rePO and generated greater numbers of splenocytes secreting IFN-γ, IL-4, and IL-17A upon rePO restimulation, indicating adjuvant contributions from both the LS nanoparticle scaffold and the co-displayed TTFC antigen. For the TTFC arm, rePO-TTFC@LS produced anti-TTFC IgG titers that were significantly higher than those elicited by soluble reTTFC and comparable to those achieved with TTFC@LS. Together, these findings highlight the practical advantage of the bivalent nanoparticle vaccine for emergency, single-dose use.

Interestingly, a seemingly paradoxical phenomenon was observed with the single-antigen formulations. The rePO@LS vaccine effectively restricted PA bacterial proliferation at the infection sites to levels comparable to the bivalent vaccine, yet it failed to prevent mortality (Fig. 5). This occurs because the rePO antigen solely elicits anti-PA immunity, leaving the host vulnerable to the acute lethality of the co-administered tetanus toxin. Conversely, the reTTFC@LS formulation ensured 100% survival by neutralizing the lethal tetanus toxin; however, due to the absence of PA-specific antigens, it permitted uncontrolled PA bacterial growth. This local infection resulted in severe morbidity, evidenced by high bacterial loads identical to the negative control, marked weight loss, and elevated clinical disease scores. These divergent results demonstrate that protection against one threat does not confer cross-protection against the other. Ultimately, only the bivalent rePO-TTFC@LS vaccine bridged this critical gap. By concurrently eliciting neutralizing antitoxin antibodies and robust antibacterial immunity, it simultaneously prevented systemic toxic shock and cleared local bacterial colonization, thereby ensuring both survival and minimal clinical morbidity.

Immunological memory is central to vaccine efficacy and is mediated by adaptive lymphocytes, including memory B cells and multiple T-cell subsets [38,39]. The long-lived plasma cells (LLPCs) derived from B cells sustain humoral immunity by continuously secreting antigen-specific antibodies. Upon re-exposure to the same pathogen, these memory populations expand rapidly and execute effector functions, enabling swift pathogen control [40]. In this study, the bivalent nanoparticle vaccine rePO-TTFC@LS induced durable immune responses and long-lasting protection. Mechanistically, nanoparticle carriers can shield antigens from proteolytic degradation, prolong antigen half-life and lymphoid retention, and thereby extend germinal-center reactions and antibody durability; such properties likely underpin the sustained immunity observed with rePO-TTFC@LS. Future work will delineate the contributions of memory B cells, LLPCs, and discrete T-cell memory subsets to the longevity of protection conferred by this dual-indication nanoparticle vaccine.

Selecting appropriate animal models is essential for evaluating vaccine efficacy and should be guided by pathogen biology, target populations, and environmental context. PA is a leading cause of hospital and ventilator associated pneumonia, with high lower-respiratory mortality and limited therapeutic options, whereas tetanus is mediated by a potent exotoxin from Clostridium tetani and carries a substantial case-fatality risk [21,41]. Accordingly, we established a murine acute PA pneumonia model and an independent lethal tetanus toxin challenge model to assess indication-specific protection. To reflect real-world scenarios in disaster or combat settings where trauma, fractures, and infection frequently coincide, we further developed a femoral fracture–implant co-infection model incorporating intramedullary fixation, PA contamination, and tetanus toxin exposure to evaluate mixed-threat protection by rePO-TTFC@LS. In this setting, vaccination significantly reduced PA colonization in peri-fracture soft tissue, femur, and on the implant surface, and improved survival. Future studies will examine the vaccine's impact on fracture healing and extend evaluation to a burn-injury model with PA infection and tetanus toxin challenge, with parallel mechanistic analyses to define how rePO-TTFC@LS mediates protection under mixed-infection conditions [42,43].

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, while we demonstrate durable IgG responses and protection to 28 weeks, longer follow-up is needed to define the true longevity of immunity. Second, we did not conduct head-to-head comparisons with licensed tetanus toxoid formulations or evaluate a broad dose range, adjuvant combinations, that could affect magnitude and quality of protection. Third, safety assessments were restricted to short-term hematology/chemistry and histopathology in mice; comprehensive toxicology, reactogenicity profiling, and evaluation in larger animals are needed to establish a translational safety margin. To address these limitations and further advance the development of rePO-TTFC@LS, we plan to delineate the contributions of memory B cells, long-lived plasma cells (LLPCs), and discrete memory T-cell subsets to understand the mechanisms underlying the 28-week durable protection. Furthermore, to further improve efficacy and prepare for clinical translation, we plan to evaluate various adjuvant combinations to boost the magnitude and tailor the quality of the immune response. In addition, comprehensive profiling in larger animal models to establish a definitive safety margin will also be a priority.

Collectively, this work advances a single-dose, dual-indication nanoparticle vaccine concept targeting both bacterial infection and toxin exposure in high-risk settings.

Conclusion

This study provides a unique proof-of-concept for a single-dose, dual-indication nanoparticle vaccine that co-displays antigens from Pseudomonas aeruginosa and an unrelated toxin tetanus, achieving robust protection with supportive mechanistic and safety data. By demonstrating that a platform can successfully co-present heterogeneous antigens and deliver broad efficacy, the work establishes a translatable template for combination vaccines tailored to disaster, conflict, and emergency-care scenarios where mixed exposures are common. Equally important, the single-dose regimen confers clear operational advantages, including rapid onset of immunity, durable protection for at least 28 weeks, improved coverage, and lower programmatic costs, thereby strengthening the rationale to advance this platform to clinical evaluation.
