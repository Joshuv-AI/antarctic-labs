Hiroshi Ando, Isaac Goldbring

Abstract
. We make a series of model-theoretic contributions to Connes’ bicentralizer problem, one of the most prominent open problems in the theory of von Neumann algebras. Our work builds on the recent result of Houdayer and Marrakchi who show that, for separable diffuse W ∗ -probability spaces, having trivial bicentralizer is equivalent to being selfless, that is, having the first factor inclusion into the free product be an existential embedding. We first show that the class of selfless W ∗ W^{*} -probability spaces is ∀ ∃ \forall\exists -axiomatizable. We then extend the Houdayer-Marrakchi equivalence to all diffuse W ∗ -probability spaces, removing the separability hypothesis. Combining these results, we show that for any axiomatizable class of diffuse W ∗ W^{*} -probability spaces, those with trivial bicentralizer form an ∀ ∃ \forall\exists -axiomatizable class; in particular, the class of type III 1 \mathrm{III}_{1} factors with trivial bicentralizer is ∀ ∃ \forall\exists -axiomatizable. We give concrete axioms for this class using totally bounded variants of Haagerup’s characterization of the bicentralizer, which we develop here and believe to be of independent interest. We also introduce the notion of pseudoperiodic III 1 \mathrm{III}_{1} factors and show that any such factor has trivial bicentralizer. In the final section, we prove that the bicentralizer problem has a positive solution if and only if the bicentralizer functor is a zeroset relative to the theory of III 1 \mathrm{III}_{1} factors. We use this result to give an equivalent formulation of the bicentralizer problem in terms of a uniformity condition on Haagerup’s Dixmier-type characterization of the bicentralizer.



1.
Introduction

The work presented here is a model theoretic contribution to
Connes’ bicentralizer problem
, one of the most prominent open problems in von Neumann algebra theory. To explain the problem, suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is a
W
∗
-probability space
, that is,
M
is a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
is a faithful normal state on
M
. The
bicentralizer
of
(M,\varphi)
is the set
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)
consisting of those
a\in M
satisfying the following: given
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
so that, whenever
u\in M
is a unitary for which
\|u\varphi-\varphi u\|:=\sup\{|\varphi(ub)-\varphi(bu)|\ :\ \|b\|\leq 1\}<\delta
, then one has
\|u^{*}au-a\|_{\varphi}<\epsilon
. (This is not Connes’ original definition, but rather a reformulation due to Haagerup
[
24
, Section 1]
.) The bicentralizer
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)
is always a von Neumann subalgebra of
M
. In case
M
is a III
1
factor,
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)
is either trivial, that is, equals
\mathbb{C}
, or else is a type III
1
factor again.

The relevance of the bicentralizer stems from Connes’ classification of injective von Neumann algebras. After having classified all injective factors with separable predual besides those of type III
1
, Connes proved that an injective type III
1
factor
M
with separable predual is isomorphic to the Araki-Woods factor
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
provided for some (or any) faithful normal state
\varphi
on
M
, the modular flow
\sigma^{\varphi}
is approximately inner, and he showed that this is the case if
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
, which was confirmed later by Haagerup
[
24
]
, completing the classification problem for injective factors.
Moreover, Connes
[
10
]
(also mentioned in
[
24
, Section 3]
) suggested that every type III
1
factor with separable predual should have trivial bicentralizer; this problem is now known as Connes’ bicentralizer problem. Although there are various classes of III
1
factors where the bicentralizers have been shown to be trivial
[
3
,
9
,
24
,
31
,
28
,
30
,
36
,
38
]
, the general case is still open.

The relevance of the bicentralizer problem beyond the injective case can be best understood by Haagerup’s characterization
[
24
, Theorem 3.1]
that if
M
is a factor of type III
1
with separable predual, then it has trivial bicentralizer if and only if there exists a faithful normal state
\varphi
with
large centralizer
, that is, if
M_{\varphi}^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}
. The existence of such a state has proven to be of great importance in applications, for example, in Houdayer and Isono’s works on the generalization of Popa’s free independence theorem
[
26
]
and on the unique prime factorization theorem for III
1
factors
[
27
]
. In a more recent work, the first author with Haagerup, Houdayer and Marrakchi
[
3
]
constructed a flow
\beta^{\varphi}
, called the
bicentralizer flow
, on
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)
(or more generally, on the relative bicentralizer
\operatorname{BC}(N\subset M,\varphi)
) and its ergodicity is shown to be equivalent to the existence of an irreducible hyperfinite subfactor with expectation. Since then, there has been remarkable progress made by Marrakchi, where the flow is indeed shown to be ergodic
[
36
]
, and this fact was used to show the weak relative Dixmier property for inclusion of von Neumann algebras with expectation
[
37
]
and applications to Kadison’s problem and further generalization of the bicentralizer conjecture
[
38
]
. Incidentally, Isono
[
32
]
confirmed the Haagerup–Størmer conjecture
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
about pointwise inner automorphisms of factors of type III
1
under the trivial bicentralizer condition, where Marrakchi’s works played a key role. All of these recent developments provide the motivation to study the bicentralizer problem beyond the injective case.

The starting point of our work is the recent article of Houdayer and Marrakchi
[
29
]
, who proved that, for
separable, diffuse
W
∗
-probability spaces, having a trivial bicentralizer is equivalent to being
selfless
. Here, a W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
is said to be selfless if and only if there is an embedding from the free product
(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)
into the (Ocneanu) ultraproduct
(M,\varphi)^{\mathcal{U}}
such that, when restricted to the first factor, yields the usual diagonal embedding of
(M,\varphi)
into its ultrapower.
Selflessness was first introduced and investigated by Robert
[
44
]
, motivated by Popa’s article
[
43
]
, where it was shown (using the above terminology) that any II
1
factor (equipped with its canonical trace) is selfless. Houdayer–Isono’s free independence result mentioned above in this context is the statement that a W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
is selfless if
M
is a factor with
\varphi
having large centralizer. This result played a key role in one direction of the proof of the above equivalence. Selflessness has been a very active area of study in
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra theory over the past couple of years, stemming from the recent breakthrough result by Amrutam et. al.
[
1
]
, where it is shown that the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra C
{}^{*}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
has strict comparison by establishing that it is selfless (when equipped with its canonical trace), settling a longstanding open problem.

Selflessness is really a model-theoretic concept:
(M,\varphi)
is selfless if and only if the embedding
(M,\varphi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)
is existential. (Here, we view W
∗
-probability spaces as model-theoretic structures as in
[
6
]
; see also Subsection
2.6
below.) Existential embeddings of W
∗
-probability spaces were first studied in detail by the second author and Houdayer in
[
16
]
, where it was observed that any
existentially closed (e.c.)
W
∗
-probability space (that is, any W
∗
-probability space for which any embedding into a larger W
∗
-probability space is existential) is a III
1
factor that tensorially absorbs
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
, which implies that it must have trivial bicentralizer by
[
36
, Theorem D]
. That e.c. W
∗
-probability spaces have trivial bicentralizer also follows from the aforementioned result of Houdayer and Marrakchi.

Our first main result is the following:

Theorem

(Corollary
4.11
)
.

The class of selfless W
∗
-probability spaces is an
\forall\exists
-axiomatizable class.

We do not prove this theorem by giving concrete axioms for this class, but rather prove that this class is closed under ultraproducts, ultraroots, and direct limits. In contrast, we note that the class of e.c. W
∗
-probability spaces is not closed under ultraproducts (see
[
16
, Corollary 3.11]
).

Our next main result is to remove the separability assumption from the result of Houdayer and Marrakchi:

Theorem

(Theorem
4.21
)
.

For any (not necessarily separable) diffuse W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
, we have that
(M,\varphi)
is selfless if and only if
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
.

The previous theorem is proven by showing that membership in either of the above classes can be detected by separable elementary subalgebras; for bicentralizers, this involves establishing that
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)\cap N=\operatorname{BC}(N,\psi)
whenever
(N,\psi)
is an elementary (even existential) substructure of
(M,\varphi)
. Consequently, we see that if the bicentralizer problem has a positive solution, then
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
for all III
1
factors
M
, regardless of the density character of
M
.

Combining the previous theorem with our first theorem yields the following:

Theorem

(Corollary
4.22
)
.

For any axiomatizable class
\mathcal{K}
consisting only of diffuse W
∗
-probability spaces, the elements of
\mathcal{K}
with trivial bicentralizer form an axiomatizable class, which is
\forall\exists
-axiomatizable if
\mathcal{K}
is
\forall\exists
-axiomatizable. In particular, the class of III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer is axiomatizable.

In the case that
\mathcal{K}
is the class of III
1
factors, we offer an alternative proof of the previous theorem that avoids the use of selflessness and instead relies on a result of Haagerup, namely that a separable III
1
factor
M
with trivial bicentralizer admits a state
\varphi
with “large centralizer” in the sense that
M_{\varphi}^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}
, together with a generalization of an observation of Houdayer regarding ultrapowers of states with large centralizers.

After having shown that the class of III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer is axiomatizable, we proceed to write down concrete axioms for this class. To do so, we introduce a variant of Haagerup’s description of W
∗
-probability spaces with trivial bicentralizer (which is the description given above) that is better suited to dealing with ultraproducts and which makes use of the technology around
totally bounded
elements introduced in
[
6
]
. We believe these results should be of independent interest.

Ostensibly, the previous theorem should yield many new examples of III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer. Indeed, in analogy with the case of II
1
factors (see
[
15
, Theorem 4.3]
), it is conjectured that, for any separable W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
with
M
a III
1
factor, there are continuum many W
∗
-probability spaces
(M_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})_{\alpha<2^{\omega}}
elementarily equivalent to
(M,\varphi)
but with
M_{\alpha}\not\cong M_{\beta}
for
\alpha<\beta<2^{\omega}
(see also
[
16
, Question 4.13]
); if
(M,\varphi)
is one of the III
1
factors known to have trivial bicentralizer, then each
M_{\alpha}
would also have trivial bicentralizer.

In the final section, we give a “quantitative” reformulation of the bicentralizer problem. To explain this, we follow Haagerup and define, for a W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
and
a\in M
, the quantity

\epsilon_{M}(a,\delta):=\sup\{\|uau^{*}-a\|_{\varphi}\ :\ u\in\mathbf{U}(M),\|u\varphi-\varphi u\|<\delta\}.

Here, and throughout this paper,
\mathbf{U}(M)
denotes the unitary group of a von Neumann algebra
M
. It follows that
a\in\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)
if and only if
\inf_{\delta}\epsilon_{M}(a,\delta)=0
. We then show that the bicentralizer problem is equivalent to this description of the bicentralizer being “uniform” over all III
1
factors:

Theorem

(Theorem
5.3
)
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The bicentralizer problem has a positive solution.

(2)

For each
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that, for all W
∗
-probability spaces
(M,\varphi)
with
M
a III
1
factor, and all
a\in S_{1}(\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi))
, we have that
\epsilon_{M}(a,\delta)<\epsilon
.

The key to proving this fact is the following model-theoretic reformulation of the bicentralizer problem:

Theorem

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The bicentralizer problem has a positive solution.

(2)

The bicentralizer is a
zeroset
with respect to the theory of III
1
factors. In other words: for every family
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
of W
∗
-probability spaces with each
M_{i}
a III
1
factor and each ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
I
, we have that

\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\operatorname{BC}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\subseteq\operatorname{BC}\left(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\right).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
W
∗
-probability spaces

In this subsection, we include a few reminders about W
∗
-probability spaces; the reader can find much more background in
[
6
, Section 2]
and the introduction to
[
16
]
.

A W
∗
-
probability space
is a pair
(M,\varphi)
that consists of a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra
M
endowed with a faithful normal state
\varphi
. For every
x\in M
, set

\|x\|_{\varphi}=\varphi(x^{*}x)^{1/2}\text{ and }\|x\|_{\varphi}^{\sharp}=\sqrt{\frac{\varphi(x^{*}x)+\varphi(xx^{*})}{2}}.

On uniformly bounded sets, the topology induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
) coincides with the strong (resp.
\ast
-strong) operator topology.

Given a W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
, the
centralizer
of
\varphi
, denoted
M_{\varphi}
, is defined by
M_{\varphi}:=\{x\in M\ :\ x\varphi=\varphi x\}
. One has that
M_{\varphi}
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
M
and that
\varphi|_{M_{\varphi}}
is a trace on
M_{\varphi}
. Note that
\varphi
is a trace on
M
itself if and only if
M_{\varphi}=M
. The state
\varphi
is called
ergodic
if
M_{\varphi}=\mathbb{C}
. (The terminology stems from the fact that this condition is equivalent to the modular flow
\sigma^{\varphi}
being ergodic.)

For W
∗
-probability spaces
(M,\varphi)
and
(N,\psi)
, we say that
(M,\varphi)

embeds into

(N,\psi)
, denoted
(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(N,\psi)
, if there exist a unital normal
\ast
-embedding
\iota:M\to N
such that
\psi\circ\iota=\varphi
and a faithful normal conditional expectation
E:N\to\iota(M)
such that
\varphi\circ\iota^{-1}\circ E=\psi
. For other characterizations of embeddings between W
∗
-probability spaces, see
[
6
, Lemma 2.8]
.

2.2.
Ultraproducts of W
∗
-probability spaces

Let
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of
W^{*}
-probability spaces and let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an ultrafilter on
I
.

Let
\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}M_{i}
denote the C
∗
-algebra of all bounded
I
-indexed sequences in
\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}
.

Set

\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}=\left\{(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}M_{i}\ :\ \lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|x_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}=0\right\}\text{ and }\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}=\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}\cap\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}^{*},

where
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}^{*}=\{x^{*}:x\in\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}\}
.
In other words, we have

\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}=\{(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}M_{i}\ :\ \lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\|x_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}=0\}.

Then
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
is a closed left ideal of
\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}M_{i}
. The normalizer
\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
of
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
is defined as the largest
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}M_{i}
in which
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
forms a two-sided ideal:

\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}=\left\{x\in{\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}M_{i}}\ :\ x\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}\text{ and }\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}x\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}\right\}.

Finally, the
(Ocneanu) ultraproduct
of the family
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
is defined as the quotient
C^{*}
-algebra:

\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i}):=\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}/\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}.

For
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
, we let
(x_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}
denote its image in
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
. We always view
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
as a W
∗
-probability space by equipping it with the state
\varphi
given by
\varphi((x_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}):=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\varphi_{i}(x_{i})
.

If
(M,\varphi)
is a given W
∗
-probability space and
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter on
I
, we write
(M,\varphi)^{\mathcal{U}}
or
(M^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{\mathcal{U}})
for the ultraproduct
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
, where each
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})=(M,\varphi)
and refer to this as the
(Ocneanu) ultrapower
of
(M,\varphi)
with respect to
\mathcal{U}
. There is an obvious diagonal embedding
(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(M^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{\mathcal{U}})
of
(M,\varphi)
into its ultrapower given by considering equivalence classes of constant sequences; moving forward, we always consider
M
as a subalgebra of
M^{\mathcal{U}}
via this embedding. We note that the isomorphism type of
M^{\mathcal{U}}
is independent of the choice of state
\varphi
(all choices of state lead to the same ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}
and normalizer
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}
) and so we might on occasion simply write
M^{\mathcal{U}}
if we are only considering the underlying von Neumann algebra of the ultrapower.

The following lemma is well-known. We include the proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
I
-indexed family of W
∗
-probability spaces and
{\mathcal{U}}
an ultrafilter on
I
. Set
(M,\varphi)=\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
. If
x=(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}M_{i}
satisfies
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|x_{i}\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{i}x_{i}\|=0
, then
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
and
(x_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in M_{\varphi}
.

Proof.

By
[
24
, Lemma 2.8 (b)]
, we have
\|x_{i}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}-\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i}\|=\|x_{i}^{*}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}-\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i}^{*}\|\to 0
along
{\mathcal{U}}
(the result was stated for
I=\mathbb{N}
but the same proof works in general).
Let
y=(y_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. To show that
xy
and
yx
belong to
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
, it suffices to show that
xy\in\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}^{*}
and
yx\in\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
, as
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
(resp.
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}^{*}
) is a left (resp. right) ideal of
\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}M_{i}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|x_{i}\|,\,\|y_{i}\|\leq 1
for all
i\in I
.

To see that
xy\in\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}^{*}
, we compute:

\displaystyle\|y_{i}^{*}x_{i}^{*}\|_{\varphi_{i}}

\displaystyle=\|y_{i}^{*}x_{i}^{*}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{i}^{*}(x_{i}^{*}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}-\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i}^{*})\|+\|y_{i}^{*}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i}^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{i}^{*}\|\|x_{i}^{*}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}-\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i}^{*}\|+\|x_{i}^{*}\|\|y_{i}^{*}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{i}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}-\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i}\|+\|y_{i}^{*}\|_{\varphi_{i}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}0.

Similarly, to see that
yx\in\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
, we compute:

\displaystyle\|y_{i}x_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{i}(x_{i}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}-\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i})\|+\|y_{i}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{i}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}-\xi_{\varphi_{i}}x_{i}\|+\|y_{i}\xi_{\varphi_{i}}\|

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}0.

This shows that
x\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
and thus
(x_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in M
is defined. We conclude that
(x_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in M_{\varphi}
by
[
4
, Lemma 4.36]
.
∎

We will also need the following fact:

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of W
∗
-probability spaces, where
M_{i}
is a type III
{}_{\lambda_{i}}
-factor. Set
(M,\varphi):=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
. Suppose further that
\lambda:=\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\lambda_{i}>0
. Then:

(1)

M
is a type III
λ
-factor.

(2)

If
\psi_{i}
is another faithful normal state on
M_{i}
, then
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
is
*
-isomorphic to
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\psi_{i})
. Moreover, if
\lambda=1
then the isomorphism maps
(\varphi_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}
to
(\psi_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}
and thus they are isomorphic as W
∗
-probability spaces.

Proof.

(1) Let
\widetilde{M}=\prod^{{\mathcal{U}}}M_{i}

The bicentralizer

Fix a W

2.4.
Arveson spectral subspaces

Here, we briefly recall Arveson spectral subspaces
[
7
]
. More details can be found in
[
46
, Chapter XI]
.
We identify the dual group
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
of the additive group
\mathbb{R}
with itself. For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we define the Fourier transform
\hat{f}
by

\hat{f}(\lambda):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\lambda}f(t)dt,\ \ \ \ \ \lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}.

We also consider the function
\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}:M\to M
given by
\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)dt
, where the integral is taken in the
\sigma
-weak sense.

(1)

For
x\in M
,
\text{Sp}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}(x)
is defined by

\left\{\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}\ :\ \hat{f}(\lambda)=0\text{ for all }f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\text{ with }\sigma^{\varphi}_{f}(x)=0\right\}.

(2)

The
Arveson spectrum
of
\sigma^{\varphi}
, denoted by
\text{Sp}(\sigma^{\varphi})
is the set

\left\{\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}\ :\ \hat{f}(\lambda)=0\text{ for all }f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\text{ with }\sigma^{\varphi}_{f}=0\right\}.

It is shown that
{\rm{Sp}}(\sigma^{\varphi})=\log(\sigma(\Delta_{\varphi})\setminus\{0\})
.

(3)

For a subset
E
of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
, the
spectral subspace
of
\sigma^{\varphi}
corresponding to
E
is given by

M(\sigma^{\varphi},E):=\{x\in M\ :\ \text{Sp}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}(x)\subset E\}.

The spectral subspaces have the following properties:

(i)

M(\sigma^{\varphi},E)^{*}=M(\sigma^{\varphi},-E)
.

(ii)

M(\sigma^{\varphi},E)M(\sigma^{\varphi},F)\subset M(\sigma^{\varphi},\overline{E+F})
.

(iii)

\lambda\in\text{Sp}(\sigma^{\varphi})
if and only if
M(\sigma^{\varphi},E)\neq\{0\}
for any closed neighborhood
E
of
\lambda
.

(iv)

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then
\Sp_{\sigma^{\varphi}}(\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x))\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\cap\Sp_{\sigma^{\varphi}}(x)
.

For
a>0
, the
Fejér kernel

F_{a}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

F_{a}(t):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1-\cos(at)}{\pi at^{2}}&t\neq 0\\
\ \ \ a/2\pi&t=0.\end{cases}

Its Fourier transform is given by

\widehat{F_{a}}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\lambda}F_{a}(t)dt=\begin{cases}1-\dfrac{|\lambda|}{a}&|\lambda|\leq a\\
\ \ \ 0&|\lambda|>a.\end{cases}

It holds that
0\leq F_{a}
and
\|F_{a}\|_{1}=\widehat{F}_{a}(0)=1
.
In particular, we have
\sigma_{F_{a}}^{\varphi}(x)\in M(\sigma^{\varphi},[-a,a])
for every
a>0
and
x\in M
. This fact will be repeatedly used in the sequel.

The
de la Vallée Poussin Kernel

D_{a}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

D_{a}(t)=2F_{2a}(t)-F_{a}(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\cos(at)-\cos(2at)}{\pi at^{2}}&t\neq 0\\
\ \ \ \ \ 3a/2\pi&t=0.\end{cases}

Its Fourier transform is given by

\widehat{D_{a}}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}1&|\lambda|\leq a\\
2-\frac{|\lambda|}{a}&a\leq|\lambda|\leq 2a\\
0&|\lambda|>2a.\end{cases}

More generally, for
0<b<a
, the function
D_{a,b}=\frac{1}{a-b}(aF_{a}-bF_{b})
belongs to
C_{b}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and satisfies
\widehat{D_{a,b}}=1
on
[-b,b]
and
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{D_{a,b}})\subset[-a,a]
.

For our purposes, we will need variants of the aforementioned results of Haagerup that interact better with ultraproducts. The key technical notion involved in these variants is that of a totally bounded element as introduced in

:

Suppose that

-probability space.
For

\|ba\|_{\varphi}\leq K\|b\|_{\varphi}

The next lemma is essentially due to Connes

Lemma 2.11

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a
W^{*}
-probability space. Fix

and

.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

is right

(ii)

The map

(iii)

\varphi(a^{*}xa)\leq K^{2}\varphi(x)

Proof.

It is clear that (i)

The equivalence (ii)

(cf.
[

∎

Throughout this paper, for any von Neumann algebra

Corollary 2.12

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a W
∗
-probability space and let

is totally 1-bounded if and only if

Proof.

If

Conversely, if

The following spectral subspace criterion for proving total boundedness of an element will prove useful in the sequel.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a W
∗
-probability space. Fix
x\in M
with
\|x\|\leq 1
and
a>0

(i)

x\in M(\sigma^{\varphi},[-a,a])

(ii)

The map
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)\in M
extends to an
M

\|\sigma_{z}^{\varphi}(x)\|\leq e^{a|\operatorname{Im}z|}\|x\|

(iii)

Then (i)
\iff
(ii)
\implies
(iii).

The equivalence (i)
\iff
(ii) can be seen as an

\implies
(i) is due to Haagerup
[

, and from the inequality in the proof of
[

is the Fejér kernel defined in

2.4

[

, Lemma 2.5]
. The closest result we found is Matsumoto’s Paley–Wiener–Schwartz type theorem in the C

2.6.
W
∗
-probability spaces as model-theoretic structures

In this subsection, we briefly recall the treatment of W
∗
-probability spaces as model-theoretic structures due to Arulseelan, Hart, Sinclair, and the second author as presented in
[
6
]
. (We remark that this class was first treated model-theoretically by Dabrowski in
[
13
]
, but we prefer the treatment in
[
6
]
as it allows one to speak of actual multiplication rather than the “smeared” multiplication used in
[
13
]
.)

The key insight into the model-theoretic treatment of W
∗
-probability spaces as presented in
[
6
]
is the choice of sorts
S_{K}
of totally
K
-bounded elements, as
K
varies over
\mathbb{N}
(as opposed to sorts being the operator norm balls as in Dabrowski’s approach
[
13
]
), where each sort is equipped with the metric corresponding to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
. (Note that the sorts are indeed complete with respect to this metric.) Since multiplication (as a two-variable function) is uniformly continuous when restricted to each
S_{K}
, one may allow multiplication on each sort as a distinguished binary function symbol. Moreover, since the union of the sorts is dense in the W
∗
-probability space (as mentioned earlier), this allows one to recover the W
∗
-probability space from its “dissected” version. In fact, the map which sends a W
∗
-probability space to its dissection is an equivalence of categories for which the Ocneanu ultraproduct corresponds to the model-theoretic ultraproduct. We stress that the proof of this theorem is nontrivial and uses ideas from Tomita-Takesaki theory (and the bounded operator approach to this theory developed by Rieffel and van Daele) as well as ideas of Kadison. For more details on this, the reader may consult
[
6
]
.

2.7.
Countably incomplete and good ultrafilters

In a few places in the paper, we need the notion of a
good ultrafilter
. The definition is quite technical, so we do not give it here. We simply explain the properties needed for the applications in this paper. We refer the reader to
[
17
, Chapter 8]
for complete details in the setting of classical logic and to
[
33
]
for a treatment in continuous logic.

Recall that an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
is called
countably incomplete
if there is a descending family
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements of
\mathcal{U}
with
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}=\emptyset
. The key point is that whenever
(\mathcal{M}_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of structures in a countable (or separable, in the continuous setting) language and
\mathcal{U}
is a countably incomplete ultrafilter on
I
, then the ultraproduct
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated.

In order to get higher levels of saturation, one needs to work with special kinds of countably incomplete ultrafilters called good ultrafilters. More precisely, given any cardinal
\kappa
, one can define the notion of a
\kappa^{+}
-good ultrafilter

\mathcal{U}
on a set
I
, which has the key property that whenever
(\mathcal{M}_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of structures in a language of cardinality (or density character, in the continuous setting) at most
\kappa
, then the ultraproduct
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
is
\kappa^{+}
-saturated. A particular consequence of this saturation is that any structure of cardinality (or density character) at most
\kappa^{+}
elementarily equivalent to
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
embeds elementarily into
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{M}_{i}
. We stress that, in ZFC, one can prove that
\kappa^{+}
-good ultrafilters exist for every cardinal
\kappa
.

Lemma 3.4

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose

\displaystyle=\|v^{*}u^{*}uv-1\|_{\varphi}\leq\|v^{*}(u^{*}u-1)v\|_{\varphi}+\|v^{*}v-1\|_{\varphi},

4.
Axiomatizing W
∗
-probability spaces with trivial bicentralizer

4.1.
On selfless W
∗
-probability spaces

In this subsection we prove that the class of selfless W
∗
-probability spaces is axiomatizable. First, we recall the relevant definitions from the introduction.

An inclusion
(N,\psi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)
is called
existential
if: for any quantifier-free formula
\theta(\vec{x},\vec{a})
with parameters from
N
, we have

\left(\inf_{\vec{x}}\theta(\vec{x},\vec{a})\right)^{(N,\psi)}=\left(\inf_{\vec{x}}\theta(\vec{x},\vec{a})\right)^{(M,\varphi)}.

Equivalently, the inclusion
(N,\psi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)
is existential if there is an embedding
(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(N,\psi)^{\mathcal{U}}
that restricts to the diagonal embedding
(N,\psi)\hookrightarrow(N,\psi)^{\mathcal{U}}
. If
N
is separable, then
\mathcal{U}
can be taken to be any nonprincipal ultrafilter on any index set; otherwise, one needs to choose
\mathcal{U}
to be a suitably good ultrafilter (see Subsection
2.7
). Identifying a W
∗
-probability space with its image under an embedding, one may also speak of existential embeddings.

We will need the following well-known and easy facts about existential embeddings:

Fact 4.1
.

Suppose that
i:(N,\psi)\hookrightarrow(M,\varphi)
and
j:(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(P,\rho)
are embeddings.

(1)

If both
i
and
j
are existential, then the composition
j\circ i:(N,\psi)\hookrightarrow(P,\rho)
is also existential.

(2)

If the composition
j\circ i:(N,\psi)\hookrightarrow(P,\rho)
is existential, then so is
i
.

Fact 4.2
.

Suppose that, for each
i\in I
,
j_{i}:(N_{i},\psi_{i})\hookrightarrow(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
are existential embeddings. Suppose further that
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter on
I
. Then the ultraproduct map
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}j_{i}:\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(N_{i},\psi_{i})\hookrightarrow\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
is also existential.

The W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
is called
selfless
if the first factor inclusion
(M,\varphi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)
is existential.

Before proving our axiomatizability result, we need a few lemmas.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
(M_{1,i},\varphi_{1,i})_{i\in I}
and
(M_{2,i},\varphi_{2,i})_{i\in I}
are families of W
∗
-probability spaces and
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter on
I
.
Let
(M_{k}^{{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi_{k}^{{\mathcal{U}}})=\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{k,i},\varphi_{k,i})
and
(M^{{\mathcal{U}}},\varphi^{{\mathcal{U}}})=\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
denote the corresponding Ocneanu ultraproducts, where

(M_{i},\varphi_{i})=(M_{1,i},\varphi_{1,i})*(M_{2,i},\varphi_{2,i}),\quad i\in I,\,k=1,2.

Then the canonical embeddings of
M_{1}^{\mathcal{U}}
and
M_{2}^{\mathcal{U}}
into
M^{\mathcal{U}}
are freely independent with respect to the ultraproduct state
\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}
.

Consequently, there is a canonical embedding of W
∗
-probability spaces

(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{1,i},\varphi_{1,i}))*(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{2,i},\varphi_{2,i}))\hookrightarrow\prod_{\mathcal{U}}((M_{1,i},\varphi_{1,i})*(M_{2,i},\varphi_{2,i})).

Proof.

The inclusions of W
∗
-probability spaces

(M_{k,i},\varphi_{k,i})\subset(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\quad i\in I,\,k=1,2

induce an inclusion of the W
∗
-probability spaces

(M_{k}^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi_{k}^{{\mathcal{U}}})\subset(M^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{{\mathcal{U}}})\quad k=1,2.

Here, we identify the ultrapowers
M_{k}^{\mathcal{U}}
with their respective images in
M^{\mathcal{U}}
.

To verify freeness, let
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}
be an alternating sequence of elements such that
x_{j}\in M_{\ell_{j}}^{\mathcal{U}}
and
\varphi_{\ell_{j}}^{\mathcal{U}}(x_{j})=0
for all
j=1,\dots,m
(
\ell_{j}\neq\ell_{j+1},\,j=1,\dots,m-1
).

Each
x_{j}
can be represented by a norm-bounded sequence
(x_{j,i})_{i\in I}
with
x_{j,i}\in M_{\ell_{j},i}
. The condition
\varphi_{\ell_{j}}^{\mathcal{U}}(x_{j})=0
implies that
\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\varphi_{\ell_{j},i}(x_{j,i})=0
. By replacing each representative
x_{j,i}
with
x_{j,i}-\varphi_{\ell_{j},i}(x_{j,i})1
, we can choose representatives such that
\varphi_{\ell_{j},i}(x_{j,i})=0
holds exactly, for every
i\in I
.

Thus, for each fixed
i\in I
, the elements
x_{1,i},\dots,x_{m,i}
form an alternating word of centered elements in the original free product
(M_{1,i},\varphi_{1,i})*(M_{2,i},\varphi_{2,i})
. By the free independence of
M_{1,i}
and
M_{2,i}
inside
M_{i}
with respect to
\varphi_{i}=\varphi_{1,i}*\varphi_{2,i}
, we have

\varphi_{i}(x_{1,i}x_{2,i}\cdots x_{m,i})=0\quad\text{for all }i\in I.

Passing to the ultralimit along
\mathcal{U}
, we obtain

\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{m})=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\varphi_{i}(x_{1,i}x_{2,i}\cdots x_{m,i})=0.

This shows that the subalgebras
M_{1}^{\mathcal{U}}
and
M_{2}^{\mathcal{U}}
are free with respect to the state
\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}
. By the universal property of the free product, the von Neumann algebra they generate inside
M^{\mathcal{U}}
is canonically isomorphic to
(M_{1}^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi_{1}^{\mathcal{U}})*(M_{2}^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi_{2}^{\mathcal{U}})
, which yields the desired canonical embedding.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

A free product of existential embeddings of W
∗
-probability spaces is again existential.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, let
\alpha_{i}:(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\hookrightarrow(N_{i},\psi_{i})
be existential embeddings. Lemma
4.3
shows that
\alpha_{1}*\alpha_{2}
yields an embedding

\displaystyle(M_{1},\varphi_{1})*(M_{2},\varphi_{2})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow(N_{1},\psi_{1})*(N_{2},\psi_{2})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow(M_{1},\varphi_{1})^{\mathcal{U}}*(M_{2},\varphi_{2})^{\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow((M_{1},\varphi_{1})*(M_{2},\varphi_{2}))^{\mathcal{U}},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

If
(N,\psi)
is an existential substructure of
(M,\varphi)
and
(M,\varphi)
is selfless, then
(N,\psi)
is selfless.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4
, we have that the inclusion
(N,\psi)*(N,\psi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)
is existential. Since the inclusion
(M,\varphi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)
is also existential, the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

One can prove Lemma
4.5
without using Lemma
4.4
as follows.
Since both
(N,\psi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)
and

(M,\varphi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)
are existential, there are ultrafilters
{\mathcal{U}}_{1},{\mathcal{U}}_{2}
on some index sets
I_{1},I_{2}
and embeddings of W
∗
-probability spaces of the form

(N,\psi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\subseteq}}(M,\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta}}{{\subseteq}}(N,\psi)^{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}}

and

(M,\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j_{1}}}{{\subseteq}}(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k_{1}}}{{\subseteq}}(M,\varphi)^{{\mathcal{U}}_{2}},

so that
\theta\circ\pi=\iota_{N}
and
k_{1}\circ j_{1}=\iota_{M}
; here
\iota_{N}\colon(N,\psi)\hookrightarrow(N,\psi)^{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}}
and
\iota_{M}\colon(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(M,\varphi)^{{\mathcal{U}}_{2}}
are the corresponding diagonal embeddings, and
j_{1}
is the first factor embedding.
Then we have the following embedding:

\displaystyle(N,\psi)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{1}}}{{\subseteq}}(N,\psi)*(N,\psi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi*\pi}}{{\subseteq}}(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k_{1}}}{{\subseteq}}(M,\varphi)^{{\mathcal{U}}_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta^{{\mathcal{U}}_{2}}}}{{\subseteq}}((N,\psi)^{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}})^{{\mathcal{U}}_{2}}=(N,\psi)^{{\mathcal{U}}_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{U}}_{1}}.

Here,
i_{1}
is the first factor embedding. Since
(k_{1}\circ j_{1})(y)=y
for all
y\in M
, for each
x\in N
, we have

\displaystyle(\theta^{\mathcal{U}_{2}}\circ k_{1}\circ(\pi*\pi)\circ i_{1})(x)

\displaystyle=(\theta^{\mathcal{U}_{2}}\circ k_{1}\circ j_{1})(\pi(x))

\displaystyle=\theta^{\mathcal{U}_{2}}(\pi(x))

\displaystyle=x.

Therefore, this shows that
(N,\psi)\subseteq(N,\psi)*(N,\psi)
is existential.

Theorem 4.7
.

The class of selfless W
∗
-probability spaces is axiomatizable.

Proof.

We verify that the class of selfless W
∗
-probability spaces is closed under isomorphism, ultraproduct, and ultraroots. Closure under isomorphism is clear and closure under ultraroot is a special case of Lemma
4.5
. To prove closure under ultraproducts, note that Lemma
4.3
yields

\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\subseteq\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})*\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\hookrightarrow\prod_{\mathcal{U}}((M_{i},\varphi_{i})*(M_{i},\varphi_{i}))

whose composition is the ultraproduct of the first factor embeddings. Since the ultraproduct of existential embeddings is existential by Fact
4.2
, the composition is existential, whence so is the first inclusion by Fact
4.1
(2), yielding the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

A result similar to the above was proven in the setting of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-probability spaces by Robert
[
44
, Theorem 4.5]
: the class of selfless
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-probability spaces
(A,\rho)
with
A
a simple, purely infinite
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, is axiomatizable. It is in fact
\forall\exists
-axiomatizable, which is also true in our context, as we will soon prove.

We next observe that the union of a chain of selfless W
∗
-probability spaces is again selfless. To prove this, we need the following:

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose that
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
is a directed system of W
∗
-probability spaces with injective bonding maps and direct limit
(M,\varphi)
. Further suppose that there is a W
∗
-probability space
(N,\psi)
and embeddings
\alpha_{i}:(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\hookrightarrow(N,\psi)
compatible with the directed system. Then the induced map
\alpha:(M,\varphi)\to(N,\psi)
is also injective.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let us assume that each
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\subseteq(M,\varphi)
and set
M_{0}:=\bigcup_{i\in I}M_{i}
, a dense
*
-subalgebra of
M
. For each
a\in M_{0}
, set

X_{a}:=\{i\in I\ :\ a\in M_{i}\}.

Since
I
is directed, the family
(X_{a})_{a\in M}
has the finite intersection property. Let
\mathcal{U}
be an ultrafilter on
I
such that each
X_{a}\in\mathcal{U}
. Define a map

\Phi:(M,\varphi)\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})

by
\Phi(a)=(E_{i}(a))_{\mathcal{U}}
, where
E_{i}:M\to M_{i}
is a state-preserving conditional expectation map. Note that, for all
a\in M_{0}
and
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
i\in I
, we have
E_{i}(a)=a
. It follows that
\Phi
is an embedding of W
∗
-probability spaces and hence
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i}\circ\Phi:M\to N^{\mathcal{U}}
is an embedding. But
\Phi(a)=(\alpha(a))_{\mathcal{U}}
for all
a\in M_{0}
, whence
\alpha
is also injective, as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Suppose that
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
is a directed system of W
∗
-probability spaces with embeddings as bonding maps and direct limit
(M,\varphi)
. If each
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
is selfless, then so is
(M,\varphi)
.

Proof.

We may assume, after identifying each
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
with its image in the direct limit, that

M_{i}\subseteq M_{j}\subseteq M\quad\text{whenever }i\leq j,\qquad\varphi_{j}|_{M_{i}}=\varphi_{i},\qquad\varphi|_{M_{i}}=\varphi_{i},

and that
\bigcup_{i\in I}M_{i}
is dense in
M
.

Set

A_{i}:=(M_{i},\varphi_{i})*(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\text{ and }A:=(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi).

The inclusions
M_{i}\subseteq M_{j}
induce state-preserving embeddings
A_{i}\hookrightarrow A_{j}

and the inclusions
M_{i}\subseteq M
induce state-preserving embeddings
A_{i}\hookrightarrow A
.

We prove that the first factor embedding

(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)=A

is existential. Let
\theta(\vec{x},\vec{a})
be a quantifier-free formula with parameters
\vec{a}
from
M
. Fix
\epsilon>0
. Choose a tuple
\vec{b}
from
A
such that

\theta^{A}(\vec{b},\vec{a})<\inf_{\vec{x}\in A}\theta^{A}(\vec{x},\vec{a})+\epsilon.

By uniform continuity of the quantifier-free formula
\theta
, choose
\eta>0
such that replacing the tuple of variables and parameters by tuples within
\eta
changes the value of
\theta
by less than
\epsilon
.

Since
\bigcup_{i}M_{i}
is dense in
M
, there are some
i_{0}\in I
and a tuple
\vec{a}_{0}
from
M_{i_{0}}
such that
d_{M}(\vec{a},\vec{a}_{0})<\eta
.
After increasing
i_{0}
if necessary, there are some
j\geq i_{0}
and a tuple
\vec{b}_{j}
from
A_{j}

such that
d_{A}(\vec{b},\vec{b}_{j})<\eta
.
Then

\bigl|\theta^{A}(\vec{b},\vec{a})-\theta^{A_{j}}(\vec{b}_{j},\vec{a}_{0})\bigr|<\epsilon,

where we identify
A_{j}
with its image in
A
.

Since
(M_{j},\varphi_{j})
is selfless, the first factor embedding
(M_{j},\varphi_{j})\hookrightarrow A_{j}

is existential.
Thus we may choose a tuple
\vec{c}
from
M_{j}
such that

\theta^{M_{j}}(\vec{c},\vec{a}_{0})<\theta^{A_{j}}(\vec{b}_{j},\vec{a}_{0})+\epsilon.

Viewing
\vec{c}
as a tuple from
M
, and using again uniform continuity, we obtain

\theta^{M}(\vec{c},\vec{a})<\theta^{M_{j}}(\vec{c},\vec{a}_{0})+\epsilon.

Combining the preceding inequalities gives

\displaystyle\inf_{\vec{x}\in M}\theta^{M}(\vec{x},\vec{a})

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{M}(\vec{c},\vec{a})

\displaystyle<\theta^{M_{j}}(\vec{c},\vec{a}_{0})+\epsilon

\displaystyle<\theta^{A_{j}}(\vec{b}_{j},\vec{a}_{0})+2\epsilon

\displaystyle<\theta^{A}(\vec{b},\vec{a})+3\epsilon

\displaystyle<\inf_{\vec{x}\in A}\theta^{A}(\vec{x},\vec{a})+4\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, this proves

\inf_{\vec{x}\in M}\theta^{M}(\vec{x},\vec{a})=\inf_{\vec{x}\in A}\theta^{A}(\vec{x},\vec{a}).

Therefore the first factor embedding
(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(M,\varphi)*(M,\varphi)

is existential and hence
(M,\varphi)
is selfless.
∎

It is a well-known fact in model theory (see, for example,
[
14
, Proposition 2.4.4]
), that an axiomatizable class of structures is
\forall\exists
-axiomatizable if and only if it is closed under unions of chains. As a result of the previous proposition, we have:

Corollary 4.11
.

The class of selfless W
∗
-probability spaces is
\forall\exists
-axiomatizable.

Remark 4.12
.

An alternate proof of the previous corollary would be to use the fact that existential substructures of selfless W
∗
-probability spaces are again selfless (Lemma
4.5
above) together with Keisler’s sandwich theorem (see
[
16
, Theorem 4.2]
). This then gives an alternate proof of Proposition
4.10
.

We end this section with a few further observations about selfless W
∗
-probability spaces.

Lemma 4.13
.

A W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
is selfless if and only if all of its separable existential substructures are selfless.

Proof.

The forward direction follows from Lemma
4.5
and the reverse direction follows from Lemma
4.10
and Downward Löwenheim-Skolem (as
(M,\varphi)
is the direct limit of all of its separable elementary substructures).
∎

Proposition 4.14
.

A nontrivial selfless W
∗
-probability space is a factor of type II
1
or type III
λ
for some
\lambda\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is a nontrivial selfless W
∗
-probability space. The proof of the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) in
[
29
, Theorem A]
then shows that there is a diffuse W
∗
-probability space
(N,\psi)
such that
(M,\varphi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)*(N,\psi)
is existential; we stress that even though
(M,\varphi)
is assumed to be both separable and diffuse in the statement of
[
29
, Theorem A]
, neither of these assumptions are used to deduce the aforementioned fact. By
[
47
, Theorem 3.4]
,
(M,\varphi)*(N,\psi)
is a factor. By
[
16
, Lemma 3.3]
,
M
is a factor.

We next show that
M
cannot be type I
n
. If
M\cong M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, then
(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)^{\mathcal{U}}\cong(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)
by
[
39
, Lemma 2.8]
and thus cannot contain a copy of the infinite-dimensional W
∗
-probability space
(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)*(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi)
, whence
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is not selfless.

Finally, as noted in Fact
2.4
, a
separable
selfless W
∗
-probability space cannot be type II
∞
or III
0
. By Lemma
4.5
and Downward Löwenheim-Skolem, it thus suffices to check that an elementary substructure of a II
∞
factor (resp. III
0
factor) is also a type II
∞
factor (resp. III
0
factor). This follows from the fact that types II
1
, II
∞
, and III
λ
for
\lambda\in(0,1]
are preserved under elementary equivalence (see
[
6
, Propositions 8.7 and 8.8]
).
∎

Remark 4.15
.

The main result of
[
29
, Theorem A]
is that for separable, diffuse W
∗
-probability spaces
(M,\varphi)
, being selfless is equivalent to
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
. We will soon remove the separability assumption in their result, but we comment that the diffuse assumption in their result is necessary by the previous lemma. Moreover, to deduce that selfless W
∗
-probability spaces are factors using their theorem, one needs to first assume that the underlying von Neumann algebra is diffuse to then conclude that it has trivial bicentralizer, from which factoriality follows immediately; in the proof of the previous proposition, we first deduced factoriality directly from the selflessness assumption, and then diffuseness followed quite easily.

4.2.
Selflessness and trivial bicentralizer

In
[
29
]
, it is shown that, for a
separable
diffuse W
∗
-probability space, one has that selflessness is equivalent to having trivial bicentralizer. We wish to extend this fact to all diffuse W
∗
-probability spaces. First, we need some preparation.

Proposition 4.16
.

Fix an inclusion
(N,\psi)\subseteq(M,\varphi)
of W
∗
-probability spaces. Then for all
a\in N
and
\epsilon,\delta>0
, we have:

(1)

If
\epsilon_{M}(a,\delta)<\epsilon
, then
\epsilon_{N}(a,\delta)\leq\epsilon
.

(2)

If
(N,\psi)
is an existential substructure of
(M,\varphi)
and
\epsilon_{N}(a,\delta)<\epsilon
, then
\epsilon_{M}(a,\delta/2)\leq\epsilon
.

Proof.

For (1), suppose that
\epsilon_{M}(a,\delta)<\epsilon
. Fix
u\in U(N)
such that
\|u\psi-\psi u\|\leq\delta
. Let
E:M\to N
be a state-preserving conditional expectation. Then for
b\in\operatorname{ball}M
, one has
|\varphi(ub)-\varphi(bu)|=|\psi(uE(b))-\psi(E(b)u)|\leq\delta
. It follows that
\|u\varphi-\varphi u\|\leq\delta
and thus
\|u^{*}au-a\|_{\varphi}<\epsilon
; since
u\in U(N)
was an arbitrary unitary with
\|u\psi-\psi u\|\leq\delta
, we have
\epsilon_{N}(a,\delta)\leq\epsilon
.

For (2), suppose that
\epsilon_{N}(a,\delta)<\epsilon
and fix
u\in U(M)
with
\|u\varphi-\varphi u\|\leq\delta/2
. Fix an embedding
i:(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(N,\psi)^{\mathcal{U}}
that restricts to the diagonal embedding on
(N,\psi)
and write
i(u)=(u_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}
, where
u_{i}\in\mathbf{U}(N)
for every
i\in I
. Then
\|i(u)\psi^{\mathcal{U}}-\psi^{\mathcal{U}}i(u)\|\leq\delta/2
by the argument in part (1). Therefore, by Fact
2.3
, we have
\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\|u_{i}\psi-\psi u_{i}\|<\delta
and so
\|u_{i}\psi-\psi u_{i}\|<\delta
for
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
i
. It follows that
\|u_{i}^{*}au_{i}-a\|_{\varphi_{i}}<\epsilon
for
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
i
and thus
\|u^{*}au-a\|_{\varphi}\leq\epsilon
.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

If
(N,\psi)
is an existential substructure of
(M,\varphi)
, then
\operatorname{BC}(N,\psi)=\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)\cap N
.

Remark 4.18
.

By Lemma
4.17
, one sees now that if the bicentralizer problem has a positive solution, then
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
, regardless of whether or not
M
is separable.

We can now prove the bicentralizer analog of Lemma
4.13
.

Lemma 4.19
.

If
(M,\varphi)
is a W
∗
-probability space, then
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
if and only if
\operatorname{BC}(N,\psi)=\mathbb{C}
for all separable existential substructures
(N,\psi)
of
(M,\varphi)
.

Proof.

This is an immediate application of Corollary
4.17
and Downward Löwenheim-Skolem.
∎

Lemma 4.20
.

If
(N,\psi)
is an existential substructure of a diffuse W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
, then
(N,\psi)
is also diffuse.

Proof.

Let
p
be a nonzero projection in
N
. Let
q
be a nonzero projection in
M
with
q<p
. Fix an embedding
i\colon(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow(N,\psi)^{{\mathcal{U}}}
that restricts to the diagonal embedding on
(N,\psi)
and write
q=(q_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}
with each
q_{n}\in N
a subprojection of
p
. Then for
\mathcal{U}
-almost all
n
,
q_{n}
is a nonzero proper subprojection of
p
; since
p
was an arbitrary nonzero projection in
N
, we have that
N
is diffuse.
∎

We can now remove the separability requirement in the result of Houdayer and Marrakchi:

Theorem 4.21
.

For any diffuse W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
(not necessarily separable), we have that
(M,\varphi)
is selfless if and only if
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

The theorem follows immediately from the main result of
[
29
]
using Lemmas
4.13
,
4.19
, and
4.20
.
∎

Theorem
4.7
, Corollary
4.11
, and Theorem
4.21
immediately yield:

Corollary 4.22
.

For any axiomatizable class
\mathcal{K}
of diffuse W
∗
-probability spaces, the set of elements of
\mathcal{K}
with trivial bicentralizer is axiomatizable. If
\mathcal{K}
is
\forall\exists
-axiomatizable, then so is the class of elements of

-axiomatizable.

Similarly, Lemma
4.10
and Theorem
4.21
yield:

Corollary 4.23
.

The class of diffuse W

-probability spaces with trivial bicentralizer is closed under direct limits.

4.3.
Pseudo-periodic III
1
factors

By Proposition
2.2
, if
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of W

-probability spaces, where

is a type III

-factor with

,
and

is a type III

-probability space elementarily equivalent to

. We can give an alternate characterization of such W

Proposition 4.24
.

Suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is a W
∗
-probability space. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

There is a family
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
of W

-probability spaces, where

(2)

For every sentence

.

(3)

For every sentence

\sigma^{(M,\varphi)}=0
, every
\epsilon>0
, and every
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
, there is a W
∗
-probability space
(N,\psi)
with
N
a type III
λ
-factor with
\lambda\in[\lambda_{0},1)
such that
\sigma^{(N,\psi)}<\epsilon
.

Proof.

The implications (1) implies (2) and (2) implies (3) are immediate. Now suppose that
(M,\varphi)
satisfies (3). Let
X
denote the set of sentences
\sigma
such that
\sigma^{(M,\varphi)}=0
. Let
I
denote the set of finite subsets of
X\sqcup\mathbb{N}^{>0}
that intersect both
X
and
\mathbb{N}
. For each
\sigma\in X
and
m\geq 1
, let
O_{\sigma,m}
denote those
i\in I
that contain both
\sigma
and
m
. Note that the collection of such sets
O_{\sigma,m}
has the finite intersection property, whence there is an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
I
containing each
O_{\sigma,m}
. By (3), for each
i\in I
, setting
m_{i}:=\max(\mathbb{N}\cap i)
, we may find a W
∗
-probability space
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
with
M_{i}
a III
{}_{\lambda_{i}}
factor satisfying
\lambda_{i}\geq 1-1/{m_{i}}
and with
\sigma^{(M_{i},\varphi_{i})}<1/m_{i}
for all
\sigma\in X\cap i
. Note then that
\lim_{\mathcal{U}}\lambda_{i}=1
and
M\equiv\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
, establishing (1).
∎

Note that any W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
satisfying the equivalent conditions of the previous proposition necessarily has that
M
is a III
1
factor, whence the property is independent of the state
\varphi
. We call such a III
1
factor a
pseudo-periodic III
1
factor
. Note that the use of “period” here refers to the one for the flow of weights, that is, we do not assume the states appearing in the ultraproduct are periodic.

Suppose that
M
is a pseudo-periodic III
1
factor and
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
and
\mathcal{U}
are as in condition (1) of the previous proposition. By Proposition
2.2
(2), we may assume that each
\varphi_{i}
is a
\frac{2\pi}{|\log(\lambda_{i})|}
-periodic state on
M_{i}
. Then, as mentioned above,
\operatorname{BC}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})=\mathbb{C}
for each
i\in I
, whence
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
by Corollary
4.22
above. (To apply the corollary, one may assume that each
\lambda\geq 1/2
and then one can let
\mathcal{K}
be the class of W
∗
-probability spaces whose underlying von Neumann algebra is a III
λ
-factor for
\lambda\geq 1/2
. This is indeed axiomatizable by
[
6
, Proposition 8.8]
.) This establishes the following:

Theorem 4.25
.

If
M
is a pseudo-periodic III
1
factor, then
M
has trivial bicentralizer.

In connection with Connes’ bicentralizer problem, this then leads to the following:

Question 4.26
.

Is every III
1
factor a pseudo-periodic III
1
factor?

A positive solution to the previous question could be viewed as some form of a type III
Lefschetz principle
. Recall that the classical Lefschetz principle states that an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of algebraically closed fields of positive characteristic. That being said, a question more in line with the classical Lefschetz principle would be: is every existentially closed III
1
factor a pseudo-periodic III
1
factor? However, we already know that all existentially closed III
1
factors are selfless and thus have trivial bicentralizer and are more interested in the general form of the question stated above.

Given that existential subfactors of III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer again have trivial bicentralizer, in order to give a positive answer to the bicentralizer problem, it suffices to give a positive answer to the following, a priori easier, question:

Question 4.27
.

Does every W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
with
M
a III
1
factor admit an existential embedding into an ultraproduct
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
, where each
M_{i}
is a type III
{}_{\lambda_{i}}
factor with
\lambda_{i}\in(0,1)
?

There is a connection between the previous question and the
Effros-Maréchal (EM) topology
that we now explain. Let
(M,\varphi)
be a W
∗
-probability space,
\operatorname{SA}(M)
be the set of all von Neumann subalgebras of
M
, and
\operatorname{SA}_{\varphi}(M)
be the set of all globally
\sigma^{\varphi}
-invariant von Neumann subalgebras of
M
.
We consider
\operatorname{SA}_{\varphi}(M)
as equipped with its EM-topology; for the definition of this topology, see
[
22
, Section 2]
. We will only need the following characterization of convergence in the EM-topology, as explained in
[
22
, page 575]
: for a sequence
(N_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and another element
N
in
\operatorname{SA}_{\varphi}(M)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}N_{n}=N\iff\mathrm{E}_{N_{n}}(x)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{so*}}\mathrm{E}_{N}(x),\,\,\,x\in M.

Here, for each
N\in\operatorname{SA}_{\varphi}(M)
,
\mathrm{E}_{N}
is the unique normal faithful
\varphi
-preserving conditional expectation of
M
onto
N
.

Proposition 4.28
.

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a W
∗
-probability space with
M_{*}
separable. Let
(M_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
\operatorname{SA}_{\varphi}(M)
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}M_{n}=M
in the EM-topology.
Then for each nonprincipal ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathbb{N}
, there exists an existential embedding of
(M,\varphi)
into
\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
, where
\varphi_{n}:=\varphi|_{M_{n}}
.

Proof.

For each
n
, let
\mathrm{E}_{n}
be the unique
\varphi
-preserving conditional expectation of
M
onto
M_{n}
.
Since
(M_{n},\varphi_{n})\subseteq(M,\varphi)
is an inclusion of W
∗
-probability spaces for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it induces an embedding of the W
∗
-probability spaces

\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k}}{{\subseteq}}\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M,\varphi)

given by inclusion, and the
\varphi^{{\mathcal{U}}}
-preserving conditional expectation is given by
\mathrm{E}((x_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}})=(\mathrm{E}_{n}(x_{n}))_{{\mathcal{U}}}
for
(x_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M,\varphi)
. If
x\in M
, then
(\mathrm{E}_{n}(x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
defines an element in
\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
.
Define
j\colon M\to\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
by
j(x)=(\mathrm{E}_{n}(x))_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. Since

\mathrm{E}_{n}(x)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{so*}}x
, it is an injective unital
*
-homomorphism, and it is normal and faithful, since
\psi(j(x))=\varphi(x)
, where
\psi=(\varphi_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}}
is a faithful normal state on
\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
. The
\psi
-preserving conditional expectation
\varepsilon\colon\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})\to M
is given by
\varepsilon((x_{n})_{{\mathcal{U}}}):=\mathrm{wot-}\lim_{n\to{\mathcal{U}}}x_{n}
. Thus, we obtain the embeddings of W
∗
-probability spaces

(M,\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\subseteq}}\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k}}{{\subseteq}}\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M,\varphi).

Moreover, for each
x\in M
,
(k\circ j)(x)=(\mathrm{E}_{n}(x))_{{\mathcal{U}}}=x
in
\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M,\varphi)
as
\mathrm{E}_{n}(x)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{so*}}x
. Therefore, the embedding
(M,\varphi)\subseteq\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
is existential.
∎

Corollary 4.29
.

If, in the context of Proposition
4.28
, we have that each
M_{n}
is diffuse and
\operatorname{BC}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})=\mathbb{C}
, then
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
as well.

Remark 4.30
.

We give an example showing that the existential embedding

(M,\varphi)\hookrightarrow\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})

constructed in Proposition
4.28
need not be an elementary embedding. By the proof in
[
16
, Proposition 4.4]
, for
M=R_{\infty}
and a suitable faithful normal state
\varphi
on
M
, there exists an increasing chain of embeddings

(M_{1},\varphi_{1})\subseteq(M_{2},\varphi_{2})\subseteq\dots\subseteq(M,\varphi)=\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})

of
W^{*}
-probability spaces such that every
M_{n}
is a type III
λ
factor for a fixed
0<\lambda<1
, independent of
n
.
Then
E_{M_{n}}(x)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{so*}}x
for every
x\in M
, where
E_{M_{n}}
is the unique
\varphi
-preserving conditional expectation of
M
onto
M_{n}
.
Consequently,
\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
is also a type III
λ
factor, while
M
itself is a type III
1
factor. Thus, the existential embedding
(M,\varphi)\subseteq\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
constructed in Proposition
4.28
is not an elementary embedding.

4.4.
Large centralizers

The following is a special case of a result of Haagerup, Houdayer, Marrakchi, and the first author (see
[
3
, Proposition 3.3]
):

Fact 4.31
.

Suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is a W
∗
-probability space and
\mathcal{U}
is a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Then
(M^{\mathcal{U}}_{\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}})^{\prime}\cap M^{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)^{\mathcal{U}}
.

In particular, if
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
, then
\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}
has large centralizer, that is,
(M^{\mathcal{U}}_{\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}})^{\prime}\cap M^{\mathcal{U}}=\mathbb{C}
. We wish to generalize this latter observation to arbitrary ultraproducts, but under the additional assumption that each state involved has large centralizer:

Proposition 4.32
.

Let
(M_{i},\psi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of W
∗
-probability spaces such that
(M_{i})_{\psi_{i}}^{\prime}\cap M_{i}=\mathbb{C}
for all
i\in I
. Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an ultrafilter on
I
and set
(M,\psi)=\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{i},\psi_{i})
. Then
M_{\psi}^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}.

The proof of Proposition
4.32
(as well as the proof of Fact
4.31
) uses the following result of Popa
[
42
, Lemma 2.3]
:

Fact 4.33
.

Let
M
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra, let
\varphi
be a normal faithful state on
M
, and let
N
be a von Neumann subalgebra of
M_{\varphi}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and suppose that
x\in M\setminus\{0\}
is such that
E_{N}^{\varphi}(x)=0
. Then there exists
u\in\mathbf{U}(N)
such that

\|uxu^{*}-x\|_{\varphi}^{2}>(2-\varepsilon)\|x\|_{\varphi}^{2}.

The argument below was communicated to the first author by Cyril Houdayer during the joint work that resulted in
[
3
]
. This was later extended to
[
3
, Proposition 3.3]
mentioned above. We thank him for his permission to include the proof here.

Proof of Proposition
4.32
.

For each
i\in I
, let
\mathrm{E}_{i}:M_{i}\to M_{\psi_{i}}
denote the canonical conditional expectation and set
\mathrm{E}:=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\mathrm{E}_{i}:M\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{\psi_{i}}
. We first claim that if
x=(x_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}\in M_{\psi}^{\prime}\cap M
satisfies
\mathrm{E}(x)=0
, then
x=0
. Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
\mathrm{E}_{i}(x_{i})=0
for all
i\in I
. By Fact
4.33
(applied to
N=M_{\psi_{i}},\varepsilon=1
), for each
i\in I
, there exists
u_{i}\in\mathbf{U}(M_{\psi_{i}})
such that

\|u_{i}x_{i}u_{i}^{*}-x_{i}\|_{\psi_{i}}^{2}\geq\|x_{i}\|_{\psi_{i}}^{2}.

Since each
M_{\psi_{i}}
is finite, the sequence
(u_{i})_{i\in I}
defines an element
u=(u_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in\mathbf{U}(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{\psi_{i}})\subseteq\mathbf{U}(M_{\psi})
. We then have

\|x\|_{\psi}^{2}\leq\|uxu^{*}-x\|_{\psi}^{2}=0,

whence
x=0
.

We next claim that
(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{\psi_{i}})^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}
. To see this, suppose that
x\in(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{\psi_{i}})^{\prime}\cap M
. Then
\mathrm{E}(x)\in(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{\psi_{i}})^{\prime}\cap M
. Indeed, if
a\in\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{\psi_{i}}
, then
xa=ax
, whence

\mathrm{E}(x)a=\mathrm{E}(xa)=\mathrm{E}(ax)=a\mathrm{E}(x).

It follows that
\mathrm{E}(x)\in\mathcal{Z}(\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}M_{\psi_{i}})
. Since each
M_{\psi_{i}}
is a finite factor, we have
\mathcal{Z}(\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}M_{\psi_{i}})=\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{Z}(M_{\psi_{i}})
. Therefore

\mathrm{E}(x)\in\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{Z}(M_{\psi_{i}})\subseteq\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}((M_{i})_{\psi_{i}}^{\prime}\cap M_{i})=\mathbb{C}.

By the first claim, it follows that

x=(x-\mathrm{E}(x))+\mathrm{E}(x)=0+\mathrm{E}(x)\in\mathbb{C},

as desired.

Since
\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}M_{\psi_{i}}\subseteq M_{\psi}
, we can conclude that

M_{\psi}^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Corollary 4.34
.

Let
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of W
∗
-probability spaces with each
M_{i}
a type III
1
factor with separable predual and trivial bicentralizer. Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a countably incomplete ultrafilter on
I
and let
(M,\varphi)=\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
. Then
M_{\varphi}^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

By Fact
2.6
, for each
i\in I
, there is a faithful normal state
\psi_{i}
on
M_{i}
such that
M_{\psi_{i}}^{\prime}\cap M_{i}=\mathbb{C}
. Set
(M,\psi):=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\psi_{i})
. By Proposition
4.32
, we have that
M_{\psi}^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}
. However, since
\mathcal{U}
is countably incomplete,
M
has strictly homogeneous state space, whence
M_{\varphi}^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}
.
∎

We can use Proposition
4.32
to give a different proof of the axiomatizability of the class of III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer. As in the proof of Proposition
4.24
, let
T
denote the sentences true in all III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer. It suffices to show that a model of
T
has trivial bicentralizer. Suppose
(N,\psi)
is a model of
T
; by Lemma
4.19
, we may assume that
(N,\psi)
is separable. As in the proof of Proposition
4.24
, there is a countable family
(M_{n},\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer and an ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathbb{N}
such that
(N,\psi)\equiv(M,\varphi):=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
. (One can indeed take a countable such family by replacing
T
with a countable dense subset of
T
.) Without loss of generality, by Corollary
4.22
and Downward Löwenheim-Skolem, we may assume that each
M_{n}
is separable. By Fact
2.6

and Connes-Stormer transitivity (see also
[
16
, Proposition 3.12]
), we may assume that each
\varphi_{n}
has large centralizer. By Proposition
4.32
,
\varphi
has large centralizer, whence
(M,\varphi)
has trivial bicentralizer. Since
(N,\psi)
admits an elementary embedding into
(M,\varphi)
(by
\aleph_{1}
-saturation of
(M,\varphi)

[
8
, Proposition 7.6]
), Corollary
4.17
implies that
(N,\psi)
has trivial bicentralizer.

Fact
2.6
states that a III
1
factor
M
with separable predual and trivial bicentralizer always admits a faithful normal state
\varphi
with large centralizer, that is, with
M_{\varphi}^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}
. Whether or not this result holds for nonseparable
M
seems to be unknown. Corollary
4.34
shows that the conclusion of Fact
2.6
holds when
(M,\varphi)
is a countably incomplete ultraproduct of III
1
factors with separable predual. We can use Proposition
4.32
to prove something even more general:

Proposition 4.35
.

Suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is an
\aleph_{0}
-saturated W
∗
-probability space with trivial bicentralizer for which
M
is a III
1
factor. Then
\varphi
has large centralizer.

Proof.

Let
(N,\psi)
be a separable elementary substructure of
(M,\varphi)
. By Corollary
4.17
,
(N,\psi)
still has trivial bicentralizer, whence, by Corollary
4.34
, we have that
\psi^{\mathcal{U}}
has large centralizer, where
{\mathcal{U}}
is a sufficiently good ultrafilter so that
(M,\varphi)
admits an elementary embedding into
(N,\psi)^{\mathcal{U}}
.

Suppose now that
a\in M_{\varphi}^{\prime}\cap M
. It suffices to show that
a\in(N^{\mathcal{U}}_{\psi^{\mathcal{U}}})^{\prime}\cap N^{\mathcal{U}}
. Suppose, towards a contradiction, that there is
b\in N^{\mathcal{U}}_{\psi^{\mathcal{U}}}
such that
\|[a,b]\|_{\varphi}=:\epsilon>0
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
b\in S_{1}(N^{{\mathcal{U}}})
. Then

(N^{\mathcal{U}},\psi^{\mathcal{U}})\models\inf_{y\in S_{1}}\max\left(\sup_{z\in S_{1}}|\psi^{\mathcal{U}}(yz-zy)|,\epsilon\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}\|[a,y]\|_{\psi^{{\mathcal{U}}}}\right).

By elementarity, we have that

(M,\varphi)\models\inf_{y\in S_{1}}\max\left(\sup_{z\in S_{1}}|\varphi(yz-zy)|,\epsilon\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}\|[a,y]\|_{\varphi}\right).

By
\aleph_{0}
-saturation, such an element
y\in S_{1}(M,\varphi)
must actually exist, contradicting the fact that
a\in M_{\varphi}^{\prime}\cap M
.
∎

Corollary 4.36
.

Suppose that
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer. Further suppose that
{\mathcal{U}}
is a countably incomplete ultrafilter on
I
. Set
(M,\varphi):=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
. Then
\varphi
has large centralizer.

Remark 4.37
.

One cannot remove the assumption that the ultrafilter is countably incomplete in Corollary
4.36
. Indeed, suppose that
\mathcal{U}
is a countably complete ultrafilter and
(M,\varphi)
is a W
∗
-probability space with
M
a III
1
factor with separable predual and trivial bicentralizer and
\varphi
any faithful normal state on
M
that does not have large centralizer (such as an ergodic state). Then the ultrapower
(M,\varphi)^{\mathcal{U}}
is isomorphic to
(M,\varphi)
and thus
\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}
does not have large centralizer.
One might instead ask for a variant of Corollary
4.36
for arbitrary ultrafilters that merely asks if the ultraproduct has a state with large centralizer. However, in some sense, this is tantamount to asking if all III
1
factors with trivial bicentralizer have a state with large centralizer. Indeed, if there is a countably complete ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
, then there is a measurable cardinal
\kappa
(see
[
17
, Proposition 17.3.4]
). If
M
is a III
1
factor with trivial bicentralizer and with predual of density character less than
\kappa
and
\varphi
is any state on
M
, the argument in the preceding section shows that the ultrapower
(M,\varphi)^{\mathcal{U}}
is isomorphic to
M
, so asking that this ultrapower have a state with large centralizer is the same as asking that
M
itself has a state with large centralizer.

4.5.
Axioms

We now work towards giving concrete axioms for the above classes. Fix an axiomatizable class
\mathcal{K}
of diffuse W
∗
-probability spaces.

We begin with a calculation:

Lemma 4.38
.

Suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is a W
∗
-probability space and fix
\eta>0
. For all
u\in\mathbf{U}(M)
,
a\in S_{1}(M)
and contraction
v\in\tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{1+\eta}(M)
, the following inequality holds:

\|v^{*}av-u^{*}au\|_{\varphi}\leq(2+\eta)\sqrt{2}\|v-u\|_{\varphi}^{\#}.

Proof.

Since
a\in S_{1}(M)
and
v\in\tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{1+\eta}(M)
, we have

\|(v^{*}-u^{*})av\|_{\varphi}\leq(1+\eta)\|(v^{*}-u^{*})a\|_{\varphi}\leq(1+\eta)\|v^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\varphi}.

Therefore, by using the inequality
\|x\|_{\varphi}\leq\sqrt{2}\|x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|v^{*}av-u^{*}au\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\|(v^{*}-u^{*})av\|_{\varphi}+\|u^{*}a(v-u)\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\eta)\|v^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\varphi}+\|u^{*}a\|\|v-u\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}(2+\eta)\|v-u\|_{\varphi}^{\#}.

∎

Proposition 4.39
.

For all
\varepsilon,\eta>0
, there is
N=N(\varepsilon,\eta)
such that: whenever
(M,\varphi)\in\mathcal{K}
has trivial bicentralizer and
a\in S_{1}(M)
, there are
u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\in\tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{1+\eta}(M)
with

(2)

\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{i}^{*}au_{i}-\varphi(a)\cdot 1\|_{\varphi}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

First, we observe that it suffices to show the following slightly weaker statement: for all
\varepsilon,\eta>0
, there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\varepsilon,\eta)\in\mathbb{N}
such that whenever
(M,\varphi)\in\mathcal{K}
has trivial bicentralizer and
a\in S_{1}(M)
, there are
N\leq N_{0}
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\in\tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{1+\eta}(M)
such that the inequality (
2
) holds. Indeed, for each
\varepsilon,\eta>0
, we may then set
N(\varepsilon,\eta)=N_{0}(\varepsilon,\eta)!
, and if
(M,\varphi)\in\mathcal{K}
satisfies
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)=\mathbb{C}
and
a\in S_{1}(M)
, then we may find
N_{1}\leq N_{0}(\varepsilon,\eta)
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{N_{1}}\in\tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{1+\eta}(M)
such that (
2
) holds where
N
is replaced by
N_{1}
. Set
m:=N(\varepsilon,\eta)/N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the
N(\varepsilon,\eta)
elements
u_{i,j}:=u_{i}\,i=1,\dots,N_{1},\,j=1,\dots,m
in
\tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{1+\eta}(M)
satisfy the
inequality (
2
), where the
u_{i}
’s are replaced by the
u_{i,j}
’s.

Suppose that the weaker statement does not hold for some
\varepsilon,\eta>0
. For each
n\geq 1
, take a counterexample
(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
and
a_{n}\in S_{1}(M_{n})
. Choose a nonprincipal ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathbb{N}
and set
(M,\varphi):=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{n},\varphi_{n})
and
a:=(a_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\in S_{1}(M)
. By Corollary
4.22
,
(M,\varphi)
has trivial bicentralizer, whence we may find unitaries
u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\in M
such that

\|u_{i}\varphi-\varphi u_{i}\|<\delta\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{2}(2+\eta)},\eta\right)

and

\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{i}^{*}au_{i}-\varphi(a)\cdot 1\|_{\varphi}<\varepsilon/2,

where

5.
Is the bicentralizer a zeroset?

Recall from Subsection
2.3
that if there is a counterexample to the bicentralizer problem, then there is a counterexample with a self-bicentralizing state. This allows us to prove the following equivalent formulation of the bicentralizer problem:

Theorem 5.1
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

The bicentralizer problem has a positive solution.

(2)

For every family
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
of W
∗
-probability spaces with
M_{i}
a
\mathrm{III}_{1}
factor and every ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
I

\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\operatorname{BC}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\subseteq\operatorname{BC}\left(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\right).

(3)

The same as (2), but only for ultra
powers
with respect to countable index sets.

Proof.

The implication (1) implies (2) follows from Remark
4.18
while the implication (2) implies (3) is obvious. Assume, towards a contradiction, that (3) holds and yet the bicentralizer problem has a negative solution. By the previous discussion, there is a III
1
factor
M
which has a self-bicentralizing state
\varphi
. Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. By (3), we have

(M^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{\mathcal{U}})=(\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{\mathcal{U}})\subseteq\operatorname{BC}(M^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{\mathcal{U}})\subseteq(M^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}),

whence

(M^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{\mathcal{U}})=\operatorname{BC}(M^{\mathcal{U}},\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}).

This implies that
\varphi^{\mathcal{U}}
is a self-bicentralizing state on

, hence it is an ergodic state. However, this contradicts the fact that

is a type III

is a II

∎

Condition (2) in the previous theorem has model-theoretic meaning, namely, it means that the bicentralizer is a

-zeroset

In this appendix, we give a proof of Proposition

The first lemma is well-known in Schwartz distribution theory.

Lemma A.1
.

Every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
defines a tempered distribution. More precisely,
the map

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\ni f\longmapsto T_{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\langle T_{f},\phi\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\phi(t)\,dt

is a well-defined linear embedding.

Proof.

Fix
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. For each
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, since

\phi
is rapidly decreasing, it belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, whence the
integral

\langle T_{f},\phi\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\phi(t)\,dt

is well-defined and satisfies

|\langle T_{f},\phi\rangle|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}.

Thus
T_{f}
is a linear functional on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

It remains to prove continuity for the Schwartz topology. For every

\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi(t)|\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{1+t^{2}}(1+t^{2})|\phi(t)|\,dt\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}\right)\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}(1+t^{2})|\phi(t)|.

Hence

|\langle T_{f},\phi\rangle|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}\leq\pi\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}(1+t^{2})|\phi(t)|.

The map

\phi\longmapsto\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}(1+t^{2})|\phi(t)|

is one of the standard seminorms defining the Schwartz topology on

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore
T_{f}
is continuous on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,
that is,
T_{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}).

Finally, if
T_{f}=0
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\phi(t)\,dt=0\qquad

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that

f=0
as a distribution, hence
f=0
almost everywhere. Therefore the map

f\mapsto T_{f}
is injective.
∎

The next lemma is certainly known, but we were unable to find a reference in the literature and so we provide a proof.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra, let
\alpha=(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a

\sigma
-weakly continuous one-parameter automorphism group on
M
, and fix
a>0
. Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(1)

x\in M(\alpha,[-a,a])
.

(2)

\alpha_{f}(x)=0
for every

such that
\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\cap[-a,a]=\emptyset

Proof.

We first show that (1) implies (2). Take
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})

\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\cap[-a,a]=\emptyset

\pm a\notin\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})

such that
\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\cap[-(a+r),a+r]=\emptyset
. Let

\hat{g}=1

and
\operatorname{supp}(\hat{g})\subset[-(a+r),a+r]

\operatorname{Sp}_{\alpha}(x)\subset[-a,a]

\alpha_{f}(x)=\alpha_{f}(\alpha_{g}(x))=\alpha_{f*g}(x).

Since
\widehat{f*g}=\hat{f}\hat{g}=0

\alpha_{f}(x)=0

We now prove that (2) implies (1). Take

; we show that

. Take

such that

such that

and
\operatorname{supp}(\hat{f})\subset[t-r,t+r]

Lemma A.3
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a

\sigma
-weakly continuous one-parameter automorphism group on
M
. Fix
a>0
and

x\in M(\alpha,[-a,a])
. For
\omega\in M_{*}
, define

f_{\omega}(t):=\omega(\alpha_{t}(x)).

Then
f_{\omega}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
,
and the Fourier transform of

as a tempered distribution satisfies

\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f_{\omega}})\subset[-a,a].

Proof.

Since
t\mapsto\alpha_{t}(x)
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous,
the scalar function
f_{\omega}

indeed belongs to
C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
.
Hence
f_{\omega}
defines a tempered distribution by

\langle f_{\omega},\phi\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\omega}(t)\phi(t)\,dt,\qquad\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

We must show that
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{f_{\omega}}\subset[-a,a]
.
By definition of the support of a tempered distribution, it is enough to prove
that

\langle\widehat{f_{\omega}},\psi\rangle=0

for every
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\operatorname{supp}(\psi)\cap[-a,a]=\emptyset.

So let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

\operatorname{supp}\psi\cap[-a,a]=\emptyset
. Then, by definition of the
Fourier transform on tempered distributions, we have

\langle\widehat{f_{\omega}},\psi\rangle=\langle f_{\omega},\widehat{\psi}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\omega}(t)\widehat{\psi}(t)\,dt.

Substituting
f_{\omega}(t)=\omega(\alpha_{t}(x))
, we obtain

\langle\widehat{f_{\omega}},\psi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\psi}(t)\,\omega(\alpha_{t}(x))\,dt.

Now set

g:=\widehat{\psi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

Since
x\in M
and
t\mapsto\omega(\alpha_{t}(x))
is bounded measurable, the

\eta\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\,\eta(\alpha_{t}(x))\,dt\qquad\eta\in M_{*}

defines a bounded linear functional on
M_{*}
. Hence, by the duality

, there exists a unique element

\eta(\alpha_{g}(x))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\,\eta(\alpha_{t}(x))\,dt\qquad\eta\in M_{*}.

Applying this with
\eta=\omega

\langle\widehat{f_{\omega}},\psi\rangle=\omega(\alpha_{g}(x)).

\alpha_{h}(x)=0

such that
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{h})\cap[-a,a]=\emptyset
.
It thus suffices to check that
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{g})\cap[-a,a]=\emptyset

For Schwartz functions

\widehat{\widehat{\psi}}(\lambda)=2\pi\,\psi(-\lambda).

Therefore

\widehat{g}(\lambda)=\widehat{\widehat{\psi}}(\lambda)=2\pi\,\psi(-\lambda),

and so
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{g})=-\operatorname{supp}(\psi)

Since the interval
[-a,a]

\operatorname{supp}(\psi)\cap[-a,a]=\emptyset

\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{g})\cap[-a,a]=\emptyset

Lemma A.5

Let

be holomorphic in the lower half-plane

\Pi_{-}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Im}z<0\}

and continuous on
\overline{\Pi_{-}}

|G(t)|\leq M\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R})

and that, for some constants

|G(z)|\leq C(1+|z|)^{N},\qquad z\in\overline{\Pi_{-}}.

Then

|G(z)|\leq M,\qquad z\in\overline{\Pi_{-}}.

The analogous statement holds in the upper half-plane.

Proof.

We prove the lower half-plane case. Fix
0<\alpha<1
. For
\varepsilon>0
, define

G_{\varepsilon}(z):=G(z)\exp\bigl(-\varepsilon(1+iz)^{\alpha}\bigr),\qquad z\in\overline{\Pi_{-}},

where
(1+iz)^{\alpha}
is defined using the principal branch. This is valid
because

\operatorname{Re}(1+iz)=1-\operatorname{Im}z>0

on
\overline{\Pi_{-}}

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\operatorname{Re}(1+it)^{\alpha}\geq 0,

and hence

|G_{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq|G(t)|\leq M.

We show that on the lower semicircle
\{z\in\overline{\Pi_{-}}\mid|z|=R\}
, there is a
constant
c_{\alpha}>0
, independent of
R
, such that for
R\geq 2
,

\operatorname{Re}(1+iz)^{\alpha}\geq c_{\alpha}R^{\alpha}.

Let
z\in\overline{\Pi_{-}}
with
|z|=R
and
w=1+iz
.
Then
\operatorname{Re}(w)>0
and we write

w=\rho e^{i\theta},\qquad\rho=|w|,\qquad-\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}.

Using the principal branch, we have

w^{\alpha}=\rho^{\alpha}e^{i\alpha\theta}.

Therefore

\operatorname{Re}(w^{\alpha})=\rho^{\alpha}\cos(\alpha\theta).

Since
0<\alpha<1
and
-\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
,
we get
-\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}<\alpha\theta<\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}.

Hence

\cos(\alpha\theta)\geq\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}\right)>0.

It follows that

\operatorname{Re}(w^{\alpha})\geq|w|^{\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}\right).

Since
|z|=R
, we have
|w|\geq|iz|-1=R-1\geq\frac{R}{2}
by
R\geq 2
.
Therefore

|w|^{\alpha}\geq\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{\alpha}=2^{-\alpha}R^{\alpha}.

Combining the above estimates, we obtain

\operatorname{Re}(1+iz)^{\alpha}\geq c_{\alpha}R^{\alpha},\qquad c_{\alpha}:=2^{-\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}\right)>0.

Therefore

|G_{\varepsilon}(z)|\leq C(1+R)^{N}\exp(-\varepsilon c_{\alpha}R^{\alpha})\longrightarrow 0\qquad(R\to\infty)

uniformly on the lower semicircle.

Let

D_{R}^{-}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<R,\ \operatorname{Im}z<0\}.

This is the lower half-disc of radius
R
. Its boundary consists of two parts:
[-R,R]\subset\mathbb{R}

and

\Gamma_{R}^{-}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|=R,\ \operatorname{Im}z\leq 0\}.

The function

G_{\varepsilon}(z)=G(z)\exp\bigl(-\varepsilon(1+iz)^{\alpha}\bigr)

is holomorphic in
D_{R}^{-}
and continuous on
\overline{D_{R}^{-}}
. Hence the
maximum modulus principle gives

\sup_{z\in D_{R}^{-}}|G_{\varepsilon}(z)|\leq\sup_{z\in\partial D_{R}^{-}}|G_{\varepsilon}(z)|.

On
[-R,R]
, we have already shown that
|G_{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq M.

On
\Gamma_{R}^{-}
, the preceding estimate gives

|G_{\varepsilon}(z)|\leq C(1+R)^{N}\exp(-\varepsilon c_{\alpha}R^{\alpha}).

Therefore

\sup_{z\in D_{R}^{-}}|G_{\varepsilon}(z)|\leq\max\left\{M,\,C(1+R)^{N}\exp(-\varepsilon c_{\alpha}R^{\alpha})\right\}\xrightarrow{R\to\infty}M.

Consequently, for every fixed
z_{0}\in\Pi_{-}
, choosing
R>|z_{0}|
and
letting
R\to\infty
gives

|G_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})|\leq\limsup_{R\to\infty}\max\left\{M,\,C(1+R)^{N}\exp(-\varepsilon c_{\alpha}R^{\alpha})\right\}=M.

Since
z_{0}\in\Pi_{-}
was arbitrary, we obtain

|G_{\varepsilon}(z)|\leq M,\qquad z\in\Pi_{-}.

By continuity of
G_{\varepsilon}
on
\overline{\Pi_{-}}
, the same estimate
also holds on the boundary line
\mathbb{R}
. Thus

|G_{\varepsilon}(z)|\leq M,\qquad z\in\overline{\Pi_{-}}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma A.6
.

Let
h\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, and regard
h
as a tempered distribution. Assume
that

\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{h})\subset[0,b]

for some
b\geq 0
. Then
h
has an entire extension
H
, and

|H(z)|\leq\|h\|_{\infty},\qquad\operatorname{Im}z\leq 0.

Similarly, if

\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{h})\subset[-b,0],

then
h
has an entire extension
H
, and

|H(z)|\leq\|h\|_{\infty},\qquad\operatorname{Im}z\geq 0.

Proof.

We prove the case

\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{h})\subset[0,b].

Since
\widehat{h}
is a compactly supported tempered distribution, the
Paley–Wiener theorem (see, for example,
[
25
, Theorem 7.3.1]
) gives an entire function
H
whose restriction
to
\mathbb{R}
agrees with
h
as a tempered distribution. More explicitly,
with the present Fourier transform convention,

\widehat{u}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\lambda}u(t)\,dt,

one may write

H(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\langle\widehat{h}(\lambda),e^{-iz\lambda}\right\rangle.

This formula is understood in the standard sense that a compactly supported
distribution may be paired with a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
.

Since
H|_{\mathbb{R}}
and
h
define the same tempered distribution, and
both are continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
, they agree pointwise:

H(t)=h(t),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus

|H(t)|\leq\|h\|_{\infty},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

It remains to check the growth condition in the lower half-plane. Since

\widehat{h}
is a distribution of finite order supported in
[0,b]
, there
exist constants
C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,

\left|\langle\widehat{h},\varphi\rangle\right|\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sup_{\lambda\in[0,b]}|\varphi^{(j)}(\lambda)|.

Applying this to

\varphi_{z}(\lambda):=e^{-iz\lambda},

we get, for
\operatorname{Im}z\leq 0
,

|\varphi_{z}^{(j)}(\lambda)|=|(-iz)^{j}e^{-iz\lambda}|=|z|^{j}e^{(\operatorname{Im}z)\lambda}\leq|z|^{j}\qquad(0\leq\lambda\leq b).

Therefore, there is some constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

|H(z)|\leq C^{\prime}(1+|z|)^{N},\qquad\operatorname{Im}z\leq 0.

By Lemma
A.5
,

|H(z)|\leq\|h\|_{\infty},\qquad\operatorname{Im}z\leq 0.

The case

\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{h})\subset[-b,0]

is the same, using the upper half-plane instead. Indeed, if

\operatorname{Im}z\geq 0
and
\lambda\in[-b,0]
, then

|e^{-iz\lambda}|=e^{(\operatorname{Im}z)\lambda}\leq 1.

Thus the same argument gives

|H(z)|\leq\|h\|_{\infty},\qquad\operatorname{Im}z\geq 0.

∎

We are ready to prove Lemma
A.4
.

Proof of Lemma
A.4
.

First consider the lower half-plane direction. Define

h(t):=e^{-iat}f(t).

Then

h\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\|h\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty}.

With the Fourier transform convention

\widehat{u}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\lambda}u(t)\,dt,

we see that in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
,

\widehat{h}(\lambda)=\widehat{f}(\lambda-a).

By definition of equality of tempered distributions, this means that for every

\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

\langle\widehat{h},\varphi\rangle=\langle\widehat{f},\varphi(\cdot+a)\rangle.

Indeed, recall that the Fourier transform of a tempered distribution is defined by

\langle\widehat{T},\varphi\rangle=\langle T,\widehat{\varphi}\rangle,\qquad\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{h},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle h,\widehat{\varphi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle e^{-iat}f(t),\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\lambda}\varphi(\lambda)\,d\lambda\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f(t),\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it(\lambda-a)}\varphi(\lambda)\,d\lambda\right\rangle.

Putting

\mu=\lambda-a,\qquad\lambda=\mu+a,

we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it(\lambda-a)}\varphi(\lambda)\,d\lambda=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\mu}\varphi(\mu+a)\,d\mu.

Thus

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{h},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f(t),\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\mu}\varphi(\mu+a)\,d\mu\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{f},\varphi(\cdot+a)\rangle.

This proves

\widehat{h}(\lambda)=\widehat{f}(\lambda-a)

in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
Therefore

\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{h})\subset[0,2a].

By Lemma
A.6
,
h
has an entire extension
H
such
that

|H(z)|\leq\|h\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty},\qquad\operatorname{Im}z\leq 0.

Define

F_{-}(z):=e^{iaz}H(z).

Then
F_{-}
is entire and, for real
t
,

F_{-}(t)=e^{iat}H(t)=e^{iat}h(t)=f(t).

Thus
F_{-}
is an entire extension of
f
. For
s\geq 0
, we have

|F_{-}(t-is)|=|e^{ia(t-is)}|\,|H(t-is)|=e^{as}|H(t-is)|\leq e^{as}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Hence

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{-}(t-is)|\leq e^{as}\|f\|_{\infty},\qquad s\geq 0.

Now consider the upper half-plane direction. Define

k(t):=e^{iat}f(t).

Then

k\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\|k\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty},

and

\widehat{k}(\lambda)=\widehat{f}(\lambda+a).

Therefore

\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{k})\subset[-2a,0].

By Lemma
A.6
again,
k
has an entire extension
K
such
that

|K(z)|\leq\|k\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty},\qquad\operatorname{Im}z\geq 0.

Define

F_{+}(z):=e^{-iaz}K(z).

Then
F_{+}
is entire and, for real
t
,

F_{+}(t)=e^{-iat}K(t)=e^{-iat}k(t)=f(t).

Thus
F_{+}
is also an entire extension of
f
. Since
F_{-}
and
F_{+}

agree on
\mathbb{R}
, they agree on all of
\mathbb{C}
by the identity
theorem. Denote this common entire extension simply by
f
.

If
s<0
, put
r=-s>0
. Then
t-is=t+ir
, and

|f(t-is)|=|f(t+ir)|=|F_{+}(t+ir)|=|e^{-ia(t+ir)}|\,|K(t+ir)|=e^{ar}|K(t+ir)|\leq e^{ar}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Since
r=|s|
, this gives

|f(t-is)|\leq e^{a|s|}\|f\|_{\infty}\qquad(s<0).

Combining the estimates for
s\geq 0
and
s<0
, we obtain

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|f(t-is)|\leq e^{a|s|}\|f\|_{\infty}

for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.14
.

The implication (ii)
\implies
(i) follows from
[
24
, Lemma 2.5]
while the implication (ii)
\implies
(iii) follows from Lemma
2.11
. It remains to show that (i)
\implies
(ii).
Assume
x\in M(\sigma^{\varphi},[-a,a])
.
Set
\alpha_{t}:=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}
. For each
\omega\in M_{*}
, consider
the bounded continuous scalar-valued function

f_{\omega}(t):=\omega(\alpha_{t}(x)),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

By Lemma
A.3
, the Fourier transform of
f_{\omega}
regarded as a tempered distribution is supported on
[-a,a]
. Thus, by Lemma
A.4
,
f_{\omega}
extends to an entire analytic function which satisfies:

|f_{\omega}(t-is)|\leq e^{a|s|}\|f_{\omega}\|_{\infty}\leq e^{a|s|}\|\omega\|\,\|x\|,\qquad s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, for
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in M_{*}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, the entire functions
f_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}
and
f_{\omega_{1}}+f_{\omega_{2}}
agree on
\mathbb{R}
, whence they agree on
\mathbb{C}
. Similarly,
f_{\lambda\omega}=\lambda f_{\omega}
on
\mathbb{C}
. Thus, for each fixed

, the map
M_{*}\ni\omega\mapsto f_{\omega}(z)\in\mathbb{C}

Since the function

is an

Appendix B
Zerosets

We summarize here the theory of definability in continuous logic as presented in the second author’s article
[
18
]
. This will help explain the terminology “zeroset” used after Theorem
5.1
.

Fix a theory
T
in a language
L
. (In this article,
L
will be the language of W
∗
-probability spaces introduced in the previous subsection and
T
a theory extending the theory of W
∗
-probability spaces, most often the theory T
{}_{\mathrm{III}_{1}}
of III
1
factors). We let
\operatorname{Mod}(T)
denote the category whose objects are models of
T
and whose morphisms are elementary embeddings. We also let
\operatorname{Met}
denote the category whose objects are bounded metric spaces and whose morphisms are isometric embeddings.

Fix a finite sequence of variables
\vec{x}
(possibly ranging over different sorts). By a
T
-functor over
\vec{x}
we mean a functor
X:\operatorname{Mod}(T)\to\operatorname{Met}
such that, for every model
M\models T
,
X(M)
is a closed subset of
M^{\vec{x}}
and such that
X
is given by restriction on morphisms. The main example of a
T
-functor we will have in mind is the functor which assigns to each W
∗
-probability space its bicentralizer (restricted to the set of totally
1
-bounded elements).

A main source of
T
-functors come from
T
-formulae. To explain this notion, one defines a pseudometric
d_{T,\vec{x}}
on formulae
\varphi(\vec{x})
by

d_{T,\vec{x}}(\varphi(\vec{x}),\psi(\vec{x})):=\sup\{|\varphi(\vec{a})^{M}-\psi(\vec{a})^{M}|\ :\ M\models T,\vec{a}\in M\}.

Thus, two formulae
\varphi
and
\psi
are
d_{T,\vec{x}}
close if they are uniformly close in all models of
T
; in particular, they are distance
0
from each other if they are equivalent in all models of
T
. One separates and completes this pseudometric space to obtain the complete metric space of
T
-formulae
. If
\theta(\vec{x})
is a
T
-formula, then to every model
M\models T
, one has a uniformly continuous function
\theta^{M}:M^{\vec{x}}\to\mathbb{R}
whose range is contained in some bounded interval in
\mathbb{R}
(both the modulus of uniform continuity and bound on the range of
\theta^{M}
are uniform over all models of
T
); this function is called the
interpretation of
\theta
in
M
. If
\theta(\vec{x})
is a
T
-formula, then one obtains a
T
-functor, the
zeroset of
\theta
, denoted
Z(\theta)
, defined by

Z(\theta)(M):=\{\vec{a}\in M^{\vec{x}}\ :\ \theta^{M}(\vec{a})=0\}.

More generally, by a
T
-function over
\vec{x}
we mean a mapping whose domain is the set of all pairs
(M,\vec{a})
with
M\models T
and
\vec{a}\in M^{\vec{x}}
and whose co-domain is a bounded set in
\mathbb{R}
. As we just saw, interpretations of
T
-formulae give examples of
T
-functions (the
T
-formula
\theta(\vec{x})
sends the pair
(M,\vec{a})
to the value
\theta^{M}(\vec{a})
);
T
-functions of this form are called
realized
.

If
X
is a
T
-functor, then one obtains the
T
-function
d(\vec{x},X)
which, upon input
(M,\vec{a})
, returns the value
d(\vec{a},X(M))
.

Given a
T
-function
\Phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
and a
T
-functor
X
, one may quantify over
X
to obtain a new
T
-function. More specifically, define
\sup_{\vec{x}\in X}\Phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
to be the
T
-function which, upon input
(M,\vec{b})
, returns the value
\sup\{\Phi(M,\vec{a},\vec{b})\ :\ \vec{a}\in X(M)\}
. One defines
\inf_{\vec{x}\in X}\Phi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
similarly.

We say that a nonnegative
T
-function
\Phi(\vec{x})
is
almost-near
if: for all
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that, for all
M\models T
and
\vec{a}\in M^{\vec{x}}
, if
\Phi(M,\vec{a})<\delta
, then there is
\vec{b}\in M^{\vec{x}}
with
d(\vec{a},\vec{b})\leq\epsilon
for which
\Phi(M,\vec{b})=0
.

Here is the main theorem about definability in continuous logic (see
[
18
, Theorem 2.13]
):

Fact B.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a
T
-functor over
\vec{x}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

For all
T
-formulae
\theta(\vec{x},\vec{y})
, the
T
-functions
\sup_{\vec{x}\in X}\theta(\vec{x},\vec{y})
and
\inf_{\vec{x}\in X}\theta(\vec{x},\vec{y})
are realized.

(2)

The
T
-function
d(\vec{x},X)
is realized.

(3)

X
is the zeroset of an almost-near
T
-formula.

(4)

For all families
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
of models of
T
and all ultrafilters
\mathcal{U}
on
I
, we have

X(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{i})=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X(M_{i}).

Any
T
-functor satisfying the above equivalent conditions is called a
T
-definable set
.

In order to explain the connection between Theorem
5.1
, we will need a characterization of being a zeroset in a similar spirit to Fact
B.1
. This characterization was observed by Bradd Hart, Ward Henson, and the second author several years ago. We thank them for their permission to include this observation here.

Once again suppose that
T
is a theory and
X
is a
T
-functor over
\vec{x}
. We will list three properties a
T
-functor may or may not have that will end up characterizing those
T
-functors that are zerosets.

We recall that the space
\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
of complete types in
T
over
\vec{x}
is equipped with its logic topology, which is merely the induced topology from viewing
\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
as a subset of the dual of the normed space of
T
-formulae over
\vec{x}
(see
[
18
, Section 2.4]
). More concretely, if
(p_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of complete types with realizations
\vec{a}_{i}
in models
M_{i}
of
T
and
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter on
I
, then setting
\vec{a}:=(\vec{a}_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{i}
and
p
the complete type of
\vec{a}
in
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{i}
, we have that
\lim_{\mathcal{U}}p_{i}=p
.

Set
\hat{X}:=\{p\in\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)\ :\ p=\operatorname{tp}^{M}(\vec{a})\text{ for some }M\models T\text{ with }\vec{a}\in X(M)\}
.

The above paragraph thus yields:

Lemma B.2
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

\hat{X}
is a closed subspace of
\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
.

(2)

For any family
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
of models of
T
and ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
I
, we have

\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X(M_{i})\subseteq X(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{i}).

In general, one always has
X(M)\subseteq\{\vec{a}\in M^{\vec{x}}\ \operatorname{tp}^{M}(\vec{a})\in\hat{X}\}
. In general, one cannot recover
X
from
\hat{X}
. In the case of zerosets, such a recovery is possible as shown by the following easy lemma:

Lemma B.3
.

For a
T
-functor
X
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

For any model
M\models T
, we have
X(M)=\{\vec{a}\in M\ :\ \operatorname{tp}^{M}(\vec{a})\in\hat{X}\}
.

(2)

For any pair
M,N\models T
of models of
T
with
M\preceq N
, we have

X(N)\cap M=X(M).

Proof.

It is clear that (1) implies (2). Now assume (2) and fix
M\models T
and
\vec{a}\in M^{\vec{x}}
for which
\operatorname{tp}^{M}(\vec{a})\in\hat{X}
; we wish to show that
\vec{a}\in X(M)
. By definition, we have that
\operatorname{tp}^{M}(\vec{a})=\operatorname{tp}^{P}(\vec{b})
for some
P\models T
and
\vec{b}\in X(P)
. There are then elementary embeddings
i:M\hookrightarrow N
and
j:P\hookrightarrow N
such that
i(\vec{a})=j(\vec{b})
. By applying (2) twice, we have that

\vec{b}\in X(P)\Rightarrow j(\vec{b})\in X(N)\Rightarrow\vec{a}\in X(M),

as desired.
∎

In light of the second item in the previous lemma, we call a
T
-functor satisfying the assumptions in the lemma an
elementary
T
-functor
. By Łos’ theorem, zerosets are indeed elementary
T
-functors.

Finally, we note the following:

Lemma B.4
.

If
X=Z(\theta)
for some
T
-formula
\theta
, then
\hat{X}
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
.

Proof.

For
n\geq 1
, let
U_{n}
denote the basic open subset of
\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
determined by the open condition
|\theta(\vec{x})|<1/n
. Then
\hat{X}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}U_{n}
, whence
\hat{X}
is
G_{\delta}
.
∎

Remark B.5
.

If the language is separable, then closed subsets of
\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
are automatically
G_{\delta}
.

We are ready for the promised characterization of zerosets:

Theorem B.6
.

Suppose that
X
is a
T
-functor over
\vec{x}
. Then
X
is a zeroset if and only if it is elementary and
\hat{X}
is a closed,
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
.

Proof.

We have already observed that zerosets satisfy the enumerated properties. Conversely, suppose that
X
is an elementary
T
-functor for which
\hat{X}
is a closed,
G_{\delta}
subset of
S_{\vec{x}}(T)
. Since
\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
is a compact Hausdorff space, there is a continuous function
f:\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\hat{X}=Z(f)
, where
Z(f)
denotes the zeroset of
f
. It is a well-known and straightforward consequence of the Stone-Weierstrauss theorem that such a continuous function
f
must be given by evaluation at a
T
-formula
\theta
, that is,
f(p)=p(\theta)
for all
p\in\mathbf{S}_{\vec{x}}(T)
. It follows that
X(M)=Z(\theta^{M})
for all models
M
of
T
.
∎

We now return to bicentralizers. A particular consequence of Corollary
4.17
is that, letting
T_{W^{*}}
denote the theory of W
∗
-probability spaces, we have that the
T_{W^{*}}
-functor which assigns to
(M,\varphi)
the bicentralizer
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)
(or, technically, the set of totally 1-bounded elements of
\operatorname{BC}(M,\varphi)
) is an elementary
T_{W^{*}}
-functor and is thus also an elementary
T_{\mathrm{III}_{1}}
-functor. Thus, we are entitled to use the following terminology:

Definition B.7
.

Call a type
p\in\mathbf{S}_{x}(T_{\mathrm{III}_{1}})
a
bicentralizer type
if some (equiv. any) realization of
p
lies in the bicentralizer.

To be clear, here we are treating the bicentralizer as a
T_{\mathrm{III}_{1}}
-functor over
x
, with
x
a single variable of sort
S_{1}
. To reiterate the definition: to say that
p
is a bicentralizer type is to say that, for any W
