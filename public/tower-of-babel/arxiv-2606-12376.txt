A note on a conjecture of Ng
Andrew Pearce-Crump

Abstract
. In this note we give a lower bound for the second moment of a ratio of zeta functions summed over the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function that is half the size of the conjectured value. Our result is conditional on the assumption of the Riemann Hypothesis and that all the non-trivial zeros of the zeta function are simple.



1.
Introduction

Let
\zeta(s)
denote the Riemann zeta function. In this note, we address Question 5 Problem 1 from the recent Comparative Prime Number Theory Problem List
[
3
]
. This problem was suggested by Nathan Ng, who in his thesis
[
6
]
made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Ng)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and that the non-trivial zeros
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{2}\sim\frac{T}{2\pi}

as
T\to\infty
.

The assumption of the simplicity of the zeros in the above conjecture ensures that the sum is well-defined.

Remark
.

In a private communication
[
8
]
, Ng noted that in stating Conjecture 1 he implicitly assumed the Riemann Hypothesis and the simplicity of the zeros, but that the Riemann Hypothesis may be removed: he expects the conjecture to remain true assuming only that the zeros are simple. The simplicity assumption can in turn be removed from the statement by passing to the residue formulation

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\operatorname*{Res}_{s=\rho}\frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}\right|^{2}\sim\frac{T}{2\pi},

whose summand is well-defined regardless of the multiplicity of
\rho
and equals
\zeta(2\rho)/\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)
at a simple zero, recovering Conjecture 1. As multiple zeros are expected not to occur, and in any case to be rare, the two formulations should share the same main term.

We further note that Conjecture 1 is the case
s=2
of a more general conjecture of Ng
[
7
, Conjecture 1.11]
, which for
0\leq s<3
predicts

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{s}\sim\frac{G^{2}\big(2-\tfrac{s}{2}\big)}{G(3-s)}\,a\big(-\tfrac{s}{2}\big)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{s/2}(n)}{n^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{(s/2-1)^{2}}dt,

where
d_{k}(n)
is the
k
-th divisor function,
G(z)
is the Barnes
G
-function given by

G(z+1)=(2\pi)^{z/2}\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\big(z^{2}+\gamma_{0}z^{2}+z\big)\right)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(1+\tfrac{z}{n}\Big)^{n}e^{-z+z^{2}/2n},

where
\gamma_{0}
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant, and where
a(z)
is the arithmetic factor

a(z)=\prod_{p}\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{p}\Big)^{z^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\Gamma(m+z)}{m!\,\Gamma(z)}\right)^{2}p^{-m}.

Taking
s=2
recovers Conjecture 1.

We establish the following lower bound, making the same simplicity assumption for the reason given in the above remark.

Theorem 2
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and that the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}-\varepsilon\right)T

for
T
sufficiently large.

We note that this lower bound is half the conjectured size. Ng based his conjecture on techniques from Gonek’s MSRI talk
[
2
]
, where Gonek made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3

(Gonek)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and that the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}}\sim\frac{3}{\pi^{3}}T

as
T\to\infty
.

Milinovich and Ng
[
4
]
proved a lower bound for Gonek’s conjecture, which also differs by a factor of two. Specifically, they showed the following result.

Theorem 4

(Milinovich-Ng)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and that the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple. Then, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{1}{|\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}}\geq\left(\frac{3}{2\pi^{3}}-\varepsilon\right)T

for
T
sufficiently large.

2.
Setup of the Results

We follow a similar strategy of proof to that of Milinovich-Ng
[
4
]
, who based the idea of their proof on the work of Rudnick and Soundararajan
[
9
]
.

Let
\xi=T^{\vartheta}
where
0<\vartheta<1
is fixed. Let
P(x)
be a polynomial satisfying
P(0)=0
and
P(1)=1
, and define the Dirichlet polynomial

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(s)=\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{s}}P\left(\frac{\log\xi/n}{\log\xi}\right)

where
\lambda(n)
is the Liouville function, defined by

\lambda(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n=1,\\
(-1)^{\Omega(n)}&\text{if }n>1,\end{cases}

where
\Omega(n)
denotes the number of prime factors of
n
counted with multiplicity. The Liouville function is completely multiplicative and generates the Dirichlet series of
\zeta(2s)/\zeta(s)
. That is, for
\Re s>1
,

(2.1)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}.

Thus
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(s)
is a smoothed Dirichlet polynomial approximating
\zeta(2s)/\zeta(s)
, the function whose residues at the non-trivial zeros,
\zeta(2\rho)/\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)
, are precisely the summands appearing in Conjecture 1. This motivates its use as a mollifier and explains the appearance of
\zeta(2s)/\zeta(s)
in the analysis of
M_{1}
below.

Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis, for any non-trivial zero
\rho=1/2+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
we see that
\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(\rho)}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-\rho)
. From this observation and Cauchy’s inequality, it follows that

(2.2)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{|M_{1}|^{2}}{M_{2}}

where

M_{1}=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-\rho)\quad\text{ and }\quad M_{2}=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(\rho)|^{2}.

Remark
.

The two hypotheses play distinct roles. The simplicity of the zeros is used both to guarantee that the sum in Conjecture 1 is well-defined (
\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)\neq 0
) and to ensure that the poles of
\zeta(2s)/\zeta(s)
at the non-trivial zeros are simple, with residue
\zeta(2\rho)/\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)
, in the residue computations of Sections
3
and
4
. The Riemann Hypothesis is used to place all non-trivial zeros on the critical line, which yields the symmetry
\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(\rho)}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-\rho)
underlying (
2.2
), as well as in the zero-free region and size estimates employed in Sections
3
and
4
.

To prove Theorem
2
, we must evaluate the asymptotics for
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. In Section 3 and Section 4 respectively, we establish the following asymptotic estimates.

Proposition 5
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis, let
0<\vartheta<1
be fixed, and assume the zeros of
\zeta(s)
are all simple. Then there exists a sequence
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau_{n}\}_{n=3}^{\infty}
such that
n<\tau_{n}\leq n+1
and for
T\in\mathcal{T}
we have

(2.3)

M_{1}=\frac{T}{2\pi}(\log T)\;\vartheta\int_{0}^{1}P(x)dx+O(T).

Proposition 6
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis and let
0<\vartheta<1
be fixed. Then

(2.4)

M_{2}=\frac{T}{2\pi}(\log T)^{2}\left[\vartheta\int_{0}^{1}P(x)^{2}dx+\vartheta^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}P(x)dx\right)^{2}\right]+O(T\log T).

To prove these two propositions, we will use a mean value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials
[
5
,
11
]
, which serves as a central tool for evaluating the contour integrals that will appear in our proofs.

Lemma 7

(Montgomery and Vaughan)
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be sequences of complex numbers. Then for any real number
T>0
, we have

(2.5)

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-it}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}n^{it}\right)dt=T\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}+O\left(\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right).

In Section 5, we substitute the asymptotics in these two propositions into equation (
2.2
) and optimise the polynomial
P(x)
to rigorously prove Theorem
2
.

3.
Proof of Proposition
5

Let
T\geq 4
and set
c=1+1/\log T
. We know
[
10
]
that under the Riemann Hypothesis, there exists a sequence
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau_{n}\}_{n=3}^{\infty}
,
n<\tau_{n}\leq n+1
, and a fixed constant
A>0
such that

(3.1)

|\zeta(\sigma+i\tau_{m})|^{-1}\ll\exp\left(\frac{A\log\tau_{m}}{\log\log\tau_{m}}\right)

uniformly for
1/2\leq\sigma\leq 2
. Assuming all the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple, Cauchy’s residue theorem implies that for
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}+\int_{c+iT}^{1-c+iT}+\int_{1-c+iT}^{1-c+i}+\int_{1-c+i}^{c+i}\right)\frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-s)\ ds

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},

say.

We will show that the main contribution to
M_{1}
comes from the right vertical contour
I_{1}
, and that the integrals over the other contours only contribute to the error term.

By (
2.1
), the Dirichlet series for
\zeta(2s)/\zeta(s)
is generated by the Liouville function
\lambda(m)
. For ease of notation write
P_{n}=P\left(\frac{\log\xi/n}{\log\xi}\right)
. We can rewrite
I_{1}
as

I_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda(m)}{m^{c+it}}\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\lambda(n)P_{n}}{n^{1-c-it}}dt.

Applying Lemma
7
with
a_{m}=\lambda(m)m^{-c}
and
b_{n}=\lambda(n)P_{n}n^{-1+c}
, we obtain

I_{1}=\frac{(T-1)}{2\pi}\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\lambda(n)^{2}P_{n}}{n}+O\left(\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda(m)^{2}}{m^{2c-1}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\lambda(n)^{2}P_{n}^{2}}{n^{1-2c}}\right)^{1/2}\right),

where the error term is bounded by

O\left(\zeta(2c-1)^{1/2}\left(\xi^{2c}\right)^{1/2}\right)\ll\xi\sqrt{\log T},

recalling that
c=1+1/\log T
, so that
\zeta(2c-1)\asymp\log T
and
\xi^{c}\asymp\xi
. Since
\lambda(n)^{2}=1
for all integers
n
, applying partial summation yields

\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{1}{n}P\left(\frac{\log\xi/n}{\log\xi}\right)=\log\xi\int_{0}^{1}P(x)dx+O(1).

Hence, we conclude that

(3.2)

I_{1}=\frac{T}{2\pi}(\log\xi)\int_{0}^{1}P(x)dx+O(\xi\sqrt{\log T}+T).

We now show that the remaining integrals
I_{2},I_{3},
and
I_{4}
are absorbed into the error term. To estimate the upper horizontal contour
I_{2}
, we use the convexity bound for
\zeta(s)
and the trivial bound
|\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-\sigma-iT)|\ll\xi^{\max(\sigma,0)}
. Using the bound (
3.1
), we have

I_{2}\ll\exp\left(\frac{A\log T}{\log\log T}\right)\int_{1-c}^{c}|\zeta(2\sigma+2iT)|\xi^{\sigma}d\sigma.

For
\sigma\geq 1/2
the integrand is
\ll\xi^{\sigma}\log T\leq\xi\log T
, while for
1-c\leq\sigma<1/2
the convexity bound gives
|\zeta(2\sigma+2iT)|\ll T^{1/2-\sigma+o(1)}
, so that the integrand is
\ll T^{1/2-\sigma(1-\vartheta)+o(1)}\leq T^{1/2+o(1)}
. Hence

(3.3)

I_{2}\ll\left(\xi+T^{1/2}\right)\exp\left(\frac{A\log T}{\log\log T}\right)=o(T).

To estimate the left vertical contour
I_{3}
we cannot bound the integrand trivially: on
\Re s=1-c
one has
2\Re s=-2/\log T<0
, so by the functional equation
|\zeta(2s)|\asymp|t|^{1/2}\log T
, while
|\zeta(s)|^{-1}\asymp|t|^{-1/2}\log T
, and their product
\asymp(\log T)^{2}
does not decay in
t
; the trivial bound would give
I_{3}\ll T(\log T)^{2}
, which exceeds the main term. In contrast to Milinovich-Ng
[
4
]
, where the numerator is merely
1/\zeta(s)
and decays like
|t|^{-1/2}
, here the extra factor
\zeta(2s)
cancels that decay, so we must instead exploit the oscillation of
\chi(2s)/\chi(s)
.

Applying the functional equation
\zeta(w)=\chi(w)\zeta(1-w)
to both
w=2s
and
w=s
gives

\frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}\;=\;\frac{\chi(2s)}{\chi(s)}\,\frac{\zeta(1-2s)}{\zeta(1-s)}\;=:\;G(t)\,\frac{\zeta(1-2s)}{\zeta(1-s)},\qquad s=(1-c)+it,

where, since
\Re(1-2s)=2c-1>1
and
\Re(1-s)=c>1
, both reflected
\zeta
-factors lie in the region of absolute convergence. Expanding, we have the product

\frac{\zeta(1-2s)}{\zeta(1-s)}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\beta_{n}n^{it}

with

\beta_{n}=\sum_{a^{2}bm=n,\,m\leq\xi}a^{-(2c-1)}\mu(b)b^{-c}\lambda(m)P_{m}

so that

I_{3}\;=\;-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\geq 1}\beta_{n}\,J_{n},\qquad J_{n}:=\int_{1}^{T}G(t)\,n^{it}\,dt.

By Stirling,

|G(t)|=|\chi(2s)/\chi(s)|\asymp 2^{2c-3/2}(t/2\pi)^{c-1}=2^{2c-3/2}(t/2\pi)^{1/\log T},

which is monotone increasing with
\sup_{1\leq t\leq T}|G(t)|<\sqrt{2}\,e
, an absolute constant. Also,
\arg\chi(\sigma+iu)=-u\log(u/2\pi)+u+\tfrac{\pi}{4}+O(1/u)
gives, with
\psi_{n}(t):=\arg G(t)+t\log n
,

\psi_{n}^{\prime}(t)=-\log\!\frac{2t}{\pi n}+O\!\Big(\frac{1}{t}\Big),\qquad\psi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{t}+O\!\Big(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\Big),

the second derivative being of constant sign and independent of
n
, with
|\psi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t)|\geq T^{-1}(1+o(1))
on
[1,T]
. Since
|G|
has bounded variation
\ll 1
, the van der Corput second-derivative test
[
10
]
yields, uniformly in
n
,

|J_{n}|\;\ll\;\big(\min_{[1,T]}|\psi_{n}^{\prime\prime}|\big)^{-1/2}\;\ll\;\sqrt{T}.

Finally,

\sum_{n}|\beta_{n}|\leq\zeta(2c-1)\,\dfrac{\zeta(c)}{\zeta(2c)}\,\Big(\max_{[0,1]}|P|\Big)\zeta(c)\ll(\log T)^{3},

since
\zeta(c)\ll\log T
and
\zeta(2c-1)\ll\log T
.

Therefore,

(3.4)

I_{3}\;\ll\;\Big(\sum_{n}|\beta_{n}|\Big)\,\sup_{n}|J_{n}|\;\ll\;\sqrt{T}\,(\log T)^{3}\;=\;o(T).

Finally,
I_{4}=O(1)
since the path of integration is of finite length and the integrand is bounded. Combining (
3.2
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
) with this
O(1)
from
I_{4}
and substituting
\log\xi=\vartheta\log T
completes the proof of Proposition
5
.

4.
Proof of Proposition
6

We now evaluate the sum
M_{2}
. We again let
T\geq 4
and
c=1+1/\log T
. Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis, we have

M_{2}=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(\rho)\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-\rho).

We express this sum as an integral using Cauchy, so

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}+\int_{c+iT}^{1-c+iT}+\int_{1-c+iT}^{1-c+i}+\int_{1-c+i}^{c+i}\right)\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(s)\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-s)\ ds

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4},

say.

In order to evaluate the integrals over the horizontal portions of the contour (
J_{2}
and
J_{4}
), we impose specific conditions on
T
. By a well-known argument (see Davenport
[
1
]
, p. 108), there exists a sequence of
T
such that
|\gamma-T|\gg 1/\log T
for all ordinates
\gamma
, and

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma+iT)\ll(\log T)^{2}

uniformly for all
1-c\leq\sigma\leq c
. We further use the trivial bound
|\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(\sigma+it)|\ll\xi^{1-\sigma}
. Applying these bounds, the horizontal integrals satisfy
J_{2}\ll\xi(\log T)^{2}
and
J_{4}\ll\xi
.

We relate the left vertical contour
J_{3}
to
J_{1}
via the logarithmic derivative of the functional equation, which after an application of Stirling’s formula gives

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(1-s)=-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)+\log\frac{t}{2\pi}+O\left(\frac{1}{|t|}\right)

uniformly for
1\leq|t|\leq T
.
Changing variables
s\to 1-s
and taking the complex conjugate, we find

M_{2}=K+2\Re J_{1}+O(\xi(\log T)^{2}),

where

K=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\log\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(c+it)\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-c-it)dt.

To evaluate
K
, let
A(t)=\int_{1}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(c+iu)\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-c-iu)\,du
. Since the two factors
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(c+iu)
and
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(1-c-iu)
have coefficient exponents summing to
1
, Lemma
7
gives the asymptotic estimate

(4.1)

A(t)=t\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\lambda(n)^{2}P_{n}^{2}}{n}+O(\xi\sqrt{\log T})=t\log\xi\int_{0}^{1}P(x)^{2}dx+O\big(t+\xi\sqrt{\log T}\big).

Integrating by parts, we write

K=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\log\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)A^{\prime}(t)dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\log\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)A(t)\right]_{1}^{T}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{A(t)}{t}dt.

Substituting the estimate from (
4.1
) into this expression gives

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)\log\xi\int_{0}^{1}P(x)^{2}dx-\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\xi\int_{0}^{1}P(x)^{2}dx+O(T\log T)

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)\log\xi\int_{0}^{1}P(x)^{2}dx+O(T\log T)

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta}{2\pi}T(\log T)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}P(x)^{2}dx+O(T\log T),

using
\log(T/2\pi)\log\xi=\vartheta(\log T)^{2}+O(\log T)
.

For
J_{1}
, we consider the Dirichlet series

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{\dagger}(s)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Lambda(k)k^{-s}\right)\left(\sum_{l\leq\xi}\lambda(l)P_{l}l^{-s}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}n^{-s},

where the convolution coefficients are
\alpha_{n}=\sum_{kl=n}\Lambda(k)\lambda(l)P_{l}
. Applying Lemma
7
with
a_{n}=\alpha_{n}n^{-c}
and
b_{n}=\lambda(n)P_{n}n^{c-1}
yields

J_{1}=-\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)P_{n}}{n}+O\left(\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|\alpha_{n}n^{-c}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|\lambda(n)P_{n}n^{c-1}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right).

Using the estimates
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}n^{1-2c}\ll(\log T)^{3}
and
\sum_{n\leq\xi}nP_{n}^{2}n^{2c-2}\ll\xi^{2}
, we obtain

J_{1}=-\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)P_{n}}{n}+O(\xi(\log T)^{3/2}).

To evaluate the main sum, we substitute
\alpha_{n}
and use the complete multiplicativity of the Liouville function,
\lambda(kl)=\lambda(k)\lambda(l)
, to give

\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)P_{n}}{n}=\sum_{kl\leq\xi}\frac{\Lambda(k)\lambda(l)P_{l}\,\lambda(kl)P_{kl}}{kl}=\sum_{kl\leq\xi}\frac{\Lambda(k)\lambda(k)\,P_{l}P_{kl}}{kl},

since
\lambda(l)\lambda(kl)=\lambda(l)^{2}\lambda(k)=\lambda(k)
. We carry out the sum over
k
first, retaining the dependence of
P_{kl}
on
k
. The factor
\Lambda(k)\lambda(k)
is supported on prime powers, with
\Lambda(p)\lambda(p)=-\log p
on primes and a convergent contribution from higher prime powers. Writing
a=\frac{\log(\xi/l)}{\log\xi}
, so that
P_{l}=P(a)
, the prime number theorem in the form
\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log y+O(1)
together with partial summation gives

\sum_{k\leq\xi/l}\frac{\Lambda(k)\lambda(k)}{k}P_{kl}=-\log\xi\int_{0}^{a}P(b)\,db+O(1).

Summing over
l
and applying partial summation once more, as in Section
3
, we obtain

\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)P_{n}}{n}=-(\log\xi)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}P(a)\left(\int_{0}^{a}P(b)\,db\right)da+O(\log\xi).

Writing
Q(a)=\int_{0}^{a}P(b)\,db
, the integrand equals
Q^{\prime}(a)Q(a)=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(Q(a)^{2}\big)^{\prime}
, so the double integral equals
\tfrac{1}{2}Q(1)^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\int_{0}^{1}P\big)^{2}
. Hence

\sum_{n\leq\xi}\frac{\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)P_{n}}{n}=-\frac{1}{2}(\log\xi)^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}P(x)\,dx\right)^{2}+O(\log\xi).

Substituting this into our expression for
J_{1}
, and recalling
\log\xi=\vartheta\log T
, gives

2\Re J_{1}=\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2\pi}T(\log T)^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}P(x)\,dx\right)^{2}+O(T\log T).

Combining the estimates for
K
and
2\Re J_{1}
establishes Proposition
6
.

5.
Proof of Theorem
2

Let
T\geq 4
and choose
\tau_{n}
to satisfy
T-1\leq\tau_{n}<T
. Combining (
2.2
) with Propositions
5
and
6
gives

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{\big(\tfrac{\vartheta}{2\pi}T\log T\int_{0}^{1}P\big)^{2}}{\tfrac{1}{2\pi}T(\log T)^{2}\big[\vartheta\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}+\vartheta^{2}\big(\int_{0}^{1}P\big)^{2}\big]}+o(T).

After cancelling the common factors of
T
,
\log T
and
2\pi
, this simplifies to

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{T}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\vartheta\big(\int_{0}^{1}P\big)^{2}}{\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}+\vartheta\big(\int_{0}^{1}P\big)^{2}}+o(T).

To maximise this lower bound over all valid polynomials
P(x)
, set
r=\big(\int_{0}^{1}P\big)^{2}\big/\int_{0}^{1}P^{2}
. By Cauchy’s inequality,

\left(\int_{0}^{1}P(x)dx\right)^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}1^{2}dx\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}P(x)^{2}dx\right)=\int_{0}^{1}P(x)^{2}dx,

so that
r\leq 1
, with equality holding if and only if
P(x)
is a constant. In terms of
r
, the lower bound equals

\frac{T}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\vartheta r}{1+\vartheta r}+o(T),

which is increasing in
r
and is therefore maximised at
r=1
. Since
P(x)
must satisfy
P(1)=1
, this value is attained by the constant polynomial
P(x)=1
, and we obtain

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\frac{\zeta(2\rho)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\vartheta}{1+\vartheta}\,T+o(T).

Taking the limit as
\vartheta\to 1^{-}
completes the proof of Theorem
2
.
