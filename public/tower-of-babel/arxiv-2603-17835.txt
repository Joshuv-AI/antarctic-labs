Quasi-local Edge Mode in XXX Spin Chain/Circuit with Interaction Boundary Defect
Tomaž Prosen

Abstract
We study the Heisenberg spin- 1 / 2 1/2 model on a semi-infinite chain — or, equivalently, a trotterized unitary SU(2) symmetric six-vertex quantum circuit — with a boundary defect where the interaction between the two spins nearest the edge differs from that in the bulk. For sufficiently strong boundary interaction we explicitly construct a conserved operator quasi-localized near the boundary using a matrix-product ansatz. This quasi-local edge mode leads to non-decaying boundary correlation functions, corresponding to a nonzero boundary Drude weight. The correlation length of the edge mode diverges at a finite critical value of the boundary interaction, signaling a transition to ergodic boundary dynamics for subcritical interactions.



Introduction.–

Breaking ergodicity in the dynamics of interacting quantum many-body systems is one of the most fascinating topics in contemporary research. While Yang-Baxter integrability
Ilievski et al. 2016
;
Bertini et al. 2021
, strong quenched disorder
Abanin et al. 2019
;
Sierant et al. 2025
, and kinematic constraints
Pancotti et al. 2020
;
Valencia-Tortora et al. 2022
;
Bertini et al. 2024
provide several general—albeit only partially understood—mechanisms, it is still unclear whether and how ergodicity can be broken in clean, unconstrained, non-integrable systems.

A widely accepted view is that weak integrability breaking leads to prethermalization, which ultimately gives way to thermalization (and hence ergodicity) on timescales exponentially long in the inverse effective coupling. More broadly, ergodicity breaking is typically associated with the presence of nontrivial local or quasi-local conserved operators.

While most studies have focused on spatially extended systems, an important mechanism can instead originate from system boundaries. It has been shown that integrable spin chains can—and generically do—host nontrivial conserved operators that are (quasi-)localized near the edges
Jermyn et al. 2014
;
Fendley 2016
;
Essler et al. 2025
;
Gehrmann and Essler 2026
;
Else et al. 2017
. These operators, known as strong zero modes (SZMs), can be understood as interacting analogues of Majorana edge modes in solvable quasi-free fermionic chains.

The concepts of Yang-Baxter integrability
Vanicat et al. 2018
, quasi-local conserved charges
Ljubotina et al. 2019
, and SZMs
Vernier et al. 2024
have recently been extended to (Floquet) quantum circuits. Such circuits provide a natural framework for studying discrete-time, locally interacting quantum many-body dynamics, both theoretically and experimentally in the context of digital quantum simulation (see e.g.
Morvan et al. 2022
;
Mi et al. 2024
;
Rosenberg et al. 2024
).

In this Letter, we propose a simple modification of a well-known integrable quantum circuit — a staircase version of the trotterized SU(2)-symmetric spin-
1/2
Heisenberg chain. By modifying the interaction strength between the first two spins at one boundary (or, equivalently, by considering a half-infinite chain), we show that the system hosts a conserved quasi-local edge mode above a critical boundary interaction strength. This mode can be constructed and manipulated analytically using a
16\times 16
matrix-product ansatz. Although the continuous-time limit of our model is known to be Bethe-ansatz integrable
Frahm and Zvyagin 1997
, the relevance of the standard quantum inverse scattering method for our construction appears highly nontrivial, as it would require a 16-dimensional auxiliary space. Our result is unexpected in that all previous constructions of quasi-local edge modes required either anisotropic interactions or boundary fields.

Boundary Drude weight.–
Consider a traceless operator
B=\sum_{\underline{\nu}}b_{\underline{\nu}}\sigma^{\underline{\nu}}

localized near the edge. Boundary ergodicity is characterized by averaged time autocorrelation function, dubbed Drude weight

(20)

Existence of an QLEM

we conjecture that QLEM – when exists – is unique and does not depend on any additional relevant parameter. Thus the Mazur bound has to saturate and we can write explicit expression
for the Drude weight

Discussion.–

We have uncovered a simple algebraic matrix-product structure in the XXX spin-
1/2
chain, or equivalently in the trotterized XXX qubit circuit, with an interaction defect near the boundary. This structure yields a conserved quasi-local edge operator above a critical boundary interaction strength. The existence of such operator implies a transition between ergodic and non-ergodic dynamics for observables localized near the boundary.
The central algebraic relation (
9
) can be interpreted as a ‘decorated’ version of RLL or Yang-Baxter relation,
which lies at the heart of quantum integrability. However, the precise role of the delimiter operators
S
and
\eta
in this auxiliary quantum inverse scattering problem remains to be clarified
Not
.
Furthermore, it is important to clarify the role of SU(2) symmetry—which appears to be unique to our setup compared to related literature—and whether our construction can be deformed to
U(1)
-symmetric XXZ interactions.
Conceptually, our matrix-product structure is reminiscent of a recently discovered matrix-product operator in the XYZ chain
Fendley et al. 2025
. A crucial difference, however, is that our construction contains no spectral parameter, rendering the quasi-local conserved charge unique.

The deeper relation of our construction to strong zero modes
\Psi
introduced and studied by Fendley and collaborators
Fendley 2016
;
Jermyn et al. 2014
;
Vernier et al. 2024
;
Essler et al. 2025
, and to their
recent reformulation in terms of commutant algebras
Moudgalya and Motrunich 2026
, also remains unclear.
In contrast to all known SZMs, which satisfy
\Psi^{2}=\pm\mathbbm{1}
and
\tr\Psi=0
, our QLEM
Q
appears to obey the more general quadratic relation

Q^{2}-2cQ=3c^{2}\mathbbm{1},\quad c=\sqrt{(Q|Q)/3},\quad\tr Q=0,

(22)

so that one quarter of its eigenvalues equal
3c
and three quarters equal
-c
; see Fig.
4
(inset), where
(Q|Q)
can be computed via (
15
), see App. Eq. (
47
).
We expect that these results will open a new path for exploring boundary-induced dynamical phenomena in interacting quantum many-body systems.
