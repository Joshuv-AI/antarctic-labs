Prevalence of right ventricular dysfunction and its echocardiographic predictors in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Xin Du, Wenhui Li, Yonglong Yan, Qingwen Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo determine right ventricular dysfunction (RVD) prevalence in patients with heart failure and preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF); to systematically evaluate predictive values of echocardiographic parameters for RVD.MethodsA total of 163 patients diagnosed with HFpEF between January 2022 and January 2024 were retrospectively analyzed. All patients underwent assessment of right ventricular function. RVD was characterized by the presence of either a tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) below 17 mm or a right ventricular free wall strain (RV-FWS) absolute value under 20%. Baseline clinical characteristics and echocardiographic parameters were collected, including left atrial volume index (LAVI), mean ratio of early mitral inflow velocity to early diastolic mitral annular velocity (mean E/e’), systolic pulmonary artery pressure (sPAP), and right ventricular fractional area change (FAC).ResultsThe prevalence of RVD among patients with HFpEF was 41.7% (68/163). The RVD group was characterized by substantially higher NT-proBNP levels. Echocardiographic measures including RV-FWS, sPAP, mean E/e’, LAVI, TAPSE, and FAC independently predicted the occurrence of RVD. Among individual parameters, RV-FWS showed the best predictive performance. The combined predictive model incorporating the six aforementioned parameters exhibited excellent discriminatory ability. After internal validation using the bootstrap method, the corrected AUC was 0.989.ConclusionsRV-FWS and sPAP are the strongest independent echocardiographic predictors of RVD. An integrated assessment strategy combining multiparametric echocardiography with NT-proBNP measurement may substantially improve risk stratification of RVD in HFpEF.

Introduction

Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) is now recognized as a prominent form of heart failure, and the identification, prevalence, and clinical consequences of right ventricular dysfunction (RVD) in this group have gained growing importance for disease evaluation and risk stratification [1]. As an essential component of the overall pathophysiological cascade in HFpEF, right ventricular function is crucial for disease progression. Impairment of right ventricular performance not only directly exacerbates clinical symptoms and reduces exercise capacity, but is also independently associated with adverse outcomes, often indicating transition to a more advanced and clinically complex stage of the disease [2]. Therefore, accurate and timely identification of RVD in patients with HFpEF is of clear clinical urgency and substantial relevance for therapeutic decision-making.

Echocardiography remains the most widely used imaging modality for the evaluation of right ventricular function, and ongoing technological advances have enabled the assessment of RVD using multidimensional parameters [3]. Key echocardiographic parameters used to assess right ventricular performance include tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE), right ventricular free wall strain (RV-FWS), fractional area change of the right ventricle (FAC), and systolic pulmonary artery pressure (sPAP), all of which reflect different aspects of right ventricular systolic performance and collectively form the basis of contemporary clinical evaluation [4, 5]. However, within the specific and highly heterogeneous HFpEF population, the diagnostic value, predictive performance, and interrelationships of these echocardiographic indices have not been fully elucidated. In current clinical practice, no universally accepted definition of RVD exists. The reliance on single-parameter thresholds may be inherently limited, while the potential utility and optimal combination of composite indices remain to be validated and refined specifically in HFpEF cohorts [6].

Existing studies have reported considerable variability in the prevalence of RVD among patients with HFpEF. Such discrepancies are partly attributable to differences in study populations, inclusion criteria, and definitions of RVD. More importantly, there remains a lack of systematic analysis identifying which echocardiographic parameters can serve as early and independent predictors of RVD in patients with HFpEF. Clarifying these predictors would facilitate efficient identification of high-risk individuals during routine echocardiographic examinations, reduce the likelihood of underdiagnosis, and provide mechanistic insights into right ventricular involvement during the progression of HFpEF. We aim to determine RVD prevalence in patients with HFpEF through a retrospective cohort analysis and to comprehensively evaluate the predictive performance of echocardiographic parameters for RVD. The findings are expected to provide an evidence-based foundation for early detection and risk stratification of right ventricular dysfunction in the HFpEF population.

Participants and methods

Study design

Clinical data were consecutively collected through systematic review of the electronic medical records of patients diagnosed with HFpEF at our institution between January 2022 and January 2024 in this retrospective study. All patients underwent transthoracic echocardiography within 30 days after the diagnosis of HFpEF to ensure temporal consistency between echocardiographic parameters and clinical status.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) Diagnosis of HFpEF according to the current European Society of Cardiology guidelines [7], requiring fulfillment of all four of the following criteria: Presence of typical symptoms and/or signs of heart failure; Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≥ 50%; Elevated natriuretic peptide levels (B-type natriuretic peptide > 35 pg/mL or N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide [NT-proBNP] > 125 pg/mL); Objective evidence of cardiac structural and/or functional abnormalities consistent with diastolic dysfunction; (3) Availability of complete right ventricular functional assessment on echocardiography with adequate image quality for measurement; (4) Complete clinical medical records.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Moderate-to-severe valvular heart disease; (2) Acute coronary syndrome (onset ≤ 28 days) or coronary revascularization within the preceding 6 months; (3) Uncontrolled ventricular rate in atrial fibrillation, congenital heart disease, acute or chronic pulmonary embolism, chronic pulmonary disease–related right heart failure, or other significant cardiopulmonary disorders; (4) Poor echocardiographic image quality precluding accurate measurement of key right ventricular parameters; (5) Concomitant malignant tumors, severe hepatic or renal failure (Child–Pugh class C; estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²), or other severe systemic diseases potentially affecting cardiac functional assessment.

Sample size and statistical power

Finally, 163 patients with HFpEF were ultimately enrolled after database screening and manual verification. Previous studies have reported a prevalence of RVD ranging from approximately 30% to 50% among patients with HFpEF [8]. Given the present sample size, the expected number of RVD events was estimated to be 49–82 cases. In the current study, 68 patients were identified as having RVD based on echocardiographic criteria, corresponding to a prevalence of 41.7%, consistent with prior estimates.

For multivariable logistic regression analysis, the standard guideline of having a minimum of 10 outcome events for each potential predictor variable was followed to maintain the stability and reliability of the regression model. With 68 observed events, the dataset was considered sufficient to support the inclusion and selection of 6–8 potential predictors (including clinical and echocardiographic variables). Variables entered into multivariable model was strictly controlled to minimize overfitting and enhance the robustness of the findings.

Echocardiographic examination

With a GE Voluson E8 color Doppler ultrasound diagnostic system (GE Healthcare, United States) equipped with a 1–5 MHz transducer, image acquisition and measurements were conducted in accordance with the official recommendations [9]. Patients were examined in the left lateral decubitus position with simultaneous electrocardiographic monitoring. Standard views were acquired, including the apical four-chamber view, apical two-chamber view, parasternal long-axis view, and parasternal short-axis view.

Assessment of LVEF was performed with the biplane Simpson method, utilizing data acquired from parasternal short-axis echocardiographic images. Left atrial volume index (LAVI) was calculated using the biplane area–length method from the apical four-chamber view. Peak early mitral inflow velocity (E wave) was obtained using pulsed-wave Doppler at the mitral valve tips, and early diastolic mitral annular velocity (e’) was measured using tissue Doppler imaging at the mitral annulus. The mean E/e’ value was obtained by computing the ratio between early mitral inflow velocity and early diastolic mitral annular velocity. For each parameter, three consecutive cardiac cycles were recorded and averaged. Right ventricular assessment was primarily performed in the apical four-chamber view, including measurement of TAPSE, RV-FWS, and right ventricular FAC. sPAP was estimated from the tricuspid regurgitation velocity using standard methods. For each view, three consecutive cardiac cycles were recorded.

All echocardiographic images and measurements were independently analyzed by two experienced cardiologists specializing in cardiovascular ultrasound, each with more than 5 years of experience and blinded to the study objectives and outcomes. If the coefficient of variation between the two observers exceeded 10%, a third senior cardiologist with more than 10 years of experience performed an additional measurement. The final value was defined as the average of the three measurements to minimize measurement bias.

Data collection

Clinical information was retrieved from the hospital’s electronic medical record system by two trained investigators working independently, following a predefined and standardized data abstraction protocol. The form was reviewed and revised by two cardiologists and one echocardiography specialist to ensure clarity of variable definitions and completeness of data collection.

Clinical variables included: (1) Demographic data: age, sex, body mass index (BMI); (2) Clinical characteristics: New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class (I–II vs. III–IV), diabetes mellitus, hypertension, chronic kidney disease, and atrial fibrillation; (3) Laboratory data: NT-proBNP level measured at the time of HFpEF diagnosis or concurrently with echocardiography.

Echocardiographic variables were: Left heart parameters included LVEF, LAVI, and mean E/e’. Right heart parameters and afterload indices included TAPSE, RV-FWS, FAC, and sPAP.

Outcome definition

RVD was defined using a composite criterion: TAPSE < 17 mm or an absolute value of RV-FWS < 20%. This criterion was established in accordance with the guidelines issued by the American Society of Echocardiography and the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging regarding the evaluation of right ventricular performance [10] and has been validated in multiple HFpEF-related studies for its reliability and clinical relevance.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., United States) and R version 4.2.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Austria). All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄±s), whereas non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (interquartile range). Independent-samples t tests were used for comparisons of normally distributed variables with homogeneity of variance. The Mann–Whitney U test was applied for non-normally distributed variables or when variance homogeneity was not satisfied. Categorical data were expressed as frequency (percentage), including sex, comorbidities, NYHA class, and RVD status. Comparisons were performed using the Pearson chi-square test; Fisher’s exact test was applied when the expected frequency was < 5. To identify independent predictors of RVD, univariable logistic regression analyses were first performed. Variables with P < 0.10 were entered into the initial multivariable logistic regression model. Stepwise regression was subsequently applied for variable selection. Results were reported as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor, with variance inflation factor > 10 considered indicative of significant collinearity and grounds for exclusion. Discriminatory ability was assessed by constructing receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calculating the area under the curve (AUC) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Model calibration was evaluated using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, with P > 0.05 indicating good calibration. Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling (1,000 iterations). The optimism value was calculated as the difference between the apparent area under the curve of the original model and the mean bootstrap area under the curve. The optimism-corrected area under the curve was then derived to provide a more objective estimate of model generalizability.

Sensitivity analysis

To address potential circularity between the RVD definition and predictor variables, we performed a sensitivity analysis by repeating the multivariable logistic regression and ROC analysis after excluding TAPSE and RV‑FWS from the candidate predictors. The remaining predictors included LAVI, mean E/e’, FAC, and sPAP.

Results

Prevalence of RVD and comparison of baseline characteristics

Among the enrolled HFpEF population, 68 out of 163 patients (41.7%) met the criteria for RVD. Baseline demographic parameters (age, sex, and BMI) and comorbid conditions, including NYHA class, hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, and chronic kidney disease, were comparable between the RVD and non-RVD groups (all P > 0.05). Among laboratory parameters, only NT-proBNP levels were significantly elevated in the RVD group compared with the non-RVD group (P < 0.001; Table 1).

Multivariable logistic regression for RVD in patients With HFpEF

As shown in Tables 3 and Fig. 1, after stepwise selection, six echocardiographic parameters were identified as independent predictors of RVD in patients with HFpEF (all P < 0.05). Among these, RV-FWS demonstrated the strongest association with RVD (largest odds ratio, P < 0.001), followed by sPAP (P = 0.001) and mean E/e’ (P = 0.007). LAVI (P = 0.048), TAPSE (P = 0.040), and FAC (P = 0.003) also showed significant predictive value. All variables included in the multivariable model had variance inflation factors < 2, indicating the absence of significant multicollinearity and good model stability. The Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test yielded χ²=2.380 with P = 0.967, demonstrating excellent model calibration and strong agreement between predicted and observed RVD occurrence.

Performance of the echocardiography-based predictive model

As shown in Table 4 and Figs. 2 and 3, all individual echocardiographic parameters as well as the combined model exhibited significant predictive performance for RVD (all P < 0.001). Among single parameters, RV-FWS showed the best predictive performance (AUC = 0.872; 95% CI: 0.817–0.927), with specificity of 78.9% and sensitivity of 86.8%. This was followed by sPAP (AUC = 0.846), with specificity of 68.4% and sensitivity of 83.8%. The predictive performance of TAPSE (AUC = 0.788), FAC (AUC = 0.773), mean E/e’ (AUC = 0.737), and LAVI (AUC = 0.693) decreased sequentially. The combined model incorporating all six independent predictors demonstrated markedly superior performance compared with any single parameter (AUC = 0.986; 95% CI: 0.974–0.998), with specificity of 98.9% and sensitivity of 88.2%, indicating excellent discriminative ability for identifying RVD.

Sensitivity analysis

To address potential circularity between the RVD definition and predictor variables, we repeated the analysis after excluding TAPSE and RV‑FWS from the predictor set. The model retained excellent discriminative ability (AUC = 0.942; 95% CI: 0.909–0.942) (Figure X), confirming that the predictive performance was not solely driven by variables used in the outcome definition.

Supplementary analysis of RV-PA coupling

To further explore right ventricular-pulmonary arterial (RV-PA) coupling, we calculated the TAPSE/sPAP ratio. This ratio was significantly lower in the RVD group compared with the non-RVD group (median: 0.34 vs. 0.59, P < 0.001). As a single predictor, TAPSE/sPAP achieved an AUC of 0.880 (95% CI: 0.828–0.880), comparable to RV-FWS (AUC = 0.872; DeLong test P = 0.829). When added to the multivariable model (LAVI, E/e’, FAC, sPAP), TAPSE/sPAP independently predicted RVD (P = 0.006) and significantly improved model performance (likelihood ratio test P = 0.0044; AUC increased from 0.942 to 0.952).

Discussion

This retrospective cohort study evaluated the prevalence of RVD in patients with HFpEF and systematically investigated the predictive value of echocardiographic parameters. The principal findings are as follows: (1) the prevalence of RVD in the enrolled HFpEF cohort was 41.7%; (2) patients with RVD exhibited significantly elevated NT-proBNP levels, more pronounced left atrial remodeling, diastolic dysfunction, and pulmonary hypertension; (3) among multiple echocardiographic parameters, RV-FWS, sPAP, LAVI, and mean E/e’ were identified as independent risk factors for RVD, whereas TAPSE and FAC acted as protective factors; and (4) a predictive model integrating multiple echocardiographic parameters demonstrated excellent discriminative performance for RVD.

Prevalence and clinical characteristics of RVD in HFpEF

The prevalence of RVD was 41.7%, consistent with the previously reported range of 30%–50% [11]. This relatively high prevalence suggests that right ventricular involvement is not uncommon in HFpEF but rather represents an important pathophysiological component during disease progression [12]. Although no significant differences were observed between groups in age, major comorbidities, or subjective symptom classification (NYHA class), NT-proBNP levels were significantly higher in the RVD group. This apparent dissociation carries important clinical implications: reliance solely on symptom-based assessment may fail to identify a subgroup of HFpEF patients with subclinical RVD and significant hemodynamic deterioration. In addition, the RVD group exhibited significantly higher LAVI, mean E/e’, and sPAP, supporting the classical pathophysiological cascade of elevated left ventricular filling pressure-pulmonary vascular remodeling-pulmonary hypertension-increased right ventricular afterload-RVD. These findings provide further evidence that right ventricular impairment in HFpEF is closely linked to chronically elevated left-sided filling pressures.

Echocardiographic predictors of RVD: From single parameters to integrated assessment

This study systematically validated the predictive value of echocardiographic indices reflecting left heart function, right ventricular systolic performance, and right ventricular afterload for RVD in HFpEF. Among them, the strain-derived parameter RV-FWS demonstrated the strongest predictive performance, while the independent contributions of LAVI and mean E/e’ further refined the pathophysiological linkage between left- and right-sided cardiac dysfunction, complementing prior evidence [13].

RV-FWS and sPAP emerged as the most powerful independent predictors of RVD. This finding is consistent with the growing recognition of strain imaging as a superior modality for right ventricular functional assessment [14, 15]. As a sensitive measure of intrinsic right ventricular myocardial contractility, RV-FWS achieved the highest single-parameter predictive performance in this study (AUC = 0.872), suggesting that it may detect early myocardial impairment more specifically. Although RV-FWS remains influenced by loading conditions to some extent, similar to other echocardiographic indices, it is considered more sensitive to early myocardial dysfunction [16]. sPAP primarily reflects right ventricular afterload rather than intrinsic right ventricular systolic function. As a core indicator of right ventricular afterload, sPAP also showed strong predictive value, reinforcing the concept that elevated pulmonary circulatory pressure is a key hemodynamic driver of right ventricular deterioration in HFpEF [17]. In comparison, traditional parameters such as TAPSE and FAC, although still independently associated with RVD, demonstrated relatively lower predictive performance (AUC = 0.788 and 0.773, respectively). These findings support the clinical principle of multiparametric evaluation of right ventricular function rather than reliance on a single conventional index [18].In addition to individual parameters, we evaluated the TAPSE/sPAP ratio as a surrogate marker of RV-PA coupling. This ratio reflects the efficiency of right ventricular contractile output relative to pulmonary afterload. The significantly lower TAPSE/sPAP ratio in the RVD group and its strong predictive performance (AUC = 0.880) highlight the importance of RV-PA coupling in the pathophysiology of RVD in HFpEF. Notably, adding TAPSE/sPAP to the multivariable model significantly improved risk discrimination (likelihood ratio test P = 0.0044), suggesting that assessment of RV-PA coupling may provide incremental value beyond conventional echocardiographic parameters.

Regarding left heart–related parameters, this study is among the first to simultaneously confirm LAVI and mean E/e’ as independent predictors of RVD in HFpEF. These findings should be interpreted within a unified haemodynamic cascade: elevated left ventricular filling pressure (mean E/e’) is transmitted to the left atrium, leading to left atrial enlargement (LAVI); this pressure overload is then retrogradely transmitted through the pulmonary circulation, resulting in pulmonary hypertension (sPAP) and increased right ventricular afterload, which ultimately impairs right ventricular function. Thus, LAVI and mean E/e’ are not isolated predictors but represent sequential pathophysiological steps in the left heart–pulmonary circulation–right heart axis.The significantly higher NT-proBNP levels in the RVD group (Table 1) further support this pathophysiological cascade, reflecting more severe hemodynamic burden and reinforcing the value of NT-proBNP as a complementary marker for risk stratification. Previous studies have primarily reported that HFpEF patients with mean E/e’ > 15 have a 2.3-fold increased risk of RVD [19]. The underlying mechanism involves elevated left ventricular filling pressure leading to increased left atrial pressure, which is transmitted through the pulmonary circulation to the right heart, thereby augmenting right ventricular afterload. Importantly, the additional predictive value of LAVI observed in this study suggests that left atrial remodeling, as a chronic structural consequence of sustained diastolic dysfunction, may further aggravate right ventricular burden through cumulative volume overload. This observation aligns with the previously proposed “left atrium–pulmonary circulation–right ventricle” injury cascade [20]. These findings broaden the current understanding of right ventricular involvement in HFpEF, indicating that left heart dysfunction exerts both acute hemodynamic and chronic structural effects on the right ventricle, thereby providing a theoretical basis for multitargeted therapeutic strategies [21].

Superiority and clinical implications of the integrated predictive model

The combined predictive model constructed in this study systematically integrated key echocardiographic indices reflecting left ventricular filling pressure (LAVI, mean E/e’), right ventricular systolic function (RV-FWS, TAPSE, FAC), and right ventricular afterload (sPAP), thereby achieving a comprehensive evaluation of the right ventricular “stress–function” status. This multidimensional approach markedly improved discriminative accuracy for RVD (AUC = 0.986, specificity 98.9%, sensitivity 88.2%). In addition to excellent discrimination, internal validation using bootstrap resampling demonstrated strong robustness (corrected AUC = 0.989), indicating reliable generalizability. This integrated assessment strategy provides a solid theoretical and data-driven foundation for the development of simplified clinical risk scores or automated analytical tools based on routine echocardiographic parameters. If translated into clinical practice, such models may facilitate efficient identification of high-risk HFpEF patients with concomitant RVD, enabling early warning and precision intervention to improve prognosis.

Study limitations and future directions

First, this investigation was based on a single-center retrospective cohort with a relatively small sample size; thus, external validation in larger multicenter prospective cohorts is warranted. We acknowledge that the inclusion of TAPSE and RV-FWS in both the RVD definition and predictive model may introduce circularity. However, a sensitivity analysis excluding these two variables confirmed the model remained robust (AUC = 0.942).

Second, long-term follow-up data were not available, precluding evaluation of the model’s prognostic value for outcomes such as rehospitalization and mortality.

Third, echocardiography was performed within a 30-day window after HFpEF diagnosis, but right ventricular function and sPAP may change over shorter periods. The exact time interval was not available in our database, and we acknowledge this as a limitation.

Fourth, while clinical scores such as H2FPEF or HFA-PEFF may enhance diagnostic precision, we adopted the ESC guidelines as the current clinical standard. Additionally, the impact of different RVD definitions on model performance was not explored.

Fifth, TAPSE and FAC are both measures of right ventricular systolic function and may exhibit collinearity. In our model, variance inflation factors were all < 2, indicating no significant multicollinearity. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that these parameters reflect similar physiological information, and future studies may consider including only one of them to avoid redundancy.

Future multicenter prospective studies with standardized protocols and long-term follow-up are needed to validate our findings.

Conclusion

RVD was present in 41.7% of patients with HFpEF and was more frequently observed in those with elevated NT-proBNP levels, left atrial remodeling, diastolic dysfunction, and pulmonary hypertension, often in the absence of prominent clinical symptoms. RV-FWS and sPAP were the strongest independent echocardiographic predictors of RVD. A multiparametric echocardiographic approach combined with NT-proBNP assessment significantly improved early detection and risk stratification of RVD. Routine implementation of this integrated evaluation strategy in the management of HFpEF may facilitate early identification and precision intervention, ultimately improving patient outcomes.
