Vector spin glasses with Mattis interaction II: non-convex high-temperature models
Hong-Bin Chen, Victor Issa

Abstract
. This paper constitutes the second part of a two-paper series devoted to the systematic study of vector spin glass models whose energy function involves a spin glass part and a general Mattis interaction part. In this paper, we focus on models whose spin glass part does not satisfy the usual convexity assumption. In this case, the Parisi formula breaks down, and there are no known methods to fully identify the limit free energy. It was suggested in [ 45 ] that the limit free energy may be described using the unique solution of a partial differential equation of Hamilton–Jacobi type. In the present paper, we prove the validity of this conjecture in the high-temperature regime and provide an explicit representation for the free energy in terms of critical points. Using the duality between the free energy and large deviation principles, one can then easily deduce from the previous result a large deviation principle for the mean magnetization as well as a representation for the free energy of spin glass models with additional Mattis interaction at high temperature. In the companion paper [ 18 ] , we establish similar results at all temperatures for models whose spin glass part is assumed to satisfy the usual convexity assumption. Keywords and phrases: Disordered systems, Large deviation principles, Spin glasses, Hamilton–Jacobi equations. MSC 2020: 82B44, 60F10, 35F21.

To the best of our knowledge, with the presence of Mattis interaction, identifying the limit free energy at high temperature without additional assumptions on replica symmetry has not been achieved. However, much progress has been made in related spin glass systems and neural networks. For two-layer bipartite models, such as the analogical neural network and standard Restricted Boltzmann Machines, the replica-symmetric (RS) ansatz has been derived using interpolation and self-averaging properties
[
11
,
5
]
. These methods have been extended to multi-layer geometries like Deep Boltzmann Machines. In this context, the RS ansatz can be bounded by Sherrington-Kirkpatrick models in the annealed region
[
6
]
and expressed through minimax formulas
[
34
]
. Other deep architectures rely on transport equations to handle RS and one-step replica symmetry breaking (1RSB)
[
2
]
, while exact solutions without explicit RS assumptions are limited to specific regimes like the Nishimori line
[
7
]
. Furthermore, the existence of the infinite-volume thermodynamic limit for the free energy in mean field neural networks has been rigorously established by assuming the measure-concentration of order parameters
[
3
]
. In short, computing the limit free energy in all these works requires either imposing a replica-symmetric assumption (or limited symmetry-breaking) or restricting the analysis to specific regimes where such symmetry naturally holds.

For models satisfying the usual convexity assumption, computing the free energy in the absence of Mattis interaction is a classical problem, and it is now well known that the limit of the free energy can be understood as the supremum of an explicit functional, which is the celebrated Parisi formula
[
51
,
36
,
53
]
.
For those models, the free energy can also be studied from the perspective of Hamilton–Jacobi equations, which leads to a more systematic and general treatment. The recent developments regarding this are summarized in
[
46
]
.

On the other hand, models with Mattis interaction have not yet received a similar systematic treatment. The identification of their free energy relies on model-dependent proofs. Furthermore, large deviation principles for the mean magnetization have traditionally been established by computing a constrained version of the free energy

f_{N}(A)=-\frac{1}{N}\log\left(\frac{1}{4^{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in A}e^{E_{N}(\sigma)}\right),

which leads to technical proofs
[
37
,
32
,
17
,
49
,
25
]
. Similar techniques have also been employed to prove large deviation principles in the context of classical spin glass models
[
15
,
41
]
, see also
[
42
]
. For spin glass models with additional Mattis interaction, there are no known general results that allow for a systematic identification of the free energy nor the verification of large deviation principles for the mean magnetization.

In this series of papers, we resolve this problem and establish systematic results using a new approach that relies on the computation of the following object

f_{N}^{G}=-\frac{1}{N}\log\left(\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}\times\{-1,1\}^{N}}\exp\left(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}W_{ij}\sigma_{1,i}\sigma_{2,j}+NG(m_{N})\right)\right).

The main difference of our approach is that the continuous function
G:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
encoding the Mattis interaction is now treated as a
parameter of the model
instead of being fixed. Furthermore, as opposed to the traditional approach, we do not impose constraints on the spin configuration
\sigma
. This allows for smoother, shorter, and less technical computations that one can carry out in a model-independent way.

To compute
\lim_{N\to+\infty}f_{N}^{G}
, we can proceed as follows. We let
\langle\cdot\rangle^{G}_{N}
denote the Gibbs measure associated with
f_{N}^{G}
. We start with the case
G(m)=x\cdot m
. Under this assumption, the sum structure of

NG(m_{N})=x_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}^{1}\sigma_{1,i}+x_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}^{2}\sigma_{2,i}

makes the computation of
\lim_{N\to+\infty}f_{N}^{m\mapsto x\cdot m}
extremely similar to the computation of
\lim_{N\to+\infty}f_{N}^{0}
. We show that in this case the free energy can be computed, at least in the small
\beta
region, using a critical point representation for
\lim_{N\to+\infty}f_{N}^{m\mapsto x\cdot m}
in the style of Remark
1.2
. This representation can then be connected to the aforementioned Hamilton–Jacobi equation.
Then, as a direct application of the Gärtner–Ellis theorem, we deduce from the knowledge of
\lim_{N\to+\infty}f_{N}^{m\mapsto x\cdot m}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a large deviation principle for
m_{N}
under
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N}^{0}
with an explicit rate function
I

G

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\left\langle e^{NG(m_{N})}\right\rangle_{N}^{0}=\sup_{m}\left\{G(m)-I(m)\right\}.

Observing that
\frac{1}{N}\log\left\langle e^{NG(m_{N})}\right\rangle_{N}^{0}=f_{N}^{G}-f_{N}^{0}
it follows that
\lim_{N\to+\infty}f_{N}^{G}
can be computed. Finally, as an application of Bryc’s inverse Varadhan lemma, we deduce from the previous display that
m_{N}
satisfies a large deviation principle under
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N}^{G}
with an explicit rate function
I^{G}

G(m)=m_{1}+m_{2}
we obtain Theorem

. Note that here we have used crucially that the previous display holds
for all
continuous functions
G
.

Definition 1.7

\displaystyle-\inf_{A^{\circ}}I\leqslant\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\{X_{N}\in A\}\leqslant\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}_{N}\{X_{N}\in A\}\leqslant-\inf_{\overline{A}}I,

Definition 2.1

(Fréchet differentiability)
.

Let
(E,\,|\,\cdot\,|_{E})
be a Banach space and denote by
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{E}
the canonical pairing between
E
and
E^{*}
. Let
G
be a subset of
E
and let
q\in G
. A function
g:G\to\mathbb{R}
is
Fréchet differentiable
at
q\in G
if there exists a unique
y^{*}\in E^{*}
such that the following holds:

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\to q\\
q^{\prime}\in G\end{subarray}}\frac{g(q^{\prime})-g(q)-\langle y^{*},q^{\prime}-q\rangle_{E}}{|q^{\prime}-q|_{E}}=0.

In such a case, we call
y^{*}
the
Fréchet derivative
of
g
at
q
, and we denote it by
\nabla g(q)
or
\nabla_{q}g(q)
.

More generally, let
(E^{\prime},|\,\cdot\,|_{E^{\prime}})
be another Banach space. Let
G,G^{\prime}
be subsets of
E
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively, and let
q\in G
. A function
g:G\to G^{\prime}
is
Fréchet differentiable
at
q\in G
if there exists a unique bounded operator
A:E\to E^{\prime}
such that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\to q\\
q^{\prime}\in G\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|g(q^{\prime})-g(q)-A(q^{\prime}-q)\right|_{E^{\prime}}}{|q^{\prime}-q|_{E}}=0.

Again, in such a case, we denote
A
as
\nabla g(q)
or
\nabla_{q}g(q)
.

We will use the operator norm on the set of bounded operators

, defined as

(2.1)

\|A\|_{\mathrm{Op}(E,E^{\prime})}=\sup_{q\in E\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|Aq|_{E^{\prime}}}{|q|_{E}}.

{\mathsf{p}},{\mathsf{q}}\in[1,+\infty]
satisfying
\frac{1}{{\mathsf{p}}}+\frac{1}{{\mathsf{q}}}\leqslant 1
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q;x)\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(L^{\mathsf{p}};L^{\mathsf{p}})}\leqslant 16,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q;x)-\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q^{\prime};x)\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(L^{\mathsf{p}};L^{\frac{{\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{q}}}{{\mathsf{p}}+{\mathsf{q}}}})}\leqslant C\left|q-q^{\prime}\right|_{L^{\mathsf{q}}},

for the same constant
C
in (
2.9
).

One useful case of (
2.11
) is when
{\mathsf{p}}={\mathsf{q}}=2{\mathsf{s}}
for some
{\mathsf{s}}\in[1,+\infty]
and thus
\frac{{\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{q}}}{{\mathsf{p}}+{\mathsf{q}}}={\mathsf{s}}
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

The argument is based on computing directional derivatives in
q
and
x
. The computation is straightforward for the latter, and we explain the former. Fix any
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{s}}
and let
\lambda\in[0,1]
. We want to compute the derivative in
\lambda
along
\lambda\mapsto q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q)
. The key observation is that
w^{q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q)}(\alpha)
inside
\psi(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q))
(see (
1.12
)) has the same law as
\sqrt{1-\lambda}w^{q}(\alpha)+\sqrt{\lambda}w^{q^{\prime}}(\alpha)
where
w^{q}(\alpha)
and
w^{q^{\prime}}(\alpha)
are independent. Hence, we can carry out the computation in terms of
\sqrt{1-\lambda}w^{q}(\alpha)+\sqrt{\lambda}w^{q^{\prime}}(\alpha)
. The recipe is as follows: first, we differentiate a quantity in
\lambda
to get an expression involving
w^{q}(\alpha)
and
w^{q^{\prime}}(\alpha)
, and then we apply the Gaussian integration by parts
[
29
, Theorem 4.6]
to them to get the final expression.
Since these procedures are standard, we omit the intermediate steps and refer to the computations in the proof of
[
20
, Proposition 5.1]
as an example.

Throughout the proof, we use the following notation. For
\kappa\in L^{\infty}([0,1];\mathrm{S}^{D})
and
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, we write

\displaystyle R^{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}_{\kappa}=\kappa\left(\alpha^{\ell}\wedge\alpha^{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\cdot\tau^{\ell}(\tau^{\ell^{\prime}})^{\intercal}.

where
(\tau^{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
are independent samples from some Gibbs measure that will be clear from the context.
For each
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we write

\displaystyle h^{\ell}=h\left(\tau^{\ell},\chi_{1}\right)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pth^{\ell}_{a}=a\cdot h\left(\tau^{\ell},\chi_{1}\right).

In the following, let
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Recall the definition of the Gibbs measure
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N;t,q}^{G}
in (
1.22
). For
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write

\displaystyle\left\langle\,\cdot\,\right\rangle_{\lambda}=\left\langle\,\cdot\,\right\rangle_{1;0,q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q)}^{m\mapsto m\cdot(x+\lambda(x^{\prime}-x))},

where it is also possible that we take
q=q^{\prime}
or
x=x^{\prime}
, which will be clear from the context.

Since
\alpha^{\ell}\wedge\alpha^{\ell^{\prime}}
distributes uniformly over
[0,1]
under
\mathbb{E}\left\langle\,\cdot\,\right\rangle_{\lambda}
for
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
and
\left|\tau\tau^{\intercal}\right|\leqslant 1
due to the support of
\tau
on the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
, we often use the the following estimates:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\langle\left|R_{\kappa}^{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\right|\right\rangle_{\lambda}\leqslant|\kappa|_{L^{1}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\mathbb{E}\left\langle\left|R_{\kappa}^{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}R_{\eta}^{\ell^{\prime\prime},\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}}\right|\right\rangle_{\lambda}\leqslant|\kappa|_{L^{\mathsf{p}}}|\eta|_{L^{\mathsf{q}}},

where
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
,
\ell^{\prime\prime}\neq\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}
,
\kappa,\eta\in L^{\infty}([0,1];\mathrm{S}^{D})
, and
\frac{1}{{\mathsf{p}}}+\frac{1}{{\mathsf{q}}}=1
. The last inequality follows from the Hölder’s inequality applied to the measure
\mathbb{E}\left\langle\,\cdot\,\right\rangle_{\lambda}
. Later, we will also use the generalization of the last one to three components.

We also mention that the correction term
-q(1)\cdot\tau\tau^{\intercal}
in (
1.12
) becomes
-(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q))(1)\cdot\tau\tau^{\intercal}
when we compute the direction derivative. The presence of this term will cancel all
R^{\ell,\ell}_{\kappa}
and thus in the final expression we only have terms
R^{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}_{\kappa}
with
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
. This is an important reason for the usefulness of the correction.

Lastly, slightly abusing the notation, we denote by
\left\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\,\cdot\,\right\rangle_{L^{2}}
not only the inner product in
L^{2}([0,1];\mathrm{S}^{D})
but also the natural paring in
L^{\mathsf{p}}([0,1];\mathrm{S}^{D})
and its dual.
We also use the shorthand
L^{\mathsf{p}}=L^{\mathsf{p}}([0,1];\mathrm{S}^{D})
. The only exception is that
L^{\infty}
in
|h|_{L^{\infty}}
means
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{L};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

With the above setup, we are ready to prove the lemma part by part.

Part (
1
).

Recall that
\psi
is initially defined on
\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
as in (
1.12
).
For
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can compute

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\psi\left(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q);x+\lambda(x^{\prime}-x)\right)=\mathbb{E}\left\langle R^{1,2}_{q^{\prime}-q}\right\rangle_{\lambda}-\mathbb{E}\left\langle h^{1}_{x^{\prime}-x}\right\rangle_{\lambda}.

Using (
2.12
), the last term is bounded by
|q^{\prime}-q|_{L^{1}}+|h|_{L^{\infty}}
. Therefore, we get that (
2.2
) holds for
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By this continuity, we can extend the definition of
\psi
to
\mathcal{Q}_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and accordingly extend (
2.2
) as desired.

Part (
2
).

For each
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define
\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)\in L^{2}
as the element determined through

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\mathbb{E}\left\langle R^{1,2}_{\kappa}\right\rangle_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\kappa\in L^{\infty}.

Indeed, setting
x=x^{\prime}
in (
2.13
), we get

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\psi(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q);x)=\left\langle q^{\prime}-q,\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)\right\rangle_{L^{2}},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

This suggests that
\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)
is the correct derivative, which we will justify later after gathering enough properties.

Since
\left\langle R^{1,2}_{\kappa}\right\rangle_{\lambda}\leqslant|\kappa|_{L^{1}}
due to (
2.12
) and the density of
L^{\infty}
in
L^{2}
, we get from (
2.14
) that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)\right|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant 1,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
\kappa\in L^{\infty}
,
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
, and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla_{q}\psi(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q);x)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=2\mathbb{E}\left\langle R^{1,2}_{\kappa}\left(R^{1,2}_{q^{\prime}-q}-4R^{1,3}_{q^{\prime}-q}+3R^{3,4}_{q^{\prime}-q}\right)\right\rangle_{\lambda}.

By (
2.12
), the right-hand side is bounded by
16|\kappa|_{L^{{\mathsf{r}}^{*}}}|q-q^{\prime}|_{L^{\mathsf{r}}}
with
\frac{1}{{\mathsf{r}}^{*}}+\frac{1}{{\mathsf{r}}}=1
.
Integrating in
\lambda
and using the density of
L^{\infty}
in
L^{{\mathsf{r}}^{*}}
, we get

(2.18)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)-\nabla_{q}\psi(q^{\prime};x)\right|_{L^{\mathsf{r}}}\leqslant 16|q-q^{\prime}|_{L^{\mathsf{r}}},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Setting
{\mathsf{r}}=1
and using (
2.16
), we extend
\nabla\psi(q,x)
by continuity to

and achieve the

-bound in (
2.3

) is proved in
[
20
, Corollary 5.2]
.

Setting
\kappa=q^{\prime}-q
in (
2.17
) and
{\mathsf{r}}=2
in the bound mentioned below (
2.17
) and comparing with (
2.15

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{2}}\psi(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q);x)\right|\leqslant 16|q-q^{\prime}|_{L^{2}}

) and Taylor’s expansion, gives that for any fixed

,

\displaystyle\left|\psi(q^{\prime};x)-\psi(q;x)-\left\langle q^{\prime}-q,\,\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\leqslant 8|q-q^{\prime}|^{2}_{L^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}.

Since we have shown that
\psi(\,\cdot\,;x)
and

can be extended continuously to

and that the latter is also bounded, we can now extend (

) to

. This proves that
\psi(\,\cdot\,;x)
is Fréchet differentiable everywhere on
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
satisfying (
2.4
) and that
\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)
defined via (
2.14
) is its derivative.

To complete this part, it remains to show (
2.5
).
For
\kappa\in L^{\infty}
,
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we compute from (

) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x+\lambda(x^{\prime}-x))\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\mathbb{E}\left\langle R^{1,2}_{\kappa}\left(h^{1}_{x^{\prime}-x}+h^{1}_{x^{\prime}-x}-2h^{1}_{x^{\prime}-x}\right)\right\rangle_{\lambda}.

Using (

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)-\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x^{\prime})\right|\leqslant 4|\kappa|_{L^{1}}|x-x^{\prime}|,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\ x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

As before, we can extend this relation to

, which along with (
2.18
) extended to
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
yields (
2.5
).

Part (
3
).

We can easily compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\psi(q;x)=-\mathbb{E}\left\langle h^{1}\right\rangle_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\left|\nabla_{x}\psi(q;x)\right|\leqslant|h|_{L^{\infty}},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and

, we can then compute

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\nabla_{x}\psi(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q);x)=4\mathbb{E}\left\langle h^{1}\left(R^{1,2}_{q^{\prime}-q}-R^{2,3}_{q^{\prime}-q}\right)\right\rangle_{\lambda}.

Using (
2.12
) to bound the right-hand side, we get

(2.20)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\psi(q;x)-\nabla_{x}\psi(q^{\prime};x)\right|\leqslant 8|h|_{L^{\infty}}|q-q^{\prime}|_{L^{1}}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

). Notice that we have yet to verify that the extended

q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
.

To do so, we start by computing, for

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\nabla_{x}\psi(q;x+\lambda(x^{\prime}-x))=-\mathbb{E}\left\langle h^{1}\left(h^{1}_{x^{\prime}-x}-h^{2}_{x^{\prime}-x}\right)\right\rangle_{\lambda},

which implies

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\psi(q;x)-\nabla_{x}\psi(q;x^{\prime})\right|\leqslant 2|h|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}|x-x^{\prime}|,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\ x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Again, (
2.20
) allows us to extend this bound to
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
, which along with the extension of (
2.20
) to
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
yields (
2.8
).

Multiply both sides in (
2.21
) by

, we can get
\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\psi(q;x+\lambda(x^{\prime}-x))\right|\leqslant 2|h|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}
, which by Taylor’s expansion gives that, uniformly in

\displaystyle\left|\psi(q;x^{\prime})-\psi(q;x)-(x^{\prime}-x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(q;x)\right|\leqslant|h|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}.

As before, we can extend this by continuity to

. This verifies that

is differentiable at

, (

Part (
4
).

We fix any

and write
\psi=\psi(\,\cdot\,;x)
.
For any

and

, we define
\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q)
as a linear operator from

to

by setting

(2.22)

\displaystyle\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q)\eta\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=2\mathbb{E}\left\langle R^{1,2}_{\kappa}\left(R^{1,2}_{\eta}-4R^{1,3}_{\eta}+3R^{3,4}_{\eta}\right)\right\rangle_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\kappa,\eta\in L^{\infty}.

Notice that this definition can be made simultaneously for all
{\mathsf{p}}
. Using (
2.12
), we can bound the right-hand side by
16|\kappa|_{L^{{\mathsf{p}}^{*}}}|\eta|_{L^{\mathsf{p}}}
with
\frac{1}{{\mathsf{p}}^{*}}+\frac{1}{{\mathsf{p}}}=1

(2.23)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q)\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(L^{\mathsf{p}};L^{\mathsf{p}})}\leqslant 16,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}.

Comparing with (
2.17
), we get, for
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and
\kappa\in L^{\infty}
,

(2.24)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla_{q}\psi(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q))\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q))(q^{\prime}-q)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

We will extend the definition of
\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi
to
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
and verify that it is indeed a Fréchet derivative. But first, we need to derive some estimates.
For
\kappa,\eta\in L^{\infty}
and
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
, we can compute from (

) that

(2.25)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q+\lambda(q^{\prime}-q))\eta\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\mathbb{E}\left\langle R^{1,2}_{\kappa}\left(R^{1,2}_{\eta}-4R^{1,3}_{\eta}+3R^{3,4}_{\eta}\right)\mathcal{P}\left(R_{q^{\prime}-q}\right)\right\rangle_{\lambda},

where
\mathcal{P}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree one and depends on finitely many entries in

. Using an extension of the estimate in (

. From this, we can get

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q)-\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q^{\prime})\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(L^{\mathsf{p}};L^{\frac{{\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{q}}}{{\mathsf{p}}+{\mathsf{q}}}})}\leqslant C\left|q-q^{\prime}\right|_{L^{\mathsf{q}}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}.

In this relation, setting
{\mathsf{r}}={\mathsf{p}}=\infty
and
{\mathsf{q}}=1
, we can thus extend by continuity the definition of

to every
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
as a bounded linear operator from
L^{\infty}
to
L^{1}
.
Then, we want to accordingly extend (
2.23
). Let
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
and let
(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in

in

. Then, for any

, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q)\eta\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\leqslant\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q_{n})\eta\right\rangle_{L^{2}}+\left\langle\kappa,\,\left(\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q)-\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q_{n})\right)\eta\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Using this, Taylor’s expansion, and (

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\kappa,\,\nabla_{q}\psi(q^{\prime})-\nabla_{q}\psi(q)-\nabla^{2}_{q}\psi(q)(q^{\prime}-q)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\leqslant C|\kappa|_{L^{\frac{{\mathsf{s}}}{{\mathsf{s}}-1}}}|q-q^{\prime}|_{L^{2{\mathsf{s}}}}^{2}/2.

) (with

) (with

We also record a simple result based on Proposition

.
Define the set

(2.27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,\leqslant 1}=\left\{p\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}:\>|p|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant 1\right\}.

For every

,

, and

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{P}_{t,q}(p;x)-\mathscr{P}_{t,q}(p^{x};x)\right|\leqslant C|p-p^{x}|_{L^{\infty}}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall p\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,\leqslant 1}.

For brevity of notation, we write

\displaystyle\left|\psi(q+t\nabla\xi(p))-\psi(q+t\nabla\xi(p^{x}))-\left\langle p^{x},t\nabla\xi(p)-t\nabla\xi(p^{x})\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\left|t\nabla\xi(p)-t\nabla\xi(p^{x})\right|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leqslant C_{1}|p-p^{x}|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}.

Recall

and thus we can interpret
\nabla\theta(a)=a\cdot\nabla^{2}\xi(a)
as the linear operator
\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}
defined by
a^{\prime}\mapsto a\cdot\nabla^{2}\xi(a)a^{\prime}
.
Using the smoothness of
\theta
over
B
, for some constant
C_{2}
only depending on
\xi
, we also have

\displaystyle\left|t\int_{0}^{1}\theta(p)-t\int_{0}^{1}\theta(p^{x})-\left\langle p^{x},t\nabla^{2}\xi(p^{x})(p-p^{x})\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\leqslant tC_{2}\left|p-p^{x}\right|^{2}_{L^{\infty}},

where
\nabla^{2}\xi(p^{x})(p-p^{x})
is interpreted pointwise as the path
[0,1]\ni s\mapsto\nabla^{2}\xi(p^{x}(s))(p(s)-p^{x}(s))
. Similarly, for some constant
C_{3}
only depending on
\xi
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle p^{x},t\nabla\xi(p)-t\nabla\xi(p^{x})\right\rangle_{L^{2}}-\left\langle p^{x},t\nabla^{2}\xi(p^{x})(p-p^{x})\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\leqslant tC_{3}\left|p-p^{x}\right|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}.

Combining the above three displays, we get (
2.30
).
∎

3.
Short-time smooth solution of the Hamilton–Jacobi equation

We fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In this section, we are interested in the partial differential equation

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f-\int\xi(\nabla_{q}f)=0\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{Q}_{2}\\
f(0,\cdot)=\psi(\,\cdot\,;x).\end{cases}

Equation (
3.1
) is a so-called Hamilton–Jacobi equation. First, we explain the exact meaning of the term
\int\xi(\nabla_{q}f)
. Here,
\nabla_{q}f
is path-valued. More precisely,
\nabla_{q}f(t,q)
is equal to some path
p:[0,1)\to\mathrm{S}^{D}
. The nonlinearity
\int\xi(\nabla_{q}f(t,q))
is understood as
\int_{0}^{1}\xi(p(s))\mathrm{d}s
.

We define
X(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle X(t,q;x)=q-t\nabla\xi(\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)).

In view of Proposition
2.2
(
2
), the definition of
X(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x)
can be extended to
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{Q}_{1}
. However, we restrict the domain to
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
for better regularity.

The family curves
t\mapsto X(t,q;x)
parametrized by
q
(
x
is fixed) can be understood as the characteristic lines of equation (
3.1
). More precisely, it can be checked that if
f
is a smooth solution of (
3.1
), then

(3.3)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\nabla_{q}f(t,X(t,q;x);x)=0.

Thanks to this, one can verify by differentiation that we must have

(3.4)

f(t,X(t,q;x);x)=\mathscr{P}_{t,q}(p;x),

where
p=\nabla_{q}f(t,X(t,q;x);x)=\nabla_{q}\psi(q;x)
and
\mathscr{P}_{t,q}
is the functional defined in (
2.28
). If the map
q\mapsto X(t,q;x)
is a bijection for every
t>0
, then (
3.4
) uniquely defines a function
f
and it is possible to verify that
f(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x)
is a solution of (
3.1
).

However, at least in finite dimensional spaces, the characteristic lines can cross. So, the function
q\mapsto X(t,q;x)
is sometimes non-injective. This means that the recipe given by (
3.4
) to define a differentiable solution may not work. Even worse, Hamilton–Jacobi equations may sometimes not have any differentiable solutions
[
30
, Section 3.2.5.c Example 6]
. To restore the existence and uniqueness of solutions, one needs to appeal to the notion of viscosity solution
[
30
, Section 10]
. In this setting, the notion of solution is weakened in order to also allow non-differentiable functions to solve the equation. This theory can be extended to infinite dimensional spaces, and it can be shown that (
3.1
) also admits a unique viscosity solution
[
22
]
.

The goal of this section is to show that on the time interval
[0,t_{\star})
, the characteristic lines do not cross. This allows us to define via (
3.4
) a differentiable solution
f(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x)
of (
3.1
) on the interval
[0,t_{\star})
. In addition, by the uniqueness of the viscosity solution, the function
f(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x)
we build must coincide with the viscosity solution. In the end, the differentiability of the viscosity solution on the interval
[0,t_{\star})
is going to be one of the key ingredients in the verification of (
1.20
).

Recall the definition of the operator norm in (
2.1
). We recall that to improve readability, we sometimes abbreviate
\nabla_{q}
to
\nabla
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The function
X(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
is Fréchet differentiable. At each
(t,q)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
, the derivative
\nabla_{q}X(t,q;x)
is a bounded linear operator from
L^{\infty}
to
L^{\infty}
with expression

(3.5)

\displaystyle\nabla X(t,q;x)=\mathbf{I}-t\nabla^{2}\xi(\nabla\psi(q;x))\nabla^{2}\psi(q;x),

where
\mathbf{I}:L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
is the identity operator.
Moreover, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{I}-\nabla X(t,q;x)\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(L^{\infty};L^{\infty})}\leqslant t/t_{\star}.

As a consequence, if
t<t_{\star}
, then
\nabla X(t,q;x)
is bijective on
L^{\infty}
.

The interpretation of (
3.5
) is as follows. For every
\kappa\in L^{\infty}
,
\nabla X(t,q;x)\kappa\in L^{\infty}
is the path

(3.7)

\displaystyle[0,1]\ni s\hskip 10.00002pt\mapsto\hskip 10.00002pt\kappa(s)-t\nabla^{2}\xi(p(s))(A\kappa)(s),

where
p=\nabla\psi(q;x)
is a path in
\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,\leqslant 1}
(see (
2.3
)),
\nabla^{2}\xi(p(s))
is a linear operator on
\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}
(as
\xi
is a function from
\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}
to
\mathbb{R}
),
A=\nabla^{2}\psi(q;x)
is a linear operator on
L^{\infty}
(see (
2.10
)),
A\kappa
is thus a path in
L^{\infty}
, and lastly
\nabla^{2}\xi(p(s))(A\kappa)(s)
is the action of the linear operator
\nabla^{2}\xi(p(s))
on the
\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}
-valued element
(A\kappa)(s)
.

Proof.

Since
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is fixed, for brevity we drop the
x
dependence in the notation and write
\psi(q)
,
X(t,q)
instead of
\psi(q;x)
,
X(t,q;x)
. Since the dependence of
X(t,q)
on
t
is trivial, we only show the Fréchet differentiability in
q
at fixed
t
.

Let
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
. By (
2.9
) with
{\mathsf{s}}=\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\xi\left(\nabla\psi(q^{\prime})\right)=\nabla\xi\left(\nabla\psi(q)+\nabla^{2}\psi(q)(q^{\prime}-q)+p_{\mathsf{error}}\right),

with
\left|p_{\mathsf{error}}\right|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C_{0}|q^{\prime}-q|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}
for some absolute constant
C_{0}
.
Let
B
be as in (
1.13
) and

C_{1}=\left\|\nabla^{3}\xi\lfloor_{B}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\nabla^{2}\xi\lfloor_{B}\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}
, we have, for
a,a^{\prime}\in B
,

\nabla\xi(a^{\prime})=\nabla\xi(a)+\nabla^{2}\xi(a)(a^{\prime}-a)+\mathsf{error}_{a}(a^{\prime}-a),

where
|\mathsf{error}_{a}(a^{\prime}-a)|\leqslant C_{1}|a^{\prime}-a|^{2}
. By (
2.3
),
\nabla\psi(q)
and
\nabla\psi(q^{\prime})
are paths taking values in
B
. Hence, using the above two displays, we have that,

\displaystyle\nabla\xi\left(\nabla\psi(q^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla\xi\left(\nabla\psi(q)\right)+\nabla^{2}\xi\left(\nabla\psi(q^{\prime})\right)\nabla^{2}\psi(q)(q^{\prime}-q)

\displaystyle+\nabla^{2}\xi\left(\nabla\psi(q^{\prime})\right)p_{\mathsf{error}}+\mathsf{error}_{\nabla\psi(q)}\left(\nabla\psi(q^{\prime})-\nabla\psi(q)\right).

Again by (
2.10
), the last two terms are bounded in the
L^{\infty}
-norm by
|q^{\prime}-q|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
. This shows the Fréchet differentiability of the nonlinear part in
X(t,\,\cdot\,)
(see (
3.2
)), which implies the differentiability of
X(t,\,\cdot\,)
and (
3.5
).

Next, we verify (
3.6
). Let
\kappa\in L^{\infty}
and recall the interpretation of (
3.7
). Then, we can compute

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{2}\xi\left(\nabla\psi(q^{\prime})\right)\nabla^{2}\psi(q)\kappa\right|_{L^{\infty}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.p.psi_smooth_1}}}{{\leqslant}}\sup_{a\in B}\left\|\nabla^{2}\xi(a)\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{R}^{D\times D};\mathbb{R}^{D\times D})}\left|\nabla^{2}\psi(q)\kappa\right|_{L^{\infty}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.psi_smooth(4).1}}}{{\leqslant}}\sup_{a\in B}\left\|\nabla^{2}\xi(a)\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{R}^{D\times D};\mathbb{R}^{D\times D})}\left\|\nabla^{2}\psi(q)\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(L^{\infty};L^{\infty})}|\kappa|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant t^{-1}_{\star}|\kappa|_{L^{\infty}}.

The last inequality in the display follows from (
1.14
) once we have shown the following inequality

(3.9)

\left\|\nabla^{2}\psi(q)\right\|_{\mathrm{Op}(L^{\infty};L^{\infty})}\leqslant\|\nabla\psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\mathcal{Q}_{\infty})}}.

By duality between the
L^{\infty}
and
L^{1}
norm, the previous display is equivalent to

(3.10)

\left|\left\langle\kappa^{\prime},\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\leqslant|\kappa^{\prime}|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\mathcal{Q}_{\infty})}}|\kappa|_{L^{\infty}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})\in L^{\infty}\times L^{1}.

Let us prove this inequality. Recall
\mathsf{id}
is the path
s\mapsto s\mathsf{Id}
and
\mathsf{Id}\in S^{D}_{+}
is the
D\times D
identity matrix. Let
\kappa:[0,1]\to S^{D}
, and
\kappa\in L^{\infty}
. For now we assume that
\kappa
is Lipschitz. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
q_{n}=q+n^{-1}\mathsf{id}
. Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any we have
q_{n}+\varepsilon\kappa\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
(depending on
n
and
\kappa
). Using the differentiability of
\psi
in Proposition
2.2
(
4
) (with
{\mathsf{s}}=\infty
), for any
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\kappa^{\prime},\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\langle\kappa^{\prime},\frac{\nabla\psi(q_{n}+\varepsilon\kappa)-\nabla\psi(q_{n})}{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

This implies,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\left\langle\kappa^{\prime},\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|&\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}|\kappa^{\prime}|_{L^{1}}\left|\frac{\nabla\psi(q_{n}+\varepsilon\kappa)-\nabla\psi(q_{n})}{\varepsilon}\right|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leqslant|\kappa^{\prime}|_{L^{1}}\left\|\nabla\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\mathcal{Q}_{\infty})}|\kappa|_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

This yields (
3.10
) but only under the stronger assumption that
\kappa
is Lipschitz. Now let
\kappa\in L^{\infty}
without assuming that
\kappa
is Lipschitz. We let
(\kappa_{k})_{k}
denote a sequence of approximations of
\kappa
defined by mollification. By usual properties of mollifiers, we have
|\kappa_{k}|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant|\kappa|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\kappa_{k}\to\kappa
in
L^{\mathsf{p}}
for all
\mathsf{p}\in[1,+\infty)
(see e.g.
[
30
, Theorem 7 in Appendix C.5]
). Since each
\kappa_{k}
is Lipschitz on
[0,1]
, as argued in (
3.11
), we have that

\left|\left\langle\kappa^{\prime},\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa_{k}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\leqslant|\kappa^{\prime}|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\mathcal{Q}_{\infty})}}|\kappa_{k}|_{L^{\infty}}.

In addition, since
\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})
is a bounded operator
L^{2}
according to (
2.10
) as given in Proposition
2.2
(
4
), we have that
\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa_{k}
converges to
\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa
in
L^{2}
as
k\to\infty
. By extracting a subsequence, we may assume that this convergence is also pointwise a.e. on
[0,1)
.
Proposition
2.2
(
4
) also states that
\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})
is a bounded operator on
L^{\infty}
. Hence, these
\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa_{k}
are bounded a.e. uniformly in
k
, which allows us to apply the dominated convergence theorem to get

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\langle\kappa^{\prime},\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\kappa^{\prime},\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Together, the previous two displays yield

\left|\left\langle\kappa^{\prime},\nabla^{2}\psi(q_{n})\kappa\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|\leqslant|\kappa^{\prime}|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{\mathrm{Lip(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty},\mathcal{Q}_{\infty})}}|\kappa|_{L^{\infty}}.

So we have proven (
3.10
) at
q_{n}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,\uparrow}
. Using (
2.11
) from Proposition
2.2
(with
{\mathsf{p}}={\mathsf{q}}=\infty
) and the convergence of
q_{n}
to
q
in
L^{\infty}
, we can obtain (
3.9
) at
q
.

Using (
3
) and (
3.5
), we obtain (
3.6
). Lastly, the bijectivity is a classical consequence of (
3.6
) (see
[
16
, Exercise 6.14]
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and write
\nabla=\nabla_{q}
.
There is a map
Z(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x):[0,t_{\star})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
such that

(3.12)

\displaystyle q=X(t,Z(t,q;x);x),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(t,q)\in[0,t_{\star})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}.

This in particular implies
X(t,\mathcal{Q}_{\infty};x)\supset\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
. Moreover,
Z(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x):[0,t_{\star})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
is Fréchet differentiable everywhere and its derivative at
(t,q)\in[0,t_{\star})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
is given by

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla Z(t,q;x)&=\left(\nabla X(t,Z(t,q;x);x)\right)^{-1},\\
\partial_{t}Z(t,q;x)&=-\left(\nabla X(t,Z(t,q;x);x)\right)^{-1}\left(\nabla\xi(\nabla\psi(q;x))\right).\end{split}

Here,
\nabla X(t,Z(t,q);x)
is the derivative of
X(t,\,\cdot\,;x)
evaluated at
Z(t,q;x)
;
\left(\nabla X(t,Z(t,q;x);x)\right)^{-1}
is the inverse of the operator
\nabla X(t,Z(t,q;x);x)
given as in Lemma
3.1
; and
\left(\nabla X(t,Z(t,q;x);x)\right)^{-1}\left(\nabla\xi(\nabla\psi(q;x))\right)
applies the operator at
\nabla\xi(\nabla\psi(q;x))\in L^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Again, for brevity, we omit the
x
dependence and write
\psi
,
X(\cdot,\cdot)
, and
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
in place of
\psi(\,\cdot\,;x)
,
X(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x)
, and
Z(\cdot,\,\cdot\,;x)
.
Fix any
t\in[0,t_{\star})
.
Let
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and consider the mapping from
\Phi:\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
given by

\displaystyle\Phi(q)=q^{\prime}+t\nabla\xi(\nabla\psi(q)),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}.

By (
2.3
), this is well-defined. In view of the definition of
t_{\star}
in (
1.14
), we have
\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{Lip(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty};\mathcal{Q}_{\infty})}}\leqslant t/t_{\star}<1
. Hence, by the fixed point iteration, there is a unique
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
such that
q=\Phi(q)
and thus
q^{\prime}=X(t,q)
.
Hence, for each
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{2}
, we can define
Z(t,q^{\prime})=q
. This immediately gives (
3.12
).

Next, we want to verify that
Z
is Fréchet differentiable. For this purpose, we first verify that
Z
is Lipschitz continuous on
[t,t_{1}]\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
for all
t_{1}<t_{\star}
. From

(3.14)

\displaystyle q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.q=XZ}}}{{=}}X(t,Z(t,q))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.X(t,q)=}}}{{=}}Z(t,q)-t\nabla\xi(\nabla\psi(Z(t,q))),

and the definition of

in (
1.14
), we can deduce

\sup_{t\in[0,t_{1}]}\|Z(t,\,\cdot\,)\|_{\mathrm{Lip(\mathcal{Q}_{\infty};\mathcal{Q}_{\infty})}}<\infty.

Similarly, from (
3.14
) and the boundedness of
\nabla\psi
as in (
2.3
), we can deduce the Lipschitzness of
Z(\,\cdot\,,q)
on
[0,t_{1}]
uniformly in each
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
. Together, we have the joint Lipschitz of
Z
on
[t,t_{1}]\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
.

Now, we are ready to prove the differentiability of
Z
.
Fix any
(t,q)\in[0,t_{\star})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
and write
p=Z(t,q)
. As
(t^{\prime},q^{\prime})
approaching
(t,q)
, we can use the Fréchet differentiability of
X
from Lemma
3.1
and (

) (at both

and
(t^{\prime},q^{\prime})
) to see

\displaystyle\left|q^{\prime}-q-\partial_{t}X(t,p)(t^{\prime}-t)-\nabla X(t,p)\left(Z(t^{\prime},q^{\prime})-Z(t,q)\right)\right|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=o\left(|t^{\prime}-t|+\left|Z(t^{\prime},q^{\prime})-Z(t,q)\right|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Notice that

simply due to (

).
By the Lipschitzness of

, the error is controlled by

. Using this and the invertibility of

given by Lemma

, we can see that

is Fréchet differentiable at

and has its derivative given as in (

).
∎

Associated with the characteristic line

is the function

From the definition of

clear from the context and any relevant function

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.X(t,q)=}}}{{=}}-\nabla\xi(\nabla\psi),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.f=UZ}}}{{=}}\partial_{t}U^{Z}+\left\langle\nabla U^{Z},\,\partial_{t}Z\right\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.U=}\eqref{e.grad_U=}}}{{=}}-\left\langle\nabla\xi(\nabla\psi^{Z}),\,\nabla\psi^{Z}\right\rangle+\int\xi(\nabla\psi^{Z})+\left\langle(\nabla X^{Z})^{\intercal}\nabla\psi^{Z},\,\partial_{t}Z\right\rangle.

Using this and (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=\int\xi(\nabla\psi^{Z}).

This along with (

Lastly, we verify (

\displaystyle\nabla f(t,q)=\nabla\psi(Z(t,q)).

Using (

Lemma 3.5

4.1.
Preliminaries

From

Recall the definition of

.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

. Then, for every

, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\overline{F}^{m\mapsto x\cdot m}_{N}(t,q)=\mathscr{P}_{t,q}(p;x),

where

(\overline{F}^{m\mapsto x\cdot m}_{N}(\cdot,q))_{N}

To proceed, we will need the following lemma, which states that the free energy

,

, and

Lemma 4.2
.

For every

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\overline{F}_{N}(t)+\lambda\overline{F}_{N}(t^{\prime})-\overline{F}_{N}((1-\lambda)t+\lambda t^{\prime}),\leqslant C\lambda(1-\lambda)(t-t^{\prime})^{2}

\overline{F}_{N}(\cdot)=\overline{F}^{m\mapsto x\cdot m}_{N}(\cdot,q)

Recall the Hamiltonian
H^{G}_{N;t,q}(\sigma,\alpha)
from (
1.10
). For each

\displaystyle G(s)=-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\iint\exp\left(H_{s}(\sigma,\alpha)\right)\mathrm{d}P_{1}^{\otimes N}(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{R}(\alpha).

In view of (

\overline{F}_{N}(t)=G(\sqrt{t})
. This implies,

\overline{F}_{N}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{G^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{t})}{2\sqrt{t}}-\frac{G^{\prime}(\sqrt{t})}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

The local semi-concavity of
\overline{F}_{N}
can be reformulated as
\overline{F}_{N}^{\prime\prime}

[c,+\infty)

G^{\prime}
is bounded, this can be easily shown using Gaussian integration by parts. Hence, it suffices to show that the
G^{\prime\prime}
is upper-bounded.

Let

. Using the definition of

, we have

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)G(s)+\lambda G(s^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\left(\cdots\right)^{1-\lambda}(\cdots)^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Jensen}}}{{\leqslant}}-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\iint e^{(1-\lambda)H_{s}(\sigma,\alpha)+\lambda H_{s^{\prime}}(\sigma,\alpha)}\mathrm{d}P_{1}^{\otimes N}(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{R}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\iint e^{H_{(1-\lambda)s+\lambda s^{\prime}}(\sigma,\alpha)-R}\mathrm{d}P_{1}^{\otimes N}(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{R}(\alpha).

Setting

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)G(s)+\lambda G(s^{\prime})\leqslant G((1-\lambda)s+\lambda s^{\prime})+\lambda(1-\lambda)(s-s^{\prime})^{2}M.

Setting

and

∎

Proof of Proposition

Since

Lemma 4.4

is concave.
Recall
\mathscr{P}_{t,q}
defined in (
2.28
). Since
t
and

are fixed, we write
\mathscr{P}(\,\cdot\,;x)=\mathscr{P}_{t,q}(\,\cdot\,;x)
for brevity.
By Proposition
3.3
,
\nabla_{q}f(t,q;x)
exists for every

and we denote it by

By (
2.3

defined in (

By (

\displaystyle f(t,q;x)=\mathscr{P}(p^{x};x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptp^{x}=\nabla_{q}\psi\left(q+t\nabla\xi(p^{x});x\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Using the latter relation, for

\displaystyle|p^{x}-p^{x^{\prime}}|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\left|\nabla_{q}\psi\left(q+t\nabla\xi(p^{x});x\right)-\nabla_{q}\psi\left(q+t\nabla\xi(p^{x^{\prime}});x\right)\right|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\left|\nabla_{q}\psi\left(q+t\nabla\xi(p^{x^{\prime}});x\right)-\nabla_{q}\psi\left(q+t\nabla\xi(p^{x^{\prime}});x^{\prime}\right)\right|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.t_star=}\eqref{e.p.psi_smooth_2}}}{{\leqslant}}tt_{\star}^{-1}|p^{x}-p^{x^{\prime}}|_{L^{\infty}}+4|h|_{L^{\infty}}|x-x^{\prime}|.

\mathcal{Q}_{\infty,\leqslant 1}

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{P}(p^{x^{\prime}};x^{\prime})-\mathscr{P}(p^{x^{\prime}};x)-(x^{\prime}-x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(q+t\nabla\xi(p^{x^{\prime}});x)\right|\leqslant|h|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|^{2}.

4.2.
Limit free energy and the large deviation principle

Recall the definition of the Gibbs measure
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N;t,q}^{G}
from (

) and recall that given a random variable

and

we let

denote the realization of the random variable

on the event

. Also, recall the definition of

from (

).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(t,q)\in[0,t_{\star})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}
. There exists a full-measure subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
,

under
\langle\cdot\rangle_{N;t,q}^{0,\omega}
satisfies a large deviation principle with rate function
I_{t,q}
defined by

(4.4)

I_{t,q}(m)=f^{*}(t,q;m)-f(t,q;0),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall m\,\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Using Corollary
4.3
and the concentration of free energy as in Remark
1.5
, we can find a full-measure subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
such that such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
and every

,

\lim_{N\to\infty}F^{m\mapsto y\cdot m,\omega}_{N}(t,q)=f(t,q;y).

Henceforth, we fix
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
and define for each

,

\Lambda_{N;t,q}(y)=\frac{1}{N}\log\left\langle e^{Ny\cdot m_{N}}\right\rangle^{0,\omega}_{N;t,q}.

Notice that
\Lambda_{N;t,q}(y)=-F_{N}^{m\mapsto y\cdot m,\omega}(t,q)+F_{N}^{0,\omega}(t,q)
. Hence, we have for every

(4.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Lambda_{N;t,q}(y)=-f(t,q;y)+f(t,q;0).

Since

is Lipschitz uniformly in

and

is also Lipschitz, the previous display in fact holds for every

. Finally, since by Lemma

is differentiable everywhere, the Gärtner–Ellis theorem (e.g., see

satisfies a large deviation principle under

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(t,q)\in[0,t_{\star})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}

, which allows us to apply Varadhan’s lemma (e.g., see

) to deduce the desired result from the large deviation principle in Lemma

.
∎

Proof of Theorem

from (

). Let

be any bounded continuous function. Since we have,

It follows from Lemma

along with (

) that for every

,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\left\langle e^{N\Gamma(m_{N})}\right\rangle^{G,\omega}_{N;t,q}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{G+\Gamma-I_{t,q}\right\}-\sup\left\{G-I_{t,q}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.I^G=}\eqref{e.I_t,q=f^*-f}}}{{=}}\sup\{\Gamma-I^{G}_{t,q}\}.

Since this holds for any bounded continuous

\Longleftarrow

satisfies a large deviation principle under

.
∎

Proposition 4.8

Moreover, at every

Proof.

For brevity, we drop

from the notation and write

,

, and

.
From the definition of

, it is easy to see the first relation in (

). To see the second one, let

and we have

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.proj_adjoint}}}{{=}}(y^{\prime}-y)\cdot\left(\nabla_{q}f(t,\hat{y})\right)^{\vee}.

Hence, we can deduce the differentiability of

in

Using the definition of

in (

1.34

Appendix A
Multi-species models

We adapt the main results to multi-species spin glass models.

A.1.
Setting

We fix a finite set
\mathscr{S}
containing symbols for different species.
For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}})_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}
be a partition of
\{1,\dots,N\}
. Each
I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}
contains indices belonging to the
{\mathsf{s}}
-species.
For each

, we define

\displaystyle\lambda_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}=|I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}|/N,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S};\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{N}=\left(\lambda_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}\right)_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, we consider the simplex

\displaystyle\blacktriangle_{N}=\Big\{(\lambda_{\mathsf{s}})_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}\ \big|\ \lambda_{\mathsf{s}}\in[0,1]\cap\left(\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\right),\,\forall{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S};\;\sum_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}\lambda_{\mathsf{s}}=1\Big\}.

Here

\mathbb{Z}/\infty=\mathbb{R}
when
N=\infty
. In view of (
A.1
), we have
\lambda_{N}\in\blacktriangle_{N}
for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Throughout this appendix, we assume that

(A.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\lambda_{N}=\lambda_{\infty},

for some
\lambda_{\infty}\in\blacktriangle_{N}
.

For each
{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}
, let

be a finite positive measure supported on
[-1,+1]
and we assume
\supp P_{\mathsf{s}}\supsetneqq\{0\}
(related to the fact we require the support of

to span
\mathbb{R}^{D}

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, a spin configuration takes the form

In other words, denoting by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}P_{N}(\sigma)=\otimes_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}\otimes_{i\in I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}}\mathrm{d}P_{\mathsf{s}}(\sigma_{i}).

For two spin configurations
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
of size

{\mathsf{s}}
-species:

\displaystyle R_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=\tfrac{1}{N}\sigma_{I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}}\cdot\sigma^{\prime}_{I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}}\in[-1,+1],

\mathbb{R}^{I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}}
and similarly for
\sigma^{\prime}_{I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}}
. Hence, the spin overlap is
\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}
-valued:

\displaystyle R_{N}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=\left(R_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\right)_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}.

Let
\xi:\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function and assume the existence of a centered Gaussian process
(H_{N}(\sigma))_{\sigma\in[-1,+1]^{N}}
with covariance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[H_{N}(\sigma)H_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\right]=N\xi\left(R_{N}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\right).

Again,

is not assumed to be convex.

Fix any
{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}
.
Let
L_{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathbb{N}
and let

\mathbb{R}^{L_{\mathsf{s}}}

Let

and let

For each
i\in I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}

{\mathsf{s}}
-species mean magnetization and the total mean magnetization as, respectively,

\displaystyle m_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in I_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}}h_{\mathsf{s}}(\sigma_{i},\chi_{i})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptm_{N}=\left(m_{N,{\mathsf{s}}}\right)_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}},

which are
\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathsf{s}}}
-valued and
\prod_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathsf{s}}}
-valued.
Let

is the collection of increasing càdlàg paths

For

g(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,;x)
is differentiable on
[0,t_{\star})\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}_{+}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g(t,y;x)-\xi\big(\nabla_{y}g(t,y;x)\big)=0&\quad\text{ for }(t,y)\in(0,t_{\star})\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}_{+},\\
g(0,y;x)=\phi_{\lambda_{\infty}}(y;x)&\quad\text{ for }y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}_{+}.\end{cases}

Moreover, at every
(t,y)\in(0,t_{\star})\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}_{+}
, there is a unique solution
z\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}_{+}
to the fixed point equation

\displaystyle z^{\prime}=\nabla_{y}\phi_{\lambda_{\infty}}(t\nabla\xi(z^{\prime});x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}_{+},

and we have

g(t,y;x)=\phi_{\lambda_{\infty}}(y+t\nabla\xi(z);x)-t\theta(z)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla_{y}g(t,y;x)=z.

A.3.
Recipe for adaptation

Instead of rewriting everything in detail, we want to avoid repetition and give a recipe for adapting existing results to the multi-species setting.

We mention the difference in initial conditions.
In the vector spin glass setting, we simply have
\overline{F}^{x\mapsto x\cdot m}_{N}(0,q)=\psi(q;x)
(see the line above (
1.12
)). Here, we have (see
[
24
, Lemma 4.11]
)
\overline{F}^{x\mapsto x\cdot m}_{N}(0,q)=\psi_{\lambda_{N}}(q;x)
and thus, under the assumption that
\lambda_{N}
converges to
\lambda_{\infty}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\overline{F}^{x\mapsto x\cdot m}_{N}(0,q)=\psi_{\lambda_{\infty}}(q;x).

In the current setting,
\psi_{\mathsf{s}}
has the same form as
\psi
in (
1.12
) in the vector spin glass. So, each
\psi_{\mathsf{s}}
satisfies the properties in Proposition
2.2
with
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{p}}
therein replaced by
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{p}}(1)
.

To transfer these properties to
\psi_{\lambda_{\infty}}
, we first recall the norms on
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathsf{p}}
. We view
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathsf{p}}
as embedded into
L^{\mathsf{p}}([0,1);\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}})
, equiped with the norm

\displaystyle|\kappa|_{L^{\mathsf{p}}}=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\sum_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}\kappa_{\mathsf{s}}(r)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{{\mathsf{p}}}{2}}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{\frac{1}{{\mathsf{p}}}}.

Observe that at
{\mathsf{p}}=2
, we have
|\kappa|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}|\kappa_{\mathsf{s}}|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.

One can verify that Proposition
3.3
, Corollary
4.3
, and Lemma
4.4
hold with
t_{\star}
defined in (
A.4
). Indeed, checking the proofs, one can see that the definition of
t_{\star}
(previously in (
1.14
)) are used in (
3
), the line below (
3.14
), (
3.24
), and (
4.3
). Then, one can verify that the definition in (
A.4
) still works in all these cases. To restate and reprove these four propositions, one needs to replace
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathsf{p}}
,
\psi
(see (
1.12
)),
\mathrm{S}^{D}_{+}
, and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
therein by
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathsf{p}}
,
\psi_{\lambda_{\infty}}
,
\mathbb{R}^{\mathscr{S}}_{+}
, and
\prod_{{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathscr{S}}\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathsf{s}}}
, respectively. Cited results from
[
20
]
for vector spin glasses can be replaced by counterparts in
[
24
]
for multi-species spin glasses.

Once those three results are proven in the setting of multi-species models, one can straightforwardly use the same arguments as in the rest of Section
4
to finish the proofs of Theorems
A.1
,
A.2
, and
A.3
.
