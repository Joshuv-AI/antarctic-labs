The 2 2 -Quasi-Regularizability Conjecture and Independence Polynomials of 𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p} Graphs
Kevin Pereyra

Abstract
Hoang, Levit, Mandrescu and Pham asked for structural conditions ensuring that the independence polynomial of a 𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p} graph is log-concave, or at least unimodal, and conjectured that a connected 𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2} graph is 2 2 -quasi-regularizable if and only if n ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 3 ​ α ​ ( G ) n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G) (2026). We prove the conjecture. The key point is a local expansion theorem: if G G is connected and belongs to 𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2} , then every non-maximum independent set A A satisfies | N G ​ ( A ) | ≥ 2 ​ | A | . |N_{G}(A)|\geq 2|A|. Thus the only possible obstruction to 2 2 -quasi-regularizability in a connected 𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2} graph comes from maximum independent sets, where the condition is exactly n ⁡ ( G ) − α ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ α ​ ( G ) n(G)-\alpha(G)\geq 2\alpha(G) . We also give coefficient criteria for log-concavity and unimodality of independence polynomials of 𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p} graphs. These criteria combine the standard two-sided coefficient inequalities, as collected by Hoang–Levit–Mandrescu–Pham, with λ \lambda -quasi-regularizability. The new p = 2 p=2 threshold proved here inserts the missing case into the same framework and yields explicit log-concavity and unimodality regions for connected 𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2} graphs.

Introduction

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Its independence polynomial, introduced by Gutman and Harary
[
10

, is

I(G;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha(G)}s_{k}x^{k},

where

denotes the number of independent sets of cardinality

in
G

(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{\alpha(G)})
is a natural enumerative invariant of

, but it is far from being constrained in general: Alavi, Malde, Schwenk and Erdos showed that prescribed order patterns can occur among its coefficients
[
1
]
. Nevertheless, important graph classes do force regular behavior. For instance, Heilmann and Lieb’s real-rootedness theorem for matching polynomials
[
11
]
, and Chudnovsky and Seymour’s real-rootedness theorem for independence polynomials of claw-free graphs
[
5
]
, both imply log-concavity in their respective settings. Root locations and coefficient questions for independence polynomials have been studied from several viewpoints; see, for example, Brown–Hickman–Nowakowski
[
3
]
, the survey of Levit–Mandrescu
[
20
]
, and the later work on trees and clique-cover products
[
36
,
37
,
17
]
. The broader role of log-concavity in combinatorics is surveyed by Stanley
[
31
]
, and the modern theory is also influenced by Huh’s work on chromatic and matroidal log-concavity
[
15
,
16
]
.

This paper concerns the class
\mathbf{W}_{p}
introduced by Staples
[
32

]
. Well-covered graphs originate in the work of Plummer

Definition 2.1

(
\mathbf{W}_{p}
graphs)
.

Let

. A graph

Definition 2.2

(Quasi-regularizability)
.

Let
\lambda>0
. A graph

is
\lambda

\lambda|A|\leq|N_{G}(A)|

for every independent set

]
.

Definition 2.3

(Independence polynomial, log-concavity, unimodality)
.

Let

I(G;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha(G)}s_{k}x^{k}=s_{0}+s_{1}x+\cdots+s_{\alpha(G)}x^{\alpha(G)}

be the independence polynomial of
G
, where
s_{k}
is the number of independent sets of cardinality
k
. Following
[
12
]
, we say that
I(G;x)
is

•

log-concave
if

s_{k}^{2}\geq s_{k-1}s_{k+1}\qquad(1\leq k\leq\alpha(G)-1),

•

unimodal
if there exists an index
0\leq t\leq\alpha(G)
such that

s_{0}\leq\cdots\leq s_{t-1}\leq s_{t}\geq s_{t+1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{\alpha(G)}.

Lemma 2.4

(Keilson–Gerber product lemma)
.

Let
P(x)
and
Q(x)
have nonnegative coefficients and no internal zero coefficients. If
P(x)
is log-concave and
Q(x)
is unimodal, then
P(x)Q(x)
is unimodal. If both
P(x)
and
Q(x)
are log-concave, then
P(x)Q(x)
is log-concave.

The lemma is the standard polynomial form of a convolution theorem for nonnegative sequences. Keilson and Gerber established the classical strong-unimodality principle behind the first assertion
[
18
]
; in the present nonnegative setting one must include the no-internal-zero condition, as in the corrected formulation of Levit and Mandrescu
[
19
, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3]
. The compact independence-polynomial wording also appears explicitly in
[
22
, Theorem 1]
and is the form quoted by Hoang–Levit–Mandrescu–Pham
[
12
, Lemma 1.4]
. Since independence polynomials have strictly positive coefficients from degree
0
through degree
\alpha(G)
, the hypothesis is automatic in all applications below.

Lemma 2.6

(Known coefficient inequalities)
.

Let
G
have order
n
, independence number
\alpha

(i)

(ii)

p(\alpha-k)s_{k}\leq(k+1)s_{k+1},\qquad 1\leq k\leq\alpha-1.

This is due to Hoang, Levit, Mandrescu and Pham
[

.

These two inequalities are also recorded together as

; we quote them here because they are the coefficient input for the criteria below.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
G
be a graph with connected components
H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}
. If each

I(H_{i};x)
is log-concave, then
I(G;x)
is log-concave. If each

I(H_{i};x)
is unimodal and all but possibly one of them are log-concave,
then
I(G;x)
is unimodal.

Proof.

The independence polynomial is multiplicative over disjoint unions:

I(G;x)=\prod_{i=1}^{q}I(H_{i};x).

The assertions follow from Lemma

.

3
Coefficient criteria for Problem 4.1

The following criterion is the coefficient engine behind all log-concavity applications in this paper. It separates the enumerative step from the structural question of when a
\mathbf{W}_{p}
graph is
\lambda
-quasi-regularizable.

Theorem 3.1

(General log-concavity criterion)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph in
\mathbf{W}_{p}
, with
n=n(G)
and
\alpha=\alpha(G)
. Suppose that
G
is
\lambda
-quasi-regularizable. If

\Phi_{p,\lambda,n,\alpha}(k)=(k+1)p(\alpha-k+1)-k\bigl(n-(\lambda+1)k\bigr)\geq 0

for every
1\leq k\leq\alpha-1
, then
I(G;x)
is log-concave.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
2.6
(i) with index
k
and Lemma
2.6
(ii) with index
k-1
. For
1\leq k\leq\alpha-1
,

s_{k+1}\leq\frac{n-(\lambda+1)k}{k+1}s_{k},\qquad s_{k-1}\leq\frac{k}{p(\alpha-k+1)}s_{k}.

Multiplying gives

s_{k-1}s_{k+1}\leq\frac{k\bigl(n-(\lambda+1)k\bigr)}{(k+1)p(\alpha-k+1)}s_{k}^{2}.

The hypothesis is exactly the assertion that the rational factor is at most
1
.
∎

Corollary 3.2

(The

-quasi-regularizable specialization)

Let

and
\alpha=\alpha(G)

is

Proof.

Put

(k+1)p(\alpha-k+1)-k(n-(p+1)k)\geq 0

Theorem 3.3

(Explicit interval form)
.

Let
G

p\alpha+2\sqrt{p\alpha+p}<n\leq\frac{(\alpha^{2}+1)p+(\alpha-1)^{2}}{\alpha-1},\qquad\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\alpha+1}\leq p.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.2
, it is enough to guarantee

f(k)=k^{2}-(n-p\alpha)k+p\alpha+p\geq 0\qquad(1\leq k\leq\alpha-1).

Write

. The discriminant is

\Delta=r^{2}-4c.

If

Because

(p+1)\alpha\leq p\alpha+2\sqrt{p\alpha+p},

which is equivalent to
\alpha^{2}/(4(\alpha+1))\leq p

Assume now that
\Delta>0

\rho_{1}=\frac{r-\sqrt{\Delta}}{2},\qquad\rho_{2}=\frac{r+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}

be the two real roots. Since

for every integer

where the right-hand side must be nonnegative. Squaring (1) gives

r^{2}-4(p\alpha+p)\leq(r-2\alpha+2)^{2},

or, after simplification,

n\leq\frac{(\alpha^{2}+1)p+(\alpha-1)^{2}}{\alpha-1}.

Conversely, if

It remains only to check that the displayed lower bound in the statement forces the needed nonnegativity of

\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\alpha+1}\leq p

Thus, since

forces

5
Solution of Conjecture 4.2

Theorem 5.1

(Conjecture 4.2)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph in
\mathbf{W}_{2}
. Then
G
is
2
-quasi-regularizable if and only if

n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G).

Proof.

If
G
is
2
-quasi-regularizable and
S\in\Omega(G)
, then

n(G)-\alpha(G)=|N_{G}(S)|\geq 2|S|=2\alpha(G),

so
n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G)
.

Conversely, assume
n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G)
and let
A\in\operatorname{Ind}(G)
. If
A
is not maximum, then Theorem
4.1
gives

|N_{G}(A)|\geq 2|A|.

If
A
is maximum, then

|N_{G}(A)|=n(G)-\alpha(G)\geq 2\alpha(G)=2|A|.

Thus
|N_{G}(A)|\geq 2|A|
for every independent set
A
, which is precisely
2
-quasi-regularizability.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Theorem
4.1
is stronger than Conjecture 4.2. The global inequality
n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G)
is needed only for maximum independent sets. Every non-maximum independent set in a connected
\mathbf{W}_{2}
graph satisfies the
2
-quasi-regularizability inequality automatically.

6
Consequences for Problem 4.1

We now insert the solved

.

Corollary 6.1

(Log-concavity for connected
\mathbf{W}_{2}
graphs)
.

Let

be connected,

, with

and
\alpha=\alpha(G)
. Suppose that

. If

then

is log-concave.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1
, the graph
G
is
2
-quasi-regularizable. Apply Corollary
3.2
with

.
∎

Corollary 6.2

(Explicit
p=2
interval form)
.

Let
G
be connected,
G\in\mathbf{W}_{2}
, with

and
\alpha=\alpha(G)\geq 2
. Suppose that

. Then

is log-concave whenever either

Proof.

Theorem

gives
2
-quasi-regularizability from

. The result is Theorem

with

.
∎

Corollary 6.3

(Unimodality for connected
\mathbf{W}_{2}

.

Let
G
be connected,

, with

and
\alpha=\alpha(G)
. Suppose that

. If

\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{4}\right\rceil\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2\alpha-1}{3}\right\rfloor+1,

By Theorem

, the graph is

-quasi-regularizable. Apply Theorem

.

Corollary 6.4

(A unified set of sufficient conditions for Problem 4.1)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph in
\mathbf{W}_{p}
, with
n=n(G)
and
\alpha=\alpha(G)
.

(a)

If
G
is
\lambda
-quasi-regularizable and

(k+1)p(\alpha-k+1)-k\bigl(n-(\lambda+1)k\bigr)\geq 0

for every

, then

(b)

(c)

If

(d)

If

\mathbf{W}_{2}

Remark 6.5
.

Part (d) is the missing

]
. The coefficient inequalities themselves are uniform in

7
Final remarks

The local theorem explains why the case
p=2
was delicate. In a smallest hypothetical counterexample, localizing at
A-\{x\}
creates a
K_{2}
component
\{x,m_{x}\}
. Such a component is allowed in
\mathbf{W}_{2}
, whereas higher
p
arguments usually benefit from stronger local degree constraints. The proof above eliminates precisely this obstruction by showing that the remaining connection to
G_{A}
must pass through a unique universal vertex, and then using the
\mathbf{W}_{2}
extension property one final time.

For independence polynomials, the conclusion is that the conditions in Problem 4.1 can now be applied uniformly at the quasi-regularizability threshold: for
p\neq 2
by the theorem of Hoang–Levit–Mandrescu–Pham, and for
p=2
by Theorem
5.1
. Componentwise versions follow from Lemma
2.4
, which is why log-concavity results for connected components extend cleanly to disconnected
\mathbf{W}_{p}
graphs.
