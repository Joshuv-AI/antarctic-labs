Determinants and configurational pathways of healthcare big data asset valuation:an analysis using the TOE framework and fsQCA
Authors: Rong Jiang, Zihan Yuan, Shenghu Tian, Chen Yang, Xuetao Pu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHealthcare big data represents a strategic national resource with substantial potential value. At the policy level, ongoing efforts have been made to promote the transformation of healthcare data resources into data assets. However, the realization of their value remains challenging due to variations in data quality, uncertainty in application scenarios, privacy-related risks, and imperfect benefit-sharing mechanisms. This study seeks to identify the key factors affecting the valuation of healthcare big data assets. The findings are expected to provide a theoretical basis for developing scientific valuation models, optimizing resource allocation, and promoting high-quality development in the healthcare industry.MethodThis study combines the Technology-Organization-Environment (TOE) framework with information ecosystem theory to develop a dynamic value-added cycle framework for healthcare big data assets. Based on expert and practitioner surveys, six key condition variables were identified: Data Quality and Standards, Data Sharing Interface, Benefit-Sharing Mechanism, Cross-Organizational Collaboration Mechanisms, Market Demand, and Data Security and Privacy Governance Capacity. Applying Fuzzy Set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA), the study examines complex causal pathways that lead to high asset value realization from a configurational perspective.ResultsThe study indicates that the high-level value realization of healthcare big data assets is shaped by the joint effects of technological, organizational, and environmental conditions. The configurational analysis identified three representative pathways: a Quality-security driven pathway, a Demand-driven collaborative transformation pathway, and a Market transaction-driven pathway. Among these, data quality and standardization appeared across all pathways, indicating that they constitute fundamental conditions for realizing the value of healthcare big data assets. By contrast, Data Security and Privacy Governance Capacity, benefit-sharing mechanisms, and market demand showed differentiated combinational effects across pathways, suggesting that they serve as important enabling conditions for value realization.These findings indicate that the valuation of healthcare big data assets should move beyond a single-attribute assessment approach toward a scenario-based and configurational evaluation framework. Greater attention should be paid to the alignment among data quality, security governance, collaborative mechanisms, benefit-sharing arrangements, and application demand.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-15011-1.

Introduction

With the rapid development of the digital economy and the ongoing advancement of market-oriented data element allocation, healthcare big data has become a crucial strategic asset for global health governance and digital economic connectivity. It serves as a vital foundation for improving healthcare service quality and optimizing resource allocation, gradually becoming essential for these efforts [1]. Healthcare big data refers to large-scale, highly complex, and diverse datasets generated from multiple sources within the healthcare sector. This includes not only traditional clinical data but also multi-source heterogeneous data such as genomics, proteomics, metabolomics, imaging, clinical diagnostics, and long-term continuous physiological monitoring of individuals [2]. These data are characterized by high velocity, high value, and privacy concerns [3, 4]. Therefore, how to conduct a scientific valuation of healthcare big data is not only related to the efficiency of its transformation from a “resource” into an “asset”, but also has direct implications for data circulation and sharing, collaborative industrial innovation, and the improvement of public health governance capacity.

Recognizing its importance, governments around the world have introduced relevant policies. China has also successively implemented policies such as the “14th Five-Year Plan for Digital Economic Development,” the “Three-Year Action Plan for Data Element × (2024–2026),” and the “Healthy China 2030 Plan Outline.” These policies explicitly emphasize the development and utilization, sharing and circulation, and value release of healthcare big data. However, in clinical and managerial practice, the assetization of healthcare big data still faces multiple barriers, including inconsistent data standards, difficulties in cross-institutional sharing, incompatible platform interfaces, unclear benefit-sharing mechanisms, growing pressure related to data security and privacy governance, and insufficient market demand and application transformation. Together, these barriers have created a gap between the high potential of healthcare data resources and the relatively low efficiency of value realization. This indicates that the lack of a scientific and unified valuation system has become a key obstacle to the transformation of healthcare big data from a “resource” into an “asset”. Therefore, systematically identifying and thoroughly examining the key factors influencing healthcare big data asset valuation is not only essential for building scientific valuation models and ensuring fair pricing but also fundamental to accurately identifying implementation barriers and developing effective interventions to bridge the “implementation gap” [5].

Regarding this issue, scholars have examined this issue from various perspectives, including the data lifecycle, privacy security mechanisms, and market supply-demand dynamics. Zhai [6] used fuzzy DEMATEL and the Interpretive Structural Model (ISM) from a data lifecycle perspective to identify the roles of key factors such as data sharing, transactions, and privacy protection. Huang et al. [7] explored privacy security safeguards for biomedical big data, highlighting the crucial role of data security in the assetization process. Yan et al. [8] created a machine learning-based data asset valuation model. Empirical analysis of sample data showed that data volume, scale, quality, freshness, and industry affiliation significantly influence data asset value. Rong et al. [9] studied tiered licensing mechanisms in data markets, analyzing how market supply-demand dynamics affect data asset valuation and pricing. Borup Daniel et al. [10] investigated the effects of data adjustments on asset pricing, proposing the Revised Capital Asset Pricing Model (Revised CCAPM) and confirming its effectiveness with historical data. Current domestic and international research has advanced in analyzing multifaceted influencing factors and integrating theory with empirical evidence, offering a solid theoretical foundation and practical guidance for studying the factors impacting healthcare big data asset valuation.

Although existing research has made some progress, there is still room for growth. First, existing studies lack a systematic perspective. They often examine technological, organizational, or environmental factors in isolation, while overlooking the fact that healthcare settings function as complex adaptive systems. In such contexts, the valuation and value realization of healthcare big data assets are often shaped by the interaction of multiple contextual conditions.Second, insufficient attention has been paid to complex causality. Most existing studies rely on linear thinking to analyze individual factors or net effects, making it difficult to reveal possible substitutive relationships, complementary relationships, and multiple pathways through which value realization may be achieved.Third, the industry-specific characteristics of healthcare have not been adequately considered. The healthcare sector is subject to strict regulatory requirements, including the Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Medical Services (Draft for Comment) and the Regulations on Network Data Security Management. These mandatory requirements for privacy protection and regulatory compliance directly affect asset valuation, yet they are often simplified in existing models [11]. Therefore, it is important to systematically identify the key conditions that influence the valuation of healthcare big data assets and to further reveal how these conditions combine into different configurations to produce a high level of value realization.

Building on this research, this study introduces the technology-organization-environment (TOE) framework to systematically classify the factors influencing the valuation of healthcare big data assets. It then identifies the key conditions affecting such valuation and further applies fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to examine the nonlinear configurational mechanisms underlying the valuation of healthcare big data assets. Its theoretical contribution lies in constructing an integrated framework for valuing healthcare big data assets, showing how technological empowerment, organizational management, and environmental pressures work together to influence valuation outcomes. Practically, by uncovering these factors and their interactions, this approach offers decision-making support for healthcare institutions and companies to optimize technology investments, improve organizational management, and enhance policy adaptability. This boosts resource allocation efficiency and unlocks the economic potential of data. Additionally, the findings may provide policy implications for governments seeking to improve the circulation, sharing, and value release of healthcare data.

Methods

Research approach

This study follows a logical sequence of “establishing a theoretical framework—constructing an indicator system—analyzing configuration paths.” Based on information ecosystem theory and the TOE framework, we develop a dynamic value-added cycle framework: “Data Flow → Value Extraction → Feedback Optimization → Ecosystem Expansion.” This model overcomes the limitations of single-theory approaches by adopting a three-dimensional perspective that includes technology, organization, and environment. It systematically captures the dynamic nature of asset valuation, providing a solid foundation for subsequent factor identification and path discovery.

Given that the valuation of healthcare big data assets remains at an exploratory stage, a mature and unified market pricing mechanism has not yet been established in practice. In addition, sufficient and comparable historical transaction data are still lacking. Therefore, relying solely on traditional econometric methods may not be sufficient to identify the key driving mechanisms underlying asset valuation. The Delphi method, characterized by anonymity, iterative feedback, and statistical aggregation [12, 13], is well suited to research contexts where information is incomplete, historical data are limited, and the outcome is shaped by multiple interacting factors. Accordingly, this study first used expert consultation and interviews to screen and refine the potential influencing factors.

Fuzzy-Set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) is a research method based on set theory and Boolean algebra that systematically explores complex causal relationships through cross-case comparisons [14]. Its main goal is to identify multiple, alternative combinations of conditions (i.e., “configurations”) that can produce a specific outcome. The fsQCA process includes four key steps: data calibration, analysis of necessary conditions, analysis of conditional configurations, and robustness testing [15]. The value realization of healthcare big data assets cannot be explained as a simple linear accumulation of individual factors. Instead, it is shaped by the combined effects of multiple technological, organizational, and environmental conditions, indicating a typical pattern of complex causality. In practice, different healthcare institutions, data platforms, and application contexts may achieve high-value realization through distinct combinations of conditions, reflecting equifinality. By contrast, traditional regression-based approaches mainly estimate the net effects of individual variables and are therefore limited in capturing the concurrent influence of multiple conditions and their configurational relationships.Compared with traditional approaches that emphasize the net effects of individual variables, fsQCA is better suited to examining the interactions among multiple conditions and identifying alternative pathways that lead to the same outcome. It therefore provides an appropriate methodological approach for revealing the diverse mechanisms underlying the value realization of healthcare big data assets, which is closely aligned with the central aim of this study.

Data source

This study employed a two-stage data collection strategy combining expert interviews and questionnaire surveys. The first stage aimed to identify and screen the core factors influencing the valuation of healthcare big data assets. Based on relevance to the research topic, professional representativeness, and practical experience, five experts were invited to participate in the consultation. These experts had long been engaged in healthcare data governance, medical informatization, data platform operation, policy research, and related academic research. All of them had led National Natural Science Foundation of China projects related to the topic of this study, and had accumulated substantial academic expertise and practical experience in healthcare data governance and related fields.Through two rounds of questionnaire feedback and semi-structured interviews, the potential factors were comprehensively assessed, ranked, and screened in terms of their importance and relevance to asset valuation. This process gradually narrowed the range of candidate factors and refined the core conditions to be included in the subsequent configurational analysis. The main purpose of this stage was to screen and refine candidate factors, rather than to conduct large-scale statistical inference. Therefore, the feedback obtained from the two rounds of consultation and semi-structured interviews with five experts was considered sufficient for the preliminary identification of variables.In the second stage, structured questionnaires were used to collect case data. The respondents mainly included practitioners from medical institutions, data platforms, policy research organizations, and related fields. After the questionnaires were collected, the completeness and validity of the responses were examined. Finally, 80 valid questionnaires were obtained. Each valid questionnaire, reflecting a perceived scenario of value realization, was treated as one case for the subsequent fsQCA analysis. To enhance the practical relevance and explanatory depth of the study, this research also systematically reviewed relevant national policies, industry reports, and existing academic literature, which provided important contextual support for variable identification, framework development, and interpretation of the findings.

Variable design

Outcome Variable: Considering that there is currently no unified, mature, and directly observable objective market-based evaluation standard for the valuation of healthcare big data assets, this study used the “level of value realization of healthcare big data assets” as the outcome variable. This variable describes the extent to which healthcare big data assets achieve high value in real-world application contexts.The level of value realization of healthcare big data assets refers to the overall state reflected in their value performance, potential for value transformation, long-term strategic value, and effectiveness of assetization. The outcome variable was measured using independent items in the second-stage questionnaire, covering overall value performance, value transformation potential, long-term strategic value, and assetization effectiveness. A five-point Likert scale was used for all items, and the mean score of these items was calculated as the raw value of the outcome variable.

Conditional variables: Based on the dynamic value-added cycle framework shown in Fig. 2, this study first systematically identified potential influencing factors from four stages: data flow, value mining, feedback optimization, and ecosystem expansion. Combined with a review of the literature, policy text analysis, and expert consultation, 23 candidate factors were initially identified. To improve the transparency and interpretability of condition selection, these potential factors were further screened through two rounds of expert consultation [16]. In the first round, experts evaluated each candidate factor in terms of its “importance” and “relevance to valuation”. The scoring results were analyzed using the mean, standard deviation, coefficient of variation (CV), and item-level content validity index (I-CVI) [17]. In the second round, the relative priority and core status of each factor were compared through dimension-specific ranking, Kendall’s coefficient of concordance, and the frequency with which each factor was selected as a core factor across dimensions [18]. Finally, considering expert opinions, theoretical representativeness, and the requirement of fsQCA regarding the number of condition variables, six core condition variables were selected for the subsequent configurational analysis: data quality and standards (DQS), data sharing interface (DSI), benefit-sharing mechanism (BSM), cross-organizational collaboration mechanism (COCM), market demand (MD), and data security and privacy governance capacity (DSPG). The relevant testing methods and statistical results are presented in Appendices A-C. Theoretical foundations and the dynamic value-added cycle Framework

With the continued advancement of market-oriented allocation of data elements, the value formation of healthcare big data assets is no longer merely the result of data processing within a single organization. Instead, it has become a complex process involving multiple stakeholders, continuous feedback optimization, and dynamic ecosystem expansion. Research on information ecosystem theory emphasizes the synergistic relationships among information, actors, technology, and the environment, and focuses on the mechanisms through which information is generated, circulated, shared, and utilized in specific contexts. This perspective provides a holistic lens for understanding the formation of data value [19]. According to previous studies, the value of data assets depends not only on the technical attributes of the data itself, but is also embedded in a broader system shaped by actor collaboration, platform support, institutional arrangements, and the external environment. It therefore exhibits clear ecological, networked, and evolutionary characteristics [20]. In the context of healthcare big data assets, value realization is continuously accumulated and released through ongoing interactions among data flow, collaborative application, feedback adjustment, and value expansion [21, 22].

Although traditional data value chain models clearly describe the basic stages of data generation, storage, analysis, and service provision, their linear and static analytical logic is insufficient to fully explain the dynamic process through which the value of healthcare big data assets is formed. Previous studies have shown that data value realization is not completed in a single stage, but continuously evolves through data application, feedback adjustment, and ongoing expansion [23, 24]. Abbasi et al. [25] further argued that data use itself can generate new data, thereby supporting subsequent value-creating activities. At the same time, value realization also depends on continuous feedback and optimization across dimensions such as utility, cost, and quality.These insights suggest that the value formation of healthcare big data assets is better understood from the perspective of cyclical iteration and ecosystem collaboration, rather than being explained solely through a static linear chain.

After clarifying the dynamic cyclical mechanism underlying the value formation of healthcare big data assets, this study further introduces the technology–organization–environment (TOE) framework. The TOE framework emphasizes the joint effects of technological conditions, organizational mechanisms, and the external environment on innovation adoption and value realization [26]. It therefore provides a clear analytical basis for classifying the factors influencing the valuation of healthcare big data assets and for identifying how technological, organizational, and environmental factors jointly shape their final value.

Building on this, this paper integrates information ecosystem theory with the TOE framework to build a dynamic value-added cycle: “Data Flow—Value Extraction—Feedback Optimization—Ecosystem Expansion” (as shown in Fig. 2). This framework not only accurately reflects the two main features of big data value realization—cyclical iteration and ecosystem co-creation—but also offers strong support for systematically studying the factors that influence the valuation of healthcare big data assets.

The dynamic value-added cycle framework constructed in this study is mainly used to systematically identify potential influencing factors in the value realization process of healthcare big data assets. Combined with the TOE framework, these factors are further classified from three dimensions: technology, organization, and environment. Starting from four stages—data circulation, value transformation, feedback adjustment, and ecosystem collaboration—the framework suggests that this study should comprehensively consider factors such as data quality, platform interfaces, security governance, benefit-sharing arrangements, collaborative mechanisms, market demand, policy environment, and ecosystem maturity. This provides a foundation for the subsequent construction of the indicator system.Data circulation forms the foundation of this framework. It emphasizes the compliant, secure, and orderly flow of healthcare big data both within institutions and across institutional boundaries. This stage depends not only on technological support such as data sharing interfaces, infrastructure, and security governance [27], but is also jointly influenced by organizational processes, collaborative mechanisms, and institutional rules [28, 29]. Only when data can flow stably and be shared effectively can subsequent value mining have a practical basis.

On the basis of continuous data accumulation, value mining becomes a key stage in assetization. Through technologies such as data analytics and machine learning, raw data can be transformed into insights and knowledge that support clinical decision-making, scientific research, and operational management. The effectiveness of this process depends on an organization’s data science capacity, team collaboration, and the rational allocation of resources [30]. It should also be aligned with external market demand and policy orientation to ensure that the generated insights have practical relevance and commercial value, thereby laying a solid foundation for maximizing the final value of the assets [31].

Unlike traditional static value chain models, the value of healthcare big data assets does not become fixed after value mining. Instead, it needs to be continuously iterated through feedback optimization mechanisms. During application, user feedback, changes in application scenarios, policy adjustments, and technological updates may all feed back into data governance, model optimization, and process improvement, thereby continuously enhancing the efficiency and adaptability of value realization.

Once value has been validated through continuous optimization, the cycle enters the stage of ecosystem expansion. At this stage, data value is no longer confined to a single organization, but is continuously extended through open sharing, cross-organizational collaboration, and multi-stakeholder participation. Research institutions, platform enterprises, third-party developers, and public governance actors become embedded in the process of data value realization, promoting the transition of healthcare big data from localized applications to ecosystem-based co-creation. In this stage, standardized interfaces, benefit-sharing mechanisms, cross-organizational collaboration capacity, external market demand, and the institutional environment play important roles in sustaining value release [32].

Taken together, these four stages constitute a continuous and mutually reinforcing cycle that jointly drives the ongoing circulation and value enhancement of healthcare big data assets. This cyclical framework not only explains the iterative and ecosystem-based nature of value formation in healthcare big data assets, but also provides a theoretical basis for the subsequent selection of variables.

Analysis of the configuration pathway for factors influencing the valuation of healthcare big data Assets

This study uses fsQCA 3.0 software to perform fuzzy set qualitative comparative analysis on 80 cases, aiming to identify multiple configurational pathways leading to the value realization of healthcare big data assets.

Data calibration

Given that the variables in this study were mainly derived from the mean scores of questionnaire items, and that there is currently no directly observable objective benchmark for determining the value realization of healthcare big data assets, this study adopted the direct calibration method to transform continuous perceptual measurement data into set membership scores [33]. Specifically, the 95th, 50th, and 5th percentiles of the sample data were used as the calibration anchors for full membership, the crossover point, and full non-membership, respectively [34]. To avoid ambiguity in set membership assignment when some cases had membership scores close to the crossover point, this study followed the approach used in previous research and subtracted 0.001 from membership scores below 1 [35]. This adjustment was intended to reduce the ambiguity caused by membership scores falling exactly at or very close to 0.5. After calibration, the fuzzy-set membership scores of all condition variables and the outcome variable ranged between 0 and 1, meeting the basic requirements for subsequent necessary condition analysis and configurational analysis. The specific calibration anchors are presented in Appendix D.

Single-factor necessity analysis

Before conducting the configurational analysis, this study first performed a necessary condition analysis for each antecedent condition to determine whether any single condition constituted a necessary prerequisite for achieving a high level of value realization of healthcare big data assets. In general, when the consistency score of a condition reaches 0.90 or above, it can be regarded as a necessary condition for the occurrence of the outcome [36, 37]. The results, presented in Appendix E, show that the consistency scores of all conditions were below 0.90. This indicates that no single factor independently constituted a necessary condition for a high level of value realization of healthcare big data assets. In other words, the value realization of healthcare big data assets is not driven by any single dominant factor, but rather results from the interdependence and synergistic effects of multiple internal and external factors. Therefore, it is both necessary and appropriate to adopt a configurational perspective to examine the combined effects of these factors.

Condition configuration analysis

Given that this study involves a small-to-medium sample size (n = 80), the raw consistency threshold should be appropriately raised based on existing literature [38]. At the same time, the raw consistency threshold should be set so that the rows in the truth table resulting in outcomes of 0 and 1 are roughly equal in number [39]. Ultimately, the sufficiency analysis was conducted using the following thresholds: case frequency ≥ 2, raw consistency ≥ 0.80, and PRI consistency ≥ 0.7 [40]. Ultimately, three solutions of varying complexity were generated: complex, intermediate, and parsimonious solutions [41]. Following mainstream practice, intermediate solutions were prioritized, with parsimonious solution serving as supplements. According to Fiss (2011) standards, condition configurations were mapped (Table 2) to distinguish core conditions from peripheral ones. The configurational analysis identified three pathways leading to a high level of value realization of healthcare big data assets. Pathway 1 (H1): Quality-security driven pathway

The consistency score for configuration H1（*DQS*DSI*BSM*COCM*DSPG）is 0.96, a raw coverage of 0.526, and a unique coverage of 0.082. This indicates that 52.6% of the cases could be explained by this configuration, while 8.2% of the cases were explained exclusively by this configuration.The central feature of this pathway is the formation of a trustworthy data foundation. In the value realization of healthcare big data assets, data must first be usable and trustworthy. Data quality determines whether data can be effectively integrated, analyzed, and reused, whereas security governance capacity determines whether data can enter circulation and application under conditions of compliance, trustworthiness, and controllable risk.Healthcare big data differs from general data assets because of its high sensitivity. Therefore, security governance is not merely an additional condition, but a prerequisite for value release. Without effective data security and privacy risk control, even high-quality data may fail to gain the trust of medical institutions, platform operators, and data users.The presence of data sharing interfaces (DSI), benefit-sharing mechanisms (BSM), and cross-organizational collaboration mechanisms (COCM) as peripheral conditions further suggests that this pathway does not rely solely on data itself. Instead, it requires the joint support of technical channels, interest coordination, and organizational collaboration. For example, in the construction of regional healthcare data platforms, data often come from multiple hospitals and public health institutions, where inconsistent data definitions, incompatible system interfaces, and strict privacy compliance requirements are common. Therefore, only when DQS, DSPG, DSI, BSM, and COCM are jointly present can fragmented data resources be transformed into shareable, governable, and applicable data assets.

Pathway 2 (H2): Demand-driven collaborative transformation pathway

The consistency score for configuration H2（*DQS*BSM*COCM*MD*DSPG）is 0.981, a raw coverage of 0.500, and a unique coverage of 0.052. This indicates that 50.0% of the cases could be explained by this configuration, while 5.2% of the cases were explained exclusively by this configuration.This pathway suggests that high-quality and risk-controllable data need to be transformed into asset value through strong market demand and an effective benefit-sharing mechanism. Market demand provides external pull for the value realization of data assets, while the benefit-sharing mechanism addresses incentive alignment among participating actors. The cross-organizational collaboration mechanism further reduces cooperation costs among medical institutions, data platforms, research institutions, and enterprises.Compared with H1, H2 places greater emphasis on market demand and organizational coordination. DSI does not constitute a key condition in this pathway, indicating that, in some value realization contexts, platform-based data sharing interfaces may not be the decisive condition distinguishing high levels of value realization. As long as data quality is high, security governance is effective, market demand is clear, and benefit-sharing arrangements are well established, healthcare big data assets can still achieve value transformation through organizational collaboration.For example, in areas such as drug development, medical insurance payment optimization, actuarial analysis, and public health monitoring, data users have clear demand for high-quality and compliant data. However, whether the value of data assets can be realized also depends on whether data providers have sufficient incentives to share data and whether clear benefit-sharing and collaboration mechanisms can be established among participating actors. Therefore, H2 reflects a value transformation logic characterized by “demand pull, benefit incentives, and trusted security”.

Pathway 3 (H3): Market transaction-driven pathway

The consistency of configuration H3（*DQS*DSI*BSM~COCM*MD~DSPG）is 0.964, a raw coverage of 0.225, and a unique coverage of 0.037. Compared with H1 and H2, this pathway had a lower coverage, indicating that its scope of applicability was relatively limited and that it should be understood as a complementary pathway.Unlike H2, H3 does not rely on a strong cross-organizational collaboration mechanism or prominent security governance capacity as key conditions. Instead, it promotes value realization through high-quality data, clear market demand, and well-defined benefit-sharing arrangements. In this pathway, the data sharing interface provides basic connectivity, enabling data to be accessed and used by demand-side actors.However, the absence of COCM and DSPG does not mean that cross-organizational collaboration or security governance is unimportant. Rather, it indicates that, in the specific contexts represented by this pathway, these two conditions do not constitute the main dependencies for achieving high-value realization. This pathway is more likely to occur in scenarios with clear boundaries, fewer participating actors, or relatively closed application settings, such as disease-specific data services, data support for a single research project, or the productization of data within an institution.In these contexts, transaction relationships are relatively simple, demand is more clearly defined, and benefit-sharing arrangements are easier to establish. Therefore, even when cross-organizational collaboration networks and security governance capacity are not prominent conditions, a relatively high level of value realization may still be achieved. Nevertheless, given the inherent sensitivity of healthcare big data, this pathway should not be interpreted as suggesting that security governance can be neglected. Rather, it should be understood as a limited market-oriented transformation pathway under specific application scenarios.

Robustness test

To verify the reliability of the fsQCA analysis results and the theoretical robustness of the model, this study further increased the consistency testing threshold. The original model’s consistency threshold was raised from 0.80 to 0.85 [42], and reran the configurational analysis. The results showed that the three pathways were still retained after the consistency threshold was increased. Although their raw consistency and coverage values changed to some extent, all pathways continued to meet the core criteria for robustness assessment in fsQCA. This suggests that the identified configurations maintained their causal logic under more conservative parameter settings, and their substantive interpretation remained essentially unchanged, as shown in Appendix F. Therefore, the configurational results are robust.

Conclusions and implications

Research conclusions

First, the value realization of healthcare big data assets depends fundamentally on value activation. Merely possessing a large volume of data resources does not necessarily lead to a high level of data asset value. The results show that data quality and standards (DQS) appeared in all three configurational pathways, indicating that high-quality and standardized data constitute the foundation for value realization. However, value release also depends on the joint effects of security governance, platform interfaces, benefit-sharing mechanisms, collaborative mechanisms, and market demand. Therefore, the valuation of healthcare big data assets should not remain limited to data scale or resource stock. Instead, greater attention should be paid to whether data can be accessed in a compliant manner, effectively shared, continuously applied, and transformed into actual economic and social value.

Second, the high-value realization of healthcare big data assets results from the nonlinear synergy of multiple factors. The necessary condition analysis showed that no single factor independently constituted a necessary condition for high-value realization, suggesting that the value of healthcare big data assets is not determined by any single advantageous condition. H1 emphasizes that data quality and security governance jointly form the foundation for trusted data circulation. H2 highlights that, under the pull of market demand, benefit-sharing arrangements and cross-organizational collaboration promote value transformation. H3 further indicates that, in specific application scenarios with relatively clear boundaries, data quality, platform interfaces, benefit-sharing arrangements, and market demand can also form a pathway toward high-value realization. Therefore, the valuation of healthcare big data assets should shift from single-factor assessment to configurational evaluation.

Third, the value realization of healthcare big data assets is strongly embedded in specific application contexts. The findings indicate that different configurational pathways do not correspond to a single fixed valuation logic, but instead reflect differentiated combinations of conditions under different value realization scenarios. The value of healthcare big data assets is not determined solely by the intrinsic attributes of the data itself. It also depends on the application tasks in which the data are embedded, the actors involved in circulation, the compliance boundaries faced, and whether stable expectations of returns can be established. For scenarios oriented toward public governance and research sharing, trusted circulation and collaborative governance are more likely to become central considerations in valuation. By contrast, for scenarios oriented toward market transactions and application transformation, demand intensity, benefit-sharing arrangements, and transformation efficiency may have a stronger influence on value judgment. Therefore, the valuation of healthcare big data assets should not adopt a uniform and static evaluation logic. Instead, differentiated assessments should be conducted according to data use, circulation scope, participating actors, and risk constraints, with attention to the key conditions underlying different pathways of value realization.

Practical and policy implications

Based on the research conclusions, the following practical implications are drawn:

First, data governance mechanisms should be strengthened to consolidate the foundation of data asset value. Data quality and standards played a foundational role in all pathways leading to high-value realization. This provides clear practical guidance: data governance mechanisms must be strengthened to improve the intrinsic quality of healthcare big data. Medical institutions and data platforms should regard data governance as a prerequisite for assetization and focus on improving their capacities for data collection, cleaning, annotation, standardization, and quality assessment. This can help avoid an inefficient situation in which data are available but difficult to use and value. Only when data reach a high level of accuracy, completeness, consistency, and reusability can subsequent sharing, transactions, and application transformation have a practical basis. Second, value realization should be driven by real application scenarios, rather than by static valuation detached from demand. The value of healthcare big data assets does not automatically emerge from the mere existence of data; instead, it must be transformed through specific application scenarios. Policymakers, medical institutions, and data platforms should actively align data supply with demand in areas such as drug development, scientific research, chronic disease management, and public health monitoring, thereby promoting stable connections between data providers and data users. For enterprises, the focus should also shift from simply obtaining data to using data to address specific problems, so that identifiable value can be generated in model training, risk assessment, research and development decision-making, and management optimization.

Third, benefit-sharing and cross-organizational collaboration mechanisms should be improved to reduce institutional costs in data circulation. The findings indicate that benefit-sharing mechanisms and cross-organizational collaboration mechanisms are important supports for the value transformation of healthcare big data. In practice, mechanisms such as data authorization, joint development, revenue sharing, data trusts, and standardized cooperation agreements can be used to clarify the rights, responsibilities, and benefit arrangements among data providers, data governance actors, platform operators, and data users. Only when data holders have incentives to share data, data governance actors have incentives to invest, and data users can obtain stable services can data assets truly enter circulation and application.

Fourth, flexible top-level design should be improved, and data security governance should be strengthened. Healthcare data are highly sensitive, and the control of security and privacy risks is not an additional requirement for value realization, but a prerequisite for data assets to enter markets and application scenarios. Regulatory authorities should further refine rules on the classification and grading of healthcare data, authorized use, compliance review, accountability, and cross-actor data circulation. Medical institutions and platforms should also improve mechanisms for data desensitization, access control, audit trails, and risk assessment. At the same time, the valuation of healthcare big data assets should not be based solely on data scale, the number of data fields, or construction costs. Instead, it should adopt scenario-based assessment by considering data use, participating actors, circulation boundaries, compliance costs, and approaches to revenue realization.

Limitations and future outlook

Although this study constructed a dynamic value-added cycle framework for healthcare big data assets by integrating information ecosystem theory and the TOE framework, and identified the key conditions and multiple configurational pathways for value realization through expert consultation and fsQCA, several limitations remain.First, this study mainly relied on questionnaire data for the fsQCA analysis. Each questionnaire reflected respondents’judgments about value realization scenarios based on their professional experience. Although this approach helps reveal the configurational relationships among multiple conditions in the value realization of healthcare big data assets, the results may still be influenced by differences in respondents’cognition, professional backgrounds, and subjective judgments. Future studies could incorporate real transaction cases, operational data from data platforms, and practical evidence from hospital data assetization to conduct cross-validation.Second, this study adopted the direct calibration method and used sample percentiles as calibration anchors. This approach improves the feasibility of calibration in the absence of unified external standards. However, the selection of calibration thresholds still involves a certain degree of researcher judgment. Future research could further compare the stability of configurations under different calibration schemes.Third, this study focused on six core condition variables and a sample of specific professional respondents. Therefore, the findings are more applicable to explaining the current exploratory stage of healthcare big data assetization. Future studies could test the proposed framework using larger samples, different regions, and diverse data application scenarios to improve the generalizability and external validity of the findings.
