SAFE: Stepwise Atomic Feedback for Error correction in Multi-hop Reasoning
Daeyong Kwon, Soyoung Yoon, Seung-won Hwang

Abstract
Multi-hop QA benchmarks frequently reward Large Language Models (LLMs) for spurious correctness, masking ungrounded or flawed reasoning steps. To shift toward rigorous reasoning, we propose SAFE , a dynamic benchmarking framework that replaces the ungrounded Chain-of-Thought (CoT) with a strictly verifiable sequence of grounded entities. Our framework operates across two phases: (1) train-time verification, where we establish an atomic error taxonomy and a Knowledge Graph (KG)-grounded verification pipeline to eliminate noisy supervision in standard benchmarks, identifying up to 14% of instances as unanswerable, and (2) inference-time verification, where a feedback model trained on this verified dataset dynamically detects ungrounded steps in real-time. Experimental results demonstrate that SAFE not only exposes the critical flaws of existing benchmarks at train-time, but also significantly outperforms standard baselines, achieving an average accuracy gain of 8.4 pp while guaranteeing verifiable trajectories at inference-time. 1 1 1 Code and verified benchmarks will be open-sourced upon acceptance.



1
Introduction

The frontier of Large Language Models (LLMs) has shifted towards complex reasoning, such as multi-hop question answering (QA), requiring models to synthesize evidence across multiple documents to derive a final answer. While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting has substantially improved performance on these tasks
(
22
)
, their reasoning traces are hard to ground or verify
20
. For example, intermediate steps may contain unverified entities, unsupported relations, or implicit shortcuts that nevertheless lead to the correct answer.

Our goal is to make reasoning traces verifiable and thus eliminate spurious reasoning, by shifting from free-form thought generation to rigorous traversal using Knowledge Graph (KG). Specifically, we formalize the reasoning process as a verifiable sequence of grounded entities, subject to the following strict requirements: (1) each step must be atomic, corresponding to a single valid KG triple; (2) all entities must be explicitly anchored in the provided context; and (3) every relation must be independently explainable and verifiable.

To rigorously enforce these grounding constraints on both train-time and inference-time, we build a dynamic benchmarking framework. For train-time verification, we execute two critical offline steps. First, we apply the above strict constraints to verify standard multi-hop benchmarks
(
5
;
25
;
18
)
, where we remove up to 14% of noisy instances. Second, we formalize an atomic error taxonomy to construct targeted supervision signals for training an external feedback model. At inference-time, this model dynamically verifies each atomic step of the multi-hop reasoning process, detecting and correcting any deviation from a valid KG path on-the-fly. Compared with LLM self-correction which is inherently error-prone
(
6
;
21
)
, this inference-time verification ensures strictly grounded reasoning trajectories.
Collectively, these train-time and inference-time phases constitute
SAFE
, a dynamic benchmarking framework.

Our main contributions are as follows:

•

We propose a train-time verification pipeline to filter noise from standard benchmarks, and define an error taxonomy to construct targeted training signals.

•

We develop an inference-time verification mechanism using a trained feedback model to detect and rectify reasoning deviations in real-time.

•

We introduce
SAFE
, a novel dynamic benchmarking framework that significantly outperforms baselines with an average +8.4 pp accuracy gain.

Related Works

2.1
Multi-hop QA Benchmarks and Spurious Reasoning

Multi-hop QA benchmarks such as 2WikiMultihopQA
(
5
)
, HotpotQA
(
25
)
, and MuSiQue
(
18
)
have been widely adopted for complex reasoning and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) evaluation
(
9
;
19
)
.
However,
7
report that a non-trivial portion of correct answers on standard multi-hop QA datasets are spurious.

To reduce spurious reasoning,
KG has been used before
(
3
)
and after generation
14
.
The former extracts the KG chain before generation and the latter evaluates CoT reasoning by mapping the generated steps to a KG.
Closest to our work is REVEAL
8
, a benchmark that assigns correctness labels to individual reasoning steps based on a specialized error taxonomy. Hop, Skip, and Overthink
(
23
)
is another post-hoc evaluation, curating failure modes to localize breakdowns.

Our distinction:
Unlike these post-hoc approaches that focus on error diagnosis after a full trajectory is produced,
SAFE
diagnoses such errors during generation at inference-time and prevents them at train-time.

2.2
Inference-Time Reasoning Feedback

LLMs can be prompted to critique and revise their own outputs
(
13
)
or accumulate reflective feedback across trials
(
16
)
. Approaches like Self-Refine
(
13
)
demonstrate the appeal of self-critique as a mechanism for improving output quality without requiring additional training or external models.

However, in the context of complex multi-hop reasoning, pure self-correction is unverifiable and fundamentally limited, as LLMs often inherit the same hallucinations and biases that caused the original error. Consequently, models struggle to self-correct reasoning flaws without external ground-truth signals
(
6
)
. This is further supported by
21
, who identify mistake finding as the primary bottleneck, showing that unguided models consistently fail to rectify their own logical missteps.

Our distinction:

SAFE
enhances self-correction by introducing a dedicated, externally trained feedback model. Grounded in a rigorously verified dataset and a comprehensive error taxonomy, our external evaluator validates each atomic reasoning step to enforce a strictly verifiable sequence of grounded entities, providing more reliable supervision towards grounded reasoning.

Method

SAFE
is structured into two phases: train-time and inference-time verification. During the train-time, we formalize the error taxonomy (Section
3.1
) and systematically verify standard benchmarks (Section
3.2
). For the inference-time, we introduce a dedicated feedback model, trained to dynamically verify reasoning trajectories (Section
3.3
).

We formally define a valid reasoning trajectory as a strictly verifiable sequence of grounded entities. Unlike conventional CoT, this sequence operates exclusively as a traversal over a localized Knowledge Graph (
\mathcal{G}
), extending derived entities through explicitly grounded triples (
e_{head},r,e_{tail}
). Every intermediate entity in the reasoning path serves as a verifiable anchor, effectively preventing logical hallucinations.

Toward this verification goal, we introduce a KG-grounded error taxonomy.
As Section
2
contrasted, REVEAL
8
is a similar post-hoc taxonomy without grounding, but we aim to provide a grounded verification per each entity. More specifically:

First, while REVEAL treats a reasoning step as a free-form sentence, we enforce strict atomic attribution, anchoring each step to exactly one evidence passage and one KG triple, which ensures strict grounding and enables precise step-level validation.

Second, whereas REVEAL’s post-hoc nature restricts it to static benchmarking, our taxonomy actively supports both training supervision and dynamic inference-time correction.

Specifically, we assign each atomic step exclusively to one error type from four mutually exclusive categories that we define: Procedural, Attribution, Logical, and Final Answer (Table
1
). While existing benchmarks focus too much on answer correctness, sentence-level attribution, and logical errors, our distinction is investigating atomic-level procedural integrity.

According to our error taxonomy, reasoning errors are evaluated sequentially: Procedural errors capture invalid KG traversal patterns such as loops or disconnected paths.
Once procedural validity is confirmed, Attribution errors detect missing or ungrounded evidence. Logical errors identify relational inferences unsupported by KG structure. Finally, Final Answer errors arise when the trajectory fails to reach the correct target node.
This taxonomy provides a principled foundation for step-level supervision and real-time trajectory correction, enabling reasoning processes that are not only interpretable but strictly verifiable.

3.2
KG-grounded Benchmark Verification

Having formalized the atomic operations and error taxonomy required for strict grounding, we must ensure that the evaluation environment is also verifiable. When applying our defined KG constraints to standard large-scale benchmarks, we observe pervasive noise such as missing relations, ambiguous entity mappings, and incomplete supporting facts with spurious correlations. Such noise inherently hinders the formation of a valid sequence of grounded entities (see Appendix Figure
6
and Table
6
). To systematically diagnose these deficiencies and guarantee that every evaluation instance possesses a grounded reasoning trajectory, we introduce a KG-grounded benchmark verification pipeline.
Throughout this pipeline, we utilize gpt-oss-120b
1
as our base LLM, selected for its state-of-the-art instruction-following capabilities and robust zero-shot relation extraction performance among open-weight models (exact prompts are detailed in Appendix
A.9
).
The pipeline actively enforces our atomic grounding constraints through the following steps:

1.

Triple Extraction:
We prompt the LLM to extract atomic triples (
e_{head},r,e_{tail}
) from the gold passages, conceptualizing the context as a localized knowledge graph.

2.

Iterative Gleaning:
To ensure no critical information is omitted, we perform up to two rounds of gleaning to capture valid triples that may have been overlooked during the initial extraction.

3.

Entity Resolution
: We identify and merge synonymous entities (e.g., “USA” and “United States”) to construct a cohesive and normalized knowledge graph.

4.

Logical Path Discovery:
We explicitly trace continuous relational paths within the localized KG to bridge the gap between the question entities and the ground truth answer.

5.

Validation:
An instance is systematically classified as noise and filtered out if no valid, continuous logical path can be established, confirming a fundamental break in the reasoning chain.

3.3
Inference-time Feedback Model

Defining Step-Level Feedback.

To enforce the strict grounding paradigm during inference, we train a dedicated step-level feedback model that verifies reasoning trajectories and produces corrective guidance when violations occur. Formally, we model feedback generation as a conditional prediction task over reasoning prefixes.
Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the passages provided as context, and let a reasoning trajectory
\tau
be represented as a sequence of atomic steps:

\tau=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{t})

where each step
s_{i}
corresponds to a KG operation (e.g., entity extraction or relational traversal) that must be explicitly grounded in
\mathcal{P}
. The feedback model
F
receives the question
q
, the passages
\mathcal{P}
, and the current reasoning prefix
s_{1:t}
to predict:

(y_{t},d_{t},g_{t})=F(q,\mathcal{P},s_{1:t})

where:

•

y_{t}
denotes the specific error type (e.g., Unsupported, Logical Fallacy) assigned from the taxonomy defined in Section
3.1
.

•

d_{t}
represents the natural language diagnosis, explicitly grounding why the step violates KG constraints based on
\mathcal{P}
.

•

g_{t}
provides actionable guidance, directing the generator on how to correct the trajectory and construct a valid continuous path.

This formulation transforms feedback generation into a rigorous verification problem, where the model learns to detect deviations from valid grounding.

Building Training Data.

Training feedback model requires both verified reasoning trajectories and diverse failure modes.
Using the verified environment (Section
3.2
), we construct 3.9K ideal trajectories strictly grounded to the passages
\mathcal{P}
.
To provide robust negative supervision, we then synthesize 7.6K negative examples through taxonomy-guided error injection. Starting from these valid trajectories, a high-capacity teacher model (gpt-oss-120b) systematically perturbs individual reasoning steps by introducing violations aligned with our error taxonomy. This yields corrupted trajectories where the precise error location and type are known, enabling explicit step-level supervision.

While synthetic perturbations provide controlled coverage of error types, they may not fully capture the inherent path-finding failures and subtle misgrounding behaviors specific to the target model during actual inference. To bridge this distribution gap, we adopt an iterative refinement strategy: we first train an initial feedback model on
the synthetic dataset, then conduct targeted failure analysis to identify recurring weaknesses (e.g., confusing lexically similar entities or failing at complex numerical comparisons). For each identified weakness, we leverage the teacher model to generate corrected step-level feedback labels, and reintegrate these hard cases into the training pool.
Through this cycle of
train

\rightarrow

diagnose

\rightarrow

augment
, 848 hard cases are added to the training pool. This progressively closes the distribution gap between synthetic supervision and real inference-time failures, ultimately yielding a final dataset of 11.5K instances and a verifier robust to diverse and subtle KG deviations.
We empirically validate the effectiveness of this refinement strategy in Section
5
, where it consistently improves performance over training with synthetic data alone.

The overall error statistics of this complete training set are illustrated in Appendix Figure
7
. Additional details, including the positional distribution of error steps and dataset statistics, are provided in Appendix
A.5
.

•

The model generates reasoning paths iteratively, producing a single atomic step per turn until reaching a final answer, without any verification.

•

We fine-tune the specialized feedback model using QLoRA
(
2
)
on the Qwen3-8B (non-thinking) architecture for 2 epochs with a learning rate of

and an effective batch size of 64. All experiments are conducted on 4

4.2
Main Results

Table
3
presents the results across the three structurally verified multi-hop QA benchmarks. Even without any external verification (
No Feedback
), simply decomposing the reasoning process into atomic steps based on our KG-grounded taxonomy already yields higher performance than TRACE. This suggests that formatting multi-hop reasoning as a sequence of atomic operations inherently provides a solid baseline for generation.

In contrast, the
Self-Feedback
baseline demonstrates significant instability and frequently results in performance degradation. When models attempt to verify their own grounding, the total average accuracy drops from 76.7% to 75.2%. This vulnerability is most evident in Qwen 2.5 14B, where self-feedback drastically reduces accuracy from 80.7% to 75.1%. This phenomenon highlights a critical limitation: without reliable, external KG-grounded signals for error localization, LLMs struggle to diagnose their own failures, often hallucinating corrections or reinforcing ungrounded reasoning trajectories.

When equipped with our specialized feedback model (
SAFE
), performance improves substantially across all datasets and model families. By enforcing strict grounding constraints in real time, our method achieves an average accuracy improvement of 8.4 pp (
\Delta
) over the best baselines, alongside a remarkable 11.2 pp surge in the rigorous EM metric (from 48.9% to 60.1%).
Notably,
SAFE
demonstrates exceptional robustness on MuSiQue—the most challenging dataset requiring longer relational paths—boosting average accuracy by 11.1 pp (from 63.9% to 75.0%) and EM by 9.3 pp. Furthermore, it successfully breaks the performance ceiling on already high-performing benchmarks, pushing the average accuracy on both 2WikiMultihopQA and HotpotQA beyond the 90% threshold.

Interestingly, despite variations in architecture and parameter scale, models tend to converge to a similar high-performance level (around 84%–85%) when augmented with
SAFE
. Overall, these findings demonstrate that enforcing strict grounding at the atomic step level, combined with our trained real-time feedback model, instantly corrects unverified trajectories, mitigates error propagation, narrows the performance gap across different LLMs, and provides a robust, model-agnostic strategy for verifiable multi-hop reasoning.

To empirically demonstrate the critical need for filtering unverifiable (flawed) data, we investigate its impact on the feedback model’s training phase. As shown in Table
4
, we compared our standard feedback model, trained on the strictly verified dataset (11.5K), against a variant augmented with an additional 5.3K instances initially flagged as noise by our KG pipeline. Notably, these noise instances were generated using the identical data generation pipeline and format. However, their inclusion consistently degraded performance across all five models, resulting in an average accuracy drop of 4.0%. This substantial degradation underscores that training on ungrounded data introduces contradictory signals, ultimately compromising the feedback model’s ability to act as a reliable logical guardrail.

To verify that our pipeline targets genuine unverifiable reasoning traces rather than merely difficult queries, we manually inspected 150 flagged instances (50 per benchmark). We observed a remarkably low false positive rate of 2% (Appendix Table

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced
SAFE
, a dynamic benchmarking framework that shifts multi-hop reasoning evaluation from static answer-matching to strictly verifiable grounding. During train-time, we formalized a rigorous error taxonomy and filtered ungrounded noise from standard benchmarks, providing a verified foundation for our feedback model. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that our inference-time verification yields substantial performance gains while fundamentally enhancing the faithfulness of reasoning trajectories. Ultimately, our work proves that strict step-by-step grounding is significantly more efficient and reliable than ungrounded self-correction, setting a new standard for trustworthy and explainable AI evaluation.

7
Limitations

While we utilized a Knowledge Graph (KG) to ensure the quality of our verification pipeline, we acknowledge the potential for mislabeling due to inherent noise or incomplete coverage in the KG. To address this, rather than aggressively excluding instances that might contain valuable reasoning trajectories, we provide the original dataset with our feedback labels as an augmented resource. This non-destructive approach allows future researchers to audit, refine, or leverage the full context of the data while accounting for potential label noise in automated diagnostics.

Furthermore, the efficacy of the SAFE framework inherently relies on the reasoning generator’s instruction-following capabilities. We observed instances where, despite the feedback model successfully producing accurate diagnoses and explicit corrective guidance, the reasoning generator failed to effectively incorporate these instructions into its subsequent generation step. Such instruction-following failures can lead to uncorrected errors or repeated mistakes within the iterative loop. This highlights a dependency on the base language model’s capacity to adhere to complex, stepwise constraints, suggesting that future work must address the alignment between corrective feedback and the generator’s execution.

Finally, our current experimental setting operates under the assumption that all necessary gold passages are present within the provided context. In practical open-domain multi-hop scenarios, initial retrievals may omit crucial evidence, necessitating dynamic or iterative retrieval steps. The primary scope of this study, however, is deliberately focused on isolating and evaluating the model’s reasoning capabilities—specifically, how accurately it can synthesize and navigate multi-hop logic given that the required information is already available. Extending the SAFE framework to actively trigger external information retrieval when the feedback model diagnoses a missing premise remains a promising direction for future work.

Ethics Statement

This work introduces
SAFE
, a framework designed to enhance the reliability of large language models (LLMs) in multi-hop reasoning tasks through stepwise atomic feedback and error-correction. We acknowledge several ethical considerations and potential broader impacts associated with its development and deployment.

Transparency and Interpretability

A persistent ethical challenge with LLMs is their "black-box" nature, which obscures the rationale behind generated answers.
SAFE
directly addresses this opacity by decomposing the complex multi-hop reasoning process into a fine-grained, stepwise atomic framework. This design transforms an otherwise opaque generation process into an inherently explainable system. By isolating each reasoning step and its corresponding feedback, researchers and practitioners can rigorously analyze the exact distribution of reasoning failures. This granular transparency not only facilitates precise, targeted error-correction but also promotes essential trust, accountability, and auditing capabilities in AI decision-making.

Computational Efficiency and Environmental Impact

Iterative reasoning and error-correction frameworks traditionally incur significant computational overhead due to repeated generation steps. However,
SAFE
explicitly mitigates this issue by employing efficient KV caching mechanisms, rendering the additional inference-time cost negligible compared to standard single-pass generation. By achieving robust multi-hop reasoning without heavily inflating computational demands, our approach aligns with the growing ethical need for sustainable and energy-efficient AI.

Bias and Fairness

The performance and behavior of
SAFE
inherently depend on the underlying pre-trained LLMs and the external knowledge corpora (e.g., retrieved Wikipedia passages) used for reasoning. These base models and datasets may contain historical, cultural, or demographic biases. While our error-correction taxonomy meticulously targets procedural, logical, and attribution errors, it is not explicitly designed to detect or mitigate social biases. Users must remain cautious, as the system might inadvertently reproduce or amplify existing biases present in the source material.

AI Usage

AI-based tools were used for grammar correction and language refinement. The authors take full responsibility for all scientific content, including the methodology, experiments, and conclusions.

A.4
Evaluation Dataset Preprocessing Details

For our evaluation, we standardize the context length across all benchmarks to exactly 10 passages per question. For 2WikiMultihopQA and HotpotQA, we directly utilize the 10 passages provided in their standard distractor settings. Since MuSiQue originally provides 20 passages per instance, we retain all gold supporting passages and randomly sample from the remaining distractors to strictly maintain the 10-passage constraint. Finally, to prevent language models from exploiting positional shortcuts, the sequence of the provided passages is randomly shuffled for every instance across all three datasets.

KV Caching

A primary concern with iterative inference-time feedback mechanisms is the potential for a substantial escalation in computational cost and latency. In multi-hop QA, the vast majority of the computational burden arises from the initial prefill stage, which involves processing lengthy system instructions, retrieved documents, and the original query.

As shown in Table
8
, our framework effectively mitigates this overhead through Prefix KV caching
10
. To minimize redundant computation, both the reasoning generator and the feedback model independently cache the representations of these static prefixes. Crucially, as the stepwise generation progresses, previously verified reasoning steps are iteratively appended to this cached context. Consequently, subsequent reasoning steps and targeted feedback loops only incur the marginal cost of computing attention for the newly generated tokens. Empirical results, aggregated from a total of 3,000 samples across three benchmarks, demonstrate an impressive overall prefill token saving ratio of 89.0%, confirming the high efficiency of our approach.

Reasoning Step Generation Prompt

You are a meticulous, step-by-step logical reasoner. Your task is to solve a complex question by generating **ONLY THE NEXT SINGLE, ATOMIC STEP** in a chain of thought.

## Core Task Definition

You must analyze the ‘Question‘, ‘Retrieved Passages‘, and ‘Previous Reasoning Steps‘.
Most importantly, you must analyze the ‘Feedback‘ received on the last step, which consists of three parts:

1. **Error Type**: The category of the error.

2. **Diagnosis**: An explanation about the latest previous step.

3. **Guidance**: Specific instruction on what to do in this current step. (e.g. Fixing previous step’s error, Proceeding to extract new information, or Making the final logical conclusion).

You must prioritize the **Guidance**. It tells you exactly what action to take now.

—

## Step Classifications

Every step must be strictly classified into one of three types.
Attribution and Logical actions cannot be mixed in a single step.

### 1. Attribution Step

- **Definition**: Extracts **ONE** explicit fact from a **SINGLE** retrieved passage.

- **Requirement**: You MUST explicitly cite the source (e.g., "According to Passage X…").

- **Constraint**: Do **NOT** combine information from multiple passages (e.g., "Passage 1 says X and Passage 2 says Y").

- **Format suffix**: End the sentence with ‘(Attribution)‘.

### 2. Logical Step
- **Definition**: Performs **ONE** logical operation (comparison, calculation, or inference) based **ONLY** on ‘Previous Reasoning Steps‘.

- **Requirement**: Do NOT look up new information from passages.

- **Constraint**: This step is for **Intermediate Reasoning** only.

- You must NOT output the final answer marker here.

- Even if you derived the answer mentally, just state the conclusion of the logic (e.g., "A is older than B").

- **Format suffix**: End the sentence with ‘(Logical)‘.

### 3. Final Answer Step

- **Definition**: Submits the final answer.

- **Strict Syntax Rule**: This step must ONLY generate the final answer following the format: ‘####ANSWER: <answer_value>‘.

- **Constraint**: Do not write "Therefore…", or "The answer is…". Just the marker and value.

- **Format suffix**: End with ‘(Final Answer)‘.

—

## Strict Formatting Rules

1. **Numbering**: Start your response with ‘Step K:‘, where ‘K‘ is the next integer after the last step number.

2. **Atomic Nature**: Adhere strictly to the "One Step = One Action" rule defined above.

3. **Suffix Mandatory**: Every step must end with one of ‘(Attribution)‘, ‘(Logical)‘, ‘(Final Answer)‘.

4. **Final Answer Pattern**: The pattern for the final step is immutable: ‘Step K: ####ANSWER: <answer_value> (Final Answer)‘

—

Reasoning Step Evaluation Prompt 1

# Role

You are a Precision Reasoning Evaluator. Your goal is to critically assess a specific reasoning step (Step to evaluate) within the context of a multi-hop QA task. You must verify if the step is logically sound, factually grounded in the provided ‘Retrieved Passages‘, and efficiently moves towards the answer.

# Input Data Context

- **Question**: The main query to answer.

- **Retrieved Passages**: The only source of truth. External knowledge is strictly forbidden.

- **Previous Steps**: The chain of thought leading up to the Step to evaluate.

- **Step to evaluate**: The specific step you need to evaluate. It must end with one of these tags:

- ‘(Attribution)‘: Extracting facts directly from a passage.

- ‘(Logical)‘: Intermediate reasoning (comparing, calculating) without the final answer marker.

- ‘(Final Answer)‘: Strict submission of the final answer ONLY (Format: ‘####ANSWER: <answer_value>‘).

# Task

1. Compare the ‘Step to evaluate‘ against the ‘Retrieved Passages‘ and ‘Previous Steps‘.

2. Choose one ‘error_type‘ from the given categories based on the Evaluation Protocol.

3. Generate a structured evaluation with ‘diagnosis‘ and ‘guidance‘.

# Feedback Guidelines

You must follow this Evaluation Protocol sequentially to determine the ‘error_type‘.

## Phase 1: Assess (Final Answer) Steps

**Condition**: If the ‘Step to evaluate‘ is tagged as ‘(Final Answer)‘.

- **Check 1 (Consistency)**: Does the submitted answer value match the conclusion derived from the preceding steps?

- If NO -> error_type: Wrong Conclusion

- **Check 2 (Correctness)**: Are sufficiency and consistency met?

- If YES -> error_type: Correct (No Error)

## Phase 2: Assess Utility & Progress (For Attribution/Logical Steps)

**Condition**: If the ‘Step to evaluate‘ is ‘(Attribution)‘ or ‘(Logical)‘.

- **Check 1 (Necessity)**: Can the final answer be fully derived only from previous steps?

- If YES -> error_type: Overthinking

- **Check 2 (Relevance)**: Is this step deals with necessary information to answer the question?

- If NO (e.g., deriving true but useless facts, focusing on wrong entities) -> error_type: Off-topic

- **Check 3 (Novelty)**: Does this step provide new meaningful information or deduction not present in previous steps?

- If NO -> error_type: Redundancy

- **Check 4 (Efficiency)**: Does this step actually perform a meaningful action (extraction/deduction)?

- If NO (e.g., purely planning, stating "I will now…", or summarizing without progress) -> error_type: Inefficiency

## Phase 3: Assess Validity & Soundness (For Attribution/Logical Steps)

**Condition**: If the step passes Phase 2 (it is useful and relevant), now check its truthfulness.

**[If Attribution Step]**

- **Check 1 (Consistency)**: Does it contradict the Passage?

- If YES -> error_type: Contradictory

- **Check 2 (Grounding)**: Is the fact explicitly present in the referenced Passage?

- If NO (Hallucination) -> error_type: Unsupported

- **Check 3 (Completeness)**: Does it claim information is missing when the Passage actually has it?

- If YES -> error_type: Information Miss

- **Check 4 (Ordering)**: Does this step extract an attribute (e.g., nationality, birth date) of an entity before establishing the necessary relationship (e.g., "is the director of…") that connects this entity to the question’s subject?

- If YES -> error_type: Premature Attribution

**[If Logical Step]**

- **Check 1 (Soundness)**: Is the calculation, comparison, or inference logically valid?

- If NO -> error_type: Logical Fallacy

## Priority Rules

This protocol is hierarchical. You must stop at the first error type with highest priority.

1. **Phase 1 (Final Answer Checks)** take precedence over everything else for ‘(Final Answer)‘ steps.

2. **Phase 2 (Utility Checks)** take precedence over Phase 3.

- If a step is useless (e.g., Redundant, Off-topic, Overthinking, Inefficiency), it is an error regardless of whether it is factually true or false.

- Do NOT check for Hallucinations (Phase 3) if the step has already failed a Utility Check (Phase 2).

- Report ONLY the first error encountered.

# Output Generation Instructions

After determining the ‘error_type‘ using the Evaluation Protocol (Phase 1-3), you must generate the ‘diagnosis‘ and ‘guidance‘ fields following these rules.

## 1. How to Write "Diagnosis"

The diagnosis must be a self-contained explanation of *why* the specific ‘error_type‘ was chosen.

NO Protocol References: DO NOT explicitly mention "Phase 1", "Phase 2", "Check 1", etc. The protocol is for your internal reasoning only. In the output, describe the content issue directly.

Important: The guidance must focus ONLY on the single, atomic next action. Do not provide a long-term plan or list multiple future steps (e.g., "Do A, then B, then C"). Just tell the model to do "A".

If your guidance instruct to generate the final answer step, your guidance must say to include the exact format required: ‘####ANSWER: <answer_value>‘.

LLM Judge Prompt

You are an expert evaluator for a Question Answering task.

Your goal is to determine if the ’Generated Answer’ is correct based on the ’Ground Truth List’.

**Evaluation Criteria:**

1. **Semantic Equivalence:** If the ’Generated Answer’ refers to the same real-world entity, concept, or event as **ANY** item in the ’Ground Truth List’, it is "correct". This includes aliases, abbreviations, and common synonyms.

2. **Granularity & Hierarchy:** Accept answers that are factually accurate but differ in specificity or granularity, provided they refer to the same location or entity.

- **Geographic Inclusion:** Accept constituent countries, states, or specific locations if they are part of the broader Ground Truth entity (e.g., "England" is correct for "United Kingdom"; "New York" is correct for "USA" if the context implies origin).

- **Specificity:** Accept broader correct terms if they encompass the specific Ground Truth (e.g., "UK" is correct for "England" if the question asks for country).

3. **Logical Entailment & Event Description:** Accept answers that describe the same event or fact using different but factually compatible attributes.

- **Cause vs. Nature:** If Ground Truth specifies the mechanism (e.g., "shot") and Generated Answer specifies the nature of the event (e.g., "homicide" or "murder"), and both describe the same factual occurrence, it is "correct".

- **Implication:** If the Generated Answer logically implies the Ground Truth or vice versa in the given context (e.g., "shot by father" implies "killed by family member"), it is "correct".

4. **Robustness:** Ignore minor formatting, casing, punctuation, or conversational fillers (e.g., "The answer is…").

5. **Contextual Correctness:** If the answer is factually different, references a completely distinct entity, or introduces contradictory information compared to the ground truth (e.g., "died of old age" vs "shot"), it is "wrong".

**Output Format:**

You must output ONLY a valid JSON object with exactly two keys: "is_correct" and "reasoning". Do not include any markdown styling or extra text.

- "is_correct": Must be either "correct" or "wrong".

- "reasoning": A brief, high-density explanation of why the answer was judged this way, specifically mentioning any logical entailment or hierarchy logic if applicable.

Triple Extraction Prompt

You are an expert Knowledge Graph Engineer and Computational Linguist. Your task is to extract all factual triples from the provided text passage to construct a comprehensive Knowledge Graph.

### CRITICAL INSTRUCTION: COREFERENCE RESOLUTION (DECONTEXTUALIZATION)

Before extracting any triples, you must strictly perform **Coreference Resolution** internally.

- **Never use pronouns** (e.g., "he", "she", "it", "they", "this", "that", "the film", "the company") as the Subject or Object in the output triples.

- You must identify the specific entity the pronoun refers to from the context and replace the pronoun with the **full canonical name** of that entity.

- Example: If the text says "Barack Obama: Barack Obama was born in Hawaii. He served as the 44th president.", the extracted triple must be ‘["Barack Obama", "served_as", "44th president"]‘, NOT ‘["He", "served_as", "44th president"]‘.

### EXTRACTION GUIDELINES

1. **Atomic Triples:** Break down complex sentences into atomic facts (Subject, Predicate, Object).

2. **Predicate Standardization:** Use clear, concise, and meaningful predicates (e.g., "born_in", "directed_by", "spouse", "occupation"). Avoid vague verbs like "is" or "has" if a more specific relation exists.

3. **Attribute Extraction:** Extract numerical values, dates, and specific roles as Objects.

- Dates should be in ‘YYYY-MM-DD‘ format if possible.

- Numbers should be kept as raw values suitable for comparison.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Output **ONLY** a standard JSON list of lists. Do not include any explanation or markdown formatting outside the JSON.

Triple Gleaning Prompt

You are a meticulous Knowledge Graph Auditor and QA Specialist.
Your goal is to review the extraction results from a previous step and identify **MISSING** factual triples that were overlooked.

### INPUTS

You will be provided with:

1. **Original Text Passage**: The source text.

2. **Existing Triples**: A list of triples already extracted from the text.

### TASK

Compare the **Original Text** against the **Existing Triples**. Extract **NEW** factual triples that are present in the text but **NOT** in the Existing Triples list.

### CRITICAL RULES

1. **NO DUPLICATES**: Do not output any triple that is semantically identical to one in the "Existing Triples" list.

2. **Focus on Detail**: Look specifically for:
- Secondary entities mentioned in the text (not just the main subject).

- Specific dates, numbers, or measurements previously missed.

- Relationships between secondary entities.

- Adjectives or roles acting as attributes (e.g., "Polish-French" -> nationality).

3. **Coreference Resolution (Mandatory)**: Just like the first step, you MUST resolve pronouns (he, she, it, they) to their full canonical entity names. **Never** use pronouns in your output.

Entity Resolution Prompt

You are an expert Knowledge Graph Engineer specializing in Entity Resolution (Coreference Resolution).
Your task is to identify and group entities that refer to the **same real-world object** (Named Entities) from the provided list of entities and context triples.

### INSTRUCTIONS

1. **Analyze Entities:** Review the provided list of entities.

2. **Check Context:** Use the "Context Triples" to verify if similar names refer to the exact same unique entity.

3. **Group Synonyms:** Group variations (abbreviations, acronyms, partial names, typos) of the same **Specific Named Entity** into a single list.

4. **Filter Noise:** **STRICTLY EXCLUDE** generic terms, common nouns, numbers, dates, and abstract concepts.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Output **ONLY** a JSON list of lists. Each inner list represents a group of synonymous entities.

Example: ‘[["USA", "United States", "U.S."], ["JFK", "John F. Kennedy"], ["The Green Album", "Green"]]‘

### RULES

1. **Target Proper Nouns Only:** Only group Specific People, Places, Organizations, Events, and Works.

2. **Exclude Generics:** Do NOT include or group common nouns or roles.

- **Bad:** "president", "mother", "clothes", "the album", "the band", "different stage names", "two sons".

- **Good:** "President Obama", "Mother Teresa", "The White Album", "System 7".

3. **Exclude Literals:** Do NOT include or group Values.

- **Dates:** "2020-01-01", "1990s", "May 5th".

- **Numbers:** "3", "100", "first", "one".

- **Measurements:** "3 years", "10kg", "50%".

4. **Avoid Super Nodes:** Never group a specific entity with a generic category (e.g., DO NOT group ‘["Barack Obama", "President"]‘). "President" is a title/role, not the person’s unique identifier.

5. **Context is King:** If "Jordan" appears in a triple about basketball and another about the Middle East, DO NOT group them.

Logical Path Discovery Prompt

You are an expert Knowledge Graph Reasoner.
Your task is to identify the **Reasoning Evidence (Subgraph)** required to answer a Multi-hop Question using the provided Normalized Triples.

### GOAL

Select the **minimal set of triples** that acts as the necessary premises to logically derive the **Ground Truth Answer**.

### UNDERSTANDING REASONING PATTERNS

The "Reasoning Path" is NOT always a single connected chain. It can be:

1. **Sequential (Bridge):** (e1, r1, e2) -> (e2, r2, e3)

* *Ex:* "When is the birthplace of the director of the movie Parasite?"

2. **Parallel (Comparison/Intersection):** (e1, r1, v1) and (e2, r1, v2)

* *Ex:* "Are Obama and Trump born in the same country?" (Requires birthplaces of BOTH persons, even if they are disjoint nodes).

* *Ex:* "Who is older, A and B?" (Requires birthdates of BOTH persons).

### INSTRUCTIONS

1. **Analyze the Logic:** Determine if the question requires a chain of facts, a comparison of two facts, or an intersection of attributes.

2. **Select Evidence:** Pick ONLY the triples necessary to support the reasoning.

* **Ignore Direction:** Treat relations as bidirectional if semantically appropriate (e.g., ‘["Movie", "directed_by", "Director"]‘ supports finding the director).

* **Semantic Match:** Match predicates loosely (e.g., "born_in" is equal to "place of birth").

3. **Verify & Explain:**

* Do these selected triples logically lead to the Ground Truth Answer?

* If the required triples are missing (e.g., one side of a comparison is missing), mark as invalid.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Output **ONLY** a JSON object:

{

"is_valid": true, // true if ALL necessary evidence is present to derive the Ground Truth Answer

"reasoning_path": [ // List of selected triples. Can be disjoint (e.g., two separate birth dates).
["Subject1", "Predicate1", "Object1"],
["Subject2", "Predicate2", "Object2"],
…
],

"explanation": "Briefly explain the logic (e.g., ’Found birth dates for both persons to compare them’)."

}
