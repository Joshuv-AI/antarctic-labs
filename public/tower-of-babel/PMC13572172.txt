Dietary mangosteen polyphenols and rosmarinic acid supplementation improves production performance and alleviates oviduct inflammation in LPS challenged laying hens
Authors: Yalu Zhou, Meng Wang, Xuemei Ding, Qiufeng Zeng, Shiping Bai, Xiangbing Mao, Gang Jia, Qian Huang, Yan Liu, Jianping Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to investigate the effects of dietary plant polyphenols on laying performance, egg quality and oviduct inflammatory responses in laying hens challenged with LPS. A total of 192 Hy-Line Gray hens (55 weeks old) were randomly assigned to two dietary treatments for 8 weeks: a control group (CON, basal diet) and a plant polyphenols group (PP, 200 mg/kg mangosteen polyphenols + 200 mg/kg rosmarinic acid), with 8 replicates of 12 birds each. After 8 weeks, sixteen hens per group were selected and allocated to lipopolysaccharide (LPS) challenged and no challenged groups, forming a 2 × 2 factorial arrangement with four treatments and 4 replicates of 2 birds each. During the feeding phase, PP supplementation significantly reduced feed conversion ratio (FCR) and serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity (P < 0.05). During the challenge phase, there were significantly increased serum concentrations of the pro-inflammatory cytokines interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), along with markedly elevated levels of IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α and interferon-γ (IFN-γ) in the magnum, isthmus and uterus (P < 0.05). However, the concentrations of the anti-inflammatory cytokines interleukin-4 (IL-4) and interleukin-10 (IL-10) in these tissues were significantly decreased (P < 0.05). Notably, dietary PP supplementation during the challenge phase significantly alleviated LPS-induced oviduct inflammation, as evidenced by decreased pro-inflammatory cytokine levels and increased anti-inflammatory cytokine levels in the oviduct (P < 0.05). Furthermore, significant interaction effects between LPS challenge and dietary PP supplementation were observed for inflammatory cytokines and glutathione (GSH) activity in the oviduct (P < 0.05). In summary, dietary PP supplementation improved production performance and alleviated oviduct inflammation in laying hens, potentially by modulating immune responses. These findings suggest that PP has potential as a functional feed additive for enhancing production and reproductive health.

Introduction

To improve efficiency in the poultry industry, extending the laying cycle of high-producing hens has become essential. However, aging in laying hens is associated with declines in both laying rate and egg quality. This deterioration is primarily driven by structural and functional declines across multiple organs (including the intestine, oviduct and ovary), along with chronic inflammation and metabolic dysregulation (Bain et al., 2016; Hou, et al., 2024; Ketut, et al., 2024; Rusli, et al., 2015). In particular, inflammation of the oviduct is a common histopathological condition that impairs egg production and eggshell quality, often manifesting as thin, soft, or misshapen shells. Consequently, this condition poses a major threat to flock health and causes substantial economic losses within the industry (Landman, et al., 2013; Poulsen, et al., 2020; Wang, et al., 2020; Wen, et al., 2021). Crucially, accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and subsequent oxidative stress are recognized as upstream drivers of age-related oviductal involution (Liu, et al., 2022a). As hens age, the declining antioxidant capacity of the oviduct leads to excessive ROS accumulation, which directly damages mitochondrial function and cellular integrity within the magnum and uterus (Chen, et al., 2024). This persistent oxidative damage not only triggers mucosal epithelial cell apoptosis but also activates pro-inflammatory signaling pathways, thereby aggravating salpingitis and disrupting the calcium deposition required for eggshell formation (Feng, et al., 2023). Accordingly, the identification of effective nutritional interventions capable of mitigating oviduct dysfunction during the late laying period and reducing inflammatory incidence has become a critical priority for ensuring the sustainable advancement of the poultry industry.

Plant polyphenols are structurally diverse bioactive compounds with well-documented anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antioxidant and antitumor properties (Chen, et al., 2018; Rahman, et al., 2021; Wani, et al., 2023). Rosmarinic acid, a caffeic acid ester (C₁₈H₁₆O₈) widely found in Lamiaceae plants, exhibits amphiphilic properties, enabling both membrane interaction and aqueous solubility, which underlie its potent antimicrobial activity (Nieto, et al., 2018). Mangosteen polyphenols are concentrated in the pericarp of Garcinia mangostana and comprise over 100 characterized compounds, among with oxygenated xanthones are considered the most bioactive. Other phenolic subclasses, such as proanthocyanidins and anthocyanins, also contribute to antioxidant capacity and biological versatility (Ovalle-Magallanes, et al., 2017). Layer diets enriched with rosemary leaf powder (RML) improve feed conversion ratio (FCR), egg production and eggshell quality, with carnosic and rosmarinic acids contributing to increased eggshell weight and thickness (Abo Ghanima, et al., 2020; Alagawany and El-Hack, 2015; Gerzilov, et al., 2015; Kedir, et al., 2023; Torki, et al., 2018). In broilers, rosemary extracts or essential oil blends promote body weight gain and meat quality (Cetin, et al., 2016; Liu, et al., 2022b). Mangosteen extracts have also been reported to improve egg quality and poultry health, with α-mangostin enhancing Haugh unit, reducing egg deformities and prolonging egg shelf life (Ketut, et al., 2024; Zhu, et al., 2024). Additionally, mangosteen peel extract exhibits hepatoprotective effects, mitigating liver oxidative damage, regulating ALT and AST activity, and supporting lipid metabolism (Huang, et al., 2025; Widjastuti, et al., 2020). While the anti‑inflammatory effects of polyphenols are well documented in mouse models (Lin, et al., 2017; Nava Catorce, et al., 2016), fewer studies have been reported in poultry. In particular, the effects of combined mangosteen polyphenols and rosmarinic acid on production performance and egg quality during peak laying periods is yet to be fully explored.

During the natural aging process, the function of the oviduct declines and is closely related to long-term, low-grade chronic inflammation. To systematically evaluate the anti-inflammatory effects of plant polyphenols and clarify their mechanism of action, researchers usually adopt acute inflammation models to obtain controllable and repeatable experimental conditions. To systematically investigate the pathological mechanisms of oviduct inflammation and evaluate the efficacy of green nutritional interventions, the lipopolysaccharide (LPS) -induced immune stress model has been widely adopted in poultry research (Li, et al., 2023). LPS, a major glycolipid component of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria, serves as a potent endotoxin that triggers robust acute inflammatory responses and oxidative stress via the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway (Jing, et al., 2024; Wang, et al., 2026). In laying hens, LPS challenge effectively simulates clinical bacterial salpingitis, leading to histopathological damage to the oviduct mucosa, upregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α), and disruption of tissue integrity (Li, et al., 2023; Wang, et al., 2026). This immune hyperactivation shifts nutrient partition away from production, culminating in a severe decline in egg production and shell quality (Li, et al., 2023). Utilizing the LPS challenged laying hen model provides a reliable, controllable, and non-infectious experimental platform to elucidate whether and how dietary mangosteen polyphenols and rosmarinic acid can mitigate oviduct inflammation and restore production performance. Therefore, in this study, we added a compound PP to the diet of laying hens to assess its impact on egg production performance. Furthermore, we used an LPS acute challenge model as a research tool to simulate inflammatory damage and determine the anti-inflammatory potential of PP, in order to provide theoretical basis and mechanism clues for further exploration of PP in alleviating natural aging-related tubal dysfunction.

Materials and methods

The composition of the plant polyphenols

The plant polyphenols (PP, mangosteen polyphenols and rosmarinic acid) used in the experiment was formulated by combining mangosteen-derived polyphenols and rosmarinic acid at a 1:1 mass ratio. The purity of the mangosteen polyphenol preparation is 50%, while the purity of the rosmarinic acid preparation is 20%, with starch and dextrin serving as the carrier, the total phenols in PP were ultimately 140mg/kg. The PP was generously donated by Hunan Agricultural University.

Establishment of the LPS model

One person restrained the laying hen in an upright position with the cloaca facing upward. The oviduct opening was exposed by pressing on both sides of the cloaca with hands. Another person then injected 2 mL of LPS solution into the uterine part of the oviduct. After injection, the hen was held upside down for 5 minutes before being returned to the cage. The injection volume per hen was 2 mL. LPS was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Corporation.

Experimental animals and design

The experiment comprised a feeding phase followed by a challenge phase, with a total duration of 60 days. The feeding phase adopted a single-factor design. A total of 192 healthy 55-week-old Hy-Line Gray laying hens were randomly allocated to two dietary treatments, each consisting of eight replicates with 12 hens per replicate. Birds received either a basal diet (CON) or the basal diet supplemented with 200 mg/kg mangosteen polyphenols + 200 mg/kg rosmarinic acid (PP) for 8 weeks. The subsequent challenge phase employed a 2 × 2 factorial arrangement with two factors: LPS challenge (PBS infusion or LPS infusion) and PP supplementation (with or without PP). At the end of the feeding phase, 16 healthy hens were selected from both the CON and PP groups (two hens per replicate). Selection criteria included normal behavior, a laying rate at or above the average for the corresponding period according to the Hy-Line Gray Management Guide, a clean vent without exudate, and eggs with clean, smooth shells free of stains or blood. The selected hens were randomly reassigned within each original dietary group to two subgroups, resulting in four treatments: CON, PP, LPS and LPS + PP. After 48 h acclimation period, hens in the challenge groups were administered a single infusion of LPS solution, whereas hens in the no challenged groups received an equivalent volume of PBS.

The experiment was conducted at the Teaching and Research Experimental Base of the Institute of Animal Nutrition, Sichuan Agricultural University. All birds were maintained under standardized husbandry conditions, the laying hens live in a cage that measures 45 × 45 × 42 cm3. Housing facilities were cleaned regularly and disinfected weekly. Ambient temperature was controlled at 20-22°C, with relative humidity maintained between 50% and 65%. A lighting schedule of 16 h light and 8 h dark (16L:8D) was applied throughout the experimental period. To reduce potential environmental confounding, birds from each treatment group were evenly allocated within the poultry house. Hens had ad libitum access to feed and water, and feed was provided twice daily at 09:00 and 15:00. The experimental diets were formulated to meet or exceed the nutrient requirements recommended by the NRC (1994) (Table S1). All experimental procedures were approved and conducted in accordance with the Guidelines for the Breeding and Use of Laboratory Animals issued by Sichuan Agricultural University (Chengdu, China; No. 20221116004).

Data and sample collection

In the whole experiment, egg production and egg weight were recorded daily, feed intake was recorded weekly on a replicate basis. And laying rate, average daily feed intake (ADFI), FCR and egg mass were calculated on the basis of average. In this study, feed conversation ratio was determined as the ratio of total feed intake (g) to total egg weight (g), egg mass was expressed as the laying rate (%) multiplied by the average weight of eggs (g).

On days 28 and 56, four eggs per replicate were randomly collected for assessment of egg quality. On the 56th and 60th days of the experiment, one laying hen was randomly selected from each replicate for blood sampling from the jugular vein. The samples were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 15 min to isolate serum. Serum samples were stored at −20°C for subsequent determination of biochemical parameters, antioxidant indices and inflammatory cytokines. Following euthanasia and dissection, the mid-portions of the magnum, isthmus and uterus were excised, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C pending further analysis.

Egg quality measurement

On the days of 28 and 56 of the experiment, 4 eggs were randomly selected from each replicate group (32 eggs per group), Eggshell strength was determined using an electronic eggshell strength tester (ETG-1601A, Japan). Albumen height and Haugh unit values were assessed with an automated egg quality analyzer (EMT-5200, Japan). Eggshell thickness was measured with a digital micrometer, calculated as the mean of measurements taken at three locations: the equatorial region, blunt end, and sharp end.

Serum biochemical indices

Serum biochemical parameters were analyzed using an automatic biochemistry analyzer to determine the activities of ALT, AST and ALP.

Serum and oviduct antioxidant parameters

The concentrations of reduced GSH in serum and oviduct tissues (magnum, isthmus and uterus) were determined using commercial assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China) according to the manufacturer's protocols.

Serum and oviduct concentrations of inflammatory cytokine

The concentrations of IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-4, IL-10 and IFN-γ in serum and oviduct tissues (magnum, isthmus and uterus) were measured using commercial ELISA kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China) following the manufacturer's instructions.

Oviduct inflammatory factor related mRNA expression

The mRNA expression of inflammation-related genes (TLR-4, NF-κB and MyD88) in the magnum, isthmus and uterus segments of the oviduct was measured using real-time quantitative PCR. The RNA extraction kits, reverse transcription kits, and real-time fluorescence quantitative reagents used in the experiment were all purchased from Takara Company, Dalian, China. β-actin served as the internal reference gene. Gene and primer sequences are presented in Table S2.

Histopathological analysis and scores of the oviductlevel

After dissecting the laying hens, the isthmus, magnum, and uterus sections of the oviduct were carefully removed intact. Approximately 1 cm³ of tissue from each of these regions-the isthmus, magnum, and middle part of the uterus-was collected and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, then sent to Beijing Dakomei Technology Co., Ltd. for analysis. Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining was performed to observe the morphology of the oviduct sections. The main steps included deparaffinization of paraffin-embedded sections to water, followed by hematoxylin staining, eosin staining, dehydration, and mounting. Finally, the samples were examined under an upright optical microscope, and images were captured and analyzed using an imaging system. All equipment used was from Nikon, Japan. Histopathological Scoring Criteria for oviduct Sections: The mucosal epithelium is regularly arranged, with no evidence of degeneration, necrosis, or vacuolar changes. The lamina propria shows no inflammatory reaction (Score 0). The mucosal epithelium exhibits disorganized arrangement, with epithelial cell degeneration, necrosis, and vacuolar changes. The lamina propria displays mild inflammatory infiltration (Score 1). The mucosal epithelium is disordered, with epithelial cell degeneration, necrosis, and vacuolar changes. The lamina propria exhibits moderate inflammatory infiltration (Score 2). The mucosal epithelium shows marked disorganization, accompanied by epithelial cell degeneration, necrosis, and vacuolar changes. The lamina propria presents with pronounced inflammatory infiltration (Score 3). The mucosal epithelium is severely disorganized, with extensive epithelial cell degeneration, necrosis, and vacuolar changes. The lamina propria demonstrates intense inflammatory infiltration (Score 4). The pathological scoring criteria were provided by Beijing Dakomai Technology Co., Ltd.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SAS software (version 9.4). The feeding phase data were subjected to one-way ANOVA, with group comparisons performed by Student's t-test. The challenge phase data were analyzed using two-way ANOVA, with the model including the main effects of LPS challenge, dietary PP supplementation and their interaction. All data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05, and a tendency was considered at 0.05 < P < 0.1.

Oviduct histopathological analysis and scores in laying hens under LPS challenge

In the CON and PP groups, the magnum mucosa formed prominent folds projecting into the lumen. The tissue architecture remained intact, with densely arranged cells in the lamina propria and no evidence of inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig. 2A). In contrast, the LPS group exhibited marked histopathological alterations. Compared with the CON group, the lamina propria showed loosely arranged cells with a significant reduction in cell density. Extensive mucosal fissures were observed, accompanied by evident structural disruption (Fig. 2A). Relative to the LPS group, the LPS + PP group demonstrated better preservation of mucosal architecture. The mucosal layer remained largely intact, with more compact cellular organization in the lamina propria and a partial restoration of cell numbers. In the CON and PP groups, the isthmus mucosa displayed an intact histological architecture. Cells within the lamina propria were densely arranged, and no structural abnormalities were observed (Fig. 2C). Compared with the CON group, the numerous mucosal folds in the LPS group showed extensive disrupted or fragmented, the lamina propria showed markedly sparse cellular distribution with a sharp decline in cell numbers, indicating severe destruction of the isthmus mucosal architecture (Fig. 2C). In contrast, the LPS + PP group showed substantial attenuation of these pathological alterations. The extent of fold disruption was markedly reduced, and the lamina propria demonstrated increased cellular density and partial recovery of cell numbers. Although some degree of structural damage persisted, the overall integrity of the mucosal architecture was considerably improved (Fig. 2C).

The endometrial tissue of the uterus region in both the CON and PP groups exhibited well-preserved morphology, with densely arranged cells in the lamina propria and no observable structural damage (Fig. 2E). In contrast, the endometrium of the LPS group displayed altered fold morphology, a markedly sparse arrangement of lamina propria cells, and a drastic reduction in cell number, indicating severe disruption of the uterus mucosal architecture (Fig. 2E). Compared with the LPS group, the endometrial folds in the LPS + PP group were partially restored, with increased cell density and number in the lamina propria, reflecting a mitigation of the structural damage to the uterus mucosa (Fig. 2E).

The effect of dietary PP supplementation on the oviduct histopathological scores of laying hens with salpingitis is shown in Fig. 2. LPS challenge significantly increased oviduct histopathological scores (Fig. 2B, D and F, PChallenge < 0.01). Adding PP to the diet significantly reduced the histopathological score of the isthmus of the oviduct (Fig. 2D, PPP < 0.01), while there were no significant differences in the oviduct of the magnum and the isthmus part (Fig. 2B and F, PPP > 0.05). Furthermore, the isthmus showed a significant interaction effect in terms of challenge and diet. Compared to the CON group, the LPS group significantly increased the histopathological score, while compared to the LPS group, the LPS + PP group significantly reduced the histopathological score (Fig. 2D, PPP*Challenge < 0.05).

Antioxidant indices and inflammatory cytokines in the magnum of laying hens under LPS challenge

LPS group significantly increased the concentrations of IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ in the magnum of laying hens compared with the CON group (Fig. 3B-D and G, PChallenge < 0.05), while decreasing the levels of IL-4 and IL-10 (Fig. 3E-F, PChallenge < 0.05). Dietary PP supplementation significantly reduced the magnum content of IL-1β, TNF-α and IFN-γ (Fig. 3B, D and G, PPP < 0.05), and increased IL-10 levels (Fig. 3F, PPP < 0.05). Discussion

Extending the laying cycle of hens is a critical objective for improving production efficiency. However, advancing age is associated with a progressive decline in oviduct epithelial cell function and an elevation in local inflammatory status, which together reduce reproductive efficiency and consequently impair laying performance and egg quality (Ketut, et al., 2024; Landete, 2013; Liao, et al., 2023; Rusli, et al., 2015). Therefore, nutritional interventions aimed at preserving oviduct health and attenuating aging-associated degenerative processes are of paramount importance. Plant extracts, rich in polyphenolic bioactive compounds, have demonstrated beneficial effects in livestock production, including improved animal health and growth performance (Landete, 2013). Mangosteen extracts, for instance, are abundant in polyphenols and can enhance poultry growth performance through nutritional regulatory pathways. Dietary supplementation with 3.5% and 5.2% RML improved FCR in Lohmann-Brown layers (Kedir, et al., 2023). Similarly, dietary supplementation with a rosemary-containing herbal mixture in Katunitsa chickens from hatch to 52 weeks reduced FCR by 3.37% and accelerated the onset of lay by one week (Abo Ghanima, et al., 2020). In addition, dietary inclusion of 300 mg/kg rosemary essential oil in ISA Brown hens (28-76 weeks) significantly improved egg production, egg weight and FCR (Gerzilov, et al., 2015). Mangosteen-derived products have also shown comparable effects. Supplementation with 133 ml/kg mangosteen peel extract improved egg production and FCR in Sentul chickens (Widjastuti, et al., 2020), while 500 mg/kg mangosteen extract enhanced egg quality and reduced harmful bacteria in 52-week-old Hy-Line Brown hens (Zhu, et al., 2024). In the present study, PP supplementation reduced the FCR and improved production performance, which is consistent with most previous findings. These beneficial effects may be attributed to the nutritional components of PP, which facilitate digestion and absorption, improve nutrient utilization, and exert direct or indirect anabolic actions on host tissues. Alternatively, the aromatic properties of PP or the bioactivity of its phenolic compounds may also contribute to the enhanced FCR by promoting overall production performance.

Egg quality reflects the physical, chemical, and sensory characteristics of eggs, directly influencing their market value and consumer acceptance. Common indicators include egg shape index, eggshell thickness, eggshell weight, yolk weight, yolk color, albumen height and Haugh units (Alig, et al., 2023; Cheng and Ning, 2023). Previous studies have shown that 500 mg/kg mangosteen extract (containing 20.67% α-mangostin) reduces defective egg and increases Haugh units in Hy-Line Brown hens (Zhu, et al., 2024). Moreover, mangosteen peel extract has been reported to extend egg storage life, with 10% supplementation yielding the highest Haugh unit values during 4-week storage period (Ketut, et al., 2024). Dietary mangosteen peel extract (133 ml/kg) rich in flavonoids also significantly affects egg weight, yolk color, eggshell thickness and cholesterol content (Widjastuti, et al., 2020). Similarly, rosemary-derived compounds exhibit beneficial effects, as evidenced by improved eggshell strength and Haugh units following supplementation with 5.2% rosemary leaf meal (Kedir, et al., 2023) or 0.02% rosmarinic acid (Cai, et al., 2020). Collectively, these studies suggest that bioactive compounds from mangosteen and rosemary positively regulate egg quality. However, in the present study, PP supplementation did not significantly affect eggshell strength, eggshell thickness, Haugh unit or albumen height. These discrepancies may be attributed to differences in dosage, supplementation strategy, experimental duration, as well as the breed and age of the animals used.

As important indicators of metabolic balance and organ function, blood parameters effectively reflect the nutritional status and pathological changes in animals. Among these, ALP is a key marker of liver function, and elevated ALP activity is generally indicative of hepatic parenchymal injury (Ingawale, et al., 2014). In the present study, dietary supplementation with PP significantly reduced serum ALP activity in laying hens, suggesting an improvement in liver function. This result aligns with previous findings showing that xanthone derivatives from mangosteen reduce serum ALP activity (Mohamed, et al., 2022). Additional evidence from a cholestatic liver injury model further supports this observation, where γ-mangostin markedly decreased serum ALT, AST, and ALP activities in mice (Khayat, et al., 2023). Similarly, rosmarinic acid has been reported to reduce serum ALT and AST levels and alleviate hepatocellular damage in rats (Yu, et al., 2021).

Salpingitis is a major reproductive disorder in laying hens, leading to reduced egg production, impaired egg quality, and increased mortality, thereby causing substantial economic losses to the poultry industry. The alleviation of oviduct inflammation is therefore of critical importance for maintaining reproductive tract health and productivity. Inflammatory responses in salpingitis are largely mediated by cytokines, among which pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α play pivotal roles in initiating and amplifying tissue damage (Landman, et al., 2013; Poulsen, et al., 2020; Wang, et al., 2020; Wen, et al., 2021). Previous studies have demonstrated that LPS-induced salpingitis significantly elevates inflammatory markers, including serum interleukins and TNF-α (Song, et al., 2023). Consistent with these findings, the present study showed that LPS challenge markedly increased serum IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α concentrations, confirming the successful establishment of a inflammatory model. More importantly, dietary supplementation with PP prior to LPS exposure significantly reduced serum TNF-α levels, suggesting that PP exerts a protective effect against excessive inflammatory responses. This reduction in pro-inflammatory cytokines suggests that PP may act through the suppression of key signaling pathways responsible for cytokine production. To understand why PP is effective in alleviating oviduct inflammation, it is essential to examine its molecular targets. In this study, prophylactic PP supplementation attenuated inflammatory signaling by downregulating the expression of TLR4, MyD88, and NF-κB in the oviduct—key components of the innate immune response to inflammatory stimuli (Song, et al., 2023). These molecular changes were accompanied by reduced levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IFN-γ and IL-6) and increased levels of anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-4 and IL-10) in the oviduct, indicating that PP suppresses inflammatory responses through the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling axis.

The oviduct-protective effect of PP can be attributed to the combined actions of its two primary bioactive components: α-mangostin from mangosteen polyphenols and rosmarinic acid. Regarding mangosteen-derived compounds, α-mangostin has been shown to inhibit LPS-induced inflammatory responses by blocking the TAK1/NF-κB signaling pathway and suppressing the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in macrophages (Guan, et al., 2020; Majdalawieh, et al., 2025). More recent studies have directly demonstrated that dietary supplementation with α-mangostin at 120 mg/kg significantly lowers the concentration of IL-1β in both serum and uterine tissue of aging laying hens, and upregulates uterine genes involved in biomineralization and eggshell formation (Huang, et al., 2026). Similarly, rosmarinic acid has been extensively documented for its anti-inflammatory properties. Taravat, et al. (2023) demonstrated that rosmarinic acid alleviates LPS-induced endometritis in mice by suppressing the TLR4-NLRP3 signaling pathway, significantly reducing the expression of TLR4, NLRP3 and pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-18 and TNF-α. In a cholestatic rat model, rosmarinic acid suppressed TLR4/NF-κB pathway activation and reduced serum levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Lin, et al., 2017).

The combination of mangosteen polyphenols and rosmarinic acid in the present study may produce synergistic anti-inflammatory effects beyond those achieved by either compound alone. Synergistic interactions among polyphenolic compounds have been increasingly recognized as a strategy to enhance therapeutic efficacy through the simultaneous modulation of multiple signaling pathways. Phytogenic combinations can improve gut health, support immune function, and lower inflammation through multi-target mechanisms, as recent evidence indicates that plant-derived compounds exert anti-inflammatory effects by acting on TLR4-mediated pathways independent of their free radical scavenging capacity (Najjar, et al., 2024; Oni and Oke, 2025). In the present study, the dual inhibition of the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB axis by PP reflects such cooperative action, which may explain the enhanced protective effect observed at the tissue level. In addition to systemic cytokine changes, histopathological observations further supported the anti-inflammatory role of PP. LPS challenge induced severe histopathological damage in the oviduct, characterized by epithelial cell degeneration, inflammatory cell infiltration, and tissue structural disruption. However, these histopathological alterations were markedly alleviated in hens receiving PP supplementation, indicating that PP effectively mitigates oviduct injury at the tissue level. This dual evidence from both biochemical and histological analyses highlights the importance of PP in protecting reproductive tract integrity under inflammatory stress.

Interestingly, in non-challenged hens, PP supplementation increased IL-6 and IFN-γ levels, suggesting that its immunomodulatory effects may depend on the host's inflammatory status. This inflammation status–dependent effect suggests that PP possesses bidirectional immunomodulatory properties, enhancing immune responsiveness under normal conditions while attenuating excessive inflammation to maintain immune homeostasis. Such dual functionality is consistent with recent findings that plant polyphenols can act as mild immunostimulants in healthy animals but as potent anti-inflammatory agents under pathological conditions, a property that is increasingly recognized as a key advantage of natural immunomodulators over conventional anti-inflammatory drugs (Zhang, et al., 2024).

Overall, dietary supplementation with 200 mg/kg mangosteen polyphenols + 200 mg/kg rosmarinic acid attenuated LPS-induced oviductal inflammation in laying hens, likely through its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. These results suggest that PP ameliorates acute oviduct inflammation induced by LPS, but further studies are needed to clarify the molecular mechanisms and to evaluate effects on reproductive hormones and long‑term reproductive outcomes.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that PP supplementation during the feeding phase improves production performance and supports health status in laying hens. In an LPS-induced oviduct model, PP reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines and increased anti-inflammatory cytokines, thereby alleviating inflammatory responses. These effects may be associated with modulation of the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway.
