Bone lid technique versus standard curettage for maxillofacial cysts: a prospective 3D CBCT study of bone regeneration and tooth stability
Authors: Beibei Zhang, Ruixiang Li, Tingyi Gao, Shengkai Liao, Kai Zhang, Yongfeng Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPreservation of cortical bone during surgical management of maxillofacial cysts may enhance early bone regeneration and functional recovery. This study aimed to compare the clinical and radiographic outcomes of the Bone Lid Technique versus standard curettage.MethodsIn this single-center randomized controlled trial, 60 patients with maxillofacial cysts ≥ 3 × 3 × 3 mm were assigned to the Bone Lid group n=30 or standard curettage group n=30. CBCT-based three-dimensional analyses assessed defect volume, surface area, and grayscale value. Periodontal outcomes (probing depth [PD], clinical attachment level [CAL]) and tooth mobility were evaluated at baseline, 3, 6, and 9 months. Percentage changes between consecutive visits were analyzed using linear mixed-effects models, while longitudinal tooth mobility was analyzed using ordinal logistic mixed-effects models. Baseline lesion size was additionally evaluated as a potential confounding factor. A lesion-size sensitivity analysis was performed after excluding lesions in the lowest quartile of the pooled baseline defect-volume distribution. Binary logistic regression was additionally used to assess tooth stability at 9 months.ResultsCompared with the standard curettage group, the Bone Lid group showed significantly greater reductions in defect surface area (β = 13.782, 95% CI 5.924–21.640, P = 0.001) and volume (β = 22.436, 95% CI 14.285–30.587, P < 0.001), and greater increase in grayscale values (β = − 4.615, 95% CI − 8.292 to − 0.938, P = 0.014). PD and CAL improved more favorably in the Bone Lid group (β = 22.548 and 53.913, both P < 0.001). Tooth mobility decreased progressively in both groups, but recovery was faster and more stable in the Bone Lid group. Binary logistic regression confirmed a higher probability of achieving stable teeth at 9 months in the Bone Lid group (OR = 4.42, 95% CI 1.15–17.03, P = 0.031).ConclusionThe Bone Lid Technique was associated with more favorable early bone regeneration and enhanced tooth stability compared with standard curettage. However, the robustness of the surface-area finding should be interpreted cautiously because baseline lesion morphology differed between groups and the corresponding sensitivity estimate was less stable. It may be particularly beneficial for moderate-sized cystic defects, lesions involving multiple adjacent teeth, or cases where preservation of cortical bone and early functional recovery are priorities.Trial registrationThis clinical trial was registered at chictr.org.cn on June 16, 2025 and had registration number ChiCTR2500104364.Retrospectively registered.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-09389-x.

Introduction

Odontogenic jaw cysts are common lesions in the oral and maxillofacial region, often leading to jaw expansion, bone destruction, and tooth mobility [1]. In severe cases, they may even cause pathological fractures, compromising masticatory function and facial aesthetics. Surgical management remains the primary treatment approach, with commonly employed techniques including standard curettage and the Bone Lid Technique [2–4]. Standard curettage involves removal of the cystic lining along with surrounding bone to ensure complete debridement; however, this approach may result in significant bone defects and carries a risk of incomplete lesion removal [5–8]. In contrast, the Bone Lid Technique preserves a cortical bone flap and maintains periosteal attachments, providing structural support and a favorable structural support for postoperative bone regeneration [9]. By retaining the three-dimensional morphology of the defect and minimizing soft tissue collapse, this approach may facilitate more orderly bone repair and contribute to the stability of adjacent teeth [10].

Despite these theoretical advantages, quantitative evidence comparing the extent of bone regeneration and tooth stability between these surgical approaches remains limited. Therefore, this study employed cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT)-based three-dimensional quantitative analysis to evaluate bone regeneration and changes in tooth stability following the Bone Lid Technique versus standard curettage, aiming to provide objective evidence to guide clinical decision-making in the management of maxillofacial cysts.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This prospective randomized controlled trial followed the CONSORT reporting guidelines [11]. The single-center study enrolled 60 patients with maxillofacial cysts. Consecutive eligible patients who were scheduled to receive surgical treatment at the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University, between September 2022 to July 2025.

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Bengbu Medical University (Approval No. 2021–174) and was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent revisions. All patients were fully informed of the research purposes. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients or their guardians before the initiation of treatment.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) diagnosis of an odontogenic maxillofacial cyst confirmed by clinical examination and CBCT imaging; (2) presence of adjacent teeth affected by the lesion; and (3) availability of complete CBCT imaging and clinical follow-up data at all designated time points.Exclusion criteria: (1) systemic conditions known to affect bone healing (e.g., uncontrolled diabetes, osteoporosis);(2) previous surgery, trauma, or radiation therapy in the affected jaw region;(3) development of postoperative infection, dehiscence, or surgical complications requiring additional intervention;(4) active periodontitis or severe periodontal breakdown in adjacent teeth (to avoid confounding periodontal outcomes); and (5) incomplete follow-up or missing data.

Sample size, randomization, and blinding

No a priori sample size calculation was performed in this study, and the 60 enrolled patients constituted a convenience sample. Thirty patients were randomly assigned to two groups via sealed envelopes with an allocation ratio of 1:1 and divided into: Group A Thirty patients (n = 30) in whom the lesion was accessed with bone lid technical. Group B Thirty patients (n = 30) in whom the lesion was accessed with standard technical.

All 60 patients met the inclusion criteria and completed the study without loss to follow-up. Preoperative periodontal parameters (probing depth [PD] and clinical attachment level [CAL]) were recorded. CBCT imaging was also performed to assess lesion extent, including lesion volume, surface area, and anatomical distribution.The participant screening, random allocation, and follow-up process are summarized in the CONSORT flow diagram (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Participant flow diagram of this randomized controlled trial (RCT)

Surgical procedures

All patients underwent cyst enucleation under general anesthesia.

Group A (Bone Lid Technique): Preoperative CBCT images were used for surgical planning, with osteotomy lines designed on the buccal aspect of the lesion according to the extent of bone destruction and proximity to adjacent tooth roots. The mesial and distal osteotomy boundaries were extended approximately 4 mm beyond the radiographic lesion margins.

A mucoperiosteal incision was made from the gingival margin to the vestibular sulcus, connecting the mesial and distal extensions. Full-thickness flap elevation was performed to expose the planned osteotomy area. Using an ultrasonic bone knife, mesial, distal, and coronal osteotomies were performed to create a three-sided bone window.

The bone flap was carefully mobilized by completing the basal osteotomy, allowing full access to the cystic lesion. The cyst was completely enucleated, followed by copious irrigation and hemostasis. Finally, the bone flap was repositioned to its original anatomical position and stabilized using interrupted fixation with 4–0 sterile absorbable sutures (Vicryl™, polyglactin 910; Ethicon, Johnson & Johnson, USA; VCP422H) (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Bone Lid Technique Surgical Procedure

Bone Lid Technique Surgical Procedure

Group B (Standard Curettage): Preoperative CBCT images were used for surgical planning. A conventional mucoperiosteal flap was designed using a triangular or trapezoidal incision to provide adequate access to the lesion area. Full-thickness flap elevation was performed to expose the underlying cortical bone.

The overlying bone was removed to gain access to the cystic cavity and lesion wall. The cyst was completely enucleated under direct visualization, and the bony cavity was thoroughly debrided and smoothed as needed to remove residual tissue and sharp margins.

Following copious irrigation and hemostasis, the surgical site was closed in layers using interrupted 4–0 sterile absorbable sutures (Vicryl™, polyglactin 910; Ethicon, Johnson & Johnson, USA; VCP422H) (Fig. 3).Fig. 3Standard Curettage Surgical Procedure

Standard Curettage Surgical Procedure

All procedures were performed by the same experienced surgeon following standardized protocols to minimize operator-dependent variability. Teeth with grade III mobility were extracted intraoperatively.Special care was taken in both groups to preserve adjacent periodontal structures and maintain aseptic conditions.

Postoperative care

The postoperative care was consistent for all patients. Following surgery, the patients were given antibiotics, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medications, and a mouth rinse containing 0.12% chlorhexidine Di-gluco-nate twice daily for 7 days. The stitches were removed after a week.

Postoperative evaluation

Radiographic evaluation

Radiographic measurements mainly involved bone defect volume, surface area and grayscale value,CBCT scans were acquired using an i-CAT 17–19 system (Imaging Sciences International, Hatfield, PA, USA) and analyzed with Invivo 5 software. Imaging parameters were standardized at 120 kVp and 5 mA, with an isotropic voxel size of 0.25 mm and a field of view (FOV) of 16 × 13 cm. Images were reconstructed using the manufacturer’s standard reconstruction algorithm without additional sharpening or smoothing filters.To minimize scanning-related variability, all CBCT examinations were performed under identical acquisition parameters by the same radiologist.Each measurement was independently repeated three times by two experienced surgeons, and the mean values were used for analysis to ensure reliability. Inter-rater reliability was validated using 10 randomly selected cases,with ICC values of 0.85–0.92, showing good inter-examiner consistency.Operating surgeons were unblinded due to the nature of the surgical procedures.

CBCT examinations were performed preoperatively and at 3, 6, and 9 months postoperatively (Fig. 4) [12–14]. Imaging data were imported into Mimics 21.0 software (Materialise, Leuven, Belgium) for three-dimensional reconstruction (Fig. 5). Semi-automatic threshold segmentation combined with manual fine adjustment was adopted to outline the range of cystic lesions. Bone defect volume and surface area were automatically calculated at 3-month, 6-month and9-month follow-ups. Mean grayscale values of lesion areas were measured at all follow-up time points based on fixed anatomical landmarks and standardized region-of-interest (ROI) placement.Fig. 4Workflow of CBCT-based three-dimensional reconstruction and quantitative analysis of the cystic cavityFig. 5CBCT-derived two-dimensional sections and three-dimensional reconstructions of the cystic cavity. Clinical evaluation

Clinical measurements included periodontal probing depth (PD), clinical attachment level (CAL), and tooth stability of the affected teeth.

Periodontal parameters (PD and CAL) of cyst-affected teeth were evaluated at six sites per tooth, and the mean value was used for statistical analysis. PD was defined as the distance from the gingival margin to the base of the periodontal pocket, while CAL was defined as the distance from the cemento-enamel junction to the base of the pocket.

Tooth mobility was assessed at baseline and all postoperative follow-up time points using dental forceps. Anterior teeth were gently mobilized by applying force at the incisal edge, while posterior teeth were evaluated by applying pressure to the occlusal surface to assess the amplitude and direction of tooth movement.

A standardized clinical tooth mobility grading system was used for evaluation. Grade I mobility was defined as buccolingual movement within 1 mm; Grade II mobility referred to 1–2 mm movement in both buccolingual and mesiodistal directions; Grade III mobility indicated movement exceeding 2 mm in buccolingual, mesiodistal, and vertical directions. Tooth mobility was classified according to the Miller mobility classification system.Teeth involved in cystic lesions were included for periodontal evaluation. Teeth with preoperative Grade III mobility were extracted during surgery and excluded from longitudinal analysis. A total of 17 teeth in Group A and 19 teeth in Group B were extracted due to Grade III mobility. Only retained teeth (Grade 0–II) were included in follow-up assessments.

All clinical measurements were performed by two blinded and calibrated examiners.Inter-examiner reliability for tooth mobility grading was assessed using weighted Cohen’s kappa based on independent ratings by the two blinded examiners, demonstrating good agreement (weighted κ = 0.87). Intra-examiner reliability was not assessed because repeated mobility grading of the same teeth by the same examiner under comparable clinical conditions was not performed.

All patients completed the study without dropout, and all radiographic and periodontal outcomes were analyzed at the patient level according to the predefined protocol.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were assessed for distributional characteristics before analysis. Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas variables with skewed distributions were presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. All statistical analyses were performed using R software, version 4.3.1.

Baseline characteristics were compared between groups using independent-samples t-tests for normally distributed continuous variables, Mann–Whitney U tests for skewed continuous variables, and chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables, as appropriate. Preoperative defect volume and defect surface area were evaluated as indicators of baseline lesion size. Because both variables showed skewed distributions, they were summarized as median (IQR) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test.

Percentage changes between consecutive follow-up visits were calculated for grayscale value, defect surface area, defect volume, probing depth (PD), and clinical attachment level (CAL). Linear mixed-effects models were applied to analyze longitudinal group differences, with treatment group, time phase, and the group × time interaction included as fixed effects and patient ID included as a random intercept. Group × time interactions were examined to determine whether between-group differences varied across follow-up intervals. The Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate method was used to adjust for multiple comparisons.

Because lesion size may influence spontaneous bone regeneration, additional adjusted models were performed in which baseline defect volume was included as a covariate in the defect-volume model and baseline defect surface area was included as a covariate in the defect-surface-area model. Baseline defect volume was also considered in the model for grayscale-value change as an indicator of the initial extent of the lesion.

Tooth mobility grade was treated as an ordinal categorical outcome and analyzed using an ordinal logistic mixed-effects model. Treatment group, time, and the group × time interaction were included as fixed effects, with patient ID included as a random effect. In an additional endpoint analysis, tooth mobility at 9 months was dichotomized as stable versus persistently mobile and analyzed using binary logistic regression adjusted for baseline PD and CAL.

A lesion-size sensitivity analysis was performed after excluding lesions with baseline defect volumes below the first quartile of the pooled distribution. The cutoff value was 680.36 mm3. The same model specifications as those used in the primary longitudinal analyses were applied to the restricted cohort.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Outcome measures

Six outcome measures were evaluated in this study, including three radiographic outcomes (bony defect volume, defect surface area, and CBCT grayscale value) and three clinical outcomes (periodontal probing depth [PD], clinical attachment level [CAL], and tooth mobility grade).

The primary outcomes were CBCT grayscale value and tooth mobility grade. These indicators were selected because they directly reflect the two principal objectives of the study: bone regeneration and functional recovery of cyst-affected teeth. CBCT grayscale value was used as a relative radiographic indicator of postoperative bone repair and tissue maturation, whereas tooth mobility grade represented the functional stability of adjacent teeth during healing.

Secondary outcomes included defect volume, defect surface area, PD, and CAL. These variables were used to provide complementary information regarding morphological bone healing and periodontal tissue recovery throughout the follow-up period.

All outcomes were assessed at baseline and at 3-, 6-, and 9-month follow-up visits.

Sensitivity analysis excluding the smallest lesions

To assess whether the observed radiographic differences between the two surgical groups were disproportionately influenced by small lesions with a greater potential for spontaneous healing, a sensitivity analysis was conducted after excluding lesions in the lowest quartile of the pooled baseline defect-volume distribution. The cutoff value was 680.36 mm3.

A total of 15 patients were excluded, leaving 45 patients in the sensitivity-analysis cohort, including 24 patients in the Bone Lid Technique group and 21 patients in the standard curettage group. The same linear mixed-effects model specifications used in the primary analyses were applied, with treatment group and time phase included as fixed effects, patient ID included as a random intercept, and the corresponding baseline lesion-size variable included as a covariate.

After exclusion of the smallest lesions, the Bone Lid Technique group continued to demonstrate a greater reduction in defect volume than the standard curettage group. The adjusted between-group difference in volume-change rate was β = 19.840 (95% CI, 9.950 to 29.730; P < 0.001). Although this effect estimate was slightly attenuated compared with the primary analysis, its direction and clinical interpretation remained unchanged.

Similarly, the adjusted between-group difference in defect-surface-area change remained statistically significant in favor of the Bone Lid Technique group (β = 11.270; 95% CI, 2.360 to 20.180; P = 0.014). The magnitude of the effect was moderately smaller and the confidence interval was wider than in the full cohort, reflecting the reduced sample size.

The grayscale-value analysis also remained directionally consistent with the primary analysis. Compared with the Bone Lid Technique group, the standard curettage group showed a lower increase in grayscale values during follow-up (β = − 4.210; 95% CI, − 8.130 to − 0.290; P = 0.036). No significant Group × Time interactions were identified for the three radiographic outcomes in the restricted cohort (all P > 0.05).

Discussion

This single-center prospective randomized controlled study demonstrated that the Bone Lid Technique provides more favorable early postoperative bone regeneration and faster recovery of tooth stability compared with standard curettage in the management of maxillofacial cysts. Quantitative CBCT analysis showed significantly greater reductions in defect volume and surface area, as well as a higher grayscale value change rate in the Bone Lid group during the early healing phase. Linear mixed-effects modeling further confirmed distinct temporal trajectories between groups, indicating a consistently more favorable radiographic and functional recovery pattern associated with the Bone Lid Technique, a finding consistent with previous research [10].

Previous CBCT-based volumetric studies have demonstrated progressive spontaneous bone regeneration after cyst enucleation, particularly during the early postoperative period [2, 6, 7, 16]. The observed advantages may be primarily attributed to the preservation and repositioning of the cortical bone flap. This approach maintains the native three-dimensional architecture of the defect, preserves spatial stability, and provides mechanical support during early healing. In addition, improved surgical access and direct visualization of the lesion through the bone lid approach may facilitate more complete lesion enucleation and reduce intraoperative trauma to adjacent structures. These factors together may contribute to more organized early bone formation and enhanced structural continuity during repair [10, 16].

Grayscale value change rate analysis further supported differences in tissue reconstruction dynamics between groups. Although CBCT-derived grayscale values do not represent absolute bone mineral density, their longitudinal changes may reflect relative differences in radiographic density trends during healing [12, 14, 15]. The mixed-effects model results suggest that the Bone Lid Technique may be associated with a more favorable radiographic healing pattern, particularly in the early and middle healing phases.

Tooth stability recovery showed a similar pattern, with faster and more stable improvement observed in the Bone Lid group. Longitudinal mixed-effects analysis demonstrated divergent mobility reduction trajectories between groups over time, with differences becoming more evident during mid- and late-stage follow-up. These findings suggest that preservation of cortical architecture may confer additional biomechanical support to periodontal structures during healing. This effect is likely multifactorial, involving restoration of alveolar bone support, periodontal ligament adaptation, and resolution of cyst-related inflammatory effects [17, 18].

Both groups demonstrated progressive improvement in periodontal parameters (PD and CAL), with relatively small intergroup differences. This indicates that while both surgical approaches are effective in resolving local inflammatory and periodontal conditions after cyst removal, the primary advantages of the Bone Lid Technique appear to be related to structural preservation and functional stability rather than conventional periodontal parameter changes [17].

Defect size and cyst type may influence spontaneous bone healing and postoperative regeneration [19, 20]. Baseline lesion characteristics were considered as potential confounders. Although baseline defect volume did not differ significantly between groups, defect surface area was greater in the Bone Lid group. After adjustment in mixed-effects models and sensitivity analysis excluding lesions in the lowest quartile of baseline defect volume, the main findings remained consistent. The magnitude of effect was moderately attenuated in the restricted cohort, and confidence intervals became wider due to reduced sample size; however, the direction of all primary outcomes remained unchanged. These results support the general consistency of the findings while acknowledging that lesion morphology may still influence healing dynamics.

From a surgical perspective, the Bone Lid Technique offers both biological and technical advantages. In addition to preserving cortical bone integrity, it allows enhanced intraoperative access and visualization, which may improve the precision of cyst removal and protection of adjacent anatomical structures. However, standard curettage remains a reliable and widely applicable approach, particularly for smaller lesions or cases without significant cortical plate involvement. Therefore, treatment selection should be individualized based on defect size, anatomical location, and clinical requirements.

This study has several limitations. As a single-center study with a 9-month follow-up period, long-term outcomes such as complete bone maturation and recurrence could not be fully evaluated. CBCT grayscale values were used as relative indicators rather than absolute measurements of bone density. Although inter-examiner reliability for tooth mobility grading was confirmed to be good, intra-examiner reliability was not assessed, which may introduce minor measurement variability. Larger multicenter randomized trials with extended follow-up are warranted to further validate these findings.

Conclusion

Based on CBCT-based three-dimensional quantitative analysis, the Bone Lid Technique was associated with more favorable early postoperative bone repair and faster recovery of tooth stability than standard curettage in patients with maxillofacial cysts, although both approaches demonstrated progressive healing during follow-up. After excluding the smallest lesions, the volume-related treatment effect remained directionally consistent but was less precise because of the reduced sample size, whereas the defect-surface-area finding was less stable and should be interpreted cautiously. Surgical strategy should therefore be individualized according to lesion extent, baseline defect morphology, anatomical location, adjacent tooth involvement, and the need to preserve cortical and periodontal structures. The Bone Lid Technique may be particularly useful for patients with substantial bone defects, involvement of adjacent teeth, or a priority for early structural preservation and functional recovery; however, larger multicenter studies with longer follow-up are required to confirm these findings.
