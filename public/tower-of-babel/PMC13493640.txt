Nemolizumab Versus Dupilumab in the Treatment of Prurigo Nodularis: Systematic Literature Review and Network Meta-analysis
Authors: Gil Yosipovitch, Katarzyna Borkowska, James Frampton, Kassandra Schaible, Marissa Betts, Katrin Jack, Zarif Jabbar Lopez, Binod Neupane, Christophe Piketty, Ramkumar Subramanian, Jorge Puelles Fernández de Trocóniz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrurigo nodularis (PN) is a chronic skin condition characterized by severe, persistent pruritus and impairment in health-related quality of life. Dupilumab and nemolizumab are the only treatments approved by the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency for PN. In the absence of head-to-head comparisons, this study evaluated the comparative clinical efficacy and safety of nemolizumab versus dupilumab in an indirect treatment comparison. A systematic literature review (SLR) identified clinical trials reporting efficacy and/or safety outcomes in PN. Searches were conducted on 1 December 2025 in Embase, MEDLINE and MEDLINE In-Process, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and PsycInfo. Evidence from the SLR was synthesized using a Bayesian network meta-analysis (NMA) to generate comparative estimates. Sensitivity analyses assessed robustness to assumptions regarding missing data and differences in concomitant topical corticosteroid or calcineurin inhibitor use. Five trials were included. In base-case analyses, nemolizumab generally demonstrated greater estimated efficacy than dupilumab across pruritus-related outcomes, with statistically significant differences for selected endpoints and time points. Estimated treatment differences significantly favored nemolizumab for change from baseline in Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Scale (PP-NRS) at 24 weeks (mean difference − 1.30; 95% credible interval [CrI] − 2.49 to − 0.18), PP-NRS response (≥ 4-point improvement) at 12 weeks (odds ratio [OR] 3.27; 95% CrI 1.41 to 7.27), and the composite endpoint of Investigator Global Assessment success and PP-NRS response at 12/16 weeks (OR 3.35; 95% CrI 1.19 to 10.09). At earlier time points (4–6 weeks), change from baseline in PP-NRS also significantly favored nemolizumab. Sensitivity analyses were consistent with base-case findings. Treatment-emergent adverse events were similar between treatments. These findings suggest that nemolizumab may provide greater improvement in key pruritus and disease severity outcomes compared with dupilumab in patients with PN, although uncertainty remains for several endpoints.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13555-026-01827-6.Keywords: Prurigo nodularis, Nemolizumab, Dupilumab, Indirect treatment comparison

Introduction

Prurigo nodularis (PN) is a chronic skin condition characterized by intensely pruritic hyperkeratotic and/or fibrotic nodules and significant skin lesions, commonly located on the extremities and trunk [1]. PN is associated with a significant reduction in health-related quality of life, increased psychological distress, and substantial healthcare costs [2–4]. Current treatment options for PN are limited; only two therapies, dupilumab and nemolizumab, are currently approved by the US Food & Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medicines Agency (EMA). As the only approved systemic therapies for PN, these agents represent the primary treatment options available to clinicians, making comparative evidence essential to guide treatment selection and address clinical uncertainty.

Dupilumab, an interleukin-4 receptor alpha antagonist, has been approved by the FDA and EMA for the treatment of PN since 2022. Pivotal studies have indicated that dupilumab can significantly reduce pruritus and improve skin lesions in patients with PN [5]. Nemolizumab is an anti-interleukin-31 receptor alpha monoclonal antibody that was approved by the FDA in 2024 and by the EMA in 2025 for the treatment of PN. Phase 2 and 3 randomized clinical trials have suggested that nemolizumab can significantly reduce pruritus and improve the quality of life in patients with PN [6, 7].

The comparative efficacy and safety of nemolizumab relative to dupilumab have not been systematically evaluated. In the absence of head-to-head clinical trials, indirect treatment comparisons can be used. The objective of this study was to generate evidence on the comparative clinical efficacy and safety of nemolizumab relative to dupilumab via indirect treatment comparisons, using a systematic literature review (SLR) and network meta-analysis (NMA).

Materials and Methods

Systematic Literature Review

This SLR was registered at PROSPERO under the registration number CRD42023480142. This article presents data from the clinical efficacy SLR specified in the registration record.

Eligible trials were identified via an SLR conducted according to the standards of the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions and reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses. Embase, MEDLINE and MEDLINE In-Process, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and PsycInfo databases were searched via OvidSP on 1 December 2025 (Tables S1–S3). Conference proceedings (2021–2025), health technology assessments, and clinical trial registers were also searched (Table S4).

The SLR was limited to clinical trials (randomized clinical trials, single-arm trials, non-randomized trials) of investigational and approved treatments in adults (aged ≥ 18 years) with PN. Trials must have also assessed at least one of the following outcomes: responses in PP-NRS (PP-NRS and worst-itch numeric rating scale were considered equivalent for this SLR) [8], Investigator Global Assessment (IGA), average pruritus, dynamic pruritus score, sleep disturbances, wakefulness after sleep onset, PN-associated pain, patient global assessment of disease, Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI), hospital anxiety and depression scale, discontinuations, treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), grade 3 and 4 adverse events (AEs), serious AEs, and AEs of special interest (asthma, conjunctivitis, infusion-related reactions, peripheral edema, skin or systemic infection, headache, elevated aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase in combination with bilirubin, neurodermatitis, atopic dermatitis, eczema). No restrictions to publication date and geographical regions were applied. Only relevant publications published in English were included.

Screening of identified records at the title/abstract and full-text level was performed by two investigators using Nested Knowledge software (St. Paul, MN), and any conflicts were resolved by a third investigator. Relevant data on trial characteristics, patient characteristics, and outcomes (Tables S5 and S6) were extracted into a data extraction table by a single investigator and validated by a second investigator.

Quality assessment for RCTs was performed using the Cochrane Risk-of-Bias Assessment Tool 2.0 [9]. This assessment summarized the risk of bias in the findings of RCTs by considering five domains of potential bias: (1) bias arising from the randomization process, (2) bias due to deviations from intended interventions, (3) bias due to missing outcome data, (4) bias in measurement of the outcome, and (5) bias in selection of the reported result. An overall risk-of-bias judgment was assigned to each trial, using the following categories: low risk of bias, some concerns, or high risk of bias. Quality assessment was performed by a single investigator and validated by a second investigator, and any conflicts were resolved by a third investigator.

Feasibility Assessment

A feasibility assessment was performed for each outcome of interest to evaluate the potential for conducting an NMA. This included examining whether the data were adequate to create an evidence network and assessing the presence of clinical heterogeneity across studies, which could prevent comparisons or influence analysis results. The feasibility assessment excluded two trials: OLYMPIA LTE (excluded because it was a single-arm trial) and OLYMPIA DURABILITY (excluded because all participants had previously responded to nemolizumab).

Statistical Analysis

An evaluation of DLQI, PP-NRS (change from baseline, improvement ≥ 4 points), IGA success, a composite outcome for which patients had to achieve both PP-NRS improvement ≥ 4 and IGA success (IGA score of 0 or 1), and TEAEs were feasible for time points ranging from 4 to 24 weeks. Because of the allowance of topical corticosteroid (TCS) and topical calcineurin inhibitor (TCI) therapies in the dupilumab trials (PRIME and PRIME2), which were not permitted in the OLYMPIA trials, the analysis approach for this comparison was distinctive and a pre-specified sensitivity analysis was conducted on the basis of TCS/TCI usage.

Analyses prioritized observed case (OC) data from trials based on methodological guidelines [10–12]. Because of an absence of OC data from comparator trials, a pre-specified sensitivity analysis using data derived from alternate imputation methods was conducted.

Multiple treatments were compared for efficacy and safety outcomes using Bayesian NMA. Continuous outcomes (change from baseline in DLQI and PP-NRS) were analyzed under normal assumptions with an identity link. Binary outcomes (proportions of responders for IGA 0/1, PP-NRS ≥ 4-point improvement, composite outcomes, and TEAEs) were analyzed using a binomial framework with a logit link. Each NMA provided a central estimate of the relative effect (odds ratio (OR) for binary outcomes, mean difference for continuous outcomes) with a 95% CrI. Additionally, the posterior probability that nemolizumab is better (more efficacious or safer) than a comparator was reported.

Both fixed-effects and random-effects Bayesian NMAs were conducted, depending on the appropriateness for the outcomes listed. The choice between fixed-effects and random-effects models was evaluated using deviance information criteria. Convergence was checked by inspecting Monte Carlo error ratios to posterior standard deviations (> 5% indicating issues) and confirmed with Brooks-Gelman-Rubin plots if needed; adjustments were made if convergence was not achieved. Heterogeneity for each comparison was assessed by conducting classical pairwise meta-analysis and calculating I2, an estimate of the percentage of variability due to heterogeneity. Global statistical heterogeneity was assessed by considering the size of the τ from the Bayesian NMA under the random-effects model. If statistical heterogeneity was found, study and patient characteristics were reviewed from a clinical perspective to determine whether they were likely to modify the effects. Bucher indirect treatment comparisons were also performed for each analytical scenario. No subgroup analyses or meta-regression was planned. Data were presented as forest plots alongside effect estimates, CrIs, and probability treatment better for all comparisons with nemolizumab. Additional details on the Bayesian NMA methods are provided in the electronic supplementary material.

Results

Systematic Literature Review

A total of 2464 unique articles were identified from electronic databases. Following screening, 71 articles reporting on seven unique clinical trials were included in the SLR (Fig. S1). Following a feasibility assessment, five trials were deemed eligible for analysis (Fig. S2): LIBERTY PN PRIME; PRIME2; Ständer, 2020; OLYMPIA 1; and OLYMPIA 2 [5–7, 13, 14]. All were placebo-controlled randomized controlled trials; four were phase 3 trials and one was a phase 2 trial. Total sample size ranged from 70 to 286 patients with PN, and the trial duration ranged from 18 to 36 weeks. All trials were conducted double-blind and recruited patients across multiple continents, except for Ständer, 2020, which exclusively recruited European patients. Year of enrollment ranged from 2017 to 2023 (Table S7). Baseline demographic and disease characteristics were broadly comparable across trials, with similar mean PP-NRS and DLQI scores and comparable distributions of IGA severity (Table S8). Quality assessment using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Assessment Tool 2.0 [9] was performed on four of these trials (no full-text publication was available for OLYMPIA 1 at the time of analysis [15]; Table S9). Three (LIBERTY PN PRIME, PRIME2, OLYMPIA 2) of the four trials were deemed to have an overall low risk of bias. Ständer, 2020, was judged to have some concerns in the overall risk of bias because of imbalances in baseline disease severity (PN IGA) between arms.

Network Meta-analysis

Heterogeneity (I2 = 0%) was absent in 50 of 55 direct comparisons involving two or more studies. Low to moderate heterogeneity (I2 = 21.7–33.5%) was observed in three comparisons of dupilumab versus placebo in PRIME and PRIME2, specifically for PP-NRS change from baseline at 10 and 12 weeks, IGA success at 8 weeks, and IGA success at 8 weeks in the imputation sensitivity analysis. A high level of heterogeneity (I2 = 73.3%) was observed in the base-case and sensitivity analysis of PP-NRS response for dupilumab versus placebo at 6 weeks. This heterogeneity was driven by the differences in response observed in PRIME compared with PRIME2. The reason for this difference between PRIME and PRIME2 trials is unclear, given that study methods were identical and baseline patient characteristics were similar across treatment arms.

All network structures lacked loops, preventing inconsistency investigation.

Base-Case Analysis

Change from Baseline in PP-NRS

In the base-case analysis, reductions in PP-NRS favored nemolizumab over dupilumab at all time points (Fig. 1). The estimated mean differences (MD) favored nemolizumab at 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, and 24 weeks and ranged from − 1.23 to − 1.90. Significant differences were observed at 4 weeks (MD − 1.86; 95% CrI − 2.95 to − 0.84), 6 weeks (MD − 1.90; 95% CrI − 3.15 to − 0.59), and 24 weeks (MD − 1.30; 95% CrI − 2.49 to − 0.18). The posterior probability that nemolizumab was more effective than dupilumab exceeded 90% for all time points and reached 99.9% at 4 weeks.

Forest plot of network meta-analysis results for change from baseline in Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Score at 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, and 24 weeks

Compared with placebo, nemolizumab was associated with significantly greater reductions in PP-NRS at all time points.

PP-NRS Response (Improvement of ≥ 4 Points)

Nemolizumab was associated with higher odds of achieving a ≥ 4-point improvement in PP-NRS compared with dupilumab at all assessed time points (Fig. 2). The estimated ORs favored nemolizumab at 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, and 24 weeks, ranging from 1.23 to 3.27. However, a significant difference was observed only at 12 weeks (OR 3.27; 95% CrI 1.41 to 7.27). The posterior probability that nemolizumab was more effective than dupilumab exceeded 85% at all time points and was highest at 12 weeks (99.8%).

Forest plot of network meta-analysis results for response in Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Score (≥ 4-point improvement) at 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, and 24 weeks

Nemolizumab demonstrated significantly higher odds of achieving a ≥ 4-point improvement in PP-NRS compared with placebo at all time points.

IGA Success

Nemolizumab was associated with higher odds of achieving IGA success compared with dupilumab at 8 and 12 weeks, whereas the estimated effect at 24 weeks was closer to the null (Fig. 3). The largest relative effect was observed at 12 weeks (OR 2.27; 95% CrI 0.94 to 5.93), but no significant differences were observed. Posterior probabilities indicated a high likelihood that nemolizumab was more effective than dupilumab at 12 weeks (96.6%), with lower probabilities at 8 weeks (76.6%) and 24 weeks (49.1%).

Forest plot of network meta-analysis results for Investigators Global Assessment success at 8, 12, and 24 weeks

Relative to placebo, nemolizumab consistently demonstrated significantly higher odds of achieving IGA success across all time points.

IGA Success and PP-NRS Response Composite

Nemolizumab showed higher odds of achieving the composite endpoint (an improvement of ≥ 4 points from baseline in PP-NRS and IGA score of 0 or 1) compared with dupilumab at both evaluated time points (Fig. 4). At 12/16 weeks (input data are from 12 weeks for dupilumab and 16 weeks for nemolizumab), the odds ratio significantly favored nemolizumab (OR 3.35; 95%: CrI 1.19 to 10.09) and a high posterior probability of superiority (98.8%). At 24 weeks, the estimated effect continued to favor nemolizumab (OR 1.32; 95% CrI 0.40 to 5.38), but the effect was non-significant and the posterior probability was also lower (67.1%).

For the analysis at 12/16 weeks, data are reported at 12 weeks for dupilumab vs placebo, and reported at 16 weeks for nemolizumab vs placebo

Compared with placebo, nemolizumab was associated with significantly higher odds of achieving the composite endpoint at both time points.

Change from Baseline in DLQI

Reductions in DLQI scores favored nemolizumab over dupilumab at all evaluated time points (Fig. 5). The estimated MDs favored nemolizumab at 4, 12/16, and 24 weeks, ranging from − 0.94 to − 2.13. However, no significant differences were observed. Posterior probabilities suggested a moderate-to-high likelihood that nemolizumab was associated with greater improvement in DLQI compared with dupilumab, exceeding 70% across assessments and reaching 89.4% at 4 weeks.

Forest plot of network meta-analysis results for Dermatology Life Quality Index scores at 4 weeks, 12/16 weeks, and 24 weeks. For the analysis at 12/16 weeks, data are reported at 12 weeks for dupilumab vs placebo, and reported at 16 weeks for nemolizumab vs placebo

In comparisons with placebo, nemolizumab was associated with significantly greater improvements in DLQI at all time points.

TEAEs

The odds of TEAEs were similar for nemolizumab and dupilumab (OR 0.96; 95% CrI 0.53 to 1.73); there was a posterior probability of 55.0% that nemolizumab was associated with fewer TEAEs (Fig. 6). Compared with placebo, nemolizumab showed slightly higher estimated odds of TEAEs (OR 1.38; 95% CrI 0.95 to 2.01). Despite this difference being non-significant, the posterior probability that nemolizumab was associated with fewer TEAEs was low (4.6%).

Sensitivity Analyses: No TCS/TCI

Differences in the allowance of concomitant topical therapies across trials prompted a sensitivity analysis based on TCS and TCI use. In the base-case analysis, intention-to-treat populations from the dupilumab trials (PRIME and PRIME2), in which TCS/TCI use was permitted, were pooled with intention-to-treat populations from the nemolizumab trials, in which topical therapies were not allowed. For outcomes and time points in which the PRIME and PRIME2 trials reported subgroup results without TCS/TCI use, sensitivity analyses restricted to patients without concomitant topical therapy were conducted to assess the robustness of the base-case results.

PP-NRS Response (Improvement of ≥ 4 Points)

The direction of effect for PP-NRS response remained consistent with the base-case analysis, and there were higher odds of achieving a ≥ 4-point improvement favoring nemolizumab over dupilumab (Fig. S3). Compared with the base case, the estimated effect was more pronounced at 12 and 24 weeks. Results versus placebo were consistent with the base case, with robust and sustained superiority of nemolizumab across time points.

IGA Success

The estimated odds of achieving IGA success favored nemolizumab over dupilumab at 24 weeks (OR 1.93; 95% CrI 0.68 to 5.86) (Fig. S4). Compared with the base-case analysis at the same time point, the effect estimate was closer to the null (OR 0.99; 95% CrI 0.42 to 2.58). Although the results of the sensitivity analysis yielded a higher OR, uncertainty remained, and both effect estimates were non-significant. As in the base-case analysis, comparisons with placebo favored nemolizumab at 24 weeks.

Change from Baseline in DLQI

At 24 weeks, the sensitivity analysis showed greater reductions in DLQI with nemolizumab than with dupilumab (MD − 3.04; 95% CrI − 6.04 to 0.08) (Fig. S5), and a high posterior probability favored nemolizumab. Relative to the base-case analysis at the same time point, the estimated effect was larger in magnitude, although uncertainty remained. Consistent with the base case, comparisons with placebo significantly favored nemolizumab.

IGA Success and PP-NRS Response Composite

In the sensitivity analysis at 24 weeks, the estimated odds of achieving the composite endpoint favored nemolizumab over dupilumab (OR 2.50; 95% CrI 0.63 to 11.30) (Fig. S6). Compared with the base-case analysis at the same time point, the point estimate was larger in magnitude, although uncertainty remained. As in the base-case analysis, comparisons with placebo favored nemolizumab, with higher odds of achieving the composite outcome.

Sensitivity Analyses: Imputation Methods

OC data were prioritized for the base-case analyses. However, for outcomes and time points in which OC data were not available for all trials, sensitivity analyses using alternative imputation approaches (worst observation carried forward, last observation carried forward, or multiple imputation, where available) were conducted (Table S9). These sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness of the base-case findings to assumptions regarding the handling of missing data.

Change from Baseline in PP-NRS

In the sensitivity analysis at 4 weeks, the estimated mean difference in PP-NRS favored nemolizumab over dupilumab (MD − 1.89; 95% CrI − 3.04 to − 0.83) (Fig. S7). This estimate was consistent with the base-case analysis at the same time point (MD − 1.86; 95% CrI − 2.95 to − 0.84) and had a similar magnitude of effect and overlapping CrIs. Comparisons between nemolizumab and placebo showed similar estimates, and there was no clear difference between treatments.

PP-NRS Response (Improvement of ≥ 4 Points)

The sensitivity analysis yielded results broadly aligned with the base-case findings; nemolizumab demonstrated higher odds of PP-NRS response than dupilumab at all assessed time points (Fig. S3). Relative to the base case, effect estimates were typically attenuated, although posterior probabilities continued to favor nemolizumab. As in the base-case analysis, comparisons with placebo consistently showed substantially higher odds of response with nemolizumab across all time points.

IGA Success

Results from the sensitivity analysis were broadly consistent with the base-case findings for IGA success, and nemolizumab demonstrated higher odds of response compared with dupilumab across assessed time points (Fig. S4). Comparisons with placebo continued to favor nemolizumab at all time points, in line with the base-case results.

Change from Baseline in DLQI

In the sensitivity analysis at 4 weeks, the estimated mean difference in DLQI favored nemolizumab over dupilumab (MD − 2.41; 95% CrI − 5.94 to 0.95). This estimate was consistent in direction with the base-case analysis at the same time point (MD − 2.12; 95%: CrI − 5.34 to 1.05) but had a slightly larger magnitude of effect and overlapping CrIs. Comparisons between nemolizumab and placebo also showed similar estimates.

IGA Success and PP-NRS Response Composite

In the sensitivity analysis at 12/16 weeks, nemolizumab demonstrated higher odds of achieving the composite of IGA success and PP-NRS response compared with dupilumab (OR 3.09; 95% CrI 1.06 to 9.53) (Fig. S6). Relative to the base-case analysis, the magnitude and direction of the estimated effect were consistent. Comparisons with placebo continued to favor nemolizumab, and it had substantially higher odds of achieving the composite endpoint.

Discussion

In this NMA, nemolizumab generally demonstrated greater estimated efficacy than dupilumab across most evaluated outcomes and analytic scenarios; however, statistically significant differences were observed only for selected endpoints and time points. In the base-case analyses, nemolizumab showed larger improvements in pruritus-related outcomes, including change from baseline in PP-NRS at 24 weeks and PP-NRS response (≥ 4-point improvement) at 12 weeks, as well as for the composite endpoint of IGA success and PP-NRS response at 12/16 weeks, for which differences were statistically significant. At early time points (4–6 weeks), change from baseline in PP-NRS also demonstrated significant estimated treatment effects favoring nemolizumab.

Across analytic scenarios, concordant findings for change from baseline in PP-NRS and PP-NRS response support the internal consistency of the estimated comparative effects between nemolizumab and dupilumab, although uncertainty remains for several outcomes. Larger mean reductions in PP-NRS with nemolizumab were accompanied by higher probabilities of achieving a clinically meaningful PP-NRS response (≥ 4-point improvement), indicating that estimated benefits were not driven solely by shifts in average scores but reflected a greater proportion of patients experiencing substantial itch relief. This consistency across both early and later time points increases confidence that the estimated treatment effects represent clinically meaningful improvements at the individual patient level rather than isolated statistical differences.

Improvements in dermatology-related quality of life, assessed using DLQI, numerically favored nemolizumab over dupilumab, particularly at 24 weeks. Although uncertainty around the magnitude of effect remained, these findings suggest that improvements in pruritus and disease severity may also translate into meaningful quality-of-life benefits for patients.

Safety profiles were broadly comparable between active treatments (nemolizumab and dupilumab) and placebo in all trials, with high proportions of participants reporting any TEAEs in both active and placebo arms. SAEs were uncommon overall but numerically variable across trials (dupilumab 2.6% to 6.7%; nemolizumab 2.2% to 11.8%), and discontinuations due to AEs were low for dupilumab (0% in the two reported trials) and infrequent but non-zero for nemolizumab (2.2% to 6.3%). While overall TEAEs were evaluated in the NMA, comparative evaluation of individual AEs between treatments was not feasible, and cross-trial comparisons of specific AEs should be interpreted with caution due to differences in study design and reporting practices.

During the feasibility assessment process, TCS/TCI use emerged as a potential effect modifier, leading to a sensitivity analysis for patients without stable TCS/TCI use in dupilumab trials. In these sensitivity analyses, estimated treatment effects generally favored nemolizumab, and there was evidence of a larger effect on PP-NRS response at 12 weeks compared with the base-case analysis. Sensitivity analyses based on alternative imputation assumptions, including non-responder imputation, yielded results broadly consistent with the base-case findings, supporting the robustness of the comparative estimates.

The findings of our analysis are consistent with results from recently published studies. In a recent NMA, nemolizumab showed greatest efficacy in reducing severity of skin lesions [16]. Although the results are consistent, this NMA did not consider any differing analysis methods across the trials or explore differences in patient characteristics that might modify treatment effects.

In clinical practice, the choice between systemic therapies for prurigo nodularis is influenced by a range of patient- and treatment-related factors beyond efficacy and overall safety. These may include comorbid conditions, prior treatment history, route and frequency of administration, and patient preferences. While a detailed evaluation of these factors was beyond the scope of the present analysis, treatment decisions should be individualized, and the findings of this study should be considered alongside broader clinical considerations.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. The NMA was based on a limited number of trials, all of which were placebo-controlled, resulting in an evidence network without closed loops. Consequently, formal assessment of inconsistency between direct and indirect evidence was not possible, and comparative estimates between nemolizumab and dupilumab were derived entirely from indirect comparisons via placebo. Although such comparisons are methodologically valid under the assumption of transitivity, the absence of closed loops increases uncertainty and limits the ability to empirically evaluate this assumption, which should be considered when interpreting comparative treatment effects.

Differences in the timing of the included trials may represent a potential source of heterogeneity. The dupilumab trials were conducted earlier than the nemolizumab trials, raising the possibility of temporal changes in patient populations. However, baseline disease characteristics, including PP-NRS, DLQI, and IGA severity, were broadly comparable across studies. In the nemolizumab trials, recent use of dupilumab was restricted through protocol-defined washout periods prior to enrolment, and prior biologic use was low in both phase 3 trials (OLYMPIA 1 and OLYMPIA 2). Nevertheless, prior treatment history was not reported across all studies, and the potential for unmeasured differences in patient populations cannot be fully excluded.

Differences in outcome assessment timing represent an additional limitation. For the composite endpoint, efficacy data were compared at 12 weeks for dupilumab and 16 weeks for nemolizumab, reflecting differences in data availability across trials. Although this approach enabled inclusion of the maximum available evidence, the misalignment of assessment time points may have influenced the magnitude of the estimated treatment effect and should be considered when interpreting results for this endpoint.

In addition, differences in the availability of early efficacy assessments across trials represent a further limitation. Nemolizumab trials reported outcomes at early time points (e.g., within the 48 h of treatment), whereas comparable data were not available for dupilumab. As a result, comparative analyses of treatment effects at these early time points could not be conducted.

Although all sensitivity analyses were pre-specified, the sensitivity analysis restricted to patients without concomitant topical therapy relied on a subgroup from the dupilumab trials for which baseline patient characteristic data were not reported. As a result, comparability between this subgroup and the nemolizumab OLYMPIA trial population could not be assessed, and differences in unmeasured patient characteristics may have influenced the observed treatment effects.

More broadly, variations in patient populations and trial methods used in the current analysis were noted, potentially influencing NMA results. However, limited data exist on effect modifiers in patients with PN. In the present analysis, only TCS/TCI use was investigated as a potential effect modifier. No additional subgroup data were available, highlighting limitations in evaluating race/ethnicity, PN duration, atopy background, and disease severity as effect modifiers in the PN population. Differences in the time points evaluated, which resulted in the necessity to combine data from different weeks, may have also affected results. These limitations highlight the need for further research, including head-to-head trials or real-world comparative studies, to better characterize treatment effects across patient subgroups.

Conclusion

Overall, the NMA findings suggest that nemolizumab may provide greater improvement than dupilumab for some pruritus and disease severity outcomes in patients with PN while maintaining a comparable safety profile. However, given the indirect nature of the evidence and remaining uncertainty for some endpoints and time points, these findings should be interpreted with appropriate caution.
