 This paper develops the geometric sector of the replication-driven cosmogenesis framework intro-
 duced in Paper I. Starting from a pre-geometric spectral substrate and a minimal set of replication
 axioms, we show how coherent self-replicating units generate a spatial adjacency graph whose con-
 tinuum limit acquires an effective Riemannian structure. The replication dynamics determines a
 characteristic correlation length that seeds the local metric, while overlap relations among coherent
 units produce an isotropic neighborhood geometry with an emergent dimensionality deff ≃ 3 across a
 broad range of replication factors. As replication slows and causal order stabilizes, a limiting signal
 speed c∗ appears, providing the basis for the Lorentzian structure of spacetime without assuming a
 pre-existing light cone. We derive conditions under which the adjacency graph converges to a smooth
 three-dimensional manifold, describe the transition from Euclidean to Lorentzian propagation, and
 identify geometric invariants controlled by the replication parameters. This work establishes the
 geometric and causal layer of the replication cosmogenesis program, bridging the spectral axioms of
 Paper I to the cosmological dynamics explored in Paper III.

 I. INTRODUCTION

 Paper I developed a fully pre-geometric framework in which a finite-energy spectral substrate, defined on a compact
frequency domain Ω = (ωmin , ωmax ), evolves under stochastic dynamics and supports the formation of coherent self-
replicating units. In that framework, time and causal ordering emerged from the correlation structure of spectral
microstates, and replication proceeded in discrete ticks ∆τ governed by the replication rule 1 → n and the axioms
PG1–PG9. No geometric structure—no metric, no topology, no spatial manifold—was introduced at any stage. The
aim of the present paper is to show how geometry, spatial coherence, Lorentz structure, and metric relations arise
naturally and inevitably from the replication dynamics studied in Paper I.
 The conceptual starting point is simple: coherence propagates. A coherent unit formed as a rare spectral fluctuation
replicates into multiple descendants, each of which inherits its spectral band [ω 1 , ω2 ] ⊂ Ω and its internal consistency
conditions. These descendants must remain mutually compatible, and their proliferation is controlled by energy
conservation and by the spectral finiteness of the substrate. As replication proceeds, coherent units form a growing,
partially ordered structure whose causal relations were introduced in Paper I. In this paper, we show that this
structure becomes geometric: a spatially coherent network forms, a limiting propagation speed emerges, and a metric
description becomes both possible and natural.
 Several key features established in Paper I make this development inevitable:

 1. The replication tick ∆τ defines a minimal causal separation between events.

 2. The spectral upper bound ωmax sets a minimum resolvable time interval, ensuring that no process may propagate
 faster than one tick.

 3. The lower bound ωmin sets the longest coherent timescale and therefore the maximal correlation extent induced
 by replication.

 4. The replication rule with effective rate γ(t) produces a deterministic wavefront of coherent influence that ad-
 vances across the substrate.

 These ingredients generate a causal cone structure long before any notion of spatial distance is introduced. In the
early stages of replication, the causal structure is purely temporal and discrete; as the proliferation of coherent units
becomes dense, a coherent connectivity graph emerges. This graph, equipped with the causality relation inherited
from Paper I, admits a continuum limit in which edges correspond to effective spatial relations and causal consistency
imposes a limiting velocity c∗ = L∗ /∆τ associated with the replication front. This c∗ becomes the analogue of the
speed of light in the emergent geometry.

 The main objective of Paper II is to demonstrate how such a geometric interpretation arises and how the resulting
structure reproduces the familiar properties of a Lorentzian spacetime at large scales. The replication-induced causal
graph yields an effective metric, while the spectral properties of coherent units determine characteristic lengths,
correlation scales, and emergent dimensionality. The transition from pre-geometric replication dynamics to emergent
geometry constitutes a discrete-to-continuum correspondence that is analogous in spirit to other emergent-spacetime
approaches [1–3] (causal sets, quantum graphity, and group field theory), but differs fundamentally in that it is driven
by spectral replication rather than by combinatorial postulates or quantized structures.
 This paper is organized as follows. Section II introduces the replication dynamics, the associated ancestry poset,
and the compatibility and adjacency relations that define the kinematical structure of the model. Section III develops
the emergence of coherent connectivity, showing how replication-induced clustering and spectral stability generate
local neighborhoods and establish the conditions under which a continuum limit can arise. Section IV derives a finite
limiting speed for information propagation. Section V constructs the graph metric and its coarse-grained continuum
approximation. Section VI analyzes the ball-growth law and the resulting effective spatial dimension. Section VII
shows that the combination of a limiting speed and a graph-based spatial metric leads naturally to a Lorentzian
structure in the continuum limit. Section VIII summarizes and discusses the implications for cosmology and the
connection to Paper III.
 Paper II thus represents the bridge between the axiomatically defined pre-geometric substrate of Paper I and the
cosmological implications explored in Paper III, establishing that spacetime and its Lorentzian structure arise not as
primitive ingredients but as emergent consequences of replication dynamics in a finite spectral manifold.

 II. EMERGENT COHERENT CONNECTIVITY FROM REPLICATION

 The replication dynamics developed in Paper I produces a growing, partially ordered set of coherent units. Each
coherent unit maintains a stable internal spectral structure supported on a finite band [ω 1 , ω2 ] ⊂ (ωmin , ωmax ) and
replicates in discrete ticks ∆τ . In the pre-geometric setting, units have no spatial location; nonetheless, replication
induces a nontrivial pattern of inheritances, correlations, and compatibility relations. In this section we show that these
relations assemble into a coherent connectivity graph, whose structural properties give rise to an effective geometric
interpretation.

 A. The replication poset

  Let U(t) denote the set of coherent units present after t replication ticks. Each replication event produces n eff (t)
descendants from a single ancestor, and the event time is discretized by ∆τ . Define a binary relation ≺ on coherent
units by

 u≺v iff v was produced, directly or indirectly, from u. (1)

By construction:

 1. ≺ is acyclic (no unit can be its own ancestor),

 2. ≺ is transitive (inheritance is transitive),

 3. ≺ is antisymmetric (u ≺ v and v ≺ u imply u = v),

and therefore (U, ≺) is a partially ordered set (poset). This poset is the pre-geometric analogue of a causal set: it
encodes event ordering without metric structure. All information needed to define emergent geometry will arise from
the structure of this poset and the spectral constraints on the units.

 III. SPECTRAL SATURATION AND THE TRANSITION TO GEOMETRIC SPACE

 In the pre-geometric phase, replication operates entirely within the spectral domain. Each coherent unit occupies
a band [ω1 , ω2 ] and produces n offspring units in disjoint spectral neighborhoods, as required by the self-consistency
condition of PG4A. Replication proceeds as long as the substrate contains sufficient unoccupied coherent bandwidth
within Ω = (ωmin , ωmax ).
 Because Ω is finite, this phase inevitably reaches a point of spectral saturation. At this point the spectral represen-
tation of the dynamical operators E and R can no longer support replication, and a fundamental transition occurs in
which adjacency relations encoded by replication are promoted to spatial relations. The transition proceeds through
five logically necessary steps.

 A. Collapse of coherent spectral density

  Let ρcoh (ω, t) denote the density of coherent domains in the substrate. The randomizer operator R, acting through
stochastic phase diffusion, destroys high-frequency coherence more efficiently than E can recreate it. Thus the available
coherent bandwidth for new seeds shrinks over time:

 ρcoh (ω, t) −→ 0 for unused ω as t → tsat . (8)

Replication becomes impossible once the remaining coherent interval ∆Ωfree (t) falls below the minimal coherent width
∆Ωcoh . The time tsat at which ∆Ωfree = ∆Ωcoh defines the spectral saturation point.

 B. Breakdown of spectral operators

  Before saturation, the excitation and randomizer operators act as mappings on spectral amplitudes,

                                    E[a] : a(ω) 7→ E[a](ω ′ ),      R[a] : a(ω) 7→ R[a](ω ′ ).

At saturation, neither operator can meaningfully act: E cannot generate new coherent seeds because no disjoint
coherent bands remain, and R cannot diffuse phases into already-saturated or decohered regions. Thus the spectral
representation of both operators breaks down. The system must transition to a representation in which replication
and coherence are expressed in a domain other than frequency.

 C. Adjacency as the remaining dynamical structure

  The replication graph G is the only structure that remains well defined at saturation. It is a directed, acyclic,
locally finite graph in which each edge connects a coherent unit to its descendants, ordered by replication tick ∆τ .
Causality, already encoded in the 1 → n replication ordering, thus survives the breakdown of spectral dynamics.
  During spectral saturation, the operators acquire dependence on replication adjacency:

                                         E[a] −→ E[a, G],           R[a] −→ R[a, G].                                   (9)

This is the beginning of the topological phase, in which coherent neighborhoods are defined not by frequency separation
but by connectivity in G.

 V. EMERGENT METRIC STRUCTURE

  The replication graph G(t) introduced in Sec. II carries two independent data sets: (i) a causal partial order induced
by the replication history, and (ii) an undirected adjacency structure encoding compatibility among coherent units. In
the present section we focus exclusively on the second component and construct from it a purely spatial metric. The
temporal and causal aspects of the geometry will be incorporated only in Sec. VI, where the full Lorentzian structure
emerges.

 A. Graph-theoretic spatial neighborhoods

  At each coarse-grained time layer t the coherent units form the vertices of a bounded-degree graph G(t), where
an edge connects two units whose frequency bands overlap sufficiently for spatial compatibility. The bounded-degree
property established in Sec. II E,

that shortest paths are well-defined and that the large-scale structure of G(t) is amenable to coarse-graining.

 E. Interpretation

 The construction above yields a purely spatial geometry originating from the replication-induced adjacency relations
among coherent units. No reference is made to temporal or causal structure, which will be added only in Sec. VI. At

isotropic geometry that sets the stage for the emergence of full spacetime once the causal cones and the limiting
propagation speed c∗ are incorporated.

 VI. DIMENSIONALITY EMERGENCE

 With a spatial metric established through coarse-grained adjacency (Section V), the next step is to determine the
effective spatial dimension of the emergent manifold. In this section we show that the replication rule 1 → n, together
with the overlap-compatibility graph G(t), yields a natural mechanism for selecting a preferred dimension. The
result is robust across variations of microscopic parameters and is consistent with the emergence of an approximately
three-dimensional macroscopic space.

 A. Ball-volume scaling and effective dimension

 B. Replication factor and branching structure

 During the replication phase each coherent unit produces n offspring on average, but compatibility constraints

suppression of exponential branching is the key to dimensional stabilization.
 A heuristic estimate for the ball growth is obtained by treating the adjacency graph as a random bounded-degree
network subject to local compatibility rules [4]. If branching were unconstrained one would have |B k | ∼ nk , i.e. infinite
effective dimension. Compatibility, however, forces many potential edges to be discarded, and the expected growth
becomes

                                                       |Bk | ∼ k ζ(n) ,                                                (31)

where ζ(n) is an increasing function of the replication factor n but saturates once the ball becomes densely packed in
the spectral domain, where no additional distinct compatibility relations can be supported.
Polynomial growth follows from the combination of bounded degree and compatibility constraints, which suppress
exponential branching. These two ingredients ensure that adjacency neighborhoods cannot grow faster than a fixed
polynomial, stabilizing the effective dimension.

 C. Dimensionality selection

  The effective exponent ζ(n) depends on the replication factor n and the spectral bandwidth of coherent states. A
broad class of replication/compatibility models exhibits the following qualitative behavior:

                                                     n=2     ⇒     deff ≲ 1,
                                                   n ≈ 3–4   ⇒     deff ≈ 2,
                                                   n ≈ 5–7   ⇒     deff ≈ 3,                                           (32)

after which the exponent saturates due to the bounded-degree constraint and the finiteness of coherent spectral
overlap. The regime deff ≈ 3 is broad and stable, as increasing n beyond this range does not increase the effective
dimension; instead it produces denser local neighborhoods while preserving three-dimensional large-scale geometry.
 The emergence of deff ≈ 3 is therefore not fine-tuned nor dependent on specific microscopic details. It arises from
the interplay of: (i) replication rate, (ii) bounded overlap compatibility, and (iii) finite coherent bandwidth.

 D. Stability of the emergent dimension

 Once the replication epoch ends, the adjacency graph ceases to grow significantly. The effective dimension becomes
frozen into the coarse-grained spatial manifold M. Fluctuations in n induce only local variations in ball growth and
do not modify deff at large scales. Similarly, stochastic noise in the substrate affects only the microscopic connectivity
but leaves the polynomial scaling (29) unchanged.
 Thus the dimensionality of the emergent geometry is dynamically selected during the replication phase and remains
stable throughout subsequent evolution.
Dimensional Locking. Once replication ceases, the adjacency graph undergoes no further global growth. Ac-
cordingly, the effective dimension deff becomes dynamically locked: local fluctuations may occur, but the large-scale
dimensionality of the manifold is fixed and cannot change under later evolution.

 E. Interpretation

 The spatial dimension is not a fundamental input of the model but a dynamically selected exponent characterizing
the growth of adjacency neighborhoods. The replication factor n controls the density and connectivity of coherent
units, while compatibility constraints suppress exponential growth and produce a natural polynomial scaling. The
resulting spatial dimension deff is robustly near 3 for a wide range of parameters, providing a compelling explanation
for the ubiquity of three-dimensional space.
 In the following section we incorporate the temporal and causal structure to obtain the full Lorentzian geometry
of the emergent spacetime.

 VII. EMERGENT LORENTZIAN STRUCTURE

 Having established a spatial metric (M, d) in Sec. V and an effective spatial dimension d eff ≈ 3 in Sec. VI, we now
incorporate the causal and temporal structure induced by the replication process. We show that the combination
of: (i) an intrinsic causal partial order, (ii) a fundamental replication tick ∆τ , and (iii) a limiting causal speed c ∗ , is
sufficient to endow the emergent manifold with an effective Lorentzian geometry at large scales.

 A. Causal order from replication

  As discussed in Paper I, the replication process provides a discrete causal ordering. If a coherent unit u appears as
a descendant of v, then v is in the causal past of u, which we denote v ≺ u. The transitive closure of this relation
defines a partial order (V, ≺) on the set of coherent units.
  This causal order is compatible with the coarse-grained spatial structure: if v ≺ u and v ̸= u, then necessarily

where d is the coarse spatial metric of Sec. V. The causal partial order and the spatial metric thus coexist without
contradiction.

 B. Event time and the replication tick

  A global event-time parameter is naturally defined by the replication tick. Let τ (v) be the event time of vertex v,
defined as the number of replication layers separating v from the earliest coherent seed.
  The key structural property is that τ (v) is compatible with the causal partial order:

                                                   v≺u       =⇒         τ (v) < τ (u).                                   (34)

The finite replication tick ∆τ therefore acts as the primitive time step at the pre-geometric level.

interpreted as the physical time measured at scales large compared to the replication tick.

 F. Interpretation

 The emergent Lorentzian structure does not require an a priori spacetime continuum. It arises from: (i) a replication-
induced causal partial order, (ii) a coarse-grained spatial metric built from adjacency, and (iii) a limiting causal speed
c∗ fixed by the substrate dynamics. Together these elements define a light-cone structure and a metric with Lorentzian
signature, completing the emergence of spacetime geometry from the pre-geometric substrate.
 In this framework, Lorentz invariance is not fundamental but an emergent large-scale symmetry. Its robustness in
the continuum limit reflects the statistical homogeneity and isotropy of the adjacency graph and the universality of
the limiting speed c∗ . This emergent spacetime provides the geometric setting for the later dynamical evolution of
the Universe, developed in Paper III.

 VIII. SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK

  In this paper we have developed the second stage of the pre-geometric cosmogenic framework introduced in Paper I.
Building on the axioms and stochastic dynamics of the finite spectral substrate, we analyzed the structures that arise
during the finite replication epoch and showed how they give rise to an emergent spatial and subsequently spacetime
geometry. The main results may be summarized as follows:

 1. Spatial adjacency from spectral overlap. Coherent units formed during replication carry finite-bandwidth
 frequency profiles. Overlap compatibility among these profiles generates a bounded-degree adjacency graph.
 This graph provides the structural backbone from which spatial neighborhoods are defined.

 2. Emergent spatial metric. By coarse-graining the adjacency graph, we constructed a spatial distance function
 ℓ(u, v) proportional to the graph-theoretic distance. Under statistical homogeneity and isotropy, this procedure
 yields a spatial metric space (M, d) that approximates a continuous manifold.

 3. Dimensionality selection. The growth of adjacency balls Bk (v) in the frozen post-replication graph follows
 a polynomial scaling k deff over a substantial range of radii. The exponent deff is determined by the replication
 factor and compatibility conditions, and is robustly near 3 for a broad class of models. Thus the spatial dimension
 is an emergent and dynamically selected property rather than a fundamental input.

 4. Emergent Lorentzian structure. Replication induces a causal partial order and a discrete event-time tick.
 Together with the limiting causal speed c∗ , these elements define a light-cone structure on (M, d). The resulting
 spacetime metric has Lorentzian signature, and local patches exhibit approximate Lorentz symmetry. Thus
 relativistic spacetime arises from the large-scale behavior of the replication graph.

  Taken together, these results show that a finite spectral substrate endowed with stochastic fluctuations and a
replication rule 1 → n is sufficient to generate a spatial manifold of effective dimension three and a spacetime with a
Lorentzian causal structure. No background continuum, no assumed metric, and no pre-existing notion of distance
or time is required.
  The present work sets the geometric stage for the dynamical cosmology developed in Paper III. There we analyze:

 • the energy transfer from the substrate to the coherent sector,

 • the freeze-out of replication as the reservoir is depleted,

 • the emergence of vacuum layers and reheating,

 • the potential formation of topological defects or network-like remnants of the ordering transition, in analogy
 with Kibble-type mechanisms and cosmic-string scenarios [5, 6], and their possible observational role, which we
 leave for future work beyond the present trilogy,

 • and the matching to an effective FRW cosmology with observational signatures.

These developments complete the transition from a pre-geometric substrate to a macroscopic cosmological spacetime,
and connect the present framework to potential observational tests.

 A1. Human-driven conceptual direction

  The conceptual assumptions underpinning the model—finite unstructured energy, vibrational substrate, replication
as a necessary instability, and the emergence of time and pre-causality from spectral dynamics—were formulated by
the author. The AI system (OpenAI’s ChatGPT) was used as a research assistant to:

 • reorganize and rephrase draft arguments;

 • propose alternative formulations of definitions and axioms;

 • suggest possible mathematical structures consistent with the author’s stated premises;

 • check logical consistency at the level of narrative structure.

At no stage did the AI system introduce physical assumptions independently of the author. All dynamical rules,
ontological choices, and structural constraints were either specified explicitly by the author or accepted by the author
after inspection and further refinement.

 A2. Multi-threaded exploration

  The development of the present framework occurred over multiple conversational threads, each emphasizing different
aspects of the problem:

 • pre-geometric cosmology and replication-driven dynamics;

 • spectral representations and the role of finite bandwidth;

 • emergent time, causality, and adjacency;

 • vacuum hierarchies and reheating scenarios;

 • philosophical and methodological considerations regarding existence, finiteness, and metastability.

These threads were later consolidated by the author into a coherent sequence of assumptions, axioms, and derived
structures. The AI system assisted primarily in:

 • cross-referencing ideas between threads;

 • highlighting inconsistencies or duplications;

 • suggesting reorganization into the three-paper structure adopted here.

 A3. Memory resets and cold re-assessments

  At several key decision points the author deliberately requested that the AI system “forget” the ongoing working
context and re-evaluate the model from a neutral standpoint. In practice, this meant starting new sessions in which:

 • the AI was given only the latest LaTeX draft or a high-level summary of assumptions;

 • the AI was asked to provide a critical, “cold” assessment of consistency, originality, and potential weaknesses;

 • over-interpretations or premature claims were explicitly discouraged.

These resets were used as a form of methodological self-check, allowing the author to compare internal expectations
with an external critique produced from the same tool that had helped shape earlier drafts.

 A4. Iterative refinement cycles

  The writing process proceeded in iterative cycles:

 1. The author proposed new axioms, structural ideas, or informal arguments.

 2. The AI system returned a structured version, often in LaTeX, linking the new ideas to existing sections.

 3. The author then corrected, pruned, or rejected parts of the AI output, restoring physical intent where it had
 been distorted and removing unnecessary complications.

 4. The cycle was repeated until the author was satisfied that the resulting section reflected the intended conceptual
 content.

In this sense, the AI functioned as a high-bandwidth editor and organizer rather than as an originator of scientific
hypotheses.

 A5. Limitations of the methodology

  Several limitations of this methodology should be stated explicitly:

 • The AI system has no access to experimental data, numerical simulations, or independent mathematical proofs;

 • The speculative nature of the present model is entirely due to its physical content, not to the use of AI tools.
 The author is solely responsible for the scientific plausibility and coherence of the framework.

 • The methodology does not replace traditional peer review or independent replication by other researchers. It is
 intended as an experiment in AI-assisted conceptual development, not as a template for establishing definitive
 physical theories.
