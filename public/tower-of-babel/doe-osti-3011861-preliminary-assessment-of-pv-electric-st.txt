 Analysis Overview

• NREL used the REopt® platform to evaluate the
 techno-economic potential of adding solar PV and
 electric storage at the Energy Coordinating Agency
 (ECA) in Philadelphia, PA.

• The site has access to 600 PV panels (rated at 305 W
 each) and would like to evaluate the potential of
 pairing them with battery storage to offset electricity
 costs, increase renewable energy usage, and establish
 an onsite microgrid.

 Conclusion
• The cost optimal PV capacity for the site is 28.7 kW, with 7 kW connected behind the first-floor meter and
  21.7 kW connected behind the second-floor meter. Battery energy storage is not cost-effective for grid-
  connected operation.
 o Installing the cost optimal system configuration results in a net present value of just under $6,000
 (the equivalent of a 1.6% reduction in total lifecycle costs) over the 15-year analysis period.

• Installing all 183 kW of the donated PV panels behind the second-floor meter results in a net cost to the site
  of approximately $123,000 over the analysis period, a total lifecycle cost increase of 34%.
 o Splitting the PV connection by floor based on the fraction of total energy consumption (46 kW for
 Floor 1 and 137 kW for Floor 2) reduces the net cost to the site over the analysis period to $93,000.

• Adding an 8-hour 0.5 MW or 1 MW battery to support the site in operating as a microgrid is estimated to
  cost $1.6M and $3.3M, respectively. This does not include the cost of upgrading/adding any electrical
  equipment at the building that may be necessary for the building to operate as an islanded microgrid.
 o Splitting the PV connection by floor based on the fraction of total energy consumption and connecting
 the battery behind the second-floor meter can lower lifecycle costs by 0.7 - 1.1% compared to
 connecting the full PV capacity and battery to the second-floor circuit only.
