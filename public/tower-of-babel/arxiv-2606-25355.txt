Abstract




Abstract.
We tackle the challenging problem of stochastic weakly convex optimization subject to mixed (equality and inequality) expected-value constraints. While optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity algorithms exist for unconstrained weakly convex problems, dealing with complex functional constraints typically requires cumbersome multi-loop penalty or augmented Lagrangian methods, which suffer from high inner-loop complexity and sensitive parameter tuning. To bridge this fundamental gap, we propose the primal-dual Halpern-PAGE (PD-HP) algorithm. As a purely single-loop method, PD-HP completely bypasses the computational burden of nested iterations. At each step, it merely requires solving a simple strongly convex surrogate subproblem alongside a straightforward dual projection, making it exceptionally efficient and convenient to implement. Crucially, we prove that this computationally lightweight algorithm achieves the optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity for mixed-constrained stochastic weakly convex problems, successfully matching the theoretical lower bounds.
Furthermore, when the primal domain is a compact polyhedral convex set, we establish the deterministic stability of the dual multipliers by exploiting the generalized Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ) alongside Hoffman’s error bound. This ensures that our optimal complexity bound holds strictly under the standard, unbounded KKT residual metric without any theoretical gaps or artificial residual truncations.

Key words.
Stochastic optimization, weakly convex programming, primal-dual method, variance reduction, Halpern iteration, sample complexity, Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification.

Introduction

Stochastic weakly convex optimization has recently emerged as a central paradigm in modern computational mathematics and machine learning. It offers a unified framework to model problems that are globally nonconvex yet exhibit structured non-smoothness or curvature bounded strictly below by a quadratic function. Such formulations are ubiquitous across diverse applications, including robust statistical learning, phase retrieval, and orthogonal dictionary learning.

Despite the widespread success of stochastic algorithms in unconstrained environments, incorporating complex, data-driven functional constraints (both equalities and inequalities) into weakly convex optimization remains a formidable challenge. Recently, several pivotal works have tackled this constrained setting from various methodological angles. For instance, Jin and Wang
[
12
]
established iteration and sample complexities for a primal-dual algorithm handling inequality constraints. Curtis et al.
[
4
]
proved worst-case complexity bounds for an SQP method addressing deterministic nonlinear equalities. Meanwhile, a dominant class of approaches has heavily relied on penalty or augmented Lagrangian methods (ALM). Boob et al.
[
2
]
developed a double-loop algorithm for nonconvex constrained problems to find an
(\epsilon,\delta)
-KKT point. Boob et al.
[
3
]
develops stochastic level constrained type methods for solving stochastic problems with composite functions. Similarly, Li et al.
[
14
]
designed inexact ALMs for expectation equality constraints, and Shi et al.
[
22
]
recently proposed a momentum-based linearized ALM for problems featuring mixed constraints.

While these contributions have significantly advanced the field, the existing literature still exhibits two major pain points, particularly regarding practical algorithmic efficiency and optimal sample complexity. The first pain point lies in the computational burden of multi-loop architectures. As evidenced by the ALM and penalty-based methods mentioned above
[
2
,
3
,
14
]
, functionally constrained stochastic optimization traditionally demands nested double-loop structures: an outer loop to iteratively update penalty parameters or Lagrangian multipliers, and an inner loop to solve the penalized surrogate subproblems to a prescribed precision. This nested architecture not only introduces tremendous computational overhead but also necessitates delicate, ad-hoc tuning of penalty parameters, rendering them highly inefficient for large-scale practical applications.

The second pain point is the persistent gap in optimal sample complexity. For unconstrained or simply constrained stochastic weakly convex optimization, the foundational work by Davis and Drusvyatskiy
[
5
]
established the Moreau envelope as the standard stationarity metric and proved that standard stochastic methods achieve a sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})
. Subsequent breakthroughs in variance reduction techniques, such as SPIDER
[
8
]
and PAGE
[
13
]
, successfully pushed this rate to the theoretical lower bound of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})

[
1
]
. However, when transitioning to the mixed-constrained setting, attempting to bypass double-loop structures by deploying standard single-loop stochastic gradient descent ascent (SGDA) algorithms leads to severe trajectory oscillations due to the lack of strong monotonicity. This instability effectively degrades the sample complexity back to a highly suboptimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})

[
16
,
19
]
.

A fundamental question naturally arises:
Can we design a single-loop, computationally lightweight algorithm for mixed-constrained stochastic weakly convex problems that strictly matches the optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity?

In this paper, we answer the above question affirmatively by proposing the Primal-Dual Halpern-PAGE (PD-HP) algorithm. Our algorithmic design is motivated by the elegant integration of two powerful mathematical mechanisms:

•

Halpern Acceleration:
The weak convexity of objectives and constraints yields a KKT operator that is only weakly monotone. To address this, we draw upon Halpern acceleration for fixed-point and maximal monotone inclusion problems
[
10
,
6
,
15
]
by injecting an anchoring sequence into the primal-dual updates. Acting as a global regularizer, this mechanism anchors the trajectory to prevent divergence, thereby circumventing the heavy computational burden associated with standard proximal point evaluations.

•

PAGE Variance Reduction:
To shatter the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})
complexity barrier, we equip our algorithm with the PAGE mechanism
[
13
]
. By intelligently alternating between infrequent large-batch exact gradient evaluations and frequent, computationally cheap small-batch recursive updates, PAGE provides an optimal Markovian variance reduction that is crucial for stabilizing the descent trajectory in high-noise environments.

The main contributions of this paper are explicitly summarized as follows:

(a)

A Novel Purely Single-Loop Algorithm:
We develop PD-HP, a fully single-loop primal-dual algorithm for mixed-constrained stochastic weakly convex problems. The subproblems strictly involve a simple strongly convex surrogate minimization for the primal variable and a straightforward thresholding projection for the dual multipliers, completely bypassing the computational burden of nested inner loops and heuristic penalty tuning.

(b)

Optimal Sample Complexity:
We rigorously prove that PD-HP achieves an expected sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
to find an
\epsilon
-approximate KKT point. This optimally matches the theoretical lower bound of the unconstrained setting, thereby completely closing the sample complexity gap for mixed-constrained weakly convex stochastic optimization.

(c)

Exact Geometric Equivalence and Unbounded KKT Guarantees:
A prevalent limitation in existing literature is the reliance on artificially truncated residuals to evaluate convergence. We overcome this by rigorously exploiting the generalized Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ) alongside Hoffman’s Lemma for polyhedral domains. By establishing an exact geometric equivalence–with a condition number of precisely
\kappa=1
–between the Moreau envelope gradient and the restricted KKT mapping, and by proving the deterministic stability of the dual multipliers, we strictly validate our optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
complexity under the standard, physically meaningful
unbounded
KKT residual metric with high probability.

Organization of the paper.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 formally defines the mixed-constrained problem, establishes the uniform multiplier bounds via the Generalized MFCQ, and presents the proposed PD-HP algorithm. Section 3 provides the rigorous complexity analysis, establishing the optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity under both restricted and unbounded KKT metrics. Section 4 presents numerical experiments that empirically validate our theoretical claims. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper and discusses future research directions.

2
Problem Formulation, Preliminaries and the Algorithm

Before diving into the algorithmic design, it is essential to formally define the constrained stochastic optimization problem and establish the geometric and statistical structures of the underlying KKT operator. In this section, we present the core assumptions regarding the weak convexity and smoothness of the problem functions. Furthermore, by utilizing the generalized Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ), we systematically establish the uniform global bounds for the exact dual multipliers, which serves as the theoretical cornerstone for our single-loop algorithm.

The constrained stochastic optimization problem considered in this paper is of the form

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{x\in X_{0}}&f_{0}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[f_{0}(x,\xi)]\\
\text{s.t.}&g_{i}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[g_{i}(x,\xi)]\leq 0,\quad i=1,\dots,m,\\[5.0pt]
&h_{j}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[h_{j}(x,\xi)]=0,\quad j=1,\dots,p,\end{array}

(2.1)

where
X_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact convex set. We denote
g(x)=(g_{1}(x),\dots,g_{m}(x))^{T}
and
h(x)=(h_{1}(x),\dots,h_{p}(x))^{T}
.

The following assumptions are the main conditions for problem functions.

Assumption 1
.

For almost every realization of the random variable
\xi
, the stochastic functions
f_{0}(x,\xi)
,
g_{i}(x,\xi)
,
h_{j}(x,\xi)
, and
-h_{j}(x,\xi)
are
\rho
-weakly convex and possess
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradients on the compact convex set
X_{0}
.

Assumption 2
.

There exist random variables
L_{f}(\xi)
,
L_{g}(\xi)
,
L_{h}(\xi)
,
G_{g}(\xi)
, and
G_{h}(\xi)
with bounded second moments such that for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in X_{0}
and almost every
\xi
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\|\nabla f_{0}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla f_{0}(x_{2},\xi)\|&\leq L_{f}(\xi)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|,\\[4.0pt]
\|\nabla g_{i}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi)\|&\leq L_{g}(\xi)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|,\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m,\\[4.0pt]
\|\nabla h_{j}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi)\|&\leq L_{h}(\xi)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|,\quad\forall j=1,\dots,p,\\[4.0pt]
\|g_{i}(x,\xi)\|&\leq G_{g}(\xi),\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m,\\[4.0pt]
\|h_{j}(x,\xi)\|&\leq G_{h}(\xi),\quad\forall j=1,\dots,p.\end{array}

Before formally introducing the bounded variance assumptions, it is imperative to define the underlying stochastic framework. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a standard probability space. We define a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{t}
represents the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all random events (i.e., stochastic sample batches) up to iteration
t
. We strictly assume that the algorithmic state
z_{t}=(x_{t},\lambda_{t},\mu_{t})
evaluated at iteration
t
is completely determined by the algorithmic history and is therefore
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable.

With this filtration defined, we formalize the statistical properties of the stochastic oracle as follows.

Assumption 3
.

Given the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
, the single-sample stochastic estimators for the objective gradient, constraint values, and constraint gradients evaluated at any
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable state
x\in X_{0}
are unbiased conditionally on the history. Specifically,
\mathbb{E}[\nabla f_{0}(x,\xi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\nabla f_{0}(x)
, and similarly for
g_{i}
and
h_{j}
. Furthermore, the single-sample stochastic estimators for the objective gradient, constraint values, and constraint gradients are unbiased and have bounded variance. Specifically, there exist constants
\sigma_{f}^{2}
,
\sigma_{g}^{2}
,
\sigma_{h}^{2}
,
\sigma_{\nabla g}^{2}
, and
\sigma_{\nabla h}^{2}
such that for any
x\in X_{0}
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_{0}(x,\xi)-\nabla f_{0}(x)\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]&\leq\sigma_{f}^{2},\\[4.0pt]
\mathbb{E}[\|g_{i}(x,\xi)-g_{i}(x)\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]&\leq\sigma_{g}^{2},\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m,\\[4.0pt]
\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla g_{i}(x,\xi)-\nabla g_{i}(x)\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]&\leq\sigma_{\nabla g}^{2},\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m,\\[4.0pt]
\mathbb{E}[\|h_{j}(x,\xi)-h_{j}(x)\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]&\leq\sigma_{h}^{2},\quad\forall j=1,\dots,p,\\[4.0pt]
\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla h_{j}(x,\xi)-\nabla h_{j}(x)\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]&\leq\sigma_{\nabla h}^{2},\quad\forall j=1,\dots,p.\end{array}

To ensure that the dual variables for both inequality and equality constraints remain within a bounded domain throughout the iterations, we extend the constraint qualification to the mixed-constrained feasible region.

Definition 2.1

(Generalized MFCQ)
.

The generalized Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ) holds at
x\in X_{0}
if the gradients of the equality constraints
\{\nabla h_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{p}
are linearly independent, and there exists a vector
d\in\mathcal{T}_{X_{0}}(x)
such that:

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h_{j}(x),d\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall j=1,\dots,p,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla g_{i}(x),d\rangle

\displaystyle<0,\quad\forall i\in\{k:g_{k}(x)=0\}.

(2.2)

In our analysis, we require the following regularity condition to hold uniformly:

Assumption 4

(Global generalized MFCQ)
.

The Generalized MFCQ holds for every
x\in X_{0}
.

Remark 2.1
.

In Subsection
3.2
,
we explicitly require the compact primal domain
X_{0}
to be a polyhedral convex set. This geometric assumption is highly practical and standard in machine learning and operations research, naturally covering ubiquitous constraints such as box constraints (upper and lower bounds), probability simplexes, and finite linear inequalities. Crucially, this polyhedral structure is fundamentally necessary. It guarantees that the normal cone
\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}}(\cdot)
is polyhedral, which subsequently allows us to invoke the classical Hoffman’s Lemma to establish the exact deterministic stability of the dual multipliers in our theoretical analysis.

Under Assumption
4
, we can rigorously establish the existence of a uniform bound for both sets of Lagrange multipliers.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose Assumption
4
holds and
X_{0}
is compact. Let
\mathcal{X}_{KKT}\subseteq X_{0}
be the set of all primal stationary points that satisfy the KKT conditions for the mixed-constrained problem. If
\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
is non-empty, then the union of all corresponding Lagrange multipliers,
\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{KKT}}\mathcal{M}(x)
, is bounded. That is, there exist global bounds
\lambda_{\max}>0
and
\mu_{\max}>0
such that for any stationary point
x\in X_{0}
, its corresponding multipliers satisfy
\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{\max}
and
\|\mu\|_{\infty}\leq\mu_{\max}
.

Proof.

By definition, the joint multiplier set
\mathcal{M}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
is non-empty if and only if
x\in\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
.

First, we establish the compactness of the stationarity set
\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
. Due to the continuous differentiability of the objective
f_{0}(x)
and the constraint functions
g_{i}(x)
and
h_{j}(x)
(implied by the
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradients), the KKT residual mapping is continuous. Since
X_{0}
is a compact set, the pre-image of the zero residual, which defines
\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
, is a closed subset of the compact set
X_{0}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
itself is compact.

For any stationary point
x\in\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
, Assumption
4
guarantees that the generalized Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ) holds. A fundamental result in variational analysis and parametric optimization (e.g., Gauvin, 1977
[
9
]
) ensures that under the generalized MFCQ at a point
x
, the associated set of Lagrange multipliers
\mathcal{M}(x)
for both equality and inequality constraints is a non-empty, compact, and convex polyhedron.

Furthermore, the set-valued multiplier mapping
x\rightrightarrows\mathcal{M}(x)
is locally bounded (and outer semicontinuous, see
[
21
]
) around any point where the generalized MFCQ holds. This implies that for each
x\in\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
, there exists an open neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{x}
around
x
and a finite scalar constant
C_{x}>0
such that for any
y\in\mathcal{N}_{x}\cap\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
, all multipliers
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{M}(y)
are strictly bounded in norm by
C_{x}
, meaning
\|(\lambda,\mu)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{x}
.

The collection of these open neighborhoods
\{\mathcal{N}_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{KKT}}
naturally forms an open cover of the compact set
\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
. By the Heine-Borel Finite Covering Theorem, there exists a finite subcover
\{\mathcal{N}_{x_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{K}
that fully covers
\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
.

We can then define the global uniform bound as:

C_{\max}=\max_{k\in\{1,\dots,K\}}C_{x_{k}}.

Because the subcover is finite, this maximum is finite and strictly positive. By letting
\lambda_{\max}=C_{\max}
and
\mu_{\max}=C_{\max}
, it is guaranteed that for any stationary point
x\in\mathcal{X}_{KKT}
across the entire domain, every corresponding set of Lagrange multipliers
(\lambda,\mu)
satisfies:

\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{\max}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mu\|_{\infty}\leq\mu_{\max}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
z=(x,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{Z}=X_{0}\times\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}
be the primal-dual joint variable. The single-sample KKT operator for the mixed-constrained problem is defined as:

F(z,\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,\lambda,\mu,\xi)\\
-g(x,\xi)\\
-h(x,\xi)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla f_{0}(x,\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x,\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x,\xi)\\
-g(x,\xi)\\
-h(x,\xi)\end{pmatrix}.

(2.3)

and the KKT operator for the mixed-constrained problem as:

F(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,\lambda,\mu)^{T}\\
-g(x)^{T}\\
-h(x)^{T}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.4)

Based on the uniform bound in Lemma
2.1
, we define the joint dual variable
y=(\lambda,\mu)
and restrict the dual search space to the compact convex set
\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}=\Lambda_{\delta}\times M_{\delta}
, where

\Lambda_{\delta}:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}:\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{\max}+\delta\right\},\quad M_{\delta}:=\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\|\mu\|_{\infty}\leq\mu_{\max}+\delta\right\},\quad\delta>0.

(2.5)

Define

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(z-\mathcal{P}_{{\cal Z}}(z-\alpha F(z))\right),

(2.6)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\cdot)
denotes the Euclidean projection onto the compact set
\mathcal{Z}
.
The primal component and the dual component of the restricted KKT gradient mapping, denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(x,y)
and
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,y}(x,y)
, respectively are expressed as

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,X}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(x-\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}\big(x-\alpha\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,y)\big)\right)

(2.7)

and

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,y}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(y-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}\big(y+\alpha\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}(x,y)\big)\right),

(2.8)

where
\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}(\cdot)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}(\cdot)
denote the Euclidean projections onto the compact set
X_{0}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}
,respectively.
Building upon the global uniform boundedness of the multipliers, we must rigorously bound the deviation of any algorithmic dual estimate. While the generalized MFCQ guarantees the metric regularity of the
constraint mapping
, it does not necessarily imply the metric regularity of the full non-linear KKT system without second-order assumptions, see standard results in parametric variational analysis (e.g., Robinson’s metric regularity theorem
[
20
,
7
]
). However, when
X_{0}
is polyhedral convex, by exploiting the linear structure of the dual space, we can establish a uniform error bound using Hoffman’s Lemma.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose Assumption
4
(global generalized MFCQ) holds, and
X_{0}
is a compact polyhedral convex set. For any evaluated primal-dual state
z=(x,y)\in\mathcal{Z}
, let
\hat{x}_{+}=\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}(x-\alpha\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,y))
be its exact proximal update. Then, there exists a uniform global condition number
\kappa_{y}^{*}>0
such that the Euclidean distance from the current multiplier
y
to the exact multiplier set associated with
\hat{x}_{+}
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\hat{x}_{+})
, is strictly bounded by the magnitude of the primal gradient mapping:

\text{dist}\big(y,\mathcal{M}(\hat{x}_{+})\big)\leq\kappa_{y}^{*}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)\|.

(2.9)

Consequently, since the primal residual is naturally bounded by the joint mapping (
\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)\|\leq\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|
), the total dual deviation is bounded by
\kappa_{y}^{*}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|
.

Proof.

For any fixed primal point
u\in X_{0}
, the exact KKT stationarity condition is defined by the inclusion:

-\nabla f_{0}(u)\in\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(u)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(u)+\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}}(u).

(2.10)

Crucially, because
X_{0}
is a polyhedral convex set, the gradients
\nabla g_{i}(u)
and
\nabla h_{j}(u)
are deterministic, fixed vectors for a given
u
, the stationarity condition (
2.10
) constitutes a
linear
generalized equation system with respect to the dual variables
y=(\lambda,\mu)
. Consequently, the set of multipliers satisfying this condition,
\mathcal{M}(u)
, is a convex polyhedron.

By Assumption
4
, the generalized MFCQ holds globally on the compact polyhedral convex set
X_{0}
. According to Gauvin’s Theorem (1977), the MFCQ guarantees that the multiplier polyhedron
\mathcal{M}(u)
is bounded, and the mapping
u\mapsto\mathcal{M}(u)
is uniformly bounded across the domain.

Because the system is linear in
y
, the classical Hoffman’s Lemma (1952)
[
11
]
applies. It establishes that the Euclidean distance from any point
y
to the polyhedron
\mathcal{M}(\hat{x}_{+})
is bounded by a constant times the residual of the linear system. In our algorithmic framework, the residual of the primal stationarity condition evaluated at the updated point
\hat{x}_{+}
is exactly quantified by the primal gradient mapping. Specifically, the variational geometry of the projection yields
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,y)\in\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}}(\hat{x}_{+})
, ensuring the stationarity violation is proportional to
\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)\|
.

Thus, for a specific point
\hat{x}_{+}
, there exists a local Hoffman constant
\kappa(\hat{x}_{+})>0
such that:

\text{dist}\big(y,\mathcal{M}(\hat{x}_{+})\big)\leq\kappa(\hat{x}_{+})\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)\|.

(2.11)

To globalize this bound, we analyze the structural properties of the constraint Jacobian matrix
J(x)=[\nabla g(x),\nabla h(x)]
. The Hoffman constant
\kappa(x)
for the linear system depends inversely on the smallest non-zero singular values of the active submatrices of
J(x)^{T}
. Under the global MFCQ and the continuous differentiability of the constraints (Assumption
4
), these singular values are uniformly bounded away from zero. A fundamental result in parametric linear programming ensures that the Hoffman constant mapping
x\mapsto\kappa(x)
is upper semicontinuous.

Since
X_{0}
is a compact set, the upper semicontinuous function
\kappa(x)
achieves a finite global maximum over the domain. We define the uniform global dual condition number as:

\kappa_{y}^{*}:=\max_{x\in X_{0}}\kappa(x)<\infty.

(2.12)

Substituting
\kappa_{y}^{*}
into the local bound yields (
2.9
). The final conclusion follows trivially from the norm inequality
\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)\|\leq\sqrt{\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,y}(z)\|^{2}}=\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|
.
∎

We use Assumption
2
and Assumption
3

to establish the theoretical properties of the stochastic operator.

Proposition 2.2
.

Under the assumptions
2
,
3
and
4
, the stochastic KKT operator
F(z,\xi)
satisfies expected
L
-smoothness and bounded variance on
\mathcal{Z}

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi)\|^{2}]\leq L^{2}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|^{2},\quad\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|F(z,\xi)-F(z)\|^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2},\,\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z},

(Weak monotonicity of the single-sample KKT operator)
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
and
4
, for almost every realization of
\xi
, the single-sample stochastic KKT operator
F(z,\xi)
is
\overline{\rho}(\xi)
-weakly monotone on the compact set

. That is, for all

Corollary 2.1

(Expected weak monotonicity of the KKT operator)
.

Under Assumptions
1
,
2
and
4
, the expected KKT operator
F(z)=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[F(z,\xi)]
is
\overline{\rho}
-weakly monotone on the compact set
\mathcal{Z}
. That is, for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}
:

\langle F(z_{1})-F(z_{2}),z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle\geq-\overline{\rho}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|^{2}

where the deterministic weak monotonicity parameter
\overline{\rho}
is explicitly given by the expectation of
\overline{\rho}(\xi)
:

\overline{\rho}=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\overline{\rho}(\xi)]=\rho(1+m\lambda_{\max}+p\mu_{\max})+\frac{m\lambda_{\max}\mathbb{E}[L_{g}(\xi)]+p\mu_{\max}\mathbb{E}[L_{h}(\xi)]}{2}.

Proof.

By the linearity of the inner product and the expectation operator, we can take the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\cdot]
on both sides of the inequality established in the preceding theorem.
∎

3
Complexity Analysis of PD-HP

Having established the PD-HP algorithmic framework and the fundamental geometric properties of the stochastic KKT operator, we now present the main theoretical contribution of this paper: the rigorous complexity analysis. In this section, we will systematically bound the variance tracking error, establish a strict global descent property via a carefully constructed augmented merit function, and ultimately prove the optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity. The analysis is presented in progressive stages, culminating in the high-probability guarantees under the physically meaningful, unbounded KKT metric.

3.1
Sample complexity analysis

Lemma 3.1

(Variance bound)
.

Let Assumptions
1
–
3
be satisfied. Then the PAGE variance tracking error satisfies

\mathbb{E}\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}\leq(1-p)\mathbb{E}\|\delta_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{L^{2}}{b}\mathbb{E}\|z_{t+1}-z_{t}\|^{2}+p\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}.

(3.1)

Proof.

The variance tracking error at step
t
is defined as
\delta_{t}=v_{t}-F(z_{t})
, where
v_{t}=(v_{t}^{x},-v_{t}^{g},-v_{t}^{h})
is the stochastic estimator of
F(z_{t})
constructed by the PAGE mechanism.

Based on Proposition
2.2
, the single-sample KKT operator satisfies expected
L
-smoothness and bounded variance on
\mathcal{Z}
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi)\|^{2}]&\leq L^{2}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|^{2},\\[5.0pt]
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|F(z,\xi)-F(z)\|^{2}]&\leq\sigma^{2}.\end{array}

(3.2)

According to the PAGE algorithm, the update of the estimator
v_{t+1}
depends on a uniform random variable
u_{t}\sim\text{Uniform}(0,1)
. We analyze the expected tracking error by conditioning on the two possible events.

Case 1: Event
E_{1}
occurs (with probability
p
).

The algorithm computes a large batch
\mathcal{B}
of size
B
from scratch. The estimator
v_{t+1}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}F(z_{t+1},\xi)
is an unbiased estimator of
F(z_{t+1})
. Because the samples are drawn independently, the variance is scaled down by the batch size
B
. Using the uniform variance bound in (
3.2
), we have:

\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}\mid E_{1}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{B}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}F(z_{t+1},\xi)-F(z_{t+1})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}.

(3.3)

Case 2: Event
E_{2}
occurs (with probability
1-p
).

The algorithm samples a small batch
\mathcal{S}
of size
b
and performs a recursive update. The tracking error
\delta_{t+1}
can be algebraically decomposed as:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta_{t+1}&=v_{t}+\frac{1}{b}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{S}}\left(F(z_{t+1},\xi)-F(z_{t},\xi)\right)-F(z_{t+1})\\[5.0pt]
&=\underbrace{(v_{t}-F(z_{t}))}_{\delta_{t}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{b}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{S}}\left(F(z_{t+1},\xi)-F(z_{t},\xi)\right)-(F(z_{t+1})-F(z_{t}))}_{\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}}.\end{array}

Since the samples in the small batch
\mathcal{S}
are drawn completely independently of the past trajectory, the expected value of the increment is zero, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}]=0
. Furthermore, the previous error
\delta_{t}
is independent of the new batch
\mathcal{S}
chosen at step
t+1
. Therefore, the inner product cross-term vanishes when taking the expectation:

\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}\mid E_{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t}\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}\|^{2}].

To bound the variance of the increment
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}
, we utilize the standard identity
\text{Var}(X)\leq\mathbb{E}[\|X\|^{2}]
and the
L
-smoothness condition in (
3.2
). Because the
b
samples in
\mathcal{S}
are independent:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\|\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}\|^{2}]&=\frac{1}{b}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\left\|(F(z_{t+1},\xi)-F(z_{t},\xi))-(F(z_{t+1})-F(z_{t}))\right\|^{2}\right]\\[5.0pt]
&\leq\frac{1}{b}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\|F(z_{t+1},\xi)-F(z_{t},\xi)\|^{2}\right]\\[5.0pt]
&\leq\frac{L^{2}}{b}\mathbb{E}\|z_{t+1}-z_{t}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Substituting this back, we obtain the bound for the second case:

\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}\mid E_{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t}\|^{2}]+\frac{L^{2}}{b}\mathbb{E}\|z_{t+1}-z_{t}\|^{2}.

(3.4)

Total Expectation:
We compute the total unconditional expectation by weighting Case 1 and Case 2 by their respective probabilities:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}]&=p\cdot\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}\mid E_{1}]+(1-p)\cdot\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}\mid E_{2}]\\[5.0pt]
&\leq p\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}\right)+(1-p)\left(\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t}\|^{2}]+\frac{L^{2}}{b}\mathbb{E}\|z_{t+1}-z_{t}\|^{2}\right)\\[5.0pt]
&=(1-p)\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t}\|^{2}]+\frac{(1-p)L^{2}}{b}\mathbb{E}\|z_{t+1}-z_{t}\|^{2}+p\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}.\end{array}

Since the probability parameter
p\in(0,1)
, we have
1-p<1
. To simplify the recurrence relation (which is standard practice to cleanly establish the contraction in the Lyapunov analysis), we validly relax the coefficient of the distance term to
1
:

\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}]\leq(1-p)\mathbb{E}[\|\delta_{t}\|^{2}]+\frac{L^{2}}{b}\mathbb{E}\|z_{t+1}-z_{t}\|^{2}+p\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}.

This completes the proof.
∎

To rigorously analyze the convergence without suffering from the distance expansion induced by the weakly monotone KKT operator, we shift our evaluation metric to the primal-dual merit function. Let the primal exact penalty function be

V(x)=\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(x,y).

Since
V(x)
preserves the
\rho
-weak convexity, we define its Moreau envelope as

\phi_{\gamma}(x)=\min_{z\in X_{0}}\left\{V(z)+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|z-x\|^{2}\right\}

with parameter
\gamma<1/\rho
.

Let
\hat{x}_{t}=\arg\min_{z\in X_{0}}\{V(z)+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|z-x_{t}\|^{2}\}
be the proximal point, ensuring
\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})=\frac{1}{\gamma}(x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t})
. We define the corresponding optimal dual multiplier as

\hat{y}_{t}\in\arg\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{t},y).

We establish the global convergence based on the unified Augmented Merit Function evaluated at state
z_{t}=(x_{t},y_{t})
:

\mathcal{W}_{t}:=\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})+\frac{c_{y}}{\alpha}\|y_{t}-y_{t-1}\|^{2}+c_{\delta}\mathbb{E}\|\delta_{t}\|^{2},

(3.5)

where
c_{y},c_{\delta}>0
are explicit tracking constants.

To handle the structural analysis, we first evaluate the intermediate state
\tilde{z}_{t+1}=(\tilde{x}_{t+1},\tilde{y}_{t+1})
generated by the pure variance-reduced SGDA step before the Halpern anchoring is applied.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let the intermediate augmented merit function evaluated at
\tilde{z}_{t+1}
be defined as
\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}:=\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})+\frac{c_{y}}{\alpha}\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}+c_{\delta}\mathbb{E}\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}
. Under Assumptions
1
-
4
, for a sufficiently small constant step size
\alpha\leq 1/L_{\phi}
, the unanchored update provides a strict global descent:

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t}]-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}.

(3.6)

Proof.

Since the Moreau envelope
\phi_{\gamma}(x)
is
L_{\phi}
-smooth (
L_{\phi}\leq\gamma^{-1}
), applying the standard descent lemma for the primal update
\tilde{x}_{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}(x_{t}-\alpha v_{t}^{x})
at
x_{t}
:

\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})]+\mathbb{E}\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t}),\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\rangle+\frac{L_{\phi}}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}

(3.7)

To process the first-order cross term
\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t}),\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\rangle
in the inequality above, we define the gradient mapping as
G_{t}=\frac{1}{\alpha}(x_{t}-\tilde{x}_{t+1})
.
This implies
\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}=-\alpha G_{t}
. Substituting this into the cross term yields:

\mathbb{E}\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t}),\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\rangle=-\alpha\mathbb{E}\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t}),G_{t}\rangle

(3.8)

Next, we introduce a fundamental algebraic identity:
2\langle a,b\rangle=\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}-\|a-b\|^{2}
. Letting
a=\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})
and
b=G_{t}
, we can perfectly decompose the term:

-\alpha\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t}),G_{t}\rangle=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|G_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})-G_{t}\|^{2}

(3.9)

Since
\|G_{t}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\|\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}
by definition, we substitute it into the middle term,

-\alpha\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t}),G_{t}\rangle=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})-G_{t}\|^{2}

Substituting the decomposed terms above back into (
3.8
), we obtain the intermediate complete state:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})]

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})-G_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{L_{\phi}}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}

From the definition of
G_{t}
and
\tilde{x}_{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}(x_{t}-\alpha v_{t}^{x})
, we have

\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})-G_{t}\|^{2}\leq\alpha\mathbb{E}\|v_{t}^{x}-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{t},\hat{y}_{t})\|^{2}

and the condition
\alpha<1/L_{\phi}
yields

\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})]-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{x}_{t+1}-x_{t}\|^{2}+\alpha\mathbb{E}\|v_{t}^{x}-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{t},\hat{y}_{t})\|^{2}

(3.10)

The approximation error decouples into the tracking variance, primal Lipschitz error, and the critical dual cross-term:

\displaystyle\alpha\mathbb{E}\|v_{t}^{x}-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{t},\hat{y}_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\alpha\mathbb{E}\|\delta_{t}^{x}\|^{2}+3\alpha L^{2}\mathbb{E}\|x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}\|^{2}+3\alpha L^{2}\mathbb{E}\|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\alpha\mathbb{E}\|\delta_{t}^{x}\|^{2}+3\alpha L^{2}\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}+3\alpha L^{2}\mathbb{E}\|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}\|^{2}.

(3.11)

Substituting this back, we expose the exact geometric penalty introduced by the non-cocoercive weakly monotone operator: the term
3\alpha L^{2}\mathbb{E}\|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}\|^{2}
.

We now analyze the dual projection
\tilde{y}_{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}(y_{t}+\alpha v_{t}^{y})
. By the variational characterization of the Euclidean projection with respect to the optimal reference
\hat{y}_{t}
:

2\alpha\langle v_{t}^{y},\hat{y}_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t+1}\rangle\leq\|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}\|^{2}-\|\hat{y}_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t+1}\|^{2}-\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}.

By decoupling the inner product using
v_{t}^{y}=\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}(x_{t},y_{t})+\delta_{t}^{y}
, and leveraging the concavity of the Lagrangian with respect to the dual variable, we establish:

\langle\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}(x_{t},y_{t}),\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}\rangle\geq\mathcal{L}(x_{t},\hat{y}_{t})-\mathcal{L}(x_{t},y_{t})\geq V(\hat{x}_{t})-\frac{L\gamma^{2}}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\mathcal{L}(x_{t},y_{t}).

Rearranging the projection inequality and dividing by
\alpha
, we isolate the dual step descent and a telescoping distance metric:

\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\hat{y}_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t+1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}-2\langle\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}(x_{t},y_{t}),\hat{y}_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t+1}\rangle-2\langle\delta_{t}^{y},\hat{y}_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t+1}\rangle.

(3.12)

We multiply the dual metric (
3.12
) by the constant
c_{y}
and sum it with the primal descent inequality. Crucially, by setting
\alpha
sufficiently small, the positive expansion term
3\alpha L^{2}\mathbb{E}\|y_{t}-\hat{y}_{t}\|^{2}
generated in (
3.1
) is strictly absorbed by the negative gap inside the telescoping coefficient
\frac{c_{y}}{\alpha}(\|\hat{y}_{t}-y_{t}\|^{2}-\|\hat{y}_{t}-\tilde{y}_{t+1}\|^{2})
.

Taking the total expectation to eliminate unbiased noise components, all cross-terms are completely neutralized. The unanchored SGDA step guarantees the global energy contraction:

\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})+\frac{c_{y}}{\alpha}\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})+\frac{c_{y}}{\alpha}\|y_{t}-y_{t-1}\|^{2}\right]-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}.

Adding the variance tracking term
c_{\delta}\mathbb{E}\|\delta_{t+1}\|^{2}
via the PAGE properties yields the stated unanchored descent inequality.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Single-step descent of the anchored merit function)
.

Under the same assumptions, the full alternating updates generated by the PD-HP algorithm (including the Halpern anchoring step) strictly satisfy the following expected descent inequality:

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t+1}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t}]-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\|y_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{D^{2}}{\alpha}\beta_{t}^{2},

(3.13)

where
D
is a constant proportional to the bounded diameter of the compact joint space
\mathcal{Z}
.

Proof.

Because the Moreau envelope
\phi_{\gamma}(x)
has an
L_{\phi}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient, any spatial perturbation from
\tilde{x}_{t+1}
to
x_{t+1}
induces a bounded function value expansion. Applying the standard quadratic upper bound:

\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t+1})\leq\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})+\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1}),x_{t+1}-\tilde{x}_{t+1}\rangle+\frac{L_{\phi}}{2}\|x_{t+1}-\tilde{x}_{t+1}\|^{2}.

Substitute the Halpern update rule
x_{t+1}-\tilde{x}_{t+1}=\beta_{t}(x_{0}-\tilde{x}_{t+1})
:

\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t+1})\leq\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})+\langle\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1}),\beta_{t}(x_{0}-\tilde{x}_{t+1})\rangle+\frac{L_{\phi}}{2}\|\beta_{t}(x_{0}-\tilde{x}_{t+1})\|^{2}.

To decouple the inner product, we apply Young’s inequality
\langle a,b\rangle\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}\|a\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|b\|^{2}
. Noting that the primal domain is compact with diameter
D_{x}
, we bound
\|x_{0}-\tilde{x}_{t+1}\|^{2}\leq D_{x}^{2}
:

\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t+1})\leq\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})+\frac{\alpha}{4}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{L_{\phi}}{2}\right)D_{x}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}.

(3.14)

The dual tracking metric evaluates the distance between successive multipliers. The Halpern step introduces the perturbation
y_{t+1}-y_{t}=(\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t})+\beta_{t}(y_{0}-\tilde{y}_{t+1})
.
Using the standard algebraic inequality
\|a+b\|^{2}\leq 2\|a\|^{2}+2\|b\|^{2}
, we establish:

\|y_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}\leq 2\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}+2\|\beta_{t}(y_{0}-\tilde{y}_{t+1})\|^{2}.

Given the compact dual domain diameter
D_{y}
, we have
\|y_{0}-\tilde{y}_{t+1}\|^{2}\leq D_{y}^{2}
. Rearranging the inequality to isolate
-\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}
, we get:

-\|\tilde{y}_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|y_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}+D_{y}^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}.

(3.15)

We now substitute the primal expansion bound (
3.14
) and the dual inversion bound (
3.15
) into the unanchored descent inequality (
3.6
). Taking the expectation yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t+1}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t}]

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(\tilde{x}_{t+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\|y_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{D_{x}^{2}+D_{y}^{2}}{\alpha}\beta_{t}^{2}.

By defining a unified spatial diameter constant
D
proportional to
D_{x}^{2}+D_{y}^{2}
bounded in the compact joint space
\mathcal{Z}
, and noting that the positive
\frac{\alpha}{4}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}\|^{2}
expansion error is securely absorbed by the negative
-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}\|^{2}
descent (yielding a net negative coefficient of
-\alpha/4
), we perfectly arrive at the strict one-step descent inequality:

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t+1}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t}]-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\|y_{t+1}-y_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{D^{2}}{\alpha}\beta_{t}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.1

(Optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity via merit function)
.

Suppose we parameterize the PD-HP algorithm for a target accuracy
\epsilon>0
to find the stationary point as follows:

•

Total iterations
T=\lceil K_{T}\epsilon^{-2}\rceil
.

•

Step size
\alpha>0
is a strictly positive constant independent of
T
.

•

Halpern sequence
\beta_{t}\in(0,1)
such that the series of its squares is strictly convergent:
\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\beta_{t}^{2}=B_{\beta}<\infty
(e.g.,
\beta_{t}=\frac{c}{t+t_{0}}
).

•

Batch sizes
B=\lceil K_{B}\epsilon^{-2}\rceil
and
b=\lceil K_{b}\epsilon^{-1}\rceil
, with PAGE probability
p=\frac{b}{B}
.

Then, the uniformly sampled output
x_{\tau}
exactly satisfies the expected stationarity condition on the Moreau envelope:

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{\tau})\|^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}.

(3.16)

Furthermore, the total stochastic oracle complexity to achieve this exact
\epsilon
-stationary point is optimal and strictly bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
.

Proof.

By rearranging the descent inequality of the anchored merit function, we isolate the gradient mapping of the Moreau envelope:

\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{t+1}]+\frac{D^{2}}{\alpha}\beta_{t}^{2}.

(3.17)

Summing this inequality over the trajectory from
t=0
to
T-1
, we can drop the non-negative terminal term
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{W}_{T}]\geq 0
. Leveraging the fact that the Halpern perturbation sequence has a strictly bounded sum of squares (
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\beta_{t}^{2}\leq B_{\beta}
), we obtain the cumulative gradient bound:

\frac{\alpha}{4}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{W}_{0}+\frac{D^{2}B_{\beta}}{\alpha}.

To evaluate the expected output, we divide both sides by
\frac{\alpha}{4}T
. Noting that the random output index
\tau
is drawn uniformly from the set
\{0,\dots,T-1\}
, the expected squared gradient norm mathematically evaluates to the trajectory average:

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{\tau})\|^{2}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\mathcal{W}_{0}}{\alpha T}+\frac{4D^{2}B_{\beta}}{\alpha^{2}T}.

(3.18)

Now we verify total iteration and oracle complexity.
First, we establish the total iterations
T
. Crucially, since the step size
\alpha
is a fixed positive constant and the Halpern squared sum
B_{\beta}
is finite, the numerator of the right-hand side in (
3.18
) forms an absolute constant:

C_{\text{bound}}:=4\mathcal{W}_{0}+\frac{4D^{2}B_{\beta}}{\alpha}.

(3.19)

Thus, the convergence rate simplifies exactly to
\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{\tau})\|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{\text{bound}}}{\alpha T}
. To strictly ensure an
\epsilon
-approximate stationary point (
\leq\epsilon^{2}
), we require
T\geq\frac{C_{\text{bound}}}{\alpha\epsilon^{2}}
. This mathematically justifies our parameterized iteration complexity of
T=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
.

Second, we calculate the expected stochastic oracle cost per iteration,
\mathbb{E}[\text{Cost}_{t}]
, dictated by the PAGE mechanism. At each step, PAGE triggers a large batch
B
with probability
p
, and a small recursive batch
b
with probability
1-p
. Based on our parameterization
p=b/B
:

\mathbb{E}[\text{Cost}_{t}]=p\cdot B+(1-p)\cdot b=\left(\frac{b}{B}\right)B+(1-p)b=b+(1-p)b<2b.

(3.20)

Since the small batch is parameterized as
b=\lceil K_{b}\epsilon^{-1}\rceil
, the expected per-iteration cost is strictly bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
, despite the occasional use of massive batches.

Finally, the total stochastic oracle complexity is the product of the total iterations and the expected per-iteration cost:

\text{Total Complexity}=T\times\mathbb{E}[\text{Cost}_{t}]=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})\times\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3}).

(3.21)

This rigorously confirms that by migrating to the Moreau Envelope merit metric, the PD-HP algorithm algebraically absorbs the Halpern sequence as a finite perturbation, simultaneously enabling a constant step size and achieving the optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity.
∎

Corollary 3.1

(Equivalence to the KKT residual metric)
.

For the uniformly sampled output
x_{\tau}
generated by the PD-HP algorithm, let
\hat{x}_{\tau}
be its corresponding proximal point on the Moreau envelope. Under Assumption
4
, there exists an optimal dual multiplier
\hat{y}_{\tau}\in\arg\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},y)
such that for the combined proximal state
\hat{z}_{\tau}=(\hat{x}_{\tau},\hat{y}_{\tau})
, the expected KKT gradient mapping is strictly bounded by the Moreau envelope gradient with a condition number of exactly
\kappa=1
:

\mathbb{E}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{\tau})\|^{2}.

(3.22)

Consequently, achieving the
\epsilon
-stationary point on the Moreau envelope strictly guarantees an exact
\epsilon
-approximate restricted KKT point with an optimal expected sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
.

Proof.

Let
w=\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{\tau})=\frac{1}{\gamma}(x_{\tau}-\hat{x}_{\tau})
be the gradient of the Moreau envelope. By the definition of the Moreau envelope for a
\rho
-weakly convex exact penalty function
V(x)=\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(x,y)
, the first-order optimality condition for the proximal subproblem at
x_{\tau}
implies:

w\in\partial V(\hat{x}_{\tau})+\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}}(\hat{x}_{\tau}),

(3.23)

where
\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}}(\hat{x}_{\tau})
denotes the normal cone to the compact convex set
X_{0}
at
\hat{x}_{\tau}
.

Since
\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}
is a compact convex set and the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(x,y)
is affine with respect to
y
, the subdifferential of the max-function
V
at
\hat{x}_{\tau}
is exactly characterized by:

\partial V(\hat{x}_{\tau})=\left\{\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},y)\mid y\in\arg\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},y)\right\}.

(3.24)

Consequently, there must exist an optimal dual multiplier
\hat{y}_{\tau}\in\arg\max_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},y)
such that:

w-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},\hat{y}_{\tau})\in\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}}(\hat{x}_{\tau}).

(3.25)

We define
\hat{z}_{\tau}=(\hat{x}_{\tau},\hat{y}_{\tau})
as the joint proximal state.

We now evaluate the KKT gradient mapping
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})=(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(\hat{z}_{\tau}),\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,y}(\hat{z}_{\tau}))
.
For the dual component, since
\hat{y}_{\tau}
is a maximizer of
\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},\cdot)
over
\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}
, the first-order optimality condition
\langle-\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},\hat{y}_{\tau}),y-\hat{y}_{\tau}\rangle\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}
guarantees that
\hat{y}_{\tau}
is a fixed point of the dual projection:

\hat{y}_{\tau}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}(\hat{y}_{\tau}+\alpha\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},\hat{y}_{\tau})).

(3.26)

Thus, the dual residual is identically zero:

\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,y}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|=\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\hat{y}_{\tau}-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Y}_{\delta}}(\hat{y}_{\tau}+\alpha\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},\hat{y}_{\tau}))\|=0.

(3.27)

For the primal component, let
v=\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\hat{x}_{\tau},\hat{y}_{\tau})
and define the projected update as
u=\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}(\hat{x}_{\tau}-\alpha v)
. The primal KKT mapping is explicitly given by
\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|=\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\|
. By the variational characterization of the Euclidean projection onto the convex set
X_{0}
, for any
x\in X_{0}
, we have:

\langle(\hat{x}_{\tau}-\alpha v)-u,x-u\rangle\leq 0.

(3.28)

Substituting
x=\hat{x}_{\tau}\in X_{0}
into the inequality yields:

\|\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\|^{2}\leq\alpha\langle v,\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\rangle.

(3.29)

Simultaneously, from (
3.25
), the definition of the normal cone
\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}}(\hat{x}_{\tau})
ensures that for any
x\in X_{0}
:

\langle w-v,x-\hat{x}_{\tau}\rangle\leq 0.

(3.30)

Since the projected point
u\in X_{0}
, we can substitute
x=u
to obtain
\langle w-v,u-\hat{x}_{\tau}\rangle\leq 0
, which implies:

\langle v,\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\rangle\leq\langle w,\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\rangle.

(3.31)

Combining (
3.29
) and (
3.31
), and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we establish:

\|\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\|^{2}\leq\alpha\langle w,\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\rangle\leq\alpha\|w\|\|\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\|.

(3.32)

Dividing both sides by
\alpha\|\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\|
(the inequality trivially holds if
\hat{x}_{\tau}=u
), we obtain the primal geometric bound:

\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\hat{x}_{\tau}-u\|\leq\|w\|\implies\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|\leq\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{\tau})\|.

(3.33)

Finally, summing the squared primal and dual residuals established in (
3.27
) and (
3.33
), we arrive at the exact equivalence bound:

\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}=\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}+\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,y}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}\leq\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{\tau})\|^{2}+0.

(3.34)

Taking the total expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
on both sides completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Rigorous complexity under standard unbounded KKT residual

To evaluate the algorithm under the standard, physically meaningful stationarity measure, we define the unbounded gradient mapping corresponding to the natural, un-truncated feasible set
\mathcal{Z}^{\infty}=X_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
:

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(z-\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}(z-\alpha F(z))\right).

(3.35)

We first establish a deterministic geometric property: approximate KKT points evaluated within the restricted domain are naturally bounded away from the artificial boundaries.
We first establish a deterministic geometric property: approximate KKT points evaluated within the restricted domain are naturally bounded away from the artificial boundaries.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
z=(x,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{Z}=X_{0}\times\Lambda_{\delta}\times M_{\delta}
be any evaluated joint state. Under Assumption
4
(global generalized MFCQ) and the polyhedral property of
X_{0}
, the exact dual multiplier sets are globally bounded, and the dual variables satisfy the uniform Hoffman error bound with modulus
\kappa_{y}^{*}>0
in Proposition
2.1
. We define the explicit critical threshold
c>0
as:

c:=\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{2\kappa_{y}^{*}},\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{m}\alpha},\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{p}\alpha}\right\}.

(3.36)

This constant is strictly positive and depends only on structural problem parameters and the constant step size
\alpha
. If the restricted gradient mapping satisfies
\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|\leq c
, the exact unbounded dual updates
\lambda^{\infty}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}(\lambda+\alpha g(x))
and
\mu^{\infty}=\mu+\alpha h(x)
naturally fall strictly within the interior of
\Lambda_{\delta}
and
M_{\delta}
. Consequently, the unbounded gradient mapping exactly coincides with the restricted one:

\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|\leq c\implies\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(z)=\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z).

(3.37)

Proof.

Let
\hat{z}_{+}=(\hat{x}_{+},\hat{\lambda}_{+},\hat{\mu}_{+})=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}}(z-\alpha F(z))
be the actual bounded update from state
z
.

By Proposition
2.1
, the polyhedral structure of the dual space under the Generalized MFCQ guarantees the existence of a global uniform Hoffman constant
\kappa_{y}^{*}>0
. For the proximal primal update
\hat{x}_{+}
, this ensures that the Euclidean distance from the current multipliers
(\lambda,\mu)
to the exact optimal multiplier set
\mathcal{M}(\hat{x}_{+})
is strictly bounded by the magnitude of the primal gradient mapping. Since the primal mapping is naturally upper-bounded by the joint mapping, there exists a specific exact multiplier pair
(\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})\in\mathcal{M}(\hat{x}_{+})
such that:

\displaystyle\|\lambda-\lambda^{*}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda-\lambda^{*}\|_{2}\leq\kappa_{y}^{*}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)\|\leq\kappa_{y}^{*}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|,

(3.38)

\displaystyle\|\mu-\mu^{*}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu-\mu^{*}\|_{2}\leq\kappa_{y}^{*}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,x}(z)\|\leq\kappa_{y}^{*}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|.

(3.39)

From Lemma
2.1
, all exact multipliers across the domain are globally bounded by
\lambda_{\max}
and
\mu_{\max}
. Applying the triangle inequality, the current evaluated multipliers are strictly bounded by:

\displaystyle\|\lambda\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda^{*}\|_{\infty}+\|\lambda-\lambda^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{\max}+\kappa_{y}^{*}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|,

(3.40)

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu^{*}\|_{\infty}+\|\mu-\mu^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\mu_{\max}+\kappa_{y}^{*}\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|.

(3.41)

By the premise that
\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|\leq c
, and utilizing the first term in the minimum definition of
c
from (
3.36
), we have
c\leq\frac{\delta}{2\kappa_{y}^{*}}
. Substituting this ensures the current multipliers are safely bounded away from the artificial boundaries by at least
\delta/2
:

\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{\max}+\frac{\delta}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mu\|_{\infty}\leq\mu_{\max}+\frac{\delta}{2}.

(3.42)

For the bounded dual projections, the Euclidean distances from the current multipliers to their updated states are bounded by the dual gradient mapping blocks:
\|\lambda-\hat{\lambda}_{+}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\lambda-\hat{\lambda}_{+}\|_{2}\leq\alpha\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)\|\leq\alpha c
. Utilizing the remaining terms in (
3.36
), we have
\alpha c\leq\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{m}}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
. By the triangle inequality:

\|\hat{\lambda}_{+}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\lambda\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{\lambda}_{+}-\lambda\|_{\infty}\leq\left(\lambda_{\max}+\frac{\delta}{2}\right)+\frac{\delta}{2}=\lambda_{\max}+\delta.

(3.43)

An identical bound holds for
\hat{\mu}_{+}
:

\|\hat{\mu}_{+}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\mu\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{\mu}_{+}-\mu\|_{\infty}\leq\left(\mu_{\max}+\frac{\delta}{2}\right)+\frac{\delta}{2}=\mu_{\max}+\delta.

(3.44)

Since the infinity norms of the updated multipliers are strictly less than or equal to the predefined boundary limits
\lambda_{\max}+\delta
and
\mu_{\max}+\delta
, the artificial constraint planes forming
\Lambda_{\delta}
and
M_{\delta}
are entirely inactive. Consequently, projecting onto the artificially truncated compact sets
\Lambda_{\delta}
and
M_{\delta}
is mathematically identical to projecting onto the natural, un-truncated orthant and space
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Thus, the unbounded gradient mapping perfectly aligns with the restricted one, yielding
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(z)=\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(z)
.
∎

Building on this deterministic multiplier stability and the exact near-stationarity equivalence derived from the Moreau envelope, we now rigorously establish the final optimal complexity under the standard unbounded residual metric using probability tail bounds.

Theorem 3.2

(Optimal complexity under standard unbounded KKT residual)
.

Let
x_{\tau}
be the uniformly sampled output of the PD-HP algorithm, and let
\hat{z}_{\tau}
be its corresponding exact proximal near-stationary point in the joint space. Assuming the primal domain
X_{0}
is a compact polyhedral convex set, and under the parameters specified in our complexity analysis, the expected squared norm of the standard, unbounded KKT residual at
\hat{z}_{\tau}
satisfies:

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}]\leq\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

(3.45)

This mathematically confirms that the algorithm preserves the optimal sample complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
under the standard unbounded KKT metric without any theoretical gaps.

Proof.

Let
E_{\tau}
be the event that the restricted residual at the proximal state is strictly within the stability threshold:
E_{\tau}=\{\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|\leq c\}
, where
c>0
is the critical threshold from Lemma
3.4
. Let
E_{\tau}^{c}
be its complement.

From the exact metric equivalence established in Corollary
3.1
, the expected restricted KKT residual is tightly bounded by the Moreau envelope gradient with a condition number of exactly
1
. Thus, we have:

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla\phi_{\gamma}(x_{\tau})\|^{2}]\leq\epsilon^{2}.

(3.46)

Applying Markov’s Inequality, the probability of the complement event (i.e., the residual exceeding the stability threshold) is bounded by:

\mathbb{P}(E_{\tau}^{c})=\mathbb{P}(\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}>c^{2})\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}]}{c^{2}}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{c^{2}}.

(3.47)

Next, we establish a global deterministic upper bound for the unbounded gradient mapping when
E_{\tau}^{c}
occurs. Because the defined trajectory and proximal states strictly reside within the joint compact set
\mathcal{Z}=X_{0}\times\Lambda_{\delta}\times M_{\delta}
(where
X_{0}
is explicitly a polyhedral convex set), the exact single-sample evaluation of the continuous KKT operator is globally bounded:

F_{\max}:=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|F(z)\|<\infty.

(3.48)

By the non-expansiveness of the projection operator onto the natural, un-truncated convex set
\mathcal{Z}^{\infty}=X_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we have for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}
:

\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(z)\|=\frac{1}{\alpha}\|z-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Z}^{\infty}}(z-\alpha F(z))\|\leq\|F(z)\|\leq F_{\max}.

(3.49)

Finally, we apply the Law of Total Expectation. Utilizing Lemma
3.4
(which strictly ensures
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\hat{z}_{\tau})=\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})
given event
E_{\tau}
) alongside bounds (
3.47
) and (
3.49
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{E_{\tau}}]+\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{E_{\tau}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{E_{\tau}}]+F_{\max}^{2}\cdot\mathbb{P}(E_{\tau}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\tau})\|^{2}]+F_{\max}^{2}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{c^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{2}+\frac{F_{\max}^{2}}{c^{2}}\epsilon^{2}=\left(1+\frac{F_{\max}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)\epsilon^{2}.

(3.50)

Since
F_{\max}
and the threshold
c
are explicit structural constants strictly independent of the target accuracy
\epsilon
, the coefficient
(1+F_{\max}^{2}/c^{2})
is an
\mathcal{O}(1)
absolute constant. This confirms that the expected squared norm of the standard, un-truncated KKT residual cleanly converges to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
, fully validating the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity limit without requiring artificial constraint bounding at the algorithmic output.
∎

Numerical Experiments

To empirically validate the theoretical breakthroughs established in the previous sections, we evaluated the performance of the proposed Primal-Dual Halpern-PAGE (PD-HP) algorithm on synthetic constrained stochastic weakly convex optimization problems. We conducted two groups of experiments: one with only inequality constraints, and the other with mixed (equality and inequality) constraints. The objective function is constructed using a cosine-based weakly convex model, which introduces non-convexity while preserving smooth stochastic gradients. Our main objective is to corroborate the optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity and demonstrate the deterministic stability of the KKT residual in a highly stochastic environment.

All numerical experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-9700K CPU @ 3.00GHz and 16GB of RAM. The algorithm was implemented in

Conclusion

In this paper, we have successfully bridged the sample complexity gap between constrained and unconstrained stochastic weakly convex optimization by introducing the Primal-Dual Halpern-PAGE (PD-HP) algorithm. Moving beyond the cumbersome double-loop architectures prevalent in existing literature, PD-HP operates as a highly efficient, entirely single-loop method.

The core innovation of our work lies in the synergistic integration of two potent mathematical tools: Halpern anchoring, which intrinsically stabilizes the weakly monotone KKT operator without resorting to expensive proximal point evaluations, and PAGE variance reduction, which optimally manages stochastic noise. Together, these mechanisms empower PD-HP to match the theoretical lower bounds, achieving the optimal
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
sample complexity for mixed-constrained problems.

Furthermore, we provided a mathematically rigorous treatment of the KKT residual metric. By invoking the generalized Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ) and exploiting Hoffman’s Lemma for the polyhedral dual space, we proved the deterministic stability of the dual multipliers. This elegant geometric analysis ensures that our optimal convergence rates and high-probability guarantees hold cleanly under the standard, unbounded KKT residual metric, eliminating the theoretical gaps associated with artificially truncated residuals.

The experimental results on both inequality-only and mixed-constrained problems consistently validate our theoretical findings. PD-HP provides a computationally efficient, single-loop, and theoretically optimal solution for constrained stochastic weakly convex optimization.

Future research could explore several promising directions. Extending the PD-HP framework to accommodate non-smooth functional constraints or decentralized data sources presents an exciting challenge. Additionally, the development of fully adaptive step-size rules that do not require prior knowledge of the weak convexity constant could further enhance the algorithm’s practical robustness in complex machine learning deployments.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
:

Proof.

First of all, we aim to bound
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi)\|^{2}]
. Expanding this using the definition of the Euclidean norm for the joint primal-dual space yields:

\|F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi)\|^{2}=\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{1}-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{2}\|^{2}+\|g(x_{1},\xi)-g(x_{2},\xi)\|^{2}+\|h(x_{1},\xi)-h(x_{2},\xi)\|^{2},

(5.1)

where
\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{k}=\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\lambda_{k},\mu_{k},\xi)
for
k\in\{1,2\}
.

For the primal block (the first term), we add and subtract the cross-evaluation terms
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{1,i}\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi)
and
\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{1,j}\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi)
, and apply the standard algebraic inequality
(a+b+c+d)^{2}\leq 4(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2})
:

\begin{array}[]{l}\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{1}-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{2}\|^{2}=\left\|(\nabla f_{0}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla f_{0}(x_{2},\xi))+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{1,i}(\nabla g_{i}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi))\right.\\
\quad\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{1,j}(\nabla h_{j}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi))+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Delta\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Delta\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi)\right)\right\|^{2}\\
\leq 4\|\nabla f_{0}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla f_{0}(x_{2},\xi)\|^{2}+4\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{1,i}(\nabla g_{i}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi))\right\|^{2}\\
\quad+4\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{1,j}(\nabla h_{j}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi))\right\|^{2}+4\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Delta\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Delta\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi)\right\|^{2}.\end{array}

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, along with the uniform multiplier bounds
\|\lambda_{1}\|^{2}\leq m\lambda_{\max}^{2}
and
\|\mu_{1}\|^{2}\leq p\mu_{\max}^{2}
, and the Lipschitz assumptions (Assumption
2
), we bound the inner terms:

\begin{array}[]{l}4\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{1,i}(\Delta\nabla g_{i})\right\|^{2}\leq 4m\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{1,i}^{2}\|\Delta\nabla g_{i}\|^{2}\leq 4m^{2}\lambda_{\max}^{2}L_{g}(\xi)^{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2},\\[5.0pt]
4\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{1,j}(\Delta\nabla h_{j})\right\|^{2}\leq 4p\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{1,j}^{2}\|\Delta\nabla h_{j}\|^{2}\leq 4p^{2}\mu_{\max}^{2}L_{h}(\xi)^{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}.\end{array}

For the dual cross-term, we define the concatenated Jacobian matrix

J=[\nabla g_{1},\dots,\nabla g_{m},\nabla h_{1},\dots,\nabla h_{p}]

and the dual difference vector
\Delta y=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2},\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})^{T}
. Using the Frobenius norm bound
\|J\Delta y\|^{2}\leq\|J\|_{F}^{2}\|\Delta y\|^{2}
and Assumption 4, we obtain:

\begin{array}[]{ll}4\|J\Delta y\|^{2}&\leq 4\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi)\|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\|\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi)\|^{2}\right)\left(\|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\|^{2}+\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|^{2}\right)\\[5.0pt]
&\leq 4(mG_{g}(\xi)^{2}+pG_{h}(\xi)^{2})\left(\|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\|^{2}+\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|^{2}\right).\end{array}

For the constraint evaluation blocks (the second and third terms in (
5.1
)), the Mean Value Theorem and the gradient boundedness assumption imply that the constraint functions are
G_{g}(\xi)
and
G_{h}(\xi)
Lipschitz, respectively:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|g(x_{1},\xi)-g(x_{2},\xi)\|^{2}&\leq mG_{g}(\xi)^{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2},\\[5.0pt]
\|h(x_{1},\xi)-h(x_{2},\xi)\|^{2}&\leq pG_{h}(\xi)^{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Substituting all derived bounds back into (
5.1
) and taking the total expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\cdot]
yields:

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbb{E}[\|F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi)\|^{2}]\\[5.0pt]
\leq\Big(4\mathbb{E}[L_{f}^{2}]+4m\lambda_{\max}^{2}\mathbb{E}[L_{g}^{2}]+4p\mu_{\max}^{2}\mathbb{E}[L_{h}^{2}]+m\mathbb{E}[G_{g}^{2}]+p\mathbb{E}[G_{h}^{2}]\Big)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}\\[5.0pt]
\quad+4(m\mathbb{E}[G_{g}^{2}]+p\mathbb{E}[G_{h}^{2}])\left(\|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\|^{2}+\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|^{2}\right).\end{array}

Since the joint distance metric satisfies
\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|^{2}=\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}+\|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\|^{2}+\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|^{2}
, the global expected Lipschitz constant
L^{2}
can be safely chosen as the maximum of the primal and dual block coefficients.

We now bound the variance
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|F(z,\xi)-F(z)\|^{2}]
. Expanding the squared norm block by block:

\mathbb{E}[\|F(z,\xi)-F(z)\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|g(x,\xi)-g(x)\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|h(x,\xi)-h(x)\|^{2}].

Applying the inequality
(a+b+c)^{2}\leq 3(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})
to the Lagrangian gradient variance, we decouple the objective, inequality, and equality constraints:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}\|^{2}]&\leq 3\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_{0}(x,\xi)-\nabla f_{0}(x)\|^{2}]+3m\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla g_{i}(x,\xi)-\nabla g_{i}(x)\|^{2}]\\[5.0pt]
&\quad+3p\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla h_{j}(x,\xi)-\nabla h_{j}(x)\|^{2}].\end{array}

Using the bounded variance assumptions (Assumption
3
) and the uniform bounds
\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{\max}
and
\|\mu\|_{\infty}\leq\mu_{\max}
, this immediately implies:

\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}\|^{2}]\leq 3\sigma_{f}^{2}+3m^{2}\lambda_{\max}^{2}\sigma_{\nabla g}^{2}+3p^{2}\mu_{\max}^{2}\sigma_{\nabla h}^{2}.

For the variance of the constraint evaluations, the independence across components directly gives

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbb{E}[\|g(x,\xi)-g(x)\|^{2}]\leq m\sigma_{g}^{2},\quad\mathbb{E}[\|h(x,\xi)-h(x)\|^{2}]\leq p\sigma_{h}^{2}.\end{array}

Summing these bounds together naturally defines the global stochastic variance bound on
\mathcal{Z}
:

\sigma^{2}:=3\sigma_{f}^{2}+3m^{2}\lambda_{\max}^{2}\sigma_{\nabla g}^{2}+3p^{2}\mu_{\max}^{2}\sigma_{\nabla h}^{2}+m\sigma_{g}^{2}+p\sigma_{h}^{2},

which matches the stated parameter and concludes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
:

Proof.

Let
z_{1}=(x_{1},\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})
and
z_{2}=(x_{2},\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})
be two arbitrary points in the compact domain
\mathcal{Z}
. For a given realization
\xi
, we evaluate the inner product of the stochastic operator difference:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi),z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle=&\langle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(z_{1},\xi)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(z_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle-\langle g(x_{1},\xi)-g(x_{2},\xi),\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\rangle\\[5.0pt]
&\quad-\langle h(x_{1},\xi)-h(x_{2},\xi),\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\rangle.\end{array}

Expanding the Lagrangian gradient differences and applying the symmetric algebraic identity
\lambda_{1}a_{1}-\lambda_{2}a_{2}=\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}{2}(a_{1}-a_{2})+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}
, we can perfectly decouple the primal and dual interactions:

\langle F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi),z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle=\langle\nabla f_{0}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla f_{0}(x_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle

\begin{array}[]{l}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda_{1,i}+\lambda_{2,i}}{2}\langle\nabla g_{i}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\\[4.0pt]
+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{\mu_{1,j}+\mu_{2,j}}{2}\langle\nabla h_{j}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\\[4.0pt]
+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\lambda_{1,i}-\lambda_{2,i})\left[\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla g_{i}(x_{1},\xi)+\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle-(g_{i}(x_{1},\xi)-g_{i}(x_{2},\xi))\right]\\[4.0pt]
+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}(\mu_{1,j}-\mu_{2,j})\left[\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla h_{j}(x_{1},\xi)+\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle-(h_{j}(x_{1},\xi)-h_{j}(x_{2},\xi))\right].\end{array}

Now, we bound each component from below using the structural properties of the stochastic functions defined in Assumption
1
and Assumption
2
:

1)

Since
f_{0}(\cdot,\xi)
is
\rho
-weakly convex,
\langle\nabla f_{0}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla f_{0}(x_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq-\rho\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}
.

2)

Since
g_{i}(\cdot,\xi)
is
\rho
-weakly convex and
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{\max}]
, the coefficient
\frac{\lambda_{1,i}+\lambda_{2,i}}{2}\leq\lambda_{\max}
, yielding a lower bound of
-m\rho\lambda_{\max}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}
.

3)

Since both
h_{j}(\cdot,\xi)
and
-h_{j}(\cdot,\xi)
are
\rho
-weakly convex, we have

|\langle\nabla h_{j}(x_{1},\xi)-\nabla h_{j}(x_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle|\leq\rho\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}.

With
\mu\in[-\mu_{\max},\mu_{\max}]
, the magnitude of the midpoint is bounded by
\mu_{\max}
, yielding
\geq-p\rho\mu_{\max}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}
.

4)

For the cross-evaluation error terms, we utilize the standard trapezoidal inequality for
L_{g}(\xi)
-smooth functions:

\left|g_{i}(x_{1},\xi)-g_{i}(x_{2},\xi)-\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla g_{i}(x_{1},\xi)+\nabla g_{i}(x_{2},\xi),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\right|\leq\frac{L_{g}(\xi)}{4}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}.

Bounding the absolute difference
|\lambda_{1,i}-\lambda_{2,i}|
by the domain width
2\lambda_{\max}
, this term is bounded below by
-2\lambda_{\max}\frac{L_{g}(\xi)}{4}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}=-\frac{\lambda_{\max}L_{g}(\xi)}{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}
.

5)

Applying the exact same smooth trapezoidal bound for
h_{j}(\cdot,\xi)
with domain width
|\mu_{1,j}-\mu_{2,j}|\leq 2\mu_{\max}
yields
\geq-\frac{\mu_{\max}L_{h}(\xi)}{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}
.

Summing all the respective bounds across the
m
inequality and
p
equality constraints, we obtain:

\langle F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi),z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle\geq-\left[\rho(1+m\lambda_{\max}+p\mu_{\max})+\frac{m\lambda_{\max}L_{g}(\xi)+p\mu_{\max}L_{h}(\xi)}{2}\right]\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}

Finally, since the squared joint Euclidean distance satisfies
\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|^{2}=\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}+\|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\|^{2}+\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|^{2}\geq\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}
, we conclude:

\langle F(z_{1},\xi)-F(z_{2},\xi),z_{1}-z_{2}\rangle\geq-\overline{\rho}(\xi)\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|^{2}

which completes the proof.
