From 18F‑MC225 to Conformationally Free Derivatives
Authors: Anna Teresa Lisi, Francesco Mastropasqua, Carmen Abate, Marialessandra Contino, Maria Grazia Perrone, Giovanni Graziano, Antonio Carrieri, Nicola Antonio Colabufo, Cristina Filosa, Maria Lucia Calcagni, Alessandro Giordano, Daniela Di Giuda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PET radiotracer [18F]­MC225 is in two different clinical trials for imaging P-glycoprotein (P-gp) at the BBB level. This radiotracer permits to evaluate both the inset of neurodegenerative pathologies such as MCI, AD, and PD and the drug resistance of antidepressant drugs. Although these studies are ongoing, it is known that in MCI, AD, and PD the activity and expression of P-gp at the BBB level is lower than in healthy subjects, whereas the activity of the same pump is high in human subjects with resistance to antidepressant therapy. This level of P-gp is measured by PET with [18F]­MC225 that displayed high brain uptake in neurodegenerative diseases and low brain uptake in subjects with resistance versus several antidepressant drugs. Starting from the mentioned PET radiotracer, a series of conformationally free derivatives have been synthesized testing the role of isosteric substitution in terms of P-gp interactions. Aryl-(oxy)-derivative 2c showed in vitro higher P-gp activity than MC225 (EC50 = 1.8 μM vs EC50 = 6.35 μM). This result has been corroborated with molecular docking, RMSD, and Rog timeline plot. The results disclosed a class of compounds, among them, 2c could be easily [11C]/[18F] radiolabeled for in vivo studies. Considering the lower lipophilicity of 2c than MC225, this ligand could be radiolabeled for imaging P-glycoprotein function and expression that is involved in several heart failures, where a low log P is an important and prelaminar parameter to drug targeting.

Introduction

Recently, [18F]­MC225 (Figure ) has been approved in two different clinical trials as a PET 18-F radiotracer, in human, Phase II, because all ADME described properties in in vivo studies are largely satisfied. As reported in European Portal Ctis, Luurtsema et al. − are investigating the involvement of P-glycoprotein at the BBB level in Alzheimer and Parkinson patients. The expected results should demonstrate that in Alzheimer patients the uptake of the PET radiotracer [18F]­MC225 is more conspicuous than in the control group because in these patients there is a loss of P-gp activity and expression at the BBB level. P-glycoprotein is involved in efflux of many drugs and of [18F]­MC225, which is a weak P-gp substrate. − In the recently approved clinical trial study, it was demonstrated that in patients being treated with antidepressant drugs the brain uptake of PET radiotracer should be more abundant that in patients that are not responsive to antidepressant drugs because the hypothesis is that there is an induced overexpression of P-gp in the nonresponsive patients. − In these studies, the control group is the first whereas the second group of patients should demonstrate a reduced brain uptake of radiotracer in PET because the nonresponsive state seems to be due, as reported in several papers, to overexpression and/or over-activity of P-glycoprotein at the BBB level.

[18F]­MC225, a PET radiotracer.

Summarizing this finding, [18F]­MC225 is the first radioligand PET approved in two different clinical trials that after the studies of Phase I, in humans, carried out by Toyohara et al., is useful to in vivo targeting P-glycoprotein at the BBB level. These studies should lead to employing the promising radiotracer [18F]­MC225, to give early diagnosis in CNS pathologies such as AD, PD, and MCI. Moreover, we defined if P-gp is involved in drug-resistant treatment in a specific class of antidepressive drugs.

In order to improve SAR studies regarding the potency and to better explore P-gp–MC225 interactions, the tetraline fragment present in the lead compound reported in Figure has been simplified to obtain aryloxyalkyl and arylthioalkyl 2a–d and 2e–h, respectively, (Figure ) that display high free conformation of spacer and the presence of isosteric substitution, to evaluate the interacting point with P-glycoprotein domains. Moreover, in order to evaluate different P-gp interacting points and the role of a basic moiety, amine and amide derivatives, 5a–b and 4a–b, respectively, have been prepared. In addition, P app redution values with respect to MC225, it leads to hypothesizing a potential radiotracer to target P-gp in different biological peripheral areas such as the heart tissue. In fact, P-gp modulates drug concentrations at the cardiac tissue whereas serious cardiotoxicity aspects were observed. A c Log P value lower than that of lead compound MC225 could lead to improved drug targeting at P-gp at the heart tissue with respect to P-gp at the BBB level. However, this is our hypothesis that should be validated in preclinical studies.

An adequate radiotracer to image P-gp by PET in the heart tissue could give additional information for the optimization of drugs used for heart failure and their unwanted effects.

General Procedure for the Synthesis of Alkyl Bromide Derivatives 1a–h

The synthesis of several intermediates reported in Schemes and has been carried out as reported in our previous papers with minor modifications. − A mixture of the appropriate phenol/thiol (1 equiv), K2CO3 (2 equiv), and 1,2-dibromoethane or 1,3-dibromopropane (3 equiv) in CH3CN was heated to reflux under magnetic stirring ON. The reaction mixture was concentrated under reduced pressure and the residue was taken up in H2O (20 mL) and extracted with CH2Cl2 (3 × 20 mL). The combined organic layers were dried (Na2SO4) and evaporated under vacuum to afford a crude residue, which was purified by column chromatography using n-hexane/EtOAc 9:1. For compound 1g were employed different experimental conditions because H2O was the solvent, and NaOH (1.5 equiv) and KI (0.1 equiv) were the added reagents. The mixture was heated to reflux under stirring for 48 h.

General Procedure for Obtaining Final Amines 2a–h

A mixture of the appropriate bromoalkyl derivative 1a-e, 1g-h (1 equiv), K2CO3 (2 equiv), and 6,7-dimethoxy-1,2,3,4-tetrahydroisoquinoline (1.3 equiv) in CH3CN containing a few drops of anhydrous DMF was heated to reflux under magnetic stirring ON. The reaction mixture was concentrated under reduced pressure and the residue was taken up in H2O (20 mL) and extracted with CH2Cl2 (3 × 20 mL). The combined organic layers were dried (Na2SO4) and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude material was purified by flash column chromatography using CH2Cl2/MeOH 95:5. The corresponding hydrochloride salts were prepared by adding HCl saturated ethereal solution to a solution of the amine. The hydrochloride salt was purified by crystallization in MeOH/Et2O. The synthesis of compound 2f was performed starting from intermediate 1f in CH3CN under microwave irradiation at 160 °C and 20 bar for 65 min. The crude was purified by the chromatography technique.

General Procedure for the Synthesis of Anilides 3a–b

The synthesis of these intermediates reported in Schemes and has been carried out as reported in our previous papers with minor modifications. The appropriate aniline (1 equiv) was dissolved in CH2Cl2, and an aqueous 2% NaOH solution (1.2 equiv) was added. The mixture was cooled to 0 °C, and 2-chloroacetyl chloride was added dropwise under stirring. After 5 min at 0 °C, the reaction mixture was allowed to warm to room temperature. If necessary, the pH was adjusted to basicity by addition of NaOH. The mixture was extracted with CH2Cl2 (3 × 20 mL). The combined organic layers were dried (Na2SO4) and concentrated under reduced pressure. No further purification was required.

General Procedure for the Synthesis of Final Anilides 4a–b

A mixture of the appropriate amide (3a or 3b) (1 equiv), K2CO3 (2 equiv), and 6,7-dimethoxy-1,2,3,4-tetrahydroisoquinoline (1.3 equiv) in CH3CN containing a few drops of anhydrous DMF was heated to reflux under magnetic stirring. After completion, the solvents were removed under reduced pressure. The residue was taken up in H2O (20 mL) and extracted with CH2Cl2 (3 × 20 mL). The combined organic layers were dried over (Na2SO4) and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by packed-column chromatography: elution was first performed followed by DCM/MeOH 95:5. The corresponding hydrochloride salts were prepared by adding a HCl saturated ethereal solution to a solution of the amine. The hydrochloride salt was purified by crystallization in MeOH/Et2O.

General Procedure for the Synthesis of Final Amines 5a–b

A solution of BH3·Me2S 2 M (3 equiv) in anhydrous THF (10 mL) was carefully added dropwise to a stirred and ice bath-cooled solution of the appropriate amides 4a–4b (1 equiv) in anhydrous THF (15 mL). The reaction mixture was heated to reflux overnight. The excess borane complex was quenched by dropwise addition of MeOH (10 mL) at 0 °C, followed by addition of 3 N HCl (10 mL), and the mixture was refluxed with stirring for 1 h. After cooling, the reaction mixture was alkalized with NaOH 2 M and the residue was extracted with CH2Cl2 (3 × 20 mL). The collected organic layers were dried (Na2SO4) and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by packed-column chromatography eluting with CH2Cl2/MeOH 98:2 or CH2Cl2/MeOH 95:5. The corresponding hydrochloride salts were prepared by adding a HCl saturated ethereal solution to a solution of the amine. The hydrochloride salt was purified by crystallization in MeOH/Et2O.

Biology

All synthesized compounds have been studied for their activity toward P-glycoproteins determining for each EC50 (μM) and Apparent Permeability (P app) in order to predict the mechanism to interact with P-gp: P app > 2 substrate, P app < 2 inhibitor.

Biology

P-gp Activity

MDCK-MDR1 cells were cultured in DMEM (high glucose) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 2 mM l-glutamine, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. The P-glycoprotein activity for each tested compound has been determined with Calcein-AM Transport Assay.

The experiment was carried out as described by Riganti et al., with minor modifications. ,

The MDCK-MDR1 cell line (30,000 cells per well) was seeded into black CulturePlate 96/wells plate with 100 μL medium and allowed to become confluent overnight. 100 μL of test compounds were solubilized in culture medium and added to monolayers, with final concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 100 μM. 96/Wells plate was incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. Calcein-AM was added in 100 μL of Phosphate Buffered Saline (PBS) to yield a final concentration of 2.5 μM and the plate was incubated for 30 min. Each well was washed 3 times with ice-cold PBS. Saline buffer was added to each well and the plate was read with Victor3 (PerkinElmer) at excitation and emission wavelengths of 485 and 535 nm, respectively. In these experimental conditions, Calcein cell accumulation in the absence and in the presence of tested compounds was evaluated and the fluorescence basal level was estimated with untreated cells. In treated wells, the increase of fluorescence with respect to the basal level was measured. EC50 values were determined by fitting the fluorescence increase percentage versus log­[dose].

Apparent Permeability

Caco-2 cells were seeded onto 96-well MultiScreen plates at a density of 10,000 cells per well. The culture medium was replaced every 48 h during the first 6 days and subsequently every 24 h. After 21 days of differentiation, the Caco-2 monolayers were used for permeation studies. Transepithelial Electrical Resistance (TEER) was measured daily, before and after each experiment, using an epithelial volt-ohmmeter (Millicell-ERS; Millipore, Billerica, MA). Fully differentiated monolayers typically displayed TEER values > 1000 Ω at day 21.

Drug Transport Assay

The apical-to-basolateral (P app A → B) and basolateral-to-apical (P app B → A) permeability of the test compounds was evaluated at 120 min using a concentration range from 1 to 100 μM. Compounds were dissolved in HBSS buffer (pH 7.4). After 21 days of culture, the medium was removed from both the donor (filter well) and receiver chambers. Filter wells were filled with 75 μL of fresh HBSS and receiver wells with 250 μL HBSS; this equilibration step was performed twice, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 30 min

After incubation, HBSS was removed and compound solutions (74 μL) were added to the donor compartment. HBSS without compounds (250 μL) was added to the receiver compartment. Plates were then incubated at 37 °C for 120 min. At the end of the transport period, samples were collected from both apical (donor) and basolateral (receiver) chambers and stored at −20 °C until analysis. UV spectroscopy quantified compound concentrations.

The apparent permeability coefficient (P app), expressed in nm/s, was calculated according to Papp=(VAArea×time)×([drug]acceptor[drug]initial) where: V A is the volume (mL) in the acceptor well, area is the membrane surface area (0.11 cm2), time is the total transport period (7200 s), [drug] acceptor is the concentration measured by UV spectroscopy, and [drug] initial is the initial drug concentration (1 × 10–4 M) in the donor well.

Computational Methods

Compound’s molecular skeletons were built in both the charged state by converting the relative SMILES strings to a three-dimensional structure within Maestro software package and performing thereafter with Open Babel 10000 steps of Steepest Descent minimization using the Universal Force Field. The X-ray solved structure of elacridar in complex with P-gp (pdb code 7A6C) was prepared with the Protein Preparation Wizard interface of Maestro removing the ligand and water molecules, adding hydrogen atoms, optimizing their position, and assigning the ionization states of acid and basic residues according to PROPKA prediction at pH 7.0. Electrostatic charges for protein atoms were loaded according to AMBER UNITED force field, while the molcharge complement of QUACPAC was used in order to achieve Marsili-Gasteiger charges for the inhibitors. Affinity maps were first calculated on a 0.375 Å spaced 120 × 120 × 120 Å3 rectangular box, having the barycenter on both elacridar crystallized molecules, and the accessibility of the binding site was exploited throughout 1000 runs of Lamarckian Genetic Algorithm (LGA) implemented in AUTODOCK 4.2.640 using the GPU-OpenCL algorithm version. Explicit water contribution was considered according to the hydration force field, and the population size and the number of energy evaluations figures were set to 300 and 10000000, respectively. The best free energy/most populated binding poses were selected as a representative of ligand binding mode. Poses were evaluated based on the ESP rule and accepted when at least four out of five parameters met specific thresholds: E (FEB < −8.00, ΔE < 2.00, EFF < – 0.250), S > 0.500, and POP >100 and served as an input for the subsequent molecular dynamics (MD) simulations.

These runs were carried out using Desmond , in a canonical ensemble under NPT (constant number of particles, pressure, and temperature) with a constant temperature of 300 K and pressure of 1.01325 bar. First, in the ligand–receptor complexes, the transmembrane helices (namely residues 45–67, 117–137, 187–208, 216–236, 293–316, 331–352, 712–732 , 757–777, 833–853, 855–874, 935–957, 974–995) were embedded in a prebuilt phosphatidylcholine (POPC) molecule model mimicking the membrane milieu, and then the whole molecular assemblies were then enclosed in an orthorhombic box containing SPC model water molecules. Na+ and Cl– at 0.15 M final salt concentration to simulate the physiological environment.

Before starting MD, the entire structures were passed to the default relaxing protocol, including a series of restrained minimizations and short MD runs to gradually lower the overall energy of the system, and then the simulation started with the first 100 ns equilibration stint followed by a 500 ns trajectory for data collection. Collecting, hence, a total of ten thousand conformations. The time step integration, calculated using the RESPA integrator, was set to 2.0 fs, and the default temperature, pressure control protocols, and bonded or nonbonded interactions short-range and long-range electrostatic interactions cutoffs values were applied.

The simulation was computed using an NVidia RTX A5000 GPU within a CentOS Linux environment. Drawing of the protein structures and ligands was prepared using PyMOL (http://www.pymol.org).

Results and Discussion

All compounds have been tested for determining the P-gp activity and the P app values to distinguish substrates and inhibitors toward P-gp. Since all tested compounds displayed P app > 2, they are all estimated P-gp substrates as for as [18F]­MC225 although, it shows higher P app (BA/AB = 18) with respect to the compounds listed in Table , where these values are ranging from 3.1 to 4.7. Moreover, the reduction of P app values meets the employment of these ligands as potential radiotracers to imaging P-gp in the heart tissue with a very low uptake at the BBB level. The results demonstrated that the isosteric substitution on the spacer led to compounds more active than MC225. In particular compound 2c and the corresponding thiol derivative 2g displayed the best P-gp activities (EC50 = 1.8 μM and 3.3 μM, respectively). The corresponding unsubstituted derivatives 2a (EC50 = 7.5 μM) and 2e (EC50 = 5.6 μM) were the lowest active. In Table are reported the values of P-glycoprotein substrate, verapamil, and P-glycoprotein inhibitor, Tariquidar, that showed atypical P app values due to antagonist activity toward P-gp and BCRP agonist activity. These findings have been corroborated in several in vivo studies. Finally, c Log P values demonstrated that, with respect to MC225 (c Log P = 5.988), all listed compounds displayed lower c Log P than the lead compound. Molecular Modeling of 2c, 2e, and 5b as P-gp Modulators

It is already largely accepted that P-gp controls the intracellular accumulation of xenobiotics and contributes to multidrug resistance with an alternate between inward-facing and outward-facing conformations during the transport cycle of its large, amphipathic binding cavity, allowing then substrate binding and release. Hence, ligands that stabilize one conformational state may therefore modulate the transporter’s efficiency and substrate selectivity. To focus on this topic, closely related ligands 2c, 2e, and 5b were comparatively analyzed through molecular docking and molecular dynamics (MD) simulation (Table ). Although sharing the same tetralin scaffold, their activities toward P-gp differ markedly, with 2c (EC50 = 1.8 μM) being more potent than 2e and 5b (EC50 = 5.6 μM and EC50 = 31.6 μM). The goal of this analysis is to clarify how a single-atom substitution in the linker (O, S, or NH) modifies intramolecular hydrogen bonding, bioactive preorganization, and consequently the coupling between ligand conformation and the P-gp inward/outward transition. To fulfill this goal, binding modes were generated starting from the crystallographic insights gained from the complex between P-gp and the potent inhibitor elacridar recently published by Nosol et al. The EPS rule was used as criteria for pose ranking and selection.

As long as these results were achieved, docking poses were then investigated in terms of three-dimensional and conformational features and complementarity with the anchoring cleft. The inward-facing conformation of P-gp includes a hydrophobic cavity (Phe336, Phe728, Phe732, Phe957, Phe978, and Phe983) bordered by polar residues (Tyr950, Ser952, Tyr953, Ser979, and Gln725), and more than one of these have been proven upon the site-directed mutagenesis study to be mandatory for ligand binding and activity , and were then figured in the interaction pattern scheme of our binders, all able to be spotted in this large cleft facing the intracellular side of the receptor (Figure ).

Front (left) and top (right) view of binding mode for derivatives 2c, 2e, and 5b to the P-gp binding site. The TM1-TM6 and TM7-TM12 segments are depicted in red and blue, respectively.

In detail, 2c contains an ether linkage and forms distinct hydrogen bonds (HBs) with Tyr953 recruiting both the charged NH of tetrahydroisoquinoline and the ether O, producing at the same time a folded, preorganized conformation that mirrors its bound state. As it might be perceived in Figure , this internal HB rigidifies the linker, lowers entropic cost, and might thereafter ensure precise alignment of aromatic moieties within a large cluster of aromatic residues while maintaining strong H-bonds with Ser952 and Ser979.

On the other side, the less active compound 5b, lacking the oxygen atom, shows a less extended HB network and might therefore fluctuate among multiple conformations, losing directional polar contacts and favoring transient pose features typical of a less coupled, more outward-mobile ligand. Halfway through the previous observation, thioether ligand 2e shows intermediate behavior: weaker HBA character, increased hydrophobicity, and partial stabilization through dispersion rather than electrostatic complementarity (figure not shown).

According to these pieces of evidence and taking into account ESP scores, a comparative interaction profile suggests for the superimposed poses a conserved hydrophobic anchoring but divergent polar organization: the most active compound 2c produces stable and persistent HB, strong π–π stacking, and the highest docking convergence (POP = 752), and to a less extent, similar stabilization, with indeed lower convergence (POP = 585), characterizes 2e, while 5b shows only poor intramolecular stabilization and transient H solvent-exposed binding orientation (Table ). It is to be anticipated that some of these mere, but critical instances, will be confirmed in a following molecular dynamics simulation, where a diverse set of features, persisting throughout the trajectory, promote for 2c a more firm stabilization within the inward cavity and a compact orientation consistent with high-affinity binding. Indeed, these results align with our previous P-gp docking studies showing that tetrahydroisoquinoline derivatives bearing polar para-substituted linkers achieve enhanced P-gp inhibition by stabilizing inward-facing binding poses. In both, that study and the present study, intramolecular hydrogen bonding emerges as the key feature driving conformational preorganization and stronger receptor complementarity.

To give a proof of concept for our working hypothesis on the role of oxygen atom of 2c acting as a promoter of proper hydrogen bonds stabilizing a more compact and preorganized ligand conformation in contrast to 5b, a different type of investigation was thereafter performed on the lone 2c and 5b P-gp complexes by means of long-time scale molecular dynamics (MD) simulations (Figure ). Initially, a detailed analysis of protein–ligand contact frequencies was performed to characterize the persistent interaction patterns stabilizing the complexes of P-gp with 2c and 5b. The 2D interaction maps (Figure ) summarize the individual contacts observed across the MD trajectories, reporting their occurrence probability and categorizing them into hydrophobic, polar, and water-mediated interactions.

Scaled view of 2c (left) and 5b (right) binding modes to P-gp. The cyan (top), magenta (middle), and yellow (bottom) dashed lines indicate, respectively, hydrogen bonds, face-to-face and edge-to-face π–π aromatic stacking interactions with receptor binding sites.

2c exhibits a highly coherent and compact network of stabilizing contacts, consistent with the ligand’s preorganized conformation. The aromatic moieties show persistent hydrophobic interactions with Phe343 and Trp232, forming a triad of π-stacking that anchor the molecule deeply within the transmembrane pocket. Notably, the ether oxygen participates in a frequent intramolecular polar contact with the charged nitrogen, supporting the previously postulated view of this group as central in orienting the ligand in the binding site. The same nitrogen engages Glu875, creating a salt-bridge-like stabilization, and the proximity of structured water molecules to Gln990 further supports a well-defined electrostatic microenvironment that is compatible with sustained, directional contacts. Additional polar contacts are also gained by the methoxy substituent of the tetrahydroisoquinoline upon the recruitment of Tyr310.

In contrast, the interaction map of 5b reveals a more fragmented, heterogeneous, and water-dominant interaction profile. Hydrophobic contacts with residues Phe343 are still present but markedly less structured compared to 2c, but water molecules play a more central role in mediating interactions with multiple bridges linking the protonated amine ring to Gln990, Gly872, or other polar groups. Besides this evidence, the most important indication refers to the increased conformational freedom of the ligand, most likely due to the absence of the intramolecular link, which might then result in multiple transient orientations, and this might be indicative of a binding mode not fully exploiting the hydrophobic depth and the large cavity of the binding site, and this feature is further supported by the other MD insights. Indeed, a comprehensive analysis of some key structural, geometrical, and dynamic descriptors was developed.

Particularly, global stability and compactness of the complexes were monitored through root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) and radius of gyration (RoG), while center-of-mass (CoM) distances between the ligand and the protein cavity tracked binding persistence. At the same time, differential flexibility in the transmembrane helices were evaluated according to backbone and per-residue fluctuations (RMSF). As a specific monitor of P-gp profile and structural features, rocking and gating motions of TM1–6 and TM7–12 domains were screened to assess large-scale conformational changes, as well as Buried Surface Area (BSA) analyses to quantify differences in ligand exposure and hydrophobic engagement. Additional interpretation of P-gp ligand-dependent inward–outward states was achieved by conformational clustering and principal component analysis (PCA).

Plots of RMSD show that both systems exhibit an initial equilibration phase within the first ∼50 ns, followed by stabilization at distinct RMSD plateaus: for P-gp/2c, the RMSD rapidly increases to approximately 3.5–4.0 Å, after which it remains relatively constant with moderate fluctuations, indicating that the complex achieves a stable conformational ensemble with no major backbone rearrangements during the production run, in contrast, P-gp/5b displays a slightly higher and more variable RMSD profile, oscillating around 4.5–5.0 Å with persistent local fluctuations even after 300 ns. RoG provides complementary insights into the overall compactness of P-gp during the simulation. For P-gp/2c, the RoG decreases slightly over time, from ∼39.8 Å to ∼39.2 Å, exhibiting low-frequency oscillations typical of large membrane proteins but no signs of unfolding or domain separation. This gradual compaction suggests a minor tightening of the transmembrane helices and intracellular loops, possibly reflecting stabilization of an inward-facing conformational state. Conversely, the P-gp/5b complex maintains a higher RoG throughout the trajectory (∼40.4 Å to ∼39.6 Å), with broader amplitude oscillations. Such enhanced flexibility and delayed compaction point to a looser packing of the transmembrane domains was likely caused by a less cohesive interaction pattern at the ligand-binding site.

With respect to RSMF, the Cα atom profiles reported in Figure display the typical fluctuation pattern of P-gp, with moderate flexibility (RMSF ≈ 1–3 Å) along the transmembrane (TM) helices and larger oscillations at the extracellular loops and N-/C-terminal segments. However, distinct amplitude differences are observed between the 2c and 5b systems, revealing ligand-dependent modulation of backbone dynamics. In the P-gp/2c complex, most TM helices remain relatively rigid, and the fluctuation peaks are confined to peripheral regions. The largest RMSF values (∼6–8 Å) are localized in cytosolic linker loops connecting TM4-TM5 and TM10-TM11, which are known to undergo breathing motions associated with gating transitions. Importantly, the amplitudes of these fluctuations are smaller than those observed in the 5b complex, suggesting that 2c stabilizes the interdomain coupling through tighter packing and reduced local disorder. Conversely, P-gp/5b exhibits significantly higher fluctuations across several transmembrane segments, with RMSF maxima exceeding 10–12 Å near residues 50–70 and 850–870, corresponding to helices TM1–TM2 and TM10. These helices flank the inner cavity and are crucial for ligand-induced conformational exchange between inward- and outward-facing states. The enhanced flexibility of these regions in the 5b complex points to a less restrained conformational landscape, consistent with a weaker or more transient anchoring of the ligand within the binding pocket.

Percentage of ligand–receptor interactions of 2c (left) and 5b (right) molecular dynamics trajectories.

Time evolution of the center-of-mass (CoM) distance between the protein backbone and the ligand was also monitored along the trajectories to evaluate and quantify possible detachment or repositioning events during the simulation (Figure ). In the P-gp/2c complex, the CoM distance remains remarkably stable throughout the entire trajectory, oscillating narrowly around 24 ± 1 Å with only minor fluctuations, thus 2c remains tightly anchored within the transmembrane cavity without a significant drift toward the cytosolic or extracellular vestibules. The small-amplitude oscillations observed after ∼200 ns likely reflect local breathing motions of the transmembrane domains rather than true ligand dissociation events. By contrast, the P-gp/5b trajectory displays a consistently larger CoM separation, fluctuating around 43 ± 1 Å, with broader oscillations and no clear long-term stabilization. The gradual downward trend after 400 ns may reflect partial repositioning or looser coupling between the ligand and the central cavity pointing to a less cohesive protein–ligand association inside the binding pocket.

The BSA between P-gp and the bound ligand provides a quantitative measure of the extent of hydrophobic and van der Waals contacts stabilizing the complex and represents a reliable indicator of how deeply and persistently the ligand is embedded within the protein (Figure ). The buried surface of P-gp/2c complex oscillates within a relatively narrow range (880–970 Å2) and stabilizes around an average of approximately 930 Å2 after the initial equilibration phase.

RMSF and Rog timeline plot of the last 500 ns of 2c (left) and 5b (right) P-gp complex trajectory.

These values denote a compact and deeply buried ligand configuration, endowing 2c with extensive van der Waals and hydrophobic contacts with the surrounding transmembrane helices. The narrow amplitude of fluctuations further confirms that the ligand remains firmly anchored within the central cavity, with only minor adjustments in side-chain packing and cavity breathing during the 500 ns production window. Conversely, the P-gp/5b system exhibits a markedly higher interfacial area, fluctuating around 1400–1500 Å2, with broader and less regular oscillations. While a larger BSA might appear to indicate a stronger binding, in this context, it reflects partial unfolding and increased surface exposure of the binding cavity rather than enhanced ligand engagement. The extended fluctuations suggest a less compact protein–ligand interface, where 5b interacts more diffusely across multiple transient pockets instead of maintaining a single, well-defined pose. Such dynamic instability likely results from the absence of the intramolecular hydrogen bond present in 2c, leading to increased conformational entropy and weaker overall packing efficiency.

To probe large-scale conformational transitions associated with the inward–outward cycle of P-gp, two collective geometric parameters were monitored: the rocking angle between transmembrane domain (TMD) bundles TM1–6 and TM7–12, and the cytosolic gating distance measured between the intracellular ends of TM4/TM6 and TM10/TM12. These descriptors provide a quantitative assessment of how ligand binding modulates the global dynamics of P-gp’s transmembrane scaffold (Figure ). In the P-gp/2c complex, both parameters exhibit restricted oscillations and maintain relatively narrow distributions throughout the 100–600 ns trajectory. The rocking angle remains confined between 33° and 39°, with a smooth oscillatory pattern centered around ∼36°, indicating a balanced interdomain alignment typical of the inward-closed conformation. Similarly, the cytosolic gate distance fluctuates modestly between 26 and 28 Å, stabilizing after the first 200 ns near 27 Å. This stability implies that the two transmembrane halves of P-gp remain compact and symmetrically coupled, preventing premature opening of the cytosolic vestibule. Such restrained internal motion is consistent with a ligand-induced stabilization of the inward-closed (occluded) state, where 2c acts as a structural wedge reinforcing interhelical contacts. In stark contrast, the P-gp/5b complex reveals significantly broader and lower amplitude gating behavior. The rocking angle fluctuates widely between 12° and 19°, approximately half the range observed for 2c, and the mean cytosolic distance is shifted upward to 30–32 Å (Figure ). These features correspond to a partially open, inward-facing conformation, reflecting enhanced flexibility of the transmembrane helices and weaker coupling between the two TMD lobes. The increased opening of the cytosolic gate and reduced rocking coherence suggest that 5b binding is less effective in constraining the large-scale motions required for the transition toward the outward-facing state.

The distinct dynamical signatures observed for 2c and 5b directly correlate with their binding stability and functional potency. The oxygen atom in 2c serving as a hydrogen bond acceptor enables the formation of an intramolecular hydrogen bond that preorganizes the ligand, ensuring tighter complementarity within the central binding pocket. This preorganization reduces entropic flexibility and strengthens coupling across the TMD interface, yielding a rigid, inward-closed conformation that is less prone to large-scale domain separation. Conversely, 5b, lacking this feature, induces a looser packing that facilitates cytosolic opening and interdomain rocking, a hallmark of the lower affinity state.

Finally, principal component analysis (PCA) was employed to project the multidimensional atomic fluctuations of P-gp onto a reduced conformational space defined by the two dominant eigenvectors (PC1 and PC2), which collectively capture the major correlated motions of the transmembrane helices. By discrimination of frames according to the sign of PC1, two distinct conformational basins were identified: inward-facing (blue) and outward-facing (red) states (Figure ). This dimensionality reduction enables a quantitative comparison of how ligand binding modulates the conformational landscape of P-gp.

In the P-gp/2c trajectory, the projection onto the PC1-PC2 space reveals a strongly bimodal distribution dominated by the inward-facing conformational cluster. Most frames populate the negative PC1 region, corresponding to a compact, occluded structure, where the cytosolic vestibule remains closed and the extracellular side is partially sealed. Transitions toward positive PC1 values, associated with an outward-opening motion, are limited and transient, suggesting that 2c effectively stabilizes the inward/occluded basin.

The high density and narrow dispersion of the blue cluster confirm that this conformational state is not only dominant but also structurally homogeneous, reflecting a reduction in conformational entropy consistent with the preorganized and internally hydrogen-bonded geometry of 2c. These results corroborate the restricted gating distances and moderate rocking angles observed earlier, highlighting a cohesive allosteric response to ligand binding.

In contrast, the P-gp/5b system displays a broader and more continuous trajectory along PC1, with a significant sampling of the positive (red) region corresponding to outward-facing conformations. The transition pathway between inward- and outward-states is clearly more populated, indicating an enhanced propensity for domain separation and cytosolic opening. This conformational flexibility mirrors the elevated RMSD, expanded CoM distance, and fluctuating BSA detected in the same system, underscoring a less-constrained and more dynamic binding mode. The absence of the oxygen substituent, and therefore of the stabilizing intramolecular bond, renders 5b unable to maintain the same degree of preorganization, resulting in greater ligand-induced plasticity and a weaker capacity to lock the protein in its inward closed configuration.

From a mechanistic standpoint, these PCA-derived energy landscapes support ligand-dependent modulation of the P-gp conformational equilibrium. 2c favors the inward-occluded ensemble, compatible with an inhibitory, substrate-trapping mode that reduces transporter cycling efficiency, whereas 5b promotes transitions toward outward-open geometries, consistent with a less potent or transient interaction (Figure ).

Collectively, these results highlight a unified mechanistic principle: intramolecular hydrogen bonding promotes conformational preorganization that enhances ligand efficiency, stabilizes the inward-facing P-gp state, and modulates efflux activity.

Conclusions

Compound 2c (EC50 = 1.8 μM) is the lead compound of this series because it displayed higher P-gp activity and c log P than radiolabeled compound [18F]­MC225 that was used in two human clinical trials. This result is corroborated by docking where the compound is shown and an important interacting point with P-gp due to isosteric substitution on the spacer, whereas amine and amides were inactive.

This compound will be [11C] radiolabeled and tested in preclinical studies, in fact, the precursor, the phenol derivative, has been prepared as a precursor of radiolabeled compounds. This no less could be radiolabeled in the same way as reported for [18F]­MC225 (Figure ) that permits a potential clinical employment in particular to target P-gp at the health level for several pathologies and to study the failure of therapies of heart failure.
