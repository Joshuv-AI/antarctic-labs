Weak minimizing property and reflexivity
Vladimir Kadets, Geivison Ribeiro

Abstract
. For an operator T : X → Y T:X\to Y denote m ⁡ ( T ) = inf { ‖ T ​ x ‖ : x ∈ S X } m(T)=\inf\{\|Tx\|:x\in S_{X}\} . A sequence ( x n ) n (x_{n})_{n} in S X S_{X} is said to be minimizing for T T if ‖ T ​ x n ‖ \|Tx_{n}\| tends to m ⁡ ( T ) m(T) . In 2020 U.S. Chakraborty introduced and studied the following weak minimizing property (WmP): a pair ( X , Y ) (X,Y) of Banach spaces is said to have the WmP if, for every bounded linear operator T : X → Y T:X\to Y that admits a non-weakly null minimizing sequence, the function x ↦ ‖ T ​ x ‖ x\mapsto\|Tx\| attains its minimum on the unit sphere. We present the following new results about the WmP for pairs of infinite-dimensional separable Banach spaces: (i) If ( X , Y ) (X,Y) has the WmP, then X X is reflexive. (ii) If X X is reflexive and Y Y does not contain isomorphic copies of X X , then ( X , Y ) (X,Y) has the WmP. The first result extends to non-separable X X if and only if X X possesses a countable total set of functionals.

1.
Introduction

For Banach spaces
X,Y
over the same field of scalars, denote
L(X,Y)
the space of all bounded linear operators
T:X\to Y
and, for every
T\in L(X,Y)
, denote
\phi_{T}
the real-valued function
\phi_{T}(x)=\|Tx\|
defined on the unit sphere
S_{X}
of
X
. In this notation,
\|T\|=\sup\{\phi_{T}(x):x\in S_{X}\}
. For the infimum of
\phi_{T}
there is no standard notation. In this paper, the notation

m(T)=\inf\{\|Tx\|:x\in S_{X}\}

from
[
7
]
will be used.

For a finite-dimensional
X
,
\phi_{T}
attains its maximum and minimum, but for infinite-dimensional
X
this is not always the case. An operator
T\in L(X,Y)
is called
norm-attaining
if
\phi_{T}
attains its maximum. There is a number of results regarding norm-attainment, which happens to be a non-trivial question even for functionals. Say,
X
is reflexive if and only if every functional
f\in X^{*}
attains its norm (R.C. James
[
15
]
); for every
X
the set of norm-attaining functionals is dense in
X^{*}
(Bishop and Phelps
[
4
]
), see
[
9
, Chapter 1]
. For general operators, the classification of pairs
(X,Y)
for which the set of norm-attaining operators
L_{NA}(X,Y)
is dense in
L(X,Y)
was originated by J. Lindenstrauss
[
18
]
who in particular presented the first examples in which the density is absent. This study was advanced by many authors and a number of great results were obtained, see the survey
[
1
]
by María D. Acosta. Among these results let us mention the famous J. Bourgain’s theorem
[
5
]
that
X
has the Radon-Nikodým property if and only if, for every equivalent norm on
X
and for every Banach space
Y
, the set
L_{NA}(X,Y)
is dense in
L(X,Y)
. Despite of extensive study the classification of those pairs that
\overline{L_{NA}(X,Y)}=L(X,Y)
remains unaccomplished, with a striking example of an open problem of whether
\overline{L_{NA}(X,Y)}=L(X,Y)
for every two-dimensional
Y
and every
X
, see discussion in
[
17
]
.

An operator
T\in L(X,Y)
is said to be
min-attaining
if
\phi_{T}
attains its minimum. Denote
\MA(X,Y)
the set of all min-attaining operators
T\in L(X,Y)
. Although both norm attainment and min-attainment are natural optimization problems, the behavior of
\MA(X,Y)
and
L_{NA}(X,Y)
is not the same by the simple reason that
\MA(X,Y)
contains all non-injective operators. We refer the reader to
[
11
, Section 1.2]
by Domingo García, Manuel Maestre, Miguel Martín, and Óscar Roldán for references and a short account of corresponding results.

In this short article the following
weak minimizing property
is addressed.

Definition 1.1

(U.S. Chakraborty,
[
7
, Definition 2.7]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces.

•

For
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, a sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
in
S_{X}
is said to minimize
T
if
\|Tx_{n}\|\to m(T)
. In this case,
(x_{n})_{n}
is called a minimizing sequence for
T
.

•

A pair
(X,Y)
is said to have the weak minimizing property (WmP) if every bounded linear operator
T:X\to Y
that admits a non-weakly null minimizing sequence is min-attaining.

The WmP is a direct analog of the
weak maximizing property
(WMP) that was introduced by Richard M. Aron, Domingo García, Daniel Pellegrino and Eduardo V. Teixeira
[
3
]
as follows: a pair of Banach spaces
(E,F)
is said to have
the WMP if for any
T\in L(E,F)
, the existence of a non-weakly null maximizing sequence for
T
implies that
T
attains its norm.

WmP is a useful tool for study of approximation of operators by members of
\MA(X,Y)
. In particular, as it was remarked by Manwook Han
[
14
]
, WmP of
(X,Y)
implies the density of
\MA(X,Y)
in
L(X,Y)
.

In the already cited papers
[
7
]
and especially
[
14
]
an extensive study of WmP was performed, in particular for all pairs of classical sequence spaces and for many other concrete pairs of spaces it was determined whether they possess the WmP or not. Below we complement this study by exhibiting the connection of WmP of
(X,Y)
with the reflexivity of
X
. Namely,
we demonstrate that

–

If
(X,Y)
has the WmP,
X
possesses a countable total set of functionals and
Y
is infinite-dimensional, then
X
is reflexive (Theorem
2.2
).

–

If
X
is reflexive and
Y
does not contain isomorphic copies of
X
, then
(X,Y)
has the WmP (Theorem
2.4
).

–

If
X
is reflexive and
Y
contains an isomorphic copy of
X
, then there is an equivalent norm on
Y
such that, for this equivalent norm,
(X,Y)
does not have the WmP (Theorem
2.4
).

In the preliminary version of the paper (
arXiv:2604.18534v1
), the first result for nonseparable
X
was stated as an open problem. The solution of that problem by the second author leaded to the strengthening of the result and to the extension of the list of the authors.

Remark that for
(X,Y)
with the WMP the reflexivity of
X
was demonstrated in
[
3
, Corollary 2.5]
, but the proof does not work for WmP because it is based on the existence of not norm-attaining functionals on every nonreflexive space, which is evidently not true for the min-attainment.

As an application in Corollary
2.9
we present a new solution of
[
3
, Questions 3.2(2)]
that provides a wide range of reflexive spaces
X
for which
(X,X)
does not have the WMP.

2.
The results

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a non-reflexive separable Banach space. Then, there is a biorthogonal system
(u_{n},u_{n}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset S_{X}\times X^{*}
such that

(1)

(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a complete system of vectors in
X
and
(u_{n}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a total system of functionals over
X
(together this means that
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an
M
-basis of
X
).

(2)

(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not a weakly null sequence.

Proof.

According to D.P. and V.D. Milman characterization of non-reflexive spaces
[
21
]
, there is a basic sequence
(e_{n})
in
X
and a functional
u^{*}\in X^{*}
such that
\|e_{n}\|=1
and
\inf_{n}\re u^{*}(e_{n})>0
(see
[
2
, Lemma 1.1]
for a short proof). Then
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not a weakly null sequence.

It remains to apply V.I. Gurarii and M.I. Kadets’ Theorem
[
12
]
, see
[
13
, Theorem 1.45]
which guaranties the existence of an
M
-basis
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
that contains
(e_{n})
as a subsequence.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be a non-reflexive Banach space that possesses a countable total set of functionals and
Y
be an infinite-dimensional Banach space, then
(X,Y)
does not have the WmP.

Proof.

We will consider first the case of separable
X
(when the existence of a countable total set of functionals is automatic, see
[
16
, Section 17.2.4, Corollary 2]
) and the nonseparable case will come afterwards.

Proof for the separable
X
.
Pick
(u_{n},u_{n}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset S_{X}\times X^{*}
like in Lemma
2.1
and let
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a normalized basic sequence in
Y
(see
[
19
, Theorem 1.a.5]
). Define
T:X\to Y
by means of the formula

Tx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}^{*}(x)}{2^{n}\|u_{n}^{*}\|}v_{n},

(2.1)

where the absolute convergence of the series and the inequality
\|T\|\leq 1
may be checked as follows:

\|Tx\|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|u_{n}^{*}(x)\right|}{2^{n}\|u_{n}^{*}\|}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\|u_{n}^{*}\|\|x\|}{2^{n}\|u_{n}^{*}\|}=\|x\|.

For this operator,
Tu_{n}=\frac{v_{n}}{2^{n}\|u_{n}^{*}\|}
; consequently

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Tu_{n}\|=0
. This means that
m(T)=0
and
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the required minimizing not weakly null sequence. Finally,
T
is not min-attaining: if
T(x)=0
, then
u_{n}^{*}(x)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
which means that
x=0
(this is exactly the definition of a total system of functionals over
X
).

Proof for the nonseparable
X
. Let now
(u_{n}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X^{*}
be a total system of functionals over
X
with
\|u_{n}^{*}\|=1
. The nonseparability of
X
implies the nonseparability of
X^{*}
, so we can pick a
g\in X^{*}
with
\dist(g,\spann(u_{n}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})>1
. Denote
M_{k}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\ker u_{j}^{*}
. By the Bipolar Theorem,
M_{k}^{\bot}=\spann\{u_{n}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{k}
. Taking in account the duality between subspaces of
X
and quotient spaces of
X^{*}

[
16
, Section 9.4.2, Theorem 1]
, we see that

\left\|g|_{M_{k}}\right\|_{M_{k}^{*}}=\left\|[g]\right\|_{X^{*}/M_{k}^{\bot}}=\dist(g,M_{k}^{\bot})=\dist(g,\spann\{u_{n}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{k})>1.

This allows us, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, to select a
u_{n}\in S_{M_{n}}
with
g(u_{n})>1
. As in the first part of the proof, pick a normalized basic sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset Y
and define
T:X\to Y
by means of the formula (
2.1
). Then

\|Tu_{k}\|=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}^{*}(u_{k})}{2^{n}\|u_{n}^{*}\|}v_{n}\right\|=\left\|\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}^{*}(u_{k})}{2^{n}\|u_{n}^{*}\|}v_{n}\right\|\leq\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{n}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0.

Consequently
m(T)=0
and
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a minimizing sequence for
T
. The condition
g(u_{n})>1
,
n=1,2,\ldots
, implies that
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not weakly null. Finally,
T
is not min-attaining by the same reason as in the first part of the proof: if
T(x)=0
, then
u_{n}^{*}(x)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
; but
(u_{n}^{*})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X^{*}
is total over
X
, which means that
x=0
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

o

(1)

Let
Y=\ell_{2}
and
X
be a non-reflexive space that does not possesses a countable total set of functionals. Then every operator
T\in L(X,Y)
is not injective so it is min-attaining, which means that
(X,Y)
possesses the WmP in spite of the non-reflexivity of
X
. Thus, the condition of existence of a countable total set of functionals on
X
in Theorem
2.2
cannot be weaken.

(2)

Similarly, if
X
is infinite-dimensional and
\dim Y<\infty
then every operator
T\in L(X,Y)
is not injective, so
(X,Y)
has the WmP independently of reflexivity or non-reflexivity of
X
. This means that the condition
\dim Y=\infty
in Theorem
2.2
cannot be dropped neither.

The next two results deal with the case of reflexive
X
. The first of them is a very simple remark somehow overlooked in the previous papers.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be reflexive and
Y
be a Banach space that does not contain an isomorphic copy of
X
, then
(X,Y)
possesses the WmP.

Proof.

Let
T:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator that admits a non-weakly null minimizing sequence
(x_{n})\subset S_{X}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Tx_{n}\|=m(T).

(2.2)

By our assumption on
Y
,
T
is not an isomorphic embedding, so
m(T)=0
.
Together with (
2.2
) this says that
\lim_{n\to\infty}Tx_{n}=0
.

By the reflexivity of
X
,
(x_{n})
contains a subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})
that weakly converges to a non-zero element
x\in X
. Then,

T(x)=w-\lim_{k\to\infty}Tx_{n_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}Tx_{n_{k}}=0,

which means that
T
attains its
m(T)=0
at the point
\frac{x}{\|x\|}\in S_{X}
.
∎

As a simple consequence one gets the WmP for all pairs of the form
(L_{p}[0,1],\ell_{q})
,
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\infty
, except of the pair
(L_{2}[0,1],\ell_{2})
for which the WmP is also valid but by other argument, see
[
7
, Theorem 2.9]
where in particular the WmP is demonstrated for pairs of infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert spaces. Remark, that the WmP of the pairs
(\ell_{p},\ell_{q})
for
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\infty
and
p\neq q
demonstrated in
[
7
, Theorem 2.9]
follow from our Theorem
2.4
, but
[
7
, Theorem 2.9]
includes also the case of
p=q
. The above result covers also several examples from
[
14
]
, say
(X_{r},Y_{0})
from
[
14
, Corollary 2.8]
, but it does not cover many other positive results from
[
14
]
where the specific geometry of the involved spaces plays crucial role. We refer to the classical Lindenstrauss-Tzafriri book
[
19
,
20
]
where an extensive information about embeddability or non-embeddability of classical spaces into one another is given.

Theorem
2.2
demonstrates a strong isomorphic restriction (reflexivity) on the first space in any pair
(X,Y)\in\mathrm{WmP}
of infinite-dimensional separable spaces. Now we are going to demonstrate that there are no other possible restriction on
X
: every reflexive
X
serves as the first space in some pair
(X,Y)\in\mathrm{WmP}
of infinite-dimensional spaces.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
X
be a infinite-dimensional reflexive Banach space and
Y
be an infinite-dimensional Banach space that does not contain infinite-dimensional reflexive subspaces. Then the pair
(X,Y)
possesses the WmP. In particular, the pairs
(X,\ell_{1})
and
(X,c_{0})
possess the WmP.

Proof.

The main part evidently follows from Theorem
2.4
. The fact that
\ell_{1}
and
c_{0}
do not have infinite-dimensional reflexive subspaces follows from
[
19
, Proposition 2.a.2]
.
∎

Recall that a separable Banach space is called isomorphically (isometrically)
universal

if it contains isomorphic (respectively isometric) copies of every separable Banach space. The next corollary says something about possible second space in an WmP pair.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
Y
be a separable infinite-dimensional Banach space that is not isomorphically universal. Then there is an infinite-dimensional reflexive Banach space
X
such that
(X,Y)
possesses the WmP.

Proof.

According to J. Bourgains’s theorem
[
6
]
, see also
[
13
, Theorem 2.14]
, there is a reflexive separable Banach space
X
which does not embed isomorphically in
Y
. It remains to apply Theorem
2.4
for this pair
(X,Y)
.
∎

Theorem
2.2
and
2.4
addressed the cases when either
X
is not reflexive, or
X
is reflexive and
Y
does not contain a copy of
X
. The remaining case when
X
is reflexive and
Y
contains a copy of
X
is addressed in the next theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces,
X
be reflexive and let
Y
contain an isomorphic copy of
X
. Then there is an equivalent norm on
Y
such that, for this equivalent norm, the pair
(X,Y)
does not possess the WmP.

Proof.

Let
Z\subset Y
be the promised subspace isomorphic to
X
and let
T:X\to Y
,
T(X)=Z
be the corresponding isomorphic embedding. We are going to build an equivalent norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
on
Y
such that, for
\widetilde{Y}:=(Y,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})
the operator
\widetilde{T}:X\to\widetilde{Y}
has the following properties that ensure that
(X,\widetilde{Y})
does not possess the WmP:

(1)

m(\widetilde{T})=1
.

(2)

\widetilde{T}
is not min-attaining.

(3)

There is a not weakly null sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset S_{X}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widetilde{T}(u_{n})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=1
.

At first, remark that it is sufficient to define the required norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
only for elements of
Z
, because every equivalent norm on a subspace extends to an equivalent norm on the whole space (see
[
10

), and for the properties

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
on

Let
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset S_{X}
be a basic sequence in
X
. By reflexivity of
X
,
(e_{n})
tends weakly to 0 (weak convergence criterion
[
16
, 17.2.3, Theorem 1]
together with the fact that in a reflexive space every basis is shrinking
[
19
, Theorem 1.b.5]
). Select coefficients
t_{n}
,
n=2,3,\ldots
in such a way that
\|\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{n}e_{n}\|=1-\frac{1}{2^{n}}
and define the unit ball

of the required new norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
on

as the closed convex hull of
T\left(\frac{1}{2}B_{X}\bigcup\left\{\pm\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{n}e_{n}\right)\right\}_{n=2}^{\infty}\right)
.
Then

\frac{1}{2}T(B_{X})\subset U\subset T(B_{X})

which means that
U
generates an equivalent norm on
Z
(recall that
T
is an isomorphism of
X
and

, so
\frac{1}{\|T^{-1}\|}B_{Y}\subset T(B_{X})\subset\|T\|B_{Y}
).

Let us demonstrate the properties
(1)-(3)
of the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
on

. At first, the inclusion

means that for all

we have
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|Tx\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\geq 1
. On the other hand,
T\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{n}e_{n}\right)\in U
, so for the elements

we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T(u_{n})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\frac{1}{\|\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{n}e_{n}\|}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{n}e_{n}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{n}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{n}e_{n}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}1.

Together this means that

as required in (1). Also, for these

\frac{1}{2}e_{1}
, so

as required in (3).

So, it remains to demonstrate (2). Assume to the contrary that there exist a point

such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T(v)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=1

which, due to the definition of

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle\in\conv\left(\frac{1}{2}B_{X}\bigcup\left\{\pm\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{n}e_{n}\right)\right\}_{n=2}^{\infty}\right),

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n,1}\frac{1}{2}z+\sum_{k=2}^{m_{k}}\lambda_{n,k}\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{k}e_{k}\right),z\in B_{X},\sum_{k=1}^{m_{k}}|\lambda_{n,k}|\leq 1,

such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|T(v)-T(v_{n})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0

Denote

\displaystyle\leq\left|v^{*}\left(\lambda_{n,1}\frac{1}{2}z\right)\right|+\sum_{k=2}^{m_{k}}\left|v^{*}\left(\lambda_{n,k}\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{k}e_{k}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{n,1}|+\sum_{k=2}^{N}\left|v^{*}\left(\lambda_{n,k}\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{k}e_{k}\right)\right)\right|+\sum_{k=N+1}^{m_{k}}\left|v^{*}\left(\lambda_{n,k}\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+t_{k}e_{k}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{n,1}|+\sum_{k=2}^{N}\left|\lambda_{n,k}\right|\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)+\sum_{k=N+1}^{m_{k}}\frac{3}{4}\left|\lambda_{n,k}\right|\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{N}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{m_{k}}|\lambda_{n,k}|\leq 1-\frac{1}{2^{N}}.

But, on the other hand,
\|v-v_{n}\|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
, so
\left|v^{*}\left(v_{n}\right)\right|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\left|v^{*}\left(v\right)\right|=1
which means that we reached a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
E
be a separable infinite-dimensional Banach space that is isometrically universal (for example
E=C[0,1]
). Then for every infinite-dimensional reflexive Banach space
X
the pair
(X,E)
does not possess the WmP.

Proof.

According to Theorem
2.7
, there is a separable Banach space
Y

. Then

witnesses the absence of WmP for the pair
(X,E)
.
∎
