Second-Order Optimality Conditions for Sparse Differentiable Optimization Problems via Limiting Second-Order Subdifferentials
Luu Thi Thu Huyen, Liguo Jiao, Nguyen Van Tuyen

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a class of sparse optimization problems in which both the objective and constraint functions are Fréchet differentiable and possess locally Lipschitz continuous gradient mappings. More precisely, by utilizing the limiting (Mordukhovich) second-order subdifferential of the associated Lagrangian function, we establish new second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for local optimal solutions. The obtained results are derived under mild assumptions and extend several existing results in the literature. In addition, we apply our theoretical developments to sparse multiobjective optimization problems and derive second-order sufficient optimality conditions for efficient solutions. Several examples are also presented to demonstrate the applicability and effectiveness of the proposed results.

Introduction

Sparse optimization has attracted significant attention in recent years due to its wide range of applications in signal processing, machine learning, statistics, compressed sensing, image reconstruction, portfolio optimization, and control theory. A standard sparse optimization problem (SP) can be formulated as

\min f(x)\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ x\in\Phi,\|x\|_{0}\leq s,

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a given objective function,
\Phi\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a constraint set,
s\in(0,n)
is a given positive integer, and
\|\cdot\|_{0}
denotes the number of nonzero components of a vector. The cardinality constraint
\|x\|_{0}\leq s
is a fundamental tool for enforcing sparsity of solutions.

The growing interest in sparse optimization is mainly motivated by its practical importance. In compressed sensing, sparse optimization is used to recover signals from incomplete measurements; see, e.g.,
CandesRombergTao2006
;
Donoho2006
. In statistics and machine learning, sparse models play a central role in variable selection and high-dimensional regression; see, e.g.,
Tibshirani1996
;
Hastie2009
. Sparse portfolio selection has also been extensively studied in mathematical finance, while sparse control problems arise naturally in networked systems and optimal control; see, e.g.,
Bienstock1996
;
Tillmann2024
. Since sparse solutions are often more interpretable, stable, and computationally efficient, sparse optimization has become one of the most active topics in modern optimization theory.

From the mathematical viewpoint, sparse optimization problems are highly challenging because the cardinality function is discontinuous and nonconvex. Even when the objective function and the remaining constraints are smooth, the feasible set generated by the cardinality constraint is nonconvex and lacks a regular geometric structure. Consequently, many classical techniques from nonlinear programming cannot be directly applied. In particular, the derivation of optimality conditions and constraint qualifications becomes substantially more delicate than in standard smooth optimization problems.

To overcome these difficulties, several approaches have been proposed in the literature. One important direction reformulates sparse optimization problems as mathematical programs with complementarity constraints or disjunctive programs; see, e.g.,
Benko-et-al-22
;
BucherSchwartz2018
;
BurdakovKanzowSchwartz2016
;
Gfrerer2014
;
KanzowSchwartz2013
;
LiangYe2021
;
Mehlitz
;
Xiao-Ye-24
;
XuYe
. Another influential framework is based on variational analysis and generalized differentiation; see the monographs of Mordukhovich
Mordukhovich_2006
;
Mordukhovich_2018
and Rockafellar and Wets
Rockafellar1998
. These techniques provide powerful tools for analyzing nonsmooth and nonconvex structures arising in sparse optimization.

First- and second-order optimality conditions for sparse optimization have been intensively investigated over the last decade. For instance, Beck and Elda
BeckEldar2013
introduced and investigated three types of first-order necessary optimality conditions for the (
SP
) problem with a single sparsity constraint, namely, basic feasibility, L-stationarity, and coordinate-wise optimality. Kanzow and Schwartz
KanzowSchwartz2013
studied continuous reformulations of sparse optimization problems and derived several stationarity conditions. Subsequently, Beck and Hall
BeckHalla
generalized these results to optimization problems involving a continuously differentiable objective function over sparse symmetric sets, which constitute a particular class of feasible sets for the (
SP
) problem. Recently, Lu
Lu-2015
studied the notion of strong L-stationarity for optimization problems over sparse symmetric sets considered in
BeckHalla
. In addition, for the (
SP
) problem, Lu and Zhang
LuZhang
established first-order necessary optimality conditions by employing subspace techniques under the Robinson constraint qualification. In
Pan-Xiu-Zhou
, Pan et al. derived explicit formulas for the Bouligrand/Clarke tangent cone and the regular/Clarke normal cone to

\mathcal{S}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\|x\|_{0}\leq s\}

and established the corresponding first- and second-order optimality conditions. More recently, Pan et al.
Pan-Luo-Xiu_2017
and Pan et al.
Pan-Xiu-Fan
introduced the so-called restricted linear independence constraint qualification, restricted Robinson constraint qualification, and restricted Mangasarian–Fromovitz constraint qualification, and established first- and second-order optimality conditions for problem (
SP
) with operator or functional constraints. Other constraint qualifications, together with first- and second-order optimality conditions for problem (
SP
) with functional constraints, can be found in
BurdakovKanzowSchwartz2016
;
Flegel-et-al-07
;
Henrion-Outrata-05
;
Movahedian-et-al-19
;
Movahedian-et-al
;
Xiao-Ye-24
and the references therein. We emphasize that all the aforementioned results concerning second-order optimality conditions for problem (
SP
) were derived under rather strong assumptions, such as the
C^{2}
-smoothness of the objective and constraint functions.

However, many practical optimization models involve functions that are only continuously differentiable rather than twice continuously differentiable. Such situations naturally arise in machine learning, composite optimization, and robust optimization models, for example, sparse Huber regression
Pan-Wang-etal
and
\ell_{2}-\ell_{p}
Tikhonov regularization
Borges-etal
. In this framework, classical Hessian-based approaches are generally no longer applicable, and one must instead employ generalized second-order constructions such as second-order epi-derivatives
Rockafellar1998
;
Borwein2005
;
Khanh-Tuan_2007
;
Mohammadi-etal
and second-order subdifferentials
Mordukhovich_2006
;
Mordukhovich_2018
;
Mordukhovich_2024
;
An-Xu-Yen
;
An-Tuyen
;
Huy-Tuyen-16
;
Huy-Tuyen-19
;
Tuyen-Huy-Kim
;
ChieuLeeYen2017
;
Khanh-etal-25
. Therefore, the development of second-order optimality conditions for sparse optimization problems with non-
C^{2}
data is of significant theoretical and practical importance.

In
ChenJW-et_al_2024
, Chen et al. established second-order optimality conditions for nonsmooth sparse multiobjective optimization problems by employing Dini directional derivatives of the objective functions together with the Bouligand tangent cone and the second-order tangent set of the sparse set. Kan and Song
Kan-Song
derived second-order optimality conditions for the existence of augmented Lagrange multipliers in optimization problems with sparsity and abstract constraints via the second-order epi-derivative of the augmented Lagrangian. More recently, Chen et al.
ChenJW-et-at-2026
investigated second-order optimality conditions for sparsity-constrained optimization problems under assumptions involving the Fréchet second-order subdifferential of the Lagrangian function, instead of the twice continuous differentiability of the objective and constraint functions. Nevertheless, the Fréchet second-order subdifferential is, in some sense, rather restrictive, since it may be empty even for
C^{1,1}
functions; see Example
2
below. In such cases, the corresponding second-order necessary optimality conditions become meaningless.

Motivated by the above observations, this paper is devoted to the study of second-order optimality conditions for sparse optimization problems with
C^{1,1}
data by employing the so-called limiting (Mordukhovich) second-order subdifferential. It is worth noting that the limiting second-order subdifferential of
C^{1,1}
functions is always nonempty and compact. The results obtained in this work are new.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall preliminary notions and basic results from variational analysis and generalized differentiation. Section
3
is devoted to the derivation of second-order necessary optimality conditions. In Section
4
, we establish second-order sufficient conditions for local optimality. Applications to multiobjective
optimization problems are presented in Section
5
. Finally, concluding remarks are presented in the last section.

Throughout this work we deal with the Euclidean space

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to{\bar{x}}}F(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,:\,\exists x_{k}\to{\bar{x}},\exists y_{k}\in F(x_{k}),y_{k}\to y\}.

Definition 1
(

Let

(i)

(ii)

T^{C}_{X}(\bar{x}):=\big\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\forall x_{k}\xrightarrow{X}\bar{x},\forall t_{k}\to 0^{+},\exists u_{k}\to u,x_{k}+t_{k}u_{k}\in X\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

(iii)

The
radial tangent cone

\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x})
to

at

\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x}):=\big\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\exists\tau>0,\bar{x}+tu\in X\ \ \forall t\in[0,\tau]\big\}.

Bonnans-Shapiro
;
Borwein2005

Remark 1

(i)

The contingent and Clarke tangent cones are closed, whereas it can happen that the radial cone is not closed; see Example
1
(ii) below. The Clarke tangent cone is always convex.

(ii)

For
X_{1},\ldots,X_{p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{x}\in X:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}X_{i}
, it holds that

T_{X}(\bar{x})\subset\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}T_{X_{i}}(\bar{x}).

(iii)

The following inclusions hold:

\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x})\subset T_{X}(\bar{x}),\ \ T^{C}_{X}(\bar{x})\subset T_{X}(\bar{x}),

and when
X
is convex, then
\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x})=\mathrm{cone}(X-\bar{x})
and

T^{C}_{X}(\bar{x})=T_{X}(\bar{x})=\mathrm{cl}\,\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x}).

The next example shows that there is no inclusion relation between
\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x})
and
T^{C}_{X}(\bar{x})
.

Example 1

(i)

Let

. Then, it is easy to check that

(ii)

Let

and
\bar{x}=(0,0)
. Then
X
is convex,

\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x})=\mathrm{cone}(X-\bar{x})=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,x_{2}>0\}\cup\{(0,0)\},

and

T^{C}_{X}(\bar{x})=T_{X}(\bar{x})=\mathrm{cl}\,\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x})=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,x_{2}\geq 0\}.

Hence
T^{C}_{X}(\bar{x})\nsubseteq\mathcal{R}_{X}(\bar{x})
.

Let

(ii)

3
Second-Order Necessary Optimality Conditions

Consider the following sparse optimization problem

\min f(x)\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ x\in\Omega:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\phi(x)\in D,\|x\|_{0}\leq s\},

(SP)

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\phi_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, are of class
C^{1}
functions,
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a closed and convex cone, and
s\in(0,n)
is any given positive
integer. Let us denote
\Phi
and
\mathcal{S}
, respectively, by

\Phi:=\phi^{-1}(D)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{S}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\|x\|_{0}\leq s\}.

Then
\Omega=\Phi\cap\mathcal{S}
and the sparse constraint set
\mathcal{S}
can be formulated as

\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J},

where
\mathcal{J}:=\{J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\,:\,|J|=s\}
and

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J}:=\mathrm{span}\{e_{i}\,:\,i\in J\}.

For
x\in\mathcal{S}
, the support index set
\Gamma(x)
of
x
is defined by

\Gamma(x):=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\,:\,x_{i}\neq 0\}.

For a subset
I
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
x_{I}:=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
and the cardinality of
I
by
|I|
. The complement of
I
is denoted by
I^{c}
.

For
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, we denote
hereafter

\bar{J}:=\Gamma(\bar{x})
and
\mathcal{J}(\bar{x}):=\{J\in\mathcal{J}\,:\,J\supset\bar{J}\}
.

Definition 6
(
see
(
Pan-Luo-Xiu_2017
, Definition 3.1)
)

Let
0\neq\bar{x}\in\Omega
and
\bar{\lambda}\in N_{D}(\phi(\bar{x}))
. We say that:

(i)

The
Robinson’s constraint qualification
(RCQ) for
\Phi
holds at
\bar{x}
if

0\in\mathrm{int}\,\{\phi(\bar{x})+\nabla\phi(\bar{x})\mathbb{R}^{n}-D\}.

(ii)

The
restricted Robinson’s constraint qualification
(RRCQ) holds at
\bar{x}
if

0\in\mathrm{int}\,\{\phi(\bar{x})+\nabla\phi(\bar{x})\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}-D\}.

(iii)

The
restricted strict Robinson’s constraint qualification
(RSRCQ) holds at the pair
(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})
if

0\in\mathrm{int}\,\{\phi(\bar{x})+\nabla\phi(\bar{x})\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}-D_{0}\},

where
D_{0}:=\{y\in D\,:\,\langle\bar{\lambda},y-\phi(\bar{x})\rangle=0\}
.

Remark 2

(i)

The following implications hold:

\text{RSRCQ}\Rightarrow\text{RRCQ}\Rightarrow\text{RCQ}.

(ii)

(see
(
Bonnans-Shapiro
, Corollary 2.91)
) If the RCQ holds for
\Phi
at
\bar{x}
, then

T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\nabla\phi(\bar{x})d\in T_{D}(\phi(\bar{x}))\}.

(iii)

(see
(
Bonnans-Shapiro
, p. 150)
) Let
\bar{x}\in\Phi
. Then, one has

N_{D}(\phi(\bar{x}))=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,:\,\lambda\in D^{*},\langle\lambda,\phi(\bar{x})\rangle=0\},

where
D^{*}:=\{y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,:\,\langle y^{*},y\rangle\leq 0\ \ \forall y\in D\}
is the dual cone of

.

(
see
(

Let

. If the RRCQ holds at

, then

T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})\cap T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=(\nabla\phi(\bar{x}))^{-1}(T_{D}(\phi(\bar{x})))\cap T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x}).

Lemma 2
(
see
(
Pan-Xiu-Zhou
, Theorems 2.1–2.2)
)

For any
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{S}
, we have:

(i)

T^{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
and
N^{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{{\bar{J}}^{c}}
;

(ii)

T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\bigcup\{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J}\,:\,J\supseteq\bar{J},|J|=s\}
,

\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{{\bar{J}}^{c}},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ |\bar{J}|=s,\\
\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ |\bar{J}|<s,\end{cases}

and

{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{{\bar{J}}^{c}},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ |\bar{J}|=s,\\
\bigcup\{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J^{c}}\,:\,J\supseteq\bar{J},|J|=s\},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ |\bar{J}|<s.\end{cases}

The Lagrangian associated with problem (
SP
) is defined by

L(x,\lambda):=f(x)+\langle\lambda,\phi(x)\rangle\ \ \forall(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Definition 7
(
see
Pan-Luo-Xiu_2017
)

Let
\bar{x}\in\Omega
. The sets of Lagrange multipliers in the sense of Fréchet and Clarke are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{S}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\,:\,-\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\lambda)\in\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x}),\lambda\in N_{D}(\phi(\bar{x}))\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\,:\,-\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\lambda)\in{N}^{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x}),\lambda\in N_{D}(\phi(\bar{x}))\}.

Definition 8

Let
\bar{x}\in\Omega
. We say that:

(i)

\bar{x}
is a
local optimal solution
to problem (
SP
) if there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that
f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})
for all
x\in\Omega\cap U
.

(ii)

(see
(
Auslender_1984
, Definition 2.1)
)
\bar{x}
is an

to problem (

) if there exist a constant

and a neighborhood

of
\bar{x}

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{2}\,c\,\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\cap U.

(
see
(

)

Let
\bar{x}\neq 0
be a local optimal solution to problem (

) and
\bar{\lambda}\in N_{D}(\phi(\bar{x}))
.

(i)

If the RRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then
\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})

(ii)

3.1
Sparse Optimization with Equality Constraints

We first consider second-order optimality conditions for problem (
SP
) with
D=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\}
.

Theorem 3.1

Suppose that
\bar{x}\neq 0
is a local optimal solution to (
SP
) with
D=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\}
, and
f,\phi
are of class
C^{1,1}
around
\bar{x}
.

(i)

If the RRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
and
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=s
, then for each
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
and
\bar{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})
, there exists
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
such that
\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0
.

(ii)

If the RSRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
and
\bar{\lambda}
is the unique Lagrange multiplier in
\Lambda^{S}(\bar{x})
, then for each
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
, there exists
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
such that
\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0
.

(iii)

If
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
to (
SP
) and the RSRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
with respect to the unique Lagrange multiplier
\bar{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{S}(\bar{x})
, then for each
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\setminus\{0\}
, there exists
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
such that
\langle z,u\rangle>0
.

Proof

(i) Since the RRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
,
\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})
is nonempty due to Lemma
3
. Fix any
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
and
\bar{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})
. Then
\bar{\lambda}\in N_{D}(\phi(\bar{x}))
and by Lemma
2
one has

-\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})\in{N}^{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{{\bar{J}}^{c}}.

By the definition of the contingent cone, there exist sequence
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
and
u_{k}\to u
such that
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k}\in\Omega
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
x_{k}\in\mathcal{S}
and
\phi(x_{k})=\phi(\bar{x})=0_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}
, and so
\langle\bar{\lambda},\phi(x_{k})-\phi(\bar{x})\rangle=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})=f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\geq 0

(1)

for all
k
large enough. Since the RRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
,
T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})\cap T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})
. Furthermore, it follows from the fact that
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=s
and Lemma
1
that
T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
. Hence,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
and so
\langle\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),u\rangle=0
. Clearly
\bar{x}_{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
. Thus

\bar{J}=\Gamma(\bar{x})\subset\Gamma(x_{k})

for all
k
larger enough. This and the facts that
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=|\bar{J}|=s
and
|\Gamma(x_{k})|\leq s
imply that
\Gamma(x_{k})=\Gamma(\bar{x})
and so
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
for all
k
large enough. Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
is a linear subspace,
u_{k}=\frac{1}{t_{k}}(x_{k}-\bar{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
and we therefore get
\langle\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),u_{k}\rangle=0
for all
k
large enough.

We now rewrite
L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})
as follows:

\displaystyle L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})=[L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda})

\displaystyle-L(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,\bar{\lambda})]

\displaystyle+[L(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,\bar{\lambda})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})-t_{k}\langle\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),u\rangle].

Put
L_{1k}:=L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda})-L(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,\bar{\lambda})
and

L_{2k}:=L(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,\bar{\lambda})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})-t_{k}\langle\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),u\rangle.

By the assumption, it is clear that
L(\cdot,\bar{\lambda})
is of class
C^{1,1}
around
\bar{x}
. Let
\ell
be the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla_{x}L(\cdot,\bar{\lambda})
around
\bar{x}
.

By the mean value theorem (see, e.g.,
(
Rudin_PMA1976
, Theorem 5.10)
), there exists
\xi_{k}\in(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,x_{k})
such that

{L}_{1k}=\langle\nabla_{x}{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda}),t_{k}(u_{k}-u)\rangle.

This and the Lipschitzness of
L(\cdot,\bar{\lambda})
imply that

\displaystyle|{L}_{1k}|

\displaystyle=|\langle\nabla_{x}L(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda}),t_{k}(u_{k}-u)\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle\nabla_{x}{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda})-\nabla_{x}{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),t_{k}(u_{k}-u)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq t_{k}\|\nabla_{x}{L}(\xi_{k},\bar{\lambda})-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})\|\cdot\|u_{k}-u\|

\displaystyle\leq\ell t_{k}\|\xi_{k}-\bar{x}\|\cdot\|u_{k}-u\|

\displaystyle\leq\ell t^{2}_{k}(\|u_{k}-u\|+\|u\|)\|u_{k}-u\|,

where the second equality follows from the facts that

\langle\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),u\rangle=\langle\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),u_{k}\rangle=0.

This, together with the fact that
u_{k}\to u
, implies that
\frac{L_{1k}}{t^{2}_{k}}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

On the other hand, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by applying the Taylor formula (Theorem
2.1
) for the function
L(\cdot,\bar{\lambda})
on
[\bar{x},\bar{x}+t_{k}u]
, we can find
v_{k}\in\partial^{2}{L}(\eta_{k},\bar{\lambda})(t_{k}u)
for some
\eta_{k}\in[\bar{x},\bar{x}+t_{k}u]
such that

\displaystyle{L}_{2k}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\langle v_{k},t_{k}u\rangle.

By Proposition
1
(i), there exists
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}{L}(\eta_{k},\bar{\lambda})(u)
such that
v_{k}=t_{k}z_{k}
. Hence

\displaystyle{L}_{2k}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\langle z_{k},u\rangle.

This, together with (
1
), implies that

\displaystyle 0\leq{L}_{1k}+{L}_{2k}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\langle z_{k},u\rangle+{L}_{1k},

or, equivalently,

0\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\langle z_{k},u\rangle+\dfrac{{L}_{1k}}{t_{k}^{2}}.

(2)

By the Lipschitzness of
{L}(\cdot,\bar{\lambda})
around
\bar{x}
,
\partial^{2}L(\cdot,\bar{\lambda})(d)
is locally bounded around
\bar{x}
. This, together with the fact that
\eta_{k}\to\bar{x}
, implies that
\{z_{k}\}
is bounded. Hence, we may assume that
z_{k}
converges to some
z\in\partial^{2}{L}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
. By passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
in (
2
), we obtain

\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0,

as required.

(ii) Since the RSRCQ implies the RRCQ, it suffices, in proving this assertion, to consider the case where
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}<s
. For
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
, there exist sequences
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
and
u_{k}\to u
such that
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k}\in\Omega
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
2
(ii), one has
\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\}
. Hence, for
\bar{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{S}(\bar{x})
,
\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})=0
and so
\langle\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),u\rangle=\langle\nabla L_{x}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),u_{k}\rangle=0
. An argument similar to that used in the proof of part (i) shows that there exists
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
satisfying
\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0
. The proof is complete.

(iii) Since
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
to problem (
SP
), there exist a constant
c>0
and a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{2}\,c\,\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\cap U.

For
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\setminus\{0\}
, there exist
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
and
u_{k}\to u
such that
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k}\in\Omega\cap U
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence

L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})=f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\geq\frac{1}{2}\,c\,t_{k}^{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

By this and a similar argument as in the proof of part (ii), we can show that there exists
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
such that

\frac{1}{2}\langle z,u\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\,c\,\|u\|^{2}>0

as required. ∎

Remark 3

If the RSRCQ does not hold at
\bar{x}
, then so is Theorem
3.1
; see
(
ChenJW-et-at-2026
, Example 3.1)
. Furthermore, in Theorem
3.1
, the claim
“there exists
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
”
cannot be replaced by
“for all
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
”
. To see this, let us consider the following example.

Example 2

Let
n=3
,
s=1
,
m=1
,
\phi(x)=x_{1}-1
,
D=\{0\}
, and

f(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x_{2}-1)|x_{2}-1|.

Then

\Omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,x_{1}-1=0,\|x\|_{0}\leq 2\}

and
\bar{x}=(1,1,0)\in\Omega
. Then

\nabla f(x)=(0,|x_{2}-1|,0)\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Clearly,
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution to (
SP
). We see that
\bar{J}=\Gamma(\bar{x})=\{1,2\}
and so by Lemma
2
, one has
T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\text{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and

N_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=N_{\mathcal{S}}^{C}(\bar{x})=\text{span}\{e_{3}\}.

An easy computation shows that
\nabla f(\bar{x})=(0,0,0)^{\top}
,
\nabla\phi(\bar{x})=(1,0,0)^{\top}
, and so
\bar{\lambda}=0
is the unique Lagrange multiplier of (
SP
) at
\bar{x}
. Clearly, the RSRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
with respect to
\bar{\lambda}
. Hence

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})\cap\text{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}=\{u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,u_{1}=u_{3}=0,u_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Thus

\displaystyle\partial L^{2}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)

\displaystyle=\partial f^{2}(\bar{x})(u)=\partial\langle u,\nabla f\rangle(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\{0\}\times\partial(u_{2}|\cdot-1|)(1)\times\{0\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\{0\}\times[-u_{2},u_{2}]\times\{0\},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ u_{2}>0,\\
\{0\}\times\{-u_{2},u_{2}\}\times\{0\},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ u_{2}<0,\\
\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\},\ \ &\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and so the inequality
\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0
does not hold for all
z\in\partial L^{2}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
when
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\setminus\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\}
. We note that when
u=(0,u_{2},0)
with
u_{2}<0
, then
\widehat{\partial}^{2}L^{2}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)=\emptyset
. Hence, in this case, the second-order necessary optimality condition (3.7) in
(
ChenJW-et-at-2026
, Theorem 3.2)
has no meaning.

3.2
Sparse Optimization with Polyhedral Constraints

The next result gives second-order necessary optimality conditions for (
SP
) when
\Phi
is a polyhedral convex set.

Proposition 2

Assume that
\Phi
is a polyhedral convex set and
f
is of class
C^{1,1}
around
\bar{x}\in\Omega
with
\bar{x}\neq 0
. If
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
SP
) and the RRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then for each
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\mathrm{ker}\nabla f(\bar{x})
, there exists
z\in\partial^{2}f(\bar{x})(u)
such that
\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0
.

Proof

The proof is trivial when
u=0
. Thus, we assume that
0\neq u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\mathrm{ker}\nabla f(\bar{x})
. Then by the RRCQ, one has
u\in T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})\cap T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})
. Since
\Phi
is a polyhedral convex set,
T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})=\mathrm{cone}\,(\Phi-\bar{x})
. Hence, we can find some
t>0
and
x\in\Phi
satisfying
u=t(x-\bar{x})
. Let
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\frac{t}{k_{1}}<1
. Then by the convexity of
\Phi
, one has

x_{k}:=\bar{x}+\frac{1}{k}u=\bar{x}+\frac{t}{k}(x-\bar{x})=\Big(1-\frac{t}{k}\Big)\bar{x}+\frac{t}{k}x\in\Phi\ \ \forall k\geq k_{1}.

We claim that
x_{k}\in\mathcal{S}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and so
x_{k}\in\Omega
for all
k\geq k_{1}
. Indeed, if
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=s
, then by Lemma
2
, one has
T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
. Hence,
\bar{x},u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
is a linear subspace,
x_{k}=\bar{x}+\frac{1}{k}u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}\subset\mathcal{S}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We now assume that
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}<s
. Then by Lemma
2
,

T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\bigcup\{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J}\,:\,J\supseteq\bar{J},|J|=s\}.

Thus there exist
J_{0}\supset\bar{J}
with
|J_{0}|=s
such that
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J_{0}}
. Clearly
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J_{0}}
and we therefore get
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J_{0}}\subset\mathcal{S}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, as required.

Since
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution to (
SP
) and
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
, we may assume that
f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\geq 0
for all
k\geq k_{1}
. Hence

0\leq f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})=f(\bar{x}+\tfrac{1}{k}u)-f(\bar{x})-\frac{1}{k}\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),u\rangle\ \ \forall k\geq k_{1}.

By applying the Taylor formula (Theorem
2.1
) for
f
on
[\bar{x},x_{k}]
we can find
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}f(\xi_{k})(u)
for some
\xi_{k}\in[\bar{x},x_{k}]
such that

0\leq f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\leq\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\langle z_{k},u\rangle.

Hence,
\langle z_{k},u\rangle\geq 0
for all
k\geq k_{1}
. Since
f
is of class
C^{1,1}
around
\bar{x}
and
\xi_{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
, we see that the sequence
z_{k}
is bounded. Hence, we may assume that
z_{k}
converges to some
z\in\partial^{2}f(\bar{x})(u)
and so
\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0
. ∎

The following theorem gives second-order necessary optimality conditions for (
SP
) when
\phi
is an affine mapping and
D
is a polyhedral convex set.

Theorem 3.2

Assume that
\phi(x)=Ax+b
is an affine mapping, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
D
is a polyhedral convex set,
0\neq\bar{x}\in\Omega
is a local optimal solution to problem (
SP
), and
f
is of class
C^{1,1}
around
\bar{x}
. If the RRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then for each
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\mathrm{ker}\nabla f(\bar{x})
and
\bar{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})
, there exists
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)
such that
\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0
.

Proof

By
(
Rockafellar1970
, Theorem 19.3)
, we see that
\Phi=A^{-1}(D-b)
is a polyhedral convex set. Now let
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\mathrm{ker}\nabla f(\bar{x})
and
\bar{\lambda}\in\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})
. Then, we have

\nabla_{x}L(x,\bar{\lambda})=\nabla f(x)+A^{\top}\bar{\lambda}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Hence

\displaystyle\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})(u)

\displaystyle=\partial\langle u,\nabla_{x}L(\cdot,\bar{\lambda})\rangle(\bar{x})=\partial\langle u,\nabla f(\cdot)+A^{\top}\bar{\lambda}\rangle(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\partial\langle u,\nabla f(\cdot)\rangle(\bar{x})=\partial^{2}f(\bar{x})(u).

and the conclusion follows directly from Proposition
2
. ∎

By using the radial cone of
\Phi
, we can derive second-order necessary optimality conditions for (
SP
) when
\phi
is an affine mapping and
D
is a closed and convex cone, not necessarily polyhedral.

Theorem 3.3

Assume that
\phi(x)=Ax+b
is an affine mapping, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
D
is a closed and convex cone,
0\neq\bar{x}\in\Omega
is a local optimal solution to problem (
SP
), and
f
is of class
C^{1,1}
around
\bar{x}
. Then for each
u\in\mathcal{R}_{\Phi}(\bar{x})\cap T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})\cap\mathrm{ker}\nabla f(\bar{x})
, there exists
z\in\partial^{2}f(\bar{x})(u)
such that
\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0
.

Proof

Since
u\in\mathcal{R}_{\Phi}(\bar{x})
, there exists
\tau>0
such that
\bar{x}+tu\in\Phi
for all
t\in[0,\tau]
. This implies that
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+\frac{1}{k}u\in\Phi
for all
k
large enough. An argument similar to that used in the proof Proposition
2
shows that
x_{k}\in\mathcal{S}
and so does
x_{k}\in\Omega
for all
k
large enough. Hence, we can repeat the final part of the proof of Proposition
2
to get the conclusion. ∎

3.3
Sparse Optimization with Mixed Constraints

Consider problem (
SP
) with
\phi=(g,h)
and
D=\mathbb{R}^{p}_{-}\times\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\}
, where
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
are vector-valued functions. Then

\Phi=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,g_{i}(x)\leq 0,i=1,\ldots,p,\,h_{j}(x)=0,j=1,\ldots,q\},

(3)

\Omega=\Phi\cap\mathcal{S}
, and the Lagrangian function associated with problem (
SP
) is defined as

L(x,\lambda,\mu):=f(x)+\langle\lambda,g(x)\rangle+\langle\mu,h(x)\rangle,\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+},\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{q}.

Let
\bar{x}\in\Omega
. The active index set to
\bar{x}
is defined by

I(\bar{x}):=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\,:\,g_{i}(\bar{x})=0\}.

Then the sets of Lagrange multipliers at
\bar{x}
in the sense of Fréchet and Clarke are given, respectively, by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{S}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=\{(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}:-\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda,\mu)\in\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x}),\lambda_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x})=0,i=1,\ldots,p\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle:=\{(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}:-\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda,\mu)\in{N}^{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x}),\lambda_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x})=0,i=1,\ldots,p\}.

If
\Lambda^{S}(\bar{x})
(resp.,
\Lambda^{C}(\bar{x})
) is nonempty, then
\bar{x}
is called an S-stationary point (resp., C-stationary point) of problem (
SP
).

Definition 9
(
see
(
Pan-Xiu-Fan
, Definition 2.4)
)

We say that:

(i)

The
restricted linear independence constraint qualification
(R-LICQ) holds at
\bar{x}
if

\bullet
when
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=s
,
\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x})
,
i\in I(\bar{x})
,
\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x})
,
j=1,\ldots,q
, are linearly independent;

\bullet
when
0<\|\bar{x}\|_{0}<s
,
\nabla_{\bar{J}}\,g_{i}(\bar{x})
,
i\in I(\bar{x})
,
\nabla_{\bar{J}}\,h_{j}(\bar{x})
,
j=1,\ldots,q
, are linearly independent, where
\nabla_{\bar{J}}\,g_{i}(\bar{x}):=(\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}))_{\bar{J}}
and
\nabla_{\bar{J}}\,h_{j}(\bar{x}):=(\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}))_{\bar{J}}
.

(ii)

The
restricted Mangasarian–Fromovitz constraint qualification
(R-MFCQ) holds at
\bar{x}
if

\bullet
when
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=s
,
\nabla_{\bar{J}}\,h_{j}(\bar{x})
,
j=1,\ldots,q
, are linearly independent, and there exist a vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),y\rangle<0,i\in I(\bar{x})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),y\rangle=0,j=1,\ldots,q;

\bullet
when
0<\|\bar{x}\|_{0}<s
,
\nabla_{\bar{J}}\,h_{j}(\bar{x})
,
j=1,\ldots,q
, are linearly independent, and for any
J\in\mathcal{J}(\bar{x}):=\{J\in\mathcal{J}\,:\,J\supset\bar{J}\}
, there exists a vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{J}
such that

\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),y\rangle<0,i\in I(\bar{x})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),y\rangle=0,j=1,\ldots,q.

Remark 4

(i)

Clearly, if the R-LICQ holds at
\bar{x}
then so does R-MFCQ. Furthermore, when
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=s
, these conditions reduce to the classical LICQ and the classical MFCQ, respectively.

(ii)

It is clear that if the R-LICQ (resp., R-MFCQ) holds at
\bar{x}
then so does the LICQ (resp., MFCQ).

(iii)

(see
(
Zhao-et-al
, Remark 1)
) If
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=s
and the RRCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then so does the R-LICQ.

Lemma 4
(
see
(
Pan-Xiu-Fan
, Proposition 2.5, Proposition 2.11 and Remark 2.6)
and
(

Assume that

.

(i)

If the R-MFCQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then
T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})\cap T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})
and

{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\subset{N}_{\Phi}(\bar{x})+{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x}).

Furthermore, we have

\widehat{N}_{\Phi}(\bar{x})={N}_{\Phi}(\bar{x})=\bigg\{\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x})+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x})\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{|I(\bar{x})|}_{+},\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\bigg\}.

(ii)

If the R-LICQ holds at
\bar{x}
, then
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=\widehat{N}_{\Phi}(\bar{x})+\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})

{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})={N}_{\Phi}(\bar{x})+{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x}).

When

4
Second-Order Sufficient Optimality Conditions

In this section, we present some second-order sufficient optimality conditions for problem (
SP
) with mixed constraints, i.e.,

\Phi=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,g_{i}(x)\leq 0,i=1,\ldots,p,\,h_{j}(x)=0,j=1,\ldots,q\}

and
\Omega=\Phi\cap\mathcal{S}
. We assume that
f,g,
and
h
are of class
C^{1,1}
around the considered feasible solution
\bar{x}\in\Omega
. The
cone of critical directions
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\bar{x}):=\big\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0,\ \

\displaystyle\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),u\rangle\leq 0,i\in I(\bar{x}),

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0,j=1,\ldots,q\big\}.

Theorem 4.1

Let
\bar{x}\in\Omega
be an S-stationary point with
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\Lambda^{S}(\bar{x})
. If for every
u\in[T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})]\setminus\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\}
and for any
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
one has

\langle z,u\rangle>0,

then
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of problem (
SP
).

Proof

Suppose to the contrary that
\bar{x}
is not an isolated local solution of order
2
of problem (
SP
). Then, there exists a sequence
x_{k}\in\Omega\setminus\{\bar{x}\}
such that
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
and

f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})<\frac{1}{k}\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(4)

Put
t_{k}:=\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|
and
u_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}
, then
\|u_{k}\|=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
as
k\to\infty
. By the boundedness of
u_{k}
, without loss of generality we may assume that
u_{k}
converges to some
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|u\|=1
. Clearly,
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
and so
u\in T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})\cap T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})
. We claim that
u\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})
. Indeed, since
x_{k}\in\Omega
, for each
i\in I(\bar{x})
one has

0\geq g_{i}(x_{k})=g_{i}(\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k})-g_{i}(\bar{x})=t_{k}\langle\nabla g_{i}(\theta_{k}),u_{k}\rangle

for some
\theta_{k}\in(\bar{x},x_{k})
. Dividing both sides by
t_{k}
and then letting
k\to\infty
, we obtain
\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),u\rangle\leq 0
for
i\in I(\bar{x})
. A similar argument applied to the equality constraints yields
\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0
for all
j=1,\ldots,q
. On the other hand, it follows from (
4
) that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one has

t_{k}\langle\nabla f(\xi_{k}),u_{k}\rangle<\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2}

for some
\xi_{k}\in(\bar{x},x_{k})
. Dividing both sides by
t_{k}
and letting
k\to\infty
, we obtain
\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),u\rangle\leq 0
. We now show that
\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),u\rangle\geq 0
and so
\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0
. To this end, it suffices to show that
\langle\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),u\rangle=0
. Indeed, if it is this case, then we have

\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),u\rangle=-\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),u\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\bar{\mu}_{j}\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),u\rangle\geq 0,

as required. Since
\bar{x}
is an S-stationary, one has
-\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})
. If
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}=s
, then
\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{{\bar{J}}^{c}}
and
T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\bar{J}}
. This, together with the fact that
u\in T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})
, implies that
\langle\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),u\rangle=0
. If
\|\bar{x}\|_{0}<s
, then
\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})=\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\}
and so
\langle\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),u\rangle=0
. Hence
\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0
and so
u\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})
.

Now, by the definition of the Lagrangian function and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}[g_{i}(x_{k})-g_{i}(\bar{x})]

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\bar{\mu}_{j}[h_{j}(x_{k})-h_{j}(\bar{x})]<\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2}

(5)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\langle\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),u\rangle=0
, one has

\displaystyle L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})

\displaystyle=[L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-L(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})]

\displaystyle+[L(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-t_{k}\langle\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),u\rangle].

Put
L_{1k}:=L(x_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-L(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
and

L_{2k}:=L(\bar{x}+t_{k}u,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-t_{k}\langle\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),u\rangle.

An argument similar to that used in the proof Theorem
3.1
shows that
\frac{L_{1k}}{t_{k}^{2}}\to 0
when
k\to\infty
. By applying the Taylor formula (Theorem
2.1
) for the function
L(\cdot,\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
on
[\bar{x},\bar{x}+t_{k}u]
, we can find
z_{k}\in\partial^{2}L(\eta_{k},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(u)
for some
\eta_{k}\in[\bar{x},\bar{x}+t_{k}u]
such that

L_{2k}\geq\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\langle z_{k},u\rangle

and
z_{k}
converges to some
z\in\partial^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})(u)
. This, together with (
5
), implies that

\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\langle z_{k},u\rangle+L_{1k}\leq L_{1k}+L_{2k}\leq\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2}\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Dividing both sides by
\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}
and then letting
k\to\infty
, we obtain
\langle z,u\rangle\leq 0
, a contradiction. Hence
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of problem (
SP
). ∎

.

,

5
Application to Sparse Multiobjective Optimization

In this section, we consider the following sparse multiobjective optimization problem:

\mathrm{Min}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}F(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{d}(x))\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ x\in\Omega:=\Phi\cap\mathcal{S},

(SMP)

where
\Phi
is defined as in (
3
). The Lagrangian function associated with problem (
SMP
) is defined as

L^{M}(x,\alpha,\lambda,\mu):=\langle\alpha,F(x)\rangle+\langle\lambda,g(x)\rangle+\langle\mu,h(x)\rangle,

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\setminus\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\},

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+},
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{q}.

We assume that
F,g,
and
h
are of class
C^{1,1}
around
\bar{x}
.

Definition 10

For
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, put

\varphi(x):=\max\{\varphi_{l}(x)\,:\,l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\varphi_{l}(x):=f_{l}(x)-f_{l}(\bar{x})
. We say that
\bar{x}
is an
isolated local efficient solution of order
2
of (
SMP
) if
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of
\varphi
on
\Omega
.

Then the sets of
S-Lagrange multipliers
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}^{S}(\bar{x}):=\Big\{(\alpha,\lambda,\mu)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\setminus\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\})\times\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\,:\,

\displaystyle-\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\alpha,\lambda,\mu)\in\widehat{N}_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x}),

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \lambda_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x})=0,i=1,\ldots,p\Big\}.

If
\mathrm{A}^{S}(\bar{x})
is nonempty, then
\bar{x}
is called an
S-stationary point
of (
SMP
) at
\bar{x}
.

We say that
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
critical direction
of (
SMP
) at
\bar{x}
if

\begin{cases}&\langle\nabla f_{l}(\bar{x}),u\rangle\leq 0,l=1,\ldots,d,\\
&\langle\nabla f_{l}(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0\ \ \text{for at least one}\ \ l\in\{l=1,\ldots,d\},\\
&\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),u\rangle\leq 0,i\in I(\bar{x}),\\
&\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0,j=1,\ldots,q.\end{cases}

The set of critical directions of (
SMP
) at
\bar{x}
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}(\bar{x})
.

The following theorem gives a second-order sufficient optimality condition for an isolated local efficient solution of order
2
of problem (
SMP
).

Theorem 5.1

Let
\bar{x}\in\Omega
be an S-stationary point with
(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\mathrm{A}^{S}(\bar{x})
. If for every
u\in[T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\mathcal{K}(\bar{x})]\setminus\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\}
and for any
z\in\partial^{2}L^{M}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
one has

\langle z,u\rangle>0,

then
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of problem (
SMP
).

Proof

Suppose to the contrary that
\bar{x}
is not an isolated local solution of order
2
of problem (
SMP
). Then, there exists a sequence
x_{k}\in\Omega\setminus\{\bar{x}\}
such that
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
and

\varphi(x_{k})=\max\{f_{l}(x_{k})-f_{l}(\bar{x})\,:\,l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\}<\frac{1}{k}\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies that

f_{l}(x_{k})-f_{l}(\bar{x})<\frac{1}{k}\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\ \ \forall l=1,\ldots,d,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(6)

Put
t_{k}:=\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|
and
u_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}
, then
\|u_{k}\|=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t_{k}\to 0^{+}
as
k\to\infty
. Since
\|u_{k}\|=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, without loss of generality we may assume that
u_{k}
converges to some
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|u\|=1
. Then,
u\in T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
and so
u\in T_{\Phi}(\bar{x})\cap T_{\mathcal{S}}(\bar{x})
. We claim that
u\in\mathcal{K}(\bar{x})
. Indeed, analysis similar to that in the proof of Theorem
4.1
shows that
\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),u\rangle\leq 0
for
i\in I(\bar{x})
, and
\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0
for all
j=1,\ldots,q
. Furthermore, by (
6
), it is easy to see that
\langle\nabla f_{l}(\bar{x}),u\rangle\leq 0
for all
l=1,\ldots,d
. We now show that
\langle\nabla f_{l}(\bar{x}),u\rangle=0
for at least one
l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and so
u\in\mathcal{K}(\bar{x})
. If otherwise, then
\langle\nabla f_{l}(\bar{x}),u\rangle<0
for all
l=1,\ldots,d
. This, together with the fact that
\bar{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\setminus\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\}
, implies that
\sum_{l=1}^{d}\alpha_{l}\langle\nabla f_{l}(\bar{x}),u\rangle<0
. Since
\bar{x}
is an S-stationary point, analysis similar to that in the proof of Theorem
4.1
shows that
\langle\nabla_{x}L^{M}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),u\rangle=0
. Hence

0>\sum_{l=1}^{d}\alpha_{l}\langle\nabla f_{l}(\bar{x}),u\rangle=-\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\langle\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x}),u\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\bar{\mu}_{j}\langle\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x}),u\rangle\geq 0,

a contradiction.

Now, by the definition of the Lagrangian function and (
6
), we have

\displaystyle L(x_{k},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})-L(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=\sum_{l=1}^{d}

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{l}[f_{l}(x_{k})-f_{l}(\bar{x})]+\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\bar{\lambda}_{i}[g_{i}(x_{k})-g_{i}(\bar{x})]

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\bar{\mu}_{j}[h_{j}(x_{k})-h_{j}(\bar{x})]<\frac{1}{k}\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{d}\bar{\alpha}_{l}\Big)t_{k}^{2}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. An argument similar to that used in the proof Theorem
4.1
shows that there exists
z\in\partial^{2}L^{M}(\bar{x},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
such that
\langle z,u\rangle\leq 0
, contradicting the assumption. Hence
\bar{x}
is an isolated local solution of order
2
of problem (
SMP
). The proof is complete. ∎

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we investigated second-order optimality conditions for sparse optimization problems with
C^{1,1}
data. By employing the limiting (Mordukhovich) second-order subdifferential of the associated Lagrangian function, we established new second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for the considered problems. In contrast to approaches based on Fréchet second-order subdifferentials, the present framework is applicable to a broader class of nonsmooth problems since the limiting second-order subdifferential of
C^{1,1}
functions is always nonempty and compact. As an application, we also derived second-order sufficient optimality conditions for sparse multiobjective optimization problems. Several examples were presented to illustrate the applicability and effectiveness of the obtained results.

Second-order optimality conditions for sparse optimization problems, and even for optimization problems without sparse constraints, with merely
C^{1}
data remain open. Dealing with this challenging problem requires new ideas and techniques. We plan to investigate this issue in future work.
