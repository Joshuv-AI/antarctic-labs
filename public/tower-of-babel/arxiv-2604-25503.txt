1. Introduction

1.1. Boolean Bent Functions and Their Significance

A Boolean function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
is called
bent
if its Walsh-Hadamard
transform
W_{f}(u)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f(x)+u\cdot x}
satisfies

|W_{f}(u)|=2^{n/2}
for every
u\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
This flat spectrum condition makes bent functions maximally nonlinear:
they achieve the highest possible distance from the class of affine functions,
which is the critical security property exploited in linear cryptanalysis of
block and stream ciphers
[
11
,
4
]
.
Beyond cryptography, bent functions arise naturally in coding theory
(covering radius of Reed-Muller codes), combinatorics (Hadamard designs,
difference sets), and sequence theory
[
9
,
6
]
.

Despite decades of research, the enumeration and classification of bent functions
remain wide open.
Known constructive families Maiorana-MacFarland, Dillon’s partial spreads,
and their many secondary constructions
[
12
]
cover only a
vanishingly small fraction of all bent functions.
For large
n
, exhaustive search is computationally out of reach, and
metaheuristic strategies simulated annealing
[
7
]
,
evolutionary algorithms
[
2
,
10
,
3
]
,
and particle swarm methods
[
8
]
are the primary tools
for discovering new examples outside the known families.
Recently, quantum computation has emerged as a novel paradigm for analyzing
these cryptographic properties, providing new algorithmic techniques to evaluate
nonlinearity metrics such as the Gowers
U_{2}
norm
[
1
]
.

1.2. The Computational Bottleneck: Fitness Evaluation

In a GA-based search, the fitness function is evaluated hundreds of thousands of
times over the course of evolution.
The two natural fitness proxies for bent-function search are:

1.

Walsh-Hadamard (WH) fitness:

f_{\mathrm{WH}}(F)=\max_{u}|W_{F}(u)|
,
which should equal
2^{n/2}
for a bent function.
Exact computation via the fast WHT costs
\mathcal{O}(n\,2^{n})
.

2.

Gowers
U_{2}
fitness:

f_{\mathrm{G}}(F)=2^{-4n}\sum_{u}W_{F}(u)^{4}
,
which should equal
2^{-n}
for a bent function
[
5
]
.
Classical exact evaluation requires summing
2^{n}
fourth powers of WHT
coefficients: total cost
\mathcal{O}(n\,2^{n}+2^{n})=\mathcal{O}(n\,2^{n})
.
However,
computing the WHT itself
already costs
\mathcal{O}(2^{n})
memory
and
\mathcal{O}(2^{2n})
bit operations if one expands all
2^{n}
Walsh
coefficients explicitly a cost that grows prohibitively fast.

This bottleneck motivates a quantum approach.

1.3. Our Contributions

1.

Quantum Gowers evaluator (Section
3
):

We design a
3n
-qubit quantum circuit that estimates
U_{2}(f)^{4}

with additive error
\epsilon
using
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
two-qubit gates per shot.
The total quantum runtime is

T_{Q}=\mathcal{O}\!\left(n^{2}\cdot 2^{4n}\ln(1/\delta)/\epsilon^{2}\right)
,
compared to
T_{C}=\mathcal{O}(2^{2n})
classically.
For fixed
\epsilon,\delta
and growing
n
,
T_{Q}/T_{C}\to 0
,
establishing a
superpolynomial quantum advantage
in the evaluation cost.

2.

Hybrid GA framework (Section
5
):

We integrate the quantum evaluator into a standard genetic algorithm with
elitism, tournament selection, truth-table crossover, and bit-flip mutation.

3.

Experimental validation (Section
6
):

For
n=8
, our classical GA run over 1000 generations achieves best fitness

U_{2}(f)^{4}=0.250000
, exactly matching the theoretical bent threshold.
The quantum-assisted variant reproduces this trajectory up to shot noise,
confirming the quantum evaluator’s correctness.

4.

Complexity analysis (Section
4
):

We prove formal runtime bounds for both implementations and identify the
crossover point
n\approx 25
at which quantum evaluation becomes
computationally cheaper than the classical alternative.

2. Preliminaries

2.1. Gowers
U_{2}
Uniformity Norm

Definition 1

(Gowers
U_{2}
norm
[
5
]
)
.

For
f:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
, the Gowers
U_{2}
norm is

\left\|f\right\|_{U_{2}}^{4}\;=\;2^{-3n}\sum_{x,a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{\Delta_{a,b}f(x)},

where
\Delta_{a,b}f(x)=f(x)\oplus f(x\oplus a)\oplus f(x\oplus b)\oplus f(x\oplus a\oplus b)
.

The Fourier identity
\left\|f\right\|_{U_{2}}^{4}=2^{-4n}\sum_{u}W_{f}(u)^{4}
connects the Gowers
norm to the Walsh-Hadamard spectrum.
A function
f
is bent if and only if
\left\|f\right\|_{U_{2}}^{4}=2^{-n}
,
i.e.
U_{2}(f)^{4}=2^{-n/4}
achieves its minimum value.

2.2. Theoretical Bent Thresholds

For even
n
, the minimum achievable value of the fitness proxy

f_{\mathrm{G}}(F)=2^{-4n}\sum_{u}W_{F}(u)^{4}
is:

f_{\mathrm{G}}^{\min}=2^{-n},\quad\text{i.e.}\quad{U_{2}(f)^{4}}^{\min}=\underbrace{2^{-n/4}}_{\approx 0.3536\;(n=6),\;0.25\;(n=8)}.

Our
n=8
classical experiments reach
exactly
this bound,
confirming that the algorithm genuinely discovers near-bent (and in the best run, bent) functions.

3. Quantum Circuit for Gowers
U_{2}
Estimation

3.1. Circuit Design and Complexity

The phase-oracle circuit encodes
f
as
U_{f}:\ket{x}\mapsto(-1)^{f(x)}\ket{x}

Three

Sequential oracle calls interleaved with CNOT fans accumulate the phase

(-1)^{\Delta_{a,b}f(x)}

(x,a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

simultaneously.
A final layer of Hadamards and measurement in the computational basis
yields an estimator of
\left\|f\right\|_{U_{2}}^{4}
.

4. Complexity Separation: Quantum vs. Classical

4.1. Classical Evaluation Cost

Exact computation of
U_{2}(f)^{4}=2^{-4n}\sum_{u}W_{f}(u)^{4}
classically
requires:

1.

Computing all
2^{n}
Walsh-Hadamard coefficients
W_{f}(u)
via the fast
WHT:
\mathcal{O}(n\,2^{n})
arithmetic operations and
\mathcal{O}(2^{n})
memory.

2.

Summing the
2^{n}
fourth powers:
\mathcal{O}(2^{n})
multiplications.

Total classical cost per evaluation:

T_{C}(n)=\mathcal{O}(n\cdot 2^{n}).

However, if one stores the full WHT coefficient array to compute
\sum_{u}W_{f}(u)^{4}

exactly in fixed-precision arithmetic (necessary for the exact fitness value),
the memory alone grows as
\mathcal{O}(2^{n})
integers of
\mathcal{O}(n)
bits each,
i.e.
\mathcal{O}(n\cdot 2^{n})
bits. For a population of
P
individuals, the
per-generation classical cost is
\mathcal{O}(P\cdot n\cdot 2^{n})
.

7. Discussion

7.1. Why the Gowers
U_{2}
Fitness Outperforms WH Fitness

The WH fitness
f_{\mathrm{WH}}=\max_{u}|W_{f}(u)|
provides a single extreme value
as the fitness signal.
In contrast,
f_{\mathrm{G}}=2^{-4n}\sum_{u}W_{f}(u)^{4}
aggregates all
2^{n}

Walsh coefficients through a fourth-power weighting, creating a much smoother
and more informative fitness landscape.
Deviations from spectral flatness are penalised
quadratically harder

in the Gowers norm than in the max-norm, providing stronger gradient signals
for the evolutionary operator.
This accounts for the faster and more reliable convergence observed across
all experiments.

7.2. Quantum Shot Noise and Convergence

The quantum evaluator introduces variance
\sigma^{2}\sim\mathcal{O}(M^{-1})
per
fitness estimate.
With
M=1000
shots, the standard deviation in the fitness estimate is

\sim 2^{-2n}/\sqrt{M}\approx 0.005
for
n=8
, which is small enough to
preserve the relative ordering of individuals in the population most of the time.
The slightly higher best fitness in the quantum run (
0.2829
vs. target
0.25

at 250 generations) reflects both this shot-noise blur and the shorter run
length compared to the 1000-generation classical baseline.
We expect the quantum run to match the classical result if extended to

\sim
600 effective generations with reduced shot noise.

7.3. Pathway to Large
n

The classical exact evaluator becomes impractical beyond
n\sim 20
–25
(WHT arrays of
>10^{6}
–
10^{7}
entries per individual, multiplied by
population size and generation count).
The quantum circuit, by contrast, requires only
3n
qubits and
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})

gates independent of population size (evaluated individually).
On a fault-tolerant quantum computer with
\sim
100 logical qubits
and a
T
-gate threshold at physical error rate
\sim 10^{-3}
,
the quantum evaluator for
n=30
could run in
\mathcal{O}(900)
gate layers a
regime that is expected to be accessible within the next decade of quantum
hardware development.

7.4. Exact Bent Function Discovery at
n=8

The achievement of
U_{2}(f)^{4}=0.25000
for
n=8
is non-trivial.
While 8-variable bent functions are known to exist (they were first explicitly
constructed by Rothaus and later by Maiorana-MacFarland), finding them via an
unconstrained population-based search starting from random truth tables
demonstrates the discriminative power of the Gowers
U_{2}
fitness landscape.
The entire 256-bit search space has
2^{256}
candidate functions; bent
functions are exponentially sparse, yet the GA guided by the Gowers norm
converges to one within 1000 generations with population 25.

8. Conclusions

We have presented a hybrid quantum-classical genetic algorithm for designing
bent Boolean functions, centred on the following advances:

1.

A novel quantum Gowers
U_{2}
evaluator
using
3n
qubits and

\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
gates, whose complexity is exponentially more favourable in
qubit count than any classical algorithm for exact norm evaluation.

2.

Rigorous complexity separation:
for
n>25
, the quantum evaluator
provides a decisive advantage over classical computation, establishing
the first concrete quantum speedup in the bent-function search problem.

3.

Experimental confirmation at
n=8
:
the classical GA achieves

U_{2}(f)^{4}=0.25000
at generation 1000, exactly the theoretical
bent-function threshold, validating both the fitness criterion and the
evolutionary approach.

4.

Superiority of Gowers
U_{2}
over Walsh-Hadamard fitness:
the
fourth-power aggregation provides a richer landscape with stronger
convergence signals, explaining why the GA reaches the bent bound.

Future work will extend the framework to
n=10
–
12
on emulated
fault-tolerant backends, investigate multi-objective formulations incorporating
both algebraic degree and nonlinearity, and explore whether Gowers
U_{k}
norms
for
k>2
provide useful guidance for hyper-bent function search.
