 1. INTRODUCTION

This report documents Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s (ORNL’s) FY 2025 progress toward establishing
readiness to use the Battelle Energy Alliance (BEA) Research Reactor (BRR) shipping cask in support of
the Nuclear Science User Facilities (NSUF). The BRR cask will provide a new shared infrastructure
capability for transporting irradiated fuels and materials between ORNL and Idaho National Laboratory
(INL), thereby supporting NSUF’s mission of enabling user access to advanced nuclear research facilities.

In FY 2025, ORNL advanced the regulatory, contractual, safety review, and planning activities necessary
to qualify its facilities and staff for BRR cask handling. Although loaded shipments were delayed due to
fabrication lead times for Orano Federal Services LLC’s internal basket hardware, the program
progressed to the point where an empty cask dry run is scheduled for October 2025. This dry run
represents a critical step in demonstrating ORNL’s ability to receive, handle, and return the BRR cask.

 2. FY 2025 ACCOMPLISHMENTS

2.1 REGULATORY AND SAFETY READINESS

 • Certificate of Compliance (CoC): The US Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) issued
 BRR CoC Amendment (Rev. 11) on June 16, 2025, posted to RAMPAC (Radioactive
 Material Packaging, DOE/NRC’s official database of certified transport packages).
 • Institutional Approvals: The project completed ORNL’s Screening Work Acceptance
 process (SWA, the internal process for authorizing nuclear work in nonreactor facilities).
 This included a review by the Operational Safety Board (OSB, the facility-level safety
 committee) which confirmed facility-level safety readiness and found no issues with planned
 work. However, because the activity involves the receipt of significant amounts of irradiated
 nuclear fuel, the OSB referred the work to the Nuclear Review Panel (NRP, ORNL’s highest-
 level nuclear safety oversight body). The NRP approved the BRR activities on August 5,
 2025 with only minimal actions, confirming the proposed work aligned with laboratory
 mission needs and would not interfere with existing programs. Final approval for actual
 shipments will still require consultation between directorate leadership and the ORNL Site
 Office.
 • Route Designations: The NRC assigned truck routes for both INL→ORNL (Route 272) and
 ORNL→INL (Route 259A).

2.2 INFRASTRUCTURE AND PROCEDURES

 • ORNL issued Work Plan WP063420 (Rev. 0) in September 2025; this work plan covers cask
 receipt, handling, and dry run operations.
 • Draft procedures were developed for cask receipt (NNFD-3525-OP-146) and use of the Dry
 Transfer System (DTS) (NNFD-3525-OP-145).
 • Hazards were evaluated, with normal hot cell dose projections and established waste
 pathways for the following:
 o Contact-handled solid low-level waste (CH-SLLW) (e.g., lightly contaminated
 gloves, gowns, and tools).
 o Remote-handled transuranic waste (RH-TRU), which is highly radioactive material
 that requires heavy shielding and remote handling.

2.3 CONTRACTING AND VENDOR WORK

 • DTS Maintenance (AFC-funded): ORNL executed a contract with Secured Transportation
 Services (STS) in April 2025, supported by the Advanced Fuels Campaign (AFC). STS
 completed maintenance on September 18, 2025, delivering a detailed report documenting
 corrosion remediation, hardware inspection, and readiness. The DTS was shipped to ORNL
 immediately thereafter.
 • Rod-in-tube canister (RITC) Hardware (INL–Orano contract): INL placed a fabrication
 contract with Orano Federal Services LLC on July 3, 2025. The quoted 35 week lead time,
 with delivery projected for March 2026, became a pivotal factor in shifting the program
 timeline.

2.4 INTERLABORATORY COORDINATION

 • Biweekly coordination calls tracked action items across ORNL, INL, STS, Westinghouse,
 and NSUF.
 • Susan Case (INL) confirmed cask availability after experiment unloading in June and
 formally scheduled shipment for the week of September 29, 2025, with delivery to ORNL on
 October 2.
 • ORNL (Jason Harp) and INL aligned on retaining the cask through mid-October for dry run
 activities, with return shipment planned for the week of October 20, 2025.
 • Westinghouse confirmed the segments that would be included in the first set of BRR
 shipment early in FY25 which allow both ORNL and INL to perform detailed planning
 assessments on the exact material to be transported between laboratories.

 3. SCHEDULE PIVOT

The defining change of FY 2025 occurred in April, when INL communicated Orano’s 35 week
fabrication lead time for the RITC basket hardware. The following were envisioned in the original
FY 2025 plan:

 • May 2025: BRR cask and DTS dry run at ORNL.
 • July 2025: ORNL→INL irradiated fuel shipment.

With hardware unavailable until approximately March 2026, loaded shipments were no longer feasible in
FY 2025. ORNL and INL pivoted to focus on an empty cask dry run to preserve value for NSUF. This
adaptation ensured that ORNL would still complete meaningful readiness activities (procedures, safety
approvals, and physical handling qualification) rather than allowing progress to stall.

 5. FUNDING AND PROGRAMMATIC ALIGNMENT

The FY 2025 funding provided by NSUF enabled ORNL to focus on infrastructure readiness: procedures,
hazard evaluations, safety reviews, and dry run preparations at the 3525 Irradiated Fuels Examination
Laboratory. These activities will culminate in the October 2025 dry run, a critical step in qualifying
ORNL to handle the BRR cask.

At the same time, AFC resources supported complementary work by funding the DTS maintenance
contract with STS. Together, these parallel investments advanced readiness for both programs,
positioning ORNL to complete the dry run and prepare for loaded shipments in FY 2026.

 6. PATH FORWARD (FY 2026)

 • Q1 (Oct–Dec 2025): ORNL to receive the BRR cask, conduct dry run with DTS and mockup
 components, return cask to INL.
 • Q2–Q3 (Jan–Jun 2026): Incorporation of Orano-fabricated basket and STS-fabricated grapple
 hardware into procedures, with readiness demonstrations for loaded shipments.
 • Q3 (Apr–Jun 2026): The first loaded shipment is expected to be an AFC-supported transfer
 between ORNL and INL. Once basket hardware is available and qualification complete, the BRR
 cask will also be available to support NSUF-sponsored shipments.

 7. CONCLUSION

FY 2025 efforts advanced ORNL to regulatory, institutional, and procedural readiness for BRR cask
operations. The April schedule pivot transformed the year’s scope, but the program responded by
executing the steps necessary to stage an empty cask dry run in October 2025.

This dry run will complete NSUF’s immediate objective of qualifying ORNL to handle the BRR cask.
Once the Orano-fabricated basket is delivered in FY 2026, BRR cask shipments will become an
operational capability within NSUF, enabling irradiated fuel shipments between ORNL and INL.

The outcome benefits the following stakeholders:

 • NSUF: Expanded infrastructure and new transport pathway for user-driven projects.
 • AFC: Immediate application in support of Accident-Tolerant Fuel (ATF) and High-burnup
 Effects in Reactivity-initiated Accidents (HERA) shipments.
 • DOE: Strengthened, coordinated capability across national laboratories, enhancing the resilience
 of US nuclear R&D infrastructure.
