Ondansetron exhibits potent antiviral activity against enterovirus 71 infection in vitro and in vivo
Authors: Zhenyang Li, Jianqiao Rui, Jiazheng Li, Sichen Ren, Yan Luo, Weiyan Zhu, Huimin Tao, Mingzhu Guo, Qingsong Dai, Wan Peng, Siqi Li, Xiaojia Guo, Wei Li, Jingjing Yang, Ruiyuan Cao, Wu Zhong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Ondansetron inhibits EV-A71 infection in vitro and in vivo.•Ondansetron acts at the early stage of viral attachment and entry.•Cholesterol metabolism genes (ABCA1, ABCG1, HMGCS1) are modulated.•Targeting host lipid homeostasis represents a novel antiviral strategy.

Introduction

Enterovirus A71 (EV-A71) is a non-enveloped enterovirus of the family Picornaviridae, comprising a positive-sense single-stranded RNA genome of approximately 7.4 kb encapsulated within an ∼30 nm icosahedral capsid composed of VP1-VP4 (Baggen et al., 2018; Huang et al., 2019; Kinobe et al., 2022). EV-A71 is one of the major etiological agents of hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD). Clinical manifestations range from mild symptoms such as fever and vesicular lesions on the hands and feet to severe complications including brainstem encephalitis, acute flaccid paralysis, and cardiopulmonary failure. Severe pediatric cases have poor outcomes and high mortality (Chang et al., 2019; Teoh et al., 2016; Hsia et al., 2020). Studies have identified SCARB2 as a primary receptor mediating EV-A71 entry, and the naturally high expression of SCARB2 on neurons and glial cells provides a prerequisite for central nervous system (CNS) infection (Yamayoshi et al., 2009; Yamayoshi et al., 2014; Nishimura et al., 2009). EV-A71 can invade the CNS via multiple routes: retrograde axonal transport, disruption of the blood-brain barrier (BBB), and immune cell-mediated translocation across the blood-brain barrier (Chen et al., 2007; Wang and Liu, 2009; Fujii et al., 2013). Moreover, because the immune system of children is not yet fully mature and the nervous system is still developing, their resistance to viral infection is relatively weak, and CNS involvement often leads to severe consequences. At present, there are no approved specific antiviral drugs for EV-A71 infection in the clinic. Only vaccines are available for prevention (Wang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2017). Therefore, screening and identifying therapeutics for EV-A71 infection have the important clinical significance. Given the stringent safety requirements for pediatric drug approvals, drug repurposing-screening and evaluating already marketed drugs-represents an important strategy for developing new treatments.

Previous studies have found that 65.5% of severe EV-A71 patients show abnormal signals in the brainstem on MRI, primarily localized in the raphe nucleus group, which contains a high density of 5-HT neurons. Further research has shown that EV-A71 infection can promote excessive abnormal secretion of 5-HT and, by acting on serotonin receptors, lead to alterations in consciousness, delirium, increased muscle tone, and muscle spasms in patients (Zhu et al., 2022; Hoyer et al., 2002). Serotonin receptors constitute a receptor family for which 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) serves as the endogenous ligand. These receptors are widely distributed in both the central and peripheral nervous systems, particularly abundant in the gastrointestinal system in the periphery. Based on their molecular subtypes, they can be classified into seven types (5-HT1–5-HT7), among which the 5-HT3 receptor is a ligand-gated ion channel, while the others are G protein-coupled receptors (Kitson, 2007; Hannon and Hoyer, 2008; Hagbom et al., 2017). On light of these findings, we propose that 5-HT and its receptors may play a critical role in the disease progression of EV-A71 infection.

Ondansetron is a highly selective and potent 5-HT3 receptor antagonist, clinically used primarily to treat vomiting associated with chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgical anesthesia, and acute gastroenteritis. Previous studies have found that ondansetron can alleviate clinical symptoms and promote recovery in patients infected with rotavirus and norovirus, suggesting that it may also possess certain antiviral effects (Li et al., 2014). However, its role in antiviral research remains unclear, and no studies have reported on this to date. Considering this background, we became highly interested in whether ondansetron could exert effects against EV-A71 infection. Through both in vitro and in vivo experiments, we indeed found that ondansetron exhibits a clear inhibitory effect on EV-A71 infection.

Materials and methods

Cells, viruses, and reagents

Human rhabdomyosarcoma (RD) cells (ATCC CCL-136) and SK-N-SH cell lines (ATCC HTB-11) were maintained in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Gibco 2367,374) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco 1807,622) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (PS; Gibco 2321,152) under 5% CO₂ at 37 °C. The EV-A71 H strain (ATCC VR-1432) and immunological reagents were obtained from the following sources: mouse anti-EV-A71 VP1 monoclonal antibody (12D7) from Professor Tong Cheng's laboratory (Hao et al., 2019). anti-α-tubulin antibody (Abcam ab7291), HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (Abcam ab205719), Goat anti-Mouse IgG (H+L) Secondary Antibody, FITC (ThermoFisher, A16067), and Hoechst 33,342 nuclear stain (Invitrogen H21492). Pharmacological agents included ondansetron hydrochloride (TargetMol T6616), and NITD008 (MCE HY-12,957). HTR3A Polyclonal antibody (29,685-1-AP) was from Proteintech. Granisetron (HY-B0071), Tropisetron (HY-B0072), and Palonosetron (HY-A0018) are from MCE. All compounds were dissolved in DMSO to 100 mM stock concentrations and stored at −20 °C for in vitro studies. For in vivo administration, ondansetron was prepared in saline.

Antiviral activity assay

Antiviral evaluation was conducted in 96-well plates per established protocols (Li et al., 2022). RD cells (1.5 × 10⁴ cells/well) were seeded in white-walled clear-bottom plates and cultured for 24 h prior to infection. Test compounds (ondansetron) underwent 3-fold serial dilution (100–0.01 μM) in DMEM containing 2% FBS, with DMSO-treated wells as controls. EV-A71 inoculum (100 TCID₅₀ in 2% FBS DMEM) was introduced to compound-pretreated cells. Following 72 h incubation at 37 °C, cell viability was determined using the CellTiter-Glo assay (Promega, G7570) according to manufacturer specifications, with luminescence quantification via SpectraMax M5 microplate reader (Molecular Devices). Dose-response curves and EC₅₀ values were generated using Origin 9.0.

Viral suppression was further investigated through plaque formation units (PFU) and qRT-PCR quantification. RD cells (4 × 10⁵ cells/well in 12-well plates) were infected with EV-A71 (MOI=0.001) in compound-containing medium. Supernatant virion production and intracellular viral RNA levels were assessed at 36hpi using plaque assays and SYBR Green-based RT-PCR respectively.

Immunofluorescence assay

RD cells (1.5 × 10⁴ cells/well) were cultured in 96-well plates for 24 h prior to EV-A71 H strain infection (MOI=0.001) with compound co-treatment. Following 48 h incubation, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (30 min, RT), permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 (30 min, RT), and blocked with 5% BSA (30 min, 37 °C). Sequential incubations were performed with mouse anti-VP1 antibody (12D7; 1:5000, 37 °C, 1 h) and Goat anti-Mouse IgG (H+L) Secondary Antibody, FITC (ThermoFisher, A16067) (1:500, 37 °C, 1 h). Nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst 33,342 (30 min, RT) after thrice-washed PBS steps. Fluorescent images were acquired using a Leica DMi8 inverted microscope. Amplex Red Cholesterol and Cholesteryl Ester Assay Kit (S0211S) is from Beyotime.

Time-of-drug-addition assay

The temporal drug efficacy was evaluated as previously established (Deng et al., 2014). RD cells (4 × 10⁵ cells/well) were seeded in 12-well plates and cultured overnight. Following EV-A71 H infection (MOI=0.001), cells received ondansetron (30 μM) or NITD008 (1 μM) according to predefined treatment schedules. Total RNA was extracted at 12hpi for subsequent qRT-PCR analysis of viral replication dynamics.

Quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, Cat. 74,106). EV-A71 RNA levels were quantified by one-step qRT-PCR (One Step PrimeScript RT-PCR Kit, Takara, Cat. RR064A) with virus-specific primers (Forward: 5′-CCAATCTCAGCGGCTTGGAG-3′; Reverse: 5′-CACTCAAGCTCTACCGGCAC-3′) and a dual-labeled probe (FAM-TCCAATCGATGGCTGC

-TCACCTGCGT-BHQ1). Absolute RNA copy numbers were determined by linear regression of Ct values against a standard curve generated from serial dilutions of plasmid controls. Relative quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) was performed in triplicate qPCR reactions for both target and reference genes (ABCA1: Forward 5′-GGAAGAACAGTCATTGGGACAC-3′; Reverse: 5′-GCTACAAA-CCCTTTTAGCCAGT-3′; ABCG1: Forward 5′- ATTCAGGGACCTTTCCTATTCGG-3′; Reverse: 5′- CTCACCACTATTGAACTTCCCG-3′; HMGCS1: Forward 5′- GATGTGGGAATTGTTGCC

-CTT-3′; Reverse: 5′-ATTGTCTCTGTTCCAACTTCCAG-3′; GAPDH Forward 5′- GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT-3′; Reverse: 5′- GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG −3′;) and calculated by using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Western blot assay

RD cells were seeded in 12-well plates (4.0 × 10⁵ cells/well) and cultured overnight. Cells were subsequently infected with EV-A71 (MOI=0.001) in 2% FBS DMEM containing serially diluted compounds: ondansetron (30–0.37 μM). Control groups received virus- and compound-free 2% FBS DMEM. Following 36 h incubation, cells were washed thrice with ice-cold PBS and lysed in 150 μL RIPA buffer supplemented with protease/phosphatase inhibitors. Protein lysates were resolved through 10% SDS-PAGE and electro transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were sequentially blocked with 5% non-fat milk (1 h, RT), probed overnight at 4 °C with anti-VP1 (1:1000) or α-tubulin (1:5000) antibodies, and incubated with HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (1:5000, 1 h, RT). Chemiluminescent signals were captured using a FluorChem imaging system (ProteinSimple).

EV-A71 infection in mice

Neonatal ICR mice (1-day-old; Vital River Laboratory, Beijing) were intraperitoneally inoculated with EV-A71 H strain (10⁶ PFU/mouse) in 25 μL volume. At 4 h post-infection (hpi), treatment groups received daily intraperitoneal injections for 7 consecutive days: ondansetron (1.5, 0.75, 0.37mg/kg). Virus controls received equivalent saline injections. Body weights and survival rates were monitored through 21 days post-infection (dpi), with survival analysis performed using Mantel-Cox log-rank test.

Clinical symptoms were scored as follows: 0, healthy; 1, ruffled fur and hunchbacked appearance; 2, weakness in the hind limbs; 3, paralysis in a single hind limb; 4, paralysis in both hind limbs; 5, moribundity and death.

Four experimental groups (n = 10/group) received pre-exposure prophylaxis: ondansetron (1.5 mg/kg), or 0.5% CMCNa vehicle, administered every 12 h for 72 h prior to EV-A71 challenge (10⁵ PFU/mouse). At 24 h post-challenge, mice were euthanized for tissue collection (brain, hind limp muscle, liver, lung, spleen). Viral RNA quantification was performed via absolute qRT-PCR using tissue homogenates.

Transcriptome analysis

RD cells were treated with Ondansetron (0.1% DMSO control) and EV-A71 (MOI=0.001) in 2% FBS DMEM with 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C for 24 h. Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol (Invitrogen), with purity verified by NanoDrop One spectrophotometry. RNA sequencing libraries were prepared and sequenced by Majorbio Biotech (Shanghai) on the Illumina platform. Bioinformatics analysis was conducted via the Majorbio Cloud Platform (www.majorbio.com; accessed 2024–08–20).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 7.0. Survival curves were compared by Mantel-Cox log-rank test. Intergroup differences were assessed using unpaired two-tailed Student's t-test (two groups) or one-way ANOVA (multiple groups). Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Results

Ondansetron inhibits EV-A71 infection in vitro

To assess the antiviral efficacy of ondansetron against EV-A71, a cytopathic effect (CPE) protection assay was conducted in rhabdomyosarcoma (RD) cells. The results demonstrated that ondansetron show the dose-dependent protective effects against EV-A71-induced CPE. The half-maximal effective concentrations (EC₅₀) for these compounds were determined as follows: ondansetron (2.02 ± 1.42 μM) (Fig. 1B). Notably, ondansetron also exhibited minimal cytotoxicity, with half-maximal cytotoxic concentrations (CC₅₀) exceeding 100 μM. Corresponding selectivity index (SI) values in RD cells were calculated as >48.63 for ondansetron (Figs. 1B). These data collectively indicate that ondansetron exerts significant antiviral activity by mitigating EV-A71-induced cytopathic damage. Additionally, we also test the other 5-HT3 antagonists such as granisetron, tropisetron, and palonosetron. The results showed that these antagonists did not have the antiviral activity (Supplementary Figure 1A-C). To further delineate the inhibitory effects of ondansetron on viral replication kinetics and progeny virion production, quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) and plaque-forming unit (PFU) assays were performed. Ondansetron reduces the production of EV-A71 virus protein

To investigate the inhibitory effects of ondansetron on EV-A71 viral protein synthesis, viral capsid protein VP1 expression was analyzed via immunofluorescence staining and Western blot. Immunofluorescence imaging demonstrated that treatment with the ondansetron markedly suppressed VP1 protein production (Fig. 2A). Western blot analysis further corroborated this finding, revealing a dose-dependent attenuation of VP1 protein levels following ondansetron treatment (Fig. 2B). Collectively, these results indicate that ondansetron significantly inhibits EV-A71 viral protein expression by reducing VP1 synthesis in a concentration-dependent manner. Besides, we also detected the expression of 5-HTR3A in RD cells. It turned out that RD cells also express this protein. To figure out whether ondansetron exerted it antiviral effects dependent on 5-HT3 receptor, we knockdown the expression of 5-HTR3A in RD cells and then infected cells with EV-A71. By detected the expression of viral capsid VP1, we found that knockdown of 5-HTR3A did not affect the VP1 synthesis. Ondansetron acts at the pre-infection stage of EV-A71 infection

To elucidate the specific phase of EV-A71 replication targeted by ondansetron, a time-of-addition pharmacological assay was performed. The compound was introduced into cell culture systems at precisely defined intervals relative to viral inoculation, as illustrated in Fig. 3A. NITD008, a pharmacologically validated flavivirus inhibitor with documented anti-EV-A71 activity (Shang et al., 2014; Lo et al., 2016; Westerterp et al., 2014), was used as the positive control. Quantitative real-time PCR analysis revealed significant suppression of viral RNA synthesis by ondansetron during Stages I and II, indicating potent inhibitory effects on viral pre-adsorption, cellular attachment, and subsequent internalization processes (Fig. 3B). To further explore the specific viral entry stage that ondansetron primarily inhibited, we performed the viral attachment and viral internalization experiment. The results showed that ondansetron was able to inhibit the viral attachment stage, while had few effect on viral internalization (Fig. 3C-D). Ondansetron confers protection in neonatal ICR mice against lethal EV-A71 challenge and reduce tissue viral loads

To evaluate the in vivo therapeutic efficacy of ondansetron against EV-A71 infection, we employed a lethal 1-day-old ICR suckling mouse model (Deng et al., 2014). Mice were intraperitoneally (i.p.) infected with 10⁶ plaque-forming units (PFU) of EV-A71 virus (H strain), followed by i.p. administration of varying concentrations of ondansetron (1.5, 0.37mg/kg) for six consecutive days. Treatment with ondansetron at 1.5mg/kg yielded a survival rate of 70% (Fig. 4A). Body weight and survival rates were monitored up to 21 days post-infection (d.p.i.) (Fig. 4B). And treatment with ondansetron at 1.5mg/kg reduces the clinical scores, indicating an alleviation of EV-A71 infection-induced clinical symptoms (Fig. 4C). To further explore the antiviral effects of ondansetron in vivo, we quantified viral loads in various tissues of EV-A71-infected mice. Mice pretreated with ondansetron (1.5mg/kg) through twice-daily i.p. injections for three consecutive days, starting one day prior to EV-A71 infection (10⁵ PFU of H strain), were euthanized for tissue collection. As shown in (Fig. 4D-H), treatment with ondansetron significantly reduced viral loads across all tested tissues. These findings indicate that ondansetron effectively inhibits EV-A71 viral replication in vivo.

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was subsequently conducted to annotate the functional categories of DEGs in the ondansetron-treated group, including molecular functions, cellular components, and biological processes (Fig. 5D). In parallel, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis identified 20 significantly enriched signaling pathways (Fig. 5E). A heatmap was generated to illustrate the expression profiles of representative DEGs (Fig. 5F). Among these genes, ABCA1 (ATP-binding cassette subfamily A member 1) and ABCG1 (ATP-binding cassette subfamily G member 1) mediate cholesterol efflux from the plasma membrane to high-density lipoprotein (HDL), thereby maintaining lipid homeostasis and membrane integrity. In contrast, HMGCS1 (3‑hydroxy‑3-methylglutaryl-CoA synthase 1) contributes to membrane stability by regulating cholesterol biosynthesis. Previous studies have demonstrated that ABCA1 and ABCG1 suppress EV-A71 infection by modulating membrane cholesterol content, whereas HMGCS1 facilitates viral entry through enhanced cholesterol synthesis (Sanders et al., 2021; Singh et al., 2024; Matsuo, 2022; Zhou et al., 2021; Navari, 2013). We next validated the expression of these three genes in RD cells. Consistently with the result of the RNA-seq data, the mRNA levels of ABCA1 and ABCG1 were elevated, while HMGCS1 was decreased (Fig. 5G). Meanwhile, the cholesterol level was also decreased in ondansetron plus EV-A71 treatment group (Fig. 5H). Furthermore, the rescue experiment showed that supplementing cholesterol–methyl-β-cyclodextrin complex partially restored the viral RNA copy numbers (Fig. 5I) . Collectively, these findings suggested that cholesterol-related homeostasis, mediated by host genes ABCA1, ABCG1 and HMGCS1, played a critical role in EV-A71 pathogenesis.

Discussion

EV-A71 remains a major global health concern due to its association with severe neurological complications and the lack of effective antiviral therapies. In the present study, we, for the first time, demonstrate that the clinically approved ondansetron exhibits significant antiviral activity against EV-A71 infection both in vitro and in vivo and may have potential roles in the clinical treatment of EV-A71 infection. Our findings also provide novel evidence and important preclinical experimental data supporting drug repurposing strategies and hold significant implications for the development of new clinical indications for ondansetron.

Previous antiviral strategies against EV-A71 have primarily focused on targeting viral proteins or replication processes. However, virus-directed therapies are often limited by rapid viral mutation and the emergence of drug resistance. In contrast, targeting host factors required for viral infection offers a complementary strategy with potentially broader antiviral activity and a higher barrier to resistance. In this context, time-of-addition assays revealed that the inhibitory effect is most pronounced during the pre-entry phase. Further data indicate that ondansetron exerts its antiviral effects predominantly at the early stage of infection, especially for viral attachment. The transcriptomic analysis, which identified significant alterations in cholesterol metabolism-related genes, including ABCA1, ABCG1, and HMGCS1. Cholesterol is a critical component of the plasma membrane and plays an essential role in maintaining membrane fluidity and the formation of lipid rafts, which are often exploited by viruses for entry. ABCA1 and ABCG1 are key regulators of cholesterol efflux, whereas HMGCS1 is involved in cholesterol biosynthesis. Previous studies have demonstrated that modulation of membrane cholesterol can significantly affect viral entry and infectivity. In agreement with these findings, our results suggest that ondansetron disrupts cholesterol homeostasis, thereby altering membrane properties and impairing EV-A71 attachment. Importantly, our in vivo data showed the significantly improved survival in EV-A71-infected neonatal mice and reduced viral loads among multiple tissues by ondansetron treatment. These findings highlight the translational potential of ondansetron as a therapeutic candidate, particularly given its well-established clinical safety profile.

Despite these promising findings, several limitations and unresolved issues remain in this study. Firstly, we only investigated the antiviral effects of ondansetron as a 5-HT3 receptor inhibitor. Currently, multiple 5-HT3 receptor antagonists have been identified, including granisetron, tropisetron, and palonosetron (Rojas et al., 2010; Owino and Chu, 2019). While our data showed that these antagonists could not inhibit EV-A71 infection. Besides, knockdown of 5-HTR3A did not affect the antiviral activity of ondansetron. These results indicated that the antiviral activity of ondansetron might be an off-target effect. Or in another word, it is a kind of drug repurposing. Drug repurposing has emerged as a highly attractive strategy to shorten development timelines and mitigate safety risks. Andrographolide, which inhibits EV-D68 maturation in acidic endosomes, or Pirlindole, which suppresses CV-B3 replication has demonstrated significant promise. Furthermore, natural products, particularly flavonoids like Quercetin and Apigenin, offer multi-target potential by blocking viral attachment or inhibiting IRES-mediated translation [36]. In our research, the specific protein target that ondansetron interacts with is still unclear and it needs more experiments to explore. Secondly, we find that ondansetron can affect the expression of cholesterol metabolism genes ABCA1, ABCG1, and HMGCS1, thereby decrease the cell cholesterol level and inhibit EV-A71 attachment. However, the precise molecular mechanisms of the cell membrane cholesterol homeostasis and its interaction model with ondansetron in viral attachment remain to be further elucidated. Finally, although ondansetron is an approved drug, its pharmacokinetic optimization, dose–response characteristics, and clinical efficacy against EV-A71 infection have not yet been established, and further clinical studies are needed. Nonetheless, our work is the first to reveal that the ondansetron exhibits significant inhibitory effects on EV-A71 infection both in vitro and in vivo in preclinical studies. This provides a potential therapeutic option for the clinical treatment of EV-A71 infection and identifies important potential host targets for developing antivirals against EV-A71.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that ondansetron exhibits significant antiviral activity in both in vitro and in vivo models of EV-A71 infection. Mechanistically, ondansetron may exert its antiviral effect by modulating cholesterol metabolism-related genes (ABCA1, ABCG1, HMGCS1), thereby affecting the stability of the cell cholesterol homeostasis, which in turn influences viral attachment and inhibit virus entry.
