Non-Commutative Wiener-Wintner theorem for amenable group actions
Panchugopal Bikram 1,2, Sudipta Kundu 1,2, Hariharan G 1,2

Abstract
. Let G G be a locally compact second countable amenable group acting on a finite von Neumann algebra ( ℳ , τ ) (\mathcal{M},\tau) by trace-preserving automorphisms. In this article, we establish a Jacobs-de Leeuw-Glicksberg decomposition for this action, obtaining a decomposition of ℳ \mathcal{M} into its almost periodic and weakly mixing components. As an application, we prove a noncommutative Wiener–Wintner theorem for amenable group actions on finite von Neumann algebras.

1.
Introduction

In this article, we study a Wiener-Wintner theorem using a Jacobs-de Leeuw-Glicksberg (JdLG) decomposition of a finite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
acted upon continuously, and ergodically, by a locally compact second countable (l.c.s.c.) amenable group
G
. Wiener-Wintner theorems are a class of pointwise convergence results for weighted ergodic averages on a full measure set independent of the weight. The study of Wiener-Wintner theorems was first started by Wiener and Wintner in their 1941 paper
[
WW41
]
for the case of a single measure preserving transformation
T
acting on a measure space
X
, the theorem states that given an ergodic measure preserving system
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
and a complex number (referred to as weight)
\lambda
of modulus 1, for any
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
there is a full measure set
\Omega_{f}
independent of
\lambda
such that the average of the form (hereafter referred to as a Wiener-Wintner average)

\displaystyle a_{n}(f,\lambda):=\frac{1}{n}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{k}f(T^{k}x)

converges for all
x\in\Omega_{f}
. Further advances were made by Bellow and Losert in their paper
[
BL85
]
, where pointwise a.e. convergence was proved for different weight classes and for subsequential counterparts of the Wiener-Wintner averages in the measure space setting.

A noncommutative counterpart of this theorem in the setting of a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, equipped with an ergodic automorphism
\alpha\in Aut(\mathcal{M})
was studied by Litvinov in his 2014 work,
[
Lit14
]
, where the uniform convergence in the bilaterally almost uniform (b.a.u.) sense, of Wiener-Wintner averages of the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\lambda^{k}\alpha^{k}(x),\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{1}

is proved. Similar results were studied for different weight classes in the multiparameter case by Hong
[
HS18
]
and later, by
[
O’B21
]
.

Parallel to these developments, a Wiener-Wintner theorem for the case of a continuous and ergodic action of a l.c.s.c. amenable group
G
, preserving a probability measure
\mu
on the compact metric space
X
, was also studied by Pavel Zorin-Kranich in his 2014 paper
[
ZK14
]
.

In
[
Bäh19
]
, M. Bahring proved, using a finitary version of the van der Corput lemma, the uniform convergence of the weighted average

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{n}(f)(x):=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\xi(g)f(gx)dm(g)

on a full
\mu
-measure subset of
X

for continuous and ergodic action of a locally compact abelian (and thereby amenable) group
G
on a compact space
X
, where
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
and
\mu
is probability measure on
X
preserved by the action. A proof using the joining of two actions of
G
on the measure space was given by
[
EA21
]
.

A result that is used in proving Wiener-Wintner theorems (both classical and noncommutative) is the Jacob deLeeuw Glicksberg (JdLG) decomposition. It states that given a unitary representation
\pi
of a locally compact group
G
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
equipped with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, there exists a decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{c}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{wm}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{c}=\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}:\{U_{g}{\xi}:g\in G\}\text{ is totally bounded in }\mathcal{H}\}
, is called the compact part and
\mathcal{H}_{wm}=\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{c}
, is called the weak mixing part. If
G
is amenable and equipped with a Haar measure
m
, we may further describe
\mathcal{H}_{wm}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{wm}=\{\xi\in\mathcal{H}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}|\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle|dm(g)=0,~\text{for all}~\eta\in\mathcal{H}\}.

In the present article, our set up for the non-commutative Wiener-Wintner theorem is the following:
G
is a l.c.s.c. unimodular amenable group with a Følner sequence
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, acting by
*
-automorphisms continuously and ergodically (meaning,
\alpha_{g}(x)=x,
for all
g\in G\text{ implies }x=k1
for some
k\in\mathbb{C}
) on a finite von Neuman algebra
\mathcal{M}
while preserving a faithful normal tracial state
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}
. The action of
G
on
\mathcal{M}
, given by
\alpha:G\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
, may be extended unitarily to the non-commutative
L^{2}
-space associated to
\mathcal{M}
to give rise to a unitary group representation on the GNS Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
associated to
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
\tau
. In this case, the corresponding Wiener-Wintner average is of the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\phi(\pi(g)u)dm(g),

where
(F_{n})
is a Følner sequence satisfying Tempelman condition,
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}_{d}
is a finite dimensional unitary representation and
\phi
is a complex valued continuous function on
\mathcal{U}_{d}
, the unitary group of operators on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, and
u\in\mathcal{U}_{d}
is a unitary.

The aim of this article is to establish the convergence of the abovementioned noncommutative Wiener-Wintner averages in the b.a.u. sense:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let G be a l.c.s.c. amenable unimodular group acting continuously on
\mathcal{M}
by
*
-automorphisms. Let
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}_{d}
be a finite dimensional unitary representation of
G
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
where
\mathcal{U}_{d}
is the group of unitary operators on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Then for any continuous function
\phi
on
\mathcal{U}_{d}
the following weighted average

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\phi(\pi(g)u)dm(g)

converges in b.a.u where
u\in\mathcal{U}_{d}
. Moreover if
x
is in the weak-mixing part of the representation
\alpha
then the average converges to
0
in b.a.u.

In order to prove this result, we use the JdLG decomposition of the GNS space
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. We establish the following version of the JdLG decomposition for the finite von Neumann algebra.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra with a f.n trace
\tau
and
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a
\tau
-preserving noncommutative dynamical system. Consider the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{c}

\displaystyle=\{a\in\mathcal{M}:a\widehat{1}\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\},\text{ is a von Neumann subalgebra, and,}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{wm}

\displaystyle=\biggl\{a\in\mathcal{M}:\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\left|\tau(b\alpha_{g}(a))\right|dm(g)=0,~\text{ for all }b\in\mathcal{M}\biggr\}.

Then
\mathcal{M}
can be written as

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{c}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{wm}
such that

\overline{\mathcal{M}\cap L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}=L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)~\text{ and }~\overline{\mathcal{M}\cap L^{2}_{\text{wm}}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}~=L^{2}_{\text{wm}}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

Another result of significance in ergodic theory, classical and noncommutative, is the van der Corput inequality.
It is often stated in literature in various forms, but of particular importance to us, is a van der Corput inequality for the setting of von Neumann algebras. A noncommutative version of this inequality was first proved by
[
NSZ03
]
for bounded functions
a:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a C*-algebra, and was used in
[
Lit14
]
. A finitary version of this lemma was proved for bounded functions
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, for a abelian group
G
, by
[
Bäh19
]
. In section 5 of this article, we prove the following van der Corput inequality:

(A van der Corput inequality for amenable groups)
.

Let
G
be an l.c.s.c amenable group equipped with a right Haar measure

, and let
(F_{n})
be a right Følner sequence in
G
and
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra. Let
f:G\to\mathcal{M}
be a bounded measurable function. Then for every

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle~~~+3\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}+\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\left(\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\right)^{2}.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article, let
\mathcal{M}
denotes a von Neumann algebra acting on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, i.e.
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. The operator norm inherited from
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is written as
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty}
; when no confusion can arise, we abbreviate it simply by
\lVert\cdot\rVert
. The commutant of
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. We further write
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
for the collection of projections contained in
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra, and let
\mathcal{M}_{*}
denote its predual. Elements of
\mathcal{M}_{*}
are called
normal linear functionals
on
\mathcal{M}
. A functional
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
is said to be
positive
if

\phi(x)\geq 0,~~\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{M}_{+},

where
\mathcal{M}_{+}
denotes the cone of positive elements of
\mathcal{M}
.
A positive functional
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
is called a
state
if
\phi(1)=1.

It is called
faithful
if for every
x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
,

\phi(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=0.

For notational convenience, the term
faithful normal
will be abbreviated by
f.n.
Given an f.n. state
\phi
on
\mathcal{M}
, we denote by
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\phi)
the Hilbert space arising from the GNS construction associated with
\phi
. For each
a\in\mathcal{M}
, we write
\widehat{a}
for the corresponding vector in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\phi)
. Moreover, we identify
\mathcal{M}
with its image in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\phi))
under the canonical GNS representation.

2.1.
Non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces (
1\leq p\leq\infty
)

Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful, normal, semifinite (f.n.s.) trace
\tau
. An (possibly unbounded) operator

x:\mathcal{D}(x)\subseteq\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H},

which is densely defined, closed, and affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
, i.e, satisfying
u^{\prime*}xu^{\prime}=x
for every unitary
u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, will be denoted as
x\,\eta\,\mathcal{M}
. Such an operator
x
is said to be
\tau
-measurable if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(1-e)<\varepsilon
and
e\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(x)
. The collection of all
\tau
-measurable operators affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
is then defined by

L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{\,x\,\eta\,\mathcal{M}:x\text{ is }\tau\text{-measurable}\,\}.

The space
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
forms a
∗
-algebra under the adjoint operation, strong sum
x+y:=\overline{x+y}
, and strong product
x\cdot y:=\overline{xy}
, where
\overline{x}
denotes the closure of
x
. Detailed descriptions may be found in
[
SZ19
,
Hia21
]
.

Several natural topologies arise on
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. The first relevant one is the
measure topology
, whose neighborhoods are of the form:
x+\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\delta),~~\varepsilon,\delta>0,\;x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau),

where

\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\delta):=\{\,x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\text{there exist}~e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})\text{ with }\tau(1-e)<\delta\text{ and }\|exe\|<\varepsilon\,\}.

A net
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
converges to
x
in the measure topology precisely when, for every
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, there exists
i_{0}\in I
such that for all
i\geq i_{0}
one may find
e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
with
\tau(1-e_{i})<\delta
and

\|\,e_{i}(x_{i}-x)e_{i}\,\|<\varepsilon.

By
[
Nel74
, Theorem 1]
, the space
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is complete in the measure topology. Furthermore, this topology is metrizable and
\mathcal{M}
is dense in
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.
The topologies of interest are the
bilateral almost uniform
(b.a.u.) and almost uniform (a.u.) topology.

Definition 2.1
.

A net
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is said to converge
bilaterally almost uniformly
(b.a.u.) (resp.
almost uniformly
(a.u.)) to
x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(1-e)<\varepsilon
and

\displaystyle\lim_{i}\|\,e(x_{i}-x)e\,\|=0~~~(\text{resp. }\lim_{i}\|\,(x_{i}-x)e\,\|=0).

Clearly, almost uniform(a.u.) convergence of a net implies the bilateral almost uniform(b.a.u) convergence.
The trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}_{+}
extends in the standard way to
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)_{+}
by

\tau(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,d\tau(e_{\lambda}),

where
x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,de_{\lambda}
is the spectral decomposition of
x
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
,
consider the set

L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\tau(|x|^{p})<\infty\}.

This forms a normed linear space with norm
\|x\|_{p}:=\tau(|x|^{p})^{1/p}
. For
p=\infty
, we define
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\mathcal{M}
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, the usual operator norm
\|\cdot\|
in
\mathcal{M}
. The space
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is called the
non-commutative
L^{p}
- space
.
We recall the following theorem from
[
Hia21
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For every
p\in[1,\infty]
,
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a Banach space with the norm

\|\cdot\|_{p}
. In particular,
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a Hilbert space with the inner
product

\langle x,y\rangle=\tau(y^{*}x).

Moreover,
\mathcal{M}\cap L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

is dense in
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
.

2.2.
Noncommutative dynamical systems

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(E,\|\cdot\|)
be a real ordered Banach space.
Then a
noncommutative dynamical system
is a triple
(E,G,\gamma)
, where
\gamma:G\to\mathcal{B}(E)
is a mapping satisfying
\gamma_{s}\circ\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{st}
for all
s,t\in G
, and the following conditions hold:

(1)

for every
a\in E
, the orbit map
s\mapsto\gamma_{s}(a)
is continuous on
G
, where
E
is equipped with the norm topology (and the
w^{*}
-topology in the case
E=\mathcal{M}
);

(2)

the operators are uniformly bounded, i.e.,
\sup_{s\in G}\|\gamma_{s}\|<\infty
;

(3)

positivity is preserved: if
a\geq 0
, then
\gamma_{s}(a)\geq 0
for all
s\in G
.

We further assume that if
E=\mathcal{M}
,
\gamma_{s}
is a *-automorphism on
\mathcal{M}
for all
s\in G
.
Further, suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a finite von Neumann algebra with a f.n state
\tau.
We say that
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
is
\tau
-preserving if
\tau\circ\alpha_{g}=\tau
, for all
g\in G.

Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a noncommutative dynamical system, where
G
is a locally compact second countable amenable group acting on a finite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau.

The action is given by a homomorphism

\alpha:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}),

where each
\alpha_{g}
is a
*
-automorphism of
\mathcal{M}
. This means that for every
g\in G
and every
x\in\mathcal{M}
,

\alpha_{g}(x^{*})=\alpha_{g}(x)^{*}.

We also assume that the action preserves the trace, that is,
\tau\circ\alpha_{g}=\tau\quad\text{for all }g\in G
. We also have

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in G}\|\alpha_{g}\|\leq 1.
Now let
x\in\mathcal{M}\cap L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Then

|\alpha_{g}(x)|^{2}=\alpha_{g}(x)^{*}\alpha_{g}(x)=\alpha_{g}(x^{*}x)=\alpha_{g}(|x|^{2})=\alpha_{g}(|x|)^{2}.

Hence,
|\alpha_{g}(x)|=\alpha_{g}(|x|)
and
|\alpha_{g}(x)|^{n}=\alpha_{g}(|x|^{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence, by the continuous functional calculus and Weierstrass’ theorem,

|\alpha_{g}(x)|^{p}=\alpha_{g}(|x|^{p}),~~~\text{ for all }~~1\leq p<\infty.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{g}(x)\|^{p}_{p}=\tau(|\alpha_{g}(x)|^{p})=\tau(\alpha_{g}(|x|^{p}))=\tau(|x|^{p})=\|x\|^{p}_{p}\implies\|\alpha_{g}(x)\|_{p}=\|x\|_{p}.

Since
\mathcal{M}\cap L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is dense in
L^{p}(M,\tau)
, it follows that
\alpha_{g}
extends uniquely to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

3.
Amenable Groups

We recall one of the many equivalent definitions of amenability.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable (l.c.s.c.) group equipped with a left (resp. right) Haar measure
m
.
The group
G
is called
amenable
if it admits a left (resp. right) Følner sequence in the sense of the following definition.

(i)

A sequence
(F_{n})
of nonempty compact subsets of
G
is called a
left (resp. right) Følner sequence
if

\displaystyle\frac{m(KF_{n}\,\triangle\,F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\longrightarrow 0~~(\text{resp.}\frac{m(F_{n}K\,\triangle\,F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\longrightarrow 0)\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty

for every compact set
K\subset G
.

(ii)

A Følner sequence
(F_{n})
is said to be
tempered
if there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

m\bigl(\bigcup_{k<n}F^{-1}_{k}F_{n}\bigr)\leq Cm(F_{n}).

(iii)

A Følner sequence
(F_{n})
is said to satisfy the
Tempelman
condition if there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

m(\bigcup_{k\leq n}F_{k}^{-1}F_{n})\leq Cm(F_{n}).

(iv)

We also require the definition of admissible Følner sequences, for this definition we refer to
[
CW22
]
.

Possibly, some of the following results concerning amenable groups are folklore in the literature. However, since we have been unable to locate a reference containing these statements in the required form, we include the proofs for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a l.c.s.c amenable unimodular group with Følner sequence
(F_{n})
. Given a compact symmetric set
K\subseteq G,e\in K
and
k\in\mathbb{N},k\geq 2
there exists
E_{n}\subseteq F_{n}
and
N(K,k)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq N(K,k)
,
E_{n}K^{k}\subseteq F_{n}
and
\frac{m(F_{n}\setminus E_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\leq\frac{1}{k}.

Proof.

Let us define
E_{n}:=\{x\in F_{n}:xK^{k}\subseteq F_{n}\}=F_{n}\cap F_{n}K^{k}.
Clearly,
E_{n}\subseteq F_{n}
and
E_{n}K^{k}\subseteq F_{n}
, so we use the Følner condition to obtain the inequality.
∎

.

Let
G

(F_{n})

(1)

(2)

m({F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k})\leq C\frac{k}{k-1}m(F^{\prime}_{n,k})\leq 2Cm(F^{\prime}_{n,k})

\frac{m(K{F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k}\Delta{F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k})}{m({F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k})}\leq\frac{2C}{k}

Proof.

Lemma
3.2
implies that there exists

Define
A_{i}:=K^{i}{E_{n}}^{-1}E_{n}K^{i}~~\text{for}~~i\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\}

K
is symmetric, it follows that
A_{i}\subseteq F_{n}^{-1}F_{n}
.
It also follows that
A_{i}\subseteq A_{i+1}
for
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,k-1\}
and

(3.1)

\displaystyle~~\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}m(A_{i+1}\setminus A_{i})=m(A_{k}\setminus A_{0})\leq m(F_{n}^{-1}F_{n})\leq Cm(F_{n}).

Hence, there exists
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,k-1\}
such that

m(A_{i+1}\setminus A_{i})\leq\frac{Cm(F_{n})}{k}.

For this
i
we define
F^{\prime}_{n,k}:=E_{n}K^{i}
. Clearly,
E_{n}\subseteq F^{\prime}_{n,k}\subseteq F_{n}
, which implies that

(3.2)

\displaystyle F_{n}\setminus F^{\prime}_{n,k}\subseteq F_{n}\setminus E_{n}\implies\frac{m(F_{n}\setminus F^{\prime}_{n,k})}{m(F_{n})}\leq\frac{1}{k}.

Since

).

Note that
{F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k}=A_{i}

{KF^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k}\subseteq A_{i+1}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{m(K{F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k}\Delta{F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k})}{m({F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m(A_{i+1}\setminus A_{i})}{m({F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cm(F_{n})}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{m({F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{k}\frac{m(F_{n})}{m(F^{\prime}_{n,k})}\cdot\frac{m(F^{\prime}_{n,k})}{m({F^{\prime-1}_{n,k}}F^{\prime}_{n,k})}\leq\frac{2C}{k}.

The last inequality from the second last line follows the fact that

and the Ineq. (

).
∎

Suppose

.

).

4.
Jacob de Leeuw Glicksberg decomposition for finite von Neumann algebra

In this section we study a JdLG decomposition for finite von Neumann algebras for the action by an amenable group on a finite von Neumann algebra.
In October 2025, Sohail Farhangi,Y.Kuznetsova and Mickey Barthmann proved the following version of JdLG decomposition for the amenable group action on a Banach space.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
BFK25
]

Let
G
be a locally compact amenable group,
m
a left Haar measure,

(F_{i})_{i\in I}
a left Følner net,
E
a Banach space, and

\pi
a relatively weakly compact representation of
G
on
E
.
Then
\pi
is JdLG-admissible, and it follows that

\displaystyle E_{c}:

\displaystyle=\{\,\xi\in E:\pi(G)\xi\text{ is relatively compact in }E\,\}\text{ and }

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{wm}}:

\displaystyle=\left\{\,\xi\in E:\lim_{i}\frac{1}{m(F_{i})}\int_{F_{i}}\bigl|\langle\pi(g)\xi,x^{\prime}\rangle\bigr|\,dm(g)=0\text{ for all }x^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}\right\}.

E_{c}
is referred to as the
compact
part and
E_{\mathrm{wm}}
is refered to as the
weak mixing
part.
When

, a Hilbert space, and
\pi
to be a unitary representation of

on
\mathcal{H}
. Since
\|\pi(g)\|=1
for all
g\in G

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
under WOT is compact, and the representation is JdLG admissible by
[
EFHN15
, Example 16.25, Chapter 16]
. Moreover, we have
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{c}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\text{wm}}
.

We recall the following definition:

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. A set
\mathcal{B}\subset X
is totally bounded if, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists a finite set
\mathcal{F}(\epsilon,\mathcal{B})=\{x_{1},x_{2}...,x_{n}\}\subset X
such that for all
b\in\mathcal{B}

x_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(\epsilon,\mathcal{B})

d(x_{i},b)<\epsilon.

Therefore, one may characterise the compact part in terms of totally boundedness. We will also use the following characterisation of compactness:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. Let
A\subset X
. Then,
\overline{A}\subset X
is compact iff every sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in

admits a convergent subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau

(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)

\tau
-preserving noncommutative dynamical system.

Let

be the GNS Hilbert space associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.
Suppose
\widehat{1}
denotes the cyclic and separating vector associated with
\tau.

Consider the family of unitaries
\{u_{g}:g\in G\}
in

defined by

u_{g}\widehat{x}=\widehat{\alpha_{g}(x)},~\text{ for all }g\in G.

We note that

can be thought as an extension of the

\alpha_{g}
of

to

We apply the JdLG decomposition to the family

and
L^{2}_{\text{wm}}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for the compact and weak mixing part respectively. Now we like to obtain a JdLG decomposition for

(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
. Consider the following sets;

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{c}

\displaystyle=\{a\in\mathcal{M}:a\widehat{1}\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\},\text{ is a von Neumann subalgebra, and,}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{wm}

\displaystyle=\biggl\{a\in\mathcal{M}:\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\left|\tau(b\alpha_{g}(a))\right|dm(g)=0,~\text{for all}~b\in\mathcal{M}\biggr\}.

We wish to prove the following theorem, which is a version of the JdLG decomposition for finite von Neumann algebras.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra with a f.n trace
\tau
and
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a
\tau
-preserving noncommutative dynamical system. Then
\mathcal{M}

\overline{\mathcal{M}\cap L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}=L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)~\text{ and }~\overline{\mathcal{M}\cap L^{2}_{\text{wm}}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}~=L^{2}_{\text{wm}}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

Remark 4.5
.

Since the definition of the compact part
\mathcal{M}_{c}
is not dependent on the Følner sequence, the definition of the weak mixing part
\mathcal{M}_{wm}
is also not dependent on the Følner sequence, by the uniqueness of the orthogonal complement of the closed Hilbert subspace
L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

We prove the above Theorem
4.4
through the sequence of results. We start with the following result.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be finite von Neumann algebra with a f.n trace
\tau
and
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be
\tau
-preserving noncommutative dynamical system. Suppose

x\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, then
x^{*}\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Proof.

Let
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence, then there exists a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
a\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(x)-a\|_{2}=0.

By a property of noncommutative
L^{2}
-spaces, we know that
\|b^{*}\|_{2}=\|b\|_{2}
for all
b\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

We note that
\alpha_{g}(x^{*})=(\alpha_{g}(x))^{*}
for all
g\in G\text{ and }x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Indeed, given
x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
b\in\mathcal{M}
such that

, and since

is a contraction for all

for all

. Since

for all

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{g}(x^{*})-(\alpha_{g}(x))^{*}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|\alpha_{g}(x^{*})-(\alpha_{g}(x))^{*}+\alpha_{g}(b^{*})-(\alpha_{g}(b))^{*}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha_{g}(x^{*})-\alpha_{g}(b^{*})\|_{2}+\|(\alpha_{g}(x))^{*}-(\alpha_{g}(b))^{*}\|_{2}

Therefore, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(x^{*})-a^{*}\|_{2}=0.

Therefore,

is also in

.

∎

We note that the above decomposition is merely a vector space direct sum. However, the compact part of the decomposition has more structure than that of a vector space.

Lemma 4.7
.

\mathcal{M}_{c}
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
.
Given the sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and

, there exists a subsequence
(g_{n_{r}})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

is Cauchy. Given the subsequence
(g_{n_{r}})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a further subsequence
(g_{n_{r_{l}}})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
u_{g_{n_{r_{l}}}}(\widehat{b})
is Cauchy. Since every subsequence of a Cauchy subsequence is also Cauchy, we have that
u_{g_{n_{r_{l}}}}(\widehat{a})
is also Cauchy. We call this subsequence
(g_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Choosing this new subsequence, we obtain that
u_{g_{n_{k_{l}}}}(\widehat{a})+u_{g_{n_{k_{l}}}}(\widehat{b})=u_{g_{n_{k_{l}}}}(\widehat{a+b})
is Cauchy in
L^{2}
norm, and consequently, that
a+b\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
.

In order to show that
ab\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
, we need to show that
(\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(\widehat{ab}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy. Let
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(a)\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(b)-\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(a)\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(b)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|(\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(a)-\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(a))\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(b)+(\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(a))(\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(b)-\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(b))\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|(\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(a)-\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(a))\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(b)\right\|_{2}+\left\|(\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(a))(\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(b)-\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(b))\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\{{\|a\|,\|b\|\}}\bigl(\left\|(\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(a)-\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(a))\right\|_{2}+\left\|(\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(b)-\alpha_{g_{n_{k^{\prime}}}}(b))\right\|_{2}\bigr),~~\text{(Since $a,b\in\mathcal{M})$}.

As
k,k^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty
, we get the desired result.

By Lemma
4.6
,
\mathcal{M}_{c}
is closed under taking adjoints.
Now we show that it is closed in the SOT. Let
a\in\overline{\{m\in\mathcal{M}:m\widehat{1}\in L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\}}^{\text{SOT}},
then for given
\epsilon>0
and

.

We recall the Powers-Strømer inequality from
[

]
in order to prove the next lemma:

Proposition 4.8

(Powers–Størmer inequality)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra with a f.n trace
\tau
.
Let
x,y\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)_{+}
, then,

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|x^{2}-y^{2}\|_{1}\leq\|x-y\|_{2}\|x+y\|_{2}.

Consequently, it follows that for
x,y\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

\displaystyle\||x|-|y|\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2max\{\|x\|_{2},\|y\|_{2}\}\|x-y\|_{2}.

If
b\in\mathcal{M}\cap L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, we know that
\alpha_{g}(|b|)=|\alpha_{g}(b)|
for all
g\in G
. We note, by the following corollary, that the same is true when
\alpha_{g}
is extended to
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
as a unitary for all

.

Corollary 4.9
.

\alpha_{g}(|b|)=|\alpha_{g}(b)|
for all
b\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Proof.

First we claim that the map
x\mapsto|x|
, from

to itself, is continous in the
L^{2}
-norm.
Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in

converging to

. Then, by Powers Størmer inequality, we have

\displaystyle\||x_{n}|-|x|\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2max\{\|x_{n}\|_{2},\|x\|_{2}\}\|x_{n}-x\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2max\{M,\|x\|_{2}\}\|x_{n}-x\|_{2}.

As
n\rightarrow\infty
, we prove the claim.

We know that
\alpha_{g}(|x|)=|\alpha_{g}(x)|
holds for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. By the density of
\mathcal{M}
in

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{g}(|x|)-|\alpha_{g}(x)|\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|\alpha_{g}(|x|)-\alpha_{g}(|y_{n}|)+\alpha_{g}(|y_{n}|)-|\alpha_{g}(x)|\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha_{g}(|x|)-\alpha_{g}(|y_{n}|)\|_{2}+\|\alpha_{g}(|y_{n}|)-|\alpha_{g}(x)|\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha_{g}(|x|)-\alpha_{g}(|y_{n}|)\|_{2}+\||\alpha_{g}(y_{n})|-|\alpha_{g}(x)|\|_{2},~(\text{as }y_{n}\in\mathcal{M}\text{ and }|\alpha_{g}(y_{n})|=\alpha_{g}(|y_{n}|)).

By our claim, and the fact that for all

is a contraction on

,

-norm implies

and

-norm as

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{g_{n_{k}}}(|x|)-\alpha_{g_{n_{l}}}(|x|)\|^{2}_{2}

For

\displaystyle max\{a,b\}

\displaystyle=\frac{a+b+|a-b|}{2}

\displaystyle min\{a,b\}

\displaystyle=\frac{a+b-|a-b|}{2}.

Theorem 4.11

Let

Proof.

We begin by considering the following sequence of functions

\displaystyle f_{n}(t)=min(1,n(max(t-a),0)),~\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{R}.

We note that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded above by
1
, and converges pointwise to the function
1_{(a,\infty)}.
Applying the unbounded operator functional calculus to
x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, we obtain that the corresponding sequence of operators
(f_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}
and converge in SOT to
1_{\{s\in\mathbb{R}:s>a\}}(x)=e_{(a,\infty)}(x)
, which is a spectral projection of
x
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}(x)\xi=e_{(a,\infty)}(x)\xi

in
L^{2}
-norm for all
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, and in particular for
\xi=\widehat{1}
.
By Lemma
4.10
and definition of max and min, we have that
n(max(x-a),0)\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)=min(1,n(max(x-a),0))\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

and as a consequence,
e_{(a,\infty)}(x)\in L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let,
y\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be self adjoint.
Then
y
can be written as
y=y_{+}-y_{-}
, where
y_{+}=max\{y,0\}
and
y_{-}=-min\{y,0\}
. By the definition of
max
and
min
,
y_{+},~y_{-}\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Thus, we may assume that
y
is positive.

By the spectral theorem for unbounded operators we have
y=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda de_{\lambda}
where the spectral projections
e_{\lambda}
belong to
\mathcal{M}_{c}
.
Let us define
y_{n}=\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{n}\lambda de_{\lambda}
. Since
y_{n}
is a bounded operator and the spectral projections of
y_{n}
is in
\mathcal{M}_{c}
, therefore, we have
y_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
. We also note that

\displaystyle|y-y_{n}|^{2}=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\lambda^{2}de_{\lambda}+\int_{n}^{\infty}\lambda^{2}de_{\lambda}\leq y^{2}.

Since
y\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
,
y^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
y_{n}
converges to
y
in measure topology, by Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, we have that
y_{n}\to y
in
\|\cdot\|_{2}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Hence
\mathcal{M}\cap L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is dense in
L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

∎

Now we are ready to prove the decomposition theorem.

Proof.

of Theorem
4.4
:
We note that
\mathcal{M}_{c}
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
,
L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
L_{wm}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
are orthogonal subspaces. Since
\mathcal{M}
is a finite von Neumann algebra with f.n trace
\tau
, there exists a f.n conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}_{c}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}
with
\tau\circ\mathcal{E}_{c}=\tau
, which implements the decomposition as follows:

Let
a\in\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{c}(a)\widehat{1}\in\mathcal{M}\cap L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. We need to then show that
(a-\mathcal{E}_{c}(a))\widehat{1}
is orthogonal to
L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Since
\mathcal{M}\cap L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
in
L^{2}
-norm, it is enough to show the orthogonality to elements of the form
b\widehat{1}
, where
b\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
.
Indeed, let
b\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
as above. We have,

\displaystyle\langle a\widehat{1}-\mathcal{E}_{c}(a)\widehat{1},b\widehat{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}=

\displaystyle\tau(b^{*}a-b^{*}\mathcal{E}_{c}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(b^{*}a)-\tau(\mathcal{E}_{c}(b^{*}a))~~\text{ (as $b\in\mathcal{M}_{c}$ )}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0~~\text{ since }\tau\circ\mathcal{E}_{c}=\tau.

therefore,
(a-\mathcal{E}_{c}(a))\widehat{1}\in\mathcal{M}\cap L_{wm}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
,
a-\mathcal{E}_{c}(a)\in\mathcal{M}_{wm}
, and we are done.

To show that
\mathcal{M}\cap L_{wm}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is dense in
L^{2}_{wm}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, we first note that
L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is the GNS Hilbert space associated to the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}_{c}
. Therefore the conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}_{c}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{c}
extends to a projection
p_{c}:L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\rightarrow L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Let
\xi\in L_{wm}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, then, by the density of
\mathcal{M}
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
a\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\|\xi-a\widehat{1}\|_{2}<\epsilon
, and

\displaystyle\|(I-p_{c})(\xi-a\widehat{1})\|_{2}<\epsilon

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\|\xi-(I-\mathcal{E}_{c})(a\widehat{1})\|_{2}<\epsilon.

Since
p_{c}(a\widehat{1})=\mathcal{E}_{c}(a)\widehat{1}
when
a\in\mathcal{M}
, our required element is
a-\mathcal{E}_{c}(a)
, which is in the weak mixing part by the decomposition.
∎

5.
Non-commutative Wiener-Winter theorem

In this section, we establish a noncommutative Wiener-Wintner theorem. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal trace
\tau
, and let
G
be a locally compact second countable group. Suppose that
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
is a
\tau
-preserving dynamical system.

Let
\mathrm{Rep}^{d}_{u}(G)
denote the collection of all finite-dimensional unitary representations

\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}_{d}(\mathbb{C}),

where
\mathcal{U}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
denotes the group of all unitary matrices in
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Further, let
C_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{d}(\mathbb{C}))

denote the collection of all continuous functions on
\mathcal{U}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
with norm equal to
1
. Define

\mathbb{S}:=\mathrm{Rep}^{d}_{u}(G)\times\mathcal{U}_{d}(\mathbb{C})\times C_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{d}(\mathbb{C})).

For
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
,
1<p<\infty
, and
\Gamma=(\pi,u,\phi)\in\mathbb{S}
, consider the averages

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\,d\mu(g),

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\,\phi(\pi(g))u\,d\mu(g).

In this setting, we aim to establish the b.a.u convergence of the averages
(\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma))
for
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
with
1\leq p<\infty
,
uniformly over
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}
; that is, the projection appearing in the definition of bilaterally almost uniform (b.a.u) convergence can be chosen independently of
\Gamma
.

Throughout this section, we assume that
(F_{n})
is an admissible Følner sequence, unless stated otherwise. We begin by recalling the following theorem from
[
CW22
]
, which will be used in the sequel.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(F_{n})
be an admissible Følner sequence on
G
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal trace
\tau
, and let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be a
\tau
-preserving dynamical system. Then, for every
x\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
\lambda>0
, there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)e\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda,~~\text{ and }~~\lambda\,\tau(1-e)\lesssim\|x\|_{1}.

Moreover, for every
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)_{+}
with
1<p<\infty
, there exists
a\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)_{+}
satisfying

\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)\leq a,~~\text{ for all }n\geq 1,\text{ and }\|a\|_{p}\lesssim\|x\|_{p}.

In
[
Cad19
]
, Cadilhac and Wang further proved that, for
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, the averages
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)
converge bilaterally almost uniformly whenever
1\leq p<2
, and converge almost uniformly whenever
2\leq p<\infty
.

For convenience, we record the following consequence, which will be used in later results. We also include a short proof for completeness.

Lemma 5.2
.

(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be as above. Then we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}^{G}}^{\|\cdot\|_{2}}=L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)^{G},

where
\mathcal{M}^{G}
is the fixed point subalgebra of the action
\alpha:G\rightarrow Aut(\mathcal{M})
and
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)^{G}
is the fixed point subspace of the unitary representation of
G
obtained by extending
\alpha_{g}
to
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for all
g\in G
.

Proof.

Inclusion
\subseteq
is immediate. Conversely, suppose
x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)^{G}
. Since
\alpha_{g}(x^{*})=x^{*}
, we can assume that
x
is self-adjoint. Corollary
4.9
implies that we may assume that
x
is positive self-adjoint. By the spectral theorem we have

x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda de_{\lambda}~~\text{and}~~e_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{M}.

Since
\alpha_{g}
is normal, for all
g\in G
,

\alpha_{g}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda d\alpha_{g}(e_{\lambda}).

Hence
\alpha_{g}(x)=x
and the uniqueness of spectral projections implies
e_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{M}^{G}
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{G}
is a von Neumann algebra, we use an argument similar to the proof of Theorem
4.11
to conclude that
x\in\overline{\mathcal{M}^{G}}^{\|\cdot\|_{2}}
and this completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra with f.n trace
\tau
and

G
be a l.c.s.c. amenable group with (left) Haar measure
m
and
(F_{n})
be an admissible Følner sequence. Moreover, we assume that the action is ergodic (i.e.
\mathcal{M}^{G}=\mathbb{C}1
).
Then for every
x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
,

\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)\longrightarrow\tau(x)1\quad\text{almost uniformly (a.u.)}\text{ and in }\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}.

Proof.

By the mean ergodic theorem for amenable group actions,

\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)\xrightarrow{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}}\bar{x}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau),

where
\bar{x}
is the orthogonal projection of
x
onto the invariant subspace

L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)^{G}=\{y\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\alpha_{g}(y)=y,~\text{for all }g\in G\}.

For
h\in G
,

\alpha_{h}(\mathbb{A}_{n}(x))=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{hg}(x)\,dm(g)=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{hF_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\,dm(g).

Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\alpha_{h}(\mathbb{A}_{n}(x))-\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\left(\int_{hF_{n}}-\int_{F_{n}}\right)\alpha_{g}(x)\,dm(g)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\left(\int_{(hF_{n}\setminus F_{n})\cup(F_{n}\cap hF_{n})}-\int_{(F_{n}\setminus hF_{n})\cup(F_{n}\cap hF_{n})}\right)\alpha_{g}(x)\,dm(g)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{2}\frac{m(hF_{n}\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0

Therefore, we have
\alpha_{h}(\bar{x})=\bar{x}
for all
h\in G
.
Thus
\bar{x}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)^{G}
. Ergodicity of
\alpha
(see Lemma
5.2
) implies that
\bar{x}=c(x)1
. Further, since the action is
\tau
-invariant,

\tau(\mathbb{A}_{n}(x))=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\tau(\alpha_{g}(x))\,dm(g)=\tau(x).

Taking limits,
\tau(\bar{x})=\tau(x).

Since
\bar{x}=c(x)1
, it follows that
c(x)=\tau(x),

hence
\bar{x}=\tau(x)\,1.

We also know that the average
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)
converges in a.u. to some
\tilde{x}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Almost uniform convergence implies convergence in measure, and since
L^{2}
-convergence also implies convergence in measure, uniqueness of the limit gives
\tilde{x}=\bar{x}.
Thus,
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)\longrightarrow\tau(x)1,~~\text{ in a.u}.

∎

We begin by recalling the following definition.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a normed space. A sequence
a_{n}:X\to L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
of additive maps is called
bilaterally uniformly equicontinuous in measure (b.u.e.m.)
at
0\in X
if for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\delta>0
there exists
\gamma>0
such that for every
x\in X
with
\|x\|<\gamma
there is
e_{x}\in P(\mathcal{M})
for which

\tau(e_{x}^{\perp})\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{n}\|e_{x}a_{n}(x)e_{x}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta.

Now we first derive the following theorem from Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\lambda>0
, then there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\sup_{n}\left\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)e\right\|\lesssim\lambda~~\text{ and }\tau(1-e)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{\left\|x\right\|_{p}}{\lambda}.

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
, there exists
\gamma>0
such that for every
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
with
\|x\|_{p}<\gamma
, there exists
e_{x}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
for which

\tau\bigl(e_{x}^{\perp}\bigr)\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{n}\bigl\|e_{x}\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)e_{x}\bigr\|_{\infty}\lesssim\delta,~~\text{ for all }\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}.

Proof.

The first part of the theorem follows from standard argument. Indeed, for
p=1
, nothing to show and for
p>1
, let
e=\chi_{[0,\lambda]}(x)
, then it is straightforward to check the following

\sup_{n}\left\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}(x)e\right\|\lesssim\lambda~~\text{ and }\tau(1-e)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{\left\|x\right\|_{p}}{\lambda}.

Now let
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and note that

x
can be written as
x=x_{1}-x_{2}+i(x_{3}-x_{4})
where
x_{j}\geq 0
and
\|x_{j}\|_{p}\leq\|x\|_{p}
for all
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. By the first part of the theorem
there exists
e_{j}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e_{j}\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j})e_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda~~\text{ and }~~\ \tau(1-e_{j})\lesssim\frac{\|x_{j}\|_{p}^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}

Hence we have

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e_{j}\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j})e_{j}\|^{p}\leq\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e_{j}\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j}^{p})e_{j}\|\leq\lambda^{p}\implies\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e_{j}\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j})e_{j}\|\leq\lambda.

Define
e:=\wedge_{j=1}^{4}e_{j}
, then we note that
\tau(1-e)\lesssim\frac{\|x\|_{p}^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}
and
\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j})e\|\leq\lambda
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now consider the following;

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{n}^{(R)}(x_{j},\Gamma):=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x_{j})Re(\phi(\pi(g)u))dm(g)+\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j},\Gamma)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{n}^{(I)}(x_{j},\Gamma):=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x_{j})Im(\phi(\pi(g)u))dm(g)+\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j},\Gamma).

Since
1+Re(\phi(v))\leq 1+\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 2
and
x_{j}\geq 0
, we have
\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}^{(R)}(x_{j},\Gamma)e\|\leq 2\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j})e\|

and this implies
\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}^{(R)}(x_{j},\Gamma)e\|\lesssim\lambda.

Similarly we get
\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}^{(I)}(x_{j},\Gamma)e\|\lesssim\lambda.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j},\Gamma)e\|=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e(\mathbb{A}_{n}^{(R)}(x_{j},\Gamma)+i\mathbb{A}_{n}^{(I)}(x_{j},\Gamma)-\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j})-i\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{j}))e\|\lesssim\lambda.

This proves that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)e\|=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e(\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{1},\Gamma)-\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{2},\Gamma)+i\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{3},\Gamma)-i\mathbb{A}_{n}(x_{4},\Gamma))e\|\lesssim\lambda.

By the first part there exists
C>0,e_{x}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\displaystyle\tau(1-e_{x})\leq C^{p}\frac{\|x\|_{p}^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}~~\text{and}~~\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e_{x}\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)e_{x}\|\lesssim\lambda,~~\text{for all}~\lambda>0.

Given
\epsilon,\delta>0
, we can choose
0<\gamma<\epsilon^{1/p}\frac{\delta}{C}
.
Then for all
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
with
\|x\|_{p}<\gamma
, there exists
e_{x}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
for which we have

\displaystyle\tau(1-e_{x})\leq\epsilon~~\text{and}~~\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e_{x}\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)e_{x}\|\lesssim\delta.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Note that the projection
e_{x}
in the proof of Theorem
5.5
does not depend on
\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}
. Consequently
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)
is b.u.e.m at
0
.

Analogous to the classical case, the following Banach principle provides a connection between maximal inequalities and bilateral almost uniform
(b.a.u.) convergence, and the proof can be found in
[
Lit14
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
, then the following set

\mathcal{C}=\{x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)\text{ converges in b.a.u uniformly in }\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}\}

is a closed set in
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Thus, to prove the b.a.u convergence of the sequence
(\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma))
for
x\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},x)
for
1\leq p<\infty
uniformly for all
\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}
, it remains to verify such convergence on a dense set of
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

The van der Corput is often used to establish the convergence of various ergodic averages, both in classical and noncommutative settings.
Here we state and prove a noncommutative version of this lemma for a bounded function
f:G\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
, where
G
is an amenable group and
\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann algebra.

Lemma 5.8

(A van der Corput inequality for amenable groups)
.

Let
G
be a l.c.s.c. amenable group equipped with a right Haar measure
m
, and let
(F_{n})
be a right Følner sequence in
G
and
\mathcal{M}
be von Neumann algebra. Let
f:G\to\mathcal{M}
be a bounded measurable function. Then for every
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following estimate holds:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}\left\|\left(\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)^{*}~f(gh_{1}^{-1}h_{2})dm(g)\right)\right\|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

(5.2)

\displaystyle~~~+3\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}+\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\left(\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

We fix a
h\in F_{k}
. We first estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(gh)\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}h}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)~dm(g)+\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}h}f(g)~dm(g)-\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)~dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}h\cap F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)+\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}h\setminus F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|+\|f\|_{\infty}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|+\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\|f\|_{\infty}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}.

Thus, we obtain

(5.3)

\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(gh)\,dm(g)\right\|\leq\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|+\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\|f\|_{\infty}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}.

Next, we compare the average of
f
with its averaged translates:

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)-\frac{1}{m(F_{n})m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh)\,dm(h)\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh)\,dm(h)\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}+\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh)\,dm(h)\,dm(g)\right\|.

Therefore, by squaring both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh)\,dm(h)\,dm(g)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\|f\|_{\infty}\left(\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\right)\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh)\,dm(h)\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\left(\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{n}}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh)\,dm(h)\,dm(g)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{\infty}\left(\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\left(\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\right)^{2}.

Now we observe that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\frac{1}{m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh)\,dm(h)\,dm(g)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\left|\frac{1}{m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh)\,dm(h)\right|^{2}dm(g)\right\|~~\text{ by Jensen Inequality}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}f(gh_{1})^{*}f(gh_{2})~dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})\,dm(g)\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}\left\|\left(\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(gh_{1})^{*}~f(gh_{2})dm(g)\right)\right\|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}\left\|\left(\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}h_{1}}f(g)^{*}~f(gh_{1}^{-1}h_{2})dm(g)\right)\right\|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}\left\|\left(\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)^{*}~f(gh_{1}^{-1}h_{2})dm(g)\right)\right\|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})+\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}.

Combining these estimates, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)\,dm(g)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}\left\|\left(\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)^{*}~f(gh_{1}^{-1}h_{2})dm(g)\right)\right\|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle~~~+3\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}+\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}\left(\sup_{h\in F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{n}h\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}\right)^{2}.

∎

Remark 5.9
.

The above Lemma can also be proved for left amenable groups using left Følner sequences. However, in our main theorem, we assume that
G
is unimodular. Therefore, this difference will not cause any problem.

We are now in a position to identify a dense subset on which the averages
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)

converge a.u., uniformly over all
\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}
. More precisely, we shall show that for every

x\in(L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau))+(\mathcal{M}\cap L_{wm}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)),

the averages
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)

converge a.u. and uniformly in
\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}
. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.10
.

\alpha_{g}(L^{2}_{wm}(\mathcal{M},\tau))\subset L^{2}_{wm}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, for all
g\in G
.

Proof.

Proof follows from the fact that
L_{c}^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is
\alpha_{g}
-invariant for all
g\in G
.
∎

We refer to
[
EFHN15
, Theorem 15.14]
or
[
Gla03
, Theorem 3.5]
, for the following characterization of the compact part
L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
be as above. Then we have,

\displaystyle L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)=\overline{\text{span}\{\text{ $\cup\mathcal{K}$: $\mathcal{K}$ is a finite-dimensional, $\alpha$-invariant subspace of $L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)$}\}}^{\|.\|_{2}}.

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
x\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, then the following weighted average

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\langle\pi(g)(\xi),\eta\rangle dm(g)

converges in
a.u.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a finite dimensional invariant subspace of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and suppose

\{u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{k}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{K}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{K}
, then we can write
x=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}u_{i}
, for constants
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,k
. Therefore, we have

\alpha_{g}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\alpha_{g}(u_{i}),~\text{ for all }g\in G.

Hence, it is enough to prove the convergence of the average (
5.4
) for
x=u_{i}
. Now
\alpha_{g}(u_{i})=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle\alpha_{g}(u_{i}),u_{j}\rangle u_{j}.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{n}(u_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(u_{i})\langle\pi(g)(\xi),\eta\rangle dm(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle\alpha_{g}(u_{i}),u_{j}\rangle u_{j}\langle\pi(g)(\xi),\eta\rangle dm(g)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\langle\alpha_{g}(u_{i}),u_{j}\rangle\langle\pi(g)(\xi),\eta\rangle dm(g)u_{j}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\langle\alpha_{g}(u_{i})\otimes\pi(g)(\xi),u_{j}\otimes\eta\rangle dm(g)u_{j}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(u_{i})\otimes\pi(g)(\xi)dm(g),u_{j}\otimes\eta\rangle u_{j}.

Now by the mean ergodic theorem, the averages

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(u_{i})\otimes\pi(g)(\xi)dm(g)=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}\otimes\pi(g)(u_{i}\otimes\xi)dm(g)

converge in
\|.\|_{2}
. Hence, the averages

\displaystyle\langle\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(u_{i})\otimes\pi(g)(\xi)dm(g),u_{j}\otimes\eta\rangle

converge for each
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
. For each
j
, there exists a projection
e_{j}
with
\tau(e_{j}^{\perp})<\frac{\epsilon}{k}
and
u_{j}e_{j}\in\mathcal{M}
. Taking
e=\displaystyle\wedge_{j=1}^{k}e_{j}
, we get

\displaystyle\tau(e^{\perp})<\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tau(e_{j}^{\perp})<\epsilon

and
u_{j}e\in\mathcal{M}
for each
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
. Hence
\mathbb{B}_{n}(u_{i})e
converges in
\|.\|_{\infty}
. By the Theorem
5.7
and Lemma
5.11
, we conclude the convergence of
\mathbb{B}_{n}(\cdot)
for all
x\in L^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

∎

Before our next results, we have the following useful remark.

Remark 5.13
.

Note that by Theorem
3.4
for a given admissible Følner sequence
(F_{n})
which satisfies Tempelman condition we can construct a sequence
(F^{\prime}_{n})
with

(5.5)

\displaystyle F_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq F_{n}~~\text{and}~~\frac{m(F_{n}\setminus F_{n}^{\prime})}{m(F_{n})}\to 0~~\text{as}~~n\to\infty

such that for large enough
n\in\mathbb{N},(F_{n}^{\prime})
satisfies Tempelman condition and
F_{n}^{\prime-1}F_{n}^{\prime}
is Følner sequence for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, for
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
\Gamma\in\mathbb{S}
, we note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)=\frac{m(F_{n}^{\prime})}{m(F_{n})}\frac{1}{m(F^{\prime}_{n})}\int_{F^{\prime}_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\phi(\pi(g)u)dm(g)+\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}\setminus F^{\prime}_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\phi(\pi(g)u)dm(g).

Therefore,
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)
converges in a.u. (resp. b.a.u.) iff
\frac{1}{m(F^{\prime}_{n})}\int_{F^{\prime}_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)\phi(\pi(g)u)
converges in a.u. (resp. b.a.u.).

Theorem 5.14
.

Let G be a l.c.s.c. amenable unimodular group with an admissible Følner sequence
(F_{n})
satisfying Tempelman condition and
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra with a f.n trace
\tau
. Suppose
(\mathcal{M},G,\alpha)
is a
\tau
-preserving dynamical system. Let
x\in\mathcal{M}\cap L^{2}_{wm}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
\Gamma=(\pi,u,\phi)\in\mathbb{S}
, then
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)
converges to
0
in a.u.

Proof.

In view of Remark
5.13
without any loss of generality, we may assume that
(F_{n}^{-1}F_{n})
is also a Følner sequence.

As
\mathcal{U}_{d}
is a compact group, the Stone–Weierstrass theorem implies that every continuous function
\phi
on
\mathcal{U}_{d}
can be uniformly approximated by matrix coefficient functions of the form

u\mapsto\langle u\xi,\eta\rangle,~~\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

Therefore, it suffices to prove the convergence of
\mathbb{A}_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
for functions of the form
\phi(u)=\langle u\xi,\eta\rangle.

By Theorem
5.7
, it is enough to establish the convergence of
\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)
for
x\in\mathcal{M}\cap L^{2}_{wm}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}\cap L^{2}_{wm}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and let
L=\{g_{i}:\,i\in\mathbb{N}\}

be a countable dense subset of
G
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Proposition
5.3
, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a projection
e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(e_{i}^{\perp})<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i}}

and

\mathbb{A}_{n}\bigl(x^{*}\alpha_{g_{i}}(x)\bigr)e_{i}\longrightarrow\tau\bigl(x^{*}\alpha_{g_{i}}(x)\bigr)e_{i}

in
\|\cdot\|
.
Define
e:=\bigwedge_{i\in\mathbb{N}}e_{i}.

Then
\tau(e^{\perp})<\varepsilon,

and

\mathbb{A}_{n}\bigl(x^{*}\alpha_{g_{i}}(x)\bigr)e\longrightarrow\tau\bigl(x^{*}\alpha_{g_{i}}(x)\bigr)e

in
\|\cdot\|
and uniformly in for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since the action
\alpha
is continuous and
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in
G
, it follows that

\mathbb{A}_{n}\bigl(x^{*}\alpha_{g}(x)\bigr)e\longrightarrow\tau\bigl(x^{*}\alpha_{g}(x)\bigr)e,\text{ in }\left\|\cdot\right\|\text{ for all }g\in G.

Now consider the function
f:G\to\mathcal{M}\otimes M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
,
defined by

f(g)=\alpha_{g}(x)e\otimes\pi(g)u,

where
u\in\mathcal{U}_{d}
. Clearly,
f
is bounded.
We now compute the first term on the right-hand side of Ineq. (
5.1
) for this function. In this case, we obtain

\displaystyle f(g)^{*}f(gh_{1}^{-1}h_{2})=e\alpha_{g}(x^{*}\alpha_{h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}}(x))e\otimes u^{*}\pi({h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}})u.

Consequently, we note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)^{*}~f(gh_{1}^{-1}h_{2})dm(g)\right)\right\|dm(h_{1})dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}\left\|\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}(e\alpha_{g}(x^{*}\alpha_{h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}}(x))e\otimes u^{*}\pi(h_{1}^{-1}h_{2})u)\right\|dm(h_{1})dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}\left\|e\mathbb{A}_{n}(x^{*}\alpha_{h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}}(x))e\right\|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}|\tau(x^{*}\alpha_{h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}}(x)|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}|\langle\alpha_{h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}|\langle\alpha_{h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{2})\,dm(h_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{{h_{1}}^{-1}F_{k}}|\langle\alpha_{h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{2})\,dm(h_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{G}\chi_{{h_{1}}^{-1}F_{k}}(h_{2})|\langle\alpha_{h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{2})\,dm(h_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{G}\int_{F_{k}}\chi_{{h_{1}}^{-1}F_{k}}(h_{2})|\langle\alpha_{h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{G}\int_{F_{k}}\chi_{F_{k}h_{2}^{-1}}(h_{1})|\langle\alpha_{h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}^{-1}F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{k}\cap F_{k}h_{2}^{-1})}{m(F_{k})}|\langle\alpha_{h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{2})

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{m(F_{k}^{-1}F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}^{-1}F_{k}}\frac{m(F_{k}\cap F_{k}h_{2}^{-1})}{m(F_{k})}|\langle\alpha_{h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{2})

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{m(F_{k}^{-1}F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}^{-1}F_{k}}|\langle\alpha_{h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{2}).

Taking limit as
k\to\infty
we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{m(F_{k})^{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\int_{F_{k}}

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}f(g)^{*}~f(gh_{1}^{-1}h_{2})dm(g)\right)\right\|dm(h_{1})\,dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{C}{m(F_{k}^{-1}F_{k})}\int_{F_{k}^{-1}F_{k}}|\langle\alpha_{h_{2}}(x),x\rangle|dm(h_{2})

\displaystyle=0.

Hence the Lemma
5.8
implies that the following average

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{\prime}_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{m(F_{n})}\int_{F_{n}}\alpha_{g}(x)e\otimes\pi(g)udm(g)

converges to
0
in
\mathcal{M}\otimes M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
.
Consider the linear map
id\otimes w_{\xi,\eta}:\mathcal{M}\otimes M_{d}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{M}
defined as
id\otimes w_{\xi,\eta}(x\otimes u)=\langle u\xi,\eta\rangle x
.
Since
id\otimes w_{\xi,\eta}
is a bounded linear map, by the Bochner integral property,

\displaystyle id\otimes w_{\xi,\eta}(\mathbb{A}^{\prime}_{n}(x))=\mathbb{A}_{n}(x,\Gamma)e

converges to
0
in
\mathcal{M}
. This completes the proof.
∎
