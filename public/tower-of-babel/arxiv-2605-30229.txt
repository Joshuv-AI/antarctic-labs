Anti Mode-Collapse in Mean-Field Transformer via Auxiliary Variables
Masaaki Imaizumi 1,2,3 , Masanori Koyama 1 , Noboru Isobe 2 , Kohei Hayashi 1

Abstract
. We use a mean-field-based transformer model to theoretically investigate how auxiliary variables, such as positional encoding, prevent mode collapse of self-attention mechanisms. The use of mean-field transformers to analyze the properties of self-attention mechanisms has garnered significant attention in recent years due to their ability to comprehensively analyze token interactions. However, analysis of this simple model suggests that mode collapse, where token distributions degenerate to a single point, occurs during long inferences (i.e., many layers), indicating a discrepancy with reality. This study investigates this mean-field transformer model and demonstrates that the introduction of auxiliary variables, such as positional encoding, acts as a counterforce against theoretical mode collapse. Specifically, we show that in the theoretical scheme, the energy-maximizing distribution does not degenerate to a single point; instead, it is characterized by a pushforward of the auxiliary variable distribution, thereby avoiding concentration in the Dirac measure. Our main examples are the positional encoding and the fixed prompt insertion treated as a parallel auxiliary-variable mechanism. Furthermore, we demonstrate that positional encoding and prompt insertion possess universality of representation in the limit, meaning that the limit distribution of inference can exactly represent a wide class of distributions. We also analyze several key properties of positional encoding and metastability, and validate our theoretical results through mathematical experiments.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.

•

A fixed-auxiliary variational framework for anti-collapse.

We formulate self-attention with auxiliary variables (e.g., positional encodings and prompt prefixes) in the

•

2.
Fixed-Auxiliary Variational Framework

2.1.
Preparation: Mean-Field Framework for Analyzing Transformers

We first introduce the framework of mean-field transformers, which arises from a standard self-attention block
VSP+ 17
. For a single-head attention layer with
n
token inputs, one may write an update of the
i
-th token
x_{i}^{\ell}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, at an
\ell
-th layer as

x_{i}^{\ell+1}=\mathrm{LN}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}^{\ell}W_{V}x_{j}^{\ell}\right),

where
\alpha_{ij}^{\ell}\propto\exp(\langle Qx_{i}^{\ell},Kx_{j}^{\ell}\rangle)
,
Q,K,W_{V}
are weight matrices, and
\mathrm{LN}(\cdot)
denotes the (RMS) layer normalization without a scale parameter.

The interacting-particle and mean-field viewpoints developed for Transformer self-attention
GLPR 25
;
BKK+ 25
justify the following idealization: take tied layers and a small residual step, use layer normalization to keep
x_{i}^{\ell}
on the sphere, impose a symmetric query–key structure and absorb the value map into coordinates, i.e.,
Q=K=W_{V}=I
, and replace the softmax denominator by a fixed normalization or time rescaling.
In the resulting continuous-depth limit, the token states follow an interacting system of the form named the
unnormalized self-attention
(USA) model for the
i
-th token
x_{i}
:

\dot{x}_{i}=P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp(x_{j}^{\top}x_{i})x_{j}\right),\qquad x_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

Here,
P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}
denotes the projection onto a tangent space of the sphere.
This is exactly the unnormalized self-attention flow used below.

2.2.
USA-AV Model and its Dynamics

Let
(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}})
be a compact metric space, and let
\rho
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathcal{A}
.
We set
X:=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathcal{A}
, where the content variable is
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and the auxiliary variable is
\xi\in\mathcal{A}
.
Throughout, we consider admissible joint laws preserving the auxiliary marginal
\rho
.
In Section
3
, dedicated to the role of positional encoding, we specialize to
I:=(0,1]
and set
\mathcal{A}=I
.
For Section
4
, dedicated to the role of prefixes, we continue with the idea of fixed marginals and consider a standard Borel prompt space
\mathcal{Z}
with Borel probability law
\eta
.

The point of this section is to record only the minimal dynamical and variational structure needed for the main results. In particular, the interaction kernel is reused along depth/time, so the model should be read as a shared-weight or looped-block surrogate
FDRL 24
;
XS 24
;
ACLY 25
;
Che 26
rather than as a full untied-depth transformer.
When a metric on
X
is needed, we use
d_{X}\bigl((x,\xi),(y,\zeta)\bigr):=\|x-y\|+d_{\mathcal{A}}(\xi,\zeta)
.

We first specify the class of kernels used throughout the fixed-auxiliary framework.

Definition 1

(Regular kernel)
.

Let
X:=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathcal{A}
. A function
h:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
regular kernel
if it satisfies:

(H1)

Boundedness and symmetry:

\sup|h|<\infty
, and
h(z,z^{\prime})=h(z^{\prime},z)
.

(H2)

Continuity:
the map
((x,\xi),(y,\zeta))\mapsto h((x,\xi),(y,\zeta))
is continuous on
X\times X
.

(H3)

Regularity of
\nabla_{x}h
:

\nabla_{x}h
is continuous in
(x,y)
and satisfies that there exist
L_{x},M_{h}<\infty
such that
\bigl\|\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),(y,\zeta))-\nabla_{x}h((x^{\prime},\xi),(y,\zeta))\bigr\|\leq L_{x}\|x-x^{\prime}\|
and
\|\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),(y,\zeta))\|\leq M_{h}
hold for all
(x,x^{\prime},y,\xi,\zeta)
.

Condition
H3
is the main dynamical assumption: it guarantees that, after averaging over the other particles, the force field remains uniformly Lipschitz in the content variable. Throughout the finite-particle system the auxiliary labels are fixed and only the content variables evolve.

Definition 2

(USA-AV model: finite-particle ODE)
.

Fix auxiliary labels
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
. For
i=1,\dots,n
,

\dot{x}_{i}(\tau)=P_{x_{i}(\tau)}^{\perp}\Bigl[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nabla_{x}h\bigl((x_{i}(\tau),\xi_{i}),(x_{j}(\tau),\xi_{j})\bigr)\Bigr],\qquad x_{i}(0)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

(1)

We write the initial condition as
x_{i}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.

The auxiliary-blind USA model is recovered by choosing a kernel that ignores the auxiliary labels altogether.

Example 1

(Original USA
GLPR 25
)
.

The standard unnormalized self-attention (USA) model corresponds to the content kernel

h\bigl((x,\xi),(y,\zeta)\bigr)=\beta^{-1}e^{\beta\langle x,y\rangle}

. Then the auxiliary labels are passive and (
1
) reduces to the usual USA flow. In the positional specialization this is precisely the familiar position-free baseline.

Two positional specializations, obtained by taking
I:=(0,1]
and
(\mathcal{A},\rho)=(I,\mathrm{d}s)
, will be used repeatedly later.

Example 2

(Distance-bias type)
.

Relative-position and distance-bias mechanisms were introduced into transformer self-attention by
SUV 18
and later simplified in linear-bias form by ALiBi
PSL 21
. Given a bounded continuous symmetric positional factor
b_{p}(s,t)
, we consider

h\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr)=b_{p}(s,t)\beta^{-1}\exp\bigl(\beta\langle x,y\rangle\bigr).

Example 3

(RoPE type)
.

Rotary positional embeddings were introduced in RoFormer
SLP+ 21
. Under rotational positional encoding (RoPE), the kernel takes the form

h\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr)=\beta^{-1}\exp\bigl(\beta\langle x,R_{\theta(t)-\theta(s)}y\rangle\bigr),

with
\theta(s)=\omega s
. Here
R_{\theta}
is the orthogonal transformation that rotates by angle
\theta
on a fixed two-dimensional subspace
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and acts as the identity on
E^{\perp}
.

Basic ODE well-posedness and the empirical-law weak formulation are standard under Definition
1
; for completeness they are recorded as Propositions
2
and
3
in Appendix
D.1
.

The empirical law of the particle system is
\mu^{(n)}_{\tau}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{(x_{i}(\tau),\xi_{i})}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. When the empirical auxiliary law
\rho_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\xi_{i}}
converges to
\rho
, the natural fixed-marginal mean-field class is
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X):\mu(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}\xi)=\mu^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}x)\rho(\mathrm{d}\xi)\}
. Since
X
is a standard Borel space, every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
admits the disintegration
\mu(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}\xi)=\mu^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}x)\rho(\mathrm{d}\xi)
. This fixed-marginal decomposition is the basic structural constraint throughout the paper.

Definition 3

(Mean-field equation for USA-AV)
.

A time-dependent measure
\mu_{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
is called a mean-field solution if it satisfies
nonlocal continuity equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\mu_{\tau}+\diver_{x}\bigl(\mu_{\tau}V[\mu_{\tau}]\bigr)=0\quad\text{on }X,

(2)

where
V[\mu_{\tau}](x,\xi):=P_{x}^{\perp}\bigl[\int_{X}\nabla_{x}h\bigl((x,\xi),(y,\zeta)\bigr)d\mu_{\tau}(y,\zeta)\bigr]
and
where no divergence is taken in the auxiliary variable.

Because the vector field acts only on the content coordinate, the auxiliary label is frozen, while the conditional content law at each label is transported on the sphere by the mean attention force generated by all other conditional laws.
In particular, any solution starting in
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
remains in
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
for all times.

Under strengthened Lipschitz hypotheses on
\nabla_{x}h
in both arguments, stated in Appendix
D.1
, the mean-field equation is globally well posed and satisfies a standard Dobrushin-type stability estimate. In particular, empirical laws converge to the mean-field solution when the initial data converge in
W_{1}
; see Proposition
4
.

2.3.
Energies under a fixed auxiliary marginal

To extend the variational arguments as in
GLPR 23
to our framework with
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
, we need only to establish the corresponding mean-field energy and Lyapunov monotonicity.
We provide the explicit form of the corresponding energy and monotonicity claims for the discretely supported
\mu_{\tau}^{(n)}
in the Appendix
E.1
.
In this section we provide two general results to be used later.

Definition 4

(Mean-field kernel energy)
.

For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
, we define the energy as

\mathcal{E}_{h}[\mu]:=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{X\times X}h(z,z^{\prime})d\mu(z)d\mu(z^{\prime}).

When
\mu(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}\xi)=\mu^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}x)\rho(\mathrm{d}\xi)
, this functional simply averages pairwise interactions over the auxiliary labels and the conditional content laws. The point is that the mean-field equation in Definition
3
is an ascent flow for this functional.

Proposition 1

(Monotonicity of the mean-field energy)
.

Along solutions of (
2
),
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\mathcal{E}_{h}[\mu_{\tau}]=\int_{X}\|P_{x}^{\perp}F_{\tau}(x,\xi)\|^{2}d\mu_{\tau}(x,\xi)\geq 0
, where
F_{\tau}(x,\xi):=\int_{X}\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),(y,\zeta))d\mu_{\tau}(y,\zeta)
.

3.
Anti-Collapse via Positional Encoding

We now specialize the general USA-AV framework to positional variables. Thus throughout this section
I:=(0,1]
,
X:=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times I
, and
\mathcal{P}_{I}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X):\mu(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}x)=\mathrm{d}s\mu^{s}(\mathrm{d}x)\}
. For periodic positional kernels we identify
I
with the torus
\mathbb{T}
by gluing the endpoints. In this section, we discuss two points: (i) RoPE kernels admit exact maximizing laws whose conditional slices remain Dirac while the marginalized content law is genuinely non-Dirac, and (ii) once a rotation orbit is fixed, the marginalized maximizing law represents measures along that orbit.

3.1.
RoPE constructions and orbit-wise exact realization

We consider a special case of the RoPE-type embedding in Example
3
: fix
\beta>0
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, and consider
h_{R}\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr):=\frac{1}{\beta}\exp\bigl(\beta\langle x,R_{\omega(t-s)}y\rangle\bigr)
; write
\mathcal{E}_{R}:=\mathcal{E}_{h_{R}}
.
For a measurable path
x:I\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we write
\Gamma[x]:=(s\mapsto(s,x(s)))_{\#}ds
for its graph law.

Theorem 1

(RoPE anti-collapse)
.

For any
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we define

\displaystyle x^{\ast}(s):=R_{-\omega s}u,\mbox{~~and~~}\mu^{\ast}:=\Gamma[x^{\ast}].

(3)

Then,
\mathcal{E}_{R}[\mu]\leq\mathcal{E}_{R}[\mu^{\ast}]=e^{\beta}/(2\beta)
holds for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{I}
,
hence
\mu^{\ast}
is a global maximizer on
\mathcal{P}_{I}
. Its marginalized content law is written as
\bar{\mu}^{\ast}=(x^{\ast})_{\#}ds
.

Thus RoPE already gives an exact maximizing configuration whose output law is spread along an orbit rather than collapsing to a single point. The choice of
u
is arbitrary, and changing the component of
u
orthogonal to the active rotation plane changes the orbit without changing the maximal value. The next theorem shows that the orbit law need not be uniform: every probability law on the chosen orbit can be realized exactly.

Corollary 1

(Maximizer with RoPE)
.

In the setting of Theorem
1
, assume moreover that
d=3
and
\omega=2\pi m
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
Choose a vector
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
whose polar angle is
\theta\in[0,\pi]
. Then
x^{\ast}(s)=R_{-\omega s}u
is still a maximizer, and
\bar{\mu}^{\ast}
becomes the uniform measure on the small circle at latitude
\theta
.

More generally, fix a two-dimensional subspace
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for each
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
let
R_{\theta}\in O(d)
denote the orthogonal transformation that rotates by angle
\theta
on
E
and acts as the identity on
E^{\perp}
. Given a measurable antisymmetric phase kernel
g:I\times I\to\mathbb{R}
, set
h_{g}((x,s),(y,t)):=\frac{1}{\beta}\exp(\beta\langle x,R_{g(s,t)}y\rangle)
with
g(t,s)=-g(s,t)
, and write
\mathcal{E}_{g}:=\mathcal{E}_{h_{g}}
. Our goal is to realize prescribed orbit-type maximizers
x^{\psi}(s):=R_{\psi(s)}u
for a fixed
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and arbitrary measurable phase fields
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 2

(Orbit-wise exact realization and uniqueness)
.

Fix
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and let
\mathcal{O}_{u}:=\{R_{\theta}u:\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
be its rotation orbit. Then the following hold.

(i)

Construction.
Given a measurable function
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
, set
g(s,t):=-\bigl(\psi(t)-\psi(s)\bigr)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2\pi)
and
x^{\psi}(s):=R_{\psi(s)}u
, so that the antisymmetry condition holds. Then,

\mu^{\ast}:=\Gamma[x^{\psi}]

(4)

satisfies
\mathcal{E}_{g}[\mu]\leq\mathcal{E}_{g}[\mu^{\ast}]=({2\beta})^{-1}e^{\beta}
for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{I}
. Hence
\mu^{\ast}
is a global maximizer and saturates the upper bound.

(ii)

Exact realization.
For every
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O}_{u})
, there exists a measurable
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that the corresponding maximizer (
4
) satisfies
\bar{\mu}^{\ast}:=(\pi_{x})_{\#}\mu^{\ast}=(x^{\psi})_{\#}ds=\nu
.

(iii)

Uniqueness.
If, for
\psi:I\to\mathbb{R}
, a maximizer
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{I}
satisfies
\mathcal{E}_{g}[\mu]=({2\beta})^{-1}e^{\beta}
, then there exists a measurable map
s\mapsto x(s)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that
\mu=\Gamma[x]
and
x(t)=R_{\psi(t)-\psi(s)}x(s)
a.e. in
(s,t)
. Hence, there exists
u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that
x(s)=R_{\psi(s)}u^{\prime}
holds for a.e.
s
.

Part (ii) precisely describes the phrase
orbit-wise universality
: once the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{u}
is fixed, every probability law on that orbit can occur exactly as a maximizing marginalized output law. Positional encoding therefore does not merely exhibit one noncollapsed maximizer.

3.2.
Energy maximization with conditional Dirac measures

We present the principle that explains the results obtained above, namely the result of energy maximization using the conditional Dirac distribution.
In the auxiliary-variable setting of Section
2
, once the auxiliary marginal
\rho
is fixed and nonatomic, the diagonal in
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}
is negligible and the energy is affine in each conditional slice
\mu^{\xi}
.
Maximizers can therefore be Diracized auxiliary value by auxiliary value, and the problem reduces to maximizing over graph laws.

Theorem 3

(Conditional Dirac measure)
.

Under Conditions
H1
–
H2
, assume in addition that
\rho
is nonatomic. Then
\mathcal{E}_{h}[\cdot]
attains its maximum on
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
. Moreover, there exists a measurable map
x^{\ast}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

\mu^{\ast}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}\xi)=\delta_{x^{\ast}(\xi)}(\mathrm{d}x)\rho(\mathrm{d}\xi)

is a maximizer. In addition, for the potential
\Phi^{\ast}_{\xi}(x):=\int_{\mathcal{A}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}h\bigl((x,\xi),(y,\zeta)\bigr)d\mu^{\ast\zeta}(y)\rho(\mathrm{d}\zeta)
, one has
x^{\ast}(\xi)\in\arg\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Phi^{\ast}_{\xi}(x)
for
\rho
-a.e.
\xi
.

This theorem does
not
say that the marginalized content law must be a Dirac mass. It only says that, at each fixed auxiliary value
\xi
, one may choose an optimizing conditional law that is Dirac. If the maximizing point
x^{\ast}(\xi)
varies with
\xi
, then the marginalized law
\bar{\mu}(\mathrm{d}x):=\int_{\mathcal{A}}\delta_{x^{\ast}(\xi)}(\mathrm{d}x)\rho(\mathrm{d}\xi)
can be continuous or otherwise nondegenerate.

Specializing Theorem
3
to
(\mathcal{A},\rho)=(I,\mathrm{d}s)
, the optimization reduces to the pathwise problem

\max_{x:I\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}[x(\cdot)],\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}[x(\cdot)]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}h\bigl((x(s),s),(x(t),t)\bigr)dsdt.

(5)

This gives a short proof outline for Theorems
1
and
2
. In both cases,
h\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr)\leq e^{\beta}/\beta
pointwise, so
\mathcal{E}_{h}[\mu]\leq e^{\beta}/(2\beta)
for every admissible law. The candidate paths are then chosen so that this upper bound is attained for every pair
(s,t)
: for RoPE,
R_{\omega(t-s)}x^{\ast}(t)=x^{\ast}(s)
; for the phase-field construction,
R_{g(s,t)}x^{\psi}(t)=x^{\psi}(s)
. Hence every interaction term is exactly
e^{\beta}/\beta
, so the reduced energy in (
5
) attains the maximal value
e^{\beta}/(2\beta)
, and therefore the original mean-field energy is maximized as well.

The same conditional-Dirac reduction is also the starting point for the separated-kernel analysis below. If
h\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr)=b(s,t)k(x,y)
with
k
positive definite and
\Phi(x):=k(x,\cdot)
the feature map into an RKHS
\mathcal{H}
, then by Theorem
3
any maximizer in the positional setting may be written as
\mu^{\ast}=\Gamma[x^{\ast}]
, and with
m(s):=\Phi(x^{\ast}(s))
one has
\mathcal{E}_{h}(\mu^{\ast})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{I}\int_{I}b(s,t)\langle m(s),m(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}dsdt
and
\|m(s)\|\equiv R
.
Thus the maximization reduces to arranging a unit-vector field against the spectrum and sign pattern of

5
and the supplementary separated-kernel statements in Appendix
G
.

4.
Fixed Prompts as Auxiliary Variables

This section records prefix tokens as a second concrete realization of the same fixed-auxiliary mechanism. The auxiliary variable is now an externally chosen prompt
z\in\mathcal{Z}
with fixed law
\eta
, while only the content variable is optimized. The main point is that the prompt law itself can be transported directly into a maximizing marginalized output law through an orthogonal gauge.

4.1.
Main fixed-prompt realization theorem

Given a fixed prompt distribution
\eta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})
, set
X_{\mathcal{Z}}:=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathcal{Z}
and define the admissible class
\mathcal{P}_{\eta}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X_{\mathcal{Z}}):(\pi_{\mathcal{Z}})_{\#}\mu=\eta\}
. Thus any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta}
admits a disintegration
\mu(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}z)=\mu^{z}(\mathrm{d}x)\eta(\mathrm{d}z)
, and the prompt marginal is frozen exactly as the positional marginal was in Section
2
. For a measurable map
G:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, write
\Gamma_{\eta}[G]:=(z\mapsto(G(z),z))_{\#}\eta
. In this prompt setting we use the distinguished prompt energy notation on
X_{\mathcal{Z}}
, namely
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\rm pr}[\mu]:=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}}\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}h\bigl((x,z),(y,w)\bigr)\mu^{z}(\mathrm{d}x)\mu^{w}(\mathrm{d}y)\eta(\mathrm{d}z)\eta(\mathrm{d}w)
.

Given a measurable map
\Psi:\mathcal{Z}\to O(d)
and
\beta>0
, consider the kernel

h_{\Psi}\bigl((x,z),(y,w)\bigr):=\frac{1}{\beta}\exp\Bigl(\beta\bigl\langle x,\Psi(z)\Psi(w)^{\mathrm{T}}y\bigr\rangle\Bigr).

(6)

We also write
\mathcal{E}_{\Psi}:=\mathcal{E}_{h_{\Psi}}^{\rm pr}
.

The next theorem is the main result of the section. It is the prompt analogue of the phase-field construction from Section
3
, but also a genuine strengthening: the maximizing law is no longer confined to a single one-parameter orbit.

Theorem 4

(Prefix tokens and exact realization)
.

Fix
\eta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})
,
\beta>0
, and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then the following hold.

(i)

Construction.
Given a measurable map
\Psi:\mathcal{Z}\to O(d)
, let
x^{\Psi}(z):=\Psi(z)u
and
\mu^{\ast}:=\Gamma_{\eta}[x^{\Psi}]
. Then, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\eta}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\Psi}[\mu]\leq\mathcal{E}_{\Psi}[\mu^{\ast}]=e^{\beta}/(2\beta)
holds. Therefore
\mu^{\ast}
is a global maximizer on
\mathcal{P}_{\eta}
. Furthermore, its
x
-marginal distribution is given by

\bar{\mu}^{\ast}:=(\pi_{x})_{\#}\mu^{\ast}=(x^{\Psi})_{\#}\eta=\bigl(\Psi(\cdot)u\bigr)_{\#}\eta.

(7)

(ii)

Exact realization.
For every measurable target map
G:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, there exists a measurable map
\Psi_{G}:\mathcal{Z}\to O(d)
such that
\Psi_{G}(z)u=G(z)
for all
z
. Consequently,
\mu^{G}:=\Gamma_{\eta}[G]
is a global maximizer for the kernel
h_{\Psi}
with
\Psi=\Psi_{G}
, and its marginalized content law is exactly
G_{\#}\eta
.

(iii)

Full exact realization.
If
\mathcal{Z}
is a standard Borel space and
\eta
is nonatomic, then for every
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
there exist a measurable target map
G:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and a measurable gauge
\Psi_{G}:\mathcal{Z}\to O(d)
such that the maximizer in (ii) satisfies
(\pi_{x})_{\#}\mu^{G}=G_{\#}\eta=\nu
.

Theorem
4
can be viewed as replacing

s\mapsto R_{-\omega s}
in the RoPE case by the more general prompt-dependent orthogonal transformation

z\mapsto\Psi(z)
. Therefore the fixed reference measure
\eta(\mathrm{d}z)
plays the same role as the reference measure
\rho(\mathrm{d}\xi)
in USA-AV, specialized to
\mathrm{d}s
in the positional sections. The difference is that positional encoding realizes laws on a one-dimensional orbit, whereas prefix tokens allow those on general spaces and hence an arbitrary
\eta
-pushforward law on the sphere.

At the variational level, prefix tokens again reduce to a fixed-marginal maximization problem. The prompt-side Diracization principle is recorded in Appendix
E.5
, and the finite-prompt discrete analogue is recorded in Appendix
F
.

5.
Examples

We give several explicit positional kernels as examples and the maximizing marginalized laws they produce. Throughout this section, we keep the positional setting
I=(0,1]
with uniform reference law
\mathrm{d}s
, and for concreteness we write the orbit formulas in
d=3
.
The first and third examples are consequences of the separated-kernel analysis recorded in Appendix
G
, while the second is the canonical RoPE construction from Theorem
1
.

Example 4

(Symmetric distance-bias kernel)
.

Fix
\lambda_{\rm pos}>0
and consider the explicit distance-bias kernel
h_{\lambda_{\rm pos}}\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr)=e^{-\lambda_{\rm pos}|s-t|}\beta^{-1}e^{\beta\langle x,y\rangle}
. This is a symmetric distance-bias positional weighting: nearby positions interact more strongly, but every interaction remains attractive. Proposition
11
in Appendix implies that every maximizing path is constant a.e. Hence every maximizer has the form
\mu^{\ast}=\Gamma[s\mapsto u]
for some
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and the maximizing marginalized content law is exactly
\bar{\mu}^{\ast}=\delta_{u}
. Thus even a nontrivial concrete positional bias fails to prevent collapse as long as the positional factor stays pointwise nonnegative.

Example 5

(Single-frequency RoPE)
.

Take
d=3
, let the active rotation plane be
E=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
, and choose one full turn across the interval,
\omega=2\pi
. For a polar angle
\theta\in[0,\pi]
, set
u^{\theta}:=(\sin\theta,0,\cos\theta)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then the RoPE kernel
h_{R}\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr)=\beta^{-1}\exp\bigl(\beta\langle x,R_{2\pi(t-s)}y\rangle\bigr)
is maximized by the path
x^{\theta}(s)=R_{-2\pi s}u^{\theta}=\bigl(\sin\theta\cos(2\pi s),-\sin\theta\sin(2\pi s),\cos\theta\bigr)
. Hence the marginalized output law is
\bar{\mu}^{\theta}=(x^{\theta})_{\#}\mathrm{d}s
, namely the uniform measure on the latitude circle
C^{\theta}=\{(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta):\phi\in[0,2\pi)\}
.
When
\theta=\pi/2
, this becomes the uniform law on the equatorial great circle; when
\theta=0
or
\pi
, the orbit degenerates to a Dirac mass.

Example 6

(Cosine Toeplitz kernel)
.

Consider the explicit sign-changing Toeplitz kernel
h_{c}\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr)=\cos(2\pi(t-s))\langle x,y\rangle
. Its Fourier coefficients satisfy
\widehat{c}(\pm 1)=\frac{1}{2}
and
\widehat{c}(m)=0
for
m\neq\pm 1
. Proposition
10
in Appendix therefore yields the maximizing path
x^{c}(s)=\bigl(\cos(2\pi s),\sin(2\pi s),0,\dots,0\bigr)
. Consequently,
\bar{\mu}^{c}=(x^{c})_{\#}\mathrm{d}s
is the uniform measure on the great circle
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
.

These three examples summarize the positional message in concrete terms. Auxiliary variables do not automatically prevent collapse; what matters is the geometry of the coupling. A pointwise nonnegative distance-bias kernel still gives a Dirac maximizer, single-frequency RoPE gives a uniform latitude-circle law, and a cosine Toeplitz kernel gives a uniform great-circle law.

7.
Conclusion and Future Directions

We developed an auxiliary variational framework for the mean-field
transformer model. By optimizing joint laws for tokens and auxiliary variables, we describe its update through conditional Diracization as a structural anti-collapse
mechanism. In particular, we showed that RoPE and prefix tokens prevent mode collapse and yield exact realization of
maximizing laws.
These results suggest that collapse in simplified mean-field transformer models partly reflects an auxiliary-free
model class.

A limitation of this study is its limited treatment of dynamics.
While we provide a brief dynamical metastability analysis in Section
C
, we need a more concrete connection between the anti-mode collapse effect and the inference dynamics of transformers.
Future work should connect this variational picture to training dynamics and characterize which noncollapsed output laws are useful for representation and
downstream computation.

Appendix A
Related Work

A.1.
Shared-weight and looped transformer architectures

A growing body of work studies transformers that reuse the same block across several computation steps. Looped Transformers improve length generalization on algorithmic tasks
FDRL 24
, their expressive power and the role of timestep encoding are analyzed in
XS 24
, recursive latent-space reasoning is studied in
ACLY 25
, and depth-recurrent transformers have recently been proposed for compositional generalization
Che 26
. These papers differ in goals and training setups, but they share the architectural motif most relevant to the present work: repeated application of a shared-weight attention block. Our model should be read as a continuous-depth mean-field surrogate of that regime, not as a theorem about arbitrary untied-depth transformer stacks.

A.2.
Dynamical and mean-field analyses of self-attention

Another line of work interprets repeated self-attention updates as interacting-particle or mean-field dynamics
DGCC 21
;
GLPR 23
;
GLPR 25
;
III 26
. Within this framework, clustering and dynamic metastability have been studied in
GKPR 24
. Our paper belongs primarily to this dynamical stream. The point of departure is that the standard attractive position-free baseline is collapse-prone, and we ask how fixed auxiliary variables alter both the maximizing laws and the long-time picture.

A.3.
Collapse, oversmoothing, and attention sinks

Recent work studies several degeneration phenomena in transformer models. Oversmoothing is not inevitable
DBK 24
, masking and layer normalization can qualitatively change the long-depth behavior
WAW+ 24
, and localization-type collapse has also been analyzed in simplified settings
BHK 24
. Attention sinks arise in standard softmax language models as disproportionate attention to initial or dedicated sink tokens
XTC+ 24
. That phenomenon is conceptually adjacent but mathematically different from the present paper: our collapse statements concern shared-weight attractive mean-field dynamics and Dirac concentration of marginalized content laws, rather than first-token sink behavior in untied-depth softmax language models.

A.4.
Positional encodings, prefix tokens, and kernel viewpoints

Practical transformers use a wide variety of positional mechanisms. After the original absolute encodings of
VSP+ 17
, later work developed relative position representations and segment-level recurrence
SUV 18
;
DYY+ 19
, relative-bias formulations such as T5 and DeBERTa
RSR+ 20
;
HLGC 21
, linear-bias schemes such as ALiBi
PSL 21
, and rotational encodings such as RoPE
SLP+ 21
. For long contexts these are often combined with sparse or local attention patterns, such as Longformer, BigBird, and Reformer
BPC 20
;
ZGD+ 20
;
KKL 20
. On the prompting side, in-context conditioning and prefix/prompt tuning show that prepended tokens can steer model behavior
BMR+ 20
;
LL 21
;
LARC 21
;
LJF+ 21
. Our contribution is not a new practical PE or prompting recipe, but a variational theory explaining how fixed auxiliary marginals can prevent marginalized collapse. The energy viewpoint is also related to kernel theory: kernel mean embeddings give a language for functionals of probability measures
MFSS 17
, indefinite kernels can support richer geometries than positive-definite ones
OMCS 04
, and Toeplitz positional kernels naturally invite Fourier analysis
Rud 62
;
Gra 06
.

Appendix C
Dynamic complement: Metastability under positional kernels

We consider the distance-bias kernel
h\bigl((x,s),(y,t)\bigr)=b(s,t)\frac{1}{\beta}e^{\beta\langle x,y\rangle}
with
b\geq 0
, and assume that the initial configuration is organized into
K\geq 2
well-separated clusters
C_{1},\dots,C_{K}
. More precisely, cluster
C_{p}
starts inside a spherical cap of radius
r_{0}
around a unit vector
\bar{u}_{p}
, and the initial inter-center angles are bounded below by
2\sigma_{0}>0
. Let
\Delta_{p}(\tau):=\max_{i,j\in C_{p}}\arccos\langle x_{i}(\tau),x_{j}(\tau)\rangle
be the diameter of cluster
p
, let
u_{p}(\tau)
be the normalized cluster average, and let
\Theta_{pq}(\tau):=\arccos\langle u_{p}(\tau),u_{q}(\tau)\rangle
be the angle between cluster centers. We denote by
B_{\parallel}
and
B_{\times}
the effective intra- and inter-cluster couplings, and by
w_{p}=|C_{p}|/n
and
W_{pq}
the corresponding coarse-grained weights; the exact definitions are given in Appendix
H
.

Theorem 5

(Metastability for nonnegative separated kernels)
.

Assume the setting above. For sufficiently large
\beta
, the finite-particle positional specialization of USA-AV exhibits two well-separated time scales:

(1)

Fast intra-cluster contraction.

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},C_{1}>0
such that
\Delta_{p}(\tau)\leq c_{1}r_{0}e^{-c_{2}\beta B_{\parallel}\tau}+C_{1}e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}
for every cluster
p
. In particular, each cluster becomes essentially point-like by
T_{f}=O\left((\beta B_{\parallel})^{-1}\log r_{0}^{-1}\right)
.

(2)

Metastable trapping.

If
r\in(0,\sigma_{0}/4)
and
\beta\geq(1-\cos\sigma_{0})^{-1}\log\bigl(8B_{\times}/(B_{\parallel}r)\bigr)
, define

T_{m}:=\inf\{\tau\geq T_{f}:\max_{p}\Delta_{p}(\tau)>2r\ \text{ or }\ \min_{p\neq q}\Theta_{pq}(\tau)<\sigma_{0}\}.

Then for all
\tau\in[T_{f},T_{m}]
one has
\Delta_{p}(\tau)\leq 2r
and
\Theta_{pq}(\tau)\geq\sigma_{0}
for
p\neq q
, while
T_{m}\geq c_{3}(B_{\parallel}/B_{\times})e^{\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}
for some
c_{3}>0
.

(3)

Slow reduced dynamics.

On
[T_{f},T_{m}]
, the cluster centers follow the coarse-grained system

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{p}

\displaystyle=P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}\left[\sum_{q=1}^{K}w_{q}W_{pq}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle}u_{q}\right]+R_{p}(\tau),\|R_{p}(\tau)\|\leq C_{2}\bigl(r+e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}\bigr).

Hence the observed
K
-cluster configuration is accurately described by a reduced
K
-point flow up to exponentially small error.

(4)

First-merger time.

The time until the first cluster merger is of order
e^{\beta\delta_{\rm gap}}
, where
\delta_{\rm gap}
is the initial angular gap of the reduced configuration.

This theorem makes precise the picture that the static maximizer alone misses. Even when the energy landscape eventually favors one-direction aggregation, the dynamics first contracts within each cluster and then remains for an exponentially long time near a reduced multi-cluster configuration. The detailed assumptions, constants, and proof are given in Appendix
H
.

D.3.
Auxiliary well-posedness material

This subsection collects the precise weak-solution formulation behind Definition
3
. The strategy is standard but worth making explicit: we first define weak solutions, then study frozen linearized flows, and finally close the nonlinear problem by a contraction argument on curves of measures. This discussion is summarized in Proposition
5
.

Let the state space be

M:=X=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathcal{A},

where
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
) is the sphere and
(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}})
is the compact metric auxiliary space introduced in Section
2
. Then
M
is compact. We equip it with the distance

d_{X}\bigl((x,\xi),(y,\zeta)\bigr):=\|x-y\|+d_{\mathcal{A}}(\xi,\zeta).

Assume that a kernel
h:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is given and is differentiable with respect to the first argument
x
.
We write its derivative as

\nabla_{x}h\bigl((x,\xi),(y,\zeta)\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Using the orthogonal projection
P_{x}^{\perp}
onto the tangent space of the sphere and the force

F(x,\xi;\mu):=\int_{M}\nabla_{x}h\bigl((x,\xi),(y,\zeta)\bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}\zeta),

we define the velocity field

V[\mu](x,\xi):=P_{x}^{\perp}F(x,\xi;\mu)\in T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(17)

Since the auxiliary component is frozen, the characteristic flow acts only on the sphere coordinate.

D.3.1.
Mean-field (measure) equation and weak solutions

For a curve of probability measures
(\mu_{\tau})_{\tau\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{P}(M)
, we write the USA-AV mean-field equation (continuity equation) as

\partial_{\tau}\mu_{\tau}+\diver_{x}\bigl(\mu_{\tau}V[\mu_{\tau}]\bigr)=0\quad\text{on }M,\qquad\mu_{\tau=0}=\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(M).

(18)

Here
\diver_{x}
denotes the surface divergence with respect to the spherical variable
x
.
Since
V[\mu_{\tau}](\cdot,\xi)\in T_{x}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, this equation is intrinsically well defined.

Definition 5

(Weak solution (measure solution))
.

A continuous curve
\mu_{\cdot}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{P}(M))
is called a weak solution of (
18
) if, for every
\varphi\in C^{1}(M)
, the map
\tau\mapsto\int_{M}\varphi\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tau}
is absolutely continuous and

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\int_{M}\varphi(x,\xi)\mu_{\tau}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}\xi)=\int_{M}\bigl\langle\nabla_{x}\varphi(x,\xi),V[\mu_{\tau}](x,\xi)\bigr\rangle\mu_{\tau}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}\xi)

(19)

holds for a.e.
\tau\in[0,T]
.

D.3.2.
Assumptions (strong regularity: Lipschitz type)

Assumption 1

(Lipschitz-type assumptions)
.

There exist constants
M_{h},L_{x},L_{\mu}<\infty
such that for all

and

(1)

Boundedness

\|\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),(y,\zeta))\|\leq M_{h}

(2)

-Lipschitz continuity

\|\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),(y,\zeta))-\nabla_{x}h((x^{\prime},\xi),(y,\zeta))\|\leq L_{x}\|x-x^{\prime}\|

(3)

\|\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),(y,\zeta))-\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),(y^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}))\|\leq L_{\mu}d_{X}((y,\zeta),(y^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}))

Remark 1
.

Assumption

for any

Taking the infimum over

Lemma 2

(Lipschitz continuity of the tangent projection)

(Dobrushin-type transition inequality for frozen flows)

Under Assumption

Lemma

Fix

Proposition 5

(Global well-posedness via the flow-map method)
.

Under Assumption
1
, for every initial measure
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(M)

\mu_{\cdot}\in C([0,\infty);\mathcal{P}(M))
on
[0,\infty)
.
Moreover, for any two solutions

holds.

Proposition
5
.

Step 1: short-time existence and uniqueness (contraction mapping).

Equip
\mathcal{X}_{T}:=C([0,T];\mathcal{P}(M))
with the metric

\|\gamma-\widetilde{\gamma}\|_{T}:=\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}W_{1}(\gamma_{\tau},\widetilde{\gamma}_{\tau})
.
Since
M
is compact,
\mathcal{P}(M)
is complete under

\mathcal{X}_{T}
.

By Lemma
6
,

\|\mathcal{T}\gamma-\mathcal{T}\widetilde{\gamma}\|_{T}\leq\sup_{\tau\leq T}L_{\mu}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{L_{V}(\tau-r)}\|\gamma-\widetilde{\gamma}\|_{T}\mathrm{d}r=\kappa(T)\|\gamma-\widetilde{\gamma}\|_{T},

If

\mu_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{X}_{T}
of
\mathcal{T}

Proposition

Existence and uniqueness of the mean-field solution under the strengthened hypotheses follow from Proposition

established above in this appendix. We therefore focus on the quantitative stability estimate, using the classical Dobrushin–Gronwall coupling method

;

.

We first record the Lipschitz constants of the velocity field. Let

F_{\mu}(x,\xi):=\int_{X}\nabla_{x}h\bigl((x,\xi),(y,\zeta)\bigr)d\mu(y,\zeta),\qquad V[\mu](x,\xi):=P_{x}^{\perp}F_{\mu}(x,\xi).

By the bound
\|\nabla_{x}h\|\leq M_{h}
from Condition
H3
,
\|F_{\mu}(x,\xi)\|\leq M_{h}
for all
(x,\xi)
and all
\mu
. Hence, using Lemma
2
together with (
3
), we obtain for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and every

\displaystyle\|V[\mu](x,\xi)-V[\mu](x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(P_{x}^{\perp}-P_{x^{\prime}}^{\perp})F_{\mu}(x,\xi)\|+\|P_{x^{\prime}}^{\perp}(F_{\mu}(x,\xi)-F_{\mu}(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}))\|

\displaystyle\leq 2M_{h}\|x-x^{\prime}\|+L_{x}d_{X}\bigl((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq L_{V}d_{X}\bigl((x,\xi),(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\bigr),

Likewise, by (

), for every
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and every coupling
\pi\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
,

\displaystyle\|F_{\mu}(x,\xi)-F_{\nu}(x,\xi)\|

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{X\times X}\bigl\|\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),z)-\nabla_{x}h((x,\xi),z^{\prime})\bigr\|d\pi(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mu}\iint_{X\times X}d_{X}(z,z^{\prime})d\pi(z,z^{\prime}).

Taking the infimum over
\pi
and using
\|P_{x}^{\perp}\|=1
, we get

\sup_{(x,\xi)\in X}\|V[\mu](x,\xi)-V[\nu](x,\xi)\|\leq L_{\mu}W_{1}(\mu,\nu).

Now let
\mu_{\tau}
and
\nu_{\tau}
be two weak solutions of (
2
) with initial data
\mu_{0}
and
\nu_{0}
, respectively. Let
\Phi^{\mu}_{\tau}
and
\Phi^{\nu}_{\tau}
be the associated characteristic flows. For any coupling
\pi_{0}\in\Gamma(\mu_{0},\nu_{0})
, define

\Pi_{\tau}:=(\Phi^{\mu}_{\tau},\Phi^{\nu}_{\tau})_{\#}\pi_{0}\in\Gamma(\mu_{\tau},\nu_{\tau}).

Write
\Phi^{\mu}_{\tau}(z)=(X^{\mu}_{\tau}(z),\Xi(z))
and
\Phi^{\nu}_{\tau}(z^{\prime})=(X^{\nu}_{\tau}(z^{\prime}),\Xi(z^{\prime}))
, noting that the auxiliary component is frozen along the flow. Then, for
\pi_{0}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}d_{X}\bigl(\Phi^{\mu}_{\tau}(z),\Phi^{\nu}_{\tau}(z^{\prime})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\tau}\|X^{\mu}_{\tau}(z)-X^{\nu}_{\tau}(z^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|V[\mu_{\tau}](\Phi^{\mu}_{\tau}(z))-V[\nu_{\tau}](\Phi^{\nu}_{\tau}(z^{\prime}))\|

This is stronger than the stated estimate, since the additional

(36)

The following observables separate the two geometric effects that matter dynamically: fast contraction inside each cluster and slow drift between different clusters.

Definition 9

(Cluster diameter, center, and angular separation)
.

For cluster
p
, define the diameter

\Delta_{p}(\tau):=\max_{i,j\in C_{p}}\arccos\langle x_{i}(\tau),x_{j}(\tau)\rangle,

the normalized center direction

u_{p}(\tau):=\frac{m_{p}(\tau)}{\|m_{p}(\tau)\|},\quad m_{p}(\tau):=\frac{1}{n_{p}}\sum_{i\in C_{p}}x_{i}(\tau),

and the angle between cluster centers

\Theta_{pq}(\tau):=\arccos\langle u_{p}(\tau),u_{q}(\tau)\rangle.

In the sequel we assume
b\geq 0
, i.e. the matrix
\mathbf{B}=(b_{ij})
is entrywise nonnegative. This corresponds to the natural situation where the self-attention weights are not suppressive (negative).

Detailed form of Theorem
5
.

Under (
30
)-(
36
) and the initial condition (
34
), the following hold.

(1)

Rapid aggregation (fast time scale).

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\Delta_{p}(\tau)\leq c_{1}r_{0}e^{-c_{2}\beta B_{\parallel}\tau}+C_{1}e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}\qquad(p=1,\dots,K),

so each cluster contracts exponentially and becomes essentially point-like by time

T_{f}:=O\big((\beta B_{\parallel})^{-1}\log r_{0}^{-1}\big)
.

(2)

Propagation of smallness and trapping (metastable well).

Choose
r\in(0,\tfrac{\sigma_{0}}{4})
sufficiently small, and assume

\beta\geq\beta_{\star}:=\frac{1}{1-\cos\sigma_{0}}\log\Bigl(\frac{8B_{\times}}{B_{\parallel}r}\Bigr).

(37)

Define

T_{m}:=\inf\{\tau\geq T_{f}:\max_{p}\Delta_{p}(\tau)>2r\ \text{ or }\ \min_{p\neq q}\Theta_{pq}(\tau)<\sigma_{0}\}.

Then for all
\tau\in[T_{f},T_{m}]
one has

\Delta_{p}(\tau)\leq 2r,\qquad\Theta_{pq}(\tau)\geq\sigma_{0}\qquad(\forall p\neq q).

Moreover,

T_{m}\geq c_{3}\frac{B_{\parallel}}{B_{\times}}e^{\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}.

(3)

Asymptotic reduction on the slow manifold (coarse-grained
K
-point system).

For
\tau\in[T_{f},T_{m}]
, the center directions satisfy

\dot{u}_{p}=P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}\Bigl[\sum_{q=1}^{K}w_{q}W_{pq}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle}u_{q}\Bigr]+R_{p}(\tau),\qquad\|R_{p}(\tau)\|\leq C_{2}\bigl(r+e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}\bigr).

(38)

Hence
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{K})
follows the projected gradient-ascent flow of a
K
-point system up to an error of order
O(r+e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})})
.

(4)

Stepwise collapse (time scale to the first merger).

For the effective energy of the reduced system,

\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(u):=\frac{1}{2\beta}\sum_{p,q=1}^{K}w_{p}w_{q}W_{pq}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle},

let

\delta_{\rm gap}:=\min_{p\neq q}\bigl(1-\cos\Theta_{pq}(T_{f})\bigr)
.
Then there exist constants
c_{4},c_{5}>0
such that

c_{4}\frac{1}{\Lambda}e^{\beta\delta_{\rm gap}}\leq\tau_{\rm merge}-T_{f}\leq c_{5}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm cross}}e^{\beta\delta_{\rm gap}},

where
\tau_{\rm merge}
is the first merger time,

\Lambda:=\max_{p}\sum_{q}w_{q}W_{pq}
, and

\lambda_{\rm cross}:=\min_{p\neq q}\bigl(w_{q}W_{pq}+w_{p}W_{qp}\bigr)
.
Thus the time until the first merger is of order
e^{\beta\delta_{\rm gap}}
, and is bounded from below proportionally to
T_{m}
.

H.2.
Proof of Theorem
5

Theorem
5
.

The proof consists of four steps. Universal constants
C
may depend on the model parameters, but we make the dependence on
n
and
\beta
explicit when needed.

Step 1: Contraction inside the cap (part (i)).

The first step is to estimate the decay of pairwise angular distances inside a single cluster. This is the microscopic inequality from which the later fast-time contraction follows.

Lemma 9

(Differential inequality for pairwise angular distance)
.

Let
i,j\in C_{p}
and set
\theta_{ij}:=\arccos\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle\in[0,\pi]
. Then

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\bigl(1-\cos\theta_{ij}\bigr)\leq-\frac{2}{n}\sum_{\ell\in C_{p}}b_{i\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}\bigl(1-\cos\theta_{ij}\bigr)+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{\ell\notin C_{p}}b_{i\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}\bigl(1-\langle x_{j},x_{\ell}\rangle\bigr).

The same inequality holds with
i
and
j
interchanged.

Lemma
9
.

Differentiate
\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle
in time, substitute (
32
), and use

P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}v=v-\langle v,x_{i}\rangle x_{i}
together with the monotonicity estimate
\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle\leq 1
. Since
b\geq 0
, the signs of all terms are favorable.
∎

Using the initial closeness (
34
), the estimate
\cos\theta\geq 1-\theta^{2}/2
,

e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}\geq e^{\beta(1-\Delta_{p}(\tau)^{2}/2)}
, and

1-\langle x_{j},x_{\ell}\rangle\leq 2
, we obtain

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\bigl(1-\cos\theta_{ij}\bigr)\leq-2B_{\parallel}e^{\beta(1-\Delta_{p}^{2}/2)}\bigl(1-\cos\theta_{ij}\bigr)+4B_{\times}e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}.

Gronwall’s lemma then yields

1-\cos\theta_{ij}(\tau)\leq\bigl(1-\cos\theta_{ij}(0)\bigr)e^{-2B_{\parallel}e^{\beta(1-\Delta_{p}^{2}/2)}\tau}+\frac{2B_{\times}}{B_{\parallel}}e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}.

Iterating with
\Delta_{p}^{2}\leq Cr_{0}^{2}
gives part (i).

Step 2: Propagation of smallness (part (ii)).

By part (i), one may choose
T_{f}
so that
\Delta_{p}(T_{f})\leq r
. For
\tau\geq T_{f}
, the first term on the right-hand side of Lemma
9
is bounded below by

2B_{\parallel}e^{\beta(1-r^{2}/2)}(1-\cos\theta_{ij})
,
whereas the second term is bounded above by

4B_{\times}e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}
.
Under the choice (
37
), this implies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\bigl(1-\cos\theta_{ij}\bigr)\leq-B_{\parallel}e^{\beta/2}\bigl(1-\cos\theta_{ij}\bigr),

so that the region
\Delta_{p}(\tau)\leq 2r
is invariant. A similar argument yields

\Theta_{pq}(\tau)\geq\sigma_{0}
for
p\neq q
, because the cross terms are exponentially small in
e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}
.

Step 3: Reduction to the coarse-grained
K
-point system (part (iii)).

Once the diameters remain uniformly small, the full
n
-particle system should be close to a reduced
K
-point dynamics for the cluster centers. The next lemma quantifies precisely the error made in passing to this coarse-grained description.

Lemma 10

(Error estimate for the equation of motion of the centers)
.

Let
m_{p}=\frac{1}{n_{p}}\sum_{i\in C_{p}}x_{i}
. Then

\dot{m}_{p}=\frac{1}{n_{p}}\sum_{i\in C_{p}}P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}\Bigl[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{q=1}^{K}\sum_{j\in C_{q}}b_{ij}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle}x_{j}\Bigr].

Under the bounds
\Delta_{p}\leq 2r
and
\Theta_{pq}\geq\sigma_{0}
,

\dot{m}_{p}=P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}\Bigl[\sum_{q=1}^{K}w_{q}W_{pq}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle}u_{q}\Bigr]+r_{p},\qquad\|r_{p}\|\leq C\bigl(r+e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}\bigr).

Lemma
10
.

Use the expansions

P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}=P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}+O(\|x_{i}-u_{p}\|)
,

x_{j}=u_{q}+O(r)
, and

e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle}=e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle}+O(\beta r)
,
and average the resulting errors over each cluster. The double sums in
b_{ij}
reduce to the coarse-grained matrix by definition of (
35
).
∎

Since
m_{p}=\|m_{p}\|u_{p}
and
\|m_{p}\|=1+O(r^{2})
, one has

\dot{u}_{p}=P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}\dot{m}_{p}+O(r\|\dot{m}_{p}\|)
.
Lemma
10
therefore yields (
38
).

Step 4: Exponential estimate for the first merger time (part (iv)).

The reduced energy

\mathcal{E}^{(K)}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\sum_{p,q}w_{p}w_{q}W_{pq}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle}

is itself a gradient-ascent functional. Differentiating the pairwise angle
\Theta_{pq}
for
p\neq q
gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\bigl(1-\cos\Theta_{pq}\bigr)=-\bigl\langle P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}\sum_{r}w_{r}W_{pr}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{r}\rangle}u_{r},u_{q}\bigr\rangle-(p\leftrightarrow q)+O(r+e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}).

Under
\lambda_{\rm cross}>0
and
\sum_{q}w_{q}W_{pq}\leq\Lambda
, geometric estimates on the configuration of the
u_{r}
imply that the dominant terms contributing to merger are of order
e^{\beta}
, whereas the slow directions are only of order

\Lambda e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}
.
Thus the time needed to reach the critical angle at which two clusters merge is of order
e^{\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}
. Incorporating the error terms from part (iii) yields the two-sided estimate in the theorem.
∎

H.3.
Supplement to the metastability analysis

This subsection fills in the differential inequalities used in Section 6. The emphasis is on transparency of the estimates rather than on introducing new conceptual ingredients.
In this appendix, under the USA-AV ODE

\dot{x}_{i}=P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}\Bigl[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle}x_{j}\Bigr],\qquad P_{x}^{\perp}=I-xx^{\top},\quad x_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

we derive the exact time derivative of the two-point inner product
\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle
and fully justify the inequality used in Lemma
9
.
Below we assume that
b_{ij}=b(s_{i},s_{j})\geq 0
is symmetric and write

\theta_{ij}:=\arccos\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle\in[0,\pi]
and

f_{ij}:=1-\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle=1-\cos\theta_{ij}
.

H.3.1.
Exact expansion of the time derivative of inner products

Lemma 11

(Basic identity)
.

For any
i\neq j
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}F_{i},x_{j}\big\rangle+\big\langle x_{i},P_{x_{j}}^{\perp}F_{j}\big\rangle\quad\text{with}\quad F_{i}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}b_{i\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}x_{\ell}

(39)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}b_{i\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}\big\langle x_{\ell},P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}x_{j}\big\rangle+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}b_{j\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{j},x_{\ell}\rangle}\big\langle x_{\ell},P_{x_{j}}^{\perp}x_{i}\big\rangle.

(40)

Consequently,

{\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}f_{ij}=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}b_{i\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}\big\langle x_{\ell},P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}x_{j}\big\rangle-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}b_{j\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{j},x_{\ell}\rangle}\big\langle x_{\ell},P_{x_{j}}^{\perp}x_{i}\big\rangle.\quad}

(41)

Lemma
11
.

Since

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle=\langle\dot{x}_{i},x_{j}\rangle+\langle x_{i},\dot{x}_{j}\rangle
,
substituting
\dot{x}_{i}=P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}F_{i}
and using self-adjointness of
P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}
together with
P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}x_{i}=0
gives the claim.
∎

Remark 2

(Geometric interpretation)
.

The quantity
\langle x_{\ell},P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}x_{j}\rangle=\langle x_{\ell},x_{j}-\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle x_{i}\rangle

is the projection of
x_{\ell}
onto the component of
x_{j}
tangent to the sphere at
x_{i}
.

H.3.2.
Signs of the projection terms and basic estimates

We use the following two estimates.

Lemma 12

(Contraction of the self-terms (
\ell=j
and
\ell=i
))
.

\displaystyle-\big\langle x_{j},P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}x_{j}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\|P_{x_{i}}^{\perp}x_{j}\|^{2}=-\bigl(1-\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle^{2}\bigr)\leq-f_{ij},

(42)

\displaystyle-\big\langle x_{i},P_{x_{j}}^{\perp}x_{i}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\|P_{x_{j}}^{\perp}x_{i}\|^{2}=-\bigl(1-\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle^{2}\bigr)\leq-f_{ij}.

(43)

Lemma 13

(Upper bound for general terms)
.

For any unit vectors
a,b,c
,

-\langle c,P_{a}^{\perp}b\rangle=-\langle c,b\rangle+\langle a,c\rangle\langle a,b\rangle\leq 1-\langle b,c\rangle.

Lemma
13
.

Since
\langle a,c\rangle\langle a,b\rangle\leq|\langle a,c\rangle||\langle a,b\rangle|\leq 1
,
we have

-\langle c,b\rangle+\langle a,c\rangle\langle a,b\rangle\leq-\langle c,b\rangle+1=1-\langle b,c\rangle
.
∎

H.3.3.
Derivation of the inequality in Lemma
9

Applying Lemma
12
to the self-terms
\ell=j
and
\ell=i
in (
41
), and Lemma
13
to all remaining terms, gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}f_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2}{n}b_{ij}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle}f_{ij}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell\neq j}b_{i\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}(1-\langle x_{j},x_{\ell}\rangle)+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell\neq i}b_{j\ell}e^{\beta\langle x_{j},x_{\ell}\rangle}(1-\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle).

(44)

Now decompose the sums according to a cluster partition
\{1,\dots,n\}=C_{p}\sqcup\cdots
.
Assuming
i,j\in C_{p}
, one bounds the terms inside
C_{p}
using

1-\langle x_{j},x_{\ell}\rangle\leq 1-\cos\Delta_{p}(\tau)
and
e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}\leq e^{\beta}
,
while for
\ell\notin C_{p}
one uses

1-\langle x_{j},x_{\ell}\rangle\leq 2
and

\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle\leq\cos\sigma_{0}
,
so that
e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{\ell}\rangle}\leq e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}
.
Combining these bounds with

e^{\beta\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle}\geq e^{\beta\cos\Delta_{p}(\tau)}\geq e^{\beta(1-\Delta_{p}(\tau)^{2}/2)}

yields

{\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}f_{ij}\leq-2B_{\parallel}e^{\beta(1-\Delta_{p}^{2}/2)}f_{ij}+Ce^{\beta}\Delta_{p}^{2}+4B_{\times}e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}.\quad}

(45)

This is the rigorous differential inequality used in the main text.

The concise display in the main text is equivalent to the cluster-wise upper bound of (

), once the estimates involving
\Delta_{p}
and
\sigma_{0}
are inserted.

H.4.
Full expansion of the angular-velocity estimate in Step 4

The purpose of this section is to make the slow-merger estimate completely explicit. Starting from the reduced
K
-point dynamics, we isolate the geometric bounds that ultimately determine the exponential merger time scale.
In this appendix we derive the upper and lower bounds for the time derivative of the inter-center angle
\Theta_{pq}
used in Step 4 of the main text, starting from the exact coarse-grained
K
-point system.

H.4.1.
Exact formula for the coarse-grained
K
-point system

By part (iii) of the main text, the centers
u_{p}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
satisfy

\dot{u}_{p}=P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}\Bigl[\sum_{r=1}^{K}w_{r}W_{pr}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{r}\rangle}u_{r}\Bigr]+R_{p},\qquad\|R_{p}\|\leq\varepsilon_{\rm red},

(46)

where
\varepsilon_{\rm red}:=C(r+e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})})
is the error bound.
For the pairwise angle
\Theta_{pq}:=\arccos\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\cos\Theta_{pq}=\langle\dot{u}_{p},u_{q}\rangle+\langle u_{p},\dot{u}_{q}\rangle.

Substituting (
46
) and using self-adjointness of
P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}
gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\cos\Theta_{pq}=\sum_{r=1}^{K}w_{r}W_{pr}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{r}\rangle}\big\langle u_{r},P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}u_{q}\big\rangle+\sum_{r=1}^{K}w_{r}W_{qr}e^{\beta\langle u_{q},u_{r}\rangle}\big\langle u_{r},P_{u_{q}}^{\perp}u_{p}\big\rangle+\langle R_{p},u_{q}\rangle+\langle u_{p},R_{q}\rangle.

(47)

Hence

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\bigl(1-\cos\Theta_{pq}\bigr)=-\sum_{r}w_{r}W_{pr}e^{\beta\langle u_{p},u_{r}\rangle}\big\langle u_{r},P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}u_{q}\big\rangle-\sum_{r}w_{r}W_{qr}e^{\beta\langle u_{q},u_{r}\rangle}\big\langle u_{r},P_{u_{q}}^{\perp}u_{p}\big\rangle+O(\varepsilon_{\rm red}).

(48)

H.4.2.
Application of spherical trigonometry (law of cosines)

For three points
u_{p},u_{q},u_{r}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, the spherical law of cosines gives

\langle u_{r},u_{q}\rangle=\langle u_{r},u_{p}\rangle\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle+\sin\Theta_{rp}\sin\Theta_{pq}\cos\varphi_{r|p,q},

where
\varphi_{r|p,q}
is the angle at the vertex
p
of the geodesic triangle
pqr
.
Therefore

{\quad\big\langle u_{r},P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}u_{q}\big\rangle=\langle u_{r},u_{q}\rangle-\langle u_{r},u_{p}\rangle\langle u_{p},u_{q}\rangle=\sin\Theta_{rp}\sin\Theta_{pq}\cos\varphi_{r|p,q}.\quad}

(49)

Similarly,

\langle u_{r},P_{u_{q}}^{\perp}u_{p}\rangle=\sin\Theta_{rq}\sin\Theta_{pq}\cos\varphi_{r|q,p}
.
Hence

-\sin\Theta_{rp}\sin\Theta_{pq}\leq\big\langle u_{r},P_{u_{p}}^{\perp}u_{q}\big\rangle\leq\sin\Theta_{rp}\sin\Theta_{pq},

(50)

since
\cos\varphi\in[-1,1]
.

H.4.3.
Upper and lower bounds for the angular velocity

Upper bound.

From (
48
) and (
50
),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\bigl(1-\cos\Theta_{pq}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq-w_{q}W_{pq}e^{\beta\cos\Theta_{pq}}(1-\cos^{2}\Theta_{pq})-w_{p}W_{qp}e^{\beta\cos\Theta_{pq}}(1-\cos^{2}\Theta_{pq})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sin\Theta_{pq}\sum_{r\notin\{p,q\}}w_{r}\Bigl(W_{pr}e^{\beta\cos\Theta_{pr}}\sin\Theta_{rp}+W_{qr}e^{\beta\cos\Theta_{rq}}\sin\Theta_{rq}\Bigr)+O(\varepsilon_{\rm red}).

(51)

On time intervals where the initial separation
\Theta_{pr},\Theta_{qr}\geq\sigma_{0}
is maintained, one has

\sin\Theta_{rp},\sin\Theta_{rq}\leq 1
,

e^{\beta\cos\Theta_{pr}},e^{\beta\cos\Theta_{rq}}\leq e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}
,
and
\sum_{r}w_{r}W_{pr}\leq\Lambda
.
Thus

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\bigl(1-\cos\Theta_{pq}\bigr)\leq-\bigl(w_{q}W_{pq}+w_{p}W_{qp}\bigr)e^{\beta\cos\Theta_{pq}}(1-\cos^{2}\Theta_{pq})+C\Lambda e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}\sin\Theta_{pq}+O(\varepsilon_{\rm red}).

(52)

Lower bound.

Using the lower side of (
50
) in the same way gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\bigl(1-\cos\Theta_{pq}\bigr)\geq-\bigl(w_{q}W_{pq}+w_{p}W_{qp}\bigr)e^{\beta\cos\Theta_{pq}}(1-\cos^{2}\Theta_{pq})-C\Lambda e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}\sin\Theta_{pq}+O(\varepsilon_{\rm red}).

(53)

Remark 3

(Dominant term and critical angle)
.

The first term on the right-hand side is of order
e^{\beta}\sin^{2}\Theta_{pq}
for small angles, while the second term is of order
e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}\sin\Theta_{pq}
.
Hence in the regime
\sin\Theta_{pq}\gg e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}
, the first term dominates and the angle decreases rapidly; the two compete only near the critical scale

\sin\Theta_{pq}\approx e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}
.

H.4.4.
Two-sided estimates for the merging time by time integration

For convenience, write
y(\tau):=\sin\Theta_{pq}(\tau)\in(0,1)
.
Rewriting (
52
) and (
53
) using

1-\cos\Theta=\tfrac{1}{2}y^{2}+O(y^{4})
and
1-\cos^{2}\Theta=y^{2}(1-\tfrac{1}{4}y^{2})
yields differential inequalities of the form

\dot{y}\leq-\alpha e^{\beta}y+\gamma e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}+O(\varepsilon_{\rm red}),\qquad\dot{y}\geq-\bar{\alpha}e^{\beta}y-\bar{\gamma}e^{\beta\cos\sigma_{0}}+O(\varepsilon_{\rm red}),

where
\alpha\simeq w_{q}W_{pq}+w_{p}W_{qp}
and
\gamma\simeq C\Lambda
.
By comparison for linear differential inequalities, the time needed to reach the threshold

y_{c}\approx ce^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}

from an initial angle
y(T_{f})=y_{0}
satisfies

\frac{1}{\bar{\alpha}}e^{-\beta}\log\frac{y_{0}}{y_{c}}\lesssim\tau_{\rm merge}-T_{f}\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha}e^{-\beta}\log\frac{y_{0}}{y_{c}}+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}.

Substituting
y_{c}\asymp e^{-\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}
gives the estimate stated in the main text,

c_{4}\frac{1}{\Lambda}e^{\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})}\leq\tau_{\rm merge}-T_{f}\leq c_{5}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm cross}}e^{\beta(1-\cos\sigma_{0})},

using the rough comparison
\lambda_{\rm cross}\lesssim w_{q}W_{pq}+w_{p}W_{qp}\lesssim\Lambda
.
