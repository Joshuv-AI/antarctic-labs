Krall-type orthogonal polynomials and integrable isomonodromic deformations
Luc Haine

Abstract
. Krall-type polynomials are orthogonal polynomials for a Stieltjes’ measure obtained by adding jumps at the boundary of the interval of orthogonality of either the generalized Laguerre polynomials or the Jacobi polynomials. We show that both the recurrence relations and the second order linear differential equations defining these polynomials, are explicitly determined in terms of specific solutions of some integrable systems. When there is only one jump, we are led to integrable cases of the Painlevé III or the Painlevé V equation. In the case of two jumps, first studied by Koornwinder [ 22 ] , we obtain a new integrable system of partial differential equations of Schlesinger type. When the jumps are equal and the starting polynomials are the Gegenbauer polynomials, this system reduces to an integrable case of the Painlevé V equation.

1.
Introduction

Let
w(x)
be the weight function of the generalized Laguerre or the Jacobi orthogonal polynomials. For the generalized Laguerre polynomials, the interval of orthogonality is
[a,b[=[0,\infty[
, and for the Jacobi polynomials we shall pick it to be either
[a,b]=[0,1]
or
[a,b[=[-1,1]
, for convenience. Krall-type polynomials are orthogonal polynomials with respect to the Stieltjes’ measure
d\sigma(x)
with

\sigma(x)=\begin{cases}0,\;\mbox{if}\;x\leq a,\\
\frac{1}{t_{1}}+\int_{a}^{x}w(t)\;dt,\;\mbox{if}\;a<x<b,\\
\frac{1}{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{t_{2}}+\int_{a}^{b}w(t)dt,\;\mbox{if}\;x\geq b,\end{cases}

(1.1)

with
t_{1},t_{2}>0
, with the understanding that eventually
t_{1}
or
t_{2}=\infty
, in which case there is only one jump. Equivalently, Krall-type polynomials are orthogonal for the weight distribution

\sigma^{\prime}(x)=w(x)\Big(H(x-a)-H(x-b)\Big)+\frac{1}{t_{1}}\delta(x-a)+\frac{1}{t_{2}}\delta(x-b),

(1.2)

where
H(x)
denotes the Heaviside function. In the sequel, we shall always normalize orthogonal polynomials
p_{n}(x),n\geq 0
, to be monic, so that they satisfy a three-term recurrence relation

xp_{n}=p_{n+1}+b_{n}p_{n}+a_{n}p_{n-1},\;n\geq 0,

(1.3)

with
p_{-1}=0,p_{0}=1
. As a justification of our terminology, some special cases of Krall-type polynomials appeared first in H.L. Krall work
[
24
,
25
]
, devoted to the problem of finding all orthogonal polynomial sets which are eigenfunctions of a differential operator of arbitrary order, generalizing the classical orthogonal polynomials, for which the order is two. Except for the first non trivial case of order four, which was completely solved in
[
25
]
, the problem remains partially unsolved. We refer the reader to A.M. Krall
[
26
,
27
]
and Littlejohn
[
29
]
, who resurrected the subject in the 1980’s, for more references on the subject, and to Kwon an Lee
[
23
]
for characterizations of Bochner-Krall orthogonal polynomials of Jacobi type. Using an explicit formula for Krall-type polynomials discovered by Koornwinder
[
22
]
, J. Koekoek and R. Koekoek
[
20
,
21
]
have shown that Krall-type polynomials solve in general an ”infinite order version” of H.L. Krall problem, reducing to finite order only for special choices of the classical weights
w(x)
.

In
[
12
]
, we showed that the polynomials discovered by H.L. Krall
[
25
]
can be obtained by the method of Darboux transformations, starting from some instances of the classical orthogonal polynomials. Further work in this direction
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
led to new solutions of H.L. Krall problem as posed in
[
24
]
. Krall-type polynomials, as defined in (
1.1
), were also constructed by Zhedanov
[
39
]
, using Darboux transformations. The present work doesn’t rely on Darboux transformations, instead it constructs explicitly Krall-type polynomials, using a method which originates in a remarkable work by Laguerre
[
28
]
. We refer the reader to
[
5
]
for an historical review of this work. As mentioned by Nuttall
[
36
]
(Section 5.1), G. Chudnovsky observed that, using Laguerre’s method, orthogonal polynomials corresponding to some generalized Jacobi weight with three factors, can be constructed in terms of a specific solution of the Painlevé VI equation, see also
[
2
,
33
]
. In a similar vein, in
[
17
]
we characterized some orthogonal polynomials, arising in the context of the Jacobi polynomial ensemble, in terms of the Painlevé VI equation, using at first sight unrelated tools from the theory of the Toda lattice hierarchy and its master symmetries
[
1
,
3
,
8
]
. As we shall see, these tools are closely tight up with isomonodromic deformations. The central result of this work is that Krall-type polynomials lead to
integrable
cases of the Painlevé equations and more generally to
integrable
isomonodromic deformations. The
integrability
of the equations leads to explicit expressions for Krall-type polynomials. In recent years, several intriguing connections between Painlevé equations and orthogonal polynomials have been unravelled, some in relation with rational solutions, but the
integrable
cases are ruled out in most of these studies, see the recent monograph
[
38
]
for an updated account.

Introducing the Stieltjes’ transform

f(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{d\sigma(s)}{x-s},\;x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[a,b],

(1.4)

our starting point is to observe that Krall-type polynomials satisfy Laguerre’s hypothesis in
[
28
]
, that is there exists polynomials
U(x),V(x),W(x)
such that

Wf^{\prime}=2Vf+U.

Under this condition, the functions
p_{n}(x)
and
\frac{\varepsilon_{n}(x)}{v(x)}
with

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{p_{n}(s)}{x-s}\;d\sigma(s),x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[a,b],

(1.5)

\displaystyle v(x)=e^{\int\frac{2V}{W}dx},

(1.6)

form a basis of solutions of a linear second order differential equation

W\Theta_{n}g_{n}^{\prime\prime}+[(2V+W^{\prime})\Theta_{n}-W\Theta_{n}^{\prime}]g_{n}^{\prime}+K_{n}g_{n}=0,

(1.7)

with
\quad{}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{dx}
, and
\Theta_{n}
and
K_{n}
some polynomials in
x
(depending on
n
and
t_{1},t_{2}
) but of degrees independent of
n
. When
t_{1},t_{2}\to\infty
, Laguerre’s equation reduces to the second order differential equation satisfied by the generalized Laguerre or the Jacobi polynomials, in which case the polynomials
\Theta_{n}
is independent of
n
. It follows from (
1.2
) and (
1.5
) that

\varepsilon_{n}(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{p_{n}(s)}{x-s}w(s)ds+\frac{1}{t_{1}}\frac{p_{n}(a)}{x-a}+\frac{1}{t_{2}}\frac{p_{n}(b)}{x-b},

(1.8)

hence the monodromy in the complex plane of the solutions of (
1.7
) is independent of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. When there is only one free parameter involved i.e.
t_{1}=t
and
t_{2}=\infty
or
t_{1}=t_{2}=t
, this indicates that some Painlevé equations should be lurking around.

Among the six Painlevé equations
P_{I}-P_{VI}
, the only ones which admit a first integral which is an elementary function of
q,\dot{q}
and
t
, with
\dot{\quad}=\frac{d}{dt}
, are the
P_{III}
equation

(P_{III})\;\ddot{q}=\frac{\dot{q}^{2}}{q}-\frac{\dot{q}}{t}+\frac{1}{t}(aq^{2}+b)+cq^{3}+\frac{d}{q},

(1.9)

when
a=c=0
, with first integral

\Big(\frac{t\dot{q}}{q}\Big)^{2}-\frac{2t\dot{q}}{q}+\frac{dt^{2}}{q^{2}}+\frac{2bt}{q}=C,

or when
b=d=0
, with first integral

\Big(\frac{t\dot{q}}{q}\Big)^{2}+\frac{2t\dot{q}}{q}-2atq-ct^{2}q^{2}=C,

(1.10)

and the
P_{V}
equation

(P_{V})\;\ddot{q}=\Big(\frac{1}{2q}+\frac{1}{q-1}\Big)\dot{q}^{2}-\frac{\dot{q}}{t}+\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{t^{2}}\Big(aq+\frac{b}{q}\Big)+\frac{cq}{t}+\frac{dq(q+1)}{q-1},

(1.11)

when
c=d=0
, with first integral

\frac{1}{2q}\Big(\frac{t\dot{q}}{q-1}\Big)^{2}-aq+\frac{b}{q}=C,

(1.12)

with
C
an arbitrary constant. This result was first established by Gromak
[
10
,
11
]
, following work by Lukashevich
[
32
]
where some integrable cases where discovered. A new and different proof was provided by Zoladek and Filipuk
[
40
]
. Precisely, we shall establish the following Theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

The recurrence relation (
1.3
) and the Laguerre differential equation (
1.7
) for

(a) the Krall-Laguerre type polynomials, with weight distribution

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}x^{\alpha}e^{-x}H(x)+\frac{1}{t}\delta(x),\;\alpha>-1,

(1.13)

(b) the Krall-Jacobi type polynomials, with weight distribution

\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+2)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}x^{\beta}(1-x)^{\alpha}\big(H(x)-H(x-1)\big)+\frac{1}{t}\delta(x),\;\alpha,\beta>-1,

(1.14)

(c) the Krall-Gegenbauer type polynomials with weight distribution

\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+3/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\;\Gamma(\alpha+1)}(1-x^{2})^{\alpha}\big(H(x+1)-H(x-1)\big)+\frac{1}{t}\big(\delta(x+1)+\delta(x-1)\big),\;\alpha>-1,

(1.15)

are completely determined by a sequence of solutions
q_{n}(t),n\geq 1
, of integrable cases of the
P_{III}
or the
P_{V}
equation, namely for cases (a), (b) and (c) it is

\begin{cases}(a)\;P_{III}\;\mbox{with}\;a=-\frac{2n+1+\alpha}{(\alpha+1)^{2}},\;b=0,\;c=\frac{1}{(\alpha+1)^{2}},\;d=0,\\
(b)\;P_{V}\;\mbox{with}\;a=\frac{(\alpha+\beta+2n+1)^{2}}{2(\beta+1)^{2}},\;b=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2(\beta+1)^{2}},\;c=0,\;d=0,\\
(c)\;P_{V}\;\mbox{with}\;a=\frac{(2\alpha+2n+1)^{2}}{8(\alpha+1)^{2}},\;b=-\frac{1}{8(\alpha+1)^{2}},\;c=0,\;d=0.\end{cases}

For Koornwinder’s polynomials sudied in
[
22
]
, with weight distribution

\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+2)}{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}\big(H(x+1)-H(x-1)\big)\\
+\frac{1}{t_{1}}\delta(x+1)+\frac{1}{t_{2}}\delta(x-1),\;\alpha,\beta>-1,

(1.16)

one has a similar result, except that now the polynomials
\Theta_{n},K_{n}
in Laguerre’s equations (
1.7
) involve two functions depending on two variables
t_{1},t_{2}
, while in Theorem
1.1
only one function depending of one variable
t
is involved. These two functions satisfy a Schlesinger system of partial differential equations, which we prove to be
integrable
by exhibiting its first integrals and solving it by separation of variables. When
t_{1}=t_{2}=t
and
\alpha=\beta
, the two functions are equal and the system reduces to an integrable case of
P_{V}
, as stated in Theorem
1.1
(c).

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 summarizes some known results on semi-classical orthogonal polynomials, isomonodromic deformations and the master symmetries of the Toda lattice hierachy, that will be needed in the rest of the paper. In Sections 3 and 4, we establish respectively cases (a) and (b) of Theorem

2.
Semi-classical orthogonal OP and Toda master symmetries

In this section we give a brief survey of the theory of semi-classical orthogonal polynomials and its relation with isomonodromic deformations, specializing it to the case of Krall-type polynomials, which concerns us in this paper. We refer the reader to Magnus
[
33
]
and Magnus and Ronveaux
[
34
]
for a review of Laguerre’s work
[
28
]
, as well as for bibliographical references. We normalize the orthogonal polynomials to be monic, which is the normalization chosen by Laguerre, keeping most of his notations. At the end of the section, we remind the reader about the theory of the Toda lattice hierarchy and its master symmetries, which will turn out to be closely related to isomonodromic deformations.

Let
\sigma(x)
be a non-decreasing function with infinitely many points of increase in the finite or infinite interval
[a,b]
, and let the moments

\mu_{k}=\int_{a}^{b}x^{k}\;d\sigma(x),\;k\geq 0,

exist. Let
p_{n}(x),n\geq 0
, be the system of orthogonal polynomials with respect to the Stieltjes’ measure
d\sigma(x)
, normalized to be monic, satisfying the orthogonality relations

\int_{a}^{b}p_{m}(x)p_{n}(x)d\sigma(x)=h_{n}\delta_{mn},

(2.1)

with
\delta_{mn}
the Kronecker symbol. The orthogonal polynomials (normalized to be monic) have a well known expression in terms of the moments

p_{n}(x)=\begin{vmatrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\ldots&\mu_{n}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\ldots&\mu_{n+1}\\
\vdots&\dots&\ldots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\ldots&\mu_{2n-1}\\
1&x&\ldots&x^{n}\end{vmatrix}/\Delta_{n-1},\;n=1,2,\ldots,

(2.2)

with

\Delta_{n-1}=\det(\mu_{i+j-2})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.

The Stieltjes’ transform
f(x)
, defined in (
1.4
), admits in general an asymptotic expansion

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{k}}{x^{k+1}},\;x\to\infty,

(2.3)

which is convergent when the interval of orthogonality is compact. The motivation of Laguerre’s work
[
28
]
, which predates and influenced Stieltjes (see the introduction of
[
37
]
), was to approximate explicitly such an asymptotic expansion by a convergent continuous fraction. The starting point is the identity

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{p_{n}(x)-p_{n}(s)}{x-s}\;d\sigma(s)+\int_{a}^{b}\frac{p_{n}(s)}{x-s}\;d\sigma(s),

\displaystyle=q_{n}(x)+\varepsilon_{n}(x),\;x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[a,b],

(2.4)

which defines
q_{n}(x)
, the so-called numerator polynomials, and the functions
\varepsilon_{n}(x)
. The orthogonal polynomials

p_{n}(x)=x^{n}+c_{n}x^{n-1}+d_{n}x^{n-2}+e_{n}x^{n-3}+\ldots,

(2.5)

satisfy a three-term recursion relation (
1.3
) with

a_{n}=\frac{h_{n}}{h_{n-1}},\;n\geq 1,\;b_{n}=c_{n}-c_{n+1},\;n\geq 0,

(2.6)

h_{n}
as in (
2.1
). The numerator polynomials satisfy the same three-term recursion relation

q_{n+1}(x)=(x-b_{n})q_{n}(x)-a_{n}q_{n-1}(x),\;n\geq 1,

with
q_{0}=0
and
q_{1}=\mu_{0}
. It follows from (
2.4
), using the orthogonality relation (
2.1
), that

\varepsilon_{n}=h_{n}\Big(\frac{1}{x^{n+1}}-\frac{c_{n+1}}{x^{n+2}}+\frac{c_{n+1}c_{n+2}-d_{n+2}}{x^{n+3}}+\\
\frac{c_{n+3}(d_{n+2}-c_{n+1}c_{n+2})+c_{n+1}d_{n+3}-e_{n+3}}{x^{n+4}}+\ldots\Big),\;x\to\infty.

(2.7)

One computes that

\displaystyle c_{n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b_{i},

(2.8)

\displaystyle d_{n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq n-1}b_{i}b_{j},

(2.9)

\displaystyle e_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}\Bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}b_{j}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}b_{j}\Bigg)-\sum_{0\leq i<j<k\leq n-1}b_{i}b_{j}b_{k}.

(2.10)

Formula (
2.2
) is often impracticable for explicit computation. In
[
28
]
, Laguerre introduced a remarkable condition, now called ”semi-classical” (see
[
33
,
34
]
for comprehensive references on the subject), which sometimes leads to an explicit determination of the orthogonal polynomials.

Definition 2.1
.

A family of orthogonal polynomials is called
semi-classical
if there exists polynomials
U(x),V(x),W(x)
such that

Wf^{\prime}=2Vf+U,

(2.11)

with
f
the Stieltjes’ transform (
1.4
). Notice that necessarily
deg\;U\leq\max(deg\;W-2,deg\;V-1)
.

With this definition, as reminded in the introduction, Laguerre
[
28
]
showed that the two functions

p_{n}(x)\;\mbox{and}\;\frac{\varepsilon_{n}(x)}{v(x)},\;x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[a,b],

with
\varepsilon_{n}(x)
defined by (
2.4
) and
v(x)
as in (
1.6
), form a basis of solutions of a linear second order differential equation already displayed in (
1.7
). The polynomials
\Theta_{n}
and
K_{n}
in (
1.7
) are defined as follows

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{W(p_{n}q^{\prime}_{n}-q_{n}p^{\prime}_{n})-2Vq_{n}p_{n}-Up_{n}^{2}}{h_{n}},

(2.12)

\displaystyle K_{n}

\displaystyle=(V-\Omega_{n})^{\prime}\Theta_{n}-(V-\Omega_{n})\Theta_{n}^{\prime}+\frac{(a_{n}\Theta_{n-1}\Theta_{n}+V^{2}-\Omega_{n}^{2})\Theta_{n}}{W},

(2.13)

with
q_{n}
the numerator polynomials as defined in (
2.4
), and

\Omega_{n}=\frac{W(q^{\prime}_{n}p_{n-1}-p^{\prime}_{n}q_{n-1})-V(p_{n}q_{n-1}+q_{n}p_{n-1})-Up_{n}p_{n-1}}{h_{n-1}}.

(2.14)

The fact that
K_{n}
is a polynomial follows from establishing that the polynomial

a_{n}\Theta_{n-1}\Theta_{n}+V^{2}-\Omega_{n}^{2}=W\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Theta_{i},

(2.15)

i.e. it is divisble by
W
. Using (
2.4
) and (
2.11
), one checks that the two polynomials
\Theta_{n}
and
\Omega_{n}
can be written as follows

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{W(\varepsilon_{n}p^{\prime}_{n}-p_{n}\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n})+2V\varepsilon_{n}p_{n}}{h_{n}},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{W(\varepsilon_{n-1}p^{\prime}_{n}-\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}p_{n-1})+V(\varepsilon_{n-1}p_{n}+\varepsilon_{n}p_{n-1})}{h_{n-1}},

(2.17)

which shows that the degree of these polynomials (which are
n
dependent) is bounded by

\deg\Theta_{n}\leq\max(\deg W-2,\deg V-1),\quad\deg\;\Omega_{n}\leq\max(\deg\;W-1,\deg\;V),

hence
K_{n}
is also of bounded degree. Knowing that
\Theta_{n}
and
\Omega_{n}
are polynomials, formulas (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) allow to compute these polynomials in terms of
c_{n},d_{n},e_{n},...
from (
2.5
) and (
2.7
).

Putting

Z=\begin{pmatrix}p_{n}&\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{v}\\
p_{n-1}&\frac{\varepsilon_{n-1}}{v}\end{pmatrix},

(2.18)

one can write Laguerre’s equation (
1.7
) as a linear system of two first order equations

Z^{\prime}=A\;Z,

(2.19)

with

A=\frac{1}{W}\begin{pmatrix}\Omega_{n}-V&-a_{n}\Theta_{n}\\
\Theta_{n-1}&-\Omega_{n}-V\end{pmatrix}.

(2.20)

The matrix form (
2.19
) of (
1.7
) is given in
[
33
]
and doesn’t appear in Laguerre’s work. The matrix (
2.18
) was introduced by Fokas, Its, Kitaev and Deift, to characterize orthogonal polynomials as a solution of a Riemann-Hilbert problem, see
[
4
,
9
]
for complete references.

When the Stieltjes’ measure depends on parameters
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots
, as it is the case for the Krall-type polynomials, one can form the matrices

H_{i}=\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t_{i}}Z^{-1},

(2.21)

which using that

\det Z=\frac{p_{n}\varepsilon_{n-1}-p_{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}}{v}=\frac{h_{n-1}}{v},

h_{n}
defined as in (
2.1
), and denoting for convenience of notation
{}^{.}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}
, are explicitly given by

H_{i}=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\begin{pmatrix}\dot{p}_{n}\varepsilon_{n-1}-p_{n-1}\dot{\varepsilon}_{n}+p_{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}\frac{\dot{v}}{v}&p_{n}\dot{\varepsilon}_{n}-\dot{p}_{n}\varepsilon_{n}-p_{n}\varepsilon_{n}\frac{\dot{v}}{v}\\
\dot{p}_{n-1}\varepsilon_{n-1}-p_{n-1}\dot{\varepsilon}_{n-1}+p_{n-1}\varepsilon_{n-1}\frac{\dot{v}}{v}&p_{n}\dot{\varepsilon}_{n-1}-\dot{p}_{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}-p_{n}\varepsilon_{n-1}\frac{\dot{v}}{v}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.22)

When one is dealing with an isomonodromic deformation, the matrices
H_{i}
are single valued as functions of
x
with only pole singularities. From (
2.19
) and (
2.21
), one computes

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial x\partial t_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t_{i}}=\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial x}Z+H_{i}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial x}Z+H_{i}AZ,

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial A}{\partial t_{i}}Z+A\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t_{i}}=\frac{\partial A}{\partial t_{i}}Z+AH_{i}Z,

whence the equations

\frac{\partial A}{\partial t_{i}}=\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial x}+[H_{i},A],\;i=1,2,\ldots

(2.23)

which are called the
Schlesinger equations
.

For Krall-type polynomials, the matrices
H_{i},i=1,2,
can easily be determined. Indeed, from (
1.8
) with
n=0
, we have

f(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{w(s)}{x-s}ds+\frac{1}{t_{1}(x-a)}+\frac{1}{t_{2}(x-b)},

hence

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t_{1}}=-\frac{1}{t_{1}^{2}(x-a)},\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial t_{2}}=-\frac{1}{t_{2}^{2}(x-b)}.

Also, the polynomials
V
and
W
will turn out to be independent of
t_{1},t_{2}
, thus
v(x)
defined in (
1.6
) is independent of
t_{1},t_{2}
, i.e.

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{1}}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial t_{2}}=0.

Hence, from (
2.4
) and (
2.22
), one easily computes that

H_{1}=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\begin{pmatrix}p_{n-1}\dot{q}_{n}-\dot{p}_{n}q_{n-1}&\dot{p}_{n}q_{n}-p_{n}\dot{q}_{n}\\

(2.32)

T_{2}:\begin{cases}T_{2}(a_{n})=a_{n}(a_{n+1}-a_{n-1}+b_{n}^{2}-b_{n-1}^{2}),\\
T_{2}(b_{n})=a_{n+1}(b_{n+1}+b_{n})-a_{n}(b_{n}+b_{n-1}).\end{cases}

It is known that the Toda lattice hierarchy admits a family of master symmetries

, satisfying the commutation relations

[V_{k},V_{l}]=(l-k)V_{k+l},\quad[V_{k},T_{l}]=(l+1)T_{k+l},

forming a semi-infinite Virasoro algebra, as shown by the first commutation relations. These master symmetries were studied by Damianou
[

and by Adler and van Moerbeke
[

]
, for Jacobi matrices normalized to be symmetric. Defining the matrices

\frac{dp}{dx}=Pp,\;p=(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},\ldots)^{T},\quad\frac{dp^{*}}{dx}=(P^{*})^{T}p^{*},\;p^{*}=\Big(\frac{p_{0}}{h_{0}},\frac{p_{1}}{h_{1}},\frac{p_{2}}{h_{2}},\ldots\Big)^{T},

Faybusovich and Gekhtman
[

found a version adapted to the non symmetric form of the Jacobi matrix (

), namely the master symmetries acting on

are given by

V_{k}(L)=L^{k+1}+\big[(PL^{k+1})_{+}-(L^{k+1}P^{*})_{--},L],\;k=-1,0,1,2,\ldots,

with

denoting the upper and

the stritcly lower part of the corresponding matrices. The first few master symmetries are given by

V_{-1}:\begin{cases}V_{-1}(a_{n})=0,\\
V_{-1}(b_{n})=1,\end{cases}

(2.34)

V_{0}:\begin{cases}V_{0}(a_{n})=2a_{n},\\
V_{0}(b_{n})=b_{n},\end{cases}

(2.35)

V_{1}:\begin{cases}V_{1}(a_{n})&=2a_{n}\big(nb_{n}-(n-2)b_{n-1}\big),\\

(2.36)

V_{2}:\begin{cases}V_{2}(a_{n})=2a_{n}\Big((3-n)(a_{n-1}+b_{n-1}^{2})+a_{n}+n(a_{n+1}+b_{n}^{2})+(b_{n}-b_{n-1})\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b_{i}\Big),\\

\qquad+(5-2n)a_{n}b_{n-1}+2(a_{n+1}-a_{n})\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b_{i}.\end{cases}

(2.37)

The equation (
