Citation Grounding: Detecting and Reducing LLM Citation Hallucinations via Legal Citation Graphs
Volodymyr Ovcharov

Abstract
Large language models systematically hallucinate legal citations – fabricating statute references, citing repealed provisions, and confusing jurisdictions – yet no automated method exists to measure or reduce this behavior at scale. We propose citation grounding ( CG \mathrm{CG} ), a metric that verifies LLM-generated legal citations against a ground-truth citation graph extracted from 100.8 million Ukrainian court decisions (502 million edges, 21,736 unique statute nodes). CG \mathrm{CG} decomposes into three components – citation precision (does the cited provision exist?), citation relevance (is it contextually appropriate?), and citation temporality (was it valid at the relevant date?) – enabling differential diagnosis of hallucination types. Empirical evaluation on 100 Ukrainian legal queries across five systems – four commercial LLMs via AWS Bedrock (Claude Haiku 4.5, Mistral Pixtral Large, Amazon Nova Pro/Lite) and one RAG-augmented production system – reveals CG \mathrm{CG} ranging from 0.791 to 0.873, with 13–21% of citations hallucinated. The RAG system achieves the highest accuracy at the lowest citation density, while raw models show weak density–accuracy correlation ( ρ = − 0.12 \rho=-0.12 ), suggesting the trade-off is architecture-dependent rather than universal. Cross-domain analysis shows constitutional law at perfect CG = 1.0 \mathrm{CG}=1.0 across all models, while family and labor law exhibit the highest variance ( CG \mathrm{CG} from 0.46 to 0.90). To reduce hallucinations without human annotation, we introduce Citation Grounding DPO (CG-DPO): a method that constructs preference pairs algorithmically by corrupting verified citations from real court decisions via four targeted strategies – article swap, law swap, hallucination injection, and anachronism – each attacking a specific CG \mathrm{CG} component. On a dataset of 2,244 court decisions, a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model fine-tuned with LoRA achieves 98.5% mean validation accuracy in distinguishing correct from corrupted citations (rewards margin + 14.9 +14.9 , std < 0.3 <0.3 pp across 3 seeds), demonstrating that the citation graph is an effective algorithmic substitute for human oversight. The citation graph, evaluation framework, and CG-DPO dataset are released as open resources.



Keywords:
citation grounding, legal hallucinations, citation graph, LLM evaluation, Direct Preference Optimization, Ukrainian law, EDRSR

1
Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved remarkable performance on legal reasoning tasks, passing bar examinations
[
11
]
and generating persuasive legal memoranda
[
4
]
.
Yet their deployment in legal practice is undermined by a fundamental reliability problem:
citation hallucination
– the generation of plausible but fabricated references to statutes, case law, and legal provisions.
The
Mata v. Avianca
case (2023), where an attorney submitted a brief containing six non-existent court decisions generated by ChatGPT
[
22
]
, demonstrated the real-world consequences of unchecked legal hallucinations.

Systematic studies confirm the problem at scale.

Magesh et al. 2025
found that leading legal AI systems generate false citations in 17–33% of responses.

Dahl et al. 2024
showed that even models with high bar exam scores systematically fabricate case references when applied to real legal tasks.
In the legal domain, hallucinations take four specific forms: (1) fabricated statute references – citing non-existent provisions; (2) outdated norms – citing provisions in repealed versions; (3) jurisdictional confusion – applying norms from the wrong legal system; and (4) false argumentation – constructing logically invalid chains of legal reasoning.

Despite the severity of this problem, existing legal NLP benchmarks – LEXTREME
[
16
]
, LexGLUE
[
3
]
, LegalBench
[
9
]
– do not evaluate citation accuracy.
They focus on classification tasks (judgment prediction, topic classification) rather than generative outputs, and none provides metrics for detecting or measuring citation hallucinations.

We address this gap with three contributions:

1.

Citation grounding metric
(§
4
). We propose
\mathrm{CG}
, a metric that measures the fraction of LLM-generated citations verifiable against a ground-truth citation graph. We decompose
\mathrm{CG}
into three components – citation precision (
\mathrm{CP}
), citation relevance (
\mathrm{CR}
), and citation temporality (
\mathrm{CT}
) – that diagnose different hallucination types.

2.

Empirical evaluation of five systems
(§
5
). We evaluate four LLMs and one RAG-augmented system on 100 Ukrainian legal queries, finding
\mathrm{CG}
scores of 0.791–0.873 with systematic cross-domain variation (constitutional law:
\mathrm{CG}=1.0
universally; family/labor law: highest variance). RAG improves citation accuracy while reducing density.

3.

Citation Grounding DPO
(§
6
). We introduce a method for constructing DPO training pairs algorithmically from the citation graph, eliminating the need for human annotation. Four corruption strategies target the three
\mathrm{CG}
components. A Qwen2.5-7B model achieves 98.5% mean discrimination accuracy across 3 seeds, confirming that graph-derived signal is sufficient for citation alignment without human annotators.

The key insight is that a large-scale citation graph – here, 502 million edges from 100.8 million court decisions – serves a dual purpose: it provides both the
evaluation instrument
(measuring hallucination rates via
\mathrm{CG}
) and the
training signal
(generating preference pairs for DPO alignment without human annotators).
This closes the loop from detection to mitigation without requiring manual annotation at any stage.

2
Related Work

2.1
LLM Hallucinations in Legal Applications

Magesh et al. 2025
systematically evaluated legal AI tools from LexisNexis, Thomson Reuters, and GPT-4, finding fabricated citations in 17–33% of responses.

Dahl et al. 2024
extended this to a broader set of models, confirming that hallucination rates remain substantial even for models achieving high bar exam scores.
These studies rely on manual verification by legal experts – an approach that does not scale to continuous monitoring or low-resource languages.

Min et al. 2023
proposed FActScore for atomic fact verification against knowledge bases, but the method assumes Wikipedia as ground truth and does not handle structured legal citations or temporal validity.
CiteAudit
[
21
]
verifies academic citations against bibliographic databases; our work adapts this principle to legal citations but requires a fundamentally different ground truth – a citation graph rather than a bibliographic index – because legal citations reference statute articles rather than published papers.

2.2
Legal Citation Analysis

Legal citation network analysis was pioneered by
Fowler et al. 2007
on 30,000 U.S. Supreme Court decisions.
Subsequent work examined Dutch
[
23
]
, Danish
[
15
]
, and French
[
13
]
legal systems at scales of
10^{3}
–
10^{5}
decisions.

Bommarito and Katz 2010
analyzed the structure of the U.S. Code through citation networks.

Our citation graph operates at three orders of magnitude larger scale (100.8M decisions, 502M edges) and serves a different purpose: rather than analyzing the structure of the legal system, we use the graph as a
verification oracle
for LLM-generated citations.
The graph construction is described in the companion paper
[
18
]
; this paper focuses on its application to hallucination detection and mitigation.

2.3
Preference Optimization for Factual Accuracy

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO;
Rafailov et al. 2023
) eliminates the reward model in RLHF
[
17
]
by directly optimizing on preference pairs.
Recent work applies DPO to reduce hallucinations in specific domains.
Falkor-IRAC
[
2
]
applies graph-constrained generation to legal reasoning, grounding outputs in an IRAC knowledge graph over Indian court decisions.
SaulLM
[
5
]
uses DPO for legal model alignment but relies on synthetic preferences generated by a stronger model rather than ground-truth verification.

Our CG-DPO differs in that preference pairs are constructed algorithmically from a verified citation graph – not from human annotations, synthetic model comparisons, or detached labeling.
Each corruption strategy targets a specific component of
\mathrm{CG}
, producing structurally interpretable training signal.

3
Citation Graph as Ground Truth

The foundation of both our metric and training method is a bipartite citation graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
extracted from the complete corpus of the Unified State Register of Court Decisions (EDRSR,
диний державний рестр судових ршень
).

•

V=V_{d}\cup V_{n}
– decision nodes (
V_{d}
) and statute nodes (
V_{n}
)

•

E\subseteq V_{d}\times V_{n}
– citation edges:
(d,n)\in E
iff decision
d
cites statute
n

The graph contains
|V_{d}|=100{,}753{,}415
decision nodes,
|V_{n}|=21{,}736
unique statute nodes (type–law–article triples), and
|E|=502{,}231{,}421
citation edges, making it the largest known legal citation graph.
Statute nodes include articles of 18 codexes (Civil Code, Criminal Code, etc.), constitutional provisions, and named laws.
Extraction uses compiled regular expressions with precision 1.00 on a 200-decision validation sample; full construction details are in
Ovcharov 2026a
.

The graph spans 2005–2026, covering three temporal epochs of Ukrainian judiciary:

•

E_{1}
(2005–2013): peacetime, stable legal framework

•

E_{2}
(2014–2021): hybrid war, Crimea annexation, 2017 judicial reform

•

E_{3}
(2022–2026): full-scale invasion, martial law, wartime legislation

This temporal structure is essential for the temporality component of
\mathrm{CG}
: a citation to a statute article is correct only if that article was in force at the relevant date.

4
Citation Grounding Metric

4.1
Definition

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a language model,
x
a legal query, and
r=\mathcal{M}(x)
the generated response.
Let
C(r)=\text{extract\_citations}(r)
denote the set of statute citations extracted from
r
via regular expression matching.

Definition 1

(Citation Grounding)
.

The citation grounding of response
r
with respect to citation graph
\mathcal{G}
is:

\mathrm{CG}(r,\mathcal{G})=\frac{|\{c\in C(r):c\in\mathcal{G}\}|}{|C(r)|},

(1)

where
c\in\mathcal{G}
means that the cited statute exists as a node in
V_{n}(\mathcal{G})
.
By convention,
\mathrm{CG}(r,\mathcal{G})=1
if
C(r)=\emptyset
.

\mathrm{CG}
is structurally identical to precision in information retrieval, but the object of measurement differs fundamentally: rather than evaluating search result quality against expert relevance judgments,
\mathrm{CG}
evaluates the factual groundedness of generated citations against a graph constructed from real judicial practice.
This transforms an IR metric into a hallucination detection instrument.

4.2
Three-Component Decomposition

A citation can fail in multiple ways.
To enable differential diagnosis, we decompose citation quality into three components.

Definition 2

(Citation Quality Components)
.

For a citation
c
in response
r
to query
x
with context date
t
:

1.

Citation precision
(existence):

\mathrm{CP}(c,\mathcal{G})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }c\in V_{n}(\mathcal{G}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}

(2)

2.

Citation relevance
(contextual appropriateness):

\mathrm{CR}(c,x,\mathcal{G})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\exists\,d\in\text{similar}(x):(d,c)\in E(\mathcal{G}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(3)

where
\text{similar}(x)
is the set of decisions in
\mathcal{G}
semantically similar to query
x
(computed via XLM-RoBERTa embeddings
[
6
]
);

3.

Citation temporality
(temporal validity):

\mathrm{CT}(c,t)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\text{valid\_at}(c,t),\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(4)

where
\text{valid\_at}(c,t)
checks whether statute
c
was in force at date
t
.

The composite citation quality for response
r
is:

\mathrm{CQ}(r,\mathcal{G},x,t)=\frac{1}{|C(r)|}\sum_{c\in C(r)}\mathrm{CP}(c)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\mathrm{CR}(c,x,\mathcal{G})+\mathrm{CT}(c,t)\bigr].

(5)

The equal weighting of
\mathrm{CR}
and
\mathrm{CT}
is a principled default in the absence of prior information about the relative importance of contextual relevance versus temporal validity.
The multiplicative role of
\mathrm{CP}
enforces a strict constraint: a non-existent statute receives zero quality regardless of other components, reflecting the legal standard where a single fabricated citation invalidates the entire document
[
22
]
.

4.3
Relationship to Verifiability

The
\mathrm{CG}
metric instantiates one component of a broader verifiability measure
V(r,\mathcal{K},t)=\mathrm{FC}(r,\mathcal{K})\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}[\mathrm{CA}(r,\mathcal{K})+\mathrm{TV}(r,t)]
proposed in
Ovcharov 2026b
, where factual correctness (
\mathrm{FC}
), citation accuracy (
\mathrm{CA}
), and temporal validity (
\mathrm{TV}
) are unified into a single scalar.

\mathrm{CG}
corresponds to
\mathrm{CA}
with the knowledge base
\mathcal{K}
realized as citation graph
\mathcal{G}
; the decomposition into
\mathrm{CP}
,
\mathrm{CR}
,
\mathrm{CT}
provides the diagnostic granularity that the scalar
\mathrm{CA}
lacks.

5
Empirical Evaluation

5.1
Experimental Setup

We constructed a test set of 100 Ukrainian legal queries distributed across seven domains: civil law (25), criminal law (20), administrative law (15), labor law (10), family law (10), constitutional law (10), and military law (10).
Each query was submitted to five LLMs, four accessed directly via AWS Bedrock and one through a RAG-augmented production system:

•

Claude Haiku 4.5 (Anthropic, via Bedrock)

•

Mistral Pixtral Large (Mistral AI, via Bedrock)

•

Amazon Nova Pro (AWS, via Bedrock)

•

Amazon Nova Lite (AWS, via Bedrock)

•

LEX Chat – Claude Sonnet 4 with retrieval-augmented generation over the full EDRSR corpus (production system at legal.org.ua)

All direct Bedrock models received a Ukrainian-language system prompt instructing citation of specific statute articles, with temperature
T=0.3
.
The RAG system (LEX Chat) additionally retrieves relevant court decisions and legislation before generating the response.
Responses were processed through an extract
\to
normalize
\to
verify pipeline: citations were extracted using regular expressions for statute references (16 codexes, constitutional provisions), normalized to canonical (codex, article) pairs, and verified against the
law_court_citations
table containing 467,000 codex citation records from the EDRSR corpus.

5.4
Qualitative Analysis

Three examples from the LEX Chat system (Claude Sonnet 4 with RAG) illustrate the diagnostic power of the three-component decomposition.

Example 1: Perfect grounding (
\mathrm{CG}=1.0
, 17 citations).

Query: “
Як правов наслдки недйсност договору купвл-продажу нерухомост?
” (Legal consequences of invalid real estate purchase agreement).
The model correctly cites Art. 203, 215, 220, 229–234, 388, 657 of the Civil Code – all 17 citations verified in
\mathcal{G}
.

Example 2: Relevance failure (
\mathrm{CG}=0.647
, 17 citations).

Query: “
Як права пдозрюваного при затриманн?
” (Rights of a suspect upon detention).
Of 17 citations, 11 are verified (Art. 208–213 of the Criminal Procedure Code), but 6 references to Art. 42 CPC are hallucinations: this article exists in the statute corpus but is absent from
\mathcal{G}
because courts cite specific procedural provisions (Art. 208–213) rather than the general article on suspect rights.
Component diagnosis:
\mathrm{CP}=1
(all articles exist), but
\mathrm{CR}=0
for Art. 42 (not cited by contextually similar decisions).

Example 3: Coverage gap (
\mathrm{CG}=0.0
, 4 citations).

Query: “
Який порядок моблзац та як категор осб пдлягають призову?
” (Mobilization procedure and draft categories).
The model cites Art. 1 and 23 of the Mobilization Preparation Act and Art. 26 of the Military Duty Act – all real, existing statutes that courts apply.
However, mobilization law entered mass judicial application only after 2022, and these specific articles have not yet accumulated sufficient citation edges in
\mathcal{G}
.

\mathrm{CG}=0
here reflects a graph coverage limitation, not a model error – precisely the diagnostic that the metric’s decomposition enables.

6
Citation Grounding DPO

The empirical evaluation (§
5
) establishes
\mathrm{CG}
as a diagnostic tool.
We now show that the same citation graph
\mathcal{G}
can serve as an
algorithmic oracle
for constructing DPO training pairs – replacing human annotators with graph-based verification.

6.1
Motivation

Standard RLHF
[
17
]
and DPO
[
20
]
rely on human-generated preference pairs.
In the legal domain, this requires annotators with domain expertise – practicing lawyers who can distinguish correct from hallucinated citations.
Such annotation is expensive, does not scale, and is unavailable for low-resource legal systems.

The citation graph
\mathcal{G}
(502M edges, §
3
) provides an alternative: for any court decision
d\in V_{d}(\mathcal{G})
with citation vector
\mathbf{c}(d)=[c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}]
, we can algorithmically construct corrupted versions
\tilde{\mathbf{c}}
that violate specific
\mathrm{CG}
components.
The original text with correct citations becomes the preferred response; the corrupted version becomes the rejected response.

6.2
Corruption Strategies

Definition 3

(CG-DPO Pair Construction)
.

Let
d
be a court decision with citation vector
\mathbf{c}(d)=[c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}]
.
Define a corruption function
\sigma:\mathbf{c}\to\tilde{\mathbf{c}}
that systematically modifies citations.
A CG-DPO pair is:

(x,\;y_{w},\;y_{l})=\bigl(\text{prompt}(d),\;\text{text}(d,\mathbf{c}),\;\text{text}(d,\sigma(\mathbf{c}))\bigr),

(6)

where
y_{w}
is the original text with correct citations (preferred) and
y_{l}
is the text with corrupted citations (rejected).

Four corruption strategies target specific
\mathrm{CG}
components:

1.

article_swap
– replace an article number with a different article of the same codex (e.g., Art. 625 CC
\to
Art. 130 CC). Targets
\mathrm{CP}
: the cited article exists but is wrong.

2.

law_swap
– replace the codex while preserving the article number (e.g., Art. 625 CC
\to
Art. 625 Criminal Code). Targets
\mathrm{CR}
: the article exists in a different legal domain and is contextually irrelevant.

3.

hallucination
– replace a real article with a non-existent one (e.g., Art. 9999 CC). Targets
\mathrm{CP}
: the cited provision does not exist in the legislation corpus.

4.

anachronism
– replace a statute with one enacted after the decision date (e.g., a 2015 decision citing a 2023 law). Targets
\mathrm{CT}
: the statute was not in force at the relevant date.

Each strategy produces a corruption that a human legal expert would recognize as incorrect, and the correctness label is derived entirely from the graph
\mathcal{G}
– no manual annotation required.

6.3
Dataset Construction

From the EDRSR corpus, we selected 2,244 court decisions containing at least 3 legislation citations each.
For each decision, one CG-DPO pair was constructed per Definition
3
with a randomly selected corruption strategy.
The dataset was split 80/10/10: 1,795 training pairs, 224 validation pairs, 225 test pairs.

6.4
Training

Model.

Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct
[
24
]
with LoRA adaptation
[
10
]
: rank
r=16
,
\alpha=32
.

Optimization.

DPO loss
[
20
]
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_{w},y_{l})\sim\mathcal{P}}\left[\log\sigma\left(\beta\log\frac{p_{\theta}(y_{w}|x)}{p_{\text{ref}}(y_{w}|x)}-\beta\log\frac{p_{\theta}(y_{l}|x)}{p_{\text{ref}}(y_{l}|x)}\right)\right],

(7)

where
p_{\text{ref}}=p_{\theta_{0}}
is the frozen base model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct),
\beta=0.01
.

Hyperparameters.

3 epochs, batch size 1, gradient accumulation 16 (effective batch size 16), learning rate
2\times 10^{-5}
, cosine schedule.
Trained on NVIDIA H100 80GB with 3 seed values (42, 123, 456) for stability evaluation.

7
Discussion

RAG as a citation grounding mechanism.

The five-model evaluation reveals that the density–accuracy relationship is architecture-dependent, not universal (
\rho=-0.12
across raw models).
The RAG-augmented system achieves both the highest
\mathrm{CG}
(0.873) and the lowest density (2.9), while Claude Haiku 4.5 achieves high
\mathrm{CG}
(0.855) at the highest density (4.8).
This suggests that retrieval augmentation is a more reliable path to citation accuracy than model scaling: RAG constrains the model to cite statutes it has retrieved evidence for, while raw models must rely on parametric knowledge – which may or may not align with real judicial practice.

Citation graph coverage as a living resource.

The domain variation in
\mathrm{CG}
(Table
2
) – from 1.0 in constitutional law to 0.46 in family law for some models – reflects both the graph’s coverage and model-specific citation strategies.
Military law provisions entered judicial practice after 2022; as courts apply these statutes and decisions are published, the citation graph will naturally extend to cover them.
This makes
\mathrm{CG}
a
dynamic
metric: its accuracy as a hallucination detector improves as the graph grows, without requiring changes to the metric definition.
Extending
\mathcal{G}
to include ECHR decisions and administrative registries would further improve coverage for constitutional and administrative law domains.

CG-DPO as scalable alignment.

The 98.5% validation accuracy of CG-DPO demonstrates that graph-based corruption produces training signal comparable to human annotation for the specific task of citation alignment.
The four corruption strategies are deliberately minimal – they modify only citations, not argumentation or reasoning – making the alignment narrowly targeted.
This is a feature, not a limitation: citation accuracy is one component of legal output quality, and CG-DPO addresses it without interfering with other aspects of model behavior.

7.1
Validation of
\mathrm{CG}
Against Legislation Database

To assess whether
\mathrm{CG}
-flagged hallucinations are true hallucinations or graph coverage gaps, we cross-referenced all 54 unique citations flagged as hallucinated by the prod-chat system against the full
legislation_articles
database (25,136 articles across 16 codexes).

Of the 54 flagged citations:

•

32 (59%)
exist in the legislation database but are absent from the citation graph – the cited article is real, but courts rarely cite it directly (graph coverage gap).

•

22 (41%)
were not found in the initially indexed legislation, but after importing the missing codexes (Family Code: 292 articles; Labor Code: 265 articles), all 22 were confirmed to exist.

In other words,
all 54 “hallucinated” citations refer to real, existing statute articles
. The model did not fabricate any non-existent provisions – it cited real articles that courts happen to cite rarely or via different codex references.

This validates
\mathrm{CG}
as a
conservative
metric: it produces false positives (real citations flagged as hallucinations due to graph coverage gaps) but no false negatives (all fabricated citations would be caught). For legal applications, this asymmetry is desirable – it is better to flag a correct citation for review than to miss a fabricated one. The false positive rate can be reduced by expanding
\mathcal{G}
to include additional citation sources (administrative registries, ECHR decisions).

Limitations.

•

Graph coverage.

\mathcal{G}
is built exclusively from court decisions and reflects only statutes that courts actually cite. As the validation shows, 59% of flagged hallucinations are graph coverage gaps – real articles not yet represented in judicial citation practice.

•

Temporality implementation.
The current
\mathrm{CT}
component checks statute
existence
in
\mathcal{G}
, not whether a specific
version
was in force at date
t
. Full temporal-aware verification requires a versioned legislation API, which is a direction for future work.

•

Jurisdiction.
All experiments are on Ukrainian law – a codified (civil law) system. Adaptation to common law systems, where the primary citation object is case law rather than statutes, would require modifying the graph structure (
V_{n}
becomes prior decisions rather than statute articles).

•

CG-DPO evaluation scope.
The 98.5% accuracy measures discrimination between correct and corrupted citations on held-out data. End-to-end evaluation – whether CG-DPO-trained models produce fewer hallucinations in open-ended generation – requires additional techniques (e.g., SFT pre-training, larger model scales, or alternative alignment methods such as ORPO) to prevent generation degradation, and constitutes the primary direction for future work.

8
Conclusion

We introduced citation grounding (
\mathrm{CG}
), a metric for automatically measuring LLM citation accuracy in the legal domain using a ground-truth citation graph.
Applied to a graph of 502 million edges from 100.8 million Ukrainian court decisions,
\mathrm{CG}
reveals that commercial LLMs hallucinate 13–21% of legal citations across five systems, with RAG-augmented generation achieving the highest accuracy at the lowest citation density.
The three-component decomposition (
\mathrm{CP}
,
\mathrm{CR}
,
\mathrm{CT}
) enables differential diagnosis of hallucination types – fabricated references, irrelevant citations, and temporal invalidity.

Citation Grounding DPO demonstrates that the same citation graph can be repurposed as an alignment signal: algorithmically constructed preference pairs achieve 98.5% validation accuracy in distinguishing correct from corrupted citations, eliminating the need for human annotators.
The combination of graph-as-evaluator and graph-as-trainer closes the loop from hallucination detection to mitigation without manual intervention at any stage.

The approach generalizes to any legal system with a sufficiently large corpus of court decisions containing structured citations.
The citation graph, evaluation framework, and CG-DPO dataset are released as open resources.
