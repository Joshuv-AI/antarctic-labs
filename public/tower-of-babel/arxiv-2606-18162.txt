1.
Introduction

The operator algebras generated by creation and annihilation operators on Fock-type spaces provide a rich source of examples at the interface of operator algebras and non commutative probability. Classical instances include the free Fock space and its associated free group factors, as well as their deformations arising from generalized commutation relations. In many situations, algebraic modifications of the underlying Fock structure produce new von Neumann algebras together with remarkable probabilistic phenomena reflected in the distributions of the corresponding position operators.

Interacting Fock spaces, introduced by Accardi, Lu and Volovich in connection with stochastic limits in quantum theory
[
5
]
, provide a general framework for studying weighted deformations of the full Fock space. Since their introduction, they have found applications in several areas of non commutative probability. Connections with monotone probability were established in
[
16
]
, while more recent developments relate interacting Fock spaces to subproduct systems
[
13
]
and shift-invariant states
[
10
]
. In the one-dimensional setting, interacting Fock spaces are closely related to orthogonal polynomials through the Jacobi parameters of probability measures
[
1
]
; further developments and generalizations may be found in
[
6
,
3
,
4
]
.
The case when the underlying Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has dimension at least 2 and interacting functions are almost everywhere constant is commonly referred to as the
one-mode type
or
multi-mode
setting
[
2
,
11
]
, in order to distinguish it from the Accardi-Bożejko case.

Roughly speaking, a one-mode type interacting Fock space is obtained from the usual full Fock space by deforming the inner product on the
n
-particle subspace by means of a sequence of positive weights
(\lambda_{n})_{n}
. If the ratio sequence
\omega_{n}:=\lambda_{n}/\lambda_{n-1}
is constant, the construction reduces, up to isomorphism, to the full Fock space. When the underlying Hilbert space is separable infinite dimensional, the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by annihilation operators provides a natural generalization of the Fock representation of the Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}

[
12
]
. In this framework, Xu in
[
24
]
proved that whenever the underlying Hilbert space has dimension at least two, the vacuum state is tracial on the von Neumann algebra generated by position operators
if and only if
(\omega_{n})_{n}
is constant.
Recall that the constant case corresponds to the
q
-commutation
relations
[
9
,
7
]
for
q=0
, or equivalently the free group algebra with
{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}
generators. In this setting, the
resulting von Neumann algebra is well known to be a factor (when
{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}\geq 2
), and this is also true for the
q
-deformation
[
20
]
.

It is therefore natural to investigate what happens beyond the
constant case, where any tracial state, if it exists, cannot coincide
with the vacuum state. The simplest generalization arises when the
sequence
(\omega_{n})_{n}
takes only two distinct values. This was first investigated in
[
21
]
, where the case

\omega_{1}=1
, and
\omega_{n}=t
,
n>1
was considered. Another interesting example can be realized by fixing positive values for both
\omega_{2n}
and
\omega_{2n+1}
. In this case, the
*
-algebra generated by annihilation operators, as well as its self-adjoint part, becomes
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded, and for this reason we refer to it as the
period-2
setting.

Our main goal is to understand how periodicity influences both the structure of the generated von Neumann algebra and the spectral distributions of the position operators. In particular, we show that period-2 interacting Fock spaces provide a natural setting in which free probabilistic structures arise, even though position operators are not free. Moreover, the von Neumann algebra generated by position operators is a factor of type
II_{1}
, with the unique normal tracial state given by a convex combination involving the vacuum.

Our setting deals with period-2 interacting Fock spaces with separable infinite dimensional underlying Hilbert space. Here, we show that the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by annihilation operators is irreducible, and we provide a space-free characterization of it. The unit circle
\mathbb{T}
acts on the universal
C^{*}
-algebra by
*
-automorphisms, and this action is equivariant with respect to the natural action of
\mathbb{T}
on the Fock representation, induced by conjugation with unitaries on the Fock space. As in the Cuntz case, one finds that the Fock representation is, up to isomorphism, the unique faithful covariant
*
-representation of the universal
C^{*}
-algebra (Theorem
4.4
). Since the projection onto the vacuum does not belong to the image of the Fock representation, separability and irreducibility imply that the universal
C^{*}
-algebra is not of type
I
.

For the von Neumann algebra generated by position operators, we first prove that the vacuum vector is cyclic and separating for the even subalgebra with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading, and that this subalgebra is a type
II_{1}
factor with the vacuum state as its unique trace. The existence of a unique tracial state on the odd subalgebra requires more delicate arguments, and marks the most difficult problem in Section
5
, involving two technical lemmas that reflect the different behavior of the kernel of the position operator depending on the order relation between
\omega_{2n}
and
\omega_{2n+1}
. A suitable convex combination of the corresponding marginal normal traces, induced by the commutation relations, yields a unique normal trace on the whole algebra. Consequently, the full von Neumann algebra is a type
II_{1}
factor (Theorem
5.12
).

In the final part of the paper, we study the vacuum distribution of the position operators. Using combinatorial techniques adapted to the interacting Fock space structure in the period-2 case, we show that the distribution of the squared position operator belongs to the Marchenko-Pastur family
[
17
]
, whose parameters depend only on the two periodic weights (Theorem
6.3
). As is well known, this family coincides with the free Poisson laws of free probability
[
19
]
and includes, as a special case, the square of the (standard) Wigner distribution. In our framework, this is consistent with the fact that the vacuum distribution of the position operator reduces to a (not necessarily standard) Wigner law when the weights
(\omega_{n})_{n}
are constant (Proposition
6.5
).

Furthermore, we show that the right endpoint of the support of the vacuum distribution coincides with the norm of each position operator (Corollary
6.4
), even though the vacuum vector is not separating for the entire von Neumann algebra.

We also investigate independence properties of the position operators from the viewpoint of free probability. We prove that freeness admits a sharp characterization in terms of the weight sequence: in the vacuum state, the position operators are free if and only if the weights are constant, while their squares (and hence all even powers) form a free family if and only if the sequence is 2-periodic (Proposition
6.6
). In particular, periodicity recovers free probabilistic structures after passing to even observables. As a consequence, the von Neumann algebra generated by the position operators contains a copy of the free group factor
L(\mathbb{F}_{\infty})
, and one can identify free additive and multiplicative convolution structures in this framework.
These results show that periodicity constitutes a new mechanism through which type
II_{1}
factors and free-probabilistic structures emerge in interacting Fock spaces, extending several phenomena previously known only in the constant-weight case.

Preliminaries

Let
S
be a finite set with cardinality
|S|
. A partition
\pi
of
S
is a collection of mutually disjoint subsets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
, whose union is
S
. The elements of
\pi
are called
blocks
. Throughout the paper we set
S=[m]:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, and we denote by
P(m)
the set of all partitions of
[m]
.

A partition
\pi
is called a
pair partition
if each block has cardinality
2
, that is,
|B_{j}|=2
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
. In this case, the total number of elements must be even, and the set of pair partitions of
[2n]
is denoted by
PP(2n)
. It is convenient to represent each block in this case by its left and right endpoints, writing

B_{j}=(l_{j},r_{j}),\qquad l_{j}<r_{j}\,,

and use the convention that
l_{j}<l_{j+1}
for each
j=1,\ldots,n-1
. Therefore, automatically
l_{1}=1
for each
\pi\in PP(2n)
.

A partition
\pi
is said to have a
crossing
if there exist two distinct blocks
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
and elements
v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{i}
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in B_{j}
such that

v_{1}<w_{1}<v_{2}<w_{2}.

If no such configuration exists, the partition is called
non crossing
. We denote by
NCPP(2n)
the set of non crossing pair partitions of
[2n]
. Thus, for
\pi=\{(l_{h},r_{h})\}_{h=1}^{n}\in NCPP(2n)
, one has
(l_{n},r_{n})=(l_{n},l_{n}+1)
.

The set
NCPP(2n)
can be naturally identified with a subset of
\{-1,1\}
-valued maps on
[2n]
. More precisely, let
\{-1,1\}^{2n}
denote the set of maps
\varepsilon:[2n]\to\{-1,1\}
, and define

\{-1,1\}^{2n}_{+}:=\left\{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{2n}\ \middle|\ \sum_{h=1}^{2n}\varepsilon(h)=0\ \text{and}\ \sum_{h=1}^{r}\varepsilon(h)\leq 0\,,r\in[2n]\right\}.

Then the correspondence

NCPP(2n)\ni\{(l_{h},r_{h})\}_{h=1}^{n}\mapsto\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{2n}_{+},

defined by setting
\varepsilon(l_{h})=-1
and
\varepsilon(r_{h})=1
for each
h
, establishes a bijection.

Removing one block, say
(l_{k},r_{k})
, from a partition
\{(l_{h},r_{h})\}_{h=1}^{n}
belonging to
NCPP(2n)
gives rise to a non crossing pair partition
\{(l^{\prime}_{h},r^{\prime}_{h})\}_{h=1}^{n-1}
in
NCPP(2n-2)
. Indeed, if
d:\{(l_{h},r_{h})\mid h=1,\ldots,n,h\neq k\}\rightarrow[2n-2]
is the order preserving map, one denotes
l^{\prime}_{h}=d(l_{h})
,
r^{\prime}_{h}=d(r_{h})
if
h<k
and
l^{\prime}_{h}=d(l_{h+1})
,
r^{\prime}_{h}=d(r_{h+1})
if
h>k
. The new partition is referred to as the
reduction
of the original partition. Under the above correspondence, this operation yields a reduced sequence
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{2n-2}_{+}
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
with finite moments, and denote by
(m_{n}(\mu))_{n\geq 0}
its moment sequence. The associated
moment generating function
is defined, for
z\in\mathbb{C}
, by

\mathcal{M}_{\mu}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}m_{n}(\mu),

which is understood as a formal power series whenever the series does not converge absolutely.

Denote by
\mathbb{C}^{+}
and
\mathbb{C}^{-}
the upper and lower complex half-planes, respectively. The
Cauchy transform
of
\mu
is defined by

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z):=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\mu(dx)}{z-x}.

The function
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
is analytic on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathop{\rm supp}(\mu)
, satisfies
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(\overline{z})=\overline{\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z)}
, and maps
\mathbb{C}^{+}
into
\mathbb{C}^{-}
. As a consequence, it suffices to consider
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
on
\mathbb{C}^{+}\cup\bigl(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathop{\rm supp}(\mu)\bigr)
. In this region, the Cauchy transform uniquely determines the measure
\mu
(see,
e.g.
,
[
15
]
):

(1) The limit

(2.1)

\rho_{\mu}(x):=-\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}\mathop{\rm Im}\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(x+iy)

exists for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\rho_{\mu}(x)\,dx
coincides with the absolutely continuous part of
\mu
. Formula (
2.1
) is known as the
Stieltjes inversion formula
.

(2) The function
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
has a simple pole at
z=a\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
a
is an isolated point of
\mathop{\rm supp}(\mu)
. In this case,

\mu=c\,\delta_{a}+(1-c)\nu,\qquad 0\leq c\leq 1,

where
\nu
is a probability measure satisfying
\nu(\{a\})=0
. Moreover,
c=\operatorname*{Res}_{z=a}\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z)
.

A direct computation shows that

(2.2)

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{z}\,\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\!\left(\frac{1}{z}\right),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

We conclude this section by recalling the Marchenko-Pastur distribution
\mu
(see,
e.g.
,
[
17
]
). In free probability, this distribution is also known as the
free Poisson distribution
, since it arises as the Poisson limit law for two-point processes with respect to additive free convolution (see,
e.g.
,
[
19
]
). For parameters
\lambda\geq 0
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, the measure
\mu
is defined by

\mu:=\begin{cases}(1-\lambda)\delta_{0}+\widetilde{\mu},&0\leq\lambda\leq 1,\\
\widetilde{\mu},&\lambda>1,\end{cases}

where

d\widetilde{\mu}(t):=\frac{1}{2\pi\gamma t}\sqrt{4\lambda\gamma^{2}-(t-\gamma(1+\lambda))^{2}}\,\chi_{[\gamma(1-\sqrt{\lambda})^{2},\ \gamma(1+\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}]}(t)\,dt.

In analogy with the classical setting,
\lambda
is called the
rate
and
\gamma
the
jump size
.

The Cauchy transform of
\mu
is given by

(2.3)

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z)=\frac{z+\gamma-\lambda\gamma-\sqrt{(z-\gamma(1+\lambda))^{2}-4\lambda\gamma^{2}}}{2\gamma z}.

Finally, note that
\mu
coincides with the law of the square of a Wigner random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}
in the special case
\lambda=1
and
\gamma=\sigma^{2}
.

3.
On one-mode type interacting Fock spaces

In this section we introduce one-mode type interacting Fock spaces and study vacuum moments of the associated creation and annihilation operators.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a separable Hilbert space with canonical basis
(e_{n})_{n}
, and fix a sequence
(\lambda_{n})_{n}
of non-negative numbers with
\lambda_{0}=1
. For each
n
,
\lambda_{n}
naturally induces a deformation of the usual tensor product
({\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes n},(\cdot,\cdot)_{n})
by defining

\langle f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n},\,g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n}\rangle_{n}:=\lambda_{n}(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n},\,g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})_{n}.

For any
n
, denote by
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
the Hilbert space completion with respect to this new inner product, and note that
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}:={\mathbb{C}}\Omega
, for a distinguished unit vector
\Omega
, called the vacuum. The gradation

{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\lambda_{n}):=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{H}}_{n}

defines a Hilbert space called
one-mode type interacting Fock space
, see, for example,
[
2
]
. If each
\lambda_{n}
is equal to
1
, one recovers the usual full Fock space.

The creation operator with test function
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
is defined by

a^{\dagger}(f)\Omega:=f,\qquad a^{\dagger}(f)\,f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}:=f\otimes f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}\,.

where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
Note that if there exists an integer
n
such that
\lambda_{n}=0
, then one must have
\lambda_{n+m}=0
for all
m
: this condition ensures that the creation operator is well defined. The annihilation operator is given by

a(f)\Omega:=0,\qquad a(f)\,f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}:=\omega_{n}\langle f,f_{1}\rangle f_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n},

where
\omega_{n}:=\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n-1}}
. When
(\omega_{n})_{n}
is bounded, both operators extend linearly to the whole space
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\lambda_{n})
and are adjoint to each other. To simplify notation, in what follows we write
a_{i}
and
a_{i}^{\dagger}
instead of
a(e_{i})
and
a^{\dagger}(e_{i})
, respectively.

If
N
denotes the number operator, then for each
f,g\in{\mathcal{H}}
, one has

(3.1)

a(f)a^{{\dagger}}(g)=\langle f,g\rangle\,\omega_{N+1},

where
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker symbol and
\omega_{N+1}
is determined by
N
and the sequence
(\omega_{n})_{n}
via functional calculus. In addition, one has

(3.2)

\omega_{N+1}a^{\dagger}(f)=a^{\dagger}(f)\omega_{N+2},\qquad\omega_{N+1}a(f)=a(f)\omega_{N}.

Since the subject of these notes is a particular one-mode type interacting Fock space in which the sequence
(\omega_{n})_{n}
takes only two values infinitely many times, from now on we assume that
\omega_{n}>0
for each
n
and
(\omega_{n})_{n}
is bounded. Equivalently, the latter condition gives the creation and annihilation operators are bounded on
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\lambda_{n})
. The truncated case
\omega_{n}=0
for some
n
has been treated in
[
10
]
.

As in
[
4
]
, one observes that, for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, each finite sequence of
a_{j}
and
a_{j}^{\dagger}
can be written in the so-called normal (or Wick) order, that is, with all creation operators appearing to the left of all annihilation operators. More precisely, (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and an induction argument yield that for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\sigma(1),\ldots\sigma(n)\in\{1,{\dagger}\}

a_{j}^{\sigma(1)}a_{j}^{\sigma(2)}\cdots a_{j}^{\sigma(n)}=(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{n_{+}}a_{j}^{n_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{N+h_{k}}\,,

where
n_{0}
is the number of couples in the sequence of the type appearing in (
3.1
),
n_{+}
(
n_{-}
) is the number of creators (annihilators) in the sequence minus
n_{0}
, and finally
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n_{0}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. When the sequence contains an even number of operators, say
2n
, then
n_{+}+\,n_{-}=2(n-n_{0})
, that is
n_{+}+\,n_{-}\in\{2m\mid m\in[0,n]\}
, and consequently
n_{+}-n_{-}\in\{2m\mid m\in[-n,n]\}
. The following proposition will be exploited in Section
6
.
We use the convention that
\omega_{n}=0
if
n<0
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
{\mathfrak{D}}^{0}_{j}
be the linear span of

\bigg\{(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{n_{+}}a_{j}^{n_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{N+h_{k}}:n_{0},n_{-},n_{+}\in{\mathbb{N}},\,n_{+}+n_{-}\in 2{\mathbb{N}},\,h_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,k\in[n_{0}]\bigg\}.

Then
{\mathfrak{D}}^{0}_{j}
is a
*
-algebra containing the identity operator of
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\lambda_{n})
.

Proof.

For any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\mathfrak{D}}^{0}_{j}
is closed under involution, and, if
\prod_{k\in\emptyset}:=1
, then
\displaystyle{I_{{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\lambda_{n})}=(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{0}a_{j}^{0}\prod_{k\in\emptyset}\omega_{N+h_{k}}}
. It remains to prove that, if

b:=(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{n_{+}}a_{j}^{n_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{N+h_{k}}\,,\quad c:=(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{m_{+}}a_{j}^{m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\omega_{N+r_{k}}\,,

where
n_{+}+\,n_{-},m_{+}\,+m_{-}\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
and
h_{k},r_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for each
k\in[1,n_{0}]
, then
bc\in{\mathfrak{D}}^{0}_{j}
. Indeed, (
3.2
) gives

bc=(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{n_{+}}a_{j}^{n_{-}}(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{m_{+}}a_{j}^{m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{N+h_{k}+m_{+}+m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\omega_{N+r_{k}}\,.

A repeated use of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) yields

a_{j}^{n_{-}}(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{m_{+}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{(m_{+}-n_{-})}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{-}}\omega_{N+k+m_{+}-n_{-}}}&\text{ if }n_{-}\leq m_{+}\\
\displaystyle{a_{j}^{(n_{-}-m_{+})}\prod_{k=1}^{m_{+}}\omega_{N+j}}&\text{ if }n_{-}>m_{+}\,.\end{cases}

This means that
bc
is

\displaystyle{(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{(n_{+}+m_{+}-n_{-})}a_{j}^{m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{-}}\omega_{N+k+m_{+}-n_{-}-m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{N+h_{k}+m_{+}-m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\omega_{N+r_{k}}}

or

\displaystyle{(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{n_{+}}a_{j}^{n_{-}-m_{+}+m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{m_{+}}\omega_{N+k-m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{N+h_{k}+m_{+}-m_{-}}\prod_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\omega_{N+r_{k}}}

according to whether
n_{-}\leq m_{+}
or
n_{-}>m_{+}
, respectively.

The assumptions on
n_{+},n_{-},m_{+}-m_{-}
imply that, when
n_{-}\leq m_{+}
, then
n_{+}-n_{-}+m_{+}+m_{-}\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
, whereas, if
n_{-}>m_{+}
, one finds
n_{+}+n_{-}-m_{+}+m_{-}\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

The self-adjoint part of the creation and annihilation operators, usually referred to as the Gaussian operator, is defined by
s_{j}:=a_{j}+a_{j}^{\dagger}
, for
j\geq 1
. The vector state associated with
\Omega
is usually referred to as the vacuum state or the vacuum expectation, and often denoted by
\tau_{\Omega}
.

Recalling that for each natural integers
j
and
n
one has

(3.3)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Omega}(s_{j}^{n})=\langle\Omega,s_{j}^{n}\Omega\rangle=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $n$ is odd},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{2n}_{+}}\langle\Omega,a_{j}^{\varepsilon(1)}\cdots a_{j}^{\varepsilon(2m)}\Omega\rangle&\text{if $n=2m$},\end{cases}

where

a_{j}^{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}a_{j}&\text{if }\varepsilon=-1,\\
a_{j}^{\dagger}&\text{if }\varepsilon=1,\end{cases}

we state a result that will be needed later.

Proposition 3.2
.

For each
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{2n}_{+}
and each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\{l_{h},r_{h}\}_{h=1}^{n}
be the corresponding non crossing pair partition associated with
\varepsilon
. If
f,g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{2n}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, then

(3.4)

\langle\Omega,a^{\varepsilon(1)}(f_{1})\cdots a^{\varepsilon(2n)}(f_{2n})\Omega\rangle=\prod_{h=1}^{n}\omega_{2h-l_{h}}\langle f_{l_{h}},f_{r_{h}}\rangle,

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,a(f)a^{\varepsilon(1)}(f_{1})\cdots a^{\varepsilon(2n)}(f_{2n})a^{\dagger}(g)\Omega\rangle=\omega_{1}\langle f,g\rangle\prod_{h=1}^{n}\omega_{2h-l_{h}+1}\langle f_{l_{h}},f_{r_{h}}\rangle.

Proof.

We fix
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and proceed by induction. The case
n=1
reduces to

\langle\Omega,a^{\varepsilon(1)}(f_{1})a^{\varepsilon(2)}(f_{2})\Omega\rangle=\langle\Omega,a(f_{1})a^{\dagger}(f_{2})\Omega\rangle=\omega_{1}\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle

and

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,a(f)a^{\varepsilon(1)}(f_{1})a^{\varepsilon(2)}(f_{2})a^{\dagger}(g)\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega,a(f)a(f_{1})a^{\dagger}(f_{2})a^{\dagger}(g)\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\langle f,g\rangle\,\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle\,,

where we exploited (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).

Suppose now that (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) hold for any
n\leq m
. We consider the case
n=m+1
. For any
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{2m+2}_{+}
, we denote by
\varepsilon^{\prime}
the reduction of
\{l_{h},r_{h}\}_{h=1}^{m+1}
corresponding to the erasing of the most inner block
(l_{m+1},r_{m+1})
. Then one has

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle\Omega,a^{\varepsilon(1)}(f_{1})\cdots a^{\varepsilon(2m+2)}(f_{2m+2})\Omega\rangle\\
=&\langle\Omega,a^{\varepsilon(1)}(f_{1})\cdots a^{\varepsilon(l_{m+1}-1)}(f_{l_{m+1}-1})a(f_{l_{m+1}})a^{\dagger}(f_{r_{m+1}})\cdots a^{\dagger}(f_{2m+2})\Omega\rangle\\
=&\omega_{2m+2-l_{m+1}}\langle f_{l_{m+1}},f_{r_{m+1}}\rangle\prod_{h=1}^{m}\omega_{2h-l^{\prime}_{h}}\langle f_{l^{\prime}_{h}},f_{r^{\prime}_{h}}\rangle\end{split}

where the last equality comes from the induction assumption. As for each
h=1,\ldots,m
one has
l^{\prime}_{h}=l_{h}
and
f_{r^{\prime}_{h}}=f_{r_{h}}
, (
3.6
) turns out to be

\langle\Omega,a^{\varepsilon(1)}(f_{1})\ldots a^{\varepsilon(2m+2)}(f_{2m+2})\Omega\rangle=\prod_{h=1}^{m+1}\omega_{2h-l_{h}}\langle f_{l_{h}},f_{r_{h}}\rangle\,.

The same arguments also show that (
3.5
) holds when
n=m+1
.
∎

4.
The
C^{*}
-algebra generated by annihilation operators in period-2 interacting Fock spaces

In this section, we investigate a class of one-mode type interacting Fock spaces associated with period-2 interacting sequences. Our main goal is to analyze the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the annihilation operators and to provide a space-free characterization of it.

Let us start with the sequence
(\lambda_{n})_{n}
defined, for fixed
\alpha,\beta>0
, by

(4.1)

\lambda_{2n}:=\alpha^{n}\beta^{n},\qquad\lambda_{2n+1}:=\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{n},\quad n\geq 0.

As a consequence,

(4.2)

\omega_{2n-1}=\alpha,\qquad\omega_{2n}=\beta,\quad n\geq 1.

The corresponding one-mode type interacting Fock space is said to have
period 2
and is denoted by
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\alpha,\beta)
. The creation and annihilation operators are bounded. Indeed, for any
f\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

(4.3)

\|a^{\dagger}(f)\|=\|a(f)\|\leq\max(\sqrt{\alpha},\sqrt{\beta})\|f\|.

Let
{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
denote the
*
-algebra generated by
\{a_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, and let
{\mathfrak{A}}
be its uniform closure. The definition of the creation operators implies that
\Omega
is cyclic for
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
Note that
{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
is unital. Indeed, from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) one obtains

(4.4)

a_{i}a_{j}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{i}^{\dagger}=a_{i}\omega_{N+1}a_{i}^{\dagger}=\omega_{N+2}a_{j}a_{j}^{\dagger}=\omega_{N+2}\omega_{N+1}=\alpha\beta I,\quad i,j\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
I
is the identity operator.

Let
P
be the projection of
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\alpha,\beta)
onto
\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{H}}_{2n}
, and define
Q:=I-P
. By (
4.2
), the relations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) take the form

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{i}a_{j}^{\dagger}=\delta_{i,j}(\alpha P+\beta Q),\quad i,j\in{\mathbb{N}},\\
&a_{i}P=Qa_{i},\qquad a_{i}^{\dagger}P=Qa_{i}^{\dagger},\quad i\in{\mathbb{N}}\,.\end{split}

The relations above immediately yield a linear basis for
{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
:

Proposition 4.1
.

The words of the form

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}X&:=a^{\dagger}_{i_{1}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{i_{n}}a_{j_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{m}}P,\\
\widetilde{X}&:=a^{\dagger}_{l_{1}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{l_{p}}a_{k_{1}}\cdots a_{k_{q}}Q,\end{split}

where
n,m,p,q\geq 0
and

i_{1},\dots,i_{n},j_{1},\dots,j_{m},l_{1},\dots,l_{p},k_{1},\dots,k_{q}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, form a Hamel basis of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
L
denote the index set such that
(Y_{l})_{l\in L}:=(X_{l},\widetilde{X}_{l})_{l\in L}
consists of all words of the form (
4.6
), and consider

Y:=\sum_{l\in F}\delta_{l}X_{l}+\lambda_{l}\widetilde{X}_{l},

where
F\subset L
is finite and
\delta_{k},\lambda_{l}
are complex scalars. From (
4.5
), the set
(Y_{l})_{l\in L}
generates
{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
. Assume that
Y=0
. We may assume that the identity
I
does not appear in
Y
. Otherwise, denoting by
l_{0}
the index such that
Y_{l_{0}}=I
, one can take a unit vector
e_{k}
in the kernel of each word in
Y
ending with an annihilator. Thus, for
l\neq l_{0}
, the vector
Y_{l}e_{k}
is orthogonal to
e_{k}
whenever it is nonzero, and consequently
\lambda_{l_{0}}=\langle Ye_{k},e_{k}\rangle=0
. Similarly,
\delta_{l_{0}}=\langle Y(e_{k}\otimes e_{k}),e_{k}\otimes e_{k}\rangle=0
.

Now, for any
l\in L
, write explicitly

X_{l}=a^{\dagger}_{i_{l_{1}}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{i_{l_{n}}}a_{j_{l_{1}}}\cdots a_{j_{l_{m}}}P,\qquad\widetilde{X}_{l}=a^{\dagger}_{i_{l_{1}}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{i_{l_{n}}}a_{j_{l_{1}}}\cdots a_{j_{l_{m}}}Q,

and choose vectors

\xi_{l}:=e_{j_{l_{m}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{j_{l_{1}}},\qquad\eta_{l}:=e_{i_{l_{1}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{l_{n}}},

with the convention that
\xi_{l}=\Omega
if
m=0
and
\eta_{l}=\Omega
if
n=0
. Then, depending on whether
m=2r
or
m=2r+1
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\langle Y\xi_{l},\eta_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\delta_{l}\langle X_{l}\xi_{l},\eta_{l}\rangle,&\text{if }m=2r,\\
\delta_{l}\langle\widetilde{X}_{l}\xi_{l},\eta_{l}\rangle,&\text{if }m=2r+1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\alpha^{r}\beta^{r}\lambda_{n}\delta_{l},&\text{if }m=2r,\\
\alpha^{r+1}\beta^{r}\lambda_{n}\delta_{l},&\text{if }m=2r+1.\end{cases}

Since
\alpha,\beta>0
and
\lambda_{n}>0
by (
4.1
), it follows that
\delta_{l}=0
in all cases.
∎

The projection onto the vacuum is denoted by
P_{\Omega}
. As shown below, it does not belong to
{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
does not contain
P_{\Omega}
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose
P_{\Omega}\in{\mathfrak{A}}
. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
X_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
such that
\|X_{\varepsilon}-P_{\Omega}\|<\varepsilon
. By Proposition
4.1
, each word in
{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
can be put in normal order; thus, for a finite set
F\subset{\mathbb{N}}
,

X_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{l\in F}a^{\dagger}_{i_{l_{1}}^{\varepsilon}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{i_{l_{n}}^{\varepsilon}}a_{j_{l_{1}}^{\varepsilon}}\cdots a_{j_{l_{m}}^{\varepsilon}}(\delta_{l}^{\varepsilon}P+\lambda_{l}^{\varepsilon}Q),

where
\delta_{l}^{\varepsilon}
and
\lambda_{l}^{\varepsilon}
are complex numbers. Note that
X_{\varepsilon}
contains
I
. Indeed, otherwise
\varepsilon>\|(X_{\varepsilon}-P_{\Omega})\Omega\|=\|\xi_{\varepsilon}-\Omega\|
, where
\xi_{\varepsilon}\perp\Omega
, which yields the contradiction
\varepsilon>1
.

Let
l_{0}\in F
be the index corresponding to
n=m=0
. Then
\|(X_{\varepsilon}-P_{\Omega})\Omega\|=\left\|\delta_{l_{0}}^{\varepsilon}\Omega-\Omega+\xi_{\varepsilon}\right\|
,
where
\xi_{\varepsilon}
is orthogonal to
\Omega
. Hence,

(4.7)

\left|1-\delta_{l_{0}}^{\varepsilon}\right|<\varepsilon.

On the other hand, let
m
be larger than all indices of creators and annihilators appearing in
X_{\varepsilon}
. Applying
X_{\varepsilon}-P_{\Omega}
to
e_{m}\otimes e_{m+1}
yields
\|\delta_{l_{0}}^{\varepsilon}(e_{m}\otimes e_{m+1})+\eta_{\varepsilon}\|<\varepsilon
,
where
\eta_{\varepsilon}\perp e_{m}\otimes e_{m+1}
. Therefore,
|\delta_{l_{0}}^{\varepsilon}|<\varepsilon
,
contradicting (
4.7
).
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

The
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
is irreducible.

Proof.

We prove that
{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime}={\mathbb{C}}I
. First, note that
P_{\Omega}\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime\prime}
. Indeed, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\xi\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\lambda_{n})\ominus{\mathbb{C}}\Omega
, (
4.2
) gives

\left(a_{i}a_{i}^{\dagger}+\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}\right)(\xi\oplus\Omega)=(\alpha+\beta)\xi\oplus\alpha\Omega,

so that

\beta P_{\Omega}=(\alpha+\beta)I-a_{i}a_{i}^{\dagger}-\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}.

If
b\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime}
, then
b\Omega=bP_{\Omega}\Omega=P_{\Omega}b\Omega=\langle\Omega,b\Omega\rangle\Omega
. Since
\Omega
is cyclic for
{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime\prime}
, it follows that
b=\tau_{\Omega}(b)I
.
∎

It is possible to provide a space-free construction for the
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
. More precisely, for fixed
\alpha,\beta>0
, let
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
be the universal unital
*
-algebra generated by a system
\{b_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
realizing the following relations:

(4.8)

\displaystyle b_{i}b_{j}^{*}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}b_{k}b_{k}^{*},

\displaystyle i,j,k\in{\mathbb{N}},

(4.9)

\displaystyle b_{i}b_{i}^{*}-\beta{1}\!\!{\rm I}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}(b_{i}b_{i}^{*}-\beta{1}\!\!{\rm I})^{2},

\displaystyle i\in{\mathbb{N}},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}b_{j}(b_{i}b_{i}^{*}-\beta{1}\!\!{\rm I})

\displaystyle=b_{j}-\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}(b_{i}b_{i}^{*}-\beta{1}\!\!{\rm I})b_{j},

\displaystyle i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

where
{1}\!\!{\rm I}
is the unit of
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
.
Observe that (
4.8
)-(
4.9
) imply that the self-adjoint element

(4.11)

p:=\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}(b_{i}b_{i}^{*}-\beta{1}\!\!{\rm I})

is an idempotent independent of
i
. Then (
4.10
) can be written simply as

(4.12)

b_{j}p=({1}\!\!{\rm I}-p)b_{j}\,,\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}\,.

Note that it also yields
(1-p)b_{j}=b_{j}p
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The universal
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{B}}
generated by
\{b_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
satisfying (
4.8
)-(
4.10
) exists and is isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{A}}.

Proof.

Consider the maximal
C^{*}
-seminorm on
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
given by
\|\cdot\|_{\max}:=\sup\{\|\pi(\cdot)\|\mid\pi\,\text{$*$-representation}\}
.
Since (
4.8
) and (
4.11
) are equivalent to

(4.13)

b_{i}b^{*}_{j}=\delta_{i,j}(\alpha p+\beta({1}\!\!{\rm I}-p))\,,\quad i,j\,,

the map
\Phi_{0}:{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{A}}_{0}
defined by
\Phi_{0}(b_{i})=a_{i}
preserves (
4.5
) and thus extends by universality to a
*
-homomorphism. Pick now the words in
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
of type

(4.14)

x=b^{*}_{i_{1}}\cdots b^{*}_{i_{n}}b_{j_{1}}\cdots b_{j_{m}}p,\qquad\widetilde{x}=b^{*}_{i_{1}}\cdots b^{*}_{i_{n}}b_{j_{1}}\cdots b_{j_{m}}({1}\!\!{\rm I}-p),

with
n,m\geq 0
,
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n},j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. As usual,
x=p
and
\widetilde{x}={1}\!\!{\rm I}-p
when
n=m=0
. Using (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), one obtains
b_{i}b_{j}b_{j}^{*}b_{i}^{*}=\alpha\beta{1}\!\!{\rm I}
for each
i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore,
\Phi_{0}
is unital by (
4.4
) and sends
p
to
P
. This shows that
\Phi_{0}(x)=X
and
\Phi_{0}(\widetilde{x})=\widetilde{X}
, where
X
and
\widetilde{X}
are given in (
4.6
). Hence, linear combinations of words of type
x
and
\widetilde{x}
make up a Hamel basis in
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
. Thus,
\Phi_{0}
is injective and
\|\cdot\|_{\max}
is actually a norm.

Now let
\pi
be a
*
-representation of
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
. By (
4.13
), for each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\|\pi(b_{i})\|^{2}=\|\pi(b_{i}b_{i}^{*})\|=\|\alpha\pi(p)+\beta\pi({1}\!\!{\rm I}-p)\|=\max\{\alpha,\beta\}
, since
\pi(p)
and
\pi({1}\!\!{\rm I}-p)
are orthogonal. Consequently, for each
y\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
, there exists a constant
C_{y}
such that
\|\pi(y)\|\leq C_{y}
, and therefore
\|\cdot\|_{\max}
is finite.

The universal
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{B}}
is then the completion of
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
with respect to the maximal norm, and
\Phi_{0}
extends to a
*
-epimorphism
\Phi:{\mathfrak{B}}\to{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Proving that
\Phi
is isometric requires more work. We follow standard arguments based on the gauge action.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid|z|=1\}
be the unit circle: For each
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
, define the map
\gamma_{z}(b_{i}):=zb_{i}
for each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. As
\gamma_{z}(b_{i})
satisfies (
4.8
)-(
4.10
),
\gamma_{z}
extends uniquely by universality to a
*
-homomorphism which is also bijective with inverse
\gamma_{\overline{z}}
. This gives a strongly continuous action of
{\mathbb{T}}
on
{\mathfrak{B}}
by
*
-automorphisms. This action is referred to as the gauge action, and the gauge-invariant subalgebra
{\mathfrak{B}}^{\gamma}:=\{b\in{\mathfrak{B}}\mid\gamma_{z}(b)=b,\,\,z\in{\mathbb{T}}\}
is algebraically spanned by the words in the Hamel basis of
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
containing the same number of
b_{i}
and
b_{i}^{*}
. Averaging this action with respect to the Haar-Lebesgue measure yields the map
T
on
{\mathfrak{B}}
such that
b\in{\mathfrak{B}}\mapsto T(b):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma_{z}(b)dz
. Note that for each
x
or
\widetilde{x}
in (
4.14
), one has
T(x)=\delta_{n,m}x
,
T(\widetilde{x})=\delta_{n,m}\widetilde{x}
. More precisely,
T
is a faithful conditional expectation onto
{\mathfrak{B}}^{\gamma}
, and consequently for any
b\in{\mathfrak{B}}
,
\|b\|_{\max}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T((b^{*}b)^{n})\|_{\max}^{1/2n}
.

Similarly, the family of unitaries
U_{z}
on the Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}},\alpha,\beta)
such
that
U_{z}\lceil_{{\mathcal{H}}_{n}}:=z^{n}I_{{\mathcal{H}}_{n}}
for each
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
and

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, implement a gauge representation
\widetilde{\gamma}_{z}
of

{\mathbb{T}}
on
{\mathfrak{A}}
, namely
\widetilde{\gamma}_{z}(b):=U_{z}bU_{z}^{*}
, with

z\in{\mathbb{T}}
and
b\in{\mathfrak{B}}
. Let us denote by
\widetilde{T}
the faithful
conditional expectation onto the fixed-point subalgebra

\widetilde{T}(\cdot):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\widetilde{\gamma}_{z}(\cdot)dz
. As the
two actions described above are equivariant, one has

(4.15)

\widetilde{T}\circ\Phi_{0}=\Phi_{0}\circ T\,.

Now fix
k
and let
\mathcal{A}_{k}
be the linear span of words as in (
4.14
) for which
n=m
,
i_{1},\ldots i_{n},j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}\leq k
, and
0\leq n\leq k
. Each
\mathcal{A}_{k}
is then finite-dimensional,
\mathcal{A}_{k}\subset\mathcal{A}_{k+1}
, and for any
b\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
,
T(b)\in\mathcal{A}_{K}
with
K
sufficiently large, and
{\mathfrak{B}}^{\gamma}=\overline{\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}}
. Moreover, since
\Phi_{0}
is injective on
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
, it is isometric on each finite-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{A}_{k}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
.

Finally, for any
b\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
,

\|b\|_{\max}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T((b^{*}b)^{n})\|_{\max}^{1/2n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|T((b^{*}b)^{n})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}^{1/2n}\,.

But(
4.15
) gives that

\|T((b^{*}b)^{n})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}=\|\Phi_{0}(T((b^{*}b)^{n}))\|=\|\tilde{T}(\Phi_{0}((b^{*}b)^{n}))\|\leq\|\Phi_{0}(b)\|^{2n}\,

since
\tilde{T}
is norm one. We then get
\|b\|_{\max}=\|\Phi_{0}(b)\|
for all
b\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
, and this extends to
{\mathfrak{B}}
by density.
∎

We end the section by noting that
{\mathfrak{B}}
is not a type
I

C^{*}
-algebra. In fact, by Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
, the Fock representation
{\mathfrak{A}}
is irreducible but does not contain the compact operators, which contradicts a characterization of separable type
I

C^{*}
-algebras, see
[
14
]
.

5.
The von Neumann algebra generated by position operators

In this section we investigate the von Neumann algebra generated by the position operators associated with period-2 interacting Fock spaces. We prove that this algebra is a type
II_{1}
factor. The key steps in the identification of the unique tracial state are the construction of a normal trace on the odd subalgebra and the fact that the vacuum vector is cyclic and separating for the even subalgebra.

The self-adjoint part of
{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime\prime}
, namely the von Neumann algebra generated by
\{a_{i}+a^{\dagger}_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, will be denoted by
\mathcal{S}
. The proof of Proposition
4.3
shows that
P_{\Omega}
belongs to
{\mathfrak{A}}^{\prime\prime}
. The next result shows that the identity operator is the unit of
\mathcal{S}
and that both
P
and
Q
belong to it.

Proposition 5.1
.

The von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{S}
is unital with unit
I
and contains both the projections
P
and
Q
.

Proof.

Fix
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. From (
4.5
), we have

s_{i}^{2}=a_{i}a_{i}+(\alpha P+\beta Q)+a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{i}+a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{i},

which shows that
\alpha P+\beta Q\in\mathcal{S}
after taking the weak limit as
i\rightarrow\infty
.
As
\alpha\neq\beta
, by functional calculus,
P,Q\in\mathcal{S}
and hence
I=P+Q\in\mathcal{S}
.
∎

For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we introduce the normal (or Wick) ordered words

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&W(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a^{\dagger}_{i_{1}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{i_{k}}a_{i_{k+1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}}P,\\
&\widetilde{W}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a^{\dagger}_{i_{1}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{i_{k}}a_{i_{k+1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}}Q.\end{split}

If
n=0
, we denote by
W(\emptyset)
and
\widetilde{W}(\emptyset)
the projections
P
and
Q
, respectively. From now on, we denote by
\bf{W}
the collection of all normal ordered words defined in (
5.1
).

We introduce the subalgebras
\mathcal{S}_{P}:=P\mathcal{S}P
and
\mathcal{S}_{Q}:=Q\mathcal{S}Q
, and call them the even and odd parts of
\mathcal{S}
, respectively.

Lemma 5.2
.

The strong closure of the linear span of the words in (
5.1
) coincides with
\mathcal{S}
. We also have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{P}=\overline{\{W(i_{1},\ldots,i_{2n})\}}^{w*}=\langle W(i_{1},i_{2}),P\rangle^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\,,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{Q}=\overline{\{\widetilde{W}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{2n})\}}^{w*}=\langle\widetilde{W}(i_{1},i_{2}),Q\rangle^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\,,

where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

The vector space generated by
\bf{W}
is unital, since
I=W(\emptyset)+\widetilde{W}(\emptyset)
. It is invariant under the
*
-operation
as
W(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})^{*}=W(i_{n},\ldots,i_{1})
if
n
is even and

W(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})^{*}=\widetilde{W}(i_{n},\ldots,i_{1})
if
n
is odd. Moreover, for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have
s_{j}P=W(j)
and
s_{j}Q=\widetilde{W}(j)
. Therefore, it suffices to show that for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, both
s_{j}W(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
and
s_{j}\widetilde{W}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
belong to the linear span of
\bf{W}
.

To this end, introduce the functions, for
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

o(h):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }h=2m,\\
1&\text{if }h=2m+1,\end{cases}\qquad e(h):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }h=2m+1,\\
1&\text{if }h=2m,\end{cases}

and note that from (
4.5
) we obtain

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}s_{j}W(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})&=(a_{j}+a^{\dagger}_{j})\sum_{k=0}^{n}a^{\dagger}_{i_{1}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{i_{k}}a_{i_{k+1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}}P\\
&=W(j,i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})+\delta_{j,i_{1}}(\alpha P+\beta Q)W(i_{2},\ldots,i_{n})\\
&=W(j,i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})+\delta_{j,i_{1}}\alpha^{o(n)}\beta^{e(n)}W(i_{2},\ldots,i_{n}),\end{split}

and

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}s_{j}\widetilde{W}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})&=(a_{j}+a^{\dagger}_{j})\sum_{k=0}^{n}a^{\dagger}_{i_{1}}\cdots a^{\dagger}_{i_{k}}a_{i_{k+1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}}Q\\
&=\widetilde{W}(j,i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})+\delta_{j,i_{1}}(\alpha P+\beta Q)\widetilde{W}(i_{2},\ldots,i_{n})\\
&=\widetilde{W}(j,i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})+\delta_{j,i_{1}}\alpha^{e(n)}\beta^{o(n)}\widetilde{W}(i_{2},\ldots,i_{n}).\end{split}

We now prove (
5.2
). For the first equality, one inclusion is
obvious as
W(i_{1},i_{2})\in\mathcal{S}_{P}
. Let us denote

W_{n}:=\overline{\{W(i_{1},\ldots,i_{2k});k\leq n\}}^{w*}
. Then, from
(
5.4
),

s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}W(i_{3},...,i_{2n})-W(i_{1},...,i_{2n})\in W_{n-1}
. In
particular,
s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}P\in\langle W(i_{1},i_{2}),P\rangle^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
and one
concludes that

W(i_{1},...,i_{2n})\in\langle W(i_{1},i_{2}),P\rangle^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
by induction on
n
. Similarly, one gets (
5.3
).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The arguments in the above proof, together with Proposition
4.1
, show that the words in
\bf{W}
form a Hamel basis for the
*
-algebra generated by the position operators.

Proposition 5.4
.

The vacuum vector
\Omega
is cyclic and separating for
\mathcal{S}_{P}
on
\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{H}}_{2n}
.

Proof.

First, observe that for each
i_{1},...,i_{2n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

Remark 6.8
