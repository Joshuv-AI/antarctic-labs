The distance between homotopy classes of Sobolev maps on spheres
Rupert L. Frank, Paata Ivanisvili

Abstract
. We consider self-maps of a sphere in the critical Sobolev space with a given Brouwer degree. Our main result is that the (directed) distance between maps of different degrees is equal to an explicit constant times the difference in degrees. In the case of the 2-sphere this resolves an open problem by Brezis.

1.
Introduction and main result

1.1.
The critical degree classes

In this paper we are interested in maps from the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:|x|=1\}
,
n\geq 2
, to itself that belong to the Sobolev space

(1.1)

W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n}):=\{u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n+1}):|u|=1\text{ a.e.}\}.

As we will recall in detail in Section
2
below, such maps have a well-defined Brouwer degree. For
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

(1.2)

\mathcal{E}_{d}^{(n)}:=\{u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n}):\deg u=d\}.

Our main result gives an explicit formula for the min-max quantity

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}_{\tau}f-\mathrm{D}_{\tau}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,

where
D_{\tau}u
denotes the weak (tangential) derivative of
u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
and we employ the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, that is, if
(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})
is a local orthonormal frame of
T\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then

|\mathrm{D}_{\tau}f-\mathrm{D}_{\tau}g|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\partial_{\tau_{i}}f-\partial_{\tau_{i}}g|^{2}.

Also,
\,\mathrm{d}\sigma
denotes surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Introducing the constant

\kappa_{n}:=n^{n/2}|\mathbb{S}^{n}|=n^{n/2}\frac{2\pi^{(n+1)/2}}{\Gamma((n+1)/2)},

we can state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

(1.3)

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}_{\tau}f-\mathrm{D}_{\tau}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|\,.

The equality (
1.3
) is also true for
n=1
, as shown in
[
BMS16
, Theorem 1.4(1)]
. For
n=2
, the inequality
\leq
in (
1.3
) was already known (see
[
Bre23
]
and references therein) and the inequality
\geq
was known when
0\leq d_{1}<d_{2}
(see
[
BMS16
, Proposition 7.3(2)]
), but the inequality
\geq
in the general case is new and gives an affirmative answer to Problem 5.9 on Brezis’s list ‘Some of my favorite open problems’
[
Bre23
]
. To the best of our knowledge, before the present work no exact all-degree formula was known beyond the one-dimensional case and the two-dimensional ranges above.

Theorem
1.1
has the following consequence. If we set, following
[
BMS16
]
,

(1.4)

\operatorname{Dist}_{n}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}_{\tau}f-\mathrm{D}_{\tau}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{1/n},

then we obtain the symmetry relation

(1.5)

\operatorname{Dist}_{n}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)})=\operatorname{Dist}_{n}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)})\,.

Indeed, by Theorem
1.1
, both sides are equal to
(\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|)^{1/n}
. The equality (
1.5
) is new for all
n\geq 3
. In particular, it gives an affirmative answer to Open Problem 1 in
[
BMS16
]
in the case
s=1
,
p=n
. Also, Theorem
1.1
answers Open Problem 2 in
[
BMS16
]
for
p=n
.

Background

Before describing the strategy of our proof, let us mention some previous results on this and related problems. The interaction between degree and concentration in critical Sobolev spaces arose prominently in the work of Brezis and Coron on harmonic maps
[
BC83
, Lemmas 1–2]
; related relaxation phenomena were developed by Bethuel, Brezis, and Coron
[
BBC90
, Theorems 2–3]
. Brezis and Nirenberg subsequently developed degree theory in
\mathrm{VMO}

[
BN95
, Section I.3 and Theorem 1]
.

Brezis, Mironescu, and Shafrir
[
BMS16
]
introduced the ‘directed distance’

(1.6)

\operatorname{Dist}_{p}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n,p)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n,p)}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n,p)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n,p)}}\|f-g\|_{\dot{W}^{n/p,p}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})}\,,

extending (
1.4
) to more general Sobolev spaces
W^{n/p,p}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
with
1\leq p<\infty
. Moreover,
\mathcal{E}_{d}^{(n,p)}
consists of maps in
W^{n/p,p}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
with degree
d
(which is well-defined in
W^{n/p,p}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{n})\subset VMO(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
). In their Open Problem 1 they asked whether
\operatorname{Dist}_{p}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n,p)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n,p)})=\operatorname{Dist}_{p}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n,p)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n,p)})
or, even better, whether it only depends on
|d_{1}-d_{2}|
. In their Open Problem 2 they asked whether one has a lower bound by some constant times
|d_{1}-d_{2}|^{1/p}
. Since the directed distance is bounded above by the corresponding Hausdorff distance,
[
BMS16
, Theorem 1.7(1)]
gives

(1.7)

\operatorname{Dist}_{p}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n,p)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n,p)})\leq C_{p,n}|d_{1}-d_{2}|^{1/p}.

For a fixed source degree,
[
BMS16
, Proposition 7.8]
gives

(1.8)

\operatorname{Dist}_{p}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n,p)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n,p)})\geq c_{p,n,d_{1}}|d_{1}-d_{2}|^{1/p},

where the constant may depend on
d_{1}
. When
1\leq p\leq n+1
and
d_{1}d_{2}\leq 0
, they proved the uniform estimate

(1.9)

\operatorname{Dist}_{p}(\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n,p)},\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n,p)})\geq c_{p,n}|d_{1}-d_{2}|^{1/p}\,,

see
[
BMS16
, Proposition 7.5]
. For the case
p=n
that we are interested in, these estimates give the correct order of growth in every fixed-source regime and uniformly for opposite-sign degrees, but do not identify the sharp constant or imply symmetry for arbitrary pairs.

As we have already mentioned, the analogous problem for
n=1=p
was completely settled by
[
BMS16
, Theorem 1.4(1)]
. Optimal results for
n=1
and
1<p<\infty
appear in
[
Sha18
, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2]
.

In passing, we note that if one replaces the outer supremum in (
1.6
) by an infimum, one obtains zero; see
[
BMS16
, Theorem 4(1)]
. For related results in sub-critical Sobolev spaces we refer to
[
LS14
, Theorem 3.4]
and
[
RS07
, Theorem 1 and Remark 2.1]
.

A recent development, which is decisive for our proof, is the heterotopic energy of Detaille and Van Schaftingen. Their formula identifies the least
W^{1,n}
energy of maps in one homotopy class converging almost everywhere to a map in another class; for sphere targets, the defect is exactly
\kappa_{n}
times the difference of degree
[
DVS26
, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2]
. Their bubbling theorem is stronger than the total-energy inequality: it locates the topological defect in atoms of the limiting energy measure
[
DVS26
, Theorem 5.1]
. In the introduction of their paper, they note explicitly that the heterotopic energy has “no apparent formal mathematical connection” with the earlier directed-distance functionals. A by-product of our paper is to develop such a connection.

Outline of the proof

To prove Theorem
1.1
we will prove an upper and a lower bound on the left side in (
1.3
).

The proof of the upper bound is much easier than that of the lower bound and is presented in Section
3
. Similar arguments have appeared before in the literature. The idea is to locally insert a bubble. The minimal energy carried by one unit of Brouwer degree is
\kappa_{n}
, as is made precise in
3.1
. The intuition is that therefore it costs an energy of at most
|d_{2}-d_{1}|
times
\kappa_{n}
to change the degree
d_{1}
of a given map
f
to
d_{2}
of some other map.

The proof of the lower bound takes up Sections
4
,
5
and

6
. In Section
5
we first construct a finite collection of rotations, all arbitrarily close to the identity, such that the orbit of every
p\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
affinely spans
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Applying these rotations to a fixed smooth map
f_{0}
in rapidly alternating phases gives sphere-valued maps
F_{j}
of the same degree as
f_{0}
. The maps
F_{j}
are ‘hard to approximate’ in the sense that if a sequence
g_{j}
of sphere-valued maps satisfies a uniform
L^{n}
-bound on
\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g_{j})
, compactness and periodic averaging force
F_{j}-g_{j}\to 0
in
L^{n}
; this is
5.5
. We call this mechanism oscillatory pinning.

Now for near-minimizers
g_{j}
corresponding to
F_{j}
, we remove the oscillation from the difference and radially normalize:

u_{j}:=\frac{f_{0}-(F_{j}-g_{j})}{|f_{0}-(F_{j}-g_{j})|}.

Because
F_{j}
is uniformly close to
f_{0}
, the denominator stays away from zero and
u_{j}
has the degree of
g_{j}
. Pinning gives
u_{j}\to f_{0}
in
L^{n}
. Thus, we are in the situation where all the maps
u_{j}
have a fixed degree
d_{2}
, while their
L^{n}
limit has degree
d_{1}
. A special case of the bubbling theorem of Detaille and Van Schaftingen (see
4.1
) forces at least
\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|
of the limiting energy of
u_{j}
into atoms. The derivative of the fixed smooth background
f_{0}
contributes only an absolutely continuous measure. An atom-by-atom comparison therefore transfers the entire topological cost to the limiting measure of
|\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g_{j})|^{n}
. Comparing atoms, rather than total
L^{n}
energies, is the step that works uniformly in every dimension. The details of this argument appear in Section
6
.

2.
Degree and homotopy

In this section we collect some well-known facts that we need in what follows. Throughout, derivatives are tangential derivatives on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and we often write
\mathrm{D}u
for
\mathrm{D}_{\tau}u
.

Let
(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})
be a positively oriented local orthonormal frame of
T\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For
u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
, define

(2.1)

\operatorname{Jac}u:=\det\bigl(u,\partial_{\tau_{1}}u,\dots,\partial_{\tau_{n}}u\bigr).

The definition is independent of the positively oriented orthonormal frame. Since
|u|=1
a.e., one has

u\cdot\partial_{\tau_{i}}u=0\qquad\text{a.e. for }i=1,\dots,n.

Consequently, if
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
are the singular values of
\mathrm{D}u
, then

(2.2)

|\operatorname{Jac}u|=\prod_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\leq\left(\frac{s_{1}^{2}+\cdots+s_{n}^{2}}{n}\right)^{n/2}=n^{-n/2}|\mathrm{D}u|^{n}.

Thus
\operatorname{Jac}u\in L^{1}
and

(2.3)

\deg u:=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\operatorname{Jac}u\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

is well-defined.

For smooth functions
u
,
\deg u
coincides with one of the standard definitions of the Brouwer degree and, in particular, it is an integer. In
2.1
we recall that
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
is strongly dense in
W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
and that the degree is continuous under strong
W^{1,n}
convergence. It follows from these facts that
\deg u\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
. For further discussion of the degree and its properties, we refer to
[
BN95
,
BMS16
]
and references therein.

Proposition 2.1

(Critical density and classification)
.

The following hold:

(i)

C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
is strongly dense in
W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
;

(ii)

degree is continuous under strong
W^{1,n}
convergence;

(iii)

for
u,v\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
, we have
\deg u=\deg v
if and only if
u
and
v
lie in the same
W^{1,n}
path component;

(iv)

for every
u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
,

(2.4)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}u|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\geq\kappa_{n}|\deg u|.

Proof.

Assertion (i) for
n=2
is due to
[
SU83
]
. The same proof extends to
n\geq 3
, see
[
BL01
, Proposition A.2]
and also
[
BMS16
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Assertion (ii) is
[
BMS16
, Theorem 2.3]
. It also follows directly from the Jacobian formula for the degree, the multilinearity of the determinant, Hölder’s inequality, and dominated convergence.

For (iii),
[
BL01
, Proposition 0.3]
identifies the
W^{1,n}
homotopy classes with the ordinary continuous homotopy classes. By the classical Hopf classification, continuous maps
\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
are homotopic if and only if they have the same Brouwer degree. Together with (ii), this proves the equivalence.

For (iv), we observe that, by (
2.3
) and the Jacobian bound (
2.2
),

|\mathbb{S}^{n}|\,|\deg u|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\operatorname{Jac}u\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\operatorname{Jac}u|\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq n^{-n/2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}u|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.\qed

We will often make use of the radial projection map
\Pi
, defined by

\Pi(z):=\frac{z}{|z|},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}.

Its derivative is

(2.5)

\mathrm{D}\Pi(z)[A]=\frac{1}{|z|}\left(I-\frac{z}{|z|}\otimes\frac{z}{|z|}\right)A,

so

(2.6)

|\mathrm{D}\Pi(z)[A]|\leq\frac{|A|}{|z|}.

Lemma 2.2

(Uniformly close maps have the same degree)
.

Let
u,v\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
. If

\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|u-v|<2,

then
u
and
v
are homotopic in
W^{1,n}
. In particular,
\deg u=\deg v
.

Proof.

Set

w_{t}:=(1-t)u+tv\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{t}:=\Pi(w_{t})\,.

Since
|u|=|v|=1
, setting
c:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|u-v|
gives

|w_{t}|^{2}=1-t(1-t)|u-v|^{2}\geq 1-c^{2}/4>0\,,

so
H_{t}
is well-defined. On the set
\{|z|^{2}\geq 1-c^{2}/4\}
, both
\mathrm{D}\Pi
and
\mathrm{D}^{2}\Pi
are bounded by constants depending only on
c
. Therefore, for
s,t\in[0,1]
,

\|H_{t}-H_{s}\|_{L^{n}}\leq C_{c}|t-s|\|u-v\|_{L^{n}}

and, using the chain rule,

\|\mathrm{D}H_{t}-\mathrm{D}H_{s}\|_{L^{n}}\leq C_{c}|t-s|\bigl(\|\mathrm{D}u\|_{L^{n}}+\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{n}}\bigr).

Thus
t\mapsto H_{t}
is a continuous path in
W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
from
u
to
v
. In particular,
2.1
gives
\deg u=\deg v
.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Excision and additivity of degree)
.

Let
u,v\in C(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
, and suppose that
u=v
on the complement of open balls
B_{1},\dots,B_{I}
whose closures are pairwise disjoint. For each
i
, form a map
U_{i}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
by taking
u
on one copy of
\overline{B}_{i}
and
v
on a second copy of
\overline{B}_{i}
with the opposite orientation. The two pieces match on the boundary because
u=v
there. Put

q_{i}:=\deg U_{i}.

With this orientation convention,

(2.7)

\deg u-\deg v=\sum_{i=1}^{I}q_{i}.

Proof.

This is the standard additivity, or excision, property of the Brouwer degree. To see the sign convention concretely, first assume that the maps are smooth and that the common boundary values are regular enough so that one may compute degrees by signed counting of preimages of a regular value
y
that is not hit on the boundaries
\partial B_{i}
. The preimages of
y
outside the balls are the same for
u
and
v
and therefore cancel in
\deg u-\deg v
. Inside
B_{i}
, the remaining signed count is exactly the degree of the glued map
U_{i}
, because the second copy carries the opposite orientation. Summing over the balls gives (
2.7
). The general continuous case follows by uniform approximation and homotopy invariance of the degree.
∎

3.
The sharp bubble upper bound

Our goal in this section is to prove the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
, viz.,

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}_{\tau}f-\mathrm{D}_{\tau}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|\,.

This will be the content of
3.3
below.

The following proposition is, essentially,
[
DVS26
, Proposition 4.10]
. Since we need a slight refinement and since the method is interesting, we include a complete proof. We let
B^{n}
denote the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proposition 3.1

(Energy of a degree bubble)
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
p\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Among maps
b\in W^{1,n}(B^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
whose trace is the constant
p
and whose relative degree is
q
,

(3.1)

\inf_{b}\int_{B^{n}}|\mathrm{D}b|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\kappa_{n}|q|.

The same infimum is obtained if
b
is required to be smooth and constant in a neighborhood of
\partial B^{n}
.

Here and below the relative degree is understood as follows. If
b\in W^{1,n}(B^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
has trace equal to
p
, extend it by the constant value
p
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B^{n}
. This gives a map
\widetilde{b}\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
with finite
n
-energy. Compactifying
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by adding the point at infinity, equivalently precomposing
\widetilde{b}
with an orientation-preserving stereographic chart from
\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\{N\}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and setting the value at
N
equal to
p
, gives a map
\widehat{b}\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
. We define the relative degree of
b
to be
\deg\widehat{b}
. This is independent of the chosen orientation-preserving chart.

Proof.

Let
b
be admissible and let
\widehat{b}
be its compactification as above. Since stereographic compactification is conformal and the
n
-energy is conformally invariant,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}\widehat{b}|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}\widetilde{b}|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B^{n}}|\mathrm{D}b|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}x.

By
2.1
(iv) the left side is bounded from below by
\kappa_{n}|\deg\widehat{b}|=\kappa_{n}|\deg b|=\kappa_{n}|q|
, so we obtain that for any admissible
b
we have

\int_{B^{n}}|\mathrm{D}b|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\kappa_{n}|q|.

For the reverse inequality, let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\{p\}
be inverse stereographic projection, chosen so that its compactification has degree
1
and so that
\Phi(x)\to p
as
|x|\to\infty
. It is conformal, it has
n
non-zero singular values and they are all equal to
2/(1+|x|^{2})
. Hence

(3.2)

|\mathrm{D}\Phi(x)|^{n}=n^{n/2}\left(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\right)^{n},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}\Phi|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\kappa_{n}.

Choose
\chi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty);[0,1])
with
\chi=1
on
[0,1]
and
\chi=0
on
[2,\infty)
. Since
|\Phi(x)-p|=O(|x|^{-1})
and
|\mathrm{D}\Phi(x)|=O(|x|^{-2})
, we may choose
R
so large that
|\Phi(x)-p|\leq 1/2
for
|x|\geq R
. For
x\in B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}
, the vector
p+\chi(|x|/R)(\Phi(x)-p)
has norm at least
1/2
. Thus, we can set

\Phi_{R}(x):=\Pi\!\left(p+\chi(|x|/R)(\Phi(x)-p)\right)\qquad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.

Then
\Phi_{R}=\Phi
on
B_{R}
,
\Phi_{R}=p
outside
B_{2R}
and, on
B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}
,

|\mathrm{D}\Phi_{R}(x)|\leq CR^{-2}\,.

Consequently,

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}|\mathrm{D}\Phi_{R}|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\leq CR^{n}R^{-2n}=CR^{-n}\to 0\,.

Together with (
3.2
), this gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}\Phi_{R}|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\to\kappa_{n}.

Moreover, the homotopy

\Pi\!\left(p+((1-t)+t\chi(|x|/R))(\Phi(x)-p)\right),\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1,

is well-defined for large
R
, is equal to
p
near infinity, and connects
\Phi
to
\Phi_{R}
after compactification. Hence the compactification of
\Phi_{R}
has degree
1
.

Since
\Phi_{R}=p
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}
, the restriction of
\Phi_{R}
to
B_{3R}
is constant near
\partial B_{3R}
. After rescaling
B_{3R}
to
B^{n}
, conformal invariance under dilations gives a smooth degree-one map on
B^{n}
, constant near
\partial B^{n}
, whose energy tends to
\kappa_{n}
. Composing with an orientation-reversing target isometry that fixes
p
gives degree
-1
with the same boundary value and the same energy.

For
q\neq 0
, choose
|q|
pairwise disjoint balls compactly contained in
B^{n}
, place in each a rescaled copy of the degree-
\operatorname{sgn}q
construction, and set the map equal to
p
elsewhere. The map is smooth and constant near
\partial B^{n}
; by
Lemma

2.3
, its relative degree is
q
, and its energy is the sum of the energies of the copies. Letting the error of each copy tend to zero gives the upper bound
\kappa_{n}|q|
. The case
q=0
is given by the constant map.
∎

The following approximation result complements
2.1
(i) and is a special case of
[
BMS16
, Lemma 2.2]
. We include the simple proof to make our paper selfcontained.

Lemma 3.2

(Local flattening)
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
and
a\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For all sufficiently small
r>0
, there is
\widetilde{f}_{r}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
such that

(i)

\widetilde{f}_{r}=f(a)
on
B_{r}(a)
;

(ii)

\widetilde{f}_{r}=f
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(a)
;

(iii)

\deg\widetilde{f}_{r}=\deg f
;

(iv)

\|\mathrm{D}(\widetilde{f}_{r}-f)\|_{L^{n}}\to 0
as
r\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

Choose a smooth cutoff
\eta_{r}
with

\begin{gathered}0\leq\eta_{r}\leq 1,\qquad\eta_{r}=0\text{ on }B_{r}(a),\\
\eta_{r}=1\text{ outside }B_{2r}(a),\qquad|\mathrm{D}\eta_{r}|\leq C/r.\end{gathered}

Set
p:=f(a)
and
z_{r}:=p+\eta_{r}(f-p)
. Since
f
is smooth,
|f(x)-p|\leq Cr
on
B_{2r}(a)
; hence
|z_{r}|\geq 1-Cr\geq 1/2
for all sufficiently small
r>0
. Define
\widetilde{f}_{r}:=\Pi(z_{r})
. Properties (i) and (ii) are immediate.

For (iii), set

H_{t}(x):=\Pi\bigl(p+((1-t)+t\eta_{r}(x))(f(x)-p)\bigr),\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1.

The vector inside
\Pi
has norm at least
1-Cr\geq 1/2
, so
H
is a smooth homotopy with
H_{0}=f
,
H_{1}=\widetilde{f}_{r}
, and
H_{t}=f
outside
B_{2r}(a)
. Therefore, either by classical theory or by
2.1
, the degree is unchanged.

On
B_{2r}(a)
,

\mathrm{D}z_{r}=\eta_{r}\mathrm{D}f+(f-p)\otimes\mathrm{D}\eta_{r},

and therefore

|\mathrm{D}z_{r}|\leq|\mathrm{D}f|+|f-p||\mathrm{D}\eta_{r}|\leq C,

with
C
independent of
r
. By (
2.6
),
|\mathrm{D}\widetilde{f}_{r}|\leq 2C
there. Hence

|\mathrm{D}(\widetilde{f}_{r}-f)|\leq|\mathrm{D}\widetilde{f}_{r}|+|\mathrm{D}f|\leq C\quad\text{on }B_{2r}(a).

Outside
B_{2r}(a)
the two maps agree, so

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}(\widetilde{f}_{r}-f)|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq C|B_{2r}(a)|\to 0.\qed

Proposition 3.3

(Sharp upper bound)
.

For every
n\geq 2
,
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}
,

(3.3)

\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{D}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|.

Proof.

Assume first that
f
is smooth. Fix
a\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
p:=f(a)
. By
Lemma

3.2
, for all sufficiently small
r>0
there is
\widetilde{f}_{r}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
with
\deg\widetilde{f}_{r}=d_{1}
, with
\widetilde{f}_{r}=p
on
B_{r}(a)
and
\widetilde{f}_{r}=f
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus B_{2r}(a)
and such that
\|\mathrm{D}(f-\widetilde{f}_{r})\|_{L^{n}}\to 0
. Choose an orientation-preserving conformal diffeomorphism
\psi:B_{r}(a)\to B^{n}
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. Conformal invariance of the
n
-energy and
3.1
imply that there is

b_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{B^{n}};\mathbb{S}^{n})

that is constant equal to
p
near
\partial B^{n}
, has relative degree
d_{2}-d_{1}
, and satisfies

\int_{B^{n}}|\mathrm{D}b_{0}|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|+\varepsilon.

Set
b:=b_{0}\circ\psi
. Then
b
is smooth, is constant equal to
p
near
\partial B_{r}(a)
, has relative degree
d_{2}-d_{1}
, and

\int_{B_{r}(a)}|\mathrm{D}b|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|+\varepsilon.

Define
G:=b
on
B_{r}(a)
and
G:=\widetilde{f}_{r}
outside. This piecewise map is continuous, and the one-ball case of
Lemma

2.3
gives
\deg G=\deg\widetilde{f}_{r}+d_{2}-d_{1}=d_{2}
. Moreover,

\|\mathrm{D}(\widetilde{f}_{r}-G)\|_{L^{n}}^{n}=\int_{B_{r}(a)}|\mathrm{D}b|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

The
L^{n}
triangle inequality yields

\|\mathrm{D}(f-G)\|_{L^{n}}\leq\|\mathrm{D}(f-\widetilde{f}_{r})\|_{L^{n}}+(\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|+\varepsilon)^{1/n}.

It follows that

\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{D}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\left(\|\mathrm{D}(f-\widetilde{f}_{r})\|_{L^{n}}+(\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|+\varepsilon)^{1/n}\right)^{n}.

Letting
r\downarrow 0
and then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we obtain the claimed inequality (
3.3
) for smooth
f
.

For general
f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}
, choose smooth
f_{j}
with
f_{j}\to f
strongly in
W^{1,n}
. In particular, for all large
j
,
\deg f_{j}=d_{1}
. For such
j
, choose
g_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}
such that

\|\mathrm{D}(f_{j}-g_{j})\|_{L^{n}}\leq(\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|+1/j)^{1/n}.

Then

\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\|\mathrm{D}(f-g)\|_{L^{n}}\leq\|\mathrm{D}(f-f_{j})\|_{L^{n}}+(\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|+1/j)^{1/n}.

Letting
j\to\infty
proves (
3.3
).
∎

4.
Atomic bubbling and the topological defect

An important ingredient in our proof of the lower bound is the following specialization of the bubbling theorem of Detaille and Van Schaftingen
[
DVS26
]
.

Proposition 4.1

(Sphere-valued form of the DVS bubbling theorem)
.

Let
u_{j}\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
satisfy

\deg u_{j}=d_{2},\qquad u_{j}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}),\qquad u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n}),\quad\deg u=d_{1}.

Suppose

|\mathrm{D}u_{j}|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}}\mu

as finite Radon measures. Then there are finitely many points
a_{1},\dots,a_{I}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

(4.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mu(\{a_{i}\})\geq\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|.

Proof.

Choose a fixed smooth map
v:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
\deg v=d_{2}
. Since
\deg u_{j}=\deg v=d_{2}
, Proposition
2.1

gives a
W^{1,n}
-homotopy between
u_{j}
and
v
. Since

W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{VMO}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
continuously, this is also
a
\mathrm{VMO}
-homotopy. Therefore the homotopy-class hypothesis in

[
DVS26
, Theorem 5.1]
is satisfied. Apply
[
DVS26
, Theorem 5.1]
with

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}=\mathbb{S}^{n},\qquad m=n,\qquad v_{j}=u_{j}.

The hypotheses are exactly the
L^{1}
convergence above, the common
\mathrm{VMO}
homotopy class of the sequence, and the assumed weak-star convergence of the energy measures. The theorem yields finitely many points
a_{1},\dots,a_{I}
such that, for every sufficiently small common radius
r>0
, there is a continuous map
w:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
satisfying

(i)

w
is homotopic to
u
, and hence
\deg w=d_{1}
;

(ii)

w=v
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{I}B_{r}(a_{i})
;

(iii)

(4.2)

\mu\geq|\mathrm{D}u|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{top}}^{1,n}([w,v,B_{r}(a_{i})])\,\delta_{a_{i}}.

Here
[w,v,B_{r}(a_{i})]\in\pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
is the local disparity in the convention of
[
DVS26
, Section 4.1]
, that is, the homotopy class of maps from
\mathbb{S}^{n}
to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
that are homotopic
to a map given by
w|_{B_{r}(a_{i})}
on the northern hemisphere and by
v|_{B_{r}(a_{i})}
on the southern one. We do not need to recall the definition of the energy functional
\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{top}}^{1,n}
, because the equality (
4.4
) below is all we need.

The balls can be chosen with pairwise disjoint closures. Let

q_{i}:=\deg[w,v,B_{r}(a_{i})]\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since
w=v
outside the union of the balls,
Lemma

2.3
gives

(4.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{I}q_{i}=\deg w-\deg v=d_{1}-d_{2}.

By
[
DVS26
, Proposition 4.10]
(which is essentially
3.1
), the topological energy of a sphere-valued disparity is

(4.4)

\mathfrak{E}_{\mathrm{top}}^{1,n}([w,v,B_{r}(a_{i})])=\kappa_{n}|q_{i}|.

Taking the mass of (
4.2
) at the points and using (
4.3
),

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mu(\{a_{i}\})\geq\kappa_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{I}|q_{i}|\geq\kappa_{n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{I}q_{i}\right|=\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|.\qed

5.
The oscillatory pinning construction

Our goal in this section is to construct a sequence of maps of a given degree that are hard to approximate in the sense of Proposition
5.5
. The construction is analogous in spirit to the one-dimensional zig-zag constructions in
[
BMS16
,
BMS18
]
and
[
Sha18
]
, but the higher-dimensional mechanism is different: we rapidly alternate among finitely many small target rotations whose orbits affinely span the ambient space. For this reason we will refer to the mechanism as oscillatory pinning.

5.1.
A uniform affine-spanning family of small rotations

Set
m:=n+1
and let
\mathfrak{so}(m)
denote the set of all traceless, skew-symmetric, real
m\times m
-matrices. Let
(e_{a})_{a=1}^{m}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For
1\leq a<b\leq m
, define
A_{ab}\in\mathfrak{so}(m)
by

(5.1)

A_{ab}e_{a}:=e_{b},\qquad A_{ab}e_{b}:=-e_{a},

and

A_{ab}e_{c}=0\quad\text{if}\ c\notin\{a,b\}\,.

Fix
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and put

(5.2)

Q_{0}:=I,\qquad Q_{ab}^{\pm}:=\exp(\pm\theta A_{ab}),

with

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}:=\{Q_{0}\}\cup\{Q_{ab}^{+},Q_{ab}^{-}:1\leq a<b\leq m\}.

We emphasize that the matrices in
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
are orthogonal. It will be important later that the angle
\theta
may be arbitrarily small, so the whole family may be chosen uniformly close to the identity.

Lemma 5.1

(Uniform affine spanning)
.

For every
p\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the set
\{Qp:Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\}
affinely spans
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Proof.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and suppose
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a linear functional that is constant on
\{Qp:Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\}
. We need to show that
\xi=0
, because this means that the set
\{Qp:Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\}
is not contained in any proper affine hyperplane, so it is affine spanning.

Since
I\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
, the constancy of
\xi
implies that

(5.3)

\xi\cdot Qp=\xi\cdot p\qquad\text{for all}\ Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\,.

Subtracting the equations for
Q_{ab}^{+}
and
Q_{ab}^{-}
gives

0=\xi\cdot(Q_{ab}^{+}-Q_{ab}^{-})p=2\sin\theta\,\xi\cdot A_{ab}p.

The vectors
A_{ab}p
span
p^{\perp}
: the matrices
A_{ab}
span
\mathfrak{so}(m)
, and for
z\perp p
the skew matrix
z\otimes p-p\otimes z
sends
p
to
z
. Hence
\xi=\lambda p
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Note that

Q_{ab}^{+}+Q_{ab}^{-}-2I=2(\cos\theta-1)P_{ab},

where
P_{ab}
projects onto
\operatorname{span}\{e_{a},e_{b}\}
. Inserting this into (
5.3
) gives

0=2(\cos\theta-1)\lambda\,p\cdot P_{ab}p\,.

Since
p\neq 0
, there is a pair
(a,b)
with
p_{a}^{2}+p_{b}^{2}=p\cdot P_{ab}p>0
. This, together with
\cos\theta\neq 1
, leads to the desired conclusion
\lambda=0
and therefore
\xi=0
.
∎

5.2.
Periodic averaging on the sphere

In this subsection we derive a simple homogenization result on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
that will be useful in the next subsection.

Lemma 5.2

(One-dimensional periodic averaging)
.

Let
a\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be one-periodic and
\bar{a}:=\int_{0}^{1}a(s)\,\,\mathrm{d}s
. Then

a(jt)\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}}\bar{a}\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}((-1,1))

as
j\to\infty
through positive integers.

Proof.

Replacing
a
by
a-\bar{a}
, we may assume that
a
has mean-value zero. Let

A(t):=\int_{0}^{t}a(s)\,\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
a
is one-periodic and has mean zero,
A
is one-periodic. Clearly, it is bounded. For
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}((-1,1))
, integration by parts gives

\int_{-1}^{1}a(jt)\varphi(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t=-\frac{1}{j}\int_{-1}^{1}A(jt)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t\to 0.

Uniform
L^{\infty}
boundedness and density of
C_{c}^{1}
in
L^{1}
prove the weak-star convergence.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Periodic averaging on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
)
.

Under the assumptions of
Lemma

5.2
,

(5.4)

a(jx_{n+1})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}}\bar{a}\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n}).

Proof.

We parametrize
x=(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\,\omega,t)
with
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
-1<t<1
. For
\Phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\Phi(x)a(jx_{n+1})\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(x)=\int_{-1}^{1}a(jt)G_{\Phi}(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t

with

G_{\Phi}(t):=(1-t^{2})^{(n-2)/2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Phi(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\,\omega,t)\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n-1}(\omega).

Moreover,

\|G_{\Phi}\|_{L^{1}(-1,1)}\leq\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}

and

\int_{-1}^{1}G_{\Phi}(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\Phi\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Thus,
Lemma

5.2
gives the claimed weak-star convergence.
∎

5.3.
Oscillatory pinning

We recall that the family
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
of orthogonal matrices was defined in Subsection
5.1
.

Lemma 5.4

(Smooth periodic rotation profile)
.

Fix
\rho>0
. There are a nonzero sufficiently small angle
\theta
, a smooth one-periodic map
B:\mathbb{R}\to\mathfrak{so}(n+1)
, and pairwise disjoint intervals
I_{Q}\subset(0,1)
of positive length, indexed by
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
, such that

(i)

\exp(B(t))=Q
for
t\in I_{Q}
;

(ii)

|\exp(B(t))p-p|\leq\rho
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Proof.

Choose

0<\theta<\min\{\pi,\log(1+\rho)\}.

For
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
, define

R_{Q_{0}}:=0,\qquad R_{Q_{ab}^{\pm}}:=\pm\theta A_{ab},

so that
\exp(R_{Q})=Q
. Let

\mathcal{R}_{\theta}:=\{0\}\cup\{\pm\theta A_{ab}:1\leq a<b\leq n+1\}.

If
A\in\operatorname{conv}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}
, then
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\theta
. Consequently,

\|\exp(A)-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq e^{\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}-1\leq e^{\theta}-1<\rho.

Construct a smooth closed curve

B:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\operatorname{conv}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}

which visits every vertex
R_{Q}
and is constant equal to
R_{Q}
on a nondegenerate interval
I_{Q}\subset(0,1)
. This can be done by joining consecutive vertices by line segments parametrized with smooth cutoff functions that are constant near their endpoints. Start and finish at
0
, with
B=0
near both
0
and
1
, so that the periodic extension is smooth. Then
\exp(B(t))=Q
on
I_{Q}
, and for every
p\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,

|\exp(B(t))p-p|\leq\|\exp(B(t))-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\rho.\qed

The following is the main result of this section.

Proposition 5.5

(Oscillatory pinning)
.

Let
f_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
and
\rho>0
. There is a sequence
F_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
such that

(5.5)

\deg F_{j}=\deg f_{0},\qquad\|F_{j}-f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\rho,

and with the following property: if
g_{j}\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
and

(5.6)

\sup_{j}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g_{j})|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma<\infty,

then

(5.7)

F_{j}-g_{j}\to 0\quad\text{strongly in }L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).

Proof.

Let
\theta
and
B
be as in
Lemma

5.4
, and define

(5.8)

F_{j}(x):=\exp(B(jx_{n+1}))f_{0}(x).

The claimed
L^{\infty}
estimate on
F_{j}-f_{0}
follows from
Lemma

5.4
(ii). The maps

H_{s}(x):=\exp(sB(jx_{n+1}))f_{0}(x),\qquad 0\leq s\leq 1,

form a smooth homotopy from
f_{0}
to
F_{j}
, so the degrees of
F_{j}
and
f_{0}
agree.

Let
g_{j}\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
satisfy (
5.6
) and put

h_{j}:=F_{j}-g_{j}.

Because both maps are sphere-valued,

(5.9)

|h_{j}|\leq 2

and

(5.10)

2F_{j}\cdot h_{j}=|h_{j}|^{2}\qquad\text{a.e.}

The bounds (
5.6
) and (
5.9
) make
(h_{j})
bounded in
W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
.

We are going to show that
h_{j}\to 0
strongly in
L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, and we are going to show this by proving that any subsequence has a further subsequence that converges strongly to zero.

In order to simplify the presentation, we do not reflect the choice of a subsequence in the notation. Since
n\geq 2
, Rellich’s compactness theorem gives, after taking a subsequence,

(5.11)

h_{j}\to h\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})

and we need to show that
h=0
.

Fix
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
and let
\chi_{Q}
be the one-periodic extension of the characteristic function of
I_{Q}
. Where
\chi_{Q}(jx_{n+1})=1
, one has
F_{j}=Qf_{0}
, and therefore

(5.12)

\chi_{Q}(jx_{n+1})\bigl(2Qf_{0}\cdot h_{j}-|h_{j}|^{2}\bigr)=0.

By
Lemma

5.3
,

(5.13)

\chi_{Q}(jx_{n+1})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}}|I_{Q}|\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n}).

Also, (
5.11
) implies

(5.14)

2Qf_{0}\cdot h_{j}-|h_{j}|^{2}\to 2Qf_{0}\cdot h-|h|^{2}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{1}.

Indeed,

\||h_{j}|^{2}-|h|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|h_{j}-h\|_{L^{2}}\|h_{j}+h\|_{L^{2}}\to 0.

Testing (
5.12
) against an arbitrary
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and using (
5.13
) and (
5.14
), we obtain

|I_{Q}|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\varphi\,\bigl(2Qf_{0}\cdot h-|h|^{2}\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=0.

Since
|I_{Q}|>0
, this implies

(5.15)

2Qf_{0}\cdot h=|h|^{2}\qquad\text{a.e.~on}\ \mathbb{S}^{n}\,.

This holds for every
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
.

Since
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is finite, there is a set of full measure on which (
5.15
) holds for all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
. At such a point
x
, if
h(x)\neq 0
, then every vector
Qf_{0}(x)
lies in the proper affine hyperplane

\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:y\cdot h(x)=\frac{|h(x)|^{2}}{2}\right\}.

This contradicts
Lemma

5.1
. Hence
h=0
a.e.

We have shown that every subsequence of
(h_{j})
has a further subsequence converging to zero strongly in
L^{2}
. Therefore the full sequence converges to zero in
L^{2}
. Since
|h_{j}|\leq 2
,

\|h_{j}\|_{L^{n}}^{n}\leq 2^{n-2}\|h_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to 0,

which proves (
5.7
).
∎

6.
Proof of the sharp lower bound

In this section we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
by proving that

(6.1)

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{D}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\geq\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|\,.

We fix
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and assume
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
, for otherwise there is nothing to prove.

Our proof will depend on a parameter
0<\rho<1
that will tend to zero at the very end of the argument. Also, we fix a smooth map
f_{0}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
\deg f_{0}=d_{1}
. Applying
5.5
we obtain
F_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n})
with

(6.2)

\deg F_{j}=d_{1},\qquad\|F_{j}-f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\rho

and with the property that (
5.6
) implies (
5.7
).

For each
j
, let

(6.3)

M_{j}:=\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g)|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

and set

L:=\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}M_{\ell}\,.

After passing to a subsequence we may assume that

(6.4)

M_{j}\to L\,.

By
3.3
,

(6.5)

0\leq M_{j}\leq\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|\,.

Choose
g_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}
such that

(6.6)

0\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g_{j})|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma-M_{j}\to 0\,.

It follows from (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) that
F_{j}-g_{j}
is bounded in
W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and therefore, by the construction of
F_{j}
(see
5.5
), we conclude that

(6.7)

F_{j}-g_{j}\to 0\quad\text{strongly in }L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n}).

After passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

(6.8)

|\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g_{j})|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}}\nu

as finite Radon measures. Testing against
1
and using (
6.4
) and (
6.6
) gives

(6.9)

\nu(\mathbb{S}^{n})=L.

6.1.
Removing the oscillation and preserving degree

Define

(6.10)

z_{j}:=f_{0}-F_{j}+g_{j}.

Then, by (
6.2
) and since
|g_{j}|=1
,

(6.11)

|z_{j}-g_{j}|\leq\rho,\qquad|z_{j}|\geq 1-\rho>0.

Define

(6.12)

u_{j}:=\Pi(z_{j})=\frac{z_{j}}{|z_{j}|}\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{S}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n}).

We have, using
|g_{j}|=1
and the first bound in (
6.11
),

\displaystyle|u_{j}-g_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq|u_{j}-z_{j}|+|z_{j}-g_{j}|=\bigl|1-|z_{j}|\bigr|+|z_{j}-g_{j}|

(6.13)

\displaystyle=\bigl||g_{j}|-|z_{j}|\bigr|+|z_{j}-g_{j}|\leq 2|z_{j}-g_{j}|\leq 2\rho<2\,.

Thus, by
Lemma

2.2
,

(6.14)

\deg u_{j}=\deg g_{j}=d_{2}.

Using
|f_{0}|=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|u_{j}-f_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq|u_{j}-z_{j}|+|z_{j}-f_{0}|=|1-|z_{j}||+|z_{j}-f_{0}|

(6.15)

\displaystyle=||f_{0}|-|z_{j}||+|z_{j}-f_{0}|\leq 2|z_{j}-f_{0}|\leq 2|F_{j}-g_{j}|\,.

Thus, by (
6.7
),

(6.16)

u_{j}\to f_{0}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\,.

From (
2.6
) and the second bound in (
6.11
),

(6.17)

|\mathrm{D}u_{j}|\leq\frac{|\mathrm{D}z_{j}|}{1-\rho}=\frac{|\mathrm{D}f_{0}-\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g_{j})|}{1-\rho}.

Hence
(u_{j})
is bounded in
W^{1,n}
. Passing to another subsequence,

(6.18)

|\mathrm{D}u_{j}|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}}\mu

as finite Radon measures.

6.2.
Comparison of atomic energy

By (
6.14
), (
6.16
), and
4.1
, there are points
a_{1},\dots,a_{I}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

(6.19)

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mu(\{a_{i}\})\geq\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|\,.

Fix
\delta>0
. It is elementary to observe that there is a
C_{\delta}<\infty
such that for any matrices
A,B
one has

(6.20)

|A+B|^{n}\leq(1+\delta)|A|^{p}+C_{n,\delta}|B|^{n}\,.

Applying this to (
6.17
) with
A=-\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g_{j})
and
B=\mathrm{D}f_{0}
gives

(6.21)

|\mathrm{D}u_{j}|^{n}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{n}}\left((1+\delta)|\mathrm{D}(F_{j}-g_{j})|^{n}+C_{n,\delta}|\mathrm{D}f_{0}|^{n}\right).

Passing to the weak star limit we obtain the measure inequality

(6.22)

\mu\leq\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{n}}\left((1+\delta)\nu+C_{n,\delta}|\mathrm{D}f_{0}|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right).

By Radon regularity, this inequality holds on all Borel sets, in particular on singletons. The last measure is absolutely continuous and has no atoms. Therefore, for every
a\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,

\mu(\{a\})\leq\frac{1+\delta}{(1-\rho)^{n}}\nu(\{a\})

and, after letting
\delta\downarrow 0
,

(6.23)

\mu(\{a\})\leq\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{n}}\nu(\{a\}).

Combining (
6.19
), (
6.23
), and (
6.9
),

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mu(\{a_{i}\})\leq\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\nu(\{a_{i}\})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{n}}\nu(\mathbb{S}^{n})=\frac{L}{(1-\rho)^{n}}.

Thus

(6.24)

L\geq(1-\rho)^{n}\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|\,.

Since every
F_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}
, we have

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{D}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\geq M_{j}\,.

Taking the liminf as
j\to\infty
, we obtain

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{D}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\geq L\,.

This, combined with (
6.24
) gives

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{E}_{d_{2}}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{D}g|^{n}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\geq(1-\rho)^{n}\kappa_{n}\,|d_{1}-d_{2}|\,.

Since
\rho>0
is arbitrary, we obtain the claimed inequality (
6.1
).
Together with
3.3
, this proves
Theorem

1.1
.
