Recently, Cabrera-Martínez et al.

Theorem 1.1
.

[

Additional notation and terminology, and some useful tools

We use the notation
P_{r}
to denote the path graph of order
r
. Given a graph
G
, by
attaching
a path
P_{r}
to a vertex
v_{i}\in V(G)
we mean adding the path
P_{r}
and joining
v_{i}
by an edge to a leaf of
P_{r}
. Given a set
S\subset V(G)
,
G-S
denotes the graph obtained from
G
by removing all vertices in
S
together with all edges incident to them. Next, we define some sets that are relevant to our work.

•

\mathcal{S}_{s}(G)=\{v_{i}\in\mathcal{S}(G):|N_{G}(v_{i})\cap\mathcal{L}(G)|\geq 2\}
is the set of
strong support vertices
of
G
.

•

\mathcal{L}_{s}(G)=\{v_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(G):|N_{G}(v_{i})\cap\mathcal{S}_{s}(G)|=1\}
is the set of
strong leaves
of
G
.

•

\mathcal{L}_{w}(G)=\mathcal{L}(G)\setminus\mathcal{L}_{s}(G)
is the set of
weak leaves
of
G
.

•

\mathcal{SS}(G)=\{v_{i}\in V(G)\setminus(\mathcal{S}(G)\cup\mathcal{L}(G)):|N_{G}(v_{i})\cap\mathcal{S}(G)|\geq 1\}
is the set of
semi-support vertices
of
G
.

•

\mathcal{N}_{SS}(G)=\{v_{i}\in V(G)\setminus(\mathcal{SS}(G)\cup\mathcal{S}(G)):|N_{G}(v_{i})\cap\mathcal{SS}(G)|\geq 1\}
is the set of vertices adjacent to semi-support vertices of
G
that are neither semi-support nor support vertices.

. The

Lemma 1.2
.

[
5
]

For any tree
T
of order at least three, there exists a
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))
-set
D
such that
\mathcal{S}(T)\cup\mathcal{SS}(T)\subseteq D
.

Lemma 1.3
.

The following statements hold for any nontrivial tree
T
.

(i)

If
T
is obtained from any nontrivial tree
T^{\prime}
by attaching a path
P_{1}
to any vertex in
\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})
, then
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+1
.

(ii)

If
T
is obtained from any nontrivial tree
T^{\prime}
by attaching a path
P_{2}
to any vertex in
\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{SS}(T^{\prime})
, then
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+2
.

(iii)

If
T
is obtained from any nontrivial tree
T^{\prime}
by attaching a path
P_{3}
to any vertex in
\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{L}_{w}(T^{\prime})
, then
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+4
.

(iv)

If
T
is obtained from any nontrivial tree
T^{\prime}
by identifying the central vertex of the tree
Q_{r}
with a vertex in
\mathcal{L}_{w}(T^{\prime})
, then
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+4r
.

Proof.

If
n(T^{\prime})=2
, then
T^{\prime}\cong P_{2}
, and it is straightforward to verify that (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) hold. Hence, we consider from now on that
n(T^{\prime})\geq 3
.

First, we assume that
T
is obtained from
T^{\prime}
by adding the vertex
v_{1}
and the edge
v_{1}v_{k}
, where
v_{k}\in\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})
. By Lemma
1.2
, there exists a
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))
-set
D
containing vertex
v_{k}
. Observe that
D\cup\{v^{1,k}\}
is a TOIDS of
\mathtt{S}(T)
. Hence,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))\leq|D\cup\{v^{1,k}\}|=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+1
. Let
B
be a
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))
-set which satisfies the condition given in Lemma
1.2
. This implies that
v_{k},v^{1,k}\in B
. Since
v_{k}\in\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})
, there exists a vertex
v_{l}\in N_{T^{\prime}}(v_{k})\cap\mathcal{L}(T^{\prime})
. Hence,
v^{k,l}\in B
, which implies that
B\setminus\{v^{1,k}\}
is a TOIDS of
\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime})
. Thus,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))\leq|B\setminus\{v^{1,k}\}|=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))-1
. Therefore,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+1
, which completes the proof of (i).

Next, we assume that
T
is obtained from
T^{\prime}
by adding the path
v_{1}v_{2}
and the edge
v_{1}v_{k}
, where
v_{k}\in\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{SS}(T^{\prime})
. By Lemma
1.2
, there exists a
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))
-set
D^{\prime}
containing vertex
v_{k}
. Observe that
D^{\prime}\cup\{v_{1},v^{1,2}\}
is a TOIDS of
\mathtt{S}(T)
. Hence,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))\leq|D^{\prime}\cup\{v_{1},v^{1,2}\}|=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+2
. Let
B^{\prime}
be a
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))
-set which satisfies the condition given in Lemma
1.2
, and without loss of generality, assume that
|B^{\prime}\cap\{v_{k},v^{1,k},v_{1},v^{1,2},v_{2}\}|
is minimum. Since
v_{1},v_{k}\in\mathcal{S}(T)\cup\mathcal{SS}(T)
, it follows that
v_{1},v_{k}\in B^{\prime}
. Moreover, by the minimality of
|B^{\prime}\cap\{v_{k},v^{1,k},v_{1},v^{1,2},v_{2}\}|
, we have that
v^{1,2}\in B^{\prime}
and
v^{1,k},v_{2}\notin B^{\prime}
. This implies that
B^{\prime}\setminus\{v_{1},v^{1,2}\}
is a TOIDS of
\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime})
, and as a consequence,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))\leq\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))-2
. Therefore,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+2
, which completes the proof of (ii).

Now, assume that
T
is obtained from
T^{\prime}
by adding the path
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}
and the edge
v_{1}v_{k}
, where
v_{k}\in\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{L}_{w}(T^{\prime})
. Note that any
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))
-set
D^{\prime\prime}
can be extended to a TOIDS of
\mathtt{S}(T)
by adding the set
\{v^{1,k},v_{1},v_{2},v^{2,3}\}
. Hence,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))\leq|D^{\prime\prime}\cup\{v^{1,k},v_{1},v_{2},v^{2,3}\}|=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+4
. Let
B^{\prime\prime}
be a
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))
-set which satisfies the condition given in Lemma
1.2
, and without loss of generality, assume that
|B^{\prime\prime}\cap\{v_{k},v^{1,k},v_{1},v^{1,2},v_{2},v^{2,3},v_{3}\}|
is minimum. Since

, which leads to

and

By the minimality of

and the fact that

. This implies that

is a TOIDS of

, and as a consequence,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))\leq\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))-4
. Therefore,
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+4
, which completes the proof of (iii).

Finally, assume that
T
is obtained from
T^{\prime}
by identifying the central vertex
v_{1}
of the tree

with a vertex

. Let

be the support vertex adjacent to

in

, that is,

. To facilitate the proof, we first define the structure of

. Let

\mathcal{N}_{SS}(Q_{r})=\{v_{1}\}

Let

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
T
be a tree different from
P_{5}
such that
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\frac{4n(T)-l(T)+s(T)-2}{3}
. If
\mathcal{L}_{s}(T)=\emptyset
, then
|N_{T}(v_{k})\cap\mathcal{S}(T)|=1
for every vertex
v_{k}\in\mathcal{SS}(T)
.

Proof.

Let
v_{k}\in\mathcal{SS}(T)
. By definition, we have that
N_{T}(v_{k})\cap\mathcal{S}(T)\neq\emptyset
. Suppose that there exist
v_{1},v_{2}\in N_{T}(v_{k})\cap\mathcal{S}(T)
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{s}(T)=\emptyset
, we define
N_{T}(v_{i})\cap\mathcal{L}(T)=\{v_{i+2}\}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Let
T^{\prime}=T-\{v_{1},v_{3}\}
. Observe that
T^{\prime}\not\cong P_{3}
, since
T\not\cong P_{5}
. Hence,
n(T)=n(T^{\prime})+2
,
l(T)=l(T^{\prime})+1
and
s(T)=s(T^{\prime})+1
. Now, let
D
be a
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))
-set which satisfies the condition given in Lemma
1.2
. Since
v_{k}\in\mathcal{SS}(T^{\prime})
, it follows that
v_{k}\in D
. As a consequence,
D\cup\{v_{1},v^{1,3}\}
is a TOIDS of
\mathtt{S}(T)
, which implies that
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))\leq\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+2
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))\geq\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))-2

\displaystyle=\tfrac{4n(T)-l(T)+s(T)-2}{3}-2

\displaystyle=\tfrac{4(n(T^{\prime})+2)-(l(T^{\prime})+1)+(s(T^{\prime})+1)-2}{3}-2>\tfrac{4n(T^{\prime})-l(T^{\prime})+s(T^{\prime})-2}{3},

which contradicts the upper bound given in Theorem
1.1
. Hence,
|N_{T}(v_{k})\cap\mathcal{S}(T)|=1
, which completes the proof.
∎

T

To characterize the trees attaining the lower bound given in Theorem
1.1
, we introduce the following family of trees. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the family of trees
T
that can be obtained from a sequence of trees
T_{0},\ldots,T_{k}=T
, with

T_{0}=P_{2}
. If

, then for each

, the tree
T_{i}
can be obtained from the tree
T^{\prime}=T_{i-1}
by one of the following three operations defined below.

Operation
F_{1}
:

Attach a path
P_{1}
to a vertex in
\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})
.

Operation
F_{2}
:

Attach a path
P_{2}
to a vertex in
\mathcal{S}(T^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{SS}(T^{\prime})
.

Operation
F_{3}
:

Attach a path
P_{3}
to a vertex in
\mathcal{L}_{w}(T^{\prime})
.

We first show that every tree

satisfies that
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\frac{4n(T)-l(T)-s(T)}{3}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T
be a nontrivial tree. If
T\in\mathcal{F}
, then
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\frac{4n(T)-l(T)-s(T)}{3}
.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tree belonging to the family
\mathcal{F}
. We proceed to prove that
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=(4n(T)-l(T)-s(T))/3
by induction on the order of

. If

, then

and
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=2=(4n(T)-l(T)-s(T))/3
, as required. This particular case establishes the base case. Suppose that
n(T)\geq 3
and that every tree

in
\mathcal{F}
, with

, satisfies that
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{*}))=(4n(T^{*})-l(T^{*})-s(T^{*}))/3
. Since

, it is clear that
T
can be obtained from a sequence of trees

, with
T_{0}=P_{2}
and

. Let

, which implies that

, and by induction hypothesis, it follows that
\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))=(4n(T^{\prime})-l(T^{\prime})-s(T^{\prime}))/3
. We consider the following three cases, depending on which operation is used to obtain the tree

from

.

Case 1:

is obtained from
T^{\prime}
by Operation
F_{1}
. In this case,

is formed by adding a vertex

and the edge

. By Lemma
1.3
-(i), the induction hypothesis, and the relations

, and

\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T))=\gamma_{t}^{oi}(\mathtt{S}(T^{\prime}))+1=\tfrac{4n(T^{\prime})-l(T^{\prime})-s(T^{\prime})}{3}+1=\tfrac{4(n(T)-1)-(l(T)-1)-s(T)}{3}+1=\tfrac{4n(T)-l(T)-s(T)}{3}.

Case 2:

by Operation

. In this case,

and the edge

. By Lemma
1.3
-(ii), the induction hypothesis, and the relations

,
s(T^{\prime})=s(T)-1
, and
