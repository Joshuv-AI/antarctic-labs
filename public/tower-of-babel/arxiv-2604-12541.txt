Exotic Hopf maps, weight shifting and applications to vector bundles
Jean Fasel, William Hornslien

Abstract
Using motivic homotopy theory we produce several explicit polynomial representatives of the suspension of the Hopf map defined over ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} . We derive from this computation an explicit rank 2 2 vector bundle on the Jouanolou device of ℙ ℤ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}} .



1
Introduction

This paper can be viewed as an explicit version of some results in
[
2
]
, where the problem of producing complex polynomial representatives of homotopy groups of spheres was considered. Recall that P. Baum proved in
[
13
]
that any element of the
stable
homotopy groups of spheres can be represented by an admissible quadratic map, i.e., a real polynomial map of degree at most
2
. However, Wood later observed
[
26
]
that there are no non-constant polynomial maps
S^{n}\to S^{m}
for
n\geq 2m
, showing that Baum’s result fails in the unstable range. This led to the study of complex polynomial representatives, using the fact that the complex algebraic sphere
S_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, viewed as a manifold, is homotopy equivalent to the real sphere
S^{n}
. At present, no element of a homotopy group of spheres is known that cannot be represented by such a complex polynomial map.

In
[
2
, Proposition 2.2.1]
, it was shown that the suspension of the Hopf map
S^{4}\to S^{3}
and the square of the Hopf map
S^{5}\to S^{3}
admit complex polynomial representatives. The method relied on motivic homotopy theory and a weight-shifting procedure that we briefly recall. If
k
is a field and
n\geq 1
, consider the smooth quadrics
Q_{2n-1}:=\{\sum x_{i}y_{i}=1\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2n}_{k}
(with coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
) and
Q_{2n}:=\{\sum x_{i}y_{i}=z(1-z)\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{2n+1}_{k}
(with coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z
). It is well known (e.g.
[
1
]
) that these quadrics model motivic spheres in the (unstable) motivic homotopy category
\mathcal{H}(k)
of Morel–Voevodsky
[
21
]
. Moreover, for any smooth affine
k
-scheme
X
, the map
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sch}_{k}}(X,Q_{i})\to[X,Q_{i}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}(k)}(X,Q_{i})
is surjective for
i\geq 1
(
[
9
, Theorem 4.2.1]
,
[
10
, Corollary 3.1.1]
).

If
k\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, the complex realization functor
[
21
, §3.3]

r_{{\mathbb{C}}}\colon\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}})\to\mathcal{H}

(left Kan extended from
X\mapsto X({\mathbb{C}})
endowed with the complex topology) sends
Q_{i}
to
S^{i}
. Hence, we obtain a composite map

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sch}_{k}}(Q_{j},Q_{i})\to[Q_{j},Q_{i}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to\pi_{j}(S^{i})

for any
i,j\geq 1
. Thus, to show that
\alpha\in\pi_{j}(S^{i})
admits a polynomial representative, it suffices to produce a lift
\alpha^{\prime}\in[Q_{j},Q_{i}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
. Since
Q_{j}
is a motivic sphere, we have identifications with bi-graded homotopy groups

[Q_{j},Q_{i}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}=\begin{cases}{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{n,n}(Q_{i})(k)&\text{ if $j=2n$, }\\
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{n-1,n}(Q_{i})(k)&\text{ if $j=2n-1$. }\end{cases}

These groups can sometimes be computed using fiber sequences analogous to those in topology. For example, setting
i=3,j=4
yields

{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(Q_{3})={\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(\mathrm{SL}_{2})={\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}),

which fits in an exact sequence (
[
4
, Theorem 3.3, Proposition 4.4]
)

{{\mathbf{K}}_{2}^{\mathrm{M}}}(k)/12\times_{{{\mathbf{K}}_{2}^{\mathrm{M}}}(k)/2}\mathbf{I}^{2}(k)\to{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(Q_{3})(k)\to\mathbf{GW}_{1}^{0}(k)\to 0.

If
k
has characteristic
\neq 2
, then
\mathbf{GW}_{1}^{0}(k)=\mathrm{KO}_{1}(k)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2\times k^{\times}/(k^{\times})^{2}
. When
k={\mathbb{C}}
, we obtain in particular
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(Q_{3})({\mathbb{C}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2
, and the generator maps to the generator of
\pi_{4}(S^{3})={\mathbb{Z}}/2
, namely the suspension of the Hopf map.

In
[
2
]
, a different approach called
weight shifting
was used to produce a motivic lift of the suspension of the Hopf map. We observe that
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{1,3}(Q_{3})=\mathbf{W}
(the Witt sheaf) and that its generator, as a
{{\mathbf{K}}_{0}^{\mathrm{MW}}}
-module, is
2_{\epsilon}
-torsion (with
2_{\epsilon}=\langle 1,-1\rangle\in\mathrm{GW}(k)
). It is shown in
[
2
, Theorem 2.1.11]
that choosing
-1
as a primitive
2
nd root of unity induces a morphism of sheaves

\mathbf{W}={}_{2_{\epsilon}}{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{1,3}(Q_{3})\to{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(Q_{3})/[-1]{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,3}(Q_{3}).

By construction, the image of the generator realizes to the suspension of the Hopf map. Although conceptually natural, this construction is difficult to make explicit and a geometric realization was missing.

In this article we give two explicit constructions of morphisms of quadrics
Q_{4}\to Q_{3}
, called

, whose complex realization is the suspension of the Hopf map. The first construction uses the computation of
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(Q_{3})
above, specifically the morphism
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(Q_{3})\to\mathbf{GW}_{1}^{0}(k)
and the summand
{\mathbb{Z}}/2\subset\mathrm{GW}_{1}^{0}(k)

\mathrm{GW}_{1}^{0}(k)={\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(\mathrm{Sp})

{\mathbb{Z}}

{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2,2}(\mathrm{Sp})=[Q_{4},\mathrm{Sp}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}=\mathrm{GW}_{1}^{2}(Q_{4})=\mathrm{KSp}_{1}(Q_{4}).

Maps between quadrics

2.1
Basics on quadrics

We start by exploring a bit the geometry of
Q_{2n}
for
n\geq 1
. We consider the closed subschemes

Z_{0}:=\{x_{1}=\ldots=x_{n}=z=0\}\subset Q_{2n},\phantom{i}Z_{1}:=\{x_{1}=\ldots=x_{n}=1-z=0\}\subset Q_{2n}

and set
X_{i}:=Q_{2n}\smallsetminus Z_{i}
for
i=0,1
. It is straightforward to check that
Z_{i}\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
(with coordinates
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
) and we know from
[
1

that the subschemes
X_{i}
are
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-contractible. On the other hand

X_{0}\cap X_{1}=Q_{2n}\smallsetminus(Z_{0}\sqcup Z_{1})=Q_{2n}\smallsetminus\{x_{1}=\ldots=x_{n}=0\}

and there is an obvious projection morphism

p_{n}\colon X_{0}\cap X_{1}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\},\phantom{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z)\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Lemma 2.1.1
.

The map
p_{n}\colon X_{0}\cap X_{1}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}
is a weak equivalence.

Proof.

We can cover
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}
by the open subschemes
D(x_{i})=\{x_{i}\neq 0\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}
. The preimage of
D(x_{i})
under
p_{n}
is an affine space over
D(x_{i})
with coordinates
y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1},y_{i+1},\ldots,y_{n},z
.
∎

We can construct an inverse (up to homotopy) of that map, using the morphism

\alpha_{n}\colon Q_{2n-1}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to Q_{2n}

given on coordinates by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},t)\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1}t(1-t),\ldots,y_{n}t(1-t),t)
. A direct computation shows that it factors through
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
, and that the composite

Q_{2n-1}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}X_{0}\cap X_{1}\xrightarrow{p_{n}}{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}

For further use, we also note that
\alpha_{n}
induces an isomorphism

\alpha_{n}\colon Q_{2n-1}\times({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0,1\})\simeq D(z(1-z))\subset Q_{2n}

with inverse given
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},z)\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\frac{y_{1}}{z(1-z)},\ldots,\frac{y_{n}}{z(1-z)},z)
.

2.2

Recall from

At a point

a_{2}&b_{2}\\

(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4},u_{5},u_{6})=(a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1},a_{1}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{1},a_{1}b_{4}-a_{4}b_{1},a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2},a_{2}b_{4}-a_{4}b_{2},a_{3}b_{4}-a_{4}b_{3})

we obtain that the (unique) equation describing

2.3
The Jouanolou device of
\mathbb{P}^{n}

Recall that the usual Jouanolou device
\mathbb{J}^{n}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is given by the functor associating to a
k
-algebra
R
the set of matrices
P\in M_{n+1}(R)
of rank one satisfying
P^{2}=P
. We have an explicit presentation of the algebra representing this functor via

k[\mathbb{J}^{n}]=k[x_{ij}|1\leq i,j\leq n+1]/\left(x_{ij}=\sum_{r=1}^{n+1}x_{ir}x_{rj}\forall 1\leq i,j\leq n+1,D\right)

where
D
is the set of all
2\times 2
minors of
P
.
There is a universal matrix
P=(x_{ij})
on
k[\mathbb{J}^{n}]
which satisfies
P^{2}=P
by the first set of equations. This yields a projector
P_{R}
on
R^{n+1}
for any homomorphism of
k
-algebras
k[\mathbb{J}^{n}]\to R
and we set
L=\mathrm{Im}(P)\subset R^{n+1}
. This is a projective module and we can compute its rank by considering residue fields of
R
. If
F
is a field we know that the matrix
P_{F}
satisfies
P^{2}_{F}=P_{F}
and it follows in particular that
P_{F}
is diagonalizable with possible eigenvalues
0
and
1
. The condition on the minors forces
P_{F}
to be of rank one, and consequently
L
is of rank one as required.

Remark 2.3.1
.

If

is of characteristic
0
, we can replace the conditions

by the easier condition

, i.e. the matrix is of trace

We have an obvious morphism of schemes

. We set

, where

and we observe that

is non-trivial on

, i.e.
c_{i}
generates

. On
U_{i}
, there exists then global sections

for

with

and we obtain a map

given by the global sections

. These maps glue together to yield the morphism

. It turns out that

From

and

2.4
The map

Consider the ideal

We claim that

I=\langle x_{(n+1)1},\ldots x_{(n+1),n},\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{ii}\rangle=\langle x_{(n+1)1},\ldots x_{(n+1),n},1-x_{(n+1),(n+1)}\rangle=p_{n+1}^{-1}(\{0\}).

Indeed, for

the

Thus

and the claim follows. Using again

\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{ii}&x_{i(n+1)}\\
x_{(n+1)i}&x_{(n+1)(n+1)}\end{pmatrix}=0

and summing up, we find

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i(n+1)}x_{(n+1)i}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{ii})x_{(n+1)(n+1)}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{ii})(1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{ii})

is generated by the classes of

\textstyle{ \mathbb{P}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

2.5
An explicit

-suspension map of maps between quadrics

In this section, we provide an explicit way of

-suspending morphisms of quadrics, starting with the following observation: For

Remark 3.2.4

A direct computation shows that the above map is not trivial relative
