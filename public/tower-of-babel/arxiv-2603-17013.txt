EXOTIC STRUCTURES ON 4-MANIFOLDS WITH INFINITE DIHEDRAL FUNDAMENTAL GROUP
SIMONE TAGLIENTE 1,2

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to produce infinite exotic structures on smooth closed oriented 4 − 4- manifolds with fundamental group isomorphic to the infinite dihedral group, assuming that b 2 + b_{2}^{+} and b 2 − b_{2}^{-} are at least 12 12 .



1.
Introduction

Constructing exotic structures on closed
4
-manifolds is one of the main goals of modern research in low-dimensional topology.

We say that a topological manifold
M
admits infinitely many exotic structures if there exists a family
\{M_{n}\}
of smooth manifolds homeomorphic to
M
, but pairwise non-diffeomorphic. In this paper, we will focus on manifolds with fundamental group isomorphic to the infinite dihedral group, which is the non-trivial semidirect product of
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and we will use the recent classification result of these manifolds up to homeomorphism
[
18
]
.

From now on, all manifolds are assumed to be smooth.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix positive integers
a,b
with
a,b\geq 12
. Then there exists an infinite family of homeomorphic but pairwise not diffeomorphic closed manifolds with fundamental group isomorphic to the infinite dihedral group,
b_{2}^{+}=a
, and
b_{2}^{-}=b
.

When
a=b
, the families consist of irreducible manifolds.

During the proof, we will also find new examples of exotic manifolds with infinite cyclic fundamental group and signature
0
:

Theorem 1.2
.

For
m\geq 13
, there exists a family of manifolds homeomorphic to
(S^{1}\times S^{3})\#(2m)(\CP^{2}\#\overline{\CP}^{2})
, but pairwise not diffeomorphic.

This will be proven in Theorem
5.1
.

The infinite dihedral group
D_{\infty}
is the only non-trivial semidirect product between
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, with presentations

D_{\infty}=\langle\sigma,\tau|\sigma^{2}=1,\sigma\tau\sigma=\tau^{-1}\rangle=\langle\sigma\tau,\tau|\sigma^{2}=1,(\sigma\tau)^{2}=1\rangle,

and so is also equal to the free product
\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Notice that its abelianization is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In particular, a manifold having
D_{\infty}
as fundamental group has
b_{1}
equal to
0
.

This implies that among the examples of
4
-manifolds that we produce in Theorem
1.1
, some have vanishing Seiberg-Witten invariant. In fact, a manifold having non-trivial invariants has
1-b_{1}+b_{2}^{+}
even, and in particular, if
b_{1}
is zero, then
b_{2}^{+}
must be odd.

Our examples have
b_{2}^{+}
of both parities, which is why they are particularly interesting. We will distinguish their diffeomorphism type using Seiberg-Witten invariants of their connected double covers, see Remark
2.5
.

The first exotic closed
4
-manifold was discovered by Donaldson
[
7
]
. Since then, many other examples were produced, including infinitely many exotic manifolds homeomorphic to
\CP^{2}\#2\overline{\CP}^{2}

[
1
]
. These are the smallest simply-connected exotic manifolds known to date.

Regarding non-simply-connected manifolds, a vast collection was produced by Torres
[
25
,
26
]
. In
[
19
]
, the first example of a negative definite exotic manifold with order
2
fundamental group was given. Later, in
[
5
]
, the collection of manifolds with cyclic fundamental group was extended, including manifolds with
b_{2}=1
. These are the smallest examples of exotic manifolds up to date.

Idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1

We will use simply-connected symplectic manifolds as building blocks, and glue them in such a way to obtain a symplectic manifold
W
with infinite cyclic fundamental group, and endowed with a free involution. The quotient with respect to this involution is a manifold
Y
with
\pi_{1}(Y)\cong D_{\infty}
. We can then create an infinite family of non-diffeomorphic manifolds by knot- or torus-surgery, and we will then verify that the equivariant intersection form is unchanged in this process.

However, as we will see, the classification of
4
-manifolds with
D_{\infty}
fundamental group up to homeomorphism involves both the equivariant intersection form and the so-called
k
-Postnikov invariant. We can control this invariant under knot-surgery but not under torus surgery, so only in the first case we can conclude that the manifolds are homeomorphic.

Organization

In Section
2
, we discuss basic operations with symplectic manifolds and we state the homeomorphism classification result that our work is based on. In Section
3
we describe the building blocks that we will use in the construction. In Section
4
we discuss the general case and show how to construct exotic manifolds with
D_{\infty}
fundamental group starting from a given manifold satisfying some hypothesis and lastly, in Section
5
, we provide examples where the conditions are met.

2.
Basics

In this section, we recall some general invariants and constructions of
4
-manifolds.

2.1.
Seiberg-Witten invariant

The Seiberg-Witten invariant of a
4
-manifold
M
is a function

SW:Spin^{c}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}.

We can also define the formal sum

SW_{M}:=\sum_{s\in Spin^{c}(M)}SW(s)e^{s}.

When there is no
2
-torsion in
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, the map
s\in Spin^{c}(M)\mapsto c_{1}(s)\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
gives a bijection between
Spin^{c}(M)
and the set of characteristic elements of
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. With a slight abuse of notation, we will identify these two sets (and the set of characteristic elements of
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, by Poincaré-duality).

We are not going to define the invariant explicitly, but we will use some important properties of it, which will allow us to differentiate smooth structures on a given manifold.

A closed symplectic manifold with
b_{2}^{+}>1
has non-vanishing Seiberg-Witten invariant
[
24
]
, and gluing two symplectic manifolds along symplectic submanifolds with trivial normal bundles (operation called ’normal connected sum’) produces a new symplectic manifold, if the gluing map is chosen appropriately
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
. Some operations allow us to change the Seiberg-Witten invariant in a controlled manner.

2.2.
Torus surgery

Consider a torus
T
in a
4
-manifold
M
with trivial normal bundle, and let
\lambda
be any simple closed curve on the torus. Fix a framing on the torus, which induces an identification of the normal bundle with
T\times D^{2}
, and use it to define a push-off of
\lambda
on
\partial(\nu T)
. Let
\mu
denote the meridian of
T
.

Fix
p,q
coprime. We construct the manifold
M_{T,\lambda}(p/q)
(obtained by torus surgery along
\lambda
, with respect to the chosen framing, and with
\frac{p}{q}
coefficients) by removing
\nu T
and gluing back
T\times D^{2}
, with a diffeomorphism
\phi
of the boundaries sending
\partial D^{2}
to
p\mu+q\lambda
(the choice of
\phi(\partial D^{2})
is enough to determine the manifold
M_{T,\lambda}(p/q)
).

If we fix the base point in
\partial(\nu T)
, we have

(1)

\pi_{1}(M_{T,\lambda}(p/q))=\pi_{1}(M-T)/<\mu^{p}\lambda^{q}=1>.

We can control how the Seiberg-Witten invariant changes under torus surgery. Assume that there is no
2
-torsion in the second homology group of
M
,
M_{T,\lambda}(p/q)
, and of
M_{T,\lambda}(0)
. In
[
21
]
, it was shown that, given a characteristic class
k\in H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
(see also
[
10
, Lecture 2]
)

(2)

\begin{split}\sum_{i}SW_{M_{T,\lambda}(p/q)}(k_{p/q}+2i[T_{p/q}])=p\sum_{i}SW_{M}(k+2i[T])+q\sum_{i}SW_{M_{T,\lambda}(0)}(k_{0}+2i[T_{0}]),\end{split}

where
T_{p/q}
denotes the core torus of the surgery, and
k_{p/q}
denotes a fixed class that agrees with
k
on the complement of the torus
T
(and similarly for
T_{0}
and
k_{0}
). Moreover, there are no other Spin
c
structures on
M_{T,\lambda}(p/q)
other than
k_{p/q}+2i[T_{p/q}]
whose restriction on
M_{T,\lambda}(p/q)-\nu T
agree with
k
.

We used a slight abuse of notation in the above formula: the sum is meant to be on the set of Spin
c
structures extending
k
on
M_{T,\lambda}(p/q)-\nu T
(and similarly for the other terms); in particular, if
T_{p/q}
is null-homologous, the sum consists of only one term.

This formula was used in the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
8
, Theorem 1]

Let
M
be a closed oriented smooth four-manifold,
T
a null-homologous torus, and fix a framing of its tubular neighborhood
\nu T
. Let
\lambda\in T
be a simple closed curve
with push-off null-homologous in
M-\nu T
. If the four-manifold
M_{T,\lambda}(0)
has
non-trivial Seiberg-Witten invariant, in the sense that for some basic class
k_{0}
,
\sum_{i}SW_{M_{T,\lambda}(0)}(k_{0}+2i[T_{0}])\neq 0
, then the set
\{M_{T,\lambda}(1/n)\}
contains infinitely many pairwise non-diffeomorphic four-manifolds.

2.3.
Luttinger surgery

Suppose that the
4
-manifold
M
admits a symplectic structure, and consider a Lagrangian torus
T
embedded in
M
. Then,
T
has a canonical framing (known as the Lagrangian framing), so
\nu T
is canonically identified with
T\times D^{2}
. If the parameter of the torus surgery is of the form
\frac{1}{q}
for some
q
, we call the surgery a ’Luttinger surgery’. Then, the new manifold also admits a symplectic structure
[
20
]
.

2.4.
Knot-surgery

This consists of deleting
\nu T
and gluing back
(S^{3}-\nu K)\times S^{1}
, with
K
a knot in
S^{3}
. The only restriction on the gluing diffeomorphism is to send the longitude of the knot to a meridian of
T
. Then, the Seiberg-Witten invariant of a given manifold after knot-surgery changes by multiplication by the Alexander polynomial of the knot
K

[
9
]
:

(3)

SW_{M_{K}}=SW_{M}\cdot\Delta_{K}(e^{2T}).

2.5.
Subgroups of
D_{\infty}

The following Lemma shows that there is a preferred double cover associated to a manifold with fundamental group isomorphic to
D_{\infty}
:

Lemma 2.2
.

There is a unique index
2
subgroup of
D_{\infty}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Subgroups of index
2
of

D_{\infty}=\langle\sigma,\tau|\sigma^{2}=1,\sigma\tau\sigma=\tau^{-1}\rangle

correspond to surjective maps
D_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and so are in bijection with index
2
subgroups of its abelianization, which is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. So, there are three subgroups of index
2
in
D_{\infty}
. However, only
\langle\tau\rangle
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, while the other two are isomorphic to
D_{\infty}
(these are
\langle\tau^{2},\sigma\rangle
and
\langle\tau^{2},\sigma\tau\rangle
).

An intuitive way to visualize it is the following: in the dihedral group
D_{2n}
of isometries of a
2n
-gon, we find a first copy of
D_{n}
generated by reflections along lines through opposite vertices, and a second copy generated by reflections across lines through mid-points of opposite edges.
∎

We can thus differentiate smooth structures on a manifold with
D_{\infty}
fundamental group by differentiating smooth structures on the unique double cover corresponding to
\mathbb{Z}\triangleleft D_{\infty}
.

2.6.
Homeomorphism classification

A crucial point is proving that the manifolds we will construct are homeomorphic. For manifolds with fundamental group isomorphic to
D_{\infty}
, the topological classification was established recently by Hillman, Kasprowski, Powell, Ray. Before stating the result, we introduce some notation:

•

The equivariant intersection form of
M
is the function

\lambda_{M}:\pi_{2}(M)\times\pi_{2}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(M)]

defined as
\lambda_{M}(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{g\in\pi_{1}(M)}(\alpha\cdot g^{-1}\beta)g
. Recall the identification
\pi_{2}(M)\cong H_{2}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})
, where
\tilde{M}
is the universal cover of
M
, and
\pi_{1}(M)
acts on
\tilde{M}
by deck transformations. So, in the above formula,
\alpha\cdot g^{-1}\beta
denotes the intersection of (representatives of)
\alpha
and the translated image of
\beta
under the action of
g^{-1}
. Also, notice that the sum is finite.

•

The
n
-Postnikov approximation of
M
is the complex
P_{n}M
, together with a map
p_{n}:M\rightarrow P_{n}M
, characterized by the property that
p_{n}
induces isomorphisms
\pi_{i}(M)\cong\pi_{i}(P_{n}M)
for
i\leq n
, while
\pi_{i}(P_{n}M)=0
for
i>n
.

•

P_{2}M
is classified by an element
k_{M}\in H^{3}(D_{\infty},\pi_{2}(M))
, called the Postnikov
k
-invariant of
M
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
18
, Theorem 1.10]
, Let
M
,
N
be closed, oriented, smooth 4-manifolds together with isomorphisms
\pi_{1}(M)\cong D_{\infty},

\pi_{1}(N)\cong D_{\infty}
.

Suppose the equivariant intersection forms

\lambda_{M}
and
\lambda_{N}

are isometric, via an isomorphism on
\pi_{2}
that induces an isomorphism

H^{3}(D_{\infty};\pi_{2}(M))\rightarrow H^{3}(D_{\infty};\pi_{2}(N))

sending

k_{M}\mapsto k_{N}
.

Then M and N are homotopy equivalent, and if they are either spin or both have universal cover not spin, then M and N are homeomorphic.

3.5.
Other Lemmas

(recall that, when talking about normal closures of loops in the fundamental group, we can ignore the choice of the base-point):

Corollary 3.5
.

Recall that
\Sigma_{g}^{n}

We can compute the fundamental group of

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume the notation of Lemma

be as in Section

(

for

, while

\pi_{1}(Z_{m}-(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup T_{3}))=1

Proof.

Notice that we need the assumptions on

,

,

, each one with a dual torus, as described in Section

The key point is that, when removing a torus or the surface

So, the fundamental groups of

and of

Assume the notation of Lemma

(1)

If

(2)

surfaces of the form
F_{\gamma_{i}}\cup_{\gamma_{i}}F^{\prime}_{\gamma_{i}}
, where
\gamma_{i}
are generators of

and
\partial F_{\gamma_{i}}=\gamma_{i}
(these surfaces are sent to generators of

From this we deduce that

By Theorem

Given any knot

Proof of Theorem 1.1

We will now prove Theorem
1.1
. We rephrase it here as follow, including also Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exist infinite families of irreducible, homeomorphic but pairwise not diffeomorphic non-spin manifolds
\{Y_{m,J}\}
, with
m\geq 6
, and
\{Y^{\prime}_{m,J}\}
, with
m\geq 5
, with fundamental group isomorphic to
D_{\infty}
. Moreover,
b_{2}^{+}(Y_{m,J})=b_{2}^{-}(Y_{m,J})=2m+1
, while
b_{2}^{+}(Y^{\prime}_{m,J})=b_{2}^{-}(Y^{\prime}_{m,J})=2m+2
.

The double covers associated to
\mathbb{Z}\triangleleft D_{\infty}
are, respectively, irreducible manifolds
\{W_{m,J}\}
homeomorphic but pairwise not-diffeomorphic to
(S^{1}\times S^{3})\#(4m+4)(\CP^{2}\#\overline{\CP}^{2})
, and
\{W^{\prime}_{m,J}\}
homeomorphic but pairwise not-diffeomorphic to
(S^{1}\times S^{3})\#(4m+6)(\CP^{2}\#\overline{\CP}^{2})
.

Proof.

We use Theorem
4.1
(2) with input the Baykur-Hamada manifolds
Z_{m}
described in Theorem
3.1
.

We can run the construction in two ways:

(1)

when
m\geq 6
, we pick disjointly embedded Lagrangian tori
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
,
T_{3}
as in Corollary
3.7
. We set
S_{1}=T_{1}
,
S_{2}=T_{2}
, and use
T_{3}
to perform knot surgery.

(2)

When
m\geq 5
we use the genus
2
surface
\Sigma
defined in Section
3.3
as
S_{1}
, we set
S_{2}=T_{1}
, and we use
T_{2}
to perform knot surgery, where
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are as in Corollary
3.7
.

All the surfaces described above have zero self-intersection and admit a dual surface. For example, all the tori have zero self-intersection because they are Lagrangian, and their dual is described in Section
3.3
(
2
), while the dual of
\Sigma
is given by the fiber of the fibration
{\gothfamily Z}_{m}
. Moreover, we can perturb the symplectic structure so that the tori become symplectic.

We verify that the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
(2) are satisfied: by Corollary
3.8
, the complement of the surfaces
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
in
Z_{m,J}
is simply connected. Moreover, by Lemma
3.6
, we have that
K\cup B\cup(C-\Sigma)
is simply connected, and since
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
and
T_{2}^{*}
are contained in its complement, we deduce that every curve in
\Sigma
and
T_{1}
bounds a surfaces disjoint from
T_{2}
and
T^{*}_{2}
and intersects
\Sigma\cup T_{1}
only along the boundary (and similarly, curves in
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
bound surfaces disjoint from
T_{3}
and
T^{*}_{3}
).

Thus, we can apply Theorem
4.1
. In the first case we denote the exotic families as
Y_{m,J}
and their double covers
W_{m,J}
, while in the second, we denote them
Y^{\prime}_{m,J}
and
W^{\prime}_{m,J}
. Notice that the exoticness of
Y_{m,J}
and
Y^{\prime}_{m,J}
is detected by the Seiberg-Witten invariants of
W_{m,J}
and
W^{\prime}_{m,J}
.

We can then easily compute the topological invariants of these manifolds. For
Y_{m}
: since removing tori and gluing along three-manifolds does not change the Euler characteristic, we first see that
\chi(W_{m})=2\chi(Z_{m})=2(4m+4)
(recall that
Z_{m}
is homeomorphic to
(2m+1)(\CP^{2}\#\overline{\CP}^{2})
, as explained in Section
3.3
). Since
b_{1}(W_{m})=1
and
\sigma(W_{m})=0
(in fact,
W_{m}
is cobordant to the disjoint union of two copies of
Z_{m}
), we obtain
b_{2}^{+}(W_{m})=b_{2}^{-}(W_{m})=4m+4.

By multiplicativity of the Euler characteristic and of the signature,
\chi(Y_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\chi(W_{m})=\chi(Z_{m})

\sigma(Y_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(W_{m})=0

For
W_{m}^{\prime}
and

b_{2}^{+}(W^{\prime}_{m})=b_{2}^{-}(W_{m}^{\prime})=4m+6

b_{2}^{+}(Y_{m}^{\prime})=b_{2}^{-}(Y_{m}^{\prime})=2m+2.

Since the condition
b_{2}-|\sigma|\geq 6
holds in
W_{m,J}
and
W_{m,J}^{\prime}
, their equivariant intersection form is extended from the integers by (
[
15
]
, Theorem 2). By the homeomorphism classification of manifolds with infinite cyclic fundamental group
[
11
]
, we deduce that they are homeomorphic to a connected sum of

and a simply-connected manifold with odd intersection form (in fact, by Section
3.1
(

Lastly, we argue that these examples are irreducible. In fact,
Z_{m}
is an irreducible symplectic manifold, as well as

So, an embedded

6.
Manifolds with isometric equivariant intersection form

Here we briefly see how to apply Theorem
4.1
Case (1) to produce an infinite family of non-diffeomorphic
4
-manifolds, sharing the same equivariant intersection form. As previously explained, in this case, we are not able to say that the manifolds are homeomorphic; nevertheless, we have the following:

Theorem 6.1
.

There exist infinite families of pairwise not diffeomorphic non-spin manifolds
\{Y_{5,k}\}
with
\pi_{1}(Y_{5,k})\cong D_{\infty}
, with isometric equivariant intersection form and
b_{2}^{+}(Y_{5,k})=b_{2}^{-}(Y_{5,k})=11
.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
4.1
case (1) with input the Baykur-Hamada manifold
Z_{5}
, and with
S_{1}=T_{1}
and
S_{2}=T_{2}
as in Corollary
3.7
, see also Figure
1
. The null-homologous torus
T
is defined in Definition
3.3
, and the manifold
(Z_{5})_{T}(0)
is symplectic, since
Z_{5}
is obtained from it by Luttinger surgery. Also,
T_{0}\subset(Z_{5})_{T}(0)
admits a dual torus with zero self-intersection.
Then, by Corollary
3.8
, the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
are satisfied, and thus we obtain a family
Y_{5,k}
of non-diffeomorphic manifolds with the same equivariant intersection form. The same topological computations as in Section
5
show that

b_{2}^{+}(Y_{5,k})=b_{2}^{-}(Y_{5,k})=11.\qed

The advantage of this construction is that we only need two tori with simply-connected complement. So, we do not need to enlarge the genus of the fiber of
Z_{m}
to obtain the third one; compare with Section
5
. Thus, we obtain smaller examples than in Theorem
1.1
.

Of course, you can blow-up and change orientation to get families with
b_{2}^{+}\neq b_{2}^{-}
.
