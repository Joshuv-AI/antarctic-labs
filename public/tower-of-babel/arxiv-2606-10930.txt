On the Complexity of the Bi-infinite Post Correspondence Problem
Olivier Finkel, Vesa Halava

Abstract
In the bi-infinite Post Correspondence Problem ( ℤ \mathbb{Z} PCP), it is asked whether the same bi-infinite word can be constructed correspondingly from a given finite set of pairs of words. In this article, we study its complexity with respect to the arithmetical hierarchy and prove that it is in Σ 2 0 ∖ ( Π 1 0 ∪ Σ 1 0 ) \mathsf{\Sigma}^{0}_{2}\setminus(\Pi^{0}_{1}\cup\mathsf{\Sigma}^{0}_{1}) and, therefore, at the level 2 of the arithmetical hierarchy. For the proof, we present a sequence of reductions starting from the nonhalting of the Turing machine all the way to ℤ \mathbb{Z} PCP via infinite PCP, an s s -shift infinite PCP and s s -shift ℤ \mathbb{Z} PCP for all natural numbers s s . In the process, we prove that the infinite PCP is undecidable for injective morphisms, and that the infinite injective PCP, s s -shift infinite PCP, s s -shift ℤ \mathbb{Z} PCP and the non-termination problem for (deterministic and reversible) semi-Thue systems are all Π 1 0 \Pi^{0}_{1} -complete. Keywords: Bi-infinite words, Post Correspondence Problem, Undecidability, Bi-infinite PCP, s s -shift infinite PCP, s s -shift bi-infinite PCP, injective infinite PCP.



1
Introduction

In 1946 Emil Post defined and proved undecidable a problem that is today called
Post Correspondence Problem
(PCP) in
[
19
]
. Instead of the original definition of the PCP with lists of words by Post, we shall give a definition using word morphisms:

Let
A^{*}
denote the set of all finite words over the alphabet
A
. A mapping
g\colon A^{*}\to B^{*}
is a
morphism
if
g(uv)=g(u)g(v)
for all
u,v\in A^{*}
.

Problem 1

(PCP)
.

Let
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
be a finite alphabet. Given two morphisms
g,h\colon A^{*}\to B^{*}
, does there exists a nonempty word
w=a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}\in A^{*}
such that

g(w)=h(w)\,?

(1)

The pair
(g,h)
is called an
instance
of the PCP, and the non-empty word
w
is called a
solution
of the instance
(g,h)
. Moreover, the
size
of an instance
(g,h)
of the PCP is the size
n
of the alphabet
A
. Note carefully that the alphabet
B
in the above definition can be assumed binary (and actually Post already defined the PCP with binary words).

The PCP and its many variants imply a bridge from combinatorial undecidable problems of computational systems and formal rewriting systems to decision problems in algebraic settings, and, therefore, play an important role in the Theory of Computability. Also, the boundary between decidability and undecidability is revealed by the PCP and its variants. For example, it is known that the PCP is decidable for
n=2
, see
[
5
,
10
]
, and undecidable for
n=5
, see
[
17
]
.

In this article we concentrate on variants of the PCP where instead of a finite sequence of indices we are asked for infinite or even bi-infinite sequences of indices such that the words built from the words of the corresponding pairs are equal. Let us begin with the
infinite Post Correspondence Problem
(
\omega
PCP):

Problem 2

(
\omega
PCP)
.

Let
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
and
B
be two finite alphabets. Given two morphisms
g,h\colon A^{*}\to B^{*}
, does there exist an infinite word
w=a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots
such that

g(w)=h(w)\,?

It is known that the
\omega
PCP is undecidable for
n=8
, see
[
4
]
, and decidable for instances with
n=2
, see
[
11
]
. It is also known that the
\omega
PCP is not ”more complex” than the PCP itself with respect to the arithmetical hierarchy as
\omega
PCP is
\Pi_{1}^{0}
-complete and the PCP is
\Sigma_{1}^{0}
-complete, see
[
8
]
. Indeed, this shows that
\omega
PCP is on the first level of the arithmetical hierarchy, as the PCP is.

Our main motivation for this article is the complexity of the
bi-infinite Post Correspondence Problem
(
\mathbb{Z}
PCP):

Problem 3

(
\mathbb{Z}
PCP)
.

Let
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
and
B
be two finite alphabets. Given two morphisms
g,h\colon A^{*}\to B^{*}
, does there exist a bi-infinite word
\alpha=\dots a_{i_{-2}}a_{i_{-1}}a_{i_{0}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dots
such that

g(\alpha)=h(\alpha)?

(2)

Note that already the equality of bi-infinite words needs to be defined properly: for a bi-infinite word
w
, denote by
w(j)
the letter in position
j\in\mathbb{Z}
in the sequence
w
. Then
w=v
if and only if there is a constant
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}
called the
shift
such that the letters
w(i)=v(i+\tau)
for all positions
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

An
instance
of the
\mathbb{Z}
PCP is the pair
(g,h)
of morphisms, and a bi-infinite word
\alpha
satisfying (
2
) is said to be a
solution
of the instance. The
\mathbb{Z}
PCP was originally proved to be undecidable in
[
21
]
, see
[
13
]
for a somewhat simpler proof. Recently, it was proved that the
\mathbb{Z}
PCP is in the class
\Sigma_{2}^{0}
of the arithmetical hierarchy, see
[
7
]
. Moreover, the same work proved that
\mathbb{Z}
PCP
\notin\Pi^{0}_{1}
,
using the undecidability argument of
[
13
]
.
Thus, in order to locate the problem strictly at the second level, it remains
to prove that it is not in
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
; this will follow here from the

\Pi^{0}_{1}
-hardness result. In this article we sharpen the result and show that
\mathbb{Z}
PCP is in
\Sigma^{0}_{2}\setminus(\Pi^{0}_{1}\cup\Sigma^{0}_{1})
and, therefore, at the level 2 of the arithmetical hierarchy. For this we shall define new variants of both
\omega
PCP and
\mathbb{Z}
PCP where the shift is fixed with a given natural number and prove that these variants are
\Pi_{1}^{0}
-complete. We shall define these variants along the proof. Also, we shall prove that the
\omega
PCP is undecidable for injective morphims, and that this problem is also
\Pi^{0}_{1}
-complete. Actually, we shall construct the whole chain of undecidability proof from the non-halting of the Turing machines to these variants of the PCP via the non-termination problem of the semi-Thue systems, which is also proved to be
\Pi^{0}_{1}
-complete.

Our proofs use several constructions and methods known for the special kind of rewriting systems called semi-Thue systems. A

where

of

of

. We denote by

, and by

the transitive closure of

. Note that the index

is omitted from the notation, when the semi-Thue system studied is clear from the context. If

in

Problem 4

(Non-Termination)

Given a semi-Thue system

and a word

Lemma 11

Conclusion

We have determined the exact arithmetical complexity of several infinite variants of the Post Correspondence Problem. In particular, we proved that the
