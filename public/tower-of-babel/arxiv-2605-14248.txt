Strong electron correlations and ligand hybridization for altermagnetism
Byungkyun Kang, Anderson Janotti, Dai Q. Ho, Myoung-Hwan Kim, Chul Hong Park, Sangkook Choi, Mark R. Pederson, Eunja Kim

Abstract
Spin-band splitting is a hallmark of altermagnetism, intrinsically linked to magnetic ordering driven by electron correlations. However, recent inconsistencies in the detection of altermagnetism in strongly correlated altermagnet candidates have cast doubt on the robustness of this phenomenon and its dependence on many-body effects. Here, using state-of-the-art quantum many-body frameworks, we dissect the electronic origins of altermagnetism in three prototypical candidates: MnF 2 , MnTe, and RuO 2 . In MnF 2 , we identify pronounced local electron correlations within Mn-3 d d states and uncover a distinct Mott gap in the visible range, rooted in nonlocal screening effects. The strong correlations markedly localize the Mn-3 d d electrons, leading to a narrowing of the spin-resolved bandwidth and, consequently, a suppression of spin-band splitting. By contrast, MnTe provides an ideal platform for altermagnetism, exhibiting substantial local Mn-3 d d magnetic moments due to the strong correlations and pronounced spin-band splitting, enabled by robust Mn-3 d d –Te-5 p p orbital hybridization. RuO 2 manifests as a Pauli paramagnet with vanishing local moments, even in its antiferromagnetic phase. Nonetheless, it exhibits significant spin-band splitting, indicative of itinerant altermagnetic behavior. Our results reveal that both strong local electron correlations and judicious ligand selection to promote orbital hybridization are key prerequisites to realizing altermagnetism in strongly correlated systems. These insights pave the way for the rational design and discovery of novel altermagnetic materials.

2

Itinerant altermagnetic behavior of RuO
2
.

For RuO
2
, a material that has generated considerable debate regarding its potential as an altermagnetic candidate, we investigate the nature of spin-band splitting in the AFM phase. As illustrated in Fig.

When many-body effects are explicitly taken into account using DFT+DMFT with
U

U
on electron localization,
U
was increased to 3.6 eV, as estimated by the cRPA method (see Fig.S3). Notably, this larger

The persistence of significant spin-band splitting at both values of

;
Fedchenko et al. 2024

Despite the presence of spin-band splitting, our DFT+DMFT calculations reveal that the local magnetic moment in the AFM phase remains nearly zero for both small and large values of

Nevertheless, the calculations stably converge with the sizable spin-band splitting, which is indicative of itinerant antiferromagnetism

Zhu et al. 2019

While the nature of magnetism in RuO
2

2
retains the potential for spin-band splitting, thereby leaving open the possibility for an unconventional, itinerant altermagnetic state.

Summary and Outlook

The current implementation of FGW+EDMFT is limited in its ability to access lower temperatures and to simulate AFM order, necessitating further methodological developments. Consequently, in this work, FGW+EDMFT was applied solely to MnF
2
in order to demonstrate its strongly correlated nature. Additionally, we find that the LQSGW+DMFT approach, when combined with cRPA, produces nonphysical results, such as an Mn-3
d
occupation number of 6 in MnF
2
. Due to these deficiencies, LQSGW+DMFT was employed only to calculate
U
in the three materials studied here. Beyond DFT+DMFT, the consistent application of ab initio quantum many-body methods to altermagnetic candidate materials will be the subject of future work.

Within the framework of the Hubbard model (
1
), the interplay between the on-site Coulomb interaction
U
, which favors charge localization and robust magnetic order, and the electronic hopping parameter
t
, which drives charge delocalization, electron mobility, and suppresses magnetic ordering, is paramount in dictating the emergent electronic and magnetic properties of materials. In the case of RuO
2
, the dominance of
t
over
U
stabilizes a metallic ground state characterized by enhanced electronic hybridization (Fig.
4
d) and a vanishing magnetic moment (Fig.
4
e). Conversely, both MnF
2
and MnTe manifest as insulators, exhibiting significantly reduced hybridization and substantially larger local magnetic moments compared to RuO
2
. Interestingly, the increased hybridization in MnTe relative to MnF
2
correlates with a concomitant reduction in the local magnetic moment, underscoring the delicate balance between itinerancy and localization in these systems (Fig.
4
d,e).
These observations highlight the critical role of the
U/t
ratio in governing the emergence and stabilization of altermagnetic phases. Our results underscore the necessity for systematic studies—both theoretical and computational—to map the phase space defined by
U
and
t
, enabling the rational design and discovery of optimal altermagnetic materials. Given that both
U
and
t
are inherently material-specific, our work motivates high-throughput, first-principles quantum many-body calculations, whereby these key parameters can be quantitatively determined, thus accelerating the identification of promising altermagnet candidates.

In summary, we have employed a recently developed, parameter-free fully self-consistent GW plus extended dynamical mean-field theory framework to explore the electronic structure of MnF
2
. Our analysis establishes MnF
2
as a prototypical strongly correlated insulator with a visible-range Mott gap primarily driven by nonlocal screening effects. Notably, the pronounced local electronic correlations in MnF
2
act to suppress dispersive spin-band splitting. In contrast, MnTe exhibits the coexistence of strong electron correlations and substantial ligand hybridization, giving rise to significant spin-band splitting, while RuO
2
remains largely uncorrelated. Collectively, these results highlight the essential role of ligand hybridization in stabilizing altermagnetic behavior in strongly correlated systems.

We employed the fully self-consistent GW + extended dynamical mean-field theory (FGW+EDMFT) approach
Kang et al. 2025b
;
Kang et al. 2024b

\Omega
is treated as a functional of the electron Green’s function
G
and self-energy
\Sigma

\Gamma

Almbladh et al. 1999a
;
Chitra and Kotliar 2001a

\begin{split}\Gamma[G,\Sigma,W,\Pi]=&-\mathrm{Tr}[log(-G_{H}^{-1})]-\mathrm{Tr}[log(1-G_{H}\Sigma)]\\
&-\mathrm{Tr}[G\Sigma]+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}[log(1-V\Pi)]\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}[\Pi W]+\Psi[G,W].\\
\end{split}

(3)

Here,
\mathrm{Tr}

(1/\beta)
times the sum over Matsubara frequencies together with the sums over crystal momentum, band/orbital, and spin indices.
G_{H}

\Psi

Within the Baym–Kadanoff formalism, the Luttinger–Ward functional

\begin{split}\Psi^{GW+EDMFT}(G,W)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}GWG+\Psi(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{W})\\

(4)
