On the Hilton-Zhao vertex-splitting conjecture


Abstract
Let G G be a simple graph with order n n , maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) , and chromatic index χ ′ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\prime}(G) , respectively. A graph G G is edge-chromatic critical if χ ′ ​ ( H ) < χ ′ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\prime}(H)<\chi^{\prime}(G) for every proper subgraph H H of G G . Assume that G G is an n n -vertex connected regular Class 1 1 graph, and let G ∗ G^{*} be obtained from G G by splitting one vertex into two vertices. Hilton and Zhao in 1997 proposed the vertex-splitting conjecture: if Δ ⁡ ( G ) > n 3 \Delta(G)\textgreater\frac{n}{3} , then G ∗ G^{*} is edge-chromatic critical. Recently, Cao, Chen, and Shan (Discrete Math. 2022) verified the conjecture for Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 3 ​ n 4 \Delta(G)\geq\frac{3n}{4} . In this paper, we confirm the conjecture for Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ n − 2 3 \Delta(G)\geq\frac{2n-2}{3} . Keywords: edge coloring, vertex-splitting conjecture, multi-fan, Kierstead path

Introduction

We consider simple connected graphs and generally follow the book
[
8
]
of Stiebitz et al. for notation and terminology.
Let
G
be a graph, and
V(G)
and
E(G)
be the vertex set and the edge set of
G
, respectively.
A graph
G
is
\Delta
-regular
if
\delta(G)=\Delta(G)=\Delta
, where
\delta(G)
and
\Delta(G)
are respectively the minimum degree and maximum degree of
G
.
Let
[k]:=\{1,\dots,k\}
, that is, the set of the first
k
consecutive positive integers. A
k
-edge-coloring
of a graph
G
is a map
\varphi:E(G)\to[k]
that assigns to
every edge
e
of
G
a color
\varphi(e)\in[k]
such that no two adjacent edges of
G

receive the same color.

Denote by
\mathcal{C}^{k}(G)
the set of all
k
-edge-colorings of
G
.
The
chromatic index

\chi^{\prime}(G)
is the least integer
k
such that
\mathcal{C}^{k}(G)\neq\emptyset
.
In 1960s, Vizing
[
10
]
and, independently, Gupta
[
6
]
proved that

\Delta(G)\leq\chi^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1
, which leads to a natural classification of graphs.
Following Fiorini and Wilson
[
3
]
, if
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)
, then
G
is of
Class
1
; otherwise, it is of
Class 2
. Holyer
[
5
]
proved that it is NP-complete to
determine whether an arbitrary graph is of Class
1
. We know that
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\left|E(G)\right|/\lfloor\left|V(G)\right|/2\rfloor
since every matching of
G
has at
most
\lfloor\left|V(G)\right|/2\rfloor
edges. A graph
G
is
overfull
if

\left|E(G)\right|>\Delta(G)\lfloor\left|V(G)\right|/2\rfloor
.
Clearly, if
G
is overfull then
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1
, and so
G
is of Class
2
.
Easily implied by its definition, overfull graphs have odd order.

A graph
G
is
edge-chromatic critical
if
\chi^{\prime}(H)<\chi^{\prime}(G)
for every proper
subgraph
H
of
G
. In investigating the classification problem, edge-chromatic critical
graphs of Class
2
are of particular interest. Furthermore, we call
G
a
\Delta
-critical
graph if
G
is edge-chromatic critical
and
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta+1
, where
\Delta:=\Delta(G)
.
We study sufficient conditions for a Class
2
graph to be edge-chromatic critical.
For
v\in V(G)
, let
N_{G}(v)
denote the set of neighbors of
v
in
G
. A
vertex-splitting
in a graph
G
at a vertex
v
forms a new graph
G^{*}
:
replacing
v\in V(G)
with two new adjacent vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
and partitioning

N_{G}(v)
into two nonempty subsets that serve as the set of neighbors of
v_{1}
and
v_{2}

from
V(G)
in
G^{*}
, respectively.
We say the new graph
G^{*}
is obtained from
G
by a vertex-splitting.
Vertex-splitting was called the “Möbius-type gluing technique” in
[
1
]
and
[
9
]
.
It is easy to see that a regular Class
1
graph has even order, and that every graph
obtained from a regular graph of even order by a vertex-splitting is overfull.

Hilton and Zhao
[
4
]
in
1997
proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Vertex-splitting conjecture)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex connected
\Delta
-regular Class
1
graph with
\Delta\textgreater\frac{n}{3}
. If
G^{*}
is obtained from
G
by a vertex-splitting, then
G^{*}
is

\Delta
-critical.

It is easy to check that the graph
G^{*}
above is overfull, so it is of Class
2
.
Thus the difficulty of vertex-splitting conjecture is to test that
G^{*}
is edge-chromatic critical.
Hilton and Zhao
[
4
]
verified the conjecture for graphs
G
with
\Delta(G)\geq(\sqrt{7}-1)n/2\approx 0.82n
. Song
[
7
]
in 2002 verified the conjecture
for a class of special graphs
G
with
\Delta(G)\geq 0.5n
. Recently, Cao, Chen and
Shan
[
2
]
proved the conjecture when
\Delta(G)\geq 0.75n
. In
this paper, we confirm the conjecture for
\Delta(G)\geq\frac{2n-2}{3}
as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n
and
\Delta
be positive integers such that
\Delta\geq\frac{2n-4}{3}
. If an
n
-vertex graph
G
is obtained
from an
(n-1)
-vertex connected
\Delta
-regular Class
1
graph by a vertex-splitting, then

G
is
\Delta
-critical.

2
Definitions and preliminary results

Let
G
be a graph. For
e\in E(G)
,
G-e
represents the graph obtained from
G
by
deleting the edge
e
. An edge
e
is a
critical edge
of
G
if

\chi^{\prime}(G-e)<\chi^{\prime}(G)
. Clearly, if
G
is of Class
2
and every edge is critical, then
G

is edge-chromatic critical. For
u\in V(G)
, let
d_{G}(u)
denote the degree of
u
in
G
.
The symbol
\Delta
is reserved for
\Delta(G)
in this paper. A
\Delta
-vertex in
G
is a vertex with maximum degree in
G
.
For
u\in V(G)
, a
\Delta
-neighbor of
u
is a neighbor of
u
that is a
\Delta
-vertex in
G
.
When
u,v\in V(G)
are adjacent, we call
(u,v)
a
full-deficiency pair
of
G
if

d_{G}(u)+d_{G}(v)=\Delta+2
.

Let
G
be a graph with an edge
e\in E(G)
, and an edge coloring
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(G-e)

for some integer
k\geq\Delta
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, let
\varphi(v)
denote the set of colors
assigned to edges incident with
v
, and
\overline{\varphi}(v)=[k]\setminus\varphi(v)
,
that is, the set of colors not assigned to any edge incident with
v
. We call
\varphi(v)
the
set of colors
present
at
v
and
\overline{\varphi}(v)
the set of colors
missing
at
v
. Clearly,
|\varphi(v)|+|\overline{\varphi}(v)|=k
for each vertex
v\in V(G)
.
For a vertex set
X\subseteq V(G)
, we define
\overline{\varphi}(X)=\bigcup_{v\in X}\overline{\varphi}(v)
.
The set
X
is called elementary with respect to
\varphi
or simply
\varphi
-elementary

if
\overline{\varphi}(u)\cap\overline{\varphi}(v)=\emptyset
for any two distinct vertices

u,v\in X
.
For a color
\alpha\in[k]
, let
E_{\varphi,\alpha}(G)
denote the set of edges colored
with
\alpha
.
Let
\alpha,\beta\in[k]
be two distinct colors, and
H
be the spanning subgraph induced
by
E_{\varphi,\alpha}(G)
and
E_{\varphi,\beta}(G)
.
Clearly, every component of
H
is either a path or an even cycle, each of which is referred to as an

(\alpha,\beta)
-chain
of
G
.
If we swap the colors
\alpha
and
\beta
on an
(\alpha,\beta)
-chain
C
, then we obtain a
new (proper)
k
-edge-coloring of
G
, which is also in
\mathcal{C}^{k}(G-e)
. This operation is called a
Kempe change
.
Furthermore, if
C
is a path connecting vertices
u
and
v
, then we say that an

(\alpha,\beta)
-chain
C
has endvertices
u
and
v
or say that
u
and
v
are
(\alpha,\beta)
-linked
.
For a vertex
v
of
G
, we denote by
P_{v}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi)
or simply
P_{v}(\alpha,\beta)
the unique

(\alpha,\beta)
-chain containing the vertex
v
.

For any two distinct vertices
u
,
v\in V(G)
, the two
chains
P_{u}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi)
and
P_{v}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi)
are either identical or
disjoint, which is an important fact in our proofs.

More generally, for an
(\alpha,\beta)
-chain, if it is a path and contains two vertices

a
and
b
, then we let
P_{[a,b]}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi)
be its subchain with endvertices
a
and
b
.
The operation of swapping the colors
\alpha
and
\beta
on the subchain
P_{[a,b]}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi)
is still called a Kempe change, but the resulting coloring may no longer be a proper
edge coloring.
In particular, we may change the color of one edge in our proofs and adopt the notation
uv:\alpha\rightarrow\beta
to mean recolor the edge
uv
from
\alpha
to
\beta
.
If
x,y\in P_{u}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi)
with
x
positioned between
u
and
y
on the path, then we say
P_{u}(\alpha,\beta,\varphi)
meets
x
before
y
.

Definition 1

(Multi-fan)
.

Let
G
be a graph with an edge
e_{1}=xy_{1}
, and an edge coloring
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(G-e_{1})

for some integer
k\geq\Delta
. A multi-fan at
x
with respect to
e_{1}
and
\varphi
is a
sequence
F=(x,e_{1},y_{1},\dots,e_{p},y_{p})
with
p\geq 1
consisting of edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{p}

and vertices
x,y_{1},\dots,y_{p}
satisfying the following two conditions
\colon

F1.

The edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{p}
are distinct, and
e_{i}=xy_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq p
.

F2.

For every edge
e_{i}
with
2\leq i\leq p
, there exists a
vertex
y_{j}
with
1\leq j\textless i
such that
\varphi(e_{i})\in\overline{\varphi}(y_{j})
.

Definition 2

(Kierstead path)
.

Let
G
be a graph with an edge
e_{1}=y_{0}y_{1}
, and an edge coloring
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(G-e_{1})

for some integer
k\geq\Delta
. A Kierstead path with respect to
e_{1}
and
\varphi
is a sequence

K=(y_{0},e_{1},y_{1},\dots,e_{p},y_{p})
with
p\geq 1
consisting of edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{p}
and
vertices
y_{0},y_{1},\dots,y_{p}
satisfying the following two conditions
\colon

K1.

The vertices
y_{0},\dots,y_{p}
are distinct, and

e_{i}=y_{i-1}y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p
.

K2.

For every edge
e_{i}
with
2\leq i\leq p
, there exists a
vertex
y_{j}
with
0\leq j\textless i
such that
\varphi(e_{i})\in\overline{\varphi}(y_{j})
.

Lemma 2.1

(Vizing’s Adjacency Lemma
[
10
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph of Class
2
with maximum degree
\Delta
.
If
e=xy
is a critical edge of
G
, then
x
has at least
\Delta-d_{G}(y)+1

\Delta
-neighbors in

V(G)\setminus\{y\}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph of Class
2
with a critical edge
e_{1}=xy_{1}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}(G-e_{1})
and
F=(x,e_{1},y_{1},\dots,e_{p},y_{p})
be a multi-fan at
x
with respect to

e_{1}
and
\varphi
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

V(F)
is
\varphi
-elementary;

(2)

If
\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(x)
and
\beta\in\overline{\varphi}(y_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq p
, then
x
and
y_{i}
are

(\alpha,\beta)
-linked.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph of Class
2
with a critical edge
e_{1}=y_{0}y_{1}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}(G-e_{1})
and
K=(y_{0},e_{1},y_{1},e_{2},y_{2},e_{3},y_{3})
be a Kierstead path with respect
to
e_{1}
and
\varphi
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
\min\{d_{G}(y_{1}),d_{G}(y_{2})\}\textless\Delta
, then
V(K)
is

\varphi
-elementary;

(2)

\left|\overline{\varphi}(y_{3})\cap(\overline{\varphi}(y_{0})\cup\overline{\varphi}(y_{1}))\right|\leq 1
.

It is easy to see that the subpaths
(y_{0},e_{1},y_{1})
and
(y_{0},e_{1},y_{1},e_{2},y_{2})

of the Kierstead path

above are also multi-fans with respect to
e_{1}
and
\varphi
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
2
]
)
.

If
G
is an
n
-vertex graph of Class
2
with a full-deficiency pair
(a,b)
such
that the edge
ab
is a critical edge of
G
, then
G
satisfies the following properties.

(1)

For every
x\in(N_{G}(a)\cup N_{G}(b))\backslash\{a,b\}
,

d_{G}(x)=\Delta
;

(2)

For every
x\in V(G)\backslash\{a,b\}
, if
d_{G}(x)\geq n-|N_{G}(a)\cup N_{G}(b)|
, then
d_{G}(x)\geq\Delta-1
. If also

and

d_{G}(b)\textless\Delta
, then
d_{G}(x)=\Delta
.

Now we give the proof of Theorem
1
as follows.

Proof.

It is easy to see that a regular Class
1
graph has even order, and that every graph obtained
from a regular graph of even order by a vertex-splitting is overfull.
Thus
n\geq 5
and
G
is overfull, and so
G
is of Class
2
.
Then, in order to prove that
G
is
\Delta
-critical, we only need to show that every edge of

G
is critical.
Suppose to the contrary that there exists
xy\in E(G)
such that
xy
is not a critical edge
of
G
.
Let
G^{*}=G-xy
. Thus
\chi^{\prime}(G^{*})=\Delta+1
.
Let
ab
be the edge of
G
whose contraction gets an
(n-1)
-vertex
\Delta
-regular Class

1
graph. Thus
a
and
b
have distinct neighbors, and each vertex in
V(G)\backslash\{a,b\}
has degree
\Delta
in
G
. Furthermore,
(a,b)
is a full-deficiency pair in
G
such
that
ab
is a critical edge, which implies
ab\neq xy
.
Since
G^{*}-ab\subset G-ab
, we have
\Delta\leq\chi^{\prime}(G^{*}-ab)\leq\chi^{\prime}(G-ab)=\Delta
, which
implies that
\chi^{\prime}(G^{*}-ab)=\Delta
, and so
ab
is also a critical edge of
G^{*}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta}(G^{*}-ab)
.

First suppose that
\{a,b\}\cap\{x,y\}\neq\emptyset
. Since
ab\neq xy
, we say
a=x
and
b\neq y
.
Then
d_{G^{*}}(a)+d_{G^{*}}(b)=\Delta+1
.
However,
d_{G^{*}}(a)+d_{G^{*}}(b)\geq\Delta+2
by Lemma
2.1
, a contradiction.

Therefore,
\{a,b\}\cap\{x,y\}=\emptyset
.
Then
d_{G^{*}}(x)=d_{G^{*}}(y)=\Delta-1
and
|\overline{\varphi}(x)|=|\overline{\varphi}(y)|=1
.
Since
d_{G^{*}}(a)+d_{G^{*}}(b)=d_{G}(a)+d_{G}(b)=\Delta+2
, we have
(a,b)
is a full-deficiency pair in
G^{*}
, and
x,y\notin N_{G^{*}}(a)\cup N_{G^{*}}(b)
by Lemma
2.4
(1). It follows that
d_{G^{*}}(x)+|N_{G^{*}}(a)\cup N_{G^{*}}(b)|=\Delta-1+\Delta+2=2\Delta+1\geq n
since
\Delta\geq\frac{2n-4}{3}
. Thus we have one of
a
and
b
has degree
\Delta
by Lemma
2.4
(2), say
d_{G^{*}}(b)=\Delta
, which implies
d_{G^{*}}(a)=2
.
Note that
ab
is a critical edge of
G^{*}
.
If
c
is the other neighbor of
a
in
G^{*}
, then
d_{G^{*}}(c)=\Delta
by Lemma

2.1
.
Thus
(a,c)
is also a full-deficiency pair of
G^{*}
.
If
\overline{\varphi}(b)=\{1\}
,
then
\varphi(ac)=1
since
(a,ab,b)
is a multi-fan and
\{a,b\}
is
\varphi
-elementary.
Let
\varphi^{\prime}
be obtained from
\varphi
by coloring
ab
with the color
1
and uncoloring

ac
.
Note that
\overline{\varphi^{\prime}}(a)=\overline{\varphi}(a)
,
\overline{\varphi^{\prime}}(b)=\emptyset
, and

\overline{\varphi^{\prime}}(c)=\{1\}
.
Now
\varphi^{\prime}
is a (proper)
\Delta
-edge-coloring of
G^{*}-ac

\chi^{\prime}(G^{*})=\Delta+1

G^{*}
.
Similarly, we have

2.4
