A Note on the Equivalence Between Zero-knowledge and Quantum CSS Codes
Noga Ron-Zewi, Mor Weiss

Abstract
Zero-knowledge codes, introduced by Decatur, Goldreich, and Ron [ 21 ] , are error-correcting codes in which few codeword symbols reveal no information about the encoded message, and have been extensively used in cryptographic constructions. Quantum CSS codes, introduced by Calderbank and Shor [ 13 ] and Steane [ 33 ] , are error-correcting codes that allow for quantum error correction, and are also useful for applications in quantum complexity theory. In this short note, we show that (linear, perfect) zero-knowledge codes and quantum CSS codes are equivalent. We demonstrate the potential of this equivalence by using it to obtain explicit asymptotically-good zero-knowledge locally-testable codes.



1
Introduction

In this note, we show an equivalence between two well-studied families of codes: Zero-knowledge codes and quantum CSS codes.
We first briefly describe these families of codes and their applications.

Zero-knowledge codes.

Zero-Knowledge (ZK) codes are error-correcting codes with a randomized encoding, in which few codeword symbols reveal nothing about the encoded message. More accurately, a

t
-
Zero-Knowledge
(ZK) code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
, for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
, is associated with a randomized encoding map
{\mathsf{Enc}}_{C}:\mathbb{F}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{n}
and has the following guarantee. For every
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
, and any
{\cal I}\subseteq\left[n\right]
of size
\left|{{\cal I}}\right|\leq t
, we have that
{\mathsf{Enc}}_{C}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}
and
{\mathsf{Enc}}_{C}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I}
are identically distributed, where
{\mathsf{Enc}}_{C}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}
denotes the restriction to
{\cal I}
of a randomly-generated encoding of
m
. In this note, we focus on
non-adaptive, perfect
ZK codes, as defined above. That is, the queries of an adversary to the codeword are determined non-adaptively, and the resultant distribution is identical for every pair of messages. We note that the non-adaptive and adaptive settings are known to be equivalent
[
12
, Appendix C]
, and that relaxations to the statistical setting (where the distributions
{\mathsf{Enc}}_{C}({\sf m})|_{\cal I},{\mathsf{Enc}}_{C}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I}
are statistically close) have also been considered in the literature
[
26
]
. We additionally focus on
linear
ZK codes, meaning that
C
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
, and the encoding map
{\mathsf{Enc}}_{C}
is linear in the message
{\sf m}
and the randomness used for encoding.

ZK codes were first formally defined by Decatur, Goldreich, and Ron
[
21
]
, and have been used (either explicitly or implicitly) in numerous applications in cryptography. For example, these codes have been used in Shamir’s secret sharing
[
32
]
, and lie at the heart of information-theoretically secure Multi-Party Computation (MPC) protocols (starting from
[
7
,
14
]
). ZK codes also have applications to memory delegation (e.g., in PIR schemes
[
18
]
), as well as for the design of information-theoretic proofs systems such as Probabilistically Checkable Proofs (PCPs)
[
5
,
6
]
and Interactive Oracle Proofs (IOPs)
[
11
,
30
]
with ZK guarantees (e.g., in
[
10
,
9
,
17
,
8
,
12
,
31
,
25
]
).

Many of the early applications of ZK codes relied on ZK properties of
polynomial-based
codes (in which the message is interpreted as a low-degree polynomial, and the corresponding codeword is the evaluation table of the polynomial). More recent work also studied and exploited ZK properties of other codes — such as concatenated codes
[
21
]
, interleaved codes

,

,

These parameters are tightly connected to the efficiency measures of the resulting applications. Several other works have also studied more generally the parameters achievable by ZK codes

Quantum CSS codes.

A quantum CSS code consists of a pair of linear codes
C_{X},C_{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
so that their dual codes
C_{X}^{\perp},C_{Z}^{\perp}
are orthogonal to each other (that is,
\langle c,c^{\prime}\rangle=0
for any
c\in C_{X}^{\perp},c^{\prime}\in C_{Z}^{\perp}
), with the additional guarantee that
any vector in
C_{X}\setminus C_{Z}^{\perp}
and any vector in
C_{Z}\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}
has a large weight.

Quantum CSS codes were first introduced by Calderbank and Shor
[
13
]
and Steane
[
33
]
, and have been extensively studied since then. CSS codes are used to protect quantum computation from errors, and also have applications in quantum complexity theory.
For example, the recent progress
[
4
]
on the quantum PCP conjecture
[
2
,
1
]
relied on recent breakthroughs on the construction of asymptotically good LDPC quantum CSS codes
[
29
]
.
The resemblance of CSS codes to classical codes enables one to draw on the vast literature on classical error-correcting codes for their design, and by now we know of various constructions of such codes.

Equivalence between zero-knowledge and quantum CSS codes.

In this note, we show that ZK codes and quantum CSS codes are equivalent. Specifically, we show a transformation from a ZK code into a quantum CSS code (and vice versa) so that the guarantee on the weight of vectors in
C_{Z}\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}
in the CSS code translates into the ZK property of the ZK code, while the guarantee on the
weight of vectors in
C_{X}\setminus C_{Z}^{\perp}
translates into the decoding property of the ZK code (the latter is also typically required in applications); see Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
for a formal statement of this equivalence.

While we view this equivalence as interesting in its own right, it is also motivated by the hope that this connection might shed light on our understanding of these codes, or yield new ZK codes or CSS codes by combining the equivalence with existing constructions. We demonstrate this potential by describing one concrete application of the equivalence. Specifically, in Section
4
, we translate recent breakthroughs on the construction of asymptotically-good locally testable quantum codes
[
23
,
27
,
34
]
into explicit asymptotically-good zero-knowledge locally-testable codes.

Finally, we also mention that another connection between quantum computation and zero-knowledge was discovered in
[
28
]
, who used fault-tolerance in the quantum setting to obtain leakage resilience in the classical computation setting. This gives another indication to the usefulness of exploring the connection between zero-knowledge and quantum computation.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field, and let
u,v\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
. We use
\langle u,v\rangle
to denote the inner product between
u,v
, namely,
\langle u,v\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\cdot v_{i}
. We also let

\Delta(u,v):=\left|\left\{i\in\left[n\right]:u_{i}\neq v_{i}\right\}\right|
,

\mathrm{wt}(u):=\Delta(u,0)
, and for a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
, we let
\Delta(u,S):=\min_{s\in S}\Delta(u,s)
.

A
linear (error-correcting) code
is a subspace
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
over

\mathbb{F}
. We call
\mathbb{F}
and
n
the
alphabet
and the

block length
of the code, respectively, and the elements of
C
are called
codewords
.
The
rate

of
C
is the ratio
R:=\frac{\dim(C)}{n}
, and it measures the amount of redundancy in encoding.
The
(Hamming) distance
of
C
is
\Delta(C):=\min_{c\neq c^{\prime}\in C}\Delta(c,c^{\prime})
, which for a linear code equals
\mathrm{wt}(C):=\min_{0\neq c\in C}\mathrm{wt}(c)
.
Intuitively, the minimum distance of a code measures the amount of noise tolerance of the code. Specifically, the channel might corrupt some of the entries of a transmitted codeword
c
during transmission and the receiver might receive a string
w\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
. However, if
w
and
c
differ on less than
\frac{\Delta(C)}{2}
entries, then
c
can be recovered uniquely by the decoder by searching for the unique codeword in
C
that is closest to the received word
w
.

A
generator matrix
for a linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
of dimension
k
is a (full-rank) matrix
G\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
so that
{\mathrm{image}}(G)=C
, and a
parity-check matrix
for
C
is a (full-rank) matrix
H\in\mathbb{F}^{(n-k)\times n}
so that
\ker(H)=C
(note that both
G
and
H
are not unique). The
dual code
of
C
is the code
C^{\perp}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
containing all strings
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
satisfying that
\langle c^{\prime},c\rangle=0
for all
c\in C
.
It follows by definition that
(C^{\perp})^{\perp}=C
, and that
H
is a parity-check matrix for
C
if and only if
H^{T}
is a generator matrix for
C^{\perp}
.

2.1
Zero-Knowledge (ZK) Codes

Zero-Knowledge (ZK) codes, introduced by Decatur, Goldreich, and Ron
[
21
]
, are codes in which few codeword symbols reveal no information about the message. For this to be possible, we need to associate the code
C
with a
randomized encoding map
, where
C
has ZK with respect to this randomized encoding. We will focus on an encoding map that is linear in the message and the randomness used for encoding (such a map was used in most prior works, e.g.,
[
21
,
12
,
31
]
).

Definition 2.1

(Randomized Encoding Map)
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
be a linear code of dimension
k
, let
G\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
be a generator matrix for
C
, and let
k^{\prime}<k
be a parameter.
The
k^{\prime}
-
randomized encoding map for
G
is a random map

{\mathsf{Enc}}:\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}\to C
, which on input message
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
, samples a uniformly random
r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}
, and outputs
G\cdot z
, where
z=({\sf m},r)\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
.

The zero-knowledge property of an encoding map is defined as follows.

Definition 2.2

(Zero-Knowledge (ZK) Code)
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
be a linear code of dimension
k
, let
G\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
be a generator matrix for
C
, and let
k^{\prime}<k
and
t<n
be parameters. We say that the
k^{\prime}
-randomized encoding map
{\mathsf{Enc}}:\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}\to C
for
G
is
t
-
Zero-Knowledge (
t
-ZK)
if
for any
{\cal I}\subseteq\left[n\right]
of size
t
, and for any pair of messages
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
, we have
{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}\equiv{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I}
.

Error Correction in ZK Codes.

Applications of zero-knowledge codes also typically require that one can recover the message
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
from its randomized encoding. Specifically, let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
be a linear code of dimension
k
, let
G\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
be a generator matrix for
C
, and let
k^{\prime}<k
be a parameter.
We say that the
k^{\prime}
-randomized encoding map
{\mathsf{Enc}}:\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}\to C
for
G
is
decodable from
e
errors
if there is a (deterministic) algorithm
D
so that for every message
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
and for every
y\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
with
\mathrm{wt}(y)\leq e
, it holds that
\Pr[D({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})+y)={\sf m}]=1

(see, e.g.,
[
21
, Thm. 1]
). Note that the existence of such a (not necessarily efficient) decoding algorithm
D
is equivalent to the property that for any pair of distinct messages
{\sf m}\neq{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
and (not necessarily distinct)
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}
, it holds that
\Delta(G\cdot z,G\cdot z^{\prime})>2e
, where
z=({\sf m},r)
and
z^{\prime}=({\sf m}^{\prime},r^{\prime})
. Indeed, if this latter property holds, then given
w:={\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})+y
, the decoder
D
can find
{\sf m}
by searching for
z\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
so that
G\cdot z
is closest to
w
.

2.2
Quantum CSS Codes

Quantum CSS codes, introduced by Calderbank and Shor
[
13
]
and Steane
[
33
]
, are error-correcting codes that allow for quantum error correction, and are defined as follows.

Definition 2.3

(CSS Code)
.

A
CSS code
is a pair of linear codes
C_{X},C_{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
so that the subspaces
C_{X}^{\perp}
and
C_{Z}^{\perp}
are orthogonal (that is,
\langle c,c^{\prime}\rangle=0
for any
c\in C_{X}^{\perp}
and
c^{\prime}\in C_{Z}^{\perp}
). The
rate
of
(C_{X},C_{Z})
is
\frac{\dim(C_{X})-\dim(C_{Z}^{\perp})}{n}=\frac{\dim(C_{Z})-\dim(C_{X}^{\perp})}{n}
.
The
distance

d_{X}
(
d_{Z}
, respectively) is defined as the smallest weight of a vector of
C_{X}
not in
C_{Z}^{\perp}
(
C_{Z}
not in
C_{X}^{\perp}
, respectively). The
distance
of
(C_{X},C_{Z})
is defined as
d=\min\{d_{X},d_{Z}\}
.

3
Zero-knowledge and Quantum CSS Codes Are Equivalent

We show that ZK and CSS codes (Definitions
2.2
and
2.3
, respectively) are equivalent.
Specifically, the following theorem shows how to transform ZK codes into CSS codes with similar parameters.

Theorem 3.1

(ZK Codes are CSS Codes)
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
be a linear code of dimension
k
, let
G\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times k}
be a generator matrix for
C
, let
k^{\prime}<k
be a parameter, and let
{\mathsf{Enc}}:\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}\to C

, and let

Then the following holds:

1.

C_{X}^{\perp}
and
C_{Z}^{\perp}
are orthogonal (so
(C_{X},C_{Z})
is a
\mathrm{CSS}
code).

2.

d_{X}>2e
if and only if
{\mathsf{Enc}}
is decodable from
e
errors.

3.

d_{Z}>t
if and only if
{\mathsf{Enc}}
is
t
-ZK.

.
,

Suppose that

, and let

-randomized encoding map
{\mathsf{Enc}}:\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}\to C

A similar characterization of ZK codes as in the above Lemma

Proof of Lemma
3.3
:

For
{\cal I}\subseteq[n]
, let
G|_{\cal I}
denote the restriction of
G
to the rows in
{\cal I}
.
It suffices to show that for any subset
{\cal I}\subseteq\left[n\right]
,
{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}\equiv{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I}
for any pair of messages
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
if and only if any linear combination of the rows of
G|_{\cal I}
does not result in a non-zero
w\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
such that
w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]}=0
.
We prove the lemma in two steps. First, we show (in Claim
3.4
) that the former requirement “
{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}\equiv{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I}
for any
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
” is equivalent to requiring that “
0\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
for any
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
". Then, we show (in Claim

) that the requirement “
0\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
for any
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
” is equivalent to the latter requirement “any linear combination of rows of
G|_{\cal I}
does not result in
0\neq w\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
with
w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]}=0
”. This will conclude the proof of the lemma.

Claim 3.4
.

Let
{\cal I}\subseteq\left[n\right]
. Then
{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}\equiv{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I}

for any pair of messages
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
if and only if
0\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
for any
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
.

Proof:

For the ’only if’ part, note that if
0\notin{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
for some
0\neq{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
, then since
0\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}(0)|_{\cal I})
, then we clearly have that
{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}\not\equiv{\mathsf{Enc}}(0)|_{\cal I}
.

For the ’if’ part, assume that
0\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
for any
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
. Then in this case, for any
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
, we have that
0\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime}-{\sf m})|_{\cal I})
, and so by linearity,

{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I})\supseteq{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})+{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime}-{\sf m})|_{\cal I})\supseteq{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}).

Indeed, the right containment uses the fact that
0\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime}-{\sf m})|_{\cal I})
. The left containment follows from linearity, because if
u:=G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m},r)\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
and
u^{\prime}:=G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m}^{\prime}-{\sf m},r^{\prime})\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime}-{\sf m})|_{\cal I})
, then
u+u^{\prime}=G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m}^{\prime},r+r^{\prime})\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I})
.
In summary,
{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I})\supseteq{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
for any pair of messages
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
, so we conclude that
{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})={\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I})
for any pair of messages
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
.
Finally, observe that by properties of linear algebra, for any
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
, the number of
r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}
which satisfy the system of linear equations
G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m},r)=v
is the same for any
v\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
. Consequently,
the fact that
{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})={\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I})
implies that

{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}\equiv{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I}
, and we conclude that
{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I}\equiv{\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m}^{\prime})|_{\cal I}

for any pair of messages
{\sf m},{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
.

Claim 3.5
.

Let
{\cal I}\subseteq[n]
. Then
0\in{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
for any
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}

if and only if any linear combination of the rows of
G|_{\cal I}
does not result in a non-zero
w\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
such that
w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]}=0
.

Proof:

For the ’only if’ part, suppose that there exists a linear combination
u\in\mathbb{F}^{|{\cal I}|}

of the rows of
G|_{\cal I}
which results in a non-zero
w\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
such that
w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]}=0
, and let
j\in[k^{\prime}]
be an entry so that
w_{j}\neq 0
. Let
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
be the
j
-th unit vector. We shall show that there does not exist an
r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}
so that

G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m},r)=0
, and consequently
0\notin{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
. To see the latter, suppose on the contrary that there exists an
r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}
so that

G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m},r)=0
. Then we have that

0=\langle u,G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m},r)\rangle=\langle(u^{T}\cdot G|_{\cal I})^{T},({\sf m},r)\rangle=\langle w,({\sf m},r)\rangle=\langle w|_{[k^{\prime}]},{\sf m}\rangle+\langle w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]},r\rangle=w_{j}+0=w_{j}\neq 0,

which is a contradiction.

For the ’if’ part, suppose that there exists an
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
so that
0\notin{\sf Supp}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\sf m})|_{\cal I})
. Then the linear system
G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m},r)=0
does not have a solution
r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}
. Let
A
be the matrix which consists of the first
k^{\prime}
columns of
G|_{\cal I}
, and let
B
be the matrix which consists of the last
k-k^{\prime}
columns of
G|_{\cal I}
. Then by properties of linear algebra, we have that there exists a linear combination
u\in\mathbb{F}^{|{\cal I}|}
so that
\langle u,A\cdot{\sf m}\rangle=\langle(u^{T}\cdot A)^{T},{\sf m}\rangle\neq 0
but
u^{T}\cdot B=0
.
(Indeed, the system
G|_{\cal I}\cdot({\sf m},r)=0
is equivalent to the system
B\cdot r=-A\cdot{\sf m}
, where
A,B,{\sf m}
are fixed.)
But this implies in turn that

w:=u^{T}\cdot G|_{\cal I}=(u^{T}\cdot A,u^{T}\cdot B)
is a linear combination of the rows of
G|_{\cal I}
which satisfies that
w|_{[k^{\prime}]}=u^{T}\cdot A\neq 0
, but
w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]}=u^{T}\cdot B=0
.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, based on the above Lemma
3.3
.

Proof of Thm
3.1
:

We prove each of the items separately.

Item (1):
Follows since
C_{X}^{\perp}=C^{\perp}
, and
C_{Z}^{\perp}
is the span of the last
k-k^{\prime}
columns of
G
, and so
C_{Z}^{\perp}\subseteq C
.

Item (2):
Recall that
{\mathsf{Enc}}
is decodable from
e
errors if and only if for any pair of distinct messages
{\sf m}\neq{\sf m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
and (not necessarily distinct)
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}
, it holds that

\Delta(G\cdot({\sf m},r),G\cdot({\sf m}^{\prime},r^{\prime}))>2e
. Further, by linearity, this latter property is equivalent to the property that for any non-zero
{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}
and (possibly zero)
r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}
it holds that
\mathrm{wt}(G\cdot({\sf m},r))>2e
.
Thus, it suffices to show that

d_{X}=\min_{0\neq{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}},r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}}\mathrm{wt}(G\cdot({\sf m},r))
.

But the above follows since

\displaystyle C_{X}\setminus C_{Z}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{c\in C_{X}\mid c\notin C_{Z}^{\perp}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{c\in C\mid c\;\text{is not in the span of the last $k-k^{\prime}$ columns of $G$}\;\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{G\cdot({\sf m},r)\mid 0\neq{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}},r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}\},

and so
d_{X}=\min_{c\in C_{X}\setminus C_{Z}^{\perp}}\mathrm{wt}(c)=\min_{0\neq{\sf m}\in\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}},r\in\mathbb{F}^{k-k^{\prime}}}\mathrm{wt}(G\cdot({\sf m},r))
.

Item (3):
By Lemma
3.3
,
{\mathsf{Enc}}
is
t
-ZK if and only if any linear combination of any
t
rows in
G
does not result in a non-zero
w\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
such that
w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]}=0
. We shall show that the latter condition is equivalent to the condition that
d_{Z}>t
. To see this, note that a linear combination of
t
rows in
G
resulting in
w\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
, corresponds to a vector
u\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
of weight at most
t
so that
u^{T}\cdot G=w
. Furthermore, the condition that
w\neq 0
is equivalent to the condition that
u\notin C_{X}^{\perp}=C^{\perp}
, while the condition that
w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]}=0
is equivalent to the condition that
u\in C_{Z}
. Thus, the condition that any linear combination of any
t
rows in
G
does not result in a non-zero
w\in\mathbb{F}^{k}
such that
w|_{[k]\setminus[k^{\prime}]}=0
is equivalent to the condition that there do not exist
u\in C_{Z}\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}
of weight at most
t
, which is equivalent to the condition that
d_{Z}>t
.

4
Application: Explicit Asymptotically-Good Zero-knowledge Locally-Testable Codes

We now describe an immediate application of the equivalence between ZK codes and quantum CSS codes of Section
3
. Specifically, we use recent constructions of asymptotically-good locally testable quantum codes to obtain explicit asymptotically-good ZK codes that are also
locally testable
with a few queries. We first formally define locally-testable codes (LTCs).

Definition 4.1

(Locally-Testable Code (LTC))
.

A code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
is a
q
-query Locally Testable Code (
q
-LTC)
if there exists a randomized oracle
algorithm
\mathrm{TEST}

which receives oracle access to a string
w\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
, makes
q
queries to
w
, and outputs either ’accept’ or ’reject’, so that the following conditions holds:

•

Completeness:
If
w\in C
then
\mathrm{TEST}
accepts with probability
1
.

•

Soundness:
If
w\notin C
then
\mathrm{TEST}
rejects with probability at least

\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{\Delta(w,C)}{n}
.

A ZK code that is also an LTC, with a ZK threshold that is significantly larger than the query complexity of the local tester, is called a
ZK-LTC
.
Such codes lie at the heart of ZK-PCP and ZK-IOP constructions (and are also used in other cryptographic contexts such as verifiable secret sharing). Ishai et al.
[
26
]
gave a generic method of (probabilistically) transforming any linear code into a ZK code (Their probabilistic transformation outputs a generator matrix for the ZK code, with negligible probability of error.) They then use this transformation to obtain a
probabilistic
construction of asymptotically good ZK-LTCs. Combining new quantum LTC constructions
[
23
,
27
,
34
]
, and the equivalence between quantum CSS codes and ZK codes, yields an
explicit
construction of asymptotically-good ZK-LTCs. This is formalized in Corollary
4.3
below. We first cite the relevant quantum LTCs (see
[
34
, Thm. 1.3 and Table 4]
, who build on the codes of
[
23
,
27
]
):

Theorem 4.2

(Asymptotically-good quantum LTCs
[
23
,
34
]
)
.

There exists an explicit infinite family of CSS codes
C_{X},C_{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
, where
(C_{X},C_{Z})
has constant rate and distance
\Omega(n)
, and both
C_{X}
and
C_{Z}
are locally testable with
{\mathsf{poly}}\log(n)
queries.

Combining the above Theorem
4.2
with Corollary
3.2
gives explicit asymptotically-good ZK-LTCs with linear ZK threshold that are locally testable with a poly-logarithmic number of queries. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first instance of an explicit family of asymptotically-good ZK-LTCs in which the ZK threshold is larger than the tester’s query complexity.

Corollary 4.3
.

There exists an explicit infinite family of codes
\mathcal{C}=\left(C_{n}\right)_{n}
, where
C_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
is a linear code that is locally testable with
{\mathsf{poly}}\log(n)
queries. Furthermore, there exist an explicit generator matrix
G
for
C_{n}
and
k^{\prime}=\Theta(n)
,
so that the
k^{\prime}
-randomized encoding map
{\mathsf{Enc}}:\mathbb{F}^{k^{\prime}}\to C_{n}
for
G
is
\Omega(n)
-ZK, and is
decodable from
\Omega(n)
errors.

Remark 4.4
.

We note that quantum LTCs satisfy the stronger requirement that
both

C_{X}
and
C_{Z}
are locally testable, while the application for ZK-LTCs only requires that
C=C_{X}
is locally testable. In particular, while we do not know of asymptotically-good constant-query quantum LTCs,
combining the transformation of
[
26
]
with the asymptotically-good constant-query classical LTCs of
[
22
,
29
]
gives a
probabilistic
construction of asymptotically-good ZK-LTCs,
with linear ZK threshold and
constant
query complexity.
