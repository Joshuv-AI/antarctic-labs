Parametric Statistical Inference in the Zone of Moderate Deviation Probabilities
M.S. Ermakov

Abstract
A parametric theory of statistical inference is developed for the moderate deviation probability zone. The new approach to the proofs is based on the Taylor series expansion of the logarithm of the likelihood ratio based on the Hellinger distance. The Large Deviation Principle in the moderate deviation probability zone is proven for Bayesian estimators and maximum likelihood estimators. A uniform approximation of the logarithm of the likelihood ratio and Theorem on concentration of the posterior Bayesian measure are also established for the zone of moderate deviation probabilities.

Introduction

In statistics we constantly encounter small probabilities. Significance levels in confidence estimation and error probabilities in hypothesis testing take small values. The standard approach to studying such problems is to treat them as problems of large and moderate deviation probabilities. This raises the question: “Under what conditions can results on the normal approximation of statistic distributions be extended to the zone of moderate deviation probabilities?” For maximum likelihood estimators and likelihood ratio tests, such results have been obtained in a series of works under rather restrictive conditions
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
17
]
compared to those under which their asymptotic normality was proved
[
10
,
12
,
13
,
18
]
. For Bayesian estimators, only concentration inequalities have been obtained
[
10
]
. The logarithmic asymptotics for these inequalities
[
10
]
are optimal in order.

Previously, to study the asymptotics of moderate deviation probabilities for maximum likelihood and likelihood ratio estimators, a Taylor series expansion of the logarithm of the likelihood ratio based on its derivatives was used
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
17
]
. In proving local asymptotic normality (LAN) of the likelihood ratio, alongside this technique, another technique based on the Hellinger distance has been employed
[

,
10
,
13
,
18
]
. We use this latter technique to study problems in statistical inference theory within the moderate deviation probability zone. We manage to extend to this zone the main results obtained by this approach for normal approximation. We prove a theorem on uniform LAN of the log-likelihood ratio in the moderate deviation zone, establish the large deviation principle in the moderate deviation zone for maximum likelihood and Bayesian estimators, and also prove the large deviation principle in the moderate deviation zone for the concentration of the posterior Bayesian measure. The conditions of our theorems are significantly weaker than those under which previous theorems on moderate deviation probabilities for maximum likelihood estimators and maximum likelihood ratio tests were proved

\int_{S}(g(x,\tau)-\tau^{T}\,\phi(x))^{2}\,d\,\mathbf{P}_{\theta_{0}}<|\tau|^{2}\omega(|\tau|).

(2.3)

Define the Fisher information matrix

I(\theta_{0})=4\int_{S}\phi\,\phi^{T}\,d\,\mathbf{P}_{\theta_{0}}.

(2.4)

We assume that the rank of the matrix

is
d

(2.5)

We are interested in the moderate deviation probabilities

\theta_{0}

3.3
Bayesian Estimator

Let
l_{1}\,:\,\mathbb{R}_{+}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{1}
be an increasing function such that
l_{1}(0)=0
and
l_{1}(x)>0
for
x\neq 0
. Assume
l_{1}
satisfies:

There exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
such that for any
a>1
and
x>0
,

l_{1}(ax)\,\leq\,C_{1}\,a^{\kappa_{1}}l_{1}(x),\qquad C_{2}(a-1)^{\kappa_{2}}<\frac{l_{1}(ax)-l_{1}(x)}{l_{1}(x)}.

(3.18)

The loss function has the form
l(u)=l_{1}(\|u\|)
, where
\|u\|
is some not necessarily Euclidean norm of
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
