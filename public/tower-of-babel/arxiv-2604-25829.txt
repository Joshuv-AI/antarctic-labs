Induced planar Turán numbers
Ervin Győri and Hilal Hama Karim ∗

Abstract
The planar Turán number of a graph F F is the maximum number of edges an n n -vertex F F -free planar graph can have. We study the case where F F is forbidden as an induced subgraph, thereby introducing the induced planar Turán numbers. We will determine a sharp upper bound when F F is Θ 4 \Theta_{4} , a 4 4 -cycle with a diagonal edge, and obtain exact extremal values in case F F is a path P k P_{k} on k k vertices, for k = 3 , 4 k=3,4 and 5 5 .

Introduction

The Turán number of a graph
F
,
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
, is the maximum possible number of edges in a graph on
n
vertices that does not contain
F
as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph. Ever since the seminal work of Turán
[
17
]
in 1941, in which he determined the Turán number of
K_{k}
, for every
k\geq 3
, this has been a major area of research in extremal combinatorics. In fact, if
F
is not a clique and one desires to forbid
F
as an induced subgraph, then the answer is trivially
\binom{n}{2}
, since complete graphs always avoid induced copies of
F
. Nevertheless, induced Turán numbers have been introduced and studied through adding various conditions and restrictions rather than the plausible way. We refer the interested reader to

[
1
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
15
]
. On the other hand, in the planar variant of the Turán problem, it turned out that requiring the forbidden subgraph to be induced makes a non-trivial and interesting problem.

The planar Turán number of
F
, denoted by
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F)
, is the maximum possible number of edges in an
n
-vertex planar graph that does not contain
F
as a subgraph. This was initiated by Dowden
[
3
]
in 2016, who determined sharp upper bounds for
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{4})
and
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{5})
. This variant of the classical Turán problem already offers many interesting and challenging problems, and has been studied for many classes of graphs, for example, cycles
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
16
]
, paths
[
14
]
, theta graphs
[
7
,
13
,
18
]
.
In this paper we study the planar Turán problem where the forbidden subgraph
F
is required to be induced, we will call it the
induced planar Turán number
of
F
and denote it by
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}})
. For many graphs
F
, we can have a maximal planar graph, which we call a triangulation, that does not contain
F
as an induced subgraph. However, this is still not as trivial as the non-planar case, since in general there are many different triangulations on
n
vertices, many of which may not avoid an induced
F
.

Let us define
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}})
precisely. Let
F^{\mathrm{ind}}\subseteq G
denote that
G
contains an induced subgraph isomorphic to
F
. Similarly, and use
F^{\mathrm{ind}}\nsubseteq G
to denote that
G
does not contain any induced subgraph isomorphic to
F
. In the latter case, we say
G
is
F^{\mathrm{ind}}
-free, or
G
does not contain an induced copy of
F
. Then,

\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}}):=\max\{|E(G)|:\ G\text{ is an}\ n\text{-vertex planar graph and }F^{\mathrm{ind}}\text{-free}\}

Throughout the paper, we denote by
K_{n}
and
\overline{K}_{n}
the complete and empty graphs on
n
vertices, respectively. For two graphs
H
and
G
,
H\cup G
denotes their disjoint union and
H+G
denotes the graph obtained from
H\cup G
by adding all the edges between
H
and
G
. Let
tH
denote the disjoint union of
t
copies of
H
. Let
P_{k}
and
C_{k}
denote a path and a cycle on
k
vertices, respectively.

Obviously, if
G
is
F
-free, then it is
F^{\mathrm{ind}}
-free, and hence
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}})\geq\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F)
, for any graph
F
. Observe that characterizing those graphs for which the planar Turán number is
3n-6
is an interesting and challenging problem, see
[
12
]
for some sufficient conditions. Here, in the induced version, we provide some sufficient conditions, which we believe to be an exhaustive list.

For complete graphs, any copy is an induced copy. Then
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,K_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}})=\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,K_{3})=2n-4
and
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,K_{4}^{\mathrm{ind}})=\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,K_{4})=3n-6
, for all
n\geq 6
. Also, it is easy to see that the triangulation
K_{2}+P_{n-2}
does not contain any induced cycle of length
k\geq 4
, which implies that
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k}^{\mathrm{ind}})=3n-6
, for every
k\geq 4
.
Note that the same triangulation does not contain an induced copy of two vertex-disjoint triangles, implying that
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,(2K_{3})^{\mathrm{ind}})=3n-6
.

Let
S_{k}
be a star with
k
leaves (i.e. it has
k+1
vertices). Plummer
[
8
]
characterized maximal planar graphs with no induced
S_{3}
(which are called claw-free). In particular, for every
n\geq 3
, there are
S_{k}^{\mathrm{ind}}
-free triangulations on
n
vertices, and hence,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,S_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}})=3n-6
.

Thus, the following proposition follows.

Proposition 1.1
.

For every
n\geq 6
,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}})=3n-6
, if
F
is one of the following graphs.

(i)

C_{k}
, for any
k\geq 4
,
(ii)

S_{3}
,
(iii)

K_{4}
, or
(iv)

2K_{3}
.

Clearly, if
F^{\mathrm{ind}}\subseteq H
, then
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,H^{\mathrm{ind}})\geq\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}})
. Therefore, the following stronger version of Proposition
1.1
follows immediately.

Proposition 1.2
.

For any graph
F
, and every
n\geq 3
,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}})=3n-6
whenever one of the following holds:

(i)

F
contains a
K_{4}
.

(ii)

F
contains an induced
C_{k}
, for
k\geq 4
(i.e.
F
is not chordal).

(iii)

F
contains an induced
S_{k}
, for
k\geq 3
(i.e.
F
is not claw-free).

(iv)

F
contains an induced copy of
2K_{3}
.

The full characterization of graphs
F
for which
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}})=3n-6
remains an interesting open question. We conjecture that the converse of Proposition
1.2
is also true, that is,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,F^{\mathrm{ind}})=3n-6
if and only if one of the the above four conditions holds for
F
.

Note that any complete bipartite graph on
n\geq 4
vertices contains either an induced
S_{3}
or an induced
C_{4}
, and hence, by proposition
1.2
, the induced planar Turán number for them is also
3n-6
.

Theta graphs
\Theta_{k}
are those consist of a cycle

with one extra edge joining two non-consecutive vertices on the cycle. Using the concept of triangular blocks, a contribution method introduced by Ghosh, Győri, Martin, Paulos and Xiao
[
4

, we determine an upper bound for

that is sharp for infinitely many values of

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
n\geq 5
,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,\Theta_{4}^{\mathrm{ind}})\leq\frac{8}{3}(n-2)
. Moreover, this bound is sharp for every
n\equiv 20\ (mode\ 36)
.

Note that for every
k\geq 5
, any
\Theta_{k}
contains an induced cycle of length at least
4
. Then, by Proposition
1.2
,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,\Theta_{k}^{\mathrm{ind}})=3n-6
, for every
k\geq 5
. Notice that
\Theta_{k}
is a collections of graphs that contains more than one graph if
k\geq 6
, but still any one of them contains an induced cycle of length at least
4
. Hence, whether we forbid the whole collection or any single member of
\Theta_{k}
, the extremal value is still
3n-6
.

Next we will consider paths, which are probably the most interesting class of graphs to deal with. Even in the non-induced version,
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,P_{k})
is known only for
k\leq 11

[
14
]
. Observe also that if a tree contains a vertex of degree three, then it contains an induced
S_{3}
, which means that paths are the only non-trivial trees to consider in the induced setting. We determine the exact value for induced paths on

and

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,P_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{6}{4}n&,&n\equiv 0\ (\text{mode }4)\\
\frac{6}{4}(n-1)&,&n\equiv 1\ (\text{mode }4)\\
\frac{6}{4}(n-2)+1&,&n\equiv 2\ (\text{mode }4)\\
\frac{6}{4}(n-3)+3&,&n\equiv 3\ (\text{mode }4)\\
\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the unique extremal graph is the disjoint union of
\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor
copies of
K_{4}
and maybe a smaller clique on the remaining vertices.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
n\geq 3
,

\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,P_{4}^{\mathrm{ind}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}3n-6&,&n\leq 6\\
\lfloor\frac{8}{3}(n-2)\rfloor+1&,&n\geq 7\\
\end{array}\right.

Finally, we have what we consider as the major result of the paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every
n\geq 3
,

\mathrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,P_{5}^{\mathrm{ind}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}3n-6&,&n\leq 8\\
\lfloor\frac{11n}{4}\rfloor-4&,&n\geq 9\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex planar graph with no induced
P_{3}
. Every vertex of degree at least two can be the middle (center) vertex of an induced

disjoint copies of

and a smaller clique on the remaining vertices. This completes the proof.
∎

In what follows, the following simple observations are repeatedly used. Let

be a planar graph and

be a cut-set in

of

, and a vertex

, we say

is an induced path from

into

is the starting vertex of

and apart from

is in

is an induced path in

with

a starting vertex.

Observation 1
.

If
G
is
k
connected then each vertex of
X
has at least one neighbor in any component of
G\setminus X
.

Indeed, if a vertex
v\in X
has no neighbors in some component of
G\setminus X
, then

would be a cut-set in

.

Observation 2
.

If
G
is
k
-connected and

, then

G\setminus X
has exactly two components.

Indeed, in each component of
G\setminus X
, contract all the edges repeatedly until it shrinks to one vertex, then by the first observation, this vertex is adjacent to every vertex in

. If there were at least three components, then after the contractions we would have a copy of

, which contradicts the fact that contracting edges preserves planarity.

Observation 3
.

If

, then
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
have at most one common neighbor in each component of

.

Otherwise, if they have two common neighbors in a component, say
H_{1}
. Contract the other component into one vertex, this would give the third common neighbor to give a copy of

.

Observation 4
.

If

is

-free, and

, then there is no induced

from

into any component of

.

Since, by Observation 1, we can take a neighbor of

in another component and get an induced

in

.

Observation 5
.

If

is

-free, and

, then there is no induced

from

into two components of

.

Obviously, if so, we have an induced

in

.

Observation 6
.

Let

, and

for some component of

such that

. Then, there is an induced

from

into

.

Since

has a neighbor in

, and

that joins the neighbor of

to

. Then there is a path from

into

Recall that by Observation

Thus, if

and
