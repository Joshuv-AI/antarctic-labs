Input Distribution Design for Ranging-Oriented OFDM-ISAC Systems Under Frequency-Selective Fading
Weijiang Zhao, Yifeng Xiong \corinfo yifengxiong@bupt.edu.cn

Abstract
The implementation of the isac ( isac ) feature in 6g ( 6g ) networks is most likely to be based on the framework of ofdm ( ofdm ). Input distribution design, or constellation design, is a crucial technique in ofdm - isac systems enabling a favorable balance between communication rate and sensing performance. In this treatise, we propose a computationally efficient input distribution design approach for ofdm - isac under frequency-selective channels, following the theoretical framework of capacity distortion. We highlight that under practical sensing constraints, the optimal strategy is to treat the kurtosis of constellations as a resource, and allocate it appropriately over subcarriers.

In this treatise, we propose a computationally efficient approach to design input distributions for frequency-selective

ofdm

•

We formulate the input distribution design problem as a mutual information maximization problem under power budget and

•

Under the

•

To further reduce the complexity and enable real-time design, we propose a gradient projection-based approach, in which the gradients and the projection operators are computed using only closed-form expressions and one-dimensional searches;

•

We analyse the characteristics of the input distributions obtained by the proposed method. In particular, using numerical computations, we demonstrate that uniform power allocation is more favorable when the
eisl

Approximate Optimization Algorithm

4.1
Approximating the Mutual Information

First, we note that there are mainly two drawbacks regarding the computational complexity of Algorithm 1:

1.

Given the values of
\bm{\lambda}
and
\mu
, the evaluation of the objective function involves multiple complicated numerical integrations (computed once per inner iteration);

2.

The search of
\bm{\lambda}
and
\mu

Naturally, we would like to construct algorithms that are capable of exploiting the gradient information, and preferably of reducing the complexity of numerical integration.

To this end, let us reformulate the optimization problem as follows

We note that both the deconvolution method and the entropy maximization problem do not rely on the assumption that the noise is Gaussian distributed. 5
Discussions

In this section, we discuss certain characteristics of the proposed methods.

5.1
Characteristics of the Designed Input Distributions

In this treatise, we have used the
eisl
as the sensing performance metric. However, one might also be interested in the individual sidelobe levels, namely

\mathbb{E}\{|[\bm{\mathsfbr{r}}_{\mathsfbr{x}}]_{i}|^{2}\},~i=2,\dotsc,N.

In the following proposition, we characterize the individual sidelobe levels.

Proposition 5.1
.

The expectation of the squared magnitudes of the
pacs
is given by

\mathbb{E}\{|[\bm{\mathsfbr{r}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}}]_{i}|^{2}\}=|\bm{f}_{i}^{\rm H}\bm{p}|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\kappa_{j}-1)p_{j}^{2},

(36)

where
\bm{f}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th column in
\bm{F}
,
p_{j}=\mathbb{E}\{|\mathsfbr{x}_{j}|^{2}\}
, and
\kappa_{j}=\mathbb{E}\{|\mathsfbr{x}_{j}|^{4}\}/p_{j}^{2}
denotes the kurtosis of
\mathsfbr{x}_{j}
. Especially, for
i=1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\{|[\bm{\mathsfbr{r}}_{\mathsfbr{x}}]_{1}|^{2}\}=\frac{P^{2}}{N}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\kappa_{j}-1)p_{j}^{2}.

(37)

Proof 5.2
.

See Appendix
.4
.

It is now clear that in order to reduce the sidelobe levels, one may: 1) reduce the kurtosis of
\mathsfbr{x}_{i}
’s, or 2) reduce the variance between the entries of
\bm{p}
. To elaborate, all
\bm{f}_{i}
’s (
i\neq 1
) are orthogonal to
\bm{f}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bm{1}
, which implies that the term
|\bm{f}_{i}^{\rm H}\bm{p}|^{2}
is zero when the entries of
\bm{p}
are identical. Especially, when these entries are identical, we have

\mathbb{E}\{|[\bm{\mathsfbr{r}}_{\mathsfbr{x}}]_{i}|^{2}\}=\frac{P^{2}}{N^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\kappa_{j}-1)

for
i\neq 1
, and

\mathbb{E}\{|[\bm{\mathsfbr{r}}_{\mathsfbr{x}}]_{1}|^{2}\}=\frac{P^{2}}{N}+\frac{P^{2}}{N^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\kappa_{j}-1).

In this case, we see that the sidelobe levels are not directly determined by the kurtosis of individual subcarriers. Rather, the dependence is only conveyed by the average kurtosis given by

\bar{\kappa}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{i}.

(38)

This implies that when the entries of
\bm{p}
are identical, in order to control the individual sidelobes, it suffices to control the
eisl
.

Naturally, one would ask when the power allocation over subcarriers is uniform. Apparently, when there is no
eisl
constraint, the power allocation is in general not uniform, since the optimal strategy is the well-known “water-filling” power allocation with all kurtoses being equal to
2
(corresponding to Gaussian distributions). The key fact utilized in the water-filling strategy is that the derivative of
h(\mathsfbr{z})
with respect to
\mathbb{E}\{|\mathsfbr{z}|^{2}\}
is non-negative, thus assigning more power to “better” subcarriers having larger SNRs would be more beneficial. However, in the presence of the
eisl
constraint, the derivative is not necessarily non-negative. In particular, we are interested in the partial derivative
\frac{\partial h(\mathsfbr{z})}{\partial\mathbb{E}\{|\mathsfbr{z}|^{2}\}}
, the rate of change of
h(\mathsfbr{z})
with respect to
\mathbb{E}\{|\mathsfbr{z}|^{2}\}
, given a specific value of the kurtosis
\kappa=\mathbb{E}\{|\mathsfbr{z}|^{4}\}/(\mathbb{E}\{|\mathsfbr{z}|^{2}\})^{2}
is kept unchanged.

Figure 1:
The numerically computed partial derivative

, which equals to zero when the kurtosis is around
1.57
.

As is portrayed in Fig.
1
, the (numerically computed) partial derivative is negative as long as the kurtosis is less than a specific value around
1.57

eisl

In this case, the optimal strategy would be assigning power uniformly to all subcarriers, while assigning more kurtosis to subcarriers having higher SNRs (since this will not hurt the
eisl
, or even individual sidelobe levels), as will be illustrated in Section
6
.

7
conclusions

In this treatise, we have proposed a computationally efficient approach to the design of input distributions in
ofdm
-
isac
systems undergoing frequency-selective channels. In particular, the proposed gradient projection-based method relies only on closed-form expressions and one-dimensional searches, and thus may be used in real-time scenarios. Using semi-analytical arguments, we show that the optimal strategy under practical sensing constraints is a uniform power allocation and a water-filling-like kurtosis allocation, which is in stark contrast to pure communication scenarios. Under the proposed framework, future research directions include precoding design and subcarrier selection. Our hope is that this treatise would provide useful insights for practical
isac
system design.
