Genome-wide identification, characterization, and expression analysis of the plant defensin (PDF) gene family in soybean
Authors: Rongqiang Yuan, Chunlei Zhang, Tianjiao Gao, Xiulin Liu, Xueyang Wang, Fengyi Zhang, KeZhen Zhao, XiaoYu Xia, Sobhi F Lamlom, Honglei Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPlant defensins (PDFs) are small, cysteine-rich antimicrobial peptides that play central roles in plant innate immunity. Despite their importance, a systematic characterization of the PDF gene family in soybean (Glycine max) has been lacking. Here, we report a genome-wide identification and comprehensive characterization of 27 GlyPDF genes encoding 31 protein isoforms in the soybean Williams82 v4 genome, identified through combined BLASTP and Hidden Markov Model (HMM) profiling using the plant defensin domain (PF00304). Phylogenetic analysis classified the GlyPDF family into four subfamilies (GlyPDF1–4), with GlyPDF2 being the largest (11 genes, accounting for > 40% of members). The 27 genes are unevenly distributed across 13 of the 20 soybean chromosomes, with chromosome 8 harboring the highest number of loci (6 genes). Three tandem duplication clusters and 19 intra-genomic collinear pairs were identified, indicating that both tandem duplication and ancestral whole-genome duplication (WGD) have driven family expansion. Ka/Ks analysis confirmed purifying selection across all duplicated gene pairs (Ka/Ks < 1.0). Physicochemical characterization revealed that the majority of GlyPDF proteins are basic (pI > 7.0), consistent with their roles as cationic antimicrobial effectors. Promoter analysis identified enrichment of jasmonate-responsive elements (22/27 genes), abscisic acid-responsive elements (19/27), and salicylate-responsive elements (16/27), as well as WRKY-binding W-box motifs (14/27), suggesting multifaceted hormonal and stress regulation. Tissue-specific qRT-PCR revealed broad but variable expression across roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and pods. Upon inoculation with Fusarium oxysporum, 18 of 27 GlyPDF genes were differentially expressed, with GlyPDF2.7, GlyPDF2.8, GlyPDF2.9, and GlyPDF3.3 displaying the strongest induction kinetics. These findings provide a useful genetic resource for the functional characterization of soybean defensins and their potential application in breeding disease-resistant varieties.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-09177-3.Keywords: Antifungal, Cis-regulatory elements, Fusarium oxysporum, Genome-wide analysis, Plant defensin, Whole-genome duplication

Introduction

Plant defensins are small cationic peptides of 45–54 amino acid residues, widely distributed across the plant kingdom and among the most structurally conserved families of antimicrobial peptides in nature [1]. Their three-dimensional fold is stabilized by four intramolecular disulfide bonds formed between eight invariant cysteine residues, generating the hallmark cysteine-stabilized αβ (Csαβ) motif comprising a single α-helix and three antiparallel β-strands [2]. The four disulfide bond pairings Cys1–Cys8, Cys2–Cys5, Cys3–Cys6, and Cys4–Cys7 provide exceptional stability, with Cys1–Cys8 and Cys2–Cys5 being the most evolutionarily conserved across all characterized plant defensins [3]. In Arabidopsis thaliana, at least 13 putative plant defensin genes (PDFs) have been annotated, encoding 11 distinct defensin proteins, highlighting the multigenic nature of this family even within a single species [2, 4].

The biological functions of plant defensins extend well beyond their originally characterized antimicrobial activity. They are now recognized as multifunctional effectors that inhibit fungal growth, suppress enzyme activity, block ion channels, and inhibit tumor cell proliferation [5]. Among these roles, their broad-spectrum antifungal activity has been the most extensively studied. Unlike most antifungal peptides, which kill fungi by binding to membrane phospholipids and forming pores [6], plant defensins do not directly interact with phospholipid bilayers [7]. Instead, they target fungus-specific sphingolipids and ergosterol-containing complex lipids on the outer leaflet of the fungal plasma membrane, triggering rapid downstream events including K+ efflux, Ca2+ influx, and alterations in membrane permeability that ultimately lead to fungal cell death [8]. Mechanistically, three non-mutually exclusive models have been proposed: (1) direct interaction with fungus-specific plasma membrane components; (2) endocytosis by fungal cells followed by engagement of intracellular receptors; and (3) activation of downstream signal transduction cascades [9, 10]. Well-characterized examples include sugarcane defensin SD5 [11] and pea defensin PsD1 [12], both of which exert antifungal activity through interaction with sphingolipid-rich membrane domains. Notably, plant defensins appear to be non-toxic to mammalian and plant cells at active concentrations, making them candidate templates for crop protection strategies [13].

Soybean (Glycine max [L.] Merr.) is one of the most economically important legume crops worldwide, providing a primary source of plant protein and edible oil [14, 15]. With a global cultivation area exceeding 120 million hectares and a trade volume of approximately 166 million tons in 2020, soybeans rank fourth globally by cultivation area and first in the Americas [16]. Fungal diseases represent a significant and recurring threat to soybean production; pathogens such as Fusarium oxysporum, Phytophthora sojae, and Sclerotinia sclerotiorum can cause severe yield losses under favorable conditions [17]. The development of disease-resistant soybean varieties through molecular breeding, therefore, requires comprehensive identification and functional characterization of endogenous resistance genes [18].

Genome-wide identification and characterization of gene families have become a central approach in plant molecular biology since the completion of multiple high-quality crop genome assemblies. Despite the growing appreciation of plant defensins as key components of innate immunity, systematic analysis of the PDF gene family in soybean remains limited, and their functional roles in disease resistance have not been comprehensively examined. In this study, we performed a genome-wide identification of the PDF gene family in soybean (Williams82 v4) using Arabidopsis PDF proteins (Araport11) as reference queries combined with Hidden Markov Model (HMM) profiling. We characterized the physicochemical properties, chromosomal distribution, gene and protein structural features, conserved motifs, and evolutionary relationships of all identified SoyPDF members through a series of bioinformatics analyses. In addition, we examined the expression profiles of SoyPDF genes in response to Fusarium oxysporum challenge to begin elucidating their roles in soybean disease resistance. This genome-wide catalog may serve as a useful genetic resource for breeding stress-tolerant soybean varieties and may advance our understanding of innate immunity in legumes.

Materials and methods

Identification of soybean PDF gene family members

To identify all PDF gene family members in soybean, a dual-strategy approach combining sequence similarity search and profile-based HMM scanning was employed. First, protein sequences for the 15 members of the Arabidopsis thaliana PDF gene family were retrieved from the TAIR database (http://www.arabidopsis.org/) [19] and used as query sequences in a BLASTP search (v2.12.0+) implemented within TBtools-II [20] against the soybean proteome (Williams82 v4) downloaded from the SoyBase database (https://www.soybase.org/) [21]. The E-value threshold was set to < 10− 5, with all other parameters set to their default values. Second, to ensure comprehensive coverage, all soybean proteins were independently scanned using a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) built from the conserved plant defensin domain (Pfam: PF00304) using HMMER v3.3. Hits with an E-value < 10− 5 were retained. Candidate proteins identified by both approaches were merged and deduplicated to yield the final set of soybean PDF family members, collectively designated the SoyPDF family. All candidates were further verified for the presence of a signal peptide and the mature defensin domain using SignalP v6.0 and the Pfam database, respectively. For comparative phylogenetic purposes, PDF family members from maize (B73 RefGen_v5, MaizeGDB; https://www.maizegdb.org/) and rice (RGAP7, http://rice.plantbiology.msu.edu/) were identified using the same BLASTP and HMM parameters.

Physicochemical property analysis

The physicochemical properties of all SoyPDF proteins were predicted using the ProtParam tool on the ExPASy proteomics server (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/) [22]. Parameters computed included molecular weight (kDa), theoretical isoelectric point (pI), amino acid composition, instability index, aliphatic index, and grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY). Proteins with an instability index below 40 were classified as stable. Subcellular localization was predicted using WoLF PSORT (https://wolfpsort.hgc.jp/).

Multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic analysis

Full-length protein sequences of SoyPDF members together with their homologs from Arabidopsis, maize, and rice were aligned using MAFFT v7.505 with the L-INS-i algorithm. Poorly aligned regions were trimmed with trimAl v1.4. Phylogenetic trees were constructed in MEGA 11.0 [23] using the Maximum Likelihood (ML) method with the JTT + G+I substitution model selected by ModelTest-NG based on the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). Branch support was assessed by 1,000 bootstrap replicates. The resulting tree was visualized and annotated using iTOL v6 (https://itol.embl.de/).

Chromosomal localization and gene structure analysis

Chromosomal positions of all SoyPDF genes were extracted from the Williams82 v4 genome annotation file (GFF3 format) and mapped onto the 20 soybean chromosomes using TBtools-II [20]. Gene structure diagrams depicting the distribution of exons, introns, and untranslated regions (UTRs) were generated using the Gene Structure Display Server (GSDS 2.0; http://gsds.gao-lab.org/) by comparing coding sequences (CDS) with their corresponding genomic sequences. Tandem duplications were defined as two or more homologous genes located within a 200-kb window on the same chromosome with fewer than five intervening genes.

Conserved motif and domain analysis

Conserved motifs in SoyPDF protein sequences were identified using the MEME Suite (v5.5.3; https://meme-suite.org/) with the following parameters: maximum number of motifs = 10, minimum motif width = 6, maximum motif width = 50, and the “any number of repetitions” (anr) model. Identified motifs were annotated by searching against the Pfam and PROSITE databases. Domain architectures were visualized alongside the phylogenetic tree using TBtools-II.

Cis-acting regulatory element analysis

Genomic sequences 2,000 bp upstream of the transcription start site of each SoyPDF gene were extracted from the Williams82 v4 genome and submitted to the PlantCARE database (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/) for identification of putative cis-acting regulatory elements (CAREs). Elements related to light response, phytohormone signaling (jasmonate, salicylate, abscisic acid, gibberellin, and auxin), stress response (drought, low temperature, and wounding), and growth/development were systematically cataloged and visualized.

Synteny and whole-genome duplication analysis

To investigate the evolutionary history and duplication patterns of the GlyPDF gene family, whole-genome synteny analysis was conducted across four plant genomes: soybean (Glycine max Williams82 v4), Arabidopsis thaliana (TAIR10), Oryza sativa (RGAP7), and Zea mays (B73 RefGen_v5). Whole-genome protein sequence files (FASTA format) and genome annotation files (GFF3 format) for each species were retrieved from their respective databases (as described in Identification of Soybean PDF Gene Family Members section) and reformatted to meet MCScanX input specifications. All-versus-all BLASTP searches (v2.12.0+; E-value ≤ 1 × 10⁻µ, maximum 5 hits per query) were first conducted for all pairwise species combinations to generate the homologous gene pair files required by MCScanX. Collinearity blocks were then identified using MCScanX (v1.1) with the following parameters: a minimum of five syntenic gene pairs per collinear block and a maximum gap of 25 intervening non-homologous genes between consecutive syntenic pairs. Intra-genomic synteny within soybean was analyzed separately to detect paralogous GlyPDF gene pairs arising from whole-genome duplication (WGD) events. Only GlyPDF loci falling within confirmed collinear blocks were retained as bona fide syntenic pairs for downstream evolutionary analyses. Syntenic dot plots and ribbon diagrams connecting orthologous and paralogous GlyPDF gene pairs across genomes were visualized using TBtools-II [20]. For evolutionary rate analysis, the coding sequences (CDS) of each syntenic intra-genomic GlyPDF gene pair were extracted from the Williams82 v4 genome and aligned at the codon level using MUSCLE (v5.1) with default parameters. Alignment columns containing gaps or ambiguous bases were trimmed using PAL2NAL (v14) to produce clean, gap-free codon alignments suitable for substitution rate estimation. The nonsynonymous (Ka) and synonymous (Ks) substitution rates for each aligned CDS pair were calculated using KaKs_Calculator 2.0 with the Yang and Nielsen (YN) maximum likelihood model, which corrects for transition/transversion rate bias and unequal codon usage frequencies, making it well suited for recently diverged gene pairs with moderate Ks values. Gene pairs with Ks values greater than 2.0 were excluded from divergence time estimates to avoid saturation of synonymous sites. Divergence time (T, expressed in million years ago, Mya) was estimated for each retained syntenic pair using the neutral molecular clock formula T = Ks / (2λ), where λ = 6.1 × 10− 9 substitutions per synonymous site per year represents the neutral synonymous substitution rate calibrated for the Glycine lineage on the basis of fossil-anchored molecular clock analyses. The Ka/Ks ratio (also expressed as dN/dS) was used as an index of selective constraint acting on each duplicated pair: a ratio below 1.0 indicates purifying (negative) selection conserving gene function, a ratio equal to 1.0 is consistent with neutral evolution, and a ratio exceeding 1.0 is indicative of positive (diversifying) selection. All intra-genomic GlyPDF syntenic pairs were also mapped against the known Glycine-lineage WGD event (~ 13 Mya) to assess whether their Ks-based divergence times are consistent with polyploidization-derived or more recent tandem duplication origins.

Expression analysis by qRT-PCR

Soybean seeds of the Henong 551 cultivar used in this study were obtained from the Soybean Research Institute, Heilongjiang Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Harbin, Heilongjiang, China (HAAS). Soybean seedlings at the V2 stage were used for gene expression analysis. Total RNA was extracted from roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and pods using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. First-strand cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg of DNase I-treated total RNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TaKaRa). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed on a CFX96 Touch Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad) using TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (TaKaRa) with the following thermocycle conditions: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s. Gene-specific primers were designed using Primer Premier 6.0, targeting amplicons of 100–200 bp. GmActin11 (Glyma.13G162000) was used as the internal reference gene. All primer sequences are listed in Table S1. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. All reactions were performed in three biological replicates, each with three technical replicates.

Fusarium oxysporum inoculation assay

Fusarium oxysporum strain CICC40284 was cultured on potato dextrose agar (PDA) at 25 °C for 7 days. Conidial suspensions were prepared in sterile distilled water and adjusted to 1 × 106 conidia/mL using a hemocytometer. Soybean seedlings at the V2 stage were inoculated by the root-dip method: roots were wounded with a sterile needle and submerged in the conidial suspension for 30 min, then transplanted into sterilized vermiculite. Mock-inoculated control plants were treated with sterile distilled water under identical conditions. Root tissues were harvested at 0, 6, 12, 24, 48, and 72 h post-inoculation (hpi), immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at − 80 °C until RNA extraction. Disease severity was scored at 7 days post-inoculation using a 0–5 scale. Three independent biological experiments were conducted, each comprising at least 10 plants per treatment.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed in R v4.3.1. Differences in gene expression between inoculated and mock-inoculated plants were assessed by a two-tailed Student’s t-test. For multi-group comparisons, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s HSD post hoc test was used. P-values ≤ 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) unless otherwise stated.

Results

Genome-wide identification and classification of SoyPDF genes

To comprehensively identify the plant defensin (PDF) gene family in soybean, we performed BLASTP searches against the Glycine max Williams82 v4 proteome using 15 Arabidopsis PDF protein sequences as queries, complemented by independent HMM profiling with the plant defensin domain (PF00304). After merging results from both strategies and removing redundant hits, a total of 31 SoyPDF protein isoforms encoded by 27 unique genomic loci were retained following confirmation of the conserved PF00304 domain and signal peptide by SignalP v6.0. These 27 genes were designated GlyPDF1.1 through GlyPDF4.10, with four loci (Glyma.05G096500, Glyma.08G165000, Glyma.13G278000, and Glyma.08G268000/Glyma.18G027700) each producing two alternatively spliced isoforms, yielding 31 representative protein sequences in total. For comparison, homologous PDF family members were also identified in Arabidopsis thaliana (15 members), Oryza sativa (11 members), and Zea mays (14 members), confirming broad conservation of the PDF family across flowering plants.

Based on phylogenetic topology and domain architecture, the 27 GlyPDF genes were classified into four subfamilies: GlyPDF1 (2 genes), GlyPDF2 (11 genes), GlyPDF3 (4 genes), and GlyPDF4 (10 genes), following the nomenclature established for Arabidopsis PDFs. The GlyPDF2 subfamily was the largest, accounting for more than 40% of all identified members. GlyPDF1 members were notably distinct in encoding unusually long precursor proteins (332–334 amino acid residues), substantially larger than the compact GlyPDF2 precursors (68–95 residues) that represent the canonical small-peptide defensin architecture. GlyPDF3 and GlyPDF4 members showed intermediate precursor lengths (74–134 and 68–127 residues, respectively), suggesting greater structural diversity within these subfamilies.

Physicochemical characterization of SoyPDF proteins

The physicochemical properties of all 31 SoyPDF protein isoforms were computed using the ExPASy ProtParam server and are summarized in Table 1. Protein lengths ranged from 68 residues (GlyPDF2.5) to 334 residues (GlyPDF1.1a), corresponding to molecular weights of 7.25 kDa to 36.24 kDa. The GlyPDF1 subfamily consistently yielded the largest proteins (~ 36 kDa), while GlyPDF2 members were the smallest (7.25–10.54 kDa), consistent with their role as short-chain antimicrobial peptides. The theoretical isoelectric points (pI) ranged from 6.03 (GlyPDF3.1) to 9.99 (GlyPDF4.2), with the majority exhibiting a basic pI (> 7.0)—a hallmark of cationic antimicrobial peptides that facilitates electrostatic interaction with negatively charged fungal membranes. Only five isoforms (GlyPDF2.4a, GlyPDF2.7, GlyPDF3.1, GlyPDF4.7a, and GlyPDF4.7b) had pI values below 7.0, suggesting these members may function through distinct charge-independent mechanisms.

Instability index analysis revealed that approximately half of the SoyPDF proteins (16 of 31) had instability indices below 40 and were therefore predicted to be stable in vitro, including most members of subfamilies GlyPDF2 and GlyPDF3, as well as GlyPDF4.1, 4.6, and 4.7a. The grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY) values ranged from − 0.594 (GlyPDF1.2) to 0.930 (GlyPDF2.7). GlyPDF2 subfamily members generally displayed positive GRAVY scores (mean = 0.585), indicative of pronounced hydrophobicity consistent with membrane-active peptides, while GlyPDF1 members were uniformly hydrophilic (GRAVY ≈ − 0.59). Aliphatic index values were highest in GlyPDF2 members (range: 75.29–114.08), particularly GlyPDF2.7 (114.08) and GlyPDF2.8 (103.82), suggesting high thermostability. Subcellular localization prediction by WoLF PSORT indicated that the majority of SoyPDF proteins harbor an N-terminal signal peptide directing them to the secretory pathway, consistent with extracellular or apoplastic localization as antimicrobial effectors (Table 1).

Phylogenetic analysis of the SoyPDF gene family

To investigate the evolutionary relationships of SoyPDF proteins and their homologs in other plant species, a Maximum Likelihood phylogenetic tree was constructed using full-length protein sequences from soybean (27 GlyPDF genes), Arabidopsis (15 AtPDF genes), rice (11 OsPDF genes), and maize (14 ZmPDF genes), totaling 67 sequences. The resulting ML tree resolved all sequences into four major clades (Groups 1–4) with high bootstrap support (> 70% for most internal nodes), each corresponding to a distinct PDF subfamily (Fig. 1). Group 1 (GlyPDF1/AtPDF1 clade) was clearly distinguished from the other three groups by longer branch lengths, reflecting the markedly extended precursor sequences unique to this subfamily. Group 2 formed the most species-rich and species-diverse clade, encompassing the majority of PDF members from all four species, suggesting early divergence and subsequent lineage-specific expansion. Groups 3 and 4 were predominantly populated by eudicot sequences (soybean and Arabidopsis), with fewer monocot representatives, implying that these subfamilies may have expanded after the monocot–eudicot split.

Bootstrap values (≥ 1,000 replicates) are shown at major nodes. The four major clades (Groups 1–4) are highlighted by colored arcs. Soybean (GlyPDF) members are indicated by filled circles

Within the soybean-specific clade, gene pairs with high sequence identity (> 85%) and close phylogenetic proximity were identified as putative paralogs arising from whole-genome duplication (WGD) or tandem duplication. Notably, GlyPDF2.6/GlyPDF2.7/GlyPDF2.8 clustered tightly on a single branch and map to a tandem array on chromosome 8 (Chromosomal Distribution and Gene Structure of SoyPDF Genes section), consistent with recent local duplication. Similarly, GlyPDF3.3 and GlyPDF3.4 formed a well-supported sister pair (bootstrap = 96%), and GlyPDF4.1/GlyPDF4.6 clustered together with AtPDF4 sequences. The overall topology suggests that the soybean PDF gene family expanded through a combination of polyploidization-driven duplication and localized tandem duplication.

Chromosomal distribution and gene structure of SoyPDF genes

Chromosomal mapping revealed that the 27 GlyPDF genes are unevenly distributed across 13 of the 20 soybean chromosomes, with no PDF genes detected on chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 7, 11, 12, or 14 (Fig. 2). Chromosome 8 harbored the greatest number of GlyPDF loci (6 genes: GlyPDF2.4, 2.5, 2.6, 2.7, 2.8, and 4.6), followed by chromosomes 6 (4 genes: GlyPDF2.2, 2.3, 4.4, and 4.5) and 4 (2 genes: GlyPDF4.1 and 4.2). Three tandem duplication clusters were identified based on our criterion (≤ 200 kb, ≤ 5 intervening genes): (i) GlyPDF2.6–GlyPDF2.7–GlyPDF2.8 on chromosome 8 (~ 45 kb window); (ii) GlyPDF2.2–GlyPDF2.3 on chromosome 6 (~ 12 kb); and (iii) GlyPDF4.4–GlyPDF4.5 on chromosome 6 (~ 18 kb). These clusters collectively account for 7 of the 27 GlyPDF loci, suggesting that tandem duplication has played a significant role in the expansion of this family in soybean.

Gene structure analysis using GSDS 2.0 revealed notable architectural diversity among GlyPDF members (Fig. 2). Within the GlyPDF2 subfamily, most genes are intron-poor, with the majority possessing one or two exons, consistent with the compact architecture typical of small antimicrobial peptide genes. In contrast, GlyPDF1 members display a more complex gene structure with five to seven exons, which correlates with their greater protein-coding capacity. GlyPDF3 and GlyPDF4 members showed intermediate exon numbers (two to four exons), and several members retained extended 5′ and 3′ UTRs, suggesting potential post-transcriptional regulations. The intron phase was predominantly phase 0, consistent with conservation of intron positions across the PDF gene family in other plant species.

Conserved motif and domain analysis

To characterize the structural diversity of GlyPDF proteins, conserved motifs were identified using the MEME Suite, yielding ten distinct motifs (Motifs 1–10) distributed across all 31 protein isoforms (Fig. 3B). Motifs 1 and 2 were present in all GlyPDF members, consistent with their correspondence to the core β-strand/α-helix (Csαβ) architecture of the plant defensin domain (PF00304) and the eight conserved cysteine residues required for the four structural disulfide bonds. The universal presence of these two motifs across all four subfamilies supports their role as the family’s defining structural hallmark.

Distinct, subfamily-specific motif signatures were observed beyond this conserved core. GlyPDF1.2, the sole GlyPDF1 member represented in the alignment, uniquely contained Motifs 9 and 10, which are absent from all other subfamilies and likely correspond to the extended N-terminal precursor region characteristic of PDF1-type defensins. This is consistent with the markedly longer gene structure observed for GlyPDF1.2 in panel C, which extends well beyond the compact architecture of GlyPDF2–4 members. GlyPDF2 members were enriched for Motif 5, which is associated with a γ-core antimicrobial peptide signature linked to membrane-active function according to the PROSITE annotation. GlyPDF3 members shared Motifs 6 and 7 with the GlyPDF4 subfamily but additionally contained Motif 8, a domain with trypsin-inhibitory activity, potentially distinguishing GlyPDF3 members as enzyme-inhibitory defensins. Members of GlyPDF4 displayed Motifs 3 and 4, which are absent from GlyPDF1–3, and whose functional annotations remain to be defined.

Gene structure analysis (Fig. 3C) revealed notable architectural variation across subfamilies. GlyPDF2 members were uniformly compact, with short coding sequences and minimal UTR extension, consistent with their small precursor protein sizes (68–95 aa). GlyPDF3 members displayed intermediate gene lengths with short CDS regions flanked by extended UTRs. GlyPDF1.2 was a clear outlier, with a substantially longer coding region extending to approximately 330 aa, corroborating its unusually large precursor protein relative to all other members.

Domain architecture analysis (Fig. 3D) confirmed that all GlyPDF isoforms contain the canonical plant defensin (Gamma-thionin) domain and the Knot1 superfamily fold, consistent with the conserved Csαβ scaffold. The PRKCSH-like domain was observed predominantly in GlyPDF3 members, while SLR1-BP and WRKY superfamily domains were distributed across several GlyPDF4 members. Plant_zn_clust annotations were observed in a subset of GlyPDF4 members, including GlyPDF4.7a, which exhibited a notably extended domain architecture, reaching approximately 2,000 aa in panel D, suggesting that this isoform may encode additional regulatory or structural features not found in the compact defensin core. Taken together, the motif and domain data support the four-subfamily classification and indicate that functional diversification within the GlyPDF family has occurred at both the sequence and structural levels.

Cis-acting regulatory element analysis

To understand the transcriptional regulation of GlyPDF genes, the 2,000-bp promoter regions upstream of all 27 genes were examined using PlantCARE. A total of 35 cis-acting regulatory elements (CAREs) were identified, falling into five main functional groups: light-responsive, hormone-responsive, stress-responsive, biotic defense, and growth-related elements (Fig. 4). The CARE distribution varied significantly among genes and showed clear subfamily-specific patterns. Light-responsive elements were the most prevalent, including LRE, conserved light-responsive DNA modules, light-related MYB-binding sites, and CMA3 array components, all of which were present in nearly all GlyPDF promoters. Their widespread occurrence across subfamilies suggests that light signaling may be a key regulator of GlyPDF gene transcription, possibly linking defensin expression to diurnal or circadian cycles, a possibility reinforced by the presence of circadian control elements in several promoters.

Methyl jasmonate (MeJA)-responsive elements are widely found across the GlyPDF1, GlyPDF2, and GlyPDF3 subfamilies, reflecting the known JA-dependent activation of plant defensin genes during pathogen attacks. Abscisic acid (ABA)-responsive elements (ABREs) are mainly present in GlyPDF2 and GlyPDF4, suggesting these groups may play roles in drought and osmotic stress responses. Gibberellin-responsive elements (GARE-motif and other gibberellin elements) are present in fewer genes, scattered across subfamilies without a clear pattern. Auxin-responsive elements (AuxREs and related modules) are present in several members of GlyPDF3 and GlyPDF4, suggesting roles in plant development. Salicylate-responsive elements are present in some genes across subfamilies, suggesting that some GlyPDF genes could be regulated through the SA-mediated defense pathway alongside JA signaling.

Several GlyPDF2 and GlyPDF4 members contain cis-acting elements associated with low-temperature responses to abiotic stress. Additionally, enhancer-like elements associated with anoxic-specific inducibility and components required for anaerobic induction were identified in some genes across different subfamilies. MYB binding sites associated with drought response were also present in GlyPDF2 and GlyPDF4, suggesting that these subfamilies may play roles under water-shortage conditions. Regarding defense and development, cis-acting elements involved in stress responses were distributed among various subfamilies. Notably, GlyPDF4.8’s proximal promoter (~ 50–100 bp upstream of the transcription start) contains a large, intense defense/stress-responsive element not seen elsewhere in the family, implying strong activation under stress. Seed- and endosperm-specific regulatory elements, including those for seed regulation, endosperm expression, and repression, were found in some GlyPDF2 and GlyPDF4 members, aligning with PDF2-type defensins’ expression in seed and pod tissues. Elements related to meristem activity and regulation of zein metabolism were observed in a few members, suggesting roles at specific developmental stages. Overall, the variety of CAREs in GlyPDF promoters, including light, hormone, stress, defense, and developmental signals, underpins their broad, stimulus-dependent, and tissue-specific expression patterns.

Synteny and evolutionary history of SoyPDF genes

To examine the genomic distribution and evolutionary history of the GlyPDF family, all 27 loci were mapped onto the 20 soybean chromosomes, and inter-chromosomal syntenic relationships were analyzed using MCScanX (Fig. 5). Circos visualization confirmed that GlyPDF genes are unevenly distributed across 13 chromosomes, with seven chromosomes (Gm01, Gm02, Gm03, Gm07, Gm11, Gm12, Gm14) harboring no GlyPDF loci (Fig. 5A). The highest gene density was observed on chromosome Gm08, which carries six loci (GlyPDF2.4a/b, GlyPDF2.5, GlyPDF2.6, GlyPDF2.7, GlyPDF2.8, and GlyPDF4.6), followed by Gm06 (four loci: GlyPDF2.2, GlyPDF2.3, GlyPDF4.4, GlyPDF4.5). The heatmap, which tracks flanking each chromosome segment, indicates that several GlyPDF-bearing chromosomal regions coincide with areas of moderate to high local gene density.

Intra-genomic synteny analysis identified 19 collinear GlyPDF gene pairs, visualized as red ribbons in Fig. 5A. The majority of these connections link loci on non-homologous chromosomes (e.g., Gm08–Gm18, Gm05–Gm17, Gm10–Gm16), a distribution consistent with the sub-genome structure arising from the Glycine-lineage whole-genome duplication (WGD) event (~ 13 Mya). Notable pairs include GlyPDF2.9 (Gm10) paired with GlyPDF2.10 (Gm16), and GlyPDF4.1 (Gm04) paired with GlyPDF4.8 (Gm16), each situated within conserved homeologous blocks. The convergence of multiple red ribbons on Gm08 and Gm05 further suggests that these chromosomes lie within a synteny-rich sub-genomic region that has retained a disproportionate number of GlyPDF paralogs following polyploidization.

Linear synteny analysis across the full soybean genome (Fig. 5B) confirmed the pervasive collinearity between homologous chromosome pairs (gray ribbons), within which GlyPDF syntenic connections (red lines) are embedded. The red connections span multiple chromosome pairs and are distributed across both sub-genomes, consistent with a WGD origin for most paralogous pairs rather than local tandem duplication alone. Taken together, the circos and linear synteny data suggest that both WGD-derived segmental duplication and localized tandem expansion have contributed to the present-day chromosomal architecture of the GlyPDF family in soybean.

Tissue-specific expression profiles of SoyPDF genes

The expression patterns of the 27 GlyPDF genes across five major soybean tissues (root, stem, leaf, flower, and pod) were examined by qRT-PCR at the V2 developmental stage. GlyPDF genes displayed broad but highly tissue-variable expression profiles that clustered into three major expression-pattern groups (Fig. 6). Group A (predominantly GlyPDF2 members, including GlyPDF2.5, 2.7, 2.8, and 2.9) showed the highest expression in roots and pods, with comparatively low transcript levels in leaves and stems. Group B (including GlyPDF3.3, 3.4, and several GlyPDF4 members) exhibited preferential expressions in flowers and reproductive tissues. Group C (GlyPDF1.1, GlyPDF1.2, GlyPDF2.1, and GlyPDF2.2) showed constitutive, broad expression across all tissues examined, suggesting housekeeping-like roles in basal innate immunity.

Among the most highly expressed members, GlyPDF2.7 showed over 40-fold higher transcript abundance in roots than in leaves, consistent with its predicted role as a root-exuded antimicrobial peptide that protects the rhizosphere from soilborne fungal pathogens. GlyPDF2.5 was robustly expressed in pods, paralleling the well-described seed-protective function of PDF2-type defensins in legumes. In contrast, GlyPDF3.1 exhibited the most restricted tissue expression, with significant transcript accumulation only in flower and pod tissues, suggesting a developmental role during reproductive growth. Members of the GlyPDF4 subfamily were generally expressed at lower levels than GlyPDF2 members across all tissues, although GlyPDF4.1 and GlyPDF4.6 showed moderate constitutive expression across all organs.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this study presents the first genome-wide characterization of the plant defensin (PDF) gene family in soybean, identifying 27 GlyPDF genes encoding 31 protein isoforms in the Williams82 v4 genome. The soybean PDF family is notably larger than the 15-member Arabidopsis family and comparable in size to those reported in other legumes, reflecting the expanded genetic complexity often associated with polyploid plant genomes [24, 25]. The four-subfamily classification (GlyPDF1–4) established here, following the Arabidopsis nomenclature, provides a unified framework for comparative functional studies across species.

The predominance of the GlyPDF2 subfamily (11 genes, > 40% of the family) and its members’ characteristic physicochemical properties, small size (68–95 aa), basic pI, positive GRAVY, and high aliphatic index are consistent with the well-established biochemical requirements for membrane-active antimicrobial activity [26–28]. The two GlyPDF1 members, by contrast, encode unusually large precursor proteins (~ 332–334 aa), a feature shared with PDF1-type defensins in other plants that are processed post-translationally to release the mature defensin domain. The functional significance of the extended C-terminal propeptide domain in GlyPDF1 members warrants further investigation, as propeptide regions in other defensins have been implicated in vacuolar targeting and auto-inhibition of antimicrobial activity before secretion [28].

The unequal chromosomal distribution of GlyPDF genes, with a pronounced enrichment on chromosome 8, is a recurring feature of gene families that have undergone recent tandem expansion. The three tandem clusters identified here, particularly the GlyPDF2.6–2.7–2.8 array on chromosome 8 with Ks values of 0.04–0.12, are indicative of a very recent evolutionary origin, within the last 5–10 Mya. This pattern of localized, recent duplication has been documented in other species for antimicrobial peptide gene families and is thought to reflect positive selection for expanded innate immune capacity under pathogen pressure [27, 29]. In contrast, the majority of WGD-derived homeologous GlyPDF pairs (Ks 0.18–0.92, 12–15 Mya) are consistent with the well-documented Glycine-lineage polyploidization event and have all evolved under strong purifying selection (Ka/Ks < 1.0), arguing that both gene copies have been maintained due to functional importance rather than redundancy.

The promoter analysis revealed a notable enrichment of jasmonate-responsive elements in the promoters of most GlyPDF genes (22/27), consistent with the established paradigm of JA-dependent PDF gene regulation in plant defense [30, 31]. The co-occurrence of W-box motifs (WRKY-binding sites) in 14 promoters suggests an additional layer of transcriptional control via PAMP-triggered immunity (PTI) signaling cascades, in which WRKY transcription factors act as key regulators of antimicrobial gene expression downstream of pattern recognition receptor activation. Notably, the co-occurrence of jasmonate and W-box elements in the promoters of the co-expressed chromosome 8 cluster (GlyPDF2.6–2.8) may explain their coordinated induction kinetics observed following Fusarium challenge. The prevalence of ABRE elements in 19 promoters, particularly in GlyPDF2 and GlyPDF4 members, also raises the possibility of cross-talk between biotic and abiotic stress signaling in the regulation of these defensins, a phenomenon increasingly recognized as an important adaptive strategy in plants [32, 33].

The tissue-specific expression profiling revealed that GlyPDF2.7, with its > 40-fold root enrichment, is a candidate of interest for a role in rhizosphere defense, paralleling the root-preferential expression and root-protective function of PDF2-type defensins in other legumes. The robust pod expression of GlyPDF2.5 is similarly consistent with the well-described role of PDF2-class defensins in protecting developing seeds from fungal invasion in pea and other legume species [34, 35]. Taken together, the tissue expression data suggests a spatial division of defensin-mediated immunity in soybean, with GlyPDF2 members providing frontline protection at pathogen entry points (roots and seeds), GlyPDF3/GlyPDF4 members contributing to reproductive tissue defense, and GlyPDF1 members potentially serving constitutive, housekeeping roles in systemic immunity.

The Fusarium oxysporum challenge experiment provided direct evidence that a large proportion of GlyPDF genes are transcriptionally responsive to fungal infection. The biphasic response pattern, with GlyPDF2 members responding as early as 6 hpi and GlyPDF3 members maintaining elevated expression through 72 hpi, suggests that distinct defensin subfamilies fulfill temporally non-overlapping roles in the immune response. Early-response defensins (GlyPDF2.5, 2.7, 2.9) are likely deployed as rapid-response antimicrobial effectors during pathogen recognition, while late-response defensins (GlyPDF3.3, 3.4) may contribute to containment of established infection. This functional stratification is broadly consistent with the “two-wave” model of plant innate immunity, in which an initial burst of defense activation is followed by a sustained, amplified response [36].

The transient downregulation of GlyPDF1.1 and GlyPDF1.2 at 6–12 hpi deserves further investigation. This early suppression of constitutively expressed defensins is consistent with pathogen-mediated immune evasion strategies in which hemibiotrophic fungi deploy effector proteins that suppress basal plant defenses during the biotrophic phase of infection [8]. Identifying the effectors responsible for this suppression would be a valuable direction for future research.

Despite the breadth of this study, several limitations should be noted. First, while the qRT-PCR data reveal clear transcriptional responses, protein-level validation and direct antifungal activity assays are needed to confirm that the identified GlyPDF candidates are functionally active antimicrobial peptides in vivo. Second, the inoculation experiments used a single F. oxysporum strain and a single developmental stage; broader screening across multiple pathogen strains, additional fungal species (e.g., Phytophthora sojae, Sclerotinia sclerotiorum), and multiple developmental stages would provide a more complete picture of GlyPDF gene deployment in soybean disease resistance. Third, functional characterization of the most promising candidates (GlyPDF2.7, GlyPDF2.8, GlyPDF2.9, and GlyPDF3.3) through transgenic overexpression or CRISPR-based knockout in soybean would directly test their contributions to resistance against Fusarium root rot.

Conclusion

This study reports a genome-wide identification and characterization of the plant defensin (PDF) gene family in soybean, identifying 27 GlyPDF loci across 13 chromosomes of the Williams82 v4 genome and encoding 31 predicted protein isoforms. Phylogenetic and structural analyses resolved these genes into four subfamilies (GlyPDF1–4) with distinct motif compositions, domain architectures, and exon–intron organizations, indicating functional divergence following family expansion. The GlyPDF2 subfamily was the largest (11 genes, accounting for > 40% of members) and displayed physicochemical properties broadly consistent with those of membrane-active cationic antimicrobial peptides. Synteny analysis identified 19 intra-genomic collinear pairs and three tandem duplication clusters, suggesting that whole-genome duplication and localized tandem expansion have both contributed to family size. Ka/Ks ratios below 1.0 across all syntenic pairs indicate that duplicated copies have been retained under purifying selection. Promoter analysis revealed enrichment of jasmonate-, abscisic acid-, and salicylate-responsive elements, as well as W-box motifs, providing a regulatory basis for the observed tissue-specific and stress-responsive expression patterns. Under Fusarium oxysporum challenge, 12 of 15 tested genes were transcriptionally responsive. GlyPDF2.2 showed the strongest sustained induction, GlyPDF1 members displayed a biphasic suppression-then-induction pattern, and GlyPDF2.8 and GlyPDF4.7a were progressively induced at later infection stages. These findings identify GlyPDF2.2, GlyPDF2.8, GlyPDF3.2a/b, and GlyPDF4.7a as candidates for functional validation through transgenic or CRISPR-based approaches, with potential relevance to breeding soybeans with improved resistance to soilborne fungal pathogens.
