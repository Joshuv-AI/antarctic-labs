Penrose P2 Tilings: A Study of Fully Leafed Induced Subtrees
Mathieu Cloutier, Alain Goupil, Alexandre Blondin Massé

Abstract
We present new results about fully leafed induced subtrees in Penrose P2 tilings. We first determine the graph structure of these subtrees and show that they are caterpillars, up to an appendix of at most six tiles. We then study bi-infinite fully leafed induced caterpillars in P2 tilings and their geometric properties. In particular, we refute the conjecture proposed by C. Porrier, A. Goupil and A. Blondin Massé that there is a unique bi-infinite fully leafed caterpillar in Penrose P2 tilings.

Given a tiling, one may be interested in finding connected and acyclic sets
S

Graph structure

It can be shown that every fully leafed induced subtree of a P2-graph that has 8 internal tiles (i.e. tiles of degree 2 or more) or fewer is a subcaterpillar of a caterpillar shown in Figure

There are two possibilities for extending a prime caterpillar

Theorem 1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Every fully leafed induced subtree of a P2-graph belongs to one of the following structures:

1.

A subcaterpillar of a prime caterpillar;

2.

A caterpillar obtained by grafting several prime caterpillars with at most one proper subcaterpillar of a prime caterpillar structure;

3.

A caterpillar obtained by grafting several prime caterpillars, to which an appendix of at most 2 internal tiles and 4 leaves is grafted.

Thus, in order to study fully leafed induced subtrees in P2 tilings, it is sufficient to study caterpillars obtained by grafting prime caterpillars, since any fully leafed induced subtree can be obtained from such a caterpillar by adding a small number of tiles. Caterpillars obtained by grafting prime caterpillars are precisely the fully leafed induced subtrees that have the property of saturation
[
3
]
. Let us explain what it means. The leaf function
L_{P2}(n)
gives the number of leaves of a fully leafed induced subtree of order
n

that corrects the one given in
[
10
]

Proposition 1

(
[
3
]
)
.

L_{P2}(n)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }0\leq n\leq 1,\\
\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1&\text{if }2\leq n\leq 18,\\
8\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{17}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\dfrac{n\bmod 17}{2}\right\rfloor+1+\mathbbm{1}(n\bmod 17=1)&\text{if }n\geq 19.\end{cases}

Let
\overline{L_{P2}}
be the lowest linear function that upperbounds
L_{P2}
. We say that a fully leafed induced subtree of order

if

Then, the problem of finding fully leafed caterpillars in P2-graphs becomes the problem of finding paths in star-graphs that determine fully leafed caterpillars. For
\mathcal{C}

We are now interested in the angle formed by a prime caterpillar in a star-graph. Let

The angle measured in radians of the prime caterpillars

We claim that if the star-graph path of a fully leafed saturated caterpillar

5
Bi-infinite fully leafed caterpillars

Since we now know how to graft caterpillars to construct large fully leafed caterpillars, the natural question that comes to mind is to ask if there are infinite such caterpillars. Since a caterpillar can be extended in two directions, we are interested in finding bi-infinite fully leafed caterpillars. A first one was found in
[
10
]
, and we formalise its construction in
[
3
]
. In
[
10
]
, it is conjectured that this bi-infinite caterpillar is the unique bi-infinite fully leafed caterpillar of
P2
-graphs. To approach this conjecture, we focus on results that allow us to identify caterpillars that cannot be extended to bi-infinite fully leafed caterpillars.

(

There exists no bi-infinite fully leafed caterpillar that contains two consecutive

6
Conclusion

We now have a better understanding of the structure of fully leafed induced subtrees in P2 tilings. By Theorem
1
, they are caterpillars, up to an appendix of at most six tiles, and the saturated ones are caterpillars. We disproved the conjecture from
[
10
]
that there is a unique bi-infinite fully leafed caterpillar in P2 tilings.

Since we now know that there exists more than one bi-infinite fully leafed caterpillar in Penrose P2 tilings, we aim to find them all. We have already eliminated several patterns. It is also possible to consider the star-graph chains of these bi-infinite structures as words over a 3-letter alphabet whose letters are either the colors of the vertices in these chains (red, green, and blue) or the angles of the prime caterpillars, and to study the properties of these words. We have begun this work in
[
3
]
. Of course, it would also be interesting to describe the fully leafed induced subtrees in other aperiodic tilings.
