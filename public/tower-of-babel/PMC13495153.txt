Predicting the region of tumor micro-infiltration in glioblastoma peritumoral edema using a multiparametric mri radiomics model
Authors: Yuxiao Wu, Haobin Liu, Mei Du, Yongli Gao, Yanxia Zhang, Mingguang Wang, Quanyu Sun, Jinling Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo predict tumor micro-infiltration (TMI) within glioblastoma (GBM) peritumoral edema (PTE) using MRI for postoperative radiation planning.MethodsStudy cohort consisted of a training group (TG) with 50 GBM and 50 brain metastasis tumor cases, a validation group (VG) with 25 GBM and 19 meningioma cases, and an image-pathology point-to-point VG (IPVG) with 10 additional GBM cases. Besides univariate analysis of gray-level histogram parameters (GLHP) of peritumoral edema (PTE) from contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging (CE-T1WI), T2 fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (T2FLAIR), and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps, LASSO regression removed collinear parameters. Then, forward stepwise logistic regression, SVM, and random forest were used to build TMI prediction models in PTE, with efficiency evaluated via ROC curves. In the IPVG, the coincidence rate between Python-based TMI predictions and biopsy pathology results was calculated.ResultsUnivariate analysis in the TG revealed that GLHP differences between GBM and BM PTE belts were prominent within 3 cm of PTE. After removing collinear parameters, the ADC map or CE-T1WI-based prediction model outperformed the T2FLAIR-based one. The model with the 1-cm GBM PTE belt and 2-cm BM PTE belt pairing showed superior discrimination. Incorporating ADC and CE-T1WI parameters, the RF model with 6 parameters (Ratio-maxi-enhancedT1/mean-enhancedT1, Ratio-miniADC/meanADC, etc.) demonstrated the best discrimination than other models, with an ROC curve (AUC) of 0.836 in the TG and 0.844 in the VG. In the IPVG, the TMI prediction model had an overall accuracy of 81.25%.ConclusionThe multiparametric RF model preoperative MRI can predict the TMI within PTE of GBM.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13014-026-02874-1.Keywords: Glioblastoma, Peritumoral edema, Neoplasm invasiveness, Diffusion magnetic resonance imaging, Radiomics

Importance of the study

Precisely defining glioblastoma’s microscopic invasion in peritumoral edema is critical yet unachievable with conventional MRI. This study develops a clinically practical solution by creating a multiparametric model from standard ADC maps and contrast-enhanced T1WI, enhanced with machine learning. Directly validated against histopathology, our approach provides a reliable, non-invasive tool for predicting tumor micro-infiltration. This has significant translational value, offering a viable method to optimize surgical and radiotherapy planning for improved local control, using existing clinical MRI protocols for immediate potential impact.

Introduction

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most common and highly aggressive primary malignant tumor in the central nervous system, characterized by widespread peritumoral edema (PTE) and a dismal prognosis with a 5-year survival rate of approximately 10% [1–3]. Tumor recurrence, primarily driven by microscopic tumor infiltration (TMI) beyond the radiologically visible tumor boundary, remains a key challenge [4, 5].

The formation of peritumoral edema in GBM involves complex mechanisms, including blood-brain barrier disruption mediated by vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) [6, 7], tumor angiogenesis [8–10], and aquaporin-4 (AQP4) overexpression [11–13]. Despite advances in surgical, radiotherapeutic, and chemotherapeutic interventions, precise delineation of tumor boundaries remains difficult, complicating complete resection and radiotherapy target definition [14, 15].

Conventional MRI sequences inadequately detect TMI within PTE [4, 16]. Advanced functional MRI techniques offer improved insights into tumor infiltration. These include dynamic susceptibility contrast (DSC) imaging, which reveals elevated relative cerebral blood volume (rCBV) and relative cerebral blood flow (rCBF) [17]; magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS), which shows elevated Cho/Cr and Cho/NAA ratios [18]; and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) with derived apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps [19]. Multi-parameter models integrating these techniques demonstrate superior predictive accuracy by capturing diverse aspects of tumor biology [20, 21]. However, challenges such as parameter collinearity, model overfitting, and methodological complexity hinder their clinical adoption [22, 23].

Among these techniques, ADC mapping stands out for its clinical practicality, simplicity, and cost-effectiveness [24]. Histogram analysis of ADC maps (e.g., mean, max, min ADC) shows promise in detecting TMI [25, 26]. Yet, no study has explored a multiparametric model combining ADC with conventional MRI to predict TMI in GBM’s PTE.

This study aims to develop and validate a streamlined, multiparametric model integrating ADC and conventional MRI parameters to preoperatively predict TMI in GBM’s PTE. By addressing parameter collinearity and leveraging machine learning algorithms, we seek to provide a practical tool for optimizing surgical and radiotherapy planning.

Materials and methods

Patient characteristics

This retrospective study analyzed patients with brain tumors, including 50 with brain metastasis (BM), 75 with glioblastoma (GBM), and 19 with meningioma (MG), treated between March 2019 and March 2021. All patients were strictly divided at the individual patient level into three mutually exclusive cohorts with no overlapping patients, fully preventing data leakage between training, validation, and testing sets. The cohort was divided into three distinct groups. The Training Group (TG) was composed of 50 GBM cases and all 50 BM cases, with the latter serving as a control for non-infiltrative edema. The remaining 25 GBM cases were allocated to the Validation Group (VG) along with all 19 MG cases, which provided an additional non-infiltrative control. An independent Image-Pathology Validation Group (IPVG) consisted of an additional 10 GBM cases that were not included in either the TG or VG and that underwent intraoperative stereotactic biopsy within peritumoral edema. This grouping strategy is detailed in the flow diagram (Fig. 1). Key demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients are summarized in Table 1. There were no significant differences in age or sex distribution between the groups (p > 0.05).

All GBM patients underwent total or subtotal resection followed by temozolomide-based concurrent chemoradiotherapy and adjuvant chemotherapy. MRI Acquisition.

All subjects underwent whole-brain MRI on a Siemens 3.0T scanner. Sequences included CE-T1WI, T2FLAIR, and DWI. DWI was performed using a 3-scan trace mode with b-values of 0, 1000, and 3000 s/mm². ADC maps were automatically generated by the workstation. All imaging data were stored and analyzed in standard DICOM format.

Image processing

Preoperative CE-T1WI, T2FLAIR, and ADC images were processed using the open-source software 3D Slicer (version 5.0.3). The “General Registration” module was used for spatial registration of all sequences to ensure spatial consistency. The “Segment Editor” module was then used to outline volumes of interest (VOIs). Image outlining was independently performed by two radiation oncologists with over 10 years of experience in neuroimaging. On CE-T1WI, the enhancing tumor lesion was outlined; on T2FLAIR, the peritumoral edema area was outlined. Using the “Logical operators” function, the edema area was subdivided into three concentric subregions: 0–1 cm from the tumor boundary (1-cm belt), 1–2 cm (2-cm belt), and 2–3 cm (3-cm belt). The edema around BM and MG was defined as non-infiltrative, while that around GBM was defined as tumor-micro-infiltrative. All images underwent spatial registration followed by image harmonization. This step aimed to correct for image variability induced by software updates and minor adjustments to scanning parameters during the 2-year data collection period with the same scanner.

Radiomics feature extraction

Radiomic features were extracted from the three peritumoral edema subregions (0–1 cm, 1–2 cm, 2–3 cm) on contrast-enhanced T1WI, T2FLAIR, and ADC maps using the radiomics module in 3D Slicer. For each sequence and each subregion, a comprehensive set of 18 features was extracted. These features encompassed basic histogram parameters (including mean, standard deviation, maximum, minimum, and median values), higher-order statistics (such as 10th and 90th percentile values, energy, entropy, interquartile range, skewness, and kurtosis), and custom ratio parameters (specifically, the ratios of the maximum and minimum values to the mean value). In total, 162 features were initially extracted per patient (3 sequences × 3 subregions × 18 features). The detailed definitions and formulas for all features are provided in Supplementary File 1.

Establishment of the imaging prediction model for tumor micro-invasion in the edema area around GBM

In the TG, imaging parameters from GBM and BM edema bands were paired for analysis. Pairing groups included: GBM 1-cm vs. BM 1-cm; GBM 1-cm vs. BM 2-cm; GBM 1-cm vs. BM 3-cm; GBM 2-cm vs. BM 2-cm; GBM 2-cm vs. BM 3-cm; and GBM 3-cm vs. BM 3-cm. For each pairing, univariate comparative analysis was first performed on the gray-level histogram parameters. Parameters with significant collinearity were removed using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)regression. Subsequently, forward stepwise logistic regression model (FSLRM), support vector machine (SVM), and random forest (RF) models were constructed. Model efficacy was evaluated using ROC curves, and the best-performing model was validated in the VG. The model with the best efficacy in both TG and VG was selected as the optimal model. Statistical analyses were performed using R (v4.2.1) and STATA/MP (v18.0). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Tissue biopsy and pathological detection

Under general anesthesia, preoperative CT/MRI images were imported into a stereotactic surgical planning system. After skin disinfection and registration using a navigation probe, an optimal biopsy trajectory was planned to avoid critical neurovascular structures. A small scalp incision and burr hole were created, followed by insertion of a biopsy needle along the planned path to obtain a tissue sample (approximately 0.5 × 0.2 cm). The specimen was immediately fixed in 10% neutral formalin for 12–24 h, followed by standard processing including dehydration, paraffin embedding, and sectioning into 4–6 μm slices. Sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for pathological evaluation.

Statistical analysis

For the differences in the histogram characteristics of the imaging parameters between the tumor-micro-infiltrative area and the non-infiltrative area in the edema area under different imaging sequences, the univariate analysis was performed by combining the non-parametric test and the categorical variable test. For the numerical variables, the Mann-Whitney U-test was used for comparison; for the categorical variables, the chi-square test (χ² test) was used for analysis.

In the feature selection stage, to avoid the collinearity problem among multiple parameters, the LASSO regression analysis was first used for feature screening.

The radiomics features screened via LASSO regression analysis with 10-fold cross-validation—where the Training Cohort was randomly divided into 10 subsets, nine of which were used as the training set and one as the validation set in a rotational manner, with this process repeated 10 times and the average performance across all iterations taken as the basis for feature selection—were further incorporated into three multivariate analytical models: FSLRM, SVM, and RFM for validation and optimization.The Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC curve) was used to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of the models, and the optimal model for distinguishing the tumor-micro-infiltrative edema area from the non-tumor-infiltrative edema area was screened out, and the best threshold value of the optimal model was calculated. The best threshold for the final model was determined by maximizing the Youden’s index based on the ROC curve of the Training Cohort.

In this study, P < 0.05 was set as the criterion for statistically significant differences. All statistical analysis was completed by cross-verification using the R language (version 4.2.1) and the STATA/MP statistical software (version 18.0; Stata Corp LLC, Texas, USA).

Discussion

Accurate delineation of the TMI zone in the PTE of GBM is critical for surgical resection planning and postoperative radiation target volume design, representing a persistent challenge in clinical research. This study introduces a multi-parametric imaging prediction model based on gray-scale histogram parameters derived from ADC maps and contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging (T1WI), demonstrating substantial utility in predicting the spatial distribution of TMI.

Emerging multimodal quantitative MRI techniques have provided critical biological insights into GBM microenvironment characterization, enhancing tumor infiltration assessment. For instance, Anahita et al. conducted a radiogenomic analysis of pediatric low-grade glioma (pLGG) using multiparametric MRI and machine learning, providing new methods for disease prediction and treatment [27].Khalid et al. developed a multimodal MRI radiomic model for 80 DIPG patients, achieving 87.8%/82.1%/78.3% balanced accuracy in predicting H3.1/ACVR1/TP53 mutations via leave-one-out validation, outperforming clinical models even with missing modalities [28].Su et al. investigated the H3 K27M mutation status of diffuse midline gliomas using multimodal MRI technology and established a model with an AUC of 0.898 (0.976 in the validation set) [29]. Notably, the AUC of our proposed multi-parametric model, derived from ADC and contrast-enhanced T1WI histogram parameters, consistently exceeded 0.8 in both training and validation cohorts, outperforming or equalling existing models with fewer required functional sequences, thereby underscoring its clinical applicability.

The core parameters of our predictive model, derived from ADC maps and contrast-enhanced T1WI, are physiologically interpretable. This study focused on gray-level histogram parameters because they are stable, computationally efficient, and less sensitive to ROI segmentation variability, thereby facilitating clinical translation. We did not include texture features (e.g., GLCM) because they typically require larger homogeneous regions and are more sensitive to image resolution and noise, which could increase model complexity and reduce generalizability. Future studies will integrate both histogram and texture features to further improve predictive performance. While ADC values reflect tumor malignancy, their variability due to MRI machine parameters and operational factors limits stability across cases. The RatioADC, normalized by the mean ADC of the contralateral hemisphere, has shown improved stability in assessing PTE. However, our study uniquely defines RatioADC as the ratio of the maximum to mean ADC within the region of interest (ROI), capturing the regional heterogeneity of ADC values. This approach accounts for inter-patient variability introduced by MRI equipment and environmental factors, aligning with strategies proposed by Tepe et al. for multi-parametric model optimization [30] .

In the PTE of GBM, water molecule motion is modulated by glial cells and disseminated tumor cells. Aberrant expression of AQP4, VEGFA, and MMP-9 disrupts water homeostasis, contributing to edema formation [11, 31]. Our ADC parameter findings, corroborated by biopsy pathology, suggest that early micro-infiltration may manifest as decreased mean and maximum ADC values, while advanced infiltration with necrosis may further reduce minimum ADC values. The significant changes observed in ADC histogram parameters (Ratio-maxiADC/meanADC and Ratio-miniADC/meanADC) within the edema zone indicate their potential to detect early and dynamic TMI.

Enhanced T1WI histogram parameters have been linked to tumor invasiveness and malignancy. For example, kurtosis and skewness in enhanced T1WI reflect tumor heterogeneity and growth patterns [32]. Our study extends this by demonstrating that Ratio-maxi-enhancedT1/mean-enhancedT1, skewness-enhancedT1, and mini-enhancedT1 are strongly associated with PTE infiltration. These parameters may reflect tumor cell distribution asymmetry, proliferative activity, and vascular basement membrane disruption, respectively. The introduction of ratio-based parameters (e.g., Ratio-maxi-enhancedT1/mean-enhancedT1) amplifies sensitivity to local structural heterogeneity, reducing inter-patient variability.

Several limitations of our study should be acknowledged. First, its retrospective nature may introduce selection bias. Second, the sample size, particularly for the meningioma cohort in the validation set and the IPVG, was relatively small.Notably, the absence of external validation with independent cohorts from different institutions may restrict the generalizability of our model findings. Future prospective studies with larger, multi-center cohorts are needed to further validate and refine the model. Third, the model performance might vary across different GBM molecular subtypes or BM primary origins, which we did not perform subgroup analyses for due to limited cases. Lastly, the current gold standard for TMI relies on biopsy-proven tumor cells, which might not fully capture the continuous spectrum of microstructural changes within the PTE. Correlative studies with advanced molecular pathology could provide deeper insights in the future.

In conclusion, we have developed a multiparametric MRI model based on ADC and contrast-enhanced T1WI histogram parameters that demonstrates satisfactory performance in predicting TMI within the PTE of GBM. This model, characterized by its relative simplicity and reliance on clinically available sequences, offers a practical and valuable tool for assisting in preoperative planning and postoperative radiotherapy target delineation, potentially contributing to improved local control and outcomes for GBM patients.
