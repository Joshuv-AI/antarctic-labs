Belimumab induces early and sustained resolution of lupus thrombocytopenia
Authors: Nicolai Leuchten, Ioannis Parodis, Ralph Brinks, Martin Aringer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveBelimumab has well-established efficacy in improving overall SLE disease activity and preventing flares and organ damage. Belimumab effects have been demonstrated across organ domains but have not been reported in detail for haematological manifestations. We investigated the efficacy of belimumab in thrombocytopenia and leucopenia in patients with SLE.MethodsAnonymised data on haematological parameters from five phase III randomised controlled clinical trials of belimumab were provided by GSK. These datasets included 1769 patients treated with belimumab and 1144 patients who received placebo, on top of standard therapy. We selected patients with active thrombocytopenia or leucopenia at baseline and compared sustained resolution of symptoms from the time of resolution through week 52 by means of log-rank tests.ResultsAt baseline, thrombocytopenia and leucopenia were present in 39 and 346 belimumab-treated patients and 25 and 203 placebo recipients, respectively. Thrombocytopenia resolved earlier with belimumab versus placebo, starting from week 2 on therapy and in a higher proportion of belimumab-treated patients (p=0.0267). One-third (13/39) of belimumab-treated and 8% (2/25) of placebo-receiving patients reached persistent resolution. In contrast to thrombocytopenia, observed improvements in leucopenia were slower, with no apparent difference between belimumab and placebo.ConclusionThis post hoc analysis of data from five large phase III RCTs suggests a benefit from belimumab compared with placebo in the haematological domain. A benefit from belimumab was seen in improving thrombocytopenia, but not leucopenia. While mild leucopenia is rarely clinically relevant, the data on thrombocytopenia will inform differential therapeutic decisions in patients with active SLE.Keywords: Systemic Lupus Erythematosus, Thrombocytopenia, Biological Products, Autoimmune Diseases, Clinical Trial

Introduction

SLE is a prototypical systemic autoimmune disease with highly heterogeneous clinical manifestations and disease courses across patients.1 2 Clinical trials typically either focus on lupus nephritis,3,5 which occurs in approximately one-third of patients with SLE,6 or on non-renal manifestations.7,9 The latter most commonly comprise not only mucocutaneous and/or musculoskeletal disease but also serositis and haematological manifestations. Many other manifestations are rather uncommon and thus difficult to adequately analyse.

Belimumab is an antibody against B-cell cytokine B lymphocyte stimulator/B cell activating factor. The phase III randomised clinical trials (RCTs) of belimumab BLISS-52,10 BLISS-7611 and BLISS-SC9 used SLE Responder Index 4 (SRI-4) as a trial endpoint.12 SRI-4 combines a decrease in the SLE Disease Activity Index (SLEDAI)13 by at least four points with a lack of worsening in the British Isles Lupus Assessment Group (BILAG) index14 and the physician’s global assessment.

With this endpoint, the trials were able to prove therapeutic efficacy against overall SLE disease activity. Additional analyses showed prevention of SLE flares, reduction in glucocorticoid doses and increased rates of patients in Lupus Low Disease Activity State (LLDAS) and Definition of Remission in SLE (DORIS) remission.15,17 While highly relevant from an outcome perspective, these measures do not directly inform physicians which approach is likely to control disease activity in specific organ domains. Arguably, one exception is the BLISS-LN trial, which focused on the effects of belimumab in patients with active lupus nephritis.3

In order to make adequate choices among available therapeutic options, it is important to know the spectrum of manifestations that can be successfully treated by the various agents. For belimumab, an early SLEDAI-based analysis suggested efficacy in the mucocutaneous, musculoskeletal and vascular domains and showed numerically greater effects in the serosal and constitutional domains.18 In recent post hoc analyses of phase III data, lupus arthritis and mucocutaneous SLE were analysed in detail. Sustained resolution of symptoms could be demonstrated from as early as weeks 8 and 12 of therapy for arthritis and for mucocutaneous SLE, respectively.19 20

While the original SLEDAI domain analysis could not demonstrate belimumab treatment effects in the haematological domain,18 about half of patients with SLE may present with leucopenia and about 16% with thrombocytopenia,6 typically defined as a white blood cell count of less than 4.0 x 10ˆ9/L and a platelet count of less than 100 x 10ˆ9/L, respectively. While not compatible with the treat-to-target goal of DORIS remission,21 mild haematologic manifestations usually will not require treatment. Anti-infectious prophylaxis is considered in severe leucopenia.22 For severe lupus thrombocytopenia, the EULAR recommendations suggest treatment with glucocorticoids, intravenous immunoglobulin and/or rituximab at platelet numbers of 20–30 x 10ˆ9/L or lower, and discuss maintenance therapy with mycophenolic acid.23

Even milder cytopenia may, however, influence the choice of therapy in patients with SLE with other non-renal manifestations. It is therefore relevant to also investigate the effects of belimumab on these haematological manifestations, which formed the scope of the present study on cumulated clinical trial data from five large RCTs of belimumab in SLE.

Methods

Based on a Data Sharing Agreement from 6 November 2019, GSK provided anonymised data on haematological parameters from five large RCTs of belimumab, that is, BLISS-52 (NCT00424476),24 BLISS-76 (NCT00410384),11 BLISS-NEA (NCT01345253),25 BLISS-SC (NCT01484496)9 and EMBRACE (NCT01632241).26 These datasets included 1769 patients treated with an approved dose of belimumab (10 mg/kg IV on days 0, 14, 28, and every 4 weeks thereafter, or 200 mg SC weekly) and 1144 patients who received placebo, all on top of non-biological standard therapy. All trials were conducted in compliance with good clinical practice on ethics committee approval in all trial centres, and informed consent was obtained from all involved individuals. The primary results of these trials have been published elsewhere.911 24,26 The performed analyses have to be considered exploratory, since the data of the included phase III studies were originally collected and powered for a different research question.

Haemolytic anaemia is an uncommon SLE manifestation and could not be found in numbers sufficient for analysis in these five trials. For this reason, we focused on lupus thrombocytopenia and lupus leucopenia, selecting patients with these manifestations at the baseline assessment. Thrombocytopenia and leucopenia were defined as per the European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology/American College of Rheumatology (EULAR/ACR) classification criteria as a platelet count lower than 100 x 10ˆ9/L and a white blood cell count lower than 4.0 x 10ˆ9/L, respectively. Haematology BILAG data were extracted and analysed at baseline and at 12, 24 and 52 weeks.

Since, from a curative perspective, SLE manifestations should optimally resolve and not reappear under ongoing therapy, the primary analysis of this study was on sustained resolution of the respective manifestation, defined as normal cell counts for platelets and leucocytes from the time of resolution to the 52-week visit. Subjects who achieved resolution only on week 52 were included in this analysis. Persistent resolution was compared between the belimumab and placebo arms by means of log-rank tests. In addition, the distribution of actual cell counts and percentages of patients with BILAG scores A, B and C were analysed over time.

Samples following Gaussian distributions are described by the mean±SD and compared by Welch’s t-tests. Data not normally distributed are described by the median (range) and/or IQR and compared by Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Differences between the curves of sustained resolution of the index items were analysed by log-rank tests. Analyses were performed with the GraphPad Prism V.10.2.3 and IBM SPSS Statistics V.29.0.0.0 software.

Lupus thrombocytopenia and leucopenia

We next investigated thrombocytopenia and leucopenia in more detail. At baseline, 64 patients had a platelet count lower than 100 x 10ˆ9/L, of whom 39 received belimumab and 25 received placebo. Baseline platelet counts in these patients ranged from 23 to 99 x 10ˆ9/L, with no significant differences between the belimumab and placebo arms (table 2). As expected, more patients had a white blood cell count lower than 4.0 x 10ˆ9/L, namely, a total of 549 patients, of whom 346 received belimumab and 203 received placebo. Baseline white blood cell counts in patients with leucopenia ranged from 1.1 to 3.7 x 10ˆ9/L, again with no relevant differences between the belimumab and placebo groups (table 2).

Exploratory analyses of changes (deltas, Δ) in platelet counts showed non-significant trends for differences between belimumab and placebo at week 12 (week 0 to 12: median +32 (range −12 to +222) for belimumab vs +16 (−42 to +82) for placebo, p=0.0783) and at week 52 (week 0 to 52: median +37 (range −5 to +237) for belimumab vs +13 (−43 to +87) for placebo, p=0.0566), in favour of belimumab. In line with the main results on leucocytes, an exploratory analysis on both neutrophils and lymphocytes revealed no difference in either between the belimumab and placebo groups (data not shown).

Discussion

This post hoc analysis of combined data from five large phase III RCTs of belimumab911 24,26 indicates, for the first time in a placebo-controlled setting, that belimumab is associated with an improvement in thrombocytopenia, while no such association was observed for leucopenia. As a meaningful clinical concept, sustained resolution of thrombocytopenia, defined as normal/high values maintained from normalisation through week 52, occurred in one-third of belimumab-treated patients with thrombocytopenia at baseline. A clear differentiation from placebo in favour of belimumab was visible from as early as week 12.

Most patients in our analysis only had BILAG C haematologic manifestations, predominantly driven by leucopenia, along with BILAG A and B in other organ systems. Since mild leucopenia and lymphopenia rarely serve as indications for treatment, inclusion of most of these patients in the RCTs was primarily based on the other manifestations. Hence, the conclusions of this analysis cannot be directly extrapolated to clinical scenarios where haematologic manifestations are the primary focus of treatment.

It is worth noting that the finding of a belimumab effect on thrombocytopenia is novel and in part contradicting early reports,18 but in line with documented clinical experience.27,30 The differential effects of belimumab on thrombocytopenia and leucopenia imply that these two relatively common lupus cytopenias have different underlying immunopathological mechanisms, at least in part, despite reported associations between the two on a group level.31 32

Thrombocytopenia is a relatively common manifestation, which occurs in nearly one in six patients with SLE during their disease course6 and may necessitate an escalation of therapy. Lupus thrombocytopenia ranges from relatively mild thrombocytopenia, most commonly associated with the presence of anti-phospholipid antibodies, to severe disease, similar to idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, with potentially life-threatening episodes of bleeding.33,35 In addition to these two extremes, hypothetically caused by the direct effects of rather different kinds of autoantibodies, thrombocytopenia can also be induced by immune complexes. While this cannot be directly shown post hoc from the available results of the combined clinical trials, it is tempting to hypothesise that it is the latter that shows responsiveness to belimumab treatment, even though belimumab was also shown to reduce anti-phospholipid antibodies.29

It is important to stress that the analyses performed on the combined data of five SLE RCTs of belimumab are post hoc analyses that were not a part of the original analysis plan. While the hypotheses were independently tested in this dataset, the results of the statistical tests performed are therefore not comparable with those performed on predefined endpoints addressing research questions in the original studies. The study protocols were not designed to evaluate haematologic outcomes or any other specific organ manifestations. Nevertheless, all patients with the respective organ manifestations in the five RCTs were included, the data stem from double-blind placebo-controlled trials, and the numbers of participants are large, contributing to the robustness of the findings. In addition, the inclusion of the EMBRACE26 and BLISS-NEA25 trials, encompassing African-American patients and patients from China, Japan and South Korea, respectively, contribute to worldwide representation and ethnic diversity. Although pooling data from five trials with different populations across different global regions introduces substantial heterogeneity, it also enhances the robustness of the results due to increased numbers, and the observed findings can therefore be considered true effects of belimumab.

Taken together, this post hoc analysis of data from five large belimumab RCTs in active non-renal SLE revealed a rapid effect on lupus thrombocytopenia, with sustained resolution of the manifestation documented in about one-third of the patients. By contrast, no apparent effect of belimumab on leucopenia was observed. This study indicates, for the first time, a potential benefit of belimumab within the haematological domain.
