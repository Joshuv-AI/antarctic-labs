Level Crossing in Random Matrices. III. Analogs of Girko’s circular and Wigner’s semicircle laws
Boris Shapiro

Abstract
. We study the asymptotic distribution of level crossings for random matrix pencils A n + λ ​ B n A_{n}+\lambda B_{n} in several ensembles, including complex and real i.i.d. matrices and Gaussian/Hermitian settings. We derive a representation of the normalized log-discriminant in terms of pairwise eigenvalue interactions and formulate conditions under which its limit is governed by a deterministic potential. Under assumptions combining a uniform circular law, logarithmic tail control, and small-spacing (repulsion) estimates, we prove convergence of the empirical measure of level crossings to an explicit deterministic limit. In the complex Gaussian case these assumptions are verified (modulo a uniformity step), while in the general i.i.d. setting the results are conditional and motivated by universality theory. We further analyze the real case, showing that any limiting measure does not concentrate on the real projective line under suitable hypotheses, and discuss analogous phenomena for elliptic/Hermitian ensembles. Our results highlight the role of logarithmic energy and universality in governing spectral degeneracies of random matrix pencils.

1.
Introduction

Classical random matrix theory is largely concerned with the asymptotic
distribution of eigenvalues of a single large random matrix. Wigner’s
semicircle law and Girko’s circular law are the two basic universality
statements of this kind; see e.g.
[
Gi1
,
Gi2
,
Me
,
AGZ
]
. The present paper
studies a closely related, but genuinely different, object: the distribution
of degeneracies in a random one-parameter spectral family

A+\lambda B,\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

(1.1)

A level crossing is a value of
\lambda
for which
A+\lambda B
has a multiple
eigenvalue. Thus the problem is not to describe where the eigenvalues of one
random matrix lie, but rather where the branch points of the spectral cover of
the pencil are located in the parameter sphere
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
.

Such pencils occur naturally in perturbation theory:
A
is an unperturbed
operator,
B
is a perturbation, and
\lambda
is the perturbation parameter;
see
[
Ka
]
. They also arise in questions about monodromy of spectra and
random Riemann surfaces, cf.
[
GP
]
.

The present work continues the study
initiated in
[
ShZa
,
GrShZa
]
, where several exact formulas were obtained
for Gaussian pencils.
Several results in this paper are conditional on hypotheses such as uniform local circular law (UCL)
and small-repulsion (SR). Outside the Gaussian setting, these should be viewed as conjectural inputs
motivated by universality results in non-Hermitian random matrix theory.

For complex Gaussian matrices, the picture is especially simple. If
A
and

B
are independent complex Ginibre matrices, then for every matrix size the
level crossings are uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
; in the affine
coordinate
\lambda=x+iy
this means the density

\frac{dx\,dy}{\pi(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}.

(1.2)

This exact finite-dimensional identity, recalled below from
[
GrShZa
]
, is
the starting point of the paper. Our main aim is to understand to what extent
(
1.2
) survives beyond the Gaussian situation.

The first part of the paper treats non-Hermitian complex pencils with
independent entries. We formulate a conditional asymptotic uniform law: if
the normalized eigenvalue process of

\frac{A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}}{\sqrt{n(1+|\lambda|^{2})}}

satisfies a uniform circular-law statement and if close eigenvalue pairs do
not contribute to the leading logarithmic energy, then the empirical measure
of level crossings converges in probability to (
1.2
). The
proof is potential-theoretic. The discriminant of the characteristic
polynomial records precisely the level crossings, and the Poincaré–Lelong
formula converts the asymptotics of the normalized log-discriminant into the
limiting level-crossing measure. In this sense the result is the level-crossing
counterpart of Girko’s circular law.

The real case contains an additional phenomenon absent from complex Ginibre
pencils. Since real pencils are invariant under conjugation, level crossings
may lie on the real projective line, and such real crossings could in principle
produce a singular component in the limiting measure. We show that a singular component on
\mathbb{R}P^{1}
is excluded under a
stronger non-concentration assumption near
\mathbb{R}P^{1}
. The weaker
condition that the expected number of exactly real crossings is
o(n^{2})
only
implies that the finite-
n
mass on
\mathbb{R}P^{1}
vanishes.
Under such a non-concentration assumption, the possible singular contribution
on
\mathbb{RP}^{1}
is ruled out, and the remaining problem concerns the
two-dimensional distribution in the complex plane.
We then formulate a real i.i.d. conjecture
which refines the naive uniform-law expectation by allowing a correction
depending on the real symmetry class. For GOE pencils we go further: after
normalization, the entire dependence on the parameter
\lambda
is encoded by
the pseudocovariance

\tau(\lambda)=\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}.

At the level of expected logarithmic potentials, this reduces the possible
limiting densities to expressions obtained by applying

\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}
to a potential of the form

\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G\!\left(\frac{|1+\lambda^{2}|^{2}}{(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}\right).

In particular, the GOE uniformity conjecture of
[
GrShZa
]
is reduced to the
single analytic assertion that the pseudocovariance does not affect the leading

n^{2}
-term of the log-discriminant. This identifies precisely where the
difficulty of the real symmetric case lies.

The Hermitian case is the level-crossing analogue of the Wigner regime, and it
is qualitatively different from the Ginibre case. The limiting distribution is
not expected to be uniform on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. Already for
GUE_{2}
one has the
explicit density from
[
GrShZa
]

\frac{4}{\pi}\frac{|\Im\lambda|\,dx\,dy}{(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{3}},

which vanishes on the real axis and is invariant under the natural

SO(2)
-symmetry of the pencil. For general
GUE_{n}
we prove that the
large-
n
problem can be expressed through the Gaussian elliptic ensemble. The
normalized matrix

\frac{A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}}{\sqrt{1+|\lambda|^{2}}}

has elliptic parameter determined by

|\tau(\lambda)|^{2}=\frac{|1+\lambda^{2}|^{2}}{(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}=1-Y^{2},

where
Y
is the height coordinate on the sphere. Assuming a uniform
logarithmic-energy theorem for the elliptic ensemble in the bulk, we obtain a conditional version of
the existence of the limiting
GUE_{n}
level-crossing density on every compact
subset away from the equator
Y=0
. The limiting density is given
potential-theoretically by

\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}\left[\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G(1-Y^{2})\right],

where
G
is the logarithmic energy of the corresponding elliptic law. This
proves the proposed GUE limiting picture off the real axis and gives the
natural candidate for the universal Wigner-type law for Hermitian pencils.

2.
Complex matrices

Recall that the
GE_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
-ensemble on
Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the distribution where each entry of a
n\times n
-matrix is an independent complex random variable distributed as
N(0,\frac{1}{2})+iN(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and
Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the space of all complex-valued
n\times n
-matrices. The
GE_{\sigma^{2},n}^{\mathbb{C}}
-ensemble on
Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the distribution where each off-diagonal entry of a
n\times n
-matrix is an independent complex random variable distributed as
N(0,\frac{1}{2})+iN(0,\frac{1}{2})
and each diagonal entry is distributed as
N(0,\sigma^{2})+iN(0,\sigma^{2})
.
(Obviously, the
GE_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
-ensemble is a special case of the
GE_{\sigma^{2},n}^{\mathbb{C}}
-ensemble for
\sigma=1
.) Recall the following result of
[
GrShZa
]
dealing with complex Gaussian ensembles.

Theorem A

(cf. Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 of
[
GrShZa
]
)
.

For any given size
n\geq 1
, if
A
and
B
are independently chosen from the
GE_{\sigma^{2},n}^{\mathbb{C}}
-ensemble, then the distribution of the level crossings in the affine coordinate
\lambda=x+iy
of
\mathbb{C}
is given by

\mathcal{P}_{{GE}_{\sigma^{2},n}^{\mathbb{C}}}(x,y)dxdy=\frac{dxdy}{\pi(1+x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}=\frac{dxdy}{\pi(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}.

(2.1)

Remark 1
.

An alternative way to express this fact is as follows. Consider the standard cylindrical coordinate system
(\rho,\phi,Z)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3},
where
\rho\geq 0,0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi,Z\in\mathbb{R}
. Recall that

X=\rho\cos\phi,\;Y=\rho\sin\phi,\;Z=Z.

If we consider
(\phi,Z)
,
0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi,-1\leq Z\leq 1,
as coordinates on the unit sphere
S^{2}\simeq\mathbb{C}P^{1}
(with both poles removed), then in these coordinates the usual area element on the sphere is given by

dA=d\phi dZ.

Thus, in cylindrical coordinates
(\phi,Z)
,
0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi;\;-1\leq z\leq 1
parameterising the unit sphere
S^{2}
, the measure
\mathcal{P}_{{GE}_{\sigma^{2},n}^{\mathbb{C}}}(x,y)dxdy
given by (
2.1
) transforms into

\mathcal{P}_{{GE}_{\sigma^{2},n}^{\mathbb{C}}}(\phi,Z)d\phi dZ=\frac{d\phi dZ}{4\pi}.

(2.2)

Remark 2
.

Observe that formula (
2.1
) is independent of the size
n
and also of the variance
\sigma^{2}
. For
n=1
, formula (
2.1
) gives the distribution of the quotient of two independent complex Gaussian random variables.

In fact, the right-hand side of (
2.1
) gives the distribution of level crossings for a much broader class of complex Gaussian ensembles. Namely,
consider the following
SU(2)
-action on
Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}\times Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
. A matrix
\mathfrak{U}=\begin{pmatrix}u&-\bar{v}\\
v&\bar{u}\end{pmatrix},\;

|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}=1
from
SU(2)
acts on the latter product as:

\mathfrak{U}:(A,B)\mapsto(uA+vB,-\bar{v}A+\bar{u}B).

(2.3)

Definition 1
.

We say that a linear subspace
W\subset Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
is
adjusted to the
SU(2)
-action
(
2.3
) if the product space
W\times W\subset Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}\times Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
is preserved by (
2.3
).

Theorem B

(See Proposition 2,
[
GrShZa
]
)
.

Any complex linear subspace
W\subset Mat_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
adjusted to the above
SU(2)
-action with the measure induced from
GE_{\sigma^{2},n}^{\mathbb{C}}
,
\sigma^{2}>0
has the property that the distribution of the level crossings of (
1.1
) with random
A
and
B
independently taken from
W
according to the latter induced measure is given by (
2.1
), i.e., uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

Remark 3
.

Interesting examples of linear subspaces
W
adjusted to the
SU(2)
-action include complex symmetric matrices, complex Toeplitz matrices, complex band matrices, complex band Toeplitz matrices, complex diagonal matrices etc.

Since (
2.1
) appear as the distribution of level crossings for complex Gaussian ensembles, we will call it the
uniform law
for level crossings. Let us now formulate the
asymptotic uniform law
for complex non-Gaussian ensembles which we consider as a natural analog of Girko’s circular law for the spectrum of random complex matrices.

Theorem 1

(Conditional asymptotic uniform law for full complex pencils)
.

Let
A_{n}=(a_{ij}^{(n)})
and
B_{n}=(b_{ij}^{(n)})
be independent
n\times n
random matrices with i.i.d. complex entries. Assume that

\mathbb{E}\,a_{11}^{(n)}=\mathbb{E}\,b_{11}^{(n)}=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}\,|a_{11}^{(n)}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\,|b_{11}^{(n)}|^{2}=\sigma^{2}>0.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, set

C_{n}(\lambda):=A_{n}+\lambda B_{n},\qquad\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda):=\frac{C_{n}(\lambda)}{\sigma\sqrt{n(1+|\lambda|^{2})}}.

Let
\zeta_{1}^{(n)}(\lambda),\dots,\zeta_{n}^{(n)}(\lambda)
be the eigenvalues of

\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
, and let

L_{n,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{\zeta_{k}^{(n)}(\lambda)}.

Also set

M_{n,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\delta_{(\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda),\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda))}.

Assume in addition that the following three conditions hold.

(UCL)
For every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
and every bounded continuous

f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\sup_{\lambda\in K}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\,dL_{n,\lambda}-\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\,d\sigma_{\rm circ}\right|\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{in probability},

where
\sigma_{\rm circ}
is the uniform probability measure on the unit disk.

(SR)
For every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
and every
\eta>0
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\;\limsup_{n\to\infty}\;\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{\lambda\in K}\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}{\bf 1}_{\{|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)-\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)|\leq\varepsilon\}}\,\Bigl|\log|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)-\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)|\Bigr|>\eta\right)=0.

(LT)
For every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
and every
\eta>0
,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\;\limsup_{n\to\infty}\;\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{\lambda\in K}\iint_{\max\{|z|,|w|\}>R}\log(2+|z|+|w|)\,dM_{n,\lambda}(z,w)>\eta\right)=0.

Then the empirical level-crossing measure of the pencil

A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}

converges weakly in probability to the uniform measure on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, i.e. to the probability measure with density

\frac{dx\,dy}{\pi(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}

in the affine coordinate
\lambda=x+iy
.

Proposition 1

(A sufficient uniform circular-law input)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact. Suppose that the normalized entries

X_{\lambda}:=\frac{a_{11}+\lambda b_{11}}{\sigma\sqrt{1+|\lambda|^{2}}},\qquad\lambda\in K,

form a compact family of centered, unit-variance, non-degenerate laws for which
Girko’s hermitization estimates hold uniformly in the following sense: for every
compact
D\subset\mathbb{C}
and every
\eta>0
,

\sup_{\lambda\in K}\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{z\in D}\left|\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\det\bigl(\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)-zI\bigr)\right|-\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log|w-z|\,d\sigma_{\rm circ}(w)\right|>\eta\right)\longrightarrow 0,

after the usual logarithmic regularization at the singularity. Then condition

(UCL)
in Theorem
1
holds on
K
.

Remark 4
.

The above uniform hermitization input is known in the standard subgaussian
bounded-density setting from local circular-law estimates, with constants
uniform on compact families of entry laws; see Girko’s hermitization method

[
Gi2
]
and the local circular law of Bourgade–Yau–Yin
[
BYY
]
. In
finite-moment settings the same conclusion is expected after truncation and
replacement, but this requires the corresponding uniform least-singular-value
bounds.

Proof.

By Girko’s hermitization formula, convergence of the logarithmic potentials

z\mapsto\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\det\bigl(\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)-zI\bigr)\right|

to the logarithmic potential of
\sigma_{\rm circ}
, uniformly for

\lambda\in K
and locally uniformly in the regularized variable
z
, implies
weak convergence of the empirical eigenvalue measures
L_{n,\lambda}
to

\sigma_{\rm circ}
, uniformly in
\lambda\in K
. More explicitly, let

f\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Integration by parts gives

\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\,dL_{n,\lambda}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}(\Delta f)(z)\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\det\bigl(\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)-zI\bigr)\right|\,dx\,dy,

and the same identity holds with
L_{n,\lambda}
replaced by

\sigma_{\rm circ}
. Taking the difference, then taking the supremum over

\lambda\in K
, the asserted uniform convergence of the regularized
logarithmic potentials implies

\sup_{\lambda\in K}\left|\int f\,dL_{n,\lambda}-\int f\,d\sigma_{\rm circ}\right|\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{in probability}

for all
f\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Approximation of bounded continuous test
functions by compactly supported smooth functions, together with the tightness
which follows from the same logarithmic-potential estimates, gives the stated
form of (UCL).
∎

Lemma 1

(Uniform two-point Wegner estimate implies (SR))
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact. Suppose that for every
R>0
there is a
constant
C_{K,R}
such that, uniformly in
n
and uniformly in
0<r<1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\lambda\in K}\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}{\bf 1}_{\{|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)|\leq R,|\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)|\leq R,|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)-\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)|\leq r\}}\right]\leq C_{K,R}r^{2}.

Assume also that, for every
\eta>0
,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\lambda\in K}\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}{\bf 1}_{\{\max(|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)|,|\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)|)>R\}}>\eta\right)=0.

Then condition
(SR)
holds.

Proof.

Decompose the small-distance logarithm dyadically:

{\bf 1}_{\{|z-w|\leq\varepsilon\}}|\log|z-w||\leq\sum_{m\geq 0}|\log(2^{-m}\varepsilon)|{\bf 1}_{\{|z-w|\leq 2^{-m}\varepsilon\}}.

Restrict first to
|z|,|w|\leq R
, take the supremum over
\lambda\in K
, and
then take expectations. The assumed uniform two-point estimate gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\lambda\in K}\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}{\bf 1}_{\{|\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{j}|\leq\varepsilon,\ |\zeta_{i}|,|\zeta_{j}|\leq R\}}|\log|\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{j}||\right]\leq C_{K,R}\sum_{m\geq 0}|\log(2^{-m}\varepsilon)|2^{-2m}\varepsilon^{2}=O_{K,R}(\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|).

The contribution from pairs with at least one eigenvalue outside
R\mathbb{D}
is
negligible by the second assumption, after first choosing
R
large. Markov’s
inequality now gives (SR).
∎

Proposition 2

(Fixed-parameter small repulsion for complex Ginibre pencils)
.

If
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
are complex Ginibre matrices, then the fixed-parameter
version of
(SR)
holds: for every fixed
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and every

\eta>0
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}{\bf 1}_{\{|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)-\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)|\leq\varepsilon\}}|\log|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)-\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)||>\eta\right)=0.

The compact-uniform version required in Theorem
1
follows if,
in addition, one has a modulus-of-continuity estimate in
\lambda
strong enough
to pass from fixed parameters to finite nets.

Proof.

For every fixed
\lambda
, the normalized matrix
\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
is
again a standard complex Ginibre matrix. Its eigenvalues form a determinantal
point process with two-point correlation

\rho_{2}^{(n)}(z,w)=K_{n}(z,z)K_{n}(w,w)-|K_{n}(z,w)|^{2}.

The standard Ginibre kernel bound (see, e.g.,
[
Me
, Ch. 15]
) gives,
uniformly in
n
and locally uniformly in the bulk,

\rho_{2}^{(n)}(z,w)\leq Cn^{2}|z-w|^{2}.

after the global normalization used above. Integrating this estimate over

|z-w|\leq r
gives the fixed-parameter two-point bound. The exponentially
small tail of the Ginibre spectral radius controls the contribution from outside
a large disk. The dyadic argument in Lemma
1
, without
the supremum over
K
, then proves the stated fixed-parameter small-repulsion
estimate.
∎

Remark 5

(Status of the hypotheses beyond the Gaussian case)
.

Proposition
1
shows that the global input (UCL) is
not expected to be a new obstacle: it is a uniform version of the circular law,
obtained by Girko hermitization and the standard local circular-law estimates
for i.i.d. non-Hermitian matrices; see, for example,
[
Gi2
,
BYY
]
. The
additional genuinely local input is (SR). Lemma
1

reduces (SR) to a two-point small-gap estimate, i.e. to local eigenvalue
repulsion for the pencil.

For each fixed parameter in the complex Gaussian case this repulsion follows from the determinantal
Ginibre kernel; see
[
Me
, Ch. 15]
. The compact-uniform form of (SR) additionally requires a modulus-of-continuity estimate in the pencil parameter. For general complex i.i.d. entries, we
do not claim that (SR) is presently proved in the form stated above.
Rather, it is the natural estimate predicted by bulk universality of local
non-Hermitian spectral statistics, as in the universality results of Tao and Vu

[
TV
]
, together with the uniform local circular-law technology of
Bourgade–Yau–Yin
[
BYY
]
and a uniformity argument in the parameter

\lambda
. Thus, outside the Gaussian case, Theorem
1
should
be read as conditional on this small-repulsion input. For discrete entries, or
for real ensembles near
\mathbb{RP}^{1}
, this two-point input is much more
delicate and should be kept as an explicit assumption.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let

p_{n}(\lambda,t):=\det(C_{n}(\lambda)-tI),\qquad\Delta_{n}(\lambda):=\operatorname{Disc}_{t}\,p_{n}(\lambda,t).

The zeros of
\Delta_{n}(\lambda)
, counted with multiplicity, coincide with the
level-crossing points of the pencil
A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}
. Since

\deg_{\lambda}\Delta_{n}=n(n-1),

the empirical level-crossing measure is

\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{\Delta_{n}(\lambda)=0}\delta_{\lambda}.

By the Poincaré–Lelong formula,

\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi n(n-1)}\Delta_{\lambda}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|.

Let
\xi_{1}^{(n)}(\lambda),\dots,\xi_{n}^{(n)}(\lambda)
be the eigenvalues of

C_{n}(\lambda)
. Then

\Delta_{n}(\lambda)=\prod_{i<j}(\xi_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)-\xi_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda))^{2}.

Since

\xi_{k}^{(n)}(\lambda)=\sigma\sqrt{n(1+|\lambda|^{2})}\;\zeta_{k}^{(n)}(\lambda),

we get

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|=\log\!\bigl(\sigma\sqrt{n(1+|\lambda|^{2})}\bigr)+\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j}\log|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)-\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)|.

Equivalently,

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|=\log\!\bigl(\sigma\sqrt{n(1+|\lambda|^{2})}\bigr)+\iint_{\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\mathrm{Diag}}\log|z-w|\,dM_{n,\lambda}(z,w),

where

M_{n,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\delta_{(\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda),\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda))}.

Fix a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
. For

0<\varepsilon<1<R
, define the two-sided truncation

\Phi_{\varepsilon,R}(z,w):=\max\{\log\varepsilon,\min(\log|z-w|,\log R)\}.

This is a bounded continuous function on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Moreover, for bounded
continuous functions one has

\iint\Phi_{\varepsilon,R}\,dM_{n,\lambda}=\iint\Phi_{\varepsilon,R}\,dL_{n,\lambda}(z)dL_{n,\lambda}(w)+O_{\varepsilon,R}(1/n),

because
M_{n,\lambda}
differs from
L_{n,\lambda}\otimes L_{n,\lambda}

only by the diagonal correction. Hence (UCL) implies

\sup_{\lambda\in K}\left|\iint\Phi_{\varepsilon,R}(z,w)\,dM_{n,\lambda}(z,w)-\iint\Phi_{\varepsilon,R}(z,w)\,d\sigma_{\rm circ}(z)d\sigma_{\rm circ}(w)\right|\longrightarrow 0

in probability.

We now remove the truncations. The lower truncation error is supported on

\{|z-w|<\varepsilon\}
, and is bounded by

\iint_{\{|z-w|<\varepsilon\}}|\log|z-w||\,dM_{n,\lambda}+|\log\varepsilon|\,M_{n,\lambda}\{|z-w|<\varepsilon\},

which is negligible uniformly on
K
by (SR). The upper truncation
error is supported on
\{|z-w|>R\}
and is bounded by a constant multiple of

\iint_{\max\{|z|,|w|\}>R/2}\log(2+|z|+|w|)\,dM_{n,\lambda}(z,w),

which is negligible uniformly on
K
by (LT) as
R\to\infty
. The
same upper truncation error is absent for the circular law once
R>2
, since

\sigma_{\rm circ}
is supported on the unit disk, and the lower truncation
converges by monotone convergence. Consequently

\sup_{\lambda\in K}\left|\iint\log|z-w|\,dM_{n,\lambda}-\iint\log|z-w|\,d\sigma_{\rm circ}(z)d\sigma_{\rm circ}(w)\right|\longrightarrow 0

in probability, after taking first
n\to\infty
, then

\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, and finally
R\to\infty
. Denote the limiting
energy by

I_{\rm circ}:=\iint\log|z-w|\,d\sigma_{\rm circ}(z)d\sigma_{\rm circ}(w).

Thus

\sup_{\lambda\in K}\left|\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j}\log|\zeta_{i}^{(n)}(\lambda)-\zeta_{j}^{(n)}(\lambda)|-I_{\rm circ}\right|\longrightarrow 0

in probability.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+c_{n}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)

in probability in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{C})
, where

c_{n}:=\log(\sigma\sqrt{n})+I_{\rm circ}.

This is the natural mode of convergence, since
\log|\Delta_{n}|
has logarithmic
singularities at level crossings and hence cannot converge locally uniformly on
sets meeting its zero set. Applying
\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}
in the sense of
distributions, the constants
c_{n}
disappear and we get

\mu_{n}\Longrightarrow\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})\right)\,dx\,dy=\frac{dx\,dy}{\pi(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}.

This is exactly the uniform distribution on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.
∎

If

3.2.
Reduction of the GOE conjecture to pseudocovariance-independence

In this subsection we refine Conjecture
1
by exploiting the special Gaussian structure of the GOE ensemble. We show that the conjecture reduces to a single analytic question about the dependence of the log-discriminant on the pseudocovariance parameter.

Let
A_{n},B_{n}
be independent GOE matrices, and consider the pencil

C_{n}(\lambda):=A_{n}+\lambda B_{n},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

For
i<j
, the entries of
C_{n}(\lambda)
are of the form

(C_{n}(\lambda))_{ij}=a_{ij}+\lambda b_{ij},

where
a_{ij},b_{ij}\sim N(0,1)
are independent real Gaussians. On the diagonal,

(C_{n}(\lambda))_{ii}=a_{ii}+\lambda b_{ii},\qquad a_{ii},b_{ii}\sim N(0,2).

Define the normalized matrix

\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda):=\frac{C_{n}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{1+|\lambda|^{2}}}.

Then the entries of
\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
are centered complex Gaussian random variables with

\mathbb{E}|\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)_{ij}|^{2}=1,\qquad\mathbb{E}\bigl(\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)_{ij}^{2}\bigr)=\tau(\lambda),

where

\tau(\lambda):=\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}.

Thus the law of
\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
depends on
\lambda
only through the single complex parameter
\tau(\lambda)
, and hence through

q(\lambda):=|\tau(\lambda)|^{2}=\frac{|1+\lambda^{2}|^{2}}{(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}.

Let
\Delta_{n}(\lambda)
denote the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial of
C_{n}(\lambda)
, so that the empirical level-crossing measure is given by

\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi n(n-1)}\Delta_{\lambda}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|.

Remark 7

(Status of the real-case assumptions)
.

The main technical input in both the real symmetric and Hermitian cases is
control of the logarithmic energy uniformly in the elliptic parameter. This
means two separate estimates: a global elliptic-law input and a local
small-spacing/no-clustering input which makes the logarithmic singularity
negligible. For Gaussian elliptic ensembles these estimates are supported by
explicit Pfaffian or determinantal correlation kernels; for general real i.i.d.
entries they should follow only after combining local circular/elliptic-law
estimates with bulk universality. We therefore state the real results below as
conditional statements, except in the special finite-dimensional cases quoted
from
[
GrShZa
]
.

The mode of convergence for normalized log-discriminants should be

L^{1}_{\rm loc}
in probability, or equivalently distributional convergence after
applying
(2\pi)^{-1}\Delta_{\lambda}
, rather than locally uniform convergence.
Indeed
\log|\Delta_{n}|
has logarithmic poles at the level crossings.

Proposition 3

(GOE structural reduction for expected potentials)
.

Let
A_{n},B_{n}
be independent GOE matrices and set

U_{n}(\lambda):=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|\right].

Then there exists a function
H_{n}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

U_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+H_{n}\left(\frac{|1+\lambda^{2}|^{2}}{(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}\right).

Consequently any locally uniform limit of
U_{n}
has this form.

Proof.

Write

C_{n}(\lambda)=A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}=\sqrt{1+|\lambda|^{2}}\,\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda).

Since the discriminant is homogeneous of degree
n(n-1)
in the matrix entries,

\log|\Delta_{n}(C_{n}(\lambda))|=n(n-1)\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+\log|\Delta_{n}(\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda))|.

It remains to understand the law of the second term. The entries of

\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
are centered complex Gaussian variables. Their variance
is normalized to one, and their pseudocovariance equals

\tau(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}\,\widehat{c}_{ij}(\lambda)^{2}=\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}.

For a centered complex Gaussian vector, the joint law is determined by its
covariance and pseudocovariance matrices. Hence the law of

\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
depends on
\lambda
only through
\tau(\lambda)
.

If
\tau^{\prime}=e^{2i\theta}\tau
, then multiplication of the whole matrix by

e^{i\theta}
sends the Gaussian ensemble with pseudocovariance
\tau
to the
one with pseudocovariance
\tau^{\prime}
. This multiplication rotates all eigenvalues
by
e^{i\theta}
and multiplies the discriminant by a complex number of modulus
one times the deterministic homogeneous factor; therefore

\log|\Delta_{n}(\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda))|
depends on
\tau(\lambda)
only through

|\tau(\lambda)|
. Taking expectations gives

U_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+H_{n}(|\tau(\lambda)|^{2})

for a deterministic function
H_{n}
on
[0,1]
, which is the claimed formula.
Any locally uniform subsequential limit has the same structural form by passing
to the limit in this identity.
∎

Proposition
3
shows that Conjecture
1
reduces to understanding whether the pseudocovariance parameter contributes to the leading
n^{2}
-asymptotics of the log-discriminant.

Theorem 2

(Conditional GOE asymptotic law)
.

Let
A_{n},B_{n}
be independent GOE matrices, and let
\mu_{n}
be the empirical level-crossing measure of
A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}
.

Assume that there exists a deterministic function
G\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}([0,1])

such that

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})-G\bigl(q(\lambda)\bigr)-c_{n}\longrightarrow 0

in probability in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{C})
, where
c_{n}
is independent of

\lambda
.

Then

\mu_{n}\Longrightarrow\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}\left[\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G\bigl(q(\lambda)\bigr)\right]\,dx\,dy.

In particular, if
G
is constant, then
\mu_{n}
converges to the uniform measure on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of Theorem
1
once the stated

L^{1}_{\rm loc}
asymptotic for the log-discriminant is available. The
Poincaré–Lelong formula identifies
\mu_{n}
with the distributional Laplacian
of the normalized log-discriminant, and distributional convergence of
Laplacians follows from
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
convergence of the potentials. Since

\Delta_{\lambda}c_{n}=0
, the constants disappear.
∎

Conjecture 2

(Pseudocovariance-independence)
.

Under the assumptions above, the pseudocovariance parameter does not contribute to the leading asymptotics:

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+c_{n}+o(1)

in probability in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{C})
.

Conjecture

implies Conjecture
1
. It isolates the core analytic problem: to show that the pseudocovariance

\tau(\lambda)=\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}

does not affect the leading-order logarithmic energy of the eigenvalues of
A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}
.

.

Conjecture 3

(General real i.i.d. level-crossing law)
.

Let
A_{n}=(a_{ij})
and

be independent real

matrices
with i.i.d. entries satisfying

and assume a standard moment condition strong enough to imply the circular law

Remark 10
.

The conjecture is meant for genuinely full real i.i.d. matrices, not for real
symmetric matrices. It is consistent with the numerical evidence in

[

]

appears to

\mathbb{CP}^{1}

absence of an

Remark 11

(Assumptions in the full real i.i.d. case)
.

Away from
\mathbb{RP}^{1}

is a non-Hermitian matrix with
complex entries whose real and imaginary parts are correlated. The expected
inputs are therefore the real analogue of the complex assumptions in

small-spacing estimate. The global part is in the scope of circular-law and
local circular-law results such as
[
BYY
]
; the small-spacing part is
predicted by local universality of non-Hermitian eigenvalue statistics, as in

[

]
, but should not be presented here as proved in this full
uniformity.

The additional assumption excluding concentration near
\mathbb{RP}^{1}
is a
separate real phenomenon. In the Gaussian generalized eigenvalue problem the
number of real generalized eigenvalues is only of order
n^{1/2}
in expectation

by Pfaffian formulas

. These results support the expectation
that real or almost-real crossings are negligible on the

they do not by themselves prove the stated no-concentration hypothesis for
general real i.i.d. pencils.

Theorem 3

(Conditional real i.i.d. theorem)
.

Let

Theorem 4

(No concentration near the real projective line)
.

Let
A_{n},B_{n}
be independent
n\times n
random real matrices, and let

\mu_{n}
be the empirical level-crossing measure of
A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}
.

For

N_{n}(\varepsilon):=\#\{\lambda\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{n}:\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}}(\lambda,\mathbb{RP}^{1})<\varepsilon\},

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\;\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}N_{n}(\varepsilon)}{n(n-1)}=0.

Then every subsequential weak limit in probability of
\mu_{n}
gives zero mass to

\mathbb{RP}^{1}
. Equivalently, along every almost-sure weakly convergent
subsequence extracted from a subsequence converging in probability, the limiting
measure assigns no mass to
\mathbb{RP}^{1}
.

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\mu_{n}\bigl((\mathbb{RP}^{1})_{\varepsilon}\bigr)=\frac{N_{n}(\varepsilon)}{n(n-1)}.

Hence Markov’s inequality and the assumption imply

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\;\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mu_{n}\bigl((\mathbb{RP}^{1})_{\varepsilon}\bigr)>\eta\right)=0

for every
\eta>0
.

Let
\mu_{n_{k}}\Rightarrow\mu
in probability. Passing to a further subsequence

on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
such that
\chi_{j}=1
on

and

. By another

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mu_{n_{k}}\bigl((\mathbb{RP}^{1})_{\varepsilon_{j}}\bigr)=0

almost surely for every fixed

. Therefore

\mu(\mathbb{RP}^{1})\leq\int\chi_{j}\,d\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int\chi_{j}\,d\mu_{n_{k}}\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mu_{n_{k}}\bigl((\mathbb{RP}^{1})_{\varepsilon_{j}}\bigr)=0.

.

Corollary 1

of all Hermitian

\frac{1}{Z_{n}}e^{-\frac{n}{2}{\rm{tr}}H^{2}},\;H\in\mathcal{H}_{n},

where
Z_{n}=2^{n/2}\pi^{n^{2}/2}
.

(See Theorem 3 of

.

If
A
and

then the distribution of the level crossings in

\mathcal{P}_{GUE_{2}}(x,y)dxdy=\frac{4}{\pi}\frac{|y|dxdy}{(1+x^{2}+y^{2})^{3}}.

To formulate our further results we need to impose certain assumptions.

Assumption 1

(Uniform log-energy convergence in the elliptic bulk)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, uniformly for
\tau\in[0,1-\varepsilon]
,

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\log|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|=G(\tau^{2})+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1),

where

G(\tau^{2})=\iint_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\log|z-w|\,\mu_{\tau}(dz)\mu_{\tau}(dw),

and
\mu_{\tau}
is the elliptic law.

Remark 13

(Motivation for Assumption
1
)
.

Let
\log_{\delta}r:=\max\{\log r,\log\delta\}
and split

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\log|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|=L_{n,\delta}^{\rm far}(\tau)+L_{n,\delta}^{\rm near}(\tau).

For fixed
0<\delta<1<R
, replace
\log|z-w|
by the two-sided
truncation

\Phi_{\delta,R}(z,w):=\max\{\log\delta,\min(\log|z-w|,\log R)\}.

This kernel is bounded and continuous. Since the complex elliptic Ginibre
ensemble is determinantal with an explicit kernel, the one- and two-point
correlation functions admit bulk asymptotics uniform in

\tau\in[0,1-\varepsilon]
. Hence

L_{n,\delta,R}^{\rm trunc}(\tau)=\iint\Phi_{\delta,R}(z,w)\,\mu_{\tau}(dz)\mu_{\tau}(dw)+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)

uniformly in
\tau
.

The remaining step is precisely the logarithmic-uniform-integrability input:
one lets first
n\to\infty
, then
\delta\downarrow 0
, and finally

R\to\infty
, using the local bulk two-point estimate near the diagonal and
the uniform logarithmic tail bound at infinity. This is why
Assumption
1
is stated as an assumption rather than
as a consequence of weak convergence alone.

Theorem 5

(GUE level-crossing limit away from the real axis)
.

Assume Assumption
1
for the Gaussian elliptic
ensemble on compact subsets of
|\tau|<1
. Then the empirical level-crossing
measures for the pencil

A_{n}+\lambda B_{n},\qquad A_{n},B_{n}\sim GUE_{n},

converge locally on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\setminus\mathbb{RP}^{1}
to the measure

\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}\left[\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G(1-Y^{2})\right]\,dx\,dy,

where

Y=\frac{2\Im\lambda}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}

and
G
is the logarithmic energy of the elliptic law with parameter

|\tau|=\sqrt{1-Y^{2}}.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Let

C_{n}(\lambda)=A_{n}+\lambda B_{n},\qquad A_{n},B_{n}\sim GUE_{n},

and define the normalized matrix

\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda):=\frac{C_{n}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{1+|\lambda|^{2}}}.

Let
\xi_{1}(\lambda),\dots,\xi_{n}(\lambda)
and
\hat{\xi}_{1}(\lambda),\dots,\hat{\xi}_{n}(\lambda)
denote the eigenvalues of
C_{n}(\lambda)
and
\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
respectively. Then

\xi_{i}(\lambda)=\sqrt{1+|\lambda|^{2}}\,\hat{\xi}_{i}(\lambda),

and hence

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\log|\hat{\xi}_{i}(\lambda)-\hat{\xi}_{j}(\lambda)|.

By the Gaussian covariance computation, the law of
\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
depends on
\lambda
only through the parameter

\tau(\lambda):=\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}.

Moreover, since multiplication by a complex phase rotates the spectrum, the quantity

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\log|\hat{\xi}_{i}-\hat{\xi}_{j}|

depends only on
|\tau(\lambda)|
.

Define

q(\lambda):=|\tau(\lambda)|^{2}=\frac{|1+\lambda^{2}|^{2}}{(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}.

In stereographic coordinates on
S^{2}
, one checks that

q(\lambda)=1-Y^{2},\qquad Y=\frac{2\,\Im\lambda}{1+|\lambda|^{2}}.

By Assumption
1
, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a continuous function
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}:[0,1-\varepsilon]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\log|\hat{\xi}_{i}-\hat{\xi}_{j}|=\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(|\tau|)+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1),

uniformly for
|\tau|\leq 1-\varepsilon
.

It follows that for all
\lambda
such that
|\tau(\lambda)|\leq 1-\varepsilon
,

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G_{\varepsilon}(q(\lambda))+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1),

where
G_{\varepsilon}(q):=\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(\sqrt{q})
.

Set

F_{\varepsilon}(\lambda):=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G_{\varepsilon}(1-Y^{2}).

Then
F_{\varepsilon}
is continuous and the convergence above is understood in
probability in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
on compact subsets of the region

|\tau(\lambda)|\leq 1-\varepsilon
. This is the appropriate potential-theoretic
mode of convergence because the left-hand side has logarithmic singularities at
level crossings. Thus

\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|\;\longrightarrow\;F_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)

in probability in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
on this region.

By standard potential-theoretic arguments, this implies convergence of
Laplacians in the sense of measures:

\mu_{n}^{LC}=\frac{1}{2\pi n(n-1)}\Delta_{\lambda}\log|\Delta_{n}(\lambda)|\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}F_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)\,dx\,dy,

locally on the region
|\tau(\lambda)|\leq 1-\varepsilon
.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, this proves the existence of a limiting density away from the set
|\tau(\lambda)|=1
, i.e. away from the equator
Y=0
on
S^{2}
. The density is given by

\Psi(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G(1-Y^{2})\Big],

where
G(q)
denotes the logarithmic energy of the elliptic law with parameter
\sqrt{q}
.

This proves the theorem.
∎

We do not have an exact formula for the density
\mathcal{P}^{GUE}_{n}(x,y)
, for
n>3
. However these densities have simpler formulas in cylindrical or spherical coordinates on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{(1+x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}
transforms in the constant density

Remark 14
.

The preceding theorem treats the Gaussian Hermitian case through the elliptic
ensemble. The expected universality statement is that the same limiting
level-crossing law holds for general Wigner matrices. We spell out the
corresponding ensemble and conjecture, since this is the Hermitian analogue of
Wigner’s semicircle law in the present level-crossing problem.

Definition 2
.

Fix a real-valued probability distribution
\mu
with mean zero and finite
moments, and a complex-valued probability distribution
\nu
with mean zero,
unit variance, finite moments, and satisfying the Hermitian symmetry convention

h_{ji}=\overline{h_{ij}}
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}E_{\mu,\nu,n}
the Wigner
ensemble of Hermitian
n\times n
matrices whose diagonal entries are i.i.d.
with law
\mu
and whose upper-triangular off-diagonal entries are i.i.d.
with law
\nu
.

Conjecture 6

(Hermitian Wigner universality)
.

Let
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
be independent matrices from
\mathcal{H}E_{\mu,\nu,n}
, normalized
in the usual Wigner scaling. Then the empirical level-crossing measures of

A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}
converge, as
n\to\infty
, to the same probability measure

\Psi
as in Conjecture
5
, i.e. the limiting law is universal
inside the Hermitian symmetry class and does not depend on the particular laws

\mu
and
\nu
.

Conjecture 7

(Potential-theoretic form of the Hermitian limit)
.

The universal Hermitian limiting measure is the
SO(2)
-invariant measure on

\mathbb{CP}^{1}
given away from
\mathbb{RP}^{1}
by

\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{\lambda}\left[\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G(1-Y^{2})\right]dx\,dy,\qquad Y=\frac{2\Im\lambda}{1+|\lambda|^{2}},

where
G(q)
is the logarithmic energy of the elliptic law with elliptic
parameter
\sqrt{q}
. The measure has no atom or singular component on

\mathbb{RP}^{1}
.

Theorem 6

(Conditional Hermitian Wigner theorem)
.

Assume the following two uniform inputs for the Wigner-type elliptic ensembles
arising from

\frac{A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}}{\sqrt{1+|\lambda|^{2}}}:

(i) the empirical eigenvalue distribution converges to the elliptic law with
parameter
|\tau(\lambda)|
, uniformly on compact subsets of

\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
; and (ii) the normalized logarithmic pair-energy
converges uniformly to the elliptic logarithmic energy
G(|\tau(\lambda)|^{2})
,
with negligible contribution from eigenvalue spacings tending to zero. Then
the conclusion of Conjecture
7
holds locally on

\mathbb{CP}^{1}\setminus\mathbb{RP}^{1}
. If, in addition, the empirical
level-crossing measures do not concentrate in an
o(1)
-neighborhood of

\mathbb{RP}^{1}
, then the same convergence holds on all of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

Proof.

The proof repeats the proof of Theorem
5
. The
normalization separates the deterministic factor

\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})
from the logarithmic pair-energy of the normalized
eigenvalues. By the assumed elliptic-law and log-energy convergence, the
normalized log-discriminant converges to

\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|\lambda|^{2})+G(1-Y^{2}).

Applying the Poincaré–Lelong formula gives the stated Laplacian. The final
no-concentration assumption removes the only possible missing singular mass on

\mathbb{RP}^{1}
.
∎

Proposition 4

(Gaussian Hermitian pencils)
.

For
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
independent GUE matrices, the two uniform inputs in
Theorem
6
are satisfied on every compact
subset of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Consequently the Hermitian limiting
measure is rigorously obtained there from the elliptic logarithmic energy.

Proof.

Fix a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. For

\lambda\in K
the normalized matrix

\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{A_{n}+\lambda B_{n}}{\sqrt{1+|\lambda|^{2}}}

is a Gaussian elliptic ensemble. Its correlation parameter is

\tau(\lambda)=\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{1+|\lambda|^{2}},\qquad 1-|\tau(\lambda)|^{2}=\frac{4(\Im\lambda)^{2}}{(1+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}}.

Since
K
is separated from the real axis, there exists
\delta>0
such that

|\tau(\lambda)|\leq 1-\delta
for every
\lambda\in K
.

For Gaussian elliptic ensembles with
|\tau|\leq 1-\delta
, the explicit
determinantal correlation kernels imply the global elliptic law uniformly in

\tau
; see the elliptic ensemble computations of

[
SCS
,
FZ
,
Fo
]
. Thus the empirical eigenvalue measures
of
\widehat{C}_{n}(\lambda)
converge, uniformly for
\lambda\in K
, to the
elliptic law with parameter
|\tau(\lambda)|
.

It remains to justify convergence of the logarithmic pair-energy. Split

\log|z-w|=\log|z-w|\,{\bf 1}_{|z-w|>\varepsilon}+\log|z-w|\,{\bf 1}_{|z-w|\leq\varepsilon}.

For the first term the kernel is continuous after an outer truncation and hence
the uniform elliptic law gives convergence to the corresponding truncated
elliptic energy. For the second term, the determinantal two-point correlation
function is locally bounded, uniformly for
|\tau|\leq 1-\delta
, and has the
usual quadratic repulsion at the diagonal. Therefore, for compact spectral
windows,

\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}{\bf 1}_{|\hat{\xi}_{i}-\hat{\xi}_{j}|\leq\varepsilon}|\log|\hat{\xi}_{i}-\hat{\xi}_{j}||\leq C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|+o(1),

uniformly in
\lambda\in K
. Letting first
n\to\infty
and then

\varepsilon\downarrow 0
proves that the small-spacing contribution is
negligible. Removing the outer truncation is justified by the compact support
and logarithmic-tail bounds for the elliptic law, again uniformly for

|\tau|\leq 1-\delta
. Hence the normalized logarithmic pair-energy converges
uniformly on
K
to the logarithmic energy
G(|\tau(\lambda)|^{2})
of the
elliptic law, which is precisely the pair-energy input required in
Theorem
6
.
∎

Remark 15

(Non-Gaussian Hermitian/Wigner entries)
.

For Wigner entries with smooth bounded densities, subexponential tails, and
matching first two moments, the same verification is expected to follow from
the local elliptic law and bulk universality for elliptic random matrices,
uniformly for
|\tau|\leq 1-\delta
. Thus away from
\mathbb{RP}^{1}
the
Hermitian conditional theorem is essentially reduced to known elliptic-law and
level-repulsion technology. What remains outside the present verification is
uniform control as
\Im\lambda\to 0
, where the elliptic ensemble approaches
the Hermitian regime, and the possible concentration of level crossings near

\mathbb{RP}^{1}
.
