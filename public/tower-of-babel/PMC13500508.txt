Evaluating and optimizing public service facilities within the 15-min community life circle—evidence from Nanchang, China
Authors: Min Wu, Junhong Chen, Kaige Zheng, Jiaxin Zhang, Shihang Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs urban areas expand rapidly, the development of public service facilities has become a critical strategy for optimizing urban spatial structures and addressing the diverse, high-quality living needs of residents. This study presents a referable and evidence-based closed-loop framework for evaluating and optimizing public service facilities within the "15-min community life circle," specifically applied to Nanchang, China. By analyzing the spatial distribution, accessibility, and developmental coordination of 28,082 public service facilities within the six districts and three counties of Nanchang, the research findings reveal that: (1) significant spatial heterogeneity exists in the distribution and accessibility of public service facilities within the study area; (2) the overall coverage efficiency of the 24 categories of public service facilities is low, with notable disparities in coverage among different facility types; and (3) a mismatch exists between the supply of public service facilities and the population distribution. Furthermore, by employing a location allocation model and considering the current developmental context of Nanchang City, we propose targeted optimization strategies for areas experiencing supply–demand mismatches. The findings offer decision support for policymakers in formulating targeted urban renewal strategies and have practical implications for promoting the high-quality development of the 15-min living circle.Keywords: Public service facilities, 15-min community living circle, Points of interest (POI), Optimization of spatial layoutSubject terms: Environmental social sciences, Environmental studies, Geography, Geography, Social policy

Introduction

As China transitions its urban development focus from rapid expansion to high-quality growth, consequently, the concept of the "15-min living circle" has become a pivotal topic in urban planning1. In this context, community spaces are evolving from static, single-function residential areas into integrated, co-constructed, and symbiotic spatial systems2,3. Public service facilities, serving as the material foundation of residents’ daily lives, should include a diverse range of elements, such as public spaces, commercial amenities, transportation infrastructure, and green areas4.

Amid rapid urbanization and increasing living standards, the current provision of public service facilities within the "15-min living circle" is increasingly inadequate to address the diverse and evolving needs of residents5. A significant disparity exists between the supply of community public service facilities and the rising public expectations for a high-quality urban life. The equity and efficiency of public service facility allocation directly affect both residents’ quality of life and the achievement of sustainable urban development6,7. Consequently, establishing a comprehensive and scientifically grounded evaluation framework to guide the spatial optimization of public service facilities has become essential for improving the urban living environment and enhancing residents’ overall well-being.

With accelerating urbanization, studies of public service facilities under the "15-min living circle" framework have shifted from simple accessibility measurement to assessments of spatial equity. Researchers have widely applied methods such as kernel density estimation8,9, the two-step floating catchment area method (2SFCA and its extensions)10,11, Development Coordinating Index12, network-based accessibility modeling13, and spatial autocorrelation analysis to evaluate the spatial distribution of single-category facilities14, including education15, healthcare16,17, elderly care18,19, and sports20. At the same time, rising demand for public services has produced spatial mismatches between facility supply and population distribution21,22. Consequently, scholars increasingly use GIS-based network analysis8,23 and spatial modeling with points of interest data to examine spatial equity24,25, accessibility26, the alignment of service provision with population distribution27 and the supply level of public service facilities under walking-related differences across different age groups3,28.

Nonetheless, the existing literature still shows four major limitations:

(1) Narrow typological scope: Most studies focus on a single category of facility, overlooking the synergistic supply and co-location effects of multiple facility types that characterize real urban life.

(2) Static representation of demand: The majority of studies commonly utilize resident population size or broad population density as proxy variables, failing to differentiate the diverse needs and characteristics of residents concerning public service facilities and daily life dimensions29,30.

(3) Limited methodological and operational applicability: Indicators such as accessibility and coverage rate often remain at the diagnostic level, disconnected from optimization-oriented decision-making under resource constraints (e.g., where to allocate, how to integrate, and in what sequence). Additionally, few studies conduct robustness tests on spatial heterogeneity, capacity–service intensity (supply–demand ratio), multi-scale effects (MAUP), or threshold settings23,31,32.

(4) The gap between “planning rhetoric” and “implementation practice”: Although concepts such as "community living circles" have become widely endorsed, most studies fail to translate evaluation outcomes into actionable planning tools—such as scenario-based facility augmentation plans, or block-level renewal strategies—leaving a "last-mile" gap between policy intent and technical implementation33,34.

To address these research gaps, we develop a referable and evidence-based closed-loop framework for evaluating the spatial distribution of public service facilities. Using Nanchang City as a case study, we analyze 28,082 points of interest (POIs) representing 24 facility types across eight major categories. Guided by the 15-min community living circle concept, we apply three complementary methods—kernel density analysis, spatial coverage analysis, accessibility analysis and development coordination analysis—to characterize facility clustering, identify service blind spots, and assess spatial alignment between facilities and population. This integrated approach overcomes the limits of prior single-category studies and provides a systematic basis for optimizing facility allocation. From these results, we propose targeted strategies to improve facility configuration and service efficiency, offering a scientific foundation and practical guidance for future urban planning and equitable public-service provision.

Materials and methods

Study area

Nanchang City is situated in central Jiangxi Province (28°35′–29°10′N, 115°35′–116°20′E). The urban fabric extends along both banks of the Gan River, resulting in a composite spatial pattern characterized by linear development along the river and radial expansion outward with radial expansion outward, encompassing an administrative area spanning 7,402 km2. This study focuses on Nanchang’s "six districts and three counties" (Fig. 1). As the provincial capital and a major metropolitan area, Nanchang serves as a principal economic hub and a densely populated city center in central China (Fig. 2). The city boasts well-developed infrastructure, ample public spaces, and an extensive transport network, rendering it a complex yet representative case for analyzing the spatial distribution and accessibility of public service facilities. Several factors motivate the selection of Nanchang as the study area:

Research area.

Population and GDP overview of Nanchang City.

(1) Relevance to urban livability and spatial-structure optimization: The planning and provision of public service facilities directly affect residents’ quality of life and serve as key tools for optimizing urban spatial structure35. According to the latest national census, Nanchang’s urbanization rate is 79.9%36, which is substantially higher than the national average of 67%37. This high level of urbanization increases demand for public facilities and places strict requirements on their spatial configuration, service coverage, and accessibility.

(2) Policy context and research gap: The "15-min living circle" concept was first articulated in the Shanghai 15-Minute Community Life Circle Planning Guidelines (Trial Version) published in 201638, and it has since informed local implementations such as Shanghai’s "15-Minute Community Life Circle"39,40 and Beijing’s "15-Minute Convenience Life Circle"41. Despite these precedents, Nanchang has not yet established specific regulatory or planning frameworks to implement this concept, creating an opportunity to investigate localized strategies for adapting and operationalizing the model.

(3) Recent policy initiatives and pilot programs: In 2024, Nanchang was designated as one of China’s first 15 pilot cities for urban renewal, making it a representative case for integrating community planning with urban regeneration42. In September 2025, Nanchang was named among the second batch of pilot cities for the "Comprehensive Advancement of the 15-Minute Convenience Living Circle"43. This designation ushers in a new phase of comprehensive development for the city’s living-circle strategy. In this context, a systematic study of Nanchang’s public service facilities is essential to provide data-driven guidance for spatial optimization and to inform the formulation and implementation of urban renewal policies.

Data acquisition and processing

The research data employed in this study primarily encompass the residential area of interest (AOI), public service facility POI dataset, the city’s transportation network data, and urban population data within the nine administrative districts and counties of Nanchang City in 2025.

The residential AOI dataset contains detailed spatial and attribute information, including names, classifications, total floor areas, and precise geographic coordinates for each residential area. This dataset serves as the key spatial reference for identifying the housing units that generate demand for public services. The POI dataset for public service facilities provides records of facility names, categories, service types, and geographic coordinates. It encompasses eight primary categories and 24 subcategories, covering essential urban functions such as commerce, education, healthcare, elderly care, cultural and sports services, and transportation. Residential AOI dataset and public service facility POI dataset were obtained from the Baidu Open Platform (https://lbsyun.baidu.com/, accessed on August 24, 2025), ensuring the data reflects current spatial information.

Integrating the transportation network dataset of Nanchang City is crucial for evaluating the impact of the built environment on residents’ access to services. Consequently, after systematically filtering, validating, and classifying the data, the data processing procedures were established based on specific criteria. Firstly, duplicate points of interest (POIs) were eliminated by cross-referencing geographic coordinates and facility names. Additionally, invalid POIs lacking essential attributes such as name or category, as well as those situated outside the defined study area determined by the administrative boundaries of Nanchang’s six districts and three counties, were excluded. Secondly, to ensure data accuracy and reliability, a field-based sampling verification process was conducted. This involved randomly selecting 10% of POIs to verify the consistency between their recorded locations and actual functions. Lastly, adhering to the Urban Residential Area Planning and Design Standard (GB 50,180-2018)44 and the Jiangxi Provincial Technical Guidelines for Urban Planning Management (2024 edition)45, POI data pertaining to public service facilities were categorized into eight main groups and 24 subgroups, 4,672 residential AOI dataset and 28,082 public service facility POIs were retained for spatial and quantitative analysis (Table 1).

To accurately represent the city’s transportation infrastructure and facilitate accessibility modeling, Open Street Map (OSM) was utilized to acquire a detailed road network dataset (https://www.openstreetmap.org, accessed on August 24, 2025). This dataset encompasses information on road characteristics, including type, length, and coordinates, which serve as the foundation for developing spatial network models and simulating walk routes from residential areas to public amenities.

Population data were obtained from the WorldPop database (https://www.worldpop.org/, accessed on September 10, 2025), which provides gridded population estimates for 2020 at 100 m × 100 m resolution. Because China conducts a national population census every five years, the 2020 estimates correspond to the Seventh National Population Census and thus serve as the most reliable demographic baseline available at the township (subdistrict) level. WorldPop values, however, are modeled projections that draw on diverse demographic and remote-sensing inputs; therefore, we applied a calibration procedure to improve their accuracy. This procedure aligned the WorldPop gridded estimates with official township-level census data and then aggregated the calibrated grid to the community level to better reflect the current demographic distribution in Nanchang. The final adjusted population dataset includes detailed demographic attributes, such as total urban population, gender ratios, age-group proportions, and township-level population totals.

By integrating various datasets such as public service facility POIs dataset, residential AOIs spatial dataset, detailed urban transportation network information, and precise demographic data, a robust empirical foundation is established for comprehensive spatial analyses.

Research approach

The research framework follows a four-stage technical route:

(1) Data acquisition: A GIS-based spatial database of public service facilities in Nanchang was established. Using Nanchang city’s transportation network data, a street-level road network layer was generated. Multiple dataset collection, cleaning, and spatial coordinate registration were conducted for eight categories of public service facilities and residential locations. All spatial datasets were standardized under the WGS-1984 World Mercator coordinate system to ensure spatial accuracy and interoperability.

(2) Spatial analysis: The spatial clustering characteristics of public service facilities were examined using the Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) method to identify areas of concentration and dispersion. Subsequently, spatial coverage analysis and accessibility analysis were performed to evaluate the balance and accessibility of facility distribution across the study area, thereby revealing the spatial equity of service provision.

(3) Population analysis: Urban population data were utilized to calculate population density distributions and to assess the spatial congruence between population concentration and facility locations. This analysis serves as the empirical foundation for identifying mismatches between demand and supply, providing a quantitative basis for optimizing facility allocation.

(4) Optimization strategy development: Based on the analytical results, the study proposes a set of refined strategies for optimizing the spatial configuration of public service facilities and improving their operational efficiency. These strategies aim to enhance the equity, accessibility, and functionality of urban public services, offering both scientific evidence and practical reference for future urban planning and the balanced allocation of public resources (Fig. 3).

Ideas and methods for optimizing the configuration of public service facilities

Optimizing the spatial layout of public service facilities requires balancing convenience and equity while increasing overall coverage, ensuring that the public service system aligns precisely with urban development needs. Pursuing a "larger and more comprehensive" configuration without strategic planning, however, can waste resources and produce underutilized facilities18. To mitigate this, the study employs the K-means clustering algorithm47 to address this issue, the study utilizes the K-means clustering algorithm. Under optimal conditions, it offers recommendations for enhancing facility layout and presents a priority list for spatial optimization by reducing the walking distance between newly established facilities and underserved residential areas. The optimal number of new facilities is chosen at the inflection point where the decay rate of the average distortion metric begins to level off, thereby providing a scientifically grounded basis for expansion. The detailed computational formulas appear in Eqs. (12)–(13).

To further optimize the spatial layout of public service facilities, this study, based on the previously determined number of additional facilities, employs the location-allocation model pioneered by Cooper48, implementing the “Minimize Impedance Model” (L-A model) to simulate potential facility placements. The model aims to address coverage gaps or optimize other spatial factors by maximizing or minimizing targeted objectives, thereby generating an optimal set of spatial coordinates for public service facilities. Simultaneously, in the impedance minimization procedure, residential areas that are not covered are weighted based on their respective population sizes. Through simulation analysis using this optimization model, a site selection scheme is produced that achieves an optimal balance between service coverage and allocation efficiency (Fig. 4). Results

Current status of population distribution and spatial distribution characteristics of public service facilities in Nanchang

A comprehensive analysis indicates that the spatial distribution of most public service facilities in Nanchang City is highly uneven, with significant disparities in facility numbers across districts and counties, exhibiting a pronounced “central–peripheral” differentiation pattern (Fig. 5). Specifically, public service facilities exhibit a concentric distribution pattern, with facility density declining radially from the central urban area—centered on Donghu districts and Xihu districts—toward peripheral areas (Fig. 6), this spatial pattern is closely coupled with the city’s population density distribution (Fig. 7). It indicates that there exists a certain correlation between facility supply and population demand.

The first category, exemplified by educational and transportation facilities, displays a dual-center layout typified by an "old urban core–new urban center" framework, demonstrating a fairly even, polycentric dispersion. Its service range efficiently covers conventional districts like Donghu districts, Xihu districts, and Qingshanhu districts, while also reaching out to developing regions like Honggutan districts, showcasing a relatively high level of spatial flexibility.

In contrast, the second category, exemplified by sports and elderly care facilities, is predominantly clustered in the central urban area encompassing (Donghu districts, Xihu districts and Qingshanhu districts). This category demonstrates a classic monocentric polarization trend, with peripheral regions experiencing notably scarce facility availability, leading to significant service disparities.

An in-depth analysis of all 24 facility types reveals distinct density gradients at the individual public service facility and an overall spatial configuration that is structurally imbalanced. At the aggregate level, central urban districts (particularly the six core districts centered on Donghu districts and Xihu districts) exhibit significantly higher facility density and overall provision scale compared to peripheral counties (such as Nanchang County, Jinxian County, and Anyi County), leading to a notable "high-density core–low-density periphery" pattern. These results highlight an uneven distribution of urban spatial resources and offer crucial empirical evidence for evaluating spatial equity in facility distribution, pinpointing priority areas for resource allocation, and enhancing public service provision strategies.

Characteristics of spatial coverage efficiency of public service facilities in Nanchang

Using ArcGIS 10.8, this study performed spatial overlay and quantitative coverage analyses of eight facility categories and twenty-four facility types, together with residential AOI in Nanchang City, to characterize the spatial service performance and distribution of facility types. The results reveal pronounced spatial heterogeneity in overall facility coverage across the urban area (Table 3; Fig. 9). At the city scale, coverage rates for most public service facilities are substantially higher in central districts than in peripheral zones. Donghu districts, Xihu districts, and Qingshanhu districts show consistently higher coverage across all eight facility categories relative to the citywide average, indicating a concentration of public resources in the urban core. By contrast, outer districts such as Xinjian districts, Anyi County, and Jinxian County exhibit markedly lower coverage across all categories, highlighting their structural disadvantage in accessing essential public services.

To capture the finer-scale variations in spatial equity, a detailed analysis of the 24 specific facility types was conducted (Table 3; Fig. 10). Comparing districts and counties reveals a growing spatial imbalance in the distribution of the twenty-four types of public service facilities. In Nanchang’s traditional urban core—Donghu and Xihu districts—twenty-four and twenty-three facility types, respectively, have coverage rates above the citywide average, indicating a marked concentration of high-quality public services. Peripheral districts, by contrast, show low coverage across most facility types. In Xinjian districts and Nanchang County, more than 90% of public service facilities have coverage rates well below the citywide average. This uneven spatial distribution reflects a persistent structural imbalance in urban development and underscores the need for spatially differentiated planning to improve equity in access to public services and narrow the widening gap in living quality among districts.

These findings indicate that Nanchang’s current public service configuration reinforces a “core advantage–peripheral deficit” spatial pattern, constraining equitable access to essential services for peripheral residents. Public service planning should therefore emphasize system-level coordination and spatial equity by allocating resources differentially, prioritizing underserved areas, and optimizing facility placement using accessibility and service-radius metrics. Strengthening transportation and service linkages between core and peripheral zones is also essential to reduce spatial resource imbalances. Collectively, these measures will promote justice in public service provision and help narrow the development gap between the urban core and surrounding districts.

Supply–demand perspective: accessibility assessment of residential communities

The initial phase of the Gaussian two-step floating catchment area (Ga2SFCA) model computes supply–demand ratios for various types of facilities at the community level. These metrics assess the correspondence between service capacity and population demand, as well as reveal spatial distribution patterns of facilities within the community (Fig. 11).

Supply–demand ratio of eight categories of community public service facilities.

The results reveal significant spatial heterogeneity in the distribution and service capacity of community public service facilities across the study area. Commercial, healthcare, elderly care, sports, and cultural facilities show concentrated supply clusters in various communities within the central urban area, mainly due to higher facility density and focused resource allocation. On the other hand, educational, leisure, and transportation facilities display a more widespread and evenly distributed pattern of spatial coverage.

Additional examination demonstrates that while central districts such as Donghu districts, Xihu districts, and Qingshanhu districts have a greater total number of facilities, their supply–demand ratios are comparatively low because of high population density. This disparity leads to increased service demands and limited per capita resource access, illustrating a common phenomenon known as "supply concentration-demand crowding" in densely populated urban regions. In general, the distribution of facilities at the community level follows a spatial pattern showing sufficient availability in outlying areas and insufficient availability in the city center, underscoring a continual structural incongruity between facility distribution and population needs.

The Gaussian two-step floating catchment area (Ga2SFCA) model’s second stage defines residential communities as demand origins and public service facilities as supply destinations, establishing a spatial interaction framework to depict residents’ service access. Utilizing actual road network data, an origin–destination (OD) time–cost matrix is created at a 15-min walking scale. Additionally, a distance-decay function is integrated to mimic the decrease in service utilization likelihood as distance grows, thus approximating actual travel patterns and enhancing the precision of characterizing accessibility to community public service facilities.

The results (Fig. 12) reveal pronounced spatial differentiation in accessibility across different types of public service facilities at the subdistrict scale, which can be broadly categorized into two typologies. The first category—including commercial, educational, and transportation facilities—exhibits a "multi-nodal, networked" spatial structure, characterized by widespread distribution and the formation of continuous service coverage networks. In contrast, the second category (e.g., leisure and elderly care facilities) displays pronounced spatial clustering and regional disparities. These facilities are primarily concentrated in densely populated urban cores, forming localized high-supply zones. However, accessibility analysis reveals a paradoxical "high supply–low accessibility" pattern, indicating constrained service catchments, intensified spatial competition, and elevated service pressure.

Accessibility map of eight types of community-level public service facilities.

Accessibility demonstrates a distinct spatial gradient characterized by being high in the center and low in the periphery. This observation contradicts previous analyses based on supply–demand ratios, emphasizing that a greater number of facilities in central urban areas does not guarantee better service accessibility. Despite the concentration of facilities in central urban areas, the combination of high population density and intense competition for services diminishes their marginal service efficiency. In contrast, peripheral areas experience restricted accessibility because of inadequate provision and the dispersed spatial distribution of facilities. This spatial arrangement underscores the absence of efficient coordination among facility quantity, spatial layout, and accessibility.

Characteristics of coordination in the development of public service facilities

Assessing public service facilities solely by clustering , coverage rates, and accessibility analysis is inadequate. It is also necessary to evaluate how facility provision aligns with urban population needs using coordination analysis. Guided by the Urban Residential Area Planning and Design Standards (GB50180-2018)45 and prior research, this study develops a comprehensive evaluation framework for the coordinated development of public service facilities. The framework systematically examines the spatial matching between facilities and resident demand in each community life circle, revealing patterns of supply justice and service accessibility across Nanchang City.

The results in Table 4 reveal pronounced disparities in development coordination across the twenty-four categories of public service facilities, indicating a spatially uneven facility configuration. This imbalance primarily reflects a mismatch between facility supply and residents’ everyday service needs. Specifically, nine facility categories—such as convenience stores and agricultural markets—exhibit high or adequate coordination, with wide service coverage and good spatial accessibility. In contrast, eleven facility types, including elderly activity centers and primary schools, show various degrees of mismatch, and some reach severe imbalance. The shortfall in elderly care facilities is especially acute: both elderly service centers and nursing homes are severely underprovided relative to demand. This deficit contrasts sharply with Nanchang’s rapid population aging and underscores the urgent need to optimize the spatial distribution of elderly care facilities to meet growing demand for age-friendly services. At the same time, as the city’s population grows and residents’ expectations for quality of life rise, educational and cultural facilities also display mismatched patterns. The uneven distribution of high-quality educational and cultural resources requires strategic optimization to attain more balanced spatial coverage. At the urban scale, the central districts of Nanchang generally have well-developed facility systems with high or appropriately matched provision levels. By contrast, four peripheral districts and counties, including Xinjian districts and Nanchang County, still exhibit substantial gaps between public service supply and population demand. These deficits can impede residents’ access to essential daily services and thus undermine spatial equity in public resource allocation.

Suggestions for improving public service facilities and enhancing service capacity in Nanchang

Strategically optimizing the configuration of public service facilities enhances accessibility and convenience for surrounding residential neighborhoods, significantly improving residents’ quality of life and establishing a solid foundation for the development of "15-min communities".

This study examines regions in Nanchang City characterized by an imbalance between the supply and demand of public service facilities. Its objectives are to identify the scale and location of service gaps across various types of public service facilities under optimal conditions, establish a theoretical benchmark for spatial redistribution, and produce a prioritized list of facilities for spatial optimization. In response to local needs and utilizing discrete unit coverage methodologies, employed the K-means clustering algorithm was employed to identify the critical threshold where marginal SSE values begin to show significant decline, thereby determining the optimal range for new facility additions. Subsequently, a greedy algorithm was applied alongside the Minimize Impedance Model (L-A model) to optimize the spatial layout of various public service facilities. Utilizing the ArcGIS 10.8 platform, the optimization process prioritized minimal facility deployment while balancing construction and economic costs, with the aim of maximizing residential coverage within the "15-min community" and providing actionable spatial planning schemes for different service types.

Despite high facility numbers and density in densely populated zones, the provision of services was inadequate, as indicated by development coordination analyses. The central urban districts exhibited an overconcentration of commercial and educational facilities, leading to lower facility density and coverage in peripheral regions. To achieve a more balanced and effective spatial distribution of commercial establishments, kindergartens, primary schools, and secondary schools, ensuring convenient access to education for minors, the study recommends adding 56 commercial facilities, 198 primary schools (each designed with a standard capacity of 24 classes,with a service radius of 1,500 m), 32 kindergartens (each with 9 classes, with a service radius of 1,000 m), and 54 secondary schools (each accommodating 36 classes, with a service radius of 1,500 m and catering to around 25,000 residents) in peripheral districts. This initiative is projected to achieve full coverage of commercial facilities, meeting the convenience needs of local residents. Moreover, the coverage of educational facilities is anticipated to rise to 88.1% for primary schools, 95% for kindergartens, and 86.3% for secondary schools, resulting in an overall educational coverage rate of 97.8% and significantly enhancing access for children and adolescents. Moreover, the integration of digital service platforms and the promotion of hybrid online-offline service modes have the potential to revolutionize conventional public service delivery by expanding the functionality of facilities in both physical and digital realms. In areas with high convenience, the precise targeting facilitated by digital tools can effectively fill supply gaps, thereby improving system integrity, broadening consumer choices, and fostering the vertical growth of local commercial environments. Likewise, educational institutions can utilize subscription-based online learning platforms to broaden the accessibility of top-notch education, offer educational services in a more diverse and flexible manner, and alleviate inequities in resource allocation.

Under macro-level socio-economic transformations, including population aging and rapid urbanization, medical and elderly care facilities encounter significant challenges related to insufficient supply and uneven spatial distribution. Coverage rates for general and community hospitals are 53.3% and 56.8%, respectively, while overall coverage for elderly care facilities is 45.8%. Notably, nursing homes and senior activity centers are particularly underprovided, with coverage rates of 27.9% and 33.0%. Medical facility planning must adhere to the principle of hierarchical diagnosis and treatment, strategically deploying auxiliary medical services. By planning for 46 additional community medical service facilities (each with a service radius of 1,000 m, staffed by 3–5 general practitioners and equipped with 10–15 nursing beds), coverage could extend to 98.8% of residential units, effectively addressing preventive, diagnostic, rehabilitative, and nursing needs. For elderly care, facility placement must align precisely with the spatial distribution of the aging population. This study proposes the addition of 235 elderly care facilities (The benchmark capacity for nursing home facilities is 30–50 nursing beds, and the benchmark capacity for a single elderly activity center is a service area of 300–500 m2), which would increase coverage from 45.8 to 76.5%, thereby substantially meeting the care requirements of the majority of residents. Furthermore, advancing elderly community medical complexes and promoting mixed-use land planning—integrating community centers and health stations (with a service radius of 500 m, building area of approximately 200–300 m2),community canteens (serving 100–200 meals per day,building area of approximately 400–600 m2), and senior activity centers (with a building area of approximately 500–800 m2).—can enhance land-use efficiency, improve accessibility to health resources, and foster synergy between medical and eldercare functions. This approach operationalizes the innovative concepts of "integrated medical and care services" and "nutrition-based care," ultimately enhancing service accessibility, continuity, and quality across elderly care networks.

Optimizing the spatial layout of public service facilities necessitates addressing residents’ demands for a high-quality lifestyle. Leisure, sports, and cultural facilities play a crucial role in fostering vibrant community life, impacting residents’ well-being, social interactions, and sense of belonging. Currently, these facilities face challenges such as inadequate quantity, uneven distribution, demographic mismatch, and service coverage gaps. To address these issues, this study recommends the addition of 75 leisure facilities (each with a service radius of 500 m, a site area of no less than 500 m2, and equipped with seating and children’s play facilities) and 63 sports facilities (each with a service radius of 500 m, a site area of no less than 800 m2, and equipped with basketball courts, table tennis facilities, fitness trails, and exercise equipment) in densely populated areas, aiming to cater to the daily fitness and recreational needs of over 97.6% of residents. The implementation strategy should utilize existing spaces, incorporate micro-parks and fitness layouts, and consider the organized use of school sports grounds to maximize coverage and efficiency. Regarding cultural facilities, the study proposes concentrated layouts and shared resources among different facility types. This includes establishing 288 small-scale cultural service complexes (each with a service radius of 800 m and a building area of no less than 1,200 m2,integrating multifunctional facilities such as library reading rooms, cultural activities, and intangible cultural heritage exhibitions.) to enhance coverage from 78.9 to 94.7%. Additionally, the integration of digital platforms and regular community cultural events can expand service accessibility, promoting cultural identity and community unity.

Nanchang’s transportation infrastructure currently falls short of its socio-economic development, leading to incomplete coverage. To rectify this issue, the study recommends the addition of 37 bus stops in previously underserved communities, with the goal of achieving 100% coverage. This initiative not only guarantees comprehensive daily mobility for residents but also facilitates Nanchang’s transition to a low-carbon urban environment. Despite challenges posed by roads, waterways, existing built environments, and residential distributions, the optimized facility layouts significantly improve coverage and accessibility, thereby robustly supporting residents’ pursuit of high-quality, holistic, and health-oriented urban living (Fig. 14).

Comparison of configurations of public service facilities in Nanchang City with other cities of the same status

From the perspective of comparative cities, this study adopts a comparative urban framework to situate Nanchang among a selection of peer and neighboring provincial capitals, thereby clarifying the structural characteristics of its public service facility configuration. Changsha49, Wuhan50 and Nanjing51 are selected as reference cases due to their comparable urban scale, functional hierarchy, and demographic structure.

In terms of allocation pathways,Changsha has adopted a multi-center, cluster-based development strategy to efficiently allocate public service facilities by dispersing services to sub-centers and establishing a three-tiered system of "urban cluster–subdistrict–community" to improve resident-facility interaction. In contrast, Nanchang relies mainly on project-specific interventions, resulting in limited facility coverage in peripheral areas and a lack of a structured hierarchical gradient. Unlike Wuhan and Nanjing, which employ an “incremental balancing” approach by expanding facilities along major urban corridors, Nanchang heavily relies on existing resource agglomeration. Consequently, high-quality public services are concentrated in the historic urban core, causing significant delays in service provision for new districts and peri-urban counties.

The discrepancies in urban spatial structure between Nanchang and other cities like Nanjing, Wuhan, and Changsha contribute to the observed differences. Nanchang deviates from Nanjing’s concentric-radial layout, Wuhan’s river-segmented multi-center design, and Changsha’s grid-like structure. Instead, Nanchang features a unique "one river, two banks" spatial arrangement, characterized by the simultaneous development of the emerging Honggutan new districts and the revitalization of the historical core. This dual framework has led to significant variations in urban form, population density, and infrastructure distribution between the new and old urban areas.

Discussion

Key findings

This study devised a comprehensive model to assess and enhance public service facilities, covering data preprocessing, indicator calculation, visualization, and optimization strategy development. Using the "Six Districts and Three Counties" of Nanchang as a case study, a multi-level spatial analysis was performed to assess the coverage and equity of eight categories consisting of twenty-four types of public service facilities. This analysis yielded three principal insights:

(1) Coverage analysis utilizing kernel density estimation and spatial coupling with population distribution revealed a marked "center–periphery" differentiation in the provision of public service facilities. Various facility types demonstrated significant spatial heterogeneity; central districts exhibited functional agglomeration, while peripheral districts faced "service deserts." This coexistence represents a critical structural constraint on the balanced development of urban space. Additionally, disparities in facility concentration and coverage among districts resulted in areas that are "accessible but functionally limited" alongside "functionally comprehensive but centrally concentrated" zones, thereby creating unequal opportunities for residents to access services. These findings highlight a pronounced hierarchy and spatial inequity in the distribution of functional public services relative to the actual experiences of residents, which sharply contrasts with the demands stemming from population aging and rising expectations for enhanced quality of life. Therefore, structural adjustments at the planning level are imperative to improve spatial equity and balance in service provision.

(2) At the facility optimization level, integrating K-means clustering with Location-Allocation (L-A) minimize-impedance model allows for designing facility addition schemes that maximize coverage efficiency while minimizing new facility numbers. The study establishes a referable and evidence-base closed-loop framework of "data processing → kernel density identification → accessibility analysis → coverage calculation → coordination assessment → optimization implementation" to assist similar cities in evaluating life-circle accessibility and optimizing public service facility layouts. In the case of Nanchang, simulation results show that key public service facilities’ coverage could increase to around 80–100%, effectively reducing central-periphery disparities in urban public service provision, enhancing residents’ quality of life, and improving essential services access in peripheral areas.

(3) The study proposes actionable pathways for the systematic spatial optimization of public service facilities. Specifically, targeted interventions through differentiated resource allocation and precise gap-filling strategies facilitate focused improvements in areas characterized by low coverage and poor coordination, thereby reinforcing the structural redistribution of spatial resources. First, leveraging digital platforms to extend service reach overcomes the spatial constraints inherent in traditional facility layouts. This approach enables the deep integration of offline physical infrastructure with online public services, significantly enhancing the temporal and spatial flexibility and accessibility of the service system. Second, by integrating healthcare and eldercare services and adopting community multifunctional layouts, diverse facilities—including medical, eldercare, rehabilitation, cultural, and recreational services—can operate synergistically within a unified spatial system. This integration substantially enhances land-use efficiency and overall convenience for residents. Finally, establishing a low-carbon, high-efficiency mobility system, grounded in comprehensive public transit coverage and supported by seamless connections between bus and rail networks, encourages residents to adopt sustainable, green travel behaviors while promoting the efficient integration and sustainable evolution of urban service spaces.

Differences and practical significance from previous research

This study presents a practical framework for evaluating and improving public service facilities, based on the concept of the "15-min living circle." It performs a diagnostic analysis and optimization simulation of public service facilities, utilizing six districts and three counties in Nanchang as empirical case studies. The key innovations and contributions are emphasized in five main areas:

(1) From Individual Points to Systemic Assessment: This study develops a triadic metric system characterized by “aggregation–coverage–matching” and introduces indicators for cross-category synergy and coupling. This methodology reveals the “co-constructive and symbiotic” spatial organization patterns among various facility types, offering systematic evidence for integrated life-circle planning. It also addresses the limitations of previous studies that concentrated on individual facility types and failed to produce recommendations for system-level optimization.

(2) From static analysis to human-centered evaluation: Beyond conventional coverage and kernel density analyzes, the framework emphasizes the alignment and fairness of facility supply relative to population distribution, both spatially and quantitatively. This shift will facilitate a more precise assessment of service performance and spatial equity of public service facilities.

(3) From diagnosis to actionable decision-making: By identifying "service blind spots—potential areas—redundant zones," the framework produces a prioritized list along with three types of strategic packages: incremental siting, existing stock reorganization, and multifunctional transformation. Its objective is to evaluate and optimize outcomes, thereby offering reference for implementation plans and strengthening the robustness of planning decisions.

(4) From results to reliability: Integrating multiple data sources and validating robustness. The research incorporates Public service facilities POI dataset, population raster, and road network data to analyze the impact of deviations in POI diversity and spatial aggregation on research conclusions. This approach enhances the referential value of the findings and ensures their reliable applicability across diverse scenarios.

(5) From case study to reusability: The research establishes a referable and evidence-based closed-loop with reference "data–method–output" workflow that includes standardized pipelines for data preprocessing, indicator computation, visualization, and strategy translation (Fig. 15). This framework offers a scalable technical paradigm and a practical approach for other cities to efficiently conduct life-circle assessments and optimize facilities.

Analytical critique

1. Structural tension between service efficiency and spatial equity

Current studies on community life circles often focus on achieving “optimal accessibility” or "maximized service coverage" of public service facilities. This approach typically prioritizes “service efficiency” as the key evaluation criterion. However, strategies emphasizing efficiency through intensification and economies of scale can unintentionally worsen spatial disparities and lead to unintended consequences such as facility clustering. In larger regions, densely populated areas with high demand often receive more resources over time due to economies of scale, leaving low-density or economically disadvantaged areas at risk of being marginalized in optimization processes. In urban settings, concentrated facility clustering frequently leads to population growth, traffic congestion, and rising land prices, potentially resulting in spatial exclusion and social inequality. On a community level, overreliance on centralized facilities can diminish the local character of daily life, weaken neighborhood social connections, and ultimately undermine community resilience. In the realm of public service facilities, relying on supply indicators based solely on the quantity of facilities can skew assessments of facility allocation coordination due to significant spatial variations in facility size and service capabilities. For example, a higher DCI value may indicate a concentration of POI numbers but fails to fully represent the actual service capacity and adequacy of services. For instance, the DCI value of convenience store facilities in Qingyunpu district exceeds that of the city center Donghu district, indicating potential differences in service capacity and supply between the two areas. This disparity may stem from the land use composition and urban morphology of Donghu district as an established urban zone, or the commercial configuration of Donghu districts’ retail establishments (particularly the distribution of diverse commercial facilities). Consequently, the development coordinating analysis outcomes may overstate the supply in regions with numerous small facilities while underestimating it in areas predominantly occupied by large institutions.

2. Contextual applicability risk of the standardized paradigm of "15-min living circle"

The "15-min community life circle" concept frequently guides urban planning through a standardized quantitative system. However, this method assumes population homogeneity, consistent resident mobility, and uniform spatial resource distribution. Overreliance on standardized methods could hinder local wisdom and community efforts, redirecting planning attention from accommodating various needs to strict adherence to metrics.

The service radius defined by the "15-min living circle" may be too generous given the concentrated spatial distribution of facilities in urban areas. The observed near-complete coverage, especially the 100% coverage of commercial and transportation facilities in Donghu and Xihu districts, and over 99% coverage of medical services, should be approached with caution. These findings could suggest a well-developed or saturated service network but also highlight limitations in the ability of coverage indicators to differentiate. In densely packed areas, broad service radii lead to significant overlap, making binary coverage metrics less effective and reducing spatial diversity to uniformly high levels. Furthermore, achieving nearly 100% coverage might inaccurately portray the actual service delivery due to disregarding demand pressures and capacity limitations. This situation frequently gives rise to a paradox known as "high coverage–low effective accessibility" in densely populated urban centers. Thus, extensive coverage does not automatically guarantee sufficient or optimal service delivery and could mask fundamental discrepancies between supply and demand as well as intra-urban disparities.

3. The discontinuity between “Computational optimality” and “Planning feasibility”

Existing studies have established a closed analytical loop along the technical pathway of "spatial diagnosis–model optimization–strategy output." However, a significant gap persists in translating computational optimality into planning feasibility, particularly due to the insufficient integration of key real-world constraints such as land availability, fiscal capacity, and governance conditions. Land resource constraints are often treated as normative parameters at the individual facility level rather than being elevated to city-scale spatial allocation mechanisms, hindering the integration of optimization strategies into actual urban parcel structures. This detached optimization approach undermines the implementability of proposed solutions within current planning systems.

Optimization models tend to prioritize maximum coverage with minimal facility provision, assuming unlimited public finance capacity. Without considering constraints like life-cycle costs, fiscal capacity thresholds, and phased investment strategies, the projected scale of provision may surpass fiscal affordability, resulting in technically optimal but economically infeasible outcomes. From a governance standpoint, facility allocation in Chinese urban governance is influenced by spatial efficiency, equity, land concession mechanisms, mandatory provision requirements such as public facility quotas, and multi-actor governance structures. Consequently, the outcomes of optimizing facilities should be viewed as spatial configuration schemes on a theoretical basis, rather than as immediately actionable planning recommendations. The policy significance of these schemes relies on how well they align with practical constraints that follow.

Research limitations

Although this study has introduced methodological innovations and made empirical contributions, it still has several limitations that need to be addressed in future research. These limitations should be resolved to improve the accuracy, applicability, and comprehensiveness of evaluations of public service facilities and optimization strategies.

Firstly, the study is limited by the temporal cross-section of the data used, which includes population data from the Seventh National Population Census in 2020 and residential and public service facility POI data from the Baidu Open Platform. These sources do not consider changes such as new constructions, renovations, or facility closures post-data collection, potentially overestimating the service coverage of existing public facilities. Because of the diverse spatiotemporal needs of residents, this study does not distinguish between residents’ spatiotemporal needs and the behavioral paths within the daily travel chain (home-work-services). Moreover, the study might underestimate development coordination due to unaccounted population growth and demographic shifts, introducing biases into the evaluation of supply–demand alignment within urban life circles.

Secondly, the kernel density analysis in this study utilized uniform bandwidth parameters, which may not adequately consider variations in density scales within individual facilities or substantial differences in point densities among various facility types. Furthermore,the methodology for analyzing facility accessibility and coverage involves simplifications that may compromise result accuracy by not fully considering physical facility attributes such as capacity, operational status, and service quality, which notably influence accessibility and functionality. Moreover, by assuming a constant walking speed, the model overlooks differences in individual walking capabilities, age-related mobility discrepancies, and the inherent decrease in willingness to travel longer distances, all essential for precisely depicting pedestrian behavior and facility utilization.

Furthermore, the model neglects to consider the impacts of topographic obstacles and geographical limits. Using Nanchang as an example, the Ganjiang River acts as a significant natural divide, along with Qingshan Lake and East Lake, creating substantial barriers to pedestrian mobility. This difference directly hinders pedestrian access and route selection, exacerbating the bias of walking distance assumptions, especially when assessing service coverage over river and lake boundaries. These simplifications in the model may yield evaluation results that do not accurately reflect the intricacies of walking requirements and behavioral trends in actual urban settings.

Fourth, The methodological framework demonstrates systematicity and replicability in spatial analysis and technical pathway construction. However, it lacks consideration for crucial real-world dimensions such as land availability, fiscal capacity, and governance constraints. As a result, the optimization framework shows a degree of “decontextualization” by not fully integrating intricate constraints linked to land-use conditions, socioeconomic factors, and regulatory frameworks.

Future research directions

Future research should focus on creating a dynamically updating evaluation system to better capture temporal and spatial variations in urban public service provision. This system could utilize multiple temporal datasets and real-time monitoring technologies, like integrating real-time population mobility data with facility operational status, to reduce temporal biases found in static datasets. Moreover, employing advanced computational methods such as machine learning and predictive analytics could help adjust datasets, detect emerging patterns, and facilitate ongoing, anticipatory evaluations of facility distribution and population trends. When interpreting spatial equity and coordination outcomes, it is essential to consider structural biases in facility scale attributes like building area, building capacity, service capacity, and staffing levels to improve the reliability of coordination assessments.

From a modeling perspective, the advancement of more sophisticated spatial analysis frameworks is crucial. This may involve the use of network-based distance metrics, such as Manhattan distance and Gaussian two-step floating catchment area methods, along with other high-precision spatial accessibility measures. It is also important to incorporate the physical characteristics, capacity, and operational constraints of facilities. Additionally, the integration of intelligent sensing data to simulate individual travel trajectories can enable the development of hybrid models that account for heterogeneity in resident behavior. Such models would offer a more accurate representation of pedestrian movements and spatial interactions within the built environment.

Future optimization recommendations should prioritize the establishment of context-sensitive planning objectives at the strategic and practical levels. The introduction of a "constraint-driven optimization framework" is crucial for incorporating land supply, fiscal budgets, and institutional rules as core model parameters. Through the creation of a "multi-objective, multi-constraint" planning decision model, this approach allows a transition from “spatial optimality” to "practically feasible solutions," bridging the gap between technical analysis and planning implementation.

Enhancing current spatial analysis involves integrating subjective well-being52 and intergenerational equity53 to advance spatial matching models beyond assessing the physical environment to evaluating human-centered utility. Existing studies predominantly concentrate on aligning resource provision with geographic space, overlooking users’ subjective perceptions. Therefore, a key objective is to quantify well-being to transition from “physical alignment” to "experiential adaptation." Discussions on spatial equity should evolve from intragenerational equality to intergenerational equity, taking into account "intergenerational spatial conflicts" arising from variations in behavioral patterns, temporal rhythms, and spatial needs among age groups. Future models should pinpoint and quantify these conflicts to harmonize the diverse requirements of elderly and younger populations, fostering neighborhood inclusivity and social cohesion.

Conclusion

Enhancing the service capacity of public facilities within the "15-min living circle" is essential for improving residential livability. Integrating population demand into the evaluation of facility service capacity not only enables the development of more targeted and effective improvement strategies but also aids in optimizing facility provision and dynamically aligning with residents’ changing needs. Consequently, this approach improves both the operational efficiency of public service facilities and resident satisfaction, ultimately reinforcing overall service capabilities.

This study examines Nanchang City through an analytical framework based on the concepts of the “15-min living circle”. The analysis reveals several significant findings: (1) Public service facilities in Nanchang display a pronounced “center–periphery” pattern, indicating substantial spatial heterogeneity. Facilities are highly concentrated in central urban areas, forming dense clusters, while peripheral and newly developing districts experience sparse facility coverage, resulting in localized “service deserts”; (2) Facility coverage within the "15-min living circle" exhibits considerable variability across service types, with overall coverage efficiency remaining relatively low. This suggests that many residents may encounter difficulties in accessing essential services within a 15-min walk, underscoring the necessity for strategic facility enhancement and spatial optimization. (3) Development coordination among various types of facilities varies significantly between districts, showcasing distinct spatial discrepancies arising from the mismatch between facility supply and local population needs, as well as from underlying socio-demographic aging pressures and uneven urban growth. (4) The introduction of new public service facilities has notably enhanced coverage rates and development coordination citywide. The strategic placement of these facilities not only enhances accessibility within a 15-min walking radius but also promotes a more equitable distribution of services, ultimately bolstering social inclusivity and enhancing urban livability.
