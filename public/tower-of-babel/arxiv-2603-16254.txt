Algebraicity of supermoduli of curves via Artin’s criteria
Nadia Ott

Abstract
We apply the supergeometric analogue of Artin’s algebraicity criteria [ 20 ] to prove algebraicity for four moduli problems in supergeometry: supercurves, super Riemann surfaces, stable supercurves, and stable super Riemann surfaces. The algebraicity of the moduli of (stable) super Riemann surfaces is known (see e.g. [ 8 , 13 ] ), but we give a new proof by verifying the super Artin conditions. The algebraicity of the moduli of (stable) supercurves is new.

The moduli space of super Riemann surfaces

Variants with Ramond punctures were shown to be algebraic superspaces in

Recent work in

In Section

The moduli space of genus

Once algebraicity is established, the Deligne–Mumford property follows from the finiteness of automorphism groups (see e.g.

Again, the Deligne–Mumford property follows from the finiteness of automorphism groups

A super Riemann surface is a pair

The moduli problems for supercurves and stable supercurves themselves have not been studied independently, although they form the natural base moduli spaces over which superconformal structures are imposed. In this paper we prove that both are algebraic superstacks.

Theorem 1.3
.

The moduli space of (stable) supercurves
\overline{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{C}}_{g}

The proof is given in Sections
3.2

This restriction simplifies the argument while omitting only a small class of supercurves (Lemma

4.3
).

Future directions.

For super Riemann surfaces and their stable compactification, the Deligne–Mumford property follows from algebraicity and the finiteness of automorphism groups. It is natural to ask whether the moduli of stable supercurves is Deligne–Mumford, and whether it admits a proper or compactification property analogous to the classical case.

More generally, the moduli of stable supercurves should play a foundational role for supergeometric moduli problems, serving as a base over which additional structures are imposed. It would be interesting to study moduli problems parametrizing supercurves together with additional geometric data (line bundles, vector bundles, coherent sheaves), and to determine whether these can be treated systematically using the super Artin criteria.

Another natural direction is to further develop the deformation theory of super Riemann surfaces in a more intrinsic manner. In this paper the sheaf
\mathcal{A}_{X/T}^{1}\subset\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1}
is defined via explicit local computations at Ramond and NS nodes. It would be interesting to find a more intrinsic, functorial definition of
\mathcal{A}_{X/T}^{1}
constructed directly from the quasi-superconformal structure
\delta
.

More broadly, many moduli problems in algebraic geometry and mathematical physics have not yet been developed in the supergeometric setting. We hope that this paper demonstrates that the super Artin criteria provide an effective tool for constructing such moduli spaces.

We use the heuristic term

2.2
Superstacks

Let
\operatorname{sSch}
denote the category of Noetherian superschemes over
k
.

The
functor of points
of a superscheme
X
is the contravariant functor

h_{X}:\operatorname{sSch}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Set}

sending a superscheme
T

h_{X}(T):=\operatorname{Hom}(T,X)

of grading-preserving morphisms from

A functor

representable
if there exists a superscheme

such that
F\cong h_{X}
.

The functor of points formalism provides a precise formulation of a moduli problem. Given a class of objects together with a notion of isomorphism, define a functor

M\colon\operatorname{sSch}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Set}

sending a superscheme
T
to the set of isomorphism classes of families parameterized by
T

M
is representable, then the moduli problem is represented by a superscheme, and we call it a
fine moduli space
.

Fine moduli spaces tend to not exist when the moduli objects have non-trivial automorphisms. This is especially common in supergeometry.
The standard remedy is to incorporate isomorphisms into the moduli problem itself. Instead of recording only isomorphism classes, one assigns to each
T
the groupoid of families over
T
, keeping track of both objects and their isomorphisms. A category in which every morphism is an isomorphism is called a groupoid.
One may encode such a moduli problem by a pseudo–functor

\mathcal{X}\colon\operatorname{sSch}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Groupoids}

where the category of superschemes
\operatorname{sSch}
is equipped with the big étale topology.

Every such pseudo–functor gives rise to a
category fibered in groupoids
. A category fibered in groupoids over
\operatorname{sSch}
is a functor

p:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\operatorname{sSch}

satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

For every morphism
f:S\to T
in
\operatorname{sSch}
and every object
X
of
\mathcal{X}
over
T
, there exists a cartesian morphism
\tilde{f}:Y\to X
lying over
f
.

(ii)

Cartesian morphisms are stable under composition.

A category fibered in groupoids
\mathcal{X}\to\operatorname{sSch}
is called an
étale superstack
if the following descent conditions are satisfied:

(i)

(
Descent for morphisms
.) For every
U\in\operatorname{sSch}
and
X,Y\in\mathcal{X}(U)
, the presheaf

\displaystyle\mathbf{\operatorname{Isom}}(X,Y):\operatorname{sSch}/U

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Set}

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\longmapsto\{\alpha:X|_{V}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}Y|_{V}\text{ is an isomorphism in }\mathcal{X}(V)\}

is a sheaf on the étale site

(ii)

(

, objects

, and isomorphisms

\alpha_{ij}:\operatorname{pr}_{ij}^{*}X_{i}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{pr}_{ji}^{*}X_{j}

A superstack

is

Projective system of supercurves

We will now fix notation used in the proofs of effectivity (A4) throughout the paper. At the end of a section we state a lemma used in the proof of effectivity for the moduli of strongly projective supercurves. The arguments of the lemma are used in later sections.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local Noetherian
k
-superalgebra. Set

R_{j}=R/\mathfrak{m}^{j+1},\qquad T=\operatorname{Spec}(R),\qquad T_{j}=\operatorname{Spec}(R_{j}).

The natural surjections

R\twoheadrightarrow R_{j}\twoheadrightarrow R_{i}

induce morphisms

T_{i}\to T_{j}\to T.

Let
(X_{j},\,X_{i}\to X_{j})
be a projective system of strongly projective supercurves over the system
(T_{j},\,T_{i}\to T_{j})
. For simplicity we denote all structure morphisms
X_{j}\to T_{j}
by
\pi
. The curves are related by base change

X_{i}\cong X_{j}\times_{T_{j}}T_{i},

and the transition morphisms

\operatorname{pr}_{ij}:X_{i}\to X_{j}

are the projections from this fiber product. In particular each
\operatorname{pr}_{ij}
is a nilpotent thickening and all elements of such a projective system have the same topological space.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be any coherent sheaf on a superscheme whose formation commutes with base change.
On a projective system
(X_{j},X_{i}\to X_{j})
these sheaves form a projective system
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{L}_{j},\,\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})
as follows. For each
j\geq i
, base change gives a canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{pr}_{ji}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{j}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{L}_{i}.

We define the transition morphism
\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}
to be the adjoint of this isomorphism, i.e. the composite

\mathcal{L}_{j}\longrightarrow\operatorname{pr}_{ij*}\operatorname{pr}_{ji}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{j}\longrightarrow\operatorname{pr}_{ij*}\mathcal{L}_{i}.

(12)

Since
\operatorname{pr}_{ij}
is a nilpotent thickening, all
X_{j}
have the same underlying topological space, and we will identify sheaves on
X_{i}
with their direct images on
X_{j}
. This gives us morphisms
\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}
that we refer to as
standard transition maps
.

If
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{L}_{j},\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})
is a system of canonically defined coherent sheaves on
X_{\bullet}=(X_{j},X_{i}\to X_{j})
, we say that
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}
has property
P
if each
\mathcal{L}_{j}
has property
P
. If
F
is a functor on coherent sheaves,
we write
F(\mathcal{L}_{\bullet})
for the induced system

F(\mathcal{L}_{\bullet})=(F(\mathcal{L}_{j}),\,F(\mathcal{L}_{j})\to F(\mathcal{L}_{i})).

Lemma 3.5
.

Using the above notation, for any projective system of strongly projective supercurves
(X_{j},X_{i}\to X_{j})
, there exists an integer
N
such that the system

\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{L}_{j},\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{j}:=\omega_{X_{j}/T_{j}}^{\otimes N}

is strongly relatively very ample and satisfies
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=0
.

Proof.

Choose an integer
n
such that
\omega_{X_{0}/T_{0}}^{\otimes n}
is strongly relatively very ample. Since this property can be checked after restricting along the nilpotent extension
X_{0}\to X_{j}
, it follows that
\omega_{X_{j}/T_{j}}^{\otimes n}
is strongly relatively very ample for all
j
, and

\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{L}_{j},\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{j}:=\omega_{X_{j}/T_{j}}^{\otimes n}

is a strongly relatively very ample projective system
of line bundles on
X_{\bullet}=(X_{j},X_{i}\to X_{j})
.

We now replace
n
by a positive multiple to ensure vanishing of higher cohomology. Since
X_{0}/T_{0}
is projective with polarization
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, the super version of Serre vanishing
[
6
]
implies that there exists an integer
m\geq 1
such that

R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X_{0}/T_{0}}^{\otimes mn}=0.

Redefine

\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{L}_{j},\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{j}:=\omega_{X_{j}/T_{j}}^{\otimes mn}.

We claim that this system is strongly relatively very ample and satisfies
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=0
. The first statement holds since taking a positive tensor power preserves strong relative very ampleness.

For the vanishing of
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}
, we argue by induction on
j
. The base case
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{0}=0
holds by construction. Assume
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{j-1}=0
, and set
M=\mathfrak{m}^{j}/\mathfrak{m}^{j+1}
. Since
X_{j}
is
T_{j}
-flat, we have a short exact sequence

0\to M\otimes_{R_{j}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{j}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{j}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{j-1}}\to 0.

(13)

after pulling back
0\to M\to R_{j}\to R_{j-1}\to 0
.

Because
\mathfrak{m}\cdot M=0
, the
R_{j}
-module structure on
M
factors through
k=R/\mathfrak{m}
, so
M
is a super vector space of some finite dimension
(p|q)
. Thus,

M\otimes_{R_{j}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{j}}\cong M\otimes_{k}(R_{0}\otimes_{R_{j}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{j}})\cong M\otimes_{k}\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}^{p|q},

since
R_{0}\otimes_{R_{j}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{j}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}
. Then tensoring (

) with
\mathcal{L}_{j}
gives

0\to\mathcal{L}_{0}^{p|q}\to\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\mathcal{L}_{j-1}\to 0

where we used that

\mathcal{L}_{j-1}\cong\mathcal{L}_{j}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{j}}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{j-1}}
since any power of the dualizing sheaf commutes with base change.
Applying

, we obtain the exact sequence

0\to\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{0}^{p|q}\to\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{j}\to\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{j-1}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{0}^{p|q}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{j}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{j-1}\to\cdots.

Since
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{0}^{p|q}=0
and

.
∎

3.1
Deformation theory of strongly projective supercurves

Let
A^{\prime}\to A
be a square-zero extension of superalgebras with kernel
M=\ker(A^{\prime}\to A)
a finitely generated
A
–module. Set
T=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, and
T^{\prime}=\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\prime})
.

A
deformation of
X/T
over
T^{\prime}
is a flat, proper superscheme
X^{\prime}/T^{\prime}
such that

X^{\prime}\times_{T^{\prime}}T\cong X.

A
projective deformation
is a deformation that is a strongly projective supercurve. An isomorphism of (projective) deformations is a
T^{\prime}
-linear isomorphism restricting to the identity on the central fiber.

Lemma 3.6
.

Every deformation of a stable supercurve
X/T
is projective. In particular, the set of isomorphism classes of projective deformations of
X/T
is a torsor for the group

H^{1}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+},

where
\mathcal{T}_{X/T}
is the relative tangent sheaf.

Proof.

We need to show that every
deformation
X^{\prime}/T^{\prime}
of
X/T
is a strongly projective supercurve. It is flat by definition, so smoothness can be checked on geometric fibers. Since
T\hookrightarrow T^{\prime}
is a nilpotent extension, the only geometric fiber is the central fiber
X/T
, which is smooth by assumption.

X^{\prime}/T^{\prime}
is strongly projective since
\omega_{X^{\prime}/T^{\prime}}
pulls back to the relatively ample line bundle
\omega_{X/T}
along the nilpotent extension
X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
and the property of being relatively ample is preserved under nilpotent thickening.

For the second claim, since
X/T
is smooth,
\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1}=\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{2}=0
and so there are no obstructions to deforming
X/T
along square-zero extensions. Then, by Theorem
7.1
, the deformations of
X/T
over
T^{\prime}
form a torsor for the group

H^{1}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}.

∎

3.2

Let

5
Stable supercurves

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and let
X
be a proper, Cohen–Macaulay superscheme of finite type over
k
.
We assume that the bosonic reduction

C=(|X|,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{J})

is a one-dimensional scheme, and that
X
has superdimension
1|1
at every smooth point: a point
q\in X
is
smooth
if there exists an isomorphism

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}\cong k[[z,\theta]],

in which case
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q,\operatorname{bos}}\cong k[[z]]
.

Let
q\in X
be a closed point. We denote by

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}=\varprojlim\mathcal{O}_{X,q}/\mathfrak{m}_{q}^{n}

the completion of the local superring at
q
with respect to its maximal ideal.

We say that
q
is a
Ramond node
if there exists an isomorphism

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}\cong k[[x,y,\theta]]/(xy),

and a
Neveu–Schwarz (NS) node
if there exists an isomorphism

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}\cong k[[x,y,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]]/(xy,\,x\theta_{2},\,y\theta_{1},\,\theta_{1}\theta_{2}).

In both cases, the bosonic reduction of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}
is isomorphic to
k[[x,y]]/(xy)
.

By a
node
on
X
we mean either a Ramond node or an NS node.

We say that
X
is a
nodal supercurve
if every point of
X
is either smooth or a node, and the bosonic reduction

C=(|X|,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{J})

is an ordinary nodal curve.

By definition, the bosonic reduction
C
is obtained by quotienting
\mathcal{O}_{X}
by the nilpotent ideal
\mathcal{J}
, so the canonical morphism

X\to C

induces a homeomorphism on underlying topological spaces. In particular,
|X|=|C|
.

Moreover, at a point
q\in X
, the completed local rings satisfy

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{C,q}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}/\mathcal{J}_{q}.

It follows from the explicit local models that
q
is smooth (respectively a node) on
X
if and only if its bosonic reduction is smooth (respectively a node) on
C
. Therefore, the set of singular points of
X
coincides with the singular locus of
C
.

In particular, if
\Sigma\subset X
denotes the singular locus of
X
, then

|\Sigma|=\mathrm{Sing}(C),\qquad|X\setminus\Sigma|=C_{\mathrm{sm}}.

Since
X
has superdimension
(1|1)
, the ideal
\mathcal{J}
is locally free of rank
(0|1)
and satisfies
\mathcal{J}^{2}=0
, so that

\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{J}.

The inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{C}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}
induces a morphism

X\to C,

whose pre-composition with the canonical closed immersion

is the identity on
C
.

This projection is used to define the normalization of
X
. Specifically, since
\mathcal{J}^{2}=0
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{J}
have the same integral closure, and the normalization
\tilde{X}
of
X
satisfies
\tilde{X}\cong X\times_{C}\tilde{C}
. The normalization map
\nu:\tilde{X}\to X
is the top horizontal arrow in the cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu}

where

is the normalization of

.

Since
|X|=|C|
, the schemes
X
and
C
have the same set of irreducible components, which we denote by
X_{i}
and
C_{i}

of a component

is defined to be the genus of the corresponding component

.

A point

is called

Definition 5.1
.

A connected nodal supercurve

is

if

2g_{i}-2+\#\{\text{special points in }\tilde{X}_{i}\}>0

for every irreducible component

Definition 5.2
.

A prestable supercurve over a superscheme
T
is a proper, flat, relatively Cohen–Macaulay morphism

such that for every geometric point
\overline{t}\to T
the fiber

is a connected nodal supercurve over

.
In this case, the bosonic reduction

is automatically a family of ordinary prestable curves.

A family

of prestable supercurves is called

if for every geometric point

is a stable supercurve over

Historically, stable curves are preferred to prestable ones because stable curves have finite automorphism groups, which implies that their moduli stack is Deligne–Mumford. If one instead works with prestable curves, the resulting moduli stack generally has non-finite automorphism groups and is therefore only algebraic.
For ease of reference we will work with stable supercurves, though the arguments presented here apply equally to prestable supercurves.

Proposition 5.3
.

Stable supercurves
X/T
over a purely bosonic base scheme

For the forward direction, let

Since

Once we

5.2
Moduli of stable supercurves is an algebraic superstack

Let
\overline{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{C}}_{g}
denote the category over
\operatorname{sSch}
whose fiber over a superscheme

T
is the groupoid of genus
g
stable supercurves over
T
. The pullback
in
\overline{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{C}}_{g}
is given by fiber products, and the cartesian morphisms are
the canonical morphisms induced by these fiber products.

Proposition 5.9
.

\overline{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{C}}_{g}
is an étale superstack over
\operatorname{sSch}
.

Proof.

The statement can be proved using the same arguments as in

[
12
, Proposition 7.6]
. That proposition is
stated for stable super Riemann surfaces, but the proof applies
verbatim to stable supercurves, since a stable super Riemann surface
is a stable supercurve equipped with additional structure.

We also provide a alternative proof, more aligned with out previous arguments.
We first treat the case in which the bosonic reduction
T_{\operatorname{bos}}
has even dimension at least
1
. Since dimension is invariant under étale base change, the same holds for each
T_{i,\operatorname{bos}}
. Let
\Sigma_{i}\subset X_{i}
denote the singular locus, let
Y_{i}:=X_{i}\setminus\Sigma_{i}
be the smooth locus, let
C_{i}:=(X_{i})_{\operatorname{bos}}
, and let
U_{i}:=Y_{i,\operatorname{bos}}\subset C_{i}
be the smooth locus of
C_{i}
. Since each geometric fiber of
X_{i}/T_{i}
is a nodal supercurve and
T_{i,\operatorname{bos}}
has positive dimension, the subset
\Sigma_{i}
has codimension at least
2
in
X_{i}
. Hence, by Hartogs, morphisms and isomorphisms defined on
Y_{i}
extend uniquely to
X_{i}
.

Choose a line bundle
L_{i}
on
X_{i}
lifting
\omega_{C_{i}/T_{i}}^{\otimes n}
for some fixed
n\geq 3
. We claim that such a lift is unique up to unique isomorphism. Indeed, if
L_{i}^{\prime}
is another lift, then
L_{i}|_{Y_{i}}
and
L_{i}^{\prime}|_{Y_{i}}
are both lifts of
\omega_{C_{i}/T_{i}}^{\otimes n}|_{U_{i}}
, because the smooth loci of
X_{i}
and
C_{i}
agree after bosonic reduction. Now
U_{i}\to T_{i}
is affine, and since
Y_{i}
is a nilpotent thickening of
U_{i}
,
Y_{i}
is also affine. Choose a factorization of the nilpotent thickening
U_{i}\hookrightarrow Y_{i}
into square-zero extensions. At each step, the set of lifts of a line bundle is a torsor under
H^{1}
of the corresponding square-zero ideal sheaf, and these groups vanish because all intermediate thickenings are affine. Thus the lift of
\omega_{C_{i}/T_{i}}^{\otimes n}|_{U_{i}}
to
Y_{i}
is unique up to unique isomorphism, so
L_{i}|_{Y_{i}}\cong L_{i}^{\prime}|_{Y_{i}}
. By Hartogs, this extends uniquely to an isomorphism
L_{i}\cong L_{i}^{\prime}
on
X_{i}
. This proves the claim.

Now set
X_{ij}:=X_{i}\times_{T}T_{j}
, and similarly define
Y_{ij},U_{ij},C_{ij},L_{ij}
. Since
\alpha_{ij}:X_{ij}\xrightarrow{\sim}X_{ji}
is an isomorphism of stable supercurves, its bosonic reduction identifies
C_{ij}
with
C_{ji}
, and therefore
\alpha_{ij}^{*}L_{ji}
is again a lift of
\omega_{C_{ij}/T_{ij}}^{\otimes n}
. By the uniqueness just proved, there exists an isomorphism

\phi_{ij}:L_{ij}\xrightarrow{\sim}\alpha_{ij}^{*}L_{ji}.

We show that the
\phi_{ij}
can be chosen to satisfy the cocycle condition. On the smooth locus
Y_{ij}
the lift of
\omega_{C_{ij}/T_{ij}}^{\otimes n}|_{U_{ij}}
is unique up to unique isomorphism by the affine argument above, so the isomorphisms
\phi_{ij}|_{Y_{ij}}
are uniquely determined. In particular, on a triple overlap
Y_{ijk}
the two isomorphisms

L_{ijk}\longrightarrow\alpha_{ij}^{*}\alpha_{jk}^{*}L_{ki}

given by
\phi_{ik}|_{Y_{ijk}}
and by
\alpha_{ij}^{*}(\phi_{jk})\circ\phi_{ij}|_{Y_{ijk}}
coincide. Therefore

(\alpha_{ij}^{*}\phi_{jk})\circ\phi_{ij}\circ\phi_{ik}^{-1}

is the identity on
Y_{ijk}
. Since this is an automorphism of the line bundle
L_{ijk}
and
X_{ijk}\setminus Y_{ijk}
has codimension at least
2
, Hartogs implies that it is the identity on all of
X_{ijk}
. Thus the
\phi_{ij}
satisfy the cocycle condition on triple overlaps.

We can now repeat the argument of Proposition
3.7
, setting
\mathcal{E}_{i}=\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}_{i}
, and get a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
T
with
\operatorname{pr}_{j}^{*}\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{E}_{j}
, and a closed subscheme
X\subset\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{E})
, flat and proper over
T
, with
\operatorname{pr}_{j}^{*}X\cong X_{j}
.

The superscheme
X/T
is a stable supercurve. Indeed, it is proper, flat, and relatively Cohen–Macaulay because these properties are étale local on the base, and its geometric fibers are stable supercurves because this is also checked after étale base change. The closed singular subschemes
\Sigma_{i}\subset X_{i}
, are compatible on overlaps and thus glue to singular locus
\Sigma\subset X
. The defining conditions of the nodes can be checked on geometric fibers.

This proves descent when
T_{\operatorname{bos}}
has even dimension at least
1
. The argument for even dimension
0
is follows from
\operatorname{Isom}
being a sheaf.
∎

0\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}\to\operatorname{Def}_{X/T}(T^{\prime})\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{T}^{1}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}\to 0,

so to verify conditions (A6)–(A8) it suffices to check them for the two outer modules.

For (A7) and (A8) this follows immediately from the arguments of Propositions
3.14
and
3.15
applied to the coherent sheaves
\mathcal{T}_{X/T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{X/T}
on the proper morphism
X\to T
, since those proofs only use coherence of the deformation sheaves, and flat and properness of
X
over
T
.

For (A6) we apply the argument of Proposition
3.15
. The proof there uses that the deformation module can be written as
H^{1}(X_{0},\mathcal{F}\otimes M)^{+}
for a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X_{0}
that is flat over
T_{0}
. For stable supercurves,
\mathcal{T}_{X_{0}/T_{0}}
and
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{X_{0}/T_{0}}
are not necessarily flat over
T_{0}
, but they are coherent. This is enough to apply the same arguments, see Remark
4.7
.
∎

6
Stable super Riemann surfaces

We now recall several properties of smooth super Riemann surfaces that will be relevant for the definition of a stable super Riemann surface.

Let
(X/T,\mathcal{D})
be a
smooth
super Riemann surface. Applying
\operatorname{Ber}
to the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{D}^{-2}\to\Omega_{X/T}^{1}\to\mathcal{D}^{-1}\to 0

(28)

gives a canonical isomorphism

\omega_{X/T}:=\operatorname{Ber}(\Omega_{X/T}^{1})\cong\mathcal{D}^{-1}.

In particular, we find that the superconformal structure on
X
induces a surjective
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism
\Omega_{X/T}^{1}\to\omega_{X/T}
, and thus also a grading-preserving
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-linear derivation

\delta:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\omega_{X/T}.

A fundamental fact about super Riemann surfaces is the existence of étale local coordinates
(z,\theta)
on
X
, called
superconformal coordinates
, in which
\mathcal{D}
is locally generated by the odd vector field:

D_{\theta}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

(29)

In these superconformal coordinates
(z,\theta)
, the derivation
\delta
takes the form

\delta(f)=D_{\theta}(f)[dz|d\theta].

(30)

Conversely, a surjective
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-linear grading-preserving derivation
\delta:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\omega_{X/T}
arises from a superconformal structure on
X
if and only if the following conditions hold.

(1)

\mathcal{D}=\ker(\delta)^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{T}_{X/T}
is maximally non-integrable in the sense that the composition

\mathcal{D}\otimes\mathcal{D}\xrightarrow{[\ ,\ ]}\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\to\mathcal{T}_{X/T}/\mathcal{D}

is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear isomorphism.

(2)

Locally,

\delta(f)=D(f)[D^{2}/D]^{-1},

where
D
and
D^{2}
are local generators of
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}{}^{\otimes 2}
, respectively, and

[D^{2}/D]^{-1}:=(D^{-1}\otimes D^{2})^{-1}=D\otimes D^{-2}

is a local generator of
\omega_{X/T}
under the identification
\omega_{X/T}\cong\mathcal{D}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{-2}=\mathcal{D}^{-1}
.

If
\delta
arises from a superconformal structure, then there exist étale local coordinates
(z,\theta)
on
X
such that
\delta
is locally of the form (
30
) with
D_{\theta}
replaced by
D
.

Definition 6.1
.

A family of
stable super Riemann surfaces
over a superscheme
T
consists of a pair

(X\to T,\;\delta\!:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\omega_{X/T}),

where
X\to T
is a family of stable supercurves and
\delta
is a grading-preserving
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-linear derivation such that

(i)

the restriction of
\delta
to the smooth locus
U\subset X
is superconformal;

(ii)

for every geometric point
\overline{t}\to T
, the induced morphism

\delta^{-}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}^{-}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\omega_{X_{t}}^{-}

(31)

is an
\mathcal{O}_{C_{t}}
-linear isomorphism.

We refer to
\delta
as a
quasi superconformal structure
(qscf structure) on
X/T
. An isomorphism of stable super Riemann surfaces
X/T\to Y/T
is an isomorphism
\phi\!:X\to Y
of stable supercurves over
T
such that
\phi^{*}(\delta_{Y})=\delta_{X}
. Such an isomorphism is called a
superconformal isomorphism
.

A
quasi spin curve

(C/T,\mathcal{L},b)
over an ordinary scheme
T
, as in Definition 2.1.2 of
[
16
]
, is an ordinary family of stable curves
C/T
together with a pair
(\mathcal{L},b)
where
\mathcal{L}
is a torsion-free sheaf of rank one and degree
g-1
, and

b:\mathcal{L}{}^{\otimes 2}\to\omega_{C}

is a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules such that
b
is an isomorphism on the open subset where
\mathcal{L}
is locally free, and for each singular point
q
of
C
where
\mathcal{L}
is not locally free, the length of the cokernel of
b
at
q
is one.
These conditions on
b
are equivalent to requiring that
b
induce an isomorphism

\mathcal{L}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{L},\omega_{C}).

Proposition 6.2
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between families of stable super Riemann surfaces over an ordinary base scheme
T
and families of quasi spin curves over
T
.

Proof.

Let
(X/T,\delta)
be a stable super Riemann surface over an ordinary scheme
T
. By definition,
C=X_{\operatorname{bos}}
is an ordinary family of stable curves over
T
, and we showed in Proposition
5.3
, that
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a torsion-free sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules of rank
(0|1)
. We therefore set

\mathcal{L}:=\Pi\mathcal{J}.

We are left to show that
(X/T,\delta)
determines a morphism

b:\mathcal{L}{}^{\otimes 2}\to\omega_{C/T}

satisfying the conditions of a quasi spin structure.

The proof is from
[
9
]
.
Let
p:X\to C
denote the morphism onto
C
induced by the inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{C}\oplus\Pi L
. Then

\omega_{X/T}:=p^{!}\omega_{C/T}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\omega_{C/T}).

The assumption that
X/T
is relatively Cohen–Macaulay, implies
\omega_{X/T}
is flat over
T
, with formation compatible with base change. We have:

\displaystyle\omega_{X/T}^{+}

\displaystyle=\omega_{C/T},

\displaystyle\omega_{X/T}^{-}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{L},\omega_{C/T}).

The derivation
\delta
therefore induces morphisms

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}^{+}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{O}_{C}\to\omega_{C/T}\qquad\text{(a derivation)},

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}^{-}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{L}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{L},\omega_{C/T})\qquad\text{($\mathcal{O}_{C}$-linear)}.

Since
\Pi\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{X}{}^{-}
, the map
\tilde{\delta}^{-}
is an isomorphism on every geometric fiber. Since both sides are coherent
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules compatible with base change, it follows that
\tilde{\delta}^{-}
is an isomorphism globally. This induces the desired morphism

b:\mathcal{L}{}^{\otimes 2}\to\omega_{C/T}.

∎

6.1
Superconformal coordinate charts

Let
X/T
be a stable super Riemann surface. A
superconformal deformation
of
X/T
over a square-zero extension
T\hookrightarrow T^{\prime}
is a stable super Riemann surface
X^{\prime}/T^{\prime}
with a superconformal identification

X\cong X^{\prime}\times_{T^{\prime}}T

over
T
.

By Theorem 3.1 of
[
13
]
(following
[
9
]
), the Ramond and NS models of the two kinds of nodes on a stable supercurve admit miniversal superconformal deformations.
The base of the miniversal superconformal deformation for both models is
\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
, and the total space for the Ramond model is

\mathcal{Y}=\operatorname{Spec}k[t][x,y,\theta]/(xy-t).

For the NS model the total space is

\mathcal{Y}=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,y,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]/(xy-t^{2},x\theta_{2}-t\theta_{1},y\theta_{1}-t\theta_{2},\theta_{1}\theta_{2}).

The canonical qscf structure on
\mathcal{Y}
are described in
[
13
]
. We provide them here for reference.

•

Ramond model.
The node in
\mathcal{Y}
is the point
y
associated to the maximal ideal
(x,y,\theta)
. The complement of the node
y\in\mathcal{Y}
is the union of two branches
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, where
x
is invertible on
U_{1}
and
y
on
U_{2}
. The sheaf
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}/k[t]}
is free with basis
b
given by

b=\begin{cases}\left[\dfrac{dx}{x}\,\middle|\,d\theta\right]&\text{on }U_{1},\\[10.00002pt]
-\left[\dfrac{dy}{y}\,\middle|\,d\theta\right]&\text{on }U_{2}.\end{cases}

There is a canonical qscf structure
\delta
on
\mathcal{Y}
given by:

\delta(f)=\begin{cases}(\partial_{\theta}+\theta x\partial_{x})(f)\cdot b&\text{on }U_{1},\\
(\partial_{\theta}-\theta y\partial_{y})(f)\cdot b&\text{on }U_{2}.\end{cases}

•

NS model.
The node in
\mathcal{Y}
is the point
y
associated to the maximal ideal
(x,y,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
.
The complement of the node is again the union of two branches
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, on which
x
and
y
are respectively invertible.

The generators
\left[dx\mid d\theta_{1}\right]
and
\left[dy\mid d\theta_{2}\right]
of
\omega_{U_{1}}
and
\omega_{U_{2}}
extend to sections of
\omega_{Y/A}
(vanishing on the opposite branch), but they do not generate
\omega_{\mathcal{Y}/k[t]}
. There is an additional section

s_{0}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\theta_{1}}{x}\left[dx\mid d\theta_{1}\right]&\text{on }U_{1},\\[10.00002pt]
-\dfrac{\theta_{2}}{y}\left[dy\mid d\theta_{2}\right]&\text{on }U_{2}.\end{cases}

There is a canonical qscf structure
\delta
on
\mathcal{Y}
given by:

\delta(f)=\begin{cases}(\partial_{\theta_{1}}+\theta_{1}\partial_{x})(f)\cdot b&\text{on }U_{1},\\
(\partial_{\theta_{2}}+\theta_{2}\partial_{y})(f)\cdot b&\text{on }U_{2}.\end{cases}

The next theorem is really a corollary of the existence of miniversal superconformal deformations of the two kinds of models, the proof is entirely analogous to that of Lemma
5.8
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(X/S,\delta)
be a stable super Riemann surface, let
x\in X
be a node, and let
s\in S
be its image. Then there exists an étale neighborhood
S^{\prime}\to S
of
s
, a morphism

f:S^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Spec}k[t],

and an étale neighborhood
X^{\prime}\to X\times_{S}S^{\prime}
of
x
, and a superconformal isomorphism

X^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{Y}\times_{k[t]}S^{\prime}

where
\mathcal{Y}\to\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
is the miniversal superconformal deformation corresponding to the type of node
x
, and
\mathcal{Y}\times_{k[t]}S^{\prime}
has the induced qscf structure. In particular, by the same reasoning as Lemma
5.8
, there are no obstructions to deforming a stable super Riemann surface over a square–zero extension
S\hookrightarrow S^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We prove in Section
6.4
that the moduli of stable super Riemann surfaces is colimit preserving. This allows us to assume that the base
S
is finite type over
k
, and so we can repeat the arguments of Lemma
5.8
.

∎

6.2
Deformation theory of stable super Riemann surfaces

In this section we describe the deformation theory of stable super
Riemann surfaces. Conceptually, it is obtained from the
deformation theory of the underlying stable supercurve by imposing
a superconformal condition along the nodes.

Our main result is that this deformation theory is governed by certain
coherent
subsheaves

\mathcal{A}^{i}_{X/T}\subset\mathcal{T}^{i}_{X/T}

of the sheaves controlling the deformation theory of the underlying
stable supercurve.

The following theorem is a direct consequence of the definition of
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{X/T}
together with the
T^{i}
–formalism for deformation theory applied to these subsheaves.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
T\hookrightarrow T^{\prime}
be a square-zero extension with kernel
M
, and let

(X/T,\delta)
be a stable super Riemann surface.
Upon fixing a superconformal deformation
(X_{1}^{\prime}/T^{\prime},\delta_{1}^{\prime})
of
(X/T,\delta)
—one always exists by Proposition
6.3
— we have a short exact sequence

0\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}\to\operatorname{sDef}_{X/T}(T^{\prime})\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X/T}^{1}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}\to 0

(32)

where
\operatorname{sDef}_{X/T}(T^{\prime})
denotes the set of superconformal deformation
classes.

We explain the structure of the proof. By Proposition
5.6
, the underlying
supercurve
X/T
has unobstructed deformations and there is an
exact sequence

0\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}\to\operatorname{Def}_{X/T}(T^{\prime})\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{T}^{1}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}\to 0.

A deformation
X^{\prime}/T^{\prime}
of
X/T
is superconformal if it admits a qscf
structure extending the one on
X
. Such a structure, if it exists,
is unique, so the forgetting map

\operatorname{sDef}_{X/T}(T^{\prime})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Def}_{X/T}(T^{\prime}),\qquad(X^{\prime}/T^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})\mapsto X^{\prime}/T^{\prime}

is injective.

Composing this inclusion with the right arrow in the sequence above
gives a map

\operatorname{sDef}_{X/T}(T^{\prime})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{T}^{1}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}.

(33)

The kernel consists of superconformal deformations locally
isomorphic to the fixed deformation
(X_{1}^{\prime}/T^{\prime},\delta_{1}^{\prime})
,
and is naturally identified with

H^{1}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X/T}\otimes_{A}M)^{+}.

Thus Theorem
6.4
reduces to
constructing a subsheaf

\mathcal{A}_{X/T}^{1}\subset\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1}

whose global sections describe the image of
(
33
).

6.3
Definition of
\mathcal{A}^{1}

Recall from the supercurve deformation theory that the sheaf

\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1}
controls local deformations of the nodes of
X/T

and is supported on the singular locus
\Sigma\subset X
.
Étale locally along
\Sigma
the curve is modeled on the superconformal Ramond
and NS node charts described in the previous section. Let

Y=\operatorname{Spec}(R)\xrightarrow{\phi}\Sigma\subset X

be one such chart.

Since
\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1}
is compatible with étale localization, for such a chart we have

\Gamma(Y,\phi^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1})\cong T^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M).

The appendix computes these modules explicitly. Writing

N=M\otimes_{A}R/I,

one obtains

T^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)\cong\begin{cases}N\oplus N\theta&\text{(Ramond node)},\\
N\theta_{1}\oplus N\theta_{2}\oplus N\theta_{1}\oplus N\theta_{2}&\text{(NS node)}.\end{cases}

Thus deformations of the Ramond node are parametrized by pairs

(a,\alpha)
, while deformations of the NS node are parametrized
by
(a_{1},a_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
.

By
[
9
]
, such a deformation is superconformal precisely when

\alpha=0\qquad\text{(Ramond case)},

and

a_{1}=a_{2},\qquad\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=0\qquad\text{(NS case)}.

These conditions define kernels of linear maps.
For the Ramond node define

\pi_{2}:T^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)\to N,\qquad(a,\alpha)\mapsto\alpha

and set

A^{1}(R/A,P)=\ker(\pi_{2}).

For the NS node define

\sigma(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},a_{1},a_{2})=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},a_{1}-a_{2},a_{2}-a_{1})

and set

A^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)=\ker(\sigma).

In both cases
A^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)^{+}
parametrizes superconformal
node deformations.

Since
Y
is affine these modules define coherent sheaves

\mathcal{A}_{Y/T}^{1}
satisfying

\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y/T}^{1})=A^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M).

Moreover
R/I=\Gamma(Y,\phi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma})
, so the maps above define

\mathcal{O}_{Y}
–linear morphisms

\phi^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1}\longrightarrow M\otimes_{A}\phi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}

whose kernels are precisely
\mathcal{A}_{Y/T}^{1}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

The subsheaves
\{\mathcal{A}_{Y/T}^{1}\subset\phi^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1}\}

agree on overlaps and therefore glue to a coherent subsheaf

\mathcal{A}_{X/T}^{1}\subset\mathcal{T}_{X/T}^{1}.

Proof.

The étale charts intersect
\Sigma
in a single point, so their
only overlaps are self–overlaps. Since the defining morphisms
have canonically identified sources and targets, their pullbacks
along the two projections coincide, and the kernels therefore
agree on overlaps.
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

The definition of
\mathcal{A}_{X/T}^{1}
is independent of the choice of
superconformal coordinates.

Proof.

Let

\phi\colon Y=\operatorname{Spec}(R)\to\Sigma\subset X\qquad\text{and}\qquad\phi^{\prime}\colon Y=\operatorname{Spec}(R)\to\Sigma\subset X

be two superconformal coordinate charts of the same type, so that

\phi^{\prime}=\phi\circ\rho

for some superconformal automorphism

\rho\colon(Y,\delta)\to(Y,\delta)

over
T=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
. We must show that the two submodules of

T^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)
obtained from the charts
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
coincide.

The automorphism
\rho
lifts to an action of
T^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)
. Indeed, let

Y^{\prime}/A^{\prime}
be a superconformal deformation of
Y
with superconformal identification denoted by

i\colon Y\xrightarrow{\sim}Y^{\prime}\times_{A^{\prime}}A.

Define
\rho\cdot Y^{\prime}
to be the same
A^{\prime}
–superscheme
Y^{\prime}
, but with superconformal identification twisted by
\rho
:

i\circ\rho\colon Y\xrightarrow{\sim}Y^{\prime}\times_{A^{\prime}}A.

This gives an action of
\operatorname{sAut}(Y,\delta)
on deformation classes of

Y
over
A^{\prime}
. Using
Y^{\prime}
as a base point, we have a canonical identification of deformation
classes of
X/T
, forgetting the qscf structure, with
T^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)^{+}
. The
\rho
action clearly descends to an
R{}^{+}
-linear action on
T^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)^{+}
by superconformal automorphisms
\rho\in\operatorname{sAut}(Y,\delta)
.

To prove the lemma, it suffices to show that
A^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)^{+}\subset T^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)^{+}

is stable under this action. Let
[Y^{\prime}]\in A^{1}(R/A,R\otimes_{A}M)^{+}
, so by
definition
Y^{\prime}
admits a qscf structure
\delta^{\prime}
extending the given
qscf structure
\delta
on
Y
. We claim that
\rho\cdot Y^{\prime}
is again
superconformal. Indeed, we equip the same total space
Y^{\prime}
with the
same qscf structure
\delta^{\prime}
. With respect to the new identification
of the special fiber, its reduction is

(i\circ\rho)^{*}(\delta^{\prime}|_{Y^{\prime}\times_{A^{\prime}}A})=\rho^{*}\bigl(i^{*}(\delta^{\prime}|_{Y^{\prime}\times_{A^{\prime}}A})\bigr)=\rho^{*}(\delta)=\delta,

because
\rho
is superconformal. Thus,
(Y^{\prime},i\circ\rho)
is a superconformal deformation of
Y
.
∎

6.4
Moduli of stable super Riemann surfaces is an algebraic superstack

Let
\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g}}
be the category over
\operatorname{sSch}
whose fiber over
T\in\operatorname{sSch}
is the groupoid
\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g}}(T)
whose objects are families
(X\to T,\delta)
of stable super Riemann surfaces and whose morphisms are superconformal isomorphisms.
The pullback in
\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g}}
of an object
(X/T,\delta)
by a morphism
S\to T
in
\operatorname{sSch}
is the pair

(X_{S},\delta_{S}):=(X\times_{T}S,\ p^{*}\delta:p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{S}}\to p^{*}\omega_{X/T}\cong\omega_{X_{S}/S})

where
p:X_{S}\to X
is the canonical morphism, and both isomorphisms are canonical.
We claim
\delta_{S}
is quasi superconformal: For (i): if
U
is the smooth locus in
X
, then
U_{S}
is smooth in
X_{S}
. Let
\mathcal{D}
denotes the superconformal structure on
U
determined by
\delta
. Then by definition the derivation
\tilde{\delta}
associated to
\delta
fits into the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{D}^{-1}\to\Omega_{U/T}^{1}\xrightarrow{\tilde{\delta}|_{U}}\mathcal{D}^{-1}\cong\omega_{X/T}\to 0

Since
\mathcal{D}
pulls back to a superconformal structure on
U_{S}
, the sequence remains exact after pullback:

0\to p^{*}\mathcal{D}^{-1}\to\Omega_{U_{S}/S}^{1}\xrightarrow{p^{*}\tilde{\delta}|_{U}}p^{*}\mathcal{D}^{-1}\cong\omega_{X_{S}/S}|_{U_{S}}\to 0

where
\delta_{S}
is the derivation associated to
p^{*}\tilde{\delta}|_{U}
.

For (ii): for each geometric point
\overline{s}\to S
the corresponding geometric fiber of
X_{S}
is canonically identified with the geometric fiber of
X
over the point
\overline{s}\to S\to T
and
\delta_{S}
and
\delta
restrict to the same derivation under this identification.

Proposition 6.7
.

\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g}}
is an étale superstack.

Proof.

Descent for isomorphisms.
Let
X,Y
in
\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g}}(U)
, and let
\{U_{i}\to U\}
be an étale covering, and let
\alpha_{i}:\operatorname{pr}_{i}^{*}X\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{pr}_{i}^{*}Y
be superconformal isomorphisms agreeing on overlaps. Morphisms of superschemes satisfy etale descent, and so there exists an isomorphism
\alpha:X\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}Y
of superschemes over
U
such that
\operatorname{pr}_{i}^{*}\alpha=\alpha_{i}
, and since
\alpha_{i}
is superconformal the derivation

\alpha^{*}(\delta_{Y})-\delta_{X}:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\omega_{X/U}

vanishes along the étale cover
\operatorname{pr}_{i}^{*}X\to X
and thus vanishes globally, implying
\alpha^{*}(\delta_{Y})=\delta_{X}
.

Descent for objects.
We are given a collection of stable super Riemann surfaces
\{(X_{i}/U_{i},\delta_{i})\}
in
\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g}}(U_{i})
together with superconformal isomorphisms
\alpha_{ij}:\operatorname{pr}_{ij}^{*}X_{i}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{pr}_{ji}^{*}X_{j}
satisfying the cocycle condition on triple overlaps.

By Proposition
5.9
, we know that the
X_{i}/U_{i}
glue to a unique stable supercurve
X/U
such that
\operatorname{pr}_{i}^{*}X\cong X_{i}
, so we are left to show that
X
admits a quasi superconformal structure
\delta
satisfying
\operatorname{pr}_{i}^{*}\delta=\delta_{i}
. Since the
\alpha_{ij}
are superconformal,

\operatorname{pr}_{ij}^{*}\delta_{i}=\alpha_{ij}^{*}\operatorname{pr}_{ji}^{*}(\delta_{j}),

and so the
\delta_{i}
agree on all overlaps and thus glue to a global derivation,
\delta:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\omega_{X/U}
such that
\operatorname{pr}_{i}^{*}\delta=\delta_{i}
.

We are left to check the two conditions for a derivation
\delta
to be a qscf structure. For condition (i): First note that smoothness is an étale local property, so the smooth loci
Y_{i}\subset X_{i}
glue to the smooth locus
Y\subset X
, and
\delta|_{Y}
pulls back to
\delta_{i}|_{Y_{i}}
for all
i
. The
\delta_{i}|_{Y_{i}}
come from a superconformal structure
\mathcal{D}_{i}
defined on
Y_{i}\subset X
. The isomorphisms

The next lemma is a statement about ordinary algebraic geometry. We include it for completeness.

Lemma 7.3

We may assume

\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}

By Grauert’s theorem if

\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}

C\to\mathbb{P}_{T}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{L})

.
