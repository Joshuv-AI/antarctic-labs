Surgical excision for giant condyloma acuminatum (Buschke–Löwenstein tumor): A case report
Authors: Akari Hirakawa, Sayuri Miyoshi, Ryusuke Murakami, Itaru Tsuge, Ryota Nakagawa, Masahito Hoki, Yukiko Okada, Yosuke Kawamura, Takuto Shimamura, Takuma Oosuga, Taito Miyamoto, Rin Mizuno, Masumi Sunada, Mana Taki, Koji Yamanoi, Masaki Mandai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Giant condyloma acuminatum (Buschke–Löwenstein tumor; BLT) is a rare HPV-associated disease characterized by progressive proliferation, high recurrence rates (60–70%), and potential for malignant transformation to squamous cell carcinoma. Although histologically benign, aggressive local behavior and involvement of functionally critical anatomical structures present substantial therapeutic challenges. This report describes the successful function-preserving surgical management of extensive treatment-refractory BLT. A 69-year-old woman with a history of malignant lymphoma and diabetes mellitus developed vulvar condyloma that was resistant to sequential imiquimod therapy, cryotherapy, and laser ablation. The lesion progressively enlarged to 15 × 9.5 × 2.5 cm, encompassing the entire vulva and perianal area. Clinical examination and imaging confirmed the absence of urethral and rectal mucosal invasion. Preoperative imiquimod was administered to the urethral meatus, vaginal introitus, and perianal area for 4 weeks to control microscopic disease at functionally critical sites. Surgical resection prioritized function preservation: ≥1 cm margin at the periphery, while mucosal boundaries were approached closely at critical sites. Residual rough mucosal areas underwent bipolar cauterization. Primary closure was achieved without the need for flap reconstruction. Mild postoperative wound dehiscence without infection resolved with conservative management, permitting secondary healing. Five months postoperatively, complete epithelialization was achieved with no recurrence, preserved urinary and bowel function, and the absence of vaginal symptoms. This case demonstrates that combining preoperative imiquimod, function-prioritizing surgical margins, local ablation, and tolerance of secondary healing can achieve oncologic control while maintaining quality of life in extensive BLT. Long-term surveillance remains essential.

Introduction

Condyloma acuminatum is a verrucous lesion caused by low-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) types 6 and 11 [1]. While most cases remain localized and amenable to conservative management, rare progressive cases develop into giant lesions that extend across the vulvar, perineal, and perianal regions. The giant condylomas present substantial clinical challenges owing to their size, anatomical location, and biological behavior [2]. Although histologically benign, these lesions demonstrate aggressive local growth with recurrence rates of 60–70% and carry the risk of malignant transformation to squamous cell carcinoma [3]. The subset exhibiting locally destructive characteristics was first described by Buschke and Löwenstein in 1925 and termed Buschke–Löwenstein tumor (BLT) [4].

Wide surgical excision with adequate margins remains the first-line treatment for giant condyloma [5], [6]. However, lesions involving the urethral meatus, vaginal wall, or anal canal present substantial therapeutic dilemmas: achieving adequate surgical margins risks considerable functional compromise, including potential urinary or fecal diversion [7], [8]. Reports specifically addressing conservative surgical approaches combined with local ablative techniques and their short-term outcomes remain limited, particularly regarding female patients.

This report presents an extensive vulvar BLT development after malignant lymphoma treatment in a patient with diabetes who was refractory to multiple therapies. Through a multidisciplinary approach incorporating preoperative imiquimod therapy, function-preserving resection margins, local ablation, and secondary healing acceptance, favorable short-term outcomes were achieved.

Case Presentation

A 69-year-old woman (gravida 2, para 2, menopausal age 52 years) with a history of malignant lymphoma (chemotherapy completed at age 66 years 11 months, currently in remission) and diabetes mellitus developed vulvar pruritus and discomfort at age 67 years 5 months following new HPV exposure. A biopsy confirmed condyloma acuminatum. She had not received HPV vaccination. Despite treatments with topical imiquimod (Beselna®), liquid nitrogen therapy, and CO₂ laser therapy, the treatment-refractory lesion enlarged. By the age of 69, extensive involvement of the labia minora, labia majora, clitoris, and perianal area prompted referral.

Examination revealed a body mass index of 15.8 with normal nutritional parameters (serum protein 6.9 g/dL, albumin 4.5 g/dL). A papillary tumor measuring 15 × 9.5 × 2.5 cm was observed, extending throughout the vulva to the perianal area (Fig. 1A). MRI showed no deep invasion. Cystoscopy revealed a papillary tumor near the external urethral meatus without bladder involvement, and urine cytology revealed koilocytes, suggesting an HPV infection. Colorectal examination confirmed the absence of invasion beyond the dentate line. Clinical and pathological findings confirmed the diagnosis of Buschke–Löwenstein tumor. Given the resistance to treatment and progressive enlargement, surgical excision was performed. As the lesion spared the urethra and rectum with preservation of the urethral meatus and anus deemed feasible, preoperative imiquimod cream was applied to the external urethral meatus, vaginal introitus, and perianal canal for four weeks to control microscopic disease at functionally critical sites (Fig. 1B). Objective tumor reduction assessment proved difficult, given the massive size of the tumor.

Resection margins incorporated ≥1 cm peripherally, prioritizing function preservation medially to spare the external urethral meatus, vaginal wall, and anus. Near the urethral meatus and vaginal introitus, the resection was extended to the mucosal boundary to maintain function (Fig. 1C). The initially planned bilateral gracilis flap reconstruction proved unnecessary as skin advancement permitted primary closure. During closure, the perineal body was reinforced with the vaginal introital and anal-side mucosa sutured vertically to prevent stenotic scar contractures.

Post-resection, the rough mucosal surfaces around the vaginal introitus and urethral meatus were subjected to bipolar cauterization to address potential microscopic residual disease. Microscopic tumor positivity at the medial margins was anticipated, given the function-preservation priority. Intraoperative blood loss totaled 750 mL, with hemoglobin declining from a preoperative value of 9.3 g/dL to 6.0 g/dL intraoperatively, necessitating transfusion of four units of packed red blood cells.

Histologically, the stratified squamous epithelium showed outward papillary proliferation with superficial koilocytosis (Fig. 1D). RNAscope™ in situ hybridization confirmed low-risk HPV positivity. The absence of significant cellular atypia or invasive proliferation established the final diagnosis of giant condyloma acuminatum (Buschke–Löwenstein tumor) (Fig. 1E).

Postoperatively, the mild wound dehiscence without infection responded to conservative management with favorable granulation. Postoperative topical imiquimod was omitted because of the dehiscence. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 30. Nearly complete epithelialization was achieved at two months (Fig. 2). No local recurrence was confirmed at five-month follow-up. No urethral stenosis or voiding dysfunction occurred, and anal function was preserved without stenosis. Discussion

This case exemplifies the effective multidisciplinary management of extensive treatment-refractory vulvar BLT using a comprehensive approach combining preoperative imiquimod therapy, function-prioritizing surgical resection, local ablation techniques, and acceptance of secondary healing. Five months postoperatively, the patient remained recurrence-free with preserved organ function and an excellent quality of life.

BLT typically emerges in immunocompromised states and exhibits aggressive local behavior with high recurrence rates and an inherent risk of malignant transformation. A focused narrative review of female BLT surgical treatments reported between 2014 and 2024 was conducted. Among the 10 surgically managed cases identified, the mean patient age at onset was relatively young at 30.3 years, with substantial proportions demonstrating immunocompromised backgrounds including HIV infection, autoimmune disease states, pregnancy-associated immunomodulation, or diabetes mellitus (Table 1) [[3], [7], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]]. The patient's malignant lymphoma was in documented complete remission at the time of BLT diagnosis, suggesting that concurrent diabetes mellitus and late-age HPV exposure likely contributed significantly to tumor development. Histopathologically, BLT characteristically demonstrates papillary proliferation of well-differentiated stratified squamous epithelium, prominent superficial koilocytosis indicating HPV cytopathic effect, marked acanthosis with a thickened spinous layer, and frequently exhibits a pushing pattern of deep tissue invasion rather than infiltrative growth [14]. The present case predominantly showed extensive superficial tumor extension without obvious deep pushing invasion, though RNAscope™ molecular analysis confirmed robust active replication of low-risk HPV, supporting characteristic BLT molecular pathology. The molecular mechanism involves HPV E6 and E7 viral oncoproteins, which cause p53 tumor suppressor dysfunction and drive abnormal epithelial cell proliferation [14].

Surgical excision with adequate margins remains the first-line treatment for BLT [15]. When conservative therapeutic approaches prove ineffective, as in this case, timely surgical intervention becomes imperative to prevent progressive anatomical disruption and to mitigate the risk of malignant transformation [7]. Standard oncologic principles recommend achieving surgical margins of ≥1 cm to minimize recurrence risk [16], yet achieving such margins near the vulva, urethral meatus, vaginal introitus, and anal canal directly conflicts with functional preservation. Preoperative imiquimod therapy was introduced with concentrated application to the mucosal boundary regions, targeting macroscopically undetectable microscopic disease extensions. Although massive tumor dimensions precluded reliable objective assessment of tumor reduction, the intraoperative approach of resecting the mucosal boundaries followed by bipolar electrocoagulation provided multimodal addressing of potential microscopic residual disease. The documented absence of disease recurrence at five months provides encouraging preliminary evidence, although a longer follow-up is necessary. The indication for colostomy exists when the tumor invades the anal sphincter complex or mucosa proximal to the dentate line [7], [17]. Successful avoidance of colostomy greatly benefits quality of life.

The reconstruction strategy considerations included high BLT recurrence rates and a probable microscopic positive margin status, favoring simple primary closure over complex flap reconstruction. The vulvar perianal region represents a contaminated surgical field with an elevated risk of wound dehiscence, particularly under tension closure. The patient developed postoperative wound dehiscence without infectious complications and achieved excellent epithelialization through managed secondary intention healing. Secondary healing management in immunosuppressed populations, when not accompanied by an infection, is a reasonable, effective, and safe option. An additional theoretical consideration involves HPV biology. Viral proliferation requires intact normal stratified squamous epithelium as a cellular substrate, suggesting that fibrous scar tissue formed during secondary healing may function as a structural barrier that mechanically suppresses subsequent HPV re-proliferation [5].

However, the limitations of this study warrant further investigation. Despite BLT representing a disease characterized by high long-term recurrence rates, the 5-month postoperative follow-up proved inadequate for a comprehensive recurrence risk evaluation. Additionally, an objective assessment of preoperative imiquimod therapy tumor reduction was precluded by the massive, irregular lesion configuration. Rigorous long-term surveillance, including the systematic evaluation of HPV-related lesions at other sites, remains essential.

For extensive conservative treatment-refractory BLT, multidisciplinary therapeutic approaches combining preoperative imiquimod therapy, function-prioritizing surgical resection, local ablative techniques, and the acceptance of secondary healing can achieve curative resection while maintaining organ function and the patient's quality of life. Female BLT frequently occurs in immunocompromised patients, necessitating individualized treatment planning and flexible adaptive management strategies.
