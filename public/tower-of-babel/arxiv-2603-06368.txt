One-sided large deviations for the ground-state energy of spin glasses
Hong-Bin Chen, Alice Guionnet, Justin Ko, Bertrand Lacroix-A-Chez-Toine, Jean-Christophe Mourrat

Abstract
. We describe the large deviations above its typical value of the maximal energy of a spin glass with ± 1 \pm 1 spins. Thanks to the relatively explicit description of the rate function we identify, we then show that the latter is asymptotically quadratic near its minimum if and only if an external magnetic field is present. The proof starts from a Parisi-type formula for the fractional moments of the partition function, which we then leverage to obtain the limit of the Laplace transform of the maximum energy. Using convex-duality arguments, we then rewrite this Laplace transform as a supremum over martingales, and thereby deduce the large-deviation principle with explicit rate function.



1.
Introduction and main results

Let
(\beta_{p})_{p\geqslant 2}
be a sequence of nonnegative real numbers such that the function
\xi(r):=\sum_{p\geqslant 2}\beta_{p}^{2}r^{p}
is finite for every
r\in\mathbb{R}
. For every integer
N\geqslant 1
, we let
(H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma))_{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be the centered Gaussian field with covariance such that, for every
\sigma,\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma)H^{\prime}_{N}(\tau)]=N\xi\left(\frac{\sigma\cdot\tau}{N}\right).

For a fixed
h\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider

(1.2)

H_{N}(\sigma):=H_{N}^{\prime}(\sigma)+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}.

In order to minimize the number of minus signs, we define the “ground state” energy
L_{N}
as the maximum of
H_{N}
over
\{\pm 1\}^{N}
, normalized by
N^{-1}
, that is,

(1.3)

L_{N}:=\max_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}\frac{H_{N}(\sigma)}{N}.

Our main result is an upper large-deviation principle for
L_{N}
, with a relatively explicit rate function. We also show that this rate function is quadratic near its minimum if and only if
h\neq 0
.

As a preliminary step, we obtain a representation of the large-
N
limit of
L_{N}
that takes the form of a supremum, in the spirit of the “un-inverted” formulas introduced in
[
41
]
. We denote this limit by

\mathsf{gs}:=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[L_{N}]=\lim_{N\to+\infty}L_{N}\hskip 20.00003pt(\text{a.s.}).

We let
\mathscr{P}=(\Omega,(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]},\mathbf{P})
be a filtered probability space with complete
\sigma
-algebras (that is, for every
t\in[0,1]
, the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{t}
contains every subset of any null-measure set). We assume that the probability space
\mathscr{P}
is sufficiently rich that one can define a Brownian motion
(W_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
over
\mathscr{P}
. We denote by
\mathbf{E}
the expectation associated with
\mathbf{P}
, and by
\mathbf{Mart}
the space of bounded martingales over
\mathscr{P}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Un-inverted formula for the ground-state energy)
.

We have

(1.4)

\mathsf{gs}=\sup\bigg\{\mathbf{E}\left[h\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right]\ \Big|\ \alpha\in\mathbf{Mart},\ |\alpha_{1}|\leqslant 1,\text{ and }\\
\forall t\in[0,1],\ \int_{t}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(s)(\mathbf{E}[\alpha_{s}^{2}]-s)\,\mathrm{d}s\geqslant 0\bigg\}.

Moreover, the supremum in (
1.4
) is achieved at a unique
\alpha\in\mathbf{Mart}
.

We now state the large-deviation principle for deviations of
L_{N}
above its typical value; we also refer to Theorem
5.1
for a more general statement. For every
r>\mathsf{gs}
, we set

(1.5)

\Lambda^{*}(r)=\inf\Bigg\{\frac{\left(r-\mathbf{E}\left[h\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right]\right)^{2}}{2\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\alpha^{2}_{t}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t}\ \bigg|\ \ \\
\alpha\in\mathbf{Mart},\ |\alpha_{1}|\leqslant 1,\text{ and }\forall t\in[0,1],\ \int_{t}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(s)(\mathbf{E}[\alpha_{s}^{2}]-s)\,\mathrm{d}s\geqslant 0\bigg\}.

We also set
\Lambda^{*}(\mathsf{gs})=0
. Later in the paper, we will define
\Lambda
to be the limit Laplace transform of
L_{N}
, and show that the convex dual of
\Lambda
is given by the expression in (
1.5
), hence the notation
\Lambda^{*}
for this quantity.

Theorem 1.2

(Large deviations for the ground-state energy)
.

For every
r\geqslant\mathsf{gs}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}-\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left[L_{N}\geqslant r\right]=\Lambda^{*}(r).

Thanks to the relatively explicit description of the large-deviation rate function, we can obtain some information on its asymptotic behavior near its minimum. At a heuristic level, this information should be related to the fluctuations of the ground-state energy, as discussed in more details below.

Theorem 1.3

(quadratic/non-quadratic behavior near the minimum)
.

If
h\neq 0
, then there exists a constant
C\in(0,+\infty)
such that for every
r\geqslant\mathsf{gs}
,

C^{-1}(r-\mathsf{gs})^{2}\leqslant\Lambda^{*}(r)\leqslant C(r-\mathsf{gs})^{2}.

Conversely, if
h=0
, then

\lim_{r\to\mathsf{gs}^{+}}\frac{\Lambda^{*}(r)}{(r-\mathsf{gs})^{2}}=+\infty.

In order to prove these results, we first derive a Parisi-type formula for the fractional moments of the partition function of the model. In other words, for every
s\in(0,1)
, we give a variational formula in the form of an infimum for the large-
N
limit of

(1.6)

\frac{1}{sN}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}\right)^{s}\right].

This is Theorem
2.1
, whose proof occupies Section
2
. In Section
3
, we obtain the asymptotic behavior of the Laplace transform of
NL_{N}
. The starting point is the observation that, for every
s>0
,

(1.7)

\Lambda(s):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{sNL_{N}}\right]=\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}e^{\beta H_{N}(\sigma)}\right)^{\frac{s}{\beta}}\right].

We use this together with the result of the previous section to obtain a variational formula for the limit of this Laplace transform.

Once this is done, we would only need to assert the
C^{1}
regularity of
\Lambda
in order to be able to state an abstract upper large-deviation principle for
L_{N}
, with a rate function that is given by the convex dual of
\Lambda
. However, this representation of the large-deviation rate function would be rather inexplicit. Instead, in Section
4
we use convex-duality arguments to obtain a more direct “un-inverted” representation of
\Lambda

5
to show that
\Lambda
is a

function and compute an explicit representation of its dual, and we thereby deduce a general upper large-deviation principle in Theorem

1.2

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is along similar lines as those of Theorem
1.2
, as it formally amounts to taking

4.2
. In Section

1.3
by showing that, for

(1.8)

h=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{if and only if}\hskip 20.00003pt\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\alpha_{t}^{2}\right]-t\right)\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

2.
Fractional moments of the partition function

Throughout this section, we consider the following generalization of the Hamiltonian (
1.2
) with a random external field:

(2.1)

H_{N}(\sigma)=H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}\sigma_{i},

where
H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma)
is given as in (
1.1
) and
(h_{i})
is a collection of i.i.d. random variables satisfying
\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda|h_{i}|}<\infty
for all
\lambda\geqslant 0
. Notice that if
h_{1}
is a fixed constant, then (
2.1
) is equivalent to (
1.2
).

We denote the uniform measure on
\{-1,1\}
by
\mu
, and set
\mu_{N}:=\mu^{\otimes N}
.
We define the partition function
Z_{N}
associated with the Hamiltonian in (
2.1
) by

(2.2)

\displaystyle Z_{N}:=\int e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{N}(\sigma).

Theorem 2.1

(Fractional moments of
Z_{N}
)
.

For every
s\in(0,1)
, we have

(2.3)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{sN}\log\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{N}^{s}\right]=\inf_{\zeta\in\Pr_{s}[0,1]}\bigg(\frac{1}{s}\log\mathbb{E}_{h_{1}}\left[e^{s\Phi_{\zeta}(0,h_{1})}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}t\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\zeta([0,t])\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg),

where
\Phi_{\zeta}
solves

(2.4)

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}\Phi_{\zeta}=\frac{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi_{\zeta}+\zeta([0,t])(\partial_{x}\Phi_{\zeta})^{2}),&\quad\text{for }(t,x)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}\\
\Phi_{\zeta}(1,x)=\log\int e^{x\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\sigma),&\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}\,.

and where
\Pr_{s}[0,1]
is the space of probability measures
\zeta
on
[0,1]
such that
\zeta(\{0\})\geqslant s
. Moreover, the infimum in (
2.3
) is achieved at a unique
\zeta\in\Pr_{s}[0,1]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Formally taking
s=0
in (
2.3
), we recover the Parisi formula for the limit free energy, that is,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\log Z_{N}\right]=\inf_{\zeta\in\Pr[0,1]}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi_{\zeta}(0,h)\right]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}t\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\zeta([0,t])\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg).

As was already mentioned, special cases of Theorem
2.1
were already obtained in
[
56
]
and in
[
22
]
. In this section, we provide a direct proof of this result, much in the spirit of
[

]

The key observation is that the fractional moments can be accessed by coupling the Hamiltonian with a Poisson–Dirichlet process. Let
(H_{N}(\cdot,n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be independent copies of
H_{N}(\cdot)
, and
(h_{i}^{(n)})_{i,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be independent copies of

.
We define

Z_{N}(n)=\int e^{H_{N}(\sigma,n)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{N}(\sigma)=\int e^{H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma,n)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}^{(n)}\sigma_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{N}(\sigma)

to be the partition function associated with independent copies

of

and

. Let

It remains to compute the limit of

(2.5)

\frac{1}{sN}\log\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{N}^{s}\right]=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\sum_{n}v_{n}Z_{N}(n)\right].

2.1.
Upper bound on the free energy

This follows from the classical Guerra’s interpolation argument. We want to find an upper bound of (

The proof follows from Guerra’s interpolation argument

\xi^{\prime}(q_{\widetilde{\alpha}^{1}\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}^{2}})

\widetilde{\alpha}

and

and

H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma,\alpha,n,t)=\sqrt{t}H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma,n)+\sqrt{1-t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}(\alpha,n)\sigma_{i}+\sqrt{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_{i}(\alpha,n)

We define the interpolating free energy

\phi(t)=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha}\int e^{H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma,\alpha,n,t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}^{(n)}\sigma_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{N}(\sigma)v_{n\alpha}

By Gaussian integration by parts, we have that

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\xi(R_{12}\mathbf{1}(n^{1}=n^{2}))-R_{12}\xi^{\prime}(\widetilde{Q}_{12})+\theta(\widetilde{Q}_{12})\right\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{1}(n^{1}=n^{2})(\xi(R_{1,2})-\xi(Q_{1,2})-\xi^{\prime}(Q_{12})(R_{1,2}-Q_{1,2}))\rangle_{t}

is negative for all

If

under the perturbed Gibbs measure tends to zero

In either case, we can integrate

to conclude that

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha}\int e^{H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma,n)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}h^{(n)}_{i}\sigma_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\sigma)v_{n\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha}\int e^{Z(\alpha,n)\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{N}h^{(n)}_{i}\sigma_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\sigma)v_{n\alpha}-\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha}e^{Y(\alpha,n)}\,v_{n\alpha}

The right hand side can also be computed explicitly and follows from the recursive formulation of averages with respect to the Poisson–Dirichlet cascades
[

X_{r}=\log\int e^{\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime}(q_{k})-\xi^{\prime}(q_{k-1})}z_{k}\sigma+h_{1}\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\sigma)

where the

independent from

We define for

Thus,

Finally, we arrive at the formula

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha}\int e^{Z(\alpha,n)\sigma+h_{1}^{(n)}\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\sigma)v_{n\alpha}\right]=\frac{1}{s}\log\mathbb{E}_{h_{1}}e^{s\Phi_{\zeta}(0,h_{1})}.

Similarly, the recursion implies that

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha}e^{Y(\alpha,n)}\,v_{n\alpha}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant r-1}\zeta_{k}(\theta(q_{k+1})-\theta(q_{k})).

Combining (

We now extend to the case when

[

We now prove that the

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\widetilde{\alpha}=(n,\alpha)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
v_{n\alpha}
denote the weights of the Poisson–Dirichlet cascades corresponding to some discrete probability measure
\zeta
such that
\zeta(\{0\})=s
. Similarly, let
v_{n}
denote the weights of a Poisson–Dirichlet process with parameter
s
. For any sequence of random variables
(F(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that is independent of
(v_{n\alpha})
and
(v_{n})
, we have that

\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha}F(n)v_{n\alpha}=\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}F(n)v_{n}.

Proof.

This proof follows from the characterization of the Poisson–Dirichlet cascades by the Ghirlanda–Guerra identities
[
26
, Chapter 5.6]
. Consider i.i.d.
\widetilde{\alpha}^{\ell}=(n^{\ell},\alpha^{\ell})
sampled according to the weights
v_{n\alpha}
. By construction, the corresponding overlap array
\widetilde{R}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=\widetilde{\alpha}^{\ell}\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}^{\ell^{\prime}}
satisfies the Ghirlanda–Guerra identities
[
26
, Theorem 5.28]
or
[
46
, Theorem 2.10]
: for any
n\geqslant 1
, bounded and measurable function
f
of the overlaps
\widetilde{R}^{\leqslant n}=(\widetilde{R}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}})_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\leqslant n}
and any bounded measurable function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}\langle f(\widetilde{R}^{\leqslant n})\psi(\widetilde{R}_{1,n+1})\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\langle f(\widetilde{R}^{\leqslant n})\rangle\mathbb{E}\langle\psi(\widetilde{R}_{1,n+1})\rangle+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=2}^{n}\mathbb{E}\langle f(\widetilde{R}^{\leqslant n})\psi(\widetilde{R}_{1,\ell})\rangle.

We now consider the distribution of the marginal overlaps
S_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=\mathbf{1}(n^{\ell}=n^{\ell^{\prime}})
. It follows that the
S
overlaps inherit the Ghirlanda–Guerra identities satisfied by the full overlaps
\widetilde{R}
, by taking
f(S^{\leqslant n})=f(\mathbf{1}(\widetilde{R}^{\leqslant n}\geqslant 0))
and
\psi(S_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}})=\psi(\mathbf{1}(\widetilde{R}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\geqslant 0))
, for some functions
f
and
\psi
. Furthermore, we have that the overlap array
S_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}
takes values
0
or
1
, and

\mathbb{E}\langle S_{1,2}=0\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{1}(n^{\ell}\neq n^{\ell^{\prime}})\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{1}(\widetilde{R}_{1,2}=0)\rangle=s

and
\mathbb{E}\langle S_{1,2}=1\rangle=1-\mathbb{E}\langle S_{1,2}=0\rangle=1-s
. Since the overlaps
S
takes values
0
or
1
, and they satisfy the Ghirlanda–Guerra identities, it must be the overlaps corresponding to samples according to a Poisson–Dirichlet point process with parameter
s
, see
[
26
, Exercise 5.10]
or
[
46
, Theorem 2.8]
.

In particular, if
F(n)
is a function that only depends on the first coordinate of
\widetilde{\alpha}=(n,\alpha)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
independent of
v_{\widetilde{\alpha}}
, then the average of
\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha}F(n)v_{n\alpha}
only depends on paths in the first level corresponding to
n

[
26
, Theorem 5.25]
. Since the
n
level is determined by a Poisson–Dirichlet process with parameter
s
, its average is equivalent to
\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}F(n)v_{n}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The validity of (

) can also be observed from the recursive representation of the Poisson–Dirichlet cascades
[

, Theorem 2.9]

where the

are independent uniform random variables encoding the randomness at each level of the RPC. The random variable

Since the right hand side of

of (

. These observations imply that both the left and right hand side of (

Yet another proof of (

[

2.2.
Lower bound on the free energy

We now prove the matching lower bound.

Lemma 2.6
.

For independent copies
H_{N}(\sigma,n)
of
H_{N}(\sigma)
defined in
(
2.1
),

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\log\sum_{n}\int e^{H_{N}(\sigma,n)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{N}(\sigma)v_{n}

\displaystyle\geqslant\inf_{\zeta\in\Pr_{s}[0,1]}\bigg(\frac{1}{s}\log\mathbb{E}_{h_{1}}\left[e^{s\Phi_{\zeta}(0,h_{1})}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}t\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\zeta([0,t])\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg).

Proof.

The proof of the lower bound is mostly identical to the usual proof of the lower bound for the mixed
p
-spin models in
[
46
, Chapter 3]
. The main challenge is that we have to justify why the lower bound can be written in terms of the infimum over a single order parameter characterized by the Poisson–Dirichlet cascades. To simplify the presentation, we will focus on the key modifications and explain where synchronization
[
47
]
and the structure of the covariance of
H_{N}(\sigma,n)
plays its critical role.

The starting point is the Aizenman–Sims–Starr scheme
[
1
]
. We define the cavity fields
Z(\sigma,n)
and
Y(\sigma,n)
to be Gaussian processes indexed by points in
\Sigma_{N}\times\mathbb{N}
with covariance

\mathbb{E}\left[Z(\sigma^{1},n^{1})Z(\sigma^{2},n^{2})\right]=\xi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\sigma^{1}\cdot\sigma^{2}}{N}\mathbf{1}(n^{1}=n^{2})\right)=\xi^{\prime}(R_{12}S_{12})

and

\mathbb{E}\left[Y(\sigma^{1},n^{1})Y(\sigma^{2},n^{2})\right]=\theta\left(\frac{\sigma^{1}\cdot\sigma^{2}}{N}\mathbf{1}(n^{1}=n^{2})\right)=\theta(R_{12}S_{12}).

We use the notation
R_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\frac{\sigma^{\ell}\cdot\sigma^{\ell^{\prime}}}{N}
and
S_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\mathbf{1}(n^{\ell}=n^{\ell^{\prime}})
to denote the two overlaps. The appearance of the
S
overlap

terms in these cavity fields is the only difference with the usual cavity fields in
[
46
, Equation 3.60]
.

To understand the distribution of the arrays

C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\xi^{\prime}(R_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}S_{\ell\ell^{\prime}})\text{ and }D_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\theta(R_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}S_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}),

it suffices to understand the limiting behavior of
R_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}S_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
, since both
C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
and
D_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
are continuous functions of this array.

We now argue that we can synchronize the overlaps
R_{12}
and
S_{12}
to recover the distribution of
R_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}S_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
, without changing the limit of the free energy. This is achieved by adding a perturbation Hamiltonian with covariances given by monomials
R_{12}^{n}S_{12}^{m}
and apply
[
46
, Theorem 4]
or
[
38
, Section 5]
to conclude that the limiting joint law of
(R_{12},S_{12})
along any converging subsequence can be written as
(F^{-1}_{R}(U),F^{-1}_{S}(U))
where
F^{-1}_{R}
and
F^{-1}_{S}
are the quantile transforms of the marginal distributions of
R_{12}
and
S_{12}
and
U
is uniform on
[0,1]
. Furthermore, both the marginals distribution of

and
S_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
satisfy the Ghirlanda–Guerra identities in the limit, so their distributions are encoded by their corresponding order parameters (the distribution of the first off diagonal entry) by
[
46
, Theorem 2.14 and Theorem 2.15]
. We conclude that the limiting distribution of

can be encoded by one order parameter.

It remains to argue that the order parameter of
C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
and
D_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
has a point mass of at least
s
at
0
, i.e. it lives in the space
\Pr_{s}[0,1]
. By the properties of the Poisson–Dirichlet process
[
46
, Equation 2.34]
we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{1}(S_{12}=0)\rangle^{\prime}\right]=s\text{ and }\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{1}(S_{12}=1)\rangle^{\prime}\right]=1-s

where

is the average with respect to the perturbed Gibbs measure that will arise from the cavity computations. Therefore, we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{1}(R_{12}S_{12}=0)\rangle^{\prime}\right]\geqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{1}(S_{12}=0)\rangle^{\prime}\right]=s

so the distribution of

belongs to
\Pr_{s}[0,1]
.

We have shown that the limiting joint behavior of the overlaps
(R_{12},S_{12})
is encoded by an order parameter

. This gives us a complete characterization of all possible limit points of the distribution of

under the perturbed Gibbs measure. The rest of the proof is now a standard application of the Aizenman–Sims–Starr scheme
[
46
, Chapter 3]
. By the Aizenman–Sims–Starr representation
[
46
, Theorem 3.6]
, we can express the lower bound as

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\sum_{n}v_{n}Z_{N}(n)\right]\\
\geqslant\liminf_{N\to\infty}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\left[\log\bigg\langle\int e^{Z(\sigma,n)\epsilon+h^{(n)}\epsilon}\mathrm{d}\mu(\epsilon)\bigg\rangle^{\prime}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\log\langle e^{Y(\sigma,n)}\rangle^{\prime}\right]\bigg)

where the

is a Gibbs average with respect to the bulk measure plus a perturbation term that enforces the Ghirlanda–Guerra identities and synchronization. From above, we have shown that the covariance of the cavity fields
Z(\sigma,n)
,
h^{(n)}
and
Y(\sigma,n)
are encoded by an order parameter
\zeta\in\Pr_{s}[0,1]
. The simplification of these cavity fields using the recursive computation along the levels of the Poisson–Dirichlet cascades is identical to the computations in the proof of Lemma

, which gives us the matching lower bound.
∎

3.
Laplace transform of the ground-state energy

Compared with the previous section, we now slightly restrict the class of admissible distributions for the random external field, by specializing to Hamiltonians of the form

\displaystyle H_{N}(\sigma)=H^{\prime}_{N}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}\sigma_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}\sigma_{i},

Theorem 3.1

(Laplace transform of the ground-state energy)
.

For every

\begin{split}&\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{sN}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(sNL_{N})\right]\\

where

solves

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}\Psi_{\gamma}=\frac{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}\Psi_{\gamma}+\gamma(t)(\partial_{x}\Psi_{\gamma})^{2}),&\quad\text{for }(t,x)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}\\
\Psi_{\gamma}(1,x)=|x|,&\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}\,.

and where

(3.4)

\mathcal{M}_{s}:=\big\{\gamma:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}\ \mid\ \gamma\text{ is right-continuous, nondecreasing, }\\
\text{integrable, and }\gamma(0)\geqslant s\big\}.

When

4.
Un-inverted formula for the Laplace transform

From now on, we exclusively focus on the case in which the external field is deterministic, as in (

Definition 4.1

We define

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}=\left\{g\in L^{1}([0,1]):\>\int_{r}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(g(t)-t\right)\mathrm{d}t\geqslant 0,\ \forall r\in[0,1]\right\}.

We consider the solution

is still well-defined. Moreover, regularity properties of

are also given in
[

Given an admissible probability space

Lemma 5.2

(Basic properties)
.

The function
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}

\Lambda^{*}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

Proof.

Since
g(\cdot,\alpha)
is convex for each
\alpha
,
\Lambda
is convex. Due to
|\alpha_{t}|\leqslant 1
,
g(\cdot,\alpha)
is locally Lipschitz uniformly over
\alpha\in\mathbf{Mart}_{1}
, which implies that
\Lambda
is also locally Lipschitz. Fix
\mathbf{1}
to be the martingale constantly equal to

. Then,
\frac{\Lambda(s)}{s}\geqslant\frac{g(s,\mathbf{1})}{s}=h+\frac{s}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)(1-t)\mathrm{d}t

\Lambda
grows super-linearly as

, which implies that
\Lambda^{*}
is finite everywhere. The convexity of
\Lambda^{*}
is clear from the definition. Since any convex function that is finite everywhere is continuous (e.g., see
[

), we deduce the continuity of
\Lambda^{*}
. Since the supremum in
\Lambda^{*}
is taken over

, we see that
\Lambda^{*}
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

(Continuous differentiability of

be admissible as in Definition

(5.7)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(s)=\partial_{s}g(s,\alpha)=\mathbf{E}\left[h\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right]+s\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\alpha_{t}^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t

We first show that
\Lambda

Step 1.

We show the upper bound in the definition of differentiability. Fix any

Using this,

and

\displaystyle\Lambda(s^{\prime})\geqslant\Lambda(s)+(s^{\prime}-s)\partial_{s}g(s,\alpha)-C|s^{\prime}-s|^{2}.

Step 2.

We derive a matching lower bound and deduce the differentiability. Fix any

\alpha

\Lambda(s)

, we can always extract a subsequence
(s_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{\left|s_{n_{k}}-s\right|}\left(\Lambda(s_{n_{k}})-\Lambda(s)-(s_{n_{k}}-s)\partial_{s}g(s,\alpha)\right)=0.

Indeed, this along with the first step implies that
\Lambda
is differentiable with the announced derivative. Now, let us verify this. For each
n
, let
\alpha^{(n)}
be the maximizer for
\Lambda(s_{n})
. Since
\alpha^{(n)}
takes values in
[-1,1]
, using the same argument as around the display (
4.28
), we get that
(W,(\alpha^{(n)}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]})
converges along a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in law to some
(W,(\overline{\alpha}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]})
in a possibly different probability space
\overline{\mathscr{P}}
, where
\overline{\alpha}_{t}
is a martingale.
For convenience, we denote the subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
still by
(n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
\overline{g}
be the corresponding functional as
g
but with underlying probability space
\overline{\mathscr{P}}
. We also write
\mathbf{E}_{\mathscr{P}}
and
\mathbf{E}_{\overline{\mathscr{P}}}
as expectations with respect to the ambient probability spaces displayed. Using Skorokhod’s representation, we may turn the convergence in law to a.s. convergence on another common probability space. Using this and the boundedness of these martingales, we can show that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}_{\mathscr{P}}\left[h\alpha^{(n)}_{0}+\alpha^{(n)}_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right]&=\mathbf{E}_{\overline{\mathscr{P}}}\left[h\overline{\alpha}_{0}+\overline{\alpha}_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right],\\
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mathscr{P}}\left[\left(\alpha_{t}^{(n)}\right)^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t&=\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}_{\overline{\mathscr{P}}}\left[\overline{\alpha}_{t}^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Then, we use this to show

(5.10)

\displaystyle\alpha\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{=}}\overline{\alpha}.

First, from (
5.9
) and the definition of
g
in (
5.1
), we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}g(s_{n},\alpha^{(n)})=\overline{g}(s,\overline{\alpha})
.
The continuity of
\Lambda
by Lemma
5.2
together with
g(s_{n},\alpha^{(n)})=\Lambda(s_{n})
implies
\lim_{n\to\infty}g(s_{n},\alpha^{(n)})=\Lambda(s)
. Hence, we have
\Lambda(s)=\overline{g}(s,\overline{\alpha})
and recall
\Lambda(s)=g(s,\alpha)
. The characterization of the unique maximizer in Theorem
4.2
ensures that
\alpha
and
\overline{\alpha}
have the same law, verifying (
5.10
).

Now, let us return to proving (
5.8
) and recall the shorthand
(n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
We start with

\displaystyle g\left(s_{n},\alpha^{(n)}\right)-g\left(s,\alpha^{(n)}\right)=\int_{0}^{1}(s_{n}-s)\partial_{s}g\left(ts_{n}+(1-t)s,\alpha^{(n)}\right)\mathrm{d}t.

Then, using
\Lambda(s_{n})=g\left(s_{n},\alpha^{(n)}\right)
and
\Lambda(s)\geqslant g\left(s,\alpha^{(n)}\right)
on the left-hand side and comparing the right-hand side with
(s_{n}-s)\partial_{s}\overline{g}(s,\overline{\alpha})
, we get

\displaystyle\Lambda(s_{n})-\Lambda(s)\leqslant(s_{n}-s)\partial_{s}\overline{g}(s,\overline{\alpha})

\displaystyle+|s_{n}-s|\int_{0}^{1}\left|\partial_{s}g\left(ts_{n}+(1-t)s,\alpha^{(n)}\right)-\partial_{s}\overline{g}(s,\overline{\alpha})\right|\mathrm{d}t.

Due to (
5.9
) and (
5.1
), the integral vanishes as
n\to\infty
. Using (
5.10
), we also have
\partial_{s}\overline{g}(s,\overline{\alpha})=\partial_{s}g(s,\alpha)
. These together yield (
5.8
), completing the proof of differentiability with derivative as in (
5.7
).

Step 3.

We show that the derivative is continuous in
s
. Again, fix any

and let
\alpha
be the maximizer for
\Lambda(s)
. It suffices to show that for any
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to

we can extract subsequence
(s_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\partial_{s}g(s_{n_{k}},\alpha^{(n_{k})})=\partial_{s}g(s,\alpha)
, where
\alpha^{(n)}
is the maximizer for
\Lambda(s_{n})
. We argue as in Step 2. Writing
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
still as
(n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we extract a subsequential limit
\overline{\alpha}
on a possibly different probability space
\overline{\mathscr{P}}
as before. Then, we still have (
5.9
) and (
5.10
). In view of the definition of
g
in (
5.1
), these two imply the desired convergence. Now, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.4

(Strict convexity of
\Lambda

.

Let
\underline{s}:=\sup\{s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\>\Lambda(s)=s\,\mathsf{gs}\}
. Then, we have that
\Lambda(s)=s\,\mathsf{gs}
for every

and satisfies

for every

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\alpha^{s}_{t}\right)^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\alpha^{0}_{t}\right)^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t=0.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\alpha^{s}_{t}\right)^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t>0.

Since

\underline{s}\in[0,\infty)

\underline{s}=0
and thus

\mathbb{R}_{+}

Proof.

We first give a characterization of the condition
\Lambda(s)=s\,\mathsf{gs}
and then prove the statement on different intervals. Throughout, let
\alpha^{s}
be the unique optimizer for
\Lambda(s)
and we express

\displaystyle g_{1}(\alpha):=\mathbf{E}\left[h\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right]\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptg_{2}(\alpha):=\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\alpha_{t}^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t.

We often use that

In particular, we have

Step 1.
For

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\alpha^{s}_{t}\right)^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t=0\hskip 20.00003pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 20.00003pt\Lambda(s)=s\,\mathsf{gs}.

To see “

\displaystyle s\,\mathsf{gs}=sg_{1}(\alpha^{0})\leqslant sg_{1}(\alpha^{0})+\frac{s^{2}}{2}g_{2}(\alpha^{0})\leqslant\Lambda(s)=sg_{1}(\alpha^{s})+\frac{s^{2}}{2}g_{2}(\alpha^{s})=sg_{1}(\alpha^{s})\leqslant s\,\mathsf{gs}

yielding the r.h.s. Next, to show “

\displaystyle s\,\mathsf{gs}=sg_{1}(\alpha^{0})\leqslant sg_{1}(\alpha^{0})+\frac{s^{2}}{2}g_{2}(\alpha^{0})\leqslant\Lambda(s)=s\,\mathsf{gs}

which implies that
\alpha^{0}
is a maximizer for
\Lambda(s)

Notice that in the proof of “
\Longleftarrow

(5.15)

\displaystyle\Lambda(s)=s\,\mathsf{gs}\hskip 20.00003pt\Longrightarrow\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha^{s}=\alpha^{0}

which will be needed below.

Step 2.
We show that
\Lambda(s)=s\,\mathsf{gs}
for

\Lambda(\underline{s})=\underline{s}\,\mathsf{gs}

Then, convexity implies
\Lambda(s)\leqslant\frac{s}{\underline{s}}\Lambda(\underline{s})+\frac{\underline{s}-s}{\underline{s}}\Lambda(0)=s\,\mathsf{gs}

By convexity and (

5.14

5.15

and (
5.12
).

Step 3.
We show that
\Lambda

(\underline{s},\infty)
and
\Lambda(s)>s\,\mathsf{gs}
for every
s>\underline{s}
. First, the latter must hold, because otherwise (namely,
\Lambda(s)=s\,\mathsf{gs}

\Lambda(s^{\prime})=s^{\prime}\mathsf{gs}

\displaystyle\Lambda(s_{\lambda})<(1-\lambda)g(s_{0},\alpha^{s_{\lambda}})+\lambda g(s_{1},\alpha^{s_{\lambda}})\leqslant(1-\lambda)\Lambda(s_{0})+\lambda\Lambda(s_{1}),

verifying the strict convexity.

Due to

for every

5.13
).

Lemma 5.5

(Maximizers of
\Lambda^{*}
)
.

Let
\underline{s}
be given as in Lemma
5.4
.
The maximizers of
\Lambda^{*}(\mathsf{gs})
are exactly the real numbers in
[0,\underline{s}]
and we have
\Lambda^{*}(\mathsf{gs})=0
.
For every
r>\mathsf{gs}
,
\Lambda^{*}(r)
admits a unique maximizer
s\in(\underline{s},\infty)
, and this maximizer is such that
r=\Lambda^{\prime}(s)
. We also have
\Lambda^{*}(r)>0
for every
r>\mathsf{gs}
.

Proof.

Recall
\Lambda^{*}(\mathsf{gs})=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\{s\,\mathsf{gs}-\Lambda(s)\}
. Lemma
5.4
implies that
s\,\mathsf{gs}-\Lambda(s)=0
for
s\in[0,\underline{s}]
and
s\,\mathsf{gs}-\Lambda(s)>0
for
s>\underline{s}
, which yields the first part of the lemma.

Then, let
r>\mathsf{gs}
. Since
\Lambda
grows super-linearly (see Lemma
5.4
),
\Lambda^{*}(r)
admits maximizers. Let
s
be any such maximizer. If
s=0
, then
\Lambda^{*}(r)=0-\Lambda(0)=0\geqslant s^{\prime}r-\Lambda(s^{\prime})
for every
s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, implying
r\leqslant\Lambda^{\prime}(0)=\mathsf{gs}
due to (
5.11
), which is not possible. Hence, we must have
s>0
. Then, the maximality condition implies that
r=\Lambda^{\prime}(s)
. Since
r>\mathsf{gs}
, we must have
s>\underline{s}
, because otherwise we would have
\Lambda^{\prime}(s)=\mathsf{gs}<r
(see Lemma
5.4
). Hence,
\Lambda^{*}(r)
achieves the maximum over
(\underline{s},\infty)
. Then, the strict convexity in Lemma
5.4
implies the uniqueness of maximizers.

Let
r>\mathsf{gs}
and suppose that
\Lambda^{*}(r)\leqslant 0
. Then, we have

for every
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Due to (
5.11
), we must have

, reaching a contradiction. Hence, the last part of this lemma is verified.
∎

5.2.
Inverting variations inside

Lemma 5.6

(Interchanging

and

)
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{0}:=\left\{\left(\mathbf{E}\left[h\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right],\ \left(\mathbf{E}[\alpha^{2}_{t}]\right)_{t\in[0,1]}\right)\ \bigg|\ \alpha\in\mathbf{Mart}_{1}\right\}

From (

\mathsf{K}_{1}

\mathsf{K}_{0}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{*}(r)=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\inf_{(\chi,g)\in\mathsf{K}_{1}}\left\{G(s,\chi,g)\ \big|\ g\in\mathsf{G}\right\}.

5.3.
One-sided large-deviation principle

Since we only consider
\Lambda(s)
for
s\geqslant 0
, we cannot derive the most general form of the LDP with testing sets being arbitrary subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Instead, we need a “one-sided” version, stated in the following proposition, whose proof is elementary but included here for completeness.
The setting is general and independent of the rest of the paper.

Proposition 5.8

(One-sided LDP)
.

Let
(L_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of random variables and, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
\Lambda_{N}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
the function:

(5.22)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{N}(s):=\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\left(sNL_{N}\right),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Suppose that there exists a continuously differentiable function
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\Lambda_{N}
converges pointwise to
\Lambda
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and that
m:=\Lambda^{\prime}(0)\geqslant 0
. Then, for every Borel measurable subset
A\subseteq[m,\infty)
, we have

(5.23)

\displaystyle-\inf_{A^{\circ_{\mathbb{R}}}}\Lambda^{*}\leqslant\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left\{L_{N}\in A\right\}\leqslant\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\left\{L_{N}\in A\right\}\leqslant-\inf_{\overline{A}}\Lambda^{*}

where
A^{\circ_{\mathbb{R}}}
and

5.4
implies
\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\big(\mathbf{E}\left[\alpha^{2}_{t}\right]-t\big)\mathrm{d}t=0
for the optimal martingale
\alpha
from Theorem
1.1
. By Lemma
6.4
, this implies
h=0
, reaching a contradiction. Now with
\underline{s}=0
, the properties of
\Lambda
follow from Lemma
5.4
.
∎

We conclude by proving Theorem
1.3
, treating the two distinct cases separately.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
in the case
h\neq 0
.

Let
\overline{\alpha}
be the unique maximizer given in Theorem
1.1
, which satisfies
\mathsf{gs}=\mathbf{E}\left[h\overline{\alpha}_{0}+\overline{\alpha}_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right]
.
Lemma
6.4
gives
c_{0}:=\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\big(\mathbf{E}\left[\overline{\alpha}^{2}_{t}\right]-t\big)\mathrm{d}t>0
. Using these and (
1.5
), we get
\Lambda^{*}(r)\leqslant\frac{(r-\mathsf{gs})^{2}}{2c_{0}}
.

On the other hand, we set
c_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)(1-t)\mathrm{d}t>0
. Then, for any
\alpha
admissible in the variational formula for
\Lambda^{*}(r)
in (
1.5
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left(r-\mathbf{E}\left[h\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}W_{t}\right]\right)^{2}}{2\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\alpha^{2}_{t}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e.gs}}}{{\geqslant}}\frac{(r-\mathsf{gs})^{2}}{2c_{1}}

implying
\Lambda^{*}(r)\geqslant\frac{(r-\mathsf{gs})^{2}}{2c_{1}}
and completing the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
in the case
h=0
.

It suffices to show that for any sequence
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
r_{n}>\mathsf{gs}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}=\mathsf{gs}
, we can extract a subsequence
(r_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such
\frac{\Lambda^{*}(r_{n_{k}})}{(r_{n_{k}}-\mathsf{gs})^{2}}
diverges to
+\infty
. For each
n
, we invoke Theorem
5.1
to get
s_{n}
satisfying
r_{n}=\Lambda^{\prime}(s_{n})
and minimizing
\Lambda^{*}(r_{n})
, and
\alpha^{(n)}
maximizing
\frac{\Lambda(s_{n})}{s_{n}}
.

First, we identify the limit of
s_{n}
.
Let
\underline{s}
be given in Lemma
5.4
, which gives that
\Lambda
is strictly convex on
(\underline{s},\infty)
. Hence,
\Lambda^{\prime}
is strictly increasing and bijective from
(\underline{s},\infty)
to
(\Lambda^{\prime}(\underline{s}),\Lambda^{\prime}(\infty))
where
\Lambda^{\prime}(\infty):=\lim_{s\to\infty}\Lambda^{\prime}(s)
. The definition of
\underline{s}
in Lemma
5.4
also gives
\Lambda^{\prime}(\underline{s})=\mathsf{gs}
. This along with
\Lambda^{\prime}(s_{n})=r_{n}\to\mathsf{gs}
and the continuity of
\Lambda^{\prime}
due to Lemma
5.3
implies

(6.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}=\underline{s}.

Next, we turn to
\alpha^{(n)}
. By a similar argument used in Step 2 of the proof of Lemma
5.3
, we can extract a subsequence
(W,(\alpha^{(n_{k})}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]})
that converges in law to
(W,(\overline{\alpha}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]})
for some martingale
\overline{\alpha}
defined on a possibly larger probability space. Recall the expression of
\frac{\Lambda(s)}{s}
from (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) which is exactly the left-hand side in (
4.2
) of Theorem
4.2
. The choice of
\alpha^{(n)}
ensures
\frac{\Lambda(s_{n})}{s_{n}}=\frac{g(s_{n},\alpha^{(n)})}{s_{n}}
. At
s=0
, we understand
\frac{\Lambda(0)}{0}=\Lambda^{\prime}(0)=\mathsf{gs}
(see (
5.11
)). Then, using the continuity of
(s,\alpha)\mapsto\frac{g(s,\alpha)}{s}
, the aforementioned convergence of
\alpha^{(n_{k})}
, and (
6.6
), we can pass
n_{k}
to the limit to get
\frac{\Lambda(\underline{s})}{\underline{s}}=\frac{g(\underline{s},\overline{\alpha})}{\underline{s}}
. In other words,
\overline{\alpha}
is the unique optimizer of
\frac{\Lambda(\underline{s})}{\underline{s}}
.
If
\underline{s}>0
, we invoke Lemma
5.4
to see

(6.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\overline{\alpha}^{2}_{t}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t=0.

If
\underline{s}=0
, then
\overline{\alpha}
is the optimizer of
\frac{\Lambda(0)}{0}=\mathsf{gs}
, which by Lemma
6.4
and the assumption
h=0
also implies (
6.7
). Then, the convergence of
\alpha^{(n_{k})}
and (
6.7
) yield

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\alpha^{(n_{k})}_{t}\right)^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t=0.

Recall that
\alpha^{(n)}
is the optimizer for
\Lambda^{*}(r_{n})
in (
1.5
) which along with (
1.4
) gives

\displaystyle\Lambda^{*}\left(r_{n_{k}}\right)\geqslant\frac{(r_{n_{k}}-\mathsf{gs})^{2}}{2\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\alpha^{(n_{k})}_{t}\right)^{2}\right]-t\right)\mathrm{d}t}.

Combining the above two displays, we have that
\frac{\Lambda^{*}\left(r_{n_{k}}\right)}{(r_{n_{k}}-\mathsf{gs})^{2}}
diverges to
+\infty
as
k\to\infty
. As explained in the beginning, this gives the desired result.
∎
