Low field magneto-tuneable photodetection in Dy3+ doped Co-Zn ferrite based heterojunctions
Authors: Manoj Kumar Rout, Sunita Keshri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents the fabrication and magnetically tunable photoresponse behavior of p⁺-Si/CZFO/AZO and p⁺-Si/CZFDO/AZO trilayer heterostructures prepared by radio frequency magnetron sputtering. The structural, morphological, optical, and magnetic characteristics of Co0.7Zn0.3Fe2O4 (CZFO) and Dy3+ doped Co0.7Zn0.3Fe2O4 (CZFDO) spinel ferrite thin films were studied thoroughly. Photoresponse characteristics of the fabricated trilayer devices under broadband illumination (39.6 , 300–1100 ) demonstrate that the CZFDO based photodetector exhibits nearly threefold higher photocurrent density ( J), faster rise () and decay times (), and greater responsivity and specific detectivity () compared to the CZFO based device at + 5 bias. The application of an external magnetic field (0 to 3000 ) further enhances the photocurrent by promoting spin aligned carrier transport and reducing recombination losses, confirming magneto-photoelectric coupling. The values of R, , , also change significantly with the application of a magnetic field for both devices. The best obtained values of rise and fall times are 40 and for the CZFDO based device at + 5 bias and 3000 Oe field. Interestingly, both devices retain partial photocurrent even after removing the field. These heterostructure devices with high photocurrent to dark current ratios and fast switching action can play the role of magnetically tunable broadband photodetectors, suitable for potential applications in magneto-optoelectronic sensors and spintronic devices.Subject terms: Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

Photodiodes and photodetectors are optoelectronic devices which convert incident light of different wavelengths to electrical signals through the phenomenon of photoelectric effect1. They play a vital role in various modern technologies, such as optical and space communication2,3, imaging4, environmental monitoring5, wearable electronics6,and medical instrumentation7. Performance and applicability of photodetectors depend upon their spectral responsivity, response speed, photosensitivity, and operational stability. Photodetectors are generally classified as broadband and narrowband types depending upon the spectral bandwidth of the respective photoactive materials. Conventional semiconductor based photodetectors, such as , , and show high detectivity, but are usually limited by their narrow spectral response, complexities of fabrication, and comparatively expensive production costs8,9. These issues caused an increase in interest on oxide and ferrite based heterostructures that offer tunable optoelectronic functionality, low dark current, and broadband photoresponse.

Among oxide semiconductors, spinel ferrites with the general formula of MFe2O4(M = Co, Zn, Ni, etc.) have received great interest due to their unique combination of magnetic, optical, and electrical properties. These materials are used for applications in magnetoelectric coupling, resistive switching, sensors, microwave devices, and photoelectrochemical water splitting10–12. In particular, Co-Zn ferrite has drawn great attention for its simple synthesis, low cost, chemical stability, high magnetization and good optical absorption10,13. It shows a mixed spinel structure; thus, the distribution of cations between tetrahedral and octahedral sites greatly influences its optical, electrical and magnetic properties. This sensitivity allows fine tuning of the optical and magnetic properties of this material, by properly chosen doping or cation substitution, resulting in the possibility to tune band structure, carrier concentration and magnetic anisotropy to meet the demands of particular optoelectronic or magneto-optical applications. Incorporation of rare earth ions (RE3+) into ferrite lattices is an effective way of improving optical and magnetic properties due to their large magnetic moments and strong 3d-4fexchange interactions13. Doping with Dy3+ions introduces localized electronic states in the band gap and alters the cation distribution, leading to band gap narrowing and increased photocarrier generation14,15. Due to their high magnetic moment and strong spin–orbit coupling, Dy3+ ions cause lattice strain and facilitate redistribution of Co2+, Zn2+, and Fe3+ ions between the and sites, leading to improved magnetization16. As a result, Dy3+ doped Co–Zn ferrite would be expected to act as a good photoactive layer for broadband photodetection with magnetic tunability. Incorporation of such rare earth ions adds new degrees of freedom for optimizing photodetector performance and magneto-optoelectronic behavior.

With the rapid development of multifunctional technologies, there is an increasing demand for materials that integrate magnetic, optical, and electrical coupling in a single system. Understanding the relationship between these properties is vital for the design of high performance sensors and multifunctional optoelectronic devices. The performance of these devices can be enhanced by the application of an external magnetic field, which can suppress the recombination of photoexcited carriers and help to improve charge separation through spin polarization17–19. In this regard, magnetic field assisted modulation has emerged as a viable, contactless, and energy efficient method to enhance optoelectronic performance. Previous studies have demonstrated such magneto-tuneable photo responses. For example, Sheng et al.20 reported approximately enhancement in photocurrent in case of La0.7Sr0.3MnO3 film developed on SrTiO3 under a 6 magnetic field. Pan et al.21 achieved magnetic field control of photocurrent in CoFe2O4/PbZrTiO3 composites through magnetoelectric coupling. However, for practical applications, significant modulation under relatively low magnetic fields is more desirable. Conventional non-magnetic semiconductors are relatively insensitive to weak magnetic fields, whereas ferrimagnetic semiconductors such as spinel ferrites provide a favourable platform for investigating low field magnetic effects on charge transport and photoresponse.

Several ferrite based heterojunction photodiodes have been reported in recent years, including NiFe2O4:PANI composite paper-based devices22, p-Si/NiFe2O411, p-ZnCo2O4/n-Si23, and Au/(CoFe2O4-PVP)/n-Si24. These studies have revealed the potential of ferrite materials in optoelectronic applications; however, most are limited to narrow spectral response or lack magnetic control. Developing a ferrite based trilayer heterostructure that operates under broadband illumination and offers magnetic tunability could open new pathways for multifunctional photodetectors. Despite numerous reports on ferrite thin films, systematic studies on ferrite based thin film heterostructure devices and their magnetic field dependent photoresponse remain scarce. Integrating ferrite layers with semiconductor junctions provides an effective means of coupling photon driven and spin dependent transport processes, leading to magnetically controllable photoresponse.

In this context, the present work investigates p⁺-Si/CZFO/AZO and p⁺-Si/CZFDO/AZO trilayer heterostructures fabricated by RF magnetron sputtering. The p⁺-Si substrate serves as a low resistivity, photoconductive base with response extending to ~ 1100 nm, while the ferrite layer acts as the primary photo absorber. The top aluminium doped zinc oxide (AZO) layer functions as a conductive electrode, enhancing optical transparency, electrical conductivity, and carrier collection25. AZO is a cost-effective, non-toxic, and stable transparent oxide widely used in optoelectronic and photovoltaic devices25,26. In such trilayer architectures, multiple junction barriers promote charge separation and suppress dark current, thereby improving photosensitivity and responsivity. This thus creates a versatile platform for efficient carrier transport as well as magnetically modulated photoresponse across the broadband spectrum by combining ferrite with AZO and Si substrates. Furthermore, the inherent magnetism of the ferrite layer provides the possibility to externally control carrier dynamics through spin dependent scattering and magneto-transport effects, offering an additional degree of freedom for performance control. Despite extensive research into ferrite materials, there are a few reports on CZFO based photodetectors and to date Dy³⁺ doped CZFO thin film heterostructures have not been investigated for broadband photodetection, which is magnetically tunable. To address this issue, this work systematically investigates the photoresponse characteristics of CZFO and CZFDO devices under broadband illumination in the range of 300 -1100 nm and varying magnetic fields to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of magneto-photoelectric coupling.

Experimental

Target preparation & device fabrication

Sputtering targets of Co0.7Zn0.3Fe2O4 (CZFO) and Co0.7Zn0.3Dy0.02Fe2O4(CZFDO) were prepared individually using the sol gel auto combustion method for subsequent thin film depositions. The complete synthesis procedure for CZFO and CZFDO nanoparticles has been detailed in our previous study13,16. Following synthesis, the resulting nanoparticles in powder form were compressed into rectangular targets with dimensions of 6 inches (length) × 1 inch (breadth) × 5 (thickness) and subjected to heat treatment at for 4 . Thin films of CZFO and CZFDO were deposited at room temperature onto boron doped p+-type <100> silicon substrates using radio frequency (RF) magnetron sputtering. The substrates had a thickness of and a resistivity ranging from 1 to . Before each deposition cycle, the sputtering chamber was pumped down to a base pressure of . Sputtering was carried out in an argon environment with a target to substrate distance of 2.2 , an RF power of 150 , and a deposition duration of 2 . The working pressure during deposition was maintained at with an argon flow rate of 30 .

To fabricate the photodetector devices, an aluminium doped zinc oxide (AZO) layer was deposited on top of the CZFO and CZFDO films on 1 cm2 p⁺-Si substrates, forming a trilayer heterostructure of p⁺-Si/CZFO or CZFDO/AZO. The AZO films were deposited under the same RF sputtering parameters as the ferrite layers, but with a reduced deposition time of 45 minutes. The AZO layer served as a transparent conducting electrode, allowing incident light to pass through and be absorbed by the underlying ferrite layers. This trilayer configuration was selected to improve device photo response, sensitivity, specific detectivity, and to effectively minimize dark current contributions. The film thicknesses were approximately nm for the ferrite layers and nm for the AZO layers.

Photoresponse characteristics of devices

The current density–voltage (J-V) characteristics of the p+-Si/CZFO/AZO and p⁺-Si/CZFDO/AZO heterojunction photodetectors were investigated under dark conditions and varying illumination power intensities from 3.3 to 39.6 , within a bias range of to [Figure 5(a–b)]. Both devices exhibit nonlinear and asymmetric J-Vbehavior, confirming the presence of heterojunction barriers at the AZO/CZFO (or CZFDO) and CZFO (or CZFDO)/p⁺-Si interfaces. Such characteristics are typical for heterojunction photodiodes involving multiple interface barriers38. The observed nonlinearity can be attributed to several interconnected physical mechanisms, including Schottky like barrier formation, space charge limited conduction, interface trap states, oxygen vacancies, and barrier height inhomogeneities38. Under dark conditions, both devices demonstrate negligible current density, suggesting the formation of high quality heterojunctions at the interfaces with suppressed leakage. Low dark current is particularly beneficial for achieving high specific detectivity in photodetector applications39. As seen in Fig. 5(a–b), the photocurrent density increases steadily with light intensity, due to enhanced photon absorption by the trilayer structure and the subsequent increase in carrier generation17. Upon light irradiation, both CZFO and CZFDO layers absorb photons with energies above their respective band gaps (2.60 and 2.25 ), producing electron–hole pairs. These photoexcited carriers are driven toward the electrodes, contributing to the measured photocurrent. Furthermore, higher illumination intensities and applied bias facilitate faster charge carrier transport toward the electrodes while simultaneously reducing electron-hole recombination probability38,39.–40.

Magnetically tuned photoresponse characteristics of devices

The ability to modulate photoresponse through external magnetic fields provides an additional degree of freedom for device optimization and functionality enhancement, making devices attractive for next generation magneto-optoelectronic applications193452.,, To investigate the magnetic field tuning capabilities, the current density-voltage characteristics and photoresponse of both p⁺-Si/CZFO/AZO and p⁺-Si/CZFDO/AZO photodetectors were systematically evaluated under an applied magnetic field ranging from 0 to . During magnetic field-dependent measurements, the external magnetic field was applied parallel to the plane of the thin film, while the current flows perpendicular to the film plane of the trilayer structure, resulting in a perpendicular (transverse) configuration.

In this configuration, the applied magnetic field primarily influences carrier transport through spin-dependent scattering and spin alignment effects. A schematic of the experimental setup is shown in Fig. 6. Figure 7(a-b) presents the J-V characteristics of both devices measured at illumination intensity and within a bias range of to .

Both photodetectors exhibit a significant and progressive enhancement in photocurrent density with increasing magnetic field strength, demonstrating magneto-photoelectric coupling effects1720., For the CZFO device, the photocurrent density increases from an initial at to at under a bias, corresponding to a 16.75-fold enhancement relative to the zero field condition. The CZFDO device shows an even stronger magnetic field response, with photocurrent rising from at to approximately at , corresponding to a 6.5-fold enhancement. This magnetic field induced enhancement is substantially greater than the 3-fold improvement achieved through doping alone, indicating that magnetic field application provides an additional and highly effective mechanism for performance optimization.

The systematic photocurrent enhancement with a magnetic field can be attributed to several interconnected magneto-transport mechanisms operating within the ferrite layers. In general, ferrite materials exhibit negative magnetoresistance, which causes a decrease in electrical resistance under an applied field, thereby improving charge transport17. Magnetic fields induce spin alignment, alter the electronic band structure in the ferrite layers through magneto-electronic coupling, which also causes the changes in photoresponse172152.,, In the absence of an external magnetic field, the magnetic moments of CZFO and CZFDO are randomized, creating a disordered spin state. Charge carriers traversing this environment undergo frequent spin disorder scattering, reducing their mean free path, suppressing mobility, and diminishing photocurrent. When a magnetic field is applied, these magnetic moments align along the field, creating an ordered spin environment. Charge carriers with parallel spin orientation experience fewer scattering events due to favourable exchange interactions, which increases their mean free path and enhances carrier transport toward the electrodes. Conversely, antiparallel spins are still strongly scattered. This spin selective transport across heterojunction interfaces results in greater net charge carrier collection and enhanced photocurrent. In addition, external magnetic fields affect the spin-orbit coupling and the magnetic domain structure within the ferrite layers, resulting in reduced recombination losses and improved carrier transport21. Magnetically induced changes to the depletion region width and barrier height at the heterojunction also facilitate charge separation and collection52. Prathipkumar et al.17 observed magnetic field enhanced photocurrent in composites, attributing the effect to negative tunnelling magnetoresistance. Similarly, Pavlov et al.53 reported that applying a magnetic field to heterostructures reduce electron–hole recombination and decrease the barrier height, which in turn enhances the current. Singh et al.34 demonstrated that band gap narrowing in CoFe2O4 nanostructures could be magnetically tuned with field strength.

The temporal photoresponse characteristics under different magnetic field strengths are illustrated in Fig. 7(c-d) by on/off the light in 5 s intervals under the same conditions as the zero field measurements. Both devices maintained excellent switching stability and reproducibility across all field strengths. Figure 7(e–f) details the response kinetics (rise and fall times) under a 3000 field. Interestingly, magnetic field application not only increases photocurrent amplitude but also accelerates response kinetics relative to zero field conditions. At 3000 and bias, the CZFO device shows rise/fall time of 65/72 (representing and improvement), and the CZFDO device achieved ( and faster, respectively). These improvements are attributed to enhanced carrier mobility, suppression of spin dependent scattering, and reduced trapping effects in the presence of an applied magnetic field, as discussed earlier. The CZFDO device exhibits superior photocurrent performance and faster switching speeds both in the absence and presence of an external magnetic field as compared to the CZFO device, highlighting the beneficial effects of doping. In our earlier study16, we reported that ions, with their large magnetic moment of 10.6 , enhance the net magnetic moment in CZFDO relative to CZFO, leading to improved magnetic properties, as evidenced by VSM analysis results. These enhanced magnetic and optical characteristics strengthen magneto-photoelectric coupling within the device, which accounts for the consistently superior photoresponse of CZFDO based photodetectors under both zero field and applied magnetic field conditions.

Both devices show step like increases in photocurrent upon field application. Unlike the optical ON/OFF cycles under a constant magnetic field, where the photocurrent rapidly reaches its maximum value, here the rise is more gradual, and the current does not immediately attain the peak level. Similarly, when the field is removed, the photocurrent does not fully return to the baseline. This behavior is attributed to magnetic relaxation and domain wall pinning in the ferrite layers, which delay spin reconfiguration after field modulation.

The stepwise photocurrent increase resembles magnetic hysteresis (M-H)loops, where remanence reflects memory of prior states; similarly, these devices show partial photocurrent retention (memory like effects) after field removal4754., The effect is more pronounced in the doped device, consistent with its higher coercivity and remanence (Sect. 3.2). This memory like effect highlights the potential of these devices for non volatile optoelectronic memory applications, where device states can be programmed and retained using external magnetic fields. Additionally, the partial current retention behavior in the field off state makes these devices suitable for non-volatile, magnetically controlled optoelectronic memory and spintronics applications.

Energy band diagram analysis

The energy band diagrams of the p⁺-Si/CZFO (or CZFDO)/AZO heterostructures under different conditions are illustrated in Fig. 8. At equilibrium [Figure 8(a)], the band gaps of the individual layers are taken as 1.12 eV (p⁺-Si),112.60 eV (CZFO), 2.25 eV (CZFDO), and 3.55 eV (AZO)55. From the literature, the reported electron affinities are ≈ 4.05 eV for p⁺-Si11, ≈ 4.60 eV for AZO25, and ≈ 3.72 eV for Co–Zn ferrites43,while the corresponding work function values are ~ 4.80, ~ 4.98, and ~ 3.86 eV, respectively1125264355.,,,, The AZO and ferrites act as n-type semiconductors252643.,, These device configurations form a type-II heterostructure, with a p–n junction at the p⁺-Si/ferrite interface and an n–n junction at the ferrite/AZO interface. At equilibrium, the p⁺-Si/ferrite junction develops a depletion region accompanied by band bending, which forms a potential barrier that suppresses carrier flow in the dark, consistent with the very low measured dark current. At the ferrite/AZO interface, although both are n-type, a conduction band offset arises due to differences in electron affinity and Fermi level alignment43. The top layer AZO with a wider bandgap (3.55 eV) maintains high optical transparency and facilitates maximum photon penetration to the photoactive ferrite layer25..

In energy band diagrams, electrons naturally migrate toward lower energy states within the conduction band (appearing as downward movement), while holes, being the absence of electrons, move toward higher energy positions in the valence band (appearing as upward movement)43. Under reverse bias (positive bias on AZO with respect to p⁺-Si), the Fermi level of the p⁺-Si is shifted upward relative to the n-type ferrite and AZO semiconductors, as shown in Fig. 8(b)56.The applied electric field points toward the p⁺-Si side, driving electrons to the left. The depletion region at the p⁺-Si/ferrite junction widens, and the barrier height in Si increases in reverse bias conditions. Illumination under this condition provides a large depletion volume for photon absorption, producing more electron–hole pairs. This elevated carrier density also lowers the effective junction barriers of the p⁺-Si/ferrite interface. Under these favourable conditions, where the depletion region enhances carrier separation and electrons naturally move to lower energy states in the conduction band, the photogenerated electrons rapidly drift toward the AZO electrode, while the holes migrate into the p⁺-Si substrate4345., The rapid drift reduces recombination losses, resulting in a stable and strong photocurrent, as confirmed experimentally under reverse bias.

Under forward bias (positive bias on p⁺-Si relative to AZO) [Figure 8(c)], the AZO energy levels shift upward, and the applied electric field now points toward AZO, drives electrons to the right56. In this configuration, the built in potential at the Si/ferrite junction is reduced, narrowing the depletion width and lowering the barrier heights. Illumination in this state produces photocarriers in a much smaller active volume, and the weaker internal electric field reduces separation efficiency. Consequently, recombination becomes more prominent, leading to more nonlinear and less stable photocurrent compared to reverse bias.

Application of an external magnetic field (0–3000 G) further modulates carrier transport by altering the spin and charge dynamics within the ferrite layer. Magnetic moment alignment reduces spin disorder scattering, lowering interfacial transport barriers and improving carrier mobility via ordered spin states at the junctions. At the same time, spin dependent transport suppresses recombination, thereby enhancing photocurrent stability. In CZFDO device, these effects are more pronounced because ions enhance the net magnetic moment, leading to stronger spin alignment and reduced scattering. In addition, the CZFDO device absorbs a broader spectral range due to its narrower band gap (2.25 eV), increasing photogeneration and raising the quasi Fermi level splitting52. Together, these effects account for the superior photoresponse and stronger magnetic field sensitivity observed in CZFDO based devices. Altogether, the trilayer heterostructure benefits from favourable band alignment, efficient carrier separation under reverse bias, and magnetically assisted transport. The ferrite intermediate layer simultaneously functions as an absorber and a selective transport medium, where magnetic properties tune carrier mobility through spin dependent scattering. These combined effects highlight the potential of CZFO and especially CZFDO heterostructures as next generation magnetically tunable broadband photodetectors.

Conclusions

Trilayer heterostructures of p⁺-Si/CZFO/AZO and p⁺-Si/CZFDO/AZO were successfully fabricated through RF magnetron sputtering and systematically investigated for photodetection and magnetic field tunable photoresponse. Structural, morphological, optical, and magnetic characteristics of the CZFO and CZFDO thin films were thoroughly studied. Under broadband illumination (39.6 , 300–1100 ) and bias, both fabricated trilayer devices displayed nonlinear rectifying behaviour with low dark current. The CZFDO based device produced much higher photosensitivity, responsivity, and detectivity and faster temporal response than CZFO device. Band diagram analysis confirmed a type-II heterostructure that widens the depletion region under reverse bias, promoting efficient separation of photogenerated carriers. The application of an external magnetic field further enhances the photocurrent, responsivity, specific detectivity, and response speed of the devices through magnetic modulation of carrier transport by suppressing spin-dependent scattering. Interestingly, both devices retained partial photocurrent after field removal. At 3000 , the rise/fall times are 65/72 (representing and improvement), and ( and faster) for CZFO and CZFDO based devices, respectively. The interplay of doping and magnetic field assisted carrier modulation allow precise tuning of photoresponse, establishing the CZFDO based trilayer device as an efficient magnetically controllable broadband photodetector. In both devices, ferrite layers act simultaneously as the main photon absorber and a spin-selective transport medium, yielding wide spectral coverage, rapid response, and low field magnetic control. These capabilities make the heterostructure devices promising for adaptive optical communication, magnetic-field sensors, and magneto-optoelectronic switching.
