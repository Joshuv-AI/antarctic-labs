Enveloping algebras via motivic Hall algebras Thanks: * Corresponding author.
Xinyi Feng*, Fan Xu

Abstract
. We give a geometric realization of the whole universal enveloping algebra of the Borcherds-Bozec algebra using quiver with loops via the motivic semi-derived Hall algebra approach. In particular, using acyclic quivers, we give a geometric realization of the whole universal enveloping algebra of a certain generalized Kac-Moody algebra using the motivic Bridgeland’s Hall algebra given in [ 12 ] .



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
16G20, 17B37

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Given a finite quiver
Q=(\mathbb{I},\mathbb{H},s,t)
, where
\mathbb{I}
is the set of vertices,
\mathbb{H}
is the set of arrows and
s,t:\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{H}
are the source and target maps, respectively.

1.1.1.
Realization of enveloping algebras via acyclic quivers

If
Q
is acyclic, then there is an associated generalized Cartan matrix which yields a Kac-Moody Lie algebra with a triangular decomposition
\mathcal{\mathfrak{g}}_{Q}=\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{Q}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{-}
.
A lot of work has been done to find interpretations for
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
, in particular, for the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})
of
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
, in terms of representation theory of
Q
.

As for realization of the positive part
U(\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{+})
, Ringel introduced the so-called Ringel-Hall algebra approach in
[
31
]
[
30
]
.
He proved the existence of Hall polynomials for any representation-directed algebra
\Lambda
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and constructed an associative algebra
\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Lambda)
over
\mathbb{Z}[q]
.
After evaluating at
q=1
, the free
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule
\mathcal{L}(\Lambda)
generated by the isomorphism classes of indecomposable
\Lambda
-modules becomes a Lie subalgebra whose universal enveloping algebra is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\Lambda)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
after a complex extension.
In particular, if
\Lambda
is representation-finite,
\mathcal{L}(\Lambda)
is exactly the Chevalley
\mathbb{Z}
-form of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{Q}
and then
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\Lambda)
realizes the Kostant
\mathbb{Z}
-form of
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+}_{Q})
, see
[
32
]
.
Later, using Euler characteristics of filtration varieties, Riedtmann
[
29
]
obtained a similar result for any representation-finite algebra
\Lambda
over
\mathbb{C}
and realized the positive half of the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra generated by indecomposables.
In general, for any finite dimensional algebra
\Lambda
over
\mathbb{C}
, Ding, Xiao and Xu generalized Riedtmann’s result in
[
10
]
using characteristic functions of constructible sets of stratified Krull-Schmidt, see
[
10
, Section 3]
for details.

On the other hand, Lusztig introduced a geometric approach in
[
23
]
[
24
]
using perverse sheaves on the varieties of quiver representations over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.
Later, he constructed the semicanonical basis for
U(\mathfrak{n}_{Q}^{+})
in
[
25
]
in terms of constructible functions on the set of irreducible components of the Lagrangian variety, which differs from the specialization of the canonical basis at
q=1
.
In addition, Geiss, Leclerc and Schröer studied the properties of the dual of Lusztig’s semicanonical basis in
[
13
]
[
14
]
, which can also be interpreted as varieties of modules over preprojective algebras.

In terms of realizing the whole enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})
, motivated by Lusztig’s works, Nakajima defined a new family of varieties called quiver varieties in
[
27
]
.
In the subsequent work
[
28
]
, he considered the Cartesian product of quiver varieties with certain Lagrangian subvarieties inside, defined the convolution product on homogeneous groups and then realized
U(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})
using the corresponding geometric construction of integrable highest weight representations given in
[
27
]
.
In particular, if
Q
is of finite type, Lusztig showed in
[
26
]
that any arbitrary large finite-dimensional quotient of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})
can be realized in terms of constructible functions on the triple variety.

1.1.2.
Quivers with loops

If
Q
has loops, let
g_{i}
be the number of loops at vertex
i\in\mathbb{I}
and
c_{ij}
be the number of arrows in
\mathbb{H}
from
i
to
j
if
i\neq j
, then one can associate
Q
with a symmetric Borcherds-Cartan matrix
A_{Q}=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{I}}
, where

\displaystyle a_{ij}=\begin{cases}2-2g_{i}&\text{ if }i=j\\
-c_{ij}-c_{ji}&\text{ if }i\neq j\end{cases},

which yields a generalized Kac-Moody algebra in the sense of
[
1
]
(also called Borcherds algebra), or more generally, yields a Borcherds-Bozec algebra, with infinitely many generators for each imaginary index
i\in\mathbb{I}^{\mathrm{im}}
.

As a generalization of the quantum group, the quantum generalized Kac-Moody algebra and its representation theory were introduced in
[
18
]
.
Later, Kang and Schiffmann constructed the canonical basis for its negative half in
[
17
]
using certain semisimple perverse sheaves, recovering the negative half of the corresponding enveloping algebra after specialization at
q=1
.

The quantum Borcherds-Bozec algebra arises from Bozec’s work of perverse sheaves on the representation varieties of quivers with loops in
[
2
]
[
3
]
, where he developed the corresponding canonical and crystal basis theories.
As a further generalization of the quantum generalized Kac-Moody algebra, its negative half can be realized via a Ringel-Hall approach, see
[
19
]
.
Later, Lu recovered the whole quantum Borcherds-Bozec algebra in
[
22
]
using the semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra of nilpotent
Q
-representations over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

1.2.
Main result

Let
Q=(\mathbb{I},\mathbb{H},s,t)
be a finite quiver, possibly having loops, and
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
be the associated Borcherds-Bozec algebra.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{rep}^{\mathrm{nil}}_{\mathbb{C}}(Q)
be the category of finite dimensional nilpotent representations of
Q
over
\mathbb{C}
.

This paper is divided into two parts.

1.2.1.

In the first part, we give a geometric realization of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathcal{G}_{Q})
.

We construct a motivic version of the semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra over
\mathbb{C}(t)
following the framework of
[
16
]
, denoted by
\mathcal{SDH}_{t}^{\mathrm{red}}(\mathcal{A})
, and define its classical limit at
t=-1
, see Definition
3.6
.
This process is strongly motivated by the recent work of Fang-Lan-Xiao in
[
12
]
and the canonical process of taking the classical limit in
[
11
]
.

Then, inspired by the injective homomorphism at the quantum group level, see Theorem
3.5
, we define a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathcal{SU}_{-1}(\mathcal{A})
, see Definition
3.8
, which is proved to be isomorphic to
U(\mathcal{G}_{Q})
in Theorem
3.9
.
Moreover, we construct a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathcal{SDH}_{-1}^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal{A})
which is generated by certain elements corresponding to indecomposable objects and certain Cartan elements, see Definition
3.10
for details.

Our first main result is the following, see also Corollary
3.17
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an injective homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

\mathcal{S}\Phi:U(\mathcal{G}_{Q})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{SDH}^{\mathrm{ind}}_{-1}(\mathcal{A}).

1.2.2.

In the second part, from a different perspective, we use representations of acyclic quivers to give a geometric realization of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g}_{B,\mathfrak{c}})
of a certain generalized Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{B,\mathfrak{c}}
associated with a symmetric Borcherds-Cartan matrix
B
with charge
\mathfrak{c}
.

Assume that
Q
is acyclic and let
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
be the (derived) Kac-Moody algebra determined by
Q
, then
\mathcal{A}
has enough projective objects.
Using a similar argument as in
[
21
, Corollary 4.13]
, it can be proved that the motivic semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra
\mathcal{SDH}_{t}^{\mathrm{red}}(\mathcal{A})
, defined using two-periodic complexes in
\mathcal{A}
, coincides with the motivic Bridgeland’s Hall algebra
\mathcal{DH}_{t}^{\mathrm{red}}(\mathcal{A})
defined in
[
12
, Section 4]
using two-periodic projective complexes.

Thus, in this special case, we turn to
\mathcal{DH}_{t}^{\mathrm{red}}(\mathcal{A})
and define a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathcal{DH}^{\mathrm{ind}}_{-1}(\mathcal{A})
in an analogous way, see Definition
4.9
.
Similarly to the first main result, we obtain an analogous corollary, see also Corollary
4.11
.

Corollary 1.2
.

There is an injective homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

\displaystyle\Phi:U(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{DH}_{-1}^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal{A}).

Moreover, we note that
\mathcal{DH}_{-1}^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal{A})
contains a larger
\mathbb{C}
-Lie subalgebra by
[
12
, Definition 6.6, Theorem 6.7]
in a natural way, denoted by
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
, see also Definition
4.12
.

We prove the the following result using a similar method as in Ringel
[
31
]
, Riedtmann
[
29
]
and Ding-Xiao-Xu
[
10
]
, see Section
4.4
and Corollary
4.21
for details, which also gives the description of the elements in
\mathcal{DH}_{-1}^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal{A})
at a vector space level.

Proposition 1.3
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

\Psi:U(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{DH}_{-1}^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal{A}).

Note also that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
contains a
\mathbb{C}
-Lie subalgebra
\mathcal{GL}(\mathcal{A})
, which is isomorphic to a generalized Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{B,\mathfrak{c}}
for some
B
and
\mathfrak{c}
, see Section
4.3
and Lemma
4.15
for details.
As a consequence, we obtain our second main result, see also Corollary
4.22
.

Theorem 1.4
.

There is an injective homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

\displaystyle\Theta:U(\mathfrak{g}_{B,\mathfrak{c}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{DH}_{-1}^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal{A}).

1.3.
Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we construct the motivic semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra
\mathcal{SDH}_{t}^{\mathrm{red}}(\mathcal{A})
over
\mathbb{C}(t)
.

Section
3
is divided into the following three parts:

In Section
3.1
, we give a geometric realization of the quantum Borcherds-Bozec algebra using

, we give the definition of the classical limit

.

Section

In Section

\mathbb{C}

\mathbb{C}
-Lie subalgebra

and prove that

for some

and
\mathfrak{c}
, as a revision of
[

, Section 6.2]
.
In Section
4.4
, we give the proof of Proposition
1.3
and deduce our second main result.

2.
Motivic form of semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebras

Let
Q=(\mathbb{I},\mathbb{H},s,t)
be a finite quiver,
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{rep}^{\mathrm{nil}}_{\mathbb{C}}(Q)
be the category of finite dimensional nilpotent representations of
Q
over
\mathbb{C}
, then
\mathcal{A}
is hereditary and abelian.
If
Q
has loops,
\mathcal{A}
does not have enough projective objects.

2.1.
Moduli stacks of objects and filtrations in
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{A})

For any dimension vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}\mathbb{I}
, consider a
\mathbb{C}
-affine space

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(Q)=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathbb{H}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{s(h)}},\mathbb{C}^{\alpha_{t(h)}}).

Any point
x=(x_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{H}}
in
E_{\alpha}(Q)
defines a representation
M(x)=(\mathbb{C}^{\alpha},x)
of Q in the fixed vector space
\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\mathbb{C}^{{\alpha}_{i}}
.

Given a point
x\in E_{\alpha}(Q)
and a path
\rho=h_{1}\cdots h_{n}
in
Q
, set
x_{\rho}=x_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{n}}
, then
x
is called nilpotent if there exists
N\geqslant 2
such that for any
n>N
,
x_{\rho}=0
for any path
\rho=h_{1}\cdots h_{n}
in
Q
of length
n
.
Define a closed subvariety

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{nil}}(Q)=\{x\in E_{\alpha}(Q)\mid x\text{ is nilpotent }\}.

Let

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(Q)=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\mathrm{GL}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{C})

be the algebraic group which acts on
E_{\alpha}(Q)
by
(g_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\cdot(x_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{H}}=(g_{t(h)}x_{h}g_{s(h)}^{-1})_{h\in\mathbb{H}}
.
This induces an action of
G_{\alpha}(Q)
on
E_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{nil}}(Q)
, with the quotient stack denoted by
[E_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{nil}}(Q)/G_{\alpha}(Q)]
.

There is a canonical bijection

\displaystyle\left\{G_{\alpha}(Q)\text{-orbits in }E_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{nil}}(Q)\right\}\;

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\;\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}isomorphism classes of\\
nilpotent $\mathbb{C}$-representations of $Q$\\
of dimension vector $\alpha$\end{tabular}\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{x}

\displaystyle\longmapsto[M(x)].

Moreover, the stabilizer
\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{\alpha}(Q)}(x)\cong\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{A}}(M(x))
is special and affine.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
be the category of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded complexes in
\mathcal{A}
.
Any object in
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
is of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{0}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d^{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{0}}

For any dimension vector pair

\underline{\alpha}=(\alpha^{1},\alpha^{0})\in\mathbb{N}\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{N}\mathbb{I}
, similarly as in
[

, Section 3.2]
, we define a closed subvariety

\displaystyle C_{2}(Q,\underline{\alpha})=

\displaystyle\{(x^{1},x^{0},d^{1},d^{0})\in E_{\alpha^{1}}^{\mathrm{nil}}(Q)\times E_{\alpha^{0}}^{\mathrm{nil}}(Q)\times\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\alpha^{1}},\mathbb{C}^{\alpha^{0}})\times\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\alpha^{0}},\mathbb{C}^{\alpha^{1}})\mid

\displaystyle d^{1}_{t(h)}x_{h}^{1}=x_{h}^{0}d^{1}_{s(h)},\quad d^{0}_{t(h)}x_{h}^{0}=x_{h}^{1}d^{0}_{s(h)},\quad d_{i}^{1}d_{i}^{0}=0,\quad d_{i}^{0}d_{i}^{1}=0\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{H},i\in\mathbb{I}\}.

where

\displaystyle[X]

, define a constructible subset

then

Consider the diagram
