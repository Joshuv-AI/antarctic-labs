Global well-posedness for nonlinear generalized Camassa-Holm equation
Nesibe Ayhan, Nilay Duruk Mutlubaş, Bao Quoc Tang

Abstract
We establish local and global well-posedness for the Cauchy problem of a generalized Camassa-Holm equation where orders of the momentum and the nonlinearity can be arbitrarily high. More precisely, we consider the equation m t + m x u p + b m u p − 1 u x = − ( g ( u ) ) x + ( b + 1 ) u p u x , m = ( 1 − ∂ x 2 ) k u , m_{t}+m_{x}u^{p}+bmu^{p-1}u_{x}=-(g(u))_{x}+(b+1)u^{p}u_{x},\quad m=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{k}u, where p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 , k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 are arbitrary, b b is a real parameter, and g ⁡ ( u ) g(u) is a smooth function. The local well-posedness is shown by using Kato’s semigroup approach, where we treat the nonlinearity directly using commutator estimates and the fractional Leibniz rule without having to transform it in any specific differential form. This well-posedness is obtained in the phase space H s H^{s} for s > 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) + 3 / 2 s>2(k-1)+3/2 , which is consistent with the results for the classical Camassa-Holm equation. We also prove the global existence of solutions by obtaining conserved quantity and applying the same idea from our local theory.

Introduction

The Camassa-Holm (CH) equation is a fundamental model in the study of nonlinear waves. It is usually written as

(1.1)

m_{t}+2u_{x}m+um_{x}=0,\quad\text{with}\quad m=u-u_{xx}.

This equation possesses a deeply rich mathematical structure, which includes special solutions called peakons and the phenomenon of wave breaking. It was introduced by Camassa and Holm
[
CH93
]
to model the unidirectional propagation of shallow water waves over a flat bottom. Over the years, there has been many studies this equation, see e.g.
[
Con01
,

,
CM99
,
JZ22
,
RB01
]
and its various generalizations the literature, see e.g.
[
CdR23
,
DA19
,
DHH02
,
GLT08
,
ELY06
,
ELY07
,
GLT08
,
JZ21
,
Nov09
]
and references therein.

The first approach is to generalize the momentum density
m
. The standard CH equation uses
m=u-u_{xx}
, and the common higher order generalization is to consider
m=(I-\partial_{x}^{2})^{k}
for
k\geq 1
. Note that this generalization can be derived from an abstract Euler flow with a suitable
H^{k}
metric, see e.g. McLachlan & Zhang
[
MZ09
]
. The analysis of such modified CH equation has been studied in many works, see e.g.
[
MZZ11
,
TZX11
,
TL15
]
for single equation or
[
CZ17
]
for coupled systems. There are also other generalizations for the Camassa-Holm equation. For instance, the
\mu-
Camassa-Holm equation uses the momentum density
m=\mu(u)-u_{xx}
, where
\mu
is the mean of
u
, which was introduced in
[
KLM08
]
and has been studied in other works like
[
FLQ12
]
and
[
Yam20
]
. Another example is the
\alpha-
Camassa-Holm equation, which uses
m=u-\alpha^{2}u_{xx}
, where
\alpha
is a constant. This was considered by Holm, Naraigh & Tronci
[
HNT09
]
. In a previous work D. Mutlubaş & Ayhan
[
DMA25
]
, a general differential operator
L
was used to study such dispersive generalizations.

The second approach is to consider general nonlinearity in the equation, that is, instead of the term
2u_{x}m+um_{x}
, which corresponds to
p=1
in (
1.2
), one can study a more general form
bu^{p-1}u)xm+u^{p}m_{x}
with an arbitrary power
p\geq 1
. This generalized family includes several notable integrable equations as particular cases, such as the Degasperis-Procesi equation (with
p=1
,
b=3
)
[
CL09
]
and the Novikov equation (with
p=2,b=3
)
[
ZL25
]
. For the case of

in the operator, Zhou & Ji
[
ZJ22
]
established well-posedness and wave breaking for this class of equations with general
p\geq 1
using Kato’s semigroup approach.

Although there are many studies that consider these two directions of generalization, they have typically been developed separately. Our work fills this gap by combining these two directions. More precisely, we study the general case where a higher-order operator and a generalized nonlinearity are present at the same time, allowing us to analyze a much wider class of equations under a single framework. In this work, we investigate the Cauchy problem for the following generalized Camassa-Holm equation

(1.2)

m_{t}+m_{x}u^{p}+bmu^{p-1}u_{x}=-(g(u))_{x}+(b+1)u^{p}u_{x},\quad m=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{k}u

where
p\geq 1
,
k\geq 1
are arbitrary,
b
is a real parameter, and
g(u)
is a smooth function.

Our first objective is to prove local well-posedness for this general equation using Kato’s semigroup theory:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{s},s>2(k-1)+3/2
be given. Then, there exists a maximal time of existence
T>0
, depending on
u_{0}
, such that there is a unique solution
u
satisfying

u\in C([0,T);H^{s})\cap C^{1}([0,T);H^{s-1}).

Moreover, the solution
u(t,x)
depends continuously on the initial data, i.e., the solution mapping

\displaystyle\Psi:H^{s}

\displaystyle\mapsto C([0,T);H^{s})\cap C^{1}([0,T);H^{s-1})

\displaystyle u_{0}

\displaystyle\mapsto u(\cdot,u_{0})

is continuous.

We prove this theorem by rewriting the equation in the quasi-linear form

u_{t}+A(u)u=f(u),

then applying Kato’s semigroup method. This is done by verifying key properties of the operator
A(u)
and the nonlinearity
f(u)
. In many previous studies, a standard technique involved a tricky manipulation of the equation to express the nonlinear term
f(u)
as a single spatial derivative of a certain
F
,
f(u)=\partial_{x}(F(u))
. This reformulation is highly effective, as it significantly simplifies the proof of the Lipschitz estimates needed for
f(u)
. For equations where either
k=1
or
p=1
, such a manipulation is indeed possible. However, since we combine both
k>1
and
p>1
, the algebraic structure becomes too complicated and it seems not possible to manipulate the terms into this convenient
\partial_{x}(F(u))
form.

Our work therefore takes a different approach. The core of our idea lies in breaking down these terms directly and using the fractional Leibniz rule and some commutator estimates. This technique is particularly helpful because it allows us to eliminate the highest-order derivatives that would otherwise be problematic. This strategy of using commutators to handle higher order derivative terms was used in the recent work of D. Mutlubaş & Ayhan
[
DMA25
]
, which we extend here to address the more general structure. Through this direct and systematic approach, we establish all necessary bounds for the local well-posedness proof in the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>2(k-1)+3/2
. For the standard CH equation where
k=1
, this regularity requirement reduces to
s>3/2
, demonstrating that our result aligns naturally with the classical theory. Moreover, this result is also a clear improvement over the previous work
[
DMA25
]
, which required the stronger regularity
s>2(k-1)+5/2
.

Our second objective is to establish the global existence of solutions when
b=p+1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k\geq 2,p\geq 1
, and
b=p+1
. Assume that
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>2(k-1)+3/2
, and
m_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, the Cauchy problem for the generalized Camassa-Holm equation (
1.2
) admits a unique global solution

u\in C([0,\infty),H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty),H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R})),

and the solution depends continuously on the initial data.

We achieve this by identifying a conserved quantity that provides a uniform control on the solution, which explains the requirement
p=b+1
. To extend this global persistence to higher regularity Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
with

s>k
, we utilize the idea from our local theory. The commutator estimates and fractional Leibniz rule allow us to control the growth of the
H^{s}
-norm, leading to a global bound via Gronwall’s lemma.

: In Section
2

Proof of Assumption (A2):

Lemma 2.9
.

Let the operator
A(u)=u^{p}\partial_{x}
with

, where

\lambda_{1}
is a constant independent of

Proof.

For given

, we have

\displaystyle\|(A(u)-A(v))w\|_{H^{s-1}}=\|(u^{p}-v^{p})\partial_{x}w\|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|u^{p}-v^{p}\|_{H^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{H^{s-1}},

\displaystyle=c\left\|(u-v)\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}u^{p-1-j}v^{j}\right\|_{H^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{H^{s-1}},

\displaystyle\leq c\|u-v\|_{H^{s-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\|u\|_{H^{s-1}}^{p-1-j}\|v\|_{H^{s-1}}^{i}\|w\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\|u-v\|_{H^{s-1}}\|w\|_{H^{s}},

is an algebra for the estimates. Then, take

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u
be the local solution obtained in Theorem
1.1
in the maximal interval
(0,T_{\max})
and assume that
b=p+1
. Then we have the following conversation law

\displaystyle I_{1}(t):=\int u(t)m(t)\ dx=\int u_{0}m_{0}dx\quad\text{ for all }t\in(0,T_{\max}).

Proof.

Compute

\frac{d}{dt}\int I_{1}\ dx=\int(u_{t}m+um_{t})\ dx.

From the definition of
m=(I-\partial_{x}^{2})^{k}u
and integration by parts we have

\int u_{t}m\ dx=\int u_{t}(I-\partial_{x}^{2})^{k}u\ dx=\int(I-\partial_{x}^{2})^{k}u_{t}u\ dx=\int m_{t}u\ dx.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}I_{1}

\displaystyle=2\int um_{t}\ dx

\displaystyle=2\int u(-m_{x}u^{p}-bmu^{p-1}u_{x}-(g(u))_{x}+(b+1)u^{p}u_{x})\ dx

\displaystyle=-2\int u^{p+1}m_{x}\ dx-2b\int u^{p}u_{x}m\ dx-2\int u(g(u))_{x}\ dx+2(b+1)\int u^{p+1}u_{x}\ dx.

For the terms on the right hand side, we evaluate them as

\int u^{p+1}m_{x}\ dx=-\int(u^{p+1})_{x}m\ dx=-\int(p+1)u^{p}u_{x}m\ dx,

-\int u(g(u))_{x}\ dx=\int\frac{d}{dx}G(u)\ dx=0,

where
G(u)
is an antiderivative of
g
, and

(b+1)\int u^{p+1}u_{x}\ dx=\frac{b+1}{p+1}\int(u^{p+1})_{x}\ dx=0.

Collecting these terms, we have

\frac{d}{dt}I_{1}=2(p+1-b)\int u^{p}u_{x}m\ dx=0

due to the assumption
b=p+1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem

, there exists a constant

such that for

Moreover, by Sobolev embedding, we have

The conservation of
I_{1}
implies that
I_{1}(t)=I_{1}(0)
for all

. Since

|(*_{2})|=|(\Lambda^{s}u,[\Lambda^{s},u^{p}]u_{x})_{L^{2}}|\leq\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\|[\Lambda^{s},u^{p}]u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}=C\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Thus we get the desired estimate
|(*)|\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
for
(*)
. Now we turn to the second term
(**)
, which fulfills

|(**)|=|(\Lambda^{s}u,\Lambda^{s}f(u))|\leq\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|\Lambda^{s}f(u)\|_{L^{2}}.

It remains to show

(3.3)

\|\Lambda^{s}f(u)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

Recall the formulation of
f(u)
in (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}f(u)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\underbrace{\|\Lambda^{s-2k}([\Gamma,u^{p}]u_{x})\|_{L^{2}}}_{(**_{1})}+b\underbrace{\|\Lambda^{s-2k}(u^{p-1}u_{x}\Gamma u)\|_{L^{2}}}_{(**_{2})}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\|\Lambda^{s-2k}((g(u))_{x})\|_{L^{2}}}_{(**_{3})}+(b+1)\underbrace{\|\Lambda^{s-2k}(u^{p}u_{x})\|_{L^{2}}}_{(**_{4})}.

We estimate each term separately:

i)

Estimate of
(**_{1})
: We have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s-2k}[\Gamma,u^{p}]u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\Lambda^{s-2k}\big(\Gamma(u^{p}u_{x})-u^{p}\Gamma(u_{x})\big)\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle=\left\|\Lambda^{s-2k}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}(u^{p}u_{x})-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\right)\right\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \sum_{j=0}^{k}\left\|\Lambda^{s-2k}\big(\partial_{x}^{2j}(u^{p}u_{x})-u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\big)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|\partial_{x}^{2j}(u^{p}u_{x})-u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\right\|_{H^{s-2k}},

where we used the binomial formula for the
\Gamma
and the fact that when
j=0
,
\partial_{x}^{2j}(u^{p}u_{x})=u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u
. Let’s divide into cases:

(a)

For the case
1\leq j\leq k-1
, the terms are lower order and can be controlled using Sobolev embeddings and the fact that
u\in H^{s}
with
s>2k-\frac{1}{2}
. Each term involves at most
2j+1\leq 2k-1
derivatives, which is less than
s
, so the estimates are straightforward using product rules and the boundedness of
\|u\|_{H^{2k}}
from the second conserved quantity. More precisely,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{x}^{2j}(u^{p}u_{x})-u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\right\|_{H^{s-2k}}\lesssim\|u^{p}u_{x}\|_{H^{s-2k+2j}}+\|u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}.

If
s-2k>1/2
, then we estimate (
3.4
) further as

\displaystyle\|u^{p}u_{x}\|_{H^{s-2k+2j}}+\|u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{p+1}\|_{H^{s-2k+2j+1}}+\|u^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}+\|u^{p}\|_{H^{s-2k}}\|\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{s-2k+2j+1}}+\|u\|_{H^{s-2k+2j+1}}+\|u\|_{W^{2j+1,\infty}}C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{s-2k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}

where we used
\|u\|_{W^{2j+1,\infty}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{2j+2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{2k}}\leq C(t)
. If
1/2\geq s-2k
, we choose
\alpha=s-2k
and
\beta=1/2+\delta
for a sufficiently small
\delta>0
. It can be easily checked that
\beta\geq\alpha>-\beta
, and thus we can apply the fractional Leibniz rule in Lemma
2.1
with
f=u^{p}
and
g=\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u
to estimate

\displaystyle\|u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{p}\|_{H^{1/2+\delta}}\|\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{1/2+\delta}}\|u\|_{H^{s-2k+2j+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

From both two cases, we conclude the estimate for (
3.4
)

\left\|\partial_{x}^{2j}(u^{p}u_{x})-u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u\right\|_{H^{s-2k}}\lesssim C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

(b)

For the case
j=k
, we need to estimate

\displaystyle T_{k}

\displaystyle:=\partial_{x}^{2k}(u^{p}u_{x})-u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2k+1}u.

By writing
\partial_{x}^{2k}(u^{p}u_{x})=\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2k-1}(u^{p}u_{x}))
and

u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2k+1}u=\partial_{x}\big[u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2k}u-\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\partial_{x}(u^{p})\big]+\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\partial_{xx}(u^{p})

we have

\displaystyle\|T_{k}\|_{H^{s-2k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1}(u^{p}u_{x})-u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2k}u+\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\partial_{x}(u^{p})\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\partial_{xx}(u^{p})\|_{H^{s-2k}}\lesssim(C)+(D).

For the part
(D)
, we use Lemma
2.4

\displaystyle(D)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{xx}(u^{p})\|_{H^{s-2k}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}\|\partial_{xx}(u^{p})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{2k}}\|u^{p}\|_{H^{s-2k+2}}+\|u\|_{H^{s-1}}\|u^{p}\|_{H^{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}

since
\|u\|_{H^{3}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{2k}}\leq C(t)
. For the term
(C)
we use product rule and Lemma
2.4
again to estimate

\displaystyle(C)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}\binom{2k-1}{j}\partial_{x}^{2k-1-j}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{j+1}u-u^{p}\partial_{x}^{2k}u+\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\partial_{x}(u^{p})\right\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{2k-2}\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1-j}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{j+1}u\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{2k-2}\left(\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1-j}(u^{p})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{j+1}u\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1-j}(u^{p})\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}\|\partial_{x}^{j+1}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{2k-2}\left(\|u^{p}\|_{H^{2k-j}}\|u\|_{H^{s-2k+j+2}}+\|u^{p}\|_{H^{s-j}}\|u\|_{H^{j+2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{2k}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

Therefore, we have

(**_{1})\lesssim C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

ii)

Estimate of
(**_{2})
: We expand
\Gamma
to have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s-2k}(u^{p-1}u_{x}\Gamma u)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{p-1}u_{x}\Gamma u\|_{H^{s-2k}},

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}u\Big\|_{H^{s-2k}},

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{2j}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{2k}u\|_{H^{s-2k}},

\displaystyle=(E)+(F).

Applying the fractional product estimate in Lemma
2.4
to
(E)
yields

\displaystyle(E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{2j}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2j}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\|_{H^{s-2k}}\|\partial_{x}^{2j}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\|u^{p}\|_{H^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{s-2k+2j}+\|u^{p}\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}\|u\|_{H^{2j+1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{2k}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

It remains to estimate
(F)
, where we exploit

\partial_{x}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{2k}u=\partial_{x}[\partial_{x}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u]-\partial_{xx}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u

and Lemma
2.4
to get

\displaystyle(F)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}+\|\partial_{xx}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{p})\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\partial_{xx}(u^{p})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\|_{H^{s-2k}}+\|\partial_{xx}(u^{p})\|_{H^{s-2k}}\|\partial_{x}^{2k-1}u\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{p}\|_{H^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}+\|u^{p}\|_{H^{s-2k+2}}\|u\|_{H^{2k}}+\|u^{p}\|_{H^{3}}\|u\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|u\|_{H^{s-2k+2}}\|u\|_{H^{2k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u\|_{H^{2k}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

Combining the estimates of
(E)
and
(F)
, we obtain

(**_{2})\leq C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

iii)

Estimates of
(**_{3})
and
(**_{4})
. These are straightforward thanks to

\|\Lambda^{s-2k}(g(u))_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g(u)\|_{H^{s-2k+1}}\leq C(\|u\|_{\infty})\|u\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}},

by Lemma
2.3
, and the same for
(**_{4})
.

Combining all these estimates, we get the desired estimate (
3.3
). Thus, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C(t)\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

which yields, due to Gronwall’s lemma

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq\exp\Big(\int^{t}C(r)dr\Big)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

which concludes the global existence.
∎
