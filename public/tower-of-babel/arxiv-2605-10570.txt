Existence of Positive Solutions to Semilinear Equations with Sublinear Nonlinearities and Compact Positivity-Improving Resolvent


Abstract
. We prove a Brézis–Oswald type existence theorem for positive solutions of semilinear equations in an abstract setting in which the underlying linear operator has a compact positivity-improving resolvent. The assumptions imposed on the sublinear nonlinearity are comparable to those used in the classical elliptic theory, but no additional regularizing properties of the resolvent, such as ultracontractivity or smoothing, are required. The proof combines the method of sub- and supersolutions with spectral ideas of Brézis–Oswald type and several new arguments adapted to the abstract operator-theoretic framework. In this way, the paper provides a bridge between order-theoretic methods and the classical energy-based theory of sublinear problems.

1.
Introduction

Positive solutions of semilinear elliptic equations have been studied by several classical
methods, each emphasizing a different mechanism of existence. Among them are the method of
sub- and supersolutions and monotone iteration
[
1
,
26
]
, fixed-point
theorems on cones and the abstract theory of ordered Banach spaces
[
2
,
18
,
23
]
, approaches based on asymptotic linearity, principal eigenvalues, and
spectral radius
[
16
,
17
,
25
]
, as well as methods relying on fixed-point index,
topological degree, and
a priori
estimates

[
9
,
20
,
23
,
30
]
. In the
sublinear setting, the paper of Brézis and Oswald
[
8
]
is a particularly
important point of reference, mainly because of its method, which provides a remarkably
effective variational treatment of the existence problem in the presence of the trivial branch.

The purpose of the present paper is to establish a Brézis–Oswald type existence theorem in an
abstract operator-theoretic setting, under essentially no assumptions on the resolvent beyond
those required by the basic Krein–Rutman theory. More precisely, we study positive solutions
on a Lusin measure space
(E,\mathcal{B},m)
to the following equation

(1.1)

-Lu=f(x,u)\quad\text{in }E,

where
L
is the generator of a strongly continuous positive semigroup on
L^{p}(E;m)
,
p\geq 1
,
whose resolvent is compact and positivity improving, and
f
is
a Carathéodory nonlinearity of asymptotically sublinear type.
Our main result shows that the existence of a strictly positive solution to (
1.1
) is governed by suitable
generalized lower and upper asymptotic slopes of
f(x,u)/u
at zero and at infinity.
More precisely, if these quantities are denoted by
a_{0}(\cdot)
and

a_{\infty}(\cdot)
, then the condition

(1.2)

\lambda_{1}(a_{0})<0<\lambda_{1}(a_{\infty})

implies the existence of a strictly positive solution to (
1.1
), where
\lambda_{1}(a_{0}),\lambda_{1}(a_{\infty})

are the principal eigenvalues of the operator
-L
perturbed by
a_{0},a_{\infty}
, respectively.
Thus the familiar Brézis–Oswald
criterion
[
8
]
survives in a framework that is no longer tied to a concrete symmetric elliptic differential
operator, a smooth domain, or pointwise boundary arguments.

The main point is that essentially the same level of generality for the nonlinearity

f
as in the classical sublinear theory can be treated without symmetry, without any
additional smoothing property of the semigroup or resolvent, and without geometric information
on the space
E
. Such additional assumptions permit minimization techniques and often imply
boundedness of solutions, or at least provide enough regularization to reduce the nonlinear
analysis to a bounded range of values. No such mechanism is available here in general.
Consequently, the standard nonlinear solution operator
u\mapsto Rf(\cdot,u)
, where
R:=(-L)^{-1}
),
cannot, in general, be treated even as a locally defined compact self-map on a cone, which prevents
a direct application of standard cone fixed-point, degree,
or index arguments
[
3
,
15
,
18
]
.

The novelty of the paper lies not only in the results themselves, but also in the method of proof.
We make extensive use of supermedian functions, that is, non-negative functions satisfying
\alpha R_{\alpha}\gamma\leq\gamma
for
\alpha>0
,
a notion well known in potential theory. The proof combines several ingredients of independent interest.
In the first part of the paper, we additionally assume that the semigroup
(T_{t})
is sub-Markovian, that is,

T_{t}1\leq 1
. Under this assumption, we first establish a Deny-type compactness result for supermedian functions,
assuming only compactness of the resolvent. We then derive a Kato-type inequality for sub-Markovian semigroups with compact
positivity-improving resolvent, using a probabilistic representation by right Markov processes.
Finally, we develop a method of sub- and supersolutions for equations of the form

(1.3)

u=\gamma+Rg(\cdot,u),

where
\gamma
is a supermedian function.
Finally, we show that the sub-Markovian restriction can be removed by means of a
Doob transform built from the principal eigenfunction. In this way the existence theorem is
extended from the sub-Markovian situation to the full positivity-improving setting considered
here. The abstract approach also leads naturally to uniqueness results. Under additional monotonicity
assumptions on the quotient
u\mapsto f(x,u)/u
, we prove that positive solutions are unique.

It should be emphasized that a central role in the sub-Markovian part of the analysis is played by
the realization result (see
Remark
3.5
) proved by Beznea, Boboc, and Röckner in
[
6
]
. This result makes it possible to
represent the
L^{p}
-resolvent as the resolvent of a right Markov process on a larger Lusin
space while preserving the analytic content of the original problem. This bridge is essential. It
opens the way to probabilistic tools, in particular Itô–Meyer and Doob–Meyer-type arguments,
while remaining fully compatible with the
L^{p}(E;m)
framework in which the
equation is posed. In this manner, supermedian functions can be lifted to excessive versions,
represented probabilistically, and then transferred back to the analytic setting in a form suitable
for the nonlinear analysis.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
collects the analytic preliminaries
and spectral facts used throughout the paper.
Section
3
recalls the probabilistic background on right Markov processes and the realization result used in the sequel.
Section
4
establishes a Deny-type compactness theorem for sub-Markovian semigroups,
while Section
5
is devoted to a Kato inequality in the same setting. In Section
6
we develop the method of sub- and supersolutions
for abstract nonlinear resolvent equations. Section
7
contains the main existence theorem in the sub-Markovian case.
In Section
8
we remove the sub-Markovian assumption by means of a Doob transform and obtain the general existence result.
The final section is devoted to the uniqueness.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we work on a Lusin measurable space
(E,\mathcal{A})
equipped with a finite measure

.
Let us recall that
(E,\mathcal{A})

, and a bijection

\phi

are measurable.
We fix

and denote by

the space of all
\mathcal{A}
-measurable real functions

on
E
such that

is integrable, modulo the usual
m
-a.e. equivalence relation.
For a measurable function

,

means
m(\{u>0\})>0
and
m(\{u<0\})=0
, while

means
m(\{u\leq 0\})=0
.
Recall that a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S_{t})

is called irreducible if

and

are the only closed ideals
that are invariant under all the operators
S_{t},t\geq 0
. Let
(S_{t})
be a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}(E;m)

with generator
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
and resolvent
(G_{\alpha})
. By

we denote the spectral bound of

We say that
(G_{\alpha})
is

if, whenever
f>0
,

, and
\alpha>s(A)
one has
G_{\alpha}f\gg 0
.
By
[
7
, Proposition 14.10]
a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S_{t})
on

with a generator
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
is irreducible if and only if its resolvent
(G_{\alpha})
is positivity improving.

In the following proposition we collect some known results from spectral theory that shall be frequently used in the sequel.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(S_{t})
be a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}(E;m)
that generates an operator
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))

with compact positivity-improving resolvent.
Then the following hold:

(i)

\sigma(A)\neq\emptyset
, hence
s(A)>-\infty
.

(ii)

The number
\lambda_{1}(-A):=-s(A)
is an eigenvalue of
-A
.

(iii)

There exists
u\in\mathscr{D}(A)
such that
u\gg 0
and
-Au=\lambda_{1}(-A)u
.

(iv)

The geometric multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(-A)
is one.

(v)

There exists
f\in\mathscr{D}(A^{*})
such that
f\gg 0
and
-A^{*}f=\lambda_{1}(-A)f
.

(vi)

If
u\in\mathscr{D}(A)
is positive and non-trivial, and
-Au=\lambda u
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
then
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(-A)
.

Proof.

The assertion (i) is a consequence of a result of de Pagter
[
10
, Theorem 3]
.
For (ii) see
[
7
, Corollary 12.9]
.
The assertion (iii) follows from the Krein-Rutman theorem (see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 12.15]
)
and the positivity-improving property of the resolvent. The assertions (iv) and (v) follow from

[
7
, Proposition 14.12(d)]
(note that by
[
12
, Chapter IV, Corollary 1.19]
, the spectrum of an operator with compact resolvent consists only of poles of the resolvent of finite algebraic multiplicity). For (vi) see e.g.
[
28
, Theorem V.5.2(iv)]
.
∎

In what follows, any pair
(\lambda_{1}(-A),u)
as in Proposition
2.1
will be called a principal eigenpair associated with
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
;
here
\lambda_{1}(-A)
is the principal eigenvalue and
u
is a principal eigenfunction. Similarly, any pair

(\lambda_{1}(-A),f)
as in Proposition
2.1
will be called a dual principal eigenpair associated with
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
, and

will be called a dual principal eigenfunction.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
(A,\mathscr{D}(A)
) generate a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S_{t})
on
L^{p}(E;m)
.
Assume that
A
has compact and positivity-improving resolvent and
a\in L^{\infty}(E;m)
.
Then the operator
A-a

generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S_{t}^{a})
on
L^{p}(E;m)
that
has compact positivity-improving resolvent.
Finally, if
a_{1},a_{2}\in L^{\infty}(E;m)
and

a_{1}\leq a_{2}

m
-a.e., then

\lambda_{1}(-A+a_{1})\leq\lambda_{1}(-A+a_{2}).

Proof.

Denote by
M_{a}
the bounded
multiplication operator
M_{a}f=af
. By the bounded perturbation theorem,

A-M_{a}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}(E;m)
; see

[
12
, Theorem III.1.3]
.
Since the semigroup generated by
-M_{a}
is the positive multiplication semigroup

f\mapsto e^{-ta}f
, positivity of
(S_{t}^{a})_{t\geq 0}
follows from the Trotter product
formula, see
[
12
, Corollary III.5.8]
.
To prove irreducibility of
(S_{t}^{a})_{t\geq 0}
, note that

A-a=(A-\|a\|_{\infty})+M_{\|a\|_{\infty}-a},\qquad M_{\|a\|_{\infty}-a}\geq 0.

Hence, by
[
12
, Corollary VI.1.11(i)]
,

0\leq e^{-\|a\|_{\infty}t}S_{t}\leq S_{t}^{a},\qquad t\geq 0.

Therefore every closed ideal invariant under
(S_{t}^{a})
is also invariant under

(S_{t})
, and since
(S_{t})
is irreducible, so is
(S_{t}^{a})
.
Compactness of the resolvent of
A-a
follows from the resolvent identity

R(\lambda,A-a)=R(\lambda,A)\bigl(I-M_{a}R(\lambda,A)\bigr)^{-1},\qquad\lambda\in\rho(A)\cap\rho(A-a).

Since
R(\lambda,A)
is compact and
\bigl(I-M_{a}R(\lambda,A)\bigr)^{-1}
is bounded,

R(\lambda,A-a)
is compact.
If now
a_{1}\leq a_{2}

m
-a.e., then

A-a_{1}=(A-a_{2})+M_{a_{2}-a_{1}},\qquad M_{a_{2}-a_{1}}\geq 0.

By monotonicity of the spectral bound under positive bounded perturbations,

[
12
, Corollary VI.1.11(ii)]
,

s(A-a_{2})\leq s(A-a_{1}),

which, combined with Proposition
2.1
(ii), completes the proof.
∎

3.
Right Markov processes and potential theory

Recall that a topological space is called Lusin if it is homeomorphic to a
Borel subset of a Polish space.
Let
Y
be a Lusin topological space.
We set
Y_{\partial}:=Y\cup\{\partial\}
, where
\partial
is an isolated point, and
denote by
\mathcal{B}(Y_{\partial})
the
\sigma
-algebra of all Borel subsets of
Y_{\partial}
.
For
B\in\mathcal{B}(Y_{\partial})
, we denote
\mathcal{B}(B):=\{C\in\mathcal{B}(Y_{\partial}):C\subset B\}
.
We always assume that any numerical (i.e.
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}\cup\{-\infty\}
valued) function on
Y
is automatically extended to
Y_{\partial}
by setting
it equal to zero at
\partial
.
We use the same notation
\mathcal{B}(B)
for the set of all numerical
\mathcal{B}(B)
-measurable functions
on
B
.

A subset
A
of
Y_{\partial}
is called universally measurable
provided that for every positive finite measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{B}(Y_{\partial})

there exist
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(Y_{\partial})
such that
A_{1}\subset A\subset A_{2}

and
\mu\left(A_{2}\backslash A_{1}\right)=0
.
The set of all universally measurable subsets of
B
is a
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
B

denoted by
\mathcal{B}^{u}(B)
.
It is straightforward that any
\sigma
-finite positive measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{B}(B)
may be extended
uniquely to a
\sigma
-finite positive measure on
\mathcal{B}^{u}(B)
.
If
\mathcal{H}
is any collection of numerical functions, then
p\mathcal{H}
denotes the
set of nonnegative functions in
\mathcal{H}
and
b\mathcal{H}
denotes the set of bounded functions in

\mathcal{H}
.
It is readily verified that a positive numerical function

on

belongs to

if and only if for every finite measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{B}(B)
there exist

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space.
A function

(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
with state space

if for any

the mapping

is
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{B}(Y_{\partial})
-measurable,

X(\infty,\omega)=\partial
for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
and the following property holds for any
\omega\in\Omega
: if for some

,
X(t_{0},\omega)=\partial
, then
X(t,\omega)=\partial

for any
t\geq t_{0}
. In what follows we use the notation

for any stochastic process

. The function
\zeta:\Omega\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
defined by

\zeta(\omega)=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}(\omega)=\partial\}

is called the lifetime of the stochastic process
X
.

A stochastic process
X
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
with state space
Y

is called right-continuous if for every
\omega\in\Omega
the function

is right continuous on
[0,\infty)
.

A filtration on a measurable space

is a family
\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
of
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{F}_{t}

included in
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{s}\subset\mathcal{F}_{t}

.
We let

.
For every

we put
\mathcal{F}_{t+}=\bigcap_{s>t}\mathcal{F}_{s}
. A filtration
\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}

is called right-continuous provided that
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\mathcal{F}_{t+}
for all
t\geq 0
.
A stochastic process
X
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})

with state space
Y
is called
\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)
-adapted
provided that the map
X_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}/\mathcal{B}(Y_{\partial})
-measurable for any
t\geq 0
.

A transition function on
Y_{\partial}
is a family of kernels
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}

on
(Y_{\partial},\mathcal{B}^{u}(Y_{\partial}))

such that
P_{t}1=1
, and
P_{t}(P_{s}f)=P_{s+t}f

for all
f\in p\mathcal{B}^{u}(Y_{\partial})
and

.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space and

.

and

4.
Deny’s theorem for sub-Markovian semigroups

In what follows, for a given Banach space

and

. Note that throughout the paper the space

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a positive function

is supermedian if

for any

A simple example of a supermedian function is

for any positive

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
(T_{t})
is sub-Markovian. Then, for any concave function

and any supermedian function

, the function
\phi(u)
is supermedian.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is concave, there exist

such that
\phi(u)\leq au+b,\,u\geq 0
.
Therefore,
\phi(u)\in L^{p}(E;m)
. Furthermore, we have
\phi=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}l_{q}
, where
(l_{q})
is a family of affine functions.
Thus,

T_{t}\phi(u)=T_{t}[\inf_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}l_{q}(u)]\leq\inf_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}T_{t}[l_{q}(u)]\leq\inf_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}l_{q}(u)=\phi(u).

In the last inequality we used the facts that

is supermedian and
(T_{t})
is sub-Markovian. This proves the assertion.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
(T_{t})
is sub-Markovian. For any sequence
(v_{n})
of supermedian functions there exists a subsequence
(n_{k})
such that
(v_{n_{k}})

is convergent a.e.

Proof.

Since
v_{n}\wedge k
are also supermedian, we may assume without loss of generality that
0\leq v_{n}\leq 1
.
Then
\sup_{n\geq 1}\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}
, and hence, after passing to a subsequence,
(v_{n})
converges weakly
⋆

in
\sigma(L^{\infty},L^{1})
. Let us denote the limit by
v
. By the assumptions made, the resolvent of

is compact; hence, after passing to a further

(Rv_{n})
is convergent in

to a function

. We have for any

\langle w,\eta\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle Rv_{n},\eta\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle v_{n},R^{*}\eta\rangle=\langle v,R^{*}\eta\rangle=\langle Rv,\eta\rangle.

Thus,
(Rv_{n})
converges to

in

for any

By the diagonal method one can choose a further subsequence such that

for any

\liminf_{n\to\infty}v_{n}\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\alpha R_{\alpha}v_{n}=\alpha R_{\alpha}v\quad\text{a.e. for }\alpha\geq 0,\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}.

Letting

By

5.

Let us recall some standard notions from probability theory. A stochastic process
(X_{t})

on a probability space

-a.e.

considered in the present paper may not be càdlàg, however, see Lemma

-excessive function

is càdlàg).

Definition 5.1

(ii)

(iii)

\mathbb{E}_{x}M^{x}_{\tau_{k}}=0,\,k\geq 1
. Consequently, by (
5.2
),

u(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}u(X_{\tau_{k}})+\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\tau_{k}}g(X_{s})\,ds.

Letting

, we deduce that
\mathbb{E}_{x}u(X_{\tau_{k}})\searrow 0
, since
u(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(X_{s})\,ds
. This proves (3).
Assertion (4) follows from
[
27
, Theorem 51.7]
, after a standard restriction argument of
\mathbb{X}
to
\{u<\infty\}
; see
[
27
, Theorem 12.23]
).
Finally, (5) follows from
[
4
, Proposition II.2.8,Proposition II.2.2(c)]
.
The property (6) can be found in
[
13
, Lemma 5.2]
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

(T_{t})

Set

, and there exists a right Markov process
\mathbb{X}

5.2

m
-versions

of

are

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
g\in L^{p}(E;m)
be positive and
\varphi\in C^{1}_{b}([0,\infty))
be convex.
Set
u:=Rg
. Then there exists a supermedian function
v

\varphi(u)=\varphi(0)+R(\varphi^{\prime}(u)g)-v.

Proof.

By Remark
3.5
we may assume that
E

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}(E)
,
and there exists a right Markov process
\mathbb{X}
that represents
the semigroup
(T_{t})
(see Definition
3.3
). In particular
w:=Ug=u

5.2
for a.e.

6.
The method of sub- and supersolutions for sub-Markovian semigroups

Now we shall focus on solutions to

(6.1)

u=\gamma+Rg(\cdot,u),

where
\gamma
is a supermedian function and
g:E\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

is a Carathéodory function.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that a function
w\in L^{p}(E;m)
is a subsolution to
(
6.1
)
if
g^{+}(\cdot,w)\in L^{p}(E;m)
and there exists a supermedian function
v

such that

w+Rg^{-}(\cdot,w)=\gamma+Rg^{+}(\cdot,w)-v.

Definition 6.2
.

We say that a function
w\in L^{p}(E;m)
is a supersolution to
(
6.1
)
if
g^{+}(\cdot,w)\in L^{p}(E;m)
and there exists a supermedian function
v

such that

w+Rg^{-}(\cdot,w)=\gamma+Rg^{+}(\cdot,w)+v.

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume that
(T_{t})
is sub-Markovian. If
u,w
are positive subsolutions to (
6.1
), then
u\vee w
is a subsolution to (
6.1
).

Proof.

Since
u
is a subsolution to (
6.1
) if and only if
u_{0}:=u-\gamma

is a subsolution to (
6.1
) with
\gamma=0
and
g
replaced by
g_{0}(x,y):=g(x,y+\gamma(x))
,
we may and will assume without loss of generality that
\gamma=0
.

By the definition of a subsolution to (
6.1
) there exist supermedian functions
v_{1},v_{2}
such that

u=Rg(\cdot,u)-v_{1},\quad w=Rg(\cdot,w)-v_{2}.

By Remark
3.5
we may assume that
E
is a Lusin topological space,

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}(E)
, and there exists a right Markov process
\mathbb{X}
that represents
the semigroup
(T_{t})
(see Definition
3.3
).
We choose
\mathcal{B}(E)
-measurable representatives of the
m
-equivalence classes of
u
and
w

and continue to denote them by
u
and
w
. By Lemma
5.2

there exist
m
-versions
\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{v}_{2}
of
v_{1},v_{2}
, respectively, that are
\mathcal{U}
-excessive.
Let
\tilde{u}:=Ug(\cdot,u)-\tilde{v}_{1}
on the set
\{U|g(\cdot,u)|+\tilde{v}_{1}<\infty\}
and zero on its complement, and

\tilde{w}:=Ug(\cdot,w)-\tilde{v}_{2}
on the set
\{U|g(\cdot,w)|+\tilde{v}_{2}<\infty\}
and zero on its complement.
Set
E_{0}:=\{U|g(\cdot,u)|+\tilde{v}_{1}+U|g(\cdot,w)|+\tilde{v}_{2}<\infty\}
.
By Lemma
5.2
there exist uniformly integrable martingales
M^{u,x},N^{w,x}
, local martingales
M^{v_{1},x},N^{v_{2},x}
,
and positive additive functionals
A,B
such that, for every
x\in E_{0}
,

\begin{gathered}\tilde{u}\left(X_{t}\right)+\tilde{v}_{1}(X_{t})=\tilde{u}\left(X_{0}\right)+\tilde{v}_{1}\left(X_{0}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}g\left(X_{r},u\left(X_{r}\right)\right)dr+\int_{0}^{t}dM^{u,x}_{r},\quad t\geq 0,\,\mathbb{P}_{x}\text{-a.s.},\\
\tilde{w}\left(X_{t}\right)+\tilde{v}_{2}(X_{t})=\tilde{w}\left(X_{0}\right)+\tilde{v}_{2}(X_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}g\left(X_{r},w\left(X_{r}\right)\right)dr+\int_{0}^{t}dN^{w,x}_{r},\quad t\geq 0,\,\mathbb{P}_{x}\text{-a.s.},\\
\tilde{v}_{1}\left(X_{t}\right)=\tilde{v}_{1}\left(X_{0}\right)-A_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}dM^{v_{1},x}_{r},\quad\tilde{v}_{2}\left(X_{t}\right)=\tilde{v}_{2}\left(X_{0}\right)-B_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}dN^{v_{2},x}_{r},\quad t\geq 0,\,\mathbb{P}_{x}\text{-a.s.}\end{gathered}

Set
M^{x}:=M^{u,x}-M^{v_{1},x}
and
N^{x}:=N^{w,x}-N^{v_{2},x}
.
Then for
x\in E_{0}
,

\begin{gathered}\tilde{u}\left(X_{t}\right)=\tilde{u}\left(X_{0}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}g\left(X_{r},u\left(X_{r}\right)\right)dr+A_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}dM^{x}_{r},\quad t\geq 0,\,\mathbb{P}_{x}\text{-a.s.},\\
\tilde{w}\left(X_{t}\right)=\tilde{w}\left(X_{0}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}g\left(X_{r},w\left(X_{r}\right)\right)dr+B_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}dN^{x}_{r},\quad t\geq 0,\,\mathbb{P}_{x}\text{-a.s.}\end{gathered}

By the Itô-Meyer formula (see, e.g.,
[
24
, IV.Theorem 70]
)
and Lemma
5.2
there exists a positive additive functional
C
such that

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{t}\right)=

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{0}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r}\geq\tilde{w}(X)_{r}\}}g\left(X_{r},u\left(X_{r}\right)\right)\,dr+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}\geq\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}dA_{r}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}\geq\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dM^{x}_{r}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}<\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}g\left(X_{r},w\left(X_{r}\right)\right)\,dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}<\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dB_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}<\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dN^{x}_{r}+C_{t},\quad t\geq 0,\,\mathbb{P}_{x}\text{-a.s.}

for
x\in E_{0}
.
By (
3.1
),
Ug(\cdot,u)=Ug(\cdot,\tilde{u})
a.e. and
Ug(\cdot,w)=Ug(\cdot,\tilde{w})
a.e. Consequently,
for a.e.
x\in E
,

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{t}\right)=

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{0}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}g\left(X_{r},(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{r}\right)\right)\,dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}\geq\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dA_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}<\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dB_{r}+C_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}\geq\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dM^{x}_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}<\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dN^{x}_{r},\quad t\geq 0,\mathbb{P}_{x}\text{-a.s. }

Let
(\tau_{k})
be a localizing sequence for
N^{x}
and
M^{x}
that consists of terminal times
(take e.g.
\tau_{k}:=\inf\{t>0:\tilde{v}_{1}(X_{t})+\tilde{v}_{2}(X_{t})\geq k\}
, see the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 51.1]
). Then substituting
t\mapsto\tau_{k}

and applying
\mathbb{E}_{x}
to both sides of the above equality gives

(6.2)

\begin{split}(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})(x)=&\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{\tau_{k}}\right)+\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\tau_{k}}g\left(X_{r},(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{r}\right)\right)\,dr\\
&-\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\tau_{k}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}\geq\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dA_{r}-\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\tau_{k}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}<\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dB_{r}-\mathbb{E}_{x}C_{\tau_{k}}\text{ a.e. }\end{split}

Observe that

(6.3)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{\tau_{k}}\right)=-\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tilde{v}_{1}\wedge\tilde{v}_{2})\left(X_{\tau_{k}}\right).

Indeed, for
x\in E_{0}
,

\begin{split}(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})(x)&=(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w})^{+}(x)+\tilde{w}(x)\\
&=\left(Ug(\cdot,u)(x)-Ug(\cdot,w)(x)-\tilde{v}_{1}(x)+\tilde{v}_{2}(x)\right)^{+}+Ug(\cdot,w)(x)-\tilde{v}_{2}(x)\end{split}

On the other hand

-(\tilde{v}_{1}\wedge\tilde{v}_{2})(x)=(-\tilde{v}_{1}(x))\vee(-\tilde{v}_{2}(x))=(-\tilde{v}_{1}(x)+\tilde{v}_{2}(x))^{+}-\tilde{v}_{2}(x),\quad x\in E.

Consequently, for
x\in E_{0}
,

\begin{split}&|(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})(x)+\tilde{v}_{1}(x)\wedge\tilde{v}_{2}(x)|\\
&\quad=\left|\left(Ug(\cdot,u)(x)-Ug(\cdot,w)(x)-\tilde{v}_{1}(x)+\tilde{v}_{2}(x)\right)^{+}-(-\tilde{v}_{1}(x)+\tilde{v}_{2}(x))^{+}\right|\\
&\quad\quad+U|g(\cdot,w)|(x)\leq U|g(\cdot,u)|(x)+2U|g(\cdot,w)|(x).\end{split}

Now using the fact that
E_{0}
is an absorbing set (see Lemma
5.2
(6)) and the strong Markov property of
\mathbb{X}
, we conclude

\mathbb{E}_{x}|(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})(X_{\tau_{k}})+\tilde{v}_{1}(X_{\tau_{k}})\wedge\tilde{v}_{2}(X_{\tau_{k}})|\leq\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{\tau_{k}}^{\infty}|g(X_{s},u(X_{s}))|\,ds+2\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{\tau_{k}}^{\infty}|g(X_{s},w(X_{s}))|\,ds.

The right-hand side of the above inequality tends to zero whenever the integrals are finite; by the assumptions, this holds for
m
-a.e.
x\in E
.
From (
6.3
) and Lemma
5.2
, we infer that
h(x):=-\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})\left(X_{\tau_{k}}\right)

is a supermedian function.
Letting
k\to\infty
in (
6.2
) yields

\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w}=Ug(\cdot,\tilde{u}\vee\tilde{w})-\tilde{v},\quad\text{ a.e., }

where

\tilde{v}(x):=h(x)+\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}\geq\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dA_{r}+\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tilde{u}(X)_{r-}<\tilde{w}(X)_{r-}\}}\,dB_{r}+\mathbb{E}_{x}C_{\infty}.

It is routine to check that each term in the definition of
\tilde{v}
is supermedian (use additivity of
A,B,C
and the Markov property of
\mathbb{X}
).
Therefore,
v
is supermedian, which in turn implies that
u\vee w
is a subsolution to (
6.1
).
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Suppose that there exist positive
\mathcal{A}
-measurable functions
h_{1},h_{2}
on
E
such that

(6.4)

-h_{1}(x)\leq g(x,y)\leq h_{2}(x),\quad x\in E,y\in\mathbb{R},

and assume that
Rh_{1}\in L^{p}(E;m)
and
h_{2}\in L^{p}(E;m)
. Let
\gamma
be a bounded supermedian function.
Then there exists a solution to (
6.1
).

Proof.

Step 1
. Assume additionally that
h_{1}\in L^{p}(E;m)
.
Set
r:=\|\gamma\|+\|R(h_{1}+h_{2})\|
. For
u\in L^{p}(E;m)
, we let

\Phi(u)=\gamma+Rg(\cdot,u).

Observe that by the assumptions made

|\Phi(u)|\leq\gamma+Rh_{1}+Rh_{2}.

Hence

\|\Phi(u)\|\leq r

By (
6.4
) and the fact that
g
is a Carathéodory function and
h_{1},h_{2}\in L^{p}(E;m)
, we easily deduce that
\Phi
is continuous.
The compactness of
\Phi(L^{p}(E;m))
follows easily from (
6.4
),
h_{1},h_{2}\in L^{p}(E;m)
and the compactness of the resolvent
(R_{\alpha})
.
The existence result follows from Schauder’s fixed-point theorem.

Step 2
. By step 1 for any
n\geq 1
there exists a solution
u_{n}\in L^{p}(E;m)
to

(6.5)

u=\gamma+Rg_{n}(\cdot,u),

where
g_{n}(x,y):=g(x,y)\vee(-n)
. Note that
Rg^{+}(\cdot,u_{n})
,
Rg^{-}_{n}(\cdot,u_{n})
are supermedian functions.
Therefore, by Deny’s theorem (see Theorem
4.3
) there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
(n)
, such that
(Rg^{+}(\cdot,u_{n}))

and
(Rg^{-}_{n}(\cdot,u_{n}))
are convergent
m
-a.e., which implies that
u_{n}
is convergent
m
-a.e. to an
\mathcal{A}
-measurable function
u
.
Furthermore, by the right-hand side inequality in (
6.4
), the dominated convergence theorem and the compactness
of the resolvent
(R_{\alpha})
,
(g^{+}(\cdot,u_{n}))
and
(Rg^{+}(\cdot,u_{n}))
are convergent in
L^{p}(E;m)
.
On the other hand, by (
6.4
),
|u_{n}|\leq\gamma+Rh_{1}+Rh_{2}\in L^{p}(E;m)
.
Thus, by the dominated convergence theorem again,
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{p}(E;m)
.
Now, we want to pass to the limit in

(6.6)

u_{n}+Rg^{-}_{n}(\cdot,u_{n})=\gamma+Rg^{+}(\cdot,u_{n}).

To this end, observe that by Kato’s inequality (Proposition
5.3
) for any
M>0
,

(u_{n}-\gamma-M)^{+}+R(\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma>M\}}g_{n}^{-}(\cdot,u_{n}))\leq R(\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma>M\}}g^{+}(\cdot,u_{n})).

From now on, we choose
M>0
such that
m(\{u-\gamma=M\})=0
.
Let
r_{0}(x,dy)
be the kernel that represents the operator
R
(see (
2.2
))
and
N
be the set of
x\in E
for which
u_{n}(x)
does not converge to
u(x)
.
We have
0=(R\mathbf{1}_{N})(x)=r_{0}(x,N)
a.e. Consequently,
u_{n}

converges to
u

r_{0}(x,\cdot)
-a.e. for a.e.
x\in E
.
Thus, by using Fatou’s lemma, we deduce from the preceding inequality
that

R(\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma>M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u))\leq R(\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma>M\}}g^{+}(\cdot,u)).

From this and (
6.4
), we infer that

\begin{split}\int_{E}R|g^{-}(\cdot,u_{n})-g^{-}(\cdot,u)|&\leq\int_{E}R|\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma>M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u_{n})-\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma>M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u)|\\
&\quad+\int_{E}R|\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma\leq M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u_{n})-\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma\leq M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u)|\\
&\leq\int_{E}R\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma>M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u_{n})+\int_{E}R\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma>M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u)\\
&\quad+\int_{E}R(|\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma\leq M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u_{n})-\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma\leq M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u)|)\\
&\leq\int_{E}R\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma>M\}}g^{+}(\cdot,u_{n})+\int_{E}R\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma>M\}}g^{+}(\cdot,u)\\
&\quad+\int_{E}R(|\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma\leq M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u_{n})-\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma\leq M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u)|)\\
&\leq c_{p}\left(\|\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma>M\}}h_{2}\|+\|\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma>M\}}h_{2}\|\right)\\
&\quad+2\int_{E}R(|\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{n}-\gamma\leq M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u_{n})-\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma\leq M\}}g^{-}(\cdot,u)|\wedge h_{1}).\end{split}

By the dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{E}R|g^{-}(\cdot,u_{n})-g^{-}(\cdot,u)|\leq c_{p}\|\mathbf{1}_{\{u-\gamma>M\}}h_{2}\|.

Hence

\int_{E}|Rg^{-}\left(\cdot,u_{n}\right)-Rg^{-}\left(\cdot,u\right)|\to 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Letting
n\to\infty
in (
6.6
) gives the result.
∎

In what follows we impose on
g
the following integrability assumptions:

Assumption (Int):

(i)

if
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
,
u_{1},u_{2}\in L^{p}(E;m)
, and furthermore

,
then the functions

;

(ii)

if
u\in L^{p}(E;m)
, then
g^{+}(\cdot,u)\in L^{p}(E;m)

and if
(u_{n})\subset L^{p}(E;m)
,

5.3
)

0\leq(u_{k}-M)^{+}\leq R(\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{k}>M\}}f_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})).

Consequently,

R(\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{k}>M\}}f^{-}_{k}(u_{k}))\leq R(\mathbf{1}_{\{u_{k}>M\}}f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})).

Thus, by (F1),

\theta(M,\eta):=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\{u_{k}>M\}}f^{-}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})R^{*}\eta\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\{u_{k}>M\}}f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})R^{*}\eta\leq\lambda\int_{\{u>M\}}(h+u)R^{*}\eta.

As a result, for any positive
\eta\in L^{p^{\prime}}(E;m)
,
\lim_{M\to\infty}\theta(M,\eta)=0
. Let us rewrite the equation for
u_{k}
in the following form

\int_{E}u_{k}\eta+\int_{\{u_{k}\leq M\}}f^{-}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})R^{*}\eta+\int_{\{u_{k}>M\}}f^{-}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})R^{*}\eta=\int_{E}f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})R^{*}\eta.

We now take
\limsup_{k\to\infty}
on both sides of the equality. Then applying the dominated convergence theorem
to the second term on the left-hand side (with the aid of (F3)) and the term on the right-hand side (with the aid of (F1)) yields

\int_{E}u\eta+\int_{\{u\leq M\}}f^{-}(\cdot,u)R^{*}\eta+\theta(M,\eta)=\int_{E}f^{+}(\cdot,u)R^{*}\eta

for any positive
\eta\in L^{p^{\prime}}(E;m)
. Now, letting
M\to\infty
gives that
u
is a solution to (
1.1
).

Suppose now that
\sup_{k\geq 1}\|u_{k}\|=\infty
.
Set
c_{k}:=\|u_{k}\|
and
w_{k}:=c_{k}^{-1}u_{k}
.
By Deny’s theorem (see Theorem
4.3
) there exists a subsequence such that
(w_{k})
is convergent a.e.
Indeed,

w_{k}=R[f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})/c_{k}]-R[f^{-}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})/c_{k}],

and both terms on the right-hand side are supermedian. Consequently, by Deny’s theorem (see Theorem
4.3
),
both sequences
(R[f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})/c_{k}])_{k\geq 1}
,
(R[f^{-}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})/c_{k}])_{k\geq 1}
converge a.e. after passing to a subsequence.
Furthermore,

R[f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})/c_{k}]\leq\lambda R[h/c_{k}+u_{k}/c_{k}]\leq\lambda Rh+\lambda Rw_{k},\quad c_{k}\geq 1,

and this implies that the limit of
(R[f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})/c_{k}])_{k\geq 1}
is finite a.e.,
which in turn allows us to conclude that
(w_{k})
is convergent a.e.
Since

w_{k}\leq R[f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})/c_{k}]\leq\lambda Rh+\lambda Rw_{k},\quad c_{k}\geq 1,

we conclude, by using compactness of the resolvent, that
(w_{k}^{p})
is uniformly integrable,
which in turn combined with (F1) implies that

(c_{k}^{-1}f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})+[c_{k}^{-1}f^{-}(\cdot,u_{k})]\wedge\kappa)_{k\geq 1}

is uniformly integrable for any
\kappa>0
.
Let
w:=\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{k}
a.e. By the uniform integrability of
(w_{k}^{p})
, we obtain
\|w\|=1
.
Thus,
w
is non-trivial. We have

\langle w_{k},-L^{*}\varphi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle\leq\langle c_{k}^{-1}f^{+}_{k}(u_{k}),\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\rangle-\langle[c_{k}^{-1}f^{-}(u_{k})]\wedge\kappa,\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\rangle.

Let
A:=\{\sup_{k}u_{k}=\infty\}
. Since the sequence
(c_{k}^{-1}f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})+[c_{k}^{-1}f^{-}(\cdot,u_{k})]\wedge\kappa)_{k\geq 1}

is uniformly integrable, we may apply Fatou’s lemma to the right-hand side of the above inequality and obtain

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\to\infty}&\left[\langle c_{k}^{-1}f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k}),\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\rangle-\langle[c_{k}^{-1}f^{-}(\cdot,u_{k})]\wedge\kappa,\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\rangle\right]\\
&\leq\int_{E}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(c_{k}^{-1}f^{+}_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})-[c_{k}^{-1}f^{-}(\cdot,u_{k})]\wedge\kappa\right)\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{E}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left([c_{k}^{-1}f_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})]\vee(-\kappa)\right)\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{E}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left([w_{k}f_{k}(\cdot,u_{k})/u_{k}]\vee(-\kappa)\right)\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\\
&\leq\langle\mathbf{1}_{A}wa_{\infty}^{\kappa},\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle wa_{\infty}^{\kappa},\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\rangle\end{split}

since
A^{c}\subset\{w=0\}
. Therefore

\langle w,a_{\infty}^{l}\varphi^{\prime}_{1}+\lambda_{1}(a_{\infty}^{l})\varphi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle=\langle w,-L^{*}\varphi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle\leq\langle wa_{\infty}^{\kappa},\varphi_{1}^{\prime}\rangle.

As a result

\lambda_{1}(a_{\infty}^{l})\langle w,\varphi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle\leq 0,

which implies, since
\varphi_{1}^{\prime}
is strictly positive and
w
is positive non-trivial, that
\lambda_{1}(a_{\infty}^{l})\leq 0
, a contradiction.
∎

8.
Main result: the general case

In this section, we prove that Theorem
7.2
remains true without the additional assumption that
(T_{t})
is sub-Markovian.
This generalization relies on the existence of a strictly positive principal eigenfunction with a strictly positive eigenvalue.
This allows us to transform the semigroup into a sub-Markovian one by means of the well-known Doob transform.

Let us consider the following semigroup

(8.1)

T^{\phi_{1}}_{t}(f):=T_{t}(\phi_{1}f)/\phi_{1}

for
f\in L^{p}(E;\bar{m})
, where
\bar{m}:=\phi_{1}^{p}\cdot m
. One easily calculates that
(T^{\phi_{1}}_{t})

is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}(E;\bar{m})
and its resolvent is compact and positivity improving. Observe that

(T^{\phi_{1}}_{t})
generates the operator
(L^{\phi_{1}},\mathscr{D}(L^{\phi_{1}}))
given by

(8.2)

\mathscr{D}(L^{\phi_{1}})=\{u\in L^{p}(E;\bar{m}):u\phi_{1}\in\mathscr{D}(L)\},\quad L^{\phi_{1}}u=L(\phi_{1}u)/\phi_{1},\quad u\in\mathscr{D}(L^{\phi_{1}}).

Remark 8.1
.

The Doob transform also allows one to extend the results of Sections
5
and
6

beyond the sub-Markovian setting.

Theorem 8.2
.

If

(8.3)

\lambda_{1}(a_{0})<0<\lambda_{1}(a_{\infty}),

then there exists a solution to (
1.1
).

Proof.

Observe that if

solves

-L^{\phi_{1}}v=f^{\phi_{1}}(\cdot,v),\quad v\gg 0,

Consequently, in order to get the existence result for (

.

First, one easily verifies that

(which belongs to

).
The limits in (

By the form of

9.
Uniqueness under strict monotonicity of
f(\cdot,u)/u

Consider a Lusin topological space
Y
and a bounded Borel measure
\mu
on
Y
.
Let
(S_{t})
be a sub-Markovian
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}(Y;\mu)
with generator
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
.
Assume that the associated resolvent
(G_{\alpha})
is compact and positivity improving and that
s(A)<0
.
Suppose further that there exists a right Markov process
\mathbb{X}
that represents
(S_{t})
.
Let
V:Y\to\mathbb{R}
be a Borel measurable function such that

(9.1)

V^{-}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(Y),\quad\mathbb{P}_{x}(\int_{0}^{t}V(X_{s})\,ds<\infty,\,t<\zeta)=1\quad\text{a.e.}

By
[
14
, Theorems 3.14, Lemma 3.15]
the following family of operators can be regarded,
with the convention adopted in Remark
2.3
, as a strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{p}(Y;\mu)
,

P^{V}_{t}f(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{t}V(X_{s})\,ds}f(X_{s})\right].

This means that whenever
f,\tilde{f}\in p\mathcal{B}(Y)
and
f=\tilde{f}

\mu
-a.e., then
P^{V}_{t}f=P^{V}_{t}\tilde{f}

\mu
-a.e.
Furthermore, if for

we define
T^{V}_{t}f
as the
\mu
-equivalence class of the function

of the

-equivalence class

We denote by

the operator generated by the semigroup
(T^{V}_{t})
on

, and by
(G^{V}_{\alpha})
its resolvent.

Theorem 9.1
.

Assume (
9.1
). Then the following holds.

(i)

(G^{V}_{\alpha})
is positivity improving, and
for any
\alpha>s(A-V)
the operator

(ii)

For any
\alpha>s(A-V)
, the spectral radius of

(denoted by

) satisfies:

and

with strictly positive eigenfunction.

(iii)

(iv)

If

satisfy the same conditions as

Lemma 9.2
.

Assume that
V
satisfies (
9.1
).
Suppose that
u\in L^{p}(Y;\mu)
and
U(|u|V^{+})
is finite
\mu
-a.e.
Furthermore, suppose that

u=-G(Vu).

Then
u=\alpha G^{V}_{\alpha}(u)
for any
\alpha>s(A-V)
. Consequently,
u\in\mathscr{D}(A-V)
and
(A-V)u=0
,
which in turn implies that

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, there exists an
\mu

(we continue to denote it by

)
such that for
\mu
-a.e.

there exists a uniformly integrable martingale

such that
