Spatiotemporal information fusion for photon-level dynamic imaging
Authors: Changzhi Yu, Shuangping Han, Chengbing Qin, Kai Song, Hongrui Liu, Pengyu Zan, Ao Li, Yaoxing Bian, Liantuan Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the photon-starved regime, dynamic reconstructions often exhibit attenuated contrast, loss of fine structure, and temporal flicker, which obstruct high-frame-rate, high-fidelity imaging. We present PhotonST-Net, a spatiotemporal deep reconstruction network that fuses spatial structure with inter-frame dependencies through a frame-difference–aware architecture, thereby enforcing temporal coherence and restoring high-frequency detail at ultra-low photon counts. Operating at a sampling ratio as low as 2− 9, the system achieves 256 × 256 imaging at 200 frames per second with fewer than 2 photons per pixel. It is robust to noise, occlusion, and non-rigid deformation, and an adaptive separation module enables dual-target reconstruction under scattering interference and overlapping motion. Experiments across diverse dynamic conditions validate the approach, showing improvements of 43.12 dB in PSNR and 0.8756 in SSIM compared with conventional block compressive sensing imaging, with further improvements of 15.27 dB and 0.0118 over U-Net under photon-starved conditions. The method offers a scalable solution for high-speed, photon-limited imaging in remote sensing, transient-event observation, and traffic monitoring.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58616-6.

Introduction

With the advancement of photon-level imaging technologies, applications such as satellite remote sensing, astronomical observation, infrared detection, and biomedical imaging increasingly demand reliable operation under extremely photon-limited conditions1–7. In scenarios such as long-range nighttime surveillance, deep-space observation, and ultrafast biological processes, the photon flux often approaches the few-photon-per-pixel level. Under these conditions, imaging systems must simultaneously achieve high temporal resolution and efficient photon utilization to capture fast-moving targets, a combination that poses substantial challenges to conventional multi-pixel sensors. Single-photon avalanche diode (SPAD) arrays offer single-photon sensitivity and a hundred-Hertz frame rate. Still, their performance is degraded by non-ideal factors such as dark counts, afterpulsing, and inter-pixel crosstalk, limiting image quality under ultra-low-photon and high-speed conditions. Alternatively, single-pixel compressive sensing imaging (SCSI) employs high-sensitivity detectors to recover spatial information from a sequence of modulation patterns projected by a digital micromirror device (DMD)8–10. While this approach circumvents some limitations of array sensors, it is fundamentally constrained by the refresh rate of DMDs11–15. Even with optimized strategies, high-resolution imaging is often limited to several tens of hertz. At photon-level flux, the need for longer acquisition further reduces the frame rate, rendering fast dynamic imaging impractical16,17.

Block compressive sensing imaging (BCSI) addresses the speed and scalability limitations of conventional photon-level imaging by partitioning the target scene into smaller spatial blocks for parallel sampling and reconstruction18–27. This strategy significantly reduces the number of modulation patterns required per frame, thereby enabling higher frame rates while maintaining sensitivity, which makes it attractive for dynamic and real-time applications. However, the independent reconstruction of separate blocks often introduces block artifacts, spatial discontinuities, and contextual information loss; these disadvantages become particularly pronounced in dynamic scenes28–30. Moreover, most existing reconstruction algorithms rely solely on intra-frame sparsity and fail to exploit inter-frame temporal correlations, which constrains their capacity to ensure both spatial fidelity and temporal coherence. Recent advances in deep learning (DL) provide an alternative pathway for block compressive sensing reconstruction, demonstrating the ability to reduce block artifacts and simultaneously improve reconstruction accuracy and computational efficiency. Data-driven structural priors suppress seams, recover high-frequency detail, and amortize the cost of iterative solvers to enable fast inference once trained31,32. When combined with multi-channel or block-parallel sampling, DL further increases fidelity and throughput, with accurate recovery reported even at sampling ratios near 3% in dynamic scenes33–35. However, many existing networks are designed for single-frame inputs and do not explicitly model temporal continuity, which limits robustness to motion, occlusion, and non-rigid deformation under photon-starved conditions. Therefore, despite the advances brought by DL, there remains a pressing need for new frameworks that explicitly integrate spatiotemporal modeling into block-based acquisition to achieve high photon efficiency and robust, high-resolution dynamic imaging.

In this work, we present a spatiotemporal information fusion framework for photon-level dynamic imaging, addressing the challenges of limited imaging speed, reconstruction degradation, and insufficient modeling of temporal dependencies in extremely photon-limited environments. The system proposes a spatiotemporal fusion network, PhotonST-Net, which leverages learned priors to enhance image fidelity under photon-limited and high-speed conditions. The network includes hierarchical encoder–decoder layers together with ConvLSTM modules that fuse and propagate spatiotemporal features. The network learns inter-frame dependencies to improve motion modeling, which enhances image integrity and detail preservation under parallel acquisition. Even under the extreme condition of a 2–9 sampling ratio and an average photon count below 2 per pixel, the proposed framework reliably reconstructs 256 × 256 dynamic scenes at 200 fps with high structural fidelity. Experiments demonstrate accurate structure recovery and faithful extraction of motion and deformation. In challenging scenarios such as dual-target tracking with overlapping trajectories and transient shape changes, PhotonST-Net maintains robust spatial separation and continuous tracking. These results highlight the strong potential of the framework for real-time target recognition in photon-limited environments, with applications in remote sensing, transient-event observation, and traffic monitoring.

PhotonST-Net: spatiotemporal information fusion network

Spatiotemporal fusion method

In photon-limited imaging, treating frames independently makes reconstructions vulnerable to shot and readout noise, which lowers signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and attenuates contrast, and suppresses high-frequency details. Compressive sensing, while effective in reducing the number of measurements, inherently suffers from weak spatial constraints, leading to boundary discontinuities and artifacts in the reconstruction. In dynamic scenes, inter-frame inconsistency from object motion or exposure jitter further enlarges these degradations, manifesting as motion-induced aliasing and temporal flicker.

To address these issues, we proposed a spatiotemporal fusion strategy, in which a short sequence of consecutive frames is reconstructed jointly, so that measurement fidelity is enforced while spatiotemporal priors are simultaneously leveraged (Fig. 1(a)). Specifically, for a dynamic target undergoing gradual temporal changes, spatial modulation is performed through block-wise parallel encoding with structured masks, enabling rapid image reconstruction. However, the reconstructed frames are often degraded by system noise, block misalignment, and information loss, which manifest as blocking artifacts. In our approach, consecutive frames are jointly exploited within a fusion framework that leverages inter-frame correlations to recover missing or corrupted details. Unlike traditional geometric alignment methods, the network learns and propagates temporal dependencies across frames, integrating complementary information from adjacent frames for noise suppression and structural restoration. PhotonST-Net network

We designed PhotonST-Net, a spatiotemporal fusion network that jointly models spatial structure and inter-frame dependencies to recover fine detail and enforce temporal coherence under ultra-low photon count conditions. The architecture adopts a hierarchical encoder–decoder with lateral skip connections, where each encoder stage consists of two 3 × 3 convolutions followed by Batch Normalization and ReLU activation, with downsampling performed using 2 × 2 MaxPool layers. The decoder mirrors this structure using ConvTranspose2d for upsampling, followed by concatenation with the corresponding encoder features and another convolution block to refine the features. At the bottleneck, we insert a ConvLSTM unit to explicitly capture temporal dependencies. The ConvLSTM replaces fully connected operations with convolutions, allowing the network to preserve spatial locality while learning motion patterns across frames. The ConvLSTM cell concatenates the current frame with the previous hidden state, followed by a 3 × 3 convolution, and splits the result into input, forget, output, and candidate gates. These gates are activated by sigmoid for the first three and tanh for the candidate, allowing the network to propagate temporal memory and filter out noise. The output of the ConvLSTM is combined with features from the decoder, enhancing temporal coherence while maintaining high spatial resolution. The network’s input consists of a multi-frame window, from which spatiotemporal correlations are learned to improve reconstruction fidelity under block-parallel acquisition. The overall architecture is illustrated in Fig. 1b, where the hierarchical encoder-decoder structure and the ConvLSTM integration are depicted. Beyond single-target reconstruction, PhotonST-Net can be flexibly extended to handle double-target separation, as illustrated in Fig. 1(c). Specifically, we augment the decoder with two parallel segmentation heads to generate candidate masks for potential targets, and introduce a classification branch that predicts the target count. This branch first applies global average pooling (GAP) to aggregate spatial features, followed by fully connected (FC) layers and a softmax output to produce a probability distribution over target numbers. A conditional strategy activates dual outputs only when the predicted probability for multiple targets exceeds 0.5. Moreover, the same design scales to multi-target scenarios by instantiating M segmentation heads and expanding the counting branch to M classes, with inference retaining the top- M masks according to the predicted count.

System design and implementation

Implementation strategy of BCSI based on spatiotemporal information fusion

To make spatiotemporal multiplexing effective in practice, the input sequence must be co-registered across blocks, temporally synchronized, and sufficiently informative under extreme compression. The acquisition pipeline in Fig. 2 is configured to meet these conditions before the reconstruction stage. As summarized in Fig. 2, the proposed strategy consists of several key components, including pattern construction, target segmentation, spatiotemporal fusion mechanism, and the constructed neural network PhotonST-Net. Specifically, Fig. 2(a) illustrates the schematic of the pattern loaded onto the DMD. Hadamard patterns possess desirable properties such as strict orthogonality, balanced energy distribution, and strong robustness to noise, which make them well-suited for computational imaging, particularly under high compression ratios. Therefore, we generate a large composite modulation pattern by tiling multiple identical Hadamard patterns, and this composite mask is then loaded onto the DMD to enable efficient parallel modulation and reliable reconstruction. As shown in Fig. 2(b), the imaging target is first actively illuminated by a laser, and the reflected light is collected by an imaging lens and directed onto the DMD via a fast-steering mirror (FSM). The composite mask illustrated in Fig. 2(a) is displayed on the DMD, and the modulated signals are directed to different sub-regions of the detector array. As shown in Fig. 2(c), the scene is partitioned into spatial sub-blocks that align with the DMD’s modulation tiles. Each tile carries an identical Hadamard mask, so every sub-block is modulated by the same pattern in parallel. The modulated light from each block is then reflected and mapped onto the dedicated detection units of the array detector, ensuring spatial independence and real-time synchronization during the imaging process.

In addition to block count, the photon number per pixel is a decisive factor in determining reconstruction quality. Therefore, we compare the imaging performance of SCSI and BCSI under different photon levels. For one reconstructed frame, the average photon count per pixel is defined as the total photon counts accumulated during the playback of all modulation masks and all detection channels used for that frame, divided by the number of pixels in the final reconstructed image. The detailed calculation formula and variable definitions are provided in Supplementary Information (Sect. 2). Under this definition, the illumination intensity required for compressed sensing under the same average number of photons per pixel is much lower than that of direct array measurement (e.g., with a sampling ratio of 2–6, the required illumination equals the array illumination × 0.5 × number of patterns per frame, where 0.5 is the duty cycle of Hadamard masks). This further underlines the efficiency of the proposed system in photon-limited scenarios. Using the target image in Fig. 3(a) as the reference, the comparative results of reconstruction quality are shown in Figs. 3(d) and 3(e). The relative improvement ratio RPSNR and RSSIM are defined as the quality improvement of the 4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\times\:$$\end{document}4 block configuration over SCSI, normalized by the SCSI baseline. At lower photon counts, both PSNR and SSIM exhibit pronounced gains, as block-based acquisition effectively increases photon utilization. For RPSNR in Fig. 3(d), the improvement ratio shows an initial increase followed by a decrease. This phenomenon can be attributed to the fact that the PSNR metric retains a finite baseline value even when the reconstructed image is severely blurred and the target is visually indistinguishable. Thus, under extremely low photon counts, although visual inspection reveals noticeable improvements from block-based reconstruction, the PSNR change remains limited. Once the photon count rises to a level where the target becomes partially resolvable, the improvements are more strongly reflected in PSNR, leading to an initial increase before the ratio decreases again at higher photon levels. As the photon number increases further, SCSI itself already receives sufficient photons to recover high-quality images directly, and the relative benefit from block-based accumulation becomes less significant. Moreover, the independent reconstruction of spatially partitioned blocks may introduce blocking artifacts and structural discontinuities, which can limit or even reduce reconstruction quality at higher photon levels. These results demonstrate that, owing to the high sensitivity of photon-level detectors and the quality gains introduced by block-based reconstruction, the proposed system is particularly well-suited for image reconstruction under photon-limited conditions. Experimental results

We evaluate the proposed framework in three representative experimental scenarios. The first involves a candle flame undergoing continuous deformation, serving as a proxy for weak-light fire sources such as distant flames. This setting is well-suited for evaluating reconstruction fidelity under temporally varying structures. The second scenario considers traffic-like scenes with car and airplane models, designed to assess the ability to separate overlapping targets and remain robust against background interference and scattering. The third scenario involves static objects, representing cases without motion or structural variation, and is used to assess the applicability of the system under static imaging conditions. Collectively, these experiments validate the fidelity and robustness of PhotonST-Net across diverse and challenging situations. The acquisition of ground-truth images and the implementation details of BCSI, U-Net, and PhotonST-Net are provided in Supplementary Information (Sect. 6). U-Net and PhotonST-Net were trained and evaluated under the same data, preprocessing, loss, and evaluation settings, while BCSI was reconstructed using fixed parameters for all compared cases.

To evaluate the reconstruction performance of the proposed PhotonST-Net under photon-limited conditions, we conduct imaging experiments on a candle flame. The imaging setup is configured with a sampling ratio of 2− 8, a resolution of 256\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\times\:$$\end{document}256, and an acquisition speed of 100 fps, corresponding to an average photon count below 1.5 photons per pixel. The detailed photon budget, including the sampling ratio, number of modulation masks per frame, detector channels, integration time, total detected photon number, and calculation of the average photon count per pixel, is provided in Supplementary Information (Sect. 2). To realize such photon-limited conditions, a neutral density filter with 0.1% transmittance was placed in front of the detector. Figure 4(a) compares three methods: traditional BCSI, a conventional deep-learning approach (U-Net)36, and the proposed PhotonST-Net. To isolate the contribution of spatiotemporal fusion, U-Net was implemented without the ConvLSTM module and used only the current frame as input. The first row of Fig. 4(a) shows the flame in a steady burning state, where its position and shape vary only slightly. Due to the extremely low photon count and block-wise reconstruction, traditional BCSI produces severe artifacts with PSNR of 6.17 dB and SSIM of 0.1233. Both deep learning methods substantially improve the results, with U-Net reaching PSNR of 34.02 dB and SSIM of 0.9871, and PhotonST-Net achieving PSNR of 49.29 dB and SSIM of 0.9989. In the magnified details at the top right corner, PhotonST-Net further preserves fine structural features compared with U-Net. The second row shows the flame perturbed by airflow, which induces abrupt deformation. Methods that rely primarily on intra-frame spatial structure, exemplified by BCSI and U-Net in our setup, struggle to recover complete flame contours, leading to missing or distorted regions. In contrast, PhotonST-Net integrates temporal information from adjacent frames to produce coherent and accurate reconstructions. The third row highlights an anomalous frame within the steady burning sequence. In this case, U-Net introduces spurious adjacent contours near the flame, which may lead to misinterpretation of the target. PhotonST-Net suppresses these artifacts by leveraging temporal consistency, yielding more stable and reliable recovery. Figure 4(b) shows reconstructions sampled every five frames, illustrating the multi-frame dynamic recovery of the candle flame with continuous deformation and preserved fine details. Figures 4(c) and 4(d) show vertical intensity profiles extracted along the orange dashed lines in Fig. 4(a), corresponding to columns 120 and 64, respectively. Figure 4(e) presents the quantitative results in PSNR and SSIM. Compared with BCSI, PhotonST-Net achieves an improvement of 43.12 dB in PSNR and 0.8756 in SSIM. Compared with U-Net, it further improves PSNR by 15.27 dB and SSIM by 0.0118. The improvement over U-Net indicates that temporal inputs and ConvLSTM-based fusion contribute to the reconstruction of dynamic targets beyond single-frame spatial reconstruction. These results confirm that PhotonST-Net consistently outperforms both baselines in restoring dynamic targets at extremely low photon levels. The 100 fps reconstruction videos of the candle flame under different methods are provided in Visualization 1, further demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed approach in dynamic photon-limited environments.

Imaging of traffic scenes under extreme conditions

In addition, PhotonST-Net can be augmented with an auxiliary classification branch trained with a classification loss, enabling the network to estimate the number of dynamic targets and disentangle them even under severe overlap. By jointly optimizing reconstruction and classification, the method achieves robust target separation and effective background suppression in photon-limited conditions. To validate its effectiveness under extreme dynamics, we design dual-target experiments with controlled interference scenarios.

To further evaluate the dynamic imaging capability of the proposed framework, we design experiments where car and airplane models are projected onto the same plane and undergo accelerated motion, aiming to validate its effectiveness in capturing fast dynamics under photon-limited conditions. These results demonstrate the capability of the method to capture object motion and extract dynamic parameters in photon-limited conditions. By further reducing the compression ratio to 2–9 and increasing the modulation frequency to 25.6 kHz, we achieve dynamic imaging at 200 fps, with the average photon count below 2 per pixel. As shown in Fig. 5(a), the car and airplane models follow distinct motion trajectories within the field of view. Figure 5(b) presents their tracked motion trajectories in two-dimensional space over time. The derived velocity and acceleration curves in Fig. 5(c) closely match the predefined experimental motion parameters. Under these conditions and at an imaging distance of 100 cm, a dynamic target moving at 19.55 cm/s with an acceleration of 9.78 cm/s² is successfully identified. These findings confirm that the proposed method enables accurate, temporally resolved motion analysis.

After validating performance under photon-limited conditions, we further examine robustness in more complex dynamic environments involving target overlap, background interference, and scattering. Dynamic imaging experiments are conducted in a laboratory environment using two moving vehicle models, which travel at constant speed in opposite directions on different planes. Figure 5(d) shows the CCD-captured image of two partially overlapping vehicles and the corresponding separated reconstructions obtained by our method, demonstrating its effectiveness in isolating individual targets in overlapping scenes. To emulate complex real-world interferences, a static object is placed behind the vehicles as background, while a dynamic thin scattering medium is introduced in front of the imaging system, thereby simulating background clutter and scattering effects during the imaging process. Notably, although the CCD is unable to form clear images of the targets due to the strong occlusion caused by the dynamic thin scattering medium, PhotonST-Net still reliably reconstructs the vehicles. Despite compounded challenges from occlusion, background interference, and scattering, the proposed method consistently enhances the reconstruction of dynamic targets and enables reliable motion information extraction, demonstrating robustness in complex dynamic environments.

Imaging of static targets

In practical applications, targets of interest are not limited to deforming or moving objects, static targets must also be considered. Static targets lack the motion-induced inter-frame diversity available in dynamic scenes, whereas PhotonST-Net benefits from temporal cues across adjacent frames. To adapt the framework to static imaging, we used the FSM to slightly shift the field of view and generate a pseudo-temporal sequence. The shifted observations provide controlled inter-frame structural diversity and were then processed by PhotonST-Net. Using this procedure on resolution charts, digits, and letters, reconstructions with PhotonST-Net yield high-quality results, confirming its applicability to static scenes under this FSM-assisted acquisition strategy. This also indicates that, for static scenes, the current implementation requires controlled field-of-view shifting to construct the pseudo-temporal input sequence. Detailed procedures and results are provided in Supplementary Information (Sect. 7).

Conclusion

In summary, PhotonST-Net enables photon-level, high-speed dynamic imaging by integrating inter-frame temporal information into its spatiotemporal reconstruction process, which effectively guides feature recovery and significantly boosts image quality under photon-starved conditions. Under compressed sampling with parallel acquisition, the system delivers high-fidelity 256\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\times\:$$\end{document}256 reconstructions at 200 fps with fewer than 2 photons per pixel, even at a sampling ratio of 2⁻⁹. It outperforms conventional compressive-sensing reconstructions by 43.12 dB in PSNR and 0.8756 in SSIM, and further surpasses U-Net by 15.27 dB and 0.0118 under photon-starved conditions. Beyond improvements in imaging quality and speed, the method enables reliable dynamic target identification and continuous trajectory tracking. Dual-target recognition is experimentally demonstrated under challenging conditions, including scattering interference and overlapping motion, with potential extensibility to multi-target tracking scenarios. With continued advances in spatial resolution, detector parallelization, and real-time network inference, the system is expected to scale to megapixel-level, high-frame-rate dynamic imaging, offering a scalable solution for photon-limited, high-speed imaging in fields such as remote sensing, transient-event observation, and traffic monitoring.
