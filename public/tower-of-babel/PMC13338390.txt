Clinical large language model centered on electronic medical records
Authors: Yan Zhuang, Bo Wang, Chengliang Yin, Junyan Zhang, Fanqing Meng, Jianfei Zhao, Qingyong Su, Xuan Zhao, Xiuxing Li, Ping Hu, Shiyuan Liu, Rilige Wu, Yun Hua, Wei Dong, Bing Wei, Li Zhang, Lei Zheng, João Conde, Ge Shi, Chong Feng, Kunlun He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the quest to enhance medical consultation, our study introduces AI4Doctor, a sophisticated large-language model (LLM) tailored for the clinical domain. At the heart of AI4Doctor is an innovative integration strategy that synergizes distilled data extracted from electronic medical records (EMR) with empirical insights gathered from practicing physicians during the supervised fine-tuning. Although existing platforms offer informative responses, they fall short of replicating the nuanced decision-making processes of medical professionals, particularly in complex, integrative diagnostic scenarios. Motivated by the need to create a realistic medical practice environment, we propose that a combination of direct knowledge transfer from seasoned doctors and the strategic use of EMR can augment the abilities of LLM, enabling it to more closely mimic the clinical acumen of healthcare practitioners. To navigate the complexities of merging diverse instructional sources, we employ a curriculum learning approach during the fine-tuning process. Moreover, we advance our model’s performance by developing a reward system that incentivizes the alignment of the LLM’s outputs with the valuable attributes inherent in both doctors’ expertise, including diagnostic priors, risk thresholds, and heuristic saliencies accumulated from practice and EMR data. This is achieved through a novel reinforcement-learning approach. Besides, we introduce a new benchmark involving a comparative evaluation. We utilize a subjective evaluation system wherein experts critically assess the responses from a professional perspective as well. Our research underscores the potential of this hybrid model to serve as a robust tool in medical consultations, bridging the gap between artificial intelligence and real-world clinical practice.

Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable potential in healthcare applications1,2 achieving performance comparable to medical professionals in knowledge-based assessments such as medical licensing examinations3. Despite these advances, LLMs exhibit significant limitations when applied to complex clinical scenarios that require contextual understanding and practical expertise3–8. Most concerning is their tendency to generate hallucinations—confident but factually incorrect information—which poses substantial safety and ethical risks in clinical settings9–12. These limitations highlight the critical gap between theoretical medical knowledge and practical clinical application in current LLM implementations.

Recent years have witnessed rapid development in medical LLMs across multiple languages and scales. In English, Med-PaLM 23 has achieved remarkable performance on medical licensing exams, surpassing the passing threshold on both the US Medical Licensing Exam (USMLE) and Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs). GPT-4V Medical demonstrates advanced capabilities in medical image understanding and clinical reasoning. Clinical-BERT and BioBERT1 have shown effectiveness in specific clinical NLP tasks such as named entity recognition and relation extraction. Among Chinese medical LLMs, HuaTuoGPT13, trained on 400B tokens of medical text, exhibits strong performance in medical dialogue and knowledge retrieval. MING and DoctorGLM14, while showing promise in medical question answering, generally lag behind their English counterparts due to limited high-quality Chinese medical corpus.

Medical LLMs currently rely primarily on medical literature, textbooks, and online resources for training1,2,15,16, with limited integration of real clinical data. This approach, while providing broad medical knowledge coverage, often falls short in practical clinical scenarios. The disconnect between theoretical knowledge resources and the complex, contextual nature of clinical decision-making contributes significantly to current limitations.

This fundamental limitation in training data composition highlights the need for more clinically relevant resources to enhance medical LLMs. Electronic Medical Records (EMRs) represent a valuable yet underutilized solution for addressing these deficiencies17–22. As comprehensive repositories of clinical workflows, diagnostic reasoning, and treatment patterns, EMRs capture the nuanced decision-making processes essential for effective clinical practice13,15,16 Unlike static knowledge bases, EMRs provide dynamic, patient-specific information reflecting real clinical decision-making processes.

Although they contain rich, structured clinical evidence that captures relationships between diagnoses, treatments, and outcomes. Incorporating EMR data could potentially bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical application in medical LLMs, though significant challenges exist in processing their structured formats, varying quality, and specialized terminology requirements. Prior EMR‑based systems (e.g., EMR‑conditioned LLMs and temporal decision‑support models) often rely on explicit rule templates, fixed‑interval resampling, or retrieval‑centric pipelines. In contrast, AI4Doctor emphasizes scalable pre-training/fine‑tuning with de‑identified, episode‑structured EMR context and introduces STG to capture multi‑resolution temporal dynamics and missingness. This design prioritizes case‑grounded reasoning over heuristic prompting or post‑hoc retrieval.

We introduce AI4Doctor, a unified framework that unlocks the clinical value of electronic medical records (EMRs) through a sequence of methodical innovations. At its core is an ontology-driven standardisation pipeline applied to more than three million multi-centre inpatient records spanning multiple specialties. Coupled with a novel Special Temporal Grid representation, this pipeline resolves long-standing challenges in structuring longitudinal EMR data.

To evaluate real-world clinical reasoning, we release 301MedQA, a benchmark that distils EMR-derived scenarios from four representative specialties. Our contributions are threefold: (1) End-to-end EMR processing: an integrative workflow that fuses ontology-based normalisation with fine-grained temporal modelling. (2) 301MedQA benchmark: the first public test bed for EMR-anchored clinical reasoning, mirroring authentic inpatient workflows. (3) AI4Doc-LLM: a large-language model in EMR understanding, clinical decision support, and medical documentation, while retaining alignment with expert-level reasoning. Together, these components establish a scalable path for deploying trustworthy language models in routine clinical practice. By integrating real-world clinical data with novel model and benchmark design, this study lays a foundation for advancing trustworthy medical LLMs from lab settings to practical deployment.

Our framework utilizes structured EMR data spanning the complete hospitalization period, encompassing Progress Notes, Vital Signs, Diagnostic Records, Medical Orders, Lab Results, and Test Results, as shown in Fig. 1. This comprehensive medical record system is systematically organized into a temporal sequence, starting from admission records through regular progress notes to discharge summaries. The structured EMR data serves as the foundation for all four training stages: continue pre-training uses the complete EMR corpus for domain adaptation; instruction fine-tuning leverages the logical relationships between different medical records; reinforcement preference alignment incorporates expert-reviewed EMR cases; and retrieval integration utilizes indexed EMR knowledge bases. Evaluation on 301MedQA

This study utilizes a large-scale, multi-center clinical database encompassing electronic medical records from nine medical centers of the PLA General Hospital. The data spans 46 clinical departments, covering various fields of medicine, and thus possesses broad representativeness and diversity. The data has undergone thorough parsing and governance: content was segmented from medical records, and terms for diagnoses, symptoms, and other medical ontologies were standardized. This improved data interoperability and semantic consistency, facilitating cross-center and cross-disciplinary data integration and comparative analysis. To cover a more comprehensive range of results, we have categorized our benchmark, 301MedQA, into three types: Foundational Medical Queries, Advanced Clinical Queries, and Real-World Clinic Questions. We focused on representative clinical departments, covering common diseases and critical conditions, which allowed us to evaluate the method’s validity and robustness effectively, and detail of the benchmark construction are illustrated in Supplementary Materials B.

Retrieval-augmented clinical reasoning

Intuitively, we hope that our model can also obtain diagnostic as output based on the patient information as input, through the complex effects of internal parameters, or refer to relevant information acquired from similar diagnostic records through retrieval. Specifically, we built high-quality QA from multiple data sources such as electronic medical records, medical guidelines, and clinical pathways as the external datastore. For Medical Consultation, given patient information as the query, our system retrieves similar medical records and corresponding diagnoses on the datastore according to the vectorized similarity, and prepends them to the query together as input to the language model to obtain more accurate output. Similar methods can be extent to Differential Diagnosis and Diagnosis and Treatement Protocol.

We shows that (1) retrieval on our vector base can obtain relevant and consistent information to be further leveraged by the LM, and (2) our LLM takes advantage of retrieval augmentation and its effectiveness for real-world medical tasks.

Retrieval accuracy

Conclusively, we report the retrieval accuracy automatically and manually evaluated, to verify that retrieval on our vector base can obtain relevant and consistent information to be further leveraged by the model.

For manual evaluation, we organize doctors to score the relevance between the query and the retrieved results form 4 (highly related) to 1 (not relevant). All retrieved results on the test set were scored by two clinicians, and those with large differences were additionally evaluated by a third, more professional expert. Augmentation Performance

Through manual & automatic evaluation, we illustrate that our LLM takes advantage of retrieval augmentation and its effectiveness for real-world medical tasks such as Medical Consultation.

We use 8 different metrics to evaluate the effectiveness of the augmentation. For a detailed introduction to each metric, please see Supplementary Materials Metrics. First, we organize doctors to perform manual evaluation to give model outputs a comprehensive score (Manual Eval, 1–5). In addition, we prompt GPT-4 to score the model outputs (GPT Score, 1–5) using the same rule as instruction, where the results showed high consistency with the manual evaluation. Furthermore, we use ROUGE-L and F1 Score to evaluate the consistency of the generation with the standard answers provided by clinicians. We use Chinese tokenized MAUVE to evaluate the fluency, and calculate 2-gram counts (Distinct Score) to evaluate the diversity of the generated content.

For the comprehensive evaluation, shown in Fig. 5, of Medical Consultation on different retrieval tasks (e.g. EMR, LAB result, etc.), scores given by human and GPT-4 improved up to 10.4% and 6.0% respectively. Consistency improved significantly for both ROUGE ( + 3.30) and F1 Score ( + 5.12). For fine-grained metrics, EM increased 4.89 and Hit increased 4.00. Such results on these dimensions illustrate that our LLM do take advantage of retrieval augmentation and is effective for Medical Consultation. Discussion

Large language models (LLMs) have progressed from general-purpose knowledge engines to domain-tuned medical assistants2,4,13–16,26–29 yet questions remain about calibration30, guideline adherence31, long-context retrieval9,32–34 and workflow fidelity35. Mid-sized, EHR-centric encoders such as TransformEHR show that judicious scaling plus clinical corpora can rival larger baselines36, while multi-modal works37 and modular RAG systems38–52 illustrate the value of retrieval-aware design. Governance frameworks53 and deployment strategies54 further stress safety and cost-consciousness, while RL alignment alternatives support low-resource clinical use55. Against this backdrop, we present AI4Doctor, a 32B medical LLM combining longitudinal EMR pre-training, curriculum tuning, preference alignment, and hybrid retrieval—offering senior-clinician reasoning within a deployable compute envelope.

AI4Doctor demonstrates that a mid-scale, 32-B parameter backbone—when paired with longitudinal EMR pre-training, curriculum-based instruction tuning, Odds-Ratio Preference Optimisation (ORPO) and hybrid retrieval—can achieve senior-clinician competence while remaining deployable on commodity hardware. This trajectory echoes recent calls to translate foundation-model promise into dependable bedside tools without resorting to billion-scale systems56.

Continued pre-training on three million anonymised inpatient timelines proved decisive: CMMLU-Med rose by 8.4 absolute points and clinician-rated answer quality improved in infection-related topics. These gains corroborate evidence that domain-specific corpora sharpen probabilistic calibration more effectively than parameter inflation30, and align with the EHR-centred improvements reported in TransformEHR36.

Scaling analysis reinforces a “right-sized” principle: our 13 B variant outperforms larger 34 B baselines on CMMLU-Med and C-Eval-Med, further supporting the notion that capacity exceeding data yields diminishing returns36. Expert evaluation across yielded an overall score of 80.52/100, a + 6.5 margin over the strongest general Chinese LLM of comparable size and +7.5 over senior physicians. This human-aligned assessment advances the broader evaluation agenda for clinical LLMs35 and highlights the value of temporal EMR conditioning.

Hybrid retrieval offered complementary benefits. These improvements resonate with hepatology-guideline experiments31 and long-context partitioning strategies9, confirming that targeted external memory mitigates context-window limits.

ORPO alignment improved all eight clinical dimensions by 3–10% while consuming one-tenth the compute of full RLHF, dovetailing with reinforcement-learning guidance for resource-constrained healthcare settings55.

Two limitations remain. Performance dips for rare conditions, and encounters exceeding the current context-window admit only a single retrieval chunk. Future work will explore knowledge-graph reranking and sliding-window decoding, drawing inspiration from multi-modal transformers that marry images and text37. Parallel efforts will embed GREAT-PLEA–style governance53 and balance on-prem versus cloud deployment trade-offs54 before large-scale roll-out.

AI4Doc-LLM’s exceptional performance across various medical benchmarks demonstrates its unparalleled capability in clinical knowledge comprehension and problem-solving. The model’s adeptness at providing reliable clinical decision support, while mitigating issues like subjective hallucinations, exemplifies its practical utility in real-world medical scenarios. Beyond its current achievements, the AI4Doc-LLM’s design philosophy of continuous learning and adaptation mirrors the lifelong learning trajectory of medical professionals, ensuring its relevance and effectiveness in an ever-evolving healthcare landscape.

AI4Doctor addresses the gap between exam‑style medical QA and episode‑grounded clinical decision‑making. Our contributions are threefold: (1) an end‑to‑end EMR pipeline with ontology standardization and the STG temporal representation; (2) a clinician‑curated evaluation protocol and 301MedQA benchmark emphasizing closed‑book patient‑episode reasoning; and (3) an alignment recipe that integrates clinician preferences with safety‑aware weighting. Method

EMRs offer medical LLMs unparalleled real-world clinical insights—capturing patient journeys, cross-specialty reasoning, and treatment patterns in actual practice. Unlike textbooks, they encode how doctors truly diagnose and treat over time. This enables models to learn authentic medical decision-making, not just textbook knowledge.

Medical adaption through temporal coherence EMR

Our approach is structured around the seven key processes in EMRs, including consultation, laboratory assessment, diagnosis, treatment planning, medication management, inpatient care, and follow-up. By systematically organizing and filtering these processes, we extract temporally coherent and clinically relevant features from medical records. To ensure privacy, we employ a two-stage de-identification protocol that combines automated methods—medical NER, rule-based matching, and dictionary screening—with expert verification via stratified sampling. This strategy effectively removes protected health information while preserving essential clinical utility across multiple medical centers, time spans, departments, and record lengths. For EMR-specific corpus construction, hospitalization episodes are modelled as temporal graph sequences, and daily medical events are compared to standard clinical pathways. This captures both typical patterns and meaningful variations, while the knowledge structure retains core medical concepts and their clinical relationships, ensuring balanced representation across diseases, treatments, and departments. To further enhance model robustness and mitigate catastrophic forgetting, we augment the dataset with high-quality corpora from multiple sources, including real-world EMRs, Chinese medical textbooks, PubMed literature, and filtered general Chinese data. All data undergoes thorough preprocessing and quality assurance, as detailed in the Supplementary Materials D. Unlike fixed‑interval resampling or purely event‑sequence models, STG aligns multi‑modal EMR signals to multi‑resolution bins anchored to clinical indices (e.g., admission) and separates values, deltas/trends, time‑since‑last‑seen, and explicit missingness into distinct channels, preserving dynamics essential for reasoning.

Leveraging EMR for advanced multi-task model optimization and instruction fine-tuning

We utilize EMRs as a comprehensive resource for multi-task instruction tuning, optimizing the model’s capacity to answer domain-specific questions. Our pipeline includes: (1) a task cube organizing medical tasks by NLP abilities, specialties, and application domains; (2) task-aware data selection for diverse and high-quality EMR instances; and (3) collaborative medical question construction combining expert knowledge with data-driven filtering. This structured process transforms raw EMRs into high-quality instruction examples, covering diverse clinical scenarios and retaining natural interconnections in medical practice. To maintain general conversational and reasoning abilities, we supplement EMR-based instruction data with both real-world medical Q&A and general-domain dialogue data. Further implementation details appear in Supplementary Materials E.

For preference modelling, we integrate implicit supervision from structured EMR data with explicit evaluation from medical professionals. EMRs provide rich clinical decision signals, which are enhanced by systematic expert assessment of model outputs across real-time performance, safety, and comprehensiveness. Candidate responses are generated using multi-model sampling—including both self-sampling and specialized open-source models—and high-scoring outputs are selected as preference data. Training employs distributed computing with ZeRO Stage 3 optimization and two epochs to ensure both computational efficiency and clinical robustness. The overall framework ensures that the model aligns with professional medical standards while retaining real-time performance and safety. Data statistics and further details are available in the Supplementary Materials E.

Knowledge-enhanced medical dialogue generation via EMR-based retrieval

The integration of historical EMR cases and expert knowledge bases significantly enhances the model’s ability to handle complex medical scenarios while reducing hallucination risks. Our approach combines parametric knowledge stored in model weights with non-parametric knowledge accessed through efficient retrieval mechanisms, enabling both memorization and dynamic reference of medical expertise. These details in EMR provide the foundation for constructing various medical tasks, such as diagnostic discussions, treatment plan recommendations, and admission diagnosis assessments. For ontology-enhanced EMR knowledge representation and retrieval, we construct dual representations combining dense vectors and structured medical concepts for each EMR document. The dense embedding is encoded by a medical-domain-pretrained encoder, while sparse ontological features are extracted from medical concepts and attributes. These ontological features derive from structured EMR fields, including chief complaints, medical history, examination results, medications, surgical records, and diagnostic discussions.

The knowledge base integrates three primary information sources: EMR abstractions, medical guidelines, and expert dialogues. The approach incorporates three primary information sources: EMR abstractions, medical guidelines, and expert dialogues. EMR abstractions systematically capture essential clinical information through structured core elements. The medical guideline component maintains version-controlled clinical protocols, with standardized frameworks like NCCN Guidelines serving as authoritative references. Expert dialogues are structured as question-answer pairs enriched with medical context metadata, including specialty, difficulty level, and verification status. During inference, the model generates responses by incorporating retrieved knowledge from these integrated sources. For each retrieved document, we construct a context-enriched prompt template. This unified framework enables the model to leverage both structured medical knowledge and practical clinical experience, significantly improving response accuracy and reducing hallucination in complex medical scenarios.
