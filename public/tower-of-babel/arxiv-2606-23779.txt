Excitability of Gaussian states with VEVs
Jacqueline Caminiti, Federico Capeccia, and Jonathan Sorce

Abstract
In 6 , we gave general criteria for when one zero-mean Gaussian state can be excited out of another in a (generalized) free field theory. Here we extend this analysis to the case of nonzero mean, i.e., to Gaussian states with vacuum expectation values (VEVs). We prove that excitability is possible exactly when (i) the connected two-point functions satisfy criteria like those in 6 , and (ii) the difference of the VEVs is bounded relative to the two-point functions. As an application, we give an explicit computation showing that in anti-de Sitter spacetime, a VEV shift can be excited from the Klein-Gordon vacuum if and only if its boundary extrapolation can be excited from the vacuum of the dual conformal field theory.

Introduction

Excitability is a central notion in quantum field theory.
Particles are excitations of the vacuum;
the Hilbert space associated to a reference state characterizes its possible excitations;
and nontrivial superselection structure arises when distinct states cannot be excited out of one another using local operators. Understanding when one state of a quantum field theory can be (locally) excited out of another is therefore an important question, and one to which the algebraic approach is well-suited.

In algebraic quantum field theory, one begins with a
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}
of quantum fields, living on a spacetime manifold
\mathcal{M}
.
One then considers abstract states on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
that play the role of correlation functions.
For each choice of abstract state
\omega
, the associated GNS Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
characterizes the space of local excitations around
\omega

14

Hence we say that

can be excited out of
\omega_{1}
if
\omega_{2}
admits a representative inside
\mathcal{H}_{\omega_{1}}
as a density matrix
\rho_{\omega_{2}}
satisfying

\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\omega_{1}}}(\rho_{\omega_{2}}a)=\omega_{2}(a)\,,\quad a\in\mathcal{A}_{0}\,.

(1)

In a prior paper
6

describes a (generalized) free field theory, and where

In the present paper, we generalize the results of

States with nonvanishing VEVs are physically relevant, with important examples including generic coherent states,

In the pure-state case, relevant VEV excitability criteria were already stated in

As our main result, we establish excitability criteria for general mixed Gaussian states

Setting up the toolkit

In this section, we briefly review the concepts in (generalized) free field theory needed below. We then prove that for Gaussian states, excitability requires the VEV difference to be appropriately bounded, and we explain how this will be used to reduce the problem of excitability for general Gaussian states to that for zero-mean Gaussian states.

2.1
(Generalized) free field theory

A generalized free theory of a real scalar field
\phi
is defined by an abstract
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}
generated by smeared fields
\phi[f]
. These fields satisfy linearity relations, the reality condition
\phi[f]^{*}=\phi[f^{*}]
, and the c-number commutation relation

[\phi[f],\phi[g]]=-i\Omega[f,g].

(7)

Different generalized free theories correspond to different choices of the antisymmetric functional
\Omega
.
The theory of a Klein-Gordon scalar arises as a special case; there,
\Omega
is chosen to be the Poisson bracket of the classical smeared field observables, and the equation of motion is imposed as a further algebraic relation.

The algebraic relations obeyed by smeared fields do not specify a canonical Hilbert space.
Instead, given a set of correlation functions
\omega
, a natural representation of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is provided by the GNS construction. The resulting Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
is the set of states accessible from
\omega
by local excitations.
Within
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
, one may then complete
\mathcal{A}_{0}
into a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
.
1
1

1

See
(
15
, appendix A)
for a general explanation of how to complete a
*
-algebra into a von Neumann algebra within one of its GNS representations.

Among the possible choices of state on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
, the simplest are Gaussian states.
One starts with a set of correlators
\omega
satisfying, as a formal power series in
\phi[f]
, the Gaussian property

\omega(e^{i\phi[f]})=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega(\widetilde{\phi}[f]^{2})}e^{i\omega(\phi[f])},

(8)

where
\widetilde{\phi}[f]=\phi[f]-\omega(\phi[f])
denotes the mean-subtracted field.
Expanding both sides in powers of
\phi[f]
and matching order by order shows that all correlators of the form
\omega(\phi[f]^{n})
are determined by
\omega(\phi[f])
and
\omega(\widetilde{\phi}[f]^{2})
.
By the multilinear polarization identities, together with the commutation relations (
7

Explicitly, correlators of mean-subtracted fields factorize into two-point functions, so that (
8
) compactly encodes the familiar combinatorics of Wick contractions.

For equation (
8
) to define the correlation functions of a quantum state, one needs to verify the positivity condition
\omega(a^{*}a)\geq 0
for any
a\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
The c-number commutation relation (
7
) guarantees that positivity is satisfied
whenever it holds at the level of two-point functions of mean-subtracted fields, provided that one has
\omega(\phi[f])^{*}=\omega(\phi[f^{*}])
.
When this is the case, we say that
\omega
defines a Gaussian state on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
.

Consistency with equation (
7
) fully fixes the antisymmetric part of the two-point function of
\omega
. Consequently, the two-point function is completely specified by its symmetric part.
This symmetric part defines a positive semidefinite inner product
\mu
on the space of smearing functions, namely

\innerproduct{f}{g}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\omega(\widetilde{\phi}[f^{*}]\widetilde{\phi}[g])+\omega(\widetilde{\phi}[g]\widetilde{\phi}[f^{*}])\right).

(9)

Quotienting by
\mu

\mathcal{K}_{\mu}
, which encodes all the information about
\omega
that is relevant for excitability.
Moreover, on
\mathcal{K}_{\mu}
, one can introduce an operator

implementing the commutator
\Omega
via the formula

\matrixelement{f}{R}{g}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\Omega[f^{*},g].

(10)

The operator

is bounded, maps real smearing functions to real smearing functions, is anti-hermitian, and has spectrum lying between
-i
and
i
.
Furthermore, the spectrum of

diagnoses the purity of
\omega
: it is equal to
\{-i,i\}
when
\omega
is pure (i.e. when its GNS representation introduces no purifying degrees of freedom,
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}
), and is equal to
\{-i,0,i\}
when
\omega
is centrally pure (i.e. when the only purifying degrees of freedom are limits of the original ones,
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\omega}

Centrally pure states are simpler to study than general mixed states, and their importance stems from the fact that any state
\omega

2.2
A necessary criterion for excitability

As a first step towards proving our excitability theorem
1
,
we show that whenever it is possible to excite
\omega_{2}

\omega_{1}

Excitability means we have a density matrix representative

Here we have defined the

2.3
Proof strategy for excitability criteria

In the previous subsection, we showed that the excitability relation
\omega_{2}\prec\omega_{1}
imposes a nontrivial constraint on the one-point functions of the states. Our goal is now to show that this constraint, together with the zero-mean excitability criteria of
6
applied to the VEV-subtracted two-point functions, give necessary and sufficient conditions for excitability. The key step is to leverage the necessary condition found in the previous section to reduce the excitability problem for Gaussian states with nonzero mean to the corresponding zero-mean problem.

To do so, we first introduce notation for shifting the one-point function of a Gaussian state while leaving its connected two-point function unchanged. Let
\omega
be a Gaussian state. In equation (
8
), the one-point function enters only through the phase factor. Hence, given a linear functional
v
satisfying the reality condition
v[f]^{*}=v[f^{*}]
, we define a VEV-shifted Gaussian state
\omega^{v}
by

\omega^{v}(e^{i\phi[f]})=\omega(e^{i\phi[f]})e^{-iv[f]}.

(20)

From this, correlation functions of fields
\phi[f]
in the state
\omega^{v}
coincide with those of the shifted fields
(\phi[f]-v[f])
in the state
\omega
.
6
6

6

Since connected

-point functions for

are insensitive to c-number shifts,
\omega
and
\omega^{v}
share the same connected

-point functions for all

For

\omega^{v}
is Gaussian if and only if
\omega
is, since Gaussian states can equivalently be characterized by having vanishing connected

-point functions for

This set of correlators inherits positivity from
\omega
, and hence defines a Gaussian state with one-point function

\omega^{v}(\phi[f])=\omega(\phi[f])-v[f]\,.

(21)

Note that choosing

subtracts away the full one-point function, yielding a zero-mean version of
\omega
, whose two-point function is given by the

\omega
.
7
7

7

Explicitly, in the case where

\displaystyle\omega^{v}(\phi[f]\phi[g])=\omega\left((\phi[f]-\omega(\phi[f]))(\phi[g]-\omega(\phi[g]))\right)=\omega(\phi[f]\phi[g])-\omega(\phi[f])\omega(\phi[g])\,.

In particular, this means that
\omega^{v}
and

Given two Gaussian states
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
, with respective means

That is,

\quad\quad\omega_{2}\prec\omega_{1}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{aligned} &\omega_{2}^{v_{2}}\prec\omega_{1}^{v_{1}},\;\text{and}\\
&\omega_{2}(\phi[f])-\omega_{1}(\phi[f])\text{ is }\mu_{1}\text{-bounded}.\end{aligned}

(23)

To prove this, we first note that

Excitability under simultaneous VEV shifts

Let

Abelian excitability

4.3
Centrally pure excitability

We now address the case in which
\omega

We begin by decomposing the completed phase space

\mathcal{K}_{\mu}=\mathcal{K}_{\mu}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{\mu}^{\perp}\,.

(47)

Correspondingly, we decompose

\ket{v}_{\mu}=\ket{v_0}_{\mu}+\ket{v_\perp}_{\mu}.

(48)

We then claim that the desired representative of
\omega^{v}

\ket{\omega^v}=e^{-\frac{1}{4}\innerproduct{v_0}{v_0}_{\mu}}e^{\frac{i}{2}\phi[Rv_{\perp}]}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\phi[v_{0}]}\ket{\omega}.

(49)

The rationale behind this choice is as follows.
Without modifying the von Neumann algebra of physical observables, one can extend the

\phi[\psi]

The resulting
\ast

Moreover, since
\psi_{0}

\psi_{\perp}

\phi[\psi_{0}]

\phi[\psi_{\perp}]

\omega(\phi[\psi_{\perp}]\phi[\psi_{0}])

Consequently, the state
\omega

\omega\cong\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{\perp}

\omega_{\perp}

\omega^{v}

48

\omega_{0}^{v_{0}}\otimes\omega_{\perp}^{v_{\perp}}

The state
|\omega^{v}\rangle

\omega_{0}^{v_{0}}

\omega_{\perp}^{v_{\perp}}.

To explicitly verify that equation (

We begin by writing these expectation values explicitly as

\displaystyle\expectationvalue*{e^{i\phi[f]}}{\omega^v}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle v_{0}|v_{0}\rangle_{\mu}}\langle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\phi[v_{0}]}\omega|e^{-\frac{i}{2}\phi[Rv_{\perp}]}e^{i\phi[f]}e^{\frac{i}{2}\phi[Rv_{\perp}]}|e^{-\frac{1}{2}\phi[v_{0}]}\omega\rangle.

\displaystyle\expectationvalue*{e^{i\phi[f]}}{\omega^v}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle v_{0}|v_{0}\rangle_{\mu}}e^{i\langle f|R^{2}|v_{\perp}\rangle_{\mu}}\langle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\phi[v_{0}]}\omega|e^{i\phi[f]}|e^{-\frac{1}{2}\phi[v_{0}]}\omega\rangle.

\displaystyle\expectationvalue*{e^{i\phi[f]}}{\omega^v}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle v_{0}|v_{0}\rangle_{\mu}}e^{i\langle f|R^{2}|v_{\perp}\rangle_{\mu}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega(\phi[f+iv_{0}]^{2})}.

\displaystyle\expectationvalue*{e^{i\phi[f]}}{\omega^v}

4.4
General excitability

To address the problem of exciting a VEV shift

Here, we are using that by

In the present setting, where

Going forward, we will assume that

To show

-boundedness of

satisfying

\widehat{v}[f_{1}\oplus f_{2}]=\innerproduct{\hat v }{f_1\oplus f_2}_{\widehat{\mu}}.

Using

5.2
Boundary dual perspective

We now demonstrate that the VEV excitability condition (
71
) for free fields in AdS has a simple boundary interpretation through the holographic dictionary.

In the semiclassical regime, where backreaction is suppressed,
the holographic dictionary implies a duality between free fields
\phi(X)
in AdS and generalized free fields
\varphi(x)
living on the conformal boundary — see e.g.
7
for an explanation of this duality in algebraic terms.
The key formula underlying the duality is the extrapolate dictionary, namely the operator equation

\varphi(x)=N_{\nu}\lim_{z\to 0}z^{-\Delta}\phi(z,x)\,,

(75)

where we have added a factor of
N_{\nu}=2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)
as a normalization convention.
In the case of the Poincaré patch, the boundary is
d
-dimensional Minkowski spacetime,
and the boundary
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\partial,0}
is generated by fields
\varphi[f]
obeying

[\varphi[f],\varphi[g]]=-i\Omega_{\partial}[f,g],

(76)

where, following equation (
75
),
\Omega_{\partial}
is defined distributionally by

\Omega_{\partial}(x_{1},x_{2})=N_{\nu}^{2}\lim_{z_{j}\to 0}\left[z_{1}^{-\Delta}z_{2}^{-\Delta}\Omega(X_{1},X_{2})\right],

(77)

and where
\Omega
is the “advanced-minus-retarded” kernel (
62
) in the Poincaré patch.
Note that
\Omega_{\partial}
is not induced by any Klein-Gordon equation in the boundary Minkowski spacetime, which means that the boundary algebra is that of a generalized free field.

The vacuum state
\omega
in the bulk defines a natural zero-mean Gaussian state
\omega_{\partial}
on
\mathcal{A}_{\partial,0}
via the extrapolate dictionary:

\displaystyle\omega_{\partial}(\varphi(x_{1})\varphi(x_{2}))

\displaystyle=N^{2}_{\nu}\lim_{z_{j}\to 0}\left[z_{1}^{-\Delta}z_{2}^{-\Delta}\omega(\phi(X_{1})\phi(X_{2}))\right]

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{V_{+}^{*}}\frac{\differential{^d k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}|k^{2}|^{\nu}e^{-ik_{\mu}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{\mu}}\,.

By checking the behavior under scalings

\Delta=d/2+\nu
.
Smearing with boundary test functions gives

\omega_{\partial}(\varphi[f_{1}]\varphi[f_{2}])=\pi\int_{V_{+}^{*}}\frac{\differential{^d k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}|k^{2}|^{\nu}\widehat{f}_{1}(k)\widehat{f}_{2}(-k),

(79)

and it follows that the
\mu_{\partial}
-norm of a real boundary smearing function

\innerproduct{f}{f}_{\mu_{\partial}}=\pi\int_{V_{+}^{*}}\frac{\differential{^d k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}|k^{2}|^{\nu}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}\,.

(80)

We now consider the coherent excitation of the bulk AdS vacuum
\omega
by the VEV shift
v_{s}(X)
.
By the extrapolate dictionary, this makes the boundary state acquire a one-point function

\omega_{\partial}(\varphi(x))=N_{\nu}\lim_{z\to 0}z^{-\Delta}\omega(\phi(z,x))\,,

(81)

so that the corresponding boundary VEV shift is given by a limit of equation (
69

\displaystyle v_{s,\partial}(x)

\displaystyle=N_{\nu}\lim_{z\to 0}z^{-\Delta}v_{s}(z,x)

(82)

\displaystyle=\int_{V_{+}^{*}}\frac{\differential^{d}k}{2(2\pi)^{d}}|k^{2}|^{\nu/2}\left[\widetilde{v}_{s}(k)e^{ik_{\mu}x^{\mu}}+\widetilde{v}_{s}(-k)e^{-ik_{\mu}x^{\mu}}\right]\,.

Smearing leads to a boundary VEV functional that for real

(83)

We now show that the condition (
71
), which characterizes bulk VEV excitability, is also necessary and sufficient for excitability of the boundary VEV (

\displaystyle|v_{\partial}[f]|^{2}

Conversely, suppose that the VEV shift (

By equation (

To summarize, we have shown that the condition (

In particular, this proves that whether a bulk VEV is excitable depends only on its asymptotic behavior near the AdS boundary, as witnessed by the extrapolate dictionary (

This is compatible with the general principle that failures of excitability in physical states of a free field theory always result from infrared effects, rather than from details of the state at finite distance.
