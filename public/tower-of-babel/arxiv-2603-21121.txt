Weak Harnack inequality and Cartan property for nonlocal W s , 1 W^{s,1} -minimizers
Panu Lahti, Yuxin Li, Khanh Nguyen

Abstract
. We establish a weak Harnack inequality for nonlocal W s , 1 W^{s,1} -subminimizers in a complete, connected, doubling metric measure space where 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 . As a corollary, we prove that W s , 1 W^{s,1} -subminimizers are semicontinuous, up to a suitable choice of pointwise representative. We then prove Cartan-type properties for W s , 1 W^{s,1} -superminimizers. The theory turns out to be mostly analogous with the local case of BV \mathrm{BV} super- and subminimizers. Our results seem to be new even in the classical Euclidean setting.

Introduction

In 2016, Di Castro–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
DKP16
]
studied a weak Harnack inequality for the following problem:
for an open set
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

f\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<s<1
, and
1<p<\infty
,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u\,\leq\,0&\ \ {\rm in\ \ }\Omega,\\
u=f&\ \ {\rm in\ \ }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where the symbol
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
denotes the standard fractional
p
-Laplacian operator.
Here
W^{s,p}(A),
where

A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is measurable, denotes the collection of all
p
-integrable functions

f
on
A
for which the following semi-norm

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}(A)}:=\left(\int_{A}\int_{A}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is finite. Denote by
W^{s,p}_{0}(A)
the space consisting of all functions
u
in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for which
u=0

almost everywhere (a.e.) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A
. We say that
u
is a weak subsolution to
the problem (
1.1
) if
u=f
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and for every
nonnegative function
\eta\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\eta(x)-\eta(y))}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dx\,dy\geq 0.

By
[
DKP16
, Theorem 1.1]
, for every
0<\delta<1
and
every weak subsolution to the problem (
1.1
), there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
n,p,s
such that for all balls
B(x_{0},r)\subset\Omega
with radius
r>0
and center at
x_{0}
,

\displaystyle\quad\esssup_{y\in B(x_{0},r/2)}u(y)

\displaystyle\leq\delta\cdot{\rm Tail}{(u_{+},x_{0},r/2)}+C\cdot\delta^{-\frac{(p-1)n}{sp^{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{|B(x_{0},r)|}\int_{B(x_{0},r)}u_{+}^{p}(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
u_{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and

{\rm Tail}(u,x,r):=\left(r^{ps}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,r)}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

We have that
u
is a weak subsolution to the problem (
1.1
) if and only if
u
is
subminimizer of the following functional

F_{s,p}(u):=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}+2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\right)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dx\,dy,

i.e.
F_{s,p}(u)\leq F_{s,p}(u+\eta)
for every nonpositive function
\eta\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
see
[
DKP16
, Theorem 2.3]
. Here
\Omega^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.
The aim of this paper is to study a weak Harnack inequality for subminimizers, or alternatively
weak subsolutions to the problem (
1.1
), in the case of
p=1
.

It is natural to consider the problem in the more general setting of metric measure spaces
(X,d,\mu)
.
Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set.
For
0<s<1
, we consider the following functional

of two measurable functions

\displaystyle F(u,v):=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}+2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\right)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|-|v(x)-v(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y),

where

and

.

is said to be a

-sub(super)minimizer

It is an

Here

We denote by

.

{\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq c\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{Q}}

Our first main result is a weak Harnack inequality for subminimizers
in a metric space.

Theorem 1.1

(Weak Harnack inequality)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, connected metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure.
Let
0<s<1
and let
Q
be a doubling dimension such that

.
Let
\Omega\subset X
be a nonempty open set and let

be an
s
-subminimizer

Then there exists a constant

depending only on

such that for every
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
every
x_{0}\in\Omega
, and all

\esssup_{y\in B(x_{0},r)}(u(y)-k_{0})\leq C\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{Q}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{0},R)}(u-k_{0})_{+}\,d\mu.

The weak Harnack inequality that we establish in Theorem

.
This stability of the constant is essentially obtained

]

As a consequence of

, see for instance

]

s
-minimizer

in an open set
on the plane for which

is a nonlocal minimal surface, see
[

,

]

Nevertheless, using the weak Harnack inequality of Theorem

of

s
-sub(super)minimizers,
as in the corollary below.

Let
u:X\to[-\infty,\infty]
.
The

of
u
at

are defined respectively by

u^{\wedge}(x):=\sup\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(\{y\in B(x,r):u(y)<t\})}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0\right\}

and

(1.3)

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, connected metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure. Let
0<s<1
. Let
\Omega\subset X
be a nonempty open set.
Then

is lower semicontinuous on
\Omega
for every
s
-superminimizer
u
in
\Omega
, and
u^{\vee}
is upper semicontinuous on
\Omega

s
-subminimizer
u
in
\Omega
.

A set

is said to be

-thin

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\capp_{s,1}(A\cap B(x,r),B(x,2r))}{\capp_{s,1}(B(x,r),B(x,2r))}=0.

Here the condenser

is defined as in Subsection

.

With the help of the weak Harnack inequality of Theorem

,
we are able to prove the following

for superminimizers.

Theorem 1.3

(Weak Cartan property)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, connected metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure.
Let
0<s<1
.
Let
A\subset X
be
s
-thin at
x\in\overline{A}\setminus A
.
Then there exist

k\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
s
and the doubling constant,
R>0
, and
sets
E_{0},E_{1},\ldots,E_{k-1}\subset X
that satisfy the following properties:

(1)

each
{\bf 1}_{E_{j}}
is an
s
-superminimizer in
B(x,R)
for each
j=0,\ldots,k-1
;

(2)

{\bf 1}_{E_{j}}\in W^{s,1}(X)
for each
j=0,\ldots,k-1
;

(3)

\max\{{\bf 1}_{E_{0}}^{\wedge},{\bf 1}_{E_{2}}^{\wedge},\,\ldots,{\bf 1}_{E_{k-1}}^{\wedge}\}\equiv 1
on
A\cap B(x,R)
;

(4)

{\bf 1}_{E_{0}}^{\vee}(x)={\bf 1}_{E_{2}}^{\vee}(x)=\ldots={\bf 1}_{E_{k-1}}^{\vee}(x)=0
;

(5)

the set

\left\{y\in X:\max_{0\leq j\leq k-1}{\bf 1}_{E_{j}}^{\vee}(y)>0\right\}

is
s
-thin at
x
.

The claim
3
in Theorem
1.3
gives that there is at least one
j\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
such that
x\in\overline{E_{j}}\setminus E_{j}
and
\lim_{E_{j}\cap A\ni y\to x}{\bf 1}_{E_{j}}^{\wedge}(y)=1
,
in particular
{\bf 1}_{E_{j}}^{\wedge}
fails to be upper semicontinuous at
x
.
In Example
2.11
, we construct a set
A

in a Euclidean space that is
s
-thin at the origin

,
such that

and moreover

.

and

are applicable.

Notice that Theorem

-superminimizers

whose

If only one superminimizer is needed, then we use the term

If

has strictly positive capacity, we can show such a property.

Theorem 1.4

(Strong Cartan property)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, connected metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure. Let

.
Let

be

-thin

\lim_{A\ni y\to x}u^{\wedge}(y)=\infty>u^{\vee}(x).

2.1.
Nonlocal minimizers

A triple
(X,d,\mu)
is said to be a metric measure space if
(X,d)

is a nonempty separable metric space and
\mu
is a locally finite Borel-regular
outer measure on

.
Hence for every

A\subset X
, we have

\mu(A)=\inf\{\mu(O):A\subset O\subset X,\,O\ {\rm is\ open}\},

see e.g.
[

, Proposition 3.3.37]
.
Then
\mu(A)
is well-defined for every
A\subset X
.

Recall that we denote by
B(x,r)
the open ball with radius
r>0
and center
x\in X
.
For
\tau>0
, we set
\tau B(x,r):=B(x,\tau r)
.
We always assume
\mu(B)>0
for every ball
B=B(x,r)
.
On a few occasions, we use closed balls, denoted by

\overline{B}{(x,r)}:=\{y\in X:d(y,x)\leq r\}
.

For an open set
\Omega\subset X
and
a measurable function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty]
, we set

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y),

where
B_{x,y}:=B(x,d(x,y))\cup B(y,d(y,x))
, and

\|u\|_{W^{s,1}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(\Omega)}.

We denote by
\dot{W}^{s,1}(\Omega)
and
W^{s,1}(\Omega)
the spaces consisting of all measurable functions
u
on
\Omega
for which the quantities
\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(\Omega)}
and
\|u\|_{W^{s,1}(\Omega)}
are respectively finite. We denote by
W^{s,1}_{0}(\Omega)
the collection of all functions
u\in W^{s,1}(X)
such that
u=0
a.e. on
\Omega^{c}
, where
\Omega^{c}:=X\setminus\Omega
.
We also notice
that if
\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(\Omega)}<\infty
, then

is locally integrable on

;

we have for a.e.

and
\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)<\infty
. Hence for each ball
B\subset\Omega
with radius

, we have that for a.e.

,

\displaystyle\leq|u(y)|+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\,d\mu(x)

because

.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set.
We consider the following functional
F
of
two measurable functions

on

F(u,v):=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}+2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\right)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|-|v(x)-v(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y).

A measurable function
u:X\to[-\infty,\infty]
is an
s
-superminimizer

(resp.
s
-subminimizer
) in
\Omega

and for every nonnegative (resp. nonpositive)

, we have

Note that such

is assumed to belong to

. It follows from adding

2.2.
Doubling measure and Poincaré inequality

We say that
(X,d,\mu)
is a
metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure
if there exists a doubling constant
C_{\mu}>0
such that

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{\mu}\mu(B(x,r))

for all balls

We say that

if there exist Poincaré constants

such that

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|u-u_{B(x,r)}|\,d\mu\leq C_{P}r\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,\lambda r)}g\,d\mu

, where

2.3.
Obstacle problem

Given a nonempty open set
\Omega\subset X
, a function
\psi:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty)
,
and a function
f\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
, we set

\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega):=\{u\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X):u\geq\psi\text{ a.e. on }\Omega\text{ and }u=f\text{ a.e. on }X\setminus\Omega\}.

We say that
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
is a
solution of the
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
-obstacle problem

(or briefly a
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
-solution
) if

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}\leq\|v\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}\quad\text{for all $v\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)$.}

If the characteristic function of a set
E
is a solution of the obstacle problem, we simply refer to
E
as a solution. Moreover, when
\psi=\mathbf{1}_{W}
for some
W\subset X
, we denote
\mathcal{K}_{W,f}:=\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}
.

We observe some useful properties of solutions.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

x\in X
and
0<r<\frac{1}{4}\diam X
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)\neq\emptyset
where
\Omega\subset B(x,r)

is a nonempty open set,
\psi:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty)
, and
f\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
.
Then there exists a
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
-solution
u\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
.

Furthermore, such a
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
-solution
u\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
is an
s
-superminimizer
in
\Omega
.

If moreover
f\in W^{s,1}(X)
, then such
u
also belongs to
W^{s,1}(X)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)\neq\emptyset
, there is
a sequence
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of functions
u_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\|u_{i}\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}=I:=\inf\{\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}:u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)\}<\infty.

We may assume that
\|u_{i}\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}<I+1
.
By the isoperimetric inequality (
2.7
),
we have for each
i\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\int_{X}|u_{i}-f|\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|u_{i}-f|\,d\mu\quad\textrm{since\ }u_{i}=f\ {\rm a.e.\ on\ }X\setminus\Omega

\displaystyle\leq{C_{1}r^{s}}\|u_{i}-f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq{C_{1}r^{s}}\left(I+1+\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}\right),

which implies that
u_{i}-f
is bounded in
L^{1}(X)
.
Combining this with the fact that both
u_{i}
and
f
are bounded in
\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
, we obtain that

u_{i}-f
is a bounded sequence in
W^{s,1}(X)
. Thus by
[
Kl25
, Theorem 5.2]
, there exists a subsequence (not relabeled and denoted
\{u_{i}-f\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
) and a function
v\in W^{s,1}(X)
such that
u_{i}-f\to v
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X)
. We can select a further subsequence (still not relabeled and denoted
\{u_{i}-f\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
) such that
u_{i}(x)-f(x)\to v(x)
for a.e.
x\in X
. Notice from
u_{i}\geq\psi
a.e. on
\Omega
and
u_{i}=f
a.e. on
X\setminus\Omega
that
v\geq\psi-f
a.e. on
\Omega
and
v=0
a.e. on
X\setminus\Omega
. Letting
u:=v+f
, we then have that
u\geq\psi
a.e. on
\Omega
and
u=f
a.e. on
X\setminus\Omega
.
Moreover,
u_{i}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X)
, and so Fatou’s lemma gives that

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\|u_{i}\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)}=I,

which implies
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
with minimal energy. Therefore,
u\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
is a
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
-solution.

We now prove the last two claims. First,
notice that
u+\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
for every nonnegative function
\varphi\in W^{s,1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and hence by the definition of a
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,f}(\Omega)
-solution, we obtain that
u\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
is an
s
-superminimizer
in
\Omega
.

Moreover,
if
f\in W^{s,1}(X)
, then we have from (
2.9
) that the above sequence
u_{i}
is bounded in
L^{1}(X)
and
u_{i}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
, and so Fatou’s lemma gives that
u\in L^{1}(X)
. This implies that
u\in W^{s,1}(X)
since
u\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
.
The proof is completed.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

2.4.
Capacity, thinness, and Hausdorff measure

As before, let
0<s<1
. We define the
(s,1)
-capacity
of
A\subset X
by

C_{s,1}(A):=\inf\|u\|_{W^{s,1}(X)},

where the infimum is taken over all functions
u\in W^{s,1}(X)
satisfying
u\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
A
.

We denote
A\Subset F
if
\overline{A}
is a compact subset of
F
, and we recall that

\supp u:=\overline{\{x\in X:u(x)\neq 0\}}
is the support of
u
.
For a bounded set
A
and a bounded open set
F
with
A\Subset F
, we define the
condenser
(s,1)
-capacity
by

\capp_{s,1}(A,F):=\inf\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)},

where the infimum is taken over all functions
u\in\dot{W}^{s,1}(X)
such that

u\equiv 1
in a neighbourhood of
A
and
\supp u\subset F
. Such
u
are called

admissible
for
\capp_{s,1}(A,F)
. For each given bounded open set
F
, the definition
gives that the condenser capacity
\capp_{s,1}(.,F)
is an outer capacity in the sense that
for any
A\Subset F
,

(2.11)

\capp_{s,1}(A,F)=\inf\{{\rm cap}_{s,1}(G,F):A\subset G\Subset F,\ G\text{\ is open}\}.

Recall that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete, connected metric measure space equipped with a doubling measure. Then we have from
[
BB23
, Proposition 4.1]
that for all balls
B(x,r)\subset B(x,R)\subsetneq X
with
\theta R\leq 2r\leq R
for a given
0<\theta<1
,

(2.12)

\frac{1}{C_{2}}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{s}}\leq\capp_{s,1}(B(x,r),B(x,R))\leq C_{2}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{s}},

where
C_{2}
depends only on
\theta
,
s
and the doubling constant.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
x\in X
and
0<r<r_{1}<r_{2}<\frac{1}{4}\diam X
.
Then there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
s
and the doubling constant such that
for every
A\subset B(x,r)
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\capp_{s,1}(A,B(x,r_{2}))\leq

\displaystyle\,\capp_{s,1}(A,B(x,r_{1}))

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\left(1+\frac{r_{2}^{s}}{(r_{1}-r)^{s}}\right)\capp_{s,1}(A,B(x,r_{2})).

Proof.

We may assume that
A\neq\emptyset
.
The first inequality is obtained by the definition of the condenser
(s,1)
-capacity.
We will prove the second inequality. Let
u
be an arbitrary admissible function for

\capp_{s,1}(A,B(x,r_{2}))
. Let
\eta
be a
\frac{2}{r_{1}-r}
-Lipschitz function such that

\eta\equiv 1
in a neighbourhood of
A
,
\supp\eta\Subset B(x,r_{1})
and
0\leq\eta\leq 1
.
Then
h:=u\eta
is admissible for
\capp_{s,1}(A,B(x,r_{1}))
.
Let
A_{j}:=B_{j+1}\setminus B_{j}
where
B_{j}:=B(y,2^{j}(r_{1}-r))
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
for

y\in X
.
We have from the doubling property that for
y\in X
,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{X}\frac{|\eta(y)-\eta(x)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{|\eta(y)-\eta(x)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{|\eta(y)-\eta(x)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{\frac{2}{r_{1}-r}d(x,y)}{\mu(B_{j})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{2}{\mu(B_{j})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{r_{1}-r}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}(2^{j}(r_{1}-r))^{1-s}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2^{j}(r_{1}-r))^{-s}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{(r_{1}-r)^{s}}\left(\frac{1}{1-2^{-(1-s)}}+\frac{1}{1-2^{-s}}\right)

(2.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{s(1-s)}\frac{1}{(r_{1}-r)^{s}},

where the third line is obtained since
\eta
is
\frac{2}{r_{1}-r}
-Lipschitz and

0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and the fourth line is obtained since the radius of
B_{j}
is
2^{j}(r_{1}-r)
.
Hence

\displaystyle\quad\capp_{s,1}(A,B(x,r_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|\eta(x)}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(y)||\eta(x)-\eta(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{s(1-s)}\frac{1}{(r_{1}-r)^{s}}\int_{B(x,r_{2})}{|u(y)|}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{isoperimetric-func.}}{\lesssim_{s}}\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

(2.16)

\displaystyle\,+\frac{r_{2}^{s}}{(r_{1}-r)^{s}}\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\mu(B_{x,y})d(x,y)^{s}}\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y).

Here the comparison constant in the last estimate depends only on
s
and the doubling constant.
Since
u
is an arbitrary admissible function for
\capp_{s,1}(A,B(x,r_{2}))
, this gives the desired inequality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space.
A function
u:X\to[-\infty,\infty]
is said to
be
C_{s,1}
-quasicontinuous
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an open set

G\subset X
such that
C_{s,1}(G)<\varepsilon
and
u|_{X\setminus G}
is continuous.

A point
x\in X
is said to be a
Lebesgue point
of a locally integrable function
f:X\to[-\infty,\infty]

if

(3.33)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|f-{f(x)}|\,d\mu=0.

We denote by
\mathcal{N}_{f}
the set of non-Lebesgue points of
f
.
The Lebesgue representative
f^{*}
of
f
at
x\in X
is defined by

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}f(y)\,dy=:f^{*}(x).

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
f\in W^{s,1}(X)
.
Then
C_{s,1}(\mathcal{N}_{f})=0
and
the Lebesgue representative
f^{*}
is
C_{s,1}
-quasicontinuous.

Proof.

By
[
KYZ11
, Proposition 4.1]
, we have
W^{s,1}(X)=M^{s}_{1,1}(X)
,
where
M^{s}_{1,1}
denotes the
Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin space
.
Moreover, the two norms
\|f\|_{W^{s,1}(X)}

and
\|f\|_{M^{s}_{1,1}(X)}
are comparable, with comparisons constants depending only
on
s
and the doubling constant.
In particular, the capacity associated with the
M^{s}_{1,1}
-norm is comparable with

C_{s,1}
.
By
[
HKT17
, Theorem 8.1]
, the claim holds for

-functions.

See also
[
BBS22

for an alternative proof of the above proposition

Lemma 3.8
