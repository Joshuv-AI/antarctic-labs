Sharp local sparsity of regularized optimal transport
Alberto González-Sanz, Rishabh S. Gvalani, Lukas Koch

Abstract
In recent years, the use of entropy-regularized optimal transport with L p L^{p} -type entropies has become increasingly popular. In this setting, the solutions are sparse, in the sense that the support of the regularized optimal coupling, supp ⁡ ( π ε ) \mathrm{supp}(\pi_{\varepsilon}) , shrinks to the support of the original optimal transport problem as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . The main open question concerns the rate of this convergence. In this paper, we obtain sharp local results away from the boundary. We prove that the supports supp ( π ε ( ⋅ ∣ x ) ) \mathrm{supp}(\pi_{\varepsilon}(\cdot\mid x)) of the conditional measures, π ε ( ⋅ ∣ x ) \pi_{\varepsilon}(\cdot\mid x) , behave like balls of radius ε 1 d ⁡ ( p − 1 ) + 2 \varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}} . This allows us to show that the regularized potentials are uniformly strongly convex and to derive the rate of convergence of these potentials toward their unregularized limit. Our results generalize the results of (González-Sanz and Nutz, SIAM J. Math. Anal.) and (Wiesel and Xu, Ibid) to the multivariate case and beyond the case of self-transport.

Introduction

The optimal transport (OT) problem between two probability measures
\lambda
and
\mu
is defined as (cf.
[
15
]
)

\displaystyle{\rm OT}:=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\lambda,\mu)}\int\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\pi,

(OT)

where
\pi\in\Pi(\lambda,\mu)
if
\pi(\cdot\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=\lambda
and
\pi(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\cdot)=\mu
.
In this paper we consider the problem

\displaystyle{\rm ROT}_{\varepsilon,p}:=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\lambda,\mu),\pi\ll\lambda\otimes\mu}\int\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\pi+\varepsilon\int h_{p}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}(\lambda\otimes\mu)}\right)\mathrm{d}(\lambda\otimes\mu),

(ROT)

where
h_{p}(z)=\frac{|z|^{p}-1}{p-1}
for
p\in(1,2]
,
\lambda\otimes\mu
denotes the product measure and
\ll
stands for absolute continuity. A minimizer of (
ROT
) (resp. (
OT
)) is called ROT plan (resp. OT plan) and denoted by
\pi_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
\pi_{0}
).
Recently, ROT has gained popularity as an alternative to entropic optimal transport (EOT).
Recent results show that ROT can be computed in linear time
[
9
]
and avoids the curse of
dimensionality
[
8
,
7
]
—exactly the same as EOT
[
12
]
. However, the transport plans of EOT and ROT
are quite different. While the EOT plan has maximal support, ROT plans have sparse support
[
17
]
.
This is fundamentally because ROT plans
\pi_{\varepsilon}
have density with respect to the product measure
given by (see
[
13
,
1
,
8
]
)

\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\frac{d\pi_{\varepsilon}}{d(\lambda\otimes\mu)}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{q-1}q^{q-1}}\left(f_{\varepsilon}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}(y)-\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}\right)_{+}^{q-1},

(1)

where
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
are the solutions of the dual problem

{\rm ROT}^{*}_{\varepsilon,p}=\sup_{a,b}\int a(x)+b(y)-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{q-1}q^{q}}\left({a(x)+b(y)-\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}}\right)_{+}^{q}\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y).\\

(D-ROT)

Here
q
denotes the Young conjugate of
p
, i.e.,
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
.
In EOT, the solutions are of the form

\pi_{\varepsilon}^{EOT}(x,y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=e^{\frac{f_{\varepsilon}^{EOT}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}^{EOT}(y)-\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}}{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}(\lambda\otimes\mu)(x,y)

(2)

with
(f_{\varepsilon}^{EOT},g_{\varepsilon}^{EOT})
being the dual solutions of EOT. The positive part in
(
1
) allows the density to vanish and the support to narrow. In view of (
2
), the density of the EOT plan cannot vanish. Indeed, under some conditions (see
[
13
]
) the support of
\pi_{\varepsilon}
converges to the support of the OT plan. In this paper we characterize the rate of convergence at which the diameters of the sections

\mathcal{S}_{x}=\{y:\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y)>0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{y}=\{x:\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y)>0\}

decrease. This problem has been studied by
[
10
]
and
[
16
]
, where the sharp rates have been obtained for univariate data and general marginals as well as multivariate data but
\mu=\lambda
. In this paper we obtain, under mild conditions, that, for
x
in the interior
{\rm int}(\Omega_{0})
of the support
\Omega_{0}
of
\lambda
,

\mathbb{B}\left(\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x),\frac{1}{R_{0}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}}\right)\subset\mathcal{S}_{x}\subset\mathbb{B}\left(\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x),R_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}}\right),

where
R_{0}
depends on the distance from
x
to the boundary of
\Omega_{0}
. Here

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{x}}y\xi(x,y)^{q-2}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{x}}\xi(x,y)^{q-2}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(y)},\\
\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(y)=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{T}_{y}}x\xi(x,y)^{q-2}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)}{\int_{\mathcal{T}_{y}}\xi(x,y)^{q-2}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)},\end{cases}\quad{\rm for}\ \xi(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\psi_{\varepsilon}(y),

(3)

are the gradients of the convex functions
(\varphi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon})=(\|\cdot\|^{2}/2-f_{\varepsilon},\|\cdot\|^{2}/2-g_{\varepsilon})
(cf.
[
16
]
or
[
11
]
for the convexity of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
and

). This result is

3.1
, which is proved in
Section

5.1
. In light of the examples in
Section

6

\|\nabla^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}}\leq C
derived in
[
11
]
. We underline that, with our proof techniques, a uniform bound

would generalize most of our local-type results up to the boundary.

Our second contribution is to show that the ROT convex potentials

are uniformly strongly convex in

. In particular,

3
Main results

In this paper, we assume that
\lambda
and
\mu
are probability measures with compact supports
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
, respectively. We denote

\Omega_{0}:=\mathrm{supp}\,\lambda,\qquad\Omega_{1}:=\mathrm{supp}\,\mu.

We further assume that both
\lambda
and
\mu
admit
C^{0,\alpha}
densities, for some
\alpha>0
, which are bounded above and bounded away from zero on their supports. Finally, we assume that the optimal transport map
T=\nabla\varphi:\Omega_{0}\to\Omega_{1}
between
\lambda
and
\mu
is bi-
C^{1,\alpha}
, that is,
T
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism with
C^{1,\alpha}
inverse. In particular, due to
[
2
,
4
]
, all our results hold when
\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1}
are
\mathcal{C}^{2}
convex domains.

Our main result, showing the precise sparsity for the section
\mathcal{S}_{x}=\{y:\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y)>0\}
is the following. The same result holds for
\mathcal{T}_{y}=\{x:\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y)>0\}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Interior sharp sparsity)
.

For any smooth domain
K_{0}\Subset\Omega_{0}
, there exists
R_{0}=R_{0}(K_{0})
and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(K_{0})
such that for every
x\in K_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}\left(\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x),\frac{1}{R_{0}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}}\right)\subset\mathcal{S}_{x}\subset\mathbb{B}\left(\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x),R_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}}\right).

(4)

If
\Omega_{1}
is convex, the result holds for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

Theorem

3.1
states that the sections
\mathcal{S}_{x}
behave like balls of radii approximately
\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}}
. In light of the examples in
Section

6
this rate is sharp. The proof uses the estimate

\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{K_{0}}|\nabla^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}|\lesssim 1
shown in
[
11
]
.
In the following result we show that the restriction
\varphi_{\varepsilon}|_{K_{0}}
of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
to
K_{0}\Subset\Omega_{0}
is uniformly strongly convex as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Corollary 3.2

(Interior strong convexity)
.

Let the assumptions of
Theorem

3.1
hold. Then for every
K_{0}\Subset\Omega_{0}
there exists
C,\varepsilon_{0}>0
, such that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}|_{K_{0}}
is
C
-strongly convex on any convex subset of
K_{0}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.

Theorem

3.1
describes completely how the support contracts as
\varepsilon
decreases. From here it is easy to derive that the Lebesgue measure
|\mathcal{S}_{x}|
of
\mathcal{S}_{x}
is of order
\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d+2}}
.
We now focus on analyzing how it shrinks toward the support of the transport plan
\pi_{0}
.
Recall that the support of
\pi_{0}
is contained in the graph of the gradient map
\nabla\varphi
. We follow a strategy very similar to that of
[
10
, Chapter 6]
.
More precisely, we first control the projection onto the barycenter using the arguments of
[
3
, Section 4.2]
.
We then exploit the sparsity of the support.
Define

T_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\int y(\xi(x,y))_{+}^{q-1}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\int(\xi(x,y))_{+}^{q-1}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}=\int y\pi_{\varepsilon}(y|x),

where
\pi_{\varepsilon}(y|x)
denotes the conditional measure of the ROT plan of
y
given
x
. The same argument employed by
[
3
]
or
[
10
]
yields

\|T_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}\lesssim\int\|x-y\|^{2}d(\pi_{\varepsilon}-\pi_{0})(x,y)\leq{\rm ROT}_{\varepsilon}-{\rm OT}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{d(p-1)+2}},

(5)

where the last inequality follows from
[
5
]
.
Let
{\rm d}(\mathcal{S}_{(\cdot)},\nabla\varphi)
denote the mapping
x\mapsto\sup_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{x}}\|y-\nabla\varphi(x)\|
.
Since
\mathcal{S}_{x}
is convex and
T_{\varepsilon}(x)
is an average on
\mathcal{S}_{x}
,
T_{\varepsilon}(x)\in\mathcal{S}_{x}
.
Hence, for
K_{0}\Subset\Omega_{0}
, we get

\|{\rm d}(\mathcal{S}_{(\cdot)},\nabla\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(K_{0})}\leq\|T_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(K_{0})}+\sup_{x\in K_{0}}{\rm diam}(\mathcal{S}_{x})\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}},

where we used
Theorem

3.1
and (
5
).
Since

\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{x}}y(\xi(x,y))_{+}^{q-2}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{x}}(\xi(x,y))_{+}^{q-2}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}\in\mathcal{S}_{x},
we have shown the following result.

Corollary 3.3

(Rates of ROT map)
.

Fix
K_{0}\Subset\Omega_{0}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
,

\|\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(K_{0})}\leq\|{\rm d}(\mathcal{S}_{(\cdot)},\nabla\varphi)\|_{L^{2}(K_{0})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}}.

If
\Omega_{1}
is convex, the result holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

4.1
Regularized optimal transport

Recall that
\lambda
and
\mu
are probability measures with compact supports
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
, respectively and
\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu
satisfy our assumptions.

(i)

The strong duality

{\rm ROT}_{\varepsilon,p}={\rm ROT}_{\varepsilon,p}^{*}

holds.

(ii)

The primal problem (
ROT

\pi_{\varepsilon}\in\Pi(\lambda,\mu)

(iii)

The dual problem (

(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})\in L^{\infty}(\lambda)\times L^{\infty}(\mu)

(iv)

A pair
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})\in L^{\infty}(\lambda)\times L^{\infty}(\mu)
is an optimizer of the dual problem (
D-ROT
) if and only if there exists a version of
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
such that

\begin{cases}\int\left(f_{\varepsilon}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}(y)-\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}\right)_{+}^{q-1}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=\varepsilon^{q-1}q^{q-1}\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega_{0},\\
\int\left(f_{\varepsilon}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}(y)-\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}\right)_{+}^{q-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=\varepsilon^{q-1}q^{q-1}\quad\text{for all }y\in\Omega_{1}.\end{cases}

(6)

For such a version
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
\Omega_{i}
and

Moreover,
f_{\varepsilon}
and
g_{\varepsilon}
are uniformly Lipschitz in
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
.

(v)

Let
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
solve (
6
) and abbreviate
\xi(x,y):=f_{\varepsilon}(x)+g_{\varepsilon}(y)-\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}
.
Then there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int\left(\xi(\cdot,y)\right)_{+}^{q-2}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\geq\delta\quad\mbox{on }\Omega_{0},\qquad\int\left(\xi(x,\cdot)\right)_{+}^{q-2}\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\geq\delta\quad\mbox{on }\Omega_{1}.

Now we show higher order of differentiability. It is convenient to work instead with

\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}-f_{\varepsilon}\quad{\rm and}\quad\psi_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}-g_{\varepsilon},

which are convex functions on convex subsets of
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
, respectively, as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 4.2
.

It follows that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is convex and
\mathcal{C}^{1}
with gradient

\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{x}}y(\xi(x,y))^{q-2}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{x}}(\xi(x,y))^{q-2}\;\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}

(7)

on any convex subset of
\Omega_{0}
.
As a consequence, for all

, if

Proof.

To show that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is convex on convex subsets of

, we follow the same strategy as in the proof of
[

for

gives

\displaystyle\int\left(\langle(1-t)x+tz,y\rangle-(1-t)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-t\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)-\psi_{\varepsilon}(y)\right)_{+}^{q-1}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int\left((1-t)(\langle x,y\rangle-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\psi_{\varepsilon}(y))+t(\langle z,y\rangle-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)-\psi_{\varepsilon}(y))\right)_{+}^{q-1}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

4.2
Convex analysis

The
convex conjugate
(or
Legendre–Fenchel transform
) of
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is the function

f^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
defined by

f^{*}(y):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big\{\langle x,y\rangle-f(x)\big\}.

The following result is well known. It relates the boundedness of the second derivative of
f
with the strong convexity of
f^{*}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
f\colon\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and has second derivatives that are uniformly bounded in
\Omega
by
L>0
almost everywhere. Then for
x\in\Omega,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the following are equivalent:

(i)

x\in\partial f^{\ast}(y)
,

(ii)

\nabla f(x)=y
,

(iii)

f^{\ast}(y)+f(x)=\langle x,y\rangle
.

Further, for any
y,y_{0}\in\textup{dom}\;f^{\ast}
and
z\in\partial f^{\ast}(y_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(y)\geq f^{\ast}(y_{0})+\langle z,y-y_{0}\rangle+\frac{1}{2L}\|y-y_{0}\|^{2}.

Proof.

Setting
f=+\infty
outside of
\Omega
, we obtain a convex, proper and lower-semicontinuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The first claim is
[
14
, Theorem 23.5]
. While the second statement is certainly not new, we provide a proof for the convenience of the reader. Due to the gradient Lipschitzness of
f
, there is
L>0
such that for any
x,z\in\Omega
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\langle y,x\rangle-f(x)\geq\langle y,x\rangle-f(z)-\langle\nabla f(z),x-z\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|z-x\|^{2}.

Taking supremums over
x
, and choosing
x=z+\frac{y-\nabla f(z)}{L}
on the right-hand side, we find

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(y)\geq

\displaystyle\left\langle y,z+\frac{y-\nabla f(z)}{L}\right\rangle-f(z)-\left\langle\nabla f(z),\frac{y-\nabla f(z)}{L}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2L}\|y-\nabla f(z)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle y_{0},z\rangle-f(z)+\langle y-y_{0},z\rangle+\frac{1}{L}\|y-\nabla f(z)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2L}|y-\nabla f(z)|^{2},

(9)

for every
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In particular, taking

z\in\partial f^{\ast}(y_{0})
in (
9
), we deduce

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(y)\geq

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(y_{0})+\langle y-y_{0},z\rangle+\frac{1}{2L}\|y-y_{0}\|^{2},

which concludes the proof.
∎

6
Explicit solution for self-transport with Lebesgue marginals

We now specialise to the case of

with Lebesgue marginals, i.e.
\lambda=\mu=\mathcal{L}

the normalised Lebesgue measure on the flat unit torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{d}

equipped with the geodesic distance

In this setting, the problem (

\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}),\;\pi\ll\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}}\int\frac{1}{2}\,d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(x,y)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\pi\;+\;\varepsilon\int h_{p}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L})}\right)\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L})\,,

(23)

and the identity map
T=\mathrm{Id}
is the unique OT map from
\mathcal{L}
to itself.

By the symmetry of (
23
) under the exchange
(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)
,
the dual optimisers
(f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
of (

) satisfy
f_{\varepsilon}=g_{\varepsilon}

\mathcal{L}

Substituting into (

(24)

Moreover, since the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}
is invariant under all translations

\tau_{z}\colon x\mapsto x+z\pmod{1}
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}

is a dual optimizer of (

), then so is

(f_{\varepsilon}(\cdot+z),f_{\varepsilon}(\cdot+z))
for every

. As a result, uniqueness of dual optimizers up to an additive constant

[
8

) forces

to be constant,

f_{\varepsilon}\equiv C_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
.
The density (
24

\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{q-1}q^{q-1}}\Bigl(2C_{\varepsilon}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(x,y)^{2}\Bigr)_{+}^{q-1},

(25)

which depends on

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Bigl(2C_{\varepsilon}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(0,z)^{2}\Bigr)_{+}^{q-1}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\int_{0}^{R_{\varepsilon}}\Bigl(2C_{\varepsilon}-\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}\Bigr)^{q-1}r^{d-1}\;\mathrm{d}r.

q-1=\frac{1}{p-1}
and

q-1+\frac{d}{2}=\frac{d(p-1)+2}{2(p-1)}
to simplify the exponent of
\varepsilon
.
In particular,
C_{\varepsilon}\approx\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{d(p-1)+2}}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
and the support radius satisfies
R_{\varepsilon}=2\sqrt{C_{\varepsilon}}\approx\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d(p-1)+2}}
,
consistent with
Theorem

3.1
.

Remark 6.1
.

A similar explicit solution can be found for the self-transport problem on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
with the marginals chosen to be the normalised surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Appendix A
Reynolds’ transport theorem

For the reader’s convenience we sketch here the proof of a slightly non-standard version of Reynolds’ transport theorem.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded domains, and assume that
\Omega_{1}

is Lipschitz. Let

\mathcal{R}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ f(x,y)>0\}.

Assume that:

(i)

for every convex
K_{0}\subset\Omega_{0}
and
K_{1}\subset\Omega_{1}
, the map

f
is concave in
x
on
K_{0}\times\Omega_{1}
and concave in
y
on
\Omega_{0}\times K_{1}
;

(ii)

|\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}|>0
for every
x\in\Omega_{0}
;

(iii)

f\in C^{1}(U)
for some open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

containing

\{(x,y):x\in\Omega_{0},\ y\in\overline{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}}\};

(iv)

h\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{1}})
;

(v)

\mu
admits a density, also denoted by
\mu
, which is bounded above and bounded away from

0
on
\Omega_{1}
.

For
s\geq 0
, define

g(x):=\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,f(x,y)^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,f(x,y)^{s}\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Then
g\in W^{1,\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega_{0})
and, for almost every
x\in\Omega_{0}
,

\nabla g(x)=\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,\nabla_{x}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\\
+\int_{\partial\mathcal{R}(x)\cap{\Omega_{1}}}h(y)\,f(x,y)_{+}^{s}\,\langle V,n\rangle\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(y),

(28)

where
n
is the unit normal to the moving part of the boundary

\partial\mathcal{R}(x)\cap{\Omega_{1}}
, and
V
is its velocity field
as
x
varies. (When
s=0
, the first term is understood to be absent. When
s>0
, the boundary term
vanishes because
f=0
on
\partial\mathcal{R}(x)\cap{\Omega_{1}}
.)

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in\Omega_{0}
. Since
|\mathcal{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega_{1}|>0
and

\mathcal{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega_{1}
is a Lipschitz domain, we may find a finite number
M
of strongly star-shaped domains
\omega_{i}\ni y_{i}
such that
\mathcal{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega_{1}=\cup\omega_{i}
, i.e. there exists
y_{i}\in\omega_{i}
,
r_{i}>0
such that
\omega_{i}
is star-shaped with respect to each
y\in\mathbb{B}(y_{i},r_{i})
. By continuity of
f
, after shrinking to a neighborhood
N\Subset\Omega_{0}
of
x_{0}
there exists
c_{0}>0
such that
\min_{i,y\in\mathbb{B}(y_{i},r_{i})}f(x,y)\geq c_{0}
for all
x\in N
. For
x\in N
, write

\displaystyle\Gamma(x)\coloneqq\partial\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}.

Note that for
x\in N
,
\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}
is a Lipschitz set and hence, we may write
\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}=\cup\omega_{i}(x)
for a finite collection of
M(x)
star-shaped domains
\omega_{i}
. Since the map
x\to\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}
is continuous with respect to Hausdorff distance, we may ensure, upon reducing
N
if required, that
M(x)=M
and moreover,
B(y_{i},r_{i}/2)\in\omega_{i}(x)
for each
x\in N
and
i\leq M
.

Now, for
y\in\Gamma(x)
let
i
be such that
y\in\omega_{i}(x)
. Note that
Z(y)=\cup_{z\in B(y_{i},r_{i}/2)}[z,y]\subset\omega_{i}
is a convex subset of
\Omega_{1}
containing
y
and
y_{i}
. Consequently, due to the concavity of
y\to f(x,y)
on
Z(y)
,

f(x,y_{i})-f(x,y)\leq\langle\nabla_{y}f(x,y),y_{i}-y\rangle.

Because
f(x,y)=0
on
\Gamma(x)
and
f(x,y_{i})\geq c_{0}
, we obtain

c_{0}\leq\langle\nabla_{y}f(x,y),y_{i}-y\rangle\leq\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y)\|\,\|y_{i}-y\|.

Since
\Omega_{1}
is bounded,
\|y_{i}-y\|\leq\operatorname{diam}(\Omega_{1})<\infty
, and therefore

|\nabla_{y}f(x,y)|\geq c_{1}>0\qquad\text{for all }x\in N,\ y\in\Gamma(x),

for some constant
c_{1}
depending only on
N
,
\{y_{i}\}
,
\{r_{i}\}
and
\Omega_{1}
. In particular, the level set
\Sigma:=\{(x,y)\in N\times\Omega_{1}:\ f(x,y)=0\}

is, by the implicit function theorem, a
C^{1}
hypersurface in

N\times\Omega_{1}
. Thus
\Gamma(x)
is a
C^{1}
hypersurface in
\Omega_{1}
,
depending
C^{1}
on
x
.

Because
\nabla_{y}f
is continuous and bounded away from
0
on
\Sigma
,
after shrinking
N
if needed there exist
\tau>0
and constants
0<c_{2}\leq C_{2}<\infty

such that for every
x\in N
and every

y\in\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}
with
\operatorname{dist}(y,\Gamma(x))<\tau
,

c_{2}\,\operatorname{dist}(y,\Gamma(x))\leq f(x,y)\leq C_{2}\,\operatorname{dist}(y,\Gamma(x)).

(29)

Indeed, this follows from the mean value theorem along the normal segment joining

y
to its projection onto
\Gamma(x)
.
Fix an index
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Now we claim that

A_{i}(x):=\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}}|h(y)|\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,|\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)<\infty,

(30)

for every
x\in N
when
s>0
. We derive

\displaystyle A_{i}(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}\cap\{\operatorname{dist}(y,\Gamma(x))\geq\tau\}}|h(y)|\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,|\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}\cap\{\operatorname{dist}(y,\Gamma(x))<\tau\}}|h(y)|\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,|\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{\infty}s\tau^{s-1}\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}+\|h\|_{\infty}s\tau^{s-1}\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}\cap\{\operatorname{dist}(y,\Gamma(x))<\tau\}}f(x,y)^{s-1}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

and obtain (
30
) as a consequence of (
29
).

Next, consider the boundary term. Let
n
be the unit normal to
\Gamma(x)
.
If
\gamma(t)
is a curve on
\Sigma
representing the motion of the free boundary when

x_{i}
varies, then differentiating the identity

f(x+te_{i},\gamma(t))=0
at
t=0
yields

\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)+\langle\nabla_{y}f(x,y),V_{i}(x,y)\rangle=0,

where
V_{i}
is the velocity of the boundary when
x_{i}
varies.
Taking the scalar product with
n
gives

\langle V_{i},n\rangle=-\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}f}{\langle\nabla_{y}f,n\rangle}.

Since
|\langle\nabla_{y}f,n\rangle|=\|\nabla_{y}f\|\geq c_{1}
on
\Gamma(x)
, we obtain
|\langle V_{i},n\rangle|\leq C
on
\Gamma(x)

for a constant
C
independent of
x\in N
.
Hence

B_{i}(x):=\int_{\Gamma(x)}|h(y)|\,f(x,y)_{+}^{s}\,|\langle V_{i},n\rangle|\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(y)<\infty.

(31)

(For
s>0
, this is in fact identically zero since
f=0
on
\Gamma(x)
.)

Choose a sequence of smooth sets
\{\Omega_{1}^{m}\}_{m}
such that
\Omega_{1}^{m}\subset\Omega_{1}^{m+1}\Subset\Omega_{1}
and
\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{1}^{m}=\Omega_{1}.

Set

D_{m}(x):=\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}^{m},\qquad g_{m}(x):=\int_{D_{m}(x)}h(y)\,f(x,y)^{s}\,d\mu(y).

The moving part of
\partial D_{m}(x)
is

\Gamma_{m}(x):=\Gamma(x)\cap\Omega_{1}^{m},

while the part on
\partial\Omega_{1}^{m}
is fixed in
x
. Because the integrability estimates (
30
) and (
31
) hold uniformly on compact subsets of
N
,
dominated convergence yields, for every
x\in N
,

g_{m}(x)\longrightarrow g(x),\qquad\text{as }\ m\to\infty,

(32)

and similarly

\int_{D_{m}(x)}h(y)\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\\
\longrightarrow\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y),

(33)

as well as

\int_{\Gamma_{m}(x)}h(y)\,f(x,y)_{+}^{s}\,\langle V_{i},n\rangle\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(y)\\
\longrightarrow\int_{\Gamma(x)\setminus\partial\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,f(x,y)_{+}^{s}\,\langle V_{i},n\rangle\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(y),

(34)

as
m\to\infty
and
for all
x\in N
. Therefore it is enough to prove (
28
) on each smooth domain
\Omega_{1}^{m}
.
So, from now on, we may assume that the ambient domain is smooth.

Fix
m
and
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.
Since the moving boundary
\Gamma_{m}(x)
is
C^{1}
in
x
and the remaining part of

\partial D_{m}(x)
is fixed, Reynolds’ transport theorem (cf.
[
6
, Appendix C.4.]
) gives

\partial_{x_{i}}g_{m}(x)=\int_{D_{m}(x)}\partial_{x_{i}}\!\big(h(y)\,f(x,y)^{s}\,\mu(y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\Gamma_{m}(x)}h(y)\,f(x,y)_{+}^{s}\,\langle V_{i},n\rangle\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(y).

Since
h
and
\mu
do not depend on
x
, we have, for
s>0
,

\partial_{x_{i}}\!\big(h(y)\,f(x,y)^{s}\,\mu(y)\big)=h(y)\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)\,\mu(y).

Thus

\partial_{x_{i}}g_{m}(x)=\int_{D_{m}(x)}h(y)\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\\
+\int_{\Gamma_{m}(x)}h(y)\,f(x,y)_{+}^{s}\,\langle V_{i},n\rangle\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(y).

(35)

Set
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(N)
.
For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

-\int_{N}g_{m}(x)\,\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{N}\varphi(x)\,\partial_{x_{i}}g_{m}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Substituting (
35
) and using the limits (
32
), (
33
) and (
34
),
together with dominated convergence, we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{N}g(x)\,\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\varphi(x)\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{N}\varphi(x)\int_{\partial\mathcal{R}(x)\setminus\partial\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,f(x,y)_{+}^{s}\,\langle V_{i},n\rangle\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence, the distributional derivative
\partial_{x_{i}}g
is represented by the right-hand
side above. Since this holds for every
i
, we conclude that

g\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(N)
and

\partial_{x_{i}}g(x)=\int_{\mathcal{R}(x)\cap\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,s\,f(x,y)^{s-1}\,\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\\
+\int_{\partial\mathcal{R}(x)\setminus\partial\Omega_{1}}h(y)\,f(x,y)_{+}^{s}\,\langle V_{i},n\rangle\,\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(y),

for almost every
x\in N
. Since
x_{0}\in\Omega_{0}
was arbitrary, we conclude that
g\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{0})
. From the estimates (
30
) and (
31
), we derive
g\in W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{0})
. The result follows.
∎
