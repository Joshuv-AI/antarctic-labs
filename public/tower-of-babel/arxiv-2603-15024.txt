Abstract
. We prove the local Lipschitz continuity of viscosity solutions for two-phase free boundary problems for the p p -Laplacian with non-zero right hand side, where p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) . This is the optimal regularity for the problem. We also obtain the local Hölder continuity for a larger class of problems. The results introduced here are new even in the homogeneous situation, that is, when the right hand side is zero. Our work applies to merely viscosity solutions, which allows a wide applicability.



1.
Introduction and main results

In this article we obtain the local Lipschitz continuity of viscosity solutions to two-phase free boundary problems governed by the

p
-Laplacian with non-zero right hand side.
This continues our work in
[
FL1
]
,
[
FL2
]
and
[
FL3
]
(see
[
FLS
]
as well).

More precisely, we denote by

\Delta_{p}u:=\mbox{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u),

where
p
is a constant,
1<p<\infty
. Then, the problem we investigate here is the following:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}u=f,&\hbox{in $\Omega^{+}(u)\cup\Omega^{-}(u)$},\\
&\\
u_{\nu}^{+}=G(u_{\nu}^{-},x),&\hbox{on $F(u):=\partial\Omega^{+}(u)\cap\Omega,$}\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain and

\Omega^{+}(u):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>0\},\qquad\quad\Omega^{-}(u):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)\leq 0\}^{\circ},

while
u_{\nu}^{+}
and
u_{\nu}^{-}
denote the normal derivatives in the inward
direction to
\Omega^{+}(u)
and
\Omega^{-}(u)
respectively.
F(u)
is called the
free boundary
of
u
.
Furthermore,
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is continuous in
\Omega^{+}(u)\cup\Omega^{-}(u)
.

The function

G(t,x):[0,\infty)\times\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty),

G(\cdot,x)\in C^{2}(0,\infty)
for
x\in\Omega
,
satisfies:

(P1)

G(\cdot,x)
is strictly increasing for
x\in\Omega
and
G(t,x)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, uniformly in
x\in\Omega
.

In our main result we assume that
G(t,x)
behaves like
t
, for
t
large, uniformly in
x
. More precisely, we assume that :

(P2)

\frac{\partial G(t,x)}{\partial t}\to 1
,
\frac{\partial^{2}G(t,x)}{\partial t^{2}}=O(\frac{1}{t})
, as
t\to\infty
, uniformly in
x\in\Omega
,

(P3)

G(t,\cdot)\in C^{0,\bar{\gamma}}(\Omega)
uniformly in
t
, for some
0<\bar{\gamma}<1
.

When
f\not\equiv 0
and
p\neq 2
we assume as well that:

(P4)

G(t,x)\equiv G(t)
, with
t^{-1}G(t)
strictly decreasing, for
t
large.

This research includes
G(t,x)=(g(x)+t^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
,
p>1
, where
g\in C^{0,\bar{\gamma}}(\Omega)
, for some
0<\bar{\gamma}<1
,
g>0
, which arises in several applications (see for instance, Appendix A in
[
FL3
]
).

Our assumptions on
f
are

(1.2)

f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\qquad f\text{ is continuous in }\Omega^{+}(u)\cup\Omega^{-}(u).

Nevertheless, the arguments also apply when
f
is merely bounded measurable, but we require (
1.2
) to avoid technicalities.

The main result in this work is the following one (for notation and the precise definition of viscosity solution to (
1.1
) we refer to Section
2
).

Theorem 1.1

(Optimal regularity)
.

Let
u
be a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) in
B_{1}
. Assume that (
1.2
) holds in
B_{1}
and
G
satisfies assumptions (P1),
(P2) and (P3) in
B_{1}
. If
f\not\equiv 0
and
p\neq 2
assume that also (P4) holds, then

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}+1),

where
C=C(n,p,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},G)
is a positive constant.

Let us point out that this is the optimal regularity for the problem.

We here follow the approach introduced in
[
DS
]
for uniformly elliptic fully nonlinear operators.

The heuristics behind the proof of Theorem
1.1
is that, in the regime of
big gradients
, the free boundary condition becomes a continuity condition for the gradient (i.e., no-jump).
As a consequence, the desired gradient bound follows from interior
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for the
p
-Laplace operator and from free boundary regularity results for two-phase viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) proven in our recent paper
[
FL3
]
.

In the research on two-phase free boundary problems a central question is the optimal regularity of solutions. In
[
CJK
]
the authors proved a monotonicity formula that allows to obtain the local Lipschitz continuity of solutions to (
1.1
) in the case of
p=2
, that is, when the equation is linear.
No such monotonicity formula is known up to now for the general case
p\neq 2
.

We remark that in the present article we are concerned with the question of local Lipschitz continuity of
viscosity solutions
for two-phase free boundary problems for the
p
-Laplacian
with non-zero right hand side
.

The results introduced here are new even in the homogeneous situation, that is, when
f\equiv 0
, since, to our knowledge, the only regularity result existing in literature, about Lipschitz continuity across the free boundary, concerns minima of the
p
-Bernoulli functional in the homogeneous case, see
[
DK
]
.

Our work applies to merely viscosity solutions, which allows a wide applicability.

We point out that in the one phase case, i.e., when
u\geq 0
, the local Lipschitz continuity of solutions to (
1.1
) follows from our previous paper
[
FL2
]
, where a more general one phase free boundary problem was treated.

We remark that carrying out for the inhomogeneous
p
-Laplace operator a strategy originally devised for

uniformly elliptic operators
presents
challenging difficulties due to the type of nonlinear behavior of the
p
-Laplacian. This was already the case in our previous works
[
FL1
]
,
[
FL2
]
and
[
FL3
]
for the treatment of this problem. In fact, the
p
-Laplacian is a nonlinear operator that appears naturally in divergence structure from minimization problems, i.e., in the form
{\rm div}A(\nabla u)=f(x)
, with

(1.3)

\lambda|\eta|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial\eta_{j}}(\eta)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda|\eta|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
. This operator is singular when
1<p<2
and degenerate when
p>2
. Dealing with this problem in the presence of
a non-zero right hand side
is very delicate since, in this case,
we can not neglect the factor
|\eta|^{p-2}
in (
1.3
)
.

In particular, the discussion at the end of Section 3.2 in
[
DS
]
, concerning the generalization of their results to some equations with zero right hand side,
does not apply to our inhomogeneous problem (
1.1
)
.

The process of obtaining the local Lipschitz continuity of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) we follow requires to prove first the local Hölder continuity of viscosity solutions to this problem —which is interesting by itself. In fact, we get this result under more general conditions on
G
. The precise result is the following:

Theorem 1.2

(Local Hölder continuity)
.

Let
u
be a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) in
B_{1}
. Assume that, for
\sigma>0
and
M>0
, there holds

(1.4)

\sigma t\leq G(t,x)\leq\sigma^{-1}t,\quad\mbox{for}\>\>t>M,\>x\in B_{1}.

Then
u\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})
, for some
0<\alpha<1
, and

\|u\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})}+1),

where
\alpha
depends only
\sigma,n
and
p
, and
C>0
depends only
\sigma,M,n,p
and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
.

An important contribution of this article can be found in Section
3
, where we revisit
[
FL3
]
. More precisely, we refine a
C^{1,\gamma}
regularity result for flat free boundaries we proved in
[
FL3
]
, obtaining Theorem
3.1
. We develop, in addition, some improvements of it for the particular cases
f\equiv 0
,
p=2
and
G(t,x)\equiv G(t)
. See Theorems
3.5
,
3.7
and
3.9
—which are key tools in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In this way, we are able to get Theorem
1.1
for very general free boundary conditions.
Moreover, we obtain some preliminary results, based on these theorems, that play a fundamental role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(see Remark
3.11
).

Let us mention that when the problem is truly nonlinear singular / degenerate with a non-zero right hand side —i.e., when
f\not\equiv 0
and
p\neq 2
— due to the difficulties inherent to this situation, we are assuming in our main result that (P4) also holds. We point out that assumptions of these type appear frequently in the study of free boundary problems. See, for instance,
[
C1
,
C2
,
DFS1
,
DFS2
]
.

Let us also stress that we include here the case in which
p=2
—i.e., the case of Laplace operator— relying on our work
[
FL3
]
(see Section
3
). Notice that, although
[
DS
]
includes Laplace operator, the case of a non-zero right hand side and a general free boundary condition
u_{\nu}^{+}=G(u_{\nu}^{-},x)
was not explicitly developed there. Hence, Theorem
1.1
gives an explicit proof for the case
p=2
which is different from the one that can be deduced from the monotonicity formula of
[
CJK
]
—
only available for this particular situation
.

As already mentioned, the main tool in the approach we follow in order to prove the local Lipschitz continuity, inspired by
[
DS
]
, is a regularity result for flat free boundaries for viscosity solution to free boundary problem (
1.1
). Let us point out that in the uniformly elliptic linear and fully nonlinear cases, the corresponding results were obtained in
[
DFS1
]
and
[
DFS2
]
respectively. It is worth mentioning that in these papers it was assumed, all along the works, the Lipschitz continuity of the viscosity solutions under consideration. However, in
[
DS
]
it was stated a theorem —that can be derived from the results in
[
DFS2
]
— concerning the free boundary regularity for the fully nonlinear uniformly elliptic case, which holds under a nondegenerate situation, that does not assume the Lipschitz continuity of solutions.

The present article makes use of a free boundary regularity theorem for viscosity solutions to problem (
1.1
), that we proved in
[
FL3
]
, where the Lipschitz continuity of solutions was not assumed. We believe that this will contribute to the comprehension of the powerful tools developed in
[
DS
]
—both for the problem studied in the present paper and for the uniformly elliptic case studied in
[
DS
]
.

Let us emphasize once more that the fact that we are dealing with a nonlinear singular / degenerate operator with a non-zero right hand side introduces challenging difficulties that we overcome in the present research, with the aid of the companion paper
[
FL3
]
.

2.
Basic definitions, notation and preliminaries

In this section, we provide notation, basic definitions and some preliminaries that will be relevant for our work.

Notation.
For any continuous function
u:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
we denote

\Omega^{+}(u):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>0\},\quad\Omega^{-}(u):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)\leq 0\}^{\circ}

and

F(u):=\partial\Omega^{+}(u)\cap\Omega.

We refer to the set
F(u)
as the
free boundary
of
u
, while
\Omega^{+}(u)
is its
positive phase
(or
side
) and
\Omega^{-}(u)
is the
nonpositive phase
.

We begin with some remarks on the
p
-Laplacian. In particular, we recall the relationship between the different notions of solutions to
\Delta_{p}u=f
we are using, namely, weak and viscosity solutions. Then we give the definition of viscosity solution to problem (
1.1
) and we deduce some consequences. We here refer to the usual definition of
C
-viscosity sub/supersolution and solution of an elliptic PDE, see e.g.,
[
CIL
]
.

We start by observing that
direct calculations show that, for
C^{2}
functions
u
such that
\nabla u(x)\not=0
in some open set,

(2.1)

\begin{split}&\Delta_{p}u=\mbox{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{p-2}\left(\Delta u+(p-2)\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u\right),\end{split}

where

\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u:=\Big\langle D^{2}u\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\,,\,\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\Big\rangle

denotes the normalized
\infty
-Laplace operator.

We deduce, in addition, that

(2.2)

\begin{split}&|\nabla u|^{p-2}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0}}^{-}(D^{2}u)\leq\Delta_{p}u\leq|\nabla u|^{p-2}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0}}^{+}(D^{2}u),\end{split}

where
\lambda_{0}:=\min\{1,p-1\}
and
\Lambda_{0}:=\max\{1,p-1\}.

As usual, if
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
are numbers, and
e_{i}
is the
i-
th eigenvalue of the
n\times n
symmetric matrix
M,
then
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
denote the extremal Pucci operators and are defined (see
[
CC
]
) as

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M)&=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i},\\
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(M)&=\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}+\lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}.\end{split}

First we need

Definition 2.1
.

Assume that
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

We say that
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)

is a weak supersolution of

(2.3)

\Delta_{p}u=f,\quad\mbox{in}\>\>\Omega,

if for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

\varphi\geq 0
, there holds that

-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,f\,dx.

Analogously, we say that
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)

is a weak subsolution of
(
2.3
),
if for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

\varphi\geq 0
, there holds that

-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,f\,dx.

Finally,
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
2.3
) if it is both a weak sub- and supersolution.

Next we recall the following standard notion.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
u,v\in C(\Omega)
, we say that
v

touches
u
by below (resp. above) at
x_{0}\in\Omega
if
u(x_{0})=v(x_{0})
and

u(x)\geq v(x)\quad(\text{resp. $u(x)\leq v(x)$})\quad\text{in a neighborhood $O$ of $x_{0}$.}

If
this inequality is strict in
O\setminus\{x_{0}\}
, we say that

v
touches
u
strictly by below (resp. above).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
u
and
f
be continuous functions in

\Omega
. We say that
u
is a viscosity solution to
\Delta_{p}u=f
in

\Omega
, if the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

u
is a viscosity subsolution in
\Omega
. That is, for every
v\in C^{2}(\Omega),
if
v
touches
u
from above at
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\nabla v(x_{0})\not=0,
then
\Delta_{p}v(x_{0})\geq f(x_{0})
.

(b)

u
is a viscosity supersolution in
\Omega
. That is, for every
v\in C^{2}(\Omega),
if
v
touches
u
from below at
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\nabla v(x_{0})\not=0,
then
\Delta_{p}v(x_{0})\leq f(x_{0})
.

.

We point out that in

We denote

.

Let

\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(n,p,\sigma)=\min{\left\{1,\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)^{(p-1)}\right\}}\,\left((\frac{c_{0}}{2^{\gamma}-1}\gamma)^{p-1}2^{\gamma(1-p)-p}\Lambda_{0}(n-1)\right).

Then, recalling (
4.4
), we obtain

\Delta_{p}w\geq||f||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\qquad\text{ in }D\cap\left(\{w>0\}\cup\{w<0\}\right).

Let us now show that

(4.14)

u\geq w\quad\text{ on }\ D,

if
\varepsilon_{0}
is sufficiently small.

In fact, on one hand,
u\geq w
in
\overline{B_{d}(x_{0})}
follows as a consequence of (
4.12
) and the maximum principle.

On the other hand, it is not hard to check that
u\geq w
in
\{u\geq 0\}\cap(B_{2d}(x_{0})\setminus\overline{B_{d}(x_{0})}).
We next want to show that
u\geq w
in the set
\{u<0\}\cap(B_{2d}(x_{0})\setminus\overline{B_{d}(x_{0})}).
To be able to employ the maximum principle we only need to see that
u\geq w
on
\partial B_{2d}(x_{0})\cap\{u<0\}.
We use that, by (
4.10
) and (
4.13
), in this set we have
u\geq-\varepsilon_{0}
and

w=-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\frac{c}{d^{\gamma}}\,\frac{2^{\gamma}-1}{2^{\gamma}}=-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\frac{c_{0}}{2^{\gamma}}.

Therefore, it is enough to fix
\varepsilon_{0}
small universal, depending on
\sigma
, and then (
4.14
) follows.

We now observe that, since (
4.14
) holds and
u
and
w
touch at
y_{0}\in F(u)
, then, by Definition
2.5
-(ii)(1), we have

|\nabla\psi(y_{0})|\leq G(\frac{\sigma}{2}|\nabla\psi(y_{0})|,y_{0}).

However, the application of (
4.5
) and (
4.13
) yields

|\nabla\psi(y_{0})|>G(\frac{\sigma}{2}|\nabla\psi(y_{0})|,y_{0}),

provided
m
is chosen small enough universal, depending on
\sigma
. Namely,

\frac{\sigma}{2}|\nabla\psi(y_{0})|=\gamma\frac{\sigma}{2}cd^{-\gamma-1}>\gamma\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\frac{c_{0}}{2^{\gamma}-1}>m.

This gives a contradiction and proves (
4.11
), which implies the claim (
4.6
).

In order to conclude the proof, let us now suppose that
0\not\in F(u).
If
B_{1/2}\cap F(u)=\emptyset
, we can apply interior estimates for the
p
-Laplace operator (see, for instance, Proposition 2.2 in
[
Fa
]
). If there is
{\hat{x}}\in B_{1/2}\cap F(u)
, the argument above in
B_{1/2}(\hat{x})
gives the
Hölder bound in
B_{1/4}(\hat{x}).
We finally use a covering argument and in this way we obtain the stated result.
∎

5.
Lipschitz continuity

In this section we prove the local Lipschitz continuity of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
), namely, Theorem
1.1
.
This proof will make use of regularity theorems obtained in Section
3
(i.e., Theorems
3.5
,
3.7
and
3.9
).
We emphasize that the application of these results requires a careful preliminary preparation (see Remark
3.11
).

We will first prove an approximation lemma that will be a key device for Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u_{k}
be a viscosity solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}u_{k}=f_{k},&\hbox{in $B_{1}^{+}(u_{k})\cup B_{1}^{-}(u_{k})$},\\
&\\
(u_{k})_{\nu}^{+}=G_{k}((u_{k})_{\nu}^{-},x),&\hbox{on $F(u_{k})$},\end{array}\right.

with
f_{k}\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
and continuous in
B_{1}^{+}(u_{k})\cup B_{1}^{-}(u_{k})
and assume that
G_{k}
satisfies (P1) in
B_{1}
. Assume moreover that

(5.1)

u_{k}\to u^{*},

f_{k}\to 0,

and

(5.2)

G_{k}(x,t)\to G^{*}(t),

with

(5.3)

G^{*}(t)=t,

where the convergences hold uniformly on compact sets. Then,

\Delta_{p}u^{*}=0\quad\mbox{in}\>\>B_{1}.

Proof.

Arguing in a similar way as in
[
FL2
]
, Theorem 1.2, Step III (letting
p_{k}(x)\equiv p
in that proof), we show that

\Delta_{p}u^{*}=0\ \quad\text{in $B_{1}\cap\{u^{*}\neq 0\}$}.

We will next prove that the equation is satisfied across
\{u^{*}=0\}.
In fact, we have to see that if
P
is a quadratic polynomial with
\Delta_{p}P>0,
then
P
can not touch
u^{*}
strictly from below at a point
x^{*}
where
u^{*}(x^{*})=0
and
\nabla P(x^{*})\neq 0.
Suppose by contradiction that there exists such a point.

Without loss of generality, we will assume that
\nabla P(x^{*})=\gamma e_{n}
, with
\gamma>0.

We define

\ \psi:=(1+\varepsilon)P^{+}-P^{-}.

For some
\varepsilon>0
small,
\psi
still separates strictly from
u^{*}
on
\partial B_{\rho}(x^{*})
, for
\rho>0
small enough, and coincides with it at
x^{*}
.
Let us consider

\psi_{t}(x)=\psi(x+te_{n}),\quad x\in B_{\rho}(x^{*}).

Then, for
t=-C\varepsilon
,
C>0
large, we have that
\psi_{t}
is strictly below all
u_{k}
’s with
k
large enough, since (
5.1
) holds. We increase
t
until a small
c_{0}>0
to guarantee that
\psi_{t}
crosses
u^{*}
and all the
u_{k}
’s, with
k
large. Hence,
\psi_{t}
must touch the
u_{k}
’s for the first time at
t=t_{k}
small. Since the separation of
\psi
and
u^{*}
on
\partial B_{\rho}(x^{*})
is strict, the first touching point
x_{k}
can not occur there (if
c_{0}
is small depending on the separation on
\partial B_{\rho}(x^{*})
).
Recalling that
\Delta_{p}P>0
and
f_{k}\to 0
uniformly, as
k\to\infty,
we conclude that
x_{k}\in F(u_{k}).
However, taking into account (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we deduce that

(1+\varepsilon)|\nabla P(x_{k}+t_{k}e_{n})|>G_{k}(|\nabla P(x_{k}+t_{k}e_{n})|,x_{k}+t_{k}e_{n}).

Then, we get a contradiction to Definition
2.5
(ii)-(1) for
u_{k}
and the result follows.
∎

The main tool in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following result

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u
be a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) in
B_{2}
. Assume
G
satisfies assumptions (P2) and (P3) in
B_{1}
and
0\in F(u)
. If
f\not\equiv 0
and
p\neq 2
we assume that (P4) also holds.

There exist positive constants
L_{0}
,
\delta
and
C
(depending only on
n
,
p
,
G
and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}
) such that one of the following alternative holds:

(i)

u
is Lipschitz in
B_{\delta}
and
|\nabla u|\leq C\max\{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},L_{0}\}
in
B_{\delta}
.

(ii)

\frac{1}{\delta}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},L_{0}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
be fixed, to be precised later. We will assume by contradiction that there exist a sequence of constants
L_{k}\to\infty
, and a sequence of solutions
u_{k}
to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}u_{k}=f_{k},&\hbox{in $B_{2}^{+}(u_{k})\cup B_{2}^{-}(u_{k})$},\\
&\\
(u_{k})_{\nu}^{+}=G((u_{k})_{\nu}^{-},x),&\hbox{on $F(u_{k})$},\end{array}\right.

in
B_{2}
, with
0\in F(u_{k})
and
\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2})}\leq M_{0}
for some constant
M_{0}
, such that

u_{k}
does not satisfy neither option
(i)
nor
(ii).

Let

C_{k}:=\max\{\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},L_{k}\}

and

\tilde{u}_{k}:=\frac{u_{k}}{C_{k}},\quad\tilde{f}_{k}:=\frac{f_{k}}{C_{k}^{p-1}},\quad\tilde{G}_{k}(t,x):=\frac{G(C_{k}t,x)}{C_{k}}.

Then, every
\tilde{u}_{k}
is a viscosity solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}\tilde{u}_{k}=\tilde{f}_{k},&\hbox{in $B_{2}^{+}(\tilde{u}_{k})\cup B_{2}^{-}(\tilde{u}_{k})$},\\
&\\
(\tilde{u}_{k})_{\nu}^{+}=\tilde{G}_{k}((\tilde{u}_{k})_{\nu}^{-},x),&\hbox{on $F(\tilde{u}_{k})$},\end{array}\right.

with

\|\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1

and

(5.4)

\|\tilde{f}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\to 0,\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Now, using (P2) (first assumption) and (P3), we can apply Theorem
1.2
and conclude that, as
k\to\infty
, for a subsequence,

\tilde{G}_{k}(t,x)\to G^{*}(t),\quad\text{$G^{*}(t)=t$},

uniformly on compact sets of
(0,\infty)\times B_{2}
and

\tilde{u}_{k}\to u^{*},

uniformly on compact sets of
B_{1}
.

We can thus employ the compactness Lemma
5.1
to deduce that

\Delta_{p}u^{*}=0\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},\quad\text{ with }\ \|u^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1.

Next, from the local
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for the
p
-Laplace operator (see, for instance,
[
Fa
]
, Theorem 1.1), we conclude that

(5.5)

\|u^{*}-l\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq Cr^{1+\alpha},\quad\text{ for all }r\leq 1/2.

Here
C
and
\alpha
depend only on
n
and
p
, and
l(x)=a\cdot x
for a vector
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
|a|\leq C
.

Two different cases can occur:

Case 1.

|a|\leq\frac{1}{4}.

In this case, (
5.5
) yields

\frac{1}{\delta}|u^{*}|\leq\frac{1}{4}+C\delta^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{3}\quad\text{in $B_{\delta}$,}

if
\delta\leq 1/2
is chosen small enough depending only on
n
and
p
.
In this way, all
u_{k}
’s with
k
large satisfy
(ii)
, which gives a contradiction.

Case 2.

|a|>\frac{1}{4}

In this case we will apply flatness regularity results, derived from
[
FL3
]
, that we developed in Section
3
(see Theorems
3.5
,
3.7
and

3.9
, and Remark
3.11
).

In fact, using that
\tilde{u}_{k}
converges uniformly to
u^{*}
and (
5.5
) holds, we obtain, for every
k
large,

(5.6)

|\tilde{u}_{k}-a\cdot x|\leq\hat{C}\delta^{1+\alpha},\quad\text{in $B_{2\delta}$},

with
\hat{C}=\hat{C}(n,p)
. Let us now define

\beta_{k}:=|a|,\quad\alpha_{k}=\tilde{G}_{k}(|a|,0),\quad\omega:=\frac{a}{|a|}.

Then, from (
5.6
), recalling that
\tilde{G}_{k}
converges to the identity uniformly on compact sets, we deduce

|\tilde{u}_{k}-U_{\beta_{k}}|\leq 2\hat{C}\delta^{1+\alpha},\quad\text{in $B_{2\delta}$},

for
k
large, where

U_{\beta_{k}}(x):=\alpha_{k}(x\cdot\omega)^{+}-\beta_{k}(x\cdot\omega)^{-}.

Now, employing (
5.4
), we conclude from Theorems
3.5
,
3.7
and
3.9
(see Remark
3.11
) that, for large
k
,

\tilde{u}_{k}\in C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{B^{+}_{\delta}(\tilde{u}_{k})})\cap C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{B^{-}_{\delta}(\tilde{u}_{k})}),

with
C^{1,\gamma}
norms bounded by a universal constant, if
\delta
is chosen small enough universal.

As a consequence, the
\tilde{u}_{k}
’s are uniformly Lipschitz with universal constant. Therefore,

|\nabla u_{k}|\leq CC_{k}\quad\text{in $B_{\delta}$},

for
k
large.
This is in contradiction with the fact that the
u_{k}
’s do not satisfy
(i)
and concludes the proof.
∎

We are finally ready to prove our main result

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
L_{0}
,
\delta
and
C
be the universal constants in Lemma
5.2
.

We first assume
0\in F(u)
and define

L:=\max\{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})},L_{0}\}

and

a(r):=\frac{1}{r}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})},\quad r\leq 3/4.

We want to obtain that

(5.7)

a(\delta^{k})\leq CL,\quad\forall k\geq 1.

In fact, by Lemma
5.2
either alternative
(i)
or
(ii)
holds.

In case alternative
(i)
holds,
u
is Lipschitz in
B_{\delta}
and

|\nabla u|\leq CL\quad\text{in $B_{\delta}$}.

This shows that (
5.7
) is satisfied for every
k\geq 1.

In case alternative
(ii)
holds, we get

a(\delta)\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})},L_{0}\}\leq L.

Let us next rescale and iterate. We define, for
k\geq 1
and
x\in B_{1}
,

u_{k}(x):=\frac{u(\delta^{k}x)}{\delta^{k}},\quad{f}_{k}(x):=\delta^{k}f(\delta^{k}x),\quad G_{k}(t,x):=G(t,\delta^{k}x).

Then, every
{u}_{k}
is a viscosity solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}{u}_{k}={f}_{k},&\hbox{in $B_{1}^{+}({u}_{k})\cup B_{1}^{-}({u}_{k})$},\\
&\\
({u}_{k})_{\nu}^{+}={G}_{k}(({u}_{k})_{\nu}^{-},x),&\hbox{on $F({u}_{k})$}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover,

\|{f}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\|{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

Then, Lemma
5.2
applies to every
u_{k}
and thus, each
u_{k}
satisfies the conclusion of this lemma.

Suppose first that the
u_{k}
’s satisfy indefinitely the alternative
(ii)
of Lemma
5.2
. Therefore,

(5.8)

a(\delta^{k})\leq L,\quad\forall k\geq 1,

and then, (
5.7
) holds.

Otherwise, let us denote
\bar{k}\geq 1
the smallest
k
for which
u_{k}
does not satisfy
(ii)
. It is not hard to see that (
5.8
) holds for all
1\leq k\leq\bar{k}
and the same happens with (
5.7
). Moreover,

u_{\bar{k}}
satisfies the alternative
(i)
of Lemma
5.2
, which implies that
u_{\bar{k}}
is Lipschitz in
B_{\delta},
with

|\nabla u_{\bar{k}}|\leq C\max\{\|u_{\bar{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4})},L_{0}\}\quad\text{in $B_{\delta}.$}

Now, (
5.8
) for
k=\bar{k}
gives

|\nabla u_{\bar{k}}|\leq C\max\{\frac{1}{\delta}\|u_{\bar{k}-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta})},L_{0}\}\leq CL\quad\text{in $B_{\delta}$.}

Therefore (
5.7
) holds for every
k\geq\bar{k}+1
as well.

We now take
0<r<3/4
and
k
such that
{\delta}^{k+1}\leq r\leq{\delta}^{k}
. Then (
5.7
) implies

||u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq||u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{{\delta}^{k}})}\leq{CL}{\delta}^{k}\leq\frac{CL}{\delta}r.

The Lipschitz continuity of
u
in
B_{1/2}
then follows, which is the desired result.
∎
