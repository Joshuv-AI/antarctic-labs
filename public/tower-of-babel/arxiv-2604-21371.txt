Nonsmooth Nonconvex-Concave Minimax Optimization: Convergence Criteria and Algorithms
Jinyang Shi, Luo Luo

Abstract
This paper considers constrained stochastic nonsmooth minimax optimization problem of the form min 𝐱 ∈ 𝒳 ⁡ max 𝐲 ∈ 𝒴 ⁡ f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) = 𝔼 ⁡ [ F ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 , ξ ) ] \min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f\left(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\right)=\mathbb{E}[F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\mathbf{\xi})] , where the objective f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) is concave in 𝐲 \mathbf{y} but possibly nonconvex in 𝐱 \mathbf{x} , the stochastic component F ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 , ξ ) F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\mathbf{\xi}) indexed by random variable ξ \mathbf{\xi} is mean-squared Lipschitz continuous, and the feasible sets 𝒳 {\mathcal{X}} and 𝒴 {\mathcal{Y}} are convex and compact. We introduce the notion of ( η x , η y , δ , ϵ ) (\eta_{x},\eta_{y},\delta,\epsilon) -Goldstein saddle stationary point (GSSP) to characterize the convergence for solving constrained nonsmooth minimax problems. We then develop projected gradient-free descent ascent methods for finding ( η x , η y , δ , ϵ ) (\eta_{x},\eta_{y},\delta,\epsilon) -GSSPs of the objective function f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) with non-asymptotic convergence rates. We further propose nested-loop projected gradient-free descent ascent methods to establish the non-asymptotic convergence for finding ( η , δ , ϵ ) (\eta,\delta,\epsilon) -generalized Goldstein stationary points (GGSP) ( Liu et al. 2024 ) of the primal function Φ ⁡ ( 𝐱 ) ≜ max 𝐲 ∈ 𝒴 ⁡ f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) \Phi(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}{f}\left(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\right) . It is worth noting that our algorithm designs and theoretical analyses do not require additional assumptions such as the weak convexity used in prior works on nonsmooth minimax optimization ( Lin et al. 2025 ; Bo0t and Böhm 2023 ) .

Introduction

We consider the following constrained stochastic nonsmooth minimax optimization problem

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\triangleq\mathbb{E}[F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\bm{\xi})],

(1)

where the objective
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is concave in
\mathbf{y}
but possibly nonconvex in
\mathbf{x}
, the stochastic component
F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\bm{\xi})
is indexed by random variable
\bm{\xi}\sim{\mathcal{D}}
, and feasible sets
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}
are convex and compact.
The nonconvex-concave minimax optimization includes broad applications in machine learning, such as generative adversarial networks
(
Goodfellow et al. 2014
;
Arjovsky et al. 2017
)
, distributional robust optimization
(
Rahimian and Mehrotra 2019
;
Jin et al. 2021
;
Sinha et al. 2018
;
Levy et al. 2020
;
Gao and Kleywegt 2023
;
Biggio et al. 2012
)
, AUC maximization
(
Guo et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2020a
;
Yuan et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2023
)
, and reinforcement learning
(
Jin and Sidford 2020
;
Qiu et al. 2020
;
Wai et al. 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2019
)
.

Most existing studies on nonconvex-concave minimax optimization focus on the problem with smooth objective function
(
Lin et al. 2020a
;
Jin et al. 2020
;
Thekumparampil et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Lin et al. 2020b
;
Lu et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2023b
;
Rafique et al. 2022
;
Huang et al. 2022
;
Luo et al. 2022
;
Luo et al. 2020
;
Xu et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Yang et al. 2023
;
Li et al. 2021a
;
Zhang et al. 2020a
;
Mahdavinia et al. 2022
)
.
For example,
Lin et al. 2020a
showed that gradient descent ascent (GDA) and its stochastic variants can achieve the approximate stationary points of smooth nonconvex-concave minimax problems with non-asymptotic convergence rates.
Later,
Lin et al. 2020b
provided inexact proximal point methods to achieve the improved first-order oracle complexity, nearly matching the lower bound in the specific nonconvex-strongly-concave setting
(
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2021a
)
.
In addition,
Xu et al. 2024
;
Huang et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2020
incorporated the two-point gradient estimate
(
Nesterov and Spokoiny 2017
)
into GDA and its variants, establishing efficient zeroth-order methods for nonconvex-concave minimax optimization.

In real-world applications, the objectives in minimax formulations are usually nonsmooth

(
Madry et al. 2018
;
Rahimian and Mehrotra 2019
;
Jiang and Chen 2023
;
Sinha et al. 2018
)
.
One popular setting is the composite minimax optimization, i.e., the objective consists of a smooth term and a simple nonsmooth convex regularization.
In this case, we can extend the algorithms for the solving smooth minimax problems by additionally introducing the proximal mapping, which leads to the similar analysis and convergence rates to the smooth counterparts
(
Lu et al. 2020
;
Zhao 2024
;
Barazandeh and Razaviyayn 2020
;
Huang et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2024
)
.
However, the algorithms with proximal mapping are impractical when the nonsmooth part in the objective is complicated.
Another line of research considers
the nonsmooth nonconvex-concave minimax optimization with Lipschitz continuity.
Specifically,
Lin et al. 2025
;
Bo0t and Böhm 2023
establish the convergence guarantees of GDA methods for solving nonsmooth nonconvex-concave minimax problems with Lipschitz continuous objective,
while their analyses require that the Moreau envelope
(
Moreau 1965
)
of the primal function be well-defined, which depends on an additional assumption of weak convexity.
Unfortunately, the weak convexity does not always hold,
such as in the applications of statistical learning
(
Fan and Li 2001
;
Zhang et al. 2006
;
Zhang 2010a
;
Mazumder et al. 2011
;
Zhang 2010b
)
and training neural networks with ReLU activations
(
Nair and Hinton 2010
;
Zhang et al. 2020b
)
.
Very recently,
Masiha et al. 2026
established results without weakly convex assumption, while their analysis does not cover the fully nonsmooth setting since it still requires the objective
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
be smooth in
\mathbf{y}
.
This leads to a natural question:
Can we establish the convergence guarantees for the general nonsmooth nonconvex-concave minimax optimization?

In this paper, we give an affirmative answer to the above question.
Specifically, we introduce the notion of
(\eta_{x},\eta_{y},\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein saddle stationary point (GSSP) to describe the convergence of solving constrained nonsmooth minimax problem (
1
),
which goes beyond the existing criteria of the approximate (generalized) Goldstein stationary points for nonsmooth minimization problem
(
Liu et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2020b
;
Lin et al. 2022
)
.
We then propose single-loop zeroth-order methods for achieving
(\eta_{x},\eta_{y},\delta,\epsilon)
-GSSPs of the objective
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
with the non-asymptotic convergence guarantees.
In addition, we investigate the convergence with respect to the primal function
\Phi(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, which is guaranteed to be Lipschitz continuous when the objective function
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is Lipschitz continuous and concave in
\mathbf{y}

(
Lin et al. 2020a
)
.
Therefore, the
(\eta,\delta,\epsilon)
-generalized Goldstein stationary point (GGSP) for minimizing
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
over the feasible set
{\mathcal{X}}
is well-defined
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
.
This motivates us to propose double-loop zeroth-order methods for achieving approximate GGSPs of the primal function
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
.
It is worth noting that the previous algorithm designs and theory for nonsmooth nonconvex-concave minimax optimization requires the additional assumption that the objective function
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is weakly-convex in
\mathbf{x}
to guarantee the Moreau envelope of
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
and the (inexact) proximal point iteration to be well-defined
(
Lin et al. 2025
;
Bo0t and Böhm 2023
)
, which
is restrictive in real applications
(
Nair and Hinton 2010
;
Zhang et al. 2020b
)
.
In contrast, all of our results do not depend on the assumption of weak convexity.

Preliminaries

In this section, we first formalize assumptions for our stochastic nonsmooth nonconvex-concave minimax optimization problem,
then review the notions of approximate (generalized) Goldstein stationary points used for the convergence analysis of nonsmooth optimization.

2.1
Assumptions

For constrained stochastic minimax problem (
1
), we introduce the augmented sets as follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{X}}_{\delta}=\{\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u}:\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{X}},\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}},\|\mathbf{u}\|\leq\delta\}~~\text{and}~~{\mathcal{Y}}_{\delta}=\{\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{v}:\mathbf{y}\in{\mathcal{Y}},\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}},\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq\delta\}

(2)

for some
\delta>0
.
Our assumptions for the stochastic component
F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\bm{\xi})
and the objective function
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
are with respect to the augmented sets
{\mathcal{X}}_{\delta}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}_{\delta}
to align with the notation of
\delta
-Goldstein subdifferential (see Definition
2
) for nonsmooth analysis.

We suppose the stochastic component
F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\bm{\xi})
in our problem (
1
) is mean-squared Lipschitz continuous, which follows the standard setting in existing studies on stochastic zeroth-order nonsmooth nonconvex optimization
(
Lin et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2023a
;
Kornowski and Shamir 2024
)
.

Assumption 1
.

We suppose that the stochastic component
F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\bm{\xi})
is
L(\bm{\xi})
-Lipschitz continuous in
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
on the augmented set
{\mathcal{X}}_{\delta}\times{\mathcal{Y}}_{\delta}
for any
\bm{\xi}
, i.e. it holds

\displaystyle|F(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{1};\bm{\xi})-F(\mathbf{x}_{2},\mathbf{y}_{2};\bm{\xi})|\leq L(\bm{\xi})\left(\left\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{y}_{1}-\mathbf{y}_{2}\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for all
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{\delta}
and
\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{\delta}
.
Moreover, we suppose

has bounded second-order moment, i.e. there exists some constant

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\bm{\xi}}\big[L(\bm{\xi})^{2}\big]\leq L^{2}.

Note that Assumption
1

F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\bm{\xi})
is mean-squared Lipschitz continuous with parameter

for all
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{\delta}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|F(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{1};\bm{\xi})-F(\mathbf{x}_{2},\mathbf{y}_{2};\bm{\xi})|^{2}\right]\leq L^{2}\left(\left\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{y}_{1}-\mathbf{y}_{2}\right\|^{2}\right).

We impose the following assumption on the feasible sets
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{y}}
.

Assumption 2

We suppose the feasible sets
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}

{\mathcal{Y}}

.

We suppose the objective

is concave in
\mathbf{y}
.

Assumption 3
.

We suppose the objective function
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is concave in
\mathbf{y}
on the augmented set
{\mathcal{Y}}_{\delta}
, i.e.,
for all
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{\delta}
,

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x},\lambda\mathbf{y}_{1}+(1-\lambda)\mathbf{y}_{2})\;\geq\;\lambda f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{1})+(1-\lambda)f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{2}).

We also consider the stronger assumption that the objective

We suppose the objective function
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

(3)

. We suppose the function

.

We suppose the primal function

2.2
Generalized Goldstein Stationary Points

We first review the background in the analysis of nonsmooth minimization problem.
Recall that Rademacher’s theorem says Lipschitz continuous functions are differentiable almost everywhere. Hence, we define the Clarke subdifferential as follows
(
Clarke 1990
)
.

Definition 1

(
Clarke 1990
)
.

The Clarke subdifferential of a Lipschitz continuous
function

h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
at a point
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is defined as

\displaystyle\partial h(\mathbf{z})=\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{\,\mathbf{g}:\mathbf{g}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\nabla h(\mathbf{z}_{k}),\ \mathbf{z}_{k}\to\mathbf{z}\,\right\}\right),

where
\mathrm{conv}(\cdot)
is the convex hull.

It is known that finding an
\epsilon
-Clarke stationary point
\mathbf{z}
such that
\min\{\left\|\mathbf{g}\right\|:\mathbf{g}\in\partial h(\mathbf{z})\}\leq\epsilon
is computational intractable
(
Zhang et al. 2020b
)
,
so that we further consider the
\delta
-Goldstein subdifferential which is defined as follows
(
Goldstein 1977
)
.

Definition 2

(
Goldstein 1977
)
.

For given
\delta>0
and a Lipschitz continuous function
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, the
\delta
-Goldstein subdifferential at a point
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is defined as

\displaystyle\partial_{\delta}h(\mathbf{z}):=\operatorname{conv}\left(\cup_{\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{d}(\mathbf{z},\delta)}\partial h(\hat{\mathbf{z}})\right),

where
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}(\mathbf{z},\delta)=\{\mathbf{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\right\|\leq\delta\}
is the
d
-dimensional Euclidean ball centered at point
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with radius
\delta
.

Consequently, we introduce the notion of the
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point (GSP), which is widely used as the convergence criteria in the analysis of unconstrained nonconvex nonsmooth minimization problems
(
Zhang et al. 2020b
)
.

Definition 3

(
Zhang et al. 2020b
)
.

For given a Lipschitz function
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we say the point
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point of
h
if it holds

\displaystyle\min\big\{\|\mathbf{g}\|:\mathbf{g}\in\partial_{\delta}h(\mathbf{z})\big\}\leq\epsilon.

For the constrained nonconvex nonsmooth problem, we can generalize the standard
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point by replacing the subgradient in its definition with the subgradient mapping,
resulting in the criteria of
(\eta,\delta,\epsilon)
-generalized Goldstein stationary point (GGSP)
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
.

Definition 4

(
Liu et al. 2024
)
.

Consider minimization problem

\min_{\mathbf{z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}h(\mathbf{z})
with the Lipschitz continuous objective
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and the convex and compact feasible set
{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{z}}
.
We say the point
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
is a
(\eta,\delta,\epsilon)
-generalized Goldstein stationary point (GGSP) of the above minimization problem if it holds

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{g}\in\partial_{\delta}h(\mathbf{z})}\|G(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{g},\eta)\|\leq\epsilon,

where

G(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{g},\eta)=(\mathbf{z}-\Pi_{\mathcal{Z}}\left(\mathbf{z}-\eta\mathbf{g}\right))/{\eta}

is the subgradient mapping at point
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
with respect to
\mathbf{g}\in\partial_{\delta}h(\mathbf{z})
and
\eta>0
.
Here, we use the notation
\Pi_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathbf{w}):=\arg\min_{\hat{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}
to present the projection of the point
\mathbf{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{z}}
onto the feasible set
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Remark 1
.

It is worth noting that solving minimization problem

\min_{\mathbf{z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}h(\mathbf{z})
by projected subgradient descent with stepsize
\eta>0
can be written as
(
Nesterov 2013
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{+}=\mathbf{z}-\eta G(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{g},\eta).

Hence, the term
G(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{g},\eta)
in the definition of
(\eta,\delta,\epsilon)
-GGSP for constrained optimization corresponds to descent direction of projected subgradient descent, which aligns with the fact that term
\mathbf{g}\in\partial_{\delta}h(\mathbf{z})
in the notion of
(\delta,\epsilon)
-GSP (Definition
5
) for unconstrained problem corresponds to the descent direction of subgradient descent.

Remark 2
.

In fact, the Lipschitz continuity for the objective
h
in Definition
4
is only required on the set
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\delta}=\{\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w}:\mathbf{z}\in{\mathcal{Z}},\mathbf{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{z}},\|\mathbf{w}\|\leq\delta\}
to align with the notion of
\delta
-Goldstein subdifferential in Definition
2
.

3
Stationarity for Nonsmooth Minimax Optimization

We first introduce the notation of
(\eta_{x},\eta_{y},\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein saddle stationary point (GSSP) to characterize the convergence with respect to the objective
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
for our nonsmooth nonconvex-concave minimax problem (
1
).

Definition 5
.

We say the point
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
is a
(\eta_{x},\eta_{y},\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein saddle stationary point (GSSP) of the minimax optimization problem (
1
) if it holds

\displaystyle\min_{(\mathbf{g}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y})\in\partial_{\delta}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}\|G_{x}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}_{x},\eta_{x})\|\leq\epsilon\quad\text{and}~~\quad\min_{(\mathbf{g}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y})\in\partial_{\delta}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}\|G_{y}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}_{y},\eta_{y})\|\leq\epsilon,

where
\partial_{\delta}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is the
\delta
-Goldstein subdifferential that follows Definition
2
and

\displaystyle G_{x}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}_{x},\eta_{x})=\frac{\mathbf{x}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}-\eta_{x}\mathbf{g}_{x}\right)}{\eta_{x}}\quad\text{and}\quad G_{y}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}_{y},\eta_{y})=\frac{\Pi_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\mathbf{y}+\eta_{y}\mathbf{g}_{y}\right)-\mathbf{y}}{\eta_{y}}

(4)

are subgradient mapping at point
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}
with respect to
(\mathbf{g}_{x},\mathbf{g}_{y})\in\partial_{\delta}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and stepsizes
\eta_{x},\eta_{y}>0
for variables
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
, respectively.

The definition of our approximate GSSP is motivated by the subgradient descent ascent for solving constrained nonsmooth convex-concave minimax problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

which performs the iteration

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}_{+}=\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}-\eta_{x}\mathbf{g}_{x}\right),\\[2.84544pt]
\mathbf{y}_{+}=\Pi_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\mathbf{y}+\eta_{y}\mathbf{g}_{y}\right),\end{cases}

(5)

where

are stepsizes and

Note that the iteration scheme (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{x}_{+}=\mathbf{x}-\eta_{x}G_{x}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}_{x},\eta_{x}),\\[2.84544pt]
\mathbf{y}_{+}=\mathbf{y}+\eta_{y}G_{y}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{g}_{y},\eta_{y}),\end{cases}

Hence, terms

and

which is similar to the intuition of GGSP for constrained nonsmooth minimization problem described (Remark

Remark 3
.

5.2
The Complexity Analysis for PGFDA

We first consider finding approximate GSSPs in the nonconvex-strongly-concave setting.
For the smooth surrogate
f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and its primal function
\Psi_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=\max_{\mathbf{y}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\begin{split}&G_{x}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\nabla_{x}f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\eta_{x})=\frac{\mathbf{x}-\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}-\eta_{x}\nabla_{x}f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right)}{\eta_{x}},\\
&G_{y}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\nabla_{y}f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\eta_{y})=\frac{\Pi_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\mathbf{y}+\eta_{y}\nabla_{y}f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\right)-\mathbf{y}}{\eta_{y}},\\

(15)

where

G_{y}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\nabla_{y}f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\eta_{y})
,
and

Lemma 3
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
4
,

\displaystyle\left\|G_{x}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\nabla_{x}f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\eta_{x})\right\|\leq\left\|G(\mathbf{x},\nabla\Psi_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}),\eta_{x})\right\|+\frac{M_{f_{\delta}}}{\mu}\|G_{y}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\nabla_{y}f_{\delta}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),\tilde{\eta}_{y})\|,

for all
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{X}}
,
\mathbf{y}\in{\mathcal{Y}}
,
\eta_{x}>0
and
\tilde{\eta}_{y}\in(0,1/M_{f_{\delta}}]

where

(c).

6.1
Nested-Loop Projected Gradient-Free Descent Ascent

Recall that Proposition

\displaystyle\Phi_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}\sim{\mathrm{Unif}}({\mathbb{B}}^{d_{x}}(\mathbf{0},1))}\left[\max_{\mathbf{y}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{w},\mathbf{y})\right]

(21)

for some
\delta>0

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{X}}}\Phi_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}).

Following the idea of uniform smoothing, we introduce the zeroth-order gradient estimator for the function
\Phi_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})
as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}^{*}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{w},\bm{\xi})\triangleq\frac{d_{x}}{2\delta}(F(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{w},\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{w});\bm{\xi})-F(\mathbf{x}-\delta\mathbf{w},\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{x}-\delta\mathbf{w});\bm{\xi}))\cdot\mathbf{w},

\mathbf{w}\sim{\rm Unif}(\mathbb{S}^{d_{x}-1})

Since the exact
\mathbf{y}^{*}(\cdot)

and
\mathbf{y}^{-}\approx\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{x}-\delta\mathbf{w})

\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{w}
in practice, leading to the estimator

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{+},\mathbf{y}^{-};\mathbf{w},\bm{\xi})\triangleq\frac{d_{x}}{2\delta}\big(F(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{w},\mathbf{y}^{+};\bm{\xi})-F(\mathbf{x}-\delta\mathbf{w},\mathbf{y}^{-};\bm{\xi})\big)\cdot\mathbf{w}.

Based on above ideas, we propose Nested-Loop Projected Gradient-Free Descent Ascent (NL-PGFDA), which is detailed in Algorithm
3
.

7
Comparison with Related Work

In a recent work,
Lin et al. 2025
considered the nonsmooth minimax problem

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}}\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

(25)

where the objective
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is
L_{f}
-Lipschitz continuous and concave in
\mathbf{y}
.
They provided two-timescale first-order methods to find the
\epsilon
-stationary point of the Moreau envelope of the primal function
\Phi(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
,
i.e., achieving the point
\mathbf{x}_{\rm out}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi_{1/2\rho}(\mathbf{x}_{\rm out})\|\leq\epsilon,

where the Moreau envelope is defined as

\displaystyle\Phi_{1/2\rho}(\mathbf{x})=\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}}\left(\Phi(\mathbf{w})+\rho\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{x}\|^{2}\right).

(26)

For achieved
\epsilon
-stationary point
\mathbf{x}_{\rm out}
,
Lemma 9 of
Lin et al. 2025
indicates there exists some
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{x}}
, such that

\min_{\mathbf{g}\in\partial\Phi(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}\|\mathbf{g}\|\leq\epsilon

and

\|\mathbf{x}_{\rm out}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq{\epsilon}/{(2\rho)}
.
Furthermore, it holds

\displaystyle\left\{\partial\Phi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}):\|\mathbf{x}_{\rm out}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2\rho}\right\}\subseteq\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigcup_{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x}_{\rm out},\epsilon/2\rho)}\partial\Phi(\mathbf{x})\right)=\partial_{\epsilon/2\rho}\Phi(\mathbf{x}).

Therefore, the point
\mathbf{x}_{\rm out}
is a
(\epsilon/2\rho,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point of
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
.

We summarize the main difference between results of
Lin et al. 2025
and ours as follows.

1.

Lin et al. 2025
requires the Moreau envelope (
26
) be well-defined, which depends on the weakly convex assumption, i.e., the function
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}
is convex in
\mathbf{x}
for some
\rho>0
.
In contrast, our results do not depend on the weak convexity, which includes more practical applications.

2.

The results of
Lin et al. 2025
with respect to the Moreau envelope only focus on the case where
\mathbf{x}
is unconstrained, while our results with respect to approximate Goldstein saddle stationary points and generalized Goldstein stationary points address the more general constrained case.

3.

In the view of
(\delta,\epsilon)
-Goldstein stationary point of
\Phi(\mathbf{x})
, the result of
Lin et al. 2025
restricts the value of
\delta
to depend on the weakly convex parameter
\rho
.
In contrast, our results (Theorem
4
and Corollary
5
for the unconstrained case) holds for any
\delta>0
.

4.

Lin et al. 2025
focus on the first-order methods, which requires the objective function is
L_{f}
-Lipschitz continuous and the stochastic first-order oracle has bounded variance
\sigma^{2}
.
In contrast, our work focus on the zeroth-order methods, which requires the stronger mean-squared Lipschitz continuity (Assumption
1
) but does not need the bounded variance assumption.

9
Conclusion and Future Directions

In this work, we introduce approximate Goldstein saddle stationary points to characterize the stationarity of general constrained nonsmooth nonconvex-concave minimax problem without weak convexity.
We then establish gradient-free descent ascent methods with non-asymptotic convergence rates with respect to this measure.
Furthermore, we propose nested-loop method to achieve approximate generalized Goldstein stationary points of the primal function.
The convergence of proposed methods also have been verified by numerical experiments.

In future work, we would like to study first-order methods for nonsmooth nonconvex-concave minimax problem.
It is also interesting to consider the convergence of specific nonsmooth nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problem with respect to proposed approximate Goldstein saddle stationary points
(
Li et al. 2025
;
Lu and Wang 2025
;
Khan and Xu 2026
)
.

Proof.

For the ease of presentation, we denote

then we can write

(106)

Combining equations (
