Abstract
. We study distributions of polynomials in conditionally free ( c c -free) random variables, a notion of independence for two-state noncommutative probability spaces ( 𝒜 , φ , ψ ) (\mathcal{A},\varphi,\psi) introduced by Bożejko, Leinert and Speicher. To this end we establish recursive relations between the joint Boolean cumulants of c c -free random variables, analogous to previously found recursions for Boolean cumulants of free random variables. The algebraic reformulation of these recursions on the free associative algebra provides an effective formal machinery for the computation of the moment generating functions and thus the distributions of arbitrary self-adjoint polynomials in c c -free random variables. As an application of a recent observation, our approach can be used to determine conditional expectations of the form E ⁡ [ a | P ⁡ ( a , b ) ] E\left[a|P(a,b)\right] , where P P is a self-adjoint polynomial in free (in the sense of Voiculescu) random variables a , b a,b . We illustrate this with an example where P ⁡ ( a , b ) = i ⁡ [ a , b ] P(a,b)=i[a,b] . Finally we define orthogonal projections that formally play the role of conditional expectations in the framework of c c -freeness and share some properties with the conditional expectations of free variables. In particular they can be used to re-derive by purely algebraic methods the formula of Popa and Wang for the Σ \Sigma -transform for the c c -free multiplicative convolution.

1.
Introduction

The notion of
c
-freeness (or conditional freeness) was introduced by
Bożejko, Leinert, and Speicher in
[
10
,
9
]
. It is a
noncommutative independence in the framework of a two-state noncommutative
probability space
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
. This concept generalizes
freeness in the following sense: random variables
a,b\in\mathcal{M}
are

c
-free with respect to the pair
(\varphi,\psi)
, if they are free in the sense of
Voiculescu with respect to the second state
\psi
and satisfy
a factorization condition with respect to the first state
\varphi
.
For precise definitions of both types of independence see Section
2
below.

The distribution of a self-adjoint element
a
in a two-state probability
space is described by a pair of measures
\mu_{a}^{\varphi}
and

\mu_{a}^{\psi}
, corresponding to the distributions with respect to

\varphi
and
\psi
, respectively. In this paper, we present a method for
determining the distribution of polynomials in
c
-free variables. More
precisely, for
c
-free variables
a,b
and a noncommutative polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle
, we provide a method for determining the (ordinary) moment
generating function of
P(a,b)
with respect to
\varphi
(see Theorem

4.16
). For simplicity, our presentation focuses on the
case of two variables, but this generalizes easily to the case of
n

variables. Our main tools are the same as in

[
18
]

where we studied polynomials in free variables:

•

derivations on the free algebra which translate combinatorial
recursions for Boolean cumulants into effective algebraic identities
between their generating functions;

•

linearization, which allows us to express the resolvent

(1-zP(X,Y))^{-1}
in the form
u^{t}(I-zAX-zBY)^{-1}v
for vectors

u,v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
n\times n
matrices
A
and
B
.

On the combinatorial side of this development we prove a new characterization
of
c
-freeness in terms of Boolean cumulants (see Theorem

3.5
). It turns out to be very similar to the characterization
of freeness from
[
12
]
, and involves the so-called Vertical-No-Repeat
property which identifies maximal elements among coloured noncrossing
partitions with respect to a partial order denoted by
\ll
, which arises
naturally in the context of free probability (see

[
6
]
).

Our motivation to study this problem comes from the recent developments in

[
13
]
where a surprising connection between

c
-freeness and so called free denoising was established, which we briefly
describe below.
Let
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
be a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann algebra with a faithful, normal trace

\psi
.
Assume further that two random variables
a,b
are free with respect to

\psi
. We interpret the random variable
a
as a “signal” of interest and

b
as a “noise”, and we view
P(a,b)
as a noisy version of
a
, where

P\in{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle
. We are interested in the conditional expectation

E[a|P(a,b)]
, which is the best approximation of

a
by a function of
P(a,b)
with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm with respect to
\psi
.
It was shown in
[
13
]
that, under the assumptions

a\geq 0
and
\psi(a)=1
, if we define a new state
\varphi(c)=\psi(ac)
then

a,b
are
c
-free with respect to the pair
(\varphi,\psi)
, and moreover we
have
E[a|P(a,b)]=h(P(a,b))
where

h=d\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}/d\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\psi}
is the Radon-Nikodym
derivative. Methods of determining distributions of polynomials in free
variables are known (see

[
5
,
18
]
),
and thus we fill this gap and provide a method to compute the Radon-Nikodym
derivative above, and consequently the conditional expectation.

In order to illustrate our results we present one example, which is fully
discussed in Section
5
. Suppose
a
is a standard
semicircular element and
b
has distribution

\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{-1}+\delta_{1}\right)
.
Let
c=i[a,b]
be their commutator, then
for
s<1/2
we have

E\bigl[(1-sa)^{-1}|c\bigr]=h(c)

with

h(t)=\frac{2(2+\tilde{\eta}_{a}^{2}(s))}{(1-s\tilde{\eta}_{a}^{2}(s))\left((2+\tilde{\eta}_{a}(s))^{2}-\tilde{\eta}_{a}^{2}(s)t^{2}\right)},

where
\tilde{\eta}_{a}(s)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4s^{2}}}{2s}
is the shifted Boolean transform of the standard semicircle distribution.

Expanding the above power series at
s=0
we obtain

\displaystyle E[a^{2}|c]

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{2}+2}{4},

\displaystyle E[a^{4}|c]

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{4}+6c^{2}+12}{16}.

The same formulas hold more generally for the anticommutator and in fact
any element of the form
c=\theta ab+\bar{\theta}ba
, with
\theta\in{\mathbb{C}}

arbitrary such that
\lvert\theta\rvert=1
.

In the framework of general
c
-free random variables in a two-state

W^{*}
-noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
it is natural
to ask whether conditional expectations onto
c
-free subalgebras
with respect to
\varphi
exist. This
question is non-trivial because in general
c
-freeness is incompatible with
a tracial state.
Indeed the examples in Section
6
below show that in the
c
-free case
conditional expectations in the strict sense do not exist,
however the projection in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
with respect to the inner product

\langle a,b\rangle=\varphi(ab^{*})

exists and serves as a formal analog of the conditional expectation.
We call this projection a
right quasi-conditional expectation
, as it has only the right module property and is not positive. We denote it by
\rE_{b}^{\varphi}
, when we compute the projection on subalgebra generated by
b
. We show that this projection shares some properties with the conditional expectations with respect to
\varphi
. In particular for any polynomial
P
in
c
-free variables
\rE_{b}^{\varphi}[P(a,b)]
is a polynomial in
b
of degree at most
\mathrm{deg}(P)
. Moreover we provide recursive formulas for
\rE_{b}[P(a,b)]
similar to those obtained for conditional expectations for polynomials in free variables from
[
18
]
. Using this machinery, we show that the right quasi-conditional expectation of the resolvent of a sum of
c
-free variables is again a resolvent, multiplied by an analytic function. This resembles the phenomenon of subordination for free additive convolution from
[
7
]
. In Section
7
we show that using the right quasi-conditional expectation one can derive a formula for the
\Sigma
-transform for
c
-free multiplicative convolution from
[
22
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall basic definitions and facts concerning freeness and
c
-freeness. In Section
3
we prove a characterization of
c
-freeness in terms of Boolean cumulants. In Section
4
we provide a method of computing distributions of polynomials in
c
-free variables. In Section
5
we present an application of our result to a free denoising problem. In Section
6
we study right quasi-conditional expectations and present a recursion for them based on Boolean cumulants. In Section
7
we derive the
\Sigma
-transform for
c
-free multiplicative convolution using right quasi-conditional expectations.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Noncommutative probability spaces

A
noncommutative probability space
is a pair

(\mathcal{M},\psi)
, where
\mathcal{M}
is a unital
*
-algebra and

\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\mapsto{\mathbb{C}}
is a positive unital linear functional, commonly
called a
state
. When
\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann algebra and
\psi
is a
faithful normal state, the pair
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
is called a
W^{*}
-probability space
.

A
two-state noncommutative probability space
is a triple

(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
where
\mathcal{M}
is equipped with
two states
\varphi
and
\psi
.
We will assume throughout that the second state
\psi
is tracial.

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

A family of subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
of a noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
is called
free
or
free independent
if

\psi(u_{1}u_{2}\dotsm u_{n})=0

for any choice of
u_{j}\in\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
such that
\psi(u_{j})=0
and

u_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{j}}
with
i_{j}\neq i_{j+1}
for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
.

(ii)

A family of subalgebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
of a two-state noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
is called

conditionally free
or
c
-free
if

\psi(u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{n})=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\varphi(u_{1}u_{2}\dotsm u_{n})=\varphi(u_{1})\varphi(u_{2})\dotsm\varphi(u_{n})

whenever
u_{j}\in\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
with
\psi(u_{j})=0
and

u_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{i_{j}}
with
i_{j}\neq i_{j+1}
for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
,
i.e., the family is free with respect to
\psi
and in addition satisfies the
factorization property with respect to
\varphi
.

2.2.
Cumulants

To quote Fisher
[
14
]
,
when it comes to the description of joint distributions of random variables,

the formulae are
much simplified by the use of cumulative
moment functions, or semi-invariants, in
place of the crude moments
.
This is even more true for non-commuting ones. In the present paper we will deal with three kinds of cumulants.

Notation 2.2
.

(i)

We set
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
A
set partition of order
n

is a collection
\pi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\}

of non-empty pairwise disjoint subsets

V_{i}\subseteq[n]
, called
blocks
,
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}=[n]
.
Every set partition
\pi
of order
n
induces an equivalence relation
on
[n]
, denoted by
\sim_{\pi}
,
such that
k\sim_{\pi}l
if and only if
k
and
l
are elements of the
same block of
\pi
.

(ii)

Conversely, any function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[n]\to C
into some set of “colours”

C
induces an equivalence relation
i\sim j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\iff{}f(i)=f(j)
.
The corresponding set partition
\pi=\ker f
is called
the
kernel
of
f
.

(iii)

We say that a set partition
\pi
of
[n]
is

noncrossing
if there is no pair of blocks
V,W\in\pi
with
elements
a,c\in V
and
b,d\in W
such that
a<b<c<d
. The collection of
noncrossing partitions of
[n]
is denoted
\NC(n)
.

(iv)

An
interval partition
of
[n]
is a noncrossing partition

\pi\in\NC(n)
such that every block is an interval of
[n]
. The
collection of interval partitions of
[n]
is denoted by
\IntPart(n)
.

(v)

Let
\pi\in\NC(n)
and
V,W\in\pi
be two different blocks.
We say that a block

W
is
nested
inside a block
V

if
\min(V)\leq w\leq\max(V)
for any
w\in W
. A block
W\in\pi
is called an
outer block
if there is no
V\in\pi
such that
W
is nested inside
V
. A block
W
that is not outer is called an

inner block
. We denote the sets of inner and outer blocks of

\pi\in\NC(n)
by
\IB(\pi)
and
\OB(\pi)
, respectively. An interval
partition contains no inner blocks.

(vi)

For a family of multilinear functionals

(L_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}^{n}\to\mathbb{C})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and a partition
\pi
we denote by

L_{\pi}
the partitioned multilinear functional

L_{\pi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})=\prod_{V\in\pi}L_{V}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}),

where for a block
V=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{s}\}

we denote
L_{V}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=L_{s}(a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{s}}).

This notation is extended to linear functionals by setting

\varphi_{n}(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varphi(b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{n}).

(vii)

Both
\NC(n)
and
\IntPart(n)
are lattices with respect to
the
reversed refinement order
:
two partitions
\pi
and
\rho
of
[n]
satisfy the relation
\pi\leq\rho

for every block
V\in\pi
there is a block
W\in\rho
such that
V\subseteq W
.

Definition 2.3
.

(i)

Let
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
be a noncommutative probability space. The following identities uniquely determine families of multilinear
functionals
(\beta^{\psi}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,

(r^{\psi}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
called
Boolean
and
free
cumulants:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\psi(a_{1}a_{2}\dotsm a_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\IntPart(n)}\beta^{\psi}_{\sigma}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\psi(a_{1}a_{2}\dotsm a_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\NC(n)}r^{\psi}_{\sigma}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}).

(ii)

Let
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
be a two-state noncommutative probability space. The following identity uniquely determines the family of
c
-free cumulants
:
(r_{n}^{\varphi,\psi})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\NC(n)}\prod_{V\in\OB(\sigma)}r_{V}^{\varphi,\psi}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\prod_{W\in\IB(\sigma)}r_{W}^{\psi}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}).

Remark 2.4
.

Note that when dealing with a two-state noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
we will use Boolean cumulants
\beta^{\varphi}
and
\beta^{\psi}
with respect to both states
\varphi
and
\psi
.

Proposition 2.5
.

(i)

Subalgebras are free if and only if mixed free cumulants vanish
[
24
]
.

(ii)

Subalgebras are
c
-free if and only if mixed free cumulants and mixed

c
-free cumulants vanish
[
9
]
.

A similar result holds for Boolean independence which however we do not
consider in this paper. Although one might conclude from this characterization
that every notion of independence is best studied in terms of its “native”
variant of cumulants, it turned out recently that Boolean cumulants are the
most universal ones and useful for different kinds of independences

[
15
]
,
in particular
free probability
[
12
,
17
,
18
]
,
but there are also applications in classical probability

[
23
,
27
]
.
In the present paper we show that they are useful for
the study of
c
-freeness as well.

The following recursive reformulations of the Boolean moment-cumulant relations
turn out to be most useful for our study:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}a_{2}\dotsm a_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{k}^{\varphi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})\,\varphi(a_{k+1}a_{k+2}\dotsm a_{n}),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\psi(a_{1}a_{2}\dotsm a_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{k}^{\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})\,\psi(a_{k+1}a_{k+2}\dotsm a_{n}).

Remark 2.6
.

The previous recursion has a natural translation into the language of
generating functions. More precisely, let
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
be a noncommutative
probability space and let
a\in\mathcal{M}
. Then, the (ordinary)
moment
generating function
(or
moment transform
) of a random variable
a
with respect to
\psi
is defined by

M_{a}^{\psi}(z)\coloneqq 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi(a^{n})z^{n}.

Similarly, we define the
Boolean transform
and the
shifted Boolean
transform of a random variable
a
as the generating function of its cumulants

\eta_{a}^{\psi}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{\psi}(a)z^{n},\qquad\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{a}(z)\coloneqq\frac{1}{z}\eta_{a}^{\psi}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{\psi}(a)z^{n-1},

respectively, where
\beta_{n}^{\psi}(a)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\beta_{n}^{\psi}(a,a,\ldots,a).

It is not difficult to verify that the recurrence
(
2.5
) is equivalent to the functional equation

M_{a}^{\psi}(z)=\frac{1}{1-\eta_{a}^{\psi}(z)}.

2.3.
Closure operators and Möbius inversion

The following simple lemma subsumes most combinatorial proofs
of the present paper.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
P
be a poset. A map
c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\to P
is called
closure operator

(or
sample operator

[
2
]
)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

it is
increasing
, i.e.,
x\leq c(x)
for every
x\in P
;

(ii)

it is
order preserving
, i.e., if
x\leq y
then
c(x)\leq c(y)
;

(iii)

it is
idempotent
, i.e.,
c\circ c=c
.

An element
x\in P
is called
closed
if
x=c(x)
.
The set of closed elements is denoted by
\overline{P}

and inherits the order.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(P,\leq)
be a poset and
c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\to P
a closure operator
and
(\overline{P},\leq)
the subposet of closed elements with the induced order.
Given a function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\to{\mathbb{C}}
, define its partial sums

F(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in P\\
y\leq x\end{subarray}}f(y).

Assume that there is a function
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{P}\to{\mathbb{C}}

such that for every
x\in\overline{P}
we also have

F(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\overline{P}\\
y\leq x\end{subarray}}g(y)
.
Then

g(y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in P\\
c(z)=y\end{subarray}}f(z)

for all
y\in\overline{P}
.

Proof.

For every
x\in\overline{P}
we can write

F(x)=\sum_{z\leq x}f(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\overline{P}\\
y\leq x\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in P\\
c(z)=y\end{subarray}}f(z)

and apply Möbius inversion on
\overline{P}
.

∎

2.4.
Cumulants in terms of cumulants

Definition 2.9
.

The
interval closure

\overline{\pi}
of a noncrossing partition
\pi

is the smallest interval partition
\rho
such that
\pi\leq\rho
.
Its blocks are the convex hulls of the outer blocks of
\pi
.
A noncrossing partition
\pi\in\NC(n)
is called
irreducible
if

\overline{\pi}=\hat{1}_{n}
, i.e., it has a unique outer block or equivalently,

1\sim_{\pi}n
.
The restrictions of
\pi
to the blocks of
\overline{\pi}

are called its
irreducible components
and
it follows that
\pi
is the concatenation of its irreducible components.
We denote the set of irreducible noncrossing partitions by

\NCirr(n)
.

Irreducible partitions are a natural means to express mutual relations between
different kinds of cumulants

[
16
,
1
]
,
in particular:

Proposition 2.10
.

(i)

Let
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
be a noncommutative probability space and

(r^{\psi}_{n})_{n\geq 1},(\beta_{n}^{\psi})_{n\geq 1}
be the sequences of free and

(2.6)

\beta_{n}^{\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in\NCirr(n)}r_{\pi}^{\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})

for any

and

.

(ii)

Let
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
be a noncommutative probability space,
then for any

(2.7)

\beta_{n}^{\varphi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})=\sum_{\pi\in\NCirr(n)}\prod_{V\in\OB(\pi)}r_{V}^{\varphi,\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})\prod_{W\in\IB(\pi)}r_{W}^{\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}).

Proof.

We note that (
2.7
) does not
appear explicitly in the literature,
but as (
2.6
)
it follows
from Lemma

applied to the poset
\NC(n)
with
the interval closure operator
from Definition

,
where for
\pi\in\NC(n)
, we set

\displaystyle F(\pi)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\pi}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),

\displaystyle f(\pi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{V\in\OB(\pi)}r_{V}^{\varphi,\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})\prod_{W\in\IB(\pi)}r_{W}^{\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}),

and for
\pi\in\IntPart(n)
we set

\displaystyle g(\pi)

\displaystyle=\beta_{\pi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}).

∎

Remark and Definition 2.11
.

(i)

Let
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
be a noncommutative probability space.
Assume that
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{s}
are free subalgebras of
\mathcal{M}
.
As an immediate consequence of the vanishing of mixed cumulants
(Proposition

) and the preceding

,
in short property (CAC):

\displaystyle\beta_{n}^{\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})

\displaystyle=0

whenever
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{A}_{i}
and

a_{1}
and
a_{n}
come from different subalgebras.

(ii)

Similarly, in a two-state noncommutative probability space

(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)

space property (CAC) is satisfied
by the Boolean cumulants with respect to both states:
Assume
that
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{s}

2.6.
Cumulants with products as entries

The classical formula of Leonov-Shiryaev for cumulants with products as entries
has its natural analogue for Boolean cumulants.
It is again a consequence of Lemma
2.8
,
this time the closure operator being
c(\pi)=\pi\vee\rho
.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
be random variables
then

(2.10)

\beta_{m+1}(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{d_{1}},a_{d_{1}+1}a_{d_{1}+2}\cdots a_{d_{2}},\ldots,a_{d_{m}+1}a_{d_{m}+2}\cdots a_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\IntPart(n)\\
\pi\vee\rho=1_{n}\end{subarray}}\beta_{\pi}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}),

where
\rho=\{\{1,2,\ldots,d_{1}\},\{d_{1}+1,d_{1}+2,\ldots,d_{2}\},\ldots,\{d_{m}+1,\ldots,n\}\}\in\IntPart(n)
, and
\vee
is the join in the lattice of interval partitions.
The condition
\pi\vee\rho=1_{n}
is equivalent to

\pi\geq\{\{1\},\{2\},\dots,\{d_{1}-1\},\{d_{1},d_{1}+1\},\{d_{1}+2\},\dots,\{d_{m}-1\},\{d_{m},d_{m}+1\},\dots,\{d_{n}\}\}.

The expansion (
2.10
) has the following recursive reformulation

[
18
]
.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
be random variables.
Given an interval partition

\rho=\{\{1,\ldots,d_{1}\},\{d_{1}+1,\ldots,d_{2}\},\ldots,\{d_{m-1}+1,\ldots,n\}\}\in\IntPart(n)
, the Boolean cumulant with the corresponding products as entries
is

(2.11)

\begin{multlined}\beta_{m}(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{d_{1}},a_{d_{1}+1}a_{d_{1}+2}\cdots a_{d_{2}},\ldots,a_{d_{m-1}+1}a_{d_{m-1}+2}\cdots a_{n})\\
=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{d_{k-1}<j<d_{k}}\beta_{j}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{j})\beta_{m-k+1}(a_{j+1}a_{j+2}\cdots a_{d_{k}},a_{d_{k}+1}a_{d_{k}+2}\dotsm a_{d_{k+1}},\ldots,a_{d_{m-1}+1}\cdots a_{n}).\end{multlined}

3.
VNRP for
c
-freeness

In this section, we derive a formula for the Boolean cumulants of
c
-free
elements, generalizing the results from
[
12
]
which express Boolean
cumulants of free elements using noncrossing partitions with so-called

vertical no-repeat property
.

In order to state the next combinatorial result, we fix
m\in\mathbb{N}

and a colouring
c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{1,\ldots,m\}\to\{1,\ldots,s\}
.
Then, we define

\NC(m;c)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\sigma\in\NC(m)\;|\;\sigma\leq\ker c\},

the set of noncrossing partitions compatible with
c
. We also set
\NCirr(m;c)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\NC(m;c)\cap\NCirr(m)
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

For every
\sigma\in\NC(m;c)
there exists a unique maximal
\rho\in\NC(m;c)
such that
\sigma\ll\rho
, which is characterized by the

Vertical-No-Repeat property (VNRP)
, that is,
every inner block of
\rho
is nested immediately inside a block of a different colour.

Our goal is to extend the characterization of freeness from
[
12
]
to the setting of a two-state
noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
and give a similar characterization of conditional freeness of Bożejko, Leinert and Speicher

[
9
]
.

The following “
c
-free Boolean cumulant functionals”

\beta^{\varphi,\psi}
,
depending on both states like the
c
-free cumulants (
2.3
),
appear naturally in the generalization of (
2.7
).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
be a two-state noncommutative probability space, and fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\pi\in\NC(n)
.
We define the
nested two-state Boolean cumulant functional

\beta^{\varphi,\psi}_{\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
by the formula

\displaystyle\beta^{\varphi,\psi}_{\pi}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{V\in\OB(\pi)}\beta_{V}^{\varphi}\bigl(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\bigr)\prod_{W\in\IB(\pi)}\beta_{W}^{\psi}\bigl(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\bigr),

for every
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Observe that for an interval partition
\pi\in\IntPart(n)
we have

\beta^{\varphi,\psi}_{\pi}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\beta^{\varphi}_{\pi}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
be a two-state noncommutative probability space,
fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\pi\in\NC(n)
. Then for every

a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
the nested two-state Boolean cumulants are given by

\beta_{\pi}^{\varphi,\psi}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\sum_{\rho\ll\pi}\prod_{V\in\OB(\rho)}r_{V}^{\varphi,\psi}\bigl(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\bigr)\prod_{W\in\IB(\rho)}r_{W}^{\psi}\bigl(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\bigr).

Proof.

We know that

\displaystyle\beta^{\varphi}_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\ll 1_{n}\\
1\in V_{0}\end{subarray}}r_{V_{0}}^{\varphi,\psi}\bigl(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\bigr)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\in\rho\\
V\neq V_{0}\end{subarray}}r_{V}^{\psi}\bigl(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\bigr),

\displaystyle\beta_{n}^{\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\NCirr(n)}r_{\pi}^{\psi}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})

Plugging both formulas into the formula from Definition
3.2
we immediately obtain the claim.
∎

The above proposition is crucial in the proof of the characterization of
c
-freeness in terms of Boolean cumulants below.

4.
Distribution of polynomials in
c
-free variables

In this section, we derive a method for determining the distribution of
polynomials in
c
-free variables. Our main tool is a kind of differential calculus on
the algebra of noncommutative polynomials, and more generally on formal power
series whose coefficients are noncommutative polynomials. This framework is
essential for analyzing distributions of
c
-free variables. In a general
noncommutative probability space, elements may satisfy nontrivial relations
(e.g.,
a^{2}=1
) that invalidate the Leibniz rule of the derivations defined
below. No such obstructions arise in the algebra of noncommutative
polynomials, making this setting more suitable for our purposes.

4.1.
Noncommutative polynomials and formal power series

Notation 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\}
be an alphabet, i.e. a set of letters.
We denote by

\mathcal{X}^{+}=\{X_{i_{1}}X_{i_{2}}\cdots X_{i_{k}}\mid k\in{\mathbb{N}},i_{j}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}\}

the free semigroup generated by
\mathcal{X}
and by

\mathcal{X}^{*}=\mathcal{X}^{+}\cup\{1\}
the free monoid.
Furthermore, we also denote by
{\mathbb{C}}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle={\mathbb{C}}\langle X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\rangle
the free associative algebra generated by the
variables
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
, i.e., the linear span of
\mathcal{X}^{*}
with the
concatenation product, also known as the algebra of noncommutative
polynomials.

For elements
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
and

P\in{\mathbb{C}}\langle X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
we denote by

P(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
the evaluation of a polynomial

P\in{\mathbb{C}}\langle X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\rangle
, i.e., the element of
\mathcal{M}

obtained after substituting every
X_{i}
with
a_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Definition 4.2
.

Fix a noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
.
The
noncommutative joint distribution
of a tuple of random variables

a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
is the linear functional

\psi_{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{C}}\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\rangle\to{\mathbb{C}}

given by the evaluation

\psi_{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}}(P(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}))\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\psi\left(P(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\right).

Remark 4.3
.

1.

The distribution of any polynomial
Q(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
with respect to

\psi
is the same as the distribution of
Q(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
with respect to

\psi_{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}}
,
and it suffices to study the distribution of polynomials in

({\mathbb{C}}\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\rangle,\psi_{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}})
.
This has the following advantages.

(i)

The augmentation homomorphism
\epsilon\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}

which maps a polynomial to its constant coefficient
\epsilon(P)=P(0)

allows the identification of the free product with the tensor algebra
and thus the unambiguous translation of multilinear maps into
linear maps on the free product.

(ii)

The derivations on the free algebra to be defined below
turn the combinatorial recursions for Boolean cumulants and conditional expectations
into concise algebraic identities.

The disadvantage is a possible loss of faithfulness and positivity which has
to be kept in mind.
Nevertheless we will abuse notation and write
\psi
instead of

\psi_{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}}
, which should not lead to any confusion.

2.

For the sake of simplicity we will stick to the two-variable case,
i.e., focus on the noncommutative probability space
\mathbb{C}\langle X,Y\rangle
with states
\psi=\psi_{a,b}
and
\varphi=\varphi_{a,b}

The generalization to the multivariate case is straightforward and left to
the reader.

3.

It is straightforward to extend the formulas from polynomials
to formal power series with polynomial coefficients and
we will tacitly do this without further comment.

4.2.
Boolean cumulant functionals and derivations

It will be important to factor monomials into alternating blocks of “pure”
monomials coming from different subalgebras.

Definition 4.4
.

The
support
of a polynomial is the set of letters which appear in its
terms.
The
block factorization
of a monomial

W\in{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle
is the (unique) decomposition

W=U_{1}U_{2}\cdots U_{n}
into nontrivial monomials
U_{i}
, each supported either on
X
alone or on
Y
alone, alternating between the two.
The block length of the monomial
W
is then equal to the number
n
of blocks. For example the block length of the word
W=X^{3}Y^{2}X
is equal to 3.

We can now define two kinds of linear functionals related to Boolean cumulants,
one which operates on blocks, and another which operates in letters.

Definition 4.5
.

The
block Boolean cumulant functional
is the linear functional

\beta^{b,\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle\to\mathbb{C}

determined by the following values on the monomial basis:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\beta^{b,\psi}(1)

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\beta^{b,\psi}(W)

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}^{\psi}(U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{n})

where
W=U_{1}U_{2}\cdots U_{n}
is the block factorisation of the monomial
W
.
Similarly we define the
partial block Boolean cumulant functional

\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle\to\mathbb{C}
as

\displaystyle\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}(1)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}(W)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\beta^{b,\psi}(W)&\text{if $W=XVX$ and
$V\in{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle$}\\
$0$&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

The linear functional
\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
is defined analogously.

For example we have
\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}(X^{3}Y^{2}X)=\beta_{3}^{\psi}(X^{3},Y^{2},X)
.

Definition 4.6
.

The
letter-wise Boolean cumulant functional
is the linear functional

\beta^{\delta,\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\to{\mathbb{C}}

determined by the following values on the monomial basis:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\beta^{\delta,\psi}(1)

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\beta^{\delta,\psi}(Z_{1}Z_{2}\cdots Z_{k})

\displaystyle=\beta^{\psi}_{k}(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{k}),

where
Z_{i}\in\{X,Y\}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
In a similar way to
\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}
, we define the

partial letter-wise Boolean cumulant functional

\beta^{\delta,\psi}_{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
on the monomial basis by

\displaystyle\beta^{\delta,\psi}_{X}(1)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\beta^{\delta,\psi}_{X}(Z_{1}Z_{2}\cdots Z_{k})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{l l }$\beta^{\psi}_{k}(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{k})$&if $Z_{1}=Z_{k}=X$,\\
$0$&otherwise\end{tabular}\right.

For example we have
\beta^{\delta,\psi}_{X}(X^{3}Y^{2}X)=\beta_{6}^{\psi}(X,X,X,Y,Y,X)
.

Remark 4.7
.

These cumulant functionals can be constructed for any state on
{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle
, in particular we will
consider these functionals
\beta^{b,\varphi}
and
\beta^{\delta,\varphi}

for both states of a two-state noncommutative probability space.
Note that if the joint distribution of
X
and
Y
satisfies Property (CAC)
with respect to a state
\varphi
then

(4.3)

\beta^{b,\varphi}=\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}+\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}-\epsilon\qquad\beta^{\delta,\varphi}=\beta^{\delta,\varphi}_{X}+\beta^{\delta,\varphi}_{Y}-\epsilon.

The following derivations will be useful for the algebraic
reformulation
of the recursive version (
2.11
) of the Leonov-Shiryaev formula
for Boolean cumulants with products as entries.

Definition 4.8
.

The

free difference quotient

is the unique derivation such that

\partial_{X}(X)=1\otimes X
and
\partial_{X}(Y)=0
.
Thus we have

\partial_{X}(X^{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X^{k}\otimes X^{n-k-1}.

This operator is coassociative and we define

\partial_{X}^{2}=(\id\otimes\partial_{X})\circ\partial_{X}=(\partial_{X}\otimes\id)\circ\partial_{X}

and similarly with higher powers
\partial_{X}^{k}
.
We will also need the
free divided power derivations

or
partial deconcatenation operators
defined by

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muX}(P)

\displaystyle=(1\otimes X)\partial_{X}(P),

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5mu{X}}(P)

\displaystyle=(X\otimes 1)\partial_{X}(P),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muX}(X^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X^{k}\otimes X^{n-k}

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muX}(X^{n})

Remark 4.9
.

Observe that the linear functionals

4.4.
Amplification to matrices

Recall that the
amplification
of a linear map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}

is the linear map

f^{(N)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\id_{M_{N}}\otimes f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M_{N}(\mathcal{A})\to M_{N}(\mathcal{B})
,
i.e., the entry-wise application
f^{(N)}([a_{ij}]_{ij})=[f(a_{ij})]_{ij}
.
This is then a matrix bimodule map in the sense that

f^{(N)}(UAV)=Uf^{(N)}(A)V

for any
A\in M_{N}({\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle)
and scalar matrices

U\in M_{k\times N}(\mathbb{C})
and
V\in M_{N\times k}({\mathbb{C}})
.
As discussed in
[
18
]
the functionals
and derivations introduced above have amplifications to noncommutative
polynomials with matrix coefficients.
In particular,

(M_{N}({\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle),\psi^{(N)})

is a matrix-valued noncommutative probability space
and we will
identify
M_{N}({\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle)\cong M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle

and write
AX
resp.
BY
for
A\otimes X
resp.
B\otimes Y
.
Observe that freeness of
X
and
Y
implies that for any
A,B\in M_{N\times N}(\mathbb{C})
the elements
AX
and
BY
are free
with amalgamation over
M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
in the matrix-valued probability
space
(M_{N}({\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle),\psi^{(N)})
.

When it is clear from the context that we are working in a matrix-valued
probability space, we will omit the superscript and write

\varphi,\psi,\beta^{b}_{X}
and
\beta^{\delta}_{X}
for the matrix-valued functionals and

I
for the identity matrix
I_{N}\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
.

Remark 4.11
.

In the matrix-valued probability space
(M_{N}({\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle),\psi^{(N)})
for an element
A\in M_{N}({\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle)
, we define its moment transform for
B\in M_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
by

M_{A}^{\psi}(B)=I_{N}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi^{(N)}\left((AB)^{n}\right),

and similarly

\eta_{A}^{\psi}(B)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{\psi,(N)}\left((AB)^{n}\right).

We still have

M_{A}^{\psi}(B)=\left(I_{N}-\eta_{A}^{\psi}(B)\right)^{-1}.

In what follows we will always assume that
B=zI_{N}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Remark and Notation 4.12
.

1.

The derivations
\partial_{X},\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muX},\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\leftharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muX}
introduced in the previous
section can also be extended to the matrix-valued framework (see

[
18
, Cor. 6.6]
). We apply the respective
derivations entry-wise to a matrix with polynomial coefficients.
The Leibniz rule for the derivations from (
4.8
)
takes the form

D(a\cdot b)=D(a)\cdot(1\otimes b)+(a\otimes 1)\cdot D(b),

and the amplified version is

D^{(N)}(A\cdot B)=D^{(N)}(A)\cdot(1\odot B)+(A\odot 1)\cdot D^{(N)}(B),

where for a matrix
A\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
with entries
A=[a_{ij}]_{ij}

we denote
1\odot A=[1\otimes a_{ij}]_{ij}

and

A\odot 1=[a_{ij}\otimes 1]_{ij}
.

2.

A simple argument using Leibniz’ rule shows that for invertible matrices

(4.7)

D^{(N)}(A^{-1})=-(A^{-1}\odot 1)D^{(N)}(A)(1\odot A^{-1}).

3.

More generally, we define
A\odot B=(A\odot 1)\cdot(1\odot B)
, i.e.,

(A\odot B)_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{ik}\otimes b_{kj}.

4.

The entry-wise application of linear functionals then turns
\odot
into a simple
multiplication of scalar matrices:

(f\otimes g)^{(N)}(A\odot B)=f^{(N)}(A)\cdot g^{(N)}(B)

and the formulas
(
4.4
)–(
4.6
)
from Proposition
4.10
remain valid after amplification to the
matrix valued case.

4.5.
Matrix-valued
c
-free additive convolution

Assume that we have a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}\langle X,Y\rangle
of degree

m
together with a linearization of its resolvent:

(1-z^{m}P)^{-1}=u^{t}(I_{N}-zL)^{-1}v,

where

and

. Here

\mathbb{C}\langle X,Y\rangle

\varphi\left((1-z^{m}P)^{-1}\right)=u^{t}\varphi^{(N)}\left((I_{N}-zL)^{-1}\right)v

and since
the moment generating function uniquely determines the distribution of

problem of computing the distribution of a polynomial

boils down to

)
reads

\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})=I+z\beta_{X}^{b,\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})\beta_{X}^{\delta,\psi}(AX\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}),

and hence

H_{X}=\beta_{X}^{b,\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})=\left(I-zA\beta_{X}^{\delta,\psi}(X\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})\right)^{-1}=(I-zAF_{X})^{-1}.

Finally, it is not difficult to show that

\partial_{X}^{n}(X\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})=z^{n-1}I\odot(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}A)^{\odot n-1}\odot\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}+z^{n}X(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}A)\odot(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}A)^{\odot n-2}\odot\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}

and after plugging this into the recurrence (
4.6
) we conclude

\displaystyle F_{X}^{\varphi}=\beta_{X}^{\delta,\varphi}(X\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}^{\varphi}(X)z^{k-1}\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}A)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\varphi}\bigl(z\beta_{Y}^{b,\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})A\bigr).

∎

The solutions of the previous fixed point equations can now be used to
compute distributions of polynomials in
c
-free variables via linearization
as shown in the next theorem.

Theorem 4.16
.

With the notation from Proposition
4.13
,
assume that
X
and
Y
are
c
-free
in the two-state noncommutative probability space
(\mathbb{C}\langle X,Y\rangle,\varphi,\psi)
.
Assume moreover that the resolvent of a given polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}\langle X,Y\rangle
of degree
m
has a linearization given by
\Psi=(1-z^{m}P)^{-1}=u^{t}\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}v
, where
\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}=\bigl(I-z(AX+BY)\bigr)^{-1}
, with
A,B\in M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
and
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Then

(4.20)

M^{\varphi}_{AX+BY}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varphi(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})=(I-zAF^{\varphi}_{X}-zBF_{Y}^{\varphi})^{-1}

and
consequently

M^{\varphi}_{P(X,Y)}(z^{m})=u^{t}M^{\varphi}_{AX+BY}(z)v.

Proof.

By the definition of the resolvent, observe that

(4.21)

\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}=I+zAX\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}+zBY\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\eta^{\varphi}_{AX+BY}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{\varphi}(AX+BY)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta^{\delta,\varphi}\left((AX+BY)^{n}\right)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\beta^{\delta,\varphi}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})-I

\displaystyle=\beta^{\delta,\varphi}(zAX\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}+zBY\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})

\displaystyle=zA\beta_{X}^{\delta,\varphi}(X\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})+zB\beta_{Y}^{\delta,\varphi}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})

\displaystyle=zAF^{\varphi}_{X}+zBF^{\varphi}_{Y},

where we simply use the definition of
\eta^{\varphi}
,
properties of
\beta^{\delta}
and
(
4.3
).
The result follows from the identity
M^{\varphi}(z)=(I-\eta^{\varphi}(z))^{-1}
.
∎

5.
Applications to free denoising

We will now apply the above result to the problem of free denoising. First, we briefly recall the framework of free denoising.

5.1.
Review of free denoising

Consider a tracial
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
. Fix two
self-adjoint elements
a,b\in\mathcal{M}
. We view
a
as a signal and
b
as
a noise. For a self-adjoint polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}\,\langle X,Y\rangle
we
consider the element
P(a,b)
, which represents a noisy element. Our goal is to
recover the signal
a
from the noisy element
P(a,b)
, and we observe that

E[a|P(a,b)]
solves this problem in the sense of
L^{2}
distance, i.e.,

\lVert a-E[a|P(a,b)]\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert a-g(P(a,b))\rVert_{2}
among all bounded
Borel functions
g
. We will work with a slightly more general problem and we
will consider
E[f(a)|P(a,b)]
. The crucial observation from
[
13
]
is that
c
-freeness provides a framework to find
E[f(a)|P(a,b)]
. Fix a non-negative function
f
such that
\psi(f(a))=1
and define a new state
\varphi(c)=\psi(f(a)c)
. The main observations from
[
13
]
are the following:

1.

The elements
a,b
are
c
-free with respect to the pair
(\varphi,\psi)
.

2.

Given the distributions of
P
with respect to both states

\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\psi}
and
\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}
, the measure

\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}
is absolutely continuous with respect to

\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\psi}
, and therefore there exists the Radon-Nikodym
derivative
\tfrac{d\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\varphi}}{d\mu_{P(a,b)}^{\psi}}
which we
denote by
h
.

3.

The Radon-Nikodym derivative above coincides with the conditional
expectation
in the sense that

E[f(a)|P(a,b)]=h(P(a,b)).

This reduces the problem of free denoising to the computation of the
distributions of
polynomials in
free variables and
c
-free variables. In
[
13
]

this general framework is provided, however it was not possible to apply it
beyond addition and multiplication of variables for lack of a general
method to determine distributions of polynomials in
c
-free
variables. Theorem
4.16
fills this gap.

5.2.
A worked out example

Given a noncommutative probability space
(\mathcal{M},\psi)
,
let
a
be a standard semicircular element and

b
free from
a
with distribution
\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{-1}+\delta_{1})
.
In this subsection we present a worked out example which shows how to find for fixed
-1/2<s<1/2
the conditional expectation

E\bigl[(1-sa)^{-1}\big|i[a,b]\bigr],

where
i[a,b]=i(ab-ba)
is the commutator of
a
and
b
. Define a state

\varphi(c)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\psi\left((1-sa)^{-1}c\right)}{M_{a}^{\psi}(s)}.

As we observed above, we need to determine the distribution of
i[a,b]
with
respect to both states
\psi
and
\varphi
.
As discussed in Subsection
4.1
,
we will work on the free algebra
\left({\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle,\varphi_{a,b},\psi_{a,b}\right)

where we have
\varphi_{a,b}(R(X,Y))=\varphi(R(a,b))
and

\psi_{a,b}(R(X,Y))=\psi(R(a,b))

and after fixing
a
and
b
we will simply write
\varphi,\psi

instead of
\varphi_{a,b},\psi_{a,b}
, which should not lead to any confusion.

For
z
in some neighbourhood of zero we have

\displaystyle M_{X}^{\varphi}(z)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left((1-zX)^{-1}\right)=\frac{\psi\left((1-sX)^{-1}(1-zX)^{-1}\right)}{M_{X}^{\psi}(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi\left(\frac{s}{1-sX}-\frac{z}{1-zX}\right)}{(s-z)M_{X}^{\psi}(s)}=\frac{sM_{X}^{\psi}(s)-zM_{X}^{\psi}(z)}{(s-z)M_{X}^{\psi}(s)},

or, equivalently,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(z)=\frac{M^{\psi}_{X}(s)-M_{X}^{\psi}(z)}{sM^{\psi}_{X}(s)-zM_{X}^{\psi}(z)}.

The linearization is

\Psi(z^{2})=(1-z^{2}i[X,Y])^{-1}=\left[(1-zC_{X}X-zC_{Y}Y)^{-1}\right]_{1,1}

with

\displaystyle C_{X}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&i\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}\qquad C_{Y}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-i&0\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{bmatrix}

and we can construct the system of equations for matrices
F_{X},F_{Y},F_{X}^{\varphi},F_{Y}^{\varphi}
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}H_{X}&=(I-zC_{X}F_{X})^{-1},\\
H_{Y}&=(I-zC_{Y}F_{Y})^{-1},\\
F_{X}&=\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{X}(zH_{Y}C_{X}),\\
F_{Y}&=\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{Y}(zH_{X}C_{Y}),\\
F_{X}^{\varphi}&=\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\varphi}(zH_{Y}C_{X}),\\
F_{Y}^{\varphi}&=\tilde{\eta}_{Y}^{\varphi}(zH_{X}C_{Y}).\\
\end{cases}

Note that by the freeness assumption, the distributions of
Y
with respect to

\varphi
and
\psi
coincide and hence

\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{Y}(zH_{X}C_{Y})=\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{Y}(zH_{X}C_{Y})=zH_{X}C_{Y}
,
where the latter equality follows from the fact that
b
has the Bernoulli
distribution.
As a consequence
F_{Y}^{\varphi}=F_{Y}
as well.

Since
a
was assumed to be the standard semicircular element, its moment generating function satisfies the equation

M_{X}^{\psi}(z)=z^{2}M_{X}^{\psi}(z)^{2}+1.

For the
\eta
-transform we get
\eta_{X}^{\psi}(z)=1-1/M_{X}^{\psi}(z)
. This implies that
\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\psi}=zM_{X}^{\psi}(z)
. This together with (
5.1
) implies that

\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(z)=\frac{s^{2}M_{X}^{\psi}(s)^{2}-z^{2}M_{X}^{\psi}(z)^{2}}{sM_{X}^{\psi}(s)-zM_{X}^{\psi}(z)}=sM_{X}^{\psi}(s)+zM_{X}^{\psi}(z)=\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\psi}(s)+\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\psi}(z)

and finally

F_{X}^{\varphi}=\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\psi}(s)I+\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\psi}(zH_{Y}C_{X})=\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\psi}(s)I+F_{X}.

In particular we have

\eta_{C_{X}X+C_{Y}Y}^{\varphi}(z)=zC_{X}F_{X}^{\varphi}+zC_{Y}F_{Y}^{\varphi}=zC_{X}\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{X}(s)+\eta_{C_{X}X+C_{Y}Y}^{\psi}(z)

and solving this system (and noticing that the involved moment and
\eta
-transforms have analytic continuation to the upper half-plane), for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
we get

G^{\varphi}_{i[a,b]}(z)=G^{\varphi}_{i[X,Y]}(z)=\frac{(2+\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s))\sqrt{z^{2}-8}+(\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s)-2)z}{2\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s)z^{2}-2(\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s)+2)^{2}}.

Stieltjes inversion formula and a direct calculation gives

d\mu^{\varphi}_{i[a,b]}(x)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{(2+\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s))\sqrt{8-x^{2}}}{2\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s)x^{2}-2(\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s)+2)^{2}}.

Since it is well known that the distribution of the commutator of a semicircular
variable and a Bernoulli variable is equal to the free convolution of two semicircle distributions of variance one, we obtain the density
\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\sqrt{8-x^{2}}
.
We finally obtain the Radon-Nikodym derivative and the conditional expectation

h(t)=M_{a}^{\psi}(s)\frac{d\mu^{\varphi}_{i[a,b]}}{d\mu^{\psi}_{i[a,b]}}=\frac{2(2+\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s))}{(1-s\tilde{\eta}_{X}(s))\left((2+\tilde{\eta}_{X}(s))^{2}-\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{2}(s)t^{2}\right)}.

Extracting the first few coefficients from the generating function we get

\displaystyle E[a^{2}|i[a,b]]

\displaystyle=\frac{(i[a,b])^{2}+2}{4},

\displaystyle E[a^{4}|i[a,b]]

\displaystyle=\frac{(i[a,b])^{4}+6(i[a,b])^{2}+12}{16}.

6.
Algebraic conditional expectations of
c
-free variables

6.1.
Conditional expectations

In this section, we study formal analogues of conditional expectations for

c
-free random variables.
It turns out that after some modifications the algebraic machinery from

[
18
]
still works in the
c
-free setting,
and we obtain projections onto subalgebras which formally
act like conditional expectations,
but lack important analytic properties like positivity.
Yet they are useful for practical computations and as an example
in Section
7
we recompute the

c
-free analog of the
\Sigma
-transform from
[
22
]

by purely algebraic means.

Recall that a
conditional expectation

is a state-preserving projection
E_{\mathcal{A}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{A}

from a noncommutative probability space
\mathcal{M}
onto a subalgebra
\mathcal{A}

such that in addition
for every
u\in\mathcal{M}
and
every
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}

(6.1)

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1}u)

\displaystyle=\varphi(a_{1}E_{\mathcal{A}}[u]),

\displaystyle\varphi(ua_{2})

\displaystyle=\varphi(E_{\mathcal{A}}[u]a_{2}).

In general such a map does not necessarily exist.
A sufficient condition is that
\mathcal{M}
is a finite
von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
is tracial,
i.e.
\varphi(ab)=\varphi(ba)
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{M}

[
26
, Proposition 5.2.36]
;
for a characterization in the general case see
[
25
]
.
If it does exist and the state
\varphi
is faithful, then the conditional expectation

E_{\mathcal{A}}[u]
is uniquely determined by the invariance property (
6.1
)
and it follows that

E_{\mathcal{A}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{A}
is a unital completely positive
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule map, i.e.,

(6.2)

E_{\mathcal{A}}[a_{1}ua_{2}]=a_{1}E_{\mathcal{A}}[u]a_{2}

for all
u\in\mathcal{M}
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
.

A note on notation:
The standard notation for the conditional expectation onto a subalgebra

\mathcal{A}
is
E_{\mathcal{A}}
and when the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
is generated
by a single element
a
we also write
E_{a}

Occasionally we write the latter also as
E[x|a]=E_{a}[x]
,
for example in Section
5
in order to avoid clumsy expressions like

E_{i[a,b]}
.

6.2.
Left and right quasi-conditional expectations

From a technical point of view,
the computation of the conditional expectation
of an element
u\in\mathcal{M}

onto a subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{M}

boils down to the construction
of an element
E_{\mathcal{A}}[u]\in\mathcal{A}
which satisfies
the conditions (
6.1
).
This amounts to the computation of the orthogonal projection

L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)\to L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
be a
W^{*}
-probability space with faithful state
\varphi
,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{M}

a subalgebra and
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})_{r}
and
L^{2}(\mathcal{A})_{r}
the right
L^{2}
-spaces,
i.e., the respective completions
of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{A}
with respect to the scalar product

\langle x,y\rangle=\varphi(xy^{*}).

Then the orthogonal projection
\rE_{\mathcal{A}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)_{r}\to L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)_{r}
exists
and satisfies the right module property
\rE_{\mathcal{A}}[ua]=\rE_{\mathcal{A}}[u]a

for
u\in\mathcal{M}
and
b\in\mathcal{A}
.
Similarly,
the left Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)_{l}
and
L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)_{l}

are obtained from the scalar product

\langle x,y\rangle=\varphi(y^{*}x)

and the projection
\lE_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)_{l}\to L^{2}(\mathcal{A},\varphi)_{l}
satisfies
the left module property
\lE_{\mathcal{A}}[au]=a\lE_{\mathcal{B}}[u]
.
Note that it is not necessarily true
that the restriction of a map
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L^{2}(\mathcal{M})_{r}\to L^{2}(\mathcal{A})_{r}

to the subspace
\mathcal{A}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{M})_{r}
automatically
gives rise to a map
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{A}
.
In the
c
-free setting below both
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{A}
will be algebras of
polynomials and we will show that the projections
indeed restrict to mappings from
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{A}
.
We will call these mappings
left
and
right quasi-conditional expectations
.

Another issue to note here is faithfulness: if the state
\varphi
is not faithful,
then the scalar product is not positive definite and the projection not unique,
i.e., it is only defined up to a one-sided ideal.
However we will simply ignore this issue
and define a formally consistent
map
satisfying all essential algebraic requirements needed for the actual computations.

6.3.
Failure of positivity and the modular property

The following examples show that in the case of
c
-freeness the quasi-conditional expectations with
respect to
\varphi
are not positive and fail the modular property
(
6.2
).
More precisely,
positive elements are not necessarily mapped to positive elements
the left modular property does
not hold for the right quasi-conditional expectation and vice versa.

Example 6.1

(Failure of the left modular property)
.

Assume
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
c
-free subalgebras in

(\mathcal{M},\varphi,\psi)
.
Then for
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
we have

\varphi(ba^{n})=\varphi(b)\varphi(a^{n})

for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

and consequently the right conditional expectation onto
\mathcal{A}
is

\rE^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{A}}[b]=\varphi(b)1_{\mathcal{M}}.

On the other hand, the conditional free pyramidal law asserts

\varphi(a_{1}ba_{2})=\varphi(a_{1}a_{2})\psi(b)+\varphi(a_{1})\varphi(a_{2})(\varphi(b)-\psi(b))

i.e.,

\displaystyle\varphi(aba^{n})

\displaystyle=\varphi(a^{n+1})\psi(b)+\varphi(a)\varphi(a^{n})(\varphi(b)-\psi(b))

\displaystyle=\varphi((a\psi(b)+\varphi(a)(\varphi(b)-\psi(b)))a^{n})

and consequently

(6.3)

\displaystyle\rE^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{A}}[ab]

\displaystyle=a\psi(b)+\varphi(a)(\varphi(b)-\psi(b))1_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\neq a\rE^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{A}}[b]=a\varphi(b)

unless
\varphi(b)=\psi(b)
.

Example 6.2

(Failure of positivity)
.

The example at the origin of conditional freeness has its roots
in harmonic analysis of the free group

[
9
]
.
Let
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}*{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}=\langle a,b\mid a^{2}=b^{2}=1\rangle

be the free Coxeter group with two generators.
Then
a
and
b
are free with respect to the von Neumann trace

\psi(w)=\delta_{e,w}
and
c
-free with respect
to the regular state
\varphi(w)=e^{-\lambda\lvert w\rvert}
with
0<\lambda<\infty
,
where by
\lvert w\rvert
we denote length of a (reduced) word
w\in\Gamma
.
These functionals define states on the group von Neumann algebra

L(\Gamma)
.
Let
x=1+a
and
y=b
. Then
x
is positive and so is
bxb
,
however plugging
bxb
into (
6.3
) we obtain

\rE^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{B}}[bxb]=b^{2}+(1+e^{-\lambda})e^{-\lambda}b

which is not positive for sufficiently small
\lambda
.

Note however, that in the context of denoising in Section
5

the left and right conditional expectations coincide with the free one.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
f
be a strictly positive function on the spectrum of
a

such that
\psi(f(a))=1
.
Then the state
\varphi(x)=\psi(f(a)x)
is faithful
and the right quasi-conditional expectation
\rE_{a}^{(\varphi)}
coincides with
the free conditional expectation
E^{\psi}_{a}
.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\varphi\bigl(E_{a}^{\psi}[x]g(a)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\psi\bigl(f(a)E_{a}^{\psi}[x]g(a)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\psi(f(a)xg(a))

\displaystyle=\varphi(xg(a))

and therefore
\rE^{\varphi}_{a}[a]=E_{a}^{\psi}[a]
.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

This does not contradict (
6.3
) as we have
\varphi(b)=\psi(b)
when

b
is free from
a
with respect to
\psi
.

6.4.
A recurrence for the right quasi-conditional expectation

Before embarking on the
c
-free problem,
we recall the algebraic framework for the free conditional expectation from

[
18
]
.

Recall that for the construction of the conditional expectation of
a noncommutative polynomial
u

in variables
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{M}

onto the algebra
\mathcal{A}
generated by a subset of the variables

a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}
one has to find suitable expressions for moments of the form

\varphi\left(a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\dotsm a_{i_{r}}b\right),

where
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
b\in\mathcal{A}
.
It is a fundamental property of freeness
(as one of the universal notions of independence in the sense of
[
19
]
)
that all joint moments of
freely independent random variables are uniquely determined by the marginal moments
of the variables in question. Thus for each moment of the form indicated above
there is a universal formula (not depending on the particular choice of the
distributions of
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
) which expresses any joint moment as a sum
of products of marginal moments.

We will work in the formal setting of noncommutative polynomials

{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle
with
c
-free state
\varphi
and free state
\psi

as described in Subsection
4.1
.
The states are possibly not faithful and the conditional expectation
therefore not unique, yet the following formula
consistently defines a conditional expectation
with respect to
\psi
and specifies to the correct one when
evaluated on free elements in von Neumann algebras.

Proposition 6.5

(
[

)
.

\psi
is a state on
{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle
such that
X
and

are free.
Given a word in block-factorization

(with
X_{0}
and
X_{n}
possible being empty),
we define the linear map
E^{\psi}_{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathbb{C}}\langle X,Y\rangle\to{\mathbb{C}}\langle X\rangle

by

(6.4)

\psi
,

\psi

7.
c
-free multiplicative convolution

Our goal in this section is to show, as an application of the developments presented in this paper, how to compute the distribution of the product of two
c
-free random variables. Moreover, we also reproduce the multiplicativity of the
Popa-Wang
\Sigma
-transform
from
[
22
]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
(\mathbb{C}\langle X,Y\rangle,\varphi,\psi)
be a two-state noncommutative probability space such that
X
and
Y
are
c
-free. Then the moment generating function of
XY
with respect to
\varphi
is given by

(7.1)

M_{XY}^{\varphi}(z)=\frac{1}{1-z\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z))},

where
\omega_{X}(z)
and
\omega_{X}(Y)
are the subordination functions for the free multiplicative convolution of
X
and
Y
with respect to
\psi
. Furthermore, if we set
\Sigma^{\varphi}=\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}\circ(\eta^{\psi})^{-1}
, then

(7.2)

\Sigma^{\varphi}_{XY}=\Sigma^{\varphi}_{X}\cdot\Sigma^{\varphi}_{Y}.

Proof.

In order compute the moment generating function
M_{XY}^{\varphi}(z)
, we consider

(7.3)

\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(1-zXY)^{-1}=1+zXY\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}

so that
M_{XY}^{\varphi}(z)=\varphi(\rE_{X}^{\varphi}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}])
. It is easy to compute the following block derivations:

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muX}\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}

\displaystyle=z\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}\otimes XY\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muY}\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}

\displaystyle=z\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X\otimes Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}\otimes(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}-1)

Using resolvent identity in (
7.3
), we can resort to the simpler recurrence
(
6.25
) in Theorem
6.9
and obtain

\displaystyle\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

\displaystyle=1+z\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[XY\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

\displaystyle=1+zXE^{\psi}_{X}[Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]+z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}\otimes(\rE^{\varphi}_{X}-E^{\psi}_{X})\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muY}[XY\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}].

A short calculation reveals

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\displaystyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$\cr$\textstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.6}[.9]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}{{\vbox{\halign{\hfil#\hfil\cr\scalebox{.5}[.8]{$\scriptstyle\rightharpoonup$}\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\delta$\cr}}}}_{\mkern-4.5muY}[XY\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]=\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X\otimes Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}

and thus

(7.4)

\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]=E^{\psi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]+z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)\bigl(\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]-E^{\psi}_{X}[Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]\bigr)

The recurrence
(
6.24
)
for
\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]
in Theorem
6.9
is identical to the one (
6.7
) for
E^{\psi}_{X}[Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]
. This means:

\displaystyle\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

\displaystyle=\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})+z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[XY\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

\displaystyle=\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

and

(7.29)

E^{\psi}_{X}[Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]=\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})E^{\psi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}].

Therefore, substituting (
7
) and (
7.29
) in (
7.4
), we obtain

\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]=E^{\psi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]+z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)\bigl(\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]-\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})E^{\psi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]\bigr),

which implies

(7.30)

\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]=\frac{1-z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})}{1-z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})}E^{\psi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

Now, we look at the factor
E_{X}^{\psi}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]
in the right-hand side of the above equation. The free recurrence (
6.7
) together
with (
7.29
) yields

\displaystyle E^{\psi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

\displaystyle=1+zXE^{\psi}_{X}[Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

\displaystyle=1+zX\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})E^{\psi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

and solving

\displaystyle E^{\psi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-z\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})X\bigr)^{-1}.

It follows that

\omega_{X}(z)=z\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})

is the

, i.e.,
\omega_{X}(z)
satisfies

Analogously, we have that

(7.33)

E^{\psi}_{Y}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}]=\bigl(1-z\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)Y\bigr)^{-1}

and

(7.34)

\omega_{Y}(z)=z\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X).

Our next step is to deduce a system of equations for the subordination functions
\omega_{X}(z)
and
\omega_{Y}(z)
. First, observe that all terms in the expansion

are alternating words, with each block of letters in the alternation having length one. In particular, this implies that
\beta_{Y}^{b,\psi}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})=\beta_{Y}^{\delta,\psi}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})
. Hence

\omega_{X}(z)=z\beta_{Y}^{b,\psi}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})=z\beta_{Y}^{\delta,\psi}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}).

Now, since
\partial_{Y}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})=z\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X\otimes\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}
and, we can show by induction that

\partial_{Y}^{n}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})=z^{n-1}1\otimes(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}+z^{n}Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X\otimes(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}.

Then, by (
4.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{X}(z)

\displaystyle=z\beta^{\delta,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})

\displaystyle=z\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{Y}(z\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X))

\displaystyle=z\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z)).

Analogoulsy, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{Y}(z)

\displaystyle=z\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z)).

A similar argument for
\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)
and
\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})
but now using (
4.6
) yields

\displaystyle\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)

\displaystyle=\beta^{\delta,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(z\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z)),

and

\displaystyle\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})

\displaystyle=\beta^{\delta,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{Y}(z\beta^{b,\psi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z)).

Finally, we can plug the above identities into (
7.30
) in order to obtain

\displaystyle M_{XY}^{\varphi}(z)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\rE^{\varphi}_{X}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1-z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})}{1-z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})}\varphi(E_{X}^{\psi}[\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1-z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})}{1-z\beta^{b,\varphi}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}X)\beta^{b,\varphi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}})}M_{X}^{\varphi}(z\beta^{b,\psi}_{Y}(Y\boldsymbol{\Psi{}}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1-z\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))\frac{\omega_{X}(z)}{z}}{1-z\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z))}M^{\varphi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z)).

Recalling that
M_{X}^{\varphi}(s)=(1-\eta^{\varphi}_{X}(s))^{-1}
and
\eta_{X}^{\varphi}(s)=s\tilde{\eta}_{X}^{\varphi}(s)
. By taking

, we conclude (
7.1
):

M_{XY}^{\varphi}(z)=\frac{1}{1-z\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z))}.

Now we prove (
7.2
). According to
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
, it is known that

\displaystyle\eta^{\psi}_{XY}(z)

\displaystyle=\eta^{\psi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))

\displaystyle=\eta^{\psi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z)).

If we set
u=\eta^{\psi}_{XY}(z)
, then

\displaystyle\omega_{X}(z)

\displaystyle=(\eta^{\psi}_{X})^{-1}(u)

\displaystyle\omega_{Y}(z)

\displaystyle=(\eta^{\psi}_{Y})^{-1}(u).

Also, we have

\displaystyle\eta^{\psi}_{XY}(z)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))\omega_{X}(z)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))z\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z)).

Hence

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{XY}(z)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z))

so that

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{XY}\circ(\eta^{\psi}_{XY})^{-1}(u)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{X}\circ(\eta^{\psi}_{X})^{-1}(u)\cdot\tilde{\eta}^{\psi}_{Y}\circ(\eta^{\psi}_{Y})^{-1}(u).

On the other hand, from (
7.1
) we infer that the
\tilde{\eta}
-transform of
XY
with respect to
\varphi
is given by

\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{XY}(z)=\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}(\omega_{X}(z))\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{Y}(\omega_{Y}(z)).

By setting again
u=\eta_{XY}^{\psi}(z)
, we conclude that

\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{XY}\circ(\eta^{\psi}_{XY})^{-1}(u)=\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{X}\circ(\eta^{\psi}_{X})^{-1}(u)\cdot\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}_{Y}\circ(\eta^{\psi}_{Y})^{-1}(u)

which reproduces
[
22
, Lemma 2.1]
upon setting
\Sigma^{\varphi}=\tilde{\eta}^{\varphi}\circ(\eta^{\psi})^{-1}
, and is precisely (
7.2
).
∎
