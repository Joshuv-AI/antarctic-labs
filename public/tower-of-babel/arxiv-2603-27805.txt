The counterjet dominates the production of PeV photons from Cyg X-3
Andrzej A. Zdziarski, Anton Dmytriiev, Karri I. I. Koljonen

Abstract
We study the physical mechanisms underlying the production of orbitally-modulated PeV photons from Cyg X-3, recently discovered by the LHAASO collaboration. Our key findings are as follows. Helium nuclei are accelerated in a compact and strongly magnetized region within the jet, but they then quickly advect downstream to regions with a weaker field, allowing them to diffuse out of the jet, where they produce pions in hadronic collisions with both the stellar photons and the stellar wind of the Wolf-Rayet donor. The optical depths across the binary are ≲ \lesssim 1 for both types of interactions, implying that their rates are proportional to the column densities along the particle paths. Given the low viewing angle of Cyg X-3 ( i ≈ 26 ​ ° i\approx\!26\arcdeg – 28 ​ ° 28\arcdeg ), most of the observed photons are produced by the relativistic hadrons accelerated in the counterjet (for which the column densities toward the observer are much longer than for the jet). This also explains the peak of the phase-folded PeV photon flux to be on the opposite side of the superior conjunction than that for the (also orbitally-modulated) GeV photons, which are produced by collisions of relativistic electrons with stellar photons in the optically thick regime. This then implies that the emission is produced in the approaching jet.

Importantly, the donor of Cyg X-3 is a WR star; thus, the main species in the accretion flow and jet is He. Thus, the He nuclei undergo acceleration rather than the protons. However, the binding energy of He is 28.3 MeV, which is substantially lower than the threshold for pion production. In addition, the cross-section for photodisintegration in the giant dipole resonance region is
\sim

25

Importantly, the full photodisintegration of each He nucleus results in two neutrons, while partial disintegration channels typically produce at least one neutron. In addition, neutrons are also produced in photo-pion interactions of protons through the
\pi^{+}

\gamma_{n}878

(

III
The acceleration and emission regions

The site of the hadron acceleration in Cyg X-3 is located most likely within the jet. The jet moves with a relativistic velocity, implying the hadrons are advected away from the compact object. The crucial issue is whether the hadrons are in the slow- or fast-cooling regime. In the latter case, the hadrons would lose their energy within the acceleration site, and thus the acceleration and emission regions would be nearly coincident. In the former case, the hadrons will be advected downstream in the jet and emit outside the acceleration site. Below, we find that the hadrons are in the slow-cooling regime. If the jet is conical, the magnetic field quickly declines downstream, with the toroidal and poloidal field strengths of
B\propto h^{-1}
and
\propto h^{-2}
, respectively.

The size of the
acceleration
region,
R_{\rm acc}
is constrained by the requirement that particles remain magnetically confined during acceleration, i.e. their Larmor radius at the highest energies
{\cal E}_{\rm max}
, should not exceed the size of the system
(
15
)

R_{\rm L}\equiv\frac{{\cal E}_{\rm max}}{ZeB}\approx 3.3\times 10^{10}\,{\rm cm}\,\frac{{\cal E}_{\rm max}}{20\,{\rm PeV}}\frac{2}{Z}\frac{10^{3}\,{\rm G}}{B},

(10)

where
Z
is the particle’s atomic number. More generally, efficient acceleration requires that the acceleration timescale is shorter than both the escape time from the acceleration region and the advection time along the jet. For diffusive shock acceleration (first-order Fermi), the acceleration time-scale can be written as
t_{\rm acc}\simeq\eta{\cal D}/(c^{2}\beta_{\rm sh}^{2})
(e.g.,
9
;
37
), where
\beta_{\rm sh}c
is the shock velocity,
{\cal D}
is the diffusion coefficient, and
\eta
is a factor of a few accounting for the shock compression and geometry. In the Bohm limit,
{\cal D}\simeq(1/3)R_{\rm L}c
, yielding
t_{\rm acc}\simeq\eta R_{\rm L}/(3\beta_{\rm sh}^{2}c)
. The escape time depends on the confinement regime. In the diffusive limit (
R_{\rm L}\ll R_{\rm acc}
),
t_{\rm esc}\simeq R_{\rm acc}^{2}/{\cal D}
, while in the ballistic limit (
R_{\rm L}\sim R_{\rm acc}
) applicable to particle escape close to
{\cal E}_{\rm max}
, one expects
t_{\rm esc}\sim R_{\rm acc}/c
. The advection time along the jet is
t_{\rm adv}\sim\Delta h/\beta_{\rm j}c
, where
\beta_{\rm j}
is the jet velocity and
\Delta h
is the length of the acceleration zone along the jet. Requiring
t_{\rm acc}<t_{\rm esc}
in the ballistic regime implies a lower limit on the magnetic field strength,

B\gtrsim\frac{\eta}{3}\frac{{\cal E}_{\rm max}}{\beta_{\rm sh}^{2}ZeR_{\rm acc}},

(11)

where we assumed
\Delta h/\beta_{\rm j}c>R_{\rm acc}/c
. Taking
R_{\rm acc}=10^{10}
cm,
Z=2
,
{\cal E}_{\rm max}=20
PeV,
\eta/3\sim 1

\beta_{\rm sh}\sim 0.6

\beta_{j}
;
8

G.

We compare it to the limiting field for pair production by

(
11
)

. An approximate threshold for this reaction is

, where

is the electron charge. The reported detection of

VI
Discussion and summary

We have pointed out that the hadron acceleration region in the jet of Cyg X-3, which needs to be confined by strong magnetic fields, is spatially separate from a much larger emission region with weaker magnetic fields. This conclusion follows from our measurements of the optical depths of the entire binary for production of pions by the accelerated hadrons on both stellar photons and the He nuclei of the stellar wind of the WR donor, which we find to be
<1
, i.e., in the slow cooling regime. This allows the accelerated hadrons to advect downstream in the jet to regions with weak magnetic fields, leave the jet, and then leave the binary.

We have approximated the motion of the accelerated hadrons as along straight lines. This is a conservative assumption; accounting for the curvature of the charged hadron trajectories along field lines would increase the pion production rate. Accounting for this effect would require knowledge of the magnetic field strength and structure. On the other hand, accelerated He nuclei undergo photodissociation before producing pions (due to the larger photodissociation cross section and lower photodissociation threshold). Photodissociation produces neutrons, which travel in straight lines.

Given the slow cooling regime, there is no upper limit on the magnetic field strength in the acceleration region due to e
±
pair production by PeV photons interacting with the magnetic field because very few such photons are produced within that region.

In the slow-cooling regime, the rates of pion production are proportional to the column densities along the particle path. The viewing angle of Cyg X-3 is very low,
\approx\!26\arcdeg
–
28\arcdeg
, as recently measured from the X-ray polarization
(
36
)
. Therefore, paths along the line of sight are much longer for the counterjet than for the jet. Consequently, most of the PeV
\gamma
-rays are produced by hadrons accelerated in the counterjet, as long as the jet bulk motion is at most mildly relativistic (as measured, e.g., by
8
).

In contrast, accelerated electrons lose energy rapidly when they scatter stellar photons, producing GeV photons; they are in the fast-cooling regime. Therefore, the scattering takes place mostly in the acceleration regime. Given the boosting due to the jet bulk motion, this causes the jet emission to dominate
(
8
)
. This can explain the peak phase of the GeV-photon orbital modulation being on the opposite side of the superior conjunction from that of the PeV photons. Thus, it is possible that the acceleration of both the hadrons and electrons takes place in the same regions of the jet and counterjet.

In our calculations of the spatially dependent rate of photopion production, we have taken into account details of the photon field around the WR donor star’s hydrostatic core. The wind is optically thick, which causes a diffusive enhancement of its temperature below the photospheric radius. This results in a relatively complex radial temperature dependence. We stress that the commonly used parameters
R_{*}
and
T_{*}
correspond to the effective temperature at the hydrostatic core, i.e., the temperature the star would have in the absence of the wind. The actual radiation temperatures outside and inside the optically thick wind are substantially lower and higher than
T_{*}
, respectively, which we have taken into account. However, we stress an uncertainty in the actual values of
T_{*}
and
R_{*}
, with different combinations of them producing the same observed spectrum
(
21
)
.

Furthermore, a major caveat affecting the accuracy of our formalism concerns the structure of the stellar photon field and the wind. We used a model of an isolated WR star. In reality, the stellar wind will be gravitationally focused toward the compact object, and it will also be ionized by the X-ray source. As shown in the WR model we used, the wind of Cyg X-3 strongly overflows the Roche lobe of its WR star, while the recent model of
38
postulates even a standard Roche lobe overflow through the L1 point. Those modifications will increase the wind density in the equatorial plane, which, in turn, will increase the rate of pion production by relativistic hadrons on He nuclei in the wind.

Also, we approximated the treatment of pion production in collisions of stellar photons by assuming that the latter follow radial trajectories. While the trajectories of photons above the photosphere can be approximated by that, the distribution of photons below
R_{2/3}
is closer to isotropic. Still, we do not expect this to introduce a major bias into the result, since the assumed photon trajectory can be considered an average one. We have also neglected the effect of e
±
pair absorption of the PeV photons on the stellar field (see
31
).

In our follow-up detailed treatment, we will address these issues. We will also account for the jet misalignment with respect to the orbital axis and perform detailed fits to the observed spectrum and the folded light curve.
