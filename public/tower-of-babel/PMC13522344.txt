State-variable analysis of pulsed EM-fields in temporally layered media
Authors: Martin Stumpf, Giulio Antonini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pulsed wave propagation in temporally discontinuous media is described with the aid of standard state-variable theory of piecewise time-invariant systems. A novel recursive analytical procedure for analyzing the pulsed wave propagation in temporally discontinuous media is introduced. An illustrative analytical description is provided for (1+1)-D space-time waves along an equivalent transmission line whose electromagnetic parameters change abruptly, thus representing a temporally layered slab.

Introduction

Temporally modulated EM structures offer additional degrees of freedom for manipulating the EM wave field via purposely engineered, time-varying (TV) EM systems and devices1. The change of EM constitutive parameters gives rise to EM wave effects that are not encountered in the time-invariant EM case. For instance, a transverse electromagnetic (TEM) wave propagated in a temporally discontinuous homogeneous medium undergoes the time refraction and reflection at a temporal boundary2,3.

TV structures can outperform their fixed counterparts4,5, yet analyzing them often remains a difficult task (e.g.,6,7). In this paper, we address this challenge by employing a standard state-variable approach8, which provides a practical framework for engineering design. The state-variable approach has been previously employed to put forward both analytical and numerical methods for EM wave propagation and scattering in linear time-invariant and spatially inhomogeneous media9–11. Building on the pioneering work of Fante12, subsequent early studies of EM propagation in a temporally piecewise-constant media focused dominantly on the effects of time modulation on time-harmonic EM waves (e.g.,13,14). TV media, however, also enable the tailoring of the pulse shape of general EM waveforms (e.g.,3). To facilitate such investigations, the state-variable approach was employed in15 to examine (1+1)-D (= 1D space + 1D time) pulse propagation along a temporally inhomogeneous TV TL. This methodology was later adopted in16 to investigate general (1+1)-D wave fields and their associated properties (see also17). Unlike the present study, Ref.15 is limited to a TL with a TV wave speed and thus does not incorporate scattering effects. In this paper, these scattering phenomena are fully taken into account.

Abrupt temporal interfaces assume that the transition time is negligibly small compared to the characteristic time scale of pertaining EM phenomena occurring within it. A similar assumption is routinely applied to their spatial counterparts that essentially represent smooth, but (relatively) rapid transitions between two different materials. While an abrupt temporal boundary is difficult to realize in practice, step-modulation models remain valuable for understanding EM effects in switched optical waveguides18,19, for example. If such a sudden change is not appropriate, the concept of gradual switching can be employed instead6. The TEM wave propagation in time periodic temporally layered media is described in20 with the aid of Hill’s differential equation. The TEM plane-wave interaction with a temporally layered structure is also analyzed in21, assuming a time-harmonic dependence. The solution methodology introduced in this paper provides an analytical means to solve the pulsed wavefield propagation in arbitrarily piecewise time-invariant (i.e., temporally discontinuous) structures exactly.

Equations (8)–(12) describe the time evolution of general EM fields in TV piecewise-constant media. After discretizing the differential and integral operators in (8)–(11), this solution provides a natural starting point for constructing a general numerical procedure for solving EM Cauchy problems in TV media. While standard direct-discretization methods may fail to provide results with controlled accuracy near temporal interfaces, where the EM fields lack continuous differentiability, the TD solution (8)–(11) is inherently free of such difficulties, as it is formulated in terms of extensive quantities that remain bounded across these time interfaces.

The recursive solution scheme (7) is very general, but it has been derived via the spatial Fourier transform under the assumption of spatial shift invariance. This assumption holds in particular for homogeneous media, for which the EM Green’s tensor can be derived analytically. The TD EM-field solution (8)–(12) describes pulsed EM-wave propagation in a temporally piecewise homogeneous, isotropic, and lossless medium. Extending the framework to include dissipation, relaxation, or/and an inhomogeneous background is feasible in principle, but currently not available. Spatial stratification can be incorporated using generalized-ray theory24. Regarding the time dependencies, the presented solutions are not restricted to the time-harmonic or/and time-periodic waveforms, as is often the case in the literature. Consequently, the methodology enables the exploration signal-processing operations such as pulse stretching and compression (e.g.,3). On the other hand, the TD solutions are limited to piecewise-constant temporal media. Beyond this type of modulation, the concept of stair-case switching6 remains applicable within the presented framework.

Conclusion

We have described a novel recursive analytical procedure for analyzing the pulsed EM-wave propagation in temporally discontinuous media. Each iteration of the solution scheme is associated with a temporal jump discontinuity that generates reflected and transmitted wave constituents, the pulse shape of which can be determined exactly using the initial field distribution. The corresponding time-evolution solution to the EM Cauchy problem is presented and discussed. The solution has been demonstrated and validated using a canonical TL configuration that represents a classic problem of TEM wave propagation in a temporally layered slab.
