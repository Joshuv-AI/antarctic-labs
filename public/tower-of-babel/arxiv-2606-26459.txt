The devil in the (de)tails: an improved recovery guarantee for sparse approximation
Ben Adcock, Simone Brugiapaglia, Avi Gupta

Abstract
Many functions exhibit approximate sparsity in their coefficients with respect to a given dictionary. In recent literature, sparse approximation in such a dictionary from i.i.d. pointwise samples, underpinned by compressed sensing, has become a powerful tool for high-dimensional function approximation. A key step in this framework is truncating the (typically countably-infinite) dictionary to a finite index set of size n n , so that compressed sensing tools can be used to approximate the function by a sparse combination of these truncated dictionary elements. This introduces a discrete L 2 L^{2} -truncation error over the sample points, which in standard approaches, is bounded by the continuous L ∞ L^{\infty} -norm. Such a deterministic, worst-case bound ignores the randomness of the sample points entirely. As a result, n n must be taken unnecessarily large to keep the truncation error under control, which directly inflates the size of the matrix involved in the sparse recovery algorithm and increases computational cost. In this paper, we show that by exploiting the i.i.d. structure of the sample points, the discrete L 2 L^{2} truncation error admits a bound that instead reflects the faster decay behaviour of the continuous L 2 L^{2} -norm truncation error and yields significantly smaller truncation sets and decreased computational cost. We demonstrate this through applications to weighted Wiener spaces and anisotropic Sobolev spaces, in each case obtaining significantly smaller truncation sets than recent works. In addition, we also present an improved bound of independent interest for sparse approximation in bounded Riesz systems, where the measurement condition exhibits a smaller (and scale-invariant) dependence on the Riesz constants than in previous works.

High-dimensional approximation, Sparse approximation, Nonlinear approximation, Compressed sensing, Random samples, Universal algorithms, Riesz bases

1.3
Main contribution

The main contribution of this paper is a new bound which improves Theorem

for some universal constant

Then the following holds with probability at least

m\geq c\cdot\left(\frac{K^{2}_{\phi}}{a_{\phi}}\right)\cdot s\cdot\log^{2}\left(2\frac{K^{2}_{\phi}}{a_{\phi}}s\right)\cdot\log(2n),

(1.23)

thus reducing the dependence on the Riesz constants
a_{\phi},b_{\phi}
. In particular, the upper Riesz constant
b_{\phi}
does not appear in our measurement condition, and the dependence on
a_{\phi}
is lessened. It is notable that our result is invariant to scaling, i.e., the operation
\phi_{i}\rightarrow\gamma\phi_{i}
leaves (
1.23
) unchanged. This is not the case for (
1.22
), which would increase by at least
\gamma^{2}
.

(b) Sparse approximations.
The solution of an
\ell^{1}
-minimization is generally not sparse. Hence, procedures based on
\ell^{1}
-minimization do not generically produce sparse approximations. A secondary contribution of this paper is to show that one can always postprocess the

-minimization program by hard thresholding to obtain a genuinely sparse approximation. In Lemma

, which is based on
[

, Cor. 3.2]
, we show that hard thresholding an approximate solution does not substantially worsen the error, and the resulting sparse approximation satisfies the same error bounds up to numerical constants.

(c) General weighted Wiener-type spaces.
Our results in §
1.4
consider weighted mixed Wiener spaces
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, which have been studied in many recent works. However, our results in §

apply to general weighted Wiener-type spaces defined by arbitrary orthonormal or Riesz bases (i.e., not just the Fourier basis) and with general weights. In Theorem
4.3
we present a general result for weights of the form
w_{i}=i^{r}/\log^{t}(i+1)
, from which Theorem
1.4
follows as a special case. Thus are results are substantially more general than recent works
[

,
29
,
42
,
39
,
36
,
37
,

,

,

]
, which are specific to the spaces

1.7
Further discussion and related work

Our work is inspired by Krieg & Ullrich’s contributions
[
32
,
33
]
. In a pair of breakthrough papers they showed that function values are essentially as powerful as arbitrary linear information for
L^{2}
recovery, by proving that the sampling numbers are upper bounded by tail averages of the approximation numbers of the embedding. This resolved the open question of whether function values achieve the same rate of convergence as optimal linear information for
L^{2}
recovery. Subsequent works
[
40
,
28
,
23
]
built upon and refined these results, proving similar bounds with improved logarithmic factors.
These works use (weighted) linear least-squares estimators as their recovery algorithm, and thus are applicable to function classes for which linear algorithms are optimal.
Our work uses nonlinear sparse approximation, and is applicable to spaces such as the mixed Wiener spaces and universal recovery in the anisotropic Sobolev spaces, where nonlinear algorithms are required in order to achieve optimal rates.

More concretely, and using our notation, the results of
[
32
,
33
]
establish error bounds involving the
j
-term approximation errors
\tau_{j}(c)
only: namely,

{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{v}\sqrt{\frac{b_{\phi}}{a_{\phi}}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{j}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}}

(1.24)

where
\hat{f}
is a linear least-squares estimator, subject a number of i.i.d. samples scaling log-linearly in
n
, i.e.,

m\geq c\cdot\left(\frac{K^{2}_{\phi}}{a_{\phi}}\right)\cdot n\cdot\log\left(2n/\varepsilon\right).

(note that
[
32
,
33
]
also show the existence of a set of sample points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
for
m\geq cn
samples suffice). Our result involves a measurement condition (
1.10
) that is linear in the sparsity
s
, and only logarithmic in
n
, with additional terms involving the best
s
- and
j
-term approximation errors
\sigma_{s}(c)_{1}
and
\sigma_{j}(c)_{2}
,
j>s
.

The results in
[
32
,
33
]
are obtained by (i) carefully decomposing the tail term
e
in (
1.6
) into dyadic sums, and (ii) making repeated use of Chernoff bounds to estimate the norms of the ensuing matrices. In combination with a careful balancing of parameters, this leads to the desired error bound (
1.24
). Our approach is similar, although substantially more involved as we deal nonlinear best
s
-term approximation errors
\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}
in addition to the linear
n
-term approximation errors
\tau_{n}(c)_{p}
. Instead of (i), we partition the tail term into a carefully-constructed countable collection of sparse index sets lying within certain dyadic ranges. And instead of (ii) we use sophisticated deviation bounds (whose proofs are based on advanced chaining techniques) to upper bound the action of the resulting matrices on arbitrary sparse vectors. See §
3.2
for an overview of our proof.

As noted, our work is in part motivated by computational considerations. Notably, iterative algorithms for sparse approximation incur a computational cost of the form
\mathcal{O}(Tmn)
, where
T
is the number of iterations. In OMP, for instance,
T=\mathcal{O}(s)
. In this work, we consider the SR-LASSO optimization program. This was introduced in statistics in
[
13
]
and first used in the context of function approximation in
[
1
]
. See also
[
2
,
36
,
37
]
. As shown in
[
2
]
, it can be solved efficiently using the primal-dual iteration
[
19
,
18
]
combined with a restart scheme
[
5
]
. In this case,
T=\mathcal{O}(\log(1/\varepsilon))
iterations suffice to achieve an error within
\varepsilon
of the exact minimizer.
Crucially, in all such approaches, the truncation set size
n
directly determines the size of the matrix
A
and hence the computational cost, which is one of the main motivations for keeping
n
small. We remark in passing that
sublinear time
algorithms
[
20
,
21
]
can avoid incurring a cost scaling with
n
. However, these use specialized sample points, and to date, do not attain optimal error bounds under a sampling budget scaling log-linearly with the sparsity
s
.

2.1
The sparse approximation procedure

We first specify how the sparse approximation
\hat{f}
is computed. As noted, there are many ways to do this. Following
[
13
]
, we will employ the so-called
Square-Root LASSO
decoder. Given a regularization parameter
\lambda>0
, a matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times N}
and vector
b\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, the
(unconstrained) Square Root (SR)-LASSO problem
is the optimization problem

\min_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\lambda{\|z\|}_{1}+{\|Az-b\|}_{2},

(2.1)

The advantage of this decoder over, say, the classical LASSO is that a theoretically-optimal choice of
\lambda
is independent of the noise term
e
in (
1.5
) which, in our setting, depends on the unknown expansion tail
f-f_{n}
.

The program (
2.1
) does not generally yield sparse solutions. To obtain a
2s
-sparse approximation, we hard threshold the coefficients. Let
H_{2s}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the hard-thresholding operator, i.e., for
z=(z_{i})^{n}_{i=1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
H_{2s}(z)
is the
2s
-sparse vector with
i
th entry
z_{i}
if
|z_{i}|
is one of the
2s
largest entries of
z
in absolute value and zero otherwise. A secondary contribution of this work shows that solutions of (
2.1
) that are postprocessed by
H_{2s}
still satisfy the same error bounds, up to constants.

Another potential complication is that (
2.1
) generally has infinitely-many solutions. This is easily dealt with by picking one. In practice, this could be the output of some optimization algorithm for solving (
2.1
). But, theoretically, arguably the simplest choice is to pick the solution with the minimal
\ell^{2}
-norm (which is unique, as the
\ell^{2}
-norm is strictly convex and the set of minimizers of (
2.1
) is a nonempty, closed and convex set).

The sparse approximation procedure in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
We now specify this procedure. Given samples (
1.3
) of a function
f
and
A,b
as in (
1.5
), we define
\hat{f}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\hat{c}_{i}\phi_{i}
, where
\hat{c}=H_{2s}(\check{c})
and

\check{c}={\underset{}{\operatorname{argmin}}}\left\{{\|\tilde{c}\|}_{2}:\text{$\tilde{c}$ is a minimizer of \eqref{eq:srlasso} with }\lambda=\frac{3}{14}\sqrt{\frac{a_{\phi}}{s}}\right\}.

Note that the choice of
\lambda
is based on the theory we develop later in this section.

The choice of
2s
leads to a slightly more appealing error bound. As can be seen in the proofs of the main results in §
3
, if one were to use
s
-sparse approximation
\hat{c}=H_{s}(\check{c})
then the error bounds in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
would involve an additional term of the form
s^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\sigma_{s}(c)_{2}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

and

Let

\displaystyle\leq{\|(c_{T}-\check{c}_{S})_{S}\|}_{p}+{\|c_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}+\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}

\displaystyle={\|(c_{T}-\check{c})_{S}\|}_{p}+{\|c_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}+\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}

\displaystyle\leq{\|c_{T}-\check{c}\|}_{p}+{\|c_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}+\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}

\displaystyle\leq{\|c-\check{c}\|}_{p}+{\|c_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}+2\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}.

Now consider the second term. We have

{\|c_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}\leq{\|(c-\check{c})_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}+{\|\check{c}_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}\leq{\|c-\check{c}\|}_{p}+{\|\check{c}_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}.

Hence
{\|c_{T\backslash S}\|}_{p}\leq 2{\|c-\check{c}\|}_{p}+\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}

We now combine this with the above inequality.

\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Riesz basis. Therefore, the condition on
m
can be replaced by

m\geq c\frac{(1+1/\rho)^{2}}{(1-1/\tau^{2})^{2}}\frac{K^{2}_{\phi}}{a_{\phi}}s\log(2N)\log^{2}\left(\frac{2(1+1/\rho)^{2}}{(1-1/\tau^{2})}\frac{K^{2}_{\phi}}{a_{\phi}}s\right)\log^{2}\left(\frac{2}{(1-1/\tau^{2})}\right).

This condition is implied by (
2.3
). Hence we deduce that
A
has the rNSP with constant
\rho
and
\tau/\sqrt{a_{\phi}}
, with probability at least
1-p
, where
p
is as above. However, (
2.3
) also implies that
p\leq\varepsilon
. The result now follows.
∎

2.4
A deviation bound for bounded Riesz systems

Another component of our analysis is the following estimate, that bounds how much
{\|Ax\|}^{2}_{2}
can exceed its mean for sparse vectors. Notice that this result does not place a condition on
m
such as (
2.3
). This is crucial in our later estimates.

Theorem 2.8
.

There exist universal constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
with
c_{2}\leq 1
such that the following holds. Let
\{\phi_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
,
a_{\phi}
,
K_{\phi}
and
A
be as in Theorem
2.5
.
Let
1\leq s\leq n
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
g>0
,
B
be the unique positive definite square-root of the matrix
\mathbb{E}(A^{*}A)
and
T=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:{\|x\|}_{0}\leq s,\ {\|Bx\|}_{2}=1\}
. Suppose that

\frac{sK^{2}_{\phi}\log^{2}(2c_{1}K^{2}_{\phi}s/a_{\phi})\log(2n)}{a_{\phi}m}+1\leq c_{2}g.

(2.8)

Then

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in T}{\|Ax\|}_{2}\geq\sqrt{g+1}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-c_{3}g\frac{a_{\phi}m}{K^{2}_{\phi}s}\right)

To prove the above theorem, we require the following result (see
[
16
, Thm. 4.2]
).

Theorem 2.9
.

There exist absolute constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
such that the following holds. Let
X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
be as in Theorem
2.6
,
T\subseteq\sqrt{s}B^{N}_{1}
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in T}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum^{m}_{i=1}|\langle f,X_{i}\rangle|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|\langle f,X\rangle|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{sK^{2}\log^{2}(sK^{2}/\delta)\log(\mathrm{e}N)\log^{2}(1/\delta)}{m}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in T}\frac{1}{m}\sum^{m}_{i=1}|\langle f,X_{i}\rangle|^{2}}

\displaystyle~~~+c_{2}\delta\ \mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in T}\frac{1}{m}\sum^{m}_{i=1}|\langle f,X_{i}\rangle|^{2}+c_{3}\delta.

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Define the random variable
Z=\sup_{x\in T}\left|{\|Ax\|}^{2}_{2}-{\|Bx\|}^{2}_{2}\right|
.
We first bound
\mathbb{E}(Z)
.
As in the previous proof, let
X=(\phi_{i}(x))^{m}_{i=1}
for
x\sim\rho
and
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
be independent copies of
X
. Recall that (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) hold and observe that
T\subseteq\sqrt{s/a_{\phi}}B^{n}_{1}
.
Hence Theorem
2.9
gives that

\mathbb{E}(Z)\leq C_{1}\sqrt{\frac{sK^{2}_{\phi}\log^{2}\left(sK^{2}_{\phi}/(a_{\phi}\delta)\right)\log^{2}(1/\delta)\log(\mathrm{e}n)}{a_{\phi}m}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\sup_{x\in T}{\|Ax\|}^{2}_{2}}+C_{2}\delta\mathbb{E}\sup_{x\in T}{\|Ax\|}^{2}_{2}+C_{3}\delta

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and some universal constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
.
Observe that
\mathbb{E}\sup_{x\in T}{\|Ax\|}^{2}_{2}\leq\mathbb{E}(Z)+1
.
Hence

\mathbb{E}(Z)\leq C_{1}\sqrt{\frac{sK^{2}_{\phi}\log^{2}\left(sK^{2}_{\phi}/(a_{\phi}\delta)\right)\log^{2}(1/\delta)\log(\mathrm{e}n)}{a_{\phi}m}}\left(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(Z)}+1\right)+C_{2}\delta(\mathbb{E}(Z)+1)+C_{3}\delta.

Assuming without loss of generality that
C_{2}\geq 1
, we now pick
\delta=1/(2C_{2})
to obtain

\mathbb{E}(Z)\leq C_{4}\sqrt{\frac{sK^{2}_{\phi}\log^{2}\left(2C_{2}sK^{2}_{\phi}/a_{\phi}\right)\log(\mathrm{e}n)}{a_{\phi}m}}\left(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(Z)}+1\right)+C_{5},

for some universal constants
C_{4},C_{5}>0
.
This is a quadratic inequality for
\mathbb{E}(Z)
. Completing the square, we deduce that

\mathbb{E}(Z)\leq c\left(\frac{sK^{2}_{\phi}\log^{2}\left(2C_{2}sK^{2}_{\phi}/a_{\phi}\right)\log(\mathrm{e}n)}{a_{\phi}m}+1\right),

(2.9)

for some universal constant
c>0
.
Having done this, we now look to bound
Z
in probability. For this we use Talagrand’s concentration inequality. See, e.g.,
[
16
, Thm. 4.1]
. For this example, we have
\mathcal{F}=\{|\langle x,\cdot\rangle|^{2}/m:x\in T\}
,
\sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\leq K^{2}_{\phi}s/(a_{\phi}m)
and
\beta_{\mathcal{F}}\leq K^{2}_{\phi}s/(a_{\phi}m)
. Therefore

\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq\mathbb{E}(Z)+\sqrt{2\frac{uK^{2}_{\phi}s}{a_{\phi}m}(1+2\mathbb{E}(Z))}+\frac{1}{3}\frac{K^{2}s}{a_{\phi}m}u\right)\leq 2\exp(-u),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall u>0.

Now, let
u_{0}>0
be the unique solution of
\sqrt{2u_{0}K^{2}_{\phi}s/(a_{\phi}m)(1+2\mathbb{E}(Z)))}+K^{2}_{\phi}su_{0}/(3a_{\phi}m)=g/2
. Then
\mathbb{P}(Z\geq\mathbb{E}(Z)+g/2)\leq 2\exp(-u_{0})
and we have

u_{0}\geq\frac{g^{2}/4}{2K^{2}_{\phi}s/(a_{\phi}m)(1+2\mathbb{E}(Z))+K^{2}_{\phi}sg/(3a_{\phi}m)}.

Further, we have
Z\geq\sup_{x\in T}{\|Ax\|}^{2}_{2}-1
.
Hence

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in T}{\|Ax\|}_{2}\geq\sqrt{g+1}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq g\right)

Now suppose that
\mathbb{E}(Z)\leq g/2
,
which, due to (
2.9
), is implied by (
2.8
) with
c_{1}=C_{2}
and
c_{2}\leq\min\{1,1/2c\}
. Moreover, since the left-hand side of (
2.8
) is at least
1
, we have
c_{2}g\geq 1
. Hence
c_{2}\leq 1
implies
g\geq 1
. This along with
\mathbb{E}(Z)\leq g/2
implies that
1+2\mathbb{E}(Z)\leq 1+g\leq 2g
. Hence, by (
2.4
),
u_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle u_{0}\geq\frac{3g}{52}\cdot\frac{a_{\phi}m}{K^{2}_{\phi}s}.

Then, for an appropriate constant
c_{3}>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in T}{\|Ax\|}_{2}\geq\sqrt{g+1}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq g\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq\mathbb{E}(Z)+\frac{g}{2}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-c_{3}g\frac{a_{\phi}m}{K^{2}_{\phi}s}\right),

as required.
∎

We now use the following interpolation inequality, which is a standard result (see, e.g.,

∎

The rest of this section proves Theorem

. These index sets have two key properties. First, the norm of the corresponding coefficients it controllable: namely,

{\|c_{T_{k}}\|}_{2}\leq\sigma_{s_{k-1}}(c)_{2}+\tau_{n_{k-1}}(c)_{2}.

Second, each
T_{k}
have controlled cardinality specified by

, and it lies within a finite range specified by

The construction of this partition allows us to bound the term

Proof of Theorem
1.2

We first present the two lemmas that form the crux of our analysis.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
z\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
and consider integers
1=n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
and
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots
. Let

,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and define

as the index set of the largest
t_{1}
entries of

in absolute value in
\{n_{0}+1,\ldots,n_{1}\}

T_{1}=\{n_{0}+1,\ldots,n_{1}\}
if

. Then define
T_{2}
as the index set of the largest
t_{2}
entries of
z
in absolute value in
\{n_{0}+1,\ldots,n_{2}\}\backslash T_{1}
,
T_{3}
as the index set of the largest
t_{3}
entries of
z
in absolute value in
\{n_{0}+1,\ldots,n_{3}\}\backslash(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

T_{1},T_{2},\ldots
forms a partition of

\mathrm{supp}(z)=\{i:z_{i}\neq 0\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Clearly the sets are disjoint. Hence we only need to show that their union is
\mathrm{supp}(z)
. Suppose first that
r:=|\mathrm{supp}(z)|<\infty
. Then
\mathrm{supp}(z)\subseteq\{n_{0}+1,\ldots,n_{k}\}
for some
k
. Since
|T_{i}|=t_{i}\geq 1
, it follows that
T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{k+r}\supseteq\mathrm{supp}(z)
, as required.

Now suppose that
|\mathrm{supp}(z)|=+\infty
. Consider an index
i\in\mathrm{supp}(z)
. Define

S_{i}=\{j:|z_{j}|\geq|z_{i}|\},\hskip 10.00002ptm_{i}=|S_{i}|,\hskip 10.00002pta_{i}=\max S_{i}.

Notice that
m_{i},a_{i}<\infty
since
z\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
. Now choose
k
sufficiently large so that
n_{k}\geq a_{i}
and
s_{k}>m_{i}
. This is possible, since
\{n_{i}\}
and
\{s_{i}\}
are strictly increasing sequences of positive integers. We now show that
i\in T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{k}
, by arguing by contradiction. Suppose that
i\notin T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{k}
. Then every

is such that

. Hence

.
However, by construction,

|T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{k}|=t_{1}+\cdots+t_{k}=s_{k}-s_{0}=s_{k},

while

. This is a contradiction. Therefore

, as required.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Tail bound)
.

Suppose that
f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\phi_{i}\in L^{2}_{\rho}(D)
be defined everywhere and let
e
be as in (
1.6
). Consider integers
n=n_{0}<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
and
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots

{\|e\|}_{2}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\theta_{k}\left(\sigma_{s_{k-1}}(c)_{2}+{\|c-c_{[n_{k-1}]}\|}_{2}\right),

where

\theta_{k}=\sup\left\{{\|A_{k}z\|}_{2}:z\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{k}},{\|z\|}_{0}\leq s_{k},{\|z\|}_{2}=1\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptA_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\left(\phi_{j}(x_{i})\right)_{i\in[m],j\in[n_{0}+1,n_{k}]}.

Proof.

Let
z=(c_{i})^{\infty}_{i=n+1}
. We first use Lemma

{\|e\|}_{2}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\theta_{k}{\|c_{T_{k}}\|}_{2}.

{\|c_{T_{2}}\|}^{2}_{2}\leq\sigma_{s_{1}}(c)^{2}_{2}+{\|c-c_{[n_{1}]}\|}^{2}_{2}.

\displaystyle{\|c_{T_{k}}\|}^{2}_{2}\leq\sum_{i\in[n_{0},\ldots,n_{k}]\backslash(T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{k-1})}|c_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[n_{0},\ldots,n_{k-1}]\backslash(T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{k-1})}|c_{i}|^{2}+{\|c-c_{[n_{k-1}]}\|}^{2}_{2}

to the matrix

, with

N=n_{k}
we see that

\mathbb{P}\left(\theta_{k}\geq\sqrt{2b_{\phi}g_{k}}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{{\|B_{k}x\|}_{2}=1,{\|x\|}_{0}\leq s_{k}}{\|A_{k}x\|}_{2}\geq\sqrt{g_{k}+1}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C_{1}g_{k}\frac{a_{\phi}m}{K^{2}_{\phi}s_{k}}\right),

(3.7)

for some universal constant
C_{1}>0
, provided, for some universal constants
C_{2},C_{3}>0
with
C_{3}\leq 1
,
g_{k}
satisfies

\frac{s_{k}K^{2}_{\phi}\log^{2}(2C_{2}K^{2}_{\phi}s_{k}/a_{\phi})\log(\mathrm{e}n_{k})}{a_{\phi}m}+1\leq C_{3}g_{k}.

(3.8)

Applying the condition (
1.10
) and the fact that
s_{k}=2^{k}s
and
n_{k}=2^{k}n
we see that

\displaystyle\frac{s_{k}K^{2}_{\phi}\log^{2}(2C_{2}K^{2}_{\phi}s_{k}/a_{\phi})\log(\mathrm{e}n_{k})}{a_{\phi}m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{k}\log^{2}(2^{k+1}C_{2}K^{2}_{\phi}s/a_{\phi})\log(2^{k}\mathrm{e}n)}{\log^{2}(2K^{2}_{\phi}s/a_{\phi})\log(2n)+\log(2/\varepsilon)}\leq c_{1}k^{3}2^{k}

(3.9)

for some universal constant
c_{1}>0
.
Hence we now pick
g_{k}=g_{*}k^{3}2^{k}
, where
g_{*}\geq 2C_{3}/c_{1}
so that (
3.8
) holds. In this case, (
3.7
) and the condition (
1.10
) imply that

\mathbb{P}\left(\theta_{k}\geq\sqrt{2b_{\phi}g_{*}}k^{3/2}2^{k/2}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{g_{*}}{c_{2}}k^{3}\log(2/\varepsilon)\right),

where
c_{2}>0
is a universal constant. Notice that

\frac{g_{*}}{c_{2}}k^{3}\log(2/\varepsilon)\geq(k+2)\log(2)+\log(1/\varepsilon)

after potentially increasing
g_{*}
. Hence

\mathbb{P}\left(\theta_{k}\geq r_{k}\right)\leq\varepsilon/2^{k+1}=\varepsilon_{k},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where }r_{k}=\sqrt{2b_{\phi}g_{*}}k^{3/2}2^{k/2},

which implies that
\mathbb{P}(F^{c}_{k})\leq\varepsilon_{k}
with this choice of
r_{k}
.

Since
\mathbb{P}(F^{c})\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}(F^{c}_{k})\leq\varepsilon/2
, we deduce that with probability at least
1-\varepsilon/2
,
e
satisfies

{\|e\|}_{2}\lesssim\sqrt{b_{\phi}}\left(\tau_{n}(c)_{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}k^{3/2}2^{k/2}\left(\sigma_{2^{k}s}(c)_{2}+\tau_{2^{k}n}(c)_{2}\right)\right).

Since
\sigma_{s}(c)_{2}
is nonincreasing in
s
, we have

s(2^{k}-1)(\sigma_{2^{k}s}(c)_{2})^{u}\leq\sigma_{s+1}(c)^{u}_{2}+\cdots+\sigma_{2^{k}s}(c)^{u}_{2}\leq\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}

and therefore

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}k^{3/2}2^{k/2}\sigma_{2^{k}s}(c)_{2}\leq\left(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{k^{3/2}2^{k/2}}{(2^{k}-1)^{\frac{1}{u}}}\lesssim_{u}\left(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}},

(3.10)

where we recall that
u<2
. By the same argument, we also have

\tau_{n}(c)_{2}+\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}k^{3}2^{k}\tau_{2^{k}n}(c)_{2}\lesssim_{v}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{n}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}},

as
v<2
. Thus, with probability at least
1-\varepsilon/2
, we have

\displaystyle{\|e\|}_{2}\lesssim_{u,v}\sqrt{b_{\phi}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}}+\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{n}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}}\right).

(3.11)

We now substitute this into (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) and apply the union bound to deduce that, with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle\|c_{[n]}-\hat{c}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u,v}\sigma_{s}(c_{[n]})_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{sb_{\phi}}}{\sqrt{a_{\phi}}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}}+\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{j}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}}\right],

\displaystyle\|c_{[n]}-\hat{c}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u,v}\frac{\sigma_{s}(c_{[n]})_{1}}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{\sqrt{b_{\phi}}}{\sqrt{a_{\phi}}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}}+\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{j}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}}\right].

Notice that this holds for all
f
with at least probability
1-\varepsilon
, since the events
E,F
are independent of
f
. Consider the
L^{2}_{\rho}
-norm error. Using (
1.1
) and the bound

\tau_{n}(c)_{2}\leq\left(\frac{\tau_{n/2+1}(c)^{v}_{2}+\cdots+\tau_{n}(c)^{v}_{2}}{n/2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}}\lesssim_{v}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{j}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}},

we get

\displaystyle{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{b_{\phi}}\left({\|c_{[n]}-\hat{c}\|}_{2}+\tau_{n}(c)_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u,v}\sqrt{b_{\phi}}\left\{\frac{\sigma_{s}(c_{[n]})_{1}}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{\sqrt{b_{\phi}}}{\sqrt{a_{\phi}}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}}+\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{j}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}}\right]\right\}.

While for the
L^{\infty}_{\rho}
-norm error we use the bound

{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}\leq K_{\phi}\left({\|c_{[n]}-\hat{c}\|}_{1}+\tau_{n}(c)_{1}\right)

to obtain

\displaystyle{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u,v}K_{\phi}\left\{\sigma_{s}(c_{[n]})_{1}+\tau_{n}(c)_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{s}\sqrt{b_{\phi}}}{\sqrt{a_{\phi}}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}}+\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{j}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}}\right]\right\}

with the same probability. We now use (
3.5
).
∎

4
Application to weighted Wiener-type spaces

In this section, we consider the mixed weighted Wiener spaces
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
discussed in §
1.4
. However, to illustrate the generality of our approach, we work with abstract Wiener-type spaces, from which the main result, Theorem
1.4
, for
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
follows as a special case.

4.1
Abstract Wiener-type spaces

Throughout this section, we consider the abstract setting where
(D,\mathcal{D},\rho)
is a probability space and
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded orthonormal basis of functions defined everywhere. In particular, this need not be the Fourier basis on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Note that we could easily consider a Riesz basis, in which case the various error bounds would involve the constants
a_{\phi},b_{\phi}
. For simplicity, we will not do this.
Let
\theta\in(0,\infty]
and let
w=(w_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive weights satisfying

w\in\ell^{1/(1-1/\theta)_{+}}(\mathbb{N}).

Consider the set of functions

F=F_{w,\theta}=\left\{f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\phi_{i}:\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{|c_{i}|}{w_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\leq 1\right\}.

(4.1)

Notice that the series
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\phi_{i}(x)
converges absolutely for all
x
, due to the boundedness of the
\phi_{i}
’s. For
\theta>1
this follows from Hölder’s inequality and the fact that
w\in\ell^{1/(1-1/\theta)}(\mathbb{N})
. For
\theta\in(0,1]
, it follows from first noting that
\|w\|_{\ell^{\infty}}<\infty
and second observing that the condition
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{|c_{i}|}{w_{i}}\right)^{\theta}\leq 1
implies that
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{|c_{i}|}{w_{i}}\leq 1
. Hence any
f\in F
is defined everywhere.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\phi_{i}\in F
. Then the following holds for any
\theta\in[1,\infty]
.

(i)

If
1\leq p<\theta
then
\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}\leq\sigma_{s}(w)_{\frac{1}{1/p-1/\theta}}.

(ii)

If
\theta\leq p\leq\infty
then

\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\\
s_{1}+s_{2}=s\end{subarray}}\left\{\sigma_{s_{1}}(w)_{\infty}\cdot s^{1/p-1/\theta}_{2}\right\}.

Moreover, we also have

\sup_{f=\sum_{i}c_{i}\phi_{i}\in F}\tau_{n}(c)_{p}=\tau_{n}(w)_{\tilde{p}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where }\tilde{p}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1/p-1/\theta}&1\leq p<\theta\\
+\infty&\theta\leq p\leq\infty\end{cases}.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a bijection that gives a nonincreasing rearrangement of
w
and set
d_{i}=c_{i}/w_{i}
. Consider case (i). Then, by Hölder’s inequality,

\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}\leq\left(\sum_{i>s}|d_{\pi(i)}|^{p}w^{p}_{\pi(i)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>s_{1}\end{subarray}}|d_{\pi(i)}|^{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>s_{1}\end{subarray}}|w_{\pi(i)}|^{\frac{p\theta}{\theta-p}}\right)^{\frac{\theta-p}{p\theta}}.

For the first term, the definition of
F
gives
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>s\end{subarray}}|d_{\pi(i)}|^{\theta}\leq 1
.
For the second term, the fact that
\pi
is a nonincreasing rearrangement gives

\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>s\end{subarray}}|w_{\pi(i)}|^{\frac{p\theta}{\theta-p}}\right)^{\frac{\theta-p}{p\theta}}\leq\sigma_{s}(w)_{\frac{p\theta}{\theta-p}}.

This completes the proof of case (i).

Now consider case (ii). Fix
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
with
s_{1}+s_{2}=s
. Let
T\subseteq\{\pi(i):i>s_{1}\}
,
|T|=s_{2}
be the index set of the largest
s_{2}
coefficients of
(d_{\pi(i)})_{i>s_{1}}
in absolute value. By Stechkin’s inequality, we have

\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}\leq\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i>s_{1}\\
\pi(i)\notin T\end{subarray}}|d_{\pi(i)}|^{p}w^{p}_{\pi(i)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\sigma_{s_{1}}(w)_{\infty}\sigma_{s_{2}}(d)_{p}\leq\sigma_{s_{1}}(w)_{\infty}s^{1/p-1/\theta}_{2}{\|d\|}_{\theta}\leq\sigma_{s_{1}}(w)_{\infty}s^{1/p-1/\theta}_{2}.

This completes the proof of case (ii).

Consider the second result. For
1\leq p<\theta
, arguing in a similar way, we have

\tau_{n}(c)_{p}=\sum_{i>n}|d_{i}|^{p}w^{p}_{i}\leq\left(\sum_{i>d}|d_{i}|^{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left(\sum_{i>n}w^{\frac{p\theta}{\theta-p}}_{i}\right)^{\frac{\theta-p}{p\theta}}\leq\tau_{n}(w)_{\frac{1}{1/p-1/\theta}}.

Conversely, for
p\geq\theta
, we have

\tau_{n}(c)_{p}\leq\tau_{n}(c)_{\theta}=\left(\sum_{i>n}|d_{i}|^{\theta}w^{\theta}_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq\tau_{n}(w)_{\infty},

as required. Having shown the desired upper bound, we only need to find an
f\in F
for which
\tau_{n}(c)_{p}\geq\tau_{n}(w)_{\tilde{p}}
. Suppose that
1\leq p<\theta
and define
f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\phi_{i}
, where

c_{1}=\cdots=c_{n}=0,\hskip 20.00003ptc_{i}=\frac{w^{\tilde{p}/p}_{i}}{\tau_{n}(w)^{\tilde{p}/\theta}_{\tilde{p}}},\hskip 10.00002pti>n.

Notice that
f\in F
by construction, and also that

\tau_{n}(c)^{p}_{p}=\sum_{i>n}\frac{w^{\tilde{p}}_{i}}{\tau_{n}(w)^{p\tilde{p}/\theta}_{\tilde{p}}}=\tau_{n}(w)^{\tilde{p}-p\tilde{p}/\theta}_{\tilde{p}}=\tau_{n}(w)^{p}_{\tilde{p}},

Hence
\tau_{n}(c)_{p}=\tau_{n}(w)_{\tilde{p}}
, as required. Now consider
p\geq\theta
and let
f=w_{n^{*}}\phi_{n^{*}}
, where
n^{*}>n
is such that
\tau_{n}(w)_{\infty}=w_{n^{*}}
. Then
f\in F
and we trivially have
\tau_{n}(c)_{p}=w_{n^{*}}=\tau_{n}(w)_{\infty}
. This gives the result.
∎

We now state the following lemma, which is a short exercise.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
w_{i}\asymp i^{-r}\log^{t}(i+1)
for some
r>(1-1/\theta)_{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
w\in\ell^{1/(1-1/\theta)_{+}}(\mathbb{N})
and, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
, we have

\sigma_{s}(w)_{p}\leq\tau_{s}(w)_{p}\lesssim_{r,t,p}s^{\frac{1}{p}-r}\log^{t}(s+1).

Next, we establish the main result for the class (
4.1
), which is based on weights of the form of the previous lemma.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\infty]
,
r>(1-1/\theta)_{+}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and consider the class

F=\left\{f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\phi_{i}:\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\frac{i^{r}}{\log^{t}(i+1)}|c_{i}|\right)^{\theta}\leq 1\right\}.

(4.2)

Let

n=\begin{cases}\lceil s^{(r+1/\theta-1/2)/(r-(1-1/\theta)_{+})}\rceil&r\leq 1/2\\
\max\{s,\lceil s^{(r+1/\theta-1/2)/r}\rceil\}&r>1/2\end{cases},

0<\varepsilon<1
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\sim_{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}\rho
, where
m
satisfies

m\geq c\cdot K^{2}_{\phi}\cdot s\cdot\left(\log^{2}(2K^{2}_{\phi}s)\log(2n)+\log(2/\varepsilon)\right).

Then the following holds with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
. For any
f\in F
the approximation
\hat{f}=\mathcal{R}_{s,n}(f)
is at most
2s
-sparse and satisfies

{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{p}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{r,t}K^{1-2/p}_{\phi}s^{1-1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1).

Proof.

Since
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded orthonormal basis,
a_{\phi}=b_{\phi}=1
. Let
f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\phi_{i}
and
c=(c_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. We treat the cases
0<\theta<1
,
1\leq\theta<2
and
\theta\geq 2
separately.

Case 1:
0<\theta<1
.
Combining Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
we get

\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}\lesssim\sigma_{s/2}(w)_{\infty}s^{1/p-1/\theta}\lesssim_{r,t}s^{1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall p\in[1,\infty]

and

\tau_{n}(c)_{p}\lesssim\tau_{n}(w)_{\infty}\lesssim_{r,t}n^{-r}\log^{t}(n+1).

Applying Theorem
1.1
with
a_{\phi}=b_{\phi}=1
, we have

{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{p}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{r,t}K^{1-2/p}_{\phi}\left(s^{1-1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1)+s^{1/2-1/p}n^{-r}\log^{t}(n+1)\right).

We now use the fact that
n=\lceil s^{(r+1/\theta-1/2)/r}\rceil
(since
(1-1/\theta)_{+}=0
) in this case to obtain the desired bound.

Case 2:
1\leq\theta<2
.
Combining Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
we get

\sigma_{s}(c)_{1}\lesssim\sigma_{s}(w)_{\frac{1}{1-1/\theta}}\lesssim_{r,t}s^{1-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1)

and

\sigma_{s}(c)_{2}\lesssim\sigma_{s/2}(w)_{\infty}s^{1/2-1/\theta}\lesssim_{r,t}s^{1/2-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1),

as well as

\tau_{n}(c)_{1}\leq\tau_{n}(w)_{\frac{1}{1-1/\theta}}\lesssim_{r,t}n^{1-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(n+1)

and

\tau_{n}(c)_{2}\leq\tau_{n}(w)_{\infty}\lesssim_{r,t}n^{-r}\log^{t}(n+1).

We now divide into two cases: (a)
1-1/\theta<r\leq 1/2
and (b)
r>1/2
.

Case 2(a):
1-1/\theta<r\leq 1/2
.
In this case, we apply Theorem
1.1
once more to get

{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{p}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{r,t}K^{1-2/p}_{\phi}\left(s^{1-1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1)+s^{1/2-1/p}n^{1-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(n+1)\right).

The result follows after using the fact that
n=\lceil s^{(r+1/\theta-1/2)/(r-1+1/\theta)}\rceil
in this case.

Case 2(b):
r>1/2
.
In this case, we shall apply Theorem
1.2
. Since
r>1/2
, there exists a
v=v(r)\in(0,2)
such that
rv>1
. Using the above estimate for
\tau_{n}(c)_{2}
, we deduce that

\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{j}(c)^{v}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{v}}\lesssim_{r,t}n^{-r}\log^{t}(n+1).

Similarly, since
r>1-1/\theta
, there exists a
u=u(r)
such that
r-1/\theta-1/2)u>1
. It follows that

\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)^{u}_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{u}}\lesssim_{r,t}s^{1/2-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1).

Applying Theorem
1.2
we now see that

{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{p}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{r,t}K^{1-2/p}_{\phi}\left(s^{1-1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1)+s^{1/2-1/p}n^{-r}\log^{t}(n+1)\right).

The result follows after recalling that
n=\lceil s^{(r+1/\theta-1/2)/r}\rceil
in this case.

Case 3:
\theta\geq 2
.
Combining Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
we get

\sigma_{s}(c)_{p}\lesssim\sigma_{s}(w)_{\frac{1}{1/p-1/\theta}}\lesssim_{r,t}s^{1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1),\hskip 10.00002ptp=1,2,

and

\tau_{n}(c)_{p}\leq\tau_{n}(w)_{\frac{1}{1/p-1/\theta}}\lesssim_{r,t}n^{1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(n+1),\hskip 10.00002ptp=1,2.

Recall that
r>1-1/\theta
and therefore
r+1/\theta-1/2>1/2
. Hence we can find
u=u(r)\in(0,2)
such that
(r+1/\theta-1/2)u>1
. We now apply Theorem
1.2
(with
v=u
) to obtain

{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{p}_{\rho}}\lesssim_{r,t}K^{1-2/p}_{\phi}\left(s^{1-1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(s+1)+s^{1/2-1/p}n^{1/2-1/\theta-r}\log^{t}(n+1)\right).

The result follows after recalling that
n=s
in this case.
∎

4.2
Application to weighted mixed Wiener spaces

We now consider the spaces
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}
introduced in Definition
1.4
. In particular, we derive Theorem
1.4
as a corollary of Theorem
4.3
.
In order to connect
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}
to the setting of Theorem
4.3
, we show that the weights of
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}
, when ordered in nonincreasing order, are of the type considered therein. The following lemma was also used in part of the proof of
[
38
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
v=(v_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
with
v_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+|k_{i}|)^{-r}
, and
\pi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a bijection that gives nonincreasing rearrangement of
v
. Then

\displaystyle v_{\pi(i)}\lesssim_{d,r}i^{-r}\log^{(d-1)r}(i+1).

Proof.

For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\pi
orders
v
in nonincreasing order, the set
\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:v_{k}\geq v_{\pi(i)}\}
contains at least
i
elements. Observe that

\displaystyle\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:v_{k}\geq v_{\pi(i)}\}=\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+|k_{i}|)\leq v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}\right\}=\Lambda^{\mathsf{HC}}_{v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}}.

Since each factor
(1+|k_{i}|)
is a positive integer, the product
\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+|k_{i}|)
is always a positive integer, so
\Lambda^{\mathsf{HC}}_{v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}}=\Lambda^{\mathsf{HC}}_{\lfloor v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}\rfloor}
. Applying the size estimate at the integer order
\lfloor w_{n}^{-1/\alpha}\rfloor\in\mathbb{N}
(see, e.g.,
[
34
, Prop. A.1]
) and using the fact that
\lfloor v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}\rfloor\leq v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}
, we get

\displaystyle i\leq|\Lambda^{\mathsf{HC}}_{\lfloor v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}\rfloor}|\leq\lfloor v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}\rfloor\log^{d-1}(\mathrm{e}\cdot\lfloor v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}\rfloor)\leq v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}\log^{d-1}(\mathrm{e}\cdot v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}).

This gives
v_{\pi(i)}^{-1/r}\gtrsim_{d}i/\log^{d-1}(i+1)
, where
c_{d}
is a constant dependent on
d
, and therefore
v_{\pi(i)}\lesssim_{d,r}i^{-r}\log^{(d-1)r}(i+1)
, as required.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
v
and
\pi
be as in the previous lemma, and define the weights

w_{i}=i^{-r}\log^{(d-1)r}(i+1)
. Then the previous lemma implies that

f\in S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hskip 10.00002pt\Longrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{f}:=\frac{f}{C_{d,r,\theta}{\|f\|}_{S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}}}\in F_{w,\theta},

where
C_{d,r,\theta}>0
depends on
d
,
r
and
\theta
only. Notice that the sparse recovery procedure
\mathcal{R}_{s,n}
satisfies
\mathcal{R}_{s,n}(Cf)=C\mathcal{R}_{s,n}(f)
for any
C>0
. Hence we may apply Theorem
4.3
with
t=(d-1)r
to
\tilde{f}
and
K_{\phi}=1
to deduce that

{\|f-\hat{f}\|}_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{r,d}s^{1-1/p-1/\theta-r}\log^{(d-1)r}(s+1){\|f\|}_{\mathcal{S}^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}},

as required.
∎

4.3
Comparison to the sampling widths of
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

As in
[
38
]
, we now consider the nonlinear sampling width
\varrho_{m}

6
Conclusion

A standard approach to function approximation from random samples is to truncate the basis expansion at index
n
, form the measurement matrix from i.i.d. samples, and apply compressed sensing to recover a sparse coefficient vector. Truncating introduces the discrete error term
{\|e\|}_{2}
(defined in (
1.6
)), which in current approaches is bounded by
{\|f-f_{n}\|}_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}
(which is further bounded by
\tau_{n}(c)_{1}
). This is a worst-case bound that ignores the i.i.d. structure of the sample points, and with high probability i.i.d. points do not realise the pointwise maximum, so the standard bound is overly pessimistic. Its slow decay in
n
forces one to use large truncation sets, yielding large measurement matrices and high computational cost. Since
{\|e\|}_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f(x_{i})-f_{n}(x_{i})|^{2}
is an empirical sum-of-squares of the tail, at i.i.d. sample points it should track
{\|f-f_{n}\|}_{L^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}
with high probability rather than the
L^{\infty}
norm.

Our alternate approach to bounding
{\|e\|}_{2}
, inspired by the upper bounds in the form of scaled tail sum of approximation numbers and variants in works such as
[
32
,
33
,
40
,
23
,
28
]
, yields depending on terms of the form
(\frac{1}{s}\sum_{j>s}\sigma_{j}(c)_{2}^{u})^{1/u}
and
(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j>n/2}\tau_{j}(c)_{2}^{v})^{1/v}
, for any
0<u,v<2
. For functions with sufficient smoothness, these quantities decay no worse than the corresponding terms
\sigma_{s}(c)_{2}
and
\tau_{n}(c)_{2}
asymptotically. In the applications to weighted Wiener spaces and anisotropic Sobolev spaces, this translates to a truncation set that is essentially linear in
s
, giving smaller matrices and lower computational cost.

There are several avenues for future work. First, our results require
\ell^{u}
- and
\ell^{v}
-summability of the terms
