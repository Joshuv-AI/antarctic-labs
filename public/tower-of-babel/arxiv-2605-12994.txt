DP-Muon: Differentially Private Optimization via Matrix-Orthogonalized Momentum
Jihwan Kim, Chenglin Fan

Abstract
We study differentially private (DP) training with Muon, a matrix-valued optimizer that updates hidden-layer weights using momentum followed by Newton–Schulz orthogonalization. While DP-SGD is well understood, the interaction between per-example clipping, Gaussian noise, momentum, and nonlinear orthogonalization in Muon has not been systematically analyzed. We formulate DP-Muon, a private Muon procedure that clips per-example matrix gradients, adds Gaussian noise to the clipped lot average, and then applies momentum and Newton–Schulz orthogonalization as post-processing. We prove that DP-Muon inherits the privacy guarantee certified by the corresponding same-lot subsampled Gaussian accountant, with no additional privacy cost from Muon-specific post-processing. On the optimization side, we establish finite-horizon and vanishing stationarity guarantees under per-matrix clipping, with bounds that separate optimization error, clipping residual, privacy noise, and Newton–Schulz approximation error. We further show that the DP-induced bias in Muon arises not in the linear momentum buffer itself, but after the nonlinear Newton–Schulz map, where Gaussian noise induces a matrix-valued heat-smoothing bias. This motivates DP-MuonBC, a bias-corrected variant that removes the leading output-level bias term while preserving the same privacy guarantee. Experiments on E2E and DART show that Muon-style matrix updates improve private fine-tuning, and that DP-MuonBC further improves utility without increasing the privacy budget.

The basic recipe of DP is by now familiar: sample a minibatch, clip per-example gradients to control sensitivity, add Gaussian noise, and compose the privacy loss over training steps using an accountant.

DP-SGD has been extended in many directions, including adaptive and preconditioned methods

A recent example is
Muon

)
, an optimizer for hidden-layer matrix parameters that replaces the raw update direction with an orthogonalized momentum matrix, typically computed in practice using a short Newton–Schulz iteration. Empirically, this practical SVD-free implementation performs strongly at scale, making Muon a promising alternative to conventional vector-based optimizers
(

. On the theory side, several recent works have begun to analyze the convergence behavior of Muon

. On the privacy side, however, the picture is even less developed.

This paper studies a natural
DP-Muon
procedure that adds Gaussian noise to the clipped
per-example average
before
the momentum and Newton–Schulz steps. This placement is attractive
for two reasons. First, it preserves the standard DP-SGD accounting template: clipping bounds the
sensitivity of the released lot average, Gaussian noise gives a per-step private mechanism, and privacy
amplification plus composition yield the full-run guarantee

(
1
;
5
;
26
)
. Second, it lets us analyze the optimizer in
a way that remains faithful to practical Muon, where the update direction is generated from a matrix
momentum buffer rather than from an exact SVD-based polar factor.

Our focus is on the hidden-layer matrix blocks that are updated by Muon. For these blocks, we formulate
a per-matrix clipping mechanism, record a clean privacy guarantee whose scaling depends on the clipping
threshold, sampling rate, number of steps, and noise multiplier but not explicitly on the ambient matrix
dimension, and then prove a first vanishing stationarity guarantee for the resulting noisy orthogonalized
method. The optimization argument isolates two quantities that are especially relevant in the private
setting: an explicit
clipping residual
, which captures the bias introduced by per-matrix clipping,
and a
Newton–Schulz quality parameter
, which captures the approximation error from using only a
finite number of orthogonalization steps.

Our contributions.

First, we formulate DP-Muon, a private Muon variant that clips per-example
matrix gradients, adds Gaussian noise to the released lot average, and treats
momentum and Newton–Schulz orthogonalization as post-processing.
Second, we prove that DP-Muon inherits the privacy guarantee of the
corresponding same-lot joint sampled Gaussian transcript, clarifying that
the per-matrix releases should be accounted jointly rather than as
independently subsampled mechanisms.
Third, we establish a finite-horizon stationarity bound under per-matrix
clipping, separating optimization error, clipping residual, privacy noise,
and Newton–Schulz approximation error.
Fourth, we identify an output-level bias induced by applying Newton–Schulz
to noisy momentum and introduce DP-MuonBC, a post-processing correction that
cancels the leading fixed-path bias term. Experiments on E2E and DART show
that DP-MuonBC improves private fine-tuning without increasing the privacy
budget.

Setting and sampling.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the dataset. At step

For fixed-size sampling,

For Poisson sampling,

The privacy accountant must match the actual sampling rule, adjacency notion,
noise scales, and number of iterations.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be the set of hidden-layer matrix parameters updated by
Muon. Non-Muon parameters are omitted from the notation; if they are updated
privately, their clipped-and-noised releases are appended to the same transcript

Per-matrix clipping and joint release.

For each
W\in\mathcal{W}

C_{W}>0
. For a

W

\operatorname{clip}_{C_{W}}(A):=\frac{A}{\max\{1,\|A\|_{F}/C_{W}\}}.

At step

G_{t}(z;W):=\nabla_{W}\ell(\theta_{t-1};z),\qquad H_{t}(z;W):=\operatorname{clip}_{C_{W}}\bigl(G_{t}(z;W)\bigr).

Thus each block contribution satisfies
\|H_{t}(z;W)\|_{F}\leq C_{W}
. DP-Muon
releases the per-matrix noisy average

\widetilde{G}_{t}(W)=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{B}_{t}}H_{t}(z;W)+Z_{t}(W),\qquad Z_{t}(W)\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}C_{W}^{2}}{B^{2}}I_{\dim(W)}\right),

with independent noises across
W
and
t
. The full per-step release is

\mathcal{R}_{t}:=\bigl(\widetilde{G}_{t}(W)\bigr)_{W\in\mathcal{W}}.

Although the noise is calibrated per matrix block, all blocks in

\mathcal{R}_{t}
share the same sampled lot
\mathcal{B}_{t}
. Hence privacy is
accounted for the same-lot joint product Gaussian mechanism, not for
independently subsampled blocks. For replace-one adjacency, the corresponding
per-block sensitivity is
2C_{W}/B
, equivalently the effective multiplier is

\sigma/2
if the above noise scale is kept.

Muon post-processing.

After
\mathcal{R}_{t}
is formed, DP-Muon updates momentum buffers and applies
a finite Newton–Schulz routine. These operations use only privatized
quantities and public hyperparameters, so they are post-processing.

For a matrix block
W
of shape
m_{W}\times n_{W}
, define

\mathcal{T}_{W}(A):=\begin{cases}A^{\top},&m_{W}>n_{W},\\
A,&m_{W}\leq n_{W}.\end{cases}

The inverse map
\mathcal{T}_{W}^{-1}
is the same transpose operation when

m_{W}>n_{W}
, and the identity otherwise.

2.2
Privacy Guarantee via Joint Accounting

Let

\mathcal{R}_{1:T}:=(\mathcal{R}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{R}_{T})

be the full noisy gradient transcript. The accountant is applied to the
mechanism
D\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{1:T}
, not to a single matrix block in
isolation. It must account for the actual same-lot joint product Gaussian
release at each step.

Theorem 2.1

(Privacy of DP-Muon)
.

Assume add/remove adjacency. At each step
t
, for every
W\in\mathcal{W}
,
clip per-example matrix gradients to Frobenius norm
C_{W}
and release

\widetilde{G}_{t}(W)=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{B}_{t}}H_{t}(z;W)+Z_{t}(W),\qquad Z_{t}(W)\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}C_{W}^{2}}{B^{2}}I_{\dim(W)}\right).

Let
\mathcal{R}_{t}=(\widetilde{G}_{t}(W))_{W\in\mathcal{W}}
be the resulting
same-lot joint product Gaussian release. If a valid accountant for the actual
sampling rule and this joint transcript certifies

D\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{1:T}
as
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-differentially
private, then Algorithm
1
, including the final parameters and
any released intermediate Muon quantities, is also

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-differentially private.

If additional non-Muon parameters are updated privately, their releases must be
included in the same accountant or validly composed. For replace-one adjacency,
use per-block sensitivity
2C_{W}/B
, equivalently effective multiplier

\sigma/2
when the above noise scale is kept.

The proof follows from per-block sensitivity, valid accounting of the same-lot
joint product Gaussian transcript, adaptive composition, and post-processing;
see Appendix
A

3
A Vanishing Stationarity Bound for DP-Muon with Per-Matrix Clipping

We analyze one Muon-target matrix block

W_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, with

r=\min\{m,n\}
and
d=mn
. Let

f(W)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{D}}[\ell(W;z)],\qquad g_{t}=\nabla f(W_{t-1}),\qquad D_{0}=f(W_{0})-\inf_{W}f(W).

The natural stationarity measure for Muon is the nuclear norm

\|g_{t}\|_{*}
, rather than the Frobenius norm. This is because the Muon
direction is approximately the polar factor of the momentum matrix and has
operator norm at most one. Thus the descent pairing is controlled by the
duality between the nuclear norm and the operator norm.

At step
t
, DP-Muon forms the private gradient

\widetilde{G}_{t}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\frac{G_{t}^{(i)}}{\max\{1,\|G_{t}^{(i)}\|_{F}/C\}}+Z_{t},\qquad Z_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}I_{d}\right),

and updates

M_{t}=\beta M_{t-1}+\widetilde{G}_{t},\qquad O_{t}=\mathrm{NewtonSchulz}_{q}(M_{t}),\qquad W_{t}=W_{t-1}-\eta O_{t}.

The analysis separates the effect of clipping, privacy noise, momentum
averaging, and the finite-step Newton–Schulz approximation.

Assumptions and finite-step Newton–Schulz quality.

We state the stochastic assumptions separately from the deterministic
Newton–Schulz approximation guarantee used in the analysis. Let

\mathcal{F}_{t}
denote the filtration generated by the algorithm up to the end
of step
t
. Thus
W_{t-1}
and
M_{t-1}
are

\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable, while the samples and Gaussian privacy noise
at step
t
are drawn after conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
.

Assumption 3.1

(Operator–nuclear smoothness)
.

For all
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,

\|\nabla f(X)-\nabla f(Y)\|_{*}\leq L\|X-Y\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

Assumption 3.2

(Sampling, unbiasedness, and independent Gaussian noise)
.

For every
t
, conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
, the samples

z_{t,1},\ldots,z_{t,B}
are i.i.d., and the unclipped per-sample gradients
are unbiased:

\mathbb{E}[G_{t}^{(i)}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=g_{t},\qquad i=1,\ldots,B.

Moreover, the Gaussian privacy noise
Z_{t}
is independent of

\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
and of
\{z_{t,i}\}_{i=1}^{B}
, and satisfies

\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[\|Z_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=d\,\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}.

The Gaussian noises
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
are independent across time.

Lemma 3.3

(Finite-step Newton–Schulz quality)
.

Let
O_{t}=\mathrm{NewtonSchulz}_{q}(M_{t})
be the output of the
q
-step Newton–Schulz routine
used in Algorithm
1
, with the transpose convention and
Frobenius startup normalization described in Section
2
.
For the single matrix block analyzed in this section, write
\mathcal{T}
for
the corresponding shape convention, and let

Y_{t,0}:=\frac{\mathcal{T}(M_{t})}{\max\{1,\|\mathcal{T}(M_{t})\|_{F}\}}

be the normalized initializer of the routine. Let
\Pi_{t}
be the orthogonal
projector onto
\operatorname{range}(\mathcal{T}(M_{t}))
, and define

\delta_{t,0}:=\|\Pi_{t}-Y_{t,0}Y_{t,0}^{\top}\|_{\mathrm{op}},

with
\delta_{t,0}=0
when
M_{t}=0
. For a fixed horizon
T
, set

\delta_{0,T}:=\max_{1\leq t\leq T}\delta_{t,0},\qquad\varepsilon_{q}:=1-\sqrt{1-\delta_{0,T}^{(\kappa+1)^{q}}},\qquad\chi_{q}:=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{q}},

where
\kappa
is the degree of the Newton–Schulz polynomial. If

\delta_{0,T}<1
, then the finite-step analysis of

15
gives, for all
1\leq t\leq T
,

\|O_{t}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1,\qquad\|O_{t}-\mathrm{Polar}(M_{t})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\varepsilon_{q},\qquad\chi_{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{0,T}^{(\kappa+1)^{q}}}}.

Thus, for fixed
\delta_{0,T}<1
, the factor
\chi_{q}
approaches
1

doubly exponentially fast in
q
.

Define the effective clipping residual and noise scale by

\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C):=\sqrt{r}\,\sup_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}-C\bigr)_{+}\right],\qquad v_{C}^{2}:=\frac{C^{2}}{B}+\frac{d\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}.

The quantity
\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C)
measures the bias caused by Frobenius
clipping. It is small when the clipping threshold dominates the tail of the
per-sample matrix-gradient norm. The scale
v_{C}
combines the stochastic
minibatch fluctuation after clipping and the Gaussian privacy noise.

For the normalized momentum weights

w_{t,j}:=\frac{\beta^{t-j}}{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^{k}},\qquad 1\leq j\leq t,

define

\Gamma_{T}(\beta):=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

This term captures how much stochastic noise remains after momentum averaging.
When
\beta
is close to one, the momentum buffer averages many past noisy
gradients, but it also introduces a drift term because those past gradients are
evaluated at earlier iterates.

Theorem 3.4

(Finite-horizon stationarity bound)
.

Under Assumptions
3.1

and
3.2
, for every horizon
T\geq 1
,

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|g_{t}\|_{*}\leq\chi_{q}\left[\frac{D_{0}}{\eta T}+\frac{L\eta}{2}+(2-\varepsilon_{q})\left(\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C)+\sqrt{r}\,v_{C}\Gamma_{T}(\beta)+\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}\right)\right].

Theorem
3.4
separates the main sources of error.
The terms
D_{0}/(\eta T)
and
L\eta/2
are the usual finite-horizon
optimization terms. The term
\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C)
is the clipping residual.
The term
\sqrt{r}\,v_{C}\Gamma_{T}(\beta)
is the stochastic error from minibatch
sampling and Gaussian privacy noise after normalized momentum averaging. The
term
L\eta\beta/(1-\beta)
is the tracking drift caused by using stale
gradients in the momentum buffer. Finally, the multiplicative factor

\chi_{q}
captures the finite-step Newton–Schulz approximation quality; when
the Newton–Schulz direction is close to the polar factor,
\chi_{q}
is close
to one. The proof is deferred to Appendix
B
.

The next corollary shows that the bound can vanish with an appropriate
horizon-dependent choice of the stepsize, momentum parameter, and clipping
threshold. The light-tail condition is used only to control the clipping
residual
\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C)
.

Corollary 3.5

(Vanishing rate under a light-tail condition)
.

Assume additionally that there exists
K>0
such that

\sup_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\exp\!\left(\frac{\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}^{2}}{K^{2}}\right)\leq e.

For
T\geq 2
, choose

\eta_{T}=\eta_{0}T^{-3/4},\qquad\beta_{T}=1-T^{-1/2},\qquad C_{T}=K\sqrt{2\log T}.

Then

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|g_{t}\|_{*}\leq\chi_{q}\cdot O\!\left(T^{-1/4}\sqrt{\log T}\right).

In particular, if
\chi_{q}
is bounded uniformly in
T
, then the average
stationarity measure converges to zero.

The choice
C_{T}=K\sqrt{2\log T}
makes the clipping residual negligible under
the light-tail assumption, while
\beta_{T}=1-T^{-1/2}
balances the averaging
benefit of momentum against the tracking drift. The resulting rate is therefore
driven mainly by the privacy-noise term after momentum averaging.

4
DP-MuonBC: Output-Level Bias Correction

In DP-Muon, Gaussian privacy noise is added to the clipped minibatch average
before the momentum and Newton–Schulz steps. The momentum recursion is linear,
so the injected Gaussian perturbation remains additive at the momentum-buffer
level. The nontrivial bias appears only after applying the nonlinear
Newton–Schulz map that produces the Muon direction.

This section isolates this output-level effect and introduces DP-MuonBC, a
bias-corrected post-processing variant of DP-Muon. The analysis is stated in a
frozen clipped-path model. In this model, the clipped means are fixed
independently of the Gaussian privacy noises, and expectations are taken over
the privacy noises and the additional data-independent probe variables used by
DP-MuonBC. This distinction is important: in the fully adaptive training
trajectory, the iterate
W_{t-1}
, and hence the clipped mean

\bar{G}_{t}
, generally depends on previous privacy noises. Therefore the
fixed-path calculation below should be read as an output-bias identity rather
than a conditional Gaussian theorem for the fully adaptive trajectory. The
privacy guarantee, however, remains exact because DP-MuonBC is a randomized
post-processing of the same privatized gradients used by DP-Muon.

Frozen clipped-path model.

Fix one Muon-managed matrix parameter with shape
m\times n
, and write

d=mn
. Let

\bar{G}_{1},\ldots,\bar{G}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

be a fixed sequence of clipped-but-unnoised minibatch means. The privatized
gradient at step
s
is

\widetilde{G}_{s}=\bar{G}_{s}+Z_{s},\qquad Z_{s}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\nu^{2}I_{d}),\qquad\nu^{2}:=\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}},

where matrices are vectorized when interpreting the Gaussian covariance, and
the noises
Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots
are independent.

Let
0\leq\mu<1
be the momentum parameter. Define the clipped-but-unnoised
and private momentum buffers by

H_{s}=\mu H_{s-1}+\bar{G}_{s},\qquad M_{s}=\mu M_{s-1}+\widetilde{G}_{s},\qquad H_{0}=M_{0}=0.

For startup normalization, set

s_{t}:=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{j}=\frac{1-\mu^{t}}{1-\mu},\qquad\widehat{H}_{t}:=\frac{H_{t}}{s_{t}},\qquad\widehat{M}_{t}:=\frac{M_{t}}{s_{t}}.

Then the normalized private momentum satisfies the fixed-path Gaussian identity

\widehat{M}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\widehat{H}_{t}+\rho_{t}E_{t},\qquad E_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}),

where

\rho_{t}^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}\frac{1+\mu^{t}}{1-\mu^{t}}.

In particular,

\rho_{\infty}^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}.

Thus momentum averaging reduces the effective privacy-noise scale by the factor

(1-\mu)/(1+\mu)
in the long-run frozen-path limit.

Newton–Schulz output bias.

Let
Q_{\kappa,q}
denote the
q
-step Newton–Schulz output map used to
produce the Muon direction. In the frozen clipped-path model, the normalized
DP-Muon direction is

O_{t}^{(0)}:=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t}).

Since
Q_{\kappa,q}
is nonlinear, even centered Gaussian noise in

\widehat{M}_{t}
induces a bias after applying
Q_{\kappa,q}
. Let

(P_{\rho}F)(X):=\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}[F(X+\rho U)]

be the Gaussian heat operator. Then

\mathbb{E}_{\rm fp}[O_{t}^{(0)}]=(P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t}),

where
\mathbb{E}_{\rm fp}
denotes expectation in the frozen clipped-path
model. Under a local smoothness condition on
Q_{\kappa,q}
at

\widehat{H}_{t}
, the heat expansion gives

(P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t})=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+\frac{\rho_{t}^{2}}{2}\Delta Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho_{t}^{4}),

where
\Delta
is the entrywise Laplacian, applied componentwise. Hence the
leading privacy-induced bias is a matrix-valued curvature term at the output of
the Newton–Schulz map. It is not a scalar additive bias in the momentum buffer.

DP-MuonBC.

DP-MuonBC cancels the leading heat-smoothing term by an additional randomized
post-processing step. Let

U_{t,1},\ldots,U_{t,J}\overset{\rm i.i.d.}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})

be data-independent Gaussian probe matrices, independent of the privacy noises.
Define the antithetic probe average

O_{t}^{(2)}:=\frac{1}{2J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left[Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t}+\rho_{t}U_{t,j})+Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t}-\rho_{t}U_{t,j})\right],

and define the bias-corrected direction

O_{t}^{\rm bc}:=2O_{t}^{(0)}-O_{t}^{(2)}.

The corresponding DP-MuonBC update is

W_{t}=W_{t-1}-\eta_{t}O_{t}^{\rm bc}.

The correction is applied at the output of the Newton–Schulz map, rather than
to the momentum buffer. This is essential because the momentum noise is already
centered before the nonlinear map. The leading bias is created by the curvature
of
Q_{\kappa,q}
, so DP-MuonBC estimates the locally heat-smoothed output and
extrapolates back toward the unsmoothed Newton–Schulz direction. The
antithetic probes reduce Monte Carlo variance while preserving the same
expectation.

Theorem 4.1

(Fixed-path cancellation of the leading output bias)
.

In the frozen clipped-path model, suppose the Newton–Schulz output map

Q_{\kappa,q}
is locally smooth at
\widehat{H}_{t}
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{\rm fp}[O_{t}^{\rm bc}]=\bigl(2P_{\rho_{t}}-P_{\sqrt{2}\rho_{t}}\bigr)Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t}).

Moreover, if the local heat expansion is valid up to order six, then

\mathbb{E}_{\rm fp}[O_{t}^{\rm bc}]=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})-\frac{\rho_{t}^{4}}{4}\Delta^{2}Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho_{t}^{6}).

In particular,

\mathbb{E}_{\rm fp}[O_{t}^{\rm bc}]=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho_{t}^{4}),

so DP-MuonBC removes the leading
O(\rho_{t}^{2})
output-level bias in the
frozen clipped-path model.

The theorem explains the role of the extrapolation

2O_{t}^{(0)}-O_{t}^{(2)}
: the expectation of
O_{t}^{(0)}
corresponds to one
application of Gaussian heat smoothing, while the expectation of
O_{t}^{(2)}

corresponds to an additional independent smoothing step. The linear
combination
2P_{\rho_{t}}-P_{\sqrt{2}\rho_{t}}
cancels the leading
second-order heat term and leaves only fourth-order bias. The full proof,
including the local heat-expansion condition and the finite-probe Monte Carlo
error, is deferred to Appendix
C
.

Privacy.

The privacy statement for DP-MuonBC does not rely on the frozen-path
approximation. In the actual adaptive algorithm, DP-MuonBC uses the same
privatized gradients
\widetilde{G}_{t}
as DP-Muon. All subsequent operations,
including momentum normalization, evaluation of
Q_{\kappa,q}
, generation of
data-independent probes, formation of
O_{t}^{\rm bc}
, and the parameter
update, are randomized post-processing of already privatized quantities.
Therefore, by the post-processing property of differential privacy, DP-MuonBC
satisfies exactly the same
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP guarantee as DP-Muon for
any number of probes
J
.

Discussion.

Across both benchmarks, the Muon-based private optimizers are the strongest
methods among the compared optimizers. DP-Muon consistently improves
evaluation NLL over DP-Adam, suggesting that per-matrix clipped Muon is an
effective optimizer for private fine-tuning. The training curves further show
that this advantage persists throughout optimization rather than only appearing
at the final checkpoint. DP-MuonBC further improves this strong Muon baseline on
the primary likelihood metric for both datasets and achieves the best BLEU on
both benchmarks. On DART, DP-MuonBC ranks first on all three reported metrics.
Overall, these results suggest that Muon-style matrix updates are useful in the
private fine-tuning regime, and that the proposed bias correction can further
improve utility without increasing the privacy budget.

Conclusion and future directions.

We introduced DP-Muon, a differentially private Muon optimizer that clips
per-example matrix gradients, adds Gaussian noise to the released lot average,
and treats momentum and Newton–Schulz orthogonalization as post-processing.
We proved its joint-accounting privacy guarantee and established stationarity
bounds separating optimization error, clipping residual, privacy noise, and
Newton–Schulz approximation error. We also proposed DP-MuonBC, an output-level
bias-corrected variant with the same privacy guarantee. Experiments on E2E and
DART show consistent gains for private GPT-2 fine-tuning. Extending the theory
to fully adaptive multi-block trajectories and evaluating larger models and
broader tasks are natural future directions.

Proof of Theorem
2.1

Proof.

Fix a step
t
and condition on the past transcript

\mathcal{R}_{1:t-1}
, public randomness, and hyperparameters. Then

\theta_{t-1}
, the current Muon-managed parameters, and the clipping
thresholds are fixed.

For each
W\in\mathcal{W}
, per-matrix clipping gives

\|H_{t}(z;W)\|_{F}\leq C_{W}.

Hence, under add/remove adjacency, changing one example changes the unnoised
averaged release for block
W
by at most

\Delta_{W}=\frac{C_{W}}{B}

in Frobenius norm. Under replace-one adjacency, the corresponding bound is

2C_{W}/B
.

The released block is

\widetilde{G}_{t}(W)=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{B}_{t}}H_{t}(z;W)+Z_{t}(W),\qquad Z_{t}(W)\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}C_{W}^{2}}{B^{2}}I_{\dim(W)}\right).

Thus each block release has Gaussian noise calibrated to its own per-block
sensitivity
C_{W}/B
. The full release

\mathcal{R}_{t}=(\widetilde{G}_{t}(W))_{W\in\mathcal{W}}

is therefore a product of blockwise Gaussian releases formed from the same
sampled lot
\mathcal{B}_{t}
. Since the blocks share the same sampling event,
the privacy accountant is applied to this same-lot joint product Gaussian
mechanism, not to independently subsampled blocks.

By assumption, the chosen valid accountant for the actual sampling rule,
adjacency notion, blockwise clipping thresholds, Gaussian noise scales, number
of released groups, and number of iterations certifies the adaptive transcript
mechanism

D\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{1:T}(D)

as
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-differentially private. This gives privacy for the
entire noisy gradient transcript.

It remains only to check that Muon-specific operations add no privacy loss.
Given the initialization, public hyperparameters, and transcript

\mathcal{R}_{1:T}
, the momentum buffers are computed recursively as

M_{t}(W)=\beta M_{t-1}(W)+\widetilde{G}_{t}(W),\qquad W\in\mathcal{W}.

The Newton–Schulz directions

O_{t}(W)=\mathrm{NewtonSchulz}_{q}(M_{t}(W))

and parameter updates, including optional decoupled weight decay, are functions
of the same transcript and public hyperparameters. Any additional randomness
used by the implementation is data-independent. Hence the final output is a
possibly randomized post-processing of
\mathcal{R}_{1:T}
.

Therefore, by the post-processing property of differential privacy, the full
DP-Muon output, including final parameters and any released intermediate Muon
quantities, is also
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-differentially private. For
replace-one adjacency, the same argument applies with per-block sensitivity

2C_{W}/B
, equivalently with effective multiplier
\sigma/2
if the displayed
noise scale is kept fixed.

Appendix B
Proofs for Vanishing Stationarity Bound for DP-Muon with Per-Matrix Clipping

In this appendix, we give the full proof of the finite-horizon stationarity
bound for DP-Muon with per-matrix clipping. The analysis is for one fixed
Muon-target matrix block.

B.1
Single-block setup

Fix one Muon-target matrix parameter

W_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\qquad r:=\min\{m,n\},\qquad d:=mn.

Let

f(W):=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{D}}[\ell(W;z)],\qquad f^{\star}:=\inf_{W}f(W)>-\infty,\qquad D_{0}:=f(W_{0})-f^{\star},

and define

g_{t}:=\nabla f(W_{t-1}).

At iteration
t
, let

G_{t}^{(i)}:=\nabla\ell(W_{t-1};z_{t,i}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,B.

Per-matrix clipping is

H_{t}^{(i)}:=\frac{G_{t}^{(i)}}{\max\{1,\|G_{t}^{(i)}\|_{F}/C\}}=\alpha_{t}^{(i)}G_{t}^{(i)},\qquad\alpha_{t}^{(i)}:=\min\left\{1,\frac{C}{\|G_{t}^{(i)}\|_{F}}\right\}.

The released private gradient is

\widetilde{G}_{t}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}H_{t}^{(i)}+Z_{t},\qquad Z_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}I_{d}\right).

(1)

The DP-Muon recursion is

M_{t}=\beta M_{t-1}+\widetilde{G}_{t},\qquad M_{0}=0,\qquad O_{t}=\mathrm{NewtonSchulz}_{q}(M_{t}),\qquad W_{t}=W_{t-1}-\eta O_{t},

(2)

where
\beta\in[0,1)
and
\eta>0
may depend on the horizon
T
.

We use the filtration

\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma\!\left(W_{0},M_{0},\{z_{s,i}\}_{1\leq s\leq t,\,1\leq i\leq B},\{Z_{s}\}_{1\leq s\leq t}\right),

augmented by all deterministic quantities generated by the algorithm up to time

t
, including
\widetilde{G}_{s},M_{s},O_{s},W_{s}
for
s\leq t
. Thus

W_{t-1}
and
M_{t-1}
are
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable, while the
minibatch samples and Gaussian privacy noise at time
t
are drawn after
conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
.

Throughout,
\mathrm{Polar}(X)
denotes a polar factor satisfying

\|\mathrm{Polar}(X)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1,\qquad\langle X,\mathrm{Polar}(X)\rangle_{F}=\|X\|_{*},

with the convention
\mathrm{Polar}(0)=0
.

B.2
Assumptions

Assumption B.1

(Operator–nuclear smoothness)
.

For all
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,

\|\nabla f(X)-\nabla f(Y)\|_{*}\leq L\|X-Y\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

Assumption B.2

(Sampling, unbiasedness, and independent Gaussian noise)
.

For every
t
, conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
, the samples

z_{t,1},\ldots,z_{t,B}
are i.i.d., and

\mathbb{E}[G_{t}^{(i)}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=g_{t},\qquad i=1,\ldots,B.

Moreover,
Z_{t}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
and of

\{z_{t,i}\}_{i=1}^{B}
, and satisfies

\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[\|Z_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=d\,\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}.

The Gaussian noises
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
are independent across time.

B.3
Finite-step Newton–Schulz quality

The proof uses the Newton–Schulz direction only through spectral norm control
and its approximation quality to the polar factor of the momentum. We use the
finite-step Newton–Schulz analysis of
15
.

Consider the degree-
\kappa
Newton–Schulz polynomial

p_{\kappa}(\lambda)=\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa}\frac{(2s)!}{4^{s}(s!)^{2}}(1-\lambda)^{s}.

For a given
M_{t}
, initialize

X_{t,0}=\frac{M_{t}}{\alpha_{t}},\qquad\alpha_{t}:=\max\{1,\|M_{t}\|_{F}\},

using the usual transpose convention when
m>n
, and perform
q

Newton–Schulz steps

X_{t,j+1}=p_{\kappa}(X_{t,j}X_{t,j}^{\top})X_{t,j},\qquad j=0,\ldots,q-1.

Let
O_{t}:=X_{t,q}
. If
\Pi_{t}
is the orthogonal projector onto

\operatorname{range}(M_{t})
and

\delta_{t,0}:=\|\Pi_{t}-X_{t,0}X_{t,0}^{\top}\|_{\mathrm{op}},

with
\delta_{t,0}=0
when
M_{t}=0
, then the results of

15
imply

\|O_{t}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1,\qquad\|O_{t}-\mathrm{Polar}(M_{t})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1-\sqrt{1-\delta_{t,0}^{(\kappa+1)^{q}}}.

For a fixed horizon
T
, define

\delta_{0,T}:=\max_{1\leq t\leq T}\delta_{t,0},\qquad\varepsilon_{q}:=1-\sqrt{1-\delta_{0,T}^{(\kappa+1)^{q}}},\qquad\chi_{q}:=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{q}}.

Then, for all
1\leq t\leq T
,

\|O_{t}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1,\qquad\|O_{t}-\mathrm{Polar}(M_{t})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\varepsilon_{q},\qquad\chi_{q}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{0,T}^{(\kappa+1)^{q}}}}.

(3)

Thus, for fixed
\delta_{0,T}<1
, the factor
\chi_{q}
approaches
1

doubly exponentially fast in the number of Newton–Schulz steps
q
.

B.4
Clipping residual and private-gradient decomposition

Define the effective clipping residual and effective noise scale by

\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C):=\sqrt{r}\,\sup_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl(\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}-C\bigr)_{+}\right],

(4)

where
(x)_{+}:=\max\{x,0\}
. Also define

v_{C}^{2}:=\frac{C^{2}}{B}+\frac{d\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}.

(5)

Lemma B.3

(Clipped oracle decomposition)
.

Under Assumption
B.2
, the private gradient admits the
decomposition

\widetilde{G}_{t}=g_{t}+b_{t}+\xi_{t},

where
\xi_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable and

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}\|b_{t}\|_{*}\leq\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C),\qquad\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq v_{C}^{2}.

Proof.

Define

h_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[H_{t}^{(1)}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}],\qquad b_{t}:=h_{t}-g_{t},\qquad\xi_{t}:=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}(H_{t}^{(i)}-h_{t})+Z_{t}.

Then
\widetilde{G}_{t}=g_{t}+b_{t}+\xi_{t}
. By conditional i.i.d. sampling and the
independence and mean-zero property of
Z_{t}
,

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\mathbb{E}[H_{t}^{(i)}-h_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]+\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0.

For the bias term,

b_{t}=\mathbb{E}[(\alpha_{t}^{(1)}-1)G_{t}^{(1)}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}],

hence

\|b_{t}\|_{*}\leq\mathbb{E}[(1-\alpha_{t}^{(1)})\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{*}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq\sqrt{r}\,\mathbb{E}[(1-\alpha_{t}^{(1)})\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}].

Since

(1-\alpha_{t}^{(1)})\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}=(\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}-C)_{+},

taking expectation yields

\mathbb{E}\|b_{t}\|_{*}\leq\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C).

For the fluctuation term, conditional independence gives

\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\frac{1}{B^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\mathbb{E}[\|H_{t}^{(i)}-h_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]+\mathbb{E}[\|Z_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}].

Moreover,

\mathbb{E}[\|H_{t}^{(i)}-h_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|H_{t}^{(i)}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq C^{2},

and by Assumption
B.2
,

\mathbb{E}[\|Z_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=d\,\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq\frac{C^{2}}{B}+\frac{d\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}=v_{C}^{2}.

∎

B.5
Normalized momentum tracking

The raw momentum
M_{t}
does not track
g_{t}
sharply enough because it is not
startup-normalized. We therefore use the normalized momentum

s_{t}:=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^{k}=\frac{1-\beta^{t}}{1-\beta},\qquad\bar{M}_{t}:=\frac{M_{t}}{s_{t}}.

Since
s_{t}>0
, the polar factor is scale-invariant:

\mathrm{Polar}(M_{t})=\mathrm{Polar}(\bar{M}_{t}).

Lemma B.4

(Muon inner-product bound with normalized momentum)
.

Suppose the Newton–Schulz quality bounds in
(
3
) hold. Then

\langle g_{t},O_{t}\rangle_{F}\geq(1-\varepsilon_{q})\|g_{t}\|_{*}-(2-\varepsilon_{q})\|\bar{M}_{t}-g_{t}\|_{*}.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Polar}(\bar{M}_{t})=\mathrm{Polar}(M_{t})
, the bounds in
(
3
) imply

\displaystyle\langle\bar{M}_{t},O_{t}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{M}_{t},\mathrm{Polar}(\bar{M}_{t})\rangle_{F}+\langle\bar{M}_{t},O_{t}-\mathrm{Polar}(M_{t})\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle\geq\|\bar{M}_{t}\|_{*}-\|\bar{M}_{t}\|_{*}\|O_{t}-\mathrm{Polar}(M_{t})\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon_{q})\|\bar{M}_{t}\|_{*}.

Also,

\langle g_{t}-\bar{M}_{t},O_{t}\rangle_{F}\geq-\|g_{t}-\bar{M}_{t}\|_{*}\|O_{t}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq-\|g_{t}-\bar{M}_{t}\|_{*}.

Therefore

\langle g_{t},O_{t}\rangle_{F}\geq(1-\varepsilon_{q})\|\bar{M}_{t}\|_{*}-\|g_{t}-\bar{M}_{t}\|_{*}.

Using

\|\bar{M}_{t}\|_{*}\geq\|g_{t}\|_{*}-\|\bar{M}_{t}-g_{t}\|_{*}

gives the claim.
∎

Define the exponential weights

w_{t,j}:=\frac{\beta^{t-j}}{s_{t}},\qquad 1\leq j\leq t,

so that
\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}=1
and

\bar{M}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}\widetilde{G}_{j}.

Also define

\Gamma_{T}(\beta):=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(6)

Lemma B.5

(Normalized momentum tracking)
.

Under Assumptions
B.1
and

B.2
, and under the Newton–Schulz quality bounds
(
3
),

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|\bar{M}_{t}-g_{t}\|_{*}\leq\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C)+\sqrt{r}\,v_{C}\Gamma_{T}(\beta)+\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}.

(7)

Moreover, there exist universal constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\Gamma_{T}(\beta)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{T\sqrt{1-\beta}}+c_{2}\sqrt{1-\beta}.

(8)

Proof.

Using
M_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\beta^{t-j}\widetilde{G}_{j}
, we have

\bar{M}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}\widetilde{G}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}(g_{j}+b_{j}+\xi_{j}).

Thus

\bar{M}_{t}-g_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}b_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}\xi_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}(g_{j}-g_{t}).

For the bias part,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}b_{j}\right\|_{*}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}\mathbb{E}\|b_{j}\|_{*}\leq\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C).

For the stochastic part,
\{\xi_{j},\mathcal{F}_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is a martingale
difference sequence by Lemma
B.3
. Hence, for

i<j
,

\mathbb{E}\langle\xi_{i},\xi_{j}\rangle_{F}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\langle\xi_{i},\mathbb{E}[\xi_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{j-1}]\right\rangle_{F}\right]=0,

because
\xi_{i}
is
\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
-measurable. Therefore

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\xi_{j}\|_{F}^{2}\leq v_{C}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}^{2}.

Using
\|A\|_{*}\leq\sqrt{r}\|A\|_{F}
and Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}\xi_{j}\right\|_{*}\leq\sqrt{r}\,v_{C}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

For the drift part, Assumption
B.1
and

\|O_{s}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
from (

) imply

\|g_{j}-g_{t}\|_{*}\leq L\|W_{j-1}-W_{t-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L\eta(t-j).

Therefore

\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}\|g_{j}-g_{t}\|_{*}\leq L\eta\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}(t-j).

Writing
k=t-j
,

\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}(t-j)=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}k\beta^{k}}{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^{k}}\leq\frac{\beta}{1-\beta},

where the last inequality follows from

\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}k\beta^{k}=\sum_{m=1}^{t-1}\sum_{k=m}^{t-1}\beta^{k}\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta}\sum_{m=1}^{t-1}\beta^{m}\leq\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^{k}.

Combining the three bounds and averaging over
t
yields
(
7
).

It remains to bound
\Gamma_{T}(\beta)
. A direct calculation gives

\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}^{2}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^{2k}}{\left(\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^{k}\right)^{2}}=\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\cdot\frac{1+\beta^{t}}{1-\beta^{t}}.

Let
t_{\beta}:=\lceil(1-\beta)^{-1}\rceil

t\leq t_{\beta}
, there is a

such that

1-\beta^{t}\geq c\,t(1-\beta),

and hence

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\sqrt{t}}

for a universal constant
C^{\prime}>0
. For
t>t_{\beta}
, there is a universal
constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{t}w_{t,j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\sqrt{1-\beta}.

Averaging the two regimes gives

\Gamma_{T}(\beta)\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{t_{\beta}}t^{-1/2}+C^{\prime\prime}\sqrt{1-\beta}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{T\sqrt{1-\beta}}+c_{2}\sqrt{1-\beta}.

∎

B.6
Proof of the finite-horizon theorem

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

By Assumption
B.1
and the update

W_{t}=W_{t-1}-\eta O_{t}
,

f(W_{t})\leq f(W_{t-1})-\eta\langle g_{t},O_{t}\rangle_{F}+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}\|O_{t}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\leq f(W_{t-1})-\eta\langle g_{t},O_{t}\rangle_{F}+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2},

where the last inequality uses
\|O_{t}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
from
(
3
). Applying
Lemma
B.4
,

f(W_{t})\leq f(W_{t-1})-\eta(1-\varepsilon_{q})\|g_{t}\|_{*}+\eta(2-\varepsilon_{q})\|\bar{M}_{t}-g_{t}\|_{*}+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{2}.

Taking expectation and summing from
t=1
to
T
,

\eta(1-\varepsilon_{q})\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|g_{t}\|_{*}\leq D_{0}+\eta(2-\varepsilon_{q})\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|\bar{M}_{t}-g_{t}\|_{*}+\frac{L\eta^{2}T}{2}.

Dividing by
\eta T(1-\varepsilon_{q})
and invoking
Lemma
B.5
gives

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|g_{t}\|_{*}\leq\chi_{q}\left[\frac{D_{0}}{\eta T}+\frac{L\eta}{2}+(2-\varepsilon_{q})\left(\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C)+\sqrt{r}\,v_{C}\Gamma_{T}(\beta)+\frac{L\eta\beta}{1-\beta}\right)\right],

which proves the claim.
∎

B.7
Vanishing-rate corollaries

We now specialize Theorem
3.4
to two concrete
regimes. In the following
O(\cdot)
statements,
B,d,r,\sigma,L,D_{0}
and
the Newton–Schulz factor
\chi_{q}
are treated as horizon-independent unless
their dependence on
T
is explicitly displayed. Equivalently, if one keeps
the horizon dependence of
\chi_{q}
, the rates below hold with
\chi_{q}

replaced by the corresponding
\chi_{q,T}
.

Corollary B.6

(Vanishing bound under a uniform second-moment condition)
.

Assume in addition that there exists
G_{2}>0
such that

\sup_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}^{2}\leq G_{2}^{2}.

(9)

Choose the horizon-dependent parameters

\eta_{T}=\eta_{0}T^{-5/6},\qquad\beta_{T}=1-T^{-2/3},\qquad C_{T}=C_{0}T^{1/6},

(10)

where
\eta_{0},C_{0}>0
are constants independent of
T
. Then

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|g_{t}\|_{*}\leq\chi_{q}\cdot O(T^{-1/6}).

(11)

In particular, if
\chi_{q}
is bounded uniformly in
T
, then

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|g_{t}\|_{*}\to 0\qquad\text{as }T\to\infty.

Proof.

For any nonnegative
x
,
(x-C)_{+}\leq x^{2}/C
. Hence
(
9
) implies

\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C)\leq\frac{\sqrt{r}\,G_{2}^{2}}{C}.

With
C=C_{T}
, this gives

\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C_{T})=O(T^{-1/6}).

Next, by Lemma
B.5
,

\Gamma_{T}(\beta_{T})\leq\frac{c_{1}}{T\sqrt{1-\beta_{T}}}+c_{2}\sqrt{1-\beta_{T}}=O(T^{-1/3}).

Therefore

v_{C_{T}}\Gamma_{T}(\beta_{T})=C_{T}\sqrt{\frac{1}{B}+\frac{d\sigma^{2}}{B^{2}}}\,\Gamma_{T}(\beta_{T})=O(T^{1/6})\cdot O(T^{-1/3})=O(T^{-1/6}).

Also,

\frac{D_{0}}{\eta_{T}T}=O(T^{-1/6}),\qquad\eta_{T}=O(T^{-5/6}),\qquad\frac{\eta_{T}\beta_{T}}{1-\beta_{T}}=O(T^{-1/6}).

Substituting these estimates into Theorem
3.4

proves (
11
).
∎

Corollary B.7

(Improved vanishing rate under a light-tail condition)
.

Assume in addition that there exists
K>0
such that

\sup_{1\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\exp\!\left(\frac{\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}^{2}}{K^{2}}\right)\leq e.

(12)

For
T\geq 2
, choose

\eta_{T}=\eta_{0}T^{-3/4},\qquad\beta_{T}=1-T^{-1/2},\qquad C_{T}=K\sqrt{2\log T}.

(13)

Then

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|g_{t}\|_{*}\leq\chi_{q}\cdot O\!\left(T^{-1/4}\sqrt{\log T}\right).

(14)

In particular, if
\chi_{q}
is bounded uniformly in
T
, then

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\|g_{t}\|_{*}\to 0\qquad\text{as }T\to\infty.

Proof.

By Markov’s inequality, (
12
) implies

\mathbb{P}(\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}\geq u)\leq e\exp(-u^{2}/K^{2}),\qquad u\geq 0.

Hence

\mathbb{E}[(\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}-C)_{+}]=\int_{C}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\|G_{t}^{(1)}\|_{F}\geq u)\,du\leq e\int_{C}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}/K^{2}}\,du\leq\frac{eK^{2}}{2C}e^{-C^{2}/K^{2}}.

Therefore

\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C)\leq\frac{e\sqrt{r}\,K^{2}}{2C}e^{-C^{2}/K^{2}}.

With
C=C_{T}=K\sqrt{2\log T}
,

\mathfrak{B}_{T}(C_{T})\leq\frac{e\sqrt{r}\,K}{2\sqrt{2\log T}}T^{-2}=o(T^{-1/4}).

Next, by Lemma
B.5
,

\Gamma_{T}(\beta_{T})\leq\frac{c_{1}}{T\sqrt{1-\beta_{T}}}+c_{2}\sqrt{1-\beta_{T}}=O(T^{-1/4}).

Thus

v_{C_{T}}\Gamma_{T}(\beta_{T})=C_{T}\sqrt{\frac{1}{B}+\frac{d\sigma^{2}}{B^{2}}}\,\Gamma_{T}(\beta_{T})=O(\sqrt{\log T})\cdot O(T^{-1/4})=O(T^{-1/4}\sqrt{\log T}).

Finally,

\frac{D_{0}}{\eta_{T}T}=O(T^{-1/4}),\qquad\eta_{T}=O(T^{-3/4}),\qquad\frac{\eta_{T}\beta_{T}}{1-\beta_{T}}=O(T^{-1/4}).

Substituting these estimates into Theorem
3.4

yields (
14
).
∎

Appendix C
Details for DP-MuonBC

This appendix gives the technical details for DP-MuonBC, the output-level bias
correction method introduced in Section
4
. The key point is
that the following bias calculation is carried out in a frozen clipped-path
model. The privacy guarantee, however, is exact for the fully adaptive
algorithm because DP-MuonBC is a randomized post-processing of the same
privatized gradients used by DP-Muon.

C.1
Frozen clipped-path model

Fix one Muon-managed matrix parameter with shape
m\times n
, and write

d=mn
. Let

\bar{G}_{1},\ldots,\bar{G}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

be a fixed sequence of clipped-but-unnoised minibatch means. Equivalently, the
sequence may be random but independent of the Gaussian privacy noises introduced
below; in that case, all identities hold conditionally on the realized sequence

\bar{G}_{1:t}
.

The privatized gradient at step
s
is

\widetilde{G}_{s}=\bar{G}_{s}+Z_{s},\qquad Z_{s}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\nu^{2}I_{d}),\qquad\nu^{2}:=\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}},

where the covariance is understood after vectorizing matrices. The matrices

Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots
are independent.

Let
0\leq\mu<1
be the momentum parameter. Define the clipped-but-unnoised
and private momentum buffers by

H_{s}=\mu H_{s-1}+\bar{G}_{s},\qquad H_{0}=0,

and

M_{s}=\mu M_{s-1}+\widetilde{G}_{s},\qquad M_{0}=0.

For the usual momentum startup normalization, set

s_{t}:=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{j}=\frac{1-\mu^{t}}{1-\mu},\qquad\widehat{H}_{t}:=\frac{H_{t}}{s_{t}},\qquad\widehat{M}_{t}:=\frac{M_{t}}{s_{t}}.

Throughout this appendix,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}[\cdot]

denotes expectation in the frozen clipped-path model, conditional on the fixed
clipped path
\bar{G}_{1:t}
. More precisely,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}

averages over the Gaussian privacy noises
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{t}
, and, for
quantities involving the DP-MuonBC probes, it also averages over the independent
probe variables
U_{t,1},\ldots,U_{t,J}
.

Lemma C.1

(Fixed-path Gaussian identity)
.

Under the frozen clipped-path model,

\widehat{M}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\widehat{H}_{t}+\rho_{t}E_{t},\qquad E_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}),

where

\rho_{t}^{2}=\nu^{2}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{2j}}{\left(\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{j}\right)^{2}}=\nu^{2}\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}\frac{1+\mu^{t}}{1-\mu^{t}}.

Equivalently,

\rho_{t}^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}\frac{1+\mu^{t}}{1-\mu^{t}}.

In particular,

\rho_{\infty}^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}C^{2}}{B^{2}}\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}.

Proof.

By subtracting the two momentum recursions,

M_{t}-H_{t}=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mu^{t-s}Z_{s}.

Since the
Z_{s}
’s are independent centered Gaussian matrices,

M_{t}-H_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\,\nu^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{2j}I_{d}\right).

Dividing by
s_{t}
gives

\widehat{M}_{t}-\widehat{H}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\rho_{t}^{2}I_{d}),\qquad\rho_{t}^{2}=\nu^{2}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{2j}}{\left(\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{j}\right)^{2}}.

Using

\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{2j}=\frac{1-\mu^{2t}}{1-\mu^{2}},\qquad\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\mu^{j}=\frac{1-\mu^{t}}{1-\mu},

yields the stated formula.
∎

C.2
The Newton–Schulz output map

We analyze the exact real-arithmetic analogue of the
q
-step Newton–Schulz
routine used in DP-Muon, omitting mixed-precision casts. Define the
shape-dependent transpose operator

\mathcal{T}(G):=\begin{cases}G^{\top},&m>n,\\
G,&m\leq n.\end{cases}

Let
p_{\kappa}
be the fixed degree-
\kappa
Newton–Schulz polynomial used
by the optimizer. For
G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, initialize

X_{0}=\frac{\mathcal{T}(G)}{\alpha(G)},\qquad\alpha(G):=\max\{1,\|\mathcal{T}(G)\|_{F}\}.

This is the same no-
\varepsilon
startup normalization used in the idealized
real-arithmetic analysis. For
j=0,1,\ldots,q-1
, iterate

X_{j+1}=p_{\kappa}(X_{j}X_{j}^{\top})X_{j}.

Define the
q
-step Newton–Schulz output map

Q_{\kappa,q}(G):=\begin{cases}X_{q}^{\top},&m>n,\\
X_{q},&m\leq n.\end{cases}

The map
Q_{\kappa,q}
is deterministic and nonlinear. The Newton–Schulz
recursion is polynomial in the normalized initializer
X_{0}
, but the full map

G\mapsto Q_{\kappa,q}(G)
is not globally polynomial because of the
normalization

\alpha(G)=\max\{1,\|\mathcal{T}(G)\|_{F}\}.

The map is smooth on each of the open regions

\|\mathcal{T}(G)\|_{F}<1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\mathcal{T}(G)\|_{F}>1,

but it may fail to be
C^{2}
on the switching boundary

\mathcal{S}:=\{G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}:\|\mathcal{T}(G)\|_{F}=1\}.

The point
G=0
is not singular for this no-
\varepsilon
max-normalized
map, since
\alpha(G)=1
in a neighborhood of the origin. Nevertheless, in the
heat expansions below we impose a local smoothness condition that keeps the
expansion point away from the nonsmooth switching boundary; we also state the
condition in a form that covers alternative implementations whose normalization
may have a singularity at the origin.

For every fixed
p_{\kappa}
and finite
q
, the map
Q_{\kappa,q}
is
uniformly bounded. Indeed,
\|X_{0}\|_{F}\leq 1
, and the
q
Newton–Schulz
steps are a finite polynomial recursion in
X_{0}
on the compact Frobenius
unit ball. Hence there exists a finite constant

K_{Q}=K_{Q}(p_{\kappa},q,m,n)

such that

\sup_{G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}\|Q_{\kappa,q}(G)\|_{F}\leq K_{Q}.

C.3
Local heat-expansion condition

For a matrix-valued map
F:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
let

\Delta:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial X_{ij}^{2}}

denote the entrywise Laplacian, applied componentwise.

Assumption C.2

(Local heat regularity at
\widehat{H}_{t}
)
.

For the expansion point
\widehat{H}_{t}
, there exist constants

r_{0}>0
and
M_{\ell}<\infty
such that:

1.

\widehat{H}_{t}\neq 0
;

2.

the closed ball

\overline{B}(\widehat{H}_{t},r_{0}):=\{X:\|X-\widehat{H}_{t}\|_{F}\leq r_{0}\}

does not intersect the switching boundary
\mathcal{S}
;

3.

if the implementation being analyzed has a normalization singularity at
the origin, then the same ball also does not intersect the origin;

4.

all derivatives of
Q_{\kappa,q}
up to the order required in the
corresponding expansion are bounded by
M_{\ell}
on

\overline{B}(\widehat{H}_{t},r_{0})
.

Under Assumption
C.2
, the Gaussian tail outside
the smooth ball is negligible at every polynomial order as
\rho\to 0
. Indeed,
for
U\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
,

\mathbb{P}(\rho\|U\|_{F}>r_{0})\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{cr_{0}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)

for a constant
c>0
and all sufficiently small
\rho
. Since

Q_{\kappa,q}
is uniformly bounded, the contribution of the event

\{\rho\|U\|_{F}>r_{0}\}
is exponentially small and hence is
O(\rho^{k})
for
every fixed
k
. Therefore the heat expansion may be proved by Taylor
expanding
Q_{\kappa,q}
inside the smooth ball and controlling the Gaussian
tail by the uniform bound above.

For a matrix-valued function
F
, define the Gaussian heat operator

(P_{\rho}F)(X):=\mathbb{E}_{U\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})}\bigl[F(X+\rho U)\bigr].

Here
P_{\rho}
acts componentwise on matrix-valued maps. Under the local heat
regularity condition with derivatives bounded up to order
4
,

(P_{\rho}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t})=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\Delta Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho^{4}).

Under the same condition with derivatives bounded up to order
6
,

(P_{\rho}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t})=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\Delta Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+\frac{\rho^{4}}{8}\Delta^{2}Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho^{6}).

C.4
Fixed-path output bias

Let

O_{t}^{(0)}:=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t})

be the normalized DP-Muon direction. By Lemma
C.1
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}\!\left[O_{t}^{(0)}\right]=(P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t}),

where this expectation is over the privacy noises, with the clipped path fixed.
Thus the fixed-path Newton–Schulz output bias is

\mathcal{B}_{t,\mathrm{fp}}^{\mathrm{NS}}:=(P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t})-Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t}).

Under Assumption
C.2
with derivatives bounded
up to order
4
, as
\rho_{t}\to 0
,

(P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t})=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+\frac{\rho_{t}^{2}}{2}\Delta Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho_{t}^{4}).

Consequently,

\mathcal{B}_{t,\mathrm{fp}}^{\mathrm{NS}}=\frac{\rho_{t}^{2}}{2}\Delta Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho_{t}^{4}).

Thus, in the frozen clipped-path model, the leading privacy-induced bias is a
matrix-valued output-level curvature term. It is not a scalar additive bias in
the momentum buffer.

C.5
Definition and proof of DP-MuonBC

DP-MuonBC estimates and cancels the leading heat-smoothing term by an additional
randomized post-processing step. Let

U_{t,1},\ldots,U_{t,J}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})

be data-independent Gaussian probe matrices, independent of the DP noise.
Define

O_{t}^{(2)}:=\frac{1}{2J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left[Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t}+\rho_{t}U_{t,j})+Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t}-\rho_{t}U_{t,j})\right].

The bias-corrected direction is

O_{t}^{\mathrm{bc}}:=2O_{t}^{(0)}-O_{t}^{(2)}.

The corresponding parameter update is

W_{t}=W_{t-1}-\eta_{t}O_{t}^{\mathrm{bc}}.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Conditionally on
\widehat{M}_{t}
, the antithetic probe average satisfies

\mathbb{E}\!\left[O_{t}^{(2)}\,\middle|\,\widehat{M}_{t}\right]=(P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{M}_{t}),

where this conditional expectation is over the probe variables only. By
Lemma
C.1
,

\widehat{M}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\widehat{H}_{t}+\rho_{t}E_{t},\qquad E_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}),

and the probe noise is independent of
E_{t}
. Therefore the heat semigroup
identity gives

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}\!\left[O_{t}^{(2)}\right]=(P_{\rho_{t}}P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t})=(P_{\sqrt{2}\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t}).

Similarly,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}\!\left[O_{t}^{(0)}\right]=(P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\widehat{H}_{t}).

Combining the last two identities yields

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}\!\left[O_{t}^{\mathrm{bc}}\right]=\bigl(2P_{\rho_{t}}-P_{\sqrt{2}\rho_{t}}\bigr)Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t}).

For the expansion, use the local heat expansion justified by
Assumption
C.2
and the Gaussian tail argument
above.
For any sufficiently smooth matrix-valued map
F
,

P_{\rho}F=F+\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\Delta F+\frac{\rho^{4}}{8}\Delta^{2}F+O(\rho^{6}).

Applying this expansion with
\rho=\rho_{t}
and with

\rho=\sqrt{2}\rho_{t}
gives

P_{\rho_{t}}F=F+\frac{\rho_{t}^{2}}{2}\Delta F+\frac{\rho_{t}^{4}}{8}\Delta^{2}F+O(\rho_{t}^{6}),

and

P_{\sqrt{2}\rho_{t}}F=F+\rho_{t}^{2}\Delta F+\frac{\rho_{t}^{4}}{2}\Delta^{2}F+O(\rho_{t}^{6}).

Therefore

(2P_{\rho_{t}}-P_{\sqrt{2}\rho_{t}})F=F-\frac{\rho_{t}^{4}}{4}\Delta^{2}F+O(\rho_{t}^{6}).

Applying this identity to
F=Q_{\kappa,q}
at
X=\widehat{H}_{t}
gives

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}\!\left[O_{t}^{\mathrm{bc}}\right]=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})-\frac{\rho_{t}^{4}}{4}\Delta^{2}Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho_{t}^{6}).

In particular,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}\!\left[O_{t}^{\mathrm{bc}}\right]=Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{H}_{t})+O(\rho_{t}^{4}),

so the leading
O(\rho_{t}^{2})
output-level bias is canceled.
∎

C.6
Finite-probe Monte Carlo error

The finite-
J
estimator is unbiased for the probe expectation used in
Theorem
4.1
. Define

\zeta_{t}:=O_{t}^{(2)}-\mathbb{E}\!\left[O_{t}^{(2)}\,\middle|\,\widehat{M}_{t}\right],

where the conditional expectation is over the probe variables only. Then

\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{t}\mid\widehat{M}_{t}]=0.

Let

Y_{j}:=\frac{1}{2}\left[Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t}+\rho_{t}U_{t,j})+Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t}-\rho_{t}U_{t,j})\right].

Then
O_{t}^{(2)}=J^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{J}Y_{j}
, the
Y_{j}
’s are conditionally
i.i.d. given
\widehat{M}_{t}
, and the uniform boundedness of

Q_{\kappa,q}
gives

\|Y_{j}\|_{F}\leq K_{Q}.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\zeta_{t}\|_{F}^{2}\,\middle|\,\widehat{M}_{t}\right]\leq\frac{K_{Q}^{2}}{J}.

Thus
J
controls only the Monte Carlo variance of the correction; it does not
change its expectation.

C.7
Stationary clipped-path interpretation

If the frozen clipped path is stationary over a time window, namely

\bar{G}_{s}\equiv\bar{G}\qquad\text{for }s=1,\ldots,t,

then

H_{t}=s_{t}\bar{G},\qquad\widehat{H}_{t}=\bar{G}.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}\!\left[Q_{\kappa,q}(\widehat{M}_{t})\right]=(P_{\rho_{t}}Q_{\kappa,q})(\bar{G})=Q_{\kappa,q}(\bar{G})+\frac{\rho_{t}^{2}}{2}\Delta Q_{\kappa,q}(\bar{G})+O(\rho_{t}^{4}),

whereas Theorem
4.1
gives

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{fp}}\!\left[O_{t}^{\mathrm{bc}}\right]=Q_{\kappa,q}(\bar{G})+O(\rho_{t}^{4}),

provided the corresponding local heat regularity condition holds at

\bar{G}
. Thus the target recovered by the fixed-path correction is the
Newton–Schulz direction computed from the clipped-but-unnoised gradient

\bar{G}
. It is not the direction computed from the raw unclipped gradient.

C.8
Privacy of DP-MuonBC

The privacy statement for DP-MuonBC does not rely on the frozen clipped-path
approximation. In the actual adaptive algorithm, DP-MuonBC uses the same
privatized gradients
\widetilde{G}_{t}
as DP-Muon. All subsequent operations,
including momentum normalization, evaluation of
Q_{\kappa,q}
, generation of
data-independent Gaussian probes, formation of
O_{t}^{\mathrm{bc}}
, and the
parameter update, are randomized post-processing of already privatized
quantities using data-independent randomness.

Therefore, by the post-processing property of differential privacy, DP-MuonBC
satisfies exactly the same privacy guarantee as DP-Muon for any number of
probes
J
.

Data preprocessing and evaluation.

For E2E, each example is serialized as

\texttt{source}~\texttt{<bos>}~\texttt{target}~\texttt{<eos>},

and the source prefix is masked out of the language-modeling loss. The maximum
sequence length is 100. The raw E2E splits contain 42,061 training examples,
4,672 validation examples, and 4,693 test examples; after filtering, the
training set used for accounting contains
N=42{,}043
examples.

For DART, triples are serialized as

\texttt{subject : relation : object}~|~\cdots~\texttt{<bos>}~\texttt{text}~\texttt{<eos>},

with relation strings lower-cased. The maximum sequence length is 120. The raw
DART splits contain 30,526 training examples, 2,768 development examples, and
6,959 test examples. After the filtering and expansion used by the training
script, the training set used for accounting contains
N=60{,}591
examples.

For generation, we use deterministic beam search with beam size 5,

do_sample=False
, length penalty 1.0,

\texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=100
,
\texttt{min\_new\_tokens}=5
, and early
stopping. Newline tokens are blocked using the bad-words mechanism. BLEU is
computed with
sacrebleu
using
effective_order=True
.
ROUGE-L is computed with the
rouge_score
implementation using
stemming; for multi-reference examples, we use the best ROUGE-L over
references. For E2E, generation checkpoints are selected by validation BLEU,
while NLL reports the best evaluation NLL. For DART, generation is evaluated
from the saved checkpoint nearest to each seed’s best-NLL step.

DP-MuonBC implementation.

DP-MuonBC uses one Gaussian probe,
J=1
, with antithetic evaluations at

A+\rho_{t}z
and
A-\rho_{t}z
. The probe scale
\rho_{t}
is computed
from the DP noise standard deviation after momentum and startup normalization.
Thus each corrected matrix update uses three Newton–Schulz evaluations: one
for the original noisy momentum and two for the antithetic probes. Since the
probes are data-independent and are applied after the privatized gradient
release, changing
J
or the probe distribution would affect optimization and
computation but not the privacy accountant.
