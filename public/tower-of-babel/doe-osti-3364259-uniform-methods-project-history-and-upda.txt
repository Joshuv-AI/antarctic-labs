 What is the UMP?
• The UMP develops and maintains model protocols for determining gross
  energy savings that result from energy efficiency measures and
  programs implemented through rate payer-funded state and utility
  energy efficiency programs.
• As technology has changed over the last several years, the UMP is
  starting a maintenance phase to include demand savings and address
  new measurement and verification (M&V) approaches.

UMP: Background

 Why Is This Project Needed?
• Seventeen technical reference manuals had been identified, covering 21
  states and Washington, D.C. (as of spring 2012).
• Different methods were used to calculate savings for the same measures.
• Savings estimates for the same measures varied widely, with no clear
  explanation of the source.
• Widespread use of the UMP protocols could provide consistency across
  technical reference manuals.

 Project Goals and Benefits
• Create greater consistency of savings calculations
 ◦ Quickly establish good measurement and verification practices
 ◦ Facilitate meaningful comparisons
• Provide transparency, thereby reducing uncertainty
• Support development of best practices for energy efficiency
 ◦ Set data requirements early on
 ◦ Provide confidence when setting and meeting savings targets
• Provide educational value to the broad stakeholder community
 ◦ Identify key inputs
 ◦ Document methods and calculations
 ◦ Educate those new to evaluation, measurement and verification (EM&V).

 What is “Peak” Demand?
System-Level Peak (Line Side)
• Absolute System Peaks = when system load stresses generation, transmission, or
  imports
• Local System Peaks = when particular areas or feeders may be congested
• “Typical” Peak Periods = the times, seasons, or conditions when peaks most
  often occur
Building / Measure-Level Peak (Load Side)
• Building Peak = when the aggregate system loads of a building are at a maximum
• Equipment Peak = when an individual end-use is at its maximum

Building / Measure Peaks and System Peaks don’t necessarily coincide
