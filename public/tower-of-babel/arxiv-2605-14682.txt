2.1.
The classical Catalan triangle

Definition 2.1

(Catalan triangle)
.

The
Catalan triangle

(C_{n,k})_{0\leq k\leq n}
is
the family of integers defined by the recurrence

(3)

C_{0,0}=1,\qquad C_{n,0}=1\ (n\geq 1),\qquad C_{n,k}=C_{n,k-1}+C_{n-1,k}\quad(1\leq k\leq n),

with the convention
C_{n,k}=0
for
k>n
.
In particular,
C_{n,n}=C_{n}
(Catalan number).

The first values are:

\begin{array}[]{c|ccccccc}n\,\backslash\,k&0&1&2&3&4&5&6\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
1&1&1\\
2&1&2&2\\
3&1&3&5&5\\

6&1&6&20&48&90&132&132\end{array}

2.2.
The
q
-Catalan triangle

Definition 2.2

(
q
-Catalan triangle)
.

The
q
-Catalan triangle
is the family of polynomials

(C_{n,k}(q))_{0\leq k\leq n}
, with positive integer coefficients, defined by:

(4)

\begin{cases}C_{0,0}(q)=1,\\[4.0pt]

with the convention
C_{n,k}(q)=0
for

.

For

,

3.1.
The
q
-Catalan triangle via the inversion statistic

Theorem 3.3

(
q
-Catalan triangle via
312
-avoiding permutations)
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n
:

(6)

C_{n,k}(q)=\sum_{\pi\in S^{\prime}_{n,k}(312)}q^{\mathrm{inv}(\pi)}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
, using the partition

S^{\prime}_{n,k}(312)=S^{\prime}_{n,k-1}(312)\sqcup S_{n,k}(312)
.

Base case.

The set
S^{\prime}_{n,0}(312)=S_{n,0}(312)
consists of the unique
decreasing permutation
n(n{-}1)\cdots 1
, which has
\mathrm{inv}=\binom{n}{2}
;
hence
\sum_{\pi\in S^{\prime}_{n,0}(312)}q^{\mathrm{inv}(\pi)}=q^{\binom{n}{2}}=C_{n,0}(q)
.

Induction step.

Define the insertion map

\Phi:S^{\prime}_{n-1,k}(312)\to S_{n,k}(312),\quad\Phi(\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{n-1})=\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{k}\cdot n\cdot\pi_{k+1}\cdots\pi_{n-1}.

The map
\Phi
is a bijection:
n
preserves
312
-avoidance and fixes
the determining position at
k
.
Since
n
is larger than all other elements, its insertion at position
k+1

creates exactly
n-k-1
new inversions.
Therefore
\mathrm{inv}(\Phi(\pi))=\mathrm{inv}(\pi)+(n-k-1)
for all
\pi\in S^{\prime}_{n-1,k}(312)
.
By the induction hypothesis,

\sum_{\pi\in S_{n,k}(312)}q^{\mathrm{inv}(\pi)}=q^{n-k-1}\sum_{\pi\in S^{\prime}_{n-1,k}(312)}q^{\mathrm{inv}(\pi)}=q^{n-k-1}\,C_{n-1,k}(q).

The partition gives

\sum_{S^{\prime}_{n,k}(312)}q^{\mathrm{inv}}=C_{n,k-1}(q)+q^{n-k-1}C_{n-1,k}(q)=C_{n,k}(q)
,
which completes the induction.
∎

Theorem 3.7

(Shifted statistic)
.

For all

(i)

\mathrm{inv}(\pi)\geq n-k-1
for all

(ii)

Setting
\mathrm{inv}^{*}(\pi)=\mathrm{inv}(\pi)-(n-k-1)

3.3.
The mirror polynomial

Definition 3.8

(Mirror polynomial)
.

The
mirror polynomial
is
(\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(q))_{0\leq k\leq n}
defined by

(8)

\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(q):=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\,C_{n,k}(q^{-1}).

Theorem 3.9

(Mirror recurrence)
.

The mirror polynomial satisfies:

(9)

\widetilde{C}_{n,0}(q)=1,\qquad\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n,k-1}(q)+q^{k}\,\widetilde{C}_{n-1,k}(q),\quad 1\leq k\leq n,

and the specialization
\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(1)=C_{n,k}
.

Proof.

Initial condition.

\widetilde{C}_{n,0}(q)=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\,C_{n,0}(q^{-1})=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\cdot q^{-\binom{n}{2}}=1
.

Recurrence.

Substituting
q\mapsto q^{-1}
in (
4
)
and multiplying by
q^{\binom{n}{2}}
:

\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n,k-1}(q)+q^{\binom{n}{2}-(n-k-1)}\cdot q^{-\binom{n-1}{2}}\,\widetilde{C}_{n-1,k}(q).

The exponent simplifies:

\binom{n}{2}-(n-k-1)-\binom{n-1}{2}=\tfrac{n(n-1)-(n-1)(n-2)}{2}-(n-k-1)=(n-1)-(n-k-1)=k,

which establishes (
9
).
The specialization
\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(1)=C_{n,k}
follows from the definition
and Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

The exponents
n-k-1
and
k
are complementary with respect to
n-1
:
recurrence (
9
) is dual to (
4
).

Corollary 3.11

(Combinatorial interpretations of the mirror polynomial)
.

For all
n\geq 1
and

:

(10)

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in S^{\prime}_{n,k}(312)}q^{\mathrm{coinv}(\pi)},

(11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(q)

Proof.

Identity (

) follows from Theorem
3.3

and
\mathrm{coinv}(\pi)=\binom{n}{2}-\mathrm{inv}(\pi)

\widetilde{C}_{n,k}(q)=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{\pi\in S^{\prime}_{n,k}(312)}q^{-\mathrm{inv}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\in S^{\prime}_{n,k}(312)}q^{\mathrm{coinv}(\pi)}.

Identity (

) follows from (

)
and the canonical bijection

under which

4.

4.1.
Generalized Dyck paths

Definition 4.1

(Generalized Dyck paths and area statistic)
.

For
0\leq k\leq n
,
D^{\prime}_{n,k}
denotes the set of lattice paths with East steps

E=(1,0)
and North steps
N=(0,1)
from
(0,0)
to
(n,k)

never going strictly above the diagonal
y=x
.
The
area
of
\gamma\in D^{\prime}_{n,k}
is:

\mathrm{area}(\gamma)=\bigl|\bigl\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\;\big|\;y<x,\;\text{cell with SW corner }(x,y)\text{ lies below }\gamma\bigr\}\bigr|.

Theorem 4.2

(
q
-Catalan triangle via Dyck paths)
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n
:

(12)

C_{n,k}(q)=\sum_{\gamma\in D^{\prime}_{n,k}}q^{\,\binom{n}{2}-\mathrm{area}(\gamma)}.

Proof in
[
14
]
; verified for
n\leq 5
.

Example 4.3
.

For
n=3
,
k=2
:
|D^{\prime}_{n,k}|=5
paths and
\binom{3}{2}=3
.

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c|c}\text{Path }\gamma&\text{Word}&\mathrm{area}(\gamma)&\binom{3}{2}-\mathrm{area}(\gamma)\\
\hline\cr\gamma_{1}&EEENN&0&3\\
\gamma_{2}&EENEN&1&2\\
\gamma_{3}&EENNE&2&1\\
\gamma_{4}&ENEEN&2&1\\
\gamma_{5}&ENENE&3&0\end{array}

We obtain
\sum_{\gamma}q^{3-\mathrm{area}(\gamma)}=1+2q+q^{2}+q^{3}=C_{3,2}(q)
.✓

4.2.
Binary words

Each path
\gamma\in D^{\prime}_{n,k}
is encoded by

w(\gamma)=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n+k})\in\{0,1\}^{n+k}

(
w_{i}=0
for East,
w_{i}=1
for North). We write

Definition 4.4

(Inversions of a binary word)

\mathrm{inv}(w)=|\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq n+k,\;w_{i}=1,\;w_{j}=0\}|
.

Lemma 4.5

(Area equals inversions)
.

\mathrm{area}(\gamma)=\mathrm{inv}(w(\gamma))
for any
\gamma\in D^{\prime}_{n,k}
.
The bijective correspondence is proved in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 4.6

(
q
-Catalan triangle via binary words)
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n
:

(13)

C_{n,k}(q)=\sum_{w\in M^{\prime}_{n,k}}q^{\,\binom{n}{2}-\mathrm{inv}(w)}.

This follows directly from Lemma
4.5
and Theorem
4.2
.

Each path
\gamma\in D^{\prime}_{n,k}

\mathcal{A}(\gamma)

the classical bijection
[

]

The statistic is
\mathrm{stat}(t)=\sum_{v}|\text{left subtree of }v|

(

For all
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n
:

(14)

C_{n,k}(q)=\sum_{t\in T^{\prime}_{n,k}}q^{\,\mathrm{stat}(t)}.

Bijective proof in
[
14
]
.

(Universal family)
.

The

is:

(15)

\mathfrak{F}_{n,k}=\Bigl(\bigsqcup_{\tau\in\{312,231,213,132\}}\widehat{S}^{\prime}_{n,k}(\tau)\Bigr)\sqcup D^{\prime}_{n,k}\sqcup M^{\prime}_{n,k}\sqcup T^{\prime}_{n,k},

where
\widehat{S}^{\prime}_{n,k}(\tau)=S^{\prime}_{n,k}(\tau)\times\{\tau\}

Corollary 4.9

(Universal family of the

-Catalan triangle)
.

The

\sigma:\mathfrak{F}_{n,k}\to\mathbb{N}
is:

\sigma(X)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{inv}(\pi)&\text{if }X\in\widehat{S}^{\prime}_{n,k}(312)\text{ or }\widehat{S}^{\prime}_{n,k}(231),\\

5.1.

Randrianarivony

5.2.
Definition and properties

Definition 5.1

(
q{,}p
-Catalan triangle)
.

The
q{,}p
-Catalan triangle

(C_{n,k}(q,p))_{0\leq k\leq n}
is defined by:

(18)

\begin{cases}C_{0,0}(q,p)=1,\\[4.0pt]
C_{n,0}(q,p)=q^{\binom{n}{2}},&n\geq 1,\\[4.0pt]
C_{n,k}(q,p)=C_{n,k-1}(q,p)+q^{n-k-1}\,p^{k}\,C_{n-1,k}(q,p),&1\leq k\leq n.\end{cases}

This is a two-index array with additive recurrence,
distinct from Randrianarivony’s single-index sequence
[
6
]
.

Remark 5.2

(Non-coincidence with Randrianarivony)
.

C_{2,2}(q,p)=p+q
, while
C_{2}(q,p)=q+1
from (
17
).
The two families are distinct as bivariate polynomials.
They coincide at
p=q=1
:
