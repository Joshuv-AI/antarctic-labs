Prospects of boosted magnetic dipole inelastic fermion dark matter at ILC-BDX
I. V. Voronchikhin, D. V. Kirpichnikov

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the projected sensitivity of the Beam-Dump eXperiment at the International Linear Collider (ILC-BDX) to inelastic fermionic dark matter coupled to the Standard Model photon through an off-diagonal magnetic dipole operator. We compute the production rate of dark matter states in the bremsstrahlung like process e − N → e − N γ ∗ ( → χ 1 χ ¯ 0 ) e^{-}N\to e^{-}N\gamma^{*}(\to\chi_{1}\bar{\chi}_{0}) , induced by the scattering of high-energy electrons on target nuclei. The resulting boosted dark matter fluxes are then propagated to the detector, where the signal events arise from scattering off detector electrons. The projected exclusion limits are derived using the expected numbers of electrons on target (this implies a typical rate of 4.0 × 10 21 / year 4.0~\times~10^{21}/\mbox{year} ) corresponding to 1 year and 10 years of data taking. To characterize the impact of inelasticity, we consider two benchmark relative mass splittings, Δ = 0.05 \Delta=0.05 and Δ = 0.001 \Delta=0.001 , motivated by thermal dark matter scenarios. Our results show that ILC-BDX can probe inelastic magnetic-dipole dark matter over a phenomenologically relevant region of parameter space.



I
Introduction

Observational evidence from cosmology and astrophysics strongly supports the existence of dark matter (DM), providing one of the clearest indications of physics beyond the Standard Model (SM)
Aghanim et al. 2020
. However, the specific particle nature of DM and the structure of its interactions with the visible sector remain unknown
Alemany Fernández et al. 2025
.

Thermalization of DM with the visible sector relates the present-day cosmological abundance to microscopic interactions in the early Universe
Gondolo and Gelmini 1991
. Considerable attention has focused on light thermal DM, for which a freeze-out mechanism is required to deplete the relic abundance to the observed value. Such a mechanism can be realized through a hidden mediator of various possible spins
Voronchikhin and Kirpichnikov 2026a
;
Wang 2025
;
Izaguirre et al. 2015
;
Betancur et al. 2026
;
Kang and Lee 2020
;
Voronchikhin and Kirpichnikov 2025
.
Instead of introducing a new light sub-GeV portal interaction, one may consider couplings of DM directly to the ordinary photon
Dienes et al. 2023
;
Jodłowski 2023
;
Kling et al. 2023
;
Chu et al. 2019
.

Specifically, we consider an inelastic dark matter (iDM) scenario with
two 4-component Majorana
states,
\chi_{0}
and
\chi_{1}
, described by the Lagrangian
Batell et al. 2021
;
Dreiner et al. 2010

\mathcal{L}^{\rm DM}_{\rm kin.}\supset\sum_{i=0,1}\left[\frac{1}{2}\overline{\chi}_{i}\,i\gamma^{\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}\chi_{i}-\frac{1}{2}m_{\chi_{i}}\,\overline{\chi}_{i}\chi_{i}\right]\,,

(1)

where
m_{\chi_{i}}
denotes the physical fermion masses, satisfying
m_{\chi_{0}}~<~m_{\chi_{1}}
. We introduce the relative mass splitting:

\Delta=(m_{\chi_{1}}-m_{\chi_{0}})/m_{\chi_{0}}\geq 0.

(2)

These states are coupled to the Standard Model photon via an off-diagonal dimension-five magnetic dipole operator.
Specifically, the effective Lagrangian includes the
term
Chang et al. 2010
;
Jodłowski 2023
;
Dienes et al. 2023

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{\Lambda_{\rm M}}\bar{\chi}_{1}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\chi_{0}F_{\mu\nu},

(3)

where the scale
\Lambda_{\rm M}
characterizes the strength of
this non-renormalizable interaction from new physics,

F_{\mu\nu}
denotes the electromagnetic field strength
tensor, and
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=i[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]/2

represents the commutator of the Dirac gamma matrices.

For DM masses below
\mathcal{O}(1)~\mathrm{GeV}
, accelerator-based
searches combine the benefits of sufficiently high beam energies and
relatively large primary-beam intensities (for a recent review, see
e. g. Refs.
Izaguirre et al. 2017
;
Mongillo et al. 2023
;
Alemany Fernández et al. 2025
;
Feng et al. 2023
;
Gninenko et al. 2026a
;
Cazzaniga et al. 2021
;
Voronchikhin and Kirpichnikov 2026b
and references therein).

The ILC-BDX is a proposed beam-
dump facility at the future energy-frontier linear
e^{+}e^{-}
-collider planned to be
constructed in the Kitakami area, Tohoku, Japan.
The ILC-BDX experiment is expected to have strong sensitivity to a light dark
sector
Zarnecki 2020
;
Asai et al. 2021
;
Nojiri et al. 2022
;
Asai et al. 2024
; however, its
sensitivity to inelastic magnetic dipole dark matter
Chang et al. 2010
;
Jodłowski 2023
;
Dienes et al. 2023
has not yet been investigated in
detail.
In this paper, we therefore focus on expected reach of the
inelastic magnetic dipole dark matter model through its scattering off
electrons
Batell et al. 2014
in the proposed ILC-BDX detector.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we outline the benchmark ILC-BDX experiment. In Sec.
III
we discuss the rate of
iDM pair production at ILC-BDX associated with bremsstrahlung-like emission. In Sec.
IV
, we derive the projected exclusion limits
of the ILC-BDX fixed target facility in the iDM parameter space.
We conclude in Sec.
V
.

II
The benchmark Experiment

Feynman diagrams illustrating the iDM processes of interest: (a) bremsstrahlung-like production of iDM via an off-shell photon emission

\chi_{i}e\to\chi_{j}e

We assume that the ILC-BDX experiment
operates with a primary beam of energy
250~\mathrm{GeV}

Fujii et al. 2017
;
Zarnecki 2020
or
125~\mathrm{GeV}

Asai et al. 2021
in the laboratory frame and an
intensity corresponding to
4.0~\times~10^{21}
electrons accumulated on target
per year
Asai et al. 2021
. Downstream of an
11
m-long water beam dump, there is a lead muon shield of length
L_{\rm sh.}~=~70~\mbox{m}
, followed by a decay volume of length
L_{\rm dec.}~=~50~\mbox{m}

Asai et al. 2024
. The tracking system in the decay volume can be used for
searches for visible dark matter decays. The decay volume is followed by a cylindrical
CsI(Tl) electromagnetic calorimeter with radius
2~\mathrm{m}
and length
0.64~\mathrm{m}
,
characterized by an electron number density
n_{\rm det}^{e}~=~1.1~\times~10^{24}~\mbox{cm}^{-3}
.

The sufficiently large shielding volume implies that the relevant background events are
primarily induced by the neutrino flux produced along the beamline and in the muon shield.
The irreducible background for this setup arises from elastic scattering of beam-
originated neutrinos off electrons in the calorimeter, which can initiate an
electromagnetic shower. For a threshold energy of
E_{\rm th}=1~\mathrm{GeV}
, this
contribution is estimated to be at the level of
\sim~\mathcal{O}(1)
event per year. In
particular, the corresponding upper limits on the number of signal events are
N_{\rm upper}~=~3.8
and
N_{\rm upper}~=~7.3
at
95\%
CL for statistics of 1 and 10 years,
respectively. The background from cosmic neutrinos and muons is expected to be
negligible
Asai et al. 2024
.

The dominant reducible background channels are neutrino-nucleon interactions and neutral-pion production induced by beam-originated neutrinos in the detector, which may mimic electron-recoil signals. This reducible background can potentially be suppressed through an analysis of the electromagnetic-shower profile.

As was pointed out above, the high beam energy and intensity
of this setup can provide a viable sensitivity to dark matter
models. We focus on a scenario of inelastic dipole dark
matter, in which a flux of
\chi_{i}
particles is produced
when the primary electrons strike the target. The resulting
boosted dark matter
Kim et al. 2017
;
Agashe et al. 2014
can
reach the detector and be probed through scattering in the
detector material
Batell et al. 2014
.

III
Signatures in the ILC experiment

In this section, we discuss signal events at ILC-BDX induced by scattering of dark matter particles off detector electrons.
In particular, scattering of the primary high-energy electron beam off target nuclei can produce dark matter via an off-shell Standard Model photon, while the emitted hidden-sector fermions carry away part of the beam energy.
The dark matter production process is
2\to 4
reaction
Chu et al. 2019
;
Dienes et al. 2023
depicted in Fig.
1
(a):

e^{-}N\to e^{-}N\gamma^{*}\to e^{-}N\bar{\chi}_{0}\chi_{1}.

(4)

We use the CalcHEP
Belyaev et al. 2013

Gninenko et al. 2026a

Gninenko et al. 2026b

Gninenko et al. 2026c

Arefyeva et al. 2022

we denote by
\sigma_{2\to 4}^{\rm cut}

2\to 4
process integrated over the dark matter energy
E_{\chi_{0}}
with the angular cut
\theta_{\chi}^{\rm max}\simeq 0.874^{\circ}
.
Similarly, we introduce the total cross section for the considered process by
\sigma_{2\to 4}^{\rm tot}

The corresponding dark-matter production cross sections,
\sigma_{2\to 4}^{\rm cut}
and
\sigma_{2\to 4}^{\rm tot}

We also provide their ratio
\sigma_{2\to 4}^{\rm cut}/\sigma_{2\to 4}^{\rm tot}

As can be seen, more than half of the dark matter particles are emitted toward the detector for small dark matter masses
\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})~\mbox{GeV}-\mathcal{O}(10^{-1})~\mbox{GeV}
. However, this acceptance drops rapidly to the level of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-1})
over the relevant mass range
\mathcal{O}(10^{-1})~\mbox{GeV}-\mathcal{O}(1)~\mbox{GeV}

For both primary beam energies, the total cross section ranges from
\mathcal{O}(10^{5})~\mbox{pb}
to
\mathcal{O}(10^{2})~\mbox{pb}

After the primary beam impinges on the target, a flux of boosted dark matter is produced.
Boosted dark matter can subsequently scatter off detector electrons as depicted in Fig.
1
(b):

\chi_{i}(p)+e(k)\to\chi_{j}(p^{\prime})+e(k^{\prime})

where
q=k^{\prime}-k
and
Q^{2}=-q^{2}
.
Assuming that the initial-state electron is at rest, the momentum transfer is given by
Q=\sqrt{2m_{e}E_{\rm R}}~\ll~m_{Z}
, where
E_{\rm R}=E_{e}^{\prime}-m_{e}
is the electron recoil energy.
For instance, retaining the leading terms in
Q,m_{\chi_{1}}~\ll~E_{\chi_{1}}
, the differential cross section of the
\chi_{0}e\to\chi_{1}e
process reads
Jodłowski 2023
:

\frac{d\sigma_{\chi_{0}e}}{dE_{R}}\simeq\frac{4\alpha}{\Lambda_{M}^{2}E_{\chi_{0}}^{2}}\left[\frac{E_{\chi_{0}}^{2}}{E_{R}}+\frac{m_{\chi_{0}}^{2}}{2m_{e}}-\Delta\frac{m_{\chi_{0}}^{2}E_{\chi_{0}}}{m_{e}E_{\rm R}}\right].

(5)

The decay length of
\chi_{1}
in the lab-frame can be expressed for sufficiently small splitting
\Delta\ll 1
, as

\displaystyle d_{\chi_{1}}\simeq\;

\displaystyle 1.91\times 10^{5}\;\text{m}

\displaystyle\times\!\left(\!\frac{E_{\chi_{1}}}{10^{2}\text{GeV}}\!\right)\!\!\left(\!\frac{0.1\text{GeV}}{m_{\chi_{0}}}\!\right)^{\!4}\!\!\!\left(\!\frac{0.001}{\Delta}\!\right)^{3}\!\!\!\left(\!\frac{\Lambda_{\rm M}}{10^{3}\text{GeV}}\!\right)^{2}\!\!,

(6)

The resulting dark matter flux of
\chi_{0}
leads to the probability of scattering inside the detector:

P_{\rm scat.}^{\chi_{0}}=n_{\rm det}l_{\rm det}\sigma^{\rm cut}_{2\to 2}(E_{\chi_{0}}),

(7)

where
\sigma^{\rm cut}_{2\to 2}
is obtained by integrating the differential cross section (
5
) over recoil energies above the threshold
E{\rm th}
.
Also, the heavier dark matter state can propagate to the detector and scatter with probability:

P_{\rm scat.}^{\chi_{1}}=n_{\rm det}l_{\rm det}\sigma^{\rm cut}_{2\to 2}(E_{\chi_{1}})\exp\left(\!-\frac{L_{\rm sh}+L_{\rm dec.}}{d_{\chi_{1}}}\!\right),

(8)

where the exponential factor takes into account the
fraction of survived
\chi_{1}
that reaches the
detector.

We define electron-recoil event in the experiment detector with the energy threshold
E_{\rm th}~=~1~\mathrm{GeV}
as:

\displaystyle N_{\rm det.}^{\chi_{i}}

\displaystyle\simeq\mbox{EOT}\times\frac{\rho N_{A}}{A}\times L_{T}

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{E_{\rm min}}^{E_{\rm max}}dE_{\chi_{i}}\int\limits_{0}^{\theta_{\rm max}}d\theta_{\chi_{i}}\frac{d\sigma_{2\to 4}}{dE_{\chi_{i}}d\theta_{\chi_{i}}}P_{\rm scat.}^{\chi_{i}}(E_{\chi_{i}}),

(9)

where we assume that the spectra of the mass eigenstates are identical.
Indeed, in high-energy fixed-target experiments, the effect of the mass splitting on the spectrum is small.
Thus, the number of signal events for inelastic magnetic dipole dark matter in the ILC-BDX experiment is given by

N_{\rm sign.}=N_{\rm det.}^{\chi_{0}}+N_{\rm det.}^{\chi_{1}}.

(10)

The mass splitting also makes the heavier dark matter state unstable, thereby opening the channel
\chi_{1}~\to~\chi_{0}~\gamma
.
However, the threshold requirement on the deposited energy in the detector system,
E_{\gamma}~>~E_{\rm th}
, does not provide any feasible signal,
\Delta~\gtrsim~E_{\rm th}/E_{\chi_{1}}
, and could not lead to new expected reach of ILC-BDX for
\Delta~<~\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})
(see e. g. Refs.
Dienes et al. 2023
and references therein for detail).

The dark matter flux traverses a
70
m lead shielding volume on its way to the detector.
For
1/\Lambda_{\rm M}~=~10^{-3}~\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
, the characteristic total cross section for dark matter scattering off electrons is given by
\sigma_{\chi e}~\propto~4\alpha/\Lambda_{\rm M}^{2}~\simeq~\mathcal{O}(10^{-35})~\mathrm{cm^{2}}
.
Accordingly, for the electron number density of lead,
n_{e}~\simeq~2.7~\times~10^{24}~\mathrm{cm^{-3}}
, the corresponding mean free path is of order
\lambda_{\chi}~=~(n_{e}\sigma_{\chi e})^{-1}~\simeq~\mathcal{O}(10^{8})~\mathrm{m}
.
Therefore, dark matter interactions inside the shielding volume are negligibly small, and changes in the flux during its passage through the shield can be safely neglected
Batell et al. 2014
.

We note that, the effective operator (
3
) provides a valid description of the interaction at energy scales below the electroweak symmetry-breaking scale
Arina et al. 2021
(that implies the typical new
\Lambda_{\rm M}\simeq\mathcal{O}(10^{2})~\mbox{GeV}
).
In particular, the momentum entering vertex
\gamma^{\ast}~\to~\chi_{0}\chi_{1}
is subject to the condition
Q_{2\to 4}^{2}~<~m_{Z}^{2}
. For the process under consideration, the corresponding momentum satisfies:

Q_{2\to 4}^{2}\leq(Q_{2\to 4}^{\max})^{2}=\left(\sqrt{s_{2\to 4}}-m_{e}-m_{N}\right)^{2},

where
s_{2\to 4}~=~m_{e}^{2}~+~m_{N}^{2}~+~2m_{N}E_{\rm beam}
in the lab frame. For an incident electron with energy
250~\mbox{GeV}
scattering off an oxygen nucleus, the relation
Q_{2\to 4}^{\max}~\simeq~72~\mbox{GeV}~<~m_{Z}\ll\Lambda_{\rm M}\simeq\mathcal{O}(10^{3})~\mbox{GeV}
holds
Izaguirre et al. 2016
.
Thus, for the experimental parameters under consideration, the effective operator remains valid
Jodłowski 2023
. For a discussion of the ultraviolet-completed iDM scenario, see Ref.
Izaguirre et al. 2016
and references therein.

IV
The experimental reach

In this section, we discuss the sensitivity of the ILC-BDX fixed-target experiment to the thermal inelastic magnetic dipole dark matter
model in the mass range

10~\mbox{MeV}\lesssim m_{\chi_{0}}\lesssim 1~\mbox{GeV}
.
The corresponding experimental reach are shown in the Fig.
3
for mass splittings as
\Delta=0.001
and
\Delta=0.05
in the left and right panels, respectively.
We focus on small mass splittings as
\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})\lesssim\Delta\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})
, which correspond to the viable region of parameter space of the considered model (
3
).
To derive the constraints, we use the
95\%
CL upper limits,
N_{\rm upper}=3.8
and
N_{\rm upper}=7.3
, for the 1-year and 10-year of data taking, respectively, as discussed in Sec.
II
.
To calculate the number of signal events, we use Eq. (
10
), where the dominant channel is dark-matter scattering off the detector material.

We consider primary beam energies of
E_{\rm beam}=250~\mbox{GeV}
and
E_{\rm beam}=125~\mbox{GeV}
as benchmark values. We find that the bound on the interaction coupling becomes stronger by factor of
\mathcal{O}(1)
for masses in the range from
10~\mbox{MeV}
to
1~\mbox{GeV}
when the primary beam energy is doubled.
At small masses, the weaker constraints for a primary beam energy of
E_{\rm beam}=125~\mbox{GeV}
arise from the logarithmic energy dependence of the total cross section.
At larger masses, lowering the primary beam energy from
E_{\rm beam}=250~\mbox{GeV}
to
E_{\rm beam}=125~\mbox{GeV}
likewise weakens the constraints because of the reduced acceptance, as seen in Tabs.
1
and
2
.
We conservatively use the
95\%
CL upper limits,
N_{\rm upper}=3.8
and
N_{\rm upper}=7.3
, for both primary beam energies under consideration.

The production mechanism for thermal freeze-out via magnetic dipole
inelastic dark matter is examined thoroughly in
Ref.
Jodłowski 2023
. Based on that work,
Fig.
3
displays the standard thermal
target lines for two representative mass splittings,

\Delta=0.001
and
\Delta=0.05
. We also display the relic-density
curve for
\Delta=0.06
, obtained through a
straightforward exponential
rescaling
Voronchikhin and Kirpichnikov 2026b
;
Jodłowski 2023
, that can be
justified for sufficiently small variation of mass splitting.

The ILC-BDX experiment with a primary beam energy of
E_{\rm beam}=250~\mbox{GeV}
and a 1-year exposure can set new bounds on the coupling constant in the mass range from 10 MeV to 100 MeV for
\Delta=\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})
, at the level of
1/\Lambda_{\rm M}\simeq(9-12)\times 10^{-4}~\mbox{GeV}^{-1}
.
With an exposure increased to 10 years, ILC-BDX can exclude a region of parameter space for mass splittings
\Delta~=~\mathcal{O}(6~\times~10^{-2})
and
E_{\rm beam}=250~\mbox{GeV}
in the mass range from 50 MeV to 100 MeV at the level of
1/\Lambda_{\rm M}\simeq 10^{-3}~\mbox{GeV}^{-1}
.

The heavy dark matter state,
\chi_{1}
, produced in the target can propagate to the detector and create a signal event.
The probability of
\chi_{1}
reaching the detector is characterized by an exponential factor with decay length (
III
). For
\Delta=0.001
the dominant portion of
\chi_{1}
can reach the detector for the mass range
10~\mbox{MeV}\lesssim m_{\chi_{0}}\lesssim 1~\mbox{GeV}
, thus
N_{\rm det.}^{\chi_{1}}\simeq N_{\rm det.}^{\chi_{0}}
.
As a result, one has
N_{\rm sign.}\simeq 2N_{\rm det.}^{\chi_{0}}
.
However, for
\Delta=0.05
only

\chi_{1}
with masses around
m_{\chi_{1}}\lesssim 20~\mbox{MeV}
can reach the detector,
resulting in signal duplication,
N_{\rm sign.}\simeq 2N_{\rm det.}^{\chi_{0}}
, for these relatively small masses. Furthermore, the sensitivity enhancement due to signal duplication,
N_{\rm sign.}\simeq 2N_{\rm det.}^{\chi_{0}}
,
for entire mass region
10~\mbox{MeV}\lesssim m_{\chi_{0}}\lesssim 1~\mbox{GeV}

implies only sufficiently small mass splitting
\Delta=0.001
(see Fig.
3
).

To conclude this section we note that the ILC-BDX experiment can set new constraints on the parameter space of inelastic dark matter models. However, a detailed analysis
of the additional signatures
Schuster et al. 2022
;
Asai et al. 2021
;
Asai et al. 2024
;
Jodłowski 2023
;
Essig et al. 2025
of iDM at ILC-BDX we leave to future work.

V
Conclusion

In our analysis, we show that the ILC-BDX experiment is sensitive to thermal inelastic
magnetic dipole dark matter with sub-GeV masses through dark matter scattering off
detector electrons.
We conclude that the ILC-BDX experiment can potentially probe the parameter space of this
model, especially for mass splittings of
\Delta\gtrsim 0.05
and light DM,

m_{\chi_{0}}\lesssim 100~\mbox{MeV}
.
We argue that doubling the primary beam energy relative to the planned value would
improve the sensitivity in the high-mass region due to the increased acceptance.
