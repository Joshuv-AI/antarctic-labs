Tooth Implantation Following Socket Preservation Using Diode Laser Treatment Combined With “Hood” Closure Technique: A Case Report
Authors: Shanshan Ma, Kui Su, Rongyu Huang, Xue Gao, Yan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of diode laser‐assisted socket preservation with “hood” closure for dental implantation in advanced periodontitis.MethodsA 31‐year‐old female with Stage III Grade C periodontitis underwent minimally invasive extraction of Tooth #46, followed by combined laser therapy and flap closure for socket preservation.ResultsAt 2‐year follow‐up, the results demonstrated: (1) complete resolution of inflammation (BOP 0%); (2) Elimination of pathological pockets (PD ≥ 4 mm 0%); and (3) successful implant placement with stable bone volume.ConclusionThis technique ensures optimal wound healing and ridge preservation, enhancing implant outcomes in compromised sites.

1. Introduction

Socket preservation refers to protective or restorative interventions performed during or after tooth extraction to minimize alveolar bone resorption, preserve soft and hard tissue, thus providing a reliable foundation for future dental prosthetic restoration [1]. However, conventional socket preservation techniques often face challenges in achieving primary wound closure. This may lead to complications such as graft exposure, graft material loss, and potential secondary infection [2].

The “hood” closure technique involves elevating a full‐thickness mucoperiosteal flap composed of granulation tissue along with its overlying soft tissue from the alveolar bone surface, which is then mobilized to the gingival margin for socket coverage [3]. This technique ensures reliable wound healing. Yet the granulation tissue flap may lead to prolonged postoperative bleeding and significant pain.

Diode laser demonstrates significant bactericidal effects against obligate anaerobes, reduces inflammatory response, and enhances periodontal tissue attachment. Clinically, it can markedly decrease postoperative bleeding and pain following flap surgery.

This report describes the treatment of a 31‐year‐old female patient with advanced chronic periodontitis (Stage III, Grade C), focusing on the application of a diode laser‐assisted socket preservation technique with “hood” closure to address severe alveolar bone loss and facilitate successful dental implantation. The case illustrates the clinical challenges in managing periodontally compromised sites and the efficacy of this minimally invasive approach in achieving stable wound healing, ridge preservation, and long‐term implant success, as detailed in the subsequent case presentation.

2. Case Report

2.1. Patient

In July 2022, a 31‐year‐old female was referred to the Department of Periodontology, Hospital of Stomatology, Zhongshan City, China, with chief complaint of recurrent gingival swelling and pain in the right posterior mandible for several months. The patient reported gingival swelling, pain, and tooth mobility in the right posterior mandible for 3 months, accompanied by masticatory weakness. Previous scaling at a local dental clinic provided no improvement in mobility. Recent recurrence of swelling and pain prompted this visit. The patient had no significant systemic diseases or drug allergies.

This study introduces an innovative “hood” closure technique, where reactive soft tissue within the extraction socket is utilized for wound closure following minimally invasive tooth extraction. The “hood” closure technique is a minimally invasive surgical technique used in extraction socket management and implant‐related surgery, aiming to achieve tension‐free primary wound closure. The technique involves preparing a partial‐thickness or full‐thickness mucoperiosteal flap, advancing and suturing it to form a protective “hood‐like” coverage over the entrance of the extraction socket without extensive flap elevation or extensive relaxation of the surrounding tissues.

3.3. Comparison With Traditional Techniques

Traditional extraction socket closure techniques often rely on extensive elevation of the mucoperiosteal flap, which is prone to tissue trauma, postoperative pain, gingival recession, and impaired blood supply, thus affecting soft tissue healing and alveolar ridge preservation. In contrast, the “hood” closure technique can reduce the range of flap elevation, maintain local blood supply, stabilize the wound environment, and is especially suitable for complex sites with poor periodontal conditions, thin gingival biotype, or anatomical limitations.

This study combined the “hood” closure technique with diode laser‐assisted disinfection and bone augmentation with bone graft material combined with CGF, which further optimized the wound closure effect, reduced the risk of infection and wound dehiscence, improved the soft tissue morphology, and achieved predictable alveolar ridge preservation.

4. Conclusion

Due to its tissue coagulation and hemostatic properties, the diode laser is an excellent choice for soft tissue procedures in dentistry, offering precision, safety, and cost‐effectiveness.

4.1. Diode Laser‐Assisted “Hood” Closure Technique for Ridge Preservation

The combination of diode laser and the “hood” closure technique for ridge preservation achieves primary wound healing while avoiding complications such as persistent postoperative hemorrhage. Furthermore, it promotes optimal hard tissue regeneration, establishing a solid foundation for subsequent implant placement and significantly enhancing the long‐term outcomes of implant‐supported prostheses.

Within the limitations of this case report, the combined application of diode laser‐assisted debridement, socket preservation with Bio‐Oss and CGF, and the hood closure technique achieved favorable clinical outcomes. The treatment achieved complete soft tissue healing, effective alveolar ridge preservation, and successful osseointegration of the subsequent implant. This minimally invasive treatment protocol has good predictability and can provide a valuable clinical option for preimplant restoration of extraction sockets with poor periodontal conditions.
