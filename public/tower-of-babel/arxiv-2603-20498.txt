Optimal Transport and Generalized Lagrangian Mean Curvature Flows on Kim-McCann Metrics
Arunima Bhattacharya, Micah Warren, and Daniel Weser

Abstract
. We express the mean curvature flow of Lagrangian submanifolds in pseudo-Riemannian manifolds endowed with the Kim-McCann-Warren metric within the framework of generalized mean curvature flow on Kim-McCann manifolds. While generalized mean curvature flow has been studied in Kähler geometry, our work shows that techniques from para-Kähler geometry arise naturally in the Kim-McCann setting. Using this perspective, we prove that the Lagrangian condition is preserved along the flow. By identifying generalized mean curvature flow with Lagrangian mean curvature flow, we show that the Ma-Trudinger-Wang regularity theory applies to this setting. In particular, the cross-curvature positivity condition of Kim-McCann yields smoothly converging flows of Lagrangian submanifolds. Under the cross-curvature condition, any Lagrangian submanifold avoiding the cut locus converges exponentially to a stationary submanifold, which locally arises as the graph of an optimal transport map. Our framework substantiates the analogy between special Lagrangian geometry in almost Calabi–Yau manifolds and optimal transport theory in the Kim-McCann setting. In particular, we show that Kim–McCann manifolds equipped with a para-holomorphic volume form serve as the natural counterpart to almost Calabi-Yau manifolds.



1.
Introduction

It was observed by Kim-McCann-Warren in
[
16
]
that graphs of optimal transport maps are calibrated submanifolds of a conformal modification of the Kim-McCann metric
[
17
]
introduced in the study of
regularity theory of optimal transport. The Kim-McCann metric is an
(n,n)
signature metric on
M\times\bar{M}
with a natural Kähler form in which graphs of optimal transportation maps are space-like Lagrangian submanifolds. While this suggests an analogy to the special Lagrangian geometry occurring in Calabi-Yau manifolds, neither the modified Kim-McCann-Warren metric
[
16
]
nor the Kim-McCann metric
[
17
]
is Ricci-flat.

In this paper, we make an observation that completes the analogy, opening up a broader bridge between the theory of Calabi-Yau metrics and Kim-McCann metrics. The Kim-McCann metrics should be thought not to correspond to Calabi-Yau manifolds, but rather
almost
Calabi-Yau manifolds, also called special Kähler manifolds by Bryant (cf. Bryant
[
7
]
and Joyce
[
14
, pg 43]
). The Kim-McCann-Warren metric then corresponds to conformal modifications of almost Calabi-Yau metrics, with a conformal factor chosen so that special Lagrangian calibrations exist. Such
almost
Calabi-Yau metrics have been studied in the Kähler setting for well over two decades (see Goldstein
[
12
]
) and give rise to generalized mean curvature flow, which flows towards a special Lagrangian submanifold, preserving the Lagrangian condition, even though the ambient metric is not Einstein. Note that mean curvature flow fails to preserve the Lagrangian condition when the ambient manifold is not Einstein (see Bryant
[
6
]
).

Here we show that the exact analogue is true in the Kim-McCann setting; the corresponding generalized mean curvature flow preserves the Lagrangian condition and is stationary on calibrated submanifolds. This analogy is rich with interesting avenues to explore.

The Kim–McCann metric continues to be of significant interest, as reflected in the recent geometric exposition in Léger-Vialard
[
20
]
and the advancements in Brendle-Legér-McCann-Rankin
[
5
]
. In this work, we build on these developments by extending the results of
[
5
]
from the elliptic to the parabolic setting, demonstrating that the Ma-Trudinger-Wang
[
22
]
regularity theory ensures not only the smoothness of stationary solutions, but also the regularity of the flow.

This generalizes and combines two aspects of regularity theory.
The first is the result of Brendle-Legér-McCann-Rankin in
[
5
]
(see also Warren
[
29
]
for the two-dimensional case), which shows that optimal transport maps satisfying the Ma-Trudinger-Wang condition can be proven regular using purely minimal surface techniques. Here, we generalize this from the elliptic to the parabolic setting using generalized mean curvature flow. At the same time, we extend the parabolic Monge-Ampère flow introduced in Kim-Streets-Warren
[
18
]
to spacelike submanifolds that do not arise as the cost-exponential of a global scalar function, and show this flow is well-behaved even without the global scalar potential function. In particular, the result can be applied to a wide variety of spacelike immersions, including non-graphical immersions.

Generalized mean curvature flow was introduced independently by Behrndt
[
3
]
and Smoczyk-Wang
[
24
]
, with each approach highlighting different structural aspects: One centered on a torsion connection and the other on a Ricci potential. While we draw heuristic insights from these generalized flows, particularly from the para-complex setting, our analysis does not rely on their machinery. Instead, we present self-contained arguments that mirror those in the elliptic case, providing direct proofs of our main results without requiring the broader para-complex framework.
Our perspective differs from that of Chursin-Schäfer-Smoczyk
[
8
]
, which, following Smoczyk-Wang
[
24
]
, analyzes the geometry via comparisons between different connections. In contrast, we adopt an approach more in line with Behrndt
[
3
]
, emphasizing the role of calibrating forms as introduced in Kim-McCann-Warren
[
16
]
.

We present our main results below, with formal definitions deferred to later sections to keep the introduction streamlined.
Our first result draws inspiration from a result of Smoczyk
[
26
]
, subsequently extended by Behrndt
[
3
]
and Smoczyk-Wang
[
24
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
M\times\bar{M}
is a Kim-McCann manifold endowed with a para-holomorphic
n
-form
\Omega
and
L(t)
is a family of immersed Lagrangian submanifolds flowing by the generalized mean curvature flow. If
L(0)
is Lagrangian, then so is
L(t)
for
t>0
.

For the precise definition of a Kim-McCann manifold, we refer the reader to Definition
2.1
. The definition of the para-holomorphic
n
-form is given in Definition

, and the generalized mean curvature flow is defined in Definition
3.2
.

Our next result extends the elliptic regularity theory of Brendle-Legér-McCann-Rankin
[
5
]
to the parabolic setting and generalizes the parabolic Monge-Ampère flow of Kim-Streets-Warren
[
18
]
to spacelike submanifolds beyond cost-exponentials of global potentials.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
M\times\bar{M}
is a Kim-McCann manifold with a positive cross-curvature condition, and
L(t)
is a family of compactly immersed Lagrangian submanifolds flowing by the generalized mean curvature flow. If
L(t)
stays in a compact set in the complement of the cut-locus, on
[0,T)
, then the flow extends beyond

. If the flow stays in a compact set avoiding the cut-locus for all time, then it converges to an immersed calibrated submanifold.

For the precise definition of the cut locus, we refer the reader to Definition
2.2
. Here, the term “calibrated” is taken with respect to the metric introduced by Kim-McCann-Warren
[
16
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

The convergence of Lagrangian mean curvature flow in the Calabi-Yau setting has long been studied following the Thomas-Yau conjecture
[
28
]
; they conjectured that the Lagrangian mean curvature flow in a Calabi-Yau manifold should exist for all time and converge to a special Lagrangian, provided the initial Lagrangian is stable in an appropriate sense (see Joyce
[
15
]
for subsequent refinements of this conjecture). Here, we show that the cross-curvature condition provides a framework in which an analog of the Thomas-Yau conjecture holds.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
uses an energetic method and is inspired in part by a strategy first mapped out in Smoczyk
[
25
]
(see also Behrndt
[
3
]
and Wood
[
30
]
). Necessarily, we use para-complex numbers and para-Kähler geometry.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on a geometric maximum principle for a Riemannian metric on the product space along the graph, building on the work of Brendle-Legér-McCann-Rankin
[
5
]
while extending their method to the parabolic setting. To obtain exponential convergence, we apply a Li-Yau Harnack inequality, in the spirit of Kim-McCann-Warren
[
16
]
and Abedin–Kitagawa
[
1
]
.

Rather than aiming to construct a comprehensive dictionary linking para-complex geometry and optimal transport, our goal is more focused: To demonstrate that key results in optimal transport theory admit self-contained proofs within the framework of generalized Lagrangian mean curvature flow. We direct the reader to Cruceanu-Fortuny-Gadea
[
9
]
for a history of para-complex numbers and para-complex geometry.

1.1.
Relation to the Sinkhorn Algorithm

Recent work by Deb-Kim-Pal-Schiebinger
[
11
]
has identified a continuous-time limit of Sinkhorn’s algorithm for entropy-regularized optimal transport as the regularization parameter tends to zero, yielding a Wasserstein mirror gradient flow for the transport cost on the space of probability measures. For smooth optimal transport plans induced by a potential, that admits sufficient regularity, this measure-valued flow can be formally rewritten in new coordinates, leading to a parabolic Monge-Ampère type flow; Berman
[
4
]
shows that Sinkhorn iterations, in a joint limit (mesh size
\rightarrow 0,\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, iterations
\varepsilon^{-1}
), converge to a parabolic Monge-Ampère equation for the optimal transport potential.

From this perspective, the parabolic Monge-Ampère flow appearing in optimal transport may be viewed as a refined, geometric realization of the Sinkhorn limit, obtained after restricting to graphical Lagrangian submanifolds and passing from Wasserstein space to the Kim-McCann geometric framework. Our work operates directly at this geometric level: We interpret the parabolic Monge-Ampère equation as a generalized Lagrangian mean curvature flow on Kim-McCann manifolds, establish preservation of the Lagrangian condition, and prove long-time existence and convergence under the positive cross-curvature condition.

1.2.
Organization

In Section 2, we develop the appropriate notions and constructions adapted from almost Calabi-Yau theory. We introduce the Lagrangian angle and the generalized mean curvature flow, and explain their relationship. In Section 3, we prove that the Lagrangian condition is preserved along the flow by showing that the integral of the Kähler form restricted to

L
vanishes identically, using a Gronwall-type argument. In Section 4, we incorporate the MTW condition and present a parabolic analogue of certain results from
[
5
]
. Finally, in Section 5, we establish higher regularity and exponential convergence.

2.
Geometric Background

2.1.
Kim-McCann Manifold

We define a Kim-McCann manifold by slightly generalizing the setting of
[
17
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Kim-McCann manifold
is a product manifold
M^{n}\times\bar{M}^{n}
equipped with a metric
h
satisfying the following property: for every point
(x,\bar{x})\in M^{n}\times\bar{M}^{n}\setminus\mathscr{C}
, where
\mathscr{C}
denotes the cut locus (defined below), there exists a function
c
locally defined near
(x,\bar{x})
such that for all
V\in T_{x}M^{n}
and
\bar{W}\in T_{\bar{x}}\bar{M}^{n}
,

h(V,\bar{W}):=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,V\bar{W}c,

and moreover,
h
vanishes on pairs of vectors tangent to the same factor.

Definition 2.2
.

We define the set
\mathscr{C}
where the metric is not well-defined to be the
cut-locus
. The term derives from the set of points on a Riemannian manifold at which the squared distance function encounters differentiability issues.

We will assume the function
c
is at least
C^{4}
away from the set
\mathscr{C}
.
The metric
h
can be written in the following form:

h:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&-c_{i\bar{s}}\\
-c_{\bar{s}i}&0\end{array}\right).

Remark 2.3
.

By permitting the metric-defining function to be locally defined, our framework extends naturally to cases like the flat torus. For instance, the Kim–McCann metric derived from the squared distance remains smooth, even though the underlying cost function has a singularity; singularities that our definition is designed to bypass.
A rationally-sloped geodesic in a torus, in this case, need not encounter the cut-locus.

Note that the form

\omega=-\frac{1}{2}c_{i\bar{s}}\,dx^{i}\wedge d\bar{x}^{s}

is a symplectic form. To interpret the symplectic form as a para-Kähler form, consider the map

K:T_{\left(p,\bar{p}\right)}M\times\bar{M}\rightarrow T_{\left(p,\bar{p}\right)}M\times\bar{M}

which is represented as

(2.1)

K_{p,\bar{p}}=I_{T_{p}M}\oplus\left(-I\right)_{T_{\bar{p}}\bar{M}}.

For a basic introduction to para-Kähler geometry, we refer the reader to
[
9
]
. The
important fact is that the complex structure map
J
satisfying
J^{2}=-I
is
replaced by a para-complex structure map,
K
, satisfying
K^{2}=I
. Then one has

\omega(\cdot,\cdot)=h(K\cdot,\cdot).

It was noted in
[
17
]
that graphs
\left(x,T(x)\right)
of optimal
transport maps are Lagrangian with respect to this symplectic form, and calibrated with respect to a conformal metric depending on the mass densities
[
16
]
. In
[
29
, Claim 2.2]
, it was observed that
K
is parallel with respect to the
Kim-McCann metric.

2.2.
Para-complex Geometry

A para-complex valued form
\eta
is of
\left(n,0\right)
type if

\mathbf{k}\cdot\eta(V_{1},..,V_{n})=\eta(KV_{1},..,V_{n})=\eta(V_{1},KV_{2},..,V_{n}),\mbox{ etc.}

Here
\mathbf{k}
is an algebraic object akin to the more familiar

\mathbf{i}
, but instead satisfies
\mathbf{k}^{2}=1.
Define the numbers
\tau
and
\bar{\tau}
by

(2.2)

\tau=\frac{1}{2}(1+\textrm{\bf k})\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\overline{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\textrm{\bf k})\,,

which satisfy

(2.3)

\tau^{2}=\tau\,,\quad\overline{\tau}^{\,2}=\overline{\tau}\,,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tau\overline{\tau}=0\,.

We note that two standard notational conventions collide here. In the optimal transport literature, the bar is commonly used to denote the target manifold, while in the complex and para-complex setting, the overline denotes conjugation. The equations below necessarily employ both conventions, although the distinction should be clear from the context.

We define

\frac{\partial}{\partial{z^{i}}}=\tau\frac{\partial}{\partial{x^{i}}}+\overline{\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bar{x^{i}}}}

and

d{z^{i}}=\tau d{x^{i}}+\overline{\tau}d{\bar{x^{i}}}.

Noting that

d{z^{1}}\wedge...\wedge d{z^{n}}=\tau d{x^{1}}\wedge...\wedge d{x^{n}}+\overline{\tau}d\bar{x^{1}}\wedge...\wedge d\bar{x^{n}}

and using the relations in (
2.3
), one can check that any para-holomorphic
(n,0)
-form can be written as

\eta=f(z)d{z}

(using
dz=dz^{1}\wedge...dz^{n},
etc.) for
f
satisfying

\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z^{i}}}f=0.

Writing

f(x)=\tau u(x,\bar{x})+\bar{\tau}v(x,\bar{x})

the Cauchy-Riemann equations become

\displaystyle\partial_{y}u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\partial_{x}v

\displaystyle=0.

Thus
for real valued
u
and
v

(2.4)

\Omega=\tau\,u(x)dx+\overline{\tau}\,v(\bar{x})d\bar{x}

is a para-holomorphic
\left(n,0\right)
-form.

2.3.
Almost Calabi-Yau Manifold

Our first goal is to demonstrate that there is indeed a strong analogy between almost Calabi-Yau manifolds and Kim-McCann manifolds.

As motivation, we now briefly recall the classical complex setting, for example, see the work of Joyce
[
13
, Section 8.4]
. Given
n\geq 2
, an
almost Calabi-Yau

n
-fold is a
quadruple
(X,J,g,\Omega)
such that
(X,J,g)
is a compact
n

(complex)-dimensional Kähler manifold, and
\Omega
is a non-vanishing
holomorphic
(n,0)
-form on
X.

In this setting, one defines a function
\psi
(see
[
2
, (15)]
, cf.
[
13
, 8.24]
) such that

(2.5)

e^{2n\psi}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\left(\frac{i}{2}\right)^{n}\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}.

We would like to explore the analogy in the para-complex setting.
Recalling (
2.4
) we see

\displaystyle\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}^{{}_{\textrm{\hskip 0.85355pt\bf k}}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\tau\,u(x)dx+\overline{\tau}\,v(\bar{x})\,d\bar{x}\Big)\wedge\Big(\overline{\tau}\,u(x)dx+\tau\,v(\bar{x})\,d\bar{x}\Big)

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}\,uv\,dx\wedge d\bar{x}+\overline{\tau}^{\,2}\,uv\,d\bar{x}\wedge dx

\displaystyle=\left(\tau^{2}+\left(-1\right)^{n^{2}}\,\overline{\tau}^{\,2}\right)\,uv\,dx\wedge d\bar{x}

\displaystyle=uv\,\mathbf{k}^{n}\,dxd\bar{x}.

In particular, if

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Omega\;

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{2}\big(\rho(x)dx+\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})d\bar{x}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{k}\big(\rho(x)dx-\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})d\bar{x}\big)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\;\tau\rho(x)dx+\overline{\tau}\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})d\bar{x}

one has

(2.8)

\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}^{{}_{\textrm{\hskip 0.85355pt\bf k}}}=\rho(x)\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})\mathbf{k}^{n}dxd\bar{x}.

With this setup, consider a function
\psi
that satisfies an equation similar to (
2.5
), replacing
i
with
\bf{k}

(2.9)

e^{2n\psi}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=(-1)^{n\left(n-1\right)/2}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}}{2}\right)^{n}\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}^{{}_{\textrm{\hskip 0.85355pt\bf k}}}.

Now as

\omega=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,\bar{s}}\left(-c_{i\bar{s}}\right)dx^{i}\wedge d\bar{x}^{s}

one can check that

\omega^{n}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}n!(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\det\left(-c_{i\bar{s}}\right)dxd\bar{x},

so to satisfy our version of equation (
2.5
), we require

e^{2n\psi}=\frac{\rho\bar{\rho}}{\det\left(-c_{i\bar{s}}\right)},

that is

(2.10)

\psi=\frac{1}{2n}\left(\ln\rho+\ln\bar{\rho}-\ln\det\left(-c_{i\bar{s}}\right)\right).

Recall (cf.
[
13
, pg 166]
) that defining

\tilde{g}=e^{2\psi}g

the (real)
n
-form
\operatorname{Re}(\Omega)
becomes a calibration that detects special Lagrangian submanifolds in the conformal metric.

On our side of the analogy, this would suggest that (recalling (
2.6
))

\operatorname{Re}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho(x)dx+\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})d\bar{x}\right)

would be a calibration for the metric defined by

(2.11)

\tilde{h}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\rho(x)\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})}{\det\left(-c_{i\bar{s}}\right)}\right)^{1/n}\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&-c_{i\bar{s}}\\
-c_{\bar{s}i}&0\end{array}\right).

But this is precisely the conclusion of
[
16
]
, namely that the graph of the optimal transport map pairing
\rho
to

\bar{\rho}
with cost
c
, is a volume maximizing submanifold of
M\times\bar{M}
with the above metric and calibration.

To continue exploring the analogy, we define a
Generalized Kim-McCann manifold
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
. A
Generalized Kim-McCann (GKM)

n
-fold is a
quadruple
(M\times\bar{M},K,h,\Omega)
such that
(M\times\bar{M},K,h)
is a compact
n+n

dimensional Kim-McCann manifold, and
\Omega
is a non-vanishing
para-holomorphic
(n,0)
-form on
M\times\bar{M}.

Given a GKM manifold, let
\psi
be defined implicitly by (
2.9
), and let
L
be a Lagrangian submanifold. The generalized mean curvature is then defined as

\vec{H}_{G}:=\vec{H}-n\,(\hat{\nabla}\psi)^{\perp}

where
\vec{H}
is the standard mean curvature.
This coincides with the definition used in the almost Calabi–Yau setting. We will later show that

\mathcal{L}_{\vec{H}-n\,(\hat{\nabla}\psi)^{\perp}}\omega=0

implying that the flow generated by this vector field preserves the Lagrangian condition. A rigorous proof of this claim will be given using the maximum principle.

We also remark that the mean curvature of
L
with respect to the conformal metric (
2.11
) is

e^{-2\psi}\vec{H}_{G}.

2.4.
Lagrangian Submanifolds of Almost Calabi-Yau Manifolds

We consider the following immersions

F:L^{n}\to M^{n}\times\bar{M}^{n}.

For an arbitrary chart on
L
we may take
\partial_{i}F
to be a basis for the tangent space in
M\times\bar{M}
. Before proceeding, we make the following observation.

Lemma 2.5
.

Any spacelike submanifold is locally a graphical submanifold over
M.

Proof.

Since the induced metric is Riemannian, we are free to choose an orthonormal chart
for
T_{(x,\bar{x})}
at any point
(x,\bar{x})\in L
. Denote the basis at
(x,\bar{x})
by

\partial_{i}F=\left(\xi_{i},\zeta_{i}\right)\in T_{x}M\times T_{\bar{x}}\bar{M}.

We claim that
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}
are independent: If not, then
we have for some nontrivial
a_{j}

\xi_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}\xi_{j}

in which case, we have the vector equality:

\partial_{i}F-\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}\partial_{j}F=\left(0,\zeta_{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}\zeta_{j}\right).

Observe that the left-hand side has length

\left\|\partial_{i}F-\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}\partial_{j}F\right\|^{2}=1+\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}^{2}

whereas

\left\|\left(0,\zeta_{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}a_{j}\zeta_{j}\right)\right\|^{2}=0

a contradiction.
It follows that
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}
do form a basis for
T_{x}M.
The tangent space to
L
is then transverse to
\{\bar{x}\}\times\bar{M}
and it follows (see
[
19
, Corollary 6.33]
) that L is locally a graph over
M
.

∎

It is worth noting that if the graph
(x,T(x))
is Lagrangian, it will be
locally described via a cost-exponential: Consider the one-form

\eta=-c_{i}(x,T(x))dx^{i}.

One can check that
\eta
is closed when the graph is Kähler. Thus, locally, we get

(2.12)

Du+D_{x}c(x,T(x))=0

for some (local) potential
u
. In fact, on any simply connected neighborhood on which the Lagrangian submanifold is represented as a graph, the function
u
will exist.
Note that a differentiable Kantorovich potential, if it exists, will satisfy (
2.12
). However, we do not assume a priori that such a potential is available.

Thus, it is possible to invoke gradient-type graphs locally whenever a disk-shaped region projects down to a simply connected neighborhood in
M.

This can be accomplished by fixing a metric
m
on
M
and working locally within balls whose diameters are smaller than the injectivity radius with respect to
m
.

2.5.
The Lagrangian Angle

Building on the framework introduced in
[
2
]
, we define the Lagrangian angle
\theta
through the relation

(2.13)

\Omega\big|_{L}=e^{{\bf k}\theta+n\psi}d\textrm{Vol}_{L},

where

\displaystyle e^{{\bf k}\theta}\;=\;\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{2l}}{(2l)!}+{\bf k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}

\displaystyle\;=\;\cosh\theta+{\bf k}\sinh\theta\,

\displaystyle\;=\;e^{\theta}\tau+e^{-\theta}\bar{\tau}.

Note that, unlike in the Calabi–Yau setting, the angle is defined as a real number.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
L
is a Lagrangian spacelike submanifold of a GKM, then the Lagrangian angle is well-defined.

Proof.

Take a basis of an immersion
F
that is locally defined as a graph, that is
F_{i}=\partial_{i}F=\left(E_{i},T_{i}^{s}E_{\bar{s}}\right),

where
E_{i}
form a coordinate basis for
T_{p}M
and
E_{\bar{s}}
form a coordinate basis for
T_{\bar{T(p)}}\bar{M}
for some function
T:M\to\bar{M}
.

Then recalling

\Omega=\tau\rho(x)dx+\bar{\tau}\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})d\bar{x}

we have

\displaystyle\Omega_{|_{L}}\left(F_{1},...,F_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\rho(x)dx\left(E_{1},E_{2},..,E_{n}\right)+\bar{\tau}\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})d\bar{x}(T_{1}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}},T_{2}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}},...)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\rho(x)+\bar{\tau}\bar{\rho}(\bar{x})\det DT.

On the other hand

dVol_{L}(F_{1},...F_{n})=\sqrt{g_{ij}}

for

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{i}\cdot F_{j}=\left(E_{i},T_{i}^{s}E_{\bar{s}}\right)\cdot\left(E_{j},T_{j}^{s}E_{\bar{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(-c_{i\bar{s}}T_{j}^{s}-c_{j\bar{s}}T_{i}^{\bar{s}}\right).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{W+W^{T}}{2})_{ij}

where

W_{ij}=-c_{i\bar{s}}T_{j}^{s}.

Recall that
W
is symmetric when
L
is Lagrangian and positive definite when
L
is spacelike. Thus

dVol_{L}(F_{1},...F_{n})=\sqrt{\det W}=\sqrt{\det\left(-c_{i\bar{s}}\right)\det DT}.

By (
2.10
)

e^{n\psi}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho(x)\bar{\rho}\left(\bar{x}\right)}{\det(-c_{i\bar{s}})}}

so

\displaystyle e^{n\psi}dVol_{L}(F_{1},...F_{n})

\displaystyle=

3.
Short-time Existence and Preservation of Lagrangian Condition

We rely on standard results for mean curvature and generalized mean curvature flow in the Riemannian setting to establish short-time existence. This is well known in the Riemannian case (see
[
27
, Prop 3.2]
), and an analogous result for spacelike surfaces in pseudo-Riemannian manifolds appears in
[
21
, Prop 5.1]
. The extension to generalized mean curvature flow involves only lower-order terms, so short-time existence follows by standard arguments (cf.
[
24
, Proof of Theorem 1]
).

The following formulas allow us to perform an energetic argument to show that the Lagrangian condition is preserved. Necessarily, these computations need to be in the setting of totally real submanifolds (those containing no para-complex planes), which is an open set containing Lagrangian submanifolds.

We briefly recall the following definition for the reader’s convenience:

Definition 3.1
.

A
totally real submanifold

L
in the para-Kähler setting is a submanifold whose tangent space does not contain any para-complex planes; that is, for every
V\in T_{p}L
we have
KV\notin T_{p}L.

For our purposes, it is convenient to consider immersed submanifolds with flows determined locally.
Namely, given some abstract manifold
L_{0}
, consider a family of immersions

F:L_{0}\times[0,t_{0})\rightarrow M\times\bar{M}

governed by a flow that is determined locally by geometric quantities in
L_{0}
.

Definition 3.2
.

An immersed submanifold of a GKM manifold flows by
generalized mean curvature flow
if

(3.1)

\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\right)^{\perp}=\vec{H}-n\left(\hat{\nabla}\psi\right)^{\perp}

where
\psi
is as defined in (
2.10
).

We note here that the parabolic flow discussed in
[
18
]
provides a
vertical flow of Lagrangian submanifolds. One can check that the normal projection of
the flow is the generalized mean curvature flow. This flow requires the map
T

[
18
]
to be globally described as a cost-exponential of a potential.

In this section, we will show that the Lagrangian condition is preserved. The mean curvature flow itself evolves in the space of totally real submanifolds. We use a Gronwall type argument to show that the integral of the Kähler form must remain 0 for any short time on which the flow exists.

We also recall
[
17
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 3.3

(Riemann curvature tensor and Christoffel symbols)
.

Use a non-degenerate cost
c\in C^{4}(N)
to define a pseudo-metric on the domain

on

and

\Gamma_{ij}^{\phantom{ij}m}=c^{m\bar{k}}c_{kij}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Gamma_{i\bar{j}}^{\phantom{i\bar{j}}\bar{m}}=c^{\bar{m}k}c_{ki\bar{j}}.

Furthermore, the components of the Riemann curvature tensor vanish except when the
number of barred and unbarred indices is equal, in which case the value of the component

Proposition 3.7

Suppose that

\frac{d}{dt}\int\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|_{g}^{2}dV_{g}(t)\leq C(G,G^{\prime},t_{0})\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|_{g}^{2}(t).

Proof.

Note that along the flow,

\frac{dF}{dt}=\vec{H}-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\vec{H}-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}}\omega=d\left(\iota_{\vec{H}-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}}\omega\right)+\iota_{\vec{H}-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}}d\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(\alpha_{\vec{H}}-n\alpha_{\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-dd|_{L}^{\ast}\omega+\omega\ast G+\nabla\omega\ast G^{\prime}.

Thus

\frac{d}{dt}\int\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|_{g}^{2}dV_{g}(t)=\int 2\langle\omega,\frac{d}{dt}\omega\rangle_{g}dV_{g}(t)+\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|_{g}^{2}\frac{d}{dt}dV_{g}(t).

Now

\displaystyle\int 2\langle\omega,\frac{d}{dt}\omega\rangle_{g}dV_{g}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int 2\langle\omega,-dd|_{L}^{\ast}\omega+\omega\ast G+\nabla\omega\ast G^{\prime}\rangle_{g}dV_{g}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int\langle d|_{L}^{\ast}\omega,d|_{L}^{\ast}\omega\rangle dV_{g}(t)+\int 2\langle\omega,\omega\ast G+\nabla\omega\ast G^{\prime}\rangle_{g}dV_{g}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\int\left\|d|_{L}^{\ast}\omega\right\|^{2}dV_{g}(t)+C_{1}(G)\int\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|^{2}dV_{g}(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{2}(G^{\prime})}{\varepsilon}\int\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|^{2}dV_{g}(t)+\varepsilon\int\left\|\nabla\omega|_{L}\right\|^{2}dV_{g}(t).

By The Weitzenböck Formula
[
23
, Theorem 9.8, see
Definition 9.6, Proposition 9.7 and (2.1.26, 2.1.28, 9.3.1, 1.0.3) ]

we know that

-\nabla^{\ast}\nabla\omega=-dd^{\ast}\omega-\mathfrak{Ric(\omega)}

where
\mathfrak{Ric}
is a linear operator
\Lambda^{2}(L)\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}(L)
with coefficients that depend linearly on the Riemannian
curvature tensor of
\left(L,g\right)
:

\mathfrak{Ric=}\sum_{i,j,k,l}R_{ijkl}dx_{i}\wedge(dx_{j}\vee(dx_{k}\wedge(dx_{l}\vee\cdot))).

Next

\displaystyle\int\left\|\nabla\omega|_{L}\right\|^{2}dV_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\langle\nabla^{\ast}\nabla\omega,\omega\rangle_{g}dV_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\langle dd^{\ast}\omega+\mathfrak{Ric}(\omega),\omega\rangle_{g}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\left\|d|_{L}^{\ast}\omega\right\|^{2}dV_{g}(t)+C_{3}(\mathfrak{Ric})\int\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|^{2}dV_{g}(t).

Putting this together, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\int\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|_{g}^{2}dV_{g}(t)\leq-\int\left\|d|_{L}^{\ast}\omega\right\|^{2}dV_{g}(t)+C_{4}\int\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|^{2}dV_{g}(t).

∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
L
is flowing by generalized mean curvature flow on a
time interval
\left[0,t_{0}\right]
and
\omega|_{L}\equiv 0
when

This follows from applying a Gronwall argument:

\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-C_{4}t}\int\left\|\omega|_{L}\right\|_{g}^{2}dV_{g}(t)\right)\leq 0.

\frac{d}{dt}\theta(x,t)\,dVol_{L}=n\,d\psi\wedge*d\theta+d(*d\theta),

and

n\,\frac{d}{dt}\psi(F(x,t))+\frac{d}{dt}dVol_{L}=d\theta\wedge*d\theta.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\theta(x,t)

\displaystyle=*^{-1}d(*d\theta)+*^{-1}\big(n\,d\psi\wedge*d\theta\big)

\displaystyle=-d^{*}d\theta+n\,g(\nabla\psi,\nabla\theta)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g}\theta+n\,g(\nabla\psi,\nabla\theta).

Finally,

\frac{d}{dt}dVol_{L}=|\nabla\theta|^{2}-n\,\frac{d}{dt}\psi(F(x,t)).

∎

It follows from Claim
4.2
that
\theta
must satisfy a maximum principle.

Corollary 4.3
.

Given a generalized Lagrangian mean curvature flow, the value of
\theta
is bounded above and below by its initial values.

4.1.
Slope Estimates for the Graph

To make a global sense of slope bounds, we follow the approach of
[
5
]
and introduce a global Riemannian metric

\hat{S}
on the product space. In this framework, the slope can be measured by computing the

\hat{S}
-length of tangent vectors to the immersion that have unit length with respect to the pseudo-Riemannian metric.

4.1.1.
Construction of the Auxiliary Metric
\hat{S}

We use the indefinite metric to define a fully Riemannian metric on

M\times\bar{M}
. Pick an arbitrary Riemannian metric
m
on
M
.
For vectors
\bar{W}_{i}\in T_{\bar{x}}\bar{M}
define

\bar{m}(\bar{W}_{1},\bar{W}_{2})=4\,m^{ij}\,h(V_{i},\bar{W}_{1})\,h(V_{j},\bar{W}_{2}),

where the trace is taken over some basis
\{V_{i}\}
for
T_{p}M
.
We can check that
\bar{m}
defines a positive definite symmetric bilinear form
on the tangent space of
\bar{M}
at any point in the product where
h

is defined.

Now define the auxiliary metric

\hat{S}=m+\bar{m},

which is a positive definite metric on the product space.
Note that this is not a product metric, as the second factor depends
on the first point.

Our goal is to bound the ratio

(4.3)

\mathcal{R}(V)=\frac{\hat{S}(V,V)}{h(V,V)}

over all vectors
V
in the tangent space to the graph.

Note that this quotient can be maximized for two different geometric reasons.
One is that the tangent vector is very flat, in which case
h(V,V)
is small
relative to
\hat{S}(V,V)
.
The other is that the tangent vector is very steep.

4.1.2.
Coordinates at the Maximum Point

Since
L
is compact, the quantity defined by (
4.3
) is bounded whenever the immersion is spacelike, in which case it represents the ratio of two Riemannian metrics on the same tangent space. Consequently, (
4.3
) attains a maximum on the unit tangent bundle. Let
\left(x,\bar{x}\right)
be a point where this maximum is achieved.
We now describe the tangent space at this point more precisely.

At this point, take normal coordinates with respect to
m
. Then choose Type I special coordinates for
\bar{M}
.
We have a product coordinate chart with

h=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\delta_{i\bar{s}}\\
\delta_{\bar{s}i}&0\end{array}\right)

at the point
\left(x,\bar{x}\right)
. We also know that the first chart
was chosen normal by
m.
Note that all derivatives in the chart are
determined by normal coordinates in the original metric, linearly modifying
by the inverse of
c_{is}
which is controlled on a compact region.
In this choice of coordinates, at the point, we have

\displaystyle\hat{S}\left(E_{i},E_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle\hat{S}\left(E_{\bar{k}},E_{\bar{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=4\delta^{ij}\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ki}\frac{1}{2}\delta_{lj}=\delta_{kl}.

We know that the Kähler form at the point is still

\omega(\cdot,\cdot)=h(K\cdot,\cdot)

so a graphical tangent vector over
M
of the form

\widetilde{F}_{i}=E_{i}+T_{i}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}}

must satisfy
\omega(\widetilde{F}_{i},\widetilde{F}_{j})=0
which, using
c_{i\bar{s}}=-\delta_{i\bar{s}}
yields

T_{i}^{\bar{j}}=T_{j}^{\bar{\imath}}

at the point
x.
In particular the matrix
DT
is symmetric, and we may
diagonalize

DT=\left(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\right)

where we are choosing the order of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq...\geq\lambda_{n}
and noting that the space-like condition requires the
eigenvalues of
DT
to be positive. Thus at a point we have

\widetilde{F}_{i}=E_{i}+\lambda_{i}E_{\bar{\imath}}

and

\frac{\hat{S}(\widetilde{F}_{i},\widetilde{F}_{i})}{h(\widetilde{F}_{i},\widetilde{F}_{i})}=\frac{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}}.

Next we claim that the maximum of the ratio must occur at either
\lambda_{1}
or
\lambda_{n}.

First, we note that the functiom

\lambda\rightarrow\frac{1+\lambda^{2}}{\lambda}

is convex so the maximum over a set of finite values is either at the largest or
the smallest. So either

\frac{1+\lambda_{1}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\geq\frac{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}}

or

\frac{1+\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\lambda_{n}}\geq\frac{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}}.

A basic linear algebra argument gaurantees that the quotient will be maximized along one of the these two eigenvectors.
So at the point where the maximum occurs, we have chosen coordinates
\widetilde{F}_{i}=E_{i}+\lambda_{i}E_{\bar{\imath}}
and the maximum ratio
\mathcal{R}(V)
is either at
\widetilde{F}_{1}
or
\widetilde{F}_{n}

Rescale the tangent vectors diagonally by

\widetilde{F}_{i}\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}\,\widetilde{F}_{i}
.
The resulting vectors form an orthonormal basis with respect to the induced metric
g
. We then exponentiate these vectors with respect to
g
in a neighborhood, obtaining a new coordinate system on
M
that agrees with the original coordinates at the point

x
and is normal there. The purpose of this construction is to allow us to carry out computations in normal coordinates at the point.

Define the set of tangent vectors
F_{i}
, to be the coordinate tangent frame obtained by exponentiating as

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i}

with respect to the induced metric. Note that at this point, these are represented in the ambient (original coordinates) as

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i}+\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}E_{\bar{i}}.

4.1.3.
Maximum Principle Formulas for the Slope Ratio

The following is a general statement concerning the maximum of ratios for tensors on a Riemannian manifold; it does not depend on any specific properties of the manifold or submanifolds considered here.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
S
be a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor on
M
.
Suppose
p_{0}
is a point where the function
R(V)
achieves its maximum,
and let
F_{\mathord{\xi}}
be a maximizing vector.
If
S
is diagonal at
p_{0}
with respect to normal coordinates

\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}\}
containing
F_{\mathord{\xi}}
, then

\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\leq 0\quad\text{for all }i.

Proof.

Working entirely intrinsically, we get

\displaystyle\nabla\nabla S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},F_{i})

\displaystyle=F_{i}\nabla S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i})

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla S(\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i})-\nabla S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i})-\nabla S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{i}).

Evaluating this at the origin (note all intrinsic connection terms vanish), we get

\displaystyle\nabla\nabla S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},F_{i})

\displaystyle=F_{i}\nabla S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i})

\displaystyle=F_{i}\left(F_{i}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-S(\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}})\right)

\displaystyle=S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}-2\big[\nabla S(\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i})+S(\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})+S(\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}})\big]

\displaystyle=S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}-2S(\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}}).

Let

v=\left[(\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}})\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right]F_{\mathord{\xi}}=\beta F_{\mathord{\xi}}.

Since
S
is diagonal,

\displaystyle\nabla\nabla S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},F_{i})

\displaystyle=S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}-2S(\beta F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}-2\beta S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}.

Next compute derivatives of

\frac{S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}{g(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}.

The first derivative is:

F_{i}\left(\frac{S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}{g(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}\right)=\frac{S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}-g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}}{g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}}.

The second derivative is:

\displaystyle F_{i}F_{i}\left(\frac{S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}{g(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}\right)=\frac{S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}+S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}-g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}-g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}}{g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2(S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}-g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}})}{g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{3}}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},i}.

At the origin, this simplifies to

F_{i}F_{i}\frac{S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}{g(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}=S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}-g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}.

Next compute

\displaystyle g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}

\displaystyle=F_{i}F_{i}g(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=F_{i}\,2g(\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=2g(\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})+2g(\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=2g(\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\quad(\text{at origin})

\displaystyle=2g(\beta F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})=2\beta.

So we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq F_{i}F_{i}\frac{S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}{g(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})}

\displaystyle=S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},ii}-2\beta S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}

\displaystyle=\nabla\nabla S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},F_{i}).

Thus, the tensorial statement is

\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\leq 0.

∎

Next, we state the following proposition, which is based on
[
5
, Proposition 1]

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
L
be a Lagrangian submanifold and let
\hat{S}
be a tensor on the ambient manifold.
Denote by
S=\hat{S}|_{L}
its restriction to
L
. Suppose that at a point
p\in L
the induced metric
g
is diagonalized by a frame

\{F_{1},\dots,F_{n}\}
. Then at
p
we have

\displaystyle g^{mm}\nabla_{F_{m}}\nabla_{F_{m}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})+\hat{\nabla}_{\vec{H}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+4g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{mm}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}))

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{mm}\hat{S}\!\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}A)^{\perp}(F_{m},F_{m}),F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{mm}g^{kl}\hat{R}(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{m},KF_{k})\,\hat{S}(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad-2g^{mm}A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\cdot A(F_{m},F_{j})g^{ij}\,\hat{S}(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),

where
\{KF_{l}\}
denotes a basis for the normal space.

Proof.

The proof follows from the argument used in Proposition 1 of
[
5
]
. For
the convenience of the reader, we include the details here.

Take tangent vectors
\{V,X,Y,W\}
(assume these are in normal coordinates at
a point with respect to the induced metric):

\displaystyle\nabla_{V}S(X,Y)

\displaystyle=VS(X,Y)-S(\nabla_{V}X,Y)-S(X,\nabla_{V}Y)

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=V\hat{S}(X,Y)-\hat{S}(\hat{\nabla}_{V}X,Y)-S(X,\hat{\nabla}_{V}Y).

Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{V}S(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)+\hat{S}(\hat{\nabla}_{V}X,Y)+S(X,\hat{\nabla}_{V}Y)-S(\nabla_{V}X,Y)-S(X,\nabla_{V}Y)

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)+\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)+S(X,A(V,Y)).

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{W}\nabla_{V}S(X,Y)

\displaystyle=W\nabla_{V}S(X,Y)-\nabla_{\nabla_{W}V}S(X,Y)-\nabla_{V}S(\nabla_{W}X,Y)-\nabla_{V}S(X,\nabla_{W}Y)

\displaystyle=W\left[\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)+\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)+S(X,A(V,Y))\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\nabla_{\nabla_{W}V}S(X,Y)-\nabla_{V}S(\nabla_{W}X,Y)-\nabla_{V}S(X,\nabla_{W}Y)

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=W\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)-\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\nabla}_{W}V}\hat{S}(X,Y)-\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(\hat{\nabla}_{W}X,Y)-\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,\hat{\nabla}_{W}Y).

So

\displaystyle\nabla_{W}\nabla_{V}S(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\nabla}_{W}V}\hat{S}(X,Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(\hat{\nabla}_{W}X,Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,\hat{\nabla}_{W}Y)

\displaystyle\qquad+W\left[\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)+S(X,A(V,Y))\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\nabla_{\nabla_{W}V}S(X,Y)-\nabla_{V}S(\nabla_{W}X,Y)-\nabla_{V}S(X,\nabla_{W}Y)

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)

\displaystyle\quad+\left[\hat{\nabla}_{\hat{\nabla}_{W}V}\hat{S}-\nabla_{\nabla_{W}V}S\right](X,Y)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(\hat{\nabla}_{W}X,Y)-\nabla_{V}S(\nabla_{W}X,Y)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,\hat{\nabla}_{W}Y)-\nabla_{V}S(X,\nabla_{W}Y)

\displaystyle\quad+W\left[\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)+\hat{S}(X,A(V,Y))\right].

Since
\hat{\nabla}_{W}V=A(W,V)
, this becomes

\displaystyle\nabla_{W}\nabla_{V}S(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{A(W,V)}\hat{S}(X,Y)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(A(W,X),Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,A(W,Y))

\displaystyle\quad+W\left[\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)+\hat{S}(X,A(V,Y))\right].

Expanding the last term:

W\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)=\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)+\hat{S}\left(\left(\hat{\nabla}_{W}A\right)(V,X),Y\right)+\hat{S}(A(V,X),\hat{\nabla}_{W}Y).

Now

\left(\hat{\nabla}_{W}A\right)(V,X)=\nabla_{W}^{\perp}A(V,X)+g^{ij}h\left((\hat{\nabla}_{W}A)(V,X),F_{j}\right)F_{i}

and by the Codazzi equation

\nabla_{W}^{\perp}A(V,X)=\nabla_{X}^{\perp}A(W,V)+g^{kl}\hat{R}(W,X,V,KF_{k})KF_{l}

(here using the fact the
K
is a sign inverting isometry to the normal space,
giving a positive sign)

Also note that

h\left((\hat{\nabla}_{W}A)(V,X),F_{j}\right)+h\left(A(V,X),\hat{\nabla}_{W}F_{j}\right)=0

Thus

\displaystyle W\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)

\displaystyle+\hat{S}\left(\nabla_{X}^{\perp}A(W,V)+g^{kl}\hat{R}(W,X,V,KF_{k})KF_{l},Y\right)-g^{ij}\hat{S}\left(h\left(A(V,X),\hat{\nabla}_{W}F_{j}\right)F_{i},Y\right)

\displaystyle+\hat{S}(A(V,X),\hat{\nabla}_{W}Y).

and we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{W}\nabla_{V}S(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{A(W,V)}\hat{S}(X,Y)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(A(W,X),Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}(X,A(W,Y))

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{S}(A(V,X),Y)+\hat{\nabla}_{W}\hat{S}(X,A(V,Y))

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{S}(A(V,X),A(W,Y))+\hat{S}(A(W,X),A(V,Y))

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{S}\left(\nabla_{X}^{\perp}A(W,V)+g^{kl}\hat{R}(W,X,V,KF_{k})KF_{l},Y\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-g^{ij}\hat{S}\left(h\left(A(V,X),A(W,F_{j})\right)F_{i},Y\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{S}\!\Big(X,\left(\hat{\nabla}_{Y}A\right)^{\perp}(W,V)+\sum\hat{R}(W,Y,V,KF_{l})KF_{l}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-g^{ij}\hat{S}\left(X,h\left(A(V,Y),A(W,F_{j})\right)F_{i}\right)

Tracing over
V=W=F_{m}
, we obtain

\displaystyle g^{mm}\nabla_{F_{m}}\nabla_{F_{m}}S(X,Y)

\displaystyle=g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(X,Y)+g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{A(F_{m},F_{m})}\hat{S}(X,Y)

\displaystyle\quad+g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},X),Y)+g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(X,A(F_{m},Y))

\displaystyle\quad+g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},X),Y)+g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(X,A(F_{m},Y))

\displaystyle\quad+g^{mm}2\hat{S}(A(F_{m},X),A(F_{m},Y))

\displaystyle\quad+g^{mm}\hat{S}\!\Big(\left(\hat{\nabla}_{X}A\right)^{\perp}(F_{m},F_{m})+g^{kl}\hat{R}(F_{m},X,F_{m},KF_{k})KF_{l}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-A(F_{m},X)\cdot A(F_{m},F_{j})g^{ij}F_{i},\,Y\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+g^{mm}\hat{S}\!\Big(X,\left(\hat{\nabla}_{Y}A\right)^{\perp}(F_{m},F_{m})+g^{kl}\hat{R}(F_{m},Y,F_{m},KF_{k})KF_{l}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-A(F_{m},Y)\cdot A(F_{m},F_{j})g^{ij}F_{i}\Big).

Plugging in
X=Y=F_{\mathord{\xi}}
yields the result.

∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Along the generalized Lagrangian mean curvature flow,
at a point where
S
is diagonalized, the following holds:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}

\displaystyle-g^{mm}\nabla_{F_{m}}\nabla_{F_{m}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-n\hat{\nabla}_{\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-2n\hat{S}\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp})^{T},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-2n\hat{S}\left(\nabla_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}^{\perp}\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

Proof.

First, recall the formula for evolution in the normal direction
V

\frac{d}{dt}S_{ij}=\hat{\nabla}_{V}\hat{S}\left(F_{i},F_{j}\right)+\hat{S}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}V,F_{j}\right)+\hat{S}\left(F_{i},\hat{\nabla}_{F_{j}}V\right).

V=\vec{H}-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}.

Choosing

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}

Next, recall the formula

\displaystyle g^{mm}\nabla_{F_{m}}\nabla_{F_{m}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})+\hat{\nabla}_{\vec{H}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+4g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{mm}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}))

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{mm}\hat{S}\!\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}A)^{\perp}(F_{m},F_{m}),F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{mm}g^{kl}\hat{R}(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{m},KF_{k})\hat{S}\left(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2g^{mm}A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\cdot A(F_{m},F_{j})g^{ij}\hat{S}\left(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right).

Compute the difference:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}-g^{mm}\nabla_{F_{m}}\nabla_{F_{m}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{V}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-2n\hat{S}((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}(\hat{\nabla}\psi)^{\perp})^{T},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-2n\hat{S}((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}(\hat{\nabla}\psi)^{\perp})^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+2\hat{S}((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{T},F_{\mathord{\xi}})+2\hat{S}((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad-g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-\hat{\nabla}_{H}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad-4g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad-2g^{mm}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}))

\displaystyle\quad-2g^{mm}\hat{S}\!\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}A)^{\perp}(F_{m},F_{m}),F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2g^{mm}g^{kl}\hat{R}(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{m},KF_{k})\hat{S}(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{mm}A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\cdot A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}.

Finally, collecting terms and using that
S
is diagonalized, also noting that

g^{mm}\hat{S}\!\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}A)^{\perp}(F_{m},F_{m}),F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)=\hat{S}((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

the expression becomes

\displaystyle=-n\hat{\nabla}_{(\nabla\psi)^{\perp}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-2n\hat{S}((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}(\nabla\psi)^{\perp})^{T},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-2n\hat{S}(\nabla_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}^{\perp}(\nabla\psi)^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad-g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+2\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}H)^{T})\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}-4g^{mm}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{m}}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad-2g^{mm}\hat{S}(A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}}))

\displaystyle\quad-2g^{mm}g^{kl}\hat{R}(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{m},KF_{k})\hat{S}(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+2g^{mm}A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\cdot A(F_{m},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
The Maximum Principle Applied to the Slope Ratio

Suppose that
(x_{0},t_{0})
, with
x_{0}\in L
and
t_{0}\in(0,T)
, is a point at which the maximum of
\mathcal{R}
is attained. At this point and time we again choose a frame that diagonalizes the metric and the tensor
S
, so that

h(F_{i},F_{j})=\delta_{ij},

and

S(F_{i},F_{j})=\delta_{ij}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}+\lambda_{i}\right).

Let
\mathord{\xi}=1
or
\mathord{\xi}=n
be chosen so that

S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}+\lambda_{1},\,\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}+\lambda_{n}\right\}.

For simplicity, define the function

Q(x,t)=\frac{S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},t)}{g(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},t)}

in a neighborhood of
(x_{0},t_{0})
. We now state the following key claim.

Claim 4.7
.

There exist constants
\bar{C}>0
and
\kappa>0
such that the following holds.
If
S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}\geq 10
and
F_{\mathord{\xi}}
is the maximizer of the ratio
\mathcal{R}(F_{\mathord{\xi}})
, then at the maximum point we have

\partial_{t}Q(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\leq\bar{C}\,S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}-\kappa\,S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2+\frac{1}{n-1}}.

Proof.

We start by computing

\partial_{t}Q(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})=\partial_{t}\left(\frac{S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},t)}{g(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}},t)}\right)\Big|_{x_{0},t_{0}}=S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},t}-S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},t}.

We subtract the nonpositive quantity (nonpositive by Proposition
4.4
) to obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\leq\partial_{t}Q(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-\sum_{i}\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},t}-\sum_{i}\nabla_{F_{i}}\nabla_{F_{i}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},t}.

Next, we plug in (
4.4
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\leq-\hat{\nabla}_{n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-2\hat{S}\left(\nabla_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}^{\perp}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-\sum_{i}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad-4\sum_{i}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{S}(A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+2\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{T}\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-2\hat{S}\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp})^{T},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},t}

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{i}\hat{S}(A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}))-2\sum_{i,l}\hat{R}(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},KF_{l})\hat{S}(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{i}A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\cdot A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}.

We rearrange the above quantities as follows:

(1)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{i}A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\cdot A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}})\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}

(2)

\displaystyle\quad+2\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{T}\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-2\hat{S}\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n(\nabla\psi)^{\perp})^{T},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},t}

(3)

\displaystyle\quad-4\sum_{i}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{S}(A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),F_{\mathord{\xi}})\;-\;2\sum_{i}\hat{S}(A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}))

(4)

\displaystyle\quad-\hat{\nabla}_{n(\nabla\psi)^{\perp}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-\sum_{i}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

(5)

\displaystyle\quad-2\hat{S}\left(\nabla_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}^{\perp}n(\nabla\psi)^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{i,l}\hat{R}(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},KF_{l})\hat{S}(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

We will deal with these lines in order. For lines 1-5 we will show that each
expression is bounded by

C(1+S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2})

for some controlled constant
C.

Line 1:
Note that this is the sum of metric pairings of two time-like
vectors, so this is non positive.

Line 2:
Note that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}

\displaystyle=2h(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\left(\vec{H}-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}\right),F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=2(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{T}\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}-2\left(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}\right)^{T}\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}

and that

\hat{S}\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp})^{T},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)=(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp})^{T}\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}\,S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}}).

So

\displaystyle\quad+2\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{T}\!\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-2\hat{S}\!\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n(\nabla\psi)^{\perp})^{T},\,F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}g_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi},t}

\displaystyle=2\hat{S}_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{T}\!\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)-2\hat{S}\!\left((\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n(\nabla\psi)^{\perp})^{T},\,F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}2(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\vec{H})^{T}\!\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}+S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}2\left(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n(\nabla\psi)^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}\!\cdot F_{\mathord{\xi}}

\displaystyle=0.

Line 3:
Note that as a fixed tensor on the ambient manifold, we have

\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{S}(X,Y)\leq C_{1}\left\|F_{i}\right\|_{\hat{S}}\left\|X\right\|_{\hat{S}}\left\|Y\right\|_{\hat{S}},

so

-4\sum_{i}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{S}(A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),F_{\mathord{\xi}})\leq 2\sum_{i}\hat{S}(A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}))+2\sum_{i}C_{1}^{2}\hat{S}(F_{i},F_{i})\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

hence

\displaystyle-4\sum_{i}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{S}(A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),F_{\mathord{\xi}}-2\sum_{i}\hat{S}(A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}),A(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}}))

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i}C_{1}^{2}\hat{S}(F_{i},F_{i})\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle\leq CS_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}.

Line 4:

-\hat{\nabla}_{n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}}S(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})-\sum_{i}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{\nabla}_{F_{i}}\hat{S}(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}}).

Note that the first term involves a projection, so

\left\|n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}\right\|_{\hat{S}}=\left\|h(n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp},KF_{l})g^{lm}KF_{m}\right\|_{\hat{S}}\leq S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}.

This shows that the first term is bounded by
S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}.

The second is an ambient 4 tensor, so is bounded by
S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}.

Line 5:
This is Claim
4.9
below.

We finally claim that the contribution from line 6 is bounded above by

-\kappa S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2+\frac{1}{n-1}}+CS_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2},

for some constant
\kappa>0
, bounded away from zero,
depending on the cross-curvature condition.

To set this up, recall

m+\bar{m}=\delta_{ij}+\delta_{\bar{\imath}\bar{j}}

at the point, and the orthonormal basis with respect to
g
has the
expression

F_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i}+\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}E_{\bar{\imath}}.

Next, we observe the term

\sum_{i,l}\hat{R}(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},KF_{l})\hat{S}(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

and note that

\hat{S}(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}})=\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)\delta_{l\mathord{\xi}}.

and

\hat{R}(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},KF_{\mathord{\xi}})=-\frac{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}\hat{R}_{\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}}.

Thus (see Claim
4.8
below)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,l}\hat{R}(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},KF_{l})\hat{S}(KF_{l},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(-\frac{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}\hat{R}_{\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}+\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}^{2}\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}^{2}}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\hat{R}_{\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}}-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\hat{R}_{\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}}.

If
\mathord{\xi}=1
then the term

\lambda_{1}^{2}\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\bar{1}i\bar{1}}

dominates. Recall (here we use Corollary
4.3
)

\det DT=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}=\frac{e^{-2\theta}\rho}{\bar{\rho}}\leq\Lambda_{0}

so

\Lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{n})^{\,n-1}

and hence

\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\geq\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)^{\!\frac{1}{n-1}}.

Thus

\lambda_{1}^{2}\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\bar{1}i\bar{1}}\geq\lambda_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)^{\!\frac{1}{n-1}}\hat{R}_{n\bar{1}n\bar{1}}\geq\kappa S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2+\frac{1}{n-1}}-C.

Of the remaining terms,

\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}^{2}}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\hat{R}_{\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}\overline{\imath}\mathord{\xi}}

is positive, and the other two are bounded by
\sum_{i}S_{ii}.

If
\mathord{\xi}=n,
then

\det DT=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}=\frac{e^{-2\theta}\rho}{\bar{\rho}}\geq\frac{1}{\Lambda_{0}}

so

\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{1}{\Lambda_{0}(\lambda_{n})^{\,n-1}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\hat{R}_{\bar{\imath}n\bar{\imath}n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Lambda_{0}(\lambda_{n})^{\,n-1}}\hat{R}_{\bar{1}n\bar{1}n}

\displaystyle\geq\kappa S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2+\frac{1}{n-1}}-C.

∎

Claim 4.8
.

\hat{R}(F_{i},F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{i},KF_{\mathord{\xi}})=-\frac{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}\hat{R}_{\overline{i}\mathord{\xi}\overline{i}\mathord{\xi}}.

Proof.

This can be verified by expanding

\displaystyle\hat{R}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i}+\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}E_{\bar{i}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}}E_{\mathord{\xi}}+\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}E_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i}+\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}E_{\bar{i}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}}E_{\mathord{\xi}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}E_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{R}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i},\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}E_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i},-\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}E_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\hat{R}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i},\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}E_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}},\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}E_{\bar{i}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}}E_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle+\hat{R}\!\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}E_{\bar{i}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}}E_{\mathord{\xi}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}E_{i},-\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}E_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\hat{R}\!\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}E_{\bar{i}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}}E_{\mathord{\xi}},\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}E_{\bar{i}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}}E_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}+\hat{R}_{i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\bar{i}\mathord{\xi}}-\hat{R}_{\bar{i}\mathord{\xi}i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}\hat{R}_{\bar{i}\mathord{\xi}\bar{i}\mathord{\xi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}{\lambda_{i}}\hat{R}_{i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}i\overline{\mathord{\xi}}}+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}\hat{R}_{\bar{i}\mathord{\xi}\bar{i}\mathord{\xi}}.

∎

Claim 4.9
.

2\hat{S}\left(\nabla_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}^{\perp}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)\leq\left\|F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right\|_{S}\left\|\left(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}\right\|_{\hat{S}}.

Proof.

Unwinding

-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}=\left(n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot KF_{i}\right)g^{ij}KF_{j}

and

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\left[\left(n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot KF_{i}\right)g^{ij}KF_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=F_{\mathord{\xi}}\left(\left(n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot KF_{i}\right)g^{ij}\right)KF_{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot KF_{i}\right)g^{ij}K\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}F_{j}.

Projecting this to the normal direction, we note that
\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}F_{j}

is already normal at the point, so
K
projects that to the tangential direction,
and this term doesn’t survive the projection. We are left with

\hat{S}\left(\nabla_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}^{\perp}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)=-F_{\mathord{\xi}}\left(\left(n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot KF_{i}\right)g^{ij}\right)\hat{S}\left(KF_{j},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right).

We have taken diagonalizations so that only the

term survives, so suffice to control

F_{\mathord{\xi}}\left(n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot KF_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)\hat{S}\left(KF_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right).

Next compute

(4.5)

F_{\mathord{\xi}}\left(n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot KF_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)=\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot KF_{\mathord{\xi}}+n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot K\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}F_{\mathord{\xi}}.

To get control of

\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}F_{\mathord{\xi}}

observe that

(4.6)

\left(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)^{\perp}=A(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})

is tensorial, so we may compute using

\displaystyle F_{\mathord{\xi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}}\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}}\text{ at }x,

\displaystyle\left(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=A(F_{\mathord{\xi}},F_{\mathord{\xi}})=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}A(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}\left(A(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})\cdot K\widetilde{F}_{k}\right)\widetilde{g}^{kk}K\widetilde{F}_{k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}\left(A(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{k})\cdot K\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)\widetilde{g}^{kk}K\widetilde{F}_{k}.

Recall that

\widetilde{F}_{i}=E_{i}+T_{i}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}}.

Compute directly

\displaystyle A(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{k})

\displaystyle=\hat{\nabla}_{\left(E_{\mathord{\xi}}+T_{\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}}\right)}\left(E_{k}+T_{k}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}+T_{\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}}\Gamma_{\bar{s}k}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}+T_{k\,\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}}+T_{k}^{s}\Gamma_{\bar{s}\,\mathord{\xi}}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}+T_{k}^{\bar{p}}(E_{\bar{p}}T_{\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}})E_{\bar{s}}+T_{\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}}T_{k}^{\bar{p}}\Gamma_{\bar{p}\bar{s}}^{\alpha}E_{\alpha}.

After simplification,

A(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{k})=\Gamma_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{m}E_{m}+T_{k\,\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}}.

Thus

\displaystyle A(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{k})\cdot K\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}}

\displaystyle=\left(\Gamma_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{m}E_{m}+T_{k\,\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}}E_{\bar{s}}\right)\cdot(E_{\mathord{\xi}}-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}E_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}})

\displaystyle=T_{k\,\mathord{\xi}}^{\mathord{\xi}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}c_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,\mathord{\xi}}\right)-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}\Gamma_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\mathord{\xi}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}c_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,\mathord{\xi}}\right)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{k\,\mathord{\xi}}^{\mathord{\xi}}-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}\Gamma_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\mathord{\xi}}\right)

Now differentiate

\frac{S(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})}{h(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})}

in any
E_{k}
direction, using

S(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})=m_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}+\bar{m}_{\bar{s}\bar{p}}T_{\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}}T_{\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{p}},\qquad h(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})=-c_{\mathord{\xi}\bar{s}}T_{\mathord{\xi}}^{\bar{s}},

(recalling we’re using special coordinates type II (recall Corollary
3.5
) and evaluating at the point) gives

\displaystyle E_{k}S(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=\bar{m}_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,\overline{\mathord{\xi}},k}\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}^{2}+2\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}T_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\mathord{\xi}},

\displaystyle E_{k}h(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})

\displaystyle=-c_{\mathord{\xi}\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,k}\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}+T_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\bar{\mathord{\xi}}}.

Using that the maximum occurs at this point,

h(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})E_{k}S(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})=S(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})E_{k}h(\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}},\widetilde{F}_{\mathord{\xi}})

we obtain

\left(2\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}-(1+\lambda^{2}_{\mathord{\xi}})\right)T_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\mathord{\xi}}=-(1+\lambda^{2}_{\mathord{\xi}})c_{\mathord{\xi}\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,k}\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}-\bar{m}_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,\overline{\mathord{\xi}},k}\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}^{3},

hence

T_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\mathord{\xi}}=\frac{-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}\left((1+\lambda^{2}_{\mathord{\xi}})c_{\mathord{\xi}\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,k}-\bar{m}_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,\overline{\mathord{\xi}},k}\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}^{2}\right)}{-(1-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}})^{2}}.

From (
4.7
) and (
4.6
)

\displaystyle\left(\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}\left(\frac{1}{2}(T_{k\,\mathord{\xi}}^{\mathord{\xi}}-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}\Gamma_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\mathord{\xi}})\right)\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}K\widetilde{F}_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}}T_{k\,\mathord{\xi}}^{\mathord{\xi}}-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\mathord{\xi}}\right)\frac{(E_{k}-\lambda_{k}E_{\bar{k}})}{\lambda_{k}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}\left((1+\lambda^{2}_{\mathord{\xi}})c_{\mathord{\xi}\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,k}-\bar{m}_{\overline{\mathord{\xi}}\,\overline{\mathord{\xi}},k}\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}}^{2}\right)}{(1-\lambda_{\mathord{\xi}})^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{\mathord{\xi}\,k}^{\mathord{\xi}}\right)\frac{(E_{k}-\lambda_{k}E_{\bar{k}})}{\lambda_{k}}.

Thus (we may assume that
\lambda_{n}<\frac{1}{2}
))

\left|n\left(\nabla\psi\right)\cdot K\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right|\leq\|nD\psi\|_{\hat{S}}C\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{n}}\right)\leq C\left(S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}+1\right).

Finally, recall (
4.5
)

\hat{S}\left(\left[\hat{\nabla}_{F_{\mathord{\xi}}}\left(n(\nabla\psi)\right)^{\perp}\right]^{\perp},F_{\mathord{\xi}}\right)\leq\|nD\psi\|_{\hat{S}}\|F_{\mathord{\xi}}\|_{\hat{S}}^{2}+C\left(S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}+1\right).

∎

We conclude that there is a universal bound on
DT
as shown below.

Proposition 4.10
.

Suppose that
(x,T(x))
locally represents the generalized mean curvature flow.
Assume that the flow remains in a compact set
Z
that avoids the cut locus and on which the cross-curvature condition is positive.
Then

\left\|DT\right\|=\sup_{m(V,V)=1}\left\|DT(V)\right\|_{\bar{m}}\leq C(Z,\kappa,c,\rho,\bar{\rho},F_{0}).

Proof.

At the initial time there is an initial bound on
DT.
Observe that by taking
normal coordinates for
m
and diagonalizing

\widetilde{F}_{i}=E_{i}+\lambda_{i}E_{\bar{\imath}}

we get

\sup_{m(V,V)=1}\left\|DT(V)\right\|_{\bar{m}}^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}^{2}.

Recall that

\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\geq\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

so if
\lambda_{1}
is large we will have

S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}},\lambda_{1}\right\}>C_{2}

big enough so that

C_{1}S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2}-\kappa S_{\mathord{\xi}\mathord{\xi}}^{2+\frac{1}{n-1}}<0

in which case, the ratio given by (
4.3
) must be decreasing at the maximum
point by the previous Proposition. An upper bound on the ratio gives an upper bound on
\lambda_{1}
. ∎

5.
Higher Regularity and Convergence

5.1.
Preliminary Estimates

For the remainder, we assume the submanifold is flowing under a cross-curvature condition.

Recalling (
2.12
), we note that locally there exists a scalar function
u
satisfying

Du+D_{x}c(x,T(x))=0.

Claim 5.1
.

Let
u
be as in (
2.12
). The GMCF is the projection of a
vertical flow described by

u_{t}=-2\theta.

Proof.

Since
\theta
is geometric, we can choose any coordinates for
\bar{M}
and
verify this at that point. We compute the vertical change of
T
by
differentiating

\displaystyle u_{i}+c_{i}(x,T(x))

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle u_{it}+c_{i\bar{s}}(x,T(x))T_{t}^{\bar{s}}

\displaystyle=0.

So at the origin, in special coordinates, we have

T_{t}^{\bar{s}}=-2\theta_{s}.

That is for the vertical flow

\frac{d}{dt}(x,T(x,t))=(0,-2D\theta).

Project this to the normal direction.

\displaystyle(0,D\theta)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=-\left((0,-2D\theta)\cdot K\tilde{F}_{i}\right)\tilde{g}^{ij}K\tilde{F}_{j}

\displaystyle=\theta_{i}\tilde{g}^{ij}(KF_{j})

\displaystyle=K\left(\theta_{i}\tilde{g}^{ij}F_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=K\nabla\theta

\displaystyle=(\vec{H}-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}).

∎

Observe that differentiating, we get

(5.1)

u_{ij}+c_{ij}+c_{i\bar{s}}(x,T(x))T_{j}^{\bar{s}}=0

or

\det(u_{ij}+c_{ij})=\det(-c_{i\bar{s}})\det T_{j}^{\bar{s}}.

Recall

\displaystyle 2\theta

\displaystyle=\ln\rho-\ln\bar{\rho}-\ln\det DT

\displaystyle=\ln\rho-\ln\bar{\rho}-\ln\det(u_{ij}+c_{ij})+\ln\det(-c_{i\bar{s}})

so plugging in, we get

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=-2\theta.

\displaystyle=\ln\det(u_{ij}+c_{ij})-\ln\rho+\ln\bar{\rho}-\ln\det(-c_{i\bar{s}}).

Thus
u
locally solves a Monge-Ampère type equation, and enjoys
C^{2}

estimates (from the estimate on
DT
and (
5.1
)) in choices of coordinates. On a compact manifold, every domain is
interior, and by the Evans-Krylov theorem, we conclude that
u
enjoys
C^{2,\alpha}

estimates. Then, by Schauder theory, we obtain uniform bounds of
all orders.

At this stage, we have uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
bounds for
T
up to time
t_{0}
.
Because the coefficients of the parabolic system are uniformly parabolic and Hölder continuous, Schauder theory applies and yields bounds on derivatives of all orders. In particular, the flow extends past any time
t_{0}
, and global
C^{k,\alpha}
bounds hold for all
k
.

Once we have fixed a set of charts, interior estimates will apply along the flow.
We have shown:

Claim 5.2
.

Suppose that
S
is bounded above along the flow on
[0,t_{0})
. Then all higher-order derivatives exist and the flow extends past
t_{0}
.

5.2.
Harnack Inequality and Exponential Convergence

Recalling claim
4.2
, we note that
\theta
satisfies

\theta_{t}-\Delta_{-n\psi}\theta=0

where
\Delta_{-n\psi}
is the weighted Laplacian. Note that this flow tracks the change of
\theta
as
x
evolves under the normal flow. This is different from the vertical flow, which produces a similar weighted operator. Note that the vertical and normal evolution expressions for a quantity will always differ by a gradient term for that quantity.

Given the bounds on third derivatives of
T
, we obtain a lower bound on the weighted Ricci curvature associated with the operator
\Delta_{-n\psi}
. We are therefore exactly in the setting of
[
1
, Prop. 2.7]
. The argument given there applies directly, and we conclude the same by defining

f=\log\left(\theta+c\right)

for an appropriate
c
and

F=t\left(\left\|\nabla_{g}f\right\|_{g}^{2}-\alpha\partial_{t}f\right).

So we get (for
\alpha=2)

LF+2\langle\nabla f,\nabla F\rangle\geq\frac{1}{t}\left(C_{1}F^{2}-F-C_{2}t^{2}+C_{3}t\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{g}^{2}F\right).

Applying this at the maximum for
F
we get

C_{1}F^{2}-F-C_{2}t^{2}+C_{3}t\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{g}^{2}F\leq 0

that is

C_{1}F^{2}-F-C_{2}t^{2}\leq 0

or

F(x,t)\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4C_{1}C_{2}t^{2}}}{2C_{1}}\leq\tilde{C}_{1}+\tilde{C}_{2}t.

That is, for positive
t

\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}-2f_{t}\right)\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t}+\tilde{C}_{2}

so we have

f_{t}\geq\frac{\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t}+\tilde{C}_{2}\right).

Now consider a path
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow L\times[t_{1},t_{2}]
such
that

\displaystyle\gamma(0)

\displaystyle=(y,t_{2})

\displaystyle\gamma(1)

\displaystyle=\left(x,t_{1}\right).

Assume that the path projects to a geodesic in the metric on
L
at
t_{1}
with
constant speed. Then

f(x,t_{1})-f\left(y,t_{2}\right)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\left|\nabla f(\gamma(s),s)\right|d_{(t_{1})}(x,y)+\left(t_{1}-t_{2}\right)f_{t}(\gamma(s),s)\right\}ds.

As
t_{1}<t_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(t_{1}-t_{2}\right)f_{t}(\gamma(s),s)

\displaystyle\leq\left(t_{1}-t_{2}\right)\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}-\left(\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t}+\tilde{C}_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}-\left(\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t}+\tilde{C}_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\left(-\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t}+\tilde{C}_{2}\right)\right)

and

f(x,t_{1})-f\left(y,t_{2}\right)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\left|\nabla f(\gamma(s),s)\right|d_{(t_{1})}(x,y)+\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\left(-\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t(s)}+\tilde{C}_{2}\right)\right)\right\}ds.

Using

\left|\nabla f(\gamma(s),s)\right|d_{(t_{1})}(x,y)\leq\left|\nabla f(\gamma(s),s)\right|^{2}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)+\frac{d_{(t_{1})}^{2}(x,y)}{4\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)}

we have

\displaystyle f(x,t_{1})-f\left(y,t_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\frac{d_{(t_{1})}^{2}(x,y)}{4\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)}+\left|\nabla f(\gamma(s),s)\right|^{2}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)-\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)+\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\left(\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t(s)}+\tilde{C}_{2}\right)\right\}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\frac{d_{(t_{1})}^{2}(x,y)}{4\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)}+\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\left(\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t(s)}+\tilde{C}_{2}\right)\right\}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d_{(t_{1})}^{2}(x,y)}{4\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)}+\tilde{C}_{2}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)-\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}}{t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{(t_{1})}^{2}(x,y)}{4\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)}+\tilde{C}_{2}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)+\tilde{C}_{1}\log\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right)

noting that

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{ds}

\displaystyle=-\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)ds

\displaystyle t(0)

\displaystyle=t_{2}

\displaystyle t(1)

\displaystyle=t_{1}.

This gives us that

\log\left(\theta+c\right)(x,t_{1})\leq\log\left(\theta+c\right)(y,t_{2})+\frac{d_{(t_{1})}^{2}(x,y)}{4\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)}+\tilde{C}_{2}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)+\tilde{C}_{1}\log\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right)

which we can exponentiate to get

\left(\theta+c\right)(x,t_{1})\leq\left(\theta+c\right)(y,t_{2})\exp\left(\frac{d_{(t_{1})}^{2}(x,y)}{4\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)}+\tilde{C}_{2}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\right)\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right)^{\tilde{C}_{1}}.

If we assume that
\theta>0
we can let
c\rightarrow 0

\theta(x,t_{1})\leq\theta(y,t_{2})\exp\left(\frac{\text{diam}_{g_{t_{1}}}^{2}\text{(}M)}{4\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)}+\tilde{C}_{2}\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\right)\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right)^{\tilde{C}_{1}}.

Thus for
t_{1}\geq 1
and
t_{2}=t_{1}+1/2
we have

\theta(x,t_{1})\leq C_{3}\theta(y,t_{2})

for all
x,y.

Now we may apply the standard convergence argument - see for example
[
18
, Section 7.1]
.
It follows that the oscillation of
\theta
decreases exponential to a
constant. The convergence of the flow is in all orders, so we conclude that the limit as

t\rightarrow\infty
must be a manifold satisfying

D\theta\equiv 0

that is

\vec{H}-n\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{\perp}=0.
