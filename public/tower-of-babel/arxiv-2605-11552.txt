Structure of Torus Fibration Under the First Betti Number Restriction
Xin Peng, Bing Wang, Zhenjian Wang

Abstract
. We study torus bundles with affine structure groups. First, we establish a rigidity result under constraints on the first Betti numbers: If b 1 ​ ( M ) − b 1 ​ ( N ) = dim M − dim N \text{b}_{1}(M)-\text{b}_{1}(N)=\dim M-\dim N holds for a torus bundle M M with an affine structure group over a closed manifold N N , then M M can be classified. Second, we obtain some necessary and sufficient conditions for the topological splitting of principal torus bundles. These results improve the understanding of the geometry of collapsing sequences under the first Betti number constraints, thereby extending the prior work by Huang-Wang [ 15 ] .

Theorem 5.1

Under the assumption of Theorem

\Deck(\hat{M}/M)=\{(\alpha,\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}+\id_{T^{m-k}})|~\alpha\in\Deck(\hat{N}/N)\},

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}(\beta(x))-T_{\alpha}(x)

Lemma 5.2

The global torus action on

Proof.

For any

and

Let

(5.4)

\displaystyle\text{pr}_{M}(\text{Ad}(g)(\hat{\alpha})(y,g^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\text{pr}_{M}(g^{-1}\circ\hat{\alpha}\circ g(y,g^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\cdot\text{pr}_{M}(\hat{\alpha}\circ g(y,g^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\cdot\text{pr}_{M}(g(y,g^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\cdot g\cdot\text{pr}_{M}(y,g^{\prime})=\text{pr}_{M}(y,g^{\prime}).

By the continuity of
\text{Ad}(g)(\hat{\alpha})
and the connectedness of

\text{Ad}(g)(\hat{\alpha})\in\Deck(\hat{M}/M)

(5.5)

Since

which shows that the actions commute.
∎

\displaystyle=(\alpha(y),\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}(y)+g+g^{\prime})

\displaystyle=g\cdot(\alpha(y),\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}(y)+g^{\prime})

\displaystyle=g\cdot(\alpha,\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}+\id_{T^{m-k}})\cdot(y,g^{\prime}),

which shows that the torus action commutes with
\Deck(\hat{N}\times T^{m-k}/M)
.

We define a smooth fibration
f:M\to N
by

f(\text{pr}_{M}(y,g^{\prime})):=\text{pr}_{N}(y)
for
y\in\hat{N}
and
g^{\prime}\in T^{m-k}
. This map is well-defined. Moreover, the commutativity established above guarantees that
M
is a principal
T^{m-k}
-bundle over
N
;

(2)

For all
\alpha,\beta\in\Deck(\hat{N}/N)
, the commutativity of
\Deck(\hat{N}/N)
and the cocycle condition imply:

(5.28)

\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\beta}(\alpha(x))+\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}(x)=\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}(\beta(x))+\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\beta}(x).

Hence, the function
T_{\beta}(\alpha(x))+T_{\alpha}(x)-T_{\alpha}(\beta(x))-T_{\beta}(x)
takes values in
\mathbb{Z}^{m-k}
. By the continuity of
\{T_{\alpha}\}
, there exists a constant
\vec{a}_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m-k}
such that

(5.29)

T_{\beta}(\alpha(x))+T_{\alpha}(x)-T_{\alpha}(\beta(x))-T_{\beta}(x)=\vec{a}_{\alpha,\beta}

for any
x\in\hat{N}
.

Suppose that
\alpha^{L_{1}}=\beta^{L_{2}}=\id_{\hat{N}}
. Fix a basepoint
x_{0}\in\hat{N}
. For any
x^{\prime}\in\hat{N}
, consider the points
x^{\prime},\alpha(x^{\prime}),\dots,\alpha^{L_{1}-1}(x^{\prime})
. Substituting these into equation (
5.29
) and summing over
j=0
to
L_{1}-1
yields

(5.30)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{L_{1}-1}[T_{\alpha}(\alpha^{j}(x^{\prime}))-T_{\alpha}(\beta\cdot\alpha^{j}(x^{\prime}))]=L_{1}\vec{a}_{\alpha,\beta},~~\forall x^{\prime}\in\hat{N}.

Now take
x^{\prime}=x_{0},\beta(x_{0}),\dots,\beta^{L_{2}-1}(x_{0})
. Substituting each of these into equation
(\ref{eqn 5.30})
and summing over
l=0
to
L_{2}-1
gives

(5.31)

0=\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{L_{2}-1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{L_{1}-1}[T_{\alpha}(\beta^{l}\cdot\alpha^{j}(x_{0}))-T_{\alpha}(\beta^{l+1}\cdot\alpha^{j}(x_{0}))]=L_{2}L_{1}\vec{a}_{\alpha,\beta}.

Therefore,
\vec{a}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
, and the commutativity condition follows;

(3)

Let
\text{pr}_{M}:\hat{N}\times T^{m-k}\to M
be
the covering (quotient) map. By Property
2.7
(2)(a),
\text{pr}_{M}
is a normal map. We first verify that
\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}
is a normal covering space of
M
.

For any
x\in M
and any
(y_{1},g_{1}),(y_{2},g_{2})\in(\text{pr}_{M}\circ(\id_{\hat{N}}\times\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}))^{-1}(x)
, the normality of the covering space
\hat{N}\times T^{m-k}
implies the existence of an element
(\alpha,\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}+\id_{T^{m-k}})
such that
(\alpha,\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}+\id_{T^{m-k}})(y_{1},\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}(g_{1}))=(y_{2},\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}(g_{2}))
. By Proposition
2.5
, there exists a lift
\alpha^{\prime\prime}:\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}\to\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}
of
(\alpha,\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}+\id_{T^{m-k}})
satisfying:

(5.32)

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime\prime}(y_{1},g_{1})

\displaystyle=(y_{2},g_{2}),

\displaystyle(\id_{\hat{N}}\times\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}})\circ\alpha^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=(\alpha,\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}+\id_{T^{m-k}})\circ(\id_{\hat{N}}\times\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}).

Thus,
\text{pr}_{M}\circ(\id_{\hat{N}}\times\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}})\circ\alpha^{\prime\prime}=\text{pr}_{M}\circ(\id_{\hat{N}}\times\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}})
and
\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in\Deck(\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}/M)
, confirming that
\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}
is a normal covering of
M
.

We next show that:

(5.33)

\Deck(\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}/M)=\{(\alpha,T_{\alpha}+\vec{a}+\id_{\mathbb{R}^{m-k}})|~\alpha\in\Deck(\hat{N}/N),\vec{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m-k}\}.

Lemma
5.5
provides a family of smooth maps
TT_{\alpha}:\hat{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{m-k}
indexed by
\alpha\in\Deck(\hat{N}/N)
satisfying (
5.14
) with
\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ TT_{\alpha}=\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ T_{\alpha}
. Thus,
TT_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}
is constant in
\mathbb{Z}^{m-k}
; after replacing
TT_{\alpha}
by
T_{\alpha}
, we obtain the desired form (
5.33
).

Finally, the commutativity condition forces
\Deck(\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}/M)
to be abelian;

(4)

Fix a base point
y_{0}\in\hat{N}
. Define a fiber embedding map
l:T^{m-k}\to M
by
l(g):=\text{pr}_{M}(y_{0},g)
for any
g\in T^{m-k}
. Let
l_{y_{0}}:T^{m-k}\to\hat{N}\times T^{m-k}
be given by
l_{y_{0}}(g):=(y_{0},g)
. Then,
l=\text{pr}_{M}\circ l_{y_{0}}
,

is an injective homomorphism:

(5.34)

l_{*}:\pi_{1}(T^{m-k})\xhookrightarrow{(l_{y_{0}})_{*}}\pi_{1}(\hat{N}\times T^{m-k})\xhookrightarrow{\text{Pr}_{M}}\pi_{1}(M).

Combining this with Theorem
2.2
, we get
\operatorname{Im}(\partial_{1})=\Ker(l_{*})=\{e\}
, yielding the short exact sequence (
1.4
) of fundamental groups.

Consider the exact sequence of covering spaces:

(5.35)

0\xrightarrow{}\pi_{1}(\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k})\xrightarrow{\text{Pr}_{\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}/M}}\pi_{1}(M)\xrightarrow{\text{Pr}_{\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}/M}^{*}}\Deck(\hat{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-k}/M)\xrightarrow{}0~.

By part (3), for any

Together with diagram (

) in homology;

(5)

From part (4), we deduce:

(5.37)

\text{b}_{1}(M)-\text{b}_{1}(N)=\text{b}_{1}(T^{m-k})=m-k~.

6.
Further Discussions

In this section, we outline several related results and open questions concerning Betti number bounds and topological splitting in broader geometric and topological settings.

6.1.
Geometric Generalizations

We present supplementary results on the upper bounds of the first Betti number in various geometric contexts.

Theorem 6.1

(Asymptotic Cones Case, see
[
24
, Theorem A]
)
.

Given an open manifold
(M^{m},g)
with nonnegative Ricci curvature, suppose that an asymptotic cone of
M
is properly contained in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
(where
k\geq 1
). Then:

(1)

The first Betti number satisfies
\text{b}_{1}(M)\leq m-k
;

(2)

If equality holds, then
M
is flat and isometric to either

•

\mathbb{R}^{k}\times T^{m-k}
, or

•

\mathbb{R}^{k-1}\times N^{m-k+1}
, where
N
is also flat and diffeomorphic to the product of an open M
\ddot{o}
bius band or
T^{m-k-1}
.

Theorem 6.2

(General Ricci Limit Spaces, see
[
31
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Assume that there is a sequence of closed manifolds
(M_{i}^{m},g_{i})
with
\Ric(g_{i})\geq-(m-1)
,
\diam(M_{i})\leq D
and
\text{b}_{1}(M_{i})\geq r
. Suppose that this sequence converges to
(X,d)
in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense. If
(X,d)
has a k-regular point, then:

(6.1)

r-\text{b}_{1}(X)\leq m-k.

We recall some known estimates for higher Betti numbers; see
[
23
, Theorem 9.4.6]
.

Theorem 6.3

(Upper Bounded Estimation of Higher-Order Betti Numbers)
.

Given a positive integer
m
and nonnegative constants
k
and
D
, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
m
and
kD^{2}
, such that for any closed manifold
(M^{m},g)
with
\mathcal{R}m\geq-k\cdot g\odot g
and
\diam(M)\leq D
, the
i
-th Betti number satisfies:

(6.2)

\text{b}_{i}(M)\leq\binom{m}{i}\exp(C(n,kD^{2})(kD^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}).

When
k=0
, the classification theorem for compact manifolds with nonnegative curvature operator, established by Meyer and Gallot
[
23
, Theorem 10.3.7]
, provides the equality condition.

Theorem 6.4
.

For any compact manifold
(M^{m},g)
with a nonnegative curvature operator (
\mathcal{R}m\geq 0
), if the
i
-th Betti number satisfies
\text{b}_{i}(M)=\binom{m}{i}
for some
i\in\{1,2,\dots,m-1\}
, then
M
is diffeomorphic to a torus.

We propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 6.5
.

Given a positive integer
m
, there exists an
\epsilon(m)>0
such that for any compact manifold
(M^{m},g)
with diameter
\diam(M)=1
, if the curvature operator
\mathcal{R}m\geq-\epsilon g\odot g
, then the i-th Betti number satisfies
\text{b}_{i}(M)\leq\binom{m}{i}
. Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
M
is diffeomorphic to a torus.

Appendix A
Bijection Between Smooth and Topological Principal Bundles

In this appendix, we review the theorem on the one-to-one correspondence between smooth principal bundles and topological principal bundles with torus structure group (here, a smooth principal bundle means that the total space, fibration map, structure group, and local trivializations are all smooth), along with its proof, where the method of proof employs the Poincar
\acute{e}
lemma in
\check{C}
ech cohomology. We include an elementary proof here for completeness and we do not claim any originality for this part.

Theorem A.1

(See
[
25
, Section 4.3 in Preliminaries]
)
.

Let
N^{k}
be a closed
k
-dimensional smooth manifold and
T^{m-k}
an
(m-k)
-dimensional torus. Denote by
\operatorname{Prin}^{\text{Smooth}}_{T^{m-k}}(N)
the set of smooth equivalence classes of smooth principal
T^{m-k}
-bundles over
N
, and denote by
\operatorname{Prin}^{\text{Top}}_{T^{m-k}}(N)
the set of topological equivalence classes of topological principal
T^{m-k}
-bundles over
N
. Then the natural map

(A.1)

\displaystyle\Phi:\operatorname{Prin}^{\text{Smooth}}_{T^{m-k}}(N)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Prin}^{\text{Top}}_{T^{m-k}}(N),

\displaystyle M^{\text{Smooth}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\text{Topological bundle structure on }M^{\text{Smooth}}

is a bijection.

We adopt the following notations, along with a brief explanation of the rationale behind some definitions.

\bullet

\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}:\mathbb{R}^{m-k}\to T^{m-k}
denotes the standard universal covering map of
T^{m-k}
;

The element
[0]
denotes the identity of
T^{m-k}
;

The metric
g_{\text{Eucl}}
denotes the standard flat product metric on
T^{m-k}=\displaystyle\prod_{1}^{m-k}S^{1}
;

d
denotes the distance function induced by
g_{\text{Eucl}}
;

\inj(T^{m-k},g_{\text{Eucl}})
denotes the injectivity radius of
g_{\text{Eucl}}
;

\bullet

Choose a small positive number
\epsilon<\frac{\inj(T^{m-k},g_{\text{Eucl}})}{10000}
such that the restriction

(A.2)

\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}|_{B(0,100\epsilon)}:B(0,100\epsilon)\to B([0],100\epsilon)

admits a smooth inverse map
\log_{T^{m-k}}
near
[0]\in T^{m-k}
and
\log_{T^{m-k}}([0])=0
;

\bullet

By compactness of
N
, we can choose a finite open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
U_{i}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We choose a partition of unity
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in I}
subordinate to
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}

such that:

(A.3)

\supp(\rho_{i})\subset U_{i},~~\text{and}\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\rho_{i}=1;

\bullet

For any continuous (resp. smooth) map
A:U_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{m-k}
, we obtain a well-defined continuous (resp. smooth) map

(A.4)

\rho_{i}\cdot A:N\to\mathbb{R}^{m-k}

on
N
;

\bullet

Let
\{g_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}
with
g_{ij}:U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to G
be the family of continuous (resp. smooth) transition maps corresponding to the topological (resp. smooth) principal bundle
M
over
N
. They satisfy
g_{ii}=[0]
and the cocycle condition:

(A.5)

g_{ij}+g_{jl}+g_{li}=[0]

on
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\cap U_{l}
;

\bullet

M_{1}\cong_{\text{Top bundle iso}}M_{2}
denotes the topological equivalence of topological bundle
M_{1},M_{2}
over
N
.

M_{1}\cong_{\text{Diffeo bundle iso}}M_{2}
denotes the smooth equivalence of smooth bundle
M_{1},M_{2}
over
N
.

We will need the following three lemmas.

Lemma A.2

(Corollary of Whitney’s Approximation Theorem
[
30
, Section 6.7]
)
.

Let
U
be a manifold. For any continuous map
A:U\to T^{m-k}
, there exists a smooth map
A^{\prime}:U\to T^{m-k}
such that
d(A(x),A^{\prime}(x))<\epsilon
for all
x\in U
.

Lemma A.3

(See
[
30
, Section 2, Lemma 2.10]
)
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group and
N
a compact manifold. For two topological (resp. smooth) principal
G
-bundles
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
over
N
, let
\{g^{(a)}_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}
(
a=1,2
,
g^{(a)}_{ij}:U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to G
) be the family of continuous (resp. smooth) transition maps corresponding to
M_{a}
.
Then
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are topological (resp. smooth) bundle isomorphic if and only if there exists a family of continuous (resp. smooth) maps
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in I}
(
\lambda_{i}:U_{i}\to G
) such that

(A.6)

g^{(1)}_{ij}=(\lambda_{i})^{-1}\cdot g^{(2)}_{ij}\cdot\lambda_{j}.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be two topological principal
T^{m-k}
-bundles over
N
and
\{g^{(a)}_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}
(
a=1,2
,
g^{(a)}_{ij}:U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to G
) the family of transition maps corresponding to
M_{a}
.

(1)

If

for all
i,j\in I
, then
M_{1}\cong_{\text{Top bundle iso}}M_{2}
;

(2)

If
g^{(a)}_{ij}
(
i,j\in I
,
a=1,2
) are all smooth and
M_{1}\cong_{\text{Top bundle iso}}M_{2}
, then
M_{1}\cong_{\text{Diffeo bundle iso}}M_{2}
.

Proof.

(1)

Denote

(A.7)

\phi_{ij}:=g^{(1)}_{ij}-g^{(2)}_{ij}.

Then
\{\phi_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}
satisfies the cocycle condition:

(A.8)

\displaystyle\phi_{ij}+\phi_{jl}+\phi_{li}

\displaystyle=(g^{(1)}_{ij}-g^{(2)}_{ij})+(g^{(1)}_{jl}-g^{(2)}_{jl})+(g^{(1)}_{li}-g^{(2)}_{li})

\displaystyle=(g^{(1)}_{ij}+g^{(1)}_{jl}+g^{(1)}_{li})-(g^{(2)}_{ij}+g^{(2)}_{jl}+g^{(2)}_{li})=[0],

and

(A.9)

d(\phi_{ij},[0])=d(g^{(1)}_{ij}-g^{(2)}_{ij},[0])=d(g^{(1)}_{ij},g^{(2))}_{ij})<4\epsilon.

We need to find a family of maps
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\in I}
(
\mu_{i}:U_{i}\to T^{m-k}
) such that

(A.10)

\phi_{ij}=\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}.

Define

(A.11)

\mu_{i}(x):=\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}(-\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I}\rho_{j}(x)\cdot(\log_{T^{m-k}}\circ\phi_{ij})(x))

for all

.

Then
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a family of maps satisfies:

(A.12)

\displaystyle\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}

\displaystyle=\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}(-\displaystyle\sum_{l\in I}\rho_{l}\cdot(\log_{T^{m-k}}\circ\phi_{jl}))-\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}(-\displaystyle\sum_{l\in I}\rho_{l}\cdot(\log_{T^{m-k}}\circ\phi_{il}))

\displaystyle=\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}(\displaystyle\sum_{l\in I}\rho_{l}\cdot(\log_{T^{m-k}}(\phi_{il}-\phi_{jl})))

\displaystyle=\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}(\displaystyle\sum_{l\in I}\rho_{l}\cdot(\log_{T^{m-k}}\circ\phi_{ij}))

\displaystyle=\text{pr}_{T^{m-k}}\circ\log_{T^{m-k}}\circ\phi_{ij}=\phi_{ij}.

Consequently, the family
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of maps satisfies

(A.13)

g^{(1)}_{ij}-g^{(2)}_{ij}=\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}

on
