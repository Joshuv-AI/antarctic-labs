ℓ 1 ​ - ​ 2 \ell_{1\text{-}2} Regularization for Sparse Optimization: Consistency and Global Convergence
Yaohua Hu, Hao Wang, Xiaoqi Yang

Abstract




Abstract:
The
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization method has a strong sparsity promoting capability in approaching sparse solutions of linear inverse problems and gained successful applications in various mathematics and applied science fields. This paper aims to investigate the consistency theory and global convergent algorithms for the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem. In the theoretical aspect, we introduce a notion of restricted eigenvalue condition relative to the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
penalty, and employ it to establish an oracle property and a recovery bound for the global solution of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem. In the algorithmic aspect, we propose two types of iterative thresholding algorithms with the truncation technique and the continuation technique, respectively, to solve the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem. Moreover, under the assumption of the well-known restricted isometry property, we establish the convergence of the proposed algorithms to the ground true sparse solution within a tolerance relevant to the noise level and the recovery bound. Preliminary numerical results show that our proposed algorithms can approach the ground true sparse solution and significantly enhance the sparsity recovery capability, compared with the popular sparse optimization algorithms in the literature.

Key words:
Sparse optimization,
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization, consistency theory, iterative thresholding algorithm, global convergence, ground true solution.

1
Introduction

Sparse optimization aims to find a sparse solution of an underdetermined linear system:

b=Ax+\varepsilon,

(1.1)

where the sensing matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
with
m\ll n
is known, the observation
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is collected with an unknown noise
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, and the variable
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is to be estimated. The sparsity of variable
x
accounts to the number of its nonzero components, denoted by
\|x\|_{0}
. A popular and practical technique for approaching a sparse solution of (
1.1
) is the following regularization formulation:

\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\,\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|x\|_{0},

(1.2)

where
\lambda>0
is a regularization parameter providing a tradeoff between data fidelity and sparsity. Unfortunately, due to the nonconvex and combinational natures of the
\ell_{0}
norm, it is NP-hard to compute a global/local solution of problem (
1.2
); see
[
26
]
.

To overcome this difficulty in designing numerical algorithms, the following (convex)
\ell_{1}
regularization problem has been widely studied:

\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\;\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|x\|_{1},

where
\|x\|_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|
is a sparsity promoting norm being as the tightest convex approximation to the
\ell_{0}
norm. Benefiting from the convexity property, a great deal of attention has been attracted to explore the theoretical property and develop the numerical algorithms for the
\ell_{1}
regularization problem. Its nice statistical property and consistency theory, including the oracle property and recovery bound, have been well studied under several types of regularity conditions on matrix
A
, such as the mutual incoherence property (MIP)
[
5
]
, restricted isometry property (RIP)
[
7
]
, null space property (NSP)
[
14
]
and restricted eigenvalue condition (REC)
[
2
]
. Many exclusive and efficient algorithms have been proposed and developed with convergence guarantee, such as the iterative soft thresholding algorithm (ISTA)
[
9
]
, proximal gradient algorithm (PGA)
[
1
]
, alternative direction method of multipliers (ADMM)
[
35
]
and block coordinate descent method (BCD)
[
32
]
.
However, the
\ell_{1}
regularization method suffers from several frustrations in practical applications, including over-penalizing the large absolute entries of the variable
[
13
]
and requiring a large number of measurements when applied to compressive sensing
[
34
]
.

To overcome these drawbacks in applications of the
\ell_{1}
regularization, several nonconvex regularization methods, e.g., the smoothly clipped absolute deviation (SCAD)
[
13
]
, minimax concave penalty (MCP)
[
38
]
,
\ell_{p}
penalty with
p\in(0,1)

[
8
]
,
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
penalty
[
36
]
and
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
penalty
[
12
]
, have been proposed and developed. It has been shown in the literature
[
20
,
36
]
that the contours of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
norm are more close to those of the
\ell_{0}
norm than the
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{p}
norms, and hence the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem

\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\,\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|x\|_{1}-\|x\|_{2})

(1.3)

would likely outperform the
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{p}
regularizations in the sense that it admits a significantly stronger sparsity promoting capability and allows to obtain a more sparse solution from fewer linear measurements when sensing matrix
A
is highly coherent.
The
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization method has gained successful applications in a wide range of fields, such as point source super-resolution
[
22
]
, image restoration
[
23
]
and MRI reconstruction
[
24
]
.
Motivated by these significant applications, many efforts have been devoted to the development of theoretical properties and optimization algorithms of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
1.3
). Although the nonconvexity and the non-separability of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
penalty may lead to some difficulties in the theoretical and algorithmic study of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
1.3
), the exact recovery property of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
minimization problem with observation measurements constraint has been established under the assumptions of RIP
[
36
]
and NSP
[
15
]
, respectively.
To the best of our knowledge, there is still no study devoted to investigating the oracle property and consistency theory for the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
1.3
).
Many practical algorithms have been developed to solve the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
1.3
) such as the PGA and ADMM
[
21
]
, and difference of convex functions algorithm (DCA)
[
36
]
. However, limited by the nonconvexity of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization, only the convergence to a stationary point of problem (
1.3
) has been established in the literature, while there is still no theoretical evidence to guarantee the “global convergence”, that is, the convergence to a global minimum or the ground true solution. This is a typical deficiency of nonconvex optimization algorithms.

Recently, under the framework of iterative limited shrinkage thresholding algorithms, the convergence of the PGA to the approximate global minimum or the ground true solution has been investigated in
[
17
]
for certain nonconvex sparse regularization problems, including the SCAD
[
13
]
, MCP
[
38
]
and
\ell_{p}
regularization
[
8
]
, under the assumption of RIP
[
7
]
. However, the framework in
[
17
]
only concentrates on separable penalties, and cannot be applied to deal with the nonseparable
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
penalty problem (
1.3
).

This paper aims to explore the consistency theory and the global convergence of some optimization algorithms for the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
1.3
). In the aspect of consistency theory, we first introduce a notion of restricted eigenvalue condition (REC) relative to the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization and investigate its sufficient conditions in terms of classical regularity conditions such as the MIP and RIP; see Proposition
2.1
. Under the assumption of the REC, we obtain an oracle inequality and a recovery bound of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\lambda^{2}(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}\right)
for any point in a level set of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
1.3
) at the ground true solution, including the global optimal solution of problem (
1.3
); see Theorem
2.1
. In the algorithmic aspect, we introduce a thresholding operator to the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization by simplifying the analytical formulation of its proximal mapping
[
21
]
, which is able to overcome the over-penalization phenomenon of the soft thresholding operator on the components with large absolute magnitudes; see the details in Remark
3.1
. By virtue of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator, we propose one iterative thresholding algorithm with the truncation technique
[
4
]
(ITAT) and one with the continuation technique
[
16
]
(ITAC); see Algorithms
3.1
and
3.2
respectively. The proposed ITAT and ITAC are of simple formulation and low storage requirement, and thus are extremely efficient for large-scale sparse optimization problems.
More importantly, under the assumption of the RIP, we show that the sequence generated by the ITAT converges to an approximate true sparse solution of (
1.1
) at a geometric rate within a tolerance relevant to the noise level plus the recovery bound and that the output of the ITAC approaches an approximate true sparse solution of (
1.1
) within a tolerance proportional to the noise level; see Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
respectively. Preliminary numerical experiments show that the ITAT and the ITAC have strong sparsity promoting capability and outperform the standard PGA on both accuracy and robustness.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we establish the consistency theory for the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
1.3
). In Section 3, we propose the ITAT and the ITAC for solving problem (
1.3
) and establish their convergence to an approximate true sparse solution of (
1.1
) under the assumption of the RIP. In Sections 4, we present preliminary numerical results.

2
Consistency theory

Throughout this section, we assume that the observation
b
is collected from a linear transform
A
on a ground true
s
-sparse solution
\bar{x}
with support
\mathcal{S}
:

b=A\bar{x}\,\mbox{ with }\,\mathcal{S}:=\{i:\bar{x}_{i}\neq 0\}\,\mbox{ and }\,s:=|\mathcal{S}|.

(2.1)

To approach the sparse solution
\bar{x}
, we investigate the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem:

\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\,\,\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|x\|_{1}-\|x\|_{2}).

(2.2)

This section is devoted to exploring the consistency theory of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
), including the oracle property and the recovery bound. To this end, we first introduce a notion of regularity condition and discuss its property.

2.1
Regularity conditions

In the scenario of sparse optimization, certain regularity conditions on the sensing matrix
A
are required to guarantee the consistency theory of sparse regularization problems; see
[
2
,
5
,
18
,
25
]
and references therein. For example, the restricted eigenvalue condition (REC) was introduced in
[
2
]
to investigate the consistency theory of the
\ell_{1}
regularization problem.

Inspired by the idea of REC in
[
2
]
, we introduce a notion of REC relative to the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
penalty, so as to guarantee the consistency theory of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
).
To proceed, let
\bar{x}
and
x^{*}
be the ground true
s
-sparse solution defined by (
2.1
) and a global optimal solution of problem (
2.2
), respectively.
Note that the residual
\hat{x}:=x^{*}-\bar{x}
always satisfies that

\|\hat{x}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}\leq\|\hat{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}+\|\hat{x}\|_{2};

see the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Associated to this, we introduce a notion of REC relative to the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
penalty as follows.
It is worth mentioning that when the REC relative to
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
penalty is similar to the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
null space property introduced in
[
39
]
.
Given
1\leq s\leq t\ll n
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[n]
, we use
\mathcal{I}(x;t)
to denote the index set of the first
t
largest coordinates in the absolute value of
x
in
\mathcal{I}^{c}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
is said to satisfies the restricted eigenvalue condition relative to
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
penalty and
(s,t)

(\ell_{1\text{-}2}
-
{\rm REC}(s,t)inshort)
if

\phi(s,t):=\min\,\left\{\frac{\|Ax\|_{2}}{\|x_{\mathcal{J}}\|_{2}}:|\mathcal{I}|\leq s,\|x_{\mathcal{I}^{c}}\|_{1}\leq\|x_{\mathcal{I}}\|_{1}+\|x\|_{2},\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{I}(x;t)\cup\mathcal{I}\right\}>0.

Several types of regularity conditions have been proposed to investigate the consistency theory and nice statistical property of sparse regularization problems, recalled as follows.

Definition 2.2

(RIC
[
7
]
)
.

(i)

The
s
-restricted isometry constant of
A
is denoted by
\delta_{s}
and defined to be the smallest quantity such that, for
any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
J\subseteq[n]
with
|J|\leq s
,

(1-\delta_{s})\|x_{J}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|Ax_{J}\|_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\delta_{s})\|x_{J}\|_{2}^{2}.

(2.3)

(ii)

The
(s,t)
-restricted orthogonality constant of
A
is denoted by
\theta_{s,t}
and defined to be the smallest quantity such
that, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
J,T\subseteq[n]
with
|J|\leq s
,
|T|\leq t
and
J\cap T=\emptyset
,

|\langle Ax_{J},Ax_{T}\rangle|\leq\theta_{s,t}\|x_{J}\|_{2}\|x_{T}\|_{2}.

(2.4)

Definition 2.3

(MIC
[
10
]
)
.

The mutual incoherence constant of
A
is defined by

\mu:=\sup\{|A_{j}^{\top}A_{i}|:\forall 1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}.

(2.5)

Definition 2.4

(SEC
[
11
]
)
.

The
s
-sparse minimal eigenvalue and
s
-sparse maximal eigenvalue of
A
are respectively defined by

\sigma_{\min}(s):=\min_{\|x\|_{0}\leq s}\sqrt{\frac{x^{\top}A^{\top}Ax}{x^{\top}x}},\quad\sigma_{\max}(s):=\max_{\|x\|_{0}\leq s}\sqrt{\frac{x^{\top}A^{\top}Ax}{x^{\top}x}}.

(2.6)

It is natural to investigate the relationships between the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
-REC and other types of regularity conditions mentioned above. The following proportion provides some sufficient conditions for the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
-REC in terms of SEC, RIC and MIC, respectively. The proofs are deferred to the Appendix
A
.

Proposition 2.1
.

A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
satisfies the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
-
{\rm REC}(s,t)
provided that one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

(\sqrt{t}-1)\sigma_{\min}(s+t)>(\sqrt{s}+1)\sigma_{\max}(t).

(ii)

(\sqrt{s}+1)\theta_{t,s+t}<(\sqrt{t}-1)(1-\delta_{s+t})
.

(iii)

(\sqrt{t}-1)\sigma_{\min}(s+t)>2\theta_{s+t,1}\sqrt{t}(\sqrt{s}+1)
.

(iv)

(\sqrt{t}-1)\sigma_{\min}(s+t)>2\mu\sqrt{t(s+t)}(\sqrt{s}+1)
.

(v)

\|A_{i}\|_{2}=1
for each
i\in[n]
, and
\mu<\frac{\sqrt{t}-1}{(s+t)(\sqrt{t}-1)+2\sqrt{t(s+t)}(\sqrt{s}+1)}
.

The following lemma is recalled from
[
18
]
and will be useful in establishing the recovery bound for problem (
2.2
) in Theorem
2.1
.
Let
{\rm rank}_{i}(x)
denote the rank of
|x_{i}|
among
\mathcal{I}^{c}
(in a decreasing order), and separate
\mathcal{I}^{c}
into a series of disjoint index sets
\{\mathcal{I}_{k}\}
with each set consisting of
t
indexes in an increasing order of
{\rm rank}_{i}(x)
. That is,
r:=\left\lceil\frac{n-|\mathcal{I}|}{t}\right\rceil
(where
\lceil u\rceil
denotes the largest integer no greater than
u
) and

\mathcal{I}_{k}(x;t):=\left\{i\in\mathcal{I}^{c}:{\rm rank}_{i}(x)\in\{kt+1,\dots,(k+1)t\}\right\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad k\in[r].

(2.7)

Lemma 2.1

(
[
18
, Lemma 7]
)
.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
\mathcal{I}\subset[n]
and
p\geq 1
, and write
\mathcal{J}:=\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{I}(x;t)
and
\mathcal{I}_{k}:=\mathcal{I}_{k}(x;t)
. Then it holds that

\|x_{\mathcal{J}^{c}}\|_{p}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{r}\|x_{\mathcal{I}_{k}}\|_{p}\leq t^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\|x_{\mathcal{I}^{c}}\|_{1}.

2.2
Oracle property and recovery bound

In this subsection, we present the consistency theory of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
), including its oracle property and recovery bound, under the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
-REC. For the sake of simplicity, let
h(x):=\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|x\|_{1}-\|x\|_{2})
denote the objective function of problem (
2.2
) and define its level set by

{\rm lev}_{h}(\bar{x}):=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:h(x)\leq h(\bar{x})\}.

(2.8)

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\bar{x},\mathcal{S},s)
be defined in (
2.1
). Let
x^{*}\in{\rm lev}_{h}(\bar{x})
, and suppose that
A
satisfies the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
-REC
(s,s)
.
Then the following statements are true.

(i)

The oracle inequality holds

\frac{1}{2}\|Ax^{*}-b\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{2})\leq\frac{2\lambda^{2}(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}}{\phi^{2}(s,s)}.

(2.9)

(ii)

The recovery bound holds

\|x^{*}-\bar{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(4+\max\left\{\frac{\sqrt{s}+1}{\sqrt{s}},\frac{4}{\sqrt{s}-2}\right\}^{2}\right)\frac{\lambda^{2}(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}}{\phi^{4}(s,s)}.

(2.10)

Proof.

(i) Let
x^{*}\in{\rm lev}_{h}(\bar{x})
. Then it follows from (
2.8
) and
\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}=0
(cf. (
2.1
)) that

\frac{1}{2}\|Ax^{*}-b\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|x^{*}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}\|_{2})\leq\lambda(\|\bar{x}\|_{1}-\|\bar{x}\|_{2})=\lambda(\|\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}-\|\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}).

(2.11)

Note that

\|x^{*}\|_{1}=\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}+\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|x^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}+\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{2}

(2.12)

(by the subadditivity of
\ell_{2}
norm). Then (
2.11
) is reduced to

\frac{1}{2}\|Ax^{*}-b\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{2})\leq\lambda(\|\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1})+\lambda(\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}-\|\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}).

(2.13)

Note by the subadditivity of
\ell_{p}
norm (
p=1,2
) that

\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}-\|\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}\leq\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}\leq\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{s}\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}

(2.14)

(due to (
1.4
)).
Then (
2.13
) is reduced to

\frac{1}{2}\|Ax^{*}-A\bar{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{2})\leq\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}.

(2.15)

On the other hand, noting by (
2.1
) that
\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}=0
, we obtain by (
2.14
) and the subadditivity of
\ell_{2}
norm that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}-\bar{x}\|_{2}&\leq\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}-(\|\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{1})-(\|x^{*}\|_{2}-\|\bar{x}\|_{2})\\
&=\|x^{*}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}\|_{2}-(\|\bar{x}\|_{1}-\|\bar{x}\|_{2})\\
&\leq 0\end{array}

(thanks to (
2.12
) and (
2.11
)). This shows that
x^{*}-\bar{x}
falls in the constraint set of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
-REC
(s,s)
. It thus follows from Definition
2.1
that

\|x_{\mathcal{S}}^{*}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\phi(s,s)}\|Ax^{*}-A\bar{x}\|_{2}.

Thus (
2.15
) is reduced to

\frac{1}{2}\|Ax^{*}-A\bar{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda(\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{2})\leq\frac{\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\phi(s,s)}\|Ax^{*}-A\bar{x}\|_{2};

(2.16)

consequently,

\|Ax^{*}-A\bar{x}\|_{2}\leq\frac{2\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\phi(s,s)}.

(2.17)

This, together with (
2.16
), yields the oracle inequality (
2.9
).

(ii) Define
{\mathcal{N}}:=\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{S}(x^{*};s)
. The
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
-REC
(s,s)
implies that

\|x_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\|Ax^{*}-A\bar{x}\|_{2}}{\phi(s,s)}\leq\frac{2\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\phi^{2}(s,s)}

(2.18)

(by (
2.17
)). By Lemma
2.1
(with
2
,
s
,
\mathcal{N}
,
\mathcal{S}
in place of
p
,
t
,
\mathcal{J}
,
\mathcal{I}
), we get that

\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\|x_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{*}\|_{1};

(2.19)

consequently,

\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\|x_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}(\|x_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{*}\|_{1}-\|x_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}).

(2.20)

Note by (
2.16
) and the Cauchy inequality
a^{2}+b^{2}\geq 2ab
that

\|Ax^{*}-A\bar{x}\|_{2}\sqrt{2\lambda(\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{2})}\leq\frac{\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\phi(s,s)}\|Ax^{*}-A\bar{x}\|_{2};

hence

\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{1}-\|x^{*}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}}{2\phi^{2}(s,s)}.

This, together with (
2.20
), implies that

\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\|x_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}}{2\sqrt{s}\,\phi^{2}(s,s)}.

(2.21)

Below we consider the following two cases.

Case 1: Suppose that
\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{s}}\|x_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}
. Then (
2.21
) is reduced to

\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}}{\sqrt{s}\,\phi^{2}(s,s)}.

(2.22)

Case 2: Suppose that
\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}<\frac{2}{\sqrt{s}}\|x_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}
. Noting that
{\mathcal{N}}=\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{S}(x^{*};s)
being a disjoint decomposition and
\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}(x^{*};s)}=0
as
\mathcal{S}(x^{*};s)\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{c}
, we obtain that

\|x_{\mathcal{S}(x^{*};s)}^{*}\|_{2}=\|x_{\mathcal{S}(x^{*};s)}^{*}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{S}(x^{*};s)}\|_{2}\leq\|x_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{2}\leq\frac{2\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\phi^{2}(s,s)}

(due to (
2.18
)), and then we get by the subadditivity of
\ell_{2}
norm that

\|x_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}+\|x_{\mathcal{S}(x^{*};s)}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}+\frac{2\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\phi^{2}(s,s)}.

This, together with the assumption of Case 2, implies that

\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{s}-2}\frac{\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\phi^{2}(s,s)}.

(2.23)

Combining (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) yields that

\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}\leq\max\left\{\frac{\sqrt{s}+1}{\sqrt{s}},\frac{4}{\sqrt{s}-2}\right\}\frac{\lambda(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\phi^{2}(s,s)}.

This, together with (
2.18
) and
\bar{x}_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}=0
, one has that

\|x^{*}-\bar{x}\|_{2}^{2}=\|x_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|x_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}^{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(4+\max\left\{\frac{\sqrt{s}+1}{\sqrt{s}},\frac{4}{\sqrt{s}-2}\right\}^{2}\right)\frac{\lambda^{2}(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}}{\phi^{4}(s,s)},

which establishes (
2.10
), and the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem
2.1
is an important theoretical result in that it provides the oracle inequality (
2.9
) and the recovery bound (
2.10
) for the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
). In particular, the oracle inequality (
2.9
) provides an upper bound on the square error of the linear system plus the violation of the nonzero components of
x^{*}
, and the recovery bound (
2.10
) provides an upper bound on the distance from
x^{*}
to the ground true solution
\bar{x}
.

Remark 2.1
.

One can observe from (
2.10
) that the global optimal solution
x^{*}(\lambda)
of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
) has a recovery bound:

\|x^{*}(\lambda)-\bar{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\lambda^{2}(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}\right).

Particularly, when
s\geq 16
, it follows from (
2.10
) that

\|x^{*}(\lambda)-\bar{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{8}{\phi^{4}(s,s)}\lambda^{2}(\sqrt{s}+1)^{2}.

3
Iterative Thresholding Algorithms

The type of iterative thresholding algorithms (ITA) is one of the most popular and efficient numerical algorithms, with a simple formulation and a low computational complexity, for solving (convex or nonconvex) sparse optimization problems; see
[
4
,
9
,
14
,
18
,
34
]
and references therein. Particularly, the ITA for solving the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
) was proposed in
[
21
]
. However, limited by the nonconvexity of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization, the convergence theory of its ITA is still far from satisfactory: only convergence to a stationary point of problem (
2.2
) was established in
[
21
, Theorem 1]
; while there is still no theoretical evidence to guarantee the convergence to a global minimum or the ground true sparse solution.

To fill this typical gap of nonconvex optimization algorithms, this section aims to propose two novel ITA-type algorithms for solving the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
) and investigate their convergence to the ground true sparse solution.

3.1
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator

Proximal gradient algorithm (PGA)
[
1
,
18
]
is one of the most popular and practical algorithms for composite optimization problem

\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\;f(x)+\varphi(x),

(3.1)

where
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a differentiable function and
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is of some special structures. The iterative formula of the PGA for solving (
3.1
) is

x^{k+1}:={\rm Prox}_{\varphi}(x^{k}-v\nabla f(x^{k})).

(3.2)

where the proximal mapping
{\rm Prox}_{\varphi}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of
\varphi
is defined by

{\rm Prox}_{\varphi}(y):={\rm arg}\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\,\varphi(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}\quad\,\mbox{for each }y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

The type of ITAs can be understood as the applications of the PGAs to solve the composite optimization problem (
3.1
) with certain regularization term. For example, the iterative soft (resp., hard, half) thresholding algorithm can be understood as the PGA for solving the
\ell_{1}
(resp.,
\ell_{0}
,
\ell_{1/2}
) regularization problem; see
[
9
,
4
,
34
]
respectively.

Particularly, when applied to problem (
2.2
) (with
\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}
and
\lambda(\|x\|_{1}-\|x\|_{2})
in place of
f(x)
and
\varphi(x)
in (
3.1
)), the ITA for solving the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
) has the following iterative formula

x^{k+1}:={\rm Prox}_{v\lambda(\|x\|_{1}-\|x\|_{2})}(x^{k}-vA^{\top}(Ax^{k}-b)).

(3.3)

It is presented in
[
21
, Lemma 1]
that
{\rm Prox}_{\lambda(\|x\|_{1}-\|x\|_{2})}
has the following analytical formula.

Lemma 3.1
.

The solution
x^{*}\in{\rm Prox}_{\lambda(\|x\|_{1}-\|x\|_{2})}(y)
has the following formulas.

(i)

If
\|y\|_{\infty}>\lambda
, then
x^{*}=\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{\|z\|_{2}}\right)z
with
z:=(|y|-\lambda)_{+}\odot{\rm sign}(y)
.

(ii)

If
\|y\|_{\infty}=\lambda
, then
\|x^{*}\|_{2}=\lambda
with
x_{i}^{*}y_{i}\geq 0
for each
i\in[n]
and
x_{i}^{*}=0
whenever
|y_{i}|<\lambda
.

(iii)

If
\|y\|_{\infty}<\lambda
, then
x^{*}
is an
1
-sparse vector satisfying
\|x^{*}\|_{2}=\|y\|_{\infty}
with
x_{i}^{*}y_{i}\geq 0
for each
i\in[n]
and
x_{i}^{*}=0
whenever
|y_{i}|<\|y\|_{\infty}
.

Associated to the consistency theory (cf. Theorem
2.1
), the regularization parameter
\lambda
should always be set to be quite small in numerical experiments, so as to guarantee the perfect recovery and numerical performance. In this case when
\lambda
is set to be small, the situation in Lemma
3.1
(i) always appears in the process of numerical computation. Hence to simplify the computation of proximal mapping of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization, we define an
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator
by

\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x),

(3.4)

where
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}
are the soft-thresholding operator and the enlargement operator defined respectively by

\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x):=(|x|-\lambda)_{+}\odot{\rm sign}(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}(x):=\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{\|x\|_{2}}\right)x.

(3.5)

It is obtained from Lemma
3.1
(i) and (
3.4
) that

\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(y)={\rm Prox}_{\lambda(\|x\|_{1}-\|x\|_{2})}(y)\quad\mbox{whenever }\|y\|_{\infty}>\lambda.

(3.6)

Below we discuss some useful properties of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The thresholding property:

(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}=0\quad\mbox{whenever }\,|x_{i}|\leq\lambda.

(3.7)

(ii)

The shrinkage property:

\|\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x)-x\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda.

(3.8)

Proof.

(i) It directly follows from (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) that

|x_{i}|\leq\lambda\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}=0.

(ii) One can observe from (
3.7
) that

|(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}-x_{i}|\leq\lambda\quad\mbox{whenever }\,|x_{i}|\leq\lambda.

(3.9)

Then it remains to discuss the case when
|x_{i}|>\lambda
. In this case, it follows from (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) that

(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}=(|x_{i}|-\lambda)\,{\rm sign}(x_{i})=x_{i}-\lambda\,{\rm sign}(x_{i}),

(3.10)

and

(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}=(\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\circ\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}=\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{\|\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x)\|_{2}}\right)(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}.

Combining the above two inequalities, we obtain that

\displaystyle(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}-x_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{\|\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x)\|_{2}}\right)(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}-\left((\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}+\lambda\,{\rm sign}(x_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\|\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x)\|_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}-\lambda\,{\rm sign}(x_{i})

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\lambda\,{\rm sign}(x_{i})\left(\frac{|(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}|}{\|\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x)\|_{2}}-1\right)

(due to the fact that
(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}
and
x_{i}
have the same sign). Noting that
1-\frac{|(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}|}{\|\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x)\|_{2}}\in[0,1)
, it follows that
|(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}-x_{i}|<\lambda
. This, together with (
3.9
), shows (
3.8
). ∎

Remark 3.1
.

(i)
It is indicated from Lemma
3.2
that the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
inherits the thresholding and shrinkage properties, that are important for approaching a sparse solution.

(ii)
The enlargement operator
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}
enlarges the absolute values of nonzero components, hence the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
is able to overcome the over-penalization phenomenon of the soft thresholding operator
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
on the components with large magnitude. In particular, we have by (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that

0<\frac{x_{i}-(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}}{x_{i}-(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}}=1-\frac{|(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}|}{\|\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x)\|_{2}}\leq 1,

and hence

|(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}|\leq|(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x))_{i}|\leq|x_{i}|.

Associated with the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
, the standard ITA for solving the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
) has the following iterative form:

x^{k+1}:=\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}(x^{k}-vA^{\top}(Ax^{k}-b)).

(3.12)

Remark 3.2
.

It is pointed out by Lemma
3.1
(i), also by (
3.6
), that the thresholding operator
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
equals to the proximal operator of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization whenever
\lambda
is small. Consequently, when
\lambda
is set to be small, the ITA (
3.12
) is equivalent to the PGA (
3.3
) for the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
).

There is an open gap between the theoretical and algorithmic studies of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
). In particular, the consistency theory in Theorem
2.1
provides a recovery bound for the global minimum of problem (
2.2
) to the ground true sparse solution; while the convergence theory in
[
21
, Theorem 1]
only presents the convergence of the standard ITA (
3.12
) to a stationary point of problem (
2.2
). However, there is still no theoretical evidence to guarantee the convergence to global minimum or the ground true sparse solution. This is also a typical gap of nonconvex optimization algorithms.

Throughout this paper, we assume that the observation
b
is collected from a linear transform
A
on a ground true
s
-sparse solution
\bar{x}
with support
\mathcal{S}
and an unknown noise
\varepsilon
:

b=A\bar{x}+\varepsilon\,\mbox{ with }\,\mathcal{S}:=\{i:\bar{x}_{i}\neq 0\}\,\mbox{ and }\,s:=|\mathcal{S}|.

(3.13)

In the remainder of this section, we aim to fill this gap by providing a positive theoretical guarantee for the sparsity recovery capability of the ITA-type algorithms for solving the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
). In particular, we will propose two types of ITAs by combining with the truncation technique
[
4
]
and the continuation technique
[
37
]
, respectively, and investigate their convergence to the ground true sparse solution
\bar{x}
of (
1.1
) under the assumption of the restricted isometry property (RIP)
[
7
]
.

Remark 3.3
.

The RIP
[
7
]
has been widely used for
the convergence analysis of sparse optimization algorithms
[
4
,
14
,
27
,
33
]
.
Many types of random matrices, including Gaussian, Bernoulli, and partial Fourier matrices, have been shown to satisfy the RIP with exponentially high probability
[
3
]
.

We end this subsection with some useful properties of the RIP in the following lemma, which are taken from
[
17
, Lemma 1]
, also
[
27
, Proposition 3.1]
and
[
14
, Lemmas 6.16 and 6.20]
, and will be helpful in convergence analysis of our proposed algorithms.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
A
satisfies the RIP with
\delta_{s}<1
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}\subseteq[n]
, and
v\geq 0
. Then the following assertions are true.

(i)

If
|\mathcal{I}\cup\operatorname{supp}(x)|\leq s
, then
\|((\mathbb{I}-vA^{\top}A)x)_{\mathcal{I}}\|_{2}\leq(|1-v|+v\delta_{s})\|x\|_{2}
.

(ii)

If
|\mathcal{I}|\leq s
, then
\|A^{\top}_{\mathcal{I}}\varepsilon\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{1+\delta_{s}}\|\varepsilon\|_{2}
.

(iii)

If
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{J}=\emptyset
and
|\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{J}|\leq s
, then
\|A_{\mathcal{J}}^{\top}A_{\mathcal{I}}x_{\mathcal{I}}\|_{2}\leq\delta_{s}\|x_{\mathcal{I}}\|_{2}
.

3.2
Iterative thresholding algorithm with truncation technique

In the sequence generated by sparse optimization algorithms, the components with large magnitude are viewed as good approximation to the ground true sparse solution, while the small components can only be attributed to the presence of noise in observation. In order to avert the noise causing by the small components, the truncation operator, denoted by
\mathbb{H}_{s}
that sets all but the largest
s
elements of a vector (in magnitude) to zero,
is widely applied in sparse optimization algorithms to ensure the sparsity structure of the solution by discarding the small components; see
[
4
,
14
,
40
]
and references therein.

By virtue of the standard
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator (
3.4
) and the truncation operator, we propose an iterative thresholding algorithm with the truncation technique (ITAT) to solve the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
) and approach the ground true sparse solution of (
1.1
).

Algorithm 3.1

(ITAT)
.

Select a regularization parameter
\lambda>0
, a truncation parameter
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and set a random initial point
x^{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and stepsize
v>0
.
For each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, having
x^{k}
, we determine
x^{k+1}
via

x^{k+1}:=\mathbb{H}_{s}\circ\mathbb{T}_{v\lambda}\left(x^{k}-vA^{\top}(Ax^{k}-b)\right).

(3.14)

Remark 3.4
.

(i)
The ITAT adopts the truncation operator
\mathbb{H}_{s}
to maintain the sparsity level
s
of the sequence
\{x^{k}\}
, which is helpful for guaranteeing the convergence to the ground true solution with the required sparsity of (
1.1
); see Theorem
3.1
.

(ii)

Since the truncation operator
\mathbb{H}_{s}
and the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}
are very simple to calculate, the ITAT inherits the significant advantages of simple formulation and low computational complexity, and thus is extremely efficient for large-scale problems.

The following lemma recalls from
[
28
, Theorem 1]
a tight bound of truncation operator
\mathbb{H}_{s}(\cdot)
, which is useful in convergence analysis.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an
s
-sparse vector and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an arbitrary vector. Then

\|\mathbb{H}_{s}(z)-x\|_{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}\|z-x\|_{2}.

The main result of this subsection is as follows, in which we establish the convergence of the ITAT to an approximate true sparse solution of (
1.1
) under the assumption of the RIP.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
A
satisfies the
3s
-RIP with
\delta_{3s}<\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}\approx 0.618
, and let
\{x^{k}\}
be a sequence generated by Algorithm
3.1
with stepsize satisfying

\frac{0.382}{1-\delta_{3s}}\approx\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2(1-\delta_{3s})}<v<\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2(1-\delta_{3s})}\approx\frac{1.618}{1-\delta_{3s}}.

(3.15)

Then
\{x^{k}\}
converges approximately to
\bar{x}
at a geometric rate; particularly,

\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|_{2}\leq\rho^{k}\|x^{0}-\bar{x}\|_{2}+\frac{v(\sqrt{5}+1)}{2(1-\rho)}(\sqrt{1+\delta_{2s}}\|\varepsilon\|_{2}+\sqrt{2s}\lambda),

(3.16)

where
\rho:=\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}(|1-v|+v\delta_{3s})\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

To proceed the convergence analysis, we rewrite the process of Algorithm
3.1
into the following three steps:

y^{k}:=x^{k}-vA^{\top}(Ax^{k}-b),\quad z^{k}:=\mathbb{T}_{v\lambda}(y^{k}),\quad x^{k+1}:=\mathbb{H}_{s}(z^{k}).

(3.17)

Moreover, for the sake of simplicity, we write

r^{k}:=x^{k}-\bar{x},\quad\mathcal{S}_{k}:={\rm supp}(x^{k})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{I}_{k}=\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k};

(3.18)

and then one observes from the
s
-sparsity of
\bar{x}
and the third equality of (
3.17
) that

|\mathcal{S}|=s,\quad|\mathcal{S}_{k}|\leq s\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\mathcal{I}_{k}|\leq 2s\quad\mbox{for each $k\in{\mathbb{N}}$}.

(3.19)

Noting by (
3.18
) that

{\rm supp}(\bar{x})=\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{k+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rm supp}(x^{k+1})=\mathcal{S}_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{k+1},

(3.20)

we get by (
3.17
) and by Lemma
3.4
(with
z^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}
and
\bar{x}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}
in place of
z
and
x
) that

\displaystyle\|r^{k+1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|x^{k+1}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}\|z^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}(\|\bar{x}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}-y^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}+\|y^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}-z^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}).

Noting by (
3.17
) that
z^{k}=\mathbb{T}_{v\lambda}(y^{k})
, we have by Lemma
3.2
that
\|z^{k}-y^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq v\lambda
. Combining this with (
3.19
), we achieve by (
1.4
) that

\|y^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}-z^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{|\mathcal{I}_{k+1}|}\|y^{k}-z^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2s}v\lambda.

(3.22)

On the other hand, we obtain by the first equality of (
3.17
) and (
3.13
) that

y^{k}=x^{k}-vA^{\top}(Ax^{k}-b)=x^{k}-vA^{\top}Ar^{k}+vA^{\top}\varepsilon

(due to (
3.18
)); hence it follows that

\|y^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}\leq\|((I-vA^{\top}A)r^{k})_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}+v\|A_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}^{\top}\varepsilon\|_{2}.

(3.23)

Note by (
3.20
) that
|\mathcal{I}_{k+1}|\leq 2s
and
|\mathcal{I}_{k+1}\cup{\rm supp}(r^{k})|=|\mathcal{I}_{k+1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k}|\leq 3s
. Then by the assumption of
3s
-RIP of
A
and (
3.15
), we obtain by Lemmas
3.3
(i) and (ii) that

\|((I-vA^{\top}A)r^{k})_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}\leq(|1-v|+v\delta_{3s})\|r^{k}\|_{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|A_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}^{\top}\varepsilon\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{1+\delta_{2s}}\|\varepsilon\|_{2},

respectively. By the above two inequalities, (
3.23
) is reduced to

\|y^{k}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}\|_{2}\leq(|1-v|+v\delta_{3s})\|r^{k}\|_{2}+v\sqrt{1+\delta_{2s}}\|\varepsilon\|_{2}.

This, together with (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), yields that

\|r^{k+1}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}(|1-v|+v\delta_{3s})\|r^{k}\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}v(\sqrt{1+\delta_{2s}}\|\varepsilon\|_{2}+\sqrt{2s}\lambda)

(3.24)

Let
\rho:=\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}(|1-v|+v\delta_{3s})
. By the RIP assumption that
\delta_{3s}<\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}
, we can check that
\rho\in(0,1)
when either
\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2(1-\delta_{3s})}<v\leq 1
or
1\leq v<\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2(1-\delta_{3s})}
, that is under the assumption (
3.15
). Then we obtain inductively by (
3.24
) that

\displaystyle\|x^{k+1}-\bar{x}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\rho\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}v(\sqrt{1+\delta_{2s}}\|\varepsilon\|_{2}+\sqrt{2s}\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{k+1}\|x^{0}-\bar{x}\|_{2}+\frac{v(\sqrt{5}+1)}{2(1-\rho)}(\sqrt{1+\delta_{2s}}\|\varepsilon\|_{2}+\sqrt{2s}\lambda).

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

(i)
Theorem
3.1
shows a geometric convergence rate of the ITAT to an approximate true sparse solution of (
1.1
) within a tolerance. The tolerance in (
3.16
) has an additive form of a term on noise level
\mathcal{O}(\|\varepsilon\|_{2})
and a term on regularization parameter
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{s}\lambda)
, that has the same order with the recovery bound in (
2.10
). The term
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{s}\lambda)
could be small when a small regularization parameter
\lambda
is selected, and it will be illustrated in our numerical experiments in Section
4
that the best regularization parameter is about
\lambda=10^{-3}
.

(ii)
Theorem
3.1
improves the convergence result in
[
17
, Theorem 2]
in the sense that it requires the weaker RIP and stepsize assumptions.
In particular, the assumptions that
\delta_{3s}<0.618
and (
3.15
) are weaker than the ones that
\delta_{3s}<\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{2(1-\delta_{3s})}<v\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta_{3s}}
assumed in
[
17
, Theorem 2]
.

(iii)
By (

3.3
Iterative thresholding algorithm with continuation technique

Note that the regularization parameter
\lambda
plays an important role in the numerical performance of sparse optimization algorithms. According to the recovery bound theory, the regularization parameter
\lambda
should be small to guarantee the better recovery; however, the computational mathematics theory and extensive numerical studies show that a too small parameter will result in the ill-posedness of the subproblems and the convergence is faster if the parameter is properly larger.
To inherit both advantages in theoretical and numerical aspects, the continuation technique is a commonly used acceleration strategy for sparse optimization algorithms, that
adopts a geometrically decreasing sequence of regularization parameters
\{\lambda_{k}\}
starting at a large one in place of a fixed but small one; see, e.g.,
[
16
,
31
,
33
,
37
]
.

Inspired by the idea of the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
thresholding operator (
3.4
) and the continuation technique, we propose an iterative thresholding algorithm with the continuation technique (ITAC) to solve the
\ell_{1\text{-}2}
regularization problem (
2.2
).

Algorithm 3.2

(ITAC)
.

Select the regularization parameters
\lambda_{0}>0
,
\lambda>0
and a continuation parameter
\gamma\in(0,1)
, and set the initial point
x^{0}:=0
and stepsize
v>0
.
For each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, having
x^{k}
and
\lambda_{k}
, if
\lambda_{k}<\lambda
, then it stops and outputs
x^{*}:=x^{k}
; otherwise, update the regularization parameter
\lambda_{k+1}:=\gamma\lambda_{k}
and determine
x^{k+1}
via

x^{k+1}_{i}:=\mathbb{T}_{v\lambda_{k}}\left(x^{k}-vA^{\top}(Ax^{k}-b)\right).

(3.26)

Remark 3.6
.

The only difference between ITAC and ITA is the use of a dynamical sequence of regularization parameters.
The ITAC inherits the significant advantages of simple formulation and low computational complexity, and thus is extremely efficient for large-scale sparse optimization problems.

The main theorem of this subsection is as follows, which provides certain parameters setting (relevant to the RIP) in the ITAC to guarantee its convergence to an approximate true sparse solution of (

|\langle Ax_{\mathcal{J}},Ax_{\mathcal{J}^{c}}\rangle|\leq\|x_{\mathcal{J}^{c}}\|_{1}\|A_{\mathcal{J}^{c}}^{\top}Ax_{\mathcal{J}}\|_{\infty}\leq\theta_{s+t,1}\frac{\sqrt{t}(\sqrt{s}+1)}{\sqrt{t}-1}\|x_{\mathcal{J}}\|_{2}^{2}.

(A.11)

On the other hand, we obtain by (
2.6

(A.12)

By (

(iv) Note by the mutual incoherence (

(v) Separating the diagonal and off-diagonal terms of

\|Ax_{\mathcal{J}}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}}\langle A_{i}x_{i},A_{i}x_{i}\rangle+\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{J}:i\neq j}\langle A_{i}x_{i},A_{j}x_{j}\rangle.
