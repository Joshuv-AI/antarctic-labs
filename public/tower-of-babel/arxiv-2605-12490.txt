DNN predictions for pp reference p T p_{\mathrm{T}} spectra at unmeasured s \sqrt{s\,}
Maria A. Calmon Behling , Mario Krüger , Jerome Jung and Henner Büsching

Abstract
Studies of the properties of the Quark–Gluon Plasma in high-energy heavy-ion collisions commonly facilitate proton–proton (pp) collisions at the same center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair as a reference measurement. In this paper, a deep neural network-based approach for interpolating and extrapolating pp reference transverse-momentum spectra to unmeasured energies is presented. The model is trained with ALICE data from LHC Runs 1 and 2 and provides predictions for center-of-mass energies relevant to LHC Run 3 and beyond.



1
Introduction

The measurement of high transverse momentum (
p_{\mathrm{T}}
) particles has proven to be very successful in characterizing properties of the hot and dense deconfined QCD matter, the Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP), formed in high-energy heavy-ion collisions (AA). Commonly, this is studied by comparing the particle production in heavy-ion collisions with the particle production in proton–proton (pp) collisions at the same center of mass energy per nucleon pair (
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}\,}
) by means of the nuclear modification factor (
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
)
[
14
,
15
,
21
,
7
]
.
In the absence of a suitable pp measurement, a pp reference is typically constructed from available measurements at other collision energies:
Either a baseline
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectrum is scaled by a theoretically predicted energy dependence
[
34
,
11
,
2
]
, or the pp reference is obtained directly by interpolating between two or more experimental measurements
[
14
,
21
,
1
,
10
,
4
]
.
The theory-driven approach is most reliable at high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, where perturbative QCD (pQCD) calculations are applicable, while at intermediate
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, the energy dependence can only be derived from Monte Carlo event generators such as PYTHIA
[
35
]
with limited accuracy
[
13
]
.
Data-driven interpolations, on the other hand, require the assumption of a functional form of the
\sqrt{s\,}
dependence.
At high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, functional forms assuming
x_{\mathrm{T}}
-scaling of the spectra can be employed
[
20
]
, while at lower
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, simple power-law parametrizations provide a reasonable description
[
10
]
. In
[
33
]
, however, it was shown that deep neural networks (DNNs) can be used to parametrize
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra as a function of
\sqrt{s\,}
. In contrast to the classical approaches, DNNs do not rely on assumptions about the energy dependence of particle production.

In this work, a data-driven, DNN-based method for constructing pp reference
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra is presented, and its applicability to ALICE data at LHC energies is demonstrated.

2
Dataset and data preparation

The DNN is trained with inclusive charged-particle
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra measured in pp collisions at five different LHC energies
[
9
]
(
{\sqrt{s\,}=2.76,}

5.02,\,7,\,8,

{\mathrm{and}\,13\,\mathrm{TeV}}
).
For each measured
\sqrt{s\,}
, the dataset contains 46
\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}p_{\mathrm{T}}
values with
{0.15<p_{\mathrm{T}}/(\mathrm{GeV}/c)<10}
.

To optimize the performance of the DNN model in terms of computation time and prediction accuracy, a logarithmic transformation to the input (
p_{\mathrm{T}}
,
\sqrt{s\,}
), and output (
\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}p_{\mathrm{T}}
) values of the DNN is applied to similarize their scale. This is especially relevant for
\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}p_{\mathrm{T}}
, spanning several orders of magnitude.
An additional scaling of
\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}p_{\mathrm{T}}
by
1/p_{\mathrm{T}}
prior to the logarithmic transformation eases the extrapolation of the training data towards low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
values.
To account for uncertainties of the data, each point is varied multiple times (500) within the bounds of the corresponding systematic uncertainty of the measurement.
Subsequently, the data are randomly reordered and split into training (80%) and validation (20%) datasets to prevent overfitting.
Additionally, the impact of low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
data points during the training is increased by using sample weights.

3
Model implementation

A fully connected DNN is implemented in Tensorflow
[
6
]
and trained by minimizing the mean absolute error (MAE) between the data and DNN predictions using the Nadam optimizer
[
24
]
.
A simulation-based hyperparameter scan is performed to determine the most suitable architecture for accurate DNN predictions at unmeasured
p_{\mathrm{T}}
and
\sqrt{s\,}
values, employing PYTHIA-simulated data (v.8.3.06, Monash 2013 tune)
[
36
]
.
For this study, only ALICE-equivalent data points in the simulation are used to train the model.
Simulated data points at unmeasured
p_{\mathrm{T}}
values and collision energies (selected in the range
{1.5\leq\sqrt{s\,}\leq$27\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$}
) are then used to test the corresponding prediction accuracy. The upper limit of this energy range is motivated by the proposed upgrade of the LHC, the High-Energy Large
Hadron Collider (HE-LHC), which foresees a potential increase in the maximum center-of-mass energy to
\sqrt{s\,}
=
27\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}

[
5
]
.
Six hyperparameters are varied in the scan: the number of hidden layers, the number of nodes per layer, the activation function, the initializer, the learning rate, and the batch size.
The optimal architectures are identified using Bayesian optimization
[
25
]
as an efficient hyperparameter search strategy, implemented using the Keras Tuner
[
31
]
package.
The overall best-performing architecture from the hyperparameter scan is chosen as the nominal architecture for the DNN model.

4
Uncertainty estimation

To estimate the uncertainties of the model predictions, two different contributions are considered: the aleatoric uncertainty from inherent randomness in the training process and the epistemic uncertainty from limited or sparse training data
[
26
]
.
Ensemble methods are used to determine these uncertainties.
The aleatoric uncertainty is quantified by training an ensemble of 20 DNN models with the nominal architecture, but different initialization values.
The nominal prediction of the DNN is defined as the average over all predictions within this ensemble, and the aleatoric uncertainty as the corresponding standard deviation.
To quantify the epistemic uncertainty, a second ensemble of five DNNs is trained with different architectures (the five best-performing architectures from the hyperparameter scan). The epistemic uncertainty for a given prediction is then calculated as the RMS of the deviations between the DNN model predictions in the ensemble to that of the nominal architecture.
Finally, the total model uncertainty is given by the quadratic sum over the aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties.

Performance evaluation

The extrapolation performance of the DNN architecture determined with PYTHIA (’PYTHIA-based DNN’) in the hyperparameter scan is evaluated using an independent dataset of EPOS LHC-simulated data
[
32

, as the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra in PYTHIA and EPOS LHC are different.
Again, only ALICE-equivalent data points in the simulation are used to train the DNN ensemble (’EPOS LHC-based DNN’).

Figure 1
shows the corresponding DNN predictions within a range of
{\sqrt{s\,}=1.5}

The interpolation performance within the energy range of the training data is equally precise.
However, the prediction accuracy decreases with energy difference to the training data: The largest deviations are observed at
{\sqrt{s\,}=$1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$}

Furthermore, the high-

Since the PYTHIA-simulated data was used to select the model architectures, the PYTHIA-based DNN is slightly more accurate than the EPOS LHC-based DNN. However, both provide very good predictions across the whole studied
\sqrt{s\,}
range, and deviations from the test data are generally covered by the assigned model uncertainties.

The ALICE-based DNN predictions can now be used to construct ’pp reference

As the event normalization of the training data and the pp reference spectra are different, this needs to be corrected for.
The approach used in the following is based on the ratios of DNN predictions and a correctly normalized spectrum at a baseline energy:
The pp reference
p_{\mathrm{T}}

\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{T}}}
is constructed from the baseline spectrum at an energy

\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{B}}}

is independent of the overall event normalization.
In order to scale

-differential cross sections, an additional normalization by the corresponding ratio of total inelastic pp cross sections
\sigma^{\mathrm{inel}}_{\mathrm{pp}}(\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{T}}})/\sigma^{\mathrm{inel}}_{\mathrm{pp}}(\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{B}}})

The values and uncertainties of

8
Summary

In this paper, a DNN-based method for constructing pp reference
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra at unmeasured
\sqrt{s\,}
is presented.
The DNN is trained with inclusive charged-particle
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra measured by the ALICE collaboration in pp collisions at five different LHC energies.
A hyperparameter scan based on PYTHIA simulations is performed to determine the most suitable architecture for accurate DNN predictions within and beyond the LHC energy range.
The extrapolation performance of the DNN architecture is evaluated using an independent dataset of EPOS LHC simulations.
The final DNN model is trained on the ALICE dataset and then used to construct pp reference
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra for calculating
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
at selected energies.
The DNN approach compares well with PYTHIA at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, with a power-law approach at low and intermediate
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, and with NLO pQCD calculations as well as
x_{\mathrm{T}}
-based interpolations at high
p_{\mathrm{T}}
.
It provides continuous predictions across a wide
p_{\mathrm{T}}
range without making assumptions about the physical processes underlying the spectra’s energy dependence.
