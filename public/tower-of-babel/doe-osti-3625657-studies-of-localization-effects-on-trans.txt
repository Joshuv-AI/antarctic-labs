 Abstract
 The forthcoming Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) requires precise modeling
 of neutrino–nucleus interactions to achieve its neutrino-oscillation measurement goals. GENIE,
 the Monte Carlo event generator used by DUNE and other Fermilab-based experiments as
 a central value model for neutrino-nucleus cross sections, exhibits known discrepancies in
 comparison to MicroBooNE cross section data, especially when considering neutrino scattering
 events’ transverse kinematic imbalance (TKI). For quasielastic-like measurements, the data
 show a larger transverse missing momentum tail while maintaining a peak similar to that
 predicted by the baseline GENIE model. Previous attempts at a solution include variations
 of final-state interaction (FSI) strengths, incorrectly decreasing both the peak and tail, thus
 leaving the discrepancy unresolved. This work investigates whether inconsistencies between local
 and global treatments of intranuclear physics contribute to the observed mismodeling. In this
 work, GENIE FSI routines have been modified to directly utilize intranuclear particle positions,
 thereby introducing a newly localized momentum treatment in correlation with the nominal
 density in both the hA and hN intranuclear cascade models for their 2018 and 2025 variants.
 Meson-exchange-current (MEC) localization was also tested for the hN 2018 model. Comparisons
 of the intranuclear nucleons’ ("scattering center") momentum and its radial dependence validate
 the localization implementation. However, while such consistent localization produces modest
 changes in the proton momentum spectrum at low-to-medium momenta, only small changes are
 observed in the TKI distributions. These changes are insufficient to account for the discrepancy
 with MicroBooNE data. Although a localized treatment improves the internal consistency of the
 GENIE model, the origin of the TKI discrepancy remains unresolved by such a solution.

1 Introduction
The Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE), currently under construction, will play
an important role in advancing our understanding of neutrino properties. To obtain precise
measurements of neutrino oscillations, DUNE must minimize both statistical and systematic
uncertainties. One major source of systematic uncertainty remains the modeling neutrino scattering
cross sections, which when convolved with modeled fluxes generates event distributions (spectra)
which can ultimately be measured in a given detector. Monte Carlo event generators are used
to simulate such neutrino interactions and model this relationship; consequently, the accuracy of

 1

these generators directly affects the interpretive precision of the experiment when actually fitting
oscillation parameters.
 DUNE, along with the suite of Fermilab based liquid argon time projection chamber experi-
ments of the Short Baseline Neutrino Facility, utilizes GENIE, an open-source Monte Carlo event
generator which can simulate neutrino, electron, and hadron scattering interactions on nuclei. It
is essential to validate GENIE event predictions coming from their available internal cross section
models against external cross section measurements from existing experiments, hopefully identifying
potential mismodeling of neutrino–nucleus interactions. One known issue is a discrepancy between
GENIE predictions and MicroBooNE measurements of transverse kinematic imbalance (TKI). The
MicroBooNE data exhibit a larger high-TKI tail while retaining a peak similar to that predicted by
the baseline GENIE model. Previous attempts to vary the strength of final-state interactions (FSI)
altered both the peak and the tail. The objective of this project is therefore to determine whether a
modified treatment of intranuclear interactions can increase the predicted TKI tail while preserving
the peak and thereby improve agreement with the data.

1.1 Transverse Kinematic Imbalance
Transverse kinematic imbalance is an effective measure of several nuclear effects, including among
others the initial momentum of the struck nucleon within the nucleus along with the strength of
intranuclear rescattering of generated products before exiting the nucleus (final state interactions).
As seen in Fig. ??, this can be considered by looking at the transverse components of the outgoing
particles’ momenta, and summing them together to find the resultant transverse momentum. If the
nucleus had no momentum when struck, because the neutrino is moving directly perpendicular to
the transverse plane, by momentum conservation the transverse momenta should be balanced and
the resultant would be zero. When the nucleus has momentum it introduces an imbalance in the
transverse momentum that can be measured as the resultant transverse momentum. As a result, the
transverse momentum imbalance (or transverse kinematic imbalance) gives information about the
momentum of the nucleus, which can be used to try to find modeling issues in nuclear behavior. The
incident neutrino travels approximately parallel to the beam direction, so its transverse momentum
is negligible. The transverse momentum imbalance is therefore defined as the vector sum of the
outgoing lepton and proton transverse momenta:

 This work examines inconsistencies in the spatial treatment of processes within GENIE. In the
baseline configuration considered here, the FSI and meson-exchange-current (MEC) routines use
global nuclear models, whereas other intranuclear effects are modeled locally. We modify the FSI and
MEC routines to incorporate local position information and construct corresponding comprehensive
model configurations (CMCs). The FSI modification is evaluated using the hA and hN intranuclear
models in both their 2018 and 2025 implementations. MEC localization is also examined for hN
2018.

 The study addresses the model–data discrepancies reported in Ref. [1] by varying one component
of the generator at a time, allowing the effect of each modification to be isolated. Section 2 describes
the generator modifications, configurations, event samples, and analysis procedure. Section 3

2 Methods
2.1 Generator modifications
To localize FSI, a function call within the GENIE INTRANUKE code was modified. The change
was applied to HAIntranuke2018, HAIntranuke2025, HNIntranuke2018, and HNIntranuke2025. In
each case, the position of the propagating particle was passed to the GenerateNucleon function,
enabling nucleon generation to depend on the local nuclear environment.
 MEC localization was implemented in a similar manner by passing the position of the nucleon
cluster to GenerateNucleon. The FSI and MEC modifications were tested both independently
and together where event samples were available. Because of time constraints and the small effect
observed in preliminary results, MEC localization was evaluated only for the hN 2018 model.
 The GENIE master-branch G18_10a CMC was used as the baseline configuration. Modified
configurations were then constructed to enable or disable the localized INTRANUKE routines.
For each principal FSI comparison, equal numbers of events were generated with the baseline and
localized configurations. The hA 2018 samples each contain 1 million events, while the hA 2025,
hN 2018, and hN 2025 samples each contain 5 million events. The hN 2018 localized-MEC and
combined localized-FSI-and-MEC samples each contain 100,000 events.

2.2 Analysis procedure
Following event generation, each output ROOT file was converted to RooTracker format for analysis
with a ROOT plotting macro developed for this project. The macro produces distributions for more
than 30 observables, permitting direct comparisons among the generator configurations. TKI is
the primary observable of interest, while the proton momentum spectrum provides a more direct
measure of the effects of FSI and MEC localization. The position and momentum of the intranuclear
scattering center are also examined to verify that the localized FSI treatment behaves as intended.

3 Results
The first stage of the analysis verifies the implementation of localization. As shown in Fig. 2,
the intranuclear scattering-center momentum distributions clearly differ between the baseline and
localized samples. In the baseline sample, the distribution rises to a pronounced feature near
0.25 GeV/c. The localized sample instead exhibits a broader, smoother distribution with a maximum
near 0.2 GeV/c.
 A local-density treatment should also produce a decrease in characteristic nucleon momentum
with increasing distance from the center of the nucleus. To examine this further, the momentum
magnitude of the inferred initial intranuclear scattering center is reconstructed by momentum
conservation from a clean two-body FSI cascade vertex. We sum the momentum vectors of the
daughter particles, and subtract the momentum vector of the parent particle. As shown in Fig. 3,
In the baseline GENIE model, the scattering-center momentum shows little dependence on the
nuclear radius. In the localized model, the momentum decreases with increasing radius, as expected.
Together, these comparisons indicate that the FSI localization was successfully implemented.
 The proton momentum spectra were then compared between the localized and nonlocalized
configurations. In Fig. 4, the red and pink curves represent the localized samples without and with
MicroBooNE-like thresholds and selection cuts, respectively; the blue and black curves represent
the corresponding nonlocalized samples. Without thresholds or cuts, a difference is visible between
approximately 0.2 GeV/c and 0.5 GeV/c, while the remainder of the distributions is largely unchanged.
After applying the thresholds and cuts most relevant to the TKI comparison, the observable difference
is restricted primarily to the range from 0.2 GeV/c to 0.5 GeV/c and is substantially reduced.
 As in Fig. 5, the TKI distributions show only a small difference between the localized and
nonlocalized configurations, concentrated between approximately 0.25 GeV and 0.40 GeV. This
change is considerably smaller than the discrepancy previously observed between the GENIE baseline
prediction and the MicroBooNE data.

4 Conclusion
Localizing the GENIE FSI treatment produces the expected changes in the intranuclear scattering-
center momentum and its radial dependence, confirming that the modification functions as intended.
The localized treatment also produces modest changes in the proton momentum spectrum. Its effect
on the TKI distribution, however, is small and cannot account for the discrepancy between GENIE
predictions and MicroBooNE measurements.
 Although localization does not resolve the TKI discrepancy, incorporating it may improve the
internal consistency of GENIE by applying a local treatment across intranuclear processes. Further
investigation is required to identify the physical or modeling effects responsible for the enhanced
TKI tail observed by MicroBooNE.
