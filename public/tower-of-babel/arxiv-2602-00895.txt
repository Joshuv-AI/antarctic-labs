Abstract
. Assuming A A has maximal L p L^{p} -regularity, this paper investigates perturbations of A A by time-dependent operators B B that are unbounded and satisfy a critical L q L^{q} -integrability condition in time. We establish two main results. The first proves maximal L p L^{p} -regularity for the critical endpoint case, generalizing previous work by Prüss and Schnaubelt (2001). The second develops a weighted maximal regularity theory for mixed-scale perturbations, motivated by the linearized skeleton equations appearing in large deviations theory for stochastic PDEs.

1.
Introduction

The theory of maximal
L^{p}
-regularity has for many years played an important role in the study of nonlinear evolution equations. For details, the reader is referred to
[
4
,
12
,
18
,
27
]
and the surveys
[
11
,
25
,
30
]
.
Recently, there have also been significant developments in the setting of stochastic evolution equations. An overview can be found in
[
3
]
.

While this paper focuses exclusively on deterministic evolution equations, our primary motivation stems from large deviations theory for stochastic evolution equations.
Establishing a large deviation principle requires the analysis of a nonstandard perturbed equation known as the skeleton equation (discussed below). A main objective of this paper is to prove maximal
L^{p}
-regularity for its linearized variant – a problem that has previously been understood only in special cases.

For
p=2
, within a variational setting,
this was considered in our recent paper
[
28
, Cor. 3.5]
(see also the references therein). A similar problem was studied in
[
8
, §4]
to allow for certain singular coefficients in a variational setting.
The linear part of the analysis in these papers treats the case where
A\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})
is a
coercive
time-dependent operator in a variational framework. Here, we are given a Gelfand triple
(X_{1},X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},X_{0})
of Hilbert spaces, where
X_{1}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
densely and continuously and
X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}=[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
is the complex interpolation space. From Lions’ theory, it is well-known that
A
has maximal
L^{2}
-regularity.

The linearized
skeleton equation
appearing in large deviations theory takes the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}+Au+Bu=f+g,

with
u(0)=u_{0}\in X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
. Here,
B\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))
,
f\in L^{2}(0,T;X_{0})
and
g\in L^{1}(0,T;X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})
. For details on the precise form of
B
, we refer to Subsection
1.2
.
The perturbation
B
is singular in time, as
\|B(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})}
is allowed to be unbounded. Moreover, the perturbation is critical: the space-time Sobolev indices of the leading term
Au
and the perturbation
Bu
coincide since
-\frac{1}{2}+0=-\frac{1}{2}
and
-\frac{1}{1}+\frac{1}{2}=-\frac{1}{2}
, respectively.
1
1

1

In general the space-time Sobolev index of
v\in L^{p}(0,T;X_{\theta})
is
-\frac{1}{p}+\theta
.

Thus,
B
is a borderline perturbation regarding the space-time Sobolev index.
Consequently, it is not obvious that the perturbed operator
A+B
preserves maximal
L^{2}
-regularity.

In
[
28
, §3]
, we developed new techniques to establish maximal
L^{2}
-regularity of
A+B
, relying on the coercivity of
A
. In the current paper, we also cover the
non-coercive
case in Section
4
, thus enabling applications to large deviations in
L^{2}
-settings where the leading operator is not necessarily coercive.

To develop an
L^{p}
-theory of large deviations with
p\in(2,\infty)
, we require an
L^{p}
-variant of the aforementioned results. In this setting, the endpoint
L^{1}
case is no longer required, allowing us to use a different approach compared to previous work.
Indeed, replacing
L^{2}
by
L^{p}
with
p\in(2,\infty)
in the above setting, the natural condition on
B
turns out to be
B\in L^{r}(0,T;\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{r}
, and thus
r\in(1,2)
. Here, the space-time Sobolev indices of
Au
and
Bu
still coincide (both are
-\frac{1}{p}
), showing that
B
is in this sense a critical perturbation of
A
.

While the literature on perturbation results is vast, most works are restricted to the autonomous setting. For time-dependent and singular perturbations, results on maximal
L^{p}
-regularity are scarce. The works most related to our setting include
[
5
,
26
]
.

In this paper, we obtain two perturbation theorems for maximal
L^{p}
-regularity in an abstract evolution equation setting:

(1)

A generalization of the perturbation result on maximal
L^{p}
-regularity obtained in
[
26
, Th. 3.1]
.

(2)

A maximal
L^{p}
-regularity theory for linear skeleton equations as they appear in large deviations theory, and generalizations thereof.

The starting point will always be a time-dependent operator family
A
which has maximal
L^{p}
-regularity on a Banach space
X_{0}
. The operator
B
will be a lower order
perturbation which is singular in time, and which satisfies a borderline integrability condition.
While our two perturbation results are related, their strongest versions are obtained by treating the settings separately. This separation also benefits the exposition, as the first result involves fewer technical complexities.

In the next two subsections, we state special cases of the main results.

1.1.
Setting with full maximal
L^{p}
-regularity

Suppose that the following assumption holds:

Assumption 1.1
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
be Banach spaces such that
X_{1}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
densely and continuously.
Suppose that
A\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})
is strongly measurable in the strong operator topology.

Note that we allow
\|A(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})}
to be unbounded in time.
Given that
A
has maximal
L^{p}
-regularity for a given
p\in(1,\infty)
(see Definition
2.1
), we want to find conditions under which
A+B
has maximal
L^{p}
-regularity as well, where
B
is a singular lower order perturbation.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in(p,\infty)
. Suppose that
A
has maximal
L^{p}
-regularity. Let
B\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1},X_{0})
be strongly measurable in the strong operator topology, and suppose that

\|B(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1},X_{0})}\leq b(t),\qquad t\in[0,T],

where
b\in L^{q}(0,T)
. Then
A+B
has maximal
L^{p}
-regularity.

The above theorem is a special case of Theorem
2.5
.
The main difficulty in the proof of the above is that
B
is a singular perturbation which is also critical in the sense that the
L^{q}
-integrability condition on
b
is optimal.
Indeed, by trace theory, the maximal regularity space
L^{p}(0,T;X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,T;X_{0})
embeds into
L^{s}(0,T;X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1})
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{s}
(see
[
18
, Th. L.4.4]
).
Combined with Hölder’s inequality, we see that

\|Bu\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})}\leq\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}\|u\|_{L^{s}(0,T;X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1})}\lesssim C_{b}\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,T;X_{0})},

whenever
u
belongs to the maximal regularity space. This shows that the integrability condition on
b
is natural, but also critical. Note that
X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1}
can be replaced by the complex interpolation space
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}
or any intermediate space
X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}
which is of class
J_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}
between
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
(see
[
22
, Chap. 1]
). Indeed, this follows from
X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1}\hookrightarrow X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}
.

The limiting case
q=p
can also be included if the space
X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1}
is replaced by the trace space
X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}
. The latter case is easier to prove and is presented in Theorem
2.6
.

Our findings generalize the perturbation result of
[
26
, Th. 3.1]
. Therein, maximal
L^{p}
-regularity of
A+B
is also established under the condition
\|B(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1},X_{0})}\leq b(\cdot)
, but it is required that
b\in L^{r}(0,T)
with
r>q
. We address the critical endpoint
r=q
.
Other differences between our result and
[
26
, Th. 3.1]
are
that we do not assume continuity of
A
and we include temporal weights to cover rough initial data.

1.2.
Setting beyond maximal
L^{p}
-regularity

In this section, we state a special case of our second main result. This setting is designed specifically to cover the linear maximal regularity theory required for our follow-up work on nonlinear skeleton equations and their applications to large deviations.
In the introduction, for simplicity, we only consider the case where
A
is time-independent, and refer for the case of time-dependent
A
to Theorem
3.11
.

In the weak convergence approach
[
10
]
to large deviations for SPDE (see
[
15
,
28
]
and references therein), a multiplicative stochastic term
\mathcal{B}(t)u(t)dW(t)
, where
\mathcal{B}(t)\colon X_{1}\to X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
is replaced by
\mathcal{B}(t)\phi(t)dt
with
\mathcal{B}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))
and
\phi\in L^{2}(0,T)
. Here
W
could be a standard Brownian motion, or (with minor modifications to
\mathcal{B}
and
\phi
), a cylindrical Brownian motion. It turns out that we need to understand maximal regularity for the problem

(1.2)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(t)+Au(t)+B(t)u(t)=f(t)+g(t),

with
u(0)=u_{0}
, and where
B(t)\coloneqq\mathcal{B}(t)\phi(t)u(t)
. Nonlinear terms can be included afterwards by linearization techniques. Thus, we first focus on the leading order linear terms.
Typically, one has
B\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}))
, where
X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
could be the real interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},q}
(with
q\in[1,\infty]
) or the complex interpolation space
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
. As mentioned before in (
1.1
), such a
B
is a critical perturbation.

Now if
A
has maximal
L^{p}
-regularity, then we need to prove that for
f\in L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})
and
g\in L^{r}(0,T;X_{\gamma})
with
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2}
, the problem above has a solution
u\in L^{p}(0,T;X_{1})
. For stochastic problems, we need
p\in[2,\infty)
, so that
r\in[1,2)
. The case
p=2
,
r=1
is an exceptional endpoint, which was already mentioned in (
1.1
), but we return to it in Section
4
. Below, we focus on
p\in(2,\infty)
for convenience.

There is a connection to Theorem
1.2
in the case
q=2
. However, a difference is that the solution to (
1.2
) cannot be in
W^{1,p}(0,T;X_{0})
. Indeed,
Bu
and
g
are not assumed to be in
L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})
, but only in
L^{r}(0,T;X_{\gamma})
. Thus, the time regularity has to be described in a different way. The best strategy seems to be the use of a sum of maximal regularity spaces for
u
:

(1.3)

\Big(L^{p}(0,T;X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,T;X_{0})\Big)+\Big(L^{r}(0,T;X_{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}})\cap W^{1,r}(0,T;X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})\Big).

Indeed, since
Bu
and
g
are in
L^{r}(0,T;X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})
, the above appears to be the optimal space for
u
if we apply both maximal
L^{p}
-regularity and maximal
L^{r}
-regularity to the respective parts of the equation. Note that the space-time Sobolev indices of the components in the sum above coincide:
1-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{r}
. We prove that this sum maximal regularity space still embeds into

L^{p}(0,T;X_{1})\cap C([0,T];X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}),

which is a typical regularity class for stochastic evolution equations.

We highlight here the following special case of Theorem
3.11
and Corollary
3.13
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
holds, and that
A
is time-independent. Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and suppose that
A
has maximal
L^{p}
-regularity. Let
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2}
and suppose that
B\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})
is strongly measurable in the strong operator topology, and suppose that

\|B(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})}\leq b(t),\qquad t\in[0,T],

where
b\in L^{2}(0,T)
. Then for all
u_{0}\in X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}
,
f\in L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})
and
g\in L^{r}(0,T;X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})
, there exists a unique strong solution
u
to (
1.2
) in the space (
1.3
). Moreover,
u\in C([0,T];X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})
.

In the main text we also cover the more general case
B(t)\in\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\gamma})
, where again
X_{\gamma}
could, for instance, be any complex or real interpolation space.

Finally, the limiting case
r=1
is considered in Section
4
.
In particular, we present a version of Theorem
1.3
with
p=2
, where
X_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
is the real interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},2}
.

2.
Perturbations of maximal
L^{p}
-regularity

In this section, we establish a new perturbation result for maximal
L^{p}
-regularity, which implies Theorem
1.2
. We begin by outlining the necessary preliminaries regarding maximal
L^{p}
-regularity for operator families
A
that are singular in time.

2.1.
Maximal
L^{p}
-regularity

Let
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
be Banach spaces such that
X_{1}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
densely and continuously.
Suppose that
A\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})
is strongly measurable in the strong operator topology.
In contrast to standard literature, we allow the map
t\mapsto\|A(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})}
to be unbounded, a level of generality required for Theorem
1.2
.

For
f\in L^{1}(0,T;X_{0})
, we consider the problem

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+Au&=f,\\
u(0)&=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

A function
u\in L^{1}(0,T;X_{1})\cap W^{1,1}(0,T;X_{0})
is called a
strong solution
to (
2.1
) if
Au\in L^{1}(0,T;X_{0})
and for all
t\in[0,T]
,

u(t)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}A(s)u(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds.

Note that in this case
u\in C([0,T];X_{0})
and this is the version of
u
that we will use from now on. Clearly, this definition can be extended to other intervals
(a,b)
.

To relax the regularity conditions on the initial data, we employ power-weighted
L^{p}
-spaces. We adopt the following notation. For
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
, let
w_{\kappa}(t)\coloneqq|t|^{\kappa}
and let
L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X)
denote the space of all strongly measurable
f\colon(a,b)\to X_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\coloneqq\Big(\int_{a}^{b}t^{\kappa}\|f(t)\|_{X_{0}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}<\infty

One can check that
L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(a,b;X_{0})
. The space
W^{1,p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
is defined in a similar way.

In the sequel, for
0\leq a<b\leq\infty
, we will use the shorthand notation

(2.2)

\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})\coloneqq L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0}).

The above space will be equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})}\coloneqq\max\{\|u\|_{L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{1})},\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\},

where we emphasize that we do not shift the weight. When
\kappa=0
, we will write
\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b)
. Note that for
a>0
, one has
\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})=\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b)
isomorphically since the weight is bounded from above and below.

We only consider
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
.
The lower bound on
\kappa
is motivated by the goal of enlarging the space of initial values by including the power weight.
The upper bound on
\kappa
ensures that the functions we consider are integrable on
[0,T]
.

Recall from
[
18
, Cor. L.4.6]
that the following trace embedding results hold:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C([0,T];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,T];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}),

where the embedding constants can be taken independent of
T
if one restricts to functions that vanish at
t=0
. Here,
C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,T];Y)
is the space of continuous functions
u\colon(0,T]\to Y
for which
\sup_{t\in(0,T]}t^{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}\|u(t)\|_{Y}<\infty
.

Although
A
is allowed to be unbounded in time, we will often suppose that there is a constant
C_{A}
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|Av\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\leq C_{A}\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})},\qquad v\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa}).

Due to this condition, it is clear that for
v\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
one has
v^{\prime},Av\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
and

\displaystyle\|v^{\prime}+Av\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\|Av\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\leq(1+C_{A})\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})}.

Maximal regularity theory addresses the validity of the converse estimate:

Definition 2.1
.

Let Assumption
1.1
be satisfied.
Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
. The operator family
A
is said to have
maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
-regularity
if
for every
f\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
to (
2.1
) with
u_{0}=0
, and there is a constant
C
independent of
f
such that for any
\tau\in(0,T]
and any strong solution
v\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})
to (
2.1
) with
u_{0}=0
one has

(2.6)

\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

The infimum of all admissible constants
C
will be denoted by
M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
and is called the
maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
-regularity constant
.

If the underlying space
X_{0}
is clear from the context, we will write ‘maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity’.

The definition above extends to subintervals
(a,b)\subseteq(0,T)
by replacing
(0,T)
by
(a,b)
,
u(0)
by
u(a)
and taking
\tau\in(a,b]
.

Note that the existence and uniqueness for all
f\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
require
X_{1}
to be chosen appropriately.
Also, one can conclude
Au\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
not only from (
2.5
), but also from the identity
Au=f-u^{\prime}
that holds a.e.
[
16
, Lem. 2.5.8]
.

Since we incorporated some flexibility into the above definition by allowing for arbitrary
\tau
in (
2.6
), we obtain the following result on restriction to subintervals. Without the flexibility with
\tau
in (
2.6
), it is unclear whether the uniqueness of solutions extends to subintervals
(0,\tau)
. In the autonomous setting, these problems can be solved in a different way (see
[
18
, §17.2]
). Some discussion on the nonautonomous setting can also be found in
[
7
]
.

We consider the following problem on
(a,b)\subset(0,T)
:

(2.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+Au&=f,\\
u(a)&=u_{a}.\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 2.2

(Change of interval)
.

Let Assumption
1.1
be satisfied. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
. Suppose that

A
has maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity.
For any
0\leq a<b\leq T
and
f\in L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
, there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})
to (
2.7
) with
u_{a}=0
, and

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})}\leq M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)\|f\|_{L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

In particular,
A
has maximal
L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})
-regularity and

M_{p,\kappa,A}(a,b)\leq M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
.

Proof.

We first prove uniqueness. Let
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})
be a strong solution to
(
2.7
) with
u_{a}=0
.
Extending
u
by zero on
(0,a)
, one has
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa})
and
u
is a strong solution to (
2.1
) on
(0,b)
with
u_{0}=0
. Thus (
2.6
) implies that
u=0
.

For existence let
f\in L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
. Extend
f
by zero outside
(a,b)
. Let
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
be the corresponding strong solution to (
2.1
) with
u_{0}=0
. Then
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})
,
u
solves (
2.7
) with
u_{a}=0
, and for every constant
C
admissible for (
2.6
), we have

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})}\leq\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}=C\|f\|_{L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

∎

The following lemma is standard (see
[
2
, Prop. 3.10]
and
[
7
, Lem. 2.2]
) and holds in our generalized setting with only minor modifications.
As a consequence of the lemma, we can always assume
u_{0}=0
when analyzing (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let Assumption
1.1
be satisfied.
Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
. Suppose that (
2.5
) holds and
A
has maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity with constant
M\coloneqq M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

Let
0<b\leq T
. For every
u_{0}\in X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}
and
f\in L^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa})
to (
2.1
), and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa})}\leq\tfrac{12p}{1+\kappa}\big(M(1\vee C_{A})+1\big)\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

(2)

Let
0<a<b\leq T
. For every
u_{a}\in X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}
and
f\in L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})
to (
2.7
), and

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})}\leq 12pT^{\kappa/p}\big(M(1\vee C_{A})+1\big)\|u_{a}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

Proof.

(1): Uniqueness is clear. To prove the existence and estimate, we exploit the trace method for real interpolation (see
[
18
, §L.2]
and
[
29
, §1.8]
). Let
z\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})
be such that
z(0)=u_{0}
.
Note that
z^{\prime},Az\in L^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
, using (
2.5
).
Let
v\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa})
be the unique solution to the problem
v^{\prime}+Av=f-z^{\prime}-Az
with
v(0)=0
. Then
u\coloneqq v+z
satisfies (
2.1
).
Moreover,

\displaystyle\|u

\displaystyle\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa})}\leq\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa})}+\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq M\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\|z^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\|Az\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\big)+\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\big(M(1\vee C_{A})+1\big)\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})},

applying (
2.5
) in the last line.
Taking the infimum over all
z\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})
, the stated estimate follows from the trace method for real interpolation, see
[
18
, Th. L.2.3]
for the constant.

(2): Uniqueness is clear from Lemma
2.2
. To prove existence, we use the same strategy as above. Let
z\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,\infty)
be such that
z(a)=u_{a}
. We repeat the construction of
v
of (1), but this time on
(a,b)
, and we again put
u\coloneqq v+z
. By Lemma
2.2
and a computation similar to that in part (1), we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\big(M(1\vee C_{A})+1\big)\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,T,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}(a,b,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+T^{\kappa/p}\big(M(1\vee C_{A})+1\big)\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,\infty)}.

Taking the infimum over all
z\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(a,\infty)
, the claim follows as before and by a simple shift.
∎

A consequence of the theory above, is that if
A
satisfies (
2.5
) and has maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity then the following mapping is an isomorphism:

\mathcal{J}\colon X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}\times L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})\to\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa}),\ \ \mathcal{J}(u_{0},f)=u,

where
u
is the strong solution to (
2.1
).
Indeed, (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) give respectively

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\leq(1+C_{A})\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})}.

Combining this with Lemma
2.3
(1), we obtain the required two-sided estimates showing that
\mathcal{J}
is an isomorphism.

Many standard results of the autonomous setting fail for the setting of time-dependent
A
. Some of them we collect in the next remark.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

Extrapolation in
p
fails in general.
Counterexamples to extrapolation for singular families
A
appear in
[
26
]
. Counterexamples also exist in the uniformly bounded setting, for instance with differential operators in divergence form, as considered in
[
9
,
19
]
. Therein, the coefficients are bounded and uniformly elliptic, and measurable in
(t,x)
.

(2)

Extrapolation in
p
can be obtained if
A
is (relatively) continuous in time (see
[
7
,
26
]
), in which case one can reduce to the autonomous setting. This is, for instance, considered in
[
18
, Th. 17.2.26]
.

(3)

Maximal
L^{p}
-regularity does not seem to imply that
A
generates an evolution family on
X_{0}
. Indeed, in the case
A
depends on time in a measurable way, maximal
L^{p}
-regularity is known to hold in various situations (cf.
[
13
,
14
]
), but it is unclear whether
A
generates an evolution family on
X_{0}
. An evolution system can be constructed on certain real interpolation spaces via Lemma
2.3
.
Moreover, for
\kappa=0
, and continuous
t\mapsto A(t)\in\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})
, an evolution family does exist as was shown in
[
26
, Th. 2.5]
, but even that case is highly nontrivial.

2.2.
Main result

We now present the main result of this section. It contains Theorem
1.2
as a special case.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
and
q\in(p,\infty)
. Suppose that Assumption
1.1
and (
2.5
) hold and that
A
has maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity. Let
B\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1},X_{0})
be strongly measurable in the strong operator topology and suppose that

\|B(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1},X_{0})}\leq b(\cdot)
, where
b\in L^{q}(0,T)
. Then
A+B
has maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
–regularity with
M_{p,\kappa,A+B}(0,T)\leq C
, where
C
only depends on
p,q,\kappa,X_{0},X_{1},M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
and
\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}
.

Recall that nonzero initial values can be added as a consequence of Lemma
2.3
.
From Step 0 of the proof below, it follows that
B
satisfies

\|Bv\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})},\qquad v\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa}),t\in(0,T],

see (
2.8
). Consequently,
A+B
also has the mapping property (
2.5
).

For relatively bounded perturbations
B
, maximal
L^{p}
-regularity of
A+B
is typically established via the method of continuity
[
18
, Prop. 17.2.49]
. This approach appears inapplicable in our setting.
Consequently, the proof of Theorem
2.5
utilizes a gluing procedure with nonstandard features. In particular, due to the weight, it is nontrivial to show that the constant
C
only depends on
\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}
and not on the shape of the function
b
itself. The dependence on
\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}
is exponential, and this is sharp in general. In Theorem
2.6
we will treat the case
p=q
, which turns out to be much simpler.

A repeated application of Theorem
2.5
further extends the result to
A+\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}
, where
\|B_{i}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q_{i}}}},X_{0})}\leq b_{i}(t)
and
b_{i}\in L^{q_{i}}(0,T)
with
q_{i}\in(p,\infty)
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Step 0:
As
T,p,\kappa,
and
A
are fixed throughout the proof, we simplify notation by writing
M
for
M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
.
Let
\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(s,t,w_{\kappa})
be the subspace of
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(s,t,w_{\kappa})
such that
u(s)=0
.

Let
t\in(0,T]
. For
v\in\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})
, we have by Hölder’s inequality:

\|Bv\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\leq\big\|b\|v\|_{X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1}}\big\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})}\leq\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,t)}\|vw_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}\|_{L^{\frac{pq}{q-p}}(0,t;X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1})}.

Note that
(X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p},X_{1})_{1-\frac{p}{q},1}=X_{1-\frac{1}{q},1}
by reiteration (see
[
18
, Th. L.3.1]
), so

\displaystyle\|vw_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}\|_{X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1}}\lesssim\|vw_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1}{p}},p}^{\frac{p}{q}}\|vw_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}\|_{X_{1}}^{1-\frac{p}{q}}.

Combined with (
2.4
),
we obtain the following estimates with
t
-independent constants (since
v\in\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})
):

\displaystyle\|vw_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}\|_{L^{\frac{pq}{q-p}}(0,t;X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|v(s)w_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}(s)\|_{X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}}^{\frac{p}{q}}\|vw_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}\|_{L^{p}(0,t;X_{1})}^{1-\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})}^{\frac{p}{q}}\|v\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})}^{1-\frac{p}{q}}=\|v\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})}.

We conclude that for a constant
C_{0}
depending on
X_{0},X_{1},p,q
and
\kappa
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|Bv\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\leq C_{0}\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,t)}\|v\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})}.

The estimates above also hold for
v\in{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})
, but in the latter case, the constant would depend on
t
.

The strategy for the remainder of the proof is to construct local solutions on small time intervals and glue them together. In Step 1, the initial condition is zero, but in Step 2, we address nonzero initial conditions as well.

Step 1:

Let
\tau
be such that
\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,\tau)}=\frac{1}{2C_{0}M}
if possible, and otherwise set
\tau=T
. In the latter case, no further steps are needed.

Let
\Phi\colon\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})\to\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})
be given by
\Phi(v)=u
, where
u
solves

u^{\prime}+Au=-Bv+f.

By the maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity of
A
and Lemma
2.2
, the mapping
\Phi
is well-defined and with (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq M\|B(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq MC_{0}\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,\tau)}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}.

By the contraction mapping theorem there exists a unique
u\in\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})
such that
u=\Phi(u)
, which implies that there is a unique strong solution
u\in\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})
to
u^{\prime}+(A+B)u=f
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}=\|\Phi(u)\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(0)\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}+\|\Phi(0)\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

Thus

(2.9)

\|u\|_{{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}\leq 2M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

Step 2:
We proceed iteratively.

Let
\tau_{1}\coloneqq\tau
. Let
n\geq 1
be given and suppose that
0<\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{n}<T
are given and
u\in\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa})
is the unique strong solution within
\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa})
to
u^{\prime}+Au+Bu=f
. We will extend the solution uniquely to
u\in\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
defined on
[0,\tau_{n+1}]
. Additionally, we will establish a recursive energy estimate that will be utilized in Step 3.

Choose
\tau_{n+1}
such that
\|b\|_{L^{q}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1})}=\frac{1}{2C_{0}M}
if possible, and otherwise set
\tau_{n+1}\coloneqq T
and turn to Step 3.
From (
2.4
) we know that
u(\tau_{n})\in X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}
. By Lemma
2.3
there exists a unique strong solution
\widetilde{z}\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \widetilde{z}\kern 0.89996pt^{\prime}+A\widetilde{z}&=0,\\
\widetilde{z}(\tau_{n})&=u(\tau_{n}).\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\Phi_{n}\colon\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})\to\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
be given by
\Phi_{n}(v)=z
, where
z
solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} z^{\prime}+Az&=-B(v){{\bf 1}}_{[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]}-B(\widetilde{z}){{\bf 1}}_{[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]}+f{{\bf 1}}_{[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]},\\
z(0)&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

By maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity,
\Phi_{n}
is well-defined, and by (
2.8
),

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{n}(v_{1})-\Phi_{n}(v_{2})\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq M\|B(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{L^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq MC_{0}\|b\|_{L^{q}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1})}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}.

Therefore, there exists a unique
z\in\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
such that
z=\Phi_{n}(z)
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{n}(z)-\Phi_{n}(0)\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}+\|\Phi_{n}(0)\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}+M\|B\widetilde{z}\|_{L^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

Thus

\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}\leq 2M\|B\widetilde{z}\|_{L^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+2M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

Now extend
u
by defining

u=z|_{[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]}+\widetilde{z}\quad\text{ on $[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]$}.

Then
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
is a strong solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} y^{\prime}+Ay+By&=f,\\
y(\tau_{n})&=u(\tau_{n}).\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, the estimates above yield

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|u

\displaystyle\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}\leq\|\widetilde{z}\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}+\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{z}\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}+2M\|B\widetilde{z}\|_{L^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+2M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

It remains to bound the norms of
\widetilde{z}
and
B\widetilde{z}
. To this end, consider
v\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
defined by
v=u
on
[0,\tau_{n}]
and
v=\widetilde{z}
on
[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]
. Then
v
is a strong solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} v^{\prime}+Av&=-Bv{{\bf 1}}_{[0,\tau_{n}]}+f{{\bf 1}}_{[0,\tau_{n}]},\\
v(0)&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and by maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity and (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq M\|Bv\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq MC_{0}\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,\tau_{n})}\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa})}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{2}\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa})}+M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

For the last inequality above, we use that
v=u
on
[0,\tau_{n}]
, and by construction,

\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,\tau_{n})}=\frac{n^{1/q}}{2C_{0}M}\leq\frac{n}{2C_{0}M}.

Now we estimate
\widetilde{z}
and
B\widetilde{z}
in terms of
v
.
For the
\widetilde{z}
-term, we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{z}\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})},

and for the
B\widetilde{z}
-term, by (
2.8
) applied to
{{\bf 1}}_{[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]}B
, we have

\displaystyle\|B\widetilde{z}\|_{L^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|{{\bf 1}}_{[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]}Bv\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|b\|_{L^{q}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1})}\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq(2M)^{-1}\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}.

Combining (
2.10
) with the estimates for
B\widetilde{z}
,
\widetilde{z}
and
v
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa})}+\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa})}+2\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})}+2M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq(1+n)\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n},w_{\kappa})}+4M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

Note that the extended
u
is a strong solution on
[0,\tau_{n+1}]
. Moreover,
u
is continuous at
\tau_{n}
, so by
[
16
, Prop. 2.5.9]
, we conclude that
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
.
To complete the induction step, it remains to prove that
u
is the unique strong solution within
\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
. Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa})
both be strong solutions. By uniqueness of the solution
u
on
[0,\tau_{n}]
, we find
u_{1}=u_{2}
on
[0,\tau_{n}]
and
u_{1}(\tau_{n})=u_{2}(\tau_{n})=u(\tau_{n})
in
X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}
by (
2.4
). Therefore, defining
w_{i}\coloneqq(u_{i}-\widetilde{z}){{\bf 1}}_{[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]}
for
i=1,2
, we have
w_{i}\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1})
(since
w_{i}(\tau_{n})=0
) and
w_{i}=\Phi_{n}(w_{i})
. By uniqueness of the fixed point of
\Phi_{n}
, we can conclude that
w_{1}=w_{2}
, hence
u_{1}=u_{2}
on
[0,\tau_{n+1}]
.

Step 3:
Let
N
be the least integer such that
\tau_{N}=T
. Then from Steps 1 and 2, we can conclude that there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\prescript{}{0}{\mathrm{MR}}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
to
u^{\prime}+Au+Bu=f
.
Moreover, the recursive estimate (
2.11
) together with (
2.9
) yield

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})}\leq 4M\textstyle{\sum_{j=1}^{N}}\frac{N!}{j!}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\leq 7N!M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})},

using that
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{j!}\leq e-1\leq\frac{7}{4}
.

It remains to bound
N
in terms of
\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}
. To do so, set
\tau_{0}\coloneqq 0
and note that

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}^{q}\geq\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\|b\|_{L^{q}(\tau_{n-1},\tau_{n})}^{q}=\frac{N-1}{(2C_{0}M)^{q}}.

Therefore,
N\leq 1+(2C_{0}M)^{q}\|b\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}^{q}\eqqcolon K
. Hence, using the
\Gamma
-function, the required bound holds with constant

C=7\Gamma(K+1)M\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

∎

2.3.
Limiting case
p=q

The limiting case
q=p
is significantly more straightforward and is known to experts, at least in the unweighted setting. We will assume that each
B(t)
is an element of
\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p},X_{0})
, which is more restrictive than the condition in Theorem
2.5
, since
q>p
. On the other hand, we allow for
b\in L^{p}(0,T)
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
. Suppose that Assumption
1.1
and (
2.5
) hold and that
A
has maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity. Let
B\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p},X_{0})
be strongly measurable in the strong operator topology and suppose that

\|B(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p},X_{0})}\leq b(t)
, where
b\in L^{p}(0,T)
. Then
A+B
has maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
–regularity with
M_{p,\kappa,A+B}(0,T)\leq C
, where
C
only depends on
p,\kappa,X_{0},X_{1},M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
and
\|b\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}
.

We include a short proof based on the method of continuity and Gronwall’s lemma.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Suppose that
u\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})
is a solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+Au+\lambda Bu&=f,\\
u(0)&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Observe that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|Bu\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}^{p}\leq\int_{0}^{t}b(s)^{p}s^{\kappa}\|u(s)\|_{X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty,

where the finiteness holds by (
2.4
) and since
b\in L^{p}(0,T)
.

By maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
-regularity of
A
with constant
M
, and by applying (
2.4
) (with embedding constant uniform in
\tau
for functions vanishing at zero), we thus have for any
t\in(0,T]
:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})}+\|u\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,t];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}\leq CM\|Bu\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+CM\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}.

Taking
p
-th powers and using (
2.12
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})}^{p}+\|u\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,t];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}C^{p}M^{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}^{p}+2^{p-1}C^{p}M^{p}\int_{0}^{t}b(s)^{p}s^{\kappa}\|u(s)\|_{X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s.

By Gronwall’s lemma applied to
\phi(s)\coloneqq\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,s,w_{\kappa})}^{p}+s^{\kappa}\|u(s)\|^{p}_{X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}
, it follows that for all
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\phi(t)\leq 2^{p-1}C^{p}M^{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}^{p}\exp(2^{p-1}C^{p}M^{p}\|b\|_{L^{p}(0,t)}^{p}).

In particular, this shows that

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa})}\leq 2CM\exp(p^{-1}2^{p-1}M^{p}\|b\|_{L^{p}(0,t)}^{p})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})},

which is independent of
\lambda
.

It remains to apply the method of continuity (see
[
18
, Lem. 16.2.2]
to the mapping
L_{\lambda}:\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})\to L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})
given by
L_{\lambda}u=u^{\prime}+Au+\lambda Bu
, where
\tau\in(0,T]
is fixed. This gives surjectivity of
L_{1}
, hence the existence of solutions. Uniqueness and the upper bound for
M_{p,\kappa,A+B}(0,T)
follow from (
2.13
).
∎

3.
Perturbations beyond maximal
L^{p}
-regularity

Throughout this section, we assume that
\gamma_{*}\in(0,1]
and
X_{0}
and
X_{1+\gamma_{*}}
are Banach spaces such that
X_{1+\gamma_{*}}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
densely and continuously.
We assume that
\{X_{\gamma}:\gamma\in(0,1+\gamma_{*})\}
is a ‘reasonable intermediate scale’ of Banach spaces, in the sense that

(3.1)

X_{\gamma,1}\hookrightarrow X_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow X_{\gamma,\infty}\qquad\text{ for all }\gamma\in(0,1+\gamma_{*}).

Here, for
\gamma\in(0,1+\gamma_{*})
and
q\in[1,\infty]
,
X_{\gamma,q}
denotes
the following real interpolation space:

(3.2)

X_{\gamma,q}\coloneqq(X_{0},X_{1+\gamma_{*}})_{\frac{\gamma}{(1+\gamma_{*})},q}.

In particular, the theory in this section can be applied with
X_{\gamma}
defined as the complex interpolation space
[X_{0},X_{1+\gamma_{*}}]_{\gamma/(1+\gamma_{*})}
or as the real interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1+\gamma_{*}})_{\gamma/(1+\gamma_{*}),q}
with
q\in[1,\infty]
or even as the fractional domains of a sectorial operator.

3.1.
A mixed-scale problem

For
f\in L^{1}(0,T;X_{0})
, we revisit the problem (
2.1
) and its perturbed version:

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+(A+B)u=f,\\
u(0)=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

We consider
strong solutions
as defined in Subsection
2.1
.

In the classical theory of maximal
L^{p}
-regularity, one considers
f\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
and seeks solutions
u
in the maximal regularity space
\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})
as defined in (
2.2
).

In this section, we consider inhomogeneities
f
belonging to a sum space larger than
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
. Recall that a
k
-tuple of Banach spaces
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{k})
each continuously embedded into a given Hausdorff topological vector space
\mathcal{H}
(i.e. the
ambient space
),

Y_{+}:=\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{589827}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{589827}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{412879}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{294913}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}\coloneqq\{y\in\mathcal{H}:y=\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}y_{i}\text{ in }\mathcal{H},\,y_{i}\in Y_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,k\},

is equipped with the sum norm:

(3.4)

\|y\|_{Y_{+}}\coloneqq\inf\{\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}\|y_{i}\|_{Y_{i}}:y=\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}y_{i}\text{ in }\mathcal{H},\,y_{i}\in Y_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,k\}.

We define the sum space for inhomogeneities as follows:

(3.5)

\displaystyle{L_{+}}(s,t)\coloneqq L^{p}(s,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})+\,{\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{589827}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{589827}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{412879}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{294913}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}}L^{r_{i}}(s,t,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{\gamma_{i}})

with ambient space
L^{1}(s,t;X_{0})
, for suitable parameters
(r_{i},\nu_{i},\gamma_{i})
, which will be specified below. Let us emphasize that we still write
w_{\mu}(t)=t^{\mu}
,
so the weights are not shifted throughout this section.

We will consider perturbations of the form
B=\sum_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}
, where
B_{i}\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\gamma_{i}})
. Note that there is a shift compared to Section
2
(where
B
maps into
X_{0}
).
As noted in the introduction, this framework is motivated by the skeleton equations arising in the weak convergence approach to large deviations for stochastic evolution equations.

To handle such
B
and
f\in{L_{+}}(0,T)
, we introduce the mixed-scale maximal regularity space
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
. For
0\leq s<t\leq T
, we define

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{MR}_{+}(s,t)\coloneqq&\Big(L^{p}(s,t,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(s,t,w_{\kappa};X_{0})\Big)\\
&+\,\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{589827}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{589827}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{412879}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{294913}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}\vphantom{\sum}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}\Big(L^{r_{i}}(s,t,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{1+\gamma_{i}})\cap W^{1,r_{i}}(s,t,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{\gamma_{i}})\Big),\end{split}

again with ambient space
L^{1}(s,t;X_{0})
.

The aim is to prove well-posedness of (
3.3
) and regularity estimates for strong solutions
u\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
, under suitable assumptions on
A
,
B
, and
f
, which will be discussed in Subsection
3.3
.

We require the parameters
(r_{i},\nu_{i},\gamma_{i})
appearing in
{L_{+}}(0,T)
and
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
to be

(p,\kappa)
-admissible
, which is defined next. In Corollary
3.6
, it will be shown that
(p,\kappa)
-admissibility ensures that the solution space
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
embeds into
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
and into certain spaces of continuous functions.

Definition 3.1

(
(p,\kappa)
-admissibility)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\kappa\geq 0
. A triple
(r,\nu,\gamma)
is
(p,\kappa)
-admissible if
\gamma\in(0,1)\cap(0,\gamma_{*}]
,
\nu\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\nu+1<r\leq p,\qquad\frac{\kappa}{p}\leq\frac{\nu}{r},\qquad\frac{1+\kappa}{p}<\frac{1+\nu}{r}\leq\frac{1+\kappa}{p}+\gamma.

Remark 3.2
.

In the definition above, we require
\gamma\in(0,1)\cap(0,\gamma_{*}]
, so we can use the spaces
X_{\gamma,q}
and
X_{1+\gamma,q}
defined in (
3.2
) (which are e.g. essential in the proof of Proposition
3.4
). However, elements of
{L_{+}}(0,T)
and
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
include an
X_{0}
-valued part (see (
3.5
) and (
3.6
)), which will be treated via classical maximal
L^{p}
-regularity techniques. The techniques for the
X_{0}
-valued part cannot be used for the
X_{\gamma}
-valued parts with
\gamma>0
and vice versa. Consequently, we distinguish between these components in the definitions and proofs.

Remark 3.3
.

In applications to large deviations, the
(p,\kappa)
are given, and
\gamma=1/2
, and the parameters
(r,\nu)
of Definition
3.1
will be such that
\frac{\kappa}{p}=\frac{\nu}{r}
. Note that in that case the last condition of the definition becomes
\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2}
.

3.2.
Embeddings of the mixed-scale maximal regularity space

Here, we will prove some embeddings for the space
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
(defined in (
3.6
)) that will be essential for establishing our global well-posedness result for the evolution equation (
3.3
) (Theorem
3.11
).

To begin, we show that the
L^{r}\cap W^{1,r}
-parts of the space
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
embed into
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
, under the
(p,\kappa)
-admissibility assumption.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
.
Let
(r,\nu,\gamma)
be
(p,\kappa)
-admissible.
Then,

(3.7)

\begin{split}L^{r}(0,T&,w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma})\cap W^{1,r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{1}).\end{split}

The operator norm of the embedding depends on
p,\kappa,r,\nu,\gamma
, and
T
, and blows up if
T\downarrow 0
, unless we assume that the functions vanish at
t=0
.

Proof.

Firstly, we note that due to
[
18
, Prop. L.4.5]
and by reflection (see also
[
2
, Prop. 2.5]
), there exists a bounded extension operator

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\colon L^{r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma})

\displaystyle\cap W^{1,r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})\to L^{r}(\mathbb{R},w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma})\cap W^{1,r}(\mathbb{R},w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})

satisfying
[\mathrm{E}f]|_{[0,T]}=f
.
By composing with
\mathrm{E}
and restricting to
[0,T]
at the very end, it thus suffices to prove the following scheme of embeddings:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&W^{1,r}(\mathbb{R},w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})\hookrightarrow B_{\hat{r}\hat{r}}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R},w_{\hat{\nu}};X_{\gamma})\\
&L^{r}(\mathbb{R},w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma})\hookrightarrow B_{\hat{r}\hat{r}}^{s}(\mathbb{R},w_{\hat{\nu}};X_{1+\gamma})\end{cases}

(3.9)

\displaystyle B_{\hat{r}\hat{r}}^{s+1}(\mathbb{R},w_{\hat{\nu}};X_{\gamma})\cap B_{\hat{r}\hat{r}}^{s}(\mathbb{R},w_{\hat{\nu}};X_{1+\gamma})\hookrightarrow B_{\hat{r}\hat{r}}^{\hat{s}}(\mathbb{R},w_{\hat{\nu}};X_{1})

(3.10)

\displaystyle B_{\hat{r}\hat{r}}^{\hat{s}}(\mathbb{R},w_{\hat{\nu}};X_{1})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{\kappa};X_{1})

for some
s<0
.
In this embedding scheme, we factorize through Besov spaces to utilize a mixed time-space derivative embedding (
3.9
) with microscopic improvement. This allows us to eliminate the real interpolation parameter
\infty
.
Moreover, (
3.8
) and (
3.10
) will be proved using weighted Sobolev embeddings.

Combining (
3.8
)–(
3.10
) at once yields (
3.7
), so it remains to show that there exist parameters
s,\hat{s},\hat{r},\hat{\nu}
such that the embeddings are valid.

We begin with (
3.8
). First, observe that due to
[
23
, Prop. 3.12]
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&W^{1,r}(\mathbb{R},w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})\hookrightarrow F_{r\infty}^{1}(\mathbb{R},w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})\\
&L^{r}(\mathbb{R},w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma})\hookrightarrow F_{r\infty}^{0}(\mathbb{R},w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma}).\end{cases}

Also, we recall that
B_{rr}^{s}=F_{rr}^{s}
.
Therefore, (
3.8
) follows from the above combined with the weighted Sobolev embeddings with microscopic improvement for
F
-spaces from
[
23
, Th. 1.2]
, imposing for
i=0,1
:

(3.11)

\hat{r}\geq r,\quad\frac{\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}\leq\frac{\nu}{r},\quad\frac{1+\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}<\frac{1+\nu}{r}\quad-\frac{1+\nu}{r}\geq s-\frac{1+\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}.

This in particular implies that the conditions in
[
23
, (1.6)]
are satisfied for the following parameters for
i=0,1
(the parameters with tilde referring to those in
[
23
]
):
\tilde{d}=1
,
\tilde{\gamma}_{0}=\nu>-1
,
\tilde{\gamma}_{1}=\hat{\nu}>-1
,
\tilde{s}_{0}=-i
,
\tilde{s}_{1}=s+1-i
,
\tilde{p}_{0}=r
,
\tilde{p}_{1}=\hat{r}
,
\tilde{q}_{0}=\infty
,
\tilde{q}_{1}=\hat{r}
. Thus, if (
3.11
) is satisfied, then (
3.8
) is valid. At the end of this proof, we will show that the range of parameters is non-empty. Note that (
3.11
) implies that
s\leq 0
.

Next, for (
3.9
) we apply
[
24
, Th. 3.1]
with
\tilde{\theta}=1-\gamma
and spaces
\tilde{X}_{0}=X_{\gamma}
,
\tilde{X}_{1}=X_{1+\gamma}
,
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{\theta}}=X_{1}
. These
spaces satisfy
[
24
, (3.1)]
, since we have by virtue of (
3.1
) and reiteration (see
[
18
, Th. L.3.1]
):

(X_{\gamma},X_{1+\gamma})_{\tilde{\theta},1}\hookrightarrow(X_{\gamma,\infty},X_{1+\gamma,\infty})_{\tilde{\theta},1}=X_{1,1}\hookrightarrow X_{1}.

Moreover, we use the following parameters for the application of
[
24
, Th. 3.1]
:
\tilde{\alpha}=1
,
\tilde{s}=s
,
\tilde{p}_{0}=\tilde{q}_{0}=\hat{r}
,
\tilde{p}_{1}=\tilde{q}_{1}=\hat{r}
,
\tilde{p}=\tilde{q}=\hat{r}
,
\tilde{w}_{0}=w_{\hat{\nu}}
,
\tilde{w}_{1}=w_{\hat{\nu}}
,
\tilde{w}_{\theta}=w_{\hat{\nu}}
. These parameters satisfy the conditions of
[
24
, Th. 3.1]
for the embedding (
3.9
) if and only if

(3.12)

s+\gamma=\hat{s},

where we note that the correct condition for the weights is
\tilde{w}_{0}^{({1-\tilde{\theta}})/{\tilde{p}_{0}}}\tilde{w}_{1}^{{\tilde{\theta}}/{\tilde{p}_{1}}}=\tilde{w}_{\theta}^{{1}/{\tilde{p}}}
, see also
[
1
, Th. 3.12]
.

Finally, we turn to (
3.10
). We use again that
B_{rr}^{s}=F_{rr}^{s}
and apply
[
23
, Prop. 3.12: (3.10)]
(used for
F_{p,1}^{0}\hookrightarrow L^{p}
) and
[
23
, Th. 1.2]
, imposing that

(3.13)

p\geq\hat{r},\quad\frac{\kappa}{p}\leq\frac{\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}},\quad\frac{1+\kappa}{p}<\frac{1+\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}},\quad\hat{s}-\frac{1+\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}\geq 0-\frac{1+\kappa}{p}.

Now, conditions (
3.11
)–(
3.13
) can be rewritten as

(3.14)

\begin{cases}&p\geq\hat{r}\geq r,\\
&\frac{\kappa}{p}\leq\frac{\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}\leq\frac{\nu}{r},\\
&\frac{1+\kappa}{p}<\frac{1+\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}<\frac{1+\nu}{r}\\
&s\leq\frac{1+\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}-\frac{1+\nu}{r},\\
&s\geq-\gamma+\frac{1+\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},\\
&s+\gamma=\hat{s}.\end{cases}

Now, note that the first two conditions imply that
\frac{1+\hat{\nu}}{\hat{r}}<\frac{1+{\nu}}{{r}}
. Using this, and combining the lower and upper bounds for
s
, we conclude that
there exist
s,\hat{s},\hat{r},\hat{\nu}
satisfying (

) if and only if:

(3.15)

p\geq r,\quad\frac{\kappa}{p}\leq\frac{\nu}{r},\qquad\frac{1+\kappa}{p}<\frac{1+\nu}{r},\qquad\frac{1+\nu}{r}\leq\gamma+\frac{1+\kappa}{p}.

This last condition is satisfied due to the
(p,\kappa)
-admissibility of
(r,\nu,\gamma)
.
∎

Next, we will prove trace embeddings. The embedding into
C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,T];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})
plays a crucial role in the proof of our global well-posedness result, Theorem
3.11
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(r,\nu,\gamma)
be
(p,\kappa)
-admissible. Then

\displaystyle Z_{r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq L^{r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma})\cap W^{1,r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C([0,T];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},r})\cap C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,T];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},r})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C([0,T];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})\cap C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,T];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}).

Proof.

The last embedding is immediate, since
r\leq p
,
so
X_{\lambda,r}\hookrightarrow X_{\lambda,p}
for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
. It remains to
prove the first embedding.

From (
3.1
) and
[
18
, Cor. L.4.6]
we see that

\displaystyle Z_{r}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow L^{r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma,\infty})\cap W^{1,r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma,\infty})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow C([0,T];(X_{\gamma,\infty},X_{1+\gamma,\infty})_{1-\frac{1+\nu}{r},r}).

This gives the first part of the first embedding, since by reiteration
[
18
, Th. L.3.1]
:

\displaystyle(X_{\gamma,\infty},X_{1+\gamma,\infty})_{1-\frac{1+\nu}{r},r}=X_{1+\gamma-\frac{1+\nu}{r},r}\hookrightarrow X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},r},

where we used
(p,\kappa)
-admissibility.

For the second part of the first embedding, as before, we see that

\displaystyle Z_{r}\hookrightarrow C_{\nicefrac{{\nu}}{{r}}}((0,T];(X_{\gamma,\infty},X_{1+\gamma,\infty})_{1-\frac{1}{r},r})=C_{\nicefrac{{\nu}}{{r}}}((0,T];X_{1+\gamma-\frac{1}{r},r}).

This is not the correct spatial smoothness and weight yet. However, we can set
\theta=\frac{\kappa/p}{\nu/r}
(convention
0/0=0
), which is in
[0,1]
by
(p,\kappa)
-admissibility, and by interpolation estimates (or reiteration) we find that for all
t\in(0,T]
:

\displaystyle t^{\kappa/p}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1-\frac{1}{p},r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\gamma-\frac{1+\nu}{r},r}}^{1-\theta}(t^{\nu/r}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\gamma-\frac{1}{r},r}})^{\theta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{Z_{r}}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{Z_{r}}^{\theta}=\|u\|_{Z_{r}},

where we used
(p,\kappa)
-admissibility again, ensuring that
1-\frac{1}{p}\leq s\coloneqq 1+\gamma-\frac{1+\nu}{r}+\frac{\kappa}{p}
, so
X_{1-\frac{1}{p},r}\hookrightarrow X_{s,r}=(X_{1+\gamma-\frac{1+\nu}{r},r},X_{1+\gamma-\frac{1}{r},r})_{\theta,r}
for the case
\theta\in(0,1)
.
∎

By adding the
L^{p}\cap W^{1,p}
-part of
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
to the results above, we obtain the following embedding of the mixed-scale maximal regularity space.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
and let

u\in F(0,\tau)
and
u
is a strong solution to (
3.25
). Moreover, by maximal regularity, Lemma
2.2
, and (
3.4
),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{F(0,\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{r}(0,\tau,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma+1})\cap W^{1,r}(0,\tau,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})}+\|w\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\big(1+M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)(C_{A_{r,\nu,\gamma}}+C_{A})K\|\mathrm{E}\|\big)\|v\|_{L^{r}(0,\tau,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma+1})\cap W^{1,r}(0,\tau,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\leq M_{r,\nu,A_{r,\nu,\gamma}}(0,T)\big(1+M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)(C_{A_{r,\nu,\gamma}}+C_{A})K\|\mathrm{E}\|\big)\|f\|_{L^{r}(0,\tau,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})}.

∎

The following lemma is an extension of Lemma
2.3
, in which we assume that
A
has the mapping property (
3.16
) instead of (
2.5
). We consider the homogeneous problem

(3.27)

\begin{cases}{u}^{\prime}+A{u}=0,\\
{u}(0)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.15
.

Let Assumption
1.1
be satisfied.
Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\kappa\in[0,p-1)
. Suppose that the condition for
A
in (
3.16
) holds and
A
has maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
-regularity.

For every
\tau\in(0,T]
and
u_{0}\in X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}
, there exists a unique strong solution
u\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
to (
3.27
), and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\|u\|_{C([0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}+\|u\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}\leq R_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}},

where
R_{T}
is a constant that depends only on
p,\kappa
, and nondecreasingly on
M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
and
C_{A}
. In particular,
R_{T}
is independent of
\tau
and
u_{0}
.

Proof.

Uniqueness follows from (
2.6
). For the existence and estimate, we exploit the trace method for real interpolation (see
[
18
, §L.2]
and
[
29
, §1.8]
). Using the definition of the
\mathrm{MR}^{p}
-space in (
2.2
), let
z\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})
be such that
z(0)=u_{0}
.
Note that
z^{\prime},Az\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
thanks to (
3.16
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).
Let
v\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})
be the unique solution to the problem
v^{\prime}+Av=-z^{\prime}-Az
with
v(0)=0
.
Then,
u\coloneqq v+z
satisfies (
3.27
). It remains to prove the estimates for
u
.

Write
M\coloneqq M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
.
By maximal regularity and by (
3.16
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq M\big(\|z^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\|Az\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}\big)

\displaystyle\leq M\|z^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\quad+MC_{A}\big(\|z\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}+\|z\|_{C([0,T];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}+\|z\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,T];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}\big)

\displaystyle\leq M(1\vee C_{A})\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})}+MC_{A}\big(\|z\|_{C([0,\infty);X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}+\|z\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,\infty);X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}\big)

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq\big(M(1\vee C_{A})+MC_{A}(K_{\kappa,p}+K_{0,p})\big)\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})},

where
K_{\kappa,p}\coloneqq 64p(\frac{1}{p-1+\kappa}\vee\frac{1}{1+\kappa})
and
K_{0,p}\coloneqq 64p(\frac{1}{p-1}\vee 1)
are constants independent of
\tau
and
T
, since (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) are applied on
[0,\infty)
in the last line (see
[
18
, Th. L.4.1]
for the constants).

We conclude that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}+\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})}

\displaystyle\leq\big(M(1\vee C_{A})+1+MC_{A}(K_{\kappa,p}+K_{0,p})\big)\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})}.

Taking the infimum over all
z\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})
, the first stated estimate follows from the trace method for real interpolation (with an additional constant
\frac{12p}{1+\kappa}
, see
[
18
, Th. L.2.3]
).

Moreover, by (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C([0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{C([0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}+\|z\|_{C([0,\infty);X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\kappa,p}C_{\kappa,p}\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}+K_{\kappa,p}\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})},

where
C_{\kappa,p}\coloneqq 2+3(1-\frac{\kappa}{p-1})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}
is a constant that is also independent of
\tau
and
T
since
v(0)=0
, see
[
18
, Th. L.4.1, Prop. L.4.5]
.

Similarly, by (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}+\|z\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,\infty);X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{0,p}C_{\kappa,p}\|v\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa})}+K_{0,p}\|z\|_{\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})},

see again
[
18
, Th. L.4.1, Prop. L.4.5]
) and recall that
v(0)=0
. Combining these estimates with (
3.28
), and taking the infimum over all
z\in\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,\infty,w_{\kappa})
, the trace method for real interpolation completes the proof of the last two estimates (again with extra constant
\frac{12p}{1+\kappa}
, see
[
18
, Th. L.2.3]
).
∎

In the sequel we will often use a weighted Hölder inequality, which we state explicitly for convenience. For
1<r<p,q<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}

and
\kappa,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
one has that

(3.29)

\|fg\|_{L^{r}(0,T,w_{\nu})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{q}(0,T,w_{\frac{\nu p-\kappa r}{p-r}})}\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa})},\ \ \ \ f,g\in L^{0}(0,T).

Combining earlier results, we obtain a useful estimate for the
B
-term in (

).

Lemma 3.16
.

Let Assumptions
3.7
and
3.9
hold.
Then for every
\tau\in(0,T]
and
v\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
, one has
B_{i}v\in L^{r_{i}}(0,\tau,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{\gamma_{i}})
for
1\leq i\leq k
, so
Bv\in{L_{+}}(0,\tau)
. Moreover,

\|Bv\|_{{L_{+}}(0,\tau)}\leq b_{\scriptscriptstyle[0,\tau]}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})},

where
b_{\scriptscriptstyle[0,\tau]}
was defined in (
3.17
).

Proof.

By Assumption
3.9
, we have
\|B_{i}(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{\gamma_{i}})}\leq b_{i}(\cdot)
. The claim thus follows from the weighted Hölder inequality (
3.29
).
∎

The above lemmas allow us to prove well-posedness of the problem (
3.30
), along with a useful energy estimate.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let Assumptions
3.7
and
3.9
hold.
Then for any
\tau\in(0,T]
,
u_{0}\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}
,
f\in{L_{+}}(0,\tau)
and
v\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
, there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
to

(3.30)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}+Au+Bv=f,\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{cases}

on
[0,\tau]
. Furthermore, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}+\|u\|_{C([0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}+\|u\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq R_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}}+L_{T}\big(b_{\scriptscriptstyle[0,\tau]}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}+\|f\|_{{L_{+}}(0,\tau)}\big),

where
R_{T}
is the constant from Lemma
3.15
, and
L_{T}
is a constant depending only on
p,\kappa
, and
r_{i},\nu_{i},\gamma_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and nondecreasingly on
M_{T}
defined by (
3.18
), and
C_{A}
and
C_{A_{r_{i},\nu_{i},\gamma_{i}}}
from (
3.16
). In particular,
R_{T}
and
L_{T}
are independent of
\tau,u_{0},f
, and
v
.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in(0,T]
.
First, by Lemma
3.15
, the problem

\tilde{u}^{\prime}+A\tilde{u}=0,\tilde{u}(0)=u_{0}
has a unique strong solution
\tilde{u}\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
, and

(3.31)

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\|u\|_{C([0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}+\|u\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}\leq R_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}},

where
R_{T}
is a constant that depends only on
p,\kappa
, and nondecreasingly on
M_{p,\kappa,A}(0,T)
and
C_{A}
.

Next, let
f=f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}
with
f_{0}\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
and
f_{i}\in L^{r_{i}}(0,\tau,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{\gamma_{i}})
. Put
g_{0}=f_{0}
and
g_{i}=f_{i}-B_{i}v
for
1\leq i\leq k
. For
i=0
, set
(r_{0},\nu_{0},\gamma_{0})=(p,\kappa,0)
. By Lemma
3.16
, we have
g_{i}\in L^{r_{i}}(0,\tau,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{\gamma_{i}})
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
By Lemma
3.14
, there exists a strong solution
u^{i}\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
to

(3.32)

\begin{cases}(u^{i})^{\prime}+Au^{i}=g_{i},\\
u^{i}(0)=0.\end{cases}

It follows that
u\coloneqq\tilde{u}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}u^{i}\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
is a strong solution to (
3.30
).

For uniqueness of strong solutions belonging to
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
, suppose that
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
are strong solutions to (
3.30
). Then
w\coloneqq u_{1}-u_{2}
satisfies (
3.27
) with
u_{0}=0
.
In particular,
w^{\prime}=-Aw
a.e. on
[0,\tau]
(see
[
16
, Lem. 2.5.8]
).
Now we extend
w
to
[0,T]
using a bounded extension operator
\mathrm{E}\colon\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)\to\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
(see
[
18
, Prop. L.4.5]
and note that boundedness is inherited by the map between the sum spaces
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
and
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
). Then
Aw=A\mathrm{E}w
on
{[0,\tau]}
,
so (
3.16
) applied to
\mathrm{E}w\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
and Corollary
3.6
yield
w^{\prime}=-(A\mathrm{E}w)|_{[0,\tau]}\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
.
The latter and Corollary
3.6
imply that
w\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
.
Now the maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
-regularity of
A
, specifically (
2.6
), implies that
w=0
, proving uniqueness.

It remains to prove the energy estimate. Let
f\in{L_{+}}(0,\tau)
and
let
f=f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}
be an arbitrary composition for
f
with
f_{i}\in L^{r_{i}}(0,\tau,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{\gamma_{i}})
.
We can define
\tilde{u}
and
u^{i}
from Lemma
3.14
as above (now with potentially different

) and obtain that
\tilde{u}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}u^{i}
is a strong solution to (
3.30
) on
[0,\tau]
that belongs to
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
,
thus uniqueness gives
u=\tilde{u}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}u^{i}
.
Moreover, by (
3.31
) and by Lemmas
3.14
and
3.16
, putting
C_{*}\coloneqq C_{A}+\max_{1\leq i\leq k}C_{A_{\gamma_{i},\nu_{i},r_{i}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{u}\|_{\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)}+\textstyle{\sum_{i=0}^{k}}\|u^{i}\|_{\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq R_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}}+M_{T}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}+\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}M_{T}(1+M_{T}LC_{*})\|g_{i}\|_{L^{r_{i}}(0,\tau,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{\gamma_{i}})}

\displaystyle\leq R_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}}+M_{T}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{0})}

(3.33)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+M_{T}(1+M_{T}LC_{*})\Big(b_{\scriptscriptstyle[0,\tau]}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}+\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{r_{i}}(0,\tau,w_{\nu_{i}};X_{\gamma_{i}})}\Big),

where we recall (
3.17
) for the last line.
Taking the infimum over all decompositions
f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}
of
f
, we obtain the
\mathrm{MR}_{+}
-estimate.

Finally, we prove the remaining estimates. For
i=1,\ldots,k
, Corollary
3.6
and
u^{i}(0)=0
imply

\|u^{i}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}+\|u^{i}\|_{C([0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p})}+\|u^{i}\|_{C_{\nicefrac{{\kappa}}{{p}}}((0,\tau];X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p})}\leq K\|u^{i}\|_{\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)}

for a constant
K
depending only on
p,\kappa,
and
r_{i},\nu_{i},\gamma_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
Moreover, (
3.31
) already provided the necessary estimates for
\tilde{u}
. Combining these estimates with those in (
3.33
) completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.18
.

In Theorem
3.11
, the arbitrary but fixed admissible triples
(r_{i},\nu_{i},\gamma_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,k
are the same for
f
and
B
, but this is just for ease of notation. Indeed,
f_{i}
or
B_{i}
may be chosen as zero, so their admissible triples can just be united with each other.

After these preparations, we are ready to prove the main result, Theorem
3.11
. The proof follows a structure similar to that of Theorem
2.5
, with the following main differences:

•

The solution now belongs to
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,T)
, which is larger than
\mathrm{MR}^{p}(0,T)
. In particular, we no longer have
u^{\prime},Bu\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
.

•

The perturbation
Bu
can already be considered if
u\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
.
The fixed points of
\Phi
and
\Phi_{n}
are constructed in
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
.

Proof of Theorem
3.11
.

Step 1 (local solution):

For
\tau\in(0,T]
, we define a map
\Phi\colon L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})\to L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
by
\Phi(v)\coloneqq u
, where
u\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)\subset L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
is the strong solution to (
3.30
) on
(0,\tau)
, which exists uniquely by Proposition
3.17
.
Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
. Then
u\coloneqq\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}+Au+B(v_{1}-v_{2})=0\quad\text{ on }(0,\tau),\\
u(0)=0.\end{cases}

Therefore, Proposition
3.17
gives

(3.34)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}\leq C_{0}b_{\scriptscriptstyle[0,\tau]}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})},

where we let
C_{0}\coloneqq R_{T}\vee L_{T}
, with
R_{T}
and
L_{T}
the constants from Proposition
3.17
, and we use that
\tau\in(0,T]
.
Note that for (
3.34
), the constant
L_{T}
suffices, but we define
C_{0}
as the maximum for later use.

Choose
\tau\in(0,T]
such that
b_{\scriptscriptstyle[0,\tau]}=1/(2C_{0})
(see (
3.17
)) if possible, and otherwise set
\tau=T
.
It follows from the above that

(3.35)

\displaystyle\|\Phi(v_{1})-\Phi(v_{2})\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}.

By the Banach fixed-point theorem, there exists a unique
u\in L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})
such that
\Phi(u)=u
. Recall that
\mathrm{Ran}(\Phi)\subset\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
by definition of
\Phi
. Thus, within
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
,
u
is the unique strong solution to (
3.3
) on
[0,\tau]
.

Step 2 (
L^{p}
-estimate for local solution):

For
u
from Step 1, we have

\displaystyle\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi({u})\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi({u})-\Phi(0)\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}+\|\Phi(0)\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}+\|\Phi(0)\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}.

Combined with Proposition
3.17
applied to
v=0
, we obtain

(3.36)

\displaystyle\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}=2\|\Phi(0)\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau,w_{\kappa};X_{1})}\leq 2C_{0}\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}}+2C_{0}\|f\|_{{L_{+}}(0,\tau)}.

Step 3 (iterative construction):

We iteratively extend the solution
u
from Step 1 to intervals
[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]
. Although the power weights for
u
,
f
, and
b_{i}
behave like the unweighted case (
w_{0}=1
) on these intervals, we cannot simply apply Step 1 with
\kappa=0
(to a shifted equation), because our final constants should only depend on
T
and
b_{\scriptscriptstyle[0,T]}
and not on the lengths of the subintervals.

Instead, we use the following recursive procedure. Set
\tau_{0}\coloneqq 0
and
\tau_{1}\coloneqq\tau
(from Step 1). Let
{C}_{0}
be the same constant as in Step 1.
Then, for
n\geq 2
, choose
\tau_{n}\in(\tau_{n-1},T)
such that

(3.37)

\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}\|b_{i}\|_{L^{\frac{pr_{i}}{p-r_{i}}}(\tau_{n-1},\tau_{n},w_{\frac{\nu_{i}p-\kappa r_{i}}{p-r_{i}}})}=1/(2{C}_{0}),

recalling that

b_{i}\in L^{\frac{pr_{i}}{p-r_{i}}}(0,T,w_{\frac{\nu_{i}p-\kappa r_{i}}{p-r_{i}}})
by Assumption
3.9
.
If this is no longer possible, we set
N=n
and
\tau_{N}=T
.

Now fix
n\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, and suppose that a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau_{n})
to (
3.3
) has been constructed on
[0,\tau_{n}]
. (For
n=1
, this holds since
u=\Phi(u)\in\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
. )
Define
u_{n}\coloneqq u(\tau_{n})
. Note that
u_{n}\in X_{1-\frac{1}{p},p}
thanks to
Corollary
3.6
. We will now split the problem on
[\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]
into two subproblems. In the first problem, we take care of the initial value problem without inhomogeneity:

(3.38)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{w}^{\prime}+A\tilde{w}=0\;\,\text{ on }(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}),\\
\tilde{w}(\tau_{n})=u_{n},\end{cases}

The second problem treats the inhomogeneous part, and it is crucial to formulate it on
[0,\tau_{n+1}]
in order to be able to apply the weighted form of maximal
L^{p}
-regularity with constants which do not depend on
\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}
:

(3.39)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w^{\prime}+Aw+(B{{\bf 1}}_{(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1})})v=f{{\bf 1}}_{(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1})}-B\tilde{w}{{\bf 1}}_{(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1})}\;\,\text{ on }(0,\tau_{n+1}),\\
w(0)=0,\end{cases}

where
v\in L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{1})
is given but arbitrary and
\tilde{w}
is given by (
3.38
).

Firstly, (
3.38
) has a unique strong solution

\tilde{w}\in L^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{0}),

due to maximal
L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{0})
-regularity of
A
and Lemma

\displaystyle\|_{L^{p}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{1})}\leq\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{1})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{0}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}}+\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle[0,\tau_{n+1}]}\|\tilde{v}{{\bf 1}}_{(0,\tau_{n})}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1},w_{\kappa};X_{1})}+\|f{{\bf 1}}_{(0,\tau_{n})}\|_{{L_{+}}(0,\tau_{n+1})}\Big)

are UMD spaces and
X_{\gamma}=[X_{0},X_{1+\gamma_{*}}]_{\gamma/(1+\gamma_{*})}
, then
X_{\gamma}
and
X_{1+\gamma}
are also UMD, and Proposition
3.4
admits a shorter proof via (
3.50
):

\displaystyle L^{r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma})\cap W^{1,r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow[L^{r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{1+\gamma}),W^{1,r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{\gamma})]_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=H^{\gamma,r}(0,T,w_{\nu};X_{1})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X_{1}),

where the last embedding follows from
[
23
, Th. 1.2, Prop. 3.12]
, using
(p,\kappa)
-admissibility, and we use reiteration for complex interpolation
[
29
, §1.10.3 Th. 2]
in the second line.

4.
Perturbations beyond maximal
L^{p}
-regularity: the case
r=1

In this section, we present a version of Theorem
3.11
for the limiting case
r=1
. The latter necessitates setting
\nu=0
(to ensure integrability of
f
), hence
\kappa=0
, due to the admissibility condition
\kappa/p\leq\nu/r
.
Standard maximal
L^{1}
-regularity is often an unsuitable tool, notably failing on reflexive Banach spaces (see
[
18
, Cor. 17.4.5]
). Consequently, the maximal
L^{r}
-regularity requirement in Assumption
3.7
becomes problematic, rendering Section
3
inapplicable.

Instead, we establish estimates directly, using a modified solution space to replace
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(s,t)
:

(4.1)

\displaystyle E_{p}(s,t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq L^{p}(s,t;X_{1})\cap C([s,t];X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle F_{p}(s,t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq L^{p}(s,t;X_{0})+L^{1}(s,t;(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}).

In the current section we treat
p\in(1,\infty)
and set
\gamma=1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}
(cf. Definition
3.1
). Furthermore, the perturbation
B(t)
maps from
X_{1}
to
(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}
.

4.1.
Main result

The following assumption will be sufficient for our main result.

Assumption 4.1
.

Assumption
1.1
holds and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Moreover:

(1)

A\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})
has maximal
L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})
-regularity and

(4.3)

\|Au\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})}\leq C_{A}\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,T)},\qquad u\in E_{p}(0,T)

for a constant
C_{A}
.

(2)

There exists a closed operator
A_{0}
on
X_{0}
such that
-A_{0}
generates an analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X_{0}
and
D(A_{0})=X_{1}
.

(3)

B\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(X_{1},(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})
is strongly measurable in the strong operator topology, and

\|B(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})}\leq b(\cdot)
for some
b\in L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
.

As in Section
3
, it would be possible to consider
\sum_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}
, where each
B_{i}
satisfies the condition of Assumption
3.9
or Assumption
4.1
(
3
) (see Remark
3.20
).

When
X_{0}
is a Hilbert space, Assumption
4.1
simplifies considerably:

Remark 4.2
.

Suppose that
X_{0}
is a Hilbert space. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

If
A
is time-independent and
X_{1}=D(A)
, the assumption on
A
is equivalent to
-A
being the generator of an analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup by De Simon’s theorem. (see
[
18
, Cor. 17.3.8]
). In particular, we can take
A_{0}=A
.

(2)

Assumption
4.1
(
2
) holds automatically.
Indeed, the space

X_{0}
can be identified with
X_{1}^{*}
using the duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
between
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
induced by
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{1/2}

[
6
, §5.5.2]
. Consequently, we can define
A_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})
by
\langle A_{0}x,y\rangle\coloneqq(x,y)_{X_{1}}
, which in particular satisfies
\langle A_{0}x,x\rangle=\|x\|_{X_{1}}^{2}
. Then
A_{0}
is invertible, positive, and self-adjoint. In particular,
-A_{0}
generates an analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup.

(3)

If
p=2
, then the real interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}
coincides with the complex one (see
[
16
, Corollary C.4.2]
). Therefore,
E_{2}(s,t)=L^{2}(s,t;X_{1})\cap C([s,t];[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})
.

Our main perturbation result for
r=1
can now be stated as follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that Assumption
4.1
holds and let
E_{p}(0,T)
and
F_{p}(0,T)
be defined as in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
). Then for all
u_{0}\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}
and
f\in F_{p}(0,T)
, the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+(A+B)u&=f,\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

has a unique strong solution
u\in E_{p}(0,T)
and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,T)}\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}+\|f\|_{F_{p}(0,T)}\big)

where
C
is a constant independent of
u_{0}
and
f
, which depends only on
A_{0},X_{0},X_{1},p
, and nondecreasingly on
\|b\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)},T,C_{A}
, and
M_{p,0,A}(0,T)
.

Even in the unperturbed case (
B=0
), it is noteworthy that the space of inhomogeneities can be enlarged to
F_{p}(0,T)
while preserving the regularity
u\in L^{p}(0,T;X_{1})
.
Also, in case
X_{0}
is a Hilbert space and
p=2
, Theorem
4.3
extends
[
28
, Cor. 3.5]
to the nonvariational setting (see Remark
4.2
).

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
4.3

We start with a general semigroup lemma. We thank Emiel Lorist for suggesting the elementary proof provided below.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
be Banach spaces and let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
A_{0}
be a closed operator on
X_{0}
such that
-A_{0}
generates an analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup with
X_{1}=D(A_{0})
. Then
for every
g\in L^{1}(0,T;X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})
and
u_{0}\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}
the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+A_{0}u&=g,\\
u(0)&=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

has a unique strong solution
u\in E_{p}(0,T)
and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}+C\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})},

where
C
is a constant independent of
u_{0}
and
g
, and depends only on
A_{0},X_{0},X_{1},p
, and nondecreasingly on
T
.

Proof.

Uniqueness follows from the fact that weak solutions are unique
[
17
, Th. G.3.2]
.

Before we proceed with the existence and estimate, observe that by
applying the invertible transformation
u\mapsto e^{\lambda\cdot}u
, we have an equivalence of the problem above to the same problem with
A_{0}
replaced by
\lambda+A_{0}
.
Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume that
A_{0}
is sectorial of angle
<\pi/2
and invertible. In particular,
e^{-tA}
is exponentially stable.

Extend
g
by zero to a function on
(0,\infty)
.
By
[
17
, Th. G.3.2]
, there exists a weak solution
u
which can be represented as a mild solution:

\displaystyle u(t)=\exp(-tA_{0})u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-(t-s)A_{0})g(s)ds\eqqcolon\exp(-tA_{0})u_{0}+v(t),\ \ t\geq 0.

It suffices to prove the bounds for
T=\infty
.

The
C([0,\infty);X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})
-regularity and the corresponding estimate follow from the fact that
t\mapsto e^{-tA_{0}}
restricts to an exponentially stable analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}
.
It is also standard that
\|t\mapsto\exp(-tA_{0})u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}
(see
[
18
, Th. L.2.4]
).

Next, we prove
L^{p}(0,\infty;X_{1})
-regularity of the mild solution
u
given above. We have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,\infty;X_{1})}

\displaystyle\eqsim\Big\|t\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}A_{0}\exp(-(t-s)A_{0}){{\bf 1}}_{(0,t)}(s)g(s)ds\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,\infty;X_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\|t\mapsto A_{0}\exp(-(t-s)A_{0}){{\bf 1}}_{(0,t)}(s)g(s)\|_{L^{p}(0,\infty;X_{0})}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|t\mapsto A_{0}\exp(-tA_{0})g(s)\|_{L^{p}(0,\infty;X_{0})}ds

\displaystyle\eqsim\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,\infty;X_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})},

where in the last step we used
[
18
, Th. L.2.4]
.

Finally, note that by
[
18
, Prop. 17.1.3]
and the previously obtained regularity, the mild solution
u
is a strong solution as well.
∎

Next, we extend this bound to the non-autonomous case.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let Assumption
4.1
be satisfied.
Then for every
\tau\in(0,T]
,
u_{0}\in(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}
,
f\in F_{p}(0,\tau)
and
v\in L^{p}(0,\tau;X_{1})
, the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\prime}+Au+Bv&=f,\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

has a unique strong solution
u\in E_{p}(0,\tau)
on
[0,\tau]
, and

\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau)}\leq C_{0}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}+\|b\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(0,\tau)}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau)}+\|f\|_{F_{p}(0,\tau)}\Big),

where
C_{0}
is a constant independent of
\tau,u_{0},f
, and
v
, which depends only on
A_{0},X_{0},X_{1},p
, and nondecreasingly on
T,C_{A}
, and
M_{p,0,A}(0,T)
.

Proof.

For uniqueness, let
w\coloneqq u_{2}-u_{1}
be the difference of two strong solutions in
E_{p}(0,\tau)
.
Then
w^{\prime}=-Aw
a.e. (see
[
16
, Lem. 2.5.8]
), and using a bounded extension operator
\mathrm{E}\colon E_{p}(0,\tau)\to E_{p}(0,T)
as before, (
4.3
) gives

(4.4)

\|Aw\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau;X_{0})}\leq\|A\mathrm{E}w\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})}\leq C_{A}\|\mathrm{E}w\|_{E_{p}(0,T)}\leq C_{A}\|\mathrm{E}\|\|w\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau)},

where the operator norm
\|\mathrm{E}\|
is
\tau
- and
T
-independent, using that
w(0)=0
. We conclude that
w^{\prime}=-Aw\in L^{p}(0,\tau;X_{0})
. Thus
w\in L^{p}(0,\tau;X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,\tau;X_{0})
and maximal
L^{p}(0,\tau;X_{0})
-regularity from Lemma
2.2
yields
w=0
.

To prove existence, we employ the transference method of
[
3
, Th. 3.13]
.
Let
A_{0}
be as in Assumption
4.1
. Let
f=g+h
with
g\in L^{1}(0,\tau;(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})
and
h\in L^{p}(0,\tau;X_{0})
. By Lemma
4.4
, there exists a unique strong solution
\tilde{v}\in E_{p}(0,T)
to
\tilde{v}^{\prime}+A_{0}\tilde{v}=-{{\bf 1}}_{[0,\tau]}Bv+{{\bf 1}}_{[0,\tau]}g
,
\tilde{v}(0)=u_{0}
. Moreover,

\|\tilde{v}\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau)}\leq\|\tilde{v}\|_{E_{p}(0,T)}\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}+\|b\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(0,\tau)}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau)}+\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,\tau;(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})}\big),

using that Assumption
4.1
(
3
) and Hölder’s inequality yield

(4.5)

\|Bv\|_{L^{1}(0,\tau;(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p})}\leq\|b\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(0,\tau)}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau;X_{1})}.

By (
4.3
), maximal
L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})
-regularity of
A
and Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we can find a unique strong solution
z\in L^{p}(0,T;X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,T;X_{0})
to
z^{\prime}+Az=(A_{0}-A)\tilde{v}+{{\bf 1}}_{[0,\tau]}h
,
z(0)=0
,
and combined with the trace embedding (
2.3
) and (
4.4
) applied to
\tilde{v}
,

\displaystyle\|z\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq\|z\|_{E_{p}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|z\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X_{1})\cap W^{1,p}(0,T;X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq M(\|A_{0}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{0})}+C_{A})\|\tilde{v}\|_{E_{p}(0,T)}+M\|{{\bf 1}}_{[0,\tau]}h\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X_{0})},

where
M\coloneqq M_{p,0,A}(0,T)
, and the constant in the second line is
\tau
- and
T
-independent since
z(0)=0
.
One can verify that
u\coloneqq\tilde{v}+z\in E_{p}(0,\tau)
is the required strong solution.
Moreover,
\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau)}\leq\|\tilde{v}\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau)}+\|z\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau)}
, so the claimed estimate follows from the estimates for
\tilde{v}
and
z
above, and by taking the infimum over all
g
and
h
as above.
∎

By adapting the proof of Theorem
3.11
, we obtain the following result.

First proof of Theorem
4.3
.

We can repeat the proof of Theorem
3.11
, replacing
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(s,t)
by
E_{p}(s,t)
and
{L_{+}}(s,t)
by
F_{p}(s,t)
. As a fixed-point space, we use
L^{p}(s,t;X_{1})
again.
Note that (
4.5
) replaces Lemma
3.16
and Proposition
4.5
replaces Proposition
3.17
.

With the substitutions above, we have
\mathrm{Ran}(\Phi)\subset E_{p}(0,\tau)
in Step 1 and
\mathrm{Ran}(\Phi_{n})\subset E_{p}(0,\tau_{n+1})
in Step 3, thus we can exploit the uniqueness within
E_{p}(0,\tau)
(instead of
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(0,\tau)
) from Proposition
4.5
. Moreover, we note that
E_{p}(0,t)
has the property that
v_{1}{{\bf 1}}_{[0,s]}+v_{2}{{\bf 1}}_{(s,t]}\in E_{p}(0,t)
when
v_{1}\in E_{p}(0,s)
,
v_{2}\in E_{p}(s,t)
and
v_{1}(s)=v_{2}(s)
. This property, previously essential for
\mathrm{MR}_{+}(s,t)
in Theorem

, also holds for
E_{p}(s,t)
.
∎

Alternatively, the proof can be simplified due to the absence of weights in this setting.

Second proof of Theorem
4.3
.

In Step 1, define
\Phi
on
L^{p}(0,\tau;X_{1})
. Note that (
3.34
)–(
3.36
) remain valid.

In Step 3, again let
\tau_{n}
be such that
\|b\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\tau_{n-1},\tau_{n})}=1/(2{C}_{0})
if possible, and otherwise set
\tau_{n}=T
. In the unweighted setting, we can iterate Step 1 using the shifted coefficients
\tilde{A}\coloneqq A(\cdot+\tau_{n})
,
\tilde{B}\coloneqq B(\cdot+\tau_{n})
, and
\tilde{f}\coloneqq f(\cdot+\tau_{n})
. Consider on
[0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{u}^{\prime}+\tilde{A}\tilde{u}+\tilde{B}v&=\tilde{f},\\
\tilde{u}(0)&=u(\tau_{n}).\end{aligned}\right.

Define
\Phi_{n}
on
L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n};X_{1}))
as
\Phi_{n}(v)=\tilde{u}
, where
\tilde{u}\in E_{p}(0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n})
is the unique strong solution to the equation above, which exists by Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
and Proposition
4.5
(with
\tilde{b}\coloneqq b(\cdot+\tau_{n})
). A unique fixed point
u^{n}=\Phi_{n}(u^{n})
is guaranteed since

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{n}(v_{1})-\Phi_{n}(v_{2})\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n})}\leq C_{0}\|b\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1})}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{p}(0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n};X_{1})},

which holds with the same constant
C_{0}
as in Step 1, thanks to Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
. The solution
u
can be extended by defining
u=u^{n}(\cdot-\tau_{n})
on
(\tau_{n},\tau_{n+1}]
.

Moreover, similarly to (
3.36
), one derives

\displaystyle\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau_{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau_{n})}+\|u^{n}\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau_{n})}+2C_{0}\|u(\tau_{n})\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}+2C_{0}\|f\|_{F_{p}(0,\tau)},

\displaystyle\leq(1+2C_{0})\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,\tau_{n})}+2C_{0}\|f\|_{F_{p}(0,\tau)}.

This recursive estimate, together with (
3.36
) (now with
E_{p}(0,\tau)
-norm) yields an estimate (
\tau_{N}=T
)

\|u\|_{E_{p}(0,T)}\leq 2C_{0}(1+2C_{0})^{N-1}\|u_{0}\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}}+\left[(1+2C_{0})^{N}-1\right]\|f\|_{F_{p}(0,\tau)},

and
N
can be bounded in terms of
\|b\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)}
as was done at the end of Step 4.
∎
