Jintao Ye and Xiaohua Zhu ‡ {\rm Zhu}^{\ddagger}

Abstract
. We prove a relative volume comparison theorem of Petersen-Wei for both L P L^{P} -bound of Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature and gradient of potential function. As an application, we give a modified proof for a volume comparison and monotonicity of Kähler-Ricci flow established in a recent work of Tian-Zhang-Zhang-Zhu-Zhu.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold, and
\mu(x)
be the smallest eigenvalue of the Ricci tensor
\mathrm{Ric}(x)

at
x
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
p>0
, we let

\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{-}(x):=\max\{-\mu(x)+(n-1)\lambda,0\},

and define its
L^{p}
norm as follows

\|\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{-}(x)\|_{p}=\left(\int_{M}\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{-}(x)^{p}d\mathrm{vol}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Clearly,
\operatorname{Ric}(x)\geq(n-1)\lambda
if and only if
\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{-}(x)\equiv 0
.

Let
M_{\lambda}^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional space form with constant sectional curvature
\lambda
, and
v(n,\lambda,r)
the volume of a geodesic
r
-ball in
M^{n}_{\lambda}
. In [
[
11
]
], Petersen-Wei proved the following volume comparison theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\lambda\leq 0
,
p>n/2
and
0<r\leq R
, there exists a constant
C(n,p,\lambda,R)
such that

(1.1)

\left(\frac{\operatorname{vol}B(x,R)}{v(n,\lambda,R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}-\left(\frac{\operatorname{vol}B(x,r)}{v(n,\lambda,r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C(n,p,\lambda,R)\|\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{-}\|_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover,
C(n,p,\lambda,R)=O(R^{1-\frac{n}{2p}})
as
R\to 0
.

The inequality (
1.1
) generalizes the classical Bishop-Gromov’s monotonicity of Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below. When
r
is sufficiently small, the volume ratio
\frac{\mathrm{vol}B(x,r)}{v(n,\lambda,r)}
in (
1.1
) becomes almost monotone. As an applications of (
1.1
), the extensions of Myer’s theorem, sphere pinching theorem and heat kernel estimate, etc., have been explored for Riemannian manifolds with integral Ricci curvature bounds [
[
12
,
13
]
]. In [
[
21
]
], Tian-Zhang used this monotonicity property to study the regularity of Kähler-Ricci flow on Fano manifolds, and in particular they proved the Hamiton-Tian conjecture for three dimensional Kähler-Ricci flow on Fano manifolds [
[
15
]
].

On the other hand, there has been a great interest in extending volume comparison results under Bakry-Émery (abbreviated as BE) Ricci curvature condition. On a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, BE Ricci curvature associated to a smooth function
f
is a modification of Ricci curvature, defined as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{f}(g)=\operatorname{Ric}(g)+\operatorname{Hess}f.

This tensor, which was first introduced by Bakry and Émery in the study of diffusion processes (cf. [
[
1
,
8
]
]), appears naturally in many contexts in differential geometry. In particular, the equation
\mathrm{Ric}_{f}(g)=\lambda g
for some constant
\lambda
is exactly related to a class of canonical metrics, called as gradient Ricci solitons, which plays an important role in the singularities analysis in Ricci flow, see [
[
9
,
7
]
], etc.. Another natural connection between Ricci curvature and BE Ricci curvature is from the conformal geometry, for examples, see [
[
20
]
,
[
23
]
]. More recently, by using the conformal geometry and BE Ricci curvature, Tian-Zhang-Zhang-Zhu-Zhu [
[
19
]
] proved a volume comparison and a monotonicity formula for Kähler-Ricci flow on Fano manifolds, which leads to a new essential proof of Hamilton-Tian conjecture
directly, also see Section
5
in this paper below.

Denoted
(M,g,e^{-f}d\mathrm{vol}_{g})
as a weighted Riemannian manifold. In [
[
24
]
], Wei-Wylie established a weighted volume comparison theorem of Bishop-Gromov for weighted Riemannian manifolds with
\operatorname{Ric}_{f}(g)
bounded below, assuming the boundedness of
|\nabla f|
or
f
. Wang-Zhu [
[
23
]
] applied their result to study the structure of limit spaces of Riemannian manifolds and Cheeger-Colding-Tian theory with BE Ricci curvature bounded below as done in [
[
5
,
6
]
] for Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below. Later, Wu also extended the Wei-Wylie’s result to the case of
L^{p}
-bound of
\mathrm{Ric}_{f}(g)
as in Theorem
1.1
by assuming that
|\nabla f|
is bounded [
[
25
]
]. However, the piecewise bounded condition of
|\nabla f|
seems very strong. Actually, on a complete Riemannian manifold with gradient shrinking Ricci soliton
(M,g;f)
,
f
has a quadratic growth of distance function of
g
and so
|\nabla f|
increases as fast as the distance function (cf. [
[
3
,
4
]
]).

The main purpose of the present paper is to prove a relative volume comparison theory for a weighted Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\mathrm{vol}_{g})
with both
L^{p}
-bounds of
\mathrm{Ric}_{f}(g)
and
|\nabla f|
.

Before stating our main result, we introduce some notations below. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we define a quantity as
\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{-}(x)
,

{\mathrm{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}(x):=\max\{(n-1)\lambda-\mu_{f}(x),\,0\},

where
\mu_{f}(x)
is the smallest eigenvalue of
\operatorname{Ric}_{f}(g)
at
x
. When
\lambda=0
, we also denote it by
{\mathrm{Ric}_{f}}_{-}(x)
. For
x\in M
,
p>0
,
a\geq 0
, we denote a weighted
L^{p}
-norm on a geodesic
R
-ball
B(x,R)

for a measurable function
\phi
by

\|\phi\|_{p,f,a}(x,R):=\left(\int_{B(x,R)}|\phi|^{p}e^{-a\cdot d(x,\cdot)}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
d\mathrm{vol}_{f}=e^{-f}d\mathrm{vol}
. When
a=0
, we also denote it as
\|\cdot\|_{p,f}(x,R)
.

For any smooth vector field
V
on
M
and
a\geq 0
, we define

\rho_{a}(V)(x):=\max\{|V(x)|-a,0\}.

Moreover, for
a\geq 0
, we fix a point
O\in M_{\lambda}^{n}
and define a weighted volume of the geodesic
r
-ball
B(O,r)
in
M_{\lambda}^{n}
as

v_{a}(n,\lambda,r):=\int_{B(O,r)}e^{a\cdot d(O,\cdot)}d\mathrm{vol}(\cdot).

Clearly,
v(n,\lambda,r)\leq v_{a}(n,\lambda,r)\leq e^{ar}v(n,\lambda,r)
.

The following is our main result in this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\operatorname{vol})
be a weighted Riemannian manifold,
x\in M
and
\lambda\leq 0
. Assume that there exist two positive constants
\kappa
and
l\in(\frac{n}{2},n]
such that

(1.3)

\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,r)\leq\kappa r^{l},~\forall~0<r\leq R_{0}.

Then for
\frac{n}{2}<p<l
and
q>\frac{pl}{l-p}
, and any
0<r\leq R\leq R_{0}
we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}-\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,r)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p,q,a,\kappa,l,\lambda,R)\times\bigg(\|{\mathrm{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}\|_{p,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R)

\displaystyle+\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R)+\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}(x,R)\bigg),

where
C(n,p,q,a,\kappa,l,\lambda,R)=O(R^{1-\frac{n}{2p}})
as
R\to 0
. In the case of
\lambda>0
, the inequality (
1.3
)
holds whenever
R\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}
.

Remark 1.3
.

When
|\nabla f|\leq a
holds pointwise,
\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\equiv 0
, and the terms involving
\rho_{a}(\nabla f)
disappear in the right-hand side. Then from the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}-\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,r)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C(n,p,q,a,\lambda,R)\|{\mathrm{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}\|_{p,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R),

without the volume growth condition (
1.3
), which is analogous to Wu’s result in [
[
25
]
].

By (
1.4
), we see that the volume ratio of
\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,r)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,r)}
is almost
decreasing as
r\to 0
when the
L^{p}
-norm of
{\mathrm{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}
and
L^{q}
-norm of
\nabla f
are uniformly bounded as well as
f
is uniformly bounnded and the volume has an Euclidean growth.
Then as an application of Theorem
1.2
, we give a modified proof of the following volume comparison for Kähler-Ricci flow established in [
[
19
]
, Corollary 1.3(b)].

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a Fano manifold of complex dimension
m=\frac{n}{2}
with its Kähler form
\omega_{g_{0}}\in 2\pi c_{1}(M)
, and
(M,g(t,\cdot))
(
t>0
) a Kähler-Ricci flow with
g_{0}
as an initial metric,

(1.5)

\partial_{t}g(t,\cdot)=-{\rm Ric}(g(t,\cdot))+g(t,\cdot).

Then for a given
s\in(m,2m)
, there exists a constant
b_{s}
depending only on
s
and the initial metric
g_{0}
such that for any
0<r\leq R\leq{\rm diam}(M,g(t))
it holds

(1.6)

\frac{|B(x,R,t)|_{g(t)}}{R^{n}}-\frac{|B(x,r,t)|_{g(t)}}{r^{n}}\leq b_{s}R^{1-\frac{m}{s}},

where
|B(x,r,t)|_{g(t)}
is the volume of geodesic
r
-ball associated to metric
g(t)
.

This paper is organized as follows. We first review some basic notation and derive a Riccati-type inequality in Section
2
, then we give some types of
L^{p}
-estimate for the Laplacian of distance function
in Section
3
. Theorem
1.2
, and Corollary
1.4
will be proved in Section
4
and Section
5
, respectively.

2.
Riccati-type inequality

Classical volume comparison theorems are often reduced to comparing the mean curvature of geodesic spheres, i.e. the Laplacian of distance function.
In this section, we define a mean curvature error term
\psi
between a weighted manifold
(M,g,e^{-f}d\mathrm{vol})
and the form space
M_{\lambda}^{n}
,
and then we derive a Riccati-type inequality for it.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Fix a point
x\in M
and consider its segment domain

\displaystyle\mathrm{seg}(x):=\{

\displaystyle v\in T_{p}M\,|\,\operatorname{exp}_{x}(tv),\,0\leq t\leq 1\,\text{is the}

\displaystyle\text{unique minimal geodesic from}\,x\,\text{to}\,\operatorname{exp}_{x}(v)\}.

On an open set
\operatorname{exp}_{x}(\mathrm{seg}(x))\subset M
, we use polar coordinates
(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times S^{n-1}
so that the volume form can be written as

d\mathrm{vol}=\omega(r,\theta)dr\wedge d\theta,

where
d\theta
denotes the volume form on the standard unit sphere
(S^{n-1},g_{S^{n-1}})
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\omega=0
outside
\operatorname{exp}_{x}(\mathrm{seg}(x))
.

Let
r(\cdot)=d(\cdot,x)
be a distance function on
M
from
x
and h the Laplacian of
r
.
An immediate computation in the polar coordinates shows that

\displaystyle\partial_{r}w

\displaystyle=hw,

\displaystyle\omega(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=O(r^{n-1}),\,r\to 0,

\displaystyle h(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{r}+O(r),\,r\to 0.

In particular, on
(M_{\lambda}^{n},O)
, the Laplacian
h_{\lambda}
of distance function satisfies

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}(r)=(n-1)\frac{\operatorname{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\operatorname{sn}_{\lambda}(r)},

where
\omega_{\lambda}(r)=\operatorname{sn}_{\lambda}^{n-1}(r)
is the volume element on the Gauss sphere
\partial B(O,r)
, and the function
\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)
is defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{sn}_{\lambda}(r)=\begin{cases}&\,r,\lambda=0\\
&\,\frac{\operatorname{sin}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{\sqrt{\lambda}},\lambda>0\\
&\,\frac{\operatorname{sinh}(\sqrt{-\lambda}r)}{\sqrt{-\lambda}},\lambda<0.\end{cases}

Associated to BE Ricci curvature, it is natural to consider the following weighted Laplacian

\Delta_{f}:=\Delta-\nabla f\cdot\nabla,

to see [
[
23
,
22
]
], etc.. Then
\Delta_{f}r=\Delta r-\partial_{r}f=h-\partial_{r}f
, which is denote by
h_{f}
for convenience. Since the weighted volume form is given by

d\operatorname{vol}_{f}=e^{-f}\omega(r,\theta)dr\wedge d\theta=w_{f}(r,\theta)dr\wedge d\theta,

It follows that

\partial_{r}\omega_{f}=h_{f}\omega_{f}.

Motivated by [
[
11
]
], we compute the mean curvature error term

(2.2)

\psi(r,\theta)=(h_{f}-h_{\lambda}-a)_{+}(r,\theta).

By the Bochner formula, for any function
u\in C^{3}(M)
, we see that

(2.3)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}=\langle\nabla(\Delta_{f}u),\nabla u\rangle+|\operatorname{Hess}u|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u).

Applying (
2.3
)
to the distance function
r
, it yields

\partial_{r}h_{f}+|\operatorname{Hess}r|^{2}=-\operatorname{Ric}_{f}(\partial_{r},\partial_{r}).

By choosing a unit orthonormal frame
(e_{i})
with
e_{1}=\partial_{r}
, it follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

|\operatorname{Hess}r|^{2}=\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{ii}^{2}=\sum_{i=2}^{n}s_{ii}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}(\Delta r)^{2}.

Thus we obtain

h_{f}^{\prime}+\frac{h^{2}}{n-1}\leq-\operatorname{Ric}_{f}(\partial_{r},\partial_{r}).

Recall that in the model space,

h_{\lambda}^{\prime}+\frac{h_{\lambda}^{2}}{n-1}=-(n-1)\lambda.

Substracting this into the previous inequality gives

(2.4)

\left(h_{f}-h_{\lambda}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(h-h_{\lambda}\right)\left(h+h_{\lambda}\right)\leq{\operatorname{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}.

Lemma 2.1

(Riccati-type Inequaity)
.

For
\lambda\leq 0
and
r>0
. we have

(2.5)

\psi^{\prime}+\frac{\psi^{2}}{n-1}\leq-\frac{2\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}}\psi+\frac{2}{n-1}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\psi+\frac{2\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)+{\operatorname{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}.

In the case of
\lambda>0
, (
2.5
) holds whenever
r\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\psi>0
and
\psi=h_{f}-h_{\lambda}-a
. Substituting it into (
2.4
) and by the fact
h=h_{f}+\partial_{r}f
, we have

\psi^{\prime}+\frac{1}{n-1}(\psi+\partial_{r}f+a)(\psi+2h_{\lambda}+\partial_{r}f+a)\leq{\operatorname{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}.

Expending the second terms at the left-hand side yields

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}+\frac{\psi^{2}}{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}}\psi-\frac{1}{n-1}\big[(\partial_{r}f+a)^{2}+(2\psi+2h_{\lambda})(\partial_{r}f+a)\big]+{\operatorname{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}.

Note that
\partial_{r}f+a\geq-\rho_{a}(\nabla f)
and
h_{\lambda}\geq 0
. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}+\frac{\psi^{2}}{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}}\psi-\frac{1}{n-1}(2\psi+2h_{\lambda})(\partial_{r}f+a)+{\operatorname{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}}\psi+\frac{1}{n-1}(2\psi+2h_{\lambda})\rho_{a}(\nabla f)+{\operatorname{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-},

which is equivalent to the desired inequality (
2.5
).
∎

Compared to the corresponding Riccati-type inequality considered in [
[
24
]
] or [
[
25
]
], there are two new terms
\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\psi
and
\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)
in (
2.5
), which will be handled in next section.

3.
L^{p}
-estimate for
\psi

In this section, we use Lemma
2.1
to compute
L^{p}
-estimate for
\psi
.
First we have

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\frac{n}{2}<p<n
,
\lambda\leq 0
and
R>0
, it holds

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(n,p)\left(\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{p}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle+C_{2}(n,p)\int_{0}^{R}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle+C_{3}(n,p)\int_{0}^{R}({\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-})^{p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr,

where the angular coordinates
\theta
are fixed. In the cae of
\lambda>0
, the inequality holds whenever
R\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

Multiplying both sides of (
2.5
) in Lemma
2.1
by
\psi^{2p-2}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2p-1}(\psi^{2p-1})^{\prime}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}+\frac{1}{n-1}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}+\frac{2}{n-1}\psi^{2p-1}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\omega_{f}e^{-ar}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\psi^{2p-2}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\omega_{f}e^{-ar}+\psi^{2p-2}{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\quad(\psi^{2p-1})^{\prime}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}

\displaystyle=(\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar})^{\prime}-\psi^{2p-1}h_{f}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}+a\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}

\displaystyle\geq(\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar})^{\prime}-\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}-(n-1)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}.

Substituting this into the previous inequality, it yields

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2p-1}(\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar})^{\prime}+\left(\frac{1}{n-1}-\frac{1}{2p-1}\right)\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{n-1}{2p-1}-2\right)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}+\frac{2}{n-1}\psi^{2p-1}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\omega_{f}e^{-ar}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\psi^{2p-2}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\omega_{f}e^{-ar}+\psi^{2p-2}{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n-1}\psi^{2p-1}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\omega_{f}e^{-ar}+\frac{2r\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\psi^{2p-2}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)}{r}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}

\displaystyle+\psi^{2p-2}{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}.

Here we used the fact

\left(\frac{n-1}{2p-1}-2\right)\frac{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\leq 0,

which can be easily checked if
\lambda\leq 0
, and it holds when
r\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}
if
\lambda>0
. Integrating the inequality (
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle\quad\,\frac{1}{2p-1}\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}\bigg|_{0}^{R}+\frac{2p-n}{(n-1)(2p-1)}\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n-1}\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-1}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle+\max_{0\leq r\leq R}\frac{2r\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-2}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)}{r}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-2}{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr.

Since
\omega_{f}=O(r^{n-1})
as
r\to 0
, we have

\psi^{2p-1}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}\big|_{0}^{R}\geq 0
. Note that

\frac{2p-n}{(n-1)(2p-1)}>0
by
p>n/2
. Thus we derive

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(n,p)\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-1}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle+C_{2}(n,p)\max_{0\leq r\leq R}\frac{r\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(r)}\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-2}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)}{r}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle+C_{3}(n,p)\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-2}{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr,

where
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
.

By Hölder’s inequality, it is easy to see that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-1}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{2p-1}{2p}}\left(\int_{0}^{R}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-2}{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{2p-2}{2p}}\left(\int_{0}^{R}({\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-})^{p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p-2}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)}{r}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{2p-2}{2p}}\left(\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that the integral
\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}\omega_{f}dr
is finite since
p<n
.
Let

X=\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr.

Hence, substituting (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) into (
3.3
), we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle X^{\frac{2}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(n,p)\max_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\left(\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle+C_{2}(n,p)\left(\int_{0}^{R}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}X^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle+C_{3}(n,p)\left(\int_{0}^{R}({\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-})^{p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By considering an inequality of the form,

X^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq A\cdot X^{\frac{1}{2p}}+B,

we see that

X^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq\frac{A+\sqrt{A^{2}+4B}}{2}\leq A+\sqrt{B}.

In our case of (
3.7
), we set

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=C_{2}(n,p)\left(\int_{0}^{R}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=C_{1}(n,p)\max_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\left(\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle+C_{3}(n,p)\left(\int_{0}^{R}({\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-})^{p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then by the fact
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
(
a,b>0
), we get from (
3.7
),

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(n,p)\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle+C_{2}(n,p)\left(\int_{0}^{R}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle+C_{3}(n,p)\left(\int_{0}^{R}({\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-})^{p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}.

By applying an equality
(a+b+c)^{2p}\leq 3^{2p-1}(a^{2p}+b^{2p}+c^{2p})
, we finally obtain the desired estimate (
3.1
).
∎

Using the volume growth condition (
1.3
), we can handle the term occured in (
3.1
),

\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}dr

by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}d\mathrm{vol},x)
be a pointed weighted Riemannian manifold satisfying (
1.3
). Then for
0<p<l
and
q>\frac{lp}{l-p}
, there exists a constant
C(p,q,\kappa,l)
such that

\left(\int_{B(x,R)}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}e^{-ar}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C(p,q,\kappa,l)R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R).

Proof.

Since
q>\frac{lp}{l-p}>p
, we can use Hölder’s inequality to deduce

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(x,R)}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}e^{-ar}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}(x,R)\left(\int_{B(x,R)}\frac{e^{-ar}}{r^{s}}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}},

where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{p}
. Note that
\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)\leq\kappa R^{l}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,R)}\frac{e^{-ar}}{r^{s}}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{-i}R)\backslash B(x,2^{-i-1}R)}\frac{1}{r^{s}}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\kappa(2^{-i}R)^{l}}{(2^{-i-1}R)^{s}}

\displaystyle=C(p,q,\kappa,l)R^{l-s}.

Here we used
q>\frac{lp}{l-p}
, which implies
l>s
. Substituting this estimate into (
3.8
), we will finish the proof.
∎

By Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we get the following

L^{2p}
-estimate for
\psi
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(M,g,e^{-f}d\mathrm{vol},x)
be a pointed weighted Riemannian manifold satisfying (
1.3
). Then for
\lambda\leq 0
,
\frac{n}{2}<p<l
and
q>\frac{pl}{l-p}
, and for any
0<r\leq R
, it holds

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R)

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p,q,\kappa,l,\lambda,R)\times\bigg(\|{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\|_{p,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R)

\displaystyle+\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R)+\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}(x,R)\bigg),

where the constant
C(n,p,q,\kappa,l,\lambda,R)
is non-decreasing in
R
.
In the case of
\lambda>0
, (
3.9
)
holds whenever
R\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
and the Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{R}\psi^{2p}\omega_{f}e^{-ar}drd\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg[C_{1}(n,p)\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{p}\int_{B(x,R)}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}e^{-ar}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,+C_{2}(n,p)\int_{B(x,R)}\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{2p}e^{-ar}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,+C_{3}(n,p)\int_{B(x,R)}({\mathrm{Ric}_{f}^{\lambda}}_{-})^{p}e^{-ar}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2p}}.

By an inequality
a^{2p}+b^{2p}+c^{2p}\leq(a+b+c)^{2p}
(
a,b,c>0
), it follows that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(n,p)\left(\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{B(x,R)}\frac{\rho_{a}(\nabla f)^{p}}{r^{p}}e^{-ar}d\mathrm{vol}_{f}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle+C_{2}(n,p)\left(\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R)+\|{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\|_{p,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R)\right).

On the other hand, since
q>\frac{lp}{l-p}>2p
as
p<l\leq n<2p
, by the Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R)

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}(x,R)\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,q,\kappa)R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{q}}\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}(x,R).

Thus inserting this inequality into (
3.10
)
with help of Lemma
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle\quad\,\|\psi\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(n,p,q,\kappa,l)R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R)

\displaystyle+C_{2}(n,p,q,\kappa)R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{q}}\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}(x,R)

\displaystyle+C_{3}(n,p)\|{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\|_{p,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R).

By choosing the constant

\displaystyle\quad\,C(n,p,q,\kappa,l,\lambda,R)

\displaystyle=\max\Bigg\{C_{1}(n,p,q,\kappa,l)R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\,\,\,\,C_{2}(n,p,q,\kappa)R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{q}},C_{3}(n,p)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=C(n,p,q,\kappa,l)\left(\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\max\big\{R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}},R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{q}},1\big\}

\displaystyle=C(n,p,q,\kappa,l)\left(\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\max\big\{R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{q}},1\big\},

which is non-decreasing in
R
,
as well as
C(n,p,q,\kappa,l,\lambda,0)=C(n,p,q,\kappa,l)>0
,
we obtain (
3.9
) immediately.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
by the
L^{p}
-estimate of
\psi
in Proposition
3.3
.

As in [
[
11
,
25
]
], we need to compute the ratio of volume as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
x\in M
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\geq 0
and
R>0
we have

\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)\leq\frac{e^{aR}\omega_{\lambda}(R)|S^{n-1}|}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\left(\max_{0\leq s\leq R}\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr}{\omega_{\lambda}(s)}\right)\int_{B(x,R)}\psi d\operatorname{vol}_{f},

where
\psi=(h_{f}-h_{\lambda}-a)_{+}
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dR}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{S^{n-1}}\omega_{f}(r,\theta)d\theta dr=\int_{S^{n-1}}\omega_{f}(R,\theta)d\theta

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dR}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{S^{n-1}}\omega_{\lambda}^{a}(r)d\theta dr=\int_{S^{n-1}}\omega_{\lambda}^{a}(R)d\theta,

where
\omega_{\lambda}^{a}(r)=e^{ar}\omega_{\lambda}(r)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{S^{n-1}}\omega_{f}(R,\theta)d\theta\int_{0}^{R}\int_{S^{n-1}}\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(r)d\theta dr-\int_{0}^{R}\int_{S^{n-1}}\omega_{f}(r,\theta)d\theta dr\int_{S^{n-1}}\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(R)dr}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S^{n-1}|\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(r)\int_{S^{n-1}}\left(\frac{\omega_{f}(R,\theta)}{\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(R)}-\frac{\omega_{f}(r,\theta)}{\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(r)}\right)d\theta dr.

By Newton-Leibniz formula and the fact
(\omega_{f}/\omega^{a}_{\lambda})^{\prime}=(h_{f}-h_{\lambda}-a)\cdot\omega_{f}/\omega^{a}_{\lambda}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v(n,\lambda,R)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|S^{n-1}|\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(r)\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{r}^{R}\left(\frac{\omega_{f}}{\omega^{a}_{\lambda}}\right)^{\prime}(s)dsd\theta dr

\displaystyle=\frac{|S^{n-1}|\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(r)\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{r}^{R}(h_{f}-h_{\lambda}-a)\frac{\omega_{f}(s,\theta)}{\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(s)}dsd\theta dr.

By exchanging the order of integration, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\lambda}^{a}(R)|S^{n-1}|}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{r}^{R}(h_{f}-h_{\lambda}-a)\frac{\omega_{f}(s,\theta)}{\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(s)}\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(r)dsdrd\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\lambda}^{a}(R)|S^{n-1}|}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{R}(h_{f}-h_{\lambda}-a)(s)\frac{\omega_{f}(s,\theta)}{\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(s)}\int_{0}^{s}\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(r)drdsd\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{\lambda}^{a}(R)|S^{n-1}|}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{R}(h_{f}-h_{\lambda}-a)_{+}(s)\omega_{f}(s,\theta)\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(r)dr}{\omega^{a}_{\lambda}(s)}dsd\theta.

Thus,

\displaystyle\quad\,\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{aR}\omega_{\lambda}(R)|S^{n-1}|}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\left(\max_{0\leq s\leq R}\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr}{\omega_{\lambda}(s)}\right)\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{R}\psi(s,\theta)\omega_{f}(s,\theta)dsd\theta.

∎

Furthermore, we have

Proposition 4.2
.

For
p>0
and
\lambda\leq 0
, and for
R>0
, we have

(4.1)

\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C(n,p,a,R)R^{\frac{-n}{2p}}\|\psi\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R),

where
C(n,p,a,R)=C(n,p)e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}
. In the case of
\lambda>0
, (
4.1
) holds whenever
R\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
and the Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}\omega_{\lambda}(R)|S^{n-1}|}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{2}}\left(\max_{0\leq s\leq R}\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr}{\omega_{\lambda}(s)}\right)\|\psi\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R)\mathrm{vol}_{f}{B(x,R)}^{1-\frac{1}{2p}}.

Consequently,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}\omega_{\lambda}(R)|S^{n-1}|}{2p}\left(\max_{0\leq s\leq R}\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr}{\omega_{\lambda}(s)}\right)\frac{R^{\frac{n}{2p}}}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{1+\frac{1}{2p}}}

\displaystyle\times R^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\|\psi\|_{2p,f,a}(x,R).

Now we claim that

\alpha_{\lambda}(s):=\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr}{\omega_{\lambda}(s)}

is non-decreasing.

When
\lambda=0
, it is obvious. When
\lambda>0
, we only need to consider
\alpha_{\lambda}(s)
for
\lambda=1
,
0\leq s\leq\pi/2
since
\alpha_{\lambda}(s)=\alpha_{1}(\sqrt{\lambda}s)/\sqrt{\lambda}
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin^{n}(s)-(n-1)\cos(s)\int_{0}^{s}\sin^{n-1}(r)dr}{\sin^{n}(s)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sin^{n}(s)-(n-1)\int_{0}^{s}\sin^{n-1}(r)d\sin(r)}{\sin^{n}(s)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{n-1}{n}>0.

Thus
\alpha_{1}
is increasing.

When
\lambda<0
, we also consider
\alpha_{\lambda}(s)
for
\lambda=-1
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{-1}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh^{2(n-1)}(s)-(n-1)\sinh^{n-2}(s)\cosh(s)\int_{0}^{s}\sinh^{n-1}(r)dr}{\sinh^{2(n-1)}(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh(s)}{\sinh^{n}(s)}\left(\frac{\sinh^{n}(s)}{\cosh(s)}-(n-1)\int_{0}^{s}\sinh^{n-1}(r)dr\right).

Let

\beta_{-1}(s)=\frac{\sinh^{n}(s)}{\cosh(s)}-(n-1)\int_{0}^{s}\sinh^{n-1}(r)dr.

It follows that

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}_{-1}(s)

\displaystyle=n\sinh^{n-1}(s)-\frac{\sinh^{n+1}(s)}{\cosh^{2}(s)}-(n-1)\sinh^{n-1}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh^{n-1}(s)}{\cosh^{2}(s)}\left(\cosh^{2}(s)-\sinh^{2}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh^{n-1}s}{\cosh^{2}s}\geq 0.

Since
\beta_{-1}(0)=0
,
\beta_{-1}(s)\geq 0
. Thus
\alpha^{\prime}_{-1}(s)\geq 0
, and consequently,
\alpha_{-1}
is non-decreasing. The claim is checked.

By the claim above we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}\omega_{\lambda}(R)|S^{n-1}|}{2p}\left(\max_{0\leq s\leq R}\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr}{\omega_{\lambda}(s)}\right)\frac{R^{\frac{n}{2p}}}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{1+\frac{1}{2p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}|S^{n-1}|}{2p}\frac{R^{\frac{n}{2p}}\int_{0}^{R}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)^{1+\frac{1}{2p}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}|S^{n-1}|}{2p}\frac{R^{\frac{n}{2p}}\int_{0}^{R}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr}{\left(|S^{n-1}|\int_{0}^{R}\omega_{\lambda}(r)dr\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2p}}}

\displaystyle=C(p)e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}\left(\frac{R^{n}}{v(n,\lambda,R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}.

On the other hand, by the volume monotonicity of Bishop-Gromov,
R^{n}/v(n,\lambda,R)
is bounded by a constant
C(n)
. Hence, inserting (
4.3
) into (
4.2
), we get (
4.1
) immediately.
∎

Now we can finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
3.3
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dR}\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{f}B(x,R)}{v_{a}(n,\lambda,R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p,q,a,\kappa,l,\lambda,R)R^{\frac{-n}{2p}}\bigg(\|{\mathrm{Ric}^{\lambda}_{f}}_{-}\|_{p,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R)

\displaystyle+\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,R)+\|\rho_{a}(\nabla f)\|_{q,f,a}(x,R)\bigg),

where

\displaystyle\quad\,C(n,p,q,a,\kappa,l,\lambda,R)

\displaystyle=C(n,p,q,\kappa,l)\left(\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\max\big\{R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{q}},1\big\}\times C(n,p)e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}

\displaystyle=C(n,p,q,\kappa,l)e^{aR(1+\frac{1}{2p})}\max\big\{R^{\frac{l}{2p}-\frac{l}{q}},1\big\}\left(\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq R}\frac{t\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathrm{sn}_{\lambda}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which is increasing in
R
and
C(n,p,q,a,\kappa,l,\lambda,0)=C(n,p,q,\kappa,l)>0
. Since
p>\frac{n}{2}
, the right-hand side of (
4.4
) is integrable. Integrating (
4.4
) from
r
to
R
yields the desired inequality
(
1.4
) immediately.
∎

An application

In this section, we apply Theorem
1.2
to prove Corollary
1.4
for the evolved metrics along the Kähler-Ricci flow (
1.5
).
First we note that the volume growth condition (

) has been proved by Zhang in [
[

]
] as

|B(x,r,t)|_{g(t)}\leq c_{1}r^{n},~\forall~r\leq{\rm diam}(M,g(t)),

where
c_{1}>0
is a uniform constant independent of
t
.
Actually, by Perelman’s non-collapsing result in [
[

]
] and the scalar curvature estimate [
[
10
]
] (also see [
[
14
]

|B(x,r,t)|_{g(t)}\geq c_{2}r^{n},~\forall~r\leq{\rm diam}(M,g(t)).

Note that
{\rm diam}(M,g(t))
is also uniformly bounded [
[
10
]
].

The potential function
f=f_{t}
for BE Ricci curvature in (

) associated to the metric
g=g(t)
has been constructed in a recent work of Tian-Zhang-Zhang-Zhu-Zhu [
[

]
] as follows.
Let
V=\mathrm{Ric}_{-}(g)\geq 0

L_{g}(\phi)=-\Delta-V
associated to
g
to solve the first eigenfunction equation

\Delta\phi+V\phi-\rho_{0}\phi=0,

where
-\rho_{0}
is the first eigenvalue of
L_{g}
.
Since
\phi
is strictly positive,
f=\ln\phi
is well defined and it
satisfies

(5.1)

\Delta f+|\nabla f|^{2}+V=\rho_{0}.

It was proved in [
[
19
]
] that
\rho_{0}\leq B_{0}
for some uniform positive constant
B_{0}
independent of
t
. Moreover, we have

Lemma 5.1
.

There exist positive constants
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
and
b_{p}\,(p\geq 1)
only depending on
p
and the initial metric
g_{0}
such that

(5.2)

|f(x)|\leq B_{1},\quad\forall x\in M,

(5.3)

\|f\|_{C^{1/2}(M)}\leq B_{2},

and

(5.4)

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq b_{p},\quad\forall p\geq 1.

By smoothing the function
V
, we may assume that the solution
f
of (
5.1
) is smooth and all estimates of
f
in Lemma
5.1
hold.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Let
\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}(t)=e^{2f_{t}}g(t)
be a conformal metric of
g(t)
. Then
by a relationship of Ricci curvatures between two conformal metrics (cf. [
[
2
]
]), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}(\tilde{g})+(n-2){\rm Hess}_{g}(f)

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ric}(g)-\Delta f\cdot g+(n-2)df\otimes df-(n-2)|\nabla f|^{2}g.

On the other hand, by an identity,

\operatorname{Hess}_{\tilde{g}}(f)=\operatorname{Hess}_{g}(f)-2df\otimes df+|\nabla f|^{2}g,

we have

|{\rm Hess}_{g}(f)-{\rm Hess}_{\tilde{g}}(f)|\leq C|\nabla f|^{2}.

Thus replacing
f
by
\hat{f}=(n-2)f
, we get from (
5
) and (
5.1
) with help of (
