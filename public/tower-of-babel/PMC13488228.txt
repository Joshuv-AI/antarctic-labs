A nomogram for predicting 30-day mortality in patients with carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae infection: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Pei Zhang, Liping Wang, Caihua Jiang, Xiaohua Qin, Xin Zhou, Yunfeng Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCarbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP) has emerged as a significant global health threat due to its high virulence. This study aims to analyze the risk factors of 30-day mortality in patients with CRKP infection and to construct a nomogram model for predicting 30-day mortality risk, offering valuable insights for clinical decision-making.MethodsThis study retrospectively enrolled 290 patients with CRKP infection from January 2022 to December 2025 at the Public Health Clinical Center Affiliated to Shandong University. Patients were categorized into the survival (n = 230) and non-survival (n = 60) groups based on clinical outcomes at 30 days. Multivariate logistic analysis was conducted to identify independent risk factors. A nomogram model was developed using R software. The model’s performance was evaluated using the area under the ROC curve (AUC), the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, and calibration plots. The clinical utility of the model was evaluated using decision curve analysis (DCA), while the bootstrap technique was applied for the model’s internal validation.ResultsThe 30-day mortality rate was 20.7% in patients with CRKP infection. Septic shock (aOR 16.850, 95% CI 5.915–48.000, P < 0.001), Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) score (aOR 1.242, 95% CI 1.032–1.493, P = 0.022), and appropriate antibiotic therapy within 3 days of infection onset (aOR 0.200, 95% CI 0.074–0.542, P = 0.002) were identified as independent predictors of mortality. Compared with thrombocytopenia (< 100 × 10⁹/L), normal platelet count (aOR 0.046, 95% CI 0.014–0.151) and thrombocytosis (aOR 0.030, 95% CI 0.006–0.155) were independently protective (both P < 0.001). A nomogram incorporating these predictors demonstrated excellent discrimination (AUC 0.908, 95% CI 0.865–0.951) and adequate calibration (Hosmer–Lemeshow P = 0.989, Brier score = 0.092). The DCA curve demonstrated significant potential for clinical application. Internal validation was conducted using 1,000 bootstrap cycles. The model bias-corrected C-index was 0.903 (95% CI 0.835–0.966).ConclusionsThe study identified independent risk factors associated with the 30-day mortality of CRKP-infected patients. The nomogram model exhibited promising discriminatory capability and clinical applicability, providing an effective tool for early identification of at-risk patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-026-13777-7.Keywords: Carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae, Mortality, Risk factor, Nomogram

Introduction

Carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP) is a significant nosocomial pathogen distinguished by its rapid spread, substantial virulence, and high mortality rates [1]. In recent years, there has been a consistent increase in both the global incidence of CRKP infections and the corresponding mortality rates [2–4], posing considerable challenges to clinical management and hospital infection control measures.

The current therapeutic landscape remains constrained. As the prevalence of carbapenem-resistant pathogens escalates, the effectiveness of traditional antibiotics has waned [5]. Alternative therapeutic agents, including polymyxins, tigecycline, and fosfomycin, are often limited by their inadequate efficacy, significant toxicity, and other adverse effects, or restricted availability [6, 7]. Additionally, the slow pace of novel antimicrobial agent development has severely affected clinical decision-making processes and treatment strategies for infections.

Emerging clinical evidence suggests that early detection and swift, targeted therapeutic interventions can significantly enhance clinical outcomes and survival rates for patients afflicted with CRKP infections [8]. As a result, there has been a growing interest among researchers in the development of predictive models based on clinical risk factors, aimed at identifying patients at high risk and facilitating timely interventions [9, 10]. Nevertheless, many of the existing studies are plagued by limitations such as small sample sizes, the omission of crucial biochemical parameters, and a narrow focus on specific patient populations, which hinder their generalizability and practical implementation. This study seeks to build upon previous research by expanding the sample size and integrating routine biochemical indicators to create a prognostic prediction model for CRKP infections, with the goal of providing actionable insights for antimicrobial selection and clinical decision-making.

Materials and methods

Study population

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at the Public Health Clinical Center Affiliated to Shandong University. A total of 290 patients with CRKP infection were enrolled between January 2022 and December 2025 after applying exclusion criteria (Fig. 1). Exclusion criteria: (a) duplicated patients, with multiple hospitalizations; only the first infection recorded in their medical records is documented; (b) incomplete clinical data; (c) The infection was diagnosed within 48 h of admission [11]; (d) age < 18 years old.

Flowchart of patient selection and enrollment. CRKP: Carbapenem-resistant K. pneumoniae

Data collection

Demographic information, clinical manifestations, laboratory findings, and treatment-related details at the point of initial infection diagnosis, as well as outcomes at 30 days, were systematically extracted from the electronic medical records of participating institutions using standardized, pre-established data collection forms. The data retrieved from the patient’s medical records included age, gender, site of infection, underlying diseases (such as hypertension and diabetes, etc.), Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), use of invasive devices, surgical history within 1 month prior to positive culture, and empirical therapy. Additionally, we gathered data in the aftermath of a positive culture result, which encompassed particular hematological parameters, antibiotic therapy, complications, and clinical outcomes. The outcome measure was the crude mortality rate at 30 days after the index culture.

Microbiology

Bacterial isolates were identified using MALDI-TOF MS (Bruker Daltonics Inc., Billerica, Massachusetts, USA). Antimicrobial susceptibility tests (AST) were performed by the VITEK 2 system or the broth microdilution method, and the results were interpreted using the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) documentation standards (CLSI, 2023) [12]: If the MIC of meropenem or imipenem was ≥ 4 mg/L, or the MIC of ertapenem was ≥ 2 mg/L, it was considered to be carbapenem resistance.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 27.0 software and R version 4.4.1. The categorical variables were expressed as percentages (%) and compared using the χ2 test. The Shapiro-Wilk test was employed to evaluate the normality of continuous variables. Continuous variables that did not follow a normal distribution were presented as median with interquartile range and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. All significant factors (P < 0.05) in univariate regression were incorporated into multivariate regression to determine independent risk factors utilizing the forward stepwise selection approach. Additionally, variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis verified the absence of multicollinearity among the selected variables in the final multivariate model. The obtained independent risk factors for mortality in patients with CRKP infection were used to construct a nomogram-based predictive model, which was evaluated with ROC curve. The evaluation of the model’s calibration was conducted using a calibration curve and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. The DCA curve was employed to assess the clinical benefit of the model. The internal validation of the model was assessed using the bootstrap resampling technique, which involved performing repeated sampling 1,000 times.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

This study enrolled 290 patients, predominantly male (71.4%). The mean age of the participants was 67 years. The most commonly reported comorbidities were hypertension (39.0%) and diabetes (34.5%). The primary invasive procedures included urinary catheterization (74.5%) and central venous catheterization (63.8%). Among the treatment options for CRKP infections, ceftazidime/avibactam-based regimens (31.4%) and colistin-containing regimens (27.2%) were the most common. The most common types of infection were the respiratory system (76.6%) and urinary tract (9.3%). The clinical results were as follows: 60 cases died and 230 cases survived.

Patients in the non-survival group exhibited a significantly higher incidence of septic shock and a markedly elevated CCI score. Concurrently, white blood cell counts, PCT, and C-reactive protein levels also increased, whereas platelet counts were significantly lower compared to the survival group.

The patients with CRKP infection were divided into survivor and non-survivor groups, according to clinical outcomes after a 30-day period. The univariate analysis revealed that age, bloodstream infection, septic shock, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, CCI score, urinary catheterization, central venous catheterization, appropriate antibiotic therapy within 3 days of infection onset, colistin-containing regimens, and laboratory examination (Table 1). Unadjusted estimates were shown in Table S1. Multivariate logistic analysis was conducted on the statistically significant factors identified (P < 0.05). Using thrombocytopenia (< 100 × 10⁹/L) as the reference category, both normal platelet count (100–300 × 10⁹/L, aOR 0.046, 95% CI 0.014–0.151, P < 0.001) and thrombocytosis (> 300 × 10⁹/L, aOR 0.030, 95% CI 0.006–0.155, P < 0.001) were significantly associated with lower mortality risk. Additional independent predictors included septic shock (aOR 16.850, 95% CI 5.915–48.000, P < 0.001), CCI score (aOR 1.242, 95% CI 1.032–1.493, P = 0.022), and appropriate antibiotic therapy within 3 days of infection onset (aOR 0.200, 95% CI 0.074–0.542, P = 0.002).

Nomogram for 30-day mortality in CRKP infection

A nomogram was constructed to predict 30-day mortality in patients with CRKP infection based on the independent risk factors from the multivariate logistic analysis (Fig. 2b). The regression coefficients were used to derive scores for each independent variable (septic shock, CCI score, appropriate antibiotic therapy within 3 days of infection onset, and platelet count). By summarizing the scores of four risk factors, the 30-day mortality risk associated with CRKP infection can be assessed using the “Total Points” and compared against line 7 (“30-day mortality risk”) in the nomogram. Moreover, the tool is web-based and can be accessed remotely via the internet (Fig. 2c).

Internal validation of the nomogram

The AUC of the predictive model was 0.908 (95% CI 0.865–0.951), which suggested that the model demonstrated strong predictive capabilities (Fig. 3A). Furthermore, the Hosmer–Lemeshow test indicated a good fit for the predictive model (χ² = 1.703, P = 0.989). There was no significant discrepancy between the calibration curve and the ideal curve of the nomogram (Brier score = 0.092), indicating that the model demonstrates satisfactory calibration performance (Fig. 3B). The C-index calculated through 1000 bootstrap replications was 0.903 (95% CI 0.835–0.966), suggesting that the model demonstrates strong discriminatory ability and consistency in its performance. Figure 4 validated the nomogram’s benefit and underscores its clinical value in informing decision-making across diverse risk thresholds. The DCA curve indicated a favorable net benefit within the predicted probability range of 0.03 to 0.99 (Fig. 4a). The Clinical Impact Curves (CIC) illustrated the clinical utility of the predictive model on the number of patients across various probability thresholds (Fig. 4b).

Discussion

Carbapenem resistance in KP infection has become a notable concern for healthcare systems worldwide [13, 14]. In 2024, the World Health Organization (WHO) published an updated list of priority bacterial pathogens, reiterating the classification of CRKP as a serious threat [15]. Consequently, the prompt identification of patients with CRKP infection has become a pressing necessity in clinical settings to enhance their survival rates. Our study established independent predictors of mortality in CRKP infection and derived a nomogram-based tool to support preventive intervention and infection control.

In our study, we found that the 30-day mortality with CRKP infection was 20.7%. In contrast, this rate is lower than the overall mortality rate for CRKP infections in China, which Yang X calculated as 27% [16]. We identified multiple independent risk factors that contribute to mortality. In alignment with earlier research [17, 18], this investigation recognized an appropriate antimicrobial regimen in 3 days as a predictive indicator (aOR 0.200, 95% CI 0.074–0.542, P = 0.002). Initiating appropriate antibiotic treatment within 3 days can significantly reduce the mortality rate associated with CRKP. Therefore, the critical nature of timely disease control is evident. The Charlson Comorbidity Index is a validated clinical prediction tool designed to quantify the extent of a patient’s comorbid disease burden and assess associated frailty. Our analysis identified the higher CII score as an independent predictor of mortality in individuals infected with CRKP. Meta-analysis had indicated that mortality rates associated with CRKP were higher in patients with comorbidities [19]. This elevated risk likely stems from a convergence of factors: deteriorated physiological reserves, compromised immune function, and diminished host defense mechanisms, all of which synergistically increase the probability of death.

Existing literatures have firmly established the prognostic value of platelet counts in patients infected with CRKP. Distinct from prior CRKP prognostic investigations that relied on binary or continuous modeling frameworks [20–23], our adoption of a three-categorical approach provided two primary methodological benefits: initially, it bypasses the rigid linearity constraints typical of continuous variables; subsequently, it facilitates risk stratification that is both more nuanced and clinically actionable compared to dichotomous approaches. Our results indicated a strong protective effect of higher platelet counts against 30-day mortality in CRKP infection. This inverse relationship highlights that thrombocytopenia represents more than a trivial laboratory anomaly; rather, it serves as a potent indicator of disease severity, likely stemming from consumptive coagulopathy, endothelial activation, and suppressed thrombopoiesis. Lu J et al. and Qiu M et al. reported that septic shock was associated with 30-day mortality [24, 25]. Our research also confirmed this point. Septic shock represents a critical medical condition characterized by sepsis. The in-hospital mortality rate for this condition is notably high, fluctuating between 30% and 50%. Consequently, for this particularly vulnerable cohort, the incorporation of biomarkers, such as D-dimer, alongside the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score for evaluating risk, has the potential to enhance the predictive accuracy regarding patient outcomes.

While preceding studies have demonstrated encouraging outcomes, their scope was restricted to single infection categories or specific disease entities [24–26]. Conversely, our proposed model encompasses diverse CRKP infection types and draws upon a patient cohort aggregated from multiple clinical departments. We constructed a nomogram prediction model, which underwent successful validation. The area under the curve (AUC) was determined to be 0.908, indicating a robust predictive capability for clinical outcomes in CRKP infections. To bolster the clinical applicability of our predictive framework, we constructed an interactive web-based nomogram that enables physicians to promptly assess in-hospital mortality risk among patients with CRKP infections by leveraging early-stage clinical indicators.

We recognize that this study has several limitations. Primarily, the retrospective nature of this single-center study introduces a potential risk of selection bias. Additionally, incomplete records regarding APACHE II and SOFA scores could have compromised the robustness of our predictive model. Furthermore, given that the model was developed using a relatively small cohort, external validation is imperative prior to any clinical deployment. Finally, we did not perform a molecular epidemiological analysis of the CRKP isolates. Therefore, in future research, we need to conduct multi-center, large-scale cohort studies and perform molecular typing and sequencing analysis on both virulence genes and carbapenem resistance genes.

Conclusions

Septic shock, higher CCI score, appropriate antibiotic therapy within 3 days of infection onset, and platelet count were independently associated with 30-day mortality risk among individuals with CRKP infections. Based on this, we developed and successfully validated a nomogram-based predictive model. Once a patient is admitted to the hospital, the specific risk of infection can be promptly assessed at the bedside. For patients identified as high-risk, proactive scientific prevention measures and targeted antibacterial therapies can be implemented, ultimately leading to a decrease in mortality rates.
