Entropic Strict Minimum Message Length and Its Connections to PAC–Bayes and NML
Enes Makalic, Daniel F. Schmidt

Abstract
We introduce entropic strict minimum message length (SMML), a risk-sensitive generalization of strict minimum message length coding. The proposed criterion replaces expected two-part codelength under the prior predictive distribution with an exponential certainty equivalent, thereby defining a one-parameter family of coding rules that interpolates between Bayesian average-case coding and worst-case minimax coding. We show that ordinary SMML is recovered in the risk-neutral limit, while the extreme risk-sensitive limit yields a minimax codelength criterion; when centered by the oracle maximum likelihood codelength, this criterion coincides with the normalized maximum likelihood (NML) minimax-regret principle. We further prove that entropic SMML admits a variational characterization as a Kullback–Leibler-regularized worst-case expected codelength, giving it a PAC–Bayes-type interpretation. We establish a joint asymptotic theory linking the sample size n n and the risk parameter τ \tau , showing that in regular parametric models the transition between Bayesian, robust, and minimax coding regimes occurs on a logarithmic scale. For regular exponential families, the fixed-codebook partition remains affine in sufficient-statistic space, while the codepoints satisfy a tilted moment-matching condition and admit an interpretation as tilted Bregman centroids. These results position entropic SMML as an information-theoretic bridge between MML, PAC–Bayes, and MDL.

2
Strict Minimum Message Length

This section fixes notation and defines the two coding criteria studied in the paper. We work throughout with a countable data space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, as in the strict minimum message length (SMML) framework
24
;
26
;
14
. For the ordinary SMML criterion, the basic objects are a finite partition of
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, a collection of codepoints, and the resulting two-part codelength under the prior predictive distribution. The ordinary SMML setup and its KL-projection interpretation are reviewed in
14
.

Let
X_{n}
denote a random dataset of size
n
, taking values in the countable space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, and let
x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}
denote a realization. Let
\{p_{n}(x\mid\theta):\theta\in\Theta\}
be a parametric model with prior
\pi(\theta)
, and define the prior predictive distribution

\displaystyle r_{n}(x)=\int_{\Theta}p_{n}(x\mid\theta)\pi(\theta)d\theta.

(1)

An admissible finite codebook is a triple
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, where

•

\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
is a finite partition of
\mathcal{X}_{n}
into nonempty cells,

•

q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})
satisfies
q_{j}>0
and
\sum_{j=1}^{k}q_{j}=1
,

•

\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k})
contains one codepoint
\theta_{j}\in\Theta
per cell.

We write
j(x)
for the unique index such that
x\in P_{j(x)}
, and let
\mathcal{C}_{n}
denote the class of admissible finite codebooks at sample size
n
.
Given
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, the associated two-part SMML codelength is

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)=-\log q_{j(x)}-\log p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{j(x)}).

(2)

The first term encodes the cell index (the
assertion
), and the second encodes the data conditional on the selected codepoint (the
detail
). The ordinary SMML objective is the expected codelength under the prior predictive law:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\!\left[\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})\right].

(3)

A SMML codebook is any minimizer

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{*},q_{n}^{*},\theta_{n}^{*})\in\argmin_{(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\mathcal{I}_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta).

(4)

The codebook formulation in (
4
) is equivalent to the classical partition-based formulation of SMML. Indeed, for fixed
(\mathcal{P},\theta)
, the minimizing assertion probabilities are

\displaystyle q_{j}^{*}=r_{n}(P_{j}):=\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x),\qquad j=1,\dots,k,

(5)

and for fixed
(\mathcal{P},q)
, each cellwise codepoint solves

\displaystyle\theta_{j}^{*}\in\argmax_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\log p_{n}(x\mid\theta),\quad j=1,\dots,k.

(6)

Equivalently, each ordinary SMML codepoint is the KL projection of the normalized cellwise distribution onto the model family
14
.

2.1
Entropic Strict Minimum Message Length

We now introduce the entropic, or risk-sensitive, generalization of SMML. For
\tau>0
, define the entropic SMML objective

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\frac{1}{\tau}\log\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\!\left[\exp\!\big(\tau\,\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})\big)\right].

(7)

An
entropic SMML
codebook is any minimizer

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{n,\tau}^{*},q_{n,\tau}^{*},\theta_{n,\tau}^{*})\in\argmin_{(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta).

(8)

The parameter
\tau
controls sensitivity to large codelengths. For small
\tau
, the criterion in (
7
) behaves like the ordinary SMML expected codelength; for large
\tau
, it increasingly emphasizes the upper tail of the codelength distribution and approaches a worst-case coding principle.

For a fixed codebook, the partition rule remains pointwise minimum codelength:

\displaystyle x\in P_{j}\iff j\in\argmin_{1\leq\ell\leq k}\left\{-\log q_{\ell}-\log p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{\ell})\right\},

(9)

so the entropic deformation does not alter the local decision rule, only the global criterion used to choose the codebook.

For a fixed partition
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
, define

\displaystyle A_{j,\tau}(\theta):=\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\,p_{n}(x\mid\theta)^{-\tau},\qquad j=1,\dots,k.

(10)

Then (
7
) may be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\frac{1}{\tau}\log\sum_{j=1}^{k}q_{j}^{-\tau}A_{j,\tau}(\theta_{j}),

(11)

and the fixed-partition codepoint equation becomes

\displaystyle\theta_{j,\tau}^{*}\in\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}A_{j,\tau}(\theta)=\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\,p_{n}(x\mid\theta)^{-\tau},

(12)

for
j=1,\dots,k
. This is the risk-sensitive analogue of the ordinary SMML cellwise fit in (
6
). The corresponding fixed-partition assertion probabilities will be derived in the next section from the variational form of the entropic criterion.

2.2
Connection to generalized source coding

The entropic SMML criterion defined in (
7
) is closely related to Campbell’s exponential
source-coding criterion
5
where the ordinary expected code length is replaced by an exponential average of the form

L_{t}(\ell)=\frac{1}{t}\log\sum_{i}p_{i}e^{t\ell_{i}},\qquad t>0,

where
p_{i}
is the source probability of symbol
i
and
\ell_{i}
is the corresponding codeword length. Campbell showed that the optimal value of this criterion is governed by Rényi entropy
16
with order
\alpha=(1+t)^{-1}
, providing an operational source-coding interpretation of Rényi entropy that is analogous to Shannon’s theorem for ordinary expected length.

The entropic SMML criterion may be viewed as the same exponential-type deformation applied not to arbitrary source-codeword lengths, but to SMML two-part codelengths. Indeed, for a fixed admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
, define the induced coding distribution

Q_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)=q_{j(x)}p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{j(x)}),

so that

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)=-\log Q_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x).

Then the entropic SMML objective can be written as

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\frac{1}{\tau}\log\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}r_{n}(x)Q_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)^{-\tau},

which is Campbell’s exponential-average length criterion with the source distribution
p_{i}
replaced by the prior predictive distribution
r_{n}(x)
, and with ordinary codeword lengths replaced by SMML two-part codelengths. If optimisation were carried out over all probability distributions
Q
on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, the optimum would satisfy

\inf_{Q}\frac{1}{\tau}\log\sum_{x}r_{n}(x)Q(x)^{-\tau}=H_{1/(1+\tau)}(r_{n}),

where

H_{\alpha}(r_{n})=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\sum_{x}r_{n}(x)^{\alpha},\qquad\alpha=\frac{1}{1+\tau},

is the Rényi entropy
16
. Since SMML restricts
Q
to the two-part statistical form
Q_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)=q_{j(x)}p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{j(x)})
, we have

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\geq H_{1/(1+\tau)}(r_{n})

for every admissible codebook. The excess over
H_{1/(1+\tau)}
quantifies the cost of the SMML statistical restriction to two-part messages with the first part encoding a model, and the second part encoding the data conditional on the model. Thus ordinary SMML is lower bounded by Shannon entropy in the limit
\tau\downarrow 0
, while entropic SMML is lower bounded by the corresponding Rényi entropy for finite
\tau>0
.

This viewpoint clarifies the role of the risk parameter. Campbell’s exponential length criterion
5
penalises long codewords more strongly than ordinary expected length; analogously, entropic SMML penalises large two-part statistical codelengths. As
\tau\downarrow 0
, the criterion reduces to the ordinary prior-predictive expected codelength. As
\tau\to\infty
, it approaches a worst-case codelength criterion. Thus entropic SMML can be interpreted as lifting exponential source-coding principle from ordinary source codes to structured statistical two-part codes.

Remark 1
(Escort interpretation)

Campbell’s exponential source-coding theorem
5
admits a natural interpretation in terms of escort distributions
3
;
7
. In the unconstrained coding problem, minimising the exponential-average codelength

\frac{1}{\tau}\log\sum_{x}r_{n}(x)Q(x)^{-\tau}

over all probability distributions
Q
yields the optimal solution

Q_{\tau}^{*}(x)=\frac{r_{n}(x)^{1/(1+\tau)}}{\sum_{y}r_{n}(y)^{1/(1+\tau)}},

which is the escort distribution of the prior predictive law
r_{n}
of order
\alpha=(1+\tau)^{-1}
. Entropic SMML imposes the additional structural constraint that the coding distribution factorises as

Q_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)=q_{j(x)}p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{j(x)}),

so that the entropic SMML problem may be viewed as a constrained
escort-coding problem. The corresponding redundancy

\Delta_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{SMML}}=\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{SMML}}-H_{1/(1+\tau)}(r_{n})

measures the Rényi-divergence projection error of the escort distribution onto the SMML two-part code class. Within each cell, the tilted moment-matching weights

w_{j,\tau}(x;\theta)\propto r_{n}(x)p_{n}(x\mid\theta)^{-\tau}

induce a local, model-dependent escort tilt of the prior predictive
distribution.

The next section gives a complementary variational interpretation of enropic SMML. The same exponential criterion is equivalent to a Kullback-Leibler (KL)-regularised
13
worst-case expected codelength over posterior tilts of the prior predictive distribution.

3
Variational and PAC–Bayesian Characterization

We now give the variational representation of entropic SMML and derive its PAC–Bayesian interpretation. The key observation is that the exponential certainty equivalent in (
7
) admits the Gibbs–Donsker–Varadhan Kullback–Leibler (KL) dual form, turning entropic SMML into a KL-regularized robust codelength criterion
8
;
6
. We then use this representation to derive the optimal assertion probabilities for a fixed partition.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
denote the set of probability distributions on the countable data space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, and for
s,r\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
write
s\ll r
if
s
is absolutely continuous with respect to
r
. For such
s
and
r
, define the KL divergence
13

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s\|r)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}s(x)\log\frac{s(x)}{r(x)},

(13)

with the usual convention that
0\log 0=0
. The following theorem is the basic variational identity underlying the
entropic criterion.

Theorem 1
(Variational representation of entropic SMML)

For any admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
and any
\tau>0
, such that

Z_{n,\tau}:=\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\exp\{\tau\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})\}<\infty,

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
can be written as

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X}_{n}):\,s\ll r_{n}}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{s}\!\left[\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})\right]-\frac{1}{\tau}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s\|r_{n})\right\}.

(14)

Moreover, the supremum is attained by the exponentially tilted distribution

\displaystyle s_{n,\tau}^{*}(x)=\frac{r_{n}(x)\exp\!\big(\tau\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)\big)}{\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}r_{n}(y)\exp\!\big(\tau\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(y)\big)},\quad x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}.

(15)

Proof.
Fix
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
and write
\Lambda(x)=\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)
. For any
s\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
with
s\ll r_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s}[\Lambda(X_{n})]-\frac{1}{\tau}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s\|r_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}s(x)\Lambda(x)-\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}s(x)\log\frac{s(x)}{r_{n}(x)}=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}s(x)\log\frac{r_{n}(x)e^{\tau\Lambda(x)}}{s(x)}.

(16)

Define

\displaystyle Z_{n,\tau}:=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}r_{n}(y)e^{\tau\Lambda(y)}.

(17)

Then

\displaystyle\frac{r_{n}(x)e^{\tau\Lambda(x)}}{s(x)}=Z_{n,\tau}\,\frac{s_{n,\tau}^{*}(x)}{s(x)},

(18)

where
s_{n,\tau}^{*}
is given by (
15
). Substituting (
18
) into (
16
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s}[\Lambda]-\frac{1}{\tau}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s\|r_{n})=\frac{1}{\tau}\log Z_{n,\tau}-\frac{1}{\tau}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s\|s_{n,\tau}^{*}).

(19)

Since KL divergence is nonnegative, the right-hand side is maximized when
s=s_{n,\tau}^{*}
, proving

\sup_{s\ll r_{n}}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{s}[\Lambda]-\frac{1}{\tau}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s\|r_{n})\right\}=\frac{1}{\tau}\log Z_{n,\tau}.

By (
7
), the latter equals

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
, proving the claim.

\square

Theorem
1
shows that entropic SMML can be interpreted as the optimization of worst-case expected codelength over all data distributions
s
that remain close to the prior predictive distribution
r_{n}
in KL divergence. The parameter
\tau
controls the strength of this robustness penalty: small
\tau
enforces
s\approx r_{n}
, whereas large
\tau
permits the tilted distribution to concentrate increasingly on high-codelength events. The optimal tilted distribution may also be interpreted as a divergence-controlled deformation of the prior predictive law
r_{n}
, placing the entropic SMML criterion within a broader class of escort and Rényi-type transformations of probability measures. Closely related entropy-regularized minimax formulations also appear in entropic risk measurement and robust Bayesian decision theory, where worst-case expected loss is penalized by relative entropy with respect to a reference distribution; see Berger
4
and Föllmer and Knispel
10
.

3.1
PAC–Bayesian Interpretation

The variational form from Theorem
1
immediately yields a PAC–Bayesian codelength bound.

Corollary 1
(PAC–Bayes-type codelength bound)

For any admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, any
\tau>0
, and any distribution
s\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
with
s\ll r_{n}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s}\!\left[\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})\right]\leq\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)+\frac{1}{\tau}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s\|r_{n}).

(20)

Proof.
This follows immediately from (
14
) by evaluating the supremum at the given distribution
s
.

\square

Corollary
1
identifies entropic SMML as a PAC–Bayes-type coding rule based on the standard KL change-of-measure inequality. Specifically, the entropic codelength is the smallest value that upper bounds expected codelength under every posterior tilt
s
, up to a KL complexity penalty relative to the prior predictive distribution. In this sense, entropic SMML is a KL-regularized robustification of ordinary SMML.

3.2
Fixed-partition optimization

The variational representation also clarifies the fixed-partition optimization. Let
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
be fixed, and define
A_{j,\tau}(\theta)
as in (
10
).
The next proposition gives the optimal assertion probabilities for fixed partition and codepoints.

Proposition 1
(Optimal assertion probabilities for fixed partition)

Fix a partition
\mathcal{P}
and codepoints
\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k})
. Then any minimizer of (
11
) over
q_{1},\dots,q_{k}>0
subject to
\sum_{j=1}^{k}q_{j}=1
satisfies

\displaystyle q_{j,\tau}^{*}=\frac{A_{j,\tau}(\theta_{j})^{1/(1+\tau)}}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}A_{\ell,\tau}(\theta_{\ell})^{1/(1+\tau)}},\qquad j=1,\dots,k.

(21)

Substituting (
21
) into (
11
) yields the profiled entropic SMML criterion

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},\theta)=\frac{1+\tau}{\tau}\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}A_{j,\tau}(\theta_{j})^{1/(1+\tau)}\right).

(22)

Proof.
Since the logarithm is monotone, minimizing (
11
) over
q
is equivalent to minimizing

F(q)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}q_{j}^{-\tau}A_{j,\tau}(\theta_{j})

subject to
\sum_{j}q_{j}=1
. The Lagrangian is

\mathcal{L}(q,\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}q_{j}^{-\tau}A_{j,\tau}(\theta_{j})+\lambda\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}q_{j}-1\Big),

with first-order condition

-\tau A_{j,\tau}(\theta_{j})q_{j}^{-\tau-1}+\lambda=0,\qquad j=1,\dots,k.

This implies

q_{j}=\left(\frac{\tau A_{j,\tau}(\theta_{j})}{\lambda}\right)^{1/(1+\tau)}.

Normalizing by
\sum_{j}q_{j}=1
gives (
21
). Substituting the result
back into
F(q)
yields (
22
).

\square

Proposition
1
reduces the fixed-partition problem to the optimization of the profiled criterion (
22
) over the codepoints alone.
For fixed partition and codepoints with finite likelihood terms, (
21
) reduces to the ordinary SMML cell probabilities as
\tau\downarrow 0
(see (
5
)).
The corresponding endpoint limits are developed formally in the next section.

4
SMML, Worst-Case Codelength, and Normalized Maximum Likelihood

This section establishes the two endpoint limits of the entropic SMML criterion. We show first that, as
\tau\downarrow 0
, entropic SMML reduces to the ordinary expected-codelength criterion of strict minimum message length. We then show that, as
\tau\to\infty
, the objective converges to a worst-case codelength criterion. Since NML is characterized by minimax regret rather than by minimax absolute codelength, the precise connection to NML is obtained by applying the same soft-max limit to regret relative to the oracle maximum likelihood code. This is the classical setting of minimax-regret coding, as formalized by Shtarkov
22
and developed further by Rissanen
21
and Grünwald
11
.

Before proving the endpoint limits, we record a basic interpolation property of the entropic criterion. For each fixed codebook, the entropic codelength lies between the ordinary expected SMML codelength and the worst-case codelength, and it is monotone in the risk parameter
\tau
.

Proposition 2
(Monotonicity and endpoint bounds)

Fix
n
and an admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
, and write

\Lambda(X_{n})=\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})
.
Let

\mathcal{D}=\left\{\tau>0:\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\exp\{\tau\Lambda(X_{n})\}<\infty\right\}.

For every
\tau\in\mathcal{D}
,

\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\Lambda(X_{n})\leq\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\leq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{r_{n}}\Lambda(X_{n}),

where the lower bound is interpreted whenever
\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\Lambda(X_{n})
is well defined. Moreover, if

0<s<t
and
s,t\in\mathcal{D}
, then

\mathcal{I}_{n,s}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\leq\mathcal{I}_{n,t}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta).

Thus
\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
is nondecreasing on its moment domain.

Proof.
For brevity, write

\mathcal{I}_{\tau}=\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
.
The lower bound follows from Jensen’s inequality:

\log\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\exp\{\tau\Lambda(X_{n})\}\geq\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\log\exp\{\tau\Lambda(X_{n})\}=\tau\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\Lambda(X_{n}).

Dividing by
\tau
gives
\mathcal{I}_{\tau}\geq\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\Lambda(X_{n})
.
For the upper bound, let

M=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{r_{n}}\Lambda(X_{n})
.
If
M=\infty
, the bound is trivial. Otherwise,

\Lambda(X_{n})\leq M\;r_{n}\text{-a.s.}
and hence

\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\exp\{\tau\Lambda(X^{n})\}\leq\exp\{\tau M\}.

Therefore
\mathcal{I}_{\tau}\leq M
. It remains to prove monotonicity. Let
0<s<t
, with
s,t\in\mathcal{D}
, and set
Y=\exp\{\Lambda(X_{n})\}
. Then
Y\geq 0
, and

\mathcal{I}_{s}=\log\left(\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}Y^{s}\right)^{1/s},\qquad\mathcal{I}_{t}=\log\left(\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}Y^{t}\right)^{1/t}.

Since
r_{n}
is a probability measure,
L^{p}
-norms are nondecreasing in
p
. Hence

\left(\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}Y^{s}\right)^{1/s}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}Y^{t}\right)^{1/t}.

Taking logarithms gives
\mathcal{I}_{s}\leq\mathcal{I}_{t}
, proving monotonicity.

\square

4.1
Recovery of ordinary SMML

For optimizer-convergence statements in this section, we use the following standard convention. Either the admissible class
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is finite, or it is equipped with a topology under which the relevant objectives are continuous, their sublevel sets are compact, and the displayed pointwise convergences are uniform over
\mathcal{C}_{n}
. Under these conditions, convergence of minimizers follows from the usual argmin-continuity theorem. For purely pointwise statements about a fixed codebook, no compactness assumption on
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is required.

Theorem 2
(Recovery of ordinary SMML)

For any admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, such that
\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\!\left[\exp\{u|\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})|\}\right]<\infty
for all
u
in a neighbourhood of zero,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to 0}\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\mathcal{I}_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\!\left[\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})\right].

(23)

Moreover, under the uniform convergence and compactness convention stated above, if
(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{n,\tau},q^{*}_{n,\tau},\theta^{*}_{n,\tau})
is any entropic SMML optimizer and the ordinary SMML optimizer is unique, then

(\mathcal{P}_{n,\tau}^{*},q_{n,\tau}^{*},\theta_{n,\tau}^{*})\to(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{*},q_{n}^{*},\theta_{n}^{*})\qquad\text{as }\tau\downarrow 0.

Proof.
Fix
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
and write

\Lambda(X_{n}):=\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n}).

By definition,

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\frac{1}{\tau}\log\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\!\left[e^{\tau\Lambda(X_{n})}\right].

Expanding the logarithmic moment generating function around
\tau=0
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}[\Lambda(X_{n})]+\frac{\tau}{2}\mathrm{Var}_{r_{n}}(\Lambda(X_{n}))+O(\tau^{2}).

(24)

The leading term is the ordinary SMML objective
\mathcal{I}_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
from (
3
), which proves (
23
). Under the uniform convergence/compactness convention stated above, the convergence of minimizers under uniqueness follows from the standard argmin-continuity theorem.

\square

Theorem
2
shows that entropic SMML is a genuine risk-sensitive deformation of ordinary SMML rather than a different coding criterion. The first-order correction term in (
24
) is proportional to the codelength variance under the prior predictive law, so entropic SMML may be viewed locally as a variance-penalized perturbation of ordinary SMML. In particular, the small-
\tau
regime preserves the ordinary Bayesian coding interpretation of SMML, in which the expected two-part codelength is minimized under the prior predictive distribution.

4.2
Recovery of NML

We now turn to the opposite endpoint
\tau\to\infty
. Let

\displaystyle M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta):=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)

(25)

denote the worst-case codelength associated with a codebook.

Lemma 1
(Soft-max limit)

For any admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
such that
M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta).

(26)

Proof.
Fix
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
and write

\Lambda(x):=\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x),\qquad M_{n}:=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}\Lambda(x).

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\log\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}r_{n}(x)e^{\tau\Lambda(x)}=M_{n}+\frac{1}{\tau}\log\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}r_{n}(x)e^{\tau(\Lambda(x)-M_{n})}.

(27)

Since
(\Lambda(x)-M_{n})\leq 0
for all
x
, the second term is at most zero. Conversely, for any
\varepsilon>0
, choose
x_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{X}_{n}
such that
\Lambda(x_{\varepsilon})\geq M_{n}-\varepsilon
. Since
r_{n}(x)>0
on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
,

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}\geq M_{n}-\varepsilon+\frac{1}{\tau}\log r_{n}(x_{\varepsilon}).

Taking
\liminf_{\tau\to\infty}
, and then letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, gives the lower bound
M_{n}
. The upper bound follows from the preceding display, proving (
26
).

\square

Lemma
1
shows that entropic SMML converges pointwise to a minimax codelength objective. Specifically, the criterion chooses a SMML-style two-part codebook whose longest possible message is as short as possible. The remaining question is how this relates to the minimax
regret
criterion that defines normalized maximum likelihood.

Let
\hat{\theta}(x)
denote a maximum likelihood estimator and define the oracle ML codelength

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{ML}}(x)=-\log p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x)).

(28)

For any codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)
, define the regret relative to the ML code by

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x):

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)-\Lambda_{\mathrm{ML}}(x)=\log\frac{p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x))}{q_{j(x)}p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{j(x)})}.

(29)

With this notation,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}\bigl(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ML}}(x)+R_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)\bigr).

(30)

Equation (
30
) highlights the distinction between
worst
-
case absolute codelength and worst
-
case regret. The following theorem makes this distinction precise in the large
-
\tau
limit.

Theorem 3
(Worst-case codelength limit and regret-centered NML)

For fixed sample size
n
, let

M_{n}(P,q,\theta):=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x).

For any admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
such that

M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta).

(31)

Consequently, if
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is finite, or if the convergence in (
31
) is uniform over
\mathcal{C}_{n}
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\inf_{(P,q,\theta)\in C_{n}}\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\inf_{(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in C_{n}}M_{n}(P,q,\theta).

(32)

If the infima are attained, the same statement holds with

\inf
replaced by
\min
.
Moreover, define the regret of a coding distribution
Q
on
\mathcal{X}_{n}

by

R_{Q}(x)=-\log Q(x)+\log p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x)),

where
\hat{\theta}(x)
is a maximum likelihood estimate for
x
. Let
\mu_{n}
be any full-support probability distribution on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, and define the regret-entropic objective

\mathcal{J}_{n,\tau}(Q)=\frac{1}{\tau}\log\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}\mu_{n}(x)\exp\{\tau R_{Q}(x)\}.

For every
Q
such that
\sup_{x}R_{Q}(x)<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{n,\tau}(Q)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}R_{Q}(x).

(33)

Assume now that the Shtarkov sum

\displaystyle S_{n}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x))

(34)

is finite. By Shtarkov’s minimax-regret theorem
22
, the solution of

\inf_{Q}\sup_{x\in X^{n}}\left\{-\log Q(x)+\log p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x))\right\}

is the normalized maximum likelihood distribution

p_{\mathrm{NML}}(x)=\frac{p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x))}{S_{n}},

and the minimax regret is
\log S_{n}
. Thus the large-
\tau
limit of entropic SMML is a worst-case codelength criterion, while the corresponding regret-centered large-
\tau
limit recovers the NML minimax-regret principle.

Proof.
The pointwise convergence in (
31
) is the soft-max limit from Lemma
1
.
Uniform convergence over
\mathcal{C}_{n}
, or finiteness of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
, permits interchange of the limit and infimum, giving (
32
). If the infima are attained, the corresponding minimum statement follows. The same soft-max argument applied to
R_{Q}
gives (
33
). The
remaining statement is Shtarkov’s minimax-regret theorem for NML
22
. For completeness, the argument is as follows. If

\sup_{x}\{-\log Q(x)+\log p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x))\}\leq R,

then

Q(x)\geq e^{-R}p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x))

for all
x
. Summing over
x
gives

1\geq e^{-R}S_{n},

so
R\geq\log S_{n}
. Equality is achieved by

Q(x)=p_{n}(x\mid\hat{\theta}(x))/S_{n}.

Thus
p_{\mathrm{NML}}
is minimax optimal, with regret
\log S_{n}
.

\square

Remark 2

The distinction between
\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau}
and
\mathcal{J}_{n,\tau}
is essential. The former applies the entropic soft maximum to absolute codelengths
\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)
, and therefore converges to a worst-case absolute codelength criterion. NML, by contrast, is characterized by minimax regret relative to the oracle maximum likelihood codelength. The regret-centered objective
\mathcal{J}_{n,\tau}
makes this centering explicit.

Theorems
2
and
3
show that entropic SMML interpolates between Bayesian SMML at
\tau=0
and worst-case codelength coding as
\tau\to\infty
. The regret-centered version of the same limiting construction recovers the NML minimax-regret principle, motivating the joint
n
–
\tau
asymptotic analysis that follows. When the Shtarkov sum in (
34
) is infinite, ordinary NML is not defined. In such cases, refined MDL often replaces NML by restricted, conditional, or luckiness-weighted variants; the latter introduce a nonnegative weight on the parameter space and lead to luckiness-normalized maximum likelihood (LNML) codes
11
;
15
;
12
. This suggests a natural extension of the regret-centered entropic construction in which the ML oracle term is augmented by a luckiness penalty.

5
Joint
n
–
\tau
Asymptotics

Sections
3
and
4
show that, for fixed sample size
n
, entropic SMML interpolates between ordinary SMML and a worst-case codelength criterion as
\tau
ranges from
0
to
\infty
, with NML recovered by the corresponding regret-centered construction. We now study the corresponding asymptotics when the sample size
n
also tends to infinity.
For each
n
, the entropic criterion admits an exact KL-regularized variational representation, so the main asymptotic question is how its behavior depends jointly on the growth of
\tau_{n}
and on the scales governing typical codelength fluctuations and worst-case soft-max concentration under the prior predictive law.
Throughout this section, let
\tau=\tau_{n}>0
depend on
n
, and let

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\log\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\!\left[\exp\!\big(\tau_{n}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})\big)\right]

denote the entropic SMML objective. The theorem below is stated in terms of two auxiliary sequences,
v_{n}
and
a_{n}
, which quantify these effects. Roughly speaking,
v_{n}
controls the scale of stochastic fluctuations of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})
about its mean under
r_{n}
, while
a_{n}
controls the rate at which the entropic soft maximum concentrates on near worst-case observations. Precise definitions and assumptions are given in the appendix. In regular parametric models, existing asymptotic theory for ordinary SMML suggests that both scales grow logarithmically with
n
, but no such structure is assumed in the statement of the theorem.

Theorem 4
(Joint
n
–
\tau
asymptotics)

Let
\tau_{n}>0
be a sequence, and suppose Assumptions 1 and 2 in the appendix hold. Then the following conclusions obtain uniformly over admissible codebooks
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
.

(1)

SMML regime.

If
\tau_{n}\to 0
and
\tau_{n}v_{n}\to 0
, then

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\!\left[\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})\right]+o(1).

In particular, any sequence of entropic SMML minimizers is asymptotically ordinary-SMML-optimal. If, in addition, the ordinary SMML minimizer is unique, then the entropic minimizers converge to it.

(2)

Minimax regime.

If
a_{n}/\tau_{n}\to 0
, then

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)+o(1).

Consequently, entropic SMML converges to a worst-case codelength criterion on
\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})
. The corresponding regret-centered formulation recovers the NML principle under the standard Shtarkov finite-complexity condition and when the admissible coding class ranges over all probability assignments on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
.

The proof is deferred to the appendix. Theorem
4
provides a scale-explicit characterization of the joint
n
–
\tau
asymptotics. When
\tau_{n}\to 0
and
\tau_{n}v_{n}\to 0
, entropic SMML is asymptotically equivalent to ordinary SMML, whereas when
a_{n}/\tau_{n}\to 0
, it reduces to a worst-case codelength criterion on the prior-predictive support. At the same time, for every
n
, the criterion admits an exact KL-regularized variational representation (see Theorem
1
), so the intermediate regime is naturally interpreted as a robust coding regime rather than as a separate asymptotic approximation. In settings where both the fluctuation scale and the soft-max penalty scale are logarithmic, that is,
v_{n}=O(\log n)
and
a_{n}=O(\log n)
, the preceding bounds recover a logarithmic separation between the Bayesian average-case regime and the worst-case codelength regime; the analogous regret-centered formulation gives the corresponding minimax-regret/NML transition.

The next section specializes the theory to regular exponential families, where the fixed-partition codepoint equation and the induced cell geometry simplify substantially.

6
Regular Exponential Families

This section specializes entropic SMML to regular exponential families. In the ordinary SMML setting, codepoints in exponential families satisfy a moment-matching condition, and the exact fixed-codebook partition is the pullback of a polyhedral partition of sufficient-statistic space. These properties were established for ordinary SMML in
14
. We show here that the same affine cell structure survives under the entropic deformation, while the codepoint equation is replaced by a tilted moment-matching condition.

Assume the joint model belongs to a regular exponential family of the form

\displaystyle p_{n}(x\mid\theta)=h(x)\exp\!\big(\eta(\theta)^{\top}T(x)-nA(\eta(\theta))\big),

(35)

where
T(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a sufficient statistic,

\eta(\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the natural parameter, and
A
is the log-partition function. We write
\nu=\eta(\theta)
when it is convenient to work directly in natural-parameter coordinates. For a fixed partition cell
P_{j}
, recall from (
12
) that the entropic SMML codepoint solves

\displaystyle\theta_{j,\tau}^{*}\in\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\,p_{n}(x\mid\theta)^{-\tau}.

(36)

Substituting (
35
) into
(
36
) gives

\displaystyle p_{n}(x\mid\theta)^{-\tau}=h(x)^{-\tau}\exp\!\big(-\tau\eta(\theta)^{\top}T(x)+\tau nA(\eta(\theta))\big),

(37)

so the fixed-cell objective becomes

\displaystyle A_{j,\tau}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\exp\!\big(\tau nA(\eta(\theta))\big)\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\,h(x)^{-\tau}\exp\!\big(-\tau\eta(\theta)^{\top}T(x)\big).

(38)

To express the first-order condition cleanly, let

\displaystyle B_{j,\tau}(\nu):=\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\,h(x)^{-\tau}e^{-\tau\nu^{\top}T(x)},

(39)

so that

\displaystyle A_{j,\tau}(\nu)=e^{\tau nA(\nu)}B_{j,\tau}(\nu).

(40)

Differentiating
\log A_{j,\tau}(\nu)
with respect to
\nu
yields

\displaystyle\tau n\nabla A(\nu)-\tau\frac{\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\,h(x)^{-\tau}e^{-\tau\nu^{\top}T(x)}T(x)}{\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\,h(x)^{-\tau}e^{-\tau\nu^{\top}T(x)}}.

(41)

Setting (
41
) equal to zero gives the following result.

Proposition 3
(Tilted moment matching)

Let
\nu_{j,\tau}^{*}=\eta(\theta_{j,\tau}^{*})
. Then

\displaystyle n\,\nabla A(\nu_{j,\tau}^{*})=\sum_{x\in P_{j}}w_{j,\tau}(x;\nu_{j,\tau}^{*})\,T(x),

(42)

where

\displaystyle w_{j,\tau}(x;\nu)=\frac{r_{n}(x)\,h(x)^{-\tau}e^{-\tau\nu^{\top}T(x)}}{\sum_{y\in P_{j}}r_{n}(y)\,h(y)^{-\tau}e^{-\tau\nu^{\top}T(y)}},\qquad x\in P_{j}.

(43)

In particular, in a regular canonical exponential family, the entropic SMML codepoint is the model parameter whose mean-value parameter matches a
tilted
average of the sufficient statistic over the cell.

Proof.
Equation (
40
) implies

\log A_{j,\tau}(\nu)=\tau nA(\nu)+\log B_{j,\tau}(\nu).

Differentiating both sides with respect to
\nu
gives
(
41
). Setting the gradient equal to zero and normalizing the resulting weights yields (
42
)–(
43
).

\square

Proposition
3
is the entropic analogue of the ordinary SMML moment-matching condition in canonical exponential families. In the ordinary case, the codepoint matches the
r_{n}
-weighted average of the sufficient statistic over the cell; under the entropic deformation, the weights are exponentially tilted toward observations that are costly under the current codepoint. In the limit
\tau\downarrow 0
, the tilted weights reduce to the ordinary cellwise weights and (
42
) collapses to the classical moment-matching equation established in
14
.

Regular exponential families carry a dually flat information geometry in which KL divergence is the Bregman divergence generated by the log-partition function. In this geometry, ordinary SMML codepoints are KL/Bregman centroids of the cellwise distributions
14
. Under entropic SMML, the same geometric picture persists, but with the cellwise distribution replaced by the exponentially tilted weights (
43
). Thus the entropic codepoint is a
tilted Bregman centroid

2
that minimizes a risk-sensitive deformation of the ordinary cellwise KL projection.

Lemma 2
(Entropic SMML as a tilted
m
-projection)

Fix a partition cell
P_{j}
and consider a (possibly curved) regular exponential family
\{p_{n}(\cdot\mid\theta):\theta\in\Theta\}
. Let

\tilde{s}_{j}(x)=\frac{r_{n}(x)}{r_{n}(P_{j})},\qquad x\in P_{j},

denote the normalized cellwise predictive distribution. Then the ordinary SMML codepoint
\theta_{j}^{*}
is the
m
-projection of
\tilde{s}_{j}
onto the model manifold
14
, i.e.,

\theta_{j}^{*}=\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\tilde{s}_{j}\,\middle\|\,p_{n}(\cdot\mid\theta)\right).

For
\tau>0
, the entropic SMML codepoint
\theta_{j,\tau}^{*}
is the
m
-projection of a
\tau
-tilted distribution

s_{j,\tau}(x)\;\propto\;r_{n}(x)\,p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{j,\tau}^{*})^{-\tau},\qquad x\in P_{j},

onto the same model manifold, i.e.,

\theta_{j,\tau}^{*}=\argmin_{\theta\in\Theta}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(s_{j,\tau}\,\middle\|\,p_{n}(\cdot\mid\theta)\right).

Proof.

For the entropic criterion, the fixed-cell objective is

A_{j,\tau}(\theta)=\sum_{x\in P_{j}}r_{n}(x)\,p_{n}(x\mid\theta)^{-\tau}.

Define the tilted distribution

s_{j,\tau}(x)=\frac{r_{n}(x)\,p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{j,\tau}^{*})^{-\tau}}{\sum_{y\in P_{j}}r_{n}(y)\,p_{n}(y\mid\theta_{j,\tau}^{*})^{-\tau}},\qquad x\in P_{j}.

Up to a positive multiplicative constant independent of
\theta
,
minimizing
A_{j,\tau}(\theta)
is equivalent to minimizing

\sum_{x\in P_{j}}s_{j,\tau}(x)\,[-\log p_{n}(x\mid\theta)]=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(s_{j,\tau}\,\middle\|\,p_{n}(\cdot\mid\theta)\right)+\text{const}.

Therefore the entropic SMML codepoint
\theta_{j,\tau}^{*}
is the
minimizer of

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s_{j,\tau}\|p_{n}(\cdot\mid\theta))
, i.e., the

m
-projection of the tilted distribution
s_{j,\tau}
onto the
model manifold.

\square

Remark 3
(Amari

–
m

Lemma

admits a direct interpretation in terms of Amari’s dual affine connections

m

6.1
Affine Cell Structure

We now consider the partition geometry for a fixed codebook. By (
9
), an observation
x
is assigned to cell
j
rather than cell
\ell
whenever

\displaystyle-\log q_{j}-\log p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{j})\leq-\log q_{\ell}-\log p_{n}(x\mid\theta_{\ell}).

(44)

Substituting the exponential-family form (
35
) and canceling the common term

\displaystyle\big(\eta(\theta_{j})-\eta(\theta_{\ell})\big)^{\top}T(x)\geq\log\frac{q_{\ell}}{q_{j}}+n\Big(A(\eta(\theta_{j}))-A(\eta(\theta_{\ell}))\Big).

(45)

T(x)
.

Theorem 5
(Polyhedral cells in sufficient-statistic space)

Fix a codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
for the exponential family (
35

\displaystyle V_{j}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\ell\neq j}\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\big(\eta(\theta_{j})-\eta(\theta_{\ell})\big)^{\top}t\right.

\displaystyle\left.\geq\log\frac{q_{\ell}}{q_{j}}+n\big(A(\eta(\theta_{j}))-A(\eta(\theta_{\ell}))\big)\right\}.

(46)

Then each
V_{j}
is a convex polyhedron, and, up to ties on boundaries,

\displaystyle P_{j}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}:T(x)\in V_{j}\}.

(47)

Proof.
For fixed
j
and
\ell
, (
45
) defines a closed half-space in
t
-space. The intersection over
\ell\neq j
is therefore a convex polyhedron. The pullback characterization (
47
) follows directly from the fixed-codebook decision rule.

\square

shows that the affine cell structure of ordinary exponential-family SMML

6.2
A Binomial Example

As an illustration of the exponential family theory, consider the binomial model

\displaystyle X_{n}\mid p\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,p),\qquad p\in(0,1),

(48)

with uniform prior

\displaystyle\pi(p)=1,\qquad 0<p<1.

(49)

The data space is
\mathcal{X}_{n}=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
, and the model likelihood is

\displaystyle p_{n}(x\mid p)=\binom{n}{x}p^{x}(1-p)^{n-x},\qquad x=0,1,\dots,n.

(50)

This is a one-dimensional canonical exponential family with sufficient statistic
T(x)=x
, natural parameter

\eta(p)=\log\frac{p}{1-p},

and log-partition function

A(\eta)=\log(1+e^{\eta}).

The uniform prior yields an especially simple prior predictive distribution

\displaystyle r_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n+1},\qquad x=0,1,\dots,n.

(51)

Because the binomial model is a regular canonical exponential family, the general entropic SMML codepoint equation from Section
6
simplifies to a scalar tilted moment equation.

Proposition 4
(Binomial entropic codepoints and cell geometry)

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
be a fixed partition of
\{0,1,\dots,n\}
, and let
p_{j}\in(0,1)
denote the codepoint associated with cell
P_{j}
.

1)

The fixed-cell entropic objective is

\displaystyle A_{j,\tau}(p)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{x\in P_{j}}\binom{n}{x}^{-\tau}p^{-\tau x}(1-p)^{-\tau(n-x)}.

(52)

2)

Any entropic SMML codepoint
p_{j,\tau}^{*}
satisfies

\displaystyle n\,p_{j,\tau}^{*}=\sum_{x\in P_{j}}w_{j,\tau}(x;p_{j,\tau}^{*})\,x,

(53)

where

\displaystyle w_{j,\tau}(x;p)=\frac{\binom{n}{x}^{-\tau}\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right)^{-\tau x}}{\sum_{y\in P_{j}}\binom{n}{y}^{-\tau}\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right)^{-\tau y}},\qquad x\in P_{j}.

(54)

3)

For a fixed codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,p_{1},\dots,p_{k})
,
each cell is an interval in
\{0,1,\dots,n\}
. More precisely, the pairwise
boundary between cells
j
and
\ell
is determined by

\displaystyle x\log\frac{p_{j}(1-p_{\ell})}{p_{\ell}(1-p_{j})}\geq\log\frac{q_{\ell}}{q_{j}}+n\log\frac{1-p_{j}}{1-p_{\ell}},

(55)

which is affine in
x
.

Proof.
Part 1 follows by substituting (
50
) and (
51
) into the definition of
A_{j,\tau}
.

For Part 2, the canonical exponential family result (
42
) gives

n\,\nabla A(\eta(p_{j,\tau}^{*}))=\sum_{x\in P_{j}}w_{j,\tau}(x;\eta(p_{j,\tau}^{*}))\,x.

Since
\nabla A(\eta(p))=p
in the binomial model, this yields (
53
), and the explicit weights are exactly (
54
).

For Part 3, the fixed-codebook decision rule compares

-\log q_{j}-\log p_{n}(x\mid p_{j})\quad\text{and}\quad-\log q_{\ell}-\log p_{n}(x\mid p_{\ell}).

Substituting (
50
) and cancelling the common term
\log\binom{n}{x}
gives (
55

, each pairwise decision boundary is an interval boundary, and hence each cell is an interval in
\{0,1,\dots,n\}
.

Proposition
4
makes the general theory especially transparent. The fixed-codebook partition remains one-dimensional and affine, so the polyhedral cell structure of Section
6
reduces here to interval cells. At the same time, the entropic codepoint is the solution of the scalar fixed-point equation (
53
), which is a tilted version of the ordinary SMML cellwise mean.

The ordinary SMML limit is recovered as
\tau\downarrow 0
. Indeed, the weights in (
54
) become asymptotically uniform over each cell, so

\displaystyle p_{j,\tau}^{*}\to\frac{1}{n|P_{j}|}\sum_{x\in P_{j}}x,\qquad q_{j,\tau}^{*}\to\frac{|P_{j}|}{n+1},\qquad\tau\downarrow 0,

(56)

recovering the ordinary SMML codebook for the binomial model. Thus, in this one-dimensional setting, entropic SMML can be viewed as a continuous deformation of the classical SMML interval partition toward increasingly robust codepoints and assertion weights.

\tau

to an intermediate value and then to

, the optimized codebook shifts from minimizing expected codelength toward controlling the worst-case absolute codelength over the count space.

7
Conclusion

We introduced entropic SMML, a risk-sensitive generalization of strict minimum message length that replaces expected codelength under the prior predictive distribution with an exponential certainty equivalent. This yields a single criterion that interpolates smoothly between Bayesian average-case coding and worst-case codelength coding: ordinary SMML is recovered as
\tau\downarrow 0
, while the corresponding regret-centered limit recovers normalized maximum likelihood as
\tau\to\infty
.

The entropic formulation admits a KL-regularized variational representation and a PAC–Bayesian-type interpretation, positioning entropic SMML at the intersection of MML, PAC–Bayes, and MDL.
We established a joint
n
–
\tau
asymptotic theory showing that, when the relevant fluctuation and soft-max scales are logarithmic, the average-case SMML regime holds for
\tau_{n}\log n\to 0
, whereas the worst-case regime holds for
\tau_{n}/\log n\to\infty
. For regular exponential families, partitions remain affine in sufficient-statistic space and codepoints are tilted Bregman centroids. Information-geometrically, entropic SMML applies an
m
-projection after an exponential (
e
-geodesic) tilt, explaining the stability under curvature and the smooth recovery of ordinary SMML.

Several directions remain open, including global geometric characterizations beyond fixed codebooks, broader divergence-based deformations, and non-asymptotic finite-blocklength bounds.

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
4

This appendix states the assumptions and proof underlying Theorem
4
in a form that matches the scale-explicit formulation used in Section V. The assumptions isolate the two quantities that control the joint
n
–
\tau
asymptotics: a fluctuation scale
v_{n}
for centered codelengths and a soft-max penalty scale
a_{n}
governing concentration on worst-case observations.

Assumption 1
(Local cumulant control)

There exist a sequence
v_{n}>0
and constants
u_{0}>0
and
C_{0}<\infty
such that, for all sufficiently large
n
, every admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, and every
0\leq u\leq u_{0}
,

\log\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}\!\exp\!\Bigl(u\bigl(\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})-\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}[\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})]\bigr)\Bigr)\leq C_{0}u^{2}v_{n}.

Assumption 2
(Soft-max penalty control)

There exist sequences
a_{n}>0
and
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
such that, for all sufficiently large
n
and every admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, there exists a point
x^{\star}_{n}=x^{\star}_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})
satisfying

\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x^{\star}_{n})\geq\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)-\varepsilon_{n},\quad-\log r_{n}(x^{\star}_{n})\leq a_{n}.

Assumptions 1-2 need only hold for asymptotically optimal codebooks; uniformity over the entire admissible class is adopted for simplicity.

Proof.
Fix an admissible codebook
(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, and write

\Lambda_{n}:=\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n}),\qquad m_{n}:=\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}[\Lambda_{n}].

1) Small-risk regime.

By definition,

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\log\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}e^{\tau_{n}\Lambda_{n}}=m_{n}+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\log\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}e^{\tau_{n}(\Lambda_{n}-m_{n})}.

If
\tau_{n}\to 0
and
\tau_{n}v_{n}\to 0
, then for all sufficiently large
n
we have
0\leq\tau_{n}\leq u_{0}
, and Assumption 1 yields

\log\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}e^{\tau_{n}(\Lambda_{n}-m_{n})}\leq C_{0}\tau_{n}^{2}v_{n}.

Consequently,

0\leq\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)-m_{n}\leq C_{0}\tau_{n}v_{n}\to 0,

which proves

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{r_{n}}[\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(X_{n})]+o(1)

uniformly over admissible codebooks. Standard argmin continuity arguments then imply that any sequence of entropic SMML minimizers is asymptotically optimal for the ordinary SMML criterion; if the ordinary SMML minimizer is unique, this upgrades to convergence of the minimizers themselves.

2) Large-risk regime.

Let

M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta):=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x),

and let
x_{n}^{\star}
be as in Assumption 2. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\log\sum_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})}r_{n}(x)e^{\tau_{n}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)}

\displaystyle=M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\log\sum_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})}r_{n}(x)e^{\tau_{n}(\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)-M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta))}.

Since
\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)-M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\leq 0
for all
x
, the sum inside the logarithm is at most
1
, implying

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\leq M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta).

On the other hand, retaining only the near-maximizing point
x^{\star}_{n}
from Assumption 2 yields

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\geq M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)-\varepsilon_{n}+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\log r_{n}(x^{\star}_{n}).

By Assumption 2,
-\log r_{n}(x^{\star}_{n})\leq a_{n}
, and therefore

0\leq M_{n}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)-\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)\leq\varepsilon_{n}+\frac{a_{n}}{\tau_{n}}.

If
a_{n}/\tau_{n}\to 0
, it follows that

\mathcal{I}_{n,\tau_{n}}(\mathcal{P},q,\theta)=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(r_{n})}\Lambda_{\mathcal{P},q,\theta}(x)+o(1)

uniformly over admissible codebooks. This shows that the large-
\tau_{n}
regime reduces entropic SMML to a worst-case codelength criterion on the prior-predictive support. The corresponding regret-centered formulation recovers the NML principle under the standard Shtarkov finite-complexity condition and when the admissible coding class ranges over all probability assignments on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
.

\square
