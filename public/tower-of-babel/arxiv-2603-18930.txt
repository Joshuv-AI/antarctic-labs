Well-posedness for the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -problem relevant to the AKNS spectral problem
Junyi Zhu, Huan Liu

Abstract
The well-posedness for the Dbar problem associated with the AKNS spectral problem is considered. In general, the relevant Dbar equation with normalization condition is equivalent to an integral equation, where the kernel involves exponents e ± 2 ​ i ​ k ​ x \mathrm{e}^{\pm 2ikx} with physical variable x x as a parameter. We develop a decomposition technique to control the convergence of the integral by defining a new integral operator R ​ T ℂ ​ ( k , x ) RT_{\mathbb{C}}(k;x) . The small norm condition of the operator is obtained to show that there exists a unique solution for the Dbar problem. Moreover, the Dbar dressing method is extended to construct the AKNS spectral problem and the potential construction is presented via the Dbar data. Prior estimates are given to show that the map from the Dbar data to the AKNS potential is Lipschitz continuous. Keywords: well-posedness; Dbar problem; AKNS spectral problem; decomposition technique

In this paper, we shall develop a decomposition technique to discuss the well-posedness of the Dbar problem relevant to the AKNS spectral problem. To this end, we introduce two nilpotent matrices

\displaystyle+[\psi w_{-}T_{\mathbb{C}^{-}}(k;x)+\psi w_{+}T_{\mathbb{C}^{+}}(k;x)]\chi_{x<0},

(1.5)

Let

(1.6)

To discuss the Dbar problem relevant to the AKNS problem, one need to consider matrix functions. For the matrix

\|\phi(k)\|

\|\phi(k)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{p}}

In section 2, we present the properties of the integral on bounded domain. In section 3, we develop a decomposition technique, and define a new integral operator on

, the potential matrix
U=U(x;k)
can be regarded as an important characteristic to certain nonlinear integrable PDE. Now applying the Dbar problem

\bar{\partial}\psi(k)=\psi(k)R(k)

(3.1)

to integrable system, the spectral transformation matrix or the distribution
R(k)
will take the same role as the potential matrix
U
to character the certain nonlinear integrable PDE. It is known that the Dbar problem with normalization condition
\psi(k)\to I
as
k\to\infty
is equivalent to the Pompeiu formula

\psi(k)=I+\psi R{T}_{\mathbb{C}}(k),

(3.2)

where the operator
{T}_{\mathbb{C}}
acting to the left is defined in the following form

\psi R{T}_{\mathbb{C}}(k)=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\psi(z)R(z)}{z-k}\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}.

(3.3)

To discuss the AKNS spectral problem by the Dbar problem, one need to introduce the physical variable
x
into
R(k)
as following

\partial_{x}R=-ik[\sigma_{3},R],\quad\sigma_{3}={\rm diag}(1,-1),

(3.4)

which implies that

R(k;x)=\mathrm{e}^{-ikx\hat{\sigma}_{3}}r(k)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&r_{+}(k)\mathrm{e}^{-2ikx}\\
r_{-}(k)\mathrm{e}^{2ikx}&0\end{matrix}\right),

(3.5)

where the off-matrix function
r(k)
or
r_{\pm}(k)
are independent of
x
,
[\sigma_{3},A]=\sigma_{3}A-A\sigma_{3}
and
\mathrm{e}^{\alpha\hat{\sigma}_{3}}A=\mathrm{e}^{\alpha\sigma_{3}}A\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha\sigma_{3}}
for some
2\times 2
matrix
A
.
Thus the eigenvalue function
\psi
in (
3.1
) will dependent on the physical variable
x
, that is
\psi=\psi(k;x)
. It is emphasized that, substituting
R(k;x)
into the integral equation (
3.2
) directly, it may lead to uncertainty.
Since the exponentials
\mathrm{e}^{\pm 2ikx}
with
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{C}
are not bounded for
x{\rm Im}k\lessgtr 0
,
the function
\psi(\cdot,x)R(\cdot,x)\hat{T}_{\mathbb{C}}(k)
may not convergent in view of the integral (
3.2
).

To control the spectral transformation matrix
R(k;x)
in (
3.5
), we split it into two nilpotent matrices
w_{\pm}(k;x)
, that is
R=w_{-}+w_{+}
, where

w_{-}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0\\
r_{-}(k)\mathrm{e}^{2ikx}&0\end{matrix}\right),\quad w_{+}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&r_{+}(k)\mathrm{e}^{-2ikx}\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right).

(3.6)

Then we define a new integral operator
RT_{\mathbb{C}}(k;x)
by giving the following decomposition

\displaystyle\psi RT_{\mathbb{C}}(k;x):=

\displaystyle[\psi w_{-}T_{\mathbb{C}^{+}}(k;x)+\psi w_{+}T_{\mathbb{C}^{-}}(k;x)]\chi_{x>0}

(3.7)

\displaystyle+[\psi w_{-}T_{\mathbb{C}^{-}}(k;x)+\psi w_{+}T_{\mathbb{C}^{+}}(k;x)]\chi_{x<0},

where
\chi
is the indicator function of the half lines
x\gtrless 0
.

Theorem 5

k\psi w_{\pm}T_{\mathbb{C}^{\pm}}(k;x)\chi_{x<0}=k[\psi w_{\pm}T_{\mathbb{C}^{\pm}}(k;x)\chi_{x>0}]+\langle\psi w_{\pm}\rangle_{\pm},

then we obtain that

k\psi RT_{\mathbb{C}}=k[\psi RT_{\mathbb{C}}]+\langle\psi R\rangle,

which can be reduced to

k\psi RT_{\mathbb{C}}=k\psi-kI+\langle\psi R\rangle,

(4.8)

in terms of (
4.1
).

Substituting (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) into (
4.6
), and using (
4.2
), we can get that

\partial_{x}\psi=ik\psi\sigma_{3}+i\langle\psi R\rangle\sigma_{3}\psi-i\sigma_{3}k(I-RC_{k})^{-1}.

(4.9)

In addition, from (
4.8
) and (
4.2
), we obtain that

k(I-RC_{k})^{-1}=k\psi+\langle\psi R\rangle(I-RC_{k})^{-1}=k\psi+\langle\psi R\rangle\psi.

It is remarked that
\langle\psi R\rangle
is independent of
k
, so the last term can be obtained via (
4.2
).
The proof of the theorem is finished.
\square

Lemma 4

Let
r_{\pm}\in L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})
and
\psi_{11},\psi_{22}\in L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))
, where
\psi=(\psi_{mn})_{2\times 2}
. Let

\langle\psi_{11}r_{+}\rangle_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint_{\mathbb{C}^{\pm}}\psi_{11}(k;x)r_{+}(k)\mathrm{e}^{-2ikx}\mathrm{d}k\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{k}~\chi_{x\lessgtr 0},

\langle\psi_{22}r_{-}\rangle_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint_{\mathbb{C}^{\pm}}\psi_{22}(k;x)r_{-}(k)\mathrm{e}^{2ikx}\mathrm{d}k\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{k}~\chi_{x\gtrless 0},

then

|\langle\psi_{11}r_{+}\rangle_{\pm}|\leq M_{\pm,12}^{p,q}\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}\|\psi_{11}\|_{L_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}^{\pm})}\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C}^{\pm})},

(4.10)

|\langle\psi_{22}r_{-}\rangle_{\pm}|\leq M_{\pm,13}^{p,q}\sup\limits_{x\gtrless 0}\|\psi_{22}\|_{L_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}^{\pm})}\|r_{-}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C}^{\pm})},

(4.11)

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof
. We only give the proof of the condition (
4.10
). To this end, we give the following decomposition

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{11}r_{+}\rangle_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint_{E_{1}^{\pm}}\psi_{11}(k;x)r_{+}(k)\mathrm{e}^{-2ikx}\mathrm{d}k\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{k}~\chi_{x\lessgtr 0}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint_{E_{2}^{\pm}}\psi_{11}(k;x)r_{+}(k)\mathrm{e}^{-2ikx}\mathrm{d}k\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{k}~\chi_{x\lessgtr 0}

\displaystyle=A_{1}^{\pm}+A_{2}^{\pm}.

Then using the Hölder inequality, we find that

\displaystyle|A_{1}^{\pm}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{E_{1}^{\pm}}\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}|\psi_{11}(k;x)||r_{+}(k)|\mathrm{d}\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\iint_{E_{1}^{\pm}}(\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}|\psi_{11}(k;x)||r_{+}(k)|)^{\mu}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu}}\left(\iint_{E_{1}^{\pm}}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}(\frac{\pi}{2})^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}\|\psi_{11}(k;x)\|_{L_{k}^{p}(E_{1}^{\pm})}\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(E_{1}^{\pm})},

where
\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{1}{\nu}=1,\mu>2
and
\frac{1}{\mu}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. In addition, for
A_{2}^{\pm}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint_{E_{2}^{\pm}}\psi_{11}(k;x)r_{+}(k)\mathrm{e}^{-2ikx}\mathrm{d}k\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{k}~\chi_{x\lessgtr 0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint_{E_{1}^{\mp}}\psi_{11}(k^{-1};x)r_{+}(k^{-1})\mathrm{e}^{-2ik^{-1}x}k^{-2}\bar{k}^{-2}\mathrm{d}k\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{k}~\chi_{x\lessgtr 0}.

Similarly, we obtain that

\displaystyle|A_{2}^{\pm}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{E_{1}^{\mp}}\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}|\psi_{(0),11}(k;x)||r_{(2),+}(k)||k|^{-2}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\iint_{E_{1}^{\mp}}\big(\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}|\psi_{(0),11}(k;x)||r_{(2),+}(k)|\big)^{\mu}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\left(\iint_{E_{1}^{\mp}}|k|^{-2\nu}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}},

where
\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{1}{\nu}=1,\mu>2
, then
0<\nu<1
.
Since

\iint_{E_{1}^{\mp}}|k|^{-2\nu}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{k}=\pi\int_{0}^{1}r^{1-2\nu}\mathrm{d}r=\frac{\pi}{2-2\nu},

then

|A_{2}^{\pm}|\leq\frac{1}{2-2\nu}\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}\|\psi_{(0),11}(k;x)\|_{L_{k}^{p}(E_{1}^{\mp})}\|r_{(2),+}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(E_{1}^{\mp})}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle|\langle\psi_{11}r_{+}\rangle_{\pm}|

\displaystyle\leq M_{12}^{p,q}(\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}\|\psi_{11}(k;x)\|_{L_{k}^{p}(E_{1}^{\pm})}\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(E_{1}^{\pm})}

\displaystyle+\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}\|\psi_{(0),11}\|_{L_{k}^{p}(E_{1}^{\mp})}\|r_{(2),+}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(E_{1}^{\mp})})

\displaystyle\leq M_{12}^{p,q}(\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}\|\psi_{11}\|_{L_{k}^{p}(E_{1}^{\pm})}+\sup\limits_{x\lessgtr 0}\|\psi_{(0),11}\|_{L_{k}^{p}(E_{1}^{\mp})})

\displaystyle\times(\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(E_{1}^{\pm})}+\|r_{(2),+}\|_{L_{k}^{q}(E_{1}^{\mp})})),

which proves the condition (
4.10
). The condition (
4.11
) can be proved similarly.
\square

Corollary 2

The map

L^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})\ni r_{\pm}(k)\to u(x),v(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

is Lipschitz continuous.

Proof
. Let
\tilde{r}_{\pm}(k)
and
\tilde{\psi}(k;x)=(\tilde{\psi}_{mn})
are relevant to the potential
\tilde{u}(x),\tilde{v}(x)
admitting the relation (
4.4
), and
\tilde{r}_{\pm}(k)
,
\tilde{\psi}(k;x)=(\tilde{\psi}_{mn})

4.2
), then

\displaystyle u-\tilde{u}=

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{11}(r_{+}-\tilde{r}_{+})\rangle_{+}+\langle(\psi_{11}-\tilde{\psi}_{11})\tilde{r}_{+}\rangle_{+}

\displaystyle+\langle\psi_{11}(r_{+}-\tilde{r}_{+})\rangle_{-}+\langle(\psi_{11}-\tilde{\psi}_{11})\tilde{r}_{+}\rangle_{-}.

Using (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we can get

\displaystyle|u-\tilde{u}|\leq M_{14}^{p,q}\big[\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\|\psi_{11}\|_{L_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C})}\|r_{+}-\tilde{r}_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}

\displaystyle=I(\mathcal{I}-RT_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}~(R-\tilde{R})T_{\mathbb{C}}~(\mathcal{I}-\tilde{R}T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}.

5
Conclusions and Discussions

We presented properties for the integral operator
\hat{T}_{G}
associated with the Dbar equation
\bar{\partial}\phi(k)=f(k)
on the bounded domain
G
, and showed that
\hat{T}_{G}
was a completely continuous operator on the space
L^{p}(\bar{G}),p>2
and mapped it into the space
H^{\gamma}(\bar{G}),\gamma=\frac{p-2}{p}
. Since the relevant constants were dependent on the radius of the domain, so it was difficult to let the operator was small norm, which implied that the condition for the exist of the inverse operator
(I-\hat{T}_{G})^{-1}
was difficult to achieve.

To apply the Dbar problem to integrable system, we considered the matrix Dbar problem
\bar{\partial}\psi(k)=\psi(k)R(k)
on the complex
k
plane
\mathbb{C}
, and let the spectral transformation off-diagonal matrix
R
admitting the linear evolution equation about the physical variable
x
as
\partial_{x}R(k;x)=-ik[\sigma_{3},R(k;x)]
. The Dbar problem with normalization condition was equivalent to the integral equation
\psi(k;x)=I+\psi R{T}_{\mathbb{C}}(k;x)
, where the kernel contained the exponential factors
\mathrm{e}^{\pm 2ikx}
.

To control the convergence of the integral, we split the spectral transformation function
R
into two nilpotent matrices
w_{\pm}(k;x)
containing the factors
r_{\pm}(k)\mathrm{e}^{\mp 2ikx}
, and the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
into lower and upper half planes
\mathbb{C}^{\pm}
. So the integral
\psi R{T}_{\mathbb{C}}
was decomposed into the sum of the factors
\psi w_{\mp}T_{\mathbb{C}^{\pm}}(k;x)\chi_{x>0}
and
\psi w_{\pm}T_{\mathbb{C}^{\pm}}(k;x)\chi_{x<0}
.

We further split the lower and upper half planes
\mathbb{C}^{\pm}
into lower/upper inner half unit domain
E_{1}^{\pm}
and lower/upper outer half unit domain
E_{2}^{\pm}
. By introducing the space
L^{p,\nu}(\mathbb{C}^{\pm})
, we gave the prior estimates for the decomposed integrals
\psi w_{\mp}T_{\mathbb{C}^{\pm}}(k;x)\chi_{x>0}
and
\psi w_{\pm}T_{\mathbb{C}^{\pm}}(k;x)\chi_{x<0}
, and showed that if
\psi\in{L}_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{L}_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))
,
r_{\pm}\in L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})
, and
\psi w_{\pm}\in{L}_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{L}_{k}^{\mu}(E_{1}))
as well as
\psi_{(0)}w_{(2),\pm}\in{L}_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{L}_{k}^{\mu}(E_{1}))
,
\mu>2
, where
E_{1}=\{k:|k|\leq 1\}
. Then
\psi RT_{\mathbb{C}}(k;x)\in L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}))
is Hölder continuous in the
k
plane. We found that small norm condition for the operator
RT_{\mathbb{C}}
, and showed that the inverse operator
(\mathcal{I}-RT_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}
was exist.

In this paper, we consider the no soliton case, that is no Dirac delta factors in the spectral transformation
R
. We showed that, if
r_{\pm}\in L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})
, there exists a unique solution
\psi=I(\mathcal{I}-RT_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}\in{L}_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{L}_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))
for the Dbar problem.
By using the Dbar dressing method, we constructed the AKNS spectral problem
\partial_{x}\psi=-ik[\sigma_{3},\psi]+Q\psi
,
and established the potential reconstruction
Q=-i[\sigma_{3},\langle\psi R\rangle]
. By decomposing
\langle\psi R\rangle
into
\langle\psi w_{\mp}\rangle_{\pm}
and
\langle\psi w_{\pm}\rangle_{\pm}
, we showed that the map from
r_{\pm}\in L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})
to
u,v\in L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is Lipschitz continuous.

It is remarked that, in the direct scattering analysis for the AKNS spectral problem, the potentials
u,v
are usually be taken as
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
functions. So, for the this condition, we find from (
4.13
) that

\displaystyle\|u(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M_{14}^{p,q}\|\psi_{11}\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))}\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})},

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|v(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M_{15}^{p,q}\|\psi_{22}\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))}\|r_{-}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}.

Moreover, if
\psi\in L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{L}_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))
, then we can get, from (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), that

\|\psi RT_{\mathbb{C}}(k;x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M_{10}^{p,q}(\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}+\|r_{-}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})})\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))},

(5.2)

and for
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\|\psi RT_{\mathbb{C}}(k_{1};x)-\psi RT_{\mathbb{C}}(k_{2};x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq M_{11}^{p,q}(\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}+\|r_{-}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})})\|\psi\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},L_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))}\|k_{1}-k_{2}|^{\alpha},

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{2}
, and
\alpha=1-2\big(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\big)
. So the conditions (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) imply that the operator
RT_{\mathbb{C}}
is also a map from space
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{L}_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))
to
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}))
, and

\|RT_{\mathbb{C}}(k;x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{L}_{k}^{p,0}(\mathbb{C}))\to L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}))}\leq M^{p,q}(\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}+\|r_{-}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}).

(5.4)

Under the condition of small norm, the inverse operator
(\mathcal{I}-RT_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}
is also exist, and it can be used to define the eigenfunction
\psi
in (
4.2
). We similar have

\displaystyle\|u(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{M_{14}^{p,q}\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}}{1-M_{16}^{p,q}(\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}+\|r_{-}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})})},

\displaystyle\|v(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{M_{15}^{p,q}\|r_{-}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}}{1-M_{16}^{p,q}(\|r_{+}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})}+\|r_{-}\|_{L_{k}^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})})},

which imply that the map

L^{q,2}(\mathbb{C})\ni r_{\pm}(k)\to u(x),v(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

is Lipschitz continuous.

It is also remarked that, the paper only considered the well-posedness for the AKNS spectral problem. If consider the Dbar problem associated with a nonlinear integrable equation, one needs to introduce a time evolution equation for the spectral transformation function
R
, for example
\partial_{t}R=A(k)[\sigma_{3},R]
, where
A(k)
is a polynomial.
So the exponents will take the form
e^{\pm 2i\theta(k;x,t)}
. To ensure the convergence of the relevant integral, one needs to give a more complicated decomposition about the domain.
These cases will be considered in our subsequent articles.
