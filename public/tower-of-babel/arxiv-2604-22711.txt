Asymptotic behaviour of analytic torsion and cohomological torsion for ℚ \mathbb{Q} -rank 1 1 arithmetic groups
Tim Berland

Abstract




Abstract.

We extend the refined asymptotics of analytic torsion associated to congruence subgroups of
\operatorname{SL}(n)
in
[
12
]
, to congruence subgroups in a large family of reductive groups. This is applied to give new asymptotics and bounds on the growth of torsion in the cohomology of congruence subgroups of
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{F})
for
F
a number field, and of congruence subgroups in
\operatorname{SO}(n,1)
with
n
odd.

1.
Introduction

For more than a decade, analytic torsion has been a valuable tool in studying growth of torsion in the cohomology of arithmetic groups. In the seminal paper of Bergeron and Venkatesh
[
11
]
, they use analytic torsion to establish exponential growth for an alternating sum of torsion cohomology with certain non-trivial coefficient systems of families of cocompact arithmetic groups. Below we introduce their conjectures and results before moving on to more recent generalizations.

1.1.
Conjectures and the cocompact case

Let
G
be a connected semisimple algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
\Gamma\subseteq G(\mathbb{Q})
be an arithmetic subgroup. A good example to keep in mind is
G=\operatorname{SL}(n)
and
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
. Let
M
be a
\Gamma
-module arising from a rational finite-dimensional representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
. Then the group cohomology
H^{*}(\Gamma,M)
has long been understood to have number theoretic significance, such as its link to automorphic forms by the work of Franke
[
20
]
. If one now considers a lattice
L\subseteq M
stabilized by
\Gamma
, one can also consider the cohomology groups
H^{*}(\Gamma,L)
. These are finitely generated abelian groups, and these, too, carry number theoretic information. In particular, it was conjectured by Ash
[
9
]
, and later partially proven by Scholze
[
42
]
, that there is a correspondence between Hecke eigensystems in
H^{*}(\Gamma,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and certain Galois representations
\rho:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{GL}(n,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
.

Investigating this cohomology, Bergeron–Venkatesh
[
11
]
gave the following heuristic. Let
K\subseteq G(\mathbb{R})
be a maximal compact subgroup, and define the
deficiency
of
G
as
\delta(G)\coloneqq\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}G(\mathbb{R})-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}K
. Then there is "a lot" of torsion in
H^{i}(\Gamma,L)
when
\delta(G)=1
and
i
is the middle dimension, and "little" torsion otherwise. To make this precise, they make the following conjecture, generalized in
[
8
]
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Bergeron–Venkatesh)
.

Let
\Gamma\eqqcolon\Gamma_{0}\supset\Gamma_{1}\supset\dots\supset\Gamma_{N}\supset\dots
be a sequence of congruence subgroups satisfying
\bigcap_{N}\Gamma_{N}=\{0\}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log|H^{i}(\Gamma_{N},L)_{\text{tors}}|}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{N}]}

exists for any
i
, and is equal to a computable constant
c_{\Gamma,L}
. Furthermore,
c_{\Gamma,L}
is non-zero exactly if
\delta(G)=1
and
i
is the top of the cuspidal range.

In the paper they prove an Euler-characteristic variant of this (
[
11
]
,
(1.4.2)
). Let
X=G(\mathbb{R})/K
. Then, under further assumptions, they show that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+\frac{\operatorname{dim}X-1}{2}}\frac{\log|H^{i}(\Gamma_{N},L)_{\text{tors}}|}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{N}]}=c_{\Gamma,L}.

(1.1)

Their assumptions include that
\Gamma
is cocompact in
G(\mathbb{R})
, equivalently that
G
has
\mathbb{Q}
-rank
0
, and that
L
must be a
strongly acyclic

\Gamma
-module. We will return to these assumptions in a moment.

It is striking how significant the assumption that
\delta(G)=1
seems to be, and how precise the conjecture and result are when it is satisfied. On the other hand, they both say very little about what happens when
\delta(G)\neq 1
, except that there is not full exponential growth of torsion. In fact, as phrased in (
[
8
]
, §
9
), it is still an open question whether one should expect polynomial or subexponential growth in these cases. Inspired by this question, this paper establishes subexponential upper bounds on the size of torsion in the cohomology of certain families of subgroups. On the way we also establish results on second order terms when
\delta(G)=1
. The results are obtained for non-cocompact subgroups, and we introduce this generalization now.

1.2.
Generalizations and results on analytic torsion

Since many important arithmetic subgroups are not cocompact, a lot of consequent work has focused on removing this assumption from (
1.1
). This was first successful for arithmetic groups associated to hyperbolic manifolds of finite volume, see
[
38
]
,
[
39
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
33
]
, and it was recently extended to other
\mathbb{Q}
-rank
1
arithmetic groups in
[
34
]
.

All of the results quoted above are consequences of approximation theorems on analytic torsion, combined with
Cheeger-Müller theorems
, identities relating analytic torsion to Reidemeister torsion (see
[
35
]
,
[
14
]
, also
[
36
]
,
[
13
]
). Analytic torsion and Reidemeister torsion are both invariants of Riemannian manifolds, the former built from the spectra of
p
-form Laplacians (see Section
4
), and the latter from topological data of the manifold (
[
41
]
, also
[
14
]
). The consequence of Cheeger-Müller theorems is that one may investigate cohomology by analytic methods.

At the time of the Bergeron–Venkatesh paper, there was no general definition of analytic torsion outside of compact Riemannian manifolds. To remedy this, Matz–Müller gave a definition and the necessary convergence results in
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
to show that a non-cocompact generalization of analytic torsion also satisfies the desired approximation theorem, namely (
[
27
]
, Theorem
1.5
).

Our method of getting improved bounds and second order terms on torsion cohomology is sharpening the asymptotics of their approximation theorem. This is the main technical result of this paper, and its preparation and proof will occupy most of the paper. Let us present it here. We adopt the setting from the beginning of the previous section. For simplicity, we keep the assumption that
G
is semisimple, and present a more general result later (Theorem
9.2
). We will further assume that
\Gamma_{N}=\Gamma(N)
, the principal congruence subgroup of level
N
in
\Gamma
. Assume for convenience that
G
has strong approximation. Set
\tilde{X}=G(\mathbb{R})/K
and define

\displaystyle X(N)=\Gamma(N)\backslash\tilde{X}.

For
N
large enough, this is then a locally symmetric manifold. Let
d=\operatorname{dim}\tilde{X}
. For
\tau
a finite-dimensional complex representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
we then set
T_{X(N)}(\tau)
to be the analytic torsion of
X(N)
associated to
\tau
, defined in Section
4
. We also denote by
T_{X(1)}^{(2)}(\tau)
the
L^{2}
-torsion of
X(1)=\Gamma\backslash\tilde{X}
associated to
\tau
(
[
22
]
).

We will need the notion of a
\lambda
-strongly acyclic representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
given in Definition
3.1
, first introduced in
[
12
]
. The main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\tau
be an irreducible and
\lambda
-strongly acyclic representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
, for some
\lambda>0
only depending on
G
. Assume that
G
satisfies the properties
(TWN)
and
(BD)
(as defined in
[
16
]
). Assume either Conjecture
9.1
or that
N
varies over a set with fixed prime divisors (such as
N=p^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
). Then there exists some
a>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\log T_{X(N)}(\tau)}{[\Gamma:\Gamma(N)]}=T^{(2)}_{X(1)}(\tau)+O\left(\frac{(\log N)^{a}}{N^{k(G)}}\right)\qquad\text{as}\;\;\;N\to\infty.

Here
k(G)
is a positive computable integer depending on
G
defined in (
8.5
).

We refer to Theorem
9.2
for the more general result.

Conjecture
9.1
concerns the size of certain coefficients appearing in the Arthur trace formula. It is believed to hold for all reductive groups, and has been established for
\operatorname{GL}(n)
over number fields in
[
28
]
. We note also that the properties (TWN) and (BD) hold quasi-split classical groups (see
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
), in particular for
\operatorname{GL}(n)
and
\operatorname{SL}(n)
, see also the discussion in Section
9
.

We have that
k(\operatorname{SL}(n))=n-1
when
\operatorname{SL}(n)
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, as discussed in Section
8
, and we also compute
k(G)
for certain classes of
G
in Sections
10
and
11
.

The theorem directly generalizes Theorem
1.1
of
[
12
]
. The present proof builds on the work of Matz–Müller
[
27
]
, who also establishes the same result with the worse error term
o(1)
under slightly different assumptions.

This fits into the more general framework of approximation theorems for
L^{2}
-invariants. We will not pursue this perspective, but refer to
[
23
]
for an insightful survey.

1.3.
Results on torsion cohomology

To apply the strengthened approximation theorem above to cohomology, the missing ingredient is a non-compact Cheeger-Müller theorem. The current state of the art in this respect is presented in
[
34
]
and
[
32
]
, respectively for arithmetic subgroups in
\operatorname{SL}(2,F)
for
F
a number field, and arithmetic subgroups in
\operatorname{SO}(n,1)
with
n
odd. These will be the avenues in which we may get results on cohomological torsion.

We adopt first the setting of
[
34
]
. Let
F
be a number field of degree
n=r_{1}+2r_{2}
, where
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are the number of real, respectively pairs of complex embeddings of
F
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
set
\Gamma(N)
the principal congruence subgroup of level
N
in
\Gamma\coloneqq\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{F})
. For technical reasons, let
G_{0}=\operatorname{SL}(2)
defined over
F
, and set
G=\text{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}G_{0}
to be its restriction of scalars to
\mathbb{Q}
. We now apply the notation of the previous sections to this setup, in particular the definition of
X(N)
. This is known to satisfy (TWN) and (BD). Let
\rho
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-rational finite-dimensional representation of
G
. Let
\rho_{\infty}
be the induced representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
, and let
L_{\rho}
be the associated
\Gamma
-module.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
r_{2}
is odd,
r_{1}>0
and
r_{1}+r_{2}>2
. Assume further that
\rho_{\infty}
decomposes into a sum of
\lambda
-strongly acyclic representations, with
\lambda>0
chosen as in Theorem
1.2
, and that
L_{\rho}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is acyclic. Then there exists a contant
a>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{\log|H^{i}(\Gamma(N),L_{\rho})|}{[\Gamma:\Gamma(N)]}=\log T^{(2)}_{X(1)}(\rho_{\infty})+O\left(\frac{(\log N)^{a}}{N^{n}}\right)

as
N
tends to infinity.

For
r_{2}>1
we have that
\delta(G)>1
, and hence we know that
\log T^{(2)}_{X(N)}(\rho_{\infty})=0
. This allows us to give a proper subexponential bound on the growth of torsion.

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume further that
r_{2}>1
. Then there exists
a>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{\log|H^{i}(\Gamma(N),L_{\rho})|}{[\Gamma:\Gamma(N)]}=O\left(\frac{(\log N)^{a}}{N^{n}}\right)

as
N
tends to infinity.

See Section
10
for both of these results.

For our second application, we adopt the setting of
[
32
]
and
[
33
]
. Let
n
be odd,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
G
be the algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
defined by the quadratic form

\displaystyle q(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1})=a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\dots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}-x_{n+1}^{2},

such that
G(\mathbb{R})\cong\operatorname{SO}(n,1)
. We pick
K
a maximal compact subgroup, which is then isomorphic to
\operatorname{SO}(n)
. Let
\Gamma=G(\mathbb{Z})
, and for
N\in\mathbb{N}
define the principal congruence subgroup

\displaystyle\Gamma(N)\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}(G(\mathbb{Z})\to G(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})).

We again use the setup above concerning
X(N)
and
\rho
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\rho
be such that
\rho_{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\lambda
-strongly acyclic representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
with
\lambda
as in
1.2
, and that
L_{\rho}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is acyclic. Assume (TWN) and (BD) holds for
G
, and assume either Conjecture
9.1
or that
N
varies over a set with fixed prime divisors. Then there exists
a>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{q+1}\frac{\log|H^{i}(\Gamma(N),L_{\rho})|}{[\Gamma:\Gamma(N)]}=T^{(2)}_{X(1)}(\rho_{\mathbb{R}})+O\left(\frac{(\log N)^{a}}{N^{k(G)}}\right).

Furthermore,
k(G)=n-2
when
n\geq 5
, and when
n=3
we have
k(G)=2
.

When
n=3
, we are able to give a more precise result with less assumptions.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
n=3
, such that
G(\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{SO}(3,1)
. Let
\rho
be such that
\rho_{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\lambda
-strongly acyclic representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
with
\lambda
as in
1.2
, and that
L_{\rho}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is acyclic. Then there exists
a>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\log|H^{2}(\Gamma(N),L_{\rho})|}{[\Gamma:\Gamma(N)]}=-2\cdot T^{(2)}_{X(1)}(\rho_{\mathbb{R}})+O\left(\frac{(\log N)^{a}}{N^{2}}\right).

See Section
11
for the slightly more precise results and their proofs.

1.4.
Outline of the paper

Throughout, the paper will differ from the introduction in that it heavily leans on adelic setup and notation, and that the cohomological results are stated for classifying spaces of the congruence subgroups rather than the subgroups themselves.

The paper is split into two parts. Part A is the longer and more technical part, heavily featuring analysis of the Arthur-Selberg trace formula. It gives the setup, preliminary results, and proof of Theorem
1.2
. Part B, consisting of two sections, contains the applications to cohomological torsion; Section
10
is a proof of Theorem
1.3
and its corollary, and Section
11
is a proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We give a more in-depth outline of Part A. In Section
2
, we define the adelic locally symmetric spaces with which we will work, decompose them into locally symmetric manifolds, and define the heat kernel. Section
3
then gives upper bounds on the heat kernel, needed for convergence as well as vanishing of certain error terms. Analytic torsion is defined in Section
4
using the Arthur-Selberg trace formula, and the trace formula is reviewed in Section
5
. In Section
6
, we show that we need only worry about the unipotent contribution to the trace formula. Section
7
and
8
first makes explicit, and then bounds, the local orbital integrals that constitute the unipotent contribution. Finally, everything is collected and the proof of Theorem
1.2
is given in Section
9
.

Part A
Asymptotics of analytic torsion

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
. We write
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}
for the ring of adeles of
\mathbb{Q}
, and
\mathbb{A}_{f}
for the ring of finite adeles. Let
K_{f}\subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be any open compact subgroup, and let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup in
G(\mathbb{R})
with associated Cartan involution
\theta
on
G(\mathbb{R})
. We denote by
A_{G}
the
\mathbb{Q}
-split component of the center of
G
, giving us a factorization
G(\mathbb{A})=G(\mathbb{A})^{1}\times A_{G}(\mathbb{A})^{0}
. To
K_{f}
we associate the arithmetic manifold

\displaystyle X(K_{f})\coloneqq A_{G}(\mathbb{R})^{0}G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/(K\times K_{f}),

(2.1)

We give another, more geometric description of
X(K_{f})
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
be representatives of the finite double coset space

\displaystyle A_{G}(\mathbb{R})^{0}G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/G(\mathbb{R})K_{f}.

Then we may define arithmetic groups

\displaystyle\Gamma_{j}\coloneqq(G(\mathbb{R})\times x_{j}K_{f}x_{j}^{-1})\cap G(\mathbb{Q}),\quad j=1,\dots,m

(2.2)

in
G(\mathbb{Q})
. Now define the semisimple Lie group
G(\mathbb{R})^{1}
by the factorization
G(\mathbb{R})=G(\mathbb{R})^{1}\times A_{G}(\mathbb{R})^{0}
, and set
\tilde{X}=G(\mathbb{R})^{1}/K
. We have a natural action of
G(\mathbb{R})
on the double coset space above, which induces a decomposition of
X(K_{f})
into disjoint spaces

\displaystyle X(K_{f})=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma_{j}\backslash\tilde{X}.

One calls
K_{f}

neat
if the eigenvalues of any element in
\Gamma_{j}
for all

generate a torsion free subgroup of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. This property guarantees that the spaces
\Gamma_{j}\backslash\tilde{X}
are all locally symmetric manifolds of finite volume. From now on we assume that
K_{f}
is neat, so that we may descend to the components
\Gamma_{j}\backslash\tilde{X}
, and hence we may make the simplifying assumption, when discussing the differential geometry of

, that
G
is connected and semisimple, reducing us to a single component. One then gets the general picture by gluing.

Assume now that
G
is a connected semisimple algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
. We preserve the notation above, and in particular
\tilde{X}=G(\mathbb{R})/K
and
X(K_{f})=\Gamma\backslash\tilde{X}

\Gamma=(G(\mathbb{R})\times K_{f})\cap G(\mathbb{Q})
. To a finite-dimensional unitary representation of

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\nu}

defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{E_{\nu}}\coloneqq G(\mathbb{R})\times_{\nu}V_{\nu}.

The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\nu}

G(\mathbb{R})
-invariant metric
\tilde{h}_{\nu}
on this vector bundle. Going to the quotient, let

\tilde{h}_{\nu}
.

We consider the

\qquad\qquad C^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{R}),\nu)\coloneqq\{f:G(\mathbb{R})\to V_{\nu}\mid f\in C^{\infty},\\
f(gk)=\nu(k)^{-1}f(g)\>\forall k\in K,g\in G(\mathbb{R})\}.\qquad\qquad\quad\>

(2.3)

This space is a model of the space of smooth sections of
\tilde{E}_{\nu}
, which we denote

. Indeed, by (

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(\tilde{X},\tilde{E}_{\nu})\xrightarrow{\sim}C^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{R}),\nu),

Let now

be an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of

, and let

We use the above setup to understand differential forms. Consider the vector bundle
\Lambda^{p}T^{*}(X(K_{f}))\otimes F_{\tau}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{p}(X(K_{f}),F_{\tau})\coloneqq C^{\infty}(X(K_{f}),\Lambda^{p}T^{*}(X(K_{f}))\otimes F_{\tau})

\displaystyle H_{t}^{\tau,p}(k^{-1}gk^{\prime})=\nu_{\tau,p}(k)^{-1}\circ H_{t}^{\tau,p}(g)\circ\nu_{\tau,p}(k^{\prime}),\quad\forall k,k^{\prime}\in K,\>g\in G(\mathbb{R}).

\displaystyle h_{t}^{\tau,p}(g)\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}H_{t}^{\tau,p}(g)

3.
Heat kernel estimates

In this section, we present the necessary bounds and asymptotics for the trace of the heat kernel. We preserve the assumption that
G
is semisimple and connected, and continue in the notation of the previous section. In particular,
(\tau,V_{\tau})
is a finite-dimensional irreducible representation of
G(\mathbb{R})
. Consider
\tilde{\Delta}_{p}(\tau)
as an operator on
C^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{R}),\nu_{\tau,p})
. It follows from Kuga’s lemma that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{p}(\tau)=\tau(\Omega)-R(\Omega),

(3.1)

where
\Omega
is the Casimir element of
G(\mathbb{R})
and
R
the action from the right of
G(\mathbb{R})
on
C^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{R}),\nu_{\tau,p})
. Combining this with the Harish-Chandra Plancherel formula, one is able to show exponential decay of
h_{t}^{\tau,p}
, as done in (
[
12
]
, Section
4
). The constant appearing in the exponent of the bound is
\tau(\Omega)-\pi(\Omega)
for
\pi

Definition 3.1

The

Proposition 3.2

(

Analytic torsion

We return to the setup of Section

\displaystyle\chi_{K_{f}}\coloneqq\frac{1_{K_{f}}}{\operatorname{vol}(K_{f})}

(4.1)

for

\mathcal{C}(G(\mathbb{A})^{1},K_{f})

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\operatorname{reg}}\left(e^{-t\Delta_{p}(\tau)}\right)\coloneqq J_{\operatorname{geo}}(h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi_{K_{f}}).

We then define

\displaystyle T_{X(K_{f})}(\tau)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{d}(-1)^{p}p\>\text{FP}\left(\frac{\zeta_{p,\tau}(s)}{s}\right).

(4.2)

5.
Review of the Arthur-Selberg trace formula

Fix a minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{0}
and a Levi decomposition
P_{0}=M_{0}N_{0}
, and call any subgroup
P\subseteq G
containing
P_{0}
a
standard
parabolic subgroup of
G
. Any standard parabolic subgroup
P
has a canonical Levi decomposition, namely
P=MN
such that
M_{0}\subseteq M
. Let
M
be a Levi subgroup of
G
over
\mathbb{Q}
, i.e. a Levi component of a parabolic subgroup of
G
. We write
\mathcal{L}(M)
for the set of Levi subgroups of
G
containing
M
, and
\mathcal{F}(M)
for the set of parabolic subgroups containing
M
. Then any
P\in\mathcal{F}(M)
has a canonical Levi decomposition by picking its Levi component to lie in
\mathcal{L}(M)
. Finally,
\mathcal{P}(M)
denotes the set of parabolic subgroups for which
M
is a Levi component. Note that then
\mathcal{F}(M)=\bigsqcup_{L\in\mathcal{L}(M)}\mathcal{P}(L)
. These are all finite sets. Throughout, we will pick the Haar measures on our groups such that
K
,
G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for each prime
p
, and
N_{P}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash N_{P}(\mathbb{A})
all have measure
1
, and they respect the decompositions
G(\mathbb{A})=P(\mathbb{A})K=M(\mathbb{A})N(\mathbb{A})K=M(\mathbb{A})^{1}N(\mathbb{A})A(\mathbb{R})^{0}K
.

Denote by
A_{M}
the
\mathbb{Q}
-split component of the center of
M
. We will sometimes write
A_{P}
for
A_{M_{P}}
when
M_{P}
is the canonical Levi component of
P
. We further denote by
X(M)_{\mathbb{Q}}
the group of characters of
M
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and set
\mathfrak{a}_{M}=\operatorname{Hom}(X(M)_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{R}).
Again, we may write
\mathfrak{a}_{P}\coloneqq\mathfrak{a}_{M_{P}}
. This is then a real vector space.

The Arthur trace formula is the equality

\displaystyle J_{\operatorname{geo}}(f)=J_{\operatorname{spec}}(f)

of the two distributions
J_{\operatorname{geo}}
and
J_{\operatorname{spec}}
on
C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{A})^{1})
, known as the geometric side, respectively the spectral side, of the trace formula. This was introduced in
[
3
]
, and has since been expanded on immensely, see
[
7
]
for a great overview and introduction. Here, we will only briefly and without proof introduce the coarse and fine expansions of the geometric side.

5.1.
The coarse geometric expansion

Define an equivalence relation on
G(\mathbb{Q})
by setting two elements equivalent if their semisimple parts are
G(\mathbb{Q})
-conjugate to each other. We denote by
\mathcal{O}
the set of such equivalence classes in
G(\mathbb{Q})
. Note that these are in bijection with conjugacy classes of semisimple elements. It is an elementary result that
\mathcal{O}
is finite. For any such equivalence class
\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}
, we have an associated distribution
J_{\mathfrak{o}}
giving a decomposition of the geometric side called the
coarse geometric expansion

\displaystyle J_{\operatorname{geo}}(f)=\sum_{\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}}J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f),

(5.1)

See
[
3
]
for the proper definition and the sum formula above. We give a working description of these distributions
J_{\mathfrak{o}}
for a class of equivalence classes below. For our purposes, especially one equivalence class will be important, namely the one with semisimple part being the identity, which is of course the unipotent elements
\mathfrak{o}_{\operatorname{unip}}
. For brevity we will write
J_{\operatorname{unip}}=J_{\mathfrak{o}_{\operatorname{unip}}}
going forward.

First, let
\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}
be anisotropic, and let
P
be a certain associated standard parabolic subgroup with
\gamma\in P\cap\mathfrak{o}
semisimple (anisotropic root datum, see
[
7
]
, §
10
). Write
P=MN
the canonical Levi decomposition of
P
. Then

\displaystyle J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f)=\operatorname{vol}(M(\mathbb{Q})_{\gamma}\backslash M(\mathbb{A})_{\gamma}^{1})\int_{G(\mathbb{A})_{\gamma}\backslash G(\mathbb{A})}f(x^{-1}\gamma x)v_{P}(x)dx.

(5.2)

The function
v_{P}(x)
here is a certain weight function, see (
[
7
]
, Theorem
11.2
) for a definition, and the result above. Note that not all classes
\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}
are anisotropic, in particular the formula does not hold for
J_{\operatorname{unip}}
. Rather, we provide a further decomposition of this distribution which will allow us to express it in terms of local orbital integrals.

5.2.
The fine geometric expansion

Let
S
be a finite set of places including
\infty
, and let
M
be a Levi subgroup. Choose a
\gamma=\prod_{\nu\in S}\gamma_{\nu}\in M(\mathbb{Q}_{S})
. The centralizer
G_{\gamma_{\nu}}
is an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}_{\nu}
, and we define
G_{\gamma}=\prod_{\nu\in S}G_{\gamma_{\nu}}
. It follows from the short paper of Rao (
[
40
]
) that there exists a right-
G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})
-invariant Radon measure on the quotient
G_{\gamma}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})\backslash G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})
, meaning we have a
G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})
-invariant linear form taking
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G(G_{S}))
to

\displaystyle\int_{G_{\gamma}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})\backslash G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})}f(x^{-1}\gamma x)dx.

(5.3)

We will now assume
G_{\gamma}=M_{\gamma}
to simplify the description. At the end, we explain how to go to the general case. Recall the definition of the generalized Weyl discriminant,

\displaystyle D(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\nu\in S}D(\gamma_{\nu}),

\displaystyle D(\gamma_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\det(1-\text{Ad}((\gamma_{\nu})_{s}))_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{(\gamma_{\nu})_{s}}},

where
(\gamma_{\nu})_{s}
is the semisimple part of
\gamma_{\nu}
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma_{\nu}}
the Lie algebra of
G_{\sigma_{\nu}}
. This discriminant is used for normalization purposes. Then we define the weighted orbital integral

\displaystyle J_{M}(f,\gamma)=|D(\gamma)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{G_{\gamma}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})\backslash G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})}f(x^{-1}\gamma x)v_{M}(x)dx.

(5.4)

Here
v_{M}(x)
is another weight function related to
v_{P}
, see (
[
7
]
,
(18.2)
).

If
G_{\gamma}\neq M_{\gamma}
, we have a more complicated formula. For
a\in A_{M}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})
small in general position, there exists canonical functions
r^{L}_{M}(\gamma,a)
such that the following exists and is well defined:

\displaystyle J_{M}(f,\gamma)=\lim_{a\to 1}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}(M)}r^{L}_{M}(\gamma,a)J_{L}(f,a\gamma).

This is one of the central theorems of (
[
6
]
), see also there for the relevant existence and convergence results. Considering the obvious injection of
C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})^{1})
into
C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{A})^{1})
, we may form the distribution
J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f)
for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})^{1})
. We need a finer equivalence relation to index over.

Definition 5.1
.

We say two elements
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in M(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathfrak{o}
are
(G,S)
-
equivalent
if they are
G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})
-conjugate. The set of such equivalence classes
(M(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathfrak{o})_{G,S}
is then finite.

Finally, we have the characterization of
J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f)
in terms of weighted orbital integrals.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{S})^{1})
. There exists a finite set of places
S_{\mathfrak{o}}
, only depending on
\mathfrak{o}
, containing
\infty
and such that for any finite
S
with
S_{\mathfrak{o}}\subseteq S
, we have

\displaystyle J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{L},\\
\gamma\in(M(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathfrak{o})_{M,S}\end{subarray}}a^{M}(S,\gamma)J_{M}(f,\gamma),

where
a^{M}(S,u)
are some uniquely determined coefficients.

The coefficients
a^{M}(S,\gamma)
, sometimes called the
global coefficients
, are difficult to compute in general. In the special case
\gamma=1
, we have

\displaystyle a^{M}(S,1)=\operatorname{vol}(M(\mathbb{Q})\backslash M(\mathbb{A})^{1}).

Furthermore, for
\operatorname{GL}(n)
defined over a number field
F
, a bound has been established in
[
28
]
for the global coefficients associated to unipotent
\gamma
which we will discuss later.

Assume now that
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{A}))
can be expressed as a product
f=\prod_{\nu}f_{\nu}
with
f_{\nu}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{\nu}))
and all but finitely many
f_{p}=1_{K_{p}}
. We would like to split up the weighted orbital integrals into local parts, using that the weight functions also satisfy only a decomposition into local functions, however an additive one. This gives a formula involving sums of products of local parts. Handling this in a smart way yields the following result (see
[
4
]
).

Assume
S=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
, and
f=f_{1}f_{2}
with
f_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{i}}))
along with
\gamma=\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
such that
\gamma_{i}\in M(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{i}})
. We assume
G_{\gamma}=M_{\gamma}
, implying that
G_{\gamma_{i}}=M_{\gamma_{i}}
. For
Q_{i}=MN_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
define

\displaystyle f_{i,Q_{i}}(m)=\delta_{Q_{i}}(m)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{K_{S_{i}}}\int_{N_{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{S_{i}})}f_{i}(k^{-1}mnk)dndk.

(5.5)

Here
\delta_{Q}(m)
is a certain normalization independent of
f
(see e.g.
[
7
]
, p.
50
). Then we have a general decomposition formula given by

\displaystyle J_{M}(f,\gamma)=\sum_{L_{1},L_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(M)}d^{G}_{M}(L_{1},L_{2})J_{M}^{L_{1}}(f_{1,Q_{1}},\gamma_{1})J_{M}^{L_{2}}(f_{2,Q_{2}},\gamma_{2}).

(5.6)

The orbital integral
J_{M}^{L}
is the analogous expression where one instead considers the underlying reductive group to be
L
, instead of
G
. The constant
d^{G}_{M}(L_{1},L_{2})
arises from the decomposition formula of the weight functions. Applying the formula inductively let’s us reduce to sets
S_{i}
of one element. It is the combination of Theorem
5.2
along with this decomposition, used on the terms
J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f)
on the geometric side of the Arthur-Selberg trace formula, that we will call the
fine geometric expansion
. When
G_{\gamma}\neq M_{\gamma}
, the same procedure yields an expansion in limits of sums of the terms above.

6.
Restricting to the unipotent contribution

We continue in the setup of Section
4
. In this section we show that, when compactifying your test function properly, one needs only concern themselves with the unipotent contribution to the trace formula. The result below is a generalization of (
[
12
]
, Proposition
5.3
), which is the equivalent statement for
G=\operatorname{GL}(n)
, and the proof in fact reduces to this result after choosing a suitable embedding of
G
into
\operatorname{GL}(n)
.

For now, we provide the setup. Let
G
be a connected reductive group defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with neat open compact subgroup
K_{f}=\prod_{p}K_{p}\subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
. Take a faithful
\mathbb{Q}
-rational representation
\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
with a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq V
such that
K_{f}
is the stabilizer in
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
of
\hat{\Lambda}\coloneqq\hat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{f}\otimes V
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\displaystyle K(N)=\{g\in G(\mathbb{A}_{f})\mid\forall v\in\mathbb{A}_{f}\otimes V:\>\rho(g)v\equiv v\mod N\hat{\Lambda}\}.

(6.1)

Then
K(N)\subseteq K_{f}
is a factorizable open compact subgroup in
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
. We see that these generalize the open compact subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(n)
given as

\displaystyle K_{\operatorname{GL}(n)}(N)\coloneqq\prod_{p}\operatorname{ker}(\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{e}\mathbb{Z}_{p})),

by picking
\rho
the standard representation and
\Lambda
the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of the standard basis. Furthermore, given
\Lambda\subseteq V
we may identify
\operatorname{GL}(V)
with
\operatorname{GL}(n)
by choosing a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\Lambda
as a basis of
V
, and under this identification,
K(N)
maps into
K_{\operatorname{GL}(n)}(N)
under the embedding
\rho
.

We let
h_{t}^{\tau,p}:G(\mathbb{R})^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be the trace of the heat kernel as defined in (
2.7
) and
\chi(N)
the normalized characteristic function on
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
of
K(N)
as in (
4.1
). We then take
f=h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N)\in\mathcal{C}(G(\mathbb{A})^{1},K(N))
as our test function, in the same way we did when we defined analytic torsion. Although this function is not compactly supported, the geometric side of the Arthur-Selberg trace formula as well as the coarse geometric expansion are well defined by (
[
17
]
).

Set
r(g)=d(K,gK)
to be the geodesic distance on
G(\mathbb{R})^{1}/K
of
g\in G(\mathbb{R})^{1}
from the identity, and let
\varphi_{R}:G(\mathbb{R})^{1}\to[0,1]
be any smooth function identically
1
on
B(R)=\{g\in G(\mathbb{R})^{1}\mid r(g)<R\}
and identically
0
outside
B(R+\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
small. We then define the compactification of
h_{t}^{\tau,p}
with parameter
R
as

\displaystyle h_{t,R}^{\tau,p}(g)=\varphi_{R}(g)h_{t}^{\tau,p}(g),\quad g\in G(\mathbb{R})^{1}.

(6.2)

Consider the embedding
G(\mathbb{R})^{1}\to\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})^{1}
induced by
\rho
as above. By possibly composing with an inner automorphism of
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})^{1}
, we can ensure the following properties hold (see also
[
27
]
, p.
24
):

(1)

\rho(K)\subseteq\text{O}(n)
,

(2)

If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
is the Cartan decomposition of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G(\mathbb{R})^{1}
associated to
\theta
, then
\mathfrak{k}
(resp.
\mathfrak{p}
) is embedded into the skew-symmetric (resp. symmetric) matrices of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})
under the induced map,

(3)

the Frobenius norm on the embedding of
\mathfrak{g}
in
\mathfrak{gl(n,\mathbb{R})}
coincides with the norm induced by the Killing form.

It follows from such a choice of embedding that

\displaystyle d(gK,K)=d_{\operatorname{GL}(n)}(\rho(g)\text{O}(n),\text{O}(n))

(6.3)

where
d_{\operatorname{GL}(n)}
is the geodesic distance on
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})^{1}/\text{O}(n)
. We now state and prove our reduction of the geometric side of the trace formula to the unipotent contribution.

Proposition 6.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{n}>0
only depending on
n
such that for
N
large enough and
R\leq C_{n}\log N
, we have that

\displaystyle J_{\operatorname{geo}}(h_{t,R}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))=J_{\operatorname{unip}}(h_{t,R}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N)).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be defined as in Section
5.1
, and let
\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}
with representative
\gamma
. Consider any
f\in\mathcal{C}(G(\mathbb{A})^{1},K(N))
. It follows directly from the formula (
5.2
) that
J_{\mathfrak{o}}(f)=0
if no conjugate of
\gamma
in
G(\mathbb{A})
intersects the support of
f
.

Let now
\mathfrak{o}\neq\mathfrak{o}_{\text{unip}}
. The orbit of
\gamma
under
G(\mathbb{A})
maps into the orbit of
\rho(\gamma)
in
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{A})
. Furthermore, under
\rho
, the ball
B(R)\subseteq G(\mathbb{R})^{1}
of radius
R
maps into the ball
B_{\operatorname{GL}(n)}(R)
of radius
R
under geodesic distance in
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})^{1}
by (
6.3
), and
K(N)
maps into
K_{\operatorname{GL}(n)}(N)
by construction as detailed above.

It was shown in (
[
12
]
, Proposition
5.3
) that the
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{A})
-orbit of any such
\rho(\gamma)
does not intersect
B_{\operatorname{GL}(n)}(R)K_{\operatorname{GL}(n)}(N)
for
N
large enough and
R\leq C_{n}\log N
, with
C_{n}>0
a constant depending only on
n
. By the above, this implies that the
G(\mathbb{A})
-orbit of
\gamma
does not intersect
B(R)K(N)
, which is the support of
h_{t,R}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N)
. In particular,
J_{\mathfrak{o}}(h_{t,R}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))=0
. By the coarse geometric expansion (
5.1
), this completes the proof.
∎

7.
Explicit local weighted orbital integrals

In order make full use of the fine geometric expansion of
J_{\operatorname{unip}}
, we need to have an explicit formula for the local orbital integrals appearing in (
5.6
). This is more complicated than in the case of
\operatorname{GL}(n)
as we do not in general have access to Richardson parabolic subgroups, and instead must use Jacobson-Morozov parabolics. The explicit formulas will be used both in the archimedean and non-archimedean setting in the following sections.

We continue in the same notation. In particular,
G
will be a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\nu\in\{p,\infty\}
a place of
\mathbb{Q}
. We let
K_{\nu}
be an admissible maximal compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{\nu})
as defined in (
[
4
]
, §
1
). Let
M
be a Levi subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
, and take
\gamma\in M(\mathbb{Q}_{\nu})
a representative of a unipotent conjugacy class, i.e.
\gamma_{ss}=1
. For
P\in\mathcal{F}(M)
we write
P=M_{P}N_{P}
for its Levi decomposition with
M\subseteq M_{P}
.

By the Jacobson-Morozov theorem there exists an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(X,Y,H)
in
M(\mathbb{Q}_{\nu})
such that
\gamma=\exp X
. Let
\mathfrak{m}(\mathbb{Q}_{\nu})=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{m}_{i}
be the associated eigenspace decomposition, and define

\displaystyle Z_{\nu}\coloneqq\bigg\{p^{-1}\gamma p\mid p\text{ in the normalizer in }M(\mathbb{Q}_{\nu})\text{ of }\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}_{i}\bigg\}.

It is known that
Z_{\nu}
is an open subset of
\exp\mathfrak{u}_{2}
, for
\mathfrak{u}_{k}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\geq k}\mathfrak{m}_{i}
(see
[
6
]
, p.
246
). Fix
Q\in\mathcal{F}(M)
with Levi decomposition
Q=LN_{Q}
, and take some
R\in\mathcal{P}(M)
contained in
L
. Define
\Pi_{\nu}^{Q}=Z_{\nu}N_{R}(\mathbb{Q}_{\nu})
. With Haar measures on
K_{\nu}
,

Our integral is significantly simpler than that of

To simplify the integral, we will instead integrate over the associated Lie algebras. Let

\displaystyle J_{M}(f,\gamma)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}(M)}c(Q,\gamma)\int_{K_{\nu}}\int_{\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}f(k^{-1}e^{X}k)v^{Q}_{M}(1,e^{X})|I^{Q}_{\nu}(X)|_{\nu}^{\frac{1}{2}}dXdk.

Furthermore, by a standard formula for dimensions of induced orbits (

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{O}_{X}+2\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{n}_{R}+2\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{n}_{Q}

\displaystyle=2(\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{u}_{2}+\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{n}_{R}+\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{n}_{Q})+\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{m}_{1}

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{v}_{Q}+\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{m}_{1}.

Lemma 7.1

(

There exists constants

8.
Bounds on local orbital integrals

This section utilizes the general explicit description of local orbital integrals given in the previous section to give the necessary upper bounds for our test function. We treat the archimedean and non-archimedean cases separately.

8.1.
Large
t
asymptotics of archimedean orbital integrals

We focus here on the infinite place
\nu=\infty
, and will critically use our bounds on
h_{t}^{\tau,p}
from Section
3
.

we choose an embedding of
G(\mathbb{R})^{1}
in
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})^{1}
as in the paragraph before (
6.3
). Recall that the geodesic distance function on
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})^{1}/\text{O}(n)
then agrees with the geodesic distance function on
G(\mathbb{R})^{1}/K
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\gamma\in M(\mathbb{R})
be unipotent, and assume
t>1
and that
\tau
is
\lambda
-strongly acyclic. Then there exists some
C,c^{\prime}>0
, both independent of
R
and
c^{\prime}
independent of
\lambda
, such that

\displaystyle|J_{M}^{G}(h_{t,R}^{\tau,p},\gamma)|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-(\lambda-c^{\prime})t}.

(8.1)

Proof.

By the formula in (
7.1
) and by using the bi-
K
-invariance of
h_{t,R}^{\tau,p}
, we are reduced to considering

\displaystyle J_{M}^{G}(h_{t,R}^{\tau,p},\gamma)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}(M)}c(Q,\gamma)\int_{\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}h^{\tau,p}_{t,R}(e^{X})v^{Q}_{M}(1,e^{X})|I^{Q}_{\infty}(X)|^{\frac{1}{2}}dX.

(8.2)

Let
A,c,C>0
be as in Proposition
3.2
(b), and write

\displaystyle\mathfrak{v}_{Q}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}D(k),

where

\displaystyle D(0)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\mathfrak{v}_{Q}\mid r(e^{X})<A\},

\displaystyle D(k)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{X\in\mathfrak{v}_{Q}\mid A+k\leq r(e^{X})<A+k+1\},\quad k\geq 1.

We will decompose the orbital integral (
8.2
) into a sum of integrals using this disjoint union. Throughout the following, we use the bound (
7.2
), which implies the bound

\displaystyle|v_{M}^{Q}(1,e^{X})|\leq C\left(1+r(e^{X})\right)^{b}.

We will also use that the polynomial
I^{Q}_{\infty}(X)
is bounded by some polynomial in
\lVert X\rVert
, and as
\lVert X\rVert
is bounded by
C(1+\lVert e^{X}\rVert)
for some constant
C>0
for all unipotent matrices (see e.g.
[
27
]
, Lemma
6.2
), it is furthermore bounded by a polynomial in
e^{r(e^{X})}
.
The integral over
D(0)
is then handled using Proposition
3.2
(a):

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D(0)}h^{\tau,p}_{t,R}(e^{X})v^{Q}_{M}(1,e^{X})|I^{Q}_{\infty}(X)|^{\frac{1}{2}}dX\right|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{D(0)}\left(1+r(e^{X})\right)^{b}\left(1+e^{r(e^{X})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dX

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}e^{-\lambda t}\left(1+A\right)^{b}\left(1+e^{A}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\operatorname{vol}(D(0)).

As
D(0)
is compact, its volume only depending on
A
, we see that the above is
C_{A}e^{-\lambda t}
, for some constant
C_{A}>0
only depending on
A
.

By the bound on
\lVert X\rVert
in terms of
\lVert e^{X}\rVert
above, we may bound the volume of
D(k)
by the volume of

\displaystyle\{X\in\mathfrak{v}_{Q}\mid\lVert X\rVert\leq C(1+e^{k})\}

As this is a ball in Euclidean space, we get that

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(D(k))\leq C(1+e^{k})^{\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}.

We may now estimate the integral over
D(k)
. First, use Proposition
3.2
(b) to write

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D(k)}h^{\tau,p}_{t,R}(e^{X})v^{Q}_{M}(1,e^{X})|I^{Q}_{\infty}(X)|^{\frac{1}{2}}dX\right|\leq C^{\prime}e^{-\lambda t}\left|\int_{D(k)}e^{-c\frac{r\left(e^{X}\right)^{2}}{t}}v^{Q}_{M}(1,e^{X})|I^{Q}_{\infty}(X)|^{\frac{1}{2}}dX\right|.

By the above, the latter integral can then be bounded by

\displaystyle C^{\prime}e^{-c\frac{(A+k-1)^{2}}{t}}\left(1+(A+k)\right)^{b}\left(1+e^{A+k}\right)^{d}\operatorname{vol}(D(k))

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}^{\prime}e^{-c\frac{k^{2}}{t}}e^{c_{1}k},

where
C_{A}^{\prime},d>0
are some constants and
c_{1}=\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{v}_{Q}+d+\varepsilon
for some (any) small
\varepsilon>0
. It now follows as in the end of the proof of (
[
12
]
, Proposition
6.4
) that the sum of the integrals over
D(k)
,
k\geq 1
, converges and is bounded by

\displaystyle C_{2}e^{-(\lambda-c^{\prime})t}

for some constants
C_{2},c^{\prime}>0
, with
c^{\prime}
only depending on the group
G
. This finishes the proof.
∎

8.2.
Explicit bounds on non-archimedean orbital integrals

Our setup is the following. We will stick to the notation of
K(N)
and
\chi(N)
as defined in (
6.1
). Write
K(N)=\prod_{p}K_{p}(p^{\nu_{p}(N)})
for the factorization of
K(N)
such that
K_{p}(p^{\nu_{p}(N)})
is mapped into
\operatorname{ker}(\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{\nu_{p}(N)}\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
under the embedding
G\to\operatorname{GL}(n)
. We let
M
be a Levi subgroup of
G
, and
P\in\mathcal{F}(M)
with Levi decomposition
P=LN_{P}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{M}(\mathbb{Q})
denote the set of unipotent conjugacy classes in
M(\mathbb{Q})
, and write
\gamma=\prod_{\nu}\gamma_{\nu}
for
\gamma
a representative of
\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{U}_{M}(\mathbb{Q})
, considered as an element of
M(\mathbb{A})
.

We now prove a bound on the non-archimedean part of
J_{\operatorname{unip}}
. We begin by working locally. Fix
p
and write
e=\nu_{p}(N)
. Using the explicit formula for local orbital integrals (
7.1
) on the reductive group
L
, unfolding the definition of
f_{P}
as given in (
5.5
), and using the fact that
K_{p}(p^{e})
is a normal subgroup, we may write

\displaystyle J_{M}^{L}((1_{K_{p}(p^{e})})_{P},\gamma)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}^{L}(M)}c(Q,\gamma)\int_{\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}1_{K_{p}(p^{e})}(e^{X})v^{Q}_{M}(1,e^{X})|I^{Q}_{p}(X)|_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}dX.

(8.3)

Considering the characteristic function, we may restrict to computing this integral over
\{X\in\mathfrak{v}_{Q}\mid e^{X}\in K_{p}(p^{e})\}
, which has a norm- and measure-preserving isomorphism to
p^{e}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}
equipped with the Haar measure from
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Using the bound on weight functions (
7.2
) and that
I^{Q}_{p}
is homogeneous of degree
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{m}_{1}
, we may estimate the integral on the right hand side above as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}1_{K_{p}(p^{e})}(e^{X})v^{Q}_{M}(1,e^{X})|I^{Q}_{p}(X)|_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}dX\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{p^{e}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}}(1+\log\lVert e^{X}\rVert)^{b}\lVert X\rVert^{\frac{\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{m}_{1}}{2}}dX

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}p^{-e\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}(1+\log(p^{-e}))^{b}p^{-\frac{e\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{m}_{1}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}p^{-\frac{e\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ind}_{M}^{G}\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}}{2}}(1+\log(p^{-e}))^{b}.

Here we also used the description of the dimension of orbits provided above Lemma
7.1
. In the case that
L=M
, the weight function is identically
1
, which gives a bound

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathfrak{v}_{Q}}1_{K_{p}(p^{e})}(e^{X})v^{Q}_{M}(1,e^{X})|I^{Q}_{p}(X)|_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}dX\right|\leq C_{p}^{I}p^{-\frac{e\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ind}_{M}^{G}\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}}{2}}.

Here
C_{p}^{J}
is the
p
-adic valuation of some constant depending only on
J_{p}^{Q}
. In fact, this polynomial may be globally defined: Note that
\gamma_{p}
is just
\gamma\in M(\mathbb{Q})
embedded into
M(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
through the inclusion
\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Thus, if we let
\mathfrak{m}(\mathbb{Q})
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-points of the Lie algebra of
M
, and let
X\in\mathfrak{m}(\mathbb{Q})
such that
e^{X}
represents the same orbit as
\gamma
, the Jacobson-Morozov theorem tells us that
X
is part of an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, and we have a full decomposition of
\mathfrak{m}(\mathbb{Q})
into eigenspaces as in the local case. Picking a basis for the eigenspaces
\mathfrak{m}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
globally, which is then also a basis in the local cases, we see from the definition in (
[
40
]
, Lemma
2
) that the polynomial
I^{Q}_{p}
is actually defined algebraically over
\mathbb{Q}
, and is thus global. This implies that the mentioned constant is a rational number, and hence one may pick
C_{p}^{I}=1
for all but finitely many
p
. Importantly, those
p
do not depend on
N
.

With these inequalities established, the bound on the entire non-archimedean part now follows: Let
S(N)
be the collection of primes dividing
N
. By iterative use of the decomposition formula (
5.6
), we get that

\displaystyle J_{M}(1_{K(N)},\gamma_{f})=\sum_{\underline{L}\in\mathcal{L}(M)^{|S(N)|}}d_{M}(\underline{L})\prod_{p\in S(N)}J^{L_{p}}_{M}((1_{K(N)})_{P_{p}},\gamma_{p}),

where
\underline{L}=(L_{p})_{p\in S(N)}
runs over all such tuples of Levi subgroups, with
P_{p}=L_{p}N_{p}
a certain parabolic subgroup of
G
chosen as in (
[
7
]
, §§
17
-
18
). Here

are constants which are non-zero only if at most
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{a}_{M}
many elements of
\underline{L}
is not equal to
M
(see
[
26
]
, Lemma
8.2
). This implies that the number of such
\underline{L}
that actually contributes is bounded by
|S(N)|^{\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{a}_{M}}
. As
|S(N)|\leq 2\log N
, collecting the identities and bounds in this section amounts to the following proposition.

Proposition 8.2
.

There exists constants
c,m>0
independent of
N
such that

\displaystyle\left|J_{M}(1_{K(N)},\gamma_{f})\right|\leq cN^{-\frac{\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{M}\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}}{2}}(1+\log N)^{m}.

(8.4)

Later, we will isolate the orbital integral related to the trivial orbit
\{1\}
in
G
, while needing to bound the remaining terms. As there are only finitely many unipotent orbits
\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{U}_{M}(\mathbb{Q})
for every
M
, and only finitely many standard Levi subgroups
M
, one can make the bound above independent of
M
and
\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}
by picking the exponent to be half the minimal dimension of the non-trivial unipotent orbits. Hence we set

\displaystyle k(G)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\min_{(M,\mathcal{O}_{\gamma})\neq(G,\{1\})}\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ind}_{M}^{G}\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}.

(8.5)

This means that for every
(M,\mathcal{O}_{\gamma})\neq(G,\{1\})
we have

\displaystyle\left|J_{M}(1_{K(N)},\mathcal{O}_{\gamma})\right|\leq cN^{-k(G)}(1+\log N)^{m}.

(8.6)

We immediately remark that since we allow trivial induction, i.e. from
G
to
G
, the constant
k(G)
is equal to half the dimension of the minimal (nontrivial) unipotent orbit in
G
. This constant is strictly positive, and is expressible in terms of root systems, see e.g. (
[
15
]
, §
4.3
).

Furthermore, if
G
is Richardson, such that every unipotent orbit in

is induced from the trivial orbit in some parabolic subgroup, then by the usual dimension formula for induced orbits we see that for
P=L_{P}V_{P}
the Levi decomposition of

, we have

\displaystyle k(G)=\frac{1}{2}\min_{P\neq G}(\operatorname{dim}\{1\}+2\operatorname{dim}V_{P})=\min_{P\neq G}\operatorname{dim}V_{P}.

(8.7)

In particular, from this it is easy to see that for
G=\operatorname{SL}(n)
and
G=\operatorname{GL}(n)
over
\mathbb{Q}
, we have
k(G)=n-1
. Even if
G
is not Richardson, the right-hand side above is still an upper bound of
k(G)
, with equality if and only if the minimal unipotent orbit is induced from the trivial orbit in some unipotent radical, equivalently from the trivial orbit in a unipotent radical of minimal dimension.

9.
Proof of the approximation theorem

We continue in the setup that
G
is a connected reductive group with
G(\mathbb{R})
noncompact, and let
K_{f}\subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be a neat open compact subgroup. Let

We now prove our main theorem, giving improved asymptotic behaviour in the approximation of

-torsion by analytic torsion. This is a strengthening of Theorem

of
[

]
under slightly different assumptions. In contrast to their theorem, in the proof of our theorem we need to handle the global coefficients

arising in the fine geometric expansion. This causes difficulty as no general bound is known for them. They depend on the primes dividing

(see
[

be the set of primes dividing

together with

There exists constants

such that for all

,

and

we have

\displaystyle\left|a^{M}(S_{N},\mathcal{O}_{\gamma})\right|\leq c(1+\log N)^{b}.

For the main theorem, we either have to assume the conjecture, or assume that

only varies over a set of positive integers with all prime divisors lying in some finite fixed set of primes, rendering the global coefficients bounded by a constant. As non-standard notation, let us call such a set

. A good example of a prime-fixed set is

given any

. It is interesting to compare this to the property

defined in (
[

]

We will also need to assume that

]

and

and

in (
[

]
, Proposition

), as well as for a large class of reductive groups, including quasi-split classical groups, in
[

]
and
[
19
]
.

Define
T^{(2)}_{X(N)}(\tau)
to be the

-torsion of

as in
[
22
]
. We can now state our theorem.

Theorem 9.2
.

Assume that
G
is a connected reductive quasi-split group with

noncompact. Let
\tau
be an irreducible and
\lambda
-strongly acyclic representation of

, for some

only depending on

and

(see

\displaystyle\log T_{X(N)}(\tau)=\log T^{(2)}_{X(N)}(\tau)+O\left(\operatorname{vol}(X(N))N^{-k(G)}(\log N)^{a}\right)

The rest of this section is a proof of this theorem.

9.1.
The first reduction

Assume the setup of the theorem. It follows from (

]
, Proposition

such that for any

\displaystyle\left|J_{\operatorname{spec}}(h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))\right|\leq Ce^{-\lambda(1-\varepsilon)t}\operatorname{vol}(X(N)).

This critically uses that

follows by closely examining the proof of (

]

\displaystyle\operatorname{FP}_{s=0}\left(\frac{\zeta_{p}(s,\tau)}{s}\right)=\operatorname{FP}_{s=0}\left(\frac{1}{s\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{\operatorname{geo}}(h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))t^{s-1}dt\right).

\displaystyle\operatorname{FP}_{s=0}\left(\frac{1}{s\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{T}J_{\operatorname{geo}}(h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))t^{s-1}dt\right)+\int_{T}^{\infty}J_{\operatorname{geo}}(h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))t^{-1}dt.

By the Arthur-Selberg trace formula, any bound on

Lemma 9.3
.

There exists

\displaystyle\left|\int_{T}^{\infty}J_{\operatorname{geo}}(h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))t^{-1}dt\right|\leq Ce^{-\lambda(1-\varepsilon)T}\operatorname{vol}(X(N)).

That is the second term of (

) handled, and we return to the first term. We focus on the integral. We want to replace the test function with its compactified version defined in (

). This is possible due to the following bound.

Lemma 9.4

(
[
27
]
, Proposition

)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}J_{\operatorname{unip}-1}(h_{t,R}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))t^{s-1}dt

We turn our attention to the truncated Mellin transform of

9.4.
Approximating

-torsion

Collecting the results of Section

\displaystyle\log T_{X(N)}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{FP}_{s=0}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{s\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{d}(-1)^{p}p\,h^{\tau,p}_{t}(1)t^{s-1}dt\right)\operatorname{vol}(X(N))

\displaystyle+\operatorname{FP}_{s=0}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{d}(-1)^{p}p\,\frac{1}{s\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{T}J_{\operatorname{unip}-1}(h^{\tau,p}_{t,R}\otimes\chi(N))t^{s-1}dt\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{d}(-1)^{p}p\,\left(\int_{T}^{\infty}J_{\operatorname{geo}}(h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))t^{-1}dt+E_{1}(0,R,T)+E_{2}(0,T)\right)

-torsion, as we show below. The second term was just handled in the previous subsection, Proposition

We recall from

that

for

\displaystyle t^{(2)}_{\tilde{X}}(\tau)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{d}(-1)^{p}p\,h^{\tau,p}_{t}(1)t^{s-1}dt\right)\Bigg|_{s=0}.

(9.15)

This is equal to the first term above as the meromorphic continuation of

is holomorphic at

By Proposition

\displaystyle O\left(N^{-k(G)}(\log N)^{b}\operatorname{vol}(X(N))\right)

9.5.
Correct asymptotics of error terms

In this final section, we critically use that we may choose
\lambda
,
T
, and

freely (within the constraints
\lambda>0
,
T\geq 1
,
0<R\leq C_{n}\log N
), and that no essential constants depend on their choice. To ensure that the error term

vanishes sufficiently quickly, we need to pick

. Thus, let

, with the constant
\beta>0
to be determined in a moment. We also pick the maximal allowed value for

, namely
R=C_{n}\log N
. These choices mean that our error terms take the form

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{\infty}J_{\operatorname{geo}}(h_{t}^{\tau,p}\otimes\chi(N))t^{-1}dt

\displaystyle=O\left(N^{-\lambda(1-\varepsilon)\beta}\operatorname{vol}(X(N))\right)

\displaystyle E_{1}(0,C_{n}\log N,\beta\log N)

\displaystyle=O\left((N^{-C_{4}C_{n}^{2}/\beta+C_{2}\beta}\operatorname{vol}(X(N))\right)

\displaystyle E_{2}(0,\beta\log N)

\displaystyle=O\left(N^{-\lambda\beta}\operatorname{vol}(X(N))\right).

This follows from Lemma
9.3
, and equations (
9.5
) and (
9.10
), respectively. Recall that
C_{2},C_{4},C_{n}>0
are all fixed constants, only depending on
G
.

Choose
\beta
small such that

. This ensures the correct asymptotic for

. Now, pick the spectral gap

and

as well. Note that this choice is only dependent on

. This ensures that the remaining error terms have the correct asymptotic. We have now finished the proof of Theorem

.

This is the Cheeger-Müller type formula that we will need. The result cited is more general than presented here, in particular it covers a much larger class of arithmetic subgroups. As a next step, we will need to deal with the latter terms on the right-hand side above. Write

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\rho_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\rho_{\mathbb{R}}^{*},\quad\overline{E}_{\rho_{\mathbb{R}}}\coloneqq E_{\rho_{\mathbb{R}}}\oplus E_{\rho_{\mathbb{R}}}^{*},

Definition 11.3
.

We say that a parabolic subgroup
P\subseteq G(\mathbb{R})
with unipotent radical

is
\Gamma(N)
-cuspidal if
\Gamma(N)\cap U_{P}
is a lattice in

We let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{\Gamma(N)}=\{P_{N,1},\dots,P_{N,\kappa(\Gamma(N))}\}

be the set of
\Gamma(N)
-conjugacy classes of
\Gamma(N)
-parabolic subgroups of

. We set

\displaystyle\kappa(X(N))=\kappa(\Gamma(N))=|\mathfrak{P}_{\Gamma(N)}|,

and note that this is always finite. It is clear that we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{\rho_{\mathbb{R}}}(X(N))\leq\kappa(X(N)).

The key to controlling the contribution from the cusps is the following.

Proposition 11.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{N}
be a sequence of finite index subgroups of
\Gamma
satisfying the following assumptions:

(a)

[\Gamma:\Gamma_{N}]\to\infty
as
N\to\infty

(b)

Every non-identity element of
\Gamma

\Gamma_{N}
for only finitely many
N

(c)

for
N

\Gamma_{N}
-cuspidal parabolic subgroup

P=L_{P}U_{P}
satisfy

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N}\cap P=\Gamma_{N}\cap U_{P},

Then we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa(\Gamma_{N})}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{N}]}\leq\sum_{P_{l}\in\mathfrak{P}_{\Gamma}}\frac{1}{[\Gamma\cap U_{P_{l}}:\Gamma_{N}\cap U_{P_{l}}]}.

(11.3)

Proof.

This follows directly from the proof of (

All these properties are satisfied for
\Gamma_{N}=\Gamma(N)
and are easily checked. Importantly, we can also estimate the indices appearing on the right-hand side of (
11.3
):
U_{P}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{\operatorname{dim}U_{P}}
as a topological space, and under this homeomorphism we have that
U_{P}\cap\Gamma
corresponds to the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{dim}U_{P}}
with
U_{P}\cap\Gamma(N)
corresponding to the sublattice
(N\mathbb{Z})^{\operatorname{dim}U_{P}}
. In particular, the quotient
(U_{P}\cap\Gamma)/(U_{P}\cap\Gamma(N))
corresponds to
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\operatorname{dim}U_{P}}
, giving us a precise description of the index as the cardinality of the quotient,

\displaystyle[U_{P}\cap\Gamma:U_{P}\cap\Gamma(N)]=N^{\operatorname{dim}U_{P}}.

These types of terms also showed up in our analysis of analytic torsion. Indeed, recall that

, defined in (

Corollary 11.5

We have that

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{\rho_{\mathbb{R}}}(X(N))}{[\Gamma:\Gamma(N)]}=O\left(\frac{1}{N^{k(G)}}\right).

As the constant
k(G)
is central in the error terms we get below, let us compute it explicitly. We may identify the complexified Lie algebra of
G
with

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathfrak{so}(n+1,\mathbb{C}),

which has root system
D_{d+1}
(recall

in a complex simple Lie algebra

is

, where

is the dual Coxeter number of
\mathfrak{l}
. For

\mathfrak{so}(n+1,\mathbb{C})
is simple, and as

has dual Coxeter number

(see e.g.
[
21

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{O}_{min}=4d-2=2n-4.

This is a priori the (complex) dimension of the minimal nilpotent orbit in the complexified Lie algebra, but it extends directly to over
\mathbb{Q}
, e.g. as the classification of orbits is given by Jordan normal forms which for unipotent elements are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, by the discussion following Definition

\displaystyle k(G)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{O}_{min}=n-2,\quad n\geq 5.

In the case of

, we have the exceptional isomorphism
\mathfrak{so}(4)=\mathfrak{so}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3)

by the same formula as above. We summarize:

Lemma 11.6

For

odd, we have that

\displaystyle k(\operatorname{SO}(n,1))=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }n=3\\
n-2&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
