An eigenvalue problem for a nonlocal quasilinear anisotropic equation in fractional Orlicz Sobolev spaces without the Δ 2 \Delta_{2} –condition
Julián Fernández Bonder, Martín S. Guzmán, Juan F. Spedaletti

Abstract
. In this paper we analyze an eigenvalue problem associated to fractional operators of the form ℒ a s ​ u ​ ( x ) = 2 ​ p.v. ​ ∫ ℝ n a ⁡ ( x , y , D s ​ u ​ ( x , y ) ) ​ d ​ y | x − y | n + s , \mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u(x)=2\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y))\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}, which represents a generalization model for nonlocal, nonstandard growth diffusion problems. We study this problem in the context of the fractional Orlicz Sobolev spaces without assuming the so-called Δ 2 \Delta_{2} –condition on the Young functions involved. We show existence of a sequence of eigenpairs ( u k , λ k ) → ( 0 , + ∞ ) (u_{k},\lambda_{k})\to(0,+\infty) .

1.
Introduction

Eigenvalue problems are among the most important topics in Partial Differential Equations due to their wide range of applications in both pure and applied mathematics. See, for instance, the classical books by Courant and Hilbert
[
15
,
16
]
.

In particular, for linear elliptic operators such as the Laplacian, the study of the asymptotic behavior of eigenvalues dates back to Courant. The classical Courant minimax principle ensures the existence of an infinite sequence of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda u&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{in }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
and
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a bounded open set.

In the early 1990s, attention shifted to nonlinear extensions of eigenvalue problems associated with the Laplacian. One of the most extensively studied examples is the eigenvalue problem for the
p
-Laplacian:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

introduced in
[
33
]
(see also
[
34
,
35
]
).

A standard approach to deal with nonlinear eigenvalue problems such as (
1.1
) is based on the Ljusternik–Schnirelmann theory, introduced in the early 1930s by Ljusternik and Schnirelmann
[
36
]
. This theory provides a method for finding critical points of differentiable functionals on finite-dimensional Riemannian manifolds. Its extension to infinite-dimensional settings was developed by Schwartz
[
39
,
40
]
for Riemannian manifolds modeled on Hilbert spaces, and by Palais
[
37
]
for Finsler manifolds modeled on Banach spaces.

These ideas have been widely used to establish the existence of infinitely many distinct solutions for nonlinear PDEs. A very incomplete list of contributions includes
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
30
,
42
]
.

To apply the Ljusternik–Schnirelmann theory to problem (
1.1
), one considers the functionals
F
and
G
defined on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
by

F(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\qquad\text{ and }\qquad G(u)=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dx.

In this framework, one obtains the existence of a sequence of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
. A crucial point is that
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is reflexive and separable, and the functionals
F
and
G
are differentiable (see
[
26
,
27
,
43
]
).

Another interesting nonlinear eigenvalue problem arises with the
m-
Laplacian operator, defined by

\Delta_{m}u=\mathop{\text{div}}\left(\frac{m(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\right),

where
m\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a nondecreasing function. This operator generalizes the
p-
Laplacian operator when
m(t)=t^{p-1}
.

The corresponding eigenvalue problem reads

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{m}u=\lambda g(u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{in }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Here, the function
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies suitable growth conditions. These operators are particularly appealing in applications due to the possibility of different diffusion behaviors when
|\nabla u|\ll 1
and
|\nabla u|\gg 1
, a phenomenon commonly referred to as
nonstandard growth
.

A key role is played by the primitive function of
m
, defined as
M(t)=\int_{0}^{t}m(s),ds
. When
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, namely

M(2t)\leq CM(t),

for some constant
C>1
and all
t\geq T_{0}
, problem (
1.2
) shares many properties with the
p
-Laplacian case. In particular, the Ljusternik–Schnirelmann theory can be applied to obtain a sequence of eigenvalues.

However, when
M
does not satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, the situation becomes significantly more delicate. In this case, the associated functional spaces are no longer separable or reflexive, and the corresponding functionals fail to be differentiable. In
[
41
]
, the author studied problem (
1.2
) by combining ideas from
[
29
]
with a Galerkin-type approximation method, overcoming the lack of the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. As a consequence, the Ljusternik–Schnirelmann theory can still be applied, yielding the existence of an infinite sequence of eigenvalues for (
1.2
).

Recently, considerable attention has been devoted to the study of nonlocal diffusion problems due to their numerous applications in the natural sciences. See
[
1
,
14
,
20
,
28
]
. Arguably, one of the most relevant nonlocal operators is the
fractional Laplacian
, defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C(n,s)\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy.

A natural question in this context is the study of the corresponding eigenvalue problem and its relationship with the classical Laplacian. This problem has been addressed by several authors, and a comprehensive treatment can be found in
[
5
]
.

Nonlinear extensions of this problem were considered subsequently. In particular, in
[
32
,
25
]
the authors study the eigenvalue problem associated with the fractional
p
-Laplacian, defined as

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x)=C(n,s,p)\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy.

In this setting, the classical Ljusternik–Schnirelmann theory can be applied, since the associated functional spaces, namely the fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, are reflexive and separable, and the corresponding functionals are differentiable.

More recently, in
[
24
]
, the authors analyzed an eigenvalue problem associated with the fractional
m
-Laplacian operator defined by

(-\Delta_{m})^{s}u(x)=\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}m\left(\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\,\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n+s}},

where
m=M^{\prime}
and
M
is a Young function. Note that when
m(t)=|t|^{p-2}t
, this operator reduces to the fractional
p
-Laplacian.

The corresponding eigenvalue problem is

(1.3)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{m})^{s}u=\lambda g(u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where the function
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies suitable growth conditions. In
[
24
]
, without assuming the
\Delta_{2}
-condition on either
M
or its conjugate
\bar{M}
, the authors employ a Galerkin-type method to obtain a countable sequence of distinct eigensolutions
{(u_{k},\lambda_{k})}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
to problem (
1.3
). Moreover, it is shown that
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

The aim of the present paper is to generalize the results in
[
24
]
by considering a class of anisotropic fractional operators
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}
defined by

\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u(x)=2\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}a\left(x,y,\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}},

where the function
a
is odd, symmetric, monotone, and satisfies suitable growth conditions (see Conditions
3.1
for precise assumptions).

In this framework, we study the associated nonlocal eigenvalue problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u=\lambda g(u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies appropriate growth conditions.

Our main result establishes the existence of a sequence of eigenvalues
{\lambda_{k}}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for problem (
1.4
), such that
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to\infty
(see Theorem
4.10
).

Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows: after this introduction, in Section
2
we include the preliminaries on Young functions, Orlicz spaces and fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. Even though the content is not new, there is some not so standard notation and terminology that will be used throughout the paper. In Section
3
we precisely define the operators
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}
and prove some of the main properties of these operators, namely the pseudo-monotonicity (see Theorem
3.4
). Finally, Section
4
we analyze the eigenvalue problem (
1.4
) and prove our main result about the existence of eigenpairs, Theorem
4.10
.

Preliminaries

In this section we present some preliminary definitions needed for the rest of the paper. The first subsection is well known and does not contain any new result the book
[
31
]
being the standard reference for the subject. The second subsection contains the definitions and basic results regarding fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. See for instance
[
23
]
where these spaces were introduced and
[
2
,
3
]
where several properties of these spaces were analyzed without requiring the
\Delta_{2}
–condition. The third subsection recalls the definition of complementary pairs introduced in
[

]
and the definition of the segment property, and construct a complementary pair in the context of fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. In the fourth subsection we recall an abstract result due to

.

2.1.
Young functions and Orlicz spaces

Let
M\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be an even, convex and continuous function, such that
M(t)>0
for
t>0
,
M(t)/t\to 0
as
t\to 0
and
M(t)/t\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
. Such a function
M
is called a
Young function
if it can be written as

M(t)=\int_{0}^{|t|}m(s)\,ds,

for
m\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
increasing, right continuous with
m(t)=0
if and only if
t=0
and
m(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
.

It will be helpful to extend the function
m
to the entire real line by oddness, that is
m(t)=\text{sign}(t)m(|t|)
.

We recall now some basic definitions on Orlicz spaces that can be found, for instance, in
[
31
]
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a bounded domain and let
\mu
be a Borel measure in
U
. The Orlicz class
{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(U,d\mu)
is defined as

{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(U,d\mu):=\left\{u\colon U\to{\mathbb{R}},\ \text{measurable}\colon\int_{U}M(u)\,d\mu<\infty\right\}.

The Orlicz space
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
is then defined as the linear hull of
{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(U,d\mu)
. It follows that
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
can be characterized as

L_{M}(U,d\mu)=\left\{u\colon U\to{\mathbb{R}},\ \text{$\mu-$measurable}\colon\int_{U}M\left(\frac{u}{k}\right)\,d\mu<\infty,\text{ for some }k>0\right\}.

This space is a Banach space when it is equipped, for instance, with the
Luxemburg norm
, i.e.

\|u\|_{L_{M}(U,d\mu)}=\|u\|_{M,U,d\mu}=\|u\|_{M,d\mu}:=\inf\left\{k>0\colon\int_{U}M\left(\frac{u}{k}\right)\,d\mu\leq 1\right\}.

A well-known and interesting fact is that
{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(U,d\mu)=L_{M}(U,d\mu)
if and only if
M
satisfies the so-called
\Delta_{2}
–condition, i.e.

(2.1)

M(2t)\leq CM(t),\quad\text{for }t\geq T.

Also, the Orlicz space
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
is separable if and only if
M
satisfies (
2.1
).

Next, we define the space
E_{M}(U,d\mu)
as the closure of bounded
\mu-
measurable functions in
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
, in the case
\mu(U)=\infty
the space
E_{M}(U,d\mu)
is the closure in
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
of bounded
\mu-
measurable functions with bounded support. It is easy to see that

E_{M}(U,d\mu)=\left\{u\colon U\to{\mathbb{R}},\ \text{$\mu-$measurable}\colon\int_{U}M\left(\frac{u}{k}\right)\,d\mu<\infty,\text{ for every }k>0\right\}.

Again,
E_{M}(U,d\mu)=L_{M}(U,d\mu)
if and only if
M
satisfies (
2.1
).

So, in general, we have

E_{M}(U,d\mu)\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(U,d\mu)\subset L_{M}(U,d\mu),

with equalities if and only if
M
satisfies (
2.1
).

Observe that
E_{M}(U,d\mu)
and
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
are Banach spaces and
{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(U,d\mu)
is a convex set.

Given a Young function
M
, we define its complementary function
\bar{M}
as

\bar{M}(t):=\sup\{\tau|t|-M(\tau)\colon\tau\geq 0\}.

Observe that
\bar{M}
is also a Young function and it is the optimal function in the Young inequality

(2.2)

\tau t\leq M(t)+\bar{M}(\tau),

for all
\tau,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Observe that equality in (
2.2
) is achieved if and only if
\tau=\text{sign}(t)m(t)
or
t=\text{sign}(\tau)\bar{m}(\tau)
where
\bar{m}(t)
is the derivative of
\bar{M}(t)
.

It follows directly from (
2.2
) that if
u\in L_{M}(U,d\mu)
and
v\in L_{\bar{M}}(U,d\mu)
, then
uv\in L^{1}(U)
and

\int_{U}|uv|\,d\mu\leq 2\|u\|_{M}\|v\|_{\bar{M}}.

This fact allows one to define in
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
the topology
\sigma(L_{M},L_{\bar{M}})
and it follows that
E_{M}(U,d\mu)
is dense in
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
in this topology.

It is easy to check that
\bar{\bar{M}}=M
. The Orlicz space
L_{\bar{M}}(U,d\mu)
is the dual space of
E_{M}(U,d\mu)
and so
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
is reflexive if and only if
M
and
\bar{M}
satisfy (
2.1
).

Finally, given
M
a Young function, we define

(2.3)

\text{Dom}(m):=\{u\in L_{M}(U,d\mu)\colon m(|u|)\in L_{\bar{M}}(U,d\mu)\}.

It can be checked that
E_{M}(U,d\mu)\subset\text{Dom}(m)\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(U,d\mu)
and hence,
\text{Dom}(m)=L_{M}(U,d\mu)
if and only if
M
satisfies (
2.1
). Moreover, the map
u\mapsto m(u)
from
E_{M}(U,d\mu)
to
L_{\bar{M}}(U,d\mu)
is continuous if and only if
\bar{M}
satisfies (
2.1
).

2.2.
Fractional order Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

In the product space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
we define the measure

d\nu_{n}:=\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Observe that this is a Borel measure and that if
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\setminus\Delta
is compact, then
\nu_{n}(K)<\infty
, where
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the diagonal
\Delta:=\{(x,x)\colon x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\}
.

We will consider two Orlicz spaces
L_{M}(U,d\mu)
. One with
U={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
d\mu=dx
(the Lebesgue measure) and another with
U={\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
and
d\mu=d\nu_{n}
.

We will use the notations

{\mathcal{L}}_{M}={\mathcal{L}}_{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},dx),\qquad L_{M}=L_{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},dx),\qquad E_{M}=E_{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},dx);

{\mathcal{L}}_{M}(\nu_{n})={\mathcal{L}}_{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},d\nu_{n}),\quad L_{M}(\nu_{n})=L_{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},d\nu_{n}),\quad E_{M}(\nu_{n})=E_{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},d\nu_{n}).

Now, given a fractional parameter
s\in(0,1)
, we introduce the notation for the
s-
Hölder quotient of a function
u\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

D^{s}u(x,y):=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}.

Then
D^{s}u\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\setminus\Delta\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Now, with all the notation introduced, the fractional order Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are defined as

W^{s}L_{M}:=\{u\in L_{M}\colon D^{s}u\in L_{M}(\nu_{n})\}

and

W^{s}E_{M}:=\{u\in E_{M}\colon D^{s}u\in E_{M}(\nu_{n})\}.

These spaces are naturally equipped with the norms

\|u\|_{s,M}=\|u\|_{M}+\|D^{s}u\|_{M,\nu_{n}}.

Also, these spaces can be isometrically identified as closed subspaces of
L_{M}\times L_{M}(\nu_{n})
and
E_{M}\times E_{M}(\nu_{n})
respectively using the map

u\mapsto(u,D^{s}u).

Now, given
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
a bounded open set, the space
W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega)
is then defined as the closure of
{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)
in
W^{s}L_{M}
with respect to the topology
\sigma(L_{M}\times L_{M}(\nu_{n}),E_{\bar{M}}\times E_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n}))
. By Poincaré’s inequality (see
[
23
, Corollary 6.2]
) in
W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
the quantity
\|D^{s}u\|_{M,\nu_{n}}
is equivalent to
\|u\|_{s,M}
. Therefore in
W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
we will consider the norm
\|u\|_{W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega)}=\|D^{s}u\|_{M,\nu_{n}}
.

The space
W^{s}_{0}E_{M}(\Omega)
is defined as the closure of
{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)
in
W^{s}E_{M}
in norm topology.

In order to define the dual spaces, we need to introduce the notion of fractional divergence. See
[
22
]
.

Given
F\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
, the fractional divergence of
F
is defined as

\displaystyle{\mathrm{div}}^{s}F(x)

\displaystyle:=\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{F(y,x)-F(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{F(y,x)-F(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy.

In
[
21
]
it is shown that if
F\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
, then
{\mathrm{div}}^{s}F\in(W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega))^{*}
and the following
fractional integration by parts formula
holds

(2.4)

\langle{\mathrm{div}}^{s}F,u\rangle=-\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}FD^{s}u\,d\nu_{n}.

So, we define the following spaces of distributions

W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega):=\{\phi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)\colon\phi=f+\mathrm{div}^{s}F\text{ with }f\in L_{\bar{M}},\ F\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})\}

W^{-s}E_{\bar{M}}(\Omega):=\{\phi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)\colon\phi=f+\mathrm{div}^{s}F\text{ with }f\in E_{\bar{M}},\ F\in E_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})\}.

Recall that since
E_{\bar{M}}
and
E_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
are separable then
W^{-s}E_{\bar{M}}(\Omega)
is also separable.

These spaces are endowed with the usual quotient norms,

\|\phi\|_{-s,\bar{M}}:=\inf\{\|f\|_{\bar{M}}+\|F\|_{\bar{M},\nu_{n}}\colon\phi=f+\mathrm{div}^{s}F\}.

2.3.
Complementary systems and segment property

In
[
18
,
19
]
the authors introduce the notion of complementary systems in order to work in spaces without the usual reflexivity assumption.

Let
Y
and
Z
be real Banach spaces with a pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon Y\times Z\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
Y_{0}\subset Y
and
Z_{0}\subset Z
be closed and separable subspaces. We say
(Y,Y_{0};Z,Z_{0})
is a complementary system if
Y_{0}^{*}=Z
and
Z_{0}^{*}=Y
(where equality is understood in the sense of a natural isometry via the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
).

The first natural example of a complementary system is
Y=L_{M}
,
Y_{0}=E_{M}
,
Z=L_{\bar{M}}
and
Z_{0}=E_{\bar{M}}
.

Observe that it is immediate to see that

(2.5)

\left(L_{M}\times L_{M}(\nu_{n}),E_{M}\times E_{M}(\nu_{n});L_{\bar{M}}\times L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n}),E_{\bar{M}}\times E_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})\right)

is also a complementary system.

In
[
29
]
the author provides with a general method to generate complementary systems from a previous one. More precisely

Lemma 2.1

(
[
29
]
, Lemma 1.2)
.

Given a complementary system
(Y,Y_{0};Z,Z_{0})
and a closed subspace
E
of
Y
, define
E_{0}=E\cap Y_{0}
,
F=Z/E_{0}^{\perp}
and
F_{0}=Z_{0}/E_{0}^{\perp}
.

Then, the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
between
Y
and
Z
induces a pairing between
E
and
F
if and only if
E_{0}
is
\sigma(Y,Z)
dense in
E
that is if
u\in E
then there exists
\{u_{m}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in E_{0}
such that
\langle u_{m},v\rangle\to\langle u,v\rangle
if
m\to\infty,\forall v\in Z
. In this case,
(E,E_{0};F,F_{0})
is a complementary system if
E
is
\sigma(Y,Z_{0})
closed, and conversely, when
Z_{0}
is complete,
E
is
\sigma(Y,Z_{0})
closed if
(E,E_{0};F,F_{0})
is a complementary system.

Using this Lemma, in
[
29
]
it is shown that

(W^{1}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega),W^{1}_{0}E_{M}(\Omega);W^{-1}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega),W^{-1}E_{\bar{M}}(\Omega))

is a complementary system when the domain
\Omega
satisfies the
segment property
.

See Section 8.1 in
[
17
]
for the definition of the segment property.

Using similar ideas, in
[
24
, Section 2.3]
it is shown that if
\Omega
satisfies the segment property, then

(2.6)

(W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega),W^{s}_{0}E_{M}(\Omega);W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega),W^{-s}E_{\bar{M}}(\Omega))

is also a complementary system.

3.
Nonlocal, quasilinear, anisotropic operators

In this section, we will define the operators that will be the subject of our investigation. These operators are natural extensions of the fractional order
m-
Laplace operator that were introduced in
[
23
]
. See also
[
24
]
.

To this end, let
a\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
a=a(x,y,\xi)
. On this function
a
we will impose the following conditions:

Conditions 3.1
.

We will assume from now on that
a\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies

•

(Caratheodory condition)

a(\cdot,\cdot,\xi)
is a measurable for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
a(x,y,\cdot)
is a continuous function for a. e.
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
.

•

a(x,y,-\xi)=-a(x,y,\xi),
for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a.e.
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
.

•

a(x,y,\xi)\xi>0
for a.e.
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
and all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
\xi\neq 0.

•

(Growth condition)
There exists a Young function
M
, a function
d\in E_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
and constants
b,c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that for a.e.
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
and all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}

(3.1)

|a(x,y,\xi)|\leq d(x,y)+b\bar{M}^{-1}(M(c\xi))

where
\bar{M}
is the complementary function of the Young function
M
.

•

(Monotonicity condition)
For a.e.
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
and all
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}

(3.2)

(a(x,y,\xi)-a(x,y,\xi^{\prime}))(\xi-\xi^{\prime})\geq 0.

Given a function
a\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
that satisfies conditions
3.1
, we define the operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}\colon\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})\to W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}
as

\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u(x):=-\mathrm{div}^{s}(a(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y))),

where
\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})=\{u\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)\colon a(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y))\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})\}
.

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}^{s}(a(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y)))

\displaystyle=-\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(a(y,x,D^{s}u(y,x))-a(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y)))\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle=2\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}a_{\text{sym}}(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y))\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle=2\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{|x-y|\geq\varepsilon}a_{\text{sym}}(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y))\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}},

where
a_{\text{sym}}
is the symmetric part of
a
given by

a_{\text{sym}}(x,y,\xi)=\frac{a(x,y,\xi)+a(y,x,\xi)}{2}.

Observe that
a_{\text{sym}}
also verifies conditions
3.1
whenever
a
does. So, without loss of generality, we may assume that
a
is symmetric, i.e.
a=a_{\text{sym}}
, in this way

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u(x)=2\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{|x-y|\geq\varepsilon}a(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y))\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}},

where the kernel
a
satisfies conditions
3.1
and is symmetric.

We observe that, by definition,
\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})\subset W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
. Let us now see that
W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)\subset\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0<s<1
and
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded open set and let
a
satisfies conditions
3.1
. Then, if
u\in W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
we have that
a(x,y,D^{s}u)\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
, then

(3.4)

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}M\left(|cD^{s}u|\right)\,d\nu_{n}<\infty,

for every constant
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

We claim that
b\bar{M}^{-1}(M(c|D^{s}u|))\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
. Indeed by (
3.4
)

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\bar{M}\left(\frac{b\bar{M}^{-1}(M(c|D^{s}u|))}{b}\right)\,d\nu_{n}=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}M(c|D^{s}u|)\,d\nu_{n}<\infty.

The proof concludes thanks to the growth condition (
3.1
).
∎

Next, from the properties of the fractional divergence
\text{div}^{s}
, we get the following result

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
0<s<1
be fixed and let
a
satisfies conditions
3.1
. Then for every
u\in\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})
, we have that
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u\in W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega)
. Moreover, for every
v\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
,

\langle\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u,v\rangle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n},

Proof.

Observe that if we denote
F(x,y)=a(x,y,D^{s}u(x,y))
, if
u\in\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})
then
F\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
. Therefore, the integration by parts formula (
2.4
), gives us

\langle\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u,v\rangle=\langle-\text{div}^{s}F,v\rangle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}FD^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n},

and the proof is complete.
∎

A very important property of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}
is the pseudomonotonicity. The following result is the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded and open domain that satisfies the segment property. Then, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}
is pseudomonotone. That is, If
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})
is a sequence such that it fulfills

(3.5)

\begin{cases}&u_{i}\to u\text{ for }\sigma(W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega),W^{-s}E_{\bar{M}}(\Omega))\\
&\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u_{i}\to f\in W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega)\text{ for }\sigma(W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega),W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega))\\
&\limsup_{i\to\infty}\langle\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u_{i},u_{i}\rangle\leq\langle f,u\rangle\end{cases}

then

(3.6)

\begin{cases}&u\in\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})\\
&\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u=f\\
&\langle\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u_{i},u_{i}\rangle\to\langle f,u\rangle\text{ if }i\to\infty.\end{cases}

The proof of Theorem
3.4
is divided into a series of lemmas for the reader’s convenience. It will be helpful to introduce the following notation: for a given function
u
we define the sets

R_{j}(u):=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\colon|(x,y)|\leq j\text{ and }|D^{s}u|\leq j\}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)\text{ and }v\in W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
respectevely. If
0<|\lambda|<1
then, for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}

a(x,y,D^{s}u+\lambda D^{s}v)D^{s}v,\quad a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}v\in L^{1}(R_{j}(u),d\nu_{n}).

Moreover

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\iint_{R_{j}(u)}a(x,y,D^{s}u+\lambda D^{s}v)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}=\iint_{R_{j}(u)}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
and
v\in W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
then using Young’s inequality we have

\displaystyle|a(x,y,D^{s}u+\lambda D^{s}v)D^{s}v|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{a(x,y,D^{s}u+\lambda D^{s}v)}{2b}\right|\left|2bD^{s}v\right|

\displaystyle\leq M(2bD^{s}v)+\bar{M}\left(\frac{a(x,y,D^{s}u+\lambda D^{s}v)}{2b}\right),

where
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
is given by the growth condition (
3.1
).

Since
v\in W^{s}_{0}E_{M}(\Omega)
it follows that
M(2bD^{s}v)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},d\nu_{n})
and by growth condition we have in
R_{j}(u)

\displaystyle\bar{M}\left(\frac{a(x,y,D^{s}u+\lambda D^{s}v)}{2b}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\bar{M}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{2b}+\frac{\bar{M}^{-1}(M(c(D^{s}u+\lambda D^{s}v))}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\bar{M}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{b}\right)+M(c(|D^{s}u|+|D^{s}v|)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\bar{M}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{b}\right)+M(cj+c|D^{s}v|)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\bar{M}\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{b}\right)+\frac{1}{2}M(2cj)+\frac{1}{2}M(2c|D^{s}v|)\right),

using the fact that
d\in E_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
and
v\in W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
the right hand side in the last inequality is integrable in
R_{j}(u)
. We observe that the last inequality is valid uniformly in
\lambda
.

Finally, using the fact

a(x,y,D^{s}u+\lambda D^{s}v)D^{s}v\to a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}v,

as
\lambda\to 0
a.e. in
R_{j}(u)
and the dominated convergence theorem we conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If there exists
u\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
and
\Phi\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
such that

(3.7)

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}(a(x,y,W)-\Phi)(W-D^{s}u)\,d\nu_{n}\geq 0,

for all
W\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},d\nu_{n})
with compact support then
a(x,y,D^{s}u)=\Phi\in(W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega))^{*}
that is

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\Phi D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}\qquad\forall v\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
w\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
be such that
D^{s}w\in L^{\infty}(R_{l}(u),d\nu_{n})
. Taking
l\geq j
we define

W:=D^{s}w\chi_{R_{j}(u)}-D^{s}u\chi_{R_{j}(u)}+D^{s}u\chi_{R_{l}(u)}=D^{s,j}w-D^{s,j}u+D^{s,l}u,

then using
W
in (
3.7
)

(3.8)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}(a(x,y,D^{s,j}w-D^{s,j}u+D^{s,l}u)-\Phi)(D^{s,j}w-D^{s,j}u+D^{s,l}u-D^{s}u)\,d\nu_{n}\geq 0.

The left hand side on (
3.8
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}(a(x,y,D^{s,j}w-D^{s,j}u+D^{s,l}u)-\Phi)(D^{s,j}w-D^{s,j}u)\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s,j}w-D^{s,j}u+D^{s,l}u)(D^{s,l}u-D^{s}u)\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle-\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\Phi(D^{s,l}u-D^{s}u)\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle=I+II+III.

The first integral
I
is zero outside
R_{j}(u)
, the second integral
II
is zero, and
III
integral tends to zero if
l\to\infty
. Hence, letting
l\to\infty
, we obtain

\iint_{R_{j}(u)}\left(a(x,y,D^{s}w)-\Phi\right)(D^{s}w-D^{s}u)\,d\nu_{n}\geq 0.

Now let
v\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
, using Lemma
3.5
with
\lambda>0
, first with
w=u+\lambda v
and then
w=u-\lambda v
, we have

\iint_{R_{j}(u)}\left(a(x,y,D^{s}u)-\Phi\right)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}=0\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega).

Taking the limit
j\to\infty
, we have

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left(a(x,y,D^{s}u)-\Phi\right)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}=0\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega),

and by the density,
\forall v\in W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
. Using the density of
W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)\subset W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
with respect to the
\sigma(L_{M}\times L_{M}(\nu_{n}),L_{\bar{M}}\times L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n}))
topology, we conclude the proof.
∎

Now we are in position to prove the pseudomonotonicity.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Let
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})
and
f\in W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega)
such that (
3.5
) is satisfied.
We must prove that
u=\lim u_{i}
and
f
satisfy (
3.6
).

Let us first prove that the sequence
\{a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
remains bounded in
L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
, indeed let
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},d\nu_{n})
with compact support, by the monotonicity condition (
3.2
) we have

(3.9)

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}(a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})-a(x,y,V))(D^{s}u_{i}-V)\,d\nu_{n}\geq 0,

which implies

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})V\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})D^{s}u_{i}\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle-\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,V)D^{s}u_{i}\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,V)V\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle=I+II+III,

the first integral remains bounded by (
3.5
), the convergence
u_{i}\to u
implies that the second integral is uniformly bounded in
i
and the last integral is independent of
i
, therefore there exists a constant
C
, independent of
i
, such that

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})V\,d\nu_{n}\leq C,

for all
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},d\nu_{n})
with compact support and therefore for all
V\in E_{M}(\nu_{n})
, then by the uniform boundedness principle we have
\|a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})\|_{L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})}\leq C
uniformly in
i
and so there exists a subsequence that we still denote by
\{a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\Phi\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
such that
a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})\to\Phi
in
\sigma(L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n}),E_{M}(\nu_{n}))
, using the above convergence and the fact
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u_{i}\to f\in W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega)
for
\sigma(W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega),W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega))
we have

\displaystyle\langle f,v\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\langle\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u_{i},v\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\Phi D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n},

for all
v\in W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
. This formula together with the density of
W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
in
W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
with respect to the
\sigma(L_{M}\times L_{M}(\nu_{n}),L_{\bar{M}}\times L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
) topology allows us to extend
v
to the space
W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
, that is

(3.10)

\langle f,v\rangle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\Phi D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}\qquad\forall v\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega).

Now taking limit in (
3.9
) we obtain

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}(\Phi-a(x,y,V))(D^{s}u-V)\,d\nu_{n}\geq 0,

for all
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},d\nu_{n})
with compact support and so by Lemma
3.6
we have
a(x,y,D^{s}u)=\Phi
in
(W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega))^{*}
, that is
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u=f
.

On the other hand putting
u_{i}=u
in (
3.9
), using
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u=f
and (
3.10
) we have

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)V\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\Phi D^{s}u\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle-\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,V)D^{s}u\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,V)V\,d\nu_{n},

\displaystyle<\infty,

for all
V\in E_{M}(\nu_{n})
that is
a(x,y,D^{s}u)\in L_{\bar{M}}(\nu_{n})
and so
u\in\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})
.

Finally, we prove that

\langle\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u_{i},u_{i}\rangle\to\langle f,u\rangle\quad\text{for }i\to\infty.

Let

L=\liminf_{i\to\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})D^{s}u_{i}\,d\nu_{n},

we only need to prove that
L\geq\langle f,u\rangle
.

Using the monotonicity property again we have

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left(a(x,y,D^{s}u_{i})-a(x,y,D^{s,j}u)\right)(D^{s}u_{i}-D^{s,j}u)\,d\nu_{n}\geq 0,

where
D^{s,j}u=D^{s}u\chi_{R_{j}(u)}
. Taking the limit in
i
and rewriting, we obtain

L\geq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s,j}u)(D^{s}u-D^{s,j}u)\,d\nu_{n}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s,j}u\,d\nu_{n}.

In
R_{j}(u)
the factor
D^{s}u-D^{s,j}u=0
, and in
R_{j}(u)^{c}
the factor
a(x,y,D^{s,j}u)=a(x,y,0)=0
, so the first integral in the above inequality is zero. Therefore

L\geq\iint_{R_{j}(u)}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}u\,d\nu_{n},

for arbitrary
j
, then

L\geq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}u\,d\nu_{n}=\langle f,u\rangle.

This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.
The eigenvalue problem

In this section we analyze the eigenvalue problem

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s}u=\lambda g(x,u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where
a(x,y,\xi)
verifies conditions
3.1
and, moreover, we also assume the
coercitivity condition

(4.2)

a(x,y,\xi)\xi\geq\theta M(c\xi),\quad\text{for every $\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}$ and a.e. $(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n},$}

for some constants
\theta,c>0
.

On the source term
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
we will assume that it is an odd and continuous function such that
g(t)>0
for all
t>0
and that it verifies the growth condition

(4.3)

|g(x,t)|\leq e(x)+a_{1}\bar{M}^{-1}(a_{2}M(a_{3}t))\qquad\forall t\geq 0,

where
a_{1},a_{2}
and
a_{3}
are positive constants and
e(x)\in L_{\bar{M}}
.

Next, we define the notion of eigenpair for (
4.1
):

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
(\lambda,u)
is an eigenpair for (
4.1
) if
u\in\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})
and

(4.4)

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}g(u)v\,dx,

for all
v\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
.

Recall that
\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})\subset W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega)\subset E_{M}(\Omega)
and therefore the right hand side of (
4.4
) is well defined. In fact,

\int_{\Omega}|g(u)||v|\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}e(x)|v|\,dx+a_{1}\int_{\Omega}\bar{M}^{-1}(a_{2}M(a_{3}|u|))|v|\,dx.

The first integral is well defined by Young’s inequality (
2.2
) and the second one, using again Young’s inequality it is bounded by

ka_{2}\int_{\Omega}M(a_{3}|u|)\,dx+k\int_{\Omega}M\left(\frac{|v|}{k}\right)\,dx<\infty.

In order to study problem (
4.1
) we define the functionals

{\mathcal{A}}^{s}\colon\text{Dom}({\mathcal{A}}^{s})\subset W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega)\to{\mathbb{R}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{G}\colon W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega)\to{\mathbb{R}}

as

{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(u):=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}A(x,y,D^{s}u)\,d\nu_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{G}(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx,

respectively, where
A(x,y,t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(x,y,\tau)\,d\tau,G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau
and

\displaystyle\text{Dom}({\mathcal{A}}^{s}):=\{u\in W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(u)<\infty\}.

A similar argument as the one used to check the well definition of (
4.4
) gives us that
\mathcal{G}
is a well defined functional for every
u\in W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega)
.

We observe that
W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)\subset\text{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{a}^{s})\subset\text{Dom}({\mathcal{A}}^{s})\subset W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
, and the functions
{\mathcal{A}}^{s}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
vanish only at zero. These inclusions are a direct consequence of the point wise inequality

A(x,y,\xi)\leq\xi a(x,y,\xi).

Let
B=\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots\}\subset{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)
be a countable linearly independent subset in
W_{0}^{s}E_{M}(\Omega)
such that the linear hull is norm dense, and we define the sets
V
and
V_{k}
as the linear hull of the sets
B
and
B_{k}:=\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{k}\}
respectively. We denote the continuous pairing between
W^{-s}L_{\bar{M}}(\Omega)
and
W^{s}_{0}E_{M}(\Omega)
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and the one between
V_{k}
and
(V_{k})^{*}
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{k}
.

A straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle\langle({\mathcal{A}}^{s})^{\prime}(u),v\rangle_{k}

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}v\,d\nu_{n}

\displaystyle\forall u,v\in V_{k},

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g(u)v\,dx

\displaystyle\forall u,v\in V_{k},

for each
k=1,2,\dots,
and the fact
V_{k}\subset W^{s}_{0}E_{M}(\Omega)
allows us to conclude
{\mathcal{A}}^{s},{\mathcal{G}}\in C^{1}(V_{k})
.

By the growth condition (
4.3
) and the compact immersion
W^{s}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega)\subset\subset E_{M}(\Omega)
(see Theorem B.3 in
[
24
]
), it follows that
\text{Dom}({\mathcal{G}})=W_{0}^{s}L_{M}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}(u_{k})

\displaystyle\to{\mathcal{G}}(u)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}\rangle_{k}

Lemma 4.2

a)

b)

c)

d)

Proof.

The proof of a) has been observed before.

The item b) is consequence of the uniform continuity theorem.

We prove now item c). Let

with

We observe that

{\mathcal{A}}^{s}(ru)=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}A(x,y,r|D^{s}u|)\,d\nu_{n}

On the other hand, for

Therefore, we obtain

Using now (

when

Item d) is consequence of item c). Indeed the map

Finally to prove item e) we observe, by the monotonicity of

(4.6)

A(x,y,\xi)=\int_{0}^{\xi}a(x,y,\tau)\,d\tau\leq a(x,y,\xi)\xi

Finally, if

using (

1={\mathcal{A}}^{s}(u)=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}A(x,y,D^{s}u)\,d\nu_{n}\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}a(x,y,D^{s}u)D^{s}u\,d\nu_{n},

this implies
