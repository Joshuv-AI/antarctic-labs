Association of PPARγ (rs1801282) and TCF7L2 (rs7903146) gene polymorphisms and susceptibility of Type 1 diabetes mellitus in Egyptian children
Authors: Noheer Hesham Mahmoud, Nehal E Abdel-Hakem, Randa Abd El Aziz Ghanoum, Ayat Ibrahim Ghanem, Yasser BM Ali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractType 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) is caused by the autoimmune depletion of β-cells. Non-HLA genes notably peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) and transcription factor 7-like 2 (TCF7L2)-important regulators of insulin sensitivity and secretion-may affect risk even though HLA loci predominate in heredity. However, their synergistic impact in T1DM are yet unknown, especially in Egyptian children. So, PPARγ (Pro12Ala, rs1801282 C/G) and TCF7L2 (rs7903146 C/T) polymorphisms were examined in relation to T1DM risk in Egyptian children. Ninety-five normal subjects and one hundred T1DM patients were included. The amplification-refractory mutation system-polymerase chain reaction (ARMS-PCR) utilized to genotype these single nucleotide polymorphisms. T1DM patients exhibited higher HbA1c (11.09 ± 2.46% vs. controls 5.04 ± 0.18%; P < 0.001), while there were no the demographic disparities across the groups (P > 0.05 for age, sex, and BMI). There was a strong correlation found between T1DM and the PPARγ rs1801282 variant. The T1DM group had a significantly greater heterozygous CG genotype (10.0%) than controls (1.1%) (OR = 10.44; P = 0.01). Furthermore, patients had a considerably higher minor G allele frequency (12.0% vs. 6.0%; OR = 2.22; P = 0.04), suggesting a greater risk to this condition. Reciprocally, the CC genotype provided protection from T1DM (OR = 0.32; P = 0.03). The TCF7L2 rs7903146 variant, on the other hand, did not show any significant discrepancies in the groups' genotype or allele frequencies. TCF7L2 rs7903146 fails to rise predisposition to T1DM in Egyptian children, however the PPARγ rs1801282 CG genotype and G allele do. Such outcomes point to a possible involvement of PPARγ in the pathophysiology of T1DM.Keywords: Type 1 diabetes, PPARγ, TCF7L2, Polymorphism, SNP, Children, Egyptian

Introduction

Type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) is a chronic, complex autoimmune disease distinguished by T-cell-mediated depletion of insulin-producing pancreatic β-cells, which ensues in complete insulin insufficiency and potentially fatal hyperglycemia.1, 2 The disease occurs due to complicated interplay between environmental stimuli and genetic susceptibility; the human leukocyte antigen (HLA) region on chromosome 6p21 contributes to approximately half of the genetic risk, while non-HLA loci such as insulin gene (INS), polymorphic cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA4), and interleukin 2 receptor alpha (IL2RA) contribute the remaining 57 confirmed risk regions.3, 4 T1DM can appear at any age, despite its traditional origin in childhood or adolescence. Its prevalence is rising worldwide, with an expected 9 million cases, affecting over 1.5 million children. About 500,000 emerging cases of T1DM were diagnosed globally in 2024, and the number of cases is rising annually, underscoring the necessity of proactive risk surveillance using autoantibodies and genetic testing to reduce morbidity and promote preventative therapies.5, 6

The biological structure of diabetes mellitus (DM) is multifaceted, that includes both autoimmune and metabolic abnormalities.7 Although the autoimmune demise of pancreatic β-cells, which results in total lack of insulin, remains the typical description of T1DM, there is increasing evidence that genetic variables which are typically linked to resistant insulin and β-cell impairment in type 2 diabetes (T2DM) could additionally impact the clinical manifestation and onset of T1DM.8 One particular gene is peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ), found on chromosome 3p25, a valid polymorphism predictor for lipid metabolism disorders.9 The PPARγ is a ligand-activated nuclear receptor, the induction of which may boost peripheral tissues' sensitivity to insulin, which in turn increases muscle absorption and glucose consumption.10 A frequent functional polymorphism in this gene, rs1801282 C/G, causes the PPARγ2 isoform's codon 12 (Pro12Ala) to change from proline to alanine.11 The minor (Ala) allele was unexpectedly linked to enhanced insulin sensitivity and a lower incidence of T2DM, although such missense mutation lowers the transcriptional activity of the receptor.12 Despite this, previous studies established that PPAR-γ2 rs1801282 is associated with insulin resistance in children as well as adults without diabetes, and this may be further altered by circulating lipids.13

The transcription factor 7-like 2 (TCF7L2) gene is thought to be the most significant locus for T2DM risk, as opposed to the metabolic pathways controlled by PPARγ.14 The product of the TCF7L2 gene, found on chromosome 10, is a downstream effector plays a role in the canonical Wnt/β-caternin signaling pathway, which, in addition to being essential for pancreatic islet development and synthesis and release of insulin, is additionally associated to various cancer forms.15, 16, 17 The most extensively researched variation in this gene is the intronic single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) rs7903146 C/T. The T-risk allele considerably raises the risk of T2DM in a variety of races because it is linked to decreased insulin production, a diminished incretin impact, and poor proinsulin conversion.18, 19, 20, 21 Although TCF7L2 polymorphisms do not raise the incidence of islet autoimmunity, research indicated that they may modify the course of T1DM.22 In particular, the TCF7L2 rs7903146 risk allele has been linked to higher retained C-peptide levels at the onset of T1DM and a slower progression from single to multiple autoantibodies, implying that it may affect the rate of β-cell dysfunction in the context of autoimmune attack.23

To what we know, meanwhile, no one has simultaneously examined the combined role of the PPARγ rs1801282 (Pro12Ala) and TCF7L2 rs7903146 polymorphisms in T1DM patients. Therefore, we aimed to determine if TCF7L2 (rs7903146 C/T) and PPARγ (Pro12Ala, rs1801282 C/G) polymorphisms are associated with the likely risk of or protection from T1DM in a cohort of Egyptian children.

Subject and methods

Study populations

A total of 100 T1DM patients–38 males and 62 females–were recruited for this study from the National Institute of Diabetes and Endocrinology (NIDE), Cairo, Egypt. Individuals with specific types of diabetes prompted by other ailments or those with other forms of diabetes, such as T2DM, are not eligible. Additionally, people with several autoimmune diseases–including colitis, allergic responses, pneumonia, rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), and inflammatory bowel disease–are disregarded. As controls, 95 individuals without diabetes (47 males and 48 females) with no clinical signs or a family history of autoimmune illnesses were employed. Both groups have the same race and socioeconomic standing. In accordance with the guidelines for clinical research authorized by the Health and Human Ethical Clearance Committee (IDE 00318), the participants gave informed approval for genetic test after being recruited.

Sampling and DNA isolation

All study subjects–patients and controls alike–were subjected to a physical screening which entailed weight and height measurements in order to calculate their body mass index (BMI). Everyone involved had five milliliters of venous blood drawn, which was then put into two sterile vacutainer tubes containing ethylene diamine tetra-acetic acid (K3 EDTA). One of the tubes was applied to measure hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), and the second was used to isolate genomic DNA utilizing the ABT Genomic DNA Mini Extraction Kit (Applied Biotechnology, Cat. No. Ex01, Egypt) in accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines. To confirm the presence and quality of the extracted DNA, 1% agarose gel electrophoresis was performed. A NanoDrop (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.) was used to measure the DNA purity and concentration of each sample.

Genotyping of PPARγ rs1801282 C/G and TCF7L2 rs7903146 C/T polymorphisms

The PPARγ (Pro12Ala rs1801282 C/G) and TCF7L2 rs7903146 C/T SNPs were genotyped by performing Amplification-Refractory Mutation System-Polymerase Chain Reaction (ARMS-PCR). All PCR reactions were performed in SimpliAmp thermal cycler (Applied Biosystems, ThermoFisher Scientific Inc.). PCR reaction mixtures were ABT 2× Red Master Mix (Applied Biotechnology), 10 pmoles of each primer, and 150 ng of template DNA. The PCR cycling condition was as follows: one cycle at 95 °C for 4 min and then 35 cycles of denaturing (at 95 °C for 40 s), annealing for 30 s (59 °C for PPARγ rs1801282 and 63 °C for TCF7L2 rs7903146), extension (at 72 °C for 35 s), and a final extension step at 72 °C for 7 min.

The primers for PPARγ rs1801282 C/G SNP have the following sequences; forward (C allele) 5’-CTCTGGGAGATTCTCCTATTGACC-3′, forward (G allele) 5’-CTCTGGGA GATTCTCCTATTGACG-3′ and reverse 5’-GCACCACTTGGTCCTACACTCT-’3.24 The following primers for TCF7L2 rs7903146 C/T SNP were used, forward (C allele) 5’-GAACAATTAGAGAGCTAAGCACTTTTTAGA(G)AC-3′, forward (T allele) 5’-GAACAATTAGAGAGCTAAGCACTTTTTAGAGAT-3′ and reverse 5’-AGATGAAATGTAGCAGTGAAGTGC-’3.21 The obtained PCR products were resolved on 3% agarose gel electrophoresis and seen utilizing a UV transilluminator under the gel documentation system.

Results

A total of 195 participants–100 in the T1DM group and 95 in the control group–were enrolled in this study. Baseline characteristics of the study population showed that no statistically significant differences in age (10.03 ± 5.82 vs. 11.12 ± 3.13 years; P = 0.10) or sex distribution (47/48 M/F vs. 38/62 M/F; P = 0.11) between controls and T1DM patients. Patients with T1DM were diagnosed at an average age of 22.04 ± 2.91 months. While BMI was comparable [TIDM patients (18.39 ± 4.43 kg/m2) vs. controls (18.05 ± 3.25 kg/m2); P = 0.54], T1DM patients had significantly higher HbA1c levels [TIDM patients (11.09 ± 2.46%) vs. controls (5.04 ± 0.18%); P < 0.001], consistent with disease hyperglycemia. With no significant confounding variations in age, sex, or BMI between the two groups, our results confirm the expected normoglycemic status of the control group and the typical hyperglycemia in the T1DM group. Furthermore, in the absence of demographic confounders, such clinical characteristics provide the necessary background for the interpretation of the uncovered genetic associations (Table 1).

In this study, the investigation of PPARγ rs1801282 C/G gene polymorphism showed that the T1DM and control groups differed significantly in both genotype and allele frequencies. The T1DM group had a higher frequency of the heterozygous CG genotype (10.0%) than controls (1.1%), and the risks of T1DM were considerably higher (OR = 10.44, 95% CI: 1.31–83.25, P = 0.01). In contrast, the CC genotype was linked to a lower incidence of T1DM (OR = 0.32, 95% CI: 0.12–0.87, P = 0.03) and was less prevalent in the T1DM group (83.0%) compared to controls (93.7%). There was a difference in percentages in the GG genotype across the groups, but it is not statistically significant. At the allele level, the G allele was substantially more prevalent in T1DM patients (12.0% vs. 6.0%; OR = 2.22, 95% CI: 1.05–4.67; P = 0.04), suggesting a possible link to T1DM susceptibility (Table 2).

The TCF7L2 rs7903146 polymorphism reported no statistically significant association with T1DM under any of the tested genetic models (individual genotypes or combined models). There were no statistically significant differences in the distribution of genotypes between the T1DM and control groups. The T1DM group had a slightly higher frequency of the CT genotype (21.0%) than the controls (16.8%), although this difference was not statistically significant (OR = 1.31, 95% CI: 0.63–2.70, P = 0.47). The T1DM group did not have the TT genotype, and only one control participant did; consequently, statistical comparison for this specific genotype was not performed. Allelic comparisons did not find any significant differences (p > 0.05) (Table 2).

The Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) was assessed for the genotype frequencies of the two polymorphisms, PPARγ rs1801282 C/G and TCF7L2 rs7903146 C/T, in the controls and T1DM group. The Chi-Square estimate for the PPARγ rs1801282 C/G polymorphism was 18.5, and the associated P < 0.001 indicated noticeable deviations from HWE, with observed heterozygote frequencies were significantly lower than expected in controls and T1DM patients [1(10.36) and 10(21.12), respectively]. On the other hand, the genotype distributions for TCF7L2 rs7903146 C/T polymorphism adhered to HWE in both the control and T1DM groups, with no statistically significant deviations observed P > 0.05 (Table 3).

The gene-gene interaction study between PPARγ rs1801282 and TCF7L2 rs7903146 and the risk of T1DM was shown in Table 5, which compares 100 T1DM patients with 95 controls. The PPARγ rs1801282 (GG + CG) risk genotypes paired with TCF7L2 rs7903146 CC wild genotype shows a noteworthy pattern. It was prevalent in T1DM patients (14.0%) compared to controls (6.3%), resulting in over two-fold higher odds of T1DM (OR = 2.59, 95% CI: 0.94–7.12), however, this failed to get statistical relevance P = 0.07. On the other hand, comparable rates between the patients (18.0%) and controls (17.9%) were obtained when the wild genotype CC for PPARγ rs1801282 was paired with the mutant genotypes (TT + CT) for TCF7L2 rs7903146 SNP. The probability of developing T1DM was not substantially impacted by this interaction (OR = 1.17, 95% CI: 0.56–2.47, P = 0.67). In terms of genotype combinations, 3.0% of the T1DM group had both the TCF7L2 rs7903146 (TT + CT) and PPARγ rs1801282 (GG + CG) variations, while similar combinations were not seen in the control group. Since there were no observed cases in the control group, no statistical analysis was done for this combination. As a whole, none of the interaction models reached statistical significance at P > 0.05. When PPARγ rs1801282 (GG + CG) and TCF7L2 rs7903146 (CC) genotypes were combined, the odds ratio was 2.59 (95% CI: 0.94–7.12, P = 0.07). This combination did not reach statistical significance, despite a numerical tendency to higher risk. These results underscore the necessity of additional research in bigger cohorts to ascertain whether this particular genetic combination has an epistatic impact on the predisposition to T1DM.

Discussion

The T1DM is an incurable autoimmune condition that primarily causes the selective death of pancreatic β-cells due to T-cell-mediated autoimmunity associated with HLA genes.25 PPARγ, a ligand-activated nuclear receptor that is widely expressed in adipose tissue, offers a convincing biological link between the two main types of diabetes due to its critical role in immune regulation and glucose homeostasis.26 Indeed, TCF7L2 SNPs have been linked to distinct clinical features in T1DM, such as a lower frequency of high-risk HLA genotypes, a greater likelihood of single autoantibody positivity, and a milder metabolic presentation at diagnosis.23 These findings support the ‘double diabetes’ scenario, suggesting that non-autoimmune, T2DM-associated pathways may contribute to reaching the threshold for clinical hyperglycemia in these patients.27 Although these genes' contributions in T2DM are well-established, research is still ongoing to determine how they contribute to T1DM susceptibility. We subsequently aimed to ascertain the possible association between the PPARγ (rs1801282) and TCF7L2 (rs7903146) gene polymorphisms and the risk of T1DM in the Egyptian children.

This study looked at the relationship between T1DM risk and the PPARγ rs1801282 C/G polymorphism. Our findings demonstrated a significant association, suggesting that this variation may contribute to the metabolic landscape associated with the T1DM. Specifically, we discovered that the heterozygous CG (OR = 10.44, 95% CI: 1.31–83.25, P = 0.01) genotype and the G (OR = 2.22, 95% CI: 1.05–4.67, P = 0.04) allele were significantly more prevalent in T1DM patients and associated with an increased risk of disease, while the CC genotype appeared to play a protective function. According to Holm et al.,28 this study supports the idea that PPARγ variations can impact the severity of islet autoimmunity and the genetic propensity to T1DM. The lower transcriptional activity of the variant PPARγ gene due to the proline-to-alanine mutation in codon 12 is probably the underlying basis for this interplay.29 In keeping with several previous studies, such decreased activity is thought to impede insulin signaling and hinder pancreatic islet β-cell function, resulting in a metabolic milieu that may contribute to the development of the diabetes mellitus.30, 31, 32 Meta-analyses that associate the PPARγ rs1801282 G allele with higher probability of obesity and hypercholesterolemia—factors that potentially exacerbate cardiovascular risk in diabetic patients—further indicate the involvement of this polymorphism in metabolic dysfunction.33 Although it was studied in other forms of diabetes, no research has looked at the PPARγ gene polymorphism rs1801282 C/G in T1DM, and our findings align with an abundance of research that link the PPARγ rs1801282 C/G polymorphism to a higher risk of gestational diabetes and T2DM.34, 35, 36, 37, 38 Conversely, some studies39, 40 have linked the PPARγ rs1801282 C/G variant to better insulin sensitivity and a lower risk of T2DM, it is crucial to note that these results are probably population-specific, reflecting differences in environmental exposures and genetic backgrounds. Our findings should therefore be understood in light of this, since they emphasize the diversity of genetic influences among various populations rather than necessarily contradicting these earlier publications. While other studies have not found any association.41, 42 This heterogeneity may be explained by different effects of the PPARγ gene among racial, gender, and age groups.

The TCF7L2 rs7903146 polymorphism's role in diabetes is still being thoroughly studied, especially in relation to its capacity to link the pathogenic mechanisms of T1DM and T2DM. No statistically notable relationships for the TCF7L2 rs7903146 polymorphism had been found in the current investigation. There were no statistically significant differences in the distribution of genotypes and alleles between T1DM and control groups. Our results appeared in line with a number of extensive studies22, 43, 44 that indicated the TCF7L2 is primarily a T2DM locus and could not heighten the probability of T1DM in various populations. For instance, Grant22 pointed out that although the TCF7L2 locus is the greatest common locus for T2DM, it is not generally linked to T1DM. According to Winkler et al.,43 children with T2DM-susceptible genotypes failed to have a higher chance of acquiring islet autoantibodies or a greater likelihood of progression to T1DM. Similarly, TCF7L2 genotypes did not significantly affect insulin sensitivity or secretory indices in individuals vulnerable for T1DM. This suggests that whatever metabolic effects associated with this gene could be obscured by the preclinical β-cell dysfunction that precedes T1DM.44 On the other hand, some research has emphasized the importance of the TCF7L2 rs7903146 T allele and TT genotype in particular diabetes complications. They underlined that TCF7L2 is the most crucial genetic locus for T2DM, and that the T allele is closely associated with microvascular problems and disease susceptibility.45, 46

While our work identified no overall relationship with T1DM, which is possibly related to the relatively low frequency of the risk allele, the TT genotype was not present in this study population, and Redondo et al.44 observed that even in bigger cohorts, the TT homozygous risk variant frequently only arises in around 8% of participants. A small sample size might not have enough power to identify an effect if the TT genotype, rather than the CT genotype, is the primary cause of the risk. Additionally, a previous study has pointed out that environmental variables (e.g., high BMI and physical inactivity) frequently interact with T2DM-associated genes like TCF7L2.46 As well, the impact of TCF7L2 might be exclusive to age groups or particular autoantibodies (such as GADA).47 Due to the relatively limited number of participants in both groups, we were unable to classify the children based on age, which may have obscured relationships that only exist in particular T2DM-like sub-phenotypes of T1DM. Therefore, we investigated T1DM as a homogeneous clinical entity rather than further stratifying the cohort.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this work is the first to show that the PPARγ rs1801282 G allele raises the risk of T1DM in Egyptian children, expanding its known function in T2DM to the pathophysiology of autoimmune diabetes. These results demonstrate PPARγ gene being a unique non-HLA genetic regulator linking metabolic and immunological imbalance in T1DM, despite the lack of connection with TCF7L2 rs7903146. To completely figure out how those and other metabolic genes may alter the initiation and course of autoimmune diabetes in various clinical features, prospective investigations including broader, properly characterized cohorts sorted by disease progression, and autoantibody status are required.
