Network Attractors driven by Time-Delay Plasticity Preprint: APS/123-QED
Stefan Ruschel, Emanuil Hristov, Hil G.E. Meijer, Stephen Coombes, Rachel Nicks

Abstract
We develop a framework for collective frequency selection and attractor formation by means of delay plasticity. Specifically, we consider adaptive axonal delays (AADs), motivated by activity-dependent myelination in the brain which regulates signal propagation speeds and thus communication delays. We demonstrate frequency selection and explosive network relaxation oscillations in systems of delay-coupled phase oscillators with AADs on brain connectivity data and fully coupled ring networks.



Real-world complex networks remain difficult to understand and control as topology and time delays jointly determine dynamics
Atay et al. 2004
.
Brain activity unfolds across multiple spatial and temporal scales, posing fundamental challenges for linking neuron-level dynamics, population activity, network structure and function
Rabinovich et al. 2012
. While synaptic plasticity, a central source of complexity, modifies interaction strengths in response to presynaptic activity, plasticity may also act on the timing of interactions. In particular, activity-dependent myelination mediated by oligodendrocytes
Noori et al. 2020
;
Scholz et al. 2009

changes signal propagation speeds thereby giving rise to adaptive axonal delays (AADs), which play an important role in neural processing and computation and pose a direction of inquiry complementary to current efforts in studying evolving coupling structure
Berner et al. 2023
;
Holme and Saramäki 2012
.

To study the dynamical consequences of delay plasticity, we consider an idealization of population activity to phase models
Ashwin et al. 2016
;
Breakspear et al. 2010
, such as the Kuramoto model, which are suited for analyzing collective dynamical phenomena
Pikovsky et al. 2001
. Such phase description is particularly convenient because oscillator frequency carries the notion of spike rate and, hence, population activity. We employ a phase description that explicitly retains interaction delays
Novičenko and Pyragas 2012
, rather than approximation by effective phase lags
to capture how delays affect emergent frequencies and multistability between frequencies
Yeung and Strogatz 1999
.

Considering the effects of time delays on brain dynamics
Wu et al. 2022

explicitly can potentially explain additional dynamical complexity, from pattern formation to chaos
Yanchuk and Giacomelli 2017
. In systems with multiple time scales, time delays have been shown to generate canards
Krupa and Touboul 2016
;
Campbell et al. 2009
,
intermittent chaotic behavior
Ruschel and Yanchuk 2017
,
and purely delay-induced relaxation oscillations
Ruschel and Yanchuk 2019
. In systems of coupled phase oscillators, time delays have been shown to induce (explosive) synchronization
Schöll et al. 2009
;
Peron and Rodrigues 2012
, promote chimera states
Omel’chenko et al. 2010
;
Sethia et al. 2008
, and, when adaptive, enable mode selection
and adiabatic switching between attractors
Klinshov and Nekorkin 2024
;
Karimian et al. 2019
;
Selivanov et al. 2012
. These attractor-switching dynamics are analogous to network attractors in heteroclinic and excitable networks previously proposed as models for transitions between brain states
Ashwin et al. 2024
.

In this Letter, we establish that adaptive axonal delays enable the selection of, and recurrent transitions between, collective network dynamics, including synchrony, phase-locked states, clusters, and chimera states. We demonstrate the concept by (A) an AAD plasticity rule motivated by white matter plasticity acting on phase oscillators on brain connectome data, and (B) a mechanistic delay-adaptation rule on a regular network permitting rigorous analysis.

Summary and outlook.

Time-delay plasticity provides a mechanism for shaping collective behavior in adaptive networks that is distinct from, and complementary to, synaptic plasticity. Here we have shown that AADs can generate network attractors in the form of explosive relaxation oscillations, revealing an intrinsically nonlinear route by which adaptive delays organize collective activity. More broadly, adaptive state-dependent delays can create, select, and stabilize collective rhythms, suggesting mechanisms that may extend to experimentally derived rules for white-matter plasticity.

Several directions follow naturally. First, extending the present neural-mass and phase-reduced descriptions to higher-order phase reductions
Nicks et al. 2024
could capture regimes beyond weak coupling. Second, spiking-neuron models with biologically realistic myelination dynamics, resource constraints, spatial coupling kernels, and heterogeneity in initial conditions, intrinsic frequencies, or adaptation rates should clarify how delay plasticity controls collective states
Coombes and Wedgwood 2023
. In the strong-coupling regime, delay-coupled excitable or spiking units can exhibit collective spiking patterns ranging from traveling waves to bursts
Klinshov et al. 2015
, motivating extensions of master-stability theory for traveling waves
Ruschel and Giraldo 2025
to delay-coupled systems. Finally, extending mean-field reduction techniques
Bick et al. 2020
to adaptive delays offers a route toward low-dimensional descriptions of collective states.

The rigorous analysis of delay-induced relaxation oscillations induced by time-delay plasticity requires
further development of infinite-dimensional Fenichel theory
Bates et al. 1998
, including blow up techniques. Relevant nonlinear mechanisms include slow passage through Hopf bifurcations, canards, and mixed-mode oscillations
Baer et al. 1989
;
Avitabile et al. 2020
;
Desroches et al. 2012
.

End Matter

.1
Transverse stability of phase-locked states

We consider phase-locked (twisted) states of a delay-coupled ring governed by the frequency-locking condition

\Omega=\omega+\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}\,h\!\left(j\psi-\tau_{j}\Omega\right),

(9)

(
w_{ij}=\tilde{w}_{j}
, omitting tildes) with

\psi=\frac{2\pi l}{N},\qquad l=0,\dots,N-1,

(10)

and distance-dependent delays

\tau_{j}=\tau_{1}d_{j},\qquad d_{j}:=\min\{j,N-j\}.

(11)

The corresponding
l
-twisted state is given by
\theta_{i}(t)=\Omega t+i\psi
.

Linearizing about the twisted state and exploiting translational invariance, the system is diagonalized by the discrete Fourier transform. Writing perturbations as

\delta\theta_{i}(t)=\eta_{n}(t)e^{{\rm i}q_{n}i},\qquad q_{n}=\frac{2\pi n}{N},\qquad n=0,\dots,N-1,

(12)

each (Bloch) component evolves independently. Introducing the coefficient functions

c_{j}(\Omega,\tau_{1}):=\sigma w_{j}\,h^{\prime}\!\left(j\psi-\Omega\tau_{1}d_{j}\right),

(13)

the
n
-th mode satisfies the scalar delay equation

\dot{\eta}_{n}(t)=a_{n,0}\,\eta_{n}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{n,j}\,\eta_{n}(t-\tau_{j}),

(14)

with

a_{n,0}=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}(\Omega,\tau_{1}),\qquad a_{n,j}=c_{j}(\Omega,\tau_{1}){\rm e}^{{\rm i}q_{n}j}.

(15)

The corresponding characteristic equation reads

\lambda+\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q_{n}j}e^{-\lambda\tau_{j}}=0.

(16)

The mode
n=0
satisfies

a_{0,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{0,j}=0,

(17)

and hence
\lambda=0
is always an eigenvalue. This neutral mode reflects global phase-shift symmetry and is excluded from the transverse stability analysis.

We now characterize delay-independent stability of all nontrivial modes. Consider the twisted state defined by Eq. (
9
). Excluding the symmetry mode
n=0
, the equilibrium is absolutely stable with respect to all perturbation wavenumbers
n=1,\dots,N-1
and all delays
\tau_{j}\geq 0
if and only if, for each
n\neq 0
, the scalar equation (
14
) satisfies the absolute stability conditions of Corollary 7 of Ref. [42]. In particular, since
a_{n,0}\in\mathbb{R}
, a sufficient and sharp condition is

c_{j}(\Omega,\tau_{1})\geq 0\qquad\text{for all }j=1,\dots,m,

(18)

together with the nondegeneracy condition

\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}(\Omega,\tau_{1})\bigl(e^{{\rm i}q_{n}j}-1\bigr)\neq 0\qquad\text{for all }n=1,\dots,N-1.

(19)

For each fixed
n
, Eq. (
14
) is a scalar delay differential equation of the form

\dot{x}(t)=a_{0}x(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}x(t-\tau_{j}),

(20)

with coefficients given by (
15
). Absolute stability of such equations is characterized by Corollary 7 of Ref.
Yanchuk et al. 2022
. Since
a_{n,0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the relevant condition is

a_{n,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}|a_{n,j}|\leq 0,\qquad a_{n,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{n,j}\neq 0.

(21)

Using
|a_{n,j}|=|c_{j}|
, the first inequality becomes

-\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}|c_{j}|\leq 0,

(22)

which holds if and only if
c_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
. The second condition yields (
19
). This establishes the claim.

The stability condition (
18
) translates directly into a constraint on the locked frequency and delays:

\sigma w_{j}\,h^{\prime}\!\left(j\psi-\Omega\tau_{1}d_{j}\right)\geq 0\qquad\forall j.

(23)

For attractive coupling (
w_{j}\geq 0
,
\sigma>0
), this reduces to

h^{\prime}\!\left(j\psi-\Omega\tau_{1}d_{j}\right)\geq 0\qquad\forall j.

(24)

Thus, absolute transverse stability is guaranteed when all delayed interaction channels lie on a locally attractive branch of the coupling function.

Differentiating Eq. (
9
) with respect to
\tau_{1}
yields

\frac{d\Omega}{d\tau_{1}}=-\frac{\sigma\Omega\sum_{j=1}^{m}d_{j}w_{j}h^{\prime}\!\left(j\psi-\Omega\tau_{1}d_{j}\right)}{1+\sigma\tau_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}d_{j}w_{j}h^{\prime}\!\left(j\psi-\Omega\tau_{1}d_{j}\right)}.

(25)

Under the sign condition (
18
) and assuming the denominator in (
25
) is positive, one obtains

\frac{d\Omega}{d\tau_{1}}<0.

(26)

Hence, a negative continuation slope emerges naturally within the absolutely stable regime, although it is not by itself a necessary condition for stability.
