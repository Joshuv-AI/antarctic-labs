A study of postoperative pathologic complete response (pCR) following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in limited-stage small cell lung cancer (LS-SCLC)
Authors: Wang Chunmao, Han Ming, Wang Zitong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This retrospective study evaluated the efficacy and safety of neoadjuvant chemotherapy-immunotherapy combined with radical resection in 7 patients with limited-stage small cell lung cancer (LS-SCLC). Patients received a median of 3 cycles of neoadjuvant therapy with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor-based regimens (etoposide combined with cisplatin/carboplatin), followed by surgical resection and adjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. Imaging and biomarker assessments demonstrated significant tumor regression: 3 patients achieved partial response (PR) and 3 achieved complete response (CR) per RECIST criteria. Pathological examination confirmed complete pathological response (pCR) in all 7 patients. All patients successfully underwent R0 resection with low perioperative complication rates and no perioperative mortality. At median follow-up of 42 months, 6 patients maintained durable disease-free survival, while one patient developed left supraclavicular lymph node and cerebral metastases 16 months post-surgery, resulting in death 3 months after diagnosis. Treatment-related adverse events were manageable, predominantly mild to moderate in severity, with no treatment-related deaths. The findings suggest that neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy significantly induces tumor regression, enhances resectability, and improves long-term survival in LS-SCLC patients, though larger-scale clinical trials are needed to validate these results and optimize therapeutic strategies for this aggressive malignancy.

Introduction

Lung cancer has become the most common malignant tumor globally in recent years, and it is also the leading cause of cancer-related deaths [1]. Small cell lung cancer (SCLC), accounting for approximately 15% of all lung cancer cases, is characterized by rapid growth and extensive early metastasis [1]. The overall survival (OS) rate of limited-stage SCLC (LS-SCLC) patients is only 30–35% [2]. Moreover, surgery took approximately 5% of patients with SCLC exhibit early-stage disease (T1,2N0M0) experience a more favorable prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate reaching up to 50% [3, 4].

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Guidelines strongly recommend surgical resection for stage I SCLC patients meeting appropriate staging criteria. The standard treatment protocol for these patients includes lobectomy combined with systematic mediastinal lymphadenectomy [5]. Recent studies have demonstrated that patients with early stage(T1,2N0M0) SCLC who undergo surgery followed by adjuvant chemotherapy experience significantly longer survival compared to those who undergo surgery alone or receive concurrent chemoradiotherapy [6]. However, for LS-SCLC beyond T1-2N0M0, concurrent or sequential chemoradiotherapy remains standard therapy [7, 8]. Although most patients initially respond well to the therapy, disease recurrence remains relatively common (60–80% within 2 years), and the management of recurrent cases continues to pose significant challenges [9].

In recent years, the surgical treatment paradigm for SCLC has undergone a progressive shift from conservative to proactive approaches, with certain stage II-IIIA patients now demonstrating eligibility for surgical resection [3, 10, 11]. The emergence of immunotherapy has significantly improved the survival of local advanced NSCLC patients, especially neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy has shown a pathological remission rate of 40–87%, indicating the potential to improve postoperative survival in early-stage NSCLC and reduce recurrence rates [12]. Checkmate 816 study showed that at a median follow-up of 68.4 months, the 5-year overall survival rate was 65.4% with the combination of nivolumab and chemotherapy, compared to 55.0% with chemotherapy alone [13]. In exploratory analyses, the 5-year overall survival in the nivolumab-plus-chemotherapy group was 95.3% among the patients who achieved a pathological complete response [13]. The emergence of immunotherapy has changed the treatment landscape of SCLC over the past 30 years. Median OS for immunotherapy-combination regimens ranges from 12.3 to 15.4 months in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) patients [14–16]. The ADRIATIC III trial demonstrated adjuvant therapy with durvalumab led to significant survival benefits, with a 57% three-year OS rate compared to placebo. The median progression-free survival (PFS) was 16.6 months for durvalumab versus 9.2 months for placebo. The risk of death was reduced by 27% (HR = 0.73), with consistent results observed in the Chinese subgroup (OS HR = 0.71, PFS HR = 0.67) [17].

There have been no reports on the results of neoadjuvant immunotherapy clinical studies in LS-SCLC, and it is currently unknown whether the combined chemotherapy mode can improve patients’ PFS and subsequently prolonged OS. This article presents seven cases of LS-SCLC in which patients achieved a pathological complete response(pCR) following treatment with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and subsequent radical surgery. These cases are of great significance and we anticipate that they will contribute valuable treatment insights for LS-SCLC in the future.

Methods

Patient selection and data collection

In this study, total twenty patients with LS-SCLC who received neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy followed by surgery in our department from July 2020 to April 2025 were included for analysis.All participants underwent comprehensive multidisciplinary discussion prior to treatment and demonstrated strong willingness to receive neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy combined with surgical resection. This study strictly adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, with all protocols formally approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Beijing Chest Hospital Affiliated to Capital Medical University (Ethical Approval No. 2025-NO.28).

Participants were required to meet the following conditions: a histopathologically confirmed diagnosis of LS-SCLC without prior treatment; aged above 18 years; clinical staging of II-IIIA (T1-4N0-2M0) based on the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system; absence of metastatic disease as verified through comprehensive imaging modalities (PET/CT or contrast-enhanced computed tomography of the thorax and abdomen, brain MR, and bone scintigraphy); only patients with pathologically confirmed solitary N2 mediastinal lymph node metastasis are eligible for enrollment; those with multifocal N2 metastases detected by PET-CT or endobronchial ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (EBUS) are excluded. Individuals presenting with any of the following characteristics were ineligible: documented interstitial lung disease; autoimmune disorders necessitating systemic corticosteroid therapy; seropositivity for human immunodeficiency virus; chronic hepatitis B infection; previous solid organ or bone marrow transplantation; and active pulmonary tuberculosis.Finally, seven patients obtained pCR after surgery were enrolled.

All seven patients received a median of three cycles (range: 2–3 cycles) of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, which consisted of intravenous PD-1(Serplulimab, Tislelizumabs)or PDL1(Atezolizumabs, Durvalumabs) inhibitors combined with chemotherapy (etoposide plus cisplatin, EP) administered every three weeks, followed by surgical intervention. Subsequently, patients underwent a median of three cycles (range: 2–4 cycles) of adjuvant chemoimmunotherapy post-surgery. Following six cycles of chemoimmunotherapy, all patients received maintenance immunotherapy alone for one year, accompanied by regular evaluations, including chest and abdominal CT scans, tumor marker assessments, and brain MRI, conducted every three months.The treatment responses of tumors were classified into complete remission (CR), partial remission (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease (PD) according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors RECIST, version 1.1. All adverse events will be assessed using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) version 5.0. The adverse events will be recorded during the follow-up period.

The study included twenty patients with LS-SCLC who underwent neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgical R0 resection in our department from July 2020 to April 2025. In Twenty patients who underwent surgery, three patients achieved complete response (CR), fifteen patients achieved partial response (PR), and two patients achieved stable diseases (SD) after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. The objective response rate (ORR) was 90.0% (95% CI: 68.3% -98.8%). In most patients who achieved an objective response, the response was observed at the first planned evaluation, which was 6–8 weeks after the initiation of treatment. The twenty patients who underwent surgery achieved pathological response, including seven (7/20;35.0%) patients who achieved pathological complete response (PCR), twelve (12/20; 60.0%) patients who achieved major pathological response (MPR, and eight (8/20; 40.0%) patients who did not achieve major pathological response (non-MPR). Among non-MPR patients, two cases achieved lesion PCR, with residual cancer in the lymph nodes.

The clinicopathological characteristics of patients who achieved pathological complete response are summarized in Table 1. The median age of patients was 59 years with ranged from 37 to 74 years. Three patients had a significant smoking history, with smoking indices of 30, 60 and 50, while the remaining four patients had no history of smoking. The pre-treatment imaging tumor size ranged from 2.4 cm to 8.4 cm. Pro-gastrin-releasing peptide (ProGRP), a biomarker for SCLC, was elevated in 6 patients (154.93–940.80 pg/ml). According to AJCC 8th edition TNM staging, the initial clinical stages were: cIIB (T1cN1M0), cIIIA (T1cN2M0), cIIIA (T4N0M0), cIIB (T2bN1M0), cIIIB (T4N2M0), cIIB (T3N0M0), and cIIB (T2bN1M0). Post-treatment imaging evaluation showed: three patients (cIIB, cIIIA, cIIB) achieved PR with tumor reduction to 0.9–1.6 cm, downstaged to IA. One cIIB patient showed SD. Three patients (cIIIA, cIIIB, cIIB) attained CR with T0N0M0 staging and no residual lesions on imaging (Fig. 1). ProGRP levels significantly decreased in all 6 patients (17.86–69.26 pg/ml).

Surgical information and postoperative complications

VATS, video-assisted thoracic surgery; ARDS, acute respiratory distress syndrome

Surgical resection was performed on all seven patients following neoadjuvant therapy (Table 2). The median time between the last administration of chemoimmunotherapy and radical resection was 45 (range 28–62) days. The extent of surgery included lobectomy in one patient (14.3%), bilobectomy in four patients (57.1%), and pneumonectomy in two patients (28.6%). Sleeve resection/bronchoplasty was not required for any patient.The surgical methods employed were video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) in three patients (42.9%) and thoracotomy in four patients (57.1%). There were no conversions from VATS to thoracotomy. The median chest tube duration was 3 days (range 3–9 days).

Postoperative complications were minimal. All patients were discharged from hospital within 8 median days (range 6–15 days) after surgery without severe surgical complications. There were no instances of intraoperative blood transfusion, death within 30 or 90 days, heart failure, respiratory failure, or acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). One patient developed pneumonia (14.3%).

The adverse event profile of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in SCLC appears manageable, with most toxicities being mild to moderate in severity (Table 3). Hematologic toxicities such as neutropenia and leukopenia are notable, requiring close monitoring. Immune-related adverse events like hypothyroidism and skin rashes suggest the impact of immunotherapy components. While severe (Grade 3–4) events are infrequent, they do occur—particularly neutropenia and rash, warranting vigilance and timely intervention. No Grade 4 events were recorded except for one case of Grade 4 neutropenia, which underscores the importance of supportive care and dose adjustments when necessary.

Discussion

The management of LS-SCLC remains a major clinical challenge due to its aggressive nature, rapid progression, and early metastatic potential [18]. Despite advances in systemic therapies, the prognosis for patients with LS-SCLC remains poor. Even with aggressive treatments such as concurrent chemoradiotherapy, the OS remains only 16.1%–27.7% [19]. The role of surgery in locally advanced SCLC has been a topic of debate, particularly for patients with stage III disease. Historically, surgery has been reserved for patients with early-stage disease (T1-2N0), with concurrent chemoradiotherapy being the standard of care for more advanced stages [20]. However, recent studies have suggested that surgery may offer survival benefits even in locally advanced SCLC, particularly when combined with neoadjuvant therapy [21].

The integration of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) into the treatment paradigm, particularly in the neoadjuvant setting, has shown promise in improving outcomes for patients with SCLC. The concept of neoadjuvant therapy in SCLC is not new, but the addition of immunotherapy has revolutionized the approach. Historically, neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone has been associated with modest pCR rates, ranging from 5.8% to 17.3% [22, 23]. However, recent studies have confirmed that ICIs combined with chemotherapy can significantly improve pCR rates, potentially revolutionizing treatment paradigms for LS-SCLC.

In a multicenter single-arm study by Duan et al., the neoadjuvant therapy regimen of atezolizumab combined with etoposide/platinum-based chemotherapy demonstrated remarkable efficacy: 61.5% PCR rate was achieved in the per-protocol (PP) population, with 47.1% response rate in the intention-to-treat (ITT) population [24].Furthermore, the recently published phase II LungMate-005 trial demonstrated that neoadjuvant anti-PD-L1 (TQB2450) therapy combined with chemotherapy is feasible and effective for stage I–III SCLC. Notably, this study reported a pathological complete response (pCR) rate of 42.9% and a major pathological response (MPR) rate of 61.9% in the surgical cohort [25]. These findings establish a new therapeutic benchmark for resectable SCLC through preoperative immunotherapy combination strategies. The findings demonstrated particularly significant improvements compared to historical chemotherapy-alone pCR rates. The LungMate-005 trial further corroborated these results, showing that patients who underwent surgery after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy achieved impressive 24-month event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS) rates of 61.9% and 85.7%, respectively [25]. The observed ultra-high pCR and MPR rates suggest that neoadjuvant immunotherapy not only enhances surgical resectability but may also exert positive effects on long-term survival prognosis.

Similarly, a retrospective study by Lu et al. evaluated treatment outcomes in 6 patients with LS-SCLC receiving neoadjuvant durvalumab combined with chemotherapy [26]. Despite no cases achieving pCR or MPR, all patients demonstrated partial pathological response with residual viable tumor (RVT) proportions ranging from 25% to 80%. These findings suggest the immune microenvironment may play a critical role in determining treatment efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy for small cell lung cancer. As a breakthrough therapy for LS-SCLC, neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy aims to enhance surgical resectability through tumor downstaging. The study confirmed significant tumor regression and clinical stage improvement following neoadjuvant therapy, with both radiological tumor volume reduction and decreased ProGRP levels indicating robust antitumor activity. pCR was achieved in 7 patients, further highlighting the potential of neoadjuvant therapy to induce deep tumor remission(Fig. 2).

In Duan et al.‘s study, 13 out of 17 treated patients underwent surgical resection, achieving a 92% (15/16) R0 resection rate [24]. Similarly, in the LungMate-005 trial, all patients who proceeded to surgery achieved R0 resection, and the surgical rate for patients with N1 disease was 100% [25]. Analysis of the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy-chemotherapy combination followed by surgical treatment in LS-SCLC demonstrates: High R0 resection rate combined with significant pCR and major MPR rates indicate that neoadjuvant immunotherapy-chemotherapy can enhance surgical feasibility for LS-SCLC patients with poor prognosis and limited treatment options. Our surgical outcomes further support the feasibility of this approach. The majority of patients underwent bilobectomy, and the absence of sleeve resection/bronchoplasty suggests that neoadjuvant therapy may facilitate more straightforward surgical procedures. The low rate of postoperative complications and absence of perioperative mortality highlight the safety of this approach.

The safety profile of neoadjuvant immunotherapy-chemotherapy in LS-SCLC) remains a critical consideration given the disease’s aggressive nature and potential immune-related adverse events (irAEs). In the study by Duan et al., 58.8% of patients exhibited grade 3 or higher adverse events (AEs), with 17.6% specifically experiencing irAEs [24]. In contrast, the LungMate-005 trial reported a 47.5% incidence of grade 3–5 AEs, with most treatment-related AEs being haematological toxicities; notably, the most common TQB2450-specific AE was grade 1–2 rash [25]. Lu et al. conducted a cohort study involving six patients receiving neoadjuvant durvalumab combined with chemotherapy, with no severe toxic effects observed [26]. All patients underwent timely surgical resection without significant postoperative complications. The adverse event profile of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy for LS-SCLC demonstrated good controllability, with most toxicities being mild-to-moderate. Special attention should be paid to hematologic toxicities (including neutropenia and leukopenia) requiring close monitoring. Hypothyroidism and rash as irAEs suggested the influence of immunotherapy components. These findings align with results from NSCLC studies [27, 28], where neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy has been shown to be safe and feasible, with respectively low rates of surgical complications.

Moreover, the LungMate-005 trial identified potential biomarkers for treatment response. It was demonstrated that high expression of PRSS8 was associated with poor effectiveness of immunochemotherapy and worse survival outcomes [25]. Additionally, molecular analyses revealed dynamic changes in the tumor microenvironment (TME), including a shift towards the SCLC-I subtype and neuroendocrine state transitions (from ASCL1/NEUROD1 to YAP1 dominant subtypes) following neoadjuvant therapy [25]. These translational insights provide a biological rationale for the observed clinical responses and highlight potential targets for future therapeutic stratification.

Limitations and future directions

Despite encouraging recent findings, several critical limitations must be emphasized: Firstly, the small sample size (n = 7) may compromise the generalizability of conclusions; Secondly, the specific type of neoadjuvant therapy (chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or combination regimens) was not clearly defined, precluding stratified analysis of efficacy and safety profiles; Finally, mechanistic studies on biomarkers predicting response to immunotherapy-chemotherapy combinations remain insufficient in SCLC populations, warranting dedicated investigation.

Future research should prioritize the following directions: First, systematic screening of predictive biomarkers to guide treatment decisions is essential. Second, multi-center clinical trials are needed to optimize the timing of administration, treatment cycles, and sequential regimens for neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy. Third, long-term follow-up systems based on real-world data should be established, with a focus on evaluating key endpoints such as 3-year PFS rates and 5-year OS rates. In terms of therapeutic strategy innovation, Phase III randomized controlled trials combining dual immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., PD-1/PD-L1 with CTLA-4 inhibitors) and targeted therapies (e.g.,anti-angiogenic agents or epigenetic modulators) are recommended [29]. Concurrently, exploration of synergistic effects between tumor microenvironment modulators (e.g.,TIGIT inhibitors, LAG-3 inhibitors) and neoadjuvant regimens should be prioritized [19]. Furthermore, the development of AI-driven dynamic efficacy prediction models and personalized treatment pathway design will be critical breakthroughs for enhancing translational efficiency.

Conclusion

In conclusion, neoadjuvant therapy demonstrates potent antitumor activity in patients with LS-SCLC, leading to significant tumor regression, improved clinical staging, and favorable surgical outcomes. The safety profile appears acceptable, with manageable AEs. Further research with larger sample sizes and detailed treatment information is warranted to validate these findings and optimize therapeutic strategies for this aggressive malignancy.
