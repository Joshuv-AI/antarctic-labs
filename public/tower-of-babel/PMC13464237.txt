Significant Left Atrial Appendage Leak After LAA Exclusion System Occluded by ASD Closure Device
Authors: Peyton Wall, Jack E. Simmons, Usman A. Hasnie, Mark A. Law, Garima Arora, Mouhamed Amr Sabouni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIncomplete left atrial appendage (LAA) closure (“leak”) is a challenge of LAA occlusive techniques and carries thromboembolic risk. Closure of significant leaks after surgical AtriClip is rare and less described.Case SummaryAn 81-year-old man with atrial fibrillation on apixaban, prior LAA AtriClip (2023), and multiple recent falls underwent transesophageal echocardiography that showed a residual severe central LAA ostium. Percutaneous ostia closure was performed using an atrial septal defect (ASD) plug via transseptal puncture approach. Residual flow ceased in the LAA, and anticoagulation was halted and transitioned to clopidogrel on discharge.DiscussionApproach to LAA leak closure is guided by size and morphology of the leak. In significant central leak, ASD occluders show superiority in long-term occlusion vs other methods.Take-Home MessageClosure with ASD occluders is a promising long-term solution for significant LAA leak after AtriClip, and intervention requires extensive multimodal imaging.

History of Presentation

An 81-year-old man presented to our cardiology department for evaluation of anticoagulation discontinuation. He was on apixaban in the setting of a complex history of atrial fibrillation and stroke at the preference of his previous cardiologist. Initial transesophageal echocardiogram (TEE) demonstrated a residual central leak, so anticoagulation was continued. After the patient began having hypotensive episodes with multiple falls and associated extremity hematomas, the decision was made to pursue percutaneous closure of the leak and ultimately discontinue anticoagulation.Take-Home Messages•Closure with ASD occluders is a promising solution for significant central LAA leak after surgical AtriClip in patients wishing to discontinue anticoagulation.•Procedural planning and intervention is reliant on effective and extensive multimodal imaging techniques to correctly characterize the leak and identify the most appropriate closure device.

Past Medical History

The patient had a history of ischemic cardiomyopathy status post cardiac resynchronization therapy with a defibrillator, left middle cerebral artery stroke status post thrombectomy in 2023, and atrial fibrillation on apixaban status post left atrial appendage (LAA) surgical AtriClip in 2023.

Differential Diagnosis

Differential diagnosis included residual LAA stump, AtriClip failure, and residual leak, including an eccentric and/or central leak. It was necessary to better characterize the leak to choose an appropriate closure technique, and further advanced imaging and invasive diagnostics were pursued.

Investigations

As stated previously, initial TEE demonstrated an LAA AtriClip in place with a small opening to the LAA and subsequent spontaneous contrast in the LAA. There was no thrombus visualized in the LAA. Otherwise, estimated left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was 55% to 60%; right ventricular ejection fraction was grossly normal; and mild mitral, tricuspid, and pulmonic regurgitation were present. Cardiac computed tomography angiography further characterized the leak with estimated dimensions of 15 × 11 mm, a total 124 sq mm (Figure 1A-C). Computed tomography angiography also showed marked biatrial enlargement, 3.5 cm dilated pulmonary artery, 4.2 cm dilated ascending aorta, and again absence of an LAA thrombus. A 3-dimensional intraprocedural TEE was performed before and after atrial septal defect (ASD) occluder device placement to further characterize ostium dimensions and guide intervention. Preprocedure, there was redemonstration of a surgically ligated LAA via AtriClip with a centrally located residual communication measuring 10 × 7 mm and spontaneous contrast within the LAA (Figure 2A, Video 1). The left atrium was severely dilated. The interatrial septum was seen bowing into the right atrium, consistent with elevated left atrial pressure. Management

The patient underwent percutaneous ostia closure with an implantable ASD occlusion device. The patient was brought to the cath lab and placed under general anesthesia. A TEE probe was placed for intraprocedural imaging. Access was obtained via a 6-F micropuncture needle in the right femoral vein. Intraprocedural heparin was administered. An 18-F Baylis wire and sheath were then used under TEE guidance to perform transseptal puncture. After, the sheath was exchanged for an Agilis sheath. Fluoroscopy with contrast injection demonstrated contiguous contrast blush into the LAA (Figure 3A, Video 2). The remnant opening of the LAA was crossed, and a 10-mm Abbott ASD closure device was placed (Figures 2B and 2C, Video 3). Repeat Doppler imaging showed total resolution of flow into the appendage. Repeat fluoroscopy demonstrated appropriate positioning of the closure device (Figure 3B). The delivery sheath was removed, and Perclose suture devices were used to achieve complete hemostasis. The patient was taken to recovery and remained hemodynamically stable. Apixaban was discontinued, and the patient was discharged the same day on clopidogrel.Figure 3Cardiac Catheterization Fluoroscopy Images(A) Fluoroscopy with contrast injection showing LAA leak (yellow arrow) and prior surgical clip (blue arrow). Outcome and Follow-Up

At a 1-month follow-up clinic appointment, the patient reported that he was doing well without cardiac concerns or recurrent falls. A 1-month follow-up TEE showed the occluder device well seated in the LAA without leakage across the atrial clip/occluder device. LVEF and right ventricular systolic function were normal at that time. He is currently scheduled for 6-month follow-up TEE and doing well.

Discussion

Surgical AtriClip is an epicardial approach to LAA occlusion that offers high durability but is not without risk for various postprocedure leaks.1 Central LAA leak is a rare complication after surgical AtriClip and not well defined within the current literature. In the “LAA Leak Study” by Charate et al,2 of the 160 patients enrolled in the study population, 6 had a leak after AtriClip, and all leaks were occluded using vascular plugs.2 Vascular plugs have long been established for effective occlusion of LAAs and similar structures before the advent of Watchman devices.3 However, as opposed to the central leak in our patient, nearly all of the Vascular plug cohort had a leak characterized as a large residual stump, a complication more common after AtriClip closure and related to the natural anatomy of an LAA opening. Both of these types of leak are the ideal candidates for Vascular plug (or similar device, including ASD occluder) therapy based on the characteristics and dimensions of the leaks.2,4 On the contrary, eccentric (peri-device) leaks are anatomically more suited to radiofrequency ablation or coiling.2 As with our patient, extensive analysis of the leak orientation, size, and morphology using multimodal imaging is imperative in selecting the most appropriate closure technique to ensure the highest chance of long-term occlusion.

Reliability of long-term occlusion is vital, as oftentimes the goal of undergoing these procedures is discontinuation of anticoagulation. In a study of 76 patients with postoperative atrial arrhythmia after AtriClip placement, 33 patients had reassessment of LAA occlusion, with 21% of them having indications for continued anticoagulation.5 Given this, it is reasonable to assume that patients may benefit from continued imaging surveillance for residual flow after LAA leak occlusion. Any residual flow poses a risk for thromboembolic stroke in the setting of discontinued anticoagulation.

Although outcomes concerning ASD occlusion of central leak after AtriClip are not as well defined as other leak/treatment combinations, it is reasonable to assume that the effective long-term occlusion exhibited by the use of vascular plugs in other epicardial LAA closure techniques (ie, surgical ligation or LARIAT) is translatable to the AtriClip population. Occlusion rates of 100% at 1-year follow-up have been described with the use of vascular plugs for incomplete LAA closure.2 On the contrary, leak closure with ASD occluder devices is not well-described in the literature and remains a promising solution for significant LAA leak given their similar structure and properties to vascular plugs. However, further investigation is warranted to better characterize this particular combination of LAA occlusion and leak occlusion, especially for long-term successful occlusion in the setting of discontinued anticoagulation.

Conclusions

Central LAA leak after surgical AtriClip is an uncommon complication, and closure with ASD occluders is a promising solution. Choosing and implementing appropriate interventions requires extensive assessment using multimodal imaging techniques, often in tandem. Postprocedure imaging surveillance should be considered to ensure persistent leak occlusion, especially in the setting of anticoagulation discontinuation.
