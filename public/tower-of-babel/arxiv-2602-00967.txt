Preliminaries

2.1
Moment Functionals and Sequences

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
, and let
s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
be a sequence.
We call
s
a
K
-moment sequence
, if there exists a measure
\mu
with
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\subseteq K
and

s_{\alpha}=\int_{K}x^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Let

L:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}

be a linear functional.
We call
L
a
K
-moment functional, if there exists a measure
\mu
with
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\subseteq K
and

L(p)=\int_{K}p(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)

for all
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

2.2
Frechét and LF-Spaces

A
Fréchet space
is a metrizable, complete, and locally convex topological vector space.
For our purposes it is sufficient to have the following example.

Example 2.1

(see e.g.
[
11
, pp. 91–92, Ex. III]
)
.

The vector space

\mathds{C}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]

of formal power series is a Fréchet space with the family

\{p_{d}\}_{d\in\mathds{N}_{0}}

of semi-norms

p_{d}(f)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|\leq d}|c_{\alpha}|

of
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}
.

\circ

Clearly,

\mathds{C}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]\cong\mathds{C}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}

and hence the space
\mathds{C}^{\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
of complex sequences is a Frechét space.

The vector space
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a LF-space
[
11
, p. 130, Ex. I]
.
The general definition of a LF-space can be found e.g. in
[
11
, p. 126]
or
[
10
, Ch. II, 6.4]
.
For our purposes it is sufficient to understand that a sequence of polynomials
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
converges to some
p\in\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, i.e.,
p_{i}\to p
as
i\to\infty
, if and only if
\sup_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\deg p_{i}<\infty
and each
x^{\alpha}
-coefficient of
p_{i}
converges to the
x^{\alpha}
-coefficient of
p
.

Consequently, the spaces
\mathds{C}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]
and
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
are dual to each other via

\langle f,p\rangle:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}\cdot p_{\alpha}

for

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}\in\mathds{C}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]\quad\text{and}\quad p=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}p_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}\in\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

The interested reader can find more about Fréchet and LF-spaces in e.g.
[
11
,
10
]
.

2.3
Regular Frechét Lie Groups

Definition 2.2

(see e.g.
[
8
, p. 63, Dfn. 1.1]
)
.

We call
(G,\,\cdot\,)
a
regular Fréchet Lie group
if the following conditions are fulfilled:

(i)

G
is an infinite dimensional smooth Fréchet manifold.

(ii)

(G,\,\cdot\,)
is a group.

(iii)

The map
G\times G\to G
,
(A,B)\mapsto A\cdot B^{-1}
is smooth.

(iv)

The
Fréchet Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is isomorphic to the tangent space
T_{e}G
of
G
at the unit element
e\in G
.

(v)

\exp:\mathfrak{g}\to G
is a smooth mapping such that

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\exp(tu)\right|_{t=0}=u

for all
u\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(vi)

The space
C^{1}(G,\mathfrak{g})
of
C^{1}
-curves in
G
coincides with the set of all
C^{1}
-curves in
G
under the Fréchet topology.

The interested reader can find more on infinite dimensional manifolds, differential calculus, Lie groups, and Lie algebras in e.g.
[
8

3
Representations of Linear Operators on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

To study linear operators on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, we have at first look at how they look like, i.e., we deal in this section with the different kinds of representations.
At first we have the
canonical representation
.

Theorem 3.3

(folklore,
canonical representation
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be linear.
Then, for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
, there exist unique polynomials
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}.

(1)

A proof of the proceeding result can be found e.g. in
[
7
]
.
With the representation (
1
), especially degree preserving maps, i.e.,

T\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}

for all
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, can easily be characterized, as we see in the next result.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
5
, Lem. 5.1]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\quad\text{with}\quad T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

be linear with unique
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}
for all
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

(ii)

\deg q_{\alpha}\leq|\alpha|
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

A second representation can be view as a consequence or a version of the Schwartz Kernel Theorem, see e.g.
[
11
, Ch. 50]
.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
4
, Rem. 2.3]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be linear.
Then, for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, there are linear functionals

l_{i}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}

and polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that

T=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}l_{i}\cdot p_{i},\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad Tf=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}l_{i}(f)\cdot p_{i}

(2)

for all
f\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Among the linear maps
T
on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, the linear operators with

Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad t_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}

(3)

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
are called
diagonal
.
Clearly, a diagonal operator is uniquely determined by its diagonal sequence
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and every real sequence
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
gives a well-defined diagonal operator.
They have a special structure and therefore special properties, general linear maps on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
do not possess.
Inserting (
3
) into
3.3
gives the following.

Corollary 3.6

(
[
4
, Rem. 4.2]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be a linear operator.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
is a diagonal operator, i.e.,

Tx^{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad t_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

(ii)

The operator
T
is of the form

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\cdot x^{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

(4)

with a unique real sequence
c=(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
.

If one of the equivalent statements (i) or (ii) holds, then the diagonal sequence
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
and the sequence
c=(c_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
of coefficients
c_{\alpha}
fulfill the relations

t_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:\ \beta\preceq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\cdot c_{\beta}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:\ \beta\preceq\alpha}(-1)^{|\alpha-\beta|}\cdot\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\cdot t_{\beta}

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

4
K
-Positivity Preservers

We now come to our main topic:
K
-positivity preserver
.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and let

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be linear.
If

T\mathrm{Pos}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K),

then we call
T
a
K
-positivity preserver
.
If
K=\mathds{R}^{n}
, then, for short, we call
T
a
positivity preserver
.

If
K
is empty (
K=\emptyset
), then

\mathrm{Pos}(\emptyset)=\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

and hence every linear map

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

is a
\emptyset
-positivity preserver.

K=\emptyset
is trivial and we can therefore assume
K\neq\emptyset
.

Example 4.8
.

Let
n,k\in\mathds{N}
, let
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed and non-empty, let
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
, and let

l_{1},\dots,l_{k}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}

be
K
-moment functionals.
Then

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],\quad f\mapsto Tf:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}l_{i}(f)\cdot p_{i}

is a
K
-positivity preserver.

\circ

Not every
K
-positivity preserver is of the form given in
4.8
.

Example 4.9

(
[
4
, Rem. 2.3]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and

\mathrm{id}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],\quad f\mapsto f

be the identity on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Clearly,
\mathrm{id}
is a
K
-positivity preserver for any (non-empty)
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Let
K=\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Then
\mathrm{id}
does not possess a representation as in
4.8
.
To see this, assume that
\mathrm{id}
has such a representation, i.e., there exist a
k\in\mathds{N}
, polynomials
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
, and
\mathds{R}^{n}
-moment functionals

l_{1},\dots,l_{k}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}

such that

\mathrm{id}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}l_{i}\cdot p_{i}.

But since
\mathrm{id}(x_{1}+a)^{2}=(x_{1}+a)^{2}
,
(x_{1}+a)^{2}
is an extreme point of
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})
, and since
l_{i}((x_{i}+a)^{2})\geq 0
for any
a\in\mathds{R}
, we have that
(x_{1}+a)^{2}
must be contained in
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}
.
But since
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\}
is finite (countable) and
\mathds{R}
is uncountable, we arrived at a contradiction, i.e.,
\mathrm{id}
is not of a form as in
4.8
.

\circ

A much simpler argument as in
4.9
and therefore also simpler as in
[
4
]
is, that any
K
-positivity preserver as in
4.8
has only finite dimensional image.
Hence, any
K
-positivity preserver with infinite dimensional image can not be represented as in
4.8
, since all
l_{i}\geq 0
on
\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.

Before we give a characterization of
K
-positivity preservers, we need the following definition.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
and let

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

be a linear operator.
For
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
, we define the linear map

T_{y}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\quad\text{by}\quad T_{y}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}(y)\cdot\partial^{\alpha}.

(5)

We can now give the following characterization of
K
-positivity preserver.

Theorem 4.11

(
[
5
, Main Thm. 3.5]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\quad\text{with}\quad T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

be linear,
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
is a
K
-positivity preserver.

(ii)

For all
y\in K
, the sequence
(\alpha!\cdot q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is a
(K-y)
-moment sequence.

If one of the equivalent conditions (i) or (ii) holds, then, for any
y\in K
,

(T_{y}f)(y)=\int f(x+y)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(x)

for all
f\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, where
\mu_{y}
is a representing measure of the
(K-y)
-moment sequence
(\alpha!\cdot q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
.

As mentioned before, if
K=\emptyset
, then any linear map on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a
K
-positivity preserver, i.e., (i) in
4.11
is always fulfilled, and (ii) in
4.11
is the empty condition, i.e., it is also always fulfilled.

From
4.11
with
K=\mathds{R}^{n}
we immediately gain the classical characterization of
\mathds{R}^{n}
-positivity preserver of Julius Borcea.

Corollary 4.12

(
[
1
, Thm. 3.1]
)
.

Let

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

be with
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
is a
\mathds{R}^{n}
-positivity preserver.

(ii)

For every
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
,
(\alpha!\cdot q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is a
\mathds{R}^{n}
-moment sequence.

If one of the equivalent conditions holds, then for every
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
the map
T_{y}
is given by

(T_{y}p)(x):=\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}p(x+z)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(z)

for
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, where
\mu_{y}
is a representing measure of
(\alpha!\cdot q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
.

5
Generators
A
of Linear Maps
T
:
T=e^{A}

Among the linear maps

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

a special class are the maps of the form

T=e^{A}

for a linear map

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

Not every
T
can be written as
T=e^{A}
and not every
A
gives a well-defined
e^{A}
.
Hence, at first we have to determine, which
A
give well-defined
e^{A}
.

Definition 5.13
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
We define

\mathfrak{g}:=\big\{A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\,\big|\,A\ \text{linear and}\\
\ e^{tA}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\ \text{well-defined for all}\ t\in\mathds{R}\big\}.

While
\mathfrak{g}
is an abstract set, the following gives a characterization.

Theorem 5.14

(
[
3
, Thm. 4.4]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

be linear.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

A\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\deg A^{k}x^{\alpha}<\infty
for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\deg A^{k}p<\infty
for all
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

(iv)

For all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, there exist subvector spaces
V_{i}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with

(a)

\dim V_{i}<\infty
for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
,

(b)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}V_{i}=\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, and

(c)

AV_{i}\subseteq V_{i}
for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

An immediate consequence of
5.14
is the following.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and
A\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
A
has a complex eigenvalue.

Proof.

By
5.14
, there exists a
V\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with

\dim V<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad AV\subseteq V,

i.e.,
A|_{V}:V\to V
is a linear operator on a finite dimensional vector space (matrix) and hence has at least one (complex) eigenvalue.
∎

Since by
5.14
it is sufficient to know every
A\in\mathfrak{g}
only on finite dimensional invariant subspaces
V_{i}
, we have the following.

Corollary 5.16

(
[
5
, Cor. 5.6]
and
[
3
, Cor. 4.5]
)
.

Let
A\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Then

\exp A=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{A^{k}}{k!}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathds{1}+\frac{A}{k}\right)^{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathds{1}-\frac{A}{k}\right)^{-k}.

6
Generators
A
of
K
-Positivity Preserving Semi-Groups
(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}

While we have characterized all linear maps
A
on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that
e^{A}
is well-defined, we now want to know, which of these
A
are generators of a
K
-positivity preserving semi-group
(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}
.

Definition 6.17
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and let
A\in\mathfrak{g}
.
We say
A
is a
generator of a
K
-positivity preserving semi-group

(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}
, if
e^{tA}
is a
K
-positivity preserver for all
t\geq 0
.

We start with the simpler case of constant coefficients.

Definition 6.18
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
We define

\mathfrak{D}_{c}:=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}\,\middle|\,q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R},\ q_{0}=1\right\}

and

\mathfrak{d}_{c}:=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}d_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}\,\middle|\,d_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}\ \text{for all}\ \alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

Theorem 6.19

(
[
2
, Thm. 2.17]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Then
(\mathfrak{D}_{c},\,\cdot\,)
as a Fréchet space is a commutative regular Fréchet Lie group with the commutative Fréchet Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{d}_{c},\,\cdot\,,+)
.
The exponential map

\exp:\mathfrak{d}_{c}\to\mathfrak{D}_{c},\quad A\mapsto\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\frac{A^{k}}{k!}

is smooth and bijective with the smooth and bijective inverse

\log:\mathfrak{D}_{c}\to\mathfrak{d}_{c},\quad A\mapsto-\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}}\frac{(\mathds{1}-A)^{k}}{k}.

For the constant coefficient case, we can characterize all generators of
\mathds{R}^{n}
-positivity preserving semi-groups.

Definition 6.20
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
We define the set

\mathfrak{D}_{c,+}:=\{A\in\mathfrak{D}_{c}\,|\,A\ \text{is a positivity preserver}\}

of all
positivity preservers with constant coefficients
and we define the set

\mathfrak{d}_{c,+}:=\left\{A\in\mathfrak{d}_{c}\,\middle|\,\exp(tA)\in\mathfrak{D}_{c,+}\ \text{for all}\ t\geq 0\right\}

of all
generators of positivity preservers with constant coefficients

For the characterization, we need the following result.

7
Summary

While

\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Pos}(K)

are very well-studied in (real) algebraic geometry, surprisingly, even the linear operators

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

are very little studied.
Especially under the condition that
T
is a
K
-positivity preserver, very little was known so far.
That these operators are so little studied is surprising, since in every linear algebra course at first vector spaces
V
are introduced and investigated (dimension, basis, subspaces etc.) and then the actual linear algebra starts when one looks at linear operators

T:V\to V

for finite-dimensional
V
, i.e.,
T
are matrices.
This step going from the vector spaces
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and cones
\mathrm{Pos}(K)
to linear maps on them is so far hardly considered.
There are possibly two reasons for that.
Firstly, (real) algebraic geometry heavily relies on multiplication (in
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
) and in general we have

T(f\cdot g)\neq Tf\cdot Tg,

i.e., we lose the very basic operation that makes algebra into algebra and not just the study of vector spaces.
Secondly,
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is infinite dimensional and hence topological aspects come into play, which are usually not needed by algebraists.

These limitations are only overcome by looking from an (unusual) functional analytic point of view on these matters.
While moments and measures are deeply connected to
\mathrm{Pos}(K)
via duality, “ordinary” functional analysis works on Hilbert spaces and finite dimensional Lie groups.
The restriction to work only on
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and not being allowed to make it into a Hilbert space (like is usually done by working with moments) forces us to drop the rich theory of Hilbert spaces and go to Fréchet spaces, LF-spaces, and the regular Fréchet Lie groups.
Here, more technical details have to be considered since we no longer have only one norm as in a Hilbert space but we have to work with families of semi-norm.

However, the current review about the works
[
2
,
5
,
4
,
3
]
shows that still results can be obtained and that we possibly (and hopefully) stand here only at the very beginning of the investigation of linear operators

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].
