Quantum Message Passing for Factor Graphs over Finite Abelian Groups
Avijit Mandal, Henry D. Pfister

Abstract
We develop a quantum message-passing framework for factor graphs over finite abelian groups. Our starting point is the task of discriminating between a collection of quantum states indexed by the elements of a finite abelian group 𝒢 \mathcal{G} whose overlaps respect the structure of a group-covariant pure-state channel (PSC). For such channels, we show that the Gram matrix constructed from the output states is diagonalized by the character basis of the dual group 𝒢 ^ \widehat{\mathcal{G}} . Hence, the channel is characterized, up to isometric equivalence, by its character-indexed eigen list. Based on this representation, we analyze the induced classical-quantum channels associated with check, equality, homomorphism, marginalization, and automorphism factors. For each factor, we derive explicit update rules showing that if the incoming messages are heralded mixtures of group-covariant PSCs, then the outgoing message remains in the same class. This provides a closed quantum message-passing framework for tree-structured factor graphs assembled from these primitives. The framework applies directly to several standard code families over finite abelian groups, including polar codes, LDPC codes, and convolutional and turbo codes. It recovers the previously studied q q -ary formulation as the special case 𝒢 = ℤ q \mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{q} , while extending the belief propagation with quantum messages (BPQM) framework introduced by Renes to non-cyclic alphabets and more general factor-graph constraints described by homomorphisms between products of abelian groups.

Introduction

Factor graphs provide a common language for inference in coding theory, machine learning, statistical physics, and constraint satisfaction. They isolate the local constraints of a model and thereby expose algorithmic structure that can be exploited for efficient message-passing algorithms. In coding theory, this viewpoint is particularly powerful because sparse-graph-based codes (LDPC), trellis-based codes (convolutional and turbo codes), and polar constructions can all be described in terms of local factor operations such as equality constraints, parity constraints, and more general homomorphisms
[
1
,
8
,
3
,
17
,
18
,
2
,
6
,
7
]
. For classical channels, belief propagation and related message-passing methods then yield exact marginalization on trees and effective approximate decoding rules on graphs with cycles.

For classical-quantum (CQ) channels, the corresponding decoding problem is substantially more difficult. Holevo, Schumacher, and Westmoreland
[
10
,
23
]
showed that reliable communication up to capacity is achievable in principle by collective measurements on long output blocks, but such measurements are generally difficult to describe and implement in a structured way. This motivates the search for quantum decoders that retain some of the architectural advantages of classical message passing while operating coherently on quantum outputs
[
24
,
25
,
15
]
. Belief propagation with quantum messages (BPQM) is one such approach in which, rather than measuring each channel output separately, it combines quantum sufficient statistics recursively along the factor graph, mirroring the structure of a classical belief-propagation decoder
[
20
,
21
,
4
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
19
]
.

Prior BPQM constructions and analyses have focused mainly on binary settings
[
20
,
4
,
13
,
14
]
. In
[
16
]
, BPQM was extended to symmetric
q
-ary pure-state channels whose Gram matrices are circulant, and it was shown that the effect of the local combining rules can be tracked via the eigen lists of the output Gram matrices, without regard for the particular physical realization of the channel states.
In
[
19
]
, a closely related binary BPQM representation was formulated in terms of distribution ensembles associated with BPQM messages, with applications to convolutional and turbo decoding over qubit PSCs.
These developments suggest that the relevant sufficient statistics for quantum message passing can be described using representations adapted to the symmetry of the underlying alphabet.

In this work, we develop a quantum message-passing framework for factor graphs over finite abelian groups. Our approach is based on
\mathcal{G}
-covariant pure-state channels (PSCs), where the input alphabet is a finite abelian group
\mathcal{G}
and the output states transform covariantly with respect to the group action. Our starting point is that, for such channels, the Gram matrix is diagonalized by the character basis of the dual group
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
. This implies that the channel is characterized, up to isometric equivalence, by its eigen list indexed by characters of
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
. In this sense, the eigen list representation from
[
16
]
extends naturally from the cyclic case to the abelian-group setting considered here.

Using this approach, we analyze induced CQ channels associated with basic local factors that arise in abelian-group factor graphs. These are check factors, equality factors, homomorphism factors, marginalization factors, and automorphism factors. Our main goal is to show that these local operations preserve a tractable class of messages.
Specifically, we show that each local factor preserves this class of messages. If the incoming messages are heralded mixtures of group-covariant PSCs, then the outgoing message remains in the same class and admits an explicit description through transformed eigen lists and classical side information
This gives a closed quantum message-passing calculus for tree-structured factor graphs assembled from these primitives.

The resulting framework unifies several coding constructions within a single formalism. Polar transforms over finite abelian groups can be decomposed into automorphism, check, and equality factors, so their synthetic channels can be tracked recursively using the same eigen list machinery. Tanner graphs for LDPC and more general group codes are built from equality and parity-type homomorphism constraints, and therefore admit local quantum message-passing update rules directly from our factor analysis. Trellis-based constructions such as convolutional and turbo codes also fit naturally into this framework through finite-state-machine descriptions whose branch operations are homomorphisms followed by marginalization.
For example, our approach generalizes some results in
[
19
]
for binary convolutional codes on qubit PSCs and extends the previously studied
q
-ary setting
[
16
]
to a broader group-theoretic decoding framework that includes non-cyclic alphabets and more general local constraints.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows. First, we characterize finite abelian group-covariant PSCs through the eigen decomposition of their Gram matrices in the character basis. Second, we derive explicit quantum message-passing update rules for a set of specific local factor types associated with abelian group factor graphs. Third, we show that these rules are closed under composition on trees through the class of finite heralded mixtures of group-covariant PSCs. Finally, we demonstrate how this machinery specializes to standard code families, including polar codes, LDPC, group codes, convolutional codes, and turbo codes.
Taken together, these results provide a rigorous abelian-group extension of BPQM and identify the dual-group character domain as the natural representation for structured quantum inference on such factor graphs.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section

3

4
explains how these rules apply to several standard decoding architectures over finite abelian groups. Section
5
outlines how this quantum message-passing framework is used for the density-evolution analysis of tree factor-graph based codes over finite abelian groups.

\mathbb{I}
. For a matrix
M
, the entry in row
i
and column
j
is denoted by
M_{i,j}

M_{i,:}

i

2.2
Finite Abelian Groups and Homomorphisms

Throughout the paper, all groups are finite and abelian. For group elements

g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
, the group operation is denoted by
g_{1}g_{2}
.
The identity element of
\mathcal{G}
is denoted by
e
, and for
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the inverse element is denoted by
g^{-1}
. If
\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{d}
are finite abelian groups, then
\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{G}_{d}
denotes their direct product, equipped with componentwise group operation. A map
\phi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is a homomorphism if

\phi(g_{1}g_{2})=\phi(g_{1})\phi(g_{2}),\qquad\forall g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}.

Its kernel and image are defined by

\ker\phi\coloneqq\{g\in\mathcal{G}:\phi(g)=e\},\qquad\text{Im}\phi\coloneqq\{\phi(g):g\in\mathcal{G}\}.

An automorphism of
\mathcal{G}
is a bijective homomorphism from
\mathcal{G}
to itself. If
\phi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is surjective, then for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
, the set

\{g\in\mathcal{G}:\phi(g)=h\}

has cardinality
|\ker\phi|
. In particular,

|\mathcal{G}|=|\ker\phi|\,|\mathcal{H}|.

This identity will be used in the normalization of the induced channel associated with a homomorphism factor.

2.3
Group Characters

For an abelian group
\mathcal{G}
, define the dual group
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
to be the set of characters
\chi
, which are group homomorphisms

\chi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{T}
where
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|=1\}
is the unit circle under multiplication
[
12
]
. It follows that

\displaystyle\chi(g_{1}g_{2})=\chi(g_{1})\chi(g_{2}),\forall g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{G},\forall\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is finite, each character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies
|\chi(g)|=1
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. The group
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is equipped with trivial character
\chi_{\text{triv}}
such that
\chi_{\text{triv}}(g)=1
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
.
For two characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
, we define
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
as the character such that

\displaystyle(\chi_{1}\chi_{2})(g):=\chi_{1}(g)\chi_{2}(g).

One can readily check that
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
. For character
\chi
, let
\chi^{-1}
denote its inverse which satisfies

\displaystyle\chi\chi^{-1}=\chi_{\text{triv}}.

For a character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, the inverse character
\chi^{-1}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is unique and satisfies the following relations
\forall g\in\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}(g)=\chi(g^{-1})=\frac{1}{\chi(g)}=\overline{\chi(g)}.

For characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, the following orthogonality relation holds

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\chi_{1}^{-1}(g)\chi_{2}(g)=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\overline{\chi_{1}(g)}\chi_{2}(g)=|\mathcal{G}|\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}.

For character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, define the character state as

\displaystyle\ket{\chi}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\chi^{-1}(g)\ket{g}.

Lemma 1
.

The states
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
form an orthonormal basis

Proof.

For
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, we have

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\chi_1}{\chi_2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\chi_{1}(g)\chi_{2}^{-1}(g^{\prime})\innerproduct{g}{g'}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\chi_{1}(g)\chi_{2}^{-1}(g)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}.

Since
|\widehat{\mathcal{G}}|=|\mathcal{G}|
, this orthonormal family has the full dimension of the Hilbert space and hence is an orthonormal basis.
∎

If
\phi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is a homomorphism, then it induces a dual map
\hat{\phi}:\widehat{\mathcal{H}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
defined by
\hat{\phi}(\chi)\coloneqq\chi\circ\phi
. When
\phi
is surjective, the map
\hat{\phi}
is injective.

2.4
Quantum Preliminaries

Definition 2
.

A classical-quantum (CQ) channel
W\colon x\rightarrow\rho_{x}
takes a classical input
x
from a finite alphabet
\mathcal{X}
and outputs a density matrix
\rho_{x}
.

Definition 3
.

Two CQ channels
W
and
W^{\prime}
with the same input alphabet are isometrically (or unitarily) equivalent if there exists an isometry (or unitary)
V
, such that
W^{\prime}(x)=VW(x)V^{\dagger}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 4
.

A CQ channel
W\colon x\rightarrow\rho_{x}
is called a pure state channel (PSC), if the output state
\rho_{x}=\outerproduct{\psi_x}{\psi_x}
is a pure state, i.e. a rank-1 matrix for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 5
.

Consider a finite group
\mathcal{G}
and a unitary representation
\{U_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of the group.
A CQ channel
W\colon g\rightarrow\rho_{g}
, with input alphabet
\mathcal{G}
, is called a
\mathcal{G}
-covariant CQ channel if the output states satisfy the following relation

\displaystyle\rho_{g^{\prime}g}=U_{g^{\prime}}\rho_{g}U_{g^{\prime}}^{\dagger},\quad\forall g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}.

(1)

More generally, if there exists some finite group
\mathcal{G}
and the unitary representation
\{U_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
for which the covariance relation in Eq. (
1
) holds, then we refer to
W
simply as a group-covariant CQ channel.

Definition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite group and let
\{U_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a unitary representation of
\mathcal{G}
.
A pure-state channel
W:g\mapsto\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}
is called
\mathcal{G}
-covariant if the following relation holds

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{g'g}}=U_{g^{\prime}}\ket{\psi_g},\qquad\forall g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}.

Definition 7
.

A matrix
M\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{G}|\times|\mathcal{G}|}
indexed by group elements
g\in\mathcal{G}
, is called
\mathcal{G}
-circulant, if the elements of
M
satisfy the following relation

\displaystyle M_{gg_{1},gg_{2}}=M_{g_{1},g_{2}},\qquad\forall g,g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}.

Definition 8
.

For a
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC
W\colon g\rightarrow\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}
, the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{G}|\times|\mathcal{G}|}
is a matrix which is indexed by group elements of
\mathcal{G}
and corresponds to the pairwise overlaps of output states
\{\ket{\psi_g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
. More specifically, for all
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
, the elements of
\Gamma_{W}
satisfy

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{W})_{g,g^{\prime}}=\innerproduct{\psi_{g}}{\psi_{g'}}.

Lemma 9
.

Consider a
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC
W\colon g\rightarrow\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}
where
\mathcal{G}
is a finite group. Then, the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
formed by the states
\{\ket{\psi_g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is
\mathcal{G}
-circulant; i.e., the elements of
\Gamma_{W}
satisfy

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{W})_{g,g^{\prime}}=\gamma_{g^{-1}g^{\prime}}

for all
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
and
\{\gamma_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
are the elements of the first row of
\Gamma_{W}
.

Proof.

Let
e\in\mathcal{G}
be the identity element. Then we compute
(\Gamma_{W})_{g,g^{\prime}}
as follows

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{W})_{g,g^{\prime}}=\innerproduct{\psi_{g}}{\psi_{g'}}=\bra{\psi_{e}}U^{\dagger}_{g}U_{g^{\prime}}\ket{\psi_{e}}.

Since
\{U_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is a unitary representation, it satisfies
U_{g}^{\dagger}=U_{g^{-1}}
. Hence, we get

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{W})_{g,g^{\prime}}=\bra{\psi_{e}}U_{g^{-1}g^{\prime}}\ket{\psi_{e}}.

Defining the elements of the first row of the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
as follows

\displaystyle\gamma_{g}\coloneqq(\Gamma_{W})_{e,g}=\innerproduct{\psi_e}{\psi_g}=\bra{\psi_e}U_{g}\ket{\psi_e},

completes the proof.
∎

For the rest of the paper, we will restrict ourselves to finite abelian groups. In Lemma
10
, we establish the key property for the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
associated with
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC when
\mathcal{G}
is an abelian group. More specifically, we show that the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
is diagonal in the basis formed by the
character states
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
.

Lemma 10
.

Consider the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
for a
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC
W\colon g\rightarrow\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}
. When
\mathcal{G}
is abelian, the character basis
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
diagonalizes
\Gamma_{W}
and the eigenvalue corresponding to the basis element
\ket{\chi}
is
\lambda_{\chi}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{\chi}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\gamma_{g}\chi^{-1}(g).

Proof.

For character states
\{\ket{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
and the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
, the following relation holds

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{W})_{g,:}\ket{\chi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\gamma_{g^{\prime}g^{-1}}\chi^{-1}(g^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{g^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\gamma_{g^{\prime\prime}}\chi^{-1}(g^{\prime\prime}g)

\displaystyle=\chi^{-1}(g)\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{g^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\gamma_{g^{\prime\prime}}\chi^{-1}(g^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\chi^{-1}(g)\frac{\lambda_{\chi}}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}.

This implies that
\ket{\chi}
is an eigenvector of
\Gamma_{W}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\Gamma_{W}\ket{\chi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{\chi}}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\chi^{-1}(g)\ket{g}

(2)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\chi}\ket{\chi}.\qed

(3)

We denote the eigenvalues of the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
using the ordered list
\bm{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
, which we refer to as its eigen list. Note that we use the specific ordering implied by the stated Fourier transform and its correspondence to eigenvectors. For the eigen list
\bm{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
, let
\bm{\mu}=\{\mu_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
denote the normalized eigen list where
\mu_{\chi}=\frac{\lambda_{\chi}}{|\mathcal{G}|}
.
We define the canonical choice of quantum state
\ket{\psi_g}

\forall g\in\mathcal{G}
as follows

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_g}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}}\chi(g)\ket{\chi}

Lemma 11
.

For Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
with eigen list
\bm{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
and normalized eigen list
\bm{\mu}=\{\mu_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda_{\chi}=|\mathcal{G}|,\qquad\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\mu_{\chi}=1.

(4)

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda_{\chi}=\Tr(\Gamma_W)=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\innerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}=|\mathcal{G}|.

(5)

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\mu_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda_{\chi}=1.\qquad\qed

Since the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
is positive semidefinite, we have
\lambda_{\chi}\geq 0
for all
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
. Hence, the normalized eigen list
\bm{\mu}
forms a probability distribution on
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
.

Lemma 12
.

All
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs
W\colon g\rightarrow\outerproduct{\psi_{g}}{\psi_g}
with
g\in\mathcal{G}
are determined (up to isometric equivalence) by their eigen list
\bm{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Recall the canonical choice of state
\ket{\psi_g}
for Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
with eigen list
\bm{\lambda}
as

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_g}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}}\chi(g)\ket{\chi}

(6)

The inner product
\innerproduct{\psi_{g}}{\psi_{g'}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\psi_{g}}{\psi_{g'}}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}}\chi^{\prime-1}(g)\chi(g^{\prime})\innerproduct{\chi'}{\chi}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda_{\chi}\chi(g^{-1}g^{\prime})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{G}}\gamma_{b}\chi^{-1}(b)\chi(g^{-1}g^{\prime})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{G}}\gamma_{b}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\chi(b^{-1}g^{-1}g^{\prime})

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{G}}\gamma_{b}|\mathcal{G}|\delta_{b,g^{-1}g^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\ =\gamma_{g^{-1}g^{\prime}}=(\Gamma_{W})_{g,g^{\prime}}.

Since two PSCs
W\colon g\rightarrow\ket{\psi_{g}}
and
W^{\prime}\colon g\rightarrow\ket{\psi_{g}'}
with
g\in\mathcal{G}
are isometrically (or unitarily) equivalent when there exists an isometry (or unitary)
V
such that

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{g}'}=V\ket{\psi_g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G},

thus all the isometrically (or unitarily) equivalent group-covariant PSCs with Gram matrix are well defined using eigenvalue spectrum
\bm{\lambda}
associated with Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
.
∎

2.5
Information Measures

For a group-covariant PSC
W
, for the rest of the paper, we consider uniform input distribution on group
\mathcal{G}
.
To characterize
W
under the uniform input ensemble, we consider two information measures, namely the symmetric Holevo information, denoted by
I(W)
and the channel fidelity, denoted by
F(W)
.
The symmetric Holevo information measures the maximum amount of classical information that can be sent through the channel
W
for uniform input distribution. More formally, for a CQ channel
W
, the symmetric Holevo information is computed as

\displaystyle I(W)=S\Bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}W(g)\Bigg)-\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}S\Bigg(W(g)\Bigg)

where
S(\rho)=-\Tr(\rho\log\rho)
is von Neumann entropy for the density matrix
\rho
.
When
W\colon g\rightarrow\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}

\forall g\in\mathcal{G}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC, the symmetric Holevo information satisfies

\displaystyle I(W)

\displaystyle=S\Bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}\Bigg)-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}S(\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g})

\displaystyle=S\Bigg(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}\Bigg),

where we use the fact that
S(\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g})=0

\forall g\in\mathcal{G}
.
The channel fidelity characterizes expected overlap between the non-identical output quantum states of channel
W
. Formally, it is written as

\displaystyle F(W)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|(|\mathcal{G}|-1)}\!\sum_{g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}:,g\neq g^{\prime}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\Tr\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{W(g)}W(g^{\prime})\sqrt{W(g)}}\right).

For
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC
W
, the expression reduces to

\displaystyle F(W)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|(|\mathcal{G}|-1)}\sum_{g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}\colon g\neq g^{\prime}}\left|\innerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_{g'}}\right|.

.

For abelian group
\mathcal{G}
, consider the
\mathcal{G}
-covariant

\displaystyle I(W)

\displaystyle\ =H(\bm{\mu})

\displaystyle F(W)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|-1}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G},g\neq e}|\gamma_{g}|,

where
\bm{\mu}=\{\mu_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
is the normalized eigen list with

Proof.

Consider the matrix
\Psi
whose columns correspond to the quantum states
\{\ket{\psi_{g}}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\Psi\Psi^{\dagger}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}.

The matrix
\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi=\Gamma_{W}
as it satisfies

\displaystyle(\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi)_{g,g^{\prime}}=\innerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g'}=(\Gamma_{W})_{g,g^{\prime}},

for all
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
. Since the eigenvalues of
\Psi^{\dagger}\Psi
are the same as of
\Psi\Psi^{\dagger}
, we have

\displaystyle I(W)

\displaystyle\ =S\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\Psi\Psi^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle\ =-\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\frac{\lambda_{\chi}}{|\mathcal{G}|}\log\frac{\lambda_{\chi}}{|\mathcal{G}|}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\mu_{\chi}\log\mu_{\chi}

\displaystyle=H(\bm{\mu}).

Writing the channel fidelity
F(W)
in terms of the Gram matrix elements, we obtain

\displaystyle F(W)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|(|\mathcal{G}|-1)}\sum_{g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}:g\neq g^{\prime}}|\innerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_{g'}}|

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|(|\mathcal{G}|-1)}\Bigg(\sum_{g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}:g\neq g^{\prime}}|\gamma_{g^{-1}g^{\prime}}|\Bigg)

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|(|\mathcal{G}|-1)}|\mathcal{G}|\bigg(\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G},g\neq e}|\gamma_{g}|\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|-1}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G},g\neq e}|\gamma_{g}|.\qed

2.6
Pretty Good Measurement on Group-Covariant PSC

A pretty good measurement (PGM)
[
9
,
11
]
, which is also known as the square root measurement, is defined as follows. Consider the
\mathcal{G}
-covariant CQ channel
W\colon g\rightarrow\rho_{g}
with uniform input distribution on
\mathcal{G}
. For the output state
\rho_{g}
for
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the measurement operator is
M_{g}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{g}\left(\bar{\rho}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
where
\bar{\rho}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\rho_{g}
and the inverse is taken on the support of
\bar{\rho}
. This
|\mathcal{G}|
-outcome measurement
\{M_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is shown to be optimal
[
5
]
for minimizing the probability of state-discrimination error for geometrically uniform quantum states. In this paper, to analyze the error rate in the quantum message passing framework,
we will use PGM to characterize the group symbol error probability. The following lemma establishes the relation between the error probability for discriminating the output states of
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC
W
in terms of the eigen list of the Gram matrix.

Lemma 14
.

Consider group-covariant PSC
W\colon g\rightarrow\outerproduct{\psi_{g}}{\psi_g}
with
g\in\mathcal{G}
with group-circulant Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W}
and eigen list
\bm{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
. Then, for the uniform distribution over input alphabets
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the probability of error
P_{\text{err}}(W)
for distinguishing the states optimally using pretty good measurement satisfies

\displaystyle P_{\text{err}}(W)=1-\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

For the states
\{\ket{\psi_g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
, the pretty good measurement is constructed using measurement operators
\{\Pi_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{g}=\outerproduct{\tau_g}{\tau_g},

where
\ket{\tau_g}
=
\sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}}\bar{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ket{\psi_g}
and the average density matrix
\bar{\rho}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}
. For the uniform input distribution, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\Psi\Psi^{\dagger}=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\frac{\lambda_{\chi}}{|\mathcal{G}|}\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi}.

Thus,
\ket{\tau_g}
can be written as

\displaystyle\ket{\tau_g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}(\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathcal{G}|}{\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}}}\outerproduct{\chi'}{\chi'})(\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}}\chi(g)\ket{\chi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\chi(g)\ket{\chi}.

Hence, we compute
\innerproduct{\tau_g}{\psi_g}
as

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\tau_g}{\psi_g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}}\chi^{\prime}\chi^{-1}(g)\innerproduct{\chi}{\chi'}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}}.

Finally, the average success probability of the measurement satisfies

\displaystyle P_{\text{suc}}(W)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\Tr(\Pi_g\ketbra{\psi_g}{\psi_g})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|\innerproduct{\tau_g}{\psi_g}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}}\right)^{2}.

Thus, the error probability is given by

\displaystyle P_{\text{err}}(W)=1-P_{\text{suc}}(W)=1-\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi}}\right)^{2}.

∎

Heralded Mixture of Group-Covariant PSCs

In general, the local quantum message-passing rules studied in Section
3

can produce outputs including an additional classical side register that distinguishes between multiple
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs. Such outputs are known as heralded mixtures.

Definition 15
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite abelian group. A CQ channel
W_{H}\colon g\to W_{H}(g)
,
g\in\mathcal{G}

heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs
if there exist

•

a finite set
\mathcal{X}
,

•

a probability distribution
\{p_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
,

•

an orthonormal basis

•

for each

\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC

\displaystyle W_{H}(g)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}\,W_{x}(g)\otimes\outerproduct{x}{x},\qquad g\in\mathcal{G}.

3
Message Passing on Factor Graphs

In this section, we study how local factor operations act on channels whose input alphabets are finite abelian groups. Our analysis proceeds in two steps. We first define, for each primitive factor, the induced CQ channel obtained when the incoming messages are arbitrary group-covariant CQ channels, and we show that these induced channels remain group-covariant. We then specialize to the case where the incoming messages are group-covariant PSCs, or more generally elements of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
on the appropriate input alphabets, and derive explicit quantum message-passing update rules in terms of the corresponding eigen lists and herald registers. This two-level description separates the structural covariance of the induced channels from the more refined character-domain parametrization that will be used throughout the remainder of the paper.
We begin by defining the induced CQ channel associated with a factor whose incident edges carry group-covariant CQ channels.
More specifically, consider the abelian groups
\mathcal{G}_{1}

\displaystyle f(g_{1},\dots,g_{m};h)=\bm{1}\{h=\phi(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})\}.

The preceding discussion identifies, for each primitive factor, the induced CQ channel obtained from incoming group-covariant CQ channels and shows that these induced channels remain group-covariant. This provides the structural layer of the quantum message-passing framework. We now specialize to the more structured setting of group-covariant PSCs, and more generally finite heralded mixtures of such PSCs, for which each local factor admits an explicit characterization in the character domain.

For each primitive factor considered below, we proceed in the same way. We first identify the induced channel on the relevant output group, then exhibit an isometry or unitary that reveals the natural sufficient statistic, and finally conclude whether the output is a single group-covariant PSC or a finite heralded mixture of such PSCs. In this setting, the relevant sufficient statistics are the eigen lists
\bm{\lambda}_{k}=\{\lambda^{(k)}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{k}}
associated with the incoming messages.

3.1
Quantum Message Passing for Check Factor

In this subsection, we analyze the check factor for two incoming
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs. The main point is that the check-factor output need not remain a single PSC. Rather, after a suitable change of basis, it decomposes into a finite heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs. The next lemma gives the corresponding ensemble explicitly in terms of the incoming eigen lists.

Lemma 16
.

For abelian group
\mathcal{G}
, consider
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
, with Gram matrices
\Gamma_{W_{1}}
and
\Gamma_{W_{2}}
having eigen lists
\bm{\lambda}_{1}=\{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
and
\bm{\lambda}_{2}=\{\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
respectively. Then, the induced CQ channel of the check factor
W_{1}\boxast W_{2}
can be decomposed into a heralded mixture of PSCs characterized using the ensemble
\{p^{\boxast}_{\chi},W^{\boxast}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
where
p^{\boxast}_{\chi}
is the probability of the abelian PSC
W^{\boxast}_{\chi}
. Let
\bm{\lambda}^{\boxast}_{\chi}=\{\lambda^{\boxast,\chi}\}_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
be the eigen list for
W^{\boxast}_{\chi}
for
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
. Then, for
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
,
p_{\chi}^{\boxast}
and
\bm{\lambda}^{\boxast}_{\chi}
are computed using
\bm{\lambda}_{1}
and
\bm{\lambda}_{2}
as below

\displaystyle p_{\chi}^{\boxast}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}^{(1)}\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}^{(2)},

\displaystyle\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}^{(\boxast,\chi)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|p_{\chi}^{\boxast}}\lambda_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}^{(1)}\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}^{(2)}.

Proof.

Consider the states
\bigg\{\ket{\psia_{g}}\bigg\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
that are the outputs of channel
W_{1}
satisfying

\displaystyle\ket{\psia_{g}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}}\chi(g)\ket{\chi}.

Similarly, consider the states
\bigg\{\ket{\psib_{g}}\bigg\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
which are outputs of channel
W_{2}
satisfying

\displaystyle\ket{\psib_{g}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi}}\chi(g)\ket{\chi}.

Then, the outputs of check-factor-combined channel
W_{1}\boxast W_{2}
can be written
\forall g\in\mathcal{G}
as

\displaystyle[W_{1}\boxast W_{2}](g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\outerproduct{\psia_{g'}}{\psia_{g'}}\otimes\outerproduct{\psib_{g'^{-1}g}}{\psib_{g'^{-1}g}}

Expanding
\ket{\psia_{g'}}\otimes\ket{\psib_{g'^{-1}g}}
we get

\displaystyle\ket{\psia_{g'}}\otimes\ket{\psib_{g'^{-1}g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}}\chi(g^{\prime})\chi^{\prime}(g^{\prime-1}g)\ket{\chi}\otimes\ket{\chi'}.

Consider the unitary
\tilde{U}^{\boxast}
which acts on character vectors according to

\displaystyle\tilde{U}^{\boxast}\ket{\chi}\otimes\ket{\chi'}=\ket{\chi\chi'^{-1}}\otimes\ket{\chi'}.

Then we get

\displaystyle\tilde{U}^{\boxast}\ket{\psia_{g'}}\otimes\ket{\psib_{g'^{-1}g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}}\chi(g^{\prime})\chi^{\prime}(g^{\prime-1}g)\ket{\chi\chi'^{-1}}\otimes\ket{\chi'}.

Hence for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
, we can write

\displaystyle\tilde{U}^{\boxast}[W_{1}\boxast W_{2}](g)(\tilde{U}^{\boxast})^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{3}}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\left(\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}}\chi(g^{\prime})\chi^{\prime}(g^{\prime-1}g)\ket{\chi\chi'^{-1}}\otimes\ket{\chi'}\right)\left(\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\xi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\xi^{-1}(g^{\prime})\xi^{\prime-1}(g^{\prime-1}g)\bra{\xi\xi'^{-1}}\otimes\bra{\xi'}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{3}}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\bigg(\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime},\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\lambda^{(1)}_{\xi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\chi\xi^{-1}(g^{\prime})\chi^{\prime-1}\xi^{\prime}(g^{\prime})\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi\chi'^{-1}}{\xi\xi'^{-1}}\otimes\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{3}}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime},\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\lambda^{(1)}_{\xi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\chi\xi^{-1}(g^{\prime})\chi^{\prime-1}\xi^{\prime}(g^{\prime})\right)\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi\chi'^{-1}}{\xi\xi'^{-1}}\otimes\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{3}}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime},\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\lambda^{(1)}_{\xi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\left(|\mathcal{G}|\delta_{\chi\xi^{-1},\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}}\right)\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi\chi'^{-1}}{\xi\xi'^{-1}}\otimes\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime\prime},\chi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi''}{\chi''}\otimes\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\outerproduct{\chi''}{\chi''}\otimes\left(\sum_{\chi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}\right).

Hence we can compute
p^{\boxast}_{\chi}
as

\displaystyle p^{\boxast}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\bra{\chi}\otimes\mathbb{I}\left(\tilde{U}^{\boxast}[W_{1}\boxast W_{2}](g)(\tilde{U}^{\boxast})^{\dagger}\right)\ket{\chi}\otimes\mathbb{I}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\xi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\bigg(\sum_{\chi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\xi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\bra{\chi''}\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}\ket{\chi''}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}.

Consider the state
\bigg\{\ket{\psi^{(\cnop,\chi)}_{g}}\bigg\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
such that

\displaystyle\ket{\psi^{(\cnop,\chi)}_{g}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}|}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\xi}^{(\boxast,\chi)}}\xi(g)\ket{\xi},

where
\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}^{(\boxast,\chi)}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|p_{\chi}^{\boxast}}\lambda_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}^{(1)}\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}^{(2)}
. Then we get

\displaystyle\outerproduct{\psi^{(\cnop,\chi)}_g}{\psi^{(\cnop,\chi)}_g}

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}^{(\boxast,\chi)}\lambda_{\xi^{\prime}}^{(\boxast,\chi)}}\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}

(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}p^{\boxast}_{\chi}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\xi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}.

(9)

The following relation holds for the check node combined output

\displaystyle\frac{\bra{\chi}\otimes\mathbb{I}\left(\tilde{U}^{\boxast}[W_{1}\boxast W_{2}](g)(\tilde{U}^{\boxast})^{\dagger}\right)\ket{\chi}\otimes\mathbb{I}}{\Tr\left(\bra{\chi}\otimes\mathbb{I}\left(\tilde{U}^{\boxast}[W_{1}\boxast W_{2}](g)(\tilde{U}^{\boxast})^{\dagger}\right)\ket{\chi}\otimes\mathbb{I}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bra{\chi}\otimes\mathbb{I}\left(\tilde{U}^{\boxast}[W_{1}\boxast W_{2}](g)(\tilde{U}^{\boxast})^{\dagger}\right)\ket{\chi}\otimes\mathbb{I}}{p^{\boxast}_{\chi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}p^{\boxast}_{\chi}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\xi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}}\chi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime-1}(g)\outerproduct{\chi'}{\xi'}

\displaystyle=\outerproduct{\psi^{(\cnop,\chi)}_g}{\psi^{(\cnop,\chi)}_g}.\qed

Therefore, the check-factor update maps two incoming
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs to a finite heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs. Thus, the outgoing message is completely described by the ensemble
\{p_{\chi}^{\boxast},\bm{\lambda}^{(\boxast,\chi)}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
.

It is also useful to describe the operational unitary underlying the above decomposition. The map
\tilde{U}^{\boxast}
reorganizes the two incoming character labels into a herald label and a residual character label. After this change of basis, the induced output becomes block diagonal in the herald register, and each block is a
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC. An operational realization is given by

\displaystyle U^{\boxast}=(\mathbb{I}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\dagger})(\mathrm{SWAP})\tilde{U}^{\boxast},

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform between the canonical basis indexed by
\mathcal{G}
and the character basis indexed by
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
. Notice that
U^{\boxast}
depends only on the group structure and not on the eigen lists of the incoming channels.
In
Appendix A
, we provide an example for check factor update rule for group
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

3.2
Quantum Message Passing for Equality Factor

We next consider the equality factor with two incoming
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs. In contrast to the check factor, the equality update remains within the class of single
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs after isometric compression. Thus, the outgoing message is again characterized by one eigen list rather than a nontrivial heralded ensemble.

Lemma 17
.

Consider
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
for abelian group
\mathcal{G}
with Gram matrices
\Gamma_{W_{1}}
and
\Gamma_{W_{2}}
with eigen lists
\bm{\lambda}_{1}=\{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
and
\bm{\lambda}_{2}=\{\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
respectively.
Then, the induced channel of the equality factor
W_{1}\varoast W_{2}
is isometrically equivalent to a
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC
W^{\varoast}
with Gram matrix eigen list
\bm{\lambda}^{\varoast}=\{\lambda^{\varoast}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
such that

\displaystyle\lambda^{\varoast}_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi^{\prime}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi\chi^{\prime-1}}.

Proof.

Consider the states
\bigg\{\ket{\psia_{g}}\bigg\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
and
\bigg\{\ket{\psib_{g}}\bigg\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
which are outputs of channel
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
respectively.
Then the Gram matrix entries for
\Gamma_{W_{1}}
and
\Gamma_{W_{2}}

\forall g\in\mathcal{G}
, satisfy

\displaystyle\gamma^{(1)}_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\chi(g),

\displaystyle\gamma^{(2)}_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi}\chi(g).

For the equality-factor combined channel
W_{1}\varoast W_{2}
, for all inputs
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the outputs are

\displaystyle[W_{1}\varoast W_{2}](g)=\outerproduct{\psia_{g}}{\psia_g}\otimes\outerproduct{\psib_g}{\psib_g}.

Thus, the Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W_{1}\varoast W_{2}}
for the combined channel
W_{1}\varoast W_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{W_{1}\varoast W_{2}})_{g,g^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\innerproduct{\psia_g}{\psia_{g'}}\innerproduct{\psib_g}{\psib_{g'}}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{(1)}_{g^{-1}g^{\prime}}\gamma^{(2)}_{g^{-1}g^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\xi}\xi(g^{-1}g^{\prime})\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}\xi^{\prime}(g^{-1}g^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}}\sum_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\xi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi^{\prime}}\xi\xi^{\prime}(g^{-1}g^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{2}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\left(\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\xi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi\xi^{-1}}\right)\chi(g^{-1}g^{\prime}).

Denoting
\lambda^{\varoast}_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\xi}\lambda^{(2)}_{\chi\xi^{-1}}
, we get

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{W_{1}\varoast W_{2}})_{g,g^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\lambda^{\varoast}_{\chi}\chi(g^{-1}g^{\prime}).

Thus
\Gamma_{W_{1}\varoast W_{2}}
is
\mathcal{G}
-circulant with eigen list
\bm{\lambda}^{\varoast}=\{\lambda_{\chi}^{\varoast}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
which characterizes channel
W^{\varoast}
.
∎

Hence, the equality-factor update preserves the class of
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs. Equivalently, it may be viewed as a degenerate heralded mixture with a single herald value.

Since the output of the equality factor is again a single
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC, we often write
\bm{\lambda}_{1}\varoast\bm{\lambda}_{2}
for the resulting eigen list
\bm{\lambda}^{\varoast}
. It is also useful to describe an operational unitary for this combination. The equality-factor unitary
U^{\varoast}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}
compresses the two aligned input states into one outgoing PSC together with an ancilla state independent of the channel input. Formally, for some fixed
\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
and all
g\in\mathcal{G}
, we have

\displaystyle U^{\varoast}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}\ket{\psi^{(1)}_{g}}\otimes\ket{\psi^{(2)}_{g}}=\ket{\psi^{\vnop}_{g}}\otimes\ket{\eta}.

Thus, the equality factor does not create a nontrivial herald register; rather, it performs an isometric compression of the two incoming PSC outputs into a single
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC.
To construct the unitary
U^{\varoast}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}
, we let

\displaystyle U^{\varoast}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}=U^{\text{control}}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}U_{+},

where
U_{+}
satisfies

\displaystyle U_{+}\ket{\chi}\otimes\ket{\chi'}=\ket{\chi\chi'}\otimes\ket{\chi'}

for all
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, and the unitary
U^{\text{control}}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}
is given by

\displaystyle U^{\text{control}}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi}\otimes U^{\chi}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}},

such that the unitary
U^{\chi}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}
has the following form

\displaystyle U^{\chi}_{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{I},\qquad\text{if $\ket{\zeta_{\chi}}=\ket{\eta}$}\\
\mathbb{I}-2\outerproduct{\zeta_{\chi}'}{\zeta_{\chi}'},\qquad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

with
\ket{\zeta_{\chi}'}=\frac{\ket{\zeta_{\chi}}-\ket{\eta}}{||\ket{\zeta_{\chi}}-\ket{\eta}||}
,
\ket{\zeta_{\chi}}=\frac{\ket{\tilde{\zeta}_{\chi}}}{||\ket{\tilde{\zeta}_{\chi}}||}
, and
\ket{\tilde{\zeta}_{\chi}}=\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi\xi^{-1}}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi}}\ket{\xi}
.

In
Appendix B
, we provide an example for equality factor update rule for group
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

3.3
Quantum Message Passing for Homomorphism Factor

We now consider the quantum message passing rule associated with a surjective homomorphism factor. Let
\phi:\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{2}
be a surjective homomorphism between finite abelian groups. On the dual side,
\phi
induces the injective map
\hat{\phi}:\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(\xi)(g)=\xi(\phi(g)),\qquad\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2},\ g\in\mathcal{G}_{1}.

The image of
\hat{\phi}
is precisely the subgroup of characters on
\mathcal{G}_{1}
that are trivial on
\ker\phi
, namely

\displaystyle\text{Im}\hat{\phi}\coloneqq\{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}:\chi(k)=1,\ \forall k\in\ker\phi\}.

Since
\phi
is surjective,
\hat{\phi}
is injective and
|\mathcal{G}_{1}|=|\ker\phi|\,|\mathcal{G}_{2}|
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}|}{|\text{Im}\hat{\phi}|}=\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}=|\ker\phi|.

Choose a set of coset representatives
\mathcal{T}
such that dual group
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
is expressed using following disjoint union

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}=\bigsqcup_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}\eta\,\text{Im}\hat{\phi}.

(10)

This coset decomposition is the source of the herald register in the homomorphism update where each coset label
\eta
will index one outgoing
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-covariant PSC.
Now we are ready to state the general lemma about surjective homomorphism
\phi
.

Lemma 18
.

Consider a
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-covariant PSC
W_{1}
with Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W_{1}}
and eigen list
\bm{\lambda}_{1}=\{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}
. Let
\phi:\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{2}
be a surjective homomorphism, and let
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
be a set of representatives for the cosets of
\text{Im}\hat{\phi}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
. Then, the output channel is characterized by a heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-covariant PSCs. More specifically, the output channel is characterized by the ensemble
\{p_{\eta},\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}\}_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}
, where
p_{\eta}
is given by

\displaystyle p_{\eta}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}\sum_{\chi\in\eta\,\text{Im}\hat{\phi}}\lambda_{\chi}^{(1)}

and
\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}=\{\lambda^{\eta}_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
is the eigen list of the corresponding
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-covariant PSC, where

\displaystyle\lambda_{\xi}^{\eta}=\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|\,p_{\eta}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}

for all
\eta\in\mathcal{T}
and
\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
.

Proof.

Recall the canonical state for channel
W_{1}
is

\displaystyle\ket{\psia_g}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}}\chi(g)\ket{\chi}.

Then, from the disjoint union in Eq. (
10
), for every character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
, there exists a unique pair
(\eta,\xi)
with
\eta\in\mathcal{T}
and
\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\chi=\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi),

where we use the injectivity of
\hat{\phi}
.
Next, we define the operator
V
, which acts on the state
\ket{\chi}
, using

\displaystyle V\ket{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}=\ket{\xi}\otimes\ket{\eta}.

Equivalently, we can write

\displaystyle V=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\ket{\xi}\otimes\ket{\eta}\bra{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}.

Thus, we compute

\displaystyle V^{\dagger}V

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sum_{\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\ket{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}\bra{\xi}\otimes\bra{\eta}\ket{\xi'}\otimes\ket{\eta'}\bra{\eta'\hat{\phi}(\xi')}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\outerproduct{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}.

This implies that
V
is an isometry. Applying
V
on the state
\ket{\psia_g}
with
\phi(g)=h
, we get

\displaystyle V\ket{\psia_g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}}\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)(g)\ket{\xi}\otimes\ket{\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}}\eta(g)\hat{\phi}(\xi)(g)\ket{\xi}\otimes\ket{\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}}\eta(g)\xi(\phi(g))\ket{\xi}\otimes\ket{\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}}\eta(g)\xi(h)\ket{\xi}\otimes\ket{\eta}.

On the other hand, for
h\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
,
\exists

g_{0}\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
such that
g_{0}\ker\phi\in\mathcal{G}/\ker\phi
and
\phi(g)=h

\forall g\in g_{0}\ker\phi
. Hence, we can rewrite the state
\rho_{h}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{h}=\frac{1}{|\ker\phi|}\sum_{k\in\ker\phi}\outerproduct{\psia_{g_0k}}{\psia_{g_0k}}.

Applying isometry
V
to state
\rho_{h}
, we get

\displaystyle V\rho_{h}V^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\ker\phi|}\sum_{k\in\ker\phi}V\outerproduct{\psia_{g_0k}}{\psia_{g_0k}}V^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\ker\phi|}\sum_{k\in\ker\phi}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}\sum_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T},\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta^{\prime}\hat{\phi}(\xi^{\prime})}}\eta\eta^{\prime-1}(g_{0})\eta\eta^{\prime-1}(k)\xi\xi^{\prime-1}(h)\outerproduct{\xi}{\xi'}\otimes\outerproduct{\eta}{\eta'}.

Using the fact that
\sum_{k\in\ker\phi}\eta(\eta^{\prime})^{-1}(k)=|\ker\phi|\delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}
,
\forall\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, we get

\displaystyle V\rho_{h}V^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T},\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi^{\prime})}}\xi\xi^{\prime-1}(h)\outerproduct{\xi}{\xi'}\otimes\outerproduct{\eta}{\eta}.

Hence, we compute the probability
p_{\eta}
as follows

\displaystyle p_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\mathbb{I}\otimes\bra{\eta}V\rho_{h}V^{\dagger}\mathbb{I}\otimes\ket{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}\sum_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi^{\prime})}}\xi\xi^{\prime-1}(h)\outerproduct{\xi}{\xi'}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}.

Define
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-covariant state
\ket{\psi_{h}^{\eta}}
as below

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{h}^{\eta}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{\eta}_{\xi}}\xi(h)\ket{\xi},

where
\lambda^{\eta}_{\xi}=\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|p_{\eta}}\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}
. Then, we immediately get

\displaystyle\outerproduct{\psi_{h}^{\eta}}{\psi_{h}^{\eta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|p_{\eta}}\sum_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi)}\lambda^{(1)}_{\eta\hat{\phi}(\xi^{\prime})}}\xi\xi^{\prime-1}(h)\outerproduct{\xi}{\xi'}.

Thus, for all
h\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle V\rho_{h}V^{\dagger}=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}p_{\eta}\outerproduct{\psi_{h}^{\eta}}{\psi_{h}^{\eta}}\otimes\outerproduct{\eta}{\eta}.

∎

Therefore, the homomorphism-factor update maps an incoming
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-covariant PSC to a finite heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-covariant PSCs, with herald register indexed by the coset representatives
\eta\in\mathcal{T}
. The outgoing message is thus completely described by the ensemble
\{p_{\eta},\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}\}_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}}
.

The isometry
V
also has a direct operational interpretation. It coherently separates each input character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
into its coset representative
\eta\in\mathcal{T}
and its pulled-back output character
\hat{\phi}(\xi)\in\text{Im}\hat{\phi}
. After averaging over each fiber of
\phi
, the output becomes block diagonal in the
\eta
register. Thus,
\eta
serves as the classical herald, and each block is a
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-covariant PSC.

Lemma
18
extends immediately to an arbitrary homomorphism
\phi
that is not necessarily surjective by replacing
\mathcal{G}_{2}
with the subgroup
\text{Im}\phi\leq\mathcal{G}_{2}
. Since
\text{Im}\phi
is again a finite abelian group, it is sufficient to analyze the surjective case in what follows. In
Appendix C
, we provide a few examples for homomorphism factor update rules for several groups and homomorphisms.

Next we state a special case of Lemma
18
. In Lemma
19
, we identify a support condition on the eigen list
\{\lambda_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}
under which a
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-covariant input PSC induces a single PSC through the homomorphism
\phi
rather than a heralded mixture.

Lemma 19
.

Consider
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-covariant PSC
W_{1}
with Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W_{1}}
characterized by the eigen list
\bm{\lambda}_{1}=\{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}
. Let
\phi:\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{2}
be a surjective homomorphism. Also assume that
\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}=0
,
\forall\chi\notin\text{Im}(\hat{\phi})
. Then the induced channel
W_{2}
defined by

\displaystyle\ket{\psib_{h}}\coloneqq\ket{\psia_{g}},\quad\text{$\forall g\in\mathcal{G}_{1}$ such that $\phi(g)=h$}

is a
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-covariant PSC. Moreover the channel
W_{2}
is characterized by Gram matrix
\Gamma_{W_{2}}
and eigen list
\bm{\lambda}_{2}=\{\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
, where

\displaystyle\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi}=\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}\lambda^{(1)}_{\hat{\phi}(\xi)}

for all
\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
and
\hat{\phi}:\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
is the dual map of
\phi
.

Proof.

First, observe that since
\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}=0

\forall\chi\notin\text{Im}(\hat{\phi})
, we get

\displaystyle\ket{\psia_{kg}}=\ket{\psia_g},\forall k\in\ker{\phi}.

Thus, the state
\ket{\psib_h}
is well defined.
Define the operator
V_{\phi}
based on the map
\phi
as follows,

\displaystyle V_{\phi}=\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\outerproduct{\xi}{\hat{\phi}(\xi)}.

Computing
V_{\phi}^{\dagger}V_{\phi}
we get

\displaystyle V_{\phi}^{\dagger}V_{\phi}=\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\outerproduct{\hat{\phi}(\xi)}{\hat{\phi}(\xi)}=\Pi_{\text{Im}(\hat{\phi})},

where
\Pi_{\text{Im}(\hat{\phi})}
is the projection on the image of
\hat{\phi}
. From the support assumption (
\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}=0

\forall\chi\notin\text{Im}(\hat{\phi})
), we get

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{Im}(\hat{\phi})}\ket{\psia_g}=\ket{\psia_g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G}_{1}.

Thus,
V_{\phi}
is a valid isometry for the subspace spanned by the states
\bigg\{\ket{\psia_g}\bigg\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{1}}
.
Applying
V_{\phi}
on the state
\ket{\psia_g}
, we get

\displaystyle V_{\phi}\ket{\psia_g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}}\chi(g)\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\outerproduct{\xi}{\hat{\phi}\xi}\ket{\chi}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\hat{\phi}\xi}}\hat{\phi}\xi(g)\ket{\xi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\hat{\phi}\xi}}\xi(\phi(g))\ket{\xi},

where
(1)
follows from the fact that
\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}=0

\forall\chi\notin\text{Im}(\hat{\phi})
, so the only surviving terms correspond to
\chi=\hat{\phi}(\xi)
.
Finally, setting
\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi}=\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}\lambda^{(1)}_{\hat{\phi}\xi}
and defining the canonical state for
W_{2}
to be

\displaystyle\ket{\psib_h}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi}}\xi(h)\ket{\xi}

for all
h\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle V_{\phi}\ket{\psia_g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}\lambda^{(2)}_{\xi}}\xi(\phi(g))\ket{\xi}

\displaystyle=\ket{\psib_{\phi(g)}}.\qed

In this special support regime, the herald becomes trivial and the homomorphism update reduces to a single
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-covariant PSC. Thus, Lemma
19
identifies precisely when the general heralded decomposition of Lemma
18
collapses to one PSC.

3.4
Quantum Message Passing for Marginalization Factor

We next consider marginalization over one component of a product alphabet. In the character domain, averaging over the marginalized variable enforces equality of the corresponding dual indices by character orthogonality. As a result, the discarded dual coordinate becomes a classical herald, while the retained coordinate labels the outgoing PSC on the remaining group.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
be abelian groups such that
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2}
. Consider a
\mathcal{U}
-covariant PSC
W\colon(g_{1},g_{2})\rightarrow\outerproduct{\psi_{(g_1,g_2)}}{\psi_{(g_1,g_2)}}
for
(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
characterized via the eigen list
\bm{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}\}_{(\chi,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\times\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
. Let
\phi\colon\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{1}
be a marginalization map such that
\phi(g_{1},g_{2})=g_{1}

\forall(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
. Then the induced channel
W_{\phi}
is isometrically equivalent to a heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-covariant PSCs characterized via the ensemble
\{p_{\eta},\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}\}_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
where the probability
p_{\eta}
is

\displaystyle p_{\eta}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}

and the elements of eigen list
\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}=\{\lambda^{\eta}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}
satisfy the following relation

\displaystyle\lambda^{\eta}_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|p_{\eta}}\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}.

Proof.

For a group element
(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
, we can write the canonical form for state
\ket{\psi_{(g_1,g_2)}}
as follows

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{(g_1,g_2)}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{U}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1},\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}}\chi(g_{1})\eta(g_{2})\ket{(\chi,\eta)}.

The marginalization map
\phi_{\text{marginal}}
induces the CQ channel
W_{\phi_{\text{marginal}}}\colon g_{1}\rightarrow\rho_{g_{1}}

\forall g_{1}\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{g_{1}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}\sum_{g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{2}}\outerproduct{\psi_{(g_1,g_2)}}{\psi_{(g_1,g_2)}}.

Expanding the state
\ket{\psi_{(g_1,g_2)}}
, we get

\displaystyle\rho_{g_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}\sum_{g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{2}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1},\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1},\eta^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\chi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})}}\chi(g_{1})\chi^{\prime-1}(g_{1})\eta(g_{2})\eta^{\prime-1}(g_{2})\outerproduct{(\chi,\eta)}{(\chi',\eta')}.

Using the character orthogonality, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|}\sum_{g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{2}}\eta(g_{2})\eta^{\prime-1}(g_{2})=\delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}.

Thus, we can rewrite the state
\rho_{g_{1}}
for
g_{1}\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{g_{1}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}\lambda_{(\chi^{\prime},\eta)}}\chi(g_{1})\chi^{\prime-1}(g_{1})\outerproduct{(\chi,\eta)}{(\chi',\eta)}.

Next, consider the unitary
U_{\text{split}}
defined by

\displaystyle U_{\text{split}}\ket{(\chi,\eta)}=\ket{\chi}\ket{\eta}

(11)

for all
(\chi,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\times\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
. Then applying
U_{\text{split}}
on the state
\rho_{g_{1}}
we get

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}_{g_{1}}\coloneqq U_{\text{split}}\rho_{g_{1}}U_{\text{split}}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}\outerproduct{\eta}{\eta}\otimes\left(\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}\lambda_{(\chi^{\prime},\eta)}}\chi(g_{1})\chi^{\prime-1}(g_{1})\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi'}\right).

Hence, we get a heralded mixture of PSCs which is characterized via ensemble
\{p_{\eta},\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}\}_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
. The probability
p_{\eta}
that corresponds to the eigen list
\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}
is computed as

\displaystyle p_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\bra{\eta}\otimes\mathbb{I}\rho^{\prime}_{g_{1}}\ket{\eta}\otimes\mathbb{I}\right)

(12)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}\lambda_{(\chi^{\prime},\eta)}}\chi(g_{1})\chi^{\prime-1}(g_{1})\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi'}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}.

(14)

Similarly, the post measurement output PSC after marginalization is computed as

\displaystyle\frac{\bra{\eta}\otimes\mathbb{I}\rho^{\prime}_{g_{1}}\ket{\eta}\otimes\mathbb{I}}{\Tr\left(\bra{\eta}\otimes\mathbb{I}\rho^{\prime}_{g_{1}}\ket{\eta}\otimes\mathbb{I}\right)}

(15)

\displaystyle=\frac{\bra{\eta}\otimes\mathbb{I}\rho^{\prime}_{g_{1}}\ket{\eta}\otimes\mathbb{I}}{p_{\eta}}

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|p_{\eta}}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}\lambda_{(\chi^{\prime},\eta)}}\chi(g_{1})\chi^{\prime-1}(g_{1})\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi'}.

(17)

Defining the state
\ket{\psi^{\eta}_{g_1}}
for
g_{1}\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
with

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{g_1}^{\eta}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda^{\eta}_{\chi}}\chi(g_{1})\ket{\chi}

and setting
\lambda^{\eta}_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|p_{\eta}}\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}
we get

\displaystyle\outerproduct{\psi_{g_1}^{\eta}}{\psi_{g_1}^{\eta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda^{\eta}_{\chi}\lambda^{\eta}_{\chi^{\prime}}}\chi(g_{1})\chi^{\prime-1}(g_{1})\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi'}

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|p_{\eta}}\sum_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}\lambda_{(\chi^{\prime},\eta)}}\chi(g_{1})\chi^{\prime-1}(g_{1})\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi'}.

(19)

Hence, the elements of the eigen list
\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}=\{\lambda^{\eta}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{1}}
satisfy the following relation

\displaystyle\lambda^{\eta}_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{2}|p_{\eta}}\lambda_{(\chi,\eta)}.

∎

Therefore, the marginalization update maps a
\mathcal{U}
-covariant PSC to a finite heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-covariant PSCs, with herald register indexed by
\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
. Equivalently, the outgoing message is completely described by the ensemble
\{p_{\eta},\bm{\lambda}^{\eta}\}_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{2}}
.

Operationally, the unitary
U_{\mathrm{split}}
separates the retained and discarded dual coordinates. The average over
g_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
forces equality of the discarded dual index through character orthogonality, thereby making the second register classical. This is the origin of the heralded decomposition in the marginalization update.

3.5
Quantum Message Passing for Automorphism Factor

Finally, we consider automorphism factors. In this case, no genuine mixing occurs as an automorphism simply relabels the group elements, and in the character domain this becomes a permutation induced by the dual automorphism. Consequently, the output remains a single
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC.

Lemma 21
.

Consider
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC
W\colon g\rightarrow\outerproduct{\psi_{g}}{\psi_{g}}

. Let
\phi:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}

. Then the induced CQ channel

is a
\mathcal{G}-
covariant PSC and is characterized by the eigen list
\bm{\lambda}^{\phi}=\{\lambda^{\phi}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}
such that

\displaystyle\lambda^{\phi}_{\chi}=\lambda_{\hat{\phi}(\chi)}

for all

, and
\hat{\phi}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{G}}

The induced CQ channel

\displaystyle\rho_{g}^{\phi}=\outerproduct{\psi_{\phi^{-1}(g)}}{\psi_{\phi^{-1}(g)}}

for all

be the Gram matrix for the output PSC where elements of the first row satisfy
\gamma_{g}^{\phi}=\gamma_{\phi^{-1}(g)}

\displaystyle\lambda^{\phi}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\gamma^{\phi}_{g}\chi^{-1}(g)

which completes the proof.
∎

Thus, the automorphism factor preserves the class of single
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs and does not introduce a nontrivial herald register. Operationally, it acts only by a relabeling of the character basis through the dual automorphism
\hat{\phi}

3.6
Closure of Quantum Message Passing on Tree Factor Graphs

The preceding lemmas establish local closure for each primitive factor. More precisely, conditioned on the incoming herald values, every local update produces either a single group-covariant PSC or a finite heralded mixture of such PSCs on the appropriate output alphabet. The next theorem shows that this local closure property composes over any tree factor graph assembled from the primitive factors considered in this section.

Theorem 22

(Closure of quantum message passing on trees)
.

For each finite abelian group
\mathcal{G}
, let
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
denote the class of heralded mixtures of
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs introduced in
Section
2
. Let
T
be a tree factor graph whose edge variables take values in finite abelian groups and whose local factors are check, equality, homomorphism,
marginalization, or automorphism factors. Fix a directed edge
e
of
T
, and let
\mathcal{G}_{e}
denote the alphabet on that edge. Assume that every incoming leaf message in the computation tree associated with
e
belongs to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}_{e^{\prime}})
on the corresponding leaf alphabet
\mathcal{G}_{e^{\prime}}
.

Then the message produced by quantum message passing on the directed edge
e
belongs to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}_{e})
.
Equivalently, every directed message generated by exact quantum message passing
on
T
is a finite heralded mixture of group-covariant PSCs on the alphabet of
that edge.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction on the depth of the computation tree of the directed edge
e
. If the depth is zero, then
e
is a leaf edge and the claim holds by assumption. Now suppose the claim holds for every directed edge whose computation tree has depth at most
d
, and let
e
be a directed edge whose computation tree has depth
d+1
. Let
f
be the factor node at which the message on
e
is computed, and let
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
be the incoming directed edges to
f
. Write
\mathcal{G}_{e_{i}}
for the alphabet on
e_{i}
.
By the induction hypothesis, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
the incoming message on
e_{i}
has the form

\displaystyle W_{i}(g_{i})=\sum_{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}p_{i}(x_{i})\,W_{i,x_{i}}(g_{i})\otimes\outerproduct{x_i}{x_i},\qquad g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}_{e_{i}},

where
\mathcal{X}_{i}
is finite and each
W_{i,x_{i}}
is a
\mathcal{G}_{e_{i}}
-covariant PSC. Set
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}_{r}
. For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{X}
, define
p(x)\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}(x_{i}).
Conditioned on the classical herald
x
, the incoming messages are the group-covariant PSCs
W_{1,x_{1}},\dots,W_{r,x_{r}}.

Applying the local quantum message passing rule corresponding to the factor
f
to these channels produces an outgoing CQ channel on
\mathcal{G}_{e}
which, by the results of Section
3
, is either a
\mathcal{G}_{e}
-covariant PSC or a finite heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}_{e}
-covariant PSCs. Hence, for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
, there exist a finite set

\mathcal{Y}_{x}
, a probability distribution
\{q_{x}(y)\}_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{x}}
, and

\mathcal{G}_{e}
-covariant PSCs
\{V_{x,y}\}_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{x}}
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{x}(g)=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{x}}q_{x}(y)\,V_{x,y}(g)\otimes\outerproduct{y}{y},\qquad g\in\mathcal{G}_{e}.

Therefore, the unconditional outgoing message is

\displaystyle W_{e}(g)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)\,\widetilde{W}_{x}(g)\otimes\outerproduct{x}{x},\qquad g\in\mathcal{G}_{e}.

Substituting the expression for
\widetilde{W}_{x}(g)
yields

\displaystyle W_{e}(g)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{x}}p(x)\,q_{x}(y)\,V_{x,y}(g)\otimes\outerproduct{x,y}{x,y},\qquad g\in\mathcal{G}_{e}.

This is a finite heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}_{e}
-covariant PSCs, so

W_{e}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}_{e})
. This completes the induction.
∎

4
Applications to Decoding

In this section, we explain how the quantum message-passing rules developed in Section
3
apply to decoding classical codes on group-covariant PSCs whose symbol alphabets are finite abelian groups. The factors considered earlier, namely check, equality, homomorphism, marginalization, and automorphism factors, already provide the local building blocks for several standard code families. Therefore, once the induced CQ channels for these factors are characterized, the corresponding quantum decoding rules follow by composing the same local updates on the code’s factor graph.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite abelian group, and let
W:g\mapsto\outerproduct{\psi_g}{\psi_g}
for
g\in\mathcal{G}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSC. A code over
\mathcal{G}
is described by a factor graph whose edge variables take values in finite abelian groups and whose local constraints are expressed through equality constraints, parity constraints, general homomorphisms, automorphisms, and marginalization operations. Along each edge, quantum message passing propagates the induced message in the form provided by Section
3
. Since this class of messages is preserved under the local factor updates, any code whose factor graph is assembled from these primitives can be decoded within the same framework.

This viewpoint recovers the previously studied
q
-ary setting as a special case, while extending the same decoding description to arbitrary finite abelian groups and to factor graphs whose local constraints are described by homomorphisms between products of abelian groups.

For polar codes and LDPC/group codes, the relevant eigen list based quantum message-passing updates were already identified in the symmetric
q
-ary setting
[
16
]
. Our purpose here is therefore not to repeat the full development in the symmetric
q
-ary setting, but rather to show how those constructions extend to arbitrary finite abelian groups once the local constraints are expressed through homomorphism and automorphism factors. In the polar case, this extension appears through the decomposition of the kernel into automorphism, check, and equality factors. In the LDPC/group-code case, it appears through the observation that Tanner constraints over
\mathcal{G}
are assembled from equality, check, homomorphism, and automorphism factors, so the closure result of Theorem
22
applies directly on trees. By contrast, for convolutional and turbo codes, the finite-state-machine recursion in terms of character-indexed eigen lists together with marginalization is a more substantial part of the present framework. Accordingly, we treat the convolutional decoder in greater detail and then use it as the constituent building block for turbo decoding.

4.1
Polar Codes over Abelian Groups

A polar transform over a finite abelian group
\mathcal{G}
is specified by an invertible homomorphism
K\colon\mathcal{G}^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}^{2}
.
If
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{G}^{2}
denotes the input vector and

(x_{1},x_{2})=K(u_{1},u_{2})
denotes the encoded vector, then the factor graph for the kernel can be decomposed into elementary homomorphism, check, equality, and
automorphism factors. Therefore, the synthetic channels of the polar transform are obtained recursively by repeated application of the update rules of Section
3
. In the abelian group setting, when we consider the Arıkan kernel of the input pair
(u_{1},u_{2})
, the encoded pair
(x_{1},x_{2})
satisfy the following relation

(x_{1},x_{2})=(u_{1}u_{2},u_{2}).

Let the encoded pair
(x_{1},x_{2})
be sent through the
\mathcal{G}
-covariant CQ channel
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
. Then, for polar decoding, we first construct the induced CQ channels seen by
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
. The
bad
channel, i.e. the CQ channel seen by
u_{1}
can be written as

\displaystyle W^{-}(u_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{u_{2}\in\mathcal{G}}W_{1}(u_{1}u_{2})\otimes W_{2}(u_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}}W_{1}(u)\otimes W_{2}(uu_{1}^{-1}).

Next, we define the
\mathcal{G}
-covariant CQ channel
W_{2}^{\text{inv}}
such that it satisfies

\displaystyle W_{2}^{\text{inv}}(g)\coloneqq W_{2}(g^{-1}).

Thus,
W^{-}
can be written as

\displaystyle W^{-}(u_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{G}}W_{1}(u)\otimes W_{2}^{\text{inv}}(u^{-1}u_{1})

\displaystyle=[W_{1}\boxast W_{2}^{\text{inv}}](u_{1}).

Since
W_{2}^{\text{inv}}
corresponds to an induced CQ channel from automorphism factor, the channel
W^{-}
can be analyzed via the automorphism factor and check factor.
The CQ channel seen by
u_{2}
uses the knowledge of
u_{1}
as classical side information. Thus, the induced CQ channel, which is also referred to as the
good
channel, can be written as

\displaystyle W^{+}(u_{2})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{u_{1}\in\mathcal{G}}\outerproduct{u_1}{u_1}\otimes W_{1}(u_{1}u_{2})\otimes W_{2}(u_{2}).

Let the channel
W_{1}
be
\mathcal{G}
-covariant under the unitary representation
\{U_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
such that

\displaystyle U_{g}W_{1}(u_{1})U_{g}^{\dagger}=W_{1}(u_{1}g).

Define the unitary
U^{\text{flip}}
as follows

\displaystyle U^{\text{flip}}=\sum_{u_{1}\in\mathcal{G}}\outerproduct{u_1}{u_{1}}\otimes U_{u_{1}}^{\dagger}.

Applying
U^{\text{flip}}
on the outputs of channel
W^{+}

\forall u_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
, we get

\displaystyle U^{\text{flip}}W^{+}(u_{2})(U^{\text{flip}})^{\dagger}

(20)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{u_{1}\in\mathcal{G}}\outerproduct{u_1}{u_1}\otimes U_{u_{1}}^{\dagger}W_{1}(u_{1}u_{2})U_{u_{1}}\otimes W_{2}(u_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{I}}{|\mathcal{G}|}\otimes W_{1}(u_{2})\otimes W_{2}(u_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{I}}{|\mathcal{G}|}\otimes[W_{1}\varoast W_{2}](u_{2}).

(21)

Thus, the channel
W^{+}
is isometrically equivalent to an induced CQ channel of equality factor. Since we can use this construction to recursively define polar codes of arbitrary length (
2^{n}
) with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the quantum message passing framework can be used to decode polar codes on group-covariant PSCs.
The advantage of the abelian-group formulation is that the polar kernel need not arise from field multiplication. It suffices that the local operations be homomorphisms or automorphisms of
\mathcal{G}
. Hence, the same polar quantum message based decoding description applies not only to cyclic alphabets and additive field alphabets, but also to non-cyclic groups such as direct products of cyclic groups.

4.2
LDPC Codes and Group Codes

A Tanner graph whose edge variables take values in finite abelian groups consists of variable nodes and check nodes.
Each variable node enforces equality among all edge copies of the same symbol, and therefore its local quantum message passing rule is given by the equality lemma of Section
3
.
Each check node enforces a group constraint of the form

\phi_{1}(x_{v_{1}})\phi_{2}(x_{v_{2}})\cdots\phi_{d_{a}}(x_{v_{d}})=e_{\mathcal{H}},

where
\mathcal{H}
is an abelian group associated with the check and each

\phi_{j}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is a homomorphism. Thus, the quantum message passing rule at a check node is an application of the homomorphism and check factor update.
In the simplest case,
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{G}
and each
\phi_{j}
is either the identity map or an automorphism of
\mathcal{G}
. Then the parity constraint reduces to a generalized group parity equation, and the quantum message passing update is obtained by repeated use of the check lemma
together with automorphism relabeling on the incident edges. When

\mathcal{G}=(\mathbb{F}_{q},+)
, multiplication by a nonzero field element is an automorphism of the additive group, so the present formulation recovers the standard nonbinary LDPC setting as a special case.

Accordingly, a quantum message passing decoder for a Tanner graph over a finite abelian group requires only the following local operations: equality-node combination at variable nodes, check or homomorphism combination at parity nodes, and automorphism relabeling where needed. Thus, the lemmas of Section
3
already provide the exact local decoding rules for LDPC and more general group codes.

When the Tanner graph is cycle-free, this description is exact in the same sense as belief propagation on a tree. Indeed, a Tanner tree over
\mathcal{G}
is assembled entirely from equality, check, homomorphism, and automorphism factors, so Theorem
22
applies directly. Hence every directed message produced by quantum message passing along an edge
e
belongs to the class
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}_{e})
, where
\mathcal{G}_{e}
denotes the alphabet associated with that edge. In particular, the posterior message at any target variable node is obtained by exact composition of the incoming channel observations and the parity-check constraints, and remains a finite heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}
-covariant PSCs.

4.3
Convolutional and Turbo Decoding

We now specialize the local update rules from
Section
3
to trellis-based decoding over finite abelian groups.
A convolutional encoder over an abelian group admits a finite-state-machine representation, and one trellis section can be viewed
as a local factor graph on the branch variable. The observed branch symbols are described by surjective homomorphisms, so the corresponding quantum message update is obtained by the special surjective homomorphism lemma, followed by local eigen list combination on the branch variable and marginalization to the next state.
We first describe this constituent trellis decoder for a convolutional code over
\mathcal{G}

[
19
]
, and then use the same constituent decoder as the basic ingredient in the turbo construction.

4.3.1
Convolutional Decoding

We first describe the classical finite-state-machine representation of a convolutional encoder over a finite abelian group
\mathcal{G}
. At time
t
, the encoder takes an input symbol
g_{t}\in\mathcal{G}
and stores a state
S_{t}
consisting of the previous
m
input symbols. The state
S_{t}
is given by

\displaystyle S_{t}=(g_{t-1},\dots,g_{t-m})\in\mathcal{G}^{m}.

It is convenient to regard one trellis section as being indexed by the pair
(g_{t},S_{t})\in\mathcal{G}^{m+1}
. The encoder maps this pair to
r
output symbols
x_{t}^{i}\in\mathcal{H}
, for
1\leq i\leq r
, through surjective homomorphisms
L_{i}:\mathcal{G}^{m+1}\to\mathcal{H}
. Explicitly, the map is given by

\displaystyle L_{i}(g_{t},S_{t})=x_{t}^{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq r.

This defines one trellis section. A convolutional codeword is obtained by traversing the trellis and applying the branch homomorphisms
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
at each time step. The next state is obtained by shifting the memory and appending the current input symbol. Therefore, the classical trellis description is determined by the input
(g_{t},S_{t})
, the output homomorphisms
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
, and the state update
S_{t}\mapsto S_{t+1}
.

We now specialize this trellis description to decoding on a
\mathcal{H}
-covariant PSC
W
, where each output symbol
x_{t}^{i}\in\mathcal{H}
is transmitted through the channel
W:x\mapsto\outerproduct{\psi_x}{\psi_x}
for
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Thus, after
T
trellis sections, the receiver observes the tensor-product state

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi}=\bigotimes_{t=1}^{T}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\ket{\psi_{x_t^i}}.

To analyze one trellis section, we consider the induced CQ channel whose input is the branch variable
(g_{t},S_{t})\in\mathcal{G}^{m+1}
and whose outputs are the quantum states corresponding to the symbols
\{x_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
. Since each output symbol is obtained
from
(g_{t},S_{t})
through the homomorphism
L_{i}
, the local trellis update is determined by the homomorphism factor rules from
Section
3
, followed by marginalization to the next state. Accordingly, the trellis recursion consists of three steps- lift the current state message to the branch variable by adjoining a
uniform input symbol
g_{t}\in\mathcal{G}
, combine this branch-prior message with the observed branch messages, and then marginalize to obtain the message on
S_{t+1}
.
The goal is to estimate the input sequence
(g_{1},\dots,g_{T})
from the received state
\ket{\Psi}
using the knowledge of Gram matrix eigen list
\{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}}
and homomorphisms
\{L_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
.

As in the usual trellis setting, we assume fixed initialization and termination states. More specifically, we take

\displaystyle S_{0}=(g_{-1},\dots,g_{-m}),\qquad S_{T+m+1}=(g_{T+m},\dots,g_{T+1}),

where the boundary symbols are fixed elements of
\mathcal{G}
. By group covariance, the particular choice of these boundary elements does not affect the form of the decoding recursion.

To implement the trellis recursion, we describe the state message at time
t
by a CQ channel

W_{t}\colon S_{t}\mapsto\rho_{S_{t}},\qquad S_{t}\in\mathcal{G}^{m}.

In general,
W_{t}
is not a single
\mathcal{G}^{m}
-covariant PSC. Rather, by the marginalization step from the previous trellis section, it is a finite heralded mixture of
\mathcal{G}^{m}
-covariant PSCs. Thus we write

\displaystyle W_{t}(S_{t})=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}}p_{x}^{t}\,W_{t}^{x}(S_{t})\otimes\outerproduct{x}{x},\qquad S_{t}\in\mathcal{G}^{m},

(22)

where
\mathcal{X}_{t}
is a finite herald alphabet,
\{p_{x}^{t}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}}
is a probability distribution, and for each
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
, the channel

W_{t}^{x}\colon S_{t}\mapsto\outerproduct{\psi_{S_t}^{\,t,x}}{\psi_{S_t}^{\,t,x}}

is a
\mathcal{G}^{m}
-covariant PSC characterized by an eigen list

\bm{\lambda}^{t,x}=\{\lambda_{\zeta}^{t,x}\}_{\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m}}}.

For the initialized boundary state, this description is degenerate, with
\mathcal{X}_{0}
consisting of a single element.

For each
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
, we first lift the state message on
\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m}}
to a branch-prior message on
\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m+1}}
by adjoining the trivial character on the new input coordinate. Equivalently, this corresponds to taking the current input symbol
g_{t}\in\mathcal{G}
to be uniform and independent of the state message. The resulting branch-prior eigen list

\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{In},t,x}=\{\lambda_{(\zeta,\eta)}^{\mathrm{In},t,x}\}_{(\zeta,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m+1}}}

satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{(\zeta,\eta)}^{\mathrm{In},t,x}=\begin{cases}|\mathcal{G}|\,\lambda_{\zeta}^{t,x},&\eta=\chi_{\text{triv}},\\[3.44444pt]
0,&\eta\neq\chi_{\text{triv}},\end{cases}

(23)

for
\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m}}
and
\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
.

Next, for each branch output
1\leq i\leq r
, let

\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{ch},(i),t}=\{\lambda_{\xi}^{\mathrm{ch},(i),t}\}_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}}

denote the eigen list of the observed channel message on the
i
-th parity branch. Since

L_{i}\colon\mathcal{G}^{m+1}\to\mathcal{H}
is a surjective homomorphism, Lemma
19

shows that the induced branch message is a single
\mathcal{G}^{m+1}
-covariant PSC with eigen list

\bm{\lambda}^{(i),t}=\{\lambda_{\chi}^{(i),t}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m+1}}}

given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\chi}^{(i),t}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{|\mathcal{G}^{m+1}|}{|\mathcal{H}|}\lambda_{\xi}^{\mathrm{ch},(i),t},&\chi=\hat{L}_{i}(\xi)\text{ for some }\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}},\\[8.61108pt]
0,&\chi\notin\text{Im}(\hat{L}_{i}),\end{cases}

(24)

where
\hat{L}_{i}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{H}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m+1}}
is the dual map of
L_{i}
.

Therefore, for each herald value
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
, the combined input message is again a single
\mathcal{G}^{m+1}
-covariant PSC with eigen list

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{out},t,x}=\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{In},t,x}\varoast\Bigl(\varoast_{i=1}^{r}\bm{\lambda}^{(i),t}\Bigr).

(25)

We now obtain the message on the next state
S_{t+1}
by marginalizing the input message over the symbol that leaves the memory.
Equivalently, we reparameterize the input variable
(g_{t},S_{t})\in\mathcal{G}^{m+1}
as
(S_{t+1},g_{t-m})\in\mathcal{G}^{m+1}
, where

\displaystyle S_{t+1}=(g_{t},g_{t-1},\dots,g_{t-m+1})

(26)

is the shifted state and
g_{t-m}
is the discarded memory symbol.

For each fixed
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
, write the canonical branch state as

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{(S_{t+1},g_{t-m})}^{\,t,x}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}^{m+1}|}}\sum_{\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m}},\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{(\zeta,\eta)}^{\mathrm{out},t,x}}\,\zeta(S_{t+1})\eta(g_{t-m})\ket{(\zeta,\eta)}.

(27)

Averaging uniformly over the discarded symbol
g_{t-m}\in\mathcal{G}
and applying
Lemma
20
, we obtain a heralded mixture on the next state.
More precisely, for each
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g_{t-m}\in\mathcal{G}}\outerproduct{\psi_{(S_{t+1},g_{t-m})}^{\,t,x}}{\psi_{(S_{t+1},g_{t-m})}^{\,t,x}}

(28)

is characterized by the ensemble

\Bigl\{\bigl(p_{\eta}^{t+1\mid x},\bm{\lambda}^{t+1,x,\eta}\bigr)\Bigr\}_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}},

where

\displaystyle p_{\eta}^{t+1\mid x}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{m+1}}\sum_{\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m}}}\lambda_{\zeta,\eta}^{\mathrm{out},t,x}

(29)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{\zeta}^{t+1,x,\eta}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|\,p_{\eta}^{t+1\mid x}}\lambda_{\zeta,\eta}^{\mathrm{out},t,x},\qquad\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m}}.

(30)

Hence the full next-state message is the heralded mixture

\displaystyle W_{t+1}(S_{t+1})=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}}\sum_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}p_{x}^{t}\,p_{\eta}^{t+1\mid x}\,W_{t+1}^{x,\eta}(S_{t+1})\otimes\outerproduct{x,\eta}{x,\eta},

(31)

where each
W_{t+1}^{x,\eta}
is a
\mathcal{G}^{m}
-covariant PSC with eigen list

\bm{\lambda}^{t+1,x,\eta}
.

Thus, the forward recursion propagates the state channel
W_{t}
, or equivalently the heralded family

\Bigl\{\bigl(p_{x}^{t},\bm{\lambda}^{t,x}\bigr)\Bigr\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}},

through the trellis rather than a single eigen list. This is the abelian-group quantum message-passing analogue of the classical BCJR forward recursion. The backward recursion is obtained by the same construction in reverse time, starting from the fixed terminal state. Once the forward and backward state channels are available, they are combined with the local branch observations to obtain the posterior message on
(g_{t},S_{t})
, and marginalization over the state yields the posterior message on
g_{t}
. Hence, the resulting trellis decoder performs symbol-wise MAP decoding of the input symbols.

The same construction extends in a straightforward way to multi-input convolutional encoders by replacing the single input symbol
g_{t}
with an input vector and enlarging the trellis state accordingly. Therefore, the finite-state-machine construction above
provides a complete constituent decoder for convolutional codes over finite abelian groups. When
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, this recursion reduces to the usual
q
-ary trellis
viewpoint; for general
\mathcal{G}
, the same decoder is expressed through eigen list messages indexed by characters and branch constraints given by surjective homomorphisms. This constituent trellis decoder will be the basic ingredient in the turbo decoder described
next.

4.3.2
Turbo Decoding

A turbo code is obtained by coupling two constituent convolutional encoders through the same
information sequence and an interleaver. Let
\bm{g}=(g_{1},\dots,g_{T})\in\mathcal{G}^{T}
be the information block. The first constituent encoder acts directly on
\bm{g}
, while the
second constituent encoder acts on the interleaved block
\Pi(\bm{g})=(g_{\pi(1)},\dots,g_{\pi(T)}),
where
\pi
is a permutation of
\{1,\dots,T\}
and
\Pi\colon\mathcal{G}^{T}\to\mathcal{G}^{T}
is the
induced automorphism of the product group
\mathcal{G}^{T}
.

For each constituent encoder
c\in\{1,2\}
, let
m_{c}
denote its memory and let

u_{t}^{(1)}\coloneqq g_{t},\qquad u_{t}^{(2)}\coloneqq g_{\pi(t)},\qquad t=1,\dots,T.

The trellis state of constituent
c
at time
t
is

S_{t}^{(c)}=(u_{t-1}^{(c)},\dots,u_{t-m_{c}}^{(c)})\in\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}},

and the corresponding input variable is
(u_{t}^{(c)},S_{t}^{(c)})\in\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}+1}
. For
i=1,\dots,r_{c}
, let
L_{i}^{(c)}\colon\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}+1}\to\mathcal{H}
be the surjective homomorphisms describing the parity outputs of the
c
-th constituent
encoder on one trellis section. As in the convolutional subsection, the boundary states are
taken to be fixed by initialization and termination of the trellis.
To incorporate the systematic observation and the message received from the other constituent,
we also introduce the symbol projection

L_{0}^{(c)}(u_{t}^{(c)},S_{t}^{(c)})=u_{t}^{(c)}.

Let
\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sys},(c),t}=\{\lambda^{\mathrm{sys},(c),t}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}

denote the eigen list of the observed channel message on the systematic symbol
u_{t}^{(c)}
,
and let
\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{apr},(c),t}=\{\lambda^{\mathrm{apr},(c),t}_{\chi}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}

denote the eigen list of the a priori message entering constituent
c
on the same symbol. For the parity outputs, let
\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{ch},(c),i,t}=\{\lambda_{\chi}^{\mathrm{ch},(c),i,t}\}_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}},\qquad i=1,\dots,r_{c},

denote the eigen lists of the observed channel messages on the output symbols.

Exactly as in the convolutional subsection, the constituent state message at time
t
is in
general not a single
\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}
-covariant PSC. Rather, it is a finite heralded mixture.
Thus, for each constituent
c\in\{1,2\}
, we write the forward state channel as

W_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}\colon S_{t}^{(c)}\mapsto\rho_{S_{t}^{(c)}}^{(c),t,\rightarrow},\qquad S_{t}^{(c)}\in\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}},

with representation

\displaystyle W_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}(S_{t}^{(c)})=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}}p_{x}^{(c),t,\rightarrow}\,W_{t}^{(c),x,\rightarrow}(S_{t}^{(c)})\otimes\outerproduct{x}{x},\qquad S_{t}^{(c)}\in\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}},

(32)

where
\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}
is a finite herald alphabet,

\{p_{x}^{(c),t,\rightarrow}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}}
is a probability
distribution, and for each
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}
, the channel

W_{t}^{(c),x,\rightarrow}\colon S_{t}^{(c)}\mapsto\outerproduct{\psi_{S_t^{(c)}}^{\,(c),t,x,\rightarrow}}{\psi_{S_t^{(c)}}^{\,(c),t,x,\rightarrow}}{\psi_{S_{t}^{(c)}}^{\,(c),t,x,\rightarrow}}

is a
\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}
-covariant PSC characterized by an eigen list

\bm{\lambda}^{(c),t,x,\rightarrow}=\{\lambda_{\zeta}^{(c),t,x,\rightarrow}\}_{\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}}}.

For the initialized boundary state, this description is degenerate, with

\mathcal{X}_{0}^{(c),\rightarrow}
consisting of a single element.

For each
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}
, we first lift the state message on

\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}}
to a branch-prior message on
\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}+1}}
by adjoining the
trivial character on the fresh input coordinate. The resulting branch-prior eigen list

\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{In},(c),t,x,\rightarrow}=\{\lambda_{(\zeta,\eta)}^{\mathrm{In},(c),t,x,\rightarrow}\}_{(\zeta,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}+1}}}

satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{(\zeta,\eta)}^{\mathrm{In},(c),t,x,\rightarrow}=\begin{cases}|\mathcal{G}|\,\lambda_{\zeta}^{(c),t,x,\rightarrow},&\eta=\chi_{\text{triv}},\\[3.44444pt]
0,&\eta\neq\chi_{\text{triv}},\end{cases}

(33)

for
\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}}
and
\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
.

Let

\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sym},(c),t}=\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sys},(c),t}\varoast\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{apr},(c),t}

denote the combined message on the symbol coordinate. Then, for each

x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}
, the branch message is a single

\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}+1}
-covariant PSC with eigen list

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{out},(c),t,x,\rightarrow}=\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{In},(c),t,x,\rightarrow}\varoast\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sym},(c),t}\varoast\Bigl(\varoast_{i=1}^{r_{c}}\bm{\lambda}^{(c),i,t}\Bigr).

(34)

This is the direct analogue of the local branch update in the convolutional decoder, with the
only difference that the symbol component now contains both the systematic observation and the
incoming a priori information.

Marginalizing the dropped symbol from the branch message yields, for each

x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}
, a heralded mixture on the next state. In particular,
the induced message on
S_{t+1}^{(c)}
is characterized by the ensemble

\Bigl\{\bigl(p_{\eta}^{(c),t+1,\rightarrow\mid x},\bm{\lambda}^{(c),t+1,x,\eta,\rightarrow}\bigr)\Bigr\}_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}},

where

\displaystyle p_{\eta}^{(c),t+1,\rightarrow\mid x}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|^{m_{c}+1}}\sum_{\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}}}\lambda_{(\zeta,\eta)}^{\mathrm{out},(c),t,x,\rightarrow}

(35)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{\zeta}^{(c),t+1,x,\eta,\rightarrow}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|\,p_{\eta}^{(c),t+1,\rightarrow\mid x}}\lambda_{(\zeta,\eta)}^{\mathrm{out},(c),t,x,\rightarrow},\qquad\zeta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}}.

(36)

Hence the full next-state message is

\displaystyle W_{t+1}^{(c),\rightarrow}(S_{t+1}^{(c)})=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}}\sum_{\eta\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}p_{x}^{(c),t,\rightarrow}\,p_{\eta}^{(c),t+1,\rightarrow\mid x}\,W_{t+1}^{(c),x,\eta,\rightarrow}(S_{t+1}^{(c)})\otimes\outerproduct{x,\eta}{x,\eta},

(37)

where each
W_{t+1}^{(c),x,\eta,\rightarrow}
is a
\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}
-covariant PSC with eigen list

\bm{\lambda}^{(c),t+1,x,\eta,\rightarrow}
.

Thus, the forward recursion propagates the state channel

W_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}
, or equivalently the heralded family

\Bigl\{\bigl(p_{x}^{(c),t,\rightarrow},\bm{\lambda}^{(c),t,x,\rightarrow}\bigr)\Bigr\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}^{(c),\rightarrow}},

through the constituent trellis rather than a single eigen list.
Similarly, the backward recursion is obtained by applying the same constituent trellis
construction in reverse time. Hence the backward state message is again a finite heralded
mixture of
\mathcal{G}^{m_{c}}
-covariant PSCs on
S_{t}^{(c)}
.

Once the forward and backward state channels are available, they are combined with the local
systematic and parity observations to obtain the posterior message on the branch variable

U_{t}^{(c)}=(u_{t}^{(c)},S_{t}^{(c)})
. Marginalizing this posterior over the state coordinates
yields the posterior message on the information symbol
u_{t}^{(c)}
. If, instead, one omits
the incoming a priori message
\bm{\lambda}^{\mathrm{apr},(c),t}
from the above branch
combination and uses only the state messages together with the systematic and parity channel
observations, then the resulting marginalized message on
u_{t}^{(c)}
is the
extrinsic

message produced by constituent decoder
c
.

The two constituent decoders are coupled through these extrinsic symbol messages. More
precisely, the extrinsic message produced by the first constituent on
g_{t}
is interleaved
and used as the a priori message for the second constituent on
g_{\pi(t)}
. Since

\Pi\colon\mathcal{G}^{T}\to\mathcal{G}^{T}
is an automorphism of the product group, this relabeling is an
instance of the automorphism-factor update and does not introduce any new type of message.
Conversely, the extrinsic message produced by the second constituent is deinterleaved by

\Pi^{-1}
and then used as the a priori message for the first constituent. The equality
constraint between the two copies of the same information symbol is therefore enforced through
the equality-factor combination, while the interleaver acts only by automorphism relabeling.

Therefore turbo decoding over a finite abelian group is obtained by combining the following
ingredients:

1.

the finite-state-machine quantum message passing recursion for each constituent convolutional encoder,

2.

equality-factor combination on the information symbols shared by the constituents,

3.

automorphism relabeling induced by the interleaver and deinterleaver.

Hence, no additional local quantum message-passing rule is needed to construct the turbo
decoder beyond the rules already developed in Section
3
. In particular, when

\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, the above construction reduces to the usual
q
-ary turbo-decoding
viewpoint, while the present formulation also applies to turbo constructions over arbitrary
finite abelian groups.

5
Density Evolution

In this section, we discuss density evolution (DE), which is a standard asymptotic method for analyzing message-passing decoders on sparse or locally tree-like graphs
[
22
]
. In the context of decoding binary LDPC codes on qubit classical-quantum channels, Monte Carlo based DE was proposed in
[
4
]
. Later, this was extended to the analysis of binary polar codes
[
13
,
14
]
and binary turbo codes
[
19
]
. More recently, eigen list based DE was developed for
q
-ary LDPC and polar codes on symmetric PSCs
[
16
]
.

In the present framework, DE is based on the same sufficient statistics used for quantum message passing. In particular, each message is represented by the corresponding eigen list indexed by characters of the dual group, together with the associated herald values whenever a local update produces a finite heralded mixture. By Theorem
22
, exact quantum message passing on a tree factor graph preserves this class of messages. Therefore, DE in the present setting requires no new local update rule beyond those already developed in Section
3
. When
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, this reduces to the eigen list based DE formulation of
[
16
]
. For a general finite abelian group, the same idea extends directly after replacing the cyclic Fourier index by a character of
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
and using the corresponding abelian-group updates for equality, check, homomorphism, marginalization, and automorphism factors. Hence, for LDPC and polar codes, the DE procedures in
[
16
]
generalize directly to the present setting.

Another advantage of this representation is that the performance quantities introduced in Section
2
can also be tracked during DE. Indeed, once the posterior message on a target symbol is represented by an eigen list, Lemma
13
yields the corresponding symmetric Holevo information, while Lemma
14
yields the corresponding PGM symbol error probability. Therefore, on a tree factor graph, DE can be used to track, at each stage, the symmetric Holevo information and the PGM error rate associated with decoding the root-node symbol. In particular, the posterior message obtained at the root gives the corresponding estimate of the symbol error probability for decoding that symbol.

For convolutional and turbo codes, the constituent decoder is already described in the previous subsection through the finite-state-machine recursion. Therefore, DE is obtained by tracking the extrinsic message on a typical information symbol under the random-interleaver ensemble. In each constituent decoder, the forward and backward state messages are combined with the local systematic and parity observations, and marginalization over the state coordinates yields the posterior message on the information symbol. Omitting the incoming a priori message on the target symbol gives the corresponding extrinsic message. Thus, one DE update for turbo decoding is obtained by sampling incoming a priori eigen lists, applying the constituent forward-backward recursion on a selected window, and recording the resulting extrinsic eigen list on the target symbol. The extrinsic message from one constituent is then interleaved and used as the a priori message for the other constituent, while deinterleaving returns the updated message for the next outer iteration. Since the interleaver is an automorphism of the product group, this step again introduces no new type of message.

Because the exact trellis recursion propagates finite heralded mixtures, a Monte Carlo implementation of DE may sample the herald values generated by the local updates and retain the corresponding outgoing eigen list. This is sufficient for eigen list based DE, where one iteratively updates a population of extrinsic messages and estimates the resulting symbol error probability from the posterior eigen list using the pretty-good measurement. In this sense, DE for turbo codes in the present paper is the direct abelian-group extension of the eigen list based DE viewpoint of
[
16
]
, with the constituent update supplied by the finite-state-machine recursion above. In the binary turbo setting, our eigen list based DE implementation recovers the numerical thresholds reported in
[
19
]
.

\displaystyle\ket{\psia_{(a,b,c)+(2,0,1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{G}_{1}|}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}_{1}}}\sqrt{\lambda^{(1)}_{\chi}}\,\chi\big((a,b,c)+(2,0,1)\big)\ket{\chi}

\displaystyle=\ket{\psia_{(a,b,c)}}.
