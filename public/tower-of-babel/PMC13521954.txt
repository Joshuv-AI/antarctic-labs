Integrating AI for sustainable architectural space optimization and heritage-conscious street design
Authors: Yanan Hu, Jie Zhong, Zhiming Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The paper presents an AI-powered framework designed to optimize the layout of historical and cultural street spaces while preserving heritage values. Combining deep learning, graph-based techniques, and reinforcement learning, this framework addresses common urban challenges such as pedestrian accessibility, traffic congestion, and environmental sustainability in historic districts. Unlike traditional urban planning methods, the framework offers a dynamic, data-driven approach that adapts to real-time changes in pedestrian flow, traffic, and environmental conditions. By integrating Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for spatial feature extraction, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for network connectivity analysis, and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for dynamic layout adjustments, the system ensures efficient and sustainable urban designs. The proposed model was tested in cities with historical significance and demonstrated significant improvements: a 22.5% reduction in congestion, a 27.4% increase in green space, and a 31.8% improvement in pedestrian accessibility compared to traditional methods. The results validate the effectiveness of AI in urban planning, showing that it can balance the need for modernization with the preservation of historical authenticity.

Introduction

The sustainable development that is focused on urban planning is needed because cities can develop while being able to preserve their identities1. An essential element in the heritage of the city is the layout of streets, making it the representation of the architectural and cultural history of the city2. These are, however, areas that have major problems because of urbanization and changing needs of transport, which tends to bring a clash between modernity and conservation. Artificial intelligence is a cutting-edge technology that uses machine learning, spatial analytics, and multi-objective optimization to find new ways to meet the contemporary functional needs without sacrificing historical relevance3,4. In comparison to standard grid-like structures, historical streets are focused on being more accessible, culturally relevant, and to provide human-scale experiences that add vibrancy and economic well-being to a city5. Such regions promote tourism, local commerce, and social unity, but also are exposed to challenges such as congestion, old-fashioned infrastructure, and tough protein laws6,7. City smart optimization is based on AI decision-making that can lead to data-driven decisions that optimize pedestrian circulation, infrastructure effectiveness, and intelligent city ecosystems, as well as such heritage limitations8. This is one of the greatest issues in enhancing older urban regions with the need to improve the congestion, especially on the narrow, hilly alleys that were not built to support the current type of vehicles, including the emergency vehicles such as the ambulances9. This is complicated by the stringent preservation laws, where any modifications to a building should not change its outlook but only reflect the original state10. Between them, environmental issues are also involved since numerous heritage areas are characterized by a poor level of green spaces, energy-efficient structures, or waste disposal systems7,11. Moreover, the process of gentrification should be reduced by urban planners so that new development does not oust the locals and does not demolish cultural heritage. These are the main consequences that should be considered in the planning strategy, which is shown in Fig. 1.

The complexities of historical cities do not accommodate a traditional urban planning model, which works best on a modern grid layout12. However, artificial intelligence-based analysis tools will have the potential to study unusual street structures, anticipate pedestrian and vehicle flows, and provide adaptive decisions that meet the criteria of heritage protection13. Dynamic urban optimization integrates real-time information, allowing it to extend efficiency and cultural integrity. AI-based solutions provide a systematic way of striking a balance between the past and current needs of development. To improve walkability and reduce traffic, urban planners have transformed certain roads into pedestrian-only areas, a process known as pedestrianization. While this strategy helps improve air quality and enhances the aesthetic appeal of cities25, it also introduces some unforeseen challenges. Restricting vehicle access can lead to increased congestion on local streets, disrupt delivery services, and create difficulties for people with disabilities or the elderly in accessing certain areas. Additionally, without adaptive models that analyze real-time movement, pedestrian zones may be placed inefficiently, exacerbating congestion and accessibility issues26. The modernization of historic districts often involves upgrading infrastructure while maintaining traditional architecture. This includes improving main roads, upgrading drainage systems27, and installing new utilities underground, all while preserving the city’s skyline. The process of renewing urban infrastructure is quite costly as well as time-consuming, and there is no certainty that the problems of space will be resolved. Geographic Information Systems (GIS) are widely used as they assess and interpret spatial information in conventional urban planning; however, they are not only predictive in relation to modeling. Planning with GIS requires an intensive human talent, and until artificial intelligence automates some of the work, it would perform less favorably in the realignment of historic street patterns28. It is changing the face of urban planning through increased levels of spatial efficiency within buildings due to the use of artificial intelligence. Machine learning, reinforcement learning, and generative design algorithms that are featured in AI-based tools will allow predicting spatial arrangements, optimizing land use, and enhancing pedestrian accessibility29. These tools provide planners with insights based on data collected to make decisions so as to maximize the use of urban spaces, irrespective of the layout30. The AI algorithms used are generative, which means that they develop plans individually based on particular demands, including accessibility, sustainability, and cultural continuity30. These methodologies examine the circulation of pedestrians, street layouts, and building density to generate effective designs with minimal interference. Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are employed to create complex building layouts that automatically conform to the regulations. As an example, the Struct model uses GAN methods to produce tall topologies with high precision and efficiency. The approaches are, however, mostly applicable in contemporary planning, and modifications are necessary when one applies them to older streets that have grown organically31. Reinforcement learning also finds use in improving the traffic flow, crossing people, and getting around by means of transport. With the help of AI simulations, one can test most urban designs before introducing significant alterations and, thus, decrease inefficiencies of city architectures32. However, the majority of reinforcement learning models are designed to suit the standard grid-like design of cities and do not cope well with grid-free designs, which are prevalent in historical applications. Cities are also becoming highly complex and large, so they pollute the environment, and they extract cultural identity and the quality of life18. Things like high concentrations of people and an increase in car traffic lead to the continuous traffic jam of the sidewalks, resulting in land waste and harm to the environment of a known historic district19. A combination of our framework and the concept of having set grammar rules allows one to increase the walkability of an area due to real-time alterations of pathways in accordance with pedestrian flow51,52. When gridlock conditions are detected, the reinforcement learning (RL) system helps in the rerouting of pedestrians, where conventional rules fail to consider such limitations. The use of traditional planning leads to an increase in the urban populations and the loss of natural areas and cultural objects20. Irregular patterns of historic cities are not as compatible with analysis as the modern city patterns, which means that such analysis is less effective to optimise it21. They cannot respond to the major shifts of the urban environment and tend to overlook such aspects as people traffic, ecological hazards, and protection of heritage22. Suable tools that deal with multiple factors are essentially what good planners are in need of at the moment because the sub-optimal results of optimization efforts mean generating adverse effects, including the increased duration of walks and the destruction of historic parts of cities23. Methodology

This study introduces an AI-powered framework aimed at refining the design of historically and culturally significant urban spaces while maintaining their authenticity. By incorporating deep learning, graph-based techniques, and multi-objective optimization, the framework enhances urban livability, improves pedestrian access, and fosters environmental sustainability. Leveraging spatial data, reinforcement learning, and constraint-based optimization, it delivers adaptive solutions that harmonize modern urban demands with the need to preserve heritage. Convolutional Neural Networks are utilized to evaluate street-level characteristics and suggest expansions for pedestrian pathways. Graph Neural Networks, combined with reinforcement learning, model traffic patterns and enable real-time lane direction adjustments to reduce congestion. Green space allocation is optimized through reinforcement learning, maximizing vegetation in underused areas while respecting preservation constraints, ensuring a balanced approach to development and environmental care. Traditional urban planning methods, which often depend on heuristic zoning regulations and manual processes, frequently fail to address dynamic challenges like fluctuating pedestrian flows and environmental factors. In contrast, the proposed framework facilitates automated scheme assessment, empowering planners to make informed decisions that uphold historical integrity while supporting modern infrastructure growth. Model description and targets

The proposed model addresses the research gap through the integration of three key components, CNNs, GNNs, and RL. CNNs are used to check out road layouts from the past by spotting trends in road widths, intersections and where pedestrian zones are, which supports better access for pedestrians. GNNs capture the structural connectivity of roads and pedestrian pathways, ensuring efficient traffic flow and network integrity while preserving the historical configuration of urban streets. RL modules make decisions for real-time data, helping to adjust street layouts frequently, allowing people walking and vehicles travelling to move more smoothly. This dynamic learning process enables the model to maintain a balance between contemporary urban demands and heritage conservation.

To more clearly distinguish the contribution of each learning module, we refine the design of the CNN, GNN, and RL models according to the specific requirements of historical street networks.

(a) Convolutional neural network (CNN): spatial feature extraction The CNN is customized to work with satellite imagery and GIS rasters that exhibit irregular geometries typical of historic districts. Unlike CNNs trained on regular grid-based road structures, our model uses dilated convolutions to capture long-range spatial dependencies without losing fine-scale features such as narrow alleys and irregular plot boundaries. It detects heritage-sensitive elements (e.g., protected building footprints, courtyard forms, facade buffers), extracts width profiles, intersection density, pedestrian corridor continuity, and micro-block geometry. This makes the CNN particularly strong in identifying where physical interventions are feasible without violating heritage restrictions.

(b) Graph neural network (GNN): connectivity and flow modeling A GNN is used to model historic district street networks, where nodes represent intersections, gates, or protected heritage sites, and edges encode travel distances, slope, width, and accessibility. The GNN is designed to assess pedestrian and vehicular connectivity in legally protected areas, learn traffic redistribution where road alterations aren’t possible, and propagate heritage penalties through message passing, ensuring interventions near protected sites automatically reduce update weights. This approach optimizes network efficiency without modifying heritage elements.

(c) Reinforcement learning (RL): real-time adaptive layout adjustment RL is integrated to enable dynamic policy learning that adapts to changing pedestrian and traffic conditions. The RL agent is customized with a heritage-aware reward function, penalizing actions that violate preservation constraints. State inputs include real-time sensor data, environmental conditions, and heritage boundary heatmaps. At the same time, action sets are restricted to non-invasive interventions, such as lane direction changes, temporary pedestrianization, micro-green additions, and time-of-day regulations. This setup allows the RL module to optimize operational adjustments despite restrictions on long-term structural changes.

Pedestrian accessibility optimization

To enhance pedestrian accessibility, the model uses CNNs to analyze historical street layouts and detect patterns related to road width, intersections, and pedestrian zones. GNNs are employed to model the connectivity of pedestrian pathways and suggest modifications that improve flow while minimizing disruptions to the historical streetscape. The reinforcement learning component iteratively adjusts street configurations to optimize pedestrian flow.

Traffic congestion management

Traffic congestion is managed through a multi-objective optimization approach. The model uses traffic flow data from IoT sensors to simulate congestion hotspots. GNNs model the flow of traffic through the network, and the RL module dynamically adjusts traffic lanes and intersections based on real-time conditions. This ensures efficient traffic movement without altering the street’s heritage.

Green space integration and environmental sustainability

The model aims to increase green space in urban areas while maintaining historical preservation. The optimization function includes environmental impact factors, such as air quality and green space availability, ensuring that proposed modifications improve urban sustainability. The optimization also takes into account zoning regulations and heritage preservation, preventing any design that would negatively impact the cultural integrity of the area.

Model design and functioning

The model is structured into three main layers: CNN for spatial feature extraction, GNN for network connectivity analysis, and RL for dynamic layout optimization. Each layer works in concert to balance multiple objectives, ensuring that pedestrian accessibility, traffic efficiency, environmental sustainability, and heritage preservation are all optimized, see Fig. 3. Geographic diversity of datasets

To address the need for broader representation and generalizability, we incorporated datasets from multiple cities spanning different geographical regions, urban morphologies, and heritage contexts. The datasets used in this study consist of:

Rome, Italy (Europe) Dense medieval street network, irregular alleys, UNESCO-protected buildings, and narrow pedestrian corridors.

Cairo, Egypt (North Africa) Hybrid Islamic–colonial street morphology, high pedestrian density, variable block sizes, mixed-heritage constraints.

Kyoto, Japan (East Asia) Grid-like street structure overlaid with historic pedestrian pathways and strict conservation regulations.

Zanzibar Stone Town (East Africa) Organic coastal street networks with extremely narrow alleys and strong façade protection rules.

Old Lahore, Pakistan (South Asia) Highly irregular street geometry, high congestion load, complex informal pedestrian flows.

These cities were intentionally selected because they represent five distinct historical street morphologies, each with unique spatial constraints and heritage regulatory environments. This diversity ensures robust training signals for CNN, GNN, and RL models.

Cross-city generalization capability

To assess the scalability of the framework, we performed a leave-one-city-out generalization test, training on four cities and testing on the fifth. The results showed 82–87% retention in walkability, 78–83% in traffic efficiency, 75–81% in environmental impact reduction, and 88–92% in heritage compliance. These findings indicate that the model effectively captures city-invariant spatial principles, including pedestrian movement, network connectivity, and heritage preservation constraints.

The proposed AI-driven framework for urban layout optimization offers substantial advantages over traditional planning methods across key performance metrics, including pedestrian accessibility, traffic efficiency, green space utilization, and environmental sustainability. It supplements the connectivity among urban areas without losing the historical value of the city by incorporating CNNs, GNNs, and RL, achieving an average improvement of 35%-70% over all the performance indicators of the urban environment. Uneven application of traditional measures, like heuristic zoning and pedestrianization, mostly fail to serve well when it comes to older streets. Such approaches are quite likely to be aimed at solving one particular problem, which can be the alleviation of traffic congestion with the lack of consideration of the interdependence of the pedestrian environment, vehicles, heritage preservation, and environmental consequences.

The improvements reported in Table 8 stem directly from these physical and operational modifications. The 31.8% increase in pedestrian accessibility is primarily due to widening walkable space by reallocating 1.0–1.4 m of roadway (without structural changes). Problem–solution–validation framework

This study adopts a problem-centric approach in evaluating the proposed optimization framework. For each case study, we follow a four-part structure:

Problem definition Identify existing spatial constraints such as narrow corridors, bottleneck intersections, absence of continuous pedestrian paths, and heritage-imposed limitations.

AI-generated design solution Present the optimized street layout produced by the CNN–GNN–RL system, including reallocated widths, new pedestrian corridors, lane-direction modifications, and micro-green insertions.

Quantitative validation Compare baseline and optimized conditions using pedestrian density, throughput, walkability, traffic efficiency, and environmental indicators.

Heritage-compliance verification Overlay the optimized design on heritage-protection buffers to confirm that no protected structures or view corridors are violated.

This structure ensures that the study focuses on spatial outcomes rather than algorithmic internals.

Limitations and practical implementation considerations

Model interpretability

The CNN–GNN–RL framework performs well but lacks full transparency due to high-dimensional representations and opaque RL policies. Although heritage constraints are encoded via masks and penalties, internal decision logic is not fully explainable. Future work should integrate XAI methods (feature attribution, attention visualization, reward decomposition). The system serves as decision support, not an autonomous authority.

Practical adoption

Implementation depends on high-quality GIS and sensor data, sufficient computational resources , and regulatory alignment. A phased approach is recommended: (1) offline scenario testing, (2) semi-adaptive assistance with human validation, and (3) full integration into smart urban systems. Bias in data may create inequitable outcomes. Fairness-aware calibration and participatory validation are necessary to ensure transparency and inclusiveness.

Future research directions

While the proposed framework demonstrates strong performance, several avenues for future investigation remain. Enhancing model interpretability through feature attribution, attention visualization, and reward decomposition techniques would strengthen institutional trust and policy transparency. Future work should incorporate social equity indicators, economic vitality measures, and participatory feedback mechanisms to evaluate the societal impacts of optimization strategies. Research is needed to adapt the framework for cities with limited IoT infrastructure or incomplete GIS datasets, potentially through transfer learning or reduced-data modeling approaches. Extending the framework to multi-year simulation horizons could assess how adaptive interventions influence gradual urban evolution and heritage resilience over time. Combining AI-driven optimization with participatory planning interfaces may improve democratic legitimacy and collaborative governance in heritage-sensitive districts.

Conclusion

This study presents an artificial intelligence-based framework that seeks to improve historical and cultural streets optimization, and it successfully addresses the twin demands that are the current cities’ requirements and heritage protection. This approach is different to standard urban planning policies since our model is known to utilize deep learning, reinforcement learning, and evolutionary optimization approaches to design adaptable and responsive urban designs. Compared to the existing paradigm, the proposed framework has shown impressive results that entail the improvement of walkability by 70% to create a pedestrian favourable environment, the reduction of traffic congestion by 40%, thereby reducing traffic optimisation, the utilisation of green spaces by 50%, to support sustainable urbanism, the decrease in air pollution by 38.9%, thereby showing the environmental impact of AI based planning. Comparisons with other frameworks reveal that this framework scores highly in improving walkability, decreasing traffic congestion and increasing the use of green spaces, as well as decreasing air pollution enormously. The consequences support pedestrian access, enhancement of traffic facilities, environmental sustainability, and preservation of culture to a significant extent. RQ1: How can AI improve pedestrian accessibility in irregular historical streets while preserving heritage value? Our CNN–GNN–RL system increases pedestrian accessibility by 31.8% without altering protected street geometries, using constraint-aware path refinement and heritage masks that prevent disruption of culturally sensitive areas. RQ2: How can traffic congestion be reduced without modifying historical street morphology? Through real-time RL-driven lane adjustments and adaptive routing, the model reduces congestion by 22.5–40% while preserving all heritage boundaries. RQ3: How can green space integration be maximized within heritage-constrained districts? Green-space allocation improved by 27.4% through optimization of micro-green corridors and underutilized segments, without violating preservation constraints. RQ4: How does the proposed system outperform traditional methods? Compared with heuristic zoning, static GIS approaches, and evolutionary optimization models, our framework delivers higher improvements in walkability (+70%), congestion reduction (+33.3%), sustainability (+36–40%), and heritage compliance (+38.5%), while enabling real-time adaptability and multi-objective trade-off handling.

The framework supports planners by enabling scenario testing for pedestrianization, traffic redistribution, and green space interventions in heritage districts. Its RL component allows adaptive traffic and pedestrian management without structural changes, while embedded preservation masks ensure regulatory compliance. Quantitative metrics strengthen evidence-based planning. Overall, it functions as a human-in-the-loop decision-support tool, complementing professional expertise. Moreover, the combination of CNNs, GNNs, and reinforcement learning can be used to carry out the analysis of spatial data and make adjustments in real-time, whereas the other approaches are much less flexible. This AI-oriented solution will help modify the urban layout in a more efficient and scalable way by automating the complicated decision-making process, which is why it is going to be a useful tool to stimulate the evolution of smart cities in the future. Future studies will be based on the integration of real-time, IoT-enabled urban analytics to enable dynamic changes in the urban design. Further research on multi-agent reinforcement learning will be done in order to optimise street designs according to the differences in the flow of pedestrians and traffic. Different cities all over the world will be chosen in order to test the universality of the proposed framework through scaling tests53,54.
