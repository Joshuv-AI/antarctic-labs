Omics signature of new-onset mild cognitive impairment and dementia in a population-based study
Authors: Yanxia Lu, Crystal Tze Ying Tan, Xinyi Gwee, Anis Larbi, Tze Pin Ng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Plasma proteomics and metabolomics snapshots reveal a molecular signature in circulation delineating pathophysiology of major and minor neurocognitive disorder. To identify new cues to disease aetiology and diagnostic approach, we applied plasma proteomics and metabolomics profiling platforms to samples collected in a population-based study of the Singapore Longitudinal Ageing Studies Wave 2 (SLAS-2). In this longitudinal study, blood samples were analysed with standard clinical chemistry, plasma proteomics (Sengenics) and metabolomics (Nightingale) panels. Participants were followed up for the development of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and dementia for 3-5 years. Of the total 1,892 molecules in all assay types, 463 demonstrated significant associations with baseline prevalent MCI and dementia. We trained an automatic linear modelling of predictors for follow-up new-onset MCI and dementia. The best model consists of 10 variables including ZSCAN18, PRKD3, SPANXN4, DDX43, saturated fatty acids, PPP3CA, NFATC4, IL-8, PAK6, and PDGFB. In terms of molecular function, these molecular markers are involved in immunological dysfunction and inflammatory reaction, protein coding, lipids, DNA-binding transcription factor activity, and nervous system development. In conclusion, our current research has identified an omics signature linked to new-onset mild cognitive disorder and dementia, which we hope can help enhance the accuracy of their diagnosis using circulating blood samples.

Introduction

Brain and cognitive ageing, which induce great medical, social, and economic impact worldwide, are mostly concerned by the elderly. Currently there are over 55 million patients worldwide who suffer from dementia, and every year, there are nearly 10 million new cases. Dementia is the 7th leading cause of mortality globally and represents a primary contributor to disability and care dependency worldwide. The growing prevalence of dementia and associated mortality and morbidity pose a heavy burden to many families and the society. Research on mild cognitive impairment (MCI), a recognized dementia prodrome, enables earlier diagnosis and preventive interventions of dementia.

The pathophysiology of dementia and MCI is complex and still not fully understood. So far, scholars have identified cerebrospinal fluid amyloid β, p-tau and t-tau as the central biomarker of dementia, and peripheral markers, which facilitate non-invasive and non-expensive diagnostic method and novel treatment, is not found yet. Brain pathology starts to be generated in the preclinical phase and MCI which can be years before the symptoms of dementia1. There is a broad understanding that individuals with dementia have a dysregulation of physiological and metabolic processes involving pathogenic tau and amyloid conformations, modulated by neuroendocrine, hormonal and immune inflammatory mediators2. Evidence indicates chronic inflammation serves as a key pathophysiological mechanism driving β-amyloid peptide dysregulation, including both metabolic alterations and pathological accumulation3. Metabolomics/ Lipidomics dysregulation may constitute a fundamental pathophysiological mechanism in dementia4. Limited evidence documents alterations in metabolites, lipids, and associated pathways in dementia—spanning amino acid/cholesterol/neurotransmitter biosynthesis, lipid metabolism, and urea/TCA cycle dynamics5. Proteomics, predominantly target synaptic architecture and immune regulation pathways, may have the potential to be key biomarkers of dementia and cognitive decline. Elevated cerebrospinal fluid concentrations of Chromogranin A, neurexin-3, and neuronal pentraxin 1 were demonstrated in patients transitioning from MCI to dementia6. A separate investigation identified significant associations between dementia and elevated blood levels of specific proteins (kinases, pancreatic polypeptide, β-microglobulin, carcinoembryonic antigen, superoxide dismutase)7. However, subsequent replication attempts failed, likely attributable to methodological variations across studies or inadequate statistical power from limited sample sizes. So far, most studies are limited to only utilizing information from one type of -omics with tests of not many markers. Integrative multi-omics approaches are essential to decode the molecular complexity underlying multifactorial diagnostics and precision therapeutics8,9.

Recent advances in omics technology offer new opportunities for comprehensive discovery of proteins and metabolites in biological fluids as biomarkers for comprehensive delineation of multi-tiered endophenotypic architectures and dementia-associated pathobiological interactomes. In contrast to genomic and transcriptomic data, proteomics and metabolomics data represent downstream perturbations in disease mechanisms that have direct implications in the biological functions and processes, influenced by environmental factors. By profiling large-scale -omics data across individuals, such as proteomics and metabolomics, can provide a more comprehensive view of the complexity of dementia and MCI aetiology than individual -omics analysis alone10. In the present study, we had extensively profiled both proteomics and metabolomics on top of standard clinical blood tests in a population-based Chinese sample from the Singapore Longitudinal Ageing Study Wave 2 (SLAS-2). This is, to the best of our knowledge, the first study in multi-omics of 1,892 molecules in a population-based sample of elderly with dementia and MCI. By using a multi-omics approach, we aim to identify joint molecular signatures of dementia and MCI that help reveal rich new insights into the disease mechanisms and provide new leads for novel and potential targets for treatment or prevention.

Methods

Study design and participants

Participants comprised community-residing older adults (≥55 years) from the Ageing Immunology and Multi-omics Study sub-cohort11 within the Singapore Longitudinal Ageing Study Wave 2 (SLAS-2)12. Participants completed 5–6 structured interview sessions, clinical/performance-based assessments, and venesection procedures to enable multidimensional profiling of demographic, neurocognitive, medical, psychosocial, and biological parameters. The enrolled participants at baseline were followed up for a median of 3.8 years for the incidence of MCI and dementia. Ethical approval was obtained from the National University of Singapore Institutional Review Board, with written informed consent acquired from all participants.

Diagnosis of MCI and dementia

A standardized dementia assessment protocol was employed for cognitive surveillance (detecting impairment and decline) and clinical diagnosis of MCI and dementia. Initial screening for MCI and dementia employed the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) using education-stratified normative data13. Participants scoring <27 on MMSE, or demonstrating ≥2-point decline from baseline or ≥1-point/year deterioration, underwent subsequent comprehensive neuropsychological assessment and Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR).

The standardized neuropsychological battery assesses five domains: 1) memory (Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test immediate/delayed recall14, visual reproduction immediate/delayed recall)15; 2) executive function (Symbol Digit Modality Test16, Design Fluency17, Trail Making Test Part B18); 3) language (categorical verbal fluency17); 4) visuospatial skills (block design17); and 5) attention (digit span forward/backward19, spatial span forward/backward 17). Psychology research assistants with standardized training administered the tests. The specifics of neuropsychological testing protocols and associated normative standards were documented in prior work20,21.

Certified research nurses conducted the Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR)22 assessment following standardized protocols (http://www.biostat.wustl.edu/~adrc/cdrpgm/). Functional status was quantified through dependency levels in 10 basic activities of daily living (BADL)23: bowel control, bladder control, grooming, toilet use, feeding, transfers, ambulation, dressing, stair navigation, and bathing.

A multidisciplinary panel of geriatricians, psychiatrists, and neurologists established MCI and dementia diagnoses through review of clinical assessments, with final consensus adjudication based on Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (Fourth Edition, DSM-IV)24 criteria. MCI diagnosis required fulfillment of: 1) Subjective memory/cognitive complaints or the Informant Questionnaire on Cognitive Decline in the Elderly (IQCODE)25,26 score >3.3; 2) Objective impairment in ≥1 cognitive domain: MMSE 24-27 or ≥2-point MMSE decline from baseline plus neuropsychological score 1-2 standard deviations (SDs) below age/education-adjusted norms or 0.5 SD decline during follow-up; 3) CDR global = 0.5; 4) Essentially preserved BADL independence; and 5) Absence of dementia. Dementia diagnosis mandated: 1) objectively verified cognitive impairment (MMSE <24 or age/education-adjusted neuropsychological domain score ≥2 SD below normative mean), and 2) functional disability evidenced by either dependency in ≥1 basic ADL or CDR global score ≥1. Individuals failing to satisfy diagnostic thresholds for MCI or dementia were designated as cognitively unimpaired.

Baseline characteristics of study population

These variables encompassed age, sex, educational level (none or primary, secondary and above), smoking status (non-smoker, ex-smoker, current smoker), body mass index (BMI, defined as weight divided by height squared), depression, frailty, and APOE-ε4 carrier status. Depression was defined by Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS-15 items) score greater than 5 or a medical history of depression27. Frailty was evaluated according to the framework proposed and validated by Fried et al. in the Cardiovascular Health Study (CHS)28, comprising five physical components: (1) Involuntary weight loss (BMI < 18.5 kg/m2 and/or unintentional loss exceeding 10 lbs (4.5 kg) over the preceding 6 months); (2) Slowness (average of two 6-meter fast gait speed tests falling in the sex- and height-stratified lowest quintile); (3) Weakness (average knee extension strength from three trials on the dominant knee in the sex- and BMI-adjusted lowest quintile); (4) Exhaustion (score below 10 on the 12-Item Short Form Survey (SF-12) vitality domain); (5) Low physical activity level (based on self-reported daily hours engaged in light, moderate, and vigorous activities). Low physical activity was determined by the lowest sex-specific quintile of weekly time devoted to moderate or vigorous activities. Participants exhibiting three or more components were classified as frail, those with one or two as pre-frail, and those without any as robust. APOE genotyping was determined using polymerase chain reaction (PCR) with subsequent restriction endonuclease digestion of the amplified product. Individuals possessing an APOE-ε4 allele (ε2/4, ε3/4, ε4/4) were categorized as carriers; whereas those without an ε4 allele (ε2/2, ε2/3, ε3/3) were categorized as non-carriers.

Standard clinical blood testing

We performed quantification of 153 plasma proteins from multiplexed Luminex® assays (MILLIPLEX® immunoassays) which were reported previously11,12. This included the commercially available assays of Human Cardiovascular Disease Panel 3, Human Metabolic Hormone Panel, Human Soluble Cytokine Receptor Panel, Human Soluble Cytokine Receptor Panel, Human Sepsis Panel 2, Human Cytokine/ Chemokine Panel 2, Human Bone Panel, Human Kidney Injury Panel 6, Human MMP Panel 1, Human TIMP Panel 2.

Proteomics assay

A functional protein microarray platform (i-Ome® Protein Array Kit, Sengenics) was used for proteomics profiling against 1600 autoimmune proteins. The biomarker discovery platform utilizes a KREXTM protein folding technology that produces highly specific signals with low background noise, enabling the quantification of protein analytes that are correctly folded, full-length and functional29,30. The platform is a patented technology that uses a biotin carboxyl carrier protein (BCCP) domain affinity tag to promote the fusion of post-translationally biotinylated proteins to an arrayed surface. The platform attaches each arrayed protein to the surface exclusively via an affinity tag, specifically the BCCP domain of the E. coli acetyl CoA carboxylase.

Metabolomics assay

Quantification of plasma metabolites was performed via high-throughput nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy (Nightingale Health Plc). The assay measures 250 metabolic analytes, including triglycerides and cholesterols, lipid concentrations across 14 lipoprotein subclasses, fatty acids, and low-molecular-weight metabolites such as amino acids, ketones, and glycolysis metabolites. The Nightingale NMR platform quantifies these measures robustly in a single experiment. It has obtained multiple regulatory clearances, such as CE marking, with 37 biomarkers in its panel authorized for diagnostic applications. Details of the experimental methods and applications of this assay in epidemiological studies are documented in prior publications31,32.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS software (version 23). We compared demographic and clinical characteristics between the cognitively impaired (MCI/dementia) and cognitively normal groups using χ2 tests for categorical variables and independent t-tests for continuous variables. For the single variable analysis, the associations of omics with baseline prevalent and follow-up new-onset MCI and dementia (versus normal cognition) were investigated using logistic regression without adjustment, with adjustment of age and sex, and with additional adjustment of formal education level, smoking, and ApoE4 carrier. We reported odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and corresponding p-values. All p-values were corrected for multiple-testing using the Q-value method33 and those with Q-value < 0.1 were considered statistically significant given the moderate number of significant findings. Automatic linear modellings of predictors for baseline prevalent and follow-up new-onset MCI and dementia were performed.

Discussion

In our current research, we’ve taken a systematic look at 1,892 molecules across standard clinical chemistry, plasma proteomics, and metabolomics panels. To the best of our knowledge, this marks the first time we’ve developed and optimized a model tailored to newly diagnosed MCI and dementia within a population-based study framework. The model consists of 10 variables including ZSCAN18, PRKD3, SPANXN4, DDX43, saturated fatty acids, PPP3CA, NFATC4, IL-8, PAK6, and PDGFB. We hope our findings can support a non-invasive, cost-effective diagnostic approach that could be useful for Alzheimer’s disease research and global dementia care networks, and potentially help enhance treatment and disease management efforts.

Our results show that in standard clinical chemistry, inflammatory cytokine IL-8 is a biomarker of MCI and dementia. This is consistent with studies indicating that the development of MCI and dementia has strong interactions with immune mechanisms and chronic inflammation34. IL-8's role in neurodegeneration appears complex and multifaceted, involving inflammation, neurotoxicity, blood-brain barrier disruption, and oxidative stress; yet it remains poorly characterized35. IL-8, a proinflammatory cytokine primarily produced by macrophages, acts chiefly as a chemotactic signal recruiting neutrophils to sites of inflammation within the bloodstream. Dementia patients exhibited significantly higher cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) IL-8 concentration compared to non-demented controls. Furthermore, the presence of CXCR2 (the IL-8 receptor) in dystrophic neurites implies its involvement in dementia-related neuroinflammation. Polymorphisms in the IL-8 gene may influence susceptibility to dementia. IL-8 may promote Tau phosphorylation, leading to the formation of NFTs. Moreover, elevated IL-8 concentrations in CSF demonstrated significant correlations with the Aβ-42/Aβ-40 ratio, MMSE scores, total Tau, and pTau181 levels across the entire cohort36. It is postulated that pro-inflammatory cytokines participate in divergent and opposing processes within a harmful cyclical pattern. Amyloid aggregation might trigger excessive IL-8 production37. Conversely, pro-inflammatory cytokines may also contribute to Aβ plaque deposition by modulating amyloid precursor protein (APP) processing38 and regulation of Aβ clearance39. Furthermore, pro-inflammatory cytokines might stimulate tau hyper-phosphorylation40.

A key feature of dementia pathology, especially Alzheimer’s disease, is the accumulation of insoluble protein aggregates, specifically amyloid plaques composed of Aβ fibrils and neurofibrillary tangles composed of tau fibrils41. Thus we speculate that proteomics is important in the study/context of MCI and dementia. The results support our hypothesis, and among the 10 markers in the final best model of new-onset mild cognitive impairment and dementia, 8 were observed as proteins, providing novel insights into biological changes potentially occurring before dementia onset. There have been several studies have employed proteomic approaches to identify disparities in blood proteins between individuals with and without dementia42–44. Nevertheless, the majority of these studies employed a cross-sectional design, failing to account for potential reverse causality. They also did not investigate whether aberrant protein levels existed prior to the clinical onset of dementia, or how long before diagnosis such abnormalities might be detectable. Recently, two longitudinal studies utilized proteomic profiling to forecast the development of dementia45,46. Yet, these two studies and our study, all three studies generated different protein analytes which could successfully predict the onset of dementia. The reasons could be various, especially that our study is special in terms human population and predicted outcomes. Most studies have been performed in Caucasians, but our study was conducted in an Asian population. The predicted outcomes in our study include not only dementia, but also the onset of MCI, which could facilitate the early detection and prevention of dementia. A recent study found that alterations in the CSF proteome in dementia correlate with the progression of Aβ plaque and tau tangle pathology47. Dementia-associated proteins exhibit differential relationships with the neurobiological processes of dementia. Some show stronger associations with neurodegeneration in Alzheimer’s-vulnerable regions, while others are more closely linked to white matter pathology48. PRKD3, a serine/threonine kinase, is a member of the protein kinase D family comprising PRKD1, PRKD2, and PRKD3. PRKD3 activation is induced by numerous stimuli, such as phorbol esters and agonists of G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). PRKD3 drives cancer cell proliferation, growth, migration, and invasion across multiple tumor types, such as colorectal, gastric, hepatic, prostate, and breast cancer. Mounting evidence supports PRKD3 as a promising therapeutic target in cancer treatment49. According to a new study, PRKD3 is a risk marker for dementia50. As the catalytic subunit of calcineurin (Serine/threonine-protein phosphatase 2B), PPP3CA participates in both nuclear factor-activated T cell 1 (NFATc1) and calcium signaling pathways, and is targeted by immunosuppressive blocking drugs. PPP3CA splice variants are linked to Alzheimer’s disease onset51, while the protein exhibits broader functions and dementia associations52. Initially discovered in T cells, NFAT is a key transcription factor driving inflammatory gene expression and regulating immune responses. Calcineurin (CaN), a Ca2⁺-dependent protein phosphatase, regulates NFAT-dependent inflammatory mediator production by dephosphorylating NFAT and enhancing its transcriptional activity. Robust evidence links aberrant CaN/NFAT signalling to key dementia pathologies, including neuronal apoptosis, synaptic deficits, and glia activation. Consequently, NFAT isoforms contribute to Alzheimer’s disease progression across stages, with specific phases correlating with cognitive decline. Targeted inhibition of astrocytic NFAT activation – using classical CaN/NFAT inhibitors (tacrolimus/cyclosporine) or adeno-associated viruses – alleviated dementia symptoms by reducing β-amyloid neurotoxicity and neuroinflammation53. PAKs comprise a family of proteins that significantly involved in neurodegenerative diseases, including dementia54. Native PAK6 localizes to both the centrosome and cilium, promoting ciliogenesis in tumor cells, neurons, and astrocytes55. Double knockout of PAK5/PAK6 in mice results in locomotor deficits and impaired learning and memory56,57. PDGFB is a gene that encodes a growth factor involved in cell proliferation, migration, survival and chemotaxis. There is a study which reported the association of PDGFB mutations and brain calcifications and dementia58. We report in this study firstly the role of ZSCAN18, SPANXN4, and DDX43 in dementia and cognitive function.

Metabolomic profiling of human tissues and fluids identifies disease-linked metabolite alterations across cognitive health, MCI, and dementia, serving as predictors of AD progression59. Diverse biological matrices and robust analytical platforms establish metabolomics as a vital, rigorous tool for elucidating dementia pathophysiology60. Specifically, metabolomics provides unique advantages including: (I) elucidating disease mechanisms through dynamic biological system alterations; (II) discovering novel candidate biomarkers and associated pathways; (III) enabling more effective clinical translation61; and (IV) facilitating biomarker panels that reduce confounding effects60. Regarding specific metabolomics, our results show that saturated fatty acids are a biomarker of incident MCI and dementia. This is consistent with several lines of existing studies suggesting that saturated fatty acids increase dementia risk62. Takechi et al. observed compromised BBB integrity and cerebrovascular inflammation in amyloid transgenic mice fed a high saturated fatty acid diet (20% of energy). After 12 weeks, mice fed the high saturated fatty acid diet exhibited a 30-fold increase in BBB dysfunction63. In transgenic mice, compared to a soy oil-based diet, a 40% saturated fat Western diet administered for 4 months elevated cerebral Aβ levels64. In an epidemiology study of the Chicago Health and Aging Project (CHAP), higher intake of saturated fatty acids correlated with elevated AD risk65. Chronic high-level saturated fat intake may disrupt the BBB, promote Aβ aggregation, impair cognition, and reduce glucose metabolism in critical brain regions.

The strengths of our study, which include the longitudinal design, community-based sampling, and high-throughput approach, enabled both replication of existing findings and identification of unprecedented plasma biomarkers. Employing state-of-the-art technology, we conducted a systematic multi-omics assessment (clinical blood chemistry, proteomics, and metabolomics) to identify dementia biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets. Nevertheless, these findings should be interpreted within the context of older Chinese adults. Population characteristics limit the generalizability of the findings to other racial/ethnic groups. It is also a limitation of the present study that the clinical blood chemistry does not include the neurodegenerative disease panel of Luminex assays which may predict the occurrence of dementia and MCI. Furthermore, although automatic linear modelling automatically selects the best subset of variables for the model66, validation of the identified markers is not performed. It could not exclude the possibility that the resulting models are susceptible to overfitting. Further studies should also evaluate the prognostic significance of these blood multi-omic markers in predicting MCI progression to dementia and related health outcomes.
