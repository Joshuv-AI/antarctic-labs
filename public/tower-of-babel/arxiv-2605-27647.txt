A Note on Boosting Uncloneable Encryption in Microcrypt
James Bartusek, Eli Goldin

Abstract
In this note, we consider the setting of uncloneable encryption satisfying uncloneable indistinguishability, a form of symmetric key encryption that prevents the cloning of ciphertexts in a very strong sense. Our goal is to minimize the assumptions under which (many-time secure) uncloneable encryption is known to exist, assuming the existence of an information-theoretic “uncloneable bit”, i.e. a one-time secure uncloneable encryption scheme for one-bit messages. We observe that if a t → t ′ t\to t^{\prime} uncloneable bit exists, then the following implications hold. 1. If many-time secure symmetric key encryption exists, then many-time secure t → t ′ t\to t^{\prime} uncloneable encryption for arbitrary-length messages exists. Since many-time secure uncloneable encryption implies many-time secure symmetric key encryption, this result is tight. 2. If pseudorandom unitaries exist, then many-time secure t → t ′ t\to t^{\prime} uncloneable encryption for arbitrary-length messages with identical copy security exists. These results together show that many-time secure uncloneable encryption may follow from concrete assumptions in “microcrypt”, the world of unstructured quantum cryptography that plausibly exists even if P = N ​ P P=NP .

1.1
Results

First, we show that if an uncloneable bit exists, then reusable UE is
equivalent
to reusable (plain) SKE. In fact, we consider a generalization of the typical 1-to-2 UE security game to any
t<t^{\prime}
, and show the equivalence between reusable
t\to t^{\prime}

t\to t^{\prime}

Theorem 1.1

(Informal)
.

For any
t^{\prime}>t
, assuming the existence of a
t\to t^{\prime}
uncloneable bit and reusable SKE, there exists reusable
t\to t^{\prime}
UE.

Next, we consider variants of the definition of uncloneable encryption. In particular, the flavor of UE that results from the above theorem statement comes with the following caveats.

•

The encryption and decryption key are different. That is, one needs “more” information to encrypt than decrypt, and the cloning game adversaries are only given what is necessary to decrypt. A stronger notion of UE is one in which the encryption and decryption key are identical, which we refer to as “normal form” UE.

•

The
t
challenge ciphertexts given to the cloning adversary are
mixed states
, sampled independently as encryptions of the same message
m

single
encryption of
m
is sampled and the adversary is given
t

We next show how to obtain normal form UE as well as UE with identical copy security, though we do not know how to do so from the generic assumption of reusable SKE. Instead, we assume the existence of
pseudorandom unitaries
, which is nevertheless a standard microcrypt assumption.

Theorem 1.2

(Informal)
.

For any
t^{\prime}>t
, assuming the existence of a
t\to t^{\prime}
uncloneable bit and pseudorandom unitaries, there exists reusable
normal form

t\to t^{\prime}
UE with
identical copy security
.

1.2
Techniques

These results follow from two main compilers, which we first introduce below and then describe in some more detail. While each has appeared in some form in the literature previously, we adapt and generalize the techniques in order to establish the theorems stated above.

Plaintext expansion via DQRE.

1.2.1
Sketch of plaintext expansion

As mentioned above,
[
14
]
shows how to construct
1\to 2
encryption for
\lambda
-bit messages from a
1\to 2
uncloneable bit using a tool known as a decomposable quantum randomized encoding (DQRE). Essentially, DQRE is a procedure that takes a circuit
C
and (potentially quantum) inputs
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}
and produces labels
\widehat{C},\widehat{x}_{1},\dots,\widehat{x}_{\ell}
such that given
\widehat{C},\widehat{x}_{1},\dots,\widehat{x}_{\ell}
, it is possible to recover
C(x)
. Furthermore,
\widehat{C},\widehat{x}_{1},\dots,\widehat{x}_{\ell}
together should reveal nothing about
C
or
x
besides
C(x)
(and the size of the circuit
C
). To satisfy decomposability, we further require that each of the
\widehat{x}_{i}
should depend only on
x_{i}
and not on any of the other (qu)bits of
x
.

To build a (one-time)
1\to 2
scheme for long messages using DQRE and a
1\to 2
uncloneable bit,
[
14
]
gives the following construction. The encryption key (and also the corresponding decryption key) will be a one-time secure uncloneable encryption key
\mathsf{udk}
as well as
\lambda
one-time pads
\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[\lambda]}
. To encrypt a message
m
, first consider the circuit
C[m]
that outputs
m
on all inputs, and compute the decomposable randomized encoding
\widehat{C[m]},\widehat{0},\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{\ell}
, where
\widehat{0}
is the label for a
quantum
input on sufficiently many qubits set to 0. Then, for all
i\in[\lambda]
, set
c_{i,\mathsf{udk}_{i}}=R_{i}\oplus\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{i}
and
c_{i,1-\mathsf{udk}_{i}}\leftarrow\{0,1\}^{\lambda}
. Finally, output

\mathsf{ct}=\left(\widehat{C[m]},\widehat{0},\{\mathsf{ct}_{i,b}\}_{i\in[\lambda],b\in\{0,1\}}\right).

To decrypt, recover
\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{i}=R_{i}\oplus c_{i,\mathsf{udk}_{i}}
and evaluate
\widehat{C[m]},\widehat{0},\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{\ell}
to obtain
m
.

To see why this is secure, consider a pair of challenge messages
m_{0},m_{1}
. The challenge ciphertext is then

\widehat{C[m]},\widehat{0},\{\mathsf{ct}_{i,b}\}_{i\in[\lambda],b\in\{0,1\}}.

Define
D[m_{0},m_{1}]
to be the circuit that on input
(\rho,\mathsf{udk})
applies
\Dec(\mathsf{udk},\rho)\to b
and outputs
m_{b}
. Note that
D[m_{0},m_{1}](\Enc(m_{b}),\mathsf{udk})=C[m_{b}](0,\mathsf{udk})
. And so by the security of the randomized encoding, the challenge ciphertext looks like
\widehat{D[m_{0},m_{1}]},\widehat{\Enc(b)}
followed by
\{\mathsf{ct}_{i,b}\}_{i\in[\lambda],b\in\{0,1\}}
.

But this challenge ciphertext can be simulated given a challenge in the uncloneable bit game (i.e. either
\Enc(0)
or
\Enc(1)
), and so a cloning attack on the construction gives a cloning attack against the
1\to 2
uncloneable bit.

Reducing assumptions to reusable SKE

We first observe that DQRE can be constructed generically from reusable SKE. This follows by combining
[
5
]
, which shows that reusable SKE can be used to build decomposable randomized encodings for
classical
circuits, and
[
10
]
, which constructs DQRE from decomposable classical randomized encodings. Although this observation is folklore, as far as we are aware it has not been written explicitly in the literature. This gives the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assuming the existence of a
1\to 2
uncloneable bit and reusable SKE, there exists one-time secure
1\to 2
UE for any message length.

Lifting to
t\to t^{\prime}
security

Unfortunately, this construction does not work in the
t\to t^{\prime}
setting for
t>1
. This is due to the fact that the encryption key contains one-time pads, which cannot be reused. We resolve this with a natural approach: replace the one-time pad with a reusable secret key encryption scheme. Since secret key encryptions are possibly distinguishable from random strings, we also need to replace the random values
c_{i,1-\mathsf{udk}_{i}}
with encryptions of
0
under some fixed key.

Our resulting scheme thus involves a one-time encryption key
\mathsf{udk}
as well as
2\lambda
additional reusable encryption keys
\{\mathsf{ek}_{i,b}\}_{i\in[\ell],b\in\{0,1\}}
. The decryption key consists of
\mathsf{udk}
as well as the decryption keys indexed by the bits of
\mathsf{udk}
:
\mathsf{dk}_{1,\mathsf{udk}_{1}},\dots,\mathsf{dk}_{\ell,\mathsf{udk}_{\ell}}
. To encrypt, compute the DQRE
\widehat{C[m]},\widehat{0},\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{\ell}
. Then, for all
i\in[\lambda]
, set
c_{i,\mathsf{udk}_{i}}=\Enc(\mathsf{ek}_{i,\mathsf{udk}_{i}},\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{i})
and
c_{i,1-\mathsf{udk}_{i}}=\Enc(\mathsf{ek}_{i,1-\mathsf{udk}_{i}},0)
. Finally, output

\mathsf{ct}=\left(\widehat{C[m]},\widehat{0},\{\mathsf{ct}_{i,b}\}_{i\in[\lambda],b\in\{0,1\}}\right).

As before, decryption follows by recovering
\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{i}=\Dec(\mathsf{dk}_{i,\mathsf{udk}_{i}},c_{i,\mathsf{udk}_{i}})
and evaluating
\widehat{C[m]},\widehat{0},\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\mathsf{udk}}_{\ell}
. Security also follows from a similar argument.

It turns out that we also obtain many-time security for free using this compiler. In particular, an adversary for the uncloneable bit game simulating an adversary for this construction can simulate encryption queries without making use of the decryption key
\mathsf{udk}
. This can be done by setting for all
i\in[\ell],b\in\{0,1\}
,
\mathsf{ct}_{i,b}=\Enc(\mathsf{ek}_{i,b},\widehat{b})
. When
b=\mathsf{udk}_{i}
, this is exactly of the right form, and when
b\neq\mathsf{udk}_{i}
,
\mathsf{dk}_{i,b}
is never revealed and so this is indistinguishable from the honest ciphertext produced by the construction.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
t^{\prime}>t
, assuming the existence of a
t\to t^{\prime}
uncloneable bit and reusable SKE, there exists reusable
t\to t^{\prime}
UE.

A normal form construction.

However, this protocol is not of normal form. Even if the reusable encryption scheme is of normal form and has
\mathsf{ek}_{i,b}=\mathsf{dk}_{i,b}\eqqcolon\mathsf{sk}_{i,b}
, we still are not able to reveal
\mathsf{sk}_{i,1-\mathsf{udk}_{i}}
to the adversary. We would thus like encryption to not make use of
\mathsf{sk}_{i,1-\mathsf{udk}_{i}}
at all. We can do this by ensuring that the reusable encryption scheme used is
pseudorandom
, i.e. honest encryptions should be indistinguishable from the maximally mixed state. In this case, the encryption protocol can sample a random string instead of computing
\Enc(\mathsf{sk}_{i,1-\mathsf{udk}_{i}},0)
. Setting
\mathsf{ek}=\mathsf{dk}=(\mathsf{udk},\mathsf{sk}_{1,\mathsf{udk}_{1}},\dots,\mathsf{sk}_{\ell,\mathsf{udk}_{\ell}})
gives a normal form reusable
t\to t^{\prime}
secure uncloneable encryption scheme from a
t\to t^{\prime}
uncloneable bit along with normal form, pseudorandom, reusable SKE.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any
t^{\prime}>t
, assuming the existence of a
t\to t^{\prime}
uncloneable bit and normal form, pseudorandom, reusable SKE, there exists normal form, reusable
t\to t^{\prime}
UE.

1.2.2
Sketch of identical copy security

Recent work has shown how to construct identical copy secure uncloneable encryption schemes from regular uncloneable encryption along with one-way functions
[
1
,
12
]
. A key result of these works is encapsulated in the following lemma.

Lemma 1.6

(Theorem 1.1 from
[
1
]
)
.

If one-way functions exist, then there exists a quantum channel
\Sim_{t}
such that for all efficiently samplable mixed states
\sigma
there exists a family of efficiently samplable purifications
\{\ket{\phi_k}\}
such that

1.

t
copies of a random sample
\ket{\phi_k}
can be efficiently approximated using
t
i.i.d. copies of
\sigma
. That is,

\Sim_{t}(\sigma^{\otimes t})\approx\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\left[\outerproduct{\phi_k}{\phi_k}^{\otimes t}\right].

2.

\{\ket{\phi_k}\}
is a purification of
\sigma
. That is,

\sigma\approx\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\left[\Tr_{\mathbf{B}}(\outerproduct{\phi_k}{\phi_k}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}})\right].

where here
\approx
refers to computational indistinguishability.

This lemma intuitively means that for any cryptographic primitive with mixed state output, we can replace
t
copies of mixed state output with
t
copies of a random sample of the purification ensemble
\{\ket{\phi_k}\}
. This gives a generic approach for lifting “many copy security” to “identical copy security”. In this note, we reduce the requirements for this lemma from one-way functions to pseudorandom unitaries.

Similar techniques have simultaneously appeared in the quantum learning literature
[
17
,
15
,
13
]
. While the simulator for these works is a bit more complex, the resulting purification is significantly simpler. The “purification channel” stemming from the learning theory literature produces truly random purifications, not just random samples from some ensemble.

In detail, these works show that there exists an efficient quantum channel
\Sim_{t}
such that for any mixed state
\sigma_{\mathbf{A}}=\Tr_{\mathbf{B}}(\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}
,

\Sim_{t}(\sigma^{\otimes t})=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{U\leftarrow\mathsf{Haar}(M)}\left[\left((I_{\mathbf{A}}\otimes U_{\mathbf{B}})\outerproduct{\phi}{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}(I_{\mathbf{A}}\otimes U_{\mathbf{B}}^{\dagger})\right)^{\otimes t}\right].

Replacing
U
with a pseudorandom unitary
U_{k}
establishes that
\Sim_{t}(\sigma^{\otimes t})
is indistinguishable from
U_{k}^{\otimes t}
applied to the
\mathbf{B}
registers of
\ket{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}^{\otimes t}
. That is,
Lemma

1.6
holds under the assumption that pseudorandom unitaries exist. This immediately gives a new version of many of the transformations in
[
1
,
12
]
starting from the assumption that pseudorandom unitaries exist.

Corollary 1.7
.

Suppose pseudorandom unitaries exist. Then

1.

If public key quantum money exists, then multi-copy secure mini-schemes exist.

2.

For any class of functionalities
\mathcal{F}
, if there exists a copy-protection scheme for
\mathcal{F}
satisfying i.i.d.-copy security, then there exists a copy-protection scheme for
\mathcal{F}
satisfying identical-copy security.

3.

If there exists
t\to t^{\prime}
search-secure uncloneable encryption, then there exists
t\to t^{\prime}
identical-challenge search-secure uncloneable encryption.

Although the above-mentioned papers do not explicitly consider indistinguishable secure UE, we observe that the techniques do directly apply to this setting. In detail, the identical copy secure encryption algorithm will take in as its randomness a pseudorandom unitary key
k
. It will then set
\ket{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}
to be the purification of
\Enc(\mathsf{ek},m)
, and output
(I_{\mathbf{A}}\otimes(U_{k})_{\mathbf{B}})\ket{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}}
. Now, challenge ciphertexts will be indistinguishable from
\Sim_{t}(\Enc(\mathsf{ek},m)^{\otimes t})
, and so identical copy security follows from
t\to t^{\prime}
security of the underlying encryption scheme.

Note that if the underlying
t\to t^{\prime}
UE scheme is of normal form, so is the resulting identical copy secure protocol. Recalling that pseudorandom unitaries can be used to construct normal form, pseudorandom, reusable SKE
[
5
]
, we combine this technique with
Theorem

1.5
to establish the following.

Corollary 1.8
.

For any
t^{\prime}>t
, assuming the existence of pseudorandom unitaries and an uncloneable
t\to t^{\prime}
bit, there exists reusable, normal form
t\to t^{\prime}
UE with identical copy security.

Definition 2.1
.

.

We say that

We say that a symmetric encryption scheme

Definition 2.9

(Identical copy security)

Let

be a pure symmetric encryption scheme that satisfies correctness. For any algorithms

2.

Remark 2.10
.

The key difference between standard security and identical copy security is that in the standard security model, the adversary gets many copies of the mixed state encoding the challenge message

. However, in the identical copy setting, the adversary gets many copies of the pure state corresponding to encryptions of

under some fixed randomness
r
.
