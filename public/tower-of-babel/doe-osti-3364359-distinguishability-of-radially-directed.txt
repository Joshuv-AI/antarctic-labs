1. Executive Summary
This project supported a U.S. contribution to the TJ-II stellarator research program focused on distinguishing
radially directed particle transport from energy transport in magnetized fusion plasmas. The motivating physics
issue is that turbulent particle flux and turbulent heat or energy flux are not necessarily locked together: changes
in density, electron temperature, plasma potential, and electric-field fluctuations can produce different phase
relationships and therefore different radial fluxes. Resolving these relationships is important for understanding
improved-confinement behavior, transport-barrier-like regimes, and the broader ability to predict and control
confinement in stellarators and related toroidal devices. The project approach combined diagnostic
development, international experimental collaboration, and advanced fluctuation analysis. The proposed
diagnostic element was a compact magnetically insulated baffled probe (MIBP) array for TJ-II edge
measurements. The MIBP concept exploits magnetic insulation and baffle geometry to control the relative
electron and ion current collected by a probe. In appropriate configurations, this enables improved
interpretation of space-potential fluctuations and temperature-fluctuation contamination, which are central
quantities for phase-resolved particle- and energy-transport analysis. The results of this work also contributed
to the broader TJ-II model-validation program and to international studies of turbulent transport, zonal flows,
Alfvén eigenmodes, radial-electric-field effects, and nonlinear turbulence coupling. Complementary HIBP-
based and probe-based measurements support the interpretation of coherent and broadband fluctuations, and
bispectral analysis helps identify nonlinear wave-wave coupling and spectral energy-transfer pathways. The
public benefit of the project is a stronger experimental basis for validating transport models and diagnosing
turbulence-regulated confinement in magnetic-fusion plasmas.

2. Project Goals and Objectives
The original project was organized around the following goals and objectives:

1. Design, fabricate, install, and evaluate a compact MIB probe array suitable for TJ-II edge-plasma
 measurements, including baffled, shadowed, exposed, grounded, and ball-pen-like probe geometries.
2. Use MIB probe measurements to obtain phase-resolved space potential, electron temperature, density,
 electric-field, particle-flux, and energy-flux information in the TJ-II edge and edge-core overlap region.
3. Complement edge MIB probe measurements with HIBP measurements in the TJ-II core in order to compare
 independent diagnostics and connect boundary transport measurements to core turbulence and zonal-
 flow physics.
4. Quantify the correlation, cross-phase, and nonlinear coupling among density, temperature, potential, and
 electric-field fluctuations to identify mechanisms that decouple particle and energy transport.
5. Strengthen the U.S.-CIEMAT/TJ-II collaboration, promote U.S. participation in Eurofusion stellarator
 research, and train students and early-career researchers in international magnetic-confinement research.

4. Summary of Project Activities over the Entire Period of Funding
4.1 Original hypotheses and scientific rationale
The project was motivated by the hypothesis that turbulent particle transport and turbulent energy transport
can be experimentally distinguished by measuring phase-resolved fluctuations in density, electron
temperature, potential, and electric field. In this picture, the fluctuating radial particle flux depends on the
correlation and cross-phase between density and radial E x B velocity, while the fluctuating energy or heat flux
also depends on temperature fluctuations and their correlations with velocity and density. A diagnostic system
capable of measuring these quantities locally, with sufficient temporal resolution, can determine whether
particle and energy transport are coupled or decoupled in particular regimes.

A second hypothesis was that combining edge MIBP measurements with core HIBP measurements would
provide a stronger basis for diagnosing transport-channel decoupling than either diagnostic alone. MIBP
measurements offer localized access to edge potential and temperature fluctuations, while HIBP
measurements provide plasma potential, density, and magnetic-potential information in the core or interior
plasma. Together these diagnostics can connect boundary transport signatures to global confinement and
zonal-flow behavior.

• The MIBP measurements were intended to complement HIBP measurements in TJ-II. HIBP provides

4.3 MIBP methodology and diagnostic maturation
Yuan et al., 2021 outlines the dependence on baffle geometry, magnetic-field alignment, and the relative
collection of electron and ion saturation currents. It discusses multiple design families, including plug probes,
baffled probes, Katsumata-type probes, ball-pen-style probes, and flush-mounted fusion-boundary
implementations. This synthesis is important for the present project because TJ-II edge conditions require a
robust, interpretable, and minimally perturbative diagnostic that can operate near fusion-boundary-relevant
plasmas while preserving sensitivity to potential and temperature fluctuations. Single-MIBP measurement was
achieved prior to reporting period. Sequential measurements with single MIBP can characterize oscillation
amplitudes and, in selected cases, constrain cross-coherency and cross-phase between potential and
electron-temperature contributions. This is directly relevant to constrained stellarator experiments, where
space, heat load, magnetic geometry, or access limitations may prevent deployment of a full probe cluster at
the desired location (Li et al, 2022). Additional comparison between open-style and baffled probe designs were
compared in preliminary experiments in fusion relevant environments.

Conclusions

Conventional floating probes in magnetized plasmas do not generally measure the plasma space
potential directly. Their floating potential can include significant contributions from electron
temperature, ion temperature, magnetic-field-aligned transport, sheath effects, and probe geometry.
This is a central limitation for fluctuation studies because temperature fluctuations can contaminate the
interpretation of potential fluctuations and, therefore, electric-field and transport estimates. The project
attempts to address this limitation by developing and applying magnetically insulated baffled probe
concepts that deliberately control the relative collection of electron and ion currents. In the open or
exposed configuration, the floating-potential signal retains sensitivity to the usual electron-temperature
contribution. In a properly aligned and optimally baffled configuration, magnetic insulation and probe
geometry suppress the electron-current contribution so that the floating potential more closely tracks
the local plasma space potential. By comparing open, baffled, shadowed, and grounded probe-tip
configurations on the TJ-II probe assemblies, the experiment was designed to separate space-potential
behavior from temperature-related contributions and to benchmark the degree of baffling through
swept I–V characteristics. Yuan et al., (2021) shows that MIBP measurements can reveal oscillations
in electron and ion temperature, electrostatic plasma potential, electron and ion density, instabilities,
waves, and fluctuation-driven transport quantities. It also identifies practical limitations, including the
need for appropriate probe theory, magnetic-field alignment, baffle design, and careful interpretation of
I–V traces in magnetized plasmas.

5.1 Diagnostic finding: a single MIBP can still provide useful fluctuation information
The work of Li et al., (2022) provides a practical reduced-instrumentation pathway. Although a MIBP cluster
gives the most complete instantaneous information, this work shows that one MIBP, used sequentially with
different effective electron-temperature weighting, can provide useful information about oscillation amplitudes
and can sometimes constrain cross-coherency and cross-phase. For the reported WVU Q-machine case, the
analysis distinguished two types of oscillations and showed how to determine minimum and maximum possible
values of electron-temperature fluctuations. This result is directly relevant to TJ-II-style experimental
constraints. In a stellarator campaign, available ports, probe survivability, magnetic-field orientation, heat
loads, and insertion geometry can limit the number of simultaneously deployable probe tips. The single-MIBP
analysis therefore increases the robustness of the overall diagnostic strategy by documenting what can still be
extracted when the ideal full-cluster configuration is not available.

5.2 Physics finding: nonlinear coupling links GAMs and broadband turbulence
The HIBP-based T-10 analysis demonstrated that a 17 kHz geodesic acoustic mode is statistically involved in
three-wave interactions with broadband quasi-coherent turbulence (Riggs et al., 2021). The study used cross-
bicoherence and the real part of the bispectrum to identify both the degree of nonlinear coupling and the
direction of energy transfer. This is important because GAMs and zonal-flow-like structures can regulate
turbulence through flow shear and can therefore influence radial transport.

5.3 Physics finding: TJ-II observations support the ubiquity of zonal-flow-like structures
TJ-II research reported direct experimental observation of long-range correlated structures throughout the
plasma cross-section. These structures were observed in plasma-potential fluctuations but not in density
fluctuations, which is consistent with zonal-flow interpretation. The TJ-II program also showed that radial
electric fields can influence turbulence correlation length and propagation from the edge toward the scrape-off
layer. These results support the broader project goal of using local fluctuation diagnostics to interpret transport
regulation in stellarator plasmas.

5.4 Broader conclusion
The award advanced the diagnostic and analysis foundation for distinguishing particle and energy transport in
stellarator-relevant plasmas. While the MIBP was not used extensively, analysis from HIBP data demonstrates
the ability to identify mode-mode interactions and energy transfer mechanisms in fusion relevant systems.
