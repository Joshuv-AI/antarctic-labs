On the existence of optimizers for nonlinear time-frequency concentration problems: the Born–Jordan distribution
Federico Stra, Erling A. T. Svela, S. Ivan Trapasso

Abstract
We study the L p L^{p} concentration problem for the Born–Jordan distribution in dimension d > 1 d>1 , thus extending the one-dimensional analysis in [Stra–Svela–Trapasso, J. Math. Pures Appl. (2026)]. We show that the existence of concentration optimizers depends on the exponent p p with a critical threshold at p ∗ ​ ( d ) = 2 ​ d d − 2 p_{*}(d)=\frac{2d}{d-2} for d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 (with the understanding that p ∗ ​ ( 2 ) = ∞ p_{*}(2)=\infty ). In particular, for subcritical exponents 1 ≤ p < p ∗ ​ ( d ) 1\leq p<p_{*}(d) we prove that the supremum is finite and is attained, whereas for supercritical exponents p > p ∗ ​ ( d ) p>p_{*}(d) we show that the functional is unbounded. We also provide the complete solution in the (significantly more) challenging critical regime in dimension d = 2 d=2 .

1
Introduction

The Wigner distribution, defined for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\displaystyle Wf(z)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot y}f(x+y/2)\overline{f(x-y/2)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},

is one of the most popular quadratic phase-space representations for signals in view of its many
desirable properties — including being real-valued, continuous and energy preserving. However, as
no time-frequency representation may possess every desirable property at once
[
10
]
, even
the Wigner transform comes with some features that may represent challenging drawbacks in several
applications. For instance, although it may be informally interpreted as a phase-space energy
density of the signal
f
,
Wf
will in general take both positive and negative values. Moreover,
the inherent sesquilinear structure of the Wigner transform produces peculiar
ghost interferences
.
To be more precise, this means that the magnitude of the cross-term
2\Re W(f,g)
in the expansion
of
W(f+g)
may be significantly larger than that of the main terms

Wf+Wg

[
1
]
. Such scattered interference patterns further
obstruct the interpretation of
Wf
as a genuine energy density, hence several alternative phase
space distributions have been introduced in order to tame or circumvent these phenomena. In this
connection, the
Cohen class

[
4
]
consists of distributions of the form

Qf=Wf*\sigma
, obtained by smoothing the Wigner transform with a suitable convolution kernel.

A distinguished member of this family is the
Born–Jordan distribution

[
8
,
9
]
, originally arising in the context of quantization problems in
[
3
]
and popularized as an average of certain generalized Wigner distributions that is effectively able to reduce the magnitude of the ghost frequencies
[
1
,
6
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
\tau\in(0,1)
, define the
\tau
-Wigner distribution

\displaystyle W_{\tau}f(z)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot y}f\bigl(x+\tau y\bigr)\overline{f\bigl(x-(1-\tau)y\bigr)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

(1.1)

The
Born–Jordan distribution
of
f
is defined by

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f(z)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}W_{\tau}f(z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

(1.2)

As a member of the Cohen family, the Born–Jordan distribution has a kernel, which was explicitly determined in
[
5
]
:

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f=Wf*\Theta_{\sigma},\qquad\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\xi)=\begin{cases}-2\mathrm{Ci}(4\pi|x\cdot\xi|)&(d=1)\\
\mathcal{F}(\bm{1}_{\{|s|\geq 2\}}|s|^{d-2})(x\cdot\xi)&(d>1),\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{Ci}(t)=-\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{\cos s}{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s
is the cosine integral function. This peculiar kernel is the root of the poor behavior of the Born–Jordan distribution regarding regularity and decay, since
\Theta_{\sigma}
is not globally integrable in any
p
-norm, and not even locally
L^{\infty}

[
5
]
. In fact, many of the properties of the Born–Jordan distribution, such as continuity and boundedness, are only known to hold when
d=1
due to the decay of the kernel degrading as
d
grows.

In spite of these considerations, the empirical improvement in terms of interference reduction of the Born–Jordan distribution compared to the Wigner one has been recently confirmed in the context of phase-space concentration problems, a classical topic in time-frequency analysis. To be precise, in this note we study the maximal
L^{p}
concentration of the Born–Jordan distribution on a
prescribed phase space subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with positive finite Lebesgue measure, and in particular whether the optimal concentration

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=1\end{subarray}}\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\sup_{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}

(1.3)

is finite and attained. The existence of
local
concentration optimizers has been
investigated only recently in
[
11
]
for the ambiguity transform and
in
[
12
]
by the authors in the case of the Wigner transform in the
framework of concentration compactness at the phase space level. As a byproduct, in that work we
were able to address the Born–Jordan concentration problem in the
1
-dimensional case, as
summarized below.

Theorem 1.2

(
d=1
,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
be a measurable set with
0<\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Omega)<\infty
.
Then for every
p\in[1,\infty)
the functional

J_{\mathrm{BJ}}^{p}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to[0,\infty):f\mapsto\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

is sequentially weakly continuous in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. As such, the supremum

\sup_{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}}<\infty

is attained by an optimizer. When
p=\infty
we have

\sup_{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}}=\pi,

but the supremum is not attained.

These findings are particularly interesting as they show that, in spite of the challenging nature of the Born–Jordan kernel already discussed, the underlying interference reduction mechanism occurs already at the topological level — it is the first non-trivial example of a time-frequency concentration functional that is proved to be weakly continuous. The goal of the present article is to provide a fairly complete picture of the matter in higher dimensions, solving a problem raised at the end of
[
12
]
.

1.1
Main results

We show that the answer to the
L^{p}
concentration problem for the Born–Jordan distribution changes
drastically depending on the exponent
p
and we must therefore distinguish three regimes, where a
crucial role is played by the following
critical exponent
.

Definition 1.3
.

For every positive integer
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
let
p_{*}(d)
be the
critical exponent
defined as

\displaystyle p_{*}(1)

\displaystyle=p_{*}(2)=\infty,

\displaystyle p_{*}(d)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d}\right)^{-1}=\frac{2d}{d-2}\qquad\forall\ d\geq 3.

(1.4)

We stress that the critical exponent for
d>2
coincides with the exponent
2^{*}
of the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p_{*}(d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and this occurrence is not coincidental. Indeed, in (symplectic) Fourier variables the Born–Jordan distribution is obtained from the Wigner distribution by multiplication with
\Theta(x,\xi)=\operatorname{sinc}(\pi\,x\cdot\xi)
, and the asymptotic behavior
\lvert\operatorname{sinc}(t)\rvert\sim|t|^{-1}
as
\lvert t\rvert\to+\infty
suggests heuristically that the Cohen smoothing acts like an inverse first-order multiplier, so that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f
gains roughly one derivative compared with
Wf
. In the natural
L^{2}
-Sobolev scale this is modeled by the Riesz potential
I=(-\Delta)^{-1/2}
, which is bounded
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
precisely at the critical exponent
p=p_{*}(d)
for
d>2
. An equivalent viewpoint that will serve as a main motivation for several constructions in our proofs stems from scale invariance: under the
L^{2}
-preserving scaling
F_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{d/2}F(\lambda x)
one has
\|IF_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}}=\lambda^{d/2-1-d/p}\|IF\|_{L^{p}}
, so scale invariance occurs exactly at
p_{*}(d)
. In dimension
d=2
the critical exponent escapes to
p_{*}(2)=\infty
, which is consistent with the fact that the only borderline case is the endpoint
p=\infty
.

The three following theorems are our main results and they settle the concentration
problem (
1.3
) in high dimension, with the exception of the critical case
in dimension greater than
2
, which is left for future investigation.

Theorem 1.4

(Subcritical case in
d\geq 2
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set with

0<\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega)<\infty
. Then for every
p\in\bigl[1,p_{*}(d)\bigr)
the functional

J_{\mathrm{BJ}}^{p}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,\infty):f\mapsto\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

is sequentially weakly continuous in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
As a consequence,

\sup_{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}<\infty

is attained by an optimizer.

Theorem 1.5

(Critical case in
d=2
)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a measurable set with
\mathscr{L}^{4}(\Omega)>0
. Then

\sup_{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}}=\infty.

Theorem 1.6

(Supercritical case in
d>2
)
.

Let
d>2
be an integer and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set with

\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega)>0
. Then for every
p\in\bigl(p_{*}(d),\infty\bigr]

\sup_{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}=\infty.

Let us emphasize that, regarding the critical and supercritical cases, the previous
Theorem 1.5
and

Theorem 1.6
do not provide information about the attainment or non-attainment of the
supremum, hence leaving open the question whether there exist a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\not\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\infty
. The picture is completed by the following companion result.

Theorem 1.7
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem 1.5
or
Theorem 1.6
, there
exists
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\not\in L^{p}(\Omega)
; in other words, the suprema
in
Theorem 1.5
and
Theorem 1.6
are attained.

Notation and known facts

The Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure (in any ambient dimension)
are denoted by
\mathscr{L}^{d}
and
\mathscr{H}^{d}
respectively;

\omega_{d-1}=\mathscr{H}^{d-1}(S^{d-1})=2\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2)
denotes the surface area of the

(d-1)
-sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with unit radius.

If there is a constant
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
we write
a\lesssim b
, and if
C
depends on a parameter
\lambda
we write
a\lesssim_{\lambda}b
. We write
a\asymp b
to mean that both
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
hold.

For
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote translation by
x
as
T_{x}f(y)=f(y-x)
and modulation by
\xi
by
M_{\xi}f(y)=e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot y}f(y)
. For
z=(x,\xi)
we denote the time-frequency shift by
z
as
\pi(z)f(y)=M_{\xi}T_{x}f(y)
. If a sequence
\{f^{(n)}\}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
converges weakly to
f
we write
f^{(n)}\rightharpoonup f
.

For
\tau\in(0,1)
and
f,f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the
\tau
-Wigner distributions are known to be elements of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\cap C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, covariant under time-frequency shifts,
W_{\tau}(\pi(z)f)=T_{z}W_{\tau}f
, and the associated cross-
\tau
-Wigner distributions
W_{\tau}(f,g)(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot y}f\bigl(x+\tau y\bigr)\overline{g\bigl(x-(1-\tau)y\bigr)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y
satisfy Moyal’s identity:

\langle W_{\tau}(f_{1},g_{1}),W_{\tau}(f_{2},g_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\langle g_{2},g_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We also have the symmetry relation
\overline{W_{\tau}f}=W_{1-\tau}f
.
When
d>1
the Born–Jordan distribution is known to be real-valued and covariant under
time-frequency shifts.

3.1
Preliminary lemmas: Wigner and Born–Jordan estimates

We now arrive at the crucial estimates necessary to prove
Theorem 1.5
and

Theorem 1.6
:
Subsection 3.1
provides a lower bound for

\Re W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,\xi)
in terms of
W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)
,

W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R\geq 1
,
\tau\in(0,1)
and
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

4(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\leq 1.

Then

\Re W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,\xi)\geq\cos\bigl(4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0).

Naturally, the lemma becomes more useful when
8(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert<1
, so that

\big\lvert 4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\big\rvert<\pi/2
and therefore

\cos\bigl(4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)>0
.

Proof.

The crucial observation is that in the definition of
W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,\xi)
we can restrict the
domain of integration to just

\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lvert y\rvert\leq 2(R+\lvert x\rvert)\}.

Indeed, for the integrand function to be nonzero we must have both
1\leq\lvert x+\tau y\rvert\leq R
and

1\leq\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert\leq R
, from which in particular
\tau\lvert y\rvert-\lvert x\rvert\leq R
and

(1-\tau)\lvert y\rvert-\lvert x\rvert\leq R
, hence

\lvert y\rvert\leq\min\{\tau^{-1},(1-\tau)^{-1}\}(R+\lvert x\rvert)\leq 2(R+\lvert x\rvert).

Using the fact that
f_{R}\geq 0
and
\lvert 2\pi i\xi\cdot y\rvert\leq 2\pi\lvert\xi\rvert 2(R+\lvert x\rvert)\leq\pi
,
we finally deduce

\begin{split}\Re W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,\xi)&=\int_{\{\lvert y\rvert\leq 2(R+\lvert x\rvert)\}}\cos(2\pi\xi\cdot y)f_{R}\bigl(x+\tau y\bigr)f_{R}\bigl(x-(1-\tau)y\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&\geq\cos\bigl(4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)\int_{\{\lvert y\rvert\leq 2(R+\lvert x\rvert)\}}f_{R}\bigl(x+\tau y\bigr)f_{R}\bigl(x-(1-\tau)y\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\cos\bigl(4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0).\qed\end{split}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R\geq 1
,
\tau\in(0,1/2]
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\frac{1+\lvert x\rvert}{\tau}\leq R-\lvert x\rvert.

Then

W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)\geq 2^{-d/2}\omega_{d-1}\tau^{-d/2}\log\mathopen{}\left(\frac{R-\lvert x\rvert}{1+\lvert x\rvert}\tau\right).

Proof.

By definition

\begin{split}W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{R}\bigl(x+\tau y\bigr)\overline{f_{R}\bigl(x-(1-\tau)y\bigr)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert x+\tau y\rvert^{-d/2}\bm{1}_{[1,R]}(\lvert x+\tau y\rvert)\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert^{-d/2}\bm{1}_{[1,R]}\bigl(\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Consider the nonempty set

A_{R,\lvert x\rvert,\tau}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{1+\lvert x\rvert}{\tau}\leq\lvert y\rvert\leq R-\lvert x\rvert\right\}.

Using
\tau\leq 1-\tau\leq 1
, for all
y\in A_{R,\lvert x\rvert,\tau}
we have

\displaystyle 1\leq\tau\lvert y\rvert-\lvert x\rvert\leq\lvert x+\tau y\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert+\tau\lvert y\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert\leq R,

\displaystyle 1\leq\tau\lvert y\rvert-\lvert x\rvert\leq(1-\tau)\lvert y\rvert-\lvert x\rvert\leq\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert\leq R,

therefore

\bm{1}_{[1,R]}(\lvert x+\tau y\rvert)\bm{1}_{[1,R]}\bigl(\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert\bigr)=1;

moreover, using
\lvert x\rvert\leq\tau\lvert y\rvert
, we have also

\displaystyle\lvert x+\tau y\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert+\tau\lvert y\rvert\leq 2\tau\lvert y\rvert,

\displaystyle\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert+(1-\tau)\lvert y\rvert\leq\lvert y\rvert.

From these considerations, integrating in spherical coordinates we deduce that

\begin{split}W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)&\geq\int_{A_{R,\lvert x\rvert,\tau}}(2\tau\lvert y\rvert)^{-d/2}\lvert y\rvert^{-d/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=(2\tau)^{-d/2}\int_{(1+\lvert x\rvert)/\tau}^{R-\lvert x\rvert}r^{-d}\omega_{d-1}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\\
&=2^{-d/2}\omega_{d-1}\tau^{-d/2}\log\mathopen{}\left(\frac{R-\lvert x\rvert}{1+\lvert x\rvert}\tau\right).\qed\end{split}

The assumptions of
Subsection 3.1
are satisfied, therefore

\begin{split}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(x,\xi)=\Re W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(x,\xi)&=\int_{0}^{1}\Re W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\geq\cos\bigl(4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)\int_{0}^{1}W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&=2\cos\bigl(4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)\int_{0}^{1/2}W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau,\end{split}

where in the last equality we used also that
W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)=W_{1-\tau}f_{R}(x,0)
for every
\tau\in(0,1)
.
Notice that
\cos\bigl(4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)\geq 0
since
4\pi(R+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert<\pi/2
,
so we need to find a lower bound for the last integral.

Both assumptions on
R
ensure that
C\coloneqq\frac{R-\lvert x\rvert}{1+\lvert x\rvert}\geq 2
.
It is clear that for every
\tau\in[0,1/2]
we have
W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)\geq 0
; furthermore, for every
\tau\in[1/C,1/2]

we can apply
Subsection 3.1
and obtain

\int_{0}^{1/2}W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\geq\int_{1/C}^{1/2}W_{\tau}f_{R}(x,0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\geq 2^{-d/2}\omega_{d-1}\int_{1/C}^{1/2}\tau^{-d/2}\log(C\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.2)

To conclude the treatment of this lower bound, we distinguish the two cases of the statement.

(i)

In this case the lower bound (
3.2
) can be evaluated explicitly as

\pi\int_{1/C}^{1/2}\tau^{-1}\log(C\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=\pi\int_{1}^{C/2}\frac{\log t}{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\pi\left[\frac{(\log t)^{2}}{2}\right]_{1}^{C/2}=\frac{\pi}{2}\log\mathopen{}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{R-\lvert x\rvert}{1+\lvert x\rvert}\right)^{2},

from which the thesis follows.

(ii)

The stricter assumption on
R
ensures that
C\geq 4
, therefore the lower bound
(
3.2
) can be estimated as

\begin{split}2^{-d/2}\omega_{d-1}\int_{1/C}^{1/2}\tau^{-d/2}\log(C\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau&=2^{-d/2}\omega_{d-1}C^{d/2-1}\int_{1}^{C/2}t^{-d/2}\log(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\geq 2^{-d/2}\omega_{d-1}C^{d/2-1}\int_{2}^{C/2}t^{-d/2}\log(2)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&=2^{-d/2}\omega_{d-1}\log(2)C^{d/2-1}\left[\frac{t^{1-d/2}}{1-d/2}\right]_{2}^{C/2}\\
&=\frac{2^{1-d/2}\omega_{d-1}\log(2)}{d-2}C^{d/2-1}\left[2^{1-d/2}-(C/2)^{1-d/2}\right]\\
&=\frac{2^{2-d}\omega_{d-1}\log(2)}{d-2}\bigl(C^{d/2-1}-2^{d-2}\bigr),\end{split}

from which the thesis follows. ∎

From the above results we deduce the following asymptotic lower bounds for
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}
,
which will be key for proving
Theorem 1.5
and
Theorem 1.6
.

Corollary 3.5
.

In any dimension
d\geq 2
there exists a constant
C_{d}>0
such that for all
R
sufficiently large one has:

(i)

if
d=2

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}(\log R)^{2}

\displaystyle\forall(x,\xi)\in B(0,1)\times B\bigl(0,1/(9R)\bigr);

(ii)

if
d>2

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq C_{d}R^{d/2-1}

\displaystyle\forall(x,\xi)\in B(0,1)\times B\bigl(0,1/(9R)\bigr).

3.2
Main results

We are now almost in a position to proceed with the proofs of the main results. Since the blow-up
behavior in
Theorem 1.5
and
Subsection 3.2.3
arises from a concentration
phenomenon (namely,
\lvert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}\rvert
diverges on a shrinking set), we first require a variant of
the Lebesgue density theorem for sets of product form, to ensure that a non-vanishing fraction of
this diverging contribution remains inside
\Omega
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set with

0<\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega)
. Then for every
\rho>0
there exist
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\liminf_{\sigma\to 0}\frac{\mathscr{L}^{2d}\bigl(B(x_{0},\rho)\times B(\xi_{0},\sigma)\cap\Omega\bigr)}{\mathscr{L}^{2d}\bigl(B(x_{0},\rho)\times B(\xi_{0},\sigma)\bigr)}>0.

Proof.

Since

\mathscr{L}^{2d}\mathopen{}\left(\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B\mathopen{}\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{d}}x,\rho\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\cap\Omega\right)=0

we deduce that there exists a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\Omega^{\prime}=B(x_{0},\rho)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\cap\Omega
is non-negligible, i.e.
\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega^{\prime})>0
.
Define the function
m\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by

\displaystyle m(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathscr{L}^{d}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\xi})}{\mathscr{L}^{d}\bigl(B(x_{0},\rho)\bigr)},

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\{x\in B(x_{0},\rho):(x,\xi)\in\Omega^{\prime}\}.

We have that
m\geq 0
and
m
is not identically zero, therefore by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem
there exists
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\liminf_{\sigma\to 0}\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(\xi_{0},\sigma)}m(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi>0.

The thesis follows from the observation that, by Fubini–Tonelli,

\begin{split}\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(\xi_{0},\sigma)}m(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi&=\frac{1}{\mathscr{L}^{d}\bigl(B(x_{0},\rho)\bigr)\mathscr{L}^{d}\bigl(B(\xi_{0},\sigma)\bigr)}\int_{B(\xi_{0},\sigma)}\mathscr{L}^{d}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\xi})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\frac{\mathscr{L}^{2d}\bigl(B(x_{0},\rho)\times B(\xi_{0},\sigma)\cap\Omega^{\prime}\bigr)}{\mathscr{L}^{2d}\bigl(B(x_{0},\rho)\times B(\xi_{0},\sigma)\bigr)}\\
&=\frac{\mathscr{L}^{2d}\bigl(B(x_{0},\rho)\times B(\xi_{0},\sigma)\cap\Omega\bigr)}{\mathscr{L}^{2d}\bigl(B(x_{0},\rho)\times B(\xi_{0},\sigma)\bigr)}.\qed\end{split}

3.2.1
Subcritical case in dimension
d\geq 2

The treatment of the subcritical case is based on the techniques already employed in

[
12
]
to address the case
p\in[1,\infty)
in dimension
1
.
It uses the time-frequency profile decomposition introduced in
[
11
]
,
to which we refer the reader for details on the technique.

Proof of
Theorem 1.4
.

Let us first show that the supremum
\sup_{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}
is finite. By the triangle inequality it is sufficient to consider
\frac{\int_{0}^{1}\lVert W_{\tau}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}.
For the case
p\in[1,2]
Hölder’s inequality and Moyal’s identity for
W_{\tau}
gives, for all
\tau\in(0,1)
,

\lVert W_{\tau}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert W_{\tau}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

which shows finiteness for
p\in[1,2]
. For any
p>2
interpolation between
2
and
\infty
gives the estimate

\lVert W_{\tau}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert W_{\tau}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2/p}\lVert W_{\tau}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{1-2/p}\leq(\tau(1-\tau))^{-\frac{d}{2}(1-\frac{2}{p})}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

where we have used Moyal’s identity and the Cauchy-Schwarz estimate
\lVert W_{\tau}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq(\tau(1-\tau))^{-\frac{d}{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.
This function is integrable in
\tau
precisely when
p<p_{*}(d),
which shows finiteness. For future reference note that for any
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\tau\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
we have the estimate

\lVert W_{\tau}(f,g)\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim H_{p}(\tau;f,g,\Omega)=\begin{cases}\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega)^{1/p-1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&(1\leq p\leq 2)\\
(\tau(1-\tau))^{-d/2(1-2/p)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&(p>2)\end{cases}

and
H_{p}
is in
L^{1}(0,1)
if and only if
p<p_{*}(d)
.

Now let
f^{(n)}
be a sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
converging weakly to
f
. It is enough to consider the case
f=0
, as the general case will then follow by bilinearity and covariance, see
[
12
, Prop. 6.4]
. We may also assume
\lVert f^{(n)}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1
without loss of generality. We now perform a time-frequency profile decomposition on
f^{(n)}
: for each fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
we can write

f^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi(z_{j}^{(n)})\varphi_{j}+w_{k}^{(n)},

with asymptotic separation
|z_{j}^{(n)}-z_{j^{\prime}}^{(n)}|\to\infty
(
j\neq j^{\prime}
),
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 1
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|w_{k}^{(n)}\|_{M^{\infty}}=0.

Since
f^{(n)}\rightharpoonup 0
all sequences
\{z_{j}^{(n)}\}
diverge: if some
z_{j_{0}}^{(n)}
were bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, then we would have
\pi(z_{j_{0}}^{(n)})^{*}f^{(n)}\rightharpoonup\varphi_{j_{0}}\neq 0
, contradicting
f^{(n)}\rightharpoonup 0
. Hence

|z_{j}^{(n)}|\to\infty\quad\text{for every }j\geq 1.

By bilinearity we now have

\begin{split}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f^{(n)})&=\sum_{j=1}^{k}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(\pi(z_{j}^{(n)})\varphi_{j})+\sum_{1\leq j\neq j^{\prime}\leq k}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(\pi(z_{j}^{(n)})\varphi_{j},\pi(z_{j^{\prime}}^{(n)})\varphi_{j^{\prime}})\\
&+W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})+W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(w_{k}^{(n)},F_{k}^{(n)})+W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(w_{k}^{(n)}),\end{split}

where
F_{k}^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi(z_{j}^{(n)})\varphi_{j}
.
We now proceed to control each term in the expansion.

Step 1.

Vanishing of the pure profiles.

By covariance we want to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(\pi(z_{j}^{(n)})\varphi_{j})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{z_{j}^{(n)}}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0.

By
C_{0}
-decay and covariance of
W_{\tau}
for all
\tau\in(0,1)
we therefore get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{z_{j}^{(n)}}W_{\tau}\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0

for any
p\in[1,\infty)
. When
p<p_{*}

H_{p}(\tau)
dominates
\|T_{z_{j}^{(n)}}W_{\tau}\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, so the triangle inequality and dominated convergence yields

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{z_{j}^{(n)}}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(\varphi_{j})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\|T_{z_{j}^{(n)}}W_{\tau}(\varphi_{j})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{z_{j}^{(n)}}W_{\tau}(\varphi_{j})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=0\end{split}

for any profile
\varphi_{j}
.

Step 2.

Vanishing of the cross terms.

This is completely analogous to the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 6.7]
, except that dominated convergence can only be invoked for
p<p_{*}
.

Step 3.

Vanishing of remainder terms.

The argument will be the same for
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})
,
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(w_{k}^{(n)},F_{k}^{(n)})
and
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(w_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})
, so we will only show it for
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})
.
By
[
7
, Theorem 4.12]
and a slight modification of
[
12
, Section 4]
we get for
\tau\in(0,1)
that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|W_{\tau}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0,

again for all
p\in[1,\infty)
. Since both
F_{k}^{(n)}
and
w_{k}^{(n)}
are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\ H_{p}(\tau;F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)},\Omega)
will dominate
\|W_{\tau}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
for all
\tau\in(0,1)
. For
p<p_{*}
we now use, in order, the triangle inequality, the reverse Fatou lemma (which we can use because
H_{p}
dominates) and dominated convergence to get

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\|W_{\tau}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|W_{\tau}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|W_{\tau}(F_{k}^{(n)},w_{k}^{(n)})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=0.\end{split}

Step 4.

Conclusion.

Since all the terms in the profile decomposition vanish in the limit, so must
\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f^{(n)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
by the triangle inequality. We conclude that
J_{\mathrm{BJ}}^{p}
is sequentially weakly continuous on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

.

It is easy to realize that the proof of

If

, since

and

is continuous near the origin, by dominated convergence
applied to
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}\phi(x,\xi)
as
(x,\xi)\to 0
. Therefore
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}\phi
is nonzero on some
ball
B(0,r)
, and the claim follows by covariance along a Lebesgue density point of
\Omega
.

L_{p,\Omega}>0
, then by homogeneity

3.2.3
Supercritical case in dimension
d>2

Proof of
Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

Subsection 3.2
such that

\mathscr{L}^{2d}\bigl(B(x_{0},1)\times B\bigl(\xi_{0},1/(9R)\bigr)\cap\Omega\bigr)\geq\varepsilon\mathscr{L}^{2d}\bigl(B(x_{0},1)\times B\bigl(\xi_{0},1/(9R)\bigr)\bigr)

g_{R}=\pi(x_{0},\xi_{0})f_{R}

\lVert g_{R}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\lVert f_{R}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\lvert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}g_{R}(x,\xi)\rvert=\lvert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(x-x_{0},\xi-\xi_{0})\rvert\geq W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(x-x_{0},\xi-\xi_{0}).

3.3
Proofs of attainment

3.3.1
Indirect proof of attainment

Proof of
Theorem 1.7
.

We can assume without loss of generality
\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega)<\infty
, otherwise it is enough to
choose a measurable set
E\subseteq\Omega
with
0<\mathscr{L}^{2d}(E)<\infty
and prove that

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\notin L^{p}(E)
, implying
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\notin L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Assume by contradiction that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
By the polarization identity

4W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f,g)=W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f+g)-W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f-g)+iW_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f+ig)-iW_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f-ig)

and Minkowski’s inequality we have
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f,g)\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

For
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
T_{g}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p}(\Omega)
be the linear map
T_{g}(f)=W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f,g)
.
We claim that
T_{g}
has closed graph. Indeed,
T_{g}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is continuous
because

\lVert T_{g}(f)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f,g)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(see
Subsection 3.3.1
or
[
6
, Theorem 1.4]
), hence the graph

\graph(T_{g})=\left\{\bigl(f,T_{g}(f)\bigr):f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p}(\Omega)

is closed with respect to the coarser topology induced by the immersion in

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, and therefore with respect to the native product topology too.
By the closed graph theorem this means that
T_{g}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p}(\Omega)
is continuous,
i.e.
\lVert T_{g}(f)\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(g)\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Since
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f,g)=\overline{W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(g,f)}
, we have also

\lVert T_{g}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f,g)\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(g,f)\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\lVert T_{f}g\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(f)\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Consider the family of continuous linear operators

\mathcal{T}=\{T_{g}:g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\}\subset\mathcal{B}\bigl(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),L^{p}(\Omega)\bigr).

It is pointwise bounded because for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\sup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\lVert Tf\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\lVert T_{g}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\end{subarray}}C(f)\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(f).

By Banach–Steinhaus theorem the family of operators is uniformly bounded, that is

C\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\lVert T_{g}\rVert_{B\bigl(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),L^{p}(\Omega)\bigr)}<\infty.

This means that

\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f,g)\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which in particular implies

\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

contradicting
Theorem 1.5
or
Theorem 1.6
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

\tau
-Wigner can be written as a Fourier transform

\displaystyle W_{\tau}(f,g)(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}h_{x}(\xi),

\displaystyle h_{x}(y)

\displaystyle=f(x+\tau y)\overline{g\bigl(x-(1-\tau)y\bigr)}.

\begin{split}\lVert W_{\tau}(f,g)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert\mathcal{F}h_{x}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert h_{x}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\

(u,v)=\bigl(x+\tau y,x-(1-\tau)y\bigr)

Integrating in

Lemma 3.9

Proof of
Theorem 1.7
, case
d=2
.

The elementary building block of our construction is the annular profile
f_{r,R}
, which can be seen as a truncation of the scale-invariant function
\lvert x\rvert^{-1}
in dimension
2
. The Born–Jordan distribution of such profiles grows like
\log(R/r)^{2}
near the origin, while in the same regime we have
\lVert f_{r,R}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\sim\log(R/r)
. This motivates forming a superposition like

f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}f_{n},\qquad f_{n}=f_{r_{n},R_{n}},

so that the
n
-th annulus contributes at scale
r_{n}
roughly like
a_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}
, where we set
\lambda_{n}=\log(R_{n}/r_{n})
, while the total
L^{2}
norm is controlled by
\sum_{n}a_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}
. We will choose the radii such that the annuli are pairwise disjoint and shrink rapidly to the origin, allowing the
n
-th annulus to dominate
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f
on a shrinking phase-space box
B(0,r_{n})\times B(0,r_{n})
.

A convenient, explicit way to implement this program is to choose
\lambda_{n}\coloneqq n^{3}+2
and
a_{n}\coloneqq n^{-5/2}
, so that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}<\infty,\qquad a_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\to\infty.

Moreover, set
R_{1}=\dfrac{1}{10}
and define recursively
r_{n}\coloneqq R_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}}
and
R_{n+1}\coloneqq\dfrac{r_{n}}{8}\quad(n\geq 1)
, so that

5r_{n}<R_{n},\qquad R_{n+1}<r_{n},\qquad R_{n}\leq\frac{1}{10}\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

In particular, the annuli
\{r_{n}\leq\lvert x\rvert\leq R_{n}\}
are pairwise disjoint and contained in
B(0,1/10)
, hence
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}=2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}<\infty
.

Let us now prove that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f
is unbounded in every neighborhood of the origin. Fix
n\geq 1
and
(x,\xi)\in B(0,r_{n})\times B(0,r_{n})
. Since
\supp f\subseteq B(0,1/10)
and
r_{n}\leq r_{1}\leq 1/10
, the assumptions of
Subsection 3.3.2
are satisfied by
f
with
R_{0}=R_{1}=R=1/10
, hence

\Re W_{\tau}(f,f)(x,\xi)\geq\frac{1}{2}W_{\tau}(f,f)(x,0)\qquad\forall\tau\in(0,1).

Integrating in
\tau
, we obtain

\lvert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f(x,\xi)\rvert\geq\Re W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f(x,\xi)\geq\frac{1}{2}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f(x,0).

At frequency
\xi=0
all cross-terms are nonnegative since
f\geq 0
, so by Tonelli’s theorem we have

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f(x,0)=\sum_{m,k\geq 1}a_{m}a_{k}\,W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(f_{m},f_{k})(x,0)\geq a_{n}^{2}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{n}(x,0).

Next, since
\lvert x\rvert\leq r_{n}
and
5r_{n}<R_{n}
, we have
\lvert x\rvert\leq\dfrac{R_{n}-2r_{n}}{3}
, so
Subsection 3.3.2
with
\xi=0
implies

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{n}(x,0)\gtrsim\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{R_{n}-\lvert x\rvert}{r_{n}+\lvert x\rvert}\right)^{2}\geq\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{R_{n}-r_{n}}{2r_{n}}\right)^{2}=\log\left(\frac{e^{\lambda_{n}}-1}{4}\right)^{2}.

Since
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
, it is clear that for
n
sufficiently large (say,
n\geq N
for a suitable
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
) we have

\log\left(\frac{e^{\lambda_{n}}-1}{4}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{n},

hence the previous arguments show that there is a constant
C>0
such that

\lvert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f(x,\xi)\rvert\geq Ca_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\qquad\forall(x,\xi)\in B(0,r_{n})\times B(0,r_{n}),\quad n\geq N.

Since
a_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}=n^{-5}(n^{3}+2)^{2}\to\infty
, this bound implies that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f
is not essentially bounded in any neighborhood of the origin.

To conclude, let
z_{0}=(x_{0},\xi_{0})
be a Lebesgue density point of
\Omega
and set
g=\pi(z_{0})f
. Covariance yields
\lvert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}g(x,\xi)\rvert=\lvert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f(x-x_{0},\xi-\xi_{0})\rvert
, hence

\lvert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}g(x,\xi)\rvert\geq Ca_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\qquad\forall(x,\xi)\in E_{n}\coloneqq B(x_{0},r_{n})\times B(\xi_{0},r_{n}),\quad n\geq N.

Since
z_{0}
is a density point, we have
\mathscr{L}^{4}(E_{n}\cap\Omega)>0
for all
n
. Therefore
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfies
\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}g\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\infty
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

It is worth noting that the mechanism behind the critical case

is inherently

in nature, as it relies on forcing controlled pointwise blow-up. As such, it cannot be transplanted to critical finite
p_{*}(d)=2d/(d-2)

3.3.3
Supercritical case in dimension
d>2

For every parameter
\alpha\in(0,d/2)
, define the radial function
F_{\alpha}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by

F_{\alpha}(x)=\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}\bm{1}_{[0,1]}(\lvert x\rvert),

whose squared
L^{2}
norm is

\lVert F_{\alpha}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\int_{B(0,1)}\lvert x\rvert^{-2\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}r^{-2\alpha}\omega_{d-1}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{d-2\alpha}.

The real part of the corresponding
\tau
-Wigner distribution is

\Re W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\cos(2\pi\xi\cdot y)\lvert x+\tau y\rvert^{-\alpha}\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert^{-\alpha}\bm{1}_{[0,1]}(\lvert x+\tau y\rvert)\bm{1}_{[0,1]}(\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

As in the proof of
Subsection 3.1
we have that the conditions

\displaystyle\tau\lvert y\rvert-\lvert x\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert x+\tau y\rvert\leq 1

and

\displaystyle(1-\tau)\lvert y\rvert-\lvert x\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert\leq 1

imply

\lvert y\rvert\leq\min\bigl\{\tau^{-1},(1-\tau)^{-1}\bigr\}(1+\lvert x\rvert)\leq 2(1+\lvert x\rvert).

As such, when computing
\Re W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)
we can restrict the integral to the ball

B(0,2(1+\lvert x\rvert))
and use the estimate

\cos(2\pi\xi\cdot y)\geq\cos\bigl(4\pi(1+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)

whenever
4\pi(1+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\pi
, leading to the lower bound

\Re W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)\geq\cos\bigl(4\pi(1+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\bigr)W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,0).

We now look for sufficient conditions on
x
and
\tau
such that

\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert\leq 1\implies\lvert x+\tau y\rvert\leq 1\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

This means

B\left(\frac{x}{1-\tau},\frac{1}{1-\tau}\right)\subseteq B\left(\frac{-x}{\tau},\frac{1}{\tau}\right),

which is ensured by

\left\lvert\frac{x}{1-\tau}-\frac{-x}{\tau}\right\rvert+\frac{1}{1-\tau}\leq\frac{1}{\tau}.

A direct computation shows that this condition is equivalent to
\lvert x\rvert+2\tau\leq 1
. Under this assumption, we may also simplify

\bm{1}_{[0,1]}(\lvert x+\tau y\rvert)\bm{1}_{[0,1]}(\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert)=\bm{1}_{[0,1]}(\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert).

Moreover, with the substitution

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=(1-\tau)y-x,

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=(1-\tau)^{d}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle x+\tau y

\displaystyle=\frac{x+\tau u}{1-\tau},

we get

\begin{split}W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,0)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert\frac{x+\tau u}{1-\tau}\right\rvert^{-\alpha}\lvert u\rvert^{-\alpha}\bm{1}_{[0,1]}(\lvert u\rvert)(1-\tau)^{-d}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\\
&=(1-\tau)^{\alpha-d}\int_{B(0,1)}\lvert x+\tau u\rvert^{-\alpha}\lvert u\rvert^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\\
&\geq(1-\tau)^{\alpha-d}\int_{B(0,1)}(\lvert x\rvert+\tau)^{-\alpha}\lvert u\rvert^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\\
&=\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{d-\alpha}(1-\tau)^{\alpha-d}(\lvert x\rvert+\tau)^{-\alpha}\\
&\geq\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{d-\alpha}(\lvert x\rvert+\tau)^{-\alpha},\end{split}

where in the last step we used
\alpha<d
and
0<\tau<1
.

With regard to Born–Jordan, we restrict the integral to
\tau\leq(1-\lvert x\rvert)/2
in order to use the
previous lower bound for
W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,0)
and obtain

\begin{split}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}(x,0)&=2\int_{0}^{1/2}W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\geq 2\int_{0}^{\frac{1-\lvert x\rvert}{2}}W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\geq\frac{2\omega_{d-1}}{d-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\frac{1-\lvert x\rvert}{2}}(\lvert x\rvert+\tau)^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&=\frac{2\omega_{d-1}}{d-\alpha}\left[\frac{(\lvert x\rvert+\tau)^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right]_{0}^{\frac{1-\lvert x\rvert}{2}}\\
&=\frac{2\omega_{d-1}}{(d-\alpha)(\alpha-1)}\left[\lvert x\rvert^{1-\alpha}-\left(\frac{1+\lvert x\rvert}{2}\right)^{1-\alpha}\right].\end{split}

In particular, if
\alpha>1
and
\lvert x\rvert
is small, then
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}(x,0)\gtrsim\lvert x\rvert^{1-\alpha}
. To conclude, we need to upgrade this lower bound from the slice
\xi=0
to a full phase-space neighborhood of the origin. To this aim, choose
\rho,\sigma>0
so small that

4\pi(1+\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\frac{\pi}{3}\qquad\forall\lvert x\rvert\leq\rho,\ \lvert\xi\rvert\leq\sigma.

Then the previous estimate for
\Re W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)
yields

\Re W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)\geq\frac{1}{2}W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,0)\qquad\forall\lvert x\rvert\leq\rho,\ \lvert\xi\rvert\leq\sigma,\ \tau\in(0,1).

Since
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}
is real-valued for
d>1
, integrating in
\tau
gives

\begin{split}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)&=\Re W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}\Re W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}W_{\tau}F_{\alpha}(x,0)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{2}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}(x,0)\end{split}

for all
\lvert x\rvert\leq\rho
and
\lvert\xi\rvert\leq\sigma
. Consequently, we infer

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}(x,\xi)\gtrsim\lvert x\rvert^{1-\alpha}\qquad\forall x\in B(0,\rho)\setminus\{0\},\ \xi\in B(0,\sigma).

In the case where
p\in\bigl(p_{*}(d),\infty\bigr)
it is enough (and possible) to choose
\alpha
so that

1+\frac{d}{p}<\alpha<\frac{d}{2}.

Then
p(1-\alpha)<-d
, so
\lvert x\rvert^{1-\alpha}\notin L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and therefore
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}\notin L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, as claimed.

If
p=\infty
, choose any

1<\alpha<\frac{d}{2},

which is possible because
d>2
. Then
\lvert x\rvert^{1-\alpha}\to\infty
as
x\to 0
, and the previous bound shows that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}
is unbounded on every neighborhood of the origin. As a consequence,
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}F_{\alpha}\notin L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

This yields a completely explicit local counterexample for every
p\in\bigl(p_{*}(d),\infty\bigr]
.
The full statement of
Theorem 1.7
on an arbitrary measurable set
\Omega
follows,
after picking a Lebesgue point of
\Omega
, from the usual covariance argument.

Appendix A
Incomplete Born–Jordan distributions

It is intuitively clear at this point that the endpoint singularities at
\tau=0
and
\tau=1
are the ultimate source of the pathologies of the full Born–Jordan distribution in dimension
d\geq 2
. It is therefore natural to isolate and study suitably truncated averages:

Definition A.1
.

Given
\delta\in[0,1/2]
, the
incomplete Born–Jordan distribution
of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}^{\delta}f(z)\coloneqq\int_{\delta}^{1-\delta}W_{\tau}f(z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

(A.1)

It is clear that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f=W_{\mathrm{BJ}}^{0}f
. More interestingly, a local incompleteness property for annular supports holds: we show below that if
f
is supported on an annulus, then
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f=W_{\mathrm{BJ}}^{\delta}f
near the origin for some
\delta
. This property, already used implicitly in the main text, comes with additional benefits. For instance, it unlocks the (local) continuity of
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}
— this is, to the best of our knowledge, the first continuity result for
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}
when
d\geq 2
. In turn, this regularity can be leveraged to provide an alternative proof of the unboundedness of the concentration problem (
1.3
) for
p=\infty
.

We start by giving the precise statement of the incompleteness property.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that

\supp f\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:r_{1}\leq\lvert x\rvert\leq r_{2}\}

for some radii
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
.
If
\delta\in[0,1/2]
and
\rho>0
satisfy

(r_{1}+r_{2})\delta+\rho\leq r_{1}

(A.2)

then

\displaystyle W_{\tau}f(z)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall z=(x,\xi)\in B(0,\rho)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\forall\tau\in[0,\delta]\cup[1-\delta,1],

(A.3)

and, as a consequence, we have

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f(z)

\displaystyle=W_{\mathrm{BJ}}^{\delta}f(z)

\displaystyle\forall z=(x,\xi)\in B(0,\rho)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(A.4)

Proof.

Suppose that
W_{\tau}f(x,\xi)\neq 0
for some
x\in B(0,\rho)
.
Then there must exist
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that both factors
f\bigl(x+\tau y\bigr)
and

f\bigl(x-(1-\tau)y\bigr)
are nonzero, which requires

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert x+\tau y\rvert\leq r_{2}

and

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert x-(1-\tau)y\rvert\leq r_{2}.

Applying the triangle inequality to the first and last inequalities leads to

Alternative proof of

We will consider the functions

with

in

we deduce that

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}
is continuous in this very open set. By continuity and covariance, unboundedness will now follow if we can show that
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(0,0)
grows faster than
\|f_{R}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\omega_{d-1}\log(R)
as
R\to\infty.
We will prove this by direct computation.
Let
\tau\in(0,1/2]
. By definition

\begin{split}W_{\tau}f_{R}(0,0)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{R}\bigl(\tau y\bigr)\overline{f_{R}\bigl(-(1-\tau)y\bigr)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert\tau y\rvert^{-d/2}\bm{1}_{[1,R]}(\lvert\tau y\rvert)\lvert(1-\tau)y\rvert^{-d/2}\bm{1}_{[1,R]}\bigl(\lvert(1-\tau)y\rvert\bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

As usual, the indicator functions are both non-zero when

\frac{1}{\tau}\leq\lvert y\rvert\leq\frac{R}{\tau}
and
\frac{1}{1-\tau}\leq\lvert y\rvert\leq\frac{R}{1-\tau}
,
which is equivalent to

\frac{1}{\tau}\leq\lvert y\rvert\leq\frac{R}{1-\tau}.

Therefore

\begin{split}W_{\tau}f_{R}(0,0)&=\bigl(\tau(1-\tau)\bigr)^{-d/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{1}_{\left[\frac{1}{\tau},\frac{R}{1-\tau}\right]}(\lvert y\rvert)\lvert y\rvert^{-d}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\begin{cases}\omega_{d-1}\bigl(\tau(1-\tau)\bigr)^{-d/2}\log(R\tau/(1-\tau))&(\tau>\frac{1}{R+1})\\
0&(\tau\leq\frac{1}{R+1}).\end{cases}\end{split}

We may now compute the Born–Jordan distribution at
0
, but we must consider
d=2
and
d>2
separately. For
d=2
we have, using the change of variables
u=\tau/(1-\tau)

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(0,0)=2\omega_{1}\int_{1/(R+1)}^{1/2}\frac{\log(R\tau/(1-\tau))}{\bigl(\tau(1-\tau)\bigr)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=4\pi\int_{1/R}^{1}\frac{\log(Ru)}{u}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u=2\pi(\log R)^{2}.

The unboundedness of the supremum clearly follows. For
d>2
we use the same change of variables, plus integration by parts:

\begin{split}W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f_{R}(0,0)&=2\omega_{d-1}\int_{1/(R+1)}^{1/2}\frac{\log(R\tau/(1-\tau))}{\bigl(\tau(1-\tau)\bigr)^{d/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=2\omega_{d-1}\int_{1/R}^{1}\frac{\log(Ru)}{u^{d/2}}(u+1)^{d-2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\\
&\geq 2\omega_{d-1}\int_{1/R}^{1}\frac{\log(Ru)}{u^{d/2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\\
&=2\omega_{d-1}\left[\frac{\log(Ru)}{(1-d/2)u^{d/2-1}}-\frac{1}{(1-d/2)^{2}u^{d/2-1}}\right]_{1/R}^{1}\\
&=2\omega_{d-1}\left(\frac{\log(R)}{1-d/2}-\frac{1}{(d/2-1)^{2}}+\frac{R^{d/2-1}}{(d/2-1)^{2}}\right)\\
&\gtrsim R^{d/2-1}.\qed\end{split}

Proposition A.5

Let

The pointwise estimate for

Appendix B
Born–Jordan quantization of local Lebesgue symbols

Let us briefly examine some consequences of our main results on Born–Jordan
pseudodifferential operators. Recall the standard weak correspondence between time-frequency representations and quantization rules: the
Born–Jordan operator with symbol
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is defined by

\big\langle\Op_{\mathrm{BJ}}(a)f,g\big\rangle=\big\langle a,W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(g,f)\big\rangle,\qquad\forall\ f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Since

W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(g,f)=\int_{0}^{1}W_{\tau}(g,f)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau,

this is precisely the operator
T_{Q}^{a}
studied in
[
1
, Section 6]
.
Born–Jordan operators are a priori only well-defined operators from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, but the subcritical concentration scenario immediately yields
L^{2}
-boundedness results for such operators for symbols with local Lebesgue summability.
To be precise, let

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be measurable with
0<\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega)<\infty
, let

1\leq p<p_{*}(d)
and set
q=p^{\prime}
. If
a\in L^{q}(\Omega)
and
\supp a\subseteq\Omega
, then

\begin{split}\lvert\big\langle\Op_{\mathrm{BJ}}(a)f,g\big\rangle\rvert&=\lvert\big\langle a,W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(g,f)\big\rangle\rvert\\
&\leq\lVert a\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}(g,f)\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C_{p,\Omega}\lVert a\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\end{split}

for some
C_{p,\Omega}>0
. As a consequence,
\Op_{\mathrm{BJ}}(a)
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\lVert\Op_{\mathrm{BJ}}(a)\rVert_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C_{p,\Omega}\lVert a\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)},\qquad q>\begin{cases}\dfrac{2d}{d+2}&(d>2)\\
1&(d=2).\end{cases}

This result is already contained in
[
1
, Theorem 6.10]
. Indeed, in the case
p=2
this reads

a\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\longmapsto T_{Q}^{a}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\qquad\frac{2d}{d+2}<q\leq 2.

For symbols supported in a finite-measure set, the case
q>2
also follows from their
q=2
result and the embedding
L^{q}(\Omega)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
.

On the other hand, these sufficient conditions seem to lack a companion negative counterpart. We will now show that the complementary failure below the critical threshold actually follows by the results in this note. Assume indeed
q<2d/(d+2)
, so that
q^{\prime}>p_{*}(d)
. If a uniform estimate

\lvert\big\langle\Op_{\mathrm{BJ}}(a)f,g\big\rangle\rvert\leq C\lVert a\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

held for all
a\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, then taking
g=f
would imply by duality

\lVert W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\rVert_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\qquad\forall\ f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Such a claim clearly contrasts the supercritical counterexamples of
Theorem 1.6
and

Theorem 1.7
, hence no such local
L^{q}
-symbol estimate can hold below the threshold. In dimension
d=2
, the endpoint
q=1
is similarly ruled out. Indeed,
q^{\prime}=\infty
, and

Theorem 1.5
together with
Theorem 1.7
gives functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
W_{\mathrm{BJ}}f\notin L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

The endpoint
q=2d/(d+2)
in dimension
d>2
remains open, being equivalent by duality to the open critical concentration problem at
p=p_{*}(d)
.
