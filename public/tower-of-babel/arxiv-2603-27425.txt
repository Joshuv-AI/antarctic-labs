Abstract
. In this article, we complete the study of the equivalences between the properties of h h –dichotomy, h h –noncriticality and h h –expansiveness of a linear nonautonomous ODE system which had been initiated in a previous work. Moreover, we extend a result of the generalized Floquet theory developed by T.A. Burton and J.S. Muldowney by providing a necessary and sufficient condition for h h –dichotomy. It should be noted that all the results have been obtained by using a characterization of the h h –dichotomy by a group theory approach recently developed by J.F. Peña and S. Rivera–Villagrán.

1.
Introduction

The uniform
h
–
dichotomy
is a property of non autonomous linear systems of differential
and difference equations which; roughly speaking; splits any non trivial solution in a contractive and an expansive
part which are dominated by a growth rate
h(\cdot)
that will be formally described later.

In the recent years we have witnessed a renewed interest in studying
h
-dichotomies
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
18
,
22
,
23
]
and this work revisits the uniform
h
–dichotomy
for non autonomous linear systems of differential equations:

(1.1)

x^{\prime}=A(t)x\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in J:=(a_{0},+\infty)$},

where
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
and the map
t\mapsto A(t)\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is continuous on
J
. From now on, a fundamental matrix of (
1.1
) will be denoted by
\Phi(t)
, while the corresponding transition matrix will be
\Phi(t,s)
. On the other hand, given
any norm
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its induced matrix norm will be denoted by
||\cdot||
.

Definition 1
.

A growth rate
h(\cdot)
is a strictly increasing homeomorphism
h\colon J=(a_{0},+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
. In addition, if
h
is differentiable on
J
, we will say that
h
is a differentiable growth rate.

A formal definition of the uniform
h
–dichotomy is given by:

Definition 2
.

Given a growth rate
h\colon(a_{0},+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
and an interval
\mathcal{I}\subseteq J
, the linear system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dicohotomy on
\mathcal{I}

if there exists a projector
P(t)
and a pair of constants
K\geq 1
and
\alpha>0
such that

(1.2)

P(t)\Phi(t,s)=\Phi(t,s)P(s)\quad\textnormal{for any $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$},

and

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}||\Phi(t,s)P(s)||&\leq&\displaystyle K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}\quad\textnormal{for any $t\geq s$ with $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$}\\
\\
||\Phi(t,s)Q(s)||&\leq&\displaystyle K\left(\frac{h(s)}{h(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}\quad\textnormal{for any $s\geq t$ with $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$},\end{array}\right.

where
Q(\cdot)
verifies
P(t)+Q(t)=I
for any
t\in\mathcal{I}
.

There exists an alternative characterization for the uniform
h
–dichotomy on
\mathcal{I}
which is
carried out in terms of constants projectors. We refer to
[
8
, Prop.2]
for additional details:

Proposition 1
.

The system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
\mathcal{I}
if and only if, given a fundamental matrix
\Phi(t)
, there exists a constant projector
P
and constants
K\geq 1
and
\alpha>0

such that:

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rccr}||\Phi(t)P\Phi^{-1}(s)||&\leq K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}&\textnormal{if}&t\geq s,\quad t,s\in\mathcal{I}\\
||\Phi(t)(I-P)\Phi^{-1}(s)||&\leq K\left(\frac{h(s)}{h(t)}\right)^{-\alpha}&\textnormal{if}&t\leq s,\quad t,s\in\mathcal{I}.\end{array}\right.

The uniform
h
–dichotomies generalizes the exponential dichotomy introduced by O. Perron
[
19
]
,
which considers the growth rate
h(t)=e^{t}
and
J=\mathbb{R}
. In fact, R. Martin Jr.
[
11
]
, J.S. Muldowney
[
12
]
and M. Pinto
\&
R. Naulin
[
20
,
13
]
considered different growth rates.

In general, the study of
h
-dichotomies has been carried out in an attempt to emulate the results already known for the exponential dichotomy such as the topological conjugacy between the solutions of (
1.1
) with a quasilinear perturbation
[
21
]
, robustness of the dichotomy under small perturbations
[
14
,
25
]
and spectral theories arising from the dichotomy
[
22
,
24
]
.

The main goals of this article are two: the first one is to achieve the study initiated in
[
8
]
where equivalences between uniform
h
–dichotomy with the properties of
noncritical uniformity

and
expansiveness
(formal definitions will be given later) was established for the case
\mathcal{I}=[h^{-1}(1),+\infty)
and now will be stated for
\mathcal{I}=J
. The second objective of this article is to obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for uniform
h
–dichotomy in the framework of the generalized Floquet theory developed by T.A. Burton and J.S. Muldowney in
[
4
]
.

The section 2 reviews basic properties and recent results about the uniform
h
–dichotomy

.
The section 3 provides a complete study of the equivalences between the properties of uniform
h
–dichotomy,
uniform
h

\mathcal{I}=J
. The section 4 expands some results of the generalized Floquet
theory and provides a criterion for uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J
.

2.
The uniform
h
–dichotomy: a short review

Given any differentiable
growth rate
h(\cdot)
and
\alpha>0
it is easy to construct illustrative examples of systems having a uniform
h
–dichotomy as follows:

(2.1)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{x}_{1}\\
\dot{x}_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle-\alpha\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{h(t)}&0\\
0&\displaystyle\alpha\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{h(t)}\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{array}\right)\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in J$}.

In fact, notice that (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are verified with
K=1
and

(2.2)

\Phi(t)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{h(t)^{\alpha}}&0\\
0&h(t)^{\alpha}\end{array}\right]\quad\textnormal{and}\quad P(t)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in J$}.

Now, notice that if the linear system (
1.1
) has uniform
h
–dichotomy on
\mathcal{I}\subset J
it is
straightforward to see that any nontrivial solution
t\mapsto x(t,t_{0},x_{0})
of (
1.1
) passing through
x_{0}
at
t=t_{0}\in\mathcal{I}
can be decomposed as follows:

(2.3)

x(t,t_{0},x_{0})=\Phi(t,t_{0})x_{0}=\underbrace{\Phi(t,t_{0})P(t_{0})x_{0}}_{:=x^{+}(t,t_{0},x_{0})}+\underbrace{\Phi(t,t_{0})Q(t_{0})x_{0}}_{:=x^{-}(t,t_{0},x_{0})}.

The following result, stated without proof in
[
8
]
, is a direct consequence from
h
–dichotomy on
\mathcal{I}
combined with (
2.3
):

Lemma 2.1
.

If (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
\mathcal{I}
, then any non trivial solution of (
1.1
) has the following forward and backward behavior:

a)

If
t,t_{0}\in\mathcal{I}
and
t\geq t_{0}
, the maps
x^{+}(\cdot)
and
x^{-}(\cdot)
have the following estimations:

(2.4)

|x^{+}(t,t_{0},x_{0})|\leq K\left(\frac{h(t_{0})}{h(t)}\right)^{\alpha}|x_{0}|\,\,\textnormal{and}\,\,\,\frac{|Q(t_{0})x_{0}|}{K}\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(t_{0})}\right)^{\alpha}\leq|x^{-}(t,t_{0},x_{0})|,

b)

If
t,t_{0}\in\mathcal{I}
and
t\in(a_{0},t_{0})
, the maps
x^{+}(\cdot)
and
x^{-}(\cdot)
have the following estimations:

(2.5)

\left(\frac{h(t_{0})}{h(t)}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{|P(t_{0})x_{0}|}{K}\leq|x^{+}(t,t_{0},x_{0})|\quad\textnormal{and}\quad|x^{-}(t,t_{0},x_{0})|\leq K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(t_{0})}\right)^{\alpha}.

Proof.

The left inequality of (
2.4
) and the right inequality of (
2.5
) follows directly from the inequalities (
1.3
).
Now, if
t\geq t_{0}
, by using (
1.2
) combined with
Q^{2}(t_{0})=Q(t_{0})
it follows that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}Q(t_{0})x_{0}&=&\Phi(t_{0},t)\Phi(t,t_{0})Q^{2}(t_{0})x_{0}\\
\\
&=&\Phi(t_{0},t)Q(t)\Phi(t,t_{0})Q(t_{0})x_{0}\\
\\
&=&\Phi(t_{0},t)Q(t)x^{-}(t,t_{0},x_{0}),\end{array}

therefore by (
1.3
) we can deduce that

|Q(t_{0})x_{0}|\leq K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(t_{0})}\right)^{-\alpha}|x^{-}(t,t_{0},x_{0})|,

and the right inequality of (
2.4
) follows. Finally, the left inequality of (
2.5
) can be proved similarly
by noticing that

P(t_{0})x_{0}=\Phi(t_{0},t)P(t)x^{+}(t,t_{0},x_{0}).

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If the linear system (
1.1
) has an uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J
, the unique bounded solution is the trivial one.

Proof.

Let
t\mapsto x(t):=x(t,t_{0},x_{0})
be a nontrivial solution of (
1.1
) passing through
x_{0}
at
t=t_{0}
. We
can prove that this solution cannot be bounded on
J
. In fact, by Lemma
2.1
we know that this solution can be splitted
as described in (
2.3
). Moreover, by (
2.4
) we have that
x^{+}(t,t_{0},x_{0})\to 0

and
|x^{-}(t,t_{0},x_{0})|\to+\infty
when
t\to+\infty
, thus
t\mapsto x(t)
cannot be bounded on
[t_{0},+\infty)
. Similarly, by (
2.5
)
we also have that
|x^{+}(t,t_{0},x_{0})|\to+\infty

and
x^{-}(t,t_{0},x_{0})\to 0
when
t\to a_{0}^{+}
, therefore
t\mapsto x(t)
cannot be bounded on
(a_{0},t_{0}]
and the Lemma follows.
∎

2.1.
Uniform
h
–dichotomies from a group theory perspective

An innovative approach introduced by J.F. Peña and S. Rivera–Villagrán in
[
18
]
proved that, for any growth rate
h\colon J\to(a_{0},+\infty)
,
a topological and totally ordered abelian group
(J,*)
can be constructed by considering the following operation:

(2.6)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}J\times J&\to&J\\
(t,s)&\mapsto&t\ast s:=h^{-1}\left(h(t)h(s)\right).\end{array}

Moreover, the unit element and the inverse for any
t\in J
are respectively defined by

(2.7)

e_{\ast}:=h^{-1}(1)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad t^{\ast-1}:=h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{h(t)}\right).

As any abelian group is a
\mathbb{Z}
–module by considering
the external composition law

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}&\to&\mathbb{R}\\
(k,t)&\mapsto&t^{*k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\underbrace{t\ast\cdots\ast t}_{k-\textnormal{times}}&\textnormal{if}&k>0\\
e_{\ast}&\textnormal{if}&k=0\\
\underbrace{t^{\ast-1}\ast\cdots\ast t^{\ast-1}}_{k-\textnormal{times}}&\textnormal{if}&k<0.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

By using recursively the identities (
2.7
) combined with the fact that

h(\cdot)
is an homeomorphism we can see that

t^{*k}=h^{-1}(h(t^{*k}))=h^{-1}(h(t)^{k})\quad\textnormal{for any $k\in\mathbb{Z}$},

and the above external composition law can be revisited as

(2.8)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{Z}\times J&\to&J\\
(k,t)&\mapsto&t^{*k}=h^{-1}\left(h(t)^{k}\right),\end{array}

which leads to the property

(2.9)

h(t^{*k})=h(t)^{k}\quad\textnormal{for any $k\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

The following identities for any pair
t,s\in J
are stated in
[
8
]
:

(2.10a)

\displaystyle h(t\ast s)=h(t)h(s)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad h(s^{\ast-1})=\frac{1}{h(s)},

(2.10b)

\displaystyle(t\ast s)^{\ast-1}=h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{h(t\ast s)}\right)=h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{h(t)h(s)}\right),

(2.10c)

\displaystyle t\ast s^{\ast-1}=t\ast h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{h(s)}\right)=h^{-1}\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right),

(2.10d)

\displaystyle(t\ast s^{\ast-1})^{\ast-1}=h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{h(t)h(s^{\ast-1})}\right)=h^{-1}\left(\frac{h(s)}{h(t)}\right)=s\ast t^{\ast-1}.

In addition, in
[
8
]
it was noticed that the group
(J,\ast)
is also totally ordered,
where the order
\leq_{\ast}
on
J
:

(2.11)

s\leq_{\ast}t\quad\textnormal{if and only if}\quad e_{\ast}\leq t\ast s^{\ast-1}

verifies that

(2.12)

t\leq_{\ast}s\quad\textnormal{if and only if}\quad t\leq s\quad\textnormal{for any $t,s\in J$},

It was also noticed in
[
8
]
that, as the group is ordered, the following absolute value
|\cdot|_{\ast}\colon J\to[e_{\ast},+\infty)
can be introduced:

(2.13)

|t|_{\ast}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccr}t&\textnormal{if}&e_{*}\leq t,\\
t^{\ast-1}&\textnormal{if}&\,\,t<e_{\ast},\end{array}\right.

moreover, as
(J,\ast)
is abelian, the triangle inequality is satisfied:

(2.14)

|t\ast s|_{\ast}\leq|t|_{\ast}\ast|s|_{\ast}.

These facts allow to define a distance
d\colon(J,\ast)\times(J,\ast)\to([e_{\ast},+\infty),\ast)

as follows:

(2.15)

d(t,s):=|t\ast s^{\ast-1}|_{\ast}.

It will be useful to note that, given
e_{\ast}<L
it follows that

(2.16)

|t\ast s^{\ast-1}|_{\ast}\leq L\iff L^{\ast-1}\leq t\ast s^{\ast-1}\leq L\iff\frac{1}{h(L)}\leq\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\leq h(L).

As pointed out in
[
8
, Sect.2]
, the group
(J,\ast)
and his properties makes possible two alternative characterizations for
the uniform
h
–dichotomy. Firstly, the property (
2.10c
) allow us to see the uniform
h
–dichotomy from the following perspective:

(2.17)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}||\Phi(t,s)P(s)||&\leq&\displaystyle Kh(t\ast s^{\ast-1})^{-\alpha}\quad\textnormal{for any $t\geq s$ with $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$},\\
\\
||\Phi(t,s)Q(s)||&\leq&\displaystyle Kh(s\ast t^{\ast-1})^{-\alpha}\quad\textnormal{for any $s\geq t$ with $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$}.\end{array}\right.

Secondly, notice that (
2.11
) combined (
2.15
) allow to see uniform
h
–dichotomy from the alternative perspective:

(2.18)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}||\Phi(t,s)P(s)||&\leq&\displaystyle Kh(d(t,s))^{-\alpha}\quad\textnormal{for any $t\geq s$ with $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$}\\
\\
||\Phi(t,s)Q(s)||&\leq&\displaystyle Kh(d(t,s))^{-\alpha}\quad\textnormal{for any $s\geq t\geq a$ with $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$}.\end{array}\right.

2.2.
Uniform bounded growth

A careful reading of the dichotomies literature shows its strong relation with the properties of bounded growth and decay
which, tailored for an specific growth rate
h(\cdot)
, are defined as follows:

Definition 3
.

Given a growth rate
h\colon J\to(0,+\infty)
and its corresponding group
(J,*)
, the linear system (
1.1
) has:

a)

A uniform
bounded
h
–growth
on
\mathcal{I}\subseteq J
if for each
T>e_{\ast}
there exists

C_{T}\geq 1
such that any solution
t\mapsto x(t)
verifies

(2.19)

|x(t)|\leq C_{T}|x(s)|\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in[s,s\ast T]\cap\mathcal{I}$}.

b)

A uniform
bounded
h
–decay
on
\mathcal{I}\subseteq J
if for each
T>e_{\ast}
there exists
C_{T}\geq 1
such that any solution
t\mapsto x(t)
verifies

(2.20)

|x(t)|\leq C_{T}|x(s)|\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in[s\ast T^{\ast-1},s]\cap\mathcal{I}$}.

c)

A uniform
bounded
h
–growth and
h
–decay
on
\mathcal{I}\subseteq J
if for each
e_{\ast}<T
there exists
C_{T}\geq 1
such that any solution
t\mapsto x(t)
verifies

(2.21)

|x(t)|\leq C_{T}|x(s)|\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in[s\ast T^{\ast-1},s\ast T]\cap\mathcal{I}$}.

The characterization (
2.8
) allow us to
make a partition of
\mathcal{I}

\mathcal{I}=\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{k}\quad\textnormal{where $I_{k}=[s\ast T^{\ast(k-1)},s\ast T^{\ast k})$},

and, as noticed in
[
8
]
by using (
2.13
) combined with (
2.15
) and
e_{\ast}<T
, we can verify that the above partition of
J
is uniform since is composed by intervals of large
T
:

d(s\ast T^{\ast k},s\ast T^{\ast(k-1)})=d(T\ast s\ast T^{\ast(k-1)},s\ast T^{\ast(k-1)})=T.

The above mentioned uniform partition was key to prove the following result:

Proposition 2
.

[
8
, Lemmas 3.1,3.2,3.3]

The linear system (
1.1
) has:

a)

A uniform bounded
h
–growth on
\mathcal{I}
if and only if there exist

K_{0}\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 0
such that

(2.22)

||\Phi(t,s)||\leq K_{0}\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{\beta}=K_{0}h(t\ast s^{\ast-1})^{\beta}\quad\textnormal{for any $t\geq s$ with $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$.}

b)

A uniform bounded
h
–decay on
\mathcal{I}
if and only if there exist

K_{0}\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 0
such that

(2.23)

||\Phi(t,s)||\leq K_{0}\left(\frac{h(s)}{h(t)}\right)^{\beta}\quad\textnormal{for any $s\geq t$ with $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$.}

c)

A uniform bounded
h
–growth and
h
–decay on
\mathcal{I}
if and only if there exist

K_{0}\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 0
such that

(2.24)

||\Phi(t,s)||\leq K_{0}h(|t\ast s^{\ast-1}|_{\ast})^{\beta}\quad\textnormal{for any $t,s\in\mathcal{I}$}.

2.3.
Uniform
h
–noncriticality and
h
–expansiveness

In this subsection we will recall two properties which are essential
to have alternative characterizations of the
h
–dichotomy.

Definition 4
.

The system (
1.1
) is uniformly
h
–noncritical on an interval
\mathcal{I}\subseteq J
if there exists
T>e_{\ast}

and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that any solution
t\mapsto x(t)
of (
1.1
) satisfies

(2.25)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|x(t)|\leq\theta\sup\limits_{|u\ast t^{\ast-1}|_{\ast}\leq T}|x(u)|&=&\theta\sup\{|x(u)|\colon T^{\ast-1}<u\ast t^{\ast-1}\leq T\}\\
\\
&=&\theta\sup\{|x(u)|\colon t\ast T^{\ast-1}<u\leq t\ast T\},\end{array}

for all
t
such that
[t\ast T^{\ast-1},t\ast T]\subset\mathcal{I}
.

It will be useful to note that:

\bullet

If
\mathcal{I}=J^{+}:=[e_{\ast},+\infty)
, the inequality (
2.25
) must be verified for any
t\geq T
, which coincides with the Definition stated in
[
8
]
.

\bullet

If
\mathcal{I}=J^{-}:=(a_{0},e_{\ast}]
, the inequality (
2.25
) must be verified for any
t\in(a_{0}\ast T,T^{\ast-1}]
.

\bullet

If
\mathcal{I}=J
, the inequality (
2.25
) must be verified for any
t\in J
.

Remark 1
.

It direct to see that if (
1.1
) is uniformly
h
–noncritical on
J
, then it is also uniformly
noncritical on
(a_{0},e_{*}]
and
[e_{*},+\infty)
. In addition, it is useful to note that (
2.25
) also can be written as follows;

|x(t)|\leq\theta\sup\limits_{|u\ast t^{\ast-1}|_{\ast}\leq T}|x(u)|=\theta\sup\{|x(u)|\colon\frac{1}{h(T)}<\frac{h(u)}{h(t)}\leq h(T)\}

for all
t
such that
[t\ast T^{\ast-1},t\ast T]\subset\mathcal{I}
.

Definition 5
.

The system (
1.1
) is
h
–expansive on an interval
\mathcal{I}\subseteq J
if there exists positive constants
L
and
\beta
such that if
t\mapsto x(t)
is any solution of (
1.1
) and
[a,b]\subset\mathcal{I}
, then for
a\leq t\leq b
:

(2.26)

|x(t)|\leq L\{h(t\ast a^{\ast-1})^{-\beta}|x(a)|+h(b\ast t^{\ast-1})^{-\beta}|x(b)|\}.

Remark 2
.

By using the alternative characterization of the uniform
h
–dichotomy provided by (
2.18
) we also have an additional formulation for
(
2.26
) as follows

|x(t)|\leq L\{h(d(t,a))^{-\beta}|x(a)|+h(d(b,t))^{-\beta}|x(b)|\}

The thought–provoking article of K.J. Palmer
[
16
]
provides a characterization of the uniform
h
–dichotomies in terms of properties of uniform
h
–noncriticality and
h
–expansiveness restricted to the specific growth rate
h(t)=e^{t}
. A first attempt to generalize the Palmer’s results has been carried out in
[
8
]
. In fact it was proved that

Proposition 3
.

[
8
, Th.1]

If the linear system (
1.1
) has the properties of uniform bounded
h
–growth and uniform
h
–noncriticality on

J^{+}:=[e_{\ast},+\infty)
then it has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
[e_{\ast},+\infty)
.

The following result can be proved
mutatis mutandi
from the proof of the previous result.

Proposition 4
.

If the linear system (
1.1
) has the properties of uniform bounded
h
–decay and uniform
h
–noncriticality on

J^{-}:=(a_{0},e_{\ast}]
then it has a uniform
h

A direct consequence of Proposition

[

Assume that the linear system (

3.
h
–dichotomies on
J

The goal of this section is double, the first goal is to generalize the Proposition
5
for the case
\mathcal{I}=J

whereas the second one is to provide necessary and sufficient conditions ensuring that if the property of uniform
h
–dichotomy is verified simultaneously on
J^{-}=(a_{0},e_{*}]
and

J^{+}=[e_{*},+\infty)
then it is also verified on
J
. Both objectives will be achieved through several intermediate results.

With respect to the second goal, it is important to emphasize that if (
1.1
) has
a uniform
h
–dichotomy on the intervals
(a_{0},e_{*}]
and
[e_{*},+\infty)
this
not implies that (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J=(a_{0},e_{*}]\cup[e_{*},+\infty)
. In order to illustrate the above situation, let us consider the differential equation

(3.1)

x^{\prime}=a(t)x\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in J:=(0,+\infty)$},

where
a\colon(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as follows:

a(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\frac{1}{t}&\textnormal{if}&0<t\leq 1-\ell,\\
\\
\phi(t)&\textnormal{if}&1-\ell<t<1+\ell,\\
\\
-\frac{1}{t}&\textnormal{if}&1+\ell\leq t,\end{array}\right.

with
\ell\in(0,1)
and
\phi\colon[1-\ell,1+\ell]\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function such that

\phi(1-\ell)=\frac{1}{1-\ell}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\phi(1+\ell)=-\frac{1}{1+\ell}.

It is straightforward to verify that

\Phi(t)=\frac{1}{t}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\Phi(t,s)=\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{-1}\quad\textnormal{for any $t\geq s>1+\ell$},

then, it is easy to deduce that the equation (
3.1
) has an
h
–dichotomy on

\mathcal{I}_{1}=(1+\ell,+\infty)
with
P=K=\alpha=1
and the growth rate
h(t)=t
defined on
J=(0,+\infty)
.

Similarly, we can verify that

\Phi(t)=t\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\Phi(t,s)=\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{-1}=\frac{t}{s}\quad\textnormal{for any $0<t\leq s<1-\ell$},

and, as before, we can deduce that the equation (
3.1
) has an
h
–dichotomy on

\mathcal{I}_{2}=(0,1-\ell)
with
P=0
,
K=\alpha=1
and the same growth rate
h(t)=t
.

As
h\colon(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a growth rate, we can see that the unit element
of the corresponding group
(J,*)
is
e_{*}=1
. Moreover, as
\ell\in(0,1)
it follows that

\mathcal{I}_{1}\subset[e_{\ast},+\infty)
and
\mathcal{I}_{2}\subset(0,e_{\ast}]
. By using
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
we can see that (
3.1
)
has an
h
dichotomy on
(0,e_{\ast}]
and
[e_{\ast},+\infty)
. Nevertheless, by using Lemma
2.2
we can deduce that
the equation (
3.1
) has not an
h
–dichotomy on
J=(0,+\infty)
since any nontrivial solution is bounded
on
J=(0,+\infty)
. In fact, notice that
for any
x_{0}\neq 0
we have that

t\mapsto x(t,t_{0},x_{0})=e^{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}a(s)\,ds}x_{0}

is the solution of (
3.1
) passing through
x_{0}
at
t=t_{0}
. It is straightforward
to check that the boundedness follows since
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}x(t,t_{0},x_{0})=0
and
\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}x(t,t_{0},x_{0})=0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If the linear system (
1.1
) has a uniform

–dichotomy on
[e_{\ast},+\infty)
with projectors
P^{+}(s)
and constants

, then

(3.2)

\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\sup\limits_{t\geq s\geq e_{\ast}}|\Phi(t,s)\xi|<+\infty\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)
with
s\geq e_{\ast}
. As the system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
[e_{*},+\infty)
, for any
t\geq s\geq e_{\ast}
we have that

\displaystyle|\Phi(t,s)\xi|

\displaystyle=|\Phi(t,s)P^{+}(s)\xi|\leq K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}|\xi|,

and, by using
h(t)\geq h(s)

(3.3)

\sup\limits_{t\geq s\geq e_{\ast}}|\Phi(t,s)\xi|<+\infty.

Now, let us consider
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that (
3.3
) is fulfilled. By recalling that (
1.1
) has a uniform

–dichotomy on

I-P^{+}(s)=\Phi(s,t)\Phi(t,s)[I-P^{+}(s)]=\Phi(s,t)[I-P^{+}(t)]\Phi(t,s),

combined with
t\geq s>e_{\ast}
, we can deduce that

|[I-P^{+}(s)]\xi|\leq K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{-\alpha}|\Phi(t,s)\xi|.

By letting
t\to+\infty
combined with (
3.3
) we can deduce that
P^{+}(s)\xi=\xi
,
which implies that
\xi\in\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If the system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
(a_{0},e_{\ast}]
with projectors

, then

(3.4)

\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\sup\limits_{a_{0}<t\leq s\leq e_{\ast}}|\Phi(t,s)\xi|<+\infty\right\}.

Proof.

Let and
\xi\in\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)
with

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that the system (
1.1
) has uniform
h
–dichotomies on both intervals
[e_{\ast},+\infty)
and
(a_{0},e_{\ast}]

with projectors
P^{+}(\cdot)
and
P^{-}(\cdot)
respectively which are defined on

in the sense of Remark
3
. Then (

) has no nontrivial bounded solution if and only if
the projectors fulfill the condition:

(3.7)

P^{+}(s)P^{-}(s)=P^{-}(s)P^{+}(s)=P^{+}(s)\quad\textnormal{ for any }s\in J.

Proof.

Firstly, let us assume that the unique bounded solution of the linear system (
1.1
) is the trivial one, then by using lemmas
3.1
and
3.2

we can conclude that
\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)\cap\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)=\{0\}
, this fact combined with the identities

\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)\oplus\mathcal{N}P^{+}(s)=\mathcal{R}P^{-}(s)\oplus\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)

implies the existence of a subspace
V
such that
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)\oplus V\oplus\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)
. This allows us to take

(3.8)

\mathcal{N}P^{+}(s)=V\oplus\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathcal{R}P^{-}(s)=\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)\oplus V

and so
\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)\subseteq\mathcal{N}P^{+}(s)
and
\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)\subseteq\mathcal{R}P^{-}(s)
.

Now, let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
with
\xi_{1}\in\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)
and
\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{N}P^{+}(s)
. Then
P^{+}(s)\xi=\xi_{1}
and, since
\xi_{1}\in\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)\subset\mathcal{R}P^{-}(s)
, we get

P^{-}(s)P^{+}(s)\xi=P^{-}(s)\xi_{1}=\xi_{1}=P^{+}(s)\xi,

which shows that
P^{-}(s)P^{+}(s)=P^{+}(s)
for any
s\in J
.

Similarly, we can write
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\xi_{1}\in\mathcal{R}P^{-}(s)
and
\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)
, which leads to
P^{-}(s)\xi=\xi_{1}
. Moreover, as
\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{N}P^{-}(s)\subset\mathcal{N}P^{+}(s)
we obtain

P^{+}(s)P^{-}(s)\xi=P^{+}(s)\xi_{1}=P^{+}(s)\xi_{1}+P^{+}(s)\xi_{2}=P^{+}(s)\xi,

and we have that
P^{+}(s)P^{-}(s)=P^{+}(s)
for any
s\in J
. Finally, we conclude that
P^{-}(s)P^{+}(s)=P^{+}(s)P^{-}(s)=P^{+}(s)
for any
s\in J
.

Conversely, suppose that the projections
P^{+}(s)
and
P^{-}(s)

1.1

3.1
and
3.2

∎

2.26
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|x(t)|&\leq&L\left\{h(T)^{-\beta}|x(t*T^{*-1})|+h(T)^{-\beta}|x(t*T)|\right\}\\
\\
&\leq&\theta\sup\limits_{u\in[t*T^{-1},t*T]}|x(u)|,\end{array}

and the property of uniform
h
–noncriticality on
J
is fulfilled.

Finally, in order to proof the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i), we will suppose that (
1.1
) is uniformly
h
–noncritical on
J
. Then, by Remark
1
, the system (
1.1
) is uniformly
h
–noncritical in both
[e_{*},+\infty)
and

(a_{0},e_{*}]
. Now, as (
1.1
) has a uniform bounded
h
–growth on
[e_{\ast},+\infty)
and a uniform bounded
h
–decay on
(a_{0},e_{\ast}]
it follows by Propositions
3
and
4
that (
1.1
) has uniform
h
–dichotomies on
[e_{\ast},+\infty)
and
(a_{0},e_{\ast}]
respectively.

Now let
t\mapsto x(t)
be a bounded solution of (
1.1
) such that

\|x\|:=\sup\limits_{t\in J}|x(t)|<+\infty.

As (iii) is verified, by using Definition
4
with
\mathcal{I}=J
it follows that for any
t\in J
we have the existence of
\theta\in(0,1)
and
T>e_{\ast}
such that

|x(t)|\leq\theta\sup\limits_{|u\ast t^{\ast-1}|_{\ast}\leq T}|x(u)|\leq\theta\|x\|,

which implies that
\|x\|\leq\theta\|x\|
. Hence,
\|x\|=0
and so
x(t)=0
for all
t\in J
. This concludes that (
1.1
) has no nontrivial solution and the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

3.3.
Uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J

As we have seen at the beginning of this section, if the linear system (
1.1
) has simultaneously the properties of uniform
h
-dichotomy on the half lines
J^{-}
and
J^{+}
this does not always implies the existence of an
h
–dichotomy on the full line
J
. Now, we will provide a necessary and sufficient condition ensuring the uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J
. In this context, we recall the following definition adapted from
[
2
, p.536]
to a general uniform
h
–dichotomy.

Definition 6
.

The index of a
n
–dimensional linear system
\dot{x}=A(t)x
with uniform
h
–dichotomies both on
J^{-}=(a_{0},e_{*}]
and
J^{+}=[e_{*},+\infty)
respectively, is given by

i(A)=\textnormal{\text{dim}}\,\mathcal{R}P^{+}(\cdot)+\textnormal{\text{dim}}\,\mathcal{N}P^{-}(\cdot)-n,

where
P^{-}(\cdot)
and
P^{+}(\cdot)
are the projections for the dichotomies on
J^{-}
and
J^{+}
respectively.

We point out that as
P^{-}(\cdot)
and
P^{+}(\cdot)
fulfills the invariance property (
1.2
)
on
J^{-}
and
J^{+}
respectively then the dimensions are invariant.

The following result generalizes Proposition 1 from
[
2
]
for the uniform
h
–dichotomy:

Theorem 2
.

The system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J

if and only if:

a)

The system has uniform
h
–dichotomies on
J^{-}
and
J^{+}

with projections
P^{-}(\cdot)
and
P^{+}(\cdot)
respectively,

b)

The system has no nontrivial bounded solutions,

c)

The index of the linear system is
i(A)=0
.

Proof.

Firstly, if the linear system has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J
with projector
P(\cdot)
,
the properties a) and c) are verified straightforwardly with
P(\cdot)=P^{-}(\cdot)=P^{+}(\cdot)
. In addition,
the property b) follows from Lemma
2.2
.

Secondly, let us assume that the properties a),b) and c) are fulfilled. Now, we will prove that:

(3.16)

\mathcal{N}P^{-}(t)=\mathcal{N}P^{+}(t)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathcal{R}P^{+}(t)=\mathcal{R}P^{-}(t).

In order to prove the above identity, notice that by a) and b) we can use the Lemma
3.3
and Remark
4
, which implies the contentions

\mathcal{N}P^{-}(t)\subseteq\mathcal{N}P^{+}(t)
and
\mathcal{R}P^{+}(s)\subseteq\mathcal{R}P^{-}(s)
.

Now, if (
3.16
) is not verified, we will have that
\mathcal{N}P^{-}(t)\subset\mathcal{N}P^{+}(t)

and/or
\mathcal{R}P^{+}(t)\subset\mathcal{R}P^{-}(t)
.

In case that
\mathcal{N}P^{-}(t)\subset\mathcal{N}P^{+}(t)
, we have

\text{dim}\,\mathcal{N}P^{-}(t)<\text{dim}\,\mathcal{N}P^{+}(t)
, which combined with
i(A)=0
leads to

\begin{array}[]{rcl}n&=&\text{dim}\,\mathcal{N}P^{-}(t)+\text{dim}\,\mathcal{R}\,P^{+}(t)\\
&<&\text{dim}\,\mathcal{N}P^{+}(t)+\text{dim}\,\mathcal{R}\,P^{+}(t)=n,\end{array}

obtaining a contradiction and
\mathcal{N}P^{-}(t)=\mathcal{N}P^{+}(t)
follows. Moreover, the
identity
\mathcal{R}\,P^{+}(t)=\mathcal{R}\,P^{-}(t)
can be proved in
a similar way.

As
P^{-}(t)\xi\in\mathcal{R}P^{-}(t)=\mathcal{R}P^{+}(t)
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we can easily conclude that

P^{+}(t)P^{-}(t)\xi=P^{-}(t)\xi
. Moreover, by using the statements a) and b) combined with Lemma
3.3

and the identity (
3.7
) we also have that
P^{+}(t)P^{-}(t)\xi=P^{+}(t)\xi
. By gathering these identities we can see that

P^{-}(t)\xi=P^{+}(t)\xi\quad\textnormal{for any $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

and the identity
P^{+}(t)=P^{-}(t)
follows, this implies that the linear system has
a uniform
h
-dichotomy on
J
with the same projector
P(\cdot)=P^{+}(\cdot)=P^{-}(\cdot)
.
∎

The above result allow us to revisit the scalar equation
x^{\prime}=a(t)x
described by (
3.1
) at the beginning
of this section 3 with
J=(0,+\infty)
,
e_{*}=1
and
h(t)=t
, where the equation has
h
dichotomies on
(0,1]

and
[1,+\infty)
with projectors
P^{-}(t)=0
and
P^{+}(t)=1
respectively. As
\mathcal{R}P^{+}=\mathcal{N}P^{-}=\mathbb{R}
,
it follows that
i(a)=1
and the
h
–dichotomy on
J
is not verified.

4.
Generalized Floquet theory and uniform
h
–dichotomy

In
[
4
, p.180]
, T.A. Burton and J.S. Muldowney proposed a generalization of the Floquet theory for
(
1.1
) by coining the following definition tailored for our purposes:

Definition 7
.

The linear system (
1.1
) is a generalized Floquet system with respect to
f
if
f
is an absolutely
continuous function on
J:=(a_{0},+\infty)
such that

(4.1)

f^{\prime}(t)A(f(t))=A(t)

for almost all
t>a_{0}
and

(4.2)

f(t)>t

for all
t>a_{0}
.

In this section we will work with the specific function
f(t)=t*T
, where the group

(J,*)
is defined in terms of a differentiable growth rate
h(\cdot)
and
T>e_{*}
. In addition, we will assume
that the matrix valued function
A(\cdot)
from the system (
1.1
)
fulfills the following property:

(4.3)

(t*T)^{\prime}A(t*T)=\frac{h^{\prime}(t)h(T)}{h^{\prime}(t*T)}A(t*T)=A(t)\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in J$},

where the first identity follows by
h(t*T)=h(t)h(T)
from (
2.10a
) which implies that
h^{\prime}(t*T)(t*T)^{\prime}=h^{\prime}(t)h(T)
. Moreover,
t*T>t
for any
t\in J
since
T>e_{*}
. Notice that if
h(t)=e^{t}
then

(J,*)=(\mathbb{R},+)
and (
4.3
) becomes
A(t+T)=A(t)
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

In
[
4
]
it is shown that the properties (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) allow to emulate
the construction of the monodromy matrix similarly as in the classical Floquet’s theory
[
1
, Ch.1]
,
[
9
, pp.118–121]
. Obviously,
these results can be applied for the particular case of Definition
7
given by the condition (
4.3
). Nevertheless, we will see that
our restriction to this specific case combined with the underlying properties of the group
(J,*)
will provide us a criterion for detect the uniform
h
–dichotomy.

4.1.
Technical results

In order to make this section self contained, we will revisit some results from
[
4
]
tailored for the assumption (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

If the linear system (
1.1
) fulfills the property (
4.3
) and
\Phi(t)
is a fundamental matrix of (
1.1
) then

\Psi(t):=\Phi(t*T)
is also a fundamental matrix of (
1.1
).

Proof.

By using the fact that
\Phi(\cdot)
is a fundamental matrix combined with the chain rule and (
4.3
) it follows that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Psi^{\prime}(t)&=&(t*T)^{\prime}\Phi^{\prime}(t*T)\\
\\
&=&(t*T)^{\prime}A(t*T)\Phi(t*T)\\
\\
&=&A(t)\Psi(t).\end{array}

∎

As the columns of
\Phi(t)
and
\Phi(t*T)
conform basis of solutions of (
1.1
), there exists
a non singular matrix
V
such that

(4.4)

\Phi(t*T)=\Phi(t)V\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in J$}.

Similarly as in the classical Floquet’s theory, the matrix
V
will be called as the
monodromy matrix
.
The eigenvalues of
V
will be called as the
h
–Floquet multipliers.

Remark 5
.

Some direct consequences of (
4.4
) are:

i)

The ”biperiodicity” property

\Phi(t*T,s*T)=\Phi(t,s)\quad\textnormal{for any $t,s\in J$}.

ii)

The property

(4.5)

\Phi(t*T^{*n})=\Phi(t)V^{n}\quad\textnormal{for any $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

iii)

If
\Phi(e_{*})=I
, then it follows that
\Phi(T)=V
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the assumption (
4.3
), a number
\rho\in\mathbb{C}
is an
h
–Floquet’s multiplier of the system (
1.1
) if and only if there exists a nontrivial solution
t\mapsto x(t)
such that

(4.6)

x(t*T)=\rho\,x(t)\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in J$}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, it can be assumed that
\Phi(e_{*})=I
, which leads to
\Phi(T)=V
according to Remark
5
.

Firstly, it will be assumed that
\rho
is an eigenvalue of the monodromy matrix
\Phi(T)=V
. Then, by definition we have the existence of
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such
that
\Phi(T)\xi=\rho\,\xi
. Now, let us consider the solution
t\mapsto x(t)
of the initial value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}x^{\prime}&=&A(t)x\\
x(e_{*})&=&\xi.\end{array}\right.

It is straightforward to see that
x(t)=\Phi(t)\xi
for any
t\in J
, and we can see that (
4.4
) implies that

x(t*T)=\Phi(t*T)\xi=\Phi(t)V\xi=\rho\,x(t)

and the identity (
4.6
) follows.

Secondly, let us assume that (
4.6
) is verified. By evaluating this identity at
t=e_{*}
we obtain that

x(T)=\rho\,x(e_{*}).

On the other hand, as any non trivial solution
t\mapsto x(t)
of (
1.1
) is given by
x(t)=\Phi(t)x(e_{*})
, this allows to
see that

x(T)=\Phi(T)x(e_{*})=V\,x(e_{*}).

As the right parts of the above identities are equal to
x(T)
we have the identity

Vx(e_{*})=\rho\,x(e_{*})

and it follows that
\rho
is an eigenvalue of
V
.
∎

4.2.
Uniform
h
–dichotomy for generalized Floquet systems

Theorem 3
.

If the linear system (
1.1
) fulfills the property (
4.3
) the following properties
are equivalent:

i)

The eigenvalues of the monodromy matrix
V
are not in the unit circle,

ii)

The linear system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J
.

Proof.

If we assume that the property i) is fulfilled, we can study the difference equation

(4.7)

x_{n+1}=Vx_{n}.

As the eigenvalues of
V
are not in the unit circle, it can be proved (see
e.g.

[
17
]
) that the autonomous system (
4.7
)
has an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}
, that is, there exists a constant projector
P^{2}=P

and positive constants
K_{0}
and
\alpha
such that

(4.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}||V^{n}P[V^{k}]^{-1}||&\leq&K_{0}e^{-\alpha(n-k)}\quad\textnormal{for any $n\geq k$}\\
\\
||V^{n}(I-P)[V^{k}]^{-1}||&\leq&K_{0}e^{-\alpha(k-n)}\quad\textnormal{for any $k\geq n$}.\end{array}\right.

As it was stated in the subsection 2.2, we can construct the uniform partition for
J
:

J=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{n}\quad\textnormal{where $J_{n}=[T^{\ast(n-1)},T^{\ast n})$}.

Now, when considering a pair
(t,s)
with
t>s
, there exists a pair of integers
n,k
with
n\geq k
such that

(4.9)

s\in[T^{*(k-1)},T^{*k})\quad\textnormal{and}\quad t\in[T^{*(n-1)},T^{*n}).

As
h(\cdot)
is strictly increasing the properties (
4.9
) combined with (
2.9
) implies that

h(T^{*(k-1)})\leq h(s)<h(T^{*k})\iff h(T)^{k-1}\leq h(s)<h(T)^{k},

then we can deduce that

(4.10)

k-1\leq\frac{\ln(h(s))}{\ln(h(T))}<k,

whereas the inequality

(4.11)

n-1\leq\frac{\ln(h(t))}{\ln(h(T))}<n,

can be deduced similarly. As
\alpha>0
, the estimations (
4.10
)–(
4.11
) imply that

(4.12)

\alpha(k-n)<\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{\ln(h(T))}\ln\left(\frac{h(s)}{h(t)}\right)=\alpha+\ln\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{\ln(h(T))}}.

Now, by (
4.9
) we can deduce that

(4.13)

s=T^{*(k-1)}*\tilde{s}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad t=T^{*(n-1)}*\tilde{t}\quad\textnormal{for some $\tilde{t},\tilde{s}\in[e_{*},T)$}.

By using (
4.5
) we can deduce that

\Phi(t)=\Phi(\tilde{t}*T^{*(n-1)})=\Phi(\tilde{t})V^{n-1}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\Phi(s)=\Phi(\tilde{s}*T^{*(k-1)})=\Phi(\tilde{s})V^{k-1},

which combined with (
4.8
) and (
4.12
) leads to

\begin{array}[]{rcl}||\Phi(t)P\Phi^{-1}(s)||&=&||\Phi(\tilde{t})V^{n-1}P[V^{k-1}]^{-1}\Phi^{-1}(\tilde{s})||\\
&\leq&K_{0}K_{1}K_{2}e^{-\alpha(n-k)}\\
&\leq&K_{0}K_{1}K_{2}e^{\alpha}\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{\ln(h(T))}},\end{array}

where

K_{1}=\max\limits_{u\in[e_{*},T]}||\Phi(u)||\quad\textnormal{and}\quad K_{2}=\max\limits_{u\in[e_{*},T]}||\Phi^{-1}(u)||.

The case
t<s
can be addressed similarly and it can be obtained that

(4.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}||\Phi(t)P\Phi^{-1}(s)||&\leq&K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{-\tilde{\alpha}}\quad\textnormal{for any $t\geq s$ with $t,s\in J$}\\
\\
||\Phi(t)(I-P)\Phi^{-1}(s)||&\leq&K\left(\frac{h(s)}{h(t)}\right)^{-\tilde{\alpha}}\quad\textnormal{for any $s\geq t$ with $t,s\in J$},\end{array}\right.

with
K=K_{0}K_{1}K_{2}e^{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha/\ln(h(T))
. Now, by using Proposition
1
we can conclude that the uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J
follows.

If we assume that the linear system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy, the property i) can be verified by contradiction.

In fact, if
\rho
is an
h
–Floquet multiplier with
|\rho|=1
by Lemma
4.2
we have the existence of
a non trivial solution
t\mapsto x(t)=\Phi(t)\xi
of (
1.1
) such that (
4.6
) is verified with

V\xi=\rho\xi
.

If
t>e_{*}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t\in[T^{*(n-1)},T^{*n})
, then there exists
\tilde{t}\in[e_{*},T)

such that

x(t)=\Phi(t)\xi=\Phi(T^{*(n-1)}*\tilde{t})\xi=\Phi(\tilde{t})V^{n}\xi=\Phi(\tilde{t})\rho^{n}\xi,

which implies that

(4.15)

|x(t)|\leq M_{\xi}:=\max\limits_{u\in[e_{*},T]}||\Phi(u)|||\xi|

is fulfilled for any
t\geq e_{*}

We will see now that the bound (
4.15
) is fulfilled for any
t\in J
. In fact, if
t\in(T^{*-1},e_{*}]
, then
it follows that
e_{*}<t*T<T
. Then, by using (
4.6
) combined with
|\rho|=1
it follows that
|x(t)|=|x(t*T)|\leq M_{\xi}

and we can proceed recursively.

As (
4.15
) is verified for any
t\in J
, this implies that
t\mapsto x(t)
is bounded on
J
, then the
statement b) from Theorem
2
would imply that
t\mapsto x(t)
is the trivial solution, obtaining a contradiction and the Theorem follows.
∎

Corollary 1
.

If the linear system (
1.1
) fulfills the property (
4.3
) and
t\mapsto x(t,t_{0},x_{0})
is a nontrivial solution of (
1.1
) passing trough
x_{0}\neq 0
at
t=t_{0}\in J
,
then

a)

If all the
h
–Floquet multipliers are inside the unit circle then the origin is uniformly
h
–stable, that is, there exists
K>0
and
\alpha>0
such that

(4.16)

|x(t,t_{0},x_{0})|\leq K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(t_{0})}\right)^{-\alpha}|x_{0}|\quad\textnormal{for any $t\geq t_{0}$}.

b)

If all the
h
–Floquet multipliers are outside the unit circle then the origin is uniformly
h
–unstable, that is,
there exists
K>0
and
\alpha>0
such that

(4.17)

|x(t,t_{0},x_{0})|\geq\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(t_{0})}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{|x_{0}|}{K}\quad\textnormal{for any $t\geq t_{0}$}.

Proof.

As the
h
–Floquet multipliers are either inside or outside the unit circle, it follows by Theorem

3
that the system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy.

If the monodromy matrix
V
has all its eigenvalues inside the unit circle, it is easy to see that

P=I
in (
4.8
) and, by following the lines of the proof of Theorem
3
, we can conclude
that the system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy with projector
P=I
. Now, the statement a) follows by using Lemma
2.1
combined with the left estimation of (
2.4
).

Similarly, if the monodromy matrix
V
has all its eigenvalues outside the unit circle, it is easy to see that

P=0
in (
4.8
) and by following the lines of the proof of Theorem
3
we have
that the system (
1.1
) has a uniform
h
–dichotomy with projector
P=0
. Now, the statement a) follows by using Lemma
2.1
combined with the right estimation of (
2.4
).
∎

The above result generalizes Theorem 1 from
[
4
, p.190]
which states that the solutions of a generalized Floquet
system (
1.1
) are either asymptotically stable or unstable according to the location of the
h
-Floquet multipliers
either inside or outside the unit circle. The originality of our result is that the properties of the group
(J,*)
allow us to provide
a sharper characterization of the contractions (
4.16
) and expansions (
4.17
) by noticing that they are dominated
by the growth rate
h(\cdot)
associated to the group
(J,*)
.

4.3.
An example

Given
\alpha>0
, let us consider the diagonal system

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{x}_{1}\\
\dot{x}_{2}\end{array}\right)=A(t)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{array}\right)\quad\textnormal{with}\quad A(t)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle-\alpha\frac{1}{t}&0\\
0&\displaystyle\alpha\frac{1}{t}\end{array}\right]\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in J=(0,+\infty)$},

which is a particular case of the system (
2.1
) studied in section 2 by considering
J=(0,+\infty)
and
the growth rate
h\colon J\to J
defined by
h(t)=t
.

The corresponding group
operation for this growth rate is
t*s=ts
and the unit element is
e_{*}=1
which, given any
T>e_{*}
, allows to deduce that

\frac{h^{\prime}(t)h(T)}{h^{\prime}(t*T)}A(t*T)=T\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle-\alpha\frac{1}{tT}&0\\
0&\displaystyle\alpha\frac{1}{tT}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle-\alpha\frac{1}{t}&0\\
0&\displaystyle\alpha\frac{1}{t}\end{array}\right]=A(t),

and the identity
(
4.3
) follows for any
t\in J
.

When considering a fundamental matrix

\Phi(t)=\textnormal{Diag}\{t^{-\alpha},t^{\alpha}\}
, we can see that
\Phi(e_{*})=I

and

\Phi(t*T^{*n})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{(tT^{n})^{\alpha}}&0\\
0&(tT^{n})^{\alpha}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}&0\\
0&t^{\alpha}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{T^{\alpha}}&0\\
0&T^{\alpha}\end{array}\right]^{n}=\Phi(t)V^{n}

and the identity (
4.5
) is verified. Moreover, as
T>1
and
\alpha>0
it follows that
the eigenvalues of
V
are
T^{-\alpha}<1<T^{\alpha}
. By Theorem
3
we have that the diagonal
system has a uniform
h
–dichotomy on
J=(0,+\infty)
, which is consistent with the more general example studied at the beginning of section 2.
Finally, notice that the difference system
x_{n+1}=Vx_{n}
has an exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}
and the projector

P(t)
coincides with the one stated in (
2.2
).
