Variational Dynamics of Open Quantum Spin Systems in Phase Space
Jacopo Tosca, Zejian Li, Francesco Carnazza, Cristiano Ciuti

Abstract
We introduce a variational method for simulating the dynamics of interacting open quantum spin systems. The method is based on the spin phase-space representation and variationally targets the Husimi- Q Q function with an ansatz based on a multi-dimensional mixture of spin-coherent states. Crucially, the mixture coefficients are allowed to take negative values, enabling the faithful capture of quantum correlations beyond semiclassical descriptions. The resulting equations of motion are derived from the Dirac-Frenkel variational principle and can be evaluated efficiently without resorting to Monte Carlo sampling by exploiting the analytical structure of the ansatz. As a first application, we demonstrate that this approach accurately captures both the full quantum dynamics and the non-equilibrium steady states of the transverse-field quantum Ising model, in excellent agreement with exact diagonalization. Furthermore, we show that the method scales efficiently to large two-dimensional lattices, a regime that remains challenging for other techniques.



Introduction.—

The realization of scalable quantum technologies is one of the main challenges of modern physics. A wide range of platforms, including quantum annealers

;

Modeling this coupling remains a major challenge for spin-based quantum simulation and computation. While the interaction with the environment is necessary for implementing external control and quantum gates, it also induces non-unitary dissipative processes that typically lead to decoherence and loss of quantum correlations. At the same time, the interplay between unitary dynamics and dissipation gives rise to a rich phenomenology, and suitably engineered dissipation can stabilize and prepare non-trivial quantum states

;
Beige et al. 2000
;

Li et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2023
. A proper theoretical description therefore requires going beyond the wave function formalism to the density matrix representation, whose dynamics, when weakly coupled to a Markovian bath, is governed by the Lindblad master equation.

The exponential growth of the Hilbert space makes exact approaches intractable beyond very small system sizes, creating a pressing need for scalable and efficient approximation methods capable of capturing non-equilibrium dynamics in strongly correlated open quantum systems. Existing numerical methods range from mean-field approaches
Jin et al. 2016
;
Biella et al. 2018
to semiclassical
Schachenmayer et al. 2015
;
Huber et al. 2021
;
Verstraelen et al. 2023

. More recently, neural network quantum states have enabled very accurate ground state calculations
Carleo and Troyer 2017
;
Pfau et al. 2024
, yet their extension to non-equilibrium dynamics and steady states remains computationally demanding due to the reliance on Monte Carlo sampling at each time step
Hartmann and Carleo 2019
;
Vicentini et al. 2019
;
Luo et al. 2022
. Overall, a common limitation of these approaches is their difficulty in treating large two-dimensional (2D) open spin systems, where both entanglement growth and dissipative dynamics pose severe challenges.

A different route has recently been introduced for bosonic systems with infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces, based on a phase space formulation in which the Wigner function is approximated with a multi-Gaussian ansatz. This approach allows for a fully analytical and sampling-free evaluation of the dynamics
Tosca et al. 2025b
, which exploits automatic differentiation and reaches accuracy comparable to exact diagonalization for much larger system sizes.
Here, we extend and generalize this phase-space strategy to interacting spin systems by adopting a different representation, namely the Husimi-
Q
function, and constructing a variational ansatz in terms of a multi-coherent state decomposition. The resulting equations of motion are derived from the Dirac-Frenkel variational principle
Dirac 1930
and evaluated analytically, exploiting the phase space structure of the ansatz and automatic differentiation to achieve both high numerical efficiency and precision without Monte Carlo sampling. This approach opens a route to the accurate simulation of non-equilibrium quantum dynamics in large two-dimensional spin lattices.

Conclusion and Perspectives.—

We have introduced an efficient variational method to simulate the driven-dissipative dynamics of interacting spin systems, based on a phase-space representation of the many-spin density matrix via the Husimi-
Q
function. By parameterizing it with a mixture of spin-coherent states (v-MCS ansatz), whose coefficients are allowed to take negative values, the method is able to faithfully capture genuine quantum correlations beyond semiclassical descriptions. Combined with the fully analytical evaluation of the variational equations of motion, this approach bypasses stochastic sampling while achieving controlled convergence of both transient dynamics and non-equilibrium steady states.

We benchmarked the approach on the dissipative transverse-field Ising model in one and two dimensions, finding excellent agreement with exact results and improved accuracy over state-of-the-art neural-network architectures, at a fraction of the computational cost. The method further extends straightforwardly to arbitrary lattice geometries and dimensionalities.

The v-MCS ansatz establishes a sampling-free paradigm for variational simulations of dissipative spin dynamics, opening several promising directions. Its extension to higher-spin systems (
S>1/2
) is conceptually straightforward within the same phase-space framework, requiring adapted ansätze on generalized Bloch spheres.
Moreover, the ability to efficiently capture real-time dynamics makes it well-suited to exploring intrinsically dynamical phenomena in open quantum systems, such as dissipative time crystals
Iemini et al. 2018
;
Cabot et al. 2022
;
Delmonte et al. 2025
. Finally, the analytical structure of the framework can be naturally combined with different variational ansatz families—for instance, by incorporating inter-spin correlations directly into the ansatz components—to efficiently parameterize strongly entangled regimes and critical points of dissipative phase transitions.

Variational phase-space equations of motion.—

We summarize here the phase-space formulation of spin dynamics for a single spin-
S
. The generalization to
N_{s}
spins follows directly from the Cartesian-product structure of the phase space
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

The action of the operator
\hat{S}^{z}
is represented in phase space by the operator acting on the Husimi-
Q
function
Klimov and Chumakov 2009
:

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\rho}\hat{S}^{z}\\
\hat{S}^{z}\rho\end{array}}}\right\}\leftrightarrow[\mp\frac{l_{z}}{2}+\Lambda_{0}(\varphi,\vartheta)]Q(\varphi,\vartheta)\,,

(A1)

where
(\varphi,\vartheta)
are spherical coordinates, and

l_{z}=-i\partial_{\varphi}\,,\penalty\ \Lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[2S\cos\vartheta-\sin\vartheta\partial_{\vartheta}}}\right]\,.

(A2)

The ladder operators
\hat{S}^{\pm}
admit analogous phase-space representations
Klimov and Chumakov 2009
:

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\rho}\hat{S}^{+}\\
\hat{S}^{+}\hat{\rho}\end{array}}}\right\}\leftrightarrow[\mp l_{+}/2+\Lambda_{+}(\varphi,\vartheta)]Q(\varphi,\vartheta)\,,

(A3)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{\rho}\hat{S}^{-}\\
\hat{S}^{-}\hat{\rho}\end{array}}}\right\}\leftrightarrow[\mp l_{-}/2+\Lambda_{-}(\varphi,\vartheta)]Q(\varphi,\vartheta)\,,

(A4)

with

\Lambda_{\pm}=\mathrm{e}^{\pm i\varphi}\frac{\sin\vartheta}{2\varepsilon}\pm\frac{1}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\cos\vartheta l_{\pm}-\mathrm{e}^{\pm i\varphi}\sin\vartheta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l_{z}\pm 1}}\right)}}\right]\,,

(A5)

and

l_{\pm}=\mathrm{e}^{\pm i\varphi}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pm\partial_{\vartheta}+i\cot\vartheta\partial_{\varphi}}}\right)\,,

(A6)

where
\varepsilon=(2S+1)^{-1}
.

\displaystyle+[l_{-}/2+\Lambda_{-}(\varphi,\vartheta)]Q

\displaystyle-[-l_{+}/2+\Lambda_{+}(\varphi,\vartheta)]Q

\displaystyle-[-l_{-}/2+\Lambda_{-}(\varphi,\vartheta)]Q\big)

The computation of the quantum geometric tensor

\displaystyle T_{kl}({\bm{\theta}})

(A8)

\displaystyle F_{k}({\bm{\theta}})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\theta^{L}_{k}}\mathcal{I}_{F}({\bm{\theta}}^{L},{\bm{\theta}}^{R})|_{{\bm{\theta}}^{L}={\bm{\theta}}^{R}={\bm{\theta}}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}({\bm{\theta}}^{L},{\bm{\theta}}^{R})

with the shorthand notation

\displaystyle Q({\bm{\theta}})\equiv(\bm{\Omega}\mapsto Q(\bm{\Omega};{\bm{\theta}}))

(A10)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle\equiv\int_{(\mathbb{S}^{2})^{N_{s}}}f(\bm{\Omega})g(\bm{\Omega}){\rm{d}}\bm{\Omega}\,.

analytically
, completely avoiding the stochastic sampling required in other variational approaches.

We now derive the equations of motion for the model considered in the main text within the v-MCS ansatz for the Husimi-
Q
function, focusing on spin
S=1/2
.
Given the structure of the ansatz (
6
),

\displaystyle Q({\bm{\theta}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{c}}c_{k}\prod_{i=1}^{N_{s}}q(\bm{m}_{ki})\,,

(A12)

the variational parameters are
{\bm{\theta}}=\{c_{k},\bm{m}_{ki}\}
, and the integrals introduced above simplify accordingly. The first integral evaluates to

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T}({\bm{\theta}}^{L},{\bm{\theta}}^{R})

\displaystyle=\sum_{kl}c^{L}_{k}c^{R}_{l}\prod_{i}\langle q(\bm{m}^{L}_{ki}),q(\bm{m}^{R}_{li})\rangle\,,

(A13)

where the local overlap integral on a single Bloch sphere is

\displaystyle\langle q(\bm{m}^{L}),q(\bm{m}^{R})\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}q({\bm{n}};\bm{m}^{L})q({\bm{n}};\bm{m}^{R}){\rm{d}}\mu({\bm{n}})

(A14)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{12\pi}(3+\bm{m}^{L}\cdot\bm{m}^{R})\,.

The second integral is model dependent, yet remains tractable due to the local structure of the Liouvillian. In particular,
\mathcal{L}
can be decomposed into one- and two-local contributions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i}\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(1)}+\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\mathcal{L}_{ij}^{(2)}\,,

(A15)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-{\rm{i}}g[\hat{S}_{i}^{x},\bullet]+\gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\hat{S}_{i}^{-}\bullet\hat{S}_{i}^{+}-\dfrac{1}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\hat{S}_{i}^{+}\hat{S}_{i}^{-},\bullet}}\right\}}}\right)\,,

(A16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{ij}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-{\rm{i}}V[\hat{S}_{i}^{z}\hat{S}_{j}^{z},\bullet]\,.

It follows that the relevant contributions reduce to the evaluation of the following integrals:

\displaystyle\langle q(\bm{m}_{i}^{L}),\mathcal{L}^{(1)}_{i,Q}q(\bm{m}_{i}^{R})\rangle

(A17)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{g}{12\pi}(\bm{m}_{i}^{R}\times\bm{m}_{i}^{L})_{x}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\gamma}{24\pi}[\bm{m}_{i}^{L}\cdot\bm{m}_{i}^{R}+m^{L}_{i,z}(2+m^{R}_{i,z})]\,,

\displaystyle\langle q(\bm{m}_{i}^{L})q(\bm{m}_{j}^{L}),\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{ij,Q}q(\bm{m}_{i}^{R})q(\bm{m}_{j}^{R})\rangle

(A18)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{V}{288\pi^{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(m^{L}_{j,z}+3m^{R}_{j,z})(\bm{m}^{R}_{i}\times\bm{m}^{L}_{i})_{z}}}\right.

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.+(m^{L}_{i,z}+3m^{R}_{i,z})(\bm{m}^{R}_{j}\times\bm{m}^{L}_{j})_{z}}}\right]\,.

These expressions lead to the Liouvillian overlap integral generating the force vector:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{F}({\bm{\theta}}^{L},{\bm{\theta}}^{R})=\sum_{kl}c_{k}^{L}c_{l}^{R}\prod_{\mu}\langle q(\bm{m}^{L}_{k\mu}),q(\bm{m}^{R}_{l\mu})\rangle

(A19)

\displaystyle\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sum_{i}\dfrac{\langle q(\bm{m}^{L}_{ki}),\mathcal{L}^{(1)}_{i,Q}q(\bm{m}^{R}_{li})\rangle}{\langle q(\bm{m}^{L}_{ki}),q(\bm{m}^{R}_{li})\rangle}}}\right.

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.+\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\dfrac{\langle q(\bm{m}^{L}_{ki})q(\bm{m}^{L}_{ij}),\mathcal{L}^{(2)}_{ij,Q}q(\bm{m}^{R}_{li})q(\bm{m}^{R}_{lj})\rangle}{\langle q(\bm{m}^{L}_{ki}),q(\bm{m}^{R}_{li})\rangle\langle q(\bm{m}^{L}_{kj}),q(\bm{m}^{R}_{lj})\rangle}}}\right\}\,.

The quantum geometric tensor
T_{kl}
and the force vector
F_{k}
are then obtained by analytically differentiating this expression with respect to the variational parameters, which can be performed efficiently, for instance, using automatic differentiation.

Phase-space evaluation of observables.—

In the phase-space formulation of quantum mechanics, the expectation value of an observable
\hat{A}
can be obtained via its
P-
symbol, which is a phase-space function
P_{A}(\bm{\Omega})
defined as
Klimov and Chumakov 2009

\displaystyle\hat{A}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dfrac{2S+1}{4\pi}}}\right)^{N_{s}}\int\outerproduct{\Omegavec}{\Omegavec}P_{\hat{A}}(\bm{\Omega}){\rm{d}}\bm{\Omega}\,,

(B1)

such that one has,

\displaystyle\Tr[\hat{\rho}\hat{A}]

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dfrac{2S+1}{4\pi}}}\right)^{N_{s}}\int\bra{\Omegavec}\hat{\rho}\ket{\Omegavec}P_{\hat{A}}(\bm{\Omega}){\rm{d}}\bm{\Omega}

(B2)

\displaystyle=\int Q(\bm{\Omega})P_{\hat{A}}(\bm{\Omega}){\rm{d}}\bm{\Omega}\,,

where the integrals above are performed over
{(\mathbb{S}^{2})^{N_{s}}}
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle P_{\hat{\mathds{1}}}(\bm{\Omega})=1\,,\penalty\ P_{\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}}(\bm{\Omega})=(S+1)n_{i}^{\alpha}\,,

(B3)

and that the
P
symbol of the product of commuting operators (such as those acting on different spins) is simply the product of the individual
P
symbols. Therefore, for our v-MCS ansatz (with
S=1/2
), the expectation value of a single-site spin operator is simply,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{S}_{i}^{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int Q(\bm{\Omega})P_{\hat{S}^{\alpha}_{i}}(\bm{\Omega}){\rm{d}}\bm{\Omega}

(B4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{c}}c_{k}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\dfrac{1}{4\pi}(1+{\bm{n}}\cdot\bm{m}_{ki})\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{2}+1}}\right)n^{\alpha}_{i}{\rm{d}}\Omega({\bm{n}})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k}c_{k}m^{\alpha}_{ki}\,,

where we recall the notations defined in the main text that
\bm{\Omega}\equiv({\bm{n}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{n}}_{N_{s}})
, and
{\rm{d}}\bm{\Omega}
denotes the spherical integration measure.
