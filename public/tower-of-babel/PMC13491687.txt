Effects of electrical stimulation on lower limb function after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction: a systematic review and network meta-analysis
Authors: Shihong Zhang, Haoyang Wang, Ran Zhang, Hongxiang Zhang, Lei He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo compare the therapeutic effects of different electrical stimulation modalities combined with rehabilitation training on lower limb function improvement in patients following anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction.MethodsPubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, and CNKI databases were systematically searched from inception to March 2, 2026, and a meta-analysis was subsequently conducted to evaluate the included studies (PROSPERO registration number: CRD420261364272).ResultsA total of 11 randomized controlled trials involving 629 patients were included. Traditional meta-analysis showed that electrical stimulation combined with rehabilitation training was significantly superior to rehabilitation training alone in Visual Analog Scale (SMD = -0.596, 95% CI -0.990 to -0.202) and range of motion (SMD = 0.716, 95% CI 0.138 to 1.294), but showed no significant difference in International Knee Documentation Committee (MD = 1.931, 95% CI -3.663 to 7.526, P = 0.499). Network meta-analysis revealed that neuromuscular electrical stimulation significantly improved Visual Analog Scale (SMD = -0.837, 95% CI -1.420 to -0.254) and range of motion (SMD = 1.274, 95% CI 0.927 to 1.622) compared with rehabilitation alone, and was superior to transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation in range of motion (SMD = -1.185, 95% CI -1.717 to -0.653). The surface under the cumulative ranking curve results indicated that neuromuscular electrical stimulation ranked first for Visual Analog Scale (92.5%) and range of motion (99.9%). No significant difference was found compared with rehabilitation alone for International Knee Documentation Committee scores (P > 0.05).ConclusionElectrical stimulation combined with rehabilitation training can effectively improve Visual Analog Scale and range of motion after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction, with neuromuscular electrical stimulation demonstrating the optimal effect, although its improvement on International Knee Documentation Committee was limited. Based on the current evidence, rehabilitation training combined with neuromuscular electrical stimulation is recommended as an adjunctive treatment protocol.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-026-10069-8.

Introduction

Anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) injury exhibits a high incidence rate among athletes [14]. Its occurrence is closely associated with biomechanical factors, neuromuscular control deficits, and high-risk athletic movements (e.g., abrupt deceleration and change of direction), with approximately 2 million new cases reported annually worldwide [12]. The ACL serves as a critical structure for maintaining knee stability and plays an important role in preventing anterior tibial translation and internal rotation relative to the femur [5]. Following ACL injury, knee instability frequently develops,if left untreated or inadequately managed, this condition may lead to further damage to the meniscus and articular cartilage, and even result in complications such as patellofemoral pain and knee osteoarthritis [9].

Currently, clinical treatment for ACL injuries often involves anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction (ACLR), which restores knee joint stability by fixing a graft in the same position as the ACL [19]. However, patients who undergo ACLR frequently experience decreased quadriceps strength and function, often accompanied by pain, resulting in delayed recovery of knee function [3]. Among athletes who return to sport following ACLR, the ipsilateral graft re-rupture rate can reach 7%, while the total re-injury rate (including both ipsilateral and contralateral ACL injuries) among athletes under 25 years of age is as high as 23% [10]. Therefore, the exploration of effective and safe adjunctive rehabilitation interventions to accelerate lower limb functional recovery, alleviate pain, and reduce re-injury risk following ACLR has become a critical issue that urgently requires resolution in the field of sports rehabilitation.

Electrical stimulation (ES) has been widely applied as an adjunctive rehabilitation modality in postoperative recovery following ACLR. Currently, the primary electrical stimulation protocols include neuromuscular electrical stimulation (NMES) and transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS). These different types of electrical stimulation exhibit significant differences in target sites, stimulation parameters, and underlying mechanisms: NMES acts on skeletal muscle to elicit contraction and attenuate muscle atrophy, whereas TENS primarily activates sensory neural pathways to achieve analgesia. Although previous studies have individually demonstrated certain rehabilitative effects of various electrical stimulation modalities on patients following ACLR, existing research has merely compared a single electrical stimulation protocol combined with rehabilitation training versus rehabilitation training alone. To date, no network meta-analysis has directly evaluated the relative efficacy of NMES and TENS in terms of pain (VAS), range of motion (ROM), and subjective knee function (IKDC) following anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction.

Therefore, this study aims to systematically compare and rank the therapeutic effects of NMES combined with rehabilitation training, TENS combined with rehabilitation training, and rehabilitation training alone on pain, range of motion, and subjective knee function following ACLR through a systematic review and network meta-analysis, thereby providing evidence-based guidance for clinical rehabilitation decision-making. This study hypothesizes that, compared with TENS, NMES may demonstrate superior efficacy in improving range of motion because it can directly induce tetanic muscle contraction; in terms of pain relief, TENS may exhibit greater advantages over NMES due to its specific activation of sensory nerves and modulation of analgesic pathways; and no significant difference may exist between the two electrical stimulation modalities regarding comprehensive subjective knee function (IKDC).

Materials and methods

Protocol and registration

This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The study protocol was prospectively registered at the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (registration number: CRD420261364272).

Search strategy

Two researchers independently conducted a systematic literature search of Chinese and English databases from inception to March 2, 2026. The following electronic databases were searched: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI). The English search strategy was developed based on Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms and free-text words derived from PubMed. Using Web of Science as an illustrative example, the search strategy was formulated as follows: TS = ("anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction" OR "ACL reconstruction" OR "anterior cruciate ligament surgery") AND TS = ("electric stimulation" OR "neuromuscular electrical stimulation" OR "transcranial direct current stimulation" OR "percutaneous electrical stimulation") AND TS = ("lower limb function" OR "range of motion" OR "visual analog scale" OR "VAS" OR "knee score" OR "International Knee Documentation Committee" OR "IKDC" OR "muscle strength").

The Chinese search strategy was correspondingly developed as follows: SU = (前交叉韧带重建 + 前交叉韧带手术 + ACL重建) AND SU = (电刺激 + 神经肌肉电刺激 + 经颅直流电刺激 + 经皮电刺激) AND SU = (下肢功能 + 关节活动度 + 活动范围 + 视觉模拟评分 + VAS + 膝关节评分 + 国际膝关节文献委员会 + IKDC + 肌力).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) Participants underwent ACLR surgery; (2) The intervention group received a rehabilitation protocol combined with any form of electrical stimulation, whereas the control group received sham electrical stimulation combined with rehabilitation training (i.e., electrode placement without electrical current or sub-sensory threshold microcurrent to control for placebo effects) or rehabilitation training alone; (3) Randomized controlled trials; (4) Outcome measures: VAS score, ROM score, and IKDC score.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Animal studies; (2) Conference abstracts; (3) Full text unavailable; (4) Articles with no extractable data; (5) Duplicate publications.

Data extraction methods

During the literature screening and data extraction phase, two reviewers collaboratively completed deduplication, screening, and data extraction of the retrieved records. First, duplicate records were removed. Subsequently, both reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts to preliminarily exclude studies that were clearly irrelevant, did not meet the inclusion criteria, had unavailable full texts, or lacked valid data. For articles of uncertain eligibility, full-text review was conducted to determine inclusion. When disagreements arose between the two reviewers and consensus could not be reached through discussion, a third reviewer was invited to adjudicate until agreement was achieved.

Data extracted from the included studies were organized in Microsoft Excel and categorized as follows: (1) Basic information: first author, publication year, sample size, sex, and age; (2) Intervention details: intervention frequency (sessions per week), intervention duration (total weeks), type of electrical stimulation (neuromuscular electrical stimulation or transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation), and other relevant parameters; (3) Outcome measures: pain assessment (VAS), range of motion, subjective knee function score (IKDC), and other relevant indicators; (4) Other information: time points of outcome measurement (pre-intervention and post-intervention).

Reviewers classified and organized the extracted data for network meta-analysis according to the types of outcome measures (pain-related, range of motion-related, functional scale-related), the modality of electrical stimulation combined with exercise (rehabilitation training combined with neuromuscular electrical stimulation group, rehabilitation training combined with transcutaneous electrical stimulation group, rehabilitation training alone group), and intervention duration.

Literature quality assessment

The literature quality assessment was conducted using a methodological quality evaluation checklist adapted from the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, which has been recommended in the Cochrane methodology guidelines for updating systematic reviews. The assessment was independently completed by two evaluators through blinded evaluation, and each item was assessed as "Yes," "No," or "Unclear," representing low risk of bias, high risk of bias, and unclear risk of bias, respectively. The evaluation mainly included the following domains: (1) random allocation method; (2) baseline comparability; (3) allocation concealment; (4) blinding of patients, therapists, and evaluators; and (5) completeness of data reporting.

Data analysis

All outcome measures in this study were continuous variables. According to the consistency of measurement units across the included studies, different effect indicators were used for pooled analysis: when units were consistent, the mean difference (MD) and its 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were used as effect indicators; when units were inconsistent, the standardized mean difference (SMD) and its 95% CI were used as the pooled effect size. Heterogeneity was quantitatively assessed using P values and I2 statistics. A random-effects model was adopted when substantial heterogeneity was detected among studies (I2 ≥ 50%, P < 0.10), whereas a fixed-effects model was used when heterogeneity was low (I2 < 50%, P ≥ 0.10). Subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses were conducted to explore potential sources of heterogeneity. If the source of heterogeneity could not be identified, only qualitative descriptive analysis was performed.

To explore the sources of heterogeneity, this study pre-specified the following subgroup and meta-regression analyses based on clinical relevance: (1) Subgroup analysis: intervention duration (≤ 8 weeks vs. > 8 weeks). The median duration of electrical stimulation interventions among the included studies was approximately 8 weeks. Moreover, several studies on electrical stimulation after ACLR, such as Han [13] and Zheng [21], adopted 8 weeks as the standard intervention period. Therefore, 8 weeks was selected as the cut-off to distinguish between short-term and mid-to-long-term electrical stimulation protocols. (2) Meta-regression analysis: electrical stimulation frequency (Hz). Considering that frequency is a continuous variable, this study performed a meta-regression analysis on electrical stimulation frequency to further quantitatively assess its linear moderating effect on the effect sizes and to explain the sources of heterogeneity.

This study performed the network meta-analysis using R software (version 4.3.0), primarily implemented through the netmeta and BUGSnet packages. The evidence network in this study exhibited a star-shaped structure without closed loops; therefore, the transitivity assumption could not be verified through inconsistency testing. Two reviewers independently examined the baseline data of each intervention node, with a particular focus on comparing participant age (NMES group: 25.7–44.1 years; TENS group: 26.0–31.6 years), sex composition, and intervention duration (NMES group: median 8 weeks; TENS group: 4–6 weeks). The assessment indicated that the clinical characteristics across nodes were generally comparable, the network model essentially satisfied the transitivity assumption, and indirect comparisons were methodologically feasible. The surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) was generated to rank the intervention effects of different modalities. A SUCRA value of 1 indicates the optimal intervention effect, whereas a SUCRA value of 0 indicates the poorest intervention effect. The orchaRd package was used to generate an orchard plot to present the comparison results between each study and the reference group: colored dots in the plot represent the point estimates of the effect size for individual studies, with dot size proportional to study precision (precision = 1/SE2); thick black solid lines represent the 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) of the effect size for each study; and thin black dashed lines represent the 95% prediction intervals, reflecting the possible range of effect size variation attributable to between-study heterogeneity.

Literature quality assessment

Most studies reported appropriate random sequence generation methods (e.g., random number tables, random number generators, and block randomization) and were rated as low risk. However, two studies [2, 7] employed inadequate or unclear randomization methods. Only three studies [3, 6, 8]), explicitly described allocation concealment methods (e.g., sealed opaque envelopes or allocation by a statistician not involved in recruitment) and were rated as low risk. The remaining studies did not clearly describe allocation concealment methods and were consequently rated as unclear risk. None of the 11 studies implemented blinding of both participants and therapists simultaneously, therefore, performance bias was rated as high risk across all studies. The primary reason was that electrical stimulation interventions (NMES and TENS) constitute active interventions requiring electrode placement and producing muscle contraction or sensory sensations, thereby rendering participant blinding unfeasible. Regarding blinding of outcome assessors, Ediz et al. [6] was rated as high risk because the outcomes were assessed by the same unblinded physician. Meta-analysis results

Conventional meta-analysis

A total of 7 studies [3, 6, 7, 8], [11, 15, 20] were included in this analysis, comprising 172 patients in the electrical stimulation group and 172 patients in the control group. The results of the meta-analysis indicated significant heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 66.1%, P = 0.007),therefore, a random-effects model was adopted. The combination of electrical stimulation with rehabilitation training was significantly more effective in reducing VAS scores than rehabilitation training alone (SMD = −0.596, 95% CI: −0.990 to −0.202, Z = −2.97, P = 0.003) See Fig. 3.Fig. 3Effect of electrical stimulation versus control on VAS

A total of 6 studies [3, 8], [1, 11, 20, 21] were included in this analysis, comprising 200 patients in the electrical stimulation group and 200 patients in the control group. For the VAS outcome, the subgroup with intervention duration ≤ 8 weeks showed no statistically significant effect (SMD = −0.249, P = 0.591, I2 = 70.3%), whereas the > 8 weeks subgroup demonstrated a significant analgesic effect (SMD = −0.714, P = 0.003, I2 = 70.2%). Funnel plots combined with Egger's regression test were employed to assess publication bias for the three outcome measures separately. The funnel plots displayed some asymmetry in the distribution of scattered points across the indicators. Discussion

This study systematically evaluated the intervention effects of different electrical stimulation modalities (NMES and TENS) combined with rehabilitation training on pain (VAS), range of motion (ROM), and subjective function (IKDC) in patients following anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction through both traditional meta-analysis and network meta-analysis. The results of the traditional meta-analysis demonstrated that electrical stimulation overall significantly alleviated pain (SMD = −0.596, 95% CI: −0.990 to −0.202, P = 0.003) and improved range of motion (SMD = 0.716, 95% CI: 0.138 to 1.294, P = 0.015); however, the effect on improving IKDC scores was not significant (MD = 1.931, 95% CI: −3.663 to 7.526, P = 0.499).

Electrical stimulation is widely recognized to possess significant analgesic effects. It reduces pain by activating inhibitory mechanisms and decreasing central nervous system excitability [17, 18]. In terms of pain relief (VAS), the traditional meta-analysis demonstrated that electrical stimulation overall was significantly superior to the control group (SMD = −0.596, 95% CI: −0.990 to −0.202, P = 0.003), which is consistent with previous research findings [16]. Furthermore, the results of the network meta-analysis indicated that rehabilitation training combined with NMES exhibited significantly superior analgesic effects compared with rehabilitation training alone (SMD = −0.837, 95% CI: −1.420 to −0.254, P = 0.005), whereas no significant difference was observed between rehabilitation training combined with TENS and rehabilitation training alone (SMD = −0.417, 95% CI: −0.944 to 0.110, P = 0.121). Direct comparison between TENS and NMES revealed comparable effects on VAS (SMD = 0.420, 95% CI: −0.360 to 1.200, P = 0.291). The surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) results demonstrated that rehabilitation combined with NMES yielded the optimal treatment effect (SUCRA = 92.5%), followed by rehabilitation combined with TENS (SUCRA = 54.3%), with rehabilitation training alone showing the lowest efficacy (SUCRA = 3.1%). TENS did not demonstrate superior efficacy compared with NMES, which is inconsistent with the study hypothesis. This finding may be attributed to the fact that NMES directly acts on skeletal muscle to enhance muscular strength, thereby conferring greater advantages for patients with pain accompanied by muscular dysfunction.

Electrical stimulation enhances muscle strength by preferentially activating type II motor units through externally applied electrical signals [16] and improves neuromuscular recruitment and coordination [4], thereby facilitating the recovery of range of motion. In terms of range of motion (ROM), the traditional meta-analysis demonstrated that electrical stimulation overall was significantly superior to the control group (SMD = 0.716, 95% CI: 0.138 to 1.294, P = 0.015). The results of the network meta-analysis indicated that rehabilitation training combined with NMES significantly improved range of motion compared with rehabilitation training alone (SMD = 1.274, 95% CI: 0.927 to 1.622, P < 0.001), whereas no significant difference was observed between rehabilitation training combined with TENS and rehabilitation training alone (SMD = 0.090, 95% CI: −0.291 to 0.470, P = 0.644). Furthermore, the effect of rehabilitation combined with TENS was significantly inferior to that of rehabilitation combined with NMES (SMD = −1.185, 95% CI: −1.717 to −0.653, P < 0.001). The results of the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) analysis showed that rehabilitation training combined with NMES ranked first (SUCRA = 99.9%), followed by rehabilitation training combined with TENS (SUCRA = 33.9%), while rehabilitation training alone ranked the lowest (SUCRA = 16.1%). Therefore, NMES demonstrates a significant advantage in improving ROM, which is consistent with the research hypothesis of this study.

Subjective knee function was assessed using the International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC) subjective knee evaluation form, which encompasses multiple dimensions including activities of daily living, sports activity capabilities, and self-perceived knee function status, thereby reflecting patients' comprehensive evaluation of their knee recovery. This instrument represents a combined subjective-feeling and functional indicator. In terms of the International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC) score, the traditional meta-analysis demonstrated no significant difference between electrical stimulation overall and the control group (MD = 1.931, 95% CI: −3.663 to 7.526, P = 0.499). The results of the network meta-analysis indicated that neither rehabilitation training combined with NMES (MD = 1.948, 95% CI: −9.430 to 13.326, P = 0.737) nor rehabilitation training combined with TENS (MD = 1.756, 95% CI: −8.822 to 12.333, P = 0.745) showed significant differences compared with rehabilitation training alone, which is consistent with the research hypothesis of this study. The results of the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) analysis showed that rehabilitation training combined with NMES had the optimal effect (SUCRA = 57.1%), followed by rehabilitation training combined with TENS (SUCRA = 55.9%), while rehabilitation training alone had the lowest effect (SUCRA = 37.1%). These findings suggest that although electrical stimulation demonstrates favorable effects on pain relief and range of motion improvement, it does not produce significant effects on combined subjective-function indicators. This implies that electrical stimulation, as an adjunctive modality, primarily acts on fundamental aspects such as pain control and range of motion recovery, but remains insufficient for the multidimensional functional reserves required for patients to return to high-level sports, including dynamic stability, muscular coordination capabilities, sports confidence, and daily life integration abilities. Therefore, relying solely on electrical stimulation combined with conventional rehabilitation training may not fully meet the needs of patients returning to high-level sports or complex daily activities following anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction. Clinical rehabilitation should further integrate comprehensive intervention strategies including targeted functional training, psychological support, and sport-specific training.

Based on the aforementioned results, rehabilitation training combined with NMES can be considered the preferred adjunctive electrical stimulation modality in postoperative rehabilitation following anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction. However, this study has several limitations. First, the number of included studies was limited (n = 11), and most had small sample sizes. Second, significant heterogeneity was observed across all outcome measures (VAS: I2 = 66.1%; ROM: I2 = 85.7%; IKDC: I2 = 86.2%), with particularly high heterogeneity in some subgroups (ROM > 8 weeks subgroup: I2 = 94.6%), suggesting that the results should be interpreted with caution. Third, due to the inherent characteristics of electrical stimulation interventions, blinding of participants was not feasible in any of the included studies, which may amplify the placebo effect on subjective outcomes (VAS and IKDC) and introduce expectancy bias. Fourth, there is a lack of direct head-to-head comparative evidence between NMES and TENS, and the core conclusions rely on indirect comparisons. Fifth, regarding publication bias, although Egger's test was not significant (P > 0.05), the funnel plot displayed some asymmetry, suggesting a potential risk of bias given the limited number of included studies. Sixth, differences in electrical stimulation parameters and measurement time points were observed across studies; however, more detailed subgroup analyses could not be performed due to limited data. Finally, language bias may exist, as this study only included Chinese and English databases and did not search databases in other languages, potentially excluding relevant studies published in other languages and thereby affecting the completeness and generalizability of the findings.

Conclusion

This systematic review and network meta-analysis indicates that electrical stimulation combined with rehabilitation training can effectively alleviate pain and improve range of motion in patients following anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction; however, it does not demonstrate a significant effect on subjective knee function (IKDC). Among the various electrical stimulation modalities, neuromuscular electrical stimulation (NMES) is superior to rehabilitation training alone in both pain relief and range of motion improvement, and exhibits a trend toward superiority over transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) in improving range of motion. Nevertheless, this study has limitations, including a limited number of included studies and a lack of direct comparative evidence between NMES and TENS. Therefore, based on the current indirect evidence, this study offers a cautious recommendation: rehabilitation training combined with NMES can be considered one of the alternative adjunctive modalities for improving pain and range of motion following anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction. Future high-quality randomized controlled trials directly comparing the efficacy of NMES and TENS are warranted to provide more robust evidence.
