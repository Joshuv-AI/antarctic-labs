Bollobás–Meir TSP Conjecture Holds Asymptotically
Alexey Gordeev

Abstract
In 1992, Bollobás and Meir showed that for every k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 there exists a constant c k c_{k} such that, for any n n points in the k k -dimensional unit cube [ 0 , 1 ] k [0,1]^{k} , one can find a tour x 1 , … , x n x_{1},\dots,x_{n} through these n n points with ∑ i = 1 n | x i − x i + 1 | k ≤ c k \sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-x_{i+1}|^{k}\leq c_{k} , where x n + 1 = x 1 x_{n+1}=x_{1} and | x − y | |x-y| is the Euclidean distance between x x and y y . Remarkably, this bound does not depend on n n , the number of points. They conjectured that the optimal constant is c k = 2 ⋅ k k / 2 c_{k}=2\cdot k^{k/2} and showed that it cannot be taken lower than that. This conjecture was recently revised for k = 3 k=3 by Balogh, Clemen and Dumitrescu, who showed that c 3 ≥ 2 7 / 2 > 2 ⋅ 3 3 / 2 c_{3}\geq 2^{7/2}>2\cdot 3^{3/2} . It remains open for all k > 2 k>2 , with the best known upper bound c k ≤ 2.65 k ⋅ k k / 2 ⋅ ( 1 + o k ​ ( 1 ) ) c_{k}\leq 2.65^{k}\cdot k^{k/2}\cdot(1+o_{k}(1)) . We significantly narrow the gap between lower and upper bounds on c k c_{k} , reducing it from exponential to linear. Specifically, we prove that c k ≤ 2 ​ e ​ ( k + 1 ) ⋅ k k / 2 c_{k}\leq 2\mathrm{e}(k+1)\cdot k^{k/2} and c k = k k / 2 ⋅ ( 2 + o k ​ ( 1 ) ) c_{k}=k^{k/2}\cdot(2+o_{k}(1)) , the latter establishing the conjecture asymptotically. We also obtain analogous results for related problems on Hamiltonian paths, spanning trees and perfect matchings in the unit cube. Our main tool is a new generalization of the ball packing argument used in earlier works.

1.1
New results

The main contribution of this work is a new generalization of the ball packing argument.
The key idea is to allow balls to intersect in a controlled manner instead of requiring them to be disjoint.
This not only leads to improved bounds but, more importantly, allows for direct application to Hamiltonian cycles, eliminating the need for approximation.
As a result, we can show that the
Conjecture 1.6

(Bollobás–Meir conjecture)
.
holds asymptotically.

Theorem 1.7
.

For any finite set
X\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
, there exists a Hamiltonian cycle
H
on
X
such that, if
e_{1},e_{2}\in H
are two longest edges of
H
, then

\sum_{e\in H\setminus\{e_{1},e_{2}\}}|e|^{k}=o_{k}(k^{k/2}),\quad\text{so}\quad S_{k}(H)=\sum_{e\in H}|e|^{k}\leq k^{k/2}\cdot(2+o_{k}(1)).

In particular,

S_{k}^{\mathrm{HC}}=k^{k/2}\cdot(2+o_{k}(1)).

Theorem
1.7
is a direct corollary of the following result for Hamiltonian paths, which asserts that Conjecture 5.6 in
[
2
]
, the analogue of the
Conjecture 1.6

(Bollobás–Meir conjecture)
.
for Hamiltonian paths, also holds asymptotically.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any finite set
X\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
, there exists a Hamiltonian path
P
on
X
such that, if
e_{1}\in P
is the longest edge of
P
, then

\sum_{e\in P\setminus\{e_{1}\}}|e|^{k}=o_{k}(k^{k/2}),\quad\text{so}\quad S_{k}(P)=\sum_{e\in P}|e|^{k}\leq k^{k/2}\cdot(1+o_{k}(1)).

In particular,

S_{k}^{\mathrm{HP}}=k^{k/2}\cdot(1+o_{k}(1)).

Proof of Theorem
1.7
using Theorem
1.8
.

For a finite set
X\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
, let the Hamiltonian path
P
from Theorem
1.8
have endpoints
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in X
.
Then
H:=P\cup\{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}\}
is the desired Hamiltonian cycle.
∎

Informally speaking, Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
imply that for large

the points of an extremal set

S_{k}^{\mathrm{HC}}(X)=k^{k/2}\cdot(2+o_{k}(1))

Turning to exact bounds, we prove the following theorem, bringing the upper bound on

Theorem 1.9

For any finite set

Denote the open ball of radius

\B_{r}(\mathbf{a}):=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\,:\,|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}|<r\}

For an edge

with

, define

\mathbf{m}_{e}:=\frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}{2},\quad\B_{\alpha}^{e}:=\B_{\alpha|e|}(\mathbf{m}_{e}),\quad\hB_{\alpha}^{e}:=\begin{cases}\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\B_{\alpha}^{e}\,:\,\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{m}_{e}\rangle<|\mathbf{m}_{e}|^{2}\right\}&\text{if }\mathbf{m}_{e}\neq\mathbf{0},\\
\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\B_{\alpha}^{e}\,:\,x_{1}<0\right\}&\text{if }\mathbf{m}_{e}=\mathbf{0},\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{0}=(0,\dots,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
is the origin.
In other words,
\mathbf{m}_{e}
is the midpoint of
e
,
\B_{\alpha}^{e}
is the open ball of radius
\alpha|e|
centered at
\mathbf{m}_{e}
, and
\hB_{\alpha}^{e}
is the open half-ball with the same center and radius oriented towards the origin, or consisting of points with negative first coordinate when
\mathbf{m}_{e}
itself is the origin.

The next definition follows the Handbook of Discrete and Computational Geometry
[
15
, Section 2.3]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
t
be a positive integer.
A family
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of sets

, each of finite volume, is a
t

if each point of
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
belongs to the interior of at most
t
sets in
\mathcal{A}
.
A
packing
is a 1-fold packing.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
be a finite set.
For a set
E
of edges on
X
, let
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{E}:=\{\B_{\alpha}^{e}\}_{e\in E}
and
\mathfrak{hB}_{\alpha}^{E}:=\{\hB_{\alpha}^{e}\}_{e\in E}
.
The next two lemmas provide an upper bound on
S_{k}(E)
when it is known that
\mathfrak{hB}_{\alpha}^{E}
is a
t
-fold packing for some
\alpha
and
t
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
e=\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
be an edge with
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and
|\mathbf{a}|,|\mathbf{b}|\leq r
.
Then
\hB_{\alpha}^{e}\subseteq\B_{r}(\mathbf{0})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\hB_{\alpha}^{e}
.
If
\mathbf{m}_{e}=\mathbf{0}
, then
|\mathbf{x}|<\alpha|e|\leq\frac{|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}|}{2}\leq\frac{|\mathbf{a}|+|\mathbf{b}|}{2}\leq r
.
Otherwise,
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{m}_{e}+\mathbf{z}
for some
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
with
|\mathbf{z}|<\alpha|e|
and
\langle\mathbf{m}_{e}+\mathbf{z},\mathbf{m}_{e}\rangle<\langle\mathbf{m}_{e},\mathbf{m}_{e}\rangle
, i.e.
\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{m}_{e}\rangle<0
.
It follows that

|\mathbf{x}|^{2}=|\mathbf{m}_{e}+\mathbf{z}|^{2}=|\mathbf{m}_{e}|^{2}+|\mathbf{z}|^{2}+2\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{m}_{e}\rangle<|\mathbf{m}_{e}|^{2}+|\mathbf{z}|^{2},

so

|\mathbf{x}|<\sqrt{|\mathbf{m}_{e}|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|e|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\left|\frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}}{2}\right|^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{|\mathbf{a}|^{2}+|\mathbf{b}|^{2}}{2}}\leq r.\qed

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X\subseteq\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{k}
be a finite set and
E
be a set of edges on
X
.
If
\mathfrak{hB}_{\alpha}^{E}
is a
t
-fold packing for some
0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
and a positive integer

, then

S_{k}(E)\leq\frac{2t}{(2\alpha)^{k}}\cdot k^{k/2}.

(3)

Proof.

Note that
|\mathbf{a}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2}
for all
\mathbf{a}\in X
, thus, by Lemma
2.2
,
\hB_{\alpha}^{e}\subseteq\B_{\sqrt{k}/2}(\mathbf{0})
for each edge
e\in E
.
Since
\mathfrak{hB}_{\alpha}^{E}
is a
t
-fold packing, the sum of volumes of half-balls
\hB_{\alpha}^{e}
is at most
t
times the volume of
\B_{\sqrt{k}/2}(\mathbf{0})
.
Denoting by

the volume of the

\sum_{e\in E}\frac{V_{k}}{2}\alpha^{k}|e|^{k}\leq t\cdot V_{k}\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2}\right)^{k},\quad\text{so}\quad S_{k}(E)\leq\frac{2t}{(2\alpha)^{k}}\cdot k^{k/2}.\qed

Remark 2.4
.

Note that if
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{E}
is a
t
-fold packing, then so is

\hB_{\alpha}^{e}\subseteq\B_{\alpha}^{e}

In all applications of Lemma

The idea of using half-balls was introduced by the present author in

]
.
Earlier works applied the volume bound directly to the collection of balls

, which produced exponentially weaker bounds on

In the same case, Lemma

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

.
We already have the first ingredient, Lemma
2.3
.
To complete the proof, for any point set
X\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
of even size, it would suffice to find a perfect matching
M
on
X
such that
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{M}
is a
t
-fold packing for some appropriate
t
and
\alpha
.

Restricting ourselves for a moment to the case
t=1
, note that
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{M}
is a packing if and only if for any
e=\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b},f=\mathbf{c}\mathbf{d}\in M
, the inequality
|\mathbf{m}_{e}-\mathbf{m}_{f}|\geq\alpha(|e|+|f|)
holds.
We can express the distance
|\mathbf{m}_{e}-\mathbf{m}_{f}|
in terms of distances between points
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}
using the following classical formula for the length of a bimedian of a tetrahedron (see, for example, the textbook of Altshiller-Court
[
1

).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
,

\left|\frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}}{2}-\frac{\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{d}}{2}\right|^{2}=\frac{|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{c}|^{2}+|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{d}|^{2}+|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}|^{2}+|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}|^{2}-|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}|^{2}-|\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{4}.

Proof.

Both sides are equal to

\frac{|\mathbf{a}|^{2}+|\mathbf{b}|^{2}+|\mathbf{c}|^{2}+|\mathbf{d}|^{2}}{4}+\frac{\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{b},\mathbf{d}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\rangle}{2}.\qed

The following key lemma for perfect matchings easily follows from this formula.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
be a set of even size, and let
M
be a perfect matching on
X
with minimal possible
S_{2}(M)
.
Then, for any
e,f\in M
,

|\mathbf{m}_{e}-\mathbf{m}_{f}|^{2}\geq\frac{|e|^{2}+|f|^{2}}{4}.

Proof.

Denote
e=\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
,
f=\mathbf{c}\mathbf{d}
,
M_{1}:=(M\setminus\{e,f\})\cup\{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{c},\mathbf{b}\mathbf{d}\}
,
M_{2}:=(M\setminus\{e,f\})\cup\{\mathbf{a}\mathbf{d},\mathbf{b}\mathbf{c}\}
.
Note that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are also perfect matchings on
X
, thus
S_{2}(M_{1})\geq S_{2}(M)
and
S_{2}(M_{2})\geq S_{2}(M)
, or

|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{c}|^{2}+|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{d}|^{2}\geq|e|^{2}+|f|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}|^{2}+|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}|^{2}\geq|e|^{2}+|f|^{2}.

Then, by Lemma
3.1
,

|\mathbf{m}_{e}-\mathbf{m}_{f}|^{2}=\frac{|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{c}|^{2}+|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{d}|^{2}+|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{d}|^{2}+|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}|^{2}-|e|^{2}-|f|^{2}}{4}\geq\frac{|e|^{2}+|f|^{2}}{4}.\qed

Combining Lemma
3.2
with the inequality

\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{M}
is a packing for
\alpha=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}
and any
S_{2}
-minimal perfect matching
M
on
X
.
Together with Lemma

, this implies

, see
[

.
However, we can get a much stronger bound by considering

-fold packings with

Namely, we will show that, under the same assumptions as in Lemma

-fold packing, using properties of

We will also investigate an alternative approach in Section

Let

\mathrm{S}_{k}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\,:\,|\mathbf{x}|=1\}

Lemma 3.3

.

A

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\mathbf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
.
For any
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i<j}|\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{a}_{j}|^{2},

with equality only for
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{m}
, where
\mathbf{m}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{a}_{i}
is the centroid of points
\mathbf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{n}
.

Proof.

Note that

|\mathbf{m}|^{2}=\langle\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|\mathbf{a}_{i}|^{2}}{n^{2}}+\sum_{i<j}\frac{2\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},\mathbf{a}_{j}\rangle}{n^{2}}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i<j}|\mathbf{a}_{i}-\mathbf{a}_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle=n|\mathbf{x}|^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},\mathbf{x}\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbf{a}_{i}|^{2}-\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbf{a}_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i<j}\frac{2\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},\mathbf{a}_{j}\rangle}{n}

\displaystyle=n|\mathbf{x}|^{2}-2n\langle\mathbf{m},\mathbf{x}\rangle+n|\mathbf{m}|^{2}=n|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}\geq 0,

with equality only when
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{m}
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
be a finite set,
H
be a Hamiltonian cycle on

with minimal possible
S_{2}(H)
,
t\geq 1
be a positive integer and
\alpha:=\sqrt{\frac{t}{4(t+1)}}
.
Then
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{H}
is a
(2t+1)
-fold packing.

Proof.

Fix any point
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and let it be contained in
2n
balls
\B_{\alpha}^{e_{1}},\B_{\alpha}^{f_{1}},\dots,\B_{\alpha}^{e_{n}},\B_{\alpha}^{f_{n}}
, with the edges appearing on the cycle in the order
e_{1},f_{1},\dots,e_{n},f_{n}\in H
.
We have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|\mathbf{m}_{e_{i}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{f_{i}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}\right)<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha^{2}\left(|e_{i}|^{2}+|f_{i}|^{2}\right)=\frac{t}{4(t+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|e_{i}|^{2}+|f_{i}|^{2}\right).

On the other hand, applying Lemma
4.2
two times for the sets of points
\{\mathbf{m}_{e_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{\mathbf{m}_{f_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, and then Lemma
4.1
for edges
e_{i},f_{i},e_{j},f_{j}
for all
i<j
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|\mathbf{m}_{e_{i}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{f_{i}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i<j}\frac{|\mathbf{m}_{e_{i}}-\mathbf{m}_{e_{j}}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{f_{i}}-\mathbf{m}_{f_{j}}|^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i<j}\frac{|e_{i}|^{2}+|e_{j}|^{2}+|f_{i}|^{2}+|f_{j}|^{2}}{4n}=\frac{n-1}{4n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|e_{i}|^{2}+|f_{i}|^{2}\right).

It follows that
\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4n}=\frac{n-1}{4n}<\frac{t}{4(t+1)}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4(t+1)}
, which implies
n<t+1
, i.e. no point is contained in
2(t+1)
balls of
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{H}
.

1.8

,

(4)

On the other hand, for any two endpoints

i_{0}
again,

|\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}|^{2}\geq S_{2}(E_{i_{0}+1})-S_{2}(E_{i_{0}})>\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\quad\text{ for any two endpoints }\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in X\text{ of different paths in }E_{i_{0}}.

(5)

Set

\ell:=\left\lceil\log_{1+\frac{1}{k}}\left(0.9k^{\frac{3}{4}}\right)\right\rceil=O(k\log k),\quad a_{i}:=\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{i}}{k^{\frac{3}{4}}}

for integers
0\leq i\leq\ell
.
Note that
a_{i}<0.9
for
i<\ell
, and
a_{\ell}\geq 0.9
.

Construct a Hamiltonian path
P
by starting from
E_{i_{0}}
and successively adding the shortest edge connecting endpoints of two different paths.
For
0\leq i\leq\ell
, let
P_{i}\subseteq P
be the path family consisting of all edges
e\in P
with
|e|\leq a_{i}\sqrt{k}
.
Then
P_{0}\subseteq P_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq P_{\ell}\subseteq P
, and for any two endpoints
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in X
of different paths in
P_{i}
, we have
|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|>a_{i}\sqrt{k}
.

Due to (
4
) and (
5
), we have
P_{0}=E_{i_{0}}
, so
P_{0}
is
S_{2}
-minimal among all path families of size
i_{0}
.
Then, by Proposition
5.4
,
\mathfrak{B}_{1/\sqrt{8}}^{P_{0}}
is a 3-fold packing.
It is well-known that the volume
V_{k}
of the
k
-dimensional unit ball is

V_{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{\pi^{k/2}}{(k/2)!}&\text{if }k\text{ is even},\\
\frac{2^{k}\cdot\pi^{(k-1)/2}((k-1)/2)!}{k!}&\text{if }k\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

By Stirling’s approximation,
V_{k}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{k\pi}}(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{e}}{k})^{k/2}
.
Since
|e|\leq a_{0}\sqrt{k}=k^{-1/4}
for any
e\in P_{0}
, we have
\bigcup_{e\in P_{0}}\B_{1/\sqrt{8}}^{e}\subseteq\left[-k^{-1/4},1+k^{-1/4}\right]
, so

\sum_{e\in P_{0}}V_{k}\cdot\left(\frac{|e|}{\sqrt{8}}\right)^{k}\leq 3\cdot\left(1+2k^{-1/4}\right)^{k},\quad\text{thus}\quad\sum_{e\in P_{0}}|e|^{k}\leq\frac{3\cdot 8^{k/2}\cdot\left(1+2k^{-1/4}\right)^{k}}{V_{k}}\leq 0.69^{k}\cdot k^{k/2},

for sufficiently large
k
.

Now, for
0\leq i<\ell
, let
X_{i}
be a set containing one endpoint of each path in the path family
P_{i}
, so
|X_{i}|=|X|-|P_{i}|
.
Then
|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|>a_{i}\sqrt{k}
for any distinct
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in X_{i}
, and
|P_{i+1}\setminus P_{i}|\leq|P|-|P_{i}|=(|X|-1)-(|X|-|X_{i}|)<|X_{i}|
.
By Lemma
5.2
, we have
|X_{i}|\cdot a_{i}^{k}\leq 0.999^{k}
.
Then

\sum_{e\in P_{i+1}\setminus P_{i}}|e|^{k}<|X_{i}|\cdot\left(a_{i+1}\sqrt{k}\right)^{k}=|X_{i}|\cdot\left(a_{i}\sqrt{k}\right)^{k}\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)^{k}\leq\mathrm{e}\cdot 0.999^{k}\cdot k^{k/2},

where
\mathrm{e}=2.718..
is Euler’s number.
Thus

\sum_{e\in P_{\ell}}|e|^{k}=\sum_{e\in P_{0}}|e|^{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\sum_{e\in P_{i+1}\setminus P_{i}}|e|^{k}<0.69^{k}\cdot k^{k/2}+\mathrm{e}\ell\cdot 0.999^{k}\cdot k^{k/2}=o_{k}(k^{k/2})

for sufficiently large
k
since
\ell=O(k\log k)
.

Since for any two endpoints
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
of different paths in
P_{\ell}
we have
|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|>a_{\ell}\sqrt{k}\geq 0.9\sqrt{k}>\sqrt{\frac{2k}{3}}
,
by Lemma
5.1
,
P_{\ell}
consists of at most two paths, i.e.
|P\setminus P_{\ell}|\leq 1
, so
P
is the required Hamiltonian path.
∎

6
Concluding remarks

Theorems
1.7
and
1.9
are non-constructive and do not give a computationally efficient way of finding the Hamiltonian cycle in question.
To obtain a constructive result, we can combine these theorems with an approximation algorithm.

A complete graph on a vertex set
X
with edge weights
w(a,b)
,
a,b\in X
, satisfies
the
\tau
-relaxed triangle inequality
for
\tau\geq 1
if
w(a,c)\leq\tau\cdot(w(a,b)+w(b,c))
for all
a,b,c\in X
.
Bender and Chekuri
[
3
, Theorem 1]
gave a polynomial-time
4\tau
-approximation algorithm for the travelling salesman problem in graphs satisfying the
\tau
-relaxed triangle inequality.
By Hölder’s inequality, for any
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
we have

|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{c}|^{k}\leq\left(|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}|+|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{c}|\right)^{k}\leq\left(1^{\frac{k}{k-1}}+1^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\right)^{k-1}\left(|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}|^{k}+|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}|^{k}\right)=2^{k-1}\left(|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}|^{k}+|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{c}|^{k}\right).

In other words, for a finite set
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, the complete graph
K(X)
with edge weights
w(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}):=|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}|^{k}
,
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in X
, satisfies the
2^{k-1}
-relaxed triangle inequality, which implies the following corollary.

Corollary 6.1
.

For any
X\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
,
|X|=n
, one can construct a Hamiltonian cycle
H
on
X
in time polynomial in
n
such that
S_{k}(H)\leq 2^{k+2}\cdot\mathrm{e}(k+1)\cdot k^{k/2}
, or
S_{k}(H)\leq 2^{k+2}\cdot k^{k/2}\cdot(1+o_{k}(1))
.

In the conclusion of their paper
[
2
]
, Balogh, Clemen and Dumitrescu proposed a variant of the problem with a different condition in place of
X\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
: they asked to determine the extremal value of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{HC}}(X)
over all finite sets
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
with
\diam X\leq 1
.
They noted that the set of
k+1
vertices
X_{k}
of the unit simplex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
satisfies
\diam X_{k}=1
and
S_{k}^{\mathrm{HC}}(X_{k})=k+1
, and asked if this is optimal.
With present tools, we can prove the following upper bound.

Theorem 6.2
.

For any finite set
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
with
\diam X\leq 1
, there exists a Hamiltonian cycle
H
on
X
with

S_{k}(H)\leq 2\mathrm{e}\left(\frac{2k}{k+1}\right)^{k/2}\cdot(k+1)\leq 2\mathrm{e}\cdot 2^{k/2}\cdot(k+1),

where
\mathrm{e}=2.718..
is Euler’s number.

Proof.

According to Jung’s theorem
[
8
]
(see
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
for a more contemporary treatment), there exists a closed ball of radius
r:=\sqrt{\frac{k}{2(k+1)}}
that contains
X
.
Then, after a parallel translation, we may assume that
|\mathbf{a}|\leq r
for any
\mathbf{a}\in X
.
Let
H
be a Hamiltonian cycle on
X
which minimizes
S_{2}(H)
, and let
\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{t}{4(t+1)}}
, where
t
is given by Lemma
4.4
, thus

\frac{2t+1}{\alpha^{k}}\leq(k+1)\mathrm{e}\cdot 2^{k}.

By Proposition
4.3
,
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{H}
is a
(2t+1)
-fold packing, then so is
\mathfrak{hB}_{\alpha}^{H}
.
Due to Lemma
2.2
,
\hB_{\alpha}^{e}\subseteq\B_{r}(\mathbf{0})
for each edge
e\in H
.
Denoting by
V_{k}
the volume of the
k
-dimensional unit ball, we have

\sum_{e\in H}\frac{V_{k}}{2}\alpha^{k}|e|^{k}\leq(2t+1)\cdot V_{k}r^{k},\quad\text{so}\quad S_{k}(H)\leq 2\cdot\frac{2t+1}{\alpha^{k}}\cdot r^{k}\leq 2\mathrm{e}(k+1)\cdot\left(\frac{2k}{k+1}\right)^{k/2}.\qed

Appendix A
Alternative exact upper bounds

In this section, we prove an exact upper bound on
S_{k}^{\mathrm{HC}}
using properties of spherical codes, i.e. Lemma
3.3
, and an exact upper bound on
S_{k}^{\mathrm{PM}}
using properties of centroids, i.e. Lemma
4.2
.
The resulting Theorems
A.2
and
A.4
are slightly weaker than, respectively, Theorems
1.9
and
1.11
.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
be a finite set, and let
H
be a Hamiltonian cycle on
X
with minimal possible
S_{2}(H)
.
Then
\mathfrak{B}_{1/2}^{H}
is a
3(k+1)
-fold packing.

Proof.

Fix any point
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and let it be contained in the ball
\B_{1/2}^{e_{i}}
for
n
distinct edges appearing in the cycle in the order
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in H
.
Denoting
\mathbf{m}_{i}:=\mathbf{m}_{e_{i}}
, we have
|\mathbf{m}_{i}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}<|e_{i}|^{2}/4
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
By Lemma
4.1
, for any
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}<i_{4}\leq n
we have

|\mathbf{m}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{m}_{i_{3}}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{i_{2}}-\mathbf{m}_{i_{4}}|^{2}\geq\frac{|e_{i_{1}}|^{2}+|e_{i_{2}}|^{2}+|e_{i_{3}}|^{2}+|e_{i_{4}}|^{2}}{4}>|\mathbf{m}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{i_{2}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{i_{3}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{i_{4}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}.

Thus, either
|\mathbf{m}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{m}_{i_{3}}|^{2}>|\mathbf{m}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{i_{3}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}
, and so

\langle\mathbf{m}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{x},\mathbf{m}_{i_{3}}-\mathbf{x}\rangle=\frac{|\mathbf{m}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}+|\mathbf{m}_{i_{3}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}-|(\mathbf{m}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{x})-(\mathbf{m}_{i_{3}}-\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{2}<0,

or, similarly,
\langle\mathbf{m}_{i_{2}}-\mathbf{x},\mathbf{m}_{i_{4}}-\mathbf{x}\rangle<0
.

Consider an auxiliary graph
G
with the vertex set
\{1,\dots,n\}
and an edge between
i\neq j
whenever
\langle\mathbf{m}_{i}-\mathbf{x},\mathbf{m}_{j}-\mathbf{x}\rangle\geq 0
.
Draw this graph on the plane, placing vertices at the corners of a regular
n
-sided polygon, and drawing edges of
G
as segments.
We have shown that for any
i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}<i_{4}
at most one of
i_{1}i_{3}
and
i_{2}i_{4}
is an edge of
G
, which implies that the drawing has no crossing edges.
Thus
G
has a planar drawing with all vertices belonging to the outer face, i.e.
G
is
outerplanar
.
Then (see, for example, Proskurowski and Sysło
[
11
, Theorem 1]
) vertices of
G
can be colored with three colors such that no edge has endpoints of the same color.
Taking the largest color class, we obtain a subset
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
,
|I|\geq\lceil\frac{n}{3}\rceil
, such that for any
i,j\in I
,
i\neq j
,
ij
is not an edge of
G
, that is,
\langle\mathbf{m}_{i}-\mathbf{x},\mathbf{m}_{j}-\mathbf{x}\rangle<0
.
Then the set
\{\frac{\mathbf{m}_{i}-\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{m}_{i}-\mathbf{x}|}\,:\,i\in I\}
is a
(k,|I|,s)
spherical code for some
s<0
, thus, due to Lemma
3.3
,
\lceil\frac{n}{3}\rceil\leq|I|\leq k+1
, i.e.
n\leq 3(k+1)
.
∎

Theorem A.2
.

For any finite set
X\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
, there exists a Hamiltonian cycle
H
on
X
such that
S_{k}(H)\leq 6(k+1)\cdot k^{k/2}
.
In other words,

S_{k}^{\mathrm{HC}}\leq 6(k+1)\cdot k^{k/2}.

Proof.

After a parallel translation, we may assume that
X\subseteq\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{k}
.
Let
H
be a Hamiltonian cycle on
X
which minimizes
S_{2}(H)
.
By Proposition
A.1
,
\mathfrak{B}_{1/2}^{H}
is a
3(k+1)
-fold packing, then so is
\mathfrak{hB}_{1/2}^{H}
.
Applying Lemma
2.3
with
E=H
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, we get

S_{k}(H)\leq\frac{2\cdot 3(k+1)}{\left(2\cdot\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}\cdot k^{k/2}=6(k+1)\cdot k^{k/2}.\qed

Proposition A.3
.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
be a set of even size,
M
be a perfect matching on
X
with minimal possible
S_{2}(M)
,
t\geq 1
and
\alpha:=\sqrt{\frac{t}{4(t+1)}}
.
Then
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{M}
is a
t
-fold packing.

Proof.

Fix any point
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and let it be contained in the ball
\B_{\alpha}^{e_{i}}
for
n
distinct edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in M
.
Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbf{m}_{e_{i}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha^{2}|e_{i}|^{2}=\frac{t}{4(t+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|e_{i}|^{2}.

On the other hand, using Lemma
4.2
and then Lemma
3.2
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbf{m}_{e_{i}}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i<j}|\mathbf{m}_{e_{i}}-\mathbf{m}_{e_{j}}|^{2}\geq\sum_{i<j}\frac{|e_{i}|^{2}+|e_{j}|^{2}}{4n}=\frac{n-1}{4n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|e_{i}|^{2}.

It follows that
\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4n}=\frac{n-1}{4n}<\frac{t}{4(t+1)}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4(t+1)}
, which implies
n<t+1
, i.e.
n\leq t
.
∎

Theorem A.4
.

For any integer
k\geq 2
and any set
X\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
of even size, there exists a perfect matching
M
on
X
such that

1.

for any integer
t\geq 1
,
S_{k}(M)\leq 2t\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{k/2}\cdot k^{k/2}
;

2.

S_{k}(M)\leq k\mathrm{e}\cdot k^{k/2}
, where
\mathrm{e}=2.718..
is Euler’s number;

In particular,

S_{k}^{\mathrm{PM}}\leq k\mathrm{e}\cdot k^{k/2}.

Proof.

1.

After a parallel translation, we may assume that
X\subseteq\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{k}
.
Let
M
be a perfect matching on
X
which minimizes
S_{2}(M)
and let
\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{t}{4(t+1)}}
.
Due to Proposition
A.3
,
\mathfrak{B}_{\alpha}^{M}
is a
t
-fold packing, then so is
\mathfrak{hB}_{\alpha}^{M}
.
Applying Lemma
2.3
with
E=M
, we get

S_{k}(M)\leq\frac{2t}{\left(2\cdot\sqrt{\frac{t}{4(t+1)}}\right)^{k}}\cdot k^{k/2}=2t\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{k/2}\cdot k^{k/2}.

2.

If
k
is even, then, using item
1
with
t=k/2
and the inequality
(1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}\leq\mathrm{e}
,

S_{k}(M)\leq 2\cdot\frac{k}{2}\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{t}\cdot k^{k/2}\leq k\mathrm{e}\cdot k^{k/2}.

If
k
is odd, then, using item
1
with
t
such that
k=2t+1
,

S_{k}(M)\leq 2t\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{t+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot k^{k/2}=2\sqrt{t(t+1)}\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{t}\cdot k^{k/2}\leq 2\sqrt{t(t+1)}\cdot\mathrm{e}\cdot k^{k/2}.

Combining this with the inequality
2\sqrt{t(t+1)}\leq 2\frac{t+(t+1)}{2}=k
, we again have
S_{k}(M)\leq k\mathrm{e}\cdot k^{k/2}
.∎
