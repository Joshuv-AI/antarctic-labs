Error estimates for the patch bubble method for convection-dominated channel flow problem
Eberhard Bänsch, Pedro Morin, Itatí Zocola

Abstract
We present error estimates for the BMZ (Bubble Mesh Zoom) residual-free bubble method applied to a convection–diffusion equation in the convection-dominated regime. The method incorporates both element bubbles and residual-free bubbles supported on patches of two adjacent elements. We focus on the case of a parallel flow in a square domain and derive error estimates in an energy norm that remain valid as diffusion becomes small. The theoretical findings are corroborated by numerical experiments, which also exhibit a competitive performance of the method.



Keywords:

Residual free bubbles; convection-diffusion; finite elements

65M60 (Primary) 65N30, 65N12 (Secondary)

Introduction

The goal of this article is to present a theoretical analysis of the residual-free bubble method introduced in
[
1
]
for advection-dominated problems, where the diffusion coefficient
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. In that work, we proposed augmenting the discrete space not only with element-based residual-free bubbles (RFB), but also with bubbles defined on two-element patches. This enrichment increases the flexibility of the approximation space and enables a more accurate resolution of boundary layers. The numerical experiments reported in
[
1
]
demonstrate competitive performance and excellent stabilization properties on a variety of stationary and instationary test problems. However, no error analysis was provided in that article. In the present article we prove error estimates for the case of channel flow.

For a detailed discussion of both element-based and patch-based residual-free bubble methods, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
and the references therein.

Theoretical results and error estimates for the classical RFB method can be found, for example, in

[
2
,
7
,
8
,
3
]
. The idea of employing patch bubbles was first introduced and analyzed in
[
5
,
6
]
, where the authors referred to the approach as the enhanced residual-free bubble method (RFBe). In
[
5
]
, error estimates are derived for a flow directed along a diagonal relative to the square domain. These estimates, however, degenerate when the advective field aligns with one of the coordinate axes.

The available error estimates reveal several structural features of these formulations. For the classical RFB method
[
3
, Thm. 4.2]
, the bounds typically involve derivatives of the exact solution, which may become unbounded as
\epsilon
becomes small; thus, the estimate deteriorates precisely in the convection-dominated regime. For the RFBe method, the derived bound
[
5
, Thm. 2]
has the form

\max\{(\epsilon/h)^{1/2},\epsilon^{1/4}\}+h
. The error estimates we obtain in this article are of the form
(\epsilon/h)^{1/4}
, but for a norm that is a bit stronger than the one considered in
[
5
, Thm. 2]
; see Theorem
6
.
It is worth observing that none of these estimates guarantees convergence purely as the mesh parameter
h
tends to
0
, but they are all valid in the convection-dominated regime
\epsilon\ll h
. In the diffusion regime, when
h<\epsilon
, the methods do not require stabilization and the usual estimates for the pure Galerkin approximation are valid, providing optimal convergence rates.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section

2
we introduce the problem, its discretization, our proposal of stabilization using bubbles,
as well as our main result, Theorem
6
.
Section
3
is devoted to the proof of the main result.
To this end, we rely on the asymptotic expansion introduced and analyzed in
[
9
]
, and we devote Subsection
3.3
to the construction of a suitable interpolant for this expansion.
In Section
4
we present numerical experiments that demonstrate how our method compares computationally with existing approaches.
Conclusions are drawn in Section
5
.

Finally, two appendices provide technical auxiliary results, which are necessary to prove the main theorem.
They were left to the end in order to ease the reading of Section
3
.

The problem and its discretization

2.1
The problem

We consider the advection-dominated advection diffusion problem defined, for
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\ll 1

and
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},|\mathbf{a}|=1
as follows:

-\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}+\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla u^{\epsilon}=f\quad\text{in $\Omega$,}\qquad u^{\epsilon}=0\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}

(1)

with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
open and bounded, and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
In order to define a weak solution we let the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot):H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined as

a(u,v):=\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}v)\,d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla u\,v\,e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,d\mathbf{x},

and the linear form
F
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
:

F(v):=\int_{\Omega}f\,v\,e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,d\mathbf{x}.

We consider the following weak formulation of (
1
):

\text{Find }u^{\epsilon}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\qquad a(u^{\epsilon},v)=F(v),\quad\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2)

Due to the exponential term inside the bilinear form, we have the following coercivity result, which immediately implies the well-posedness of (
2
).

Lemma 1

(Coercivity)
.

There exists a constant
\alpha>0
, independent of
\epsilon
, and solely depending on
\Omega
such that

a(v,v)\geq\alpha\|v\|_{\epsilon}^{2}\quad\text{for all }v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

where

\|v\|_{\epsilon}^{2}:=\epsilon\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2},

(3)

with
\|\cdot\|
the usual
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm.

Whenever we say that a constant is independent of
\epsilon
, we understand that it may depend on
\epsilon_{0}
and that we are considering
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
.
We will usually denote
x\lesssim y
to denote
x\leq Cy
, with a constant
C>0
which may depend on
\epsilon_{0}
and
\Omega
, but is otherwise independent of the other involved quantities.

Proof.

Notice that for any
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

a(v,v)=\epsilon\int_{\Omega}e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,d\mathbf{x}+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}(-\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla v)\,ve^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla v\,ve^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,d\mathbf{x}.

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla v)\,ve^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}v\Div(\mathbf{a}\,v\,e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}})\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla v\,ve^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega}e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}|\mathbf{a}|^{2}|v|^{2}\,d\mathbf{x},

whence, using that
|\mathbf{a}|=1
,

\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla v)\,ve^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,d\mathbf{x}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}|v|^{2}\,d\mathbf{x}
.
Thus,

a(v,v)=\epsilon\int_{\Omega}e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,d\mathbf{x}+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}}|v|^{2}\,d\mathbf{x}\gtrsim\|v\|_{\epsilon}^{2}.

∎

Remark 2
.

The classical RFB method considers
V_{B}=\bigoplus_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}B_{T}
, i.e., only the
element bubbles
without
edge bubbles
.
An advantage of this method is the simplicity with which the final discrete solution can be computed. After testing separately with functions in
V_{L}
and
V_{B}
a static condensation eliminates the bubble unknowns, yielding a linear system solely for
v_{L}
. Importantly, this requires no explicit evaluation of the bubble functions themselves: only elementwise integrals involving the residual appear, and these are straightforward to approximate numerically.
Because of this elimination procedure, the resulting method
coincides exactly with the well-known SUPG formulation
[
2
]
, with the RFB framework providing a precise expression for the stabilization parameters.

Remark 3

(Another method using patch-bubbles)
.

The first papers that proposed the use of patch bubbles were those by Cangiani and Süli
[
5
,
6
]
with the so called RFBe method. They considered bubbles which satisfy the homogeneous equation in each element of the patch, and are equal to the analytical 1D solution (corresponding to the projected wind) on the interelement edge. Besides some impressive numerical experiments, they obtain some error estimates for the case of a somewhat diagonal wind, by resorting to the asymptotic approximation presented by Schieweck
[
11
]
. Those error estimates blow up when the angle between the wind direction and part of the boundary vanishes, in principle, by a limitation in Schieweck’s formulas.
Besides, we predict that their method will not be as good in such a case because those bubbles will not be able to capture precisely the (parabolic) boundary layer.
Instead, we propose to use bubble functions that solve the same advection-diffusion problem on the patches, thereby capturing the essential behavior of the solution near the boundaries, regardless of the wind direction.

Remark 4

(Computing the bubbles)
.

The reader might have noticed that the residual-free bubbles are solutions to a problem as difficult as the original one; albeit with an important difference: the domain is now of size
h
.
Cangiani and Süli use Shishkin meshes in order to compute these bubbles.
Our approach, instead, leverages
recursion
.
The algorithm, which from now on we refer to as the BMZ (Bubble-Mesh-Zoom) method, is based on the idea of zooming in locally to solve bubble sub-problems over each mesh element or patch.
The entries of the system matrix are computed within a function called
element_contributions
, in which we generate finite element partitions on the
patches
, and use the same method with patch bubbles to compute the bubbles on the coarser mesh, if
h>\epsilon/|\mathbf{a}|
; otherwise, a usual Galerkin method with bilinear finite elements is used.
The recursion will thus reach the limit of
h<\epsilon/|\mathbf{a}|
in
O(\log\frac{|\mathbf{a}|h}{\epsilon})
recursive calls.
For further details on the implementation and performance we refer the reader to
[
1
]
.
We finally mention that, as in
[
5
]
, we perform the numerical analysis of this article assuming that the bubbles are computed exactly; this allows us to understand the essence of the method, without digging into extremely technical details.

In order to quickly show the advantage of using patch bubbles, we present in Figure
2
the graphs obtained for the residual-free bubble stabilization and the proposed method, corresponding to (
1
) with
f=1
,
\mathbf{a}=(-1,0)
and
\epsilon=10^{-6}
, on meshes of size
50\times 50
, i.e.
h=0.02
.
The solution obtained using the newly proposed approach effectively eliminates the oscillations near the boundary layer and the sharp spike in the corner observed for the RFB solution. Furthermore, the solution produced by the BMZ method exhibits a satisfactory global behavior, reinforcing its reliability for this problem. Notably, the maximum value achieved with the RFB method is
1.4099
, whereas the proposed method yields a significantly improved maximum value of
0.9841\cong 1-h
.

We end this section with the following observation regarding the optimality of the approximation
u_{h}
, which will be useful for our analysis, and is an immediate consequence of the coercivity stated in Lemma
1
:

\sup_{0\not=v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{a(w_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}}\geq\alpha\|w_{h}\|_{\epsilon}\quad\text{ for all }w_{h}\in V_{h}.

(5)

This inf-sup condition implies the well-posedness of the discrete problem (
4
) and the following optimal approximation result, with a slightly modified energy norm, which coincides with the one used by Schieweck
[
11
]
.

Lemma 5

(Cea’s Lemma)
.

Let
u^{\epsilon}
denote the exact solution and
u_{h}
the discrete one. Then

|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u_{h}|\!|\!|\lesssim\inf_{z_{h}\in V_{h}}|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-z_{h}|\!|\!|,

where the norm
|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|
is defined in
H^{1}(\Omega)
by

|\!|\!|v|\!|\!|:=\|v\|_{\epsilon}+\sup_{0\not=z_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{a(v,z_{h})}{\|z_{h}\|_{\epsilon}}.

(6)

Proof.

Let
z_{h}\in V_{h}
. Due to (
5
), there exists
v_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

\|z_{h}-u_{h}\|_{\epsilon}\lesssim\frac{a(z_{h}-u_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}}.

Due to Galerkin orthogonality,

\|z_{h}-u_{h}\|_{\epsilon}\lesssim\frac{a(z_{h}-u^{\epsilon},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}}\leq\sup_{0\not=w_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{a(z_{h}-u^{\epsilon},w_{h})}{\|w_{h}\|_{\epsilon}}\leq|\!|\!|z_{h}-u^{\epsilon}|\!|\!|.

Using the triangular inequality we arrive at

\|u^{\epsilon}-u_{h}\|_{\epsilon}\leq\|u^{\epsilon}-z_{h}\|_{\epsilon}+\|z_{h}-u_{h}\|_{\epsilon}\lesssim|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-z_{h}|\!|\!|.

Moreover, again by Galerkin orthogonality one gets

\|u^{\epsilon}-u_{h}\|_{\epsilon}=|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u_{h}|\!|\!|,

which leads to the desired assertion.
∎

2.3
Main result

In this section we state the main theoretical result of this paper,
which is an error estimate in the particular case of
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
,
\mathbf{a}=(-1,0)
and
f\equiv 1
.
It is worth noticing that, in this setting, the solution exhibits three boundary layers: one (elliptic) of width
\epsilon
at
x=0
, and two (parabolic) of width
\sqrt{\epsilon}
at
y=0
and
y=1
.

Estimates on general domains and with general right-hand sides as precise and robust as the ones we obtain here seem to be unreachable for the moment.
We focus on a particular case which allows us to infer the potential capabilities of our proposed method.

We will perform the error estimates in the particular case that only one bubble per element and one per each side touching the boundary are used:

•

for each element

, we consider the space
B_{T}:=\text{span}\{\psi_{T}\}
, where
\psi_{T}
is the
element bubble
defined as the solution to:

-\epsilon\Delta\psi_{T}+\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla\psi_{T}=l^{T}(x,y)\quad\text{in $T$},\qquad\psi_{T}=0\quad\text{on $\partial T$};

where

l^{T}(x,y)=\begin{cases}l_{b}(y):=1-y/h,\quad&\text{if $T$ touches $\{y=0\}$},\\
l_{t}(y):=1-(1-y)/h,\quad&\text{if $T$ touches $\{y=1\}$},\\
1,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(7)

•

and also for each edge
S\in\Sigma_{h}^{+}
, we consider the space
B_{S}:=\text{span}\{\psi_{S}\}
, with the residual-free bubble
\psi_{S}
defined as follows:

-\epsilon\Delta\psi_{S}+\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla\psi_{S}=l^{S}(x,y),\quad\text{in $\omega_{S}$},\qquad\psi_{S}=0\quad\text{on $\partial\omega_{S}$},

where:

l^{S}(x,y)=\begin{cases}l_{b}(y),\quad&\text{if $S$ is vertical and touches $\{y=0\}$},\\
l_{t}(y),\quad&\text{if $S$ is vertical and touches $\{y=1\}$},\\
1,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(8)

Here,
\Sigma_{h}^{+}
denotes the set of interior edges of the partition that touch the outflow boundary

Theorem 6

(Main result)
.

Let

be the exact solution of (

be the discrete solution to (

,

and

3.2
Some auxiliary estimates and abstract error estimates

Below, we present two lemmas, based on results from

Lemma 7
.

Let
u^{\epsilon}
be the exact solution of (
2
) and
u^{as}
be its asymptotic expansion. Then

|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}|\!|\!|\lesssim\epsilon+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|\lesssim\epsilon+\sqrt{\epsilon}\lesssim\sqrt{\epsilon}.

Proof.

From Lemma
18
we have

\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|_{\epsilon}\lesssim\epsilon.

For
v_{h}\in V_{h}
we estimate

\displaystyle a(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as})\nabla(v_{h})e^{x}dx\,dy+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x}(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as})v_{h}e^{x}dx\,dy

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x}(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as})v_{h}e^{x}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|_{\epsilon}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}-(1-\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x}(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as})v_{h}e^{x}dx\,dy.

Integrating by parts, one gets

\displaystyle-(1-\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x}(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as})v_{h}e^{x}dx\,dy

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as})v_{h}e^{x}dx\,dy

\displaystyle+(1-\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as})\partial_{x}v_{h}e^{x}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|_{\epsilon}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}+\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|\;\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|,

so that

|a(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as},v_{h})|\lesssim\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|_{\epsilon}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}+\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|\;\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|.

Since
\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|\leq 1/\sqrt{\epsilon}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}
and
\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|\lesssim\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}\|_{\epsilon}\lesssim\epsilon
we have

|a(u^{\epsilon}-u^{as},v_{h})|\lesssim\epsilon\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}+\sqrt{\epsilon}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon},

and the assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 8

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{\varphi}_{\lambda}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\|\partial_{\hat{x}}(\hat{v}_{h}e^{x})\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\|\partial_{x}(v_{h}e^{x})\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(\|\partial_{x}v_{h})\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}+\|v_{h}\|_{T})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\epsilon}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\sqrt{\epsilon}(\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}+\|v_{h}\|_{T}).

Therefore, summing up all elements and using Lemma
11
, we obtain

\displaystyle a(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda},v_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\sqrt{\epsilon}(\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}+\|v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}})+\text{e.s.t}\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\hat{v}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}\epsilon\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}+\text{e.s.t}\,\|v_{h}\|

\displaystyle\leq\hat{\epsilon}^{1/4}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}.

We can obtain an analogous bound for
a(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}}\alpha_{\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda},v_{h})
, whence

\displaystyle a(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}}\alpha_{\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda},v_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{\epsilon}^{1/4}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}.

The
\varphi
–correctors at the outflow boundary are
e.s.t.
by construction, see Section
3.3.3
; and the assertion of the lemma thus follows.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
\xi_{B}
denote the
\xi
-corrector corresponding to
u_{B}
given by (
12
).
Then,

a(\xi_{B},v_{h})\lesssim(\epsilon/h)^{1/4}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon},\qquad\text{for all $v_{h}\in V_{h}$}.

Proof.

Recall that

\xi_{B}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}}\alpha_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{L}}\alpha_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda},

and let us focus first on the integrals corresponding to the indices in
\Lambda_{B}
.
Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, then

\displaystyle a\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda},v_{h}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\left(\epsilon\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\nabla\xi_{\lambda}\nabla(v_{h}e^{x})-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\partial_{x}\xi_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\|\nabla\xi_{\lambda}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\sqrt{\epsilon}\|\nabla(v_{h}e^{x})\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\partial_{x}\xi_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi_{B}\|_{\epsilon}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\partial_{x}\xi_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x}.

The first term is bounded as required by Lemma
11
. We now turn our attention to the second term. Integrating by parts in
x
, we obtain:

\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\partial_{x}\xi_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x}=-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\xi_{\lambda}\partial_{x}v_{h}e^{x}-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\xi_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x},

where the boundary terms vanish because
\xi=0
on the left and right sides of
\Omega_{\lambda}
.
The first term on the right-hand side is treated as follows:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\xi_{\lambda}\partial_{x}v_{h}e^{x}\Big|

\displaystyle=h\Big|\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\hat{\xi}_{\lambda}\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{v}_{h}e^{x}\Big|\lesssim h\|\hat{\xi}_{\lambda}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\|\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{v}_{h}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}\|\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{v}_{h}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}=h\epsilon\,\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}},

where, in the last inequality, we have used Lemma
26
.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\xi_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x}\Big|

\displaystyle=h^{2}\Big|\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\hat{\xi}_{\lambda}\hat{v}_{h}e^{x}\Big|\lesssim h^{2}\|\hat{\xi}_{\lambda}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\,\|\hat{v}_{h}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\lesssim h^{3}\hat{\epsilon}\|\hat{v}_{h}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}=h\epsilon\,\|v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}.

Then, since
|\alpha_{\lambda}|\leq 2N
,
\#\Lambda_{B}\lesssim N
and
N=1/h
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\partial_{x}\xi_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\,h\,\epsilon\,\big(\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}+\|v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\epsilon\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\epsilon\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}^{2}+\|v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\epsilon}{h}\Big)^{1/2}\,\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}.

Analogously we can obtain a similar bound for
a\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}}\alpha_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda},v_{h}\Big)
and also for
a\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{L}}\alpha_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda},v_{h}\Big)
, whence the assertion of the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 14
.

Let
\zeta_{B}
denote the
\zeta
-corrector corresponding to
u_{B}
given by (
12
). There is
\hat{\epsilon}_{0},0<\hat{\epsilon}_{0}<1

such that

a(\zeta_{B},v_{h})\lesssim(\epsilon/h)^{1/4}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon},\qquad\text{for all $v_{h}\in V_{h}$}

for all
0<\epsilon/h<\hat{\epsilon}_{0}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\zeta_{B}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\zeta_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}}\alpha_{\lambda}\zeta_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{L}}\alpha_{\lambda}\zeta_{\lambda},

and let us focus first on the integrals corresponding to the indices in
\Lambda_{B}
.
Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, then, analogously as before,

a\big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\zeta_{\lambda},v_{h}\big)\lesssim\big\|\zeta_{B}\big\|_{\epsilon}\big\|v_{h}\big\|_{\epsilon}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\Big|\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\partial_{x}\zeta_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x}\Big|.

(21)

Like above, by scaling and integration by parts we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\partial_{x}\zeta_{\lambda}v_{h}e^{x}

\displaystyle=-h\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\partial_{\hat{x}}(\hat{v}_{h}e^{x})-h\int_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\hat{v}_{h}e^{x}+h\int_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{right}}}\text{\text{\,e.s.t.} }\hat{v}_{h},

(22)

where we consider
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}
and
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{right}}
, the left and right boundary of the domain
\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}
. That is,

\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}:=\left\{(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in\partial\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}\,:\,\hat{x}=x_{\min}\right\}\text{\quad and \quad}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{right}}:=\left\{(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in\partial\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}\,:\,\hat{x}=x_{\max}\right\},

where
x_{\min}
and
x_{\max}
denotes the minimal and maximal
\hat{x}
-coordinate of
\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}
, correspondingly.

Since
\partial_{\hat{x}}e^{x}=he^{x}
, the first term of the last line of (
22
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\Big|h\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\partial_{\hat{x}}(\hat{v}_{h}e^{x})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|h\int_{{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\partial_{\hat{x}}(\hat{v}_{h})e^{x}\Big|+\Big|h^{2}\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\hat{v}_{h}e^{x}\Big|

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\big(h\big\|\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{v}_{h}\big\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}+h^{2}\big\|\hat{v}_{h}\big\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim h\,\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\,\big(h\big\|\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{v}_{h}\big\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}+h^{2}\big\|\hat{v}_{h}\big\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\big)

\displaystyle=h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\big(\big\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\big\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}+\big\|v_{h}\big\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big),

where in the last inequality, we have used Lemma
27
.

The integral over
\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}
in (
22
) can be handled as follows:
Let
{v_{h}=q_{h}+b_{h}}
with
q_{h}\in V_{L}
being piecewise bilinear and
b_{h}\in V_{B}
a bubble function.

Using Lemma
27
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Big|h\int_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\hat{v}_{h}e^{x}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|h\int_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}\hat{y}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}e^{x}\frac{\hat{v}_{h}}{\hat{y}}\Big|\lesssim h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\bigg\|\frac{\hat{v}_{h}}{\hat{y}}\bigg\|_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}

\displaystyle\leq h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\Big(\bigg\|\frac{\hat{q}_{h}}{\hat{y}}\bigg\|_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}+\bigg\|\frac{\hat{b}_{h}}{\hat{y}}\bigg\|_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}\Big).

For the first term, using Hardy’s one-dimensional inequality and the equivalence of norms on finite dimensional spaces, we obtain that:

\displaystyle h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\left\|\frac{\hat{q}_{h}}{\hat{y}}\right\|_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\|\partial_{\hat{y}}\hat{q}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}\lesssim h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\|\hat{\nabla}\hat{q}_{h}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\lesssim h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}.

For the second term, due to the support of the bubbles that define
V_{B}
, we have
\hat{b}_{h}=c\,\hat{b}_{S_{0}}
at
\hat{x}=x_{\min}
, where
\hat{b}_{S_{0}}
denotes the patch bubble associated with the side
\hat{x}=x_{\min}
, for some constant
c
. Applying Proposition
33
, we obtain:

h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\bigg\|\frac{\hat{b}_{h}}{\hat{y}}\bigg\|_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}\lesssim h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\|\nabla\hat{b}_{h}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}.

Then, using Proposition
35

one finds
\hat{\epsilon}_{0},0<\hat{\epsilon}_{0}<1
such that

|h\int_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\hat{v}_{h}e^{x}|\lesssim h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}.

(24)

for all
0<\epsilon/h<\hat{\epsilon}_{0}
.
For the last term in (
22
), by equivalence of norms, we obtain

h\int_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{right}}}\text{\text{\,e.s.t.} }\hat{v}_{h}\leq h\text{\,e.s.t.}\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\hat{v}_{h},

(25)

By combining (
21
), Lemma
11
, and equations (
23
),(
24
),(
25
), using that
|\alpha_{\lambda}|\leq 2N
,
\#\Lambda_{B}\lesssim N
, and
N=1/h
, we conclude that

\displaystyle a\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\zeta_{\lambda},v_{h}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{\epsilon}^{1/4}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}h^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{3/4}(\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}+\|v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{\epsilon}^{1/4}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}+\hat{\epsilon}^{1/4}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}h\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\epsilon\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}^{2}+\|v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{\epsilon}^{1/4}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}.

Analogously we can prove the same bound for
a\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\zeta_{\lambda},v_{h}\Big)
, whence

\displaystyle a\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{B}}\alpha_{\lambda}\zeta_{\lambda}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}}\alpha_{\lambda}\zeta_{\lambda},v_{h}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{\epsilon}^{1/4}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}.

We finally consider the outflow boundary. That is, we now consider the indices
\lambda\in\Lambda_{L}
, and
proceed again as for the parabolic boundary layer. The estimate is even simpler,
since the boundary term

h\int_{\Gamma_{\lambda}^{\text{left}}}\hat{\zeta}_{\lambda}\hat{v}_{h}e^{x}=0,

because
v_{h|\partial\Omega}=0
.
∎

As a consequence of the last three lemmas and Lemma
11
we arrive at the following result.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\varphi_{B},\xi_{B},\zeta_{B}
denote the correctors corresponding to
u_{B}
given by (
12
). There is
\hat{\epsilon}_{0},0<\hat{\epsilon}_{0}<1
such that

|\!|\!|\varphi_{B}|\!|\!|+|\!|\!|\xi_{B}|\!|\!|+|\!|\!|\zeta_{B}|\!|\!|\lesssim(\epsilon/h)^{1/4}

for all

.

Lemma 16
.

For the difference between
u_{B}
defined by (
11
) with the coefficients given by (
16
), (
18
) and its asymptotic expansion
u_{B}^{as}
defined by (
13
) we have the following bound:

|\!|\!|u_{B}-u_{B}^{as}|\!|\!|\lesssim(\epsilon/h)^{1/2}.

Proof.

First we bound
u_{B}-u_{B}^{as}
in the
\|\cdot\|_{\epsilon}
–norm. Using Lemma
18
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{B}-u_{B}^{as}\|_{\epsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}\|\psi_{\lambda}-\psi_{\lambda}^{as}\|_{\epsilon,\Omega_{\lambda}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}(\epsilon\|\nabla(\psi_{\lambda}-\psi_{\lambda}^{as})\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}^{2}+\|\psi_{\lambda}-\psi_{\lambda}^{as}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}(h\hat{\epsilon}\|\hat{\nabla}(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}-(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda})_{as})\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}^{2}+h^{2}\|(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}-(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda})_{as})\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}h\|\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}-(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda})_{as}\|_{\hat{\epsilon},\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}^{2}hh^{2}\hat{\epsilon}^{2}\lesssim\hat{\epsilon}^{2}.

Next, take
v_{h}\in V_{h}
,

a(u_{B}-u_{B}^{as},v_{h})\lesssim\|u_{B}-u_{B}^{as}\|_{\epsilon}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\partial_{x}(u_{B}-u_{B}^{as})v_{h}.

The last term on the right hand side can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\partial_{x}(u_{B}-u_{B}^{as})v_{h}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}h\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\partial_{\hat{x}}(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}-(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda})_{as})\hat{v}_{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}h\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}-(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda})_{as})\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{v}_{h}+\text{\text{\,e.s.t.} }\|\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{v}_{h}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}},

since
(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}-(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda})_{as})_{|\partial\hat{T}}=

e.s.t.
Further, by Lemma
18
we get:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\lambda}h\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}-(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda})_{as})\partial_{\hat{x}}\hat{v}_{h}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}-(\hat{\psi}_{\lambda})_{as}\|_{\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}h\hat{\epsilon}\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sqrt{h}\sqrt{\hat{\epsilon}}\sqrt{\epsilon}\|\partial_{x}v_{h}\|_{\Omega_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\hat{\epsilon}^{1/2}(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}h)^{1/2}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}\lesssim\hat{\epsilon}^{1/2}\|v_{h}\|_{\epsilon}.

The result now follows from the definition of the
|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|
–norm.
∎

3.4
Proof of main error estimate

Putting all things from above together we finally get:

Theorem 17
.

(Final error estimate)
Let the notations and assumptions from the previous sections hold.
Then there is
\hat{\epsilon}_{0}<1
, such that

|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u_{h}|\!|\!|\lesssim(\epsilon/h)^{1/4}.

for all
0<\epsilon/h<\hat{\epsilon}_{0}.

Proof.

By Cea’s Lemma
5
we can estimate

|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u_{h}|\!|\!|\lesssim|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{I}|\!|\!|\lesssim|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}|\!|\!|+|\!|\!|u^{as}-u^{\epsilon}_{I}|\!|\!|.

From Lemma
7
we infer

|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u^{as}|\!|\!|\lesssim\sqrt{\epsilon}.

With the choice of the interpolant
u^{\epsilon}_{I}=u_{L}+u_{B}
,
u^{as}=u^{0}+\text{correctors for }u^{\epsilon}
and
u_{B}^{as}=u_{B}^{0}+\text{correctors for }u_{B}

we get by Proposition
10
, Lemma
8
, Lemma
12
and Lemma
16

\displaystyle|\!|\!|u^{as}-u^{\epsilon}_{I}|\!|\!|

\displaystyle=|\!|\!|u^{as}-(u_{L}+u_{B})+u_{B}^{as}-u_{B}^{as}|\!|\!|

\displaystyle\leq|\!|\!|u^{0}-(u_{L}+u_{B}^{0})|\!|\!|+|\!|\!|\text{correctors for }u^{as}|\!|\!|+|\!|\!|\text{correctors for }u_{B}|\!|\!|

\displaystyle+|\!|\!|u_{B}^{as}-u_{B}|\!|\!|

\displaystyle=|\!|\!|\text{correctors for }u^{as}|\!|\!|+|\!|\!|\text{correctors for }u_{B}|\!|\!|+|\!|\!|u_{B}^{as}-u_{B}|\!|\!|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{1/4}+\hat{\epsilon}^{1/4}+\hat{\epsilon}^{1/2},

and the theorem is proved.
∎

In this section we present an experimental study comparing the performance of three methods: BMZ, the newly proposed patch bubble method; RFB, the classical Residual-Free Bubbles method

A sequence of uniform partitions of

We have presented error estimates for the BMZ method
[

]
applied to a convection dominated convection-diffusion equation, in the particular case of a channel flow in a square domain.
The error estimate is obtained in the energy norm

|\!|\!|v|\!|\!|:=\|v\|_{\epsilon}+\sup_{0\not=z_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{a(v,z_{h})}{\|z_{h}\|_{\epsilon}}.

and states that, if

is the exact solution of the problem and

the solution obtained with the BMZ method, then

|\!|\!|u^{\epsilon}-u_{h}|\!|\!|\leq C(\epsilon/h)^{1/4},

with a constant

independent of
h
and
\epsilon
, if
\epsilon/h<\hat{\epsilon}_{0}
for some

small enough.
