Domination polynomial of co-maximal graphs of integer modulo ring
Bilal Ahmad Rather

Abstract
We investigate the domination polynomial of the co-maximal graph Γ ⁡ ( ℤ n ) \displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n}) related to the ring of integers modulo n \displaystyle n . Explicit formulas are derived for n = p n 1 \displaystyle n=p^{n_{1}} and n = p n 1 ​ q n 2 \displaystyle n=p^{n_{1}}q^{n_{2}} , demonstrating that the resulting polynomials exhibit unimodality and log-concavity. For general n \displaystyle n , we present structural expressions that connect D ⁡ ( Γ ⁡ ( ℤ n ) , x ) \displaystyle D(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n}),x) to appropriate induced subgraphs. Finally, we examine domination roots and establish bounds for their moduli using the Eneström–Kakeya theorem.



Keywords: Domination polynomial; unimodal; log-concave; co-maximal graph; integer modulo ring

3
Unimodal and log-concave properties of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n})

For a real sequence of numbers
\displaystyle e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}
, if there is an index
\displaystyle\ell
(
\displaystyle 1\leq\ell\leq n
) such that the following holds

1.

\displaystyle e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq\dots\leq e_{\ell-1}\leq e_{\ell}

2.

\displaystyle e_{\ell}\geq e_{\ell+1}\geq\dots\geq e_{n-1}\geq e_{n}.

Such a sequence
\displaystyle e=\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is known as
unimodal
sequence. The index
\displaystyle\ell
is called the
mode
or
peak
of sequence
\displaystyle e.
A sequence of numbers that approaches toward a maximum term and then decreases (at least does not increase) is referred to as a unimodal sequence. We note that the mode of sequence
\displaystyle e
may not be unique.
A polynomial
\displaystyle e(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}e_{i}x^{i}
is unimodal polynomial (or simply unimodal) if its sequence of coefficients
\displaystyle e_{i}
is a unimodal sequence. Equivalently,
\displaystyle e(x)
is unimodal if its coefficient sequence has a single peak, meaning that it rises to a certain point before falling. We note that with
\displaystyle\ell=0
,
\displaystyle e
decreases and with
\displaystyle\ell=n
,
\displaystyle e
increases. With such situation
\displaystyle e(x)
is unimodal.
The number of changes of directions (increasing or decreasing) in the sequence
\displaystyle e
is known as the
oscillations
of
\displaystyle e
and for polynomial
\displaystyle e(x)
, it is denoted by
\displaystyle\zeta(e(x))
.
For a unimodal polynomial
\displaystyle e(x)
with
\displaystyle\ell=0
(or
\displaystyle\ell=n
),
\displaystyle\zeta{e(x)}=1
. For polynomial
\displaystyle 1+7x+2020x^{2}+1990x^{3}+2024x^{4}+2000x^{5}
, we observe that there are two changes of increasing (decreasing) directions. Thus, its oscillations are
\displaystyle 2
and is not a unimodal polynomial. Therefore, it is evident that
\displaystyle e(x)
is a unimodal polynomial if
\displaystyle\zeta(e(x))\leq 1.

A polynomial
\displaystyle e(x)
is
symmetric

\displaystyle e_{i}=e_{n-i},
for
\displaystyle 0\leq i\leq n

e_{i}^{2}\geq e_{i-1}e_{i+1},~\text{for all}~1\leq i\leq n-1.

\displaystyle D(\mathbb{Z}_{pq},x)

\displaystyle=\left(\binom{pq}{1}-\binom{p+q-1}{1}\right)x+\left(\binom{pq}{2}-\binom{p+q-1}{2}\right)x^{2}

\displaystyle\vdots

Now, since
\displaystyle pq
is greater or equal to
\displaystyle p+q-1
and strictly grater than
\displaystyle p-1
(and
\displaystyle q-1
). Thus, the binomial coefficients in
\displaystyle(1+x)^{pq}-(1+x)^{p+q-1}
retain their unimodal and log-concave patter. Also, after evaluating
\displaystyle x\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{i}x^{i}\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\binom{q-1}{j}x^{j}+x^{p}+x^{q}
and summing it up with coefficients of
\displaystyle(1+x)^{pq}-(1+x)^{p+q-1}
, it clear that the oscillation of coefficients of
\displaystyle D(\mathbb{Z}_{pq},x)
is unimodal (with exactly one oscillation) and log-concave. Thus, we state following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

For prime power
\displaystyle n
, The independent domination polynomial
\displaystyle D(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n}),x)
is log-concave and unimodal.
