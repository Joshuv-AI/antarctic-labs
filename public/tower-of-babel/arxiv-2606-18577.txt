Factorizations of linearized polynomials and extremal curves in odd characteristic
Tetsushi Ito, Daichi Takeuchi, Takahiro Tsushima

Abstract
. We give a complete recipe for constructing extremal van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves over finite fields of odd characteristic. The input data consist of a nonzero element of the base field together with a linear subspace satisfying a certain trace condition. This construction may be viewed as an odd-characteristic analogue of the one previously obtained by the authors in characteristic two.

Introduction

Let
p_{0}
be a prime number,
p
a power of
p_{0}
, and
q
a power of
p
.
An
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial is a polynomial of the form
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}b_{i}x^{p^{i}}
.
A
van der Geer–van der Vlugt curve
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is a smooth compactification of the affine curve

C_{R}\colon y^{p}-y=xR(x),

where
R(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
is a nonzero
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial.
Van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves have been studied from the viewpoints of number theory and coding theory: see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
]
.
This family of curves is related to the geometry of Lubin–Tate spaces, which play a central role in the local Langlands correspondences for general linear groups; see
[
5
,
12
]
.

Recall that a smooth projective curve
C
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal
(resp.
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal
) if

\#C({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=q+1+2g(C)\sqrt{q}\qquad\left(\textrm{resp.\ }\#C({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=q+1-2g(C)\sqrt{q}\right),

where
g(C)
denotes the genus of
C
. Following
[
8
]
,
we say that
C
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal
if it is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal or
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal.

In this paper, we give a complete recipe for constructing
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves in odd characteristic.
Our construction may be viewed as an odd-characteristic analogue of the construction developed in
[
8
]
for the characteristic-two case.

Assume that
p_{0}
is odd. A key ingredient in our approach is the notion of the adjoint of an

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial.
For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}b_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
,
its
adjoint
is defined by

f^{\ast}(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{e}(b_{i}x)^{p^{-i}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p^{-\infty}}],

where
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p^{-\infty}}]
denotes the perfection of

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
.

Let
R(x)
be a nonzero
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial. The key observation is that a factorization of the form

(1.1)

R+R^{\ast}=F^{\ast}\circ(aF),

where
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
and
F
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial, yields an explicit formula for the
L
-polynomial of the curve
\overline{C}_{R}
. As a consequence, it becomes straightforward to determine whether

\overline{C}_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal or
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal.

We now explain the content in more detail. The construction starts with a pair
\rho=(W,a)
, where
W\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear subspace and
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
.
Write

\prod_{t\in W}(x-t)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}c_{i}x^{p^{i}}

and
define

F_{W}(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{e}c_{i}^{p^{-i}}x^{p^{e-i}}.

There exists a unique
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial

R(x)
satisfying
(
1.1
).
We define
D_{\rho}
to be the curve
C_{R}
.

Let
\overline{D}_{\rho}
denote the smooth compactification of the curve
D_{\rho}
. Our first theorem gives an explicit formula for the

L
-polynomial of
\overline{D}_{\rho}
.

Let
\Tr_{q/p}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
denote the trace map.
Fix a prime number
\ell
different from
p_{0}
. Set
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathbb{F}}_{p},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times})
.
For a character
\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}
, set

\psi_{q}:=\psi\circ\Tr_{q/p}
.
For a power
r
of
p_{0}
, let
\left(\frac{\cdot}{r}\right)
denote the unique nontrivial character
{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\times}\to\{\pm 1\}
.
Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be an algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

Assume that
p_{0}
is odd. Let
n\coloneqq[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
.
For each
\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\}
, set

G_{\psi}:=-\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\psi(x^{2}).

Then the
L
-polynomial

L(\overline{D}_{\rho}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},T):=\det(1-\mathrm{Fr}_{q}T\mid H^{1}(\overline{D}_{\rho,{\mathbb{F}}},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}))

satisfies

L(\overline{D}_{\rho}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q},T)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}\setminus\{1\}\\
v\in W\end{subarray}}(1-\tau_{\psi,v}T),

where

\tau_{\psi,v}:=\psi_{q}(-2^{-1}a^{-1}v^{2})\cdot\left(\frac{2a}{q}\right)\cdot G_{\psi}^{n}.

The following theorem
provides an explicit construction
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves. Recall that
[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
is even if there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal van der Geer–van der Vlugt curve.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

Assume that
p_{0}
is odd and that
[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
is even. Then the following hold.

(i)

Let
\rho=(W,a)
be a pair as above.
Then the curve
\overline{D}_{\rho}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal if and only if

\Tr_{q/p}(a^{-1}v^{2})=0\qquad\text{for all }v\in W.

Moreover, if these conditions are satisfied, then
\overline{D}_{\rho}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal (resp.
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-minimal) if and only if

\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{-1}{\sqrt{q}}\right)=-1\qquad\text{(resp. }\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{-1}{\sqrt{q}}\right)=1).

(ii)

Let
R(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial. Assume that
\overline{C}_{R}
is

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal. Then there exists a pair
\rho=(W,a)
satisfying

R=R_{\rho}.

Consequently, we have
C_{R}=D_{\rho}
.

Remark 1.3
.

In
[
6
]
, the authors studied van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves in odd characteristic by a different method. They gave a criterion for

to be

-maximal or

-minimal in
[

. An important ingredient of their criterion is

As a consequence of Theorem

-maximal curves.

Theorem 1.4

(Corollary

)
.

Assume that
p_{0}
is odd and that
[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]

\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

Let

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomials

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p^{-\infty}}]
denote the perfection of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
. Define
\mathcal{R}
to be the subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p^{-\infty}}]
consisting of all finite sums of the form

\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\qquad(a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}).

An element of
\mathcal{R}
of the form
\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}
is called an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial
.

For two elements
f,g\in\mathcal{R}
, define

f\circ g\coloneqq f(g(x)).

It is straightforward to check that
f\circ g\in\mathcal{R}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{R}
becomes a non-commutative ring under the usual addition and the multiplication
\circ
.

For an element
f=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in\mathcal{R}
, define its
adjoint
by

f^{\ast}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{i}(a_{i}x)^{p^{-i}}=\sum_{i}a_{i}^{p^{-i}}x^{p^{-i}}.

The operator
(-)^{\ast}
satisfies the relations

(f+g)^{\ast}=f^{\ast}+g^{\ast},\quad(f\circ g)^{\ast}=g^{\ast}\circ f^{\ast},\quad f^{\ast\ast}=f,\quad a^{\ast}=a\quad(f,g\in\mathcal{R},\ a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}).

We now recall some basic properties of the adjoint.
Let
A
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-algebra. For
f,g\in A
,
write

f\sim_{A}g

if there exists
h\in A
such that

f-g=h^{p}-h.

When
A
is clear from the context, we simply write
\sim
instead of
\sim_{A}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

Let
B
be the perfection of
A
. For
f,g\in A
, regard
f
and
g
also as elements of
B
. Then

f\sim_{A}g\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad f\sim_{B}g.

(ii)

Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{R}
, and let
A
be the perfection of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x,y]
.
Then

xf(y)\sim_{A}f^{\ast}(x)y.

Proof.

(i) Let
\wp(x)\coloneqq x^{p}-x
.
By Artin–Scheier theory, we have

H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}({\rm Spec}(A),{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\cong A/\wp(A),\qquad H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}({\rm Spec}(B),{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\cong B/\wp(B).

The assertion therefore follows from the invariance of étale cohomology under perfection, which yields an isomorphism

H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}({\rm Spec}(A),{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\overset{\cong}{\to}H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}({\rm Spec}(B),{\mathbb{F}}_{p}).

(ii) By linearity, it suffices to treat the case
f(x)=ax^{p^{i}}
. For every
z\in A
, we have
z^{p}\sim_{A}z
. Hence,

xf(y)=xay^{p^{i}}\sim_{A}(xa)^{p^{-i}}y=f^{\ast}(x)y.

The assertion follows.
∎

Each element
f\in\mathcal{R}
defines an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear endomorphism of
{\mathbb{F}}
. We write

\ker f\coloneqq\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}\mid f(x)=0\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{R}
, and set
V_{q}\coloneqq\ker f\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Then the sequence

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\xrightarrow{f^{\ast}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\xrightarrow{a\mapsto\bigl(x\mapsto\Tr_{q/p}(ax)\bigr)}\Hom_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(V_{q},{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\to 0

is exact.

Proof.

Any
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear map
V_{q}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
extends to
an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear endomorphism of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Moreover, every
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear endomorphism of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is of the
form
x\mapsto\Tr_{q/p}(ax)
for some
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Therefore,
the homomorphism
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to\Hom_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(V_{q},{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is surjective.

Let
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
x\in V_{q}
. Since
f^{\ast}(u)x\sim uf(x)=0
,
we have

\Tr_{q/p}(f^{\ast}(u)x)=0.

Hence the sequence

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\overset{f^{\ast}}{\to}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to\Hom_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(V_{q},{\mathbb{F}}_{p})

is a complex. Let

\varphi\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/f^{\ast}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\to\Hom_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(V_{q},{\mathbb{F}}_{p})

denote the induced map.
It remains to show that
\varphi
is an isomorphism. Since
\varphi
is
surjective, it suffices to show that the source and target have the same
cardinality. We have

\#(\ker f^{\ast}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\#({\mathbb{F}}_{q}/f^{\ast}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))\geq\#V_{q}=\#(\ker f\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}).

Applying the same argument with
f^{\ast}
in place of
f
, we obtain

\#(\ker f^{\ast}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q})\leq\#(\ker f\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\#V_{q}.

Therefore,

\#({\mathbb{F}}_{q}/f^{\ast}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}))=\#V_{q}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove two lemmas that are key ingredients for our main results.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
p_{0}
is odd.
Let
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
and
F\in\mathcal{R}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial. Then the following hold.

(i)

The composition
F^{\ast}\circ(aF)
can be written uniquely in the form

(2.1)

F^{\ast}\circ(aF)=R+R^{\ast},

where
R(x)
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial.

(ii)

Let
R(x)
be as in
(i)
. Then

aF(x)^{2}\sim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]}2xR(x).

Proof.

(i) Write

F^{\ast}\circ(aF)=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}.

Since
(F^{\ast}\circ(aF))^{\ast}=F^{\ast}\circ(aF)
, we have
a_{-i}=a_{i}^{p^{-i}}
for every
i\geq 0
.
Therefore,

R(x)=\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}+\frac{a_{0}}{2}x

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial satisfying (
2.1
). The uniqueness follows immediately from the construction.

(ii) Let
A\coloneqq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p^{-\infty}}]
. By Lemma
2.1
(i), it suffices to show that

aF(x)^{2}\sim_{A}2xR(x).

We compute

\displaystyle aF(x)^{2}=F(x)\bigl(aF(x)\bigr)\sim_{A}xF^{\ast}\bigl(aF(x)\bigr)=x(R+R^{\ast})(x).

Since
xR^{\ast}(x)\sim_{A}R(x)x
, we obtain

aF(x)^{2}\sim_{A}2xR(x).

The assertion follows.
∎

We recall the characteristic-two analogue of the above lemma. For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-algebra
A
, let
W_{2}(A)
denote the ring of Witt vectors of length two. As a set,

W_{2}(A)=A\times A

with addition and multiplication given by

(a,b)+(c,d)=(a+c,b+d+ac),\qquad(a,b)\cdot(c,d)=(ac,a^{2}d+c^{2}b).

Assume that
p_{0}=2
and that
A
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-algebra.
For
\alpha,\beta\in W_{2}(A)
, write

\alpha\sim\beta

if there exists
(a,b)\in W_{2}(A)
such that

\alpha-\beta=(a^{p},b^{p})-(a,b).

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
p_{0}=2
.
Let
F\in\mathcal{R}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial satisfying
F^{\ast}(1)=0
. Then the following hold.

(i)

There exists a unique
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial
R(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
of the form

R(x)=\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}

such that

F^{\ast}\circ F=R+R^{\ast}.

(ii)

Let
R(x)
be as in
(i)
. Write

F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}b_{i}x^{p^{i}},

and define

\gamma_{F}\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq e}b_{i}^{p^{-i}}b_{j}^{p^{-j}}.

Then, in the ring
W_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x])
, we have

(F(x),0)\sim(0,xR(x)+\gamma_{F}x^{2}).

Proof.

(i) Since
(F^{\ast}\circ F)^{\ast}=F^{\ast}\circ F
, we may write

F^{\ast}\circ F=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}

with
a_{-i}=a_{i}^{p^{-i}}
. Moreover, writing
F(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}b_{i}x^{p^{i}}
, we compute

a_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{e}(b_{i}^{2})^{p^{-i}}=F^{\ast}(1)=0.

Therefore,
R(x):=\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}
satisfies the required property.

(ii) The assertion is proved in
[
8
, Lemma 4.6]
.
∎

2.2.
Artin–Schreier sheaves

Fix a prime number
\ell
not dividing
p
, and set

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}({\mathbb{F}}_{p},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}).

For each
\psi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\vee}
, let
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}
denote the

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaf on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}=\Spec({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x])
defined by the étale
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-torsor

y^{p}-y=x
and
\psi
.
For a morphism of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-schemes

f\colon X\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, let
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(f)

denote the pullback of
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}
along
f
.

We will repeatedly use the following fact.
For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-scheme
X
and any
f,g\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
satisfying

f\sim_{\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})}g,

we have an isomorphism of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaves on
X

\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(f)\cong\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(g).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
F(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
be a separable
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial of degree
p^{e}
and set

W\coloneqq\ker F^{\ast}
. Assume that
W\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Regard
F
as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-morphism
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\ x\mapsto F(x)
.
Then there is an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{v\in W}\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(vx)\xrightarrow{\cong}F_{\ast}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}.

Proof.

For every
v\in W=\ker F^{\ast}
, we have

vF(x)\sim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]}F^{\ast}(v)x=0
.
Hence,

F^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(vx)\cong\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(vF(x))\cong\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(F^{\ast}(v)x)\cong\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}.

By adjunction, this yields a nonzero morphism

\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(vx)\to F_{\ast}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}.

Taking the direct sum over all
v\in W
, we obtain a morphism

\bigoplus_{v\in W}\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(vx)\to F_{\ast}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}.

We claim that this morphism is an isomorphism.
To prove injectivity, we note that the summands

\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(vx)
are pairwise non-isomorphic rank-one smooth

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaves for distinct
v\in W
. Since every morphism
\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(vx)\to F_{\ast}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
is nonzero, the induced morphism is injective.

Finally, both sides are smooth
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaves of rank
\#W
. Therefore, the above injective morphism is necessarily an isomorphism.
∎

3.
Construction of van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves

Let
p
be a power of
p_{0}
, and
q
be a power of
p
. Let
R(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
be a nonzero
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial. The van der Geer–van der Vlugt curve associated with
R(x)
is the smooth affine curve over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
defined by

C_{R}\colon y^{p}-y=xR(x).

We recall some basic geometric properties of the curves
C_{R}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The curve
C_{R}
is smooth and geometrically connected. Its smooth compactification
\overline{C}_{R}
has genus
(\deg R)\cdot(p-1)/2
.
Moreover, the complement
\overline{C}_{R}\setminus C_{R}
consists of a single
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rational point.

Proof.

Smoothness follows from the Jacobian criterion. The other properties follow readily from
[
10
, Proposition 6.4.1]
.
∎

We also recall the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
\overline{C}_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal. Then
[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
is even, and

(3.1)

V_{R}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}\mid R(x)+R^{\ast}(x)=0\}

is contained in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from
[
8
, Lemma A.3]
. Indeed, it is proved there that

[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
is even and that

H_{R}:=\{(a,b)\in V_{R}\times{\mathbb{F}}\mid b^{p}-b=aR(a)\}

is contained in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Since the projection

H_{R}\to V_{R},\qquad(a,b)\mapsto a,

is surjective, it follows that
V_{R}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
∎

In this section, we give a recipe for constructing van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves that is suitable for computing their
L
-polynomials. The construction is divided according to the parity of
p_{0}
.

3.1.
The case where
p_{0}
is odd

In this subsection, we assume that
p_{0}
is odd. In this case, the construction starts from a pair

\rho=(W,a),

where
W\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

Proof of Theorem

Define

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

Assume that

if

is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal, and that

\overline{C}_{R}

Then the set

Proof.

By Artin–Schreier theory, for a given

if and only if
\Tr_{q/p}(a)=0

p

Assume first that

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

-minimal. In this case,

Since

Thus

The following lemma is useful to construct a quotient morphism between van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves.

Lemma 4.3
.

be

-linearized polynomials

It is straightforward to check that this morphism is finite, étale, and surjective.

4.2.1.
Quadratic forms and extremal curves

Assume that
p
is odd. By Theorem
3.5
, totally isotropic
subspaces for the quadratic form

give rise to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal curves. In this subsection, we give an alternative proof of Theorem
4.6
from this viewpoint.

Recall that a quadratic space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is a finite-dimensional

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-vector space
V
equipped with a quadratic form

Q:V\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}.

The associated symmetric bilinear form is

B_{Q}(x,y):=2^{-1}(Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)).

We say that
Q
is nondegenerate if

B_{Q}
is nondegenerate.
In this case,
the
discriminant
of
Q
is defined by

\disc(Q):=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\cdot\det B_{Q}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}/({\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times})^{2},

where
n=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}V
and
\det B_{Q}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}/({\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
denotes the determinant of
B_{Q}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Assume that
n=[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
is even.
Let
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
and let

Q_{a}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\ x\mapsto\Tr_{q/p}(a^{-1}x^{2}).

This is a nondegenerate quadratic form on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Then

\left(\frac{\disc(Q_{a})}{p}\right)=-\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{\sqrt{q}}\right).

The quadratic space
({\mathbb{F}}_{q},Q_{a})
is split if and only if

\disc(Q_{a})=1
.

Proof.

Let
\{\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{p^{n-1}}\}

be a normal basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
Then the discriminant of the field extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is

D^{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}/({\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times})^{2},\qquad\textrm{where}\quad D:=\prod_{0\leq i<j\leq n-1}(\alpha^{p^{i}}-\alpha^{p^{j}}).

By assumption,
n
is even.
Since
D^{p}=(-1)^{n-1}D=-D
,
we have
\bigl(\frac{D^{2}}{p}\bigr)=D^{p-1}=-1
.
The discriminant of
Q_{1}
is given by

\left(\frac{\disc(Q_{1})}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}D^{2}}{p}\right)=-\left(\frac{(-1)^{n/2}}{p}\right)=-\left(\frac{-1}{\sqrt{q}}\right).

Using the norm map
\Nr_{q/p}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}
, we obtain

\left(\frac{\disc(Q_{a})}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{\Nr_{q/p}(a)^{-1}\disc(Q_{1})}{p}\right)=-\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{\sqrt{q}}\right).

The final assertion follows from the classification of
nondegenerate quadratic spaces over finite fields (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Chapter II, §3, p. 30 and p. 36]
).
∎

n=[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}]
is even. Let

R(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]

be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial with
a_{e}\neq 0
such that the curve
\overline{C}_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal.
It was proved in
[
3
, Theorem 6.12]
that such curves are quotients of the smooth affine curve

H\colon y^{p^{n/2}}+y=x^{p^{n/2}+1},

whose compactification is isomorphic to the Hermitian curve.

The Hermitian curve is known to be
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal. Therefore, every quotient of the Hermitian curve is also
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal. The above theorem
[
3
, Theorem 6.12]
provides an interesting converse for van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves.

In this subsection, we give an alternative proof of the following more precise result using Theorem
4.1
(i).

Theorem 4.10
.

Let

R(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{e}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]

be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial with
a_{e}\neq 0
. Assume that
\overline{C}_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal. Then there exists a finite étale surjective morphism

H\to C_{R}.

Assume that
\overline{C}_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal. Then, by Remark
4.5
, we have
e\leq n/2
. Therefore, by Theorem
4.1
(i), there exists a finite étale surjective morphism

C_{R_{1}}\to C_{R}

where
R_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearized polynomial of degree
p^{n/2}
and
\overline{C}_{R_{1}}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal.

Thus it suffices to prove the theorem in the case
e=n/2
.
In this case, Theorem
4.10

follows from the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.11
.

Assume that
\overline{C}_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal and that

e=n/2
. Then
R(x)=ax^{p^{e}}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}

satisfying
a^{p^{e}-1}=-1
.

Proof.

Define

E_{R}:=(R+R^{\ast})^{p^{e}},\qquad V_{R}:=\ker E_{R}.

Since
\overline{C}_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-extremal, we have

V_{R}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by Lemma
3.2
.
Moreover,

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}V_{R}=2e=n=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

Hence
V_{R}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

We have

E_{R}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n/2}a_{i}^{p^{e}}x^{p^{i+e}}+\sum_{i=0}^{n/2}a_{i}^{p^{e-i}}x^{p^{e-i}}.

Since
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\subset V_{R}
, the polynomial
E_{R}(x)
is divisible by
x^{q}-x
. Since
\deg E_{R}=q
, it follows that

E_{R}(x)=\xi(x^{q}-x)

for some
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
.
Comparing coefficients, we obtain

2a_{0}=0,\qquad a_{1}=\cdots=a_{e-1}=0,\qquad a_{e}^{p^{e}}=\xi=-a_{e}.

This shows that
R(x)=a_{e}x^{p^{e}}+a_{0}x
with
a_{e}^{p^{e}-1}=-1
. When
p
is odd, we further have
a_{0}=0
, and the claim follows.

Assume now that
p
is even. In this case,
a_{e}^{p^{e}-1}=-1=1
, and hence
a_{e}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{e}}
.
Since
\overline{C}_{R}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-maximal, we have

\#C_{R}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=q+p^{e}(p-1)\sqrt{q}.

Since
e=n/2
, we obtain

\#C_{R}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=pq.

Thus

\#\left\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\mid\Tr_{q/p}(xR(x))=0\right\}=q,

and hence,

\Tr_{q/p}(xR(x))=0\qquad\text{for all }x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

Since
a_{e}x^{p^{e}+1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{e}}
and
[{\mathbb{F}}_{q}:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{e}}]=2
, we have

\Tr_{q/p}(a_{e}x^{p^{e}+1})=0
. Therefore,

\Tr_{q/p}(a_{0}x^{2})=\Tr_{q/p}(xR(x))=0.

Since
x\mapsto x^{2}
is bijective on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, it follows that
\Tr_{q/p}(a_{0}x)=0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Since the pairing

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\qquad(x,y)\mapsto\Tr_{q/p}(xy)

is nondegenerate, we obtain
a_{0}=0
. Hence the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Assume that
R(x)=ax^{p^{e}}
where
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
satisfies
a^{p^{e}-1}=-1
. Moreover, assume that
e=n/2
. Then there exists a finite étale surjective morphism

H\to C_{R}.

Proof.

A direct computation shows that the morphism

H\longrightarrow C_{R},\qquad(x,y)\longmapsto\left(x,\,\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-ay)^{p^{i}}\right)

is finite, étale, and surjective.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.10
.
