Rapidly rotating internally heated convection: bounds on long-time averages Publication type: Paper
Yutong Zhang ∗ \orcid 0000-0003-4811-2055, Ali Arslan, Stefano Maffei, and Andrew Jackson

Abstract
Convection on geophysical and astrophysical scales is subject to rapid rotation and strong heating from within the domain. In studying the long-time behaviour of the solutions for such a system, energy identities fail to capture the effects of rotation because the Coriolis force does no work, and rapid rotation can be prohibitive for direct numerical simulations. Instead, we derive an asymptotically reduced model for rapidly rotating convection driven by uniform internal heating between isothermal stress-free boundaries in a plane periodic layer. The main contribution is the proof of bounds on the mean temperature, and the mean vertical convective heat transport, in terms of the Rayleigh and Ekman numbers, in the limit of infinite Prandtl number. The first quantity represents the mixing of the flow, and the second the asymmetry in heat leaving the bottom and top boundaries due to convection, and unlike Rayleigh-Bénard convection, the two are not a priori related. We employ alternative estimation techniques to those used in previous studies (Grooms & Whitehead, 2014 Nonlinearity , 28, 29) and identify two distinct scaling behaviours for both quantities. Finally, our bounds are optimised, within the methodology, and provide a rigorous constraint for future studies of rotation-dominated internally heated convection.

Rigorous analysis of the governing partial differential equations (PDEs) provides an alternative route to insight. An approach, first developed by Doering and Constantin, called the
background field method

[
19
,
20
,
16
,
21

[
23

First, instead of optimising a mean quantity over exact PDE solutions, one considers a broader set of incompressible flows constrained only by integral balances such as energy and flux conservation. Second, the flow is decomposed into fluctuations and an arbitrary steady background field that satisfies the boundary conditions. By prescribing the geometry of this background field, the original variational problem over the full state space is reduced to an optimisation over the parameters of the background field. The resulting bounds depend on the chosen function and are valid, provided a quadratic form, termed the
spectral constraint
, is non-negative. This method has been demonstrated to be a special case of the
auxiliary functional method

[
13
,
14
]
with quadratic functionals, in which bounded functionals encode the constraints from dynamics, and the background field serves as a perturbation of an energy norm.

In the present work, we employ auxiliary functionals to investigate a new model of rapidly rotating convection driven by internal heating. We consider a uniformly heated Boussinesq fluid confined between stress-free, isothermal horizontal parallel plates, with gravity directed vertically downward, opposite to the axis of rotation.
The principal quantities characterising mean dissipation and energy transport at statistical equilibrium are the mean temperature

These quantities are not

,
3
,

A standard energy identity of the Navier-Stokes equation fails to capture the effects of the Coriolis force and therefore such bounds contain no information on this crucial addition to the momentum balance compared with the non-rotating case. To progress, rather than working with the standard Navier-Stokes equations, we derive a model inspired by the

(NH-QG) model

, an asymptotically reduced PDE system valid for

[

]

The derivation of the NH-QG model proceeds by decomposing the flow fields into horizontally averaged components and fluctuations, expanding them in powers of

The proof of bounds can be further aided by working in the infinite Prandtl number limit. Bounds in this limit are relevant for compositional driven convection

For the case of rapidly rotating RBC with stress-free boundaries, Grooms and Whitehead

The present work provides the first application of NH-QG rescaling and bounding methods to rapidly rotating IHC systems.

3
Bounds for mean temperature

3.1
Bounding framework

To bound
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
, we follow the auxiliary functional method
[
23
]
, which is based on the observation that for any bounded functional
\mathcal{V}\{\Theta,\theta\}
along the solutions of the governing equations (
6
), the time derivative of
\mathcal{V}\{\Theta,\theta\}
has zero long-time average. Moreover, the time average of a quantity is bounded from below by its pointwise infimum, so that, where
\mathcal{V}
is independent of spatial coordinates,

\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}=\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\langle\Theta\rangle_{V}-\frac{{\rm d}{\mathcal{V}}}{{\rm d}t}\,\rm dt\geq\inf\left(\langle\Theta\rangle_{V}-\frac{{\rm d}{\mathcal{V}}}{{\rm d}t}\right)\geq B.

(16)

Therefore,
B
, which may ultimately be shown to be a function of
{R}
and
E
, serves as a lower bound for
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
, provided that there exists an auxiliary functional
\mathcal{V}\{\Theta,\theta\}
, such that

Q\coloneqq\langle\Theta\rangle_{V}-\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{V}}{{\rm d}t}-B\geq 0.

(17)

We consider the following quadratic auxiliary functional,

\mathcal{V}\{\Theta,\theta\}=\frac{\beta}{2}\left\langle\theta^{2}+E^{-\frac{2}{3}}\Theta^{2}\right\rangle_{V}-E^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left\langle\phi\,\Theta\right\rangle_{V},

(18)

where
\beta>0
is referred to as the balance parameter, and

we call the background field, although in most prior work

is typically referred to as the background field. The boundedness of
\mathcal{V}\{\Theta,\theta\}
is demonstrated in appendix
B

\phi
is piece-wise differentiable with a square-integrable derivative, and satisfies the homogeneous boundary conditions

\phi(0)=0,\quad\phi(1)=0.

(19)

Now, we show that condition (

is a lower bound on
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
, provided that an associated quadratic form is non-negative.

\displaystyle Q_{0}=-B+\int_{0}^{1}\,|\Theta^{\prime}|^{2}-\phi^{\prime}\Theta^{\prime}+\phi\,{\rm d}Z,

(25a)

\displaystyle Q_{\bm{k}}=k^{2}\|{\theta}_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}_{2}+\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\prime}\,\mathrm{Re}\left\{{w}_{\bm{k}}{\theta}_{\bm{k}}^{*}\right\}\,{\rm d}Z.

3.3
Sufficient conditions for the spectral constraint

As a central step in proving

using

and

, from

given by

3.3.1
For large wavenumbers
k\geq k_{c}

Lemma 1

(Sufficient conditions for
Q_{\bm{k}}\geq 0
, large
k
)
.

Let
Q_{\bm{k}}
be specified by
eqs.

31
and
32
, and
k_{c}>0
be a lower threshold wavenumber. Suppose that
A>0
satisfies

{\mathcal{R}}A\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}k_{c}.

(33)

Then,
Q_{\bm{k}}\geq 0
for all
k\geq k_{c}
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by constructing a lower bound on
Q_{\bm{k}}
which is non-negative under the condition (
33
). The contribution of
\phi
from (
29
) to
I_{B}
and
I_{T}
, as defined in
eqs.

32a
and
32b
, is symmetric. Hence, apart from deriving a lower bound on
P
, which has been defined in (
31
), we only prove upper and lower bounds on
I_{B}
, since the identical estimates hold for
I_{T}
. Using the boundary condition
w_{\bm{k}}(0)=0
and integrating by parts, the
O(k^{-4})
term in
eq.

32a
can be written as

\int^{\delta}_{0}(\delta-Z){\rm{Re}}\left\{w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}w_{\bm{k}}^{*}\right\}\mathrm{d}Z=\int^{\delta}_{0}\frac{1}{2}\left(\left|w_{\bm{k}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\prime}-(\delta-Z)|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\mathrm{d}Z=\frac{1}{2}|w_{\bm{k}}(\delta)|^{2}-\int^{\delta}_{0}(\delta-Z)|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\mathrm{d}Z.

(34)

Then,

I_{B}=\int^{\delta}_{0}(\delta-Z)\left(k^{2}|w_{\bm{k}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{k^{4}}|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}Z-\frac{1}{2k^{4}}|w_{\bm{k}}(\delta)|^{2}\leq\int^{\delta}_{0}(\delta-Z)\left(k^{2}|w_{\bm{k}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{k^{4}}|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}Z.

(35)

Next, using that
\left|w\right|\leq\|w\|_{\infty}
and
\left|w^{\prime}\right|\leq\|w^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
, we get that

I_{B}\leq\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(\delta-Z\right)\left(k^{2}\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{1}{k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}Z=\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\left(k^{2}\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{1}{k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

(36)

On the other hand, the fundamental theorem of calculus implies

\left|w_{\bm{k}}(Z)\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{Z}w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}(\eta)\,{\rm d}\eta\right|\leq\int_{0}^{Z}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}\,{\rm d}\eta=Z\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty},

(37)

hence from the identity in (
35
),
I_{B}
is lower bounded as

I_{B}\geq-\frac{1}{2k^{4}}\left|w_{\bm{k}}(\delta)\right|^{2}\geq-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

(38)

Since
I_{T}
is upper bounded by the right hand side of (
36
), inserting (
36
) and (
38
) into
eq.

31
yields a sufficient condition for
Q_{\bm{k}}\geq 0
,

P-{\mathcal{R}}A\left(k^{2}\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{2}{k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)\geq 0.

(39)

To proceed we estimate
P
from below. Denote
w_{\bm{k}}(Z)=w_{r}(Z)+\mathrm{i}w_{i}(Z)
, with
w_{r}
and
w_{i}
being real functions. As
w_{\bm{k}}(1)=w_{\bm{k}}(0)=0
, the mean value theorem guarantees the existence of points
Z_{r}
,
Z_{i}\in\left[0,\,1\right]
such that
{w}^{\prime}_{r}(Z_{r})={w}^{\prime}_{i}(Z_{i})=0
. Hence, for any
Z\in[0,\,1]
, the pointwise estimate holds that

\displaystyle\left|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}(Z)\right|^{2}=\left|w^{\prime}_{r}(Z)\right|^{2}+\left|w^{\prime}_{i}(Z)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{Z_{r}}^{Z}{w_{r}^{\prime}}(\eta)w_{r}^{\prime\prime}(\eta)\,{\rm d}\eta+2\int_{Z_{i}}^{Z}{w_{i}^{\prime}}(\eta)w_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\eta)\,{\rm d}\eta

(40)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}\left|w_{r}^{\prime}(\eta)\right|\left|w_{r}^{\prime\prime}(\eta)\right|+\left|w_{i}^{\prime}(\eta)\right|\left|w_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\eta)\right|{\rm d}\eta.

Taking the supremum over
Z
and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality then gives

\displaystyle\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}=\sup_{Z}\left|w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}(Z)\right|^{2}\leq 2\left(\|w_{r}^{\prime}\|_{2}\|w_{r}^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}+\|w_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}\|w_{i}^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\|w_{r}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}+\|w_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}}\,\sqrt{\|w_{r}^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}^{2}+\|w_{i}^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}^{2}}

(41)

\displaystyle=2\|w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\|_{2}\|w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}.

Similarly, one obtains

\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 2\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}\|w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\|_{2}.

(42)

By Young’s inequality, together with inequalities (
41
) and (
42
), the quantity
P
in
eq.

31
is bounded from below as

\displaystyle P=k^{6}\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2}{5}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{8}{5}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{k^{6}}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2k^{3}\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}+\frac{4}{k^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}\left(k^{3}\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{2}{k^{3}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right).

(43)

Next, substituting (
43
) into (
39
) gives the condition

Q_{\bm{k}}\geq\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}k-{\mathcal{R}}A\right)\left(k^{2}\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{2}{k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)\geq 0,

(44)

which is satisfied for all
k\geq k_{c}
if and only if (
33
) holds.
∎

3.3.2
For small wavenumbers
0<k\leq k_{c}

Lemma 2

(Sufficient conditions for
Q_{\bm{k}}\geq 0
, small
k
)
.

Let
Q_{\bm{k}}
be specified by
eqs.

31
and
32
,
k_{c}>0
be an upper threshold wavenumber, and
a\in(0,\,1)
be an arbitrary weight. Suppose that the positive pair
(A,\,\delta)
satisfies

Then,
Q_{\bm{k}}\geq 0
for all
0<k\leq k_{c}
.

Proof.

First, given that
w_{\bm{k}}(0)=0
, we can use the fundamental theorem of calculus and Hölders inequality to write (
37
) and also that

\left|w_{\bm{k}}(Z)\right|=\left|\int^{Z}_{0}w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\right|\leq\sqrt{Z}\|w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\|_{2}.

(46)

Then, substituting (
37
) and (
46
) into
eq.

32a
, integrating in

and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives,

\displaystyle I_{B}

\displaystyle\leq k^{2}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}\int^{\delta}_{0}(\delta-Z)Z\mathrm{d}Z+\frac{1}{k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}\int^{\delta}_{0}(\delta-Z)Z\,|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}|\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}\delta^{3}k^{2}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\delta^{5/2}}{\sqrt{30}\,k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}.

(47)

By symmetry,
I_{T}
is bounded from above by the right hand side of (
3.3.2
). A similar procedure yields

I_{B}\geq-\frac{1}{k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}\int^{\delta}_{0}(\delta-Z)Z\,|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}|\mathrm{d}Z\geq-\frac{\delta^{5/2}}{\sqrt{30}\,k^{4}}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}.

(48)

Next, consider
P
. Let
a,\,b\in(0,\,1)
be weighting parameters. By Young’s inequality and (

), the non-negative term in
eq.

31
can be written as

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=k^{6}\|w_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+2a\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+2(1-a)\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{(1-b)}{k^{6}}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{b}{k^{6}}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2a\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2}{k^{3}}\sqrt{2(1-a)(1-b)}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}+\frac{b}{k^{6}}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2a\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{k^{3}}\sqrt{2(1-a)(1-b)}\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{b}{k^{6}}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}

Here we fix

Substituting estimates (

) to (
3.3.2
) into

Q_{\bm{k}}\geq\left(2a-\frac{1}{3}k^{2}\delta{\mathcal{R}}A\right)\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2}{k^{9/2}}\left[\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}(1-a)^{\frac{1}{4}}-2\sqrt{\frac{k\delta}{30}}{\mathcal{R}}A\right]\|w^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{\infty}\|w^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|_{2}.

Bounds for mean convective heat flux

4.1
Bounding framework

The same method as that outlined in
section

3.1
is used to bound the mean convective heat flux
\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V,\,t}
from above. Since the time average of a quantity does not exceed the pointwise supremum, we have that

\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V,\,t}=\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V}+\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{V}}{{\rm d}t}\,{\rm d}t\leq\sup\left(\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V}+\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{V}}{{\rm d}t}\right)\leq B,

(64)

where
\mathcal{V}
is defined by (
18
). It thereby suffices for
B
to be an upper bound on
\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V,\,t}
provided that

Q\coloneqq B-\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V}-\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{V}}{{\rm d}t}\geq 0.

(65)

On this basis, we state the problem.

Proposition 2

(bound on
{\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V,\,t}}
)
.

Let
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying the boundary conditions (
19
), and
\beta>0
, then the upper bound
B
on
\langle w\theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
is given by

B\coloneqq\frac{\beta}{48}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{4\beta}{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\phi\,{\rm d}Z,

(66)

provided that
\phi
and
\beta
further satisfy the
spectral constraint
:

Q_{\bm{k}}\left\{w,\,\phi,\,\beta\right\}\geq 0,\quad\forall k>0,\quad\forall\left.w\,\right|\,{w}(0)={w}(1)=0,

(67)

in which

Q_{\bm{k}}\coloneqq k^{6}\|{w}_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}_{2}+2\|{w}^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{k^{6}}\|{w}^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{{\mathcal{R}}}{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\phi^{\prime}-1\right)\left(k^{2}\left|w_{\bm{k}}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{k^{4}}\mathrm{Re}\left\{{w}^{\prime\prime}_{\bm{k}}{w}^{*}_{\bm{k}}\right\}\right)\,{\rm d}Z.

(68)

Proof.

Similar to the demonstration of
proposition

1
, inserting
eq.

23
into
eq.

65
, and expanding

and
\theta
in horizontal Fourier modes leads to the decomposition of
Q
in
eq.

24

becomes two independent conditions,

\displaystyle Q_{0}=B+\int_{0}^{1}\beta\,|\Theta^{\prime}|^{2}-\left(\phi^{\prime}-\beta Z\right)\Theta^{\prime}+\phi\,\,{\rm d}Z\geq 0,

(69a)

\displaystyle Q_{\bm{k}}=\beta k^{2}\|{\theta}_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}_{2}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\phi^{\prime}-1\right)\mathrm{Re}\left\{{w}_{\bm{k}}{\theta}_{\bm{k}}^{*}\right\}\,{\rm d}Z\geq 0.

(69b)

Since

is positive, the integrand in
Q_{0}
is convex in

. Then the worst case value of

is given by

Substituting

Using the diagnostic relation (

Q_{\bm{k}}=\frac{\beta}{{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}\left(k^{6}\|{w}_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}_{2}+2\|{w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{k^{6}}\|{w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}\|^{2}_{2}\right)+\frac{1}{{\mathcal{R}}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\phi^{\prime}-1\right)\left(k^{2}\left|w_{\bm{k}}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{k^{4}}\mathrm{Re}\left\{{w_{\bm{k}}^{\prime\prime}}{w_{\bm{k}}}^{*}\right\}\right)\,{\rm d}Z,

the non-negativity of which for all admissible
\bm{k}
and

is equivalent to the spectral constraint. Hence, the condition (

as given in

66

Lemma 4

5
Discussion

In this work, we present the asymptotically reduced equations for rapidly rotating internally heated convection and prove that, in the limit of infinite
Pr
, under stress-free and fixed temperature boundary conditions, the mean temperature of the system is bounded from below by
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}\gtrsim R^{-3/4}E^{-1}
, while the mean heat transport is bounded from above by
\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V,\,t}\lesssim R^{3/5}E^{4/5}
, for
RE^{4/3}
larger than certain thresholds. We demonstrate that the bounds scale optimally within the methodology and for the specific class of background profiles selected.

\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
from

and
60
as functions of
R
for different values of

(coloured solid lines). The black solid line shows the bound for non-rotating convection,

. Dotted lines indicate regions where the bounds no longer apply.

compares lower bounds of
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}

and

to the non-rotating lower bound from
[
57
]
of,

\langle\Theta\rangle^{non}_{V,\,t}\geq 0.6910R^{-\frac{5}{17}}\left(1-2.849R^{-\frac{5}{17}}\right).

(91)

At fixed
E
, the bound on
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}

scales with a smaller power of
R

than
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}^{non}
.
Although the bound scales with a smaller power of
R
under rotation,
the absolute magnitude of
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
remains larger than
\langle\Theta\rangle^{non}_{V,\,t}
while the flow is rotationally constrained.
That means
theorem

1
provides better bounds only for
R\lesssim E^{-68/31}
. For larger
R
, however, the least stringent criterion reported for rotation dominance
[
38
]
,
R\lesssim E^{-2}
, is not satisfied, and the flow lies outside of the QG regimes of interest. For the other quantity of interest, lower bounds on
\mathcal{F_{B}}
can be obtained from
theorem

2
, by
eq.

12b
, and more heat tends to exit from the lower boundary as rotation becomes more rapid. Although a non-rotating bound
\mathcal{F}_{B}\geq 1/2-2^{-21/5}R^{1/5}
has been demonstrated for no-slip boundary conditions
[
2
]
, its validity is restricted to
R\leq 65536
, hence a comparison cannot be made.

A diagnostic Rayleigh number,
R_{diag}\coloneqq R/Nu_{p}
with
Nu_{p}\sim\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}^{-1}
being a proxy Nusselt number quantifying the intensity of mixing
[
27
]
, can be introduced to facilitate comparison between bounds on
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
in internally heated convection and those on the Nusselt number
Nu
, quantifying the convective enhancement of heat transport, in Rayleigh-Bénard convection. With this definition,
theorem

1
can be written as
Nu_{p}\lesssim R_{diag}^{3}
for fixed
E
, which coincides with the upper bound on
Nu
for NH-QG RBC with stress-free and fixed-temperature boundary conditions
[
28
]
. The equivalence between our bounds on
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
and those on
Nu
arises from the fact that the spectral constraint in both variational problems (equation 18 in
[
28
]
and
eqs.

21
and
22
) is structurally identical. By using the Green’s function solution of the diagnostic equation (
15
) to estimate
w_{\bm{k}}
in terms of
\theta_{\bm{k}}
, and assuming
a priori
a threshold wavenumber
k_{c}
scaling as
\delta^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, Grooms and Whitehead
[
28
]
establish a sufficient condition with the optimal scaling
{\mathcal{R}}\lesssim\delta^{-\frac{1}{3}}
. This is equivalent to (
51
) upon fixing
A=\frac{1}{2}
, despite differences in the definition of
\phi
. In contrast to the Green’s function approach, we invoke (
15
) to eliminate
\theta_{\bm{k}}
, demonstrate conditions for the validity of the spectral constraint at large and small wavenumbers with integral inequalities, and
solve for
k_{c}
that delineates the two regions. Solving for
k_{c}
provides a single sufficient condition for the validity of the spectral constraint, and this condition has the same scaling as that Grooms and Whitehead find in their problem.

The upper bound for
\langle{w\theta}\rangle_{V,\,t}
given by
theorem

2
exceeds the uniform upper bound
\frac{1}{2}
at large
{\mathcal{R}}
. This has been demonstrated to correspond to a violation of the minimum principle
T\geq 0
in previous literature
[
2
]
. Previous studies of IHC address this issue by imposing the minimum principle as an additional constraint in (
64
)
[
2
,
3
]
, which changes the expression of the bound (
66
), without affecting the spectral constraint. Despite pursuing a similar strategy, we were unable to establish a bound that tends to
1/2
from below, using background profiles featuring quadratic, linear or multiple sub-layered boundary layers. The difficulty arises from the
\beta
-dependence of the bound
B
when
\delta
is constrained by (
81
), where
\beta
now varies with
\mathcal{R}
as opposed to being a constant, as is the case for bounding
\langle\Theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
.
That being said, the governing equations, (
6
), are valid up to a finite
R
. When we fix
E
, if
R
is sufficiently large enough, convection is dominated by buoyancy as opposed to rotation, and (
6
) are no longer valid. Therefore, the bound on
\langle w\theta\rangle_{V,\,t}
is relevant for the geostrophic regime valid in the range
0\leq\langle w\theta\rangle_{V,\,t}\leq C
, where
C<1/2
and the asymptotic limit of
1/2
is not relevant to our study but is instead addressed by bounds on IHC without rotation. The value of the aforementioned
C=C(E,R)
remains an open question, and the parameter regime over which the reduced equations are valid could be investigated in future work by comparison with solutions of the full PDE system.

Unlike the non-rotating bounds in
[
40
]
, our methodology for proving
theorems

1
and
2
cannot be directly extended to general
Pr
. Without the diagnostic relation (
15
) arising in the infinite-
Pr
limit, or supplying additional information, the non-negativity of
Q_{\bm{k}}
cannot be ensured, even when the kinetic energy balance is taken into account. Nevertheless, future work may follow the approaches of
[
55
]
, where the solutions at infinite
Pr
are treated as perturbations from the solutions when
Pr<\infty
, and
estimates on inertia terms lead to corrected bounds applicable to large but finite
Pr
. In
[
53
]
, a similar approach is taken to obtain a semi-analytical bound on rotating convection, albeit by only using the maximum principle from the temperature equation and not any dynamical information.
Another approach would be to use different auxiliary functionals,
\mathcal{V}
,
although higher-order polynomials remain analytically intractable such that non-polynomial alternatives may be required
[
25
,
12
]
.

A.2
Remarks

The vertical component of
eq.

101
reveals the hydrostatic balance

\partial_{Z}\,\overline{p}_{0}=\frac{{\mathcal{R}}}{Pr}{\overline{T}_{0}}

(113)

attained between the leading-order mean pressure and buoyancy fields. Meanwhile, (
108
) implies the geostrophic balance between the leading-order Coriolis force and horizontal pressure gradients, capturing the dominant force balance characteristic of the QG regimes. Compared to the classical geostrophic theory
[
52
]
, in (
96
) higher-order force balances are adjusted by the vertical convection, which renders the vertical pressure gradient
non-hydrostatic

[
33
]
and the horizontal gradient
quasi-geostrophic
. The balance (
108
) does not account for boundary-layer effects, hence stress-free boundary conditions must be applied to ensure the physical consistency. For no-slip boundaries, Ekman boundary layers form and influence the bulk flow through Ekman pumping. Such effects can be parametrized as a modified mechanical boundary condition, following the approach of
[
32
]
.

Although the non-dimensionalisation itself involves no assumptions, the condition (
95
) imposes that the chosen velocity, temperature, and pressure scales represent the largest amplitudes in powers of
\epsilon
, so that after non-dimensionalization, as
\epsilon\to 0
, no dynamics of order
O(\epsilon^{n})
with
n<0
arises. The chosen velocity scale filters out fast inertial waves. For the temperature scale, since rapid rotation tends to suppress convection, the conductive temperature scale is a natural choice. Finally, the geostrophic and hydrostatic balances satisfied by the leading-order pressure fields provide justification for the adopted pressure unit.

The other assumption
\epsilon=E^{\frac{1}{3}}
, originally introduced by Julien et al.
[
33
]
, is motivated by the
E^{\frac{1}{3}}
-scaling of the characteristic horizontal length scale in rotating Rayleigh–Bénard convection near the onset of convection
[
9
]
. The same scaling has been proven to hold asymptotically for rotating internally heated convection
[
47
,
30
,
5
]
. Whether this scaling persists in the geostrophic turbulence regime remains an open question. Nevertheless, simulations of the rescaled incompressible Navier–Stokes equations
[
35
,
54
]
on rotating Rayleigh–Bénard convection, which employ the same anisotropic non-dimensionalisation as the NH-QG equations but do not assume this scaling to be characteristic, demonstrate convergence toward NH-QG solutions as
E
decreases.

\mathcal{V}

Proposition 3

(boundedness of the auxiliary functional)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\left\{\Theta,\,\theta\right\}
be

\mathcal{V}=\frac{\beta}{2}\left\langle\theta^{2}+E^{-\frac{2}{3}}\Theta^{2}\right\rangle_{V}-E^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left\langle\phi(Z)\,\Theta\right\rangle_{V},

(114)

and evaluated along trajectories of
eq.

6
, subject to the stress-free, isothermal boundary conditions
eq.

8
and regular initial data. Therein,
\beta
is a positive parameter, and
\phi:[0,\,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is square-integrable. Then, for any fixed choice of
\phi
and
\beta
, and for any fixed value of
E
, the functional
\mathcal{V}
remains uniformly bounded in time.

Proof.

The argument begins with the evolution equation (
9
) for the total temperature energy,

\frac{E^{-\frac{2}{3}}}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\langle\Theta^{2}+E^{\frac{2}{3}}\theta^{2}\rangle_{V}=-\langle\left|\nabla_{h}\theta\right|^{2}+(\partial_{Z}\Theta)^{2}\rangle_{V}+\langle\Theta\rangle_{V},

(115)

According to the decomposition (
7
), as
\overline{\theta}=0
, we have

\langle\Theta^{2}+E^{\frac{2}{3}}\theta^{2}\rangle_{V}=\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}.

(116)

In
eq.

9
, as a consequence of horizontal periodicity, the horizontal dissipation satisfies

\langle\left|\nabla_{h}\theta\right|^{2}\rangle_{V}\geq 4\pi^{2}E^{\frac{2}{3}}\min\left\{\frac{1}{L_{x}^{2}},\,\frac{1}{L_{y}^{2}}\right\}\,\langle\theta^{2}\rangle_{V}.

(117)

On the other hand, the isothermal boundary conditions together with the Poincaré inequality imply

\langle(\partial_{Z}\Theta)^{2}\rangle_{V}\geq\pi^{2}\langle\Theta^{2}\rangle_{V}.

(118)

Combining (
117
) and (
118
), one may bound the volumetric thermal diffusion rate by

\langle\left|\nabla_{h}\theta\right|^{2}+(\partial_{Z}\Theta)^{2}\rangle_{V}\geq\mu^{2}\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V},

(119)

in which

\mu\coloneqq\pi\min\left\{\frac{2}{L_{x}},\,\frac{2}{L_{y}},\,1\right\}.

(120)

Additionally, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the source term in
eq.

9
satisfies

\left|\langle\Theta\rangle_{V}\right|\leq\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}.

(121)

Substituting (
116
), (
119
), and (
121
) into
eq.

9
and dividing by
\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}
leads to

E^{-\frac{2}{3}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}\leq-\mu^{2}\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}+1.

(122)

Then, by Grönwall’s inequality,

\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}(t)\leq\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}(0)\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{2}E^{\frac{2}{3}}t}+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\mu^{2}E^{\frac{2}{3}}t}\right)<\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}(0)+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}},\quad\forall t>0.

(123)

Moreover, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality also gives

\left|\langle\phi(Z)\Theta\rangle_{V}\right|\leq\|\phi(Z)\|_{2}\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}.

(124)

By substituting (
116
) and (
124
) into the auxiliary functional (
114
), and combining with
eq.

123
, we obtain the upper bound,

\left|{\mathcal{V}}\right|<E^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left[\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}(0)+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\right)^{2}+\|\phi(Z)\|_{2}\,\left(\sqrt{\langle T^{2}\rangle_{V}}(0)+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\right)\right],\quad\forall t>0,

(125)

which concludes the proof under the regularity of initial conditions.
