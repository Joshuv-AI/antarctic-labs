Developmental linearization of functional connectivity and its alteration in males with autism spectrum disorder
Authors: Xia Qiu, Weili Dang, Bingxiang Ma, Wei Xing, Xi Zhang, Jianghua Du, Yamin Kong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeBrain development is accompanied by gradual refinement of large-scale functional communication. Recent work indicates that functional connectivity can be characterized by both linear and nonlinear dependencies, raising the possibility that maturation involves a shift toward more linearly organized coupling. Whether this developmental trajectory differs in autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is not yet clear.MethodsWe analyzed resting-state fMRI data from the ABIDE cohort, including 188 males with ASD and 216 age-matched typically developing (TD) males. The ASD group had a mean age of 15.66 ± 66.13 years (range = 6–35), and the TD group had a mean age of 15.71 ± 5.97 years (range = 7–35). After standard preprocessing, we estimated linear and nonlinear connectivity for each participant and derived an index reflecting the extent to which nonlinear interactions were accounted for by linear coupling. This index was examined at the whole-brain scale and within canonical large-scale networks defined by the Yeo 7-network framework. Group differences and age-related effects were assessed, with multiple-comparison correction applied to network-level analyses.ResultsAt the whole-brain level, ASD participants showed significantly lower R² values than TD participants. At the network level, significant between-group differences were observed in the visual, somatomotor, dorsal attention, ventral attention, and default mode networks. Across the typically developing group, the index increased significantly with age, suggesting a gradual strengthening of linear structure in functional interactions during maturation. In contrast, the ASD group showed only a weak relationship with age. At the network level, age-related associations were more widespread in the TD group than in the ASD group. In TD, significant positive age associations were observed in multiple networks, whereas in ASD they were limited to fewer systems. The clearest between-group inconsistency in age-related pattern was observed in the limbic network.ConclusionThese findings support the idea that normative brain development is associated with increasing linear organization of functional interactions, and suggest that this pattern is attenuated or altered in males with ASD. The proposed index quantifying the goodness of fit of the linear model predicting distance-correlation-based functional connectivity (FC) from Pearson-correlation-based FC and may provide an operational summary for characterizing atypical maturation of large-scale FC patterns.

Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition marked by persistent impairments in social communication, restricted behaviors, and atypical sensory responses. Increasing neuroimaging evidence suggests that ASD does not arise from focal structural abnormalities, but from disrupted developmental trajectories of large-scale functional brain networks (Geschwind and Levitt, 2007, Uddin et al., 2013). Resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) research has reported atypical connectivity within and between key systems including the default mode network (DMN), frontoparietal control network (FPN), dorsal and ventral attention networks, and sensorimotor networks (SMN) (Cerliani et al., 2015, Hull et al., 2017). These alterations do not follow a consistent direction; instead, hyper- and hypo-connectivity appear at different developmental stages, suggesting that ASD reflects deviations in brain network maturation rather than a static state of dysconnectivity (Nomi and Uddin, 2015). Such atypical network patterns have been associated with core cognitive and behavioral difficulties, including disrupted social cognition, executive function, and sensory modulation (Just et al., 2004).

Most studies examining functional connectivity rely on linear metrics, particularly Pearson correlation, to evaluate synchrony between brain regions (Friston, 2011). Although widely applied, these measures assume proportional relationships and are unable to characterize nonlinear dependencies that arise from feedback loops, mixed-frequency communication, and state-dependent neural transformations. The human brain exhibits nonlinear dynamical organization, including cross-frequency coupling and recurrent signaling (Bassett and Sporns, 2017, Breakspear, 2017). Empirical and modeling studies demonstrate that nonlinear dependencies may encode meaningful aspects of neural communication that are not captured by linear correlations alone (Honey et al., 2009, Stam and van Straaten, 2012). Accordingly, nonlinear measures such as mutual information, transfer entropy, and distance correlation (dCorr) have been introduced to estimate broader forms of statistical dependence (Iraji et al., 2023, Motlaghian et al., 2022). More recently, Kinsey and colleagues proposed the explicit nonlinear functional connectivity (ENL-wFC) framework (Kinsey et al., 2024), which mathematically separates functional connectivity into linear and nonlinear components and quantifies their relationship using a coefficient of determination (R2). In this framework, R² is the goodness-of-fit statistic of the linear model predicting distance-correlation-based FC from Pearson-correlation-based FC. Thus, in the present study, R² was treated as an operational index of how strongly the FC pattern estimated by distance correlation is captured by conventional Pearson-like synchronous coupling, rather than as a direct measure of nonlinear neural communication.

Neural development is characterized by progressive optimization of network topology, temporal stability, and metabolic efficiency (Fair et al., 2007, López-Vicente et al., 2021, Nomi and Uddin, 2015, Supekar et al., 2010). Prior work has shown that developmental maturation is accompanied by substantial reorganization of neural dynamics and large-scale communication patterns, although different studies emphasize different aspects of this process, including changes in variability, dimensionality, and system-level coordination (McIntosh et al., 2008). In the present study, we focus on a more specific question: whether distance-correlation-based FC patterns become increasingly captured by Pearson-based synchronous coupling across development. From this perspective, increasing R² may reflect a developmental strengthening of functional linearization, rather than a simple reduction of neural complexity per se.

ASD provides a useful framework for testing whether age-related patterns of functional linearization resemble those observed in TD individuals, and if not, how they differ. Previous research suggests delayed or atypical emergence of network segregation and long-range integration in ASD (Bernhardt et al., 2025, Supekar et al., 2010). Longitudinal and cross-sectional evidence indicates that developmental trajectories of functional connectivity in ASD diverge from those observed in typically developing (TD) populations, often showing early increases in local connectivity followed by reduced large-scale coordination later in development (Supekar et al., 2013). From the perspective of nonlinear systems, such deviations may indicate a failure to transition from high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics toward more efficient and predictable connectivity patterns. Emerging findings using nonlinear FC methods support this hypothesis (Dosenbach et al., 2010, Motlaghian et al., 2022), yet few studies have quantified how the balance between linear and nonlinear dependencies changes developmentally in ASD.

In this study, we applied the ENL-wFC framework (Kinsey et al., 2024) to resting-state fMRI data from the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange I (ABIDE-I) (Di Martino et al., 2014). Using DPARSF-processed data and the Schaefer2018 parcellation (Schaefer et al., 2018), we calculated linear FC (LIN-wFC), nonlinear FC (NL-wFC), and an R2 index representing the goodness of fit of the linear model relating distance-correlation-based NL-wFC to Pearson-correlation-based LIN-wFC. This index was examined at the whole-brain, network, and node levels to assess developmental trends and group differences. Based on prior work and theoretical accounts of neural development, we expected that R2 would increase with age in TD individuals, operationally reflecting stronger dominance of Pearson-like synchronous coupling within the distance-correlation-based FC pattern. We further hypothesized that this trend would be reduced or absent in ASD, especially within association networks such as the DMN and FPN, which are known to mature later and are consistently implicated in ASD. By characterizing age-dependent patterns of functional linearization, this work aims to clarify how the balance of linear and nonlinear coupling evolves during development and how this process is altered in ASD.

Methods

Participants

Data were obtained from the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange I (ABIDE-I) (Di Martino et al., 2014), which aggregates resting-state fMRI datasets from 17 research centers worldwide (Di Martino et al., 2014). Preprocessed versions were downloaded from the publicly available Preprocessed Connectomes Project (PCP). For the present study, we selected the DPARSF filt_noglobal pipeline because it retains low-frequency BOLD structure while avoiding global signal regression (GSR), which may disproportionately suppress distributed variance and distort nonlinear dependencies.

A multi-step quality-control procedure was applied. First, participants with a mean framewise displacement (FD) > 0.2 mm were excluded. Second, to minimize inter-site heterogeneity while maintaining representative sample size, analyses were restricted to seven contributing sites with sufficient available data (KKI, NYU, PITT, UCLA_1, UM_1, USM, YALE). Third, participants older than 35 years or missing relevant phenotypic variables were excluded. After quality control and sample selection, 497 participants met the inclusion criteria, including 404 males and 93 females. Because females represented less than 20% of the eligible sample, we considered the female subgroup insufficient for reliable sex-specific or sex-stratified analysis in the present study. We therefore focused the main analyses on males to improve the stability and interpretability of the group comparisons. fMRI Data Preprocessing

All data had been preprocessed by the Data Processing Assistant for Resting-State fMRI (DPARSF) pipeline (Chao-Gan and Yu-Feng, 2010), as implemented in the PCP-ABIDE framework. The workflow included slice-timing correction, head-motion realignment, co-registration of the structural T1-weighted image to the mean functional image. Each participant’s anatomical data was then segmented into gray matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid, and spatially normalized to MNI152 standard space with a voxel size of 3 × 3 × 3 mm³ .

Temporal denoising involved regression of nuisance variables, including 24 Friston motion parameters, white-matter and CSF signals, and linear and quadratic trends. Crucially, global signal regression was not performed in this version, as the objective was to preserve large-scale nonlinear dependencies (Murphy and Fox, 2017). The residual time series were then smoothed using a Gaussian kernel of 6 mm full width at half maximum and temporally band-pass filtered between 0.01 and 0.1 Hz to capture spontaneous low-frequency BOLD fluctuations. To control for head motion, subjects were excluded if time points with framewise displacement (FD) greater than 0.2 mm accounted for more than 20% of the total time series. The resulting four-dimensional BOLD data were spatially aligned to MNI space and served as inputs for region-of-interest (ROI) time-series extraction.

Parcellation and time-series extraction

Functional parcellation was performed using the Schaefer 2018 atlas comprising 400 functionally homogeneous cortical parcels (Schaefer et al., 2018). This atlas is organized hierarchically and aligned with the Yeo seven-network framework, enabling both fine-grained and network-level analyses. For each participant, the mean BOLD signal within each ROI was extracted from gray-matter voxels only, yielding a time-series matrix of dimension T × 400, where T denotes the number of available time points. ROIs showing signal dropout or fewer than ten valid voxels were excluded from analysis. All regional time series were z-scored before subsequent computation of functional connectivity matrices.

Computation of linear and nonlinear functional connectivity

Functional connectivity was estimated at the regional level for every participant using the extracted timeseries. Two forms of inter-regional dependence were computed. First, linear functional connectivity (LIN-wFC) was obtained by calculating Pearson correlations between each pair of regions. This measure captures synchronous, proportional fluctuations in BOLD activity and represents the most widely applied index of resting-state coupling in developmental and clinical neuroimaging research. In addition to Pearson-based FC, we computed distance-correlation-based FC. Distance correlation is sensitive to both linear and nonlinear statistical dependence and therefore should not be interpreted as a measure of purely nonlinear coupling. In addition to linear relationships, we quantified broader dependencies that may reflect nonlinear neural communication. Nonlinear functional connectivity (NL-wFC) was computed using distance correlation, a dependency measure capable of detecting both linear and non-linear associations without assuming a monotonic mapping between signals. In the present study, its computation followed the ENL-wFC framework described by Kinsey et al. (Kinsey et al., 2024), where the formal definition, mathematical details, and implementation are provided. Unlike mutual information or entropy-based estimators, distance correlation provides a bounded, interpretable metric and performs reliably with moderate-length time series, making it well suited for large developmental datasets such as ABIDE.

For each participant, vectorized NL-wFC values were entered as the dependent variable and vectorized LIN-wFC values as the predictor in a linear regression model. The resulting R² was used as the goodness-of-fit statistic of this model. Higher R² values indicate that the distance-correlation-based FC pattern is more strongly captured by the Pearson-correlation-based FC pattern, whereas lower R² values indicate weaker FC-pattern correspondence. This interpretation is consistent with the original ENL-wFC framework proposed by Kinsey et al. (Kinsey et al., 2024), in which distance correlation is used as a dependence measure sensitive to both linear and nonlinear relations, and R2 serves as the goodness-of-fit statistic of the linear model relating NL-wFC to LIN-wFC. R2 values were summarized at three hierarchical scales. At the whole-brain level, R² was computed from the regression model fitted across all pairwise edges. At the network level, R² was computed using edges within each of the seven canonical Yeo networks (visual, somatomotor, dorsal attention, ventral attention, limbic, frontoparietal, and default mode). Finally, exploratory node-level R² values were computed separately for each parcel to provide parcel-wise characterization of linearization across cortical regions.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed in MATLAB R2024a. Group differences in the degree of functional linearization were assessed by comparing mean R2 values between ASD and TD participants at both the whole-brain and network levels using independent-samples t-tests. For network-level comparisons across the seven canonical Yeo systems, Bonferroni correction (Weisstein, 2004) was applied to control for multiple comparisons (corrected significance threshold: p < 0.05/7). Developmental associations were examined descriptively by correlating R2 with chronological age separately for the ASD and TD groups using Pearson correlation analyses. To further characterize between-group inconsistency in age-related patterns, correlation coefficients were additionally compared using the cocor framework(Diedenhofen and Musch, 2015). The same Bonferroni correction was applied to the seven network-level correlation tests. Violin plots were used to visualize group differences, and developmental effects were illustrated using scatterplots with fitted regression lines to show age-related trajectories.

Results

Whole-brain R2 group comparison

At the whole-brain level, the ASD group demonstrated significantly lower mean R² values compared with the TD group. An independent-samples t-test confirmed a significant group difference ( t(402) = −3.61, p = 3.46 × 10⁻⁴, Cohen's d = −0.36). This result indicates that the distance-correlation-based FC pattern was less well captured by the Pearson-correlation-based FC pattern in ASD than in TD participants. Network-level R2 group comparison

Group differences were further examined across the seven canonical Yeo networks. After Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons, five networks showed significantly lower R2 values in the ASD group relative to the TD group: the visual network (p_Bonf = 0.045, Cohen's d = −0.28), somatomotor network (p_Bonf = 4.74 × 10⁻⁵, Cohen's d = −0.45), dorsal attention network (p_Bonf = 0.009, Cohen's d = −0.32), ventral attention network (p_Bonf = 0.018, Cohen's d = −0.30), and default mode network (p_Bonf = 0.015, Cohen's d = −0.31). The limbic and frontoparietal networks did not retain significance after correction, although nominal differences were present prior to adjustment. Whole-brain R2 values showed significant positive associations with chronological age in both diagnostic groups. In ASD, the correlation between age and whole-brain R² was significant (r = 0.19, p = 0.009), and a similar positive association was observed in the TD group (r = 0.23, p = 0.0008). Discussion

The present study examined developmental and diagnostic differences in the relationship between Pearson-correlation-based and distance-correlation-based functional connectivity patterns. In the present framework, R² should be understood primarily as the goodness-of-fit statistic of the linear model predicting distance-correlation-based FC from Pearson-correlation-based FC. Therefore, we use the term “functional linearization” operationally. It does not indicate that neural communication becomes strictly linear, nor does it quantify the amount of purely nonlinear neural interaction. Rather, higher R² indicates that the FC pattern estimated by distance correlation is more strongly captured by conventional Pearson-like synchronous coupling.

From a developmental perspective, the positive age-related associations of R² may suggest that large-scale BOLD coupling patterns become more regular, stable, or consolidated with maturation. Because Pearson correlation primarily reflects synchronous and proportional co-fluctuation between regional BOLD time series, increasing R² may indicate that distance-correlation-based FC patterns become increasingly dominated by such conventional synchronous coupling. This interpretation is broadly consistent with previous reports of developmental reductions in neural signal complexity, entropy, and other indices of irregularity, which have been linked to progressive refinement of inter-regional communication and system-level organization (Anderson et al., 2011, López-Vicente et al., 2021, McIntosh et al., 2008, Uddin et al., 2013).

Compared with TD participants, males with ASD showed lower whole-brain R². In the revised analysis, this group difference remained significant after controlling for age, site, and mean framewise displacement. Thus, the lower R² observed in ASD relative to TD was not fully explained by these major nuisance factors. Biologically, this finding may indicate that the distance-correlation-based FC pattern in ASD is less dominated by conventional Pearson-like synchronous coupling. This could reflect less stable or less consolidated large-scale BOLD coupling organization. However, this interpretation should remain cautious, because lower R² may also arise from reduced stability of FC estimates, residual motion-related variability, site-related measurement effects, or other sources of noise. Therefore, we do not interpret lower R² in ASD as direct evidence for more meaningful nonlinear neural communication. This interpretation is broadly consistent with prior reports of increased variability, altered segregation–integration balance, and atypical developmental refinement in ASD (Courchesne et al., 2011, McIntosh et al., 2008, Nomi and Uddin, 2015, Petanjek et al., 2011, Supekar et al., 2013). Nevertheless, the specific neurobiological mechanisms underlying reduced R2 remain to be clarified in future work.

The exploratory clinical association analyses provided limited preliminary support for behavioral relevance. Within the ASD group, lower whole-brain R² showed nominal negative associations with ADOS Gotham severity-related measures, including ADOS Gotham severity and restricted/repetitive behavior scores, whereas no significant association was observed with SRS raw total. These findings suggest that R² may be related to symptom-relevant variation in ASD, but the effect sizes were modest and the behavioral data were incomplete across ABIDE sites. Therefore, these results should be interpreted as exploratory and do not establish R² as a validated clinical biomarker.

At the network level, between-group differences were selective rather than global. After correction for multiple comparisons, the visual, somatomotor, dorsal attention, ventral attention, and default mode networks showed significantly higher R² values in TD relative to ASD. Among these, the visual and somatomotor systems were particularly notable. One possible interpretation is that these lower-order sensory and sensorimotor networks, which normally develop relatively early and support highly recurrent patterns of functional communication, may be especially sensitive to reductions in functional linearization. In typical development, such systems may be expected to show increasingly stable and well-organized coupling, whereas in ASD this process may remain less consolidated. This interpretation is also broadly consistent with longstanding evidence that atypical sensory processing and sensorimotor integration are prominent features of ASD (Belmonte et al., 2004, Bullmore and Sporns, 2012, He, 2011, Sydnor et al., 2021). By contrast, the relatively weaker baseline ASD–TD differences in the frontoparietal network may suggest that this system is less strongly characterized by large static separation in R² and more by variability in developmental organization. Given the central role of the FPN in executive control and flexible cognitive coordination, its weaker group difference in the present data may reflect the fact that atypicality in this system is more context-dependent and developmentally heterogeneous, rather than uniformly expressed as a stable reduction in functional linearization.

The developmental associations further refine this interpretation. Although both groups showed positive age-related trends in R2, the descriptive pattern suggested that age-related effects were more widespread in TD than in ASD (Nomi and Uddin, 2015, Uddin et al., 2013). Notably, the clearest between-group inconsistency in age-related pattern was observed in the limbic network. This finding suggests that limbic regions may be less distinguished by large baseline group separation in R2 and more by divergence in developmental organization. Such a pattern is plausible given the central role of the limbic system in affective valuation, socio-emotional processing, and motivational development, all of which are frequently reported to show atypical or asynchronous maturation in ASD (Adolphs, 2010, Cerliani et al., 2015, Nomi and Uddin, 2015, Pessoa, 2017, Uddin et al., 2013). In this sense, the limbic finding may reflect not merely a static group difference, but a developmental deviation in how functional linearization emerges over age.

The present findings also help refine the interpretation of prior nonlinear FC research. Previous studies have shown that nonlinear dependence measures can reveal functional relationships that are not fully captured by conventional linear FC metrics. For example, Motlaghian et al. (Motlaghian et al., 2022) emphasized that nonlinear coupling may contain behaviorally and biologically relevant information beyond Pearson-based estimates, whereas Kinsey et al. (Kinsey et al., 2024) showed that nonlinear connectivity-derived networks can reveal spatial variation and diagnostic sensitivity not evident in linear analyses. The present study is broadly consistent with this literature, but differs in emphasis. Rather than focusing on nonlinear FC magnitude or explicitly nonlinear network extraction alone, we examined the extent to which the broader dependency structure becomes increasingly aligned with linear coupling across development. In this sense, our results suggest that atypicality in ASD may not only involve altered connectivity strength, but also a less coordinated developmental shift toward functional linearization.

Methodologically, the present findings suggest that R² can provide an operational summary of the correspondence between distance-correlation-based and Pearson-correlation-based FC patterns. This measure may complement conventional FC analyses by capturing how strongly nonlinear-sensitive FC estimates are dominated by Pearson-like synchronous coupling. From a clinical perspective, its relevance remains preliminary. The observed group differences and nominal ADOS associations suggest potential behavioral relevance, but independent replication and more complete behavioral phenotyping are required before R² can be considered a clinically useful marker.

Limitations

Several methodological considerations merit discussion. First, the present analyses were restricted to males because the number of eligible female participants after sample selection was relatively small and substantially imbalanced compared with the male sample, which limited the feasibility of reliable sex-specific analysis. Therefore, the present findings should not be generalized to females with ASD. Second, although the analysis minimized multisite variability through strict inclusion criteria, residual scanner and acquisition differences inherent to ABIDE cannot be fully removed. Third, the cross-sectional design restricts developmental inference to inferred trajectories rather than observed longitudinal changes. Finally, the interpretation of R² requires caution. In the present framework, R² is the goodness-of-fit statistic of the linear model relating distance-correlation-based FC to Pearson-correlation-based FC. Therefore, R² should be regarded as an operational summary of FC-pattern correspondence rather than as a direct measure of a specific neurobiological mechanism. Although higher R² may suggest that distance-correlation-based FC patterns are more strongly captured by conventional Pearson-like synchronous coupling, it does not demonstrate that neural communication itself becomes strictly linear. Similarly, lower R² should not be interpreted as direct evidence for a larger amount of meaningful nonlinear neural communication. In addition, R² may be sensitive to acquisition- and motion-related variability. To address this concern, we performed additional covariate-adjusted sensitivity analyses controlling for age, site, and mean framewise displacement. The ASD–TD difference in whole-brain R² remained significant after this adjustment, suggesting that the lower R² observed in ASD relative to TD was not fully explained by these major nuisance factors. Nevertheless, residual noise sources, scanner differences, temporal signal instability, and unmeasured site-related effects cannot be fully excluded, particularly in a multisite dataset such as ABIDE. An additional limitation is that the present findings were not independently replicated in ABIDE-II. Future work should test the robustness and generalizability of the current results using independent multisite ASD datasets with comparable preprocessing and inclusion criteria. Future studies integrating ENL-wFC with dynamic or causal connectivity frameworks may clarify mechanistic significance.

Conclusion

This study suggests that functional connectivity shows increasing linearization across development, operationally defined as stronger correspondence between distance-correlation-based and Pearson-correlation-based FC patterns. Compared with TD controls, males with ASD showed lower whole-brain R². At the network level, group differences were mainly observed in sensory, attentional, and default-mode systems, suggesting that atypical FC-pattern correspondence in ASD is not uniformly distributed across the brain. These findings indicate that R² provides a useful operational index for probing developmental differences in large-scale functional connectivity organization and may help characterize atypical maturation patterns in males with ASD.
