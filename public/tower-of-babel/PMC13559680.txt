Kynurenine metabolism-related gene signature for prognostic stratification in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Yurui Liu, Caixia Zhong, Mengshan Cai, Xianghua Lin, Ling Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major global health burden with high mortality rates and limited therapeutic options. The identification of reliable biomarkers for early diagnosis and prognosis prediction is urgently needed. Kynurenine metabolism, a critical pathway in immune regulation and tumor progression, has been implicated in various cancers. However, its prognostic value in HCC has not been fully elucidated. This study aimed to develop a prognostic risk model based on kynurenine metabolism-related genes (KMRGs) for HCC patients.MethodsTranscriptomic and clinical data of HCC patients were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC) databases. A prognostic risk model was established using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and Cox regression analyses. Survival analysis and functional enrichment analysis were conducted to validate the predictive performance of the model and to investigate the underlying mechanisms. ALDH8A1 was ultimately identified as a target gene based on survival analysis, and its impact on tumor cell migration was assessed using the HCC cell line.ResultsA prognostic model based on seven KMRGs was established. The high-risk group exhibited significantly worse overall survival compared to the low-risk group. Functional enrichment analysis in high-risk patients highlighted significant enrichment in core biological processes, including spliceosome assembly and ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis. Furthermore, a nomogram integrating the risk score and clinical pathological features was developed, demonstrating moderate predictive performance for HCC prognosis.ConclusionsThis study successfully constructed a prognostic risk model based on seven KMRGs, providing a valuable tool for predicting clinical outcomes in HCC patients. These findings highlight the potential role of kynurenine metabolism in HCC progression and offer new insights for future therapeutic strategies.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), kynurenine metabolism, prognostic risk model, ALDH8A1

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains one of the most prevalent cancers, with its incidence rates continuing to rise (1). Despite significant advancements in the diagnostic techniques and treatment strategies, the prognosis for HCC patients, especially those diagnosed at an advanced stage, is still dismal (2). This grim outlook underscores the urgent need to identify novel and robust biomarkers that can improve early diagnosis, accurately predict clinical outcomes, and guide personalized therapeutic strategies for HCC. Recently, the metabolic reprogramming of the tumor microenvironment has emerged as a critical factor driving HCC progression and resistance to therapy (3). Central to this process is the dysregulation of tryptophan metabolism, in which the hyperactivation of the kynurenine pathway is instrumental in fostering an immunosuppressive milieu and facilitating tumor immune escape (4).

The kynurenine pathway is the primary route for the catabolism of the essential amino acid tryptophan, playing a critical role in maintaining immune homeostasis and regulating various physiological processes (5). Key enzymes in this pathway, such as Trp-2,3-dioxygenase (TDO), indoleamine-2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1), and IDO2, catalyze the conversion of tryptophan into kynurenine (6). In the context of cancer, the activation of the kynurenine pathway is a major mechanism of immune evasion (7). Tumor and stromal cells overexpress kynurenine pathway enzymes, leading to tryptophan depletion in the microenvironment and the accumulation of immunosuppressive metabolites, which collectively inhibit T-cell function and promote regulatory T-cell differentiation (8-10). Consequently, the kynurenine pathway has emerged as a promising therapeutic target in oncology.

Emerging evidence indicates that dysregulation of the kynurenine pathway is intimately involved in the pathogenesis and progression of HCC (4), with elevated serum kynurenine levels being associated with poor prognosis (11). In HCC tissues, abnormal expression of kynurenine metabolism-related genes (KMRGs), including KMO, TDO2, and IDO1, has been frequently observed and is closely correlated with tumor aggressiveness, metastasis, and reduced patient survival (12-15). However, the prognostic value of KMRGs in HCC has not yet been systematically evaluated, and the development of predictive models based on their expression profiles remains insufficiently explored.

In this study, we aimed to systematically analyze the association between KMRGs and the prognosis of HCC, and to construct a robust prognostic risk model for the disease. By mining transcriptomic data from public databases, we evaluated the expression levels of KMRGs in HCC. Utilizing least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)-Cox regression analysis, we developed a predictive risk signature based on the expression profiles of seven KMRGs. The prognostic performance of this model was subsequently validated in an independent cohort. Our findings not only elucidate the critical role of kynurenine metabolism in HCC progression but also provide a novel set of prognostic stratification biomarkers. Subsequent survival analysis revealed that ALDH8A1 may serve as a potential favorable prognostic biomarker for HCC. In vitro experiments further demonstrated that ALDH8A1 could inhibit the migratory capacity of HepG2 and Huh-7 cells. This research may contribute to the development of new therapeutic interventions targeting the kynurenine pathway and ultimately improve clinical outcomes for patients with HCC. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD and MDAR reporting checklists (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0739/rc).

Methods

Data acquisition and patient cohorts

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The genes related to kynurenine metabolism were derived from the “GOBP_KYNURENINE_METABOLIC_PROCESS” dataset in the GSEA database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/). RNA sequencing data and clinical information of HCC patients were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://cancergenome.nih.gov/) or the International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC) database (https://www.icgc.org). Patients with incomplete survival or expression data were excluded. After exclusion, the TCGA-Liver Hepatocellular Carcinoma (LIHC) cohort comprised 402 patients with complete survival and expression data (149 deaths), among whom 380 patients had complete tumor stage information (132 deaths). The ICGC-LIRI-JP cohort comprised 240 patients (43 deaths).

Construction of a prognostic gene signature

Based on univariate analysis, we employed 10-fold cross-validation for LASSO regression analysis with a significance threshold set at 0.05. Each regression iteration involved 1,000 random simulations, followed by calculation of the occurrence frequency for each variable combination across these iterations. Variable combinations appearing more than 100 times were retained for subsequent univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses and model construction. The area under the curve (AUC) values for each model were calculated and visualized. The computational process terminated when the graphical representation reached its peak (i.e., the maximum achievable AUC value), indicating the optimal model selection. Subsequently, 1-, 3-, and 5-year receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted for the selected model. Patient risk scores were calculated using the following formula: KYNU × 0.0540952942 + KMO × (−0.0284046427) + ALDH8A1 × (−0.006099403) + KYAT3 × 0.0144828837 + KYAT1 × 1.9365242431 + AFMID × (−0.0002066348) + IDO2 × 0.0002054585.

Risk stratification and survival assessment

We employed the Kaplan-Meier method for survival analysis and utilized the log-rank test to assess the differences in overall survival (OS) between high-risk and low-risk groups. The survival analysis was performed using the R packages “survival” and “survminer”. Based on each patient’s risk score, we constructed risk score curves.

Independent prognostic validation of the signature

We employed univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models to statistically analyze pathological characteristics and risk scores, aiming to determine the role of risk score as an independent predictor of OS. A factor was considered an independent risk factor when the P value was less than 0.05.

Development and validation of a predictive nomogram

To predict the 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS rates of HCC patients, we constructed a nomogram integrating risk scores with clinical information. This nomogram was generated using the R package “rms” and evaluated for discriminative performance through calculation of the concordance index using 1,000 bootstrap resampling iterations.

Functional enrichment analysis of the signature

The Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) software (version 4.0.3) was obtained from the official GSEA website (http://software.broadinstitute.org/gsea/index.jsp) for Windows-based systems, along with the gene set database file c5.bp.v7.1.symbols.gmt. GSEA was employed to perform Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis, utilizing c5.bp.v7.1.symbols.gmt as the gene set file for GO terms. Phenotype labels (CLS files) were generated to distinguish between high- and low-risk patient groups. Additionally, a gene expression matrix (GCT file) was constructed using messenger RNA (mRNA) expression data from HCC samples acquired from TCGA database, with the analysis configured to perform 1,000 permutations.

Cell culture

The human HCC cell line HepG2 and Huh-7 was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, USA). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM, Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution (HyClone, USA). All cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. Mycoplasma contamination was routinely tested using polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based methods. The ALDH8A1 overexpression lentivirus and corresponding vector lentivirus were purchased from Tsinghua Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). HepG2 cells and Huh-7 cells were infected according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Ambion, Austin, TX, USA; Cat# 15596026), followed by reverse transcription to complementary DNA (cDNA) using the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Vazyme, Nanjing, China; Cat# R323-01). qRT-PCR was performed with SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Cat# Q331-02) and gene-specific primers (Table 1). The relative gene expression levels were determined using the 2−ΔΔCT method.

Transwell migration assay

Cell migration was assessed using 24-well Transwell chambers (Corning, NY, USA) with polycarbonate membranes (pore size: 8.0 µm). For the migration assay, HepG2 or Huh-7 cells were harvested and resuspended in serum-free medium at a density of 1×105 cells/mL. A volume of 200 µL of the cell suspension was added to the upper chamber. The lower chamber was filled with 600 µL of medium containing 10% FBS or specific chemoattractant as a chemoattractant. Cells were allowed to migrate for 48 h at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. After incubation, non-migrated cells on the upper surface of the membrane were gently removed with a cotton swab. The migrated cells on the lower surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min at room temperature. The number of migrated cells was counted in five random fields per well under a light microscope at 200× magnification. The experiment was performed independently three times, each with three replicates.

Statistical analysis

Comparisons between the two groups were performed using independent samples t-tests. All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism software version 9.5.0. Statistical significance was defined as a P value less than 0.05.

Results

Development of a kynurenine metabolism-based prognostic signature for HCC stratification

Based on data from TCGA, we compared the mRNA expression levels of these KMRGs between HCC tissues and adjacent non-tumor liver tissues. The results showed that four KMRGs (AFMID, CCBL1, CCBL2, and IDO1) exhibited significantly higher expression in HCC tissues compared to adjacent non-tumor tissues, while the remaining six KMRGs (AADAT, ALDH8A1, IDO2, KMO, KYNU, and TDO2) showed a significant downregulation trend (Figure 1A). In the liver cancer data from the ICGC database, one KMRG (CCBL1) displayed significantly higher expression in HCC tissues than in adjacent non-tumor tissues, while the same six KMRGs (AADAT, ALDH8A1, IDO2, KMO, KYNU, and TDO2) as in the TCGA database exhibited a significant downregulation trend (Figure 1B). These findings indicate similar kynurenine metabolic reprogramming in liver cancer. Through systematic screening of KMRGs in the TCGA-LIHC cohort, we employed LASSO penalized Cox regression to develop a novel prognostic framework. This analysis yielded a seven-gene signature (including KYNU, KMO, ALDH8A1, KYAT3, KYAT1, AFMID and IDO2) with optimal penalty parameter (λ) determined by 10-fold cross-validation (Figure 1C). The subsequent univariate logistic proportional hazards model based on the patient’s survival status indicated that the expression of ALDH8A1 could significantly enhance the survival benefit of the patients (Figure 1D). The signature’s discriminative capacity was rigorously evaluated using time-dependent ROC analysis. Notably, the model achieved AUC values of 0.653 (1-year), 0.619 (3-year), and 0.627 (5-year) in the training cohort (Figure 1E), with consistent performance (AUCs >0.50) maintained in the independent validation set (Figure 1F), indicating a moderate predictive ability that may be useful for clinical decision-making. Stratification of patients based on a cutoff value of 0.189 revealed that high-risk patients experienced markedly worse OS compared to low-risk patients. This significant survival disparity was consistently observed in the training set [Figure 1G; hazard ratio (HR) =0.44, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.317–0.609] and further corroborated in the independent validation set (Figure 1H; HR =0.44, 95% CI: 0.241–0.805).

Development and validation of an integrated clinical-molecular nomogram

To assess the independence of our risk model from confounding clinical factors, multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression was conducted. This analysis confirmed that both the risk score and tumor stage retained statistical significance as independent prognostic determinants (P≤0.05), indicating the model’s robustness against clinical covariates (Figure 2A). Subsequently, we developed a comprehensive prognostic nomogram by integrating the risk score with tumor stage. The nomogram incorporates these variables to generate a quantitative scoring system, where each component contributes proportionally to the prediction of 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS probabilities (Figure 2B). The calibration curves demonstrated strong concordance between predicted and observed survival rates at all three time points, with no significant deviations detected (Figure 2C). The model’s discriminatory ability was assessed by the C-index, which yielded an unadjusted value of 0.644 (95% CI: 0.585–0.703) and a bootstrap‑corrected value of 0.637 (based on 1,000 internal bootstrap resamples). Calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed probabilities at 1-, 3-, and 5-year follow-up, with corrected calibration slopes of 0.933, 0.934, and 0.927, respectively (ideal value =1). The overall calibration bias (U) was approximately 0.001 at all time points, indicating acceptable calibration performance across different time horizons. Overall, the model shows moderate discriminative power with satisfactory calibration. We compared the time-dependent AUCs of the three models. As illustrated in Figure 2D, the combined nomogram consistently demonstrated superior predictive accuracy across almost all time points compared to stage alone and the gene signature alone. Specifically, the 5-year AUC of the nomogram reached 0.697, higher than that of tumor stage (0.644) and risk score (0.654). These results validate the nomogram’s clinical utility as a potential tool for individualized survival prediction in HCC patients.

Functional characterization of high-risk HCC through pathway enrichment analysis

To elucidate the underlying mechanisms driving HCC progression, we conducted GSEA using the training dataset. Mechanistic investigations through GSEA uncovered distinct biological programs between risk groups. High-risk tumors exhibited activation of RNA processing machinery, including spliceosome assembly and ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis (Figure 3A). Conversely, low-risk samples showed enrichment in metabolic homeostasis pathways, particularly amino acid catabolism and retinol metabolism (Figure 3B). These pathway alterations suggest that high-risk HCC may be driven by post-transcriptional dysregulation, while low-risk tumors maintain metabolic flexibility.

ALDH8A1 predicts favorable survival outcomes and is linked to impaired migration in HCC cell lines

To evaluate the prognostic value of ALDH8A1 in HCC, we performed Kaplan-Meier survival analysis using the optimal cutoffs determined separately for each dataset (TCGA: 2.41; ICGC: 3.87). Elevated ALDH8A1 expression was significantly correlated with prolonged OS in both the TCGA (Figure 4A; HR =2.25, 95% CI: 1.54–3.28) and ICGC (Figure 4B; HR =3.75, 95% CI: 2.05–6.86) validation cohorts, collectively indicating that ALDH8A1 holds potential as a protective prognostic factor for HCC. To preliminarily explore the potential biological function of ALDH8A1 in HCC, we established ALDH8A1-overexpressing cell lines via lentiviral transduction in both HepG2 and Huh-7 cells, with overexpression efficiency confirmed by qRT-PCR (Figure 4C,4D). Transwell migration assays revealed that ALDH8A1 overexpression consistently suppressed the migratory capacity of both cell lines compared to vector controls (Figure 4E-4H). Collectively, these in vitro observations indicate that ALDH8A1 expression is correlated with reduced cell motility; however, they do not establish a direct mechanistic link to tumor growth or metastatic dissemination. Instead, our findings suggest a potential role for ALDH8A1 in modulating cellular migration, which may offer a phenotypic basis that partially aligns with its favorable prognostic significance in HCC patients.

Discussion

HCC remains a global health challenge with persistently poor prognosis, particularly for patients diagnosed at advanced stages (1,2). In recent years, metabolic reprogramming within the tumor microenvironment has emerged as a hallmark of cancer progression, with growing evidence implicating the kynurenine pathway of tryptophan metabolism in HCC pathogenesis (3,4). The kynurenine pathway serves as the primary route for tryptophan catabolism, and its hyperactivation promotes immune evasion through tryptophan depletion and accumulation of immunosuppressive metabolites such as kynurenine (5-7). Elevated serum kynurenine levels have been associated with poor prognosis in HCC patients, and dysregulated expression of KMRGs has been frequently observed in HCC tissues (11-13). However, a systematic evaluation of the prognostic value of KMRGs and the development of predictive models based on their expression profiles have remained insufficiently explored.

In this study, we systematically analyzed the association between KMRGs and HCC prognosis, successfully constructing a robust seven-gene prognostic signature (comprising KYNU, KMO, ALDH8A1, KYAT3, KYAT1, AFMID, and IDO2) using LASSO-Cox regression analysis. Our gene signature achieved AUC values of 0.653, 0.619, and 0.627 in the training cohort and 0.564, 0.557, and 0.578 in an independent external test set, falling below the ≥0.7 threshold suggested by Costa Filho and Passos for acceptable models. While this rigorous external validation is methodologically stronger than internal data-splitting, it inevitably yields lower performance due to population heterogeneity, reflecting the well-known gap between discovery and generalizability (16). Despite these modest AUCs, the model showed moderate discriminative ability in stratifying patients into high- and low-risk groups, as suggested by time-dependent ROC analyses in both cohorts. It retained independent prognostic significance after adjusting for conventional clinical factors, albeit with limited predictive accuracy. When combined with tumor stage in a nomogram, it facilitated the estimation of 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities; however, calibration, prospective validation, and head-to-head comparisons with standard clinicopathologic models remain unaddressed. We have verified the biological plausibility of the included genes, yet the overall performance indicates that the model is currently a preliminary research tool rather than a clinically ready instrument. Further refinement and comprehensive validation are required before any clinical application can be considered, as emphasized by Costa Filho and Passos (16).

Among the seven KMRGs identified, ALDH8A1 emerged as a particularly compelling candidate. Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that high ALDH8A1 expression conferred significant survival benefits, and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis further confirmed that elevated ALDH8A1 expression was significantly associated with improved OS in both TCGA and ICGC cohorts. These findings align with recent multi-omics studies that have identified ALDH family members as critical modulators of HCC prognosis. Liu et al. established a four-gene signature comprising ALDH2, ALDH5A1, ALDH6A1, and ALDH8A1 that robustly stratified HCC patients into distinct prognostic subgroups, with high expression defining a low-risk cohort (17). Similarly, machine learning-based analyses have independently identified ALDH8A1 as one of seven core metabolism-related biomarkers for liver cancer prognosis, with its downregulation validated in clinical cohorts (18). Comprehensive analyses of the aldehyde dehydrogenase family have further underscored the prognostic significance of ALDH members in HCC (19).

To assess the functional relevance of ALDH8A1, we performed Transwell migration assays in ALDH8A1-overexpressing HepG2 and Huh-7 cells. The results showed that ALDH8A1 overexpression significantly inhibited cell migration in both lines, suggesting a potential role in modulating cell motility. This finding is consistent with accumulating evidence that certain ALDH isoforms may function as tumor suppressors rather than oncogenic drivers in liver cancer. For instance, ALDH2 has been shown to impede HCC progression by modulating regulatory T cell differentiation via suppression of the β-catenin/transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) signaling axis (20). Moreover, ALDH2 overexpression dramatically reduces HCC cell migration, with its downregulation linked to poor prognosis (21). The experimental results of this study further confirmed the aforementioned observations and identified ALDH8A1 as a potential therapeutic target worthy of in-depth investigation.

Our GSEA provided mechanistic insights into the distinct biological programs underlying risk stratification. High-risk tumors exhibited activation of RNA processing machinery, including spliceosome assembly and ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis, suggesting that post-transcriptional dysregulation may drive aggressive HCC behavior. In contrast, low-risk tumors showed enrichment in metabolic homeostasis pathways, including amino acid catabolism and retinol metabolism. Notably, retinol metabolism is closely linked to aldehyde dehydrogenase activity, as ALDH family members play essential roles in the oxidation of retinaldehyde to retinoic acid, a key regulator of cellular differentiation and proliferation (22,23). The enrichment of retinol metabolism in low-risk tumors may reflect preserved ALDH8A1 function, further supporting its role in maintaining metabolic homeostasis and suppressing tumor progression.

Although this study has yielded significant findings, several limitations must be acknowledged. The primary limitation lies in the fact that our nomogram was constructed based solely on a limited set of prognostic variables (namely age and tumor stage), and due to reliance on public databases, it failed to incorporate other critical clinical factors. Given the absence of detailed clinical characteristics and other tumor marker indicators in the TCGA and ICGC datasets, we plan to establish an independent clinical cohort in subsequent research to further refine the predictive model, thereby enhancing its comprehensiveness and reliability. Future work will involve conducting additional experiments to investigate the role of ALDH8A1 in HCC pathogenesis, prognostic evaluation, and treatment strategies, as well as exploring its potential as a therapeutic target. Concurrently, we intend to undertake prospective studies to assess the practical application value of this model in clinical settings.

Conclusions

Our study establishes a novel prognostic model for HCC based on KMRGs, integrated with tumor stage to construct a predictive nomogram. This model effectively stratifies patient risk and accurately predicts survival outcomes, providing a valuable tool for clinical prognosis assessment. Finally, we demonstrated that ALDH8A1 may delay HCC progression by inhibiting tumor cell migration, and this mechanism could serve as a potential favorable prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for HCC.
