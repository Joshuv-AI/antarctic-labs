Poster 165. Decellularization and Revitalization of Allografts for Functional Osteochondral Tissue Repair
Authors: Lindsey G. Droz, Lauren Morris, Carlos Kengla, Gustavo Moviglia, William Vaughn, Johanna Bolander
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Osteochondral (OC) injuries represent a prevalent clinical concern due to the limited regenerative capacity of articular cartilage and the poor integration capability of multi-tissue implants. The current clinical standards are costly with availability and logistical difficulties. Decellularized allografts could provide an 'off the shelf' alternative to mitigate these challenges. We hypothesize that by optimizing the decellularization method, followed by recellularization with primed progenitor cells, improved cell maturation, integration and functionality of the allograft can be achieved.Methods:OC samples harvested from porcine articular femurs were cut into 4x4mm cubes and decellularized. Protocol 1 (P1) used freeze thawing followed by lyophilization, Protocol 2 (P2) used thermal shock followed by treatment with triton-x, and Protocol 3 (P3) used freeze thaw cycles combined with a hypotonic solution, SDS, and nucleases. Adipose derived mesenchymal stromal cells (aMSCs) and placental derived cells (PLCs) were used to assess their ability to revitalize the implants upon co-culture with pro-regenerative immune cells. The expanded progenitor cells were combined with joint-activated mononuclear cells and seeded onto the plugs. Decellularization was analyzed by DNA quantification and cell nuclei staining with hematoxylin and eosin and DAPI. The structural differences between the decellularized plugs were observed under scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Cell distribution and extracellular matrix (ECM) production was evaluated by H&E, Alcian blue and Safranin O staining.Results:P1 resulted in robust decellularization but had unfavorable loss of osseous architecture. P2 showed poor decellularization overall with P3 resulting in adequate decellularization and preservation of mechanical integrity. Upon seeding, P1 showed minimal cellular repopulation. P2 and P3 showed increased cellular presence with the PLCs showing increased proliferation in comparison to the aMSCs.Conclusions:Our experimental findings indicate that P3 provides significant reduction of xenogeneic DNA effective decellularization while maintaining the osseous integrity required for mechanical stability and the cellular matrix environment necessary for human recellularization of the porcine allograft.
