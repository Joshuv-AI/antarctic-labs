Integrative single-cell and spatial transcriptomics with machine learning identify a Luminal-inflam malignant program and reveal an RPN1–PERK UPR vulnerability in triple-negative breast cancer
Authors: Jinpeng Wu, Jingjing Fan, Tong Sha, Hongtao Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Triple-negative breast cancer is marked by extensive cellular heterogeneity and limited availability of actionable targeted treatments, which contributes to an unfavorable prognosis. In this work, single-cell and spatial transcriptomic profiling was integrated with network-based analyses and machine-learning approaches to characterize malignant epithelial programs in TNBC and to pinpoint prognostic biomarkers.Single-cell RNA sequencing identified a malignant epithelial subpopulation, Luminal_inflam, characterized by elevated inferred copy number variation, a terminal pseudotime state, and enrichment of cell cycle–associated transcriptional programs. Cell–cell communication analysis indicated microenvironmental remodeling in TNBC and prioritized a fibroblast-associated S100A4–EGFR axis that may regulate Luminal_inflam-associated gene expression. Gene regulatory network analysis further revealed increased activities of transcription factors including MYBL2, TFDP1, CEBPD, and MBD2. A 12-gene risk signature constructed from Luminal_inflam-associated modules and survival cohorts effectively stratified overall survival and captured differences in immune features and potential drug sensitivities. In our MDA-MB-231 model, RPN1 knockdown was associated with reduced cell viability, which could be partially rescued by 4-PBA. We further found that RPN1 depletion was accompanied by increased intracellular ROS and Ca2+ levels, altered cell-cycle distribution, and elevated apoptosis, and that these changes were also partially reversible upon 4-PBA treatment. These data support the view that loss of RPN1 perturbs ER homeostasis and is linked to stress-associated cell fate changes in TNBC cells. At the same time, these findings should be interpreted in the context of prior work showing that RPN1 depletion can induce ER-stress-dependent apoptosis in breast cancer models. Thus, rather than establishing an entirely new mechanism, our study extends previous observations by connecting this stress-related phenotype to a specific malignant epithelial program and a clinically derived risk framework.Collectively, this study delineates a key malignant epithelial state in TNBC and suggests that RPN1-associated proteostasis vulnerability may represent a potential therapeutic opportunity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-56434-4.

Introductions

Among breast cancer phenotypes, Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC)—which lacks ER, PR, and HER2 expression—represents a particularly aggressive subgroup. Although it comprises only 10–15% of newly diagnosed cases, TNBC is notorious for its rapid progression, elevated risk of early relapse, and poor prognosis relative to receptor-positive subtypes1–4. Consequently, the limited repertoire of targeted therapies makes TNBC a formidable entity to manage effectively in the clinical setting5,6.At present, systemic chemotherapy continues to serve as the cornerstone of TNBC treatment and is frequently integrated with additional modalities within multimodal therapeutic regimens7–9. Accordingly, clarifying the key pathogenic mechanisms underpinning TNBC and developing novel, translatable therapeutic targets remain major scientific and clinical priorities for improving patient outcomes.

However, TNBC is not a single disease entity. Instead, it comprises a biologically heterogeneous spectrum shaped by complex genomic alterations and lineage plasticity. Consequently, TNBC shows pronounced intertumoral and intratumoral heterogeneity and frequently develops therapeutic resistance10–12. Single-cell omics encompasses a suite of technologies that capture molecular information at single-cell resolution. It enables profiling of the transcriptome, genome, epigenome, and proteome, and supports multimodal measurements within the same cell13,14 These approaches can precisely characterize cellular heterogeneity and rare cell states in tissues. They also dissect cell-state transitions and intercellular interactions, providing a critical framework for elucidating disease mechanisms and identifying actionable vulnerabilities15.The exponential growth of single-cell multi-omics coupled with sophisticated computational frameworks has revolutionized the study of oncology and complex pathologies.These advances provide unprecedented insights into disease heterogeneity, the microenvironmental ecosystem, and potential therapeutic targets.

Building on these findings, we integrated single-cell transcriptomics to resolve epithelial heterogeneity with co-expression network inference and prognostic machine-learning modeling. This strategy identified malignant epithelial programs associated with poor outcomes and highlighted RPN1 as a hub gene with translational potential. RPN1 (ribophorin I) is an essential subunit of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) oligosaccharyltransferase (OST) complex. It facilitates co-translational N-glycosylation and supports protein-folding homeostasis. Notably, ER proteostasis and stress-adaptive capacity are increasingly recognized as key determinants of tumor cell survival and therapeutic resistance16–18. We then conducted in vitro functional assays and pharmacological perturbation experiments to further evaluate the biological relevance of RPN1 in TNBC cells. We examined whether RPN1 depletion was associated with activation of PERK-mediated unfolded protein response (UPR) signaling and with downstream changes in oxidative stress, Ca2+ homeostasis, and cell fate. Collectively, this study adopts an integrated workflow that links single-cell–resolved tumor program dissection with network- and machine-learning–based clinical association analyses and functional validation of a prioritized candidate vulnerability. Our findings provide translatable evidence and candidate targets to advance the understanding of TNBC pathogenesis and inform targeted therapeutic strategies.

Methods

Data collection

The single-cell transcriptomic dataset GSE161529 was retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO)19,20. Following initial quality control, 21 samples (13 normal breast tissues and 8 triple-negative breast cancer tissues) comprising 102,878 cells were retained for downstream analyses.A total of 117 TNBC cases and 113 normal breast tissue samples were selected from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database6, including their bulk transcriptomic signatures and clinical records. These data were utilized to identify differentially expressed genes and to construct the prognostic classifier.An independent validation cohort was obtained from GEO (GSE58812), providing bulk transcriptomic data and complete survival information for 107 TNBC cases21. Spatial transcriptomic data were sourced from GEO (GSE210616)22, and five samples (GSM6433585_092A, GSM6433587_093A, GSM6433592_094B, GSM6433595_095A, and GSM6433597_117B) were used for spatial validation and subsequent analyses.

Patient sample

With the authorization of the Ethics Committee at the Affiliated Tumor Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University, we obtained thirty pairs of TNBC tumors and matched normal adjacent tissues. All study procedures strictly adhered to established ethical guidelines. Informed consent was documented in writing for all subjects before tissue procurement. The harvested specimens were processed primarily for immunohistochemistry (IHC) and downstream analysis.

Cell culture

MDA-MB-231 human breast cancer cells were purchased from Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (China). Cells were cultured in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (P/S) at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. Quality control measures included regular mycoplasma testing to confirm mycoplasma-negative status and cell line authentication by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling prior to use.

Lentiviral vector construction and transduction

GeneChem (Shanghai, China) synthesized shRNA sequences directed against human RPN1 and a corresponding negative control, which were subsequently cloned into the GV493 vector. Following verification of the sequence by Sanger analysis, the vectors were packaged into lentiviruses. MDA-MB-231 cells were inoculated into six-well plates at a density of 5 × 104 cells/mL. Once the monolayers attained ~ 90% confluence, they were transduced using a viral MOI of 20 combined with HitransG P. The medium was refreshed 24 h post-infection. Knockdown efficiency was verified 72 h later by quantitative RT-PCR and Western blot analysis. Due to its superior suppression of RPN1, the LV-sh-RPN1-3 target sequence was adopted for further experimental investigation.

scRNA-seq data processing

We employed the Seurat R package (version 4.5.1) to analyze the single-cell expression profiles23,24.Initial quality control (QC) was performed as follows: genes expressed in ≤ 3 cells were removed, and cells were excluded if they had < 600 detected genes, > 25% mitochondrial/ribosomal gene expression, or < 300 or > 7000 detected RNA features.To obtain standardized expression profiles, global-scaling normalization was executed via the NormalizeData command. Data normalization was executed using the ‘LogNormalize’ method, where feature counts for each cell are divided by the total counts, scaled by a factor of 10,000, and then log-transformed. We subsequently isolated the top 2,000 highly variable genes via the FindVariableFeatures function. To mitigate batch discrepancies, the Harmony algorithm was applied. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was conducted on these variable features, utilizing the first 42 Principal Components (PCs) for downstream steps. We established cell clusters using FindNeighbors and FindClusters at an optimized resolution, followed by visualization through RunUMAP. Cell identities were assigned based on established canonical markers: endothelial cells (PECAM1, VWF, CLDN5, KDR), T cells (CD3D, CD3E, TRAC, CD8A), epithelial lineages (EPCAM, KRT8, KRT18, KRT14), fibroblasts (COL1A1, DCN, LUM, FAP), and B cells (MS4A1, CD79A, CD79B, BANK1), myeloid populations (LYZ, LST1, C1QA, FCN1), plasma cells (MZB1, JCHAIN, XBP1, SDC1), and pericytes (RGS5, PDGFRB, CSPG4, NOTCH3) were recognized. Finally, cluster-specific differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were computed using the FindMarkers tool.

The copy number variation analysis

CNV inference was performed on tumor-derived epithelial cells via infercnv (v1.24.0)25, using the corresponding normal epithelial cells as a negative reference. The run was configured with denoise = TRUE and a cutoff value of 0.1. All subsequent parameters adhered to the software’s default specifications.

Pseudotime trajectory analysis

Differentiation states were assessed using CytoTRACE226, which facilitated the identification of the starting lineage state (root) for subsequent analysis. Trajectory inference was then performed on the epithelial subpopulations using the Monocle2 algorithm (v2.36.0)27. Specifically, we constructed a CellDataSet by importing raw counts from the Seurat object and modeling the expression data with the negbinomial.size() function. Pre-processing steps included the calculation of size factors (estimateSizeFactors) and gene dispersion values (estimateDispersions). To define the trajectory, ordering genes were curated through a two-step selection strategy. First, genes detected by detectGenes (min_expr = 0.1) and expressed in ≥ 10 cells were retained. Second, these genes were intersected with Seurat highly variable genes (VariableFeatures; n = 2000) to define the final ordering gene set. Dimensionality reduction was performed using reduceDimension with the DDRTree method to reconstruct the trajectory (max_components = 2; num_dim was left as default). Pseudotime was subsequently calculated using orderCells. The pseudotime root was defined according to CytoTRACE2 results. Specifically, the state enriched for ELF5_prog cells, which exhibited the highest developmental potential and represented an earlier stage, was selected as root_state. The pseudotime direction was then adjusted by re-running orderCells(root_state = root_state) without changing the DDRTree topology.

Analysis of cell–cell communication profiles

The CellChat R package (v2.1.2) was used to systematically compare intercellular communication involving Luminal_inflam between normal breast tissues and the TNBC microenvironment28. The NicheNetR package (v2.2.1) was subsequently applied to identify TNBC-specific ligand–receptor interactions associated with Luminal_inflam, thereby enabling a more refined characterization of cell–cell communication29.

Transcription factor analysis

TF-centric regulatory networks and regulon activities in tumor epithelial cells were inferred using the SCENIC workflow implemented in omicverse30,31. Specifically, the cal_grn function was deployed with the argument layer = ‘raw_count’, leveraging the raw expression matrix for robust GRN calculation.Regulons were then identified using the local hg38 rankings feather database in conjunction with the motifs-v10 motif file. Regulon activity was subsequently quantified at single-cell resolution using AUCell.

Cell2location analysis

Deconvolution of cell types within the Visium spatial transcriptomics dataset was achieved using Cell2location32. Five Visium samples were loaded, gene identifiers were converted to SYMBOL format, and mitochondrial genes (MT-) were excluded. A RegressionModel was trained on the single-cell reference dataset (batch_key = sample_id, labels_key = cell_type) using GPU acceleration (max_epochs = 250) to derive cell type–specific reference expression signatures (inf_aver). Genes shared between the spatial dataset and the reference signatures were retained. A Cell2location model was then constructed (batch_key = sample, N_cells_per_location = 30, detection_alpha = 20) and trained on the full dataset (max_epochs = 30,000). Posterior estimates of cell-type abundance per spot were exported using export_posterior, and both the trained model and processed data were saved for downstream analyses.

HdWGCNA analysis

To identify stable gene co-expression clusters and determine their relationship with phenotypic features, we utilized the hdWGCNA approach33. hdWGCNA analysis was performed following the standard workflow. Genes were first selected using SetupForWGCNA (fraction = 0.05). Metacells were constructed using MetacellsByGroups across celltype_fine × sample_id in Harmony space (k = 25, max_shared = 10). The metacell expression matrix was then normalized and scaled, followed by PCA/Harmony integration and UMAP visualization. TestSoftPowers was performed using a signed network. Although a soft power of 1 yielded a high scale-free topology fit, it also retained extremely high network connectivity, indicating an overly dense network. We therefore selected a soft-thresholding power of 12 as the lowest non-trivial value at which the scale-free topology fit had entered a relatively stable plateau while connectivity had decreased substantially. ConstructNetwork was then used to construct the topological overlap matrix (TOM) and identify co-expression modules.Module eigengenes and intramodular connectivity were subsequently computed, and module structure and hub-gene networks were visualized using RunModuleUMAP and HubGeneNetworkPlot.

Machine learning and prognostic modeling

We identified potential markers by cross-referencing the Luminal_inflam profile with the Epi-M1 and Epi-M3 modules from hdWGCNA. Survival-associated genes were filtered via univariate Cox regression on pooled data (TCGA/GSE58812). We then trained seven distinct machine learning classifiers, evaluating their efficacy through ROC and Precision-Recall analysis. The definitive prognostic model was formulated by applying LASSO penalization followed by multivariate Cox modeling34.

Evaluation of risk model efficacy and analysis of immune infiltration profiles

We validated the prognostic accuracy of the 12-gene risk model through time-dependent ROC analysis. Using the median risk score as a cutoff, the cohort was divided into high- and low-risk categories, and differences in overall survival were assessed via the Kaplan–Meier method (log-rank test). Regarding the immune landscape, we utilized CIBERSORT to quantify immune cell infiltration and the ESTIMATE method to calculate tumor purity and immune scores35. Additionally, we compared the expression of immune checkpoints and predicted differential drug responses to identify potential therapeutic options for each risk stratum.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical analysis was conducted on paraffin-embedded sections derived from a cohort of 30 paired tumor specimens and their corresponding adjacent normal tissues. First, samples underwent deparaffinization in xylene and rehydration using descending alcohol concentrations. Epitopes were exposed by heating in a pressure cooker for 20 min. We then blocked endogenous peroxidase activity using 3% H2O2 (10 min, ambient temperature) and non-specific sites using 5% BSA (30 min, 37 °C). Primary antibody incubation was conducted overnight at 4 °C. Following a PBS wash, HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies were applied for 40 min at 37 °C. Immunoreactivity was visualized with DAB for 1–5 min, after which sections were counterstained with hematoxylin, differentiated in acid alcohol, dehydrated, and cleared with xylene. The procedure concluded with neutral resin mounting and microscopic analysis.

Detection of PERK–UPR axis proteins

MDA-MB-231 cells were allocated to Control, LV-sh-NC, LV-sh-RPN1, 4-PBA, and LV-sh-RPN1 + 4-PBA groups. Lentiviral infection lasted 24 h, followed by medium renewal and 48 h of subsequent culture. Treatment with 0.5 μM 4-PBA was conducted for 2 h, introduced either 24 h post-seeding or immediately after transduction in the combination group, preceding the final 48-h incubation. Following treatment, total protein was extracted from harvested cells (1 × 106 per group). The protein levels of ER stress indicators, including total PERK, phosphorylated PERK, ATF4, and CHOP, were determined by Western blot analysis.

Cell viability assessment using the CCK-8 assay

To assess cell viability, logarithmically growing MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded into 96-well plates (5 × 104 cells/mL, 100 μL/well) with five technical replicates. Once cells attached (24 h), they were subjected to the interventions described in “Detection of PERK–UPR axis proteins” section. We then replaced the supernatant with 100 μL of medium supplemented with 10% CCK-8 reagent. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for one hour, after which the optical density at 450 nm was recorded via a microplate reader.

Flow cytometric measurement of intracellular ROS and Ca2+ levels

To quantify intracellular Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS), treated MDA-MB-231 cells were collected and rinsed twice with PBS. The cell density was adjusted to 1 × 106 cells/mL in PBS. A 1 mL volume of this suspension was exposed to DCFH-DA (10 μM final concentration) and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min in the absence of light. Following incubation, the cells underwent two additional PBS washes, were resuspended in 500 μL of buffer, and analyzed via flow cytometry using the FITC channel (Ex: 500 nm; Em: 525 nm).

For the assessment of cytosolic Ca2+ concentration, cells were harvested post-treatment and washed twice with ice-cold PBS. We resuspended the pellets to a concentration of 1 × 106 cells/mL. A 500 μL aliquot was subsequently stained with 1 μL of 10 mM Fluo-3 AM working solution. This reaction proceeded at room temperature for 20 min in the dark. After removing excess dye through washing, cells were resuspended in 500 μL PBS and examined using the same flow cytometric settings (FITC channel).

Evaluation of apoptotic rates and cell cycle kinetics via flow cytometry

To evaluate apoptosis, we pooled the culture supernatant with trypsinized adherent cells to ensure the collection of all apoptotic and necrotic populations. The suspension was spun down at 1000 rpm for 5 min and rinsed two times using chilled PBS. Cells were subsequently suspended in a 500 μL volume of 1 × Binding Buffer, filtered through a 200-mesh strainer, and labeled with 5 μL Annexin V-PE alongside 10 μL 7-AAD. Flow cytometry was performed immediately following a 10-min incubation at 4 °C in the dark. Regarding cell cycle distribution, harvested cells were suspended in 500 μL PBS and fixed in 3.5 mL of cold 80% ethanol overnight at 4 °C. After centrifugation (2000 rpm, 5 min) and two PBS washes, pellets were resuspended in 500 μL PI/RNase staining solution. The samples were filtered (200-mesh), incubated for 30 min at 4 °C without light, and analyzed based on red fluorescence under 488 nm excitation.

Statistical analysis

Statistical data analyses were performed utilizing a suite of software tools, including R(v4.5.1; https://www.r-project.org/), Python (v3.10; https://www.python.org/), SPSS (v19.0; https://www.ibm.com/products/spss-statistics), and GraphPad Prism (v9; https://www.graphpad.com/). Prior to hypothesis testing, continuous variables were evaluated for normal distribution and homogeneity of variance. Variables conforming to Gaussian distribution and homogeneity of variance are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For such datasets, we employed the Student’s t-test to compare two distinct groups, while one-way ANOVA with LSD or Dunnett’s T3 post hoc tests was applied for multiple cohorts. In contrast, data failing normality assumptions are summarized using medians and Interquartile Ranges (IQR). Inference for these skewed distributions relied on non-parametric techniques, including Mann–Whitney U, Wilcoxon signed-rank, or Kruskal–Wallis tests with Bonferroni adjustment. Bivariate associations were evaluated via Pearson or Spearman correlation indices. All calculated P-values were two-tailed, with statistical significance established at 0.05.

Identification of epithelial subpopulations and assessment of malignant features

Epithelial cells were extracted for secondary clustering and annotation, resulting in the identification of five epithelial subpopulations (Fig. 2A; Fig. S1C–D), including Luminal_secretory, Luminal_inflam, ELF5_prog, Basal_myo, and IEG_stress (Fig. 2B). Marker gene expression and functional enrichment analyses revealed distinct transcriptional programs across these subpopulations. In ELF5_prog, BASP1 and related genes were highly expressed, and enriched terms were predominantly linked to ribosome biogenesis and cytoplasmic translation, indicative of elevated protein synthesis capacity. Basal_myo was characterized by prominent expression of MALAT1 and enrichment in wound healing, actin filament bundle assembly/organization, and myoid development, suggesting active cytoskeletal remodeling and contractility–migration programs consistent with a myofibroblast-like phenotype. IEG_stress showed high expression of genes such as RABAC1 and was enriched for hypoxia response and chaperone-mediated protein folding/refolding, together with negative regulation of death receptor–mediated extrinsic apoptotic signaling, reflecting enhanced stress adaptation and survival programs under hypoxic conditions. Notably, differentially expressed genes between Luminal_inflam and Luminal_secretory were significantly enriched in mitosis-related processes, including chromosome/sister chromatid segregation and nuclear division, as well as cell-cycle checkpoint signaling, indicating strong cell cycle– and division-associated transcriptional activity in these luminal epithelial states (Fig. 2E). To further delineate malignant epithelial populations, copy number variation (CNV) profiles were inferred in TNBC-derived epithelial cells using normal epithelial cells as the reference. Differentiation potential and pseudotime trajectory place Luminal_inflam at a terminal state

We used CytoTRACE2 to quantitatively assess the differentiation potential of TNBC epithelial cells. Among the five epithelial subpopulations, ELF5_prog exhibited the highest potency score, whereas Luminal_inflam showed the lowest, indicating that ELF5_prog is closer to the lineage origin and Luminal_inflam represents a low-potency state toward the terminal end of the trajectory (Fig. 3A). Monocle2-based pseudotime analysis further designated ELF5_prog as the root node, and the inferred trajectory consistently positioned Luminal_inflam at the terminal end of pseudotime, in line with its higher CNV features (Fig. 3B–C). To elucidate the molecular drivers at the primary trajectory bifurcation (intersection of states 1, 2, and 3), we utilized Branched Expression Analysis Modeling (BEAM). This analysis revealed a progressive upregulation of LBHD2 and NOS1AP during the lineage progression from ELF5_prog to Luminal_inflam. Furthermore, functional enrichment assays underscored a significant association with mitotic processes, including chromosome segregation and nuclear division (Fig. 3D). Collectively, these multi-level lines of evidence consistently pointed to S100A4–EGFR as a prioritized signaling axis, providing a key entry point for subsequent mechanistic validation. Hub regulons and spatial colocalization define the Luminal_inflam state

Transcription factor regulatory analysis showed that MYBL2(+), TFDP1(+), CEBPD(+), and MBD2(+) exhibited higher regulatory activity/expression levels in the Luminal_inflam subpopulation (Fig. 6A–C). The distribution of regulon activity calculated by AUCell revealed pronounced heterogeneity in AUC values for MYBL2(+), CEBPD(+), TFDP1(+), and MBD2(+) across the cell population (Fig. 6D). Further GRN network topology analysis indicated that these factors occupied highly connected hub positions and formed tightly coupled regulatory modules with downstream target genes, suggesting that they may serve as key upstream nodes driving Luminal_inflam-specific transcriptional programs (Fig. 6E). To validate the spatial presence of single-cell–defined Luminal_inflam and its microenvironmental architecture, we performed cell2location-based deconvolution on spatial transcriptomics data from five TNBC samples and projected the abundance of each cell type onto tissue spatial coordinates. Construction of a machine learning–based prognostic signature from Luminal_inflam-associated genes

To determine the prognostic significance of Luminal_inflam and define a robust gene set, we initially identified 1,646 overlapping genes by crossing the Luminal_inflam signature with the Epi-M1/Epi-M3 modules (Fig. 8A). Using a merged cohort of 220 patients (TCGA and GSE58812), we applied univariate Cox regression to screen this pool, isolating 150 candidates with significant survival correlations (Fig. 8B). These genes served as the input for seven distinct machine learning algorithms. We comprehensively assessed the models’ discriminatory power and stability via ROC/PR curves and residual analysis (Fig. 8C–F). Support Vector Machine (SVM), LASSO, and Random Forest (RF) emerged as the top performers, exhibiting superior AUC metrics (Fig. 8C–F). Consequently, we extracted the top 60 pivotal features from each of these three optimal models and intersected them to generate a “consensus” candidate list (Fig. 8G). Prognostic stratification by the 12-gene risk model and its associations with immune phenotypes and drug sensitivity

Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated strong discriminatory capability in the training cohort, yielding an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.899 (Fig. 9A). Additionally, time-dependent ROC curves verified the model’s consistent predictive stability at 1, 3, and 5 years (Fig. 9B). RPN1 knockdown activates PERK–UPR and drives cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis via Ca2+/ROS dysregulation

Three lentiviral shRNA vectors were designed to target RPN1, followed by the establishment of stably transduced MDA-MB-231 populations to support subsequent analyses. Evaluation by qRT–PCR revealed that each shRNA effectively downregulated RPN1 transcripts compared to both the blank control and the negative control (LV-sh-NC). Among them, LV-sh-RPN1-3 displayed superior inhibitory efficiency (Fig. 11A) and was selected as the optimal candidate for further study. Consistent with the mRNA data, Western blotting confirmed a distinct reduction in RPN1 protein synthesis by LV-sh-RPN1-3 (Fig. 11B–C). We subsequently explored the influence of RPN1 depletion on the ER stress response by monitoring key elements of the PERK–UPR pathway. Our data showed that levels of PERK, p-PERK, ATF4, and CHOP were significantly increased in the RPN1-knockdown cells. However, this activation was reversible; treatment with 4-PBA, an inhibitor of ER stress, effectively suppressed these signaling molecules, resulting in significantly lower expression levels in the combined treatment group (LV-sh-RPN1 + 4-PBA) relative to the knockdown-only group (Fig. 11D–E).Functionally, the CCK-8 assay showed that RPN1 knockdown significantly reduced cell viability, whereas 4-PBA alone increased cell viability. Notably, 4-PBA treatment partially rescued the suppressed viability phenotype under RPN1 knockdown conditions (Fig. 11F). These results indicate that RPN1 loss activates the PERK–ATF4–CHOP–mediated ER stress response and concomitantly suppresses proliferative capacity, and that this effect is partially reversible upon 4-PBA intervention. As ER stress is known to perturb intracellular Ca2+ homeostasis and elevate ROS production, we next quantified cytosolic Ca2+ and ROS levels by flow cytometry18,36. Fluo-4 staining showed that RPN1 knockdown significantly increased cytosolic Ca2+ levels, whereas 4-PBA alone reduced Ca2+ levels; combined treatment also partially attenuated the Ca2+ elevation (Fig. 11H). Consistently, DCFH-DA staining demonstrated that RPN1 knockdown markedly increased ROS fluorescence intensity, while 4-PBA substantially decreased ROS levels and partially reversed the elevation in the combination group (Fig. 11G). Discussion

TNBC is distinguished by its rapid progression and highly aggressive biological behavior. Compared to other breast cancer subtypes, it is associated with significantly inferior survival rates, primarily attributable to the limited repertoire of specific therapeutic agents available for this disease37,38. In this study, single-cell transcriptomics was used to identify a malignant epithelial subpopulation in TNBC, termed Luminal_inflam, and to systematically characterize its functional relevance within the tumor microenvironment. The presence of Luminal_inflam was further supported by spatial transcriptomic validation.

NicheNet analysis revealed that non-epithelial cells such as fibroblasts may regulate the expression of Luminal_inflam-associated target genes via the S100A4–EGFR axis, consistent with findings by Klingelhöfer et al., who showed that extracellular S100A4 can activate EGFR/ErbB2 and ERK1/2 phosphorylation and enhance amphiregulin (AREG)-mediated cell proliferation. Maria Laggner and colleagues also reported that extracellular S100A4 can function as a cytokine-like molecule that cooperates with multiple ligands and can engage EGFR signaling39,40. These observations suggest that, within the TNBC tumor microenvironment, non-tumor cells such as fibroblasts may promote malignant tumor progression through the S100A4–EGFR ligand–receptor pair. In addition, transcription factor analysis indicated that transcription factors such as MYBL2(+), TFDP1(+), CEBPD(+), and MBD2(+) were markedly upregulated in malignant epithelial subpopulations such as Luminal_inflam. MYBL2 is a canonical driver of proliferation and cell-cycle progression, and its upregulation has been linked to enhanced proliferation, therapeutic resistance, and metastasis across multiple cancer types, making it a core regulator within the cell-cycle transcriptional network41. Activation of TFDP1 and CEBPD is more consistent with inflammatory and hypoxia-adaptive programs and pro-tumor microenvironmental remodeling features42,43. MBD2, as an epigenetic reader, can promote tumor progression through EMT/metastasis-related programs, and its causal role has been validated in breast cancer models44.

To evaluate the prognostic relevance of Luminal_inflam, we used hdWGCNA to define its associated gene modules and constructed a TNBC prognostic model by integrating multiple machine-learning approaches. Among the 12 genes retained in the final signature, RPN1 drew our attention because of its high hazard ratio. RPN1 is a type I transmembrane component of the rough endoplasmic reticulum and an essential subunit of the oligosaccharyltransferase complex that supports N-linked glycosylation of nascent proteins45,46.Previous studies have shown that RPN1 is upregulated in multiple malignancies and may contribute to adverse clinical outcomes47,48. In TNBC, RPN1 has also been implicated in PD-L1 glycosylation and stability, linking this molecule to immune evasion and tumor progression49. Importantly, however, the pro-survival role of RPN1 in breast cancer is not entirely unprecedented. Ding et al. previously reported that RPN1 knockdown in TNBC-relevant breast cancer models induced endoplasmic reticulum stress, accompanied by upregulation of PERK, IRE1α, ATF6, and BiP, and that the apoptotic phenotype could be partially alleviated by 4-PBA50. Our findings are therefore concordant with, rather than independent of, this prior work. The advance of the present study lies in placing RPN1 within a TNBC-specific malignant epithelial program defined by integrated single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, linking it to the Luminal_inflam state and to a 12-gene high-risk signature with prognostic relevance.

Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress is defined as a perturbation in proteostasis arising when the influx of nascent proteins overwhelms the organelle’s folding machinery, causing the aggregation of misfolded species within the lumen51. To counteract this imbalance, the cell initiates the Unfolded Protein Response (UPR). This adaptive network is orchestrated by three transmembrane sensors—PERK, IRE1α, and ATF6—which synergize to attenuate global protein synthesis while simultaneously upregulating molecular chaperones and ER-associated degradation (ERAD) pathways. However, should the stress prove irresolvable or chronic, the UPR machinery transitions from a cytoprotective mode to a pro-apoptotic state, ultimately executing programmed cell death52. In our MDA-MB-231 model, RPN1 knockdown was associated with reduced cell viability, which could be partially rescued by 4-PBA. We further found that RPN1 depletion was accompanied by increased intracellular ROS and Ca2+ levels, altered cell-cycle distribution, and elevated apoptosis, and that these changes were also partially reversible upon 4-PBA treatment. These data support the view that loss of RPN1 perturbs ER homeostasis and is linked to stress-associated cell fate changes in TNBC cells. At the same time, these findings should be interpreted in the context of prior work showing that RPN1 depletion can induce ER-stress-dependent apoptosis in breast cancer models. Thus, rather than establishing an entirely new mechanism, our study extends previous observations by connecting this stress-related phenotype to a specific malignant epithelial program and a clinically derived risk framework.

Our findings should also be interpreted in the context of the two previous studies from which the single-cell and spatial datasets were derived. The GSE161529 study by Pal et al. established a large single-cell atlas of normal, preneoplastic, and tumorigenic human breast tissue and highlighted broad epithelial and microenvironmental diversity across breast cancer subtypes, including the presence of cycling tumor-cell subsets19. The GSE210616 study by Bassiouni et al. defined a conserved spatial architecture in TNBC across a diverse patient cohort and emphasized the spatial organization of tumor and stromal compartments22. Building on these foundational datasets, the present study did not aim to redefine the original atlases, but rather to integrate them in a TNBC-focused framework that prioritizes malignant epithelial states with clinical relevance. In this context, our main additions are the identification of the Luminal_inflam state as a high-risk malignant epithelial program, the integration of candidate microenvironmental and transcriptional features linked to this state, and the derivation of a clinically relevant 12-gene prognostic signature followed by functional prioritization of RPN1.

In conclusion, by integrating single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial transcriptomic validation, and machine-learning analyses, this study identified a Luminal_inflam epithelial state with highly malignant features in TNBC and derived a clinically relevant 12-gene prognostic signature. These analytical layers can be interpreted within a common framework centered on the Luminal_inflam malignant state, in which the fibroblast-associated S100A4–EGFR axis represents a candidate microenvironmental context, MYBL2, TFDP1, CEBPD, and MBD2 reflect core internal transcriptional features, and the hdWGCNA modules together with the 12-gene signature capture downstream transcriptional outputs with clinical relevance. Functional experiments in MDA-MB-231 cells further supported that RPN1 depletion perturbs ER homeostasis and is associated with ROS/Ca2+ imbalance, altered cell-cycle distribution, and apoptosis. At the same time, several limitations should be acknowledged. The direct functional links among the upstream signaling context, the transcriptional regulatory layer, and the prognostic output were not experimentally established in the present study, and the selection of RPN1 was primarily based on its prominence within the prognostic framework rather than on validated positioning as the central connector across these layers. In addition, the in vitro validation was performed in a single TNBC cell line, and additional studies in independent models will be required to assess the robustness and generalizability of this phenotype. Thus, rather than defining a complete mechanistic chain, the present work provides an integrative framework linking a malignant epithelial program to clinically relevant risk stratification and nominates RPN1-associated proteostasis vulnerability as a candidate direction for further investigation in TNBC.
