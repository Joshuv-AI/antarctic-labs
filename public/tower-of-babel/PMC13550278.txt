Bibliometric analysis and knowledge mapping of thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor
Authors: Yujun Lin, Di Wu, Lujing Jiang, Qin Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor (SMARCA4-UT) is a rare and highly aggressive malignancy with poor prognosis and no established standard therapy. This study aimed to map the research landscape of SMARCA4-UT through bibliometric analysis to guide clinical decision-making and research prioritization.MethodsPublications on thoracic SMARCA4-UT were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus from 2000 to 2025. After screening, 132 articles and reviews were included. Bibliometrix and CiteSpace were used to analyze publication trends, geographic and institutional distribution, author productivity, collaboration networks, keyword evolution, thematic structure, and highly cited articles.ResultsSMARCA4-UT research expanded rapidly, with 31.95% annual growth rate and 32.25 average citations per article, reflecting rising academic impact. China (28.8%) and Japan (23.5%) led global output, forming a China–US dual-core collaboration network. Mechanistic studies on the SWI/SNF complex and BRG1 are mature, while clinical and therapeutic research remains underdeveloped. Temporal analysis revealed a shift toward diagnostic standardization after the 2021 WHO classification.ConclusionsSMARCA4-UT research is in a rapid growth phase, with a substantial gap between mechanistic advances and clinical translation. Immunochemotherapy is the current first-line backbone, while targeted and combinatorial strategies remain exploratory. These findings provide evidence-based guidance for clinical practice and research design, highlighting the urgent need for biomarker-driven therapy and large-scale international collaboration to improve patient outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s12672-026-05504-0.

Introduction

SMARCA4 (BRG1), a core component of the SWItch/sucrose non-fermentable (SWI/SNF) chromatin remodeling complex, functions as a critical tumor suppressor through pRb-mediated cell cycle regulation [1–3]. Its dysregulation occurs across diverse human cancers [4], but the recognition of thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor (SMARCA4-UT) as a distinct clinicopathological entity is recent. First described in 2015 as “SMARCA4-deficient thoracic sarcomas” [5–7]. Subsequent research by Rekhtman et al. reclassified these as smoking-associated undifferentiated carcinomas, leading to formal designation in the 2021 WHO Classification [8–12].

SMARCA4-UT presents significant clinical challenges, predominantly affecting middle-aged male heavy smokers with large, aggressive intrathoracic masses and dismal median survival of 4–7 months [5, 7]. Diagnosis relies on combined undifferentiated morphology and BRG1 loss by immunohistochemistry (IHC) [13–18]. Morphological assessment serves as the foundational step, revealing a dedifferentiated or undifferentiated cellular phenotype that lacks typical epithelial architecture, such as glandular or squamous differentiation. Histologically, these tumors present as diffuse sheets of large, undifferentiated cells—frequently exhibiting rhabdoid morphology—and demonstrate weak or absent keratin expression [5–7]. IHC is crucial for definitive diagnosis, which is established by the characteristic loss of BRG1 protein expression. The frequent co-loss of BRM (SMARCA2) serves as a valuable diagnostic clue. Supporting IHC findings may include expression of stem cell markers (e.g., CD34, SOX2, SALL4), while epithelial markers such as claudin-4 are typically negative or only focally positive [19]. Finally, molecular profiling shows truncating SMARCA4 mutations providing genetic evidence to support the diagnosis and rule out other entities. Various molecular techniques, including Sanger sequencing (Sanger-seq) [5, 20], fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) [8], and next-generation sequencing (NGS), encompassing both RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) [5, 21] and targeted NGS [7], have been utilized to diagnose SMARCA4-UT and characterize its genetic alterations. Each method has significant limitations; FISH detects only 35% of alterations due to prevalent copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity, while NGS can miss 12.5% of cases, and IHC sometimes shows reduced rather than negative expression [8]. Therefore, a combination of NGS and IHC is recommended for accurate diagnosis to overcome the individual shortcomings of each technique. This composite diagnostic profile is essential for differentiating SMARCA4-UT from SMARCA4-deficient non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC), which typically manifests as conventional adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma with retained epithelial features [22].

Therapeutic management remains problematic with no established standard of care. The tumor is characterized by aggressive biological behavior [5–8], with most patients presenting at advanced stages and a poor prognosis [11, 23]. Even patients who undergo surgery have a high recurrence risk; therefore, surgical intervention may be considered primarily for Stage I patients [24]. Beyond surgery, SMARCA4-UT demonstrates marked resistance to conventional chemotherapy and radiotherapy [25–27]. ICI efficacy in SMARCA4-UT is highly variable. Exceptional responses occur in some patients, but many derive no benefit due to an immune-desert tumor microenvironment lacking T-cell infiltration [11, 28–33]. Response variability is influenced by biomarker interplay: high TMB may predict benefit, while STK11/KEAP1 co-mutations are associated with primary resistance [33]. This therapeutic void has spurred extensive preclinical efforts to identify synthetic lethal vulnerabilities. Promising strategies include targeting epigenetic regulators, such as Enhancer of Zeste Homolog 2 (EZH2) inhibitors [34, 35] and Bromodomain and Extra-Terminal domain protein (BET) inhibitors [36]; exploiting cell cycle and DNA damage vulnerabilities with Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors [37, 38], Ataxia Telangiectasia and Rad3-related protein kinase (ATR) inhibitors [39, 40], and Poly(ADP-ribose) Polymerase (PARP) inhibitors [41]; and targeting metabolic dependencies such as Oxidative Phosphorylation (OXPHOS) inhibition [42, 43]. Aurora Kinase A (AURKA) inhibitors [44], KRASG12C inhibitors [8, 30] and immune microenvironment regulators ZG16B [45] also represent active areas of investigation.” However, these strategies are predominantly in the preclinical or early clinical trial phase, and no robust clinical guidelines currently exist [46].

SMARCA4-UT research remains fragmented, constrained by small sample sizes and retrospective designs. Critical knowledge gaps persist in molecular heterogeneity, immunotherapy response determinants, and therapy resistance mechanisms [47]. The tumor’s recent 2021 WHO classification underscores unmet needs: absence of dedicated clinical trials, predictive biomarkers, and standardized diagnostic criteria. Translating preclinical vulnerabilities—targeting CDK4/6, EZH2, or ATR pathways—into clinical applications remains largely unexplored. Recent reviews [19, 22, 46, 48–51] emphasized that SMARCA4-UT represents a paradigm of epigenetically driven malignancies with immune-metabolic interdependence, yet quantitative mapping of global research dynamics remains absent.

Bibliometric analysis provides a robust quantitative framework for evaluating research trends [52, 53]. widely applied across medical fields including oncology [54], inflammation [55], autoimmune disease [56], genetic disease [57]. This study of SMARCA4-UT aims to: (1) quantify research output growth and impact; (2) identify leading contributors and institutions; (3) visualize collaboration networks; (4) map thematic evolution and emerging hotspots; and (5) provide a comprehensive field overview. Our findings offer strategic insights to guide future research directions and accelerate progress against this aggressive malignancy.

Materials and methods

Data source and search strategy

A comprehensive literature search was performed in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and Scopus. The search period was restricted to publications indexed between January 1, 2000, and December 30, 2025. To improve disease specificity, the search strategy prioritized terms directly related to thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor (SMARCA4-UT), while retaining broader supplementary terms to capture earlier or inconsistently indexed publications. The WoSCC search strategy was as follows: TS = (“SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor” OR “SMARCA4 deficient undifferentiated tumor” OR “SMARCA4-UT” OR ((“SMARCA4” OR “BRG1”) AND (“undifferentiated tumor” OR sarcoma OR sarcomatoid) AND (thoracic OR lung OR pulmonary))). The Scopus search strategy was: TITLE-ABS-KEY (“SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor” OR “SMARCA4 deficient undifferentiated tumor” OR “SMARCA4-UT” OR ((“SMARCA4” OR “BRG1”) AND (“undifferentiated tumor” OR sarcoma OR sarcomatoid) AND (thoracic OR lung OR pulmonary))). The searches identified 205 records in WoSCC and 252 records in Scopus. After restricting the results to English-language articles and reviews, 160 WoSCC records and 215 Scopus records were retained. These records were imported into Zotero for merging and deduplication, yielding 246 unique records after removal of 129 duplicates. Manual screening was then performed by two independent investigators using predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, with disagreements resolved by discussion or consultation with a third investigator. Studies were included if they primarily focused on thoracic SMARCA4-UT, including its clinicopathological characteristics, molecular features, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, or related mechanisms. Studies limited to SMARCA4-mutant NSCLC, SCLC, other non-SMARCA4-UT malignancies, or general SWI/SNF/BRG1 biology without direct relevance to thoracic SMARCA4-UT were excluded. Finally, 132 eligible English-language publications were included in the bibliometric analysis, comprising 129 records indexed in WoSCC and 3 additional records retrieved from Scopus (Fig. 1).

Data extraction and analysis

All eligible records were exported in plain text format from WOSCC and Scopus for bibliometric analysis. The bibliometrix R package (version 4.1.3) was employed to analyze research landscape evolution, annual publication trends, author productivity, and strategic theme mapping. CiteSpace (version 6.1.R6) was utilized for constructing collaboration networks, timeline visualizations, keyword burst detection, and interdisciplinary knowledge flow analysis.

Geographic distribution mapping was performed using R language with specialized geospatial coding. Graphpad Pism 9.0 provided data organization and supplementary visualization support. The literature screening flowchart was created using Microsoft PowerPoint.

Descriptive statistics summarized publication outputs, citation patterns, and authorship metrics. Keyword co-occurrence and thematic evolution analyses identified emerging research trends. All analyses underwent rigorous quality control, including removal of duplicate records and manual validation to ensure accuracy.

Results

Main information of thoracic SMARCA4-UT research

A total of 132 publications on thoracic SMARCA4-UT published between 2015 and 2025 were identified across 78 sources, with an annual growth rate of 31.95%, indicating rapidly increasing research interest. These studies involved 845 authors, with an average of 7.23 co-authors per document and only 5 single-authored papers, reflecting a highly collaborative research pattern; international co-authorship accounted for 7.576%. The publications cited 2,106 references, with a mean document age of 3.19 years and an average of 32.25 citations per document, suggesting notable academic impact. In total, 281 authors’ keywords were identified, highlighting the diversity of research themes in this field.

Distribution by publication year

Figure 2 shows the temporal trend of thoracic SMARCA4-UT research. Annual publications rose from 2 in 2015 to 32 in 2025, with a sharp increase after 2020 and a notable surge in 2023 (24 articles). Early output was limited (2016–2018: 0–3 per year), followed by steady growth since 2019. Citation impact was higher for earlier studies (264.0 in 2015, 125.3 in 2017) but declined in recent years (7.3 in 2024, 1.0 in 2025), reflecting citation lag. Publications from 2022 also showed relatively high citations (117.1). Overall, research activity has accelerated rapidly.

Figure 3 illustrates the global distribution of publications on thoracic SMARCA4-UT according to country scientific production. A full counting approach was applied, in which each publication was assigned to all contributing countries; therefore, the counts represent national participation rather than mutually exclusive publication totals. Based on the updated dataset, research output remains concentrated in a limited number of countries. Japan ranked second with 31 publications, accounting for 23.5%, followed by the United States with 23 publications, accounting for 17.4%. Other countries contributed fewer studies, including India with 5 publications, Italy with 4 publications, and England with 3 publications. Austria, South Korea, Canada, and Australia each contributed 2 publications. Germany, Singapore, Tunisia, Turkiye, Morocco, Switzerland, South Africa, Spain, Belgium, and Scotland each contributed 1 publication. Overall, the geographical distribution suggests that research on thoracic SMARCA4-UT is primarily driven by countries in East Asia, North America, and Western Europe, while contributions from other regions remain relatively limited.

Distribution by keyword over time

Figure 7 presents the top 25 keywords with the strongest citation bursts, outlining the temporal evolution of research hotspots in thoracic SMARCA4-UT from 2015 to 2025. Citation bursts reflect periods during which specific topics received heightened scholarly attention, thereby highlighting emerging trends and shifting research priorities.

In the early stage (2015–2018), burst keywords such as “epithelioid sarcoma” (strength = 2.22), “hypercalcemic type” (1.06), and “rhabdoid cells” (1.24) dominated, indicating that initial studies focused on clinicopathological characterization and histological features of SMARCA4-related tumors. The presence of “small cell carcinoma” and early mentions of “SMARCA4-deficient thoracic sarcoma” (burst beginning in 2017, strength = 2.91) further reflects efforts to define and differentiate this rare entity.

During the intermediate phase (2018–2021), research attention shifted toward molecular mechanisms and functional alterations. Keywords such as “BAF complex,” “SWI/SNF complex,” “inactivation,” “inhibition,” and “BRG1” exhibited notable bursts, highlighting increasing interest in chromatin remodeling and gene inactivation pathways. Concurrently, more general pathological terms such as “cell carcinoma,” “deficiency,” and “thoracic sarcoma” (strength = 3.13) gained prominence, suggesting ongoing refinement of disease classification and diagnostic criteria.

In the most recent period (2021–2025), emerging bursts indicate a transition toward therapeutic exploration and updated disease concepts. Keywords including “immunotherapy” (1.18), “adenocarcinoma” (1.51), and “expression” (0.53) reflect expanding interest in treatment strategies and molecular profiling. Notably, terms such as “SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor” (2.85), “undifferentiated tumor” (1.41), “thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor” (1.41), and “sarcoma” (2.56) show strong and ongoing bursts through 2025, indicating a recent shift toward reclassification and consensus on disease nomenclature. Additionally, the late emergence of “EZH2” suggests growing attention to potential targeted therapies.

Overall, the burst analysis reveals a clear progression from early histopathological recognition to mechanistic insights and, more recently, to therapeutic strategies and refined disease classification, underscoring the dynamic and evolving nature of thoracic SMARCA4-UT research over the past decade.

Strategic theme mapping: density-centrality analysis

Figure 9 presents the strategic thematic map of thoracic SMARCA4-UT research, which characterizes the conceptual structure of the field by integrating thematic centrality (x-axis, representing relevance) and density (y-axis, representing development). Themes are distributed across four quadrants that reflect their relative importance and maturity within the research domain.

The quadrant boundaries are defined using the median values of centrality and density across all identified themes, as commonly implemented in bibliometrix-based analyses. The quadrant boundaries were defined using the median values of Callon’s centrality (5.385) and density (72.872), calculated from the thematic clustering results.Thus, themes located to the right of the vertical dashed line exhibit above-median centrality (high relevance), while those above the horizontal dashed line show above-median density (high development).

In the upper-right quadrant (motor themes), well-developed and highly relevant topics dominate the field. Notably, the cluster centered on “BRG1,” “SWI/SNF complex,” and “expression” is positioned in this region, indicating that molecular mechanisms related to chromatin remodeling represent a core and mature driving force in thoracic SMARCA4-UT research.

The upper-left quadrant (niche themes) includes highly developed but less central topics, such as “lung,” “thoracic,” and “cancer,” as well as themes related to “chromatin-remodeling complex,” “epigenetics,” and “identification.” These topics are internally cohesive but relatively specialized, with limited connectivity to the broader research network.

In the lower-right quadrant (basic themes), clusters including “SMARCA4,” “sarcoma,” and “carcinoma,” along with “immunotherapy,” “lung cancer,” and “chemotherapy,” display high centrality but relatively low density. This suggests that these themes are fundamental and widely connected across the field but remain underdeveloped, representing essential yet still evolving research directions.

The lower-left quadrant (emerging or declining themes) contains topics such as “SMARCA4-deficient thoracic sarcoma,” “SWI,” and “BRM,” which exhibit both low centrality and density. These themes may represent either emerging areas of investigation or topics that are gradually losing prominence.

Overall, the thematic structure indicates that thoracic SMARCA4-UT research is driven primarily by molecular mechanisms of the SWI/SNF complex, supported by core pathological entities such as SMARCA4-related sarcomas and carcinomas, while therapeutic strategies and specific disease classifications are still in the process of development and consolidation.

Discussion

Major findings and research landscape

This study provides the first comprehensive bibliometric overview of thoracic SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumor (SMARCA4-UT). Bibliometric evidence clearly places SMARCA4-UT research in the growth phase of its lifecycle, supported by a 31.95% annual growth rate, exponential publication growth since 2020, sustained keyword bursts, and an early-stage clinical research landscape. Two core contradictions define the field: mechanistic research is relatively mature, yet clinical translation lags substantially; China leads in publication volume, but exhibits weak international collaboration and limited high-impact output. Consistent with this imbalance, mechanistic studies are well-established while clinical applications remain nascent, reflecting a rapidly expanding but immature field with rising academic influence, as indicated by an average of 32.25 citations per article. Geographically, research is dominated by China (28.8%) and Japan (23.5%), consistent with the concentrated expertise typical of rare diseases. The dual-core collaboration network led by China and the United States, with an international co-authorship rate of only 7.58%, further highlights the urgent need for global cooperation to overcome limitations in case availability and data sharing.

At the conceptual level, the field is characterized by a stable mechanistic foundation centered on the SWI/SNF complex and BRG1, alongside comparatively underdeveloped clinical and therapeutic domains. This imbalance suggests that, despite substantial progress in understanding the molecular basis of SMARCA4 deficiency, translation into clinical application remains limited. Collectively, these findings position SMARCA4-UT research as an emerging but still maturing field, with a clear need to bridge mechanistic insights and clinical practice.

Evolution of research topic

The temporal evolution of SMARCA4-UT research reflects a progressive shift from basic mechanisms to clinical application. Early studies focused primarily on chromatin remodeling [58], SWI/SNF complex dysfunction, and tumor suppressor mechanisms [6, 7]. establishing the biological foundation of the disease. Between 2018 and 2020, research emphasis moved toward clinicopathological characterization and diagnostic refinement, particularly in differentiating SMARCA4-UT from morphologically similar entities [8]. Since 2021, the introduction of standardized terminology, especially following the WHO classification, has marked a transition toward diagnostic consolidation and improved disease recognition [12]. This shift underscores the critical role of classification systems in shaping research direction in rare malignancies.

From a thematic perspective, mechanistic research—represented by the SWI/SNF complex and BRG1—remains central, supporting ongoing efforts to identify therapeutic vulnerabilities. Meanwhile, clinically oriented topics, including SMARCA4-related tumors and immunotherapy, are gaining prominence but remain relatively underdeveloped.

Basic themes such as “SMARCA4,” “sarcoma,” and “carcinoma,” together with therapeutic strategies including immunotherapy [61, 69–72], and chemoimmunotherapy [26, 69, 70, 73–75] reflect increasing clinical awareness but also highlight unresolved questions regarding treatment response, resistance mechanisms, and tumor microenvironment interactions.

In addition, the dynamic behavior of emerging and declining keywords—such as “SMARCA4-deficient thoracic sarcoma,” “SWI,” and “BRM”—suggests ongoing refinement of disease classification and research priorities.

Overall, these patterns indicate a gradual transition from mechanistic exploration to translational research. However, a substantial gap remains in converting molecular insights into effective and standardized therapeutic strategies.

Therapeutic landscape

Thoracic SMARCA4-UT is a rare, highly aggressive malignancy with no standardized treatment and poor prognosis. Conventional chemotherapy and radiotherapy offer limited efficacy and poor disease control, representing an unmet clinical need [11, 23, 73, 76].

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) monotherapy shows inconsistent activity. Case reports have demonstrated rapid responses to agents such as tislelizumab [71], pembrolizumab [31, 77] or nivolumab [29, 78]. In addition, combination immunotherapy (e.g., ipilimumab plus nivolumab or pembrolizumab regimens) has also shown encouraging activity in individual cases [30, 72]. A retrospective study involving 18 patients reported an objective response rate (ORR) of 42%, with improved efficacy observed in first-line ICI treatment and in patients with sarcomatoid features [61]. However, the efficacy of ICI monotherapy remains inconsistent: PD-L1 and TMB are unreliable predictors, and median progression-free survival is limited (2.4 months), with frequent early progression [61].

By contrast, immunochemotherapy has emerged as the preferred first-line backbone. Retrospective data demonstrate superior PFS compared with chemotherapy alone (26.8 vs. 2.73 months), with consistent benefits across molecular subgroups [25, 69, 70, 74, 75, 79–81].

Combination strategies incorporating immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) with anti-angiogenic agents or radiotherapy have demonstrated potential synergistic antitumor effects in selected patient populations, including those with immunocompromised status or intermediate PD-L1 expression [82]. in a retrospective cohort, this regimen yielded significantly longer OS (not reached vs. 21.67 months; p = 0.02) than chemoimmunotherapy, chemotherapy plus anti-angiogenics, or chemotherapy alone [83],

Triple therapy combining immunotherapy, chemotherapy, and anti-angiogenics has shown encouraging activity even in PD-L1-negative patients, supporting broader applicability [84]. Similarly, in three patients with SMARCA4-deficient thoracic sarcoma treated with the first-line ABCP regimen (atezolizumab, bevacizumab, paclitaxel, and carboplatin), all achieved partial response, with a median PFS of over 6 months and one patient maintaining remission for more than one year [25].

In addition to combination immunotherapy strategies, targeted therapy has emerged as a promising research direction for SMARCA4-deficient thoracic tumors, particularly in patients with specific molecular alterations or those refractory to conventional treatment. Current approaches mainly focus on targeting SWI/SNF-related vulnerabilities, including SMARCA4/2 inhibition, small molecules targeting SWI/SNF bromodomains, and proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs) [66], as well as BET, HDAC, and CDK4/6 inhibitors [64, 85]. Although ALK fusions are generally considered rare in SMARCA4-deficient undifferentiated tumors (SMARCA4-UT), a case report [86] described a nonsmoking female patient harboring concurrent ALK rearrangement and SMARCA4 deficiency who achieved marked tumor regression with the ALK inhibitor alectinib, highlighting the importance of comprehensive molecular profiling. Epigenetic therapies, particularly EZH2 inhibition, have also shown potential [87] Although objective responses were not achieved, prolonged stable disease was observed in 25% of refractory pediatric tumors, including one case with SMARCA4 loss. In addition, emerging evidence from broader oncological research suggests that tumor-associated secretory factors and immune microenvironment regulators, such as ZG16B [71], may represent novel therapeutic targets. Nevertheless, several challenges remain. Most SWI/SNF-targeted strategies are still in the preclinical stage, actionable alterations such as ALK fusions are rare, and EZH2 inhibitors have shown limited efficacy in inducing objective responses.

In summary, immunochemotherapy is the current first-line standard, while anti-angiogenic and triple combinations provide synergistic benefits. Targeted and epigenetic approaches remain exploratory. Future efforts should focus on optimizing combination regimens, identifying predictive biomarkers, and validating novel agents in prospective trials.

Future directions

Future research on SMARCA4-UT should prioritize three core directions, which are expected to dominate the field over the next 3–5 years. First, large-scale, multi-institutional prospective and real-world studies are essential to validate current combination regimens and establish standardized treatment guidelines. Second, dissecting immune resistance mechanisms and identifying reliable predictive biomarkers—including TMB, STK11/KEAP1 co-mutations, and immune microenvironment profiling—will be critical to optimize patient selection and overcome immunotherapy failure. Third, exploring SWI/SNF-targeted agents (e.g., EZH2, BET, or CDK4/6 inhibitors) combined with immunotherapy represents a promising strategy to enhance antitumor efficacy and achieve personalized treatment. These focused efforts will help bridge the gap between mechanistic insights and clinical practice, ultimately improving long-term outcomes for patients with this aggressive disease.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, although both WoSCC and Scopus were searched to minimize database-selection bias, relevant publications indexed in other databases (e.g., PubMed and Embase) may have been missed. Second, the restriction to English-language publications may have excluded pertinent studies published in other languages, particularly from China and Japan, which are major contributors to SMARCA4-UT research. Third, due to the rapidly evolving nature of the field, recently published high-impact studies may be underrepresented because of citation time lag. In addition, the rarity and heterogeneity of SMARCA4-UT may contribute to fragmented research outputs and limited statistical robustness in certain clusters.

A key limitation of bibliometric analysis is citation inflation from broadly cited, non-specific publications. In this study, highly cited articles (e.g., WHO classifications and SWI/SNF-related studies) may inflate aggregate metrics due to their cross-disciplinary relevance rather than specific focus on SMARCA4-UT. To address this, publications were stratified into disease-specific and broadly related categories, allowing clearer delineation of the field’s core knowledge base.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive bibliometric overview of thoracic SMARCA4-UT, highlighting a rapidly expanding yet still evolving research field. While mechanistic insights into SWI/SNF dysfunction are well developed, clinical translation—particularly in diagnosis and treatment—remains limited. Immunotherapy-based strategies show promise but lack consistent efficacy and reliable biomarkers. Overall, advancing translational research, fostering global collaboration, and developing personalized therapeutic approaches will be critical to improving outcomes in this aggressive malignancy. Future research should strengthen international collaboration, prioritize mechanism-driven clinical trials, and establish standardized diagnostic and therapeutic guidelines for SMARCA4-UT.
