Rendering Separoid Information: Rate–Distortion Reconstruction of Convex Apartness Scenes
Faruk Alpay, Barış Başaran

Abstract
A convex scene communicates more than shape: the pattern of which groups of objects are mutually apart and which cross is a discrete, logical payload that a picture must carry to a viewer or a downstream decoder. We make this payload explicit. The apartness table of a finite family of convex bodies, a separoid , is treated as a source signal, a renderable convex scene as its encoder, and the rendered image as a noisy visual channel from which the apartness structure is decoded. Within this view we pose apartness-preserving rendering as a rate–distortion problem : minimise a geometry description length subject to faithfully transmitting the separation certificates (the maximal separations and minimal Radon partitions that generate the table by closure) rather than minimising pixel error. The distortion is closure-aware (a high-consequence certificate bit costs more than a redundant one), the rate is a differentiable geometric code length, and apartness is encoded as a distribution over separating directions whose breadth sets its robustness to viewpoint. We give a differentiable support-function realisation of the pipeline, a variational lower bound on the apartness mutual information I ⁡ ( Σ , Y ) I(\Sigma;Y) , and an information-theoretic account of view selection. Experiments on planar convex scenes show that (i) scenes are recovered from the apartness table alone at 99.9% bit accuracy, with the certificate skeleton alone already determining the full table; (ii) a clean operational rate–distortion frontier emerges, along which the consequence-weighted certificate distortion exceeds plain Hamming error, since low-rate rendering preferentially damages the high-consequence structure; (iii) a rendered 48 × 48 48\times 48 image delivers 0.72 of the apartness-graph entropy, with the information score rising with resolution and collapsing with noise like a capacity-limited channel; and (iv) information-optimal views certify most separations from a single direction, and a small rate surcharge makes them robust across a wide cone of viewpoints.



1
Introduction

Rendering is usually evaluated by how closely an image reproduces a reference
appearance. Yet many figures exist to communicate a
relational
fact:
that these objects form a cluster while those stand apart, that two groups
overlap, that a configuration cannot be pulled apart by any straight cut. In a
diagram of a mechanism, a molecular cartoon, a scene graph, or a statistical
embedding, the geometry is only a carrier; the message is the pattern of
separations and crossings. When that message is what matters, the right
objective is not pixel fidelity but
how many bits of relational structure
the picture transmits, and at what geometric cost.

We formalise this for convex scenes. A finite family of convex bodies

C_{1},\dots,C_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
induces, for every pair of disjoint index sets

A,B
, a single bit

\sigma_{A,B}=1\iff\operatorname{conv}\!\Big(\textstyle\bigcup_{a\in A}C_{a}\Big)\cap\operatorname{conv}\!\Big(\textstyle\bigcup_{b\in B}C_{b}\Big)=\varnothing.

The collection of these bits is the
apartness table
; the abstract
structure it satisfies is a
separoid

[
2
,
26
]
, and a crossing pair (
\sigma_{A,B}=0
) is exactly a

Radon partition

[
22
]
. This table is the relational payload
of the scene. Our thesis is that the table is a
source
, a convex scene
is an
encoder
of it, and a renderer is a
channel
that carries it
to a decoder:

\Sigma\;\xrightarrow{\ f_{\theta}\ }\;V\;\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal{R}_{\omega}\ }\;Y\;\xrightarrow{\ q_{\phi}\ }\;\hat{\Sigma},

(1)

where
V

Y

\hat{\Sigma}
the decoded
apartness.

Merely placing bodies so that the apartness

3
Apartness structures as a source

Let
E=\{1,\dots,n\}
index the sites (bodies) and let

D(E)=\{(A,B):A,B\subseteq E,\ A,B\neq\varnothing,\ A\cap B=\varnothing\}

be the disjoint pairs. The
apartness table
of a scene is

\Sigma=\{\sigma_{A,B}\}_{(A,B)\in D(E)}
, with
\sigma_{A,B}=1
iff the group
hulls are disjoint. As an abstract object
\Sigma
is a
separoid
[
2
]
: the relation
\dagger
defined by

A\dagger B\Leftrightarrow\sigma_{A,B}=1
is symmetric, every separated pair is
disjoint, and it is closed under taking subsets,

A\dagger B,\ A^{\prime}\subseteq A,\ B^{\prime}\subseteq B\ \Longrightarrow\ A^{\prime}\dagger B^{\prime}.

(2)

We regard
\Sigma
as a binary
source
to be transmitted.

Certificate skeleton.

Two families of pairs summarise
\Sigma
. A
maximal separation
is a
separated pair
(A,B)
with
\sigma_{A,B}=1
that admits no single-site
extension staying separated; write
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
for their set. A

minimal Radon partition
is a crossing pair
(A,B)
with
\sigma_{A,B}=0

such that removing any one site (keeping both parts non-empty) yields a
separation; write
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
. Because separations are downward closed
(
2
) and crossings are upward closed (enlarging either part
keeps the hulls intersecting), these two antichains generate the table.

Proposition 1

(The skeleton determines the table)
.

For every disjoint pair
(A,B)\in D(E)
,

\sigma_{A,B}=1\iff(A,B)\sqsubseteq(M_{1},M_{2})\ \text{for some}\ (M_{1},M_{2})\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma),

where
(A,B)\sqsubseteq(M_{1},M_{2})
means
A\subseteq M_{1},\ B\subseteq M_{2}
.
Equivalently,
\sigma_{A,B}=0
iff
(A,B)
contains some

(P_{1},P_{2})\in\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
. Hence
\Sigma
is recoverable from

(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P})
alone.

Proof.

If
(A,B)\sqsubseteq(M_{1},M_{2})
with
\sigma_{M_{1},M_{2}}=1
, then
(
2
) gives
\sigma_{A,B}=1
. Conversely, if
\sigma_{A,B}=1
,
repeatedly add a free site to
A
or to
B
whenever the pair stays separated;
the process terminates (only finitely many sites) at a maximal separation
containing
(A,B)
. The crossing statement is the contrapositive together with
upward closure of crossings, by the same single-site argument applied to
removals.
∎

Thus the source has a sufficient statistic

Z_{\Sigma}=(M_{\Sigma},P_{\Sigma})
formed by the skeleton bits, and we may transmit

Z_{\Sigma}
in place of
\Sigma
. In our scenes the skeleton is far smaller than
the table (
Table

1
), which is what makes the certificate view
worthwhile.

Source entropy.

This redundancy can be quantified. The apartness
table
\Sigma
has
|D(E)|=\tfrac{1}{2}(3^{n}-2^{n+1}+1)
entries, but its entropy is
far lower: when the bodies are points,
\Sigma
is a function of the
configuration’s order type (oriented matroid), since the Radon partitions are
fixed by the chirotope
[
5
]
. As there are only

2^{\Theta(n\log n)}
order types of
n
points in the plane
[
9
]
,
the source obeys

H(\Sigma)\;=\;O(n\log n)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{bits},

(3)

exponentially below the table size. The certificate skeleton is the structural
witness of this gap, and the rate–distortion and channel results below turn it
into operational statements: a faithful carrier and a good view need only convey
these
O(n\log n)
bits, not the full table.

4
Rendering as a channel

Encoder.

A scene is
V=\{v_{e,j}\}
,
e\in E
,
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
with
C_{e}(V)=\operatorname{conv}\{v_{e,1},\dots,v_{e,m}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The encoder

f_{\theta}:\Sigma\mapsto V
produces a scene realising the source; in this work

f_{\theta}
is the optimisation of
Section

7
.

Renderer / channel.

A renderer
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
with parameters

\omega
(camera, resolution, palette, sensor) maps a scene to an image,

Y\sim p_{\omega}(\,\cdot\mid V)
. We use a soft support-function rasteriser
(
Section

7
): a point
x
lies in
C_{e}
iff

\langle u,x\rangle\leq h_{e}(u)
for all directions
u
, where

h_{e}(u)=\max_{j}\langle u,v_{e,j}\rangle
is the support
function
[
24
]
; sites are alpha-composited under a fixed
palette and corrupted by Gaussian sensor noise. Occlusion, finite resolution,
colour collisions, and noise make
Y
a genuinely lossy channel.

Decoder and apartness mutual information.

A decoder
q_{\phi}(\hat{\Sigma}\mid Y)=\prod_{(A,B)}q_{\phi}(\hat{\sigma}_{A,B}\mid Y)

estimates apartness bits from the image. We want
Y
to carry as much of the
source as possible, i.e. to maximise
I(\Sigma;Y)
(or, for any chosen
sub-structure such as the certificate
Z_{\Sigma}
or the pairwise apartness graph
of
Section

9
, the corresponding
I(\cdot\,;Y)
). Using the standard
variational bound
[
3
,
21
]
,

I(Z_{\Sigma};Y)\;=\;H(Z_{\Sigma})-H(Z_{\Sigma}\mid Y)\;\geq\;H(Z_{\Sigma})+\mathbb{E}_{Z,Y}\big[\log q_{\phi}(Z_{\Sigma}\mid Y)\big],

(4)

which is tight when
q_{\phi}
matches the true posterior. The bound turns
maximising apartness information into minimising the decoder cross-entropy, with
the operational reading:
how many bits of apartness does the rendered
image deliver?
We report the normalised

\mathrm{IS}\;=\;\frac{\hat{I}(\Sigma;Y)}{H(\Sigma)}\in[0,1],\hskip 20.00003pt\hat{I}(\Sigma;Y)=H(\Sigma)-\hat{H}(\Sigma\mid Y),

(5)

the fraction of source entropy the channel transmits, with
H(\Sigma)
and

\hat{H}(\Sigma\mid Y)
estimated as sums of per-bit Bernoulli entropies.

What the pipeline can and cannot do.

The encoding, rendering and
decoding of
eq.

1
form a Markov chain

\Sigma\to V\to Y\to\hat{\Sigma}
, so the data-processing
inequality
[
6
]
bounds every stage at once,

I(\Sigma;\hat{\Sigma})\;\leq\;I(\Sigma;Y)\;\leq\;I(\Sigma;V)\;\leq\;H(\Sigma).

(6)

No decoder recovers more apartness than the rendered image carries, and no
renderer carries more than the scene encodes. Two design goals follow: the
encoder
f_{\theta}
should keep
I(\Sigma;V)
near
H(\Sigma)
(lose no structure
in the geometry), and the renderer should keep
I(\Sigma;Y)
near
I(\Sigma;V)

(lose no structure in the picture). The information score IS estimates the
middle term normalised by
H(\Sigma)
and is therefore a property of the
encoder–channel pair, not of any particular decoder.

5
Certificate-aware distortion

A naive distortion is the Hamming error over the table,

d_{H}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma})=\sum_{(A,B)\in D(E)}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\sigma_{A,B}\neq\hat{\sigma}_{A,B}\right]
.
This is misleading: by
Proposition

1
a single wrong
maximal

separation falsifies all separations beneath it, and a wrong
minimal

Radon partition falsifies all crossings above it. Distortion should be measured
in logical consequences. Define the consequence weights

w_{M}=\big|\mathrm{Down}(A,B)\big|=(2^{|A|}-1)(2^{|B|}-1),\hskip 20.00003ptw_{P}=\big|\mathrm{Up}(A,B)\big|=3^{\,n-|A|-|B|},

(7)

counting, respectively, the non-empty sub-pairs a separation implies and the
extensions a crossing forces. The
certificate (closure-aware) distortion

is

d_{\mathrm{sep}}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma})=\sum_{(A,B)\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)}w_{M}\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[\hat{\sigma}_{A,B}\neq 1\right]+\sum_{(A,B)\in\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)}w_{P}\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[\hat{\sigma}_{A,B}\neq 0\right].

(8)

Its differentiable surrogate replaces each indicator by the soft separation
hinge of
Section

7
, so that distortion is
logical-consequence
loss
, not pixel error.

6
Geometry rate

The rate is the description length of the carrier
V
. Counting vertices is too
crude; we use a differentiable geometric code length with three terms:

R(V)=\underbrace{\sum_{e,j}\log\!\Big(1+\tfrac{\|v_{e,j}\|^{2}}{\Delta_{q}^{2}}\Big)}_{\text{quantised coordinates}}\;+\;\underbrace{\sum_{e}H(\kappa_{e})}_{\text{boundary-curvature entropy}},

(9)

where
\Delta_{q}
is the coordinate quantisation step. The first term is the cost
of locating quantised vertices; the second is the entropy of the discrete
turning-angle (curvature) profile of each body,

\kappa_{e,j}=\angle(v_{e,j+1}-v_{e,j})-\angle(v_{e,j}-v_{e,j-1})
, normalised to

p_{e,j}=|\kappa_{e,j}|/\sum_{i}|\kappa_{e,i}|
and

H(\kappa_{e})=-\sum_{j}p_{e,j}\log p_{e,j}
. Low rate favours compact, smooth
silhouettes that still encode the apartness, exactly the graphics intuition that
a clean figure should not waste boundary complexity.

7
The objective and its differentiable channel

Directional separation channel.

For unit direction
u
, the group support is
h_{A}(u)=\max_{a\in A}h_{a}(u)
and the
signed margin

s_{A,B}(u)=-\,h_{A}(u)-h_{B}(-u)

(10)

is positive iff
u
separates the groups with that margin (a separating
hyperplane normal). Sampling
L
directions
\{u_{\ell}\}
, a smooth max margin and
a direction distribution are

\Delta_{A,B}=\tau\log\!\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{s_{A,B}(u_{\ell})}{\tau}\Big),\hskip 20.00003ptp_{A,B}(\ell)=\softmax_{\ell}\!\Big(\tfrac{s_{A,B}(u_{\ell})}{\tau}\Big).

(11)

As
\tau\to 0
,
\Delta_{A,B}\to\max_{u}s_{A,B}(u)
, which is positive exactly when
the groups are separable. The
apartness of a pair is thus encoded as a
distribution
p_{A,B}
over separating directions
, with entropy

H_{\mathrm{dir}}(A,B)=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}p_{A,B}(\ell)\log p_{A,B}(\ell)

(12)

measuring how broadly the separation is witnessed. The set

\{u:s_{A,B}(u)>0\}
is the
witnessing cone
of the separation; its
breadth is what governs recoverability across viewpoints (
Section

8
),
and we widen it directly with the robustness term below.

Separation and certificate losses.

With a target margin
\gamma
,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sep}}=\!\!\sum_{(A,B):\sigma=1}\!\!\big[\max(0,\gamma-\Delta_{A,B})\big]^{2}+\!\!\sum_{(A,B):\sigma=0}\!\!\big[\max(0,\Delta_{A,B})\big]^{2},

(13)

and the certificate-weighted version applies the weights (
7
) over

\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{P}
, the differentiable surrogate of
d_{\mathrm{sep}}
.

Radon witnesses.

A crossing
(A,B)\in\mathcal{P}
can be certified
constructively by a witness point in both hulls: barycentric weights

\alpha,\beta\geq 0
with
\sum\alpha=\sum\beta=1
and

\sum_{a,i}\alpha^{P}_{a,i}v_{a,i}=\sum_{b,j}\beta^{P}_{b,j}v_{b,j}
. Penalising the
squared mismatch yields
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{radon}}
, and the witness entropy

H(\alpha),H(\beta)
selects between a
sparse
certificate (few vertices)
and
distributed visual evidence
(a crossing spread over a legible region
rather than a single pixel of contact).

Viewpoint robustness.

To make a separation readable from many
directions we widen its witnessing cone, rewarding the soft fraction of
directions that already separate the pair,

R_{\mathrm{view}}(V)=\!\!\sum_{(A,B):\sigma=1}\!\!\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{L}\textstyle\sum_{\ell}\varsigma\!\big(s_{A,B}(u_{\ell})/\tau_{C}\big)\Big),

(14)

with
\varsigma
the logistic. Minimising
R_{\mathrm{view}}
spreads the
apartness over more separating directions (the broad, high-entropy regime of
the directional distribution
p_{A,B}
) at a small geometric-rate cost.

Graphics regularity.

A shape prior
G(V)
favours compact,
near-regular bodies by penalising the squared coefficient of variation of vertex
radii about each centroid, keeping silhouettes legible.

Full objective.

Putting the pieces together, with

V=f_{\theta}(\Sigma)
and
Y\sim p_{\omega}(\cdot\mid V)
,

\boxed{\;\mathcal{J}=\underbrace{w_{\mathrm{sep}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sep}}+w_{\mathrm{cert}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cert}}}_{\text{distortion}}\;+\;\lambda_{R}\,R(V)\;+\;\lambda_{G}\,G(V)\;+\;\lambda_{C}\,R_{\mathrm{view}}(V),\;}

(15)

optionally augmented by
-\lambda_{I}\,\mathbb{E}_{Z,Y}\log q_{\phi}(Z\mid Y)
to push the
rendered channel toward maximal apartness information via (
4
). The
Lagrangian form
\mathcal{J}_{\beta}=\mathbb{E}[d_{\mathrm{sep}}]+\beta\,\mathbb{E}[R(V)]
traces the
operational rate–distortion curve as
\beta
(here
\lambda_{R}
) varies, while

\lambda_{C}
trades a little rate for viewpoint robustness.

8
Information-optimal viewpoints

A single orthographic view along direction
u
observes each body’s

shadow
, the support interval
[\,{-h_{e}(-u)},\,h_{e}(u)\,]
. A separation

(A,B)
is
certified
by view
u
iff the group shadows are disjoint,
i.e. iff
s_{A,B}(u)>0
; a view never reports a false separation, so it recovers
exactly the separations whose witnessing cone contains
u
. The

viewpoint informativeness
is the fraction of separations a view certifies,

\mathcal{I}(\omega)=I(Z_{\Sigma};Y_{\omega}),\hskip 20.00003pt\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(\omega)=\mathbb{E}_{Z,Y_{\omega}}\log q_{\phi}(Z_{\Sigma}\mid Y_{\omega}),

(16)

and the best view is
\omega^{\star}=\argmax_{\omega}\widehat{\mathcal{I}}(\omega)
.
For several views, the information-optimal set trades coverage against
redundancy,

\Omega_{k}^{\star}=\argmax_{|\Omega_{k}|=k}I\big(Z_{\Sigma};Y_{\omega_{1}},\dots,Y_{\omega_{k}}\big)-\eta\sum_{i<j}I\big(Y_{\omega_{i}};Y_{\omega_{j}}\big),

(17)

which we approximate greedily by maximal coverage of as-yet-uncertified
separations. Finally, for a rendering family

\mathcal{F}=\{f_{\theta},\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\}
under a rate budget
R_{0}
, the

apartness channel capacity

C_{\mathcal{F}}(R_{0})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p(\Sigma),\,f,\,\mathcal{R}\\
\mathbb{E}R(V)\leq R_{0}\end{subarray}}I(Z_{\Sigma};Y)

(18)

asks how many bits of separation information a renderable convex scene family can
carry, the natural endpoint of the channel view.

) with

). The

) with Adam
[

]

over

from

The effect in

stab all arcs. If every arc has angular measure at least

\lceil 1/\gamma\rceil

robustness term (
14

which lowers this bound; sharper guarantees follow from

10
Discussion and limitations

Our scenes are planar and modest in size; computing exact tables and skeletons
is exponential in
n
, so the source itself is the scaling bottleneck, not the
optimisation. Reconstruction is a non-convex problem and occasionally lands in a
local optimum at
n=7
, which restarts mitigate but do not eliminate. The
renderer is deliberately simple; richer cameras, true 3D bodies, and learned
renderers fit the same channel formulation and are natural extensions. The
capacity (
18
) is defined but only bounded empirically here.
Finally, our distortion privileges the certificate skeleton; other consequence
weightings (e.g. task-specific) slot into the same framework.

11
Conclusion

We recast rendering a convex scene as transmitting its apartness structure over
a visual channel, and posed apartness-preserving rendering as a rate–distortion
problem with a closure-aware fidelity criterion. Maximal separations and minimal
Radon partitions are the certificates; a differentiable support-function scene is
the low-rate carrier; and the rendered image is the channel whose apartness
mutual information we lower-bound and measure. Experiments confirm recovery from
the table alone, a clean rate–distortion frontier on which the
consequence-weighted distortion is the binding criterion, a measurable
information score for rendered scenes, and an information-theoretic account of
viewpoint selection and robustness. We hope the
certificate-as-payload view encourages rendering objectives that are honest about

what a picture is supposed to make recoverable.
