Jianning Fu

Abstract
. Let μ λ \mu_{\lambda} be the Bernoulli convolution measure with parameter λ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \lambda\in(0,1) . 1.
Introduction

Consider the random variable

X(\lambda):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}\lambda^{m-1},\lambda\in(0,1)

where
a_{1},a_{2},\dots
are i.i.d random variables taking values in
\{-1,1\}
with probability
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. Denote by
\mu_{\lambda}
the law of
X(\lambda)
. It is the infinite convolution product of
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{-\lambda^{n}}+\lambda_{\lambda^{n}})
, and is hence termed (unbiased)

infinite Bernoulli Convolution.
The study of Bernoulli Convolution has been an active research area since the
1930
’s, and numerous results have been established regarding its dimension, singularity vs. absolute continuity,
L^{q}
-density, multifractal spectrum, and many other aspects. Some classical works include
[
19
]

[

]

[

]

[

]

[

]

[

]

[
24
]
, but this list is far from comprehensive.

In the present work we study weak smoothness of the map
\lambda\mapsto\mu_{\lambda}
, which we call

the linear response problem
for Bernoulli convolution. Let
\phi(x):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. Define

(1.1)

h(\lambda)=h_{\phi}(\lambda):=\int\phi(x)\,d\mu_{\lambda}(x),

whenever the integral is well-defined and finite. We would like to study how the smoothness of
h
depends on the Hölder regularity of
\phi
.

The linear response theory originated in Ruelle’s work and is motivated by applications to homogenization and mathematical physics, see
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

.
Currently,there is a well-developed linear response theory for many smooth or piecewise smooth dynamical systems, see

[

Most papers above discuss the smoothness of averages such as (
1.1
) for smooth observables
\phi.
On the other hand in applications one often has to deal dynamically defined observables
such as the directions of the invariant splitting, and those observables are only Hölder in general,
[
33
,
30
,
29
,

]
. This motivates the study of regularity of
(
1.1
) for Hölder functions.

Another reason for us to study Hölder observables is that if
\phi
is smooth, trivially (
1.1
) is smooth. Indeed,
X(\lambda)
is an analytic function in
\lambda
and the image of
X(\lambda)
is uniformly bounded on any compact subset of
[0,1)
. So, if
\phi
is differentiable, then
h(\lambda)
is automatically differentiable. We will discuss this in more detail in §
3.3
. It is therefore natural to consider observables with worse regularity like Hölder functions, which turns out to be more subtle. In this paper we present several results demonstrating both regularity of (
1.1
) for some values of the parameters
\lambda
and the lack thereof for other parameters. In particular, our results, shows, the phase transition: as
\lambda
increases the averages seem to become more regular, compare Theorems
1.1
and

1.3
below.

Next, we provide precise formulations of our results.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\gamma=\gamma(\epsilon)>0
so that for any Hölder continuous test function
\phi
with Hölder exponent at least
1-2\gamma
, the average
h_{\phi}
is absolutely continuous
on the interval
(2^{-\frac{1}{3}}+\epsilon,1-\epsilon)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1

uses Fourier decay and it can be of independent interest in a more general setting.

To state our next result let
\dim\mu_{\lambda_{0}}
stand for the (upper) Hausdorff dimension of
\mu_{\lambda_{0}}
:
\dim\mu_{\lambda_{0}}=\inf\{\dim_{H}(A):\mu_{\lambda_{0}}(A)=1\}
, where
\dim_{H}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension
(see Section
3
for precise definition of the Hausdorff dimension).

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
is such that
\dim\mu_{\lambda_{0}}<1
, then for any
\theta\in(0,1)
,
h_{\phi}
is not differentiable at
\lambda_{0}
for generic
\phi\in C^{\theta}
(the space of Hölder continuous functions with Hölder exponent
\theta
).

Note that for
\dim\mu_{\lambda}<1
, for all
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and Theorem
1.2
yields non-differentiability of
h_{\phi}
at
\lambda
for generic
\phi\in C^{\alpha}
. One could take a step further to ask if for generic
\phi
,
h_{\phi}
is non-differentiable at almost every
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, or at a positive-Lebesgue-measure set of
\lambda
. We present a partial result in this direction.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4}),\alpha\in(0,1)
there exists a
\phi\in C^{\alpha}
such that
h_{\phi}
is not differentiable at Lebesgue almost every
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
.

Note
that differentiability of
\lambda\mapsto\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda}
reflects the Hausdorff dimension
\dim\mu_{\lambda}
of
\mu_{\lambda}
, which has long been the central topic in the study of Bernoulli convolution. It is well known that
\dim\mu_{\lambda}\leq\frac{\log 2}{-\log\lambda}

[
11
, Chapter 3,10]
, and equality holds if
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. For
\lambda\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, according to Hochman
[
31
]
and Varju
[
51
]
,
\dim\mu_{\lambda}=1
except possibly at a certain class of algebraic parameters that satisfy the “exact overlap assumption”. And for the moment the only known examples in this class are the reciprocals of Pisot numbers

[
26
]

[
23
]
. Moreover, the smallest Pisot number is the plastic constant
\psi\approx 1.3247

[
47
]
, so the largest reciprocal is
\frac{1}{\psi}\approx 0.7548
. It is conjectured that for all
\lambda
sufficiently close to
1
,
\mu_{\lambda}
should have dimension
1
.
Note that
\frac{1}{\psi}\approx 0.7548<2^{-\frac{1}{3}}
and therefore Theorem
1.2
does not give any non-differentiable points in
(2^{-\frac{1}{3}},1)
where Theorem
1.1
applies.

Although the results presented above provide some insight on the regularity of (
1.1
), there are many open questions, and we highlight some of them below.

Question 1.1
.

It it true that
\lambda\mapsto\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda}

is differentiable for
all

\lambda
sufficiently close to 1,
provided that the Hölder exponent of
\phi
is sufficiently close to 1?

Note that Theorem
1.2
shows that if the answer to this question is affirmative, then
\mu_{\lambda}
has dimension 1 for all
\lambda
sufficiently
close to 1. Unfortunately, such a result is out of reach at the moment.

Question 1.2
.

Does Theorem
1.3
hold for generic Hölder functions?

Intuitively, this would mean that for most
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, the support
K_{\lambda}
of
\mu_{\lambda}
does not intersect much with
K_{\lambda^{\prime}}
as
\lambda^{\prime}\to\lambda
, so that
\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda}
is quite “independent” to
\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda^{\prime}}
for generic
\phi
, which causes non-differentiability.

We note that the counterexamples of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
rely on carefully
designed functions. Question
1.2
asks what happens in the typical case. Another way to approach this question is to consider what happens for classical non smooth functions.

Question 1.3
.

Given
a,b>1
let
\displaystyle W_{a,b}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(a^{n}x)}{b^{n}}
. Study the smoothness of the map

(a,b,\lambda)\mapsto\int W_{a,b}(x)d\mu_{\lambda}(x)
.

The above questions pertain to Bernoulli convolutions. However the problem is interesting in the more general context.

Question 1.4
.

Study the regularity of (
1.1
) in the case where
\mu_{\lambda}
is a family of SRB measure for Anosov diffeomorphsims
f_{\lambda}
depending smoothly
on a parameter
\lambda.

We note that for
C^{1}
observables the linear response is well understood, see references above.

Let us end the introduction by pointing out that, though (

) is readily differentiable whenever
\phi
is, it is highly nontrivial to decide if

[

is Lipschitz in the Wasserstein

2.
Linear Response with Holder-Continuous Test Function

2.1.
Proof strategy

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. Let us describe the strategy. We will show that there exists
\rho:(2^{-\frac{1}{3}},1)\to\mathbb{R}
such that for almost every
2^{-\frac{1}{3}}<a<b<1
,

(2.1)

h(b)-h(a)=\int_{a}^{b}\rho(t)\,dt.

and that
\rho
is locally integrable. Then Theorem
1.1
follows by Lebesgue differentiability theorem. Indeed,
h
will be absolutely continuous with
h^{\prime}=\rho
almost everywhere.

To obtain
\rho
, we convert the integrand defining
h
into their Fourier transforms

h(b)-h(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\phi}(\xi)(\hat{\mu}_{b}(\xi)-\hat{\mu}_{a}(\xi))\,d\xi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\phi}(\xi)\int_{a}^{b}\frac{d\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)}{d\lambda}\,d\lambda\,d\xi,

where
\hat{\phi}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}\phi(x)\,dx
and
\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\xi x}\,d\mu_{\lambda}(x)
. The first equality is not true in general, but will hold if both
\mu_{a}
and
\mu_{b}
are absolutely continuous with
L^{2}
-densities, thanks to Parseval identity. By the famous result of Solomyak
[
49
]
, for a.e.
\lambda\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
\mu_{\lambda}
has such property. So we will first deal with the case where
\mu_{a}
,
\mu_{b}
have
L^{2}
densities, and then pass to every
a,b
.

As for the second equality, it is due to the fact that
\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)
is an analytic function in
\lambda
. In fact, by the definition of Bernoulli convolution,

(2.2)

\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\widehat{(\delta_{\lambda^{n}}+\delta_{-\lambda^{n}})}(\xi)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\cos(\lambda^{n}\xi).

Therefore, it suffices to show that

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{\phi}(\xi)\frac{d\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)}{d\lambda}|\,d\xi\,d\lambda<\infty.

To that end, we will need a result from
[
39
]
, which establishes

\int_{I}||\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}||_{\gamma,2}\,d\lambda:=\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2\gamma}\,d\xi\,d\lambda<\infty

for some suitable choice of interval
I
and some
\gamma>0
.

The rest of §
2
is organized as follows.
§
2.2

contains the necessary background.
In §
2.3
we bound the term
|\frac{d}{d\lambda}\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)|
. Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
in §
2.4
.

2.2.
Finiteness of Fractional Sobolev Norm

Recall that the Fourier transform of a finite Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}

is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\xi x}\,d\nu(x).

For
\gamma>0
, the standard
2,\gamma
-Sobolev space
L^{2}_{\gamma}
is defined by the norm

||\nu||_{2,\gamma}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{\nu}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{2\gamma}\,d\xi.

The result we rely on is the following.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma
5.5
of
[
39
]
)
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\gamma=\gamma(\epsilon)>0
so that,

\int_{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}}||\mu_{\lambda}||_{2,\gamma}^{2}\,d\lambda<\infty.

We also need a well-known identity due to the infinite convolution structure of
\mu_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\lambda\in(0,1),\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

(2.3)

\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)=\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{m}}(\xi)\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{m}}(\lambda\xi)\cdots\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{m}}(\lambda^{m-1}\xi).

Proof.

In the infinite product

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)=\prod_{k}\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)

divide the powers of
\lambda
according to their congruence classes modulo
m
.
∎

2.3.
An Upper Bound for |
\frac{d}{d\lambda}\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)
|

This subsection includes a simple lemma giving an upper bound for the derivative (with respect to parameter
\lambda
) of the Fourier transform
\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\cos(\lambda^{n}\xi).
Since it is an analytic function in
\lambda
for each fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2.4)

\frac{d}{d\lambda}\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)=-\xi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\lambda^{n-1}\sin(\lambda^{n}\xi)\prod_{k=0,k\neq n}^{\infty}\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi).

And one can bound the term
\displaystyle\prod_{k=0,k\neq n}^{\infty}\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)
by following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\lambda\in(0,1),\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\prod_{k=0,k\neq n}^{\infty}|\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)|\leq|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\xi)|^{2}+|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda\xi)|^{2}+|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda^{2}\xi)|^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)=\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\xi)\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda\xi)\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda^{2}\xi).

Suppose
n\equiv 0\text{ mod }3
, then

\prod_{k=0,k\neq n}^{\infty}|\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)|\leq\prod_{k\equiv 1\text{ mod 3}}|\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)|\prod_{k\equiv 2\text{ mod }3}|\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)|=|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda\xi)||\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda^{2}\xi)|

where we bound all the terms
\displaystyle|\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)|,k\equiv 0\text{ mod }3
by
1
. Similarly,

\prod_{k=0,k\neq n}^{\infty}|\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)|\leq|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\xi)||\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda^{2}\xi)|,\text{ if }k\equiv 1\text{ mod }3

and

\prod_{k=0,k\neq n}^{\infty}|\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)|\leq|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\xi)||\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda\xi)|,\text{ if }k\equiv 2\text{ mod }3.

Therefore we conclude

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0,k\neq n}^{\infty}|\cos(\lambda^{k}\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\xi)||\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda^{2}\xi)|+|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\xi)||\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda^{2}\xi)|+|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\xi)||\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda^{2}\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\xi)|^{2}+|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda\xi)|^{2}+|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{3}}(\lambda^{2}\xi)|^{2}

as desired.
∎

a
and
b_{l}
increases to
b
, and that
\mu_{a_{l}}
and
\mu_{b_{l}}
have

density for all

. Then the proceeding argument yields, for any

\displaystyle|h(b_{l})-h(a_{l})|\leq M\int_{a_{l}}^{b_{l}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}mb_{l}^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{2\gamma}|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,d\lambda\leq M\int_{a}^{b}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}mb^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{2\gamma}|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,d\lambda

where

depends only on
\phi
.

Moreover,
\lambda\mapsto\mu_{\lambda}
is a continuous in the weak
∗

[

]
, so

is continuous. Letting

(2.9)

|h(b)-h(a)|\leq M\int_{a}^{b}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}mb^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{2\gamma}|\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,d\lambda.

Finally

\displaystyle h(b)-h(a)=\lim_{l\to\infty}h(b_{l})-h(a_{l})=\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{a_{l}}^{b_{l}}\rho(t)\,dt=\int_{a}^{b}\rho(t)\,dt

as

is locally integrable, completing the proof.
∎

We end this section by remarking a possible way to remove “almost everywhere” in the conclusion. Since the above proof implies that
\rho
is the a.e. derivative of

, it suffices to show that
\rho
is a continuous function on
(2^{-\frac{1}{3}},1)
(or at least on some interval very close to

). One could repeat the trick above and write

\displaystyle|\rho(b)-\rho(a)|

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\phi}(\xi)\left(\frac{d\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)}{d\lambda}|_{\lambda=b}-\frac{d\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)}{d\lambda}|_{\lambda=a}\right)\,d\xi\right|\!\!=\!\!\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{a}^{b}\hat{\phi}(\xi)\frac{d^{2}\hat{\mu}_{\lambda}(\xi)}{d\lambda^{2}}|_{\lambda=t}\,dt\,d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{\phi}(\xi)|\xi^{2}\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{4}}^{2}(\xi)\,d\xi\,d\lambda\leq C\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{2-\alpha}\hat{\mu}_{\lambda^{4}}^{2}(\xi)\,d\xi\,d\lambda

for some absolute constant

. Therefore, if one could improve the constant

to be greater than

will be the integral of a locally integrable function and would be continuous. Unfortunately, the author does not know how to obtain larger

Another remark is that, it is well known that for

3.1.
Proof strategy.

In this section we prove Theorem

Lemma 3.1

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, there exists
\phi\in C^{\theta}
such that

\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{h_{\phi}(\lambda_{0}+\epsilon)-h_{\phi}(\lambda_{0})}{\epsilon}=\infty.

Lemma 3.2

Under the assumptions
of Theorem

,
if there exists
\phi\in C^{\theta}
and
\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to

Clearly the above two lemmas imply Theorem

The proof of Lemma

at all. Below we sketch our strategy to prove Lemma

.

We will take advantage of the fact that if

has dimension less than

, then there are sets with small Lebesgue measure but large

-measure. One can thus arrange a sequence of

test functions

so that it concentrate more and more on these sets, that they converge to a

blows up to infinity. This method works in general for the stationary measure of contracting on average iterated function systems.

More precisely, we will reduce the proof to the existence of a family of special test functions

, as in the following proposition

.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.2
, there exists a family of
C^{1}
, nonnegative functions

such that:

(1)

There exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for all

exist and are nonnegative. Here

.

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n}h_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda_{0})=\infty
.

(3)

\displaystyle\phi:=\sum\phi_{n}
is bounded and H
\ddot{o}
lder continuous, with H
\ddot{o}
lder exponent
\theta
.

Note that it is part of the proposition that

’s are differentiable. This is because
\phi_{n}
’s are

as will be explained in §

.

In the rest of this section, we first show how to prove Theorem
1.2
assuming Proposition
3.3
in §

. Then we go to construction of such family

in §
3.3
. Finally, we present the proof of Lemma

in §

We end this subsection by recalling the notion the Hausdorff dimension of subsets of
\mathbb{R}

[
22
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix any subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}
. For any
s>0,\delta>0
, define

H_{\delta}^{s}(A):=\inf\{\sum_{j}|I_{j}|^{s}:\{I_{j}\}\text{ is a cover of }A\text{ s.t. the diameter }|I_{j}|<\delta,\forall j\}.

Here the diameter
|X|
of any subset of
\mathbb{R}
is defined as
\displaystyle|X|:=\sup_{x,y\in X}|x-y|
.

By construction for fixed

,
H^{s}_{\delta}(A)
is increasing in
\delta
, so the limit

\displaystyle H^{s}(A):=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}H^{s}_{\delta}(A)
is well defined (but may equal infinity). Moreover, there exists a unique

such that

s_{0}=\inf\{s\geq 0:H^{s}(A)=0\}=\sup\{s\geq 0:H^{s}(A)=\infty\}.

The Hausdorff dimension of

is then defined to be
\dim_{H}A=s_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem

using Proposition

Proof.

Given
\{\phi_{n}\}
and
\phi
, define
h(\lambda)=\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda},\lambda\in(\lambda_{0}-\epsilon_{0},\lambda_{0}+\epsilon_{0})
. We will show that
\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{h(\lambda_{0}+\epsilon)-h(\lambda_{0})}{\epsilon}=\infty
, so that

is not differentiable at
\lambda_{0}
.

Let
F(\epsilon,n):=\frac{h_{n}(\lambda_{0}+\epsilon)-h_{n}(\lambda_{0})}{\epsilon},n\in\mathbb{N},0<|\epsilon|<\epsilon_{0}
. Note that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}F(\epsilon,n)=h_{n}^{\prime}(0),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

and

\displaystyle F(\epsilon,n)\geq 0,\quad\forall 0<|\epsilon|<\epsilon_{0},n\in\mathbb{N}

since

for all

.
Thus

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{h(\lambda_{0}+\epsilon)-h(\lambda_{0})}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\int\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{n}\,d\mu_{\lambda_{0}+\epsilon}-\int\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{n}\,d\mu_{\lambda_{0}}}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\int\phi_{n}\,d\mu_{\lambda_{0}+\epsilon}-\int\phi_{n}\,d\mu_{\lambda_{0}})}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\int F(\epsilon,n)\,dc(n)\quad c\text{ is the counting measure on }\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\geq\int\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}F(\epsilon,n)\,dc(n)\quad\text{ by Fatou's Lemma }

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}^{\prime}(0)=\infty,

which implies

is not differentiable at

. Note that the second equality holds because

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\int\phi_{n}\,d\mu_{\lambda_{0}+\epsilon}
is absolutely convergent.
∎

3.3.

In this subsection we prove Proposition

3.3.1.
Linear Response for

Observables

Recall that
\mu_{\lambda}
is the law of

\displaystyle X(\lambda)\!\!=\!\!\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}\lambda^{m-1}
,
\lambda\in(0,1)

where the

’s are i.i.d. random variables taking values in
\{-1,1\}
with probability
\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}
. In particular, for any fixed
a_{i}
’s, the right hand side is analytic in
\lambda
. To emphasize the dependence of

on

’s, we write it as
\displaystyle X(\lambda)=X(\lambda;a)
, where

.

Let
\nu
be the
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
-Bernoulli measure on the symbolic space
\Sigma
. Then by definition,
\displaystyle\mu_{\lambda}=\left(X(\lambda;\cdot)\right)_{*}\nu
, and therefore

h_{\phi}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\,d\mu_{\lambda}(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\phi(X(\lambda;a))\,d\nu(a).

This enables us to write down an explicit formula for the derivative if
\phi
is

:

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
\phi\in C^{1}
, then for any
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
,

is differentiable at
\lambda_{0}
with

h^{\prime}_{\phi}(\lambda_{0})=\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}(X(\lambda_{0};a))X^{(1)}(\lambda_{0};a)\,d\nu(a).

Here
\displaystyle X^{(1)}(\lambda;a):=\sum_{m\geq 1}ma_{m+1}\lambda^{m-1}
is the formal derivative
\frac{\partial X(\lambda;a)}{\partial\lambda}
.

Proof.

We wish to switch the order of derivative and integration, so that

(3.2)

\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\int_{\Sigma}\phi(X(\lambda;a))\,d\nu(a)\right)|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\phi(X(\lambda;a))\right)|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}\,d\nu(a).

The lemma follows by expanding the right hand side by chain rule.

To justify the above equality, note that
X(\cdot;\cdot)
is uniformly bounded for all
a\in\Sigma
and
\lambda
near
\lambda_{0}
. Since
\phi^{\prime}
is continuous, it is also uniformly bounded on the domain of
X
. Moreover,
X^{(1)}
is also uniformly bounded for all
a\in\Sigma
and
\lambda
near
\lambda_{0}
. It follows that

\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)

is uniformly bounded for all
a\in\Sigma
and
\lambda
near
\lambda_{0}
. Thus equation (

) is valid.
∎

We include two more technical lemmas needed later. The first is the formula for

Lemma 3.5

Suppose

Here

The proof is completely analogous to that of Lemma

The second lemma says that

is not too small if

itself is large:

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there exists
\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}(\delta)>0
satisfying the following. For any
\lambda\in(\delta,1-\delta)
and

, then
X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)>\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Fix an
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\geq 10
and that

\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}n(1-\delta)^{n-1}<\frac{1}{2}.

Clearly
m
only depends only on
\delta
. Let
\delta^{\prime}=\frac{\delta^{m-1}}{2}
. We claim that the lemma holds with this choice of
\delta^{\prime}
.

Suppose
X(\lambda;a)>\frac{1}{1-\lambda}-\delta^{\prime}
. Observe that

must satisfy

. Otherwise

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{m-1}}{2}=\delta^{\prime}>\frac{1}{1-\lambda}-X(\lambda;a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n+1}\lambda^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(1-a_{n+1})\lambda^{n}+\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}(1-a_{n+1})\lambda^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{m-1}>\delta^{m-1}

\displaystyle X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n+1}\lambda^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}na_{n+1}\lambda^{n-1}+\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}na_{n+1}\lambda^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}n\lambda^{n-1}

\displaystyle>1-\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}n(1-\delta)^{n-1}>\frac{1}{2}

G_{\delta}
set because
\phi\in G
. Moreover, any
\psi\in G
satisfies that
h_{\psi}
is not differentiable at
\lambda
.

Therefore it suffices to show that
G
is dense in
C^{\theta}
. To prove this fix any
f\in C^{\theta}
and
\kappa>0
. We claim that there exists
0\leq\kappa^{\prime}\leq\kappa
such that
f+\kappa^{\prime}\phi\in G
, which immediately implies density. Note that if
f\in G
already then the claim is trivial. Thus we assume
f\notin G
.

By definition,
f\notin G
means that there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq N_{0}
,

\left|\frac{h_{f}(\lambda+\epsilon_{n})-h_{f}(\lambda)}{\epsilon_{n}}\right|\leq n.

It follows that for any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
and
n\geq N_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{h_{f+\kappa^{\prime}\phi}(\lambda+\epsilon_{n})-h_{f+\kappa^{\prime}\phi}(\lambda)}{\epsilon_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq-\left|\frac{h_{f}(\lambda+\epsilon_{n})-h_{f}(\lambda)}{\epsilon_{n}}\right|+\kappa^{\prime}\left|\frac{h_{\phi}(\lambda+\epsilon_{n})-h_{\phi}(\lambda)}{\epsilon_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq-n+\kappa^{\prime}n^{2}

which will be greater than
n
if
n\geq N_{0}
is sufficiently large. This implies that
f+\kappa^{\prime}\phi\in G
for any
\kappa^{\prime}>0
, and in particular the claim is true.

4.
Almost everywhere Non-differentiability

4.1.
Overview

Here we prove Theorem
1.3
.
The required function is constructed via a stochastic process, so that
the increments of
\phi
on disjoint cylinders inside the support of
\mu_{\lambda}
is like a sum of independent random variables whose variance is not too small.

More precisely, we will construct a sequence
\{N_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for any fixed
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
, with probability one, the random function
\displaystyle\phi:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\phi_{N_{k}}
will be
C^{\alpha}
satisfying that
h_{\phi}
is not differentiable at
\lambda
. Then by Tonelli-Fubini, for almost every
\phi
constructed in this way,
h_{\phi}
is not differentiable at Lebesgue almost everywhere
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2})
.

The
C^{\alpha}
part is simpler to arrange. We will choose suitable parameters (in terms of
\alpha
and
\delta
) in the construction of
\phi_{N_{k}}
as well as a rapidly growing
\{N_{k}\}
such that with probability one,

||\phi_{N_{k}}||_{C^{\alpha}}:=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|\phi_{N_{k}}(x)|+\sup_{x\neq y\in[0,1]}\frac{|\phi_{N_{k}}(x)-\phi_{N_{k}}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}

is summable in
k
.

To make
h_{\phi}
non differentiable at
\lambda
, we further construct a sequence
\{\epsilon_{k}\}
decreasing to
0
such that with probability one, the main contribution of the term

\displaystyle\left|\frac{h_{\phi}(\lambda+\epsilon_{k})-h_{\phi}(\lambda)}{\epsilon_{k}}\right|

comes from

\left|\frac{h_{\phi_{N_{k}}}(\lambda+\epsilon_{k})-h_{\phi_{N_{k}}}(\lambda)}{\epsilon_{k}}\right|

and the latter blows up to infinity.

Here is the plan for the rest of this section. In §
4.2
we list several technical lemmas needed for the proof. In §
4.3

we construct
\{\phi_{N_{k}}\}
and describe how to choose
\{N_{k}\}
and
\{\epsilon_{k}\}
according to the parameters in its construction. In §
4.4

we show how to deduce Theorem
1.3
once all those data are constructed. Finally in §
4.5
we verify that such a construction can be done by an iterative scheme.

4.2.
Preliminary Lemmas

In this section we collect the auxiliary results needed later for the proof.

4.2.1.
Probability Lemmas

Lemma 4.1

(Berry-Esseen
[
21
]
)
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\cdots,\mu_{k}
be probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
with finite third moments
\gamma_{i}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{3}\,d\mu_{i}(x)
. Let
\mu:=\mu_{1}*\cdots*\mu_{k}
and
G
the Gaussian measure with the same mean and variance with
\mu
. Then there exists some absolute constant
c>0
such that, for any interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
,

|\mu(I)-G(I)|\leq c\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}}{\text{Var}(\mu)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Lemma 4.2

(Azuma
[
3
]
-Hoeffding
[
32
]
)
.

Suppose
\{S_{k}:k=0,1,2,\cdots\}
is a martingale and
\displaystyle|S_{k}-S_{k-1}|\leq c_{k},\forall k\geq 1
alsmot surely. Then for any positive integer
N
and
\epsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}(|S_{N}-S_{0}|\geq\epsilon)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}^{2}}\right).

4.2.2.
Calculus Lemma

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded
C^{1}
function with bounded derivative. Define

\displaystyle|f|_{C^{0}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|
and

\displaystyle|f|_{C^{\alpha}}:=\sup_{x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}
. Then
\displaystyle|f|_{c^{\alpha}}\leq 2|f^{\prime}|^{\alpha}_{C^{0}}|f|^{1-\alpha}_{C^{0}}
.

Proof.

If
f
is a constant function, then the conclusion is trivially true. Therefore we assume
f
is non-constant, and thus
|f^{\prime}|_{C^{0}}>0
. Let
\rho:=\frac{|f|_{C^{0}}}{|f^{\prime}|_{C^{0}}}>0
. For any
x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}
, if
|x-y|>\rho
, then

\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}<2|f|_{C^{0}}\rho^{-\alpha}=2|f^{\prime}|^{\alpha}_{C^{0}}|f|^{1-\alpha}_{C^{0}}.

If
|x-y|\leq\rho
, then

\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{|x-y|\left(\sup_{t\in[y,x]}|f^{\prime}(t)|\right)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\leq|f^{\prime}|_{C^{0}}\rho^{1-\alpha}=|f^{\prime}|^{\alpha}_{C^{0}}|f|^{1-\alpha}_{C^{0}}

and the lemma is proved.
∎

4.2.3.
Self-similar Measure Lemma

The purpose of this section is to show that the
\mu_{\lambda}
-measure of certain sets cannot be too small. To do that we need to view
\mu_{\lambda}
as the stationary measure of an iterated function system.

More precisely, let
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
f^{\lambda}_{1}(x)=\lambda x-1
and
f_{2}^{\lambda}(x)=\lambda x+1
. One can form a Markov chain with phase space
\mathbb{R}
, and the transition rule being that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, with probability
\frac{1}{2}
it moves to
f_{1}^{\lambda}(x)
and probability
\frac{1}{2}
to
f_{2}^{\lambda}(x)
.
\mu_{\lambda}
is the unique stationary measure of this Markov chain, whose support
\Lambda_{\lambda}
is a Cantor set if
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and is the interval
[-\frac{1}{1-\lambda},\frac{1}{1-\lambda}]
if
\lambda\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
.

In this section we are interested in the case
\lambda<\frac{1}{2}
, where
\mu_{\lambda}
has the following property. Let
K_{\lambda}:=[-\frac{1}{1-\lambda},\frac{1}{1-\lambda}]
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\Sigma_{m}:=\{1,2\}^{m}
to be the space of words of length
m
. For any
w=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{m})\in\Sigma_{m}
, define
\displaystyle C_{w}:=f_{w}^{\lambda}(K_{\lambda})=f^{\lambda}_{w_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f^{\lambda}_{w_{m}}(K_{\lambda})
. We call
C_{w}
a
level
m
cylinder
of
\mu_{\lambda}
. It is easy to see that the level-
m
cylinders are pairwise disjoint, and
\mu_{\lambda}(C_{w})=\frac{1}{2^{m}},\forall w\in\Sigma_{m}
.

We are ready to state the main result of this subsection. The assumptions are motivated by the proof of Proposition
4.7
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix any
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, there exists a
\rho^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime}(\delta)>0
such that the following holds. For any
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
, any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and any level-
m
cylinder
C
, suppose
Q\subset C
is a union of at most
L:=[\frac{1}{2\delta}]+1
intervals whose total Lebesgue measure is less than
\rho^{\prime}|C|=\rho^{\prime}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{1-\lambda}
. Then

\mu_{\lambda}(Q)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\lambda}(C).

Proof.

By self-similarity, we can assume
m=0
and
C=[-\frac{1}{1-\lambda},\frac{1}{1-\lambda}]
without loss of generality.

Take
l=l(\delta)=[\log_{2}(4L)]+1
, and
\rho^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime}(\delta)=\frac{1}{2}L\delta^{l}
. We claim that this will meet the requirement.

Suppose
\displaystyle Q=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}Q_{j}
is a disjoint union of
r\leq L
intervals, with

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}|Q_{j}|\leq\rho^{\prime}\frac{1}{1-\lambda}
. Here
|\cdot|
is the Lebesgue measure. Consider the level-
l
cylinders of
\mu_{\lambda}
. And denote by
n_{j}
the number of level-
l
cylinders that has nonempty intersection with
Q_{j},j=1,\cdots,r
. Let
\displaystyle n:=\sum_{j=1}^{r}n_{j}
.

Note that for any two distinct level-
l
cylinders, the distance between them is at least
\displaystyle d_{l}:=\frac{2\lambda^{l-1}(1-2\lambda)}{1-\lambda}
. Therefore,
\displaystyle|Q_{j}|\geq(n_{j}-1)d_{l}
, and it follows that

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}\frac{1}{1-\lambda}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{r}|Q_{j}|\geq\sum_{j=1}^{r}(n_{j}-1)d_{l}\geq(n-r)d_{l}\geq(n-L)d_{l}.

This implies that

n\leq\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{(1-\lambda)d_{l}}+L=\frac{\frac{1}{2}L\delta^{l}}{2\lambda^{l-1}(1-2\lambda)}+L<\frac{\frac{1}{2}L\delta^{l}}{2\delta^{l-1}2\delta}+L<2L.

Thus

\mu_{\lambda}(Q)\leq n\frac{1}{2^{l}}<2L\frac{1}{4L}=\frac{1}{2}

as desired.
∎

4.3.
Construction of
\phi_{N_{k}}

This subsection contains several technical definitions in preparation for the construction.

We start by fixing a family
\{g_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in(0,1)}
of
C^{2}
functions such that for each
\rho
,
g_{\rho}:[0,2]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfies the following

(1)

supp
g_{\rho}\subset[\frac{1-\rho}{3},2-\frac{1-\rho}{3}]
,

(2)

g_{\rho}
is constantly equal to
1
on the interval
[1-\rho,1+\rho]
.

So the shape of
g_{\rho}
is a bump function concentrated in the interval
[0,2]
, and the middle
\rho
-proportion is constantly
1
.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Divide
[0,2]
evenly into
N
sub-intervals

I_{j}^{(N)}=\left[\frac{2(j-1)}{N},\frac{2j}{N}\right],\;\;j=1,\cdots,N
and define two random functions
g_{N,\rho},\phi_{N,\rho}
in the following way. First, for any
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
and any
x\in I_{j}^{(N)}
,

g_{N,\rho}(x)=\begin{cases}N^{\theta_{1}}g_{\rho}(Nx-j+1),\quad\text{prob}=N^{-\theta_{2}};\\
-N^{\theta_{1}}g_{\rho}(Nx-j+1),\;\text{prob}=N^{-\theta_{2}};\\
0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;\,\text{prob}=1-2N^{-\theta_{2}},\end{cases}

and the outcomes of
g_{N,\rho}
on the
I_{j}^{(N)}
’s are independent. Note that by construction,
g_{N,\rho}
is always equal to
0
on the intervals
\displaystyle\left[\frac{2(j-1)}{N}+\frac{2(1-\rho)}{3N},\frac{2j}{N}-\frac{2(1-\rho)}{3N}\right],j=1,\cdots,N
, and thus it is a random
C^{2}
function.

Next define

\phi_{N,\rho}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}g_{N,\rho}(t)\,dt
for
x\in[0,2]

and let
\phi_{N,\rho}
take constant values to left of 0 and to the right of 2.
We call
\phi_{N,\rho}
a
generalized Brownian Motion
at scale
N
with parameters
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\rho
.

Definition 4.2
.

Given
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0
and
\beta>0
, let
\rho=\rho(\delta)=1-\frac{1}{100}\frac{\delta\rho^{\prime}}{2-\rho^{\prime}}
where
\rho^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2\delta}+1)\delta^{\log_{2}(\frac{2}{\delta}+4)+1}
(this choice will be explained in Lemma
4.8
).

Let
\{N_{k}\}
be a sequence of increasing natural numbers, and
\{\epsilon_{k}\}
be a sequence of positive reals decreasing to
0
. We say that
\displaystyle(\{N_{k}\},\{\epsilon_{k}\})
is an
adapted pair
to
(\delta,\alpha,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\beta)
if the following holds:

(1)

Let
\phi_{N_{k},\rho}
be a generalized Brownian motion at scale
N_{k}
with parameters
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\rho
, and suppose
\phi_{N_{k},\rho}
’s are independent. Then for any
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
,

(4.1)

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\text{ s.t. }\forall k\geq k_{0},\,||\phi_{N_{k},\rho}||_{C^{\alpha}}\leq N_{k}^{-\beta},\,\left|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N_{k}.\rho}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)\right|\geq N_{k}^{\beta}\right)=1.

(2)

For each integer
n\geq 2
,
\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}N_{l}^{\theta_{1}}<N_{n}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
.

(3)

For any integer
n\geq 1
,
\epsilon_{n}
is so small that for any
\epsilon
with
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon_{n}
,
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
and any outcome of
\phi_{N_{n},\rho}

satisfying
\displaystyle|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N_{n},\rho}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)|\geq N_{n}^{\beta}
, we have

h_{N_{n}}(\lambda+\epsilon)-h_{N_{n}}(\lambda)=\epsilon\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N_{n}}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)+R_{n}(\lambda;\epsilon)

with

\left|\frac{R_{n}(\lambda;\epsilon)}{\epsilon}\right|\leq\frac{1}{100}\left|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N_{n},\rho}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)\right|.

Here
\displaystyle h_{N_{n}}(\lambda):=\int\phi_{N_{n},\rho}(x)\,d\mu_{\lambda}(x)
.

(4)

For any integer
n\geq 1
,
\displaystyle\sum_{l=n+1}^{\infty}N_{l}^{-\beta}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{100}
.

4.4.
Existence of Adapted Pair implies Main Theorem

Proposition 4.5
.

Given
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0
and
\beta>0
, let
\displaystyle\rho=\rho(\delta)=1-\frac{1}{100}\frac{\delta\rho^{\prime}}{2-\rho^{\prime}}
where
\rho^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2\delta}+1)\delta^{\log_{2}(\frac{2}{\delta}+4)+1}
. Suppose that there exists an adapted pair
\displaystyle(\{N_{k}\},\{\epsilon_{k}\})
to
(\delta,\alpha,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\beta)
. Let
\{\phi_{N_{k},\rho}\}
be a sequence of independent generalized Brownian motion at scale
N_{k}
with parameters
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\rho
. Then with probability one,
\displaystyle\phi:=\sum_{k}\phi_{N_{k},\rho}
is
C^{\alpha}
and

h_{\phi}
is not differentiable at Lebesgue almost every
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
.

Before going to the proof, let us make a notational remark that, since in the rest of this section,
\delta
will be fixed and so is
\rho
, we will omit the subscript and write
\phi_{N_{k}}
instead of
\phi_{N_{k},\rho}
and similarly
g_{N_{k}}
instead of
g_{N_{k},\rho}
.

Proof.

We claim that for any fixed
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
, if the events in
(
4.1
) happen (which has probability
1
), then

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda+\epsilon_{k}}-\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda}}{\epsilon_{k}}\right|=\infty.

Clearly, Proposition
4.5
follows from this by Tonelli-Fubini.

To show the claim, note that since
\displaystyle\phi:=\sum_{k}\phi_{N_{k}}
and
||\phi_{N_{k}}||_{C^{\alpha}}
is summable
(by Definition
4.2
(4)),
one can interchange summation and integration, and write for any
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda+\epsilon_{k}}-\int\phi\,d\mu_{\lambda}}{\epsilon_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\frac{\int\phi_{N_{l}}\,d\mu_{\lambda+\epsilon_{k}}-\int\phi_{N_{l}}\,d\mu_{\lambda}}{\epsilon_{k}}+\frac{\int\phi_{N_{k}}\,d\mu_{\lambda+\epsilon_{k}}-\int\phi_{N_{k}}\,d\mu_{\lambda}}{\epsilon_{k}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{\int\phi_{N_{l}}\,d\mu_{\lambda+\epsilon_{k}}-\int\phi_{N_{l}}\,d\mu_{\lambda}}{\epsilon_{k}}=:S_{k}+T_{k}+R_{k}.

We will show that
|T_{k}|\to\infty
and
S_{k},R_{k}=o(T_{k})
, which readily implies the claim.

First,

(4.3)

\displaystyle|T_{k}|=\left|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N_{k}}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)\right|+\frac{R_{k}(\lambda,\epsilon_{k})}{\epsilon_{k}}\geq\frac{99}{100}N_{k}^{\beta}.

Here the inequality holds by items
(1)
and
(3)
from definition
4.2
. In particular,
|T_{k}|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

Second,

\displaystyle|S_{k}|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\int_{\Sigma}g_{N_{l}}(X(\lambda^{\prime};a))X^{(1)}(\lambda^{\prime};a)\,d\nu(a)|\quad\text{for some }\lambda^{\prime}\in(\lambda,\lambda+\epsilon_{k})\text{ by Taylor's theorem}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}N_{l}^{\theta_{1}}\quad\text{for some absolute constant }C>0\text{ because }X^{(1)}\text{ is uniformly bounded}

\displaystyle\leq CN_{k}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\quad\text{by Definition \ref{Definition of adapted pair}(2)}

\displaystyle=o(T_{k})\quad\text{by \eqref{Estimate of T_k}}.

Finally,

(4.4)

|R_{k}|\leq\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{2\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|\phi_{N_{l}}|}{\epsilon_{k}}\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon_{k}}\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}||\phi_{N_{l}}||_{C^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon_{k}}\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}N_{l}^{-\beta}\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon_{k}}\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{100}=o(T_{k}).

where the last inequality is by item
(4)
of Definition
4.2
.
The proof is finished.
∎

4.5.
Existence of Adapted Pair

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
, fix a
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
as in its statement. By Proposition
4.5
, it suffices to find suitable
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\beta>0
and construct an adapted pair
(\{N_{k}\},\{\epsilon_{k}\})
to
(\delta,\alpha,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\beta)
. This will be the goal of this subsection.

Let us describe the idea of the construction. We will construct
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\beta
,
\{N_{k}\}
and
\{\epsilon_{k}\}
in an inductive way. Namely

•

First fix suitable
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\beta
in terms of
\delta,\alpha
.

•

The order of construction is then
N_{1}\to\epsilon_{1}\to N_{2}\to\epsilon_{2}\to\cdots
.

•

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
N_{k+1}
is sufficiently large in terms of
N_{1},\cdots,N_{k}
and
\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{k}
.

•

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\epsilon_{k+1}
is sufficiently small in terms of
N_{1},\cdots,N_{k+1}
and
\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{k}
.

With these arrangements,
N_{k}
will grow rapidly enough, so that
||\phi_{N_{k}}||_{C^{\alpha}}
will be summable with probability one by Borel-Cantelli and Azuma-Hoeffding. Moreover, the quantity
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N_{k}}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)
can be written as a sum of many independent random variables with large total variance. Using Borel-Contelli and Berry-Esseen, it will be large with probability one as well. As for item
(3)
in Definition
4.2
, it will hold simply due to Taylor’s theorem, as
\epsilon_{n}
is small compared to previous data. Finally,
items (2) and (4) in Definition
4.2
are easy to arrange if
\{N_{k}\}
grows sufficiently fast.

Here is the plan for the rest of this subsection. In §
4.5.1
we state a sufficient condition for item
(1)
. In §
4.5.2

we do the same for item
(2)
and in §
4.5.3
we combine them to finish the construction.

4.5.1.
Summable Hölder Norm and Large Derivative

Here we show that for any
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, with suitable choice of
\beta,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
, item
(1)
in Definition
4.2
will be true provided
\{N_{k}\}
grows rapidly enough (in terms of
\beta,\theta_{2},\theta_{2},\delta,\alpha
). This can be broken into two steps:

Proposition 4.6
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
the following is true. If
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\beta>0
are such that
\displaystyle 2u:=1-2\theta_{1}-2\frac{\alpha\theta_{1}+\beta}{1-\alpha}>0
(clearly this can be achieved as long as
\theta_{1}
and
\beta
are sufficiently small). Then for any increasing sequence
\{N_{k}\}
of positive integers with

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\exp\{-N_{k}^{u}\}<\infty,

it holds that
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\text{ s.t. }\forall k\geq k_{0},\,||\phi_{N_{k}}||_{C^{\alpha}}\leq N_{k}^{-\beta}\right)=1.

Proposition 4.7
.

For any
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
the following is true. Fix any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\beta>0
such that
\displaystyle\beta<\theta_{1}
and
\displaystyle-\tau:=\theta_{2}+\log_{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}2<0
. For any sequence
\{N_{k}\}
of increasing positive integers satisfying

\sum_{k}N_{k}^{-\tau}<\infty.

and any
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\text{ s.t. }\forall k\geq k_{0},\,|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N_{k}}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)|\geq N_{k}^{\beta}\right)=1.

Combining the two Propositions, it follows that for any fixed small
\delta>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, as long as
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\alpha
and
\{N_{k}\}
are such that

•

\displaystyle 2u:=1-2\theta_{1}-2\frac{\alpha\theta_{1}+\beta}{1-\alpha}>0
.

•

\displaystyle\beta<\theta_{1}
and
\displaystyle-\tau:=\theta_{2}+\log_{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}2<0
.

•

\displaystyle\sum_{k}N_{k}^{-\tau}<\infty
and hence
\displaystyle\sum_{k}\exp\{-N_{k}^{u}\}<\infty
,

then item
(1)
from Definition
4.2
is true. The rest of this subsection is devoted to proving the propositions.

Proof of Proposition
4.6
.

Given a function
f:\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[0,2]\to\mathbb{R}
let

\displaystyle|f|_{C^{0}}=\sup_{x\in[0,2]}|f(x)|
and
\displaystyle|f|_{C^{\alpha}}=\sup_{x\neq y\in[0,2]}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

We will show that

(4.5)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N_{k}}|_{C^{0}}>\frac{1}{2}N_{k}^{-\beta}\right)<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N_{k}}|_{C^{\alpha}}>\frac{1}{2}N_{k}^{-\beta}\right)<\infty.

This together with the Borel-Cantelli Lemma and the identity
||\cdot||_{C^{\alpha}}=|\cdot|_{C^{0}}+|\cdot|_{C^{\alpha}}
implies the proposition.

We begin with
|\cdot|_{C^{0}}
norm and aim to bound
\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N_{k}}|_{C^{0}}>\frac{1}{2}N_{k}^{-\beta}\right)
. For simplicity we fix
k
and write
N
for
N_{k}
.

Observe that by the construction of
\phi_{N}
,
\displaystyle|\phi_{N}|_{C^{0}}=\max\{|\phi_{N}(x_{j})|:j=0,1,\cdots,N\}
where
x_{j}:=\frac{2j}{N}
. Define
X_{0}=0,X_{1}=\phi_{N}(x_{1})
and
X_{j}=\phi_{N}(x_{j})-\phi_{N}(x_{j-1}),j=2,3,\cdots,N
. Then
\phi_{N}(x_{j})=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{j}=:S_{j}
, and it is not hard to see that
\{X_{j}:j\geq 1\}
is a sequence of i.i.d with mean zero. Moreover,
|X_{j}|\leq N^{\theta_{1}-1}
. Thus we can apply Lemma
4.2
to the martingale
\{S_{j}\}
to conclude that for any
l\in[1,N]\cap\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{l}|>\frac{1}{2}N^{-\beta}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\frac{1}{4}N^{-2\beta}}{2lN^{2\theta_{1}-2}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{1}{8}N^{1-2\beta-2\theta_{1}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{N^{2u}}{8}\right)

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
1-2\beta-2\theta_{1}>2u
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N}|_{C^{0}}>\frac{1}{2}N^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\max\{|S_{l}|:l=1,2,\cdots,N\}>\frac{1}{2}N^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{l}|>\frac{1}{2}N^{\beta}\right)\leq N\exp\left(-\frac{N^{2u}}{8}\right)\leq\exp(-N^{u})

where the last inequality holds for all sufficiently large
N
. Thus

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N_{k}}|_{C^{0}}>\frac{1}{2}N_{k}^{\beta}\right)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{N_{k}^{u}}{8}\right)<\infty

as desired.

Next we turn to
|\cdot|_{C^{\alpha}}
norm and aim to bound
\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N_{k}}|_{C^{\alpha}}>\frac{1}{2}N_{k}^{-\beta}\right)
. Again we fix
k
and write
N
for
N_{k}
.

Since
\phi_{N}
is
C^{1}
, by Lemma
4.3
,
\displaystyle|\phi_{N}|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq 2|\phi_{N}^{\prime}|_{C^{0}}^{\alpha}|\phi_{N}|_{C^{0}}^{1-\alpha}\leq 2N^{\alpha\theta_{1}}|\phi_{N}|_{C^{0}}^{1-\alpha}
. Therefore an easy calculation shows that
|\phi_{N}|_{C^{\alpha}}>\frac{1}{2}N^{-\beta}
implies
|\phi_{N}|_{C^{0}}>4^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}N^{-\frac{\alpha\theta_{1}+\beta}{1-\alpha}}.

Applying Lemma
4.2
again we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N}|_{C^{\alpha}}>\frac{1}{2}N^{-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N}|_{C^{0}}>4^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}N^{-\frac{\alpha\theta_{1}+\beta}{1-\alpha}}\right)\leq

\displaystyle N\exp\left(-\frac{4^{\frac{2}{\alpha-1}}N^{-2\frac{\alpha\theta_{1}+\beta}{1-\alpha}}}{2NN^{2\theta_{1}-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\exp\left(-2^{\frac{5-\alpha}{\alpha-1}}N^{1-2\theta_{1}-2\frac{\alpha\theta_{1}+\beta}{1-\alpha}}\right)\leq N\exp\left(-\frac{1}{8}N^{2u}\right)\leq\exp\left(-N^{u}\right).

Here the last inequality is true as long as
N
is sufficiently large in terms of
\alpha
. Thus

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|\phi_{N_{k}}|_{C^{\alpha}}>\frac{1}{2}N_{k}^{\beta}\right)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{N_{k}^{u}}{8}\right)<\infty

and the proposition is proved.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.7
.

By Borel-Cantelli Lemma, it suffices to show that

(4.6)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N_{k}}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)\right|<N_{k}^{\beta}\right)<\infty.

N
for

.

Let

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta\right)^{n}<\frac{1}{4(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}n\left(\frac{1}{2}-\delta\right)^{n-1}<\frac{1}{2}.

Clearly such

Consider the level-

(for the definition of level-

).
Label them by
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{2^{m}}

To apply Berry-Esseen Theorem we need to estimate the variance and third moment of

Lemma 4.8
.

\mathbb{E}[D_{l}^{2}]\geq c_{1}2^{-2m}N^{2\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}},\;\forall l\in[1,2^{m-n_{0}}]\cap\mathbb{Z}

\mathbb{E}[|D_{l}^{3}|]\leq c_{2}2^{-3m}N^{3\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}},\forall l

We postpone the proof of these two lemmas to the end of this section, and proceed to finish proving Proposition
4.7
. Let
N(0,\sigma^{2})

where the first inequality above is due to independence. Then by Berry Esseen Theorem
(Lemma

)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)|<N^{\beta}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(N(0,\sigma^{2})\in[-N^{\beta},N^{\beta}]\right)+c\frac{\sum_{l=1}^{2^{m}}\mathbb{E}[|D_{l}|^{3}]}{\sigma^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(N(0,1)\in\left[-\frac{N^{\beta}}{\sigma},\frac{N^{\beta}}{\sigma}\right]\right)+c\frac{2^{m-n_{0}}c_{2}2^{-3m}N^{3\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}}{(2^{m}c_{1}2^{-2m}N^{2\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq c_{7}\frac{N^{\beta}}{\sqrt{2^{m}c_{1}2^{-2m}N^{2\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}}}+c_{8}2^{-\frac{m}{2}}N^{\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{9}2^{-\frac{m}{2}}(N^{\beta-\theta_{1}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}}+N^{\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}})\leq c_{10}N^{\frac{\theta_{2}+\log_{\lambda}2}{2}}\leq c_{10}N^{\frac{\theta_{2}+\log_{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}2}{2}}=:c_{10}N^{-\tau}.

Here in the second inequality we used Lemmas
4.8
and
4.9

has continuous density near

, and
\displaystyle\frac{N^{\beta}}{\sigma}\approx N^{\beta-\theta_{1}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}+\frac{\log_{\lambda}2}{2}}\ll 1

. We also used the fact that

and

is an absolute constant depending only on

.

Thus (
4.6
) holds as long as

which is the desired conclusion.
∎

and

4.5.2.
Small Remainder

The purpose of this subsection is to justify property
(3)
from Definition
4.2
. More precisely, we will prove the following

Proposition 4.10
.

Given any small
\delta>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\beta>0
,
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0
and positive integers
N
, there exists
\epsilon_{*}>0
such that the following is true.

Let
\phi_{N}
be a generalized Brownian motions at scale
N
with parameters
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\rho(\delta)
. Then for any
\epsilon

|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon_{*}
, any

Here
\displaystyle h_{N}(\lambda):=\int\phi_{N}(x)\,d\mu_{\lambda}(x).

Clearly one can take the
N_{n},\epsilon_{n}
in Definition
4.2
(3) to be the
N,\epsilon_{*}
in the above proposition. In the rest of this subsection we prove Proposition

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.4
,
3.5
and Taylor’s theorem,

h_{N}(\lambda+\epsilon)-h_{N}(\lambda)=\epsilon\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)+R_{N}(\lambda;\epsilon)

where

\displaystyle R_{N}(\lambda;\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}h^{\prime\prime}_{\phi_{N}}(\lambda+\epsilon^{\prime})\quad\text{ for some }\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon^{\prime}(\lambda,\epsilon)\in(0,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}\int_{\Sigma}\phi_{N}^{\prime\prime}(X(\lambda+\epsilon^{\prime};a))\left(X^{(1)}(\lambda+\epsilon^{\prime};a)\right)^{2}+\phi_{N}^{\prime}(X(\lambda+\epsilon^{\prime};a))X^{(2)}(\lambda+\epsilon^{\prime};a)\,d\nu(a).

Recall that here,
\displaystyle X^{(2)}(\lambda;a):=\sum_{m\geq 2}m(m-1)a_{m+1}\lambda^{m-2}
is the formal derivative
\frac{\partial^{2}X(\lambda;a)}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

Given
C^{2}

define
\displaystyle||f||_{C^{2}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}(x)|+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime\prime}(x)|
.
Then it is easy to see from the definition of
\phi_{N_{n}}
that

||\phi_{N}||_{C^{2}}\leq N^{2+\theta_{1}}||g_{\rho}||_{C^{2}}

and the right hand side is a constant independent of
\epsilon
. Here it is important that we have a fixed
\rho=\rho(\delta)
. Moreover, it is not hard to see that both

are no greater than
10
for any
\lambda\in(\delta,\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
and

. It follows that

Therefore, as long as
\displaystyle|\epsilon_{*}|\leq\frac{1}{500}N^{\beta-2-\theta_{1}}||g_{\rho}||^{-1}_{C^{2}}
,

\left|\frac{R_{N}(\lambda;\epsilon)}{\epsilon}\right|\leq 5N^{2+\theta_{1}}||g_{\rho}|||\epsilon|\leq\frac{N^{\beta}}{100}\leq\frac{1}{100}\left|\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}_{N}(X(\lambda;a))X^{(1)}(\lambda;a)\,d\nu(a)\right|

as desired.
∎
