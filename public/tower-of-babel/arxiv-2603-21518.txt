On the discriminant locus of a generic projection
Si-Yang Liu, Yilong Zhang

Abstract
. For a smooth projective variety X ⊆ ℙ N X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N} over an algebraically closed field of char 0 0 , we show that the discriminant locus of a generic projection of X X is projectively dual to a general linear section of the dual variety, and deduce a purity statement for the discriminant. Over ℂ \mathbb{C} , we also show that the fundamental group of the complement of the branch divisor arising from generic projection of a normal hypersurface surjects onto a braid group via braid monodromy.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of char
0
. Given an embedding
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
, a
Lefschetz pencil
on
X
is a choice of a general one-parameter family of hyperplane sections of
X
, which induces a rational map

X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}.

The general member is smooth and the number of the singular members is called the
class
of
X
. When the dual variety
X^{\perp}
is a hypersurface, this equals
\deg X^{\perp}
and is computed from the topology of the Lefschetz pencil
[
21
,
24
]
. When
\Bbbk=\mathbb{C}
, the fibration over finite discriminant complement
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta
induces a braid monodromy representation

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta,*)\to\textup{Aut}H^{*}(X\cap H,\mathbb{Z}),

where
X\cap H
is a smooth hyperplane section, which carries rich topological information. This is the classical Picard–Lefschetz theory
[
38
]
.

The motivation of this paper is to generalize this investigation to a Lefschetz net, and more broadly, a higher-dimensional linear subsystem of
|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)|
. Such a system arises from projectivization
\mathbb{P}V
of a general
(k+1)
dimensional subspace
V\subseteq H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathcal{O}(1))
. There are two natural families associated with the linear system. The first comes from observing that
\mathbb{P}V
is naturally a subspace of the dual space
(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
, so the base change of the universal family of hyperplane sections of
X
over
(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
to
\mathbb{P}V
gives rise to the family

(1)

\mathcal{X}_{V}\to\mathbb{P}V,

whose fiber over
H\in\mathbb{P}V
is the hyperplane section
X\cap H
.

The second family is a generic projection

(2)

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}V^{\vee},\ x\mapsto[l_{0}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}l_{1}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ldots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}l_{k}(x)],

where
l_{0},\ldots,l_{k}
are
k+1
general linear forms forming a basis of
V
. The two families are related by a linear dual on the ambient projective space. When
k=1
, up to blowing up the base locus, the two families coincide and are precisely the Lefschetz fibration. When
k\geq 2
, they are different: The fiber of generic projection is a higher codimension linear section. We want to explore the relationship between the two families, particularly where the fibers become singular. This is captured by the notion of discriminant.

Definition 1.1

(Discriminant locus)
.

Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{Y}\to B
be a flat family of projective varieties. Assume that
B
is smooth. Define the
discriminant locus

\Delta\subseteq B
as the closed subspace consisting of points
b
where the fiber
\mathcal{Y}_{b}
is singular.

When
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{Y}\to B
is a proper non-dominant map, we use the convention that
\Delta=f(\mathcal{Y})
. See more discussion in Section
3
.

For the hyperplane sections family (
1
), the discriminant locus is precisely the linear section by
\mathbb{P}V
of the dual variety
X^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{P}V

[
15
]
, which is reduced as long as
\mathbb{P}V
is in general position, and is moreover irreducible if it has positive dimension. When
k=2
, a general plane section
\mathbb{P}^{2}\cap X^{\perp}
is expected to have only ordinary nodes and cusps, then their numbers can be computed explicitly using intersection theory (cf.
[
9
, (2.2), (2.3)]
,
[
31
]
).

In this paper, we want to investigate similar questions for generic projection:

Question 1.2
.

How to characterize the discriminant locus
\Delta
of the generic projection of
X
?

In particular, one may ask about its dimension, irreducibility, degree, and singularities. Here we interpret the discriminant of the rational map (
2
) as the discriminant of the blowup
Bl_{B}X\to\mathbb{P}V^{\vee}
of the base locus.

1.1.
Duality of discriminant locus

We begin by addressing the question with projective duality.
All varieties are over an algebraically closed field of char
0
.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a smooth projective variety, and
\mathbb{P}V
is a general
k
-dimensional linear subspace in the dual space
(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
. Let
\Delta^{\perp}
denote the dual variety of the discriminant locus of the generic projection (
2
). Under the natural identification
(\mathbb{P}V^{\vee})^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{P}V
, one has

\Delta^{\perp}=\mathbb{P}V\cap X^{\perp}.

In particular, the discriminant loci of two families (
1
) and (
2
) are projectively dual to each other.

When
\pi
is not dominant (i.e.,
\dim(X)<k
),
\pi
is a generic immersion, and the discriminant coincides with the projection image
\pi(X)
. In this case, a version of Theorem
1.3
is discussed in
[
36
, Theorem 1.21]
and
[
18
, Propsition 6.1]
; When
X
is a hypersurface, Theorem
1.3
is consistent with
[
29
, Corollary 4.3]
. We are particularly interested in the case where the generic projection is dominant. We also show that the result still holds when
X
is normal and
\pi
is finite and dominant (cf. Theorem
5.7
).

1.2.
Purity of discriminant locus

Recall that the Zariski–Nagata purity theorem (see Theorem
3.4
) implies that a finite morphism from a normal variety to a smooth variety is either branched along a hypersurface, or is étale with empty branch locus. We prove an analogue for the discriminant locus of generic projection.

From now on,
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a smooth projective variety, and let

(3)

\pi_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k}

be a generic projection to
\mathbb{P}^{k}
and assume it is dominant (equivalently,
\dim(X)\geq k
). For simplicity, we identify
\mathbb{P}V^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{P}^{k}
once the generic projection is fixed.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
5.3
)
.

Depending on the codimension of the dual variety
X^{\perp}\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
, the discriminant locus
\Delta(\pi_{k})
of the generic projection
\pi_{k}
satisfies one of the following:

(i)

\Delta(\pi_{k})
is an irreducible hypersurface, if
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})<k
;

(ii)

\Delta(\pi_{k})
is a finite union of hyperplanes, if
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})=k
;

(iii)

\Delta(\pi_{k})
is empty, if
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})>k
.

In particular, the discriminant locus of a dominant generic projection is either of pure codimension one or is empty.

Note that case (i) is generic when
k\geq 2
. Indeed, smooth projective varieties with dual variety not a hypersurface are called
dual defective
, which are special (see Remark
2.7
); Case (ii) generalizes Lefschetz pencil in
k=1
; Case (iii) has the following application:

Corollary 1.5

(Corollary
6.1
)
.

A smooth projective variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is
k
-dual defective (i.e.,
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})>k
) if and only if the extension of the generic projection (
3
) to the blowup of base locus
B

\tilde{\pi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\textup{Bl}_{B}X\to\mathbb{P}^{k}

is a smooth morphism.

As a special case, when
k=1
, it implies dual defective varieties admit a smooth Lefschetz pencil. Corollary
1.5
recovers a classical result that dual varieties for smooth curves and surfaces are hypersurfaces unless they are linear (see Proposition
6.2
).

Remark 1.6

(Singular total space)
.

A generalization of Theorem
1.3
and
1.4
still hold without smoothness assumption of
X
, but the claims only apply to a closed subspace of the discriminant captured by the smooth locus of
X
(see Definition
3.1
, Theorem
4.1
and
5.3
).

1.3.
Degree of discriminant

A projective variety
X
and its dual
X^{\perp}
is related by a natural correspondence:

X\xleftarrow{\pi_{1}}N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}\xrightarrow{\pi_{2}}X^{\perp},

where
N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}
is the conormal variety of
X
and lives in
\mathbb{P}^{N}\times(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
. For a general
\mathbb{P}V\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
, the variety
\pi_{1}\big(\pi_{2}^{-1}(\mathbb{P}V\cap X^{\perp})\big)
is called the polar locus in the literature, whose degree is computed in
[
23
]
. We show that the discriminant locus
\Delta
of a generic projection is the projection image of the polar locus (which is called smooth critical locus in our Definition
3.1
). We show that this map is birational, hence they have the same degree and we obtain the following

Proposition 1.7
.

The discriminant locus
\Delta_{k}
of the generic projection (
3
) has degree

\deg(\Delta_{k})=\int_{N^{\perp}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(X)}\pi_{1}^{*}H^{k-1}\cdot\pi_{2}^{*}(H^{\prime})^{N-k},

where
H=c_{1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)
and
H^{\prime}=c_{1}\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}}(1)
are hyperplane classes.

We use Theorem
1.3
to deduce some classical results on the degree of polar loci. See Section
8
.

1.4.
Monodromy and braid groups.

For varieties over
\mathbb{C}
, fundamental groups of complements of hypersurfaces are closely related to the geometry of singular hypersurfaces and have been studied for a long time
[
42
,
20
,
27
,
9
,
8
]
. In some of the interesting examples, it’s computed that the fundamental groups of hypersurface complements are isomorphic to
braid groups
,
B_{m}
, which are roughly speaking the group of trajectories of a finite collection of points moving on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
(see Section
9
).

We extended the investigation to generic finite projection of a normal hypersurface, and relate to special dual varieties.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N+1}
be a normal irreducible hypersurface of degree
m
. Let
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the generic linear projection (note this is a finite branched cover). Let
\Delta
be the branch divisor.

•

Then
\deg(\Delta)=m(m-1)
, and there exists a surjective braid monodromy homomorphism

(4)

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\Delta,*)\twoheadrightarrow B_{m}.

•

When
Y
is the dual variety of a smooth projective variety
\mathcal{X}
, then the branch divisor
\Delta
is identified with
X^{\perp}
, where
X=H\cap\mathcal{X}
is a general hyperplane section. In particular, there is a surjection

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus X^{\perp},*)\twoheadrightarrow B_{m}.

Remark 1.9
.

Varieties
\mathcal{X}
with normal dual varieties are studied by Zak
[
40
]
. It is shown that such condition guarantees that
\mathcal{X}
is non-extendable, i.e., not a smooth hyperplane section of one-dimensional higher, if it is not a quadric hypersurface. Examples of such
\mathcal{X}
include degree two Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(see
n=2
below) and Severi varieties
[
41
]
.

Example 1.10
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=v_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
be the 2nd Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then
\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
is a normal cubic hypersurface (see Section
7.1
). A general hyperplane section
X=\mathcal{X}\cap H
is the rational normal quartic, and
X^{\perp}
is the hypersurface of degree
6
parameterizing the set of unordered
4
points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with at least two points colliding, using the identification
\mathbb{P}^{4}\cong\textup{Sym}^{4}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
. A general plane section
X^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a rational curve with
4
nodes and
6
cusps. This is a
maximal cuspidal rational curve
studied by Zariski
[
43
]
. It is shown that the fundamental group of the complement
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus X^{\perp},*)
is the braid group
B_{4}
. Hence the braid monodromy representation from Theorem
1.8
gives surjection
B_{4}\twoheadrightarrow B_{3}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we revisit classical projective duality from the viewpoint of linear algebra and conormal varieties, setting up the main geometric framework for our later results.

2.1.
Linear algebra

For vector space
\Bbbk^{N+1}
, we denote
(\Bbbk^{N+1})^{\vee}
its dual space consisting of linear functionals on
\Bbbk^{N+1}
. For any linear subspace
V\subseteq\Bbbk^{N+1}
, we denote
V^{\perp}\subseteq(\Bbbk^{N+1})^{\vee}
be the subspace consisting of linear functionals vanishing on
V
. Note that
\textup{codim}(V^{\perp})=\dim(V)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V^{\vee}
denote the dual space of
V
. Then there is a natural isomorphism of vector spaces

V^{\vee}\cong(\Bbbk^{N+1})^{\vee}/V^{\perp}.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence

0\to V\to\Bbbk^{N+1}\to\Bbbk^{N+1}/V\to 0.

Dualizing yields

0\leftarrow V^{\vee}\leftarrow(\Bbbk^{N+1})^{\vee}\leftarrow(\Bbbk^{N+1}/V)^{\vee}\leftarrow 0.

The subspace
(\Bbbk^{N+1}/V)^{\vee}
consists of those linear functionals on
\Bbbk^{N+1}
that vanish on
V
. Hence, it is canonically identified with
V^{\perp}
. This proves the claim.
∎

Passing to projectivization,
\mathbb{P}V\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a projective subspace, then
\mathbb{P}V^{\perp}\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
and induces the rational projection

(5)

p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}\big((\Bbbk^{N+1})^{\vee}/V^{\perp}\big)\cong\mathbb{P}V^{\vee}.

The indeterminacy locus of
p
is exactly
\mathbb{P}V^{\perp}
.

Identifying linear functionals with the set of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, the map (
5
) can be described by sending a hyperplane
H
of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
to a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}V
by intersection:

H\mapsto H\cap\mathbb{P}V.

The image defines a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}V
if and only if
H
is transversal to
\mathbb{P}V
, if and only if
\mathbb{P}V\nsubseteq H
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{P}V\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a point with dual hyperplane
x^{\perp}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{P}^{N}\right)^{\vee}
, then the projection
p\colon\left(\mathbb{P}^{N}\right)^{\vee}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}V^{\vee}
send the hyperplane
x^{\perp}
in
(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
to the dual hyperplane
x_{V}^{\perp}
of
x
in
\mathbb{P}V
. Conversely,
x^{\perp}
is the cone of
x_{V}^{\perp}
with center being the base locus
\mathbb{P}V^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Note that the dual of any
\alpha\in x^{\perp}\setminus\mathbb{P}V^{\perp}
is a hyperplane
\alpha^{\perp}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
containing
x
and is transversal to
\mathbb{P}V
, therefore the intersection
\alpha^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{P}V
is a hyperplane of
\mathbb{P}V
whose dual is
p(\alpha)\in\mathbb{P}V^{\vee}
. Let
x_{V}^{\perp}
be the dual of
x
in
\mathbb{P}V
. As
x\in\alpha^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{P}V
, we know that
p(\alpha)\in x_{V}^{\perp}
, and therefore
p(x^{\perp})\subseteq x_{V}^{\perp}
. Dimension counting tells us that
p(x^{\perp})=x_{V}^{\perp}
.
∎

2.2.
Projective duality

Now let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible subvariety. Let
x\in X^{sm}
be a closed point of
X
contained in the smooth locus
X^{sm}
, then the tangent space
T_{x}X
at
x
has the same dimension as
X
. We may regard
T_{x}X
as a projective subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
of dimension
\dim(X)
. We use this convention throughout the paper. Using our notation,
(T_{x}X)^{\perp}\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
is the conormal space and has dimension
N-\dim(X)-1
.

The
conormal variety

N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}X
of
X
is defined to be the closure of the union of conormal spaces at smooth points
[
17
]

(6)

N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}X=\overline{\{(x,H)\in X^{sm}\times(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}|T_{x}X\subseteq H\}}.

It records pairs of smooth points and their tangent hyperplanes.
It is irreducible and has dimension
N-1
. Note that here, to require the hyperplane
H
to contain
T_{x}X
is equivalent to finding a hyperplane
H
tangent to
X
at
x
. In the case when
X
is a hypersurface
f=0
of degree at least two, and
x
is a smooth point, such
H
is unique and coincides with the tangent hyperplane
T_{x}X
, which is determined by the normal vector
\nabla f_{x}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
dual variety
of
X
, denoted
X^{\perp}
throughout the paper, is defined to be the image of the projection map
N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}X\to(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is more common in the literature to denote the dual variety by
X^{*}
or
X^{\vee}
. We instead write
X^{\perp}
to emphasize that, by definition,
X^{\perp}
is the (closure of) the union of the conormal spaces

(T_{x}X)^{\perp}\subset(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee},\qquad x\in X^{\mathrm{sm}}.

In particular, we reserve the notation
(\ \cdot\ )^{\vee}
for dual vector spaces, and use
(\ \cdot\ )^{\perp}
for orthogonal complements and for dual varieties.

There is an incidence correspondence between
X
and
X^{\perp}
shown below.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\perp}\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}

Hence, the conormal space
(T_{x_{0}}X)^{\perp}
is precisely the fiber
\pi_{1}^{-1}(x_{0})
, and is identified with
\pi_{2}(\pi_{1}^{-1}(x_{0}))
, a linear subvariety of the dual variety
X^{\perp}
.

The classical projective duality is a reflexivity property:

Theorem 2.5

(Classical projective duality
[
23
]
)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over an algebraically closed field of char
0
. Then the dual variety of
X^{\perp}
is isomorphic to
X
, i.e., there is an isomorphism

X^{\perp\perp}\cong X.

Equivalently, projective duality says that on a Zariski dense open subspace of conormal variety

(N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}X)^{\circ}=N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}X\cap(X^{sm}\times(X^{\perp})^{sm}),

a pair
(x,\alpha)\in(N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}X)^{\circ}
satisfies

(7)

T_{x}X\subseteq\alpha^{\perp}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ T_{\alpha}(X^{\perp})\subseteq x^{\perp},

where
\alpha^{\perp}
(resp.
x^{\perp}
) is the corresponding hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
(resp.
(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
).

The proof is to show there is an isomorphism of conormal varieties
N_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\perp}X\cong N_{(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}}^{\perp}X^{\perp}
by switching the two coordinates. Over
\mathbb{C}
, the affine cone of the conormal variety is a conical Lagrangian subvariety, and the biduality is to switch the two factors. One refers to
[
36
, Section 1.3]
,
[
15
]
for details of this perspective.

Example 2.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth hypersurface of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
of degree
d\geq 2
, then its dual variety is also a hypersurface (in
(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
) with degree
[
13
, Proposition 2.9]

(8)

\deg(X^{\perp})=d(d-1)^{N-1}.

When
d\geq 3
, the dual hypersurface
X^{\perp}
is singular in codimension one, and in particular non-normal. As an example, when
X
is a smooth cubic curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, its dual curve is a sextic curve with 9 cusps.

Projective duality implies that if a pair
(x,H)\in X^{sm}\times(X^{\perp})^{sm}
is contained in the smooth locus of the product, then
H
is tangent to
X
at
x
iff
x^{\perp}
as a hyperplane in
(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
is tangent to
X^{\perp}
at
H
.

Remark 2.7

(Dual defect)
.

For "most" smooth projective varieties
X
, the dual variety is a hypersurface. Indeed, this happens iff there exists a hyperplane
H
such that
H\cap X
has an ordinary node.

In general, for a smooth projective variety
X
, the
dual defect
is defined to be

\textup{def}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=N-1-\dim(X^{\perp}),

which measures how far the dual variety is from a hypersurface. Varieties with positive defects are often called
dual defective varieties
.

For example, in dimensions one and two, dual defective varieties are linear. The classification is done up to dimension 10 (cf.
[
12
,
11
,
25
,
3
]
). We will also study defect from the point of view of generic projection in Section
6.1
.

Remark 2.8

(Char
p
)
.

In positive characteristic, Theorem
2.5
is not valid in general. Those varieties that satisfy Theorem
2.5
are called
reflexive varieties
. Many theorems on dual varieties in char
0
still hold for reflexive varieties in char
p
. One refers to
[
23
,
17
]
for details.

2.3.
Plücker formula

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a planar curve of degree
d
with only ordinary nodes and cusps and let their numbers be
\delta
and
\kappa
respectively. Then the degree
d^{\perp}
and genus
g^{\perp}
of the dual curve
C^{\perp}
satisfy Plücker relations (cf.
[
10
]

[
22
]
)

(9)

\displaystyle d^{\perp}

\displaystyle=d(d-1)-2\delta-3\kappa,

Definition 3.1

(1)

When the total space

(2)

Corollary 5.2
.

Let

be a

5.2.
Proof of purity theorem

Now, Theorem
1.4
will follow from the following and Lemma
3.3
:

Theorem 5.3

(cf. Theorem
1.4
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible projective variety. Assume that the generic projection

X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k}

is dominant (equivalently,
\dim(X)\geq k
). Then the smooth discriminant locus
\Delta
is either

(i)

an irreducible hypersurface, if
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})<k
, or

(ii)

union of hyperplanes, if
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})=k
, or

(iii)

empty, if
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})>k
.

Proof.

If
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})>k
, the
X^{\perp}
does not intersect with a general linear space of dimension
k
, hence by Theorem
1.3
, the discriminant locus is empty; If
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})=k
, the intersection
X^{\perp}
with a general
\mathbb{P}^{k}
consists of finitely many points, whose dual variety is union of hyperplanes; If
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})<k
, then Bertini theorem implies
\mathbb{P}^{k}\cap X^{\perp}
is irreducible, hence by Theorem
1.3
, its dual variety, the smooth discriminant
\Delta
is irreducible as well.

It remains to show
\Delta
has codimension one under the assumption that
\textup{codim}(X^{\perp})<k
. Since
n\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\dim(X)\geq k
, we can factor the generic projection as a sequence of generic projections to hyperplanes, and we have a sequence of generic projections:

X\xrightarrow{\pi_{n+1}}\mathbb{P}^{n}\supseteq\Delta_{n}\xrightarrow{\pi_{n}}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\supseteq\Delta_{n-1}\to\cdots\supseteq\Delta_{k+1}\xrightarrow{\pi_{k+1}}\mathbb{P}^{k},

where each
\Delta_{l}
is defined as the smooth discriminant locus of the finite branched cover
\pi_{l+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Delta_{l+1}\to\mathbb{P}^{l}
.
\Delta_{l}
is nonempty by our assumption and Lemma
5.1
. Also, notice that by taking normalization
\tilde{\Delta}_{l+1}
of
\Delta_{l+1}
. The finite branched cover

\tilde{\pi}_{l+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\tilde{\Delta}_{l+1}\to\Delta_{l+1}\to\mathbb{P}^{l}

has the same smooth discriminant:
\Delta(\tilde{\pi}_{l+1})=\Delta_{l}
. Now, since
\Delta_{l}
is an irreducible component of the branch loci of
\tilde{\pi}_{l+1}
, Zariski–Nagata purity Theorem
3.4
implies that each
\Delta_{l}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{l}
is a hypersurface. Finally, by Lemma
5.1
,
\Delta_{k}
coincides with the smooth discriminant
\Delta
, thus the claim follows.
∎

Remark 5.4

(Genericity of the projection center)
.

When generic projection
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k}
has positive dimensional fiber, the purity relies on the genericity of the projection center. For example, projecting a quadric surface

from a point

lying on it yields a birational map

contracting the two lines through

Corollary 6.1

Proposition 6.2

. The discriminant
\Delta^{\perp}
is a general plane section of the dual variety
v_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{\perp}
. By classical invariant theory
[
10
, p.16]
, this dual variety parameterizes degenerate quadrics on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Equivalently, it is isomorphic to the space of degenerate
4\times 4
symmetric matrices:

v_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{\perp}\cong\{A\in Mat_{4\times 4}|A^{T}=A,\ \det(A)=0\}.

In particular,
v_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{3})^{\perp}
is a normal quartic hypersurface. Hence
\Delta^{\perp}
is a smooth plane quartic curve, and
[
35
]
guarantees that it is general in the moduli. Therefore, by classical algebraic geometry,
\Delta^{\perp}
has 28 bitangents and 24 flex lines. The rest follows from Theorem
1.3
and Plücker formulas (
9
), (
10
).

Recall that the Mordell–Weil group of an elliptic fibration is the group of rational sections, whose rank is called the Mordell–Weil rank. We recover the computation of the Mordell–Weil rank of the elliptic 3-fold (

) below, which is first obtained in
[

, Theorem 7.2]
.

Proposition 7.5
.

[
37
]

The Mordell–Weil rank of the elliptic threefold (

) is 7.

Proof.

By construction, the elliptic threefold
\widetilde{v_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{3})}
has Picard rank 9.
The discriminant locus is irreducible, and a general fiber over it is nodal curve, so by Shioda-Tate-Wazir formula
[

\rho(\widetilde{v_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{3})})=1+\rho(\mathbb{P}^{2})+r+0,

where

as claimed.
∎

Note that there is an interest in constructing an elliptic threefold with a large Mordell–Weil rank over

(or rational surfaces), see

]
. The current record is 10
[

]

.

By Theorem

8.
Polar locus and degree of smooth discriminant

In this section, we show that the smooth critical locus of a generic projection (cf. Definition
4.6
) coincides with the notion of polar locus in the literature. As a consequence, the degree of smooth discriminant admits an intersection theory formula. We recover some classical identities on the degree of polar loci.

8.1.
Polar locus

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible projective variety. Recall that
N^{\perp}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(X)
is the conormal variety (
6
). It has dimension
N-1
and admits two projections: the first projection
\pi_{1}
to
X
is generically a projective bundle, and the second projection
\pi_{2}
has image the dual variety
X^{\perp}
. Following
[
23
, Cha. II]
, for each
0\leq i\leq N
, one defines the intersection number

(21)

r_{i}(X)=\int_{N^{\perp}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(X)}\pi_{1}^{*}H^{i}\cdot\pi_{2}^{*}(H^{\prime})^{N-1-i},

where
H=c_{1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)
and
H^{\prime}=c_{1}\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}}(1)
are hyperplane classes on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
and the dual space
(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
, respectively.

These intersection numbers have special geometric meanings. For example, we assume
X
is irreducible and denote
n=\dim(X)
as usual, then note that by the projection formula,
r_{n}=\deg(X)
is the degree of
X
;
r_{i}=0
when
i>n
; and

r_{0}=\begin{cases}\deg(X^{\perp}),\ \textup{if}\ X^{\perp}\ \textup{is a hypersurface};\\
0,\ \textup{otherwise}.\end{cases}.

In general,
r_{i}
counts the number of
N-1-i
planes that are tangent to
X
in a general linear pencil tangent to
X
.

Definition 8.1
.

[
23
, p. 186]
The
i
-th polar locus
of
X
with respect to a general codimension
i+2
linear subspace
A_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is defined to be

X(A_{i})=\overline{\{p\in X^{sm}|\dim T_{p}X\cap A=n-i-1\}},

consisting of points where the corresponding tangent plane intersects
A_{i}
having larger dimension than expected.

The intersection number (
21
) has the following equivalent characterization:

Proposition 8.2
.

[
23
, p.187]
For
0\leq i\leq n
, the intersection number
r_{i}
coincides with the the degree of
i
-th polar locus

r_{i}=\deg(X(A_{i})).

In general, one can talk about the class of
i
-th polar locus
[X(A_{i})]
in the Chow group. There is an enumerative geometry formula to compute this class
[
22
, IV,21]
.

8.2.
Relation to generic projection

We will give an equivalent characterization from the point of view of generic projection. Note that the subspace
A_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
induces a rational map
\mathbb{P}^{N}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{i+1}
, which restricts to
X
is a generic projection

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{i+1},

as long as
A_{i}
is in general position. We observe that

Lemma 8.3
.

The polar locus
X(A_{i})
coincides with the smooth critical locus of
\pi
.

Proof.

Note that the projection
\mathbb{P}^{N}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{i+1}
coincides with the dual of the inclusion
A_{i}^{\perp}\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{N})^{\vee}
. So by
Definition
4.2
,
4.6
(and switch
X
and its dual) and Proposition
4.7
, the smooth critical locus of
\pi
is
\pi_{1}(\pi_{2}^{-1}(A_{i}^{\perp}\cap X^{\perp}))
. The general point of this set consists of
x\in X
, such that there exists a hyperplane
H_{\alpha}
satisfying
T_{x}X\subseteq H_{\alpha}
and
A_{i}\subseteq H_{\alpha}
. Hence
T_{x}X
and
A_{i}
are collinear and
\dim(T_{x}X\cap A_{i})\geq n-i-1
, so
x
is contained in the
i
-th polar locus
X(A_{i})
, and vice versa.
∎

Recall that we have showed that via the generic projection
\pi
, the smooth critical locus is generically one-to-one onto the smooth discriminant (cf. Proposition
5.5
), Lemma
8.3
implies that

Corollary 8.4
.

For each
0\leq i\leq n
, the intersection number
r_{i}
(
21
) is the degree of the smooth discriminant locus
\Delta_{i+1}
of the generic projection
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{i+1}.
In other words, we have

\deg(\Delta_{i+1})=r_{i}(X).

Proof of Proposition
1.7
.
It follows from Corollary
8.4
and (
21
).
∎.

8.3.
Classical results

As a direct consequence of the above, we recover various classical results on the degree of the polar locus, which by Corollary
8.4
also implies the relations between
\deg(\Delta_{i+1})
for various
i
.

Theorem 8.5
.

[
17
]

r_{i}\neq 0
iff
\textup{def}(X)\leq i\leq n
, where
\textup{def}(X)
is the defect of
X^{\perp}
(cf. Definition
2.7
).

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
8.4
that
r_{i}\neq 0
iff
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{i+1}
has nonempty smooth discriminant, which by Theorem
5.3
, is equivalent to
\textup{def}(X)\leq i
.
∎

Theorem 8.6
.

[
29
]

If
H
is a general hyperplane, then
r_{i}(X)=r_{i-1}(X\cap H)
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the hyperplane corresponding to a general hyperplane
\mathbb{P}^{i}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{i+1}
, the by restriction the generic projection
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{i+1}
to
H
, we have
\pi|_{H}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\cap H\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{i}
, whose smooth discriminant locus
\Delta(\pi|_{H})
is precisely the intersection
\Delta(\pi)\cap\mathbb{P}^{i}
.
∎

By induction on taking hyperplane sections, one obtain

Theorem 8.7

(Segre, 1912)
.

[
23
, p.190]
There is an equality

r_{i}(X)=r_{0}(X_{i}),

where
X_{i}
is a section of
X
by a general codimension
i
linear subspace.

We will see a simple application of Theorem
8.7
in the next section.

9.
Monodromy and Braid group

Throughout this section, we work with
\Bbbk=\mathbb{C}
. The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.8
. We start with some general facts about discriminant locus of general finite projections of hypersurfaces onto projective spaces.

Let
\textup{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
be the symmetric product of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
D^{k}=\{[x_{1},\dotsb,x_{k}]\in\textup{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{1})|\exists\ 1\leq i<j\leq k,\ x_{i}=x_{j}\}
be the fat diagonal. Then the
braid group

B_{k}
of
k
points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the fundamental group

B_{k}\coloneqq\pi_{1}\left(\textup{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\setminus D^{k},\ast\right)

for some chosen base point
\ast\in\textup{Sym}^{k}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\setminus D^{k}
.

Recall from Section
3
that for
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
and
p\colon Y\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k}
any linear projection, the (set-theoretical) discriminant locus of
p
is
\Delta_{Fitt}
. We write
\Delta=\Delta_{Fitt}
if it coincides with the smooth discriminant. In this section, we use
p
as the notation for generic projection instead of
\pi
to avoid confusion with the notation
\pi_{1}
for the fundamental group.

9.1.
Braid monodromy from generic finite projection

Lemma 9.1
.

Assume that
Z
is an irreducible plane curve with
p\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
a linear projection.
Then there is a braid monodromy representation

g\colon\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt},*)\to B_{m}

where
m=\deg(Z)
. Moreover, if
Z
is smooth and the projection center is in general position, then the map
g
is surjective.

Proof.

Write
C=Z\cap p^{-1}(\ast)
. Note that the restriction
p^{\circ}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Z\setminus p^{-1}(\Delta_{Fitt})\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt}
is étale, so the fiber over

t
is an unordered configuration of

m
distinct points in the line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. This induces a continuous map
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt}\to\textup{Sym}^{m}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\setminus D^{m}
, hence a group homomorphism

\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt},\ast\right)\to\pi_{1}\left(\textup{Sym}^{m}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\setminus D^{m},\ast\right)=B_{m}.

When
Z
is smooth and the projection is general, the projection
Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is simply branched, and
\Delta_{Fitt}=\Delta
consists of branch points. The monodromy action of each generating loop of
\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta,\ast\right)
is given by Dehn twist along a pair of points. As the covering space
p^{\circ}
is connected, the monodromy action on
C
is transitive, and there exists an ordering
C=\{z_{0},\dotsb,z_{m-1}\}
and generating loops
\gamma_{0},\dotsb,\gamma_{m-1}
of
\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta,\ast\right)
such that
\gamma_{i}
is the Dehn twist along the pair
(z_{i\mod(m-1)},z_{i+1\mod(m-1)})
. It is classically known that the action of the subgroup
\langle\gamma_{0},\dotsb,\gamma_{m-1}\rangle
on
p^{-1}(\ast)\setminus Z
is exactly the braid relation
[
2
]
, and therefore the image of
\langle\gamma_{0},\dotsb,\gamma_{m-1}\rangle
under the map
\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Delta,\ast\right)\to B_{m}
coincides with the braid group, so the map is surjective.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

Note that there is a surjection
B_{m}\to\Mod(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus C)
to the mapping class group of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus C
(see
[
14
, Section 9.1]
). The composition of
g
with this map is the induced monodromy action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus C
.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N+1}
be an irreducible hypersurface and let
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the finite branched cover induced from a linear projection
\mathbb{P}^{N+1}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Fix a regular value
\ast\in\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt}
of
p
. Then

(1)

There is a braid monodromy representation

\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt},\ast\right)\to B_{m}

where
m=\deg(Y)
.

(2)

If
Y
is normal, then
\varphi
is surjective, and
\Delta_{Fitt}=\Delta
is an irreducible hypersurface of degree
m(m-1)
.

Proof.

For part (1), note that away from the discriminant locus
\Delta_{Fitt}
the projection

p^{-1}(\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt})\to\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt}

is étale. For the same reason as the proof of Lemma
9.1
, we obtain a braid monodromy representation
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt},\ast\right)\to B_{m}
.

For part (2), Suppose
Y
is normal, Then by Lemma
3.6
implies that
\Delta_{Fitt}=\Delta
coincides with the smooth discriminant.
For a general line
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
, the preimage with
p^{-1}(\mathbb{L})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=Z
is a general plane section of
Y
. Since
Y
is normal and smooth in codimension one,
Z
is a smooth plane curve of degree
m
. The projection
\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{L}
restricts to a finite branched cover

Z=Y\cap\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{L}

with branching locus
\Delta\cap\mathbb{L}
. As
Z
is smooth and the choice of
\mathbb{L}
is general, this is simply branched. Then, applying Lemma
9.1
, we get a surjective group homomorphism
\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{L}\setminus\Delta,\ast\right)\twoheadrightarrow B_{m}
. Finally, note that there is a commutative diagram of groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{L}\setminus\Delta,\ast\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\Delta,\ast\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{m},}

and hence surjectivity of
\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{L}\setminus\Delta,\ast\right)\twoheadrightarrow B_{m}
implies the surjectivity of the second map.

For the last part, we note
\deg(\Delta)
is the number of the (simple) branch points of
Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Specifically, it follows from Theorem
8.7
that

\deg(\Delta)=\deg(Z^{\vee})=m(m-1).

∎

Remark 9.4
.

There is a natural map from braid group
B_{m}\to\mathfrak{S}_{m}
to the symmetric group, and the composition map

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus\Delta_{Fitt},\ast\right)\to B_{m}\to\mathfrak{S}_{m}

is the monodromy representation permutating the
m
points.

In the set-up of In Proposition
9.3
(1) and without additional assumption, one can still show

is surjective. This is an application of the Uniform Principle Theorem of an irreducible curve
[
1
, p.111]
.

9.2.
Varieties with normal duals

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N+1}
be a smooth projective variety. Suppose that its dual variety

is a normal hypersurface of degree
