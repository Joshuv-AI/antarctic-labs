Diagnostic Disagreement as an Information-Projection Divergence: An Information-Theoretic Reading of the Quiet-Sun Temperature Ratio
V. Edmonds

Abstract
The quiet-Sun coronal electron-temperature ratio R ≡ T EUV / T B ≈ 2.4 R\equiv T_{\mathrm{EUV}}/T_{B}\approx 2.4 , stable across an eight-year solar cycle, is read here as a measurement of relative entropy between two diagnostic projections of the coronal electron distribution onto the one-parameter Maxwellian family. The EUV ionization temperature is a moment-matching projection against a Bethe-type ionization kernel; the radio brightness temperature is the Rayleigh–Jeans source function of thermal bremsstrahlung. For a kappa distribution in the mean-energy convention, Fleishman & Kuznetsov (2014) give the radio-side projection in closed form as T B = T core T_{B}=T_{\mathrm{core}} ; the EUV side returns T eff T_{\mathrm{eff}} up to a shape-dependent correction within the Dudík et al. (2014) intensity-ratio envelope. At κ = 2.5 \kappa=2.5 the Kullback–Leibler divergences between the true distribution and its two Maxwellian projections evaluate to 0.32 0.32 and 1.20 1.20 nats, and their difference satisfies Δ ​ D KL = ( 3 / 2 ) ​ [ R 0 − ln ⁡ R 0 − 1 ] = ( 3 / 2 ) ​ d IS ​ ( T eff , T core ) \Delta D_{\mathrm{KL}}=(3/2)[R_{0}-\ln R_{0}-1]=(3/2)\,d_{\mathrm{IS}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}},T_{\mathrm{core}}) , where R 0 ≡ κ / ( κ − 3 / 2 ) R_{0}\equiv\kappa/(\kappa-3/2) is the ideal closed-form ratio and d IS d_{\mathrm{IS}} is the Itakura–Saito distance. The identity is offered as an analytical reference for observational systems in which two diagnostics project different moments of a common non-equilibrium distribution; the eight-year stability of R R expresses a stability of that projection structure.



I
Introduction

This paper re-reads a well-established coronal diagnostic observation in
the language of classical information theory. The closed-form identity
used in §
II
is due to Fleishman & Kuznetsov (2014);
the kappa-distribution interpretation of the observation is established
in Mercier & Chambe (2015) and Edmonds (2026a); the
information-theoretic framework is standard (Jaynes 1957a,b;
Csiszár 1975; Balian 1999; Cover & Thomas 2006;
Livadiotis & McComas 2013). The contribution is a specific
information-geometric reading of the coronal diagnostic pair as a
relative-entropy observable, with the Kullback–Leibler divergences
evaluated at
\kappa=2.5
as the concrete numerical output and a
closed-form Itakura–Saito identity linking those divergences to the
observed diagnostic ratio as the formal deliverable.

Two standard electron-temperature diagnostics disagree in the quiet
Sun. The effective temperature
T_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx 1.5\,\mathrm{MK}

inferred from EUV ionization equilibrium exceeds the thermal brightness
temperature
T_{B}\approx 0.62\,\mathrm{MK}
measured at 150–450 MHz by
a factor
R\equiv T_{\mathrm{eff}}/T_{B}\approx 2.4
, stable across the
2004–2011 solar cycle to within yearly variations smaller than 10%
(Mercier & Chambe 2015; Vocks et al. 2018; Zhang et al. 2022). The
established interpretation of this ratio invokes a kappa-distributed
electron population with
\kappa\approx 2.5
in the
Dzifčáková–Dudík (D&D) convention
(Dzifčáková & Dudík 2013; Edmonds 2026a), for which

\langle E\rangle=(3/2)\,k\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}
by construction while the
core of the distribution behaves as a Maxwellian at

T_{\mathrm{core}}=(\kappa-3/2)/\kappa\cdot T_{\mathrm{eff}}
(Oka et
al. 2013).

Each diagnostic operates as a projection of the full electron
distribution
f(\mathbf{v})
onto the one-parameter Maxwellian family.
The EUV diagnostic implements a moment-matching projection against a
Bethe-type ionization kernel, returning the Maxwellian temperature
whose ionization rate matches the observed intensity. The radio
brightness-temperature diagnostic implements a source-function
projection given by the ratio
j_{\nu}/\alpha_{\nu}
of thermal
bremsstrahlung in the Rayleigh–Jeans kinetic limit. Fleishman &
Kuznetsov (2014, Eq. 48) establish in closed form that this ratio
evaluates to
T_{\mathrm{core}}
for a kappa distribution in the D&D
convention. The diagnostic ratio
R
is accordingly a closed-form
function of
\kappa
alone, up to a bounded shape-dependent correction
on the EUV side.

The concrete numerical deliverables of the present paper are the
relative-entropy quantities
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_{\kappa}\|f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}}))

and
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_{\kappa}\|f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{core}}))
evaluated
at
\kappa=2.5
. Both admit closed-form expressions in the log-gamma
and digamma functions of the kappa family. Their difference satisfies
a clean identity
\Delta D_{\mathrm{KL}}=(3/2)[R-\ln R-1]
in which
the observed diagnostic ratio enters as a single term. The observed

R\approx 2.4
is a consequence, consistent with the closed-form
prediction across the EUV-side shape envelope and the Mercier & Chambe
yearly range.

The components of the information-theoretic framework used here are
standard and attributed individually in §
IV.5
; the
central identity in §
II.4
is due to Fleishman &
Kuznetsov (2014). Extensions to other observational systems are
flagged in §
IV.4
but not developed here.

II
Formalism

Each coronal electron-temperature diagnostic returns a scalar

T_{D}
by projecting the full velocity distribution
f(\mathbf{v})

onto the one-parameter family of Maxwellians

f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(\mathbf{v};T)
, replacing
f
by the Maxwellian
stand-in whose kernel-matched parameter
T
reproduces a specified
property of the diagnostic kernel. Two standard diagnostic
procedures correspond to two structurally inequivalent projection
rules, one for the EUV side and one for the radio side. The
ratio of the two returned temperatures is an algebraic function of

f(\mathbf{v})
, and, for a D&D kappa distribution, admits a
closed-form expression (§
II.4
).

II.1
The diagnostic as a projection onto the Maxwellian family

Let
K_{D}(\mathbf{v})
denote a diagnostic kernel. Two projection
rules appear in standard coronal practice.

Moment-matching.

For a measured observable of the form

D[f]=\int K_{D}(\mathbf{v})\,f(\mathbf{v})\,d^{3}v
, the

moment-matched
temperature
T_{D}^{(\mathrm{MM})}
is the parameter
of the Maxwellian whose kernel-integrated observable reproduces

D[f]
:

\int K_{D}(\mathbf{v})\,f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(\mathbf{v};T_{D}^{(\mathrm{MM})})\,d^{3}v\;=\;\int K_{D}(\mathbf{v})\,f(\mathbf{v})\,d^{3}v.

(1)

This is the maximum-likelihood projection onto the one-parameter
Maxwellian family under
K_{D}
, and it is the operation a diagnostic
performs whenever an observed rate or count is inverted to a
temperature under the assumption that the underlying distribution is
thermal. When the kernel coincides with the sufficient statistic of
the family — as it does for the Maxwellian energy moment with

K_{D}\propto E
— the moment-matched member is the

information projection
(I-projection) of
f
onto the family
in the sense of Csiszár (1975), the unique member that minimizes

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\,\|\,\cdot)
. For EUV-driven ionization
diagnostics,
K_{D}
is proportional to the ionization rate coefficient
and the projection returns an ionization-weighted temperature
(§
II.2
).

Source-function projection.

When the diagnostic is a
scattering process whose observable is the ratio of an emissivity

j_{\nu}[f]
and an absorption coefficient
\alpha_{\nu}[f]
, the
returned
source-function
temperature is

T_{D}^{(\mathrm{SF})}\;=\;\frac{j_{\nu}[f]}{\alpha_{\nu}[f]}\cdot\frac{c^{2}}{2\,k\,\nu^{2}},

(2)

i.e., the temperature at which a Maxwellian plasma would produce the
observed ratio in the Rayleigh–Jeans limit. For a Maxwellian
f
the
ratio collapses to the plasma temperature by Kirchhoff’s law; for a
non-Maxwellian
f
, the numerator and denominator are separate
moments of
f
and their ratio is not, in general, the moment-matched
temperature of either. Thermal free-free bremsstrahlung in the solar
corona is the relevant case (§
II.3
).

Both rules are projections: each collapses
f
onto the

T
-parameterized family of Maxwellians under a different kernel
structure, and their outputs need not agree when
f
departs from
Maxwellian.

II.2
The EUV side: moment-matching under ionization

The dominant EUV coronal line used in the quiet-Sun temperature
comparison (Fe
xii
195 Å; Mercier & Chambe 2015) is
populated through collisional ionization of Fe
xi
. The
ionization rate coefficient in the Bethe form is

\sigma_{\mathrm{ion}}(v)\propto(1/E)\,\ln(E/\chi)
above the
ionization threshold
\chi
, so the EUV kernel is

K_{\mathrm{EUV}}(\mathbf{v})\;=\;\sigma_{\mathrm{ion}}(v)\,v\,\Theta\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}mv^{2}-\chi\bigr),

(3)

with
\Theta
the Heaviside step function. Applying
(
1
) to
K_{\mathrm{EUV}}
and a Maxwellian
f
at
temperature
T
yields
T_{\mathrm{EUV}}^{(\mathrm{MM})}=T
trivially; a
convergence principle governing the broader class of ionization-gated
diagnostics acting on kappa distributions is given in Edmonds (2026b).
For a D&D kappa distribution
f_{\kappa}
(normalised by
\langle E\rangle=\tfrac{3}{2}kT_{\mathrm{eff}}
), the moment-matching
projection returns

T_{\mathrm{EUV}}^{(\mathrm{MM})}\;=\;T_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\bigl[\,1+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}(\kappa,\chi/kT_{\mathrm{eff}})\,\bigr],

(4)

where the correction
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}
measures the departure
of the Bethe-weighted ionization moment from the energy moment

\langle E\rangle
used in the D&D convention. The ionization rate
coefficients themselves differ between kappa and Maxwellian at the
same
T_{\mathrm{eff}}
: Dzifčáková & Dudík (2013)
document differences of a factor of several near peak temperature for
strong coronal lines, with larger deviations below the peak; these
differences are largely absorbed into the emission-measure
normalization rather than the inferred temperature. The
diagnostic
observable
, however, is the line intensity ratio, and
Dudík, Del Zanna, Mason & Dzifčáková (2014,
A&A

570
, A124) compute
\kappa
-averaged excitation collision
strengths for Fe
ix
–
xiii
and establish that strong
coronal line intensity ratios vary by less than approximately 20%
across
\kappa=2
–
25
. It is this intensity insensitivity that
bounds the
T_{\mathrm{EUV}}
inferred from observed ratios via a
Maxwellian-family projection;
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}
is therefore
constrained by this observational envelope, specifically for strong
Fe
ix
–
xiii
lines. It is
not
a bound on the
underlying rate coefficients, which is a distinct quantity. For Fe
xi
\,\to\,
Fe
xii
(
\chi=290\,\mathrm{eV}
)
and
\kappa=2.5
at
T_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.5\,\mathrm{MK}
,

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}
is shape-dependent at the
\sim 5
–
20\%

level across physically reasonable cross-section models. The pure
Bethe form gives
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}\approx 0.013
; Bethe
augmented with an excitation–autoionization contribution above the
710 eV threshold measured by Hahn et al. (2011) gives

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}\approx 0.05
–
0.12
depending on the EA
strength; Lotz-style near-threshold damping yields

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}
up to
\sim 0.19
; and a
softer
(u-1)/u^{2}\sqrt{E}
threshold shape gives

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}\approx-0.05
. All variants lie inside
the Dudík et al. (2014) intensity envelope by construction. The
rigorous atomic-data evaluation performed in §
III.2

using the matched Dz23
\kappa
-resolved and Maxwellian
ion-equilibrium tables (Dzifčáková et al. 2023) on the
CHIANTI v10.1 basis returns a single definite

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}
inside this bracket for the
Fe
xii
/Fe
xiii
line-ratio diagnostic, with a
second diagnostic (Fe
xi
/Fe
xii
) lying just
outside the lower edge while remaining inside the broader
Dudík et al. (2014) envelope. The shape-model sensitivity
is the sole approximation that survives into the final result.

II.3
The radio side: source function for thermal bremsstrahlung

The radio brightness temperature
T_{B}
in the Rayleigh–Jeans regime
is the source-function projection (
2
) with

j_{\nu}
and
\alpha_{\nu}
the emissivity and absorption coefficient of
thermal free-free bremsstrahlung (Rybicki & Lightman 1979). In the
quiet-Sun corona, Coulomb pitch-angle scattering proceeds substantially
faster than energy diffusion, so isotropization is well-established on
timescales short compared to the relaxation of the energy distribution;
the assumption
f(\mathbf{v})=f(|\mathbf{v}|)
is therefore
appropriate at the formation layers considered here. For an isotropic
electron distribution
f(\mathbf{v})
and a slowly varying free-free
Gaunt factor
g_{\mathrm{ff}}
in the kinetic limit

h\nu\ll kT_{\mathrm{eff}}
:

\displaystyle j_{\nu}[f]

\displaystyle\;\propto\;\int\!f(\mathbf{v})\,\frac{1}{v}\,g_{\mathrm{ff}}(v,\nu)\,d^{3}v\;\approx\;\bar{g}_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\bigl\langle 1/v\bigr\rangle_{f},

(5)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\nu}[f]

\displaystyle\;\propto\;-\int\!\biggl(\frac{\partial f_{v}}{\partial E}\biggr)\,\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}(v,\nu)\,v\,d^{3}v,

(6)

where the second line follows from microscopic reversibility in the
kinetic limit: the net absorption rate at frequency
\nu
is the
imbalance between absorption at velocity
\mathbf{v}
and stimulated
emission from the conjugate final state

\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
with
\tfrac{1}{2}mv^{\prime 2}=\tfrac{1}{2}mv^{2}+h\nu
,
and this imbalance reduces, at leading order in
h\nu
, to

-(h\nu)\,\partial f_{v}/\partial E
. Here
f_{v}
denotes the
velocity-space distribution expressed as a function of energy,

f_{v}(E)\equiv f(|\mathbf{v}|)|_{E=mv^{2}/2}
.

For free-free,
\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}(v,\nu)\cdot v\propto 1/v
up
to the same slowly-varying Gaunt factor. Combined with the isotropic
phase-space measure
d^{3}v=4\pi v^{2}\,dv
and the change of variable

E=\tfrac{1}{2}mv^{2}
(so that
v\,dv\propto dE
), the kernel
weight becomes
\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}(v,\nu)\cdot v\cdot 4\pi v^{2}\,dv\propto v\,dv\propto dE
, leaving a measure in
dE
alone with no
net power of
v
. The integral (
6
) collapses to
a boundary term:

\alpha_{\nu}[f]\;\propto\;-\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\partial f_{v}}{\partial E}\,dE\;=\;f_{v}(0)-f_{v}(\infty)\;=\;f_{v}(0),

(7)

up to the
\bar{g}_{\mathrm{ff}}
factor and the standard Coulomb-log
regularization of the low-
v
divergence that also appears in the
Maxwellian calculation. Equation (
7
) is the
leading-order statement that the free-free absorption coefficient at
low frequency is controlled by the zero-velocity density of the
distribution, with all kappa- or Maxwellian-specific structure
entering through
f_{v}(0)
alone.

For a Maxwellian at temperature
T
,

f_{v}^{(\mathrm{Mxw})}(0)=(2/\sqrt{\pi})\,(kT)^{-3/2}
and

\langle 1/v\rangle_{\mathrm{Mxw}}\propto(kT)^{-1/2}
, so
(
2
) with (
5
) and (
7
)
reduces to
T_{B}=T
, recovering Kirchhoff’s law as it must.

II.4
The radio source function on a kappa distribution: a known identity

For a kappa distribution
f_{\kappa}
in the mean-energy convention
(
\langle E\rangle_{f_{\kappa}}=\tfrac{3}{2}kT_{\mathrm{eff}}
for
all
\kappa>3/2
; equivalent to Dzifčáková &
Dudík 2013 and used throughout Fleishman & Kuznetsov 2014), the
Rayleigh–Jeans-limit source function
T_{B}
of thermal bremsstrahlung
in the kinetic limit satisfies

T_{B}\;=\;\frac{\kappa-3/2}{\kappa}\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}\;\equiv\;T_{\mathrm{core}},

(8)

where
T_{\mathrm{core}}
is the core temperature scalar introduced by
Oka et al. (2013) and quoted in Dzifčáková & Dudík
(2013, §2). Equation (
8
) is derived explicitly
in Fleishman & Kuznetsov (2014, Eq. 48) from the analytical free-free
expressions of their Eqs. (46)–(47), with the approximation that
slowly-varying Coulomb-log correction factors common to
j_{\nu}
and

\alpha_{\nu}
cancel in the ratio. The identity is reproduced below in
the source-function projection framework of §
II.1
to
make the information-projection reading of the ratio transparent, and
is adopted as input to the corollary that follows.

Re-derivation in the source-function
projection framework.
The following reproduces (
8
)
via the
(\langle 1/v\rangle,f_{v}(0))
moment pair defined by the
bremsstrahlung kinetic-limit structure (
5
),
(
7
), so that the identification of
T_{B}
as a
source-function projection of
f_{\kappa}
onto the Maxwellian family
(in the sense of §
II.1
) is explicit.

Let
T_{0}\equiv(\kappa-3/2)\,kT_{\mathrm{eff}}
denote the natural
energy scale of the kappa form in this convention. The energy-space
distribution satisfies

f_{E}^{(\kappa)}(E)\;=\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{\Gamma(\kappa+1)}{\Gamma(\kappa-\tfrac{1}{2})}\,T_{0}^{-3/2}\,\sqrt{E}\,\Bigl(1+\tfrac{E}{T_{0}}\Bigr)^{\!-(\kappa+1)},

(9)

with velocity-space analogue
f_{v}^{(\kappa)}(E)=f_{E}^{(\kappa)}(E)/\sqrt{E}
.
Direct evaluation at
E\to 0
gives

f_{v}^{(\kappa)}(0)\;=\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{\Gamma(\kappa+1)}{\Gamma(\kappa-\tfrac{1}{2})}\,T_{0}^{-3/2}.

(10)

The moment
\langle 1/v\rangle_{f_{\kappa}}=\langle E^{-1/2}\rangle_{f_{\kappa}}
is

\displaystyle\bigl\langle E^{-1/2}\bigr\rangle_{f_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{\Gamma(\kappa+1)}{\Gamma(\kappa-\tfrac{1}{2})}\,T_{0}^{-3/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\Bigl(1+\tfrac{E}{T_{0}}\Bigr)^{\!-(\kappa+1)}dE

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{\Gamma(\kappa+1)}{\kappa\,\Gamma(\kappa-\tfrac{1}{2})}\,T_{0}^{-1/2}.

(11)

Combining (
10
) and (
11
)
via (
2
)–(
7
),

T_{B}\;=\;\frac{\langle 1/v\rangle_{f_{\kappa}}}{f_{v}^{(\kappa)}(0)}\;=\;\frac{T_{0}}{\kappa}\;=\;\frac{\kappa-3/2}{\kappa}\,T_{\mathrm{eff}}\;=\;T_{\mathrm{core}},

(12)

recovering Fleishman & Kuznetsov (2014, Eq. 48).

Remark.
Identity (
8
) is
algebraic in the same sense as Fleishman & Kuznetsov (2014,
Eq. 48): it does not depend on frequency, ion charge, number
density, or Coulomb logarithm in the slowly-varying Gaunt
approximation. The Maxwellian limit is recovered automatically,
since
T_{\mathrm{core}}/T_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\kappa-3/2)/\kappa\to 1

as
\kappa\to\infty
and the kappa distribution itself tends to a
Maxwellian at
T_{\mathrm{eff}}
. The identity fails only where the
kinetic-limit form of
\alpha_{\nu}
in (
7
) is not
applicable: e.g., near the local plasma frequency, where
h\nu
is
no longer small compared to the thermal-bulk kinetic energy, or in
regimes where the free-free Gaunt factor cannot be treated as slowly
varying over the relevant electron-velocity range.

Attribution.
The identity
(
8
) is due to Fleishman & Kuznetsov (2014,
Eq. 48), who derive

S_{\!f,\mathrm{ff}}^{\kappa,\sigma}\approx(\kappa-3/2)/\kappa\cdot(n_{\sigma}^{2}f^{2}/c^{2})\,k_{B}T

for thermal bremsstrahlung from a kappa-distributed plasma in the
kinetic limit, crediting Chiuderi & Chiuderi Drago (2004) for the
analogous calculation at integer
\kappa
. The scalar

T_{\mathrm{core}}=(\kappa-3/2)/\kappa\cdot T_{\mathrm{eff}}
is due
to Oka et al. (2013) and is quoted in Dzifčáková &
Dudík (2013, §2). Dudík et al. (2011,
A&A

529
, A103) derive analytical
j_{\nu}
for

\kappa
-plasmas in the same convention but do not compute

\alpha_{\nu}
, and the
\kappa
-averaged free-free Gaunt factor
tabulated by de Avillez & Breitschwerdt (2015) is the numerical
Gaunt-factor ingredient. The role of the present paper is to read (
8
) in
the source-function projection framework of §
II.1

and to pair it with the moment-matching projection of the EUV
diagnostic (§
II.2
) so that the diagnostic ratio
(Corollary
1
) becomes a single closed-form function of

\kappa
with a direct relative-entropy interpretation
(§
IV
).

Corollary 1
.

For a kappa plasma probed by an ionization-based EUV diagnostic and
by thermal free-free bremsstrahlung in the radio Rayleigh–Jeans

R\;\equiv\;\frac{T_{\mathrm{EUV}}^{(\mathrm{MM})}}{T_{B}}\;=\;\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-3/2}\,\bigl[\,1+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}(\kappa,\chi/kT_{\mathrm{eff}})\,\bigr],

(13)

with the sole approximation being the EUV-side kernel-overlap
correction
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}
, bounded for strong
Fe
ix
–
xiii
coronal lines at
\kappa=2
–
25
by
the Dudík et al. (2014,
A&A

570
, A124)
intensity-ratio insensitivity.

Proof.

Combine (
8
) with (
4
) and take the
ratio.
∎

III
Application to the corona

III.1
Input parameters

The parameters entering Corollary
1
are fixed to values
established in prior work. The effective temperature
T_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.5\,\mathrm{MK}
is the scale-height temperature derived from the
Mercier & Chambe (2015) density model and the cross-regime
consistency check of Edmonds (2026a). The shape parameter

\kappa=2.5
is the value uniquely pinned in Edmonds (2026a) by the
joint cross-fit of spectroscopic EUV line ratios, radio brightness
temperatures at 150–450 MHz, and the hydrostatic scale-height
density profile: each diagnostic alone leaves
\kappa
poorly
constrained, but the three-way intersection collapses the
admissible region onto
\kappa\approx 2.5
at the quiet-Sun
base. The present paper takes this value as input and does not
re-derive it. The ionization threshold

\chi_{\mathrm{Fe\,XI\to Fe\,XII}}=290\,\mathrm{eV}
is the NIST
recommended value. At the observing frequencies of Mercier
& Chambe (150–445 MHz) and Zhang et al. (2022, 20–80 MHz), the
Rayleigh–Jeans and kinetic-limit conditions
h\nu\ll kT_{\mathrm{core}}

and
h\nu\ll\langle E\rangle
are both satisfied with

h\nu/kT_{\mathrm{core}}\lesssim 10^{-5}
.

III.3
Absence of scattering signature

The Mercier & Chambe (2015) measurement is a disk-averaged brightness
temperature at 150–445 MHz in the optically thick regime. Turbulent
scattering of coronal radio emission at these frequencies has been
modelled extensively (Bastian 1994; Thejappa & MacDowall 2008; Kontar et al. 2019;
Sharma & Oberoi 2020; Kontar et al. 2023) and is known to
redistribute brightness across the solar disk: coronal holes gain flux
from neighboring regions by factors of order two to three while active
regions lose up to half their intrinsic flux (Sharma & Oberoi 2020).
For spatially-resolved or off-limb measurements at frequencies well
below the local plasma frequency this redistribution manifests as a
net suppression of the apparent brightness temperature. For
disk-integrated measurements in the optically thick regime, however,
flux conservation bounds the net correction: scattering redistributes
photons across the disk without systematically suppressing the
disk-averaged temperature, with the residual correction estimated at

\lesssim 30\%
(Kontar et al. 2019; Kontar et al. 2023).

The theorem’s prediction
T_{B}=T_{\mathrm{core}}=0.60\,\mathrm{MK}
at

\kappa=2.5
sits at the low end of the Mercier & Chambe yearly
range of
0.60
–
0.65\,\mathrm{MK}
. The small residual goes in the
opposite sense to what a net scattering suppression would produce: the
observed disk-averaged temperature is slightly higher than, not lower
than, the intrinsic source function. The prediction lands within the
observational range without invoking a scattering correction; invoking
one would widen the residual rather than close it. This is consistent
with the independent finding of Edmonds (2026a) that turbulent
scattering alone is quantitatively insufficient to account for the
diagnostic ratio, and the residual is accommodated within the
Mercier & Chambe yearly envelope directly. Refraction at 150–445 MHz
samples a layer slightly above the
\tau=1
surface of the underlying
density profile, but the vertical temperature gradient across the
relevant scale height is shallow compared to the ratio
R\approx 2.4

and introduces no systematic correction of comparable size in the
disk-averaged measurement.

IV
Discussion

IV.1
The diagnostic ratio as an information-theoretic observable

The two diagnostic projections defined in §
II.1

collapse
f
onto the one-parameter Maxwellian family by structurally
distinct rules: the EUV side is the I-projection in the sense of
§
II.1
(Csiszár 1975), matching
\langle E\rangle
and minimizing
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f\,\|\,\cdot)
over the
family; the radio side is the algebraic source-function projection

j_{\nu}/\alpha_{\nu}
of §
II.4
, which lands at

T_{\mathrm{core}}
and is not in general the
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
-minimizing
member. Their reported temperatures are not direct measurements of a
single physical temperature; they are scalar indices of the family
member that matches a specified moment of
f
under each kernel.
Accordingly,
R=T_{\mathrm{EUV}}/T_{B}
is not a ratio of two
temperatures of the plasma. It is a single scalar functional of the
electron distribution
f
and the pair of diagnostic kernels, with the
structure of a relative-entropy observable (the specific functional
is derived in §
IV.2
; cf. Eq. (
18
)).

The eight-year stability of the ratio (Mercier & Chambe 2015;
Edmonds 2026a) is under this reading a stability of the projection
structure of
f
under the two diagnostics. The shape parameter

\kappa
is stable on solar-cycle timescales, and the closed-form
dependence of Corollary
1
transmits that stability to the
ratio directly. The ratio is not sampling any fluctuating kinetic
quantity; it is sampling a shape property of the underlying
distribution via a pair of fixed projections.

IV.2
Relative-entropy content

For a kappa distribution
f_{\kappa}
in the D&D convention, the
Kullback–Leibler divergence against a Maxwellian at temperature
T

admits a closed form using two moments of the distribution:

\langle E\rangle_{f_{\kappa}}=(3/2)kT_{\mathrm{eff}}
(by the
convention) and
\langle\ln(1+E/T_{0})\rangle_{f_{\kappa}}=\psi(\kappa+1)-\psi(\kappa-1/2)
, the latter obtained by
differentiating the beta integral

B(3/2,\,\kappa-1/2)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{1/2}(1+x)^{-(\kappa+1)}dx

with respect to the exponent on
(1+x)
. This moment enters the full

D_{\mathrm{KL}}
expression multiplied by the
-(\kappa+1)

coefficient from
\ln f_{\kappa}
:

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(f_{\kappa}\,\|\,f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\ln\!\frac{\Gamma(\kappa+1)}{\Gamma(\kappa-1/2)}+\frac{3}{2}\ln\frac{T}{T_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad-(\kappa+1)\,[\psi(\kappa+1)-\psi(\kappa-1/2)]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{T_{\mathrm{eff}}}{T},

(15)

with
T_{0}=(\kappa-3/2)kT_{\mathrm{eff}}
and
\psi
the digamma
function. Numerical evaluation at the coronal parameters
(
\kappa=2.5
,
T_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.5\,\mathrm{MK}
) gives

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(f_{\kappa}\,\|\,f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}})\bigr)

\displaystyle=0.320\,\mathrm{nats}=0.461\,\mathrm{bits},

(16)

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(f_{\kappa}\,\|\,f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{core}})\bigr)

\displaystyle=1.195\,\mathrm{nats}=1.724\,\mathrm{bits}.

(17)

These are the information-theoretic observables of the quiet-Sun
electron distribution under the two diagnostic projections.

Their difference follows from (
15
) directly.
Substituting
T=T_{\mathrm{core}}
and
T=T_{\mathrm{eff}}
and
subtracting, the
\Gamma
-ratio and digamma terms cancel; the

(3/2)\ln(T/T_{0})
pieces combine to give

(3/2)\ln(T_{\mathrm{core}}/T_{\mathrm{eff}})=-(3/2)\ln R
; and the

(3/2)T_{\mathrm{eff}}/T
pieces give
(3/2)[T_{\mathrm{eff}}/T_{\mathrm{core}}-1]=(3/2)[R-1]
. The closed-form residue between the
radio-side and EUV-side projections is therefore

\Delta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\;\equiv\;D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\cdot\|f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{core}}))-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\cdot\|f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}}))\;=\;\frac{3}{2}\,\bigl[\,R_{0}-\ln R_{0}-1\,\bigr],

(18)

where
R_{0}\equiv\kappa/(\kappa-3/2)=T_{\mathrm{eff}}/T_{\mathrm{core}}

is the ideal closed-form diagnostic ratio — the value Corollary
1

returns in the limit
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}}\to 0
, related to the
observable
R
of (
13
) by
R=R_{0}\,(1+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EUV}})
.
The identity (
18
) is algebraic and holds for any

\kappa>3/2
independent of the temperature parameter; at

\kappa=2.5
,
R_{0}=2.500
and the gap evaluates to

(3/2)[2.500-0.916-1]=0.876\,\mathrm{nats}
, matching the
direct subtraction of the two numerical divergences.

The cancellation of the
\kappa
-dependent
\Gamma
-ratio and digamma
terms in the subtraction is not algebraic coincidence; it is the
generalized Pythagorean theorem for Bregman divergences on
exponential families (Csiszár 1975; Cover & Thomas 2006). Because

f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}})
is the I-projection of
f_{\kappa}

onto the Maxwellian family — the member that matches the
sufficient-statistic moment
\langle E\rangle
and therefore minimizes

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_{\kappa}\,\|\,\cdot)
over the family
(§
II.1
) — the theorem gives, for any other family
member
f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T)
,

D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(f_{\kappa}\,\|\,f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T)\bigr)\;=\;D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(f_{\kappa}\,\|\,f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}})\bigr)\;+\;D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}})\,\|\,f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T)\bigr).

(19)

Setting
T=T_{\mathrm{core}}
and rearranging,

\Delta D_{\mathrm{KL}}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}})\,\|\,f_{\mathrm{Mxw}}(T_{\mathrm{core}}))
: the radio–EUV gap is the
relative entropy
between the two Maxwellian projections themselves
,
independent of the upstream non-equilibrium distribution. This explains
both why the
\kappa
-dependent terms drop out of the difference and
why the residue depends only on the diagnostic ratio
R_{0}
.

The functional
d_{\mathrm{IS}}(x,y)\equiv x/y-\ln(x/y)-1
is the

Itakura–Saito distance
, the canonical Bregman divergence
generated by
\phi(z)=-\ln z
and the standard relative-entropy-type
distortion measure for scale parameters within the exponential family
in signal processing and information geometry (Banerjee et al. 2005).
With
x=T_{\mathrm{eff}}
and
y=T_{\mathrm{core}}
, identity
(
18
) reads

\Delta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\;=\;\tfrac{3}{2}\,d_{\mathrm{IS}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}},T_{\mathrm{core}}),

(20)

identifying the radio–EUV gap as exactly
3/2
times the
Itakura–Saito distance between the two diagnostic temperatures.
The appearance of
d_{\mathrm{IS}}
here is structural rather than
incidental: the Itakura–Saito distance is the canonical Bregman
divergence associated with maximum-likelihood estimation of scale
parameters in the exponential family, and the kinetic temperature
parameterizes precisely the variance of the Maxwellian velocity
distribution. The
3/2
prefactor likewise has a direct physical
origin: it tracks the three translational degrees of freedom of the
3D velocity space, the same factor that sets the monatomic ideal-gas
heat capacity. The leading first-order piece
R-1
is the
mean-energy mismatch between the projections; the logarithmic piece

\ln R
is the amplitude of the relative-entropy shift; together they
give the
d_{\mathrm{IS}}
form. The quiet-Sun observation
R\approx 2.4
is
therefore a direct measurement, to within the shape envelope of
§
III.2
and the observational uncertainty of
§
III.3
, of an information-theoretic functional of the
coronal electron distribution that depends only on the shape parameter

\kappa
.

In information-theoretic units the gap

0.876\,\mathrm{nats}\approx 1.26\,\mathrm{bits}
is the relative-entropy
content the coronal distribution carries beyond what either Maxwellian
projection retains: a single diagnostic operating on a fixed projection
manifold cannot recover it, and only the comparison between two
non-aligned projections renders it observable.

IV.3
Consistency with spectroscopic Maxwellian-like appearance

Lörinčík et al. (2020) report that quiet-Sun Hinode/EIS
spectra are consistent with a Maxwellian, with only active-region
loops and moss requiring
\kappa\lesssim 3
; Del Zanna et al. (2022) reach similar conclusions for quiescent Fe
xii

diagnostics. At face value these results appear in tension with the

\kappa=2.5
value adopted from Edmonds (2026a).

The information-projection reading developed here predicts precisely
the Maxwellian-like spectroscopic appearance that Lörinčík
and Del Zanna observe. Under the D&D convention,

\langle E\rangle
is held fixed across
\kappa
by construction.
The EUV diagnostic projects
f_{\kappa}
onto the Maxwellian family
under a Bethe-weighted ionization kernel; as Dudík et al. (2014) establish, strong Fe
ix
–
xiii
line intensity
ratios vary by
\lesssim 20\%
across
\kappa=2
–
25
.
Quantitatively, the rigorous Dz23 line-ratio inversion of
§
III.2
returns
T_{M}=1.47\,\mathrm{MK}
for the
Fe
xii
/Fe
xiii
diagnostic applied to a
\kappa=2.5
distribution at
T_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.5\,\mathrm{MK}
— a
2\%

offset that falls well inside the observational precision of
Lörinčík et al. and Del Zanna et al. A spectroscopic
inversion that assumes Maxwellian rates and reads line intensities
therefore returns a temperature indistinguishable from
T_{\mathrm{eff}}

and does not detect the departure from Maxwellian form. What reveals

\kappa
is a second diagnostic that projects the same distribution
onto a different moment structure: in the corona, the radio source
function of §
II.4
.

The Lörinčík and Del Zanna results are therefore evidence
for the EUV-side projection mechanism described in §
II.2
,
not against the
\kappa
value taken from Edmonds (2026a). The
apparent tension becomes, under the information-projection reading, a
prediction: any single diagnostic operating inside the Bethe-weighted
moment class cannot resolve
\kappa
from a D&D kappa distribution at
fixed
\langle E\rangle
. Two diagnostics with different
projection kernels are required.

IV.4
Note on other systems

Whether analogous translations are productive for other observational
systems (circumgalactic medium X-ray emission measure versus fast
radio burst dispersion measure, galaxy cluster Sunyaev–Zel’dovich
versus X-ray brightness, electroencephalographic spectral diagnostics)
is a separate matter requiring independent study in each case.
It is flagged here as a direction only and not developed.

IV.5
What this paper does and does not claim

The components used here are individually established. Identity
(
8

Eq. 48), who derive it from analytical free-free expressions in the
mean-energy kappa-distribution convention. The scalar

T_{\mathrm{core}}=(\kappa-3/2)/\kappa\cdot T_{\mathrm{eff}}
is due
to Oka et al. (2013) and is quoted in Dzifčáková &
Dudík (2013, §2). The kappa-distribution interpretation of
the quiet-Sun coronal temperature ratio is due to Mercier & Chambe
(2015) and Edmonds (2026a) under the D&D convention. The
information-theoretic apparatus — maximum-entropy projection
(Jaynes 1957a,b),
I
-projection onto exponential families
(Csiszár 1975), relevant entropies under reduced descriptions
(Balian 1999), MaxEnt reading of
\kappa
via Tsallis (Livadiotis &
McComas 2013), and Kullback–Leibler-based non-Maxwellianity
measures in reconnection plasmas (Argall et al. 2022) — is
similarly standard. Nicholls, Dopita & Sutherland (2012) resolve
an H

-region / planetary-nebula diagnostic discrepancy
via a kappa distribution and are the closest astrophysical
precursor for the pattern of two diagnostics sampling different
parts of the same distribution.

The present paper’s contribution is the specific reading: the
quiet-Sun coronal ratio expressed as an information-theoretic
observable with a closed-form
\kappa
-dependence
(Corollary
1
); the relative-entropy values evaluated at

\kappa=2.5
(§

(
18

20

exactly
3/2
times the Itakura–Saito distance between the two

formalism for subsequent work where the same reading may apply.

IV.6
Scope limitations

The EUV-side projection is bounded by the Dudík et al. (2014)
intensity-ratio envelope specifically for strong Fe
ix
–
xiii

lines at
\kappa=2
–
25
; extending to ions or transitions outside
this set would require a separate bound. The radio-side
kinetic-limit approximation is exact at leading order; Coulomb-log
corrections are absorbed by the Gaunt factor in the standard way and
cancel between emissivity and absorption in the identity of
§
II.4
. The paper treats the quiet Sun as a
single-
\kappa
, single-
T_{\mathrm{eff}}
plasma; spatial structure,
multi-thermal components, and line-of-sight integration are deferred.
The two diagnostics do not, strictly, sample the same volume: EUV line
intensities are optically thin and weighted by
n_{e}^{2}
along the full
line of sight, while the 150–445 MHz radio brightness temperature is
formed at the
\tau\approx 1
surface in an optically thick regime.
Identifying the inferred
T_{\mathrm{EUV}}
and
T_{B}
as projections of a
common
f(\mathbf{v})
is therefore an idealization; in practice the
identity holds for the volume-averaged distribution to which both
diagnostics are sensitive in their respective formation layers, and
height stratification can introduce a residual the present paper does
not model. We note in passing that the sign of the residual goes
against, rather than with, a Maxwellian-stratification reading: the
quiet-Sun temperature profile is flat to slightly increasing with
height above the transition region, so a purely thermal model with

T_{B}
formed at higher altitude would predict
T_{B}\gtrsim T_{\mathrm{EUV}}
,
opposite to the observed
T_{B}\ll T_{\mathrm{EUV}}
. The observed
direction therefore cannot be produced by altitude stratification of a
single Maxwellian temperature, independent of the projection argument
developed here. Turbulent scattering is cited (§
III.3
) but
not redeveloped. The diagnostic projection is treated throughout as
a modelling description of the diagnostic operation, not as a
physical process.
The upstream mechanism setting
\kappa
at the coronal base is
inherited from Edmonds (2026a) and not engaged; whether the observed
low
\kappa
is reconcilable with perturbative nonlocal-transport
predictions of higher
\kappa
(Cranmer 2014) is a matter for the
mechanism literature (Barbieri et al. 2024; Banik et al. 2024;
Ewart et al. 2025; Landi & Pantellini 2001). The present paper is
mechanism-agnostic: the reading holds for whatever
\kappa

ultimately describes the quiet-Sun electrons.

V
Conclusion

The quiet-Sun coronal diagnostic temperature ratio
R\approx 2.4
,
stable across a solar cycle (Mercier & Chambe 2015), admits an
information-theoretic reading as a measurement of relative entropy
between two diagnostic projections of the coronal electron
distribution. The radio source function on a kappa-distributed
plasma equals
T_{\mathrm{core}}
in the D&D convention (Fleishman &
Kuznetsov 2014, Eq. 48). The EUV moment-matching projection returns

T_{\mathrm{eff}}
to within a shape-dependent correction bounded by
the Dudík et al. (2014) intensity-ratio insensitivity envelope.
The ratio is accordingly a closed-form function of
\kappa
alone,
and the two Kullback–Leibler divergences between the true
distribution and its Maxwellian projections
(§
IV.2
) satisfy the Itakura–Saito identity

\Delta D_{\mathrm{KL}}=(3/2)[R_{0}-\ln R_{0}-1]=(3/2)\,d_{\mathrm{IS}}(T_{\mathrm{eff}},T_{\mathrm{core}})
.
At
\kappa=2.5
the divergences are
0.320
and
1.195
nats and
the gap is
0.876
nats.

The contribution is the specific information-theoretic reading,
available for citation where the same structure may apply. Every
physical ingredient is attributed in §
IV.5
;
identity (
20
) is offered as an analytical
reference for classical observational systems in which two
diagnostics project different moments of a common non-equilibrium
distribution. Where such pairs exist, the Itakura–Saito form
gives the ratio of their outputs a closed-form interpretation, not
a physical one.
