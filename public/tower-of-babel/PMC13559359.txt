Prediction of postoperative pulmonary infection after video-assisted thoracoscopic anatomical pulmonary resection using an interpretable machine learning model
Authors: Maoqiang Yang, Zihan Yang, Dongxue Li, Guiting Huang, Yuwei Zhang, Kai Qian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPostoperative pulmonary infection (PPI) remains a significant complication after video-assisted thoracoscopic anatomical pulmonary resection (VAT-APR). This study aimed to develop and validate an interpretable machine learning (ML) model for perioperative prediction of PPI using Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) integrated with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) for patients undergoing VAT-APR.MethodsThis retrospective predictive modeling study included 512 patients who underwent VAT-APR between January 2020 and December 2024 at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, The First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province, Kunming, China. Preoperative and intraoperative features were extracted, and the dataset was randomly partitioned into training (70%) and validation (30%) sets. Five ML algorithms were evaluated, with the LightGBM model selected as optimal.ResultsThe LightGBM model demonstrated strong discriminative performance, achieving an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.832 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.795–0.869], with an accuracy of 0.781 and an F1-score of 0.710, outperforming five comparator algorithms. Feature importance analysis identified operative time, percentage of predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1%), age, and monocyte count as the most influential predictors. The model showed good calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test, P=0.32) and yielded a positive net clinical benefit across a range of threshold probabilities in decision curve analysis.ConclusionsThe interpretable LightGBM-SHAP model provides a robust and transparent approach for perioperative risk assessment of PPI, with potential to inform individualized perioperative management and targeted preventive strategies.

Key findings

• We developed and validated an interpretable Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM)-SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) model for perioperative prediction of postoperative pulmonary infection (PPI) after video-assisted thoracoscopic anatomical pulmonary resection (VAT-APR).

• The model achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.832 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.795–0.869], with accuracy of 78.1% and F1-score of 0.710, outperforming five comparator algorithms.

• Operative time, forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1%), age, and monocyte count were identified as the most influential predictors.

• The model demonstrated good calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test, P=0.32) and positive net clinical benefit across a range of threshold probabilities.

What is known and what is new?

• PPI remains a significant complication after VAT-APR, with incidence up to 25% in high-risk cohorts, leading to prolonged hospitalization and increased costs. Traditional risk stratification tools rely on linear methodologies that fail to capture complex, non-linear interactions between clinical variables.

• This study introduces a novel interpretable LightGBM-SHAP framework that not only achieves superior predictive performance but also provides transparent, patient-specific explanations through SHAP values, addressing the “black box” limitation of machine learning in clinical practice.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The interpretable LightGBM-SHAP model provides a robust tool for individualized perioperative risk assessment and can inform targeted preventive strategies.

• Clinicians should consider integrating operative time, pulmonary function (FEV1%), age, and inflammatory markers (monocyte count) into preoperative decision-making.

• The SHAP-based explanations enable clinicians to understand which specific factors drive risk for individual patients, facilitating personalized perioperative management rather than relying solely on population-level risk scores.

Introduction

Video-assisted thoracoscopic anatomical pulmonary resection (VAT-APR), encompassing both lobectomy and segmentectomy, has fundamentally reshaped the surgical landscape for early-stage pulmonary malignancies and benign lung diseases (1-5). By mitigating surgical trauma and preserving thoracic wall integrity, VAT-APR offers superior perioperative outcomes compared to conventional thoracotomy (6-10). Nevertheless, postoperative pulmonary infection (PPI) persists as a formidable clinical challenge, with an incidence rate of as high as 25% in certain high-risk cohorts (11-15). PPI serves as a critical driver of prolonged hospitalization, escalating intensive care unit (ICU) utilization, and substantial healthcare costs, while also exerting a deleterious impact on the long-term survival of surgical oncological patients (16-20).

Precise perioperative risk assessment is the cornerstone of personalized thoracic surgical care (21-25). While traditional clinical scoring systems, such as the ARISCAT and Arozullah scores, have been widely utilized, they primarily rely on generalized logistic regression frameworks (26-30). Such linear methodologies frequently struggle to account for the intricate, non-linear synergies between heterogeneous clinical variables, including immune senescence, cardiac reserve deficits [New York Heart Association functional (NYHA) class], and preoperative laboratory fluctuations (31-35).

The advent of machine learning (ML) has introduced a paradigm shift in predictive modeling, particularly for complex structured data (36-40). Among these, the Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) has demonstrated state-of-the-art efficiency and predictive fidelity in electronic health records (41-45). However, the “black box” nature of ML algorithms remains a significant barrier to widespread clinical adoption (46). To address this lack of transparency, we integrated SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), a method rooted in cooperative game theory, to provide intuitive, patient-specific explanations (47-50). This study aimed to develop and validate an interpretable LightGBM-SHAP framework to predict PPI risk following VAT-APR at Thoracic Surgery Department, The First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (16,27) (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1092/rc).

Methods

Study cohort and roadmap

This retrospective study involved patients undergoing VAT-APR between January 2020 and December 2024. We enrolled 512 eligible patients after applying rigorous exclusion criteria. As depicted in the Technical Roadmap (Figure 1), the research progressed through four distinct phases: (I) cohort screening and standardization; (II) extraction of 25 preoperative and intraoperative features; (III) multi-algorithm model optimization; and (IV) multidimensional performance and SHAP-based interpretability analysis. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for written informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee. All patient data were fully anonymized and de-identified.

Flowchart of the study design and analytical workflow. AUC, area under the curve; DCA, decision curve analysis; SHAP, SHapley Additive exPlanations; VAT-APR, video-assisted thoracoscopic anatomical pulmonary resection.

Feature engineering and data splitting

Preoperative laboratory markers (e.g., monocyte count), demographics, and pulmonary function metrics [forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1%)] were prioritized. Cardiac function was standardized into categorical variables (NYHA Class 0–I vs. II–IV) (51). The dataset was randomly partitioned into a training set (70%, n=358) for model training and hyperparameter tuning, and an internal hold-out validation set (30%, n=154) for independent performance verification (42).

Model evaluation and explainability

Five ML algorithms were evaluated, with hyperparameters optimized via 5-fold cross-validation. Discrimination was quantified by the AUC (13,31), and clinical utility was assessed through decision curve analysis (DCA) (14,52). To decode the optimal model, SHAP values were implemented to provide global importance (bar charts), feature interactions (dependence plots), and individual risk profiles (force plots) (10,11).

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were compared using Student’s t-test and expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Categorical variables were analyzed using the Chi-squared test and presented as frequency (percentage). Model discrimination was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Calibration was evaluated using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. A two-tailed P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using Python (version 3.9) with scikit-learn and LightGBM libraries.

Patients in the PPI group were significantly older (68.4±9.2 vs. 61.2±10.5 years, P<0.001) and had a higher prevalence of smoking history (56.9% vs. 36.1%, P<0.001). Discussion

In the present study, we developed and validated a robust ML framework based on the LightGBM algorithm to predict PPI following VAT-APR. Our model demonstrated superior discriminative power, achieving a validated AUC of 0.832, which significantly outperforms traditional scoring systems and standard logistic regression models (15,31,41). By integrating the SHAP framework, we successfully decoded the “black box” nature of the gradient boosting machine, providing clinicians with a transparent, feature-level understanding of individual patient risks (46,47).

Clinical significance of key predictors

The SHAP analysis (Figure 4) identified advanced age, smoking history, and FEV1% as the primary drivers of PPI risk. The positive correlation between senescence and respiratory complications is well-documented, often attributed to the progressive decline in mucociliary clearance and age-related immune senescence (41-44). Notably, our SHAP interaction analysis (Figure 4B,4C) revealed a critical non-linear synergy between age and pulmonary reserve. In patients aged over 65 years (53), we observed that the protective effect of a high FEV1% diminishes rapidly, and even marginal reductions in lung function lead to an exponential escalation in predicted PPI probability. This suggests that for the elderly, 'borderline' lung function carries significantly higher weight than in younger cohorts, necessitating more aggressive preoperative pulmonary prehabilitation (15,54).

Furthermore, the inclusion of NYHA Class II–IV heart failure as a significant predictor highlights the critical interplay between cardiac reserve and pulmonary outcomes (51,55). Subclinical pulmonary congestion associated with cardiac dysfunction may impair local alveolar immune responses, thereby facilitating bacterial colonization post-surgery (56). Additionally, the emergence of Monocyte Count as a top predictor reflects the influence of the preoperative systemic inflammatory state (57). An elevated monocyte count often indicates a pro-inflammatory baseline, which may predispose patients to an exaggerated systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) following the surgical insult of VAT-APR (54,57).

Advantages of the LightGBM-SHAP framework

Traditional risk stratification tools, such as the ARISCAT score, frequently rely on linear methodologies that fail to capture the high-dimensional interactions inherent in complex surgical data (26,28). In contrast, the LightGBM model efficiently handles non-linearities and missing values, demonstrating superior predictive fidelity over SVM and Logistic Regression (Table 2) (41,45).

However, the clinical utility of ML has historically been hindered by an interpretability crisis (46). By employing the SHAP framework (Figure 4A-4E), we transitioned from simple risk scores to a more granular, patient-specific diagnostic approach (47-50). The ability to visualize feature contributions through force plots allows for “precision thoracic surgery” (58). Especially when operative time exceeds “180 minutes” and “monocyte count” may benefit from targeted prophylactic strategies and early mobilization, whereas another patient flagged due to “smoking history” would require intensive preoperative cessation counseling (21,59).

Clinical utility and practical application

The clinical utility of our model was rigorously confirmed through DCA (Figure 3). The LightGBM model provided a consistently higher net benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities (10–80%) compared to “treat-all” or “treat-none” strategies (52). This confirms that the model can effectively guide the allocation of perioperative resources—such as targeted physiotherapy or high-level antimicrobial prophylaxis—without the burden of over-treating low-risk individuals (24,25). At The First People’s Hospital of Yunnan Province, this framework offers a promising tool for optimizing the surgical pathway and reducing the economic burden associated with PPI (14,16).

Limitations and future perspectives

Despite its robust performance, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a retrospective study conducted at a single center, the generalizability of our findings requires further validation in multicenter, prospective cohorts (59). Second, although preoperative and some intraoperative variables were included, certain dynamic factors—such as intraoperative fluid balance and postoperative pain management scores—were not fully integrated (34,46). Future research should focus on developing dynamic, real-time risk assessment tools that update throughout the perioperative period to provide even more precise guidance for postoperative care (58).

Conclusions

In conclusion, the SHAP-enhanced LightGBM model provides a highly accurate and clinically interpretable framework for predicting PPI after VAT-APR. By integrating key variables such as operative time, pulmonary reserve, and inflammatory markers, this tool enables clinicians to identify high-risk individuals and implement individualized preventive interventions, ultimately improving patient outcomes and optimizing perioperative resource allocation.
