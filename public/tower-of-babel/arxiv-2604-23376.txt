Hybrid conjecture in a mixed Shimura variety
Rodolphe Richard, Andrei Yafaev

Abstract
. The authors previously formulated the hybrid conjecture, unifying André-Pink-Zannier and André-Oort conjectures, and proved it in Shimura varieties of abelian type. We study its analogue for mixed Shimura varieties, and consider the prime example, the universal abelian scheme 𝒜 g → 𝔸 g \mathcal{A}_{g}\to\mathbb{A}_{g} . In a radical departure from the Pila-Zannier strategy, typically applied to such questions, we employ instead a combination of equidistribution and o-minimality Our main result strictly includes the following: the Hybrid Conjecture, in particular the André-Pink-Zannier and André-Oort conjectures, for 𝔸 g \mathbb{A}_{g} ; the mixed André-Oort conjecture for 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} ; and Manin-Mumford conjecture for arbitrary abelian varieties. It also yields an analogue of the “Manin-Mumford in arithmetic pencil”, a result of [ 2 ] , for abelian schemes over a variety. The mixed hybrid conjecture in 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} also encompasses the Mordell-Lang conjecture. We actually reduce the mixed hybrid conjecture for 𝔸 g \mathbb{A}_{g} to its ”mordellic” part. We also prove, Galois-theoretic results: uniform variants on the Ribet’s Kummer theory of Abelian varieties, and Serre’s theorem on Lang’s conjecture.

1.2.
The Mordellic case

Two of the main strategies used to tackle problems of Zilber-Pink type are: the Pila-Zannier strategy; and Equidistribution.
Both approaches rely on ”large Galois orbits”.
Moreover, the Pila-Zannier strategy needs quantitative lower bounds, in terms of the height of the points one considers, for the cardinality of the Galois orbits.

Our proof of Theorem

1.3.1.
Equidistibution

The first is based on Theorem

[

The equidistribution method, cf.
[

, traditionally proceeds as follows. We consider a sequence of subsets

(1)

(2)

One can then infer that

Theorem

A typical example is studied in this article, and this aticle can serve as an introduction to this new paradigm of the equidistribution method.

Proof.

Let

Let
\Gamma\leq A(K)

B.1.
Split case

Theorem B.1
.

Let
L/{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
be a finite extension, let
k,n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
be finite dimensional
L
-vector spaces.
Let
W\leq V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}L^{n_{i}}\otimes V_{i}
be an
O_{L}
-submodule. We consider the action

E:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\rm End}(O_{L}^{n_{i}})\otimes Id_{V_{i}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\rm End}(L^{n_{i}})\otimes{\rm End}(V_{i})\leq{\rm End}(V).

Let
\lambda\in L
be such that

\lambda\cdot E\cdot W\leq W.

Then there are
O_{L}
-modules
\Lambda_{i}\leq V_{i}
such that

\lambda\cdot\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}O_{L}^{n_{i}}\otimes_{O_{L}}\Lambda_{i}\leq W\text{ and }\lambda\cdot W\leq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}O_{L}^{n_{i}}\otimes_{O_{L}}\Lambda_{i}.

Proof of theorem
B.1
.

Let
e_{i,1},\ldots,e_{i,n_{i}}
be the standard basis of
{L}^{n_{i}}
, let

{\varepsilon}_{i,(m,m^{\prime})}:=e_{i,m}\times{}^{t}e_{i,m^{\prime}}\in{\rm End}(L^{n_{i}}):(\lambda_{n})_{1\leq n\leq n_{i}}\mapsto(\delta_{m,n}\lambda_{m^{\prime}})_{1\leq n\leq n_{i}}

be the standard elementary matrices and let

\pi_{i,m}={\varepsilon}_{i,(m,m)}\in{\rm End}(L^{n_{i}}):(\lambda_{n})_{1\leq n\leq n_{i}}\mapsto(\delta_{m,n}\lambda_{n})_{1\leq n\leq n_{i}}

be the
m
-th standard projector.

We can uniquely decompose any
v\in V
as

v=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\sum^{n_{i}}_{m=1}e_{i,m}\otimes v_{i,m}.

Let
\Lambda_{i,m}:=\{v\in V_{i}|e_{i,m}\otimes v\in W\}
. If
v\in\Lambda_{i,m}
,
then

e_{i,m^{\prime}}\otimes\lambda\cdot v=\lambda{\varepsilon}_{i,(m^{\prime},m)}(e_{i,m}\otimes v)\in\lambda\cdot E(W)\leq W.

Therefore
\lambda\cdot\Lambda_{i,m}\leq\Lambda_{i,m^{\prime}}
. We deduce

\Lambda_{i}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Lambda_{i,m^{\prime}}\geq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n_{i}}\Lambda_{i,m}\geq\lambda\cdot\Lambda_{i}.

By construction

(40)

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}O_{L}^{n_{i}}\otimes\lambda\cdot\Lambda_{i}\leq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\sum^{n_{i}}_{m=1}e_{i,m}\otimes\Lambda_{i,m}\leq W.

For
w\in W
, we have

e_{i}\otimes\lambda w_{i,m}=\lambda\pi_{i,m}(w)\in W.

Therefore
\lambda\cdot w_{i,m}\in\Lambda_{i,m}
and

\lambda\cdot w=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k,1\leq m\leq n_{i}}e_{i,m}\otimes\lambda\cdot w_{i,m}\in\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\sum^{n_{i}}_{m=1}e_{i,m}\otimes\Lambda_{i,m}\leq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}O_{L}^{n_{i}}\otimes\Lambda_{i}.

As
w\in W
is arbitrary, we conclude

(41)

\lambda\cdot W\leq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}O_{L}^{n_{i}}\otimes\Lambda_{i}.

The Theorem follows from (
40
) and (
41
).
∎

Corollary B.2
.

Let us choose a
L
-homogeneous norm on each
V_{i}
and equip
V
with the corresponding norm.
Let
e_{i}\in(O^{\times}_{L})^{n_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. Then

\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\cdot\lVert W\rVert\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\lVert(e_{i}\otimes V_{i})\cap W\rVert

Proof.

For
e\in O_{L}^{n_{i}}
, we have
\lambda\cdot(e\otimes\Lambda_{i})\leq(e\otimes V_{i})\cap W
. Therefore

\left|\lambda\right|\cdot\lVert e\rVert\cdot\lVert O_{L}^{n_{i}}\otimes\Lambda_{i}\rVert=\left|\lambda\right|\cdot\lVert e\otimes\Lambda_{i}\rVert\leq\lVert(e\otimes V_{i})\cap W\rVert.

For
e\in(O_{L}^{\times})^{n_{i}}
we have
\lVert e\rVert=1
.
We deduce

\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\cdot\left\lVert W\right\rVert\leq\left|\lambda\right|\cdot\left\lVert\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}O_{L}^{n_{i}}\otimes\Lambda_{i}\right\rVert=\left|\lambda\right|\cdot\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\left\lVert O_{L}^{n_{i}}\otimes\Lambda_{i}\right\rVert\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\left\lVert(e\otimes V_{i})\cap W\right\rVert.

∎

.

. For

Let

.

We know that

Appendix C
On abelian varieties

Let
A_{P},B\leq A
be abelian varieties over
{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
P\in A_{P}
be such that
{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot P
is Zariski dense in
A_{P}
. Let
\pi:A\to A^{\prime}:=A/B
be the quotient map.

We recall that there exists
B^{\prime}\leq A
such that
A=B^{\prime}+B
and
B\cap B^{\prime}
is finite.
There exists thus
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
be such that

(58)

B\cap B^{\prime}\leq A[n].

Theorem C.1
.

Consider
Q\in A
and
T\in{A}_{tors}
and
\phi\in\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A)
such that
d\cdot Q=\phi(P)+T
for some
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Let
d^{\prime}|d
be such that

\pi\circ\phi(A_{P}[d])\leq A^{\prime}[d^{\prime}].

Then there exists
Q^{\prime}\in Q+B
and
T^{\prime}\in{A}_{tors}
and
\phi^{\prime}\in\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A)
such that

n\cdot d^{\prime}\cdot Q^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime}(P)+T^{\prime}.

C.1.
Auxiliary Definitions and Results

We define

\Theta:=\{\phi(P)|\phi\in\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A)\}\text{ and }\Theta^{\prime}:=\{\phi(P)|\phi\in\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A^{\prime})\}.

For
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, let

\Theta_{d}:=\{Q\in A|d\cdot Q\in\Theta\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Theta^{\prime}_{d}:=\{Q\in A^{\prime}|d\cdot Q\in\Theta^{\prime}\}.

We claim that the map

\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A)\to\Theta\quad\text{ and }\quad\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A^{\prime})\to\Theta^{\prime}

given by
\phi\mapsto\phi(P)
are bijective.

Proof.

The map are subjective by definition of
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime\prime}
. Observe that
\phi\mapsto\phi(P)
is an additive morphism. The injectivity follows from

\phi(P)=0\Rightarrow P\in\ker(\phi)\Rightarrow\overline{P\cdot{\mathbb{Z}}}^{Zar}\leq\ker(\phi)\Rightarrow\phi(A_{P})=0\Rightarrow\phi=0.\qed

Recall that for an algebraic subgroup
G
of an abelian variety
A
, and a morphism
\phi:A\to B
of abelian varieties, we have

G\leq\ker(\phi)\Leftrightarrow\exists\psi\in\mbox{Hom}(A/G,B),\phi=\psi\circ\pi,

where
\pi:A\to A/G
is the quotient map. In particular, for
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
G=A[d]
,
then

(59)

\forall\phi\in\mbox{Hom}(A,A),A[d]\leq\ker(\phi)\Leftrightarrow\phi\in d\cdot\mbox{Hom}(A,A).

Lemma C.2
.

Let
n
be as in (
58
). Let
\pi_{\star}
be either of the maps

\phi\mapsto\pi\circ\phi:\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A)\to\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A^{\prime})\text{ and }\phi(P)\mapsto\pi\circ\phi(P):\Theta\to\Theta^{\prime}.

Then for any
x\in\coker(\pi_{\star})
, we have
n\cdot x=0
.

Proof.

Let
B^{\prime}\leq A
be as in (
58
), and let
p=\pi|_{B^{\prime}}:B^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}
. We recall that
\ker(p)=B\cap B^{\prime}\leq B^{\prime}[n]
by (
58
). It follows that there exists a homomorphism
\sigma:A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
such that
p\circ\sigma=nId_{A^{\prime}}
is the multiplication-by-
n
map on
A^{\prime}
.

For
\phi^{\prime}\in\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A^{\prime})
, we have

n\cdot\phi^{\prime}=\pi\circ\sigma\circ\phi^{\prime},

and thus
\phi^{\prime}=\pi_{\star}(\phi)
for
\phi=\sigma\circ\phi^{\prime}
. For
\phi^{\prime}(P)\in\Theta^{\prime}
, we have
n\cdot\phi^{\prime}(P)=\pi(\phi(P))
. The lemma follows.
∎

Lemma C.3
.

For every
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}

(1)

the map
A[d]\to A^{\prime}[d]
is surjective

(2)

and

\forall Q^{\prime}\in\Theta_{d}^{\prime},\exists\widetilde{Q^{\prime}}\in\Theta_{n\cdot d},\pi(\widetilde{Q^{\prime}})=Q^{\prime}.

Proof.

It is a fact that the exact sequence
0\to B\to A\to A^{\prime}\to 0
is split in the category
of real Lie groups, and a fortiori as a sequence of abstract groups. The first assertion follows.

Let us prove the second assertion. Let
Q^{\prime}\in\Theta^{\prime}_{d}
and choose
\phi^{\prime}\in\mbox{Hom}(A_{P},A^{\prime})
such that
dQ^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime}(P)
. Let
\sigma
be as in the proof of Lemma
C.2
and
\phi:=\sigma\circ\phi^{\prime}
, and let
Q
be such that

.
We have

\pi(n(Q))=\pi(\sigma(Q^{\prime}))=nQ^{\prime}.

Therefore
\pi(Q)-Q^{\prime}=T^{\prime}
for some

. By (
1
), we have
T^{\prime}=\pi(T)
for some

. We have

because

This proves the lemma with
{\widetilde{Q^{\prime}}}=Q+T
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

From

follows
