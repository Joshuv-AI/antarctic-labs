The distribution of the de Moivre experiment
Hacène Belbachir, Hamza Zeggada

Abstract
In this paper, we focus on de Moivre random experience which allows us to introduce the s − s- Bernoulli distribution and the bi s nomial distribution. We present some probabilistic properties such as the expectation, the variance, the skewness and kurtosis coefficients, the moments and the generating functions. Then we establish that for s ∈ ℕ s\in\mathbb{N} , the bi s nomial distribution converges to a limiting Poisson and normal distributions when n → ∞ . n\rightarrow\infty.

3.3
Relation between Eulerian numbers and the moments function

In order to introduce higher moments order, we need to consider the moment-generating function for the s-Bernoulli distribution,

M_{X}(t)=E(e^{tX})=\frac{(pe^{t})^{s+1}-q^{s+1}}{pe^{t}-q}.

(12)

The explicit expression of this function showed a relation with the explicit formula of the Eulerian numbers,
[
9
]
, given by

A(n,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{n+1}{j}(k+1-j)^{n},\qquad\ 0\leq k\leq n-1,

(13)

where
A(n,k)
are called Eulerian numbers.

By introducing the parameter
s
, we get an extended Eulerian number form.

Definition 3.1
.

For integers
n
,
k
and
s
, the extended Eulerian number is defined by

A(n,k,s)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{n+1}{j}(k+1-j-s)^{n},\qquad\ 0\leq k\leq n.

(14)

For
s=0
the summand reduces to that of (
13

A(n,k,0)=A(n,k)
for
0\leq k\leq n-1
, with the additional value
A(n,n,0)=0
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The function of the moments of the s-Bernoulli distribution is given by,

m_{r}(s)=\frac{(-1)^{r}}{(p-q)^{r+1}}p\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left(A(r,k,s+1)p^{r-k+s}q^{k}-A(r,k,1)p^{r-k}q^{k+s}\right),\quad\text{ for }p\neq q,

(15)

m_{r}(s)=\frac{1}{(r+1)(s+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{r}(-1)^{k}{r+1\choose k}B_{k}s^{r-k+1},\quad\text{ for }p=q,

(16)

the constants
B_{k}
, for
p=q
, are the Bernoulli numbers, see
[
20
]
.

Proof.

For
p\neq q
, the successive derivatives of the moment-generating function generate extended Eulerian numbers
A(r,k,s)
. We obtain in the numerator two polynomial forms. The first is generated by the coefficients
A(r,k,s+1)
and the second by the  extended Eulerian numbers
A(r,k,1)
. For
p=q
, we find a Faulhaber’s like formula
m_{r}(s)=p^{s}\sum_{k=1}^{s}k^{r}
which can be expressed in terms of the Bernoulli numbers.
∎

We denote by
\mu_{r}=E(X-E(X))^{r}
and
\sigma=\sqrt{\mu_{2}}.
Then the skewness and the kurtosis are denoted respectively by
\gamma_{1}=\mu_{3}/\sigma^{3}
and
\gamma_{2}=\mu_{4}/\sigma^{4}.

We take as an example, the third and the fourth moments for any

, which are respectively

m_{3}(s)=\frac{1}{4}s^{2}(s+1),\qquad m_{4}(s)=\frac{1}{30}s\left(6s^{3}+9s^{2}+s-1\right),

(17)

where the skewness is null and the kurtosis is as follows

\gamma_{2}=\frac{3\left(3s^{2}+6s-4\right)}{5s(s+2)}.

(18)

5
Going to the Poisson Distribution

Classically, the Poisson distribution is a limiting case of the binomial distribution which arises when the number of trials
n
increases indefinitely while the parameter
\lambda
, which is the expected value of the number of successes from the trials, remains constant. Using the same approach we do the same for the bi
s
nomial distribution.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
k
and
n
be integers, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{{n\choose k}}{n^{k}}=\dfrac{1}{k!}

(31)

Proof.

We have
{n\choose k}=\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)}{k!}
, if we take the limit

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{{n\choose k}}{n^{k}}=\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)}{k!n^{k}}=\dfrac{1}{k!}.

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
s,k
and
n
be integers, we have

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{{n\choose k}_{s}}{n^{k}}=\dfrac{1}{k!}.

(32)

Proof.

To find the limit
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{{n\choose k}_{s}}{n^{k}}
. We use the formula of Belbachir and Benmezai in
[
4
]
.

{n\choose k}_{s}=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{s}=k}{n\choose j_{1}}{n\choose j_{2}}\cdots{n\choose j_{s}}a^{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\cdots+sj_{s}},

(33)

with
a=\exp\left(-2\pi i/(s+1)\right)
, where
i^{2}=-1.

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{n\choose k}_{s}}{n^{k}}&=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{s}=k}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{n\choose j_{1}}}{n^{j_{1}}}\frac{{n\choose j_{2}}}{n^{j_{2}}}\cdots\frac{{n\choose j_{s}}}{n^{j_{s}}}a^{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\cdots+sj_{s}}\\
&=(-1)^{k}\sum\limits_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{s}=k}\frac{1}{j_{1}!}\frac{1}{j_{2}!}\cdots\frac{1}{j_{s}!}a^{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\cdots+sj_{s}}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\sum\limits_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{s}=k}{k\choose j_{1},\cdots,j_{s}}a^{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\cdots+sj_{s}}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}(a+a^{2}+\cdots+a^{s})^{k}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{a-a^{s+1}}{1-a}\right)^{k},\\
\end{split}

since
a^{s+1}=\exp\left(-2\pi i\right)=1
then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{n\choose k}_{s}}{n^{k}}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}(-1)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}.

∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
s
be a non-zero integer. Let
(p_{n})_{n}
and
(q_{n})_{n}
be two sequences of real numbers in the interval
]0,1[

(X_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of random variables, where each

takes its values in the set
\{0,1,\dots,sn\}
and follows a bi

\mathcal{B}^{(s)}(n,p_{n})

, such that:

\lim_{n\to\infty}E(X_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}np_{n}\frac{(s+1)p_{n}^{s}-1}{p_{n}-q_{n}}=\lambda_{s}>0,

(34)

p_{n}^{s}+p_{n}^{s-1}q_{n}+\cdots+q_{n}^{s}=1.

Then, the sequence of random variables

(35)

Proof.

The main goal is to evaluate the following limit

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}{n\choose k}_{s}p_{n}^{k}q_{n}^{sn-k},\text{ with }p_{n}^{s}+p_{n}^{s-1}q_{n}+\cdots+q_{n}^{s}=1.

np_{n}((s+1)p_{n}^{s}-1)-(p_{n}-q_{n})\lambda_{s}=0.\end{array}\right.

(37)

In a first step, from the first equation of the system (

f(p_{n},q_{n})=p_{n}^{s+1}-q_{n}^{s+1}-p_{n}+q_{n}
. We have

\dfrac{\partial f(p_{n},q_{n})}{\partial q_{n}}=-(s+1)q_{n}^{s}+1\Rightarrow\dfrac{\partial f(0,1)}{\partial q_{n}}=-s\neq 0.

Since in addition
f(0,1)=0
, by applying Theorem
5.2
. There are two open intervals
I
and
J
, with
0\in I
and
1\in J
, and a function
g:I\rightarrow J
of class
C^{1}
such that

\text{ for all }\qquad(p_{n},q_{n})\in I\times J,f(p_{n},q_{n})=0\Leftrightarrow q_{n}=g(p_{n}).

Moreover, since
f
is of class
C^{\infty}
,
g
is also of class
C^{\infty}
on
I
. In particular, there exists a power series of any order around 0, that of order two is written

q_{n}=g(0)+\dfrac{g^{\prime}(0)}{1!}p_{n}+\dfrac{g^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2!}p_{n}^{2}+o(p_{n}^{2}).

(38)

We already know that
g(0)=1
. To calculate
g^{\prime}(0)
and
g^{\prime\prime}(0)
, we derive twice the function

f\left(p_{n},g(p_{n})\right)=p_{n}^{s+1}-g(p_{n})^{s+1}-p_{n}+g(p_{n}).

We derive a first time and evaluate for
p_{n}=0
, we find

(s+1)p_{n}^{s}-(s+1)g^{\prime}(p_{n})g(p_{n})^{s}-1+g^{\prime}(p_{n})=0\Rightarrow g^{\prime}(0)=\dfrac{-1}{s}.

We derive a second time and evaluate for
p_{n}=0
to find

\begin{split}&(s+1)sp_{n}^{s-1}-(s+1)g^{\prime\prime}(p_{n})g(p_{n})^{s}-(s+1)sg^{\prime}(p_{n})^{2}g(p_{n})^{s-1}+g^{\prime\prime}(p_{n})=0\\
&\Rightarrow g^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\dfrac{s+1}{s^{2}}.\end{split}

Thus, by substituting in (
38
), we get

q_{n}=1-\frac{p_{n}}{s}-\frac{(s+1)p_{n}^{2}}{2s^{2}}+o(p_{n}^{2}).

(39)

In a second step, we give
p_{n}
in terms of
\lambda_{s}
from the second equation of the system (
37
). In the same way, we suppose a function
f
defined by

f(p_{n},\lambda_{s})=np_{n}((s+1)p_{n}^{s}-1)+\left(1-\frac{s+1}{s}p_{n}-\frac{(s+1)p_{n}^{2}}{2s^{2}}+o(p_{n}^{2})\right)\lambda_{s}.

We have

\begin{split}&\dfrac{\partial f(p_{n},\lambda_{s})}{\partial p_{n}}=n((s+1)p_{n}^{s}-1)+n(s+1)sp_{n}^{s}-\left(\frac{s+1}{s}+\frac{(s+1)}{s^{2}}p_{n}+o(p_{n})\right)\lambda_{s}\\
&\Rightarrow\dfrac{\partial f(0,0)}{\partial p_{n}}=-n\neq 0.\end{split}

Since in addition
f(0,0)=0
, we apply the Implicit Function Theorem, we define a function
h:I\rightarrow J
of class
C^{1}
such that
\text{ for }(p_{n},\lambda_{s})\in I\times J

f(p_{n},\lambda_{s})=0\Leftrightarrow p_{n}=h(\lambda_{s}).

So there exists a power series of any order around 0, the one of order two is given by

p_{n}=h(0)+\dfrac{h^{\prime}(0)}{1!}\lambda_{s}+\dfrac{h^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2!}\lambda_{s}^{2}+o(\lambda_{s}^{2}).

(40)

We already know that
h(0)=0
. To calculate
h^{\prime}(0)
and
h^{\prime\prime}(0)
, we derive twice the function

f(h(\lambda_{s}),\lambda_{s})=nh(\lambda_{s})((s+1)h(\lambda_{s})^{s}-1)+(1-\frac{s+1}{s}h(\lambda_{s})-\frac{(s+1)h(\lambda_{s})^{2}}{2s^{2}}+o(h(\lambda_{s})^{2}))\lambda_{s}.

We derive over
\lambda_{s}
a first time and evaluate for
\lambda_{s}=0
we find

\begin{split}&nh^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})((s+1)h(\lambda_{s})^{s}-1)+n(s+1)sh^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})h(\lambda_{s})^{s}+\\
&\left(1-\frac{s+1}{s}h(\lambda_{s})-\frac{(s+1)}{s^{2}}h(\lambda_{s})^{2}+o(h(\lambda_{s})^{2})\right)-\\
&\left(\frac{s+1}{s}h^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})+\frac{(s+1)}{s^{2}}h^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})h(\lambda_{s})+o(h(\lambda_{s}))\right)\lambda_{s}=0\\
&\text{ which gives }h^{\prime}(0)=\dfrac{1}{n}.\end{split}

We derive over
\lambda_{s}
a second time and evaluate for
\lambda_{s}=0
to find

\begin{split}&nh^{\prime\prime}(\lambda_{s})\left((s+1)h(\lambda_{s})^{s}-1\right)+2ns(s+1)h^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})^{2}h(\lambda_{s})^{s-1}+\\
&n(s+1)h(\lambda_{s})\left(sh^{\prime\prime}(\lambda_{s})h(\lambda_{s})^{s-1}+(s-1)sh^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})^{2}h(\lambda_{s})^{s-2}\right)-\\
&2\left(\frac{(s+1)h(\lambda_{s})h^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})}{s^{2}}+\frac{(s+1)h^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})}{s}\right)-\\
&\left(\frac{(s+1)h^{\prime\prime}(\lambda_{s})}{s}+\frac{(s+1)\left(2h(\lambda_{s})h^{\prime\prime}(\lambda_{s})+2h^{\prime}(\lambda_{s})^{2}\right)}{2s^{2}}\right)\lambda_{s}=0\\
&\Rightarrow h^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\dfrac{2(s+1)}{sn^{2}}.\end{split}

By substituting in (
40
), we get

p_{n}=\frac{\lambda_{s}}{n}-\frac{s+1}{sn^{2}}\lambda_{s}^{2}+o(\lambda_{s}^{2}).

(41)

We substitute
p_{n}
in (
39
) to obtain the expression of
q_{n}
in terms of
\lambda_{s}
,

q_{n}=1-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{sn}+\frac{s+1}{2(sn)^{2}}\lambda_{s}^{2}+o(\lambda_{s}^{2}).

(42)

We replace the two expressions of
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
in (
36
)

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}{n\choose k}_{s}p_{n}^{k}q_{n}^{sn-k}\\
&=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}{n\choose k}_{s}\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{n}-\frac{s+1}{sn^{2}}\lambda_{s}^{2}+o(\lambda_{s}^{2})\right)^{k}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{sn}+\frac{s+1}{2(sn)^{2}}\lambda_{s}^{2}+o(\lambda_{s}^{2})\right)^{sn-k}\\
&=\lambda_{s}^{k}e^{-\lambda_{s}}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{{n\choose k}_{s}}{n^{k}}.\end{split}

(43)

Then, using Lemma
5.2
, we obtain the convergence to the Poisson distribution

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}{n\choose k}_{s}p_{n}^{k}q_{n}^{sn-k}=\frac{\lambda_{s}^{k}}{k!}e^{-\lambda_{s}}.

∎

The Poisson distribution
\mathcal{P}(\lambda_{s})
is the limiting distribution of the bi
s
nomial distribution as
n
tends to infinity. We note that the expectation and the variance of the bi
s
nomial distribution converge towards those of the
s-
Poisson distribution when we tend to infinity.

6
Going to a Normal Distribution

The approximation of the binomial distribution in the particular limiting case where the number of possible observations
n
becomes infinite and the probability of success
p
for each is large in this case,
np>>1
, gives a normal distribution. We approach this binomial distribution by the normal distribution having the same expectation
\mu
and the same variance
\sigma^{2}
. We are going to apply this concept on the bi
s
nomial distribution and see how is converging to the normal distribution.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a bi
s
nomial random variable with parameters
n
and
p
,
\mathcal{B}^{s}(n,p)
.

The bi
s
nomial random variable
X
has, for large
n
, an approximate normal distribution with mean
\mu_{s}=np\frac{(s+1)p^{s}-1}{(p-q)}
and variance
\sigma^{2}_{s}=npq\frac{1-(s+1)^{2}(pq)^{s}}{(p-q)^{2}}.

Proof.

The MGF method

The moment generating function of the random variable
X
evaluates to

M_{X}(T)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{sn}e^{tk}{n\choose k}_{s}p^{k}q^{sn-k}=\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(e^{t}p\right)^{k}q^{s-k}\right)^{n}
.

Let
Z=\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\left(X-E(X)\right)
. Below we derive the mgf of
Z
, which is given by

M_{Z}(t)=E(e^{tZ})=\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\exp\left(\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)p^{k}q^{s-k}\right)^{n}

The Taylor series expansion for
\exp\left(\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)
gives

\begin{split}\exp\left(\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)=&1+\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}+\frac{1}{2!}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{2}+\\
&\frac{1}{3!}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{3}+\frac{1}{4!}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{4}e^{\psi(n)},\end{split}

where
\psi(n)
is a number between 0 and
\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)t
, and
\psi(n)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty.

Now substituting this equation in the last expression for
M_{Z}(t)
, we have

\begin{split}M_{Z}(t)&=\Biggl(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p^{k}q^{s-k}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)p^{k}q^{s-k}\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)+\frac{1}{2!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{3!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{3}p^{k}q^{s-k}\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{3}+\frac{1}{4!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{4}p^{k}q^{s-k}\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{4}e^{\psi(n)}\Biggr)^{n},\end{split}

(44)

where
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p^{k}q^{s-k}=1
,
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)p^{k}q^{s-k}=0
as

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}kp^{k}q^{s-k}-\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p^{k}q^{s-k}\right)\frac{E(X)}{n}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}kp^{k}q^{s-k}-\frac{E(X)}{n}=\frac{E(X)}{n}-\frac{E(X)}{n}=0
,

and
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}=\,\tfrac{1}{n}
as
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}=\frac{\sigma_{s}^{2}}{n}
, we have

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k^{2}-2k\frac{E(X)}{n}+\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\right)p^{k}q^{s-k}\\
&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}k^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}-2\frac{E(X)}{n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}kp^{k}q^{s-k}+\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p^{k}q^{s-k}\\
&=\frac{E(X^{2})}{n}-2\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\\
&=\frac{E(X^{2})}{n}-\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\\
&=\frac{\sigma_{s}^{2}}{n}.\end{split}

By taking
\sigma_{s}^{2}=n\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}k^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}-\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\right)

Now, substituting these equations in the last expression for
M_{Z}(t)
in (
44
), we have

\begin{split}M_{Z}(t)&=\Biggl(1+\frac{t^{2}}{2n}+\frac{1}{3!}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{3}p^{k}q^{s-k}}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}k^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}-\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)t^{3}\\
&+\frac{1}{4!}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{4}p^{k}q^{s-k}}{n^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}k^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}-\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}}\right)t^{4}e^{\psi(n)}\Biggr)^{n}\end{split}

The above equation may be written as

M_{Z}(t)=\Biggl(1+\frac{t^{2}}{2n}+\frac{\Psi(n)}{n}\Biggr)^{n},

where

\begin{split}\Psi(n)&=\frac{1}{3!}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{3}p^{k}q^{s-k}}{\sqrt{n}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}k^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}-\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)t^{3}\\
&+\frac{1}{4!}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(k-\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{4}p^{k}q^{s-k}}{n\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}k^{2}p^{k}q^{s-k}-\left(\frac{E(X)}{n}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}}\right)t^{4}e^{\psi(n)}.\end{split}

Since
\psi(n)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, it follows that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi(n)=0
for every fixed value of
t
. Thus,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}M_{Z}(t)=e^{t^{2}/2},\text{ where }Z\rightsquigarrow N(0,1),

for all real values of
t
.
∎

We can conclude that, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, the random variable
Z=\frac{X-E(X)}{\sigma_{s}}
has the standard normal as its limiting distribution; or equivalently, that the bi
s
nomial random variable
X
has, for large
n
, an approximate normal distribution with mean
\mu_{s}=np\frac{(s+1)p^{s}-1}{(p-q)}
and variance
\sigma_{s}^{2}=npq\frac{1-(s+1)^{2}(pq)^{s}}{(p-q)^{2}}.
