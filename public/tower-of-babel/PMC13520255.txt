Autoimmune encephalitis induced by atezolizumab and bevacizumab therapy for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma: a case report
Authors: Yoriko Nomura, Yoshiyuki Wada, Shin Sasaki, Takahiro Ikeda, Tomoki Ryu, Yuko Takami
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAtezolizumab, an immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI), became available for treating hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in 2020. It is now widely used in combination with bevacizumab for advanced HCC. However, ICI has been reported to cause severe immune-related adverse events (irAEs), and reports in the treatment of HCC are still very rare. We report a case of autoimmune encephalitis as an irAE in a patient undergoing ICI therapy for HCC.Case presentationA 66-year-old Japanese man was diagnosed with multiple HCC in the parenchyma of both liver lobes. We initiated combination treatment with atezolizumab and bevacizumab. On day 8, the patient had symptoms of fever, diarrhea, and loss of appetite and was transferred to our hospital due to impaired consciousness on day 10. He was restless and incoherent. Analysis of cerebrospinal fluid revealed an elevated monocyte count. The patient was diagnosed with autoimmune encephalitis due to ICI and started treatment with methylprednisolone 1,000 mg for 3 days. On the second day of treatment, his consciousness improved dramatically, and he was discharged on day 13.ConclusionsThe frequency of ICI therapy will increase; therefore, it is necessary to understand the rare irAEs due to ICIs. Moreover, prompt diagnosis and early administration of steroids may contribute to a successful response.

Background

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have been used for advanced malignant tumors as an effective treatment. Atezolizumab is an anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) monoclonal antibody ICI that selectively inhibits PD-L1 interaction with the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) [1]. Atezolizumab combined with bevacizumab has shown efficacy compared to sorafenib (SOR) in a prospective randomized controlled trial with SOR as the first-line treatment for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The ICI combination treatment became available for the management of advanced HCC on September 25, 2020, in Japan. However, studies have shown the negative side effects of ICIs therapy, particularly immune-related adverse events (irAEs).

Malignant cells prevent attacks from activated T-cell mediated immunity through inhibitory signals from PD-L1 and PD-L2, which interact with PD1 expressed on activated T cells [1]. Immunotherapy targeting these ligands has been proven effective in treating advanced malignancies. ICIs can cause irAEs by activating effector T cells, recognizing autoantigens [2], and generating an exaggerated inflammatory response [1]. However, the incidence of irAEs caused by atezolizumab is believed to be lower than other ICIs [3, 4]. Potential risk factors for developing irAE have not yet been identified [2]. In addition, it may present with a variety of clinical symptoms, making diagnosis difficult. Therefore, it is important to understand and recognize irAEs as the indications for ICIs therapy is expanding clinically. We report a case of autoimmune encephalitis induced by atezolizumab and bevacizumab combination therapy for unresectable HCC, which could be diagnosed early with the intervention and cooperation of various specialized departments.

Case presentation

A 66-year-old Japanese man visited a nearby doctor for abdominal distension and was then referred to our hospital after being diagnosed with multiple HCCs in the parenchyma of both lobes of the liver (Fig. 1a-b). The patient had a history of heavy alcohol use and chronic hepatitis C diagnosed at age 30, which was left untreated. There was no other past history of disease. Mononuclear cells were predominant (106/μL) in the CSF, but polynuclear cells were also found (84/μL); therefore, a bacterial infection could not be ruled out. Clinical course

After being admitted, he was treated with vitamins and supplemented with a sodium solution to see whether his condition would improve until the cause of impaired consciousness was diagnosed. Based on the clinical course of symptoms and CSF findings, we suspected that the patient showed signs of ICI-induced autoimmune encephalitis. We started steroid pulse therapy with 1,000 mg of methylprednisolone for three days. Until the test results were obtained, bacterial and herpes encephalitis could not be ruled out; therefore, we administered Ceftriaxone (CTRX) 4 g/day and acyclovir 9.25 mg/Kg × 3/day in combination. The first dose was administered at night, and the following morning his symptoms improved immediately. Based on the examination submitted for the differential diagnosis, no other disease was suspected. It was considered consistent with the diagnosis of irAE (Table 4); therefore, CTRX and acyclovir were discontinued. After three days of steroid pulse therapy, oral administration of prednisolone was not ordered as tapering. After receiving steroid pulse therapy, the patient had no fever, regained his appetite, and had normal cognitive functions. In addition, colon fiber test was performed to rule out ICI-induced colitis since the patient had diarrhea, and no ICI-induced colitis was confirmed. Discussion and conclusions

Immunotherapy with monoclonal antibodies targeting cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen 4 (CTLA4) and PD-1 has been examined in patients with advanced HCC. The phase I, II, and III studies showed signs of high effectiveness [5]. These ICIs enhance the anti-tumor immune responses by blocking the signaling from receptors that exert negative regulatory effects on T-cell activation [6]. PD-L1 is regularly expressed on tumor cells, antigen-presenting cells, and non-hematopoietic cells, such as hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, skeletal muscle cells, and vascular endothelial cells. By activating effector T cells and recognizing autoantigens, ICIs can cause irAEs, such as inflammation in the gastrointestinal tract, endocrine glands, skin, lung, liver, and nervous system. This is thought to be a mechanism for developing autoimmune side effects after administration of anti-PD-L1 antibody; however, the mechanism has to be further studied [2, 7–12]. Therefore, with an increasing patient population opting to take ICI therapy, more cases of irAE will occur as a side effect.

Generally, the most frequently reported symptoms of irAE are fatigue, asthenia, and decreased appetite. As a rare case of irAE, encephalitis of any grade assessed using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 6.0 has been reported to occur in 0.1–0.2% of patients treated with ICIs [6, 13–17]. Encephalitis occurs acutely or sub-acutely with the onset of progressive disorientation and loss of cognitive function, accompanied by various symptoms such as convulsions, psychiatric symptoms, and memory impairment. It has been reported that irAE can develop within 6 to 12 weeks after the administration of ICIs, but it could develop as early as 1 week after the onset of therapy [17]. There are reports that the time from ICI administration to the onset of irAE correlates with the response rate. However, this remains controversial [17, 18].

As in our case, the patient developed fever and malaise on the 8th day after ICI introduction, and on the 10th day, he developed consciousness disorder, which is a typical onset in retrospect, but the cause was unknown at first. The onset of irAE by ICI was relatively early, but the progression of the condition did not stop thereafter, and he died 126 days later.

We encountered a case of autoimmune encephalitis following a combination treatment of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab for advanced HCC. The examination of the CSF was very important for the correct diagnosis, and the immediate steroid pulse therapy for autoimmune encephalitis was dramatically effective.

Since there are only a few cases of irAE due to ICIs reported, especially with HCCs, it is essential to conduct future research urgently. Patients suspected of having encephalitis as an ICI-related irAE should immediately consult a neurologist until further research and widespread recognition. Clinical professionals who administer ICI need to know how to treat irAE and be prepared for cross-sectional interventions by a large number of specialized departments.
