Introduction

For each smooth sextic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}

The techniques of this work serve to settle the above case of the generalized Hodge conjecture in the Waring-rank-3 case. This case represents a projective 8-dimensional family of smooth curves

We discuss a few further aspects of the special case of the generalized Hodge conjecture treated by this work that make it especially attractive. Typical cases of the (generalized) Hodge conjecture present the difficulty whereby no purely algebraic criterion—i.e., other than that furnished tautologically by the Hodge conjecture itself—serves to identify, within the set of all complex cohomology classes, those whose vanishing the conjecture predicts. In other words, it’s hard to know which classes one must kill, if he is to establish the conjecture, in each given case.
While the Hodge filtration
F^{p}H^{k}(X,\mathbb{C})=\operatorname{im}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{H}^{k}(X,\Omega^{\geq p}_{X})\to\mathbb{H}^{k}(X,\Omega^{\bullet}_{X})}}\right)
on
H^{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
admits a purely algebraic description, the underlying Betti
\mathbb{Q}
-structure on that space doesn’t; in particular, one can’t in general detect purely algebraically which De Rham classes lie in the complexification of the rational Hodge substructure at issue.

In the case treated by this work, on the other hand, the Hodge structure whose vanishing is at stake—or rather, its complexification—can be characterized in purely algebraic terms (i.e., as that consisting of the involution-invariant algebraic De Rham classes). This fact makes Griffiths’s explicit description extremely effective in our setting. Indeed, our refinement of that description, together with the results of our Subsection
2.4
, serves to establish not just the sufficiency of the algebraic partial differential equation (
1
) (which seems more-or-less standard), but further its necessity.

Moreover, the target (
1
) of this work’s reductions can be attacked computationally, as we now explain. For each
\sigma
-invariant sextic
F
and each
\sigma
-anti-invariant homogeneous polynomial
A
of the right degree, each
candidate
denominator
D
—homogeneous, as well as involution-invariant, say—serves to induce a finite-dimensional space of solutions
v=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{5})
, itself consisting of those vector fields of the shape
v=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{A_{0}}{D},\ldots,\frac{A_{5}}{D}}}\right)
, for numerators
A_{i}
again homogeneous and of the right degree (we explain this reformulation further in Subsection
2.4
). The equation (
1
) yields a linear system on the coefficients of these numerators, whose consistency can be checked in finite time (at least in principle). The problem, then, is to find denominators
D
that cause “clever” cancellations to take place in (
1
). (This latter problem, of course, algebraically distills the very cycle-theoretic statement at issue.) While we’ve been able to achieve just this for
F
the Fermat sextic, and for a family of sextics closely related to that one, doing as much for general
F
seems difficult. This work is the first of which we’re aware that manages to reduce a case of the generalized Hodge conjecture to a further problem that’s equivalent
and
is amenable to computer-aided search.

Indeed, it is not for lack of effort that we have failed—thus far—to solve (
1
) for, say, a nonempty Zariski-open subset’s worth of
\sigma
-invariant sextics
F(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2},V_{0},V_{1},V_{2})
(this latter desideratum captures the full “challenging” question asked by Voisin
[
14
]
). Our inability—despite its concreteness and simplicity—to solve that equation for a broader class of sextics
F
reflects, presumably, the great difficulty with which algebraic cycles are inevitably produced. We suggest the equation (
1
) as a point of departure for future efforts (see also Question
3.1
below).

Effective Griffiths–Lewis

In this section, we develop an effective variant of Griffiths’s characterization
[
5
]
of the primitive cohomology of hypersurfaces. We fix a smooth hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree
d
and a pair of nonnegative integers
p
and
q
for which
p+q=n-1
. Our goal is to make effectively decidable, for each class in
H^{n-1}(X,\mathbb{C})_{\text{prim}}
, the matter of that class’s vanishing outside of some closed algebraic subset
Y\subset X
, itself given as
Y=V\cap X
, say, for some hypersurface
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
. We write
U\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus V
.

Using the degeneration at
E_{1}
of the spectral sequence associated to the Hodge filtration on the logarithmic complex
\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log X)
—itself a deep result, due to Deligne (see
[
12
, Thm. 8.35 (b)]
)—Voisin
[
13
, Thm. 6.5]
proves a modern form of Griffiths’s result, whereby the respective Poincaré residues of the differential
n
-forms
\omega_{A}=\frac{A\cdot\Omega}{F^{q+1}}
, for
A
homogeneous of degree
d\cdot(q+1)-(n+1)
, exhaust
F^{p}H^{n-1}(X,\mathbb{C})_{\text{prim}}
. Here,
\Omega\coloneqq\langle\nu,\operatorname{vol}\rangle
is the contraction of the volume form on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
by the Euler vector field
\nu\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{n}Z_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}}
. Voisin’s variant implies that the cohomology class corresponding to the residue of
\omega_{A}
dies on
X\cap U
if and only if there exists a holomorphic
n-1
-form
\beta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U\setminus X,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}}\right)
such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\beta
extends holomorphically across
X
.
A priori
, the pole order of
\beta
along
X
could be arbitrarily large. In fact,
\beta
could have essential singularities both along
X
and along the boundary of
U
, a pathology that the Grothendieck comparison theorem apparently serves only partially to rule out (we discuss this phenomenon below).

In this section, we clarify the pole order of
\beta
along
X
. In fact, we show (see Theorem
2.6
below) that the class of
\operatorname{Res}_{X}(\omega_{A})
vanishes on
X\cap U
if and only if there exists a
\beta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U\setminus X,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}}\right)
such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\beta
is holomorphic and
\beta
’s pole order along
X
is at most
q
; that is, we can freely assume
\beta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
. Moreover, we prove as much without assuming Deligne’s theorem; in fact, our technique implies a very weak form of that theorem (see Remark
2.4
). In Theorem
2.12
, we show that
\beta
can moreover be assumed algebraic.

We now sketch our approach, which adapts Lewis’s treatment
[
8
, Lec. 9]
. Lewis shows that
\text{Prim}^{p,q}(X)
, viewed as a subspace of
H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X})
(see
[
8
, (9.2)]
), is exhausted by a certain multistep procedure which, beginning with the homogeneous polynomial
A\in R^{d\cdot(q+1)-(n+1)}
, first attaches the
n
-form
\omega_{A}\coloneqq\frac{A\cdot\Omega}{F^{q+1}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, before applying to
\omega_{A}
a sequence of connecting homomorphisms (see
[
8
, 9.31. Prop.]
), and finally taking the result’s residue.

Lewis’s approach, while simple and elementary, yields just the graded pieces
\text{Prim}^{p,q}(X)
of
H^{n-1}(X,\mathbb{C})_{\text{prim}}
with respect to the Hodge filtration. We begin, in Subsection
2.2
, by producing Čech hypercohomology classes that respectively lift these graded pieces (see Lemma
2.2
). We produce these total Čech cocycles by the aid of a sort of iterative correction; that is, beginning with the cocycle
(\omega_{A},0,\ldots,0)
, we iteratively produce offsets—themselves obtained by pole-order reduction—that serve to reduce that cocycle’s worst-case pole order, while keeping intact its closedness (see Construction
2.1
). Adapting Lewis’s
[

[

[

[
4

[

, the cohomology of this total complex satisfies

and

(2)

2.2
Producing Total Cocycles

Lewis’s approach, given
A\in R^{d\cdot(q+1)-(n+1)}
, first defines the form
\omega_{A}\coloneqq\frac{A\cdot\Omega}{F^{q+1}}
. It applies to
\omega_{A}
a sequence of connecting homomorphisms, so producing a sequence of elements
\omega_{A}^{(q+1-\ell)}\in H^{q-\ell+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega^{p+\ell}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\ell X)}}\right)
, say, for
\ell\in\{q+1,\ldots,1\}
(i.e., working downwards). At the end, Lewis’s procedure applies to
\omega_{A}^{(q)}\in H^{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega^{p+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log X)}}\right)
the map
\operatorname{Res}_{X}
induced by the Poincaré residue
\Omega^{p+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\to\Omega^{p}_{X}
, and so obtains an element of
\text{Prim}^{p,q}(X)\subset H^{q}(X,\Omega^{p}_{X})
.

In this subsection, we introduce a lifting technique that mimics Lewis’s, but more closely keeps track of the intermediate data that that technique produces, and so outputs a
total
Čech cocycle, as opposed to a graded element.

We sketch our construction’s basic idea. We begin with the total cocycle consisting
just
of
\omega_{A}
in its top-left cell. That total cocycle is, of course,
not
valued in the logarithmic complex
\check{C}(\mathscr{U},\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log X))
(
\omega_{A}
’s pole order along
X
is
q+1
). For
q
iterations, we “correct” the total cycle so far constructed by adding to it a totally-closed offset. That offset causes the standing cocycle’s bottom-right-most item’s poles to become logarithmic, at the cost of spawning a
new
bottom-right-most item, whose pole order, on the other hand, is one lower than the standing cocycle’s bottom-right item’s had been. After repeating this procedure
q
times, we obtain an honest logarithmic total cocycle, to which we may apply the Poincaré residue componentwise. The
(p+1,q)
-indexed component of the final cocycle we obtain in this way coincides, as a cohomology class in
H^{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega^{p+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log X)}}\right)
, with Lewis’s element
\omega_{A}^{(q)}
above (up to a sign discrepancy).

In order to keep this section typographically manageable, we introduce a few notational devices. We suppress the braces, as well as the lower indices, attached to the various Čech cochains we use. In particular, we write each Čech cochain as a single symbol, together with a parenthesized superscript that indicates that cochain’s arity. For example, our
\omega^{(q+1-\ell)}
below is a Čech
q+1-\ell
-cochain, an element of
\check{C}^{q-\ell+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell}(\ell X)}}\right)
.

We begin with our main construction.

(Construction of Total Cocycle)
.

Input.
A smooth hypersurface

, a pair
p+q=n-1
, and a homogeneous
A\in R^{d\cdot(q+1)-(n+1)}
.

Output.
A total cocycle
s=(s^{(0)},\ldots,s^{(q)},0,\ldots,0)
in

.

•

Set
\omega_{A}\coloneqq\frac{A\cdot\Omega}{F^{q+1}}
and initialize the Čech 0-cycle
\omega^{(0)}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U_{i}}}}\right\}_{i}
in
\check{C}^{0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{n}((q+1)X)}}\right)
.

•

for

\ell\in\{q,\ldots,1\}

do
:

–

Initialize
\alpha^{(q-\ell)}_{\ell+1}\coloneqq 0
.

–

for

h\in\{\ell,\ldots,1\}

do
:

*

By induction on
\ell
(see below),
\partial\omega^{(q-\ell)}\in\check{C}^{q-\ell}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\Omega^{p+\ell+2}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log X)}}\right)
has logarithmic poles (and so
a fortiori
order-
h+1
poles). Moreover, by induction on

,
\omega^{(q-\ell)}-\partial\alpha^{(q-\ell)}_{h+1}\in\check{C}^{q-\ell}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell+1}((h+1)X)}}\right)
has order-
h+1
poles at most.
Thus, by the exact sequence

\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell}(hX)\xlongrightarrow{\partial}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell+1}((h+1)X)\mathbin{/}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell+1}(hX)\xlongrightarrow{\partial}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell+2}((h+2)X)\mathbin{/}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell+2}((h+1)X),

we can lift
\omega^{(q-\ell)}-\partial\alpha^{(q-\ell)}_{h+1}
to an element
\gamma^{(q-\ell)}_{h}\in\check{C}^{q-\ell}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(hX}}\right)}}\right)

such that

\alpha^{(q-\ell)}_{h}\coloneqq\alpha^{(q-\ell)}_{h+1}+\gamma^{(q-\ell)}_{h}
.

–

Define
\vartheta^{(q-\ell)}\coloneqq\alpha^{(q-\ell)}_{1}
in
\check{C}^{q-\ell}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell}(\ell X)}}\right)
.

–

Further, define
\omega^{(q-\ell+1)}\coloneqq(-1)^{q-\ell+1}\cdot\delta\vartheta^{(q-\ell)}
in
\check{C}^{q-\ell+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+\ell}(\ell X)}}\right)
.

•

Output the total cochain
\big(\omega^{(0)}-\partial\vartheta^{(0)},\ldots,\omega^{(q-1)}-\partial\vartheta^{(q-1)},\omega^{(q)},0,\ldots,0\big)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The total cochain
s=(s^{(0)},\ldots,s^{(q)},0,\ldots,0)
output by Construction
2.1
takes values in the log complex
\check{C}(\mathscr{U},\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log X))

in
\check{H}^{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{p+1}(\log X)}}\right)
is
(-1)^{\binom{q+1}{2}}
times the chained image of
\omega^{(0)}
under the sequence of boundary morphisms
\check{H}^{0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\ker\partial_{q+1}}}\right)\cong\cdots\cong\check{H}^{q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\ker\partial_{1}}}\right)
.

Proof.

We prove by induction that for each
\ell\in\{q+1,\ldots,1\}
, the Čech cochains
\omega^{(0)},\ldots,\omega^{(q-\ell+1)}
and

satisfy the following invariants.

1.

is
\delta
-closed.

2.

has logarithmic poles.

3.

\omega^{(q-\ell+1)}
defines a class in
\check{H}^{q-\ell+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\ker\partial_{\ell}}}\right)

(-1)^{\binom{q-\ell+2}{2}}
times the chained image of
\omega^{(0)}
under the sequence of boundary morphisms
\check{H}^{0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\ker\partial_{q+1}}}\right)\cong\cdots\cong\check{H}^{q-\ell+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U},\ker\partial_{\ell}}}\right)
.

4.

The total cochain
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\omega^{(0)}-\partial\vartheta^{(0)},\ldots,\omega^{(q-\ell)}-\partial\vartheta^{(q-\ell)},\omega^{(q-\ell+1)},0,\ldots,0}}\right)

\ell

We note that

is exploited in an essential way

These items granted, the conclusion of the lemma essentially is immediate. Indeed,

8
, 9.21. Prop.-Def.]

In the base case
\ell=q+1
, the inductive invariants above trivially hold by inspection. We fix an index
\ell\in\{q,\ldots,1\}
; i.e., we proceed by
downward
induction (i.e., in the same order as Construction
2.1
). Item
1
above is immediate, since

The output

of Construction

has simple poles. Moreover, that quantity’s holomorphic differential is
\partial\omega^{(q-\ell)}
, which has logarithmic poles (by the inductive case of
2
). We conclude that
\omega^{(q-\ell)}-\partial\vartheta^{(q-\ell)}
has logarithmic poles. Since

We note that

Since

.

The cocycles
s

2.3
The Vanishing Criterion

In the theorem below, we isolate a simple criterion that detects the exactness (up to holomorphic error) of a total cochain
s
output by Construction
2.1
on a principal open set
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
. We abbreviate
C_{U}\coloneqq\check{C}(\mathscr{U}\cap U,\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log X))
for the total complex attached to the logarithmic complex on
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}

We recall the

valid for each

(see
[

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\operatorname{Res}_{X}(s)}}\right|_{U}

if and only if there exists a total primitive
\tilde{s}=(\tilde{s}^{(0)},\ldots,\tilde{s}^{(n-1)})

such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.s}}\right|_{U}-d\tilde{s}
is componentwise-holomorphic (i.e., takes values in
\pi_{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\check{C}(\mathscr{U}\cap U,\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})}}\right)
).

For typographical convenience, we decline below to typographically indicate the fact of cochains’ having been restricted to
U
; that is, we identify the cochains
\omega^{(0)},\ldots,\omega^{(q)}
and
\vartheta^{(0)},\ldots,\vartheta^{(q-1)}
with their respective restrictions to

throughout this subsection. (These cochains are defined with respect to the cover
\mathscr{U}\cap U\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{U_{i}\cap U}}\right\}_{i}

We fix

,

, and

Theorem 2.6
.

There exists a total cochain
\tilde{s}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tilde{s}_{0},\ldots,\tilde{s}_{n-1}}}\right)

We take the theorem’s two implications one at a time.

Sufficiency.

The sufficiency of the condition of the theorem is established by the following construction, as well as by the lemma that follows it.

Lemma 2.10

\beta

We begin with Construction
2.9
’s first loop. We argue that, for each
\ell\in\{n,\ldots,0\}

1.

\psi^{(\ell)}
is
\partial
-closed.

\delta
-closed.

In the base case
\ell=n

a quantity whose summands are holomorphic (by our hypothesis on

. As for that quantity’s Čech-closedness, we note that

\varsigma^{(0)}+\xi^{(0)}-\tau^{(0)}\in\check{C}^{0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{U}\cap U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
is Čech-closed, and so represents a well-defined section
\beta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
. Finally,

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\beta

\displaystyle=\omega^{(0)}-\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varsigma^{(0)}+\xi^{(0)}-\tau^{(0)}}}\right)

(by definition of
\beta
.)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\omega^{(0)}-\partial\vartheta^{(0)}}}\right)-\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varsigma^{(0)}-\vartheta^{(0)}+\xi^{(0)}-\tau^{(0)}}}\right)

(add and subtract
\partial\vartheta^{(0)}
.)

\displaystyle=s^{(0)}-\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tilde{s}^{(0)}+\xi^{(0)}-\tau^{(0)}}}\right)

(by the definitions of
s
and
\tilde{s}
.)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s^{(0)}-\partial\tilde{s}^{(0)}}}\right)}_{\text{holomorphic}}-\underbrace{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial\xi^{(0)}}}\right)}_{\mathclap{\text{holomorphic}}}.

(using the
\partial
-closedness of
\tau^{(0)}
.)

The left-hand summand above is holomorphic by our hypothesis on
\tilde{s}
; the right-hand summand is holomorphic by construction. This proves that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\beta
is holomorphic, and so completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The proof of Lemma
2.10
completes the proof of the theorem.

2.4
Algebraic Differential Forms

In this subsection, we discuss algebraic (i.e., rational) differential forms. First, we rederive a formula stated by Griffiths (see Lemma
2.11
below). After that, using the Grothendieck comparison theorem, we prove that the differential form
\beta
of Theorem
2.6
may be freely assumed algebraic (see Theorem
2.12
). We follow the notation of Lewis
[
8
, Lec. 9]
. We work in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, with volume form
\operatorname{vol}\coloneqq\operatorname{vol}_{n+1}=dZ_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge dZ_{n}
. We write
\nu\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{n}Z_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}}
for the
Euler
vector field on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
and
\Omega\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}dZ_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{dZ_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dZ_{n}
.

By
[
8
, 9.17. Cor.]
, each rational
n
-form on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
admits an expression of the form
\frac{A\cdot\Omega}{B}
, where
A
and
B
are homogeneous and
\deg B=n+1+\deg A
. Moreover, for
V\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{A_{0}}{B},\ldots,\frac{A_{n}}{B}}}\right)
an arbitrary tuple of homogeneous rational functions in
n+1
variables, each of degree
-n
, the expression

\eta\coloneqq\frac{1}{B}\cdot\sum_{i<j}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Z_{i}\cdot A_{j}-A_{i}\cdot Z_{j}}}\right)\cdot\Omega_{ij},

(3)

where
\Omega_{ij}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{j}},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}},\operatorname{vol}}}\right>}}\right>
, represents a well-defined
n-1
-form on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and conversely every rational
n-1
-form on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
arises in this way.

We now fix a smooth hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree
d
and a pair
p+q=n-1
. If the pole order of
\eta
above along
X
is at most
q
, say, then we can further write
B=F^{q}\cdot D
, as well as
V=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{v_{0}}{F^{q}},\ldots,\frac{v_{n}}{F^{q}}}}\right)
, where, for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
,
v_{i}\coloneqq\frac{A_{i}}{D}
is
itself
a rational function, of total degree
d\cdot q-n
. We further define
v\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}}}\right)
, so that
V=\frac{v}{F^{q}}
. We identify
v=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n})
with
v\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{n}v_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}}
, and also write
\operatorname{div}(v)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial Z_{i}}
for
v
’s
divergence
.

We note first that, by manipulating the expression (
3
), we may reëxpress
F^{q}\cdot\eta=\sum_{i<j}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Z_{i}\cdot v_{j}-v_{i}\cdot Z_{j}}}\right)\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{j}},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}},\operatorname{vol}}}\right>}}\right>=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\sum_{i=0}^{n}v_{i}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}},\operatorname{vol}}}\right>}}\right>=-\sum_{i=0}^{n}v_{i}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}},\Omega}}\right>=-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\Omega}}\right>
. The first equality is essentially
[
8
, 9.19. Ex]
; to obtain the second, we interchange the first expression’s contractions by
\nu
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}}
(a similar expression appears within the proof of
[
13
, Lem. 6.11]
). Moreover, we have:

Lemma 2.11

(Griffiths
[
5
, (4.5)]
)
.

\partial\eta=\frac{\Omega}{F^{q+1}}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(q\cdot dF(v)-F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v)}}\right)
.

Proof.

Indeed, we calculate:

\displaystyle\partial\eta

\displaystyle=\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\Omega}}\right>}{F^{q}}}}\right)

(just argued.)

\displaystyle=\frac{q\cdot dF\wedge\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\Omega}}\right>}{F^{q+1}}-\frac{\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\Omega}}\right>}{F^{q}}

(by Leibniz’s rule.)

\displaystyle=\frac{q\cdot dF(v)\cdot\Omega}{F^{q+1}}-\frac{q\cdot d\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>}{F^{q}}-\frac{\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\Omega}}\right>}{F^{q}}

(by Cartan’s formula; see below.)

\displaystyle=\frac{q\cdot dF(v)\cdot\Omega}{F^{q+1}}-\frac{q\cdot d\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>}{F^{q}}-\frac{\operatorname{div}(v)\cdot\Omega}{F^{q}}+\frac{q\cdot d\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>}{F^{q}}

(by Cartan’s formula again; see below.)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega}{F^{q+1}}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(q\cdot dF(v)-F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v)}}\right).

(cancel terms.)

To achieve the third equality, we use Cartan’s formula twice. To wit,
dF\wedge\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\Omega}}\right>=dF(v)\cdot\Omega-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,dF\wedge\Omega}}\right>=dF(v)\cdot\Omega-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,dF(\nu)\cdot\operatorname{vol}-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,dF\wedge\operatorname{vol}}}\right>}}\right>=dF(v)\cdot\Omega-d\cdot F\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>
; in the last step, we use Euler’s formula to the effect that
dF(\nu)=d\cdot F
. To achieve the second-to-last equality, we use two further variants of Cartan’s formula, now pertaining to the Lie derivative
\mathscr{L}_{v}
along
v
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\Omega}}\right>

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}_{v}\Omega-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\partial\Omega}}\right>

(using Cartan’s formula
\mathscr{L}_{v}\Omega=\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\Omega}}\right>+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\partial\Omega}}\right>
.)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}_{v}\Omega-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,(n+1)\cdot\operatorname{vol}}}\right>

(using
\operatorname{div}(\nu)\cdot\operatorname{vol}=\mathscr{L}_{\nu}\operatorname{vol}=\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\partial\operatorname{vol}}}\right>=\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>
.)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\mathscr{L}_{v}\operatorname{vol}}}\right>+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<[\nu,v],\operatorname{vol}}}\right>-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,(n+1)\cdot\operatorname{vol}}}\right>

(using
\mathscr{L}_{v}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\mathscr{L}_{v}\operatorname{vol}}}\right>+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<[\nu,v],\operatorname{vol}}}\right>
.)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\mathscr{L}_{v}\operatorname{vol}}}\right>+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n+1-q\cdot d}}\right)\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>-(n+1)\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>

(using
[\nu,v]=(1-d\cdot q-n)\cdot v
.)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\operatorname{div}(v)\cdot\operatorname{vol}}}\right>-q\cdot d\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>

(using
\mathscr{L}_{v}\operatorname{vol}=\operatorname{div}(v)\cdot\operatorname{vol}
.)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}(v)\cdot\Omega-q\cdot d\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>;

this calculation completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

For
\omega_{A}=\frac{A\cdot\Omega}{F^{q+1}}
and
\eta
as above, Lemma
2.11
implies that
\omega_{A}-\partial\eta=\frac{\Omega}{F^{q+1}}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A-q\cdot dF(v)+F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v)}}\right)
.
In particular,
\omega_{A}-\partial\eta
is holomorphic if and only if

F^{q+1}\mid A-q\cdot dF(v)+F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v)

(4)

holds in the local ring
\mathbb{C}[Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{n}]_{(F)}
(we recall that
v
’s denominators are coprime to
F
, by fiat).

Below, we assume
q\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
(the case
q=0
is trivial). We
further let
A
be homogeneous of degree
d\cdot(q+1)-(n+1)
, set
\omega_{A}
again as above, and let
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a Zariski-open set for which
X\cap U\neq\varnothing
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Suppose that
\beta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
is such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\beta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}}\right)
. Then up to possibly shrinking
U
, there exists an algebraic differential form
\eta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\eta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}}\right)
.

Proof.

We abbreviate
F_{0}\coloneqq\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z_{0}}
. Up to replacing
U
with
U\cap U_{0}
, where
U_{0}=D(F_{0})
, we may assume that
U\subset U_{0}
, as well as that
U
is affine. We now have the following construction.

CONSTRUCTION 2.13

(Construction of Algebraic Primitive)
.

Input.
A holomorphic differential form
\beta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\beta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}}\right)
.

Output.
An algebraic differential form
\eta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\eta\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}}\right)
.

•

Initialize
\alpha_{q+1}\coloneqq 0
in
\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
.

•

for

h\in\{q,\ldots,1\}

do
:

–

By induction on
h
,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{h+1}
is algebraic, with pole order at most
h+1
along
X
, and so admits an expression of the shape
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{h+1}=\frac{a_{h+1}\cdot\Omega}{F^{h+1}}
, for
a_{h+1}
a homogeneous rational function of degree
d\cdot(h+1)-(n+1)
whose denominator is coprime to
F
.

–

Define
v_{h}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{a_{h+1}}{h\cdot F_{0}},0,\ldots,0}}\right)
and set
\gamma_{h}\coloneqq\frac{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v_{h},\Omega}}\right>}{F^{h}}
. By Lemma
2.11
, this choice yields

\partial\gamma_{h}=\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<v_{h},\Omega}}\right>}{F^{h}}}}\right)=\frac{\Omega}{F^{h+1}}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(h\cdot dF(v_{h})-F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v_{h})}}\right)=\frac{a_{h+1}\cdot\Omega}{F^{h+1}}-\frac{\operatorname{div}(v_{h})\cdot\Omega}{F^{h}},

so that the pole order of
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{h+1}-\partial\gamma_{h}
along
X
is at most
h
. Update
\alpha_{h}\coloneqq\alpha_{h+1}+\gamma_{h}
.

•

Inducting, we see that
\alpha_{1}\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(qX)}}\right)
is algebraic and satisfies
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{1}\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(X)}}\right)
. In particular,
\partial\beta-\partial\alpha_{1}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\omega_{A}|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{1}}}\right)-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\omega_{A}|_{U}-\partial\beta}}\right)
has simple (equivalently, logarithmic) poles.

•

Initialize
\mu_{q}\coloneqq 0
in
\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-2}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}((q-1)X)}}\right)
.

•

for

h\in\{q-1,\ldots,1\}

do
:

–

By induction on
h
,
\beta-\alpha_{1}-\partial\mu_{h+1}\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}((h+1)X)}}\right)
; moreover, its differential
\partial\beta-\partial\alpha_{1}
has logarithmic poles, by the above. Finally,
U
is affine. Thus the specialization to
U
of

\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{n-2}(hX)\xlongrightarrow{\partial}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{n-1}((h+1)X)\mathbin{/}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{n-1}(hX)\xlongrightarrow{\partial}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{n}((h+2)X)\mathbin{/}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{n}((h+1)X)

is exact, and we can lift
\beta-\alpha_{1}-\partial\mu_{h+1}
to an element
\chi_{h}\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-2}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(hX)}}\right)
, say—
not
in general algebraic—such that
\beta-\alpha_{1}-\partial\mu_{h+1}-\partial\chi_{h}
has order-
h
poles. Update
\mu_{h}\coloneqq\mu_{h+1}+\chi_{h}
.

•

Now
\operatorname{Res}_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{1}}}\right)=\operatorname{Res}_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial\beta-\partial\alpha_{1}}}\right)=\partial\operatorname{Res}_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\beta-\alpha_{1}-\partial\mu_{1}}}\right)
;
to obtain the first equality, we use
\operatorname{Res}_{X}(\omega_{A}|_{U}-\partial\beta)=0
.
We conclude that
\operatorname{Res}_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{1}}}\right)
is algebraic and exact. By the Grothendieck comparison theorem (see André, Baldassarri, and Cailotto
[
1
, Cor. 31.1.2]
),
there exists an algebraic
\kappa\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X\cap U,\Omega^{n-2}_{X}}}\right)
such that
\partial\kappa=\operatorname{Res}_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{1}}}\right)
. By the surjectivity of the residue,
we may find some algebraic
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U,\Omega^{n-1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(\log X)}}\right)
such that
\operatorname{Res}_{X}(\gamma_{0})=\kappa
. Setting
\alpha_{0}\coloneqq\alpha_{1}+\gamma_{0}
, we obtain that
\operatorname{Res}_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{0}}}\right)=0
,
so that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\omega_{A}}}\right|_{U}-\partial\alpha_{0}
is holomorphic.

•

Output
\eta\coloneqq\alpha_{0}
.

3
Producing Explicit Primitives

In this section, and for the rest of the paper, we turn our attention to the algebraic partial differential equation (
4
). We fix
n=5
and
d=6
for good. We work in coordinates
(Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{5})=(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2},V_{0},V_{1},V_{2})
equipped with the
block swap
involution

\sigma:(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2},V_{0},V_{1},V_{2})\mapsto(V_{0},V_{1},V_{2},U_{0},U_{1},U_{2}).

As we have explained, Voisin
[
14
]
has asked about a case of the generalized Hodge conjecture pertaining to smooth sextics
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
invariant under the involution

\iota:(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2},Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2})\mapsto i\cdot(Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2},-X_{0},-X_{1},-X_{2}).

Up to the projective linear change of coordinates

p:(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2},Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2})\mapsto(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2},i\cdot Y_{0},i\cdot Y_{1},i\cdot Y_{2})\eqqcolon(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2},V_{0},V_{1},V_{2}),

(5)

the involutions
\iota
and
\sigma
identify with each other, so our consideration rather of
\sigma
is no loss.
In fact, Voisin begins rather with a third involution, the
sign flip
involution

\pi:(L_{0},L_{1},L_{2},M_{0},M_{1},M_{2})\to(L_{0},L_{1},L_{2},-M_{0},-M_{1},-M_{2}).

The involutions
\pi
and
\iota
too identify, this time under a further change of coordinates
(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2},Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2})\to(L_{0},L_{1},L_{2},M_{0},M_{1},M_{2})
.
While
\pi
is the simplest and most attractive of the three involutions, the Shioda-type sextics
f(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2})-f(Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2})
do not identify—under the change of coordinates just above—with sextics in
(L_{0},L_{1},L_{2},M_{0},M_{1},M_{2})
of any particularly simple or recognizable shape. On the other hand, under the change of coordinates (
5
), these sextics assume the shape
f(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2})+f(V_{0},V_{1},V_{2})
.
For this reason, we work throughout this section in
(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2},V_{0},V_{1},V_{2})
coordinates, and with the involution
\sigma
.

We fix an arbitrary smooth sextic
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
defined by a
\sigma
-invariant equation
F(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2},V_{0},V_{1},V_{2})
. As Voisin explains, the induced action
\sigma^{*}:H^{4}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{4}(X,\mathbb{C})
acts as
-1
on
H^{4,0}(X)\subset H^{4}(X,\mathbb{C})
. Indeed, it follows from Griffiths’s theory (see again Lewis
[
8
, Lec. 9]
) that the nowhere-vanishing Calabi–Yau
4
-form
\omega\in H^{4,0}(X)
arises as
\operatorname{Res}_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Omega}{F}}}\right)
, where, again,
\Omega\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>
is the contraction of the volume form on
\mathbb{C}^{6}
with the Euler vector field. Since
F
is
\sigma
-invariant by hypothesis and
\operatorname{Res}_{X}
is
\sigma
-equivariant, it suffices to ascertain how
\Omega=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nu,\operatorname{vol}}}\right>
transforms under
\sigma^{*}
. Since, under each linear automorphism
\Sigma:\mathbb{C}^{6}\to\mathbb{C}^{6}
, the Euler vector field
\nu=\sum_{i=0}^{5}Z_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}}
is preserved, we have

\hline\cr I_{3}&\end{array}}}\right]}}\right)=-1

This completes our demonstration that

\sigma
-

In any case, the generalized Hodge conjecture predicts that, for each

By the Lefschetz theorem for Picard groups (see e.g. Lazarsfeld

Theorem 3.3
.

For
F(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2},V_{0},V_{1},V_{2})
the Fermat sextic, each

Proof.

We proceed with the aid of a handful of lemmas.
The following lemma shows that the key line of Construction

Lemma 3.4
.

For each
q\in\{1,2\}
and each term
a=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{5})
such that
a_{i}\in\{0,\ldots,4\}
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,5\}
and
\sum_{i=0}^{5}a_{i}=6\cdot q
, there exists a nonempty proper subset

such that
w_{I}(a)+|I|=6
.

Proof.

The lemma’s statement can easily be checked by brute enumeration. We record a minimal proof. Up to defining
b_{i}\coloneqq a_{i}+1
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,5\}
, we may equivalently ask instead whether each tuple
b=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{5})
of
positive

whose sum is
6\cdot(q+1)
admits a subset sum
\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}=6
, for some index subset
I\subset\{0,\ldots,5\}
(itself necessarily nonempty and proper).

Case
q=1
.

We proceed by case enumeration. If
(b_{0},\ldots,b_{5})
contains a 5, then it necessarily also contains a 1 (or else its sum would be at least
5+5\cdot 2=15>12
); these two elements yield
I
. Similarly, if
\max(b_{0},\ldots,b_{5})=4
, then
b
necessarily contains at least
two
1s (or else its sum would be at least
4+1+4\cdot 2=13>12
); the resulting three elements work. In the case
\max b=3
, if
b
contains a
2
, then it must also contain a
1
(or else its sum would be at least
3+5\cdot 2=13>12
), so that any subset consisting of a
3
, a
2
and a
1
works; otherwise,
b
consists of exactly three
3
s and exactly three
1
s, and any subset consisting of two of the three
3
s suffices. Finally, if
\max b=2
, then we must have
b=(2,2,2,2,2,2)
and any size-3 subset
I
suffices.

Case
q=2
.

We again enumerate cases. If
(b_{0},\ldots,b_{5})
contains both a
5
and a
1
, then we’re done. If
\max b=5
and
b

doesn’t
contain a
1
, then the remaining five items are in
\{2,3,4,5\}
, and further add to

. Each latter such sequence (in fact, there are only three) necessarily contains either
2
s or two
3
s; these suffice for
I
. In the case
\max b\leq 4
, if there are three
2
s, a
4
and a
2
, or two
3
s, then we’re done. If none of these conditions hold, then the counts
c_{j}\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{i\in\{0,\ldots,5\}\nonscript\>\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\>\mathopen{}b_{i}=j}}\right\}}}\right|
are such that

and
c_{2}\leq 2
, as well as that
c_{4}
and
c_{2}
aren’t
both
positive. Finally,
\sum_{i=1}^{4}c_{i}=6
and
\sum_{i=1}^{4}i\cdot c_{i}=18
. Subtracting thrice the first of these two equations from the second, we obtain
c_{4}=2\cdot c_{1}+c_{2}
. If
c_{2}>0
held, then we’d obtain
0=c_{4}=2\cdot c_{1}+c_{2}>0
; we thus conclude

, whence
c_{4}=2\cdot c_{1}
, so that the first sum above yields

. Using

These two cases taken together suffice to establish the lemma.
∎

The following lemma shows that the denominator
\nu_{I}(F)
is allowed.

Lemma 3.5
.

For each nonempty and proper subset
I\subset\{0,\ldots,5\}
,
\nu_{I}(F)\notin(F)
.

Proof.

We write
\operatorname{Lin}X\subset\text{PGL}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
for the group of projective linear automorphisms of
X
. It is classical that this very group is finite (see e.g. Poonen
[
9
, Thm. 1.3]
). We conclude that the Lie algebra
\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Lin}\,X)=0
.

For contradiction, we assume that
I\subset\{0,\ldots,5\}
, nonempty and proper, is such that
\nu_{I}(F)\in(F)
. For degree reasons, we necessarily have
\nu_{I}(F)=c\cdot F
for some constant
c
. We denote by
(\varepsilon_{0},\ldots,\varepsilon_{5})\coloneqq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbf{1}_{I}(0),\ldots,\mathbf{1}_{I}(5)}}\right)
the membership indicator functions of
I
. The diagonal matrix
A_{I}\coloneqq\text{diag}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon_{0},\ldots,\varepsilon_{5}}}\right)
in
\mathfrak{gl}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
yields the one-parameter subgroup
g_{t}\coloneqq\operatorname{exp}(t\cdot A_{I})=\operatorname{diag}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(e^{\varepsilon_{0}\cdot t},\ldots,e^{\varepsilon_{5}\cdot t}}}\right)
of
\text{GL}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
. Moreover, writing
F_{t}(Z)\coloneqq F(g_{t}\cdot Z)
, we obtain from the chain rule that

\frac{d}{dt}F_{t}(Z)=\sum_{i=0}^{5}\varepsilon_{i}\cdot e^{\varepsilon_{i}\cdot t}\cdot Z_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z_{i}}(g_{t}\cdot Z)=\sum_{i\in I}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(g_{t}\cdot Z}}\right)_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z_{i}}(g_{t}\cdot Z)=\nu_{I}(F)(g_{t}\cdot Z)=c\cdot F_{t}(Z);

the last equality is our hypothesis on
I
. In particular,
F(g_{t}\cdot Z)=e^{c\cdot t}\cdot F(Z)
holds for each
t
. We see that
g_{t}
preserves the cone in
\mathbb{C}^{6}
over
X
, and hence that
[g_{t}]\in\operatorname{Lin}\,X
for each

.

Equivalently, the class of
A_{I}
in
\mathfrak{pgl}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
is tangent at the identity to a one-parameter subgroup of
\operatorname{Lin}\,X
, and so lies in
\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Lin}\,X)
. On the other hand, this class is nonzero; indeed, since
I
is nonempty and proper,
A_{I}
is not a scalar matrix. This fact contradicts our initial conclusion
\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Lin}\,X)=0
, established above, and so shows that
\nu_{I}(F)\notin(F)
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Proof.

To prove the first of the lemma’s two assertions, we note that

To prove the second, we note first of all the partial Euler relation
\nu_{I}(Z^{a})=\sum_{i\in I}Z_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial Z^{a}}{\partial Z_{i}}=w_{I}(a)\cdot Z^{a}

3.2

In this subsection, we fully solve (

For preliminaries in multivariate division, we refer to von zur Gathen and Gerhard
[
15

. In particular, in the construction below, we fix an

(see

[

We introduce a convenient notational device. For each

T:R^{6\cdot q}\to R^{6\cdot(q-1)}
for the map that, on input

, first multivariate-divides

with respect to
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z_{0}},\ldots,\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z_{5}}}}\right)

In the following lemma, we show that each

-anti-invariant polynomial

Lemma 3.8
.

For each integer
q\geq 0
and each
\sigma
-anti-invariant polynomial

, we have
T^{(q)}(A)=0
.

Proof.

Because each

is a monomial, the action of multivariate division proceeds independently on each monomial of

. We thus first study the case in which
A=Z^{a}=Z^{a_{0}}\cdot\cdots Z^{a_{5}}
is a monomial. In this case, the composed mapping
T:A\mapsto v^{\prime}\mapsto\operatorname{div}(v^{\prime})
operates as follows. If
a_{i}\leq 4
for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,5\}
, then
T:Z^{a}\mapsto(0,\ldots,0)\mapsto 0
; otherwise, writing

, we obtain

T:Z^{a}\mapsto\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(0,\ldots,\frac{Z^{a-5\cdot e_{i(a)}}}{6\cdot q},\ldots,0}}\right)\mapsto\frac{a_{i(a)}-5}{6\cdot q}\cdot Z^{a-6e_{i(a)}}.

Above, we write

for the

Decomposing, for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,5\}
,

, where

, we see that

survives

iterations of that map (and no more).

Now if, for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,5\}
,

holds, then from
6\cdot q=\sum_{i=0}^{5}a_{i}=6\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{5}b_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{5}r_{i}
, we deduce that
\sum_{i=0}^{5}b_{i}<q
, so that the term
Z^{a}
does
not

q
iterations of the map. In other words, the only terms that survive all

, where now
\sum_{i=0}^{5}b_{i}=q
. Moreover, for each such term, the final value attained by that term is a constant, equal to

\lambda(b)\coloneqq\frac{1}{q!}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{5}\underbrace{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{6\cdot b_{i}-5}{6}\cdot\frac{6\cdot b_{i}-11}{6}\cdot\cdots\frac{1}{6}}}\right)}_{\text{$b_{i}$ terms in product}}=\frac{1}{q!}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{5}\prod_{j=0}^{b_{i}-1}\frac{6\cdot j+1}{6}.

This expression is clearly permutation-invariant in the tuple
b=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{5})
. On the other hand, by hypothesis,

, where the terms

constitute a

-orbit, and so

differ by a permutation. Each pair of that form either dies before the

th
iteration, or else yields

; here, we write,
a\eqqcolon 6\cdot b
and
a^{\prime}\eqqcolon 6\cdot b^{\prime}
, and use the permutation-invariance of

. We conclude that

itself dies; this completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

) and
\operatorname{div}(v^{\prime\prime\prime})=0
. Using these facts, together with the expression
v\coloneqq v^{\prime}+F\cdot v^{\prime\prime}+v^{\prime\prime\prime}
and linearity, we obtain

\displaystyle A-q\cdot dF(v)+F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v)

\displaystyle=A-q\cdot\bigl(dF(v^{\prime})+F\cdot dF(v^{\prime\prime})+dF(v^{\prime\prime\prime})\bigr)+F\cdot\bigl(\operatorname{div}(v^{\prime})+F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v^{\prime\prime})+dF(v^{\prime\prime})\bigr)

\displaystyle=F\cdot\bigl(\operatorname{div}(v^{\prime})-(q-1)\cdot dF(v^{\prime\prime})+F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v^{\prime\prime})\bigr)

(using
A=q\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(dF(v^{\prime})+r}}\right)
.)

\displaystyle=F\cdot F^{q}\cdot B^{\prime}

(by the recursive identity above.)

\displaystyle=F^{q+1}\cdot B^{\prime},

a calculation that completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Combining Lemma
3.8
and Theorem
3.10
, we deduce that Construction
3.9
solves (
4
) for each
q\in\{1,2\}
, each
\sigma
-anti-invariant
A\in R^{6\cdot q}
, and
F
the Fermat sextic.

Remark 3.11
.

While it would have been nice to prove Lemma
3.8
by arguing directly that the operation
T:A\mapsto\operatorname{div}(v^{\prime})
preserves anti-invariance, it in fact doesn’t in general. Thus our recursive calls to Construction
3.9
indeed supply to that construction arguments
A
that, in general, are
not
anti-invariant, though they still of course satisfy the requirement put forth by that construction.
At the cost of explicitly anti-symmetrizing
v^{\prime}
and
r
at each level of Construction
3.9
’s recursion, we could have alternatively bypassed Lemma
3.8
entirely, and in fact achieved something like that lemma’s conclusion for
each

\sigma
-invariant
F
. Since Construction
3.2
of course needs
F
to be Fermat, this generalization wouldn’t have done much for us, and in any case our approach above lets us write down a simpler algorithm (that anti-symmetrization would have turned out superfluous in the Fermat case, as Lemma
3.8
shows).

Remark 3.12
.

Lemma
3.8
in fact has nothing to do with
q
, and works for
q\in\{0,\ldots 4\}
arbitrary (or presumably even higher). Theorem
3.10
’s proof uses its hypothesis
q\in\{0,1,2\}
only to guarantee that Construction
3.9
’s calls to Construction
3.2
fulfill the hypothesis of Theorem
3.3
(recall also Remark
3.7
). Theorem
3.10
’s proof otherwise goes through without issue on arbitrary
q\in\{0,\ldots 4\}
. In fact, something like Construction
3.9
—augmented, that is, with the symmetrization step sketched in Remark
3.11
—would work for arbitrary
\sigma
-invariant sextic fourfolds
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
(or even higher-dimensional varieties, in principle), given a suitable replacement for Construction
3.2
.

Remark 3.13
.

Our Construction
3.9
above is related to Bostan, Lairez and Salvy’s
Griffiths–Dwork reduction

[
2
, Alg. 1]
, and can be viewed as an enrichment of that algorithm, albeit specific to our Fermat case (though see Remark
3.12
above). It differs from that algorithm in two important ways. For one, Construction
3.9
’s calls to Construction
3.2
, and indeed that entire latter construction, are new. Further, we prove (i.e., in Lemma
3.8
) that our algorithm’s base case input vanishes (at least if its initial input
A
is anti-invariant). Bostan, Lairez and Salvy’s algorithm
[
2
, Alg. 1]
instead accumulates all of its remainders
r
, as well as its base input, and simply reports that
\omega_{A}
is exact on
\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus X
if and only if all of these quantities vanish
[
2
, Thm. 1]
. In our setting, we must rather decide whether
\omega_{A}
becomes exact (up to holomorphic error) on some nontrivial proper open subset
U\setminus X
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus X
; this question is out of the scope of
[
2
, Alg. 1]
.

Remark 3.14
.

The solutions to (
4
) that we’ve managed to attain—i.e., using Construction
3.9
—solve that equation “strictly”, in the sense that they cause its right-hand side to vanish entirely (as opposed to merely modulo
F^{q+1}
). We don’t expect that strict solutions should exist for general
\sigma
-invariant sextics
F
; the fact that we haven’t managed to find
non-strict
solutions yet suggests that we’re leaving something on the table.

3.3
The Main Theorem

This subsection’s main theorem assembles the various results we’ve accumulated.

We begin with a few preliminaries. Given an invertible linear map
M:\mathbb{C}^{6}\to\mathbb{C}^{6}
, we have the usual
pullback
map
M^{*}
on
\mathbb{C}[Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{5}]
(effected by precomposition). Moreover, for
v=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{5})
a vector field with components in
\mathbb{C}[Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{5}]_{(F)}
, we define the
pullback
of
v
as

M^{*}v\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}\vphantom{\begin{bmatrix}M^{*}v_{0}\\
\vdots\\
M^{*}v_{5}\end{bmatrix}}\mathmakebox[4em][c]{M}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}M^{*}v_{0}\\
\vdots\\
M^{*}v_{5}\end{bmatrix}.

This coordinate-change rule coincides with that which transports
v
, interpreted as a
derivation
mapping
\mathbb{C}[Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{5}]_{(F)}\to\mathbb{C}[Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{5}]_{(F)}
, along the isomorphism
M^{*}:\mathbb{C}[Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{5}]_{(F)}\to\mathbb{C}[Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{5}]_{(F)}
.

This rule plays nicely with the various operations we’ve been using.

Lemma 3.15
.

For each
M
,
F
, and
v
, we have
M^{*}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(dF(v)}}\right)=d\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M^{*}F}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M^{*}v}}\right)
and
M^{*}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{div}(v)}}\right)=\operatorname{div}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M^{*}v}}\right)
.

Proof.

The first assertion is a basic consequence of the chain rule. Indeed,

d\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M^{*}F}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M^{*}v}}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F\circ M}}\right)\,\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\vphantom{\begin{bmatrix}M^{*}v_{0}\\
\vdots\\
M^{*}v_{5}\end{bmatrix}}\mathmakebox[4em][c]{M}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}M^{*}v_{0}\\
\vdots\\
M^{*}v_{5}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial Z_{i}}\circ M\,\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}M^{*}v_{0}\\
\vdots\\
M^{*}v_{5}\end{bmatrix}=M^{*}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(dF(v)}}\right).

To prove the second assertion, we note that
\operatorname{div}(M^{*}v)=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{j}M^{-1}_{ij}\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{j}\circ M}}\right)}}\right)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}M^{-1}_{ij}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial Z_{i}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{j}\circ M}}\right)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}M^{-1}_{ij}\cdot\sum_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\partial v_{j}}{\partial Z_{k}}\circ M}}\right)\cdot M_{ki}}}\right)=\sum_{j}\sum_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{i}M_{ki}\cdot M^{-1}_{ij}}}\right)\cdot\frac{\partial v_{j}}{\partial Z_{k}}\circ M=M^{*}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{div}(v)}}\right)
; the last equality amounts to
\sum_{i}M_{ki}\cdot M^{-1}_{ij}=\delta_{kj}
.
∎

Lemma
3.15
implies that solutions
F
,
A
and
v
to (
4
) transport under pullback, since if
F^{q+1}\mid A-q\cdot dF(v)+F\cdot\operatorname{div}(v)
, then
M^{*}F^{q+1}\mid M^{*}A-q\cdot d\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M^{*}F}}\right)(M^{*}v)+M^{*}F\cdot\operatorname{div}(M^{*}v)
.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
f(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2})
be a ternary sextic of Waring rank 3 whose associated plane curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is smooth. Then the smooth sextic
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
defined by
f(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2})-f(Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2})
is such that the
\iota
-invariant coniveau-1 Hodge substructure
H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})^{+}\subset H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is annihilated outside of a divisor
Y\subset X
.

Proof.

We fix
q\in\{0,1,2\}
and let
A\in R^{6\cdot q}
be arbitrary and
\sigma
-anti-invariant. By Lemma
3.8
and Theorem
3.10
, Construction
3.9
yields a vector field
v
that solves (
4
) for
A
and for
F(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2},V_{0},V_{1},V_{2})
the Fermat sextic. Applying our hypothesis on

, we write
f(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2})=L_{0}(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2})^{6}+L_{1}(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2})^{6}+L_{2}(U_{0},U_{1},U_{2})^{6}
, for linear forms
L_{0}
,
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
. Since
C
is necessarily singular at each projective point where the hyperplanes defined by
L_{0}
,
L_{1}
, and
L_{2}
intersect, we conclude that
L_{0}
,
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are linearly independent.

Pulling back both
v
and
A
along the change of coordinates

L:\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\begin{array}[]{c}U_{0}\\
U_{1}\\
U_{2}\\
V_{0}\\
V_{1}\\
V_{2}\end{array}}}\right]\mapsto\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\,L_{0}\,\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\\
\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\,L_{1}\,\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\\
\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\,L_{2}\,\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\end{array}&\\
\hline\cr&\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\,L_{0}\,\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\\
\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\,L_{1}\,\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\\
\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\,L_{2}\,\rule[2.15277pt]{12.48604pt}{0.5pt}\end{array}\end{array}}}\right]\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\begin{array}[]{c}U_{0}\\
