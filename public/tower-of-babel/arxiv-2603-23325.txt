Abstract
. The observable distance d conc d_{\operatorname{conc}} based on measure concentration and the box distance □ \Box based on collapsing theory are extended to geometric data sets introduced by Hanika–Schneider–Stumme. On the set 𝒟 \mathcal{D} of isomorphism classes of geometric data sets, d conc d_{\operatorname{conc}} is non-separable and □ \Box is complete and non-separable. We introduce the class 𝒟 / ℒ \mathcal{D}/\mathcal{L} of ℒ \mathcal{L} -compact geometric data sets in 𝒟 \mathcal{D} , for a monoidal subfamily ℒ \mathcal{L} of 1-Lipschitz functions Lip_1 ⁡ 1 ​ ( ℝ ) \lip 1(\mathbb{R}) , and prove its □ \Box -completeness and separability. We then construct a natural compactification of ( 𝒟 / ℒ , d conc ) (\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{L},d_{\operatorname{conc}}) by means of ℒ \mathcal{L} -pyramids when ℒ \mathcal{L} contains the clipping family. We further prove a complete limit formula for the observable diameter of Lip_1 ⁡ 1 ​ ( ℝ ) \lip 1(\mathbb{R}) -pyramids, and show that applying our construction to Hanika–Schneider–Stumme’s embedding is compatible with the compactification and preserves the polynomial-time computability of the observable diameter.

Introduction

Gromov
[
3
]
developed the geometry of mm-spaces, based on the concentration of measure phenomenon and the theory of collapsing manifolds. A triple
(X,d_{X},\mu_{X})
, or simply
X
, is an
mm-space
if
d_{X}
is a complete separable metric on
X
, and
\mu_{X}
is a Borel probability measure with full support on
(X,d_{X})
. He defined the observable distance
d_{\operatorname{conc}}
based on measure concentration and the box distance
\Box
based on collapsing theory on the set of all isomorphism classes of mm-spaces, say
\mathcal{X}
, and constructed a natural compactification of
(\mathcal{X},d_{\operatorname{conc}})
, each element of which is called a
pyramid
. Various properties of these distances and spaces are known, with particular attention given to the separability of
(\mathcal{X},d_{\operatorname{conc}})
and the completeness and separability of
(\mathcal{X},\Box)
.

Pestov
[
7
]
treated data as mm-spaces and used the
observable diameter
(see
Definition

2.19
) to explain a form of the curse of dimensionality through the concentration of measure phenomenon. However, an efficient algorithm for computing the observable diameter of an mm-space
X
is not yet known, and straightforward computation using the set of all 1-Lipschitz continuous functions on
X
, say
\lip 1(X)
, directly following the definition, takes exponential time with respect to the size
\#X
of the data sample
X
. He proposed restricting the function family used to compute the observable diameter.

Following this, Hanika, Schneider, and Stumme
[
4
]
defined the geometric data set as a generalization of mm-spaces in order to quantify the curse of dimensionality through the observable diameter, as follows: A triple
(X,F_{X},\mu_{X})
, or simply
X
, is defined to be a

if
F_{X}
is a non-empty set of real-valued functions on
X
such that

d_{F_{X}}(x,y)\coloneqq\sup_{f\in F_{X}}|f(x)-f(y)|,\ x,y\in X,

is a complete separable metric, and if
\mu_{X}
is a Borel probability measure with full support on
(X,d_{F_{X}})
. An mm-space can be interpreted as a special case where
F_{X}=\lip 1(X,d_{X})
. For a geometric data set
X
, we write
d_{X}
for
d_{F_{X}}
. We denote by
\mathcal{D}
the set of isomorphism classes of geometric data sets. They extended the observable distance between mm-spaces to geometric data sets and similarly generalized the observable diameter to geometric data sets. They then confirmed the characteristics of the observable diameter of geometric data sets as a statistical measure.

In our previous paper
[
9
]
, we proved the
non
-separability of
(\mathcal{D},d_{\operatorname{conc}})
and extended the box distance
\Box
to

, establishing its completeness and

-separability. In the present paper, we continue this study by constructing a compactification of a suitable subclass of
\mathcal{D}
equipped with the observable distance.

We first focus on a
\Box
-separable subset of
\mathcal{D}

-compact

of

is closed under left composition by elements of

and the

-covering number

Definition 2.1

(Ky Fan metric)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space and

on the set of

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be a geometric data set. For any

and

d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{X}(p\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}f,p\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}g)\leq d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{X}(f,g).

Proof.

Note that

for any

\varepsilon>d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{X}(f,g)
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{X}(\left\{x\in X\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}|p\circ f(x)-p\circ g(x)|>\varepsilon\right\})

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{X}(\left\{x\in X\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}|f(x)-g(x)|>\varepsilon\right\})\leq\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

For any

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X
be a geometric data set. For any

\overline{F_{X}}\cap B(f,r;d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{X})

Proof.

The separability of

\mu_{X}\!\left(U(\mathcal{N},r;d_{X})\right)\geq r
. Take any

there exist points

\displaystyle|f(x)-g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(x)-f(y)|+|f(y)-g(y)|+|g(y)-g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq d_{X}(x,y)+r+d_{X}(y,x)\leq 3r.

Since

Definition 3.1

(Monoidal)
.

A subfamily
\mathcal{L}\subset\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
is said to be
monoidal
if it is a monoid with the identity element
\id_{\mathbb{R}}
and is closed under pointwise convergence on
\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 3.2

(
\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{TB}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the family of translation functions,

\mathcal{T}\coloneqq\{\,x\mapsto x+c\mid c\in\mathbb{R}\,\}

and let
\mathcal{B}
be the family of bounding functions,

\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{\,b_{R}\coloneqq x\mapsto-R\lor x\land R\mid R\in[0,+\infty]\,\},

where
\lor
is the maximum and
\land
is the minimum.
We set

\mathcal{TB}\coloneqq\{\,x\mapsto l\lor(x+c)\land u\mid c\in\mathbb{R},\,l\in[-\infty,+\infty),u\in(-\infty,+\infty],\,l\leq u\,\},

which is the smallest monoidal family that contains both
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{B}
.

Hereafter, we fix a monoidal subfamily
\mathcal{L}\subset\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
. For any function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
and any family
F
of functions from
X
to
\mathbb{R}
, we denote

\mathcal{L}\circ f\coloneqq\{\,p\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}f\mid p\in\mathcal{L}\,\}\text{ and }\mathcal{L}\circ F\coloneqq\{\,p\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}f\mid p\in\mathcal{L}\text{ and }f\in F\,\}.

For any geometric data set
X
and
p\in\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
, we define

p\circ X\coloneqq(X,\lip 1(X,d_{X}),\mu_{X})/(p\circ F_{X}),

where
\lip 1(X,d_{X})
serves as the ambient feature family containing
p\circ F_{X}
.

Definition 3.3

(
(\varepsilon,\mathcal{L})
-Covering number)
.

Let
X
be a geometric data set,
F\subset\overline{F_{X}}
a subfamily, and
\varepsilon>0
a real number. We define the
(\varepsilon,\mathcal{L})
-
covering number
of
F
as

\cov(F,\varepsilon;\mathcal{L})\coloneqq\inf\{\,\#\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{N}\subset F\text{ is finite and }F\subset U(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N},\varepsilon;\,d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{X})\,\},

where
d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{X}
is the Ky Fan metric on
(X,\mu_{X})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be geometric data sets. For any real numbers
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon/2)
, if
d_{\operatorname{conc}}(X,Y)<\delta
, then

\cov(\overline{F_{X}},\varepsilon;\mathcal{L})\leq\cov(\overline{F_{Y}},\varepsilon-2\delta;\mathcal{L}).

Proof.

We may assume
\cov(\overline{F_{Y}},\varepsilon-2\delta;\mathcal{L})<+\infty
.
By the definition of
(\varepsilon-2\delta,\mathcal{L})
-covering number of
Y
, there exists a finite subset
\mathcal{N}_{Y}\subset\overline{F_{Y}}
such that

\#\mathcal{N}_{Y}=\cov(\overline{F_{Y}},\varepsilon-2\delta;\mathcal{L})\text{ and }\overline{F_{Y}}\subset U(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{Y},\varepsilon-2\delta;\,d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{Y}).

Since
d_{\operatorname{conc}}(X,Y)<\delta
, there exists a coupling
\pi\in\Pi(\mu_{X},\mu_{Y})
such that

{\left(d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi}\right)_{\operatorname{H}}}(\overline{F_{X}}\circ\pr_{1},\overline{F_{Y}}\circ\pr_{2})<\delta.

By this, there exists a finite subset
\mathcal{N}_{X}\subset\overline{F_{X}}
such that

\#\mathcal{N}_{X}=\#\mathcal{N}_{Y}\text{ and }{\left(d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi}\right)_{\operatorname{H}}}(\mathcal{N}_{X}\circ\pr_{1},\mathcal{N}_{Y}\circ\pr_{2})<\delta.

For any
f\in\overline{F_{X}}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{X}(f,\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{X})

\displaystyle\leq d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi}(f\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}\pr_{1},\overline{F_{Y}}\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}\pr_{2})+{\left(d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi}\right)_{\operatorname{H}}}(\overline{F_{Y}}\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}\pr_{2},\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{X}\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}\pr_{1})

\displaystyle<\delta+{\left(d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{Y}\right)_{\operatorname{H}}}(\overline{F_{Y}},\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{Y})+{\left(d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi}\right)_{\operatorname{H}}}(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{Y}\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}\pr_{2},\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{X}\mathbin{\mkern-3.0mu\circ\mkern-3.0mu}\pr_{1})

\displaystyle<\delta+(\varepsilon-2\delta)+\delta=\varepsilon,

which yields that
\cov(\overline{F_{X}},\varepsilon;\mathcal{L})\leq\#\mathcal{N}_{X}=\cov(\overline{F_{Y}},\varepsilon-2\delta;\mathcal{L})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.5

(
\mathcal{L}
-Closed,
\mathcal{L}
-Compact)
.

Let
X
be a geometric data set and
F\subset\overline{F_{X}}
a subfamily. The subfamily

is said to be
\mathcal{L}
-closed
if
\mathcal{L}\circ F\subset F
. It is
\mathcal{L}
-compact
if it is
\mathcal{L}
-closed and the
(\varepsilon,\mathcal{L})
-covering number of

is finite for any
\varepsilon>0
.

The geometric data set

is
\mathcal{L}
-closed
if
\overline{F_{X}}
is
\mathcal{L}
-closed. It is
\mathcal{L}
-compact
if
\overline{F_{X}}
is
\mathcal{L}
-compact.

Remark 3.6
.

As an example, consider the one-point geometric data set
(\{*\},F,\delta_{*})
where
F\coloneqq\{\,*\mapsto c\mid c\in\mathbb{R}\,\}
and
\delta_{*}
is the Dirac measure at

. This is
\mathcal{T}
-compact:
F
is
\mathcal{T}
-closed, and taking
\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\{*\mapsto 0\}
gives
\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{N}=F
, so
\cov(F,\varepsilon;\mathcal{T})=1
for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
. On the other hand, it is not
\{\id_{\mathbb{R}}\}
-compact, since

is not totally bounded in

.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
\mathcal{L}
-compact geometric data sets and let
X
be a geometric data set. If
X_{n}
concentrates to
X
, then
X
is
\mathcal{L}
-compact.

Proof.

For each
n\in\{1,2,\ldots\}
, we set
\delta_{n}\coloneqq d_{\operatorname{conc}}(X_{n},X)+\frac{1}{n}
. There exists a coupling
\pi_{n}\in\Pi(\mu_{X},\mu_{X_{n}})
such that

{\left(d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi_{n}}\right)_{\operatorname{H}}}(\overline{F_{X}}\circ\pr_{1},\overline{F_{X_{n}}}\circ\pr_{2})<\delta_{n}.

Take any
f\in\overline{F_{X}}
and any
p\in\mathcal{L}
. There exists
f_{n}\in\overline{F_{X_{n}}}
such that

d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi_{n}}(f\circ\pr_{1},f_{n}\circ\pr_{2})<\delta_{n},

and there exists
g_{n}\in\overline{F_{X}}
such that
d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi_{n}}(g_{n}\circ\pr_{1},p\circ f_{n}\circ\pr_{2})<\delta_{n}
. Thus,

\displaystyle d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{X}(p\circ f,g_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi_{n}}(p\circ f\circ\pr_{1},p\circ f_{n}\circ\pr_{2})+d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi_{n}}(p\circ f_{n}\circ\pr_{2},g_{n}\circ\pr_{1})

\displaystyle<d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\pi_{n}}(f\circ\pr_{1},f_{n}\circ\pr_{2})+\delta_{n}<2\delta_{n}\to 0

as

. Thus
p\circ f\in\overline{F_{X}}
, and we conclude that
X
is
\mathcal{L}
-closed.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n\in\{1,2,\ldots\}
such that
3d_{\operatorname{conc}}(X_{n},X)<\varepsilon
. By

, we have

\cov(\overline{F_{X}},\varepsilon;\mathcal{L})\leq\cov\left(\overline{F_{X_{n}}},\frac{\varepsilon}{3};\mathcal{L}\right)<+\infty,

is
\mathcal{L}
-compact. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.14

The monoidal subfamilies
\{\id_{\mathbb{R}}\}

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\{\{\id_{\mathbb{R}}\},\mathcal{B}\}

\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}

, there exists an isomorphism
q\in\mathcal{L}
such that
q\circ p(0)=0
: if
\mathcal{L}\in\{\{\id_{\mathbb{R}}\},\mathcal{B}\}

for all

, so we take

; if
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}
, take

. Given a sequence

such that
(q_{n}\circ p_{n})(0)=0

and
(q_{n}\circ p_{n})(0)=0

and

converges to

in

and sufficiently large

{\left(d_{\operatorname{KF}}^{\gamma}\right)_{\operatorname{H}}}(\mathcal{L}\circ p_{\iota(n)},\mathcal{L}\circ p)\leq\varepsilon

Remark 3.15

\mathcal{T}_{+}\coloneqq\{\,t\mapsto t+a\mid a\geq 0\,\}

. For any

Lemma 4.11

Lemma 5.5
.

Let

,
Y
, and

\mathcal{L}
-compact geometric data sets with

Proof.

Take dominations
\bar{\varphi}\colon\bar{Z}\to X

\bar{\psi}\colon\bar{Z}\to Y
. We define

Z\coloneqq\bar{Z}/(F_{X}\circ\bar{\varphi}\cup F_{Y}\circ\bar{\psi})

\mathcal{L}
-compact.

Take any integer

and any real number

and a closed set

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{N}_{X}\leq N,\ \mu_{X}(S_{X})>1-\varepsilon,\ F_{X}\subset U(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{X},\varepsilon;{d^{S_{X}}_{\infty}}),

\displaystyle\text{ and }\sup\{|f(x)|\mid f\in\mathcal{N}_{X},x\in S_{X}\}\leq R,

\mathcal{N}_{Y}\subset F_{Y}
and a closed set

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{N}_{Y}\leq N,\ \mu_{Y}(S_{Y})>1-\varepsilon,\ F_{Y}\subset U(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{Y},\varepsilon;{d^{S_{Y}}_{\infty}}),

\displaystyle\text{ and }\sup\{|g(y)|\mid g\in\mathcal{N}_{Y},y\in S_{Y}\}\leq R.

\displaystyle 1-\mu_{Z}(S)

\displaystyle=1-\mu_{Z}(\varphi^{-1}(S_{X})\cap\psi^{-1}(S_{Y}))

\displaystyle\leq 1-\mu_{Z}(\varphi^{-1}(S_{X}))+1-\mu_{Z}(\psi^{-1}(S_{Y}))

\displaystyle=1-\mu_{X}(S_{X})+1-\mu_{Y}(S_{Y})<2\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\subset U(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{X},\varepsilon;{d^{S_{X}}_{\infty}})\cup U(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{Y},\varepsilon;{d^{S_{Y}}_{\infty}})

\displaystyle\subset U\!\left(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{X}\circ\varphi,\varepsilon;\,{d^{\varphi^{-1}(S_{X})}_{\infty}}\right)\cup U\!\left(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N}_{Y}\circ\psi,\varepsilon;\,{d^{\psi^{-1}(S_{Y})}_{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle=U(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{N},\varepsilon;{d^{S}_{\infty}}),

and

for any

and

Remark 5.9
.

The monoidal families
\mathcal{B}
and

both satisfy the condition of

. There exists a counterexample when

. Let

6.
Staircase

In this section, we define staircases and prove that the staircase map provides a topological embedding of
\mathcal{L}
-compact classes into a compact space. Our strategy is as follows: First, we introduce the notion of extractability for
\mathcal{L}
, which allows us to bound the observable diameter of a closed subset in terms of staircases and to establish a limit formula for the observable diameter. Second, we use the Ky Fan covering number of
F_{X}
to control the convergence of
N
-measurements. Finally, combining these results, we establish an embedding theorem that realizes the concentration of geometric data sets via staircases.

6.1.
Definition and Basic Properties of Staircases

Definition 6.1

(Staircase)
.

The sequence
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathcal{S}(N)\}_{N=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{D})
is called a
staircase
if the following conditions (1), (2), and (3) are satisfied.

(1)

(measurement coherence) For any
\{N,M\}\subset\{1,2,\ldots\}
, if
M\leq N
, then
\mathcal{S}(M)=\overline{\mathcal{DM}(\mathcal{S}(N);M,M)}^{\Box}
.

(2)

(directedness) For any
\{N,M\}\subset\{1,2,\ldots\}
, any
X\in\mathcal{S}(N)
, and any
Y\in\mathcal{S}(M)
, there exists
Z\in\mathcal{S}(N+M)
such that
X\preceq b_{N}\circ Z
and
Y\preceq b_{M}\circ Z
.

(3)

(nonemptiness and closedness) Any
\mathcal{S}(N)
is a nonempty
\Box

Lemma 6.9
.

and

6.2.
Extractability and Observable Diameter

Definition 6.13

(Observable diameter of a set)
.

For a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{D}
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
, we define the
\kappa
-observable diameter
of
\mathcal{E}
by

\od(\mathcal{E};-\kappa)\coloneqq\sup_{X\in\mathcal{E}}\od(X;-\kappa).

Proposition 6.14
.

For a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{D}
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
, the function
\kappa\mapsto\od(\mathcal{E};-\kappa)
is right-continuous.

Proof.

By the monotonicity of
\od(\mathcal{E};-\kappa)
in
\kappa
, it suffices to show that

\od(\mathcal{E};-\kappa)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\od\!\left(\mathcal{E};-\left(\kappa+\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

By
Lemma

2.22
,
\od(X;-\kappa)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\od(X;-(\kappa+1/n))
for each
X
. Thus,

\displaystyle\od(\mathcal{E};-\kappa)

\displaystyle=\sup_{X\in\mathcal{E}}\od(X;-\kappa)

\displaystyle=\sup_{X\in\mathcal{E}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\od\!\left(X;-\left(\kappa+\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{X\in\mathcal{E}}\od\!\left(X;-\left(\kappa+\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\od\!\left(\mathcal{E};-\left(\kappa+\frac{1}{n}\right)\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Note that a limit formula for the observable diameter of a pyramid does not hold in general; extractability is introduced to simultaneously describe the relationship between weak convergence of pyramids and
d_{\operatorname{conc}}
, and to provide such a formula. Technically, the extraction estimate
\varphi(\kappa,r)
gives a bound on
R
such that any feature witnessing the observable diameter can be brought into an
(N,R)
-measurement without losing the observable diameter value.

Definition 6.15

(Extractable, extraction estimate)
.

We say that
\mathcal{L}
is
extractable on an open interval
(a,b)\subset(0,1)
if there exists a function
\varphi\colon(a,b)\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that for any Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, any
\kappa\in(a,b)
, any
\varepsilon\in(0,b-\kappa)
, and any
r>0
, there exists
g\in\mathcal{L}
satisfying

\min\{r,\pd(\mu;1-(\kappa+\varepsilon))\}\leq\pd((b_{\varphi(\kappa,r)})_{*}g_{*}\mu;1-\kappa)+2\varepsilon.

Such a function
\varphi
is called an
extraction estimate
.

For a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, the
Lévy mean
of
\mu
is defined by

\mathrm{Lm}(\mu)\coloneqq\inf\bigl\{m\in\mathbb{R}\bigm|\mu((-\infty,m])\geq 1/2\bigr\}.

Lemma 6.16
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
with
\mathrm{Lm}(\mu)=0
,
\kappa\in(0,1/2)
, and
R>0
. If
\pd((b_{R})_{*}\mu;1-\kappa)<R
, then
\pd(\mu;1-\kappa)=\pd((b_{R})_{*}\mu;1-\kappa)
.

Proof.

Take any
\varepsilon\in(0,R-\pd((b_{R})_{*}\mu;1-\kappa))
. There exists a closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

(b_{R})_{*}\mu(I)\geq 1-\kappa,\quad\diam(I)<\pd((b_{R})_{*}\mu;1-\kappa)+\varepsilon<R.

Since
(b_{R})_{*}\mu(I)>1/2
and
\mathrm{Lm}((b_{R})_{*}\mu)=0
, we have
0\in I
. Since
\diam(I)<R
, it follows that
I\subset(-R,R)
. Therefore,
\mu(I)=\mu(b_{R}^{-1}(I))\geq 1-\kappa
, and so

\pd(\mu;1-\kappa)\leq\diam(I)<\pd((b_{R})_{*}\mu;1-\kappa)+\varepsilon.

By the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, we obtain
\pd(\mu;1-\kappa)\leq\pd((b_{R})_{*}\mu;1-\kappa)
. The reverse inequality
\pd((b_{R})_{*}\mu;1-\kappa)\leq\pd(\mu;1-\kappa)
holds since
b_{R}
is
1
-Lipschitz: for any closed interval
I
with
\mu(I)\geq 1-\kappa
, we have
b_{R}(I)\subset\mathbb{R}
closed,
\diam(b_{R}(I))\leq\diam(I)
, and
(b_{R})_{*}\mu(b_{R}(I))=\mu(b_{R}^{-1}(b_{R}(I)))\geq\mu(I)\geq 1-\kappa
.
∎

7.

In this section, we show that when
\mathcal{L}
contains
\mathcal{TB}
, the space of
\mathcal{L}
-pyramids equipped with a suitable metric provides a compactification of
(\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{L},d_{\operatorname{conc}})

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be an
\mathcal{L}
-pyramid,
N
be natural number, and
R>0
be real number. If
\mathcal{TB}\subset\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{DM}(\mathcal{P};N,R)
is compact.

Proof.

For any
X\in\mathcal{P}
and
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\}\subset F_{X}
, since
\mathcal{L}\circ b_{R}\circ\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}\}
is
\mathcal{L}
-compact,

\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{DM}(\mathcal{P};N,R)\subset\mathcal{P}

For any

, since
\mathcal{DM}(N,R)
is compact, there exist
X\in\mathcal{DM}(N,R)

\iota

in
\Box

\mathcal{L}\circ X_{\iota(n)}\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

\mathcal{DM}(\mathcal{P};N,R)

∎

(Metrization of weak Hausdorff convergence)

Proposition 7.3

(Pyramid metric and compactification)
.

If
\mathcal{TB}\subset\mathcal{L}
, a metric

\rho(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\coloneqq\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2N\cdot 2^{N}}\cdot{{\Box}_{\operatorname{H}}}(\mathcal{DM}(\mathcal{P};N,N),\mathcal{DM}(\mathcal{Q};N,N))

Compactness.

\Pi_{\mathcal{L}}

Embedding.

For any
\mathcal{L}
-pyramid
\mathcal{P}

is extractable on
(0,1/2)
whenever
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}
. For
\mathcal{L}=\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
, the following result from
[
10
]
allows us to extend extractability to all of
(0,1)
.

Lemma 7.5

(
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
R>0
, and
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists
g\in\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
with values in
[-R/\alpha,R/\alpha]
such that

\pd(g_{*}\mu;\,\alpha)=\min\{R,\,\pd(\mu;\,\alpha)\}.

Proposition 7.6
.

\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
is extractable on
(0,1)
.

Proof.

Set the extraction estimate
\varphi(\kappa,r)\coloneqq r/(1-\kappa)
. Take any
\kappa\in(0,1)
,
\varepsilon,r>0
, and Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
. By
Lemma

7.5
applied with
\alpha=1-\kappa
and
R=r
, there exists
g\in\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
with values in
[-r/(1-\kappa),\,r/(1-\kappa)]
such that

\pd(g_{*}\mu;\,1-\kappa)=\min\{r,\,\pd(\mu;\,1-\kappa)\}.

Since
g
takes values in
[-\varphi(\kappa,r),\,\varphi(\kappa,r)]
, we have
(b_{\varphi(\kappa,r)})_{*}g_{*}\mu=g_{*}\mu
. Since
\pd(\mu;\,1-(\kappa+\varepsilon))\leq\pd(\mu;\,1-\kappa)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\min\{r,\,\pd(\mu;\,1-(\kappa+\varepsilon))\}

\displaystyle\leq\min\{r,\,\pd(\mu;\,1-\kappa)\}

\displaystyle=\pd\!\left((b_{\varphi(\kappa,r)})_{*}g_{*}\mu;\,1-\kappa\right)

\displaystyle\leq\pd\!\left((b_{\varphi(\kappa,r)})_{*}g_{*}\mu;\,1-\kappa\right)+2\varepsilon,

which verifies the definition of extractability.
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

If a sequence of
\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
-pyramids
\mathcal{P}_{n}
converges weakly to
\mathcal{P}
, then for any
\kappa\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\od(\mathcal{P};-\kappa)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to+0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\od(\mathcal{P}_{n};-(\kappa+\varepsilon))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to+0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\od(\mathcal{P}_{n};-(\kappa+\varepsilon)).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{TB}\subset\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
,
Proposition

7.2
gives
(1)\Leftrightarrow(3)
, so weak convergence is equivalent to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
. By
Proposition

7.6
,
\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
is extractable on
(0,1)
, so the conclusion follows from
Proposition

6.19
.
∎

8.
Application to mm-Spaces

In this section, we apply our compactification to the embedding of mm-spaces into geometric data sets proposed by Hanika et al., and show that it preserves the observable diameter.

8.1.
Compactification of Hanika–Schneider–Stumme’s Embedding

To compute the observable diameter efficiently, Hanika et al. proposed adopting as features the family of distance-to-point functions,

F_{\circ}(X)\coloneqq\{x\mapsto d_{X}(x,y)\mid y\in X\},

for an mm-space
X

[
4
, Definition 3.2]
. We set
X_{\circ}\coloneqq(X,F_{\circ}(X),\mu_{X})
. With this choice, for a finite mm-space
X
with
n=\#X
, the observable diameter
\od(X_{\circ};-\kappa)
can be computed in
O(n^{3})
time
[
4
, §6.1.1]
.

More generally, fix a monoidal family
\mathcal{L}
with
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}
. For an mm-space
X
, define

\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)\coloneqq(X,\mathcal{L}\circ F_{\circ}(X),\mu_{X})=(X,\{x\mapsto p(d_{X}(x,y))\mid p\in\mathcal{L},\,y\in X\},\mu_{X}).

Corollary 8.1
.

Assume
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}
. For any mm-space
X
, the geometric data set
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)
is
\mathcal{L}
-compact. In particular,
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)\in\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle d_{X}(x,y)

\displaystyle=|(\id_{\mathbb{R}}\circ d_{X}(x,\cdot))(x)-(\id_{\mathbb{R}}\circ d_{X}(x,\cdot))(y)|\leq d_{F_{\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{p\in\mathcal{L},z\in X}|p(d_{X}(z,x))-p(d_{X}(z,y))|\leq d_{X}(x,y),

we prove that
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)
is a geometric data set. The family
\mathcal{L}\circ F_{\circ}(X)
is
\mathcal{L}
-closed since
\mathcal{L}
is monoidal. By
Theorem

3.19
,
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)
is
\mathcal{L}
-compact.
∎

Proposition 8.2

(Observable diameter under Hanika–Schneider–Stumme’s embedding)
.

For any mm-space
X
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
, we have

\od(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X);-\kappa)=\od(X_{\circ};-\kappa).

Proof.

Since
\id_{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{L}
, we see that
F_{X_{\circ}}\subset F_{\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)}
, which gives
\geq
. For the reverse inequality, note that for any
f\in F_{X_{\circ}}
and
p\in\mathcal{L}\subset\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\pd(p_{*}f_{*}\mu_{X};\,1-\kappa)\leq\pd(f_{*}\mu_{X};\,1-\kappa),

which gives
\leq
. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 8.3

(Compactification of Hanika–Schneider–Stumme’s embedding)
.

If
\mathcal{TB}\subset\mathcal{L}
, then the closure of
\Pi^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)}\mid X\in\mathcal{X}\}
with respect to
\rho
provides a compactification of
(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(\mathcal{X}),d_{\operatorname{conc}})
, where
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\{\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)\mid X\in\mathcal{X}\}
.

Proof.

By
Corollary

8.1
(note
\mathcal{TB}\subset\mathcal{L}
implies
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}
), the map
X\mapsto\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(X)
takes values in
\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{L}
, so
\Pi^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}\subset\Pi_{\mathcal{L}}
. By
Proposition

7.3
,
(\Pi_{\mathcal{L}},\rho)
is a compactification of
(\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{L},d_{\operatorname{conc}})
. The closure of
\Pi^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}
in the compact space
(\Pi_{\mathcal{L}},\rho)
is compact and contains
\iota_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(\mathcal{X}))
, hence provides a compactification of
(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\circ}(\mathcal{X}),d_{\operatorname{conc}})
.
∎

In particular, when
\mathcal{L}=\lip 1(\mathbb{R})
,
Corollary

7.7
yields the complete limit formula for the observable diameter of the compactification of
\mathcal{D}^{\lip 1(\mathbb{R})}_{\circ}(\mathcal{X})
.
