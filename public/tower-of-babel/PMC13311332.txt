Bridging gaps: Demographic insights into utilization and engagement in Los Angeles County, California's TelePrEP HIV prevention program
Authors: Giovanna Lopez, Seth Wiafe, Naila Khalaf, Jim E. Banta, Salome Kapella Mshigeni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesIn alignment with the Ending the HIV Epidemic (EHE) Initiative, the Los Angeles County Department of Public Health launched TelePrEP in May 2023 to expand access to HIV prevention services. This study aimed to examine predictors of engagement in care, including demographic characteristics, and insurance status.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study used de-identified data from 412 clients during the program's operation from May 2023 through May 2025. Engagement was measured as completion of three or more quarterly visits versus fewer than three visits. Bivariate analysis and Logistic regression assessed associations between engagement and client demographics.ResultsThe cohort was predominantly male (91.70%), Hispanic/Latino (48.10%), and aged 18–39 years (72.60%). Majority of clients disenrolled (53.80%) were attributed to adherence/compliance challenges. Completion of three or more quarterly visits was not significantly associated with gender, age, race/ethnicity, or insurance status in either bivariate or adjusted analyses.ConclusionIn this large urban TelePrEP program, no statistically significant associations were detected between the measured variables and completion of three or more quarterly visits. Small subgroup sizes and wide confidence intervals limit interpretation. Findings support strengthening early engagement and reengagement strategies, although interpretation is limited by small subgroup sizes.

Introduction

The history of the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) in the United States reveals a trajectory marked by profound scientific advancement and an evolving public health response. The development of pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), a daily oral medication with an efficacy of approximately 99% when taken as prescribed, represents a pivotal breakthrough in primary prevention (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2023). Los Angeles County's (LAC) Annual HIV Surveillance Report found that PrEP uptake increased within the MSMS population from 29% to 2017 to 50% in 2023 meeting the local EHE benchmark (Los Angeles County Department of Public Health, Division of HIV and STD Programs, 2025). However, uptake remains uneven with Black and Hispanic/Latino men comprising roughly two-thirds of new diagnoses (AIDSVu, 2024; HIV, 2024). These disparities underscore an urgent, unmet need for targeted, accessible prevention strategies.

The national “Ending the HIV Epidemic” (EHE) initiative aims to reduce new HIV infections by 75% by 2025 and 90% by 2030, with “Prevent” as one of its four core pillars (Fauci et al., 2019). Telehealth models for PrEP delivery, known as TelePrEP, have emerged as a promising strategy to improve access and engagement by providing flexible, remote care (Bonett et al., 2024). Barriers such as transportation, clinic wait times, distance, stigma and privacy concerns can disproportionately affect key populations (Pinto et al., 2018; Hoth et al., 2019). Even during the pandemic, telehealth became critical for maintaining HIV prevention services amid reduced in-person visits (Ogbuagu et al., 2023). This shift also accelerated policy changes that supported telehealth expansion for sexual health services (Parker et al., 2024). By leveraging remote consultations and medication delivery, TelePrEP has the potential to expand access and engagement among populations disproportionately affected by HIV.

In May 2023, the LAC Department of Public Health launched a TelePrEP program funded through the EHE initiative, integrating PrEP navigation and telehealth to enhance medication accessibility and adherence support. While early evidence from smaller or rural TelePrEP programs is promising, indicating feasibility and high user satisfaction (Hoth et al., 2019), there remains a gap in understanding engagement patterns and their predictors within large-scale, publicly funded urban implementations. Evaluation is needed to assess whether telehealth effectively sustains engagement across diverse demographic groups and to identify modifiable factors linked to program discontinuation (Rolle et al., 2017).

Few studies have evaluated engagement patterns in large urban areas, therefore insights from this research can inform policies and programs to ensure equitable access to preventive treatment for all. Urban programs serve highly diverse populations with different language needs, insurance coverage, and competing social and structural barriers. These factors may influence whether clients remain engaged in TelePrEP after initiation. This study aimed to evaluate the LAC's TelePrEP program by analyzing utilization patterns and engagement. Specifically, it examined whether client demographics (gender, age, race/ethnicity) and insurance status were associated with three or more quarterly visits, providing evidence on the program's ability to deliver equitable care.

These findings can help optimize telehealth-based HIV prevention, guide resource allocation, and support policy decisions to ensure effective and equitable access to PrEP for all. The present study examines post-enrollment engagement among LAC's TelePrEP clients referred to through clinics. It is important to note that this study does not evaluate initial PrEP access or initiation. Instead, it focused on PrEP engagement and milestone completion after enrollment, which require laboratory visits, medication adherence, and resource coordination.

Methods

Study design and population

This retrospective cohort study used secondary, de-identified program data from LAC's TelePrEP program. The study was deemed exempt from full Institutional Review Board review by both LAC Department of Public Health and Loma Linda University. An a priori power analysis indicated that a sample size of approximately 400 would provide 80% power to detect a small-to-moderate effect (w = 0.15) in chi-square tests of association (α = 0.05). This calculation was intended to guide the overall descriptive comparison of milestone completion across the different client characteristics but was not powered to detect differences within smaller demographic subgroups. Some subgroups had small counts, therefore the effective power to detect subgroup differences was lower than the sample size suggests.

The analysis included 412 of the 421 TelePrEP clients during the operation of the program from May 2023 through May 2025 and who had complete electronic medical records (EMR) in the ORCHID system. Complete records were defined as containing essential data elements; demographic information, enrollment status, milestone visit completion, and adults over the age of 18. Since this study focused on adult TelePrEP clients, 9 clients under the age of 18 were excluded from the analytic sample Listwise deletion was used to remove clients who did not meet the complete records criteria. Additionally, clients were enrolled through one of ten sexual health clinics across LAC after seeking STI services and opting to initiate PrEP.

Measures

Primary Outcome: Milestone Completion (3 or more visits).

Milestone completion was measured using the number of documented follow-up visits completed during the observation periods. Milestone visits were scheduled at approximately 3-month intervals (i.e., at months 3, 6, and 9 following enrollment), consistent with standards set by the LAC Department of Public Health and aligned with published PrEP literature defining long-term engagement (Burns et al., 2022). This threshold was used as a practical programmatic indicator of continued engagement across multiple follow-up periods, rather than as a definitive measure of long-term retention.

Predictor Variables.

Demographic and structural factors hypothesized to be associated with completion of three or more quarterly milestone visits were analyzed as independent variables. These included gender, age category, race/ethnicity, primary language, and insurance status. Gender was categorized as male, female, non-binary, and transgender woman. Age was grouped as 18–28, 29–39, 40–50, and 50+ per the data set to maintain age anonymity. Race/Ethnicity was categorized as Hispanic/Latino, Black/African American, White/European, Asian, Other/Multiracial. Primary language was categorized as English, Spanish, or other, and insurance status was categorized as insured or uninsured.

Final program disposition and disenrollment reasons were retrieved from ORCHID electronic medical record documentation. Clients were categorized as enrolled if they remained active in the TelePrEP program at the end of the observation period. For clients who were no longer enrolled, the primary documented reason for disenrollment was recorded and classified into the following categories: adherence/compliance challenges, patient request, transfer to a primary care provider, traveling/relocation, transfer out of jurisdiction, medical condition, incomplete enrollment, or other.

As an explanatory secondary analysis, a binary logistic regression model was conducted to examine if the client characteristics were associated with completion of three or more quarterly visit after mutual adjustment. Primary language was excluded due to high collinearity with race and ethnicity. This resulted in unstable parameter estimates when both variables were included.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics summarized the cohort. A chi-square test assessed the relationship between Program Disposition and milestone visit completion. This was examined descriptively, with effect size measured by Cramer's V. Bivariate analyses (chi-square tests).

A binary logistic model was fitted to identify factors associated with three or more quarterly visits. Primary language was excluded from the multivariate model due to high collinearity with race/ethnicity in this sample, which resulted in unstable parameter estimates when both variables were included. Reference categories were set to female for gender, 18–28 years for age, Hispanic/Latino for race and ethnicity, and uninsured for insurance status. Model fit was assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow test and Cox & Snell R2. Due to complete separation for the transgender women category (all were in the more then three visits group), standard maximum likelihood estimation is reported with a note on the unstable estimate.

Reasons for clients not being retained were summarized descriptively for the disenrolled subgroup. A sensitivity analysis was also conducted to assess whether findings were affected by disenrollment reasons that may not reflect true disengagement from PrEP care. Specifically, 35 clients were excluded because their disenrollment reasons suggested possible continuation of PrEP care elsewhere, such as transfer to a primary care provider or relocation/travel. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (version 29.01).

To explore factors associated with 3 or more milestone visit outcomes, both Bivariate and logistic regression analysis were conducted. The bivariate analysis (Table 2) examined unadjusted associations between client characteristics and completion of three or more quarterly milestone visits compared with fewer than three quarterly milestone visits. Bivariate analysis showed no significant associations between gender, age, race/ethnicity, insurance status, and 3 or more milestone visits. Descriptive patterns suggested early disenrollment and adherence-related case closure were prominent, but these should not be interpreted as independent causal predictors. Clients with adherence or compliance challenges were far more likely to have completed fewer than three quarterly milestone visits (67.20%) compared to three or more visits (13.0%). The prominence of behavioral factors, such as difficulty maintaining daily medication routines, competing life demands, and varying levels of readiness for PrEP, appeared to plan an important role in ongoing engagement. Once access barriers such as transportation, clinic stigma, and scheduling are reduced through telehealth, day-to-day behavioral challenges become the primary determinants of persistence.

In the exploration logistic regression model limited to baseline client characteristics (Table 3), no demographic or insurance variable was statistically significantly predictor for completion of three or more quarterly milestone visits after mutual adjustment. Estimates should be interpreted cautiously because the model was not designed to estimate causal effects and several subgroup estimates were imprecise. Although not significant, point estimates suggested a trend toward higher odds of three or more visits among clients aged 40–50 (adjusted Odds Ratio = 2.22, p = .09) and 50+ (adjusted Odds Ratio = 2.91, p = .11) compared to the 18–28 reference group. The model demonstrated modest explanatory power (Cox & Snell R2 = 0.04) and acceptable fit (Hosmer-Lemeshow p = .12). Discussion

This study evaluated 3 or more milestone visit outcomes within a large, urban TelePrEP program funded through the Ending the HIV Epidemic initiative. The central finding is that completion of three or more quarterly milestone visits was not significantly associated with gender, age, race/ethnicity, or insurance status. These findings should be interpreted cautiously as several subgroup sizes were small, limiting their precision. Although existing literature on PrEP care documents significant disparities in uptake and persistence linked to these very factors, particularly race and ethnicity (Huang et al., 2018), no statistically significant associations were detected between the measured demographic/insurance variables and completion of three or more quarterly milestone visits.

Our analysis revealed that disenrollment typically occurred early in the care timeline, with a majority of disenrolled clients completing only one follow-up visit or none (Fig. 1). This pattern of early drop-off is a well-documented challenge in PrEP care across delivery models (Rolle et al., 2017) and highlights the initial months as a period of critical vulnerability for engagement. Adherence and compliance challenges were the predominant reason for disenrollment, cited by over half (53.80%) of those who left the program. This aligns with the broader literature on the difficulties of maintaining daily preventive medication, where regimen fatigue and competing life priorities are common barriers (Hosek et al., 2016). These findings should not be interpreted as support for moving away from demographic-targeted or equity focus intervention, but results may suggest that early intervention, proactive support mechanisms, such as intensified navigation, tailored adherence counseling, and streamlined re-engagement protocols, during this high-risk initial phase may benefit clients across the programs. Multilevel interventions incorporating such support are critical for sustaining engagement in PrEP care (Pinto et al., 2018).

The cohort demographics reflected populations heavily burdened by HIV in LAC, with strong representation of Hispanic/Latino (48.10%) and Black/African American (15.00%) clients. This recruitment suggests that community outreach and the inherent privacy of telehealth can effectively engage groups historically underserved by traditional clinic-based models. While not statistically significant, the observed trend toward 3 or more milestone visit odds among clients aged 40 and older merits mention and is consistent with life-course patterns where older adults may have more stable routines and healthcare experiences. Future research with larger samples should investigate whether age-specific support strategies are warranted. Again, these findings should not be interpreted as evidence that demographic differences are absent or unimportant in broader PrEP care.

These findings carry direct implications for public health practice and policy. First, they support continued and expanded investment in telehealth infrastructure for HIV prevention, particularly within publicly funded health departments. However, TelePrEP programs vary in their operational and financing models, with implications for patient costs and program sustainability (Dawson et al., 2022). Thes findings support the addition of universal early-stage support systems that address common adherence and engagement challenges in complement to existing equity-focused outreach and retention efforts. (Pinto et al., 2018). Policy efforts should incentivize telehealth integration, reduce medication access barriers for uninsured individuals, and fund proactive engagement initiatives like automated reminders and peer navigation. Finally, the demonstrated success in reaching priority populations underscores the importance of sustaining community-informed outreach as a core component of TelePrEP implementation.

This study has several limitations. The use of secondary program data meant key psychosocial variables, such as perceived stigma, HIV risk perception, income, or education, were unavailable, limiting our ability to explain the drivers of disengagement more fully. Adherence was assessed programmatically rather than through objective measures like tenofovir levels. The sample included clients who sought sexual health services, potentially excluding those with the greatest barriers or mistrust of healthcare systems, which may limit generalizability.

Furthermore, these findings are not generalized to all individuals who may be eligible for PrEP and should not be interpreted as evidence that telehealth reduces structural barriers to PrEP access more broadly. Clients have already overcome several barriers to PrEP access, including awareness of services and willingness to seek care, therefore the results can be applied to engagement within a clinic-referred TelePrEP cohort. The findings are also specific to the LAC context with its unique healthcare infrastructure; replication in other urban and rural settings is needed. The small sample size for certain demographic subgroups (e.g., transgender women, non-binary individuals) limited statistical power to detect differences within these groups. The study period also overlapped with the increasing availability of long-acting injectable cabotegravir for pre-exposure prophylaxis, also known as Apretude (U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2021). Since the analysis focused on clients enrolled in the TelePrEP program and did not capture whether clients transitioned to injectable PrEP, engagement outcomes may not fully reflect continued engagement in HIV prevention care.

Although the overall sample size was adequate for program evaluation, there were several small demographic groups, which prompted the study to have limited statistical power to detect engagement in subgroups. Larger studies are needed to assess whether 3 or more milestone visit outcomes differ by gender, race/ethnicity, etc. Age was also grouped into broad categories, which may have reduced variation and limited the ability to identify more nuanced age-related patterns.

Engagement measurement also had limitations. Engagement was measured using the number of documented quarterly follow-up visits completed and was dichotomized as completion of three or more quarterly visits versus fewer than three quarterly visits. This approach may have resulted in loss of information by reducing visit completion into two categories. In addition, clients who enrolled later in the study period had less opportunity to complete three quarterly visits, which may have increased the likelihood that they were classified as completing fewer than three visits. Lastly, this study did not include a comparison group of clients receiving in-person PrEP care in the same setting; therefore, the observed engagement (3 or more milestone visit outcome) patterns cannot be attributed specifically to the telehealth modality. Findings should be interpreted with caution.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the evaluation of the LAC's TelePrEP program found no statistically significant associations between the measured variables and the observed completion of three or more quarterly milestone visits. The primary challenge identified was early disengagement driven by adherence difficulties, a known barrier in PrEP persistence (Hosek et al., 2016). Therefore, the future effectiveness of TelePrEP will depend on both sustaining equitable reach across priority populations and strengthening early, proactive support to prevent disengagement (Pinto et al., 2018). These insights contribute to the growing evidence base for telehealth-driven HIV prevention and offer a clear pathway for enhancing the impact of such programs in LAC and beyond.
