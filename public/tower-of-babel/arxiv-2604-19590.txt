Minimizers for the Cahn–Hilliard Energy Functional with the Flory–Huggins Potential under Strong Anchoring Conditions
Shibin Dai, Abba Ramadan, Natasha Sharma

Abstract
In this paper, we theoretically and numerically study the minimizers for the Cahn–Hilliard energy with the Flory–Huggins potential under the strong anchoring condition, i.e., the Dirichlet boundary condition. We reveal bifurcation phenomena mediated by the boundary condition, the transition layer thickness, and the temperature of the system. Numerical simulations are also presented to approximate the minimizers of this energy by solving a gradient-flow equation, namely the Allen–Cahn equation constrained with strong anchoring conditions and random initial data. The effects of varying the transition layer thickness and temperature are presented to confirm the theoretical analysis.

Introduction

The Cahn–Hilliard functional is a classical model for the free energy of a binary mixture, and it
has been explored heavily for many other interests. In particular, it is the cornerstone for the
diffuse-interface model to characterize the free energy of a system undergoing phase separation

[
10
,
11
]
. For a system occupying a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a
C^{1}
boundary,
the functional can be written as

\displaystyle E(u)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+W(u)\right)dx.

(1)

Here,
W
is a double-well potential, with two minimizers corresponding to the two phases of the
system, and
\kappa
is a parameter measuring the thickness of the transition layer between the two
phases.

The Cahn–Hilliard functional, along with the Cahn–Hilliard and Allen–Cahn equations, provides a
central framework for modeling key phenomena in two-phase materials, including phase separation,
coarsening, and pattern formation. Since such processes are studied on a domain
\Omega
, the role
of the boundary
\partial\Omega
is equally significant. A commonly imposed condition is the Neumann
boundary condition
\partial_{n}u=0
, where
n
is the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
9
,
22
,
25
,
30
,
35
,
36
]
. Other boundary conditions are also frequently considered,
such as periodic conditions, especially in computational studies

[
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
23
,
26
,
28
,
29
,
37
]
.

Dai, Li, and Luong
[
18
]
recently studied minimizers of the Cahn–Hilliard functional with
quartic double-well potential under Dirichlet (strong anchoring) conditions, where
u
is fixed to
a prescribed boundary function
g
pointwise on
\partial\Omega
. This represents the most rigid
anchoring and is of considerable physical relevance
[
21
,
31
]
. A weaker anchoring can instead be
formulated by requiring
u
to approximate
g
within a tolerance in a suitable norm such as
L^{2}
.
Du and Nicolaides
[
21
]
introduced the Dirichlet boundary condition for studies of the
Cahn–Hilliard functional in a finite element scheme for the one-dimensional Cahn–Hilliard equation,
while Bronsard and Hilhorst
[
8
]
analyzed its asymptotic effects via energy methods. Further
work in this direction can be found in
[
7
,
24
,
32
]
.

Although a quartic potential is theoretically easier and numerically convenient, a physically more
realistic choice is the Flory–Huggins potential
[
2
,
1
,
13
,
20
,
34
,
33
]
, also commonly
referred to as the logarithmic potential,

\displaystyle W(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\theta}{2}\left((1-u)\ln(1-u)+(1+u)\ln(1+u)\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}(1-u^{2})&\mbox{if }|u|\leq 1,\\[6.0pt]
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The logarithmic terms describe the entropy of mixing of the binary system
[
33
]
, and the
singularity of
W
at
u=\pm 1
enforces the physical constraint
|u|<1
, ensuring that the order
parameter remains within the physically admissible range of concentrations. In contrast, the quartic
approximation is reasonable only when the quench is shallow, i.e., when the temperature
\theta
is
close to the critical temperature
[
33
]
. Here
\theta>0
is the rescaled temperature of the
system. If
\theta\geq 1
, then
W
is strictly convex and has only one minimizer at
u=0
,
and hence
E
is a convex functional. If
0<\theta<1
, then
u=0
is a local maximizer for

W
and there are two minimizers
\pm u_{\theta}
, where
u_{\theta}
satisfies
0<u_{\theta}<1

and
W(u_{\theta})=W(-u_{\theta})
. We only consider
0<\theta<1
. Also, see
[
6
,
9
]
for works
that focused on homogeneous Neumann or periodic boundary conditions, and for the numerical scheme
with dynamic boundary conditions see
[
27
]
.

In
[
18
]
, Dai, Li, and Luong found that, for the quartic double-well potential, when the
boundary condition is a homogeneous mixture of the two phases, there is a bifurcation phenomenon
mediated by
\kappa
, the thickness of the transition layer. To be more precise, when
\kappa
is
smaller than a critical value which is proportional to the inverse of the smallest eigenvalue of
the negative Laplacian, there will be two nontrivial symmetric minimizers for
E
. In contrast,
when the boundary value is larger (or smaller) than the homogeneous mixture, the symmetry of the
system is broken and there is only one minimizer. Their approach involves explicit calculations that
only work for the quartic potential, and it was left as an open problem whether bifurcation
results of this kind hold for double-well potentials of other forms.

In
[
19
]
, Dai and Ramadan studied the minimizers of the
de Gennes–Cahn–Hilliard energy under strong anchoring conditions. They found that, after a
nonlinear transformation, the de Gennes–Cahn–Hilliard energy can be transformed into a
Cahn–Hilliard energy with a potential that is a trigonometric function. They then took
advantage of the additional structure provided by the trigonometric function to derive bifurcation
results mediated by the transition layer thickness
\kappa
. It was still left open whether
such results extend to double-well potentials of other forms, or whether a unified approach
to generic double-well potentials is possible.

Our goal in this paper is to resolve this open problem for the physically relevant
Flory–Huggins potential. Specifically, we study the minimizers of the Cahn–Hilliard energy
E

(
1
) with the Flory–Huggins potential (
1
) for
0<\theta<1
, under the
homogeneous strong anchoring condition, i.e., the Dirichlet boundary condition
u=0
on

\partial\Omega
. In contrast to the quartic and trigonometric settings, the logarithmic
singularity of the Flory–Huggins potential requires substantially different techniques. Moreover,
the rescaled temperature
\theta
enters as an additional bifurcation parameter alongside the
transition layer thickness
\kappa
, giving rise to a richer bifurcation structure. Our main
result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\lambda_{1}
be the smallest eigenvalue of the negative Laplace operator in
\Omega
.

(i).

If
\kappa\geq(1-\theta)/\lambda_{1}
, then
E
has a unique minimizer
u^{*}\equiv 0

and
E(u^{*})=\frac{|\Omega|}{2}
.

(ii).

If
0<\kappa<(1-\theta)/\lambda_{1}
, then
E
has two nontrivial minimizers
u^{*}_{+}

and
u^{*}_{-}
, with
u^{*}_{-}=-u^{*}_{+}
, and
0<u^{*}_{+}<u_{\theta}
in
\Omega
,
and
E(u^{*})<\frac{|\Omega|}{2}
.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Sections 2–5 are devoted to the proof of
Theorem
1
, each section handling a different aspect of the problem. In Section 2
we discuss approximations and modifications of the Flory–Huggins potential. In Sections 3
and 4 we study minimizers for the Cahn–Hilliard energy with the Flory–Huggins potential and
its approximation using the Nehari manifold. In Section 5 we study properties of minimizers.
In Section 6 we present numerical simulation results. Finally, in Section 7 we discuss
challenges and topics for further exploration.

2
Approximations and modifications

Having stated the main result, we now turn to its proof, which occupies Sections 2–5.
The key difficulty is the logarithmic singularity of the Flory–Huggins potential, which
prevents direct variational arguments. In this section we construct a smooth global
approximation
{\tilde{W}}
of
W
that coincides with
W
on the physically relevant region

|u|\leq u_{\theta}
and shares the same minimizers.

The first technical difficulty we encounter is the singularity of
W
at
u=\pm 1
. To
address this, we separate the singular logarithmic terms from the smooth term. Specifically,
W

can be written as the difference of two convex functions,

\displaystyle W(u)=W_{1}(u)-W_{2}(u),

(4)

where

\displaystyle W_{1}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\theta}{2}\left((1-u)\ln(1-u)+(1+u)\ln(1+u)\right)&\mbox{if }|u|\leq 1,\\[6.0pt]
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

and
W_{2}(u)=\frac{1}{2}(u^{2}-1)
. Note that
-W_{2}(u)=\frac{1}{2}(1-u^{2})
, which is
consistent with the definition (
1
). For
-1<u<1
, we have
W^{\prime}(u)=W_{1}^{\prime}(u)-u
and

W^{\prime\prime}(u)=W_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u)-1
, where

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta}{2}\left(-\ln(1-u)+\ln(1+u)\right)=\theta\left(u+\frac{1}{3}u^{3}+\frac{1}{5}u^{5}+\cdots\right),

(7)

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta}{1-u^{2}}=\theta(1+u^{2}+u^{4}+u^{6}+\cdots).

(8)

Since
W_{1}^{\prime}(u)\to\infty
and
W_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u)\to\infty
as
u\to 1^{-}
, there exists

\hat{u}_{\theta}\in(u_{\theta},1)
such that
W_{1}^{\prime}(u)>100
and
W_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u)>100
for all

\hat{u}_{\theta}\leq u<1
. By the Taylor expansions (
7
) and (
8
),
there exists
k
such that

\displaystyle\theta\left(u+\frac{1}{3}u^{3}+\frac{1}{5}u^{5}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2k+1}u^{2k+1}\right)>100,\quad\theta(1+u^{2}+u^{4}+\cdots+u^{2k})>100

for all
u\geq\hat{u}_{\theta}
. Define a new function

\displaystyle{\tilde{W}_{1}}(u):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}W_{1}(\hat{u}_{\theta})+\displaystyle\int_{\hat{u}_{\theta}}^{u}\theta\left(t+\frac{1}{3}t^{3}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2k+1}t^{2k+1}\right)dt&\mbox{if }u>\hat{u}_{\theta},\\[6.0pt]
W_{1}(u)&\mbox{if }|u|\leq\hat{u}_{\theta},\\[6.0pt]
W_{1}(-\hat{u}_{\theta})+\displaystyle\int_{-\hat{u}_{\theta}}^{u}\theta\left(t+\frac{1}{3}t^{3}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2k+1}t^{2k+1}\right)dt&\mbox{if }u<-\hat{u}_{\theta}.\end{array}\right.

Then
{\tilde{W}_{1}}
is strictly convex in each of the intervals
(-\infty,-\hat{u}_{\theta})
,

(-\hat{u}_{\theta},\hat{u}_{\theta})
, and
(\hat{u}_{\theta},\infty)
. Define

\displaystyle{\tilde{W}}(u):={\tilde{W}_{1}}(u)-W_{2}(u).

(12)

Then
{\tilde{W}}(u)
is defined for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
, with
{\tilde{W}}(u)=W(u)
for all

|u|\leq\hat{u}_{\theta}
and
{\tilde{W}}(u)<W(u)
for all
|u|>\hat{u}_{\theta}
. In addition,
{\tilde{W}}

is strictly convex in
(-\infty,-\hat{u}_{\theta})
and
(\hat{u}_{\theta},\infty)
, respectively.

Lemma 2
.

{\tilde{W}}(u)
has exactly two minimizers
\pm u_{\theta}
, and

{\tilde{W}}(u_{\theta})={\tilde{W}}(-u_{\theta})
.

Proof.

Since
{\tilde{W}}
is an even function and
{\tilde{W}}(u)=W(u)
for all
|u|\leq\hat{u}_{\theta}
, we
only need to show that
{\tilde{W}}
is strictly increasing on
[\hat{u}_{\theta},+\infty)
.
To see this, observe that

{\tilde{W}}^{\prime}(\hat{u}_{\theta}^{+})={\tilde{W}_{1}}^{\prime}(\hat{u}_{\theta}^{+})-\hat{u}_{\theta}=\theta\left(1+\frac{1}{3}\hat{u}_{\theta}^{3}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2k+1}\hat{u}_{\theta}^{2k+1}\right)-\hat{u}_{\theta}>100-1=99>0,

and for
u>\hat{u}_{\theta}
,

\displaystyle{\tilde{W}}^{\prime\prime}(u)=\theta(1+u^{2}+u^{4}+\cdots+u^{2k})>\theta(1+\hat{u}_{\theta}^{2}+\hat{u}_{\theta}^{4}+\cdots+\hat{u}_{\theta}^{2k})>100>0.

Consequently, for all
u>\hat{u}_{\theta}
,

{\tilde{W}}^{\prime}(u)>{\tilde{W}}^{\prime}(\hat{u}_{\theta})>99>0,

and hence
{\tilde{W}}
is strictly increasing on
[\hat{u}_{\theta},+\infty)
.
∎

By a standard cutoff argument, all minimizers of
E
satisfy

-u_{\theta}\leq u(x)\leq u_{\theta}
a.e. in
\Omega
. Define the modified energy
functional

\displaystyle{\tilde{E}}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+{\tilde{W}}(u)\right)\,dx.

(13)

By the same cutoff argument, all minimizers of
{\tilde{E}}
also satisfy

-u_{\theta}\leq u(x)\leq u_{\theta}
a.e. in
\Omega
. This means that
E
and
{\tilde{E}}
have
exactly the same minimizers over
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
E(u)\geq{\tilde{E}}(u)
for all

u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

3
Nehari manifold for
E(u)

With the modified energy
{\tilde{E}}
in hand, we now study the structure of critical points of

E
using the Nehari manifold. Recall that the first variation (Fréchet derivative) of
E

at
u
in the direction
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is defined by

\langle\delta E(u),v\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+W^{\prime}(u)v\right)dx,

so that
\langle\delta E(u),v\rangle=0
for all
v
is the weak form of the Euler–Lagrange
equation (
25
).

Define the Nehari manifold for the energy
E
as

\displaystyle S:=\left\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):|u|<1\mbox{ and }\langle\delta E(u),u\rangle=0\right\}.

(14)

We note that
S
contains all critical points of
E
, since any critical point
u
of
E

satisfies
\langle\delta E(u),v\rangle=0
for all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and in particular for

v=u
. Computing directly, and using integration by parts together with the boundary condition

u=0
on
\partial\Omega
(so the boundary term
\kappa\oint_{\partial\Omega}u\,\partial_{n}u\,dS

vanishes),

\displaystyle\langle\delta E(u),u\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\kappa\Delta u+W^{\prime}(u)u\right)dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}+W^{\prime}(u)u\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}-u^{2}\right)dx+\frac{\theta}{2}\int_{\Omega}u\ln\left(\frac{1+u}{1-u}\right)\,dx.

(15)

Let
\lambda_{1}
be the smallest eigenvalue of the negative Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary
condition. We easily obtain the following (non-sharp) result.

Lemma 3
.

If
\kappa\geq 1/\lambda_{1}
, then
S=\{0\}
and hence
u\equiv 0
is the only minimizer for
E

over
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

For all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, we have
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\geq\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx
, with equality if and only if
u
is an eigenfunction corresponding to

\lambda_{1}
. Hence, if
\kappa\geq 1/\lambda_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}-u^{2})\,dx\geq 0,

(16)

with equality if and only if
\kappa=1/\lambda_{1}
and
u
is an eigenfunction corresponding to

\lambda_{1}
. Since
u\ln\left(\frac{1+u}{1-u}\right)\geq 0
for all
-1<u<1
, with equality if
and only if
u=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\ln\left(\frac{1+u}{1-u}\right)\,dx\geq 0,

(17)

with equality if and only if
u\equiv 0
. Combining these estimates, if
\kappa\geq 1/\lambda_{1}
,
then
\langle\delta E(u),u\rangle>0
unless
u\equiv 0
, i.e.,
S=\{0\}
.
∎

For any
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
with
|u|\leq u_{\theta}
, define

\displaystyle\Phi_{u}(s):=E(su)\quad\mbox{ for all }s\in\mathbb{R}.

(18)

If
u
is a minimizer for
E
, then
s=1
is a minimizer for
\Phi_{u}
. Since
|u|\leq u_{\theta}<1
,
we need only consider
0\leq s<1/u_{\theta}
. Computing,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{u}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left\{s(\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}-u^{2})+\frac{\theta u}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+su}{1-su}\right)\right\}\,dx,

(19)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{u}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}-(1-\theta)u^{2}+\theta s^{2}u^{4}(1+s^{2}u^{2}+s^{4}u^{4}+\cdots)\right)\,dx.

(20)

Using the detailed information provided by (
20
), we can sharpen
Lemma
3
.

Lemma 4
.

If
\kappa\geq(1-\theta)/\lambda_{1}
, then
u\equiv 0
is the only minimizer for
E
over

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and

\min\{E(u):u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\}=\frac{|\Omega|}{2}.

Proof.

By (
19
), we have
\Phi^{\prime}_{u}(0)=0
. If
\kappa\geq(1-\theta)/\lambda_{1}
, then

\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}-(1-\theta)u^{2}\right)dx\geq 0

for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
and all
s>0
such
that
|su|<1
a.e. in
\Omega
, we have
\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{u}(s)>0
and
\Phi^{\prime}_{u}(s)>\Phi^{\prime}_{u}(0)=0
.
Hence
\Phi_{u}(s)
has no positive critical point. Consequently,
E
has no nonzero critical
point, and
u\equiv 0
is the only critical point of
E
and must be the minimizer.
∎

Our next step is to study whether there are nontrivial minimizers when

\kappa<(1-\theta)/\lambda_{1}
. To do so, we determine whether
\Phi_{u}
has a positive
minimizer
s\in(0,1/u_{\theta})
. By the Taylor expansion,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{u}(s)

\displaystyle=s\int_{\Omega}\left\{\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}-(1-\theta)u^{2}+\theta s^{2}u^{4}\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{s^{2}u^{2}}{5}+\cdots\right)\right\}dx.

(21)

This suggests that
\Phi_{u}^{\prime}(s)
may vanish for suitably chosen
u
and
s
.
However, since there is no obvious mechanism to guarantee such
s
to be less than

1/u_{\theta}
, we proceed by studying the Nehari manifold for
{\tilde{E}}
instead, which carries no
such restriction.

4
Nehari manifold for
{\tilde{E}}(u)

The analysis of Section 3 identified an obstruction: there is no obvious way to guarantee
that the critical point
s
of
\Phi_{u}
satisfies
s<1/u_{\theta}
, which is needed for

E(su)
to be finite. We resolve this by working instead with the modified energy
{\tilde{E}}

from Section 2, which is defined on all of
\mathbb{R}
and has no such restriction. The Nehari
manifold for
{\tilde{E}}
is defined as follows.

Define the Nehari manifold for the modified energy
{\tilde{E}}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{S}:=\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\langle\delta{\tilde{E}}(u),u\rangle=0\}.

(22)

For
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, define
\tilde{\Phi}_{u}(s):={\tilde{E}}(su)
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}^{\prime}_{u}(s)

\displaystyle=s\int_{|su|\leq\hat{u}_{\theta}}\left\{\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}-(1-\theta)u^{2}+\theta s^{2}u^{4}\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{(su)^{2}}{5}+\cdots\right)\right\}dx

\displaystyle\quad+s\int_{|su|>\hat{u}_{\theta}}\left\{\kappa|\nabla u|^{2}-(1-\theta)u^{2}+\theta s^{2}u^{4}\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{(su)^{2}}{5}+\cdots+\frac{(su)^{2k-2}}{2k+1}\right)\right\}dx.

(23)

Let
\phi
be an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1}
, so that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx=\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\,dx
. If

\kappa<(1-\theta)/\lambda_{1}
, then

\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa|\nabla\phi|^{2}-(1-\theta)|\phi|^{2}\right)dx<0
. Hence by
(
23
), there exists a small interval
(0,r)
such that
\tilde{\Phi}_{\phi}^{\prime}(s)<0
for
all
s\in(0,r)
. On the other hand, by (
23
),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\phi}^{\prime}(s)>s\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa|\nabla\phi|^{2}-(1-\theta)|\phi|^{2}+\frac{\theta s^{2}\phi^{4}}{3}\right)dx>0

(24)

for any

s>\left(-\dfrac{3\int_{\Omega}\left(\kappa|\nabla\phi|^{2}-(1-\theta)\phi^{2}\right)dx}{\theta\int_{\Omega}\phi^{4}\,dx}\right)^{1/2}.

By the intermediate value theorem, there exists
s_{\phi}>0
such that

s_{\phi}=\sup\{s>0:\tilde{\Phi}_{\phi}^{\prime}(s)<0\}
. Then

{\tilde{E}}(s_{\phi}\phi)=\tilde{\Phi}_{\phi}(s_{\phi})<\tilde{\Phi}_{\phi}(0)={\tilde{E}}(0)=E(0)=\frac{|\Omega|}{2}.

Hence
\min\{{\tilde{E}}(u):u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\}<\frac{|\Omega|}{2}
. As noted above, any
minimizer
u^{*}
of
{\tilde{E}}
must satisfy
u^{*}\in[-u_{\theta},u_{\theta}]
for all
x\in\Omega
.
Hence
u^{*}
is also a minimizer for
E
, since

E(u^{*})={\tilde{E}}(u^{*})\leq{\tilde{E}}(u)\leq E(u)
for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

5
Properties of the Minimizers

Section 4 established the existence of minimizers: when
\kappa<(1-\theta)/\lambda_{1}
,
the energy
E
has at least one nontrivial minimizer
u^{*}
. In this section we analyze the
qualitative properties of these minimizers, showing in particular that they come in a symmetric
pair
\pm u^{*}_{+}
and that
0<u^{*}_{+}<u_{\theta}
pointwise in
\Omega
.

Now that we have established the existence of minimizers for the energy (
1
), we
analyze their properties. In particular, we show that these minimizers are unique within each
sign class.

We define the positive and negative minimizers as
u^{*}_{+}:=|u^{*}|
and
u^{*}_{-}=-|u^{*}|
,
respectively. Clearly
u^{*}_{+},u^{*}_{-}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
; moreover,
W(u^{*}_{+})=W(u^{*}_{-})
,
and hence
E(u^{*}_{+})=E(u^{*}_{-})
. By definition,
u^{*}_{+}\geq 0
,
u^{*}_{-}\leq 0
, and

u^{*}_{+}+u^{*}_{-}=0.

The Euler–Lagrange equation satisfied by
u_{+}^{*}
is

-\kappa\Delta u_{+}^{*}+\frac{\theta}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+u_{+}^{*}}{1-u_{+}^{*}}\right)-u_{+}^{*}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega.

(25)

An analogous equation holds for
u_{-}^{*}
, with
u_{+}^{*}
replaced by
u_{-}^{*}
.
The regularity of
u_{+}^{*}
(and similarly
u_{-}^{*}
) follows from the fact that for each

\theta\in(0,1)
there exists
C_{1},C_{2}
depending only on
\theta
such that

\frac{\theta}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+u_{+}^{*}}{1-u_{+}^{*}}\right)\leq C_{1}u_{+}^{*}+C_{2}(u_{+}^{*})^{3}.

Thus, via the Sobolev embeddings and a bootstrap argument, we have
u_{+}^{*}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,\beta}(\bar{\Omega})
with
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
for
n=3
and

\beta\in(0,1)
for
n=2.

5.1
Maximum principle

The positive minimizer
u_{+}^{*}
satisfies the boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\kappa\Delta u_{+}^{*}+\frac{\theta}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+u_{+}^{*}}{1-u_{+}^{*}}\right)-u_{+}^{*}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u_{+}^{*}&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
u_{+}^{*}&\geq 0&&\text{in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(26)

Lemma 5
.

The positive minimizer
u_{+}^{*}
satisfying (
26
) satisfies
u_{+}^{*}>0
in

\Omega.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

u_{+}^{*}(x_{0})=0.
Since
u_{+}^{*}
satisfies (
26
),
a Taylor expansion of
\frac{\theta}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+u_{+}^{*}}{1-u_{+}^{*}}\right)
around

0
gives

-\kappa\Delta u_{+}^{*}+\frac{\theta}{3}(u_{+}^{*})^{3}+\frac{\theta}{5}(u_{+}^{*})^{5}+\cdots=(1-\theta)u_{+}^{*}\geq 0.

Setting
c:=\frac{\theta}{3}(u_{+}^{*})^{2}+\frac{\theta}{5}(u_{+}^{*})^{4}+\cdots>0
, we
obtain

-\kappa\Delta u_{+}^{*}+cu_{+}^{*}\geq(1-\theta)u_{+}^{*}\geq 0.

By the strong maximum principle,
u_{+}^{*}\equiv 0.
This contradicts the assumption that

u_{+}^{*}
is a nontrivial minimizer, and hence
u_{+}^{*}>0
in
\Omega
.
∎

Next, we show that
u_{+}^{*}<u_{\theta}
in
\Omega
. Since
u_{+}^{*}=0
on

\partial\Omega
, the function
u_{+}^{*}
attains its maximum at some interior
point
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Evaluating (
25
) at
x_{0}
, where
\Delta u_{+}^{*}(x_{0})\leq 0
, gives

\frac{\theta}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+u_{+}^{*}(x_{0})}{1-u_{+}^{*}(x_{0})}\right)=\kappa\Delta u_{+}^{*}(x_{0})+u_{+}^{*}(x_{0})\leq u_{+}^{*}(x_{0}).

Suppose for contradiction that there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

u_{+}^{*}(x_{0})=u_{\theta}
, and define
w=u_{+}^{*}-u_{\theta}.
Then
w(x_{0})=0
and

-1<-u_{\theta}\leq w\leq 0.
The function
w
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle-\kappa\Delta w

\displaystyle=-\frac{\theta}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+w+u_{\theta}}{1-w-u_{\theta}}\right)-(w+u_{\theta})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\theta}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+u_{+}^{*}}{1-u_{+}^{*}}\right)+u_{+}^{*}\geq 0.

By the strong maximum principle,
w\equiv 0,
and hence

u_{+}^{*}\equiv u_{\theta}.
This contradicts the nontriviality of
u_{+}^{*}
, and therefore

0<u_{+}^{*}<u_{\theta}
in
\Omega
. Since
u_{\theta}<1
by definition, the bound
u_{+}^{*}<1
follows as
well. A symmetric argument applies to
u_{-}^{*}
.

The uniqueness of minimizers follows from Lemma 5.1 in
[
19
]
.

7
Discussion

This work investigates the minimizers of the Cahn–Hilliard energy functional with the
Flory–Huggins potential, often referred to as the logarithmic potential. The boundary
condition considered here is the Dirichlet boundary condition. The energy functional depends on two
key parameters:
\kappa
, which can be interpreted as the transition layer thickness
parameter, and
\theta
, which controls the temperature and hence the strength of the
nonlinearity.

Our analysis reveals a bifurcation phenomenon for the minimizers when the prescribed boundary value
is a homogeneous mixture of the two phases. Depending on the parameter regime, the minimizer
is either unique or there exist two distinct minimizers. The onset of bifurcation depends on the
interplay between
\kappa
,
\theta
, and the smallest eigenvalue of the negative Laplacian.

Similar analyses of minimizers have been carried out in previous work. For instance, Dai, Li, and
Luong
[
18
]
studied the Cahn–Hilliard energy with a quartic potential under the same
anchoring conditions. In that setting, the nonlinearity was smoother and technically easier to
handle. More recently, Dai and Ramadan
[
19
]
considered the
de Gennes–Cahn–Hilliard energy, where the challenge arose from the singular nature of the
potential in combination with dispersion; the authors overcame this difficulty through a
suitable nonlinear transformation.

In the present article, the logarithmic nonlinearity introduces singular behavior that
requires substantially different techniques. At the same time, the temperature parameter
\theta

plays a crucial role: depending on its value, the qualitative properties of minimizers vary,
and
\theta
enters as an additional bifurcation parameter not present in the quartic or
trigonometric settings. Furthermore, the methods developed here can be extended to other boundary
conditions, such as Neumann or dynamic boundary conditions, or mixed boundary conditions
where different parts of the boundary satisfy different constraints. Such generalizations may lead
to new and potentially richer properties of the minimizers.

Our theoretical analysis is supported by the numerical simulations presented in Section 6.
Specifically, we benchmark against the example in
[
18
]
, with the quartic
potential replaced by the Flory–Huggins potential. Our numerical findings are
consistent with the observations in
[
18
]
. The choice of the Flory–Huggins potential
introduces the additional temperature parameter
\theta
, and we report on the impacts of varying
both the transition layer thickness and the temperature on the minimizers. The singular and
nonlinear nature of the logarithmic potential poses interesting numerical
challenges, particularly in the design of robust numerical schemes. Coupled with the
incorporation of random initial data and the need for long-time simulations
, a careful numerical analysis of this problem remains an interesting open problem.
