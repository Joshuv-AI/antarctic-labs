A note on the Erdös minimal area problem
Subhajit Ghosh, Koushik Ramachandran

Abstract
. We answer a question of Erdös, Herzog, and Piranian [ 2 , Problem 4 4 , page 135 135 ] on the minimal area of polynomial lemniscates when all the zeros of the polynomial are constrained to lie on a compact set K K whose logarithmic capacity is strictly larger than 1 1 .



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a complex polynomial. The unit lemniscate of
p
is defined to be the set
\Lambda_{p}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|p(z)|<1\}
. We note that
\Lambda_{p}
is a bounded open set and hence its area is non-zero. Let
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a compact set. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}},
let
\mathscr{P}_{n}(K)
denote the space of
monic
polynomials of degree
n
, all of whose zeros lie in the compact
K,
i.e.,

\mathscr{P}_{n}(K)=\{p(z):p(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_{j}),\hskip 3.61371pt\mbox{where}\hskip 3.61371ptz_{j}\in K\hskip 3.61371pt\mbox{for}\hskip 3.61371pt1\leq j\leq n\}

Let
m
denote Lebesgue measure in the complex plane. Following in the vein of
[
2
]
we consider the minimal lemniscate area for the constrained problem and define

\displaystyle A_{n}(K):=\inf_{p\in\mathscr{P}_{n}(K)}m(\Lambda_{p}),\qquad\mu(F):=\inf_{n}A_{n}(K)

In
[
2
, Problem
4
, page
135
]
Erdös, Herzog, and Piranian posed questions regarding the relationship between
\mu(K)
and the logarithmic capacity (equivalently, the transfinite diameter)
cap(K)
. They asked

(1)

Is
\mu(K)
uniquely determined by the logarithmic capacity
cap(K)
?

(2)

Is it true that
\mu(K)=0
whenever
cap(K)\geq 1
?

This problem is also listed as Problem
1040
in the recently curated Erdös problems website
[
1
]
. In this short note, we answer both questions (the second one partially). First we prove that
(1)
does not hold in general. Recently, in the preprint
[
4
]
, with the help of an AI tool, it was shown that there exist two countably infinite compact sets (hence having capacity zero),
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, with
\mu(K_{1})\neq\mu(K_{2})
. Here, we show that given
t\in(0,1)
it is quite easy to construct compacts
K_{1},K_{2}
with

cap(K_{1})=cap(K_{2})=t,\quad\text{yet}\quad\mu(K_{1})\neq\mu(K_{2}),

These examples illustrate that not only the capacity but other geometric/metric features of
K
also play a role in determining
\mu(K)
.

Our main result, Theorem
3.1
below, shows that
(2)
is true when
cap(K)\geq 1+\varepsilon
for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
, without any other restrictions on the regularity of
K
. We in fact prove that in this case, the well known Fekete polynomials of degree
n
have lemniscate areas going to zero as
n
tends to infinity.

It is worthwhile to remark here that when
cap(K)=1
, the Fekete polynomials do not necessarily have small areas for their lemniscates. To see this, just consider
K=\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
, where
p_{n}^{*}(z)=z^{n}-1
are the unique (upto rotation) Fekete polynomials. Now it is known that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the corresponding lemniscates
\Lambda_{{p_{n}^{*}}}
have area bounded away from
0
. In their paper
[
2
]
, the authors proved that
\mu(\overline{{\mathbb{D}}})=0
. However even for
K=\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
, there are no explicit examples of a sequence
p_{n}\in\mathscr{P}_{n}(K)
with
m(\Lambda_{p_{n}})\to 0
. This already hints that the case when
K
has capacity
1
is a bit more delicate to handle. However see the recent preprint
[
6
, Theorem
6
]
where the authors have shown using an approximation argument that when
K
is the closure of a bounded
C^{2}
smooth domain with
cap(K)=1
, one has
\mu(K)=0
. The result for general capacity one sets remains open. .

2.
Capacity does not determine
\mu(K)

In
[
3
]
, Erdös and Netanyahu proved that if the compact set
K
is
connected
and
cap(K)\in(0,1)
, then
\Lambda_{p}
contains a disk of positive radius
r
, where
r
depends only on
cap(K)
. In particular, let us take
K=\overline{B(0,t)}
. It is well known that
cap(K)=t
. It follows from
[
3
]
that

\mu(K)\geq\operatorname{Area}(B(0,r(t)))=\pi r(t)^{2}.

We now give an example of a disconnected compact set
K
with capacity
t\in(0,1)
for which
\mu(K)
can be made smaller than any prescribed positive number.

Example 2.1
.

Let
t\in(0,1)
and
k\geq 2
be fixed. Let
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(t,k)>0
be a constant to be chosen later. Consider the symmetric intervals
B_{1}=[k,k+\varepsilon]
and
B_{2}=[-k-\varepsilon,-k]
. Set
B:=B_{1}\cup B_{2}.

By
[
8
, Corollary 5.2.6]
,

cap(B)=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(2k+\varepsilon)\varepsilon}.

Now we choose
\varepsilon>0
so that
cap(B)=t
. Next, consider the sequence of polynomials

p_{n}(z)=\begin{cases}(z-k)^{l}(z+k)^{l},&\text{if }n=2l,\\[2.84526pt]
(z-k)^{l+1}(z+k)^{l},&\text{if }n=2l+1.\end{cases}

We claim that

(1)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{p_{n}}\subset B\!\left(-k,\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\cup B\!\left(k,\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right).\

Assume for a moment that (
1
) holds. Then for all
n
we will have

A_{n}(K)\leq\frac{2\pi}{k}
, which in turn implies that
\mu(K)\leq\frac{2\pi}{k}.
Since
k
can be taken arbitrarily large, this will yield our example. For the proof of the claim, suppose
z\notin B\!\left(-k,\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\cup B\!\left(k,\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)
. Then
w=\min\{|z-k|,|z+k|\}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
. Now we handle two cases. If
w>\sqrt{k}
,

|p_{n}(z)|>\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}|z-k|^{l}|z+k|^{l}>\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}k^{l}>1.

Hence
z\notin\Lambda_{p_{n}}
. On the other hand if
w\leq\sqrt{k}
, say
|z-k|\leq\sqrt{k}
, then by the triangle inequality
|z+k|\geq 2k-w\geq k.
Therefore

|p_{n}(z)|>\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}|z-k|^{l}|z+k|^{l}>\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}|2k-w|^{l}w^{l}>\frac{k^{l}}{(\sqrt{k})^{l+1}}>1.

Hence once again we have
z\notin\Lambda_{p_{n}}
and the proof of (
1
) is complete.

3.
Main theorem

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
t>1
be fixed. Let
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a compact set with
cap(K)\geq t
. Then there exists a constant
\rho=\rho(t)>0
such that for all large enough
n
, we have

A_{n}(K)\leq\exp(-\rho n)

In particular,
\mu(K)=0
.

Remark 3.2
.

A very simple argument shows that
\exp(-cn)
is the right order of decay for
A_{n}(K)
. First recall that
cap(K)\leq\frac{d}{2}
, where
d
is the diameter of
K
. Hence
d\geq 2t>2
. Now let
p(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_{j})
be in
\mathscr{P}_{n}(K)
. If
z\in B\left(z_{1},\frac{1}{d^{n}}\right)
, then
|p(z)|\leq\frac{1}{d^{n}}\left(d+\frac{1}{d^{n}}\right)^{n-1}=\frac{1}{d}(1+o(1))<1
. Hence
B\left(z_{1},\frac{1}{d^{n}}\right)\subset\Lambda_{p}
. This yields
m(\Lambda_{p})\geq\frac{\pi}{d^{2n}}
which in turn gives
A_{n}(K)\geq\exp(-\kappa_{d}n)
.

Proof.

The proof is adapted from the argument in
[
5
, Theorem
2.2
(b)]
. We begin by recalling that a Fekete
n
-tuple of
K
is any
n
-tuple
(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})
which realizes the supremum

\sup_{j,k\leq n:j<k}\left\{\prod|w_{j}-w_{k}|^{\frac{2}{n(n-1)}}:w_{1},w_{2},...w_{n}\in K\right\}.

We refer to
[
8
, Section
5.5
]
for more on Fekete points and its connection to potential theory. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{z_{k,n}\}_{k=1}^{n}
denote a Fekete
n
-tuple for the compact set
K
. Let
p_{n}
denote the corresponding Fekete polynomial of degree
n
defined by

\displaystyle p_{n}(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z-z_{k,n}),\quad\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

We prove that the
\{|p_{n}|<1\}
has small area. As a first step, we show that for
1\leq j\leq n,
the size of the derivative
\Big|p^{{}^{\prime}}_{n}(z_{j,n})\Big|
is exponentially large using properties of the Fekete points. Let us consider a zero, say
z_{1,n}
. Define

\displaystyle q_{1}(z):=\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{(z-z_{j,n})}{(z_{1,n}-z_{j,n})}.

From the definition of Fekete points we infer that for each
z\in K
, the product of the pairwise distances between the
n
points
\{z,z_{2,n},z_{3,n},\ldots,z_{n,n}\}
is not larger than the corresponding product for the Fekete
n
-tuple. This implies that

\sup_{z\in K}\prod_{j=2}^{n}|z-z_{j,n}|\leq\prod_{j=2}^{n}\left|z_{1,n}-z_{j,n}\right|.

Now the denominator in the expression for
q_{1}
is simply
p_{n}^{\prime}(z_{1,n})
. Using this along with the previous estimate, we obtain

(2)

\displaystyle|p_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(z_{1,n})|=\prod_{j=2}^{n}\left|z_{1,n}-z_{j,n}\right|\geq\sup_{z\in K}\prod_{j=2}^{n}|z-z_{j,n}|\geq cap(K)^{n-1}\geq t^{n-1},

where the second inequality in (
2
) follows from the fact that the sup norm of a monic polynomial of degree
d
on a compact
K
is at least
cap(K)^{d}
, see
[
8
, Theorem
5.5.4
]
. This establishes the largeness of the derivative at
z_{1,n}
.

Next, denote by
G_{1}
the connected component of
\Lambda_{p_{n}}
that contains
z_{1,n}
. Let
d_{1}
be the diameter of
G_{1}
. Then by Bernstein’s inequality
[
7
]
, for some absolute constant
A>0
, one has

(3)

\displaystyle t^{n-1}\leq|p_{n}^{\prime}(z_{1,n})|\leq\frac{An^{2}||p_{n}||_{G_{1}}}{d_{1}}=\frac{An^{2}}{d_{1}}.

This shows
d_{1}\leq\exp(-\alpha n)
for some
\alpha=\alpha(t)>0
. Since
G_{1}\subset B(z_{1,n},d_{1})
, it follows that
m(G_{1})\leq\exp(-\beta(t)n)
. A similar area estimate obviously holds for every component of
\Lambda_{p_{n}}
. Since
\Lambda_{p_{n}}
can have at most
n
connected components, the preceding estimates along with the subadditivity of area gives
m(\Lambda_{p_{n}})\leq\exp(-\rho n)
for some positive constant
\rho=\rho(t)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

The proof above shows that the result continues to hold if instead of minimizing the area of
\{|p|<1\}
, we consider the corresponding minimum area (for
p\in\mathscr{P}_{n}(K)
) of lemniscates
\{|p|<l_{n}\}
, where
l_{n}
can grow as fast as
\exp(n^{\gamma})
for
\gamma<1
.
