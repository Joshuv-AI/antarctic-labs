Descendant and Fourier-Mukai equivalences for simple flops
Jiun-Cheng Chen, Hsian-Hua Tseng

Abstract
. For a simple flop X ⇢ X ′ X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime} , we construct a correspondence between genus 0 0 descendant Gromov-Witten theories of X X and X ′ X^{\prime} . We show that the Fourier-Mukai equivalence induced by X ⇢ X ′ X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime} is compatible, in a precise sense, with the descendant correspondence.

\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{\prime}_{0}
. Therefore, we have

(3.3)

\text{ch}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0})|_{\text{Bl}_{Z^{\prime}}X^{\prime}})+\text{ch}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0})|_{\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\prime}}(N_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})})=\Phi^{\prime}(\text{ch}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}))).

Since the birational map (
2.7
) is the identity on the first two factors, we have

\text{ch}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0})|_{\text{Bl}_{Z^{\prime}}X^{\prime}})=\text{ch}(\mathcal{F}_{0})|_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}.

Applying the above argument to the sheaf
\Omega_{\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}/\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\vee}
, we have

(3.4)

\Phi(\Gamma(T_{X}))=\Gamma(T_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X})+\Gamma(T_{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}),

and similarly for
\Gamma(T_{X^{\prime}})
. Also,

(3.5)

\Phi(c_{1}(T_{X}))=c_{1}(T_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X})+c_{1}(T_{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}),

and similarly for
c_{1}(T_{X^{\prime}})
. This implies the compatibility of
\rho^{X}
(respectively
\rho^{X^{\prime}}
) with
\Phi
(respectively
\Phi^{\prime}
).

The compatibility of
\mu^{X}
and
\text{deg}_{0}^{X}
(respectively
\mu^{X^{\prime}}
and
\text{deg}_{0}^{X^{\prime}}
) with
\Phi
(respectively
\Phi^{\prime}
) concerns grading and is straightforward:

(3.6)

\Phi\circ\mu^{X}=(\mu^{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}\oplus\mu^{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})})\circ\Phi,\quad\Phi\circ\text{deg}_{0}^{X}=(\text{deg}_{0}^{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}\oplus\text{deg}_{0}^{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})})\circ\Phi,

and similarly for
X^{\prime}
.

The map
\mathbb{U}
is constructed to satisfy

(3.7)

\mathbb{U}S_{X,\tau}^{-1}=S_{X^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}^{-1}.

We need to study (
3.7
) under the embeddings
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
.

As noted above,
\mathbb{U}
does not depend on any quantum variable. Therefore, in (
3.7
), we can set to
0
all quantum variables other than
q
and
q^{\prime}
(which index the flopping curves). Then, Gromov-Witten invariants of
X
appearing in (
3.7
) are of the form

\langle\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{k}/(z-\psi)\rangle_{0,\text{extremal}}^{X},

namely, those Gromov-Witten invariants whose degrees are multiples of the class of a line in
Z\subset X
. The flopping curves in
X
are contained in
Z\subset X
, with normal bundle
N_{Z/X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(-1)^{\oplus r+1}
. Therefore, flopping curves are constrained to lie in the divisor
\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}
in the degeneration
\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The divisor
\tilde{\mathfrak{Z}}
meets the special fiber
\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}
only at the factor
\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})
and is disjoint from the section
\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X})
where the two pieces
\text{Bl}_{Z}X
and
\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})
glued. This implies that only the factor
\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})
contributes when evaluating
\langle\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{k}/(z-\psi)\rangle_{0,\text{extremal}}^{X}
by applying the degeneration formula
[
18
]
associated to the family
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We can also choose
\tau\in H^{*}(X)
so that the
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z}))
-summand of
\Phi(\tau)
is not zero. Then, when restricted to the
q
-direction, applying the degeneration formula for Gromov-Witten invariants to
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
implies that the solution
S_{X,\tau}^{-1}
satisfies

\Phi S_{X,\tau}^{-1}|_{\text{extremal}}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}&0\\
0&S_{\mathbb{P},\tau_{\mathbb{P}}}^{-1}|_{\text{extremal}}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\Phi.

Here,
|_{\text{extremal}}
indicates setting all quantum variables to
0
except
q
.

\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})=\mathbb{P}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\prime}}(N_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})

is the projective local model described in (
0.3
).
S_{\mathbb{P},\tau_{\mathbb{P}}}
is the corresponding fundamental solution to the quantum differential equation for
\mathbb{P}
.
\tau_{\mathbb{P}}\in H^{*}(\mathbb{P})
is the
H^{*}(\mathbb{P})
-summand of
\Phi(\tau)
.

By a similar argument, when restricted to the
q^{\prime}
-direction, we have

\Phi^{\prime}S_{X^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}^{-1}|_{\text{extremal}}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}_{\text{Bl}_{Z^{\prime}}X^{\prime}}&0\\
0&S_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime},\tau_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}}^{-1}|_{\text{extremal}}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\Phi^{\prime}.

Here,
|_{\text{extremal}}
indicates setting all quantum variables to
0
except
q^{\prime}
.
S_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime},\tau_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}}
is the corresponding fundamental solution to the quantum differential equation for
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
.
\tau_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}\in H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
is the
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{\prime})
-summand of
\Phi^{\prime}(\tau^{\prime})
.

It follows that, after setting all quantum variables to
0
except
q
and
q^{\prime}
, (
3.7
) can be written as

(3.8)

\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}&0\\
0&\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}|_{\text{extremal}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}&0\\
0&S_{\mathbb{P},\tau_{\mathbb{P}}}^{-1}|_{\text{extremal}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}_{\text{Bl}_{Z^{\prime}}X^{\prime}}&0\\
0&S_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime},\tau_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}}^{-1}|_{\text{extremal}}\end{pmatrix}

after applying the embeddings
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}
. Namely,

(3.9)

\Phi^{\prime}\mathbb{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}&0\\
0&\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\Phi.

The equality
\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}S_{\mathbb{P},\tau_{\mathbb{P}}}^{-1}|_{\text{extremal}}=S_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime},\tau_{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}}}^{-1}|_{\text{extremal}}
means that
\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}
is the symplectic transformation for the projective local model. Since the projective local model is toric,
\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}
is constructed as a special case of
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Putting everything together, we have

(3.10)

\begin{split}\Phi^{\prime}\Psi^{X^{\prime}}(\mathbb{FM}(\alpha))&=\Psi^{\text{Bl}_{Z^{\prime}}X^{\prime}}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0})|_{\text{Bl}_{Z^{\prime}}X^{\prime}})+\Psi^{\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\prime}}(N_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0})|_{\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\prime}}(N_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})})\\
&=\Psi^{\text{Bl}_{Z^{\prime}}X^{\prime}}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}))+\Psi^{\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\prime}}(N_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}))\\
&=\Psi^{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X})+\Psi^{\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\prime}}(N_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}))\end{split}

and

(3.11)

\begin{split}\Phi^{\prime}\mathbb{U}\Psi^{X}(\alpha)&=\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}&0\\
0&\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\Phi\Psi^{X}(\alpha)\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}&0\\
0&\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}\end{pmatrix}(\Psi^{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X})+\Psi^{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}))\\
&=\Psi^{\text{Bl}_{Z}X}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\text{Bl}_{Z}X})+\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}\Psi^{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}).\end{split}

Since
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
are embeddings, (
3.1
) follows from

(3.12)

\Psi^{\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\prime}}(N_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})}(\mathbb{FM}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\mathbb{P}_{Z^{\prime}}(N_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})}))=\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{P}}\Psi^{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}(\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{\mathbb{P}_{Z}(N_{Z/X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z})}),

which is a special case of
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
concerning crepant toric wall-crossings. This completes the proof.

Remark 3.1
.

A class of K-equivalence generalizing simple flops is
ordinary flops

[
16
]
. For an ordinary flop, the exceptional loci are projective
bundles
over a smooth base. The invariance of genus
0
Gromov-Witten theory under ordinary flops has been proved in
[
17
,
14
]
. The projective local model of an ordinary flop is a double projective bundle, which is an example of toric bundles in the sense of
[
23
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
13
]
. For
split
toric bundles, descendant correspondence between genus
0
Gromov-Witten theory under crepant toric wall-crossings has been studied in
[
2
]
. An extension of
[
2
]
to non-split toric bundles should be possible, in light of
[
13
]
. Therefore, the approach in this paper can be used to construct the diagram (
0.2
) for ordinary flops of split type and prove its commutativity. In this paper, we want to emphasize our approach to the diagram (
0.2
) and do not pursue the more general case.
