Rigidity and gap theorems for Ricci shrinkers
Pak-Yeung Chan, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
We prove local versions of the Ricci curvature and ν \nu -entropy gap theorems for Ricci shrinkers, which respectively generalize a previous result of Munteanu-Wang [ MW11 ] and a prior result of the authors with Ma [ CMZ25 ] . The key point is that these local gaps depend only on the dimension and not on the global entropy or any other geometric information of the Ricci shrinker. As an application, we provide a local criterion for removable Type I singularities of the Ricci flow.



1
Introduction

A Ricci shrinker is a tuple
(M^{n},g,f)
of a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and a smooth potential function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
, satisfying the equation

\displaystyle\Ric+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g.

(1.1)

In this article, we always normalize the potential function such that the following equation holds:

\displaystyle|\nabla f|^{2}+R=f.

(1.2)

By
[
CZ10
]
, the potential function
f
always admits a minimum point
p
. Since this point plays a central role in the study of Ricci shrinkers, we will use the tuple
(M,g,f,p)
to represent a shrinker.

The most trivial example of shrinkers is the gaussian shrinker
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{e},|x|^{2}/4,0)
, where
g_{e}
is the standard flat metric on the Euclidean space. The rigidity of Gaussian shrinkers has attracted considerable attention in the literature. For instance, Carrillo-Ni
[
CN09
]
and Yokota
[
Yo12
]
proved a gap theorem for the gaussian shrinker with respect to Perelman’s
\mu
-functional
[
Per02
]
. This result was later localized by the authors and Ma
[
CMZ25
]
. On the other hand, Li-Wang
[
LW20
]
studied the rigidity of the gaussian shrinker with respect to the Gromov-Hausdorff distance. They proved that a normalized shrinker, if close enough to the gaussian shrinker in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense, must be isometric to the gaussian shrinker. For other rigidity results of shrinkers, especially of the cylindrical shrinkers, see
[
LW24
,
CM25
,
LZ23
,
WW25
,
Zh18
,
Zh20
]
.

In this article, we continue studying the rigidity of the gaussian shrinker. We shall find conditions as local and weak as possible that are sufficient to force a shrinker to be flat. Our work is inspired by
[
LLW21
]
. Indeed, though not explicitly stated, the arguments of
[
LLW21
]
imply the following strong rigidity result
of the gaussian shrinker.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
LLW21
]
)
.

There is a positive constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n)>0
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g,f,p)
be a Ricci shrinker, where
p
is a minimum point of
f
. Suppose

\displaystyle d_{\operatorname{GH}}\Big(B(p,1),B_{e}(1)\Big)<\varepsilon,

where
d_{\operatorname{GH}}
is the Gromov-Hausdorff distance, and
B_{e}(1)
is the unit ball in the Euclidean space,
then
(M^{n},g,f,p)
is the gaussian shrinker
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{e},|x|^{2}/4,0)
.

It is interesting to ask, whether we can find other local conditions that are sufficient to force a shrinker to be gaussian. Naber
[
Na10
, Theorem 8.1]
proved that a complete Ricci shrinker must be gaussian if
\frac{1}{2}>\Ric\geq 0
everywhere. Munteanu-Wang
[
MW11
, Corollary 1.1]
showed that a shrinker is flat if
|{\Ric}|\leq\frac{1}{100n}
globally on
M
. The authors and Ma
[
CMZ25
]
proved that a shrinker with sufficiently large curvature radius (depending only on the dimension) at a minimum point of
f
must be flat. In light of these results, as well as those of Li-Li-Wang
[
LLW21
]
, it is natural to ask whether the same conclusion holds under a small local Ricci curvature bound near a minimum point of
f
. We show that this is indeed the case.

Theorem 1.2
.

There is a positive constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n)>0
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g,f,p)
be a Ricci shrinker, where
p
is a minimum point of
f
. Suppose

\displaystyle\Ric\leq\varepsilon g\quad\text{ on }\quad B(p,1),

then
(M^{n},g,f,p)
is the gaussian shrinker
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{e},|x|^{2}/4,0)
.

Remarks.

1.

Since on a shrinker, we always have
R>0
by a result of Chen
[
Che09
]
, it makes no difference to assume
|{\Ric}|\leq\varepsilon
or
\Ric\leq\varepsilon g
; we will freely implement the former assumption in our argument.

2.

According to our argument, the theorem can be improved straightforwardly to the following: For any
r>0
, there is an
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(r,n)>0
, such that if

\displaystyle\Ric\leq\varepsilon g\quad\text{ on }\quad B(p,r),

then the shrinker is gaussian.

3.

Li-Li-Wang
[
LLW21
, Theorem 1.3]
obtained a local gap theorem for the scalar curvature of closed shrinkers, where the gap also depends on the entropy. Wang-Wang
[
WW25
]
established a rigidity theorem for shrinkers in terms of the volume ratio on a large ball centered at
p
.

In
[
CMZ25
]
, it is also proved that a shrinker with sufficiently small local
\mu
-functional in a sufficiently large ball at the minimum point of the potential function must be flat. We will also provide an improvement of this theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

There is a positive constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n)>0
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g,f,p)
be a Ricci shrinker, where
p
is the minimum point. Suppose

\displaystyle\nu\Big(B(p,1),g,1\Big)\geq-\varepsilon,

then
(M^{n},g,f,p)
is the gaussian shrinker
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{e},|x|^{2}/4,0)
.

Since, by
[
EMT11
]
, smooth Ricci shrinkers are singularity models (fixed-point blow-up limits) of Type I Ricci flows, and since a flat blow-up limit implies that the base point is not a singular point
[
EMT11
, Theorem 1.1]
, we may apply Theorem
1.2
to obtain the following result concerning removable singularities for Type I Ricci flows.

Theorem 1.4

(A local condition for removable Type I singularities)
.

There is a dimensional constant
\delta=\delta(n)>0
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in[-T,0)}
be a Type I Ricci flow (that is,
|{\Rm_{g_{t}}}|\leq C/|t|
for some
C
) on a close manifold
M^{n}
. Assume
x\in M
is a point such that
r_{c}(x,t)\geq 6n\sqrt{|t|}
for all
t\in[-T,0)
, where

\displaystyle r_{c}(x,t):=\sup\{r>0\,:\,\Ric_{g_{t}}\leq\delta r^{-2}g_{t}\ \text{ on }\ B_{g_{t}}(x,r)\}.

Then
|{\Rm_{g_{t}}}|(x)
remains bounded as
t\to T
. In other words,
x
is not a singular point according to the definition of
[
EMT11
]
.

Remarks.

1.

Note that the constant
\delta=\delta(n)
is a dimensional constant, and it is in particular independent of the Type-I-coefficient
C
. On the other hand, a global version of the theorem can be deduced from
[
MW11
]
, namely, if
r_{c}(x,t)\geq\sqrt{|t|}
for
all

(x,t)\in M\times[-T,0)
, then the flow does not admit any singularity at
t=0
.

2.

One may also apply Theorem
1.3
to obtain a condition of removable singularities formulated with the local
\nu
-functional. However, this result is not different from that which one can deduce from Perelman’s pseudolocality theorem
[
Per02
, Theorem 10.1]
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we summarize the main techniques for the proof of our main theorems.

2.1
Harmonic radius estimate

First of all, we introduce an
\varepsilon
-regularity due to Anderson
[
And90
]
. Let us recall the definition of harminic radius.

Definition 2.1

(Harmonic radius)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and
p\in M
. The
(Q,\alpha)
-harmonic radius
r_{H}(p)
at
p
, where
Q>1
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, is defined to be the maximum radius
r
such that:

1.

there is a harmonic coordinate chart
x=(x^{1},x^{2},\ldots,x^{n}):B(p,r)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, each coordinate
x^{i}
is a harmonic
function;

2.

in this chart
, it holds that:

•

\displaystyle Q^{-1}\delta_{ij}\leq g_{ij}\leq Qg_{ij}
;

•

\displaystyle\sup_{x}r|\partial g_{ij}(x)|+\sup_{x\neq y}r^{1+\alpha}\frac{|\partial g_{ij}(x)-\partial g_{ij}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\leq Q-1
.

Henceforth throughout the whole paper, we shall
fix the parameters
Q
and
\alpha
(for instance, we fix
\alpha=1/2
,
Q=2
), and will refer to
r_{H}(p)
as the harmonic radius at
p
. Should it be ambiguous, we would also use the notation
r_{H}(p,g)
to emphasize the metric under consideration. The following result is essentially the localization of
[
And90
, Theorem 3.2, Remark 3.3]
. For a proof of the local statement, see
[
H19
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 2.2

(
\varepsilon
-regularity)
.

There is a positive number
\delta=\delta(n)>0
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete manifold and
p\in M
a point. Suppose

\displaystyle|B(p,1)|\geq(1-\delta)\omega_{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad|{\Ric}|\leq\delta\quad\text{ on }\quad B(p,1),

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space, then we have

\displaystyle r_{H}(p)\geq\delta.

2.2
Conical function and approximate cone

In the Cheeger-Colding theory
[
CC97
,
CC00a
,
CC00b
]
, it is known that a tangent cone at any point of a noncollapsed Ricci limit space is a metric cone. This result can be more clearly formulated through the construction of a conical function. Let us recall the definition of conical functions.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and
p\in M
a point. A function
h:B(p,r)\to\mathbb{R}
is called an
(\varepsilon,r)
-conical function at
p
, if the following hold

1.

\Delta h=2n
;

2.

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(p,r)}|\nabla^{2}h-2g|^{2}\,dg\leq\varepsilon
;

3.

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(p,r)}||\nabla h|^{2}-4h|\,dg\leq\varepsilon r^{2}
;

4.

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(p,r)}|\nabla(h-d^{2}(p,\cdot))|\,dg\leq\varepsilon r
;

5.

\displaystyle\sup_{B(p,r)}|h-d^{2}(p,\cdot)|\leq\varepsilon r^{2}
,

where
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int
is the average integral.

If on a ball the Ricci curvature is almost nonnegative, and there exists a conical function, then the ball is close to a metric cone in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense. This is originally proved in
[
CC96
]
; see also
[
Ch01
]
. A clearer proof of the following theorem can be found in Jiang’s notes
[
J
]
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
CC96
]
, see also
[
Ch01
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete manifold. Assume that there is an
(\varepsilon,r)
-conical function at some
p\in M
. Furthermore, assume
\Ric\geq-(n-1)\varepsilon r^{-2}
on
B(p,r)
. Then there is a metric cone
(C,z^{*})
, with
z^{*}
being the tip, such that

\displaystyle d_{\operatorname{GH}}\Big(B(p,r/2),B(z^{*},r/2)\Big)\leq\Psi(\varepsilon)r.

Note that in this paper, we adopt the notation
\Psi(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\,|\,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots)
to represent a positive number such that

\displaystyle\Psi(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\,|\,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots)\to 0\qquad\text{ as }\qquad a_{1}\to 0,a_{2}\to 0,\ldots\ \text{ while }\ b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\ \text{ remain fixed.}

2.3
Structure of shrinker limit spaces

In
[
LLW21
,
HLW21
]
, Huang-Li-Li-Wang studied the “Cheeger-Colding theory for Ricci shrinkers”and established several structural results for the Ricci shrinker limit spaces. We shall introduce a couple of results which we shall apply later.

Recall that for a shrinker
(M^{n},g,f,p)
normalized as (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), the shrinker entropy
\mu(M)
is defined to be the nonpositive number such that

\displaystyle\int_{M}e^{f+\mu(M)}\,dg=(4\pi)^{n/2}.

It is observed by Carrillo-Ni
[
CN09
]
and Li-Wang
[
LW20
]
that

\displaystyle\mu(M)=\mu(g,1)=\nu(g),

where
\mu
and
\nu
are Perelman’s functionals
[
Per02
]
.

First of all, for a shrinker, volume noncollapsedness is equivalent to entropy noncollapsedness.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
LLW21
, Lemma 2.2, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,p)
be a normalized Ricci shrinker. Then for any
v>0
, there is a
Y<+\infty
, such that if
|B(p,1)|\geq v
, then
\mu(M)\geq-Y
. Furthermore, for any
Y<+\infty
, there is a
v>0
, such that if
\mu(M)\geq-Y
, then
|B(p,1)|\geq v
.

Now we summarize the properties of noncollapsed shrinker limit spaces.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
LLW21
, Theorem 1.1, Proposition 9.9]
)
.

Let
\{(M^{n}_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of normalized Ricci shrinkers. Suppose that there is a
Y<+\infty
, such that

\displaystyle\mu(M_{i})\geq-Y\qquad\text{ for all }\qquad i.

Then, after passing to a subsequence, we have

\displaystyle(M_{i},d_{g_{i}},p_{i})\xrightarrow{\ \ \text{ pointed-Gromov-Hausdorff}\ \ }(X,d,p),

where
(X,d,p)
is a metric space admitting a decomposition

\displaystyle X=\mathcal{R}\sqcup\mathcal{S},

such that:

1.

The Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{S}
is no greater than
n-4
;

2.

There is a shrinker structure on
\mathcal{R}
. In other words,
\mathcal{R}
is a smooth manifold with a Riemannian metric
g
and potential function
f
, such that

\displaystyle\Ric+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g.

3.

g_{i}
and
f_{i}
converge smoothly to
g
and
f
on
\mathcal{R}
, respectively;

4.

If
R_{g}
vanishes at some point in
\mathcal{R}
, then
(X,d,p)
is a Ricci flat cone at
p
.

3
The proof of Theorem
1.1

Though not stated explicitly in
[
LLW21
]
, the essential ideas of Theorem
1.1

are all contained therein. In this section, we briefly explain how this result
can be induced from
[
LLW21
]
.

For Theorem
1.1
, arguing by contradiction, we suppose that there is a sequence of Ricci shrinkers
\{(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with

\displaystyle d_{\operatorname{GH}}\Big(B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},1),B_{e}(1)\Big)<\varepsilon_{i},

(3.1)

where
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
, but none of them is the gaussian shrinker.

Suppose for the moment that the sequence is noncollapsed, namely, there is some
Y<+\infty
, such that for all
i

\displaystyle\mu(M_{i})\geq-Y.

Then, by Theorem
2.6
, we can take a limit for a subsequence. Denote by
(X,d,p)
the limit space and
X=\mathcal{R}\sqcup\mathcal{S}
its decomposition. Since
B(p,1)
is isometric to
B_{e}(1)
, we immediately have that,
B(p,1)\subset\mathcal{R}
and the curvature vanishes therein. Consequently,
(X,p)
is a cone at
p
by Theorem
2.6
(4). While the cone tip
p
is a smooth point, such a cone can be nothing but the Euclidean space. Thus
(M_{i},d_{g_{i}},p_{i})
converges to the Euclidean space in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense. On the other hand,
[
LW20
, Corollary 7]
shows that any shrinker close enough to the Euclidean space in the pointed-Gromov-Hausdorff sense must be the gaussian shrinker. So
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})
is a gaussian shrinker for all
i
large enough. This contradicts our assumption on the sequence.

Thus, it remains only to show that the sequence
\{(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is noncollapsed. Indeed, this is nothing but
[
LLW21
, Proposition 5.8]
. However, since that theorem is stated in terms of the global Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, while we only assume a local condition, we shall sketch a proof of the noncollapsedness of the contradictory sequence.

For each
g_{i}
, we define the conformal metric

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{i}:=e^{-\frac{2(f_{i}-f_{i}(p_{i}))}{n-2}}g_{i}.

Then, by
[
LLW21
, Theorem 3.6]
, there is a dimensional constant
D
, such that

1.

For each
\rho\in(0,D^{-1}]
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\operatorname{GH}}\Big(B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},\rho),B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},\rho)\Big)\leq D\rho^{2}.

2.

\displaystyle|{\Ric_{\bar{g}_{i}}}|\leq D^{2}
on
B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},(10D)^{-1})
.

Since (1) and (
3.1
) imply that

\displaystyle d_{\operatorname{GH}}\Big(B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},\rho),B_{e}(\rho)\Big)\leq(D\rho+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}\,|\,\rho))\rho,

the Ricci curvature bound (2) together with Colding’s volume continuity theorem
[
Co97
]
shows that, if we fix
\rho=\rho(n)\ll 1
to be small enough, then

\displaystyle|B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},\rho)|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_{n}\rho^{n}=c(n)\qquad\text{ for all $i$ large enough.}

Hence, there is a unform lower bound for
|B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},1)|
, and we have proved the noncollapsedness for the sequence by Lemma
2.5
.

4
The proof of Theorem
1.2

The main techniques of the proof are the Cheeger-Colding theory and the structure theorem of the shrinker limit spaces
[
LLW21
]
. First of all, we estimate a “critical radius” for shrinkers with small Ricci curvature in the unit ball. This radius is called the “volume radius” by
[
LLW21
, Definition 4.1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There are dimensional constants
\varepsilon(n)>0
and
r_{0}(n)>0
such that if
(M,g,f,p)
is a complete gradient shrinker with
\Ric\leq\varepsilon g
on
B(p,1)
, then

r:=\sup\{s\in(0,1]:|B_{g}(p,s)|\geq\omega_{n}(1-\delta)s^{n}\}\geq r_{0}(n)

Here
\delta=\delta(n)
is the constant in the statement of Theorem
2.2
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose there exist sequences
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
and
(M_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})
such that the corresponding

r_{i}=\sup\{s\in(0,1]\,:\,|B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},s)|\geq\omega_{n}(1-\delta)s^{n}\}\to 0.

Let
\bar{g}_{i}:=r_{i}^{-2}g_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle|B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},1)|_{\bar{g}_{i}}=\omega_{n}(1-\delta),\qquad|{\Ric}(\bar{g}_{i})|\leq\varepsilon_{i}r^{2}_{i}\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},r_{i}^{-1}).

(4.1)

It immediately follows from Theorem
2.2
that

\displaystyle r_{H}(p_{i},\bar{g}_{i})\geq\delta\quad\text{ for all $i$ large enough}.

(4.2)

Next, by the soliton equation, we have

r^{-2}_{i}\Ric(\bar{g}_{i})+\nabla^{2}_{\bar{g}_{i}}(r_{i}^{-2}f_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\bar{g}_{i}

and the Ricci curvature bounds imply that

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\varepsilon_{i}\right)\bar{g}_{i}\leq\nabla^{2}_{\bar{g}_{i}}(r_{i}^{-2}f_{i})\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{i}\right)\bar{g}_{i}\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},r_{i}^{-1})

(4.3)

for all
i
. Since
p_{i}
is the minimum point, it is easy to see that
\bar{f}_{i}:=r_{i}^{-2}f_{i}
is almost an conical function. Indeed, the reader can readily check that
\bar{f}_{i}:=r_{i}^{-2}f_{i}
satisfies Definition
2.3
(2)—(4) up to a constant multiple. We shall construct conical functions starting with
\bar{f}_{i}
.

For notational simplicity, we shall define

\displaystyle h_{i}(x)=4(\bar{f}_{i}(x)-\bar{f}_{i}(p_{i}))=4r_{i}^{-2}(f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(p_{i})).

Let us fix an arbitrary
A\gg 1
. For
i
large enough such that
r_{i}^{-1}>2A
, we define
\bar{h}_{i}:B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},2A)\to\mathbb{R}
to be the solution of the Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{g}_{i}}\bar{h}_{i}\ =\ 2n

on

\displaystyle B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p,2A)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{i}\ =\ \,h_{i}

on

\displaystyle\partial B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p,2A).

Obviously,
\bar{h}_{i}
satisfies (1) of Definition
2.3
. We shall verify conditions (2)—(5) of Definition
2.3
to show that
\bar{h}_{i}
is a
(\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}\,|\,A),A)
-conical function at
p_{i}
.

Verification of (5)
\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},A)}|\bar{h}_{i}-d_{\bar{g}_{i}}^{2}(p_{i},\cdot)|\leq\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}\,|\,A)
.

By the definition of
h_{i}
and by (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle 2(1-2\varepsilon_{i})\bar{g}_{i}\leq\nabla^{2}_{\bar{g}_{i}}h_{i}\leq 2(1+2\varepsilon_{i})\bar{g}_{i}.

(4.5)

Since
p_{i}
is the minimum point of
h_{i}
, and since
h_{i}(p_{i})=0
, picking any unit geodesic
\gamma(t)
with
\gamma(0)=p_{i}
, and integrating over

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}h_{i}(\gamma(t))=\nabla^{2}h_{i}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))=2(1\pm 2\varepsilon_{i})

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}h_{i}(\gamma(t))=\langle\nabla h_{i}(p_{i}),\gamma^{\prime}(0)\rangle=0,

we obtain that, if
t\leq 2A
, then

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\bar{g}_{i}}h_{i}|(\gamma(t))\geq\langle\nabla_{\bar{g}_{i}}h_{i},\gamma^{\prime}(t)\rangle\geq 2t-4\varepsilon_{i}A,

(4.6)

\displaystyle h_{i}(\gamma(t))=t^{2}\pm 8\varepsilon_{i}A^{2}.

In other words, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},2A)}|h_{i}-d_{\bar{g}_{i}}^{2}(p,\cdot)|\leq 8\varepsilon_{i}A^{2}.

(4.7)

To get the same bound for
\bar{h}_{i}
, let us observe that by (
4.5
)

\displaystyle 2n(1-2\varepsilon_{i})\leq\Delta_{\bar{g}_{i}}h_{i}\leq 2n(1+2\varepsilon_{i}).

In view of the definition (
4.4
) of
\bar{h}_{i}
, we consider the auxiliary function

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}:=\bar{h}_{i}-\frac{1}{1+2\varepsilon_{i}}h_{i}-\frac{16\varepsilon_{i}A^{2}}{1+2\varepsilon_{i}}.

On

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}=\frac{2\varepsilon_{i}}{1+2\varepsilon_{i}}h_{i}-\frac{16\varepsilon_{i}A^{2}}{1+2\varepsilon_{i}}\leq\frac{2\varepsilon_{i}(4A^{2}+8\varepsilon_{i}A^{2})}{1+2\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{16\varepsilon_{i}A^{2}}{1+2\varepsilon_{i}}<0.

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},2A)}|\nabla_{\bar{g}_{i}}(h_{i}-\bar{h}_{i})|^{2}\,d\bar{g}_{i}=

Verification of (4)

Verification of (2)
\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-8.13338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.41673pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.78966pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.61258pt}}\!\int_{B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},A)}|\nabla^{2}_{\bar{g}_{i}}\bar{h}_{i}-2\bar{g}_{i}|^{2}\leq\psi(\varepsilon_{i}\,|\,A)^{2}

\displaystyle\ \Delta_{\bar{g}_{i}}|\nabla_{\bar{g}_{i}}(\bar{h}_{i}-h_{i})|^{2}-2\Ric_{\bar{g}_{i}}(\nabla_{\bar{g}_{i}}(\bar{h}_{i}-h_{i}),\nabla_{\bar{g}_{i}}(\bar{h}_{i}-h_{i}))

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=0\quad

One may either implement the cut-off function of Cheeger-Colding

4.4

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},A)}|\nabla^{2}_{\bar{g}_{i}}(\bar{h}_{i}-h_{i})|^{2}\,d\bar{g}_{i}\leq\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}\,|\,A).

\displaystyle\omega_{n}(1-\delta)=\lim_{n\to\infty}|B_{\bar{g}_{i}}(p_{i},1)|=|B_{e}(1)|=\omega_{n},

Proof of Theorem

.

We again argue by contradiction. Assume that there is a sequence of Ricci shrinkers

\displaystyle r_{i}=\sup\{s\in(0,1]\,:\,|B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},s)|\geq\omega_{n}(1-\delta)s^{n}\}.

\displaystyle r_{i}\geq r_{0}(n)>0\quad\text{for all $i$ large enough.}

\displaystyle\mu(M_{i})\geq C(n)\quad\text{ for all $i$ large enough.}

On the other hand, by (

\displaystyle r_{H}(p_{i})\geq r_{0}(n)\delta>0\quad\text{ for all $i$ large enough.}

Proof of Theorem

The proof of Theorem

We prove Theorem

\displaystyle|{\Rm_{g_{t}}}|\leq\frac{C}{|t|}\quad\text{ on }\quad M\times[-T,0).

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{t}:=|t|^{-1}\nabla_{g}f\circ\phi_{t},\quad\phi_{-1}=\operatorname{id},\quad g^{\infty}_{t}:=|t|^{-1}\phi_{t}^{*}g,\quad t\in(-\infty,0).

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{g^{\infty}_{t}}(f\circ\phi_{t})\right|_{g^{\infty}_{t}}^{2}+R_{g^{\infty}_{t}}=\frac{f\circ\phi_{t}}{|t|}.

\displaystyle r_{c}(x,t)\geq 6n\sqrt{|t|}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in[-T,0),

\displaystyle R_{g_{t}}(x)\leq\frac{\delta}{|t|}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in[-T,0).

\displaystyle\ell_{x,0}(x,|t|):=\lim_{t^{\prime}\nearrow 0}\ell_{x,t^{\prime}}(x,|t|)\leq\delta,

\displaystyle f(x_{\infty})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\ell_{x,0}(x,\lambda_{i})\leq\delta.

Recall the potential function estimate of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\big(d(x,p)-5n\big)_{+}^{2}\leq f(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(d(x,p)+\sqrt{2n}\right)^{2}.

Combining (

\displaystyle d(x_{\infty},p)\leq 5n+16\delta^{2}\leq 5n+1.

(6.4)

By our assumption that
