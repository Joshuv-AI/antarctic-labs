On the Turán number of double stars
Ping Hu, Ting Lan

Abstract
The Turán number of a graph F F , ex ​ ( n , F ) \textrm{ex}(n,F) , is the maximum number of edges in a graph on n n vertices which does not contain F F as a subgraph. Let S a , b S_{a,b} denote a double star with a central edge u ​ v uv , a a leaves connected to u u and b b leaves connected to v v . The function ex ​ ( n , S a , b ) \textrm{ex}(n,S_{a,b}) has been studied for a = 1 , 2 a=1,2 , their extremal graphs are disjoint copies of K a + b + 1 K_{a+b+1} and either a small clique or a near b b -regular graph. In this paper, we further study ex ​ ( n , S 3 , b ) \textrm{ex}(n,S_{3,b}) and determine the extremal graphs, which have more structures than those of a = 1 , 2 a=1,2 . Keywords: Turán number; double star

Proof of Lemma
1.4

We first give a sketch of the proof. The proof proceeds mainly by contradiction. To characterize the structures of the extremal graphs, we first claim that the extremal graphs possess a certain property. Assume for contradiction that this property does not hold, then we can construct a new graph with more edges, which leads to a contradiction. Using these established properties, we are then able to determine the structures of the extremal graphs. Next, we outline the proof steps as follows.

Note that if

We claim that

(3.6)

Note that
G_{2}
is not connected since

. Moreover, by Lemma
2.2
, we know

has two or three connected components. Following an analysis similar to the case

) is true. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle e(G)=e(G[V_{1}])+e(V_{1},V_{2})+e(G[V_{2}])

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(b+3)(b+2)}{2}+\max\left\{\frac{(b+4)(b+3)+b(b+5+q)+2+\left\lfloor\frac{b}{2}\right\rfloor}{2},\frac{2(b+4)(b+3)+q(q+1)}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=2\cdot\frac{(b+4)(b+3)}{2}+\max\left\{\frac{b(b+4+q)-(\left\lceil\frac{b}{2}\right\rceil+4)}{2},\frac{(b+4)(b+3)+q(q-1)-(2b-2q+6)}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle<2\cdot\frac{(b+4)(b+3)}{2}+\max\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{b(b+4+q)}{2}\right\rfloor,\frac{(b+4)(b+3)+q(q-1)}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{e(2K_{b+4}\cup\hat{G}),e(3K_{b+4}\cup K_{q})\right\},

a contradiction. So we know

.

Case 3.

p=4
.

Similar to the cases

, we can prove

is also impossible, so we have

as desired. Here we omit the proof since the proof idea is the same as cases

. This proves Lemma
1.4
.
∎

.

(4.1)

5
Concluding remarks

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with
b>a
. Let
n=p(a+b+1)+q
, where
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq q\leq a+b
. We have seen from Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.5
that for
a\leq 3
,
\textrm{ex}(n,S_{a,b})=p\binom{a+b+1}{2}+\binom{q}{2}
when
q
is close to
0
or
a+b
. Now we show this behavior holds for larger
a
as well.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
n,a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with
b>a
and
n\geq a+b+1
. Let
n=p(a+b+1)+q
, where
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq q\leq a+b
. Then for
q=0,1,a+b
, we have
\textrm{ex}(n,S_{a,b})=p\binom{a+b+1}{2}+\binom{q}{2}
and
pK_{a+b+1}\cup K_{q}
is the unique extremal graph.

Proof.

Let
G\in\mathrm{Ex}(n,S_{a,b})
. Note that
pK_{a+b+1}\cup K_{q}
is
S_{a,b}
-free, then we have
e(G)\geq e(pK_{a+b+1}\cup K_{q})
. By Lemma
2.3
, we know
\Delta(G)\leq a+b
. If
q=0
, then we have

e(G)\leq\frac{p(a+b+1)(a+b)}{2}=e(pK_{a+b+1}).

So we get
e(G)=p\binom{a+b+1}{2}
and
pK_{a+b+1}
is the unique extremal graph. Now we consider
q\in\{1,a+b\}
. Let
G_{1},...,G_{t}
be all the components of
G
with
|V(G_{i})|=n_{i}
, note that
G_{i}\in\mathrm{Ex}(n_{i},S_{a,b})
. Next we prove that
n_{i}\leq a+b+1
for all
i
. Assume that there exists
j\in\{1,...,t\}
such that
n_{j}\geq a+b+2
, then by Lemma
2.3
, we have
\Delta(G_{j})\leq a+b-1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle e(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}e(G_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}e(G_{i})+e(G_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(p(a+b+1)+q-n_{j})(a+b)+n_{j}(a+b-1)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p(a+b+1)(a+b)+q(a+b)-n_{j}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p(a+b+1)(a+b)+(a+b)(q-1)-2}{2}

\displaystyle<\frac{p(a+b+1)(a+b)+q(q-1)}{2}

\displaystyle=e(pK_{a+b+1}\cup K_{q}),

a contradiction. So we obtain
n_{i}\leq a+b+1
for each
i
and then
G_{i}\cong K_{n_{i}}
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.2
and since
q\neq 0
, we know that there is exactly one component of
G
with the number of vertices less than
a+b+1
. So we have
t=p+1
, then
G\cong pK_{a+b+1}\cup K_{q}
and
e(G)=p\binom{a+b+1}{2}+\binom{q}{2}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
∎
