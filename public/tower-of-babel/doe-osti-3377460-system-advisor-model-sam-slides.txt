 What Does SAM Do?

• The System Advisor Model (SAM)
 is a free software that enables
 detailed performance and financial
 analysis for energy systems.
• Conducts technoeconomic
 analysis of energy technologies to
 facilitate planning and decision-
 making.
• Evaluates or compares the energy
 production or cost of installed systems,
 various system configurations, or
    incentive structures (NREL, 2024).         Images: NREL, PR-5600-90876, 2024

 Who Uses SAM?

• Lawmakers and utilities to study how a policy would affect the economics of a typical
 system or to analyze different types of utility rate structures for generation sources.
• Developers and engineers to compare technologies, sites, or configurations, or to
 estimate the Levelized Cost of Energy for a system.
• Researchers to examine how an innovative concept might be able to lower the Levelized
 Cost of Energy or to estimate the technical potential of a technology in a region.
• Students to learn about energy or to explore financing structures for energy.
 SAM can be used within other tools at NREL; dGen, PVWatts, REopt, HOPP, reV,
 ReEDS, PLEXOS.

Source: NREL 2024

 What Kinds of Questions Can SAM Answer?

• What system configuration will maximize earnings from electricity sales?
• What incentive structure will be most effective during implementation of new energy systems?
• How will installing a new technology change the cost of energy and system efficiency compared
 to the current system?
• What kind of system can I design to maximize localized energy production for an industrial
 electricity client?
• How can I add photovoltaics (PV) to my existing wind plant to maximize my interconnection
 agreement?
• How can I take advantage of various complementary energy generation resource profiles at my
 location?
• How can I use localized energy generation sources to complement my (fill in the blank)
 generation (using the generic system model)?

Getting Started with SAM

 How to Access and Use SAM

• SAM is available as a desktop application as well as
 programmatically through a variety of languages, including
 a popular Python wrapper.
• SAM is an open-source project (programmers can
 contribute) implemented in C++.
• NREL typically updates the tool about once a year.
• Highly tailored analysis can benefit from an experienced
 analyst, although it can be learned by newer analysts as
 well.
• Reach out to sam.support@nlr.gov for assistance or to be
 connected with the team.

 Data Parameters: Input

• Weather data- Temperature range, common weather events, etc. Sources can be found here.
 ◦ Example: One-minute weather data obtained from NREL’s Measurement and
 Instrumentation Data Center.
• System specifications- Generation capacity, efficiency, etc.
 ◦ Example: The community energy system includes a 1.5 megawatt solar array with an
 inverter efficiency of 96% and battery storage sized to support critical loads for up to four
                hours. (Source: NREL PR-6A20-64987, 2015)
• System losses- System losses capture phenomena that are not explicitly handled by the model,
 including availability and O&M downtime and other technology-specific losses.
 ◦ Example: Inverter downtime is a major source of PV system production loss, often
 exceeding the utility–scale financial model assumption of 1% production loss. (Source:
                NREL/CP-5K00-76022, 2020)
• Compensation- Offsetting electricity usage and selling excess generation back to the grid (subject
 to utility rate and billing structures) for behind-the-meter systems and power purchase agreements
 or market participation for front-of-meter systems.
 ◦ Example: Customers with rooftop solar receive bill credit for excess energy they send back
                to the grid (NREL/PR-5600-90876, 2024).

 Data Parameters: Inputs Continued

• Financing Incentives
 o SAM can model three types of incentives:
 ▪ Tax credits
 ▪ Depreciation (tax deduction)
 ▪ Cash payments.
 o Incentives may be provided by different institutions, including:
 ▪ Federal government
 ▪ State and local government
 ▪ Utilities
 ▪ Others.
 o Rules for incentives depend on who provides them. SAM is general enough to model
 most types of incentives. It does not automatically choose options based on the provider
 of the incentive. (Gilman, 2024).
 ▪ Example: The state of Tennessee offers an incentive that rebates 100% of sales
 and use tax on photovoltaics in addition to other renewable technologies (Diorio et
                  al., 2018).

 Data Parameters: Results

• This is a selection of some results; more are available in the tool.
 ◦ System output estimate- process of estimating how much energy a system can produce
 ◦ Revenue- income generated by the sales of goods and services
 ◦ Simple payback period- the minimum time it takes to recover an investment. The simple
 payback period for an energy system is the total investment cost divided by the first year's
 revenues from energy saved, displaced, or produced (NREL, 2000)
 ◦ Capacity factor- the ratio of the annual average energy production (kWhAC) of an energy
 generation plant divided by the theoretical maximum annual energy production of a plant
 assuming it operates at its peak rated capacity every hour of the year
 ◦ Levelized cost of energy- a summary metric that combines the primary technology cost and
 performance parameters: capital expenditures, operating expenditures, and capacity factor
        (NREL, n.d.)
 ◦ Net present value- is the total economic value expected to be produced by a system when its
 lifecycle costs are subtracted from the energy bill savings it will generate (NREL, 2025)

 Example of Daily Operation

• When the PV DC power output exceeds the inverter DC power input, excess power is clipped.
• In an AC connected system, even if PV power is dumped to the battery, it doesn’t reduce clipping,
 since PV power must still pass through the inverter before going to the battery.
• In a DC connected system, PV power can be dumped to the battery before passing through the
 inverter.

 PV + AC-Connected Battery PV + DC-Connected Battery
             Source: NREL/PR-6A20-71779

 Results Comparison

• Net present value is the total economic value expected to be produced by a system when its lifecycle costs are
 subtracted from the energy bill savings it will generate.
 o Installing no system is the worst option
 o Installing PV only is the best option, if the customer can qualify for the limited Customer Grid Supply option.
 o Installing a DC-connected battery mitigates all PV clipping, but it provides approximately the same return as an AC-
 connected battery in this case.

Source: NREL/PR-6A20-71779

                                                                                                        National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   17

 Sensitivity: Boost DC to AC Ratio

• Added string of seven PV modules to the inverter, boosting DC/AC ratio to 1.85.
• 13% boost in net present value over AC battery system, since PV system produces a lot of excess
 DC power that cannot be captured by AC connected battery.
• Possible reasons for high DC/AC ratio include levelizing system AC output throughout day, keeping plant
 AC output consistent over module lifetime, etc.
