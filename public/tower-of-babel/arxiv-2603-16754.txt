Ultrafilter Extesions for Veltman Semantics
Fèlix Frigola González, Joost J. Joosten, Vicent Navarro Arroyo, Cosimo Perini Brogi

Abstract
In this paper, we present a first-order frame condition for interpretability logic and show that the condition is not modally definable. Yet, the frame-condition holds both on ILM and on ILP frames and, hence, is of potential importance for the long-standing open problem about the interpretability logic of all reasonable arithmetical theories. In the light of the Goldblatt-Thomason Theorem, the modally inexpressible frame condition serves as motivation to develop ultrafilter extensions for interpretability logic. We develop the necessary algebraic tools to define these ultrafilter extensions and prove the main properties about both the tools and the ultrafilter extensions.

Introduction

Interpretability logics were introduced by Visser in the 1980s as a natural extension of provability logics (
[
29
]
). Since then, the field of modal interpretability logics has seen a steady process of becoming a mature discipline. A Kripke style semantics for interpretability logics goes by the name of Veltman Semantics. After some early modal completeness and decidability results in
[
6
]
it was seen that many interpretability logics satisfy various other good properties too, like the Finite Model Property, the Fixpoint Theorem, or Interpolation (see
[

5

Proof calculi are mostly Hilbert style but sequent style systems and cut-elimination have been studied in
[

24
,
23
]
. Topological semantics for interpretability logics have been studied in
[
15
]
. Verbrugge semantics (
[
18
]
) is a generalization of Veltman semantics that is reminiscent of neighbourhood semantics. However, plain Veltman semantics is still the predominant semantics used for interpretability logics.

Unary normal modal logics are all extensions of the basic modal logic
K
. The basic modal interpretability logic is called
IL
although on occasion logics below
IL
are studied too (
[
19
,
22
]
). Various extensions of

To the best of our knowledge, in this paper – Section

{\textup{{IL}}({\rm{\sf All}})}

(see

Our proof of modal undefinability is rather ad-hoc and this begs the question if a Goldblatt-Thomason Theorem for interpretability logic can be proven to deal with undefinability results in a more uniform way. In the remainder of the paper we develop a first natural step toward such a Goldblatt-Thomason theorem. In Section

The main results on ultrafilter extensions are presented in Section

Definition 2.2

(Veltman frames and models)
.

A
Veltman frame
is a triple
\mathcal{F}:=\langle W,R,\{S_{w}\mid w\in W\}\rangle
where
W
is a non-empty
set of
possible worlds

R
a binary relation on
W
which is transitive and upwardly well-founded. The
\{S_{w}\mid w\in W\}
is a collection of binary relations on
R[w]
– where
R[w]:=\{v\mid wRv\}
– so that the
S_{w}
are reflexive and transitive and the restriction of
R
to
R[w]
is contained in
S_{w}
, that is:
R\cap R[w]\subseteq S_{w}
.

A
Veltman model
consists of a Veltman frame together with a valuation function
\textit{ev}:\texttt{Atm}\longrightarrow{\wp}(W)
that assigns to each propositional atom
p\in\texttt{Atm}
a set of worlds
\textit{ev}(p)\in W
where
a
is stipulated to be true.

This valuation function naturally extends to a forcing relation
\Vdash\,\subseteq\,W\times\texttt{Form}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lll}w\Vdash p&\iff&w\in\textit{ev}(p);\ \mbox{ and }w\Vdash\bot\text{for no}\,w\in W;\\
w\Vdash\alpha\to\beta&\iff&w\nVdash\alpha\text{ or }w\Vdash\beta;\\
w\Vdash\mathop{\text{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{\scalebox{1.3}{$\medsquare$}}}}\nolimits\alpha&\iff&\forall v\ .(\text{if }wRv,\text{ then }v\Vdash\alpha);\\
w\Vdash\alpha\mathop{\text{\raisebox{-0.21529pt}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\medtriangleright$}}}}\nolimits\beta&\iff&\forall u\ .\Big(\text{if }wRu\text{ and }\ u\Vdash\alpha,\text{ then }\exists v\ (uS_{w}v\ \text{ and }\ v\Vdash\beta)\Big).\end{array}

Given a Veltman model
\mathcal{M}=\langle W,R,\{S_{w}\mid w\in W\},\textit{ev}\rangle
and a formula
\alpha\in\texttt{Form}
, we shall write
\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket_{\mathcal{M}}
to denote

and,

2.3
Ultrafilter Extensions

(Ultra)Filters are the algebraic counterpart of (complete) theories.

Definition 2.8
.

Given a set
W
, an ultrafilter over
W
consists of a maximal filter over
W
. That is, a collection
f\subseteq\wp(W)
is an
ultrafilter
on
W
, and we write
f\in\textsf{U}({W})
, if it satisfies the following conditions:

Properness:

\varnothing\not\in f
;

Upward Closure:
If
A\in f
and
A\subseteq B
, then
B\in f
;

Intersection Closure:
If
A,B\in f
, then
A\cap B\in f
;

Totality:
For any
A\subseteq W
, either
A\in f
or
\overline{A}\in f
, where
\overline{A}\coloneqq W\setminus A
.

Ultrafilters over a given
W
generated by a single fixed element
w\in W
by considering all subsets of
W
containing
w
are called
principal ultrafilters
, denoted by
\Pi_{w}
.

When the starting set
W
is infinite, the existence of non-principal ultrafilters over
W
is provable modulo some choice principles over a constructive base
[
20
]
as a consequence of

Lemma 2.9

(Ultrafilter Principle)
.

Every non-empty family
s
of sets with the finite intersection property (FIP) – that is, for any
t\underset{\textit{fin}}{\subseteq}s
,
\bigcap t\neq\varnothing
– generates a filter that is contained in at least one ultrafilter.

Relational frames can be extended via ultrafilters as follows.

Definition 2.10
.

Given a relational frame
\mathcal{F}=\langle W,\{R_{i}\}_{i<\omega}\rangle
, its
ultrafilter extension

{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsf{ue}}
is given by:

•

Worlds:
The new domain is
\textsf{U}({W})
, the set of all ultrafilters over
W
;

•

Accessibility Relations (
{R}^{\textsf{ue}}_{i}
):
For any two ultrafilters

:

f{R}^{\textsf{ue}}_{i}g\iff\forall X\subseteq W.\,(X\in g\implies R^{-1}_{i}(X)\in f),

R^{-1}_{i}(X):=\{w\in W\mid\exists x\in X.\,(wR_{i}x)\}
.

Given such an ultrafilter extension
{{\mathcal{F}}}^{\textsf{ue}}
, and an
evaluation

we define

{\textit{ev}}^{\textsf{ue}}(p):=\{f\in\textsf{U}({W})\mid\textit{ev}(p)\in f\}.

Therefore, given a model
\mathcal{M}

{\mathcal{M}}^{\textsf{ue}}

The central results about ultrafilter extensions of relational models are collected by

Theorem 2.11
.

Given a relational model
{\mathcal{M}}=\langle{\mathcal{F}},\textit{ev}\rangle
, we have:

•

Truth Lemma:
For any formula
\varphi
and any ultrafilter
f\in\textsf{U}({W})
, the following equivalence holds:

({\mathcal{M}}^{\textsf{ue}},f)\Vdash\varphi\iff\textit{ev}(\varphi)\in f
,

•

If a formula

An elementary class of frames

Definition 6.5

(Modal saturation)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a modal model and
x\in\mathcal{M}
. We call a set of
\Sigma
of modal sentences
locally possible
in
x

\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma
we have
\mathcal{M},x\Vdash\mathop{\text{\scalebox{1.1}{$\meddiamond$}}}\nolimits\bigwedge\Sigma^{\prime}
. The set
\Sigma

(outright) at

with

and
\mathcal{M},y\Vdash\Sigma
.

We call a modal model
\mathcal{M}

modally saturated
whenever for any

we have that any set that is locally possible at
x
is possible at
x
.

.

For any Veltman model

, the ultrafilter extension

is modally saturated.

Consider an arbitrary

and let

be locally possible at
\langle f,\sigma\rangle
in
{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\textsf{ue}}
. We have to show that

and Lemma

it is enough to see that

-successor

We also have a certain saturation at the label level.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
f\in\textsf{U}(W)
. If, for every finite
l^{\prime}\subset l
, there exists a
g
such that
f\prec_{l^{\prime}}g
, then there exists an
h
such that
f\prec_{l}h
.

Proof.

Suppose not. Then, for every
h\in\textsf{U}(W)
, there is some
A\subseteq W
and some finite choice of
S_{i}\in l
such that
S^{-1}\big(\overline{A},\bigcup_{i}\overline{S_{i}}\big)\in f
and
A\notin h
or
\widehat{R^{-1}}(A)\notin h
. Observe that the finite choice of
S_{i}\in l
is a finite choice of
S_{i}\in l^{\prime}
, for some finite
l^{\prime}\subseteq l
. By assumption, there is some ultrafilter

such that
f\prec_{l^{\prime}}g
. Since

, we have that

, which contradicts our hypothesis.
∎

7
Conclusions

Through our example of a modally undefinable first-order frame condition we motivated the need of a Goldblatt-Thomason theorem
[
8
]
for interpretability logic. We proceeded to give a definition for ultrafilter extensions of Veltman frames and models and proved the main properties of these. The definition that we present resembles the canonical model construction
[
6
,
10
]
which came slightly unexpected since the ultrafilter extension starts from a concrete model. For this reason, and in general, it would be interesting to see how ultrafilter extensions fare under Verbrugge semantics
[
18
]
.
