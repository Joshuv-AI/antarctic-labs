Towards the Relative Langlands Duality for Orthosymplectic Pairs
Dor Mezer

Abstract
In this paper we prove a conjectured equivalence of categories, showing that the S-dual of SO 2 ​ n × Sp 2 ​ n \operatorname{SO}_{2n}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n} acting on ℂ + 2 ​ n ⊗ ℂ − 2 ​ n \mathbb{C}_{+}^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{-}^{2n} is equal to SO 2 ​ n + 1 × SO 2 ​ n ↻ T ∗ ​ SO 2 ​ n + 1 \operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\circlearrowright T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1} . This result is a particular case of a non-polarized version of the (local) relative Langlands duality of [ 3 ] . Similar results for the pairs ( SO 2 ​ n + 1 , Sp 2 ​ n ) (\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1},\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}) and ( GL n , GL m ) (\operatorname{GL}_{n},\operatorname{GL}_{m}) were proved in [ 6 ] and [ 14 ] respectively, whereas the converse result was proved in [ 8 ] . As a consequence of our main result, we prove that Langlands functoriality of the Derived Satake isomorphism for the pair Sp 2 ​ n , SO 2 ​ n \operatorname{Sp}_{2n},\operatorname{SO}_{2n} is given by the theta correspondence. Our approach works (with appropriate modifications) in the general even orthosymplectic case of 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 2 ​ m | 2 ​ n ) \mathfrak{osp}(2m|2n) .

Introduction

1.1
The topic of this paper

The main result of this paper is Theorem
1.1
below, which gives an equivalence of categories. We will call them the “geometric category” and the “spectral cateogry”, in line with the common terminology in the Langlands program and that of
[
3
]
in particular.

We start with the describing the spectral category.
Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
we will denote by
D_{\mathrm{perf}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
the category of perfect modules of the dg-algebra
\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}[-2])
. Similarly, given a reductive group
G
, we will denote by
D_{\mathrm{perf}}^{[]}(T^{*}G)
the category of perfect modules for the dg-algebra
\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}[-2])
. See
2.3
as well as
[
3
, §6]
for general discussion of shearing. This algebra carries a natural action of
G\times G
. There is also the natural moment map morphism

D_{\mathrm{perf}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\otimes D_{\mathrm{perf}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\to D_{\mathrm{perf}}^{[]}(T^{*}G),

Corresponding to the usual moment map
T^{*}G\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, which under the isomorphism
T^{*}G\cong G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
corresponds to
(g,\xi)\mapsto(-\xi,\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(\xi))
. It is compatible with the (weak)
G\times G
action on both sides.
More generally, given
H_{1},H_{2}
subgroups of
G
, we can restrict the moment map to

D_{\mathrm{perf}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{*})\otimes D_{\mathrm{perf}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*})\to D_{\mathrm{perf}}^{[]}(T^{*}G),

and moreover take
H_{1}\times H_{2}
invariants to get the equivariant moment map morphism

D_{\mathrm{perf},H_{1}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{h}_{1}^{*})\otimes D_{\mathrm{perf},H_{2}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*})\to D_{\mathrm{perf},H_{1},H_{2}}^{[]}(T^{*}G).

Let us now describe the geometric category.
We consider the symplectic
4n^{2}
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{C}_{-}^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{+}^{2n}
, and its loop vector space
(\mathbb{C}_{-}^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{+}^{2n})_{\mathcal{K}}
, equipped with the symplectic form given by the residue of the natural
\mathcal{K}
-valued symplectic form defined on it. We consider the completed Weyl algebra
\mathcal{W}
of this (loop) symplectic vector space, and the category
\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}
of renormalized discrete modules over it, along with its natural t-structure (see e.g.
[
6
, §2.1]
).
There is a categorical action of
\operatorname{D-mod}_{-1/2}^{*}(\operatorname{Sp}_{4n^{2},\mathcal{K}})
on
\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}
, constructed in
[
5
, Lemma 2.4.1]
. Here the subscript
-1/2
stands for considering D-modules twisted by the inverse of the square root of the determinant line bundle on
\operatorname{Sp}_{4n^{2},\mathcal{K}}
. Note that by the determinant line bundle we mean the one whose fibers are isomorphic to the dual spaces to the relative determinant of the Lagrangian associated to
g\in\operatorname{Sp}_{4n^{2},\mathcal{K}}
. This line bundle is ample. See Appendix
B.1
for some further details and discussions on it.

We may restrict this action to
\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{K}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{K}}
to get an action of twisted D-modules on
\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{K}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{K}}
. The twisting is by inverse of the square root of the restriction of the determinant line bundle on
\operatorname{Sp}_{4n^{2},\mathcal{K}}
. However, this restriction has a square root (equivariant with respect to
\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}
), and so we may untwist by this square root to identify the above category of twisted D-modules with untwisted D-modules (this equivalence is compatible with convolution), and in particular we have an action of
\operatorname{D-mod}^{*}(\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{K}})\otimes\operatorname{D-mod}^{*}(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{K}})
.
Taking (strong)
\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}
-invariants and restricting to locally compact objects (i.e. the full subcategory consisting of objects which are compact after forgetting the equivariant structure), we obtain a categorical action of

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}})^{c}\otimes\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}})^{c}
on
\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}}
.
Here we use the notation of Section
2.2
, so that when we write
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}})^{c}\otimes\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}})^{c}
we actually mean the full subcategories of locally compact objects.
By the derived Satake isomorphism of
[
4
]
(see Section
2.3
), we have an equivalence of monoidal categories

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}})^{c}\otimes\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}})^{c}\cong D_{\mathrm{perf},\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n})\otimes D_{\mathrm{perf},\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n+1}).

We also let
E_{0}:=\mathcal{W}/(V\otimes V^{\prime})_{\mathcal{O}}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}}
(see Section
2
below).

The following is our main theorem in this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an equivalence of categories:

\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}}\cong D_{\mathrm{perf},\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}}^{[]}(T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}),

which is compatible with the actions of
D_{\mathrm{perf},\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n})\otimes D_{\mathrm{perf},\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n+1})
on both sides, and under which, the object
E_{0}
corresponds to the structure sheaf on
T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that we have

D_{\mathrm{perf},\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}}^{[]}(T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1})\cong D_{\mathrm{perf},\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n+1}).

Remark 1.3
.

One may take ind-completions to the equivalence of Theorem
1.1
to get the “large version”, which is an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{Ind}(\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}})\cong\mathrm{QCoh}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}}^{[]}(T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}),

preserving compact objects, with similar compatibilities.

1.2
Relative Langlands duality

Let
G
be a reductive group and
X
a spherical variety for
G
, and let
M:=T^{*}X
.
In
[
3
]
, it is conjectured that for any such pair, a certain S-dual pair
\check{G}\circlearrowright\check{M}
is attached, where
\check{G}
is the Langlands dual group of
G
, and
\check{M}
is a hyperspherical variety for
\check{G}
, such that the following equivalence of categories holds (with certain compatibilities):

\operatorname{D-mod}_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(X_{\mathcal{K}})\cong D^{[]}_{\mathrm{perf},\check{G}}(\check{M}).

(1)

See
[
3
, Conjecture 7.5.1]
for full details.

This is expected to generalize beyond the polarizable case, i.e. to the generality where
M
is a hyperspherical variety, not necessarily polarizable (so that
X
is not at all defined).

Note that it is a non-trivial matter to express the left hand side of the above equivalence in terms of
M
without using
X
, and as far as we know it is an open problem to define such a category for a general hyperspherical variety
M
. However, in certain cases, such a category is known to be expected to play this role.

Remark 1.4
.

While in general this generalization may give rise to an “anomaly”, which requires to include a twist while also replacing the notion of the Langlands dual group
\check{G}
, in our case the anomaly will cancel, so that no such modification is needed.

The case considered in this paper is such a situation, with
M=\mathbb{C}_{-}^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{+}^{2n},G=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
and
\check{M}=T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
.
In this framework, Theorem
1.1
can be viewed as a special case of a non-polarizable version of the above conjecture. This has been expected, e.g. by
[
13
, Table 1, row 6]
.

In
[
5
]
and
[
27
]
the authors give a definition of the S-dual for a certain class of varieties
M
. While this definition is incomplete and covers only part of the general conjectural framework, it does cover the case where
M
is a symplectic representation of
G
, which includes our situation.
By this definition, the S-dual variety
\check{M}
is given as the spectrum of the inner endomorphism algebra of the basic object in the geometric side, so that in presence of an equivalence of local categories (as in (
1
) or Theorem
1.1
) the variety appearing in the spectral side must be the S-dual.
In view of this, Theorem
1.1
implies the following:

Theorem 1.5
.

The S-dual of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\circlearrowright\mathbb{C}_{-}^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{+}^{2n}
is
\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\circlearrowright T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
.

The converse S-duality, along with the equivalence of local categories, was proved in
[
8
]
. Other similar cases of S-dualities, along with the equivalences of local categories, were proved in
[
9
]
,
[
6
]
, and
[
14
]
and
[
10
]
.

The approach taken in this paper works (with appropriate modifications) in the general even orthosymplectic case of
\mathfrak{osp}(2m|2n)
. This will be the subject of our next work. We also plan to investigate the odd orthosymplectic case of
\mathfrak{osp}(2m+1|2n)
. The conjectured S-duals are listed in
[
13
, Table 1]
.

1.3
Theta correspondence as Langlands functoriality

A consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that the theta correspondence between
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}
and
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
realizes (local geometric spherical) Langlands functorialities corresponding to the natural inclusion
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\to\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
.
The appearance of the Theta correspondence in realizing Langlands functorialities, and the functorial lifts between symplectic and orthogonal groups in particular, has a long history (for example, see
[
28
, §6]
for the classical theory and
[
22
]
for a geometric result).
However, while these results show that the theta correspondence gives a functorial lift, our result proves that the theta correspondence gives
the
functorial lift in our setting, i.e. the one corresponding under Derived Satake to the obvious map in the spectral side (up to a certain involution).

Let us explain this in more detail (cf.
[
3
, §12.3]
where the global version of the general phenomenon is discussed).

1.3.1
Local geometric Langlands functoriality

Let
H,G
be reductive groups over
\mathbb{C}
, and consider a morphism
\check{H}\to\check{G}
between reductive groups.

It defines a functor

\pi:\mathrm{QCoh}_{\check{G}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{\check{g}}^{*})\to\mathrm{QCoh}_{\check{H}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{\check{h}}^{*}),

given by restricting equivariance from
\check{G}
to
\check{H}
and then pushing forward along the projection
\mathfrak{\check{g}}^{*}\to\mathfrak{\check{h}}^{*}
.
Here the shearing is as in the derived Satake equivalence (see Section
2.3
).
We also have a functor in the opposite direction

\nu:\mathrm{QCoh}_{\check{H}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{\check{h}}^{*})\to\mathrm{QCoh}_{\check{G}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{\check{g}}^{*}),

given by pullback along
\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to\check{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
followed by inducing the
\check{H}
-action to a representation of
\check{G}
.

We define the “functorial lift” functors

\pi^{L}:\operatorname{D-mod}_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{G})\to\operatorname{D-mod}_{H_{\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{H})

and

\nu^{L}:\operatorname{D-mod}_{H_{\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{H})\to\operatorname{D-mod}_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{G})

to be the functors corresponding to
\pi
and
\nu
respectively under the derived Satake equivalence.
It is a general question of interest to describe
\pi^{L}
and
\nu^{L}
explicitly, in particular in terms intrinsic to the geometric side, without involving Langlands duality.

Rather than doing that, we will describe
i_{H}^{L}\circ\pi^{L}
and
\nu^{L}\circ i_{H}^{L}
for the pair
G=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n},H=\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
, where
i_{H}
is the involution of
\mathrm{QCoh}_{\check{H}}^{[]}(\mathfrak{\check{h}}^{*})
induced by the involution
x\mapsto-x
of
\check{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, and
i_{H}^{L}
is the involution on
\operatorname{D-mod}_{H_{\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{H})
corresponding to it under derived Satake.

Remark 1.6
.

We are currently not sure how to describe the involution
i_{H}
in terms intrinsic to the geometric side.

1.3.2
Local geometric theta correspondence

Consider the functor

\mathrm{act}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n},E_{0}}:\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}})\to\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}})

given by acting on the object
E_{0}
. Similarly, consider

\mathrm{act}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n},E_{0}}:\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}})\to\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}}).

These two functors admit right adjoints, given by inner hom from
E_{0}
(see
[
3
, Remark 8.1.2]
). We will denote them by
a_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}
and
a_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}}
respectively.

We define the Theta lift functor
\Theta:\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}})\to\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}})
to be

\Theta:=a_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n},E_{0}}\circ\mathrm{act}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}}.

We also define a functor in the opposite direction:

\Theta^{\prime}:=a_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n},E_{0}}\circ\mathrm{act}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}.

The following is a consequence of Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\pi,\nu
be as above for the pair
G=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n},H=\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
with the natural inclusion
\check{H}=\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\to\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}=\check{G}
. Then we have isomorphisms of functors
i_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}^{L}\circ\pi^{L}\cong\Theta
and
\nu^{L}\circ i_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}^{L}\cong\Theta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let

\mu_{1}:T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/(\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1})\to\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}

be the usual (equivariant) moment maps for the action of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
on
T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
.
Under the isomorphism
T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/(\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1})\cong\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}

\mu_{1}

\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n+1}\to\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n}

\mu_{2}
corresponds to the quotient morphism
\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\to\mathfrak{so}^{*}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}

All the above morphisms are compatible with the natural

1.4
Outline of the Proof

The first step of the proof, Proposition
4.8
, is to prove an identification of the two Hecke actions of
\mathrm{Rep}(\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1})
and
\mathrm{Rep}(\operatorname{SO}_{2n})
on
\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}}
. That is, that the action of the category
\mathrm{Rep}(\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1})\cong\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)})^{\heartsuit}
factors as the restriction to
\mathrm{Rep}(\operatorname{SO}_{2n})\cong\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})})^{\heartsuit}
and then the Hecke action of this category. The analogous result for
\ell
-adic sheaves was proved in
[
22
]
.
We do this by a direct computation for the generator of the category
U\in\mathrm{Rep}(\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1})
, and show that it is compatible with the self duality of this object.
The above identification is manifest on the spectral side, as the map
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\backslash T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}\to\mathrm{pt}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
factors as the map
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\backslash T^{*}\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}\to\mathrm{pt}/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
composed with the natural quotient map.
Also, taking the point of view of Section
1.3
, this result can be thought upon as the statement that the Theta correspondence gives the expected mapping of Langlands parameters.

Using the description of the spectral category given in Remark
1.2
, we see that it is isomorphic to the category of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
-equivariant perfect modules for the (dg) deequivariantized (over
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
) endomorphism algebra of the structure sheaf (see Definition
6.1
). Our strategy will be to describe the geometric category
\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}}
in the same manner, and to construct an isomorphism of the deequivariantized endomorphism algebras.

Indeed, such a description follows from rather abstract reasonings given that we know that the object
E_{0}
(which should correspond to the structure sheaf under Theorem
1.1
) generates the category
\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}}
under the Hecke action, in the sense of Definition
5.1
. This is done in Section
5
by classifying the irreducible objects in
(\operatorname{\mathcal{W}-\mathrm{mod}}^{\operatorname{SO}_{2n,\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2n,\mathcal{O}},\mathrm{lc}})^{\heartsuit}
, which are given by dominant coweights of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
.

Then theorem
1.1
reduces to a computation of the deequivariantized endomorphism algebra of
E_{0}
, which we denote by
\mathscr{E}
, along with compatibilities with moment maps. The action of
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}})
on
E_{0}
gives a map

\psi:\operatorname{Sym}(\mathfrak{so}_{2n}[-2])\cong\mathrm{End}_{\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}}),\mathrm{deeq}}(\delta_{1},\delta_{1})\to\mathscr{E}.

By Theorem
1.1
, this map is expected to be an isomorphism, and this is indeed what occupies the rest of this paper.
The compatibility with the second moment map (which is similarly constructed using the action of
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}})
) is proved by reducing it to a computation concerning Chern classes (Proposition
6.2
).

It follows from purity considerations that
\mathscr{E}
must be formal (see Theorem
6.5
), and using the localization Theorem we show that it must be concentrated in even cohomological degrees and commutative (see Claim
8.3
). This allows us to think about it geometrically, i.e. to consider the spectrum of its cohomology ring (we call this ring
\mathfrak{E}^{\bullet}
), which is a scheme carrying a
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
-action.
\psi
gives a map from this scheme to
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
, compatible with the action (the action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
on
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
is the linear one of weight 2). We also denote
\mathfrak{G}^{\bullet}:=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}]
.

Using some invariant theory for the action of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
on
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
, we show in Proposition
7.3
that the resulting map between the categorical quotients by
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
is an isomorphism. This categorical quotient is isomorphic to
\Sigma_{2n}\times\Sigma_{2n+1}:=\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}/\!\!/\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\times\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}/\!\!/\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
.
We give a finite cover
\Sigma\twoheadrightarrow\Sigma_{2n}\times\Sigma_{2n+1}
, such that the quotient map
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}\to\Sigma_{2n}\times\Sigma_{2n+1}
admits a “pseudo-slice”
\Sigma\to\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
, and such that the image of the above “pseudo-slice” becomes dense after saturation by the
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
action, with the complement being of codimension
\geq 2
(see Lemma
7.6
).
Moreover, we show that after base change along
\Sigma\twoheadrightarrow\Sigma_{2n}\times\Sigma_{2n+1}
and localization along
\Sigma
,
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}^{*}
becomes the trivial
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
torsor (see Corollary
7.8(iii)
).
Using the localization theorem, we show in Claim
8.3
that
\mathrm{Spec}\ (\mathfrak{E}^{\bullet})
too becomes the trivial
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
torsor after base change along
\Sigma\twoheadrightarrow\Sigma_{2n}\times\Sigma_{2n+1}
and localization along
\Sigma
, and deduce that the map
\psi
itself becomes an isomorphism after this base change and localization (as any equivariant map between trivial torsors is an isomorphism).

Given the above, it is enough to show that the considered pseudo slice factors through
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathfrak{E}^{\bullet})
.
This we do in Section
9

\Sigma\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathfrak{E}^{\bullet})
and show that its image under

1.5
Structure of this paper

Let us briefly describe what each section of this paper contains.

In Section

Section

Section

Section

introduces the deequivariantized endomorphism algebra

2.2
D-modules on infinite dimensional ind-schemes

We will use the theory of (possibly equivariant) D-modules on ind-schemes (not necessarily of finite type) as appears in
[
29
]
. Namely, all of our considered schemes and ind-schemes are placid, so that we have
\operatorname{D-mod}^{*}\cong\operatorname{D-mod}^{!}

In particular,

*

(!,ren)

\operatorname{D-mod}^{!}

-pushforwards and
(*,ren)
-pullbacks. For any placid ind-scheme

For an ind-scheme of ind-finite type, both of these theories coincide with the usual theory of D-modules (however the above isomorphism for placid ind-schemes
\operatorname{D-mod}^{*}\cong\operatorname{D-mod}^{!}
does not correspond to the identity functor in this case, but rather to a certain cohomological shift). In this case we will not distinguish between them and simply write
\operatorname{D-mod}(S)
.

Given an action of an algebraic group (or more commonly for us, an arc group)
G
on
S
, we will write
\operatorname{D-mod}^{*}_{G}(S)
(or
\operatorname{D-mod}_{G}(S)
for an ind-scheme of ind-finite type) for the
renormalized
category of equivariant D-modules. By definition, this is the ind completion of the category of locally compact equivariant D-modules. By locally compact equivariant D-modules we mean those equivariant D-modules that become compact after forgetting the equivariance. The compact objects in
\operatorname{D-mod}^{*}_{G}(S)
are then by construction locally compact equivariant D-modules, in the usual sense.

For in-depth discussion of this renormalization, also called
canonical renormalization
, see
[
30
]
, (see also
[
3
, Appendix B]
,
[
1
, §12]
).

3
The geometric Weil representation

In this section we recall the geometric (D-module) Weil representation, and the corresponding Hecke action on the invariants with respect to the positive loop group.
We end this section by giving a somewhat explicit formula for the Hecke action, which will be used in the rest of this paper (see Corollary
3.5
and Remark
3.6
).

We do not aim to develop this theory from scratch, but rather to recall the necessary results and constructions. For references, see
[
30
, §10]
and
[
5
, Lemma 2.4.1]
, and also
[
19
]
.
The parallel theory for
\ell
-adic sheaves is developed in
[
20
]
.

Let
M
be a symplectic finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{C}
.

3.1
The finite case

We start from the finite case, and let
Weyl
be the Weyl algebra of
M
.
We shall construct an action of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))
on
Weyl\mathrm{-mod}
.

Let
\alpha:\mathfrak{sp}(M)\to Weyl
be the map given by

\mathfrak{sp}(M)\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(M)\xrightarrow{xy\mapsto\frac{1}{2}(xy+yx)}Weyl,

It satisfies the following properties:

1.

\alpha(a)x-x\alpha(a)=a(x)
for any
a\in\mathfrak{sp}(M)
and
x\in M
. That is, the infinitesimal action of
\mathfrak{sp}(M)
on
Weyl
is given by commutation with
\alpha(-)
.

2.

\alpha(a)\alpha(b)-\alpha(b)\alpha(a)=\alpha([a,b])
. That is,
\alpha
is a map of Lie algebras.

3.

\alpha
is
\operatorname{Sp}(M)
-equivariant.

Given
s\in Weyl
, there is a natural element
\tilde{s}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Sp}(M),\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Sp}(M)}\otimes Weyl)
, whose value over
g\in\operatorname{Sp}(M)
is
g(s)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K\in\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl-\mathrm{mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}-\mathrm{mod}
be the cohomological shift by
-\dim\operatorname{Sp}(M)
of the object defined as follows:

1.

As a quasi-coherent sheaf on
\operatorname{Sp}(M)
,
K
is the vector bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Sp}(M)}\otimes Weyl
.

2.

The connection on
K
is given by the formula

\nabla_{a}(f\otimes s)=-\mathrm{Lie}_{a}(f)\otimes s-f\otimes s\alpha(a).

3.

The left
Weyl
action is given by multiplication from the left on the
Weyl
factor.

4.

The
Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}
action by
s\in Weyl
is given by multiplication from the right by
\tilde{s}
.

One easily verifies that the above connection is flat, and commutes with the left and right
Weyl
actions, so that
K
is indeed an object of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}{-mod}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

1.

K
is holonomic. That is,
K\in\operatorname{D-mod}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}{-mod}

2.

K
is weakly
\operatorname{Sp}(M)
-equivariant.

3.

K
as a kernel object defines an action of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))
on
Weyl\mathrm{-mod}
.

Let us spell out (3) explicitly. Given
A\in\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))
and
N\in Weyl\mathrm{-mod}
, we have

A*N:=\Gamma_{dR}(\operatorname{Sp}(M),A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}K\otimes_{Weyl}N).

3.1.1
Invertibility of the kernel

Let

K^{-1}\in\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}{-mod}

be defined as the pullback of
K
through the inverse automorphism of
\operatorname{Sp}(M)
.

Let

K^{-1}\star K\in\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}{-mod}

be the image of

K^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}K\in\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}{-mod}\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}{-mod}

under the map
1_{\operatorname{Sp}(M)}\otimes 1_{Weyl\mathrm{-mod}}\otimes\epsilon_{Weyl}\otimes 1_{Weyl^{op}\mathrm{-mod}}
, where

\epsilon_{Weyl}:Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}{-mod}\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod}\to\mathrm{Vect}

is the co-unit map for the algebra
Weyl
(that is,
N_{1}\boxtimes N_{2}\mapsto N_{1}\otimes_{Weyl}N_{2}
).
Then by definition of
K
, one verifies that
K^{-1}\star K
is the external tensor product of the dualizing D-module on
\operatorname{Sp}(V)
and the unit
Weyl\in Weyl{-mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{-mod}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume that
A\in\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))^{\heartsuit}
and
N\in(Weyl\mathrm{-mod})^{\heartsuit}
are simple objects. Then
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}K\otimes_{Weyl}N\in(\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod})^{\heartsuit}
is a simple object too.

Proof.

First note that
K[\dim\operatorname{Sp}(M)],K^{-1}[\dim\operatorname{Sp}(M)]
are in the hearts of the categories in which they are defined. This already implies that
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}K\otimes_{Weyl}N
is in the heart, and also that the functor

(1_{\operatorname{Sp}(M)}\otimes 1_{Weyl\mathrm{-mod}}\otimes\epsilon_{Weyl})(K^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}-)

is t-exact. Applying it to
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}K\otimes_{Weyl}N
we get

A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}(\omega_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}\boxtimes Weyl)\otimes_{Weyl}N\cong A\boxtimes N\in(\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod})^{\heartsuit},

which is a simple object. It follows that
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}K\otimes_{Weyl}N
is a simple object too.
∎

3.1.2
Calculation of the Hecke action

We now give the finite dimensional analogue of the calculation of the Hecke action on the basic object
E_{0}
.

Let
G
be a reductive group acting on

by automorphisms preserving the symplectic form (i.e. we have a map
G\to\operatorname{Sp}(M)
).
Let
L_{0}
be a Lagrangian subspace of

, and let

and

be the parabolic subgroups stabilizing it.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be the determinant line bundle on
\operatorname{Sp}(M)/P_{0}
, defined as the inverse of the determinant of the tautological vector bundle on
\operatorname{Sp}(M)/P_{0}
whose fiber over a point

is
gL_{0}
. It is an ample line bundle, and its pullback to
\operatorname{Sp}(M)
is trivial. We will denote its restriction to
G/P
by the same letter
We let
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\kappa}
be the category of twisted D-modules on an algebraic variety equipped with a morphism to
\operatorname{Sp}(M)/P_{0}
, where the twisting is by
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes\kappa}
(

).
Since the pullback of
\mathcal{D}
to
\operatorname{Sp}(M)
is trivial, we may view the previous action of
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))
as an action of
\operatorname{D-mod}_{-1/2}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))
. In particular, we view
K
as an object of

\operatorname{D-mod}_{1/2}(\operatorname{Sp}(M))\otimes Weyl-\mathrm{mod}\otimes Weyl^{\mathrm{op}}-\mathrm{mod}.

Restricting the action constructed above to
G
and passing to
P

\operatorname{D-mod}_{-1/2}(G/P)\otimes(Weyl\mathrm{-mod})^{P}\to Weyl\mathrm{-mod},

where the superscript

denotes the category of strongly

-equivariant objects (with respect to the twisted by
-1/2
action). This functor also upgrades to give an action of the Hecke algebra
\operatorname{D-mod}_{-1/2,P}(G/P)
on the category of strongly
P
-equivariant objects.

We consider
E:=Weyl/L_{0}\in Weyl\mathrm{-mod}
.
This object is strongly
P
-equivariant (this would fail if we considered the action without the twist). This is equivalent to saying that
K\otimes_{Weyl}Weyl/L_{0}
descends (with respect to the t-exact pullback) to a twisted D-module on
G/P
. This is an object of
\operatorname{D-mod}_{1/2}(G/P)\otimes Weyl\mathrm{-mod}
which we denote by
E^{\prime}
. Its image under the forgetful functor to
\operatorname{D-mod}_{1/2}(G/P)
is described in
[
19
, §1.3]
, up to a cohomological shift. In order to recover
E^{\prime}
itself from this description, one should take into the account the
Weyl
-action given by left multiplication, which commutes with all other structures given. Again, the twist here is essential.

As a result, given
A\in\operatorname{D-mod}_{-1/2}(G/P)
, whose !-pullback to
G
is
\tilde{A}
, we have

A*E:=\tilde{A}*E\cong\Gamma_{dR}(G/P,A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}E^{\prime}).

Now let us fix a Lagrangian
L_{0}^{*}\subset M
transversal to
L_{0}
, which gives an equivalence
Weyl\mathrm{-mod}\cong\operatorname{D-mod}(M/L_{0})
. Under this equivalence,
E
corresponds to the delta D-module at
0\in M/L_{0}
.
This way,
E^{\prime}
corresponds to an element of

\operatorname{D-mod}_{1/2}(G/P)\otimes\operatorname{D-mod}(M/L_{0})\cong\operatorname{D-mod}_{1/2}(G/P\times M/L_{0}),

which we also denote by
E^{\prime}
(in the last expression the twisting is only on the first factor).

Given
g\in G/P
, it defines a Lagrangian subspace
L_{g}
.
The !-restriction of
E^{\prime}
to the preimage of
g
is isomorphic to
Weyl/L_{g}
, which under the above equivalence corresponds to a D-module supported on the image of
L_{g}
in
M/L_{0}
. Let us now describe what it is as a D-module on
(L_{g}+L_{0})/L_{0}
.
The restriction of the symplectic form to
(L_{g}+L_{0})/L_{g}\cap L_{0}
is non-degenerate, and inside the resulting symplectic vector space, we have the following three Lagrangian subspaces:
L_{0}/L_{g}\cap L_{0}
,
L_{g}/L_{g}\cap L_{0}
, and
(L_{0}^{*}\cap(L_{g}+L_{0})+L_{g}\cap L_{0})/L_{g}\cap L_{0}
. The second and the third are transversal to the first, and so we can consider the third as the graph of a linear map from
L_{g}/L_{g}\cap L_{0}
to
L_{0}/L_{g}\cap L_{0}
.
This gives a symmetric bilinear form on
L_{g}/L_{g}\cap L_{0}\cong(L_{g}+L_{0})/L_{0}
, and the D-module above is the exponential of the associated quadratic form.
The reason for this is simple - this exponential D-module is defined by the differential equations given by
\partial_{\ell}-B(\ell,-)
for
\ell\in L_{g}
, where
B
is the bilinear form described above. Here
\partial_{\ell}
is a linear differential and
B(\ell,-)
is a linear function.

This isomorphism is consistent along smooth families in which the dimension of
L_{g}\cap L_{0}
is constant, and the twisting is integral. Namely, given such a family
U
, and
\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}
a square root for the determinant line bundle on
U
, we have a polynomial function
f
which over a point
g
gives the above quadratic form. Then the !-restriction of
E^{\prime}
to the preimage of
U
is isomorphic to the cohomological shift by
-\dim U
of
\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}\otimes\exp(f)
. Here
\exp(f)
is a D-module on the total space of the vector bundle on
U
whose fibers are
(L_{g}+L_{0})/L_{0}
. It is defined by the differential equations
\partial_{\ell}-B_{g}(\ell,-)
as above, where
\ell\in L_{g}
and
B_{g}
is dependent on
g
in a polynomial manner.

Given an element
A\in\operatorname{D-mod}_{-1/2}(G/P)
, the object
A*E
is given by the pushforward of
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}E^{\prime}
along the projection
G/P\times M/L_{0}\to M/L_{0}
.

In particular, let
Z
be a closed subscheme of
G/P
, and
U
a smooth open and dense subscheme of
Z
such that the Lagrangian determinant line bundle has a square root
\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}
over
U
, and the dimension of
L_{g}\cap L_{0}
is constant for
g\in U
. Let
A
be the twisted delta (IC) D-module on
Z
, that is, the intermediate extension (in the category of twisted D-modules) from
U
of
\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}\otimes\omega_{U}[\dim U]
.
Then
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}E^{\prime}
is supported on
Z\times M/L_{0}
. It is holonomic (as both
A
and
E^{\prime}
are). Its restriction to
U\times M/L_{0}
is (the pushforward of)
\sqrt{\mathcal{D}}\otimes\exp(f)
, and the !-pullback to the preimage of any
g\in Z
is the exponential D-module on
(L_{g}+L_{0})/L_{0}
described above. By corollary
3.3
, the pullback of
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}E^{\prime}
to
G\times M/L_{0}
is a simple object of the heart (here by pullback we mean the t-exact pullback). It follows that
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\otimes}}E^{\prime}
is a simple object of the heart too.

3.2
The Loop group case

We now consider the loop Weyl algebra and its category of modules, as recalled in the introduction to this paper. As there is no risk of confusion, we will denote it by

We do not aim to treat the technicalities of the situation, only recall the main results and bottom lines.

Let

Lemma 3.4

There is a categorical action of

7.1
The categorical quotient

We choose and fix a vector
u_{0}\in(U^{\prime})^{\perp}\subset U
of square length 1.
We also fix the Killing forms on
\mathfrak{so}(U),\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime}),\mathfrak{gl}(L)
associated to the representations
U,U^{\prime},L
respectively. We will use these throughout this section and subsequent ones to identify the above Lie algebras with their linear duals. We will try to formulate our results in a manner that is independent of these choices, but use these identifications freely in the proofs.

There is an
\operatorname{SO}(U^{\prime})
-equivariant isomorphism
\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})\times U^{\prime}\cong\mathfrak{so}(U)
, given by

(x,v)\mapsto-x-u_{0}v^{t}+vu_{0}^{t}.

Where
w^{t}:=\langle w,-\rangle
for any
w\in U
, and
w_{1}w_{2}^{t}:=\langle w_{2},-\rangle w_{1}
.
We will denote by

\varpi:\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})^{*}\times U^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{so}(U)^{*}

the resulting isomorphism, where we use the Killing forms above to identify the Lie algebras with their linear duals.
By
[
31
, Table 3a, row 2]
, the ring of invariants
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})^{*}\times U^{\prime}]^{\operatorname{SO}(U^{\prime})}
is a polynomial ring generated by the following invariants:

•

The generators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})^{*}]^{\operatorname{SO}(U^{\prime})}=\mathbb{C}[\Sigma_{2n}]
.

•

(x,v)\mapsto\langle v,x^{2k}v\rangle
, for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
.

Given a
k\times k
matrix
x
we denote its characteristic polynomial by

z^{k}+c_{1}(x)z^{k-1}+\ldots+c_{k}(x).

We will make use of the following formula, (see for example
[
23
, Theorem A.2]
):

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{gl}(W)
and let
w\in W,w^{*}\in W^{*}
. Then

c_{i}(x+ww^{*})=c_{i}(x)-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}c_{i-j-1}(x)\langle w^{*},x^{j}w\rangle.

Claim 7.2
.

The map

(\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})^{*}\times U^{\prime})/\!\!/\operatorname{SO}(U^{\prime})\to\Sigma_{2n}\times\Sigma_{2n+1}

whose first coordinate is given projecting on the
\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})^{*}
-coordinate and applying the quotient map for
\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})^{*}
, and whose second coordinate is given by composing
\varpi
with the quotient map for
\mathfrak{so}(U)^{*}
, is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The ring
\mathbb{C}[\Sigma_{2n+1}]
is generated by the even coefficients of the characteristic polynomial (the odd ones vanish). We have for
i
even:

\displaystyle c_{i}(-x-u_{0}v^{t}+vu_{0}^{t})=

\displaystyle c_{i}(x+u_{0}v^{t}-vu_{0}^{t})

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{i}(x+u_{0}v^{t})+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}c_{i-j-1}(x+u_{0}v^{t})\langle u_{0},(x+u_{0}v^{t})^{j}v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{i}(x+u_{0}v^{t})+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}c_{i-j-1}(x+u_{0}v^{t})\langle u_{0},u_{0}v^{t}x^{j-1}v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{i}(x)-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}c_{i-j-1}(x)\langle v,x^{j}u_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\left(c_{i-j-1}(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{i-j-2}c_{i-j-2-k}(x)\langle v,x^{k}u_{0}\rangle\right)\langle v,x^{j-1}v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{i}(x)+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\left(c_{i-j-1}(x)\right)\langle v,x^{j-1}v\rangle

We see that indeed over the ring
\mathbb{C}[\Sigma_{2n}]
, the term
c_{i}(-x-u_{0}v^{t}+vu_{0}^{t})
is equal to
\langle v,x^{i-2}v\rangle
plus lower order terms.

Proposition 7.3
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[\Sigma_{2n}\times\Sigma_{2n+1}]

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\operatorname{D-mod}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})_{\mathcal{O}}\times\operatorname{Sp}(V)_{\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})}\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)})}(\delta_{1},\delta_{1})

Claim 7.4

For

Claim 7.5

Lemma 7.6

\geq 2

in this preimage, its stabilizer in

Proof.

We set

to be the subset of quadruples
(x,v,N^{\prime},M^{\prime})
(here

are

and

and

•

is regular

•

M^{\prime}
is isotropic (that is,
(M^{\prime})^{\perp}\supset M^{\prime}
)

•

N^{\prime}=M^{\prime}\cap U^{\prime}

•

M^{\prime}
is preserved by
\varpi(x,v)

•

\varpi(x,v)\left((N^{\prime})^{\perp,U}\right)\subset\mathbb{C}v+(N^{\prime})^{\perp,U}

is cyclic for
\varpi(x,v)
modulo
(N^{\prime})^{\perp,U}

Clearly, by setting

We show that

The map
\operatorname{SO}(U^{\prime})\times\Sigma_{(e,f,h,v_{0}^{*})}\to X

2.

The image of the map

3.

The above image contains the preimage along the map

4.

Over this Zariski open dense subset, the stabilizers are trivial (as in the formulation of the lemma).

To show (1), we begin by identifying
U^{\prime}/(N^{\prime})^{\perp,U^{\prime}}\cong U/(N^{\prime})^{\perp,U}\cong(N^{\prime})^{*}

F^{i}(N^{\prime})^{*}:=\mathrm{Span}(\{\varpi(x,v)^{j}v\mid j\leq i\}).

We also consider the dual filtration, defined on
N^{\prime}
by taking the perpendicular subspaces.
Let
\mathrm{B}^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{SO}(U^{\prime})
be the subgroup formed by transformation preserving

as well as the above filtrations on

\operatorname{SO}(U^{\prime})
, and we transform

by an element of
\operatorname{SO}(U^{\prime})

.
In particular, we may assume that

Next, we note that by definition, for

One easily observes that it follows that

satisfies these same properties, and that

and

.
Moreover, taking

\displaystyle x^{2}s-v

\displaystyle=x(xs+u_{0})-\langle-xs-u_{0},v\rangle u_{0}-v-\langle xs,v\rangle u_{0}=

\displaystyle-x(\varpi(x,v)s)-\langle\varpi(x,v)s,v\rangle u_{0}+\langle\varpi(x,v)s,u_{0}\rangle v-\langle xs,v\rangle u_{0}=

\displaystyle\varpi(x,v)^{2}s-\langle xs,v\rangle u_{0}\in U^{\prime}\cap(M^{\prime}+\mathbb{C}u_{0})\subset N^{\perp,U^{\prime}}.

This implies that

From all the above, it follows that one may transform by an element of the standard torus (the centralizer of

We may further transform by an element of

Since

.
Now, write

This finishes part 1 of the proof.

For part 2, look at the spectral decomposition

with respect to the linear transformation
\varpi(x,v)
(here

is the set of eigenvalues). We write also
v=\sum_{\lambda\in S}v_{\lambda}
,
v_{\lambda}\in U_{\lambda}
. We show that the following subset

1.

\varpi(x,v)

2.

For each

\varpi(x,v)

3.

Either

.

We start by showing that the complement of

is indeed of codimension at least 2.
The first requirement indeed cuts out only a codimension
\geq 2

Now, assuming that the first requirement indeed holds, for any

, the

is cyclic.
The subset of such

for which there exists a

respectively is also of codimension 2. Last, the third requirement also forbids a space of codimension 2, as the subsets in which

Now let us show that

is contained in the image of

.
Let
(x,v)\in Y
, and let

be a subset which contains exactly one of
\lambda,-\lambda
for every

is a cyclic vector in

for
\varpi(x,v)
. Let

We also set

(note that

\tilde{U}_{0}:=\ker(\varpi(x,v)^{\frac{d-1}{2}})=\varpi(x,v)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}U_{0}.

We define

. It is an isotropic subspace of dimension

, preserved by

.

Claim 7.7
.

If

is in the image of
\varpi(x,v)

is cyclic for

Assume the

is not in the image of
\varpi(x,v)
, the

\varpi(x,v)

.
∎

It is impossible that

, as if this were the case, then it would follow from

\check{G}\times^{\check{N}}\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp}[2]
, and
\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp}\subset\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is the annihilator of
\check{\mathfrak{n}}
.

Note that this functor sends
V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}[2]}
to
(V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp}[2]})^{\check{N}}
.

Proposition B.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{T}
be the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
to a
T_{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant multiplicative line bundle on
\operatorname{Gr}_{T}
. The bilinear form
e_{\mathcal{L}_{T}}\in\check{\mathfrak{t}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{t}}
associated to it under the previous construction for the group
T
is the image of
e_{\mathcal{L}}\in(\check{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{g}})^{\check{G}}
under the map
\check{\mathfrak{g}}\to\check{\mathfrak{t}}
annihilating
\check{\mathfrak{n}}
and
\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{e_{\mathcal{L}}}\in\check{\mathfrak{t}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{t}}
be the second bilinear form described above, which we wish to show is equal to
e_{\mathcal{L}_{T}}
.
We must compare the two endomorphisms of
(V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp}})^{\check{N}}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(\check{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}[2]/\check{T})
(for
V\in\mathrm{Rep}(\check{G})
) resulting from
\overline{e_{\mathcal{L}}}
and
e_{\mathcal{L}_{T}}
. It is enough to compare them on the regular semisimple locus of
\check{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. We have
\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp}_{\mathrm{rss}}\cong\check{N}\times\check{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{\mathrm{rss}}
, with the projection on
\check{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}_{\mathrm{rss}}
given by the composition
\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp}\subset\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to\check{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
(note that this map is indeed
\check{N}
-equivariant) and a section given by
s:\check{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\to(\check{\mathfrak{b}}^{-})^{*}\cong\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp}
. Thus, it is enough to compare the two endomorphisms when restricted to
\check{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
through
s
.

This restriction of the endomorphism induced by
e_{\mathcal{L}_{T}}
is given by the image of
e_{\mathcal{L}}
under the map
(\check{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{g}})^{\check{G}}\to(\check{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{t}})
, given by annihilating
\check{\mathfrak{n}}
and
\check{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}
. However, this map factors through
\check{\mathfrak{t}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{t}}
(this can be seen by considering the weights of
\check{T}
), and the desired equality
e_{\mathcal{L}_{T}}=\overline{e_{\mathcal{L}}}
follows.
∎

In the case that
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple with Weyl group
W
, all invariant bilinear forms on
\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
are symmetric, and the above map
(\check{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{g}})^{\check{G}}\to\check{\mathfrak{t}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{t}}
defines an isomorphism
(\check{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{g}})^{\check{G}}\cong(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\check{\mathfrak{t}})^{W}
.
Combining the above results, we get the following Corollary:

Corollary B.6
.

Assume that
G
is semisimple, and let
\mathcal{L}
be a multiplicative line bundle on
\operatorname{Gr}_{G}
. Let
e_{\mathcal{L}_{T}}
be the bilinear form on
X_{*}(T)
pairing
\lambda,\mu
to the weight of
\mu
on the fiber of
\mathcal{L}
at
t^{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Gr}_{G}
. It is symmetric and
W
-invariant.
Let
e_{\mathcal{L}}
be the invariant symmetric bilinear form on
\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
whose image in
(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\check{\mathfrak{t}})^{W}
is given by
e_{\mathcal{L}^{T}}
. Given
V\in\mathrm{Rep}(\check{G})
, the element

-\cup c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\in\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{\operatorname{D-mod}_{G_{\mathcal{O}}}(\operatorname{Gr}_{G})}(\phi_{G}(V),\phi_{G}(V))

corresponds under derived Satake to the image of

e_{\mathcal{L}}\in(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\check{\mathfrak{g}})^{\check{G}}\to(\check{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\check{\mathfrak{g}})^{\check{G}}\to(\mathrm{End}(V)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}(\check{\mathfrak{g}}[-2]))^{\check{G}}[2]\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{\mathrm{QCoh}_{\check{G}}(\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}[2])}(V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}[2]},V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\check{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}[2]}).

B.1
The determinant line bundle

The missing ingredient in the above discussion is the construction of multiplicative line bundles. In this section we construct a multiplicative line bundle for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
, called the determinant line bundle, and discuss the resulting pullbacks to symplectic and orthogonal groups.

This line bundle is well studied, starting from
[
2
]
, and actually possesses a factorizable structure (which in particular implies a multiplicative structure). The reader is warned that while this line bundle, as well as its pullback from
\operatorname{SL}_{2n}
to
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}
, is widely referred to as the determinant line bundle, the pullback from
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
to
\operatorname{SO}_{n}
of this line bundle is usually not called the determinant line bundle. Rather, what is usually called the determinant line bundle (or Pfaffian line bundle, cf.
[
2
]
) on the connected component of
1\in\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}_{n}}
is the ample generator of the Picard group, which is a square root of the restriction of the above line bundle.

B.1.1
Special linear groups

Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}
. Recall that points in
\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}}
classify lattices in
\mathcal{K}^{n}
with determinant equal to
\mathcal{O}
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be the determinant line bundle on
\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}}
, whose fiber at a lattice
\Lambda
is given by
\det(\Lambda/\Lambda\cap\mathcal{O}^{n})^{-1}\otimes\det(\mathcal{O}^{n}/\Lambda\cap\mathcal{O}^{n})
, i.e. the inverse of the relative determinant of
\Lambda
with respect to
\mathcal{O}^{n}
. Due to the transitivity of relative determinants (i.e. if
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}
are lattices then the relative determinant of
\Lambda_{1}
with respect to
\Lambda_{3}
is the tensor product of the relative determinant of
\Lambda_{1}
with respect to
\Lambda_{2}
and the relative determinant of
\Lambda_{2}
with respect to
\Lambda_{3}
), we get that
\mathcal{D}
has a natural multiplicative structure.

Let
\mu=(1,0,\dots,0,-1)
be the dominant minimal cocharacter of
T
.
It acts on the fiber of
\mathcal{D}
at
t^{\mu}
with weight
2
. The associated
W
-invariant quadratic form on
X_{*}(T)
, in the sense of Corollary
B.6
is then given by
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\mapsto\sum\lambda_{i}^{2}
.

Given any reductive group
H
mapping to
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
, we get a multiplicative line bundle on
\operatorname{Gr}_{H}
by pulling back
\mathcal{D}
along the induced map
\operatorname{Gr}_{H}\to\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}}
. The associated quadratic form is then given by the restriction along
X_{*}(T_{H})\to X_{*}(T)
of the above quadratic form on
X_{*}(T)
.

B.1.2
Orthogonal and symplectic groups

We now consider the situations of
\operatorname{Sp}(V),\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})
, where we use the notation of Section
2
.
Start with
G=\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})
. Consider the quadratic form on
\mathrm{End}(U^{\prime})\cong U^{\prime}\otimes(U^{\prime})^{*}
, which can be also written as
X\mapsto\mathrm{tr}(X^{2})
. Its restriction to
\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})
is non-degenerate, and we consider inverse, which is an invariant quadratic form on
\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})^{*}
we denote by
B_{U^{\prime}}
.
In the standard choice of a basis of
X_{*}(T)
(that is,
(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0)
) the resulting
W
-invariant quadratic form on
X_{*}(T)
takes the value
1/2
on every basis element.
Considering the embedding
\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})\to\operatorname{SL}(V^{\prime})
, we get the multiplicative line bundle
\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})}
on
\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})}
.
It corresponds (in the sense of Corollary
B.6
) to the invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{so}(U^{\prime})^{*}
given by
4B_{U^{\prime}}
.

Let
Q\subset\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})}
be the minuscule orbit, isomorphic to the quadratic of isotropic vectors in
\mathbb{P}(V^{\prime})
. Any
\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})
-equivariant line bundle on
Q
is an integer power of the restriction from
\mathbb{P}(V^{\prime})
of
\mathcal{O}(1)
, with the natural equivariant structure.
Let us compute the power of
\mathcal{O}(1)
equal to the restriction of
\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})}
to
Q
. Letting
\mu
be the dominant minuscule cocharacter of
T
, we get that
\mu
acts on the fiber of
\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})}
at
t^{\mu}
with weight
2
. In comparison, a direct computation shows that the cocharacter
\mu
acts on the fiber of
\mathcal{O}(1)
, which is the dual to the tautological bundle, with weight
1
. We deduce that as
\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})_{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant line bundles, we have
\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{SO}(V^{\prime})}|_{Q}\cong\mathcal{O}(2)
.

For
G=\operatorname{Sp}(V)
the situation is similar. We define
B_{U}
in the same way as before, and again in the standard basis of cocharacters the associated
W
-invariant quadratic form on
X_{*}(T)
takes the value
1/2
on every basis element. Again we have
\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}
, which is the determinant line bundle pulled back from
\operatorname{SL}(V)
, whose associated quadratic form is
4B_{U}
.
Now considering the minimal orbit
\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}^{\mathrm{min}}\in\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}
, and
\mu\in X_{*}(T)
the minimal dominant cocharacter, we once again have that all
\operatorname{Sp}(V)_{\mathcal{O}}
-equivariant line bundles on
\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}^{\mathrm{min}}
are pulled back from
\operatorname{Sp}(V)
-equivariant line bundles on
\mathbb{P}(V)
along the projection
\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}^{\mathrm{min}}\to\mathbb{P}(V)
described in Section
4
. These are all integral powers of
\mathcal{O}(1)
with the natural equivariant structure. Again the weight of the action of
\mu
on the fiber at
t^{\mu}\in\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}^{\mathrm{min}}
of
\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}
is
2
, while the weight of the action of
\mu
on the pullback of
\mathcal{O}(1)
is
1
(by the same kind of direct computation as before). Thus, we get
\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}|_{\operatorname{Gr}_{\operatorname{Sp}(V)}^{\mathrm{min}}}\cong\mathcal{O}(2)
.
