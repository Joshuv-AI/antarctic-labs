Operator Geometry of Hilbert Ball Automorphisms
Saikat Roy

Abstract
. We consider the operator–theoretic model for the group of biholomorphic automorphisms A ​ u ​ t ​ ( B ) Aut(B) of the unit ball B B of a complex Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} by representing each automorphism as a bounded linear operator on the augmented Hilbert space ℋ ⊕ ℂ \mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C} . Any member of A ​ u ​ t ​ ( B ) Aut(B) admits a natural block operator matrix representation acting on ℋ ⊕ ℂ \mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C} . We study the geometry of the subset M ⁡ ( ℋ ) M(\mathcal{H}) of ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ⊕ ℂ ) \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}) consisting of these block operator matrices. It is shown that every element corresponding to a non-rotation automorphism is a smooth point of ℬ ⁡ ( ℋ ⊕ ℂ ) \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}) . Orthogonality between two such matrices is characterized geometrically by the antipodality of the corresponding Möbius images of a boundary point of the ball. This orthogonality characterization is applied to show that an inner automorphism of Aut ⁡ ( B ) \operatorname{Aut}(B) that preserves Birkhoff–James orthogonality in both directions if and only if it is conjugation by a pure rotation, yielding a rigidity result. The normalized block matrices are J J -unitary under a suitable normalization, where J = diag ⁡ ( I ℋ , − 1 ) J=\operatorname{diag}(I_{\mathcal{H}},-1) . We show that norm of such block matrices satisfy a submultiplicativity under a certain composition rule other than usual operator multiplication, and induce a metric on certain subsets of A ​ u ​ t ​ ( B ) Aut(B) which recover the hyperbolic metric on the Hilbert ball. The symmetric structure of Birkhoff–James orthogonality within M ⁡ ( ℋ ) M(\mathcal{H}) is also studied: there are no left-symmetric points, while the only right-symmetric points are pure rotations.

1.
Introduction

The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
of the open unit ball
B
of a complex
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is one of the central objects in infinite-dimensional complex function theory. Its elements are the Möbius transformations

\phi_{a,U}(z)=U\frac{(P_{a}+\alpha(a)Q_{a})z-a}{1-\langle z,a\rangle},\qquad z\in B,

where
U
is a unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}
,
a\in B
,
\alpha(a)=\sqrt{1-\|a\|^{2}}
, and
P_{a}
,
Q_{a}
denote the orthogonal projections onto
\mathbb{C}a
and its orthogonal complement, respectively. Every
\phi_{a,U}\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
extends continuously to the closed unit ball
\overline{B}
, and the restriction to the unit sphere
\partial B
is a homeomorphism of
\partial B
onto itself
[
13
,
5
]
. The group
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
plays a fundamental role in function theory, hyperbolic geometry, and the fixed point theory of holomorphic mappings
[
2
,
3
,
6
]
. The special elements
\phi_{0,U}
, corresponding to
a=0
, are the
pure rotations
and form a subgroup isomorphic to the group of unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
.

A natural operator–theoretic representation of
\operatorname{Aut}(B)

arises by associating to each
\phi_{a,U}
the block operator matrix

M_{\phi_{a,U}}=\begin{bmatrix}U(P_{a}+\alpha(a)Q_{a})&-Ua\\
-a^{*}&1\end{bmatrix}

acting on
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, where the entries are understood as follows:

•

U(P_{a}+\alpha(a)Q_{a}):\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a bounded linear operator,

•

-Ua:\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{H}
is the rank-one operator defined by

\lambda\mapsto-\lambda\,Ua,

•

-a^{*}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is the bounded linear functional given by

x\mapsto-\langle x,a\rangle,

•

1:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is the identity operator.

Accordingly, for
(x,\lambda)\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}
,

M_{\phi_{a,U}}(x,\lambda)=\left(U(P_{a}+\alpha(a)Q_{a})x-\lambda Ua,\;-\langle x,a\rangle+\lambda\right).

In the special case whenever
U=I_{\mathcal{H}}
, the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
, the block matrix
M_{\phi_{a,I}}
associated to the automorphism
\phi_{a,I}
is self-adjoint for any
a\in B
.

1.1.
Motivation and Main results

The assignment
\phi_{a,U}\mapsto M_{\phi_{a,U}}
realizes
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
as a subset
M(\mathcal{H})
of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
and opens a natural avenue for investigating the geometric properties of
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
through operator space geometry, and this perspective is the central theme of the present article. Two geometric properties central to our investigation are smoothness and Birkhoff-James orthogonality. A nonzero element
x
in a normed space
X
is called a
smooth point
if the norm on
X
is Gâteaux differentiable at
x

[
9
,
12
]
. In a normed space
X
, an element
x
is
Birkhoff–James orthogonal
to
y
, written
x\perp_{B}y
, if
\|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|
for all scalars
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

We show that non-unitary points of
M\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
corresponds smooth points in
B\left(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\right)
and this is one of the main results of this article.

Theorem 1.1
.

Every element of
M\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
which is not a pure rotation, represents a smooth point in
B\left(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\right)
.

Also, Birkhoff-James orthogonality in
M\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
is equivalent with the antipodality of certain points.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
a\in B
be nonzero. Then
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}
in
M\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
if and only if
\phi_{a,U}\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
and
\phi_{b,V}\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
are antipodal points in the unit sphere
\partial B
.

Unlike orthogonality in inner product spaces, Birkhoff–James orthogonality is not symmetric in general
[
15
]
. An element
x
is called
left-symmetric
(respectively,
right-symmetric
) if
x\perp_{B}y
implies
y\perp_{B}x
(respectively,
y\perp_{B}x
implies
x\perp_{B}y
) for all
y\in X
. A point is called a symmetric point, if it is both left and right-symmetric. In the present paper, we say that
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\in M(\mathcal{H})
is
left-symmetric
(respectively,
right-symmetric
) in
M(\mathcal{H})
if
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}
implies
M_{\phi_{b,V}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
(respectively,
M_{\phi_{b,V}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
implies
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}
) for all
M_{\phi_{b,V}}\in M(\mathcal{H})
,
where the Birkhoff–James orthogonality is considered in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
. A point in
M(\mathcal{H})
is said to be symmetric, if it is left as well as right-symmetric point in
M(\mathcal{H})
. Characterizing symmetric points in operator algebras has been an active direction of research
[
11
,
16
,
10
]
. However, the study of symmetricity in nonlinear subsets remain absent in the literature. We initiate this study in this article for the nonlinear subset
M(\mathcal{H})
of
B\left(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\right)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

In
M(\mathcal{H})
, there are no left-symmetric points, and the right-symmetric points are precisely the pure rotations.

The orthogonality relation in
M(\mathcal{H})
naturally induces an orthogonality relation
\perp_{Aut}
in
Aut(B)
, namely,

\phi_{a,U}\perp_{Aut}\phi_{b,V}\quad\iff\quad M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}.

We apply the preceding result to characterize inner automorphism of
Aut(B)
that preserves Birkhoff-James orthogonality in both directions.

Theorem 1.4
.

Any inner automorphism in
Aut(B)
preserves
\perp_{Aut}
in both direction if and only if it is a conjugation by a pure rotation.

As one of the important application of our result, we recover the hyperbolic metric through operator norm, and prove a submultiplicativity of norm which different that usual submultiplicativity of operator norm. For this we consider normalized block matrix representation
\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}=\frac{1}{\alpha(a)}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
, and define fibre of a unitary operator
U\in B(\mathcal{H})
, namely,

F_{U}=\{\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}:~a\in B\}.

We define a multiplication
``\odot"
in
F_{U}
by

\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\odot\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}}=\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}},

where
``\circ"
indicates the usual operation in
Aut(B)
. Consistent with the above terminologies, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
U
be any unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Then

(a)

\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\odot\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}}\|\leq\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\|\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}}\|
.

(b)

The function
d_{U}:F_{U}\times F_{U}
be defined as

d_{U}(\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}})=2~ln~\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\odot\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}^{-1}}\|

is a metric on
F_{U}
and
(F_{U},d_{U})
is isometric to
(B,d_{hyp})
, where
d_{hyp}
denote the hyperbolic metric on
B
.

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are included in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we discuss the symmetricity and preservation of orthogonality. Section
5
deals with the operator norm and hyperbolic metric. In Section
2
collect the preliminary facts to be used in the sequel.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
A
be a bounded linear operator on
X
. A sequence of unit vectors
(z_{n})
in
X
is called a
norming sequence
for
A
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Az_{n}\|=\|A\|.

Norming sequences are the key ingredient in the characterization of Birkhoff–James orthogonality of operators.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

For
A,T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
A\perp_{B}T
if and only if
there exists a norming sequence
(x_{n})
for
A
such that

\lim_{n}\langle Ax_{n},Tx_{n}\rangle=0
.

Smoothness of operators on a Banach space can also be characterized in terms of norming sequences.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
A
be a nonzero operator acting on a Banach space
X
, then
A
is a smooth point in
B\left(X\right)
if and only if the collection

\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{C}:~\lim_{n}x_{n}^{*}\left(Tx_{n}\right)=\mu,~\left((x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\right)\subset S_{X}\times S_{X^{*}},~\lim_{n}x_{n}^{*}\left(Ax_{n}\right)=\|A\|\right\}

is a singleton set for any
T\in B\left(X\right)
.

If
X=\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space, then identifying
x_{n}^{*}=\langle\cdot,y_{n}\rangle
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, via the Riesz representation theorem, the preceding lemma takes the following form.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
A
be a nonzero operator acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then
A
is a smooth point of

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if the collection

\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{C}:\lim_{n}\langle Tx_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\mu,\ (x_{n}),(y_{n})\subset S_{\mathcal{H}},\ \lim_{n}\langle Ax_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\|A\|\right\}

is a singleton set for every
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

The following lemma is concerning about norm attainment and norming sequence of operators that admit a reducing subspace.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be a closed proper subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, and let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be such that
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a reducing subspace of
A
. Then

\|A\|=\max\left\{\|AP_{0}\|,\|A(I-P_{0})\|\right\},

where
P_{0}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Moreover, if
\|A\|=\|AP_{0}\|>\|A(I-P_{0})\|
, then
(z_{n})
is a norming sequence for
A
if and only if
(z_{n})
is a norming sequence
for
AP_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a reducing subspace of
A
, let
T\oplus S
be the block matrix representation of
A
with respect to the orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
, where
T:=A|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}:\mathcal{H}_{0}\to\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
S:=A|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}}:\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}\to\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
. For any unit vector
z\in\mathcal{H}
we write
z=x+y
with
x=P_{0}z\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
y=(I-P_{0})z\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
. Since,
P_{0}
commutes with
A
, we have

\|Az\|^{2}=\|AP_{0}z\|^{2}+\|A(I-P_{0})z\|^{2}\leq\max\left\{\|AP_{0}\|^{2},\|A(I-P_{0})\|^{2}\right\}\|z\|^{2}.

Since
\|A\|\geq\|AP_{0}\|
and
\|A\|\geq\|A(I-P_{0})\|
, we conclude that

\|A\|=\max\left\{\|AP_{0}\|,\|A(I-P_{0})\|\right\}.

Now suppose
\|A\|=\|AP_{0}\|>\|A(I-P_{0})\|
, and let
(z_{n})
be a norming sequence for
A
. Write
z_{n}=x_{n}+y_{n}
with
x_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
y_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
, so that

\|x_{n}\|^{2}+\|y_{n}\|^{2}=1
for each
n
. Choose
\lambda
with

\|AP_{0}\|>\lambda>\|A(I-P_{0})\|
. Then

\displaystyle\|Az_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|AP_{0}x_{n}\|^{2}+\|A(I-P_{0})y_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|AP_{0}\|^{2}\|x_{n}\|^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|y_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|AP_{0}\|^{2}(\|x_{n}\|^{2}+\|y_{n}\|^{2})-(\|AP_{0}\|^{2}-\lambda^{2})\|y_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|AP_{0}\|^{2}-(\|AP_{0}\|^{2}-\lambda^{2})\|y_{n}\|^{2}.

Since
\|Az_{n}\|\to\|A\|=\|AP_{0}\|
and
\|AP_{0}\|^{2}-\lambda^{2}>0
,
it follows that
\|y_{n}\|\to 0
, and consequently
\|x_{n}\|\to 1
.
Therefore,

\lim_{n}\|AP_{0}z_{n}\|^{2}=\lim_{n}\|AP_{0}x_{n}\|^{2}=\lim_{n}\|Az_{n}\|^{2}=\|A\|^{2}=\|AP_{0}\|^{2},

which shows
(z_{n})
is a norming sequence for
AP_{0}
. Conversely, if

(z_{n})
is a norming sequence for
AP_{0}
, then

\|A\|^{2}\geq\|Az_{n}\|^{2}\geq\|AP_{0}z_{n}\|^{2}\to\|AP_{0}\|^{2}=\|A\|^{2},

and hence
(z_{n})
is a norming sequence for
A
. This completes the
proof.
∎

We also make note of the fact that any member
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
of
M(\mathcal{H})
satisfies the
J
-contraction identity

M_{\phi_{a,U}}^{\ast}JM_{\phi_{a,U}}=\alpha(a)^{2}J,

where
J=\operatorname{diag}(I_{\mathcal{H}},-1)
is the signature operator on

\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}
. The normalized matrix

\widetilde{M}_{a,U}=\frac{1}{\alpha(a)}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
therefore satisfies

\widetilde{M}_{a,U}^{\ast}J\widetilde{M}_{a,U}=J
, making it a

J
-unitary operator. Since
\alpha(a)
is a positive scalar, this normalization
leaves the underlying Möbius map
\phi_{a,U}
unchanged.

3.
Smoothness and orthogonality in
M(\mathcal{H})

In this section we prove that the the elements of
M(\mathcal{H})
that are not pure rotations are smooth points in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
, and translates Birkhoff–James orthogonality into antipodality on the unit sphere
\partial B
.

3.1.
Smoothness

Pure rotations are never smooth points in

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
. Let

M_{0,U}=\mathrm{diag}(U,1)
be a pure rotation. Since

M_{0,U}
is unitary,
\|M_{0,U}\|=1
and every unit vector
is a norming vector for
M_{0,U}
. Let
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}
be any two orthonormal vectors,
set
f_{i}=M_{0,U}e_{i}
for
i=1,2
, and consider the
rank-one operator

T=f_{1}\otimes e_{1},

that is,
T(x)=\langle x,e_{1}\rangle f_{1}
for
x\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}
. The constant pairs

(z_{n},y_{n})\equiv(e_{i},f_{i})
satisfy

\langle M_{0,U}z_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\langle f_{i},f_{i}\rangle=1=\|M_{0,U}\|
for
i=1,2
, so both are norming pairs for

M_{0,U}
. A direct computation gives

\langle Te_{1},f_{1}\rangle=\langle f_{1},f_{1}\rangle=1,\qquad\langle Te_{2},f_{2}\rangle=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle=\langle M_{0,U}e_{1},M_{0,U}e_{2}\rangle=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=0.

Thus
\Lambda(M_{0,U},T)
contains both
0
and
1
, and

M_{0,U}
is not a smooth point in

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
by
Corollary
2.3
. Therefore, we have proved the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Pure rotations in
M\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
are not smooth points in
B\left(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\right)
.

We now prove Theorem
1.1
, which shows non-rotations are smooth points in
B\left(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Consider
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\in M(\mathcal{H})
for some nonzero

a\in B
and unitary
U
on
\mathcal{H}
. We write
M_{\phi_{a,U}}=\widetilde{U}M_{a,I},
, where
\widetilde{U}=\mathrm{diag}(U,1)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
is unitary. Since
\widetilde{U}
is unitary,
\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|=\|M_{a,I}\|
, and

(z_{n})
is a norming sequence for
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
if and only if it is
a norming sequence for
M_{a,I}
.

It is not difficult to see that
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\left\{(z,0)\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}:z\perp a\right\}
is a subspace of co-dimension
2
in
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, and is an eigenspace of
M_{a,I}
corresponding the eigenvalue
\alpha(a)
. Since
M_{a,I}

is self-adjoint, and
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is an invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is in fact
a reducing subspace of
M_{a,I}
, giving the block decomposition
M_{a,I}=T\oplus S
with respect to the orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}=\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
, where
T=\alpha(a)I

on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
S=M_{a,I}|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}}
.

The complementary subspace
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
is
a two-dimensional subspace, and spanned by the orthonormal basis

\left\{\frac{1}{\|a\|}(a,0),\ (0,1)\right\}
. A direct computation shows that the matrix of
S
with respect to this basis is given by

[S]=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\|a\|\\
-\|a\|&1\end{bmatrix}.

Since
[S]
is self-adjoint,
\|S\|
equals to the absolute value of its its largest eigenvalue, which is
1+\|a\|
. The corresponding unit
eigenvector is

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\|a\|}(a,0)-(0,1)\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right).

By Lemma
2.4
, since

\|S\|=1+\|a\|>\alpha(a)=\|T\|
, we conclude

\|M_{a,I}\|=\|S\|=1+\|a\|,

and the norm is attained precisely on the unit sphere of the one-dimensional eigenspace:

\left\{\mu\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right):|\mu|=1\right\}.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.4

(z_{n})
is a
norming sequence for
M_{a,I}
if and only if it is a norming
sequence for
M_{a,I}P
, where
P
denotes the orthogonal projection
onto
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
.

Next, for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
, consider the
collection

\Lambda(M_{\phi_{a,U}},A)=\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{C}:\lim_{n}\langle Az_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\mu,\ (z_{n}),(y_{n})\subset S_{\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}},\right.\\
\left.\hskip 113.81102pt\lim_{n}\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|\right\}.

We show this collection is a singleton set. Let
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda(M_{\phi_{a,U}},A)
, realized by the sequences
(z_{n})
and
(y_{n})
.
Since
|\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n},y_{n}\rangle|\leq\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n}\|
, the
condition
\lim_{n}\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|

forces
(z_{n})
to be a norming sequence for
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
, hence for

M_{a,I}
, and therefore, for
M_{a,I}P
.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
is
two-dimensional, its closed unit ball is a compact subset of
B_{\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}}
. We observe that

\lim_{n}\|(I-P)z_{n}\|=0
(by the same argument as in
Lemma
2.4
), and the sequence
(Pz_{n})
lies in the closed unit ball of
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
. We may therefore pass to a
subsequence and assume
Pz_{n_{k}}\to z_{0}
for some unit vector
z_{0}
in
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
. Since
(I-P)z_{n_{k}}\to 0
, we get

z_{n_{k}}\to z_{0}
, and we know from the norm attainment set of
M_{a,I}
that the unit vector
z_{0}
is uniquely determined by:

z_{0}=\mu_{0}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right)\quad\text{for some unimodular scalar }\mu_{0}.

A parallel argument applies to
(y_{n})
. Since
M_{a,I}

is self-adjoint and
\lim_{n}\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\lim_{n}\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\lim_{n}\langle M_{a,I}z_{n},\widetilde{U}^{*}y_{n}\rangle=\lim_{n}\langle M_{a,I}\widetilde{U}^{*}y_{n},z_{n}\rangle=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|,

which shows
(\widetilde{U}^{*}y_{n})
is also a norming sequence for

M_{a,I}
. By the same compactness argument, passing to a further
subsequence gives
\widetilde{U}^{*}y_{n_{k}}\to y_{0}
where

y_{0}=\sigma_{0}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right)\quad\text{for some unimodular scalar }\sigma_{0}.

By a diagonal argument we extract a common subsequence

(n_{p})
along which both
z_{n_{p}}\to\mu_{0}\tilde{z}_{0}
and

\widetilde{U}^{*}y_{n_{p}}\to\sigma_{0}\tilde{z}_{0}
, where
\mu_{0}
and
\sigma_{0}
are unimodular scalars and
\tilde{z}_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(a/\|a\|,-1\right)
.
The condition
\lim_{p}\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n_{p}},y_{n_{p}}\rangle=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|
then gives

\mu_{0}\overline{\sigma_{0}}(1+\|a\|)=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|,

which forces
\mu_{0}\overline{\sigma_{0}}=1
, hence
\mu_{0}=\sigma_{0}
.

Therefore
\lambda_{0}
is completely determined:

\lambda_{0}=\lim_{p}\langle Az_{n_{p}},y_{n_{p}}\rangle=\left\langle A\tilde{z}_{0},\ \widetilde{U}\tilde{z}_{0}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle A\!\left(\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right),\ \widetilde{U}\!\left(\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right)\right\rangle.

Since this value depends only on
A
and not on the choice of
sequences,
\Lambda(M_{\phi_{a,U}},A)
is a singleton set for every

A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
. By
Corollary
2.3
,
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
is a smooth point
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
, completing the
proof.
∎

3.2.
Orthogonality

The same block matrix structure that identifies smooth
points also translates orthogonality into a
purely geometric condition on
\partial B
, which is Theorem
1.2

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We first prove the necessity. Suppose
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}
.
By Lemma
2.1
, there exists a norming sequence
(z_{n})

for
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
such that

\lim_{n}\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n},M_{\phi_{b,V}}z_{n}\rangle=0.

By the same compactness argument of Theorem
1.1
, we pass
to a subsequence
(z_{n_{k}})
of
(z_{n})
and obtain

z_{n_{k}}\to z_{0}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right)\quad\text{for some unimodular scalar }\mu_{0}.

The next step is to express
M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{0}
and
M_{\phi_{b,V}}z_{0}

in terms of the Möbius maps
\phi_{a,U}
and
\phi_{b,V}

evaluated at
-a/\|a\|
. A direct computation using the
block matrix structure gives

\displaystyle M_{\phi_{a,U}}\left(\frac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right)

\displaystyle=-(1+\|a\|)\left(\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),1\right),

\displaystyle M_{\phi_{b,V}}\left(\frac{a}{\|a\|},-1\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(1+\left\langle\frac{a}{\|a\|},b\right\rangle\right)\left(\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),1\right).

Note that
1+\|a\|>0
and

1+\langle a/\|a\|,b\rangle\neq 0
since

|\langle a/\|a\|,b\rangle|\leq\|b\|<1
.

By continuity of the inner product, passing to the limit
along the subsequence gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{k}\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{n_{k}},M_{\phi_{b,V}}z_{n_{k}}\rangle=\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}z_{0},M_{\phi_{b,V}}z_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(1+\|a\|)\left(1+\left\langle\frac{a}{\|a\|},b\right\rangle\right)\left[\left\langle\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right\rangle+1\right].

Cancelling both the factors, we conclude

\left\langle\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),\,\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right\rangle=-1.

Since both images lie on
\partial B
, the equality case of the
Cauchy–Schwarz inequality now forces

\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)=-\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),

that is,
\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
and

\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
are antipodal points
on
\partial B
.

We now prove the sufficiency. Suppose
\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)

and
\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
are antipodal on

\partial B
, so that

\left\langle\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),\,\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right\rangle+1=0.

Let
w=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(a/\|a\|,-1\right)
, which
is the unit vector at which
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
attains its norm. For any
scalar
\lambda
, the block matrix computations above give

\displaystyle\|(M_{\phi_{a,U}}+\lambda M_{\phi_{b,V}})w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}w\|^{2}+2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\langle M_{\phi_{b,V}}w,M_{\phi_{a,U}}w\rangle+|\lambda|^{2}\|M_{\phi_{b,V}}w\|^{2}.

The cross term vanishes by the antipodality condition:

\displaystyle\langle M_{\phi_{a,U}}w,M_{\phi_{b,V}}w\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\|a\|)\left(1+\left\langle a/\|a\|,b\right\rangle\right)}{2}\left[\left\langle\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right\rangle+1\right]

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore,

\|(M_{\phi_{a,U}}+\lambda M_{\phi_{b,V}})w\|^{2}=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}w\|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2}\|M_{\phi_{b,V}}w\|^{2}\geq\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}w\|^{2}=\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|^{2},

which shows
\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}+\lambda M_{\phi_{b,V}}\|\geq\|M_{\phi_{a,U}}\|
for
all scalars
\lambda
. By definition,
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}

in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
, completing the proof.
∎

The antipodality condition admits an explicit
formulation directly in terms of the parameters of the
automorphisms.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}
in
M\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
if and only if

U\left(-\frac{a}{\|a\|}\right)=-V\phi_{b,I}\left(\left(-\frac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right).

Proof.

We first observe that
-a/\|a\|
is a fixed point of

\phi_{a,I}
, since

\phi_{a,I}\left(-\frac{a}{\|a\|}\right)=\frac{-\frac{a}{\|a\|}-a}{1+\|a\|}=-\frac{a}{\|a\|},

and that
\phi_{b,V}=V\phi_{b,I}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}

\displaystyle\iff\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)=-\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)

\displaystyle\iff-U\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}=-V\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)

\displaystyle\iff U\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)=-V\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),

and the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For any unitary operator
U
on
\mathcal{H}
and any

a\in B\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{a,-U}
in

M(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

Since
-a/\|a\|
is a fixed point of
\phi_{a,I}
, we have

\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)=-U\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}=-\phi_{a,-U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),

so the two images are antipodal on
\partial B
. The result follows
from Theorem
1.2
.
∎

4.
Symmetry and preservation of orthogonality in
M(\mathcal{H})

We now turn to the symmetry properties of
Birkhoff–James orthogonality in
M(\mathcal{H})
. The key tool in both proofs is the following lemma, which produces an explicit unitary operator that maps one boundary point to another boundary point through Householder reflector.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
y,w\in\partial B
. Then there exists a unitary operator

V
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
Vw=y
.

Proof.

The proof is trivial in dimension
1
, since
z\mapsto y\overline{w}z
is the required unitary. If
dim~H\geq 2
, we consider the Householder reflector: if
y=w
, take
V=I
. Otherwise, set

\mu_{0}=\mathrm{sgn}\langle w,y\rangle
and

z_{0}=\frac{w-\mu_{0}y}{\|w-\mu_{0}y\|}
, and define

H=I-2(z_{0}\otimes z_{0}).

Since
\langle w,\mu_{0}y\rangle=\overline{\mathrm{sgn}\langle w,y\rangle}\langle w,y\rangle
is a positive real
number, we have
\|w-\mu_{0}y\|^{2}=2(1-\langle w,\mu_{0}y\rangle)
, and a direct computation gives

H(w)=w-2\frac{1-\langle w,\mu_{0}y\rangle}{2(1-\langle w,\mu_{0}y\rangle)}(w-\mu_{0}y)=\mu_{0}y.

The operator
H
is self-adjoint and unitary by definition,
and therefore
V=\overline{\mu_{0}}H
satisfies

Vw=\overline{\mu_{0}}\mu_{0}y=y
.
∎

We next prove Theorem
1.3
which presents a characterization left-symmetric and right-symmetric points of
M(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We divide the proof into two parts.

Part-I: No left-symmetric points

Case I: Non-rotations are not left-symmetric.

Let
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\in M(\mathcal{H})

for some nonzero
a\in B
. We construct

M_{\phi_{b,V}}\in M(\mathcal{H})
such that
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}

but
M_{\phi_{b,V}}\not\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
, showing
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
is not
left-symmetric.

We choose
b\in B
with the following specification:

Im~(\langle a,b\rangle^{2})\neq 0,\quad\|a\|\neq\|b\|.

Existence of such an element
b
is always ensured in any complex Hilbert space. By Corollary
3.2
,
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}

if and only if
V\!\left(-\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)\right)=U\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
.

Next, we specify
V
. Setting

y=U\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
and

w=-\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
, both of which
lie on
\partial B
, Lemma
4.1
produces a
unitary
V
satisfying
Vw=y
, so
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}

by construction.

It remains to show
M_{\phi_{b,V}}\not\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
. Suppose for contradiction that
M_{\phi_{b,V}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
. By Corollary
3.2
,
M_{\phi_{b,V}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
would
require
V\!\left(-b/\|b\|\right)=-U\phi_{a,I}\!\left(-b/\|b\|\right)
.
Since
V
and
U
are unitary operators, using

V\!\left(-\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\frac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right)=U\!\left(-\frac{a}{\|a\|}\right),\quad V\!\left(-\frac{b}{\|b\|}\right)=-U\phi_{a,I}\!\left(-\frac{b}{\|b\|}\right),

we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),\,\tfrac{b}{\|b\|}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle V\!\left(-\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right),\,V\!\left(-\tfrac{b}{\|b\|}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle U(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}),\,-U\phi_{a,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{b}{\|b\|}\right)\right\rangle.

\displaystyle=\left\langle\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},\,\phi_{a,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{b}{\|b\|}\right)\right\rangle.

Using

P_{b}(a/\|a\|)=\frac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\|a\|\|b\|^{2}}b

and
Q_{b}(a/\|a\|)\perp b
, the left-hand side gives

\left\langle\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),\,\tfrac{b}{\|b\|}\right\rangle=\frac{-\dfrac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\|a\|\|b\|}-\|b\|}{1+\dfrac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\|a\|}}.

with the role of
a
and
b
interchanged, the right-hand side becomes

\left\langle\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},\,\phi_{a,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{b}{\|b\|}\right)\right\rangle=\frac{-\dfrac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\|a\|\|b\|}-\|a\|}{1+\dfrac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\|b\|}}.

Equating the two sides, we get

-\frac{\langle a,b\rangle^{2}}{\|a\|\|b\|^{2}}-\|b\|=-\frac{\langle a,b\rangle^{2}}{\|a\|^{2}\|b\|}-\|a\|,

which rearranges to

\frac{\langle a,b\rangle^{2}\bigl(\|b\|-\|a\|\bigr)}{\|a\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}}=\|b\|-\|a\|.

Since
\|a\|\neq\|b\|
, we may divide both sides by

\|b\|-\|a\|
to obtain

\langle a,b\rangle^{2}=\|a\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}
, contradicting the choice of
b
with

\mathrm{Im}(\langle a,b\rangle)^{2}\neq 0
. Therefore
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
is not left-symmetric.

Case II: Pure rotations are not left-symmetric.

If
dim~\mathcal{H}=1
, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}
, consider a pure rotation
M_{0,e^{i\theta}}
and let

b\in B\cap\mathbb{R}
with
b\neq 0
. Set

e^{i\phi}=-e^{i\theta}\cdot\frac{1+ib}{1-ib}
, and
note that
e^{i\phi}\neq-e^{i\theta}
since

b\neq 0
. Taking the unit vector

z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,i)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\left\langle M_{0,e^{i\theta}}^{*}M_{b,e^{i\phi}}z,\,z\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\!\left[e^{i(\phi-\theta)}(1-ib)+(1+ib)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\!\left[-\frac{1+ib}{1-ib}(1-ib)+(1+ib)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\!\left[-(1+ib)+(1+ib)\right]

\displaystyle=0.

Since
M_{0,e^{i\theta}}
is unitary,
z
is a norming
vector for
M_{0,e^{i\theta}}
. Thus,
M_{0,e^{i\theta}}\perp_{B}M_{b,e^{i\phi}}
by
Lemma
2.1
. By Theorem
1.2
,

M_{b,e^{i\phi}}\perp_{B}M_{0,e^{i\theta}}
requires
antipodality of
\phi_{b,e^{i\phi}}(-b/|b|)=-e^{i\phi}b/|b|
and

\phi_{0,e^{i\theta}}(-b/|b|)=-e^{i\theta}b/|b|
,
which gives
e^{i\phi}=-e^{i\theta}
. However, this fails, since

e^{i\phi}=-e^{i\theta}\cdot\frac{1+ib}{1-ib}\neq-e^{i\theta}
for
b\neq 0
.
Therefore,
M_{0,e^{i\theta}}
is not left-symmetric.

If
dim~\mathcal{H}\geq 2
, consider a pure rotation
M_{0,U}
, and let
a\in B
be any nonzero element. Set

u=U\!\left(a/\|a\|\right)
and choose
w\in\partial B
with
w\perp u
. By Lemma
4.1
,
there exists a unitary
V
with
V\!\left(a/\|a\|\right)=w
. Since
M_{0,U}
is unitary, every unit vector is a norming
vector, and since

\left\langle M_{0,U}\!\left(\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},0\right),M_{a,V}\!\left(\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},0\right)\right\rangle=\left\langle u,w\right\rangle=0,

we conclude
M_{0,U}\perp_{B}M_{a,V}
. However, by
Corollary
3.2
,

-U\!\left(\phi_{0,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right)=U\!\left(\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)=u\neq-w=V\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),

since
\langle u,w\rangle=0
.
so
M_{a,V}\not\perp_{B}M_{0,U}
. Therefore pure rotations
are not left-symmetric points.

Part 2: Right-symmetric points are
precisely pure rotations.

Case I: Pure rotations are right-symmetric.

Let
M_{0,U}
be a pure rotation and suppose

M_{a,V}\perp_{B}M_{0,U}
for some

M_{a,V}\in M(\mathcal{H})
. If
a=0
, both

M_{0,V}
and
M_{0,U}
are unitary and the argument
is symmetric by Lemma
2.1
. If
a\neq 0
, By
Theorem
1.2
,
\phi_{a,V}\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
and
U\!\left(-a/\|a\|\right)
are antipodal,
so

\left\langle M_{a,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},1\right),M_{0,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},1\right)\right\rangle=0,

so the constant norming sequence

x_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\!\left(-a/\|a\|,1\right)

satisfies

\langle M_{0,U}x_{n},M_{a,V}x_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle M_{a,V}(-a/\|a\|,1),M_{0,U}(-a/\|a\|,1)\rangle=0
.
Since
M_{0,U}
is unitary, it attains its norm at every unit
vector, and by Lemma
2.1

immediately gives
M_{0,U}\perp_{B}M_{a,V}
. This shows every
pure rotation is right-symmetric.

Case II: Non-rotations are not right-symmetric.

If
dim~\mathcal{H}=1
, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}
, consider
M_{a,e^{i\theta}}\in M(\mathbb{C})
with

a\neq 0
. Choose
b\in B
with
b\neq 0
,

|b|\neq|a|
, and
b/|b|\neq\pm\,a/|a|
.
Since
\phi_{a,e^{i\theta}}(-b/|b|)
is unimodular
and
-b/|b|
is a fixed point of
\phi_{b,I}
,
define
e^{i\phi}
so that

e^{i\phi}\frac{b}{|b|}=e^{i\theta}\phi_{a,I}\!\left(-\frac{b}{|b|}\right).

which satisfies the antipodality condition, and By Theorem
1.2
, we have

M_{b,e^{i\phi}}\perp_{B}M_{a,e^{i\theta}}
.

We claim
M_{a,e^{i\theta}}\not\perp_{B}M_{b,e^{i\phi}}
. Suppose for contradiction that

M_{a,e^{i\theta}}\perp_{B}M_{b,e^{i\phi}}
. By
Theorem
1.2
, together with the fixed
point identity

\phi_{a,e^{i\theta}}(-a/|a|)=-e^{i\theta}a/|a|
,
this gives:

e^{i\theta}\frac{a}{|a|}=e^{i\phi}\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\frac{a}{|a|}\right).

Multiplying
\mathrm{(I)}
and
\mathrm{(II)}
and
cancelling
e^{i(\theta+\phi)}\neq 0
, we get

\frac{a}{|a|}\cdot\frac{b}{|b|}=\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\frac{a}{|a|}\right)\cdot\phi_{a,I}\!\left(-\frac{b}{|b|}\right).

Using the direct computations:

\phi_{b,I}\!\left(-\frac{a}{|a|}\right)=\frac{-\frac{b}{|b|}\!\left(\frac{a}{|a|}+|b|\frac{b}{|b|}\right)}{\frac{b}{|b|}+|b|\frac{a}{|a|}},\quad\phi_{a,I}\!\left(-\frac{b}{|b|}\right)=\frac{-\frac{a}{|a|}\!\left(\frac{b}{|b|}+|a|\frac{a}{|a|}\right)}{\frac{a}{|a|}+|a|\frac{b}{|b|}},

equation

\frac{\!\left(\frac{a}{|a|}+|b|\frac{b}{|b|}\right)\!\left(\frac{b}{|b|}+|a|\frac{a}{|a|}\right)}{\!\left(\frac{b}{|b|}+|b|\frac{a}{|a|}\right)\!\left(\frac{a}{|a|}+|a|\frac{b}{|b|}\right)}=1.

Expanding both sides and cancelling the common terms

and

:

(|a|-|b|)\!\left[\left(\frac{a}{|a|}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{b}{|b|}\right)^{2}\right]=0,

which contradicts
|a|\neq|b|
and

a/|a|\neq\pm\,b/|b|
. Therefore

M_{a,e^{i\theta}}\not\perp_{B}M_{b,e^{i\phi}}
,
and
M_{a,e^{i\theta}}
is not right-symmetric.

If
dim~\mathcal{H}\geq 2
, consider
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\in M(\mathcal{H})
with
a\neq 0
.
Set
u=U\!\left(a/\|a\|\right)
and choose a unit
vector

. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists
a unitary
V
with
V\!\left(a/\|a\|\right)=w
. Since

\left\langle M_{0,V}\!\left(\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},0\right),M_{\phi_{a,U}}\!\left(\tfrac{a}{\|a\|},0\right)\right\rangle=\left\langle(w,0),\,(u,-\|a\|)\right\rangle=\langle w,u\rangle=0,

and
M_{0,V}
is unitary, we get
M_{0,V}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{a,U}}
.
However, by Corollary
3.2
,

U\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)=-u\neq w=-V\!\left(\phi_{0,I}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right)\right),

since

. Thus,
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\not\perp_{B}M_{0,V}
, showing
M_{\phi_{a,U}}
is not

This completes the proof.
∎

As an immediate corollary, we get the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

M(\mathcal{H})
has no symmetric points.

As noted in the introduction, orthogonality relation in
M(\mathcal{H})
naturally induces a orthogonality relation
\perp_{Aut}
. We now characterize the inner automorphisms that preserves
\perp_{Aut}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Phi:Aut(B)\to Aut(B)
be a bijective map that preserves
\perp_{Aut}
in both directions. Then
\Phi
preserves pure rotations.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{0,U}
be a pure rotation for some unitary operator
U
on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\Phi(\phi_{0,U})=\phi_{b,V}
for some
b\in B
and unitary operator
V
on
\mathcal{H}
. We observe that for any
\phi_{c,W}\in Aut(B)
,

\phi_{c,W}\perp_{Aut}\Phi(\phi_{0,U})\iff\Phi(\phi_{0,U})\perp_{Aut}\phi_{c,W}

since
M_{\phi_{0,U}}
is a right-symmetric point in
M(\mathcal{H})
. Consequently,
\phi_{b,V}
is a right-symmetric point in
M(\mathcal{H})
, and by Theorem
1.3
we have
b=0
. Therefore,
\Phi
preserves pure rotation.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

An inner automorphism of
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
has the form
\Phi_{\psi}:\phi\mapsto\psi\circ\phi\circ\psi^{-1}
for some
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
.

We first prove the necessity. Suppose
\Phi_{\psi}
preserves
\perp_{\operatorname{Aut}}
in both directions. Then by the preceding proposition,
\psi
preserves pure rotations, meaning
\psi\circ\phi_{0,U}\circ\psi^{-1}
is a pure rotation for every unitary operator
U
on
\mathcal{H}
. We show
\psi
must be a pure rotation. Write
\psi=\phi_{c,W}
for some
c\in B
and unitary
W
. Since
\phi_{c,W}^{-1}=\phi_{-c,I}\circ W^{-1}
, we compute:

(\phi_{c,W}\circ\phi_{0,U}\circ\phi_{c,W}^{-1})(0)=\phi_{c,W}(\phi_{0,U}(c))=\phi_{c,W}(Uc).

For this to be a pure rotation, its value at
0
must be
0
. Since
\phi_{c,W}(z)=0
if and only
z=c
, the condition
\phi_{c,W}(Uc)=0
requires
Uc=c
for every unitary
U
. But
Uc=c
for all unitaries
U
forces
c=0
. Consequently,
\psi=\phi_{0,W}
is a pure rotation.

We now prove the sufficiency. Suppose
\psi=\phi_{0,W}
is a pure rotation. For any
\phi_{a,U}\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
, conjugation by
W
gives:

\phi_{0,W}\circ\phi_{a,U}\circ\phi_{0,W}^{-1}=\phi_{Wa,\,WUW^{-1}}.

By Theorem
1.2
,
M_{\phi_{a,U}}\perp_{B}M_{\phi_{b,V}}
if and only if
\phi_{a,U}(-a/\|a\|)
and
\phi_{b,V}(-a/\|a\|)
are antipodal. Therefore, to show
\Phi_{\psi}
preserves
\perp_{Aut}
, we need to prove that

\phi_{Wa,WUW^{-1}}(-Wa/\|a\|)=-\phi_{Wb,WVW^{-1}}(-Wa/\|a\|).

Using the identity

\phi_{Wc,WRW^{-1}}(Wz)=W\phi_{c,R}(z),\quad z\in B,

for any
c\in B
, and unitary operator
R
on
\mathcal{H}
, and
z\in B
, we get

\phi_{Wa,WUW^{-1}}\!\left(-\tfrac{Wa}{\|a\|}\right)=W\phi_{a,U}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right),\quad\phi_{Wb,WVW^{-1}}\!\left(-\tfrac{Wa}{\|a\|}\right)=W\phi_{b,V}\!\left(-\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}\right).

Since
W
is unitary, it preserves antipodality. Since the antipodality condition is equivalent in both directions, similar argument shows
\Phi_{\psi}^{-1}
preserves
\perp_{Aut}
. Thus,
\Phi_{\psi}
preserves
\perp_{Aut}
in both directions, and the proof is now complete.
∎

Theorem
1.4
remains open in context of general bijections that preserves
\perp_{Aut}
in both directions.

Remark 4.4
.

The symmetry of Birkhoff–James orthogonality has been extensively
studied in the linear setting
[
11
,
14
,
15
]
. In
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, it is known that the unit ball of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
has no left-symmetric points, while the only right-symmetric points are isometries and co-isometries
[
16
,
10
]
. The set
M(\mathcal{H})
,
by contrast, is a highly non-linear subset of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C})
. Nevertheless, the symmetry pattern that emerges
is almost parallel:
M(\mathcal{H})
admits no left-symmetric
points, and its right-symmetric points are precisely the pure rotations,
which are the unitary elements of
M(\mathcal{H})
. This suggests a certain rigidity in the orthogonality structure that persists beyond the linear framework, and it would be interesting to investigate whether similar phenomena appear in other non-linear
subsets of operator algebras arising naturally from complex function
theory.

5.
Hyperbolic metric induced by operator norm

We now show that the normalized
J
-unitary block matrices
recovers the hyperbolic geometry of the unit ball. We first record the norm formula, from the proof of Theorem
1.1
,

(1)

\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\|=\frac{1+\|a\|}{\alpha(a)}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\|a\|}{1-\|a\|}}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\|\phi_{a,U}(0)\|}{1-\|\phi_{a,U}(0)\|}}

We remark a Schwarz-Pick type observation for normalized block matrices associated to the automorphism group.

Remark 5.1
.

Let
f\colon B\to B
be a holomorphic self-map with
f(0)=0
. By the
Schwarz–Pick lemma for the Hilbert ball
[
6
]
,
\|f(a)\|\leq\|a\|
for all
a\in B
. Since
t\mapsto\sqrt{(1+t)/(1-t)}
is
strictly increasing on
[0,1)
, this gives

\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{f(a),I}}\|=\sqrt{\frac{1+\|f(a)\|}{1-\|f(a)\|}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1+\|a\|}{1-\|a\|}}=\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,I}}\|,

so the operator norm of
\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,I}}
decreases under
holomorphic self-maps of
B
fixing the origin. Moreover, if equality

\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{f(a_{0}),I}}\|=\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a_{0},I}}\|

holds for some nonzero
a_{0}\in B
, then
\|f(a_{0})\|=\|a_{0}\|
, and
Theorem III.2.3 of
[
5
]
gives
\|f(\zeta a_{0})\|=|\zeta|\|a_{0}\|

for all
|\zeta|<1/\|a_{0}\|
, hence

\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{f(\zeta a_{0}),I}}\|=\sqrt{\frac{1+|\zeta|\|a_{0}\|}{1-|\zeta|\|a_{0}\|}}=\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{\zeta a_{0},I}}\|\quad\text{for all }|\zeta|<\frac{1}{\|a_{0}\|}.

The following algebraic identity, derived from the
J
-unitary structure, is the key ingredient.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
a,b\in B
,

(2)

1-\|\phi_{a,I}(b)\|^{2}=\frac{(1-\|a\|^{2})(1-\|b\|^{2})}{|1-\langle b,a\rangle|^{2}}.

Proof.

Apply the
J
-unitary identity
\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}^{*}J\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}=J
to the vector
(b,1)\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}
:

\left\langle J\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\begin{pmatrix}b\\
1\end{pmatrix},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\begin{pmatrix}b\\
1\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle=\left\langle J\begin{pmatrix}b\\
1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}b\\
1\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle=\|b\|^{2}-1.

On the other hand,

\left\langle J\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\begin{pmatrix}b\\
1\end{pmatrix},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\begin{pmatrix}b\\
1\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle=\frac{|1-\langle b,a\rangle|^{2}}{\alpha(a)^{2}}\left\langle J\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{a,U}(b)\\
1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{a,U}(b)\\
1\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

Equating and rearranging we get
5.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Part (a): Submultiplicativity.

Recall that

\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\odot\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}}=\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}}.

Therefore, by (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\odot\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}}\|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1+\|\phi_{a,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}(0)\|}{1-\|\phi_{a,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}(0)\|}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1+\|U\phi_{a,I}(-Ub)\|}{1-\|U\phi_{a,I}(-Ub)\|}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1+\|\phi_{a,I}(-Ub)\|}{1-\|\phi_{a,I}(-Ub)\|}}

By Cauchy–Schwarz,
|\langle b,a\rangle|\leq\|a\|\|b\|
, so
|1-\langle-Ub,a\rangle|=|1+\langle Ub,a\rangle|\leq 1+\|a\|\|b\|
. From Lemma
5.2
applied to
-Ub
in place of
b
:

1-\|\phi_{a,I}(-Ub)\|^{2}\geq\frac{(1-\|a\|^{2})(1-\|b\|^{2})}{(1+\|a\|\|b\|)^{2}}.

Since
(1+\|a\|\|b\|)^{2}-(1-\|a\|^{2})(1-\|b\|^{2})=(\|a\|+\|b\|)^{2}
, we get:

\|\phi_{a,I}(-Ub)\|\leq\frac{\|a\|+\|b\|}{1+\|a\|\|b\|}.

Since
x\mapsto\sqrt{(1+x)/(1-x)}
is increasing:

\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\odot\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}}\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{(1+\|a\|)(1+\|b\|)}{(1-\|a\|)(1-\|b\|)}}=\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}}\|\,\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}}\|.

Part (b):
(F_{U},d_{U})
is a metric space isometric to
(B,d_{\mathrm{hyp}})
.

This follows directly, from the following observation. Since
\phi_{b,U}^{-1}=\phi_{-b,I}\circ U^{-1}
, we compute
(\phi_{a,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}^{-1})(0)=\phi_{a,U}(b)=U\phi_{a,I}(b)
. Consequently,

d_{U}(\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}})=2\ln\|\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}^{-1}}\|=\ln\frac{1+\|\phi_{a,I}(b)\|}{1-\|\phi_{a,I}(b)\|}=d_{\mathrm{hyp}}(a,b),

by
[
6
,
5
]
, which completes the proof.
∎

However, an independent proof can be obtained by using the submultiplicativity of norm proved in Part (a).

Remark 5.3
.

The positivity and symmetry can be verified directly. Triangle inequality follows from the submultiplicativity of norm. Indeed, for any
\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{c,U}}\in F_{U}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{U}(\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}})

\displaystyle=2~ln~\left\|\phi_{a,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=2~ln~\left\|\phi_{a,U}\circ{\phi_{c,U}^{-1}}\circ\phi_{c,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2~ln~\left\|\phi_{a,U}\circ{\phi_{c,U}^{-1}}\|\|\phi_{c,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=2~ln~\left\|\phi_{a,U}\circ{\phi_{c,U}^{-1}}\right\|+2~ln~\left\|\phi_{c,U}\circ\phi_{b,U}^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=d_{U}(\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{c,U}})+d_{U}(\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{c,U}},\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{b,U}}).

Now, the map
\Gamma:(F_{U},d_{U})\to(B,d_{\mathrm{hyp}})
defined by
\Gamma(\widetilde{M}_{\phi_{a,U}})=a
gives the required isometry.

Remark 5.4
.

The submultiplicativity in Part (a) differs from the usual submultiplicativity of the operator norm
\|AB\|\leq\|A\|\|B\|
; here the product
\odot
is defined via composition of automorphisms which differs from usual operator multiplication.
