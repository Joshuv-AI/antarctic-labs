Biquantization of the necklace Lie bialgebra 0 0 footnotetext: Email: xjchen@scu.edu.cn, m.huang@newuu.uz, liumeiliang@stu.scu.edu.cn, zhangjun24@stu.scu.edu.cn
Xiaojun Chen, Maozhou Huang, Meiliang Liu, Jun Zhang

Abstract
For the double of a quiver, the works of Ginzburg, Bocklandt-Le Bruyn and Schedler show that its closed paths, called the necklaces, have a natural Lie bialgebra structure. Schedler also constructed, in [Int. Math. Res. Notices, 2005 (12), 725-760], a Hopf algebra that quantizes this Lie bialgebra. In this paper, we pursue one more step in this direction by constructing its biquantization, in the sense of Turaev [Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. (4) 24 (1991), no. 6, 635-704]. Keywords: Necklace Lie bialgebra, Quantization, Hopf algebra MSC2020: 16S38, 16T05



1
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is
to construct a
biquantization

of the necklace Lie bialgebra
of quivers. Let

Q
be a quiver and
\overline{Q}

be its double, which means
doubling the edges of
Q

but with an opposite orientation.
It was proved by Ginzburg

[
5
]

and independently by
Bocklandt and LeBruyn
[
2
]

that the cyclic paths of
\overline{Q}
,
called the necklaces,
have a Lie algebra structure.
Later Schedler showed in

[
8
]
that
the necklaces
also have a Lie cobracket, which is
a Lie cocycle, and
thus form a
Lie bialgebra. Moreover,
Schedler constructed
a Hopf algebra which
quantizes such a Lie bialgebra,
which is inspired
by the quantization of
the Lie bialgebra of loops (i.e.,
the homotopy classes of closed paths)
on Riemann surfaces
(see Turaev
[
9
]
with
some preceding work due to Goldman

[
6
]
).

Recall that, given a Lie bialgebra

\mathfrak{g}
, its symmetric product

S(\mathfrak{g})

has an induced Lie bracket and cobracket,
which is in fact a bi-Poisson bialgebra.
We thus may first deform the algebra
structure of
S(\mathfrak{g})

in the direction of the Lie bracket, and then deform
the coalgebra structure on the resulted
algebra in the direction of the Lie
cobracket, or vice versa.
A priori, these two algebras
may not be the same, and if they are,
we say such an algebra is a biquantization
of
L
.

In this sense,
Schedler’s Hopf algebra
is only a (co)quantization of the necklaces
in the direction of the Lie
cobracket, and
is different from the common
definition of quantization
of Lie bialgebras in the literature
(see, for example,
[
4
]

and §
2
below).
It is natural to expect
that the quantizations
of necklaces in both directions
exist; moreover,
they are compatible in some sense.

In a series of papers

[
4
]
,
Etingof and Kazhdan constructed
a quantization of (both finite and infinite
dimensional) Lie bialgebras, by applying
the Drinfeld associator on
the corresponding vector spaces.
Later in
[
7
]
,
Kassel and Turaev continued to construct
a biquantization of finite dimensional
Lie bialgebras.
Of course, we can apply these two results
to necklace Lie bialgebras
and obtain their biquantization;
the only issue is probably to generalize
Kassel-Turaev’s biquantization to
infinite dimensional Lie bialgebras,
which seems not so difficult.

In this paper, we construct
a biquantization of the necklace
Lie bialgebra, via Schedler’s construction.
The advantage, compared to the
above proposal, is that
his construction is quite explicit
and combinatorial, and does not use
Drinfeld’s associator, which remains
a mystery
to us.

1.1
Main result

For a finite quiver
Q
,
the set of necklaces is
the commutator quotient space

(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}:=k\overline{Q}/[k\overline{Q},k\overline{Q}]
,
where
k\overline{Q}
is the path
algebra of
\overline{Q}
over
a field
k
of characteristic zero.
The Lie bracket is obtained by removing
the crossing edges of two necklaces and
then re-connecting the rest,
and the Lie
cobracket is realized by cutting a necklace in its
self-crossing edges and then reconnecting
the two components (see Definition

2.3
below for more details).
Denote by
L
the necklace Lie bialgebra
of
Q
.

According to
Turaev
[
9
]
,
there are two bialgebras

\varepsilon_{\hbar}(L_{\hbar})
and
V_{h}(L_{h})
that coquantizes and quantizes
the symmetric product
S(L)
of
L
. We
introduce the two canonical morphisms

p_{h}:\mathbf{N}(Q)_{h,\hbar}\to V_{h}(L_{h}),\qquad p_{\hbar}:\mathbf{N}(Q)_{h,\hbar}\to\varepsilon_{\hbar}(L_{\hbar}),

and prove that they
fit into a commutative biquantization diagram,
thereby realizing
the simultaneous biquantization of
L
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
Q
is a finite quiver.
Let
L:=(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}

be the commutator quotient
space of
k\overline{Q}
,
equipped with
the necklace Lie bialgebra
structure.
Then there is a commutative diagram of biquantization of
L
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{N}(Q)_{h,\hbar}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V_{h}(L_{h})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\varepsilon_{\hbar}(L_{\hbar})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S(L),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\hbar}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{\hbar}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{h}}

(1)

where the vertical maps are
the quantizations and the horizontal
maps are the coquantization maps respectively.

The above maps are motivated by and
hence are analogous
to those of Turaev
[
9
]

in the case of loops on Riemann surfaces.
Compared to his work,
the novel and technical part
of our construction lies in
that, while in
the Riemann surface case, one can choose
generic loops,
in the quiver case
we cannot avoid the situation
where necklaces go through
an edge several times;
see Section
4

and Remark
6.3

for more details.
Finally, we remark that in the above theorem,
if we set

h=1
, then
q_{\hbar}\circ p_{h}

coincides with the one of Schedler.

In the sequel to this paper,
we plan to do the following
two things: (1) comparing
our construction with
that of Kazhdan-Etingof and Kassel-Turaev;
(2) applying the above
biquantization to some
specific quivers,
such as the Jordan quiver (see Example

6.2
below for some preliminary
computations),
and studying the quantum geometry of
the associated quiver
varieties.

2
Lie bialgebras and their
quantizations

In this section, recall some
basic concepts on quantizations.
In Section
2.1
, we sketch the necklace
Lie bialgebra structure of a quiver.
In Section
2.2
, we introduce the
notion of biquantization of a Lie bialgebra.
Finally, in
Section
2.3
we introduce
the easy part of biquantization of a Lie
bialgebra, namely, its quantization
and coquantization.

2.1
Lie bialgebra and its
symmetric products

Recall the definition of Lie bialgebras.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie coalgebra
over
k
is a

k
-module
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a linear
morphism

\nu:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
such that

(1)

(Skew-symmetry)

\mathrm{Perm}\circ\nu=-\nu
,
and

(2)

(Co-Jacobi identity)

(\tau^{2}+\tau+1)\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\nu)\circ\nu=0
,

where
\mathrm{Perm}(a\otimes b)=b\otimes a
and
\tau:a\otimes b\otimes c\mapsto c\otimes a\otimes b
for all
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}.

The morphism
\nu
is
called the

Lie cobracket
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

The Lie algebra structure, together with the Lie coalgebra structure, defines a Lie bialgebra structure if the cocycle condition is satisfied.

Definition 2.2

(Drinfeld
[
3
, Section 3]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is a
k
-module.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
is called a
Lie bialgebra
over
k
if it is provided with a Lie bracket
[-,-]
and a Lie cobracket
\nu
so that for any
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
they satisfy the cocycle condition,

\nu([a,b])=a\nu(b)+\nu(a)b,

where the action of
a\in\mathfrak{g}
on
b\otimes c\in\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is given by
a(b\otimes c)=[a,b]\otimes c+b\otimes[a,c]
.

2.1.1
Example: the necklace Lie bialgebra

Let us recall the construction of
necklace Lie bialgebra.

Suppose that
\overline{Q}
is the double quiver of a
finite quiver
Q
.
Let

(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}\coloneqq\frac{k\overline{Q}}{[k\overline{Q},k\overline{Q}]}

denote the commutator
quotient space of
k\overline{Q}
,
whose elements are called
the necklaces.
(In the following, for a cyclic path

a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}
we also
use it to denote its equivalence class, if
the context is clear.)

Theorem 2.3

(
Bocklandt-Le Bruyn
[
2
]
,
Ginzburg
[
5
]
and
Schedler
[
8
]
)
.

For a finite quiver
Q
,
let
(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}
be as above.

(1)

For two necklaces

a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k},b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{l}\in(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}
,
let

\displaystyle\{a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k},b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{l}\}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant k,\,1\leqslant j\leqslant l}\langle a_{i},b_{j}\rangle(a_{i})_{t}a_{i+1}a_{i+2}\cdots a_{k}a_{1}\cdots a_{i-1}b_{j+1}\cdots b_{l}b_{1}\cdots b_{j-1},

where the pair
\langle-,-\rangle
is given by

\langle a_{i},b_{j}\rangle=-\langle b_{j},a_{i}\rangle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mbox{if $a_{i}\in Q$ and $b_{j}=a_{i}^{*}$},\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and

a_{i}^{*}
denotes the reversed edge corresponding to
a_{i}
in the double quiver
\overline{Q}
.
Then
((k\overline{Q})_{\natural},\{-,-\})

is a Lie algebra.

(2)

Let
\nu:(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}\to(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}\otimes(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}
be

\displaystyle\nu(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}):=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\langle a_{i},a_{j}\rangle(a_{j})_{t}a_{j+1}\cdots a_{k}a_{1}\cdots a_{i-1}\wedge(a_{i})_{t}a_{i+1}\cdots a_{j-1},

(2)

for any
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}
,
where
P\wedge Q\coloneqq P\otimes Q-Q\otimes P
and
\nu(\overline{Q}_{0})=\{0\}
.
Then
((k\overline{Q})_{\natural},\nu)

is a Lie coalgebra.

(3)

With the Lie bracket and Lie cobracket, then

((k\overline{Q})_{\natural},\{-,-\},\nu)

is a Lie bialgebra, called
the necklace Lie bialgebra.

Thereafter, we use

L
to denote the necklace Lie bialgebra
(k\overline{Q})_{\natural}.

2.1.2
The Poisson and co-Poisson structures

Lie bialgebras naturally arise from Poisson-Lie groups
(see
[
3
]
).
We next describe some algebraic structures
on the symmetric algebra of a Lie bialgebra.

Definition 2.4
.

(1)

A
Poisson algebra
is a commutative associative algebra equipped with a Lie bracket satisfying the
Leibniz rule
.

(2)

A
co-Poisson coalgebra

A
over a field
k
is a cocommutative coassociative coalgebra equipped with a Lie cobracket
\nu:A\to A\otimes A
satisfying the following

co-Leibniz rule
,

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\nu=(\nu\otimes\mathrm{id}+(\mathrm{Perm}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\nu))\circ\Delta,

where
\Delta
denotes the comultiplication of
A
.

Definition 2.5
.

(1)

A
bialgebra
over a field
k
is an
associative algebra and a coassociative coalgebra over
k
where the comultiplication
\Delta
is an algebra morphism.

(2)

A
Poisson bialgebra

A
over
k
is a
k
-module equipped with the structure of bialgebra and Poisson algebra with the same commutative multiplication and the comultiplication preserving the Lie bracket:

\Delta([a,b])=[\Delta(a),\Delta(b)]

for all
a,b\in A
.

(3)

A
co-Poisson bialgebra

A
is a co-Poisson coalgebra with a bialgebra structure where the same cocommutative comultiplication
\Delta
and the Lie cobracket
\nu
satisfy

\nu(ab)=\nu(a)\Delta(b)+\Delta(a)\nu(b)

(3)

for all
a,b\in A
.

(4)

A
bi-Poisson bialgebra

S
over
k
is a
k
-module equipped with the structure of the Poisson bialgebra and of the co-Poisson bialgebra with the same bialgebra structure satisfying

\nu([a,b])=[\Delta(a),\nu(b)]+[\nu(a),\Delta(b)]

for all
a,b\in S
.

Example 2.6

(Turaev
[
9
, Theorem 7.4 and Section 17.1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie bialgebra and
S(\mathfrak{g})
be the symmetric algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Put
V_{h}(\mathfrak{g}_{h})\coloneqq T(k[h]\otimes\mathfrak{g})/(ab-ba-h[a,b])
.
There is a bi-Poisson bialgebra structure on
S(\mathfrak{g})
and a co-Poisson bialgebra structure on
V_{h}(\mathfrak{g}_{h})
:

-

The bracket on
S(\mathfrak{g})
is the extension of the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
following the Leibniz rule.

-

The map
\Delta
for both
V_{h}(\mathfrak{g}_{h})
and
S(\mathfrak{g})
is given by extending

\Delta:a\mapsto a\otimes 1+1\otimes a,a\in\mathfrak{g}

following the multipicativity of
\Delta
.

-

With the cobracket
\nu_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, the cobracket on
V_{h}(\mathfrak{g}_{h})
is the map
\nu
extending
\nu(x)\coloneqq\nu_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
following formula (
3
).
With
h=0
, this map descends to
S(\mathfrak{g})
.

2.2
Biquantization of a Lie bialgebra

We now discuss the quantization problem
of the various Poisson structures in
the previous subsection.
A good reference is Turaev’s paper

[
9
]
.

Let
R
be a commutative associative
k
-algebra with augmentation
\varphi:R\to k
.
Given a
R
-module
A
and a
k
-module
S
, an additive map
p:A\to S
is
\varphi
-
linear
if
p(ra)=\varphi(r)p(a)
for any
r\in R
and
a\in A
.

Definition 2.7

(Quantization of a Poisson algebra)
.

With the above
R
and
\varphi
, fix a formal parameter
h\in\ker\varphi
.
Then a
quantization
over
(R,\varphi,h)
of a Poisson
k
-algebra
S
is a pair

(a
R
-algebra
A
, a
\varphi
-linear ring epimorphism
p:A\to S
)

satisfying

\displaystyle ab-ba\equiv hp^{-1}([p(a),p(b)])\mod h\ker p,\textup{ for any }a,b\in A

(4)

For brevity, the pair
(A,p)
is also called a quantization over
R
or over
\varphi
.
If
\ker p=h\cdot A
, then this quantization is said to be
reduced
.

Definition 2.8

(Coquantization of a co-Poisson coalgebra)
.

Fix a formal parameter
\hbar\in\ker\varphi
.
A
coquantization
over
(R,\varphi,\hbar)
of a co-Poisson
k
-coalgebra
S
is a pair

(a
R
-coalgebra
A
, a
\varphi
-linear coalgebra epimorphism
p:A\to S
)

satisfying

\Delta(a)-\mathrm{Perm}_{A}(\Delta(a))\equiv\hbar(p\otimes p)^{-1}(\nu\circ p)(a)\mod(\hbar\,\ker(p\otimes p)).

If
\ker p=\ker\varphi\cdot A
, then the coquantization
(A,p)
is said to be
reduced
.

There is a canonical way to produce a bi-Poisson bialgebra from a bialgebra:

Theorem 2.9

(Turaev
[
9
, Theorem 16.2.4]
)
.

Let
A
be a bialgebra over
k[h,\hbar]
that is free as a
k[h,\hbar]
-module.
For any
a,b\in A
, suppose that

ab-ba\in hA\text{ and }\Delta(a)-\mathrm{Perm}(\Delta(a))\in\hbar A^{\otimes 2}.

Put
S\coloneqq A/(h+\hbar)A
.
Then
S
is a bi-Poisson bialgebra over
k
with Lie bracket
[-,-]
and Lie cobracket
\nu

given as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}[-,-]:&S\otimes S\to S;&[p(a),p(b)]=p(h^{-1}(ab-ba)),\\
\nu:&S\to S\otimes S;&\nu(p(a))=(p\otimes p)(\hbar^{-1}(\Delta(a)-\mathrm{Perm}(\Delta(a)))).\end{array}

(Biquantization of bi-Poisson

.

Let
S
be a bi-Poisson bialgebra over

.
A

of

over

is a commutative diagram of a surjective bialgebra
morphism,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A_{\hbar}}}

2.3
Quantization and coquantization for
S(L)

This subsection is devoted to constructing the quantization
V_{h}(L_{h})
and the coquantization
\varepsilon_{\hbar}(L_{\hbar})
of
S(L)
. We
first have the following.

Theorem 2.12

(Turaev
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

With the notations in Example
2.6
.
Let
q_{\hbar}
be the map given by
h\mapsto 0
.
Then
V_{h}(\mathfrak{g}_{h})
is a reduced quantization of
S(\mathfrak{g})
given by
q_{\hbar}
.

Apply this theorem with
\mathfrak{g}_{h}=L_{h}:=k[h]\otimes L
. The obtained quantization
V_{h}(L_{h})\to S(L)
is the
q_{\hbar}
in Diagram (
1
).
We next turn to the coquantization.

Definition 2.13

(Spiral Lie coalgebra, Turaev
[
9
, Section 11.1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie coalgebra over
k
with cobracket
\nu
. Set inductively
\nu^{1}:=\nu
and

\nu^{n}:=(\mathrm{id}^{n-1}\otimes\nu)\circ\nu^{n-1}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes(n+1)}.

Then
\mathfrak{g}
is
spiral
if
\mathfrak{g}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\ker\nu^{n}
.
A Lie bialgebra is
spiral
if it is spiral as a Lie coalgebra.

The spiralness of a Lie coalgebra is equivalent to the nilpotency of its dual Lie algebra.

Example 2.14
.

The necklace Lie bialgebra
L
is spiral.
Indeed, if we
write
\nu(\alpha)=\sum\alpha_{1}\otimes\alpha_{2}
for
\alpha\in L
, then the
sum of the lengths of

\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
is always smaller than
the length of

\alpha
by (
2
).
Hence for any
\alpha\in L
, we have

\nu^{N}(\alpha)=0
for

N
big enough.

Let
(R,\varphi,\hbar)
be a triple as in
Definition
2.8
.
Given a spiral Lie coalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
over

(R,\varphi,\hbar)
, following Turaev
[
9
, Section 11.2]
,
the modified Campbell-Hausdorff series of
\log(e^{x}e^{y})
for any
elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
writes

\mu_{\hbar}(x,y)=x+y+\frac{\hbar}{2}[x,y]+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{12}([x,[x,y]]+[y,[y,x]])+\cdots.

This defines

\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*};(x,y)\mapsto\mu_{\hbar}(x,y).

Note that one may identify
S(\mathfrak{g})

(resp.
S(\mathfrak{g})\otimes S(\mathfrak{g})
)
with the set of polynomial functions on

\mathfrak{g}^{*}

(resp.
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
).
The precomposition

a\mapsto a\circ\mu_{\hbar}

defines a map
\nabla_{\hbar}:S(\mathfrak{g})\to S(\mathfrak{g})\otimes S(\mathfrak{g}),

)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{N}(Q)_{h,\hbar}}

We are left to prove the commutativity of the diagram.
For an arbitrary knot

By the definition of

For an arbitrary link

Remark 6.3

(Why is the quantization
so complicated?)
.

Our proof of the
quantization in Theorem
4.4

is quite long and complicated.
It is inspired by
Turaev’s technical work (see
[
9
, §12-15]
).
The necklaces are not completely
identical to the loops on Riemann surfaces.
Our situation is even more complicated
in the sense that,
for loops on surfaces, we may assume
they are in generic position,
but this is not the case for necklaces:
as in Section
4.3

(right after Step 1),
for an edge
e_{i}\in T_{X,Y}\cap P_{X}
, its
reverse
e_{i}^{*}\in\overline{Q}

may appear
multiple times in
P_{Y}
.
Concretely, in the above example,
the edge
a
in
\alpha

corresponds to two
a^{*}
’s in
\beta
;
this makes
\Delta(Y)
not
so easy to compute.
We had to deal with
such a situation carefully,
especially in
Steps 2 and 4
in Section
4.3
.
We thus have
worked out all the details for completeness.
