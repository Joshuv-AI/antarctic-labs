Meromorphic mappings into projective varieties intersecting arbitrary families of moving hypersurfaces
Si Duc Quang 1,2 and Nguyen Linh Chi 1

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

In 1926, R. Nevanlinna
[
10
]
initially established a second main theorem for meromorphic functions and fixed values in the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
. Later, in 1930, H. Cartan
[
1
]
extended Nevanlinna’s result to the case of linearly nondegenerate meromorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
with hyperplanes in general position. By introducing the notion of Nochka weights, E. Nochka
[
11
]
established a second main theorem for such mappings with families of hyperplanes in subgeneral position.

In recent years, the second main theorem for hypersurfaces (fixed or moving) has been intensively studied by many authors. First, in 1992, A. E. Eremenko and M. L. Sodin
[
6
]
proved a second main theorem for arbitrary holomorphic curves from
{\mathbb{C}}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
intersecting
q
hypersurfaces
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
in general position. In 2004, M. Ru
[
19
]
considered the case of algebraically nondegenerate meromorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
with families of hypersurfaces in general position by using the filtration of the vector space of homogeneous polynomials introduced by P. Corvaja and U. Zannier
[
3
]
. Then, in
[
20
]
, he generalized his result to the case of algebraically nondegenerate meromorphic mappings into a projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
by using the method in Diophantine approximation proposed by J. Evertse and R. Ferretti
[
7
]
. His results were further generalized by S. D. Quang to the case of families of hypersurfaces in subgeneral position in
[
14
]
, where he proposed the replacing hypersurfaces method in order to avoid using Nochka weights.

On the other hand, the results of M. Ru were also generalized to the case of moving hypersurfaces by G. Dethloff and T. V. Tan
[
4
,
5
]
. In order to establish the second main theorem for moving hypersurfaces in general position, G. Dethloff and T. V. Tan constructed a new filtration instead of the filtration of Corvaja and Zannier. Also, in 2018, S. D. Quang
[
13
]
established the second main theorem for moving hypersurfaces in subgeneral position. Recently, Q. Yan and G. Yu
[
22
]
used the methods of
[
5
,
13
,
14
]
to obtain a second main theorem for nonconstant meromorphic mappings, but their theorem does not have the usual normal form of Cartan’s theorem. More recently, S. D. Quang
[
16
]
generalized all the above-mentioned results by considering arbitrary families of moving hypersurfaces. In order to state this result, we need the following preparation.

We recall the following notions. Denote by
\mathcal{M}
the field of all meromorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. A moving hypersurface of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is a homogeneous polynomial
Q\in\mathcal{M}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
of the form

Q(z)({\bf x})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}}a_{I}(z){\bf x}^{I},

where
\mathcal{T}_{d}
is the set of all
(n+1)
-tuples
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})
satisfying
i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=d
and
i_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
,
a_{I}\in\mathcal{M}\ (I\in\mathcal{T}_{d})
are not all identically zero,
{\bf x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
, and
{\bf x}^{I}=x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
for all
I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
.
For each
z
which is neither a pole of any
a_{I}
nor a common zero of all
a_{I}\ (I\in\mathcal{T}_{d})
, we denote by
Q(z)^{*}
the support of
Q(z)
, namely,

Q(z)^{*}=\{(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})\mid\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}}a_{I}(z){\bf x}^{I}=0\}.

The moving hypersurface
Q
is said to be slow with respect to a meromorphic mapping
f
from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
if

\|\ T(r,a_{I})=o(T_{f}(r))\quad\forall I\in\mathcal{T}_{d},

where
T(r,a_{I})
and
T_{f}(r)
are the characteristic functions of
a_{I}
and
f
respectively (see Section 2 for the definitions).
Here, the notation “
\|P
” means that the assertion
P
holds for all
r\in(0,+\infty)
outside a set of finite Borel measure.

Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
given by

Q_{i}(z)({\bf x})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d_{i}}}a_{iI}(z){\bf x}^{I},

where
d_{i}=\deg Q_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
. Denote by
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{Q}}
the smallest subfield of
\mathcal{M}
containing
{\mathbb{C}}
and all ratios
\frac{a_{iI}}{a_{iJ}}
with
a_{iJ}\not\equiv 0
.

Let
\tilde{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
be a reduced representation of
f
. Denote by
I(f({\mathbb{C}}^{m}))
the ideal of all polynomials
Q
in
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{Q}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
(including the zero polynomial) such that
Q(\tilde{f})\equiv 0
. Choose a minimal generating set of
I(f({\mathbb{C}}^{m}))
, denoted by
\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{p}\}
. The algebraic dimension of
f
over
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is defined by

n_{f}:=\dim V_{z},\ \text{where }\ V_{z}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}R_{i}(z)^{*},

for generic points
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. Here, whenever we say that an assertion holds for generic points, we mean that it holds outside a proper analytic subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
.

Let
H_{V_{z}}(u)
be the Hilbert function of
V_{z}
(see Section 2 for the definition). For a fixed positive integer
u
,
H_{V_{z}}(u)
is constant for generic points
z
. By the usual theory of Hilbert polynomials,

\displaystyle H_{V_{z}}(u)=\deg V_{z}\cdot\dfrac{u^{\dim V_{z}}}{(\dim V_{z})!}+O(u^{\dim V_{z}-1}).

This implies that
\deg V_{z}
is also constant for generic points
z
. We define the algebraic degree of
f
over
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}
by

\delta_{f}:=\deg V_{z},

for generic points
z
.

Let
V
be a projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. For an analytic subset
S
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, we define

\mathrm{codim}_{V}S:=\begin{cases}\dim V-\dim(V\cap S),&\text{if }V\cap S\neq\varnothing,\\
\dim V+1,&\text{if }V\cap S=\varnothing.\end{cases}

We now recall the following definition from
[
16
]
.

Definition A.
(cf.
[
16
, Definition 3.3]
)

With the above notation, we define the distributive constant of the family
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
with respect to
f
by

\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}:={\mathrel{\mathop{{\max}}\limits_{{\varnothing\neq\Gamma\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}}}}}\dfrac{\sharp\Gamma}{\mathrm{codim}_{V_{z}}\left(\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma}Q_{j}(z)^{*}\right)}

for generic points
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
.

In
[
16
]
, the first author proved the following theorem.

Theorem B.
(see
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
)

Let
f
be a meromorphic mapping of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of slowly moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with respect to
f
, and let
n_{f}
denote the algebraic dimension of
f
over
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\|\ (q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{\deg Q_{i}}N_{(Q_{i},f)}(r).

Here,
N_{(Q_{i},f)}(r)
denote the counting function of the divisor
f^{*}Q_{i}
(see Section 2 for the definition).

Hence, the total defect bound obtained in this theorem is
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)
. If
f
is a meromorphic mapping from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into a projective subvariety
V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
which is algebraically nondegenerate over
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}
, and if
\mathcal{Q}
is in general position with respect to
V
, then
n_{f}=\dim V
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}=1
. Therefore, the bound becomes
\dim V+1
, and we recover the second main theorem of Dethloff and Tan in
[
5
]
.

However, without imposing any algebraic nondegeneracy condition on
f
, the bound
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)
depends on
f
and is generally difficult to estimate. In this paper, we establish a second main theorem whose total defect bound does not depend on the mapping, and whose counting functions are truncated at an explicit level. To state our result, we recall the following definition.

Definition C.
(see
[
16
, Definition 3.4]
)

Let
V
be a subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k
. Assume that
V
is not generically contained in any
Q_{j}(z)^{*}\ (1\leq j\leq q)
. We define the distributive constant of
\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
with respect to
V
by

\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}:={\mathrel{\mathop{{\max}}\limits_{{\varnothing\neq\Gamma\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}}}}}\dfrac{\sharp\Gamma}{\mathrm{codim}_{V}\left(\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma}Q_{j}(z)^{*}\right)}

for generic points
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
.

The family
\mathcal{Q}
is said to satisfy the Bézout condition with respect to
f
if for and subsets
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
of
\{1,\ldots,q\}
,

\mathrm{codim}_{V_{z}}\left(\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}}Q_{j}(z)^{*}\right)\leq\mathrm{codim}_{V_{z}}\left(\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma_{1}}Q_{j}(z)^{*}\right)+\mathrm{codim}_{V_{z}}\left(\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma_{2}}Q_{j}(z)^{*}\right)

for generic points
z
. Note that, this condition is automatically satisfied if
V_{z}={\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}).

In this paper, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic map of
\mathbf{C}^{m}
into a projective subvariety
V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbf{C})
of dimension
k
. Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of slowly (with respect to
f
) moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
satisfying the Bézout condition with respect to
f
and
d=lcd(\deg Q_{1},\ldots,\deg Q_{q})
. Let
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}
be the distributive constant of
\mathcal{Q}
with respect to
V
and
\delta_{f}
the algebraic degree of
f
over
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
,

\|\ (q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}(3k-1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{\deg Q_{i}}N^{[L-1]}_{(Q_{i},f)}(r),

where

L=d^{k}\delta_{f}(u+1)^{k}\left\lfloor\biggl(1+\frac{\epsilon}{4}\biggl)\cdot\mathrm{exp}\left(d^{k}\delta_{f}(u+1)^{k+q}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{4k\Delta_{Q,V}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)\right\rfloor

with
u=\lceil 2k(2k+1)\Delta_{Q,V}d^{k}\delta_{f}(2k\Delta_{Q,V}+\epsilon)\epsilon^{-1}\rceil
. Moreover, if all
Q_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
are fixed hypersurfaces then we may take

L=\left\lfloor d^{k^{2}+k}(\delta_{f})^{k+1}e^{k+\frac{3}{2}}(4k)^{k}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}^{k}(2k\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{k}\right\rfloor.

Here,
\left\lceil x\right\rceil
is the smallest integer greater than or equal to the real number
x
, and
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the the greatest integer not exceeding
x
.

If the family
\mathcal{Q}
is in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
then we will prove a more optimal result as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic map of
\mathbf{C}^{m}
into a projective subvariety
V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbf{C})
of dimension
k
. Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be a family of slowly (with respect to
f
) moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
satisfying the Bézout condition with respect to
f
and
d=lcd(\deg Q_{1},\ldots,\deg Q_{q})
. Let
\delta_{f}
the algebraic degree of
f
over
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
,

\|\ (q-3N+1-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{\deg Q_{i}}N^{[L-1]}_{(Q_{i},f)}(r),

where

L=d^{k}\delta_{f}(u+1)^{k}\left\lfloor\biggl(1+\frac{\epsilon}{4}\biggl)\cdot\mathrm{exp}\left(d^{k}\delta_{f}(u+1)^{k+q}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{4N}{\epsilon}\right)\right)\right\rfloor

with
u=\lceil 2N(2k+1)d^{k}\delta_{f}(2N+\epsilon)\epsilon^{-1}\rceil
. Moreover, if all
Q_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
are fixed hypersurfaces then we may take

L=\left\lfloor d^{k^{2}+k}(\delta_{f})^{k+1}e^{k+\frac{3}{2}}(4N)^{k}(2N\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{k}\right\rfloor.

Note that, in the case where
f:{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is algebraically nondegenerate over
\mathcal{K}_{f}
(the field of all meromorphic functions which are small with respect to
f
), and
\mathcal{Q}
is in
N
-subgeneral position, Q. Cai and C.J. Yang
[
2
]
obtained the total defect bound
\frac{3}{2}(2N-n+1).
Furthermore, if
\mathcal{Q}
is in general position and satisfies the Bézout condition with respect to
f
, and if
n\leq 8
, then L. Shi, Q. Yan, and G. Yu
[
17
]
obtained the total defect bound
2n.
However, all of these results were established without truncation levels for the counting functions.

2.
Notation and Auxialiary results

(a) Some notation from Nevanlina theory.

For
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, we set
\|z\|:=\big(|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{m}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
and define

\displaystyle v_{m-1}(z)

\displaystyle:=\big(dd^{c}\|z\|^{2}\big)^{m-1},

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(z)

\displaystyle:=d^{c}\log\|z\|^{2}\land\big(dd^{c}\log\|z\|^{2}\big)^{m-1}\text{on}\quad\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{0\}.

For a divisor
\nu
on a
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and a positive integer
M
or
M=+\infty
, as usual we denote by
N^{[M]}(r,\nu)
the counting function of
\nu
with multiplicities truncated to level
M
.

For a meromorphic function
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, denote by
\nu_{\varphi}
its divisor of zeros and set

N_{\varphi}(r)=N(r,\nu_{\varphi}),\ N_{\varphi}^{[M]}(r)=N^{[M]}(r,\nu_{\varphi})\ (r_{0}<r<R_{0}).

For brevity, we will omit the character
[M]
if
M=+\infty
.

Let
f:\mathbf{C}^{m}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbf{C})
be a meromorphic mapping with a reduced representation

\tilde{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
. Set
\|\tilde{f}\|=\big(|f_{0}|^{2}+\dots+|f_{n}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
.
The characteristic function of
f
is defined by

\displaystyle T_{f}(r)=\int\limits_{\|z\|=r}\log\|\tilde{f}\|\sigma_{m}-\int\limits_{\|z\|=1}\log\|\tilde{f}\|\sigma_{m}.

Let
\varphi
be a nonzero meromorphic function on
\mathbf{C}^{m}
, which are occasionally regarded as a meromorphic map into
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbf{C})
. The proximity function of
\varphi
is defined by

m(r,\varphi):=\int_{\|z\|=r}\log\max\ (|\varphi|,1)\sigma_{m}.

The Nevanlinna’s characteristic function of
\varphi
is defined as follows

T(r,\varphi):=N_{\frac{1}{\varphi}}(r)+m(r,\varphi).

Then

T_{\varphi}(r)=T(r,\varphi)+O(1).

The function
\varphi
is said to be small (with respect to
f
) if
\|\ T_{\varphi}(r)=o(T_{f}(r))
.

Let
Q
be a moving hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(\mathbf{C})
of degree
d\geq 1
given by

Q(z)({\bf x})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}}a_{I}{\bf x}^{I},

where
a_{I}\in\mathcal{M}
for all
I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
. We will write
N^{[M]}_{(Q,f)}(r)
for
N^{[M]}_{Q(\tilde{f})}(r)
.
The proximity function of
f
with respect to
Q
, denoted by
m_{f}(r,Q)
, is defined by

m_{f}(r,Q)=\int_{\|z\|=r}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}\|^{d}}{|Q(\tilde{f})|}\sigma_{m}-\int_{\|z\|=1}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}\|^{d}}{|Q(\tilde{f})|}\sigma_{m}.

This definition is independent of the choice of the reduced representation of
f
.

If
Q
is a slowly moving hypersurface with respect to
f
, then the first main theorem in Nevanlinna theory for meromorphic mappings and moving hypersurfaces states

dT_{f}(r)=m_{f}(r,Q)+N_{Q(\tilde{f})}(r)+o(T_{f}(r)).

3.

{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{1}},...,{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{H_{Y}(u)}}
is a basic of
{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}]_{u}/(I_{Y_{z}})_{u}
and

S_{Y_{z}}(u,{\bf c})=\sum_{i=1}^{H_{Y}(u)}{\bf a}_{i}\cdot{\bf c}.

Similarly as above, we write
{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{i}}=L_{i}(v_{1},...,v_{\xi_{u}-p})
, where
L_{1},...,L_{\xi_{u}-p}
are linearly independent linear forms. We see that

\nu^{0}_{W(\tilde{F})}(z)\geq t\sum_{i=1}^{H_{Y}(u)}\max\{0,\nu^{0}_{L_{i}(\tilde{F})}(z)-n_{u}\},

where
n_{u}=(\xi_{u}-p)t-1
. It is easy to see that

\nu^{0}_{L_{i}(\tilde{F})}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{q}a_{i,j}\nu^{0}_{Q_{j}(\tilde{f})}(z),

and hence

\max\{0,\nu^{0}_{L_{i}(\tilde{F})}(z)-n_{u}\}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{q}a_{i,j}c_{j}={{\bf a}_{i}}\cdot{\bf c}.

Thus,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\nu^{0}_{W(\tilde{F})}(z)\geq t\sum_{i=1}^{H_{Y}(u)}{{\bf a}_{i}}\cdot{\bf c}=tS_{Y}(u,{\bf c}).

Choose an index
\sigma_{i_{0}}
such that

\nu^{0}_{Q_{\sigma_{i_{0}}(1)}(\tilde{f})}(z)\geq\nu^{0}_{Q_{\sigma_{i_{0}}(2)}(\tilde{f})}(z)\geq\cdots\geq\nu^{0}_{Q_{\sigma_{i_{0}}(q)}(\tilde{f})}(z).

By Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}e_{Y_{z}}({\bf c})

\displaystyle\geq(c_{\sigma_{i_{0}}(1),z}+\cdots+c_{\sigma_{i_{0}}(\ell_{i_{0}}),z})\cdot\delta

\displaystyle=\delta\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i_{0}}}\max\{0,\nu^{0}_{Q_{\sigma_{i_{0}}(j)}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}

\displaystyle=\delta\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{q}\max\{0,\nu^{0}_{Q_{j}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}+O(\nu_{R^{i_{0}}}(z)),

where
R^{i}
is the resultant of the family
\{Q_{\sigma_{i}(j)}\}_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,q
.

On the other hand, by Theorem
2.3
we have that

\displaystyle S_{Y_{z}}(u,{\bf c})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{u(\xi_{u}-p)}{(n_{f}+1)\delta}e_{Y}({\bf c})-(2n_{f}+1)\delta(\xi_{u}-p)\max_{1\leq i\leq q}c_{i}+O(\nu_{R^{i_{0}}}(z))

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{u(\xi_{u}-p)}{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)}-(2n_{f}+1)\delta(\xi_{u}-p)\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \times\sum_{j=1}^{q}\max\{0,\nu^{0}_{Q_{j}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}+O(\nu_{R^{i_{0}}}(z)).

Combining this inequality and (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{(n_{f}+1)}{u(\xi_{u}-p)s}\nu^{0}_{W(\tilde{F})}(z)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{t}{us}\left(\frac{u}{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}}-(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\delta_{f}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=1}^{q}\max\{0,\nu^{0}_{Q_{j}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}+O(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{0}}\nu_{R^{i}}(z)).

Integrating both sides of this inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\biggl\|\ \dfrac{(n_{f}+1)}{u(\xi_{u}-p)s}N_{W(\tilde{F})}(r)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{t}{s}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}}-\frac{(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\delta}{u}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \times\sum_{j=1}^{q}\left(N_{(Q_{j},f)}(r)-N^{[n_{u}]}_{(Q_{j},f)}(r)\right)+o(T_{f}(r)).

Seting
m_{0}=\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}}-\frac{(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\delta}{u}
and combining inequalities (
3.9
) with the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\biggl\|\ \sum_{i=1}^{q}m_{f}(r,Q_{i})\leq\frac{d(n_{f}+1)t}{sm_{0}}T_{f}(r)-\frac{t}{s}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\left(N_{(Q_{i},f)}(r)-N^{[n_{u}]}_{(Q_{j},f)}(r)\right)+o(T_{f}(r)).

This inequality implies that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\biggl\|\ \left(q-\frac{(n_{f}+1)t}{sm_{0}}\right)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N^{[n_{u}]}_{(Q_{j},f)}(r)+o(T_{f}(r)).

We choose
u=\lceil 2\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}(\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)+\epsilon)\epsilon^{-1}\rceil
.
Then

u\geq\biggl\lceil\dfrac{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\delta(\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)+\epsilon)}{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)+\epsilon-\frac{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)t}{s}}\biggl\rceil,

and we have:

\displaystyle\bullet\

\displaystyle q-\frac{(n_{f}+1)t}{sm_{0}}\geq q-\frac{\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)t}{(1-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)\delta/u)s}

\displaystyle\geq q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon;

\displaystyle\bullet\

\displaystyle n_{u}+1=(\xi_{u}-p)t

\displaystyle\leq d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}(u+1)^{n_{f}}\left\lfloor\bigl(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2(n_{f}+1)\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}}\bigl)^{\bigl[\frac{d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}(u+1)^{n_{f}+q}}{\log^{2}(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2(n_{f}+1)\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}})}\bigl]+1}\right\rfloor=L;

\displaystyle\bullet\

\displaystyle\frac{(t(\xi_{u}-p)-1)t(n_{f}+1)}{2sdm_{0}u}<\frac{t(n_{f}+1)}{sm_{0}}\cdot\frac{(L-1)}{2du}.

Then, from (
3.11
) we have

\displaystyle\bigl\|\ (q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d}N^{[L-1]}_{(Q_{j},f)}(r)+o(T_{f}(r)).

Then, we get the desired inequality.

If all
Q_{i}
are fixed hypersurfaces then
t=s=1
. By choosing

u^{\prime}=\lceil\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}(\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)+\epsilon)\epsilon^{-1}\rceil

and replacing
u
in the above by
u^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle n_{u^{\prime}}+1

\displaystyle=\xi_{u^{\prime}}-p<\delta\binom{n_{f}+u^{\prime}}{n_{f}}\leq d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}\binom{n_{f}+u^{\prime}}{n_{f}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}e^{n_{f}}\left(1+\frac{u^{\prime}}{n_{f}}\right)^{n_{f}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor d^{{n_{f}}^{2}+n_{f}}(\delta_{f})^{n_{f}+1}e^{n_{f}}(2n_{f}+5)^{n_{f}}(\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)\epsilon^{-1}+\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f})^{n_{f}}\right\rfloor,

and hence we may get the truncation level

L=\left\lfloor{}^{n_{f}^{2}+n_{f}}(\delta_{f})^{n_{f}+1}e^{n_{f}}(2n_{f}+5)^{n_{f}}(\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{Q},f}(n_{f}+1)\epsilon^{-1}+\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f})^{n_{f}}\right\rfloor.

Hence, the proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

4.
Proof of Main Theorems

Motivated by the idea of using Nochka diagram for families of fixed hypersurfaces in subgeneral position of G. Heier and A. Levin in
[
8
]
, we now prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into a projective subvariety
V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k
. Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
be a family of moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
which has distributive constant
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}
with respect to
V\ (N>k)
and satisfying the Bézout property with respect to
f
. Let
n_{f}
be the algebraic dimension of
f
over the field
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then there exists a subset
\Gamma\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}
such that
\sharp\Gamma\geq q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\left(k-\left\lceil\frac{n_{f}}{2}\right\rceil\right)
and the family
\mathcal{P}=\{Q_{i}:i\in\Gamma\}
satisfies
\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f}\leq\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}
.

Proof.

Let
\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{\lambda}\}
a minimal subset of
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, which generates
I(f({\mathbb{C}}^{m}))
.
For
z
is not a pole of any
R_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq\lambda)
, set
V_{z}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{\lambda}R_{i}(z)^{*}
. Then
\dim V_{z}=n_{f}
for generic points
z
.

For any subset
\Gamma\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}
, denote

Q_{\Gamma}(z)=\bigcap_{i\in\Gamma}Q_{i}(z)^{*}\cap V_{z},\quad c(\Gamma)=\operatorname{codim}_{V_{z}}Q_{\Gamma}(z)

for generic points
z
. Note that

\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}=\max_{\Gamma\neq\emptyset}\frac{\sharp\Gamma}{c(\Gamma)}.

If
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}\leq\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}
, then the conclusion holds with
\Gamma=\{1,\ldots,q\}
.

Assume
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}>\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}
. Let

\mathcal{S}=\left\{\Gamma\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}:\frac{\sharp\Gamma}{c(\Gamma)}>\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{S}
is nonempty. Choose
\Gamma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\sharp\Gamma_{1}
is maximal. Since

\displaystyle\sharp\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\left(\dim V-\dim V\cap\bigl(\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma_{1}}Q_{j}(z)^{*}\bigl)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\left(\dim V-\dim V_{z}\cap\bigl(\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma_{1}}Q_{j}(z)^{*}\bigl)\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\left(k-n_{f}+c(\Gamma_{1})\right),

we have

\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}<\frac{\sharp\Gamma_{1}}{c(\Gamma_{1})}\leq\frac{\left(k-n_{f}+c(\Gamma_{1})\right)}{c(\Gamma_{1})}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}.

This implies that
c(\Gamma_{1})<\frac{n_{f}+1}{2}
and then
c(\Gamma_{1})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n_{f}}{2}\right\rfloor
.

Claim.
For any
\Gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{\Gamma_{1}\}
, we have
\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof of Claim.

Assume
\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}=\emptyset
. Similar as above, one has
c(\Gamma_{2})<\frac{n_{f}+1}{2}
. Using the Bézout property, we have

c(\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2})\leq c(\Gamma_{1})+c(\Gamma_{2})<n_{f}+1.

This yields that
\bigcap_{j\in\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}}(Q_{j}(z)^{*}\cap V_{z})\neq\varnothing
for generic points
z
.

Also, from

\frac{\sharp\Gamma_{1}}{c(\Gamma_{1})}>\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\sharp\Gamma_{2}}{c(\Gamma_{2})}>\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V},

by using the Bézout property we obtain

\frac{\sharp\Gamma_{1}+\sharp\Gamma_{2}}{c(\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2})}\geq\frac{\sharp\Gamma_{1}+\sharp\Gamma_{2}}{c(\Gamma_{1})+c(\Gamma_{2})}>\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}.

Thus
\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
, contradicting the maximality of
\sharp\Gamma_{1}
.
The claim is proved.

Now set

\Gamma=\{1,\ldots,q\}\setminus\Gamma_{1},\quad\mathcal{P}=\{Q_{i}:i\in\Gamma\}.

Then

\sharp\Gamma\geq q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\left(k-n_{f}+c(\Gamma_{1})\right)\geq q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\left(k-\left\lceil\frac{n_{f}}{2}\right\rceil\right).

Finally, suppose there exists
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
such that

\frac{|\Gamma^{\prime}|}{c(\Gamma^{\prime})}>\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}.

Then
\Gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
but
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{1}=\emptyset
, contradicting the claim.
Hence

\Delta_{\mathcal{P},V}\leq\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}.

This completes the proof.
∎

If the family
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
is in
N
-subgeneral position then we will have more optimal result as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
be a nonconstant meromorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into a projective subvariety
V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k
. Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
be a family of moving hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position with respect to
V\ (N>k)
and satisfying the Bézout property with respect to
f
. Let
n_{f}
be the algebraic dimension of
f
over the field
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then there exists a subset
\Gamma\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}
such that
\sharp\Gamma\geq q-N+\left\lceil\frac{n_{f}}{2}\right\rceil
and the family
\mathcal{P}=\{Q_{i}:i\in\Gamma\}
satisfies
\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f}\leq\frac{2N-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}
.

Proof.

We use the same notation and the arguments as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
. If
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}\leq\frac{2N-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}
then the conclusion holds with
\Gamma=\{1,\ldots,q\}
.

Assume
\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},f}>\frac{2N-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}
. Let

\mathcal{S}=\left\{\Gamma\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}:\frac{\sharp\Gamma}{c(\Gamma)}>\frac{2N-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{S}
is nonempty. Choose
\Gamma_{1}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\sharp\Gamma_{1}
is maximal. Since

\sharp\Gamma_{1}\leq N-n_{f}+c(\Gamma_{1}),

we have

\frac{2N-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}<\frac{\sharp\Gamma_{1}}{c(\Gamma_{1})}\leq\frac{N-n_{f}+c(\Gamma_{1})}{c(\Gamma_{1})}.

This implies that
c(\Gamma_{1})<\frac{n_{f}+1}{2}
and then
c(\Gamma_{1})\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n_{f}}{2}\right\rfloor
.

Similarly as the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we have that
\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}\neq\emptyset
for any
\Gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{\Gamma_{1}\}
.

By setting

\Gamma=\{1,\ldots,q\}\setminus\Gamma_{1},\quad\mathcal{P}=\{Q_{i}:i\in\Gamma\},

we have

\sharp\Gamma\geq q-N+n_{f}+c(\Gamma_{1})\geq q-N+\left\lceil\frac{n_{f}}{2}\right\rceil,

and
\Gamma^{\prime}\not\in\mathcal{S}
for every
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
(since
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{1}=\emptyset
). Hence

\Delta_{\mathcal{P},V}\leq\frac{2N-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Lemma
4.1
, there is a subset
\Gamma
of
\{1,\ldots,q\}
with
\sharp\Gamma\geq q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\left(k-\left\lceil\frac{n_{f}}{2}\right\rceil\right)
such that the family of hypersurfaces
\mathcal{P}=\{Q_{i};i\in\Gamma\}
satisfies
\Delta_{\mathcal{P},V}\leq\frac{2k-n_{f}+1}{n_{f}+1}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}
. Applying Theorem
3.2
for
f
and the family
\mathcal{P}
, we obtain

\biggl\|\ (q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\left(2k-n_{f}+1+k-\left\lceil\frac{n_{f}}{2}\right\rceil\right)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i\in\Gamma_{1}}\frac{1}{\deg Q_{i}}N^{[L_{1}^{\prime}-1]}(r,\nu_{(Q_{i},f)})+o(T_{f}(r)),

where

L_{1}^{\prime}=d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}(u^{\prime}+1)^{n_{f}}\left\lfloor\bigl(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2(n_{f}+1)\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f}}\bigl)^{\left\lfloor\frac{d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}(u^{\prime}+1)^{n_{f}+q}}{\log^{2}(1+\frac{\epsilon}{2(n_{f}+1)\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f}})}\right\rfloor+1}\right\rfloor

with

u^{\prime}=\lceil 2\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f}(2n_{f}+1)(n_{f}+1)d^{n_{f}}\delta_{f}(\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f}(n_{f}+1)+\epsilon)\epsilon^{-1}\rceil.

We have the following estimate:

•

1\leq n_{f}\leq k
;

•

\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f}(n_{f}+1)\leq(2k-n_{f}+1)\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\leq 2k\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}
;

•

\log(1+x)>\frac{2x}{x+2}\ \forall x>0
, and hence
(1+x)^{\frac{1}{\log^{2}(1+x)}}=e^{\frac{1}{\log(1+x)}}\leq e^{\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{2}}\ \forall x>0
.

Therefore, we have

•

u^{\prime}\leq\lceil 2k\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}(2k+1)d^{k}\delta_{f}(2k\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}+\epsilon)\epsilon^{-1}\rceil=u;

•

L^{\prime}\leq d^{k}\delta_{f}(u+1)^{k}\left\lfloor\bigl(1+\frac{\epsilon}{4}\bigl)e^{\left(d^{k}\delta_{f}(u+1)^{k+q}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{4}{\epsilon}\right)}\right\rfloor=L.

Then, the above inequalities imply that

\biggl\|\ \left(q-\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}(3k-1)-\epsilon\right)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{\deg Q_{i}}N^{[L-1]}(r,\nu_{(Q_{i},f)})+o(T_{f}(r)).

Moreover, if all
Q_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
are fixed hypersurfaces then we may take

\displaystyle L^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor d^{n_{f}^{2}+n_{f}}(\delta_{f})^{n_{f}+1}e^{n_{f}}(2n_{f}+5)^{n_{f}}(\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{P},f}(n_{f}+1)\epsilon^{-1}+\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f})^{n_{f}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor d^{k^{2}+k}(\delta_{f})^{k+1}e^{k}\frac{(2k+5)^{k}(2k)^{k}}{(k+1)^{k}}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}^{k}(2k\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{k}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor d^{k^{2}+k}(\delta_{f})^{k+1}e^{k+\frac{3}{2}}(4k)^{k}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}^{k}(2k\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{k}\right\rfloor.

Here, the last inequality comes from that

\frac{(2k+5)^{k}(2k)^{k}}{(k+1)^{k}}=\left(4k+\frac{6k}{k+1}\right)^{k}<(4k+6)^{k}\leq(4k)^{k}e^{3/2}.

Then we may take

L=\left\lfloor d^{k^{2}+k}(\delta_{f})^{k+1}e^{k+\frac{3}{2}}(4k)^{k}\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}^{k}(2k\Delta_{\mathcal{Q},V}\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{k}\right\rfloor.

Hence, the theorem is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We follow the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The only difference is that, instead of applying Lemma
4.1
, we apply Lemma
4.2
, noting that

(n_{f}+1)\Delta_{\mathcal{P},f}\leq 2N.

Since the proofs of the two theorems are essentially identical, we omit the detailed proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎
