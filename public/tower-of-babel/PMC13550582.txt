The relationship between memorable tourist experiences and subjective well-being, the mediating role of emotions, and the moderating role of psychological resilience
Authors: Zuwu Hu, Hongjuan Li, Ling Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTourism experiences have become recognized as an important factor, reflecting a positive relationship with subjective well-being. The Broaden-and-Build Theory suggests that positive experience, positive emotions, and yield lasting psychological benefits go together. However, the specific mechanisms through which memorable tourist experiences translate into subjective well-being via positive emotions have not been thoroughly explored, particularly the moderating role of psychological resilience in this process. This study used a questionnaire to gather valid responses from 665 university teachers, employing standardized scales to measure memorable tourist experiences, positive emotions, psychological resilience, and subjective well-being. The data were analyzed using SPSS software, and a moderated mediation model was constructed and examined. Memorable tourist experiences were significantly positively correlated with subjective well-being (β = 0.436, t = 14.324, p < 0.001), with positive emotions playing an important mediating role. Additionally, psychological resilience significantly moderated the relationship between positive emotion and subjective well-being (β = 0.261, t = 10.352, p < 0.001), indicating that individuals with higher psychological resilience are more adept at sustaining and integrate the positive emotions brought about by memorable tourist experiences, thereby exhibiting stronger subjective well-being. This study offers a clearer understanding of the link between memorable tourist experiences and subjective well-being, extending the Broaden-and-Build Theory’s application to tourism. The findings also indicate associations between positive emotions during tourism experience and university teachers’ well-being, with psychological resilience moderating these associations, providing a new perspective for practical interventions in university teacher care.Keywords: Memorable tourist experiences, Positive emotions, Psychological resilience, Subjective well-being, University teachersSubject terms: Psychology, Psychology, Sociology

Introduction

As universities take on increasing responsibilities and performance demands rise, teachers are required to manage multiple duties, including teaching, research, student supervision, and administrative services. A survey conducted in 2024 among Saudi Arabia university teachers found that more than 70% of respondents reported high levels of work stress. Many also report emotional exhaustion, sleep problems, and lower life satisfaction 1. Fast-paced work environments, diverse evaluation standards, and frequent deadlines increase psychological demands, highlighting the need to understand and support university teachers’ mental health2,3. In this context, subjective well-being (SWB), as an important indicator of life quality and satisfaction, reflects an individual’s overall emotional response to and assessment of life4. For university teachers, SWB is not only a key indicator of psychological health and life quality but also closely related to their adaptability and resilience in high-pressure environments5. Higher SWB is typically associated with more stable emotional regulation, positive interpersonal relationships, and sustained professional engagement. This helps teachers maintain effective teaching and academic collaboration, while also promoting student development and the overall effectiveness of the school through classroom atmosphere and teacher-student interactions6,7. Therefore, focusing on the SWB of university teachers not only contributes to individual well-being but is also crucial for the quality of education and the long-term development of the institution.

Teachers often relieve work stress and restore energy through participation in tourism activities or short-term leisure trips. Empirical studies show that participating in leisure and travel activities is associated with reduced psychological burden and higher positive emotions (PE) among university teachers8. These activities offer immersive and novel experiences that promote physical and mental relaxation, emotional recovery, and social interaction, helping to relieve psychological and physiological stress. Therefore, studying the link between tourism experiences and the SWB of university teachers can provide insight into their psychological needs and offer a theoretical foundation for understanding their well-being.

While existing research has examined the positive link between tourism experiences and well-being8, few studies have delved into how these experiences initiate the broadened process through PE, further promoting stable SWB. Many studies simplify tourism experiences to satisfaction or overall evaluation, while memorable tourist experiences (MTE) are often characterized by novelty and immersion. These features align more closely with the situational characteristics that trigger PE in the Broaden-and-Build Theory9. The lack of attention to MTE limits our deeper understanding of how they promote well-being. The Broaden-and-Build Theory posits that PE can promote more favorable life evaluations and resource accumulation. By examining PE as a mediating variable, it can explain the association between MTE and higher SWB over time10,11. However, whether the broadened process translates into building resources may vary across individuals. The same positive experience may yield different levels of psychological benefits for different individuals. Although this difference has been underexplored in relevant research, psychological resilience (PR) may be a crucial factor in this process. PR determines an individual’s capacity to maintain and integrate PE and moderates the relationship between PE and SWB12. This study is innovative in its emphasis on university teachers as a distinct group. This group faces long-term high levels of work-related stress and occupational burnout, and their well-being outcomes differs from that of other groups due to the unique characteristics of their profession. By examining the tourism experiences of university teachers, this study not only fills the research gap regarding their SWB but also systematically explores the link between MTE and SWB through the lens of the Broaden-and-Build Theory, particularly the moderating role of PR in this process. This research aims to establish a theoretical basis for psychological health interventions for university teachers and offer insights for the development of related policies.

Theoretical foundation

The Broaden-and-Build Theory13 proposes that PE is not only immediate pleasurable experiences but also expand a person’s attention and cognitive perspectives. With this expansion, individuals interpret situations in an open and flexible manner, and adopt a diverse range of actions and choices. The exploration and experimentation generated during the broadened process gradually accumulate into more stable resources over time. As a result, individuals have more resources at their disposal when facing stress or challenges, enabling them to maintain a good adaptive state, together with higher SWB13,14. Although the Broaden-and-Build Theory was originally developed in general psychology, its core mechanisms are particularly relevant to tourism contexts. Tourism is inherently a positive, emotionally charged experience, often involving novelty, aesthetic pleasure, relaxation, and social bonding, all of which are known to co-occur with PE. Unlike daily life settings where PE may be diluted by routine stressors, tourism provides a concentrated positive emotion lab, making the broadening and building processes more observable and impactful.

In the context of travel experiences, the broaden-and-build theory provides a useful framework for understanding individuals’ psychological responses. Novel and immersive travel experiences can evoke PE, which expand individuals’ attention and cognitive perspective. This expansion makes it easier for individuals to try new activities, engage in social interactions, and experience success. These behaviors and experiences gradually accumulate into sustainable psychological resources, which are associated with higher SWB15. PR is an important individual difference that reflects one’s capability to stay positive and adjust rapidly during stressful or shifting circumstances. According to the broaden-and-build theory, PR is a correlate of the efficiency with which PE translates into resource accumulation. Individuals with high PR are more likely to convert short-term PE into lasting psychological resources, which helps maintain higher SWB in complex or high-pressure situations12. In summary, the broaden-and-build theory, extended to the tourism context, provides the theoretical basis for this study. It explains why travel experiences can expand individuals’ cognition and behavior through PE, and how, with the support of PR, these experiences gradually convert into lasting psychological resources, which are closely associated with SWB. Based on this theoretical framework, the following sections of this study discuss the relationship between travel experiences and SWB, the mediating role of PE, and the moderating role of PR.

Memorable tourist experiences and subjective well-being

MTE is travel experience that remain in memory for a long time and holds significant value16. Studies have found a significant positive link between MTE and SWB9. For example, Yen, et al. 17 discovered that the more significant the tourism experience, the stronger the individual’s SWB. MTE is often characterized by novelty, uniqueness, and a strong sense of involvement, allowing teachers to temporarily step away from their usual work routine. In a new environment, they gain a greater sense of autonomy and control, and through exploration and cultural exposure, they achieve self-identity. These experiences strengthen individuals’ awareness of life goals and values, which is associated with higher SWB17,18. In addition, travel can help teachers redefine their role boundaries, restore their physical and mental state, and support a better balance between work and life, with individuals reporting greater SWB8. MTE also has strong reminiscence value; even after the trip ends, they can remain important memories in one’s life. These experiences help teachers maintain a clear sense of direction and self-worth when facing daily stress, reflecting a positive relationship with SWB19,20. Therefore, the more profound and aligned with individual values the tourism experience is, the higher the SWB teachers typically report.

The mediating role of positive emotions

PE arises from an individual’s response to internal and external triggers and occurrences, which fulfill their needs21. Studies have found a significant positive link between PE and SWB22. For example, Cheng and Jiao21 found that individuals experiencing higher PE tend to report higher SWB. When teachers frequently experience PE like happiness and hope in their daily work and life, they are more likely to interpret teaching, research pressures, and role tasks in a positive way, thereby exhibiting higher levels of SWB23. At the same time, PE is often associated with higher cognitive flexibility and problem-solving abilities, allowing individuals to identify and utilize available resources and adopt more constructive coping strategies when facing challenges. These resources and coping experiences tend to be linked with higher SWB10. Additionally, PE creates a context for more open communication, helping teachers gain more understanding and support in their work and family relationships, which is associated with higher SWB24. Therefore, the higher the level of PE in teachers, the higher their reported SWB.

Additionally, research has shown a significant positive correlation between MTE and PE 11. For example, Su 25 found that individuals who experience more MTE tend to report higher PE. MTE is often characterized by higher levels of novelty and uniqueness, allowing them to break the routine of daily teaching and research. This enables teachers to experience stronger feelings of interest and curiosity in non-work contexts, and PE such as joy and excitement accompany these experiences26. In addition, travel is often associated with higher levels of autonomy in decision-making and goal achievement, such as visiting desired destinations, completing long-awaited activities, or having unexpected experiences. These factors go together with mastery and accomplishment, along with positive emotional states25. MTE is often accompanied by higher-quality social interactions, such as shared experiences and support with family and friends, or friendly exchanges with locals. The sense of closeness and belonging from these interactions further associated with higher PE 15. PE, in turn, is positively associated with an individual’s SWB24. Current research primarily explores the link between MTE and PE, along with the link between PE and SWB. However, empirical studies examining whether PE mediates the link between MTE and SWB are still lacking. Therefore, this study aims to examine the mediating role of PE between MTE and SWB.

The moderating role of psychological resilience

PR refers to a person’s capacity to adapt positively in the face of adversity, trauma, and stress27. Based on the broaden-and-build theory13, PE can expand individuals’ attention and cognitive scope and build lasting personal resources, which in turn are associated with higher SWB. However, this process can be disrupted in high-pressure environments. Importantly, resilience theory suggests that resilient individuals not only experience PE but also capitalize on them more effectively28. That is, PR facilitates the translation of PE into lasting well-being improvements, a process referred to as the upward spiral of PE. The path from MTE to PE concerns whether meaningful positive events trigger PE, whereas the path from MTE to SWB concerns the direct relationship between positive events and well-being. Neither path centrally involves the efficiency of converting emotional experiences into enduring resources. In contrast, the relationship from PE to SWB explicitly addresses this conversion efficiency, a process where individual differences in resource accumulation should play the most prominent role. Resilience literature identifies PR as precisely such an individual difference28. As an important individual difference, PR may moderate the relationship between PE and SWB by shaping the duration of PE and the efficiency of resource building. Specifically, PR may moderate the link between PE and SWB in university teachers, reflecting its relationship with teachers’ short-term PE and overall SWB. The work pace of university teachers is typically high-intensity and full of uncertainty. Even when they occasionally experience PE such as joy, satisfaction, or excitement, these emotions can quickly be dissipated by teaching tasks and research pressures. However, teachers with higher PR possess stronger adaptability and recovery abilities. They can maintain psychological stability in changing environments, flexibly integrate their current emotional experiences, which is associated with higher levels of SWB and more positive life evaluations12,29. This reasoning aligns with the broaden-and-build theory: high PR helps individuals maintain the expanding and resource-building functions of PE under stress, supporting the development of long-term SWB. In contrast, individuals with lower PR are more easily disrupted by stress, leading to poorer persistence of PE and difficulty in accumulating these emotions in daily life. As a result, the connection between PE and SWB is weaker30. Therefore, based on the underlying logic of the broaden-and-build theory regarding the role of individual differences in the conversion of PE, PR may play a moderating role between PE and SWB, whereas alternative moderated paths (from MTE to PE and from MTE to SWB) were not hypothesized because they do not centrally involve the efficiency of resource accumulation from PE, a gap that resilience literature specifically identifies as being filled by PR.

Current research

Although existing research has explored the link between MTE and SWB, the specific mechanisms underlying this relationship have not been fully revealed. Particularly within the unique group of university teachers, who face significant teaching and research pressures, it is often difficult for them to achieve adequate relaxation and recovery. Therefore, tourism experiences may serve as an important avenue for them to experience lower stress and report higher well-being. However, there is currently no research that explicitly explores whether MTE is related to SWB through PE, and the examination of the moderating role of PR between PE and SWB is still insufficient. Therefore, based on the Broaden-and-Build Theory, this study constructs a moderated mediation model to investigate whether MTE is related to SWB through PE and to examine the moderating role of PR in this process. This study aims to reveal the underlying mechanisms of SWB among university teachers and provide a theoretical basis for psychological interventions targeting SWB. Methods

Participants and procedure

To improve the sample’s representativeness and minimize selection bias, this study used convenience sampling to survey university teachers from three universities in China between September and October 2025. Participants completed the questionnaire voluntarily. During the questionnaire design, a screening question was added to ensure participants confirmed whether they had engaged in travel activities in the past 12 months. All 716 collected questionnaires met the travel experience requirement, ensuring that participants had relevant background. Participants completed the online questionnaire via the Questionnaire Star platform (https://www.wjx.cn/), and were asked to review and sign the consent form prior to proceeding with the survey. The research process adhered to confidentiality principles, and the data was used solely for research analysis. Following the guidelines provided by Kline31, which suggest a minimum of 10 samples for each variable, this study included 53 items, requiring a minimum participant count of 530. An additional 20% was reserved to account for potential invalid or lost responses, bringing the target sample size to 636 participants (53 × 10 + 20%×53 × 10). A total of 716 questionnaires were collected, and 51 invalid responses (those with more than 20% missing answers or over 80% of items marked as “strongly agree/strongly disagree”) were excluded. After this, 665 fully answered questionnaires were collected, which corresponds to a 92.88% response percentage. In this sample, associate professors made up the largest proportion (40.5%) (Table 1).

Measurement tools

Memorable tourist experience

This study used the Memorable Tourist Experience Scale created by Kim, et al. 32 to evaluate the tourism experiences of university teachers. The scale comprises 24 items covering seven dimensions: Hedonism, Novelty, Local Culture, Refreshment, Meaningfulness, Involvement, and Knowledge. An example item is, “During this trip, I felt comfortable and relaxed.” A 5-point scale was used, where higher scores reflected higher MTE. Evidence has shown the scale to be valid and reliable among Chinese groups25. Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.958.

Psychological resilience

This study utilized the 10-item PR Scale adapted by Connor and Davidson33 to assess the level of PR in university teachers. An example item is, “I am able to cope with any unexpected situations during the trip.” The scale is unidimensional and employs a 5-point rating system, where higher scores signify greater PR. The scale has shown strong reliability and validity when applied to Chinese populations34. Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.927.

Positive emotions

This study used the Positive Emotions subscale from the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule to assess PE of university teachers. The scale consists of 10 items, such as “During this tourism experience, I felt very interested.” A 5-point scale was used, where higher ratings reflected greater PE. The scale has been tested for reliability and validity in Chinese populations35. Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.900.

Subjective well-being

This study used the Satisfaction with Life Scale (SWLS) developed by Campbell36 to assess the SWB of university teachers. The scale consists of 9 items, broken into two components: an 8‑item overall emotional index and a one‑item measure of life satisfaction, such as “I found this trip enjoyable.” A 7-point scale is used. The happiness index is derived by averaging the score of the overall emotional index (weight 1) and the life satisfaction dimension score (weight 1.1). The score range is from 2.1 (least happy) to 14.7 (most happy), with higher scores indicating higher SWB. Validation of this scale has been conducted on Chinese samples37, with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.895.

Data analysis

This research applied SPSS’s PROCESS 4.0 plugin to run data analyses. To examine the mediation effect, Model 4 was employed, while the moderation effect was tested using Model 14. The Bootstrap sampling technique (with 5000 resamples) was applied to estimate the confidence interval for the indirect effects. If the 95% confidence interval excludes zero, the indirect effect is deemed statistically significant. To ensure comparability of effects across predictors with different scales of measurement, this study uniformly reports standardized regression coefficients (β) and their 95% confidence intervals.

Common Method Bias (CMB)

The results of Harman’s single-factor test indicated that four factors with eigenvalues greater than 1 were extracted, with the first factor explaining 33.281% of the variance, which is below the 40% empirical threshold typically considered acceptable38. Additionally, this study further employed a latent variable approach by introducing a latent variable in the measurement model and linking it to all observed indicators. The model was then compared with the baseline model, which did not include the latent variable39. The findings indicated that the baseline model had χ² = 1196.728 (df = 858), while the model with the latent variable had χ² = 1196.884 (df = 857). The difference between the two was Δχ² = 0.156 (Δ df = 1), which was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). In summary, the results show no strong evidence of common method bias. However, since all core variables were self-reported at the same time point, the findings should still be interpreted with caution.

This study used Cronbach’s α and composite reliability (CR) to evaluate internal consistency. The Cronbach’s α coefficients for ranged from 0.895 to 0.958, and the CR ranged from 0.915 to 0.961, all surpassing the 0.70 benchmark. This indicates that the measurement tools have good reliability. To assess discriminant validity, the heterotrait-monotrait ratio (HTMT) was applied. All values (see Table 4) fell beneath the 0.90 critical limit, supporting good discriminability between constructs. In summary, the scales used in this study meet acceptable levels of reliability and validity.

Moderation analysis

This study tests how PR moderates the connection from PE to SWB. Excluding the interaction term, the R² for SWB was 0.488, indicating that the model could explain 48.8% of SWB’s total variation. Adding how PE and PR interact made the model’s R² climb to 0.584, with an increase in the explanatory power of 9.6%, suggesting that the moderating effect of PR enhanced the model’s explanatory ability. Further analysis revealed a significant positive link between PE and SWB (β = 0.369, t = 13.589, p < 0.001). Furthermore, the interaction term reached a significant level (β = 0.261, t = 10.352, p < 0.001), suggesting that PR significantly moderates the link between PE and SWB (Table 8).

Discussion

This study identified a significant positive link between MTE and SWB, supporting H1. This finding aligns with Aydin and ÖMÜRİŞ9, suggesting that individuals who have higher levels of MTE tend to report higher SWB. From the perspective of the broaden-and-build theory, MTE, as a novel and positive event, can expand teachers’ present thought–action repertoire, such as stimulating curiosity, exploratory behaviors, and social engagement. MTE is often accompanied by a sense of growth and accomplishment when facing new environments and challenges. Teachers, through problem-solving and collaboration with others in unfamiliar situations, tend to report higher self-confidence. This boost in self-confidence is accompanied by better professional performance and positive development in other life domains. Teachers with this boost tend to report higher overall SWB17. For this specific group of university teachers, research pressure, heavy teaching workloads, and high performance expectations place them under long-term psychological and physical demands. MTE provides an opportunity to temporarily step away from the academic environment, which may help ease everyday work stress and teaching burnout, while being associated with greater confidence and a heightened sense of control through cognitive and social activities. In addition, travel provides teachers with opportunities for in-depth interactions with family, friends, or colleagues, especially during shared experiences of enjoyable moments. In this context, social relationships are stronger, and teachers’ SWB is higher8. Therefore, MTE involves the accumulation of lasting well-being resources derived from temporarily positive experiences through the broaden-and-build process, and this accumulation is associated with higher SWB among university teachers.

This study demonstrated that PE mediate the link between MTE and SWB, supporting H2, 3, and 4. This finding is in line with Lu, et al. 15, indicating that the positive connection between memorable travel experiences and SWB can be reflected through the mediating role of PE 24. As a unique life experience, travel is often accompanied by new discoveries, relaxation, and pleasurable emotional experiences, which can bring PE in teachers, such as excitement, satisfaction, and joy11. For university teachers, the novelty and enjoyment from travel experiences are not only accompanied by PE but also provide a psychological buffer when stepping away from a high-pressure academic environment, which helps them better cope with research and teaching demands upon returning to work. PE, which is associated with teachers’ emotional states, relate to a more optimistic and positive psychological state when returning to their daily work and life after the trip26. PE not only helps individuals experience higher SWB in the short term but also shows a positive relationship with long-term SWB through individuals’ positive evaluations of life and emotional engagement10. According to the broaden-and-build theory, PE accompanies broader attention and thinking in teachers, more favorable life evaluations and emotional engagement, and stronger SWB, as well as less psychological stress in daily work. Additionally, PE makes it easier for teachers to perceive the positive aspects of life, reducing the interference of negative emotions, which is associated with higher SWB21. Therefore, PE plays an important mediating role between MTE and the SWB of university teachers.

This study demonstrated PR’s moderating role in how PE relates to SWB, lending support to H5. This finding aligns with Zhu, et al. 12, showing that the presence of PR accompanies a stronger positive relationship between PE and SWB. From the perspective of the broaden-and-build theory, teachers with higher PR can better utilize the cognitive and social resources activated by PE, converting the enjoyment from travel experiences into lasting psychological resources. PR helps teachers better manage and regulate PE. Even when facing difficulties in their daily work, teachers with higher PR show a positive relationship between the PE from their travel experiences and their SWB, while experiencing lower interference from negative stress30. In addition, for this high-pressure group of university teachers, PR helps alleviate the stress from research and teaching tasks, enabling them to maintain PE and SWB12. In other words, PR is associated with an amplified broaden-and-build process: individuals with higher resilience can better leverage the PE generated from travel experiences, converting them into lasting personal resources, such as optimism, which is associated with higher overall SWB. In this way, PR is positively associated with teachers’ positive evaluations of life, with greater optimism and enthusiasm when facing challenges, and higher overall SWB29. Therefore, PR plays an important moderating role in this dynamic process, accompanying the positive interplay between PE and SWB, while alleviating the unique research and teaching pressures of university teachers. This moderating effect aligns theoretically with the core propositions of the broaden-and-build theory.

Significance and limitations of the study

Theoretical significance

This study holds theoretical significance in three key areas. Firstly, this study extends the application of the Broaden-and-Build Theory to the specific context of tourism experiences among university teachers, a professional group with unique work stress and recovery needs. This resonates with the core concept of the theory, which suggests that specific positive situations more easily trigger PE and bring lasting psychological benefits. As a result, it provides a more theoretically supported explanatory framework for understanding the relationship between tourism and well-being within this specific population. Secondly, this study investigates the mediating role of PE, exploring how tourism experiences are linked to SWB through PE. The results indicate that PE plays a crucial role in the process, reflecting the relationship between tourism experiences and SWB. This offers a valuable perspective on understanding the mechanisms behind the formation of SWB. Finally, this study incorporates PR as a moderating variable, addressing the Broaden-and-Build Theory’s notion of individual differences in whether expansion can translate into building resources. It reveals the differences in emotional maintenance and reflects the relationship between experiences and well-being across individuals with different levels of PR. This clarifies the conditions under which MTE are associated with SWB. Overall, this study, through a unified theoretical framework, combines situational triggers, emotional mechanisms, and individual differences, providing a more detailed theoretical pathway to explain the link between MTE and SWB. It also lays a theoretical basis for subsequent studies at the intersection of tourism and positive psychology.

Practical significance

The practical value of this study is in providing an actionable framework for understanding the well-being of university teachers, based on the Broaden-and-Build Theory.

Firstly, the research findings indicate that MTE is closely related to higher SWB. Based on this, universities may consider providing high-quality leisure and travel activities to support teachers in experiencing more positive psychological outcomes. Specific measures include implementing paid leave, optimizing wellness retreats and study trips, upgrading team-building programs at the university and departmental levels, and creating more restorative environments for teachers to stimulate positive psychological returns, rather than merely providing general benefits.

Secondly, PE plays a key role in this process, indicating that teachers’ well-being is not solely associated with long-term, high-load training programs but is also linked to participation in activities and services that provide immediate emotional uplift. In travel or leisure programs, emphasis can be placed on novel exploration, immersive participation, interaction with the environment, and ritualistic experiences with memorable and shareable value. These elements can stimulate PE, reflecting a positive relationship with life evaluations, which is further associated with higher perceived cost-benefit ratios of the activities.

Furthermore, PR as a moderating variable indicates that the benefits of the same travel experience may vary among different teachers. Therefore, universities should apply a differentiated approach when designing programs and allocating resources. For teachers with lower PR or higher stress levels, a gentler, lower-burden activity pace and a more supportive environment should be provided, along with short, feasible interventions reflecting associations with PR (e.g., recovery training, mindfulness practices, emotional awareness, and reflection). These measures can strengthen the ability to maintain and integrate PE. For teachers with higher PR, more challenging and growth-oriented deep experiences can be offered to promote resource gain.

Limitations and future research directions

This study acknowledges certain limitations, which help to define the scope of the conclusions and provide directions for future research. Firstly, this study used cross-sectional self-reported data, and the relationships between variables primarily reflect associative patterns, without fully capturing dynamic changes over time. Researchers could apply longitudinal follow-up in future investigations or experience sampling methods to measure the changes in PE and well-being multiple times before, during, and after the trip. This would align more closely with the process characteristics of emotional expansion and resource accumulation as outlined in the Broaden-and-Build Theory. Secondly, this study’s main measures come largely from what participants reported themselves, leaving them open to effects from CMB as well as recall bias, especially since the memorable attribute involves reconstructing memories. Future research could integrate multiple data sources and more objective process indicators, such as evaluations from peers or family members, behavioral records (e.g., photo sharing, diary entries), and physiological or sleep recovery indicators. Additionally, using time intervals or multiple measurement methods could help reduce method bias. Thirdly, the sample for this study came from university teachers at three universities in China and was collected using convenience sampling. Participants completed the questionnaire voluntarily. Given this sampling strategy, the findings are primarily reflective of the unique cultural and structural setting within China’s university system, particularly within the selected institutions. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted with caution when generalizing to other universities, professional groups, or cultural and educational systems. Future research should prioritize cross-cultural validation by replicating this study in diverse national, institutional, and cultural settings. Comparative studies involving different professional groups, types of travel, and sociocultural backgrounds are also recommended to further examine the model’s stability and how widely it can be applied.

Conclusion

Based on the Broaden-and-Build Theory, this study sought to explore the link between MTE and SWB among university teachers, investigating the mediating effect of PE and the moderating role of PR. The research findings reveal a significant positive link between MTE and SWB, with PE playing a mediating role. This suggests that MTE is likely to be accompanied by higher levels of PE, which is associated with greater SWB. Additionally, PR moderates the key pathways, indicating that teachers with higher PR are more likely to maintain and integrate the PE brought by MTE, thereby exhibiting stronger SWB. Theoretically, this study, based on the Broaden-and-Build Theory, reflects the relationships among situational triggers, emotional expansion, and well-being, while highlighting PR as an individual characteristic associated with variations in these relationships. Practically, the study suggests that universities optimize leisure and wellness programs within their teacher care systems, reflecting associations between the novelty and immersion of experiences and higher PE, while providing differentiated support based on teachers’ levels of PR. Nonetheless, it is crucial to recognize that the information came from self-reported responses collected at just one moment in time, so the conclusions are more suitable as an explanation of associative patterns. Additionally, the sample and context may constrain the broader applicability of the findings. Future research could incorporate longitudinal or experience sampling designs, combined with multi-source data, to further test the robustness and applicability of the model across different groups and types of travel.
