The internalization of three types of carbon nanoparticles by HeLa cells in vitro
Authors: Lan Sun, Fei Peng, Bao-Quan Zhao, Hao Zhang, Yuan Luo, Yong-An Wang, Ying-Ge Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionWe have found activated carbon nanoparticles (ACNP) can be used as carriers of nanodrug delivery systems to treat cervical cancer. But how ACNP to enter into the HeLa cell, how to distribute, translocate and, metabolize and develop therapeutic effects has been unknown. In this paper, we invested the action of ACNP on HaLa cells in comparison with single walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNT) and quantum dots (QDs) in vitro.MethodsThe effects of the nanoparticles on the HeLa cells were observed by tetrazolium bromide reduction (MTT) assay, lactate dehydrogenase leakage determination (LDH), flow cytometry, apoptotic rate, DNA comet assay. The morphological influences of nanoparticles on HeLa cells were observed with inverted microscope and atomic force microscopy (AFM); The mechanism of across cell membrane translocation of ACNP, SWCNT and QDs was investigated by treating the cells with endocytosis inhibitors and 4 °C low temperature.ResultsThe three nanoparticles can inhibit the proliferation of HeLa cells in time- and concentration-dependent manners. Under the light microscope, the volume of HeLa cells treated by ACNP and SWCNT became smaller than that of control group. ACNP and SWCNT can induce HeLa cells shrinkage, weaken cell adhesion and increase the number of free cells in culture media. Under AFM, ACNP caused ill-defined cell membranes and membrane rupture; SWCNT caused cell shrinkage, reduced adhesion properties and "collapse-like" structure; QDs induced cell surface roughness and cell membrane folds. There are many irregular depressions in the cells. Flow cytometry showed that the three nanoparticles induced apoptosis of Hela cells. Electrophoresis showed the three nanoparticles induced DNA damage. Transmission electron microscopy(TEM) revealed that ACNP can pass through the cell membrane into the cytoplasm and further into the nucleus, causing disappearance of surface microvilli, mitochondrial swelling and nuclear shrinkage; SWCNT can enter into HeLa cells in the medium without inhibitors under room temperature, inducing cell and nuclear shrinkage, SWCNT was not found in the HeLa cells treated by inhibitors and low temperature; QDs was found accumulated in the cell membranes and can also cause swelling of mitochondria and increase cytoplasmic vacuoles.ConclusionThe three kinds of nanoparticles can inhibit the growth and cause apoptosis of HeLa cells; the mechanism may be nuclear DNA damage and increased membrane permeability; The internalization of SWCNT by HeLa cells is energy dependent while the internalization of ACNP is independent of energy. The DNA damage effects of ACNP may be realized through two ways, one of which is to injure cell membranes and the other is to act on DNA directly. QDs damage DNA mainly through the action of cell membranes.Keywords: Activated carbon nanoparticles, Single walled carbon nanotubes, Quantum dots, Cytotoxicity, Apoptosis, DNA damage, HeLa cells

Introduction

The revolution of nanotechnology has led to a wide variety of innovative nanomaterials and applications, especially in drug delivery system. The ultra small size and high ratio of surface area to volume of nanoparticles give them many interesting features differ considerably from the bulk material of the same mass, such as superior mechanical performance, the possibility of surface functionalization, large surface activity and tunable porosity, compared to their bulk materials [1]. These outstanding features explain why nanomaterials are the candidates in the drug delivery systems, biosensors, chemical sensors and wound dressings [2]. However, these outstanding features have also raised the concerns for potential exposure of the general population and adverse health effects. Because both of the beneficial effects and toxicity of nanoparticales were related to their size and surface properties [3–10].

Activated Carbon nanoparticles (ACNP) is a matter of porous particles with a broad absorption spectrum and has been widely used in the field of tumor localization and lymph node tracing [11–13]. In our preliminary work, we found that ACNP with spherical morphology, relatively smooth surfaces, stronger absorption, had less toxic and local irritate than activated carbon microparticles (ACMP) in vivo [14]. Therefore, there is a need for further, more in-depth research and analyses the biological effectivity of ACNP.Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) are a type of synthetic nanomaterials with distinct hollow and cylindrical structure. Due to their nanometric size and high drug loading efficiency, CNTs have been used to delivery drugs or other small therapeutic molecules to target sites [15, 16]. For example, drugs [17], proteins [18], RNA [19, 20] and DNA [21] can be internalized by mammalian cells through coating onto CNTs. Despite all of their proven potentials as drug carriers, CNTs can have the intrinsic toxicity and cause genotoxity in biological systems [22].Carbon quantum dots(QDs) are said to be zero-dimensional [23]. The features of QDs include their tuneable size, stable photoluminescence, large surface-to-volume ratio, and potential biocompatibility. Their emission spectra are closely related to their diameter. This characteristic of QDs allows for easy manipulation of QDs’ size to achieve the desired fluorescence color. QDs’ emission wavelength can be tuned from visible to infrared wavelengths, which could be useful for real-time tracking detection and in vivo imaging as fluorescence label [24]. When QDs invade the body, they may deposit in the liver, kidney and other metabolic organs to cause damage to it, and the free heavy metal ions may move around in the body, cause the body to produce an immune response or other toxicity risks [25, 26]. Although the biological effects of the three carbon nanoparticles have been widely investigated [27], there has been little information about how they to enter, distribute and translocate in cells. To address these issues, we used HeLa cells to investigate the internalization of the three nanoparticles by cells in vitro.

Materials and methods

Materials

Activated carbon microparticles (ACMP) were purchased from Shanghai Hainuo Carbon Industry Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). SWCNT were provided by Nachen Science and Technology Company (Beijing, China). QDS were provided by Henan University (Henan, China) which was a kind of CdSe/ZnS QDs. Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), penicillin and streptomycin were purchased from North China Pharmaceutical (Hebei, China). Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM), Fetal bovine serum (FBS), TrypLE and EDTA were purchased from Gibco (Billings, MT, USA). Cytochalasin B, Hoechst 33,342 and EB were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Apoptosis kit was purchased from Baosai Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Low melting point agarose (LMA) and normal melting point agarose (NMA) were obtained from Beijing Solarbio Sciences & Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Tris was obtained from ANGUS Chemical Company (Buffalo Grove, Illinis, USA). Triton X-100, and Sodium dodecyl sarcosinate were obtained from China National Pharmaceutical Group Corporation (Beijing, China). Human cervical cancer HeLa cell line was obtained from Institute of Materia Medica, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College (Beijing, China).

Preparation of activated carbon nanoparticles (ACNP)

Our lab adopts sedimentation method to get ACNP with uniform diameters and form [28]. Activated carbon microparticles (ACMP) were reground at 600 r/min for 60 h by planetary micro mill (Fritsch Pulverisette 7 premium line, Germany) to obtain a mixture of activated carbon particle with various diameters. These mixtures were added in triple distilled water and stirred at a concentration of 150 g/100L for 7 days. The larger particles had a larger sedimentation velocity because of their larger sedimentation coefficient so that the particles participated in an order of from larger to smaller. After 7 days, the suspension in the top 10 cm was taken out and put into 15 mL glass ampoules, which was placed in a − 80℃ refrigerator for freeze drying. After 24 h freeze-drying ACNP was obtained.

Characterization of nanoparticles

For characterization, the three types of nanoparticles were resuspended in triply-distilled water again, which were dropped onto the cupper mesh for transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Hitachi H7650, Hitachi Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) observation, dropped onto fresh mica surface for atomic force microscopy (AFM, JPK Nanowizard I, Germany) observation. AFM images were carried out at off-line with JPKSPM data processing 4.2.62 analysis software. The hydrodynamic diameter and Zeta potential of ACNP and QDs were examined through dynamic light scattering (DLS) in a Zetasizer Nano ZS (Malvern, UK).

The observation of the effects of nanoparticles on human cervical cancer cell line HeLa

Human cervical cancer cell line HeLa was cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated FBS and antibiotics (penicillin 100 U/mL and streptomycin 100 mg/mL). Cells were cultured under the condition of 5% CO2 at 37℃.

The ultrastructure changes of HeLa cells after treating with the three types of nanoparticles were investigated by AFM. HeLa cells were cultured on coverslips and treated with ACNP (0.4 mg/mL), SWCNT (12 μg/mL) and QDs (1.0 × 10–7 mmol/L) for 24 h. Then cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde in PBS for 10 min, washed once with PBS and twice with triple distilled water, air dried for 1 h, and subjected to AFM imaging. AFM imaging was performed in the tapping mode at a scanning rate 0.5 Hz in the air. The cantilevers had a spring constant of 20–80 N/m provided by the manufacturer. To minimize the damage of the AFM tip to the morphology and structures of the cells, the force and scanning frequency were initially adjusted to as small as possible[29]. AFM images were carried out at off-line with JPKSPM data processing 4.2.62 analysis software.

MTT assay

After the three types of nanoparticles were subjected to high-pressure sterilization, different concentrations of suspension solutions were prepared using DMEM culture medium. Before each exposure, the suspensions were ultrasonically dispersed and homogenized.

HeLa cells were seeded into 96-well plates at 5000 cells/well, respectively, and 24 h later, add the nanoparticle suspension prepared in DMEM culture medium (20 μL /well). The concentration of ACNP was 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 or 1.6 mg/mL. The concentration of SWCNT was 1.5, 3.0, 6.0, 12.0, 24.0 or 48.0 μg/mL. The concentration of QDs was 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0 or 8.0 × 10–7 mmol/L. The control group was given 20 μL of culture medium per well, and six replicate wells were set up for each group. and then grew at 37℃ in the presence of 5% CO2 for 24 h, 48 h and 72 h. The survival rate of cells was assessed by the MTT assay as reported previously [30].

Lactate dehydrogenaes (LDH) assay

HeLa cells were seeded into 6-well plates at 4 × 105 cells/well, and allowed to attach overnight. Cells were exposed to ACNP, SWCNT or QDs at different concentrations for 24, 48 and 72 h, respectively. The concentration of ACNP was 0.1, 0.4 or 1.6 mg/mL. The concentration of SWCNT was 3.0, 12 or 48 μg/mL. The concentration of QDs was 0.25, 1.0 or 4.0 × 10–7 mmol/L. At different time points, the culture media were drawn off respectively and centrifuged at 10,000 g for 10 min at 4℃. The supernatant was collected to test the lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) which the HeLa cells released.

Apoptosis effect on HeLa cells

To detect the apoptosis of various nanoparticles on the HeLa cells, HeLa cells were seeded into 50 mL culture bottles, respectively, then grown at 37℃ in the presence of 5% CO2 for 24 h. The cells were then exposed to ACNP (0.4 mg/mL), SWCNT (12 μg/mL) and DQs (1.0 × 10–7 mmol/L), respectively for 24 h or 48 h. The blank culture medium was used as a blank control. After the 24 h or 48 h treatment, the cells were harvested, washed and suspended at a density of 1 × 106 cells/mL in 200 μL Binding buffer or 10 μL AnnexinV-FITC, respectively, at 4℃ away from light for 15 min. The sample was resuspended in 500 mL of Binding buffer and then added with 50 μL PI before assayed 5 min, and assayed on a FACSCalibur (BD Biosciences, USA).

Imaging analysis

For visualization of DNA damage, observations were made of EB-stained DNA at 400 × magnification using a fluorescence microscope. And 200 randomly selected per sample were analyzed by CaspLab (CASP) image analysis software. The tail moment (TM), tail DNA content (%) and Olive tail moment (OTM) of the comet images were adopted as the evaluation indicators for DAN damage degree [31].

Transmission electron microscopy(TEM) analysis

To research the way of nanoparticles getting into cells, HeLa cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 4 × 105 cells/well, and allowed to attach overnight. The experiment was divided into three groups: normal temperature group, cytochalasin B (CB) group and low temperature (4℃) group. Normal temperature group cells were exposed to ACNP, SWCNT or QDs at normal temperature (37℃) for 24 h. CB group cells were exposed to CB for 12 h first, and then were cultured with ACNP, SWCNT or QDs at 37℃ for 24 h. Low temperature group cells were cultured at low temperature treatment (4℃) for 1 h firstly, and then cells were exposed to ACNP, SWCNT or QDs at 4℃ for 24 h. The concentration of ACNP was 0.4 mg/mL. The concentration of SWCNT was 12 μg/mL. The concentration of QDs was 1.0 × 10–7 mmol/L. At different time points, the culture media were drawn off and cells were washed with PBS three times. Then the cells were collected respectively and investigated by TEM.

Laser confocal scanning microscopy (LCSM) analysis

HeLa cells were cultured in glass-based dishes and were incubated for 24 h to allow attachment. QDs group cells were incubated with QDs (1.0 × 10–7 mmol/L)for 2 h、12 h and 24 h at 37℃. At different time points, the culture media were drawn off and cells were washed with PBS three times. Then cells were stained with Hoechst 33,342 (away from light). After 15 min, the cells were washed with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (v/v). Finally, the cells were washed with deionized water and dry with compressed air. Then all the cells were examined by confocal microscope (Leica SP2, Heidelberg, Germany).

Statistics analysis

Data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to determine significance among groups, after which post hoc tests with the Bonferroni correction were used for comparison between individual groups. A value of P < 0.05 was considered to be significant. All statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 16.0 software.

Results

Characterization of nanoparticles

The AFM and TEM images show that the ACNP prepared by the ball-milling-precipitation method has regular morphology, with uniform particle size, smoother surface and approximate globular shape (Fig. 1A). The average particle size of ACNP was 80 ± 10 nm as measured by laser particle size analyzer. The Zeta potential was determined by dynamic light scattering (DLS) in a Zetasizer Nano ZS (Malvern, UK). The zeta potential measurements of ACNP were − 18.7 ± 0.7 mV.

(C) The TEM, fluorescence spectra and XRD images of QDs (Fig. 1C was provided by Henan University (Henan, China). Note: AFM images were carried out at off-line with JPKSPM data processing 4.2.62 analysis software

The average length of SWCNT was 800 ± 200 nm, its diameter was 3 ~ 6 nm (Fig. 1B). The zeta potential measurements of SWCNT were -13.4 ± 7.7 mV.

The average particle size of QDS was 82.63 ± 33.57 nm. The zeta potential measurements of QDs were − 39.2 mV ± 8.26 mV. The XRD patterns of QDs was shown in Fig. 1C. Three broad diffraction peaks were observed in the XRD patterns. These peaks correspond to the (111), (220), and (311) crystal planes of the cubic crystal system, indicating that the QDs have the sphalerite phase. These CdSe/ZnS QDs’s excitation wavelength is 217 nm, emission wavelength is 617 nm as measured by fluorescence spectrum, and it shows very pure emitting colors under 365 nm UV lamp irradiation in the insets on the photos (Fig. 1C).

The morphological effects of nanoparticles on culture Hela cells

Figure 2 shows the AFM scanning image of HeLa cells. Under AFM, the control HeLa cells presented spindle-shape. The surface of the cell body was bumpy, with lots of filamentous pseudopodia in peripheral, which may be the shadow skeleton. In ACNP group, HeLa cells were rough. The rough membranes were covered with gathered or dispersed particle materials. According to the particle size and morphology, it was inferred that these particles are ACNP. In SWCNT group, HeLa cells were flat. And the cell bodies became some kind of concave, of which the convex edge of disk was just like “crater”. The figure shows the SWCNT treated cells had more serious damage than those treated with ACNP and QDs. In QDs group, HeLa cells also presented spindle-shape but the filamentous pseudopodia in peripheral became shorter and the height of cell body was become lower. The member of HeLa cells had some little holes.

The effects of nanoparticles on apoptosis of HeLa cells

As shown in Fig. 4A, the three nanoparticles induced apoptosis of HeLa cells as quantitatively measured in annexin V-PI staining method. The results showed that ACNP-, SWCNT- and QDs-induced apoptosis rates were 11.01%, 65.42% and 4.05% and the necrosis rates were 65.56%, 18.35% and 2.43% respectively at 24 h after treatment (Fig. 4). The apoptosis rates were 14.12%, 78.71% and 94.67% and the necrosis rate were 64.04%, 11.85% and 1.19% respectively at 48 h after treatment (Fig. 4C). HeLa cells showed no obvious apoptosis exposed to QDs for 24 h, while had obvious apoptosis at 48 h. These results were consistent with the results of MTT assay.

The cross-membrane translocation of nanoparticles into HeLa cells and the impact of them on subcellular structure

Under TEM, black nanoparticles could be found in the cytoplasm and nucleus of HeLa cells treated with ACNP for 24 h at room temperature (Fig. 7 A). After 48 h, cytoplasmic cavitation and mitochondria swelling can be seen except for the black particles, and the number of lysosomal increased. After 72 h, the nuclear of HeLa cells begin to shrink and burst with further increased lysosomes and cell collapse (Fig. 7 A). HeLa cells collapsed. Less black particles could be found in the HeLa cells treated with ACNP at 4℃ (Fig. 7 B). These results indicated that endocytosis of ACNP is energy-dependent. CB had no effect on endocytosis of ACNP in HeLa cells. This may be due to the low melting point of the lipid molecules that make up the cell membrane, which exhibit a degree of fluidity at 37℃, allowing a small number of nanoparticles to cross the cell membrane without rupture.

There were some similar rod materials in the cytoplasm of HeLa cells after treated with SWCNT for 48 h. At the same time, the nuclear started to shrink and the organelles became less. At 72 h, the cytoplasm of HeLa cells became more enrichment and the apoptosis body arisen (Fig. 7A). Treating with CB at 37℃, the small rod materials could not be found in HeLa cells. And the mitochondria ridge of HeLa cells became less or disappear. Treating at 4℃, SWCNT could not into the HeLa cells and the HeLa cells had no obvious abnormal compared with control (Fig. 7B). These results indicated that SWCNT could not enter into HeLa cells without energy. CB could hold back the SWCNT into cells. The way of SWCNT entering into HeLa cells might be an energy-dependent and actin-dependent process.

Under TEM (Fig. 7A and B), lots of nanoparticles adhere onto the QDs-treated HeLa cell membranes under room temperature and no QDs were seen in the internal of cells. These results indicated that QDs could not enter into HeLa cells at any conditions. However, the HeLa cells treated with QDs were damaged, such as the discontinuous cell membranes, mitochondrial swelling and nuclear pyknosis of. Figure 7C represents the confocal microscope image of HeLa cells treated by QDs. The nucleuses were dyed blue fluorescence by Hoechst 33,342. The red fluorescence emitted by QDs excited by light of at 488 nm uniformly distributed around HeLa cell treated with QDs for 12 h, indicating that the cell membranes were complete. Treated with QDs for 24 h, the distribution of the red fluorescence became discontinuity, indicating that the cell membranes were damaged. Even after 24 h QDs treatment, there was no red fluorescence in cytoplasm and nucleus of HeLa cells. These results indicated that QDs could accumulate in the cell membrane, but can also cause swelling of mitochondria and increased cytoplasmic vacuoles.

Discussion

In this study, the effects of three widely used carbon nanoparticles, ACNP, SWCNT and QDs, on HeLa cells and the mechanism behind these effects were primarily explored. Of these three materials, ACNP and QDs are nanoscale in three dimensions with different sizes while SWCNT is one dimensional nanoparticle with different chemical structures from ACNP and QDs. As results demonstrated, the three nanoparticles have different biological effects on Hela cells. These results indicate the biological effects of nanoparticles are related not only to their chemical composition but also to their sizes, morphologies and chemical structures and suggest the sizes, morphologies and structure must be taken into consideration when nanoparticles are used in biomedicine. Like small molecules, the biological effects of nanoparticles are determined not only by their action nature but also by action time. As our results demonstrated, the three nanoparticles have no significant effects on Hela cells within 12 ~ 24 h. However, at 48 and 72 h, the nanoparticle treatment produced significant biological effects on HeLa cells including the changes in cell morphology as revealed by optical microscope and AFM, the changes in subcellular structure as revealed by TEM, and the changes in biochemistry as revealed by MTT, LDH and comet assay. The biological effects of the three nanoparticles on HeLa cells were investigated in morphological and biochemical methods. Results demonstrated that the morphological changes were accompanied by biochemical changes and suggest that the biological changes are the reason for morphological changes while the morphological changes are the results of biological changes. All the three nanoparticles produced DNA damage, which is the fundamental mechanism for their toxic effects on HeLa cells. There are several mechanisms behind the biological effects of the three nanoparticles on HeLa cells. ACNP and SWCNT can enter into cells and nucleuses, suggesting it is possible to damage DNA through direct action on DNA. QDs accumulated in the membranes but few are found in cell, suggesting QDs may activate the signaling pathway of DNA damage through the action on the relative membrane proteins. No matter entering cells through cross-membrane translocation or accumulating in cell membranes, the interactions between nanoparticles and membranes are inevitable so that the structural and permeability changes of membranes may be other mechanisms behind the biological effects of nanoparticles on HeLa cells. The broken membranes and the increased membrane permeability led to the leaking out of the intracellular enzymes as revealed by LDH assay. DNA damage and leaking out of intracellular enzymes may commonly or separately results in the cell prolife changes as revealed by MTT assay and ultrastructural changes as observed by TEM and cell morphological changes as seen under optic microscope and AFM. Summarily, ACNP and SWCNT may cause the cell prolife changes through actions on cell membranes and DNA while QDs cause the cell prolife changes mainly through the action on membranes that leads to the activation of cell apoptosis pathway.

In morphological studies, three microscopes, optic microscope, TEM, AFM and LCSM were used to observe the cell body, ultrastructure, membrane changes and the subcellular distribution of nanoparticles in HeLa cells. AFM is currently the only microscope that allows images of native membranes at subcellular resolution [32], useful for the collection of cellular surface information such as cellular topography, ultra structure, and morphological changes at the subcellular level. Using AFM, we found the three nanoparticles can induce the decrease, even disappearance of cell pseudopodia (Fig. 2). Pseudopodia is structural basis of intercellular communication as well as the important structural embodies of cell integrity. The decrease or disappearance of pseudopodia is an important parameter of cell damage. Using AFM in combination with TEM, we obtained the integral information about the morphological effects of the three nanoparticles on HeLa cells.

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) is a kind of important enzymes which maintain the normal function of cells [33–36]. When ACNP and SWCNT enter cells through cross-membrane translocation and QDs accumulates in membranes, it is possible to break the membrane integrity, causing the changes of membrane permeability, which may be the first step for the three nanoparticles to damage cells. As LDH assay demonstrated, ACNP, SWCNT and QDs increased LDH leaking-out, indicating the three nanoparticles may break the membrane integrity and increase the permeability of membranes as the first possible mechanism for their biological effects on HeLa cells. For QDs, LDH leaking-out has more important significance because it accumulated in membranes rather than entered cells as revealed by LCSM.

MTT is an indirect biochemical method to detect cellular activity. In addition to MTT, flow cytometry (FCM) was used to further determine the extent of the cell damage. FCM is one of the most objective and internationally recognized methods for apoptosis. Annexin V in combination with PI can be used to distinguish apoptotic cells from dead cells. FCM two -parameter analysis was used to distinguish the different types of cells and calculate the corresponding percentages. In this study, HeLa cells were treated by these three types of nanoparticles for 24 h and 48 h, combined with the results of LDH (Table 2) and FCM results (Fig. 4), it was found that these three types of nanoparticles firstly promote the loss of membrane integrity and increase of permeability, and then ACNP mainly stimulated cell death, while SWCNT and QDs mainly induced apoptosis.

It was possible that the three nanoparticles act differently on HeLa cells. ACNP had strong adhesion to HeLa cells. It could be seen that almost all ACNP adhere to the cell membranes under the light microscope, while there were few free ACNP in the culture medium. Because the cell membranes is the gateway for the exchange of materials between the cell and the outside world, a large amount of ACNP covered on the cell membrane may affect the normal physiological function of the cell and lead to cell death. SWCNT, due to its similar “needle” shape, is easy to insert into the cell membrane and enter the cell interior, causing cytotoxicity and resulting in non-programmed death. QDs, which cannot enter cells, may be toxic to cells due to interaction between them and cell membranes.

We used comet assay to determine the DNA strand breaks of HeLa cells, which was exerted by the three nanoparticles. The degree of DNA damage in single cells could be determined by measuring the optical density or migration length of the DNA migration part, so as to determine the relationship between the dose of the tested substance and the DNA damage effect [37–39]. In our studies, the three nanoparticles all induced a statistically significant effects in concentration- and time-dependent manners (Fig. 7 Table 3). ACNP, a kind of porous carbon, has strong adsorption and could adsorb onto nucleic acid molecules or/and other relative proteins, resulting in DNA damage. SWCNT is a special kind of long tubular fullerene. There are a lot of active sites on the surface, which could bind easily with nucleic acid molecules, so as to damage DNA [40, 41]. The core of QDs was to be heavy metal usually. And these metal belt positively charged, which could attack the negatively charged DNA chain, causing DNA rupture [42–45]. QDs is nanoscale in three dimensions and cannot enter HeLa cells and can only accumulate in the membranes as revealed by LCSM, suggesting the DNA damage effects of QDs are secondary to their action on cell membranes. The possible mechanisms for QDs’s DNA damage may be through their action on membranes that broke the membrane integrity and increase the membrane permeability or/and activated the DNA damage pathway. CASP analysis software with high precision and easy operation was selected for comet assay. The internationally recognized evaluation indexes such as Tail Moment, Tail DNA content (Tail DNA%) and Olive Tail Moment were used in the result analysis, which could accurately and comprehensively reflect the toxicity of nanoparticles to HeLa cell DNA.

Both ACNP and SWCNT can enter cells and cause damage to cells as the results demonstrated. But how do they enter cells? The way of entering into cells for the two nanoparticles belongs two kinds of way that were energy-dependent and energy-independent respectively. Cytochalasin B (Cytochalasin B, CB), a cyto-permeable mycotoxin, is a known inhibitor of actin polymerization. CB is able to inhibit the formation of actin microfilaments, resulting in direct effects in cellular movement and phagocytosis functionality [46]. CB has been used as a phagocytosis inhibitor to study this cellular function [47]. Due to their shape, SWCNT can penetrate into cells through two different ways: the endocytic pathway or passive diffusion and translocate into different subcellular compartments. In the endocytic pathway, SWCNT are internalized in endoxomes that merge with lysosomes to form endolysosomes. This process is energy-dependent. In the Fig. 7A green arrow indicated, SWCNT penetrate through phagocytosis, and they accumulate in the lysosomal compartment. Our results demonstrated that SWCNT could be uptake primarily by energy-dependent endocytosis. CB and low temperature (4℃) significantly inhibited SWCNT uptake because endocytosis processes including phagocytosis, pinocytosis, caveolae-mediated, and clathrin-mediated endocytosis are all energy-dependent [48]. ACNP entering into cells may be by energy-independent endocytosis because both CB and low temperature had no effects on their cross-membrane translocation as the experiment results demonstrated.

Conclusion

ACNP, SWCNT and QDs can inhibit the proliferation and induce apoptosis of HeLa cells, as well as they could damage DNA of cells. The possible mechanism behind nanoparticles to damage DNA of HeLa cells may be secondary to their effects on cell membranes or direct action on DNA or the sum of their actions on cell membranes and DNA. These results suggest the biological safety of nanoparticles on Hela cells is related to their sizes and morphology as well as their chemical natures, all of which should be taken into consideration when they are used in biomedicine.
