Design and temperature assessment of analog/RF and linearity parameters on dual material gate junctionless FinFET at 7 nm technology node for nano scale applications
Authors: Rambabu Kusuma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe dual-material gate junctionless FinFET (DMG JLFinFET) has emerged as a promising device in semiconductor technology due to its enhanced capability to suppress short-channel effects (SCEs). This work presents a comprehensive study on how temperature variations affect the electrical behavior of DMG JLFinFETs, particularly for devices scaled to the 7 nm technology node. Essential DC parameters—such as subthreshold swing (SS), drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL), and the ION/IOFF ratio—were evaluated over a temperature span of 200 to 400 K. In addition, the study examines how temperature impacts key analog and radio frequency (RF) parameters, including gate-to-drain capacitance (Cgd), gate-to-source capacitance (Cgs), total gate capacitance (Cgg), transconductance (gm), cutoff frequency (ft), transconductance generation factor (TGF), intrinsic delay (τ), gain-bandwidth product (GBW), and transconductance frequency product (TFP). The investigation also addresses how thermal conditions influence linearity performance, focusing on metrics such as second-order transconductance (gm2), third-order transconductance (gm3), voltage intercept points (VIP2, VIP3), third-order input intercept point (IIP3), and the 1 dB compression point. The findings of this investigation demonstrate that the DMG JLFinFET exhibits promising electrical, analog, and RF performance characteristics, making it a viable candidate for advanced communication applications.Keywords: Dual material gate (DMG), JLFinFET, ION, IOFF, SS, DIBLSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

At present scenario all semiconductor devices will be on the nanoscale According to the International Technology Roadmap for Semiconductors (ITRS)1. As the dimensions of MOSFET transistors decrease to below nano-meters, the occurrence of short channel effects (SCEs) such as channel length modulation, threshold voltage, hot carrier effects, sub-threshold swing reduction, and velocity saturation intensifies2. Electronic domains have such a diverse range of uses, such as car, infrared detectors, military, nuclear, terrestrial systems, space and satellite communication that are extremely temperature dependent3–5. However the static RAM cells, operational amplifiers and logic gates, are most important building blocks throughout many applications. CMOS transistors must be scaled to get higher density and performance. On the other hand, deep scaling effects the performance of CMOS in the form of SCEs. To overcome these problems multi-gate transistors are best options6. FinFET is a device designed to effectively regulate SCEs (short channel effects). It achieves this by wrapping the channel with a gate on three sides, allowing for more effective gate control compared to conventional MOSFETs, but making junctions at low technology node is difficult7. To overcome these manufacturing challenges, junctionless FinFETs are required at the nano regime. Uniform doping over the silicon fins creates junctionless structures8,9. The device acts as a resistor because of the consistent doping, and the gate bias controls the device’s resistance. Furthermore, the technology must be fully depleted in order to fulfill industry requirements and to get the benefits at lower technology nodes. SOI (silicon-on-insulator) is a manufacturing technology that meets all objectives while optimizing the procedure. The essential proportions of the transistor are not altered as a result of this in compared to other technologies. As the result, we choose DMG JLFinFETs, and also they have smaller parasitic capacitances, consume less power and higher switching rates10.

Moreover, in this nano era as the number of transistors on a die are rapidly increasing, the impact of temperature on transistor behavior is a critical factor in assessing power consumption. To evolve temperature effects so many designs are proposed in the literature. The radiation effect is one of the major considerations while temperature increases rapidly. Nanoscale electronics are very sensitive to temperature changes, and temperature changes have a big impact on how well they work. In the literature survey, a very few designs are investigated with the effect of temperature in the literature to best of authors knowledge. A comparative investigation of conventional silicon-on-insulator (SOI) and junctionless (JL) FinFETs at extreme temperatures (300–500 K) was conducted in11. In this work, A research comparing the harmonic distortion characteristics, linearity, and analog performance of JL and IM FinFETs has been conducted at high temperatures. The authors of this paper investigated the analog performance metrics of JL FinFETs. JL devices exhibited superior linearity compared to IM devices over the whole temperature range. It was discovered that JL devices exhibited lower levels of harmonic distortion compared to IM devices. Because of these features, a JL FinFET is a great choice for analog/Radio Frequency systems that need to work in extreme temperatures. It might be interesting to look into the analog and RF performance of CMOS circuits built with JL devices at high temperatures in the future.

The article12 discussed a dual material gate (DMG) FinFET and its response to temperature changes in terms of electrical properties. The impact of temperature change on electrical characteristics of a FinFET was emphasized including the DC performance, including Ion/Ioff, subthreshold swing (SS), and drain current characteristics. The TCAD simulator also shows the way several RF/Analog parameters are affected by temperature, including output conductance, transconductance, intrinsic delay, cutoff frequency, gate capacitance, gain, transconductance frequency product, gain frequency product, and gain transconductance frequency product.

In13, the authors discussed the instability of negative bias temperature in body-tied FinFETs and SOI. The collected electrons cause a steeper bend in the energy band at the narrow fin compared to the wide fin. The presence of a substrate improves the resistance of a body-tied FinFET to NBT stress. In this study, Rajesh Saha et al.14 analyzed how the electrical characteristics of SOI GaAs FinFET are affected by temperature. While Si FinFETs work well in high-speed applications, GaAs FinFETs offer superior performance. The impact of temperature on the component’s behavior has been documented, particularly concerning drain current, electron mobility, and Ion/Ioff. According to the TCAD simulation, the values of Ion, Ioff, and carrier mobility all reduce as the temperature rises. Diab A et al.,15 presented a study on the effect of temperature fluctuations on gate leakage current, drain current, and transconductance throughout a temperature range spanning from ambient temperature to a high temperature of 150 °C.

A novel Germanium-based FinFET16 construction with dual material gates and an underlap region between the gate and drain. The study in17 investigated the impact of temperature on FinFET drain current by manipulating controlling dielectric material and gate parameters. The investigation of temperature effects on ultra-thin SOI and double gate transistors is detailed in18–20. Nevertheless, the influence of temperature on the radio frequency performance and the linearity metrics in dual material gate JLFinFETs has not been investigated.

The present research investigated the influence of temperature on the analog performance indicators of Fin-FET devices, namely transconductance (gm) and transconductance generation factor (gm/ID). In addition, we do calculations for other RF parameters, including gate capacitance, cut-off frequency, gain frequency product, intrinsic delay, and transconductance frequency product. In addition, we will examine the disparities in several metrics of linearity, including voltage intercept point, higher-order derivatives of transconductance (gm2, gm3) and 1-dB compression point, throughout a temperature range of 200 K to 400 K.

The structure of this research is outlined as follows: Section II offers a comprehensive description of the device fabrication process and the simulation setup. Section III presents the simulation results, including an analysis of linearity metrics and analog/RF performance. Finally, Section IV summarizes the key findings and conclusions derived from this study.

Lower distortion at the output terminals is critical for improved linearity performance. For enhanced linearity, with respect to VGS, transconductance should be constant. Due to the mobility degradation, FinFETs demonstrate nonlinearity. Hence, it is important to examine the device’s behavior at various voltage levels. Figure 9(a), (b) demonstrate that the gm2 and gm3 are derived from ID with regard to VGS. These coefficients may be utilized to assess the nonlinearity of the device. In order to obtain the most efficient direct current for a device, it is advisable to utilize the early zero-crossing point (ZCP) of gm2 and gm3 inside a circuit. The negative peaks in gm3 are caused by mobility degradation when gate bias increases. The ZCP, which should be as low as feasible, also shows the biasing voltage’s optimal suitability. Because of the less amplitude harmonics at high VGS and early ZCP, it proves its advantage in producing better linearity, demonstrating its acceleration to flatness.

The proposed DMG JL-FinFET further improves thermal stability because the dual material gate introduces enhanced electrostatic control and suppresses short channel effects. As a result, the proposed device maintains lower DIBL and relatively stable analog/RF parameters across the temperature range of 200 K to 400 K.

Self-heating effects and thermal transport mechanisms

Self-heating effects arise due to Joule heating and limited heat dissipation in nanoscale FinFET structures. The increase in channel temperature degrades carrier mobility due to phonon scattering25,26, increases intrinsic carrier concentration23, and results in higher leakage current, SS, and DIBL. Analog/RF parameters such as transconductance and cutoff frequency are also degraded at elevated temperatures12,33. The proposed DMG JL-FinFET exhibits improved thermal stability due to enhanced electrostatic control, consistent with prior studies11.

Conclusion

The TCAD tool is employed to conduct temperature analyses of DC, analog/RF, and linearity metrics in DMG JLFinFETs in this study. Results show SS and DIBL increasing, ION/IOFF is decreasing when temperature increased. Analog/RF properties such as transconductance (gm) and TGF are decreased, while Cgs and Cgd increases with increasing temperature. The total gate capacitance is monotonically increased with increasing temperature. The cut-off frequency, TFP, GBW are decreasing, on the other hand intrinsic delay is increasing with temperature increasing. The analog/RF properties of DMG JLFinFET are minimally influenced by temperature. The higher order harmonics gm2 and gm3 has high values at 200 K temperature. In contemporary the voltage intercept points VIP2 and VIP3 have high values at 400 K temperature. On overall the linearity parameters achieved superior performance. The findings suggest that temperature has a crucial role in affecting the analog/RF properties, particularly the linearity metrics. The study demonstrates that DMG JLFinFET is well-suited for nano size applications. Such has RF front-end circuits, Low-noise amplifiers, Analog integrated circuits, High-frequency nanoscale electronics, IoT sensor interfaces.
