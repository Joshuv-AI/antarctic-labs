A weighted cycle-localization inequality
Jiangdong Ai Bin Chen Ming Chen Tianxiao Zhao

Abstract
In 1959, Erdős and Gallai showed that every 2 2 -connected graph G G contains a cycle of length at least 2 ​ | E ⁡ ( G ) | | V ⁡ ( G ) | − 1 \frac{2|E(G)|}{|V(G)|-1} . This result was subsequently extended to weighted graphs by Bondy and Fan in 1991. A natural local variant of this problem arises by considering, for each edge e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e\in E(G) , the quantity c ⁡ ( e ) c(e) , defined as the length of the longest cycle in G G containing e e (with c ⁡ ( e ) = 2 c(e)=2 if e e is a bridge). Zhao and Zhang recently proved that for every graph G G on n n vertices satisfies ∑ e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) 1 c ⁡ ( e ) ≤ n − 1 2 . \sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{c(e)}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}. In this note, we establish a weighted generalization of this inequality. For a weighted graph ( G , w ) (G,w) with positive edge weights, let C w ​ ( e ) C_{w}(e) denote the maximum weight of a cycle containing e e (setting C w ​ ( e ) = 2 ​ w ​ ( e ) C_{w}(e)=2w(e) if e e is a bridge). We prove that ∑ e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) w ⁡ ( e ) C w ​ ( e ) ≤ n − 1 2 . \sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}. Our result can be viewed as a weighted local analogue of the Bondy-Fan theorem, thereby establishing a correspondence between the global and local perspectives. Furthermore, we present a broad class of graphs attaining equality and derive necessary conditions for equality.



1
Introduction

Determining the length of the longest cycle is one of the most important lines of research in graph theory.
From a global perspective (i.e., the number of edges), Erdős and Gallai
[
3
]
obtained the following result.

Theorem 1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Every
2
-edge-connected graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges contains a cycle of length at least
\frac{2m}{n-1}
.

Let
G
be a weighted graph if each of edge
e\in E(G)
is assigned a nonnegative number
\phi(e)
.
When
\phi(e)=1
for each
e\in E(G)
,
G
is a simple graph.
For any subgraph
H\subseteq G
, the
weight
of
H
is defined by

\phi(H)=\sum\limits_{e\in E(H)}\phi(e).

In 1991, Bondy and Fan
[
2
]
resolved a conjecture from
[
1
]
, thereby generalizing Theorem
1
to weighted graphs.
Concretely, their result reads as follows.

Theorem 2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a 2-edge-connected weighted graph on
n
vertices. Then
G
contains a cycle of weight at least
\frac{2\phi(G)}{n-1}
.

In the same paper, Bondy and Fan
[
2
]
further characterized the 2-edge-connected weighted graphs on
n
vertices that contain no cycle of weight more than
\frac{2\phi(G)}{n-1}
.

The aforementioned two results suggest that a suitably chosen global condition can yield a good lower bound on the length of the longest cycle in graphs.
A natural question then arises:

Is there a local condition that yields a good lower bound on the length of the longest cycle in graphs?

Recently, Zhao and Zhang
[
5
]
introduced a
localized
perspective for long cycles.
For a simple graph
G
and any
e\in E(G)
, let
c(e)
be the length of a longest cycle containing
e
(and set
c(e)=2
if
e
is a bridge).
They provided the following result.

Theorem 3

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. Then

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{c(e)}\leq\frac{n-1}{2},

(1)

with equality if and only if
G
is connected, and each block of
G
is a clique.

Here, we want to remark that Theorem
3
can be viewed as a generalization of Theorem
1
as illustrated by the following inequalities:

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{L}\leq\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{c(e)}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}\Longrightarrow{\frac{e(G)}{L}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}}\Longrightarrow\frac{2e(G)}{n-1}\leq L,

(2)

where
L
is denoted to be the length of the longest cycle in
G
.

Very recently, Li and Ning
[
4
]
observed that (
1
) admits an elegant proof by applying Theorem
2
to an
auxiliary
weighting of the form
e\mapsto\frac{1}{c(e)}
.
This is already a useful viewpoint, but it remains essentially unweighted: the input graph has no edge-weights, and the weighting is introduced only as a proof device.

Inspired by Theorems
1
and
2
, it is natural to ask the following question.

Question 1
.

Can we generalize Theorem
3
to weighted graphs?

In our view, the key to this question is how to choose a suitable local parameter for each edge, and such a choice is far from obvious.
A natural requirement of this parameter is that the inequality should depend on the given weights
w
and should localize
the heaviest cycle through each edge
.
This leads to the parameter
C_{w}(e)
, which means the maximum weight of a cycle containing
e
.
The point is that
C_{w}(e)
is defined using the same weighting
w
that we want to control, and it varies edge-by-edge in a highly non-linear way.

The central observation of this note is that the self-normalization

\phi(e):=\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}

forces
every
cycle
C
to satisfy
\phi(C)\leq 1
, despite the denominators
C_{w}(e)
depending on global maximal.
This makes the weighted problem tractable and produces a sharp analogue of Theorem
3
.
In particular, the weighted equality theory is strictly richer than the unweighted one: even on
K_{n}
, equality can hold for many non-uniform weightings (see Proposition
8
).
At the same time, equality is not “free”: on
K_{r}
with
r\geq 4
we show that equality forces the original weights themselves to be vertex-induced (see Proposition
15
).

Before stating our main result, we formally introduce several necessary definitions.
Throughout,
G
is a finite simple graph on
n
vertices and
w:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
For a subgraph
H\subseteq G
, write
w(H)=\sum_{e\in E(H)}w(e)
.

Definition 4
.

For any
e\in E(G)
, let

C_{w}(e):=\begin{cases}\max\{w(C):\ C\subseteq G\text{ is a cycle and }e\in E(C)\},&\text{if $e$ lies on a cycle},\\[2.0pt]
2w(e),&\text{if $e$ is a bridge}.\end{cases}

Note that
C_{w}(e)
generalizes
c(e)
from
[
5
]
by Zhao and Zhang in the sense that the latter corresponds to the special case where
w\equiv 1
.

Definition 5
.

Let
K_{r}
be a clique on vertex set
U
.
An edge-weighting
w
on
K_{r}
is
vertex-induced
if there exists a function
a:U\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

w(uv)=\frac{a(u)+a(v)}{2}\qquad\text{for all }uv\in E(K_{r}).

(3)

In this case, every cycle
C\subseteq K_{r}
satisfies
w(C)=\sum_{v\in V(C)}a(v)
.

2
Main theorem

Equipped with the former definitions, we are ready to state our main result.
As mentioned previously, Theorem
6
is a generalization of Theorem
3
by taking
w\equiv 1
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(G,w)
be a connected weighted graph on
n
vertices, with
C_{w}(e)
defined as above.
Then

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}.

(4)

Proof.

Define an auxiliary weight function
\phi:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by

\phi(e):=\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}.

If
C
is a cycle, then
C_{w}(e)\geq w(C)
for each
e\in E(C)
, so

\phi(C)=\sum_{e\in E(C)}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}\leq\sum_{e\in E(C)}\frac{w(e)}{w(C)}=1.

(5)

Let
B
be the set of bridges of
G
and let
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
be the components of
G-B
.
Then each
H_{i}
is
2
-edge-connected (or a single isolated vertex).
For any bridge
b\in B
,
\phi(b)=\frac{w(b)}{2w(b)}=\frac{1}{2}
.

If
H_{i}
is an isolated vertex, then
E(H_{i})=\emptyset
and
\phi(H_{i})=0=\frac{n_{i}-1}{2}
, so the bound holds trivially. We henceforth assume
n_{i}\geq 2
. Fix a component
H_{i}
, since every cycle in
H_{i}
has
\phi
-weight at most
1
by (
5
), Theorem
2
applied to
(H_{i},\phi)
gives

2\phi(H_{i})/(n_{i}-1)\ \leq\ \max\{\phi(C):\ C\subseteq H_{i}\text{ a cycle}\}\ \leq\ 1,

so
\phi(H_{i})\leq(n_{i}-1)/2
.

Summing over
i
and adding the bridges yields

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\phi(H_{i})+\sum_{b\in B}\phi(b)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{n_{i}-1}{2}+\frac{|B|}{2}.

Finally, removing all bridges increases the number of components by
|B|
, so
\sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_{i}-1)=n-k
and
k=|B|+1
, giving

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_{i}-1)+|B|=n-1,

and hence the right-hand side equals
\frac{n-1}{2}
.
∎

3
Extremals and equality

3.1
Explicit equality families

Theorem
6
is sharp.
Beyond trees and uniform complete graphs, equality holds for a broad class of “trees of cliques” with natural weight patterns.

Definition 7
.

A connected graph
G
is a
block graph
if every
block
of
G
(that is, every maximal connected subgraph with no cut-vertex) is a clique.
Equivalently, any two maximal cliques intersect in at most one vertex, and every cycle of
G
is contained in a unique clique.

Proposition 8
.

Let
K_{r}
be a complete graph with
r\geq 4
and let
w
be a vertex-induced weighting in the sense of Definition
5
.
Then
(K_{r},w)
attains equality in (
4
).

Proof.

Let
a:V(K_{r})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfy (
3
).
For any cycle
C
,
w(C)=\sum_{v\in V(C)}a(v)
, so the maximum cycle weight in
K_{r}
equals
A:=\sum_{v\in V(K_{r})}a(v)
and is attained by every Hamilton cycle.
Since every edge lies on a Hamilton cycle,
C_{w}(e)=A
for all
e\in E(K_{r})
.
Therefore

\sum_{e\in E(K_{r})}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}=\frac{w(K_{r})}{A}.

On the other hand,

w(K_{r})=\sum_{uv\in E(K_{r})}\frac{a(u)+a(v)}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(K_{r})}(r-1)a(v)=\frac{r-1}{2}\,A.

Hence the ratio equals
\frac{r-1}{2}
, which is the right-hand side of (
4
) for
n=r
.
∎

Proposition 9
.

Let
G
be a block graph on
n
vertices.
Assume that every clique block of size
r\geq 4
carries a vertex-induced weighting (with respect to its own vertex weights; triangle blocks may have arbitrary positive weights).
Then
(G,w)
attains equality in (
4
).

Proof.

We sum (
4
) block-by-block.
If
e
is a bridge, then
\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}=\frac{1}{2}
.

If
e
lies in a triangle block
T
, then
T
is the unique cycle containing
e
, so
C_{w}(e)=w(T)
and

\sum_{e\in E(T)}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}=\frac{w(T)}{w(T)}=1=\frac{3-1}{2}.

If
e
lies in a clique block
K_{r}
with
r\geq 4
and the restriction of
w
to this block is vertex-induced, then Proposition
8
shows that this block contributes
(r-1)/2
.

Since
G
is a block graph, its clique blocks can be ordered
B_{1},\dots,B_{t}
so that for each
j\geq 2
the block
B_{j}
intersects the union of the previous blocks in exactly one vertex.
Consequently,

n=|V(B_{1})|+\sum_{j=2}^{t}\bigl(|V(B_{j})|-1\bigr),\qquad\text{so}\qquad n-1=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\bigl(|V(B_{j})|-1\bigr).

Summing the block contributions computed above yields
\frac{n-1}{2}
.
∎

3.2
Necessary conditions for equality

Proposition 10
.

Let
(G,w)
be a connected weighted graph on
n
vertices.
If equality holds in (
4
), then for each component
H
of
G-B
with at least one edge (where
B
is the set of bridges) the auxiliary weighting
\phi(e)=\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}
satisfies:

({\rm i})

\phi(H)=\frac{|V(H)|-1}{2}
;

({\rm ii})
there exists a cycle
C\subseteq H
with
\phi(C)=1
;

({\rm iii})
for every edge
e\in E(C)
one has
C_{w}(e)=w(C)
.

In particular, each bridgeless component is extremal for the Bondy–Fan heavy-cycle theorem under the weighting
\phi
.

Proof.

In the proof of Theorem
6
we obtained for each bridgeless component
H
with
n_{H}=|V(H)|
the bound
\phi(H)\leq\frac{n_{H}-1}{2}
.
If equality holds in (
4
), then equality must hold for every such component, giving (i).

The Bondy–Fan theorem yields a cycle
C
in
(H,\phi)
with weight at least
\frac{2\phi(H)}{n_{H}-1}=1
; but every cycle has weight at most
1
by (
5
), so
\phi(C)=1
, proving (ii).

For (iii), note that for any cycle
C
we have
C_{w}(e)\geq w(C)
for each
e\in E(C)
, hence

\phi(C)=\sum_{e\in E(C)}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}\leq\sum_{e\in E(C)}\frac{w(e)}{w(C)}=1.

If
\phi(C)=1
, then equality must hold termwise.
Since all
w(e)>0
, this forces
C_{w}(e)=w(C)
for each
e\in E(C)
.
∎

3.3
Complete graphs

On complete graphs one can say more: equality forces the
self-normalized
weights
\phi(e)=\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}
to be induced in the sense of Definition
5
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
r\geq 3
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the set of undirected Hamilton cycles in
K_{r}
.
Then each edge of
K_{r}
lies in exactly
(r-2)!
members of
\mathcal{H}
.
Consequently,

\mathbf{1}_{E(K_{r})}=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{(r-2)!}\,\mathbf{1}_{E(H)}.

(6)

Proof.

The number of undirected Hamilton cycles in
K_{r}
is
\frac{(r-1)!}{2}
.
By symmetry, each edge lies in the same number
N
of Hamilton cycles.
Double counting edge–cycle incidences gives

N\binom{r}{2}=r\cdot\frac{(r-1)!}{2},

so
N=(r-2)!
.
Equation (
6
) is then immediate.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
r\geq 4
and let
\phi
be a positive edge-weighting of
K_{r}
.
If every Hamilton cycle of
K_{r}
has the same
\phi
-weight, then
\phi
is induced: there exists
a:V(K_{r})\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi(uv)=\frac{a(u)+a(v)}{2}
for all edges
uv
.

Proof.

Fix distinct vertices
x,y
.
We claim that for any two distinct vertices
u,v\notin\{x,y\}
,

\phi(uv)+\phi(xy)=\phi(ux)+\phi(vy)=\phi(uy)+\phi(vx).

(7)

Indeed, choose a Hamilton cycle
H
that contains the edges
ux
and
vy
and in which the four vertices
u,x,v,y
appear in the cyclic order
u-x-\cdots-v-y-\cdots-u
.
Let
H^{\prime}
be obtained from
H
by the standard
2
-opt swap replacing the edges
ux
and
vy
by
uv
and
xy
(this is possible in
K_{r}
and yields another Hamilton cycle).
The two Hamilton cycles share all other edges, so the assumption that
\phi(H)=\phi(H^{\prime})
implies
\phi(ux)+\phi(vy)=\phi(uv)+\phi(xy)
.
The second equality in (
7
) follows analogously (swap
uy
and
vx
).

Now define
a:V(K_{r})\to\mathbb{R}
by

a(t):=\phi(tx)+\phi(ty)-\phi(xy)\qquad(t\in V(K_{r})).

For distinct
s,t
, using (
7
) twice we obtain

a(s)+a(t)=\bigl(\phi(sx)+\phi(sy)-\phi(xy)\bigr)+\bigl(\phi(tx)+\phi(ty)-\phi(xy)\bigr)=2\phi(st),

so
\phi(st)=\frac{a(s)+a(t)}{2}
as required.
∎

Proposition 13
.

Let
r\geq 4
and let
(K_{r},w)
be a positively weighted complete graph.
If equality holds in (
4
), then the self-normalized weights

\phi(e)=\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}

form an induced edge-weighting of
K_{r}
.
Equivalently, there exists
a:V(K_{r})\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi(uv)=\frac{a(u)+a(v)}{2}
for all
uv
.

Proof.

Let
\phi(e)=\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}
.
As in (
5
), every cycle
C
satisfies
\phi(C)\leq 1
.
Since equality holds in (
4
), we have
\phi(K_{r})=\frac{r-1}{2}
.

Apply (
6
).
Multiplying by
\phi
and summing gives

\phi(K_{r})=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{(r-2)!}\,\phi(H)\leq\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{(r-2)!}=\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{(r-2)!}=\frac{r-1}{2}.

All inequalities must be equalities, so
\phi(H)=1
for every Hamilton cycle
H
.
In particular, all Hamilton cycles have the same
\phi
-weight, and Lemma
12
yields that
\phi
is induced.
∎

Lemma 14
.

Let
r\geq 4
and let
\mathcal{H}
be the set of undirected Hamilton cycles of
K_{r}
.
For any
H,H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
there exists a sequence
H=H_{0},H_{1},\dots,H_{t}=H^{\prime}
such that
H_{i-1}
and
H_{i}
share at least one edge for every
i
.

Proof.

Let the vertices of
K_{r}
be
1,\dots,r
. Every Hamilton cycle can be identified with a cyclic permutation of the vertex set. It is known that the symmetric group
S_{r}
is generated by all adjacent transpositions
(i,i+1)
(where
i=1,\dots,r-1
). Consequently, for the cycles
H
and
H^{\prime}
corresponding to the cyclic permutations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
respectively, there exists a sequence of adjacent transpositions
s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k}
such that
\pi^{\prime}=\pi\circ s_{1}\circ s_{2}\circ\dots\circ s_{k}
.

We now define a sequence of cycles
H_{0},H_{1},\dots,H_{k}
as follows: set
H_{0}=H
, and let
H_{i}
be the cycle corresponding to the permutation
\pi\circ s_{1}\circ\dots\circ s_{i}
, in other words,
H_{i}
is obtained from
H_{i-1}
by applying the adjacent transposition
s_{i}
. Applying an adjacent transposition
(j,j+1)
corresponds to swapping the two adjacent vertices
j
and
j+1
in the cyclic order of the cycle.

Consider any consecutive pair
H_{i-1}
and
H_{i}
in this sequence. They are related by a single 2-opt swap. Under such a swap, only four edges those incident to the two swapped vertices and their immediate neighbors can change; all other edges of the cycle remain unchanged. Since
r\geq 4
, the cycle has at least four vertices, and therefore
H_{i-1}
and
H_{i}
must share at least one edge outside the local neighborhood affected by the swap.

Thus the constructed sequence
H=H_{0},H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}=H^{\prime}
satisfies the required property.
∎

Proposition 15
.

Let
r\geq 4
and let
(K_{r},w)
be a positively weighted complete graph.
Then equality holds in (
4
) if and only if
w
is vertex-induced in the sense of Definition
5
.
In this case, every edge has the same localization denominator, namely

C_{w}(e)=\sum_{v\in V(K_{r})}a(v)\qquad\text{for all }e\in E(K_{r}).

Proof.

If
w
is vertex-induced, then Proposition
8
gives equality.

Conversely, assume equality holds in (
4
).
Set
\phi(e)=\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}
.
By Proposition
13
,
\phi
is induced.
Moreover, the proof of Proposition
13
shows that every Hamilton cycle
H
satisfies
\phi(H)=1
.

Fix a Hamilton cycle
H
.
Since
C_{w}(e)\geq w(H)
for all
e\in E(H)
, we have

1=\phi(H)=\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}\leq\sum_{e\in E(H)}\frac{w(e)}{w(H)}=1.

Thus equality holds, forcing
C_{w}(e)=w(H)
for every
e\in E(H)
.

If two Hamilton cycles share an edge
e
, then the above shows their total
w
-weights are both equal to
C_{w}(e)
, hence are equal.
By Lemma
14
, all Hamilton cycles have the same total weight; denote it by
W
.
Since every edge of
K_{r}
lies on a Hamilton cycle, it follows that
C_{w}(e)=W
for all edges
e
.
Therefore
w(e)=W\,\phi(e)
for all
e
.
Since
\phi
is induced, so is
w
.

Finally, let
a:V(K_{r})\to\mathbb{R}
be vertex weights inducing
w
.
For any cycle
C
in
K_{r}
we have
w(C)=\sum_{v\in V(C)}a(v)
.
If some vertex
x
had
a(x)<0
, then a cycle on
V(K_{r})\setminus\{x\}
would have weight

\sum_{v\neq x}a(v)=\sum_{v}a(v)-a(x)>\sum_{v}a(v)
, contradicting the fact that
W
is the maximum cycle weight.
Hence
a(v)\geq 0
for all
v
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 16
.

Let
G
be a block graph on
n
vertices.
If equality holds in (
4
), then every clique block
K_{r}
with
r\geq 4
carries a vertex-induced weighting (triangle blocks may have arbitrary positive weights).

Proof.

Let
B
be a clique block of size
r\geq 4
.
Every cycle containing an edge of
B
is contained in
B
, so the values
C_{w}(e)
for
e\in E(B)
are computed entirely inside
B
.
Apply Theorem
6
to
(B,w|_{E(B)})
to get

\sum_{e\in E(B)}\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}\leq\frac{|V(B)|-1}{2}.

Summing this bound over all blocks of
G
yields exactly the global bound
\frac{n-1}{2}
.
Indeed, for block graphs one has

n-1=\sum_{\text{blocks }B}(|V(B)|-1),

as in the proof of Proposition
9
.
Thus if equality holds in (
4
) for
G
, then equality must hold in the displayed inequality for every block
B
.
Proposition
15
then implies that
w|_{E(B)}
is vertex-induced.
∎

Remark 17

(On a full equality characterization)
.

Bondy and Fan
[
2
]
completely characterize the
2
-edge-connected weighted graphs for which their heavy-cycle bound is tight.
Proposition
10
shows that an equality characterization for (
4
) can in principle be obtained by applying that characterization to the self-normalized weighting
\phi
and keeping track of the “tight cycle” condition (iii).
Proposition
15
gives a clean instance of this program: on complete graphs, the original weights themselves must be vertex-induced.

4
Concluding remarks

In this note, we have established a weighted local analogue of the Bondy–Fan theorem, and shown that the resulting inequality is sharp by identifying a broad family of extremal graphs.
The proof relies on a simple but effective self-normalization device: replacing
w
by the auxiliary weighting
\phi(e)=\frac{w(e)}{C_{w}(e)}
forces every cycle to carry
\phi
-weight at most
1
, after which the Bondy–Fan theorem applies directly. This reduction highlights a clean correspondence between the global and local perspectives on long cycles in weighted graphs.

The inequality in Theorem
6
yields several useful consequences when one imposes a threshold on the value
C_{w}(e)
. Edges with small
C_{w}(e)
, those lying only on relatively light cycle, cannot collectively carry too much weight, as made precisely by the following estimates.

Corollary 18
.

Let
(G,w)
be a connected weighted graph on
n
vertices.
For any
T>0
,

\sum_{e\in E(G):\,C_{w}(e)\leq T}w(e)\ \leq\ \frac{T(n-1)}{2}.

(8)

Proof.

For edges with
C_{w}(e)\leq T
we have
w(e)/C_{w}(e)\geq w(e)/T
.
Summing and applying (
4
) gives

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{C_{w}(e)\leq T}w(e)\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
.
∎

Corollary 19
.

Let
(G,w)
be a connected weighted graph on
n
vertices.
For any
T>0
,

\sum_{e:\,C_{w}(e)>T}w(e)\ \geq\ w(G)-\frac{T(n-1)}{2}.

Proof.

This is immediate from (
8
) by subtracting from
w(G)=\sum_{e\in E(G)}w(e)
.
∎

Corollary 20
.

Let
(G,w)
be a connected weighted graph.
Define

F:=\{e\in E(G):\ C_{w}(e)<2w(e)\}.

Then
F
is acyclic (in particular
|F|\leq n-1
).

Proof.

If
F
contained a cycle
C
, then for any
e\in E(C)
we would have
w(C)\leq C_{w}(e)<2w(e)
.
Choosing two distinct edges
e,f\in E(C)
and using
w(C)\geq w(e)+w(f)
yields
w(f)<w(e)
and
w(e)<w(f)
, a contradiction.
∎
