{ ± 1 } \{\pm 1\} -weighted zero-sum constants
Krishnendu Paul, Shameek Paul

Abstract
Let A , B ⊆ ℤ n ∖ { 0 } A,B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}\setminus\{0\} . A sequence S = ( x 1 , … , x k ) S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}) in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} is called an ( A , B ) (A,B) -weighted zero-sum sequence if there exist a 1 , … , a k ∈ A a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in A and b 1 , … , b k ∈ B b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in B such that a 1 ​ x 1 + ⋯ + a k ​ x k = 0 a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0 and b 1 ​ a 1 + ⋯ + b k ​ a k = 0 b_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+b_{k}a_{k}=0 . The constant E A , B ​ ( n ) E_{A,B}(n) is defined to be the smallest positive integer k k such that every sequence of length k k in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} has an ( A , B ) (A,B) -weighted zero-sum subsequence of length n n . We determine the constant E A , B ​ ( n ) E_{A,B}(n) and the related constants C A , B ​ ( n ) C_{A,B}(n) and D A , B ​ ( n ) D_{A,B}(n) when A = { ± 1 } A=\{\pm 1\} and B = { 1 } B=\{1\} .

Introduction

By a sequence
S
in a set
X
of length
k
, we mean an element of the set
X^{k}
. By a subsequence we shall always mean a nonempty subsequence. Throughout this article,
M
will denote a finite module over the given ring. The following definition is given in
[
4
]
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
A,B
be nonempty subsets of
R\setminus\{0\}
. A sequence
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
in
M
is called an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum sequence if there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in A
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in B
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0
and
b_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+b_{k}a_{k}=0
.

The constant
D_{A,B}(M)
is the smallest positive integer
k
such that every sequence having length
k
in
M
has an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. The constant
C_{A,B}(M)
is the least positive integer
k
such that every sequence having length
k
in
M
has an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms.

The constant
E_{A,B}(M)
is the least positive integer
k
such that every sequence having length
k
in
M
has an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence having length
|M|
.

We let
D_{A}(M)
denote the constant
D_{A,B}(M)
where
B=\{0\}
. We define the constants
C_{A}(M)
and
E_{A}(M)
in a similar manner. Also, we let
D(M)
denote the constant
D_{\mathbf{1}}(M)
and we define
C(M)
and
E(M)
in a similar manner.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
we denote the ring
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. In the case
M=R=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we replace the symbol
M
by
n
in the notation for the above constants.

Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
be a sequence in
M
and let
x\in M
. We define the
translate
of
S
by
x
to be the sequence
S+x=(x_{1}+x,\ldots,x_{k}+x)
. We denote the set
\{1\}
by
\mathbf{1}
.

Observation 1.1
.

Suppose a sequence
S
in a module
M
is an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Then every translate of
S
is also an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence, since the following identity holds.

a_{1}(x_{1}+x)+\cdots+a_{k}(x_{k}+x)=a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k})x.

Hence, to show that a sequence
S
has an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence, it is enough to show that this is true for a translate of
S
.

In this article, we show the following results:

Let
M=R=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
A=\{\pm 1\}
. Then

•

We have
C_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=2C_{A}(n)
and
D_{A}(n)+1\leq D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)\leq 2D_{A}(n)
.

•

When
n
is odd, we have
E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=2n-1
.

•

When
n
is even, we have
E_{A}(n)\leq E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)\leq n-2+D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)
.

Let
M
be a finite
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-module. Then
\{\pm 1\}=\{1\}
and we have

C_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=2C(M),~D_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=D(M)+1,\text{ and }E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=E(M).

From this, we can show that if
M=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}
, then

C_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=2^{r+1},~D_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=r+2,\text{ and }E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=2^{r}+r.

2
Some lower bounds

Given a ring
R
, we let
R^{*}
denote the group of units of the ring
R
.

Observation 2.1
.

Let
T
be an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum sequence in
M
. If either
A\subseteq R^{*}
or
B\subseteq R^{*}
, then
T
has length at least two, since
0\notin A\cup B
.

Proposition 2.2
.

If either
A\subseteq R^{*}
or
B\subseteq R^{*}
, then
C_{A,B}(M)\geq 2C_{A}(M)
.

Proof.

Let
k=C_{A}(M)
and let
S^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1})
be a sequence in
M
which does not have any
A
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms. Let
S=(0,x_{1},0,x_{2},0,\ldots,0,x_{k-1},0)
be the sequence which is obtained from
S^{\prime}
by putting zeroes in alternate positions. Suppose
S
has an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms. Then by Observation
2.1
we get the contradiction that
S^{\prime}
has an
A
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms. Since
S
has length
2k-1
, it follows that
C_{A,B}(M)\geq 2k
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

If either
A\subseteq R^{*}
or
B\subseteq R^{*}
, then
D_{A,B}(M)\geq D_{A}(M)+1
.

Proof.

Let
k=D_{A}(M)
and let
S^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1})
be a sequence in
M
which does not have any
A
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1},0)
. Suppose
S
has an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. By Observation
2.1
we get the contradiction that
S^{\prime}
has an
A
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. Since
S
has length
k
, it follows that
D_{A,B}(M)\geq k+1
.
∎

3
Upper bounds when
A=\{\pm 1\}
and
B=\{1\}

For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
we denote the set
\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:a\leq x\leq b\}
by
[a,b]
. We let
|X|
denote the number of elements in a finite set
X
.
For sequences
T
and
T^{\prime}
in
M
, we let
T+T^{\prime}
denote the concatenation of
T
and
T^{\prime}
. For a sequence
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
in
M
, we say that the sum of
S
is
\sum S=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}
.

Observation 3.1
.

Let
A=\{\pm 1\}
and let
S
be a sequence in
M
. Suppose there exist two disjoint subsequences of
S
, say
T
and
T^{\prime}
, such that
S
is a permutation of
T+T^{\prime}
, and both the sequences
T
and
T^{\prime}
have the same sum and length. Then
S
is an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A=\{\pm 1\}
. We have
C_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)=2C_{A}(M)
.

Proof.

Let
k=C_{A}(M)
and let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2k})
be a sequence in
M
. For every
i\in[1,k]
let
y_{i}=x_{2i-1}-x_{2i}
. Consider the sequence
S^{\prime}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
. Since
C_{A}(M)=k
, we see that
S^{\prime}
has an
A
-weighted zero-sum subsequence
T^{\prime}
of consecutive terms. Thus, we see that there exist
i,j\in[1,k]
such that
i\leq j
and
T^{\prime}=(y_{i},\ldots,y_{j})
. Let
T=(x_{2i-1},x_{2i},\ldots,x_{2j-1},x_{2j})
. Since
A=\{\pm 1\}
, we see that
T
is an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of consecutive terms of
S
. Hence, it follows that
C_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)\leq 2k
. Since
A\subseteq R^{*}
, we are done by Proposition
2.2
.
∎

We give a combinatorial proof of the next result using lattice paths.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have
\binom{2k}{k}>2^{k}
.

Proof.

Let
D=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}:x+y=k\big\}
, let
\mathcal{L}
be the set of all lattice paths from
(0,0)
to
(k,k)
, and let
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be the set of all lattice paths from
(0,0)
to a point of
D
. There are
\binom{2k}{k}
paths in
\mathcal{L}
, since the paths in
\mathcal{L}
correspond to binary strings of length
2k
which have weight
k
. There are
2^{k}
paths in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, since the paths in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
correspond to binary strings of length
k
. Each path in
\mathcal{L}
passes through a unique point of
D
. So we get an onto map from
\mathcal{L}
to
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Further, there exist at least two paths in
\mathcal{L}
which are mapped to the path in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
which corresponds to a weight one binary string having length
k
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A=\{\pm 1\}
and let
k
be such that
2^{k}\geq|M|
. Then we have
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)\leq 2k
.

Proof.

Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2k})
be a sequence in
M
. By Lemma
3.3
we see that
\binom{2k}{k}>2^{k}
. Since
2^{k}\geq|M|
, it follows that
\binom{2k}{k}>|M|
. Thus, there exist two distinct subsequences
T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime\prime}
of
S
having length
k
such that both
T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime\prime}
have the same sum. Let
T
be the subsequence of
S
whose terms are the common terms of
T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime\prime}
. Since the subsequences
T^{\prime}-T
and
T^{\prime\prime}-T
are disjoint and they have the same sum and length, by Observation
3.1
we see that
S
has an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. It follows that
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)\leq 2k
.
∎

Observation 3.5
.

Let
A=\{\pm 1\}
. Suppose
\text{char }R
is
n
. When
n
is even, every
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence in
M
has even length. When
n
is odd, every
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence in
M
having length
n
is a zero-sum sequence.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\emph{char}R
be even, let
m
be an even number, and let
A=\{\pm 1\}
. Then every sequence in
M
of length
m-2+D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)
has an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length
m
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a sequence in
M
of length
m-2+d
where
d=D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)
. Let
r
be the largest integer such that
S
contains
r
pairs of repeated terms. Let
T
be the subsequence of
S
whose terms are these
r
pairs of repeated terms and let
S^{\prime}=S-T
.

Suppose
T
has at least
m
terms. In this case, we can find an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of
S
having length
m
by taking
m/2
pairs of repeated terms from
T
. Since the length of
T
is even, we may assume that
T
has at most
m-2
terms. It follows that
S^{\prime}
has at least
d
terms.

Let
T^{\prime}
be a maximal
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of
S^{\prime}
. Let the length of
T^{\prime}
be
l
. By Observation
3.5
we see that
l
is even. Since
S^{\prime}-T^{\prime}
has at most
d-1
terms and since
S^{\prime}-T^{\prime}=(S-T)-T^{\prime}=S-(T+T^{\prime})
, we see that
T+T^{\prime}
has at least
m-1
terms.

It follows that
T
has at least
m-l-1
terms. Since the length of
T
is even, we see that
T
has at least
m-l
terms. Since
m-l
is even, we may add
(m-l)/2
pairs of repeated terms from
T
to the subsequence
T^{\prime}
to get an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of
S
having length
l+(m-l)=m
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
A=\{\pm 1\}
. Suppose
\emph{char}R
is even and
m=|M|
is even. Then we have
E_{A}(M)\leq E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)\leq m-2+D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)
.
In particular, it follows that if
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)=D_{A}(M)+1
, then
E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)=E_{A}(M)
.

Proof.

Clearly, we have
E_{A}(M)\leq E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)
. Also, by Theorem
3.6
we see that
E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)\leq m-2+D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)
. Thus, if
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)=D_{A}(M)+1
, we see that
E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)\leq m-1+D_{A}(M)
. It is easy to see that
m-1+D_{A}(M)\leq E_{A}(M)
. Hence, it follows that
E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(M)=E_{A}(M)
.
∎

4
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
A=\{1\}

Observation 4.1
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and let
S
be a sequence in
M
having even length. If
S
is a zero-sum sequence, then
S
is a
(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Then
C_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=2C(M)
,
D_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=D(M)+1
, and
E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=E(M)
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a sequence in
M
having length
D(M)+1
. Then the sequence
S
has a zero-sum subsequence
T
. Let
x
be a term of
T
and let
S^{\prime}=S-(x)
. Since
S^{\prime}
has length
D(M)
, we see that
S^{\prime}
has a zero-sum subsequence
T^{\prime}
. Since
x
is a term of
T
which is not a term of
T^{\prime}
, we see that
T\neq T^{\prime}
.

Suppose both
T
and
T^{\prime}
have odd length. Let
U
be the subsequence of
S
whose terms are those terms of
T
and
T^{\prime}
which are not terms of both
T
and
T^{\prime}
. Since the length of
U
has the same parity as the sum of the lengths of
T
and
T^{\prime}
, we see that
U
has even length. Since
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we see that
\sum U=\sum T+\sum T^{\prime}
.

Thus,
U
is a zero-sum sequence. Hence, by Observation
4.1
we see that
S
has a
(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. It follows that
D_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)\leq D(M)+1
. Hence, by using Proposition
2.3
we see that
D_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=D(M)+1
. So from Corollary
3.7
we see that
E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=E(M)
. Also, from Theorem
3.2
we see that
C_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=2C(M)
.
∎

Let
M
be a finite
R
-module. Since
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a field, we see that
M=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{r}
where
r
is the dimension of
M
. From Theorems 3.1, 4.1, and 5.2 of
[
3
]
we can deduce that
C(M)=2^{r}
,
D(M)=r+1
, and
E(M)=2^{r}+r
. Hence, from Theorem
4.2
it follows that
C_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=2^{r+1}
,
D_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=r+2
, and
E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(M)=2^{r}+r
.

5
M=R=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
A=\{\pm 1\}

From
[
1
]
we see that if
D_{A}(n)=k
, then
2^{k}>n
. Hence, from Proposition
2.3
and Theorem
3.4
, we see that
D_{A}(n)+1\leq D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)\leq 2D_{A}(n)
.
When
n
is odd, by Observation
3.5
it follows that
E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=E(n)=2n-1
. When
n
is even, Corollary
3.7
shows
E_{A}(n)\leq E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)\leq n-2+D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)
.
In particular, if
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=D_{A}(n)+1
, then
E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=E_{A}(n)
. Also, from Theorem
3.2
we see that
C_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=2C_{A}(n)
.

Remark 5.1
.

We can check that the sequence
S=(0,1,2,4)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
has no
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. It follows that
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(6)\geq 5
. Suppose
S
is a sequence having length five in
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
. Since
\binom{5}{2}=10>6
, we can find two subsequences of
S
having length two which have the same sum. It follows that
S
has an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. Hence, we see that
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(6)=5
.

From
[
1
]
we see that
D_{A}(6)=3
. Thus, we have
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(6)=D_{A}(6)+2
. By considering the sequence
(0,1,2)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and the sequence
(0,1,2,4)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
and by using a similar argument as in the previous paragraph, we can show that
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(4)=D_{A}(4)+1
and
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(8)=D_{A}(8)+1
.

6
Concluding remarks

Let
M=R=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
A=\{\pm 1\}
. In
[
2
]
it is shown that
C_{A}(n)=n
when
n
is a power of
2
. As per our knowledge, the values of
C_{A}(n)
are not known for other
n
(except for some small values of
n
). So from Theorem
3.2
we see that
C_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=2n
when
n
is a power of
2
.

We have not found any value of
n
for which
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)\geq D_{A}(n)+3
. We feel that
D_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=D_{A}(n)+1
whenever
n
is a power of
2
. We also feel that
E_{A,\mathbf{1}}(n)=E_{A}(n)
for every even number
n
.

Let
|M|=m
and let
m
be a multiple of char
R
. In
[
4
]
it is shown that
D_{A,B}(M)\leq E_{A,B}(M)\leq 2m-1
and that
C_{A,B}(M)\leq m^{2}
.
Also, in
[
4
]
it is shown that
D_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(n)=E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(n)=2n-1
and
C_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(n)=n^{2}
. When
A=\{\pm 1\}
, we have obtained much smaller upper bounds for the corresponding
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum constants.
