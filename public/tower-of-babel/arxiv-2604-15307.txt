Heuristic Search for Minimum-Distance Upper-Bound Witnesses in Quantum APM-LDPC Codes
Kenta Kasai

Abstract
This paper investigates certified upper bounds on the minimum distance of an explicit family of Calderbank-Shor-Steane quantum LDPC codes constructed from affine permutation matrices. All codes considered here have active Tanner graphs of girth eight. Rather than attempting to prove a general lower bound for the full code distance, we focus on constructing low-weight non-stabilizer logical representatives, which yield valid upper bounds once they are verified to lie in the opposite parity-check kernel and outside the stabilizer row space. We develop a unified framework for such witnesses arising from latent row relations, restricted-lift subspaces including block-compressed, selected-fiber, and CRT-stripe constructions, cycle-8 elementary trapping-set structures, and decoder-failure residuals. In every case, search is used only to generate candidates; the reported bounds begin only after explicit kernel and row-space exclusion tests have been passed. For the latent part, we also identify a block-compression criterion under which the certification becomes exact. Applying these methods to representative APM-LDPC codes sharpens previously reported upper bounds and provides concrete certified values across the explored parameter range.



1
Introduction

Low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes use sparse parity-check matrices, or
equivalently sparse Tanner graphs, to support efficient decoding while retaining
nontrivial minimum distance
[
5
,
32
]
. In the classical
setting, random regular ensembles, expander/Tanner constructions,
quasi-cyclic constructions, and protograph liftings give different ways to
combine sparse local constraints with global
distance
[
29
,
4
,
25
]
.
In quantum coding theory, these classical constructions are directly relevant through
Calderbank–Shor–Steane (CSS) codes, which connect quantum codes to pairs of
classical linear codes satisfying an orthogonality relation
[
1
,
30
]
.

For quantum LDPC codes, however, the classical design principles do not apply
without additional constraints. One needs two sparse parity-check matrices that
are not only individually useful but also mutually orthogonal in the CSS sense.
This compatibility condition appears in early sparse-graph quantum code
constructions
[
24
,
8
]
, in hypergraph-product codes
with positive rate and square-root distance
[
33
]
, and in
modern constructions of asymptotically good quantum LDPC codes and quantum
Tanner codes
[
28
,
22
,
3
]
. Much of that work
gives lower bounds or asymptotic existence results.
Recent related work also studies near-bound finite-length constructions,
random quantum-LDPC constructions, and finite-degree
Gilbert–Varshamov-type constructions
[
20
,
27
,
17
]
.

For minimum-distance evaluation, a lower bound for the full code distance would
be the strongest conclusion, because it gives
guaranteed error-correction capability rather than merely excluding larger
values. In the present family, however, the known lower-bound techniques do not
transfer directly to the full distance, and the classical literature on exact
minimum-weight search is primarily effective at short and medium blocklengths.
Even the elementary tree-bound baseline for sparse Tanner graphs
[
32
]

gives only a small constant lower-bound baseline for the active girth-eight graphs used below; it
does not explain the larger distance values observed in the computations.
This paper therefore records explicit upper-bound vectors and verifies them
inside one specified construction family. More detailed comments on the lower-bound literature and on the
classical short-code literature are deferred to later sections.

Affine permutation matrix (APM) constructions impose the CSS orthogonality
condition in a controlled way
[
26
,
11
,
20
,
14
,
15
]
.
Instead of imposing commutativity on the full parent matrix, one enforces
orthogonality only on the active part that is actually used as the CSS
stabilizer, while allowing controlled non-commutativity in the latent part.
This produces high-girth binary CSS LDPC codes together with explicit latent
row spaces that may contain low-weight logical representatives. The closest antecedent is
[
15
]
, which developed the
same structured family primarily from the construction and decoding side. The
present paper is a follow-up on that same construction method, focused on how
tightly one can upper-bound and update the minimum distance within the family.
Recent nonbinary and affine-permutation studies of quantum-LDPC decoding and
high-girth design provide complementary finite-length evidence for the same
design direction
[
12
,
13
,
19
,
14
]
.
Following that terminology, we refer to this family as quantum APM-based LDPC
(APM-LDPC) codes.
A standing hypothesis in this paper is that every concrete code analyzed or
reported below has active Tanner graphs of girth 8. This girth-eight condition
is part of the search constraint, not only a regularity assumption. It
excludes four- and six-cycles, so the first cycle-based local
supports used by the elementary trapping set (ETS) method are built from
8-cycles.

The choice of lift sizes in the present search is not arbitrary. The companion
manuscript on affine commutation patterns
[
16
]
shows
that the cross-commuting nonabelian affine pattern needed on the construction
side is not available for prime-power moduli and is available for
Chinese remainder theorem (CRT)-split moduli. For that reason, the
computational search here is organized around non-prime-power lift sizes.

This leads to a limited question. The family proposed
in
[
15
]
may have distance that grows with blocklength. The present
paper does not try to decide this. Instead, it asks which low-weight logical
operators are found by the searches used here and then pass the stated
kernel and row-space tests. The upper bound reported in
[
15
]
is updated, while the
overall asymptotic behavior of the family remains unsettled.

The paper is therefore deliberately split into a proof part and a
computational verification part. The mathematical part states sufficient conditions under which
a vector in a specified subspace is a non-stabilizer logical representative.
The computational verification part supplies vectors found by searches and checks
those conditions for representative codes. Search procedures are used only to
find candidate vectors; the upper-bound claims in the paper begin only after the
appropriate parity-check-kernel and row-space-exclusion tests have been passed.
A compact classification of the upper-bound methods is included at the end of
Section
2
.

2
Parent, Active, and Latent Matrices

Let

\hat{H}_{X}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r_{X}\times n},\qquad\hat{H}_{Z}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r_{Z}\times n}

be binary parent matrices with the same number of columns.
Choose subsets of rows and call them the active rows.
The complementary rows are called latent.
After permuting rows if necessary, we may write

\hat{H}_{X}=\begin{bmatrix}H_{X}\\
\tilde{H}_{X}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\hat{H}_{Z}=\begin{bmatrix}H_{Z}\\
\tilde{H}_{Z}\end{bmatrix},

where
H_{X},H_{Z}
are the active matrices and
\tilde{H}_{X},\tilde{H}_{Z}
are the latent matrices.
This section makes no APM or block-size assumptions; it only defines the
overall, latent, and non-latent distances attached to this decomposition.

Vectors are regarded as elements of the ambient binary vector space, and their
orientation is chosen only when a matrix product is written. For

A\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r\times n}
, we write

\mathrm{Ker}(A):=\{\bm{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:A\bm{u}=0\}.

We also write
\mathrm{Row}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
for the subspace spanned by the rows of

A
. For a binary vector
\bm{u}
,
\mathrm{wt}(\bm{u})
denotes its Hamming weight, namely
the number of nonzero coordinates. When a row-space element is displayed as

\bm{x}=\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}A
,

\bm{\lambda}
is a column coefficient vector and
\bm{x}
is the corresponding row
combination. Syndrome equations are then written using the transpose, for
example
B\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=0
; equivalently, a displayed row vector
\bm{x}

belongs to
\mathrm{Ker}(B)
precisely when
B\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=0
. This convention is
used throughout to avoid introducing separate symbols for the same binary vector
in row and column form.

Definition 2.1

(Overall, latent, and non-latent distances)
.

From this point on, assume that
H_{X}H_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}=0
and define the CSS constituent codes by

C_{X}:=\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X}),\qquad C_{Z}:=\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z}).

The usual CSS distances are

d_{X}=\min\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x}):\bm{x}\in C_{Z}\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}\},\qquad d_{Z}=\min\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z}):\bm{z}\in C_{X}\setminus C_{Z}^{\perp}\}.

To isolate the contribution coming directly from the latent row spaces, define

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})}:=\min\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x}):\bm{x}\in(C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X}))\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}\},

d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{lat})}:=\min\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z}):\bm{z}\in(C_{X}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{Z}))\setminus C_{Z}^{\perp}\}.

To measure the complementary contribution outside the latent row spaces, define

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}:=\min\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x}):\bm{x}\in C_{Z}\setminus C_{X}^{\perp},\ \bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})\},

d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}:=\min\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z}):\bm{z}\in C_{X}\setminus C_{Z}^{\perp},\ \bm{z}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{Z})\}.

All four minima are interpreted as
+\infty
if the corresponding set is empty.
Then

d_{X}=\min\{d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})},d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\},\qquad d_{Z}=\min\{d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{lat})},d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\}.

∎

This definition separates logical representatives contained in the latent row
spaces from logical representatives outside those row spaces. Later sections
state upper-bound criteria for both classes.

3
APM Specialization

We now specialize the general parent/active/latent framework to the APM family used throughout the computational part.
Let
L
be even and let
F_{0},\dots,F_{L/2-1}
and
G_{0},\dots,G_{L/2-1}
be
P\times P
permutation matrices. We define the parent matrices of the APM-LDPC family by

(\hat{H}_{X})_{i,j}=F_{j-i},\qquad(\hat{H}_{X})_{i,L/2+j}=G_{j-i},

(\hat{H}_{Z})_{i,j}=G_{i-j}^{\mathsf{T}},\qquad(\hat{H}_{Z})_{i,L/2+j}=F_{i-j}^{\mathsf{T}},

with all indices interpreted modulo
L/2
.
For the active choice used in this paper, the first
J
block rows are taken as active and the remaining block rows are latent, in the sense of Section
2
.

For each
r\in\mathbb{Z}/(L/2)\mathbb{Z}
, define

\Psi_{r}:=\sum_{u=0}^{L/2-1}\bigl(F_{u}G_{r-u}+G_{r-u}F_{u}\bigr)

over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
The blocks of
\hat{H}_{X}\hat{H}_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}
depend only on the row difference and are given by the corresponding
\Psi_{r}
.

For the standard active choice, let

\Delta:=\{(k-i)\bmod(L/2):0\leq i,k\leq J-1\}.

This sufficient criterion is already present in the
(3,12)
setting of
[
15
]
; here we state it in the general
(J,L)
form.
Indeed, the
(i,k)
block of
H_{X}H_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}
is
\Psi_{k-i}
with indices taken modulo
L/2
.
Since
0\leq i,k\leq J-1
, the difference
k-i
always belongs to
\Delta
.
Thus the active orthogonality condition is guaranteed by

\Psi_{r}=0\qquad(r\in\Delta).

Thus CSS orthogonality is equivalent to the vanishing of the mixed products

\Psi_{r}
for the active residues.

In the concrete family treated computationally in this paper we fix

J=3,\qquad L=12,\qquad L/2=6.

Then

\Delta=\{0,1,2,4,5\},

so the only unconstrained residue is
r=3
. The search problem is therefore to realize

\Psi_{r}=0\quad(r\neq 3),\qquad\Psi_{3}\neq 0,

so that the only nonzero active–latent mixed product allowed by the template is

\Psi_{3}
.

Each block is an affine permutation

x\mapsto ax+b\pmod{P},\qquad\gcd(a,P)=1.

This representation is used for two reasons. First, commutativity reduces to a
linear congruence. Second, if
P=mQ
, the same affine rule induces a
well-defined permutation modulo
Q
, which is the algebraic fact used in block
compression.

4
Latent Upper Bounds

This section evaluates the latent distances for a general regular APM-LDPC
construction. Let the active row blocks be indexed by
0,\dots,J-1
and the
latent row blocks by
J,\dots,L/2-1
, and assume
H_{X}H_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.

Lemma 4.1

(General active–latent mixed product)
.

For every active index
0\leq i\leq J-1
and latent index
0\leq\ell\leq L/2-J-1
,

[H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}]_{i,\ell}=\Psi_{(J+\ell-i)\bmod(L/2)}^{\mathsf{T}},\qquad[H_{X}\tilde{H}_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}]_{i,\ell}=\Psi_{(J+\ell-i)\bmod(L/2)}.

∎

Proof.

The
(i,\ell)
block of
H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}
is the parent mixed product between active row block
i
and latent row block
J+\ell
, which is exactly

\Psi_{(J+\ell-i)\bmod(L/2)}^{\mathsf{T}}.

The
H_{X}\tilde{H}_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}
formula is analogous.
∎

The lemma reduces the latent upper-bound problem from the full parent matrix to the kernel of an explicit mixed product. The next proposition states the resulting latent upper-bound criterion, in the same spirit as the latent-distance analysis developed for this APM framework in earlier work
[
15
]
. Operationally, finding one feasible column coefficient vector
\bm{\lambda}
is enough: the row combination
\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}
or
\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{Z}
is then an explicit logical representative, and its weight gives the upper bound.

Proposition 4.2

(Latent upper bounds from mixed-product kernels)
.

The latent distances admit the exact parameterizations

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})}=\min\left\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}):\bm{\lambda}\in(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P})^{L/2-J},\ H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\lambda}=0,\ \bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}\notin C_{X}^{\perp}\right\},

d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{lat})}=\min\left\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{v}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{Z}):\bm{v}\in(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P})^{L/2-J},\ H_{X}\tilde{H}_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{v}=0,\ \bm{v}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{Z}\notin C_{Z}^{\perp}\right\},

with the convention that the minimum is
+\infty
when the corresponding set is empty.

In particular, every feasible coefficient vector

\bm{\lambda}\in(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P})^{L/2-J}
satisfying

H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\lambda}=0
and

\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}\notin C_{X}^{\perp}

produces

\bm{x}:=\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}\in(C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X}))\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}
,
hence
d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
and therefore
d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
.
The
Z
-side statement is identical.
∎

Proof.

We prove the
X
-side identity. By Definition
2.1
,

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})}=\min\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x}):\bm{x}\in(C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X}))\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}\}.

Let

\mathcal{L}_{X}:=\left\{\bm{\lambda}\in(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P})^{L/2-J}:H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\lambda}=0,\ \bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}\notin C_{X}^{\perp}\right\}.

If
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}_{X}
and
\bm{x}:=\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}
, then

\bm{x}\in\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
and

H_{Z}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\lambda}=0
,
so
\bm{x}\in C_{Z}
; by definition of
\mathcal{L}_{X}
, we also have

\bm{x}\notin C_{X}^{\perp}
.
Hence every feasible coefficient vector produces an element of

(C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X}))\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}
.

Conversely, if
\bm{x}\in(C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X}))\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}
,
then
\bm{x}\in\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, so there exists some

\bm{\lambda}\in(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P})^{L/2-J}
with

\bm{x}=\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}
.
Because
\bm{x}\in C_{Z}
,

H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\lambda}=H_{Z}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=0
,
and since
\bm{x}\notin C_{X}^{\perp}
, this
\bm{\lambda}
belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{X}
.

Therefore the image set

\{\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X}:\bm{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}_{X}\}

coincides exactly with

(C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X}))\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}.

This proves the displayed formula for
d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})}
.
Finally, since

d_{X}=\min\{d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})},d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\}
,
every latent witness also yields the overall upper bound
d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
.
The
Z
-side argument is identical.
∎

This proposition is the latent upper-bound statement used later in the paper.
Construction of the coefficient vectors is deferred to
Section
7
, where the upper-bound criteria are applied to
explicit examples.

The exact latent lower-bound theory based on block-constant compression, together
with its rank-test and satisfiability (SAT) / satisfiability modulo theories (SMT) certification machinery, is placed in
Appendix
A
. The main text next gives upper-bound
statements for restricted lifted subspaces.

5
Restricted-Lift Structural Upper Bounds

Section
4
treated logical representatives contained in the
latent row spaces. From this section onward, we look for logical
representatives outside those latent row spaces; these give the non-latent side
of the upper-bound theory. The common formulation is to restrict the search to
the image of a linear map into the full length-
LP
ambient space. A vector
found in such a subspace gives an upper bound only after it is checked to lie in
the appropriate parity-check kernel and outside the stabilizer row space. The
general statement uses a linear map

\Phi:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LP}.

The following proposition records the search-independent condition:
a vector in this image that passes the kernel and row-space tests gives an
upper bound. The three choices of
\Phi
used in this paper are introduced below in the full-fiber,
fiber-quotient, and CRT-compression cases of
Subsections
5.2
,
5.3
, and

5.4
.

Proposition 5.1

(Restricted-lift upper bound)
.

Let
\Phi_{X}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r_{X}^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LP}
and

\Phi_{Z}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r_{Z}^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LP}
be linear maps. If

\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r_{X}^{\prime}}
satisfies

H_{Z}\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})^{\mathsf{T}}=0,\qquad\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{X}),

then
\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})
is a valid
X
logical representative and

d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})).

If in addition
\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, then

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})).

The analogous statement with
X
and
Z
interchanged also holds.
∎

Proof.

The
X
side follows directly from the CSS distance definition:

H_{Z}\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})^{\mathsf{T}}=0
says that the lifted vector lies in

C_{Z}
, while
\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})=C_{X}^{\perp}
says that it is not a
stabilizer. Hence it represents a nontrivial
X
logical operator. The
additional condition
\Phi_{X}(\bar{\bm{x}})\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
places the same
representative outside the latent row space, giving the non-latent bound by
Definition
2.1
. The
Z
side is identical.
∎

We next treat the block-constant lift, the fiber-pattern lift, and the CRT
stripe subspace. The first two are written explicitly as maps
\Phi
, while
the CRT case is written as a restricted subspace. The symbol
\bar{d}^{\mathrm{rls}}
is used only
as a collective label for these restricted-lift entries; the component entries

\bar{d}^{\mathrm{blk}}
,
\bar{d}^{\mathrm{fib}}
, and
\bar{d}^{\mathrm{crt}}
are reported separately.

5.1
Block-Constant Quotient for Full-Fiber Block Compression

Assume
P=mQ
with
m\geq 2
. For
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{Q}
, define the coset

[t]_{m}:=\{t,t+Q,t+2Q,\dots,t+(m-1)Q\}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{P}.

A length-
P
vector is called
m
-block constant
if it is constant on every coset
[t]_{m}
.

Let
U_{m}(P)\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P}
be the
m
-block-constant subspace. Define the compression and lift maps

\pi_{m}:U_{m}(P)\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{Q},\qquad\iota_{m}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{Q}\to U_{m}(P)

by

[\pi_{m}(\bm{x})]_{t}:=\bm{x}_{t},\qquad[\iota_{m}(\bar{\bm{x}})]_{t+jQ}:=\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}.

Applying these maps blockwise over
N
blocks yields

\pi_{m,N}:U_{m}(NP)\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{NQ},\qquad\iota_{m,N}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{NQ}\to U_{m}(NP),

with

\mathrm{wt}(\iota_{m,N}(\bar{\bm{x}}))=m\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{x}}).

If the relevant matrices preserve block-constant structure, this construction
replaces a length-
P
equation by a length-
Q
quotient equation. This is the
APM version of the quotient operation used for classical quasi-cyclic LDPC code constructions
[
4
,
25
]
.

Lemma 5.2

(Descent of APMs on block-constant subspaces)
.

Let
M
be the
P\times P
APM corresponding to
x\mapsto ax+b\pmod{P}
, and let
\bar{M}
be the quotient APM obtained by reducing the same affine rule modulo
Q=P/m
. Then
M
preserves
U_{m}(P)
. Moreover, for every
\bar{\bm{u}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{Q}
,

M\iota_{m}(\bar{\bm{u}})=\iota_{m}(\bar{M}\bar{\bm{u}}),\qquad\pi_{m}(M\iota_{m}(\bar{\bm{u}}))=\bar{M}\bar{\bm{u}}.

The same commutativity holds blockwise for any block matrix made of APM blocks.
∎

Proof.

By definition of an APM,

M\bm{e}_{t}=\bm{e}_{at+b}.

For every
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{Q}
and
s\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
,

a(t+sQ)+b\equiv at+b\pmod{Q},

so the affine permutation maps the coset
[t]_{m}
to
[at+b]_{m}
. Hence the image of an
m
-block-constant vector is again
m
-block constant, and the action on cosets is exactly the quotient affine map modulo
Q
. The displayed identities follow directly from the definitions of
\pi_{m}
and
\iota_{m}
.
∎

This lemma shows that block compression is an exact quotient/lift operation.
It is the algebraic statement underlying the CSS full-fiber
restricted-lift special case below: a compressed vector satisfying the quotient
check equation can be lifted to a block-constant vector satisfying the original
check equation.
The next subsection adds the CSS row-space tests needed to make the lifted vector a logical representative.

5.2
Full-Fiber Block-Compression Special Case

This subsection obtains non-latent upper bounds from the block-constant lift.
The additional non-latent test is literal: on the
X
side the lifted vector
must be outside
\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, and on the
Z
side it must be outside

\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{Z})
.

Fix
m\mid P
and consider the same subspace

U_{m}(LP)\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LP}.

Since each APM descends modulo
Q=P/m
, the active matrices descend to compressed check matrices

\bar{H}_{X}^{(m)},\bar{H}_{Z}^{(m)}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{JQ\times LQ}.

Before using them, one must verify that the active check equations are preserved by compression.

Lemma 5.3

(Compression equivalence for active checks)
.

For every
\bm{x}\in U_{m}(LP)
and
\bar{\bm{x}}=\pi_{m,L}(\bm{x})
,

H_{Z}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=0\iff\bar{H}_{Z}^{(m)}\bar{\bm{x}}^{\mathsf{T}}=0.

Similarly, for every
\bm{z}\in U_{m}(LP)
and
\bar{\bm{z}}=\pi_{m,L}(\bm{z})
,

H_{X}\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}=0\iff\bar{H}_{X}^{(m)}\bar{\bm{z}}^{\mathsf{T}}=0.

Moreover,

\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})=m\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{x}}),\qquad\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z})=m\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{z}}).

∎

Proof.

Apply Lemma
5.2
blockwise to the quotient blocks of the active matrices. The active check vector of a lifted block-constant vector is exactly the lifted check vector computed by the compressed matrix. The weight formula follows from the lift.
∎

This lemma states that a quotient vector satisfying the compressed kernel
condition lifts to a vector satisfying the original active-check kernel
condition exactly.

Proposition 5.4

(Full-fiber block-compression special case)
.

Let
m\mid P
and
Q=P/m
. Take
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{H}_{Z}^{(m)})
and let
\bm{x}=\iota_{m,L}(\bar{\bm{x}})
. If
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})
and
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, then
\bm{x}
is a valid
X
logical representative outside the latent row space, and
d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})=m\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{x}})
. Similarly, if
\bar{\bm{z}}\in\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{H}_{X}^{(m)})
lifts to
\bm{z}=\iota_{m,L}(\bar{\bm{z}})
with
\bm{z}\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z})\cup\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{Z})
, then
d_{Z}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z})=m\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{z}})
.
∎

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
, the lift
\bm{x}
satisfies
H_{Z}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=0
, so
\bm{x}\in C_{Z}
. If additionally
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})=C_{X}^{\perp}
, then
\bm{x}
is a valid
X
logical representative. The extra condition
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
ensures that it lies outside the latent row space. Hence
d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})=m\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{x}})
. The
Z
case is the same.
∎

Concrete use of Proposition
5.4
is explained in
Section
7
.

The block-constant lift is only the first member of the restricted-lift
family. The next two subsections keep
Proposition
5.1
as the underlying sufficient condition
while enlarging the allowed lifted subspace.

5.3
Fiber-Quotient Special Case

The fiber-quotient bound lifts each quotient bit only to a selected fiber
pattern. The proposition below is stated for every nonempty pattern; in the
tables, the notation
\bar{d}^{\mathrm{fib}}
is reserved for proper patterns

\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, because the full pattern is already
reported as
\bar{d}^{\mathrm{blk}}
.

Definition 5.5

(Fiber-pattern lift and fiber-quotient subspace)
.

Let
m\mid P
, put
Q=P/m
, and fix a nonempty pattern

S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
. Define the single-block fiber-pattern lift

\phi_{m,S}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{Q}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P}

by

(\phi_{m,S}(\bm{y}))_{t+kQ}:=\begin{cases}y_{t},&k\in S,\\
0,&k\notin S,\end{cases}\qquad t\in\mathbb{Z}_{Q},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}.

The corresponding length-
LP
lift is the blockwise direct sum

\Phi_{m,S,L}:=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{L-1}\phi_{m,S}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LQ}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LP}.

Its image

\mathcal{F}_{m,S}:=\operatorname{im}(\Phi_{m,S,L})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LP}

is called the fiber-quotient subspace of pattern
S
.
∎

In particular, when
S=\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, one has
\mathcal{F}_{m,S}=U_{m}(LP)
.
Thus block compression is the full-fiber special case of fiber quotient.

Proper patterns
S\subsetneq\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
give additional candidates that are
supported only on selected fibers, with up to
2^{m}-1
candidate patterns for
each divisor
m
. If
M_{a,b}
denotes the APM for

x\mapsto ax+b\pmod{P}
, then in general

M_{a,b}\operatorname{im}(\phi_{m,S})\not\subseteq\operatorname{im}(\phi_{m,S}).

The method therefore restricts the original active checks to the image of
\Phi_{m,S,L}
:

H_{Z}^{(m,S)}:=H_{Z}\Phi_{m,S,L},\qquad H_{X}^{(m,S)}:=H_{X}\Phi_{m,S,L}.

These are ordinary binary matrices with
LQ
columns. They exactly encode the
parity-check products of lifted fiber-pattern vectors.

Lemma 5.6

(Check restriction for fiber quotients)
.

For every
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LQ}
and

\bm{x}=\Phi_{m,S,L}(\bar{\bm{x}})
,

H_{Z}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=0\iff H_{Z}^{(m,S)}\bar{\bm{x}}^{\mathsf{T}}=0.

Similarly, for every
\bar{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LQ}
and

\bm{z}=\Phi_{m,S,L}(\bar{\bm{z}})
,

H_{X}\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}=0\iff H_{X}^{(m,S)}\bar{\bm{z}}^{\mathsf{T}}=0.

Moreover,

\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{m,S,L}(\bm{y}))=|S|\,\mathrm{wt}(\bm{y})\qquad(\bm{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LQ}).

∎

Proof.

The first two equivalences are just the definitions of

H_{Z}^{(m,S)}
and
H_{X}^{(m,S)}
as restrictions of the original active-check
matrices to the image of
\Phi_{m,S,L}
.
For the weight identity, the sets

\{t+kQ:\ k\in S\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{P}

are disjoint for distinct quotient coordinates
t
, and different block
columns are disjoint as well. Hence every nonzero quotient coordinate
contributes exactly
|S|
ones after lifting.
∎

Proposition 5.7

(Fiber-quotient special case)
.

Let
m\mid P
,
Q=P/m
, and
\emptyset\neq S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.
If

\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z}^{(m,S)})\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{x}:=\Phi_{m,S,L}(\bar{\bm{x}})\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{X}),

then
\bm{x}
is a valid
X
logical representative and

d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})=|S|\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{x}}).

If in addition
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, then

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq|S|\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{x}})
.

Similarly, if

\bar{\bm{z}}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X}^{(m,S)})\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{z}:=\Phi_{m,S,L}(\bar{\bm{z}})\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z}),

then

d_{Z}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z})=|S|\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{z}}),

and the additional condition
\bm{z}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{Z})
gives

d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq|S|\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{z}})
.
∎

Proof.

We prove the
X
side. By Lemma
5.6
,

\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z}^{(m,S)})
implies

H_{Z}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=0
, so
\bm{x}\in C_{Z}
.
If
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})=C_{X}^{\perp}
, then
\bm{x}
is a nontrivial
X
logical
representative. Therefore the CSS distance definition gives

d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
, and the weight identity in the lemma gives

\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})=|S|\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{x}})
.
The non-latent statement follows from
Definition
2.1
after adding

\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
. The
Z
side is identical.
∎

This completes the fiber-quotient special case of the restricted-lift
upper-bound method. The practical selection of patterns and quotient
vectors is discussed only in Section
7
.

5.4
CRT-Compression Special Case

CRT-compression restricts the search space to a stripe subspace coming from CRT
coordinates. If
P=q_{1}q_{2}
with
\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})=1
, then

\mathbb{Z}_{P}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q_{2}},\qquad t\longmapsto(t\bmod q_{1},\ t\bmod q_{2}).

Definition 5.8

(CRT stripe subspace)
.

Assume that
P=q_{1}q_{2}
and
\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})=1
.
Define the single-block CRT stripe subspace by

\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{q_{1},q_{2}}:=\operatorname{span}\!\Bigl(\{\mathbf{1}_{\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{P}:\,x\equiv r\!\!\!\pmod{q_{1}}\}}:0\leq r<q_{1}\}\cup\{\mathbf{1}_{\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{P}:\,x\equiv s\!\!\!\pmod{q_{2}}\}}:0\leq s<q_{2}\}\Bigr)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P}.

The full-length CRT stripe subspace is then the blockwise direct sum

\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{crt}}_{q_{1},q_{2}}:=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{L-1}\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{q_{1},q_{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LP}.

∎

This definition gives a linear subspace larger than the block-constant subspace
and smaller than the full ambient space. CRT-compression checks the kernel and
row-space conditions inside this subspace.

Proposition 5.9

(CRT-compression special case)
.

Assume that
P=q_{1}q_{2}
and
\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})=1
.
Define

u_{X}^{\mathrm{crt}}(q_{1},q_{2}):=\min\left\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x}):\bm{x}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{crt}}_{q_{1},q_{2}}\cap\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})\right\},

u_{Z}^{\mathrm{crt}}(q_{1},q_{2}):=\min\left\{\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z}):\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{crt}}_{q_{1},q_{2}}\cap\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z})\right\},

with the convention that the minimum is
+\infty
when the corresponding set is empty.
Then
d_{X}\leq u_{X}^{\mathrm{crt}}(q_{1},q_{2})
and

d_{Z}\leq u_{Z}^{\mathrm{crt}}(q_{1},q_{2})
.

In particular, if

\bm{x}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{crt}}_{q_{1},q_{2}}\cap\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})
,
then
d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
. If in addition

\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, then also

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
.
Likewise, if

\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{crt}}_{q_{1},q_{2}}\cap\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z})
,
then
d_{Z}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z})
. If moreover

\bm{z}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{Z})
, then

d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z})
.
∎

Proof.

We treat the
X
side. Set

\mathcal{W}_{X}(q_{1},q_{2}):=\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{crt}}_{q_{1},q_{2}}\cap\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{X}).

Every vector
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{W}_{X}(q_{1},q_{2})
is, by construction, a valid

X
-type non-stabilizer logical representative. Therefore the CSS definition
implies
d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
for every
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{W}_{X}(q_{1},q_{2})
.
If the set is nonempty, taking the minimum over it yields

d_{X}\leq\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{W}_{X}(q_{1},q_{2})}\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})=u_{X}^{\mathrm{crt}}(q_{1},q_{2}).

If the set is empty, the convention
u_{X}^{\mathrm{crt}}(q_{1},q_{2})=+\infty

makes the inequality trivial.

If in addition
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, then

\bm{x}\in C_{Z}\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}
and
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
,
so Definition
2.1
gives

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
.
The
Z
side is identical.
∎

This completes the CRT-compression special case of the restricted-lift
upper-bound method.

6
Additional Witness-Based Upper Bounds

This section treats bounds that do not use a restricted lift. The search
procedure may generate candidate vectors or supports in any way. The
upper-bound statement starts only after the resulting vector is checked to lie
in the appropriate parity-check kernel and outside the stabilizer row space.

6.1
The Direct CSS-Search Upper Bound

The direct CSS-search bound records the smallest weight among a finite set of
already verified CSS logical vectors.

Proposition 6.1

(Direct CSS-search upper bound)
.

Let

\mathcal{W}_{X}\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{X}),\qquad\mathcal{W}_{Z}\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z})

be any finite candidate sets.
If they are nonempty, define

u_{X}^{\mathrm{dir}}:=\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{W}_{X}}\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x}),\qquad u_{Z}^{\mathrm{dir}}:=\min_{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{W}_{Z}}\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z}).

Then
d_{X}\leq u_{X}^{\mathrm{dir}}
and
d_{Z}\leq u_{Z}^{\mathrm{dir}}
.

In particular, if
\bm{x}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})
, then

d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
. Likewise, if

\bm{z}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z})
, then

d_{Z}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z})
.
Moreover, if some
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{W}_{X}
also satisfies

\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, then

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
, and analogously

\bm{z}\in\mathcal{W}_{Z}
with
\bm{z}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{Z})
implies

d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z})
.
∎

Proof.

We treat the
X
side. Every vector in
\mathcal{W}_{X}
belongs to

\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})
, so every such vector is already an
X
-type
non-stabilizer logical representative. Therefore

d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
for every
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{W}_{X}
.
If
\mathcal{W}_{X}
is nonempty, taking the minimum over the sampled candidate
set gives

d_{X}\leq\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{W}_{X}}\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})=u_{X}^{\mathrm{dir}}.

The one-vector statement is the special case
\mathcal{W}_{X}=\{\bm{x}\}
.

If moreover
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, then

\bm{x}\in C_{Z}\setminus C_{X}^{\perp}
and
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
,
so Definition
2.1
yields

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{nlat})}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
.
The
Z
side is identical.
∎

The proposition follows directly from the CSS
definition. Any procedure may produce the candidate sets

\mathcal{W}_{X},\mathcal{W}_{Z}
, but the upper-bound claim begins only after the
CSS membership and non-stabilizer conditions have been checked.

6.2
The Cycle-8 ETS Upper Bound

All codes considered here have girth
8
, so cycle-8 structures provide the
local supports used below. We use trapping set terminology
following
[
10
,
9
]
, only to identify
support vectors in the parity-check kernel.

Definition 6.2

(Cycle-8-connected ETS)
.

Let
H\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m\times n}
and let
G(H)
be the corresponding Tanner graph. For a variable-node set
S\subseteq V(H)
, define

\Gamma(S):=\{c\in C(H):N(c)\cap S\neq\emptyset\},\qquad d_{S}(c):=|N(c)\cap S|.

Define the odd-check boundary by

\partial S:=\{c\in\Gamma(S):d_{S}(c)\equiv 1\pmod{2}\}.

If every
c\in\Gamma(S)
satisfies

d_{S}(c)\in\{1,2\}

and the induced subgraph on
S\cup\Gamma(S)
is connected, then
S
is an ETS of type
(a,b)
, where

a:=|S|,\qquad b:=|\partial S|.

If, in addition,
S
can be written as a union of simple
8
-cycles such that each new cycle shares at least one variable node or check node with the previous union, then
S
is called a
cycle-8-connected ETS
.
∎

Figure
1
recalls representative
cycle-8-connected ETS patterns from
[
15
]
. These patterns are
examples rather than part of the definition: the definition only requires that
the support be grown by adding one 8-cycle at a time while maintaining overlap
with the union already constructed.

(6,2)
ETS (two connected 8-cycles)

(12,2)
ETS (five connected 8-cycles)

(8,2)
ETS (path-4 attachment)

Figure 1:
Representative cycle-8-connected ETS patterns used in the library
of
[
15
]
. White circles are variable nodes, light squares are even
checks, and dark squares are odd checks.

For girth
8
, cycle-based local support candidates start from
8
-cycles. The
paper uses this class only to state the
parity-check-kernel and distance-upper-bound implications below.

Lemma 6.3

(The odd-check boundary equals the induced check vector)
.

For any
S\subseteq V(H)
, let
\mathbf{1}_{S}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be its indicator vector. Then

H\,\mathbf{1}_{S}^{\mathsf{T}}=\mathbf{1}_{\partial S}^{\mathsf{T}}.

In particular, if
S
is an ETS of type
(a,b)
, then the induced check vector has weight exactly
b
.
∎

Proof.

Each component of
H\mathbf{1}_{S}^{\mathsf{T}}
is the parity of the number of neighbors of the corresponding check node inside
S
, namely the parity of
d_{S}(c)
. This is
1
exactly when
c\in\partial S
.
∎

This lemma identifies the graph-theoretic boundary with a parity-check product.
The two propositions below use only the cases needed for the upper
bound:
b=0
gives a support vector in
\mathrm{Ker}(H)
, and two
b=2
ETSs with the
same boundary give a kernel vector by a single symmetric difference.

Proposition 6.4

(Classical upper bounds from cycle-8-connected ETSs with
b=0
)
.

If
S
is a cycle-8-connected ETS of type
(a,0)
, then

\mathbf{1}_{S}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H)
,
hence the corresponding classical code
C(H)=\mathrm{Ker}(H)
satisfies
d(C(H))\leq a
.
∎

Proof.

By Lemma
6.3
,

\partial S=\emptyset
implies
H\,\mathbf{1}_{S}^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.
∎

Proposition 6.5

(Classical upper bounds from pairs of
(a,2)
ETSs with the same boundary)
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be cycle-8-connected ETSs of types
(a_{1},2)
and
(a_{2},2)
, respectively. If

\partial S_{1}=\partial S_{2}
and
S_{1}\triangle S_{2}\neq\emptyset
,
then

\mathbf{1}_{S_{1}}+\mathbf{1}_{S_{2}}=\mathbf{1}_{S_{1}\triangle S_{2}}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H)
,
and therefore
d(C(H))\leq|S_{1}\triangle S_{2}|\leq a_{1}+a_{2}
.
∎

Proof.

Lemma
6.3
gives

H\,\mathbf{1}_{S_{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}=\mathbf{1}_{\partial S_{i}}^{\mathsf{T}}
for
i=1,2
.
Since
\partial S_{1}=\partial S_{2}
, adding the two equations over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
yields

H(\mathbf{1}_{S_{1}}+\mathbf{1}_{S_{2}})^{\mathsf{T}}=0
.
The sum of indicator vectors equals the indicator vector of the symmetric difference. The
assumption
S_{1}\triangle S_{2}\neq\emptyset
guarantees that this codeword is nonzero.
∎

To use the following corollary, one converts a cycle-8 ETS support into a
support vector, checks membership in the relevant parity-check kernel and
non-membership in the opposite row space, and uses the support size as the
upper bound.

Corollary 6.6

(CSS upper bounds from cycle-8-connected ETS witnesses)
.

Let
H=H_{Z}
, and let
\bm{x}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z})
be a nonzero support vector obtained from Proposition
6.4
or Proposition
6.5
. If
\bm{x}\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})
, then
\bm{x}
is a valid
X
logical representative and
d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})
.
Similarly, if
\bm{z}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X})\setminus\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z})
is a nonzero support vector obtained on the
Z
side, then
d_{Z}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\bm{z})
.
∎

Proof.

This is immediate from the CSS distance definitions together with
\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})=C_{X}^{\perp}
and
\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z})=C_{Z}^{\perp}
.
∎

6.3
The Decoder-Failure Upper Bound

The decoder-failure bound uses a residual produced by a failed
decoding event. For a true error
\mathbf{e}
and a syndrome-matched estimate

\hat{\mathbf{e}}
, set

\mathbf{\Delta}=\mathbf{e}+\hat{\mathbf{e}}.

If this residual is pure
X
or pure
Z
and is outside the corresponding
stabilizer row space, its weight is an upper bound.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\mathbf{e}
be the true error and
\hat{\mathbf{e}}
an estimated error
with the same observed syndrome, and set the residual to

\mathbf{\Delta}=\mathbf{e}+\hat{\mathbf{e}}
.
If
\mathbf{\Delta}
is pure
X
-type and
\mathbf{\Delta}\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{X})
,
then
d_{X}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{\Delta})
.
Likewise, if
\mathbf{\Delta}
is pure
Z
-type and

\mathbf{\Delta}\notin\mathrm{Row}(H_{Z})
, then
d_{Z}\leq\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{\Delta})
.
∎

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{e}
and
\hat{\mathbf{e}}
produce the same syndrome, the
residual
\mathbf{\Delta}
has zero check syndrome.
Hence, if it is pure
X
-type then
\mathbf{\Delta}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z})
, and if it is
pure
Z
-type then
\mathbf{\Delta}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X})
.
By assumption it lies outside the corresponding stabilizer row space, so it is
a nontrivial logical representative and its weight upper-bounds the
corresponding logical distance.
∎

In the present paper, Proposition
6.7
is used
only to state the implication from a checked decoder residual to a distance
upper bound.

Discussion and Outlook

This paper organizes upper-bound witnesses, not a general lower bound
for the full CSS distance. Appendix A gives exact certification only for the
latent part. Existing quantum-LDPC lower-bound
techniques
[
33
,
28
,
22
,
3
]
and classical
minimum-weight algorithms
[
21
,
31
,
2
,
6
,
23
]

do not directly match the active/latent decomposition and stabilizer quotient
used here.

The current strategy is therefore to find smaller
upper-bound witnesses whenever possible. For each code, several independent
witness classes are checked, and the smallest value passing the stated tests is recorded. The supplementary website is a live
table of the current best values
[
18
]
; it is still being updated
as new searches finish. This use of a separately updated parameter table is
similar in role to the classical and quantum code tables maintained
in
[
7
]
.

The data currently available suggest that the best recorded upper bound grows
roughly linearly with blocklength over the explored range. This observation
should be read cautiously. The computational cost also grows with the
blocklength, so larger instances receive fewer effective samples. For distances
below about
30
, belief-propagation (BP) decoding experiments
in related finite-length quantum-LDPC studies
[
12
,
13
,
19
]
often return
low-weight logical residuals directly. Beyond that scale, BP residual witnesses
become much harder to obtain. The fact that the large-scale decoding
experiments have not produced failures corresponding to very small logical
errors is only negative evidence, not a proof that the true minimum distance is large. In
particular, the true minimum distance of this family could still plateau around
weight
30
.

Appendix A
Exact Latent Lower Bounds via Block-Constant Compression

\bar{d}^{\mathrm{lat}}
becomes exact when block-constant structure gives a matching lower bound.
We first recall the two maps used throughout this appendix:

\pi_{m,L}:U_{m}(LP)\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LQ},\qquad\iota_{m,L}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{LQ}\to U_{m}(LP).

For the principal family
(J,L)=(3,12)
, these maps are the compression and lift
between
U_{m}(12P)
and
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{12Q}
. The theorem below states that, under a
block-constant kernel hypothesis, every latent candidate descends to the
compressed image.

Theorem A.1

(Exact latent lower bounds under block-constant kernel hypotheses)
.

Let
s:=L/2-J
and assume
P=mQ
. Suppose

\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}})\subseteq U_{m}(sP),\qquad\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X}\tilde{H}_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}})\subseteq U_{m}(sP).

In other words, every latent coefficient vector lying in the kernel of either
active–latent mixed product is blockwise
m
-block constant. Under these
hypotheses, the proof below shows that the following compressed latent images
are well defined:

\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{X}:=\pi_{m,L}(C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})),\qquad\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{Z}:=\pi_{m,L}(C_{X}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{Z})).

If every nonzero vector in
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
has weight at least
\tau_{X}
, and every nonzero vector in
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{Z}
has weight at least
\tau_{Z}
, then

d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})}\geq m\tau_{X},\qquad d_{Z}^{(\mathrm{lat})}\geq m\tau_{Z}.

∎

Proof.

It suffices to prove the
X
case. Let
\bm{x}\in C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
. Then

\bm{x}=\bm{\lambda}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{H}_{X},\qquad\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{sP}.

Equivalently,
\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\lambda}
.
Since
\bm{x}\in C_{Z}
,

0=H_{Z}\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\lambda},

so

\bm{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Ker}(H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}})\subseteq U_{m}(sP).

Lemma
5.2
therefore implies that
\bm{x}
is block constant blockwise, so
\bm{x}\in U_{m}(LP)
and

\bar{\bm{x}}:=\pi_{m,L}(\bm{x})\in\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{X}

is well defined. Moreover,

\bm{x}=\iota_{m,L}(\bar{\bm{x}}).

If
\bm{x}\neq 0
, then
\bar{\bm{x}}\neq 0
and

\mathrm{wt}(\bm{x})=m\,\mathrm{wt}(\bar{\bm{x}})\geq m\tau_{X}.

Every latent
X
-logical representative is a nonzero vector in
C_{Z}\cap\mathrm{Row}(\tilde{H}_{X})
, so
d_{X}^{(\mathrm{lat})}\geq m\tau_{X}
.
The
Z
case is identical with
\mathrm{Ker}(H_{X}\tilde{H}_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}})\subseteq U_{m}(sP)
.
∎

This theorem gives the lower-bound part of exact latent certification. Once the
kernel structure is known to be block constant, excluding low-weight vectors in
the quotient space proves the stated lower bound in the original code.

To make the kernel hypothesis checkable, one replaces it by a rank computation.

Lemma A.2

(Rank test for block-constant kernels)
.

Let
\bm{e}_{0},\dots,\bm{e}_{P-1}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{P}
. Define

B_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{(m-1)Q\times P}

to be the matrix whose rows are

\bm{e}_{t}+\bm{e}_{t+jQ}\qquad(0\leq t\leq Q-1,\ 1\leq j\leq m-1).

Then

U_{m}(P)=\mathrm{Ker}(B_{m}).

Moreover, for every binary matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r\times P}
,

\mathrm{Ker}(A)\subseteq U_{m}(P)\iff\mathrm{Row}(B_{m})\subseteq\mathrm{Row}(A)\iff\mathrm{rank}\!\begin{bmatrix}A\\
B_{m}\end{bmatrix}=\mathrm{rank}(A).

∎

Proof.

The defining equations of
U_{m}(P)
are the equalities
x_{t}=x_{t+jQ}
, equivalently

(\bm{e}_{t}+\bm{e}_{t+jQ})\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}=0.

Hence
U_{m}(P)=\mathrm{Ker}(B_{m})
. Over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
one has

(\mathrm{Ker}(A))^{\perp}=\mathrm{Row}(A),

so

\mathrm{Ker}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(B_{m})\iff\mathrm{Row}(B_{m})\subseteq\mathrm{Row}(A).

The row-space inclusion is equivalent to the displayed rank equality.
∎

The lemma rewrites the block-constant kernel condition as a linear-algebraic
rank condition. This gives a finite check for the hypothesis used in
Theorem
A.1
.

Corollary A.3

(Machine-readable form of the latent kernel hypothesis)
.

Let
s:=L/2-J
and define

B_{m}^{(s)}:=I_{s}\otimes B_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{s(m-1)Q\times sP}.

Then the kernel hypothesis of Theorem
A.1
is equivalent to the two binary rank equalities

\mathrm{rank}\!\begin{bmatrix}H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
B_{m}^{(s)}\end{bmatrix}=\mathrm{rank}(H_{Z}\tilde{H}_{X}^{\mathsf{T}}),\qquad\mathrm{rank}\!\begin{bmatrix}H_{X}\tilde{H}_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
B_{m}^{(s)}\end{bmatrix}=\mathrm{rank}(H_{X}\tilde{H}_{Z}^{\mathsf{T}}).

Therefore exact latent certification at block factor
m
follows from these two
rank checks together with an explicit latent witness and an UNSAT certificate
excluding lower-weight vectors in the compressed latent image.
∎
