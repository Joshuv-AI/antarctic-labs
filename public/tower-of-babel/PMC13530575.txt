Poster 98. HTO Provides Lower Two-Year TKA Conversion Risk Than UKA in All Ages
Authors: Reza Katanbaf, Monica Misch, Oliver Sax, Drew Saltzman, Anthony Velott, Craig Bennett
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:For patients with symptomatic unicompartmental knee osteoarthritis, high tibial osteotomy (HTO) and unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA) are well-established treatment options. Patient selection is multifactorial, with age serving as an important consideration but not the sole determinant. HTO is generally reserved for patients younger than 60 years due to concerns of higher revision rates, while UKA is more commonly favored in older patients with lower activity demands. Despite these trends, there is a paucity of large database studies from the United States confirming these age-related trends. The objective of this study was to compare rates of conversion to total knee arthroplasty (TKA) following HTO and UKA in patients ≤60 years and > 60 years of age.Methods:A national, all-payer database of over 170 million deidentified patient records was queried. Patients who had unicompartmental knee osteoarthritis and underwent HTO or UKA from January 1st, 2010, to December 31st, 2023 were selected. Patients who had a history of trauma, malignancy, or rheumatoid arthritis were excluded from the study. Patients were divided into two groups based on their respective procedure: HTO or UKA. A propensity score match was completed to control for patient factors and comorbidities, resulting in two equal groups of 4,706 patients. After matching, groups were divided further based on age. The primary outcome of interest was conversion to total knee arthroplasty (TKA) at 90 days, one year, and two years postoperatively. Odds ratios (OR) were calculated with significance defined as P < 0.05. Patients who underwent UKA and were < 60 years of age served as the control group.Results:In comparison to individuals under 60 years of age who underwent UKA, patients <= 60 years of age who underwent HTO had significantly decreased odds of TKA conversion at all time points (Odds Ratio (OR) 0.32, 0.19, and 0.19 at 90 days, one year, and two years, respectively; P <= 0.035). Patients > 60 years of age who underwent HTO also had decreased odds of TKA conversion at one year (OR 0.20, P < 0.001) and two years (OR 0.25, P < 0.001). At 90 days, the HTO cohort who was over 60 years old had similar TKA conversion rates as the control group (P > 0.05). Additionally, patients who underwent UKA had comparable TKA conversion rates for all age groups at all time intervals studied (P > 0.05).Conclusions:Patients in all age cohorts who underwent HTO had decreased rate of TKA conversion at one and two years postoperatively. These findings suggest HTO may have a protective effect against early conversion to TKA, regardless of patient age.
