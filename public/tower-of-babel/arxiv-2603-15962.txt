Notes on Bilinear Bessel Potentials
Ana Čolović Xinyu Gao University of Missouri

Abstract
In analogy with bilinear Riesz potentials, we introduce bilinear Bessel potentials and characterize their boundedness from L p × L q L^{p}\times L^{q} into Lebesgue and Lorentz spaces L r , α . L^{r,\alpha}. In several cases we identify the optimal Lorentz indices by constructing explicit counterexamples.

1
Introduction

Fractional integral operators are fundamental objects in harmonic analysis,
closely related to behavior of functions in Sobolev spaces. In the linear setting,
the mapping properties of Riesz and Bessel fractional integral operators, otherwise known as Riesz and Bessel potentials have long been established and appear, for example, in
[
9
]
. Bilinear Riesz potential was introduced by Grafakos in
[
4
]
. Bilinear fractional operators exhibit new structural phenomena, including subtle endpoint behavior, first observed in the works of Grafakos–Kalton (in
[
2
]
) and Kenig and Stein (in
[
7
]
).

Motivated by the theory of bilinear Riesz potentials we introduce and study
bilinear Bessel potentials
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N},
where
n\geq 1,
and let
0<s<n
.
We let
G_{s}
denote the Bessel kernel whose Fourier transform satisfies

\widehat{G_{s}}(\xi)=(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2})^{-s/2}.

The associated linear Bessel potential of order
s
is given by

J_{s}f(x)=(G_{s}*f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(x-y)\,f(y)\,dy,

while the bilinear Bessel potential is defined by

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(y)\,f(x-y)\,g(x+y)\,dy.

These operators may be viewed as bilinear analogues of the classical Bessel
potential, interpolating between bilinear convolution operators with
integrable kernels and bilinear fractional integrals.

The main goal of this paper is to characterize the boundedness of

\mathcal{J}_{s}
from

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into Lebesgue and Lorentz spaces.
Using scaling considerations and explicit counterexamples, we prove that
boundedness can only occur when

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}\leq\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q},

and that this range is sharp up to certain endpoint phenomena. In particular,
the critical line
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{s}{n}
fails for mapping into

L^{\infty}
, as shown in Section
7.1
. Within this region, we establish strong and
weak-type bounds, including Lorentz space refinements obtained via restricted
weak-type interpolation. In several cases, we prove that the Lorentz indices
are optimal by constructing concrete counterexamples. We refer to exponents satisfying

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}

as the
Lebesgue indices
, and those satisfying

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}

as the
fractional indices
.

2
Preliminaries and Background

In this section, we recall necessary preliminaries.

2.1
Lorentz spaces and tools

We work with Lorentz spaces, are defined as follows (see
[
5
]
, Section 1.4.2).

Definition 2.1

(Lorentz spaces)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space. For a measurable function
f
on
X
, define

d_{f}(\alpha)=\mu(\{|f|>\alpha\}),\qquad f^{*}(t)=\inf\{\alpha>0:\ d_{f}(\alpha)\leq t\}.

For
0<p<\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(X,\mu)
consists of
all measurable functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}<\infty,

where

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}=\begin{cases}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{1/p}f^{*}(t))^{q}\,\dfrac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q},&0<q<\infty,\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}f^{*}(t),&q=\infty.\end{cases}

We also use the following proposition, which appears as Proposition 1.4.9 on p. 53 of
[
5
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
0<p<\infty,

0<q\leq\infty,
the following formula holds:

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}=p^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}[d_{f}(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda]^{q}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(2.1)

with the usual modification when
q=\infty
.
The following inequality represents an extension of Young’s inequality in Lorentz spaces. It was proved by O’Neil and can be found in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(O’Neil’s inequality)
Let
f\in L^{p,\alpha_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

g\in L^{q,\alpha_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
with
1<p,q,r<\infty,
and
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq\infty
such that

\frac{1}{r}+1=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\qquad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}},

and
\alpha>0.

Then
f*g\in L^{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
and

\|f*g\|_{L^{r,\alpha}}\leq 3r\|f\|_{L^{p,\alpha_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{q,\alpha_{2}}}.

2.2
Properties of Bessel Kernels

Combining Proposition 6.1.5 on p. 6 of
[
6
]
with the estimate on p. 133 of
[
9
]
, we obtain the pointwise estimates on the Bessel kernel, stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4

(Integrability of the Bessel kernel)
.

Let
0<s<n
, and let
G_{s}
denote the Bessel kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of order
s
.
Then there exist constants
C,c>0
(depending only on
n
and
s
) such that

G_{s}(x)\leq C\,|x|^{s-n}\quad\text{for }0<|x|\leq 1,\qquad G_{s}(x)\leq C\,e^{-c|x|}\quad\text{for }|x|\geq 1.

(2.2)

Consequently,

G_{s}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{for every}\quad 1\leq r<\frac{n}{n-s},

(2.3)

and

G_{s}\in L^{\frac{n}{n-s},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

3
Necessary Conditions and Exponent Geometry

We determine the possible range of exponents for boundedness of the bilinear
Bessel potentials using dimensional analysis.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
0<s<n
.
Fix
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
0<r<\infty
.

If there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}\,\|g\|_{L^{q}}\qquad\text{for all }f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

then necessarily

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{r}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}.

(3.1)

Proof.

Since
G_{s}\geq 0
and is not identically zero, there exists a
c_{0}>0
such that

c_{0}:=\int_{A}G_{s}(y)\,dy>0,\qquad\text{where }A:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:1/2\leq|y|\leq 1\}.

For
R\geq 3
, set
f_{R}=\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R)}
and
g_{R}=\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R)}
.
If
x\in B(0,R-2)
and
y\in A
, then

|x\pm y|\leq|x|+|y|\leq(R-2)+1<R.

Hence
f_{R}(x-y)=g_{R}(x+y)=1
for all
y\in A
, and
x\in B(0,R-2).
Therefore,

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{R},g_{R})(x)\ \geq\ \int_{A}G_{s}(y)\,dy\ =\ c_{0}\qquad\text{for all }x\in B(0,R-2).

By the definition of the weak-
L^{r}
quasi-norm,

\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{R},g_{R})\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\geq c_{0}\,|B(0,R-2)|^{1/r}\ \gtrsim\ R^{n/r}.

On the other hand,
\|f_{R}\|_{L^{p}}\simeq R^{n/p}
and
\|g_{R}\|_{L^{q}}\simeq R^{n/q}
.
If the stated weak-type estimate held, we would obtain for all large
R

R^{n/r}\ \lesssim\ R^{n/p+n/q},

which forces
\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
.

To obtain the lower bound

\frac{1}{r}\geq\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n},

we choose nonnegative
f,g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
supported in
B(0,1)
and satisfying

f\equiv g\equiv 1
on
B(0,1/2)
. For
\lambda\geq 1
define the
L^{p}
- and
L^{q}
-normalized dilates

f_{\lambda}(x):=\lambda^{n/p}f(\lambda x),\qquad g_{\lambda}(x):=\lambda^{n/q}g(\lambda x),

so that
\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}
and
\|g_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}}=\|g\|_{L^{q}}
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy
|x|\leq\frac{1}{4\lambda}
and let

y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|y|\leq\frac{1}{4\lambda}
.
Then

|x\pm y|\leq|x|+|y|\leq\frac{1}{2\lambda}
.

By the choice of
f
and
g
, this implies

f_{\lambda}(x-y)=\lambda^{n/p},\qquad g_{\lambda}(x+y)=\lambda^{n/q}.

Using the lower bound
G_{s}(y)\gtrsim|y|^{s-n}
for
|y|\leq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{\lambda},g_{\lambda})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(y)\,f_{\lambda}(x-y)\,g_{\lambda}(x+y)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|y|\leq 1/(4\lambda)}G_{s}(y)\,f_{\lambda}(x-y)\,g_{\lambda}(x+y)\,dy

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda^{n/p+n/q}\int_{|y|\leq 1/(4\lambda)}|y|^{s-n}\,dy\simeq\lambda^{n/p+n/q}\cdot\lambda^{-s}=\lambda^{\,n/p+n/q-s}.

Hence, the superlevel set

E_{\lambda}:=\Bigl\{x:\ \mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{\lambda},g_{\lambda})(x)\gtrsim\lambda^{n/p+n/q-s}\Bigr\}

contains a ball of radius
\simeq\lambda^{-1}
, and so
|E_{\lambda}|\gtrsim\lambda^{-n}
.
By the definition of the weak-
L^{r}
quasi-norm,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{\lambda},g_{\lambda})\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\

\displaystyle\gtrsim\ \lambda^{n/p+n/q-s}\,|E_{\lambda}|^{1/r}\ \gtrsim\ \lambda^{n/p+n/q-s}\cdot(\lambda^{-n})^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\ \lambda^{\,n/p+n/q-s-n/r}.

Therefore,
letting
\lambda\to\infty
, the bound

\lambda^{\,n/p+n/q-s-n/r}\lesssim 1

forces

\frac{1}{r}\geq\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4
Strong-Type Lebesgue Bounds

4.1
L^{p}\times L^{q}\to L^{r}

To treat the Lebesgue range

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r},\qquad p,q\geq 1,

we recall a version of a theorem of Grafakos and Soria (in
[
3
]
).
Given a nonnegative regular Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

T_{\mu}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-y)g(x-z)\,d\mu(y,z).

Theorem 4.1

(Grafakos–Soria
[
3
]
)
.

Let
1/p+1/q=1/r\leq 1
. For nonnegative
\mu
, if
\mu
is a finite measure, the following hold:

T_{\mu}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

T_{\mu}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
s<n
and
1/p+1/q=1/r\leq 1
. Then the bilinear Bessel potential satisfies

\mathcal{J}_{s}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
G_{s}
be the Bessel kernel and define
\mu(y,z)=\delta_{0}(y+z)G_{s}(y)
, where
\delta_{0}
is Dirac delta function. Then

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)=\int f(x-y)g(x-z)\,d\mu(y,z).

Since
G_{s}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
G_{s}\geq 0
,
\mu
is finite, and the claim follows from Theorem
4.1
.∎

4.2
L^{1}\times L^{1}\to L^{\frac{1}{2}}

The following lemma proves the
L^{1}\times L^{1}\to L^{\frac{1}{2}}
boundedness of the Bessel operators, allowing us to extend Corollary
4.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The operator
\mathcal{J}_{s}
is bounded between
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

For
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define

B_{k}(f,g)\coloneq\int_{|y|<2^{-k}}f(x-y)g(x+y)\,dy.

From Lemma 5(iii) of
[
7
]
, we know that

\|B_{k}(f,g)\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim 2^{-nk}\|f\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{1}}.

We decompose
\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x),
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the following way:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{-k-1}<|y|\leq 2^{-k}}|f(x-y)||g(x+y)|G_{s}(y)\,dy.

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k<0}e^{-\frac{2^{-k}}{4}}B_{k}(|f|,|g|)(x)+\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{(-k(s-n))}B_{k}(|f|,|g|)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\,B_{k}(|f|,|g|),

where,

a_{k}=\begin{cases}2^{k(n-s)}&k\geq 0\\
e^{-\frac{2^{-k}}{4}}&k<0.\end{cases}

Since we can pass the power
\frac{1}{2}
inside the sum, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)|^{\frac{1}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\,B_{k}(|f|,|g|)(x)\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}B_{k}(|f|,|g|)(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|B_{k}(|f|,|g|)\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{\frac{-nk}{2}}\|f\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}\|g\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{\frac{-nk}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{k(\frac{n-s}{2}-\frac{n}{2})}+\sum_{k<0}e^{-\frac{2^{-k}}{4}}2^{\frac{-nk}{2}}\right).

The first summand converges since it is a geometric series and the second summand converges since the limit of the subsequent terms is equal to 0. ∎

Remark 4.4
.

Combining the two results established in this section and applying the interpolation theorem (see
[
6
]
, Proposition 7.2.1), we obtain that for all
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
satisfying

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r},

the operator
\mathcal{J}_{s}
extends boundedly as

\mathcal{J}_{s}:\ L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

5
Lorentz Space Estimates on the Fractional Index Surface

5.1
L^{1}\times L^{q}\longrightarrow L^{r_{1},\alpha}
for
1<q<\infty

Kenig and Stein proved the following result on the boundedness of bilinear Riesz potentials
\mathcal{I}_{s}
(see
[
7
]
, Theorem 2, p. 4).

Lemma 5.1

(
L^{p}\times L^{1}
and
L^{1}\times L^{q}
Lorentz boundedness, Kenig–Stein (1999))
.

Let
0<s<n
.

1.

Suppose

1<p<\infty,\qquad\frac{1}{p}+1=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{s}{n}.

Then, for every
\alpha_{1}\geq p
,

\mathcal{I}_{s}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{r_{1},\alpha_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is bounded. In particular, there exists
C>0
such that

\|\mathcal{I}_{s}(f,g)\|_{L^{r_{1},\alpha_{1}}}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}\,\|g\|_{L^{1}}.

Moreover, the same bounds hold after interchanging the two input spaces.

2.

Let
0<s<n
, and set

r_{**}=\frac{n}{2n-s}.

Then the bilinear Riesz potential operator
\mathcal{I}_{s}
satisfies

\mathcal{I}_{s}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{r_{**},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

More precisely, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\mathcal{I}_{s}(f,g)\|_{L^{r_{**},\infty}}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{1}}\,\|g\|_{L^{1}},\qquad f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Remark 5.2
.

Recall that the Bessel kernel satisfies

0\leq G_{s}(x)\leq C\,|x|^{s-n}=C\,I_{s}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Consequently, for all
f,g\geq 0
,

|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)|\leq C\,\mathcal{I}_{s}(|f|,|g|)(x).

(5.1)

By replacing
f,g
with
|f|,|g|
, the same inequality holds for general
f,g
.
Since the bounds (
1
) and (
2
) have been shown to hold for the bilinear Riesz potential operator,
the corresponding bounds hold for the operator
\mathcal{J}_{s}
.

5.2
Interior of the Fractional Index Plane

In this subsection we prove interpolation results that characterize the boundedness of the bilinear operator for indices on the Fractional Index plane, as defined in the following theorem.

We first introduce the following useful theorem (see Theorem 7.2.2 in
[
6
]
).

Theorem 5.3

(Lorentz boundedness on the interpolation surface via three-point restricted weak type)
.

Let
0<s<n
and let
T
be a bilinear operator which is quasi-sublinear in each variable.
Assume that
T
is of restricted weak type
(p_{k},q_{k},r_{k})
for
k=0,1,2
, i.e.
there exist constants
C_{k}>0
such that for all measurable
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

of finite measure and all
\lambda>0
,

\lambda\,\bigl|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ |T(\mathbf{1}_{E},\mathbf{1}_{F})(x)|>\lambda\}\bigr|^{1/r_{k}}\leq C_{k}\,|E|^{1/p_{k}}\,|F|^{1/q_{k}},

(5.2)

and assume that the points
(p_{k},q_{k},r_{k}),
for
k=1,2,3
are not collinear.

Let
(p,q,r)
satisfy
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
0<r<\infty
.
Assume moreover that the point
\Bigl(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)
lies in the (relative) interior of the convex hull of the three points

\Bigl(\frac{1}{p_{k}},\frac{1}{q_{k}},\frac{1}{r_{k}}\Bigr)
, for
k=0,1,2
.

Then for every
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq\infty
and
\alpha
defined by

\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}},\qquad\frac{1}{\infty}:=0,

there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|T(f,g)\|_{L^{r,\alpha}}\leq C\,C_{0}^{\theta_{0}}C_{1}^{\theta_{1}}C_{2}^{\theta_{2}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p,\alpha_{1}}}\,\|g\|_{L^{q,\alpha_{2}}},

where
\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(0,1)
are the barycentric coordinates of

(1/p,1/q,1/r)
in the simplex spanned by the three endpoints, i.e.

\theta_{0}+\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}=1,\qquad\frac{1}{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{\theta_{k}}{p_{k}},\quad\frac{1}{q}=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{\theta_{k}}{q_{k}},\quad\frac{1}{r}=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{\theta_{k}}{r_{k}}.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
0<s<n
,
1<p,q<\infty
and define

\frac{1}{r}:=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n},

assuming
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}>\frac{s}{n}.

Then there exists an exponent
p_{0}\in(1,n/s),

such that the target exponent triple

\Bigl(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)

lies in the
relative interior
of the convex hull of three restricted bounded endpoint triples of
\mathcal{J}_{s}
.
Consequently, Theorem
5.3
applies to yield for every
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq\infty
,

\mathcal{J}_{s}:\ L^{p,\alpha_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q,\alpha_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}.

Proof.

Choose
p_{0}\in(1,n/s)
such that

\max\Bigl\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{s}{n}\Bigr\}<\frac{1}{p_{0}}<\min\Bigl\{1,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\Bigr\}.

(5.3)

Define

P_{0}=\Bigl(1,1,2-\frac{s}{n}\Bigr),\quad P_{1}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{p_{0}},0,\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{s}{n}\Bigr),\quad P_{2}=\Bigl(0,\frac{1}{p_{0}},\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{s}{n}\Bigr).

By Lemma
5.1
(
2
) together with the pointwise domination
(
5.1
), the operator
\mathcal{J}_{s}
satisfies weak type bound. Hence
\mathcal{J}_{s}
is of restricted weak type at
P_{0}
.
By Lemma
6.2
, the operator
\mathcal{J}_{s}
satisfies the restricted weak-type bounds at
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.

Set

\theta_{0}:=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\right)-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}{2-\frac{1}{p_{0}}},\qquad\theta_{1}:=\frac{\frac{1}{p}-\theta_{0}}{\frac{1}{p_{0}}},\qquad\theta_{2}:=\frac{\frac{1}{q}-\theta_{0}}{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

A direct computation gives

\theta_{0}+\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}=1
.

By (
5.3
),

\frac{1}{p_{0}}<\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
, hence
\theta_{0}>0
.
Moreover,
\frac{1}{p_{0}}>\frac{1}{q}
implies
\theta_{0}<\frac{1}{p}
, so

\theta_{1}>0
. By symmetry,
\theta_{2}>0
.

The first coordinate equals
\frac{1}{p}
, the second equals
\frac{1}{q}
, and

2\theta_{0}+\frac{1}{p_{0}}(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
.

Hence the third coordinate equals

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}=\frac{1}{r}
.
Therefore,

\theta_{0}P_{0}+\theta_{1}P_{1}+\theta_{2}P_{2}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r}\Bigr),

and by Theorem
5.3
, the conclusion follows.
∎

6
Between Two Planes

In this section, we investigate the boundedness of the bilinear Bessel operator in the case when indices can be placed between the Lebesgue and the Fractional Index Plane, and classify the behaviour at relevant endpoints.

6.1
p=\infty
or
q=\infty

We first recall the bounds for the linear Bessel potential, which can be found in the work of Aronszajn and Smith (see
[
1
]
, Section 10, p. 470).

Theorem 6.1

(Bounds for Bessel potentials, Aronszajn and Smith, 1961)
.

Let
0<s<n
. Then the linear Bessel potential satisfies:

1.

For every
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\|J_{s}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|G_{s}\|_{L^{1}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}};

2.

Assume

1\leq p<\frac{n}{s},\qquad\frac{1}{r_{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{n}\quad\Bigl(r_{*}=\frac{np}{n-sp}\Bigr).

Then

\|J_{s}f\|_{L^{r_{*},\infty}}\leq C_{n,s,p}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

By the pointwise domination, we immediately obtain:

|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)|\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\,(G_{s}*|f|)(x)=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\,(J_{s}|f|)(x).

As a consequence, we note the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
0<s<n
,
1\leq p<\frac{n}{s}
and set
r^{p}_{*}=\frac{np}{n-sp}
, i.e.

\frac{1}{r^{p}_{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{n}.

Then the following bounds hold:

\mathcal{J}_{s}:\;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{r^{p}_{*},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and

\mathcal{J}_{s}:\;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Moreover, the same bounds hold after interchanging the two input spaces.

6.2
Triangle

To complete the picture of boundedness, we next consider the boundedness of the operator into
L^{\infty}.

Lemma 6.3

(
L^{p}\times L^{q}\to L^{\infty}
bound below the critical line)
.

Let
0<s<n
and let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Assume

0\leq\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<\frac{s}{n}.

(6.1)

Then

\mathcal{J}_{s}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and set

h_{x}(y):=f(x-y)\,g(x+y)
.

Define
t\in[1,\infty]
by
\frac{1}{t}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
,
and let
t^{\prime}
be its conjugate exponent, with the convention
1/\infty=0
.
Then by Hölder’s inequality,

\|h_{x}\|_{L^{t}}\leq\|f(x-\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\,\|g(x+\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}\,\|g\|_{L^{q}},

uniformly in
x
. Hence,

|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(y)\,|h_{x}(y)|\,dy\leq\|G_{s}\|_{L^{t^{\prime}}}\,\|h_{x}\|_{L^{t}}\leq\|G_{s}\|_{L^{t^{\prime}}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}\,\|g\|_{L^{q}}.

Taking the supremum over
x
yields the claim. Indeed, by
Lemma
2.4
we have
G_{s}\in L^{t^{\prime}}
.
Note that (
6.1
) implies the inequality

t^{\prime}<\frac{n}{n-s}
.
Consequently, Lemma
2.4
applies and ensures that

G_{s}\in L^{t^{\prime}}
.
∎

6.3
Fixed-input interpolation

We have proved that the bilinear Bessel potential
\mathcal{J}_{s}
satisfies

\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\leq A\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}},\qquad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r_{1}},

and

\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)\|_{L^{r_{2},\infty}}\leq B\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}},\qquad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r_{2}}+\frac{s}{n}.

We extend the result to intermediate spaces, by interpolation.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
0<s<n
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
.
Then for every
0<\theta<1
and every
0<\alpha\leq\infty
,

\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)\|_{L^{r_{\theta},\alpha}}\lesssim A^{1-\theta}B^{\theta}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}},

where

\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{1}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{2}}.

Proof.

Let
h=\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)
.
By hypothesis,

\|h\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\leq A\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}},\qquad\|h\|_{L^{r_{2},\infty}}\leq B\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}.

Let
h^{*}
denote the decreasing rearrangement of
h
.

From Chebyshev’s inequality and the strong
L^{r_{1}}
bound,

h^{*}(t)\leq\|h\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\,t^{-1/r_{1}}\leq A\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}\,t^{-1/r_{1}},\qquad t>0.

Similarly, from the weak-type bound,

h^{*}(t)\leq\|h\|_{L^{r_{2},\infty}}\,t^{-1/r_{2}}\leq B\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}\,t^{-1/r_{2}},\qquad t>0.

Therefore,

h^{*}(t)\leq\min\Bigl(A\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}\,t^{-1/r_{1}},\;B\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}\,t^{-1/r_{2}}\Bigr).

Let
0<\theta<1
and define
r_{\theta}
by

\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{1}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{2}}
.

Let
t_{0}>0
be determined by

At_{0}^{-1/r_{1}}=Bt_{0}^{-1/r_{2}},\qquad t_{0}^{\,1/r_{2}-1/r_{1}}=\frac{A}{B}.

Assume first that
0<\alpha<\infty
.

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{r_{\theta},\alpha}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(t^{1/r_{\theta}}h^{*}(t)\bigr)^{\alpha}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\bigl(B\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}\,t^{1/r_{\theta}-1/r_{2}}\bigr)^{\alpha}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\bigl(A\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}\,t^{1/r_{\theta}-1/r_{1}}\bigr)^{\alpha}\frac{dt}{t}.

Since
r_{1}<r_{2}

\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}-\frac{1}{r_{2}}>0,\qquad\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}-\frac{1}{r_{1}}<0,

both integrals converge.

A direct computation gives

\|h\|_{L^{r_{\theta},\alpha}}^{\alpha}\leq C(A\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}})^{\alpha(1-\theta)}(B\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}})^{\alpha\theta},

where
C=C(n,s,p,q)
a constant determined only on
n,s,p,q
.
Taking
\alpha
-th roots yields

\|h\|_{L^{r_{\theta},\alpha}}\lesssim A^{1-\theta}B^{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}.

When
\alpha=\infty
, we use

\|h\|_{L^{r_{\theta},\infty}}=\sup_{t>0}t^{1/r_{\theta}}h^{*}(t),

together with the same pointwise bound.
The supremum is attained up to constants at
t\approx t_{0}
,
which gives the same estimate. ∎

7
Sharpness and Counterexamples

In this section, we construct concrete counterexamples to show the failure of boundedness of operators on a line we call a critical line. We then establish sharpness for previous fractional index bounds.

7.1
Failure on the critical line

We first prove failure of boundedness on the critical line, where for
0<s<n,
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{s}{n}.

Proposition 7.1

(Failure of
L^{p}\times L^{q}\to L^{\infty}
on the critical line)
.

Let
0<s<n
and let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
satisfy

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{s}{n},

(7.1)

excluding the trivial case
(p,q)=(\infty,\infty)
.
Then the bilinear Bessel potential

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(y)\,f(x-y)\,g(x+y)\,dy

does
not
define a bounded operator

\mathcal{J}_{s}:\ L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

We use the fact that
G_{s}
is nonnegative and satisfies the local lower bound

G_{s}(y)\geq c\,|y|^{s-n}\qquad(0<|y|<1)

(7.2)

for some
c>0
.

Case 1:
1<p,q<\infty
.

Choose
\beta,\gamma>0
such that

\beta p>1,\qquad\gamma q>1,\qquad\beta+\gamma\leq 1.

(This is possible since
1/p+1/q=s/n<1
, hence
1/p+1/q<1
and we may take

\beta\downarrow 1/p
and
\gamma\downarrow 1/q
so that
\beta+\gamma\leq 1
.)

Define

f(y):=|y|^{-n/p}\Bigl(\log\tfrac{e}{|y|}\Bigr)^{-\beta}\mathbf{1}_{|y|<1}(y),\qquad g(y):=|y|^{-n/q}\Bigl(\log\tfrac{e}{|y|}\Bigr)^{-\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{|y|<1}(y).

Then
f\in L^{p}
and
g\in L^{q}
. Evaluating at
x=0
and using
(
7.2
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(y)\,f(-y)\,g(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{|y|<1}|y|^{s-n}\,|y|^{-n/p}\,|y|^{-n/q}\Bigl(\log\tfrac{e}{|y|}\Bigr)^{-(\beta+\gamma)}\,dy.

Using (
7.1
), we have

s-n-n/p-n/q=-n
, and so

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(0)\geq c\int_{|y|<1}|y|^{-n}\Bigl(\log\tfrac{e}{|y|}\Bigr)^{-(\beta+\gamma)}\,dy.

In polar coordinates the expression is comparable to

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dr}{r\bigl(\log(e/r)\bigr)^{\beta+\gamma}},

which diverges since
\beta+\gamma\leq 1
. Hence
\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(0)=\infty
,
and we can conclude that
\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)\notin L^{\infty}
.

Case 2:
(p,q)=(n/s,\infty)
.

Suppose, for the sake of a contradiction that

\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{n/s}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Taking
g\equiv 1
gives

\|G_{s}*f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{n/s}}.

(7.3)

Fix
0<\rho<1/2
and set

f(y):=|y|^{-s}\Bigl(\log\tfrac{e}{|y|}\Bigr)^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{|y|<\rho}(y).

A direct polar-coordinate computation shows
f\in L^{n/s}
. On the other hand,
by (
7.2
),

\displaystyle(G_{s}*f)(0)

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{|y|<\rho}|y|^{s-n}\,|y|^{-s}\Bigl(\log\tfrac{e}{|y|}\Bigr)^{-1}\,dy

\displaystyle=c\int_{|y|<\rho}|y|^{-n}\Bigl(\log\tfrac{e}{|y|}\Bigr)^{-1}\,dy\sim\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{dr}{r\log(e/r)}=\infty,

contradicting (
7.3
). Thus the endpoint boundedness fails.

Case 3:
(p,q)=(\infty,n/s)
.

The contradiction follows by symmetry, i.e. by swapping the roles of
f
and
g
.∎

7.2
Sharp Lorentz indices

Next, we prove fractional index sharpness, in the cases given by the following lemma.

Lemma 7.2

(Sharpness of fractional index bounds)
.

Let
0<s<n.
For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,
let

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}.

(7.4)

The following hold:

1.

Let
1<p,q<\infty,

Then for every
0<\alpha,

\frac{1}{\alpha}>\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q},

\mathcal{J}_{s}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\not\to L^{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The bound
\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
is sharp in Lemma
5.4
.

2.

Let
1<p<\frac{n}{s}
, and fix
q\in\{1,\infty\}
. Define
r
by

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}.

Then for every
0<\alpha<p,

\mathcal{J}_{s}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\not\to L^{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In particular, the Lorentz index restrictions
\alpha\geq p
in the cases

q=\infty
(Lemma
6.2
) and
q=1
(Lemma
5.1
) are sharp.

3.

Let
p=1,

q=\infty.
Then for any
0<\alpha<\infty,

\mathcal{J}_{s}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\not\to L^{\frac{n}{n-s},\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Proof.

For
0<p,q,r<\infty,

\beta,\delta>0,
we define

\displaystyle f(x)\coloneq|x|^{-\frac{n}{p}}\log\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)\setminus\{0\}}(x);

(7.5)

\displaystyle h(x)\coloneq|x|^{-\frac{n}{r}}\log\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}\mathbf{1}_{B(0,\frac{1}{8})\setminus\{0\}}(x).

(7.6)

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we use
B(x,r)
to refer to the ball of radius
r,
centered at
x,
and for a set
A,
we use
\mathbf{1}_{A}
to be the indicator function of the set
A,
i.e. the function such that
\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)=1
if
x\in A,
and
\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)=0
otherwise.

(1)

1<p,q<\infty
:

Since
\frac{1}{\alpha}>\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q},
and
1<p,q<\infty,
we can find
0<\beta,\gamma<\infty
such that
\beta<p,

\gamma<q,
and
\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{\gamma}.
Let
f
be as in
7.5
, and let
h
be as in (
7.6
), with
\delta=\alpha.

Let

g(x)\coloneq|x|^{-\frac{n}{q}}\log\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)\setminus\{0\}}(x);

Since
\beta<p
and
\gamma<q
, it follows that
f\in L^{p}=L^{p,p}
and
g\in L^{q}=L^{q,q}
. However, because

\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{\gamma}=\frac{1}{\alpha},

the function
h
does not belong to
L^{r,\alpha}
(see Exercise 1.4.8 in
[
5
]
).

Let
|x|<\frac{1}{8},

x\neq 0.
The bilinear Bessel potential is given by

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(y)f(x-y)g(x+y)dy.

Using a change of variables, we have

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(x-y)f(y)g(2x-y)dy.

Since the integrand is nonnegative, we restrict to the region

E_{x}\coloneq\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<|y|<\frac{3}{2}|x|\},

(7.7)

where
|x|<\frac{1}{8}.

Note that for
y\in E_{x}
and
|x|<\frac{1}{8},

\displaystyle|x-y|,|x+y|\leq|x|+|y|<1;

\displaystyle|2x-y|\leq 2|x|+|y|<1.

Therefore,

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)\gtrsim\int_{E_{x}}|x-y|^{s-n}|y|^{-\frac{n}{p}}\log^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\left(\frac{e}{|y|}\right)|2x-y|^{-\frac{n}{q}}\log^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{e}{|2x-y|}\right)dy.

We observe that if
1>|z_{1}|\geq|z_{2}|,
and
a>0,
it follows that

\log^{-\frac{1}{a}}\left(\frac{e}{|z_{1}|}\right)\geq\log^{-\frac{1}{a}}\left(\frac{e}{|z_{2}|}\right).

(7.8)

For
x
such that
|x|<\frac{1}{8},
and
y\in E_{x},

|y|\geq|x|
and
|2x-y|\geq 2|x|-\frac{3}{2}|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|.
Therefore, by the equation (
7.8
), the following hold:

\displaystyle\log^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\left(\frac{e}{|y|}\right)\geq\log^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right);

(7.9)

\displaystyle\log^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{e}{|2x-y|}\right)\geq\log^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{2e}{|x|}\right)\gtrsim\log^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right).

(7.10)

For
y\in E_{x},
we have

\displaystyle|2x-y|^{-\frac{n}{q}}\gtrsim|y|^{-\frac{n}{q}};

(7.11)

\displaystyle|x-y|^{s-n}\gtrsim|y|^{s-n},

(7.12)

where the implied constants depend on
n,s
and
q.

Combining the equations (
7.9
), (
7.10
), (
7.11
) and (
7.12
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\log^{-(\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{\gamma})}\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right)\int_{E_{x}}|y|^{s-n(1+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}dy

\displaystyle\approx\log^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right)\int_{|x|}^{\frac{3}{2}|x|}\rho^{-\frac{n}{r}-1}d\rho

\displaystyle=\log^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right)|x|^{-\frac{n}{r}}\frac{r}{n}\left(1-\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{-\frac{n}{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\log^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{e}{|x|}\right)|x|^{-\frac{n}{r}},

where the fact that
1>\frac{2}{3}^{\frac{n}{r}},
implies the last inequality.

Hence, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
such that
x\neq 0
and
|x|<\frac{1}{8},

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)\geq h(x),
and since
h\notin L^{r,\alpha},
it follows that
\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)\notin L^{r,\alpha}
completing the proof in the case
1
.

(2)
:
1<p<\frac{n}{s}
and
q\in\{1,\infty\}:
Fix
0<\alpha<p
and let
f
be as in equations (
7.5
) and let
h
be as in (
7.6
), with
\delta=\alpha
, and let
g(x)\coloneq\mathbf{1}_{B(0,4)}(x).
Note that
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
q\in\{1,\infty\}
, and as before, since
\alpha<p,

f\in L^{p}=L^{p,p}.
The function
h
does not belong to
L^{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
whenever

0<\alpha<p
(see Exercise 1.4.8 in
[
5
]
).

The proof now follows similarly to the proof of item i). Namely, we observe that for
|x|<\frac{1}{8},
and
y\in E_{x}
defined as in (
7.7
),
|2x-y|<1<4,

and the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(x-y)f(y)g(2x-y)dy

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{E_{x}}|x-y|^{s-n}|y|^{-\frac{n}{p}}\log^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{e}{|y|}\right)dy.

Following the same reasoning as in item
1
, it follows that

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)(x)\gtrsim h(x)\quad\text{for }x\in B(0,1/8)\setminus\{0\}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{J}_{s}(f,g)\notin L^{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(3)
:
p=1,\ q=\infty
.

We argue by contradiction. Assume that for some
0<\alpha<\infty
,

\mathcal{J}_{s}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{\frac{n}{n-s},\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is bounded. Let
g\equiv 1
and, for
\varepsilon>0
, define

f_{\varepsilon}\coloneq\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}.

Then
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}=1
and
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}=1
, so by assumption,

\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{\varepsilon},1)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-s},\alpha}}\lesssim 1,\qquad\text{uniformly in }\varepsilon.

(7.13)

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{\varepsilon},1)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{s}(y)f_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy=(G_{s}*f_{\varepsilon})(x).

Fix
0<\varepsilon<\tfrac{1}{8}
and consider
x
such that

4\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
.
For any
y\in B(0,\varepsilon)
we have

|x-y|\leq|x|+\varepsilon\leq\tfrac{5}{4}|x|,\qquad|x-y|\geq|x|-\varepsilon\geq\tfrac{3}{4}|x|.

Since
|x-y|\leq 1
in this region and the Bessel kernel satisfies

G_{s}(z)\gtrsim|z|^{s-n}
for
|z|\leq 1
, it follows that

(G_{s}*f_{\varepsilon})(x)=\frac{1}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}G_{s}(x-y)\,dy\gtrsim\frac{1}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}|x-y|^{s-n}\,dy\gtrsim|x|^{s-n}.

Hence,

\mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{\varepsilon},1)(x)\gtrsim|x|^{s-n}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\,4\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\}}(x).

By monotonicity of Lorentz quasi-norms with respect to pointwise domination,

\|\mathcal{J}_{s}(f_{\varepsilon},1)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-s},\alpha}}\gtrsim\bigl\||x|^{s-n}\mathbf{1}_{\{\,4\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\}}\bigr\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-s},\alpha}}.

Let
r=\frac{n}{n-s}
. For
\lambda>0
, define

E_{\lambda}=\Bigl\{x:\ 4\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\ |x|^{s-n}>\lambda\Bigr\}.

For
\lambda\in[2^{\,n-s},(8\varepsilon)^{-(n-s)}]

(equivalently
\lambda^{-1/(n-s)}\in[8\varepsilon,\tfrac{1}{2}]
),
we have

E_{\lambda}=\Bigl\{x:\ 4\varepsilon\leq|x|<\lambda^{-1/(n-s)}\Bigr\},

and hence

|E_{\lambda}|=c_{n}\bigl(\lambda^{-r}-(4\varepsilon)^{n}\bigr)\gtrsim\lambda^{-r},

with constants independent of
\varepsilon
.
Indeed, for
\lambda\leq(8\varepsilon)^{-(n-s)}
one has

\lambda^{-1/(n-s)}\geq 8\varepsilon
, hence
\lambda^{-r}\geq(8\varepsilon)^{n}
.
Therefore

\lambda^{-r}-(4\varepsilon)^{n}\geq(8\varepsilon)^{n}-(4\varepsilon)^{n}=(1-2^{-n})(8\varepsilon)^{n}\geq(1-2^{-n})\,\lambda^{-r},

which shows that
|E_{\lambda}|\gtrsim\lambda^{-r}
with constants independent of

\varepsilon
.
Using the definition of the Lorentz quasi-norm,

\bigl\||x|^{s-n}\mathbf{1}_{\{\,4\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\}}\bigr\|_{L^{r,\alpha}}^{\alpha}\gtrsim\int_{2^{\,n-s}}^{(8\varepsilon)^{-(n-s)}}\Bigl(\lambda\,|E_{\lambda}|^{1/r}\Bigr)^{\alpha}\,\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\gtrsim\int_{2^{\,n-s}}^{(8\varepsilon)^{-(n-s)}}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\sim\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields

\bigl\||x|^{s-n}\mathbf{1}_{\{\,4\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\}}\bigr\|_{L^{r,\alpha}}\longrightarrow\infty,

which contradicts the assumed uniform bound in equation (
7.13
). This completes
the proof.
∎
