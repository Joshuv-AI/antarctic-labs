Bismut Formula for Intrinsic Derivative of DDSDEs with Singular Interactions The author is supported by NNSFC(12301180), RGC (21301925), NSFC/RGC JRS N-CityU165/25 and Research Centre for Nonlinear Analysis at Hong Kong PolyU.

Abstract
In recent years, remarkable progress has been made for Distribution dependent stochastic equations (DDSDEs) with singular interactions, existing results include well-posedness, propagation of chaos, entropy-cost inequality and ergodicity. As a continuation to the existing study, in this paper we establish Bismut type formulas for the intrinsic derivative of DDSDEs with singular interactions, which extends the existing formula established for the case with Lions differentiable drifts.



AMS subject Classification: 60B05, 60B10.

Keywords: Intrinsic formula, Bismut formula, Distribution dependent SDEs, singular interaction.

Introduction

Since 1996 when McKean
[
15
]
proposed a class of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with distribution dependent drifts to characterize nonlinear Fokker-Planck equations, distribution dependent SDEs (also called McKean-Vlasov SDEs) have been intensively investigated.
In recent years, some remarkable progress has been made for SDEs with singular interactions, where the drift contains convolutions of distribution with a singular kernel, which describes singular interaction
in the corresponding mean-field particle system, see
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
14
]
and reference therein for the study on well-posedness, propagation of chaos, regularity estimates, and ergodicity.

On the other hand, as a powerful tool characterizing the regularity of stochastic systems, the derivative formula initiated by Bismut
[
4
]
for the heat semigroup on manifolds
has been extensively studied and applied to various models. In recent years, Bismut type derivative formulas with respect to the initial distribution have been established for distribution dependent SDEs,
see
[
2
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
18
]
for the intrinsic/Lions derivative formulas, and see
[
16
]
for the extrinsic derivative formula.
However, the existing Bismut formula for the intrinsic derivative requires that the drift is either Lions differentiable in the distribution variable
[
18
]
,
or has half-Dini continuous extrinsic derivative
[
12
]
. These conditions excludes McKen-Vlasov SDEs with singular interactions, where the drift contains a term like

(1.1)

B_{t}(x,\mu)=(h_{t}*\mu)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h_{t}(x-y)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y),\ \ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \mu\in\hat{\mathscr{P}},

where

h:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

is measurable and singular in the spatial variable
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\hat{\mathscr{P}}
the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the convolution
with
h_{t}
is well-defined. Typical examples of
h_{t}
include vector fields satisfying

|h_{t}(x)|\leq\frac{ct^{\tau}}{|x|^{\beta}},\ \ \ x\neq 0,\ t\geq 0,

for some constants
\tau\geq 0,\beta\geq 0.
It is the case when

h_{t}(x)=\frac{ct^{\tau}x}{|x|^{\beta+1}},\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},\ t\geq 0,

for which
b_{t}(x,\mu)
is not intrinsically differentiable in
\mu
, and the extrinsic derivative is singular even if exists,
so that existing Bismut formula for the intrinsic derivative of distribution dependent SDEs do not work.

In this paper, we intend to establish the Bismut type derivative formula for the following McKean-VlSDE on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with singular interaction given by (
1.1
):

(1.2)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=\big(b_{t}(X_{t})+B_{t}(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\big)\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\ \ t\in[0,T],

where
T>0
is a fixed time,
W_{t}
is the
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on a probability base
(\Omega,\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
,
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
is the distribution of
X_{t}
,
and

b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ B:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\hat{\mathscr{P}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ \sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}

are measurable, where
\hat{\mathscr{P}}
is equipped with the weak topology. When different probability space are concerned, we use
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}|\mathbb{P}}
to denote the distribution of
X_{t}
under probability
\mathbb{P}
, and
let
\mathscr{L}_{X_{[0,T]}|\mathbb{P}}
be the distribution of the process

Definition 1.1
.

A continuous adapted process

which means that the (stochastic) integrals in the right hand side are well-defined such that the equation holds.

A couple

is called a weak solution of (
1.2
), if there exists a probability base such that
W_{t}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion and

solves (
1.2

We call (
1.2

\mu\in\mathscr{P}

with respect to probabilities
\mathbb{P}^{i},i=1,2

\mathscr{L}_{X_{[0,T]}^{1}|\mathbb{P}^{2}}=\mathscr{L}_{X_{[0,T]}^{2}|\mathbb{P}^{2}}

Let

be the set of all probability measures on

equipped with the weak topology. For any

The intrinsic derivative for measures was introduced in

1

[
2

for functions on the

For any

Then

Definition 1.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
f
be a continuous function on
\mathscr{P}_{p}

It is called intrinsically differentiable at a point

is a well defined bounded linear functional. In this case, the intrinsic derivative is the unique element

Df(\mu)\in T_{\mu,p}^{*}:=L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)

such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle Df(\mu)(x),\phi(x)\rangle\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=D_{\phi}f(\mu),\ \ \phi\in T_{\mu,p}.

In Section 2, we state the main result of the paper (Theorem 2.1), which provides a Bismut-type formula for the intrinsic derivative of McKean–Vlasov SDEs with singular interactions under assumption (H). In Section 3, we prove the first part of Theorem 2.1 (existence and moment estimate) by establishing well-posedness of the linearized derivative SDE (Lemma 3.1) and showing convergence of the solution process (Lemma 3.2). The estimates and techniques developed in Section 3 are then used in Section 4 to prove the second part of Theorem 2.1, namely the intrinsic Bismut formula (2.10) and its application to the semigroup (4.2).

\displaystyle I_{1}(\varepsilon):=\mathbb{E}\bigg[f(X_{t}^{\mu})\frac{1-R_{t}^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\bigg],\ \ I_{2}(\varepsilon):=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big(f(X_{t}^{\varepsilon})-f(X_{t}^{\mu})\big)\frac{1-R_{t}^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\bigg].

So, it remains to verify

(4.13)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}I_{1}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[f(X_{t}^{\mu})\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\zeta_{s}(X_{s})\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla h_{s}(z-X_{s}),\nabla_{\eta}X_{s}^{\mu}\rangle]|_{z=X_{s}},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\big\rangle\bigg],

(4.14)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}I_{2}(\varepsilon)=0,

By the definition of
R_{t}^{\varepsilon}
in (
4.4
) and the estimate (
4.5
), we may apply the dominated convergence theorem to derive

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{R_{t}^{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\zeta_{s}(X_{s})\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla h_{s}(z-X_{s}),\nabla_{\eta}X_{s}\rangle]|_{z=X_{s}},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\big\rangle

in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
(indeed in
L^{n}(\mathbb{P})
for any
n\in[1,\infty)
). So, (
4.13
) holds.

(b) Proof of (
4.14
). When
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it follows from (
4.5
) and the dominated convergence theorem. Below we prove
it for
f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by using a time-shift argument as in the proof of
[
18
, Proposition 4.3]
.

By
2\kappa-\frac{d}{k^{\prime}}>-1
, (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we find constants
K_{1},K_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any

T\geq t\geq r\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\big[|R_{t}^{\varepsilon}-R_{r}^{\varepsilon}||\big]=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\big[|R_{r}^{\varepsilon}(\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{r}^{t}\langle\Xi_{s}^{\varepsilon},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{r}^{t}|\Xi_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s}-1)|\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[|R_{r}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\text{\rm{e}}^{2\int_{r}^{t}\langle\Xi_{s}^{\varepsilon},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\rangle-\int_{r}^{t}|\Xi_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s}-1\big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K_{1}}{\varepsilon}\Big(\text{\rm{e}}^{C_{1}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{r}^{t}s^{2\kappa-\frac{d}{k^{\prime}}}\text{\rm{d}}s}-1\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K_{2}\sqrt{t^{\kappa+1-\frac{d}{k^{\prime}}}-r^{\kappa+1-\frac{d}{k^{\prime}}}},\ \ \varepsilon\in[0,1].

Therefore,

(4.15)

\lim_{r\uparrow t}\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{|R_{t}^{\varepsilon}-R_{r}^{\varepsilon}|}{\varepsilon}\bigg]=0,\ \ t\in(0,T].

By the same reason leading to (
2.7
), we find a constant
c\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4.16)

\|\nabla P_{r,t}^{\mu}f\|_{\infty}\leq c(t-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\infty},\ \ \ T\geq t>r\geq 0,\ f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \mu\in\mathscr{P}.

Then by the Markov property,

(4.17)

\begin{split}&|\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t}^{\varepsilon})-f(X_{t}^{\mu})|\mathscr{F}_{r}]|=|(P_{r,t}^{\mu^{\varepsilon}}f)(X_{r}^{\varepsilon})-(P_{r,t}^{\mu}f)(X_{r}^{\mu})|\\
&\leq|(P_{r,t}^{\mu^{\varepsilon}}f)(X_{r}^{\varepsilon})-(P_{r,t}^{\mu^{\varepsilon}}f)(X_{r}^{\mu})|+|(P_{r,t}^{\mu^{\varepsilon}}f)(X_{r}^{\mu})-(P_{r,t}^{\mu}f)(X_{r}^{\mu})|\\
&\leq c\|f\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{|X_{r}^{\varepsilon}-X_{r}^{\mu}|}{\sqrt{t-s}}\bigg]+|(P_{r,t}^{\mu^{\varepsilon}}f)(X_{r}^{\mu})-(P_{r,t}^{\mu}f)(X_{r}^{\mu})|.\end{split}

On the other hand, let
(\tilde{X}_{r,s}^{\varepsilon})_{s\in[r,t]}
solve the SDE

\text{\rm{d}}\tilde{X}_{r,s}^{\varepsilon}=b_{s}(\tilde{X}_{r,s}^{\varepsilon},\mu_{s}^{\varepsilon})\text{\rm{d}}s+\sigma_{s}(\tilde{X}_{r,s}^{\varepsilon})\text{\rm{d}}W_{s},\ \ \tilde{X}_{r,r}^{\varepsilon}=X_{r}^{\mu},s\in[r,t].

We have

P_{r,t}^{\mu^{\varepsilon}}f(X_{r}^{\mu})=\mathbb{E}\big[f(\tilde{X}_{r,t}^{\varepsilon})\big|\mathscr{F}_{r}\big],\ \ P_{r,t}^{\mu}f(X_{r}^{\mu})=\mathbb{E}\big[f(X_{t}^{\mu})|\mathscr{F}_{r}\big].

By (
4.7
) and Girsanov’s theorem,

R_{r,t}^{\varepsilon}:=\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{r}^{t}\langle\Xi_{s}^{\varepsilon},\text{\rm{d}}W_{s}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{r}^{t}|\Xi_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\text{\rm{d}}s},\ \ \ t\in[r,T]

is a martingale, and

\tilde{W}_{s}:=W_{s}-\int_{r}^{s}\Xi_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}\text{\rm{d}}\theta,\ \ s\in[r,t]

is a Brownian motion under
\mathbb{Q}_{r,t}:=R_{r,t}^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{P}
.
Reformulating the SDE for
(X_{s}^{\mu})_{s\in[r,t]}
as

\text{\rm{d}}X_{s}^{\mu}=\big\{b_{s}(X_{s}^{\mu})+B_{s}(X_{s}^{\mu},\mu^{\varepsilon})\text{\rm{d}}s+\sigma_{s}(X_{s}^{\mu})\text{\rm{d}}\tilde{W}_{s},\ \ X_{r}^{\mu}=\tilde{X}_{r,r}^{\varepsilon},\ \ s\in[r,t],

by the weak uniqueness we obtain

P_{r,t}^{\mu^{\varepsilon}}f(X_{r}^{\mu})=\mathbb{E}\big[R_{r,t}^{\varepsilon}f(X_{t}^{\mu})\big|\mathscr{F}_{r}\big],

so that by Pinsker’s inequality,
2\kappa-\frac{d}{k^{\prime}}>-1
and (
4.7
), we find a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

(4.18)

\begin{split}&|(P_{r,t}^{\mu^{\varepsilon}}f)(X_{r}^{\mu})-(P_{r,t}^{\mu}f)(X_{r}^{\mu})|^{2}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\big|\mathbb{E}[|1-R_{r,t}^{\varepsilon}|\big|\mathscr{F}_{r}]\big|^{2}\\
&\leq 2\|f\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{r,t}}\big[\log R_{r,t}^{\varepsilon}\big|\mathscr{F}_{0}\big]=\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{r}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{r,t}}\big[|\Xi_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\big|\mathscr{F}_{r}\big]\text{\rm{d}}s\\
&\leq c_{2}\|f\|_{\infty}\varepsilon^{2},\ \ \varepsilon\in(0,1],\ 0<r<t\leq T.\end{split}

Combining this with Lemma
3.2
, (
4.5
) and (
4.17
), we find constants
c_{3},c_{4}>0
such that

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(f(X_{t}^{\varepsilon})-f(X_{t})\big)\frac{1-R_{r}^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\Big]\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[f(X_{t}^{\varepsilon})-f(X_{t})\big|\mathscr{F}_{r}\big]\Big|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{|1-R_{r}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\|f\|_{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\mathbb{E}[|X_{r}^{\varepsilon}-X_{r}^{\mu}|]}{\sqrt{t-r}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+c_{4}\|f\|_{\infty}\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}\|f\|_{\infty}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{t-r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \ \varepsilon\in(0,1],t\in[0,T].

This together with (
4.15
) yields

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}I_{2}(\varepsilon)\leq\lim_{r\uparrow t}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\Bigg(\bigg|\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(f(X_{t}^{\varepsilon})-f(X_{t})\big)\frac{1-R_{r}^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\Big]\bigg|+2\|f\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{|R_{t}^{\varepsilon}-R_{r}^{\varepsilon}|}{\varepsilon}\Big]\Bigg)=0.

∎
