Learning Safe-by-Design Neural Network Controllers
Yang Zhao, Jungeun Lee, Jeong hwan Jeon, Sze Zheng Yong

Abstract
Safety filters constructed from control barrier functions (CBFs) are commonly appended to pre-trained neural network controllers to enforce safety requirements. However, this decoupled design with hand-tuned, fixed CBF parameters often fails to adapt to the underlying controller, yielding overly conservative solutions. Thus, given a valid CBF, we address these limitations by jointly learning a neural network controller and neural-network-parameterized CBF parameters, enforcing the resulting affine safety constraints by construction and avoiding an online quadratic program (QP) safety filter at run time. To further improve computational efficiency and scalability, we introduce a lightweight projection architecture that enforces constraints without full constraint enumeration. Extensive simulation evaluations demonstrate reliable, scalable safety constraint satisfaction at reduced computational cost.



I
Introduction

Neural networks are increasingly used as powerful function approximators for feedback control policies, enabling controllers to handle complex nonlinear dynamics and high-dimensional state spaces that are difficult to model analytically
[
4
,
12
]
.
However, in safety-critical systems, purely learned controllers may produce unsafe control actions that violate system constraints such as collision avoidance, actuator limits, or state safety requirements.
A common strategy to address this is to place a safety filter after the neural network controller, as shown in Fig.
1
(left).
While this modular design allows learned controllers to be combined with formal safety guarantees, it may lead to suboptimal behavior.

Control barrier functions (CBFs) have become one of the most widely used tools for implementing such safety filters in the process of designing control inputs
[
1
]
. In the standard CBF framework, safety is enforced by imposing a constraint of the form
\dot{h}(x,u)+\alpha(h(x))\geq 0
, with

a CBF candidate
h(\cdot)
and
an extended class-
\mathcal{K}
function
\alpha(\cdot)
that regulates how aggressively the system is pushed back toward the safe set. In practice, however, the design of this
\alpha(\cdot)
function is often heuristic, e.g., by fixing it as a simple linear function (e.g.,
\alpha(h)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\omega}h
) with a manually tuned
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\omega}
, which can lead to conservative behavior or unsafe motion
[
6
]
.

Optimal-decay CBF methods address this limitation by introducing an adaptive decay variable into the online CBF-QP, improving flexibility over fixed-gain designs
[
13
]
. However, they still require solving a (larger) QP at run time.

BarrierNet also learns higher-order CBF components for safe robot control, but the training process is complex and expensive, and it retains a CBF-QP layer. By contrast, our goal is to embed the resulting affine safety constraints directly into the neural controller
[
17
]
with optimal-decay, without any online QP.

Another line of work enforces safety constraints directly within neural network controllers.
Penalty-based methods promote the generation of safe control actions during training, but typically do not provide hard safety guarantees, especially under limited data or out-of-distribution conditions
[
8
]
. To guarantee safety, projection-based methods have been proposed to enforce hard constraints by projecting the neural network output onto a feasible set defined by safety constraints.
HardNet
[
9
]

enforces

state-dependent
affine constraints by adding correction terms, but requires
A(x)
to have full row rank, limiting the number of constraints and failing to handle linearly dependent cases or non-unique projections.
More recently, constraint-affine neural networks (CAffNet)
[
19
]
addressed these limitations by incorporating a constraint-affine layer with constraint decomposition and a trainable projection module, enabling arbitrary
state-dependent
affine constraints with provable feasibility and universal approximation properties.
However, the projection step can be computationally expensive as the number of constraints increases, limiting its scalability to complex systems.

Fig. 1
:
Framework of
the
trainable safety filter. The proposed method jointly trains a neural network controller and CBF parameters within the CAffNet
(-Lite)
architecture, ensuring hard constraint satisfaction by construction without a separate safety filtering step.

Motivated by these challenges, we propose a safe-by-design neural network control framework that integrates trainable CBFs with CAffNet(-Lite) in Fig.
1
(right).

The main contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

We introduce CAffNet-Lite, an extension of CAffNet with a lightweight projection architecture that reduces computation time while preserving hard affine constraint satisfaction and its universal approximation property.

2.

Given a valid CBF under input constraints, we leverage CAffNet-Lite to train a neural network controller that satisfies an optimal-decay-like property by construction, enabling real-time deployment without the computational overhead and reliability concerns of solving a QP online.

3.

We prove that the resulting piecewise continuous control satisfies strong invariance of the CBF in the Filippov sense.

II
Modeling and Problem Statement

Consider a nonlinear control-affine system

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=f(x)+g(x)u,

(1)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the system state and
u\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
the control input, with a
polytope
input set, i.e.,
\mathcal{U}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid Pu\leq q\}
.
The functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
are assumed to be locally Lipschitz continuous.

To characterize system safety, let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuously differentiable function that defines the safe set

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid h(x)\geq 0\},

(2)

where
\mathcal{C}
satisfies some desired state constraints
\mathcal{X}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, e.g., for collision avoidance.
The objective is to ensure forward invariance of
\mathcal{C}
, meaning that if the system starts inside the safe set, it remains inside for all future time.
A common approach to enforce safety is through control barrier functions (CBFs). A function
h(x)
is a CBF if there exists an extended class-
\mathcal{K}
function
\alpha(\cdot)
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left[L_{f}h(x)+L_{g}h(x)u\right]\geq-\alpha(h(x)),

(3)

where
L_{f}h(x)=\tfrac{\partial h(x)}{\partial x}f(x)
and
L_{g}h(x)=\tfrac{\partial h(x)}{\partial x}g(x)
are the Lie derivatives.
Condition (
3
) ensures that the safe set
\mathcal{C}
is forward invariant under an appropriate control input.
In practice, the overall (safety) CBF constraints are often enforced by modifying a nominal control input
u_{\mathrm{nom}}(x)
through a quadratic program (QP):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u^{*}(x)&=\argmin_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\|u-u_{\mathrm{nom}}(x)\|^{2}_{2}\\
&\text{s.t.}\quad\begin{aligned} -L_{g}h(x)u&\leq L_{f}h(x)+\alpha(h(x)).\end{aligned}\end{aligned}

(4)

Since the CBF condition is affine in
u
and the input set
\mathcal{U}
is
polytopic
by assumption, the constraints in (
4
) can be written equivalently as
state
-dependent affine constraints:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{S}(x):=\{u\mid A(x)u\leq b(x)\}},

(5)

with
A(x)=\begin{bmatrix}-L_{g}h(x)\\
P\end{bmatrix}
and
b(x)=\begin{bmatrix}L_{f}h(x)+\alpha(h(x))\\
q\end{bmatrix}
.

The QP safety filter in (
4
) provides a principled mechanism to enforce safety constraints, but solving the optimization problem online can introduce computational overhead and may degrade control performance due to frequent corrections to the nominal controller. Moreover, manually choosing and fixing the CBF parameters, i.e., the
\alpha(\cdot)
function, may diminish the performance of the nominal controller.

Thus, this letter aims to jointly design a neural network-based controller and neural-network-parameterized CBF parameters
(similar to optimal-decay CBFs
[
13
]
)
that satisfy the CBF safety constraints by construction without requiring an online QP safety filter. Formally, the problem is as follows:

Problem 1

(Safe-by-Design Neural Network Control)
.

Consider the control-affine system in (
1
) with state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and control input
u\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let the safe set be defined by (
2
)
with a given valid/non-empty input-constrained CBF
.
The objective is to design a neural network controller
u=\pi_{\theta}(x)\in\mathcal{U}
together with trainable CBF parameters as a neural network
\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h(x))
replacing the function
\alpha(h(x))
in (
4
) such that the safe set
\mathcal{C}
is
(strongly)
forward invariant.

To solve this problem, we develop a safe-by-design neural network controller based on a
novel lightweight CAffNet-Lite that extends
the CAffNet architecture in our prior work
[
19
]
, with a trainable control affine constraint.

III
Main Approach

In this section, we first introduce CAffNet-Lite in Section
III-A
, an improved safe-by-design neural network control framework inspired by
[
19
]
, that integrates a constraint-affine layer with neural networks (NNs) to guarantee satisfaction of state-dependent control affine constraints in (
5
) by construction, which we employ to solve Problem
1
in Section
III-B
.
Moreover, we prove its capability to enforce strong forward invariance of a given safe set in the Filippov sense, and thus, to address Problem
1
.

III-A

Constrained Neural Network Design via CAffNet-Lite

The constrained neural network framework (cf. Fig.
1
(right)) has the state measurement
x
as an input
x
and generates the feedback control input
u
as its output, and can be parameterized by any trainable universal function approximator
f_{\theta}(x)
establishing a mapping from state
x
to control
u
, including common architectures such as feedforward neural networks or transformers
[
14
,
18
]
.
Specifically, the proposed CAffNet-Lite extends our prior affine-constrained neural network architecture in
[
19
]
by improving its scalability
with an improved constraint decomposition strategy, which will be introduced in Section
III-A1
.
Then, in Section
III-A2
, we present the projection architecture, inherited and recapped from CAffNet
[
19
]
, that integrates the unconstrained output, the null-space component, and the decomposed constraints to guarantee hard constraint satisfaction and feasibility of the resulting control policy.

III-A
1
Lightweight
Constraint Decomposition

Given that (
5
) with
n_{c}
number of constraints forms a polytope, its boundary is defined by a maximum
m
number of active constraints. Then, as with CAffNet
[
19
]
, we enforce the constraints defined by (
5
) by decomposing
A(x)
,
b(x)
into subconstraints. However, instead of the CAffNet approach
[
19
]
that selects all possible combinations of active constraints up to order
\min(n_{c},m)
, we propose CAffNet-Lite that only considers

subconstraints with either the minimum or maximum order, i.e.,

k\in\{1,\min{(n_{c},m)}\}
,

with a (reduced) set of indices:

\displaystyle{\Gamma}:=\{\gamma\mid k\in\bigl\{1,\min{(n_{c},m)}\bigr\},1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n_{c}\},

where
\gamma=(j_{1},\dots,j_{k})
is the index sequence, and the original constraint is decomposed into subconstraints with each
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
i.e., with
the corresponding submatrix of
A(x)
and subvector of
b(x)
with rows
a_{j_{i}}^{\top}
and
b_{j_{i}}
selected by
\gamma
as
A_{\gamma}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{j_{1}}&\ldots&a_{j_{k}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
and
b_{\gamma}=\begin{bmatrix}b_{j_{1}}&\ldots&b_{j_{k}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
.
Note that we do not require
A_{\gamma}
to have full row rank.

Under this new design, the total number of considered constraint subsets is
n_{c}
when
\min(n_{c},m)=1
, and otherwise
n_{c}+\tbinom{n_{c}}{\min(n_{c},m)}
,
which yields a worst-case polynomial complexity of
O(n_{c}^{m})
with respect to
n_{c}
.
By contrast, CAffNet
[
19
]
that selects all possible combinations of active constraints yields
\sum_{k=1}^{\min(n_{c},m)}\binom{n_{c}}{k}
subconstraints, which is upper bounded by
2^{n_{c}}-1
and thus, has worst-case exponential complexity
O(2^{n_{c}})
with respect to
n_{c}
; consequently, the number of projection candidates for CAffNet is reduced for systems with many safety constraints or high-dimensional control inputs, leading to significantly increased scalability.

III-A
2
Projection with Sub-Constraints

The projection architecture is
adopted from CAffNet
[
19
]
, under the following assumption on the continuity and feasibility of the constraints.

Assumption 1
.

For any

state

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
A(x)
and
b(x)
are continuous in
x
, and the feasible region
\mathcal{S}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid A(x)y\leq b(x)\}
is non-empty.

With sub-constraints (
A_{\gamma}(x),b_{\gamma}(x))
from Section
III-A1
, the projected output is given by:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}}(x)=&f_{\theta}(x)-A_{\gamma}^{\dagger}(x)(A_{\gamma}(x)f_{\theta}(x)-b_{\gamma}(x))\\
&+(I-A_{\gamma}^{\dagger}(x)A_{\gamma}(x)){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}w_{\phi}(x)},\end{array}

where
A_{\gamma}^{\dagger}(x)
is the pseudoinverse of
A_{\gamma}(x)
,

f_{\theta}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the output of an unconstrained NN, and
w_{\phi}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a learned null-space component
that allows for selecting different feasible points on the hyperplane defined by the sub-constraint when the solution of
A_{\gamma}(x)y=b_{\gamma}(x)
is not unique (instead of only allowing orthogonal/parallel projections).

Then, the feasible candidate set is defined by selecting all projections that are inside the safe region, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}(x):=\left\{\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x)\,\middle|\,\gamma\in\Gamma,\;A(x)\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x)\leq b(x)\right\}}.

(8)

Finally, CAffNet-Lite outputs the unconstrained network
f_{\theta}(x)
when feasible; otherwise it selects the projection from (
8
) that is closest to
f_{\theta}(x)
under any
p
-norm (
\|\cdot\|_{p}
), i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(x){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\,\in}\begin{cases}f_{\theta}(x),&\text{if }{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f_{\theta}(x)\in\mathcal{S}(x)},\\
\displaystyle\arg\!\!\!\!\min_{y\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{P}}(x)}}\!\|y-f_{\theta}(x)\|_{p},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(9)

Note that in the event of a tie, the tie can be arbitrarily broken. Moreover,
CAffNet-Lite does not require full-rank or linearly independent constraints.

The projected output with the improved constraints decomposition in Section
III-A1
is guaranteed to be feasible.

Lemma 1

(Existence of a Feasible Solution)
.

For any
state

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if the feasible region
\mathcal{S}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid A(x)y\leq b(x)\}
is non-empty, then there exists at least one candidate
y\in\{\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x)\mid{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma\in{\Gamma}}\}
such that
A(x)y\leq b(x)
.

Proof.

Based on
the definition of the minimal face
[
16
, Theorem 8.4]
and
the proof of feasibility in CAffNet
[
19
, Appendix A]
, the projection onto the minimal face of the polyhedron
(with maximum order
k=\min{(n_{c},m)}
)
is,
by definition
, within the feasible region.

Such index set satisfies
{\Gamma}_{k}=\{\gamma\mid k=\min(n_{c},m)\}\subseteq{\Gamma}
by construction. Therefore, the corresponding projection set
\{\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x)\mid\gamma\in{\Gamma}_{\min(n_{c},m)}\}
is non-empty. Thus, with the definition of the minimal face, this projection set satisfies the constraints in (
5
), which indicates (
8
) is non-empty and Lemma
1
holds
.∎

Then, since all solutions of (
9
) satisfy
\mathcal{S}(x)
, which exist by Lemma
1
, the following holds directly.

Proposition 1

(Hard Constraint Satisfaction)
.

For any
state

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, under Assumption
1
, the output of CAffNet-Lite
\mathcal{P}^{*}(x)
in (
9
) satisfies the
state
-dependent affine constraints, i.e.,
A(x)\mathcal{P}^{*}(x)\leq b(x)
.

Furthermore, it can be shown that CAffNet-Lite preserves the universal approximation property of CAffNet.

Proposition 2

(Performance Preservation)
.

Let
y^{*}(x)
be an optimal output of CAffNet (that has the universal approximation property
[
19
, Theorem 3.5]
). CAffNet-Lite in (
9
) can approximate
y^{*}(x)
arbitrarily well.

Proof.

If
y^{*}(x)=f_{\theta}(x)
, then
f_{\theta}(x)\in\mathcal{S}(x)
by (
9
), and CAffNet-Lite returns the same output as CAffNet. Otherwise, suppose
y^{*}(x)=\mathcal{P}_{\gamma^{*}}(x)
. If
\gamma^{*}\in{\Gamma}
, the same candidate is already included in CAffNet-Lite. Thus, it remains to consider an intermediate-order subset
\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma^{o}\setminus{\Gamma}
, where
\Gamma^{o}
is the set of indices in CAffNet
[
19
, above Eq. (2)]
. Since
y^{*}(x)
satisfies
A_{\gamma^{*}}(x)y^{*}(x)=b_{\gamma^{*}}(x)
, there exists a single-constraint index
\tilde{\gamma}^{*}=(j^{*})
, where
j^{*}
is an entry of
\gamma^{*}
, such that
a_{j^{*}}(x)y^{*}(x)=b_{j^{*}}(x)
. In addition, there exists a target null-space function
w_{t}^{*}(x)=y^{*}(x)-f_{\theta}(x)
. By the universal approximation property of NNs,
w_{\phi}(x)
can approximate
w_{t}^{*}(x)
arbitrarily well. With
w_{\phi}(x)=w_{t}^{*}(x)
and omitting the argument
x
, the CAffNet-Lite projection for the single constraint with the index
(j^{*})
recovers
y^{*}
, since
\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\gamma}^{*}}=f_{\theta}-a_{j^{*}}^{\dagger}(a_{j^{*}}f_{\theta}-b_{j^{*}})+(I-a_{j^{*}}^{\dagger}a_{j^{*}})w_{t}^{*}=y^{*}-a_{j^{*}}^{\dagger}(a_{j^{*}}y^{*}-b_{j^{*}})=y^{*}
.

∎

III-B

Neural Network Based Safety Filter

To overcome the limitations of a fixed-parameter class-
\mathcal{K}
function in the standard CBF formulation, we propose to learn the CBF parameters jointly with the neural network controller within the CAffNet-Lite architecture. Specifically,
similar to optimal-decay CBFs
[
13
]
, we replace the class-
\mathcal{K}
function
\alpha(\cdot)
in the CBF condition
in (
3
)
with a neural network parameterization
\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h(x))

that satisfies
\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,0)=0
at the safety boundary when
h=0
to enforce invariance
, yielding more flexible safety constraints that better capture the system dynamics and safety requirements.

Under this construction, we have trainable
state-dependent
affine constraints
A(x)u\leq b_{\vartheta}(x)
with:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}A(x)\!=\!\begin{bmatrix}-L_{g}h(x)\\
P\end{bmatrix}\!,\ b_{\vartheta}(x)\!=\!\begin{bmatrix}L_{f}h(x)\!+\!\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h(x))\\
q\end{bmatrix}\!,\end{gathered}

which, for simplicity
and similar to optimal-decay CBF
[
13
]
, can be imposed by choosing
\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h(x))=\tilde{\alpha}_{\vartheta}(x){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha(h(x))}
with
\tilde{\alpha}_{\vartheta}(x)
being a trainable (unconstrained) neural network
and the original
\alpha(\cdot)
. Future work will explore how to more intelligently impose this constraint for better performance.

Unlike the standard CBF that relies on continuous or sufficiently regular closed-loop dynamics, the CAffNet-Lite controller in (
9
) may be discontinuous since the
\argmin
selection in the projection layer can have ties. We thus establish invariance in the Filippov sense, i.e., every Filippov solution satisfies the CBF inequality almost everywhere.

Theorem 1

(Safe-by-Design Neural Network Controllers)
.

Let the control input be given by (
9
), with the
state-dependent
affine constraints defined by (
III-B
).

Assume that
0
is a regular value of
h
,
\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h)
is locally Lipschitz in
h
with
\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,0)=0
and that
K_{\vartheta}(x):=\{u\mid A(x)u\leq b_{\vartheta}(x)\}
is nonempty,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Then, the resulting
CAffNet-Lite
controller
with
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}
in (
9
)
guarantees
strong
forward invariance of the safe set
\mathcal{C}
and thus, solves Problem
1
.

Proof.

Let
X(x)=f(x)+g(x)\mathcal{P}^{*}(x)
be the resulting piecewise continuous ‘velocity’ and consider the differential inclusion
\dot{x}\in\mathcal{F}[X](x)
, where
\mathcal{F}[X]=\overline{\text{co}}\{f(x)+g(x)k_{i}(x)\mid i\in I(x)\}
is its Filippov regularization
[
3
]
given by the closed convex hull of all limiting control inputs
k_{i}(x)
in (
9
) with active set
I(x)
(right hand side of (
9
)).
Filippov solutions exist locally under measurability and local essential boundedness of
X

[
2
, Prop. 3]
; these follow from the finite number of ‘pieces’ of (
9
) and the bounded input set
\mathcal{U}
.
Let
x(\cdot)
be any Filippov solution with
x(0)\in\mathcal{C}
. For a.e.
t
, write
x=x(t)
and by construction, each limiting solution
k_{i}(x)
in (

) satisfies

L_{f}h(x)+L_{g}h(x)k_{i}\geq-\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h(x))
. Thus, for their convex hull
\bar{u}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}k_{i}
, and since the constraint in

is linear in

,

L_{f}h(x)+L_{g}h(x)\bar{u}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}(L_{f}h(x)+L_{g}h(x)k_{i})\geq-\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h(x))

for a.e.

.

is continuously differentiable and

satisfies the above scalar differential inequality a.e. Finally, the locally Lipschitz NN parameterization of

[

to give
h(x(t))\geq 0
for all

on the interval of existence.

Remark 1

(Vector-valued CBFs)
.

Vector-valued CBFs are interpreted componentwise:
\mathcal{C}=\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathcal{C}_{j}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{j}=\{x\mid h_{j}(x)\geq 0\}
and

is continuously differentiable. The affine constraint in (

along the same Filippov solution.

Simulation Results

First, we consider a single integrator system (
\dot{x}=u
), where the state
x=[p_{x},p_{y}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the position, and
u=[v_{x},v_{y}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
represents the velocities in the
x
and
y
directions. The state constraints are
p_{x}\in[-5,1]
and
p_{y}\in[-4,2]
, which can be enforced by
h_{x}(x)=b_{x}-A_{x}x\geq 0
. The control constraints are
v_{x},v_{y}\in[-1,1]
. In addition, we consider three convex polyhedral obstacles with the hyperplane representation,
\mathcal{O}_{j}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid A_{j}x\leq b_{j}\},j\in\{1,2,3\}
. For each obstacle
\mathcal{O}_{j}
, the safety condition for its
i
-th edge is
h_{j}^{i}=a_{j}^{i}x-b_{j}^{i}\geq 0,\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n_{j}\}
, where
n_{j}
denotes the number of edges. To enforce safety satisfaction for all edges of each obstacle, we use the smooth union function in
[
10
]
to combine them into a single CBF, i.e.,

h_{j}(x)=\tfrac{1}{\kappa}\ln\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}e^{\kappa h_{j}^{i}(x)}\right)-\tfrac{\ln n_{j}}{\kappa}
,
where
\kappa=10
is a positive parameter that controls the smoothness of the union function. The corresponding Lie derivatives for
h_{j}(x)
are
L_{f}h_{j}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\lambda_{j}^{i}(x)L_{f}h_{j}^{i}(x)
and
L_{g}h_{j}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}\lambda_{j}^{i}(x)L_{g}h_{j}^{i}(x)
, with
\lambda_{j}^{i}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{\kappa(h_{j}^{i}(x)-h_{j}(x))}
.

We assume that the composed smooth CBF remains a valid CBF for the corresponding obstacle-avoidance safe set.

The safe set for obstacle avoidance is defined as
\mathcal{C}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid h_{j}(x)\geq 0,j\in\{1,2,3\}\}
, which can be enforced by

\displaystyle L_{f}h_{j}(x)+L_{g}h_{j}(x)u(x)\geq-\alpha(h_{j}(x)).

optimal-decay QP (OD-QP)

and for NN, HardNet, CAffNet, and CAffNet-Lite, we compare both the case with
\alpha(h(x))=\omega h(x)
with
\omega\in\{0.1,10\}
and the case with a neural network parameterization
\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h(x))=\tilde{\alpha}_{\vartheta}(x)h(x)
.

Each controller network uses ReLU activation functions and three hidden layers with
200
neurons per layer.
The neural network used in
\tilde{\alpha}_{\vartheta}(x)
is a fully connected network with three hidden layers of 64 neurons each. The training samples are randomly generated within the safe set, and the nominal command is
the unsafe proportional control
u_{nom}=k_{p}(x_{ref}-x)
, where
k_{p}=2
, and
x_{ref}=[0,0]^{T}
is the goal position. Moreover, nominal and actual control inputs are saturated by the control constraints.
We set the training epochs to
10000
in this simulation, and trajectories with
T_{test}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}10}s

and
dt=0.01s
are shown in Fig.

With a fixed
poorly tuned
CBF parameter
(Fig.

On the other hand, with a good choice of CBF parameter (Fig.

\alpha_{\vartheta}(x,h(x))
, CAffNet and CAffNet-Lite can successfully arrive at the goal region, while strictly satisfying all safety constraints during the entire trajectory. Furthermore, from
3

This improvement occurs because the learned CBF term allows CAffNet to choose less conservative controls while still satisfying the safety constraints, so the trajectory reaches the goal earlier and stays closer to the unsafe nominal controller.

Both NN and HardNet have some constraint violations, and QP with fixed CBF parameters has a relatively higher cost and
a longer testing time.
The result shows that system behavior may be unexpected if the CBF parameter is not well designed. However, with the proposed framework, the CBF parameter can be learned together with the control policy, which can significantly improve the performance while strictly satisfying all safety constraints.

V
Conclusion

We presented CAffNet-Lite, a safe-by-design neural control framework that jointly learns a neural network controller alongside neural-network-parameterized CBF conditions. The proposed method guarantees hard satisfaction of control-affine constraints and strong forward invariance of the safe set by construction, without requiring a separate QP-based safety filter. A lightweight constraint decomposition strategy improves scalability over the prior CAffNet approach while preserving feasibility and universal approximation guarantees. Future work will consider extending the framework to broader classes of constraints and evaluating performance on real-world systems.
