A practical alternative to live cell-based assay for AQP4 and MOG antibody detection
Authors: Qi Wang, Feng Chen, Yuqi Tang, Juanjuan Zhang, Zhengyu Sun, Huaxing Meng, Zhongzheng Wang, Yingtao Wang, Shaoxin Tao, Ming Lin, Yinan Zhao, Yue Huang, Junhong Guo, Yanghua Tian, Jiewen Zhang, Junwei Hao, Liang Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundLive cell-based assay (LCBA) is the gold standard for sensitive detection of aquaporin-4 (AQP4) and myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG) autoantibodies in neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) and MOG antibody-associated disorder (MOGAD). However, LCBA is less practical, standardisable and cost-effective than fixed cell-based assay (FCBA).MethodsWe developed a high-performance untransfected vector cells lysate, named AbFine, to process patients’ sera to pre-bind and deplete interferents before FCBA, and examined its rationality. We then conducted a multicentre, double-blind, prospective discovery study to compare the diagnostic accuracy of conventional FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA in detecting AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG. A retrospective validation cohort was used to assess generalisability. Participants included patients with NMOSD, MOGAD, and seronegative inflammatory demyelination syndromes, and controls with other neurological disorders.Findings818 participants were recruited, including 410 in the discovery and 408 in the validation cohorts with good consistency. Among 205 patients with NMOSD, AQP4-IgG was detected in 165, 185, and 178 by FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA, yielding sensitivity of 80.5% (95% CI, 74.4–85.7), 90.2% (95% CI, 85.3–93.9), and 86.8% (95% CI, 81.4–91.1), respectively. Research in context

Evidence before this study

Live cell-based assay (LCBA) is the reference standard for aquaporin-4 (AQP4) and myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG) autoantibodies detection but its use is constrained by logistical complexity and cost across clinical settings. Fixed cell-based assay (FCBA) is more practical, standardisable, and widespread, yet is associated with missed diagnoses. We queried PubMed for articles published from January 1, 1970 to December 30, 2025 using the search terms: “cell-based assay”, “neuroimmune antibodies”, “AQP4”, “MOG”, and “sensitivity improvement”, without language restrictions. Previous studies have mainly attributed the reduced sensitivity of FCBA to fixation-induced alterations in antigen conformation, a limitation inherent to standard FCBA protocols and difficult to overcome. No retrieved studies have addressed this limitation. In addition, existing comparative studies were frequently limited by small sample sizes, single centre or unblinded diagnostic design, resulting in significant variability on the sensitivity and specificity of the assays. Thus, the current guidelines lack evidence-based, balanced alternative that reconciles diagnostic sensitivity with feasibility for detecting AQP4 and MOG autoantibodies, complicating clinical decision making across centres.

Added value of this study

We developed a high-performance untransfected vector cell lysate, termed AbFine, that was used to preprocess patients’ sera to pre-bind and deplete interfering antibodies before FCBA. The rationality is also applicable to fixed HeLa cell-based NMDAR-IgG testing. We then conducted a multicentre, head-to-head, diagnostic accuracy study including prospective discovery and retrospective validation cohort to evaluate methodology for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG detection. We recruited 818 patients with neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD), MOG antibody-associated disorder (MOGAD), seronegative demyelination syndromes, and other neurological disorders to compare the diagnostic accuracy of FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA. The results indicate FCBA-AbFine increased the absolute detection yield of AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG by 9.7% and 12.2% in patients with NMOSD and MOGAD compared with conventional FCBA, and achieved performance comparable to the reference standard LCBA. Meanwhile, FCBA-AbFine maintained a high diagnostic specificity. These gains provide high-level evidence that FCBA-AbFine could be a practical alternative to LCBA in clinical practice.

Implications of all the available evidence

FCBA-AbFine performed better than FCBA and comparable to LCBA for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG detection. AbFine enables the use of FCBA as an alternative to LCBA with operationally practical and cost-efficient characteristics to reduce missed diagnoses.

Introduction

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorders (NMOSD) and myelin oligodendrocytes glycoprotein antibody-associated disease (MOGAD) are autoantibody-mediated idiopathic inflammatory demyelination diseases (IIDDs) of the central nervous system. Aquaporin-4 (AQP4) and myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG) antibodies are disease-defining serological biomarkers, and fluctuations in antibody titres may reflect disease activity and treatment response.1, 2, 3, 4

Cell-based assay (CBA) using plasmid-transfected human embryonic kidney 293T (HEK293T) cells expressing native, full-length conformational antigens demonstrates high sensitivity and specificity,5,6 and is recommended by the International Panel for NMOSD and MOGAD Diagnosis.7,8 Comparative studies have established live CBA (LCBA) as the reference standard, with superior sensitivity than fixed CBA (FCBA), particularly for MOG-IgG detection.9, 10, 11 However, LCBA requires continuous cell culture, quality control, and technical expertise, imposing logistical and economic constraints that limit its use outside specialised laboratories. By contrast, slide-based FCBA is widely adopted because it is practical, cost-efficient, high-throughput, and amenable to standardisation through commercial kits.10,11 Therefore, improving the sensitivity of FCBA has become a key technical challenge. One of the critical pathways resides in depleting high-concentration serum interferents to improve specific binding affinity and signal-to-noise ratio.12

To this end, we developed a high-performance untransfected vector cell lysate, termed AbFine, which shares the same cytoplasmic and membrane components excluding the plasmid-derived antigen, that was used to preprocess patients’ sera to pre-bind and deplete interfering antibodies before FCBA (Fig. 1). Then we conducted a multicentre, double-blind, head-to-head, prospective study to compare the diagnostic accuracy of FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG detection, and performed extensive validation.

Methods

Study design and participants

This head-to-head diagnostic accuracy study was conducted over a 2-year period in accordance with the STARD 2015 guidelines. First, we established the rationale and optimised protocols for AbFine. We then performed a prospective, multicentre, double-blind discovery study between December 2024 and December 2025 to compare the diagnostic performance of FCBA-AbFine with conventional FCBA and LCBA for the detection of AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG. Finally, the generalisability of FCBA-AbFine was assessed in a retrospective validation cohort using archived frozen serum samples collected between December 2023 and November 2024.

In the discovery cohort, we prospectively recruited Chinese patients with suspected autoantibody-mediated IIDDs from four tertiary neurological centres. The validation cohort was retrospectively assembled from a single centre using identical inclusion and exclusion criteria. In both cohorts, patients were diagnosed as AQP4-IgG seropositive NMOSD, MOGAD and double-seronegative IIDDs by two independent neurologists according to the International Panel for NMOSD and MOGAD diagnostic criteria, incorporating in-house FCBA results for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG.7,8 Controls were individuals with other neurological disorders (OND). All individuals were of Han Chinese ethnicity. Participants with incomplete clinical information or insufficient serum samples were excluded (Fig. 2).

Study flow diagram. Flow diagram of participant enrolment, inclusion, exclusion, and allocation in the discovery and validation cohorts.

Serum matrix effects investigation

In-house recombinant monoclonal antibodies against AQP4, MOG, and anti–N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR) were generated from the cerebrospinal fluid of patients with AQP4-IgG, MOG-IgG, or NMDAR-IgG positivity using B-cell receptor sequencing, and their antigen reactivity was confirmed using CBA against corresponding antigen-transfected cells. To assess the impact of serum matrix effects on FCBA performance, AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG monoclonal antibodies were serially diluted (0.04–1.0 μg/mL) in either 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA; Sigma–Aldrich, Cat# V900933) or 10% antibody-negative human serum, and the lowest detectable concentration was determined using FCBA.

To investigate whether serum matrix effects interfered with antigen-targeted antibody binding or with targeted antibody-secondary antibody interactions, we evaluated three FCBA loading sequences: (a) incubation with anti-human MOG-IgG followed by secondary antibody only; (b) incubation with anti-human MOG-IgG, followed by antibody-negative human serum and secondary antibody; and (c) incubation with antibody-negative human serum before addition of anti-human MOG-IgG and secondary antibody. An anti-human IgG Fc cross-absorbed antibody (RRID: AB_2534080) was used as the secondary antibody.13

AbFine development, application and generalisability in FCBA

Untransfected HEK293T cells (RRID: CVCL-0063), corresponding to those cultured on coverslips for FCBA, were lysed to generate a cell lysate termed AbFine. To obtain AbFine with a protein concentration exceeding 30 g/L, approximating serum globulin levels, different lysis methods were evaluated, and protein concentrations were quantified using ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometry. The optimal serum-AbFine pre-incubation ratio, incubation sequence, and dilution conditions were systematically examined. After pre-incubation, serum samples were centrifuged, and the supernatant was used for subsequent FCBA detection; this procedure was defined as FCBA-AbFine. Detailed optimisation of AbFine preparation and application is provided in Supplementary Appendix 1. To assess generalisability, untransfected HeLa cell (RRID: CVCL-0030) lysates were additionally prepared and applied to FCBA for anti-NMDAR antibody detection.

Repeatability and reproducibility of AbFine were further evaluated. Five serum samples with different AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG titres were each tested ten times on the same day using AbFine stored for 1, 3, and 6 months to assess within-lot repeatability. Between-lot reproducibility was assessed using five independent AbFine batches tested on the same samples (Supplementary Table S1).

AQP4 and MOG antibody determinations

For discovery cohort, serum specimens were collected at enrolment, centrifuged, aliquoted, and stored in a −80 °C freezer within 2 h at the local laboratory of each centre. Samples were assigned a randomisation identifier and shipped on dry ice to the central laboratory (Beijing Precigen Biopharma Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG were performed in parallel within 1 week by two skilled technicians blinded to the clinical data. For the validation cohort, serum samples cryopreserved for over 12 months (median 14 months, range 12–30 months) were tested with the same procedures.

Stable HEK293T cell lines that express AQP4 M1/M23 or MOG alpha 1 fused gene were used as antibody harbouring cells. Detailed protocols for FCBA and LCBA were provided in Supplementary Appendix 1 and Supplementary Table S2. For both FCBA and LCBA, the given cut-off of AQP4-IgG was 1:10. MOG-IgG titres of ≥1:100 were considered clear-positive in accordance with published proposed cut-off, whereas samples with titres <1:100 required supportive clinical or MRI features to meet diagnostic criteria.8

Ethics

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University (Approval No: [2021]017) and ethics committees at each participating institution. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants for study participation and the use of human biological samples before study inclusion. The study was conducted in accordance with the 2024 Declaration of Helsinki.

Statistics

We calculated that a sample size of 380 (which includes 90 subjects with NMOSD, 90 with MOGAD, and 180 controls) with the expected sensitivity and specificity of 90% for AQP4 and MOG antibodies, the allowable error was 5%, the confidence level of 1-α was 0.85, and the sample unavailable rate was 20%. The ratio of positive to negative samples in this test was 1:1.

Based on the reference standard diagnosis of NMOSD and MOGAD and the recommended cut-offs, the sensitivity, specificity, consistency rate, positive likelihood ratio (+LR), negative likelihood ratio (−LR), positive predictive value and negative predictive value with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA. Inter-reader consistency was assessed using Cohen’s kappa value. Subgroup analyses stratified by disease phase and immunotherapy status were performed.

In the receiver operated characteristic (ROC) curve assay, the sensitivity and specificity on AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG were re-assessed with the raw titre, and Delong test was applied to compare the difference in area under the curve (AUC) values among FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA. p values of 0.05 or lower are statistically significant.

Results

Rationality of AbFine

In serum matrix effect validation test, the lowest detectable concentration of anti-human AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG were 0.08 μg/mL and 0.2 μg/mL, respectively, when diluted in 5% BSA, whereas neither antibody was detectable up to 1 μg/mL when diluted in negative human serum (Supplementary Table S3). These findings indicate a marked reduction in sensitivity in the presence of serum components recapitulating clinical samples, and identify serum matrix effects as a key determinant of detection performance, thereby providing the theoretical rationale for AbFine.

Sequential loading experiments showed that sensitivity was lowest when anti-human MOG-IgG was followed by negative serum (b), intermediate when negative serum preceded anti-human MOG-IgG (c), and highest with anti-human MOG-IgG alone (a) (Supplementary Fig. S1). This pattern indicates that serum matrix effects interfere with both antigen-targeted antibody binding and targeted antibody-secondary antibody interactions, with a more pronounced impact on the latter.

The optimal conditions for AbFine preparation and application were established (Supplementary Tables S4 and S5; Supplementary Fig. S2). Lysis of HEK293T cells using a moderate-strength buffer yielded the highest protein concentrations. Before FCBA process, sera were pre-mixed with AbFine at a 1:1 ratio, followed by dilution and incubation, which could produce optimal assay performance (Supplementary Fig. S3).

HeLa cell lysate pre-processed anti-human NMDAR antibody with negative serum demonstrated an improved signal-to-noise ratio, supporting the generalisability of AbFine to other fixed cell-based immunofluorescence assays (Fig. 1A).

Repeatability and reproducibility assessment revealed 100% within-lot and between-lot agreement across samples with different titres and AbFine with different storage durations.

Discovery study

After exclusion of 12 (2.8%; two with insufficient serum samples and ten with incomplete clinical data), 410 eligible participants were enrolled, including 219 patients with IIDDs and 191 OND controls. Based on in-house FCBA results, the 219 patients were classified as 102 with AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD, 79 with MOGAD and 38 with double-seronegative IIDDs. The 191 controls presented with a differential diagnosis including multiple sclerosis, autoimmune encephalitis, systemic autoimmunity, and infections. At the time of serum sampling, 73 (70.9%) NMOSD and 54 (65.1%) patients with MOGAD were in the acute phase, and 67 (65.0%) NMOSD and 46 (55.4%) patients with MOGAD were receiving immunotherapy. Basic demographic and clinical characteristics are shown in Supplementary Table S6. Notably, none of the 38 patients with initially seronegative IIDDs were diagnosed as AQP4-IgG-negative NMOSD, while AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG were subsequently detected in one (positive across all assays) and four patients (one detected by all assays and three by FCBA-AbFine and LCBA) fulfilling the diagnostic criteria for AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD and MOGAD, respectively; these missed cases were considered true positives in analyses.

When analysing with given cut-off, of 103 patients with NMOSD, FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA identified AQP4-IgG positivity in 86, 96, and 91 samples, with sensitivity of 83.5% (95% CI, 74.9–90.1), 93.2% (95% CI, 86.5–97.2) and 88.4% (95% CI, 80.5–93.8), respectively. Among 83 patients with MOGAD, MOG-IgG was detected in 68, 78, and 79 samples by FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA, yielding sensitivity of 86.8% (95% CI, 81.4–91.1), 94.0% (95% CI, 86.5–98.0) and 95.2% (95% CI, 88.1–98.7), respectively (Table 1). The consistency of three assays was shown in Supplementary Table S7. Notably, all seven patients with NMOSD and four with MOGAD who tested negative across assays at the time of blood sampling were more than 3 months from their last attack and were receiving long-term immunosuppressive maintenance therapy. Among 191 OND controls, FCBA, FCBA-AbFine and LCBA identified 0, 1 and 0 AQP4-IgG positivity, with the specificity of 100% (95% CI, 98.8–100.0), 99.7% (95% CI, 98.2–100.0) and 100% (95% CI, 98.8–100.0); 0, 1 and 3 MOG-IgG positivity, with the specificity of 100.0% (95% CI, 99.4–100.0), 99.7% (95% CI, 98.3–100.0) and 99.1% (95% CI, 97.3–99.8), respectively (Supplementary Table S8). The single AQP4-IgG false-positive case detected by FCBA-AbFine (titre 1:10) was clinically diagnosed with hypertrophic pachymeningitis. Among the MOG-IgG false-positive cases, three patients with myasthenia gravis, hereditary cerebellar ataxia, and anti-LGI1 encephalitis were positive by LCBA (all with titres of 1:100), and the patient with myasthenia gravis was also positive by FCBA-AbFine with a titre of 1:32. Detailed information of false-positivities were presented in Supplementary Table S9. Additional diagnostic parameters were calculated in Supplementary Table S10. The relationship of titres sequentially obtained by FCBA, FCBA-AbFine and LCBA were shown in Supplementary Table S11 and Supplementary Fig. S4.

Based on ROC curve analyses, the AUC of AQP4-IgG tested by FCBA-AbFine was 0.963 (95% CI, 0.940–0.979), which was higher than that of FCBA (0.917 [95% CI, 0.887–0.942]; p = 0.002) and comparable to LCBA (0.942 [95% CI, 0.914–0.962]; p = 0.10), with comparisons performed using the DeLong test. For MOG-IgG, the AUC of FCBA-AbFine was 0.944 (95% CI, 0.917–0.964), which was higher than that of FCBA (0.901 [95% CI, 0.868–0.928]; p = 0.03) and comparable to LCBA (0.945 [95% CI, 0.918–0.965]; p = 0.86) (Supplementary Fig. S5).

Validation study

In the validation cohort, 408 archival frozen serum samples from one centre were retrospectively tested, including 102 patients with NMOSD, 88 with MOGAD, and 18 with seronegative IIDDs, and 200 controls with OND. Detailed clinical characteristics were presented in Supplementary Table S12.

Among 102 patients with NMOSD, AQP4-IgG was detected in 79, 89, and 87 samples by FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA, and the sensitivity were 77.5% (95% CI, 68.1–85.1), 87.3% (95% CI, 79.2–93.0), and 85.3% (95% CI, 76.9–91.5), respectively. MOG-IgG was identified in 66, 77, and 79 of 88 patients with MOGAD by FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA, with the sensitivity of 75.0% (95% CI, 64.6–83.6), 87.5% (95% CI, 78.7–93.6) and 89.8 (95% CI, 81.5–95.2). Nine of 13 NMOSD and six of nine patients with MOGAD negative across all assays were either in the acute phase or receiving immunosuppressive therapy at sampling. None of the antibodies were observed in seronegative groups (Table 1). The consistency and additional diagnostic metrics were presented in Supplementary Tables S13 and S14.

The AUC for AQP4-IgG detection using FCBA-AbFine was 0.936 (95% CI, 0.908–0.958), exceeding that of conventional FCBA (0.887 [95% CI, 0.852–0.916]; p = 0.0009) and comparable to LCBA (0.926 [95% CI, 0.897–0.950]; p = 0.32). For MOGAD, the AUC for MOG-IgG detection with FCBA-AbFine was 0.926 (95% CI, 0.897–0.950), which was significantly higher than FCBA (0.875 [95% CI, 0.839–0.905]; p = 0.006) but not different from LCBA (0.935 [95% CI, 0.907–0.957]; p = 0.60) (Supplementary Fig. S5).

Summary of findings

A total of 818 participants were recruited, comprising 410 in the discovery and 408 in the validation cohorts. Among 185 patients with NMOSD who were AQP4-IgG-positive by FCBA-AbFine, 165 were also positive by FCBA (κ = 0.86; p < 0.01) and 178 by LCBA (κ = 0.91; p < 0.01); among 155 MOG-IgG-positive patients identified by FCBA-AbFine, all were positive by LCBA (κ = 0.94; p < 0.01), whereas 134 were detected by FCBA (κ = 0.85; p < 0.01). Inter-reader agreement was excellent (κ = 0.94).

Across the combined cohorts, AQP4-IgG was detected in 165, 185, and 178 of 205 patients with NMOSD by FCBA, FCBA-AbFine, and LCBA, respectively, yielding sensitivity of 80.5% (95% CI, 74.4–85.7), 90.2% (95% CI, 85.3–93.9), and 86.8% (95% CI, 81.4–91.1). MOG-IgG was identified in 134, 155, and 158 of 171 patients with MOGAD, corresponding to sensitivities of 78.4% (95% CI, 71.4–84.3), 90.6% (95% CI, 85.3–94.6), and 92.4% (95% CI, 87.4–95.9). Specificity exceeded 99.5% for all three assays. ROC analyses showed that the AUC for AQP4-IgG detection was higher with FCBA-AbFine (0.950) than with FCBA (0.902; p < 0.0001) and was comparable to LCBA (0.934; p = 0.053). Similarly, for MOG-IgG detection, FCBA-AbFine achieved an AUC of 0.935, which was superior to FCBA (0.888; p = 0.0005) and not significantly different from LCBA (0.940; p = 0.58) (Fig. 3).

Subgroup analyses demonstrated that the detected rates of AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG were higher in patients sampled during the acute phase than in remission in both the discovery and validation cohorts. In the discovery cohort, detected rates were also numerically higher in patients without immunotherapy than in those receiving immunotherapy. In the validation cohort, differences according to immunotherapy status were less consistent across assays (Supplementary Tables S15 and S16). In addition, across all subgroups, FCBA-AbFine consistently demonstrated higher sensitivity than conventional FCBA and showed performance comparable to LCBA.

Discussion

In this head-to head, diagnostic accuracy study evaluating methodology for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG detection, FCBA-AbFine increased the absolute detection yield of AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG by 9.7% and 12.2% in patients with NMOSD and MOGAD compared with conventional FCBA, and achieved performance comparable to the reference standard LCBA. Meanwhile, FCBA-AbFine maintained a high diagnostic specificity. These gains provide high-level evidence that FCBA-AbFine could be a practical alternative to LCBA in clinical practice. By extending applicability to fixed HeLa cell-based NMDAR-IgG testing, we propose that AbFine could be a generalisable strategy to mitigate serum matrix interference in fixed immunofluorescence assays, enabling more reliable antibody detection in routine clinical practice.

Conventional FCBA is more practical, cost-effective and standardisable than LCBA, while it could lead to underdiagnosis for neural antibodies,14,15 with sensitivity reduced by 6.3% and 14.0% for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG in our study. The widely acknowledged cause is attributed to fixation-induced alterations in antigen conformation and epitope masking,14,16 which are inherent to FCBA protocols and difficult to improve. Accordingly, rather than modifying antigen presentation, we focused on optimising antibody-antigen interactions by reducing serum-derived interferents.17,18 Serum matrix effects have previously been mitigated through dimeric protein extraction, dilution, and commercial blocking reagents,19, 20, 21, 22 with the drawbacks of operational difficulties, high cost and external substances introduction. In contrast, we used untransfected vector cells lysates identical to slide-cells in FCBA, of which the intrinsic protein, Fc receptor, and small molecule protein could pre-neutralise interfering nonspecific antibodies, adsorb fluorescence quenching substance, alleviate steric hindrance, and consequently purify targeted antibodies before FCBA.23 Mechanistically, we showed that serum matrix predominantly impaired the targeted-secondary antibody binding, supporting the hypothesis that AbFine worked by removing nonspecific antibodies and substances coated on Fc region of targeted antibody.

The reported proportion of double-seronegative NMOSD varies from 0% to 79%,24 depending on the cohort characteristics and assay methodology.25 In our study, missed AQP4-IgG–positive NMOSD and MOGAD accounted for 2.6% and 10.5% of patients initially classified as seronegative IIDDs. However, as our cohort did not include patients initially diagnosed with AQP4-IgG-seronegative NMOSD, we were unable to determine whether the reported seronegativity reflects true absence of antibodies or limitations in the sensitivity of the detection assays. The assay-related discrepancies in sensitivity must be carefully considered when studying seronegative IIDDs, because FCBA can yield false-negatives, particularly in low-titre MOG-IgG. For false-positives, we proposed that detection errors were unlikely as positivity was concordant across two or three assay platforms. There are reports of low-titre MOG-IgG in non-demyelinating disorders.26,27 As control samples included other conditions with limited relevance for differential diagnosis, the false positive rate could not reflect the real-world prevalence of MOG-IgG in non-MOGAD populations, whereas it indicated that nonspecific MOG-IgG could occur across a boarder spectrum of neurological disorders. Nevertheless, non-specific binding and false positivity cannot be entirely excluded, as the specificity of selected methodology may be influenced by systematic errors of the reagent source and corresponding batch effects.

Our results of the diagnostic accuracy of FCBA and LCBA for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG are consistent with previous reports that the observed discrepancies are more pronounced for MOG-IgG than for AQP4-IgG.6,10,11,15,28 This difference reflects the high conformational dependence of MOG-IgG binding, which requires full-length antigen expression and bivalent antibody engagement for reliable detection.29 Accordingly, by targeting serum interferents rather than antigen integrity, AbFine achieved performance comparable to LCBA for AQP4-IgG detection, whereas its sensitivity for MOG-IgG remained marginally lower than that of LCBA. Notably, the cut-off of clear-positive MOG-IgG may be assay-specific and influenced by differences in assay design and laboratory protocols. Therefore, the thresholds of 1:100 used in our study should be interpreted with caution, and further multicenter studies using standardised assays are needed to establish more consistent threshold.

Subgroup analyses implied that AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG detection could be substantially influenced by disease phase and treatment status. In patients sampled during remission or receiving immunotherapy, more sensitive assays may be preferable and results should be interpreted cautiously. Sera in validation cohort had been cryopreserved for a median of 14 months and underwent freeze-thaw within two cycles. Comparing with discovery cohort, this was associated with a 6.0% and 11.8% reduction in detection rates and a 25.3% and 9.9% increase in low-titre results for AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG by FCBA, respectively, which could not be fully explained by differences in remission status or immunosuppressive therapy at sampling. The lower detection rate and subgroup differences observed in the validation cohort suggest that sample storage time may also represent a potential factor affecting detection results. These findings indicate that handling and storage of serum are potential sources of assay variability,30 and archived samples requires careful selection of FCBA and interpretation of low-titre results to minimise false-negatives. However, the relatively small sample size in our subgroup analyses limits definitive conclusions. Larger studies are needed to validate subgroup findings and to further clarify the potential impact of sample storage time, ultimately informing recommendations for sample handling and storage.

Strengths of this study include prospective and retrospective testing in a real-world setting consistent with routine clinical practice. Although participants with incomplete clinical information were excluded, no substantial differences in baseline characteristics were observed between included and excluded participants, suggesting a limited risk of bias due to missing data. Sampling at variable timepoints across the disease course may limit the consistency of the results. Inter-laboratory reproducibility assessments, external validation cohorts and more controls with differential diagnosis are warranted to confirm the broader applicability and reliability. Although AbFine was designed to mitigate serum interferents, the direct biochemical removal of these substances could not be experimentally verified, and further mechanistic studies are warranted to clarify the causes of missed detection in FCBA. Despite these limitations, our study provides compelling evidence that FCBA-AbFine improves diagnostic accuracy for the serodiagnosis of NMOSD and MOGAD.
