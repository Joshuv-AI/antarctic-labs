Morphologically Equivariant Flow Matching for Bimanual Mobile Manipulation
Max Siebenborn, Daniel Ordoñez Apraez, Sophie Lueth, Giulio Turrisi, Massimiliano Pontil, Claudio Semini, Georgia Chalvatzaki

Abstract
Mobile Manipulation requires coordinated control of high-dimensional, bimanual robots. Imitation Learning methods have been broadly used to solve these robotic tasks, yet typically ignore the bilateral morphological symmetry inherent in such systems. We argue that morphological symmetry is an underexplored but crucial inductive bias for learning in bimanual Mobile Manipulation : knowing how to solve a task in one configuration directly determines how to solve its mirrored counterpart. In this paper, we formalize this symmetry prior and show that it constrains optimal bimanual policies to be ambidextrous and equivariant under reflections across the robot’s sagittal plane. We introduce a ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}_{2} -equivariant Flow Matching policy that enforces reflective symmetry either via a regularized training loss or an equivariant velocity network. Across planar and 6-DoF Mobile Manipulation tasks, symmetry-informed policies consistently improve sample efficiency and achieve zero-shot generalization to mirrored configurations absent from the training distribution. We further validate this zero-shot generalization capability on a real-world manipulation task with a TIAGo++ robot. Together, our findings establish morphological symmetry as an effective, generalizable, and scalable inductive bias for ambidextrous generative policy learning.



I
Introduction

Over the last decade, deep learning has driven remarkable progress in robotics and promises to solve long-standing challenges in service robotics, including household assistance, healthcare support, and collaborative assembly.
Such applications require robots to solve
Mobile Manipulation (MoMa)
tasks involving dextrous bimanual manipulation.
Current approaches often rely on
Imitation Learning (IL)
, using datasets of expert human demonstrations to train stochastic policies via
Behavior Cloning (BC)

[
7
,
34
,
5
,
3
]
.
However, these methods typically require vast amounts of data and compute due to task complexity and low sample efficiency of generative
IL
paradigms. Given the cost and difficulty of collecting expert demonstrations, and the risks of malfunction,
methods with improved sample efficiency, generalization, and optimality are critically needed.

This paper leverages a core physics-informed inductive bias in bimanual
MoMa
frequently ignored in
IL
: the robot’s bilateral morphological symmetry
[
19
]
, rooted in the reflection symmetry of the left and right sides of the body morphologies of humans and most bimanual manipulators (see
).

Intuitively, this symmetry prior implies that observations and actions
at any time are related by known reflection transformations to those of the mirrored manipulation task (see
).
Hence, any optimal policy solving one task transfers directly to the reflected task, yielding an
ambidextrous
manipulation policy
[
16
]
.

In this paper, we formalize this symmetry prior for bimanual

Our contributions are:

(i)

II
Problem Formulation

We model each bimanual manipulation task as a
Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)
defined by

(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\tau,r,\mathbb{P}_{0},\mathcal{O},\phi)

, where
{\bm{s}}\in\mathcal{S}
is the world state,
{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{A}
the agent’s action,
\tau:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
the transition kernel,
r:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the reward function,
\mathbb{P}_{0}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
the initial state distribution, and
o\in\mathcal{O}
an observation with
\phi:\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
the observation model.
We aim to learn a control policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}
from a dataset of
N
expert trajectories,
\mathbb{D}^{\text{exp}}=\{({\bm{o}}^{(n)}_{t},{\bm{a}}^{(n)}_{t})_{t=0}^{T_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{N}
, consisting of observation–action sequences generated by an expert policy
\pi_{\text{exp}}
.
We thus frame policy learning as a
Behavior Cloning
problem to learn a parametric stochastic policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|{\bm{x}})
with parameters
{\bm{\theta}}
that approximates the expert policy
\pi_{\text{exp}}(\cdot|{\bm{x}})
, where
{\bm{x}}
a history of
H
consecutive observations,
{\bm{x}}_{t}:=[{\bm{o}}_{t-H+1},\ldots,{\bm{o}}_{t}]\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{H}
.
Formally, this objective minimizes the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the expert and learned policy:

\small{\bm{\theta}}^{*}=\argmin_{{\bm{\theta}}}\underset{{\bm{x}}\sim\mathbb{D}^{\text{exp}}_{H}}{\mathbb{E}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\pi_{\text{exp}}(\cdot|{\bm{x}})\,\|\,\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|{\bm{x}})\right).

(1)

Crucially, we observe that in bimanual manipulation the
POMDP
admits a unique reflection symmetry, denoted
g_{r}
, induced by the robot’s bilateral morphological symmetry
[
19
]
(see
).
This symmetry acts
linearly
on the state, action, and observation spaces via group actions
(\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{S}$}}}}}\,)\,{:}\,\mathbb{C}_{2}\,{\times}\,\mathcal{S}\,{\to}\,\mathcal{S}
,
(\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{A}$}}}}}\,){:}\,\mathbb{C}_{2}\,{\times}\,\mathcal{A}\,{\to}\,\mathcal{A}
, and
(\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{O}$}}}}}\,){:}\,\mathbb{C}_{2}\,{\times}\,\mathcal{O}\,{\to}\,\mathcal{O}
, such that for any tuple
({\bm{s}},{\bm{a}},{\bm{o}})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{O}
the reflected tuple is given by
(g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{S}$}}}}}\,{\bm{s}},g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{A}$}}}}}\,{\bm{a}},g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{O}$}}}}}\,{\bm{o}})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{O}
.
Here,
\mathbb{C}_{2}:=\{e,g_{r}\mid g_{r}^{2}=e\}
denotes the reflection symmetry group, consisting of the identity
e
and reflection transformation
g_{r}
.

Intuitively, the reflective symmetry of the
POMDP
implies that under reflected observations, the respective optimal control action should also be reflected.
Formally, the symmetric
POMDP
imposes a
\mathbb{C}_{2}
-invariance constraint on the learned stochastic policy
[
35
,
16
,
4
]
:

\displaystyle{\bm{\theta}}^{*}{=}

\displaystyle\argmin_{{\bm{\theta}}}\underset{{\bm{x}}\sim\mathbb{D}^{\text{exp}}_{H}}{\mathbb{E}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\pi_{\text{exp}}(\cdot|{\bm{x}})\;\,\|\,\;\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|{\bm{x}})\right),

(2a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{a}}|{\bm{x}})=\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{A}$}}}}}\,{\bm{a}}|g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{X}$}}}}}\,{\bm{x}}),\;\;\forall\;\,{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{A},{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{X}.

(2b)

Here,
g\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{X}$}}}}}\,{\bm{x}}:=[g\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{O}$}}}}}\,{\bm{o}}_{t}]_{t=1}^{H}
denotes the symmetry transformation of an observation-history sample.

III
Methodology

We address the constrained
Behavior Cloning
objective in
Eq.

2
through
Flow Matching

[
17
]
, where sampling from the policy is modeled as integrating a continuous-time ODE:

\small{\bm{a}}={\bm{a}}^{(0)}+\int_{0}^{1}{\bm{v}}_{*}({\bm{a}}^{(k)},{\bm{x}},k)\,\mathrm{d}k,\;\;\text{with}\;{\bm{a}}^{(0)}\sim\bar{\pi}_{0}(\cdot|{\bm{x}}).

(3)

Here,
{\bm{v}}_{*}
is the velocity field that governs the evolution of the probability path from initial Gaussian noise
\bar{\pi}_{0}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
to the target expert action distribution
\bar{\pi}_{1}=\pi_{\text{exp}}
.

Previous work
[
15
,
14
]
demonstrates that
\bar{\pi}_{1}
is
\mathbb{G}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$$}}
-invariant when both a
\mathbb{G}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$$}}
-invariant prior (e.g., Gaussian noise) and a
\mathbb{G}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$$}}
-equivariant velocity field are employed.
Therefore, to address the symmetry-constrained
BC
objective in
Eq.

2
, we solve a
FM
problem with equivariance constraints:

\displaystyle\argmin_{{\bm{\theta}}}\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{a}}^{(k)}\sim\bar{\pi}_{k}(\cdot|{\bm{x}})\\
{\bm{x}}\sim\mathbb{D}^{\text{exp}}_{H},k\sim U[0,1]\end{subarray}}\bigl\|{\bm{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{a}}^{(k)},{\bm{x}},k)-{\bm{v}}_{*}({\bm{a}}^{(k)},{\bm{x}},k)\bigr\|_{2}^{2},

(4a)

s.t.

\displaystyle g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{A}$}}}}}\,{\bm{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{a}},{\bm{x}},k)={\bm{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}(g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{A}$}}}}}\,{\bm{a}},g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{X}$}}}}}\,{\bm{x}},k),

(4b)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\forall\;{\bm{a}}\in\mathcal{A},\;{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{X},\;k\in[0,1].

While the velocity field error in
Eq.

4a
effectively minimizes the
BC
objective in
Eq.

1

[
21
]
, enforcing equivariance of the velocity field
{\bm{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
in
Eq.

4b
ensures that the policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}
satisfies the invariance constraint in
Eq.

2b

[
14
]
.

In this paper, we consider three ways to leverage the described symmetry prior for policy learning with
FM
:

(i)

Data Augmentation
(
SymAug
,
Eq.

5
) adds reflected observation-action pairs to the dataset
[
11
,
19
]
:

\begin{split}\small\mathbb{D}^{\text{aug}}_{H}{:=}\{(g\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{X}$}}}}}\,{\bm{x}},g\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{A}$}}}}}\,{\bm{a}})\mid({\bm{x}},{\bm{a}})\in\mathbb{D}^{\text{exp}}_{H},g\in\mathbb{C}_{2}\}.\end{split}

(5)

(ii)

The
Equivariance Consistency Loss
(
EquivReg
,
Eq.

6
) encourages reflection symmetry of the velocity field through a regularized training loss, as in
[
33
]
:

\argmin_{{\bm{\theta}}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}}({\bm{\theta}})+\lambda\underbrace{\left\|\begin{subarray}{c}g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{A}$}}}}}\,{\bm{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{a}}^{(k)},{\bm{x}},k)\\
-\,{\bm{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}(g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{A}$}}}}}\,{\bm{a}}^{(k)},g_{r}\,{\mathrel{{\triangleright_{\scalebox{0.65}{$\mathcal{X}$}}}}}\,{\bm{x}},k)\end{subarray}\right\|^{2}}_{=:\mathcal{R}({\bm{\theta}})}.

(6)

(iii)

The
Explicit Equivariance Constraint
(
EquivNet
) parameterizes
{\bm{v}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}
with a
\mathbb{C}_{2}
-equivariant
NN
, guaranteeing equivariance also for out-of-distribution inputs. We introduce a novel
\mathbb{G}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$$}}
-equivariant transformer in which all modules are equivariant to an arbitrary compact group
\mathbb{G}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$$}}
; details will be released with the final paper.

VI
Conclusions

We introduced a
FM
framework for
IL
that, by construction, respects the bilateral morphological symmetry of mobile manipulators.
Our results show improved sample efficiency, generalization, and optimality, and indicate that these benefits scale with increasing task complexity.
Further, our analysis highlights the advantages of imposing the symmetry prior as an optimality constraint (
Eq.

4b
;
EquivReg
,
EquivNet
) over data augmentation.
In future work, we will integrate visual observations
and scale our method to temporal and rotational symmetries in the environment.
