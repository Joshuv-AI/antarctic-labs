 Corrosion Kinetics & Speciation of Alloy 625 in molten
 eutectic Na-Mg chloride salt Hartmann & Levitskaia, PNNL

• Thermodynamics of alloy corrosion is driven by redox
  potentials and salt impurities (moist, hydroxide, fission
  products such as Te), the corrosion kinetics can be derived
  by chromium diffusivity and chromium species formation.
• Chromium as alloying constituent is active and chromium
  chloride formation at the alloy salt interface results in a 15-
  20 µm thick corrosion affected zone.
• Elemental line profiling indicates a 2-4 µm thick surface
  layer enriched in nickel, molybdenum, and niobium. Lines scan of Alloy 625, heat  Sealed Alloy 625 Ampoules
• The self-diffusion coefficient for chromium was determined treated for 100 days at 650 °C
  to 3.0 ( ±1.3) 10-17 m2/s and the activation energy to 102
  kJ/mol. However, standard deviation were high and
  corrosion tests at additional temperatures are needed.
• Cr K-edge XANES on corroded foil specimens of alloy 625
  was unsuccessful for corrosion product speciation.

 Alloy 709 Code Case Status and Long-term Testing

• The draft Alloy 709 100,000-hour Code Case
  is currently on ASME ballot and progressing
  toward approval for Class A elevated-
  temperature service construction under
  Section III, Division 5 (High-Temperature
  Reactors).
  • A comprehensive DOE-generated data package
 has been incorporated into the ASME record
 supporting the Alloy 709 100,000-hour Code Case.
  • Reactor vendors have expressed strong interest
 and support for the inclusion of Alloy 709 as a
 structural material for Class A elevated-
 temperature service applications.

  • DOE NE AMMT Parallel R&D activities are supporting long-term Code Case testing for future
 300,000-hour and 500,000-hour design life extensions to enable broader Alloy 709
 deployment.
