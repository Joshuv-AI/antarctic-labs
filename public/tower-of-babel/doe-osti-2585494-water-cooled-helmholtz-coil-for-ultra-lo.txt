 Water-cooled Helmholtz coil for Ultra-low Magnetic Field
 Testing
Abstract
 Ultra-low magnetic field testing requires precise and finely calibrated instrumentation interacting with

very marginal magnetic fields in highly controlled environments. We designed a large Helmholtz coil system for

implementation around a 17.825-inch outer diameter Helium-3 Cryogenic fridge, to serve as both a field-zeroing

device and to enable higher resolution on magnetic field sweeps during experiments. We present finite volume

modeling and finite element analysis results which indicate the device could safely run at 20 [A] in perpetuity,

producing a magnetic field of 65 [mT], without the surface facing the Cryogenic fridge exceeding 50°C. The

overall system parts expense is $4,882.76.

After the simulation is completed, convergence
results for the various goals were plotted to ensure
that the results did not have a glaring discrepancy or
obvious error. The converged result for a given
simulation may not be physically plausible, and some
converged results are relatively easy to check by hand
calculation (such as average flow velocity) which can
help with validating convergence results which are
more difficult (local maximum temperatures). In this
case while the maximum velocity did not converge
meaningfully, average velocities and temperatures
did converge well, and the hand calculated flow
through the system aligned well with the modeled
average velocity, indicating that this model should be
relatively representative of the physical system on
fabrication and testing.
III. DISCUSSION
 Time constraints necessitated the completion
of this report before assembly of the Helmholtz coil
system, so testing and characterization of its
performance could not be completed. However,
parameterized studies of the Helmholtz coil system
operating were performed in COMSOL and
SolidWorks to attempt to bound the potential
operating space of this system if it is assembled in the
future, and are shown in figure 5. This system would
allow for a compensatory resolution of 130 [mT] in
an assembled system, as it can produce directional
field up to 65 [mT] in operation according to
COMSOL modeling, by cycling between 1 and 20
[A]. This relationship also appeared highly linear,
indicating that the resolution of this system is most
likely to be limited by the power supply resolution.
As for heat generation, parameter sweeps were
performed of the system at 20 [A] and 10 [A], from
the lowest potential water flow rate (12 [L min-1],
based on available recirculating chiller) to the lowest
bound estimate for the tap water flow rate (300 [L
min-1]). These simulations show a strong asymptotic
behavior for the bottom surface and coil temperature
and give good indicative boundaries for potential
performance and cooling requirements if the system
is constructed.
