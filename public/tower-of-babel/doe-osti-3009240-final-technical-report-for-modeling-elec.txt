Final Report DE-SC0020436

Title: Modeling electronic interactions and multielectron reactivity of actinide ions on metal-oxide
surfaces: Synthesis, characterization, and reactivity of actinide-functionalized polyoxovanadates
Program: DOE Office of Basic Energy Sciences; Heavy Element Chemistry
Program Officer: Dr. Phillip Wilk
Award Amount: $846,955                                Project Duration: 9/1/2019 – 8/31/2025

Funding from the DOE Heavy Element Chemistry Program has launched a research program in
the Matson Laboratory focused on understanding electronic interactions between actinide
complexes and redox active ligands. While the original project proposed investigation of actinide-
polyoxovanadate complexes, synthetic challenges of the target compounds and serendipity
prompted a change in research direction. Instead of actinide-substituted polyoxovanadate
complexes, our research group spent the 5-year project period investigating the synthesis,
characterization, and reactivity of actinide centers bound to both organic (pyridine dipyrrolide) and
inorganic (thiomolybdate) redox-active ligands (vide infra). Our approach was quite successful;
DOE Heavy Element Chemistry support was acknowledged in twelve publications (eleven
research articles, one review). Two manuscripts are in preparation, with anticipated submission
dates in the first half of 2026. Manuscripts highlight collaborations between the PI and members
of the Heavy Element Chemistry research community (S. Bart, Purdue University; B.
Vlaislavejevich, University of Iowa; J. Autschbach, University at Buffalo), as well as the broader
community of inorganic and theoretical chemists (C. Milsmann, University of Delaware; K.
Knowles, University of Rochester; P. Miro, University of Iowa). The project also supported the
training of multiple postdoctoral researchers (Dr. Thomas Auvray, Dr. Brett Hakey, Dr. Kamaless
Patra), a PhD student (Leyla Valerio), two MS students (Robert Lewis, Claire Dopp), and multiple
undergraduate researchers (Isabella Florez-Monriy, Lauren Lopez, Erin Stockdale).
Synthesis and electronic structure of pyridine dipyrrolide complexes of actinides. During
the last project period, we used pyridine dipyrrolide (PDP) complexes as redox-active ligands for
high- and low-valent actinide ions (Figure 1). Initial efforts focused on the synthesis and
characterization of high-valent uranyl adducts of PDP ligands. Following this, success was found
in streamlining the mechanism of U=O bond activation in [(PDP)UO2(THF)] complexes, leveraging
Mashima’s reagent (Pyz(SiMe3)2) as a silyl transfer reagent to perform silylium-coupled electron
transfer in quantitative fashion for uranyl functionalization. More recently, we have utilized the
PDP ligand for coordination of Th(IV) and U(IV) cations, resulting in the isolation of isostructral
mono- and bis-ligated Th(IV) and U(IV) complexes. The thorium derivatives are strongly
photoluminescent, with long-lived excited states (0.250-0.300 ms) and high quantum efficiencies
between 40-45%, representing a unique study describing actinide photophysics. We have
extended this chemistry to the transuranium elements to investigate the impact of increasing f-
electron count on electronic structure and electrochemistry, targeting the synthesis and
characterization of isostructural PDP adducts of the neptunium(IV) ion. Most recently, we have
published a manuscript describing redox-switchable photoluminescence of the pentavalent
[(PDP)UVO2(THF)]1- complex, isolated following reduction of the hexavalent uranyl derivative. The
UVI species are non-emissive, but upon reduction to UV anti-Kasha ligand-based luminescence is
observed.

Heavy Element Chemistry program we investigated the synthesis and reactivity of low valent
uranium complexes supported by trimolybdenum tetrasulfide cluster capped with

have focused on the reactivity of the fully-reduced form of the U-substituted thiomolybdate cluster,
(Cp*3Mo3S4)Cp*U. We have demonstrated that (Cp*3Mo3S4)Cp*U is capable of facilitating the
four-electron reduction of azobenzene, resulting in the formation of a UV bis-imido,
(Cp*3Mo3S4)Cp*U(=NPh)2, with the imido fragments positioned in a cis configuration (Figure 2,
left). Beyond this initial proof of concept, (Cp*3Mo3S4)Cp*U was also shown to be a functional
model for the reactivity of a single U catalyst on a MoS₂ surface. Addition of water to
(Cp*3Mo3S4)Cp*U results in cooperative O-H bond activation, generating a rare UIV-OH complex,
(Cp*3Mo3S4)Cp*UOH, and H2 (Figure 2, right). The latter report provides a comprehensive
understanding of the stoichiometric reactivity of low-valent U on the MoS2 surface, modeling
multiple significant intermediates involved in catalytic water reduction by U single-atom catalysts.
 In unpublished work, we developed a synthetic route to isolate heterometal-doped
thiomolybdate clusters with the aim of studying perturbations to the electronic structure upon
metal substitution, and how this impacts electron delocalization within the assembly and reactivity
at uranium. The addition of Cp*UI2(thf)3 to syn-Cp*2Mo2S2(μ-S2) resulted in the formation of
Cp*3UI2Mo2S4. This complex was found to be a suitable precursor to generate a range of
trimetallic thiomolybdate clusters (Cp*3MMo2S4)Cp*UX2 (M = Zr, Co, U; X = Cl, I; Figure 3). We
found that reactivity at the uranium center is dependent upon the distribution of electron density
within the cluster; introduction of an electropositive Zr(IV) center to Cp*3UI2Mo2S4 forms
(Cp*3ZrMo2S4)Cp*UCl2, localizing electron density at the Mo sites. Reduction of this assembly
forms (Cp*3ZrMo2S4)Cp*U, which reacts with small molecule substrates like azobenzene to form
a UVI-(bis)imido, (Cp*3ZrMo2S4)Cp*U(NPh)2, and elemental sulfur to generate a novel disulfide-
bridged product, (Cp*3ZrMo2S4)Cp*U(µ-S2)2, with side-on and end-on coordination modes across
the U and Mo centers. During the project period we also investigated the synthesis and reactivity
of a dimolybdenum diuranium cluster, (Cp*3UMo2S4)Cp*UI2, generated from addition of
Cp*UI2(thf)3 to Cp*3UI2Mo2S4 with an excess of reductant. Further reduction of the cluster
furnishes Cp*4U2Mo2S4, containing two low-valent U and Mo centers with a formal oxidation state
distribution of Mo(III)2U(III)2, confirmed through crystallographic characterization of the reduced
assembly. Subsequent investigations focused on small molecule reactivity and the extent of
electronic communication between the U centers in the cluster. We found that the highly reducing
