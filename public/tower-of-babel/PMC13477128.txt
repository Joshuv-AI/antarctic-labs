Association between bilateral radial artery grafting and long-term survival after coronary artery bypass surgery
Authors: Garry W Hamilton, Justin Ren, David M Chye, Colin Royse, Andrea Bowyer, Siven Seevenayagam, Christopher M Reid, David L Hare, Omar Farouque, Alistair Royse
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesRadial artery (RA) is recommended as a graft for the second most important coronary target after the left internal thoracic artery-to-left anterior descending artery graft. This study assessed the survival benefit associated with bilateral RA use in coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG).MethodsThis was a retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data from a binational database including patients who underwent CABG with ≥2 grafts from 2001 to 2020. Patients were stratified into 3 groups: no RA (RA-0), single RA (RA-1), and bilateral RA (RA-2) use. Inverse probability of treatment weighting was used to adjust for baseline differences. The outcome analyzed was long-term all-cause mortality.ResultsOf the 59,608 patients included, there were 31,190, 22,741, and 5677 patients in the RA-0, RA-1, and RA-2 groups, respectively. The median postoperative follow-up was 5.0 years (interquartile range, 2.3-8.6 years), with a maximum follow-up of 17.9 years. Compared with RA-0, both RA-1 (hazard ratio [HR], 0.85; 95% CI, 0.81-0.89) and RA-2 (HR, 0.74; 95% CI, 0.67-0.82) were associated with improved survival. RA-2 also demonstrated benefit over RA-1 use (HR, 0.87; 95% CI, 0.79-0.97). Inverse probability of treatment weighting was used to balance comparisons, and the proportional hazards assumption was satisfied (P = .10). Total arterial revascularization was achieved in 7.1%, 46.2%, and 81.0% of RA-0, RA-1, and RA-2 groups, respectively.ConclusionsBilateral RA grafting was associated with superior long-term survival compared with the use of 1 RA or no RA conduit. Increased adoption of RA conduits in CABG is encouraged, and preservation of both RAs is recommended when CABG is anticipated.Key Words: coronary artery bypass grafting, radial artery, bilateral radial, transradial, invasive coronary angiography, instrumented, total arterial revascularization

An incremental survival benefit was found using 2 over 1 or 0 RAs conduits.

Central Message

These data support maximizing the use of radial artery conduit in coronary artery bypass grafting.

Perspective

In this large binational database of nearly 60,000 patients who received CABG, bilateral radial artery grafting was associated with the greatest long-term survival, exceeding that of single or no radial artery use. These findings support broader adoption of multiarterial strategies and deliberate preservation of both radial arteries when surgical revascularization is anticipated.

Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is the guideline-recommended treatment for severe multivessel coronary artery disease and remains the most commonly performed cardiac surgery operation.1, 2, 3 Using the left internal thoracic artery to graft the left anterior descending artery is considered standard of care.4 Subsequent grafts vary on the basis of several factors but usually involve use of the saphenous vein (SV). However, there is now robust evidence supporting the use of the radial artery (RA) to graft the second most important coronary target.5, 6, 7, 8, 9 Despite these long-term data showing superior patency and lower mortality, the uptake of RA grafting internationally is low.10,11 This likely relates to the learning curve associated with using RA conduit and the ongoing debate about the best graft configuration to maximize the patient's lifetime benefit from CABG.

With a growing body of observational data supporting multiarterial or total arterial grafting9,12,13 and a randomized controlled trial (the TA Trial) underway,14 arterial conduit use is very likely to increase. The durability of the RA and low complication rates associated with its harvest make it an attractive conduit in experienced hands. Using bilateral RA to facilitate total arterial grafting is therefore appealing; however, studies have not investigated whether such a practice is associated with improved outcomes. In this large binational database study, we aimed to establish whether there is an incremental survival benefit to using bilateral RA grafts in CABG. This analysis may inform surgeons concerning conduit selection and inform cardiologists performing invasive coronary angiography of the potential benefits of avoiding transradial access.

Methods

Patients who underwent primary isolated CABG with at least 2 grafts between 2001 and 2020 were included in this retrospective observational cohort study, which used data from the Australian & New Zealand Society of Cardiac & Thoracic Surgeons (ANZSCTS) Database. This binational database prospectively records information regarding patient demographics, preoperative comorbidities, procedural details, and clinical outcomes, which are checked and validated regularly via multiple administrative linkages. Hospital participation is not mandatory but highly encouraged. Patient participation uses an opt-out consent model.

Patients were grouped for the analysis according to the number of RA conduits that were harvested. Exclusion criteria included previous CABG, concomitant cardiac procedures, age younger than 18 years, single-graft procedures, or incomplete data. Ethics approval was obtained from the Melbourne Health Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC ID: 2025.089, approved on May 21, 2025), with a waiver of individual patient consent granted because of the retrospective nature of the study and the use of nonidentifiable, encrypted data. Mortality status was verified through linkage with the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare National Death Index, an independent government organization capturing all deaths, which was valid up until January 2018. Patients included in this study after this date were those for whom participating hospitals had officially reported event rates, and therefore all patients included in the quantitative analysis had complete follow-up data. Within the time frame of the dataset, there was no data field to document whether patients had undergone invasive coronary angiography via the used RA. We can report that from about 2014 onwards, RA access was increasing, reaching a majority of patients by the end of the time frame of the study.15

Established in 2001, the ANZSCTS Cardiac Surgery Database is a clinical quality assurance database encompassing Australia and New Zealand that prospectively collects detailed procedural data from participating hospitals. Parameters include patient demographics, preoperative risk factors, operative variables, and postoperative outcomes using standardized definitions and data-entry protocols similar to those of the Society of Thoracic Surgeons Adult Cardiac Surgery Database. Data are collected via a secure, web-based interface and stored within the Monash Secure eResearch Platform, a controlled, high-security research environment designed to prevent copying, exporting, or unauthorized access. All data are fully deidentified, and access to patient identifiers is strictly prohibited.

This study was conducted in full compliance with state and Commonwealth privacy legislation. All analyses were performed within the Secure eResearch Platform infrastructure under the supervision of the ANZSCTS Data Management Centre, with access restricted to approved investigators who had signed binding confidentiality agreements. No data were stored or transferred outside the secure environment, ensuring strict adherence to all ethical, legal, and data governance protocols.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was long-term all-cause mortality from the date of index operation to the event date.

Statistical Analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using R, version 4.0.5 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Baseline characteristics were described using mean ± SD or median with interquartile range for continuous variables, depending on the distributional normality, and frequencies with percentages for categorical variables.

Matching

To minimize confounding arising from nonrandom treatment allocation, propensity scores were estimated using a generalized boosted model (GBM), a machine learning–based iterative algorithm. The model hyperparameters were specified with a maximum of 10,000 trees, an interaction depth of 3, and a shrinkage rate of 0.01, with balance optimized via average standardized mean differences across the 3 grafting strategies of interest: no radial conduits (RA-0), 1 radial conduit (RA-1), or 2 radial conduits (RA-2). The GBM incorporated a comprehensive set of baseline covariates, including demographic factors (age, sex, smoking history), cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, diabetes mellitus), comorbidities (dialysis dependence, cerebrovascular disease, peripheral vascular disease, chronic respiratory disease), and relevant cardiac history (previous myocardial infarction, infective endocarditis, arrhythmia, cardiogenic shock). To further enhance model robustness, the propensity model included markers of functional status and disease severity, such as New York Heart Association class, Canadian Cardiovascular Society angina class, left ventricular ejection fraction, body mass index, serum creatinine, presence of left main coronary artery disease, and the number of diseased coronary territories. Operative variables, including the number of bypass grafts, operative urgency, use of cardiopulmonary bypass (on-pump vs off-pump), and year of surgery, were also incorporated to account for surgical complexity and temporal trends in clinical practice.

Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was then applied using the GBM-derived propensity scores to construct a weighted pseudo-population in which baseline covariates were balanced across the 3 groups. Marginally stabilized weights were used to improve precision and reduce variance inflation. Covariate balance was assessed using standardized mean differences, with a threshold of <0.1 considered indicative of adequate balance and minimal residual confounding.

The analysis evaluated long-term all-cause mortality using weighted Cox proportional hazards regression models. Adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs were reported for comparisons between RA-0, RA-1, and RA-2 groups. The proportional hazards assumption was formally tested using weighted Schoenfeld residuals. To visualize survival differences over time, IPTW-adjusted Kaplan-Meier curves were generated for the primary investigation.

Sensitivity Analysis

Two sensitivity analyses were performed to assess the robustness of the primary findings: (1) a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model adjusted for all available baseline covariates, and (2) the IPTW-weighted Cox model with completeness of revascularization, defined as the number of conduits being equal to or greater than the number of diseased vessels, included as an additional covariate.

Results

A total of 59,608 eligible patients undergoing primary isolated CABG operations with at least 2 grafts were included in this complete-case analysis, of whom 31,190 received no RA grafting, 22,741 received 1 RA and 5677 received bilateral RA grafting (Figure 1). The median postoperative follow-up was 5.0 years (interquartile range, 2.3-8.6 years), with a maximum follow-up of 17.9 years.

Baseline Characteristics

Baseline characteristics varied across patient groups. The mean age was progressively younger with increasing RA use. The prevalence of preoperative dialysis, cardiogenic shock, and resuscitation was lowest in the RA-2 group and progressively greater in the RA-1 and RA-0 groups. Elective surgery was most frequent in the RA-2 group, whereas urgent surgery was more common in patients in whom 1 or no RA conduits were used. Triple-vessel disease was most frequently observed in the RA-2 group (Table 1). The proportion of patients undergoing total arterial revascularization (TAR) increased with the number of RA conduits harvested. In the RA-0 group, 7.1% of patients underwent TAR (2199/31,190). In the RA-1 group, 46.2% received TAR (10,505/22,741), and in the RA-2 group, 81.0% underwent TAR (4600/5677) (Table 2).

Analysis

After IPTW adjustment, all standardized mean differences were below the pre-established threshold, ensuring balanced comparisons (Figure 2). The adjusted relative risk of long-term mortality remained significantly lower in both the RA-2 (HR, 0.74; 95% CI, 0.67-0.82; P < .001) and RA-1 (HR, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.81-0.89; P < .001) groups compared with RA-0. In the direct comparison between RA-2 and RA-1, procedures with bilateral RA grafts were associated with a further survival benefit (HR, 0.87; 95% CI, 0.79-0.97; P = .011). The proportional hazards assumption was satisfied (P = .10). Adjusted Kaplan-Meier survival differences and estimated time-point survival percentages are depicted in Figure 3 and Table 3, respectively. The unadjusted Kaplan-Meier curve is presented in Figure 4, demonstrating a consistent survival benefit.

Sensitivity Analysis

The multivariable Cox regression model, adjusting for all available baseline characteristics, generated results consistent with the primary investigation. Long-term survival was significantly greater in the RA-2 group compared with RA-0 (HR, 0.77; 95% CI, 0.72-0.82; P < .001) and in the RA-1 group compared with RA-0 (HR, 0.84; 95% CI, 0.81-0.88; P < .001). Survival benefits also remained significant in the RA-2 versus RA-1 comparison (HR, 0.91; 95% CI, 0.85-0.97; P = .004). The proportional hazards assumption was again satisfied (P = .15).

After adjustment for completeness of revascularization, the long-term survival benefit persisted for both RA-2 (HR, 0.74; 95% CI, 0.67-0.92; P < .001) and RA-1 (HR, 0.85; 95% CI, 0.81-0.89; P < .001) when compared against the RA-0 group and remained significant for the RA-2 versus RA-1 comparison (HR, 0.87; 95% CI, 0.79-0.96; P = .007).

Discussion

In this study of almost 60,000 patients undergoing CABG, we found that the use of both RA conduits was associated with a survival advantage. This report of bilateral RA is the largest to date, designed as a would-be intention-to-treat analysis with the view that if both RAs were harvested, then at least 2 RA grafts or distal anastomoses will be used. Although causation cannot be inferred on the basis of this observational study, IPTW in this very large cohort standardizes many confounders.

The use of any RA conduit in real-world practice internationally is very low.10,11,16 Our findings associating an incremental survival advantage with the use of multiple RA grafts is therefore particularly relevant. On the basis of these data coupled with pre-existing evidence and both US and European societal guidelines supporting the use of RA conduit, contemporary practice should evolve to consider routinely maximizing the use of the RA in CABG. Procedural cardiologists in the cardiac catheterization laboratory should also contemplate prioritizing protection of the RA at the time of angiography, particularly for those patients likely to need CABG, in order to maximize RA conduit availability.17

There is now robust evidence from randomized trials, patient-level meta-analyses, and real-world observational data supporting the use of the RA as a CABG conduit.9,18, 19, 20 There is further evidence that long-term failure of the RA is rare and may be equivalent to the left internal thoracic artery.6,21 In the uncommon event of RA graft failure, percutaneous coronary intervention is feasible, safe, and likely better tolerated then SV graft percutaneous coronary intervention.22

An inverse relationship between RA as compared with SV use is likely a driving factor of our findings. Between 6 and 7 years after surgery, there is a divergence in the survival curves, with a larger magnitude of benefit seen in those with both RA conduits used. The known attrition rate of SV grafts may be resulting in greater event rates ≥7 years after surgery in the RA-1 and RA-0 groups, respectively. This is a hypothesis-generating observation.

The use of RA was marginally more frequent in male patients, consistent with previous reports of sex-based differences in conduit selection. This disparity may reflect differences in patient characteristics, because women undergoing CABG are often older and have a greater burden of comorbidities. In addition, smaller vessel caliber in female patients may influence surgeon preference as the result of perceived technical challenges or concerns regarding graft patency. Importantly, increasing evidence supports the benefit of multiarterial grafting in both sexes,20 suggesting that lower use of RA conduits in women may represent an opportunity to improve outcomes.

Whether the RA improves outcomes compared with the right internal thoracic artery (RITA) is less certain, although the 15-year results of the RAPCO (Radial Artery Patency and Clinical Outcomes) trial suggest a lower rate of major adverse cardiac events with the use of the RA over RITA when grafting the second most important coronary target.5 The present study did not evaluate use of the RITA nor compare its efficacy with that of the RA. It is therefore uncertain whether prioritizing RA conduit use over using the RITA is associated with improved outcomes, and it may be that RITA use in aid of facilitating TAR is also beneficial.

Observational data and post-hoc randomized trial analyses have shown multiarterial grafting and TAR are beneficial. The most likely mechanism relates to avoidance of SV conduit,6,9,10,23,24 and a randomized trial (the Total Arterial Trial) is underway.14 In our study, 7.1% of patients in the RA-0 group received TAR, whereas the use of 1 RA increased TAR to 46.2% and bilateral RA use resulted in 81% TAR. Thus, RA conduits are integral to the attainment of TAR. The Arterial Revascularization Trial (ART), which investigated whether there was a survival benefit to bilateral internal thoracic artery grafting, found no benefit; however, a post hoc analysis found benefit for patients receiving TAR.24 Additional to the benefits of TAR itself, which bilateral RA conduit use facilitates, the specific choice of arterial conduit may also be prognostically important.

Conduit harvest techniques and consequences to the donor region warrant consideration. Although the upper limb and hand are vitally important, RA harvest has not been associated with donor-site complications, whereas patients prefer upper-limb to lower-limb conduit harvest.25,26 Potential future dialysis is an important consideration in conduit selection, particularly those with advanced renal dysfunction. However, RA harvest does not preclude future hemodialysis access, because both brachiocephalic and brachiobasilic fistulae remain feasible options.27 Avoiding use of the RITA, particularly in patients with diabetes mellitus, may reduce the incidence of deep sternal wound infection.28 This is another advantage to the use of bilateral RA conduit because the increased RA availability presumably corresponds to lower rates of bilateral thoracic artery use. In general, avoiding RITA harvest in pursuit of TAR appears sensible, given the RA is a safe and durable alternative to SV grafts.

Transradial access for invasive coronary angiography is now recommended and is the most common procedural access site. Many patients undergoing CABG will have therefore had previous RA instrumentation.15,29 Although instrumentation does not contraindicate use of the RA as a graft, invasive coronary angiography may render the vessel unsuitable for grafting.30 Avoidance of transradial access at the time of invasive coronary angiography is occasionally considered, although the risks of femoral vascular complications are usually deemed more significant than a theoretical benefit of maximizing RA graft availability in the event CABG is required.31,32 Nonetheless, this view is not universal.33 The risks associated with conventional femoral artery access can be significantly reduced by the use of ultrasound imaging34, 35, 36, 37 which may approximate the RA approach. Given the likelihood of needing CABG can be predicted,17 a selective RA preservation or avoidance strategy is plausible to identify patients most likely to benefit from avoiding transradial access for invasive coronary angiography.

Our study is consistent with the greater body of evidence supporting avoidance of SV grafts and preferentially grafting with the RA where feasible. Surgeons and cardiologists must work together to preserve and protect the RA in aid of both being available for CABG. Although certain geographic regions, including ours, have very high rates of RA conduit use, these and other data, as well as contemporary guidelines, suggest the international community should evolve their practices to prioritize the preservation of RA for use as a conduit, which may improve long-term survival after CABG.

Limitations

This is the largest report of bilateral RA conduit to date. Use of RA resulted in large increases in the rate of TAR, at rates 10-fold greater than is common elsewhere, which may be a mechanism for increased survival. These data do not include angiographic patency, although reports of long-term patency of arterial grafts suggest this contributes to the observed improvement in mortality. However, because of its observational nature, this study remains subject to inherent bias despite IPTW and a very large sample size and should be considered hypothesis-generating. The number of RA conduits harvested does not directly equate with the number of distal anastomoses arising from RA, because the use of sequential grafting was relatively frequent with RA but not with SV. The impact of previous instrumentation for transradial invasive coronary angiography on RA grafts is not captured in this dataset because no field was present for this purpose, but by the end of the study time frame, it is likely that most patients underwent a transradial approach. It is often considered that experience in the use of RA is essential for clinical success, but this is unlikely to be a significant factor, noting our study was a binational database experience and not confined to individual surgeons or institutions.

Conclusions

The use of bilateral RA conduits was associated with better survival compared with a single RA conduit, whereas both were superior to the absence of RA use. These data highlight the importance of prioritizing the use of the RA at the time of CABG and protecting this artery at the time of invasive coronary angiography.
