Hookworms in the Stomach: Concurrent Gastric Ancylostomiasis and Gastric Adenocarcinoma Video Case Report

Anil Mathew Philip, Victoria Alagiozian-Angelova, Anas Almoghrabi

CASE REPORT

A 79-year-old woman with heart failure and hypertension, who traveled between Honduras and the United States, presented with 3 months of epigastric pain, vomiting, diarrhea, and 60-pound weight loss. Evaluation revealed iron-deficiency anemia, eosinophilia (1,200 cells/μL), and a gastric ulcer concerning for malignancy, with retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy and hepatic lesions.

Esophagogastroduodenoscopy revealed a large, ulcerated lesion with a fibrinous base and friable margins along the lesser curvature and incisura (Video 1, Figure ), confirmed as moderately differentiated gastric adenocarcinoma with a desmoplastic stroma (Figure ). Translucent, S-shaped worms were also seen attached to the gastric mucosa (Video 1, Figure ) and duodenum, confirmed as Ancylostoma species on histology (Figure ).

The patient was treated with albendazole and neoadjuvant chemotherapy, followed by total gastrectomy. Her postoperative course was complicated by multidrug-resistant urosepsis and multiorgan failure; she died 3 weeks later.

Gastric hookworm infestation is rarely visualized endoscopically, as Ancylostoma typically localizes to the small intestine, though upper gastrointestinal involvement is reported., Parasite-malignancy coexistence is similarly uncommon, as with a reported Anisakis larva on early gastric cancer. This case underscores the importance of considering parasitic coinfection in patients from endemic regions with weight loss, anemia, and eosinophilia, even after a malignant diagnosis has been established.
