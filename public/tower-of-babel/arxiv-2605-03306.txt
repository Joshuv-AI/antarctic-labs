Exponential-Size Circuit Complexity is Comeager in Symmetric Exponential Time
John M. Hitchcock

Abstract
Lutz (1987) introduced resource-bounded category and showed the circuit size class 𝖲𝖨𝖹𝖤 ⁡ ( 2 n n ) \mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n}) is meager within 𝖤𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{ESPACE} . Li (2024) established that the symmetric alternation class 𝖲 2 𝖤 \mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2} contains problems requiring circuits of size 2 n n \frac{2^{n}}{n} . In this note, we extend resource-bounded category to 𝖲 2 𝖤 \mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2} by defining meagerness relative to single-valued 𝖥𝖲 2 𝖯 \mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2} strategies in the Banach-Mazur game. We show that Li’s 𝖥𝖲 2 𝖯 \mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2} algorithm for the Range Avoidance problem yields a winning strategy, proving that 𝖲𝖨𝖹𝖤 ⁡ ( 2 n n ) \mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n}) is meager in 𝖲 2 𝖤 \mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2} . Consequently, languages requiring exponential-size circuits are comeager in 𝖲 2 𝖤 \mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2} : they are typical with respect to resource-bounded category.

Introduction

Resource-bounded category, introduced by Lutz
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, provides a way to analyze the typical properties of complexity classes using topological notions of size. Lutz showed that the class
\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
of languages decidable by circuits of size
\frac{2^{n}}{n}
is
meager

within the exponential-space class
\mathsf{ESPACE}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Lutz
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
)

\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
is meager in
\mathsf{ESPACE}
.

The symmetric exponential-time class
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
is an exponential version of the class
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}

[
5
,
21
]
and it lies between
\mathsf{E}
and
\mathsf{ESPACE}
. Building on work by Korten
[
12
]
and Chen, Hirahara, and Ren
[
6
]
, Li
[
13
]
recently proved that
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
contains languages that require near-maximum circuit size.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Li
[
13
]
)

\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}\not\subseteq\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
.

This solved a longstanding open problem to show the second level of the exponential-time hierarchy requires exponential-size circuits
[
10
,
3
,
19
,
20
,
9
,
1
,
2
]
.
Li’s proof technique involves constructing a hard language using a
single-valued

\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm (a function problem analogue of
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
). Given these developments, a natural question is whether the category framework can be refined to operate within
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
. Can we show that languages requiring large circuits are not just present, but are in fact
typical
within
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
?

In this note, we answer this question affirmatively. We adapt the resource-bounded category machinery to the class
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
by defining meagerness relative to single-valued
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-computable constructors in the Banach-Mazur game.
Our main result shows that, analogous to Lutz’s result for
\mathsf{ESPACE}
, small circuits are atypical within
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
is meager in
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.

We prove this by demonstrating that Li’s single-valued
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm for the range avoidance problem
[
12
,
6
]
provides a winning strategy against
\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
in the appropriate Banach-Mazur game. Thus, languages requiring exponential-size circuits are not just present in
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
—they are typical in the sense of resource-bounded category: the class of such languages is comeager in
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.

4

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Resource-Bounded Category

Resource-bounded category was introduced by Lutz
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
The
Cantor space

\mathsf{C}
is the set of all infinite binary
sequences. A
language
(or
decision problem
) is a subset
of
\{0,1\}^{*}
. We identify each language with the element of Cantor
space that is its characteristic sequence according to the standard
enumeration
s_{0}=\lambda,s_{1}=0,s_{2}=1,s_{3}=00,\ldots
of
\{0,1\}^{*}
. In this way, complexity classes (sets of languages) are viewed as subsets of Cantor space.

Baire category classifies sets into two types:
first category

and
second category
. First category sets are also commonly
called
meager
. A set is meager if it is a countable union of
nowhere dense sets. An equivalent definition comes from Banach-Mazur
games using functions called constructors.

Definition 2.1
.

A
constructor
is a total, single-valued function
\delta:\{0,1\}^{*}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
which satisfies
x\underset{\neq}{\sqsubset}\delta(x)
for all
x\in\{0,1\}^{*}
.
The
result
of a constructor is the unique sequence
R(\delta)\in\mathsf{C}
that extends
\delta^{(n)}(\lambda)
for all
n
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathsf{C}
and let
\Gamma_{\rm I}
and
\Gamma_{\rm II}
be
two classes of functions. In the
Banach-Mazur game

G[X;\Gamma_{\rm I},\Gamma_{\rm II}]
there are two players I and
II. A
strategy
in the game is a constructor. In a play of
the game, player I chooses a strategy
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\rm I}
and
player II chooses a strategy
\delta\in\Gamma_{\rm II}
. The
result
of this play is the sequence
R(\gamma,\delta)=R(\delta\circ\gamma)
.
Intuitively, the result is the sequence obtained when the two
players start with the empty string and take turns extending it with
their strategies. A
winning strategy
for player II is a
strategy
\delta\in\Gamma_{\rm II}
such that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\rm I}
,
R(\gamma,\delta)\not\in X
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(Banach and Mazur)

A class
X\subseteq\mathsf{C}
is meager if and only if player II has a
winning strategy in the game
G[X;{\mathsf{all}},{\mathsf{all}}]
.

In resource-bounded category,
\Delta
denotes a
resource bound

[
17
,
16
]
. Examples of
\Delta
include:

\displaystyle{\mathsf{all}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\mid f:\{0,1\}^{*}\to\{0,1\}^{*}\}

\displaystyle{\mathsf{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\mid f\textrm{ is polynomial-time computable}\}

\displaystyle{\mathsf{pspace}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f\mid f\textrm{ is polynomial-space computable}\}

For a resource bound
\Delta
, we define the
result class

R(\Delta)=\{R(\delta)\mid\delta\in\Delta\textrm{ is a constructor}\}.

Then
R({\mathsf{all}})=\mathsf{C}
,
R({\mathsf{p}})=\mathsf{E}
,
and
R({\mathsf{pspace}})=\mathsf{ESPACE}

[
16
]
. Resource-bounded category
[
16
]
is defined by requiring
player II’s winning strategy to be computable within a resource bound
\Delta
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathsf{C}
and let
\Delta
be a resource bound.

1.

X
is
\Delta
-meager
if player II has a winning
strategy in the game
G[X;{\mathsf{all}},\Delta]
.

2.

X
is
\Delta
-comeager
if
X^{c}
is
\Delta
-meager.

3.

X
is
meager in
R(\Delta)
if
X\cap R(\Delta)

is
\Delta
-meager.

4.

X
is
comeager in
R(\Delta)
if
X^{c}
is meager in
R(\Delta)
.

The
resource-bounded Baire category theorem

[
16
]

tells us that
R(\Delta)
is not
\Delta
-meager.

2.2
Symmetric Alternation

Symmetric alternation is a concept in computational complexity theory introduced independently by Canetti
[
5
]
and Russell and Sundaram
[
21
]
with different, but equivalent definitions
[
4
]
. We follow the definition due to Canetti.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
T\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
.
We say that a language
L\in\mathsf{S}_{2}\mathsf{TIME}[T(n)]
if there exists an
O(T(n))
-time verifier

V(x,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})

that takes

x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\{0,1\}^{T(n)}

as input, satisfying

(\exists\pi_{1})(\forall\pi_{2})V(x,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=\chi_{L}(x)

and

(\exists\pi_{2})(\forall\pi_{1})V(x,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=\chi_{L}(x).

Definition 2.5
.

We define the symmetric alternation complexity classes

\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}=\bigcup_{c=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{S}_{2}\mathsf{TIME}[n^{c}].

and

\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}=\bigcup_{c=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{S}_{2}\mathsf{TIME}[2^{cn}].

Symmetric alternation is used to compute single-valued functions as follows
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

A
single‐valued

\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm
A
is specified by a polynomial
\ell(\cdot)
together with a polynomial‐time verifier

V_{A}(x,\pi_{1},\pi_{2}).
On input
x\in\{0,1\}^{*}
, we say that
A

outputs
a string
y_{x}\in\{0,1\}^{*}
if the following two conditions hold:

1.

There exists
\pi_{1}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}
such that for every
\pi_{2}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}
,

V_{A}(x,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\;\allowbreak=\;y_{x}.

2.

There exists
\pi_{2}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}
such that for every
\pi_{1}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell(|x|)}
,

V_{A}(x,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\;=\;y_{x}.

Note that
Section

2.2
prescribes a single-valued
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm for
\chi_{L}
.

Li
[
13
]
used a single-valued
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm for the range avoidance problem
[
12
,
6
]
to prove
Theorem

1.2
that
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
requires exponential-size circuits.
The following is a corollary to Li’s proof.

Theorem 2.7
.

(Li
[
13
]
)

There is an
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm
{\cal A}
that on input
{}^{2^{n}}
outputs a truth-table
f\in\{0,1\}^{2^{n}}
such that
f
has circuit complexity at least
\frac{2^{n}}{n}
.

Remark 2.8
.

The
\frac{2^{n}}{n}
bound in
Theorem

1.2
,
Theorem

2.7
, and our results in Section
3
may be replaced by
\frac{2^{n}}{n}\left(1+\frac{\alpha\log n}{n}\right)
for any fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)

[
17
,
7
,
8
]
. We use
\frac{2^{n}}{n}
to simplify notation.

3
Category in Symmetric Exponential Time

Recall that
R({\mathsf{p}})=\mathsf{E}
and
R({\mathsf{pspace}})=\mathsf{ESPACE}
. We now show that the result class of
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
is
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

R(\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2})=\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
L=R(A)
where
A
is a single-valued
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm. Let
V_{A}
be as in
Section

2.2
.
On input
x
, let
n
be the index such that
s_{n}=x
. The idea is to start with
z_{0}=\lambda
and compute
z_{i+1}=A(z_{i})
until
|z_{i}|>n
. Then the answer for
x
is
z_{i}[n]
. We make at most
n\leq 2^{|x|}
calls to
A
.
Formally, define a verifier
V
that on input
s_{n}=x
also takes two tuples of proofs
t_{1}=\langle\pi_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}\rangle
and

t_{2}=\langle\pi_{2}^{(0)},\ldots,\pi_{2}^{(k-1)}\rangle
. Then we compute
z_{0}=\lambda
and
z_{i+1}=V_{A}(z_{i},\pi_{1}^{(i)},\pi_{2}^{(i)})
for each
0\leq i<k
.
If
z_{i}\underset{\neq}{\sqsubset}z_{i+1}
for all
i
and

|z_{k}|>n
, then
V(x,t_{1},t_{2})
outputs
z_{i}[n]
. Otherwise,
V(x,t_{1},t_{2})
outputs
0
.
The total length of the proofs is polynomial in
n
, so the total proof length is
2^{O(|x|)}
. Since
V_{A}
is polynomial-time,
V
runs in exponential-time and it follows that
L\in\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.

Let
L\in\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
and let
V
be a verifier for
L
as in
Section

2.2
. On input
z
, we let
n=|z|
. We use
V
on input
s_{n}
to extend
z
by one bit. If
V(s_{n},\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=1
, we output
V^{\prime}(z,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=z1
. If
V(s_{n},\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=0
, we output
V^{\prime}(z,\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=z0
. Then
V^{\prime}
specifies a single-valued
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm because
|s_{n}|
is logarithmic in
|z|
. We have
R(V^{\prime})=L
, so
L\in R(\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2})
.
∎

We can now fully specify the symmetric alternation category notion used in this paper. We plug
Section

3
into
Section

2.1
:

1.

X
is
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-meager
if player II has a winning
strategy in the game
G[X;{\mathsf{all}},\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}]
.

2.

X
is
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-comeager
if
X^{c}
is
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-meager.

3.

X
is
meager in
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
if
X\cap\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}

is
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-meager.

4.

X
is
comeager in
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
if
X^{c}
is meager in
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.

Lutz
[
14
]
proved that
\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
is meager in
\mathsf{ESPACE}
where the strategy uses a brute force voting method inspired by Kannan
[
10
]
. We can replace this by Li’s single-valued
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
algorithm from
Theorem

2.7
.

Theorem 3.2
.

\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
is
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-meager.

Proof.

Let
{\cal A}
be the algorithm from
Theorem

2.7
.
On input
x
, compute
n
so that
2^{n-1}\leq|x|\leq 2^{n}-1
. Let
s(n)=\frac{2^{n}}{n}
.
Our constructor
\delta
outputs

\delta(x)=x0^{2^{n}-|x|-1}{\cal A}(0^{2^{n}}).

The
{}^{2^{n}-|x|-1}
is to finish defining the language at length
n-1
.
We then use
{\cal A}
to define the language at length
n
. Thus
|\delta(x)|=2^{n+1}-1
and
\delta(x)
defines a subset of
\{0,1\}^{\leq n}
.

Let
\gamma
be any constructor. Then for infinitely many lengths
n
,
R(\gamma,\delta)=R(\delta\circ\gamma)
does not have an
s(n)
-size circuit at length
n
. Therefore
R(\gamma,\delta)\not\in\mathsf{SIZE}(s(n))
and
\delta
wins the Banach-Mazur game, so
\mathsf{SIZE}(s(n))
is
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-meager.
∎

Theorem

1.3
is a corollary of
Theorem

3.2
, because
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
-meager implies meager in
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.
See
1.3

Our title result follows immediately from
Theorem

1.3
.

Corollary 3.3
.

The class of problems requiring exponential-size circuits is comeager in
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{E}}_{2}
.

Another corollary is meagerness of
\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
in
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}
under a derandomization hypothesis. This is because standard derandomization hypotheses
[
11
]
collapse
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
to
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
via derandomization of
\mathsf{FZPP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
.
Resource-bounded category in
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}
is defined analogously to category in
\mathsf{E}
, using polynomial-time constructors that have access to an
\mathsf{NP}
oracle in
Section

2.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

R(\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}})=\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}
.

1.

X
is
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
-meager
if player II has a winning
strategy in the game
G[X;{\mathsf{all}},\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}]
.

2.

X
is
meager in
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}
if
X\cap\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}

is
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
-meager.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}
requires
2^{\Omega(n)}
-size
\mathsf{NP}
-oracle circuits, then
\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
is meager in
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}
.

Proof.

Cai
[
4
]
showed that
\mathsf{S}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}\subseteq\mathsf{ZPP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
. It also holds that single-valued
\mathsf{FS}^{\mathsf{P}}_{2}
is contained in single-valued
\mathsf{FZPP}^{\mathsf{NP}}

[
6
]
. Under the hypothesis, techniques of Klivans and van Melkebeek
[
11
]
derandomize
\mathsf{FZPP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
to
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}

[
6
]
. Thus the constructor in the proof of
Theorem

3.2
is in
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
, so
\mathsf{SIZE}(\frac{2^{n}}{n})
is
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}
-meager and is meager in
\mathsf{E}^{\mathsf{NP}}
.
∎

Conclusion

Lutz and Mayordomo
[
18
]
asked if
{\mathsf{P}/\mathsf{poly}}
has measure 0 in a class smaller than
