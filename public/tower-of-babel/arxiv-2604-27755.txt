Abstract
. We introduce Gårding polynomials, a class of real multivariate polynomials defined via positivity regions invariant under translation by positive directions and closed under strictly positive affine transformations. We establish a structural theorem providing two complementary characterizations of this class: one via reduction to the multi-affine case through polarization, and another via a recursive condition involving partial derivatives. The class of Gårding polynomials strictly extends that of real stable polynomials while retaining many of their structural properties. In particular, multi-affine Gårding polynomials with nonnegative coefficients satisfy the Rayleigh property, and their positive univariate specializations yield ultra log-concave coefficient sequences. Moreover, the Gårding property for several matroid generating functions is preserved under natural matroid operations. As applications, we obtain new negative dependence results for generating functions associated with various classes of matroids and graphs—many of which lie beyond the reach of real stability or Lorentzian methods—as well as for characteristic polynomials of certain matrix classes.

Motivated by these developments, we introduce a new geometric class of multivariate polynomials based on the geometric theory of positivity regions, which strictly extends real stability and captures phenomena beyond both hyperbolic and Lorentzian theories.
Here, a positivity region means a connected component of the set where
a given non-zero real polynomial takes positive values.
An essential
requirement is that translations in positive directions remain within
the same positivity region of the given polynomial. In this case, we
say that the polynomial passes the positive ray test (PRT). This requirement
leads to a new class of polynomials, called Gårding polynomials, that
strictly extends the class of real stable polynomials while retaining
many interesting features. See Part 1.

A polynomial is called
multi-affine
if it has degree at most
1

in each variable. Multi-affine Gårding polynomials are characterized
precisely by the existence of a positivity region passing the positive
ray test. A general multivariate Gårding polynomial is defined as an
appropriate pullback of a multi-affine Gårding polynomial by a strictly
positive affine map; see Definition
4.9
. Details of
our notation are introduced in later sections.

The main result of this paper is a structural theorem that provides equivalent geometric and algebraic characterizations of Gårding polynomials.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a nonzero polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]
,
the following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is
Gårding
, i.e.
f=\mu^{*}g
where
\mu
is a strictly positive
affine map and
g
is a multi-affine
Gårding
polynomial;

(2)

The polarization of
f
is multi-affine and
Gårding
;

(3)

For each multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that
\partial^{\alpha}f\not\equiv 0
,

\partial^{\alpha}f
admits a unique positivity region satisfying the positive
ray test, and its positivity region is contained in those of its partial derivatives.

In Theorem
1.1
, the
multi-affine case serves as the fundamental building block, while general
Gårding polynomials reduce to multi-affine case via strictly positive affine pullbacks
and polarization, and are further characterized by a recursive structure
through partial derivatives. This combination of reduction and recursion
will be central throughout the paper, both in the structural theory and in applications.

{\mathrm{G\mathring{a}rding}}{}
polynomials are closely related to the real stable and Lorentzian polynomials. Real stable polynomials, up to a sign, form a proper subclass of Gårding polynomials (Theorem
4.16
). On the other hand, homogeneous
{\mathrm{G\mathring{a}rding}}{}
polynomials form a proper subclass of Lorentzian polynomials. These three classes coincide in the case of homogeneous degree
2
polynomials with nonnegative coefficients. See Section
11
for further discussion.

Similarly to real stable and Lorentzian polynomials, the class of Gårding polynomials is closed under a range of natural algebraic and topological operations and admits a linear preserver theory
[
12
,
15
]
. See Section
7

10
.

A key consequence of this structure is that Gårding polynomials provide a
systematic source of negative dependence phenomena, extending well beyond the
range of real stability and Lorentzian theory.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f(\mathbf{x})
be a Gårding polynomial with nonnegative coefficients. Let

\Gamma^{n}_{+}:=\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}>0\text{ for all }i\}.

Then:

(1)

f
is Rayleigh. Equivalently, for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Gamma^{n}_{+}}
,

\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x})\,\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{x})\geq f(\mathbf{x})\,\partial_{ij}f(\mathbf{x}).

(2)

For any
\textbf{a}\in\overline{\Gamma^{n}_{+}}
, consider the univariate specialization

g(t):=f(\textbf{a}t).

If
g(t)\not\equiv 0
, then its coefficient sequence is ultra log-concave.

(3)

If
f
is also homogeneous, then
f
is Lorentzian. In particular,
f
is log-concave on
\Gamma^{n}_{+}
. Moreover,
f
satisfies the negative association property.

The Rayleigh property, ultra log-concavity, and negative association are fundamental notions that have been extensively studied in probability theory and combinatorics
[
54
,
23
,
24
,
68
,
13
,
45

.

In this paper, we study the generating functions naturally associated with matroids,
including the basis generating function and those arising from spanning and
cospanning sets. These generating functions encode rich combinatorial information
and exhibit favorable structural properties within our framework.

Theorem 1.3
.

The Gårding property of the spanning-set (respectively, cospanning-set)
generating function is preserved under direct sums, series–parallel connections,
deletion, and contraction.

Consequently, if a matroid is obtained from matroids whose spanning-set
(respectively, cospanning-set) generating functions are Gårding via a finite
sequence of these operations, then its spanning-set (respectively, cospanning-set)
generating function is also Gårding.

As an application of our framework, we establish the Gårding property—and hence the full Rayleigh property—for generating functions associated with several important classes. Verification of the Gårding property requires elementary but delicate analytic arguments. We have the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

For each matroid in the following classes:

•

cycle matroids of series–parallel networks;

•

uniform matroids together with their single circuit-hyperplane relaxations;

•

matroids with at most six elements,

the following hold:

(1)

The spanning-set, cospanning-set, and basis generating functions
are Gårding;

(2)

Consequently, these generating functions, as well as their
independent-set generating functions,
satisfy the Rayleigh property;

(3)

Their univariate specializations are ultra log-concave, and in particular
satisfy the Mason-type log-concavity inequalities.

As a notable borderline case, the Fano matroid
F_{7}
exhibits a subtle
asymmetry: its cospanning-set generating function is Gårding, whereas its
spanning-set generating function is not, although both are Rayleigh
(see Theorem
14.10
). This reflects a subtle distinction between the
Rayleigh and Gårding properties. In particular, its independent-set generating
function is Rayleigh, a fact that is not immediate to verify by direct computation.

These examples show that the negative dependence extends beyond the reach of stability and Lorentzian theories. In particular, we establish Rayleigh-type inequalities and ultra log-concavity, providing a unified framework to study Rayleigh matroids and unrooted spanning-forest generating functions in graph theory, while recovering known cases and identifying new ones. We anticipate that the framework developed here will support more general structural results and further applications in related areas.

The proof of the main theorem combines geometric and algebraic structures
and is analytic in nature. Positivity regions satisfying the positive
ray test give rise to additional structure, including natural filtration
and further non-degeneracy conditions. We study Gårding polynomials
under operations such as partial derivatives and polarization,
together with two binary relations that encode their interaction with
positivity regions. In particular, we prove a linear preserver theorem
in the Gårding setting, extending the seminal work of Borcea and
Brändén
[
11
,
12
,
10
]
. A key challenge arises
in extending the theory beyond the multi-affine setting via polarization,
which leads to a nonlinear and generally non-convex optimization problem.
Classical complex-analytic tools, such as the Grace-Walsh-Szegö
theorem, which underlies polarization arguments in the stable setting,
do not have an analog in this context. One of our main contributions
is the identification of intrinsic geometric and algebraic structures through
which this analytic difficulty is reduced to linear optimization on
convex domains. For applications in matroid theory, the proofs of several special cases reveal delicate analytic features of generating functions, which in turn encode structural properties of the underlying matroids; the more technical arguments are carried out in detail in Appendices A–C.

The paper is organized as follows.

Section 2 introduces the geometry of positivity regions and positive
affine maps, including the definition of the positive ray test, the
NRT condition, and the associated geometric filtration. Section 3
introduces basic algebraic operations on polynomials and revisits
real stable polynomials from our new geometric perspective. In Section
4 we give the formal definition of Gårding polynomials and establish
their basic properties. The multi-affine theory is developed in Sections
5 and 6, including the study of binary relations. Section 7 proves
a linear preserver theorem. Sections 8 and 9 address the extension
to general Gårding polynomials via polarization and provide another
intrinsic and recursive characterization of the theory. Section 10
states the complete closure properties of the Gårding polynomials. Section 11 focus on homogeneous
Gårding
polynomials by examining their relation to stable and Lorentzian polynomials.
The final sections are devoted to applications,
including the interaction
with matroid generating functions, ultra log-concavity and negative dependence, and applications to characteristic
polynomials of certain classes of matrices.

Part I
Polynomials and positivity regions

2.
Geometry of positive regions

In this section, we develop the geometric framework that we use throughout
our paper. The central objects are positivity regions in Euclidean
spaces, together with their behavior under proper affine transforms.
This setting isolates crucial geometric notions of monotonicity and
invariance. Several concepts admit both a general form and a more
restrictive non-degenerate form, which are used according to different
requirements of algebraic and analytic arguments.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
[n]=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
. Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote
Euclidean space with standard basis
\mathbf{e}_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)
,

\mathbf{e}_{2}=(0,1,0,\cdots,0)
, etc. Points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will
be written as
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
with the same notation
as for
\mathbf{y},\textbf{a},\cdots
. We write
\mathbf{x}>0
(respectively,
\mathbf{x}\geq 0
)
if each
x_{i}
is positive (respectively, non-negative) for all

i\in[n]
.

2.1.
Weakly and strictly positive linear and affine maps

We introduce several basic geometric objects and transformations.
With respect to a fixed coordinate system in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
positive
hyper-octant
is defined by

\Gamma_{n}^{+}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \mathbf{x}>0\},

with closure

\overline{\Gamma_{n}^{+}}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \mathbf{x}\geq 0\}.

For any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
the set
\mathbf{x}_{0}+\Gamma_{n}^{+}
is called
an
affine positive hyper-octant
; the set
\mathbf{x}_{0}+t\mathbf{v}
for

t>0,

\mathbf{v}>0
is called a positive ray.

Remark 2.1
.

It is an elementary fact that a positive ray and
an affine positive hyper-octant have nontrivial intersection. Two
affine positive hyper-octants have a nontrivial intersection.

We introduce a geometric condition encoding positivity.

Definition 2.2

(
Positive Ray Test, PRT
)
.

A subset
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to pass the positive ray
test (PRT) if
R+\overline{\Gamma_{n}^{+}}\subset R
. Equivalently,

R
is closed under translation by positive vectors.

We also introduce a geometric non-degeneracy condition.

Definition 2.3

(
Negative Ray Terminating, NRT
)
.

A subset
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be negative ray terminating
(NRT), if for every
\mathbf{x}\in R
,
(\{\mathbf{x}\}-\overline{\Gamma_{n}^{+}})\cap R

is bounded.

Remark 2.4
.

Definitions
2.2

and
2.3
originate in nonlinear PDEs theory, where
they serve as structural conditions ensuring ellipticity.

We record some basic properties of the subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
passing PRT
or being NRT:

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(1)

If
R
passes PRT, then
R
is connected;

(2)

if
R_{1},
R_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
both pass PRT, then

R_{1}\cap R_{2}\neq\emptyset
;

(3)

if
R_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
and
R_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}

both pass PRT, then
R_{1}\times R_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
passes
PRT;

(4)

Fix an index set
I
. If
R_{i}
passes PRT for all
i\in I
, then

\cup_{i\in I}R_{i}
passes PRT. If
\cap_{I}R_{i}\not=\emptyset

then it also passes PRT.

(5)

If
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is NRT, so is any of its
open subsets.

Proof.

All statements follow directly from Remark
2.1
and Definition

2.2
.
∎

To describe proper geometric transforms, we introduce notions of positivity
for linear maps.

Definition 2.6
.

For a real
m\times n
matrix
A=(a_{ij})
, let
T_{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

be a linear map

T_{A}\mathbf{x}:=(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{1j}x_{j},\cdots\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{mj}x_{j}).

The map
T_{A}
is called
weakly positive
if

T_{A}(\overline{\Gamma_{n}^{+}})\subset\overline{\Gamma_{m}^{+}}.

The map
T_{A}
is called
strictly positive
if

T_{A}(\overline{\Gamma_{n}^{+}})\cap\Gamma_{m}^{+}\neq\emptyset.

Lemma 2.7
.

With the above notation, the operator
T_{A}
is weakly positive if and only if
a_{ij}\geq 0
for all
i\in[m]
and
j\in[n]
. Furthermore,
T_{A}
is strictly positive if and only if
a_{ij}\geq 0
for all
i\in[m]
and
j\in[n]
, and in addition
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}>0
for every
i\in[m]
.

The proof is straightforward and is therefore omitted.

Remark 2.8
.

For a weakly positive linear map
T_{A},

if
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{ij}=0,
for some
j\in[n]
, then the image
of
T_{A}
is independent of the coordinate
x_{j}.
We call
T_{A}

irreducible if
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{ij}>0
, for every
j\in[n]
.

We list some common geometric transformations used in this paper.

Definition 2.9

(Geometric Transformations)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
We
define the following basic geometric transformations between framed
Euclidean spaces. Assume
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(1)

Translation. For
\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
define
\mathrm{T}_{\textbf{a}}(\mathbf{x}):=\mathbf{x}+\textbf{a}
.

(2)

Permutation. For a permutation
\sigma
of
[n],
define
\mathbf{x}\to(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})
.

(3)

Dilation. For
\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
with
\textbf{a}>0,
define
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{\textbf{a}}}(\mathbf{x}):=(a_{1}x_{1},\cdots,a_{n}x_{n})
.

(4)

Deletion. For
i\in[n]
, define
\pi_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}):=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{n}).

(5)

Restriction. For
i\in[n],
define
(\mathbf{x})|_{x_{i}=0}:=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},0,x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{n}).

(6)

Inversion: In the domain
\{x_{i}\neq 0\},
define

\iota(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=(-\frac{1}{x_{1}},\cdots,-\frac{1}{x_{n}}).

Permutations, dilations, deletions, and restriction are special cases
of weakly positive linear maps, whereas the first three are strictly
positive. Note that inversion is
not
a linear map.

Definition 2.10

(Positive affine maps)
.

We define
\mathcal{A}_{+}
, the set of
weakly positive affine maps,

which are compositions of translations and weakly positive linear
maps. We define
\mathcal{A}_{++}
, the set of
strictly positive affine
maps
, which are compositions of translations and strictly positive
linear maps.

Remark 2.11
.

By Definions
2.6
and
2.10
, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{++}
,
\mu(\Gamma^{+}_{n})\cap\Gamma^{+}_{n}\neq\emptyset
. A crucial geometric consequence is that the composition of two strictly positive affine transformations, whenever well defined, is again a strictly positive affine transformation.

Remark 2.12
.

Closures of affine positive hyper-octants are
invariant under weakly positive affine mappings. Consequently, the
PRT
property is preserved under weakly positive affine transformations.
More
precisely, let
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be nonempty and satisfy PRT, and
let
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{+}

. Then the pre-image

\mu^{-1}(R):=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid\mu(\mathbf{x})\in R\}

also passes PRT whenever it is nonempty. Furthermore, if
R\neq\emptyset

and
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{++}
, then
\mu^{-1}(R)
is necessarily nonempty and
satisfies
PRT. Indeed, by strict positivity,
\mathrm{Im}(\mu)

contains
a positive ray and by Remark
2.4
, its
intersection
with
R
is nonempty.

Remark 2.13
.

In this paper, the positivity of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is characterized
via the PRT property. The positivity of linear and affine maps is
defined accordingly, following ideas from matrix theory. The distinction
between weakly and strictly positive affine transforms will be crucial.

3.
Algebraic and positive affine actions on polynomials

In this section, we record some transformations on the space of polynomials. We first introduce some notations.

For
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
, let
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]
denote the polynomial
ring in
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}
. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

be a multi-index with

|\alpha|:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}.

Let
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
and define

\partial^{\alpha}f:=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}f,\ \mathbf{x}^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
k
-th symmetric polynomial of
\mathbf{x}
is denoted by

\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{i_{j}}.

We use the convention
that
\sigma_{0}(\mathbf{x})=1
and
\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{x})=0
if
k>n
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
define

\mathbb{R}_{d}[\mathbf{x}]:=\{f\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}],\ \deg f\leq d\},\quad\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]:=\{f\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}],\ \deg_{x_{i}}f\leq\kappa_{i}\}.

The superscript
\mathfrak{a}
denotes the multi-affine property, the
superscript
\mathfrak{h}
denotes homogeneity, and the subscript
+

denotes nonnegative coefficients. For example:

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]:=\{f\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}],\ \ \deg_{x_{i}}f\leq 1\ \mathrm{and\ }f\ \text{has non-negative coefficients}\}.

For a finite dimensional subspace
V
of
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]
, we equip the compact-open topology, which is equivalent to the topology
of uniform convergence in compact sets.

Finally, for a univariate
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]
,
r(f)
denotes the largest
real root of
f
when it exists.

The geometric transformations introduced earlier naturally induce
corresponding operations on polynomials. Together with intrinsic polynomial
operations, such as partial differentiation, these induced maps will
be used to formulate invariance properties and to construct new examples.

We list the polynomial transformations considered in this paper, many
of which have appeared in the theory of stable polynomials.

Definition 3.1

(Polynomial Transformations)
.

Let
f,g\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}].

(1)

Pullback by affine maps. If
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is an affine transformation,
\mu^{*}:\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{y}]\to\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]
is given as
(\mu^{*}f)(\mathbf{x}):=f(\mu(\mathbf{x})).

In particular, both
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and
\mathcal{A}_{++}
act on polynomials by
pullback.

(2)

Partial differentiation. For
i\in[n]
, define
\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}f(\mathbf{x}).

More generally, for
\mathrm{\textbf{a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\textbf{a}\geq 0,
set the
directional derivative

\partial_{\textbf{a}}f(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\partial_{i}f.

(3)

Homogenization. For
f=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]

with
\deg f=d
,

\mathrm{H}(f)(\mathbf{x},y):=f(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{y})y^{d}=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}y^{d-|\alpha|}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{h}}[\mathbf{x},y].

(4)

Restriction (specialization). For
i\in[n]
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, define

f(x)|_{x_{i}=a}:=f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},a,x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{n}).

(5)

Top degree part. Let
f(x)
have degree
d
with
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}

with
f_{k}
homogeneous of degree
k.
Define
f^{\mathrm{top}}=f_{d}
.

(6)

Projection (diagonal specialization). The diagonal restriction of

f
is the univariate polynomial
\Pi^{\downarrow}(f):=f(t,\cdots,t)
.

(7)

Polarization. If
d\leq n
,
\Pi^{\uparrow}
:
\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]

is the unique linear map such that
\Pi^{\uparrow}(x^{k})=\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{x})/\binom{n}{k}.

(8)

\kappa
-Projection. For

(9)

\kappa
-Polarization. For

\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i1},\cdots,x_{i\kappa_{i}})/{\binom{\kappa_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}}

(10)

Multiplication.
(f\cdot g)(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x})\cdot g(\mathbf{x})

(11)

In this paper, a subset

4.
From stable polynomials to Gårding polynomials

4.1.
Stable and hyperbolic polynomials revisited

We briefly recall the basic facts about stable and hyperbolic polynomials
that motivate our construction. While most of the material is standard
and is included here to fix the notation, we also introduce a new geometric
viewpoint that facilitates later discussions.

Definition 4.1

(Real stable polynomials)
.

A nontrivial polynomial
f(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]
is called
real
stable
if
f(\mathbf{x})\neq 0
, for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the
imaginary part
\Im x_{i}>0
for all
i
. The zero polynomial is considered a trivial real stable polynomial. We denote
\mathrm{S}[\mathbf{x}]
to
be the set of real stable polynomials whose homogeneous top degree
part has nonnegative coefficients. We denote
\mathrm{S}_{+}[\mathbf{x}]
the set of stable polynomials with
nonnegative coefficients.

Two polynomials
f(\mathbf{x}),g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{S}[\mathbf{x}]

are said to be
in proper position
, written
f\prec g
, if

f(\mathbf{x})y+g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{S}[\mathbf{x},y].

Since the top degree part of a real stable polynomial is also real
stable
[
23
, Proposition 2.2]
, and all non-zero coefficients
of a homogeneous real stable polynomial have the same sign
[
23
, Theorem 6.1]
,
every real stable polynomial lies in either
\mathrm{S}[\mathbf{x}]
or
-\mathrm{S}[\mathbf{x}]
.
By convention, the trivial polynomial
0
is included in
\mathrm{S}[\mathbf{x}].

The set
\mathrm{S}[\mathbf{x}]
thus defines a distinguished positive cone in the
space of stable polynomials, which is natural in our geometric setting.

We also recall the following definition of Gårding.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
h(\mathbf{x})
be an
n
-variable polynomial
of
homogeneous
degree
d.
Let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
h(\mathbf{x})

is
hyperbolic with respect to
\mathbf{a}
if, for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the univariate polynomial
h(\mathbf{a}t+\mathbf{x})
has only real roots.
The
Gårding
cone of

with respect to
\mathbf{a}
is defined
by

\mathcal{C}_{h,\mathbf{a}}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ (h(\mathbf{a}))^{-1}h(\mathbf{a}t+\mathbf{x})>0,\ \forall t\geq 0\}.

We summarize some well-known facts; see, for example,
[

]
,

[

[

[

Lemma 4.3
.

(1)

f(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{S}[\mathbf{x}]
if and only if for all
\mathrm{\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}}^{n},

with
\mathrm{\textbf{a}>0},

(2)

Except for homogenization,

(3)

(4)

.

(5)

For
f(x)
a univariate polynomial of degree

(6)

If

that contains

(7)

If

(8)

For future reference, we list the following theorem, which is part
of the linear preserver theory proved by Borcea-Brändén

.

Theorem 4.4
.

For any
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

to be a linear transformation. If

We discuss the geometry of stable polynomials in our setting. First,

Proof.

Next, note that the classical construction of the Gårding cone for

For the zero polynomial, we define

4.2.
Gårding polynomial

In this subsection, we formalize the geometric positivity structure underlying stable polynomials and extend it to a broader class of polynomials.

Definition 4.8

(Gårding components)
.

For any nontrivial
f\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]
, the
Gårding component

\mathcal{C}_{f}
is defined to be the unique connected component of the set
\{f>0\}
that satisfies PRT, if such a component exists. If no such component exists, we set
\mathcal{C}_{f}=\emptyset
. For the zero polynomial, we define
\mathcal{C}_{0}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Note that the uniqueness of
\mathcal{C}_{f}
, when it exists, follows from
Lemma
2.5
.

This definition recovers all previously discussed cases. For any nontrivial

f\in\mathrm{S}
, the proof of Lemma
4.6
provides
an explicit construction of its Gårding component.

We define
Gårding
polynomials by the following geometric characterizations:

Definition 4.9

(
Gårding
polynomials)
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define

\mathrm{G}_{n}^{0}:=\{f\equiv c:c\geq 0\}.

Define the class of
multi-affine Gårding polynomials
as

{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}:=\{f(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}],\ \mathcal{C}_{f}\not=\emptyset\}.

The class of
Gårding polynomials
is defined as

\mathrm{G}_{n}:=\{\mu^{*}(f):\ f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}},\ \mu\in\mathcal{A}_{++}\}.

We denote
\mathrm{G}
,
\mathrm{G}^{d}
the set of all
Gårding
polynomials,
the set of all degree
\leq d

Gårding
polynomials respectively.

By convention, for
f\in\mathrm{G}
,
\mathcal{C}_{f}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if
f\equiv c\geq 0.

Also,
\mathrm{G}^{1}
consists of multi-linear functions with nonnegative
coefficients. In particular,
\mathrm{G}_{n}^{1}=\mathrm{S}_{n}^{1}.

Remark 4.10
.

Observe that for any
g=\mu^{*}(f)\in\mathrm{G}
, with
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{++}
,
the associated component satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{g}=\mu^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{f})\neq\emptyset
, and it passes PRT by Remark

2.12
The existence of such a component constitutes the fundamental geometric condition underlying the Gårding property. In the multi-affine setting, this condition is also sufficient. However, by itself, it is too weak to guarantee that general Gårding polynomials are closed under important algebraic operations, such as polarization.

Remark 4.11
.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{++}
, if
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}

and
\mu^{*}(f)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}],
then
\mu^{*}(f)\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
by
Remark
2.12
. In particular, if
\mu
is a translation,
permutation, or dilation in Definition
2.9
,
then
\mu^{*}
preserves
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Remark 4.12
.

It is straightforward to see that, by definition,
\mathrm{G}
is preserved under pullback by maps in
\mathcal{A}_{++}
, while stable polynomials are preserved under pullback by maps in
\mathcal{A}_{+}
. However,
\mathrm{S}
is not preserved under
\mathcal{A}_{+}
; see Lemma
4.5
. This distinction is fundamental and will be illustrated in the next example and exploited in later sections.

Example 4.13
.

To highlight a key difference between stable and Gårding polynomials, consider the restriction operation and the polynomial
f(x,y)=(x-1)y
. The polynomial
f
is both stable and Gårding; however, its restriction
f(0,y)=-y
is stable but not Gårding. Note that the restriction map
(x,y)\to(0,y)
is weakly positive linear, but not strictly positive linear in our sense.

We present more examples of
Gårding
polynomials.

Example 4.14
.

Let
f(x,y)=axy+bx+cy+d
and
\deg f\geq 1
.

f\not\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
if
a<0.
If
a>0
, a direct computation shows that

f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
if and only if

ad-bc\leq 0.

When
a=0
, then
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
if
b,c\geq 0
and one of them is non-zero.
Note that
f
is also stable.

Example 4.15
.

We give a non-stable example of a Gårding polynomial. Let
f(x,y,z)=xyz-1
. Note that

\mathcal{C}_{f}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:xyz>1,\ x>0,\ y>0,\ z>0\}

satisfies PRT. Therefore
f\in\mathcal{G}
. However,
g(t)=f(t,t,t)
has two nonreal complex conjugate roots. Hence
g
is not stable, and by Lemma
4.3
,
1
,
f
is not stable.

In fact, our new class strictly extends that of real stable polynomials.

Theorem 4.16
.

One has
\mathrm{S}\subsetneq\mathrm{G}
,
\mathrm{G}^{2}=\mathrm{S}^{2}.

Proof.

The inclusion
\mathrm{S}\subset\mathrm{G}
follows from Lemmas
4.3
,

4.6
and Theorem
4.4
.
The strict inclusion is shown by Example
4.15
.

We now prove the degree 2 statement. Let
f\in\mathrm{G}^{2}
and
f\not\equiv 0.

Thus
\mathcal{C}_{f}\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By Lemma
4.3
,
it suffices to show that for any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}

and
\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma_{n}^{+}
,
g(t):=f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+t\textbf{a})

has only real roots. Suppose otherwise. Since
\deg g\leq 2
, it follows that
g

has no real roots. Since
g(0)>0,
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

g(t)>0
. Therefore, by Definition
4.9
for
all
t\in\mathbb{R},

\mathbf{x}_{0}+t\textbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}.
For any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for sufficiently large
T,

\mathbf{z}>\mathbf{x}_{0}-T\mathbf{a}
.
By PRT,
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
. Hence
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathcal{C}_{f}
,
a contradiction.

Therefore,
g
has only real roots, and

is stable. This completes
the proof.
∎

Further examples of Gårding polynomials arise naturally in probability
theory, matrix theory, graph and matroid theory, which will be discussed
in later sections.

In summary, we define a new class of polynomials via the existence
of the Gårding component and strictly positive affine invariance. This
framework strictly extends real stable polynomials while retaining
their essential geometric features.

Part II
Multi-affine Theory

5.
Multi-affine Gårding polynomials

In this section, we study multi-affine Gårding polynomials, which
form the geometric core of the general theory.

Recall that a polynomial is multi-affine if it has degree at most
one in each variable. We denote by
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathrm{G}
the class of multi-affine
Gårding polynomials. Unless stated otherwise, all functions in this
part are assumed to be multi-affine.

We show that partial differentiation preserves the multi-affine Gårding property and analyze the induced inclusion relations between the Gårding components of a polynomial and those of its derivatives. We then introduce stronger geometric conditions that characterize the interior of
\mathcal{G}
and establish the Rayleigh property for
\mathcal{G}_{+}
.

5.1.
Partial differentiation and a domain filtration

We first establish that the class of multi-affine Gårding polynomials
is closed under partial differentiation and analyze the induced geometric
filtration on positivity regions. A key fact we exploit is that all
multi-affine polynomials are harmonic.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
f(\mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
is nonzero. Then for any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
and any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we have

\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\geq 0.

Furthermore, for each fixed
\alpha
,
\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x})>0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
or
\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x})\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Since
f
is multi-affine,
\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})=0
if

\alpha_{i}\geq 2
. Therefore, with a fixed
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}

we consider the Taylor expansion

(5.1)

f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\mathbf{x}^{\alpha},

where the sum runs over all subsets of
[n]
.

Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
such that
\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})<0
. Define
\mathbf{y}=\sum_{\alpha_{i}^{\prime}=1}\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then,
\mathbf{y}\geq 0
, and by (
5.1
), for
t>0
,

(5.2)

f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+t\mathbf{y})=\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})t^{|\alpha^{\prime}|}+O(|t|^{|\alpha^{\prime}|-1}).

For sufficiently large
t>0
, the leading term in (
5.2
)
dominates and the right hand side is then negative, contradicting
the PRT property of
\mathcal{C}_{f}
. Therefore,
\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\geq 0
.

Note that
\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})
is again multi-affine.
Therefore,
\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})
is a nonnegative harmonic
function in the open set
\mathcal{C}_{f}
. By the maximum principle,

\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x})>0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
, or
\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x})\equiv 0

as a polynomial.
∎

Next, we examine the behavior of multi-affine Gårding polynomials under
differentiation. For any
i\in[n],
recall the deletion map
\pi_{i}

in Definition
2.9
. For any
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathbf{x}),
since

f
is multi-affine,
\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x})
is independent of
x_{i}
;
Hence, it can be viewed as a function of
\pi_{i}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, which,
for convenience we will also write as
\partial_{i}f
. Therefore,

(5.3)

\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x})=\partial_{i}f(\pi_{i}(\mathbf{x})).

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
is nonzero. Then for
any
i\in[n]
,
\partial_{i}f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}.
In addition:

(1)

Either
\partial_{i}f
is identically zero, or
\partial_{i}f\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\pi_{i}(\mathbf{x})]

and
\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}f}\neq\emptyset;

(2)

If
\partial_{i}f
is nontrivial, then
\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}f}=\pi_{i}(\mathcal{C}_{f})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
;

(3)

If
\partial_{i}f
is nontrivial, then
\mathcal{C}_{f}=\pi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}f})\cap\{f>0\}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
i=1
. Denote
f_{1}:=\partial_{1}f
.
If
f_{1}
is a positive constant, then
\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{1}f}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

and
f(\mathbf{x})=cx_{1}+g(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),
where
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=(x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}).
Define

\mathcal{C}:=\{(t,\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),\ \mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\ t>-g(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\},
which is a connected
component of
\{f>0\}.
For any
(t^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{f},

(t,\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{f}\cap\mathcal{C}

if
t>>t^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{f}=\mathcal{C}.
The statement is valid.

Assume now that
\partial_{1}f
is not constant. Set
R=\pi_{1}\mathcal{C}_{f}
,
which is open, connected and also passes PRT in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Also
consider the non-constant multi-affine function
f_{1}|_{R}
, which
is harmonic. As a consequence,
f_{1}
cannot obtain its local minimum
in
R.
For any
\mathbf{y}=\pi_{1}(\mathbf{x})\in R
, by Lemma
5.1

and (
5.3
),
f_{1}(\mathbf{y})\geq 0.
We conclude that
f_{1}|_{R}>0
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the connected component of
\{f_{1}>0\}
such
that
R\subset\mathcal{C}
. We claim
\mathcal{C}=R
. We argue by contradiction,
using translation along a positive direction. If not, there exists

\hat{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathcal{C}\backslash R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Then,
there exists
\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\Gamma_{n-1}^{+}
such that,
\hat{\mathbf{p}}+\hat{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathcal{C}\cap R
.
Let
t>0
and define

\mathbf{p}(t,s):=(t,\hat{\mathbf{p}}+s\hat{\mathbf{z}}).

Let
c=\inf_{s\in[0,1]}\partial_{1}f(\mathbf{p}(0,s))
. Then
c>0
and

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{p}(t,s))

\displaystyle=t\partial_{1}f(\mathbf{p}(0,s))+f(\mathbf{p}(0,s))\geq ct+O(1).

For sufficiently large
T
,
f(\mathbf{p}(t,s))>0
for all
t\geq T

and
s\in[0,1]
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is a connected component,
\mathbf{p}(T,0)\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
.
Hence
\mathbf{\hat{p}}\in R
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,

\mathcal{C}=R
. We have proved
\partial_{1}f\in\mathrm{G}
, as well as
(1) and (2).

For (3), let
Q=\{f>0\}\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{f_{1}})
. Notice
\mathcal{C}_{f}\subset Q

by (2). On the other hand, if
\mathbf{p}\in Q\backslash\mathcal{C}_{f}
,
then
f_{1}(\mathbf{p})>0
and
f(\mathbf{p})>0
. By the same argument
as in the proof of (2), for some
\mathbf{z}\in\Gamma_{n}^{+}
and

t>>0
,
\mathbf{p}+t\mathbf{e}_{1}+s\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{C}_{f},
for all

s\in[0,1]
. Then,
\mathbf{p}+t\mathbf{e}_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
for all

t\geq 0
which violates the assumption that
\mathbf{p}\not\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
.
Hence,
Q=\mathcal{C}_{f}
. This completes the proof of (
3
).
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume that
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
is nontrivial. Then for any multi-index

\alpha,

\partial^{\alpha}f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}f}=\pi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{C}_{f})

if
\partial^{\alpha}f
is nontrivial.

We next introduce a natural filtration of positivity domains associated
with a multi-affine Gårding polynomial, which is motivated by the
sub-solution concept in PDE theory and will be used later.

Define for
k=0,\cdots,n
,

\Gamma_{n,k}^{+}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Gamma_{n}^{+}}:\mathbf{x}\text{ has at least }k\text{ positive entries}\}.

It follows that
\Gamma_{n,k}^{+}\subset\Gamma_{n,k-1}^{+}
.

Definition 5.4
.

If
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
passes PRT,
the
k
-th derived component
of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

(5.4)

\mathcal{C}^{(k)}:=\{\mathbf{x}:\forall\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma_{n,k}^{+},\ \exists\ t>0\ {\rm{s.t.}\ }\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{C}\}.

The terminology is justified by the following result:

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}\backslash\{\text{const}\}
. Let

I\subset[n]
be the set where
\partial_{i}f\not\equiv 0.
Then

(5.5)

\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}=\bigcap_{i\in I}\pi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}f}).

Proof.

By (
5.4
), we have
\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}R_{i},

where

R_{i}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists t>0,\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}\}.

By Lemma
5.2
,
R_{i}=\pi_{i}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}f}\right)

if
i\in I
and
R_{i}=\mathcal{C}_{f}
if
i\not\in I
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{f}\subset R_{i}

for any
i\in I
,
5.5
holds. This completes the
proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

By Proposition
5.5
, every degree
d
polynomial
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}

determines a canonical increasing filtration of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by PRT domains:

(5.6)

\mathcal{C}_{f}=\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(0)}\subset\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(d)}=\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Note also that
\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(k)}
may be defined for any multi-affine
f
provided that
\partial^{\alpha}f\in\mathcal{G}
for all
|\alpha|=k
.

Although the Gårding property is defined geometrically, in the
multi-affine setting, it admits an analytic verification. The resulting
recursive procedure takes the form of a non-linear optimization problem.

Proposition 5.7
.

A multi-affine function
f(\mathbf{x})
of degree

>1
is Gårding if and only if the following holds:

(1)

For any
i\in[n]
,
\partial_{i}f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}.

(2)

f\leq 0
on the boundary of

\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}=\cap_{i\in[n]}\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}f}.

Proof.

One direction follows from Proposition
5.5
. We prove
the converse.

Note that
\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}
is well defined with non-empty boundary.
For any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}\subset\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(k)},
for
k\geq 1
,
the Taylor expansion of
f
at
\mathbf{x}_{0}
has non-negative coefficients
except possibly the constant term. Thus, there exists
\mathbf{y}>\mathbf{x}
such
that
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}
, and
f(\mathbf{y})>0.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}^{(1)}\cap\{f>0\}
. By the same Taylor expansion argument,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
satisfies the PRT condition and is therefore connected by Lemma
2.5
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the connected component of
\{f>0\}
containing
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Assumption (2) implies that
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, and in particular
\mathcal{C}
satisfies PRT. This shows that
f\in\mathcal{G}
.
∎

5.2.
Multi-affine NRT polynomials

It may happen that
f(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]
is independent of certain variables.
Such a polynomial gives a degenerate elliptic operator in the theory
of PDE. To isolate a non-degenerate subclass of multi-affine Gårding
polynomials, we introduce the following

Definition 5.8
.

A multi-affine polynomial
f
is called
a negative-ray-terminating (NRT) polynomial, if it admits a connected
component of the set
\{f>0\}
that is negative ray terminating.

In the multi-affine case, the NRT condition is strictly stronger than
the PRT requirement and has significant consequences for the first derivatives.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
f
be a multi-affine polynomial and let

\mathcal{C}
be a connected component of
\{f>0\}
. The following
are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is NRT;

(2)

f
is
Gårding
with
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{f}
, and
f
does not have redundant variables;

(3)

f
is Gårding,
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{f}
, and

\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x})>0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

We prove
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(1)
.

Assume (1). Fix
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}
and
i\in[n]
. Since

f
is multi-affine,

f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+t\mathbf{e}_{i})=\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})t+f(\mathbf{x}_{0}).

By the NRT property, there exists
t_{0}<0
such that
\mathbf{x}_{0}+t_{0}\mathbf{e}_{i}\not\in\mathcal{C}
,
hence

0=f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+t_{0}\mathbf{e}_{i})=\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})t_{0}+f(\mathbf{x}_{0}).

Since
f(\mathbf{x}_{0})>0
and
t_{0}<0,
we conclude that
\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})>0
.
Thus, all first derivatives are positive on
\mathcal{C},

\mathcal{C}
satisfies
PRT. Therefore,
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
and has no redundant variables.

Next, assume (2). We argue by contradiction. Since
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
,
\partial^{\alpha}f|_{\mathcal{C}_{f}}\geq 0

for all multi-index
\alpha
by Lemma
5.1
. If for
some
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
,
\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{y})=0
, then
\partial_{i}\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{y})=0

for all
i\in[n]
, since otherwise a neighborhood of
\mathbf{x}
has a non-empty
intersection with
\{\partial^{\alpha}f<0\}
. By induction,
\partial^{\beta}f(\mathbf{y})=0

for all
\beta\geq\alpha
. Thus, if we can find
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}

and
i_{0}
such that
\partial_{i_{0}}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})=0
, the Taylor expansion
at
\mathbf{x}_{0}
has the form

f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\alpha_{i_{0}}=0}c_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}.

Then
f
is independent of
x_{i_{0}}
which is a contradiction.

Finally, assume (3). If
\mathcal{C}_{f}
were not NRT, there would exist

\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
and
\mathbf{v}\geq 0,

\mathbf{v}\neq 0
, such that
\mathbf{x}_{0}+t\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.
Restricting to the affine subspace
V:=\mathbf{x}_{0}+\text{span}\{\mathbf{e}_{i}:v_{i}>0\}
,
Lemma
5.15
we obtain a multi-affine
Gårding
polynomial
whose Gårding component is all of
V
. Therefore,
f|_{V}
is a
constant polynomial with all partial derivatives vanishing, contradicting

\partial_{i}f>0
on
\mathcal{C}_{f}.
Therefore
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is NRT.
∎

Example 5.10
.

A degree
n
polynomial
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}
if and only if a connected
component of
\{f>0\}
satisfies NRT. Suppose that the top homogenous
part of
f
is
a\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{x})
for
a>0
. Then,
f

is Gårding if there exists a component
\mathcal{C}
of
\{f>0\}

which is contained in
\{\mathbf{x}_{0}\}+\Gamma_{n}^{+}
for some

\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In fact,
\{\mathbf{x}_{0}\}+\Gamma_{n}^{+}

satisfies NRT. Thus, if
\mathcal{C}\subset\{\mathbf{x}_{0}\}+\Gamma_{n}^{+}
,
then by Lemma
6.8
,
f
is Gårding. Conversely,
if
f
is Gårding, by a translation
\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}_{0})
,
we may assume that
\hat{f}
has vanishing homogeneous degree

part.
Then, we see that
\mathcal{C}_{\hat{f}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\hat{f}}^{(n-1)}\subset\Gamma_{n}^{+}.

This implies that
\mathcal{C}_{f}\subset\{\mathbf{x}_{0}\}+\Gamma_{n}^{+}
.

Lemma
5.1
can also be interpreted as follows: any
Gårding
polynomial can be reduced to an NRT

polynomial after removing all redundant variables.

.

Let

\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}

and set

f-c\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
.
By Lemma

(5), we have the following.

Lemma 5.12
.

Suppose

for any

.

With notation as in

5.3.
Restriction to coordinate hyperplanes

As noted in Remark
4.13
, the restriction
operation does not preserve the Gårding property in general. Nevertheless, the restriction does preserve the Gårding property
in the following case.

Lemma 5.14
.

Consider a nontrivial polynomial
f(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}

and
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
.

(1)

If
\textbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
, then the restriction
f(a_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})

is a nontrivial Gårding polynomial;

(2)

For
\textbf{a}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{f}}
, then the restriction
f(a_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})

is Gårding; and, moreover, it is nontrivial or
f(\mathbf{x})=(x_{1}-a_{1})g(x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}),

where
g\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}].

Proof.

For part 1, let
\mathcal{C}=\pi_{1}(\{x_{1}=a_{1}\}\cap\mathcal{C}_{f}).
Since
f(\textbf{a})>0,

\textbf{a}\in\{x_{1}=a_{1}\}\cap\mathcal{C}_{f}
,

\mathcal{C}
is nonempty. It is straightforward to check that
\mathcal{C}
is a
connected component of the positivity region of the restricted function passing PRT. The proof
is complete.

For part 2, since
f(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}},

\mathcal{C}_{f}
satisfies PRT, so are
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{f}}
,
and
\pi_{1}(\{x_{1}=a_{1}\}\cap\overline{\mathcal{C}_{f}})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

Thus, for any
\mathbf{y}=(0,y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})\geq 0
,

(5.7)

\textbf{a}+\mathbf{y}\in\{x_{1}=a_{1}\}\cap\overline{\mathcal{C}_{f}}\neq\emptyset

If
\{x_{1}=a_{1}\}\cap\mathcal{C}_{f}\neq\emptyset
, we have
f|_{x_{1}=a_{1}}\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
.
Otherwise,

(5.8)

\{x_{1}=a_{1}\}\cap\mathcal{C}_{f}=\emptyset.

By (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), we see
\textbf{a}+\mathbf{y}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{f}

for all
\mathbf{y}=(0,y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})
, with
y_{i}\geq 0
, which indicates
that
f(\mathbf{x})
has a factor of
(x_{1}-a_{1}).
We have proved the
lemma.
∎

We also analyze the restriction to coordinate affine subspaces.

For any subset
I=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\}\subset[n]
with

k
elements, define the inclusion map and the projection
map

\displaystyle\iota_{I}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\ (x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})\mapsto\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i},

\displaystyle\pi_{I}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-k},\mathbf{x}\mapsto\pi_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\pi_{i_{k}}(\mathbf{x}).

We have the following theorem.

Lemma 5.15
.

Suppose
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
. If
\pi_{I}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\in\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{f})
,
then for
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

g(\mathbf{y}):=f(\iota_{I}(\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{x}_{0})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}.

In particular, if
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(k_{0})}
where
k_{0}\leq k
,
then
g(\mathbf{y})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
; if
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n,+}
, then

\iota_{I}^{*}f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{k,+}
.

Proof.

If
g\equiv c\geq 0
, then the result is trivial. Otherwise, denote

R:=\{\mathbf{y}:\iota_{I}(\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}\},

which is an open subset of
\{g(\mathbf{y})>0\}
. Since
\pi_{I}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\in\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{f})
,
for
\mathbf{a}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\Gamma_{n,|I|}^{+}
,
there exists
t>>0
such that
t\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
.
Then
R
is not empty and passes PRT . Thus, by Lemma
2.5
,

R
is connected. Suppose
\mathbf{y}_{0}\in\partial R
. Then
\iota_{I}(\mathbf{y}_{0})+\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{f}

and hence
g(\mathbf{y}_{0})=0
. Thus,
R
is both open and closed
in
\{g(\mathbf{y})>0\}
. Hence,
R
is a connected component of

\{g(\mathbf{y})>0\}
. Therefore,
g
is Gårding.
∎

5.4.
Multi-affine Gårding polynomials with nonnegative coefficients

As an application, we now focus on a distinguished subclass of multi-affine
Gårding polynomials, namely those with nonnegative coefficients. This
subclass, denoted by
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
, plays a central role in our linear
preserver theory and applications to probability theory and combinatorics.

We begin by recording the basic stability properties of
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
.

Lemma 5.16
.

For any
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}},
and
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
,
the translation
\text{T}_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}^{*}f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
.

Proof.

By Remark
5.1
and (
5.3
),
f(\mathbf{x}_{0})>0

and
\partial_{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\geq 0.
The claim follows from the
Taylor expansion of
f
at
\mathbf{x}_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 5.17
.

If
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+},
then for any
I\subset[n]
,

\iota_{I}^{*}f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f
is nontrivial,
since the claim holds otherwise. Since
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+},

f(0)\geq 0.

Then
0\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{f}}
, and the claim follows from Lemma
5.15

and Lemma
5.14

∎

By Remark
4.13
, the technical assumption
in Lemma
5.15
is necessary.

To formulate the analytic closure property of
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
, we equip the spaces

\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]
and
\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]
with the compact–open topology, i.e., the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets.
With this topology,
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}[\mathbf{x}]
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]
.

Lemma 5.18
.

{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}[\mathbf{x}]
is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there exists a sequence
\{f_{k}(\mathbf{x})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}[\mathbf{x}]

converging uniformly on compact subsets to a multi-affine polynomial

f
which is not
Gårding
.

By continuity,
f(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{x}\geq 0
. If
f
is a constant,
then
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}[\mathbf{x}]
. Hence, we assume that
f
is not constant.
Since
f
is multi-affine, it is harmonic, and by the maximum principle
we have
f>0
on
\Gamma_{n}^{+}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the connected
component of
\{f>0\}
containing
\Gamma_{n}^{+}
.

We claim that for any
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
if
k>N
, then
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}_{f_{k}}
. Indeed, since
\mathcal{C}
is connected,
there exists a continuous path
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal{C}
with
\gamma(0)=\mathbf{1}

and
\gamma(1)=\mathbf{w}
. Set
a:=\min_{t\in[0,1]}f(\gamma(t)).
Then

a>0
. Uniform convergence on compact sets implies the existence
of
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
k>N
,

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|f_{k}(\gamma(t))-f(\gamma(t))|<\frac{a}{2},

hence
\min_{t\in[0,1]}f_{k}(\gamma(t))>\frac{a}{2}
. Since

\mathbf{1}\in
\mathcal{C}_{f_{k}}
, it follows that
\gamma([0,1])\subset\mathcal{C}_{f_{k}}

and
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{C}_{f_{k}}
.

Since
f
is not
Gårding
, there exist
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\textbf{a}\geq 0

such that
f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\textbf{a})=0.
By the PRT property of
f_{k}
and the
previous claim, for sufficiently large
k
,
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f_{k}}
. Hence,

f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\textbf{a})=\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\textbf{a})\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\geq\frac{1}{2}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})>0,

contradicting
f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\textbf{a})=0
. Therefore,
f

is
Gårding
, and
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
is closed.
∎

Remark 5.19
.

{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]
itself is not closed. For example, let
g_{k}=\frac{1}{k}x-1

and let
k\to+\infty
. Then the limit
-1
is not a
Gårding
polynomial.
On the other hand, if
g_{k}(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]
and there exists a fixed

\mathbf{x}_{0}\in

then, by a translation
and Lemma
5.18
, the limit of

is a
Gårding

polynomial.

Next, we recall the definition of a Rayleigh polynomial.

Definition 5.20
.

A multi-affine polynomial
f
is

if it has nonnegative coefficients and for any
\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma_{+}^{n}
,

(5.9)

f(\mathbf{x})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{x})-\partial_{i}f(\mathbf{x})\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{x})\leq 0

for each
i<j
. If (
5.9
) satisfies, for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
then

is called

. By
[
13
]
, a multi-affine
polynomial is strong Rayleigh if and only if it is stable.

We show that
Gårding
polynomials are Rayleigh polynomials.

Lemma 5.21
.

(1)

For
i\not=j

\mathbf{x}

If
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}

f
is a Rayleigh polynomial.

(3)

If in a component
R
of
\{f>0\}
and for
any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in R

\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}^{*}f
is

f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
.

(4)

If
f
is a strong Rayleigh polynomial with

is Gårding.

Proof.

For (
1
), we prove for
i=1,\ j=2
. Other cases
are similar. For
\pi_{12}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\in\pi_{12}(\mathcal{C}_{f})
, let

\displaystyle g(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+xe_{1}+ye_{2})

\displaystyle=\partial_{12}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})xy+\partial_{1}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})x+\partial_{2}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})y+f(\mathbf{x}_{0}).

Then,

by Lemma
5.15
.
The conclusion follows from Example
4.14
.

For (
2
), since
\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
, by (

),

f
is Rayleigh.

Suppose that
R
is the connected component of
\{f>0\}
given in
(

5.5.

Let

Suppose that

Proof.

By Lemma

Since dilation preserves

6.
Decomposition and Binary Relations

In this section, we develop a decomposition theory for multi-affine
Gårding polynomials. This decomposition clarifies an additional geometric
and algebraic structure and naturally leads to the introduction of
two associated binary relations.

The first relation,
proper position
, is a direct analog
of the corresponding notion for stable polynomials. The second is
a
domination
relation, which appears to be specific to the
Gårding setting. For both relations, we establish geometric, algebraic,
and analytic characterizations and establish their basic properties.
Our approach differs conceptually from those typically used in the
theory of stable polynomials. Readers may find it instructive to compare
the present results and methods with their counterparts in the stable
setting, cf.
[
11
,
12
,
67
]
.

This section provides the technical backbone for the constructions
and arguments developed later in the paper and is therefore necessarily
technical.

6.1.
Definitions and a dichotomy theorem

We begin by introducing two binary relations that encode the two alternatives
in the restriction dichotomy.

Definition 6.1

(Domination)
.

Let
g(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}
. Let
f\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]

be a nontrivial multi-affine polynomial. We say that
g
dominates

f
, denoted by
f\vartriangleleft g
, if

f|_{\mathcal{C}_{g}}>0.

By convention,
0\vartriangleleft g
for any
g\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Remark 6.2
.

When
f,g\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}},

f\vartriangleleft g
if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{g}\subset\mathcal{C}_{f}.

Note that in Definition
6.1
,
f
is not required to be Gårding,
which is important for later applications.

Next, we introduce the analogue of proper position in the stable theory.

Definition 6.3

(Proper Position)
.

For nontrivial

,
we say that
(f,g)
is in a
proper position

f\prec g

if

h(\mathbf{x},y)=f(\textbf{x})y+g(\textbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x},y].

By convention,
0\prec g\prec 0
for any
g\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
.

The following theorem gives a precise formulation of the restriction
dichotomy in terms of the two relations introduced above.

Theorem 6.4
.

Suppose that
h(\mathbf{x},y)=f(\mathbf{x})y+g(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n+1}

where
f
and
g
are nontrivial. Then exactly one of the following

(1)

f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
and
g|_{\mathcal{C}_{f}}<0

-g\vartriangleleft f
.

(2)

Both

, and
\mathcal{C}_{g}=\mathcal{C}_{f}\cap\{g>0\}
.
In particular,
f\vartriangleleft g
and

Proof.

Note that
f(\mathbf{x})=\partial_{y}h
is necessarily Gårding. Since

is nontrivial and

is open, either

or

and

By Lemma
5.2
, we have
\mathcal{C}_{h}=\{h>0\}\cap\pi_{y}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{f}),

where

It follows that

is connected and passes the PRT.

If

for

and hence

(\mathbf{x}(t),0)\subset\mathcal{C}_{h}\cap\{z=0\}=G,

a contradiction. Therefore,

and
G=\mathcal{C}_{g}
.
∎

Theorem

highlights both the similarities
and the structural differences between Gårding and stable polynomials.

7.
Linear preservers of multi-affine
Gårding
polynomials

In this section, we study linear transformations that preserve the class of multi-affine
Gårding
polynomials. The results of will later be extended to general Gårding polynomials via polarization and specialization. Our approach follows the philosophy of Borcea–Brändén for stable polynomials
[
12
]
and Brändén-Huh for Lorentzian polynomials
[
15
]
.

Let
T:\mathbb{R}_{d}^{\mathfrak{a}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}]
be a linear transformation. We say that
T
preserves
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
if, for any
f\in\mathrm{G}_{n,+}^{\mathfrak{a}}
, one has
T(f)\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{m,+}
.

Here we restrict the discussion to polynomials with nonnegative coefficients. By Lemma
5.16
, for
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
, we have
T_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}^{*}f=f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
. By Lemma
5.17
, the restriction operation preserves
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
, while in general the restriction operation does not preserve
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Following similar constructions in the stable polynomial theory, we
give the following:

Definition 7.1

(Symbol)
.

For a linear transform
T:\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}]
,
its symbol is a polynomial with
m+n
variables defined by

\mathrm{sym}_{T}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})=\sum_{\alpha\subset[n]}T(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})\mathbf{u}^{[n]\backslash\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\subset[n]}T(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{u}^{[n]}.

Equivalently,
\text{sym}_{T}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})=T(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+u_{i})).

The main theorem of this section is the following:

Theorem 7.2
.

Suppose that
T:\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}]
is a linear transformation on multi-affine functions. If
\mathrm{sym}_{T}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n+m,+}
, then
T
preserves
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
.

Our proof is similar to those of related results for stable and Lorentzian polynomials. However, in order to apply our more general concepts of binary relations, it is more geometric.

To prove Theorem
7.2
, we need the following lemma, which is similar to
[
15
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose that
f(x_{1},x_{2},\hat{\mathbf{x}})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n,+}
. Then

(7.1)

h(x_{2},\hat{\mathbf{x}}):=(\partial_{1}f)(0,x_{2},\hat{\mathbf{x}})+(\partial_{2}f)(0,0,\hat{\mathbf{x}})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n,+}.

Proof.

We have the following decomposition of multi-affine functions:

f(\mathbf{x})=x_{1}\partial_{1}f(x_{2},\hat{\mathbf{x}})+g_{1}(x_{2},\hat{\mathbf{x}})=x_{1}\bigl(x_{2}\partial_{12}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}})+g_{2}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\bigr)+g_{1}(x_{2},\hat{\mathbf{x}}),

where

(7.2)

\partial_{1}f(x_{2},\hat{\mathbf{x}})=x_{2}\partial_{12}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}})+g_{2}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}).

Then

h(x_{2},\hat{\mathbf{x}})=x_{2}\partial_{12}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}})+g_{2}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})+\partial_{2}g_{1}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}).

Note that
\partial_{1}f\prec g_{1}
, which, by Lemma
6.15
, implies
\partial_{12}f\prec\partial_{2}g_{1}
. Also, by (
7.2
), we have
\partial_{12}f\prec g_{2}
. Thus, (

6.15

(vi)
.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
7.2
.

Proof of Theorem
7.2
.

Suppose
\mathrm{sym}_{T}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{n+m,+}
. We use a new set of variables to write

\mathrm{sym}_{T}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})=\sum_{\alpha\subset[n]}T(\mathbf{w}^{\alpha})\mathbf{u}^{[n]\backslash\alpha}.

By Lemma
2.5

3

We apply Lemma

Finally, we apply Lemma

∎

Part III
General
Gårding
Theory

8.
General
Gårding
polynomial

We now extend the multi-affine theory to general polynomials. The main
difficulty is that the defining geometric condition is not directly preserved
under standard algebraic operations such as polarization. The strategy is to
reduce the general case to the multi-affine setting via polarization and to
recover the geometric structure through a recursive analysis of partial
derivatives.

By Remark
2.11
, the class of Gårding polynomial is closed under strictly positive affine transformations.

One of the main results of this section is Theorem
8.2
, which establishes the second characterization in Theorem
1.1
.

8.1.
Symmetric realizations

Let
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Recall that

\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]=\{f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]:\deg_{x_{i}}f\leq\kappa_{i}\ \text{for all }i\},

and that
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
denotes the subspace of multi-affine polynomials. We write

\mathrm{G}_{n}^{\kappa}=\mathrm{G}_{n}\cap\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}],\qquad\mathrm{G}^{\mathfrak{a},\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]={\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}\cap\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}].

For
g=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]
, the
\kappa
-polarization of
g
is defined by

(8.1)

(\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}g)(x_{11},\cdots,x_{1\kappa_{1}},\cdots,x_{n1},\cdots,x_{n\kappa_{n}}):=\sum_{\alpha\leq\kappa}c_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sigma_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i1},\cdots,x_{i\kappa_{i}})}{\binom{\kappa_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}},

where
\sigma_{\alpha_{i}}
denotes the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree
\alpha_{i}
. The associated
\kappa
-projection (or
\kappa
-diagonal specialization) operator is given by

\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa}(f):=f\big|_{x_{ij}=x_{i}},

and satisfies
\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa}\circ\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}=\mathrm{Id}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]
.

Recall that a general Gårding polynomial
g
is defined as a pullback

g=\mu^{*}(f),

where
f
is a multi-affine Gårding polynomial and
\mu
is a strictly positive affine map. We refer to the pair
(f,\mu)
as a
realization
of
g
. In particular, if
f=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{\kappa}
and
\mu
is the diagonal map

\mu:(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\mapsto(\overbrace{x_{1},\cdots,x_{1}}^{\kappa_{1}},\cdots,\overbrace{x_{n},\cdots,x_{n}}^{\kappa_{n}}),

then
(f,\mu)
is called a
\kappa
-symmetric realization of
g
. Such realizations provide natural and convenient multi-affine models for general Gårding polynomials.

Remark 8.1
.

Let
\kappa_{g}=(\deg_{x_{1}}g,\ldots,\deg_{x_{n}}g)
denote the multi-degree of
g
. If
g=\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa}(f)
is realized as a
\kappa
-projection of a
\kappa
-symmetric multi-affine polynomial
f
, then necessarily
\kappa\geq\kappa_{g}
. In particular, any
\kappa
-symmetric realization of
g
requires at least
\deg_{x_{i}}g
copies of the variable
x_{i}
for each
i
.

The
\kappa
-projection map
\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa}
from the
\kappa
-symmetric multi-affine polynomials in
{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
onto
\mathrm{G}_{m}
is surjective. In particular, we have the following realization theorem.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let

\mathbf{y}=(y_{11},\cdots,y_{\kappa_{1}1},\cdots,y_{1m},\cdots,y_{\kappa_{m}m})\in\mathbb{R}^{|\kappa|},

we have

h(\mathbf{y}):=\mathrm{T}_{(-\mathbf{t}_{0})}^{*}\circ T_{\text{sym},\kappa_{m}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{\text{sym},\kappa_{1}}\circ\mathrm{T}_{\mathbf{t}_{0}}^{*}(\tilde{g})(\mathbf{y})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{|\kappa|}[\mathbf{y}].

Then,
\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa}(h)=g
and
\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)=h\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
. We have
finished the proof.
∎

8.2.
Closure under partial derivatives and domain filtration

The symmetric realization theorem (Theorem
8.2
) provides additional closure operations for general
Gårding
polynomials. In particular, we show that partial derivatives preserve general
Gårding
polynomials.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
g
be a Gårding polynomial, and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be a multi-index. Then
\partial^{\alpha}g
is Gårding. Moreover, the corresponding Gårding components satisfy

\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}g}\supseteq\mathcal{C}_{g}.

Proof.

By Theorem
8.2
, there exist multi-index
\kappa
and
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{\kappa}
such that
g=\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa}(f)
. Note

\partial_{i}g(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa}\!\bigl[\kappa_{i}(\partial_{i1}\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}g)\bigr]=\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa}\!\bigl[\kappa_{i}(\partial_{i1}f)\bigr]\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{\kappa}.

This proves the claim for the first derivatives. The inclusion of components follows directly from the projection. The general statement follows by induction on
|\alpha|
.
∎

As in the multi-affine case, we define a filtration of Gårding components.

Definition 8.4

(PRT domain filtration)
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{\mathrm{G}}

be a Gårding polynomial of total degree
d\geq 1
. For each integer

k\geq 0
, define the
k-
th derived Gårding component, or the
k-
th
derived component, as

\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(k)}\;:=\;\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\\
|\alpha|=k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}g},

where, by convention,
\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}g}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
whenever

\partial^{\alpha}g\equiv c\geq 0
.

By Theorem
8.3
the family
\{\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(k)}\}_{k\geq 0}

is well defined and forms a nested filtration

(8.3)

\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(0)}\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(1)}\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(2)}\;\subset\;\cdots\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(d-1)}\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(d)}\;=\;\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In the special case of
k=d-1
, the defining inequalities come from
nontrivial affine-linear polynomials
\partial^{\alpha}g=\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{x}-\textbf{b}_{\alpha}

with
|\alpha|=d-1,
and hence
\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(d-1)}
is a polyhedral set
cut out by finitely many hyperplanes. For this reason, it is also
called the
polyhedral derived
Gårding
component
or
polyhedral
derived component
.

8.3.
NRT
Gårding
polynomials and polyhedral components

Next we extend the NRT condition to the general setting.

Definition 8.5
.

A Gårding polynomial
g\in\mathrm{G}
is said
to be
NRT
if its Gårding component
\mathcal{C}_{g}
satisfies
the negative ray terminating condition (NRT).

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite index set.
Let

\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \textbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{x}>b_{\alpha},\ \alpha\in\Lambda\},

with
\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\in\overline{\Gamma_{n}^{+}}\setminus\{0\}
, and
let
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
be its closure. For
\mathbf{v}\in\Gamma_{n}^{+}
,
the infimum
\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{x}
is finite
if and only if there exist coefficients
\lambda_{\alpha}\geq 0
such
that
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\lambda_{\alpha}\textbf{a}_{\alpha}
. In
particular,
\mathcal{C}
is NRT if and only if for any
i\in[n],
there
exists
\alpha\in\Lambda
such that
\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\textbf{e}_{i}>0
.

Proof.

The first part is a standard consequence of linear programming duality
(equivalently, Farkas’ lemma
[
55
, pp. 200]
)
applied to the constraints
\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{x}\geq b_{\alpha}
. The
second part follows from the definition of NRT region.
∎

Proposition 8.7
.

Let
g\in\mathrm{G}
be a Gårding
polynomial of degree
d
. If
g
is NRT, then

(1)

the top homogeneous part
g^{\mathrm{top}}

is a Gårding polynomial and is NRT;

(2)

for a
\kappa
-symmetric realization

h=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)
, the polyhedral component
\mathcal{C}_{h}^{(d-1)}

is NRT.

Proof.

First, assume that
g
is multi-affine. Then, by Proposition
5.22
,

g^{\mathrm{top}}\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
g^{\mathrm{top}}

is not NRT. Then there exists
i\in[n]
such that
\partial_{i}g^{\mathrm{top}}\equiv 0
.
Writing
g(\mathbf{x})=x_{i}p(\mathbf{x})+q(\mathbf{x})
, we have
\deg q=d
and
\deg p\leq d-2
,
since the degree-
d
part of
g
is independent of
x_{i}
. This
contradicts
6.15

(iv)
, which
excludes such a decomposition. This finished the proof of (
1
)
for
g\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
.

To finish the proof for the general
Gårding
polynomial, we apply Theorem

8.2
to obtain a
\kappa
-symmetric
realization
h=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)
. Then
h^{\text{top}}
is NRT
which implies (
1
) after taking the
\kappa
-diagonal
specialization.

To prove (
2
), by the first part, for each

i
there exists
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=d-1
such that
\partial^{\alpha}g(\mathbf{x})=\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{x}-b_{\alpha}

and
\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}>0
. By Lemma
8.6
,
the polyhedral component
\mathcal{C}_{h}^{(d-1)}
is NRT.
∎

8.4.
Auxiliary classes and an approximation theorem

We introduce 2 auxiliary classes of polynomials. They will be used
to perturb general
Gårding
polynomials to have additional non-degeneracy
conditions.

Definition 8.8

(Auxiliary Classes)
.

Denote
\mathcal{A}
to be the class of polynomials

g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]
such that

\partial_{i}g\in\mathrm{G}\qquad\text{for all }i\in[n].

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the subclass
consisting of
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying the additional non-degeneracy
condition

\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}f}}\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f}\qquad\text{for all }i\neq j.

Note that for
g\in\mathcal{A}^{d}
of degree
d,
derived components
can still be defined for
k\geq 1
. Furthermore, we have

\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(1)}\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(2)}\;\subset\;\cdots\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(d-1)}\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(d)}\;=\;\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The special component

\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(d-1)}
is also polyhedra. Define the index set

\mathcal{I}_{g}:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}:|\alpha|=d-1,\ \partial^{\alpha}g\not\equiv 0\}.

For each
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{g}
, the derivative
\partial^{\alpha}g

is affine and can be written as

\partial^{\alpha}g(x)=\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{x}-b_{\alpha},\qquad\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\geq 0,\ \textbf{a}_{\alpha}\neq 0,b_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(d-1)}:=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\textbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{x}>b_{\alpha}\ \text{for all }\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{g}\}.

We call
\mathcal{C}_{g}^{(d-1)}
the
polyhedral derived component

of
g\in\mathcal{A}.

Theorem 8.9
.

Let
g\in\mathrm{G}
be an NRT
Gårding
polynomial
with
\deg g>1
and let
\kappa\geq\kappa_{g}
be a multi-index.
Set
h=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)
and assume
h\in\mathcal{A}
. Then there
exists a sequence of NRT Gårding functions
\{g_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}\subset\mathrm{G}

such that
g_{m}
converges to
g
in the compact-open topology,
and writing
h_{m}=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g_{m})
, one has
h_{m}\in\mathcal{B}

for every
m
.

Proof.

We construct a linear perturbation of
h
from the symmetric
realization of
g
.

Let
f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{\kappa_{0}}
be a symmetric realization of
g
. By
Proposition
8.7
, the derived polyhedral component

\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(d-1)}
is NRT. Write
\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(d-1)}=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\ A\mathbf{z}>\mathbf{b}\}
,
where the rows of
A
are the facet normals
\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}\in\overline{\Gamma_{N}^{+}}
.
Define
\ell(\mathbf{z}):=\sum_{\alpha}(\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{z}-b_{\alpha})

which is
\kappa_{0}
-symmetric. Lemma
8.6

implies that
\nabla\ell=\sum_{\alpha}\mathbf{a}_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{N}^{+}

and
\ell(\mathbf{z})>0
in
\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(d-1)}
.

Set
f_{c}=f-c\,\ell
. Since
\deg f=\deg g>1
, Theorem
6.8

implies that
f_{c}
is NRT multi-affine Gårding for all
c>0
.
Since
\nabla\ell>0
, each polarized partial satisfies
\partial_{x_{ij}}f_{c}=\partial_{x_{ij}}f-c\,a_{ij}

with
a_{ij}>0
, Lemma
5.12
and Remark
5.13

imply that
f_{c}\in\mathcal{B}
.

Let
g_{c}=\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa_{0}}f_{c}
and
\bar{l}=\Pi^{\downarrow}_{\kappa_{0}}l
.
Then,
g_{c}=g-c\,\bar{l}\in\mathrm{G}
for all
c>0
.

Let
h\in\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}g,
which belongs to
\mathcal{A}
by assumption.
Set
L=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(\bar{l})
. Note that
\nabla L>0
. Define

h_{c}:=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g_{c})=h-c\,L.

Then, for any
i\in[n]
and
j\in[\kappa_{i}],
\partial_{x_{ij}}h_{c}=\partial_{x_{ij}}h-ca^{\prime}_{ij},

with some constants
a^{\prime}_{ij}>0.
Lemma
5.12
and Remark

5.13
imply that
h_{c}\in\mathcal{B}
for all

c>0
.

Finally, choosing any sequence
c_{m}\downarrow 0
yields a sequence that
satisfies the conclusion of the theorem.
∎

8.5.
Closure under
\kappa
-polarization

Theorem
8.2
associates every general

Gårding
polynomial
g
with a
\kappa
-symmetric realization for some

\kappa\geq\kappa_{g}
. In this subsection, we show that we can always
pick
\kappa=\kappa_{g}
.

Proposition 8.10
.

Let
f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{B}
be
a multi-affine polynomial and symmetric in
x_{1},x_{2}
. Let
(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{0}f}
.
Then there exists a diagonal
u
with
\partial_{0}f(u_{0},u,u)=0

such that
\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\leq u\leq\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\},
and

f(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\leq f(u_{0},u,u).

Moreover, if
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
then
\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}<u<\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}.

Proof.

Considering symmetry, write

f(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})=ax_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+b_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+bx_{0}(x_{1}+x_{2})+c_{0}x_{0}+c(x_{1}+x_{2})+d.

Then

\partial_{0}f(x_{1},x_{2})=ax_{1}x_{2}+b(x_{1}+x_{2})+c_{0},\qquad f=x_{0}\,\partial_{0}f+h,

where
h(x_{1},x_{2})=b_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+c(x_{1}+x_{2})+d
.

Since
f\in\mathcal{B}
polynomials
\partial_{0}f
and
\partial_{1}f

are Gårding with degrees at most 2 and hence real–stable. In
particular,

b^{2}-ac_{0}\geq 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad bb_{0}-ac\geq 0.

Define
p(x)=\partial_{0}f(x,x)=ax^{2}+2bx+c_{0}
. Since
p
is
stable, its Gårding component is the interval to the right of
its largest real root. Let
u
be this boundary point, i.e.

u=\frac{-b+\sqrt{\,b^{2}-ac_{0}\,}}{a}\quad(a\neq 0),\ \text{or }u=-\frac{c_{0}}{2b}\quad(a=0).

Then
\partial_{0}f(u,u)=0
, so
(u_{0},u,u)\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{0}f}
.
Because
(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{0}f}
,
we also have
\partial_{0}f(u_{1},u_{2})=0
. Using
f=x_{0}\partial_{0}f+h
,

f(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})-f(u_{0},u,u)=h(u_{1},u_{2})-h(u,u).

If
a=0
, then
\partial_{0}f=b(x_{1}+x_{2})+c_{0}
and
\partial_{0}f(u_{1},u_{2})=0

imply
u=(u_{1}+u_{2})/2
. Hence
\min\{u_{1},u_{2}\}<u<\max\{u_{1},u_{2}\}

when
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
. A direct computation gives

h(u_{1},u_{2})-h(u,u)=-\frac{b_{0}}{4}(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}\leq 0,

and since
\partial_{1}f
is Gårding we have
b_{0}\geq 0
. Thus

f(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\leq f(u_{0},u,u)
.

Now assume
a>0
. Set
A_{1}:=au_{1}+b
,
A_{2}:=au_{2}+b
, and

A:=au+b
. Then
A_{1}=\partial_{02}f(u_{1},u_{2})
,
A_{2}=\partial_{01}f(u_{1},u_{2})
,
and
A=\partial_{01}f(u,u)
, all strictly positive by boundary non-degeneracy.
From
\partial_{0}f(u_{1},u_{2})=0
one finds

A_{1}A_{2}=b^{2}-ac_{0},

while by definition
A=\sqrt{b^{2}-ac_{0}}
, hence
A^{2}=A_{1}A_{2}
.
If
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
, then
A_{1}\neq A_{2}
; assuming that
A_{1}<A_{2}

gives
A^{2}=A_{1}A_{2}<A_{2}^{2}
, so
A<A_{2}
. Since
x\mapsto ax+b

is strictly increasing, this implies
u<u_{2}
, and symmetrically

u>u_{1}
.

Eliminating products using
\partial_{0}f(u_{1},u_{2})=\partial_{0}f(u,u)=0

yields

h(u_{1},u_{2})-h(u,u)=-\frac{bb_{0}-ac}{a}\,(u_{1}+u_{2}-2u).

Because
bb_{0}-ac\geq 0
and
u_{1}+u_{2}-2u\geq 0
, the right–hand
side is nonpositive. Hence
f(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\leq f(u_{0},u,u)
.
∎

Proposition 8.11
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{B}
be multi-affine and symmetric
in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Let
(u_{0},\mathbf{u})=(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{0}f}

and set
I=[\min_{i}u_{i},\max_{i}u_{i}]
. Then there exists
u^{*}\in I

such that

(u_{0},\mathbf{u}^{*})=(u_{0},u^{*},\dots,u^{*})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{0}f}

and

f(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\leq f(u_{0},u^{*},\dots,u^{*}).

Proof.

Define
\phi(\mathbf{x})=\max_{i\in[n]}x_{i}-\min_{i\in[n]}x_{i}
for
\mathbf{x}\in I^{n}
,
and set

\mathcal{F}=\bigl\{\mathbf{x}\in I^{n}:\ (u_{0},\mathbf{x})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{0}f}\ \text{and}\ f(u_{0},\mathbf{x})\geq f(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\bigr\}.

The set
\mathcal{F}\not=\emptyset
and is compact, hence,
\phi
attains
a minimum at some
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathcal{F}
.

By the non-degeneracy condition in the definition of
\mathcal{B}
,
there exists
T\geq 0
such that

\partial_{i}f(u_{0}+T,\mathbf{v})>0\qquad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,n.

Since
\partial_{0}f\equiv 0
on
\mathcal{F}
,
f(u_{0},\mathbf{v})=f(u_{0}+T,\mathbf{v})

and replacing
u_{0}
by
u_{0}+T
does not affect
\mathcal{F}

or
\phi
, and
\mathbf{v}
remains a minimizer.

Suppose that not all coordinates of
\mathbf{v}
coincide. Let
w^{(1)}<\cdots<w^{(m)}

be the distinct values among the set
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
with
m\geq 2
,
and let

K=\{k:\ v_{k}=w^{(m)}\}.

Fix an index
i
with
v_{i}=w^{(1)}
. For each
j\in K
, freeze
every coordinate
x_{k}
with
k\notin\{i,j\}
to
v_{k}
and consider
the two–variable restriction

g_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})=f(u_{0}+T,\dots,x_{i},\dots,x_{j},\dots).

Because
\partial_{\ell}f(u_{0}+T,\mathbf{v})>0
for all frozen indices

\ell
, the polynomial
g_{ij}
lies in
\mathcal{B}
by Lemma
5.15
and Proposition

8.10
applies and we may replace
(v_{i},v_{j})

by
(u_{ij},u_{ij})
with

u_{ij}\in[\min\{v_{i},v_{j}\},\max\{v_{i},v_{j}\}),

hence
u_{ij}\neq w^{(m)}
. Thus, each such replacement strictly decreases

|K|
which is the multiplicity of the maximal value
w^{(m)}
.
After at most
|K|
steps, the value
w^{(m)}
disappears from the
coordinate list, so the maximum coordinate strictly decreases and

\phi
strictly decreases. This contradicts the minimality of
\phi(\mathbf{v})
.

Therefore,
\mathbf{v}=(u^{*},\dots,u^{*})
for some
u^{*}\in I
. Since

\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
(u_{0}+T,\mathbf{u}^{*})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{0}f}
.
Hence
(u_{0},\mathbf{u}^{*})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{0}f}
and
f(u_{0},\mathbf{u}^{*})=f(u_{0}+T,\mathbf{u}^{*})
.
By the definition of
\mathcal{F}
,

f(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\leq f(u_{0}+T,u^{*},\dots,u^{*})=f(u_{0},\mathbf{u}^{*}).

We have finished the proof.
∎

Proposition 8.12
.

Let
g\in\mathrm{G}
be NRT and
\deg g>1.
Let

f=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)
. If
f\in\mathcal{B}
, then
f\in\mathrm{G}
.

Proof.

Let
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
be variables for
g
and let
(x_{ij})_{i\in[n],j\in[\kappa_{i}]}

be variables for
f
. By Proposition
5.7
,
if
f\in\mathcal{B}
, it suffices to verify that
f\leq 0
on each
boundary piece
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{ij}f}
. Without loss
of generality, we prove only the
i=1,j=1
case. Fix an arbitrary
point
p=(u_{11},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{11}f}
. Apply
Proposition
8.11
, to produce a point
\mathbf{q}=(u_{11},\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{11}f}

such that

f(\mathbf{p})\leq f(\mathbf{q}).

By the coordinate replacements allowed inthe lemma, we may first
make every block
i\neq 1
constant, then make all coordinates in
the first block equal except possibly the distinguished coordinate

x_{11}
. Finally, since
\partial_{11}f(\mathbf{q})=0
move freely,
x_{11}

will not affect
\partial_{11}f(\mathbf{q})
or
f(\mathbf{q})
.
Hence, we may assume that
\mathbf{q}
is
\kappa
-symmetric,
i.e.
q_{i,1}=\cdots=q_{i,\kappa_{i}}=:q_{i}^{*}
for each
i
.

Since
\mathbf{q}
is
\kappa
–symmetric, by the definition of polarization
we have
f(\mathbf{q})=g(\mathbf{q}^{*})
, where
q^{*}=(q_{1}^{*},\dots,q_{n}^{*})
.
Moreover
\mathbf{q}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{11}f}
implies

\mathbf{q}^{*}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{1}g}
. Because

g
is
Gårding
,
g(\mathbf{q}^{*})\leq 0
, and hence

f(\mathbf{q})=g(\mathbf{q}^{*})\leq 0.

Combining with
f(\mathbf{p})\leq f(\mathbf{q})
yields
f(\mathbf{p})\leq 0
.

Since
\mathbf{p}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{11}f}
was arbitrary,
this shows that
f\leq 0
on each
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{ij}f}
,
and therefore on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{f}^{(1)}
. By Proposition

5.7
, it follows that
f
is Gårding.
If
g\in\mathrm{G}
is NRT, then

\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)\in\mathcal{\mathrm{G}}
.

Let
f=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)
and set
d=\deg g
. We argue by induction.

Base case
d\leq 2
.
Proposition 3.4 in
[
12
]
uses Grace-Walsh-Szegö
Theorem
[
35
,
63
]
to show the

\kappa
-polarization preserves real stability in degree
\leq 2
.
Thus,
f\in\mathcal{\mathrm{G}}
.

Induction step.
Assume the statement holds for all polynomials
in
\mathrm{G}
of degree less than
d
, and let
\deg g=d>2
. Since the
polarization commutes with translation, without loss of generality,
assume that

g\in\mathrm{G}_{+}.

For each
i\in[n]
and
j\in[\kappa_{i}]
, the polarization identity

(8.4)

\partial_{x_{ij}}\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g)(\cdots,x_{i1},\cdots,x_{i\kappa_{i}},\cdots)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\Pi_{\kappa-\mathbf{e}_{i}}^{\uparrow}(\partial_{x_{i}}g)(\cdots,x_{i1},\cdots,\hat{x}_{ij},\cdots,x_{i\kappa_{i}},\cdots).

gives

\partial_{x_{ij}}f=\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\,\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa-e_{i}}\!\bigl(\partial_{x_{i}}g\bigr).

Since
\partial_{x_{i}}g\in\mathcal{\mathrm{G}}
has degree at most
d-1
,
the induction hypothesis implies

\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa-e_{i}}\!\bigl(\partial_{x_{i}}g\bigr)\in\mathcal{\mathrm{G}}.

Therefore, each first partial
\partial_{x_{ij}}f\in\mathrm{G}
, and
f

is in the auxiliary class
\mathcal{A}
.

We apply Proposition
8.9
to obtain a sequence

g^{(m)}\in\mathcal{B}
with
g^{(m)}\to g
in the compact-open
topology, and set
f^{(m)}=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}(g^{(m)})
. By the previous
paragraph, each
f^{(m)}
satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition

,
hence each

is Gårding. Finally, notice that

and hence

. Since

in compact-open topology,
for large enough

We apply Lemma

is Gårding.

This completes the induction and the proof.
∎

9.
A recursive characterization of
Gårding
polynomials

In this section, we extend the methods of the previous section to obtain a recursive characterization of
Gårding
polynomials and complete the proof of Theorem

Gårding

We first define a broader class of polynomials with weaker requirements. Let

is unique by Lemma

Definition 9.1
.

Fix

. For degrees

, let

. For degrees

be the class of

(3)

Set

We denote

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{f}^{(k)}=\bigcap_{|\alpha|=k}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\partial^{\alpha}f}.

Then

if and only if

\mathcal{G}

Remark 9.2
.

The existence of positive components is the central feature common in defintions of

In the multi-affine case,

9.2.
Right-Noetherian polynomial

In
[
50
,
49
]
, motivated by problems in geometric PDEs,
Lin introduced
right-Noetherian polynomials
. In this subsection,
we show that Lin’s constructions are special cases of
Gårding
polynomials.
Recall that for a univariate polynomial
f(x),

r(f)
denotes
the
largest real root of
f
, when it exists.

Definition 9.11
.

Let
f
be a univariate polynomial of degree
d
. Define its
root
sequence
by

R(f):=(r(f),r(f^{\prime}),\cdots,r(f^{(d-1)}))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We say that
f
has a
monotone root sequence

(MRS)

if
r(f^{(i)})
exists for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
and

r(f)\geq r(f^{\prime})\geq\cdots\geq r(f^{(d-1)}).

Definition 9.12
.

(Lin
[
50
]
) A univariate polynomial
f
is called
right-Noetherian
if it has a monotone root sequence.

We give a geometric interpretation. For univariate
f(x)\in\mathrm{G}[x]
,
the Gårding component is

(9.5)

\mathcal{C}_{f}=(r(f),+\infty)

when
r(f)
exists. A right-Noetherian polynomial
f
can be
characterized by

\emptyset\neq\mathcal{C}_{f}\subset\mathcal{C}_{f^{\prime}}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{C}_{f^{(d-1)}}.

A direct consequence of Definition
9.1
and Theorem

9.10
is that the class of right-noetherian polynomials
is exactly that of univariate Gårding polynomials.

Example 9.13
.

Given a sequence of monotone
real numbers
r_{0}\geq r_{1}\geq\cdots\geq r_{d-1}
, a right-noetherian
polynomial may be constructed inductively as follows: Set
f_{d}(x)=1
,
and for each
0\leq k\leq d-1
, define

f_{k}(x):=\int_{r_{k}}^{x}f_{k+1}(t)dt.

Then
f(x):=f_{0}(x)
is a univariate Gårding polynomial with the
prescribed root sequence. Furthermore,
f
is unique up to a positive
constant multiple.

Remark 9.14
.

An alternative way to show that right-Noetherian polynomials coincide with

\mathrm{G}_{1}
is to apply Proposition
8.11
to prove that
a
\kappa
-polarization of a right noetherian polynomial is multi-affine
Gårding. For
\kappa=d
, this result is essentially due to Lin
[
50
]

where such polarizations are called
\Upsilon
-stable. For
\kappa>d
,
especially in the degenerate case where successive derivatives have common largest real roots,
Lin’s argument requires additional
justification. The approach
in Theorem
9.10
explicitly addresses the degeneracy.

In one variable, real stability is equivalent to real-rootedness. By the classical interlacing theorem, real-rootedness implies MRS, and hence

\{\text{univariate real-stable polynomials}\}\subseteq\{\text{univariate G{\aa}rding polynomials}\}.

In particular, MRS is strictly broader than real stability in the univariate setting.

Remark 9.15
.

Let
g
be a multivariate Gårding
polynomial and let
\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma_{n}^{+},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then the univariate specialization
f(t)=g(\mathbf{a}t+\mathbf{b})

is a univariate Gårding polynomial. Equivalently,
f
satisfies
the MRS property. Thus, MRS
for all affine univariate specializations provides an analytic necessary
condition for the Gårding property.

10.
Structural Consequences

In the preceding sections, we have shown that the Gårding property is preserved under strictly positive affine changes of variables, partial differentiation, and
\kappa
-polarization. Moreover, using polarization together with diagonal restriction, most results proved in the multi-affine setting extend directly to general Gårding polynomials.

We record here three simple but useful consequences of these closure properties.

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]
. If
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\mathbf{a}\geq 0
, then the directional derivative

\partial_{\textbf{a}}g:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\,\partial_{i}g\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}].

Proof.

Fix
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{g}
and define
G(\mathbf{x},t):=g(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}_{0}+t\,\textbf{a})
. Since
\mathbf{x}_{0}+t\,\textbf{a}\in\mathcal{C}_{g}
for all
t>0
, the polynomial
G
is Gårding in
(\mathbf{x},t)
. By closure under partial differentiation,
\partial_{t}G
is Gårding. Restricting to
t=0
yields
\partial_{t}G(\mathbf{x},0)=\partial_{\textbf{a}}g(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}_{0})
, which is therefore Gårding. By translation invariance, we have proved the proposition.
∎

Proposition 10.2
.

If
f,g\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]
, then
f\cdot g\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]
.

Proof.

By polarization, we may assume that
f
and
g
are multi-affine. Consider the product in independent variables
F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{y})
. Since the multi-affine Gårding class is closed under products in disjoint variable sets,
F
is Gårding. Restricting to the diagonal
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}
preserves the Gårding property and yields
F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]
.
∎

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
T:\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\gamma}[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]

be a linear operator. The symbol of
T
is defined by

\mathrm{sym}_{T}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}):=T\bigl((\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})^{\kappa}\bigr).

If
\mathrm{sym}_{T}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})\in\mathrm{G}_{+}\cap\mathbb{R}_{\gamma\oplus\kappa}[\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}]
, then for every
f\in\mathrm{G}_{+}\cap\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]
one has

T(f)\in\mathrm{G}_{+}\cap\mathbb{R}_{\gamma}[\mathbf{y}].

Proof.

The statement is simply a reformulation of the linear preserver theorem for Lorentzian polynomials in Brändén–Huh
[
15
]
; we include a short argument for completeness.

Let
\widetilde{f}:=\Pi_{\kappa}^{\uparrow}(f)
be the
\kappa
-polarization of
f
. Polarizing the operator
T
yields a linear operator
\widetilde{T}
acting on the multi-affine space, whose symbol
\mathrm{sym}_{\widetilde{T}}
is the polarization of
\mathrm{sym}_{T}
and therefore still lies in
\mathrm{G}_{+}
. By Theorem
7.2
,
\widetilde{T}(\widetilde{f})\in\mathrm{G}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}
. Finally, applying the
\kappa
-projection operator gives

T(f)=\Pi_{\kappa}^{\downarrow}\bigl(\widetilde{T}(\widetilde{f})\bigr).

The diagonal restriction preserves
\mathrm{G}_{+}
; hence
T(f)\in\mathrm{G}_{+}\cap\mathbb{R}_{\gamma}[\mathbf{y}]
.
∎

We present a few examples of linear preservers.

Example 10.4

(Normalization)
.

Consider the univariate normalization operator
T_{N}:x^{k}\mapsto\frac{x^{k}}{k!}
defined in
\mathbb{R}_{d}[x]
. Then

\mathrm{sym}_{T_{N}}(x,1)=L_{d}(-x),
where
L_{d}(x)
is the degree
d
Laguerre polynomial and is real-rooted. By Lemma
9.13
, after homogenization,
\mathrm{sym}_{T_{N}}(x,u)\in\mathrm{S}_{+}[x,u]
. By Theorem
10.3
,
T_{N}
preserves
\mathrm{G}_{+}[x]
. By induction on the number of variables, the multivariate normalization operator

T_{N}:\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}{\alpha_{i}!}

also preserves
\mathrm{G}_{+}
.

Example 10.5

(Symmetric multiplier)
.

Let
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\geq 0
. Define a linear operator

T:\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]\to\mathbb{R}_{n}[x],\;x^{k}\mapsto\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{p})x^{k}.

Then
T
preserves
\mathrm{G}_{n}[x]
. To see that, consider
T_{1}:x^{k}\mapsto\frac{n!\,\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{p})}{\binom{n}{k}}x^{k}
. Then

\mathrm{sym}_{T_{1}}(x,u)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(p_{i}x+u)
is clearly real stable. Since the symbol of
T
is the normalization of that of
T_{1}
, by Example

,
\mathrm{sym}_{T}(x,u)\in\mathrm{S}_{+}
. By Theorem
10.3
,
T
preserves
\mathrm{G}^{n}[x]
.

10.1.
The inversion operation

Recall that the inversion operator
T_{\tau}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is defined by

T_{\tau}(f)(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=f(-1/x_{1},\dots,-1/x_{n})\,x_{1}^{\kappa_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\kappa_{n}},

so that
T_{\tau}
sends a monomial
\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}
to
(-1)^{|\alpha|}\mathbf{x}^{\kappa-\alpha}
and satisfies
T_{\tau}^{2}=\mathrm{id}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]
.

Theorem 10.6
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{G}_{+}(\mathbf{x})\cap\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[x]
and assume
f(0)>0
. Then

T_{\tau}(f)\in\mathrm{G}(\mathbf{x}).

Proof.

By polarization, it suffices to treat the multi-affine case. Thus, assume

f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{S\subset[n]}a_{S}\mathbf{x}^{S}\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x}]

with
a_{\varnothing}=f(\mathbf{0})>0
. Since
\partial_{x_{i}}f\prec f\prec y_{i}f
, we have
\partial_{x_{i}}f\vartriangleleft y_{i}f
. Hence

(y_{i}-\partial_{x_{i}})f\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x},y_{i}]\qquad(1\leq i\leq n),

and therefore, by repeated application,

g(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-\partial_{x_{i}})\Bigr)f(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}].

Write
A=\{S\subset[n]:a_{S}\neq 0\}
. Because
a_{\varnothing}>0
, the family
A
is downward closed: if
S\in A
, then every
T\subset S
lies in
A
. Hence

g(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{S\in A}\mathbf{x}^{S}\,g_{S}(\mathbf{y}),

where each coefficient polynomial
g_{S}(\mathbf{y})
has a leading coefficient
a_{S}>0
. Thus, for all sufficiently large
\mathbf{y}>0
, we have
g_{S}(\mathbf{y})\geq 0
for every
S\in A
, so
(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{y})
lies in the distinguished component
\mathcal{C}_{g}
for large
\mathbf{y}
. In particular,
g(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{y})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{y}]
.

Finally, observe that
g(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{y})=T_{\tau}(f)(\mathbf{y})
. Since diagonal specialization preserves the Gårding property, we conclude that
T_{\tau}(f)\in\mathrm{G}(\mathbf{y})
.
∎

Example 10.7
.

The condition
f(0)>0
is necessary in Theorem
10.6
.

Indeed, consider

f(x)=(x+1)^{4}-1-4x.

Then
f\in\mathrm{G}[x]
, since
R(f)=(0,0,-1,-1)
is an MRS. A direct computation yields

T_{\tau}(f)(x)=6x^{2}-4x+1,

which is a degree-
2
polynomial that is not real-stable. By Theorem

, this implies that

is not Gårding.

10.2.
Binary relations for general Gårding polynomials

We extend to general Gårding polynomials the notions of domination and proper position introduced earlier in the multi-affine setting.

Definition 10.8
.

Let
g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]
. If
f(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x}]
satisfies

\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}g}\subset\{\partial^{\alpha}f>0\}\quad\text{for every nontrivial partial derivative }\partial^{\alpha}f,

then we say that
g

dominates

f
, and write
f\vartriangleleft g
.

Similarly, we have the following definition.

Definition 10.9
.

Suppose
f(\mathbf{x}),g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]\setminus\{0\}
. The pair
(f,g)
is said to be in
proper position
if

f(\mathbf{x})\,y+g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x},y].

In this case, we also write
f\prec g
. We adopt the convention that
0\prec g\prec 0
for any
g\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]
.

It is straightforward to verify that Definitions
6.1
and
6.3
in the multi-affine case are special cases of Definitions
10.8
and
10.9
. Proceeding as in the multi-affine setting, we prove the following result.

Theorem 10.10
.

Let
f(\mathbf{x}),g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]
, and suppose
g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]
. If
f\vartriangleleft g
, then

\Pi_{\kappa}^{\uparrow}f\;\vartriangleleft\;\Pi_{\kappa}^{\uparrow}g.

Proof.

Let
N=|\kappa|
. We argue by induction on the total degree. If
\deg f,\deg g\leq 1
, the statement is immediate.

Assume that the result holds for all pairs of degree less than
d
. Let
f,g\in\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]\setminus\{0\}
with
\deg g=d
and
f\vartriangleleft g
. As in the proof of Lemma
6.6
, we have:

(1)

If
f\vartriangleleft g
, then
\deg f\leq\deg g
.

(2)

If
g
is independent of
x_{i}
, then
f
is independent of
x_{i}
.

By Lemma
9.5
, we may assume that
g
is NRT. Set

\tilde{f}=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}f(\mathbf{w}),\quad\tilde{g}=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}g(\mathbf{w}),\quad h(\mathbf{w},z)=z\tilde{g}(\mathbf{w})-\tilde{f}(\mathbf{w}).

By the induction hypothesis and Theorem
6.12
,

\partial_{w_{ij}}h\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}\ \text{for all }i\in[n],\ j\in[\kappa_{i}],\qquad\partial_{z}h=\Pi^{\uparrow}_{\kappa}g\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}.

Thus

belongs to the auxiliary class
\mathcal{A}
.

It suffices to prove the result for
h\in\mathcal{B}
. Otherwise, since
z\cdot g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x},z]
is NRT, we may find a linear function

such that

and

by Lemma
9.8
. For any positive integer

Then
f_{m}\vartriangleleft g_{m}
and
h_{m}\in\mathcal{B}
. Once we show
h_{m}\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
for all sufficiently large

, it follows that
h=\lim_{m\to\infty}h_{m}
is Gårding.

We claim that if
h\in\mathcal{B}
, then

(10.1)

\tilde{f}\big|_{\partial\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{g}}}\geq 0.

Indeed, suppose that there exists
\mathbf{w}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{g}}=\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{z}h}
such that
\tilde{f}(\mathbf{w})<0
. Applying Proposition
8.11
to

, we obtain
(\mathbf{u}^{*},z^{*})\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{z}h}
with

such that for each

,

u_{i1}^{*}=\cdots=u_{i\kappa_{i}}^{*}.

Let
\mathbf{u}=(u_{11}^{*},\dots,u_{n1}^{*})
. Then
g(\mathbf{u})=0
and
-f(\mathbf{u})>0
. Since
\mathbf{u}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{g}
, this contradicts
\mathcal{C}_{g}\subset\{f>0\}
. Hence (
10.1
) holds.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a connected component of
\{\tilde{f}>0\}
that intersects
\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{g}}
. If
\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{g}}\setminus\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing
, Lemma
6.5
gives a point
\mathbf{w}_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{g}}
such that
\tilde{f}(\mathbf{w}_{1})<0
. For any
\mathbf{w}_{2}\leq\mathbf{w}_{1}
with
\mathbf{w}_{2}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{g}}
, we have
\tilde{f}(\mathbf{w}_{2})\geq 0
by (
10.1
). Since
\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{g}}
is NRT, it follows that
\nabla\tilde{f}(\mathbf{w}_{1})<0
, and hence for all
\mathbf{v}\geq 0
with
|\mathbf{v}|\leq 1
,

(10.2)

\tilde{f}(\mathbf{w}_{1}+r\,\mathbf{v})\leq\tilde{f}(\mathbf{w}_{1})<0\quad\text{for small }r>0.

Let
G=\{\mathbf{w}:\mathbf{w}\geq\mathbf{w}_{1},\ \tilde{f}(\mathbf{w})\leq\tilde{f}(\mathbf{w}_{1})\}
. Then

is closed and, by (

), also open in

. Hence

. Choosing

on

∎

The following result is a direct consequence of Theorems

,
6.8
and Proposition

.

Corollary 10.11
.

Let
g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{\mathrm{G}}[\mathbf{x}]
. Let

be a non-trivial polynomial such that

.

(1)

For any

such that

(2)

For

, the directional derivative

(3)

One has

if and only if

contains a positive affine

g(\mathbf{x})\,y-f(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x},y].

Next, we prove

Theorem 10.12
.

Suppose that
f(\mathbf{x}),g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}_{\kappa}[\mathbf{x}]\cap\mathrm{G}[\mathbf{x}]

if and only if for each

,

(10.3)

\partial^{\alpha}g(\mathbf{x})\leq 0,\ \forall\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}f}}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}g}.

To prove Theorem

Lemma 10.13
.

For

The second lemma generalizes Lemma

Lemma 10.14
.

Suppose that

and

\partial^{\alpha}g\big|_{\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}f}}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\partial^{\alpha}g}}\leq 0\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.

If
f
is independent of
x_{i}

\deg_{x_{i}}g\leq 1
and
\partial_{i}g
and

are proportional.

Proof.

Suppose
f
is independent of
x_{i}
; then
\partial_{i}f\equiv 0
. For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{g}
, define the univariate polynomial

p_{\mathbf{x}}(t)=g(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{e}_{i}).

Since

Proof of Theorem

(

(

11.
Homogeneous Gårding Polynomials are Lorentzian

In this section, we examine the relationships among
Gårding
, real stable, and Lorentzian polynomials. We study homogenization and convexity properties of
Gårding
polynomials, and show that homogeneous
Gårding
polynomials are Lorentzian, thereby implying Hodge-type inequalities and log-concavity of their coefficient sequences. Through examples, we illustrate their structural differences, highlighting connections and suggesting directions for further investigation.

First, we recall the definition of Lorentzian polynomials in
[
15
]
.

Definition 11.1

(Lorentizian polynomials)
.

A homogeneous degree
d\geq 2
polynomial
f
with non-negative coefficients is
Lorentzian
if it satisfies 2 conditions:

(1)

For all multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=d-2
,
\partial^{\alpha}f
is real stable.

(2)

The support of
f
is M-convex.

We denote
\mathrm{L}^{d}
the class of Lorentzian polynomials of degree
d
.

Homogeneous degree
d
real stable polynomials in
\mathrm{S}^{d,\mathfrak{h}}
are Lorentzian. The same holds for
\mathrm{G}^{d,\mathfrak{h}}
, the class of homogeneous degree
d

Gårding
polynomials. In fact
\mathrm{G}^{d,\mathfrak{h}}
is sandwiched between real-stable and Lorentzian ones.

Theorem 11.2
.

Homogeneous
Gårding
polynomials are Lorentzian. Moreover, for
d\geq 2
,

(11.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}^{d,\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathrm{G}^{d,\mathfrak{h}}\subset\mathrm{L}^{d}.

Proof.

The first inclusion follows from Theorem
4.16
. A homogeneous
Gårding
polynomial
f
has non-negative coefficients. Since both
Gårding
and Lorentzian polynomials are preserved under polarization and projection, it suffices to prove the multi-affine case.

Let
f\in\mathrm{G}_{+}^{d,\mathfrak{a,h}}
. For
|\alpha|=d-2
,
\partial^{\alpha}f\in\mathrm{G}^{2,\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{h}}
. In degree 2,
\mathrm{G}^{2,\mathfrak{a}}=\mathrm{S}^{2,\mathfrak{a}}
. Hence,
f
satisfies (
1
) in Definition
11.1
.

By Theorem
5.21
,
f
is Rayleigh. By
[
15
, Theorem 2.23]
, homogeneous Rayleigh polynomials have M-convex support. Hence,
f
satisfies (
2
) in Definition
11.1
. We have finished the proof.
∎

When
d=2
, all three classes coincide. The case
d=3
is also special in the following sense.

Proposition 11.3
.

\mathrm{S}^{3,\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{G}^{3,\mathfrak{h}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.21
, it suffices to show that any
f\in\mathrm{G}^{3,\mathfrak{h}}
is real stable. Equivalently, it suffices to prove that
f(t\textbf{a}+\textbf{b})
is real-rooted for any
\textbf{a}>0
and
\textbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Since
\textbf{a}>0
, by replacing
t
with
t+T_{0}
for a sufficiently large
T_{0}
, we may assume
\textbf{b}>0
. Since
f\in\mathrm{G}^{3,\mathfrak{h}}
,

h(t,s):=f(t\textbf{a}+s\textbf{b})\in\mathrm{G}^{3,\mathfrak{h}}[t,s].

We claim that either
h(t,1)
or
h(1,s)
is real-rooted. Once proved,
h
is the homogenization of a real stable polynomial, and hence
h
is real stable by Lemma
4.3
(3).
Write

h(t,s)=a_{3}t^{3}+a_{2}t^{2}s+a_{1}ts^{2}+a_{0}s^{3},

where
a_{i}\geq 0
. We consider 2 cases.

If
a_{3}=0
, then
h(t,1)\in\mathrm{G}^{2}[t]=\mathrm{S}^{2}[t].
By symmetry,
h(1,s)
is real stable when
a_{0}=0
.

Assume
a_{3},a_{0}\not=0
. We argue by contradiction. Let
(r_{0},r_{1},r_{2})
be the root sequence of
h(t,1)
which is decreasing. Note
r_{0}<0
. Let
\rho_{0}<0
be the largest real root
h(1,s)
. Then
1/\rho_{0}
is also a root of
h(t,1)
. If
h(t,1)
is not real stable,
1/\rho_{0}=r_{0}
is a single root hence the only real root of
h(t,1)
. Note
1/r_{1}
is a root of
\partial_{t}h(1,s)
. Since
\partial_{t}h\vartriangleleft h
, we have
\partial_{t}h(1,s)\vartriangleleft h(1,s)
which implies
\rho_{0}\geq 1/r_{1}
. However, since
r_{1}<r_{0}<0
,

0>\frac{1}{r_{1}}>\frac{1}{r_{0}}=\rho_{0}.

A contradiction. Thus, we have proved the claim.

Since
h
is real rooted,
f
is real stable by Lemma
4.3
(1). We have finished the proof.
∎

The following example illustrates the strict inclusions in (
11.1
) for
d\geq 4
.

Example 11.4
.

Let
c\geq 0
. Consider the bivariate polynomial

f(x,y)=(x^{2}+cxy+y^{2})xy.

Then
f
is real stable if and only if
c\geq 2
. By
[
15
, Example 2.26]
,
f
is Lorentzian if and only if the coefficient sequence is ultra log-concave, equivalently, if and only if
c\geq 3/2
. We claim that
f
is
Gårding
if and only if
c\geq\sqrt{3}
. In fact, when this holds,

\partial_{x}f=\frac{y}{3}(3x+(c-\sqrt{c^{2}-3})y)(3x+(c+\sqrt{c^{2}-3})y),

which is real stable. The same holds for
\partial_{y}f
. Since
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{f}=\Gamma_{2}^{+}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{x}f}
for
c\geq\sqrt{3}
, it follows that
f\in\mathrm{G}
. If
c<\sqrt{3}
, then
f(x,1)
is not
Gårding
, and hence neither is
f
.

Further computation indicates that for
n\geq 1
,

f_{n}(x,y)=(x^{2}+c_{n}xy+y^{2})x^{n}y^{n}\in\mathrm{G}_{+}

if
c_{n}\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{n+2}{n+1}
. In contrast,
f_{n}\in\mathrm{S}_{+}
whenever
c_{n}\geq 2
, and
f_{n}\in\mathrm{L}^{n+2}
provided
c_{n}\geq\frac{n+2}{n+1}
.

Example 11.5
.

The basis generating polynomial of the Fano matroid
F_{7}
is an example of a polynomial that is Lorentzian and Rayleigh, but not Gårding. See Proposition
14.9
and Theorem
14.10
.

Next, we show that the homogenization of a Gårding polynomial with nonnegative coefficients need not be a Gårding polynomial, which differs notably from real stable cases.

Example 11.6
.

Let
f(t)=t(t^{2}+3t^{2}+3).

Since
f
has the monotone root sequence
R(f)=(0,-1,-1)
,
f(t)\in\mathrm{G}_{+}[t]
.
Consider the homogenization of
f

\text{H}f(t,y)=t^{3}+3t^{2}y+3ty^{2}.

Notice that
\text{H}f(1,y)
has degree 2 but is not real rooted; hence, it
is not
Gårding
. Therefore,
\text{H}f(t,y)
is not
Gårding
.

The above example also shows that if
f=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{k}x^{k}

is univariate
Gårding
with nonnegative coefficients,
\tilde{f}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{k}x^{d-k}

is not necessarily so.

The
{\mathrm{G\mathring{a}rding}}{}
component of a real-stable polynomial is convex; this property does not extend to
{\mathrm{G\mathring{a}rding}}{}
polynomials.

Example 11.7
.

Consider

f(x,y)=(x^{2}+y^{2}+\sqrt{3}xy-1)xy.

As in Example
11.4
,
\partial_{x}f
is real stable and
\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{x}f}
contains

\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{f}=\Gamma_{2}^{+}\backslash\{(x,y):x^{2}+y^{2}+\sqrt{3}xy\leq 1\}.

Similarly,
\mathcal{C}_{\partial_{y}f}
also contains

\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
. Hence,
f
is
Gårding
. Notice that
\mathcal{C}_{f}=\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{f}
is not
convex.

Remark 11.8
.

A central feature of Gårding’s theory of hyperbolic polynomials is the convexity of their associated Gårding cones, a property closely tied to analytic log-concavity. From the perspective of partial differential equations, our Gårding property corresponds only to ellipticity. In general, however, the concavity assumption required for the Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck theory fails.

It is also worth noting that Lorentzian polynomials exhibit log-concavity in the positive hyper-octant. In subsequent work, we will focus on a distinguished subclass of Gårding polynomials with additional convexity properties and establish a precise connection with Lorentzian polynomials through homogenization.

Part IV
Applications

In this part, we apply the preceding theory to generating functions that arise
in matroid theory, graph theory, probability, and matrix theory. These
functions are typically multi-affine with nonnegative coefficients, making
them a natural setting for the Gårding framework, which yields new
Rayleigh-type inequalities and negative dependence results beyond those
accessible via stability or Lorentzian methods.

12.
Generating functions on matroids

We study generating functions of matroids, which provide a natural
testing ground for the Gårding framework and its interaction with
Rayleigh properties in combinatorics. Matroid
theory occupies a central position in modern combinatorics, unifying
phenomena from graphs, linear algebra, and optimization, and has seen
much recent developments surrounding Lorentzian polynomials and combinatorial
Hodge theory.

12.1.
Generating functions on distinguished sets of a matroid

We introduce the four standard generating functions associated with
a matroid-those of independent sets, bases, spanning sets, and
cospanning sets. Throughout we use the matroid notation from
[
52
]
.

Let
M=(E,\mathcal{I},\mathcal{B},r_{M})
be a finite matroid on the
ground set
E
, where
\mathcal{I}
denotes the family of independent
sets,
\mathcal{B}
the family of bases, and
r_{M}
the rank function.
For each
e\in E,
let
w_{e}
be a variable and write
\mathbf{w}=(w_{e})_{e\in E}
.
For any set
S\subset E,

w_{S}=\prod_{e\in E}w_{e}
.

Definition 12.1

(Generating Functions)
.

Let
M
be a matroid as above. We associate with

M
the following four multi-affine generating functions.

The
independent-set generating function
(ISGF) of
M
is

I_{M}(\mathbf{w}):=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}w_{I}.

The
basis generating function
(BSGF) of
M
is

B_{M}(\mathbf{w}):=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}w_{B}.

The
spanning-set generating function
(SSGF) of
M
is

S_{M}(\mathbf{w}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq E\\
T\text{ spanning}\end{subarray}}w_{T},

where a subset
T\subseteq E
is called
spanning
if it contains
a basis of
M
.

The
cospanning-set generating function
(CSGF) of
M
, is

C_{M}(\mathbf{w}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\subseteq E\\
E\setminus X\in\mathcal{I}\end{subarray}}w_{X}.

All these polynomials are multi-affine with nonnegative coefficients and
are related by simple combinatorial transformations. In particular, taking
the dual matroid interchanges the SSGF and CSGF; ISGF and CSGF are
related by inversion; and BSGF is the homogeneous component of degree

r_{M}
of both
I_{M}
and
S_{M}
.

To simplify notation, we introduce the following definitions.

Definition 12.2
.

Let
M
be a matroid with generating
functions
I_{M},B_{M},S_{M},C_{M}
as above. For
X\in\{I,B,S,C\}
,
we say that
M
is
X
–Rayleigh
(respectively,
X
–Gårding
)
if the corresponding generating function
X_{M}
is Rayleigh (respectively
Gårding). Following Wagner
[
24
]
, a
Rayleigh matroid

is one whose basis generating function is Rayleigh; in the present
notation, this is precisely a
B
–Rayleigh matroid.

The study of negative dependence and Rayleigh-type inequalities for
matroids has developed along several distinct lines. Feder-Mihail

[
33
]
introduced the concept of negatively correlated
matroids which is equivalent to the following condition: for
i\not=j\in E
,

(B_{M}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}B_{M}-\partial_{i}B_{M}\partial_{j}B_{M})|_{\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{1}}\leq 0,

where
\mathbf{1}
is the all one vector. Later, Choe-Wagner
[
24
]

defined the class of Rayleigh matroids which is equivalent to
B
-Rayleigh
matroids in Definition
12.2
.
B
-Rayleigh
matroids include all rank 3 matroids
[
69
]
, binary
matroids without
S_{8}
factor,
[
24
]
etc. In particular, all regular matroids
are Rayleigh matroids. In fact, they are HPP matroids (matroids with half-plane-property), meaning that their BSGFs are real stable
[
23
]
. Many negative dependence conditions
can be derived from the seminal work of Borcea-Brändén-Ligget
[
13
]
.
Semple-Welsh
[
57
]
introduced correlation inequalities
for spanning sets, extending earlier probabilistic results for graphs.
I
-Rayleigh and
S
-Rayleigh matroids were introduced by Wagner in
[
68
]
.

In the following, we provide a unified framework connecting these generating functions via natural algebraic operations and show that the
Gårding
property for the spanning-set and cospanning-set generating functions implies Rayleigh-type inequalities for the basis-set and independent-set generating functions. This yields a structural approach to negative dependence across different matroid generating functions beyond the classical real stable setting. While the
Gårding
property does not capture all Rayleigh polynomials, it applies in settings where stability-based methods are not available and provides a flexible tool for studying negative dependence and other combinatorial properties of matroids.

12.2.
Main Theorems

We now state the main result of this section, formulated for the spanning-set generating function.

Theorem 12.3
.

Let
M
be a matroid. Then the spanning-set generating function
S_{M}(\mathbf{w})
is a Gårding polynomial in each of the following cases:

(1)

M
is a series–parallel network;

(2)

M=U_{r,n}
is a uniform matroid;

(3)

M=\widehat{U}_{r,n}
is the rank-
r
matroid on
n
elements with exactly one circuit-hyperplane of size
r
;

(4)

|E(M)|\leq 6
.

Moreover, the Gårding property of
S_{M}(\mathbf{w})
is preserved under deletion, contraction, series–parallel reductions and
2
-sums. Equivalently, the class of
S
-Gårding matroids is closed under these operations.

By duality, we have the following.

Corollary 12.4
.

All statements in Theorem
12.3
also hold for the cospanning-set generating function
C_{M}(\mathbf{w})
and the basis-set generating function
B_{M}(\mathbf{w})
.

Remark 12.5
.

Theorem
12.3
and Corollary
12.4
provide
natural families of Gårding polynomials that are not real stable in general.

12.3.
A formula for SSGF

In this subsection, we prepare for the proof of the main theorems by deriving a combinatorial formula for the SSGF of a matroid.

Let
M=(E,\mathcal{I},\mathcal{B},r_{M})
be a matroid as above. Define the modified weights

v_{e}=w_{e}+1.

For any subset
S\subset E
, write
v_{S}:=\prod_{e\in S}v_{e}
, and set
v_{\emptyset}=1
. Let
\mathcal{L}(M)
denote the lattice of flats of
M
, partially ordered by inclusion, with
E
as the top element and
\emptyset
as the bottom element. Let
\mu(\cdot,\cdot)
be the Möbius function of
\mathcal{L}(M)
.

Theorem 12.6
.

With the above notation, the spanning-set generating function of
M
satisfies

(12.1)

S_{M}(\mathbf{w})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{L}(M)}\mu(F,E)\,v_{F}.

Moreover,
S_{M}
satisfies the recursive identity

(12.2)

S_{M}(\mathbf{w})=v_{E}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{L}(M)\\
F\neq E\end{subarray}}S_{M|F}(\mathbf{w}),

where
M|F
denotes the restriction of
M
to flat
F
.

In many cases, (
12.1
) involves considerably fewer monomials than its defining formula.

Proof.

For each flat
\alpha\in\mathcal{L}(M)
, define a pair of polynomials

F(\alpha):=\sum_{\mathrm{cl}(T)=\alpha}w_{T},\quad G(\alpha):=\sum_{\beta\subset\alpha}F(\beta).

Since
\mathrm{cl}(T)\subset\alpha
is equivalent to
T\subset\alpha
,
we have

G(\alpha)=\sum_{\beta\subset\alpha}\sum_{\mathrm{cl}(T)=\beta}w_{T}=\sum_{T\subset\alpha}w_{T}=\prod_{e\in\alpha}(1+w_{e})=v_{\alpha}.

The Möbius inversion formula
[
56
, Proposition 2]
on
\mathcal{L}(M)

shows

F(\alpha)=\sum_{\beta\subset\alpha}\mu(\beta,\alpha)\,v_{\beta}.

Summing over all proper flats and using the standard identity

\sum_{\beta\subset\alpha\subsetneq E}\mu(\beta,\alpha)=-\mu(\beta,E),

We have proved (
12.1
).

For each proper flat
F\in\mathcal{L}(M)
, applying (
12.1
)
to
S_{M|F}
, summing them over, and interchanging the order of summation,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper }F\in\mathcal{L}(M)\end{subarray}}S_{M|F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper }F\in\mathcal{L}(M)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathcal{L}(M|F)\end{subarray}}\mu(G,F)\,v_{G}.

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{proper\ }G\in\mathcal{L}(M)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{L}(M):\;G\subseteq F\subsetneq E\end{subarray}}\mu(G,F)v_{G}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{proper\ }G\in\mathcal{L}(M)\end{subarray}}\mu(G,E)\,v_{G},

where we have used the Möbius identity

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{L}(M):\;G\subseteq F\subseteq E\end{subarray}}\mu(G,F)=0\qquad\text{for }G\subsetneq E.

Applying (
12.1
) again, we have proved (
12.2
).
∎

Remark 12.7
.

Since the CSGF of a matroid
M
is the SSGF of its dual
M^{*}
, similar formulae hold for the CSGF.

13.
Proof of Theorem
12.3

In this section, we prove Theorem
12.3
and Corollary
12.4
. The proof combines closure properties of the Gårding class with verification on fundamental classes of matroids. We begin with concrete examples, including uniform matroids, then show that the Gårding property is preserved under standard matroid operations, and finally verify the result for the base classes appearing in Theorem
12.3
. For completeness, we also record formulas for both the SSGF and the CSGF.

13.1.
Trees, cycles, uniform matroids, and matroids with one circuit-hyperplane

We introduce some concrete examples, beginning with graphic ones.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph, and let
M(G)
be the associated cycle
matroid.

Example 13.1
.

If
T
is a tree, then
E
is the unique spanning tree. Consequently,

S_{M(T)}(\mathbf{w})=\prod_{e\in E}w_{e}=w_{E},\qquad C_{M(T)}(\mathbf{w})=\prod_{e\in E}(1+w_{e})=v_{E}.

Both polynomials are stable, and hence Gårding. Therefore, every tree is both
S
–Gårding and
C
–Gårding.

Example 13.2
.

Let
C_{n}
be the cycle graph on
n
edges. A subset is spanning in
M(C_{n})
if it contains at least
n-1
elements; it is cospanning if it is nonempty. Therefore,

S_{M(C_{n})}(\mathbf{w})=\sigma_{n}(\mathbf{w})+\sigma_{n-1}(\mathbf{w}),\quad C_{M(C_{n})}(\mathbf{w})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+w_{j})-1.

Both polynomials are symmetric and their diagonal specializations satisfy the monotone root sequence (MRS) property. Hence, by Theorem
9.10
, they are Gårding. Thus, the cycle
C_{n}
is both
S
–Gårding and
C
–Gårding.

Next, we give a simple non-graphic example.

Example 13.3
.

Let
U_{r,n}
be the uniform matroid of rank
r
on the ground set
E=[n]
. The dual matroid of
U_{r,n}
is
U_{n-r,n}
. A subset of
E
is spanning if and only if it has cardinality at least
r
. Hence

S_{U_{r,n}}(\mathbf{w})=\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{w}).

Let
P_{r,n}=\Pi^{\downarrow}S_{U_{r,n}}
. It is straightforward to check that the root sequence of
P_{r,n}
is monotone and consists only of
0
and
-1
. Hence
P_{r,n}
is univariate Gårding. Therefore,
U_{r,n}
is
S
-
Gårding
. By duality,
U_{r,n}
is also
C
-
Gårding
.

We now consider a simple modification of the uniform matroid. Fix a basis

B_{0}=\{1,2,...,r\}
. Define the deleted-basis matroid

\widehat{U}_{r,n}:=U_{r,n}\setminus B_{0}.

Equivalently,
\widehat{U}_{r,n}
is the rank-
r
matroid on
n

elements with exactly one circuit-hyperplane of size
r
. The class
of matroids
\widehat{U}_{r,n}
is closed under duality; in fact,

(\widehat{U}_{r,n})^{*}\cong\widehat{U}_{n-r,n}.
This is a class
of simple sparse paving matroids, where any subset of
r
-element is
either a basis or a circuit-hyperplane.

For their corresponding SSGFs, we have the following

(13.1)

S_{\widehat{U}_{r,n}}(w)=S_{U_{r,n}}(w)-w_{B_{0}.}

Let
q=n-r
and let

g_{r,q}(x,y):=S_{U_{r,n}}(\underbrace{x,\cdots,x}_{r},\underbrace{y,\cdots,y}_{q}),\qquad f_{r,q}(x,y):=S_{\hat{U}_{r,n}}(\underbrace{x,\cdots,x}_{r},\underbrace{y,\cdots,y}_{q}).

Then

f_{r,q}(x,y)=g_{r,q}(x,y)-x^{r}.

We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 13.4
.

Notations as above. The SSGF of
\widehat{U}_{r,n},

and its two variable specialization
f_{r,q}(x,y)
are both Gårding
polynomials.

Theorem
13.4
establishes the Gårding property of an infinite
class of non-regular matroids. See Appendix A for its proof.

13.2.
Closure under minor operations

We relate the SSGF and CSGF of a matroid to those of their minors.

Given a fixed matroid
M
on
E
, for any minor
N
, we regard
the SSGF and CSGF of
N
as a polynomial in variables
\{w_{e}:e\in E(N)\}
.

Let
M=(E,\mathcal{I})
be a matroid and let
e\in E
. The
deletion

M\setminus e
is the matroid on ground set
E\setminus\{e\}
whose
independent sets are those independent sets of
M
that does not contain

e
. The
contraction

M/e
is the matroid on ground set
E\setminus\{e\}

whose independent sets are those sets
I\subseteq E\setminus\{e\}

such that
I\cup\{e\}
is independent in
M
. An element
e
is
a
loop
if it is contained in no independent set, and a
coloop

if it is contained in every basis of
M
.

Lemma 13.5

(Deletion–contraction formulae)
.

For any matroid
M
and
e\in E
, we have

(13.2)

\displaystyle S(\mathbf{w})=\begin{cases}w_{e}S_{M/e}(\mathbf{w}),&e\text{ is a coloop},\\
(w_{e}+1)S_{M\backslash e}(\mathbf{w}),&e\text{ is a loop},\\
w_{e}S_{M/e}(\mathbf{w})+S_{M\backslash{}e}(\mathbf{w}),&\mathrm{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(13.3)

C_{M}(\mathbf{w})=\begin{cases}w_{e}C_{M\backslash e}(\mathbf{w}),&e\text{ is a loop},\\
(w_{e}+1)C_{M\backslash e}(\mathbf{w}),&e\text{ is a coloop},\\
w_{e}C_{M\backslash e}(\mathbf{w})+C_{M/e}(\mathbf{w}),&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
S_{M}
, partition the spanning sets
T\subseteq E
according
to whether
e\in T
. If
e\notin T
, then
T
is a spanning set
of
M\setminus e
, contributing a term in
S_{M\setminus e}(\mathbf{w})
.
If
e\in T
and
e
is neither a loop nor a coloop, then
T\setminus\{e\}

is a spanning set of
M/e
, contributing
w_{e}\,S_{M/e}(\mathbf{w})
.
The loop and coloop cases follow directly from the definitions.

The proof for
C_{M}
is similar.
∎

Let
M
be a
C
-
Gårding
matroid. Let
f(\mathbf{w})=C_{M}(\mathbf{w}).
Then
since
f
has non-negative coefficients,
\partial_{w_{e}}f
and
f|_{w_{e}=0}

are both
Gårding
polynomials. Moreover, if
e
is not a loop,

\partial_{w_{e}}f\prec f|_{w_{e}=0}.

If
e
is a loop, then
M\backslash{}e=M/e
. Thus, by the deletion–contraction
formula in Lemma
13.5
, both
M\backslash{}e
and
M/e

are
C
-
Gårding
matroids.

Proposition 13.6
.

The class of S/C-
Gårding
matroids is closed
when taking minors. Moreover, if
M
is a
C
-
Gårding
matroid,
and
e
is not a loop, then

C_{M\backslash e}\prec C_{M/e}.

Suppose that
e\in E
is not a coloop of
M
. If
S\subset E-e

is a spanning set in
M\backslash{}e
then
\text{rk}_{M}(S)=\text{rk}(M)
.
We have

\text{rk}_{M/e}(S)=\text{rk}(M)-1=\text{rk}(M/e).

Hence,
S
is also a spanning set in
M/e
. Similar statements hold
for the co-spanning set by taking the matroid dual operation. Therefore, we have

Lemma 13.7
.

For a matroid
M,

\left(S_{M/e}-S_{M\backslash{}e}\right)|_{\mathbf{w}\geq 0}\geq 0,\ \left(C_{M\backslash{}e}-C_{M/e}\right)|_{\mathbf{w}\geq 0}\geq 0.

We define

\xi_{e}(M;\mathbf{w})=C_{M\backslash e}(\mathbf{w})-C_{M/e}(\mathbf{w}),

and obtain the following characterization of the
C
-
Gårding
matroids.

Proposition 13.8
.

With the above notation, the following are equivalent:

(1)

M
is a
C
-
Gårding
matroid.

(2)

There exists
e\in E
such that
M\backslash e
is a
C
-
Gårding
matroid and
\xi_{e}(M;\mathbf{w})\vartriangleleft C_{M\backslash e}(\mathbf{w})
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove for simple matroids. Denote
f(\mathbf{w})=C_{M\backslash e}(\mathbf{w})

and
\xi(\mathbf{w})=\xi_{e}(M;\mathbf{w}).

If
M
is a
C
-
Gårding
matroid,

C_{M}(\mathbf{w})=f(\mathbf{w})(w_{e}+1)-\xi(\mathbf{w})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{w}].

Then
\xi\vartriangleleft f
by Theorem
6.4
. Since
f\prec g
,
we have proved (1).

If
\xi\vartriangleleft f
, then
f(\mathbf{w})-\xi(\mathbf{w})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{w}]
and

f(\mathbf{w})-\xi(\mathbf{w})\leq 0,\ \mathbf{w}\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{f}.

Therefore,
f(\mathbf{w})\prec f(\mathbf{w})-\xi(\mathbf{w})
. Hence

C_{M}(\mathbf{w})=f(\mathbf{w})w_{e}+g(\mathbf{w})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}[\mathbf{w}].

We have proved (2).
∎

13.3.
Series-Parallel connections and 2-sums

Consider 2 matroids
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
over ground sets
E_{1}

and
E_{2}
respectively, and
E_{1}\cap E_{2}=\{e\}
. Let
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
.
Denote

(1)

S(M_{1},M_{2})
the
series connection
of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
,

(2)

P(M_{1},M_{2})

the
parallel connection
of
M_{1}

and
M_{2}
,

(3)

M_{1}\oplus_{2}M_{2}=S(M_{1},M_{2})/e
the
2
-sum of
M_{1}

and
M_{2}
.

We refer to
[
52
, Chapter 7]
for precise definitions
of series-parallel connections and
2
-sums.

Lemma 13.9
.

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be 2 matroids over ground sets
E_{1}

and
E_{2}
respectively.

(1)

Suppose that
E_{1}\cap E_{2}=\emptyset
. Let
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
.
Then

(13.4)

C_{M_{1}\oplus M_{2}}(\mathbf{w})=C_{M_{1}}(\mathbf{w})C_{M_{2}}(\mathbf{w}).

(2)

Suppose
\{e\}=E_{1}\cap E_{2}
. Denote
f_{e}=C_{M_{1}\backslash e}
,

f^{e}=C_{M_{1}/e}
,
g_{e}=C_{M_{2}\backslash e}
, and

g^{e}=C_{M_{2}/e}
. Then

(13.5)

\displaystyle C(P(M_{1},M_{2});\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}w_{e}f_{e}g^{e},&e\ \mathrm{is\ a\ loop\ of\ }M_{1},\\
w_{e}\left(f_{e}g^{e}+f^{e}g_{e}-f^{e}g^{e}\right)+f^{e}g^{e},&\mathrm{else.}\end{cases}

(13.6)

\displaystyle C(S(M_{1},M_{2});\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=w_{e}f_{e}g_{e}+f_{e}g^{e}+f^{e}g_{e}-f^{e}g^{e}.

Proof.

For the first statement, if
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
, then any independent
set of
M
is a disjoint union of independent subsets of
M_{1}

and
M_{2}
respectively. (
13.4
) follows immediately.

For the second statement, if
e
is a loop of
M_{1}
, then the
result reduces to the first statement. Thus we may assume that
e

is not a loop of either
M_{1}
or
M_{2}
. There are 3 families
of independent subsets in
P(M_{1},M_{2})
.

(1)

\mathcal{I}^{e}=\{I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup e:I_{1}\in\mathcal{I}(M_{1}/e),I_{2}\in\mathcal{I}(M_{2}/e)\}
.

(2)

\mathcal{I}_{e}^{\prime}=\{I_{1}\cup I_{2}:I_{1}\in\mathcal{I}(M_{1}/e),I_{2}\in\mathcal{I}(M_{2}\backslash{}e)\}.

(3)

\mathcal{I}_{e}^{\prime\prime}=\{I_{1}\cup I_{2}:I_{1}\in\mathcal{I}(M_{1}\backslash{}e),I_{2}\in\mathcal{I}(M_{2}/e)\}.

Also notice that

\mathcal{I}_{e}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{I}_{e}^{\prime\prime}=\{I_{1}\cup I_{2}:I_{1}\in\mathcal{I}(M_{1}/e),I_{2}\in\mathcal{I}(M_{2}/e)\}.

Therefore, by inclusion-exclusion principle, we have

\displaystyle C_{M}(\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{e}^{\prime}}w_{E\backslash{I}}+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{e}^{\prime\prime}}w_{E\backslash{I}}-\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{e}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{I}_{e}^{\prime\prime}}w_{E\backslash{I}}+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}^{e}}w_{E\backslash{I}}

\displaystyle=f_{e}g^{e}w_{e}+f^{e}g_{e}w_{e}-f^{e}g^{e}w_{e}+f^{e}g^{e}

The proof of (
13.6
) is similar.
∎

Lemma 13.10
.

Suppose that
f,g\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}
and
p\vartriangleleft f,q\vartriangleleft g
.
Then

f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{y})\prec f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{y})-p(\mathbf{x})q(\mathbf{y})\prec(f(\mathbf{x})-p(\mathbf{x}))\cdot(g(\mathbf{y})-q(\mathbf{y})).

Proof.

Both relations follow directly from Theorem
6.14
,

(B)
\Rightarrow
(A)
.
∎

We next show that series-parallel connections and
2
-sums preserve

S/C
-
Gårding
properties.

Proposition 13.11
.

The classes of S-
Gårding
and C-
Gårding
matroid
are preserved under series-parallel connections and 2-sums.

An direct consequence of Theorem
13.11
shows that
M

is
S
- or
C
-
Gårding
if and only if the simplification matroid

\text{si}(M)
is
S
- or
C
-
Gårding
respectively.

Proof.

By duality, it suffices to treat the
C
-
Gårding
case. We proceed
in three steps, treating the parallel connection, the series connection,
and the
2
-sum using lemmas above.

Let
p=\xi_{e}(M_{1})
,
q=\xi_{e}(M_{2}),f=f^{e},g=g^{e}
. By (
13.5
),

C_{P(M_{1},M_{2})}(\mathbf{w})=w_{e}\left(f_{e}g_{e}-\xi_{e}(M_{1})\xi_{e}(M_{2})\right)+f^{e}g^{e}.

We apply Lemma
13.10
to conclude that the parallel connection
preserves
C
-
Gårding
matroids.

By (

14.
Rayleigh properties

We record some consequences of our main theorems in terms of Rayleigh
properties.

14.1.
Relations among generating functions

We introduce some algebraic relations among the generating functions.

Definition 14.1

(Combinatorial Inversion)
.

For a multivariate polynomial
f(w)=f((w_{e})_{e\in E})
, we define
its
combinatorial inversion
by

\Inv(f)(\mathbf{w}):=\Bigl(\prod_{e\in E}w_{e}\Bigr)\,f\!\left(\left(\frac{1}{w_{e}}\right)_{e\in E}\right).

Remark 14.2
.

The inversion operator
\Inv
is
purely combinatorial and should not be confused with the inversion
operator
T_{\tau}
introduced earlier in the paper. The two operations
differ by explicit sign factors:
T_{\tau}
involves a signed transformation
arising from complex-analytic considerations, whereas
\Inv
is sign-free
and is induced simply by complement of subsets. In particular,

\Inv
preserves the Rayleigh property for multi-affine polynomials
with nonnegative coefficients.

The four generating functions are related as follows. The basis generating
function is the top-degree homogeneous component of the independent-set
generating function. It is also the bottom-degree homogenous component
of the spanning-set generating function:

(14.1)

B_{M}\;=\;(I_{M})^{\mathrm{top}}=(S_{M})^{\mathrm{bot}}.

Moreover,

(14.2)

C_{M}=\Inv(I_{M}),\quad B_{M}\;=\;\Inv((\Inv(S_{M}))^{\mathrm{top}}).

If
M^{*}
is the dual matroid of
M
, then the spanning-set and
cospanning-set generating functions are interchanged under duality:

S_{M^{*}}\;=\;C_{M}\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{M^{*}}\;=\;S_{M}.

Remark 14.3
.

In the graphic case, the CSGF admits a concrete interpretation. Let

G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
M(G)
be its cycle matroid. The
unrooted

spanning-forest generating function
(SFGF) of
G
is

F_{G}(\mathbf{w}):=\sum_{F\subseteq E,\;F\text{ a spanning forest}}\prod_{e\in F}w_{e}.

It is easy to see that the cospanning-set generating function
C_{M(G)}

is the combinatorial inversion of
F_{G}
, namely

C_{M(G)}(\mathbf{w})=\Inv(F_{G})(\mathbf{w}).

For a connected graph
G=(V,E)
, the spanning–set generating function
can be recovered from the multivariate
q
-Potts model partition
function
Z_{G}(q,\mathbf{w})

[
60
]
by

S_{M(G)}(\mathbf{w})=\lim_{q\to 0}\,\frac{1}{q}\,Z_{G}(q,\mathbf{w}).

Remark 14.4
.

By Proposition
5.22
, truncation to the top
or bottom homogeneous component preserves the multi-affine
Gårding

property for polynomials with nonnegative coefficients. Also, it is
well known that both truncation and inversion preserve the Rayleigh
property.

14.2.
Rayleigh consequences

We record several basic facts about the
Gårding
and Rayleigh properties
of the spanning-set and cospanning-set generating functions.

Lemma 14.5
.

Let
M
be a matroid with generating

as above. Then

(1)

If

(2)

For all matroids covered in Theorem

15.
Ultra log-concave sequences and
Gårding
-Rayleigh measures

Log-concavity is one of the most persistent regularity phenomena in
combinatorics and probability, appearing in settings far beyond those
governed by stability and determinantal structure. Classically, the logarithmic
concavity of the coefficient sequences can be derived through Newton’s inequalities
and hence ultimately from real-rootedness. More recently, Hodge-theoretic
methods and Lorentzian polynomials have achieved great success in the
study of log-concave sequences in combinatorics
[
1
,
45
,
15
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

In this section, we show that the Gårding framework provides a further
geometric extension of log-concavity. In particular,
univariate specializations of Gårding polynomials along positive affine
directions yield ultra log-concave sequences, thereby identifying ultra
log-concavity as a consequence of the Gårding property rather than stability.

We first recall the relevant notions

Definition 15.1
.

Let
\{a_{k}\}
be a finite or infinite sequence of nonnegative real
numbers.
\{a_{k}\}
is said to
have no internal zeros
if
for any
r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{3}
with
a_{r_{1}},a_{r_{3}}
positive,
then
a_{r_{2}}>0
.
\{a_{k}\}
is called
log-concave
if

\{a_{k}\}
has no internal zeros and satisfies

a_{k}^{2}\geq a_{k+1}a_{k-1}.

A finite sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{d}
is called
ultra log-concave
(ULC)
if
\{a_{k}/\binom{d}{k}\}_{k=0}^{d}
is log-concave.

Suppose that
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{d}
is a sequence of nonnegative real
numbers and consider

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{k}x^{k}\in\mathbb{R}[x].

If
f(x)
is real–rooted, then the classical Newton inequalities
imply that
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{d}
is ultra log–concave, which immediately
implies log–concavity. However, ultra log-concavity alone does not
imply real-rootedness. On the other hand, define the polarization

(15.1)

p(\mathbf{x})=\Pi^{\uparrow}f:=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{k}\frac{\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{x})}{\binom{d}{k}}.

Pemantle
[
54
, Theorem 2.7]
observed that
p(\mathbf{x})

in the form of (
15.1
) is Rayleigh if and only
if
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{d}
forms a ULC sequence.

If
f(x)\in\mathrm{G}_{+}[x]
, then by Theorem
8.5
,

p(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathrm{G}}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{+}[\mathbf{x}]
. See also
[
50

. Hence,
by Lemma
5.21
,
p(\mathbf{x})
is Rayleigh.
Pemantle’s result indicates that
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{d}
is ULC.
Since strictly positive affine transforms preserve the Gårding property,
we have the following theorem.

Theorem 15.2

Suppose that
g(\mathbf{x})\in\mathrm{G}_{n}

\textbf{a}\in\Gamma_{n}^{+},\mathbf{b}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{g}}
. Then
g(\mathbf{a}t+\mathbf{b})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{k}t^{k}
has a ULC coefficient sequence. In particular,
if
\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{k}t^{k}\in\mathrm{G}_{+}[t]
, then
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{d}

is ULC.

The converse of Theorem
15.2
is not true. For instance,
let

The coefficient sequence of

root sequence.

For a matroid

Let

Given an

The multivariate characteristic polynomial of

Any

∎
