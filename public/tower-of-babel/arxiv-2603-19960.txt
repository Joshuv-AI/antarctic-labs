Real radiative decays of heavy pseudoscalar mesons
Justus Tobias Tsang

Abstract
We report our ongoing lattice QCD study of radiative leptonic decays of the charged pseudoscalar mesons D D , D s D_{s} , B B , and B c → ℓ ​ ν ℓ ​ γ B_{c}\to\ell\nu_{\ell}\gamma . We carry out our analysis on a single JLQCD ensemble with lattice spacing a = 0.044 ​ fm a=0.044~\text{fm} . This work is a step towards a complete QCD+QED lattice calculation of these modes, aimed at reducing theoretical uncertainties in the extraction of | V c ​ d | |V_{cd}| and | V c ​ s | |V_{cs}| and providing first-principles estimates of the corresponding form factors in the B B sector.

We study the radiative leptonic decays
P\to\ell\nu\gamma
with an on-shell photon for the pseudoscalar mesons
P=D^{\pm}_{(s)},B^{\pm}_{(c)}
.
The contributions to leading order in
\alpha_{\text{em}}
appear in
fig.

1
; they are composed by two quark-connected pieces where the photon is emitted by either valence quark; a quark-disconnected contribution that we neglect at the moment but will include in the near future, and the final-state radiation.
These processes with very energetic photons give access to the meson’s internal structure, which is encoded in the time-ordered hadronic tensor

H_{V-A}^{\mu\nu}(k,p)=\int\text{d}^{4}x~e^{ik\cdot x}\matrixelement{0}{\text{T}\{J_{\text{em}}^\mu(x) J_{V-A}^\nu(0)\}}{P(p)},

(1)

where
p
and
k
are the meson’s and photon’s 4-momenta, respectively, and we define the electromagnetic and weak currents as

J_{\text{em}}^{\mu}(x)=\bar{q}(x)\gamma^{\mu}q(x),\qquad J_{V-A}^{\nu}(y)=\bar{q}_{B}(y)(\gamma^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{5})q_{A}(y),

(2)

with
q=q_{A}
or
q=q_{B}
. By imposing charge conservation,
k_{\mu}H^{\mu\nu}=f_{P}p^{\nu}
, the hadronic tensor
H^{\mu\nu}
can be decomposed into three terms,
H_{V-A}^{\mu\nu}=H_{V}^{\mu\nu}+H_{\text{pt}}^{\mu\nu}+H_{A}^{\mu\nu}
. Following the conventions in refs.
[
4
,
8
,
2
,
10
]
, these can be Lorentz decomposed as

\begin{split}H_{V}^{\mu\nu}(p,k)=&-i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k_{\rho}p_{\sigma}\frac{F_{V}(x_{\gamma})}{m_{P}},\\
H_{\text{pt}}^{\mu\nu}(p,k)=&f_{P}\left(g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{(2p-k)^{\mu}(p-k)^{\nu}}{2p\cdot k-k^{2}}\right),\\
H_{A}^{\mu\nu}(p,k)=&\frac{F_{A}(x_{\gamma})}{m_{P}}\left[g^{\mu\nu}(p\cdot k-k^{2})-(p-k)^{\mu}k^{\nu}\right]\\
&+\frac{H_{1}(x_{\gamma})}{m_{P}}(g^{\mu\nu}k^{2}-k^{\mu}k^{\nu})\\
&+\frac{H_{2}(x_{\gamma})}{m_{P}}\frac{k^{\mu}(p\cdot k-k^{2})-k^{2}(p-k)^{\mu}}{(p-k)^{2}-m_{P}^{2}}(p-k)^{\nu}.\end{split}

(3)

The form factors
F_{V}
,
F_{A}
,
H_{1}
, and
H_{2}
depend on the variable
x_{\gamma}=2p\cdot k/m_{P}^{2}
, while
f_{P}
is the decay constant and
m_{P}
the meson mass. For a physical decay,
0<x_{\gamma}<1-m_{\ell}^{2}/m_{P}^{2}
with
m_{\ell}
the charged lepton mass.
To compute the decay rate, we need the contraction
\epsilon^{r}_{\mu}H^{\mu\nu}
of the hadronic tensor with a set of polarization vectors
\{\epsilon^{1}_{\mu},\epsilon^{2}_{\mu}\}
; for an on-shell photon the relations

k^{2}=0,\qquad\epsilon^{r}\cdot k=0

(4)

simplify the Lorentz decomposition substantially. In particular, the contributions from
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
vanish, leaving us with
F_{V}
and
F_{A}
for the real radiative decay,

\epsilon_{\mu}^{r}H_{V-A}^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon_{\mu}^{r}\left(f_{P}\left[g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{p^{\mu}p^{\nu}-p^{\mu}k^{\nu}}{p\cdot k}\right]\right.\\
\left.+\left[g^{\mu\nu}p\cdot k-p^{\mu}k^{\nu}\right]\frac{F_{A}(x_{\gamma})}{m_{P}}-i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}k_{\rho}p_{\sigma}\frac{F_{V}(x_{\gamma})}{m_{P}}\right).

(5)

In the literature, the current knowledge of these processes differs significantly between states. The RM123 collaboration were the first to provide
F_{V}
and
F_{A}
for
D
and
D_{s}
from the lattice
[
10
,
12
]
in the electroquenched approximation, i.e., neglecting the diagram in
fig.

1(b)
. Going to the heavier systems, HQET is the main theoretical framework used for
B
decays, but even the most important coefficients parameterizing the hadron structure are poorly known
[
3
,
2
]
.
Regarding experiment, there are only upper bounds for some radiative leptonic decays of
D_{(s)}
and
B
, while
B_{c}
remains unexplored
[
18
]
, although the LHCb collaboration will study the latter for the first time to disentangle its signal from
B\to\mu\nu_{\mu}\gamma

[
5
]
.

Ultimately, real radiative decays can also be used to improve our understanding of other channels. For example, experimental cross sections of the purely leptonic decays
D_{(s)}\to\ell\nu_{\ell}
are used, together with the meson decay constants
f_{P}
obtained from the lattice, to extract the 2nd row of the CKM matrix at
\order{1\%}
uncertainty
[
18
]
. However, experiments can only provide the radiation inclusive process
P\to\ell\nu(\gamma)
, with both purely leptonic decays and processes with photons that are too soft to trigger the detector. Thus, one should consider the integration of all photon energies below that detector threshold. More generally, the most important isospin breaking effects need to be added to scrutinize just how reliable the current uncertainties really are.

and
F_{A}
from lattice simulations (in
section

Methodology

The form factors
F_{V}
and
F_{A}
for on-shell radiative decays can be extracted from the Euclidean correlation function
[
10
]

C^{\mu\nu}_{3,V-A}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{k},t_{\gamma},t_{W})=\int\text{d}^{3}x~e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\expectationvalue{J_\text{em}^\mu(\pmb{x},t_\gamma+t_W)J_{V-A}^\nu(\pmb{0},t_W) P^\dagger(\pmb{p},0)}

(6)

where
t_{W}
is the source-sink separation, the electromagnetic current is located at
t_{\gamma}+t_{W}
, and
P^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{p},0)
creates a pseudoscalar meson with momentum
\boldsymbol{p}
. To relate the correlator to the hadronic tensor
H^{\mu\nu}_{V-A}
, one needs to project to a particular photon energy
E_{\gamma}
, and form appropriate ratios comparing the spectral decomposition in Euclidean and Minkowski space
[
16
,
15
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
11
]
,

H^{\mu\nu}_{V-A}(\boldsymbol{p},\pm{k})=\lim_{t_{W}\to\infty}\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{e^{m_{P}t_{W}}2m_{P}}{\matrixelement{P(\pmb{p})}{P^\dagger(0)}{0}}\int\text{d}t_{\gamma}e^{E_{\gamma}t_{\gamma}}C_{3,V-A}^{\mu\nu}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{k},t_{\gamma},t_{W}).

(7)

In the integral,
t_{\gamma}\in[-t_{W},t_{\text{max}}]
with
t_{\text{max}}\ll T-t_{W}
to avoid unphysical time orderings
[
11
]
.
Note that the hadronic tensor as extracted from the lattice depends on the source-sink separation
t_{W}
and the time extent
T
, which need to be taken to infinity to extract the physical result. Also, different
(\mu,\nu)
components and momentum configurations may exhibit different lattice artifacts. In order to study these effects systematically, we compute all non-zero components of the
C_{3}^{\mu\nu}
correlator for every value of
t_{W}
as explained in
section

3
.

The gauge configurations are distributed according to a tree-level improved Symanzik action and saved to disk every 100 MDU.
The links are stout-smeared

To study the

(m_{P_{cc}}+3m_{V_{cc}})/4
where
P_{hh}

However, we cannot simulate directly at the physical

[

For the three-point functions
C_{3}^{\mu\nu}
, we work in the center-of-mass frame
\boldsymbol{p}=\boldsymbol{0}
and set the photon momentum with a combination of Fourier modes and twist angles,

\boldsymbol{k}=2\pi/L(\boldsymbol{n}-\boldsymbol{\theta_{t}})
. We keep
\absolutevalue{\pmb{n}}\leq 3
where
\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathds{Z}^{3}
, and we either employ
\boldsymbol{\theta_{t}}=\boldsymbol{0}
or
\boldsymbol{\theta_{t}}=(0,0,\pm\theta)
with
\theta=0.2762
. As seen in
fig.

3
, Fourier modes by themselves give us the form factors at several values of
x_{\gamma}
, but we miss data points for
x_{\gamma}\sim 0.1-0.2
. This is due to the meson mass and the choice of reference frame. We add one twist angle to remedy this gap in our dataset and increase the density of points at larger values of
x_{\gamma}
.

We use two types of sources, stochastic
Z_{2}\otimes Z_{2}
time-wall for the meson interpolator and point sources for the weak current. The meson interpolator is Jacobi smeared,

q(x)\rightarrow\left(1+\frac{w^{2}}{4N}\Delta\right)^{N}q(x)

(8)

with parameters
w=9
and
N=200
. We form the diagram in
fig.

2
in three stages,

1.

Compute separately the point-to-all quark propagators for every flavour (
ud
,
s
,
h_{0}
, …,
h_{5}
) and store them on disk. The point-source correlators can have a zero or non-zero twist angle. We locate the meson interpolator on every time-slice and the point sink on every 4th time-slice.

2.

Contract the propagators for every source-sink separation
t_{W}
, non-zero
(\mu,\nu)
combination, and value of the twist
\boldsymbol{\theta_{t}}
.
Project to all Fourier modes within a sphere of radius
\absolutevalue{\pmb{n}}\leq 3
, providing a total of 369 different photon momenta when taking the various twists into account.

From the Lorentz decomposition, it can be seen that there are ten
(\mu,\nu)
combinations of the vector tensor
H^{\mu\nu}_{V}
that vanish and therefore we do not compute: the diagonal
H_{V}^{\mu\mu}
is always zero by antisymmetry, and
H_{V}^{\mu 0}
,
H_{V}^{0\nu}
vanish in the centre-of-mass frame for a similar reason.

5
Outlook

With an initial dataset at our disposal, we are ready to study the hadronic tensor
H^{\mu\nu}
. Our method to compute the correlator enables us to study all source-sink separations and non-zero
(\mu,\nu)
components. To treat excited state contamination, we will test the new Laplace filter technique defined in ref.
[
19
]
. In the future, we plan to add data for the quark-disconnected contribution in
fig.

1(b)
on the same ensemble. Furthermore, we plan to carry out the calculation in several other lattices at a variety of spacings and quark masses to reach the physical point.
