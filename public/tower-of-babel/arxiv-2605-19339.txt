Generalized Differentiability and Second-Order Necessary Optimality Conditions for an Elliptic Optimal Control Problem with Exponential Nonlinearity and Discrete Measures
Vu Huu Nhu a , Nguyen Hai Son b , Phan Quang Sang a , and Tran Duy a

Abstract
This paper deals with generalized differentiability and second-order necessary optimality conditions for a box-constrained optimal control problem governed by an exponential semilinear elliptic equation with discrete measures as sources, where the control belongs to the space of absolutely summable sequences. The presence of the exponential nonlinearity and discrete measures makes the analysis particularly challenging. In particular, the control-to-state operator may fail to be directionally differentiable. To address this issue, we first establish finite-dimensional directional differentiability of the control-to-state operator; that is, the operator is directionally differentiable along directions contained in finite-dimensional subspaces of the control space. We then introduce a notion of generalized derivative defined as the limit of the associated finite-dimensional directional derivatives as the dimension of these subspaces tends to infinity. Based on this concept, together with estimates for first- and second-order Taylor-type expansions of the exponential Nemytskii operator associated with the control-to-state mapping, we derive first- and second-order generalized differentiability of the reduced objective functional. This leads to first- and second-order necessary optimality conditions for the optimal control problem.

Optimal control; exponential nonlinearity; discrete measure; generalized differentiability; optimality condition.

Introduction

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\min\limits_{\bm{u}=(u_{i})\in\ell^{1}}J(\bm{u}):=\frac{1}{2}\left\|y_{\bm{u}}-y_{d}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}^{2}\\

y&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma\end{aligned}\right.

Partial differential equations (PDEs) with exponential nonlinearities and singular data, particularly those modeled by Dirac delta distributions, arise at the intersection of analysis, geometry, and mathematical physics.
A prominent example is the Liouville-type equation, which appears naturally in problems involving the prescription of Gaussian curvature, conformal metrics, and mean field models; see, for instance,
[

PDE-constrained optimal control problems involving measures have attracted considerable attention from the mathematical community; see, e.g.,

For problems governed by linear PDEs with convex objective functionals, we refer to

Interestingly, although the controls belong to spaces of measures, Casas et al.
[

,
9

The work

Regarding problems subject to nonlinear elliptic PDEs,

A key assumption in these works is that the nonlinearity in the state equation satisfies a polynomial growth condition.

In that setting, the state equation is generally ill-posed in the sense that it may admit no solution, due to the interplay between the exponential nonlinearity and the singular measure data.

being the unique solution in
W^{1,s_{*}}_{0}(\Omega)

for some
s_{*}:=s_{*}(r)>2
to

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\varphi_{\bm{u}}+e^{S(\bm{u})}\varphi_{\bm{u}}&=S(\bm{u})-y_{d}&&\text{in }\Omega\\
\varphi_{\bm{u}}&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.31)

Moreover, there holds that

\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\left[J(\bm{u}+\rho\bm{h})-J(\bm{u})-\rho J^{\prime}(\bm{u})\bm{h}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}J^{\prime\prime}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{2}\right]\to 0\quad\text{as }\rho\to 0^{+},

(4.32)

where
J^{\prime\prime}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{2}
is defined by

J^{\prime\prime}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{2}:=\int_{\Omega}\left[1-e^{S(\bm{u})}\varphi_{\bm{u}}\right]z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}^{2}dx+\nu\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}^{2}.

(4.33)

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of solutions in
W^{1,s_{*}}_{0}(\Omega)

for some
s_{*}:=s_{*}(r)>2
to (
4.31
) results from the proof of
[
23
, Thm. 5.1]
.
The directional differentiability of
J
as well as the first identity in (
4.30
) follows from
Theorem

4.1
and the chain rule. For the second identity in (
4.30
), testing the equation for
z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}
(see (
4.17
)) and (
4.31
) by
\varphi_{\bm{u}}
and
z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}
, respectively, yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}+e^{S(\bm{u})}\varphi_{\bm{u}}z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi_{\bm{u}}(x_{i})h_{i}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}+e^{S(\bm{u})}\varphi_{\bm{u}}z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}dx=\int_{\Omega}(S(\bm{u})-y_{d})z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}dx.

The last two equations show the second identity in (
4.30
).

It remains to verify (
4.32
). To achieve this, for any
\rho>0
, we set for simplicity

y:=S(\bm{u}),\quad y_{\rho}:=S(\bm{u}+\rho\bm{h}),\quad z_{\rho}:=\frac{1}{\rho}(S(\bm{u}+\rho\bm{u})-S(\bm{u})),\quad z:=z_{\bm{u};\bm{h}}=S^{\prime}(\bm{u})(\bm{h}).

Obviously, by subtracting the equations for
y_{\rho}
and
y
, and then dividing the obtained result via
\rho
, we conclude that
z_{\rho}
fulfills the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta z_{\rho}+e^{y}\frac{1}{\rho}(e^{\rho z_{\rho}}-1)&=\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}&&\text{in }\Omega\\
z_{\rho}&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right.

This, along with (
4.17
), gives

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta(z_{\rho}-z)+e^{y}\left[\frac{1}{\rho}(e^{\rho z_{\rho}}-1)-z\right]&=0&&\text{in }\Omega\\
(z_{\rho}-z)&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{aligned}\right.

or, equivalently,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta(z_{\rho}-z)+e^{y}(z_{\rho}-z)&=-e^{y}\frac{1}{\rho}(e^{\rho z_{\rho}}-1-\rho z_{\rho})&&\text{in }\Omega\\
(z_{\rho}-z)&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.34)

Testing now (
4.34
) and (
4.31
), respectively, by
\varphi_{\bm{u}}
and
(z_{\rho}-z)
, and thus subtracting the obtained identities, we have

\int_{\Omega}(y-y_{d})(z_{\rho}-z)dx=-\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}e^{y}\varphi_{\bm{u}}(e^{\rho z_{\rho}}-1-\rho z_{\rho})dx.

(4.35)

On the other hand, by setting

M_{\rho}:=J(\bm{u}+\rho\bm{h})-J(\bm{u})-\rho J^{\prime}(\bm{u})\bm{h}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}J^{\prime\prime}(\bm{u})\bm{h}^{2}

and using the identity

\left\|a\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\|b\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left\|a-b\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}(a-b)bdx\quad\text{for all }a,b\in L^{2}(\Omega)

together with the first identity in (
4.30
) and the definition in (
4.33
), we derive

M_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left\|y_{\rho}-y\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}(y_{\rho}-y)(y-y_{d})dx\right]\\
\begin{aligned} &\qquad\qquad-\rho\int_{\Omega}(y-y_{d})zdx-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left[1-e^{y}\varphi_{\bm{u}}\right]z^{2}dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\left[\left\|z_{\rho}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\|z\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]+\rho\int_{\Omega}(y-y_{d})(z_{\rho}-z)dx+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\int_{\Omega}e^{y}\varphi_{\bm{u}}z^{2}dx.\end{aligned}

Combining this with (
4.35

M_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\left[\left\|z_{\rho}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\|z\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]-\int_{\Omega}e^{y}\varphi_{\bm{u}}\left[e^{\rho z_{\rho}}-1-\rho z_{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}z^{2}\right]dx\\
\begin{aligned} &=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\left[\left\|z_{\rho}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\|z\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}e^{y}\varphi_{\bm{u}}\left[\left(e^{\rho z_{\rho}}-1-\rho z_{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}z_{\rho}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}\left(z_{\rho}^{2}-z^{2}\right)\right]dx.\end{aligned}

From this, (
4.18
), and (
4.20
), we obtain (
4.32
).
∎

Definition 4.2

\bm{u}=(u_{i})\in\mathcal{D}

5
Optimal control problem (
P
)

In this section, we derive the fist- and second-order optimality conditions for a local minimizer of (
P
).
We begin by establishing the existence of minimizers of (
P
).

5.1
Existence of minimizers

The set of admissible controls for (
P
) is defined as

U_{ad}:=\left\{\bm{u}\in\ell^{1}\mid\bm{\alpha}\leq\bm{u}\leq\bm{\beta}\right\}.

(5.1)

The nonemptiness, convexity, and compactness of
U_{ad}
are shown in the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

The feasible set
U_{ad}
is nonempty, convex, and compact in
\ell^{1}
.
Moreover, there exists a constant
\bar{r}:=\bar{r}(\bm{\beta})>1
such that

e^{S(\bm{u})}\in L^{\bar{r}}(\Omega)\quad\text{for all }\bm{u}\in U_{ad}.

(5.2)

Proof.

Clearly,
U_{ad}
is nonempty, closed, and convex.
To establish its compactness, we apply the Dunford–Pettis theorem in the context of the
\ell^{1}
space and use the Schur property of
\ell^{1}
; see, e.g.,
[
30
, Thms. 15.4, 15.5]
,
[
20
]
,
[
17
, Thm. 4]
.
Indeed, for any
\bm{u}\in U_{ad}
, there holds

|u_{i}|\leq\max\{|\alpha_{i}|,|\beta_{i}|\}\leq|\alpha_{i}|+|\beta_{i}|\quad\text{for all }i\geq 1.

(5.3)

Consequently, one has

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|u_{i}|\leq\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\ell^{1}}+\left\|\beta\right\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Since
\bm{u}
is arbitrary in
U_{ad}
,
U_{ad}
is pointwise bounded. Moreover, let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary, but fixed.
Since
\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}\in\ell^{1}
, there exists an integer
n_{0}>0
such that

\sum_{i=n_{0}}^{\infty}|\alpha_{i}|+|\beta_{i}|<\epsilon.

Combining this with (
5.3
) yields

\sup\left\{\sum_{i=n_{0}}^{\infty}|u_{i}|\middle|\bm{u}\in U_{ad}\right\}\leq\sum_{i=n_{0}}^{\infty}|\alpha_{i}|+|\beta_{i}|<\epsilon.

This shows the equi-integrability of
U_{ad}
. By the Dunford–Pettis theorem, it follows that
U_{ad}
is relatively compact in the weak topology. On the other hand, since
\ell^{1}
has Schur’s property–i.e., weak convergence implies norm convergence–
U_{ad}
is also relatively compact in norm. Combining this with the closedness of
U_{ad}
, we conclude that
U_{ad}
is compact.

It remains to prove (
5.2
). To this end, we apply
Lemma

4.2
to
\bm{u}:=\bm{\beta}
and use the assumption (
2.2
) to conclude that a constant
\bar{r}:=\bar{r}(\bm{\beta})>1
exists and satisfies
e^{S(\bm{\beta})}\in L^{\bar{r}}(\Omega)
. Combining this with (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we arrive at (
5.2
).
∎

The compactness of the feasible set
U_{ad}
, together with the continuity of the control-to-state operator
S
established in
Lemma

4.1
, ensures the existence of minimizers to (
P
), as shown in the next proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Problem (
P
) admits at least one minimizer
\bm{\bar{u}}\in U_{ad}
. Moreover, any minimizer
\bm{\bar{u}}
to (
P
) satisfies

e^{S(\bm{\bar{u}})}\in L^{\bar{r}}(\Omega),

(5.4)

where
\bar{r}>1
is defined as in
Lemma

5.1
.

Proof.

Since
\bm{\beta}\in U_{ad}
, there holds

0\leq\gamma:=\inf\left\{J(\bm{u})\middle|\bm{u}\in U_{ad}\right\}\leq J(\bm{\beta})=\frac{1}{2}\left\|y_{\bm\beta}-y_{d}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\nu\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_{i}^{2}

with
y_{\bm\beta}:=S(\bm{\beta})\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,2)
. There thus exists a sequence
\{\bm{u}_{n}\}\subset U_{ad}
such that

\gamma=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}J(\bm{u}_{n}).

(5.5)

Moreover, in view of
Lemma

5.1
,
U_{ad}
is compact in
\ell^{1}
. A subsequence, denoted in the same way, of
\{\bm{u}_{n}\}
exists and converges to an element
\bm{\bar{u}}\in U_{ad}
. From this and (
4.8
), along with the continuous embedding
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,2)
, we deduce that

J(\bm{u}_{n})\to J(\bm{\bar{u}})\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

which, in combination with (
5.5
), yields
\gamma=J(\bm{\bar{u}})
. Hence,
\bm{\bar{u}}
is a minimizer of (
P
).
Clearly, any minimizer of (
P
) satisfies (
5.4
) thanks to (
5.2
).
∎

5.2
First- and second-order necessary optimality conditions

To derive the first-order necessary optimality conditions for (
P
), we first study the
k
-dimensional directions,
k\geq 1
, in the tangent cone to the admissible set
U_{ad}
at a local minimizer
\bm{\bar{u}}
(whose existence is guaranteed by
Proposition

5.1
) and then pass to the limit as
k\to\infty
. Similarly, by considering
k
-dimensional
critical
directions and passing to the limit, we obtain the desired second-order necessary optimality conditions for (
P
).

We first present a criterion in terms of tangent directions to the admissible set at a point.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
\bm{h}=(h_{i})\in\ell^{1}
. Then,

\bm{h}\in\overline{\mathrm{cone}}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}})
if and only if there holds

h_{i}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\geq 0&&\text{if }\bar{u}_{i}=\alpha_{i},\\
&\leq 0&&\text{if }\bar{u}_{i}=\beta_{i}\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{for all }i\geq 1.

(5.6)

Moreover, if
\bm{h}\in\ell^{1}
fulfills (
5.6
), then
\bm{h}_{k}\in\mathrm{cone}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}})
.

Proof.

Obviously, any
\bm{h}
in
\overline{\mathrm{cone}}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}})
satisfies (
5.6
). Conversely, let
\bm{h}=(h_{i})\in\ell^{1}
satisfy (
5.6
).
To prove
\bm{h}\in\overline{\mathrm{cone}}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}})
, it suffices to show that
\bm{h}_{k}\in\mathrm{cone}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}})
for all
k\geq 1
, thanks to the fact that
\bm{h}_{k}\to\bm{h}
in
\ell^{1}
as
k\to\infty
. To this end, we first deduce from (
5.6
) and the assumption
\beta_{i}\geq\alpha_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
that there exists a constant
t_{k}>0
fulfilling

\alpha_{i}\leq\bar{u}_{i}+t_{k}h_{i}\leq\beta_{i}\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq k.

Choosing now
\bm{u}:=(u_{i})
by

u_{i}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bar{u}_{i}+t_{k}h_{i}&&\text{if }1\leq i\leq k,\\
&\bar{u}_{i}&&\text{if }i\geq k+1\end{aligned}\right.

yields
\bm{u}\in U_{ad}
and
\bm{u}=\bm{\bar{u}}+t_{k}\bm{h}_{k}
. This shows
\bm{h}_{k}\in\mathrm{cone}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}})
. Consequently, there holds
\bm{h}\in\overline{\mathrm{cone}}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}})
.
∎

Theorem 5.1

(First-order necessary optimality conditions)
.

Assume that
\bm{\bar{u}}
is a local minimizer to (
P
). Then, there exists an adjoint state
\bar{\varphi}\in W^{1,s_{*}}_{0}(\Omega)
for some
s_{*}>2
,
which, along with
{\bm{\bar{u}}}
and
\bar{y}:=S(\bar{\bm{u}})
, fulfills the system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\bar{y}+(e^{\bar{y}}-1)&=f_{0}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bar{u}_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\bar{y}&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.7a)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\bar{\varphi}+e^{\bar{y}}\bar{\varphi}&=\bar{y}-y_{d}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\bar{\varphi}&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.7b)

and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \bar{\varphi}(x_{i})+\nu\bar{u}_{i}&\geq 0&&\text{if }\bar{u}_{i}=\alpha_{i}<\beta_{i},\\
\bar{\varphi}(x_{i})+\nu\bar{u}_{i}&=0&&\text{if }\bar{u}_{i}\in(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}),\\
\bar{\varphi}(x_{i})+\nu\bar{u}_{i}&\leq 0&&\text{if }\bar{u}_{i}=\beta_{i}>\alpha_{i}\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{for }i\geq 1.

(5.7c)

Proof.

Obviously,
\bm{\bar{u}}
and
\bar{y}
fulfill (
5.7a
).
For any
\bm{u}\in U_{ad}
, we set
\bm{h}:=\bm{u}-\bm{\bar{u}}
and thus
\bm{h}\in U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}}
.
Fixing now
k
yields

\bm{u}_{\rho}:=\bm{\bar{u}}+\rho\bm{h}_{k}\in U_{ad}\quad\text{for all }0<\rho<\rho_{0}

for some constant
\rho_{0}
depending only on
k
. From this and the optimality of
\bm{\bar{u}}
, there holds

\frac{1}{\rho}\left[J(\bm{u}_{\rho})-J(\bm{\bar{u}})\right]\geq 0\quad\text{for all }0<\rho<\rho_{0}.

In view of the
k
-dimensional directional differentiability of

; see
Proposition

4.2
, there holds

J^{\prime}(\bm{\bar{u}})\bm{h}_{k}\geq 0,

or, equivalently,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bar{\varphi}(x_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})+\nu\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bar{u}_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\geq 0,

where
{\bar{\varphi}}
satisfies (

Proposition

4.2
.
By taking

, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}[\bar{\varphi}(x_{i})+\nu\bar{u}_{i}](u_{i}-\bar{u}_{i})\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\bm{u}=(u_{i})\in U_{ad}.

This, together with a straightforward argument, gives (
5.7c
).
∎

From now on, let
\bm{\bar{u}}
,
{\bar{y}}
, and
\bar{\varphi}
satisfy (
5.7
). We define
\bm{\bar{d}}=(\bar{d}_{i})\in\ell^{1}
by

\bar{d}_{i}:=\bar{\varphi}(x_{i})+\nu\bar{u}_{i},\quad i\geq 1.

(5.8)

In view of

DJ(\bm{\bar{u}})\bm{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bar{d}_{i}h_{i},\quad\text{for all }\bm{h}=(h_{i})\in\ell^{1}.

(5.9)

As a result of the combination of (

) in
Theorem

with (

), one has

DJ(\bm{\bar{u}})\bm{h}\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\bm{h}\in\overline{\text{cone}}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}}).

(5.10)

This result extends the classical theory of first-order optimality conditions in smooth optimization.

In order to establish the second-order necessary optimality conditions,
we now define the
critial
cone to (

\bm{\bar{u}}
in the usual way

C_{\bm{\bar{u}}}:=\left\{\bm{h}=(h_{i})\in\ell^{1}\middle|\bm{h}\,\text{satisfies \eqref{eq:tangent-direction-equivalence} and }\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bar{d}_{i}h_{i}=0\right\}.

(5.11)

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that (
5.7c
) holds. Let
\bm{h}\in\ell^{1}
satisfy (

\bm{h}\in C_{\bm{\bar{u}}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad h_{i}=0\,\text{if }\bar{d}_{i}\neq 0\,\text{for all }i\geq 1.

(5.12)

Proof.

The implication “

” is straightforward. Conversely, assume that
\bm{h}=(h_{i})\in C_{\bm{\bar{u}}}
.
By definition, there holds

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h_{i}\bar{d}_{i}=0.

Owing to (

), (

), and the definition of
\bm{\bar{d}}
in (

), we have
h_{i}\bar{d}_{i}\geq 0
for all

h_{i}=0\quad\text{if}\quad\bar{d}_{i}\neq 0\quad\text{for all }i\geq 1.

This proves the converse implication “

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that
\bm{\bar{u}}
is a local minimizer to (

). Then, there exists an adjoint state
\bar{\varphi}
in
W^{1,s_{*}}_{0}(\Omega)
for some
s_{*}>2
, satisfying (

) and the following second-order necessary optimality condition

D^{2}J(\bm{\bar{u}})[\bm{h},\bm{h}]\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{h}\in C_{\bm{\bar{u}}},

(5.13)

where

D^{2}J(\bm{\bar{u}})[\bm{h},\bm{h}]=\int_{\Omega}\left[1-e^{S(\bm{\bar{u}})}\bar{\varphi}\right](DS(\bm{\bar{u}})\bm{h})^{2}dx+\nu\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h_{i}^{2}.

Proof.

On account of the limit (
4.39
), it suffices to prove that

J^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\bar{u}})\bm{h}^{2}_{k}\geq 0

(5.14)

for all
k\geq 1
and
\bm{h}\in C_{\bm{\bar{u}}}
. To this end, Let
\bm{h}\in C_{\bm{\bar{u}}}
be arbitrary but fixed.
By (

) and

and

J^{\prime}(\bm{\bar{u}})\bm{h}_{k}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{h}_{k}\in\text{cone}(U_{ad}-\bm{\bar{u}})\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1.

(5.15)

From the identity in (

) and (

J^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\bar{u}})\bm{h}^{2}_{k}=2\lim\limits_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{J(\bm{\bar{u}}+\rho\bm{h}_{k})-J(\bm{\bar{u}})}{\rho^{2}}.

Combining this with the optimality of
\bm{\bar{u}}
and the second relation in (

).
∎

6
Conclusions

We have investigated the finite-dimensional directional differentiability of the control-to-state operator for the optimal control problem governed by exponential semilinear elliptic equations with discrete measures as sources.
We then introduced the notion of generalized differentiability, defined as the limit of the directional derivatives
of the control-to-state mapping along finite-dimensional truncated directions.
Building on this concept, we analyzed the first- and second-order generalized differentiability of the objective functional
and employed these results to derive first- and second-order necessary optimality conditions for local minimizers.
The derivation of second-order sufficient optimality conditions is deferred to future work, as it requires additional technical arguments.
Finally, a practically relevant direction for further research is the establishment of error estimates for approximations of the optimal control problem, as achieved in settings where the nonlinearity in the state equation satisfies the polynomial growth assumption
[
25
,
1
]
.

(A.1)

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{e^{at}-1-at-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}t^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}\right|\leq\left|\frac{e^{at_{0}}-1-at_{0}-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}t_{0}^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{0}}\right|

(A.2)

for all
0<t<t_{0}
.

Proof.

Setting the function
\psi(t):=\frac{e^{at}-1-at}{t}
,
t\in(0,t_{0})
,
yields

\psi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{(at-1)e^{at}+1}{t^{2}}=:\frac{g(t)}{t^{2}}.

Moreover, by a simple computation, we have
g^{\prime}(t)=a^{2}te^{at}\geq 0
for all
0<t<t_{0}
and
g(0)=0
. We thus obtain
\psi^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
for all
0<t<t_{0}
. Hence, the function
\psi

is nondecreasing and continuous on
(0,t_{0})
. From this, we obtain (
A.1
).
For the estimate (
A.2
), we first set

\phi(t):=\frac{e^{at}-1-at-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}t^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}},\quad 0<t<t_{0}.

Since
\phi
is continuous on
(0,t_{0})
and
\phi(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0^{+}
, it suffices to show that
\phi
is monotone on
(0,t_{0})
. To this end, by a straightforward computation, we get

\phi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{e^{at}(at-2)+2+at}{\frac{1}{2}t^{3}}=:\frac{h(t)}{\frac{1}{2}t^{3}}.

By a simple computation, one has

h^{\prime}(t)=ae^{at}(at-1)+a\quad\text{and}\quad h^{\prime\prime}(t)=a^{3}e^{at}t.

We now consider two cases:

Case I:
a\geq 0
.
In this case, there holds
h^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in(0,t_{0})
. Consequently, we have
h^{\prime}(t)\geq h^{\prime}(0)=0
for all
t\in(0,t_{0})
. Thus,
h
is nondecreasing on
(0,t_{0})
and
h(t)\geq h(0)=0
for all
t\in(0,t_{0})
. Therefore,
\phi
is nondecreasing on
(0,t_{0})
.

Case II:
a<0
.
Similarly,
\phi
is nonincreasing on
(0,t_{0})
.
∎

Appendix B
Exponential estimates for Poisson’s equation with regularized point sources

Lemma B.1
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
0<\rho_{0}<R
be such that
B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})\subset B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0,R)=:B_{R}
.
Assume that
y_{0}
is the unique solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta y_{0}&=\phi_{\epsilon}(\cdot-x_{0})&&\text{in }B_{R},\\
y_{0}&=0&&\text{on }\partial B_{R},\end{aligned}\right.

(B.1)

where
\phi_{\epsilon}
,
\epsilon\in(0,\rho_{0})
, is a mollifier satisfying

0\leq\phi_{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad\mathrm{supp}\phi_{\epsilon}\subset\overline{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0,\epsilon)},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{\epsilon}dx=1.

Then there hold for all
m\in(0,4\pi)
that

\exp[m{y}_{0}(x)]\leq\left(\frac{2R}{\rho_{0}-\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{m}{2\pi}}

(B.2)

for a.e.
x\in B_{R}\backslash\overline{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})}

and

\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})}\exp[m{y}_{0}(x)]dx\leq\frac{2\pi(\epsilon+\rho_{0})^{2}}{2-\frac{m}{2\pi}}\left(\frac{2R}{\epsilon+\rho_{0}}\right)^{\frac{m}{2\pi}}.

(B.3)

Proof.

Let
G
denote the Green function of the Laplace operator
-\Delta
in the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Green’s representation formula; see, e.g.
[
5
, Cor. 2.2]
,
[
16
, Thm. 12, Chap. 2]
, and
[
13
]
, yields

{y}_{0}(x)=\int_{B_{R}}G\left(\frac{x}{R},\frac{x^{\prime}}{R}\right)\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}=\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)}G\left(\frac{x}{R},\frac{x^{\prime}}{R}\right)\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}

for a.e.
x\in B_{R}
. By
[
23
, Lem. A.1]
, it follows that

0\leq{y}_{0}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)}\ln\left(\frac{2R}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\right)\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}

(B.4)

for a.e.
x\in B_{R}.

We now consider the following cases:

\bullet

For a.e.
x\in B_{R}\backslash\overline{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})}
, there holds
|x-x^{\prime}|\geq\rho_{0}-\epsilon
for all
x^{\prime}\in B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)
. We then have from (
B.4
) that

0\leq{y}_{0}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)}\ln\left(\frac{2R}{\rho_{0}-\epsilon}\right)\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{2R}{\rho_{0}-\epsilon}.

This gives (
B.2
).

\bullet

For a.e.
x\in\overline{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})}
, thanks to (
B.4
) and the fact that
\mathrm{supp}\phi_{\epsilon}\subset\overline{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0,\epsilon)}
, we obtain

\exp[m{y}_{0}(x)]\leq\exp\left[\frac{m}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)}\ln\left(\frac{2R}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\right)\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}\right].

Jensen’s inequality for integrals; see, .e.g.
[
19
, Thm. B.50]
, and the identity

\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)}\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}=1

then imply that

\exp[m{y}_{0}(x)]\leq\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)}\exp\left[\frac{m}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{2R}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\right)\right]\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}\\
\begin{aligned} &=\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)}\left(\frac{2R}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2\pi}}\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}.\end{aligned}

By integrating over
{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})}
, we then have

\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})}\exp[m{y}_{0}(x)]dx\leq\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})}dx\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\epsilon)}\left(\frac{2R}{|x-x^{\prime}|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2\pi}}\phi_{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-x_{0})dx^{\prime}.

From this and a method of integration by substitution, we deduce

\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x_{0},\rho_{0})}\exp[m{y}_{0}(x)]dx\leq\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0,\rho_{0}+\epsilon)}d\eta\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0,\epsilon)}\left(\frac{2R}{|\eta|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2\pi}}\phi_{\epsilon}(\psi)d\psi\\
\\
\begin{aligned} &=\int_{B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0,\rho_{0}+\epsilon)}\left(\frac{2R}{|\eta|}\right)^{\frac{m}{2\pi}}d\eta=2\pi(2R)^{\frac{m}{2\pi}}\times\frac{1}{2-{\frac{m}{2\pi}}}\left(\rho_{0}+\epsilon\right)^{2-{\frac{m}{2\pi}}}.\end{aligned}

This shows (
B.3
).
∎
