Abstract
This paper presents a state observer design for continuous linear time-invariant (LTI) systems subject to unknown bounded disturbances, that enforces a prescribed bound on the observer residual. The proposed observer augments a continuous-time Luenberger observer with state resets, triggered when the norm of the residual equals a pre-specified bound. The reset map guarantees contraction of the residual at jump instants while preserving the uniform boundedness properties of a standard Luenberger observer. The paper also establishes forward invariance of the residual envelope and non-expansiveness of the estimation error in a Lyapunov metric. Simulation results confirm the analysis. Under bounded disturbances, the residual stays within the prescribed bound. A standard Luenberger observer with the same gains violates this bound.



I
Introduction

State observers are essential in modern control and monitoring systems, enabling output-feedback control, fault detection
[
11
,
5
]
, and supervisory decision making
[
13
]
when full state measurements are unavailable. Classical continuous-time observers provide asymptotic convergence and robustness guarantees under suitable assumptions. However, these do not explicitly regulate transient mismatches between measured and estimated outputs, referred to as the observer residual. In many fault detection applications, the residual serves as the decision signal, and large transient deviations may trigger false alarms or mask faults depending on the threshold. In practice, thresholds are tuned conservatively and in an application-dependent manner. In observer-based control, large residuals may also degrade performance when state estimates are used for feedback. Formal guarantees on worst-case transient residual behavior are generally unavailable.

Novelty of the work:
This paper introduces an observer architecture for LTI systems subject to bounded disturbances that actively regulates the observer residual and enforces a prescribed bound on it. The design augments a continuous-time Luenberger observer with state resets triggered when the residual norm reaches a specified threshold. The reset map ensures contraction of the residual at each jump, which guarantees forward invariance of the prescribed residual envelope. The reset law admits a closed-form linear expression that can be computed offline. Despite the induced state jumps, we show that the estimation error remains non-expansive in a Lyapunov metric, and the uniform boundedness properties of the Luenberger observer are preserved. The closest related work
[
14
]
employs residual-based resets to improve transient estimation performance without resorting to high continuous gains. In that setting, however, the residual threshold functions solely as a triggering mechanism. In contrast, the present work treats the residual bound as an invariant performance specification and enforces it by design. High-gain observers
[
8
,
6
]
, which employ large observer gains to accelerate convergence, can also reduce transient residual deviations under disturbances. However, they are prone to peaking phenomena and increased sensitivity to measurement noise due to large magnitude of the gains. The proposed architecture offers an alternative mechanism for regulating transient residual behavior without relying on aggressive continuous gains. It is important to distinguish the objective of this work from robustness properties of well-known observers for LTI systems. Existing designs provide bounds on the estimation error or residual, but such bounds are not directly assignable by the designer. In contrast, this work
enforces a prescribed residual bound as a design specification
, i.e.,
\|r(t)\|\leq\delta
for all
t\geq t_{0}
, where
\delta>0
is chosen a priori. This constitutes a constraint enforcement problem rather than a robustness characterization problem.

Hybrid and reset mechanisms
[
3
,
2
]
in observer design are not new, but have been studied in contexts different from those considered here. For example, reset strategies have been used to improve transient response or reduce performance indices such as the
L_{2}
gain of the estimation error
[
4
]
. Resetting adaptive observers have been developed for joint state and parameter estimation in uncertain nonlinear systems
[
9
,
10
]
, and reset observers for linear time-varying delay systems have been analyzed to establish global asymptotic and finite-gain
L_{2}
stability
[
15
]
. Event-triggered observers have also been proposed to regulate communication load by transmitting measurements only when a triggering condition is satisfied
[
12
]
. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section II introduces the system model, problem formulation, and assumptions. Section III presents the proposed hybrid observer and its flow and jump dynamics. Section IV provides the theoretical analysis. Section V presents simulation results, followed by concluding remarks in Section VI.

III-A

System Dynamics

Consider the continuous-time LTI system:

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=Ax(t)+Bu(t)+d(t)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=Cx(t)

Assumption 1
.

The disturbance is bounded and satisfies

\|d(t)\|\leq\bar{d},\qquad\forall t\geq t_{0}

(2)

Assumption 2
.

The pair

is detectable.

Assumption 3
.

The estimation error satisfies
\|\tilde{x}(t_{0})\|\leq\bar{x}_{0}
for some
\bar{x}_{0}>0
.

Assumption 4
.

The matrix

has full row rank.

III-B

Continuous-Time Observer

A Luenberger observer for the states is given by:

\dot{\hat{x}}(t)=A\hat{x}(t)+Bu(t)+L\left[y(t)-C\hat{x}(t)\right]

(3)

where
L\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is the observer gain. Define the estimation error

\tilde{x}\triangleq x-\hat{x}
. Using (
1
) and (
3
), the estimation error dynamics is given by

\dot{\tilde{x}}(t)=A_{c}\,\tilde{x}(t)+d(t),\qquad A_{c}\triangleq A-LC

(4)

Since Assumption
2
holds, the gain
L
in (
4
) is chosen such that

is Hurwitz. Consequently, for any symmetric matrix
Q=Q^{\top}\succ 0
, there exists a unique symmetric matrix
P=P^{\top}\succ 0
satisfying the Lyapunov equation
[
1
]
:

A_{c}^{\top}P+PA_{c}=-Q.

(5)

Lemma 1

(ISS of the observer error dynamics)
.

Let
Q=Q^{\top}\succ 0
be given and let
P=P^{\top}\succ 0
satisfy (
5
).
Under Assumption
1
, the error dynamics (
4
) is input-to-state stable (ISS) with respect to
d(t)
in the sense that, for all
t\geq t_{0}

V(t)\leq e^{-\alpha(t-t_{0})}V(t_{0})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\,\bar{d}^{\,2}

(6)

where
V(t)\triangleq\tilde{x}(t)^{\top}P\tilde{x}(t)
and the constants

\alpha\triangleq\frac{\lambda_{\min}(Q)}{2\,\lambda_{\max}(P)},\qquad\beta\triangleq\frac{2\,\|P\|^{2}}{\lambda_{\min}(Q)}

are positive.
Consequently,

\|\tilde{x}(t)\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\max}(P)}{\lambda_{\min}(P)}}\,e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}(t-t_{0})}\|\tilde{x}(t_{0})\|+\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha\,\lambda_{\min}(P)}}\,\bar{d}

(7)

Proof.

The time derivative of
V
along solutions of (
4
) is:

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=\tilde{x}^{\top}(A_{c}^{\top}P+PA_{c})\tilde{x}+2\tilde{x}^{\top}Pd=-\tilde{x}^{\top}Q\tilde{x}+2\tilde{x}^{\top}Pd

Using Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities yields

\displaystyle\dot{V}\leq-\lambda_{\min}(Q)\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}+2\,\|\tilde{x}\|\,\|P\|\,\|d\|

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda_{\min}(Q)\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\min}(Q)}{2}\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}+\frac{2\|P\|^{2}}{\lambda_{\min}(Q)}\|d\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\lambda_{\min}(Q)}{2}\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}+\frac{2\|P\|^{2}}{\lambda_{\min}(Q)}\|d\|^{2}

Since
V=\tilde{x}^{\top}P\tilde{x}\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}
, it follows that
\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}\geq V/\lambda_{\max}(P)
, and using the bound on
d
from Assumption
1
in the above inequality, we get

\dot{V}\leq-\alpha V+\beta\bar{d}^{\,2}

(8)

Consider the scalar comparison system

\dot{z}(t)=-\alpha z(t)+\beta\bar{d}^{\,2},\qquad z(t_{0})=V(t_{0})

Its solution is given by

z(t)=e^{-\alpha(t-t_{0})}V(t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-\alpha(t-\tau)}\beta\bar{d}^{\,2}\,d\tau

Evaluating the integral gives

\displaystyle z(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha(t-t_{0})}V(t_{0})+(\beta/\alpha)\bar{d}^{\,2}\bigl(1-e^{-\alpha(t-t_{0})}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\alpha(t-t_{0})}V(t_{0})+(\beta/\alpha)\bar{d}^{\,2}

Since
\dot{V}\leq-\alpha V+\beta\bar{d}^{\,2}
and
z
solves
\dot{z}=-\alpha z+\beta\bar{d}^{\,2}
with
z(t_{0})=V(t_{0})
,
the comparison lemma
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
implies
V(t)\leq z(t)
for all
t\geq t_{0}
, which establishes (
6
). Finally, since
P=P^{\top}\succ 0
,
\lambda_{\min}(P)\|\tilde{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq V(t)
and
V(t_{0})\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)\|\tilde{x}(t_{0})\|^{2}
. Substituting these bounds in (
6
) and then using
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
for
a,b\geq 0
yields (
7
).
∎

\displaystyle\|\tilde{x}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\max}(P)}{\lambda_{\min}(P)}}\,\|\tilde{x}(t_{0})\|+\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha\,\lambda_{\min}(P)}}\,\bar{d}\leq M

(10)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\triangleq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\max}(P)}{\lambda_{\min}(P)}}\,\bar{x}_{0}\ +\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha\,\lambda_{\min}(P)}}\,\bar{d}

Using

III-C

Motivation for a Hybrid Observer

Lemma
1
establishes that the observer in (
3
) is ISS with respect to bounded disturbances
[
7
]
, ensuring bounded estimation error. However, this does not guarantee that the residual

r(t)\triangleq y(t)-C\hat{x}(t)

(12)

remains within a prescribed envelope. In particular, rapidly varying disturbances may induce large transient residual deviations, even when the observer is stable. In many applications, including monitoring, fault detection, safety supervision, and output-feedback control, such deviations are undesirable as they may trigger false alarms or degrade performance. This motivates the following problem formulation.

Problem statement:
Given any
\delta>0
, design an observer such that

\|r(t)\|\leq\delta,\quad\forall t\geq t_{0}

for all disturbances satisfying Assumption 1.

To this end, we use
r(t)
as a supervisory signal and introduce a hybrid observer with continuous flows and discrete resets
[
2
]
. This hybrid observer preserves the continuous-time robustness of the baseline observer while enabling explicit regulation of the residual magnitude.

IV
Observer with Prescribed Residual Bound

IV-A

Hybrid Observer Formulation

We augment the continuous-time observer (
3
) with resets driven by the residual
r(t)
, defined in (
12
). Let
\delta>0
denote a designer-specified residual bound and let
\rho\in[0,1)
denote a prescribed residual contraction factor. We define the resulting hybrid observer-plant dynamics as follows:

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=Ax+Bu+d

(13a)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{x}}

\displaystyle=A\hat{x}+Bu+L\bigl(y-C\hat{x}\bigr)

\displaystyle(x,\hat{x})\in\mathcal{C}

(13b)

\displaystyle\hat{x}^{+}

\displaystyle=\hat{x}^{-}+Jr^{-}

\displaystyle(x,\hat{x})\in\mathcal{D}

(13c)

where
\hat{x}^{-}
and
\hat{x}^{+}
denote the observer states immediately before and after the observer reset,
J\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
denotes a constant reset gain (to be designed) and
r^{-}
denotes the residual immediately before the reset. It should be noted that the plant state
x
evolves continuously at all times, whereas only the observer state
\hat{x}
is subject to jumps. The flow and jump sets in (

) and (
13c
) are defined in terms of the prescribed residual envelope

as follows:

\mathcal{C}\triangleq\{(x,\hat{x}):\|r\|\leq\delta\},\qquad\mathcal{D}\triangleq\{(x,\hat{x}):\|r\|\,\geq\,\delta\}

(14)

Note that at the boundary points where both flow and jump are possible, jumps are given priority. Subtracting
x
from both sides of (
13c
) and then using
\tilde{x}=x-\hat{x}
and
r=C\tilde{x}
(which follows from (
1b
) and (
12
)) in the resulting equation, we get:

\displaystyle\tilde{x}^{+}=(\mathbb{I}-JC)\tilde{x}^{-}

(15a)

\displaystyle r^{+}=C\tilde{x}^{+}=C(\mathbb{I}-JC)\tilde{x}^{-}

(15b)

where
(\cdot)^{-}
and
(\cdot)^{+}
denote the values of
(\cdot)
immediately before and after the reset. The contraction and invariance properties of the hybrid observer
(
13

IV-B

Analysis and Performance Guarantees

The hybrid observer in (
13b
)-(
13c
) uses residual-triggered resets as a supervisory mechanism. The proposed observer architecture is constructed to enforce contraction of the residual at every reset, render a designer-prescribed residual envelope positively invariant, and preserve the robustness and boundedness properties of the baseline continuous-time observer in (

Theorem 1

(Main result)
.

1
-
3

\delta>0
and
\rho\in[0,1)
be

J\triangleq(1-\rho)P^{-1}C^{\top}\bigl(CP^{-1}C^{\top}\bigr)^{-1}

If

)-(

(i)

(
Residual contraction at jumps
)

\|r^{+}\|=\rho\,\|r^{-}\|

(17)

(ii)

(
Forward invariance of the residual envelope
) The residual envelope,
\{r:\|r\|\leq\delta\}
is forward invariant,
i.e.
,

\|r(t)\|\leq\delta,\quad\forall t\geq t_{0}

(18)

(iii)

(
Non-expansiveness of the estimation error at jumps in the

-metric

V^{+}\leq V^{-},\qquad V\triangleq\tilde{x}^{\top}P\tilde{x}

(19)

(iv)

(

)
For all

V(t)\leq V(t_{0})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\,\bar{d}^{\,2}

(20)

where
\alpha,\beta

\displaystyle\|\tilde{x}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq M

(21)

\displaystyle M\triangleq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\max}(P)}{\lambda_{\min}(P)}}\,\bar{x}_{0}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{\alpha\,\lambda_{\min}(P)}}\,\bar{d}

Proof.

We prove claims (i)-(iv) sequentially.

Proof of claim (i):

Substituting (
16
) in (
15
), we get

r^{+}=C\Bigl(\mathbb{I}-(1-\rho)P^{-1}C^{\top}(CP^{-1}C^{\top})^{-1}C\Bigr)\tilde{x}^{-}

(22)

Since
C
has full row rank from Assumption

,
CP^{-1}C^{\top}\succ 0
and
CP^{-1}C^{\top}(CP^{-1}C^{\top})^{-1}=\mathbb{I}
. Using this in (

) and simplifying, we get

r^{+}=\rho\,C\tilde{x}^{-}=\rho\,r^{-}

which proves (

) after taking norms on both sides.

Proof of claim (ii):

During flows, the residual evolves continuously. By (

), immediately after any jump,

\|r^{+}\|=\rho\,\delta<\delta

since
\rho\in[0,1)
. By definition of the flow and jump sets in (

), a jump is enforced whenever
\|r\|=\delta
. Since
\|r(t_{0})\|\leq\delta
by assumption and

is continuous during flows, the residual cannot exit the residual envelope
\{r:\|r\|\leq\delta\}
. Therefore, this set is forward invariant, which establishes (

Proof of claim (iii):

Define the matrix

\Pi\triangleq P^{-1}C^{\top}(CP^{-1}C^{\top})^{-1}C

(23)

Using (
23
) and (
16
), we can express

\mathbb{I}-JC=\mathbb{I}-(1-\rho)\Pi=(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)+\rho\Pi

Hence, from (

\tilde{x}^{+}=(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\tilde{x}^{-}+\rho\Pi\tilde{x}^{-}

Let
a\triangleq(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\tilde{x}^{-}
and
b\triangleq\Pi\tilde{x}^{-}
. Then
\tilde{x}^{-}=a+b
and
\tilde{x}^{+}=a+\rho b
.
Consider the Lyapunov function

, which defines the

V^{+}
gives

V^{+}=(a+\rho b)^{\top}P(a+\rho b)=a^{\top}Pa+2\rho\,a^{\top}Pb+\rho^{2}b^{\top}Pb

The matrices
\Pi

(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)
act as complementary projections in the

-weighted metric, so the decomposition
\tilde{x}^{-}=a+b
corresponds to

C\Pi=CP^{-1}C^{\top}(CP^{-1}C^{\top})^{-1}C=C

(25)

and therefore
C(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)=0
. Using

and
a=(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\tilde{x}^{-}
, we have

a^{\top}Pb=\tilde{x}^{-\,\top}(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)^{\top}P\,\Pi\tilde{x}^{-}

Using
P\Pi=C^{\top}(CP^{-1}C^{\top})^{-1}C

\displaystyle(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)^{\top}P\,\Pi

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)^{\top}C^{\top}(CP^{-1}C^{\top})^{-1}C

\displaystyle=\big(C(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\big)^{\top}(CP^{-1}C^{\top})^{-1}C=0

which implies

. Substituting into

V^{+}=a^{\top}Pa+\rho^{2}b^{\top}Pb\leq a^{\top}Pa+b^{\top}Pb

Finally, since

V^{-}=(a+b)^{\top}P(a+b)=a^{\top}Pa+b^{\top}Pb

Thus

, which proves (

Proof of claim (iv):

Let
\{t_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
denote the sequence of reset times of the hybrid observer, with

the initial time, and let the Lyapunov function be
V(t)=\tilde{x}(t)^{\top}P\tilde{x}(t)
. Let

be the comparison function in the

\dot{z}(t)=-\alpha z(t)+\beta\bar{d}^{\,2},\qquad z(t_{0})=V(t_{0})

We prove by induction on

V(t)\leq z(t),\qquad\forall t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})

where

if another jump occurs, and

Base case (
k=0
):

On
[t_{0},t_{1})
, the hybrid observer flows and

satisfies (

.

Inductive step:
Now, suppose (

) holds on

Since

Remark 2

(Choice of
\delta

with noisy measurements)

If the output is corrupted by bounded measurement noise, the residual contains both estimation error and noise. Since the reset law contracts only the estimation-error component,

IV-C

Minimum Dwell Time

To exclude Zeno behavior, it is necessary to show that two consecutive resets are separated by a strictly positive time interval
[
2
]
. We now establish such a minimum dwell time for the observer (
13
).

Since jumps are triggered when
\|r\|=\delta
, it suffices to bound the growth rate of the residual during flows.
During flows,
r(t)=C\tilde{x}(t)
, and therefore using (
4
)

\dot{r}(t)=C\dot{\tilde{x}}(t)=C\left[A_{c}\tilde{x}(t)+d(t)\right]

Using the triangle inequality and Assumption
1
, we get

\|\dot{r}(t)\|\leq\|C\|\,\|A_{c}\|\,\|\tilde{x}(t)\|+\|C\|\,\bar{d}

(28)

Using (
21
) in (
28
) yields

\|\dot{r}(t)\|\leq\bar{\ell},\qquad\bar{\ell}\triangleq\|C\|\,\|A_{c}\|\,M+\|C\|\,\bar{d}

(29)

Let
t_{k}
and
t_{k+1}
denote two consecutive reset times. Immediately after
t_{k}
,
\|r(t_{k}^{+})\|=\rho\delta
. A subsequent reset requires
\|r\|
to increase to
\delta
. Integrating (
29
) and taking norms, we obtain

\|r(t)\|\leq\|r(t_{k}^{+})\|+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\|\dot{r}(s)\|ds\leq\rho\delta+\bar{\ell}(t-t_{k})

Thus, the earliest time at which
\|r(t)\|
can reach
\delta

satisfies

\delta\leq\rho\delta+\bar{\ell}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})
,
which implies

t_{k+1}-t_{k}\geq\left[(1-\rho)\delta\right]/\bar{\ell}>0

(30)

Therefore, a strictly positive minimum dwell time is guaranteed between consecutive jumps.

VI
Conclusion

This letter proposed a hybrid observer that enforces a prescribed bound on the observer residual. The observer consists of a continuous-time Luenberger observer combined with state resets that are triggered whenever the residual norm reaches a specified threshold. While preserving the uniform boundedness properties of the continuous-time observer, the reset structure guarantees residual contraction at jump instants, forward invariance of the residual envelope, and non-expansiveness of the estimation error in the Lyapunov metric. Simulation results support the analysis: in the presence of bounded disturbances, the residual remains confined within the prescribed bound, whereas a standard Luenberger observer with the same gains is shown to exceed this bound. Unlike classical observer designs that provide residual bounds as a consequence of system dynamics, the proposed approach enables enforcement of a designer-specified residual bound through hybrid state resets.

In many applications, residual-based fault detection relies on comparing the residual magnitude against a fixed threshold. To avoid false positives caused by transient disturbances, such thresholds are often tuned in an ad hoc manner, which may reduce detection sensitivity or increase false-positives. By mitigating disturbance-induced residual spikes and enforcing a prescribed residual envelope, the proposed resetting observer provides a more structured characterization of nominal behavior. Thus, unusually frequent observer resets may indicate deviations from nominal conditions. Future work will investigate how reset frequency can be used to develop diagnostic strategies. Additional directions include systematic design of the reset gain, robustness under measurement noise and noise-aware selection of the residual bound, integration with output-feedback control via separation-type analysis, and extension of the framework to nonlinear and sampled-data systems. While the paper focuses on LTI systems to rigorously establish the observer mechanism, the underlying hybrid framework is not restricted to linear dynamics and motivates these extensions.
