1.0 Introduction
 The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility
 (Mather and Voyles 2013) deployed the first ARM Mobile Facility (AMF1; Miller et al. 2016) near
 LaPorte, Texas to support the Tracking Aerosol Convection Interactions Experiment (TRACER)
 (Jensen et al. 2025) near Houston, Texas. From October 2021 to September 2022, AMF1 was deployed to
 29.67° N, 95.06° W near LaPorte, Texas and the 2 nd Generation C-band Scanning ARM Precipitation
 Radar (CSAPR2) was deployed to a supplementary site at 29.53° N, 95.28° W (Figure 1). During an
 intensive operational period (IOP) from 1 June to 30 September 2022, the CSAPR2 sampled precipitation
 echoes in an adaptive scanning mode following the Multisensor Agile Adaptive Scanning (MAAS)
 framework (Kollias et al. 2020). MAAS helped optimize the CSAPR2 scan strategy to perform frequent
 plan position indicator (PPI) and range height indicator (RHI) scans (Lamer et al. 2023). Details of the
 CSAPR2 scanning, data processing, and calibration procedures used by the principal investigator (PI),
 and the PI data files are described by Oue et al. (2023). Details of the CSAPR2 operational performance,
 ARM data processing and correction procedures, and data quality masks are described by
 Feng et al. (2024a).

 TRACER objectives involved collection of high-resolution observations of convective cells and the
 characterization of the environment in which deep convective clouds (DCCs) initiate, grow, propagate,
 and decay (Jensen et al. 2025). This requires an objective analysis of each DCC’s spatial and temporal
 evolution during its lifetime. CSAPR2 precipitation echoes were provided as input to an object tracking
 algorithm called tobac (Tracking and Object-Based Analysis of Clouds; Sokolowsky et al. 2024) to
 retrieve life cycle characteristics of the sampled DCCs. This document describes the procedures followed
 to conduct the tobac cell tracking and the output data files created as part of ARM’s FY25 Radar Plan
 (Feng et al. 2024b).

           at 25, 50, and 100 km (Feng et al. 2024a).

2.0 Cell Tracking

 2.1 Input Data
 The CSAPR2 collected cell-tracking scans of convective clouds on 76 days during the TRACER IOP
 (Table 1) when the radar completed over 24,000 scan bundles to track over 1,000 cells (Oue et al. 2023).
 The CSAPR2 scan strategy was unique relative to ARM scanning radars or National Oceanic and
 Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) Next-Generation Weather Radar (NEXRAD) systems. Instead of a
 Volume Coverage Pattern (VCP) that covers the 360˚ azimuthal range over multiple PPI scans, the
 CSAPR2 performed scan bundles comprised of three PPI scans over narrow sectors (e.g., Figure 2)
 followed by four to six RHI scans within those sectors every two minutes (Oue et al. 2023). The narrow
 sector widths for the scans were designed to repeatedly target a convective cell selected by MAAS
 (Lamer et al. 2023) in rapid succession.

 The CSAPR2 data had to be adapted for tobac cell tracking, which requires a regularly spaced cartesian
 grid. Previous studies (e.g., Gupta et al. 2024) have satisfied this requirement using constant-altitude PPIs
 (CAPPIs) at a fixed altitude created by gridding radar data using the Python ARM Radar Toolkit
 (Py-ART) software (Helmus and Collis 2016). The first two PPI scans in each scan bundle sampled the
 top and middle portions of a target cell. Therefore, their elevation angles varied based on the target cell
 properties. However, the third PPI scan was always performed at a 3˚ elevation angle (e.g., Figure 2).

 Figure 2. Radar reflectivity from the 3˚ elevation angle PPI scan conducted on 11 August 2022 at 09.42
 UTC during TRACER.

 Since the first two PPIs in a scan bundle had varying elevation angles, their inclusion in the CAPPI
 resulted in inconsistent spatial grids across different scan bundles. Therefore, only the PPI scan with a
 constant 3˚ elevation angle was used to derive CAPPIs. CAPPIs derived from a single PPI scan were

 2

found to have limited spatial coverage at individual altitude levels (Figure 3). Composite reflectivity from
the 3˚-elevation-angle PPI scan was thus used as input for tobac instead of CAPPIs.

Figure 3. Spatial extent of CAPPIs at 1-, 2-, and 3-km altitude calculated using the 3˚-elevation-angle
 PPI scan from 11 August 2022 at 09.42 UTC during TRACER.

Composite reflectivity was calculated using the bias and attenuation corrected radar reflectivity factor
from the ARM b-1 level data set (Figure 4). Radar data from the 3˚-elevation-angle PPI scan was first
gridded at 0.5-km horizontal and 1-km vertical resolution. The column-maximum radar reflectivity factor
was defined as the composite reflectivity. Feng et al. (2024a) describe the CSAPR2 data availability and
the application of calibration checks, bias corrections, and gas and hydrometeor attenuation corrections to
the PI data set. See Appendix-A for an illustration of the bias and attenuation corrections.

Figure 4. Composite reflectivity from the 3˚ PPI conducted on 11 August 2022 at 00.09.42 UTC during
 TRACER.

2.2 Running tobac
tobac identifies objects based on user-defined thresholds to determine their shape or size and track their
motion over time by linking objects along a common trajectory. Daily images and animations of the
CSAPR2 radar reflectivity factor were created for users to identify cases for detailed analysis. These
images and animations are available on the ARM DQ plot browser (site = ‘HOU’, class = ‘csapr2’,
facility = ‘S2’).

tobac has three modules: feature identification or detection, areal or volumetric segmentation, and feature
linking for the tracking output. Technical details associated with these modules are described by
Sokolowsky et al. (2024) and their application toward understanding isolated deep convective cloud
properties is discussed by Gupta et al. (2024). The modules use various threshold parameters that govern
the cloud tracking algorithm. Table 2 lists the tobac thresholds used. The tobac documentation describes
the thresholds in extensive detail and accounts for any changes with the latest updates to the software.

Feature Identification. Isolated deep convective clouds are identified as regions of local maxima in
composite reflectivity using thresholds of 20, 30, 40, and 50 dBZ. Multiple thresholds allow tobac to
identify features as regions of composite reflectivity greater than 20 dBZ but also distinguish regions of
intense convection within these features using higher thresholds. The position of a feature is defined as
the weighted center of regions identified using the multiple thresholds. A Gaussian filter (standard
deviation of 0.5) was used to smooth out the data.

Segmentation. This module identifies the area associated with each feature identified in the first module.
The associated areas are segmented using the watershedding technique until a segmentation threshold is
reached. The threshold of 20 dBZ was selected to ensure regions of strong convection are included in the
areal extent of a feature. This module extends the output from the first module by adding the number of
grid points within the area associated with each feature. It also provides an additional output file that has
an area mask for each feature that is useful for identifying a feature’s shape or for calculating the feature
area.

Linking. A cell is defined as a collection of features that are identified as the same convective cloud
across multiple timesteps. This module links features identified along a common trajectory across
multiple timesteps to create a parent cell for these features. Features are linked using a prediction of the
anticipated position of a feature in the following timestep based on its position at the current and previous
timesteps. This process uses the trackpy package (Allan et al. 2025) with threshold parameters listed in
Table 2.

This module assigns a cell number to each feature that belongs to a parent cell. These features are also
assigned a time since first detection of the parent cell. Features associated with the same parent cell have
the same cell number. The velocity of the linked features is calculated for each timestep and their distance
from the nearest neighboring feature is determined. These properties are added to the list of features from
the first module to create a unified output file.

3.0 Output Data
 CSAPR2 collected data on 76 days during TRACER and at least one cloud object was tracked on 72 days
 (Table 1). Two output files are created for each of the 72 days. The first file has a time series of clouds
 identified and tracked over more than one timestep. For each timestep, a list of clouds identified within
 the radar scan is provided within the ‘cell’ variable. The corresponding variables provide various cloud
 properties for each cell at each timestep. The variables included are listed in Table 3 with their
 descriptions, units, and object type (adapted from tobac documentation). The file header is provided in
 Appendix-B.

 tobac provides this output in the form of a pandas dataframe that can be stored in the HDF format as a .h5
 file. However, ARM data file standards require the use of the NetCDF format. Therefore, the tobac output
 was converted to NetCDF4 using the NetCDF4-Python package. The following code can be used to
 convert an output file back to a pandas dataframe using the xarray Python package:

 Import xarray as xr
 x=xr.open_dataset('output_file.nc,'decode_cf=False)
 y=x.to_dataframe()
 y=y.drop(['base_time','time_offset','lat','lon','alt'],axis=1
 z=y.explode(list(y.columns))

 The second output file contains a cloud mask across the two-dimensional grid over which the radar data
 were mapped. Each pixel has a value corresponding to the cloud feature identified at that location at each
 timestep. If no cloud object was identified at a pixel at a given timestep, it was assigned a value of 0. This
 file provides the geolocation of the tracked clouds at each timestep to determine their shape, size,
 irregularity, convexity, etc. (Hahn et al. 2025). Users are referred to the tobac documentation’s example
 gallery to explore various applications for these output files.

 Although the PI data set was also used for tobac cell tracking, more numerous clouds were tracked for
 longer periods when cell tracking was conducted with b-1 level data as the input for tobac. This was due
 to the corrective procedures applied to the b-1-level data set, which typically resulted in an increase in the
 radar reflectivity factor values. The tobac cell tracking output based on the PI data set or using different
 Py-ART-based gridding procedures are not included here.

4.0 Summary
 The CSAPR2 was operated in adaptive scanning mode using the MAAS algorithm on 76 days during the
 TRACER IOP in June to September 2022. The radar completed over 24,000 scan bundles to track over
 1,000 cells. The CSAPR2 scan strategy was unique relative to traditional radars with two narrow-sector
 PPI scans at variable elevation angles and varying sector widths. These were followed by a third PPI scan
 that was always at 3° elevation angle. Composite reflectivity from the 3˚ PPIs was used as input for tobac
 cloud tracking to provide a data set to examine convective cloud propagation and evolution. Across 72
 days when 3˚ PPI scans were available and cloud objects were identified within the CSAPR2 scans,
 39,405 features associated with 7,640 cells were tracked. Cloud masks were created using tobac to
 identify distinct features within the radar scans. Two output files were provided for each date with a list of
 tracked clouds and the cloud mask, respectively. This data set is expected to be a valuable resource to

 5

understand the evolution of deep convective clouds in a polluted environment with a variety of aerosol
sources. Over 6,500 cells were tracked within 100 km of the AMF1 site. Users can supplement the cloud
properties for clouds tracked above the AMF1 site with other ARM data sets for various research avenues
(e.g., Gupta et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2024).

 Bias and Attenuation Corrections

The PI data were reprocessed by ARM to create the b-1 level data set. This had a significant impact on
the cell tracking because of increased reflectivity values (Figure 5) increased within the PPI scans (see
Feng et al. 2024 for details). Increased reflectivity meant that tobac was able to identify more numerous
and longer lived cells relative to cell tracking being conducted on the PI data set (Table 3).
