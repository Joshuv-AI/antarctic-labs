Majorana-XYZ subsystem code
Tobias Busse, Lauri Toikka

Abstract
We present a new type of a quantum error correction code, termed Majorana-XYZ code, where the logical quantum information scales macroscopically yet is protected by topologically non-trivial degrees of freedom. It is a [ n , k , g , d ] [n,k,g,d] subsystem code with n = L 2 n=L^{2} physical qubits, k = ⌊ L / 2 ⌋ k=\lfloor L/2\rfloor logical qubits, g ∼ L 2 g\sim L^{2} gauge qubits, and distance d = L d=L . The physical check operations, i.e. the measurements needed to obtain the error syndrome, are 3 3 -local and nearest-neighbour. The code detects every 1- and 2-qubit error, and every error of weight 3 and higher (constrained by the distance) that is not a product of the 3-qubit check operations, however, these products act only on the gauge qubits leaving the code space invariant. The undetected weight-3 and higher operators are confined to the gauge group and do not affect logical information. While the code does not have local stabiliser generators, the logical qubits cannot be modified locally by an undetectable error, and in this sense the Majorana-XYZ code combines notions of both topological and local gauge codes while providing a macroscopic number of topological logical qubits. Taken as a non-gauge stabiliser code we can encode k ∼ L 2 − 3 ​ L k\sim L^{2}-3L logical qubits into L 2 L^{2} physical qubits; however, the check operators then become weight 2 ​ L 2L . The code is derived from an experimentally promising system of Majorana fermions on the honeycomb lattice with only nearest-neighbour interactions.

Introduction

In order to build a quantum computer capable of demonstrating any advantage over classical computers, we either need to sufficiently suppress the physical errors in the qubits, or execute the computation within a quantum error-correction (QEC) scheme

In a topological code, a disconnected collection of simply-connected regions cannot be the support of a non-detectable logical error
5

The Majorana-XYZ code combines notions of both topological and local gauge codes while providing a macrosopic number of logical qubits scaling with linear system size. By topological protection we mean here that the logical information cannot be modified locally by an undetectable error: action by the gauge group, which constitutes the undetectable errors of the code, does not damage the encoded logical information; even if undetectable by the code, they only act on the gauge qubits.

The traditional notion of a “topological code”

;
6

logical
errors is topologically non-trivial, in surface codes and color codes undetectable logical errors are related to homologically non-trivial cycles (true for Majorana-XYZ); (ii) the number of encoded qubits

local

and a macroscopic

global degrees of freedom
, and its check operators are local, and it is in this way that the Majorana-XYZ code combines notions in a new way of topological and non-topological local gauge subsystem codes.

The physical system, termed Majorana-XYZ Hamiltonian, is defined by Majorana fermions in a honeycomb arrangement with only nearest-neighbour Majorana-Majorana interactions, originally introduced by Li and Franz

Experimentally, Majorana fermions appear in vortex cores in a topological superfluid consisting of ultra-cold fermionic atoms

1

32

From the point of view of implementation in practice, having to do only low-weight check measurements is a particularly attractive feature for a code. This is the driving idea behind subsystem codes, where the geometrical locality of check measurements is the reward for sacrificing Hilbert space to non-encoding gauge degrees of freedom. The fewer qubits that we need to involve per error correction measurement the lower the probability of introducing additional errors resulting in a lighter circuit implementing the error correction cycle and thus reducing the overhead of the QEC protocol. Most hardware approaches are limited to nearest-neighbour connectivity meaning geometrically-local low-weight check measurements are particularly desirable.

To our knowledge the Majorana-XYZ code is the first example of a static (non-Floquet) code with a macroscopic number of logical qubits that are encoded by homologically non-trivial cycles. Here, specifically, the cycles are double strings that wind around the system in two distinct directions. No gauge transformation can change the winding.
The study of gauge defects and generalisation to a Floquet subsystem code
13
is left as a future work. In the Majorana-XYZ code, such a construction arises from a sequence of gauge-fixing measurement of the gauge triangles, which don’t mutually all commute.

II.2
Identification as a subsystem code with
\frac{L}{2}
logical qubits

Partitioning the Hilbert space as a subsystem code allows us to measure the stabilisers with only local measurements instead of weight
2L
non-local measurements that span across the entire system, which comes at the cost of gauge degrees of freedom. In a subsystem code, the ambient Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with
n=L^{2}
physical qubits is partitioned as
\mathcal{H}=\left(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{G}\right)\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
, where the code space
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{G}
is divided into the logical space
\mathcal{L}
containing logical qubits with the protected quantum information and the gauge space
\mathcal{G}
containing gauge qubits. The gauge qubits are spin-
\frac{1}{2}
degrees of freedom such that any two states related by a non-trivial transformation acting only on the gauge qubits are equivalent. The set of the gauge operators forms the gauge group
G
.

For the Majorana-XYZ code, the local measurements to obtain the stabilisers are
3
-qubit measurements given by the triangle operators, which generate the gauge group
G
,

G=\left\langle T_{i}^{\nabla},T_{j}^{\Delta}\mid i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{g}\right\rangle,

(4)

where
g
is the number of gauge qubits (see below).
Since any double loop is a product of the triangle operators, we can measure the double loops by measuring the triangle operators even if the triangle operators do not commute. The physical checks correspond to the gauge operators and not to the stabiliser checks.

The centre of a group is the set of elements that commute with every element of the group. The centre of the gauge group
G
is the stabiliser group
S
. The stabiliser group is the group of operators generated by the gauge operators that commute with every element of the gauge group,
S=\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{G})\cap\mathcal{G}
, where
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{G})
is the centraliser of the gauge group
G
in the Pauli group, i.e. the set of all Pauli operators that commute with every element of the group
G
. It can be instructive to re-write the gauge group
G
as

\begin{split}G=&\left\langle\Xi_{j}^{X}\Xi_{j+1}^{X},\Xi_{j}^{Y}\Xi_{j+1}^{Y},\Xi_{j}^{Z}\Xi_{j+1}^{Z},X_{k}^{(g)},Z_{k}^{(g)}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.\mid j\in\mathbb{Z}_{L-1},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{g}\right\rangle,\end{split}

(5)

where
Z_{k}^{(g)},X_{k}^{(g)}
are the logical operators for the gauge qubits, given by products of the triangle operators that are not products of double loops.

Counting degrees of freedom, we have

k=n-g-s

(6)

logical qubits where
s=|S|
is the number of stabiliser qubits,
g=(|G|-|S|)/2
is the number of gauge qubits, and
n
is the number of physical qubits, and
|A|
is the cardinality, or the number of independent generators, of some group
A

|S|=3L-2-(L+1\;\;\text{mod }2)
, and one possible choice for the independent generators of the gauge group

|G|=2(L-1)(L-1)+1

The logical operators always leave the stabiliser qubits intact, but in general need not leave the gauge qubits intact. Only the bare logical operators leave the gauge qubits intact. As a result of the gauge freedom of the subsystem code, we are free to act on the ‘bare’ logical operators by any gauge operator giving ‘dressed’ logical operators. The set of all such dressed logical operators is given by the subset of the centralizer of the stabiliser subgroup that excludes all elements in the gauge group,

To enforce the proper spin algebra such that logical 1-qubit operators belonging to different logical qubits commute, we dress the bare logical operators. Minimally, any necessary dressing needs the space of a 6-site hexagon in between the logical qubits, restricting geometrically the number of logical qubits to
L/2

The logical degrees of freedom that encode the protected quantum information are topological in that they are necessarily non-local and insensitive to local probes or errors. The many-body degeneracy is non-accidental, i.e. we cannot locally perturb or break any symmetry to split the degeneracy. In topological order different degenerate ground states cannot be distinguished by a local measurement. Here, we see quite directly how the commutation relations and the specific triangular geometry result in logical degrees of freedom that are necessarily topological by winding around the torus in three independent ways. Specifically, an
X
logical operator must wind around both the
Y
and
Z
directions, a
Y
logical operator must wind around both the
X
and
Z
directions, and a
Z
logical operator must wind around both the
Y
and
X
directions.

III
Outlook and further work

We have presented a quantum error correction code which combines notions of topological and non-topological codes. The code is most native to Majorana based qubit modalities requiring only nearest-neighbour Majorana-Majorana coupling, but can also be implemented on spins on a triangular lattice with local 3-spin interaction terms. The logical operators are homologically non-trivial cycles, similar to topological codes where the fault-tolerant logical operations can be interpreted as instances of anyon condensation
15
and nontrivial anyon string operators
21
. The physical checks are in fact the simplest possible Majorana-Majorana measurements allowed by symmetry. All detectable errors of weight equal to or below
(d-1)/2=(L-1)/2\sim O(\sqrt{n})
are correctable, similar to topological codes
6
. It is easy to show that the magnetisation and all connected 2-point correlators vanish (Appendix) meaning all errors of weight 1 and 2 are detectable. The topological nature of the Majorana-XYZ code means here that errors that cannot be corrected but are unlikely to occur due to being larger than
O(\sqrt{n})
in weight
are not statistically important

6
. This reflects the behaviour of a threshold in topological codes
6
, below which fault-tolerance can be achieved with asymptotically perfect accuracy in the thermodynamic limit.

We leave the numerical computation of the threshold for future work. The threshold of a topological code can be mapped to a phase transition in a corresponding classical statistical model
5
;
9
;
30
. Another important aspect is the generalisation of the code presented here to a Floquet code with dynamical logical qubits. The additional logical qubits exist in the space freed by creating a gauge defect (fixing the gauge) which arises from the non-commuting check measurements, here represented by the gauge triangles. The study of such features is left to later work.

Appendix B
Detectable and undetectable errors

The dressing corresponds to an undetectable error. Action by topologically non-trivial elements in the gauge group transforms dressed logical operators into each other, and therefore cannot be detected as errors by the code. Any dressed logical operator automatically commutes with all double loops. Of course, any dressed operator is an equally valid choice.

From a condensed matter perspective, the Majorana-XYZ code defined in Eq. (
1
) has a rich symmetry group. Importantly, the symmetry structure immediately results in many vanishing correlation functions, which have a physical meaning within the code as detectable physical errors. Non-vanishing expectations correspond to undetectable errors. Detectable errors live in the space
P_{n}\char 92\relax\mathcal{Z}(S)
where
P_{n}
is the Pauli group

The common
+1
eigenspace of the stabilisers defines the codespace
\mathcal{C}
of the error correction code and any operator whose expectation value vanishes corresponds to a detectable error. A detectable error anticommutes with at least one stabiliser. The codespace is a specifically chosen symmetry sector and the correlation functions may only be non-zero if the operator in consideration acts non-trivially within the subspace. Products of the gauge operators that do not lie in
S
will have non-vanishing expectation values.

B.1
Errors of weight 1 (magnetization)

Let
A_{j}
be a Pauli matrix
X
,
Y
or
Z
acting on a site
j
.
On any lattice there is a loop operator
\Xi_{i}^{B}
with
B\neq A
and
i
chosen such that the loop runs over the site
j
. Then,
\anticommutator{A_j}{\Xi_i^{B}}=0
and it follows that
\langle A_{j}\rangle=0
for all Pauli matrices
A\in\{X,Y,Z\}
. The spins do not have a preferred direction and the magnetization vanishes.

B.2
Errors of weight 2

Let
A_{i}B_{j}
be a Pauli operator acting non-trivially on two sites
i
and
j
. Then the two Pauli matrices
A
and
B
may be either the same or different. It is always possible to find a loop operator
\Xi_{k}^{A}
that anti-commutes with one Pauli but commutes with the other, except when
i=j
and
A=B
. All connected 2-point correlators vanish, meaning any pair of spins is uncorrelated.

All errors of weight 1 and 2 are therefore detectable and correctable by the Majorana-XYZ code.

B.3
Errors of weight 3 and higher

It is always possible to find a loop operator
\Xi_{k}^{A}
such that it anti-commutes with an odd number of Paulis but commutes with the rest, except when the error is a triangle operator or their scale up. The scale up means a larger 3-qubit triangle congruent to
T^{\nabla}
or
T^{\Delta}
.

B.4
Higher correlations

The four-point correlators that do not vanish correspond to operators that are products of two edge-sharing triangle operators: To commute with the loop operators, we need an even number of Pauli operators of the opposite types along the X-, Y- and Z- directions. Here we find diamond and parallelogram shaped operators, which, again are products of the triangle operators (Fig.
5
).
