Cyber Conservative Operations

1. Cyber Conservative Operations Purpose and Overview
In an era where cyber threats are becoming increasingly sophisticated and pervasive, the need
for robust cyber resilience strategies has never been more critical. This paper introduces the
concept of Cyber Conservative Operations, a proactive approach designed to assist critical
infrastructure owners and operators in managing risk and maintaining resilience in the face of
imminent, but not yet occurring, cyber events. By leveraging the principles of Cyber-Informed
Engineering (CIE), along with the energy sector’s practice of conservative operations, Cyber
Conservative Operations provide a framework for planning, preparing and deploying
coordinated sets of active defense, engineered controls, and resilience-oriented actions ahead
of a cyber event to minimize the consequences of digitally enabled hazards and ensure quick
and efficient recovery from anticipated cyber threats.

 Cyber conservative operations practices provide owners and operators with
 the ability to plan coordinated sets of active defense and planned resilience-
 oriented actions that they can execute in advance of impacts to minimize the
 consequences of digitally enabled hazards. Cyber conservative operations are
 especially important for preventing impacts that cannot be precisely quantified
 nor forecasted and posturing the organization for quick and efficient recovery
 from an anticipated or imminent digital threat.

This paper begins with a discussion of conservative operations as it is currently applied to the
bulk power system (BPS). Conservative operations are a mechanism used to allow BPS entities
to enact defensive operating plans to reduce the impact of impending unreliability in the grid.
That operational concept served as an inspiration for the idea of cyber conservative operations,
as an approach to operate in a cyber-conservative manner, reducing unnecessary digital
pathways and expanding organizational and system resilience to digital uncertainty. Leveraging
conservative operations as a model, we describe a concept for cyber conservative operations as
it could be implemented at an asset owner facility. Finally, we describe two cases of cyber
conservative operations drawn from high-profile cyber events.

2. Cyber Conservative Operations in the Bulk Power Systems
The traditional application of conservative operations ensures a power system’s reliability during
adverse events such as severe weather or resource shortages by providing a systematic
approach to prepare for potential threats to the system’s stability. 1 The North American Electric
Reliability Corporation (NERC) determined “all entities should have a procedure, such as
‘conservative operations,’ which provides possible steps they may have to take to ensure
reliability.” The procedure includes steps for all involved parties, including neighboring systems,
to maintain situational awareness and reliability in the face of adverse operating conditions.

Conservative operations serve as a preparatory measure rather than a response. The planned
actions are implemented when an event is likely or imminent but not yet occurring and deepen
resilience in advance of a potential reliability impact. This differs from an incident response plan
or procedure, which is enacted mostly reactively to the impact of a threat. During events such as
severe weather (e.g., ice, snow, windstorms, hurricanes, tornadoes), environmental disturbances
(e.g., geomagnetic storms), 3 and physical or cybersecurity threats, 4 systems must operate with
lower risk tolerance than normal and greater redundancy to maintain acceptable performance
through the degradation in reliability these events may cause.

Conservative operations are generally declared for a temporary period spanning from a few
hours to a few days, based on a specific predicted event. Although entities use varying
terminology to describe their processes, BPS operators collectively and continuously assess the
reliability and adequacy of the BPS by identifying potential issues related to system, equipment,
entity, and human performance. 5 Risk assessment teams assess potentially adverse events, such
as wind and solar trends, temperature forecasts, load forecasts, cyber threats, etc., to determine
operational risk.

The performance of conservative operations can be structured in a variety of ways but is often
implemented via watches and warnings. A watch occurs when abnormal or extreme conditions
are possible or expected but not imminent, and a warning occurs when abnormal or extreme
conditions have started or are an imminent threat to the reliability of the power system. 6 The
distinction between a watch and a warning is useful for identifying the appropriate pre-planned
tactics to employ.

the impacts of cyber attacks remain largely undefined. Even though conservative operations can
be declared based on a looming cyber threat, there is no existing guidance to aid utilities in
reducing their digital attack surface, increasing monitoring of actual system conditions, and

3. Cyber Conservative Operations
The idea for cyber conservative operations is rooted in the belief that an entity can reduce the
operational impacts of a cyber event through the execution of pre-planned strategies. Though
this idea is not new, the idea of preparing plans that integrate cyber, operational, and
engineering impact prevention strategies is novel. Cyber conservative operations can be
enacted (at a low level), when an organization identifies the need to prepare (or watch) for
digitally-driven attack or anomaly. A broader set of actions may be executed when an
organization detects evidence of the attack or believes that it is immanent. Cyber conservative
operations could be enacted as preparation for an impending cyber event, or as part of a
response to a weather or grid instability event (e.g., during a hurricane) to prevent the potential
that attackers take advantage of a strained operational posture to initiate cyber actions.

The concept of cyber conservative operations was first introduced in a study of the cyber attack
on the Ukraine power grid. The report was designed to provide a summary of the elements of
the Ukraine attack and specific lessons learned to defend against future similar attacks. In the
report, SANS briefly describes a notion of cyber conservative operations as “intentionally
eliminating planned and unplanned changes as well as stopping any potential impactful
processes.” 8 This description is narrower and more tactical than the concept of cyber
conservative operations proposed here; however, many of the recommendations within that
lessons learned document should be considered as potential actions in an overall cyber
conservative operations strategy. They are included in Appendix C.

Entities considering cyber conservative operations begin by creating a plan identifying the
potential actions to be taken, the threshold for their activation, any operational implications of
their adoption, and the length of time they could be sustained. The planning process is defined
further below, but in general, plans would incorporate activities such as:

 • Adding Resilience and Validation
 o addition of staff or alteration of staff responsibilities,
 o activation of contract capabilities for threat reduction, response, or operational
 resilience,
 o increased digital and physical monitoring of the process, inputs, and outputs,
 o addition of redundant capabilities,
 o additional verification and validation steps or procedures,
 o additional quality assurance actions,

3.1. Cyber Conservative Operations Planning
Planning for cyber conservative operations begins with assembling a team incorporating
stakeholders from operations, information technology, operational technology and other
organizations with a perspective on operational changes needed to protect against the impact of
a cyber attack. It is also helpful to incorporate procurement (to aid in planning for contract
support), finance / risk management (to aid in prioritization and tradeoff analysis), and legal (in
case necessary actions affect warranties, contracts or terms and conditions accepted by the
organization).

Teams creating a plan for cyber conservative operations begin by asking questions drawn from
the Cyber-Informed Engineering Implementation Guide, 9 such as the ones below, to identify
advance actions which could be taken across engineering, operations, and cybersecurity to
protect the most critical systems from the impact of an attack:

 • What are the highest consequence events I can prepare for?
 • What is my risk appetite and how much should I affect my operations in preparation for a
 perceived and imminent threat?
 • How long can I hold this conservative posture without significant impact?
 • When does a cyber conservative operations watch or warning become an incident
 response and what is involved in that transition?

Other relevant questions for consideration can be found in the CIE Implementation Guide
starting with principle five, Layered Defenses, principle six, Active Defense, principle seven,
Interdependency Evaluation, principle nine, Cyber-Secure Supply Chain Controls, and principle
ten, Planned Resilience.

Well ahead of any cyber event, engineering and cyber teams develop a set of potential actions
designed to enhance communication between key points of contact with responsibilities during
the timeframe when cyber conservative operations is enacted, increase awareness of
anomalous network or operational activity adjacent to process or operational activities, reduce
unneeded cyber-attack surfaces, add specific resilience and validation measures to potentially
affected areas, and make available resources to resolve both operational and security impacts of
a cyber event. For each action, teams identify:

 • the rationale for the activity, its application context (where and under what circumstances
 it would be applied),
 • its beneficial impact,
 • action owners or Points of Contact (POCs),
 • the costs and resource requirements associated with it,
 • any requirements or preconditions before it could be enacted (e.g. needed permissions),
 • notification requirements or contracts,
 • any potential negative impacts of leveraging it, the recommended duration of its
 performance, and
 • any other details needed for the item to be well-understood for selection as a potential
 pre-incident response strategy.

A cyber conservative operations plan contains a comprehensive catalog of potential activities to
be considered for activation. Not every proposed activity will be enacted in every case, and
organizations may consider tailoring cyber conservative operations plans for specific threat
types, i.e. ransomware, or loss of specific operational capabilities. Plans should be constructed
to accommodate the unique challenges and risk tolerance of the specific critical functions and
systems that may be affected by an adversary action.

Teams should also consider whether the implementation of a set of cyber conservative
operations actions, ahead of a potential threat, should be coordinated and managed under the
same incident command structure as an actual incident. That decision may prolong the
responsibilities of incident command members; however, it will ensure that, in the event of an
actual event, the response team is fully aware of preparatory actions taken by the organization.
One potential incident command structure to consider has been developed by the International
Society of Automation (ISA), the Incident Command System for Industrial Control Systems
(ICS4ICS). 10

A cyber conservative operations plan can be constructed through answering key CIE Active
Defense and Planned Resilience questions such as:

 1. What are the critical functions that need to be protected?
 2. What are the highest consequence events that must be protected against prior to an
 incident occurring?
 3. What are the indicators, including the earliest precursors, that a high consequence event
 could be caused, intentionally or unintentionally?

The SERC Reliability Corporation guidance for conservative operations notes that operators can
adopt levels of conservative operations, describing a two-level system with watch and warning
phases as an example. 12 In adopting cyber conservative operations, organizations may find it
helpful to consider a multi-phase approach, as some actions might be supportable over an
extended period while others should only be enacted when an event is imminent or occurring to
reduce the potential impact.

3.2. Controls and Countermeasures
The definition of a cyber conservative watch and warning provides operators with increased
operational flexibility to determine the best actions for a given scenario, requiring for clear
communication and an impact/consequence assessment and forecasting to determine risk and
next actions. Additional controls and countermeasures for cyber conservative operations can be
determined by active defenses inherent to most safety systems and critical functions in the BPS.
For example, the following engineering, administrative, cybersecurity, and physical controls can
be used as a risk management technique during watches or warnings:

 • Simplifying operations and decouple from cyber dependencies wherever possible, even
 where if it slows operations or requires additional personnel.
 • Removing/Disabling non-essential components such as services, protocols, drivers, etc,
 including non-essential communication protocols not required for operational purposes.
 • Employing non-repudiable communication channels and security measures to validate
 control actions.

4. Cyber Conservative Operations Beyond the Bulk Power
System
Many industries utilize a form of conservative operations. The American Gas Association (AGA)
provides an emergency preparedness handbook aimed at “addressing potential crisis activities
impacting natural gas utilities.” 13 To prepare for a crisis, AGA members must ensure natural gas
delivery, including through alternative measures, such as trucking in natural gas or maintaining
redundant pipeline feeds to minimize system outages.

Ultimately, conservative and cyber conservative operations depend on the risk management
posture of the sector’s asset owners and operations. In the natural gas sector, rolling blackouts
or brownouts are unacceptable. Bringing a natural gas system back online is also labor-intensive
requiring system integrity tests, service shutdowns to homes and businesses, repressurizing the
system, and then restoring service to homes and businesses. Isolating portions of an affected
pipeline by shutting it down is unfeasible. 14 Additionally, remote connections are required to
control the miles of pipeline and compressor stations.

Cyber conservative operations in a natural gas pipeline scenario would focus on maintaining
pipeline and pressure service through physics-based means, e.g., increasing the flow and
pressure of natural gas in one compressor station to compensate for the loss of connected
compressor station. If possible, temporary manual operations of key systems or vulnerable
portions of the pipeline can reduce cyber impacts on those assets.

Cyber conservative operations, as described in this paper, could be implemented in any sector,
not just energy.

5. Cyber Conservative Operations Examples
Cyber conservative operations strategies could have assisted with risk mitigation in a variety of
industries and recent adversarial campaigns. For example, the 2015 Ukraine power grid attack
and the 2022 Iranian steel mill attack are presented below to demonstrate how cyber
conservative operations could have reduced the cyber attack’s impact, and how the Watch or
Warning structure could have prepared the entities in the first place.

The attack was synchronized and coordinated. It is assumed after extensive undetected

6. Conclusion
Cyber Conservative Operations represent a proactive and strategic approach to managing cyber
risks and enhancing cyber resilience for critical infrastructure. By leveraging the principles of
Cyber-Informed Engineering (CIE) and drawing parallels from conservative operations in the
bulk power system, this framework provides an approach for anticipating and mitigating the
impacts of cyber threats leveraging both traditional cyber as well as operational and engineering
controls.

The implementation of Cyber Conservative Operations involves coordinated actions across
various departments, including IT, operations, and cybersecurity, to ensure a comprehensive
defense against potential cyber events. Through the examples of the 2015 Ukraine power grid
attack and the 2022 Iranian steel mill attack, this paper has demonstrated the practical
application and benefits of Cyber Conservative Operations in real-world scenarios. As cyber

Appendix B. Alternative Conservative Operation Formats and
Details
Pennsylvania-New Jersey-Maryland (PJM) Interconnection uses the format of alerts, warnings,
and action levels to determine how to handle emergencies, including capacity shortages and the
initiation of cyber conservative operations. 30 Alerts are issued one or more days in advance to
give BPS entities enough time to prepare for a postulated event, including:

 • Getting units online within 48 hours in anticipation of a shortage of operating capacity;
 • Sending a maximum generation emergency/ load management alert to notify all BPS
 entities of the need to use emergency operating procedures;
 • Sending a primary reserve alert alerts members of a shortage of operating reserve
 capacity; and
 • Sending a voltage reduction alert to alert entities of a voltage reduction required for the
 BPS.

Warnings are issued in real-time with an estimated time/window for potential future action. They
are used to inform members of actual capacity shortages or contingencies, which may
jeopardize the reliable operation of a BPS. Actions are issued in real-time and require the RC
and BPS members to respond immediately and implement emergency operating procedures.
The target timeframes notwithstanding, shift managers and operators are allowed and expected
to deviate from the nominal time-based escalation of measures as facts and circumstances
dictate.

As an example of conservative operations, Midcontinent ISO (MISO) describes the general
workflow for conservative operations as:31

 1. Determine if conditions warrant conservative operations.
 2. Define conservative operational and time boundaries.
 a. Boundaries of conservative operations
 b. Start and end time of conservative operations
 3. Alert necessary parties of the start of conservative operations.
 a. Affected members and partners
 b. Conservative operations declaration through RC
 4. Ensure adequate staffing levels.
 5. Review system adequacy requirements.
 6. Coordinate with necessary parties to suspend non-essential maintenance and testing
 on monitoring, control, generation, or transmission equipment.
 a. If maintenance will not affect BPS monitoring, control, and reliability, then
 coordinate with relevant parties.
 7. Review all at-risk critical computer systems and suspend maintenance and testing.
