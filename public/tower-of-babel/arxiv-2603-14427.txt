On Borodin-Kostochka conjecture for correspondence coloring An extended abstract of this paper appeared at Eurocomb’25.
Zdeněk Dvořák, Ross J. Kang, David Mikšaník

Abstract
Borodin and Kostochka in 1977 conjectured that if a graph G G has maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 9 \Delta(G)\geq 9 and its clique number satisfies ω ⁡ ( G ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) − 1 \omega(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1 , then its chromatic number satisfies χ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) − 1 \chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1 . We prove this statement with respect to a stronger graph coloring parameter, the correspondence chromatic number χ D ​ P \chi_{DP} , provided the maximum degree is sufficiently large. More precisely, we prove that for every integer Δ ≥ 3 ⋅ 10 9 \Delta\geq 3\cdot 10^{9} , a graph G G of maximum degree at most Δ \Delta satisfies χ D ​ P ​ ( G ) ≤ max ⁡ ( ω ⁡ ( G ) , Δ − 1 ) \chi_{DP}(G)\leq\max(\omega(G),\Delta-1) . This strengthens earlier results of Reed (1999) for usual chromatic number and of Choi, Kierstead and Rabern (2023) for list chromatic number.

Introduction

Here is a common refrain in combinatorics:
How do local constraints influence global structure?

A classic result of this flavour in graph coloring is the celebrated theorem of Brooks
[
7
]
, which implies that for any
\Delta\geq 3

and for any graph
G
of maximum degree at most
\Delta
, if
\omega(G)\leq\Delta
, then the graph
G
is
\Delta
-colorable.
1
1

1

We use
\delta(G)
,

\Delta(G)
,
\omega(G)
, and
\chi(G)
to denote the
minimum degree
, the
maximum degree
, the
clique number
, and the
chromatic number
of the graph
G
,
respectively.

In other words, a local assumption (
\omega(G)\leq\Delta
) gives a bound on the global structure (
\chi(G)\leq\Delta
)
improved over what holds in general without this assumption (
\chi(G)\leq\Delta+1
). Since
\omega(G)\leq\chi(G)
, this bound on
\chi(G)
is the best we can obtain
based only on this assumption.
2
2

2

We remark that
\Delta\geq 3
is needed to exclude
odd cycles. Of course, we could replace this condition by including odd cycles as a special case in the outcome.

Vizing
[
29
]
asked for other extremal bounds on
\chi(G)
given stronger assumptions on
\omega(G)
.
Already the most restrictive case
\omega(G)\leq 2
(i.e., the case of triangle-free graphs) is rather challenging
and has attracted a lot of attention, see e.g.
[
6
,
8
,
25
,
21
,
26
,
4
,
13
,
20
,
23
]
.
For results on other regimes of
\omega(G)
in terms of the maximum degree
\Delta(G)
, see e.g.
[
22
,
5
,
15
,
19
]
.
Our focus in this paper is the other non-trivial extreme case of Vizing’s problem, i.e., the case that
\omega(G)=\Delta(G)-1
.
The most important topic in this case is Borodin-Kostochka Conjecture
[
6
]
.

Conjecture 1

(Borodin and Kostochka
[
6
]
)
.

For every graph
G
such that
\Delta(G)\geq 9
, if
\omega(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
, then
\chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
.

The graph
C_{5}\boxtimes K_{3}
(see Figure
1
), i.e., the strong product of the cycle on five vertices with the triangle,
is
8
-regular and has clique number
6
, yet its chromatic number is
8
(since
|V(G)|=15
and every independent set in
G
has size at most two).
This shows that the assumption
\Delta(G)\geq 9
in this conjecture is necessary.
The most substantial progress towards resolving Borodin-Kostochka Conjecture is due to Reed
[
28
]
who showed that a (much) larger lower bound assumption on
\Delta(G)
is sufficient.

The graph
C_{5}\boxtimes K_{3}
.

Theorem 2

(Reed
[
28
]
)
.

For every graph
G
such that
\Delta(G)\geq 10^{14}
, if
\omega(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
, then
\chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
.

One way to approach Borodin-Kostochka Conjecture is by considering weaker statements.
As an example, Cranston and Rabern
[
12
]
showed the conclusion
\chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
holds under the assumption that
\Delta(G)\geq 13
and
\omega(G)\leq\Delta(G)-3
.
Moreover, the conjecture has been confirmed for graphs from various restricted graph classes, see e.g.
[
16
,
9
,
11
]
for results of this form.

Another approach is to consider strengthened or more general versions of the conjecture,
e.g., to replace the chromatic number by a stronger coloring parameter.
For instance, one can consider
list coloring
, the notion introduced independently by Erdős, Rubin, and Taylor
[
18
]
and by Vizing
[
30
]
.
In this setting, each vertex has its own list of
k
colors and we need to choose its color from its list.
The
list chromatic number

\chi_{\ell}(G)
of a graph
G
then is the minimum integer
k
such that
G
can be properly colored from any such assignment of lists of size
k

(see below for a more precise definition). Clearly the list chromatic number of a graph is at least as large as its ordinary chromatic number,
which corresponds to the special case in which the lists must all be the same. Thus, it is interesting that an analogue of Theorem
2

holds in this more general setting as well.

Theorem 3

(Choi, Kierstead, and Rabern
[
10
]
)
.

For every graph
G
such that
\Delta(G)\geq 10^{20}
, if
\omega(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
, then
\chi_{\ell}(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
.

We continue the investigation along these lines by considering an even stronger coloring
parameter, the
correspondence chromatic number

\chi_{DP}
.
The main idea of correspondence coloring is to additionally generalize the way colors
of different vertices interact. This notion was introduced by Dvořák and Postle
[
17
]
to solve a list
coloring problem for planar graphs.
3
3

3

Many papers use the terms
DP-coloring
and
DP-chromatic number

based on their initials; we prefer to use the terminology they introduced, with the exception of the
\chi_{DP}

notation.
This notion also sheds light on which coloring techniques and results are “local” (only dependent on
the fact that coloring a vertex prevents us from using at most a single specific color at each of its neighbors)
and which depend on a global coherence of the colors.

More precisely, for a graph
G
, a
list assignment
is a function
L\colon V(G)\rightarrow 2^{\text{colors}}

assigning to each vertex
v
a list
L(v)
of colors that it can use. We say that
L
is a
k
-list assignment
if
|L(v)|\geq k
holds
for every
v\in V(G)
. An
L
-coloring
of
G
is a proper coloring
\psi
such that
\psi(v)\in L(v)
holds for every vertex
v\in V(G)
.
We say that the graph
G
is
k
-list colorable
if
G
has an
L
-coloring for every
k
-list assignment
L
;
and the
list chromatic number

\chi_{\ell}(G)
of the graph
G
is then defined as the minimum integer
k
such that
G
is
k
-list colorable.

In correspondence coloring, we additionally specify which colors in lists of adjacent vertices are in conflict.
Formally, a
correspondence assignment
for
G
is a pair
(L,\Pi)
, where

•

L
is a list assignment for
G
and

•

\Pi
is a function assigning to each edge
e=uv\in E(G)
a
(not necessarily perfect) matching
\Pi(e)
between
\{u\}\times L(u)
and
\{v\}\times L(v)
.

That is, for
e=uv\in E(G)
,
\Pi(e)
is a set containing pairs
\{c,d\}
, where
c\in\{u\}\times L(u)
and
d\in\{v\}\times L(v)
,
such that each element of
(\{u\}\times L(u))\cup(\{v\}\times L(v))
belongs to at most one such pair in
\Pi(e)
.
We say that a correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
is a
k
-correspondence assignment
if
L
is a
k
-list assignment.
A function
\varphi
assigning a color to each vertex of
G
is an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
if

•

\varphi(v)\in L(v)
holds for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, and

•

every edge
e=uv\in E(G)
satisfies
\{(u,\varphi(u)),(v,\varphi(v))\}\notin\Pi(e)
.

That is, the matching
\Pi(e)
describes which colors at the endpoints
u
and
v
of the edge
e
conflict, and
\varphi
must assign non-conflicting
colors to adjacent vertices.
We say that the graph
G
is
(L,\Pi)
-colorable
if there exists an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
.
Furthermore, we say that
G
is
k
-correspondence colorable
if
G
is
(L,\Pi)
-colorable for every
k
-correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
of
G
.
Finally, the
correspondence chromatic number

\chi_{DP}(G)
of
G
is defined as the smallest integer
k
such that
G
is
k
-correspondence colorable.

For a correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
and an edge
e=uv
, we say that
\Pi(e)
is the
identity matching
if
\Pi(e)=\{\{(u,\alpha),(v,\alpha)\}:\alpha\in L(u)\cap L(v)\}
.
In the special case that
\Pi
assigns the identity matching to every edge, the
(L,\Pi)
-colorings are exactly the
L
-colorings of the graph.
If
L
additionally assigns the same list to every vertex, the
(L,\Pi)
-colorings are ordinary proper colorings.
This implies that

\chi(G)\leq\chi_{\ell}(G)\leq\chi_{DP}(G)

holds for every graph
G
. Many of the techniques used to bound list chromatic number also
apply in the correspondence coloring setting; e.g., by coloring the vertices greedily one by one, it is easy to see that

\chi_{DP}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1.

On the other hand, these coloring parameters diverge significantly already on bipartite graphs, i.e., for graphs
G
with
\chi(G)=2
.
It is a classic result of Erdős, Rubin, and Taylor
[
18
]
that
\chi_{\ell}(K_{n,n})\sim\log_{2}n
as
n\to\infty
,
and Alon and Krivelevich
[
1
]
conjectured that
\chi_{\ell}(G)=O(\log\Delta(G))
holds for any bipartite graph
G
.
In contrast,
[
24
,
3
]
observed that
\chi_{DP}(G)=\Omega(\Delta/\log\Delta)
holds for
every

\Delta
-regular graph
G
(even for bipartite ones).

Thus, it is not
prima facie
intuitive if Theorems
2
and
3

should extend to the correspondence coloring setting. As our main result, we prove that this is indeed the case.

Theorem 4
.

For every integer
\Delta\geq 3\cdot 10^{9}
,
if
G
is a graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and clique number at most
\Delta-1
,
then
\chi_{DP}(G)\leq\Delta-1
.

This is equivalent to saying that for every graph
G
such that
\Delta(G)\geq 3\cdot 10^{9}
, if
\omega(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
, then
\chi_{DP}(G)\leq\Delta(G)-1
,
analogously to the statements of Theorems
2
and
3
; however, the formulation
from Theorem
4
is more convenient for the proof (since the graphs arising in the inductive argument can have maximum degree less than
\Delta(G)
).

Let us remark that the equality
\chi(G)=\chi_{\ell}(G)=\chi_{DP}(G)
clearly holds for all graphs
G
with
\omega(G)=\Delta(G)+1
.
More interestingly, the parameters also coincide for graphs
G
with
\omega(G)=\Delta(G)\geq 3
,
by the analogue of Brooks’ theorem for correspondence coloring (see
[
31
]
for a short proof).
We find the following open question to be interesting (Theorem
4
gives
\Delta_{0}\leq 3\cdot 10^{9}
).

Question 5
.

What is the smallest number
\Delta_{0}
such that every graph
G
with
\Delta(G)\geq\Delta_{0}
and
\omega(G)=\Delta(G)-1
satisfies
\chi(G)=\chi_{\ell}(G)=\chi_{DP}(G)
?

Let us now discuss the main ideas behind our proof of Theorem
4
.
At a high level, we proceed analogously to the proofs of Theorems
2
in
[
28
]
and
Theorem
3
in
[
10
]
.
We consider a hypothetical minimal counterexample
G
and an arbitrary
(\Delta-1)
-correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
,
and show that
G
is
(L,\Pi)
-colorable, thereby proving that no counterexample to Theorem
4
exists.

To this end, we first use reducible configuration arguments to obtain structural information about
G
. Most importantly, we show that for each clique
C
of size
\Delta-1
,
no vertex of
V(G)\setminus V(C)
has more than
85
neighbors in
C
. In this part, it is actually useful that we work in the correspondence coloring setting; the proof of Claim
39

exploits the fact that we can specify correspondences on edges which we add during a reduction. Thus, this structural result turns out to be somewhat stronger than
the corresponding lemma of Choi, Kierstead, and Rabern
[
10
]
.

The next part is essentially the same as in both
[
28
]
and
[
10
]
: We use the structural results to show that the graph
G

has a
sparse-dense decomposition
into large disjoint cliques (or more precisely,
near-cliques
whose non-edges are incident with a single vertex)
and
sparse
vertices with many non-edges among their neighbors.

The last part is a probabilistic argument, where we deviate substantially from both Reed
[
28
]
and Choi, Kierstead, and Rabern
[
10
]
.
In their proofs, this part was accomplished by an analysis of the
naive coloring procedure
: They first assign to each vertex
v\in V(G)
a color chosen uniformly independently at random from its list
L(v)
.
Of course, this coloring is unlikely to end up being proper. Hence, they then uncolor all vertices whose color conflicts with the color of at least one of their neighbors; let
\psi
denote the resulting partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
.
Using rather subtle “expected value and concentration” arguments,
one then shows that for each uncolored sparse vertex
v
, it is very likely that several of its neighbors retain colors that conflict with the same color
in
L(v)
. This saves a few colors in the list of
v
and shows that any partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring which extends
\psi
can be further greedily extended to
v
.
By similar (but even more technical) arguments, we can show that we also “save colors” in near-cliques of the sparse-dense decomposition, and thus this “greedy extendability”
property holds with high probability separately at each of the near-cliques.
Finally, one uses the Lovász Local Lemma to prove that these favourable circumstances occur at all sparse vertices and near-cliques at the same time
with non-zero probability; and thus we can greedily extend
\psi
to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of the whole graph
G
, by extending it to the sparse vertices and to the near-cliques of the decomposition
one by one.

It is possible to finish the argument in this way in the correspondence coloring setting as well (indeed, we have done so in an earlier version of the paper);
however, the resulting argument is very technical and leads to a rather bad lower bound on
\Delta
(somewhat worse than the one obtained in
[
10
]
).
Instead, we follow a simpler approach, inspired by
[
4
]
.

In our approach, we first fix an induced subgraph
H
of
G
such that for each vertex
v\in V(G)
, roughly
\Delta/5
neighbors of
v
belong to
H
and
roughly 4% of the non-edges among the neighbors of
v
have both endpoints in
H
, among several other similar properties. This is achieved simply by
choosing vertices independently at random with probability roughly
\tfrac{1}{5}
and applying standard concentration results and Lovász Local Lemma.
We then consider a
uniformly random

(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
of
H
, i.e., each of the
(L,\Pi)
-colorings of
H
is chosen as
\psi
with the same probability,
and apply the standard resampling argument to analyze this coloring.
Using the fact that the lists contain
\Delta-1\gg\Delta/5
colors,
it is fairly easy to show that the coloring
\psi
almost surely satisfies the “greedy extendability” property separately at each of the sparse vertices and near-cliques of the decomposition.

A small issue now is that the distant parts of the uniformly selected coloring
\psi
are not independent,
and thus we cannot directly apply the standard Lovász Local Lemma to conclude the argument.
However, it is easy to see that they are “independent enough” so that the lopsided version of Lovász Local Lemma (Theorem
14
) applies.

This approach is technically much simpler and completely avoids the use of more involved concentration arguments.
Moreover, it also gives a much better lower bound on
\Delta
. Indeed, our lower bound
3\cdot 10^{9}
is
better even than the bound given by Reed
[
28
]
for ordinary proper colorings. Of course, it should
be mentioned that Reed claims that a more careful analysis can bring the bound from his argument to
10^{6}
or even less;
but on the other hand, we have also not gone to any great lengths in optimizing our bound.

The rest of the paper is devoted to executing the outlined argument in detail. In the following section, we introduce the necessary technical definitions and statements.
In Section
3
, we argue that several reducible configurations cannot appear in a minimal counterexample.
In Section
4
, we use this to argue that a minimal counterexample admits a structural decomposition into precisely described “sparse” and “dense” parts.
Finally, in Section
5
, we use this structural information to prove that the probabilistic approach outlined above results in a correspondence coloring of the whole graph
with non-zero probability.

Definitions and Preliminaries

For a positive integer
n
, let
[n]
denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
For graphs, we generally use standard notation; let us point out a few less common or non-standard definitions.
For a graph
G
and vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, we write
u\sim_{G}u
if
u
is adjacent to
v
and
u\not\sim_{G}v
otherwise.
We let
N_{G}(v)
denote the set of neighbors of
v
and
\deg_{G}v
their number.
When the graph
G
is clear from the context, we drop the subscripts.
A vertex of
G
is
universal
if it is adjacent to all other vertices of
G
.
For another graph
H
, we write

G\cong H
to denote that
G
and
H
are isomorphic, and
H\leq G

to denote that
H
is isomorphic to an induced subgraph of
G
.
The
complement
of
G
, i.e., the graph with the same vertex set in which distinct vertices are adjacent if and only if they are non-adjacent in
G
, is denoted by
\overline{G}
.

For graphs
F
and
G
, let
F\vee G
denote the
complete join
of
G
and
F
, that is, the graph obtained
from the disjoint union of
F
and
G
by adding all edges between
V(F)
and
V(G)
.
Similarly, for a vertex
v\notin V(G)
, let
G\vee v
denote the graph obtained from
G
by adding
v

and edges from
v
to all vertices of
G
, i.e., so that
v
is a universal vertex of the resulting graph.

2.1
Correspondence coloring

Let us now introduce additional terminology, definitions, and concepts related to correspondence coloring.
Let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for a graph
G
. With a slight abuse of notation,
we also view
(L,\Pi)
as a correspondence assignment for subgraphs of
G
(by implicitly restricting
L

to the vertices and
\Pi
to the edges of the subgraph).
A
partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
is an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of an induced subgraph of
G
.

For an edge
e=uv
of
G
and colors
\alpha\in L(u)
and
\beta\in L(v)
, we say that
the color
\alpha
at
u
blocks the color
\beta
at
v
if
\{(u,\alpha),(v,\beta)\}\in\Pi(e)
.
When the vertex
u
is clear from the context, we drop the “at
u
” part. For example, if
\psi
is a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G

and a vertex
u
is assigned a color by
\psi
, then we say “
\psi(u)
blocks the color
\beta
at
v
” instead of “
\psi(u)
at
u
blocks the color
\beta
at
v
”.
In particular
\psi(u)
blocks no color at
v
means that the matching
\Pi(e)
between
\{u\}\times L(u)
and
\{v\}\times L(v)
contains no edge incident with
(u,\psi(u))
.
For another edge
e^{\prime}=u^{\prime}v\in E(G)
such that
\psi
also assigns a color to the vertex
u^{\prime}
, we say that
\psi(u)
and
\psi(u^{\prime})
together block at most one color at
v

if
\psi(u)
blocks no color at
v
, or
\psi(u^{\prime})
blocks no color at
v
, or both
\psi(u)
and
\psi(u^{\prime})
block the same color at
v
.

For a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
of a graph
G
, let
\text{dom}(\psi)
denote the set of vertices to which
\psi
assigns a color.
We define
(L_{\psi},\Pi_{\psi})
as the correspondence assignment for the induced subgraph
G-\text{dom}(\psi)
such that for each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\text{dom}(\psi)
,

L_{\psi}(v)=L(v)\setminus\{\alpha\in L(v):\text{for some vertex $u\in N(v)\cap\text{dom}(\psi)$, the color $\psi(u)$ blocks the color $\alpha$ at $v$}\}

(that is, we remove from the list of
v
the colors that conflict with the colors assigned by
\psi
to the neighbors of
v
),
and for every edge
e=uv\in E(G-\text{dom}(\psi))
,

\Pi_{\psi}(e)=\{\{(u,\alpha),(v,\beta)\}\in\Pi(e):\alpha\in L_{\psi}(u),\beta\in L_{\psi}(v)\}.

Given a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi^{\prime}
of
G
such that
\text{dom}(\psi)\cap\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})=\emptyset
, let
\psi\cup\psi^{\prime}

denote the function with domain
\text{dom}(\psi)\cup\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})
which matches
\psi
on
\text{dom}(\psi)
and
\psi^{\prime}
on
\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})
.
Note that
\psi\cup\psi^{\prime}
is not in general a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring, since the colors on vertices joined by edges between

\text{dom}(\psi)
and
\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})
may conflict. However, the correspondence assignment
(L_{\psi},\Pi_{\psi})
is defined so that the following observation holds.

Observation 6
.

Let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for a graph
G
and let
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
be partial
(L,\Pi)
-colorings of
G
such that
\text{dom}(\psi)\cap\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})=\emptyset
.
Then
\psi\cup\psi^{\prime}
is a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
if and only if
\psi^{\prime}
is a partial
(L_{\psi},\Pi_{\psi})
-coloring of
G-\text{dom}(\psi)
.

For a function
f\colon V(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, we say that
(L,\Pi)
is an
f
-correspondence assignment
if
|L(v)|\geq f(v)
holds for every
v\in V(G)
;
and that
G
is
f
-correspondence colorable
if it is
(L,\Pi)
-colorable for every
f
-correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
.
We remark that if
f
is the constant function assigning to each vertex the value
k
, then
f
-correspondence colorability coincides with
k
-correspondence colorability defined in the introduction.
We say that
(L,\Pi)
is an
exact
f
-correspondence assignment
if
|L(v)|=f(v)
holds for every
v\in V(G)
,
and an
exact
k
-correspondence assignment
if
|L(v)|=k
holds for every
v\in V(G)
.
Of course, to show that the graph
G
is
f
-correspondence colorable, it suffices to consider exact
f
-correspondence assignments,
since we can simply ignore arbitrarily chosen excess colors at vertices
v
for which
|L(v)|>f(v)
.

Throughout the paper, we often consider consider
\deg^{-}_{G}
-correspondence assignments, where
\deg^{-}_{G}
is the
function defined as
\deg^{-}_{G}(v)=\deg_{G}(v)-1
for every
v\in V(G)
.
As usual, when the graph
G
is clear from the context, we drop the subscript.
Note that a
(\Delta(G)-1)
-correspondence assignment is also a
\deg^{-}
-correspondence assignment, but not necessarily vice versa.
Observation

together with the following observation show that
\deg^{-}
-correspondence assignments are important
in the context of
(\Delta(G)-1)
-correspondence coloring.

Observation 7
.

Let
G
be a graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
, let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for
G
, and
let
\psi
be a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
.
If
(L,\Pi)
is a
\deg^{-}_{G}
-correspondence assignment (and in particular, if
(L,\Pi)
is a
(\Delta-1)
-correspondence assignment),
then
(L_{\psi},\Pi_{\psi})
is a
\deg^{-}_{G-\text{dom}(\psi)}
-correspondence assignment for
G-\text{dom}(\psi)
.

We often extend partial colorings greedily, by processing the not yet colored vertices one by one in a fixed order
and giving each of them an arbitrary color not blocked by colors of their previously colored neigbors.
Next, we give a few observations useful in showing that this procedure succeeds, i.e., that in each step the
list of the currently processed vertex contains at least one such unblocked color.
Let
\psi
be a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of a graph
G
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\text{dom}(\psi)
,
let

\deg^{-\psi}v=\deg_{G-\text{dom}(\psi)}v=\deg_{G}v-|N(v)\cap\text{dom}(\psi)|

denote the number of not yet colored neighbors of
v
.
We define the
\psi
-excess
of

as
\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)=|L_{\psi}(v)|-\deg^{-\psi}v
.
Note that the greedy coloring procedure used to extend
\psi
can never get stuck on a vertex

with positive
\psi
-excess:
Indeed, even if some of the
\deg^{-\psi}v
neighbors of
v
are given colors during the procedure before
v
,
each of them can block at most one color at
v
in addition to the colors blocked by
\psi
,
and thus at least
\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)>0
colors in
L(v)
remain unblocked. This argument also implies the following observation.

Observation 8
.

Let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for a graph
G
, let
\psi
be a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
,
and let
v
be a vertex not belonging to
\text{dom}(\psi)
. If
\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)>0
, then
\psi
extends to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G

if and only if
\psi
extends to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G-v
.

Note that deleting
v
as in this observation increases the
\psi
-excess of its neighbors by one,
which may make it possible to remove further vertices.
In particular, this has the following consequence, which we often apply in proving reducibility of configurations.

Observation 9
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for
G
, and let
\psi
be a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
.
Let
v_{1}
, …,
v_{n}
be an ordering of the vertices of
V(G)\setminus\text{dom}(\psi)
.
If for each
i\in[n]
the vertex
v_{i}
has at least
1-\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)
neighbors in
\{v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
,
then
\psi
extends (greedily) to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
.

Let us remark that if
(L,\Pi)
is a
\deg^{-}
-correspondence assignment, then
for every partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
, the
\psi
-excess of every vertex not in
\text{dom}(\psi)
is at least
-1
.
Thus, in this case the assumption for an index

is always satisfied if the vertex
v_{i}
has at least two neighbors in
\{v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.
In particular, in our applications of Observation
9
, the vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-2}
are often adjacent to both
v_{n-1}
and
v_{n}
.
In such cases, we only specify the last two vertices

and

of the ordering, as the vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-2}
can be ordered arbitrarily.

Furthermore, note that when
(L,\Pi)
is a
(\Delta(G)-1)
-correspondence assignment,
it may be easier to use the observation if

has some vertices of degree less than
\Delta(G)
. For example, if
\deg(y)\leq\Delta(G)-1
, then

y
has non-negative
\psi
-excess for every partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
with
y\not\in\text{dom}(\psi)
.

It is also useful to notice that extending
\psi
to some of the uncolored vertices cannot decrease the excess of the remaining uncolored vertices.
More precisely, if
\psi^{\prime}
is a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of

which extends
\psi
and

is a vertex not in
\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})
, then

\text{exc}_{\psi^{\prime}}(v)\geq\text{exc}_{\psi}(v).

Indeed, coloring a neighbor of

may block an additional color at
v
, but it also decreases the number of not yet colored neighbors of
v
.
By extending
\psi
carefully, we can even increase the excess of
v
.

Observation 10
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for
G
, and let
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
be partial
(L,\Pi)
-colorings of
G
,
where
\psi^{\prime}
extends
\psi
. Let
v
be a vertex of
G

\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})
.
If

•

u\in\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})\setminus\text{dom}(\psi)
is a neighbor of
v
and
\psi^{\prime}(u)
blocks no color in
L_{\psi}(v)
at
v
, or

•

u,u^{\prime}\in\text{dom}(\psi^{\prime})\setminus\text{dom}(\psi)
are distinct neighbors of
v
and
\psi(u)
and
\psi(u^{\prime})
together block at most one color in
L_{\psi}(v)
at
v
,

then
\text{exc}_{\psi^{\prime}}(v)>\text{exc}_{\psi(v)}
.

The following observation is often used to extend
\psi
to achieve the second option from Observation

.

Observation 11
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for
G
, and let
\psi
be a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring.
Let
v\in V(G)\setminus\text{dom}(\psi)
be a vertex, let
k
be an integer such that
\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)\geq k
, and let
u,u^{\prime}\in V(G)\setminus\text{dom}(\psi)
be distinct neighbors of
v
.
If
u\not\sim u^{\prime}
, the lists
L_{\psi}(u)
and
L_{\psi}(u^{\prime})
are non-empty, and
|L_{\psi}(u)|+|L_{\psi}(u^{\prime})|>\deg^{-\psi}v+k
, then
we can extend
\psi
to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi^{\prime}
of
G[\text{dom}(\psi)\cup\{u,u^{\prime}\}]
so that

\text{exc}_{\psi^{\prime}}(v)\geq k+1
.

Proof.

If
\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)>k
, then we can extend
\psi
to
\psi^{\prime}
by choosing the colors for
u
and
u^{\prime}
from their non-empty lists
L_{\psi}(u)
and
L_{\psi}(u^{\prime})
arbitrarily.
Hence,
\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)=k
, and thus
\deg^{-\psi}v+k=|L_{\psi}(v)|
.

If there exists a color
\alpha\in L_{\psi}(u)
that blocks no color in
L_{\psi}(v)
, then choose a color
\beta\in L_{\psi}(u^{\prime})

arbitrarily. Similarly, if a color
\beta\in L_{\psi}(u^{\prime})
blocks no color in
L_{\psi}(v)
, then choose a color
\alpha\in L_{\psi}(u)
arbitrarily.
Finally, if every color in
L_{\psi}(u)
blocks a color in
L_{\psi}(v)
and every color in
L_{\psi}(u^{\prime})
blocks a color in
L_{\psi}(v)
,
then since
|L_{\psi}(u)|+|L_{\psi}(u^{\prime})|>\deg^{-\psi}v+k=|L_{\psi}(v)|
, we can choose colors
\alpha\in L_{\psi}(u)
and
\beta\in L_{\psi}(u^{\prime})
that block the same color in
L_{\psi}(v)
.

In either case, the color
\alpha
at
u
and the color
\beta
at
u^{\prime}
together block at most one color in
L_{\psi}(v)
at
v
.
Therefore, we can let
\psi^{\prime}(u)=\alpha
and
\psi^{\prime}(u^{\prime})=\beta
, and the conclusion follows from Observation

.
∎

Finally, let us remark that in correspondence coloring, the names of the colors play no intrinsic role,
and thus we can rename the colors as needed. More precisely, for each vertex
v\in V(G)
, let
\sigma_{v}:\text{colors}\to\text{colors}

be any bijection, and let
\sigma
denote the system
\{\sigma_{v}:v\in V(G)\}
. Let
L^{\sigma}
be the list assignment giving to each vertex
v\in V(G)
the list
L^{\sigma}(v)=\{\sigma_{v}(\alpha):\alpha\in L(v)\}
.
Similarly, let
\Pi^{\sigma}
be the system of matchings assigning to each edge
e=uv\in E(G)

the matching
\Pi^{\sigma}(e)=\{\{(u,\sigma_{u}(\alpha)),(v,\sigma_{v}(\beta))\}:\{(u,\alpha),(v,\beta)\}\in\Pi(e)\}
.
We say that
(L^{\sigma},\Pi^{\sigma})
is the
\sigma
-renaming
of
(L,\Pi)
.
If
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
is the
\sigma
-renaming of
(L,\Pi)
for some system
\sigma
, we say that
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
is
obtained from
(L,\Pi)
by renaming colors
.
Moreover, if
A
is a subset of
V(G)
such that
\sigma_{u}
is the identity function for every vertex
u\in V(G)\setminus A
,
then we say that
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
is
obtained from
(L,\Pi)
by renaming colors on
A
.
Note that if
\varphi
is an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
, then
\sigma\circ\varphi
is an
(L^{\sigma},\Pi^{\sigma})
-coloring of
G
.
Moreover, the renaming is reversible. That is, let
\sigma^{-1}
denote the system
\{\sigma^{-1}_{v}:v\in V(G)\}
.
Then the
\sigma^{-1}
-renaming of a
\sigma
-renaming of
(L,\Pi)
is equal to
(L,\Pi)
.
These observations imply that the following claim holds.

Observation 12
.

Let
(L,\Pi)
and
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
be correspondence assignments for
G
.
If
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
is obtained from
(L,\Pi)
by renaming colors, then
G
is
(L,\Pi)
-colorable if and only if it is
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
-colorable.

For example, if all lists in
(L,\Pi)
have the same size, then we can rename the colors so that all lists are identical.
More importantly, we can use the renaming to simplify our mental image of a correspondence assignment.
An edge
e=uv
of
G
is said to be
straight
in a correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
if
every pair
\{(u,\alpha),(v,\beta)\}\in\Pi(e)
satisfies
\alpha=\beta
, i.e., if the conflicts
on
e
form a subset of the conflicts for the usual proper coloring.
Of course, by renaming colors at the vertex
v
, we can ensure that the edge
e
is straight in the resulting correspondence assignment.
This renaming can only affect the straightness of the rest of the edges incident with
v
.
Thus, if
T\subseteq G
is a tree, then we can rename the colors at vertices of
T
in order according to the distance
from a fixed root of
T
to ensure that all edges of
T
are straight
(in this case, we say that the subgraph
T
is
straight)
. Thus, we get the following useful observation.

Observation 13
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
T\subseteq G
be a tree and let
v_{0}
be a vertex of
T
. For every correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
for
G
,
there exists a correspondence assignment
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
obtained from
(L,\Pi)
by renaming the colors on
V(T)\setminus\{v_{0}\}

such that
T
is straight in
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
. Moreover, for any set
\Gamma\supseteq L(v_{0})
of size
\max\{|L(v)|:v\in V(T)\}
,
we can assume that
L(v)\subseteq\Gamma
for every vertex
v\in V(T)
.

Finally, let us introduce one more property of exact correspondence assignments. We say that an exact
k
-correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
for a graph
G
is
full

if for each edge
e=uv\in E(G)
, the matching
\Pi(e)
is perfect (pairs each element of
\{u\}\times L(u)
with exactly one element of
\{v\}\times L(v)
). To show that
a graph is
k
-correspondence colorable, it clearly suffices to consider full exact
k
-correspondence assignments, since adding extra pairs to
the matchings on edges only makes it harder to color the graph.

2.2
Probabilistic tools

We use several standard results from probability theory, which can be found e.g. in
[
2
,
27
]
.
In particular, we use the following lopsided version of Lovász Local Lemma.

Theorem 14

(Lovász Local Lemma)
.

Consider a set
\mathcal{E}
of (bad) events and let
d
be a positive integer.
Suppose that for every event
A\in\mathcal{E}
, there exists a set
\mathcal{E}_{A}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
of size at most
d

such that for every
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{A}
,

\textup{{Pr}}\left[A\Bigm|\bigwedge_{A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\lnot A^{\prime}\right]\leq\frac{1}{4d}.

Then
\textup{{Pr}}\left[\bigwedge_{A\in\mathcal{E}}\lnot A\right]>0
.

We use the following Chernoff Bounds.

Theorem 15

(Chernoff Bounds)
.

Let
X
be a sum of independent Bernoulli variables.

•

For any non-negative real numbers
\lambda
and
t
such that
\mathbb{E}[X]\leq\lambda
,

\textup{{Pr}}\left[X\geq\lambda+t\right]\leq\exp\Bigl(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\lambda+t}\Bigr).

•

For any non-negative real numbers
\eta
and
t
such that
t\leq\eta\leq\mathbb{E}[X]
,

\textup{{Pr}}\left[X\leq\eta-t\right]\leq\exp\Bigl(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\eta}\Bigr).

Also, at one spot, we need the following more general concentration result.

Theorem 16

(McDiarmid’s Inequality)
.

Let
X
be a random variable determined by
n
independent trials
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
,
where for every
i\in[n]
, changing the outcome of
T_{i}
can affect
X
by at most
c_{i}
.
For every non-negative real number
t
,

\textup{{Pr}}\left[X<\mathbb{E}[X]-t\right]\leq\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}c^{2}_{i}}\Bigr).

We also work with uniformly random correspondence colorings, where each of the possible
(L,\Pi)
-colorings of the considered graph
G

is picked with the same probability. The basic tool to argue about properties of such uniform colorings is
resampling
. Consider a function
f
that
to each
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
of
G
assigns a subset
f(\psi)
of
V(G)
; we call such a function a
subset selector
.
For a set
S\subseteq V(G)
, an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi^{\prime}
is an
S
-modification
of
\psi
if
\psi^{\prime}(v)=\psi(v)
holds for every
v\in V(G)\setminus S
.
An
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi^{\prime\prime}
is an
f
-invariant modification

of
\psi
if
\psi^{\prime\prime}
is an
f(\psi)
-modification of
\psi
such that
f(\psi^{\prime\prime})=f(\psi)
;
that is, we change the coloring only on the vertices of the set
f(\psi)
, but we are careful not to change the value of
f
.
An
f
-invariant resampling
of
\psi
is an
f
-invariant modification of
\psi
chosen uniformly at random,
i.e., each of the
f
-invariant modifications of
\psi
is picked with the same probability.

By the following observation,
f
-invariant resampling preserves the uniformity of a random
(L,\Pi)
-coloring;
thus, to show that a uniform
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
of
G
has some property with high probability, we can
instead argue that an
f
-invariant resampling of
\psi
does.
This observation can also be seen as a consequence of the so-called
spatial Markov property
for the hard-core model, see e.g.
[
14
]
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for
G
, and let
f
be a subset selector.
Let
\psi
be a uniformly random
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
, and let
\psi^{\prime}
be an
f
-invariant resampling of
\psi
.
Then
\psi^{\prime}
is also a uniformly random
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
.

Proof.

Let
n
be the number of
(L,\Pi)
-colorings of
G
, and for each set
S\subseteq V(G)
and every
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\sigma
of
G-S
,
let
n_{S,\sigma}
be the number of
(L,\Pi)
-colorings
\varphi
of
G
such that
f(\varphi)=S
and
\varphi
extends
\sigma
.

Consider a fixed
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\varphi
of
G
, let
S=f(\varphi)
, and let
\sigma
be the restriction of
\varphi
to
V(G)\setminus S
.
For the
f
-invariant resampling
\psi^{\prime}
of
\psi
to be equal to
\varphi
, we must have

•

f(\psi)=S
and
\psi
must extend
\sigma
,

which happens with probability
\frac{n_{S,\sigma}}{n}
, and furthermore

•

\psi^{\prime}
must be chosen to be
\varphi
from all
n_{S,\sigma}
colorings with these properties,

which happens with probability
\tfrac{1}{n_{S,\sigma}}
. Thus,

\textup{{Pr}}\left[\psi^{\prime}=\varphi\right]=\frac{n_{S,\sigma}}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{n_{S,\sigma}}=\frac{1}{n}.

Since this holds for every
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\varphi
of
G
, we conclude that
\psi^{\prime}
is a uniformly random
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
.
∎

3
Forbidden induced subgraphs

For a positive integer
\Delta
, a
\Delta
-counterexample
is a triple
(G,L,\Pi)
,
where
G
is a graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and clique number at most
\Delta-1

and
(L,\Pi)
is a full exact
(\Delta-1)
-correspondence assignment for
G
such that
G
is not
(L,\Pi)
-colorable.
We aim to prove Theorem
4
by showing that
no
\Delta
-counterexamples exist for any sufficiently large
\Delta
.
For contradiction, we suppose that a
\Delta
-counterexample exists, and we consider a
minimal

i.e., one with
|V(G)|
smallest possible. In this section, we restrict the structure of minimal
\Delta
-counterexamples
by finding a number of
reducible configurations
, induced subgraphs that cannot appear in a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample,
and using them to argue about the neighborhoods of large cliques.
Let us start with two simple observations.

Observation 18
.

For any positive integer
\Delta
, if
(G,L,\Pi)
is a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample, then
\delta(G)\geq\Delta-1
.

Proof.

If
G
contained a vertex of degree at most
\Delta-2
, then note that
G-v
would have an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring by the minimality
of the
\Delta
-counterexample, and that this coloring could be extended greedily to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
,
since
|L(v)|\geq\Delta-1>\deg v
.
∎

Observation 19
.

For any positive integer
\Delta
, if
(G,L,\Pi)
is a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample, then no induced subgraph
H
of

is
\deg^{-}_{H}
-correspondence colorable.

Proof.

Since
(G,L,\Pi)
is a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample, there exists an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
of

.
Since
\psi
does not extend to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of

, Observation
6
implies that

is not
(L_{\psi},\Pi_{\psi})
-colorable.
Since
(L_{\psi},\Pi_{\psi})
is a
\deg^{-}_{H}
-correspondence assignment by Observation
7
,
the induced subgraph

is not
\deg^{-}_{H}
-correspondence colorable.
∎

Given Observation
19
, our focus now will be on showing that specific graphs are
\deg^{-}
-correspondence
colorable. Let us remark that, with the exception of Lemma

, the analogous results are known to be true in the list coloring setting

]
,
and our proofs of these results are essentially just reformulations to the correspondence coloring setting.

3.1

We first aim to show that for any graph

We now need a simple observation based on the presence of the induced subgraphs considered in the preceding lemmas.

Observation 24
.

If a graph
G
does not contain any induced subgraph isomorphic to
\overline{K_{3}},2K_{2},P_{4}
, or
C_{4}
, then

\omega(G)\geq|V(G)|-1
.

Proof.

Since
P_{4}\not\leq G
, it follows that
G
is a
cograph
, i.e.,
G
is obtained by iterated complete joins and disjoint unions from single-vertex graphs.

We prove the claim by induction on
|V(G)|
. If
|V(G)|=1
, then
\omega(G)=|V(G)|
. Otherwise,
G
is the disjoint union or complete join of smaller
graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
.

•

If
G
is the disjoint union of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, then since
\overline{K_{3}}\not\leq G
, both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
must be cliques.
Moreover, since
2K_{2}\not\leq G
, one of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
must be a single vertex. Thus,
\omega(G)=\max(|V(G_{1})|,|V(G_{2})|)=|V(G)|-1
.

•

If
G
is the complete join of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, then since
C_{4}\not\leq G
, we can by symmetry assume that
G_{2}
is a clique.
Note that
G_{1}
is an induced subgraph of
G
, and thus
\overline{K_{3}},2K_{2},P_{4},C_{4}\not\leq G_{1}
.
By the induction hypothesis, we have
\omega(G_{1})\geq|V(G_{1})|-1
, and thus

\omega(G)=\omega(G_{1})+|V(G_{2})|\geq|V(G)|-1
. ∎

Lemma 25

Lemma 26

(L,\Pi)

\deg^{-}
-correspondence assignment for

\delta(G)-(|R|+1)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(n+a+4)-(a-1)=\tfrac{1}{2}(n-a+6)=\tfrac{1}{2}(|V(K)|+6).

has an

-coloring, which combines with

3.2
Vertex neighborhoods

Let us now consider graphs with at least one universal vertex.
These will be quite useful in the proof of Theorem
4
,
as using them we can restrict subgraphs induced by closed neighborhoods of vertices in a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample.
Let us start by considering the situation when this neighborhood contains three disjoint non-edges.

Lemma 27
.

Let
B
be a graph of minimum degree at least
\tfrac{1}{2}(|V(B)|+5)
.

contains a matching of size three, then the graph

is
\deg^{-}
-correspondence colorable.

Proof.

Suppose that

,
x^{\prime}
,

,
y^{\prime}
,

, and

are distinct vertices in

such that

,

, and

.
Let

.
Note that any distinct non-adjacent vertices

and

of

have at least

\deg_{B}a+\deg_{B}b-(n-2)\geq 2\delta(B)-n+2\geq 7

common neighbors in

is connected.
Moreover, by considering

and

, we conclude that the vertices

and

have a common neighbor

in

different from

,
y^{\prime}
,

, and

Let
(L,\Pi)
be an exact

-correspondence assignment for the graph

.
Note that

and

|L(x)|+|L(x^{\prime})|\geq 2\delta(B)>n-1.

Hence, by Observation
11
(with the empty precoloring) we can choose colors for

and

, obtaining
a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring

such that

-excess.

Note that

, and

By Observation

, we can extend

to a partial

-coloring

by choosing colors for

and

implies that we can extend

By combining Lemmas

and

Lemma 28
.

Let

be a graph with
\delta(B)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(|V(B)|+7)

, then the graph

-correspondence colorable.

Let

be the maximum size of a matching in
\overline{B}
.
If

, then the conclusion follows by Lemma

,
and thus assume that

\omega(B)=\alpha(\overline{B})\geq|V(B)|-2\mu\geq|V(B)|-4

This leads us to the first key result of this section, showing that in a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample, the neighborhood of each vertex contains either a large
clique or many non-edges.

Theorem 29
.

Let
H
be a graph with
n
vertices, where
H
is not a clique, and let
v
be a vertex not belonging to
H
.
Suppose that for every induced subgraph
B
of
H
, the graph
B\vee v
is not
\deg^{-}
-correspondence colorable.
Then
H
has at least

\frac{(n-\omega(H)-1)(n+\omega(H)-14)}{4}

non-edges.

Proof.

Since
H
is not a clique, we have
\omega(H)\leq n-1
.
Let
H_{1}=H
, and for
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
v_{i}
be a vertex of minimum degree in
H_{i}
and let
H_{i+1}=H_{i}-v_{i}
.
Consider any positive integer
i\leq n-\omega(H)-1
. The graph
H_{i}
has
n-i+1\geq\omega(H)+2\geq\omega(H_{i})+2
vertices.
Since the graph
H_{i}\vee v
is not
\deg^{-}
-correspondence colorable, Lemma
28
implies that

\deg_{H_{i}}v_{i}=\delta(H_{i})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(|V(H_{i})|+6)
. Consequently, the number of non-edges of
H_{i}
incident with
v_{i}
is

|V(H_{i})|-\deg_{H_{i}}v_{i}-1\geq|V(H_{i})|-\frac{|V(H_{i})|+6}{2}-1=\frac{|V(H_{i})|-8}{2}=\frac{n-i-7}{2}.

Therefore, the total number of non-edges of
H
is at least

\sum_{i=1}^{n-\omega(H)-1}\frac{n-i-7}{2}=\frac{(n-\omega(H)-1)(n+\omega(H)-14)}{4}.\qed

Next, we study neighborhoods of large cliques (or more precisely,

and

.

be distinct vertices in

. Let
y\in V(C)\setminus\{v,u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}
be a neighbor of
u_{1}
,
which exists since
u_{1}
has at least five neighbors in
C
.

Let
(L,\Pi)
be an exact
\deg^{-}
-correspondence assignment for the graph
H
.
Since
|L(x_{1})|\geq|L(u_{1})|+2
and
|L(x_{2})|\geq|L(u_{1})|+2
, we can choose colors for
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
and obtain
a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi_{1}
of
H
such that
\psi_{1}(x_{1})
and
\psi_{1}(x_{2})
do not block any colors at
u_{1}
.
Consequently,
u_{1}
has
\psi_{1}
-excess
1
.

Note that
\text{exc}_{\psi_{1}}(v)\geq-1
,
\deg^{-\psi_{1}}v=n-3
,

|L_{\psi_{1}}(u_{2})|=|L(u_{2})|=\deg u_{2}-1\geq|U_{2}\cup\{v\}|-1\geq 3,

and

|L_{\psi_{1}}(x_{3})|+|L_{\psi_{1}}(u_{2})|\geq(n-5)+3>n-4.

By Observation
11
, we can extend
\psi_{1}
to a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
by choosing colors for
x_{3}
and
u_{2}

so that that

has a non-negative
\psi
-excess.

We can now extend

to

greedily using Observation

, with the vertices

,

, and

∎

A set
D
of vertices of a graph
G
is called a
near-clique
if
\omega(G[D])\geq|D|-1
.
A subset
C\subseteq D
of size
\omega(G[D])
inducing a clique in
G
is a
core
of the near-clique (the core is unique unless
G[D]
has exactly one non-edge).
For a positive integer
\Delta
, the set
D
is
\Delta
-isolated
in
G
if every vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus D
has at most
\lceil\tfrac{3}{4}\Delta\rceil
neighbors in
D
.
In Section
4
, we show that each minimal
\Delta
-counterexample admits a sparse-dense decomposition, where the dense parts of this decomposition
are formed by
\Delta
-isolated near-cliques. Thus, we need to understand how such near-cliques interact with the rest of the graph.
We often use the following simple observation.

Observation 31
.

Let
\Delta
be a positive integer, let
D
be a
\Delta
-isolated near-clique in a graph
G
, let
C
be a core of
D
, and let
p=\Delta-|C|
.
Then the vertex in
D\setminus C
(if any) has at least one non-neighbor in
C
, and every vertex in
V(G)\setminus D
has
at least
\tfrac{\Delta-3}{4}-p
non-neighbors in
C
.

For a positive integer
m
, a vertex
u\in V(G)\setminus C
is
(C,m)
-loose
if
u
has at most
m
neighbors in
C
, and
(C,m)
-tight
otherwise.

Lemma 32
.

Let
\Delta
be a positive integer and let
(G,L,\Pi)
be a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample.
Let
D
be a
\Delta
-isolated near-clique in
G
, let
C
be a core of
D
, and let
p=\Delta-|C|
.
If
\Delta\geq 4p+8
, then every vertex
v\in C
has at most one
(C,7)
-tight neighbor.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
v
has (at least) two distinct
(C,7)
-tight neighbors
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
.
By Observation
31
,
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
each have a non-neighbor in
C
,
and since
\Delta\geq 4p+8
, at least one of them (one not belonging to
D
) has more than one non-neighbor in
C
.
Thus, we can select distinct vertices
u^{\prime}_{1},u^{\prime}_{2}\in C
such that
u_{1}\not\sim u^{\prime}_{1}
and
u_{2}\not\sim u^{\prime}_{2}
.

Let
M
be the set of common neighbors of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
in
C
(including the vertex
v
),
and consider the subgraph
H
of
G
induced by
M\cup\{u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1},u_{2},u^{\prime}_{2}\}
. Since
u_{1}\not\sim u^{\prime}_{1}
and
u_{2}\not\sim u^{\prime}_{2}
,
we have
\omega(H)\leq|V(H)|-2
. By Observation
19
, the induced subgraph
H
is not
\deg^{-}_{H}
-correspondence colorable,
and since the vertices of
M
are universal in
H
, Lemma
25
implies
|M|\leq 5
.

For
i\in[2]
, let
U_{i}
be the set of vertices of
C
adjacent to
u_{i}
and non-adjacent to
u_{3-i}
.
Since the vertices
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are
(C,7)
-tight and
|M|\leq 5
, we have
|U_{1}|,|U_{2}|\geq 3
.
However, then the induced subgraph
H^{\prime}=G[C\cup\{u_{1},u_{2}\}]
is
\deg^{-}_{H^{\prime}}
-correspondence colorable by Lemma
30
,
contradicting Observation
19
.
∎

In the probabilistic part of the argument, the vertices
v\in C
with at least
2+\deg v-\Delta

(C,7)
-loose neighbors
(i.e., with at least two
(C,7)
-loose neighbors if
\deg v=\Delta
, and with at least one
(C,7)
-loose neighbor if
\deg v=\Delta-1
)
have a reasonable probability of ending up with a positive excess. Indeed, it turns out that each of their
(C,7)
-loose neighbors has a decent chance
of belonging to the colored subgraph and blocking at
v
the same color as some vertex of
C
. If this occurs for at least two vertices
v\in C
, then we can extend the partial coloring to
C
greedily.
However, Lemma
32
only ensures that
v
has
at least
p+\deg v-\Delta

(C,7)
-loose neighbors. This is not enough when
p=1
, i.e., the core
C
has size
\Delta-1
.
Thus, we need a stronger result to deal with this special case.

To prove this stronger result, it will be necessary to employ a stronger reduction procedure, where we not only delete some of the vertices
of a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample, but also add a few edges. However, we need to be a bit careful not to create a clique of size
\Delta
by this addition.
Let
G
be a graph such that
\omega(G)\leq\Delta-1
. We say that a vertex
v^{\prime}
of
G
is a
\Delta
-troublemaker
for another vertex
v

if
G
contains a clique of size
\Delta-2
whose vertices are common neighbors of
v
and
v^{\prime}
.
Note that since
\omega(G)\leq\Delta-1
, we have
v\not\sim v^{\prime}
; and in order to avoid increasing the clique number of
G
to
\Delta
, we need to avoid adding edges between
\Delta
-troublemakers.
To aid us with this, let us show the following results on inclusionwise-maximal cliques in minimal
\Delta
-counterexamples, which we are also going to need in Section
4
.
For a real number
r
, let
\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
be the system of all inclusionwise-maximal cliques in
G
whose size is at least
r
.

Lemma 33
.

Let
\Delta
be a positive integer, let
r=\tfrac{3}{4}\Delta+1
, and let
(G,L,\Pi)
be a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample.
For all distinct cliques
A,B\in\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
, if
V(A)\cap V(B)\neq\emptyset
, then
|V(A\cap B)|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta+1
.

Proof.

For a vertex
v\in V(A\cap B)
, we have

\Delta\geq\deg v\geq|V(A\cup B)|-1=|V(A)|+|V(B)|-|V(A\cap B)|-1\geq\tfrac{3}{2}\Delta+1-|V(A\cap B)|,

and thus
|V(A\cap B)|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta+1
.
∎

Lemma 34
.

Let
\Delta\geq 20
be an integer, let
r=\tfrac{3}{4}\Delta+1
, and let
(G,L,\Pi)
be a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample.
Then every clique in
\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
intersects at most one other clique in
\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that a clique
C\in\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
intersects two distinct cliques
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
. By Lemma
33
, we
have
|V(C\cap D)|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta+1
and
|V(C\cap D^{\prime})|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta+1
. Since
|V(C)|\leq\Delta-1
, this implies that
V(C\cap D\cap D^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.
Let
v
be any vertex of
C\cap D\cap D^{\prime}
, let
B=G[(C\cup D\cup D^{\prime})\setminus\{v\}]
and let
H=B\vee v=G[C\cup D\cup D^{\prime}]
.

Note that
\omega(H)\leq|V(H)|-2
, and thus also
\omega(B)\leq|V(B)|-2
.
Indeed, if we had
\omega(H)\geq|V(H)|-1
, then
H
would be obtained from a clique by deleting some of the edges incident with one of its vertices.
However, then
H\leq G
would contain at most two inclusionwise-maximal cliques, which is not possible since
C,D,D^{\prime}\subseteq H
are three distinct inclusionwise-maximal cliques in
G
.

Since the vertex
v
is universal in
H
and each vertex of
H
belongs to a clique of size at least
r
, we have
|V(H)|\leq\Delta+1
and
\delta(H)\geq r-1\geq\frac{3}{4}\Delta
.
Consequently,
|V(B)|\leq\Delta
and
\delta(B)\geq\frac{3}{4}\Delta-1\geq\frac{1}{2}(|V(B)|+7)
.
Therefore, by Lemma
28
, the induced subgraph
H
of
G
is
\deg^{-}_{H}
-correspondence colorable; this contradicts Observation
19
.
∎

The following claim then easily follows.

Corollary 35
.

Let
\Delta\geq 20
be an integer. If
(G,L,\Pi)
is a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample, then the following claims hold.

•

There is at most one
\Delta
-troublemaker for every vertex of
G
.

•

Let
v_{1,1},v_{1,2},v_{2,1},v_{2,2}\in V(G)
be distinct vertices such that
v_{1,1}\not\sim v_{1,2}
and
v_{2,1}\not\sim v_{2,2}
.
If
v_{1,1}
is not a
\Delta
-troublemaker for
v_{1,2}
and
v_{2,1}
is not a
\Delta
-troublemaker for
v_{2,2}
,
then
\omega(G+\{v_{1,1}v_{1,2},v_{2,1}v_{2,2}\})\leq\Delta-1
.

Proof.

Let
r=\tfrac{3}{4}\Delta+1
and note that every clique of size
\Delta-1
in
G
is contained in
\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
.

For the first part, suppose for a contradiction that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are two distinct
\Delta
-troublemakers for a vertex
v\in V(G)
.
For
i\in[2]
, let
K_{i}\subseteq G
be a clique on
\Delta-2
common neighbors of
v
and
v_{i}
.
Since
\deg v\leq\Delta<2(\Delta-2)
, the cliques
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
intersect. Moreover, since
v_{1}\not\sim v
and
v_{2}\not\sim v
,
we have
v_{1},v_{2}\not\in V(K_{1}\cup K_{2})
. Thus, the clique
K_{1}\vee v\in\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)

intersects distinct cliques
K_{1}\vee v_{1},K_{2}\vee v_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
, contradicting Lemma
34
.

For the second part, suppose for a contradiction that
G+\{v_{1,1}v_{1,2},v_{2,1}v_{2,2}\}
contains a clique
K
of size
\Delta
. Since
v_{1,1}
is not a
\Delta
-troublemaker
for
v_{1,2}
and
v_{2,1}
is not a
\Delta
-troublemaker for
v_{2,2}
, we necessarily have
v_{1,1},v_{1,2},v_{2,1},v_{2,2}\in V(K)
. For
i,j\in\{1,2\}
,
let
K_{i,j}
be a clique in
G
containing
v_{1,i}
and
v_{2,j}
of largest possible size; clearly,
|V(K_{i,j})|\geq|V(K)|-2=\Delta-2\geq r
,
and thus
K_{i,j}\in\mathcal{K}_{r}(G)
. Moreover, the four cliques
K_{1,1}
,
K_{1,2}
,
K_{2,1}
, and
K_{2,2}
distinct,
since
v_{1,1}\not\sim v_{1,2}
and
v_{2,1}\not\sim v_{2,2}
. However, the cliques
K_{1,2}
and
K_{2,1}
both intersect the clique
K_{1,1}
, contradicting Lemma
34
.
∎

As a final preparatory step, we need the following observation on correspondence coloring of cliques.

Lemma 36
.

Let
H
be a clique of size
k\geq 1
and let
(L,\Pi)
be a
(k-1)
-correspondence assignment for
H
.
If
H
is
(L,\Pi)
-colorable, then at least one of the following claims holds:

(a)

There exists a vertex
v\in V(H)
such that
|L(v)|\geq k
,

(b)

there exist distinct vertices
z,v\in V(H)
and a color
\alpha\in L(z)
that does not block any color in
L(v)
at
v
, or

(c)

there exist distinct vertices
z_{1},z_{2},v\in V(H)
and colors
\alpha_{1}\in L(z_{1})
and
\alpha_{2}\in L(z_{2})

such that
\{(z_{1},\alpha_{1}),(z_{2},\alpha_{2})\}\not\in\Pi(z_{1}z_{2})
and the colors
\alpha_{1}
at
z_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
at
z_{2}
together block at most one
color in
L(v)
at
v
.

Proof.

Suppose that neither (a) nor (b) holds, and thus
(L,\Pi)
is a full exact
(k-1)
-correspondence assignment.
Let
v
be an arbitrary vertex of
H
and let
T
be the star with the vertex set
V(H)
and the center
v
.
By Observation
13
, we can assume that all vertices have the same list
\Gamma
and that
T
is straight in the correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
.

By the assumptions,
H
has an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\varphi
. Since
|\Gamma|=k-1<|V(H)|
,
there exist distinct vertices
z_{1},z_{2}\in V(H)
such that
\varphi(z_{1})=\varphi(z_{2})
.
Since the edges incident with
v
are straight and the correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
is full, we have
z_{1}\neq v\neq z_{2}
;
and moreover, the colors
\varphi(z_{1})
and
\varphi(z_{2})
together block only one color at
v
, showing that (c) holds.
∎

We are now ready to restrict the neighborhoods in cores of size
\Delta-1
.

Lemma 37
.

Let
\Delta\geq 20
be an integer and let
(G,L,\Pi)
be a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample.
Let
D
be a
\Delta
-isolated near-clique in
G
and let
C
be a core of
D
.
If
|C|=\Delta-1
, then every vertex
u\in V(G)\setminus C
has at most
85
neighbors in
C
.

Proof.

Let
U
be the set of neighbors of
u
in
C
, and for contradiction suppose that
|U|\geq 86
.
Since
\omega(G)\leq\Delta-1
, the vertex
u
has a non-neighbor
u^{\prime}
in
C
.

Claim
38
.

At most one vertex of
U
has degree
\Delta-1
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose for a contradiction that distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in U
have degree
\Delta-1
.
Since
(G,L,\Pi)
is a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample, there exists a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi_{1}
of
G

assigning colors to all vertices not in
C\cup\{u\}
. Note that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
have
\psi_{1}
-excess at least
0
.
Moreover,
\deg^{-\psi_{1}}v_{2}=|C|
,
|L_{\psi_{1}}(u^{\prime})|\geq\deg^{-\psi_{1}}u^{\prime}-1=|C|-2
, and
|L_{\psi_{1}}(u)|\geq|U|-1
,
and thus

|L_{\psi_{1}}(u^{\prime})|+|L_{\psi_{1}}(u)|>\deg^{-\psi_{1}}v_{2}.

By Observation
11
, we can extend
\psi_{1}
to a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi

by choosing colors for
u
and
u^{\prime}
so that the
\psi
-excess of

is positive.
By Observation

, we can extend

to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of

, coloring

and
v_{2}
last.
This is a contradiction.
∎

Let
S
be the set of vertices in

U
.
Since
|C|=\Delta-1
and each vertex of

of degree

has exactly one neighbor in

.
On the other hand, since the vertex

neighbors in

. Thus, by Claim

, we have

For each vertex

, choose a neighbor

has at most one neighbor in

,

\{ss^{\prime}:s\in S\}
is a matching, and

is the unique neighbor of

in

. Let

.
Let

be the auxiliary graph obtained from

by adding an edge between any distinct vertices

such that

is a
\Delta
-troublemaker for

.

Claim

.

The complement of

does not contain any matching of size two.

Proof of claim.

Suppose for a contradiction that
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in S
are distinct vertices such that

and

.
Let

be the tree consisting of the path

and the edges

for

By Observation

, we can assume that

is straight in the correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
,
and moreover, all the vertices of

have the same list
\Gamma
of size
\Delta-1
.

Let

be the graph obtained from

by adding the edges

and
x_{3}x_{4}

Let
(L,\Pi^{\prime})

,

\psi(x_{1})\in L_{\psi}(x^{\prime}_{2})

and the vertex

\psi(x_{3})\in L_{\psi}(x^{\prime}_{4})

\psi(x_{1})

\psi(x_{3})
at

(1)

By Observation

, we can extend

at

at

.
Symmetrically, we can also assume that for every

.

and

5.2

Let
\Delta
be a positive integer and let
G
be a graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
.
Let
(L,\Pi)
be a correspondence assignment for

be a set of vertices of
G
,
and let

be an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of

Let

between the vertices of

A non-edge

-monochromatic

Our first goal will be to show that, under a uniformly random choice of

Our plan for proving this is to perform resampling (see Lemma

.

As a further complication, we need to show that the claim holds somewhat independently of what happens in the rest of the graph,

(\Delta,M)
-saves

-coloring of

-fails

-coloring

such that

\textup{{Pr}}\left[\text{$\psi$ does not $(\Delta,M)$-save $v$}\mid\text{$\psi$ extends $\sigma$}\right]\leq q.

(L_{C,\mathcal{W}^{\prime},Y,\varphi},\Pi_{C,\mathcal{W}^{\prime},Y,\varphi})
as follows.
We start from the correspondence assignment
(L,\Pi)
, and for each set
X\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
and each vertex
b\in f_{C,\mathcal{W}^{\prime},Y}(\varphi)
belonging to the

(C,\varphi)
-witness protection zone for
X
, we remove all (at most two)
(C,\varphi,X)
-witness-forbidden colors at
b
from the list of
b
.
When a color is removed, we also remove the incident correspondences.

Lemma 50
.

Let
\Delta
be a positive integer, let
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},\{\mathcal{W}_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}})
be a setting for a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample
(G,L,\Pi)
,
let
S
be a transversal for this setting, and let
\varphi
be an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G[S]
.
Consider a core
C\in\mathcal{C}
, a set
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{C}
containing only elements which are
C
-bisected by
S
,
and another such element
Y\in\mathcal{W}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
, and let
f=f_{C,\mathcal{W}^{\prime},Y}
and
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})=(L_{C,\mathcal{W}^{\prime},Y,\varphi},\Pi_{C,\mathcal{W}^{\prime},Y,\varphi})
.
An
f(\varphi)
-modification
\varphi^{\prime}
of
\varphi
is
f
-invariant if and only if
\varphi^{\prime}
is an
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
-coloring.

Proof.

Let us fix an
f(\varphi)
-modification
\varphi^{\prime}
of
\varphi
, and
suppose first that
\varphi^{\prime}
is an
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
-coloring. Consider any set
X\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
.

•

If
X
is
(C,\varphi)
-successful, then this is ensured already by the colors of vertices in the
(C,\varphi)
-witness
A_{X}\subseteq(C\cap S)\setminus f(\varphi)
,
and the colors of these vertices are the same in
\varphi^{\prime}
.
Thus,
X
is also
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-sucessful. Moreover, the choice of
L^{\prime}
ensures that no vertex
b\in(C\cap S)\setminus A_{X}
such that
b\prec\max_{\prec}A_{X}

is a
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-helper, and thus
A_{X}
is also the
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-witness.

•

If
X
is not
(C,\varphi)
-successful, then the
(C,\varphi)
-witness
A_{X}\subseteq(C\cap S)\setminus f(\varphi)
contains all
(C,\varphi)
-helpers for
X
.
The coloring
\varphi^{\prime}
assigns the same colors to these vertices, and thus they are also
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-helpers.
Moreover, the choice of
L^{\prime}
ensures that no vertex in
(C\cap S)\setminus A_{X}
is a
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-helper; consequently,
A_{X}
is also the
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-witness.

Since all elements of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
have their
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-witness equal to their
(C,\varphi)
-witness, it follows that
f(\varphi^{\prime})=f(\varphi)
, and thus

\varphi^{\prime}
is an
f
-invariant modification of
\varphi
.

Conversely, assume that
\varphi^{\prime}
is an
f
-invariant modification of
\varphi
, and suppose for a contradiction that
\varphi^{\prime}
is not an
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
-coloring.
Let
b\in C\cap S
be the smallest vertex (in the ordering
\prec
) such that
\varphi^{\prime}(b)\not\in L^{\prime}(b)
.
Since
L^{\prime}(b)\neq L(b)
, we have
b\in f(\varphi)
; and moreover, since
\varphi^{\prime}(b)\not\in L^{\prime}(b)
,
there exists a set
X\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
such that
b
is in the
(C,\varphi)
-witness protection zone for
X
and the color
\varphi^{\prime}(b)
is
(C,\varphi,X)
-witness-forbidden.
Hence,
b
is a
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-helper for
X
. Since
b\in f(\varphi)=f(\varphi^{\prime})
, the vertex
b
does not belong to the
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-witness
A^{\prime}_{X}
for
X
.
This means that
X
is
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-successful, and by the lexicographical minimality of
A^{\prime}_{X}
, we have
b\succ\max_{\prec}A^{\prime}_{X}
.

Consider any vertex
b^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}_{X}
. If
b^{\prime}\not\in A_{X}
, then since
b
is in the
(C,\varphi)
-witness protection zone for
X
and
b^{\prime}\prec b
,
the vertex
b^{\prime}
belongs to the
(C,\varphi)
-witness protection zone for
X
. Moreover, by the minimality of
b
, we have
\varphi^{\prime}(b^{\prime})\in L^{\prime}(b)
,
and thus the color
\varphi^{\prime}(b^{\prime})
is not
(C,\varphi,X)
-witness-forbidden. However, this implies that
b^{\prime}
is not a
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-helper for
X
,
contradicting the claim that
b^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}_{X}
. Therefore, we have
b^{\prime}\in A_{X}
for every
b^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}_{X}
, i.e.,
A^{\prime}_{X}\subseteq A_{X}
.

It follows that
\varphi^{\prime}
assigns the same colors to the vertices of
A^{\prime}_{X}
as the coloring
\varphi
; and since already the colors of vertices
in
A^{\prime}_{X}
show that
X
is
(C,\varphi^{\prime})
-successful, the set
X
must also be
(C,\varphi)
-successful. Moreover, by the lexicographic minimality of
A_{X}
,
we have
A_{X}=A^{\prime}_{X}
. However, since
b\succ\max_{\prec}A^{\prime}_{X}
, this would imply that
b
is not the
(C,\varphi)
-witness protection zone for
X
, which is a contradiction.

It follows that if
\varphi^{\prime}
is an
f
-invariant modification of
\varphi
, then it is an
(L^{\prime},\Pi^{\prime})
-coloring.
∎

We are now ready to deal with the cores of the sparse-dense decomposition.

Lemma 51
.

Let
\Delta
be a positive integer, let
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},\{\mathcal{W}_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}})
be a setting for a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample
(G,L,\Pi)
,
let
S
be a transversal for this setting, and let
\psi
be a uniformly random
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G[S]
.
If
\Delta\geq 3\cdot 10^{9}
, then for every core
C\in\mathcal{C}
and every partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\sigma
of
G[S]
such that

\bigl(C\cup\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{C}\bigr)\cap\text{dom}(\sigma)=\emptyset
, we have

\textup{{Pr}}\left[\text{succ}_{C}(\mathcal{W}_{C},\psi)\leq 1\mid\text{$\psi$ extends $\sigma$}\right]\leq\frac{1}{4\Delta^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
m=\lceil\tfrac{1}{10^{4}}\Delta\rceil
. Since
S
is a transversal, there exist distinct elements
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\in\mathcal{W}_{C}
which are
C
-bisected by
S
.
Let
\mathcal{W}_{0}=\emptyset
and for each
i\in[m]
, let
\mathcal{W}_{i}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{i}\}
.
For each
i\in[m]
, let
v_{i}
be the unique vertex in
X_{i}\cap C
, let
f_{i}
denote the subset selector
f_{C,\mathcal{W}_{i-1},X_{i}}
, and
for any
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\varphi
of
G[S]
, let
(L_{\varphi,i},\Pi_{\varphi,i})
denote the correspondence assignment

(L_{C,\mathcal{W}_{i-1},X_{i},\varphi},\Pi_{C,\mathcal{W}_{i-1},X_{i},\varphi})
.

By Observation
6
and Lemmas
17
and
50
, we can obtain a uniformly random
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
of
G[S]
extending
\sigma

by the following process. Let
\psi_{0}
be a uniformly random
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G[S]
extending
\sigma
.
For
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, let
\psi_{i}
be an
f_{i}
-invariant resampling of
\psi_{i-1}
;
that is, letting
\sigma_{i-1}
be the restriction of
\psi_{i-1}
to
S\setminus f_{i}(\psi_{i-1})
,

\psi_{i}
is a uniformly random
(L_{\psi_{i-1},i},\Pi_{\psi_{i-1},i})
-coloring of
G[S]
extending
\sigma_{i-1}
.
Finally, let
\psi=\psi_{m}
. Let us note that for each
i\in[m]
,

•

since each
(C,\psi_{i-1})
-witness of an element of
\mathcal{W}_{i-1}
has size at most two and
S
is a transversal, we have
|C\cap f(\psi_{i-1})|\geq|C\cap S|-2(m-1)\geq\tfrac{1}{6}\Delta-\tfrac{1}{5000}\Delta
, and

•

since each element
X\in\mathcal{W}_{i-1}
contributes at most two
(C,\psi_{i-1},X)
-witness-forbidden colors at each vertex
u\in f_{i}(\psi_{i-1})
, we have

|L_{\psi_{i-1},i}(u)|\geq\Delta-1-2(m-1)\geq\Delta-\tfrac{1}{5000}\Delta-1
.

The choice of the subset selectors
f_{i}
implies that
\text{succ}_{C}(\mathcal{W}_{i},\psi_{i})\geq\text{succ}_{C}(\mathcal{W}_{i-1},\psi_{i-1})
.
Moreover, if the set
X_{i}
is
(C,\psi_{i})
-successful, then
\text{succ}_{C}(\mathcal{W}_{i},\psi_{i})\geq\text{succ}_{C}(\mathcal{W}_{i-1},\psi_{i-1})+1
.

Claim
52
.

For each
i\in[m]
, the probability that the set
X_{i}
is
(C,\psi_{i})
-successful is at least
\tfrac{1}{5000}
.

Proof of claim.

Let
M_{i}
be the set of all non-edges with one endpoint in
X_{i}\setminus\{v_{i}\}
and the other endpoint in
C\cap f_{i}(\psi_{i-1})
.
Observe that if the coloring
\psi_{i}

(\Delta,M_{i})
-saves the vertex
v_{i}
, then the set
X_{i}
is
(C,\psi_{i})
-successful.
Thus, it suffices to use Lemma
47
to give a lower bound on the probability that
\psi_{i}
, a uniformly random
(L_{\psi_{i-1},i},\Pi_{\psi_{i-1},i})
-coloring of
G[S]
extending
\sigma_{i-1}
,

(\Delta,M_{i})
-saves the vertex
v_{i}
.

Since
S
is a transversal, each vertex of
G
has at most
\gamma=\tfrac{1}{5}\Delta
neighbors in
S
.

Let
\beta=\tfrac{1}{7}\Delta
.
Since
X_{i}
is a
(\Delta,85)
-benefactor for
C
, we have
2+\deg v_{i}-\Delta=|X_{i}\setminus\{v_{i}\}|

and each vertex in
X_{i}\setminus\{v_{i}\}
is incident with at least

|C\cap f_{i}(\psi_{i-1})|-85\geq\tfrac{1}{6}\Delta-\tfrac{1}{5000}\Delta-85\geq\beta+1

non-edges from
M_{i}
. Consequently, the set
M_{i}
is
(v,\Delta,\beta)
-rich.

Let
\delta=\tfrac{1}{6}\Delta
. For each vertex
u\in N(v_{i})\cap S
, we have

|L_{\psi_{i-1},i}(u)|\geq\Delta-\tfrac{1}{5000}\Delta-1\geq\tfrac{\Delta+4\gamma+\delta-4}{2}.

Let
p=\tfrac{\delta}{\Delta^{2}}=\tfrac{1}{6\Delta}
and note that
\beta p=\tfrac{1}{42}
.
By Lemma
47
, the set
X_{i}
is not
(C,\psi_{i})
-successful
with probability at most

(1+\beta p)e^{-(\beta-1)p}=\tfrac{43}{42}\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{42}}\cdot e^{\frac{1}{6\Delta}}\leq 1-\tfrac{1}{5000},

as required.
∎

Let
q=\tfrac{1}{5000}
. The probability that
\text{succ}_{C}(\mathcal{W}_{C},\psi)\leq 1
is bounded from above by the probability that
the set
X_{i}
is
(C,\psi_{i})
-successful for at most one index

, which by Claim

is at most

\displaystyle(1-q)^{m}+mq(1-q)^{m-1}

\displaystyle\leq(1+qm)\cdot(1-q)^{m-1}\leq\frac{1+qm}{1-q}e^{-qm}

\displaystyle\leq 2q(m-1)e^{-qm}\leq\frac{\Delta}{25\cdot 10^{6}}\cdot e^{-\frac{\Delta}{5\cdot 10^{7}}}\leq\frac{1}{4\Delta^{2}}.\qed

5.3
Putting things together

We are now ready to finish the proof of our main result.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
\Delta\geq 3\cdot 10^{9}
be an integer, and suppose for a contradiction that there exists a graph of maximum degree at most
\Delta
and clique number at most
\Delta-1

which is not
(\Delta-1)
-correspondence colorable. Thus, there exists a minimal
\Delta
-counterexample
(G,L,\Pi)
.
By Corollary
45
, there exists a setting
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},\{\mathcal{W}_{C}\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}})
for this counterexample,
and by Lemma
46
, there exists a transversal
S
for this setting.
Let
\psi
be a uniformly random
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G[S]
.

•

For each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\bigl(S\cup\bigcup\mathcal{D}\bigr)
, let
A_{v}
be the event that
\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)\leq 0
, and let
B_{v}
be the set consisting of the neighbors of
v
in
S
.
By Lemma
49
, for every
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\sigma
of
G[S\setminus B_{v}]
, we have

\textup{{Pr}}\left[A_{v}\mid\text{$\psi$ extends $\sigma$}\right]\leq\frac{1}{4\Delta^{2}}.

Moreover, we have
|B_{v}|\leq\tfrac{1}{5}\Delta
.

•

For each core
C\in\mathcal{C}
, let
A_{C}
be the event that
\text{succ}_{C}(\mathcal{W}_{C},\psi)\leq 1
and let
B_{C}=\bigl(C\cup\bigcup\mathcal{W}_{C}\bigr)\cap S
.
By Lemma
51
, for every
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\sigma
of
G[S\setminus B_{C}]
, we have

\textup{{Pr}}\left[A_{C}\mid\text{$\psi$ extends $\sigma$}\right]\leq\frac{1}{4\Delta^{2}}.

Moreover, we have
|B_{C}|\leq|C\cap S|+2|\mathcal{W}_{C}|\leq\tfrac{1}{5}\Delta+2\cdot\lceil\tfrac{1}{100}\Delta\rceil\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\Delta
.
Let us remark that the bound
|C\cap S|\leq\tfrac{1}{5}\Delta
holds since
S
is a transversal and
C\cap S\subseteq N(z)
for any vertex
z\in C\setminus S
.

Let
I=\bigl(V(G)\setminus\bigl(S\cup\bigcup\mathcal{D}\bigr)\bigr)\cup\mathcal{C}
and let
\mathcal{E}=\{A_{x}:x\in I\}
be the set of all events discussed above.
For each
x\in I
, let
\mathcal{E}_{A_{x}}=\{A_{y}:y\in I,B_{y}\cap B_{x}\neq\emptyset\}
.
Note that for each
x\in I
, we have
|\mathcal{E}_{A_{x}}|<\Delta^{2}
; indeed, the set
\mathcal{E}_{A_{x}}
contains

•

events
A_{v}
for at most
|B_{x}|\cdot\Delta\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\Delta^{2}
vertices
v\in V(G)\setminus\bigl(S\cup\bigcup\mathcal{D}\bigr)
with a neighbor in
B_{x}
, and

•

events
A_{C}
for at most
|B_{x}|+|B_{x}|\cdot\Delta\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\Delta(\Delta+1)
cores
C\in\mathcal{C}
such that
either a vertex of
B_{x}
or a neighbor of a vertex of
B_{x}
belongs to
C
.

Consider any set
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{A_{x}}
and note that the validity of the events in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
only depends on the colors of the vertices in
S\setminus B_{x}
.
Let
\Phi
be the set of all
(L,\Pi)
-colorings
\sigma
of
G[S\setminus B_{x}]
for which none of the events in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
occur for the
(L,\Pi)
-colorings of
G[S]
that extend
\sigma
.
Thus

\sum_{\sigma\in\Phi}\textup{{Pr}}\left[\text{$\psi$ extends $\sigma$}\Bigm|\bigwedge_{A\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\lnot A\right]=1,

and

\displaystyle\textup{{Pr}}\left[A_{x}\Bigm|\bigwedge_{y\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\lnot A_{y}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Phi}\textup{{Pr}}\left[A_{x}\mid\text{$\psi$ extends $\sigma$}\right]\cdot\textup{{Pr}}\left[\text{$\psi$ extends $\sigma$}\Bigm|\bigwedge_{y\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\lnot A_{y}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\Delta^{2}}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\Phi}\textup{{Pr}}\left[\text{$\psi$ extends $\sigma$}\Bigm|\bigwedge_{A\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\lnot A\right]=\frac{1}{4\Delta^{2}}.

By the Lovász Local Lemma (Theorem
14
), with non-zero probability none of the events in
\mathcal{E}
holds.
Let us fix an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\psi
of
G[S]
with this property.
It is now easy to see that
\psi
extends to an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring of
G
.
Indeed, let
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}\}
be the partition of
V(G)\setminus S
such that
for each
i\in[t]
, either
X_{i}=\{v\}
for a vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\bigl(S\cup\bigcup\mathcal{D}\bigr)
,
or
X_{i}=D\setminus S
for a near-clique
D\in\mathcal{D}
.
Let
\varphi_{0}=\psi
. For each
i\in[t]
, we extend
\varphi_{i-1}
to a partial
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\varphi_{i}
by giving colors to the vertices in
X_{i}

as follows:

•

If
X_{i}=\{v\}
for a vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\bigl(S\cup\bigcup\mathcal{D}\bigr)
, then since
A_{v}
is false,
we have
\text{exc}_{\varphi_{i-1}}(v)\geq\text{exc}_{\psi}(v)>0
. Therefore, we can extend
\varphi_{i-1}
to the vertex
v
greedily.

•

Suppose now that
X_{i}=D\setminus S
for a near-clique
D\in\mathcal{D}
with core
C\in\mathcal{C}
. Recall that
\delta(G[D])\geq\tfrac{3}{4}\Delta
.
If there exists a vertex
u\in D\setminus(C\cup S)
,
then let
\varphi^{\prime}_{i-1}
be a partial
(L,\Pi)
coloring obtained from
\varphi_{i-1}
by coloring
u
greedily. This is possible,
since
u
has at least
\tfrac{3}{4}\Delta-|N(u)\cap S|\geq\tfrac{3}{4}\Delta-\tfrac{1}{5}\Delta\geq 2
neighbors in
C\setminus S
that
have not been colored yet. Otherwise, let
\varphi^{\prime}_{i-1}=\varphi_{i-1}
.

Since
A_{C}
is false, the set
C\setminus S
contains distinct vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
with positive
\psi
-excess,
and thus also with positive
\varphi^{\prime}_{i-1}
-excess. Therefore, we can extend
\varphi^{\prime}_{i-1}
to
C\setminus S
by Observation
9
, coloring
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
last.

This way, we obtain an
(L,\Pi)
-coloring
\varphi_{t}
of
G
. This contradicts the claim that
(G,L,\Pi)
is a
\Delta
-counterexample.
∎
