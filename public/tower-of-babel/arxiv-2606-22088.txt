Isometric free finite group actions on non-positively curved 3-manifolds
Zhengyu Zou

Abstract
Let M M be a closed orientable 3 3 -manifold admitting a metric of nonpositive sectional curvature (an NPC metric), and let G G be a finite group acting freely on M M by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. Previous results showed that M M admits a G G -invariant NPC metric except possibly when M M is a graph manifold. In this paper, we resolve the remaining case by proving that M M also admits a G G -invariant NPC metric when M M is a graph manifold. This result advances our understanding in dimension 3 3 of the question posed by Schoen–Yau about Nielsen realization for NPC 3 3 -manifolds.



1
Introduction

Riemannian metrics of nonpositive sectional curvature (NPC metrics) are rigid enough to impose strong topological restrictions on
3
-manifolds, yet flexible enough to occur on many closed
3
-manifolds beyond the locally homogeneous setting. Schoen–Yau
[
SY79
]
proposed the following analog of the Nielsen realization problem for manifolds admitting NPC metrics.

Problem 1.1

(Schoen–Yau)
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold admitting an NPC metric, and let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
. Does there exist an NPC metric on
M
such that
G
is realized by a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Isom}(M)
?

There has been partial progress on Problem
1.1
. In dimension
2
, it is answered by Kerckhoff’s solution to the classical Nielsen realization problem
[
Ker83
]
. When
M
is hyperbolic and
\dim M\geq 3
, Problem
1.1
is answered by Mostow rigidity. On the other hand, in dimension
n\geq 7
, Farrell–Jones
[
FJ90
, Theorem 1]
constructed exotic smooth structures on closed manifolds
M^{n}
that are homeomorphic to hyperbolic manifolds. For these exotic smooth structures, the natural map
\operatorname{Diff}(M^{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M^{n}))
is not surjective, even though
M^{n}
admits metrics of negative sectional curvature. Thus Problem
1.1
is false in dimensions at least
7
.

Our work is motivated by the dimension-
3
case of Problem
1.1
. Earlier work of Zimmermann showed that, when
M^{3}
is Haken and not Seifert fibered, every finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
can be lifted to
\operatorname{Diff}(M)

[
Zim82
, Satz 0.1]
. However, when
M
is Seifert fibered, Heil–Tollefson found counterexamples to Problem
1.1
by analyzing obstructions to realizing extensions of finite subgroups of
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
by
\pi_{1}(M)

[
HT78
, Example 2.8]
. Now let
M
be a closed, orientable
3
-manifold admitting an NPC metric. Then
M
is irreducible. If the JSJ decomposition of
M
contains more than one component, then
M
contains an incompressible embedded torus; hence
M
is Haken and is not Seifert fibered. In this setting, Problem
1.1
and Zimmermann’s smooth realization result motivate the following geometric realization problem.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed, orientable
3
-manifold admitting an NPC metric, and let
G
be a finite group acting on
M
by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. Does
M
admit a
G
-invariant NPC metric?

In this paper, we study Problem
1.2
under the additional assumption that
G
acts freely on
M
. Under this assumption, the problem is already known to have a positive answer when
M
is Seifert fibered
[
MS86
, Theorem 2.1]
, when
M
is atoroidal but not Seifert fibered
[
Per02
,
Per03a
,
Per03
,
DL09
]
, and more generally when the JSJ decomposition of
M
contains at least one atoroidal piece
[
Lee95
, Theorem 3.3]
. The remaining case is therefore the case of
graph manifolds
, namely closed
3
-manifolds whose JSJ pieces are all Seifert fibered. This case is genuinely subtle: even if each individual Seifert fibered piece admits an NPC metric, the resulting graph manifold need not admit one. Our main result addresses precisely this remaining case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a closed, orientable graph manifold admitting an NPC metric, and let
G
be a finite group acting freely on
M
by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. Then the quotient manifold
M/G
admits an NPC metric. In particular,
M
admits a
G
-invariant NPC metric obtained by pulling back an NPC metric on
M/G
.

Combining Theorem
1.3
with the previously known cases gives the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
M
be a closed, orientable
3
-manifold admitting an NPC metric, and let
G
be a finite group acting freely on
M
by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. Then
M
admits a
G
-invariant NPC metric.

Problem
1.2
is also of independent interest. More generally, one may ask whether a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Diff}(M)
must preserve some distinguished geometric structure on
M
. In dimension
2
, every finite group action on a closed surface preserves a metric of constant curvature. In dimension
3
, Meeks–Scott
[
MS86
]
and Dinkelbach–Leeb
[
DL09
]
showed that finite group actions on closed manifolds modeled on one of Thurston’s eight geometries can likewise be realized by isometries of a suitable geometric metric.

1.1
Proof Roadmap

Theorem
1.3
assumes that
G
acts freely on
M
. Set
M^{\prime}=M/G
. Then
M^{\prime}
is again a closed, orientable
3
-manifold, and it suffices to prove that
M^{\prime}
admits an NPC metric.

Let
\pi:M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
be the quotient map. To construct an NPC metric on
M^{\prime}
, we begin with the JSJ decomposition of
M^{\prime}

[
JS79
,
Joh79
]
, which splits
M^{\prime}
along a collection of JSJ tori
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Our main strategy is to choose flat metrics on the tori in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and then extend these metrics over each component of
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
(Here,
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
denote the manifold obtained by removing the interiors of tubular neighborhoods of the tori in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
) using a criterion by Leeb and Buyalo–Svetlov involving the first homology groups of the boundary tori of the JSJ pieces
[
Lee95
,
BS05
]
. We call this criterion the
metric extension criterion on homology
, abbreviated MECH.

To choose flat metrics on
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
satisfying MECH on each component of
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, we lift the tori in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
to a collection of incompressible tori
\mathcal{T}
in
M
and construct a
G
-invariant flat metric
\bar{g}
on the tori in
\mathcal{T}
satisfying MECH on each component of
M\mid\mathcal{T}
. Since
\bar{g}
is
G
-invariant, it descends to a flat metric
\pi_{*}\bar{g}
on
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. We then verify that
\pi_{*}\bar{g}
satisfies MECH on each component of
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.

It remains to construct the metric
\bar{g}
on the tori in
\mathcal{T}
. We first choose an NPC metric
g
on
M
whose restriction to each torus in
\mathcal{T}
is flat. We then apply a modified averaging argument to the flat metric
g|_{\mathcal{T}}
with respect to the action of
G
. The resulting metric
\bar{g}
is
G
-invariant and remains flat. Moreover, since MECH is a linear condition and
g|_{\mathcal{T}}
satisfies MECH on each component of
M\mid\mathcal{T}
, the averaged metric
\bar{g}
also satisfies MECH. Therefore
\pi_{*}\bar{g}
satisfies MECH on
M^{\prime}
, so the flat metrics on
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
extend over the JSJ pieces of
M^{\prime}
to give an NPC metric on
M^{\prime}
.

1.2
Future Directions

One may ask whether our approach to Theorem
1.3
can be generalized to prove the analogous statement without the freeness assumption on the group action.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
M
be a closed orientable
3
-manifold admitting an NPC metric, and let
G
be a finite group acting on
M
by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. Then
M
admits a
G
-invariant NPC metric.

When the group
G
in Conjecture
1.5
does not act freely on
M
, the quotient
M^{\prime}=M/G
is an orientable
3
-orbifold rather than a
3
-manifold. There is a decomposition theorem for such orbifolds
[
BMP03
, Theorem 3.15]
analogous to the JSJ decomposition of irreducible
3
-manifolds: an irreducible
3
-orbifold can be canonically split along a collection of incompressible, mutually disjoint, and mutually nonparallel toric
2
-suborbifolds. The resulting components are either atoroidal orbifolds or Seifert fibered
3
-orbifolds. This suggests a way to transform Conjecture
1.5
into a topological problem about the orbifold JSJ decomposition. However, it is not clear what the appropriate homological data should be in the orbifold setting. An ongoing research project studies such presentations with the goal of developing a metric extension criterion for Seifert fibered
3
-orbifolds.

2
3-Manifolds with NPC Metrics

In this section, we review several properties of
3
-manifolds admitting NPC metrics that will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.3
. We begin in
\lx@sectionsign
2.1
by reviewing the JSJ decomposition of irreducible
3
-manifolds; in particular, we show that every closed NPC
3
-manifold is irreducible and hence admits such a decomposition. Then, in
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
we introduce Waldhausen bases for Seifert fibered
3
-manifolds with torus boundary components, which arise as components of the JSJ decomposition of NPC graph manifolds. Waldhausen bases give us the language to state the metric extension criterion on homology (MECH). Finally, in
\lx@sectionsign
2.3
we recall and provide a self-contained proof of MECH.

We assume familiarity with the basic theory of compact surfaces and
2
-orbifolds, including (orbifold) Euler characteristics and (orbifold) fundamental groups. We refer the reader to
[
Sco83
,
BMP03
]
for a detailed introduction to
2
-orbifolds.

2.1
JSJ Decomposition of an Irreducible 3-Manifold

Let
M
be a closed orientable
3
-manifold. A smoothly embedded sphere
\phi:S^{2}\hookrightarrow M
is
essential
if it does not bound a
3
-ball in
M
. A
3
-manifold
M
is
irreducible
if it does not contain any essential spheres.
An embedded torus
T^{2}\subset M
is
incompressible
if the map
\pi_{1}(T^{2})\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(M)
is injective. Geometrically, this means that any embedded disk
D^{2}
in
M
with
\partial D^{2}\subset T^{2}
bounds a disk in
T^{2}
. Given two embedded tori
T_{1},T_{2}\subset M
, we say that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are
parallel
if there exists an embedded
T^{2}\times I\subset M
such that
T^{2}\times\{0\}=T_{1}
and
T^{2}\times\{1\}=T_{2}
. We say that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are
nonparallel
otherwise. Given a finite collection of mutually disjoint embedded tori
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\}
in
M
, let
N(T_{i})\cong T_{i}\times I\subset M
be a collection of mutually disjoint tubular neighborhoods of
T_{i}
in
M
. We denote by
M\mid\mathcal{T}
the
splitting of
M
along
\mathcal{T}
, where

M\mid\mathcal{T}=M\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}N(T_{i}).

If an orientable
3
-manifold is irreducible, then one can decompose
M
along a finite collection of mutually disjoint, mutually nonparallel embedded tori
\mathcal{T}
. The following theorem is due to Jaco–Shalen
[
JS79
]
and Johannson
[
Joh79
]
and appears frequently in the literature as the
JSJ decomposition
. See
[
Hat23
, Theorem 1.9]
for a more modern proof.

Theorem 2.1

(JSJ Decomposition)
.

Given a compact irreducible orientable
3
-manifold
M
, there exists a collection
\mathcal{T}
of finitely many mutually disjoint, mutually nonparallel incompressible tori such that each component of
M\mid\mathcal{T}
is either atoroidal or a Seifert fibered space. Such collection
\mathcal{T}
is unique up to isotopy.

We say that a collection of incompressible tori
\mathcal{T}
in
M
is
JSJ collection
if
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
2.1
. We call the corresponding decomposition
M\mid\mathcal{T}
a
JSJ decomposition
of
M
. By geometrization of 3-manifolds, each piece arising in the JSJ decomposition is geometric: if a component
X_{i}
of
M\mid\mathcal{T}
is Seifert fibered, then it is modeled on one of the six Thurston geometries (cf.
[
Thu97
]
); otherwise,
X_{i}
is atoroidal, and the interior of
X_{i}
is a finite-volume hyperbolic cusped manifold (cf.
[
Bes+10
]
).

A crucial part of our proof of Theorem
1.3
is understanding how the incompressible tori in the JSJ decomposition of the quotient manifold
M/G
relate to those in the JSJ decomposition of
M
. The following lemma shows that a JSJ collection of tori in
M/G
always pulls back to a collection of incompressible tori in
M
. However, the pullback tori need not be mutually non-parallel. In this case, the corresponding splitting of
M
may contain pieces diffeomorphic to
T^{2}\times I
. This phenomenon is related to the presence of twisted
I
-bundles over the Klein bottle, denoted
K\tilde{\times}I
, in the JSJ decomposition of
M/G
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M^{\prime}
be a closed orientable irreducible
3
-manifold that admits a finite-sheeted cover by a closed
3
-manifold
M
, with covering map
\pi:M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be the collection of JSJ tori in
M^{\prime}
as in Theorem
2.1
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the collection of connected components of
\pi^{-1}(T^{\prime})
over all
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{T}
is a collection of incompressible tori in
M
such that
M\mid\mathcal{T}
consists of Seifert fibered or atoroidal components. In particular,
\mathcal{T}
is a JSJ collection of
M
if
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
contains no components diffeomorphic to
K\tilde{\times}I
.

Proof.

We first note that every connected component of
\pi^{-1}(T^{\prime})
is a torus. Since
T^{\prime}
is incompressible in
M^{\prime}
, each component of
\pi^{-1}(T^{\prime})
is incompressible in
M
.
The components of
M\mid\mathcal{T}
are connected components of
\pi^{-1}(X^{\prime})
, where
X^{\prime}
is a component of
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Since each
X^{\prime}
is either Seifert fibered or atoroidal, every finite cover of
X^{\prime}
is again Seifert fibered or atoroidal. Hence every component of
M\mid\mathcal{T}
is Seifert fibered or atoroidal.

It remains to prove the mutually non-parallel statement. The only possible obstruction to
\mathcal{T}
being mutually non-parallel is that
M\mid\mathcal{T}
contains a product region
T^{2}\times I
between two parallel tori of
\mathcal{T}
. Such a component covers a component of
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
that is finitely covered by
T^{2}\times I
. By the classification of orientable irreducible
3
-manifolds finitely covered by
T^{2}\times I
, this component must be diffeomorphic to either
T^{2}\times I
or
K\tilde{\times}I
.
Since
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is a JSJ collection of
M^{\prime}
, no component of
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
T^{2}\times I
. By assumption, no component of
M^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
K\tilde{\times}I
either. Therefore
M\mid\mathcal{T}
contains no
T^{2}\times I
components, so no torus in
\mathcal{T}
is redundant. Hence
\mathcal{T}
is a JSJ collection of
M
.
∎

To conclude this section, we show that every NPC
3
-manifold is irreducible, so we can apply Theorem
2.1
on it. We first prove a general theorem about the relationship between manifolds
M
whose universal cover is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and embedded spheres in
M
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
M
be a connected, closed, orientable
n
-manifold whose universal cover is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
n\geq 3
. Then any smooth embedding of
S^{n-1}
into
M
bounds a topological
B^{n}
.

Proof.

We need to show that given any smooth embedding of an
(n-1)
-sphere
\phi:S^{n-1}\hookrightarrow M
, there exists an embedding of a closed
n
-ball
\psi:B^{n}\hookrightarrow M
such that
\psi|_{\partial B^{n}}=\phi
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow M
be the universal covering map. Since
S^{n-1}
is simply connected for
n\geq 3
, the lifting property of covering maps implies that
\phi
lifts to a smooth map
\tilde{\phi}:S^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Because
\phi
is an embedding and
\pi
is a covering map,
\tilde{\phi}
is also a smooth embedding. By the generalized Schoenflies theorem, there exists a topological embedding
\tilde{\psi}:B^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\tilde{\psi}|_{\partial B^{n}}=\tilde{\phi}
.

Consider the map
\psi=\pi\circ\tilde{\psi}
. By construction,
\psi|_{\partial B^{n}}=\phi
. To see that
\psi(B^{n})
is an embedded ball, it suffices to show that
\psi
is injective. Assume for contradiction that
\psi
is not injective. Then there exist two distinct points in
\tilde{\psi}(B^{n})
with the same image in
M
under
\pi
, so there exists a nontrivial deck transformation
\gamma\in\operatorname{Deck}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\xrightarrow{\pi}M)
sending one to the other. In particular,

\gamma(\tilde{\psi}(B^{n}))\cap\tilde{\psi}(B^{n})\neq\emptyset.

Since
\tilde{\psi}|_{\partial B^{n}}=\tilde{\phi}
, and since
\tilde{\phi}
is a lift of the embedding
\phi
, we have

\gamma(\tilde{\phi}(S^{n-1}))\cap\tilde{\phi}(S^{n-1})=\emptyset.

Hence
\gamma(\tilde{\phi}(S^{n-1}))
is disjoint from the boundary of
\tilde{\psi}(B^{n})
. Since
\tilde{\phi}(S^{n-1})
separates
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into two components and
\tilde{\psi}(B^{n})
is the closure of the bounded one, it follows, after replacing
\gamma
by
\gamma^{-1}
if necessary, that

\gamma(\tilde{\psi}(B^{n}))\subset\tilde{\psi}(B^{n}).

Since
\tilde{\psi}(B^{n})\cong B^{n}
, the Brouwer fixed-point theorem implies that
\gamma
has a fixed point in
\tilde{\psi}(B^{n})
. This contradicts the fact that a nontrivial deck transformation acts freely.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Every connected closed orientable
3
-manifold
M
that admits an NPC metric is irreducible.

Proof.

The Cartan–Hadamard theorem implies that the universal cover of
M
is
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The corollary then follows from Proposition
2.4
.
∎

2.2
Waldhausen Basis of a Seifert Fibered 3-Manifold

We are primarily interested in the following class of Seifert fibered 3-manifolds with boundary. These appear as Seifert fibered components of the JSJ decompositions of
3
-manifolds admitting NPC metrics. In the following, we assume that
X\rightarrow O
is an orientable Seifert fibered
3
-manifold with nonempty torus boundary components and satisfies
e(X)=0
and
\chi^{\text{orb}}(O)\leq 0
, where
\chi^{\text{orb}}(O)
is the orbifold Euler characteristic of
O
. Since
X
is aspherical, the regular fiber determines an infinite cyclic normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(X)
, and
\pi_{1}(X)
fits into the short exact sequence

1\longrightarrow\langle f\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(X)\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O)\longrightarrow 1,

(1)

where
f
is represented by a regular fiber. If the base orbifold
O
is orientable, then
\langle f\rangle
lies in the center of
\pi_{1}(X)
.

We define a
Waldhausen basis
of
H_{1}(\partial X)
as in
[
BS05
]
. Here and in the following, unless otherwise specified, we take first homology with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
and write
H_{1}(Y)=H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
for every space
Y
. Let
i:\partial X\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion map, and let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
denote the torus components of
\partial X
. For each
T_{i}
, consider the intersection form on first homology and the orientation on
T_{i}
coming from the orientation of
X
:

\wedge_{i}:H_{1}(T_{i})\times H_{1}(T_{i})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X\rightarrow O
be an orientable Seifert fibered
3
-manifold with nonempty boundary and satisfies
e(X)=0
and
\chi^{\text{orb}}(O)\leq 0
. A
Waldhausen basis
for

H_{1}(\partial X)\cong\bigoplus_{j}H_{1}(T_{j})

is a choice, for each
j
, of a basis
(f_{j},b_{j})
of
H_{1}(T_{j})
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

For all
j
, we have
i_{*}f_{j}=f\in H_{1}(X)
, where
f
is the image of a fixed generator of the kernel subgroup
\mathbb{Z}<H_{1}(X)\cong\pi_{1}(X)^{\text{ab}}
under the short exact sequence in Equation (
1
). When
O
is orientable,
f
is interpreted as a choice of orientation of the regular fiber.

2.

For all
j
, we have
f_{j}\wedge_{j}b_{j}=1
. In particular,
(f_{j},b_{j})
is an oriented basis of
H_{1}(T_{j})
.

3.

Let
b=\sum_{j}b_{j}
. Then
b
lies in the kernel of
i_{*}:H_{1}(\partial X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. Here, we take coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
to eliminate the torsion classes arising from the exceptional fibers.

Remark 2.7
.

It is possible for
X
to admit more than one Waldhausen bases. See Figure
2
for an example. However, if
\partial X
is a single torus, then given a Seifert fibration and an orientation on
X
, there exists a unique Waldhausen basis on
\partial X
given by the fiber direction
f
and the unique base direction
b
that vanishes in
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.

(f_{i},b_{i})
and

S_{1}^{2}\times S^{1}
, where
S_{1}^{2}

We first show that Waldhausen bases exist in the setting considered here.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X\rightarrow O
be an orientable Seifert fibered
3
-manifold with nonempty boundary and satisfies
e(X)=0
and
\chi^{\text{orb}}(O)\leq 0
. Then
X
admits a Waldhausen basis.

Proof.

Decompose the base orbifold as
O=\Sigma\cup W
, where

W=D_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup D_{m}
is the union of disjoint disk neighborhoods
D_{k}
of the cone points of
O
, and
\Sigma
is the compact surface obtained by removing the interiors of these neighborhoods from
|O|
. Since
\Sigma
has boundary, the restriction of the Seifert fibration to
\Sigma
has no exceptional fibers and admits a section. We fix such a section and identify
\Sigma
with its image in
Z=p^{-1}(\Sigma)
, where
p:X\rightarrow O
is the projection map. In particular, every boundary torus of
X
is contained in
Z
.

For each boundary torus
T_{j}\subset\partial X
, let
f_{j}\in H_{1}(T_{j})
be the class represented by a generic fiber, chosen so that
i_{*}f_{j}=f
, where
f
is the fixed regular fiber class in
H_{1}(X)
. Let
b_{j}\in H_{1}(T_{j})
be the class represented by the boundary curve in the base direction, namely the curve
T_{j}\cap\Sigma
, chosen so that
f_{j}\wedge_{j}b_{j}=1
. Let
c_{k}\in H_{1}(Z)
be the class represented by the boundary component of
\Sigma
that bounds the disk neighborhood
D_{k}
of the
k
-th cone point of
O
. After orienting these curves as boundary components of
\Sigma
, the curves represented by the classes
b_{j}
and
c_{k}
together form
\partial\Sigma
. Hence

\sum_{j}i_{*}b_{j}+\sum_{k}c_{k}=0

(2)

in
H_{1}(Z)
, and therefore also in
H_{1}(X)
.

Next, observe that the total space
S_{k}=p^{-1}(D_{k})
is a solid torus, and that
X
is obtained from
Z
by gluing the solid tori
S_{k}
back in. For each
k
, there are relatively prime integers
(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})
, with
\alpha_{k}>0
, such that the meridian of
S_{k}
is identified with the curve class
\alpha_{k}c_{k}+\beta_{k}f
. Thus

\alpha_{k}c_{k}+\beta_{k}f=0\quad\text{in $H_{1}(X)$.}

(3)

After tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
, Equation (
3
) gives
c_{k}=-\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}f
.
Substituting this into Equation (
2
), we obtain

\sum_{j}i_{*}b_{j}=\left(\sum_{k}\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\right)f\quad\text{in $H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})$}.

Since the Euler number of
X
vanishes, the right-hand side is zero. Therefore
\sum_{j}i_{*}b_{j}=0
in
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Q})
. Hence the bases
(f_{j},b_{j})
form a Waldhausen basis of
X
.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
X\rightarrow O
be an orientable Seifert fibered
3
-manifold with nonempty boundary and satisfies
e(X)=0
and
\chi^{\text{orb}}(O)\leq 0
. Let
G
be a finite group acting freely on
X
by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms, denote by
X^{\prime}=X/G
the quotient manifold and by
\pi:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
the quotient map. Write

\partial X=T_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup T_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial X^{\prime}=T^{\prime}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup T^{\prime}_{m}.

Then
X
admits a Waldhausen basis
(f_{j},b_{j})
and
X^{\prime}
admits a Waldhausen basis
(f^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i})
such that:

(a)

for all
\phi\in G
sending
T_{j}
to
T_{j^{\prime}}
, we have
\phi_{*}f_{j}=f_{j^{\prime}}
and
\phi_{*}b_{j}=b_{j^{\prime}}
;

(b)

for all
T_{j}
and
T^{\prime}_{i}
such that
\pi(T_{j})=T^{\prime}_{i}
, we have

\pi_{*}f_{j}=lf^{\prime}_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pi_{*}b_{j}=m_{i}b^{\prime}_{i},

where
l
is the degree of
\pi
in the fiber direction, and
m_{i}
is a positive integer determined by
T^{\prime}_{i}
.

Proof.

We begin with a Waldhausen basis
(f^{\prime}_{i},b^{\prime}_{i})
of
X^{\prime}
, which exists by Lemma
2.8
. We relabel the components of
\partial X
as
T_{i,j}
so that
\pi^{-1}(T^{\prime}_{i})
consists of the components
T_{i,1},\ldots,T_{i,n_{i}}
. Let
f_{i,j}\in H_{1}(T_{i,j})
be the class of the regular fiber, chosen with orientation so that
\pi_{*}f_{i,j}=lf^{\prime}_{i}
. Since
G
preserves the fiber direction and the orientation of
X
, we have
\phi_{*}f_{i,j}=f_{i,j^{\prime}}
for all
\phi\in G
sending
T_{i,j}
to
T_{i,j^{\prime}}
.

To choose
b_{i,j}\in H_{1}(T_{i,j})
, observe that
G
acts by permutation on the set
\{T_{i,1},\ldots,T_{i,n_{i}}\}
. Let
S_{i}<G
be the stabilizer of
T_{i,1}
under this action. Choose
\phi_{j}\in G
sending
T_{i,1}
to
T_{i,j}
. Then every element
\phi\in G
sending
T_{i,1}
to
T_{i,j}
can be written as
\phi_{j}\psi
for some
\psi\in S_{i}
. Since
S_{i}
acts freely and orientation-preservingly on
T_{i,1}
, it acts trivially on
H_{1}(T_{i,1})
. We choose
b_{i,1}
to be an element of
H_{1}(T_{i,1})
such that

\pi_{*}b_{i,1}=m_{i}b^{\prime}_{i},

where
m_{i}
is the positive integer for which the map
T_{i,1}\rightarrow T^{\prime}_{i}
is a covering of degree
m_{i}l
. In particular, we have

m_{i}n_{i}l=d

for all
i
, where
d
is the degree of the covering
X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
. We then set

b_{i,j}=\phi_{j*}b_{i,1}.

We now check conditions (a) and (b). For (a), let
\phi\in G
send
T_{i,j}
to
T_{i,j^{\prime}}
. Then
\phi_{j^{\prime}}^{-1}\phi\phi_{j}
sends
T_{i,1}
to itself, so
\phi_{j^{\prime}}^{-1}\phi\phi_{j}\in S_{i}
. It follows that

\phi_{*}b_{i,j}=\phi_{j^{\prime}*}(\phi_{j^{\prime}}^{-1}\phi\phi_{j})_{*}b_{i,1}=\phi_{j^{\prime}*}b_{i,1}=b_{i,j^{\prime}},

since
S_{i}
acts trivially on
H_{1}(T_{i,1})
. This proves (a). For (b), since
\pi\circ\phi_{j}=\pi
for all
j
, we have

\pi_{*}b_{i,j}=\pi_{*}\phi_{j*}b_{i,1}=\pi_{*}b_{i,1}=m_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}.

It remains to check that
(f_{i,j},b_{i,j})
is a Waldhausen basis for
X
. First, by construction of
f_{i,j}
, we have
i_{*}f_{i,j}=f\in H_{1}(X)
. Next, we verify that
f_{i,j}\wedge_{i,j}b_{i,j}=1
. By naturality of the intersection form,

m_{i}l(f_{i,j}\wedge_{i,j}b_{i,j})=(\pi_{*}f_{i,j})\wedge_{i}(\pi_{*}b_{i,j})=(lf^{\prime}_{i})\wedge_{i}(m_{i}b^{\prime}_{i})=m_{i}l(f^{\prime}_{i}\wedge_{i}b^{\prime}_{i})=m_{i}l.

Hence
f_{i,j}\wedge_{i,j}b_{i,j}=1
, so
(f_{i,j},b_{i,j})
is an oriented basis of
H_{1}(T_{i,j})
.

Finally, we must check that

b=\sum_{i,j}b_{i,j}

lies in the kernel of
i_{*}:H_{1}(\partial X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. Observe that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{1}(\partial X,\mathbb{Q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{1}(\partial X^{\prime},\mathbb{Q})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{1}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Q})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\pi|_{\partial X})_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\prime}_{*}}

Also,

i^{\prime}_{*}(\pi|_{\partial X})_{*}b=i^{\prime}_{*}\sum_{i,j}m_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}=i^{\prime}_{*}\sum_{i}n_{i}m_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}=\frac{d}{l}\left(i^{\prime}_{*}\sum_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}\right)=0.

Thus
\pi_{*}i_{*}b=0
. On the other hand, if
(\bar{f}_{i,j},\bar{b}_{i,j})
is any Waldhausen basis of
X
, then, after replacing
\bar{f}_{i,j}
by
-\bar{f}_{i,j}
if necessary, we may assume that
f_{i,j}=\bar{f}_{i,j}
and

b_{i,j}=\bar{b}_{i,j}+r_{i,j}f_{i,j}

for some
r_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that

0=\pi_{*}i_{*}b=\pi_{*}\left(\sum_{i,j}i_{*}\bar{b}_{i,j}\right)+\left(\sum_{i,j}r_{i,j}\right)\pi_{*}f=\left(\sum_{i,j}r_{i,j}\right)lf^{\prime}.

Hence
\sum_{i,j}r_{i,j}=0
, and therefore
i_{*}b=0
as required.
∎

2.3
The Metric Extension Criterion on Homology (MECH)

Let
X\rightarrow O
be an orientable Seifert fibered
3
-manifold, possibly with nonempty boundary. A Riemannian metric
g
on
X
is
compatible with the Seifert fibration of
X
if all generic fibers of
X
are geodesic curves of the same length.

If a Riemannian metric
g
compatible with the Seifert fibration of
X
is NPC, then the universal Riemannian cover decomposes as a Riemannian product

(\tilde{X},\tilde{g})=Y\times\mathbb{R},

where
Y
is non-positively curved, cf.
[
Ebe82
, Lemma 1.]
. The
\mathbb{R}
-direction projects to the fibers of
X
in the sense that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y\times\mathbb{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S^{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces O}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\pi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Here,
f
(resp.
\tilde{f}
) is the inclusion of a fixed
S^{1}
(resp.
\mathbb{R}
) fiber into
X
(resp.
Y\times\mathbb{R}
), and
\pi
(resp.
\tilde{\pi}
) is the projection to the base orbifold (resp. universal cover of the base orbifold).

The representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(Y\times\mathbb{R})
associated with the universal Riemannian cover decomposes as a pair of representations
(\rho^{\prime},\phi)
. Here,

\phi:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

(4)

is the deck action of
\pi_{1}(X)
in the
\mathbb{R}
-direction. Notice that
\phi(f)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, where
f\in\pi_{1}(X)
is the generic fiber class. On the other hand,
\rho^{\prime}:\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O)\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(Y)
is a cocompact discrete action that induces an NPC orbifold metric on
O
.

To work with the representations
(\rho^{\prime},\phi)
, we first recall standard presentations for the fundamental group of an aspherical Seifert fibered
3
-manifold. Suppose that
X\rightarrow O
has
n
boundary components and
m
exceptional fibers. Then
O
has
n
boundary components and
m
cone points, say of orders
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
. Let
g
be the genus of
|O|
after capping the boundary components of
O
. Assume first that
O
is orientable. Then, using the short exact sequence from Equation (
1
), we have the following presentation.

\pi_{1}(X)=\Big\langle x_{i},y_{i},b_{j},c_{k},f\,\Big|\,\text{$f$ central},\;\prod_{i=1}^{g}[x_{i},y_{i}]\prod_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\prod_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}f^{-b},\;c_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}}\Big\rangle.

(5)

Here, the generators
x_{i},y_{i},b_{j},c_{k}
are lifts of the generators of
\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O)
, where
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
are curve classes that generate the fundamental group of a closed orientable genus-
g
surface,
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
are curve classes of the boundary components of
O
, and
\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{k}
are curve classes of the curves around the cone points. The pairs
(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})
are the
Seifert invariants
associated to the exceptional fibers of
X
. With this convention, the Euler number of the Seifert fibration is

e(X)=-\left(b+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\right).

(6)

If
O
is non-orientable, then we may simply replace the generators
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
with a set of generators
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
that generate a closed non-orientable genus-
g
surface. This gives us the following presentation.

\pi_{1}(X)=\Big\langle z_{i},b_{j},c_{k},f\,\Big|\,z_{i}fz_{i}^{-1}f,\;[f,b_{j}],\;[f,c_{k}],\;\prod_{i=1}^{g}z_{i}^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\prod_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}f^{-b},\;c_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}f^{\beta_{k}}\Big\rangle.

(7)

The presentations in Equations (
5
) and (
7
) can be derived from the Seifert–van Kampen theorem by decomposing the base orbifold into a punctured surface together with neighborhoods of the cone points, and then lifting this decomposition to the Seifert fibration. We omit the details and refer to
[
BMP03
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.2]
and
[
Orl72
,
Sco83
]
.

If
X
is a closed Seifert fibered
3
-manifold admitting an NPC metric compatible with the Seifert fibration, then the Cartan–Hadamard theorem implies that
Y\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
, so
\chi^{\text{orb}}(O)\leq 0
. Also, applying Equation (
4
) to the presentation of
\pi_{1}(X)
in Equation (
5
) and (
7
) gives
e(X)=0
. If
X
has nonempty boundary, the double
DX
of
X
is a closed Seifert fibered
3
-manifold with a
C^{2}
-differentiable NPC metric, so again by the Cartan–Hadamard theorem,
Y
is a simply connected subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose boundary components are diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
. This gives the following.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
X\rightarrow O
be an orientable Seifert fibered
3
-manifold with nonempty boundary. If
X
admits an NPC metric
g
compatible with the Seifert fibration of
X
, such that
\partial X
is totally geodesic with respect to
g
and
g|_{\partial X}
is flat, then
e(X)=0
and
\chi^{\text{orb}}(O)\leq 0
.

More importantly, the interpretation of the pair of representations
(\rho^{\prime},\phi)
allows us to construct geometric metrics on
X
. Since every
X\rightarrow O
with
e(X)=0
and
\chi^{\text{orb}}(O)\leq 0
is modeled on
\mathbb{E}^{3}
or
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, and the metrics on these homogeneous spaces are NPC, every such
X
admits an NPC metric for which
\partial X
is totally geodesic and flat. However, if we are interested in NPC metrics on
X
that restrict to specific metrics on the boundary components, which is always the case when we study manifolds by gluing Seifert fibered pieces together, it is a nontrivial fact that not every such
X
admits an NPC metric extending a given collection of flat metrics on the boundary tori, cf.
[
Lee95
,
BS05
]
. Nevertheless, the interpretation of the pair
(\rho^{\prime},\phi)
gives us the tools to study conditions under which the extension problem has an affirmative solution.

We now recall the metric extension criterion on homology (MECH) from
[
Lee95
,
BS05
]
.
A flat metric
g
on a torus
T^{2}
induces an inner product
\sigma_{g}
on the real vector space
H_{1}(T^{2},\mathbb{R})
as follows: given an element
a\in H_{1}(T^{2})
, we let
\sigma_{g}(a,a)
be the squared length of the geodesic representative of
a
. We then extend
\sigma_{g}(a,a)
linearly to
a\in H_{1}(T^{2},\mathbb{R})\cong H_{1}(T^{2})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. Finally, we obtain
\sigma_{g}(a,b)
for all
a,b\in H_{1}(T^{2},\mathbb{R})
via polarization:

\sigma_{g}(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{g}(a+b,a+b)-\sigma_{g}(a,a)-\sigma_{g}(b,b)).

Given any diffeomorphism
f:T\rightarrow T
, we have

\sigma_{f^{*}g}(a,b)=\sigma_{g}(f_{*}a,f_{*}b).

(8)

In particular, if
f\in\operatorname{Diff}_{0}(T)
, then the induced maps
f_{*}
and
f^{*}
on homology are trivial, so
\sigma_{f^{*}g}=\sigma_{g}
.

Different versions of MECH appear in
[
Lee95
, Lemma 2.5]
and
[
BS05
, Lemma 5.3]
. We give a self-contained proof tailored to our applications.

Proposition 2.11

(MECH)
.

Given a compact orientable Seifert fibered
3
-manifold
X\rightarrow O
with boundary components
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
, such that
e(X)=0
and
\chi^{\text{orb}}(X)\leq 0
, let
(f_{j},b_{j})
be any Waldhausen basis of
H_{1}(\partial X)
.
For each
j\in[n]
, let
g_{j}
be a flat metric on
T_{j}
. Then the following are true.

(a)

The metrics
g_{j}
extend to an NPC metric
g
on
X
compatible with the Seifert fibration of
X
, for which all the
T_{j}
are totally geodesic, if and only if
\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},f_{j})
is independent of
j
and

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},b_{j})=0.

(9)

(b)

The extended NPC metric
g
can be constructed to be flat on a collar neighborhood of the boundary components.

Proof.

We first prove (a).
Assume first that
X
has an NPC metric
g
restricting to each
g_{j}
on
T_{j}
. Consider the representation
\phi:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{R})
induced by
g
, which exists because
g
is compatible with the Seifert fibration of
X
. In particular,
\phi(f)
is the length of the generic fiber, and for all
x\in\pi_{1}(X)
that preserve the orientation of the fiber circle,
\phi(x)
measures the signed translation distance in the
\mathbb{R}
-direction of the deck action of
x
on
Y\times\mathbb{R}
. Since
i_{*}b_{j}
preserves the orientation of the fiber circle, we have

\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},pf_{j}+qb_{j})=p\phi(f)^{2}+q\phi(f)\phi(i_{*}b_{j}).

Taking
p=1
and
q=0
, we see that
\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},f_{j})=\phi(f)^{2}
, so it is independent of
j
. Also, Equation (
9
) holds because

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},b_{j})=\phi(f)\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi(i_{*}b_{j})=\phi(f)\cdot\phi\left(i_{*}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\right)\right)=0.

The last equality follows from the fact that
(f_{j},b_{j})
is a Waldhausen basis.

For the converse direction, suppose
(f_{j},b_{j})
is a Waldhausen basis of
H_{1}(\partial X)
satisfying Equation (
9
) and that
\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},f_{j})
is independent of
j
. We assume that
X
is modeled on
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
(the Euclidean case is analogous, so we omit it). Pick
\rho^{\prime}:\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O)\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
so that the squared length
L_{j}^{2}
of the
j
th boundary curve is the length of the component of
b_{j}
orthogonal to
f_{j}
in the flat torus metric
g_{j}
:

L_{j}^{2}=\sigma_{g_{j}}(b_{j},b_{j})-\frac{\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},b_{j})^{2}}{\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},f_{j})}.

Such a representation exists because hyperbolic structures on
O
can realize arbitrary boundary curve lengths (this follows, for example, from the length parameters in the Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates on Teichmüller space). Construct
\phi:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{R})
by specifying the images of the generators in Equation (
5
) or (
7
). Specifically, we let
\phi
map
f
to
\sqrt{\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},f_{j})}
and
b_{j}
to
\sigma_{g_{j}}(f_{j},b_{j})/\phi(f)
. We then map
c_{k}
to
-\frac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\,\phi(f)
. If
O
is orientable, then we send
x_{i},y_{i}\mapsto 0
. If
O
is non-orientable, we send
z_{i}
to the reflection across
0
. The pair
(\rho^{\prime},\phi)
then induces a cocompact discrete faithful representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
by

\rho(a)=(\rho^{\prime}\circ\pi_{*}(a),\phi(a)),

where
\pi_{*}:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O)
is the induced homomorphism. The metric
g
induced by
\rho
satisfies the desired properties.

For (b), notice that if
X
is modeled on
\mathbb{E}^{3}
, then the geometric metric induced by the representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{E}^{3})
is already flat, so we only need to consider the case where
X
is modeled on
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
. Since we are working locally near
\partial X
, we may remove the exceptional fibers of
X
and treat it as a circle bundle over a hyperbolic surface
\Sigma
with geodesic boundary curves. Near each boundary component
T_{j}\subset\partial X
, there is a unique geodesic
\gamma_{j}
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
that projects to
T_{j}\cap\Sigma
. We can choose
r_{j}>0
small enough so that the
r_{j}
-neighborhood
N_{r_{j}}
of
T_{j}
in
X
pulls back under the universal covering map to

\tilde{N}_{r_{j}}=\gamma_{j}\times\mathbb{R}\times[0,r_{j}).

Here,
\tilde{N}_{r_{j}}
projects to
\gamma_{j}\times[0,r_{j})\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
. The metric on
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
restricts to a warped product metric
\tilde{g}_{j}
on
\tilde{N}_{r_{j}}

\tilde{g}_{j}=\cosh^{2}(t)\,ds^{2}+d\theta^{2}+dt^{2}.

Here,
s
is the coordinate in the direction of the geodesic
\gamma_{j}
,
t
is the coordinate in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
normal to the geodesic
\gamma_{j}
, and
\theta
is the coordinate in the
\mathbb{R}
-factor of
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.
In particular,
N_{r_{j}}
is the quotient of
\tilde{N}_{r_{j}}
by an isometric action of
\pi_{1}(T_{j})
given by a representation
\rho_{j}:\pi_{1}(T_{j})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Isom}(\tilde{N}_{r_{j}},\tilde{g}_{j})
sending

\rho_{j}(\alpha)(s,\theta,t)=(\rho_{j}^{\prime}(\alpha)(s,\theta),t)\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\pi_{1}(T_{j}).

Here,
\rho_{j}^{\prime}
is the action of
\pi_{1}(T_{j})
on

\displaystyle 0

Proposition 3.5

Proof of Proposition
3.1

to obtain Waldhausen bases

By Corollary

\displaystyle\sigma_{\bar{g}|_{T_{1,j}}}(f_{1,j},f_{1,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\psi\in S}\sigma_{g|_{T_{i,j}}}(f_{i,\psi(j)},f_{i,\psi(j)})

\displaystyle=|S|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{g|_{T_{i,j}}}(f_{i,j},f_{i,j}).

The last equality follows from the fact that
\sigma_{g|_{T_{i,j}}}(f_{i,j},f_{i,j})
is independent of

satisfies the conditions in Proposition

∎

3.2
Proof of Proposition
3.5

The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
3.5
.
Denote by
T^{2}
the closed, connected, orientable surface of genus
1
. Let
\mathbb{T}^{2}\cong\operatorname{SO}(2)^{2}
denote the rank-
2
compact abelian Lie group. There is a homomorphism

\alpha:\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{T}^{2})

defined by

\alpha\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\right):(z,w)\mapsto(z^{a}w^{b},z^{c}w^{d})

Here, we treat
\operatorname{SO}(2)
as the unit complex numbers.

A
torus subgroup
of
\operatorname{Diff}_{0}(T^{2})
is an equivalence class of faithful representations

\rho:\mathbb{T}^{2}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Diff}_{0}(T^{2}),

where two representations
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are declared equivalent if there exists
A\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
such that

\rho_{2}=\rho_{1}\circ\alpha(A).

Thus, we quotient by reparametrizations of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, so that a torus subgroup records only the image
\rho(\mathbb{T}^{2})\subset\operatorname{Diff}_{0}(T^{2})
and not the particular marking of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

We start by showing that faithful actions of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
on
T^{2}
are free and transitive. To do so, we need the following lemma, which follows from naturality of the Riemannian metric exponential map with isometries. For a proof, see
[
Lee19
, Proposition 5.22]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(M,g)
be a connected Riemannian manifold, and let
\phi:M\rightarrow M
be an isometry such that there exists
x\in M
with
\phi(x)=x
and
d\phi_{x}=\operatorname{Id}_{T_{x}M}
. Then,
\phi=\operatorname{Id}_{M}
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a connected Riemannian manifold, and let
G
be a Lie group acting isometrically on
M
. If there exists a point
x\in M
such that for all
\phi\in G
,
\phi(x)=x
and
d\phi_{x}=\operatorname{Id}_{T_{x}M}
, then,
G
acts trivially on
M
.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
3.6
to every
\phi\in G
to get
\phi=\operatorname{Id}_{M}
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Every faithful smooth action of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
on
T^{2}
is free and transitive.

Proof.

We first show that any faithful action of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
on
T^{2}
is free. We start by observing that there exists a Riemannian metric
g
on
T^{2}
such that
\mathbb{T}^{2}
acts isometrically: one may take
g
to be the average of an arbitrary Riemannian metric on
T^{2}
by the Haar measure of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Therefore, we may without loss of generality assume that
\mathbb{T}^{2}
acts isometrically on
(T^{2},g)
.

For all
x\in T^{2}
, let
S_{x}<\mathbb{T}^{2}
be the stabilizer subgroup of
x
. Note that
S_{x}
is always a closed Lie subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Denote by
\operatorname{Orb}(x)\subset T^{2}
the orbit of
x
under the action of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem for smooth Lie group actions, we have
\operatorname{Orb}(x)\cong\mathbb{T}^{2}/S_{x}
. In particular, since
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is abelian,
S_{x}
acts trivially on
\operatorname{Orb}(x)
. We show that
S_{x}=\{e\}
and
\operatorname{Orb}(x)=T^{2}
.

We first consider the case where
\dim S_{x}=2
. In this case,
S_{x}
is an open Lie subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Since
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is connected, we must have
S_{x}=\mathbb{T}^{2}
. It follows that
\mathbb{T}^{2}
fixes
x
. Also, the image of
S_{x}
under the differential map
d_{x}:S_{x}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(T_{x}T^{2})
given by

d_{x}(\phi)=\left(d\phi_{x}:T_{x}T^{2}\rightarrow T_{x}T^{2}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}(T_{x}T^{2})

(18)

must be contained in a maximal compact subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(T_{x}T^{2})\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, which is 1-dimensional. It follows that
\ker(d_{x})<\mathbb{T}^{2}
is nontrivial, so by Corollary
3.7
,
\ker(d_{x})
acts trivially on
T^{2}
, which contradicts faithfulness of the
\mathbb{T}^{2}
action.

Next, we consider the case where
\dim S_{x}=1
. In this case, we have
S_{x}\cong\operatorname{SO}(2)\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem for smooth Lie group actions, we get that the orbit
\operatorname{Orb}(x)\cong\mathbb{T}^{2}/S_{x}\cong S^{1}
, so
\operatorname{Orb}(x)
is a smoothly embedded simple closed curve in
T^{2}
. Now, let
d_{x}:S_{x}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(T_{x}T^{2})
be the differential map as in Equation (
18
). Since
S_{x}
acts trivially on
\operatorname{Orb}(x)
, each element of
S_{x}
fixes the tangent line
T_{x}\operatorname{Orb}(x)
pointwise. By picking a suitable basis for
T_{x}T^{2}
, we get that

d_{x}(\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}1&f_{1}(\phi)\\
0&f_{2}(\phi)\end{pmatrix}

where
f_{1}(\phi)\in\mathbb{R}
and
f_{2}(\phi)\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
. It follows that
d_{x}(S_{x})
lies in a Lie subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(T_{x}T^{2})
that is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{\times}
. Since the maximal compact subgroup of
\mathbb{R}\rtimes\mathbb{R}^{\times}
is finite, the kernel
K=\ker(d_{x})<S_{x}<\mathbb{T}^{2}
is a nontrivial subgroup and acts trivially on
T^{2}
by Corollary
3.7
, which contradicts faithfulness of the
\mathbb{T}^{2}
action.

Finally, we consider the case where
\dim S_{x}=0
. In this case,
S_{x}
is a finite subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, so
S_{x}\cong\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
for some positive integers
m,n
. Again, by the orbit-stabilizer theorem for smooth Lie group actions, we get
\operatorname{Orb}(x)\cong\mathbb{T}^{2}/S_{x}\cong T^{2}
. We claim that
\operatorname{Orb}(x)=T^{2}
. Denote by
\Phi_{x}:\mathbb{T}^{2}\rightarrow T^{2}
the orbit map sending

\Phi_{x}(\phi)=\phi(x).

Observe that
\Phi_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{2})=\operatorname{Orb}(x)
. Notice that
\Phi_{x}
is closed, since the domain is compact and the codomain is Hausdorff. To see that
\Phi_{x}
is open, we show that it is a local diffeomorphism. It suffices to show that
\Phi_{x}
is a local diffeomorphism at
e
. Notice that
\ker d(\Phi_{x})_{e}
is precisely the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}_{x}
of
S_{x}
, and from
\dim S_{x}=0
we get that
\dim\ker d(\Phi_{x})_{e}=\dim\mathfrak{s}_{x}=0
,
d(\Phi_{x})_{e}
is full-rank.
It follows that
\Phi_{x}
is both a closed map and an open map, so
\operatorname{Orb}(x)=\Phi_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
is clopen in
T^{2}
, which gives us
\operatorname{Orb}(x)=T^{2}
. Finally, since every element of
S_{x}
acts trivially on
\operatorname{Orb}(x)=T^{2}
, the faithfulness of the
\mathbb{T}^{2}
action gives us
S_{x}=\{e\}
. We conclude that
\mathbb{T}^{2}
must act freely and transitively.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}

Proof.

For (a), we start with arbitrary representatives

of the torus subgroups

\rho_{i}
is a faithful representation, each action of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
on

induced by

is free and transitive. This implies the orbit map

\Phi_{i}:\mathbb{T}^{2}\rightarrow T^{2},\qquad\Phi_{i}(g)=\rho_{i}(g)(p)

are diffeomorphims. Fix a basis
(a,b)
of

(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
be a basis of

generated by the coordinate directions of
\operatorname{SO}(2)^{2}
. Let

be the matrix of the induced homomorphism

. Set

For (c), notice that if

Remark 3.10
.

Lemma
3.8
could be avoided by defining torus subgroups to act freely and transitively. With this stronger condition in the definition, all parts of Lemma
3.9
would still hold. We instead choose to only assume faithfulness and provide a self-contained proof of Lemma
3.8
.

Corollary 3.11
.

If
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
are flat metrics on
T^{2}
, and
\rho:\mathbb{T}^{2}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Diff}_{0}(T^{2})
factors through
\operatorname{Isom}(T^{2},g_{i})
for every
i\in[n]
, then
\rho
factors through
\operatorname{Isom}(T^{2},\bar{g})
, where

\bar{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}.

In particular, the metric
\bar{g}
is flat on
T^{2}
.

Proof.

For all
\phi\in\rho(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, we know that
\phi^{*}g_{i}=g_{i}
. This in particular implies

\phi^{*}\bar{g}=\phi^{*}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi^{*}g_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}=\bar{g}.

Therefore,
\rho:\mathbb{T}^{2}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Diff}_{0}(T^{2})
factors through
\operatorname{Isom}(T^{2},\bar{g})
. By Lemma
3.9
(c),
\bar{g}
is flat.
∎

We now proceed to prove Proposition
3.5
. The central idea behind the proof is the following. Suppose

is a finite set on which a finite group
G
acts transitively, and suppose one would like to assign a structure
a_{i}
to each

in a way that is compatible with the group action, but assigning all

simultaneously is difficult. One begins by choosing a structure

on

that is compatible with the action of the stabilizer subgroup

of

. Then, by the orbit–stabilizer theorem,

can be decomposed into cosets

, where each

to

. One then defines a structure

on

by requiring that

and

are compatible. The resulting collection

is compatible with the action of the whole group

by purely group-theoretic considerations. Nevertheless, this construction is not “natural” in the sense that it depends on the choice of the basepoint

and the choice of representatives

.

In the proof of Proposition

, the set

will be the collection of torus components of

, which acts transitively on

will be diffeomorphisms
