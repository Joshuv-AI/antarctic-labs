Introduction

Consider the stochastic nonlinear oscillator equation

\ddot{x}_{t}+\beta\dot{x}_{t}+{\cal U}^{\prime}(x_{t})=\sigma\dot{W}_{t}

(1)

with
\beta>0
and
\sigma\neq 0
, where
{\cal U}(x)
is the single well potential
{\cal U}(x)=ax^{2}/2+bx^{4}/4
with
a,b>0
. In phase space we have the system

\begin{split}dx_{t}&=y_{t}\,dt\\
dy_{t}&=(-ax_{t}-bx_{t}^{3}-\beta y_{t})\,dt+\sigma\circ dW_{t}.\end{split}

(2)

It is well known, see for example Mattingly, Stuart and Higham
[
15
]
, that the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
is positive recurrent on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with unique invariant probability measure
\mu
with density
\rho(x,y)
of the form

\rho(x,y)=C\exp\left\{\frac{-2\beta}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{ax^{2}}{2}+\frac{bx^{4}}{4}+\frac{y^{2}}{2}\right)\right\}

(3)

where
C>0
is the normalizing constant.
Linearizing (
2
) along the trajectory
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
we get the derivative process
\{v_{t}:t\geq 0\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

dv_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-a-3bx_{t}^{2}&-\beta\end{bmatrix}v_{t}dt.

The (top) Lyapunov exponent is the almost sure limit

\lambda(\beta,\sigma,a,b)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|v_{t}\|.

(4)

We will see in Proposition
2.1
that the right side of (
4
) is an almost sure limit with the same value for all initial values
(x_{0},y_{0},v_{0})
with
v_{0}\neq 0
.

Arnold, Imkeller and Sri Namachchivaya
[
2
]
study the case of small noise and small dissipation by replacing
\sigma\rightsquigarrow\varepsilon\sigma
and
\beta\rightsquigarrow\varepsilon^{2}\beta
, and they state without proof that
\lambda(\varepsilon^{2}\beta,\varepsilon\sigma,a,b)\sim\varepsilon^{2/3}\overline{\lambda}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for some constant
\overline{\lambda}>0
. In this paper we present a proof of this assertion.

Replacing
(x_{t},y_{t},W_{t})
with
(Bx_{At},ABy_{At},A^{-1/2}W_{At})
gives a version of (
2
) with the parameters
(\beta,\sigma,a,b)
replaced with parameters
(A\beta,A^{3/2}\sigma/B,A^{2}a,A^{2}B^{2}b)
. It follows that

\lambda(\beta,\sigma,a,b)=\frac{1}{A}\lambda(A\beta,A^{3/2}\sigma/B,A^{2}a/B,A^{2}B^{2}b)

for all
A,B>0
, and in particular

\lambda(\beta,\sigma,a,b)=a^{1/2}\lambda(a^{-1/2}\beta,a^{-5/4}b^{1/2}\sigma,1,1).

So without loss of generality we may assume
a=b=1
.
With
a=b=1
and the replacement
\beta\rightsquigarrow\varepsilon^{2}\beta
and
\sigma\rightsquigarrow\varepsilon\sigma
the system becomes

\begin{split}dx_{t}&=y_{t}\,dt\\
dy_{t}&=(-x_{t}-x_{t}^{3}-\varepsilon^{2}\beta y_{t})\,dt+\varepsilon\sigma\circ dW_{t}.\end{split}

(5)

The system (
5
) may be written in the form

d(x_{t},y_{t})=\overline{\nabla}H(x_{t},y_{t})dt+\varepsilon^{2}V_{0}(x_{t},y_{t})dt+\varepsilon V_{1}(x_{t},y_{t})\circ dW_{t}

(6)

representing the Hamiltonian flow for a function
H(x,y)
with a small perturbation as
\varepsilon\to 0
determined by the drift and noise vector fields
V_{0}(x,y)
and
V_{1}(x,y)
. The calculations in this paper start with the framework of Baxendale and Goukasian
[
4
]
which deals with (
6
) in the case when
H(x,y)
has a single critical point at
(0,0)
and
H(x,y)\to\infty
as
\|(x,y)\|\to\infty
and
V_{0}(x,y)
and
V_{1}(x,y)
are general vector fields.

We will use the general notation of (
6
) as long as possible, before restricting to the special case (
5
) by replacing the general function
H
and vector fields
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
with the specific formulas

H(x,y)={\cal U}(x)+y^{2}/2=x^{2}/2+x^{4}/4+y^{2}/2

(7)

and

V_{0}(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-\beta y\end{bmatrix},\qquad V_{1}(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\sigma\end{bmatrix}.

(8)

The reader is cautioned to distinguish carefully between results for the general system (
6
) and results for the particular system (
5
).

1.1
Main result

Define orthogonal vector fields

U_{1}(x,y)=\overline{\nabla}H(x,y)\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad U_{2}(x,y)=\frac{\nabla H(x,y)}{\|\nabla H(x,y)\|^{2}}

(9)

for
(x,y)\neq(0,0)
. Using the notation
V.F
to denote the action of a vector field
V
as a differential operator on a function
F
, we have
U_{1}.H(x,y)=0
and
U_{2}.H(x,y)=1
.

For
h>0
let the probability measure
m_{h}
denote the normalized occupation time measure for the Hamiltonian flow around the orbit
H^{-1}(h)=\{(x,y):H(x,y)=h\}
, and let
T(h)
denote the period of the Hamiltonian flow around
H^{-1}(h)
. Define

\displaystyle G_{1}(h)

\displaystyle=-\frac{T^{\prime}(h)}{T(h)},

(10)

\displaystyle G_{2}(h)

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{-1}(h)}\bigl((\overline{\nabla}H.(V_{1}.H))(x,y)\bigr)^{2}dm_{h}(x,y),

(11)

\displaystyle G(h)

\displaystyle=|G_{1}(h)|^{2/3}\bigl(G_{2}(h)\bigr)^{1/3}.

(12)

In the following result the measure
\mu
denotes the invariant probability measure for the system (
5
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It does not depend on
\varepsilon>0
and has density
\rho(x,y)
given by (
3
) with
a=b=1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For fixed
\beta>0
and
\sigma\neq 0
the Lyapunov exponent for the system (
5
) satisfies

\lambda(\varepsilon^{2}\beta,\varepsilon\sigma,1,1)=\varepsilon^{2/3}\overline{\lambda}+{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{4/3})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where

\overline{\lambda}=\gamma_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}G(H(x,y))d\mu(x,y)\in(0,\infty)

and
\gamma_{0}=\pi/\bigl(2^{1/3}3^{1/6}[\Gamma(1/3)]^{2}\bigr)\approx 0.29
.

A related integral formula for
\overline{\lambda}
in terms of an associated action-angle coordinate system in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
is stated without proof in
[
2
]
.

Exact formulas for
T(h)
,
G_{1}(h)
and
G_{2}(h)
, and hence for
G(h)
, in terms of complete elliptic integrals are given in Section
4.1
. In particular
G_{1}(h)>0
for all
h>0
.
The property
T^{\prime}(h)<0
implies that orbits with larger
h
have smaller periods, so we may interpret
G_{1}(h)
as the time averaged shearing effect of the Hamiltonian flow in a small neighborhood of the orbit
H^{-1}(h)
. More precisely, consider
(x,y)
with
H(x,y)=h
, and a nearby point
(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})=(x,y)+\delta U_{2}(x,y)
, so that
H(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})=h+\delta+{\cal O}(\delta^{2})
. Using (
18
) and (
25
) we see that after time
T(h)
the point
(x,y)
has returned to
(x,y)
but the nearby point
(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})
is now at
(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})-\delta T^{\prime}(h)U_{1}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})+{\cal O}(\delta^{2})
.

Also
V_{1}.H(x,y)
corresponds to the change in
H(x,y)
caused by a “kick”
V_{1}(x,y)
, and then
U_{1}.(V_{1}.H))
is the variation of the effect of the kick along the orbit. Thus
G_{2}(h)
corresponds to the mean square variation along the orbit of the random kicking effect of the noise vector field
V_{1}
.
Theorem
1.1
quantifies how the shearing and the random kicking effects on the Hamiltonian orbits together contribute to the positivity of the Lyapunov exponent. Related results on the effects of shearing and periodic or random kicking on limit cycles appear in Wang and Young
[
18
]
, Lin and Young
[
14
]
and Engel, Lamb and Rasmussen
[
8
]
.

Furstenberg-Khasminskii formula

Write
v_{t}=\|v_{t}\|\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_{t}\\
\sin\theta_{t}\end{bmatrix}
. For the system (
5
) we have

d\log\|v_{t}\|=(-3x_{t}^{2}\sin\theta_{t}\cos\theta_{t}-\varepsilon^{2}\beta\sin^{2}\theta_{t})dt

(13)

and

d\theta_{t}=\widetilde{Q}(x_{t},y_{t},\theta_{t})dt

(14)

where

\widetilde{Q}_{\varepsilon}(x,y,\theta)=-3x^{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta-\varepsilon^{2}\beta\sin^{2}\theta.

(15)

Proposition 2.1
.

For the system (
5
) and for all
(x_{0},y_{0},v_{0})
with
v_{0}\neq 0
the limit

\lambda(\varepsilon^{2}\beta,\varepsilon\sigma,1,1)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|v_{t}\|

exists almost surely and has the value

\lambda(\varepsilon^{2}\beta,\varepsilon\sigma,1,1)=\int\widetilde{Q}_{\varepsilon}(x,y,\theta)d\nu_{\varepsilon}(x,y,\theta)

(16)

where
\nu_{\varepsilon}
is the unique invariant probability measure for the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t},\theta_{t}):t\geq 0\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
given by (
5
,
14
).

Proof.

We follow the scheme of
[
5
, Section 8]
. The existence of the invariant probability
\mu
for
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
together with the compactness of
\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
implies the existence of at least one invariant probability measure
\nu_{\varepsilon}
for the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t},\theta_{t}):t\geq 0\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
.
The parabolic Hörmander condition proved in Lemma
A.1
implies
\nu_{\varepsilon}
has a smooth density (see
[
11
, Theorem 4]
), and the controllability proved in Lemma
A.2
together with the support theorem of Stroock and Varadhan
[
17
]
imply
\nu_{\varepsilon}(U)>0
for all open
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
. It follows that
\nu_{\varepsilon}
is unique and
\mbox{supp}(\nu_{\varepsilon})=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover, the time
t
transition probability
P_{t}((x,y,\theta),\cdot)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu_{\varepsilon}
for all
t>0
and
(x,y,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
, see
[
11
, Prop 5.1]
.

Integrating (
13
) with respect to
t
and using the ergodic theorem we get

\frac{1}{t}\log\|v_{t}\|=\frac{1}{t}\log\|v_{0}\|+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Q}_{\varepsilon}(x_{s},y_{s},\theta_{s})ds\to\int\widetilde{Q}_{\varepsilon}(x,y,\theta)d\nu_{\varepsilon}(x,y,\theta)

almost surely as
t\to\infty
for
\nu_{\varepsilon}
almost all
(x_{0},y_{0},\theta_{0})
. Since the time 1 transition probability
P_{1}((x_{0},y_{0},\theta_{0}),\cdot)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu_{\varepsilon}
we get

\frac{1}{t}\log\|v_{t}\|=\frac{1}{t}\log\|v_{0}\|+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Q}_{\varepsilon}(x_{s},y_{s},\theta_{s})ds\to\int\widetilde{Q}_{\varepsilon}(x,y,\theta)d\nu_{\varepsilon}(x,y,\theta)

almost surely as
t\to\infty
for all
(x_{0},y_{0},\theta_{0})
, equivalently for all
(x_{0},y_{0},v_{0})
with
v\neq 0
, giving the Furstenberg-Khasminskii formula (
16
).
∎

5.1
The process

Recall that the Hamiltonian frame given by (
17

(x,y)=(0,0)
, and so the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t},\phi_{t}):t\geq 0\}
is well-defined only at times
t
when
(x_{t},y_{t})\neq(0,0)
.
In the setting of
[
4
]
with multiplicative noise the point
(0,0)
was fixed and the set
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
was invariant for the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
. Therefore
\{(x_{t},y_{t},\phi_{t}):t\geq 0\}
was a well-defined diffusion process on
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\})\times\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
and the tools of ergodic theory were available. But with additive noise the point
(0,0)
is no longer fixed. We will not investigate the issue of the attainability of
(0,0)
for the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
. We will not attempt to apply ergodic theory to
\{(x_{t},y_{t},\phi_{t}):t\geq 0\}
. Instead we will use ergodic properties of the underlying process
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in Lemma
5.1
below to obtain bounds on limiting time averages of Lebesgue and Itô integrals involving integrands of the form
F(x_{t},y_{t},\phi_{t})
with the property that
F(x,y,\phi)=0
whenever
(x,y)\neq(0,0)
. Recall
\mu
is the invariant probability measure for the system (
5
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and it does not depend on
\varepsilon>0
.

Lemma 5.1
.

(i) Suppose
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is in
L^{1}(\mu)
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
and all
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}g(x_{n},y_{n})\to 0\quad\mbox{almost surely}.

(ii) Suppose the process
\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
satisfies
|X_{t}|\leq g(x_{t},y_{t})
where
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is in
L^{1}(\mu)
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
and all
(x_{0},y_{0},v_{0})
with
v_{0}\neq 0

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\left|\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}ds\right|\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(x_{s},y_{s})ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x,y)d\mu(x,y)<\infty

almost surely.

(iii) Suppose the process
\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is non-anticipating and satisfies
|X_{t}|\leq g(x_{t},y_{t})
where
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
and all
(x_{0},y_{0},v_{0})
with
v_{0}\neq 0

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}dW_{s}=0

almost surely.

Proof.

(i) The Borel-Cantelli lemma implies
(1/n)g(x_{n},y_{n})\to 0
almost surely for
\mu
almost all
(x_{0},y_{0})
. Since the time 1 transition probability
P_{1}((x_{0},y_{0}),\cdot)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
, it follows that
(1/n)g(x_{n},y_{n})\to 0
almost surely for all
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

(ii) The first inequality is deterministic, and the second uses the ergodic theorem together the absolute continuity of the time 1 transition probability.

(iii) Applying (ii) to
(X_{t})^{2}
gives

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(X_{s})^{2}ds\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bigl(g(x,y)\bigr)^{2}d\mu(x,y)<\infty

almost surely, and hence (see for example
[
6

, also
[
12
, Theorem 3.4.6]
)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}dW_{s}=0

almost surely. ∎

\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]

\chi(h)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }h\leq 1\\
1&\mbox{ if }h\geq 2.\end{cases}

(36)

There are constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that
|\chi^{\prime}(h)|\leq c_{1}
and
|\chi^{\prime\prime}(h)|\leq c_{2}
for all
h
. For
\varepsilon>0
define
\chi_{\varepsilon}(h)=\chi(h/\varepsilon)
, and note that
|\chi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(h)|\leq c_{1}/\varepsilon
and
|\chi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(h)|\leq c_{2}/\varepsilon^{2}
.

For the system (
5
) the distribution of the process
\{(x_{t},y_{t}):t\geq 0\}
depends on
\varepsilon
, but the invariant probability measure
\mu
does not depend on
\varepsilon
. So if
g(x,y)
does not depend on
\varepsilon
then the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x,y)d\mu(x,y)
does not depend on
\varepsilon
.
The following lemma relates the truncation parameter
\varepsilon
with the rate of blow-up of
g(x,y)
as
r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\to 0
. It is designed to take advantage of the fact, seen in the estimates (
35
) for the
Q_{i}
and
t_{i}
, that functions associated with higher powers of
\varepsilon
typically have worse blow-up as
r\to 0
. Recall
h=H(x,y)\sim r^{2}/2
as
r\to 0
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\to[0,\infty)
satisfies
\int_{r>\delta}g(x,y)d\mu(x,y)<\infty
for each fixed
\delta>0
and
g(x,y)={\cal O}(r^{k})
as
r\to 0
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{\varepsilon}(h)g(x,y)d\mu(x,y)\begin{cases}\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x,y)d\mu(x,y)&\mbox{ if }k>-2,\\
={\cal O}(\log 1/\varepsilon)&\mbox{ if }k=-2,\\
={\cal O}(\varepsilon^{(k+2)/2})&\mbox{ if }k<-2\end{cases}

(37)

and

\int_{\varepsilon\leq h\leq 2\varepsilon}g(x,y)d\mu(x,y)=\begin{cases}{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{(k+2)/2})&\mbox{ if }k\neq-2,\\
{\cal O}(\log 1/\varepsilon)&\mbox{ if }k=-2\end{cases}

(38)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

This follows easily from the fact that the density
\rho(x,y)
of
\mu
satisfies
\rho(x,y)\to C>0
as
(x,y)\to(0,0)
, see (
3
). ∎

5.3
Equivalence of norms

The heuristic argument in Section
3.1
suggests to study
\|\widetilde{w}_{t}\|
in place of
\|v_{t}\|
. The following definition, which avoids the singularity in
\|\widetilde{w_{t}}\|
when
(x_{t},y_{t})
is close to
(0,0)
, was motivated by a similar construction in Chemnitz and Engel
[
6
]
. For
\varepsilon>0
and
t\geq 0
define

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)=\bigl(1-\chi_{\varepsilon}(H(x_{t},y_{t}))\bigr)\log\|v_{t}\|+\chi_{\varepsilon}(H(x_{t},y_{t}))\log\|\widetilde{w}_{t}\|.

(39)

Proposition 5.1
.

For the system (
5
), for all
\varepsilon>0
and all
(x_{0},y_{0},v_{0})
with
v_{0}\neq 0

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Big|\log\|v_{n}\|-\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(n)\Big|=0

almost surely.

Proof.

From (
17
) we have

\displaystyle v_{t}

\displaystyle=w_{1,t}\Gamma(H(x_{t},y_{t}))U_{1}(x_{t},y_{t})+w_{2.t}U_{2}(x_{t},y_{t})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{1,t}\varepsilon^{-2/3}\Gamma(H(x_{t},y_{t}))U_{1}(x_{t},y_{t})+\widetilde{w}_{2.t}U_{2}(x_{t},y_{t})

so that

\|v_{t}\|\leq\max\Bigl(\varepsilon^{-2/3}\Gamma(H(x_{t},y_{t}))\|\nabla H(x_{t},y_{t})\|,\|\nabla H(x_{t},y_{t})\|^{-1}\Bigr)\|\widetilde{w}_{t}\|,

and similarly

\|\widetilde{w}_{t}\|\leq\max\Bigl(\varepsilon^{2/3}(\Gamma(H(x_{t},y_{t})))^{-1}\|\nabla H(x_{t},y_{t})\|^{-1},\|\nabla H(x_{t},y_{t})\|\Bigr)\|v_{t}\|

It follows that

\Big|\log\|\widetilde{w}_{t}\|-\log\|v_{t}\|\Big|\leq\frac{2}{3}|\log\varepsilon|+\Big|\log\Gamma(H(x_{t},y_{t}))\Big|+\Big|\log\|\nabla H(x_{t},y_{t})\|\Big|,

(40)

and so

\displaystyle\Bigl|\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)-\log\|v_{t}\|\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{t})\Bigl|\log\|\widetilde{w}_{t}\|-\log\|v_{t}\|\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{t})\left(\frac{2}{3}|\log\varepsilon|+\Big|\log\|\nabla H(x_{t},y_{t})\|\Big|+\Big|\log\Gamma(H(x_{t},y_{t}))\Big|\right).

Using the exact formula for
\|\nabla H(x,y)\|
and the asymptotics in Lemma
4.3
(i) for
\Gamma(h)
, we see the right side is integrable with respect to the stationary measure
\mu
, and so

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Bigl|\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(n)-\log\|v_{n}\|\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\chi_{\varepsilon}(h_{n})\left(\frac{2}{3}|\log\varepsilon|+\Big|\log\|\nabla H(x_{n},y_{n})\|\Big|+\Big|\log\Gamma(H(x_{n},y_{n}))\Big|\right)

\displaystyle=0

almost surely by Lemma
5.1
. ∎

\displaystyle={\cal O}(\varepsilon^{2})

almost surely.

. Each

.

as

A.1
Parabolic Hörmander condition

Let

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
\sigma\neq 0
and
{\cal U}^{(4)}(x)\neq 0
for all

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume
\sigma=1
. Instead of looking at the vector fields
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
for the sde for

-{\cal U}^{\prime}(x)-\beta y\\
(C-{\cal U}^{\prime\prime}(x)D)v\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widehat{X}_{1}(x,y,v)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix},

where

C=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&-\beta\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}.

Note that

-\beta&-1\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bigl[C,[C,D]\bigr]=\begin{bmatrix}-\beta&-2\\

-1&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mbox{span}\Big\{D,[C,D],\bigl[C,[C,D]\bigr]\Big\}.

0\\

-{\cal U}^{(4)}(x)Dv\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{4}(x,y,v)

\displaystyle:=[\widehat{X}_{3},\widehat{X}_{0}](x,y,v)=\begin{bmatrix}-{\cal U}^{(3)}(x)\\[4.30554pt]

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose

is unbounded above. Given any initial condition
(x_{0},y_{0},\theta_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}/(2\pi Z)

The proof adapts methods used by Coti Zelati and Hairer
[

in (

function

with

and
y(T)=y_{1}
so that the solution of the ODE

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)&=y(t)\\
\dot{\theta}(t)&=g(x(t),\theta(t))\end{split}

(60)

where

sends

at time

to an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of

at time

.

The matrix in (

Lemma A.3
.

Given
(x_{0},y_{0})
and

Take
