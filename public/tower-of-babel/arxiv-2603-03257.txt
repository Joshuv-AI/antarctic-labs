Supercritical sharpness of percolation
Sahar Diskin Philip Easo Ritvik Ramanan Radhakrishnan Benny Sudakov Vincent Tassion

Abstract
We prove that for supercritical percolation on every infinite transitive graph, the probability that the origin belongs to a finite cluster of size at least n n decays exponentially in Φ ⁡ ( n ) \Phi(n) , where Φ \Phi is the isoperimetric function of the graph.

Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite connected (locally-finite, vertex-)transitive graph. Let
\mathrm{P}_{p}
be the standard percolation measure, under which each edge is open with probability
p
independently of all others. Fix a vertex
o

p_{c}
for the critical point given by

(2)

\displaystyle p_{c}\coloneqq\inf\{p\in[0,1]\colon\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\infty)>0\}.

A fundamental result in percolation theory is subcritical sharpness (
[
28
,
1
]
, see also
[
3
,
15
,
14
,
31
]
), which says that for all
p<p_{c}
, there exists
c>0
such that for all

, the cluster

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{p}(|\mathcal{C}_{o}|\geq n)\leq e^{-cn}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{n})\leq e^{-cn},

where
\mathrm{B}_{n}
is the ball of radius

\partial S
is the set of all edges with exactly one endpoint in a given set of vertices

In this paper, we will prove the following supercritical counterpart to subcritical sharpness. Write
\Phi\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
for the isoperimetric function given by

\displaystyle\Phi(n)\coloneqq\min\left\{|\partial S|\colon S\subset V\text{ and }n\leq|S|<\infty\right\}.

.

For all

there exists

such that for all

,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{p}(n\leq|\mathcal{C}_{o}|<\infty)\leq e^{-c\,\Phi(n)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{n}\text{ but }o\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.98611pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.40282pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}\infty)\leq e^{-cn}.

We will deduce Theorem
1
from the following more general result. For each finite set of vertices
W
, define

(6)

\Phi(W):=\min\left\{|\partial S|\colon W\subset S\subset V\text{ and }|S|<\infty\right\}.

Equivalently,
\Phi(W)
is the size of the smallest edge cutset from
W
to
\infty
. Also write

for the set of vertices connected to

Theorem 2
.

For all

there exists

such that for every finite set of vertices
S
and for all

(7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{p}(S\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.98611pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.40282pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}\infty\text{ and }\Phi(\mathcal{C}_{S})\geq n)\leq e^{-cn}.

1.1
Comments

Historical context.

In the first decades after its introduction by Broadbent and Hammersley
[
10
]
, percolation was primarily studied on the hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Both subcritical and supercritical sharpness of the percolation phase transition were ultimately well understood in this context. For relevant textbooks, see
[
21
,
9
]
. In 1996, Benjamini and Schramm launched the study of percolation on arbitrary infinite transitive graphs in their seminal paper,
Percolation beyond
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[
8
]
. See
[
27
, Chapters 7 and 8]
for a survey of the rich research area that has since emerged. The proofs of certain results, such as subcritical sharpness, were easily extended to this more general setting. In our paper, we focus on supercritical sharpness, a result that is well-known in the context of the hypercubic lattice but whose proofs have remained restricted to specific geometries.

Previous results on supercritical sharpness.

In the classical case that
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, supercritical sharpness was deduced in
[
11
]
from the celebrated Grimmett–Marstrand slab theorem
[
20
]
that for all
d\geq 3
,

(8)

\lim_{m\to\infty}p_{c}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\{-m,\ldots,m\}^{d-2}\big)=p_{c}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big).

At the end of our paper, we will explain why conversely, this slab theorem is easily implied by supercritical sharpness.

More recently, supercritical sharpness was extended from
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to every infinite transitive graph of
polynomial growth

[
13
]
. At this level of generality, one cannot use renormalisation schemes that rely on the symmetries of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. However, the geometry of transitive graphs of polynomial growth is nevertheless known to be highly rigid following the foundational works of Gromov and Trofimov
[
22
,
30
]
. In particular,
[
13
]
uses the special fact that minimal cutsets in such graphs are coarsely connected. By a different argument, supercritical sharpness was also established for every infinite transitive graph that is nonamenable
[
23
]
, i.e.​ such that
\inf_{n\geq 1}\Phi(n)/n>0
.

The problem of establishing supercritical sharpness for general infinite transitive graphs has been around as folklore for some time and was recently recorded in
[
23
, Conjectures 5.1–5.3]
.

Many touches suffice

A key tool that is available in the study of percolation under the hypothesis that
p>p_{c}
, but not under the mere hypothesis that
\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\infty)>0
, is
sprinkling
: one can often deduce a strong statement about percolation at
p
from a weaker statement about percolation at
p^{\prime}\in(p_{c},p)
. In this section we will deduce Theorem
2
from the following a priori weaker proposition. The main goal of our paper is to prove this proposition. The proposition is formulated in terms of the following random variable. For a finite set of vertices
S
, define

(11)

\displaystyle\Psi(S)\coloneqq\big|\{e\in\partial S\colon e\xleftrightarrow{V\setminus S}\infty\}\big|.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite connected transitive graph. Let
p\in[0,1]
be such that
\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\infty)>0
. For all
q\in(p,1)
and for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, there exists
c>0
such that for every finite set of vertices
S
and for every positive integer
t
with

,

(12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{q}(\Psi(S)\geq t)\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{t}.

The proof that Proposition
2.1
implies Theorem
2
relies on a variant of a well-known sprinkling argument: if two
p
clusters touch at many places, then the probability that they do not merge at

is small. Here we use a variant of this statement, which asserts that a
p
-cluster has low probability to touch a
(p+\varepsilon)
-cluster at many places without being contained in it. The proof itself is a simple elaboration of an argument from
[

]

3
Sketch of proof

In this section, let us present a sketch of the proof of Proposition
2.1
. Fix a connected finite set
S
in the graph
G
and consider percolation restricted to the complement of
S
.
Call an edge
e\in\partial S
a ‘touch’ if it is connected to infinity (in this restricted configuration). Our proof relies on an exploration algorithm that witnesses these touches one after another. An important feature of our algorithm is that the set of revealed edges of our
p
-percolation remains connected throughout the process. We will first describe a toy version of this algorithm and then explain how to modify it for the final construction.

Toy algorithm.

Pick a vertex that is midway between
S
and infinity, and explore its cluster
\mathcal{C}_{1}
. Here
midway
means that the vertex has constant probability to connect to both
S
and
\infty
. Then pick a vertex that is midway between
S
and
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, and explore its cluster
\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Note that at this stage,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
can at best reach the external vertex boundary of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
because the boundary of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
has been revealed to be closed. Consider all edges where
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
touch, and sprinkle here to glue these two clusters together with constant probability. Continue in this manner
t
times, obtaining sets
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{t}
, at each step choosing a point that is midway between
S
and the previously explored sets. This construction guarantees that each exploration has at least constant probability
\alpha>0
to connect to the previous explorations and witness a new touch. It follows that

(23)

\mathrm{P}_{q}(\Psi(S)\geq t)\geq\alpha^{t}.

Improved algorithm.

To improve the probability of creating a new touch from
\alpha
to
1-\varepsilon
, we split each iteration into two: First, by revealing one large ball at a time, we search for a
seed
(a fully open ball) that is midway between
S
and the previous explorations. Then we explore the cluster of this seed. To ensure that the search for seeds does not interact with future cluster explorations, we look for seeds in a
seed layer
, which is simply a low-density percolation that is independent of the original
p
-percolation.

Where does
\Phi
come into play?

Let us conclude this sketch with an important observation. In the toy algorithm, the set of all revealed edges is always connected. In contrast, in the improved algorithm, the search for seeds generates many
bad
balls in the seed layer that are disconnected from each other yet contain negative information. Consequently, while the toy algorithm can always be run until it witnesses all possible touches (i.e.​ all edges in the exposed boundary of
S
), the improved algorithm may halt after finding only order
\Phi(S)
touches if the union of the bad balls forms a cutset from
S
to infinity.

:
On the left, the blue balls are where the seed layer was revealed: each light blue ball was found to contain at least one closed edge, whereas the dark blue ball was found to be entirely open, forming a
seed
. The green region is the
p
-cluster of the seed, which connects to infinity and touches the peach-coloured set
S
. On the right, we have repeated the process of finding a seed and exploring its cluster twice more. The pink edges represent the sprinkling used to glue each new green cluster to the existing green clusters: the light pink edges are closed, and the dark pink edges are open.

4
Finding mid-points and mid-balls

A standard observation in percolation theory is that for any sets of vertices
L
and
R
in a connected graph
G
, if every vertex is connected to
L\cup R
with constant probability, then there exists a
mid-point
vertex that has both constant probability to connect to
L
and to
R
. (See
[
16
]
for a recent example.)

Now suppose that our graph contains not just one but many disjoint paths from
L
to
R
. Then we can find not just one but many mid-points. In particular, even if we are given a not-too-large set of vertices
A
that we seek to avoid, there will be at least one mid-point that is disjoint from
A
. In this section, we present a high probability version of the above argument where we construct
mid-balls
instead of mid-points. We begin with the construction of one mid-ball that avoids a forbidden region
A
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
L
and
R
be disjoint sets of vertices. Let
r\geq 1
and let
A
be a set of vertices such that
\mathrm{B}_{r}(A)
is not a vertex cutset from
L
to
R
. Let
p,\delta\in[0,1]
be such that for all
v\in V
,

(24)

\mathrm{P}_{p}(\mathrm{B}_{r-1}(v)\leftrightarrow L\cup R)\geq 1-\delta.

Then there exists a ball
B
of radius
r
that is disjoint from
A
and satisfies

(25)

\mathrm{P}_{p}(B\xleftrightarrow{V\backslash R}L),\ \mathrm{P}_{p}(B\xleftrightarrow{V\backslash L}R)\geq 1-\sqrt{\delta}.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{B}_{r}(A)
does not form a vertex cutset from
L
to
R
, there exists a path
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
from
L
to
R
that does not visit
\mathrm{B}_{r}(A)
. Note that
\mathrm{B}_{r}(v_{i})
is disjoint from
A
for all
i
. By the FKG inequality (
\sqrt{\mathsf{trick}}
) and (
24
), for all
i
,

(26)

\mathrm{P}_{p}(\mathrm{B}_{r-1}(v_{i})\xleftrightarrow{V\backslash R}L)\geq 1-\sqrt{\delta}\quad\text{or}\quad\mathrm{P}_{p}(\mathrm{B}_{r-1}(v_{i})\xleftrightarrow{V\backslash L}R)\geq 1-\sqrt{\delta}.

Note that
v_{1}
satisfies the first option and
v_{k}
satisfies the second. So we can find some
i\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
such that

(27)

\mathrm{P}_{p}(\mathrm{B}_{r-1}(v_{i})\xleftrightarrow{V\backslash R}L),\ \mathrm{P}_{p}(\mathrm{B}_{r-1}(v_{i+1})\xleftrightarrow{V\backslash L}R)\geq 1-\sqrt{\delta}.

Since
\mathrm{B}_{r}(v_{i})
contains
\mathrm{B}_{r-1}(v_{i})
and
\mathrm{B}_{r-1}(v_{i+1})
, the ball
B:=\mathrm{B}_{r}(v_{i})
satisfies (
25
).
∎

By adding a stronger cutset condition and repeatedly applying the above lemma, we next construct
many
disjoint mid-balls between two sets.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
L
and
R
be disjoint sets of vertices. Let
r,\ell\geq 1
and let
A
be a finite set of vertices such that every vertex cutset from
L
to
R
has size at least

(28)

\sup_{v\in V}|\mathrm{B}_{2r}(v)|\cdot\big(|A|+\ell\big).

Let
p,\delta\in[0,1]
be such that (

7
Collecting mass

Let
G
be an infinite connected transitive graph. By Theorem
2
, for all
p>p_{c}
, there exists a constant

such that for every finite set of vertices
S
,

(108)

\mathrm{P}_{p}(S\leftrightarrow\infty)\geq 1-e^{-c\,\Phi(S)},

which is sharp up to the choice of
c
. The following theorem says that with high probability,
S
is also connected to many vertices within its neighbourhood at every scale. Formally, given non-empty finite set of vertices
S
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

(109)

\mathcal{C}_{S}^{n}=\big\{v\in\mathrm{B}_{n}(S):v\xleftrightarrow{\mathrm{B}_{n}(S)}S\big\}.

Theorem 3
.

For all
p>p_{c}
there exist
c>0
such that for every finite non-empty set of vertices
S
we have,

(110)

\mathrm{P}_{p}\big(\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\quad|\mathcal{C}_{S}^{n}|\geq v_{n}\big)\geq 1-\sum_{n\geq|S|}e^{-c\Phi(n)},

where
v_{n}=v_{n}(S)
for
n\geq 0
is defined implicitly by

(111)

\int_{|S|}^{v_{n}}\frac{1}{c\,\Phi(t)}\,dt=n.

Remark 7.1
.

Theorem
3
may be viewed as an analogue for percolation of the well-known relationship between return probabilities and isoperimetry for random walks (see
[
27
, Chapter 6.7]
).

Remark 7.2
.

Essentially,
v_{n}(S)
is the lower bound on the size of the neighbourhood
\mathrm{B}_{n}(S)
that one obtains simply by recursively applying the isoperimetric function to smaller neighbourhoods. If
G
is of polynomial growth then
v_{n}(S)\asymp\mathrm{B}_{n}(S)
. On the other hand, if
G
is of exponential growth, then by a result of Coulhon and Saloff-Coste, at worst
\Phi(x)\asymp x/\log x
, in which case
v_{n}\asymp e^{c\sqrt{n}}
. If
G
is non-amenable (i.e.
\Phi(x)\gtrsim x
), then we always have
v_{n}\gtrsim e^{cn}
.

Proof.

Let
p\in(p_{c},1)
and let
c^{\prime}>0
be the constant guaranteed by Theorem
1
(it suffices to consider only
p<1
). We will show that the conclusion of our theorem holds with
c:=\min\big(\frac{-c^{\prime}}{4d\log(1-p)},\frac{1}{2d}\big)
, where
d
is the vertex degree of
G
. Let
S
be a finite set of vertices, and let
v_{n}
be defined by (
111
). Consider some non-negative integer
n
.
Since
\Phi
is increasing,

(112)

v_{n+1}-v_{n}\leq\int_{v_{n}}^{v_{n+1}}\frac{\Phi(v_{n+1})}{\Phi(t)}\,dt=c\,\Phi(v_{n+1}).

By rearranging this inequality and using that
c\leq 1/2d
and
\Phi(v_{n+1})\leq dv_{n+1}
, we obtain
v_{n+1}\leq 2v_{n}
. Since
\Phi(2x)\leq 2\Phi(x)
for all

, it follows that
\Phi(v_{n+1})\leq\Phi(2v_{n})\leq 2\Phi(v_{n})
. Therefore,

(113)

v_{n+1}-v_{n}\leq 2c\,\Phi(v_{n}).

For each
n\geq 0
, let
M_{n}=|\mathcal{C}_{S}^{n}|
. Observe that
M_{0}=|S|=v_{0}
. By a union bound,

(114)

\mathrm{P}_{p}(\forall n\;\;M_{n}\geq v_{n})\geq 1-\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{P}_{p}(M_{n}\geq v_{n}\text{ but }M_{n+1}<v_{n+1}).

Since
M_{n}
is always an integer, the summands here are zero whenever
[v_{n},v_{n+1})
does not contain an integer. So let
I
be the set of all
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that the interval
[v_{n},v_{n+1})
contains an integer, and consider a fixed
n\in I
. Let
\mathcal{W}
be the set of all edges

such that
e\xleftrightarrow{\mathrm{B}_{n+1}(S)}S
. By (
113
), when
M_{n}\geq v_{n}
and
M_{n+1}<v_{n+1}
, we know that

(115)

M_{n+1}-M_{n}\leq 2c\,\Phi(v_{n}),

and hence

. By revealing our percolation configuration in
\mathrm{B}_{n+1}(S)
, we can determine the set
\mathcal{W}
without revealing the status of its edges. Moreover, if every edge in
W
is closed then

is not connected to infinity. So by independence

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{p}\big(M_{n}\geq v_{n}\text{ but }M_{n+1}<v_{n+1}\big)\cdot(1-p)^{2cd\Phi(v_{n})}\leq\mathrm{P}_{p}(v_{n}\leq|\mathcal{C}_{S}|<\infty).

So by rearranging, since

has the properties guaranteed by Theorem
1
,

(116)

\mathrm{P}_{p}\big(M_{n}\geq v_{n}\text{ but }M_{n+1}<v_{n+1}\big)\leq\frac{e^{-c^{\prime}\Phi(v_{n})}}{(1-p)^{2cd\Phi(v_{n})}}\leq e^{-c\Phi(v_{n})},

where the last inequality follows from our formula for

.
Finally, plugging (

) in (

) yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{p}(\forall n\;\;M_{n}\geq v_{n})

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{n\in I}e^{-c\,\Phi(v_{n})}\geq 1-\sum_{n\geq|S|}e^{-c\,\Phi(n)}.

Remark 7.3
.

The argument in this section can be seen as a version of the ‘isoperimetry from tail estimates’ argument in
[
29
]
, restricted to balls.
