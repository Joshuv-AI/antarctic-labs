A New Kernel Regularity Condition for Distributed Mirror Descent: Broader Coverage and Simpler Analysis
Junwen Qiu, Ziyang Zeng, Leilei Mei, Junyu Zhang

Abstract
Existing convergence of distributed optimization methods in non-Euclidean geometries typically rely on kernel assumptions: (i) global Lipschitz smoothness and (ii) bi-convexity of the associated Bregman divergence function. Unfortunately, these conditions are violated by nearly all kernels used in practice, leaving a huge theory-practice gap. This work closes this gap by developing a unified analytical tool that guarantees convergence under mild conditions. Specifically, we introduce Hessian relative uniform continuity (HRUC), a regularity satisfied by nearly all standard kernels. Importantly, HRUC is closed under concatenation, positive scaling, composition, and various kernel combinations. Leveraging the geometric structure induced by HRUC, we derive convergence guarantees for mirror descent-based gradient tracking without imposing any restrictive assumptions. More broadly, our analysis techniques extend seamlessly to other decentralized optimization methods in genuinely non-Euclidean and non-Lipschitz settings.



Keywords:
Distributed mirror descent, kernel regularity, relative uniform continuity, gradient tracking, dual mixing

New regularity condition.
First, we introduce a new
Hessian relative uniform continuity
(HRUC,
Definition

3.1
) regularity condition, which, instead of assuming the upper boundedness of
\nabla^{2}h
, requires the kernel Hessian not to explode too rapidly. Informally, HRUC assumes that the kernel Hessians are close in a relative sense:

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y):=\left\|I-\nabla^{2}h(x)\left[\nabla^{2}h(y)\right]^{-1}\right\|\leq\zeta(\delta)

(3)

for any
x,y

\|\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y)\|\leq\delta
, for any
\delta>0
. The
\zeta(\cdot)

\nabla^{2}h
, and it satisfies
\zeta(\delta)\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
. This essentially implies that the kernel Hessian
\nabla^{2}h
is
uniformly continuous
with respect to the
relative difference
and the
dual space distance
. In sharp contrast to conditions
(C.1)
–
(C.2)

Table

1

Moreover, we show that the HRUC is closed under concatenation, composition, and conic combination, allowing us to synthesize problem-specific kernels by manipulating standard basic building blocks.

In addition, we would like to emphasize that the significance of HRUC regularity is beyond the application to distributed optimization in this paper. One can observe that the vanilla (centralized) mirror descent is approximately a preconditioned gradient descent with a time-varying preconditioner:

x^{t+1}=\nabla h^{*}(\nabla h(x^{t})-\eta_{t}g^{t})=x^{t}-\eta_{t}[\nabla^{2}h(x^{t})]^{-1}g^{t}+o(\eta_{t}\|g^{t}\|).

A guaranteed smooth variation of kernel Hessian (the preconditioner) potentially enables the transformation of many well-developed analysis techniques of gradient methods to mirror descent by viewing the inverse Hessian as variable metrics, which we plan to explore in future works.

Dual consensus mixing.
Second, we develop a dual consensus mixing scheme:

\nabla h(x_{i}^{t+1})={\sum}_{j=1}^{m}W_{(i,j)}\big(\nabla h(x_{j}^{t})-\eta_{t}y_{j}^{t}\big),

(4)

2
Preliminaries and basic assumptions

We introduce a few preliminary concepts, and the basic assumptions used throughout this paper.

2.1
Mirror descent and relative smoothness

First, let us formalize the assumption on
\mathcal{Z}
and the associated kernel
h
in the introduction.

Assumption 2.1
.

The set
\mathcal{Z}
is closed and convex. The associated twice continuously differentiable kernel
h
is
Legendre
and it satisfies
\mathcal{Z}=\operatorname{cl}\mathrm{dom}(h)
.

Here, by
Legendre
, we follow the standard definition in
[
27
]
, which requires the kernel
h
to be both
essentially smooth
and
essentially strictly convex
. That is, we require
\|\nabla h(x_{n})\|\to+\infty
if
x_{n}\to x\in{\rm bdry}\,\mathrm{dom}(h)
and
h
is strictly convex in
\mathrm{dom}(\nabla h)
. In addition, we make the following smoothness assumptions on the objective function.

Assumption 2.2
.

Each local objective function
f_{i}
,
i\in[m]
, is twice continuously differentiable. The global objective function
f
is lower bounded:
\inf_{x\in\mathcal{Z}}f(x)\geq\underline{f}
.

Assumption 2.3
.

Each local objective function
f_{i}
,
i\in[m]
, is smooth relative to
h
with the modulus
{L}>0
in the sense that both
Lh\pm f_{i}
are convex on
\mathcal{Z}
.

The relative smoothness condition was originally introduced by
[
3
,
17
]
to handle objective functions that are not necessarily having globally Lipschitz continuous gradient. Under twice differentiability, it is equivalent to
-L\nabla^{2}h(x)\preceq\nabla^{2}f_{i}(x)\preceq L\nabla^{2}h(x)
for any
x\in\mathcal{Z}
. As a direct consequence of
2.3
, we know the global objective function
f
is also smooth relative to
h
with modulus
L
. Based on this, the extended descent lemma
[
3
,
4
,
17
]
holds for any
(x,y)\in\mathrm{dom}(h)\times\mathrm{int\ dom}(\nabla h)
that

\big|f(x)-f(y)-\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle\big|\leq L\mathcal{D}_{h}(x,y),

(5)

leading to the (centralized) mirror descent for
f
as a majorization minimization update

x^{+}=\argmin_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\,f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(z-x)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\mathcal{D}_{h}(z,x),\quad\text{with}\quad 0<\alpha\leq\tfrac{1}{L}.

Given
2.1
, the essential smoothness and essential strict convexity implies that
x^{+}\in\mathrm{int\ dom}(h)
. As a result,
z\in\mathcal{Z}
will be inactive in the above subproblem and the update can be written as
\nabla h(x^{+})=\nabla h(x)-\alpha\nabla h(x)
.

2.2
Consensus mixing

Let
W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}_{+}
be the mixing matrix, where
W_{(i,j)}>0
if agent
i
and
j
are adjacent nodes in the communication graph. Let
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the vector of all ones, we make the following assumption on the matrix
W
.

Assumption 2.4
.

W
is symmetric, doubly stochastic, and
\rho:=\|W-\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\|<1
.

As a consequence, we have
W\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
,
\mathbf{1}^{\top}W=\mathbf{1}^{\top}
, and the following contraction result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Given
2.4
, and
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in[m]}
,
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[m]}
,
\{v_{i}^{+}\}_{i\in[m]}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}^{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
and
\bar{u},\bar{v},\bar{v}^{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be their concatenated matrix and averaged vectors. Let
H,H^{+}\succ 0
be symmetric positive definite matrices. Assume that

\mathbf{v}^{+}=W\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\big\|[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2}\leq 1+\alpha\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha\in\Big[0,\frac{1-\rho}{2}\Big].

Then,

{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|v_{i}^{+}-\bar{v}^{+}\|^{2}_{H^{+}}\leq\rho\cdot{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}\|^{2}_{H}+\frac{3}{1-\rho}\cdot{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|u_{i}\|^{2}_{H}.

For succinctness, we move the proof of the lemma to Appendix
C.1
.

3
The Hessian relative uniform continuity regularity condition

We invoke a non-Euclidean metric
\rho_{h}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
to measure the proximity of points in
\mathcal{Z}
, by incorporating their covectors in the dual space:

\rho_{h}(x,y):=\|\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y)\|.

The strict convexity of
h
indicates that
\rho_{h}(x,y)=0\Leftrightarrow x=y
and straightforward computation verifies the nonnegativity, symmetry, and triangle inequality. Thus,
\rho_{h}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
indeed induces a distance. Under this distance, we provide the formal definition of the HRUC regularity condition.

Definition 3.1

(
HRUC Regularity
)
.

We say a kernel
h
is
\zeta
-HRUC regular for a non-decreasing continuous
distortion modulus

\zeta\!:\mathbb{R}_{+}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\zeta(0)=0
, if

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y):=\big\|\nabla^{2}h(x)\big[\nabla^{2}h(y)\big]^{-1}-I\big\|\leq\zeta(\delta)

(6)

for all
\delta\geq 0
and
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(h)
that satisfy
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
.

This condition requires that the Hessian of the kernel to change smoothly in a relative sense governed uniformly by the
\zeta
-function and
\rho_{h}
-distance. In the univariate special case where
\nabla^{2}h(x)=h^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
is a positive scalar, this condition can interpreted as the uniform continuity of
\log h^{\prime\prime}(x)
.

Next, we provide a few important properties for the HRUC-regular kernels.

Proposition 3.2

(
Closedness under Concatenation
)
.

Let the kernel
h
be
block-separable
in the sense that
h(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}(x_{i})
with
x=(x_{i},\cdots,x_{m})
partitioned into
m
blocks
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}
. If each
h_{i}
is
\zeta_{i}
-HRUC regular, then the kernel
h
is
\zeta
-HRUC regular with
\zeta(\cdot):=\max_{i}\zeta_{i}(\cdot)
.

In practice, many kernels are actually
coordinate-separable
in the sense that each block
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is 1-dimensional. The proof of this proposition is straightforward is hence omitted. Next we present the closedness of HRUC regularity under composition.

Proposition 3.3

(
Closedness under Composition
)
.

Let the kernel
h
be
\zeta
-HRUC regular, and let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a non-singular matrix,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arbitrary vector,
c>0
be a positive scalar, then the kernel
\varphi(\cdot):=ch(A\cdot+b)
is
\kappa_{A}\zeta(\cdot/c)
-HRUC on its domain, where
\kappa_{A}>0
is the condition number of
A
.

Proof.

First,
we have
\nabla\varphi(x)=cA^{\top}\nabla h(Ax+b)
and
\nabla^{2}\varphi(x)=cA^{\top}\nabla^{2}h(Ax+b)A
. Then it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\big\|A^{\top}\big(\nabla^{2}h(Ax+b)\big[\nabla^{2}h(Ay+b)\big]^{-1}-I\big)A^{-\top}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{A}\big\|\nabla^{2}h(Ax+b)\big[\nabla^{2}h(Ay+b)\big]^{-1}-I\big\|.

For any
\delta\geq 0
,
\rho_{\varphi}(x,y)\leq\delta
indicates that
\rho_{h}(Ax+b,Ay+b)\leq\delta/c
. Together with the
\zeta
-HRUC regularity of
h
, we prove
\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}(x,y)\leq\kappa_{A}\zeta(\delta/c)
.
∎

Next, we discuss the closedness of HRUC regularity under additive combination.

Proposition 3.4

(
Closedness under Combination
)
.

Let the kernels
h
and
g
be
\zeta_{h}
- and
\zeta_{g}
-HRUC regular, respectively. Let
\varphi:=h+g
, then it holds that

(i).

\,\,\varphi
is
\zeta_{h}
-HRUC if
g(x)=\|x\|^{2}/2
.

(ii).

\varphi
is
\max\{\zeta_{h},\zeta_{g}\}
-HRUC if both
h
and
g
are both coordinate-separable.

(iii).

\!\!\varphi
is
(\sqrt{\kappa_{h}}\zeta_{g}+\sqrt{\kappa_{g}}\zeta_{h})
-HRUC if (1) the kernel pair
(h,g)
are cross-monotone in the sense that

\langle\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y),\nabla g(x)-\nabla g(y)\rangle\geq 0
, for
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(\nabla\varphi)
; (2) the Hessian
\nabla^{2}h
and
\nabla^{2}g
has uniformly bounded condition numbers
\kappa_{h}
and
\kappa_{g}
, respectively.

In this proposition, both (i) and (ii) satisfy the cross-monotone assumption of (iii). However, we state (i) independently because most mirror descent literature requires the kernel
h
to be strongly convex, see e.g.
[
4
,
10
,
40
]
. If needed, one can always add quadratic terms to the kernel without destroying HRUC. We also state (ii) independently because most of the kernels are indeed coordinate separable and it allows a tighter bound than (iii).

Proof of
Proposition

3.4
.

First of all, through direct computation, we obtain

\rho_{\varphi}^{2}(x,y)=\rho_{h}^{2}(x,y)+\rho_{g}^{2}(x,y)+2\langle\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y),\nabla g(x)-\nabla g(y)\rangle.

A direct consequence of the cross-monotonicity is that
\max\left\{\rho_{h}(x,y),\rho_{g}(x,y)\right\}\leq\delta
when
\rho_{\varphi}(x,y)\leq\delta
. For example, in case (i) where
h
is quadratic, and in case (ii) where both
h
and
g
are coordinate-separable, it is straight-forward to verify the cross-monotonicity. Next, let us prove the three cases given
\max\left\{\rho_{h}(x,y),\rho_{h}(x,y)\right\}\leq\delta
.

Case (ii).
In the coordinate-separable case, denote
\nabla^{2}f(z)=\mathrm{Diag}(f_{z}^{i})\succ 0
for
i\in[d]
,
z\in\{x,y\}
, and
f\in\{h,g\}
. By cross-monotonicity,

\max_{i}\left\{\left|h_{x}^{i}/h_{y}^{i}-1\right|\right\}\leq\zeta_{h}(\delta)
and
\max_{i}\left\{\left|g_{x}^{i}/g_{y}^{i}-1\right|\right\}\leq\zeta_{g}(\delta)
. Then direct computation gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\max_{i\in[d]}\left\{\left|\frac{h_{x}^{i}+g_{x}^{i}}{h_{y}^{i}+g_{y}^{i}}-1\right|\right\}

\displaystyle=\max_{i\in[d]}\left\{\left|\frac{h_{y}^{i}(h_{x}^{i}/h_{y}^{i}-1)}{h_{y}^{i}+g_{y}^{i}}+\frac{g_{y}^{i}(g_{x}^{i}/g_{y}^{i}-1)}{h_{y}^{i}+g_{y}^{i}}\right|\right\}\leq\max\{\zeta_{h}(\delta),\zeta_{g}(\delta)\}.

Case (iii).
Denote
H_{z}=\nabla^{2}h(z)\succ 0
and
G_{z}=\nabla^{2}g(z)\succ 0
for
z\in\{x,y\}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left\|(H_{x}+G_{x})(H_{y}+G_{y})^{-1}-I\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|(H_{x}H_{y}^{-1}-I)(I+G_{y}H_{y}^{-1})^{-1}+(G_{x}G_{y}^{-1}-I)(I+H_{y}G_{y}^{-1})^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{g}}\left\|H_{x}H_{y}^{-1}-I\right\|+\sqrt{\kappa_{h}}\left\|G_{x}G_{y}^{-1}-I\right\|\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{g}}\zeta_{h}(\delta)+\sqrt{\kappa_{h}}\zeta_{g}(\delta),

where the third line is due to the triangle inequality, and the fact that for all
A,B\succ 0
:

\|(I+AB)^{-1}\|=\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}(I+A^{\frac{1}{2}}BA^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-1}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{A}}.

Case (i).
Finally, using the decomposition in (iii) with
G_{x}=G_{y}=I
, we obtain

\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}(x,y)=\|(H_{x}H_{y}^{-1}-I)(I+H_{y}^{-1})^{-1}\|\leq\zeta_{h}(\delta),

which completes the proof.
∎

Combining these closedness propositions, we can easily establish the HRUC regularity of new kernels based on known kernels.

Example 3.5

(Fermi-Dirac entropy)
.

Consider the Boltzmann-Shannon entropy

h_{\rm BS}(x)={\sum}_{i=1}^{d}\,x_{(i)}\log(x_{(i)})-x_{(i)}.

By

, it is
\zeta
-HRUC with
\zeta(\delta)=\exp(\delta)-1
, which will be verified in

. Then for the Fermi-Dirac entropy
[

]

h_{\rm FD}(x):={\sum}_{i=1}^{d}\,x_{(i)}\log(x_{(i)})+(1-x_{(i)})\log(1-x_{(i)}),

we can easily assert that it is also
\zeta
-HRUC with the same
\zeta
function, which follows from the observation that

h_{\rm FD}(x)=h_{\rm BS}(x)+h_{\rm BS}(-x+\mathbf{1})-d,

where the constant

can be ignored. By
Proposition

3.3
, we assert that
h_{\rm BS}(-x+\mathbf{1})
is also

-HRUC as
\kappa_{I}=1
for identity matrix. Applying
Proposition

3.4
(ii) then completes the argument.

Next, we present a handy lemma that connects the relative smoothness and the Lipschitz continuity in terms of a local norm and the
\rho_{h}
-distance in the dual space.

Lemma 3.6

(
Lipschitz in the distortion geometry
)
.

Let a twice continuously differentiable
f
be
L
-smooth relative to some
\zeta
-HRUC regular kernel
h
. For any covector
z\in\mathrm{Im}(\nabla h)
and
H_{z}^{*}=\nabla^{2}h^{*}(z)
, we have

\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|_{H_{z}^{*}}\leq L\big(1+\zeta(\delta)\big)\cdot\left\|\nabla h(y)-\nabla h(x)\right\|_{H_{z}^{*}}

(7)

as long as the primal vectors
x,y
satisfy
\|\nabla h(x)-z\|\leq\delta
and
\|\nabla h(y)-z\|\leq\delta
.

Proof.

Denote
u=\nabla h(y)-\nabla h(x)
, then we can define the parameterized curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
\gamma(q)=\nabla h^{*}\big(\nabla h(x)+q\cdot u\big)
. Then by Jensen’s inequality, we have

\left\|\nabla h(\gamma(q))-z\right\|=\big\|q\big(\nabla h(y)-z\big)+(1-q)\big(\nabla h(x)-z\big)\big\|\leq\delta

(8)

for any
q\in[0,1]
. Meanwhile, it follows from the Leibniz integral rule that

\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)=\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\big[\nabla f(\gamma(q))\big]^{\prime}\mathrm{d}q\overset{\text{(i)}}{=}\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\nabla^{2}f(\gamma(q))\big[\nabla^{2}h\big(\gamma(q)\big)\big]^{-1}\!u\;\mathrm{d}q,

(9)

where (i) is due to the chain rule and the fact that
\nabla^{2}h^{*}(\nabla h(w))=[\nabla^{2}h(w)]^{-1}
for any
w\in\mathrm{int\ dom}(h)
. For notational simplicity, let us denote
H^{*}_{z}:=\nabla^{2}h^{*}(z)
,
H_{\gamma(q)}:=\nabla^{2}h\big(\gamma(q)\big)
and
F_{\gamma(q)}:=\nabla^{2}f\big(\gamma(q)\big)
.
Then because
H^{*}_{z}
is symmetric, we have
\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|_{H_{z}^{*}}=\|(H_{z}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x))\|
. Combined with (
9
), we have

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla f(y)\!-\!\nabla f(x)\big\|_{H^{*}_{z}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\!\!(H_{z}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{\gamma(q)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\cdot H_{\gamma(q)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}F_{\gamma(q)}\,H_{\gamma(q)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\!\cdot H_{\gamma(q)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(H_{z}^{*})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\!\cdot(H_{z}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}u\;\mathrm{d}q\right\|

(10)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\!\big\|(H_{z}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{\gamma(q)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\cdot\big\|H_{\gamma(q)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}F_{\gamma(q)}\,H_{\gamma(q)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\cdot\big\|H_{\gamma(q)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(H_{z}^{*})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\cdot\|u\|_{H_{z}^{*}}\;\mathrm{d}q.

Next, we bound each term in (
10
). Because
f
is
L
-smooth relative to
h
, we have

-LI\preceq[\nabla^{2}h(w)]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\nabla^{2}f(w)\,[\nabla^{2}h(w)]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\preceq LI,\quad\forall\,w\in\mathrm{int\ dom}(h).

Therefore,
\|H_{\gamma(q)}^{-1/2}F_{\gamma(q)}H_{\gamma(q)}^{-1/2}\|\leq L
for all
q\in[0,1]
. Moreover, by (
8
), we can apply HRUC regularity and a supporting linear algebra result
Lemma

B.2
to obtain

\big\|(H_{z}^{*})^{1/2}H_{\gamma(q)}^{1/2}\big\|=\big\|[\nabla^{2}h(\nabla h^{*}(z))]^{-1/2}\cdot[\nabla^{2}h(\gamma(q))]^{1/2}\big\|\leq\sqrt{1+\zeta(\delta)}\quad\forall\,q\in[0,1].

Similarly, we can deduce
\|H_{\gamma(q)}^{-1/2}(H_{z}^{*})^{-1/2}\|\leq\sqrt{1+\zeta(\delta)}
for all
q\in[0,1]
. Substituting
u=\nabla h(y)-\nabla h(x)
and the established bounds to (
10
) completes the proof.
∎

4.1
Separable kernels

A kernel
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
separable
if it admits the decomposition
h(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}h_{i}(x_{(i)})
, where
x_{(i)}
is the
i
-th coordinate of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
h_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. In
Proposition

4.2
, we establish sufficient conditions for such functions to be HRUC regular. Notably, all separable kernels listed in
Table

1
satisfy these criteria. Therefore, establishing HRUC regularity and deriving the modulus
\zeta(\delta)
for a specific separable kernel reduces to a direct verification of the conditions in
Proposition

4.2
. We refer the reader to Appendix
A.1
for the detailed verifications.

Proposition 4.2

(
Sufficient conditions of HRUC kernels
)
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a separable kernel and let
G,H>0
be constants. For each coordinate
i\in[d]
, assume there exists a bijection
1
1

1

A typical choice is
\phi_{i}:=h_{i}^{\prime}
.

\phi_{i}:\mathrm{int\ dom}(h_{i})\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

•

the condition
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
implies
|\phi_{i}(x_{(i)})-\phi_{i}(y_{(i)})|\leq H\delta
;

•

the mapping
g_{i}(s):=\log\big(h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{i}^{-1}(s)\big)
is
G
-Lipschitz continuous on
\mathrm{Im}(\phi)
.

Then,
h
is HRUC-regular with the modulus
\zeta(\delta):=\exp(GH\delta)-1
.

Proof.

Since
h
is separable, we have
\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)=\max_{i\in[d]}\;\mathcal{R}_{h_{i}}(x,y)
. For each
i\in[d]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{h_{i}}(x,y)=\bigg|\frac{h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(x_{(i)})}{h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(y_{(i)})}-1\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big(\big|\log\big(h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(x_{(i)})\big)-\log\big(h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(y_{(i)})\big)\big|\Big)-1

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(\big|g_{i}(\phi_{i}(x_{(i)}))-g_{i}(\phi_{i}(y_{(i)}))\big|\Big)-1.

Note that
g_{i}
is
G
-Lipschitz and
|\phi_{i}(x_{(i)})-\phi_{i}(y_{(i)})|\leq H\delta
, it follows that

\exp\Big(\big|g_{i}(\phi_{i}(x_{(i)}))-g_{i}(\phi_{i}(y_{(i)}))\big|\Big)\leq\exp(GH\delta),\qquad\forall\,i\in[d].

Therefore,
\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)=\max_{i\in[d]}\,\mathcal{R}_{h_{i}}(x,y)\leq\exp(GH\delta)-1
.
∎

4.2
Self-concordant strongly convex functions

Self-concordant functions encompass a broad spectrum of kernels, including strongly convex functions with Lipschitz continuous Hessians and norm exponential kernel.
The definition of self-concordant function
[
24
, Definition 5.1.1]
is given below.

Definition 4.3

(
Self-concordant functions
)
.

Let
f:\mathrm{dom}(f)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a closed convex function
and
f\in\mathcal{C}^{3}(\mathrm{dom}(f))
. We say that
f
is
M
-self-concordant
if

\big|\nabla^{3}f(x)[u,u,u]\big|\;\leq\;2M\|u\|^{3}_{\nabla^{2}f(x)},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathrm{dom}(f),\;\forall\,u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Next, we show that self-concordant, strongly convex functions are HRUC regular. Such result serves as a useful analytical tool that facilitate the verification and derivation of the modulus
\zeta(\delta)
for functions in this class. We refer to Appendix
A.2
for specific examples.

Proposition 4.4

(
HRUC regularity of self-concordant functions
)
.

Let
M,\mu>0
be given. Then,
M
-self-concordant and
\mu
-strongly convex functions are HRUC regular with modulus
\zeta(\delta)=\exp(\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\mu}}\delta)-1
.

Proof.

Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and set
v:=x-y
. Define the line segment and the associated Hessian:

x(s):=y+sv,\;s\in[0,1]\qquad\text{and}\qquad H(s):=\nabla^{2}h\big(x(s)\big).

By strong convexity,
H(s)\succ 0
for all
s\in[0,1]
. For any nonzero vector
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\phi_{u}(s):=\frac{1}{2}\log\big(u^{\top}H(s)\,u\big),\quad\text{then}\quad\phi_{u}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{u^{\top}\dot{H}(s)u}{u^{\top}H(s)u}=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\nabla^{3}h(x(s))[u,u,v]}{u^{\top}H(s)u}.

By the self-concordance bound
[
24
, Lemma 5.1.2]
, we have

\big|\nabla^{3}h(x(s))[u,u,v]\big|\;\leq\;2M\,\|u\|_{H(s)}^{2}\,\|v\|_{H(s)}=2M\,\big(u^{\top}H(s)u\big)\,\|v\|_{H(s)}.

Hence
|\phi_{u}^{\prime}(s)|\;\leq\;M\,\|v\|_{H(s)}.

Integrating yields

\big|\phi_{u}(1)-\phi_{u}(0)\big|\;\leq\;M\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left|\log\frac{u^{\top}H_{1}u}{u^{\top}H_{0}u}\right|\;\leq\;2M\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s,

where we denote
H_{0}:=H(0)
and
H_{1}:=H(1)
for brevity.
Thus, for all
u\neq 0
,

\exp\Big(-2M\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s\Big)\;\leq\;\frac{u^{\top}H_{1}u}{u^{\top}H_{0}u}\;\leq\;\exp\Big(2M\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s\Big).

The eigenvalues of
H_{0}^{-1/2}H_{1}H_{0}^{-1/2}
are within the extremal values of the Rayleigh quotient. Since
H_{1}H_{0}^{-1}
is similar to
H_{0}^{-1/2}H_{1}H_{0}^{-1/2}
, each eigenvalue
\lambda
of
H_{1}H_{0}^{-1}
lies in

\lambda\in\bigg[\exp\Big(-2M\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s\Big),\exp\Big(2M\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s\Big)\bigg].

This, together with
H_{0}=\nabla^{2}h(y)
and
H_{1}=\nabla^{2}h(x)
, gives

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\;\leq\;\exp\Big(2M\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s\Big)-1.

(11)

Note that
\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y)=\int_{0}^{1}H(s)v\,{\rm d}s
, then

v^{\top}\big(\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y)\big)=\int_{0}^{1}v^{\top}H(s)v\,{\rm d}s=\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}^{2}\,{\rm d}s.

On one hand, the condition
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
implies

\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}^{2}\,{\rm d}s=v^{\top}\big(\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y)\big)\;\leq\;\rho_{h}(x,y)\,\|v\|\leq\delta\,\|v\|.

On the other hand,
\mu
-strong convexity of
h
, together with
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
, yields

\|v\|=\|x-y\|\leq\rho_{h}(x,y)/\mu\leq\delta/\mu
.
Therefore,
\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}^{2}\,{\rm d}s\leq\delta^{2}/\mu
. By Jensen’s inequality,

\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s\;\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}^{2}\,{\rm d}s\Big)^{1/2}\leq\delta/\sqrt{\mu}.

Combining this bound with (
11
), we obtain

\hskip 56.9055pt\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\;\leq\;\exp\!\bigg(2M\int_{0}^{1}\|v\|_{H(s)}\,{\rm d}s\bigg)-1\;\leq\;\exp\!\bigg(\frac{2M}{\sqrt{\mu}}\,\delta\bigg)-1.

This completes the verification.
∎

4.3
Power kernels

In this subsection, we analyze the power kernels

h(x)=\frac{\mu}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\frac{1}{r+2}\|x\|^{r+2}\quad\text{with $r>0$.}

Indeed,
h(x)
is self-concordant when
r>1
. However, direct application of
Proposition

4.4
would yield modulus
\zeta(\delta)
that exhibits exponential growth on
\delta
. To obtain a sharper polynomial modulus, we provide the following analysis.

Proof.

The gradient and Hessian of
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
are

\nabla h(x)=(\mu+\|x\|^{r})\cdot x\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla^{2}h(x)=\big(\mu+\|x\|^{r}\big)\cdot I+r\|x\|^{r-2}xx^{\top}.

Clearly,
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
implies
\|x-y\|\leq\delta/\mu
.
Moreover, by
\nabla^{2}h(y)\succeq(\mu+\|y\|^{r})I
, we have

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{\mu+\|y\|^{r}}\cdot\big\|\nabla^{2}h(x)-\nabla^{2}h(y)\big\|,\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(12)

Introduce the curve
x(s):=(1-s)y+sx
with
s\in[0,1]
, then

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{\mu+\|y\|^{r}}\int_{0}^{1}\Big\|\frac{{\rm d}\,\nabla^{2}h(x(s))}{{\rm d}s}\Big\|\;{\rm d}s\overset{\text{(i)}}{\leq}\frac{1}{\mu+\|y\|^{r}}\int_{0}^{1}\big\|\nabla^{3}h(x(s))[x-y]\big\|\;{\rm d}s,

(13)

where (i) is by chain rule
{\rm d}\nabla^{2}h(x(s))/{\rm d}s=\nabla^{3}h(x(s))\,[\dot{x}(s)]=\nabla^{3}h(x(s))[x-y]

and
\nabla^{3}h(x)[\cdot]
is the directional derivative of
\nabla^{3}h(x)
at
x
. Next, consider

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla^{3}h(x)[u]\big\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|r\|x\|^{r-2}(x^{\top}u)I+r(r-2)\|x\|^{r-4}(x^{\top}u)xx^{\top}+r\|x\|^{r-2}(ux^{\top}+xu^{\top})\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\big(3r+|r(r-2)|\big)\|x\|^{r-1}\|u\|.

Using this estimate and
\|x-y\|\leq\delta/\mu
, we can further write (
13
) as

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\leq\frac{\big(3r+|r(r-2)|\big)\delta}{\mu(\mu+\|y\|^{r})}\int_{0}^{1}\|x(s)\|^{r-1}{\rm d}s.

(14)

Case I:
r>1
.
Indeed,
\|x(s)\|=\|y+s(x-y)\|\leq\|y\|+\delta/\mu
. Then (
14
) becomes

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\leq\frac{(r^{2}+3r)\delta}{\mu}\cdot\frac{(\|y\|+\delta/\mu)^{r-1}}{\|y\|^{r}+\mu}\leq\frac{4\delta r^{2}}{\mu}\cdot 2^{r-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{\|y\|+\mu/\|y\|^{r-1}}+\frac{\delta^{r-1}}{\mu^{r}}\bigg).

(15)

By Young’s inequality
a^{p}/p+b^{q}/q\geq ab
for all
a,b\geq 0,p,q\geq 1
and
1/p+1/q=1
, set
p=r/(r-1)
,
q=r
, it holds for all
t\geq 0
that

t+\mu/t^{r-1}\geq\frac{\big(t^{1/p}\big)^{p}}{p}+\frac{\big(\mu^{1/q}/t^{(r-1)/q}\big)^{q}}{q}\geq\mu^{1/r}.

Substituting
t=\|y\|
in the above estimateand merging the obtained bound into (
15
) leads to

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\leq\frac{2^{r+1}r^{2}}{\mu}\Big(\frac{\delta}{\mu^{1/r}}+\frac{\delta^{r}}{\mu^{r}}\Big).

Case II:
0<r\leq 1
.
To upper bound
\int_{0}^{1}\|x(s)\|^{r-1}\,{\rm d}s
, we introduce

z:=x-y\quad\text{and}\quad v:=\frac{x-y}{\|x-y\|}.

By Schwarz’s inequality, then
\|x(s)\|=\|y+sz\|\cdot\|v\|\geq\big|\langle y+sz,v\rangle\big|=\big|\langle y,v\rangle+s\|z\|\big|
.
With this bound, the integral is further written as

\int_{0}^{1}\|x(s)\|^{r-1}{\rm d}s\leq\int_{0}^{1}\big|\langle y,v\rangle+s\|z\|\big|^{r-1}{\rm d}s=\|z\|^{r-1}\cdot\int_{\beta}^{\beta+1}|t|^{r-1}{\rm d}t\qquad\text{with}\quad\beta:=\frac{\langle y,v\rangle}{\|z\|}

after changing variable
t:=s+\beta
. Since
\|z\|\leq\delta/\mu
and
\int_{\beta}^{\beta+1}|t|^{r-1}{\rm d}t\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}t^{r-1}{\rm d}t=\frac{2}{r},
we obtain
\int_{0}^{1}\|x(s)\|^{r-1}{\rm d}s\leq\frac{2\delta^{r-1}}{r\mu^{r-1}}
,
which, combined with (
14
), leads to

\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\leq\frac{[3r+r(2-r)]\delta}{\mu(\mu+\|y\|^{r})}\cdot\frac{2\delta^{r-1}}{r\mu^{r-1}}\leq\frac{10\delta^{r}}{\mu^{r+1}}.

In view of
Case I & II
, we obtain a uniform upper bound when
r>0
, i.e.,

\hskip 85.35826pt\mathcal{R}_{h}(x,y)\leq\zeta(\delta):=\frac{\max\{10,r^{2}2^{r+1}\}}{\mu}\Big(\frac{\delta}{\mu^{1/r}}+\frac{\delta^{r}}{\mu^{r}}\Big).

Therefore, power kernels are HRUC regular.
∎

, and is omitted. As a direct result of
Lemma

5.2
, the consecutive local iterates
\{x_{i}^{t}\}
are always forced to be close in the dual space, and one can always activate the local uniform continuity property of (
6
) and the consequential dual space Lipschitz bound (
7
), with constants explicitly quantified by the associated
\zeta
function. In addition, as the algorithm converges so that
\eta\|y_{i}^{t}\|\leq\delta
for the predetermined constant threshold
\delta
, the clipping operator
\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\eta}(\cdot)
reduces to the identity mapping, meaning that it only clips the too aggressive steps to prevent overshooting while remaining inactivated for appropriate steps. Because of this feature, the clipped step (
18
) will asymptotically reduce to (
4
), which is the reason why we regard it as a minimal algorithmic modification.

Having clarified the algorithmic design, we illustrate how the analysis of standard distributed algorithms to the mirror setting seamlessly through the lens of HRUC regularity. First, following the discussion of (
4
) in the introduction, we define the “average” primal variable by
\bar{x}^{t}:=\nabla h^{*}(\bar{z}^{t})
. And we introduce a quantity

\lambda:=1+\zeta\Big(\tfrac{\sqrt{20m}}{1-\rho}\cdot\delta\Big),

(22)

which will be used throughout the latter discussion.

Lemma 5.3
.

Given
2.1
,
2.4
and
5.1
, for any iteration
t\in\mathbb{N}
of
Algorithm

1
, there exists
\theta_{t}\in[0,1]
such that
for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
, the following holds:

\displaystyle f(\bar{x}^{t+1})-f(\bar{x}^{t})\leq

\displaystyle-L\cdot\mathcal{D}_{h}(\bar{x}^{t},\bar{x}^{t+1})-\Big[\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{2L+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}\Big]\cdot\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|s^{t}_{i}\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2m\alpha_{1}}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|y_{i}^{t}-\bar{y}^{t}\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}+\frac{L^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2m\alpha_{2}}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|\bar{z}^{t}-z_{i}^{t}\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}^{2},

where
H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}:=\nabla^{2}h^{*}(\bar{z}_{\theta_{t}})\succ 0
, with
\bar{z}_{\theta_{t}}=(1-\theta_{t})\bar{z}^{t}+\theta_{t}\,\bar{z}^{t+1}
.

This lemma captures the iterative descent (with controllable errors) of
DMGT
using the local norms induced by the kernel’s (conjugation’s) Hessian, which satisfies the relationship that
\nabla^{2}h^{*}(z)=[\nabla^{2}h(x)]^{-1}
for all
z=\nabla h(x)
. The proof of this lemma is relegated to Appendix
C.2
. In the next lemma, we capture the iterative descent and how the consensus error evolves for

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Given
2.1
,
2.4
and
5.1
, define the consensus potential

\mathcal{E}_{t}:=\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\|y_{i}^{t}-\bar{y}^{t}\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}+\xi\cdot\|\bar{z}^{t}-z_{i}^{t}\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}^{2}\Big),

(23)

with the constant
\xi=\frac{32L^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(1-\rho)^{2}}
. Then, the following bound is valid for all
t\in\mathbb{N}:

\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\leq\frac{1+\rho}{2}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{t}+\frac{108L^{2}\lambda^{2}}{(1-\rho)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|s^{t}_{i}\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}^{2},

(24)

as long as we select the clipping radius
\delta
such that
\zeta(2\delta)\leq\frac{1-\rho}{2}\in(0,1).

A key difference to standard analysis is the adoption of the local norms
\|\cdot\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}
, which are introduced to handle the non-homogeneous geometry induced by the nonlinear mirror map
\nabla h
. By the clipping bound (
Lemma

5.2
), we know
\|\bar{z}_{\theta_{t+1}}-\bar{z}_{\theta_{t}}\|\leq 2\delta
, which, together with the
\zeta
-HRUC regularity of the kernel
h
(
5.1
), gives

\big\|[H_{\theta_{t+1}}^{*}]^{\frac{1}{2}}[H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2}\leq\big\|H_{\theta_{t+1}}^{*}[H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}]^{-1}\big\|\leq 1+\zeta(2\delta),

(25)

where the first inequality is due to a simple linear algebra analysis in
Lemma

B.2
in the appendix. It provides a smooth transition between the consecutive local norms:

\frac{1}{1+\zeta(2\delta)}\leq\frac{\|\cdot\|_{H_{\theta_{t+1}}^{*}}}{\|\cdot\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}\,\,\,\,\,}\leq 1+\zeta(2\delta),

which is sufficient for a contraction when
\delta
is small enough such that
\zeta(2\delta)\leq\frac{1-\rho}{2}\in(0,1)
. For the presentation succinctness, we move the rest of proof to Appendix
C.3

In view of
Lemmas

5.3
and
5.4
, we introduce the following potential sequence

\mathcal{M}_{t}:=f(\bar{x}^{t})+\frac{1}{8L}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{t}.

The next proposition establishes the monotonicity of this sequence.

Lemma 5.5
.

Given the setting of
Lemma

5.4
, and select
\eta\leq\frac{(1-\rho)^{2}}{25L\lambda^{2}}
, then

\mathcal{M}_{t+1}\leq\mathcal{M}_{t}-\frac{1}{12\eta}\cdot\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|s^{t}_{i}\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}^{2}-L\mathcal{D}_{h}(\bar{x}^{t},\bar{x}^{t+1})-\frac{(1-\rho)}{32L}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{t}\qquad\forall\;t\in\mathbb{N}.

(26)

As a direct consequence of (
26
), the following bound holds:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\bigg[\frac{1}{12\eta}\cdot\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|s^{t}_{i}\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}^{2}+L\mathcal{D}_{h}(\bar{x}^{t},\bar{x}^{t+1})+\frac{(1-\rho)}{32L}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{t}\bigg]\leq\frac{\mathcal{M}_{0}-\mathcal{M}_{T}}{T}.

Note
\|\bar{z}^{t+1}-\bar{z}^{t}\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}=\|\bar{s}^{t}\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\bar{s}^{t}_{i}\|_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}^{2}
, we can define an optimality measure

\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}^{t},\mathbf{y}^{t},\mathbf{z}^{t}):=\frac{1}{12\eta^{2}}\|\bar{z}^{t+1}-\bar{z}^{t}\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}+\frac{L}{\eta}\mathcal{D}_{h}(\bar{x}^{t},\bar{x}^{t+1})+\frac{(1-\rho)}{32L\eta}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{t},

which is a combination of the Bregman residual of primal iterates
\mathcal{D}_{h}(\bar{x}^{t},\bar{x}^{t+1})
, the local normed dual residual
\|\bar{z}^{t+1}-\bar{z}^{t}\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}
, and the consensus error
\mathcal{E}_{t}
. With the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{0}=f(\bar{x}^{0})+\mathcal{E}_{0}/(8L)=f(x^{0})
and
\mathcal{M}_{T}\geq\underline{f}
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.6
.

Given
2.1
,
2.4
and
5.1
, select the step size
\eta\leq\frac{(1-\rho)^{2}}{25L\lambda^{2}}
, and select the clipping radius
\delta
such that
\zeta(2\delta)\leq\frac{1-\rho}{2}

\min_{t=0,\ldots,T-1}\;\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}^{t},\mathbf{y}^{t},\mathbf{z}^{t})\leq\frac{f(x^{0})-\underline{f}}{T\cdot\eta}=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\Big)

holds for the iterates generated by
Algorithm

1
, for any positive integer
T\geq 1
.

To interpret this bound, we consider two special cases: (i)
h
is the standard quadratic kernel
h(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
and
Algorithm

1
reduces to gradient tracking method; (ii)

is a general kernel, the dual iterates
\{\mathbf{z}^{t}\}_{t}
stays in a bounded set.

Case I.
By
Theorem

4.1
, the kernel
h(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
is
\zeta

\zeta(\delta)\equiv 0
. In this situation,

\mathcal{D}_{h}(\bar{x}^{t},\bar{x}^{t+1})=\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{x}^{t}-\bar{x}^{t+1}\|^{2}

. As a consequence,

\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}^{t},\mathbf{y}^{t},\mathbf{z}^{t})=\left(\frac{1}{12}+\frac{L\eta}{2}\right)\|\bar{y}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1-\rho}{32L\eta}\cdot\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\left(\|y_{i}^{t}-\bar{y}^{t}\|^{2}+\xi\|x_{i}^{t}-\bar{x}^{t}\|^{2}\right),

(27)

where

is defined in

. Then, the following inequality holds:

\left\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{t})\right\|^{2}\leq 2\,\Big\|\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\nabla f(\bar{x}^{t})-y^{t}_{i}\Big\|^{2}+2\|\bar{y}^{t}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{t})-y^{t}_{i}\right\|^{2}+2\|\bar{y}^{t}\|^{2}.

(28)

Moreover, as

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{t})-y_{i}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|(\bar{y}^{t}-y_{i}^{t})+\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{j=1}^{m}\big(\nabla f_{j}(\bar{x}^{t})-\nabla f_{j}(x_{j}^{t})\big)\Big\|^{2}

(29)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\bar{y}^{t}-y_{i}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{2L^{2}}{m}{\sum}_{j=1}^{m}\|\bar{x}^{t}-x_{j}^{t}\|^{2}.

Then combining the inequalities (

\min_{0\leq t\leq T-1}\left\{\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|^{2}+\frac{L^{2}}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|\bar{x}^{t}-x_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\right\}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{L(f(x^{0})-\underline{f})}{(1-\rho)^{2}T}\right)\qquad\forall\;T\geq 1.

Case II.
For general Legendre type kernel
h
that satisfies HRUC regularity assumption. In this case, let us assume that
f
has bounded level set in the dual space.

Assumption 5.7
.

For any given

This typically holds when

Proposition 5.8
.

Under the setting of
Theorem

5.6

1

Proof.

According to

5.5
, the sequence

for all

Therefore, the sequence
\{\bar{x}^{t}\}\in\overline{\mathrm{lev}_{0}}:=\{\nabla h(x):f(x)\leq f(x^{0})\}
, indicating that
\{\bar{z}^{t}\}=\{\nabla h(\bar{x}^{t})\}
is bounded according to
5.7
. Denote
\bar{z}^{t}_{\theta}:=(1-\theta)\bar{z}^{t}+\theta\bar{z}^{t+1}

\{\bar{z}^{t}_{\theta}\}
is bounded for any
\theta\in[0,1]
. Recall that
H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}=\nabla^{2}h^{*}(\bar{z}^{t}_{\theta_{t}})
with some

, there is
0<\mu^{*}<L^{*}<\infty
such that
\mu^{*}I\preceq H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}\preceq L^{*}I
for all

Define

Clearly,
\max_{i\in[m]}\{\|s_{i}^{t}\|\}=\delta
for all

. Then, it then follows from (

|{\cal T}|\cdot\frac{\mu^{*}\delta^{2}}{12m\eta}\leq\frac{\mu^{*}}{12\eta}\cdot\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|s^{t}_{i}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{M}_{0}-\mathcal{M}_{\infty}\leq f(x^{0})-\underline{f},

which suggests that

∎

Given the bound on
|\mathcal{T}|
, and suppose

1

\left\|\nabla f(\bar{x}^{t})\right\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}\leq\frac{4}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|\bar{y}^{t}-y_{i}^{t}\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}+\frac{4}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x}^{t})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t}_{i})\right\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}+2\|\bar{y}^{t}\|^{2}_{H_{\theta_{t}}^{*}}.

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq T-1}\!\left\{\!\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|^{2}_{H^{*}_{\theta_{t}}}+\frac{L^{2}}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|\bar{z}^{t}-z_{i}^{t}\|^{2}_{H^{*}_{\theta_{t}}}\!\right\}\leq\min_{t\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}\left\{\!\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|^{2}_{H^{*}_{\theta_{t}}}+\frac{L^{2}}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|\bar{z}^{t}-z_{i}^{t}\|^{2}_{H^{*}_{\theta_{t}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\lesssim\frac{1}{T-|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}^{t},\mathbf{y}^{t},\mathbf{z}^{t})\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{L(f(x^{0})-\underline{f})}{(1-\rho)^{2}T}\right)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad T\geq 2|\mathcal{T}|,

where

To reconstruct the image from blurred and corrupted observations, we solve the Poisson inverse problem with a smoothed total variation (TV) penalty:

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{+}}\;\frac{1}{m}{\sum}_{i=1}^{m}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\big(B_{i},{\cal A}_{i}(X_{i})\big)\;+\;\lambda\cdot\mathrm{TV}(X_{i})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad X_{i}=X_{j},\;\forall\,i,j\in[m],

Appendix A
Complete proof of
Theorem

4.1

A.1
Separable kernels

In this subsection, we develop a streamlined verification procedure for separable kernels. Our derivation is built upon
Proposition

4.2
.

\bullet

Boltzmann-Shannon entropy.
Direct computation leads to
h_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\log(t)
and
h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{t}
for each
i\in[d]
. Define mappings:

\phi_{i}(t):=h_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\log(t),\;t>0\quad\text{and}\quad g_{i}(s):=\exp(-s),\;s>0.

It is easy to verify that
|\phi_{i}(x_{i})-\phi_{i}(y_{i})|\leq\delta
provided that
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
and
|g_{i}^{\prime}(s)|\leq 1
. Applying
Proposition

4.2
, we show that HRUC regularity holds with
\zeta(\delta)=\exp(\delta)-1
.

\bullet

Regularized Burg’s entropy.
For each
i\in[d]
, direct computation shows
h_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\mu t-1/t
and
h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\mu+1/t^{2}
. Define mappings:

\phi_{i}(t):=1/t,\;t>0\quad\text{and}\quad g_{i}(s):=\log(\mu+s^{2}),\;s>0.

Then,
|g_{i}^{\prime}(z)|=|\frac{2s}{\mu+s^{2}}|\leq 1/\sqrt{\mu}
and
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
infers
|\phi_{i}(x_{i})-\phi_{i}(y_{i})|\leq\delta
. By
Proposition

4.2
, HRUC regularity holds with
\zeta(\delta)=\exp(\delta/\sqrt{\mu})-1
.

\bullet

Regularized Tsallis entropy.
For each
i\in[d]
, we have
h_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\mu t-\frac{q}{1-q}\cdot t^{q-1}
and
h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\mu+q\cdot t^{q-2}
,
q\in(0,1)
and
t>0
. Introduce mappings

\phi_{i}(t):=t^{q-1},\;t>0\quad\text{and}\quad g_{i}(s):=\log\big(\mu+q\cdot s^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}}\big),\;s>0.

Note that
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
implies
|\phi_{i}(x_{i})-\phi_{i}(y_{i})|\leq\frac{\delta(1-q)}{q}
and

g^{\prime}(s)=\frac{2-q}{1-q}\cdot\frac{s^{\frac{1}{1-q}}}{\mu+q\cdot s^{\frac{2-q}{1-q}}}\leq\frac{2}{2-2q}\cdot\frac{1}{q^{\frac{1}{2-q}}\mu^{\frac{1-q}{2-q}}}\qquad\forall s>0.

By
Proposition

4.2
, we conclude that HRUC holds with
\zeta(\delta)=\exp\big(q^{\frac{q-3}{2-q}}\mu^{\frac{q-1}{2-q}}\cdot\delta\big)-1
.

\bullet

Exponential kernel.

Defining
\phi_{i}(t):=\exp(t)
and
g_{i}(s):=\log(\mu+s)
,
s>0
and applying
Proposition

4.2
, we yield the HRUC function
\zeta(\delta):=\exp(\delta/\mu)-1
.

\bullet

Generalized harmonic sum.

Since the proof is almost identical to that of
regularized Tsallis entropy
, we thus omit the proof and conclude that HRUC holds with
\zeta(\delta)=\exp(\mu^{-\frac{p+1}{p+2}}\cdot\delta)-1
.

\bullet

Hellinger function.
For each
i\in[d]
, we have
h_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{t}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}
and
h_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{1+t^{2}}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}
,
t\in(0,1)
. Set

\phi_{i}(t):=\frac{t}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}},\;t\in(0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{i}(s):=\log(1+2s^{2})-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+s^{2}),\;s\in\mathbb{R}.

Obviously,
\rho_{h}(x,y)\leq\delta
implies
|\phi_{i}^{\prime}(x_{i})-\phi_{i}^{\prime}(y_{i})|\leq\delta
.
Direct calculation gives

g^{\prime}(s)=\frac{s(2s^{2}+3)}{(1+s^{2})(1+2s^{2})}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{\sup}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\;|g^{\prime}(s)|<1.

Applying
Proposition

4.2
, the HRUC regularity with
\zeta(\delta)=\exp(\delta)-1
.

A.2
Self-concordant functions

Let us now consider specific examples of self-concordant functions. We demonstrate that by applying
Proposition

4.4
, it is rather straightforward to show that these functions are HRUC regular and to derive the associated distortion modulus
\zeta(\delta)
.

\bullet

General
\mu
-strongly convex and
\rho
-Lipschitz Hessian functions.
Observe that any kernel in this class is
M
-self-concordant with
M=\frac{\rho}{2\mu^{3/2}}
.
By
Proposition

4.4
,
M
-self-concordant and
\mu
-strongly convex functions are HRUC regular with
\zeta(\delta)=\exp(2M\mu^{-1/2}\delta)-1=\exp(\rho\delta/{\mu^{2}})-1
.

\bullet

Norm exponential kernel.
It can be verified that such kernel is
M
-self-concordant and
\mu
-strongly convex with
M=1
and
\mu=1
. Invoking
Proposition

4.4
, we show that such kernel is HRUC regular with modulus
\zeta(\delta)=\exp(2\delta)-1
.

Appendix B
Preparatory tools

Lemma B.1

(Three points identity
[
5
]
)
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a kernel function associated with
\mathcal{Z}
. For any

x\in\mathrm{dom}(h)
, and
y,z\in\mathrm{int\ dom}(h)
, we have

\mathcal{D}_{h}(x,z)-\mathcal{D}_{h}(x,y)-\mathcal{D}_{h}(y,z)=\langle\nabla h(y)-\nabla h(z),x-y\rangle.

Lemma B.2
.

Given symmetrical matrices
A,B\succ 0
and the constant
\alpha>0
. If
\|AB^{-1}-I\|\leq\alpha
, then
\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\leq\sqrt{1+\alpha}
.

Proof.

By the similarity of
B^{-\frac{1}{2}}AB^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
AB^{-1}
, we obtain

\big\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2}=\Big\|\big(A^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\top}\big(A^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Big\|=\big\|B^{-\frac{1}{2}}AB^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\overset{\text{(i)}}{\leq}\big\|AB^{-1}\big\|,

where (i) follows the spectral radius (
\varrho
) bounds for (a-)symmetric matrices:

\big\|B^{-\frac{1}{2}}AB^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|=\varrho(B^{-\frac{1}{2}}AB^{-\frac{1}{2}})=\varrho(AB^{-1})\leq\big\|AB^{-1}\big\|

Since
\|AB^{-1}-I\|\leq\alpha
, it holds that
\|AB^{-1}\|\leq 1+\alpha
. Thus,
\big\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2}\leq 1+\alpha
.
∎

Proof of

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle(I-J)\mathbf{v}^{+}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}=(I-J)W\mathbf{v}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}+(I-J)\mathbf{u}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(W-J)(I-J)\mathbf{v}H^{\frac{1}{2}}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}+(I-J)\mathbf{u}H^{\frac{1}{2}}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From the inequality
\|ABC\|_{F}\leq\|A\|\|B\|_{F}\|C\|
,
\forall\,A,B,C\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\big\|(I\!-\!J)\mathbf{v}^{+}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\big\|_{\!F}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|H^{-\frac{1}{2}}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\Big(\|W\!-\!J\|\|(I\!-\!J)\mathbf{v}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}+\|I\!-\!J\|\|\mathbf{u}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\Big)

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1+\alpha}\Big(\rho\|(I-J)\mathbf{v}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}+\|\mathbf{u}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\Big),

where the last line is by
\big\|H^{-\frac{1}{2}}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|=\big\|[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\leq\sqrt{1+\alpha}
,
\|(I-J)\|=1
and
\|W-J\|=\rho
.
Squaring both sides and invoking the inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)a^{2}+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})b^{2}

, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|(I-J)\mathbf{v}^{+}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha)\left(\frac{2\rho^{2}}{1+\rho}\,\big\|(I-J)\mathbf{v}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{2}{1-\rho}\,\big\|\mathbf{u}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\rho\big\|(I-J)\mathbf{v}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{3}{1-\rho}\big\|\mathbf{u}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{F}^{2},

where the last inequality is because
(1+\alpha)\cdot\frac{2\rho}{1+\rho}\leq 1
and

.
Upon substituting

\|(I-J)\mathbf{v}H^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}^{2}={\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}\|^{2}_{H}
and the equality

\big\|(I-J)\mathbf{v}^{+}[H^{+}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{F}^{2}={\sum}_{i=1}^{m}\big\|v_{i}^{+}-\bar{v}^{+}\big\|^{2}_{H^{+}},

we obtain the desired bound.
∎
