Abstract
. We establish the well-posedness of the Helmholtz equation with rough and compactly supported coefficients in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} under sharp regularity assumptions. Using a paraproduct calculus in rescaled weighted Besov spaces, we rigorously define the product between the solution and the coefficient at the lowest regularity level without renormalization. A rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger formulation is shown to be equivalent to the Helmholtz equation with the Sommerfeld radiation condition. We prove existence, uniqueness, and explicit wavenumber dependent resolvent estimates in a general L p L^{p} setting, including an L 2 L^{2} theory relevant to scattering amplitudes. The results provide a sharp analytic foundation for wave propagation and scattering in highly irregular media.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we establish the well-posedness of the Helmholtz equation,

\Delta u(x)+k^{2}u(x)+V_{k}(x)u(x)=g(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.1)

subject to the Sommerfeld radiation condition

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{(d-1)/2}\big(\partial_{|x|}u-{\rm i}ku\big)=0,

(1.2)

where
d\geq 2
and
k>0
denotes the wavenumber. Throughout this paper, we assume that both the coefficient
V_{k}
and the source term
g
are supported in a ball
B(0,R)
centered at the origin with radius
R
. Both
V_{k}
and
g
are allowed to be rough and their precise regularity assumptions will be specified later. In particular, the coefficient
V_{k}
may depend on
k
and is given by

V_{k}(x)=k^{2}\varepsilon(x)+{\rm i}k\sigma(x)+\varrho(x),

where
\varepsilon
,
\sigma
, and
\varrho
represent the relative dielectric permittivity, conductivity or absorption, and potential, respectively. We emphasize that the coefficient
V_{k}
is regarded as rough whenever at least one of the parameters
\epsilon
,
\sigma
, or
\varrho
lacks regularity, even if the remaining components are smooth. This formulation provides a unified framework for treating Helmholtz equations with rough media, rough absorption, or rough potentials.

The investigation of the Helmholtz equation with rough coefficients is motivated by the modeling and analysis of wave propagation in complex and random media, where material parameters may have limited regularity and exhibit rapidly spatial variations. In many realistic applications, including geophysical exploration, nondestructive testing, medical imaging, and acoustic or electromagnetic scattering in composite or heterogeneous materials, the underlying medium cannot be adequately characterized by smooth coefficients. Instead, the dielectric permittivity, conductivity, or potential may be discontinuous, highly oscillatory, or even distributional, reflecting sharp interfaces, fine-scale heterogeneities, or unresolved microstructures. Such features naturally give rise to Helmholtz equations with rough, and possibly wavenumber dependent, coefficients, and pose significant analytical challenges that are not covered by classical smooth coefficient theory.

The Helmholtz equation is closely related to the stationary Schrödinger equation; see, for instance,
[
7
]
. The corresponding time-dependent Schrödinger equation is given by

{\rm i}\partial_{t}u(t,x)-\big(\Delta+V(x)\big)u(t,x)=F(t,x),

where
V(x)
denotes the potential and
\Delta+V
is the Schrödinger operator. The analysis of outgoing solutions to this evolution equation, as discussed in
[
7
, Section 2.1]
, leads naturally to the study of the resolvent operator
R_{V}(k)
, defined as the inverse of
\Delta+k^{2}+V
. The existence and boundedness of
R_{V}(k)
constitute a fundamental problem in scattering theory and are closely connected to the well-posedness of the Helmholtz equation.

1.1.
Meromorphic continuation and limiting absorption principle

The study of the free resolvent
(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}
, denoted by
R_{0}(k)
, is fundamental in the analysis of the Helmholtz equation. Since the symbol
k^{2}-|\xi|^{2}
of the operator
\Delta+k^{2}
vanishes on the sphere
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|=k\}
in the frequency domain, its inverse is not a well-defined distribution in general. As a consequence, the homogeneous Helmholtz equation admits infinitely many nontrivial solutions unless additional conditions are imposed. For instance, it is well known that radial solutions of the homogeneous Helmholtz equation can be expressed as linear combinations of Bessel functions,

u(x)=C_{1}J_{m}(k|x|)+C_{2}Y_{m}(k|x|),

where
J_{m}
and
Y_{m}
denote the Bessel functions of the first and second kinds with order
m
, respectively, and
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
are constants. The existence of such nontrivial solutions, including bounded or oscillatory ones, is commonly referred to as resonance. This inherent non-uniqueness shows that the Helmholtz equation is not well-posed unless an additional physical condition, such as the Sommerfeld radiation condition, is imposed to single out the physically relevant solution.

From a physical perspective, one expects a unique response of the system once an incident wave is prescribed. In realistic media, this uniqueness is enforced by the presence of dissipation or absorption mechanisms, such as material losses, friction, or radiative damping, which prevent the persistence of nonphysical standing waves. The limiting absorption principle provides a rigorous mathematical framework to incorporate this physical effect into the analysis of the Helmholtz equation. More precisely, the operator
\Delta+k^{2}
is regularized by introducing a small attenuation term, leading to the perturbed operator
\Delta+k^{2}+{\rm i}\tau
with
\tau>0
. The free resolvent
R_{0}(k)
is then defined as the limit of
(\Delta+k^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{-1}
as
\tau\to 0^{+}
in an appropriate operator topology. This procedure selects the outgoing solution and restores uniqueness. For general potentials
V
, the resolvent
R_{V}(k)
admits a meromorphic continuation in the complex plane, which plays a central role in scattering theory. In particular, the meromorphic extension for
\Im{k}>0
is called the outgoing resolvent. One of its important applications is the analysis of the long-time behavior of wave equations, leading to resonance expansions of wave fields; see
[
7
]
. The meromorphic continuation of the resolvent has been established for bounded potentials in
[
7
]
and was later extended to certain classes of unbounded potentials in
[
15
]
.

1.2.
Point spectrum of Schrödinger operators

The well-posedness of the Helmholtz equation subject to the Sommerfeld radiation condition is also closely related to the absence of point spectrum for the associated Schrödinger operator
\Delta+V
. The spectral properties of Schrödinger operators are known to depend on the behavior of the potential function
V
. The question of identifying classes of potentials for which the Schrödinger operator admits no positive eigenvalues originates from physics
[
19
]
. In 1959, Kato proved that if
V
is bounded and satisfies
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|V(x)=0
, then
\Delta+V
has no positive eigenvalues
[
9
]
. In the 1970s, Agmon developed a systematic theory in his seminal works
[
1
,
2
]
, based on weighted
L^{2}
-spaces and resolvent estimates for the outgoing resolvent
R_{V}(k)
, which yield the absence of embedded eigenvalues and singular continuous spectrum. This approach unified and significantly extended earlier results. More recently, Ionescu and Jerison established the absence of positive eigenvalues for potentials that are merely locally integrable and satisfy suitable decay assumptions
[
12
]
. We refer to
[
18
, Section XIII]
for a comprehensive discussion of this topic.

The well-posedness of the Helmholtz equation can then be established by combining the Fredholm alternative with the unique continuation principle, which in turn follows from the absence of positive eigenvalues for the corresponding Schrödinger operator; see
[
6
,
17
,
8
,
11
,
7
]
. The main analytical tools include Agmon-type estimates
[
2
]
, Sobolev inequalities
[
10
,
3
]
, and Carleman estimates; see, for instance,
[
11
, Lemma 31.4]
and
[
12
, Theorem 2.3]
. In particular, M. Goldberg and W. Schlag
[
8
, Theorem 2]
proved the well-posedness for compactly supported potentials in
L^{3/2}
in dimension three. More recently, the well-posedness of the Helmholtz equation in the high frequency regime has been studied for compactly supported potentials in
H^{\alpha}
with any
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
; see
[
16
,
4
,
14
]
. These works rely on the contraction mapping principle, which yields well-posedness only for sufficiently large wavenumbers and typically requires the potential to be independent of the wavenumber.

1.3.
Main results

The free resolvent operator
(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}
can be represented by the Green’s function
G_{k}(x,y)
satisfying the Sommerfeld radiation condition; see, for example,
[
6
,
7
]
. Explicitly, the Green’s function is given by

G_{k}(x,y)=\frac{{\rm i}}{4}\left(\frac{k}{2\pi|x-y|}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}H^{(1)}_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\left(k|x-y|\right),

(1.3)

where
H^{(1)}_{\nu}
denotes the Hankel function of the first kind of order
\nu
. Using the Green’s function representation, the boundary value problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) can be equivalently reformulated as a Lippmann–Schwinger integral equation; see, for example,
[
6
,
17
]
. Specifically, the solution
u
satisfies

u(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{k}(x-y)V_{k}(y)u(y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{k}(x-y)g(y)dy,

where the integrals are understood in a suitable weak sense. Formally, this equation may be written as

u+(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}(V_{k}u)=(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}g,

(1.4)

which highlights the role of the free resolvent in the analysis of the Helmholtz equation with rough coefficients.

We begin by briefly explaining the heuristic motivation behind the regularity assumptions in our analysis. Suppose that the coefficient
V_{k}
belongs to the Besov space
B^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}
and that the solution
u
lies in
B^{\beta}_{p,q}
. By Bony’s paraproduct decomposition, the product
V_{k}u
is well-defined only when
\alpha+\beta>0
, and its regularity cannot exceed
\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
. On the other hand, the resolvent operator
(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}
acts as a smoothing operator of order two. Consequently, one expects

(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}(V_{k}u)\in B^{\min\{\alpha,\beta\}+2}_{p,q}.

Since this term must have the same regularity as
u
in the Lippmann–Schwinger formulation, a formal consistency argument yields

\beta=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}+2,\quad\alpha+\beta>0.

These relations indicate that the regularity of
V_{k}
must satisfy
\alpha>-1
. This heuristic calculation suggests that existing results based on such arguments are not optimal and motivates the development of a refined analytical framework.

Our first main results are stated in the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\eta_{0}\in(1/2,1)
,
p_{0}\in[1,\infty)
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
satisfy

r_{0}:=2-(d+1)\theta/2-2\eta_{0}(1-\theta)>0.

Then, for any
\epsilon>0
,
r\in(0,r_{0})
,
p_{0}^{\prime}\in(d/(2-2r),\infty)
, and compactly supported distributions
g\in B^{r-2}_{2p_{0},2p_{0}}
and
V_{k}\in B^{-r+\epsilon}_{p_{0}^{\prime},p_{0}^{\prime}}
, with
1/p_{0}+1/p_{0}^{\prime}=1
, the boundary value problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) admits a unique solution
u\in B^{r}_{2p_{0},2p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta_{0}})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\eta_{0}\in(1/2,1)
, and

r\in\begin{cases}((d+1)(1/2-1/p_{0})+4\eta_{0}/p_{0},\ 2),&p_{0}\in[2,\infty),\\[6.0pt]
((d+1)(1/2-1/p_{0}^{\prime})+4\eta_{0}/p_{0}^{\prime},\ 2),&p_{0}\in[1,2),\end{cases}

with
1/p_{0}+1/p_{0}^{\prime}=1
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, and compactly supported distributions
g\in B^{r-2}_{p_{0},p_{0}}
and
V_{k}\in B^{r-2+\epsilon}_{\infty,\infty}
, the boundary value problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) admits a unique solution
u\in B^{r}_{p_{0},p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta_{0}})
.

Let
\eta_{0}
be chosen close to
1/2
and
\theta
close to
0
. Then Theorem
1.1
yields well-posedness of the boundary value problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) for coefficients
V_{k}\in B^{-r+\epsilon}_{p_{0}^{\prime},p_{0}^{\prime}}
with any
r<1
and
p_{0}^{\prime}\in(d/(2-2r),\infty)
. Moreover, as
\eta_{0}
increases, the uniqueness is obtained in a larger weighted Besov space, reflecting weaker spatial decay of the solution. On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
establishes the existence and uniqueness of solutions with higher regularity under the stronger assumption
V_{k}\in B^{r-2+\epsilon}_{\infty,\infty}
. In this case, the solution belongs to a smoother Besov space than that guaranteed by Theorem
1.1
, highlighting the complementary nature of the two results.

One of the main ideas in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is inspired by the pioneering work of
[
20
]
, where the Faddeev operator was introduced and utilized in the construction of special solutions. In the present paper, we develop this idea further by considering a broader class of operators that includes both the Faddeev operator and the free resolvent operator
(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}
. Specifically, for any
k\in(0,\infty)
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we study the family of Faddeev-type resolvent operators

(\Delta+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla)^{-1},\quad r_{k,\gamma}:=(k^{2}+|\gamma|^{2})^{1/2}.

A precise definition of these operators via the limiting absorption principle is given in Theorem
4.3
.

Our second main results establish wavenumber explicit estimates for the Faddeev-type operators, which extend Agmon’s classical resolvent estimates in several significant directions. We establish Sobolev-type estimates for the associated operators, including an
L^{2}
-based wavenumber explicit estimate and a more general
L^{p}
-based wavenumber explicit estimate. We first give an intuitive overview of the results, while the precise statements are provided in Theorems
4.3
and
4.5
, respectively.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta>\tfrac{1}{2}
, and
s\in[0,2]
. Then the following estimate holds:

\big\|(\Delta+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla)^{-1}f\big\|_{H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\min\{r_{k,\gamma},1\}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})},

where the implicit constant is independent of
k
,
\gamma
, and
f
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,d/2)
,
p_{1},p_{2},q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
1/p_{2}-1/p_{1}=\alpha/d
. Then, for any
\eta\in((d+1)/2-\alpha,\infty)
, the following estimate holds:

\big\|(\Delta+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla)^{-1}f\big\|_{B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\max\{r_{k,\gamma}^{\eta},r_{k,\gamma}^{-2}\}\|f\|_{B^{r-2}_{p_{2},q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})},

where the implicit constant is independent of
k
,
\gamma
, and
f
.

A key starting point in our analysis of the Faddeev-type operators is the identity

e^{\gamma\cdot x}(\Delta+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla)f(x)=(\Delta+k^{2})e^{\gamma\cdot x}f(x),\quad\forall\,f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Using this relation together with the transformation
u=e^{\gamma\cdot x}u_{\gamma}
, we can formally rewrite (
1.1
) as

u_{\gamma}(x)+\big(\Delta+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla\big)^{-1}\big(V_{k}(x)u_{\gamma}(x)\big)=g_{k,\gamma}(x),

(1.5)

where
g_{k,\gamma}=\big(\Delta+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla\big)^{-1}\big(e^{-\gamma\cdot x}g\big)
. We refer to Lemmas
5.4
–
5.5
and the beginning of Section
5.2
for a detailed discussion of the equivalence between (
1.4
) and (
1.5
). Establishing this equivalence constitutes one of the main technical challenges in the analysis. At the level of Fourier symbols, the resolvent
(|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi)^{-1}
exhibits a pole structure that differs from that of the free resolvent symbol
(|\xi|^{2}-k^{2})^{-1}
, so the equivalence is not immediate. The limiting absorption principle provides a natural regularization of the singular symbol
(|\xi|^{2}-k^{2})^{-1}
, and a crucial step is to show that the corresponding regularization of
(|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi)^{-1}
remains compatible with this principle after conjugation by the exponential weight
e^{\gamma\cdot x}
.
In particular, we establish a uniform limiting absorption principle with respect to
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
in Theorem
3.5
.

Another key idea in the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
is a rescaling argument, motivated by the observation that the limiting absorption principle provides additional integrability for the system. This leads naturally to the question of whether the regularizing effect of the inverse scattering operator
(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}
can dominate the growth of spatial volume induced by rescaling with a small parameter. To make this argument precise, we introduce appropriately rescaled Besov spaces and analyze their behavior under scaling transformations. This framework allows us to control the rescaled equation and to quantify the balance between regularization and volume growth. For further details, we refer the reader to the proof of Lemma
5.8
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notation and functional framework used throughout the paper, including the definition of rescaled weighted Besov spaces. In Section
3
, we define the regularized
Faddeev-type operator
(\Delta+k^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla+{\rm i}\tau)^{-1}
, where
\tau>0
is a regularization parameter. Section
4
is devoted to the definition of the Faddeev-type operator
(\Delta+k^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla)^{-1}
via the limiting absorption principle as
\tau\to 0
, together with the derivation of wavenumber explicit estimates. In Section
5
, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. The proofs of auxiliary results, including paraproduct estimates and rescaling properties of the rescaled weighted Besov spaces, are collected in the Appendix.

2.
Notations and preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Schwartz space of rapidly decaying smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be its dual space consisting of tempered distributions. In addition, we denote by
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of smooth functions with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the corresponding space of distributions.

Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}
. Let
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
denote a vector on the
(d-1)
-dimensional unit sphere, and write
\xi=r\omega
for the spherical coordinates of
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Throughout the paper, we use the notation
(f)_{\lambda}(x):=f(\lambda x)
for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the rescaling parameter
\lambda>0
, and we denote by
p^{\prime}
the Hölder conjugate of
p
such that
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
.

For any multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, we define the differential operator
D^{\alpha}
by

D^{\alpha}:=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}},

where
|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}
. The Fourier transform of a function
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi{\rm i}\xi\cdot x}f(x){\rm d}x,

and the inverse Fourier transform is given by

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f)(x)=\check{f}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{2\pi{\rm i}\xi\cdot x}f(\xi){\rm d}\xi.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
the set of all bounded linear mappings from a Banach space
X
into a Banach space
Y
. An operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is said to be the strong limit of a sequence of operators
\{T_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{n}-T\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}=0.

The operator
T
is called the weak limit of
\{T_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{n}x-Tx\|_{Y}=0,\quad\forall\,x\in X.

2.1.
Rescaled weighted Besov spaces

A pair of smooth functions
({\chi},{\varphi})
is called admissible partition pair if the following properties are satisfied:

(1)

\chi(\xi)+\sum_{n\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-n}\xi)=1
;

(2)

\text{supp}\,\varphi(2^{-i}\xi)\cap\text{supp}\,\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=\emptyset
for
|i-j|\geq 2
;

(3)

\text{supp}\,\varphi(2^{-i}\xi)\cap\text{supp}\,\chi(\xi)=\emptyset
for all
i\geq 1
.

According to
[
5
, Chapter 2]
, there exists an admissible partition pair
({\chi},{\varphi})
such that

(1)

\text{supp}\,{\chi}\subseteq B(0,4/3)=:\mathcal{B}\ \text{and}\ \chi(\xi)=1\ \text{for all}\ |\xi|\leq 3/4;

(2)

\text{supp}\,{\varphi}\subseteq\{\xi:\ 3/4\leq|\xi|\leq 8/3\}=:\mathcal{C}.

For simplicity, we define
\varphi_{n}(\cdot):=\varphi(2^{-n}\cdot)
for
n\geq 0
and set
\varphi_{-1}(\cdot):=\chi(\cdot)
. Moreover, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
\chi_{n}:=\sum_{j\leq n}\varphi_{j}=\chi(2^{-n-1}\cdot)
.
In particular, we have
\chi_{-1}(\cdot)=\chi(\cdot)
. Similar notation is adopted for any admissible partition pair.

We denote
\langle x\rangle:=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
. Following
[
23
, Section 6.1.2]
, a positive function
\rho
is said to be admissible of type
W(\eta)
,
\eta\geq 0
, if the following conditions hold:

(1)

for every multi-index
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, there exists a constant
C_{N_{0}}>0
such that
|D^{N_{0}}\rho(x)|\leq C_{N_{0}}\rho(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(2)

there exist constants
c>0
and
\eta\geq 0
such that
0<\rho(x)\leq c\rho(y)\langle x-y\rangle^{\eta}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

It is straightforward to verify that
\langle x\rangle^{\eta}
is admissible for all
\eta\geq 0
. Moreover, the following inequality holds:

\langle x\rangle^{-\eta}\lesssim\langle y\rangle^{-\eta}\langle x-y\rangle^{\eta},\quad\forall\,\eta\geq 0.

(2.1)

Hence, both
\langle x\rangle^{\eta}
and
\langle x\rangle^{-\eta}
are admissible weights of type
W(\eta)
.

For any admissible weight
\rho
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
L^{p}(\rho)
the weighted Lebesgue space consisting of all measurable functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\rho)}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}\rho(x)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p},&1\leq p<\infty,\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\rho(x),&p=\infty.\end{cases}

Next, we introduce the weighted Besov space
B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We define the Littlewood–Paley operators
\Delta_{j}
and
S_{j}
by

\Delta_{j}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\varphi_{j}\hat{f}\big),\quad S_{j}f:=\sum_{n=-1}^{j-1}\Delta_{n}f,\quad j\geq-1.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,q)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. For any admissible weight
\rho
, the weighted Besov norm
\|\cdot\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)}
is defined by

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\geq-1}2^{nrq}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p}(\rho)}^{q}\right)^{1/q},&1\leq q<\infty,\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq-1}2^{nr}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p}(\rho)},&q=\infty.\end{cases}

In particular, when the weight is trivial, i.e.,
\rho\equiv 1
, we write
B^{r}_{p,q}:=B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)
and
\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}}:=\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)}
, recovering the classical Besov space.

The following lemma clarifies the relationship between weighted Besov spaces and the classical Besov spaces; see
[
23
, Theorem 6.5]
for the proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. For any admissible weight
\rho
, the following norm equivalence holds:

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)}\sim\|\rho f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}}.

By Lemma
2.1
and
[
5
, Corollary 2.70]
, the weighted Besov space
B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)
is independent of the particular choice of the partition of unity
(\chi,\varphi)
for any admissible weight
\rho
. Moreover, weighted Besov spaces share many of the fundamental properties of the classical nonhomogeneous Besov spaces; we refer the reader to
[
5
, Chapter 2.7]
for a comprehensive discussion.

The following result follows directly from the definition of the weighted Besov space.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
be admissible weights with
\rho_{1}\leq c\rho_{2}
for some constant
c>0
. For any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, there holds

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho_{1})}\leq c\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho_{2})}.

By a direct application of the monotonicity of
l^{p}
-spaces
[
22
, Sections 2.7.1]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\rho
be an admissible weight. The following embedding properties hold.

(1)

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
,
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\infty
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q_{2}}(\rho)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q_{1}}(\rho)}.

(2)

For any
p,q_{1},q_{2}\in[1,\infty]
and
-\infty<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty
,

\|f\|_{B^{r_{1}}_{p,q_{1}}(\rho)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{r_{2}}_{p,q_{2}}(\rho)}.

Here the implicit constants above are independent of the admissible weight
\rho
.

In addition, by Lemma
2.1
together with
[
22
, Sections 2.7.1]
, one can readily establish the following embedding result for weighted Besov spaces.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
,
q\in[1,\infty]
, and
0<r_{2}\leq r_{1}<\infty
satisfying
r_{1}-d/p_{1}=r_{2}-d/p_{2}
, the following continuous embedding holds:

\|f\|_{B^{r_{2}}_{p_{2},q}(\rho)}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{r_{1}}_{p_{1},q}(\rho)}.

The following lifting property of weighted Besov spaces is proved in
[
23
, Theorem 6.1.3]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,q)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. For any admissible weight
\rho
, the following norm equivalence holds:

\big\|(I-\Delta)^{s/2}f\big\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)}\sim\|f\|_{B^{r+s}_{p,q}(\rho)},

where
I
denotes the identity operator.

We next extend the definition of weighted Besov spaces and introduce the rescaled weighted Besov spaces. Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,q)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. For any admissible weight
\rho
and scaling parameter
\lambda>0
, we define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho,\lambda)}
by

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho,\lambda)}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\geq-1}2^{nrq}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}^{q}\right)^{1/q},&1\leq q<\infty,\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq-1}2^{nr}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)},&q=\infty,\end{cases}

where
L^{p}(\rho,\lambda):=L^{p}((\rho)_{\lambda})
denotes the weighted Lebesgue space associated with the rescaled weight
(\rho)_{\lambda}(x):=\rho(\lambda x)
. It is immediate from the definition that

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho,\lambda)}=\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}((\rho)_{\lambda})}.

Although the nonhomogeneous Besov spaces do not enjoy the same scaling properties as their homogeneous counterparts (cf.
[
5
, Chapter 2.3]
), the rescaled weighted Besov spaces introduced here still satisfy useful rescaling properties. The proofs are given in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,q)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. For any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
,
\eta\in[0,\infty)
, and admissible weight
\rho
of type
W(\eta)
, the following estimate holds:

\|(f)_{\lambda}\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim_{\,r,q,\eta}\lambda^{-d/p}\max\{\lambda^{r},\lambda^{\eta},1\}\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)},

where the implicit constant is independent of
\lambda
and
\rho
.

Lemma
2.6
implies that, under suitable conditions,

\|(f)_{\lambda}\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim_{\,r,q}\lambda^{r-d/p}\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho)},

where the exponent
r-d/p
is usually referred to as the differential dimension. This quantity plays an important role in embedding theorems; see Lemma
2.4
above.

2.2.
Interpolations

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces with
Y\subseteq X
. We begin by recalling the definition of real interpolation spaces. For
x\in X
, the
K
-functional associated with the pair
(X,Y)
is defined by

K(t,x;X,Y):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=a+b\\
a\in X,\,b\in Y\end{subarray}}\big(\|a\|_{X}+t\|b\|_{Y}\big),\quad t>0.

Let
L^{p}_{\ast}(0,\infty)
denote the Lebesgue space on
(0,\infty)
with respect to the measure
t^{-1}dt
. For any
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, the real interpolation space
(X,Y)_{\theta,p}
is defined as the set of all
x\in X
such that

\|x\|_{(X,Y)_{\theta,p}}:=\big\|t^{-\theta}K(t,x;X,Y)\big\|_{L^{p}_{\ast}(0,\infty)}<\infty.

For further properties of real interpolation spaces, we refer the reader to
[
13
, Chapter 1]
.

The following result on bounded linear operators between real interpolation spaces is fundamental; see
[
13
, Theorem 1.6]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(X_{1},Y_{1})
and
(X_{2},Y_{2})
be real interpolation couples. Suppose that
L\in\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{2})\cap\mathcal{L}(Y_{1},Y_{2})
. Then, for every
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, the operator
L
extends to a bounded linear operator
L\in\mathcal{L}\big((X_{1},Y_{1})_{\theta,p},\,(X_{2},Y_{2})_{\theta,p}\big)
, and the following estimate holds

\|L\|_{\mathcal{L}((X_{1},Y_{1})_{\theta,p},(X_{2},Y_{2})_{\theta,p})}\leq(\|L\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1},X_{2})})^{1-\theta}(\|L\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{1},Y_{2})})^{\theta}.

For any
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, we define the Sobolev space
W^{r,p}:=B^{r}_{p,p}

and the weighted Sobolev space
W^{r,p}(\rho):=B^{r}_{p,p}(\rho).
In particular, for
p=2
, we write
H^{r}:=B^{r}_{2,2}
and
H^{r}(\rho):=B^{r}_{2,2}(\rho)
, which correspond to the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space and its weighted counterpart, respectively. For more general results on the equivalence and interrelations between Besov spaces, Hölder–Zygmund spaces, Bessel-potential spaces, and Sobolev spaces, we refer the reader to
[
22
, Section 2.3.5]
.

The following result on real interpolation between Besov spaces with different integrability exponents is proved in
[
21
, Section 2.4.1]
and
[
22
, Section 2.4.3]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty)
. For any
\theta\in(0,1)
, define

r:=(1-\theta)r_{1}+\theta r_{2},\quad 1/p:=(1-\theta)/p_{1}+\theta/p_{2}.

Then the following real interpolation identity holds:

\big(B^{r_{1}}_{p_{1},p_{1}},B^{r_{2}}_{p_{2},p_{2}}\big)_{\theta,p}=B^{r}_{p,p}.

2.3.
Besov spaces on bounded domains

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a nonempty bounded domain, and let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. The Besov space
B^{r}_{p,q}(\Omega)
is defined by

B^{r}_{p,q}(\Omega)\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega):\exists\,g\in B^{r}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{such that}\ f=g\big|_{\Omega}\bigr\}.

The space
B^{r}_{p,q}(\Omega)
is equipped with the quotient norm

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\inf\bigl\{\|g\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:g\in B^{r}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ g\big|_{\Omega}=f\bigr\},

which makes
B^{r}_{p,q}(\Omega)
a Banach space.

Let
\partial\Omega
be a compact
(d-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz manifold, and let
\{O_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be an open covering of
\partial\Omega
. We choose a finite family of Lipschitz charts
\kappa_{j}:U_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to O_{j}\subset\partial\Omega,j=1,\dots,N
, together with a smooth partition of unity
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
subordinate to
\{O_{j}\}
, i.e.,
\phi_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
,
\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j}\subset O_{j}
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}(x)=1
for all
x\in\partial\Omega.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. The Besov space on
\partial\Omega
is defined by

B^{r}_{p,q}(\partial\Omega):=\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\partial\Omega)\;:\;(\phi_{j}f)\circ\kappa_{j}\in B^{r}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})\ \text{for each }j=1,\dots,N\bigr\},

where
B^{r}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
denotes the usual Besov space on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. The norm on
B^{r}_{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
is given by

\|f\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\partial\Omega)}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bigl\|(\phi_{j}f)\circ\kappa_{j}\bigr\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})},

which makes
B^{r}_{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
a Banach space. Different choices of the atlas
\{\kappa_{j}\}
and the partition of unity
\{\phi_{j}\}
lead to equivalent norms. In particular, when
\partial\Omega
is smooth, the resulting space is independent of these choices. For further details, we refer the reader to
[
22
, Section 3.2.2]
.

We define the trace operator
Tu
as the restriction of
u
to the boundary
\partial\Omega
. The following lemma extends the trace operator to Sobolev spaces; see
[
22
, Section 3.3.3]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
r\in(1/p,\infty)
. Assume that
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial U
. Then the trace operator
T
extends to a bounded linear operator satisfying

\|Tu\|_{W^{r-1/p,p}(\partial U)}\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{r,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

3.
The regularized Faddeev-type operator

For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the symbol

m_{k,\gamma,\tau}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
by

m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\xi):=|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi-{\rm i}\tau.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function such that

\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset B(0,1)
and
\psi\equiv 1
on
B(0,1/2)
, where
B(0,r)
is the ball centered at
0
with radius
r
. For any
r_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
, we define the rescaled cutoff
function
\psi(\cdot;r_{0},\epsilon_{0}):\mathbb{C}\to[0,1]
by

\psi(x;r_{0},\epsilon_{0}):=\psi\big(|x-r_{0}|\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\big).

We now introduce the regularized Faddeev type operator
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\cdot\,;\psi,\epsilon_{0})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix
s\in[0,2]
,
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,\min\{r_{k,\gamma}/4,1\})
, and
\tau\in(-\epsilon_{0}^{2},0)
or
\tau\in(0,\epsilon_{0}^{2})
.
We define the operator
(I-\Delta)^{s/2}\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\,\cdot\,;\psi,\epsilon_{0}):\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

by duality as follows:

\big\langle(I-\Delta)^{s/2}\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}(f;\psi,\epsilon_{0}),\,g\big\rangle=I^{(1,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)+I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)+I^{(3,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g),

where

\displaystyle I^{(1,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\hat{f}(\omega r)\hat{g}(\omega r)}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)}\bigl(1-\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})\bigr)r^{d-1}d\omega dr,

\displaystyle I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\,\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}d\omega dr,

\displaystyle I^{(3,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\big(\hat{f}(\omega r)\hat{g}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\big)}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}d\omega dr,

for all
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Once the integrals
I^{(j,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)
,
j=1,2,3
, are well-defined, the above definition can be written in the Fourier multiplier form

\big\langle(I-\Delta)^{s/2}\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}(f;\psi,\epsilon_{0}),g\big\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\xi)}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\xi)d\xi.

(3.1)

Thus, the operator
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
can be viewed as the Fourier multiplier associated with the symbol
m_{k,\gamma,\tau}
.

We next verify that the integrals
I^{(j,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)
,
j=1,2,3
, are well-defined. We begin with the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

The integral
I^{(1,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)
is well-defined. Moreover, for any
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the following estimate holds:

\big|I^{(1,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)\big|\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

We begin with the case
s=0
. Observe that

\big||\xi|^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}\big|=|r+r_{k,\gamma}||r-r_{k,\gamma}|\gtrsim\epsilon_{0}(r_{k,\gamma}+\varepsilon_{0})\quad\text{on }\operatorname{supp}\bigl(1-\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\varepsilon_{0})\bigr).

(3.2)

Hence, we obtain

\big|I^{(1,0)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)\big|\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{|\hat{f}(\omega r)||\hat{g}(\omega r)|}{\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon_{0}+r_{k,\gamma})}r^{d-1}d\omega dr.

Applying Hölder’s inequality together with Plancherel’s identity yields

\big|I^{(1,0)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)\big|\lesssim\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We next consider the case
s=2
. Using (
3.2
) once again, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1+r^{2}}{|r^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}+r^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}}{|r^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|}\lesssim 1+\frac{1+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}}{|r^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\frac{1+r_{k,\gamma}^{2}}{\epsilon_{0}(r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0})}\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{2}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\quad\text{on supp}\big(1-\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})\big),

which implies

|I^{(1,2)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)|\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{(1+r^{2})|\hat{f}(\omega r)||\hat{g}(\omega r)|}{|r^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|}r^{d-1}d\omega dr\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{2}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For any
s\in(0,2)
, we apply an interpolation argument between the cases
s=0
and
s=2
to obtain

|I^{(1,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)|\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{|r^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|^{s/2}}\frac{|\hat{f}(\omega r)||\hat{g}(\omega r)|}{|r^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|^{1-s/2}}r^{d-1}d\omega dr\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The integral
I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)
is well-defined. Moreover, for any
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any
\eta>1/2
, the following estimate holds:

\big|I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)\big|\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}.

Proof.

By Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle|I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\ \frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{|m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)|}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{n-1}dr\ |\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|d\omega.

To proceed, we rewrite the symbol
m_{k,\gamma,\tau}
in polar coordinates as

m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(r,\omega)=r^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}+2{\rm i}r\gamma_{\omega}-{\rm i}\tau,\quad\gamma_{\omega}=\gamma\cdot\omega.

(3.3)

When
|\gamma_{\omega}|\geq\varepsilon_{0}/4
, we estimate the symbol
m_{k,\gamma,\tau}
using its imaginary part. In this case,

|m_{k,\gamma,\tau}|\geq\epsilon_{0}r/2-\tau\geq\epsilon_{0}(r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0})/2-\tau\geq\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}/8,

(3.4)

where we have used the facts that

r\geq r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0}
on
\operatorname{supp}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})
,
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,r_{k,\gamma}/4)
, and
\tau\in[0,\epsilon_{0}^{2})\subset[0,\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}/4)
. Therefore, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{|m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)|}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr\right|\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}r_{k,\gamma}^{d-1}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}2\epsilon_{0}=(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}r_{k,\gamma}^{d-2}\ .

When
|\gamma_{\omega}|<\epsilon_{0}/4
, we extend the symbol
m_{k,\gamma,\tau}
to the complex plane and deform the integration path accordingly. We introduce the contour
\Gamma
as follows. If
\tau<0
, we define

\Gamma:=\bigl\{r=r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}e^{{\rm i}\theta}:\theta\in(0,\pi)\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{r\in[r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0},r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}]\bigr\}=:\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}\cup\Gamma_{0}.

If
\tau>0
, we instead define

\Gamma:=\bigl\{r=r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}e^{{\rm i}\theta}:\theta\in(\pi,2\pi)\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{r\in[r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0},r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}]\bigr\}=:\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}\cup\Gamma_{0}.

Let
A_{\gamma}
denote the subset of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
defined by
A_{\gamma}:=\bigl\{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:|\gamma_{\omega}|<\epsilon_{0}/4\bigr\}.
To handle the possible poles enclosed by the contour
\Gamma
, we solve the equation
m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(r,\omega)=0
, which yields

r_{1,2}=-{\rm i}\gamma_{\omega}\pm\bigl(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau\bigr)^{1/2}.

Here the complex square root is understood in the principal branch, i.e.,

\bigl(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau\bigr)^{1/2}=\bigl((r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2})^{2}+\tau^{2}\bigr)^{1/4}e^{\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\arctan\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}}\Bigr)}.

Since
|\gamma_{\omega}|<\epsilon_{0}/4<r_{k,\gamma}/16
and
\tau\in(0,\epsilon_{0}^{2})
, we have

\bigl((r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2})^{2}+\tau^{2}\bigr)^{1/4}\geq\frac{r_{k,\gamma}}{2},\quad\arctan\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}}\Bigr)\leq\arctan\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\Bigr),

which in turn implies

\Re\bigl(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau\bigr)^{1/2}\geq\frac{r_{k,\gamma}}{4}.

(3.5)

Here we only consider the case
\tau>0
, as the case
\tau<0
can be treated in an analogous manner. On the semicircular part
\Gamma_{\varepsilon_{0}}
of the contour, we decompose the symbol
m_{k,\gamma,\tau}
as

m_{k,\gamma,\tau}=(r-r_{k,\gamma}+{\rm i}\gamma_{\omega})(r+r_{k,\gamma}-{\rm i}\gamma_{\omega})+2(r-r_{k,\gamma}){\rm i}\gamma_{\omega}-{\rm i}\tau-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}.

Since
|\gamma_{\omega}|<\epsilon_{0}/4
, we have

\displaystyle|r-r_{k,\gamma}+{\rm i}\gamma_{\omega}|

\displaystyle=\big|\epsilon_{0}\cos\theta+{\rm i}(\gamma_{\omega}+\epsilon_{0}\sin\theta)\big|

\displaystyle=\big(\epsilon_{0}^{2}+2\epsilon_{0}\gamma_{\omega}\sin\theta+\gamma_{\omega}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\geq\big(\epsilon_{0}^{2}/2+\gamma_{\omega}^{2}\big)^{1/2},

where we have used that
\theta\in(\pi,2\pi)
on
\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle|m_{k,\gamma,\tau}|

\displaystyle\geq\big|(r-r_{k,\gamma}+{\rm i}\gamma_{\omega})(r+r_{k,\gamma}-{\rm i}\gamma_{\omega})\big|-|2(r-r_{k,\gamma})\gamma_{\omega}|-|\tau|-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon_{0}(r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0})-\epsilon_{0}^{2}/2-\epsilon_{0}^{2}-\epsilon_{0}^{2}/16\geq\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}/4,

(3.6)

where we used the assumptions
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,r_{k,\gamma}/4)
and
\tau\in(0,\epsilon_{0}^{2})
.
This lower bound implies that

\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{|m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)|}r^{d-1}dr\lesssim\frac{2\pi\epsilon_{0}(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}r_{k,\gamma}^{d-1}\lesssim(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}r_{k,\gamma}^{d-2}.

In particular, there are no poles of the integrand on
\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}
for all

\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
satisfying
|\gamma_{\omega}|<\epsilon_{0}/4
.

On the other hand, poles may potentially lie on the segment
\Gamma_{0}
. We first observe that, since the semicircular arc
\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}
lies in the right half-plane and in the lower half of the complex plane, the only possible pole enclosed by the contour
\Gamma
is

r:=-{\rm i}\gamma_{\omega}+(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2},\quad 0<\gamma_{\omega}<\epsilon_{0}/4.

(3.7)

We next examine the imaginary part of the candidate pole (
3.7
). A direct computation shows that the imaginary and real parts of
(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}
are given by

\displaystyle\Im(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2})+\big((r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2})^{2}+\tau^{2}\big)^{1/2}}{2}\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\Re(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2})+\big(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2})^{2}+\tau^{2}\big)^{1/2}}{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Define the function
f(x):=x^{2}-\big(\Im(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-x^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}\big)^{2}
. A pole lies on
\Gamma_{0}
only if
f(x)=0
. Differentiating
f
yields

f^{\prime}(x)=x+\frac{x(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-x^{2})}{\big((r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-x^{2})^{2}+\tau^{2}\big)^{1/2}}>0,\quad x\in(0,r_{k,\gamma}),

which shows that
f
is strictly increasing on
(0,r_{k,\gamma})
.

The above analysis shows that there exists at most one value
\gamma_{\omega}\in(0,\epsilon_{0}/4)
for which
\Im(r)=0
.
We denote this critical value by
\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)
. Since
f(0)<0
and
f
is strictly increasing on
(0,r_{k,\gamma})
,
a pole lies on or inside the contour
\Gamma
if and only if
\gamma_{\omega}\geq\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)
. More precisely, the following two cases occur:

(1)

If
\gamma_{\omega}<\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)
, then
\Im(r)>0
and no pole lies inside or on the contour
\Gamma
.

(2)

If
\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)\leq\gamma_{\omega}\leq\epsilon_{0}/4
, we show that the pole
r
lies inside or on the contour
\Gamma
.

By the preceding discussion, the condition
\gamma_{\omega}\geq\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)
implies that
\Im(r)\leq 0
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
|r-r_{k,\gamma}|<\epsilon_{0}
. Let
a={r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}}
. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\Re(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}-r_{k,\gamma}

\displaystyle=\Re(a^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}-\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{a}-r_{k,\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+\tau^{2}}-a}{2}}{\big(\frac{a+\sqrt{a^{2}+\tau^{2}}}{2}\big)^{1/2}+\sqrt{a}}-\frac{\gamma_{\omega}^{2}}{\sqrt{a}+r_{k,\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau^{2}}{\big((2a+2\sqrt{a^{2}+\tau^{2}})^{1/2}+2\sqrt{a}\big)\big(a+\sqrt{a^{2}+\tau^{2}}\big)}-\frac{\gamma_{\omega}^{2}}{\sqrt{a}+r_{k,\gamma}}.

Since
|\gamma_{\omega}|\leq\epsilon_{0}/4
and
\tau\in(0,\epsilon_{0}^{2})
, we have
a\geq r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\epsilon_{0}^{2}/16\geq 16\epsilon_{0}^{2}
, which implies

|\Re(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}-r_{k,\gamma}|\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{8a\sqrt{a}}+\frac{\gamma_{\omega}^{2}}{2\sqrt{a}}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{256}.

Moreover, since
\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)\leq\gamma_{\omega}\leq\epsilon_{0}/4
, we have
\gamma_{\omega}-\Im(a+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|r-r_{k,\gamma}|

\displaystyle\leq|\Re(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}-r_{k,\gamma}|+|\gamma_{\omega}-\Im(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}|

\displaystyle\leq|\Re(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}-r_{k,\gamma}|+\gamma_{\omega}<\epsilon_{0}/2,

which proves the claim.

We define the sets
A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
and
\partial^{\mathrm{in}}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
by

A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}:=\bigl\{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)<\gamma\cdot\omega\leq\epsilon_{0}/4\bigr\},\quad\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}:=\bigl\{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)=\gamma\cdot\omega\bigr\}.

Geometrically, the set
A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
forms a spherical band on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. From the preceding analysis, the union

A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}\cup\partial^{\mathrm{in}}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
is precisely the subset of
A_{\gamma}
consisting of all directions
\omega
for which the contour
\Gamma
encloses a pole or passes through one. In particular,
\partial^{\mathrm{in}}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
characterizes the set of
\omega\in A_{\gamma}
for which a pole lies exactly on the contour
\Gamma
.

Since
\partial^{\mathrm{in}}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
is a set of Lebesgue measure zero on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, the possible presence of poles on the contour
\Gamma
does not affect the definition of
I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}
. We apply Cauchy’s integral theorem to account for possible poles lying either inside
\Gamma
or on
\Gamma
, and obtain

\bigg|\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)}r^{d-1}dr\bigg|\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{1}^{2})^{s/2}r_{1}^{d-1}}{|r_{1}-r_{2}|}\lesssim(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}r_{k,\gamma}^{d-2},

where we used the bound
(1+r_{1}^{2})^{s/2}r_{1}^{d-1}\lesssim(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s+d-1}
,
the estimate (
3.5
), and the fact that

|r_{1}-r_{2}|=2(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}\gtrsim\big|(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}\big|\gtrsim r_{k,\gamma}.

Since the support of
\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})
is contained in
\Gamma_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{|m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)|}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{|m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)|}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg|\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{|m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)|}r^{d-1}dr\bigg|+\bigg|\int_{\Gamma}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\omega r)}r^{d-1}dr\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}r_{k,\gamma}^{d-2}.

Combining the cases
|\gamma_{\omega}|>\epsilon_{0}/4
and
|\gamma_{\omega}|\leq\epsilon_{0}/4
, we obtain

\displaystyle|I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{r_{k,\gamma}}|\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|r_{k,\gamma}^{d-1}d\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{\eta}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{\eta}},\quad\eta>1/2,

where we have applied Lemma
2.6
. By Plancherel’s identity, this estimate can be rewritten as

|I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)|\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})},

which completes the proof.
∎

We have established the result for the case
\tau>0
. The case
\tau<0
can be treated in an analogous manner. The only difference is that, instead of the sets
A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
and
\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
, we consider the sets
A_{\gamma,\tau}^{-}
and
\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{-}
, defined by

A_{\gamma,\tau}^{-}:=\bigl\{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:-\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)>\gamma\cdot\omega\geq-\epsilon_{0}/4\bigr\},\quad\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{-}:=\bigl\{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)=-\gamma\cdot\omega\bigr\}.

Lemma 3.4
.

The integral
I^{(3,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)
is well-defined. Moreover, for any
f,g\in\mathcal{S}
and
\eta>1/2
, the following estimate holds:

\big|I^{(3,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)\big|\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}.

Proof.

We begin by observing that

\displaystyle I^{(3,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0}}^{r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{(1+r^{2})^{s/2}|\hat{f}(\omega r)\hat{g}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|}{r_{k,\gamma}|r-r_{k,\gamma}|}r^{d-1}d\omega dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{r_{k,\gamma}}\int_{r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0}}^{r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{|\hat{f}(\omega r)\hat{g}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|}{|r-r_{k,\gamma}|}r^{d-1}d\omega dr.

Applying Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{|\hat{f}(\omega r)\hat{g}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|}{|r-r_{k,\gamma}|}r^{d-1}d\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|r-r_{k,\gamma}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{g}(\omega r)|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{|r-r_{k,\gamma}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{g}(\omega r)-\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega:=J_{1}+J_{2}.

(3.8)

Since
r\in[r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0},r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}]
and we assume without loss of generality that
r\in[r_{k,\gamma},r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}]
. Using Hölder’s inequality again yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Big|\int_{r}^{r_{k,\gamma}}\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\hat{f}(\epsilon\omega)d\epsilon\Big|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|r-r_{k,\gamma}|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{r_{k,\gamma}}^{r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}}\Big|\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\hat{f}(\epsilon\omega)\Big|^{2}d\epsilon\,r^{d-1}d\omega.

By Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
2.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega\lesssim

\displaystyle|r-r_{k,\gamma}|\int_{r_{k,\gamma}}^{r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sum_{|\alpha|=1}|D^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\epsilon\omega)|^{2}d\omega\,r^{d-1}d\epsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|r-r_{k,\gamma}|r^{d-1}\int_{r_{k,\gamma}}^{r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}}\epsilon^{-2}\|(\hat{f})_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}^{2}d\epsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|r-r_{k,\gamma}|r^{d-1}\int_{r_{k,\gamma}}^{r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}}\epsilon^{-2}\|(\hat{f})_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{3/2}}^{2}d\epsilon.

By Lemma
2.6
, there holds

\|(\hat{f})_{\epsilon}\|_{H^{3/2}}^{2}\lesssim\epsilon^{-d}\max\{\epsilon,1\}^{3}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{3/2}}^{2}.

Since
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,r_{k,\gamma}/4)
and
r\in[r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0},r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}]
, there holds
\epsilon^{-d+1}r^{d-1}\lesssim_{d}1
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim|r-r_{k,\gamma}|\int_{r_{k,\gamma}}^{r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}}\epsilon^{-3}\max\{\epsilon,1\}^{3}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{3/2}}^{2}d\epsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon_{0}^{-2}|r-r_{k,\gamma}|\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{3/2}}^{2}.

On the other hand, we have the elementary estimate

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r)|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega+r^{d-1}r_{k,\gamma}^{-d+1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r_{k,\gamma}^{d-1}d\omega.

Since
r\in[r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0},r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}]
and
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,r_{k,\gamma}/4)
, it follows that
r^{d-1}r_{k,\gamma}^{d-1}\lesssim_{d}1
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Similar to the previous proof, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega\lesssim\epsilon_{0}^{-1}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}^{2}.

By interpolation, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r)-\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{2}r^{d-1}d\omega\lesssim\epsilon_{0}^{-1-\epsilon}|r-r_{k,\gamma}|^{\epsilon}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}^{2},

(3.9)

for some possibly another
\epsilon>0
. Substituting (
3.9
) in (
3
) yields

J_{1}\lesssim\frac{\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}}{\epsilon_{0}^{1+\epsilon}|r-r_{k,\gamma}|^{1-\epsilon}},\quad\epsilon>0,

and the same estimate holds for
J_{2}
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle I^{(3,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau}(f,g)\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{r_{k,\gamma}}\int_{r_{k,\gamma}-\epsilon_{0}}^{r_{k,\gamma}+\epsilon_{0}}\frac{\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}}{\epsilon_{0}^{1+\epsilon}|r-r_{k,\gamma}|^{1-\epsilon}}dr\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}.

Finally, applying Plancherel’s identity yields the desired estimate, which completes the proof.
∎

Therefore, the regularized Faddeev-type operator is well-defined. For notational convenience, we henceforth write
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}:=\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\,\cdot\,;\psi,\epsilon_{0}).
We are now in a position to state the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in[0,2]
, and
\eta>1/2
. Assume that
\epsilon_{0}\in\bigl(0,\min\{r_{k,\gamma}/4,1\}\bigr)
and

\tau\in(-\epsilon_{0}^{2},0)\cup(0,\epsilon_{0}^{2})
. Then the operator
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\,\cdot\,;\psi,\epsilon_{0})

extends to a bounded linear operator from
H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})
into
H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})
, and the following estimate holds:

\|\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}f\|_{H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})},

where the implicit constant is independent of
\tau
,
\eta
,
\epsilon_{0}
, and
r_{k,\gamma}
. Moreover, for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the operator
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
is a two-sided inverse of

-(\Delta+k^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla+{\rm i}\tau)
in the sense of distributions, i.e.,

-(\Delta+k^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla+{\rm i}\tau)\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}f=f=-\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}(\Delta+k^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla+{\rm i}\tau)f.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.2
–
3.4
, we have

\big\langle(I-\Delta)^{s/2}\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}f,g\big\rangle\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}.

By duality, this yields

\|(I-\Delta)^{s/2}\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}.

Since
(I-\Delta)^{r/2}
commutes with
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
, applying Lemma
2.5
yields the first statement of the theorem.

For the second statement, let
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By integration by parts, Plancherel’s identity, and the representation (
3.1
), we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Delta+k^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla+{\rm i}\tau)\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}f\overline{g}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\overline{g}dx.

That
\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)
is the only set where a pole of the multiplier
m_{k,\gamma,\tau}
lies on
(0,\infty)
which is of measure
0
on the unit sphere plays an important role in the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
so that identity above holds.
Moreover, since
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
commutes with
\Delta+k^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla+{\rm i}\tau
, the identity holds with the operators acting in the reverse order as well. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The Faddeev-type operator

This section is devoted to providing a precise definition of the Faddeev-type operators
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}
via the regularization procedure introduced in Section
3
, together with the corresponding wavenumber explicit estimates.
We begin by defining the operators
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in[0,2]
, and
\eta\in(1/2,\infty)
. We define the operator
\mathcal{H}^{+}_{k,\gamma}
as the strong limit of
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
as
\tau\to 0^{+}
in
\mathcal{L}\big(H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta}),\,H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})\big)
. Similarly, we define
\mathcal{H}^{-}_{k,\gamma}
as the strong limit of
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
as
\tau\to 0^{-}
in the same operator topology.

Remark 4.2
.

We refer to the operator
\mathcal{H}^{+}_{k}:=\mathcal{H}^{+}_{k,0}
as the inverse scattering operator corresponding to outgoing waves, and to
\mathcal{H}^{-}_{k}:=\mathcal{H}^{-}_{k,0}
as the inverse scattering operator corresponding to incoming waves. A direct consequence of Theorem
4.3
is that
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k}=-(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}
,
where the inverse is understood in the sense of the limiting absorption principle or, equivalently, via meromorphic continuation.

Weak convergence of the operators
\mathcal{H}_{k,0,\tau}
as
\tau\to 0^{+}
was established in
[
14
]
. In the present work, we not only consider a substantially broader class of Faddeev-type operators, but also prove strong convergence of
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}^{\pm}
, as stated in the theorem below. In addition, our analysis yields wavenumber explicit estimates that are valid not only in the high frequency regime considered in
[
14
]
, but also for general wavenumbers.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta>1/2
, and
s\in[0,2]
. Then the operators
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}
extend to bounded linear operators from
H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})
into
H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})
, and satisfy the estimate

\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{-2\eta}(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\min\{r_{k,\gamma},1\}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)},

(4.1)

where the implicit constant is independent of
\lambda
,
\eta
and
r_{k,\gamma}
.

Proof.

We only consider the case
\tau>0
, since the case
\tau<0
can be treated analogously. Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})
and let
0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\epsilon_{0}
.
Observe that

\max\bigl\{|2\gamma\cdot\xi-\tau_{1}|,\;|2\gamma\cdot\xi-\tau_{2}|\bigr\}\geq\frac{|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|}{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}(\xi)}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}(\xi)}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|}{|\xi^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi-{\rm i}\tau_{1}|\,|\xi^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi-{\rm i}\tau_{2}|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|}{|\xi^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|\,\bigl((\xi^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2})^{2}+(2\gamma\cdot\xi-\tau_{j})^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|}{|\xi^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|\,\bigl((\xi^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2})^{2}+(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}},

where
j\in\{1,2\}
and the implicit constant is independent of
\xi
and
\tau_{j}
. Applying Young’s inequality for products, we further obtain

\left|\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}(\xi)}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}(\xi)}\right|\lesssim\frac{|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|^{\epsilon}}{|\xi^{2}-r_{k,\gamma}^{2}|^{1+\epsilon}},\quad\forall\,\epsilon\in(0,1).

(4.2)

Proceeding in exactly the same manner as in the proofs of Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
, we obtain

\big|I^{(i,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{1}}(f,g)-I^{(i,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{2}}(f,g)\big|\lesssim|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|^{\epsilon}\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}r_{k,\gamma}}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+2\epsilon}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{1/2+2\epsilon}},

(4.3)

for
i=1,3
. Here, a slightly higher regularity assumption on
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
is required on the right-hand side.
It therefore remains to estimate the difference
\big|I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{1}}(f,g)-I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{2}}(f,g)\big|.

Let
A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
and
\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
be the sets introduced in the proof of Lemma
3.3
. As in that proof, we adopt polar coordinates in (
3.3
) and write

\displaystyle\big|I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{1}}(f,g)-I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{2}}(f,g)\big|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}(\omega r)}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}(\omega r)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr,\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})d\omega\bigg|

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+,c}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}(\omega r)}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}(\omega r)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr{\hat{f}}(\omega r_{k,\gamma}){\hat{g}}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})d\omega\bigg|,

where
\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+,c}
denotes the complement of
\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Since
\partial^{\rm in}A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}
has surface measure zero, the first term vanishes. Therefore, it suffices to estimate the second term.

Let
\Gamma_{0}
,
\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}
, and
\Gamma
be the contours defined in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, and define
B_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}:=\{\omega:\ -\epsilon_{0}/4<\gamma_{\omega}<\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle|I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{1}}(f,g)-I^{(2,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{2}}(f,g)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\left|\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}}\right|(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr\ |\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|d\omega

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}}\right)(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})d\omega\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\gamma_{\omega|\geq\epsilon_{0}/4}\}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\left|\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}}\right|(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr|\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|d\omega

\displaystyle:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.

We first estimate the term
J_{1}
. As shown in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, there are no poles inside the contour
\Gamma
. Applying Cauchy’s theorem, we obtain

J_{1}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}}\left|\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}}\right|(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})d\omega.

Using (
3
), (
4.2
), and Lemma
2.6
, we deduce

J_{1}\lesssim|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|^{\epsilon}\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}^{1+\epsilon}r_{k,\gamma}^{1+\epsilon}}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}},\quad\forall\,\epsilon\in(0,1).

(4.4)

We next estimate the term
J_{3}
. By (
3.4
) and Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\frac{|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\gamma_{\omega|\geq\epsilon_{0}/4}\}}(\omega)}{|m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}|}(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr|\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|d\omega

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}^{2}r_{k,\gamma}^{2}}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}},\quad\forall\,\epsilon\in(0,1).

We now estimate the term
J_{2}
. In this case, there exists a pole inside the contour
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}\cup\Gamma_{0}
. We decompose
J_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma_{\epsilon_{0}}}\left(\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}}\right)(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})d\omega\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}}-\frac{1}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}}\right)(1+r^{2})^{s/2}\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})r^{d-1}dr\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})d\omega\right|

\displaystyle:=J_{2,1}+J_{2,2}.

Similar to the estimate of
J_{1}
, it follows that

J_{2,1}\lesssim|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|^{\epsilon}\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\epsilon_{0}^{1+\epsilon}r_{k,\gamma}^{1+\epsilon}}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}},\quad\forall\,\epsilon\in(0,1).

(4.6)

To estimate
J_{2,2}
, we have

J_{2,2}\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\big(F(\omega;\tau_{1})-F(\omega;\tau_{2})\big)\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})d\omega\right|,

where

F(\omega;\tau):=\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}}(\omega)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\psi(r;r_{k,\gamma},\epsilon_{0})}{m_{k,\gamma,\tau}(r,\omega)}(1+r^{2})^{s/2}r^{d-1}dr.

By Cauchy’s integral theorem and recalling (
3.7
), we obtain

F(\omega;\tau)=-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\gamma,\tau}^{+}}(\omega)\pi{\rm i}}{(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}}(1+r^{2})^{s/2}r^{d-1},\quad r:=-{\rm i}\gamma_{\omega}+(r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-\gamma_{\omega}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)^{1/2}.

For any fixed
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we recall from the proof of Lemma
3.3
that the possible poles are given by

\displaystyle r=R_{\gamma,\tau}(\gamma_{\omega}),\quad R_{\gamma,\tau}(x):=-{\rm i}x+\sqrt{r_{k,\gamma}^{2}-x^{2}+{\rm i}\tau},\quad x\in(\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau),\epsilon_{0}/4).

It is straightforward to verify that
R_{\gamma,\tau}
admits an explicit inverse, i.e.,

x=R^{-1}_{\gamma,\tau}(r):=\frac{r_{k,\gamma}^{2}+{\rm i}\tau-r^{2}}{2{\rm i}r},

where
r
lies inside the contour
\Gamma
. Hence, the mapping
x\mapsto R_{\gamma,\tau}(x)
is a diffeomorphism onto its image.
As a result,
\Theta_{\gamma}(\tau)
depends continuously on
\tau
, and
F(\omega;\tau)
converges pointwise to
F(\omega;\tau_{0})
as
\tau\to\tau_{0}
in
(0,\epsilon_{0}^{2})
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
be fixed. By Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{2,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|F(\omega;\tau_{1})-F(\omega;\tau_{2})|^{(1+\epsilon)/\epsilon}d\omega\right)^{\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\hat{f}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})\hat{g}(\omega r_{k,\gamma})|^{(1+\epsilon)}d\omega\right)^{1/(1+\epsilon)}

\displaystyle=\|F(\omega;\tau_{1})-F(\omega;\tau_{2})\|_{L^{(1+\epsilon)/\epsilon}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\|(\hat{f})_{r_{k,\gamma}}\|_{L^{2(1+\epsilon)}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\|(\hat{g})_{r_{k,\gamma}}\|_{L^{2(1+\epsilon)}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}.

By Sobolev embedding on the sphere and Lemmas
2.6
, we have

\|(\hat{f})_{r_{k,\gamma}}\|_{L^{2(1+\theta)}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\lesssim\|(\hat{f})_{r_{k,\gamma}}\|_{H^{\frac{\epsilon d}{2(1+\epsilon)}}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\lesssim_{k,\gamma}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{\frac{\epsilon d}{2(1+\epsilon)}+\frac{1}{2}}},

and the same estimate holds for
\hat{g}
. Hence, we arrive at

J_{2,2}\lesssim_{k,\gamma,\epsilon}\|F(\omega;\tau_{1})-F(\omega;\tau_{2})\|_{L^{\frac{(1+\epsilon)}{\epsilon}}}\|\hat{f}\|_{H^{\frac{\epsilon d}{2(1+\epsilon)}+\frac{1}{2}}}\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{\frac{\epsilon d}{2(1+\epsilon)}+\frac{1}{2}}}.

(4.7)

By the dominated convergence theorem, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|F(\omega;\tau_{1})-F(\omega;\tau_{2})\|_{L^{(1+\epsilon)/\epsilon}}<\epsilon,\quad\forall\,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in(0,\delta).

Combining (
4.3
)–(
4.7
), we conclude that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists

\delta>0
such that, for all
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in(0,\delta)
,

\displaystyle|\langle{\mathcal{H}}_{k,\gamma,\tau_{1}}f-{\mathcal{H}}_{k,\gamma,\tau_{2}}f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{3}|I^{(j,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{1}}(f,g)-I^{(j,s)}_{k,\gamma,\epsilon_{0},\tau_{2}}(f,g)|

\displaystyle\leq\ \varepsilon\|{f}\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}\|{g}\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})},\quad\forall\,\eta>1/2.

This shows that
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
converges strongly as
\tau\to 0
, and we have proved

\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\frac{(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\min\{r_{k,\gamma},1\}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}.

By Lemma
2.2
, there holds

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\eta}\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{H^{r+s}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{-\eta}(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\min\{r_{k,\gamma},1\}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta})}\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{-2\eta}(1+r_{k,\gamma})^{s}}{\min\{r_{k,\gamma},1\}r_{k,\gamma}}\|f\|_{H^{r}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)},

which implies (
4.1
).
∎

Only one-sided limits of
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
as
\tau\to 0
are considered, since a change in the sign of
\tau
may introduce new poles inside the contour
\Gamma
. As a result, the limiting operator depends on whether
\tau
approaches zero from above or below. To illustrate this phenomenon, we consider the special case
\gamma=0
. It is straightforward to verify that no poles lie inside the contour
\Gamma
when
\tau>0
. However, when
\tau<0
, a pole may enter the contour, and the convergence of
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma,\tau}
fails due to the contribution of the associated residue.

Theorem
4.3
establishes an
L^{2}
-based estimate for the operators
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}
. We now turn to
L^{p}
-based estimates in the framework of rescaled weighted Besov spaces. We begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
p_{0}\in[1,2]
, and assume that

\eta>d/p_{0}-(d-1)/2
. Then the operators
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}
satisfy the estimate

\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\max\bigl\{r_{k,\gamma}^{-2},\,r_{k,\gamma}^{-1}\bigr\}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{1/2+\epsilon})}.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\eta-d(1/p_{0}-1/2)-1/2)
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\langle x\rangle^{-1/2-\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|\langle x\rangle^{-\eta+1/2+\epsilon}\|_{L^{2p_{0}/(2-p_{0})}}.

Applying Theorem
4.3
with
r=s=0
yields

\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{-1/2-\varepsilon})}\lesssim\max\{r_{k,\gamma}^{-2},r_{k,\gamma}^{-1}\}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\langle x\rangle^{1/2+\varepsilon})}.

Moreover, the weighted norm
\|\langle x\rangle^{-\eta+1/2+\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2p_{0}/(2-p_{0})}}
is finite provided that
{2p_{0}}(\eta-1/2-\epsilon)/{(2-p_{0})}>d
, which is ensured by the condition
\eta>d/p_{0}-(d-1)/2
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in[0,d/2)
. Let
p_{1},p_{2},q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
1/p_{2}-1/p_{1}=\alpha/d.
Then the operator
\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma}^{\pm}
extends to a bounded operator from
B^{r-2+\alpha}_{p_{2},q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)
to
B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)
. Moreover, for any
\eta\in\bigl((d+1)/2-\alpha,\infty\bigr)
, the following estimate holds:

\|\mathcal{H}_{k,\gamma}^{\pm}f\|_{B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim_{\eta,p_{1},p_{2},q,r}\lambda^{-\eta}\max\{r_{k,\gamma}^{\eta},r_{k,\gamma}^{-2}\}\|f\|_{B^{r-2+\alpha}_{p_{2},q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)},

where the implicit constant is independent of
\lambda
,
k
, and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\psi^{(-1)}
be a smooth function such that
\operatorname{supp}\psi^{(-1)}\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq 3/2\}
and
\psi^{(-1)}\equiv 1
on
\operatorname{supp}\chi
. Let
\tilde{\varphi}
be a smooth function satisfying
\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\varphi}\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 3\}
and
\tilde{\varphi}\equiv 1
on
\operatorname{supp}\varphi
. Define
\psi^{(n)}:=\tilde{\varphi}
for all
n\geq 0
, and introduce the dyadically rescaled cut-off functions

\psi_{-1}^{(-1)}(\xi):=\psi^{(-1)}(\xi),\quad\psi_{n}^{(n)}(\xi):=\psi^{(n)}(2^{-n}\xi),\quad n\geq 0.

For
n\geq 0
, set
c_{n,k,\gamma}:=2^{-n}r_{k,\gamma},\gamma_{n}:=2^{-(n-1)}\gamma
, and define
c_{-1,k,\gamma}:=r_{k,\gamma},\gamma_{-1}:=\gamma
. Finally, for
n\geq-1
, define the multipliers
\Psi_{n,k,\gamma}
by

\Psi_{n,k,\gamma}(\xi):=\frac{\psi^{(n)}(\xi)}{|\xi|^{2}-c_{n,k,\gamma}^{2}+{\rm i}\gamma_{n}\cdot\xi}.

Let
N_{0}>2
be a fixed integer. Then there exists at most one integer
n_{0}\geq 0
such that
c_{n_{0}-1,k,\gamma}^{2}\geq 16/3
and
c_{n_{0},k,\gamma}^{2}<16/3
. Moreover, for this
n_{0}
, we have

c_{n_{0}+N_{0},k,\gamma}^{2}=2^{-2N_{0}}c_{n_{0},k,\gamma}^{2}\leq 2^{-4}c_{n_{0},k,\gamma}^{2}<\frac{1}{3}<\frac{3}{8}.

Hence,
c_{n,k,\gamma}^{2}
lies outside the interval
[3/8,16/3]
for all
n>n_{0}+N_{0}
and
0\leq n<n_{0}
. Note that the index
n_{0}
depends on
(k,\gamma)
, whereas
N_{0}
is independent of both
k
and
\gamma
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{S}
, we set
g_{k,\gamma}:=\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f
. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}^{q}=\sum_{n\in[0,n_{0})}+\sum_{n\in(n_{0}+N_{0},\infty)}2^{nrq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma,s})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n\in[n_{0},n_{0}+N_{0}]}2^{nrq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma,s})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}+2^{-rq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{-1}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma,s})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q},

where we set
n_{0}=\infty
if such an index does not exist. Moreover, for any
n\geq-1
, we have the convolution representation

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{\psi^{(n)}_{n}}{|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi}\right)\ast\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{f}).

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{p_{1}}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{\psi^{(n)}_{n}}{|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi}\right)\ast\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{p_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{\psi^{(n)}_{n}}{|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi}\right)(y)\Delta_{n}f(x-y)dy\right|^{p_{1}}\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta p_{1}}dx.

Let
1-1/p_{0}=1/p_{2}-1/p_{1}=\alpha/d
. By Young’s convolution inequality and (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{\psi^{(n)}_{n}}{|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}+2{\rm i}\gamma\cdot\xi}\right)(y)\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta}\Delta_{n}f(x-y)\langle\lambda(x-y)\rangle^{\eta}dy\right|^{p_{1}}dx\right)^{1/p_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2n}\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{\psi^{(n)}(2^{-n}\xi)}{(2^{-n}|\xi|)^{2}-(2^{-n}r_{k,\gamma})^{2}+2{\rm i}2^{-n}\gamma\cdot 2^{-n}\xi}\right)\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}.

Combining the above estimates yields

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim 2^{-2n}\|2^{nd}\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(2^{n}x)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}.

(4.8)

We apply Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\|2^{nd}\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(2^{n}x)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\|2^{nd}\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(2^{n}x)\|_{L^{p_{0}}}\|\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{n\alpha}\|\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}\|_{L^{p_{0}}},

where we used the scaling relation

\|2^{nd}f(2^{n}\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{0}}}=2^{n(d-d/p_{0})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}}=2^{n\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}},\quad\alpha=d(1-1/p_{0}).

Since
p_{0}\in[1,2]
, another application of Hölder’s inequality yields

\|\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}\|_{L^{p_{0}}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(x)|^{2}\langle x\rangle^{(d+1)(2-p_{0})/p_{0}}dx\right)^{p_{0}/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle x\rangle^{-d-1}dx\right)^{(2-p_{0})/2}.

Let
d_{0}
be the smallest integer such that
2d_{0}>(d+1)(2-p_{0})/p_{0}
. Then
\langle x\rangle^{(d+1)(2-p_{0})/p_{0}}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{2d_{0}}
, and by Plancherel’s identity,

\displaystyle\|\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}\|_{L^{p_{0}}}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(x)|^{2}\langle x\rangle^{2d_{0}}dx\right)^{p_{0}/2}\lesssim\left(\sum_{|\alpha_{0}|\leq d_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{\alpha_{0}}{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{p_{0}/2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\left\|2^{nd}\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(2^{n}x)\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim 2^{n\alpha}\left(\sum_{|\alpha_{0}|\leq d_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{\alpha_{0}}{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{p_{0}/2}.

(4.9)

For the cases
n\in[0,n_{0})
or
n\in(n_{0}+N_{0},\infty)
, we have either
c_{n,k,\gamma}^{2}\in(0,3/8)
or
c_{n,k,\gamma}^{2}\in(16/3,\infty)
. A direct computation shows that, on
\text{supp}\tilde{\varphi}
, the denominator of

D^{\alpha_{0}}\Psi_{n,k,\gamma}
is uniformly bounded from below by
1/8
. Hence, all derivatives
D^{\alpha_{0}}\Psi_{n,k,\gamma}
are uniformly bounded with respect to
n
,
k
, and
\gamma
in these regimes. Combining (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we get

2^{nrq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}\lesssim 2^{n(r-2+\alpha)q}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}^{q}.

Summing over
n\in[0,n_{0})
and
n\in(n_{0}+N_{0},\infty)
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in[0,n_{0})}+\sum_{n\in(n_{0}+N_{0},\infty)}2^{nrq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma,s})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{n\in[0,n_{0})}+\sum_{n\in(n_{0}+N_{0},\infty)}2^{n(r-2+\alpha)q}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}^{q}.

(4.10)

For the case
n\in[n_{0},n_{0}+N_{0}]
, we perform a change of variables in (
4.8
) to obtain

2^{nr}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim 2^{n(r-2+\alpha)}\left\|\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(x)\langle\lambda 2^{-n}x\rangle^{-\eta}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}.

For
n\in[n_{0},n_{0}+N_{0}]
, we have
c_{n,k,\gamma}^{2}\in[3/8,16/3]
, which implies

2^{n}\sim r_{k,\gamma}
. Hence,
\langle\lambda 2^{-n}x\rangle\gtrsim\min\{\lambda r_{k,\gamma}^{-1},1\}\langle x\rangle.
It follows that

2^{nr}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim 2^{n(r-2+\alpha)}\max\{\lambda^{-\eta}r_{k,\gamma}^{\eta},1\}\left\|\check{\Psi}_{n,k,\gamma}(x)\langle x\rangle^{-\eta}\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{0}+N_{0}}2^{krq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{-\eta q}r_{k,\gamma}^{\eta q},1\}\sup_{n\in[n_{0},n_{0}+N_{0}]}\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{2^{-n}k,2^{-n}\gamma}\psi(x)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{0}+N_{0}}2^{n(r-2+\alpha)q}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}^{q}.

(4.11)

For the low-frequency block
n=-1
, an argument analogous to that used in the proof of (
4.8
) yields

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{-1}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\left\|\check{\Psi}_{-1,k,\gamma}(x)\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\|\Delta_{-1}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}.

Since
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have the elementary bound
\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta}\lesssim\lambda^{-\eta}\langle x\rangle^{\eta}
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{-r}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{-1}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\eta}\left\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}\psi^{(-1)}(x)\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}2^{-(r-2+\alpha)}\|\Delta_{-1}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}.

(4.12)

Finally, combining (
4
)–(
4.12
) and factoring out the Besov norm of
f
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}^{q}=\sum_{n\in[0,n_{0})}+\sum_{n\in(n_{0}+N_{0},\infty)}2^{nrq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma,s})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n\in[n_{0},n_{0}+N_{0}]}2^{nrq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{n}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma,s})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}+2^{-rq}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{-1}\hat{g}_{k,\gamma,s})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\in[0,n_{0})}+\sum_{n\in(n_{0}+N_{0},\infty)}2^{n(r-2+\alpha)q}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}^{q}+\max\{\lambda^{-\eta q}r_{k,\gamma}^{\eta q},1\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sup_{n\in[n_{0},n_{0}+N_{0}]}\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{2^{-n}k,2^{-n}\gamma}\psi(x)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{0}+N_{0}}2^{n(r-2+\alpha)q}\|\Delta_{n}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda^{-\eta q}\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}\psi^{(-1)}(x)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}2^{-(r-2+\alpha)q}\|\Delta_{-1}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-\eta q}\Big(\max\{r_{k,\gamma}^{\eta q},\lambda^{\eta q}\}\sup_{n\in[n_{0},n_{0}+N_{0}]}\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{2^{-n}k,2^{-n}\gamma}\psi^{(n)}(x)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}\psi^{(-1)}(x)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}^{q}\Big)\|f\|_{B^{r-2}_{p_{2},q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}^{q}.

By Proposition
4.4
and the fact that
2^{-n}k+2^{-n}\gamma\sim 1
for
n\in[n_{0},n_{0}+N_{0}]
, we deduce

\|\mathcal{H}^{\pm}_{k,\gamma}f\|_{B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}^{q}\lesssim\lambda^{-\eta q}\max\{r_{k,\gamma}^{\eta q},\lambda^{\eta q},r_{k,\gamma}^{-q},r_{k,\gamma}^{-2q}\}\|f\|_{B^{r-2}_{p_{2},q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}^{q},

which completes the proof.
∎

5.
Well-posedness

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
be the rescaling parameter throughout this section. Substituting
x=\lambda x^{\prime}
in (
1.1
), we obtain

{\Delta}u(\lambda x^{\prime})+k^{2}u(\lambda x^{\prime})+V_{k}(\lambda x^{\prime})u(\lambda x^{\prime})=g(\lambda x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Define the rescaled function
(u)_{\lambda}(x^{\prime}):=u(\lambda x^{\prime})
. Since
\Delta(u)_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})=\lambda^{2}\Delta u(\lambda x^{\prime})
, we arrive at the rescaled Helmholtz equation

\Delta(u)_{\lambda}(x)+\lambda^{2}k^{2}(u)_{\lambda}(x)+\lambda^{2}(V_{k})_{\lambda}(x)(u)_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2}g(\lambda x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For convenience, we introduce the notation
V_{k,\lambda}:=(V_{k})_{\lambda}
,
g_{\lambda}:=\lambda^{2}g(\lambda x)
, and
u_{\lambda}:=(u)_{\lambda}
. Then
u_{\lambda}
satisfies the rescaled Helmholtz equation

\Delta u_{\lambda}+\lambda^{2}k^{2}u_{\lambda}+\lambda^{2}V_{k,\lambda}u_{\lambda}=g_{\lambda},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(5.1)

Under the same rescaling, the Sommerfeld radiation condition becomes

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{(d-1)/2}(\lambda^{-1}\partial_{|x|}u_{\lambda}-{\rm i}ku_{\lambda})=0,

which is equivalently written as

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{(d-1)/2}(\partial_{|x|}u_{\lambda}-{\rm i}k\lambda u_{\lambda})=0.

(5.2)

Thus, after rescaling, the Sommerfeld radiation condition retains its form, with the original wavenumber
k
replaced by the rescaled wavenumber
\lambda k
.

To give a rigorous meaning to the product
V_{k,\lambda}u
in (
5.1
), we employ Bony’s paraproduct decomposition.
For two distributions
f
and
g
, we define the paraproduct
f\prec g
and the resonant product
f\circ g
by

f\prec g:=\sum_{j\geq-1}S_{j-1}f\ \Delta_{j}g,\quad f\circ g:=\sum_{|i-j|\leq 1}\Delta_{i}f\ \Delta_{j}g.

These operators allow us to define products of distributions with limited regularity in a consistent manner.
The mapping properties of the paraproduct and resonant product in rescaled weighted Besov spaces are collected in Appendix
A
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
k>0
, and let
\phi_{R}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy
\phi_{R}\equiv 1
on
B(0,R)
.
For any
u\in\mathcal{S}
, we define the operators
\Xi_{k,\lambda}
and
\Phi_{R,\lambda}
, mapping
\mathcal{S}
into
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, by

\displaystyle\Xi_{k,\lambda}u

\displaystyle:=V_{k,\lambda}\prec u+V_{k,\lambda}\succ u+V_{k,\lambda}\circ u,

\displaystyle\Phi_{R,\lambda}u

\displaystyle:=(\phi_{R})_{\lambda}\prec u+(\phi_{R})_{\lambda}\succ u+(\phi_{R})_{\lambda}\circ u.

When
u
is sufficiently smooth, these operators coincide with the pointwise products,
\Xi_{k,\lambda}u=V_{k,\lambda}u
and
\Phi_{R,\lambda}u=(\phi_{R})_{\lambda}u
. Thus,
\Xi_{k,\lambda}
and
\Phi_{R,\lambda}
provide natural extensions of pointwise multiplication to distributions via Bony’s paraproduct decomposition.

Rather than studying the well-posedness of the Helmholtz equation with the Sommerfeld radiation condition directly, we first establish the well-posedness of the following rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation for sufficiently small
\lambda
:

u=\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}+\lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}u,

(5.3)

where the outgoing resolvent
\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}
is chosen (cf. Remark
4.2
) to ensure that the Sommerfeld radiation condition is satisfied. We then prove that (
5.3
) is equivalent to the rescaled Helmholtz equation (
5.1
) together with the rescaled Sommerfeld radiation condition (
5.2
); see Lemma
5.11
. Finally, rescaling back yields that
u:=(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda^{-1}}
is the unique solution to the original problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), where
u_{\lambda}
denotes the unique solution to (
5.3
).

5.1.
Functional preliminaries

We begin by extending the operators
\Xi_{k,\lambda}
and
\Phi_{R,\lambda}
to appropriate function spaces.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in(1,\infty)
. For any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon>0
, the operator
\Xi_{k,\lambda}
admits a bounded extension
\Xi_{k,\lambda}:B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)\to B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda),

and satisfies the estimate

\|\Xi_{k,\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}(B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda);B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda))}\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{r-2},1\}\|V_{k}\|_{B^{r-2+\epsilon}_{\infty,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta})}.

Proof.

By Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{k,\lambda}u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\prec u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}+\|V_{k,\lambda}\succ u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}+\|V_{k,\lambda}\circ u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}.

For the paraproduct
V_{k,\lambda}\prec u
, Lemma
2.3
and (
A.2
) yield

\|V_{k,\lambda}\prec u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\prec u\|_{B^{2r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{r-2}_{\infty,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

For the term
V_{k,\lambda}\succ u
, estimate (
A.1
) gives

\|V_{k,\lambda}\succ u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{r-2}_{\infty,q}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r}_{p,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, Lemma
2.3
implies

\|u\|_{B^{r}_{p,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|u\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)},\quad\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{r-2}_{\infty,q}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{r-2+\epsilon}_{\infty,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}.

Hence, we have

\|V_{k,\lambda}\succ u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{r-2+\epsilon}_{\infty,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

For the resonant term
V_{k,\lambda}\circ u
, we deduce from Lemma
2.3
that

\|V_{k,\lambda}\circ u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\circ u\|_{B^{2r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}.

It follows from (
A.3
) that

\|V_{k,\lambda}\circ u\|_{B^{r-2}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{r-2}_{\infty,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

Combining the above estimates and applying Lemma
2.6
to
\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{r-2}_{\infty,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}
and
\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{r-2+\epsilon}_{\infty,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in(0,1)
. For any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\zeta\in(0,2-2r)
, and
\epsilon>0
, the operator
\Xi_{k,\lambda}
can be extended as
\Xi_{k,\lambda}:B^{r}_{2p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)\to B^{r-2+\zeta}_{(2p)^{\prime},q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)
, and satisfies the estimate

\|\Xi_{k,\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}(B^{r}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda);B^{r-2+\zeta}_{(2p)^{\prime},(2p)^{\prime}}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda))}\lesssim\lambda^{-d/p^{\prime}}\max\{\lambda^{-r},1\}\|V_{k}\|_{B^{-r+\epsilon}_{p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta})}.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,r)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{S}
. By Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\psi u\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|\psi\prec u\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}+\|u\prec\psi\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}+\|u\circ\psi\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}.

Since
\zeta\in(0,2-2r)
, there holds
2-r-\zeta>0
. By (
A.1
) and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi\prec u\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{B^{2-r-\zeta}_{2p,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{2p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{B^{2-r-\zeta}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

Notice that
\zeta\in(0,2-2r)
implies that
2r-2+\zeta-\epsilon<0
. Similarly, by (
A.2
) and Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle\|u\prec\psi\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{B^{2r-2+\zeta-\epsilon}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\|\psi\|_{B^{2-r-\zeta}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{B^{r}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\|\psi\|_{B^{2-r-\zeta}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

We apply
\zeta\in(0,2-2r)
again and obtain that
r-\epsilon<2-\zeta
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.3
and (
A.3
),

\|u\circ\psi\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|u\circ\psi\|_{B^{2-\zeta}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|u\|_{B^{r}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\|\psi\|_{B^{2-r-\zeta}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

Combining the above estimates yields

\|\psi u\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{B^{2-r-\zeta}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

Applying
[
22
, Section 2.11.2]
or
[
5
, Proposition 2.76]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\psi,V_{k,\lambda}u\rangle=\langle\psi u,V_{k,\lambda}\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{-r+\epsilon}_{p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}\|\psi u\|_{B^{r-\epsilon}_{p,p}(\langle x\rangle^{-2\eta},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{-r+\epsilon}_{p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}\|\psi\|_{B^{2-r-\zeta}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

By duality, this implies

\|\Xi_{k,\lambda}u\|_{B^{r-2+\zeta}_{(2p)^{\prime},(2p)^{\prime}}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{-r+\epsilon}_{p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}\|u\|_{B^{r}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}.

Finally, applying Lemma
2.6
to
\|V_{k,\lambda}\|_{B^{-r+\epsilon}_{p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\langle x\rangle^{2\eta},\lambda)}
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

,

, and

. For any

admits a bounded extension

, and satisfies the estimate

We state the following important result in the case where the potential
V_{k}
has compact support.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
V_{k}
is supported in a ball
B(0,R)
. If
v
is a solution of
v-\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v=\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}
, then
u:=\lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v+\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}
is a solution of the rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation (
5.3
). Conversely, if
u
solves the rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation (
5.3
), then
v:=\Phi_{R,\lambda}u
satisfies
v-\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v=\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

We first observe that
V_{k,\lambda}
is supported in the ball
B(0,\lambda^{-1}R)
and that

\Xi_{k,\lambda}\Phi_{R,\lambda}=\Xi_{k,\lambda}.

We begin with the proof of the first statement. Note that

\Phi_{R,\lambda}u=\Phi_{R,\lambda}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v+\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v+\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}=v.

Therefore,

\displaystyle-\lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}u+\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}\Phi_{R,\lambda}u+\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v+\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}=u,

which shows that
u
solves the rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation (
5.3
).

We now prove the converse statement. Applying
\Phi_{R,\lambda}
to (
5.3
) yields

(\Phi_{R,\lambda}u)-\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}(\Phi_{R,\lambda}u)=\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g.

Hence,
v:=\Phi_{R,\lambda}u
satisfies

v-\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v=\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda},

which completes the proof.
∎

The following result establishes a connection between the outgoing inverse scattering operator and the Faddeev-type operator.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
\alpha\in[0,d/2)
, and let
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
be admissible weights. Let
p_{1},p_{2},q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
1/p_{2}-1/p_{1}=\alpha/d.
Then, for any
f\in B^{r-2+\alpha}_{p_{2},q}(\rho_{1},\lambda)
, there holds

\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f=e^{-\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}e^{\gamma\cdot x}f,

where both sides are understood as elements in
B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\rho_{2},\lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
g_{\tau}:=\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,0,\tau}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f
,
g:=\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f
, and define

g_{\gamma,\tau}(x):=e^{\gamma\cdot x}g_{\tau}(x)
. Note that the symbol
|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}\lambda^{2}-{\rm i}\tau
of the operator
-(\Delta+k^{2}\lambda^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)
has no singularities. Hence, its inverse Fourier transform defines a tempered distribution, and we have

(\Delta+k^{2}\lambda^{2}+{\rm i}\tau)g_{\tau}=\Phi_{R,\lambda}f,

which is understood in the sense of distributions. Substituting
g_{\tau}=e^{-\gamma\cdot x}g_{\gamma,\tau}
into
the above equation, a direct computation yields

(\Delta+k^{2}\lambda^{2}+|\gamma|^{2}-2\gamma\cdot\nabla+{\rm i}\tau)g_{\gamma,\tau}=e^{\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f.

Since
f\in B^{r-2}_{p_{2},q}(\rho_{1},\lambda)
, we apply Lemma
5.3
and obtain that

\|e^{\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f\|_{B^{r-2+\alpha}_{p_{2},q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\|e^{\lambda^{-1}\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R}\|_{B^{\zeta}_{\infty,\infty}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta}\rho_{1}^{-1})}\|f\|_{B^{r-2+\alpha}_{p_{2},q}(\rho_{1},\lambda)},

where
\zeta>2-r
. By Theorem
3.5
and Remark
4.6
, there holds
{g}_{\gamma,\tau}=\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma,\tau}e^{\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f
and

\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\|{g}_{\gamma,\tau}-\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}(e^{\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f)\|_{B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}=0,\quad\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\|g_{\tau}-g\|_{B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}=0.

By applying Lemma
5.3
again, we obtain that

\displaystyle\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f

\displaystyle=\Phi_{R,\lambda}g=\Phi_{R,\lambda}\lim_{\tau\to 0}g_{\tau}=\Phi_{R,\lambda}\lim_{\tau\to 0}e^{-\gamma\cdot x}{g}_{\gamma,\tau}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\Phi_{R,\lambda}e^{-\gamma\cdot x}{g}_{\gamma,\tau}=\Phi_{R,\lambda}e^{-\gamma\cdot x}\lim_{\tau\to 0}{g}_{\gamma,\tau}=e^{-\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}(e^{\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}f),

where the limit is taken in
B^{r}_{p_{1},q}(\rho_{2},\lambda)
for any admissible
\rho_{2}
.
∎

5.2.
Well-posedness of rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation

By Lemma
5.4
, it suffices to study the equation

v-\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v=\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}.

Multiplying both sides of this equation by
e^{-\gamma\cdot x}
yields

e^{-\gamma\cdot x}v-\lambda^{2}\ e^{-\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}e^{\gamma\cdot x}\Xi_{k,\lambda}e^{-\gamma\cdot x}v=e^{-\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}.

Applying Lemma
5.5
, we obtain

e^{-\gamma\cdot x}v-\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\Xi_{k,\lambda}e^{-\gamma\cdot x}v=e^{-\gamma\cdot x}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}.

To estimate the right-hand side term, we establish the following proposition.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. For any compactly supported
g\in B^{r-2}_{p,q}
, the function
\mathcal{G}_{R,k,\lambda,\gamma}:=e^{-\gamma\cdot x}\,\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda}
belongs to
B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho,\lambda)
for any admissible
\rho

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}(g_{\lambda}))(\xi)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}-k^{2}\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}\big(g({\lambda}\cdot)\big)(\xi)=\frac{\lambda^{-d}}{(\lambda^{-1}|\xi|)^{2}-k^{2}}\hat{g}(\lambda^{-1}\xi).

Hence, we obtain the scaling relation

(\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}g_{\lambda})(x)=(\mathcal{H}_{k}^{+}g)(\lambda x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By Lemma
2.6

\|\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda}^{+}(g_{\lambda})\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}=\|\big(\mathcal{H}_{k}g\big)_{\lambda}\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\lambda^{-d/p}\max\{\lambda^{r},1\}\ \|\mathcal{H}_{k}^{+}g\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta})}.

Since both

are compactly supported, we may proceed as in the proof of Lemma

to obtain the desired result.
∎

Therefore, the problem is equivalent to studying the following fixed point equation in

v=\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\Xi_{k,\lambda}v+\mathcal{G}_{R,k,\lambda,\gamma}=:\mathcal{F}_{R,k,\lambda,\gamma}v\ ,

Proposition 5.7

We are now ready to establish the

Lemma 5.8
.

Let

\eta_{0}\in(1/2,1)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\Xi_{k,\lambda}f\|_{B^{r}_{2,2}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta_{0}},\lambda)}

By an interpolation argument, we can now establish the following

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\eta_{0}\in(1/2,1)

, and

B^{r}_{2p_{0},2p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta_{0}},\lambda)

\lambda

Proof.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy
|\gamma|\in(1,2)
. Assume that
\zeta_{0}>2-r
and
\eta
is chosen such that
\eta>(d+1)/2-d/p^{\prime}
. Applying Theorem
4.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\Xi_{k,\lambda}f\|_{B^{r}_{2p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta},\lambda)}\lesssim\lambda^{-\eta}\|\Phi_{R,\lambda}\Xi_{k,\lambda}f\|_{B^{r-2+d/p^{\prime}}_{(2p)^{\prime},q}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}.

5.3

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\Xi_{k,\lambda}f\|_{B^{r}_{2p,q}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta_{0}},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\Xi_{k,\lambda}f\|_{B^{r}_{2p,2p}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta_{0}},\lambda)}

2.7

2.8

\|\lambda^{2}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{k\lambda,\gamma}^{+}\Phi_{R,\lambda}\Xi_{k,\lambda}f\|_{B^{r}_{2p_{0},2p_{0}}(\langle x\rangle^{-\eta_{0}},\lambda)}\lesssim\lambda^{2-\eta\theta-2\eta_{0}(1-\theta)}\|\Xi_{k,\lambda}f\|_{B^{r-2+d/p_{0}^{\prime}}_{(2p_{0})^{\prime},(2p_{0})^{\prime}}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta_{0}},\lambda)},

We first show that the rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation is equivalent to the rescaled Helmholtz equation (

Lemma 5.11
.

Assume that the conditions of either Lemma
5.9
or Lemma
5.10
are satisfied. Then the rescaled Helmholtz equation (
5.1
), together with the rescaled Sommerfeld radiation condition (
5.2
), admits a unique solution in the same function space as the solution obtained in Lemma
5.9
or Lemma
5.10
for sufficiently small
\lambda

Proof.

Let
u_{\lambda}
be the unique solution to the rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation. By (
1.3
) and Remark
4.2
, the Green function

satisfies the rescaled Sommerfeld radiation condition

u_{\lambda}(x)=-\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)V_{k,\lambda}u_{\lambda}(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)g_{\lambda}(y)dy.

Moreover,
G_{k\lambda}(x-y)
is analytic outside the support of
V_{k,\lambda}
and
g_{\lambda}
. A direct calculation of
(\partial_{|x|}-{\rm i}k\lambda)G_{k\lambda}(x-y)
implies that
u_{\lambda}
also satisfies the rescaled Sommerfeld radiation. Moreover, a direct consequence of (
1.3
) is that
u_{\lambda}
satisfies the rescaled Helmholtz equation in the sense of distribution. Therefore, the solution of the rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation is a solution of the rescaled Helmholtz equation together with rescaled Sommerfeld radiation condition.

To prove uniqueness, let
u_{\lambda}
be a solution of the rescaled Helmholtz equation (
5.1
) subject to the rescaled Sommerfeld radiation condition (
5.2
) with
g=0
for
\lambda
sufficiently small. We show that
u_{\lambda}\equiv 0
.

By definition, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)(\Delta+k^{2}\lambda^{2})u_{\lambda}(y)dy=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)V_{k,\lambda}(y)u_{\lambda}(y)dy.

Since
V_{k,\lambda}
is supported in
B(0,\lambda^{-1}R)
, the right-hand side vanishes outside this ball. Consequently, for any
r>\lambda^{-1}R
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)(\Delta+k^{2}\lambda^{2})u_{\lambda}(y)dy=\int_{B(0,r)}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)(\Delta+k^{2}\lambda^{2})u_{\lambda}(y)dy.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
5.9
or Lemma
5.10
, we apply Green’s representation formula (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
)and obtain that

\displaystyle-u_{\lambda}(x)+\int_{\partial B(0,r)}[G_{k\lambda}(x-y)\partial_{\nu}u_{\lambda}(y)-\partial_{\nu}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)u_{\lambda}(y)]dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,r)}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)(\Delta+k^{2}\lambda^{2})u_{\lambda}(y)dy,

in the sense of distribution, where
\nu
denotes the outward unit normal on

u_{\lambda}
and the fundamental solution

satisfy the rescaled Sommerfeld radiation condition (
5.2
), the boundary integral vanishes as

, i.e.,

\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}[G_{k\lambda}(x-y)\partial_{\nu}u_{\lambda}(y)-\partial_{\nu}G_{k\lambda}(x-y)u_{\lambda}(y)]dy=0.

Letting

therefore yields that
u_{\lambda}
satisfies the rescaled Lippmann–Schwinger equation (
5.3
) with

. By Lemma
5.9
or Lemma
5.10

∎

As stated at the beginning of this section, it remains to show that
u:=(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda^{-1}}

)–(

Let

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we established the well-posedness of the Helmholtz equation with rough coefficients. The results are sharp in the sense that they attain the minimal regularity threshold required to define the product of between the coefficient and the solution without using renormalization. In addition, we derived general wavenumber explicit estimates in an
L^{p}
-based framework for a broad class of operators associated with the Helmholtz equation.

The present analysis is carried out under the assumption that the coefficients have compact support. While this setting already covers many physically relevant models and allows for a precise control of the resolvent behavior, extending the theory to coefficients without compact support remains an important and challenging open problem. In particular, understanding how long range or slowly decaying rough coefficients affect well-posedness and wavenumber dependence will be the subject of future investigation.

Appendix A A
Bony’s paraproduct

In this section, we present paraproduct estimates in rescaled weighted Besov spaces.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
. For any
f\in\mathcal{S}
and any weight
\rho\in W(\eta)
, the following estimate holds:

\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim_{\eta}\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)},

where the implicit constant depends only on
\rho
and is independent of
\lambda
and
j
.

Proof.

Since
\rho\in W(\eta)
, we have
\rho(\lambda x)\lesssim\langle\lambda(x-y)\rangle^{\eta}\rho(\lambda y)
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore,

\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{j})(x-y)\langle\lambda(x-y)\rangle^{\eta}f(y)\rho(\lambda y)dy\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Applying Young’s convolution inequality yields

\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{j})\|_{L^{1}(\langle x\rangle^{\eta},\lambda)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}.

Recalling that
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{j})=2^{jd}\check{\varphi}(2^{j}\cdot)
, we compute

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{j})\|_{L^{1}(\rho,\lambda)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2^{jd}\ |\check{\varphi}(2^{j}x)|\langle\lambda x\rangle^{\eta}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\check{\varphi}(x)|\langle 2^{-j}\lambda x\rangle^{\eta}dx.

Since
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
, we have
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}
, and using
j\geq-1
, it follows that

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{j})\|_{L^{1}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\check{\varphi}(x)|\langle 2^{-j}x\rangle^{\eta}dx\lesssim_{\eta}\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}.

Combining the above estimates completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be an annulus in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of smooth functions such that
{\rm supp}\,\hat{u}_{j}\subseteq 2^{j}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Then, for any weight
\rho\in W(\eta)
, the following estimate holds:

\|u\|_{B^{r}_{p,q}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jrq}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}^{q}\bigg)^{1/q},\quad u:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}u_{j},

where the implicit constant is independent of
\lambda
.

Proof.

The argument follows the same lines as in
[
5
, Lemma 2.69]
. By the spectral localization assumption, there exists an integer
N_{0}\geq 1
, independent of
\lambda
, such that
\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u_{j}=0
for
|j^{\prime}-j|>N_{0}
. By Minkowski’s inequality, we have

2^{jr}\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|<N_{0}}2^{(j-j^{\prime})r}2^{j^{\prime}r}\|\Delta_{j}u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}.

Applying Lemma
A.1
yields

2^{jr}\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|<N_{0}}2^{j^{\prime}r}\|u_{j^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(\rho,\lambda)}.

Taking the
\ell^{q}
norm in
j
and using Young’s convolution inequality for sequences completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

Lemma A.4
.

Let

,

satisfy

be admissible weights.

(1)

If
\alpha>0
, then

\|f\prec g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,\eta}\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\|g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p_{2},q}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}.

(A.1)

(2)

If
\alpha<0
, then

\|f\prec g\|_{B^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,\eta}\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\|g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p_{2},q_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}.

(A.2)

(3)

If
\alpha+\beta>0
, then

\|f\circ g\|_{B^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,\eta}\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\|g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p_{2},q_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}.

(A.3)

In all cases, the implicit constants are independent of the rescaling parameter
\lambda
.

Proof.

To prove (
A.1
), we first note that for each
j\geq-1
, the product
S_{j-1}f\Delta_{j}g
has Fourier support contained in
2^{j}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is an annulus slightly larger than
\mathcal{C}
. By Lemma
A.2
and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\prec g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j\beta q}\|S_{j-1}f\Delta_{j}g\|_{L^{p}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\sup_{j\geq-1}\|S_{j-1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j\beta q}\|\Delta_{j}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}.

Assume
\alpha>0
. Using Minkowski’s inequality and the definition of
S_{j-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|S_{j-1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\leq\sum_{k=-1}^{j-2}\|\Delta_{k-1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}

Multiplying and dividing by
2^{k\alpha}
and summing the resulting geometric series yield

\displaystyle\|S_{j-1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\leq\sum_{k=-1}^{j-2}2^{-k\alpha}\sup_{k\geq-1}2^{k\alpha}\|\Delta_{k-1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}^{q_{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}.

Combining the above estimates, we conclude that

\|f\prec g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\|g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p_{2},q}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}.

To prove the (
A.2
), applying Lemma
A.2
and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\prec g\|_{B^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j(\alpha+\beta)q}\|S_{j-1}f\Delta_{j}g\|_{L^{p}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j\alpha q_{1}}\|S_{j-1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}^{q_{1}}\bigg)^{1/q_{1}}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j\beta q_{2}}\|\Delta_{j}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}^{q_{2}}\bigg)^{1/{q_{2}}}.

Next, using Minkowski’s inequality and the definition of
S_{j-1}
, we estimate

\displaystyle 2^{j\alpha}\|S_{j-1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-1}^{j-2}2^{(j-k)\alpha}2^{k\alpha}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq-1}2^{(j-k)\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{\{j-k\geq 2\}}\ 2^{k\alpha}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}.

Since
\alpha<0
, the sequence
\{2^{\ell\alpha}\}_{\ell\geq 2}
belongs to
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})
. Therefore, it follows from Young’s convolution inequality for sequences that we get

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j\alpha q_{1}}\|S_{j-1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}^{q_{1}}\bigg)^{1/q_{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}.

Combining the above estimates yields

\|f\prec g\|_{B^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\|g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p_{2},q_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}.

To prove (
A.3
), we first observe that the resonant product can be written as

f\circ g=\sum_{i\geq-1}\Delta_{i}f\Delta_{i-1}g+\Delta_{i}f\Delta_{i}g+\Delta_{i}f\Delta_{i+1}g=:\sum_{i\geq-1}A_{i}(f,g).

We also note that for each
i\geq-1
, the term
A_{i}(f,g)
has Fourier support contained in
2^{i}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is an annulus slightly larger than
\mathcal{B}
. By Lemma
A.3
, we have, for
\alpha+\beta>0
,

\displaystyle\|f\circ g\|_{B^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j(\alpha+\beta)q}\|A_{j}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}.

Using Minkowski’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we estimate

\displaystyle 2^{j(\alpha+\beta)q}\|A_{j}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j(\alpha+\beta)q}\bigg(\sum_{|\nu|\leq 1}\|\Delta_{j}f\cdot\Delta_{j-\nu}g\|_{L^{p}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}\bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{|\nu|\leq 1}2^{j\alpha}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}2^{(j-\nu)\beta}\|\Delta_{j-\nu}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}\bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}\sum_{|\nu|\leq 1}\bigg(2^{j\alpha}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}2^{(j-\nu)\beta}\|\Delta_{j-\nu}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}\bigg)^{q}.

Summing over
j\geq-1
and applying Hölder’s inequality for sequences, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\circ g\|_{B^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\sum_{|\nu|\leq 1}\Bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}\bigg(2^{j\alpha}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}2^{(j-\nu)\beta}\|\Delta_{j-\nu}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}\bigg)^{q}\Bigg)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\sum_{|\nu|\leq 1}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j\alpha q_{1}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}^{q_{1}}\bigg)^{1/q_{1}}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{(j-\nu)\beta q_{2}}\|\Delta_{j-\nu}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)}^{q_{2}}\bigg)^{1/q_{2}}.

Since shifts by
\nu\in\{-1,0,1\}
do not affect the Besov norms, we conclude

\|f\circ g\|_{B^{\alpha+\beta}_{p,q}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2},\lambda)}\lesssim\max\{\lambda^{\eta},1\}\|f\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\rho_{1},\lambda)}\|g\|_{B^{\beta}_{p_{2},q_{2}}(\rho_{2},\lambda)},

which completes the proof.
∎
