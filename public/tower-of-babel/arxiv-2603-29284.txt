On Eisenstein series identities and new identities connecting Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction and Ramanujan’s continued fraction of order four
Shruthi C. Bhat, B. R. Srivatsa Kumar

Abstract
. By employing the classical tools from the theory of q q -series and theta functions, new fascinating identities on different continued fractions can be achieved. In this article, we use the product expansion of Jacobi’s theta function to establish identities that connect Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction with Ramanujan’s continued fraction of order four. Also, we obtain Eisenstein series identities using Ramanujan’s ψ 1 1 {}_{1}\psi_{1} summation formula.



Keywords:
Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction, Eisenstein series, theta function.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2020:
11A55, 14K25, 20C20, 30B70.

1.
Introduction

Classical theta functions provide a strong framework for studying Ramanujan’s continued fractions, which play key role in the theory of special functions. Ramanujan introduced several continued fractions which have deep connections to
q
-series, Eisenstein series, and modular equations. A detailed description of Ramanujan’s continued fractions and
q
-series identities can be found in his notebooks
[
12
,
14
]
and in extensive commentaries of Berndt
[
5
]
. In the recent years, many mathematicians found the theory of continued fractions intriguing and worth revisiting.

The Entry 12
[
5
, p. 24]
in Ramanujan’s second notebook gives the following general form of continued fraction. Let
k,l,q\in\mathbb{C}
with
|kl|<1
and
|q|<1
or that
k=l^{2m+1}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

(1)

\displaystyle\dfrac{(k^{2}q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}(l^{2}q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(k^{2}q;q^{4})_{\infty}(l^{2}q;q^{4})_{\infty}}=\dfrac{1}{(1-kl)+\dfrac{(k-lq)(l-kq)}{(1-kl)(q^{2}+1)+\dfrac{(k-lq^{3})(l-kq^{3})}{(1-kl)(q^{4}+1)+\ldots}}}.

The above continued fraction can also be found in
[
1
]
. Ramanujan
[
12
,
13
]
dealt with various special cases of the above expression and recorded algebraic relations on them. The following is a special case of general continued fraction, known as Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction.

(2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}(q):=q^{1/2}\dfrac{f(-q,-q^{7})}{f(-q^{3},-q^{5})}=\dfrac{q^{1/2}}{1+q+\dfrac{q^{2}}{1+q^{3}+\dfrac{q^{4}}{1+q^{5}+\ldots}}},

where

(3)

\displaystyle f(\gamma,\delta)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma^{j(j+1)/2}\delta^{j(j-1)/2},\quad|\gamma\delta|<1,

is the well-known Ramanujan’s general theta function
[
5
, p. 35, Entry 19]
. Ramanujan recorded the above product representation of
\mathfrak{H}(q)
in his notebook
[
12
, p. 229]
. Göllnitz
[
9
]
and Gordon
[
10
]
rediscovered and established the same product representation, being unaware of Ramanujan’s work. Later, Andrews
[
4
]
proved (
2
) as corollary of a more general result. Ramanujan
[
12
, p. 229]
also documented the following identities associated to
\mathfrak{H}(q)
, proof of which can be found in Berndt’s book
[
5
, p. 221]
:

(4)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\mathfrak{H}(q)}-\mathfrak{H}(q)=\dfrac{\varphi(q^{2})}{q^{1/2}\psi(q^{4})}

and

(5)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\mathfrak{H}(q)}+\mathfrak{H}(q)=\dfrac{\varphi(q)}{q^{1/2}\psi(q^{4})},

where

\displaystyle\varphi(q):=f(q,q)=\frac{(-q;-q)_{\infty}}{(q;-q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{j^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\psi(q):=f(q,q^{3})=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{j(j+1)/2}.

A lot of wonderful work on Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction can be found in the literature. Chan and Huang
[
6
]
established relations between
\mathfrak{H}(q)
and
\mathfrak{H}(q^{n})
using modular equations of degree
n
. Further, they obtained the explicit evaluations as well.
Vasuki and Srivatsa Kumar
[
16
]
obtained three new identities connecting
\mathfrak{H}(q)
with
\mathfrak{H}(q^{5})
,
\mathfrak{H}(q^{7})
and
\mathfrak{H}(q^{11})
. Moreover, during this process, they provided a whole new simple approach to arrive at the relation connecting
\mathfrak{H}(q)
and
\mathfrak{H}(q^{3})
which was previously established by Chan and Huang using a different method. Recently, Chaudhary and Vanitha
[
8
]
established several Eisenstein series identities associated to
\mathfrak{H}(q)
and explored the relations to combinatorial partition identities.

Naika
et al.

[
11
]
dealt with the continued fraction of order four which is defined as follows.

(6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}(q):=\dfrac{f(-q,-q^{3})}{f(-q^{2},-q^{2})}=\dfrac{1}{1+\dfrac{q}{1+\dfrac{q^{2}+q}{1+\dfrac{q^{3}}{1+\dfrac{q^{4}+q^{2}}{1+\ldots}}}}}.

They established results on
\mathfrak{I}(q)
those are analogous to Rogers-Ramanujan and cubic continued fractions.

Adiga
et al.

[
3
]
proved identities connecting Ramanujan’s cubic continued fraction with a continued fraction of of order six. Further, they established the associated Eisenstein series identities as well. Also, for similar work on continued fractions of order twelve and sixteen,
[
2
]
and
[
15
]
can be referred respectively.

On employing the product expansion of Jacobi’s theta function, the identities relating continued fractions of different orders can be derived. The purpose of this article is to obtain a family of new identities involving Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction. The evaluation of Jacobi’s theta function
\theta_{1}
at special arguments
z=\dfrac{\pi}{8},\dfrac{2\pi}{8},\dfrac{3\pi}{8}
and utilization of their product representations yield the explicit expressions for the infinite products

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}(q)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+\beta_{k}q^{n}+q^{2n}),\qquad 1\leq k\leq 3,

where the coefficients
\beta_{k}
are algebraic numbers arising from fourth root of unity. These products play an important role in relating theta functions to Ramanujan’s continued fractions. In Section 2, we provide all the definitions and preliminary results required. In Section 3, we give the identities that involve Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction and continued fraction of order four. In Section 4, we establish new Eisenstein series identities associated to Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction.

2.
Definitions and Preliminary results

Throughout the article, let
q\in\mathbb{C}
, such that
|q|<1
. Then
q
-Pochhammer symbol or
q
-shifted factorial
[
5
]
is defined as follows. For any
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z^{+}}
,

\displaystyle(\delta;q)_{0}:=1,\quad(\delta;q)_{m}:=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\delta q^{j-1})\quad\text{and}\quad(\delta;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-\delta q^{j}).

For each
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
, the above product is uniformly and absolutely convergent in every compact subset of the unit disc
|q|<1
. We use the following notation for ease.

\displaystyle(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{m};q)_{\infty}=(\delta_{1};q)_{\infty}(\delta_{2};q)_{\infty}\ldots(\delta_{m};q)_{\infty}.

The Jacobi’s triple product identity states that for
z\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{\frac{j(j-1)}{2}}z^{j}=(q;q)_{\infty}(z;q)_{\infty}(q/z;q)_{\infty}.

After Ramanujan,

\displaystyle f(\beta,\gamma)=(-\beta;\beta\gamma)_{\infty}(-\gamma;\beta\gamma)_{\infty}(\beta\gamma;\beta\gamma)_{\infty},\qquad|\beta\gamma|<1.

Dedekind-eta function is defined as

(7)

\displaystyle\eta(\tau):=q^{1/24}(q;q)_{\infty},

where
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
,
Im\tau>0
.
The following results are useful in proving the main results.

(
[

]
)
.

We have

(8)

\displaystyle f(\gamma,\gamma\delta^{2})f(\delta,\gamma^{2}\delta)

\displaystyle=f(\gamma,\delta)\psi(\gamma\delta),

(9)

\displaystyle f(\gamma,\delta)+f(-\gamma,-\delta)

\displaystyle=2f(\gamma^{3}\delta,\gamma\delta^{3}),

and

(10)

\displaystyle f(\gamma,\delta)-f(-\gamma,-\delta)

\displaystyle=2\gamma f\left(\frac{\delta}{\gamma},\gamma^{5}\delta^{3}\right).

Subtracting (

\displaystyle f(-\gamma,-\delta)=f(\gamma^{3}\delta,\gamma\delta^{3})-\gamma f\left(\frac{\delta}{\gamma},\gamma^{5}\delta^{3}\right).

3.
Identities connecting Ramanujan-Göllnitz-Gordon continued fraction and Ramanujan’s continued fraction of order four

Jacobi’s theta function,
\theta_{1}
, is defined as

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(z|\tau)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{\frac{(2j+1)^{2}}{8}}\sin(2j+1)z

\displaystyle=2q^{1/8}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{\frac{j(j+1)}{2}}\sin(2j+1)z.

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{\frac{j(j+1)}{2}}\sin(2j+1)z

(13)

\displaystyle=2\sin z(q;q)_{\infty}(qe^{2iz};q)_{\infty}(qe^{-2iz};q)_{\infty}

Combining the above two equations, one can see that

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(z|\tau)

\displaystyle=2q^{1/8}\sin z(q;q)_{\infty}(qe^{2iz};q)_{\infty}(qe^{-2iz};q)_{\infty}

(14)

\displaystyle=iq^{1/8}e^{-iz}(q;q)_{\infty}(e^{2iz};q)_{\infty}(qe^{-2iz};q)_{\infty}.

Substituting

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\left(\frac{2\pi}{8}|\tau\right)=2q^{1/8}\left(\sin\frac{2\pi}{8}\right)(q;q)_{\infty}(qe^{\frac{4\pi i}{8}};q)_{\infty}(qe^{\frac{-4\pi i}{8}};q)_{\infty},

Multiplying all the three equations, upon using the identities

\displaystyle\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{8}\right)\sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{8}\right)\sin\left(\dfrac{3\pi}{8}\right)=\dfrac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\pi}{8}|\tau\right)\theta_{1}\left(\frac{2\pi}{8}|\tau\right)\theta_{1}\left(\frac{3\pi}{8}|\tau\right)=\dfrac{2\eta^{3}(\tau)\eta(8\tau)}{\eta(2\tau)}.
