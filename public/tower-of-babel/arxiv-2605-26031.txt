The formal degree conjecture for groups over local function fields
Anantha Krishna B

Abstract
In this article, we will prove that the formal degree conjecture is compatible with the Deligne-Kazhdan correspondence for quasi-split groups, assuming that the local Langlands correspondence is compatible with the Deligne-Kazhdan correspondence. Consequently, we establish the formal degree conjecture for GL n \GL_{n} over local function fields of characteristic p > 0 p>0 , and for Sp 2 ​ n \Sp_{2n} , split SO 2 ​ n \SO_{2n} , SO 2 ​ n + 1 \SO_{2n+1} , and GSp 4 \GSp_{4} over local function fields of characteristic p > 2 p>2 .

Introduction

Let
F
be a non-Archimedean local field and
G
be a connected, reductive group over
F
. Let
Z
be the split component of the center of
G
. For a complex discrete series representation
(\pi,V)
of

which depends on the chosen Haar measure

on

such that

\int_{Z(F)\backslash G(F)}\langle\pi(g)v,v^{\vee}\rangle{\langle\pi(g^{-1})w,w^{\vee}\rangle}\,d\mu=d(\pi,\mu)^{-1}\langle w,v^{\vee}\rangle{\langle v,w^{\vee}\rangle},

for all
v,w\in V
and
v^{\vee},w^{\vee}\in V^{\vee}
, where
V^{\vee}
is the contragredient of

and

is the natural pairing between

Assuming the local Langlands conjecture (LLC), the formal degree conjecture was formulated in
[

]
and
[
HII08a
]

\gamma
-factors. To formulate the conjecture, we normalize the Haar measure on

as in
[
HII08a
]
by choosing a non-trivial additive character
\psi
of
F.
We denote this measure by
\mu_{Z(F)\backslash G(F),\psi}
or simply
\mu_{Z\backslash G}
whenever the base field
F
and the character
\psi
are clear from the context.
The formal degree conjecture has been established in many cases for groups over characteristic
0
fields, including
\GL_{n}
in
[
HII08b
]
,
\SO_{2n+1}
in
[
ILM17
]
,
\GSp_{4}

[

]

\Sp_{2n}

\SO_{2n}

[

To resolve the formal degree conjecture in the positive characteristic case, we use the Deligne-Kazhdan correspondence. Let us briefly recall this:

i)

For any local field
F
of positive characteristic and an integer
m\geq 1,
there exists a local field
F^{\prime}
of characteristic
0
such that
F
is
m
-close to
F^{\prime}
(see Definition
2.1
).

ii)

Deligne
[
Del84
]
proved that if
F
and
F^{\prime}
are
m
-close, then there exists an isomorphism
\Gal(F_{s}/F)/I_{F}^{m}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Gal(F^{\prime}_{s}/F^{\prime})/I_{F^{\prime}}^{m},
where
I_{F}^{m}
is the
m
-th upper ramification subgroup of the inertia group
I_{F}.
This induces a bijection between the sets of isomorphism classes:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}Continuous, complex, finite-dimensional\\
representations of $\Gal(F_{s}/F)$ trivial on $I_{F}^{m}$\end{tabular}\right\}

\displaystyle\updownarrow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}Continuous, complex, finite-dimensional\\
representations of $\Gal(F^{\prime}_{s}/F^{\prime})$ trivial on $I_{F^{\prime}}^{m}$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Moreover, all of the above holds when

iii)

Let

[

]
proved that for any

Ganapathy
[

]
generalized Item

and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}Irreducible admissible representations $(\sigma,V)$ of $G(F)$\\

\displaystyle\Bigg\updownarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\Kaz_{m}$\hss}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}Irreducible admissible representations $(\sigma^{\prime},V^{\prime})$ of $G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})$\\

Our main theorem is

Let

2.C. Deligne’s theory

We summarize relevant concepts from Deligne’s work
[
Del84
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
m\geq 1.
The fields
F
and
F^{\prime}
are said to be
m
-close if there is a ring isomorphism
\mathfrak{o}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\simeq\mathfrak{o}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}
.

When
F
and
F^{\prime}
satisfy the above definition, then we write
F\sim_{m}F^{\prime}.

Deligne
[
Del84
]
considered the triplet

\mathrm{Tr}_{m}(F)=(\mathfrak{o}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m},\;\mathfrak{p}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m+1},\;\varepsilon),

where
\varepsilon
is the natural projection of
\mathfrak{p}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m+1}
onto

\mathfrak{p}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}
, and proved that
\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{m}
, together with its upper
numbering filtration, is canonically determined by
\mathrm{Tr}_{m}(F)
. Hence an isomorphism of
triplets
\theta_{m}:\mathrm{Tr}_{m}(F)\to\mathrm{Tr}_{m}(F^{\prime})
gives rise to an isomorphism

\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{m}\xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{Del}_{m}\;}\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{m},

(2.1)

that is unique up to inner automorphisms. Further, we have,

\Del_{m}(\Gamma_{F}^{r}/I_{F}^{m})=\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}^{r}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{m}

(2.2)

for all
r\leq m
. The equation
2.1
holds true if we replace absolute Galois groups by Weil groups. The isomorphism
W_{F}/I_{F}^{m}\cong W_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{m}
will be again denoted by
\Del_{m}
.

More precisely, given an integer
f\geq 0
, let
\mathrm{ext}(F)^{f}
be the category of finite separable
extensions
E/F
satisfying the following condition: the normal closure
E_{1}
of
E
in
F_{s}
satisfies

\mathrm{Gal}(E_{1}/F)^{f}=1
. Deligne proved that an isomorphism
\theta_{m}:\mathrm{Tr}_{m}(F)\to\mathrm{Tr}_{m}(F^{\prime})
induces an equivalence of categories
\mathrm{ext}(F)^{m}\to\mathrm{ext}(F^{\prime})^{m}
.

Let
F\sim_{m}F^{\prime}.
Consider additive characters
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
on
F
and
F^{\prime}
respectively such that
[
Del84
, Section 3.7]

•

Both
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
have equal conductor
k
,

•

\psi|_{\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{k-m}/{\mathfrak{p}^{k}_{F}}}=\psi^{\prime}|_{\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{k-m}/{\mathfrak{p}^{k}_{F^{\prime}}}}.

If the above conditions are satisfied, we write
\psi\sim_{m}\psi^{\prime}.

]

\begin{gathered}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Irreducible admissible representations }(\sigma,V)\text{ of }G(F)\\

\updownarrow\\
\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Irreducible admissible representations }(\sigma^{\prime},V^{\prime})\text{ of }G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})\\

We fix Haar measures

and

on the quotients

and

and

, respectively. Let
\Pi_{*}^{2}(G,F)_{m}
denote the set of equivalence classes of discrete series representations of

that have non-zero

-fixed vectors. We prove the following:

.

Let
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
correspond via the bijection mentioned above. Then
\sigma\in\Pi_{*}^{2}(G,F)_{m}
if and only if
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Pi_{*}^{2}(G^{\prime},F^{\prime})_{m}
. In this case,

Proof.

This generalizes Theorem 4.6 of
[

]
, which is stated for split reductive groups. Let

and

be such that

. Let

. Note that

is constant on the double coset

, and therefore

, where

is the characteristic function on
P_{m}gP_{m}
.

By Theorem

, we have
G(F)=\bigsqcup_{\tau\in\Omega_{T,+}}G_{\tau}
. Write

Note that
X_{*}(Z)
canonically sits inside
\bar{\Omega}_{T,+}
. Every element

can be uniquely written as

, where
\mu\in X_{*}(Z)
and
z_{1}\in Z(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
. Let
\tau=\tau_{1}\oplus\tau_{2}\in\bar{\Omega}_{T,+}\oplus(\Omega_{T})_{\tor}
. Then

zP_{m}k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m}=z_{1}P_{m}k_{i}p(\mu)p(\tau_{1})s(\tau_{2})k_{j}^{-1}P_{m}.

Since

is a group homomorphism, it follows that

zP_{m}k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m}=z_{1}P_{m}k_{i}p(\mu+\tau_{1})s(\tau_{2})k_{j}^{-1}P_{m}=z_{1}P_{m}k_{i}n_{\tau+\mu}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m}.

Thus,

zP_{m}k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m}=\begin{cases}P_{m}k_{i}n_{\mu+\tau}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m},&\text{if }z_{1}\in Z(F)\cap P_{m},\\
P_{m}l_{i}n_{\mu+\tau}l_{j}^{-1}P_{m},&\text{if }z_{1}\notin Z(F)\cap P_{m},\end{cases}

where

is an element of

that is uniquely determined by the class of
z_{1}\bmod Z(F)\cap P_{m}
.

Let
A_{F}
denote the set of representatives of the elements of
\Omega_{T,+}/X_{*}(Z)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{Z(F)\backslash G(F)}\lvert h_{\sigma}(g)\rvert^{2}\,d\bar{g}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in A_{F}}\sum_{(k_{i},k_{j})\in T_{\tau}}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(P_{m}k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m};dg)}{\#Z(\mathfrak{f}_{m})\operatorname{Vol}(Z(F)\cap P_{m};dz)}\lvert h_{\sigma}(k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in A_{F}}\sum_{(k_{i},k_{j})\in T_{\tau}}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(P_{m}k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m};dg)}{\#Z(\mathfrak{f}_{m})}\lvert h_{\sigma}(k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in A_{F}}\sum_{(k_{i},k_{j})\in T_{\tau}}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(P_{m}k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m};dg)^{-1}}{\#Z(\mathfrak{f}_{m})}\lvert\langle\sigma(t_{k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}})v,v^{\vee}\rangle\rvert^{2}.

Let
\gamma\colon\sigma^{P_{m}}\to{\sigma^{\prime}}^{P^{\prime}_{m}}
be the isomorphism induced by
\Kaz_{m}
. This gives rise to an isomorphism of dual spaces
\gamma^{\vee}\colon(\sigma^{\vee})^{P_{m}}\to({\sigma^{\prime}}^{\vee})^{P^{\prime}_{m}}
. We define
h^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})={v^{\prime}}^{\vee}(\sigma^{\prime}(g^{\prime})v^{\prime})
. Let
t^{\prime}_{g^{\prime}}
denote the characteristic function of
P^{\prime}_{m}g^{\prime}P^{\prime}_{m}
. By Proposition
2.11
, it follows that

\Vol(P_{m}k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}P_{m};dg)=\Vol(P^{\prime}_{m}k^{\prime}_{i}n_{\tau^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}_{j}}^{-1}P^{\prime}_{m};dg^{\prime}),

where
k^{\prime}_{i}
satisfies the equation
k^{\prime}_{i}\bmod P^{\prime}_{m}=p_{m}(k_{i}\bmod P_{m})
. It also follows that
\langle\sigma(t_{k_{i}n_{\tau}k_{j}^{-1}})v,v^{\vee}\rangle=\langle\sigma^{\prime}(t^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}_{i}n^{\prime}_{\tau^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}_{j}}^{-1}})v^{\prime},{v^{\prime}}^{\vee}\rangle
.

The isomorphism
\Omega_{T}\simeq\Omega_{T^{\prime}}
induces an isomorphism
\Omega_{T,+}\simeq\Omega_{T^{\prime},+}

[
Gan22
, p. 329]
. Recall that we have an isomorphism
\Phi_{m}\colon\Phi(G,S)\simeq\Phi(G^{\prime},S^{\prime})
. As
X_{*}(Z)=\{\lambda\in X_{*}(S):\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle=0\quad\forall\alpha\in\Phi(G,S)\}
, it follows that the image of
X_{*}(Z)
is
X_{*}(Z^{\prime})
under the above isomorphism. Thus, we have
\Omega_{T,+}/X_{*}(Z)\cong\Omega_{T^{\prime},+}/X_{*}(Z^{\prime})
. We have
X_{*}(Z)\cong X_{*}(Z^{\prime})
and
\dim(Z)=\dim X_{*}(Z)=\dim X_{*}(Z^{\prime})=\dim(Z^{\prime})
. Note that if

and
F^{\prime}
are
l
-close as in Theorem
2.12
, then
F
and
F^{\prime}
are

-close, and thus
\#Z(\mathfrak{f}_{m})=\#Z^{\prime}(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}_{m})
. Combining all of the above, we get that

\int_{Z(F)\backslash G(F)}\lvert h_{\sigma}(g)\rvert^{2}\,d\bar{g}=\int_{Z^{\prime}(F^{\prime})\backslash G^{\prime}(F^{\prime})}\lvert h^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(g^{\prime})\rvert^{2}\,d\bar{g}^{\prime}.

Hence,
\sigma\in\Pi^{2}_{*}(G,F)
if and only if
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Pi^{2}_{*}(G^{\prime},F^{\prime})
. Now, the proof that

proceeds identically to the proof of Corollary 4.7 of
[
Gan15
]
.

\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\sharp}\cong\mathcal{S}_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{\sharp}
and
\overline{S_{\varphi}}\cong\overline{S_{\varphi^{\prime}}}
.

Let
V=\Lie(\hat{G})/\Lie(Z(\hat{G})^{\Gamma_{F}})
. Define
V^{\prime}
for
\hat{G}^{\prime}
similarly. Let
\Ad\colon{}^{L}G\to\GL(V)
be the adjoint representation defined in the following way. Let
d\rho_{G}(\sigma)\colon\Lie(\hat{G})\to\Lie(\hat{G})
denote the derivative of
\rho_{G}(\sigma)\colon\hat{G}\to\hat{G}
at the identity. For simplicity of notation, we denote

by

.
Let
\Ad_{\hat{G}}\colon\hat{G}\to\GL(\Lie(\hat{G}))
be the adjoint representation of
\hat{G}
. Then
\Ad\colon{}^{L}G\to\GL(V)
is given by the formula

\Ad(\sigma,g)([X])=[\Ad_{\hat{G}}(g)(d\sigma(X))],

for

and

, where

.
Similarly, define

. Note that

is self-dual and factors through

. The
\Del_{m}
-equivariant isomorphism

induces a
\Del_{m}
-equivariant isomorphism

that takes

to

. Thus, we have a
\Del_{m}
-equivariant isomorphism
\bar{d\psi_{m}}\colon V\to V^{\prime}
. We claim that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}G_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GL(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{L}G^{\prime}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GL(V^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ad}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{G}}

where

(7.3)

Similarly, we obtain

where

Recall that the representation
\Ad\varphi\colon\WD_{F}\xrightarrow{\varphi}{}^{L}G\to\GL(V)
is a self-dual representation. Note that

factors through

whenever

.
Whenever

, we claim that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{W_{F}/I_{F}^{m}\times\SL_{2}}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ad^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}}

To see this, let

. We need to verify the following equation:

\bar{d\psi_{m}}\Ad\varphi(\sigma,x)=\Ad^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}(\Del_{m}(\sigma),x)\bar{d\psi_{m}}.

(7.6)

First, write
\varphi(\sigma,x)=(\sigma_{1},x_{1})\in{}^{L}G_{m}
.
Then
\varphi^{\prime}(\Del_{m}(\sigma),x)=(\Del_{m}(\sigma_{1}),\psi_{m}(x_{1}))
. The right-hand side of Equation

Using Equations
7.3
and

Let
\psi^{\prime}
be an additive character of
F^{\prime}
such that
\psi\sim_{m}\psi^{\prime}
. Fix additive measures on
F
and
F^{\prime}
such that
\Vol(\mathfrak{o}_{F})=\Vol(\mathfrak{o}_{F^{\prime}})
. We have the adjoint

, the adjoint gamma factor

, and the epsilon factor

. We prove the following proposition:

.

We have
L(\varphi,V,s)=L(\varphi^{\prime},V^{\prime},s)
,
\gamma(\varphi,V,s)=\gamma(\varphi^{\prime},V^{\prime},s)
, and
\epsilon(\varphi,V,s)=\epsilon(\varphi^{\prime},V^{\prime},s)
.

Recall that we have
V=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}V_{n}\otimes\Sym^{n}
and a similar expression for

. Note that
V_{n}=\Hom_{\SL_{2}}(\Sym^{n},V)
. The action of

on

, where
\sigma\in W_{F}
,

, and

.
The isomorphism
\bar{d\psi_{m}}\colon V\simeq V^{\prime}
induces an isomorphism
\alpha_{n}\colon V_{n}\to V_{n}^{\prime}
given by

\alpha_{n}(f)=\bar{d\psi_{m}}\circ f.

The commutativity of Diagram

implies that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{F}/I_{F}^{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GL(V_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GL(V_{n}^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Del_{m}}

, and
\alpha_{n}
induces an isomorphism

such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{n}^{I_{F}}}

\det\left(1-q^{-s-\frac{n}{2}}\varphi(\Fr)\;\middle|\;V_{n}^{I_{F}}\right)^{-1}=\det\left(1-q^{-s-\frac{n}{2}}\varphi^{\prime}(\Fr^{\prime})\;\middle|\;{V_{n}^{\prime}}^{I_{F^{\prime}}}\right)^{-1},

and thus

The fact that

is
\Del_{m}
-equivariant implies that the representation

is isomorphic to
V_{n}^{\prime}
. Because local epsilon factors are equal for isomorphic representations, it follows that the epsilon factors of

and

are equal. The equality of the local epsilon factors of

and

follows from an argument similar to that of Proposition 3.7.1 of

]
, applied to Weil groups instead of absolute Galois groups. It follows that
\omega(V_{n})=\omega(V_{n}^{\prime})

. The proof of the equality of Artin conductors

8
Formal degree conjecture over close local fields

The aim of this section is to prove that the formal degree conjecture holds if the Deligne-Kazhdan theory of close local fields is compatible with the LLC. Let
\Pi(G,F)_{m}
be the set of isomorphism classes of representations
\pi
of
G(F)
such that
\depth(\pi)\leq m
. Let
\Pi^{2}(G,F)_{m}
be the set of discrete series representations in
\Pi(G,F)_{m}

with

such that

is a bijection, and that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Pi(G,F)_{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{L}(G,F)_{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Pi(G^{\prime},F^{\prime})_{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime},F^{\prime})_{m}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\LLC}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Kaz_{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Del_{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\LLC}

(8.1)

and the following equality holds:

\dim(\rho_{\pi})=\dim(\rho_{\pi^{\prime}}).

(8.2)

If

, let
\psi^{\prime}
be an additive character of
F^{\prime}
such that
\psi\sim_{m}\psi^{\prime}
.

be a discrete series representation of

, and let

be the corresponding parameter. Let

and

be obtained based on commutative Diagram

Theorem 8.1
.

Set

. There exists

such that if

, then the formal degree conjecture holds for
(F,G,\varphi,\pi,\psi)
if and only if it holds for
(F^{\prime},G^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},\pi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Suppose the conductor of
\psi
is
k
. Then the self-dual Haar measure
\mu_{\psi}
on
F
satisfies
\mu_{\psi}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})=q^{k/2}
. Because the character
\psi^{\prime}
has conductor

, we have
\mu_{\psi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{o}_{F^{\prime}})=q^{k/2}
. Therefore,
\mu_{\psi}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})=\mu_{\psi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{o}_{F^{\prime}})
.

By Theorem
5.1
, there exists
l\geq m
such that whenever

, the Hecke algebra isomorphism
\Kaz_{m}\colon\mathcal{H}(G,P_{m})\cong\mathcal{H}(G^{\prime},P_{m}^{\prime})
implies the equality

. There exist

such that
\mu_{Z\backslash G}=c\,d\bar{g}
and
\mu_{Z^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}}=c^{\prime}\,d\bar{g}^{\prime}
.
We wish to show that

d(\pi,\mu_{Z\backslash G})=d(\pi^{\prime},\mu_{Z^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}}).

(8.3)

To show Equation
8.3
, it is sufficient to prove that
c=c^{\prime}
.

Recall that
d\bar{g}
is a measure on

such that

, where

and

\Vol(P_{m};dg)=1=\Vol(Z(F)\cap P_{m};dz)
. Let

be the measure on

such that

, and similarly define

on

. Then

\Vol(P_{m};d\nu)=\Vol(Q_{m};\mu_{Z\backslash G}),

where

. By Theorem
6.1
, Corollary

, and the fact that

, it follows that

\Vol(Q_{m};\mu_{Z\backslash G})=\Vol(Q_{m}^{\prime};\mu_{(Z\backslash G)^{\prime}}).

Also, we have

\Vol(Q_{m};\mu_{Z\backslash G})=c\Vol(Q_{m};d\bar{g})=c\Vol(P_{m};dg)=c.

Similarly,

\Vol(Q_{m}^{\prime};\mu_{(Z\backslash G)^{\prime}})=c^{\prime}.

Hence,
c=c^{\prime}
.

Proposition

and
7.2
imply
\#\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\sharp}=\#\mathcal{S_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{\sharp}}
and
\gamma(\varphi)=\gamma(\varphi^{\prime}).
By our assumption

, it follows that
\dim\rho_{\pi}=\dim\rho_{\pi^{\prime}}.
Thus, the theorem follows.
